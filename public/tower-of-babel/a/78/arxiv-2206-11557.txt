Commutative Banach Algebras Generated by Toeplitz Operators on the Bergman Space
Miguel Angel Rodriguez Rodriguez

Abstract
We present and study commutative Banach algebras generated by Toeplitz operators with generalized quasi-radial pseudo-homogeneous symbols acting on the Bergman space over the unit ball. We develop the Gelfand theory of these algebras and give some structural information about them. In particular, we provide a description of the radical of these algebras. This paper generalizes and completes the results from previous works related to Toeplitz operators with quasi-radial quasi-homogeneous symbols.

Let
\mathbb{B}^{n}
denote the unit ball of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and

\lambda>-1
consider the Bergman space
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
,
defined as the subspace of analytic functions of

Since not much can be said about

As it turns out,

and such that the

These algebras represent an interesting object of study and the main tool to obtain structural information from them is the Gelfand theory.
A first step towards the understanding of these algebras was done in

1
,
2

In this paper introduce a generalized version of these symbols and study the structure of the corresponding commutative Banach algebras by means of the Gelfand theory. In particular, we describe the radical of these algebras, a problem which had remained open in the previous works.

\displaystyle u_{1,1}

\displaystyle=u_{1},\kern 5.0ptu_{1,2}=u_{2},\kern 5.0pt\ldots,\kern 5.0ptu_{1,k_{1}}=u_{k_{1}}

\displaystyle u_{2,1}

2.1 Lemma

Given a

Generalized pseudo-homogeneous symbols

For every

We also denote by

3.1 Lemma
.

Let
c_{j}
be a generalized pseudo-homogeneous symbol as before. If
p_{(j)}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k_{j}}
with
|p_{(j)}|\neq 0
and
s_{(j)}\in S^{k_{j}-1}_{+}

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{k_{j}}}c_{j}(s_{(j)},t_{(j)})t_{(j)}^{p_{(j)}}d\mu_{j}=0.

Proof.

Let
g\in\mathbb{T}
be arbitrary. Consider the change of variables
t_{(j)}^{\prime}=g\cdot t_{(j)}
. By the invariance of

and of the measure we have

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{k_{j}}}c_{j}(s_{(j)},t^{\prime}_{(j)})(t^{\prime}_{(j)})^{p_{(j)}}d\mu_{j}(t^{\prime}_{(j)})=g^{|p_{(j)}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{k_{j}}}c_{j}(s_{(j)},t_{(j)})(t_{(j)})^{p_{(j)}}d\mu_{j}(t_{(j)}).

Thus either the integrals are zero or
|p_{(j)}|=0
.
∎

Given

Here the tuple

is located at the

Imitating the calculations from the proof of Lemma 3.2 from

3.2 Proposition
.

Let

1.

For any

For every

\displaystyle\langle

Let

\boldsymbol{T_{j}}=\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\boldsymbol{T_{j}}|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{(j)}_{d}},\quad j=1,\ldots,m.

Note that

Let

\mathcal{T}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m})

4.3 Proposition
.

If
\zeta

\operatorname{sp}_{pt}(\boldsymbol{T_{j}})

\zeta\in\operatorname{ess-sp}(\boldsymbol{T_{j}})

For each
j=1,\ldots,m

\zeta\in\operatorname{ess-sp}(\boldsymbol{T_{j}})
there is a sequence of unimodular vectors
(g^{(j)}_{\zeta,k})_{k}

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|(\boldsymbol{T_{j}}-\zeta I)g_{\zeta,k}^{(j)}\|=0.

(6)

This condition holds
whenever the functions

\overline{\mathbb{B}^{k_{j}}}

We describe briefly how to see this.
Note that this construction simplifies the calculations from Section 3 of
[
2

(for the case

where the reproducing kernel of a much larger space was considered.

For each

and
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

let

denote the reproducing kernel of
\mathbf{H}^{(j)}_{d}
.
By the multinomial theorem we have

K^{(j)}_{d}(z,w)=\sum_{|\alpha_{(j)}|=d}e_{\alpha_{(j)}}^{(j)}(z)\overline{e_{\alpha_{(j)}}^{(j)}(w)}=\frac{(k_{j}+d)!}{k_{j}!d!}\langle z,w\rangle^{d},\quad z,w\in\mathbb{B}^{k_{j}},

We denote the corresponding normalized reproducing kernels by

Proof.

It follows from similar arguments as in
[

estimating the corresponding integrals in
\mathbb{B}^{k_{j}}
instead of
\mathbb{B}^{2}

∎

If

For each

4.5 Corollary

Let

If

with

∎

4.6 Proposition
.

We have

M(\mathcal{T}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}))=M(\mathcal{T}_{1})\times\cdots\times M(\mathcal{T}_{m})=\widehat{\operatorname{sp}(T_{c_{1}})}\times\cdots\times\widehat{\operatorname{sp}(T_{c_{m}})}.

Proof.

We only need to prove the inclusion
\supset

By the properties of the polynomially convex hull we have

\widehat{\operatorname{sp}(T_{c_{1}})\times\cdots\times\operatorname{sp}(T_{c_{m}})}=\widehat{\operatorname{sp}(T_{c_{1}}})\times\cdots\times\widehat{\operatorname{sp}(T_{c_{m}})}.

Since
M(\mathcal{T}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}))
is polynomially convex,
it suffices to show that

\operatorname{sp}(T_{c_{1}})\times\cdots\times\operatorname{sp}(T_{c_{m}})\subset M(\mathcal{T}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m})).

Let thus
(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{m})\in\operatorname{sp}(T_{c_{1}})\times\cdots\times\operatorname{sp}(T_{c_{m}})
.

For each
j=1,\ldots,m
define the sequence
(g^{(j)}_{\zeta_{j},k})_{k}
in
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{k_{j}})
as follows: If
\zeta_{j}\in\operatorname{ess-sp}(\boldsymbol{T_{j}})
, let
(g^{(j)}_{\zeta_{j},k})_{k}
be a sequence such that (
6

Otherwise,
\zeta_{j}\in\operatorname{sp}_{pt}(\boldsymbol{T_{j}})
and thus then there is

such that

\zeta_{j}\in\operatorname{sp}_{pt}(\boldsymbol{T_{j}}|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{(j)}_{d}})
.
In this case we choose
(g^{(j)}_{\zeta_{j},k})_{k}
to be a constant sequence
g^{(j)}_{\zeta_{j},k}=v_{\zeta_{j}}
, where
v_{\zeta_{j}}
is a eigenvector in
\boldsymbol{H}^{(j)}_{d}
such that
\|v_{\zeta_{j}}\|=1
and
\boldsymbol{T_{j}}v_{\zeta_{j}}=\zeta_{j}v_{\zeta_{j}}
.

Since
UT_{c_{j}}U^{*}=I\otimes\cdots\otimes\boldsymbol{T_{j}}\otimes\cdots\otimes I

\langle UT_{c_{j}}U^{*}(g^{(1)}_{\zeta_{1},k}\otimes\cdots\otimes g^{(m)}_{\zeta_{m},k}),g^{(1)}_{\zeta_{1},k}\otimes\cdots\otimes g^{(m)}_{\zeta_{m},k}\rangle=\langle\boldsymbol{T_{j}}g^{(j)}_{\zeta_{j},k},g^{(j)}_{\zeta_{j},k}\rangle_{\mathcal{A}^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{k_{j}})},

\zeta_{j}

\zeta_{j}\in\operatorname{sp}_{pt}(\boldsymbol{T_{j}})

Therefore, for any polynomial

we can define

\psi(T)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\langle UTU^{*}(g^{(1)}_{\zeta_{1},k}\otimes\cdots\otimes g^{(m)}_{\zeta_{m},k}),g^{(1)}_{\zeta_{1},k}\otimes\cdots\otimes g^{(m)}_{\zeta_{m},k}\rangle.

.
∎

By Proposition

5.3 Lemma
.

Let
\theta\in\Theta
and
\kappa_{\theta}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\theta}
.
Consider the sets
D_{j}
given by

D_{j}=\begin{cases}\operatorname{sp}(\mathbf{T_{j}}|_{\mathbf{H}_{\kappa_{j}}^{(j)}}),\quad&j\in J_{\theta},\\
\operatorname{ess-sp}(\mathbf{T_{j}}),\quad&j\in J_{\theta^{\mathrm{C}}}.\end{cases}

Then we have
M_{\theta}(\kappa_{\theta})\times D_{1}\times\cdots\times D_{m}\subset M(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{d}))
.

Moreover, if
\psi\in M_{\theta}(\kappa_{\theta})\times D_{1}\times\cdots\times D_{m}
,
then there is a net
(g_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
such that

\lim_{\alpha}\|(T-\psi(T)I)g_{\alpha}\|=0.

Proof.

Let
\psi=(\mu,\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{m})\in M_{\theta}(\kappa_{\theta})\times D_{1}\times\cdots\times D_{m}
.
Consider a net
(\kappa_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m}
such that
\mu=\lim_{\alpha}\kappa_{\alpha}
.
Suppose that

J_{\theta}=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{|\theta|}\}
and

\kappa_{\theta}=(\kappa_{j_{1}},\ldots,\kappa_{j_{|\theta|}})
.

Since
\zeta_{j}\in\operatorname{sp}(\mathbf{T}_{j})
for each
j
, we can define the sequences
(f^{(j)}_{\zeta_{j},k})_{k}
as in the proof of Proposition
4.6
.
If
j\in J_{\theta}
, we can assume that

(\kappa_{\alpha})_{j}=\kappa_{j}
for all
\alpha

and that
f^{(j)}_{\zeta_{j},k}\in\mathbf{H}_{(\kappa_{\theta})_{j}}^{(j)}
for each

.

Thus we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|(\mathbf{T_{j}}-\zeta_{j})f^{(j)}_{\zeta_{j},k}\|=0,

for each
j=1,\ldots,m
.

Using the same directed set of indices
\alpha
we define a net
(g_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
by

g_{\alpha}=U^{*}(f^{(1)}_{\zeta_{1},\kappa^{1}_{\alpha}}\otimes\cdots\otimes f^{(m)}_{\zeta_{m},\kappa^{m}_{\alpha}})\in H_{\kappa_{\alpha}}.

Then we have

\lim_{\alpha}\|(T_{c_{j}}-\zeta_{j})g_{\alpha}\|=0,\quad j=1,\ldots,m,

and, since
D_{\gamma}(g_{\alpha})=\gamma(\kappa_{\alpha})g_{\alpha}

\lim_{\alpha}\|(D_{\gamma}-\mu)g_{\alpha}\|=\lim_{\alpha}|\gamma(\kappa_{\alpha})-\mu|=0.

Thus, by these properties, for an operator

\lim_{\alpha}\|(A-\psi(A))g_{\alpha}\|=\lim_{\alpha}\|(A-\sum_{\rho\in F_{A}}\gamma_{\rho}(\kappa_{\alpha})\zeta^{\rho})g_{\alpha}\|=0.

We can extend this formula

and, in particular, we can define the functional

obtaining thus a multiplicative functional, corresponding to the point

5.4 Theorem
.

The compact set of maximal ideals

M(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{d}))
of the commutative
Banach algebra

\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{d})
coincides with the set

M:=\bigcup_{\theta\in\Theta}\left[\bigcup_{\kappa_{\theta}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\theta}}M_{\theta}(\kappa_{\theta})\times M_{\theta,\kappa_{\theta},1}\times\cdots\times M_{\theta,\kappa_{\theta},m}\right],

where

M_{\theta,\kappa_{\theta},j}=\begin{cases}\operatorname{sp}(\mathbf{T_{j}}|_{\mathbf{H}_{\kappa_{j}}^{(j)}}),\quad&j\in J_{\theta},\\
\widehat{\operatorname{ess-sp}}(\mathbf{T_{j}}),\quad&j\in J_{\theta^{\mathrm{C}}}.\end{cases}

The Gelfand transform is generated by the following mapping
of elements of
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{d})}
:

\displaystyle A:=\sum_{\rho\in F_{A}}D_{\gamma_{\rho}}T^{\rho}\longmapsto\sum_{\rho\in F_{A}}D_{\gamma}(\mu)\zeta^{\rho}.

Proof.

We prove the inclusion
M\subset M(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{d}))
.
The other inclusion follows from Lemmas
5.1
and
5.2
.

Fix some
\theta\in\Theta
and
\kappa_{\theta}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\theta}

and consider the sets
D_{1},\ldots,D_{m}
as in Lemma
5.3
.
By this lemma,

M_{\theta}(\kappa_{\theta})\times D_{1}\times\cdots\times D_{m}\subset M(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{d}))
.

Write
D=D_{1}\times\cdots\times D_{m}
. Then we clearly have
\widehat{D_{j}}=M_{\theta,\kappa_{\theta},j}

and
\widehat{D}=\widehat{D_{1}}\times\cdots\times\widehat{D_{m}}
,
and thus we need to prove that
M_{\theta}(\kappa_{\theta})\times\widehat{D}\subset M(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{d}))
.

Let
(\mu,\zeta)
be an element of the left-hand side set in this inclusion.
By the definition of the polynomial convex hull and Lemma
5.3
,
for any operator
A\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{d})}
of the form

A=\sum_{\rho\in F_{A}}D_{\gamma_{\rho}}T^{\rho}
we have

|\sum_{\rho\in F_{A}}\gamma_{\rho}(\mu)\zeta^{\rho}|\leq\sup_{z\in D}|\sum_{\rho\in F_{A}}\gamma_{\rho}(\mu)z^{\rho}|\leq\sup_{\varphi\in M_{\theta}(\kappa_{\theta})\times D}|\varphi(A)|\leq\|A\|.

Therefore, there is a well-defined bounded multiplicative functional
\psi
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{d})}
given by

\psi(A)=\sum_{\rho\in F_{A}}\gamma_{\rho}(\mu)\zeta^{\rho},\quad A=\sum_{\rho\in F_{A}}D_{\gamma_{\rho}}T^{\rho}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{d})}.

This functional extends then to a multiplicative functional on
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{d})
, which corresponds to the point
(\mu,\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{m})
.
∎

6
Spectral invariance

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a Banach algebra of operators acting on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
.
We say that
\mathcal{B}
is
spectral invariant
if each operator from
\mathcal{B}
invertible in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{n}))
is invertible in
\mathcal{B}
.
In this case, the spectrum of an operator
T
in
\mathcal{B}
, denoted by
\sigma_{\mathcal{B}}(T)
,
equals its spectrum in the
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{n}))
.

6.1 Proposition
.

The algebra
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{d})
is inverse closed if and only if

\operatorname{sp}(T_{c_{j}})
is polynomially convex for every
j=1,\ldots,m
.

Thus, in this case,

\sigma_{\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{d})}(T)=\operatorname{sp}(T),

for every
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{d})
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\operatorname{sp}(T_{c_{j}})
is polynomially convex for every
j=1,\ldots,m
.
Let
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{d})
be invertible in the
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{n}))
and let
T^{-1}
be its inverse.

By Lemma
5.3
and Theorem
5.4
,
for every
\psi\in M(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{d}))
there is a net of normalized vectors
(g_{\alpha})_{\alpha}

such that

\lim_{\alpha}\|(T-\psi(T)I)g_{\alpha}\|=0,\quad T\in\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{d}).

Thus we have

1=\langle T^{-1}Tg_{\alpha},g_{\alpha}\rangle=\psi(T)\langle T^{-1}g_{\alpha},g_{\alpha}\rangle+\langle T^{-1}(T-\psi(T)I)g_{\alpha},g_{\alpha}\rangle,

where the second summand converges to zero.
In particular,
\psi(T)\neq 0
and, thus,
T
is invertible in
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{d})
.

Conversely, suppose that
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{d})
is spectral invariant.
Then

\sigma_{\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{d})}(T_{c_{j}})=\operatorname{sp}(T_{c_{j}}),

but, by Theorem
5.4
,

\sigma_{\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{d})}(T_{c_{j}})=\widehat{\operatorname{sp}(T_{c_{j}})}
.
∎

7
The radical of
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{d})

In this section we will assume that
c_{j}
,
regarded as a function defined on
\mathbb{B}^{k_{j}}
,
can be continuously extended to the closed unit ball of
\overline{\mathbb{B}^{k_{j}}}
.
By Proposition
4.4
and the remarks after it, this stronger condition implies (

).
Furthermore, as is well known, in this case the operators
\mathbf{T_{j}}
acting on
\mathcal{A}^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{k_{j}})
commute with their respective adjoints
\mathbf{T_{j}}^{*}
modulo a compact operator.

Given

and
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m}
we will write

\widetilde{Q}_{\kappa_{j}}^{(j)}=\bigoplus_{0\leq d\leq\kappa_{j}}Q^{(j)}_{d}.

Thus

7.3 Proposition
.

The algebra
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{d})
is semi-simple if and only if

\mathbf{T_{j}}|_{\mathbf{H}^{(j)}_{d}}

Proof.

Suppose every matrix
\mathbf{T_{j}}|_{\mathbf{H}^{(j)}_{d}}
is as stated.
If there is
0\neq T\in\operatorname{Rad}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{d}))
,
then there is
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m}
such that

.

Our hypothesis implies that for every

there is a linear (not necessarily orthonormal) basis for
\mathbf{H}^{(j)}_{\kappa_{j}}
formed by normalized eigenvectors of
\mathbf{T_{j}}
.
Thus the corresponding tensor products form a linear basis for
U(H_{\kappa})

One can easily see that for every such tensor product

v

S(U^{*}(v))=\psi(S)U^{*}(v)
,
where
\psi
is the multiplicative functional defined by

corresponding to some point

(\kappa,\zeta)\in M(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{d}))
,
where
\zeta\in\operatorname{sp}_{pt}(\mathbf{T_{1}}|_{\mathbf{H}^{(1)}_{\kappa_{1}}})\times\cdots\times\operatorname{sp}_{pt}(\mathbf{T_{m}}|_{\mathbf{H}^{(m)}_{\kappa_{m}}})

Since
T|_{H_{\kappa}}\neq 0
, there is such a tensor

with

, but then we have the contradiction
T(U^{*}(v))=\psi(T)U^{*}(v)=0
.

Conversely, if there are some
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
and

such that

\mathbf{T_{j}}|_{\mathbf{H}^{(j)}_{d}}
fails to be diagonalizable,
then by Theorem
5.4

\operatorname{Rad}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{d}))\ni Q^{(j)}_{d}h^{j,d}_{n_{j,d}}(\mathbf{T_{j}}|_{\mathbf{H}^{(j)}_{d}})\neq 0.

∎

As in previous works, one step towards the description of the radical is the description of the subalgebra

7.4 Proposition

If

7.6 Lemma
.

Consider a tensor product of Hilbert spaces
H_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes H_{M}

and let
j\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
.
Let
w
and
w^{\prime}
be fixed vectors in
H_{j}

with
\|w\|=\|w^{\prime}\|
, let

s
be a sum of tensors of the form

v^{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v^{j-1}\otimes w\otimes v^{j+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v^{M}

and let

s^{\prime}
be equal
s
but changing in every summand
w
by
w^{\prime}
.
Then
\|s\|=\|s^{\prime}\|
.

Proof.

Suppose that
s=\sum_{i\in I}v^{1}_{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes v^{j-1}_{i}\otimes w\otimes v^{j+1}_{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes v^{M}_{i}

and

s^{\prime}=\sum_{i\in I}v^{1}_{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes v^{j-1}_{i}\otimes w^{\prime}\otimes v^{j+1}_{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes v^{M}_{i}
. Then

\displaystyle\|s\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I,i^{\prime}\in I}\langle v^{1}_{i},v^{1}_{i^{\prime}}\rangle\cdots\langle w,w\rangle\cdots\langle v^{M}_{i},v^{M}_{i^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I,i^{\prime}\in I}\langle v^{1}_{i},v^{1}_{i^{\prime}}\rangle\cdots\langle w^{\prime},w^{\prime}\rangle\cdots\langle v^{M}_{i},v^{M}_{i^{\prime}}\rangle=\|s^{\prime}\|^{2}.\qed

We recall that the image of the projection
Q^{(j)}_{d}
is
the closed span of all basic elements
e_{\alpha}
such that
|\alpha_{(j)}|=d

and that, by construction, we have
e_{\alpha}=U^{*}(e^{(1)}_{\alpha_{(1)}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e^{(m)}_{\alpha_{(m)}})
,
for every

.

For a function

with

we define
the space

as the closed span of all functions of the form

Thus, for example,

For a fixed tuple

we define

R^{(j)}_{\alpha_{(j)},g}\colon H^{(j)}(e^{(j)}_{\alpha_{(j)}})\longrightarrow H^{(j)}(g)

We note that, by Lemma

\|R^{(j)}_{\alpha_{(j)},g}(f)\|=\|f\|

Moreover, every

Hence we have

\|f\|^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha_{(j)}|=d\end{subarray}}\|f_{\alpha_{(j)}}\|^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha_{(j)}|=d\end{subarray}}\|R^{(j)}_{\alpha_{(j)},g}(f_{\alpha_{(j)}})\|^{2}.

7.7 Lemma
.

Using the notations from Lemma

the assignments

We proceed by induction on

Let

By construction we have

T_{c_{j}}
does not appear in the expression of

\displaystyle\|S^{j,d}_{0}(A)f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\sum_{|\alpha_{(j)}|=d}S^{j,d}_{0}(A)(f_{\alpha_{(j)}})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(C^{j,d}_{0})^{2}\sum_{|\alpha_{(j)}|=d}\|S^{j,d}_{0}(A)(f_{\alpha_{(j)}})\|^{2}

Now suppose we have constructed the constants

Using the decomposition from Lemma

\displaystyle S^{\prime}(A)

\displaystyle=Q^{(j)}_{d}A-S_{0}^{j,d}(A)-\cdots-S_{\ell}^{j,d}(A)h^{j,d}_{\ell}(T_{c_{j}})

\displaystyle=S^{j,d}_{n_{j,d}}(A)h^{j,d}_{n_{j,d}}(T_{c_{j}})+\cdots+S^{j,d}_{\ell+1}(A)h^{j,d}_{\ell+1}(T_{c_{j}}),

for every
A\in Q^{(j)}_{d}\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}^{\theta}
.

By induction hypothesis,
S^{\prime}
is continuous with

\|S^{\prime}\|\leq 1+C^{j,d}_{0}+\cdots+C^{j,d}_{\ell}
.
Reasoning similarly as before, let
g\in\mathbf{H}^{(j)}_{d}
be an unimodular eigenvector
such that

\mathbf{T_{j}}g=\zeta^{j,d}_{\ell+2}g

and
f\in H^{(j)}_{d}
with
f=\sum_{|\alpha_{(j)}|=d}f_{\alpha_{(j)}}
, as above.

Since

Q^{(j)}_{d}S^{\prime}(A)R^{(j)}_{\alpha_{(j)},g}(f_{\alpha_{(j)}})=S^{j,d}_{\ell+1}(A)(\zeta^{j,d}_{\ell+2}-\zeta^{j,d}_{\ell+1})\cdots(\zeta^{j,d}_{\ell+2}-\zeta^{j,d}_{1})R^{(j)}_{\alpha_{(j)},g}(f_{\alpha_{(j)}})

and the operator
T_{c_{j}}
does not appear in the expression of
S^{j,d}_{\ell+1}(A)
,
we can use similar arguments as in the case
\ell=0

and obtain

\displaystyle\|S^{j,d}_{\ell+1}(A)f\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(C^{j,d}_{0})^{2}|\zeta^{j,d}_{\ell+2}-\zeta^{j,d}_{\ell+1}|^{-2}\cdots|\zeta^{j,d}_{\ell+2}-\zeta^{j,d}_{1}|^{-2}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{|\alpha_{(j)}|=d}\|Q^{(j)}_{d}S^{\prime}(A)(R^{(j)}_{\alpha_{(j)},g}(f_{\alpha_{(j)}}))\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(C^{j,d}_{\ell+1})^{2}\|A\|^{2}\|f\|^{2},

where

C^{j,d}_{\ell+1}=|\zeta^{j,d}_{\ell+2}-\zeta^{j,d}_{\ell+1}|^{-1}\cdots|\zeta^{j,d}_{\ell+2}-\zeta^{j,d}_{1}|^{-1}C_{0}^{j,d}\left[\left(1+C^{j,d}_{0}+\cdots+C^{j,d}_{\ell}\right)\right]^{1/2}.\qed

The next corollary helps us understand what the operators from
the radical
\operatorname{Rad}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\theta})
look like on the spaces
H^{(j)}_{d}
.

7.8 Corollary
.

If
T\in\operatorname{Rad}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\theta})
,
then there are unique operators

S(T)\in\operatorname{clos}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}^{\theta}\cap\operatorname{Rad}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\theta}))

and
S^{j,d}_{\ell}(T)\in\operatorname{Rad}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\theta-\epsilon_{j}}),\ell=0,\ldots,n_{j,d}-1
,
such that

\displaystyle Q^{(j)}_{d}T

\displaystyle=S(T)

\displaystyle+S^{j,d}_{n_{j,d}-1}(T)h^{j,d}_{n_{j,d}-1}(T_{c_{j}})

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle+S^{j,d}_{1}(T)h^{j,d}_{1}(T_{c_{j}})

\displaystyle+S^{j,d}_{0}(T).

Here
S
represents the extension of the map

A\longmapsto S^{j,d}_{n_{j,d}}(A)h^{j,d}_{n_{j,d}}(T_{c_{j}})

from Lemma
7.5

to the algebra
Q^{(j)}_{d}\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\theta}
.

Proof.

By the previous lemma, the maps
S^{j,d}_{\ell}
are continuous and
so we obtain the formula from the lemma by approximating
T
with elements of
the dense subalgebra
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}^{\theta}
.

By Lemma
7.5
, we clearly have

S(T)\in\operatorname{clos}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}^{\theta}\cap\operatorname{Rad}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\theta}))
.
Thus we only need to show that

S^{j,d}_{\ell}(T)\in\operatorname{Rad}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\theta-\epsilon_{j}})
,

\ell=1,\ldots,n_{j,d}-1
.
Note that for such
\ell
we have
Q^{(j)}_{d}S^{j,d}_{\ell}(T)=S^{j,d}_{\ell}(T)
.
In particular,
\psi(S^{j,d}_{\ell}(T))=0
for every functional
\psi=(\mu,\zeta)\in M(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{d}))
such that
\mu
is reached by a net
(\kappa^{\alpha})

such that
\kappa^{\alpha}_{j}
does not converge to
d
.
Hence it suffices to prove
that
\psi(S^{j,d}_{\ell}(T))=0

where
\psi=(\mu,\zeta)\in M(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{d}))
and
\mu

is reached by a net
(\kappa^{\alpha})
with
\kappa^{\alpha}=d
.
Denote by
M_{j,d}
the set of all such multiplicative functionals
\psi
.
By Theorem
5.4
, if
(\mu,\zeta)\in M_{j,d}
,
then
\zeta_{j}\in\operatorname{sp}(\mathbf{T_{j}}|_{\mathbf{H}_{d}^{(j)}})
.

We proceed by induction.
Consider first the case
\ell=0
.
Let
\psi=(\mu,\zeta)\in M_{j,d}

and let
\zeta^{\prime}
be equal
\zeta
with the possible exception that

\zeta^{\prime}_{j}=\zeta^{j,d}_{1}
. Then
h^{j,d}_{\ell}(\zeta^{\prime}_{j})=0
,
for any
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,n_{j,d}\}
, and thus for the functional
\psi^{\prime}=(\mu,\zeta^{\prime})
we have

\psi^{\prime}(S^{j,d}_{0}(T))=\psi^{\prime}(Q^{(j)}_{d}T)=0
.

Since
S^{j,d}_{0}(T)\in\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\theta-\epsilon_{j}}
,
the operator
S^{j,d}_{0}(T)

does not depend on the operator
T_{c_{j}}
.
Then
\psi(S^{j,d}_{0}(T))
does not depend on
\zeta_{j}
and hence
\psi(S^{j,d}_{0}(T))=\psi^{\prime}(S^{j,d}_{0}(T))=0
.

Suppose we have proved that

\psi(S^{j,d}_{\ell}(T))=0
for every
\psi\in M_{j,d}
and

0\leq\ell<L<n_{j,d}-1
.

Then, by subtracting the first
L-1
summands in the expression for
Q^{(j)}_{d}T
,
we see that the operator

T_{L}=S(T)+S^{j,d}_{n_{j,d}-1}(T)h^{j,d}_{n_{j,d}-1}(T_{c_{j}})+\cdots+S^{j,d}_{L}(T)h^{j,d}_{L}(T_{c_{j}})

belongs to
\operatorname{Rad}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\theta})
.

Let
\psi=(\mu,\zeta)\in M_{j,d}
and define
\psi^{\prime}=(\mu,\zeta^{\prime})

where
\zeta^{\prime}
equals
\zeta

with the possible difference that
\zeta^{\prime}_{j}=\zeta^{j,d}_{L+1}
.
Therefore we have

(\zeta^{j,d}_{L+1}-\zeta^{j,d}_{L})\cdots(\zeta^{j,d}_{L+1}-\zeta^{j,d}_{1})\psi^{\prime}(S^{j,d}_{L}(T))=\psi^{\prime}(T_{L})=0.

Since, once again,
\psi(S^{j,d}_{L}(T))
does not depend on
\zeta_{j}
,
we conclude that
\psi(S^{j,d}_{L}(T))=\psi^{\prime}(S^{j,d}_{L}(T))=0
.
∎

Now we are able to prove the main result of this section.

7.9 Theorem
.

Let
\theta\in\Theta
.
Then
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}^{\theta}\cap\operatorname{Rad}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\theta})
is dense in

\operatorname{Rad}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\theta})
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
|\theta|
.

Suppose
|\theta|=0
.
In this case
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\theta}=\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(L^{\infty}_{k-qr})
,
which is a
C^{*}
-algebra and then
\operatorname{Rad}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\theta})=\{0\}
,
from which the claim follows.

As induction hypothesis assume that

\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}^{\theta}\cap\operatorname{Rad}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\theta})

is dense in

\operatorname{Rad}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\theta})
for every
\theta\in\Theta
such that
|\theta|\leq m^{\prime}<m
.

Let

\theta\in\Theta
with
|\theta|=m^{\prime}+1
and let
T\in\operatorname{Rad}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\theta})
.
By Lemma
7.2
,
we have
T\in\mathcal{J^{\theta}}
and therefore there
is some operator
T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{J^{\theta}}
with

\|T-T^{\prime}\|<\varepsilon

and

T^{\prime}=\sum_{j\in J^{\theta}}\widetilde{Q}^{(j)}_{\kappa_{j}}S_{j},

for some operators
S_{j}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{n}))

and
\kappa_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Let
P_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,m
, be the projections
defined by (
8
) and (
9
). By Lemma
7.1
we have
T^{\prime}=\sum_{j\in J^{\theta}}P_{j}T^{\prime}
.
Define now the projection
P_{0}=I-\sum_{j\in J^{\theta}}P_{j}
.
Note that this is the orthogonal projection from
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{n})

onto the orthogonal complement of the space generated by the images of
the operators
\widetilde{Q}^{(j)}_{\kappa_{j}}
,
j\in J^{\theta}
.

Then we have

T^{\prime}=(I-P_{0})T^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad P_{0}T^{\prime}=0,

and thus

\|T-(I-P_{0})T\|=\|P_{0}T\|=\|P_{0}(T-T^{\prime})\|\leq\|T-T^{\prime}\|<\varepsilon.

Therefore, it suffices to show that

(I-P_{0})T=\sum_{j\in J^{\theta}}P_{j}T\in\operatorname{clos}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}^{\theta}\cap\operatorname{Rad}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\theta})\right).

Since the projections
P_{j}
are mutually orthogonal,
each operator
P_{j}T
is in the radical and, by the definition of
P_{j}
,
can be written in the form
A\widetilde{Q}^{(j)}_{\kappa_{j}}T
for some

A\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}^{\theta}
.
Thus we are done if we show that

Q^{(j)}_{d}T\in\operatorname{clos}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}^{\theta}\cap\operatorname{Rad}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\theta})\right),

for every
0\leq d\leq\kappa_{j}
and
j\in J^{\theta}
.

Fix some
j
and
d
as above.
By Corollary
7.8
, we have

Q^{(j)}_{d}T=S(T)+S^{j,d}_{n_{j,d}-1}(T)h^{j,d}_{n_{j,d}-1}(T_{c_{j}})+\cdots+S^{j,d}_{1}(T)h^{j,d}_{1}(T_{c_{j}})+S^{j,d}_{0}(T),

where
S(T)\in\operatorname{clos}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}^{\theta}\cap\operatorname{Rad}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\theta}))
and

S^{j,d}_{\ell}(T)\in\operatorname{Rad}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\theta-\epsilon_{j}})

for every
\ell=0,\ldots,n_{j,d}-1
.
Since
|\theta-\epsilon_{j}|=m^{\prime}
, by induction hypothesis
the operators
S^{j,d}_{\ell}(T)
lie in the algebra

\operatorname{Rad}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\theta-\epsilon_{j}})=\operatorname{clos}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}^{\theta-\epsilon_{j}}\cap\operatorname{Rad}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\theta-\epsilon_{j}})\right)\subset\operatorname{clos}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\lambda}^{\theta}\cap\operatorname{Rad}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{\theta})\right).\qed

Taking
\theta=\mathbf{1}
, we get

7.10 Corollary
.

The algebra
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{d})}\cap\operatorname{Rad}(\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{d}))

is dense in
\operatorname{Rad}(\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{d}))
.

We recover as particular cases the characterizations of the radical for
the Banach algebras that were studied in

[
3
]
,
[
2
]
(for the case
m=1
) and
[
8
]
, and
we obtain the corresponding missing result in

[
6
]
.
Furthermore,
changing some notation and details, our method
can be adapted without much effort
to obtain an analogous characterization for the Banach algebras
generated by Toeplitz operators with quasi-radial quasi-homogeneous symbols
(for a general
m\geq 1
),
giving thus a solution to the open problem mentioned at the end of Section 6 of

[
2
]
.
In all these cases, the radical equals the closure of its intersection with
the non-closed dense subalgebra of finite sums of finite products of the generators.

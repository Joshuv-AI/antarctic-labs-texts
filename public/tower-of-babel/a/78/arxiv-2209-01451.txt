A proof of the Generalized Jacobian conjecture The author is supported by start-up funds of No.190738 in College of Sciences, China jiliang University.


Abstract
Based on the reduction of degree in polynomial mappings and some known results in algebraic geometry, by introducing the Brouwer degree, a tool from differential topology, algebraic topology and algebraic geometry, we completely prove the Generalized Jacobian conjecture in the field of real numbers, which implies the Generalized complex Jacobian conjecture. Also, for the strong real Jacobian conjecture, we present a newly sufficient and necessary condition.

F^{-1}(\{b\})=\{a\}
for some
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By the Prop.

4.2
, for
\forall\penalty\ b\in Im(F)
, there exist only finite number of solutions for

for some fixed integer

. Denote the maximum of the norm of

. For any selected

), consider the

centered at
0
. By Them.
3.3
, the Brouwer degree

d[f;B(0,R),b]=\sum_{X\in F^{-1}(b)}\text{Sign}JF(X)=\sharp\{F^{-1}(b)\}=N
, because of
b\notin F(\partial B(0,R))
by the selection of
R
and the non-zero constant Jacobian of
F
, where it is no harm to assume the
\det JF(X)

3.4
.

Since
F

F(B(0,R))

by the arbitrariness of

Roughly speaking, for a polynomial mapping
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
with

, the proof of the injectivity of

have three steps:

(1) Find a point

F

F

b

(2) Find an open bounded connected sub-set

( in (1)) and finite number of points (all pre-image points of any point

(3)By the homotopy invariance of the Brouwer degree,

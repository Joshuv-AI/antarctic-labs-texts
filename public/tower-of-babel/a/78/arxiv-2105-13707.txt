Theorem 4.3
.

Let
G
be a graph of order

. If both

3.5
(1)-(5), when

, we have
\alpha^{\prime}(\overline{G})\geq\frac{n-3}{2}+1=\frac{n-1}{2}
by Lemma
4.1
(3), and then
\alpha^{\prime}(G)+\alpha^{\prime}(\overline{G})\geq 3+\frac{n-1}{2}=\frac{n+5}{2}>\frac{n+4}{2}
.

When
\alpha^{\prime}(G)=\frac{7}{2}
, then
1\leq s\leq 3
. By Lemma
4.1
(2), we have
\alpha^{\prime}(\overline{G})\geq\frac{n-s+1}{2}
. It follows that
\alpha^{\prime}(G)+\alpha^{\prime}(\overline{G})\geq\frac{7}{2}+\frac{n-s+1}{2}\geq\frac{7}{2}+\frac{n-3+1}{2}=\frac{n+5}{2}>\frac{n+4}{2}
.

When
4\leq\alpha^{\prime}(G)=t\leq\frac{n}{4}
, since
s\geq 1
,
\alpha^{\prime}(\overline{G})\geq\frac{n-s+1}{2}
by Lemma
4.1
(2). Thus

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}(G)+\alpha^{\prime}(\overline{G})\geq t+\frac{n-s+1}{2}\geq t+\frac{n-t+1}{2}=\frac{n+t+1}{2}\geq\frac{n+5}{2}>\frac{n+4}{2}.

Then we only need to consider the case that
\frac{n}{4}<\alpha^{\prime}(G)\leq\frac{n}{2}\leq\alpha^{\prime}(\overline{G})
.

If
n\equiv 0,1(mod4)
, then
\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor+\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha^{\prime}(G)\leq\frac{n}{2}
. When
\alpha^{\prime}(G)=\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor+\frac{1}{2}
or
\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor+1
, by Lemma
4.2
,
\alpha^{\prime}(\overline{G})\geq\frac{n}{4}+3
. Thus

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}(G)+\alpha^{\prime}(\overline{G})\geq\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{n}{4}+3>\frac{n}{2}+3>\frac{n+4}{2}.

When
\alpha^{\prime}(G)\geq\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor+\frac{3}{2}>\frac{n+4}{4}
, we obtain
\alpha^{\prime}(G)+\alpha^{\prime}(\overline{G})\geq 2\alpha^{\prime}(G)\geq 2\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor+3>\frac{n+4}{2}
.

If
n\equiv 2,3(mod4)
,
\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor+1\leq\alpha^{\prime}(G)\leq\frac{n}{2}
. When
\alpha^{\prime}(G)=\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor+1
or
\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor+\frac{3}{2}
, by Lemma
4.2
,
\alpha^{\prime}(\overline{G})\geq\frac{n}{4}+3
. Thus we have

\alpha^{\prime}(G)+\alpha^{\prime}(\overline{G})\geq\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor+1+\frac{n}{4}+3>\frac{n}{2}+3>\frac{n+4}{2}
. When
\alpha^{\prime}(G)\geq\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor+2>\frac{n+4}{4}
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}(G)+\alpha^{\prime}(\overline{G})\geq 2\alpha^{\prime}(G)\geq 2(\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor+2)>\frac{n+4}{2}.

Therefore,
\alpha^{\prime}(G)+\alpha^{\prime}(\overline{G})>\frac{n+4}{2}
.

If
\alpha^{\prime}(G)+\alpha^{\prime}(\overline{G})=\frac{n+4}{2}
, then
\alpha^{\prime}(G)=2
. By Lemma
3.3
and both
G
and
\overline{G}
contain no isolated vertices, we have
G\cong K_{2}(p,q;\ell)
or
K_{1,m}\cup K_{1,n-2-m}\subseteq G\subseteq K_{2,n-2}
, where
p,q,\ell,m
are non-negative integers,
q\geq 1
and
1\leq p,m\leq n-3
. Therefore, we complete the proof.
\hfill\square

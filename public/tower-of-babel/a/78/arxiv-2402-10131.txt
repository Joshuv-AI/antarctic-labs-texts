A Study of monogenity of Binomial Composition
Anuj Jakhar, Ravi Kalwaniya, Prabhakar yadav

Abstract
. Let θ \theta be a root of a monic polynomial h ⁡ ( x ) ∈ ℤ ⁡ [ x ] h(x)\in\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}[x] of degree n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 . We say h ⁡ ( x ) h(x) is monogenic if it is irreducible over ℚ \mathbb{Q} and { 1 , θ , θ 2 , … , θ n − 1 } \{1,\theta,\theta^{2},\ldots,\theta^{n-1}\} is a basis for the ring ℤ K \mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}_{K} of integers of K = ℚ ⁡ ( θ ) K=\mathbb{Q}(\theta) . In this article, we study about the monogenity of number fields generated by a root of composition of two binomials. We characterise all the primes dividing the index of the subgroup ℤ ⁡ [ θ ] \mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}[\theta] in ℤ K \mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}_{K} where K = ℚ ⁡ ( θ ) K=\mathbb{Q}(\theta) with θ \theta having minimal polynomial F ⁡ ( x ) = ( x m − b ) n − a ∈ ℤ ⁡ [ x ] F(x)=(x^{m}-b)^{n}-a\in\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}[x] , m ≥ 1 m\geq 1 and n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 . As an application, we provide a class of pairs of binomials f ⁡ ( x ) = x n − a f(x)=x^{n}-a and g ⁡ ( x ) = x m − b g(x)=x^{m}-b having the property that both f ⁡ ( x ) f(x) and f ⁡ ( g ⁡ ( x ) ) f(g(x)) are monogenic.

1.
Introduction and statements of results

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\theta)
be an algebraic number field with
\theta
in the ring
\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}_{K}
of algebraic integers of
K
and
h(x)
having degree
n
be the minimal polynomial of
\theta
over the field
\mathbb{Q}
of rational numbers.
Let
d_{K}
denote the discriminant of
K
and
D_{h}
denote the discriminant of the polynomial
h(x)
. It is well-known that
d_{K}
and
D_{h}
are related by the following formula

D_{h}=[\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}_{K}:\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}[\theta]]^{2}d_{K}.

We say that
h(x)
is monogenic if
\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}_{K}=\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}[\theta]
, or equivalently, if
D_{h}=d_{K}
. In this case,
\{1,\theta,\cdots,\theta^{n-1}\}
will be an integral basis of
K
and
K
will be a monogenic number field. A number field
K
is called monogenic if there exists some
\alpha\in\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}_{K}
such that
\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}_{K}=\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}[\alpha]
. Note that the monogenity of a polynomial
h(x)
implies that
K=\mathbb{Q}(\theta)
is monogenic where
\theta
is a root of
h(x)
, but the converse is not true in general. For example, let
\alpha
and
\beta
be the roots of
h_{1}(x)=x^{2}-5
and
h_{2}(x)=x^{2}-x-1
respectively, then
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)=\mathbb{Q}(\beta)
. Although
h_{2}(x)
is monogenic but
h_{1}(x)
is not monogenic.

The determination of monogenity of an algebraic number field is one of the classical and important problems in algebraic number theory. An arithmetic characterisation of monogenic number fields is a problem due to Hasse (cf.
[
7
]
). Gaál’s book provides some classification of monogenity in lower degree number fields (cf.
[
4
]
). In 2016, Jhorar and Khanduja gave necessary and sufficient conditions for
\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}_{K}=\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}[\theta]
when
\theta
is a root of an irreducible binomial
x^{n}-b\in\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}[x]
(cf.
[
10
, Theorem 1.3]
. In fact, they proved the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\theta)
be a Kummer extension of
\mathbb{Q}
with
\theta
satisfying an irreducible polynomial
x^{n}-b
over
\mathbb{Z}
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}_{K}=\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}[\theta]
.

(ii)

b
is square-free integer and whenever a prime
p
divides
n
, then
p^{2}
does not divide
b^{p}-b
.

Using Dedekind’s Index Criterion, in 2016-17, Jakhar, Khanduja and Sangwan
[
9
]
extended the above result and provided necessary and sufficient conditions for
\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}_{K}=\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}[\theta]
when
\theta
is a root of an irreducible trinomial
x^{n}+ax^{m}+b\in\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}[x]
having degree
n
. As an application, they provided infinitely many monogenic trinomials.
Further in 2020, Jones and Harrington
[
6
]
investigated few pairs of binomials
f(x)=x^{n}-a
and
g(x)=x^{m}-b
having the property that both
f(x)
and
f(g(x))
are monogenic. In fact, they provided necessary and sufficient conditions for the monogenicity of the polynomial
(x^{m}-b)^{n}-a
with
n=2,3.
In 2022, Gaál
[
5
]
described monogenity properties of a class of binomial compositions of
degree six.

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\theta)
be an algebraic number field where
\theta
is a root of an irreducible polynomial
f(g(x))=(x^{m}-b)^{n}-a
over
\mathbb{Q}
.
In this article, we characterise all the primes dividing the index
[\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}_{K}:\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}[\theta]]
. As an application, we provide necessary and sufficient conditions for
\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}_{K}=\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}[\theta].
Moreover, we also give class of binomials
f(x)
and
g(x)
such that both
f(x)
,
f(g(x))
are monogenic.

Throughout the paper,
f(x)=x^{n}-a
and
g(x)=x^{m}-b
will be polynomials in
\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}[x]
having degree
n\geq 2
and
m\geq 1
, respectively.
D_{F}
will stand for the discriminant of an irreducible polynomial
F(x)=f(g(x))=(x^{m}-b)^{n}-a
. Although the formula for
|D_{F}|
is given in
[
6
, Lemma 3.1]
, for the sake of completion we provide a quick derivation of
D_{F}
in Lemma
2.3
using a different approach. We prove that
D_{F}
is given by

D_{F}=(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}+m}(mn)^{mn}a^{m(n-1)}((-b)^{n}-a)^{m-1}.

(1.1)

For an integer
z
,
\operatorname{rad}(z)
will denote the product of distinct primes dividing
z
.

We wish to point out here that in view of Theorem
1.1
we can consider
n\geq 2
while studying the monogenity of the polynomial
f(g(x))
, the composition of two polynomials
f(x)=x^{n}-a
and
g(x)=x^{m}-b
.

Precisely stated, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
K=\mathbb{\mathbb{Q}}(\theta)
be an algebraic number field with
\theta
in the ring
\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}_{K}
of algebraic integers of
K
having minimal polynomial
F(x)=(x^{m}-b)^{n}-a
over
\mathbb{Q}
with
m\geq 1
and
n\geq 2
. A prime factor
p
of the discriminant
D_{F}
of
F(x)
does not divide
[\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}_{K}:\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}[\theta]]
if and only if
p
satisfies one of the following conditions:

(i)

when
p\mid a
, then
p^{2}\nmid a
.

(ii)

when
p\nmid a
and
p\mid b
with
m=p^{j}s,~n=p^{k}s^{\prime},~j+k\geq 1
and
p\nmid ss^{\prime}
, then the polynomials
\frac{1}{p}[a^{p^{j+k}}-a-nbg(x)^{n-1}]
and
x^{ss^{\prime}}-a
are coprime
modulo~p
.

(iii)

when
p\nmid ab
and
p|n
with
k\geq 1
as the highest power of
p
dividing
n
, then
p^{2}\nmid(a^{p^{k}}-a)
.

(iv)

when
p\nmid abn
and
p\mid m
with
m=p^{j}s
, then
(x^{s}-b)^{n}-a
must be coprime to
\frac{1}{p}\left[a^{p^{j}}-a+n\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}{p^{j}\choose ip^{j-1}}(x^{s}-b)^{np^{j}-ip^{j-1}}b^{i}+n(b^{p^{j}}-b)\right]
.

(v)

when
p\nmid abmn
, then
p^{2}\nmid((-b)^{n}-a)
.

In the special case, the above theorem quickly yields the following simple result regarding monogenic polynomials.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\theta)
and
F(x)=(x^{m}-b)^{n}-a
be as in Theorem
1.2
. Assume that
\operatorname{rad}(mn)
divides
\operatorname{rad}(a)
. Then
\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}_{K}=\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}[\theta]
if and only if both
a
and
(-b)^{n}-a
are square-free.

Remark 1.4
.

Note that if
p
is prime number such that
p\nmid a
with
a\in\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}
, then for any positive integer
s
the exact power of
p
dividing
a^{p^{s}-1}-1
will be same as the exact power of
p
dividing
a^{p-1}-1
; this can be easily verified keeping in mind that
p^{s}-1=(p-1)m
with
m\equiv~1~(mod~p)
and
a^{p-1}\equiv 1~(mod~p).

Keeping in mind the above remark, the following corollary is an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.2
.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\theta)
with
\theta
having minimal polynomial
F(x)=(x^{m}-b)^{n}-a\in\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}[x]
with
m\geq 1,n\geq 2
. Let
\operatorname{rad}(m)
divide
\operatorname{rad}(an)
. Then
\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}_{K}=\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}[\theta]
if and only if each prime
p
dividing
D_{F}
satisfies one of the following:

(i)

when
p\mid a
, then
p^{2}\nmid a
.

(ii)

when
p\nmid a
, then
p^{2}\nmid(a^{p}-a)
.

(iii)

when
p\nmid abmn
, then
p^{2}\nmid((-b)^{n}-a)
.

Using Theorem
1.1
and the above corollary, we obtain the following result which extends
[
6
, Theorem 1.3]
as well as it provides necessary and sufficient conditions for the monogenity of both

and
f(g(x))
.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
f(x)=x^{n}-a
and
F(x)=(x^{m}-b)^{n}-a\in\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}[x]
with
m\geq 1,~n\geq 2
and
\operatorname{rad}(m)
divide
\operatorname{rad}(an)
. Suppose
f(x)
and

are irreducible. Then
f(x)
and

are monogenic if and only if the following hold:

(i)

a
is squarefree.

(ii)

a^{p}\not\equiv a
(mod

) for all primes

dividing
n
.

(iii)

For all primes

((-b)^{n}-a)

Lemma 2.1

Let

be monic and irreducible with

Let

D_{f}=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}~\mathcal{N}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(f^{\prime}(\theta)).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
K/F
be an extension of degree
n
and
\alpha
be an element of

[F(\alpha):F]=d
. Then

\mathcal{N}_{K/F}(\alpha)=\left(\mathcal{N}_{F(\alpha)/F}(\alpha)\right)^{n/d}.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f(x)=x^{n}-a
and
g(x)=x^{m}-b
be polynomials in
\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}[x]
. Let
F(x)=f(g(x))
be an irreducible polynomial, then the discriminant

of

is given by

D_{F}=(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}+m}(nm)^{nm}a^{m(n-1)}((-b)^{n}-a)^{m-1}.

(2.1)

Proof.

Let
\theta
be a root of
F(x)
. Since
F^{\prime}(x)=nmx^{m-1}(x^{m}-b)^{n-1}
, we have

F^{\prime}(\theta)=nm\theta^{m-1}(\theta^{m}-b)^{n-1}.

(2.2)

We simply write
\mathcal{N}
for the norm
\mathcal{N}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}
, where
K=\mathbb{Q}(\theta)
. Since
\mathcal{N}(\theta)=(-1)^{mn}((-b)^{n}-a)
and
\mathcal{N}(mn)=(mn)^{mn}
, taking norm both sides of
(
2.2
)
, we obtain

\mathcal{N}(F^{\prime}(\theta))=(-1)^{mn}(mn)^{mn}\mathcal{N}(\theta^{m}-b)^{n-1}((-b)^{n}-a)^{m-1}.

(2.3)

To calculate
\mathcal{N}(\theta^{m}-b)
, let
\theta^{m}-b=z
.
Since
F(\theta)=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle F(\theta)=(\theta^{m}-b)^{n}-a=z^{n}-a=0.

Therefore, we see that

is root of the polynomial

. We claim that

is a minimal polynomial for

. Keeping in mind that
\mathbb{Q}(z)=\mathbb{Q}(\theta^{m})
, for proving our claim, it is sufficient to show that

is irreducible polynomial over
\mathbb{Q}.
Suppose that

, which contradicts the irreducibility of

is irreducible. This proves our claim. So we have
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Q}(z)/\mathbb{Q}}(z)=(-1)^{n}(-a)
.
Using Lemma
(
2.2
)
, we obtain

\mathcal{N}(z)=(-1)^{m(n+1)}a^{m}.

(2.4)

Therefore, the theorem follows from Lemma
2.1
and Equations
(
2.3
)
and
(
2.4
)
.

for some polynomials

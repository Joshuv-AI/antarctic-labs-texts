Convergence rates of eigenvalue problems in perforated domains: the case of small volume
Zhongwei Shen, Jinping Zhuge

Abstract
. This paper is concerned with the Dirichlet eigenvalue problem for Laplace operator in a bounded domain with periodic perforation in the case of small volume. We obtain the optimal quantitative error estimates independent of the spectral gaps for an asymptotic expansion, with two leading terms, of Dirichlet eigenvalues. We also establish the convergence rates for the corresponding eigenfunctions. Our approach uses a known reduction to a degenerate elliptic eigenvalue problem for which a quantitative analysis is carried out. Keywords: Homogenization, perforated domains, eigenvalue problems, convergence rates.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivations and main results

The homogenization theory of partial differential equations in perforated domains has been extensively studied in the past four decades due to its theoretical and numerical importance. In this paper, we are concerned with the Dirichlet eigenvalue problem of Laplace operator in
periodically perforated domains: find
(\psi_{\eta,\varepsilon},\lambda_{\eta,\varepsilon})\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})\times\mathbb{R}
such that

(1.1)

-\Delta\psi_{\eta,\varepsilon}=\lambda_{\eta,\varepsilon}\psi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\quad\text{in }\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon},

where
\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}
is a bounded and periodically perforated domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 3
. This problem was introduced by J. Rauch
[
7
]
in a crashed ice problem (also see
[
8
]
), wherein the first eigenvalue of (
1.1
) determines the rate of cooling for the evenly distributed crashed ice. Since
-\Delta
is symmetric with compact resolvent in
L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
, by the classical spectral theorem, the Dirichlet eigenvalues are positive, countable and can be listed in a nondecreasing order
\{\lambda_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}:k=1,2,\dots\}
(counted with multiplicity) such that
\lambda_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
. We denote by
\psi^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
the normalized eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
. We are interested in the asymptotic behaviors of
\lambda_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}
and
\psi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

The perforated domain
\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}
is defined as follows. Let
0<\eta,\varepsilon\leq 1
be two small parameters. Let
\tau^{i}
(with
i=1,2,\cdots,m
) be
C^{1,1}
connected domains with diameters comparable to 1. Define
Y=[0,1]^{d}
. Let
\tau^{i}_{\eta}\subset Y
be a translated copy of
\eta\tau^{i}
satisfying
\text{dist}(\tau^{i}_{\eta},\tau^{j}_{\eta})>c_{0}
for
i\neq j
and
\text{dist}(\tau^{i}_{\eta},\partial Y)>c_{0}
. Let
T_{\eta}=\cup_{i=1}^{m}\overline{\tau^{i}_{\eta}}\subset Y
. In other words,
T_{\eta}
is the union of
m
holes contained in
Y
. Define

(1.2)

T_{\eta,\varepsilon}=\bigcup_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}),\qquad\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}=\Omega\setminus T_{\eta,\varepsilon}.

Note that the diameters of the holes in
\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}
are roughly
\eta\varepsilon
and thus the holes only occupy a small portion (roughly
O(\eta^{d})
) of the volume of
\Omega
.

We will impose a geometric assumption
\mathbf{A}
: for each hole
\varepsilon(z+\tau^{i}_{\eta})
of
T_{\eta,\varepsilon}
, either the hole has at least a distance
c_{0}\varepsilon\eta
away from
\partial\Omega
, or
\partial\Omega
intersects with the hole such that both
\Omega\cap\varepsilon(z+\tau^{i}_{\eta})
and
\Omega\setminus\varepsilon(z+\tau^{i}_{\eta})
(in a neighborhood of
\varepsilon(z+\tau^{i}_{\eta})
) are Lipschitz domains with a uniform Lipschitz character after rescaling. Let
\Gamma_{\eta,\varepsilon}=\partial\Omega\cap\partial\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}
and
\Sigma_{\eta,\varepsilon}=\Omega\cap\partial T_{\eta,\varepsilon}
.

Under the above assumptions with
\eta=1
, the asymptotic behavior of the Dirichlet eigenvalues was first studied by Vanninathan
[
11
]
and then the convergence rates were obtained by Oleĭnik, Shamaev and Yosifian
[
6
]
. We first describe the procedure of reduction for the case
\eta\ll 1
which is the same as
\eta=1
given in
[
11
]
. Let
Y_{\eta}=Y\setminus T_{\eta}
, viewed as a flat torus with holes. Let
(\phi_{\eta},\overline{\lambda}_{\eta})\in H^{1}_{0,{\rm per}}(Y_{\eta})\times\mathbb{R}
denote the first eigenvalue and eigenfunction of the cell problem,

(1.3)

-\Delta\phi_{\eta}=\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}\phi_{\eta}\quad\text{in}\ \ Y_{\eta},

with
\|\phi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\eta})}=1
and
\phi_{\eta}=0
in
\partial T_{\eta}
. The principal eigenfunction
\phi_{\eta}
is nonnegative and satisfies
\phi_{\eta}(x)\approx\min\{\eta^{-1}\text{dist}(x,T_{\eta}),1\}
, which means that
\phi_{\eta}
degenerates proportional to a distance function near the holes.
Let

\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}(x)=\phi_{\eta}(x/\varepsilon)
.
As in the case
\eta=1
, we have

(1.4)

\lambda_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}=\varepsilon^{-2}\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}+\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k},

where
\{\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}\}
denote the Dirichlet eigenvalues of the degenerate problem

(1.5)

-\nabla\cdot(\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k})=\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k},\quad\text{in }\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon},

with corresponding eigenfunctions
\{\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}\}\subset H^{1}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon},0}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
.
The eigenvalues
\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}
can be given by the minimax principle,

(1.6)

\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset H^{1}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon},0}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})\\
\text{dim}S=k\end{subarray}}\max_{u\in S}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}}{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|u|^{2}}.

Thus, to obtain the asymptotic behaviors of
\lambda_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}
, it suffices to study those of
\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}
. More details can be found in Section
2
.

For the case
\eta=1
, it was shown in
[
6
]
that
|\mu_{1,\varepsilon}^{k}-\mu_{1}^{k}|\lesssim\varepsilon
, where
\mu_{1}^{k}
are the eigenvalues of

(1.7)

-\nabla\cdot(\overline{A}_{1}\nabla\rho_{1}^{k})=\mu_{1}^{k}\rho_{1}^{k}\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

and
\overline{A}_{1}
is a constant coefficient matrix satisfying the ellipticity condition. In view of (
1.4
), we have

(1.8)

|\lambda_{1,\varepsilon}^{k}-\varepsilon^{-2}\overline{\lambda}_{1}-\mu_{1}^{k}|\lesssim\varepsilon.

Note that the homogenized eigenvalue problem (
1.7
) is posed in the domain
\Omega
without holes, which is well-understood. The convergence rates of
O(\varepsilon)
for the eigenfunctions were also obtained in
[
6
]
. We point out that the convergence rate of order
O(\varepsilon)
obtained in
[
6
]
is optimal in the case of
\eta=1
. However, due to several technical reasons, the implicit constants obtained via their approach is not quantitative (uncomputable) and depends essentially on the spectral gaps (appearing in the denominator).

For the small holes with
\eta\ll 1
, a special case with
d=3
and
\eta=\varepsilon^{2}
was also studied in
[
6
]
. It was shown that as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the eigenvalues of (
1.5
) converge to the Dirichlet eigenvalues of
-\Delta
in
\Omega
. More precisely, for any
k\geq 1
,

(1.9)

|\lambda_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}-\overline{\xi}-\xi_{0}^{k}|\lesssim\varepsilon,

where
\xi_{0}^{k}
is the
k
th Dirichlet eigenvalue of
-\Delta
in
\Omega
, or equivalently,
\bar{\xi}+\xi_{0}^{k}
is the eigenvalue of
-\Delta+\bar{\xi}
, and
\bar{\xi}>0
is a fixed number independent of
\varepsilon
.

In this paper, we revisit (
1.1
) with holes of arbitrary size, particularly including the case of small volume. We will show the optimal quantitative error estimate of eigenvalues under a refined asymptotic expansion.
The following is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a smooth (
C^{3}
) domain. Assume that
\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}
satisfies the geometric assumption
A
.
Then

(1.10)

|\lambda_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}-\varepsilon^{-2}\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}-\mu_{\eta}^{k}|\leq C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}},

where
\mu_{\eta}^{k}
is the
k
th Dirichlet eigenvalue (counted with multiplicity) for
-\nabla\cdot(\overline{A}_{\eta}\nabla)
in
\Omega
, i.e., the eigenpair
(\rho^{k}_{\eta},\mu^{k}_{\eta})\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{R}
satisfies

(1.11)

-\nabla\cdot(\overline{A}_{\eta}\nabla\rho^{k}_{\eta})=\mu^{k}_{\eta}\rho^{k}_{\eta},\quad\text{in }\Omega.

Moreover,
\overline{A}_{\eta}
is a constant coefficient matrix satisfying a uniformly elliptic conidtion independent of
\eta
, and
C_{k}
depends only on
k,d,c_{0}
and the geometric characters of the domain (including
\Omega
and
\tau^{i}
).

Compared with the results in
[
6
]
, our result in Theorem
1.1
has two major improvements.

(1)

In the case
\eta\ll 1
, our result shows that, compared to
-\Delta
, the refined operator
-\nabla\cdot(\overline{A}_{\eta}\nabla)
is a more accurate effective operator. In particular, we may compare the result of the special case
d=3
and
\eta=\varepsilon^{2}
with that of
[
6
]
mentioned before.
In this case, (
1.10
) is reduced to

(1.12)

|\lambda_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{k}-\varepsilon^{-2}\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}-\mu_{\eta}^{k}|\leq C_{k}\varepsilon^{2},

where
\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}\approx\eta^{d-2}=\varepsilon^{2}
. Definitely, the error in (
1.12
) is much sharper than (
1.9
).
Moreover, it can be shown that
|\overline{A}_{\eta}-I|\leq C\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}=C\varepsilon
and therefore
|\mu_{\eta}^{k}-\xi_{0}^{k}|\leq C_{k}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}=C_{k}\varepsilon
, which explains why the improvement is reasonable by using the refined operator
-\nabla\cdot(\overline{A}_{\eta}\nabla)
instead of
-\Delta
. The refined operator takes advantage of both the homogenization process at large scales, leading to the factor
\varepsilon
, and the perturbation due to the perforation of small
\eta
at small scales, leading to the factor
\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}
in (
1.10
). We should point out that, from the point of view of numerical computation, the calculation for
\overline{A}_{\eta}
for a fixed
\eta
is a one-time cost for solving a cell problem similar to
\eta=1
which makes it practical in applications.

(2)

The constant
C_{k}
in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
below is quantitative in the sense that its dependence on
k
and the geometric characters can be determined. In particular,
C_{k}
is bounded by
C_{0}k^{a}
for some computable
a=a(d)>0
, where
C_{0}
is independent of
k
. However, the constants given by
[
6
]
are not fully quantitative and may rely on the spectral gaps.

Our second result is concerned with the convergence rates of eigenfunctions, which are typically more difficult due to the multiplicity of eigenvalues or the small spectral gaps between them; see
[
12
,
2
,
3
]
for related problems.
As such, it seems natural to consider the asymptotic behaviors of an eigenspace corresponding to a collection of eigenvalues in a narrow spectral band.

To describe the result, recall that
\{\rho_{\eta}^{k}\}
are the normalized eigenfunctions of (
1.15
) corresponding to
\{\mu_{\eta}^{k}\}
. For
\theta>0,t>0
, define

S_{\eta}(\theta;t)=\text{span}\{\rho_{\eta}^{k}:|\mu_{\eta}^{k}-\theta|\leq t\}.

In other words,
S_{\eta}(\theta;t)
is the eigenspace of the homogenized eigenvalue problem (
1.15
) corresponding to the spectrum in the band
[\theta-t,\theta+t]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Under the same conditions of Theorem
1.1
, for any
k\geq 1
and
t>0
,

(1.13)

\inf_{v\in S_{\eta}(\mu_{\eta}^{k};t)}\|\psi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}-\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C_{k}(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\wedge\varepsilon+t^{-1}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}),

where
a\wedge b=\min\{a,b\}
.

Roughly speaking, the above theorem states that an eigenfunction of (
1.1
) corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{k}=\varepsilon^{-2}\overline{\lambda}+\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}
can be well approximated by
\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}u_{\eta}
, where
\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}(x)=\phi_{\eta}(x/\varepsilon)
and

u_{\eta}
is a finite linear combination of the eigenfunctions of the homogenized operator corresponding to the eigenvalues in a narrow band (with width
2t
) around
\mu_{\eta}^{k}
.
The main novelty here is that one is free to choose the width of the band and the estimate is independent of the spectral gap near
\mu_{\eta}^{k}
. In particular, if
\mu_{\eta}^{k}
is simple and has large spectral gaps from
\mu_{\eta}^{k-1}
and
\mu_{\eta}^{k+1}
(e.g., the principal eigenvalue
\mu_{\eta}^{1}
), then
\psi_{\varepsilon}^{k}
converges to
\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\rho_{\eta}^{k}
in
L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
with an error of order
O(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\wedge\varepsilon)
. This rate may not be optimal in general which is caused by the suboptimal estimates of boundary layers (two different approaches will be used). The optimal boundary layer estimate in a perforated domain is an interesting and challenging problem that is beyond the scope of this paper.
On the other hand, in case of small
t
,
the negative power
t^{-1}
in (
1.13
) cannot be avoided generally and the dominating term
t^{-1}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}
(say
t<\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}
) should be optimal.

1.2.
Strategy of the proof

First of all, we point out that the optimal upper bounds of the eigenvalues actually can be proved directly by the minimax principle. However, the optimal lower bounds of the eigenvalues are much harder and we develop a new approach to resolve the problem.

The proofs of our main results (as well as the proof in
[
6
]
for
\eta=1
) can be roughly divided into two stages. In the first stage, we need to obtain an a priori quantitative convergence rate (not optimal) of eigenvalues in dependent of the spectral gaps. By this and the classical Weyl’s law for the homogenized eigenvalue problem, we are able to locate some common large spectral gaps between eigenvalues (see Lemma
5.5
), which will play a key role in the second stage.
We mention that in
[
6
]
only a qualitative convergence was proved as their first stage and hence their results are not fully quantitative. Unlike the other homogenization problems in domains without holes (see, e.g.,
[
5
,
4
]
), our proof for the degenerate problems in perforated domains involves an intermediate problems as a bridge.

In fact, in order to study the eigenvalue problem (
1.5
), we need to consider the boundary value problem,

(1.14)

-\nabla\cdot(\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon})=\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}f\qquad\text{in }\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}\quad\text{ and }\quad u_{\eta,\varepsilon}=0\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

and the corresponding homogenized problem is given by

(1.15)

-\nabla\cdot(\overline{A}_{\eta}\nabla u_{\eta})=f\qquad\text{in }\Omega\quad\text{ and }\quad u_{\eta}=0\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

Unfortunately, the convergence from
\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}f
to
f
is very weak if
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and therefore it is impossible to establish a quantitative convergence rate from
u_{\eta,\varepsilon}
to
u_{\eta}
under the assumption

f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for some
p\leq\infty
, which is a necessary regularity assumption in the application of minimax principle to (
1.5
). To handle this difficulty, we introduce an intermediate eigenvalue problem,

(1.16)

-\nabla\cdot(\overline{A}_{\eta}\nabla\tilde{\rho}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k})=\tilde{\mu}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}{\phi}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\tilde{\rho}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}\quad\text{ in }\Omega\quad\text{ and }\quad\tilde{\rho}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}=0\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

and the corresponding equation

(1.17)

-\nabla\cdot(\overline{A}_{\eta}\nabla\tilde{u}_{\eta,\varepsilon})=\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}f\qquad\text{in }\Omega\quad\text{ and }\quad\tilde{u}_{\eta,\varepsilon}=0\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

Observe that (
1.17
) only has an oscillating factor
\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}
on the right-hand side. Thus, the solution
\tilde{u}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
, as well as the eigenfunctions in (
1.16
), admits better regularity (indeed,
\tilde{u}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
, provided
f\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
).
Now, we consider two steps of convergence via the intermediate problem: the convergence rate of the solutions from (
1.14
) to (
1.17
) can be established under the condition
f\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
for some
p>d
(see Theorem
4.6
); while the convergence rate from (
1.17
) to (
1.15
) can only be shown under the stronger condition
f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
for some
p>d
(see Theorem
4.7
). The regularity condition on
f
in either step is compatible with the a priori regularity of the corresponding eigenfunctions and thus allows us to use the minimax/maximin principle to derive the suboptimal convergence rates of eigenvalues (see Proposition
5.1
and Proposition
5.3
). We emphasize that in order to extract the small factor
\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}
in the convergence rates, we have to estimate the weight
\phi_{\eta}
and correctors
\chi_{\eta}
optimally (see Section
2
) and carry out a careful analysis throughout the entire proof (see Section
4
).

In the second stage, we prove the optimal convergence rates of eigenvalues (and the convergence rates of eigenfunctions as a byproduct). To this end, we first prove an improved convergence rate (still not optimal) directly from (
1.14
) to (
1.15
) without relying on the intermediate problem (
1.17
) under the stronger assumption
f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
for some
p>d
, i.e.,

(1.18)

\|\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}f-\mathscr{T}_{\eta}f\|_{L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)},

where
\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}:f\to u_{\eta,\varepsilon}
and
\mathscr{T}_{\eta}:f\to u_{\eta}
are the resolvents given by (
1.14
) and (
1.15
), respectively.
Then, we apply a duality argument to show the optimal convergence rate in the weak sense, i.e., for any
g\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
,

(1.19)

\bigg|\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}(\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}f-\mathscr{T}_{\eta}f)g\bigg|\leq C\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\|g\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

Combining the above two estimates with the large spectral gaps found in the first stage and the almost orthogonality of eigenfunctions (see Lemma
6.8
), we are able to analyze precisely the dimension of the eigenspace of
\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
corresponding to the eigenvalues less than
\mu^{k}_{\eta}+C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}
for each
k\geq 1
, which implies the optimal lower bounds of the eigenvalues of
\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
. This argument relies essentially on the orthogonal structure of linear eigenspaces.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, the constants
C
and
c
will never depend on
\varepsilon
and
\eta
. But they may depend on the geometric characters of
\Omega
and
T
, the number of holes
m
in
Y
, etc. We write
A\approx B
if there exist
C\geq c>0
such that
cB\leq A\leq CB
. We write
A\leq B+O(\delta)
if
A\leq B+C\delta
for some constant
C
independent of
\delta
; we write
A=B+O(\delta)
if
|A-B|\leq C\delta
. Given an arbitrary
Y
-periodic function
f
, we will apply the notation
f_{\varepsilon}(x)=(f)_{\varepsilon}(x)=f(x/\varepsilon)
.

2.1.
The cell eigenvalue problem

This subsection is devoted to the estimates of the eigenpair
(\phi_{\eta},\overline{\lambda}_{\eta})
.

Recall that
Y_{\eta}=Y\setminus T_{\eta}
.
Define

H^{1}_{\rm 0,per}(Y_{\eta})=\{v\in H^{1}(Y_{\eta}):\text{$v$ is $Y$-periodic and $v=0$ on $\partial T_{\eta}$}\}.

Here and after, a function defined in
Y_{\eta}
is said to be
Y
-periodic if it has the same trace on the opposite faces of
Y
. Thus the functions in
H^{1}_{\rm 0,per}(Y_{\eta})
can be identified as
Y
-periodic functions in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with no jumps across the cell boundaries.

Consider the eigenvalue problem

(2.1)

-\Delta u=\lambda u\quad\text{in }Y_{\eta},

with
(u,\lambda)\in H^{1}_{\rm 0,per}(Y_{\eta})\times\mathbb{R}
, where
Y_{\eta}
should be understood as a flat torus with holes (i.e., the equation continues to hold across
\partial Y
). It is well-known that the principal eigenvalue of the above cell problem, denoted by
\overline{\lambda}
, is positive and simple, and the corresponding eigenfunction, denoted by
\phi_{\eta}
, does not change sign in
Y_{\eta}
. Without loss of generality, we assume

(2.2)

\|\phi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\eta})}=1\quad\text{ and }\quad\phi_{\eta}>0\quad\text{in }Y_{\eta}.

For convenience, we extend
\phi_{\eta}
by zero in the interior of

T_{\eta}
and denote it still by
\phi_{\eta}
.

Since
\phi_{\eta}
is
Y
-periodic, by rescaling, we see that
\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}(x):=\phi_{\eta}(x/\varepsilon)
satisfies

(2.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}&=\varepsilon^{-2}\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}&\quad&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T_{\eta,\varepsilon},\\
\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}&=0&\quad&\text{on }\partial T_{\eta,\varepsilon}.\end{aligned}\right.

Lemma 2.1
.

There exist positive constants
c_{1}
and
C_{1}
such that

(2.4)

c_{1}\eta^{d-2}\leq\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}\leq C_{1}\eta^{d-2}

for
\eta\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

To show
\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}\geq c_{1}\eta^{d-2}
, we use the Poincaré inequality,

(2.5)

c_{1}\eta^{d-2}\int_{Y_{\eta}}|u|^{2}\leq\int_{Y_{\eta}}|\nabla u|^{2},

for functions
u\in H^{1}_{0,{\rm per}}(Y_{\eta})
, where
c_{1}>0
depends only on
T_{1}
(see
[
1
]
for a proof).
This gives

\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}\int_{Y_{\eta}}|\phi_{\eta}|^{2}=\int_{Y_{\eta}}|\nabla\phi_{\eta}|^{2}\geq c_{0}\eta^{d-2}\int_{Y_{\eta}}|\phi_{\eta}|^{2},

which yields
\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}\geq c_{1}\eta^{d-2}
.

To see
\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}\leq C_{1}\eta^{d-2}
, we use the fact

\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}=\inf_{u\in H^{1}_{0,{\rm per}}(Y_{\eta})}\frac{\int_{Y_{\eta}}|\nabla u|^{2}}{\int_{Y_{\eta}}|u|^{2}}.

Let
T_{\eta}^{t}=\{x\in Y:\text{dist}(x,T_{\eta})\leq t\}.
Let
c=\frac{1}{4}c_{0}
.
It is not hard to construct a function
u\in H^{1}_{0,{\rm per}}(Y_{\eta})
such that
0\leq u\leq 1
,
u=1
in
Y\setminus T_{\eta}^{c\eta}
, and
|\nabla u|\leq C\eta^{-1}
in
T_{\eta}^{c\eta}\setminus T_{\eta}
. It follows that

\int_{Y_{\eta}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C\eta^{-2}|T_{\eta}^{c\eta}\setminus T_{\eta}|\leq C\eta^{d-2}.

Also,
\|u\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\eta})}\approx 1
. This implies that
\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}\leq C_{1}\eta^{d-2}
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

There exist positive constants
c_{1}
and
C_{1}
such that the following estimates hold.

(i)

Norm estimate:

(2.6)

\|\nabla\phi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}+\|\phi_{\eta}-1\|_{L^{2^{*}}(Y)}\leq C_{1}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

where
2^{*}=\frac{2d}{d-2}
.

(ii)

Gradient estimate: For any
x\in Y_{\eta}
,

(2.7)

|\nabla\phi_{\eta}(x)|\leq C_{1}\eta^{-1}\Big\{\Big(\frac{\eta}{{\rm dist}(x,T_{\eta})}\Big)^{\frac{d}{2}}\wedge 1\Big\}.

(iii)

Degeneracy estimate: For any
x\in Y_{\eta}
,

(2.8)

c_{1}\Big\{1\wedge\frac{{\rm dist}(x,T_{\eta})}{\eta}\Big\}\leq\phi_{\eta}(x)\leq C_{1}\Big\{1\wedge\frac{{\rm dist}(x,T_{\eta})}{\eta}\Big\}.

(iv)

Interior estimate: For any
x\in Y_{\eta}
,

(2.9)

|\phi_{\eta}(x)-1|\leq C_{1}\Big\{\Big(\frac{\eta}{{\rm dist}(x,T_{\eta})}\Big)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\wedge 1\Big\}.

Proof.

First of all, the eigenvalue equation (
1.3
) gives
\|\nabla\phi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}^{2}=\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}\|\phi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}^{2}\approx\eta^{d-2}
, by Lemma
2.1
. This proves the first part of (i). The remaining estimates will be divided into several steps.

Step 1: Establish (ii) and the upper bounds in (iii). To do this, we use the observation that if

-\Delta u=\lambda u
in
B_{2r}(x_{0})
and
|\lambda|r^{2}\leq 1
, then

(2.10)

\max_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|u|\leq C\left(\fint_{B_{2r}(x_{0})}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

and

(2.11)

\max_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|\nabla u|\leq C\left(\fint_{B_{2r}(x_{0})}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+C|\lambda|r\left(\fint_{B_{2r}(x_{0})}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

The estimates above follow from the standard elliptic estimates for
-\Delta u=F
by an iteration argument.
We note that the estimates above continue to hold if we replace
B_{r}(x_{0})
by

B_{r}(x_{0})\cap Y_{\eta}
, where
x_{0}\in\partial T_{\eta}
,
0<r\leq c\eta
, and

-\Delta u=\lambda u
in
Y_{\eta}
,
u=0
on
\partial T_{\eta}
.
As a result, we see that if
x\in Y_{\eta}
and dist
(x,T_{\eta})\leq\eta
, then applying (
2.11
) to
\phi_{\eta}
gives

(2.12)

|\nabla\phi_{\eta}(x)|\leq C\eta^{-\frac{d}{2}}\|\nabla\phi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}+C\eta^{d-1}\eta^{-\frac{d}{2}}\|\phi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\leq C\eta^{-1},

where we have used the fact
\|\nabla\phi_{\eta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Y)}=\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}\approx\eta^{d-2}
.
Since
\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}=0
on
\partial T_{\eta}
, this implies that

(2.13)

\phi_{\eta}(x)\leq C\eta^{-1}\text{dist}(x,T_{\eta}),

if
x\in Y_{\eta}
and
\text{dist}(x,T_{\eta})\leq\eta
.
A similar argument shows that if
x\in Y
and
r=\text{dist}(x,T_{\eta})\geq\eta
, then

(2.14)

|\nabla\phi_{\eta}(x)|\leq Cr^{-\frac{d}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d}{2}-1}+C\eta^{d-2}r^{1-\frac{d}{2}}\leq C_{1}\eta^{-1}(\eta/r)^{\frac{d}{2}}.

Note that (
2.12
) and (
2.14
) together leads to (ii). Moreover,
it follows by integration that
\phi_{\eta}(x)\leq C
for
\text{dist}(x,T_{\eta})\geq\eta
. This together with (
2.13
) gives the upper bound in (iii).

Step 2: Prove the second part of (i). Let

L_{\eta}=\fint_{Y}\phi_{\eta}.

Then

\int_{Y}|\phi_{\eta}-L_{\eta}|^{2}\leq C\int_{Y}|\nabla\phi_{\eta}|^{2}\leq C\eta^{d-2}.

It follows that

\int_{Y}|(\phi_{\eta})^{2}-L_{\eta}^{2}|=\int_{Y}|\phi_{\eta}+L_{\eta}||\phi_{\eta}-L_{\eta}|\leq C\int_{Y}|\phi_{\eta}-L_{\eta}|\leq C\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}},

where we have used the upper bound
\phi_{\eta}\leq C
in Step 1.
Since
\int_{Y}(\phi_{\eta})^{2}=1
, we see that

|L_{\eta}^{2}-1|\leq C\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

This implies that
|L_{\eta}-1|\leq C\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}
, by the positivity of
L_{\eta}
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\eta}-1\|_{L^{p}(Y)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi_{\eta}-L_{\eta}\|_{L^{p}(Y)}+|L_{\eta}-1|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla\phi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}+|L_{\eta}-1|\leq C\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}},

where
p=\frac{2d}{d-2}
(for
d>2
).

Step 3: Prove the lower bounds in (iii). Since
T_{\eta}
is a union of finite holes
\tau^{i}_{\eta}
, it suffices to consider the behavior of
\phi_{\eta}
near an arbitrary hole. Let
x_{1}\in Y
(view
Y
as a flat torus). We first claim that there exists
C_{*}>0
such that

(2.15)

\sup_{x\in B_{C_{*}\eta}(x_{1})}\phi_{\eta}(x)\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Actually, if the above inequality is not true, then

(2.16)

\|\phi_{\eta}-1\|_{L^{2^{*}}(Y)}\geq\frac{1}{2}|B_{C_{*}\eta}(x_{1})|^{\frac{1}{2^{*}}}\geq\frac{1}{2}|B_{1}|(C_{*}\eta)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

This contradicts to (
2.6
) if we choose
C_{*}
such that
\frac{1}{2}|B_{1}|C_{*}^{\frac{d-2}{2}}>C_{1}
. Hence the claim is proved.

We next employ a standard lifting technique to reduce the nonnegative eigenfunction
\phi_{\eta}
to a nonnegative harmonic function so that the Harnack inequality applies. Define
h(x,s)=\exp(\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}^{\frac{1}{2}}s)\phi_{\eta}(x)
. Then
(\Delta+\partial_{s}^{2})h=0
in
Y_{\eta}\times(-1,1)
and
h=0
on
\partial T_{\eta}\times(-1,1)
. Now, if
B_{2C_{*}\eta}(x)\cap T_{\eta}=\varnothing
, by the Harnack inequality for the nonnegative harmonic function
h
in
Y_{\eta}\times(-1,1)
and the previous result,

(2.17)

\inf_{B_{C_{*}\eta}(x)}\phi_{\eta}\geq\exp(-\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}^{\frac{1}{2}})\inf_{B_{C_{*}\eta}(x,0)}h\geq c\sup_{B_{C_{*}\eta}(x,0)}h\geq c\sup_{B_{C_{*}\eta}(x)}\phi_{\eta}\geq c_{1},

where we have used the fact
h(x,s)\approx\phi_{\eta}(x)
for any
(x,s)\in Y_{\eta}\times(-1,1)
and (
2.15
). This shows that
\phi_{\eta}(x)\geq c_{1}
for any
x
with
\text{dist}(x,T_{\eta})\geq 2C_{*}\eta
.

Now if
x\in Y_{\eta}
with
\text{dist}(x,T_{\eta})<2C_{*}\eta
, then by a standard barrier function argument and the lower bound for
\text{dist}(x,T_{\eta})\geq 2C_{*}\eta
, we have

(2.18)

h(x,0)\approx\phi_{\eta}(x)\geq c_{1}\eta^{-1}\text{dist}(x,T_{\eta}).

This can be shown by considering each hole
\tau_{\eta}^{i}
and the boundary behavior of
h
near the
C^{1,1}
boundary
\partial\tau_{\eta}^{i}\times(-1,1)
. The lower bound in (
2.8
) then follows.

Step 4: Prove (iv). We view
\phi_{\eta}-1
as a solution of

(2.19)

-\Delta(\phi_{\eta}-1)=\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}\phi_{\eta}.

Then for
r=\text{dist}(x,T_{\eta})>\eta
, we apply the interior estimate to get

(2.20)

|\phi_{\eta}(x)-1|\leq C\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}(x)}|\phi_{\eta}-1|^{2^{*}}\bigg)^{1/2^{*}}+C\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}r\|\phi_{\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x))}\leq C\Big(\frac{\eta}{r}\Big)^{\frac{d-2}{2}},

where we have used (
2.6
) and (
2.8
) in the last inequality.
Finally for
\text{dist}(x,T_{\eta})\leq\eta
, (
2.8
) gives the desired bounded.
∎

2.2.
Weighted Sobolev spaces

Let

L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta}}(Y_{\eta})=\left\{v\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(Y_{\eta}):v\phi_{\eta}\in L^{2}(Y_{\eta})\right\},

L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})=\left\{v\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}):v\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})\right\}.

Let
H^{1}_{\phi_{\eta},{\rm per}}(Y_{\eta})
be the periodic
\phi_{\eta}
-weighted Sobolev space defined by

H^{1}_{\phi_{\eta},{\rm per}}(Y_{\eta})=\big\{v\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(Y_{\eta}):v\in L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta}}(Y_{\eta}),\nabla v\in L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta}}(Y_{\eta})^{d},\text{ and $v$ is $Y$-periodic}\big\}.

Similarly, define the
\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}
-weighted Sobolev space in
\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}
by

H^{1}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon},0}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})=\big\{v\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}):v\in L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}),\nabla v\in L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})^{d},\text{ and $v=0$ on $\Gamma_{\eta,\varepsilon}$}\big\}.

For simplicity, from now on, we will denote
H^{1}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon},0}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
by
V_{\eta,\varepsilon}
.

The corresponding norms of the above spaces are given by

\|v\|_{H^{1}_{\phi_{\eta},{\rm per}}(Y_{\eta})}:=\|\phi_{\eta}v\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\eta})}+\|\phi_{\eta}\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\eta})},

and

\|v\|_{V_{\eta,\varepsilon}}=\|v\|_{H^{1}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon},0}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}:=\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}+\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}.

Let us first list some properties of the weighted Sobolev spaces
V_{\eta,\varepsilon}
and
H^{1}_{\phi,{\rm per}}(Y_{\eta})
. These properties guarantee the solvability of the degenerate equation associated with the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
and the validity of the classical spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators in separable Hilbert spaces.

Proposition 2.3
.

The following properties regarding the space
V_{\eta,\varepsilon}
hold:

(i)

The space
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
is dense in
V_{\eta,\varepsilon}
.

(ii)

The inclusion
V_{\eta,\varepsilon}\to L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
is continuous uniformly in
\varepsilon
and
\eta
. That is, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C\|v\|_{V_{\eta,\varepsilon}},\quad\text{for all }v\in V_{\eta,\varepsilon}.

(iii)

The map
v\to\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}v
is an isomorphism from
V_{\eta,\varepsilon}
to
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
.

(iv)

The weighted Poincaré inequality holds: there exists a constant
C
independent of
\varepsilon
and
\eta
such that

\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})},\quad\text{for all }v\in V_{\eta,\varepsilon}.

(v)

The inclusion
V_{\eta,\varepsilon}\to L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
is compact.

The proof of the qualitative properties (i), (iii) and (iv) in Proposition
2.3
can be found in
[
11
, Proposition 5.1]
and the references therein. The quantitative estimates independent of
\varepsilon
and
\eta
are special cases of Theorem
3.2
and Theorem
3.3
proved in Section
3
.

Proposition 2.4
.

The following properties regarding the space
H^{1}_{\phi_{\eta},{\rm per}}(Y_{\eta})
hold:

(i)

The space
C^{\infty}_{0,{\rm per}}(Y_{\eta})
, consisting of
C^{\infty}

periodic functions that are vanishing in a neighborhood of
T_{\eta}
, is dense in
H^{1}_{\phi_{\eta},{\rm per}}(Y_{\eta})
.

(ii)

The inclusion
H^{1}_{\phi_{\eta},{\rm per}}(Y_{\eta})\to L^{2}(Y_{\eta})
is continuous. That is, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\eta
such that

\|v\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\eta})}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{1}_{\phi_{\eta},{\rm per}}(Y_{\eta})},\quad\text{for all }v\in H^{1}_{\phi_{\eta},{\rm per}}(Y_{\eta}).

(iii)

The map
v\to\phi_{\eta}v
is an isomorphism from
H^{1}_{\phi_{\eta},{\rm per}}(Y_{\eta})
to
H^{1}_{0,{\rm per}}(Y_{\eta})
.

(iv)

The inclusion
H^{1}_{\phi_{\eta},{\rm per}}(Y_{\eta})\to L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta}}(Y_{\eta})
is compact.

The proof of Proposition
2.4
can be found in
[
11
, Proposition 5.2]
and the references therein. The independence of
\eta
of the constant
C
in (ii) can be seen by the Hardy’s inequality applied in a cell as in (
3.8
).

2.3.
Two-scale expansion

One of the main tools to study the asymptotic behavior of the eigenvalues is the minimax principle (or the maximin principle). The minimax principle for the eigenvalue problem (
1.1
) states that

(2.21)

\lambda_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})\\
\text{dim}S=k\end{subarray}}\max_{u\in S}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|\nabla u|^{2}}{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}u^{2}}.

The following lemma, first discovered in
[
11
]
, provides the two-scale relationship between the eigenvalues of (
1.1
) and the principal eigenvalue of (
2.1
).

Lemma 2.5
.

For all
v\in H^{1}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon},0}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
, we have

(2.22)

\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\nabla(\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}v)\cdot\nabla(\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}v)=\varepsilon^{-2}\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}+\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}.

See
[
11
, Lemma 6.1]
for a proof of the above lemma.

Dividing
\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}
on both sides of (
2.22
), we get

\frac{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\nabla(\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}v)\cdot\nabla(\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}v)}{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}}=\varepsilon^{-2}\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}+\frac{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}}{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}}.

By Proposition
2.3
(iii) and the minimax principle (
2.21
), we see

(2.23)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}

\displaystyle=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset H^{1}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon},0}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})\\
\text{dim}S=k\end{subarray}}\max_{v\in S}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\nabla(\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}v)\cdot\nabla(\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}v)}{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{-2}\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}+\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset H^{1}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon},0}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})\\
\text{dim}S=k\end{subarray}}\max_{v\in S}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}}{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}}.

Observe that the last term on the right-hand side is the Rayleigh quotient in the weighted Sobolev space
V_{\eta,\varepsilon}
, which leads to the following eigenvalue problem

(2.24)

-\nabla\cdot(\phi^{2}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k})=\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}\quad\text{ in }\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}.

To see that this eigenvalue problem is well-posed, we first verify that the problem

(2.25)

\mathcal{L}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(u_{\eta,\varepsilon}):=-\nabla\cdot(\phi^{2}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon})=\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}f\quad\text{in }\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}

has a unique weak solution
u_{\eta,\varepsilon}\in V_{\eta,\varepsilon}
for any
f\in L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
, in the sense that for each
v\in H^{1}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon},0}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
, we have

(2.26)

\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla v=\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}fv.

This follows easily from the weighted Poincaré inequality in Proposition
2.3
(iv) and the Lax-Milgram theorem in the Hilbert space
V_{\eta,\varepsilon}
. Note that if the right-hand side of (
2.25
) is replaced by a divergence form
\nabla\cdot(\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}g)
with
g\in L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})^{d}
, then the equation is still solvable by the Lax-Milgram theorem, since
\nabla\cdot(\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}g)
is in the dual space of
V_{\eta,\varepsilon}
.

Also by Proposition
2.3
(v), we see that the linear operator
\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}:f\to u_{\eta,\varepsilon}
is compact and self-ajoint in
L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
. Thus the classical spectral theory applies and there exists a sequence of positive eigenvalues
\{\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}:k=1,2,\cdots\}
of the problem (
2.24
) such that
\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
. Moreover, the minimax principle gives

(2.27)

\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset V_{\eta,\varepsilon}\\
\text{dim}S=k\end{subarray}}\max_{v\in S}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}}{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}}.

Alternatively, the maximin principle for the compact operator
\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
implies

(2.28)

\frac{1}{\mu^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}}=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})\\
\text{dim}S=k\end{subarray}}\min_{v\in S}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(v)\cdot v}{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}}.

Now, we rigorously show the two-scale relationship between the eigenfunctions of (
1.1
) and (
2.24
).

Proposition 2.6
.

\psi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}
is an eigenfunction of (
1.1
) corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}
if and only if there exists an eigenfunction
{\rho}_{\varepsilon}^{k}
of
(
2.24
) corresponding to
\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}
such that
\psi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}=\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}
and
\lambda_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}=\varepsilon^{-2}\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}+\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}
.

Proof.

Let
\psi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}
be an eigenfunction of (
1.1
) corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}
. Since
\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}>0
in
\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}
, we may write
\psi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}=\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}
. Since
\psi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
, then it is easy to verify that
u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}\in H^{1}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon},0}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
(also see Proposition
2.3
(iii)). Now we directly compute

-\Delta\psi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}=-\Delta\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}-2\nabla\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}-\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\Delta u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}=\varepsilon^{-2}\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}-2\nabla\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}-\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\Delta u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k},

where we have used the equation (
2.3
). By
-\Delta\psi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}=\lambda_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}\psi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}
, we have

-2\nabla\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}-\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\Delta u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}=(\lambda_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}-\varepsilon^{-2}\overline{\lambda}_{\eta})\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}.

Multiplying the above equation by
\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}
and observing
\nabla\cdot(\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k})=2\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}+\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\Delta u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}
, we obtain

-\nabla\cdot(\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k})=(\lambda_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}-\varepsilon^{-2}\overline{\lambda}_{\eta})\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}.

This equation shows that
\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}=u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}
is an eigenfunction of (
2.24
) corresponding to the eigenvalue
\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}=\lambda_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}-\varepsilon^{-2}\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}
. The proof for the other direction is similar and straightforward.
∎

2.4.
Correctors and homogenized operators

By virtue of Proposition
2.6
, it suffices to study the eigenvalue problem (
2.24
) and the weighted or degenerated elliptic equation (
2.25
). For each fixed
\eta\in(0,1]
, it can be shown as in
[
11
]
that as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the eigenvalue
\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}
converges to some
\mu_{\eta}^{k}
, which is the
k
th eigenvalue of

(2.29)

\mathcal{L}_{\eta}(\rho_{\eta}^{k}):=-\nabla\cdot(\overline{A}_{\eta}\nabla\rho_{\eta}^{k})=\mu_{\eta}^{k}\rho_{\eta}^{k},\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

where
\rho_{\eta}^{k}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
\overline{A}_{\eta}
is a constant matrix satisfying the ellipticity condition uniformly in
\eta
.

The homogenized matrix
\overline{A}_{\eta}
is defined in a usual way as in the classical homogenization theory. The correctors
\{\chi^{j}_{\eta}:j=1,2,\cdots,d\}
are the unique weak solutions of

(2.30)

-\nabla\cdot(\phi_{\eta}^{2}\nabla\chi^{j}_{\eta})=\nabla\cdot(\phi^{2}_{\eta}e_{j})\quad\text{in }Y_{\eta},

satisfying
\chi^{j}_{\eta}\in H^{1}_{\phi,{\rm per}}(Y_{\eta})
and
\int_{Y_{\eta}}\chi^{j}_{\eta}=0
. Then the homogenized matrix
\overline{A}_{\eta}
is given by

\overline{A}_{\eta}=I+\int_{Y_{\eta}}\phi_{\eta}^{2}\nabla\chi_{\eta},\quad\text{or in the component form }(\bar{a}_{\eta})_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+\int_{Y_{\eta}}\phi_{\eta}^{2}\partial_{i}\chi^{j}_{\eta}.

Here and after, we will view
\nabla\chi_{\eta}
as a
d\times d
matrix given by
(\nabla\chi_{\eta})_{ij}=\partial_{i}\chi^{j}_{\eta}
.
It was shown in
[
11
]
that
\overline{A}_{\eta}
is elliptic, i.e., there exists
\Lambda>0
(independent of
\eta
) such that
\xi\cdot\overline{A}_{\eta}\xi\geq\Lambda|\xi|^{2}
for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. From the equation (
2.30
), one can also see

(2.31)

(\bar{a}_{\eta})_{ij}=\int_{Y_{\eta}}\phi_{\eta}^{2}(\delta_{ki}+\partial_{k}\chi^{i}_{\eta})(\delta_{kj}+\partial_{k}\chi^{j}_{\eta}).

It should be pointed out that the matrix
\overline{A}_{\eta}
depends only on the geometric structure of the holes in
T_{\eta}
.

There is an alternative way to see the corrector equation (
2.30
). Indeed, if we set
\widehat{\chi}_{\eta}^{j}=\phi_{\eta}\chi^{j}_{\eta}
(called weighted correctors), then the equation (
2.30
) becomes

(2.32)

-\Delta\widehat{\chi}_{\eta}^{j}=\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}\widehat{\chi}_{\eta}^{j}+2\partial_{j}\phi_{\eta}\qquad\text{in }Y_{\eta},

with
\widehat{\chi}_{\eta}^{j}\in H^{1}_{0,{\rm per}}(Y_{\eta})
.
The homogenized matrix
\overline{A}_{\eta}
can be written as

\overline{A}_{\eta}=I+2\int_{Y_{\eta}}\phi_{\eta}\nabla\widehat{\chi}_{\eta}.

This can be verified directly; also see
[
11
, Theorem 8.1]
.

When
\eta
is small, by Lemma
2.2
(i) or (iv), we know that
\phi
is close to the constant
1
. This fact will pass to the smallness of the correctors
\chi_{\eta}
through the energy estimate.
More related estimates are included in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.7
.

There exists a constant
C_{1}
such that the following estimates hold.

(i)

Norm estimate:

(2.33)

\|\phi_{\eta}\nabla\chi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\eta})}+\|\phi_{\eta}\chi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2^{*}}(Y_{\eta})}+\|\chi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\eta})}\leq C_{1}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

(ii)

Gradient estimate: For any
x\in Y_{\eta}
,

(2.34)

|\phi_{\eta}\nabla\chi_{\eta}(x)|\leq C_{1}\eta^{-1}\Big\{\Big(\frac{\eta}{{\rm dist}(x,T_{\eta})}\Big)^{\frac{d}{2}}\wedge 1\Big\}.

(iii)

Pointwise estimate: For any
x\in Y_{\eta}
,

(2.35)

|\chi_{\eta}(x)|\leq C_{1}\Big\{\Big(\frac{\eta}{{\rm dist}(x,T_{\eta})}\Big)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\wedge 1\Big\}.

(iv)

Homogenized matrix estimate:
|\overline{A}_{\eta}-I|\leq C_{1}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}
.

Proof.

To prove (i), we writing the equation (
2.30
) as

(2.36)

-\nabla\cdot(\phi_{\eta}^{2}\nabla\chi^{j}_{\eta})=2\phi_{\eta}\partial_{j}\phi_{\eta}\quad\text{in }Y_{\eta},

and using the energy estimate, we have

(2.37)

\|\phi_{\eta}\nabla\chi_{\eta}^{j}\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\eta})}\leq C\|\partial_{j}\phi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\eta})}\leq C\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

The estimates of
\|\phi_{\eta}\chi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2^{*}}(Y_{\eta})}
and
\|\chi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\eta})}
follows from the weighted Sobolev-Poincaré inequality; see the proofs of
[
10
, Lemma 2.2, 2.3]
for the case of small
\eta
. Observe that (iv) follows from (
2.37
) and the definition of
\overline{A}_{\eta}
.

Next we prove the boundary estimate of (ii) and (iii). Consider the equation (
2.32
) for
\widehat{\chi}_{\eta}=\phi_{\eta}\chi_{\eta}
. Recall that
\widehat{\chi}_{\eta}=0
on
\partial T_{\eta}
. Suppose
x_{0}\in\partial T_{\eta}
. Applying the elliptic regularity in
Y_{\eta}\cap B_{\eta}(x_{0})
, we obtain

(2.38)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\widehat{\chi}_{\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}(Y_{\eta}\cap B_{\eta}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\eta^{-1}\bigg(\fint_{Y_{\eta}\cap B_{2\eta}(x_{0})}|\widehat{\chi}_{\eta}|^{2^{*}}\bigg)^{1/2^{*}}+C\eta\|\nabla\phi_{\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}(Y_{\eta}\cap B_{2\eta}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\eta^{-1}.

Thus for
x\in Y_{\eta}
with
\text{dist}(x,T_{\eta})\leq\eta
,

(2.39)

|\widehat{\chi}_{\eta}(x)|=|\phi_{\eta}(x)\chi_{\eta}(x)|\leq C\text{dist}(x,T_{\eta})\eta^{-1}.

In view of (
2.8
), this implies
|\chi_{\eta}(x)|\leq C
for
x\in Y_{\eta}
with
\text{dist}(x,T_{\eta})\leq\eta
.
Moreover, using
\phi_{\eta}\nabla\chi_{\eta}(x)=\nabla\widehat{\chi}_{\eta}-\nabla\phi_{\eta}\chi_{\eta}
, Lemma
2.2
(ii) and the previous estimates, we have
|\phi_{\eta}\nabla\chi_{\eta}(x)|\leq C\eta^{-1}
for any
x\in Y_{\eta}
with
\text{dist}(x,T_{\eta})\leq\eta
.

Finally, we prove the interior estimates of (ii) and (iii). Note that for
\text{dist}(x,T_{\eta})>\eta
, the equation (
2.30
) is not degenerate. Let
r=\text{dist}(x,T_{\eta})>\eta
. Then the elliptic estimate combined with (
2.6
) and Proposition
2.2
(iv) leads to

(2.40)

\displaystyle|\chi_{\eta}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\fint_{B_{r/2}(x)}|\chi_{\eta}|^{2^{*}}\bigg)^{1/2^{*}}+Cr\|\phi_{\eta}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r/2}(x))}

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{-\frac{d}{2^{*}}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}+Cr\Big(\frac{\eta}{r}\Big)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\leq C\Big(\frac{\eta}{r}\Big)^{\frac{d-2}{2}},

and

(2.41)

\displaystyle|\nabla\chi_{\eta}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\fint_{B_{r/2}(x)}|\nabla\chi_{\eta}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\|\phi_{\eta}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r/2}(x))}

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{-\frac{d}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}+C\Big(\frac{\eta}{r}\Big)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\leq C\eta^{-1}\Big(\frac{\eta}{r}\Big)^{\frac{d}{2}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

The estimate (
2.34
) implies
\|\phi_{\eta}\nabla\chi_{\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}(Y_{\eta})}\leq C\eta^{-1}
. By an interpolation inequality between this and the
L^{2}
estimate in (
2.6
), we have
\|\phi_{\eta}\nabla\chi_{\eta}\|_{L^{p}(Y_{\eta})}\leq C\eta^{\frac{d}{p}-1}
for any
p>2
. Similarly, we can estimate
\|\phi_{\eta}\chi_{\eta}\|_{L^{p}(Y_{\eta})}
and
\|\chi_{\eta}\|_{L^{p}(Y_{\eta})}
.

2.5.
Three eigenvalue problems

We summarize the three different eigenvalue problems, listed as follows:

(2.42)

\displaystyle\text{Degenerate problem: }\mathcal{L}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k})

\displaystyle=\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}\quad\text{ in }\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon},

(2.43)

\displaystyle\text{Intermediate problem: }\mathcal{L}_{\eta}(\tilde{\rho}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k})

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mu}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\tilde{\rho}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

(2.44)

\displaystyle\text{Homogenized problem: }\mathcal{L}_{\eta}({\rho}_{\eta}^{k})

\displaystyle=\mu_{\eta}^{k}{\rho}_{\eta}^{k}\qquad\text{ in }\Omega.

The three types of elliptic equations corresponding to the eigenvalue problems are:

(2.45)

\displaystyle\text{Degenerate equation: }\mathcal{L}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(u_{\eta,\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}f,

\displaystyle\text{with }f\in L_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon});

(2.46)

\displaystyle\text{Intermediate equation: }\mathcal{L}_{\eta}(\tilde{u}_{\eta,\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}f,

\displaystyle\text{with }f\in L_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon});

(2.47)

\displaystyle\text{Homogenized equation: }\mathcal{L}_{\eta}(u_{\eta})

\displaystyle=f,

\displaystyle\text{with }f\in L^{2}(\Omega).

All three equations are solvable in suitable spaces. Precisely, (
2.45
) is solvable in
V_{\eta,\varepsilon}=H^{1}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon},0}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
if
f\in L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
; (
2.46
) is solvable in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
if
f\in L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
; and (
2.47
) is solvable in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
if
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Define the bounded operators (resolvents) corresponding to the above equations:

(2.48)

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}:L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})\to V_{\eta,\varepsilon},\text{ with }\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(f)=u_{\eta,\varepsilon};

(2.49)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathscr{T}}_{\eta,\varepsilon}:L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})\to H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\quad\text{ with }\widetilde{\mathscr{T}}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(f)=\tilde{u}_{\eta,\varepsilon};

(2.50)

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}_{\eta}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\qquad\text{ with }\mathscr{T}_{\eta}(f)=u_{\eta}.

Previously we have seen that
\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
is a self-ajoint compact operator in
L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
such that the spectral theory applies to the degenerate eigenvalue problem. It is classical that
\mathscr{T}_{\eta}
is a self-adjoint compact operator in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and thus the spectral theory applies to the homogenized eigenvalue problem. Now we consider the intermediate eigenvalue problem.
We will first view
\widetilde{\mathscr{T}}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
as a compact self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
since
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
restricted on
\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}
is a subspace of
V_{\eta,\varepsilon}
. Therefore,
\widetilde{\mathscr{T}}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
is also a self-adjoint compact operator in the space
L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
. Note that the eigenfunctions of
\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
and
\widetilde{\mathscr{T}}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
form two different orthonormal basis of the same space
L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
.
On the other hand, since
L^{2}(\Omega)
restricted in
\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}
is a subspace of
L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
, we can also view
\widetilde{\mathscr{T}}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
as a compact operator on
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Thus, we may also compare
\mathscr{T}_{\eta}
and
\widetilde{\mathscr{T}}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
as operators on
L^{2}(\Omega)
. However, it is important to point out that
\widetilde{\mathscr{T}}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
is not self-adjoint in the space
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
As usual, the eigenfunctions are orthonormal in the corresponding spaces, i.e., for any
i,j\geq 1
,

(2.51)

\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{i}\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}=\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\tilde{\rho}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{i}\tilde{\rho}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}=\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\eta}^{i}\rho_{\eta}^{j}=\delta_{ij}.

3.
Regularity of degenerate equations

In this subsection, we first prove some basic inequality in weighted Sobolev spaces and then use them to derive certain regularity of eigenfunctions.

3.1.
Weighted Sobolev inequalities

The proofs of the weighted Sobolev inequalities use harmonic extension. We will not include all the details of the proofs in this section as similar arguments may be found in
[
10
, Section 2]
.

Let
T_{\eta}^{*}=\{x\in Y:\text{dist}(x,T_{\eta})\leq\eta\}
.
Let

(3.1)

\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}=\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{z}\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{*}).

For
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*})
, we use
u^{*}
to denote the harmonic extension of
u
to
\Omega
,
i.e., the value of
u^{*}
in
\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{*})\subset\Omega
is defined by the solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta u^{*}&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{*}),\\
u^{*}&=u&\quad&\text{ on }\varepsilon(z+\partial T_{\eta}^{*}).\end{aligned}\right.

By the boundedness of harmonic extension in
W^{1,p}
space, we have
u^{*}\in W^{1,p}(\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{*}))
.

For the holes intersecting with the boundary
\partial\Omega
, we need to modify the harmonic extension. We distinguish between two cases. If
u=0
on
\partial\Omega\cap\partial\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}
, then for each hole
\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{*})\cap\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset
, we define
u^{*}
in
T_{\eta}^{*}\cap\Omega
by

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta u^{*}&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{*})\cap\Omega,\\
u^{*}&=u&\quad&\text{ on }\varepsilon(z+\partial T_{\eta}^{*})\cap\Omega,\\
u^{*}&=0&\quad&\text{ on }\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{*})\cap\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

This guarantees that
u^{*}=0
on
\partial\Omega
and therefore
u^{*}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

For general
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*})
, we first flatten the boundary
\partial\Omega
in a neighborhood of a hole by a change of variables. Now since the boundary is flat, a function in
W^{1,p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*})
can be extended by an even reflection across the flat boundary with a reflected hole symmetric about the flat boundary. Then a standard harmonic extension applies to this extended hole. Finally, changing variables back to the original boundary, we get the extended function
u^{*}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
D_{\eta,\varepsilon}=\{x\in\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}:{\rm dist}(x,\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*})\leq\varepsilon\eta\}\subset\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}.
Let
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*})
and
u^{*}
be the harmonic extension defined above.
Then we have the following properties:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\|u^{*}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*})}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|u\|_{L^{p}(D_{\eta,\varepsilon})}+\varepsilon\eta\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(D_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\big),

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u^{*}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(D_{\eta,\varepsilon})},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u^{*}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*})},

where
C
is independent of
\varepsilon
and
\eta
.

Proof.

These estimates may be proved by using
[
10
, Lemma 2.1]
around each hole.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
u\in W^{1,p}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
. Then there exists
C>0
such that

(3.6)

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\eta\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}+C\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}

Moreover, if
u\in W^{1,p}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon},0}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
, then

(3.7)

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}

Proof.

Using Hardy’s inequality, we obtain

(3.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{*}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\Omega}|u|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{*}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\{\text{dist}(x,\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}))\}^{p}\,dx+C\int_{\varepsilon(z+Y_{\eta}\setminus T_{\eta}^{*})\cap\Omega}|u|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon\eta)^{p}\int_{\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{*}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\Omega}|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u|^{p}+C\int_{\varepsilon(z+Y_{\eta}\setminus T_{\eta}^{*})\cap\Omega}|u|^{p},

where we have used the fact
\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}(x)\approx(\varepsilon\eta)^{-1}\text{dist}(x,\varepsilon T_{\eta})
for
x\in Y
with
\text{dist}(x,\varepsilon T_{\eta})\leq C\varepsilon\eta
; see (
2.8
).
This gives (
3.6
) by summation over
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Note that (
3.8
) also implies

(3.9)

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\eta\|\phi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}+C\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*})}.

If
u\in W^{1,p}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon},0}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
and
u^{*}
is its harmonic extension according to the above construction, then
u^{*}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Hence

(3.10)

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*})}\leq\|u^{*}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla u^{*}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*})}\leq C\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})},

where we have used the Poincaré inequality in the second inequality and (
3.5
) in the third inequality. This combined with (
3.9
) leads to (
3.7
).
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
1<p<d
and
u\in W^{1,p}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon},0}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(3.11)

\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}

Proof.

Let
u^{*}
be the harmonic extension of
u|_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}}
to
\Omega
. Then the classical Sobolev-Poincaré inequality in
\Omega
leads to

(3.12)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*})}

\displaystyle\leq\|u^{*}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla u^{*}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*})}\leq C\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}.

Let
T_{\eta}^{**}=\{x\in Y:\text{dist}(x,T_{\eta})\leq 2\eta\}
. Note that
T_{\eta}\subset T_{\eta}^{*}\subset T_{\eta}^{**}\subset Y
. By the Sobolev inequality in
\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})

(3.13)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})}|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}u|^{p^{*}}\right)^{1/{p^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})}|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u|^{p}\right)^{1/p}+\frac{C}{\varepsilon\eta}\left(\int_{\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta}^{*})}|u|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})}|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u|^{p}\right)^{1/p}+C\left(\int_{\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta}^{*})}|u|^{p^{*}}\right)^{1/{p^{*}}},

where we have used the facts
|\nabla\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}|\leq C(\varepsilon\eta)^{-1}
in
\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta}^{*})
and
|T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta}^{*}|\leq C(\varepsilon\eta)^{d}
.
By the summation over
z
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}}|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}u|^{p^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{z}\left(\int_{\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})}|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u|^{p}\right)^{p^{*}/p}+C\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}}|u|^{p^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u\|^{p^{*}}_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}+C\|u\|^{p^{*}}_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u\|^{p^{*}}_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})},

where we have used (
3.12
) in the last inequality. This and (
3.12
) give the desired estimate.
∎

3.2.
Global
L^{p}
estimate

Now, we consider the equation

(3.14)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathcal{L}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(u_{\eta,\varepsilon})&=\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}f,&\quad&\text{in }\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon},\\
u_{\eta,\varepsilon}&=0,&\quad&\text{on }\Gamma_{\eta,\varepsilon}.\end{aligned}\right.

We say
u_{\eta,\varepsilon}\in V_{\eta,\varepsilon}
is a weak solution of (
3.14
), if for any
\varphi\in V_{\eta,\varepsilon}
, we have

(3.15)

\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi=\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}f\varphi.

By the Lax-Milgram theorem, the weak solution exists and is unique if
f\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
p\in[2,\infty)
and
f\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
. Let
u_{\eta,\varepsilon}\in V_{\eta,\varepsilon}
be a weak solution of (
3.14
). Then

(i)

If
p\in[2,d)
, then

(3.16)

\|u_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}.

(ii)

If
p>d
, then

(3.17)

\|u_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}.

Proof.

(i) Let
u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}=\max\{-M,\min\{u_{\eta,\varepsilon},M\}\}
for
M>0
. We will prove a uniform estimate for
u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}
and then take the limit as
M\to\infty
. Since now
u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}
is bounded,
|u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}|^{p^{*}-2}u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}
can serve as a test function in
V_{\eta,\varepsilon}
for the equation (
4.22
). It follows that

(3.18)

(p^{*}-1)\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}|^{p^{*}-2}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}=\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}f\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}|u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}|^{p^{*}-2}u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}.

Since
\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}
is supported in
\{|u_{\eta,\varepsilon}|\leq M\}
, in which
\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}=\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}
, (
3.18
) can be simplified to

(3.19)

(p^{*}-1)\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}|^{p^{*}-2}|\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}|^{2}=\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}f\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}|u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}|^{p^{*}-2}u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}.

Let
v_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}=|u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}|^{\frac{p^{*}-2}{2}}u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}
. Then
\nabla v_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}=\frac{p^{*}}{2}|\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}|^{\frac{p^{*}-2}{2}}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}
. Let
\frac{1}{2^{+}}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{d}
and
\frac{1}{2^{+}}+\frac{1}{2^{-}}=1
. It follows from (
3.19
) that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla v_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}f|u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}|^{\frac{p^{*}-2}{2}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}v_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|f|u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}|^{\frac{p^{*}-2}{2}}|^{2^{-}}\bigg)^{1/2^{-}}\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}v_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}|^{2^{+}}\bigg)^{1/2^{+}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|f|u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}|^{\frac{p^{*}-2}{2}}|^{2^{-}}\bigg)^{1/2^{-}}\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla v_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

where we have used Theorem
3.3
in the last inequality.
Hence, Theorem
3.2
implies

(3.20)

\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}|^{p^{*}}=\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|v_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}|^{2}\leq C\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla v_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}|^{2}\leq C_{p}\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|f|u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}|^{\frac{p^{*}-2}{2}}|^{2^{-}}\bigg)^{2/2^{-}}.

Now let
\kappa>1
be such that
\frac{p^{*}-2}{2}\kappa=p^{*}
and
\frac{1}{\kappa}+\frac{1}{\kappa^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2^{-}}
. Then

\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|f|u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}|^{\frac{p^{*}-2}{2}}|^{2^{-}}\bigg)^{1/2^{-}}\leq\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|f|^{\kappa^{\prime}}\bigg)^{1/\kappa^{\prime}}\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}|^{p^{*}}\bigg)^{1/\kappa}.

Substituting this into (
3.20
), we obtain

\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}|^{p^{*}}\bigg)^{(1/2)-(1/\kappa)}\leq C\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|f|^{\kappa^{\prime}}\bigg)^{1/\kappa^{\prime}}.

This implies

\|u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{M}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}.

by noting
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\kappa}=\frac{1}{p^{*}}
and
\frac{1}{\kappa^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p^{*}}+\frac{1}{d}
. Observe that in the last inequality, the constant
C
is independent of
M
. Letting
M\to\infty
, we obtain the desired estimate.

(ii) We apply the De Giorgi’s iteration. We can even prove the more general case
u_{\eta,\varepsilon}=g
on
\partial\Gamma_{\eta,\varepsilon}
with bounded
g
. Let
\ell_{0}:=\sup_{\Gamma_{\eta,\varepsilon}}g
. Let
k>\ell_{0}
. Define
v_{k}=(u_{\eta,\varepsilon}-k)_{+}:=\max\{u_{\eta,\varepsilon}-k,0\}
and
E_{k}=\{x\in\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}:u_{\eta,\varepsilon}>k\}
. Clearly,
v_{k}\in V_{\eta,\varepsilon}
and
\nabla v_{k}=\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}
in
E_{k}
and
\nabla v_{k}=0
in
E_{k}^{c}
. It follows that

(3.21)

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla v_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla v_{k}\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{E_{k}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}fv_{k}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}v_{k}\|_{L^{2^{*}}(E_{k})}|E_{k}|^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla v_{k}\|_{L^{2}(E_{k})}|E_{k}|^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{p}}.

Thus,

(3.22)

\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla v_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}|E_{k}|^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{p}}.

On the other hand, for any
h>k>\ell_{0}

(3.23)

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla v_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\geq C^{-1}\|v_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\geq C^{-1}|E_{h}|^{\frac{1}{2}}(h-k).

It follows that

(3.24)

|E_{h}|\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}^{2}\frac{|E_{k}|^{1+\beta}}{|h-k|^{2}},

where
\beta=2(\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{p})>0
. Therefore, by a standard iteration argument,

(3.25)

|E_{\ell_{0}+k_{0}}|=0,

with

(3.26)

k_{0}=C^{\frac{1}{2}}2^{\frac{1+\beta}{\beta}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}|E_{\ell_{0}}|^{\frac{\beta}{2}}.

This implies that

(3.27)

\sup_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}u_{\eta,\varepsilon}\leq\sup_{\Gamma_{\eta,\varepsilon}}g+C_{p}|\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}|^{\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}.

The desired estimate follows easily.
∎

The above lemma can be used to show the boundedness of the eigenfunctions
\{\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}\}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

For any
1\leq j\leq k
, we have

(3.28)

\|\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C_{k}.

Proof.

We apply (
3.16
) repeatedly to the equation
\mathcal{L}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j})=\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}
in
\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}
until
p>d
. Then using (
3.17
) one more time, we obtain the
L^{\infty}
bound.
∎

3.3.
Local weighted Lipschitz estimate

Recall that
T_{\eta}^{*}=\{x\in Y:\text{dist}(x,T_{\eta})\leq\eta\}
and
T_{\eta}^{**}=\{x\in Y:\text{dist}(x,T_{\eta})\leq 2\eta\}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
p>d
. Let
u_{\eta,\varepsilon}\in V_{\eta,\varepsilon}
be a weak solution of (
3.14
). Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be such that
\varepsilon(k+T_{\eta}^{**})\subset\Omega
. Then

(3.29)

\sup_{\varepsilon(k+T_{\eta}^{*}\setminus T_{\eta})}|u_{\eta,\varepsilon}|\leq C\bigg(\fint_{\varepsilon(k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})}|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}u_{\eta,\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C(\varepsilon\eta)^{2}\bigg(\fint_{\varepsilon(k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p},

and

(3.30)

\sup_{\varepsilon(k+T_{\eta}^{*}\setminus T_{\eta})}|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}|\leq C\bigg(\fint_{\varepsilon(k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})}|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\varepsilon\eta\bigg(\fint_{\varepsilon(k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

Proof.

The local
L^{\infty}
estimate (
3.29
) can be proved via De Giorgi or Moser’s iteration as in
[
10
, Lemma 3.1]
. Here we take advantage of the particular properties of the equation to give a simple proof. Let
v(x)=u_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\varepsilon x)
. Then (
3.14
) is reduced to

(3.31)

-\nabla\cdot(\phi_{\eta}^{2}\nabla v)=\varepsilon\phi_{\eta}f(\varepsilon x),\quad\text{in }\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}.

Let
\hat{v}(x)=\phi_{\eta}(x)v(x)
. Then
\hat{v}
satisfies

(3.32)

-\Delta\hat{v}=\overline{\lambda}_{\eta}\hat{v}+\varepsilon^{2}f(\varepsilon x),\quad\text{in }\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}.

Consider the equation in a typical
k+Y_{\eta}\subset\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega
. By the classical
L^{\infty}
estimate of elliptic equations, we have

(3.33)

\|\hat{v}\|_{L^{\infty}(k+T_{\eta}^{*}\setminus T_{\eta})}\leq C\bigg(\fint_{k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta}}|\hat{v}|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\eta^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\bigg(\fint_{k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta}}|f(\varepsilon x)|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Now, using the the gradient estimate
T_{\eta}^{*}\setminus T_{\eta}
, we have

(3.34)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\hat{v}\|_{L^{\infty}(k+T_{\eta}^{*}\setminus T_{\eta})}\leq\frac{C}{\eta}\bigg(\fint_{k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta}}|\hat{v}|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\eta\varepsilon^{2}\bigg(\fint_{k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta}}|f(\varepsilon x)|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

By integration, for
x\in k+T_{\eta}^{*}\setminus T_{\eta}
,

(3.35)

|\hat{v}(x)|\leq C\frac{\text{dist}(x,k+T_{\eta})}{\eta}\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta}}|\hat{v}|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\eta^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\bigg(\fint_{k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta}}|f(\varepsilon x)|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\bigg\}.

Due to
\hat{v}=\phi_{\eta}v
and Lemma
2.2
(iii), we obtain from the above inequality

(3.36)

\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(k+T_{\eta}^{*}\setminus T_{\eta})}\leq C\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta}}|\hat{v}|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\eta^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\bigg(\fint_{k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta}}|f(\varepsilon x)|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\bigg\}.

Now, using
\nabla\hat{v}=\nabla\phi_{\eta}v+\phi_{\eta}\nabla v
, we derive from (
3.34
) and (
3.36
) that

(3.37)

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\eta}\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}(k+T_{\eta}^{*}\setminus T_{\eta})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\hat{v}\|_{L^{\infty}(k+T_{\eta}^{*}\setminus T_{\eta})}+\|\nabla\phi_{\eta}v\|_{L^{\infty}(k+T_{\eta}^{*}\setminus T_{\eta})}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg\{\frac{1}{\eta}\bigg(\fint_{k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta}}|\phi_{\eta}v|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\eta\varepsilon^{2}\bigg(\fint_{k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta}}|f(\varepsilon x)|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\bigg\}.

Note that,
v-L
with arbitrary constant
L
also satisfies the equation (
3.31
). Thus the first term on the right-hand side of (
3.37
) can be replaced by the weighted Poincaré inequality

(3.38)

\inf_{L\in\mathbb{R}}\bigg(\fint_{k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta}}|\phi_{\eta}(v-L)|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\eta\bigg(\fint_{k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta}}|\phi_{\eta}\nabla v|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It follows that

(3.39)

\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}(k+T_{\eta}^{*}\setminus T_{\eta})}\leq C\bigg(\fint_{k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta}}|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla v|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\eta\varepsilon^{2}\bigg(\fint_{k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta}}|f(\varepsilon x)|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Finally, (
3.36
) and (
3.39
) imply the desired estimates by rescaling back to
u_{\eta,\varepsilon}
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
p>d
. Let
u_{\eta,\varepsilon}\in V_{\eta,\varepsilon}
be a weak solution of (
3.14
). Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be such that
\varepsilon(k+T_{\eta})\cap\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset
. Then

(3.40)

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon(k+T_{\eta}^{*}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|u_{\eta,\varepsilon}|

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\fint_{\varepsilon(k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}u_{\eta,\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+C(\varepsilon\eta)^{2}\bigg(\fint_{\varepsilon(k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p},

and

(3.41)

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon(k+T_{\eta}^{*}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}|

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\fint_{\varepsilon(k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+C\varepsilon\eta\bigg(\fint_{\varepsilon(k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

Proof.

We apply a similar argument as Lemma
3.6
. Let
\hat{v}
be the same as in (
3.32
). We consider a boundary cell
k+Y_{\eta}
such that
(k+T_{\eta})\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset
. Without loss of generality assume
T_{\eta}
has only one hole. By the geometric assumption
A
,
(k+T_{\eta})\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega
is a Lipschitz domain. Moreover, since both
\Omega
and
T_{\eta}
are
C^{1,1}
, the intersection
(k+T_{\eta})\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega
satisfies the exterior ball condition.

By the classical
L^{\infty}
estimate of elliptic equation in Lipschitz domains, we have

(3.42)

\|\hat{v}\|_{L^{\infty}((k+T_{\eta}^{*}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega})\leq C\bigg(\fint_{(k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega}|\hat{v}|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\eta^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\bigg(\fint_{(k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega}|f(\varepsilon x)|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

To obtain the gradient estimate, we can apply a barrier argument in the Lipschitz domain satisfying the exterior ball condition to obtain

(3.43)

\displaystyle|\hat{v}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\text{dist}(x,k+T_{\eta})}{\eta}

\displaystyle\times\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{(k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega}|\hat{v}|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\eta^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\bigg(\fint_{(k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega}|f(\varepsilon x)|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\bigg\}.

Then for each
x\in(k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega
with
r=\text{dist}(x,k+T_{\eta})
, we apply the interior Lipschitz estimate to get for any
x\in(k+T_{\eta}^{*}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega
,

(3.44)

\displaystyle|\nabla\hat{v}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{r}\bigg(\fint_{(k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap B_{r/2}(x)}|\hat{v}|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+Cr\varepsilon^{2}\bigg(\fint_{(k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap B_{r/2}(x)}|f(\varepsilon x)|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\eta}\bigg(\fint_{(k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega}|\hat{v}|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\eta\varepsilon^{2}\bigg(\fint_{(k+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\Omega}|f(\varepsilon x)|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Notice that (
3.43
) and
(
3.44
)
are analogs of (
3.35
) and (
3.34
). Then the desired estimates follow from a similar argument as Lemma
3.6
.
∎

4.
First-order approximation by harmonic extension

In this subsection, we derive the error estimate of the first-order approximation for the boundary value problem,

(4.1)

\mathcal{L}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(u_{\eta,\varepsilon})=F\quad\text{in }\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}\quad\text{and}\quad u_{\eta,\varepsilon}=0\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{\eta,\varepsilon},

where
F\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
.
We show that the homogenized problem as
\varepsilon\to 0
is

(4.2)

\mathcal{L}_{\eta}(u_{\eta})=\widetilde{F}\quad\text{in }\Omega,\quad\text{and}\quad u_{\eta}=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,

where
\widetilde{F}
is the zero extension of
F
in
\Omega
. We mention that for the degenerate equation (
4.1
) in the perforated domain
\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}
, a natural boundary condition on
\Sigma_{\eta,\varepsilon}=\partial T_{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega
is satisfied automatically via the variational form of the weak solutions analogous to (
2.26
).

Let
c_{1}\in(0,\frac{1}{4}c_{0}]
be a constant. Let
\theta_{\varepsilon}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
be a cutoff function such that
\theta_{\varepsilon}=1
if
\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)>2c_{1}\varepsilon
,
\theta_{\varepsilon}=0
if
\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<c_{1}\varepsilon
, and
|\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}
. Define

\Omega(t\varepsilon)=\left\{x\in\Omega:\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<tc_{1}\varepsilon\right\}.

Observe that
\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon}
is supported in a thin layer
\Omega(2\varepsilon)\setminus\Omega(\varepsilon)=\{x\in\Omega:c_{1}\varepsilon\leq\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<2c_{1}\varepsilon\}
.

Another technical tool we need is the smoothing operator. Let
0\leq\zeta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{c_{1}}(0))
and
\int_{B_{c_{1}}(0)}\zeta=1
and define the standard smoothing operator by

\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(f)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varepsilon^{-d}\zeta(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon})f(y)dy.

Many properties about the above smoothing operators can be found in
[
9
, Chapter 3.1]
. We refer to the Appendix of
[
10
]
for several lemmas that will be used below.

Let

(4.3)

w_{\eta,\varepsilon}=u_{\eta,\varepsilon}-u_{\eta}-\varepsilon\chi_{\eta}^{\ell}(x/\varepsilon)\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{\ell}u_{\eta})\theta_{\varepsilon}.

Since
\widetilde{F}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
u_{\eta}\in H_{\rm loc}^{2}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume
u_{\eta}\in W^{2,d}(\Omega)
. Then for any
h\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla w_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla h\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|\nabla^{2}u_{\eta}\|_{L^{d}(\Omega)}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|\nabla u_{\eta}\|_{W^{1,d}(\Omega)}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}.

Proof.

First of all,
h\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
implies
h|_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\in V_{\eta,\varepsilon}
. Thus
h
can be used as a test function.
We calculate directly

(4.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla w_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla h

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla h-\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla u_{\eta}\cdot\nabla h-\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}(\nabla\chi_{\eta}^{\ell})_{\varepsilon}\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{\ell}u_{\eta})\theta_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla h

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}(\chi_{\eta}^{\ell})_{\varepsilon}\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\partial_{\ell}u_{\eta})\theta_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla h-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}(\chi_{\eta}^{\ell})_{\varepsilon}\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{\ell}u_{\eta})\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla h.

Using the equations (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we have

\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla h=\int_{\Omega}\overline{A}_{\eta}\nabla u_{\eta}\cdot\nabla h.

Inserting this equation into (
4.5
), we obtain

(4.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla w_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla h

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\overline{A}_{\eta}-\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}I-\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}(\nabla\chi_{\eta})_{\varepsilon})\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\eta})\theta_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla h

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}(\overline{A}_{\eta}-\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}I)\nabla u_{\eta}(1-\theta_{\varepsilon})\cdot\nabla h

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}(\overline{A}_{\eta}-\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}I)(\nabla u_{\eta}-\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\eta}))\theta_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla h

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}(\chi_{\eta}^{\ell})_{\varepsilon}\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\partial_{\ell}u_{\eta})\theta_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla h-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}(\chi_{\eta}^{\ell})_{\varepsilon}\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{\ell}u_{\eta})\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla h

\displaystyle=:\sum_{i=1}^{5}I_{i}.

We estimate
I_{k}
for
1\leq k\leq 5
.
To estimate
I_{1}
, we define the flux correctors. Let
B_{\eta}=\overline{A}_{\eta}-\phi^{2}_{\eta}I-\phi^{2}_{\eta}\nabla\chi_{\eta}
, or in component form

(b_{\eta})_{ij}=(\bar{a}_{\eta})_{ij}-\phi^{2}_{\eta}\delta_{ij}-\phi^{2}_{\eta}\partial_{i}\chi_{\eta}^{j}.

Clearly, by Proposition
2.7
,
(b_{\eta})_{ij}\in L^{2}(Y)
with obvious zero extension, and

\int_{Y}(b_{\eta})_{ij}=0,\qquad\partial_{i}(b_{\eta})_{ij}=0.

Then it is well-known (see, e.g.,
[
9
]
) that we can find the flux correctors
\Phi_{\eta}=(\Phi_{\eta})_{kij}\in H_{\rm per}^{1}(Y)
such that

(b_{\eta})_{ij}=\partial_{k}(\Phi_{\eta})_{kij},\qquad(\Phi_{\eta})_{kij}=-(\Phi_{\eta})_{ikj}.

Moreover,
(\Phi_{\eta})_{kij}\in C^{\alpha}(Y)
for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

Note that we can write

(4.7)

B_{\eta}=(\overline{A}_{\eta}-I)+I(1-\phi_{\eta}^{2})+\phi_{\eta}^{2}\nabla\chi_{\eta}.

By the smallness of
\phi_{\eta}-1,\overline{A}_{\eta}-I,\phi_{\eta}\nabla\chi_{\eta}
and
\chi_{\eta}
obtained in Lemma
2.2
(i) and Proposition
2.7
(i)(iv), and the construction of
\Phi_{\eta}
, we have

(4.8)

\|\phi_{\eta}-1\|_{L^{2^{*}}(Y_{\eta})}+|\overline{A}_{\eta}-I|+\|\phi_{\eta}\nabla\chi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\eta})}+\|\chi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\eta})}+\|\Phi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2^{*}}(Y_{\eta})}\leq C\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

Therefore, by the skew-symmetry of
\Phi_{\eta}
and a standard argument,

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{k}(\varepsilon(\Phi_{\eta,\varepsilon})_{kij})\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{j}u_{\eta})(\partial_{i}h)\theta_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}(\Phi_{\eta,\varepsilon})_{kij}\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{k}\partial_{j}u_{\eta})(\partial_{i}h)\theta_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}(\Phi_{\eta,\varepsilon})_{kij}\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{j}u_{\eta})\partial_{i}h\partial_{k}\theta_{\varepsilon}.

The first integral on the right-hand side is bounded by

(4.9)

C\varepsilon\|\Phi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y^{*})}\|\nabla^{2}u_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|\nabla^{2}u_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

The second integral is bounded by

(4.10)

C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\Phi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y^{*})}\|\nabla u_{\eta}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|\nabla u_{\eta}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}.

Consequently,

(4.11)

|I_{1}|\leq C\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|\nabla^{2}u_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|\nabla u_{\eta}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}.

To estimate
I_{2}
, we apply (
4.8
) and the Hölder’s inequality to obtain

(4.12)

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{\|\overline{A}_{\eta}-I\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}+\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}-1\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}\Big\}\|\nabla u_{\eta}\|_{L^{d}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}

\displaystyle\leq C\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2^{*}}}\cdot\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{d}}\|\nabla u_{\eta}\|_{W^{1,d}(\Omega)}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}

\displaystyle\leq C\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u_{\eta}\|_{W^{1,d}(\Omega)}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}.

We estimate
I_{3}
as follows:

(4.13)

\displaystyle|I_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{\|\overline{A}_{\eta}-I\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\Omega)}+\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}-1\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\Omega)}\Big\}\|\nabla u_{\eta}-\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\eta})\|_{L^{d}(\Omega\setminus\Omega(\varepsilon))}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|\nabla^{2}u_{\eta}\|_{L^{d}(\Omega)}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Finally, note that
I_{4}
has the same bound as (
4.9
), and
I_{5}
has the same bound as (
4.10
). Summing up all these estimates, we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

Now we are going to pick a particular test function
h
. We point out that
w_{\eta,\varepsilon}
itself, even with a cut-off on the boundary is not a legal choice for
h
since
\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}
may not lie in
L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
(we only know
\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
). We will use the harmonic extension to handle the possible singularity of
\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}
near the holes.
Let
\delta\in(0,c_{1}]
. Define

\Omega^{\delta}_{\eta,\varepsilon}:=\left\{x\in\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}:\text{dist}(x,T_{\eta,\varepsilon})>\delta\varepsilon\eta\right\}.

Note that
|\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega^{\delta}_{\eta,\varepsilon}|\leq C\delta\eta^{d}
. We shall
extend the function
u_{\eta,\varepsilon}
and
\chi_{\eta}(x/\varepsilon)
from
\Omega^{\delta}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
to
\Omega
with a suitable choice of
\delta
. We define
T^{\delta}_{\eta}=\{x\in Y:\text{dist}(x,T_{\eta})\leq\delta\eta\}
and
Y^{\delta}_{\eta}=Y\setminus T^{\delta}_{\eta}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{\delta}
be given as above. Let
u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}
be the harmonic extension of
u_{\eta,\varepsilon}
from
\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{\delta}
to
\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}
. It holds

(4.14)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla w_{\eta,\varepsilon}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\delta^{-1}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|\nabla^{2}u_{\eta}\|_{W^{1,d}(\Omega)}\big(\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}+\|\nabla u_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big)

\displaystyle+C\varepsilon\eta^{d-2}\|\nabla u_{\eta}\|_{W^{1,d}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\delta\eta^{d-2}\|\nabla u_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle+C\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{\delta}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}(|\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}|^{2}).

Proof.

Let
\chi_{\eta}^{*}=(\chi_{\eta}^{\ell*})
be the harmonic extension of
\chi_{\eta}
from
Y_{\eta}^{\delta}
to
Y
, and
\delta\in(0,1)
be a small parameter that can vary. Recall that we sometimes also use the notations
\chi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{\ell}=(\chi_{\eta}^{\ell})_{\varepsilon}=\chi_{\eta}^{\ell}(x/\varepsilon)
and
(\nabla\chi_{\eta})_{\varepsilon}=(\nabla\chi_{\eta})(x/\varepsilon)
(similar notations also apply to
\chi_{\eta}^{*}
and
\nabla\chi_{\eta}^{*}
).

Let
\theta_{\varepsilon}
be given as before. Define

w_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}=u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}-u_{\eta}-\varepsilon\chi_{\eta}^{\ell*}(x/\varepsilon)\mathscr{K}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{\ell}u_{\eta})\theta_{\varepsilon}.

It is easy to verify that
w_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Write

(4.15)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla w_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla w_{\eta,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla w_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla w_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}+\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla w_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla(w_{\eta,\varepsilon}-w_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*})

\displaystyle=J_{1}+J_{2}.

Estimate of
J_{1}
:
Since
w_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, we apply (
4.1
) to obtain

(4.16)

|J_{1}|\leq C\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|\nabla^{2}u_{\eta}\|_{L^{d}(\Omega)}\|\nabla w_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|\nabla u_{\eta}\|_{W^{1,d}(\Omega)}\|\nabla w_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}.

\displaystyle\|\nabla w_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+C(1+\delta^{-1}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}})\|\nabla u_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|\nabla^{2}u_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

As a result, we have

\displaystyle|J_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\delta^{-1}\|\nabla^{2}u_{\eta}\|_{W^{1,d}(\Omega)}\Big\{\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}+\|\nabla u_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big\}

\displaystyle+C\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\eta^{d-2}\|\nabla u_{\eta}\|_{W^{1,d}(\Omega)}^{2}.

\displaystyle|J_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla w_{\eta,\varepsilon}|^{2}+2\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla(w_{\eta,\varepsilon}-w_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*})|^{2}

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz domain. Let
f\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
for some
p>d
and
u_{\eta,\varepsilon}
a solution of (

\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{\delta}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}|^{2}\leq C\delta\big(\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}^{2}\big).

Proof.

This lemma essentially relies only on the small-scale interior Lipschitz estimate, i.e., Lemma
3.6
and Lemma
3.7
. Indeed, for
\delta<c_{0}/8
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{\delta}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{\delta}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset}\int_{\varepsilon(z+T^{\delta}_{\eta}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\Omega}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\varepsilon(z+T)\subset\Omega}C(\varepsilon\eta)^{d}\delta\sup_{\varepsilon(z+T^{\delta}_{\eta}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\Omega}|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{\delta}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset}C\delta\bigg\{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cap\varepsilon(z+Y_{*}^{+})}|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}|^{2}+(\varepsilon\eta)^{d+2-\frac{2d}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}^{2}\bigg\}

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
u_{\eta,\varepsilon}
be the same as in Lemma
4.3

u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}
the harmonic extension of

from
\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{\delta}

Proof.

First, we write

(4.24)

\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{\delta}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}|^{2}=\sum_{\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{\delta}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset}\int_{\varepsilon(z+T^{\delta}_{\eta}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\Omega}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}|^{2}.

We then consider a single cell
\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\Omega
. By Lemma
3.6
and Lemma
3.7
,

(4.25)

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\varepsilon(z+T^{*}_{\eta}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\fint_{\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+C\varepsilon\eta\bigg(\fint_{\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}

Recall that
\Delta u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}=0
in
\varepsilon(z+T^{\delta}_{\eta})\cap\Omega

on

In each hole, we can apply the

\displaystyle\|(\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*})^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\varepsilon(z+\partial T^{\delta}_{\eta})\cap\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla_{\rm tan}u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\varepsilon(z+\partial T^{\delta}_{\eta})\cap\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\varepsilon(z+\partial T^{\delta}_{\eta})\cap\Omega)}

Consequently,

(4.26)

\displaystyle\int_{\varepsilon(z+T^{\delta}_{\eta}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\Omega}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}|^{2}

\displaystyle\int_{\varepsilon(z+T^{\delta}_{\eta}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\Omega}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}|^{2}\leq C\delta\int_{\varepsilon(z+T_{\eta}^{**}\setminus T_{\eta})\cap\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}|^{2}+C\delta(\varepsilon\eta)^{d+2-\frac{2d}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}^{2}.

\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{\delta}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{*}|^{2}\leq C\delta\big(\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}^{2}+\eta^{d}(\varepsilon\eta)^{2-\frac{2d}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}^{2}\big),

5.
Convergence of eigenvalues

In this section, we will quantify the convergence rates from
\mu^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
to
\mu^{k}_{\eta}
. These convergence rates, though not optimal, allows us to show the Weyl’s law and find the common large spectral gaps. Our main tool is the minimax principle both in (

5.1.
Optimal upper bound

The optimal upper bound is obtained directly by (
2.27
) without using the convergence rates in the previous section.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{2}
domain satisfying the geometric assumption
A
. Then, for
k\geq 1
,

(5.1)

\mu^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\leq\mu^{k}_{\eta}+C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

Proof.

Fix
k\geq 1
. We may assume that
\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}
is sufficiently small.
We first construct a subspace
S^{k}_{\rm app}
of
H^{1}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon},0}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
with dimension
k
. Actually, for
1\leq j\leq k
, let

v^{j}_{\eta,\varepsilon}=\rho^{j}_{\eta}+\varepsilon\chi^{\ell}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\partial_{\ell}\rho_{\eta}^{j})\theta_{\varepsilon},

where we simply restrict the function
\rho^{j}_{\eta}
in
\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}
. The cutoff function
\theta_{\varepsilon}
(the same as in Section
4
) is used here to make sure that
v^{j}_{\eta,\varepsilon}=0
on
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
. The functions
v^{j}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
are supposed to be good approximations of the eigenfunctions
\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}
of the degenerate eigenvalue problem in
V_{\eta,\varepsilon}
. Recall that the regularity of the homogenized eigenvalue problem in
C^{2}
domains implies

(5.2)

\|\rho_{\eta}^{j}\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{j,p},

for any
p<\infty
.

Let
S^{k}_{\rm app}=\text{span}\{v^{j}_{\eta,\varepsilon}:1\leq j\leq k\}
.

Claim:
\text{dim}\ S^{k}_{\rm app}=k
if
\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\leq c_{k}
for sufficiently small
c_{k}
depending on
k
. In fact, we can use the orthogonality of
\rho_{\eta}^{k}
in the space
L^{2}(\Omega)
to show that for
\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}
small enough (depending on
k
or
\mu^{k}_{\eta}
)

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}v^{j}_{\eta,\varepsilon}=0\text{ if and only if }\alpha_{j}=0\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq k.

To show this, consider a general
v=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}v^{j}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\in S^{k}_{\rm app}
. We compute

(5.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq k}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\rho^{i}_{\eta}\rho^{j}_{\eta}+2\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq k}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\rho^{i}_{\eta}\varepsilon\chi^{\ell}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\partial_{\ell}\rho_{\eta}^{j})\theta_{\varepsilon}.

For each pair of
i,j
,

(5.4)

\bigg|\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\rho^{i}_{\eta}\varepsilon\chi^{\ell}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\partial_{\ell}\rho_{\eta}^{j})\theta_{\varepsilon}\bigg|\leq C\varepsilon\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\chi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\|\rho_{\eta}^{i}\|_{L^{d}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\rho_{\eta}^{j}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

and

(5.5)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\chi^{\ell}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\partial_{\ell}\rho_{\eta}^{i})\chi^{\tau}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\partial_{\tau}\rho_{\eta}^{j})\theta_{\varepsilon}^{2}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\chi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}^{2}\|\nabla\rho_{\eta}^{i}\|_{L^{d}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\rho_{\eta}^{j}\|_{L^{d}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}\varepsilon^{2}\eta^{d-2}.

Hence, (
5.3
) gives

(5.6)

\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq k}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\rho^{i}_{\eta}\rho^{j}_{\eta}+O_{k}(\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}})\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq k}|\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}|.

Next, using the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
4.7
, by (
4.33
) and (
4.34
), we have

(5.7)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\rho^{i}_{\eta}\rho^{j}_{\eta}-\int_{\Omega}\rho^{i}_{\eta}\rho^{j}_{\eta}\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon\Psi(x/\varepsilon))\rho^{i}_{\eta}\rho^{j}_{\eta}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\|\Psi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\Omega)}\|\rho_{\eta}^{i}\|_{W^{1,d}(\Omega)}\|\rho_{\eta}^{j}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

Hence, by the orthogonality
\int_{\Omega}\rho^{i}_{\eta}\rho^{j}_{\eta}=\delta_{ij}
,

(5.8)

\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}=\sum_{1\leq j\leq k}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}+O_{k}(\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}})\sum_{1\leq j\leq k}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}.

Note that the constant in
O_{k}(\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}})
depends on
k
. Then if
\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}<c_{k}
for some constant
c_{k}>0
, then
v=0
if and only if
\alpha_{j}=0
for all
1\leq j\leq k
. This finishes the proof of the claim.

Now, by the minimax principle (
2.27
),

(5.9)

\mu^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\leq\max_{v\in S^{k}_{\rm app}}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}}{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}}.

Consider a general
v=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}v^{j}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\in S^{k}_{\rm app}
.
Without loss of generality, assume
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}^{2}=1
. If
\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}<c_{k}
, (
5.8
) implies

(5.10)

\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}=1+O_{k}(\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}).

Next, we estimate the upper bound of the numerator of (
5.9
). In view of (
2.31
), we perform the following calculation

(5.11)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq k}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi^{2}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\partial_{\ell}\rho^{i}_{\eta}+(\partial_{\ell}\chi_{\eta})_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\rho^{i}_{\eta}\theta_{\varepsilon})\cdot(\partial_{\ell}\rho^{j}_{\eta}+(\partial_{\ell}\chi_{\eta})_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\rho^{j}_{\eta}\theta_{\varepsilon})+O_{k}(\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq k}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi^{2}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\delta_{m\ell}+(\partial_{\ell}\chi^{m}_{\eta})_{\varepsilon})(\delta_{n\ell}+(\partial_{\ell}\chi^{n}_{\eta})_{\varepsilon})\partial_{m}\rho^{i}_{\eta}\partial_{n}\rho^{j}_{\eta}\theta_{\varepsilon}^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq k}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi^{2}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\partial_{\ell}\rho_{\eta}^{i}\partial_{\ell}\rho_{\eta}^{j}(1-\theta_{\varepsilon}^{2})+O_{k}(\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}).

The error terms contained in
O_{k}(\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}})
above are estimated by a familiar argument, including the boundary layer estimate
\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}
(see
[
10
, Lemma A.5]
) whenever
\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon}
or
1-\theta_{\varepsilon}
involves.

To proceed, we first notice that

(5.12)

\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}I-\overline{A}_{\eta}\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}\leq\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}I-I\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}+\|I-\overline{A}_{\eta}\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2^{*}}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

Hence, for each pair of
i,j
, we estimate the last integral of (
5.11
) as

(5.13)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi^{2}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\partial_{\ell}\rho_{\eta}^{i}\partial_{\ell}\rho_{\eta}^{j}(1-\theta_{\varepsilon}^{2})-\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\bar{a}_{\eta,mn}\partial_{m}\rho_{\eta}^{i}\partial_{n}\rho_{\eta}^{j}(1-\theta_{\varepsilon}^{2})\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}I-\overline{A}_{\eta}\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}\|\nabla\rho_{\eta}^{i}\|_{L^{d}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}\|\nabla\rho_{\eta}^{j}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2^{*}}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}C_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{d}}C_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

Now consider the following periodic equation,

(5.14)

-\Delta\Xi_{mn}=\bar{a}_{\eta,mn}-\phi^{2}_{\eta}(\delta_{m\ell}+(\partial_{\ell}\chi^{m}_{\eta}))(\delta_{n\ell}+(\partial_{\ell}\chi^{n}_{\eta})),\quad\text{in }Y.

The equation is solvable since the right-hand side has mean value zero due to (
2.31
). We would like to show

(5.15)

\|\nabla\Xi_{mn}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\leq C\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

To see this, we write

(5.16)

\displaystyle\bar{a}_{\eta,mn}-\phi^{2}_{\eta}(\delta_{m\ell}+(\partial_{\ell}\chi^{m}_{\eta}))(\delta_{n\ell}+(\partial_{\ell}\chi^{n}_{\eta}))

\displaystyle=\bar{a}_{\eta,mn}-\delta_{mn}+\delta_{mn}(1-\phi_{\eta}^{2})-\phi_{\eta}^{2}(\partial_{m}\chi_{\eta}^{n}+\partial_{n}\chi_{\eta}^{m})-\phi_{\eta}^{2}\partial_{\ell}\chi_{\eta}^{m}\partial_{\ell}\chi_{\eta}^{n}.

Moreover, using the equation (
2.30
), we have

(5.17)

\displaystyle\phi_{\eta}^{2}\partial_{\ell}\chi_{\eta}^{m}\partial_{\ell}\chi_{\eta}^{n}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\ell}(\phi_{\eta}^{2}\chi_{\eta}^{m}\partial_{\ell}\chi_{\eta}^{n})+\chi_{\eta}^{m}\partial_{\ell}(\phi_{\eta}^{2}\partial_{\ell}\chi_{\eta}^{n})

\displaystyle=\partial_{\ell}(\phi_{\eta}^{2}\chi_{\eta}^{m}\partial_{\ell}\chi_{\eta}^{n})+2\chi_{\eta}^{m}\phi_{\eta}\partial_{n}\phi_{\eta}.

Combining (
5.14
), (
5.16
) and (
5.17
), we have

(5.18)

\displaystyle\|\Delta\Xi\|_{H^{-1}_{\rm per}(Y)}

\displaystyle\leq\|A_{\eta}-I\|_{L^{2}(Y)}+2\|1-\phi_{\eta}^{2}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}+2\|\phi_{\eta}^{2}\nabla\chi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}

\displaystyle+\|\phi_{\eta}^{2}\chi_{\eta}\nabla\chi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}+2\|\chi_{\eta}\phi_{\eta}\nabla\phi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\leq C\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

This implies (
5.15
).

Hence,

(5.19)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi^{2}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\delta_{m\ell}+(\partial_{\ell}\chi^{m}_{\eta})_{\varepsilon})(\delta_{n\ell}+(\partial_{\ell}\chi^{n}_{\eta})_{\varepsilon})\partial_{m}\rho^{i}_{\eta}\partial_{n}\rho^{j}_{\eta}\theta_{\varepsilon}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}\bar{a}_{\eta,mn}\partial_{m}\rho^{i}_{\eta}\partial_{n}\rho^{j}_{\eta}\theta_{\varepsilon}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon(\nabla\Xi_{mn})_{\varepsilon})\partial_{m}\rho^{i}_{\eta}\partial_{n}\rho^{j}_{\eta}\theta_{\varepsilon}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\Xi_{mn})_{\varepsilon}\nabla(\partial_{m}\rho^{i}_{\eta}\partial_{n}\rho^{j}_{\eta})\theta_{\varepsilon}^{2}-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\Xi_{mn})_{\varepsilon}\partial_{m}\rho^{i}_{\eta}\partial_{n}\rho^{j}_{\eta}2\theta_{\varepsilon}\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon}.

The first integral is bounded by

(5.20)

C\varepsilon\|(\nabla\Xi)_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\rho_{\eta}^{j}\|_{W^{1,4}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\rho_{\eta}^{i}\|_{W^{1,4}(\Omega)}\leq C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

The second integral is bounded by

(5.21)

\|(\nabla\Xi)_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}\|\nabla\rho_{\eta}^{j}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}\|\nabla\rho_{\eta}^{i}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega(2\varepsilon))}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}C_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}C_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\leq C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

It follows from (
5.13
) and (
5.19
)-(
5.21
) that

(5.22)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq k}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\int_{\Omega}\bar{a}_{\eta,mn}\partial_{m}\rho^{i}_{\eta}\partial_{n}\rho^{j}_{\eta,\varepsilon}+O_{k}(\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\rho^{i}_{\eta}\cdot\overline{A}_{\eta}\nabla\rho^{j}_{\eta}+O_{k}(\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}^{2}\mu^{i}_{\eta}+O_{k}(\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}})

\displaystyle\leq\mu^{k}_{\eta}+C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}},

where we have used the orthogonality

(5.23)

\int_{\Omega}\nabla\rho^{i}_{\eta}\cdot\overline{A}_{\eta}\nabla\rho^{j}_{\eta}=\mu_{\eta}^{i}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\eta}^{i}\rho_{\eta}^{j}=\mu_{\eta}^{i}\delta_{ij}.

Finally, combining (
5.9
), (
5.10
) and (
5.22
), we obtain the desired upper bound for
\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}
.
∎

5.2.
Suboptimal lower bound

We will use both the “maximin principle” and “minimax principle” to show a lower bound. The quantitative convergence rates in Theorem
4.6
and Theorem
4.7
will be crucial.

Recall the operators
\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon},\widetilde{\mathscr{T}}_{\eta}
and
\mathscr{T}_{\eta}
defined in Section
2.5
.
Recall that
\mathscr{T}_{\eta}
is a compact self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
and the maximin principle for the eigenvalue problem (
2.42
) is given by (see (
2.28
))

\frac{1}{\mu^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}}=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})\\
\text{dim}S=k\end{subarray}}\min_{v\in S}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(v)\cdot v}{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}}.

In fact, this maximum is attained if we pick
S=S^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}=\text{span}\{\rho^{j}_{\eta,\varepsilon}:1\leq j\leq k\}
. Therefore,

(5.24)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}}

\displaystyle=\min_{v\in S^{k}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(v)\cdot v}{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{v\in S^{k}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}(\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(v)-\widetilde{\mathscr{T}}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(v))\cdot v}{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}}+\min_{v\in S^{k}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\widetilde{\mathscr{T}}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(v)\cdot v}{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{v\in S^{k}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}(\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(v)-\widetilde{\mathscr{T}}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(v))\cdot v}{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}}+\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})\\
\text{dim}S=k\end{subarray}}\min_{v\in S}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\widetilde{\mathscr{T}}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(v)\cdot v}{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}}.

The numerator of the first term can be handled by Theorem
4.6
. Precisely, if
v=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}\in S^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
, then by the triangle inequality in
L^{\infty}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
, Proposition
3.5
and the orthogonality of
\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}
’s in
L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
, we have

(5.25)

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{1\leq j\leq k}|\alpha_{j}|\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{1\leq j\leq k}|\alpha_{j}|C_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{k}C_{k}\Big(\sum_{1\leq j\leq k}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{k}C_{k}\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}.

Consequenctly, Theorem
4.6
with
p=\infty
implies

(5.26)

\displaystyle\bigg|\max_{v\in S^{k}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}(\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(v)-\widetilde{\mathscr{T}}_{\eta}(v))\cdot v}{\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{v\in S^{k}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(v)-\widetilde{\mathscr{T}}_{\eta}(v))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}}{\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}^{2}}\leq C_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{8}}.

Next, we handle the second term on the right-hand side of (
5.24
). The key observation is that the second term of (
5.24
) is exactly the maximin principle for the intermediate eigenvalue problem (
2.43
) since
\widetilde{\mathscr{T}}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
is a compact self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
. Thus, it is equal to
1/\tilde{\mu}^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
, where
\tilde{\mu}^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
is the
k
th eigenvalue of the intermediate eigenvalue problem (
2.43
).

On the other hand, we can employ the minimax principle of (
2.43
) to get

\tilde{\mu}^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\\
\text{dim}S=k\end{subarray}}\max_{v\in S}\frac{\int_{\Omega}\overline{A}\nabla v\cdot\nabla v}{\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}}.

Proposition 5.2
.

For
\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}<c_{k}
with sufficiently small
c_{k}
depending on
k
,
|\tilde{\mu}^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}-\mu^{k}_{\eta}|\leq C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}
.

Proof.

We only show
\mu^{k}_{\eta}\leq\tilde{\mu}^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}+C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}
(which is sufficient to get the lower bound). The other direction is similar. Recall that
\tilde{\rho}^{j}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
is the
j
th eigenfunction of (
2.43
) corresponding to
\tilde{\mu}^{j}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
. By the standard normalization and orthogonality in (
2.51
), we have
\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\tilde{\rho}^{j}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
and

(5.27)

\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\tilde{\rho}^{i}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\tilde{\rho}^{j}_{\eta,\varepsilon}=\delta_{ij},\qquad\int_{\Omega}\overline{A}\nabla\tilde{\rho}^{i}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\tilde{\rho}^{j}_{\eta,\varepsilon}=\tilde{\mu}^{i}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\delta_{ij}.

Let
\widetilde{S}^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}=\text{span}\{\tilde{\rho}^{j}_{\eta,\varepsilon}:1\leq j\leq k\}.

Then (
5.27
) implies that
\dim\widetilde{S}^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}=k
by viewing
\widetilde{S}^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
as a subspace of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.
Hence,

(5.28)

\displaystyle\mu^{k}_{\eta}=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\\
\text{dim}S=k\end{subarray}}\max_{v\in S}\frac{\int_{\Omega}\overline{A}\nabla v\cdot\nabla v}{\int_{\Omega}v^{2}}\leq\max_{v\in\widetilde{S}^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\frac{\int_{\Omega}\overline{A}\nabla v\cdot\nabla v}{\int_{\Omega}v^{2}}.

Now consider a general
v\in\widetilde{S}^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
given by
v=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}\tilde{\rho}^{j}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
with
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}^{2}=1
. By (
5.27
), we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}=1,\qquad\int_{\Omega}\overline{A}\nabla v\cdot\nabla v=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}^{2}\tilde{\mu}^{j}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\leq\tilde{\mu}^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}.

Note that the elliptic regularity estimates (with a bootstrap argument) for the equation (
2.43
) imply
\tilde{\rho}^{j}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
for any
p<\infty
. Thus, similar to (
5.25
), one has

(5.29)

\|v\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq\sum_{1\leq j\leq k}|\alpha_{j}|\|\tilde{\rho}^{j}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq\sqrt{k}C_{k}\Big(\sum_{1\leq j\leq k}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leq C_{k}\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

We now claim

(5.30)

\bigg|\int_{\Omega}v^{2}-\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}\bigg|\leq C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}.

In fact, by (
4.33
), (
4.34
) and (
5.29
), an integration by parts give

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\Omega}v^{2}-\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}\bigg|

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\bigg|\int_{\Omega}\Psi_{\varepsilon}\cdot 2v\nabla v\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\|\Psi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{d}(\Omega)}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}.

This proves (
5.30
), which yields

\int_{\Omega}v^{2}\geq(1-C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}})\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}.

By letting
\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\leq(2C_{k})^{-1}
, the above estimate and (
5.28
) gives

\mu^{k}_{\eta}\leq\frac{1}{1-C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}}\max_{v\in\widetilde{S}^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\frac{\int_{\Omega}\overline{A}\nabla v\cdot\nabla v}{\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}v^{2}}=\frac{\tilde{\mu}^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}}{1-C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}}\leq\tilde{\mu}^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}+C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

This proves the desired result.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{2}
domain satisfying the geometric assumption
A
. Then for
k\geq 1
,

\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}\geq\mu_{\eta}^{k}-C_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{8}}.

Proof.

By (
5.24
), (
5.26
) and Proposition
5.2
,

\frac{1}{\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}}\leq C_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{8}}+\frac{1}{\tilde{\mu}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}}\leq C_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{8}}+\frac{1}{\mu_{\eta}^{k}}+\frac{|\tilde{\mu}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}-\mu_{\eta}^{k}|}{\mu_{\eta}^{k}\tilde{\mu}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}}\leq C_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{8}}+\frac{1}{\mu_{\eta}^{k}}.

Hence,

\mu_{\eta}^{k}-\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}\leq C_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{8}}\mu_{\eta}^{k}\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}\leq C_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{8}},

as desired.
∎

5.3.
Weyl’s law

Throughout the subsection, we will fix
k\geq 1
and allow
C_{k}
depending on
k
.
The convergence rates between the three eigenvalue problems (
2.42
)-(
2.44
) can be summarized as follows (Proposition
5.1
-
5.3
):

(5.31)

-C_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{8}}\leq\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}-\tilde{\mu}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}\leq C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad|\tilde{\mu}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}-\mu_{\eta}^{k}|\leq C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

We first recall the classical Weyl’s law for the homogenized operator in a bounded domain without holes.

Lemma 5.4

(Hermann Weyl, 1911)
.

There exist
C_{2}>C_{1}>0
such that for any
j\geq 1
, we have

C_{1}j^{\frac{2}{d}}\leq\mu_{\eta}^{j}\leq C_{2}j^{\frac{2}{d}}.

In the above lemma,
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are universal constants. The convergence rates in (
5.31
) and the Weyl’s law for the homogenized problem give the distributions of the first
k
eigenvalues for the degenerate problem and intermediate problem,
when
\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}
is sufficiently small. In particular, we have the following crucial lemma.

Lemma 5.5

(Existence of large common spectral gaps)
.

Fix
k\geq 1
. There exist
C_{k}>0
(depending on
k
) and a universal constant
M>0
such that whenever
\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}<C_{k}^{-1}
, there exists
N_{1}=N_{1}(k,\varepsilon,\eta)\in[k,Mk)
such that

(5.32)

\min\{\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{N_{1}+1},\tilde{\mu}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{N_{1}+1},\mu_{\eta}^{N_{1}+1}\}-\max\{\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{N_{1}},\tilde{\mu}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{N_{1}},\mu_{\eta}^{N_{1}}\}\geq H_{k}\approx k^{\frac{2-d}{d}},

and

(5.33)

\min\Big\{\frac{1}{\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{N_{1}}},\frac{1}{\tilde{\mu}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{N_{1}}},\frac{1}{\mu_{\eta}^{N_{1}}}\Big\}-\max\Big\{\frac{1}{\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{N_{1}+1}},\frac{1}{\tilde{\mu}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{N_{1}+1}},\frac{1}{\mu_{\eta}^{N_{1}+1}}\Big\}\geq G_{k}\approx k^{\frac{-2-d}{d}}.

Proof.

Let
M
be a universal constant such that
C_{1}M^{\frac{2}{d}}>2C_{2}
, where
C_{1},C_{2}
are given in Lemma
5.4
. The Wyel’s law for
\mu_{\eta}^{j}
tells us that
C_{1}k^{\frac{2}{d}}\leq\mu_{\eta}^{k}\leq C_{2}k^{\frac{2}{d}}
and
C_{1}(Mk)^{\frac{2}{d}}\leq\mu_{\eta}^{Mk}\leq C_{2}(Mk)^{\frac{2}{d}}
. Thus,

\mu_{\eta}^{Mk}-\mu_{\eta}^{k}\geq(C_{1}M^{\frac{2}{d}}-C_{2})k^{\frac{2}{d}}>C_{2}k^{\frac{2}{d}},

by our choice of
M
. By the pigeonhole principle, there exists
N_{1}=N_{1}(k,\varepsilon,\eta)\in[k,Mk)
such that

\mu_{\eta}^{N_{1}+1}-\mu_{\eta}^{N_{1}}\geq\frac{C_{2}k^{\frac{2}{d}}}{(M-1)k}=C_{3}k^{\frac{2}{d}-1}.

As a result, we have found a large spectral gap between two successive eigenvalues (counted with multiplicity) for the homogenized problem.
By the convergence rates of eigenvalues (
5.31
), if
\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}<C_{k}^{-1}
for sufficiently large
C_{k}
depending on
k
, we have (
5.32
) with
H_{k}=\frac{1}{2}C_{3}k^{\frac{2}{d}-1}
.

Using Lemma
5.4
again, we have

\frac{1}{\mu_{\eta}^{N_{1}}}-\frac{1}{\mu_{\eta}^{N_{1}+1}}=\frac{\mu_{\eta}^{N_{1}+1}-\mu_{\eta}^{N_{1}}}{\mu_{\eta}^{N_{1}+1}\mu_{\eta}^{N_{1}}}\geq\frac{C_{3}k^{\frac{2}{d}-1}}{C_{1}^{2}(N_{1}+1)^{\frac{4}{d}}}=C_{4}k^{-\frac{2}{d}-1}.

Again, by the convergence rates, for
\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}<C_{k}^{-1}
, we obtain (
5.33
) with
G_{k}=\frac{1}{2}C_{4}k^{-\frac{2}{d}-1}
.
∎

6.
Optimal convergence rates

For convenience in this section, we will use the notations,

\langle f,g\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}=\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}fg\quad\text{and}\quad\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{\Omega}fg,

to represent the inner products in
L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega)
and
L^{2}(\Omega)
, respectively.

6.1.
First-order approximation by higher regularity and duality

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose that
\Omega
is a bounded
C^{3}
domain and
\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}
satisfies the geometric assumption
A
. Then for any
f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
with some
p>d
,

(6.1)

\|\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}f-\mathscr{T}_{\eta}f\|_{L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

Let
u_{\eta,\varepsilon}=\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}f
and
u_{\eta}=\mathscr{T}_{\eta}f
. Then
u_{\eta,\varepsilon}\in V_{\eta,\varepsilon}
satisfies
\mathcal{L}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(u_{\eta,\varepsilon})=\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}f
in
\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}
and
u_{\eta}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfies
\mathcal{L}_{\eta}(u_{\eta})=f
in
\Omega
. Since
f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
\Omega
is a bounded
C^{3}
domain, then
u_{\eta}\in W^{3,p}(\Omega)
.

We redefine

(6.2)

w_{\eta,\varepsilon}=u_{\eta,\varepsilon}-u_{\eta}-\varepsilon\chi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla u_{\eta}+v_{\eta,\varepsilon},

where
v_{\eta,\varepsilon}
is the solution of

(6.3)

\mathcal{L}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(v_{\eta,\varepsilon})=0\text{ in }\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}\quad\text{and}\quad v_{\eta,\varepsilon}=\varepsilon\chi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla u_{\eta}\text{ on }\Gamma_{\eta,\varepsilon}.

It is not hard to see that
v_{\eta,\varepsilon}\in H^{1}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
. Thus we have
w_{\eta,\varepsilon}\in V_{\eta,\varepsilon}
since
w_{\eta,\varepsilon}=0
on
\Gamma_{\eta,\varepsilon}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
6.1
, it holds

(6.4)

\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}w_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}+\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

This lemma should be compared with Theorem
4.5
. In Theorem
4.5
, we only require
f\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
for some
p>d
; but the convergence rate is worse. While in the above lemma, we require
f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
with
p>d
and have the optimal convergence rate. This improvement, though with additional boundary layers involved, will help us to prove the optimal convergence rates of eigenvalues.

Proof of Lemma
6.2
.

The proof of this lemma is inspired by
[
6
]
and different from Lemma
4.1
.
We first perform a straightforward calculation as follows

(6.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(w_{\eta,\varepsilon})=\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}f+\partial_{j}(\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\partial_{j}u_{\eta})+\partial_{j}(\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}(\partial_{j}\chi^{\ell}_{\eta})_{\varepsilon}\partial_{\ell}u_{\eta})+\varepsilon\partial_{j}(\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\chi^{\ell}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\partial_{j}\partial_{\ell}u_{\eta}).

Using the equation for
\chi_{\eta}
(
2.30
), we have

(6.6)

\partial_{j}(\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\partial_{j}u_{\eta})+\partial_{j}(\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}(\partial_{j}\chi^{\ell}_{\eta})_{\varepsilon}\partial_{\ell}u_{\eta})=\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\partial_{j}^{2}u_{\eta}+\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}(\partial_{j}\chi^{\ell}_{\eta})_{\varepsilon}\partial_{j}\partial_{\ell}u_{\eta}.

Thus, by this identity and the equation
\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}f=-\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}(\bar{a}_{\eta})_{j\ell}\partial_{j}\partial_{\ell}u_{\eta}
, we obtain from (
6.5
) that

(6.7)

\mathcal{L}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(w_{\eta,\varepsilon})=\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}(-(\bar{a}_{\eta})_{j\ell}+\delta_{j\ell}+(\partial_{j}\chi^{\ell}_{\eta})_{\varepsilon})\partial_{j}\partial_{\ell}u_{\eta}+\varepsilon\partial_{j}(\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\chi^{\ell}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\partial_{j}\partial_{\ell}u_{\eta}).

Now we introduce new flux correctors
\Theta_{\eta}^{j\ell}\in H^{1}_{\phi_{\eta},{\rm per}}(Y_{\eta})
satisfying the degenerate cell problem

(6.8)

\nabla\cdot(\phi_{\eta}^{2}\nabla\Theta_{\eta}^{j\ell})=\phi_{\eta}^{2}(-(\bar{a}_{\eta})_{j\ell}+\delta_{j\ell}+(\partial_{j}\chi^{\ell}_{\eta})),\quad\text{in }Y_{\eta}.

Note that the above equation is solvable since the right-hand side has mean value zero in
Y
. Moreover, the energy estimate implies

(6.9)

\|\phi_{\eta}\nabla\Theta_{\eta}^{j\ell}\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\eta})}\leq C\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

As a result of (
6.7
) and (
6.8
),

(6.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(w_{\eta,\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}(\nabla\Theta^{j\ell}_{\eta})_{\varepsilon})\partial_{j}\partial_{\ell}u_{\eta}+\varepsilon\partial_{j}(\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\chi^{\ell}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\partial_{j}\partial_{\ell}u_{\eta})

\displaystyle=\partial_{i}(\varepsilon\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}(\partial_{i}\Theta^{j\ell}_{\eta})_{\varepsilon}\partial_{j}\partial_{\ell}u_{\eta})-\varepsilon\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}(\partial_{i}\Theta^{j\ell}_{\eta})_{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\partial_{\ell}u_{\eta}+\varepsilon\partial_{j}(\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\chi^{\ell}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\partial_{j}\partial_{\ell}u_{\eta}).

Integrating this equation against
w_{\eta,\varepsilon}
, we have

(6.11)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}|\nabla w_{\eta,\varepsilon}|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\nabla\Theta_{\eta}^{j\ell})_{\varepsilon}\partial_{j}\partial_{\ell}u_{\eta}\cdot\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\eta,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\nabla\Theta_{\eta}^{j\ell})_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\partial_{j}\partial_{\ell}u_{\eta}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}w_{\eta,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\chi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{\ell}\nabla\partial_{\ell}u_{\eta}\cdot\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\eta,\varepsilon}.

By the Hölder’s inequality and (
6.9
), the first integral is bounded by

(6.12)

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\nabla\Theta_{\eta}^{j\ell})_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\|\nabla^{2}u_{\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|\nabla^{2}u_{\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}.

The third integral of (
6.11
) has the same bound as (
6.12
). By Theorem
3.3
, the second integral of (
6.11
) is bounded by

(6.13)

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\nabla\Theta_{\eta}^{j\ell})_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\|\nabla^{3}u_{\eta}\|_{L^{d}(\Omega)}\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}w_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|\nabla^{3}u_{\eta}\|_{L^{d}(\Omega)}\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}.

Combining these estimates together with the Sobolev embedding thoerem, we arrive at

(6.14)

\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|u_{\eta}\|_{W^{3,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)},

for
p>d
. The proof is complete by Theorem
3.3
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

In view of Lemma
6.2
, it suffices to show

(6.15)

\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\varepsilon\chi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla u_{\eta}-v_{\eta,\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

Clearly,

(6.16)

\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\varepsilon\chi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla u_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\chi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\|\nabla u_{\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

To estimate
v_{\eta,\varepsilon}
, we consider

(6.17)

\widetilde{v}_{\eta,\varepsilon}=v_{\eta,\varepsilon}-\varepsilon\chi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla u_{\eta}\zeta_{\varepsilon},

where
\zeta_{\varepsilon}=1-\theta_{\varepsilon}
and
\theta_{\varepsilon}
is given as in Section
4
. Thus
\zeta_{\varepsilon}=1
on
\Gamma_{\eta,\varepsilon}
and
\zeta_{\varepsilon}
is supported in
\Omega(2\varepsilon)
and
|\nabla\zeta_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}
.
Then
\widetilde{v}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\in V_{\eta,\varepsilon}
and

(6.18)

\mathcal{L}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\widetilde{v}_{\eta,\varepsilon})=-\nabla\cdot(\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla(\varepsilon\chi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla u_{\eta}\zeta_{\varepsilon})).

By the energy estimate, we have

(6.19)

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla\widetilde{v}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla(\varepsilon\chi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla u_{\eta}\zeta_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\nabla\chi_{\eta})_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla u_{\eta}\zeta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}+C\varepsilon\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\chi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla^{2}u_{\eta}\zeta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle+C\varepsilon\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\chi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla u_{\eta}\nabla\zeta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u_{\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\phi_{\eta}\nabla\chi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\eta})}+C\varepsilon\|\nabla^{2}u_{\eta}\|_{L^{d}(\Omega)}\|\phi_{\eta}\chi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2^{*}}(Y_{\eta})}

\displaystyle+C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u_{\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\phi_{\eta}\chi_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}(Y_{\eta})}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

The weighted Poincaré inequality (
3.11
) then implies

(6.20)

\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\widetilde{v}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla\widetilde{v}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

It follows by (
6.17
) that

(6.21)

\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}v_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}+\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla v_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

This and (
6.16
) lead to (
6.15
).
∎

The above estimate shows that the boundary layer term
v_{\eta,\varepsilon}
causes the suboptimal rate of convergence. We can improve the convergence rate if we can improve the estimate of
v_{\eta,\varepsilon}
. One way used by
[
6
]
is the maximum principle, which leads to a rate of
O(\varepsilon)
and the factor
\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}
is lost (unless the holes do not touch the boundary
\partial\Omega
). In our case, we can only show an optimal convergence rate in a very weak sense by duality. Surprisingly, this weak convergence is sufficient for the optimal convergence rates of eigenvalues.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
v_{\eta,\varepsilon}
be given as above. Let
g\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
for some
p>d
. Then

(6.22)

|\langle v_{\eta,\varepsilon},g\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|\leq C\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\|g\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{v}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
be given by (
6.17
). Due to (
6.16
), it suffices to prove (
6.22
) for
\widetilde{v}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
in place of
v_{\eta,\varepsilon}
. Let
h_{\eta,\varepsilon}=\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(g)
and
h_{\eta}=\mathscr{T}_{\eta}(g)
. It follows from Lemma
6.2
and (
6.21
) that

(6.23)

\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\nabla(h_{\eta,\varepsilon}-h_{\eta}-\varepsilon\chi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla h_{\eta})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|g\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}

Now, observe that

(6.24)

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{v}_{\eta,\varepsilon},g\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla\widetilde{v}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla h_{\eta,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla\widetilde{v}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla(h_{\eta,\varepsilon}-h_{\eta}-\varepsilon\chi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla h_{\eta})

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla\widetilde{v}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla h_{\eta}+\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla\widetilde{v}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla(\varepsilon\chi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla h_{\eta})

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

By (
6.19
) and (
6.23
), we have

(6.25)

|I_{1}|\leq C\varepsilon\eta^{d-2}\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\|g\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

To estimate
I_{2}
, we use the variational equation of (
6.18
) to get

(6.26)

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla(\varepsilon\chi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla u_{\eta}\zeta_{\varepsilon})\cdot\nabla h_{\eta}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}(\nabla\chi_{\eta}^{\ell})_{\varepsilon}\partial_{\ell}u_{\eta}\zeta_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla h_{\eta}+\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\varepsilon\chi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla^{2}u_{\eta}\zeta_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla h_{\eta}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\varepsilon\chi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla u_{\eta}\nabla\zeta_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla h_{\eta}

\displaystyle=I_{2,1}+I_{2,2}+I_{2,3}.

Note that
\zeta_{\varepsilon}
is supported in
\Omega(2\varepsilon)
. Thus

(6.27)

\displaystyle|I_{2,1}|

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\nabla\chi_{\eta}^{\ell})_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cap\Omega(2\varepsilon))}C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u_{\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\nabla h_{\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|\nabla u_{\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\nabla h_{\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

The estimates for
I_{2,2}
and
I_{2,3}
are similar. Thus,

(6.28)

|I_{2}|\leq C\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\|g\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

By the same argument, we can estimate
I_{3}
as

(6.29)

|I_{3}|\leq C\varepsilon\eta^{d-2}\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\|g\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

Combining the estimates of
I_{1}
-
I_{3}
, we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

Theorem 6.4
.

Suppose that
\Omega
is a bounded
C^{3}
domain and
\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}
satisfies the geometric assumption
A
. Then for any
f,g\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
,

(6.30)

|\langle\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}f-\mathscr{T}_{\eta}f,g\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|\leq C\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\|g\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

This follows readily from Lemma
6.2
and Lemma

.

6.2.
Almost orthogonality

In this subsection, we show that the eigenfunctions (of different eigenvalue problems listed in (
2.42
)-(
2.44
)) are almost orthogonal if their corresponding eigenvalues are not close to each other.

Lemma 6.5

(Suboptimal almost orthogonality)
.

For any
1\leq j,\ell\leq k
,

(6.31)

|\langle\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j},\rho_{\eta}^{\ell}\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|\leq\frac{C_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}\mu_{\eta}^{\ell}}{|\mu_{\eta}^{\ell}-\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}|}.

Proof.

Since
\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
is self-adjoint in
L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
, we have

(6.32)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j})^{-1}\langle\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j},\rho_{\eta}^{\ell}\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}&=\langle\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}),\rho_{\eta}^{\ell}\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\\
&=\langle\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j},\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\rho_{\eta}^{\ell})\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\\
&=\langle\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j},\mathscr{T}_{\eta}(\rho_{\eta}^{\ell})\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}+\langle\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j},(\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}-\mathscr{T}_{\eta})(\rho_{\eta}^{\ell})\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\\
&=(\mu_{\eta}^{\ell})^{-1}\langle\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j},\rho_{\eta}^{\ell}\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}+\langle\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j},(\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}-\mathscr{T}_{\eta})(\rho_{\eta}^{\ell})\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}.\end{aligned}

Thus,

(6.33)

((\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j})^{-1}-(\mu_{\eta}^{\ell})^{-1})\langle\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j},\rho_{\eta}^{\ell}\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}=\langle\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j},(\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}-\mathscr{T}_{\eta})(\rho_{\eta}^{\ell})\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}.

Using the regularity of
\rho_{\eta}^{\ell}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, and Theorem
6.1
, we obtain

|\langle\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j},(\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}-\mathscr{T}_{\eta})(\rho_{\eta}^{\ell})\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|\leq C_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

This and (
6.33
) imply the desired estimate.
∎

To improve the error of almost orthogonality, we need a suboptimal convergence rates for the eigenfunctions. The following lemma is an abstract result taken from
[
6
]
.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
H
be a separable Hilbert space endowed with a real-valued inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H}
and the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H}=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Let
T:H\to H
be a bounded linear compact self-adjoint operator. Suppose there exist
u\in H
with
\|u\|_{H}=1
and
\sigma>0
such that

(6.34)

\|Tu-\sigma u\|_{H}\leq\delta,

for some
\delta\geq 0
. Then there exists at least one eigenvalue
\sigma^{k}
of
T
such that

(6.35)

|\sigma^{k}-\sigma|\leq\delta.

Moreover, for any
L>0
,

(6.36)

\inf_{v\in S[\sigma-L,\sigma+L]}\|u-v\|_{H}\leq 2\delta L^{-1},

where
S[\sigma-L,\sigma+L]
is the eigenspace spanned by the eigenvectors corresponding to the eigenvalues in the band
[\sigma-L,\sigma+L]
.

For each
k
, let
N_{1}=N_{1}(k,\varepsilon,\eta)
be given as in Lemma
5.5
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{N}
be the spectral projection of
\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
onto the eigenspace
S_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{N}=\text{span}\{\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{1},\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2},\dots,\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{N}\}
, namely, for any
f\in L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
,

\mathcal{P}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{N}f=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle f,\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}.

The spectral projection
\mathcal{P}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{N}
satisfies
(\mathcal{P}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{N})^{2}=\mathcal{P}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{N}
.

Similarly, we can define
\mathcal{P}_{\eta}^{N}
to be the projection of
\mathscr{T}_{\eta}^{N}
onto the eigenspace
S_{\eta}^{N}=\text{span}\{\rho_{\eta}^{1},\rho_{\eta}^{2},\cdots,\rho_{\eta}^{N}\}
.

Let
f=\rho_{\eta}^{j}
with
1\leq j\leq N_{1}
. Then by Theorem
6.1
,

(6.37)

\|\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\rho_{\eta}^{j}-\mathscr{T}_{\eta}\rho_{\eta}^{j}\|_{L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\|\rho_{\eta}^{j}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

Note that
\mathscr{T}_{\eta}\rho_{\eta}^{j}=(\mu^{j}_{\eta})^{-1}\rho_{\eta}^{j}
. Then we can apply Lemma
6.6
to
H=L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
and
T=\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
. Due to the existence of common spectral gap between
(\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{N_{1}})^{-1}
and
(\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{N_{1}+1})^{-1}
, and between
(\mu_{\eta}^{N_{1}})^{-1}
and
(\mu_{\eta}^{N_{1}+1})^{-1}
, by (
6.36
) we have

(6.38)

\inf_{v\in S_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{N_{1}}}\|\rho_{\eta}^{j}-v\|_{L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}=\|\rho_{\eta}^{j}-\mathcal{P}_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{N_{1}}\rho_{\eta}^{j}\|_{L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

In other words, in the Hilbert space
L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
, each homogenized eigenfunction
\rho^{j}_{\eta}
with
1\leq j\leq N_{1}
can be well-approximated by a linear combination of
\rho^{i}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
with
1\leq i\leq N_{1}
. Due to the orthogonality, one then can show that each
\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{i}
can be well approximated by a linear combination of
\rho_{\eta}^{j}
with
1\leq i,j\leq N_{1}
. Precisely, we have

Lemma 6.7
.

For each
1\leq j\leq N_{1}
,

(6.39)

\|\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}-\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\eta}^{N_{1}}\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}\|_{L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}},

where

(6.40)

\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\eta}^{N_{1}}f=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}\langle f,\rho_{\eta}^{i}\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\rho_{\eta}^{i}.

Proof.

By (
6.38
), there exist
\alpha_{\ell}^{j}=\langle\rho_{\eta}^{j},\rho^{\ell}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}
such that

(6.41)

\|\rho_{\eta}^{j}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{1}}\alpha^{j}_{\ell}\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

It follows that for any
1\leq i,j\leq N_{1}
,

(6.42)

\langle\rho_{\eta}^{i},\rho_{\eta}^{j}\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}=\sum_{\ell,\tau=1}^{N_{1}}\alpha_{\ell}^{i}\alpha_{\tau}^{j}\langle\rho^{\ell}_{\eta,\varepsilon},\rho^{\tau}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\rangle+O_{k}(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}).

Let
E=(\alpha_{\ell}^{j})
and
Q=\langle\rho_{\eta}^{i},\rho_{\eta}^{j}\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}
(both are
N_{1}\times N_{1}
matrices). Then the last equation can be written as

(6.43)

Q=EE^{T}+O_{k}(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}).

We would like to show that
E
is invertible and
|E^{-1}|\leq C_{k}
. To this end, we notice that
\rho^{j}_{\eta}
are almost mutually orthogonal in
L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
. In fact, by (
5.7
), we have

(6.44)

\bigg|\int_{\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{2}\rho_{\eta}^{i}\rho_{\eta}^{j}-\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\eta}^{i}\rho_{\eta}^{j}\bigg|\leq C_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

Since
\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\eta}^{i}\rho_{\eta}^{j}=\delta_{ij}
, we have

(6.45)

Q=I+O_{k}(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}).

Together with (
6.43
), we have

(6.46)

EE^{T}=I+O_{k}(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}).

This implies that if
\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}<c_{k}
for some sufficiently small
c_{k}
, then
E
is invertible and

(6.47)

E^{-1}=E^{T}(I+O_{k}(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}))^{-1}=E^{T}+O_{k}(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}).

Hence, by (
6.41
) and the last equation

(6.48)

\|\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{\ell}-\sum_{j=1}^{N_{1}}\alpha^{j}_{\ell}\rho^{j}_{\eta}\|_{L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

In view of the fact
\alpha_{\ell}^{j}=\langle\rho_{\eta}^{j},\rho^{\ell}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}
, (
6.48
) is exactly (
6.39
).
∎

Lemma 6.8

(Optimal almost orthogonality)
.

For any
1\leq j,\ell\leq k
,

(6.49)

|\langle\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j},\rho_{\eta}^{\ell}\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|\leq\frac{C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}\mu_{\eta}^{\ell}}{|\mu_{\eta}^{\ell}-\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}|}.

Proof.

In (
6.33
), we estimate right-hand side by

(6.50)

\displaystyle|\langle\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j},(\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}-\mathscr{T}_{\eta})(\rho_{\eta}^{\ell})\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|

\displaystyle=|\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\eta}^{N_{1}}\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j},(\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}-\mathscr{T}_{\eta})(\rho_{\eta}^{\ell})\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|+|\langle\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}-\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\eta}^{N_{1}}\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j},(\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}-\mathscr{T}_{\eta})(\rho_{\eta}^{\ell})\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}+C_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}},

where we have used Theorem
6.4
, Theorem
6.1
and (
6.39
).
∎

6.3.
Optimal lower bound of eigenvalues

Recall that
S^{N}_{\eta,\varepsilon}=\text{span}\{\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}:j=1,\cdots,N\}
and
S^{N}_{\eta}=\{\rho_{\eta}^{j}:j=1,\cdots,N\}
.
Previously, we have shown in Proposition
5.1
the optimal upper bound of the eigenvalues
\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
, namely,

(6.51)

\mu^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}\leq\mu^{k}_{\eta}+C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

In this subsection, we show the optimal lower bounds of the eigenvalues and thus complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

It suffices to show

(6.52)

\mu^{k}_{\eta}\leq\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}+C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

One can employ a similar argument as Subsection
5.2
. Here we use a different argument.
Consider
S_{\eta}[t]=\text{span}\{\rho_{\eta}^{j}:\mu_{\eta}^{j}\leq t\}
.
To show (
6.52
), it suffices to show

(6.53)

\text{dim}S_{\eta}[\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}+M_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}]\geq k,

for sufficiently large
M_{k}
independent of
\varepsilon
and
\eta
. Since
S_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}
has dimension
k
, if we can show that any element in
S_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}
can be well approximated by the elements of
S_{\eta}[\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}+M_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}]
, then the latter must have at least dimension
k
. This can be shown by the following two ingredients.

Ingredient 1: By (
6.39
), there exists
N_{1}\leq N_{0}k
such that for any
1\leq j\leq k

(6.54)

\|\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}-\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\eta}^{N_{1}}\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}\|_{L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

Ingredient 2: By the optimal orthogonality in Lemma
6.8
, for any
1\leq j\leq k
and
\ell\leq N_{0}k
with
\mu_{\eta}^{i}
satisfying
\mu_{\eta}^{\ell}\geq\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}+M\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}
, we have

(6.55)

\langle\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j},\rho_{\eta}^{\ell}\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\leq\frac{C_{k}^{\prime}}{M},

where
C^{\prime}_{k}
is a constant depending only on
k
and
M
is a large number to be chosen later.

From Ingredient 1 and Ingredient 2, we have, for any
M>0
,

(6.56)

\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\inf_{f\in S_{\eta}[\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}+M\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}]}\|\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}-f\|_{L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}+\frac{N_{0}kC_{k}^{\prime}}{M}.

Observe that if
\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}
is sufficiently small and
M
is sufficiently large depending on
k
, then the right-hand side of the above inequality can be small as well.

Now we claim: there exists
\gamma_{k}>0
such that if

(6.57)

\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\inf_{f\in S_{\eta}[\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}+M_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}]}\|\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}-f\|_{L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq\gamma_{k},

then

(6.58)

\text{dim}S_{\eta}[\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}+M_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}]\geq k.

To prove the claim, we assume
\text{dim}S_{\eta}[\mu_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}+M_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}]=m
and show
m\geq k
. Note that (
6.57
) implies that for each
1\leq j\leq k
, there exist
\alpha^{j}_{\ell}
(
1\leq\ell\leq m
),

(6.59)

\|\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\alpha^{j}_{\ell}\rho_{\eta}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq\gamma_{k}.

It follows

(6.60)

\langle\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{i},\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}=\sum_{\ell,\tau=1}^{m}\alpha_{\ell}^{i}\alpha_{\tau}^{j}\langle\rho_{\eta}^{\ell},\rho_{\eta}^{\tau}\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}+O(\gamma_{k}).

Let
E=(\alpha_{\ell}^{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times m}
and
Q=(\langle\rho_{\eta}^{\ell},\rho_{\eta}^{\tau}\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}})\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
.
Using the orthogonality of
\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{j}
in
L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})
, (
6.60
) gives

(6.61)

I=EQE^{T}+O(\gamma_{k}).

Note that
I
is the
k\times k
identity matrix. Thus
\text{Rank}(I-O(\gamma_{k}))=k
if we let
O(\gamma_{k})<1/k
. Hence, in this case
\text{Rank}(EQE^{T})=k
and therefore we must have
m\geq k
. This proves the claim.

Consequently, for
\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}
sufficiently small and
M=M_{k}
sufficiently large (depending on
k
), the right-hand side of (
6.56
) is bounded by
\gamma_{k}\leq O(1/k)
and thus the above claim implies (
6.53
). The proof is complete.
∎

6.4.
Convergence of eigenfunctions

In this subsection, we prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Fix
k\geq 1
. Let
N_{1}=N_{1}(k,\varepsilon,\eta)
be given as in Lemma
5.5
. It suffices to consider
t\in(0,1]
since the case
t>1
has no improvement compared to
t=1
. We would like to show

(6.62)

\|\rho^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}-\sum_{|\mu_{\eta}^{j}-\mu_{\eta}^{k}|\leq t}\langle\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k},\rho_{\eta}^{j}\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\rho_{\eta}^{j}\|_{L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C_{k}(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\wedge\varepsilon+t^{-1}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}).

In view of
\psi_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}=\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k}
from Proposition
2.6
, we see the above estimate implies the desired estimate (
1.13
).

To show (
6.62
), we consider two slightly different approaches leading two different estimates.

Case 1: The first approach is based on Lemma
6.7
and Lemma
6.8
. In fact, by Lemma
6.7
and the triangle inequality, we have

(6.63)

\displaystyle\|\rho^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}-\sum_{|\mu_{\eta}^{j}-\mu_{\eta}^{k}|\leq t}\langle\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k},\rho_{\eta}^{j}\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\rho_{\eta}^{j}\|_{L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\rho^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}-\sum_{1\leq j\leq N_{1}}\langle\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k},\rho_{\eta}^{j}\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\rho_{\eta}^{j}\|_{L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle+\|\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\mu_{\eta}^{j}-\mu_{\eta}^{k}|\leq t\\
j\geq N_{1}+1\end{subarray}}\langle\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k},\rho_{\eta}^{j}\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\rho_{\eta}^{j}\|_{L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}+\|\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\mu_{\eta}^{j}-\mu_{\eta}^{k}|>t\\
1\leq j\leq N_{1}\end{subarray}}\langle\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k},\rho_{\eta}^{j}\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\rho_{\eta}^{j}\|_{L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}

The first term on the right-hand side is handled by (
6.39
) with a bound
C_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}
. To estimate the second term, we recall that Lemma
5.5
yields
\mu_{\eta}^{N_{1}+1}-\mu_{\eta}^{N_{1}}\geq c_{k}>0
. Hence, for
j\geq N_{1}+1
, we have
|\mu_{\eta}^{j}-\mu_{\eta}^{k}|\geq c_{k}
. Then by Lemma
6.8
, we obtain

(6.64)

|\langle\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k},\rho_{\eta}^{j}\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}|\leq C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

Since we have assumed
t\leq 1
, in view of the Weyl’s law (Lemma
5.4
), the number of eigenvalues
\mu_{\eta}^{j}
dropping in
|\mu^{j}_{\eta}-\mu_{\eta}^{k}|\leq t
depends only on
k
. Hence, (
6.64
) and the triangle inequality imply that the second term on the right-hand side of (
6.63
) is bounded by
C_{k}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}
. Next, we estimate the last term in (
6.63
). We use the fact
|\mu_{\eta}^{j}-\mu_{\eta}^{k}|>t
, Lemma
6.8
and a similar argument to conclude that the last term of (
6.63
) is bounded by
C_{k}t^{-1}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}
. Combining the above estimates, we obtain

(6.65)

\|\rho^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}-\sum_{|\mu_{\eta}^{j}-\mu_{\eta}^{k}|\leq t}\langle\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k},\rho_{\eta}^{j}\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\rho_{\eta}^{j}\|_{L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C_{k}(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}+t^{-1}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}).

The proof of the first case is complete.

Case 2: We use the maximum principle to estimate the boundary layer term
v_{\eta,\varepsilon}
defined in (
6.3
). The maximum principle has been proved in (
3.27
). It follows that

(6.66)

\|v_{\eta,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq\varepsilon\|\chi_{\eta,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla u_{\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla u_{\eta}\|_{W^{1,p}(\partial\Omega)},

This together with Lemma
6.2
gives

(6.67)

\|\mathscr{T}_{\eta,\varepsilon}f-\mathscr{T}_{\eta}f\|_{L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)},

for some
p>d
. This is an alternative estimate of Theorem
6.1
, which, by the same argument as Lemma
6.7
and Case 1 above, yields

(6.68)

\|\rho^{k}_{\eta,\varepsilon}-\sum_{|\mu_{\eta}^{j}-\mu_{\eta}^{k}|\leq t}\langle\rho_{\eta,\varepsilon}^{k},\rho_{\eta}^{j}\rangle_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}\rho_{\eta}^{j}\|_{L^{2}_{\phi_{\eta,\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{\eta,\varepsilon})}\leq C_{k}(\varepsilon+t^{-1}\varepsilon\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}).

Finally, combining the estimates (
6.65
) and (
6.68
), we obtain the desired estimate (
6.62
) and complete the proof.
∎

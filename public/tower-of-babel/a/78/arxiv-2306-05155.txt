A note on the distance and distance signless Laplacian spectral radius of complements of trees
Iswar Mahato, M. Rajesh Kannan

Abstract
In this article, we show that the generalized tree shift operation increases the distance spectral radius, distance signless Laplacian spectral radius, and the D α D_{\alpha} -spectral radius of complements of trees. As a consequence of this result, we correct an ambiguity in the proofs of some of the known results.



AMS Subject Classification (2010):
05C50, 05C35.

Keywords.
Complement of tree, Distance spectral radius, Distance signless Laplacian spectral radius,
D_{\alpha}
-spectral radius.

1
Introduction

Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a finite, simple, and connected graph with the vertex set
V(G)=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}\}
and the edge set
E(G)=\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{m}\}
. The cardinality of the vertex set
V(G)
is the
order
of the graph
G
. Two vertices
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
of
G
are
adjacent
if an edge exists between them in
G
. Let
N(v)
denote the collection of vertices that are adjacent to the vertex
v
in
G
, and
N(v)
is called the
neighbour
of
v
in
G
. The
complement

\overline{G}
of
G
is the graph with
V(\overline{G})=V(G)
and two distinct vertices in
\overline{G}
are adjacent if and only if they are non-adjacent in
G
. The
distance
between the vertices
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
in
G
, denoted by
d_{G}(v_{i},v_{j})
, is the length of a shortest path between them in
G
, and define
d_{G}(v_{i},v_{i})=0
for all
v_{i}\in V(G)
. The
diameter
of
G
, denoted by
\diam(G)
, is the greatest distance between any pair of vertices in
G
. The
distance matrix

D(G)
of
G
is the
n\times n
matrix with its rows and columns are indexed by the vertex set of
G
, and the
(v_{i},v_{j})
-th entry is equal to
d_{G}(v_{i},v_{j})
. The
transmission
of a vertex
v_{i}
, denoted by
\Tr(v_{i})
, is the sum of the distances from
v_{i}
to all other vertices of
G
. Let
\Tr(G)=\diag\big(\Tr(v_{1}),\Tr(v_{2}),\ldots,\Tr(v_{n})\big)
be the diagonal matrix of vertex transmissions of the vertices of
G
. The
distance signless Laplacian matrix
of
G
is defined as
D^{Q}(G)=\Tr(G)+D(G)
. The largest eigenvalue of
D(G)
and
D^{Q}(G)
are called
the distance spectral radius
and the
distance signless Laplacian spectral radius
of
G
, respectively. We denote the distance spectral radius and the distance signless Laplacian spectral radius of
G
by
\lambda_{1}(G)
and
\mu_{1}(G)
, respectively. The
D_{\alpha}
-matrix of
G
is defined as

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}(G)=\alpha Tr(G)+(1-\alpha)D(G)\quad\text{for any real $\alpha\in[0,1]$}.

The largest eigenvalue of
D_{\alpha}(G)
is called
the
D_{\alpha}
-spectral radius
of
G
and is denoted by
\rho_{\alpha}(G)
. Let
P_{n}
and
K_{1,n-1}
denote the path and the star on
n
vertices, respectively.

Let
u,v
be two vertices in a graph
G
. Let
G^{\prime}
be the graph obtained from
G
by removing the edges between
v
and
N(v)\setminus(N(u)\cup\{u\})
and adding the edges between
u
and
N(v)\setminus(N(u)\cup\{u\})
. The above operation is known as the Kelmans transformation.

Remark 1.1
.

For a tree
T
, if
T^{\prime}
is obtained from
T
by applying the Kelmans transformation exactly once, then the number of pendant vertices in
T^{\prime}
equals the number of pendant vertices in
T
plus one. The diameter of
T^{\prime}
is either equal to
\diam(T)
or
\diam(T)-1
.

In
[
1
]
, Lin and Drury proved that the Kelmans transformation, under suitable assumptions, reduces the distance spectral radius of complements of trees.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
1
, Theorem
2
]
)
.

Let
T
be a tree on
n
vertices with
\diam(T)\geq 4
. Let
X=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})^{t}
be the unit Perron vector of
D(T^{c})
. Let
uv\in E(T)
with
x_{u}\geq x_{v}
, and

T^{\prime}=T-\{vw\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}w\in N_{T}(v)\setminus\{u\}\}+\{uw\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}w\in N_{T}(v)\setminus\{u\}\}.

Then
\lambda_{1}(\overline{T^{\prime}})\geq\lambda_{1}(\overline{T})
with equality if and only if
N_{T}(v)=\{u\}
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
1
, Theorem
3
]
)
.

Let
T
be a tree on
n
vertices with
\diam(T)\geq 4
. Suppose that
e
is a non-pendant edge of
T
. Let
T^{\prime}
be the tree obtained from
T
by collapsing
e
, identifying the vertices of
e
to a single vertex
z
, and adding a pendant edge at
z
. Then
\lambda_{1}(\overline{T^{\prime}})>\lambda_{1}(\overline{T})
.

Using these results, Lin and Drury
[
1
]
characterized the trees whose complements have the minimum distance spectral radius.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
1
, Theorem
5
]
)
.

Let
T
be a tree on
n\geq 4
vertices with
T\ncong K_{1,n-1}
. Then
\lambda_{1}(\overline{T^{\prime}})>\lambda_{1}(\overline{P_{n}})
with equality if and only if
T
is isomorphic to
P_{n}
.

In the proof of Theorem
1.3
, Lin and Drury mentioned that any tree
T
of order
n
could be obtained from
P_{n}
by repeatedly applying the Kelmans transformation as in Theorem
1.2
. But this is not true in general. Next, we give some counterexamples.

The tree
\tilde{T}
, given in Figure
1
, can not be obtained from
P_{7}
by applying the graft transformation as in Theorem
1.2
. Because
\diam(\tilde{T})=\diam(P_{7})-2
and number of pendant vertices in
\tilde{T}
equals the number of pendant vertices in
P_{7}+1
.
This example is given in
[
2
]
.

Figure 1:
The tree
\tilde{T}
on
7
vertices.

Any tree
T
of order
n
with
3
pendant vertices and
\diam(T)\leq n-3
can not be obtained from
P_{n}
by applying the graft transformation as in Theorem
1.2
.

So, the proof of Theorem
5
in
[
1
]
has some ambiguity. The same technique is used in the proofs of Theorem
4.2
by Li et al.
[
3
]
and Theorem
3.9
by Qin et al.
[
4
]
for characterizing the complements of trees with minimum distance signless Laplacian spectral radius and
D_{\alpha}
-spectral radius, respectively.

In this article, we show that the generalized tree shift transformation increases the distance spectral radius, distance signless-Laplacian spectral radius, and the
D_{\alpha}
-spectral radius of complements of trees. Using these results, we correct the proofs of Theorem
5
in
[
1
]
, Theorem
4.2
of
[
3
]
and Theorem
3.9
of
[
4
]
.

2
Main results

In this section, first, we recall the definition of generalized tree shift and some of its properties. We show that the generalized tree shift increases the distance spectral radius, distance signless-Laplacian spectral radius, and the
D_{\alpha}
-spectral radius of complements of trees. Using these results, we complete the proofs for characterizing the trees whose complements have minimum distance spectral radius, distance signless Laplacian spectral radius, and
D_{\alpha}
-spectral radius, respectively. Next we recall generalized tree shift (GTS) transformation introduced by Csikvári
[
2
]
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
2
, Definition 2.1]
)
.

Let
T
be a tree and
u,v\in V(T)
such that all the interior vertices of the path
uPv
(if they exist) have degree
2
. Let
w
be the neighbor of
v
lying on the
uPv
path (it is possible that
u=w
). Now, construct a tree
T_{1}
from
T
by deleting all the edges between
v
and
N_{T}(v)\setminus\{w\}
and add the edges between
u
and
N_{T}(v)\setminus\{w\}
.

Note that if
u
or
v
is a pendant vertex, then
T_{1}
isomorphic to
T
, otherwise the number of pendant vertices in
T_{1}
equals the number of pendant vertices in
T
plus one. In the latter case, the GTS is called proper. Define
T_{1}>T
if
T_{1}
can be obtained from
T
by some proper GTS. The relation
>
induces a poset on the tree on
n
vertices
[
2
]
.

In
[
2
]
, Csikvári proved the following theorem about the minimal element of the poset.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
2
, Theorem 2.4]
)
.

Every tree different from the path is the image of some
proper generalized tree shift.

The star is the unique maximal element, and
the path is the unique minimal element of the induced
poset of the generalized tree shift. Thus, it is easy to see that any tree on
n
vertices other than the path can be obtained from the path graph
P_{n}
by repeatedly applying the generalized tree shift.

In the following theorems, we prove that GTS increases the distance spectral radius, distance signless Laplacian spectral radius, and
D_{\alpha}
-spectral radius of complements of trees.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
T
be a tree of order
n
with
\diam(T)\geq 4
, and let
T_{1}
be the tree obtained from
T
by a proper GTS. Then
\lambda_{1}(\overline{T_{1}})>\lambda_{1}(\overline{T})
.

Proof.

Let
X=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})^{t}
be the unit perron vector of
\overline{T}
corresponding to
\lambda_{1}(\overline{T})
. Without loss of generality, assume that
x_{u}\geq x_{v}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle T_{1}=T-\{vz\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}z\in S\}+\{uz\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}z\in S\},\quad\text{where, $S=N_{T}(v)\setminus\{w\}$}.

It is easy to see that
D(\overline{T})=2A(T)+J-I
and
D(\overline{T_{1}})\geq 2A(T)+J-I
entrywise. Therefore, by Rayley’s principle, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\overline{T_{1}})-\lambda_{1}(\overline{T})\geq

\displaystyle~X^{t}\big(D(\overline{T_{1}})-D(\overline{T})\big)X

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~X^{t}\big(2A(T_{1})-2A(T)\big)X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~4(x_{u}-x_{v})\sum_{z\in S}x_{z}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~0.

If
\lambda_{1}(\overline{T_{1}})=\lambda_{1}(\overline{T})
, then for the vertex
u
, we have
0=\lambda_{1}(\overline{T_{1}})x_{u}-\lambda_{1}(\overline{T})x_{u}\geq\sum_{z\in S}x_{z}>0
, a contradiction. Therefore,
\lambda_{1}(\overline{T_{1}})>\lambda_{1}(\overline{T})
. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
T
be a tree of order
n
with
\diam(T)\geq 4
, and let
T_{1}
be the tree obtained from
T
by a proper GTS. Then
\mu_{1}(\overline{T_{1}})>\mu_{1}(\overline{T})
.

Proof.

Let
z
be the neighbour of
u
lying on the
uPv
path, and let
S_{1}=N_{T}(u)\setminus\{z\}
and
S_{2}=N_{T}(v)\setminus\{w\}
. Suppose that
X=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})^{t}
be the unit perron vector of
\overline{T}
corresponding to
\mu_{1}(\overline{T})
. Without loss of generality, assume that
x_{u}\geq x_{v}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle T_{1}=T-\{vv_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v_{2}\in S_{2}\}+\{uv_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v_{2}\in S_{2}\}.

If
v_{1}\in S_{1}
and
v_{2}\in S_{2}
, then we have

\displaystyle d_{\overline{T}}(u,v_{1})=2,\qquad d_{\overline{T_{1}}}(u,v_{1})\geq 2;

\displaystyle d_{\overline{T}}(u,v_{2})=1,\qquad d_{\overline{T_{1}}}(u,v_{2})\geq 2;

\displaystyle d_{\overline{T}}(v,v_{2})=2,\qquad d_{\overline{T_{1}}}(v,v_{2})=1;

and the distances of other pair of vertices in
\overline{T}
and
\overline{T_{1}}
are same. Now, by Rayley’s principle, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(\overline{T_{1}})-\mu_{1}(\overline{T})\geq

\displaystyle~X^{t}\big(D^{Q}(\overline{T_{1}})-D^{Q}(\overline{T})\big)X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~2x_{u}\sum_{v_{1}\in S_{1}}\big(d_{\overline{T_{1}}}(u,v_{1})-d_{\overline{T}}(u,v_{1})\big)x_{v_{1}}+2x_{u}\sum_{v_{2}\in S_{2}}\big(d_{\overline{T_{1}}}(u,v_{2})-d_{\overline{T}}(u,v_{2})\big)x_{v_{2}}

\displaystyle~+2x_{v}\sum_{v_{2}\in S_{2}}\big(d_{\overline{T_{1}}}(v,v_{2})-d_{\overline{T}}(v,v_{2})\big)x_{v_{2}}+x_{u}^{2}\sum_{v_{1}\in S_{1}}\big(d_{\overline{T_{1}}}(u,v_{1})-d_{\overline{T}}(u,v_{1})\big)

\displaystyle~+x_{u}^{2}\sum_{v_{2}\in S_{2}}\big(d_{\overline{T_{1}}}(u,v_{2})-d_{\overline{T}}(u,v_{2})\big)+x_{v}^{2}\sum_{v_{2}\in S_{2}}\big(d_{\overline{T_{1}}}(v,v_{2})-d_{\overline{T}}(v,v_{2})\big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~2x_{u}\sum_{v_{1}\in S_{1}}\big(d_{\overline{T_{1}}}(u,v_{1})-d_{\overline{T}}(u,v_{1})\big)x_{v_{1}}+x_{u}^{2}\sum_{v_{1}\in S_{1}}\big(d_{\overline{T_{1}}}(u,v_{1})-d_{\overline{T}}(u,v_{1})\big)

\displaystyle~+2(x_{u}-x_{v})\sum_{v_{2}\in S_{2}}x_{v_{2}}+(x_{u}^{2}-x_{v}^{2})|S_{2}|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~(x_{u}-x_{v})\Big(2\sum_{v_{2}\in S_{2}}x_{v_{2}}+(x_{u}+x_{v})|S_{2}|\Big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~0.

If
\mu_{1}(\overline{T_{1}})=\mu_{1}(\overline{T})
, then for the vertex
u
, we have
0=\mu_{1}(\overline{T_{1}})x_{u}-\mu_{1}(\overline{T})x_{u}\geq\sum_{v_{2}\in S_{2}}x_{v_{2}}+x_{u}|S_{2}|>0
, a contradiction. Therefore,
\mu_{1}(\overline{T_{1}})>\mu_{1}(\overline{T})
. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
T
be a tree of order
n
with
\diam(T)\geq 4
, and let
T_{1}
be the tree obtained from
T
by a proper GTS. Then
\rho_{\alpha}(\overline{T_{1}})>\rho_{\alpha}(\overline{T})
.

Proof.

Let
z
be the neighbour of
u
lying on the
uPv
path, and let
S_{1}=N_{T}(u)\setminus\{z\}
and
S_{2}=N_{T}(v)\setminus\{w\}
. Suppose that
X=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})^{t}
be the unit perron vector of
\overline{T}
corresponding to
\rho_{\alpha}(\overline{T})
. Without loss of generality, assume that
x_{u}\geq x_{v}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle T_{1}=T-\{vv_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v_{2}\in S_{2}\}+\{uv_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v_{2}\in S_{2}\}.

If
v_{1}\in S_{1}
and
v_{2}\in S_{2}
, then we have

\displaystyle d_{\overline{T}}(u,v_{1})=2,\qquad d_{\overline{T_{1}}}(u,v_{1})\geq 2;

\displaystyle d_{\overline{T}}(u,v_{2})=1,\qquad d_{\overline{T_{1}}}(u,v_{2})\geq 2;

\displaystyle d_{\overline{T}}(v,v_{2})=2,\qquad d_{\overline{T_{1}}}(v,v_{2})=1;

and the distances of other pair of vertices in
\overline{T}
and
\overline{T_{1}}
are same. Now, by Rayley’s principle, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{\alpha}(\overline{T_{1}})-\rho_{\alpha}(\overline{T})\geq

\displaystyle~X^{t}\big(D-\alpha(\overline{T_{1}})-D_{\alpha}(\overline{T})\big)X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~2(1-\alpha)x_{u}\sum_{v_{1}\in S_{1}}\big(d_{\overline{T_{1}}}(u,v_{1})-d_{\overline{T}}(u,v_{1})\big)x_{v_{1}}+2(1-\alpha)x_{u}\sum_{v_{2}\in S_{2}}\big(d_{\overline{T_{1}}}(u,v_{2})-d_{\overline{T}}(u,v_{2})\big)x_{v_{2}}

\displaystyle~+2(1-\alpha)x_{v}\sum_{v_{2}\in S_{2}}\big(d_{\overline{T_{1}}}(v,v_{2})-d_{\overline{T}}(v,v_{2})\big)x_{v_{2}}+\alpha x_{u}^{2}\sum_{v_{1}\in S_{1}}\big(d_{\overline{T_{1}}}(u,v_{1})-d_{\overline{T}}(u,v_{1})\big)

\displaystyle~+\alpha x_{u}^{2}\sum_{v_{2}\in S_{2}}\big(d_{\overline{T_{1}}}(u,v_{2})-d_{\overline{T}}(u,v_{2})\big)+\alpha x_{v}^{2}\sum_{v_{2}\in S_{2}}\big(d_{\overline{T_{1}}}(v,v_{2})-d_{\overline{T}}(v,v_{2})\big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~2(1-\alpha)x_{u}\sum_{v_{1}\in S_{1}}\big(d_{\overline{T_{1}}}(u,v_{1})-d_{\overline{T}}(u,v_{1})\big)x_{v_{1}}+\alpha x_{u}^{2}\sum_{v_{1}\in S_{1}}\big(d_{\overline{T_{1}}}(u,v_{1})-d_{\overline{T}}(u,v_{1})\big)

\displaystyle~+2(1-\alpha)(x_{u}-x_{v})\sum_{v_{2}\in S_{2}}x_{v_{2}}+\alpha(x_{u}^{2}-x_{v}^{2})|S_{2}|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~(x_{u}-x_{v})\Big(2(1-\alpha)\sum_{v_{2}\in S_{2}}x_{v_{2}}+\alpha(x_{u}+x_{v})|S_{2}|\Big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~0.

If
\rho_{\alpha}(\overline{T_{1}})=\rho_{\alpha}(\overline{T})
, then for the vertex
u
, we have
0=\rho_{\alpha}(\overline{T_{1}})x_{u}-\rho_{\alpha}(\overline{T})x_{u}\geq(1-\alpha)\sum_{v_{2}\in S_{2}}x_{v_{2}}+\alpha x_{u}|S_{2}|>0
, a contradiction. Therefore,
\rho_{\alpha}(\overline{T_{1}})>\rho_{\alpha}(\overline{T})
. This completes the proof.
∎

Now, using the above theorems, we determine the unique tree whose
complement has minimum distance spectral radius, distance signless Laplacian spectral radius, and
D_{\alpha}
-spectral
radius, respectively.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
T
be a tree of order
n\geq 4
and
T\ncong K_{1,n-1},P_{n}
. Then

1.

\lambda_{1}(\overline{T})>\lambda_{1}(\overline{P_{n}})
.

2.

\mu_{1}(\overline{T})>\mu_{1}(\overline{P_{n}})
.

3.

\rho_{\alpha}(\overline{T})>\rho_{\alpha}(\overline{P_{n}})
.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
2.1
that any tree
T
of order
n
can be obtained from
P_{n}
by repeatedly applying the graft transformation mentioned in Definition
2.1
. Now, the proof of
1
,
2
and
3
follows from Theorem
2.2
, Theorem
2.3
and Theorem
2.4
, respectively.
∎

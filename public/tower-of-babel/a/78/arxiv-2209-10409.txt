Quasi-Diabatic Propagation Scheme for Simulating Polariton Chemistry
Deping Hu, Arkajit Mandal, Braden M. Weight, Pengfei Huo

Abstract
We generalize the quasi-diabatic (QD) propagation scheme to simulate the non-adiabatic polariton dynamics in molecule-cavity hybrid systems. The adiabatic-Fock states, which are the tensor product states of the adiabatic electronic states of the molecule and photon Fock states, are used as the locally well-defined diabatic states for the dynamics propagation. These locally well-defined diabatic states allow using any diabatic quantum dynamics methods for dynamics propagation, and the definition of these states will be updated at every nuclear time step. We use several recently developed non-adiabatic mapping approaches as the diabatic dynamics methods to simulate polariton quantum dynamics in a Shin-Metiu model coupled to an optical cavity. The results obtained from the mapping approaches provide very accurate population dynamics compared to the numerically exact method and outperform the widely used mixed quantum-classical approaches, such as the Ehrenfest dynamics and the fewest switches surface hopping approach. We envision that the generalized QD scheme developed in this work will provide a powerful tool to perform the non-adiabatic polariton simulations by allowing a direct interface between the diabatic dynamics methods and ab initio polariton information.

II
Theory and Methods

II.1
The Pauli-Fierz QED Hamiltonian

The Pauli-Fierz (PF) QED Hamiltonian for one molecule coupled to quantized radiation field inside an optical cavity can be written as

\hat{H}=\hat{T}_{\mathrm{n}}+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{en}}+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{p}}+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{enp}}+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{d}},

(1)

where
\hat{T}_{\mathrm{n}}
represents the nuclear kinetic energy operator,
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{en}}
is the electronic Hamiltonian that describes electron-nucleus interactions. Further,
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{p}}
,
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{enp}}
, and
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{d}}
represent the photonic Hamiltonian, electronic-nuclear-photonic interactions, and the dipole self-energy (DSE) term, respectively. A full derivation of this Hamiltonian, as well as its connection with the various atomic cavity QED models can be found in the Appendix of Ref.
42
.

The electronic-nuclear potential
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{en}}
, which describes the common molecular Hamiltonian excluding the nuclear kinetic energy is described as follows

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{en}}=\hat{T}_{\mathrm{e}}+\hat{V}_{\mathrm{ee}}+\hat{V}_{\mathrm{en}}+\hat{V}_{\mathrm{nn}}.

(2)

The above expression includes electronic kinetic energy, electron-electron interaction, electron-nucleus interaction and nucleus-nucleus interaction. The expressions of these four terms can be found in previous work.
Doltsinis and Marx 2002
;
Schäfer et al. 2018
;
Marx and Hutter 2009
Modern electronic structure theory have been developed around solving the eigenvalue problem of
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{en}}
, providing the following electronically adiabatic energy and its corresponding state

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{en}}|\phi_{\alpha}(\mathbf{R})\rangle=E_{\alpha}({\mathbf{R}})|\phi_{\alpha}(\mathbf{R})\rangle.

(3)

Here,
|\phi_{\alpha}(\mathbf{R})\rangle
represents the
\alpha_{\mathrm{th}}
many-electron adiabatic state for a given molecular system, with the adiabatic energy
E_{\alpha}({\mathbf{R}})
.

For clarity, we restrict our discussions to the cavity with only one photonic mode, and all the formulas presented here can be easily generalized into a more realistic, many-mode cavity. The photonic Hamiltonian is written as

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{p}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{p}_{\mathrm{c}}^{2}+\omega_{\mathrm{c}}^{2}\hat{q}_{\mathrm{c}}^{2}\right)=\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{c}}\Big(\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}+\frac{1}{2}\Big),

(4)

where
\hat{q}_{\mathrm{c}}=\sqrt{\hbar/2\omega_{\mathrm{c}}}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}+\hat{a})
and
\hat{p}_{\mathrm{c}}=i\sqrt{\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{c}}/2}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}-\hat{a})
are photon field operators,
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
and
\hat{a}
are the photonic creation and annihilation operators, respectively and
\omega_{\mathrm{c}}
is the photon frequency.

The light-matter coupling term (electronic-nuclear-photonic interactions) under the dipole gauge is expressed as

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{enp}}=\omega_{\mathrm{c}}\hat{q}_{\mathrm{c}}(\bm{\lambda}\cdot{\hat{\bm{\mu}}})=g_{\mathrm{c}}\bm{\epsilon}\cdot{\hat{\bm{\mu}}}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}+\hat{a}).

(5)

where
\bm{\lambda}=\lambda\cdot{\bm{\epsilon}}
characterizes the cavity photon field strength,
{\bm{\epsilon}}
is the direction of the field polarization. The photon field strength is determined by the volume of the cavity as
\lambda=\sqrt{1/\epsilon_{0}\mathcal{V}_{0}}
, where
\epsilon_{0}
is the permittivity inside the cavity and
\mathcal{V}_{0}
is the effective quantization volume inside the cavity. Another way to characterize the light-matter coupling strength is using
g_{\mathrm{c}}=\sqrt{\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{c}}/2}\lambda
. Note that the common notation used in the literature,
Kowalewski et al. 2016
;
Mandal and Huo 2019
the definition of
g_{\mathrm{c}}
also includes
\bm{\lambda}\cdot{\hat{\bm{\mu}}}
. Further, the total dipole operator of both electrons and nuclei is defined as

{\hat{\bm{\mu}}}=-\sum_{i}e\hat{{\bf r}}_{i}+\sum_{j}Z_{j}e\hat{\bf{R}}_{j},

(6)

where
-e
is the charge of the electron and
Z_{j}e
is the charge of the
j_{\mathrm{th}}
nucleus.

Finally, the DSE term is expressed as

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{d}}=\frac{1}{2}(\bm{\lambda}\cdot\hat{\bm{\mu}})^{2}=\frac{g_{\mathrm{c}}^{2}}{\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{c}}}(\bm{\epsilon}\cdot\hat{\bm{\mu}})^{2}.

(7)

This is a necessary term in the PF Hamiltonian and ensures both gauge invariance of the Hamiltonian
Taylor et al. 2020
;
Mandal et al. 2020a
and a bounded ground state.
Rokaj et al. 2018
;
Mandal et al. 2020a
;
Schäfer et al. 2020
In this work, we do not consider the cavity loss. The cavity loss can be effectively incorporated by using Lindblad dynamics approaches with the MQC simulations.
Koessler et al. 2022

For the molecule-cavity hybrid system, a convenient basis for quantum dynamics simulations could be the photon-dressed electronic adiabatic states

|\psi_{i}({\mathbf{R}})\rangle=|\phi_{\alpha}({\mathbf{R}})\rangle\otimes|n\rangle\equiv|\phi_{\alpha}({\bf R}),n\rangle,

(8)

where quantum number
i\equiv\{\alpha,n\}
indicates both the adiabatic electronic state of the molecule and the Fock state. Note that we have introduced a shorthand notation in Eq.
8
, which will be used throughout the rest of this paper. This is one of the most straightforward choices of basis for the hybrid system because of the readily available adiabatic electronic information (
e.g.,
wavefunctions, energies, and the dipole matrix) from electronic structure calculations that we need to construct the elements of Hamiltonian.

In the MQC simulation, such as the Ehrenfest or FSSH approach, or the recently developed mapping non-adiabatic approaches, the total molecular Hamiltonian is expressed as

\hat{H}=\hat{T}_{\mathrm{n}}+\hat{V},

(9)

where
\hat{T}_{\mathrm{n}}
represents the nuclear kinetic energy operator, and
\hat{V}
represents the rest of the Hamiltonian. For a bare molecular system,
\hat{V}=\hat{H}_{\mathrm{en}}
expressed in Eq.
2
.
For a molecule-cavity hybrid system,

\hat{V}=\hat{H}_{\mathrm{en}}+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{p}}+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{enp}}+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{d}}\equiv\hat{H}_{\mathrm{pl}},

(10)

which is commonly referred to as the polariton Hamiltonian,
Flick et al. 2017b
;
Flick et al. 2017a
also denoted as
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{pl}}
. In a similar way that electronic adiabatic states are defined in Eq.
3
, one can further define the polaritonic state
Flick et al. 2017b
;
Flick et al. 2017a
as the eigenstate of
\hat{V}=\hat{H}_{\mathrm{pl}}
(see definition in Eq.
10
) through the following eigenequation

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{pl}}|\mathcal{E}_{J}({\bf R})\rangle=\mathcal{E}_{J}({\bf R})|\mathcal{E}_{J}({\bf R})\rangle,

(11)

where
|\mathcal{E}_{J}({\bf R})\rangle
is the polariton state with polariton energy
\mathcal{E}_{J}({\bf R})
. The polariton eigenstate can be expressed as

|\mathcal{E}_{J}({\bf R})\rangle=\sum_{\alpha,n}c^{J}_{\alpha,n}({\mathbf{R}})|\phi_{\alpha}({\bf R}),n\rangle,

(12)

where
c^{J}_{\alpha,n}({\mathbf{R}})=\langle\phi_{\alpha}({\bf R}),n|\mathcal{E}_{J}({\bf R})\rangle
and
\mathcal{E}_{J}({\bf R})
can be obtained by diagonalizing the matrix of
\hat{V}=\hat{H}_{\mathrm{pl}}
(constructed from the adiabatic-Fock state basis in Eq.
8
) as

\mathbf{U}^{\dagger}[V({\mathbf{R}})]\mathbf{U}=[\mathcal{E}({\mathbf{R}})],

(13)

where

[V({\mathbf{R}})]_{ij}=\langle\psi_{i}({\mathbf{R}})|\hat{V}|\psi_{j}({\mathbf{R}})\rangle.

(14)

Note that the
{\bf R}
-dependence of
|\mathcal{E}_{J}({\bf R})\rangle
is entirely coming from the
{\bf R}
-dependence of the adiabatic states
|\phi_{\alpha}({\mathbf{R}})\rangle
, and the Fock state
|n\rangle
is completely
{\bf R}
-independent. Meanwhile, the
|\mathcal{E}_{J}({\bf R})\rangle
is the eigenstate of
\hat{V}
, whereas the adiabatic state
|\phi_{\alpha}({\mathbf{R}})\rangle
is only the eigenstate of
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{en}}
, and not for
\hat{V}
.

II.2
Quasi-Diabatic Propagation Scheme for Molecular Cavity QED

The QD propagation scheme explicitly addresses the discrepancy between accurate quantum dynamics methods in the diabatic representation and the electronic structure methods in the adiabatic representation. The essential idea of the QD scheme is to use the electronic adiabatic states associated with a reference geometry as the local diabatic states during a short-time quantum propagation and dynamically updates the definition of the QD states along the time-dependent nuclear trajectory.
Mandal et al. 2018a
;
Mandal et al. 2018b
;
Mandal et al. 2019
;
Sandoval et al. 2018
;
Zhou et al. 2019
;
Weight et al. 2021

In this work, we apply the QD propagation scheme to the case of molecular cavity QED. This requires the use of a convenient basis with a reference nuclear geometry as the
\it locally
well-defined diabatic basis, in the sense that its character is fixed (which is automatically guaranteed because of the fixed reference geometry by construction) as well as it is a complete basis (which is only true when the geometry is close to this reference geometry). The potential candidate for this basis is the adiabatic-Fock state
|\psi_{i}({\mathbf{R}})\rangle=|\phi_{\alpha}({\mathbf{R}}),n\rangle
(Eq.
8
), which is not the same as the polariton states
|\mathcal{E}_{J}({\bf R})\rangle
(Eq.
12
) except for the zero-coupling limit. In this work, we use the adiabatic-Fock state as the convenient choice due to its simplicity in terms of the polariton coupling and nuclear gradient expressions in the QD propagation framework.

Consider a short-time propagation of the nuclear DOFs during
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
, where the nuclear positions evolve from
{\bf R}(t_{0})
to
{\bf R}(t_{1})
, and the corresponding adiabatic-Fock basis (defined in Eq.
8
) are
\{|\psi_{i}({\bf R}(t_{0}))\rangle\}
and
\{|\psi_{j}{\bf R}(t_{1}))\rangle\}

\{|\psi_{i}({\bf R}_{0})\rangle\equiv|\phi_{\alpha}({\mathbf{R}_{0}}),n\rangle\}
at the reference nuclear geometry

as the

|\psi_{i}({\bf R}_{0})\rangle\equiv|\psi_{i}({\bf R}(t_{0}))\rangle,\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{for}\penalty\ t\in[t_{0},t_{1}].

(15)

With the above QD basis defined independently of
{\bf R}(t)
within each propagation segment, the electronic derivative couplings vanish while
\hat{V}({\bf R}(t))

t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]

(17)

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ +\frac{(t-t_{0})}{(t_{1}-t_{0})}\big[V_{\alpha\beta,mn}({\bf R}(t_{1}))-V_{\alpha\beta,mn}({\bf R}_{0})\big].

. First, let us focus on the gradient term from the electron-nuclear coupling term
Zhou et al. 2019
as follows

\displaystyle{\nabla}H^{\mathrm{en}}_{\alpha\beta,mn}({\bf R}(t_{1}))

(24)

\displaystyle={\nabla}\langle\phi_{\alpha}({\bf R}(t_{0})),m|\hat{H}_{\mathrm{en}}({\bf R}(t_{1}))|\phi_{\beta}({\bf R}(t_{0})),n\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\phi_{\alpha}({\bf R}(t_{0}))|{\nabla}\hat{H}_{\mathrm{en}}({\bf R}(t_{1}))|\phi_{\beta}({\bf R}(t_{0}))\rangle\cdot\langle m|n\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\nu}S_{\alpha\lambda}\langle\phi_{\lambda}({{\bf R}({t_{1}})})|\nabla{\hat{H}}_{\mathrm{en}}({\bf R}(t_{1}))|\phi_{\nu}({{\bf R}(t_{1})})\rangle S^{\dagger}_{\beta\nu}\cdot\delta_{mn}.

Here, from the first to the second line, we have used the fact that neither
\langle\phi_{\alpha}({\bf R}_{0})|
nor
\langle m|
are
{\bf R}
-dependent, which allows
{\nabla}
to bypass both and directly act on
\hat{V}({\bf R}(t_{1}))
. We have also used the fact that
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{en}}
does not contain any photonic operators. The gradient term
\langle\phi_{\lambda}({{\bf R}({t_{1}})})|\nabla{\hat{H}}_{\mathrm{en}}({\bf R}(t_{1}))|\phi_{\nu}({{\bf R}(t_{1})})\rangle
can be evaluated using the following well-known equality
Hammes-Schiffer and Tully 1994

\langle\phi_{\lambda}({\bf R})|\nabla\hat{H}_{\mathrm{en}}({\bf R})|\phi_{\nu}({\bf R})\rangle=\begin{cases}\nabla E_{\lambda}\quad&(\lambda=\nu)\\
{\bf d}_{\lambda\nu}\left(E_{\nu}-E_{\lambda}\right)\quad&(\lambda\neq\nu),\end{cases}

(25)

where the non-adiabatic coupling (NAC) vector (or so-called derivative coupling) is

{\bf d}_{\lambda\nu}=\langle\phi_{\lambda}({\bf R})|\nabla|\phi_{\nu}({\bf R})\rangle.

(26)

For the gradient on the matrix
H^{\mathrm{p}}_{\alpha\beta,mn}
, because there is no nuclear DOF in
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{p}}
, thus

\nabla H^{\mathrm{p}}_{\alpha\beta,mn}({\bf R}({t_{1}}))=\nabla\big[\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{c}}(n+\frac{1}{2})\delta_{\alpha,\beta}\delta_{m,n}\big]=0.

(27)

For the gradient on the light-matter interaction term
H^{\mathrm{enp}}_{\alpha\beta,mn}
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla H^{\mathrm{enp}}_{\alpha\beta,mn}({\bf R}(t_{1}))={\nabla}\langle\phi_{\alpha}({\bf R}(t_{0})),m|\hat{H}_{\mathrm{enp}}({\bf R}(t_{1}))|\phi_{\beta}({\bf R}(t_{0})),n\rangle

(28)

\displaystyle=\langle\phi_{\alpha}({\bf R}(t_{0}))|{\nabla}\hat{\bm{\mu}}({\bf R}(t_{1}))|\phi_{\beta}({\bf R}(t_{0}))\rangle\cdot\bm{\epsilon}\cdot g_{\mathrm{c}}\big(\sqrt{n}\delta_{m,n-1}+\sqrt{n+1}\delta_{m,n+1}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\nu}S_{\alpha\lambda}\langle\phi_{\lambda}({{\bf R}({t_{1}})})|\nabla\hat{\bm{\mu}}({\bf R}(t_{1}))|\phi_{\nu}({{\bf R}(t_{1})})\rangle S^{\dagger}_{\beta\nu}\cdot\bm{\epsilon}\cdot g_{\mathrm{c}}\big(\sqrt{n}\delta_{m,n-1}+\sqrt{n+1}\delta_{m,n+1}\big),

where
S_{\alpha\lambda}
and
S^{\dagger}_{\beta\nu}
are defined in Eq.
23a
and Eq.
23b
, respectively. To evaluate the term
\langle\phi_{\lambda}({\bf R})|\nabla\hat{\bm{\mu}}({\bf R})|\phi_{\nu}({\bf R)}\rangle
that appears in Eq.
28
, we use a simple relation based on the chain rule as follows

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{\lambda}({\bf R})|\nabla\hat{\bm{\mu}}({\bf R})|\phi_{\nu}({\bf R})\rangle=\nabla\langle\phi_{\lambda}({\bf R})|\hat{\bm{\mu}}({\bf R})|\phi_{\nu}({\bf R})\rangle-\langle\nabla\phi_{\lambda}({\bf R})|\hat{\bm{\mu}}({\bf R})|\phi_{\nu}({\bf R})\rangle-\langle\phi_{\lambda}({\bf R})|\hat{\bm{\mu}}({\bf R})|\nabla\phi_{\nu}({\bf R})\rangle

\displaystyle=\nabla{\bm{\mu}}_{\lambda\nu}({\bf R})-\sum_{\gamma}\langle\nabla\phi_{\lambda}({\bf R})|\phi_{\gamma}({\bf R})\rangle{\bm{\mu}}_{\gamma\nu}({\bf R})-\sum_{\gamma}{\bm{\mu}}_{\lambda\gamma}({\bf R})\langle\phi_{\gamma}({\bf R})|\nabla\phi_{\nu}({\bf R})\rangle

\displaystyle=\nabla{\bm{\mu}}_{\lambda\nu}({\bf R})+\sum_{\gamma}\big[{\bf d}_{\lambda\gamma}({\bf R}){\bm{\mu}}_{\gamma\nu}({\bf R})-{\bm{\mu}}_{\lambda\gamma}({\bf R}){\bf d}_{\gamma\nu}({\bf R})\big],

(29)

where
{\bf d}_{\lambda\gamma}({\bf R})=\langle\phi_{\lambda}({\bf R})|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}({\bf R})\rangle=-\langle\nabla\phi_{\lambda}({\bf R})|\phi_{\gamma}({\bf R})\rangle
and
{\bf d}_{\gamma\nu}({\bf R})=\langle\phi_{\gamma}({\bf R})|\nabla\phi_{\nu}({\bf R})\rangle=-\langle\nabla\phi_{\gamma}({\bf R})|\phi_{\nu}({\bf R})\rangle
are the electronic derivative couplings (defined in Eq.
26
). Note that from the first line to the second line, we have inserted
\hat{\mathcal{P}}=\sum_{\gamma}|\phi_{\gamma}({\bf R})\rangle\langle\phi_{\gamma}({\bf R})|
, which is the resolution of identity in the electronic subspace. As one can clearly see, this term requires the evaluation of derivative coupling
{\bf d}_{\lambda\gamma}({\bf R})
and
{\bf d}_{\gamma\nu}({\bf R})
, and the derivative on dipole matrix element
\nabla{\bm{\mu}}_{\lambda\nu}({\bf R})
.
For most of the electronic structure methods, the derivatives on dipole matrix elements
\nabla{\bm{\mu}}_{\lambda\nu}({\bf R})
are not implemented. Nevertheless, recent theoretical development has made these quantities available.
Zhang et al. 2019

Finally, the gradient from the DSE term is expressed as follows

\displaystyle\nabla H^{\mathrm{d}}_{\alpha\beta,mn}({\bf R}(t_{1}))={\nabla}\langle\phi_{\alpha}({\bf R}_{0}),m|\hat{H}_{\mathrm{d}}({\bf R}(t_{1}))|\phi_{\beta}({\bf R}_{0}),n\rangle

(30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\nabla}\Big[\sum_{\gamma}(\bm{\epsilon}\cdot\langle\phi_{\alpha}({\bf R}_{0})|\hat{\bm{\mu}}({\bf R}(t_{1}))|\phi_{\gamma}({\bf R}_{0})\rangle)(\bm{\epsilon}\cdot\langle\phi_{\gamma}({\bf R}_{0})|\hat{\bm{\mu}}({\bf R}(t_{1}))|\phi_{\beta}({\bf R}_{0})\rangle)\Big]\frac{g_{\mathrm{c}}^{2}}{\hbar\omega}\delta_{m,n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[\sum_{\gamma}(\bm{\epsilon}\cdot\langle\phi_{\alpha}({\bf R}_{0})|{\nabla}\hat{\bm{\mu}}({\bf R}(t_{1}))|\phi_{\gamma}({\bf R}_{0})\rangle)(\bm{\epsilon}\cdot\langle\phi_{\gamma}({\bf R}_{0})|\hat{\bm{\mu}}({\bf R}(t_{1}))|\phi_{\beta}({\bf R}_{0})\rangle)

\displaystyle+(\bm{\epsilon}\cdot\langle\phi_{\alpha}({\bf R}_{0})|\hat{\bm{\mu}}({\bf R}(t_{1}))|\phi_{\gamma}({\bf R}_{0})\rangle)(\bm{\epsilon}\cdot\langle\phi_{\gamma}({\bf R}_{0})|{\nabla}\hat{\bm{\mu}}({\bf R}(t_{1}))|\phi_{\beta}({\bf R}_{0})\rangle)\Big]\frac{g_{\mathrm{c}}^{2}}{\hbar\omega}\delta_{m,n},

where the term
\langle\phi_{\alpha}({\bf R}(t_{0}))|\hat{\bm{\mu}}({\bf R}(t_{1}))|\phi_{\gamma}({\bf R}(t_{0}))\rangle
can be evaluated using Eq.
21b
, and the
\langle\phi_{\alpha}({\bf R}_{0})|{\nabla}\hat{\bm{\mu}}({\bf R}(t_{1}))|\phi_{\beta}({\bf R}_{0})\rangle

.

Using the matrix elements
V_{\alpha\beta,mn}({\bf R}(t))
(Eq.
17
) and the nuclear gradient
\nabla V_{\alpha\beta,mn}({\bf R})
(as outlined in Eqs.
24
-
30
), one can in principle use any trajectory-based approaches or wavepacket approaches with guiding trajectories
Joubert-Doriol and Izmaylov 2018
;
Ben-Nun and Martínez 1998
;
Saita and Shalashilin 2012
to propagate the quantum dynamics in the time step of
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
. During the next short-time propagation segment
t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
, the QD scheme adopts a new reference geometry
{{\bf R}^{\prime}_{0}}\equiv{\bf R}(t_{1})
and new
diabatic
basis
|\psi_{j}({{\bf R}^{\prime}_{0}})\rangle\equiv|\psi_{j}({\bf R}(t_{1}))\rangle=|\phi_{\beta}({\bf R}(t_{1})),m\rangle
. Between the
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
propagation and the
t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
propagation segments, all of the necessary quantities will be transformed from
\{|\psi_{i}({\bf R}_{0})\rangle\}
to the
\{|\psi_{j}({\bf R}^{\prime}_{0})\rangle\}
basis, using the relation

|\psi_{j}({\bf R}(t_{1}))\rangle=\sum_{i}\langle\psi_{i}({\bf R}(t_{0}))|\psi_{j}({\bf R}(t_{1}))\rangle|\psi_{i}({\bf R}(t_{0}))\rangle.

(31)

For the model calculations in this work, these overlap integrals are evaluated as

\langle\psi_{i}({\bf R}(t_{0}))|\psi_{j}({\bf R}(t_{1}))\rangle=\langle\phi_{\alpha}({\bf R}(t_{0}))|\phi_{\beta}({\bf R}(t_{1}))\rangle\cdot\langle n|m\rangle,

(32)

where the electronic adiabatic state overlaps
\langle\phi_{\alpha}({\bf R}(t_{0}))|\phi_{\beta}({\bf R}(t_{1}))\rangle
are directly calculated using the discrete variable representation (DVR) basis, and the Fock states are orthonormal to each other
\langle n|m\rangle=\delta_{n,m}
.
When performing the transformation in Eq.
31
(as well as in Eq.
43
for the non-adiabatic mapping methods), the eigenvectors maintain their mutual orthogonality subject to a very small error when they are expressed in terms of the previous basis due to the incompleteness of the basis.
Granucci et al. 2001
;
Plasser et al. 2012
Nevertheless, the orthogonality remains to be well satisfied among
\{|\psi_{i}({\bf R}(t_{0}))\rangle\}
or
\{|\psi_{j}({\bf R}(t_{1}))\rangle\}
. This small numerical error generated from each step can, however, accumulate over many steps and cause a significant error at longer times, leading to non-unitary dynamics.
Granucci et al. 2001
;
Plasser et al. 2012
This potential issue can be easily resolved by using orthonormalization procedure among the vectors of the overlap matrix composed by
\langle\psi_{i}({\bf R}(t_{0}))|\psi_{j}({\bf R}(t_{1}))\rangle
, as been done in our previous work
Mandal et al. 2018b
for simulating photo-induced charge transfer dynamics. Here, we perform the Löwdin orthogonalization procedure
Löwdin 1950
as commonly used in the local diabatization approach
Granucci et al. 2001
to ensure unitary propagation.

As the nuclear geometry closely follows the reference geometry throughout the propagation, the QD basis forms a convenient and compact basis. Note that, in principle, one needs an infinite set of crude adiabatic states
\{|\psi_{i}({\bf R}_{0})\rangle\}
to represent the time-dependent electronic wavefunction because the electronic wavefunction could change rapidly with the motion of the nuclei, and the crude adiabatic basis is only convenient when the reference geometry
{\bf R}_{0}
is close to the nuclear geometry
{\bf R}
. By dynamically updating the basis in the QD scheme, the time-dependent electronic wavefunction is expanded with the “moving crude adiabatic basis”
Joubert-Doriol and Izmaylov 2018
that explores the most relevant and important parts of the Hilbert space, thus requiring only a few states for quantum dynamics propagation.

II.3
Non-adiabatic Mapping Dynamics Methods

The Meyer-Miller-Stock-Thoss (MMST) formalism
Meyer and Miller 1979
;
Stock and Thoss 1997
;
Thoss and Stock 1999
maps the discrete electronic DOFs onto continuous phase space variables. In the strict diabatic basis
\{|i\rangle\}
(in the sense that
\langle i|\nabla|j\rangle=0
for all
|i\rangle
and
|j\rangle
), the total Hamiltonian in Eq.
9
is expressed as

\hat{H}={\frac{{\bf P}^{2}}{2{\bf M}}}+\sum_{i}V_{ii}(\hat{\bf R})|i\rangle\langle i|+\sum_{i\neq j}V_{ij}(\hat{\bf R})|i\rangle\langle j|,

(33)

where
V_{ij}(\hat{\bf R})=\langle i|\hat{V}(\hat{\bf r},\hat{\bf R})|j\rangle
are the matrix elements of the electronic Hamiltonian. Note that here
|i\rangle
is used to represent the strict diabatic basis, and not to be confused with the adiabatic-Fock state
|\psi_{i}({\mathbf{R}})\rangle=|\phi_{\alpha}({\bf R}),n\rangle
introduced in Eq.
8
. Nevertheless, based on the QD scheme, these adiabatic-Fock states with a reference geometry
{\bf R}_{0}
will be used as the diabatic state in the neighborhood of the reference geometries, as indicated in Eq.
15
.

In the non-adiabatic mapping approach, the Hamiltonian operator in Eq.
33
is transformed into the following MMST Hamiltonian

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}={\frac{{\bf P}^{2}}{2{\bf M}}}+{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{ij}V_{ij}({\bf R})\left(p_{i}p_{j}+q_{i}q_{j}-2\gamma_{j}\delta_{ij}\right),

(34)

where
2\gamma_{j}
is viewed as a parameter
Miller and Cotton 2016
which specifies the ZPE of the mapping oscillators.
Müller and Stock 1999
;
Miller and Cotton 2016
;
Runeson and Richardson 2019
;
Runeson and Richardson 2020
In principle,
2\gamma_{j}
is state-specific and trajectory-specific.
Cotton and Miller 2019a
The MMST mapping Hamiltonian has been historically justified by Stock and Thoss using harmonic oscillator’s raising and lowering operators as the mapping operator.
Stock and Thoss 1997
;
Thoss and Stock 1999
Recently, it has been derived using the
SU(N)
Lie group theory or so-called generalized spin mapping approach.
Runeson and Richardson 2020

Classical trajectories are generated based on Hamilton’s equations of motion (EOM)

\displaystyle\dot{q}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}/\partial p_{j};\penalty\ \penalty\ \dot{p}_{i}=-\partial\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}/\partial q_{i}

(35a)

\displaystyle\dot{\bf R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}/\partial{\bf P};\penalty\ \penalty\ \dot{\bf P}=-\partial\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}/\partial{\bf R}={\bf F},

(35b)

with the nuclear force expressed as

{\bf F}=-{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{ij}\nabla V_{ij}({\bf R})\big(p_{i}p_{j}+q_{i}q_{j}-2\gamma_{j}\delta_{ij}\big).

(36)

Overall, the MMST mapping provides a consistent classical footing for both electronic and nuclear DOFs, and the non-adiabatic transitions between electronic states are captured through the classical motion of the fictitious harmonic oscillators. The non-adiabatic dynamics obtained from this formalism have shown good performance in the
ab initio
on-the-fly dynamics.
Zhou et al. 2019
;
Hu et al. 2021
;
Weight et al. 2021

To sample the initial electronic condition and estimate the population, it is also convenient to use the action-angle variables,
\{\varepsilon_{j},\theta_{j}\}
, which are related to the canonical mapping variables
\{p_{j},q_{j}\}
through

\varepsilon_{j}=\frac{1}{2}\left(p_{j}^{2}+q_{j}^{2}\right);\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \theta_{j}=-\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{p_{j}}{q_{j}}\right),

(37)

and the inverse relations

q_{j}=\sqrt{2\varepsilon_{j}}\cos(\theta_{j});\penalty\ \penalty\ p_{j}=-\sqrt{2\varepsilon_{j}}\sin(\theta_{j}),

(38)

where
\varepsilon_{j}
is a positive-definite action variable that is directly proportional to the mapping variables’ radius in action-space.
Cotton and Miller 2019a

The SQC approach calculates the population of electronic state
|j\rangle
, which is to be evaluated as
Miller and Cotton 2016

\displaystyle\rho_{jj}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}_{\bf R}\left[\hat{\rho}(0)e^{i\hat{H}t/\hbar}|j\rangle\langle j|e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}\right]

(39)

\displaystyle\approx\int d\bm{\tau}\rho_{\mathrm{W}}({\bf P},{\bf R})W_{i}({\bm{\varepsilon}}(0))W_{j}({\bm{\varepsilon}}(t)),

where
\hat{\rho}(0)=\hat{\rho}_{\bf R}\otimes|i\rangle\langle i|
is the initial density operator,
\rho_{\mathrm{W}}({\bf P},{\bf R})
is the Wigner transform of
\hat{\rho}_{\bf R}
operator for the nuclear DOFs,
{\bm{\varepsilon}}=\{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},...,\varepsilon_{\mathcal{N}}\}
is the positive-definite action variable vector for
\mathcal{N}
electronic states,
Cotton and Miller 2019a

W_{i}({\bm{\varepsilon}})=\delta(\varepsilon_{i}-(1+\gamma_{i}))\prod_{i\neq j}\delta(\varepsilon_{j}-\gamma_{j})
is the Wigner transformed action variables,
Cotton and Miller 2016
and
d\bm{\tau}\equiv d{\bf P}\cdot d{\bf R}\cdot d{\bm{\varepsilon}}\cdot d\bm{\theta}
. For practical reasons, the above delta functions in

are broadened using a distribution function (so-called window function) that can be used to bin the resulting electronic action variables in action-space.
Miller and Cotton 2016
Further, we use the
\gamma
-SQC approach,
Cotton and Miller 2019a
which uses a
state-specific
and

\gamma_{j}
parameter in Eq.
34
to correct the initial force according to the initially populated state. This method has been proven to provide very accurate non-adiabatic dynamics in model photo-dissociation problems (coupled Morse potential), as well as outperform FSSH (with decoherence correction) in

Hu et al. 2021
;
Weight et al. 2021

For the spin-LSC approach,

;

\displaystyle\rho_{jj}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}_{\bf R}\left[\hat{\rho}_{R}\otimes|i\rangle\langle i|e^{i\hat{H}t/\hbar}|j\rangle\langle j|e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}\right]

(40)

\displaystyle\approx\int d\bm{\tau}\rho_{\mathrm{W}}({\bf P},{\bf R})[|i\rangle\langle i|]_{\mathrm{s}}(0)\cdot[|j\rangle\langle j|]_{\mathrm{\bar{s}}}(t),

where the population estimators are obtained from the Stratonovich-Weyl transformed electronic projection operators, with the expressions as follows

(41a)

V
Conclusions

In this work, we generalize the quasi-diabatic (QD) propagation scheme
Mandal et al. 2018b
;
Zhou et al. 2019
;
Mandal et al. 2019
to simulate the non-adiabatic polariton dynamics in molecule-cavity hybrid systems. The adiabatic-Fock states, which are the tensor product states of the adiabatic electronic states of the molecule and photon Fock states, are used as the
locally
well-defined
diabatic
states for the dynamics propagation.
Zhou et al. 2019
;
Mandal et al. 2019
These locally well-defined diabatic states allow using any diabatic quantum dynamics methods for dynamics propagation, and the definition of these states will be updated at every nuclear time step. The benefit of using such adiabatic-Fock states is that one can conveniently obtain the electronic adiabatic states energies, the nuclear gradient, the dipole moments and NACs between these states, which are necessary ingredients in molecular cavity QED simulations.

We use the recently developed non-adiabatic mapping dynamics approaches,
\gamma
-SQC
Cotton and Miller 2019a
and spin-LSC
Runeson and Richardson 2020
to investigate polariton dynamics of a Shin-Metiu model coupled to an optical cavity.
Hoffmann et al. 2020
;
Zhou et al. 2022
To benchmark the results of the obtained polariton dynamics, we performed simulations using the Ehrenfest dynamics and the FSSH approaches, as well as the numerically exact polariton wavepacket propagation. The results show that the mapping methods can accurately describe the population dynamics of the molecule-cavity system both at a short time and a longer time when compared to exact dynamics results. In addition, the mapping methods outperform the Ehrenfest and FSSH approaches at a long time dynamics. The numerical results also demonstrate that the performance of the mapping methods (
\gamma
-SQC and spin-LSC) becomes less accurate with an increased number of states in the simulation, indicating the need for future theoretical development.

We envision that the theoretical development in this work will provide the emerging polariton chemistry field with a general theoretical tool that enables direct
ab initio
on-the-fly simulations of polariton photochemical processes. We also anticipate that the theoretical developments in this work will enable many recently developed diabatic quantum dynamics approaches to directly simulate polariton quantum dynamics.

For practical reasons, the delta functions in Eq.
39
are broadened using well-explored window functions, which can be used to bin the electronic action variables in action-space. The triangle window
Cotton and Miller 2016
;
Cotton and Miller 2019a

W_{j}({\bm{\varepsilon}})=w_{1}(\varepsilon_{j})\prod_{j^{\prime}\neq j}^{\mathcal{N}}w_{0}(\varepsilon_{j},\varepsilon_{j^{\prime}}),

(47)

where the window functions are defined as

w_{1}(\varepsilon)=\begin{cases}(2-\varepsilon)^{2-\mathcal{N}},&1<\varepsilon<2\\
0,&\mathrm{else}\end{cases}

(48)

and

w_{0}(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime})=\begin{cases}1,&\varepsilon^{\prime}<2-\varepsilon\\
0,&\mathrm{else},\end{cases}

(49)

and trajectories are assigned to state
j
at time
t
if
\varepsilon_{j}\geq 1
and
\varepsilon_{j^{\prime}}<1
for all
j^{\prime}\neq j
. The ZPE parameter in the standard SQC method with triangle windowing is
\gamma=1/3
.

The time-dependent population of the state
|j\rangle
is computed with Eq.
39
. Using the window function estimator, the total population is no longer properly normalized due to the fraction of trajectories that are outside of any window region at any given time.
Cotton and Miller 2013a
Thus, the total population must be normalized
Cotton and Miller 2013a
with the following procedure

{{\rho}_{jj}(t)}/{\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\rho}_{ii}(t)}\rightarrow{\rho}_{jj}(t).

(50)

In the
\gamma
-SQC approach,
Cotton and Miller 2019a
it was proposed that the mapping ZPE should be chosen in such a way as to constrain the initial force to be composed purely from the initially occupied state.
Cotton and Miller 2019a
This new scheme has shown to provide a significant improvement for photo-dissociation problems with coupled Morse potentials
Cotton and Miller 2019a
and has been combined with the kinematic momentum approach
Cotton et al. 2017
to carry out on-the-fly simulations of the methaniminium cation.
Hu et al. 2021
The basic logic of
\gamma
-SQC is to choose an
\gamma_{j}
for each state
|j\rangle
in every given individual trajectory, such that the initial population is forced to respect the initial electronic excitation focused onto a single excited state. If the initial electronic state is
|i\rangle
, then

\gamma_{j}=\varepsilon_{j}-\delta_{ji},

(51)

or equivalently,

\delta_{ji}=\varepsilon_{j}-\gamma_{j},

(52)

where the
\{\varepsilon_{j}\}
are uniformly sampled inside the window function (Eq.
47
), and then the
\gamma_{j}
are chosen to satisfy Eq.
52
.

These
\gamma_{j}
will be explicitly used in the EOMs in Eqs.
35
-
36

{\bf F}=-{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{ij}\nabla V_{ij}({\bf R})\big(p_{i}p_{j}+q_{i}q_{j}-2\gamma_{j}\delta_{ji}\big),

(53)

ensuring the initial forces (at
t=0

{\bf F}=-\nabla V_{ii}({\bf R})
. Previously, without any adjustments to
\gamma_{j}

were only dependent on the windowing function itself,

\gamma_{j}=0.366

Cotton and Miller 2019a

\gamma_{j}

|j\rangle

Besides the mapping dynamics methods (

Here, we briefly present these approaches for the completeness of this work.

In the Ehrenfest dynamics, the wavefunction of the quantum subsystem (electronic-photonic DOFs) is written as

|\Psi({\bf r};{\bf R}(t))\rangle=\sum_{i}c_{i}(t)|\psi_{i}({\bf R}(t))\rangle,

(61)

where
|\psi_{i}({\bf R}(t))\rangle=|\phi_{\alpha}({\bf R}(t))\rangle\otimes|n\rangle

8

i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\Psi({\bf r};{\bf R}(t))\rangle=\hat{V}|\Psi({\bf r};{\bf R}(t))\rangle.

where

13

{\bf R}_{l}(t)

where

to calculate the populations that are not in an adiabatic representation. These methods vary on how to calculate the polaritonic state density matrix

\{c_{I}(t)\}

\rho^{\mathrm{pl}}_{IJ}({\bf R}_{l}(t))=\begin{cases}\delta_{IK},&I=J\\
0,&I\neq J,\end{cases}

where
K
is the active polaritonic state. This method explicitly assumes that the off-diagonal elements of the polaritonic state density matrix are zero, which is often not a good one. The adiabatic-Fock state population is then obtained from the same transformation described in Eq.
72

c_{I}c_{J}^{*},&I\neq J,\end{cases}

where

is the active index. The adiabatic-Fock state population is then obtained from the same transformation described in Eq.

1.
Introduction and main results

We investigate the Gabor systems generated by linear combinations of the
Cauchy kernels, i.e. by the windows of the form

(1.1)

g(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{a_{k}}{t-iw_{k}}.

We describe a new wide class of such functions for which the corresponding Gabor systems possess the frame property for all rectangular
lattices of density at least one. We also observe that for general rational windows of the form (
1.1
), the frame
property of the Gabor system
\{e^{2\pi i\beta mx}g(x-\alpha n)\}_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}
depends on the rationality
of the product
\alpha\beta
, give a precise estimate how large the density
(\alpha\beta)^{-1}
should be to garantee the frame property
of the Gabor system and consider sampling in shift-invariant spaces, generated by the window
g
, as well as some
related matters.

One of the central motives of the article is hinted by the Daubechies conjecture
[
3
, p. 981]
which assumes that Gabor system is a frame for all
\alpha\beta<1
whenever
g
is positive function with positive Fourier transform. This conjecture has been disproved by Janssen
[
8
]
, yet in all known examples of functions which generates a Gabor system for all
\alpha\beta<1
we encounter some kind of positivity.

1.1.
Gabor systems

Given a function
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
by
\pi_{x,y}g
we denote its time frequency shifts

(1.2)

\pi_{x,y}g(t)=e^{2\pi iyt}g(t-x),\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}.

For
\alpha,\,\beta>0
consider the Gabor system

(1.3)

\mathfrak{G}(g;\alpha,\beta)=\{\pi_{\alpha m,\beta n}g;\,m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

We say that
\mathfrak{G}(g;\alpha,\beta)
is a
frame
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
if the frame inequality

(1.4)

A\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{m,n}\left|\langle f,\pi_{\alpha m,\beta n}g\rangle\right|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2},\ f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

holds for some
A>0
and
B<\infty
.

Gabor systems have been widely used in signal analysis and quantum mechanics because of time-frequency localization
of its elements
\pi_{\alpha m,\beta n}g
. For sufficiently dense lattices
\alpha\mathbb{Z}\times\beta\mathbb{Z}
the supports
of
\pi_{\alpha m,\beta n}g
"cover" the whole time-frequency plane and
the frame inequality (
1.4
) provides stable reconstruction of a signal
f
from the inner products

\langle f,\pi_{\alpha m,\beta n}g\rangle
. On the other hand
\mathfrak{G}(g;\alpha,\beta)
never forms a frame if
\alpha\beta>1
(see e.g.
[
10
]
).
We refer the reader to
[
3
,
14
,
10
]
, for the detailed history, setting and discussion
of the problem.

1.2.
Frame set

The fundamental problem of the Gabor analysis is to describe the
frame set
of the window
g
:

\mathcal{F}(g)=\{(\alpha,\ \beta);\alpha,\,\beta>0\ {\rm{and}}\ \mathfrak{G}(g;\alpha,\beta)\ {\rm{is\ a\ frame\ in}}\ L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\}.

If
\alpha\beta=1
complete characterization of the frame set can be given in terms of Zak tarnsform
\mathcal{Z}g
of the window
g

(see e.g.
[
10
, Ch.8]
) but for
\alpha\beta<1
the frame set
\mathcal{F}(g)
may be very complicated even for elementary functions
g
see e.g.
[
2
,
4
]
. Even the simpler question: for which
g
does

\mathcal{F}(g)
contain the whole set
\Pi:=\{(\alpha,\beta);\alpha,\beta>0,\ \alpha\beta<1\}
is also very difficult.

The answer has been obtained for the Gaussian
e^{-x^{2}}

[
16
,
22
,
23
]
, truncated
\chi_{(0,\infty)}(x)e^{-x}
and symmetric
e^{-|x|}
exponential functions
[
6
,
7
]
, the hyperbolic secant
(e^{x}+e^{-x})^{-1}

[
9
]
. Despite numerous efforts very little progress has been done until 2011. A breakthrough was achieved in
[
12
]
and later in
[
11
]
where the authors
considered the class of totally positive functions of finite type and, by using another approach, Gaussian totally positive functions of finite type.
These results can be viewed as a contribution to the original conjecture of Daubechies which relates the frame property to the positivity of function and its Fourier transform. Our results are to large extend motivated by
[
12
]
since the Fourier transforms of totally positive functions of finite type have the form
g(t)=P(t)^{-1}
, where
P
is a polynomial with simple zeroes located on the imaginary axis, such functions of course admit representation (
1.1
).

1.3.
Herglotz functions

We suggest another approach based on techniques on interpolation by entire functions and dynamical systems. This approach allows us to describe the
frame set for Herglotz functions, study the frames with irrational densities as well as some other special cases.

By Herglotz function we mean a function of the form (
1.1
) for which
a_{k}>0
. Such functions appear naturally in the spectral theory of the Jacobi matrices and the Shroedinger equations.
This class is in a sense opposite to the class of totally positive functions: while the coefficients
a_{k}
in the representation (
1.1
) of the totally positive functions have interlacing signs (and also satisfy a number of additional relations), they are just positive in the case of Herglotz functions. We will consider Herglotz functions with poles in the upper half-plane. It seems that we encountered another kind of positivity related to the Gabor frame property.

1.4.
Main results

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
g

be a Herglotz function

(1.5)

g(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{a_{k}}{t-iw_{k}},\quad a_{k}>0,w_{k}>0.

Then

(1.6)

\mathcal{F}(g)=\{(\alpha,\ \beta);\alpha\beta\leq 1\}.

For the general function of the form (
1.1
) relation (
1.6
) does not hold generally speaking. Amazingly
we
almost always
have the frame property if
\alpha\beta\not\in\mathbb{Q}
:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
g
be of the form (
1.1
) and be such that
m_{0}(\xi)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}e^{2\pi\xi w_{k}}\neq 0
,
\xi>0
and
\Re w_{k}\neq\Re w_{l}
for
k\neq l
. Then the Gabor system
\mathfrak{G}(g;\alpha,\beta)
is a frame in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for any
(\alpha,\beta)\in\Pi
such that
\alpha\beta\not\in\mathbb{Q}
.

Observe that, if
\Re w_{k}<0
for all
k
, we have
m_{0}(\xi)=\hat{g}(\xi)
,
\xi>0
.
We normalize the Fourier transform as

\hat{g}(\xi)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(t)e^{-2\pi it\xi}dt.

The next result is an important particular case of Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
g

be a function of the form (
1.1
),
\Re w_{k}<0
,
k=1,2,\ \ldots\ ,N
,
\Re w_{k}\neq\Re w_{j}
for
j\neq k
and also

\hat{g}(\xi)\neq 0
for

\xi>0
.
Then
\mathfrak{G}(g;\alpha,\beta)
is a frame in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for all
(\alpha,\beta)\in\Pi
, such that
\alpha\beta\not\in\mathbb{Q}
.

So, for the given class of functions Daubechies conjecture holds literally. Later we will see that the assumption

\alpha\beta\not\in\mathbb{Q}
cannot be dropped generally speaking.

1.5.
Near the critical hyperbola

Another interesting question is related to the frame property of
\mathfrak{G}(g;\alpha,\beta)
when the point
(\alpha,\beta)
approaches the critical hyperbola
\alpha\beta=1
.

Let
g
be of the form (
1.1
). Consider the function

\mathcal{Z}(z,\xi)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{a_{k}e^{2\pi\xi w_{k}}}{1-ze^{2\pi w_{k}/\penalty\alpha}}.

In case
\Re w_{k}<0
,
k=1,...,N
this function coincides with the classical Zak transform of
g
up to a non-zero factor.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Re w_{k}>0
,
k=1,...,N
and also
\Re\mathcal{Z}(e^{2\pi it},\xi)>0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
. Then there exists
\alpha_{0}<1
such that
\mathfrak{G}(g;\alpha,1)
is a frame in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for all
\alpha\in(\alpha_{0},1]
.

By the natural renormalization we have

\mathfrak{G}(g(\cdot/\penalty\beta);\alpha,\beta)\text{ is a frame in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

if
\alpha\beta\in(\alpha_{0},1]
.

Combining this statement with Theorem
1.4
and some of its corollaries one can obtain.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
g
be of the form (
1.1
),
\Re w_{k}<0
,
k=1,...,N
,
\hat{g}(\xi)\neq 0
,
\xi>0
,
\Re w_{k}\neq\Re w_{j}
for
j\neq k
and also

\Re\mathcal{Z}(e^{2\pi it},\xi)>0,\quad t\in\mathbb{R},\xi\in\mathbb{R}

holds. Then
\mathfrak{G}(g;\alpha,1)
is a frame in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for all
\alpha\in(0,1]
, except perhaps a finite number of
exceptional values.

We also want to highlight the following corollary of Theorem
1.5
.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
g
be of the form (
1.1
),
w_{k}<0
,
a_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
. If
\hat{g}
is positive, decreasing, convex function on the positive semiaxis
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, then
\mathfrak{G}(g;\alpha,\beta)
is a frame for any pair
(\alpha,\beta)
sufficiently close
to the critical hyperbola
\alpha\beta=1
, (i.e. for
\alpha>\alpha(\beta)
).

1.6.
Large densities

Previous theorems deal with the Gabor frames generated by rational functions.
On the other hand it is known that there exists non-frame rational Gabor systems, in particular, if
g(t)=-\overline{g(-t)}
and
g(t)=O(t^{-2})
,
t\rightarrow\infty
, the system
\mathfrak{G}(g;\alpha,\beta)
does not constitute a frame
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for
\alpha\beta=\frac{n-1}{n},n=2,3,...
see
[
18
]
.
Note that in these cases the density of the lattice giving a non-frame Gabor system is a most
2
. It is known that for
an arbitrary (Wiener) window
g
the system
\mathfrak{G}(g;\alpha,\beta)

is a frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
if the density of lattice
\alpha\mathbb{Z}\times\beta\mathbb{Z}
exceeds some critical one
(depending on
g
and
\beta
of course). A lot of efforts have been spent in order to determine this critical density, see e.g.
[
1
,
3
,
21
]
.
Our approach allows us to construct non-frame rational Gabor systems
\mathfrak{G}(g;\alpha,\beta)
with lattices of arbitrary large density (see Proposition
5.3
). This situation becomes different if we restrict the number of summands in (
1.1
).

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
g
be of the form (
1.1
),
\Re w_{k}\neq\Re w_{l}
,
k\neq l
. Then

\biggl\{(\alpha,\beta):\alpha\beta\leq\frac{1}{N}\biggr\}\subset\mathcal{F}_{g}.

This therorem is almost precise: we will see that there exists a window
g
of the form (
1.1
) and
\alpha,\beta>0

with
\alpha\beta=\frac{1}{N-1}
(and
\Re w_{k}\neq\Re w_{l}
,
k\neq l
) such that the corresponding Gabor system
\mathfrak{G}(g;\alpha,\beta)
does not constitute a frame in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
(see Proposition
5.3
). Thus,
for the rational window one can explicitly find the lower bound for the density which guarantees
the frame property of the corresponding Gabor system. This gives a partial answer to a question formulated in
[
3
]
.

1.7.
Concluding Remarks

Infinite number of poles

We are able to generalize Theorem
1.6
to class of Herglotz functions
with infinite number of poles.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
w_{k}>0
be an increasing separated sequence,
w_{k+1}-w_{k}\geq 1
and also
0<a_{k}<2^{-2^{2^{w_{k}}}}
. Then for all
\alpha,\beta
with
0<\alpha\beta\leq 1
the system generated by the function

g(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{x-iw_{k}}

is a frame.

The proof is based on Theorem
1.6
and some perturbative arguments.
The detailed proofs will appear elsewhere.

Two kernels

Using our approach we can describe the frame set
\mathcal{F}_{g}
for all functions
g(x)=\frac{a_{1}}{x-iw_{1}}+\frac{a_{2}}{x-iw_{2}}
,
a_{1},a_{2},w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
.
In particular, for
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
we have
\Pi\subset\mathcal{F}_{g}
. The detailed proofs will appear elsewhere.

Completeness

In contrast to the frame property we always have the completeness of rational Gabor systems.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let function
g(x)=\sum\frac{a_{k}}{x-iw_{k}}
be such that
\Re w_{k}\neq\Re w_{l},k\neq l
. Then the system
\mathfrak{G}(g;\alpha,\beta)
is complete in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
if and only if
\alpha\beta\leq 1
.

Multiple poles

The right hand-side of (
1.1
) is the general form of a rational function in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
without multiple poles.
Our approach is applicable for rational functions with multiple poles. However, to avoid inessential technicalities we prefer to consider only rational functions
with simple poles.

2.
Frame Criterion

In this section we give necessary and sufficient conditions for an arbitrary rational function
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
to generate a frame for given
\alpha,\beta
. This is the key step in the proofs of Theorems
1.6
-
1.3
.

Let

(2.1)

g(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{a_{k}}{t-iw_{k}},\ a_{k},w_{k}\in\mathbb{C},\ a_{k}\neq 0,\ \Re w_{k}\neq 0.

This is the general form of a rational function in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
without multiple poles. We add the factor
i
in the denominator for the sake of convenience.

2.1.
Multipliers
m_{s}
and the main criterion

Given
\alpha,\beta>0
,
\alpha\beta\leq 1
,
we study the frame property in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
of the Gabor system

(2.2)

\mathfrak{G}(g;\alpha,\beta)=\{g_{m,n}(t)\}_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}},\ g_{m,n}(t)=e^{2\pi i\beta tn}g(t-\alpha m).

It is immediate that the system
\mathfrak{G}(g;\alpha,\beta)
is a frame if and only if the system
\mathfrak{G}(g_{\beta};\alpha\beta,1)
is a frame,
g_{\beta}(t)=g(t/\beta)
. Since
g_{\beta}
is also a rational function it sufficient to consider only the case
\beta=1
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
which we will assume from now on.

For
k=1,\ \ldots,\ N
and
s=0,\ \ldots,\ N-1
denote

(2.3)

A_{k,s}=(-1)^{s}\sum_{j_{1}<j_{2},...,<j_{s},j_{l}\neq k}e^{\frac{2\pi}{\alpha}(w_{j_{1}}+\ldots+w_{j_{s}})},

the sum is taken over pairwise different
j_{l}
’s such that
j_{l}\neq k
. Put

(2.4)

m_{s}(\xi)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}A_{k,s}e^{2\pi\xi w_{k}}.

Theorem 2.1
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\mathfrak{G}(g;\alpha,1)
is a frame in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,

(ii)

(2.5)

\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}G\left(\xi+n+\frac{s}{\alpha}\right)m_{s}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi\asymp||G||^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},\ G\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

2.2.
Proof of Theorem
2.1
. Step 1

In order to establish the frame property of (
2.2
) we need to prove

(2.6)

\sum_{m,n}|\langle g_{m,n},f\rangle|^{2}\asymp\|f\|^{2},\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

We have

(2.7)

\left(\sum_{m,n}|\langle g_{m,n},f\rangle|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\sup\left\{\left|\sum_{m,n}c_{m,n}\langle g_{m,n},f\rangle\right|;\ \sum_{m,n}|c_{m,n}|^{2}\leq 1\right\}.

2.3.
Step 2.

Given
{\bf c}=\{c_{m,n}\}\in l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z})
we fix
n
and consider

(2.8)

S_{n}=\sum_{m}c_{m,n}\langle g_{m,n},f\rangle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\overline{f(t)}e^{2\pi int}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}\sum_{m}\frac{c_{m,n}}{t-(\alpha m+iw_{k})}dt.

Denote

(2.9)

p_{j}(t)=1-e^{i\frac{2\pi}{\alpha}(t-iw_{j})};\ P(t)=\prod_{j=1}^{N}p_{j}(t).

We represent
P(t)p_{j}(t)^{-1}
as a trigonometric polynomial

(2.10)

P_{k}(t)=P(t)p_{k}(t)^{-1}=\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}A_{k,s}e^{i\frac{2\pi}{\alpha}st}.

Consider the entire function

(2.11)

h_{n}(t)=\left(1-e^{i\frac{2\pi}{\alpha}t}\right)\sum_{m}\frac{c_{m,n}}{t-\alpha m}.

We have

(2.12)

\sum_{m}\frac{c_{m,n}}{t-(\alpha m+iw_{k})}=\frac{P_{k}(t)}{P(t)}h_{n}(t-iw_{k}),

respectively

(2.13)

S_{n}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{f(t)}}{P(t)}e^{2\pi int}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}P_{k}(t)h_{n}(t-iw_{k})dt.

By combining the classical sampling and the Paley-Wiener theorems we have

(2.14)

h_{n}(t)=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}e^{2\pi it\xi}\check{h}_{n}(\xi)d\xi,

and

(2.15)

\check{h}_{n}\in L^{2}(0,1/\alpha);\ \|\check{h}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(0,1/\alpha)}\asymp\|\{c_{m,n}\}_{m}\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}.

Let
g(t)=f(t)/\overline{P(t)}
. Since
|P(t)|\asymp 1
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
\|f\|\asymp\|g\|
and also

(2.16)

S_{n}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\overline{g(t)}e^{2\pi int}\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}e^{i\frac{2\pi}{\alpha}st}M_{s}(t)dt,

where

(2.17)

M_{s}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}A_{k,s}h_{n}(t-iw_{k})=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}e^{2\pi i\xi t}\check{h}_{n}(\xi)\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}A_{k,s}e^{2\pi\xi w_{k}}d\xi.

2.4.
Step 3.

The Parseval’s identity now yields

(2.18)

S_{n}=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left[\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}\overline{G\left(\xi+n+\frac{s}{\alpha}\right)}m_{s}(\xi)\right]\check{h}_{n}(\xi)d\xi,

where

G
is the Fourier transform of
g
which satisfies

\|G\|_{L^{2}}=\|g\|_{L^{2}}\asymp\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Finally,

(2.19)

\sum_{m,n}|\langle g_{m,n},f\rangle|^{2}\asymp\sum_{n}\left[\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}\overline{G\left(\xi+n+\frac{s}{\alpha}\right)}m_{s}(\xi)\right]\check{h}_{n}(\xi)d\xi.

2.5.
Step 4.

Observe that the sequence
\{\check{h}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
runs through the whole
l^{2}(\mathbb{Z},L^{2}(0,1/\alpha))
as
\{c_{m,n}\}
runs through the whole

l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z})
and also
\|\{\check{h}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z},L^{2}(0,1/\alpha))}\asymp\|\{c_{m,n}\}\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z})}
.

Set

(2.20)

\check{h}_{n}(\xi)=\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}G\left(\xi+n+\frac{s}{\alpha}\right)\overline{m_{s}(\xi)}.

We have
\|\{\check{h}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z},L^{2}(0,1/\alpha))}\asymp\|G\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\asymp\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
and, hence, the system
\mathfrak{G}(g;\alpha,1)
is a frame if and only if

(2.21)

\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}G\left(\xi+n+\frac{s}{\alpha}\right)m_{s}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi\asymp 1,\ G\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\ \|G\|\asymp 1.

We arrive to (
2.5
).

\square

Remark 2.2
.

Since all functions
m_{s}(\xi)
are bounded, the upper estimate in
(ii)
is always true.

3.
Frame property for Herglotz functions

In this section we prove Theorem
1.6
.

3.1.
Frobenius matrices

The proof of Theorem
1.6
is based on the Lemma
3.1
about Frobenius matrices which was communicated to us by Ivan Bochkov. First we recall the definition of Frobenius matrix.

Definition 1
.

Let
p(z)=z^{n}+b_{n-1}z^{n-1}+\ldots+b_{0}
be a unitary polynomial. The Frobenius matrix associated with
p
is the following matrix

(3.1)

F(p)=\begin{pmatrix}-b_{n-1}&-b_{n-2}&\ldots&-b_{0}\\
1&0&\ldots&0\\
0&1&\ldots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\ldots&0\end{pmatrix}.

We refer the reader to
[
25
]
and
[
19
]
for the detailed presentation
of properties of such matrices. In particular,
p
is the characteristic polynomial of
F(p)
. The next lemma is the key step in the proof of Theorem
1.6
, we also think that it is of independent interest.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let the sequence
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n+1}
be such that
1>\mu_{1}>\mu_{2}>\ldots>\mu_{n+1}>0
. Consider the set
\mathcal{P}
of all polynomials
p(x)=(x-\lambda_{1})(x-\lambda_{2})\ldots(x-\lambda_{n})
such that their zeroes interlace with
\mu_{k}
’s, i.e.
\mu_{k}\geq\lambda_{k}\geq\mu_{k+1}
. Then there exist constants
C>0,0<c<1
depending only on the numbers
\mu_{k}
such that for any polynomials
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\in\mathcal{P}
we have

(3.2)

||F(p_{1})F(p_{2})\ldots F(p_{m})||\leq Cc^{m}.

We do not specify matrix norm here since all norms in finite-dimensional space are equivalent.
We postpone the proof and first obtain Theorem
1.6
from Lemma
3.1
.

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.6
Step 1

As before we assume
\beta=1
,
\alpha\leq 1
and prove the relation (
2.5
).
We truncate the integral in (
2.5
) and prove the stronger estimate

(3.3)

\int\limits_{0}^{1}\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\sum\limits_{s=0}^{N-1}G\left(\xi+l+\frac{s}{\alpha}\right)m_{s}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi\gtrsim||G||_{2}^{2},\quad G\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

We remind that the functions
m_{s}(\xi)
are determined in (
2.4
).

Put

n=N-1,\,\mu_{k}=e^{-2\pi w_{k}/\alpha},k=1,2,\ldots n+1.

We have
1>\mu_{1}>\mu_{2}>\ldots>\mu_{n+1}>0
.
It follows from the positivity of
a_{k}
’s that the roots of the polynomial

p_{\xi}(z)=\frac{m_{0}(\xi)+m_{1}(\xi)z+\ldots+m_{n}(\xi)z^{n}}{m_{n}(\xi)}

satisfy the assumptions of the Lemma
3.1
.

The estimate (
3.3
) is now equivalent to

(3.4)

||LG||_{2}\gtrsim||G||_{2},\,G\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

where the operator
L:L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is given by the formula

(LG)(\xi)=\sum\limits_{s=0}^{n}G\left(\xi+\frac{s}{\alpha}\right)m_{s}(\{\xi\}).

Here
\{\xi\}
denotes the fractional part of
\xi
.

3.3.
Step 2

From the definition of
m_{s}
we have

(3.5)

m_{n}(\{\xi\})=(-1)^{n}e^{\frac{2\pi}{\alpha}(w_{1}+\ldots+w_{N})}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}e^{2\pi\{\xi\}w_{k}-\frac{2\pi}{\alpha}w_{k}}.

The absolute value of
m_{n}
is bounded from above and from below by some positive constants.
Without loss of generality we can consider instead of
LG
the operator
PG

(PG)(\xi)=\frac{(LG)(\xi)}{m_{n}(\{\xi\})}.

It suffices to construct left inverse for
P
, i.e. solve

PG=h,\,h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

We rewrite this equation in the form

(3.6)

G\left(\xi+\frac{n}{\alpha}\right)=h(\xi)-\sum\limits_{s=0}^{n-1}\frac{m_{s}(\{\xi\})}{m_{n}(\{\xi\})}G\left(\xi+\frac{s}{\alpha}\right).

3.4.
Step 3

We transform equation (
3.6
) to a dynamical system.
Consider the vector-functions
\Gamma,H\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{n})
:

\Gamma(\xi)=\begin{pmatrix}G(\xi+\frac{n-1}{\alpha})\\
G(\xi+\frac{n-2}{\alpha})\\
\vdots\\
G(\xi)\end{pmatrix},\qquad H(\xi)=\begin{pmatrix}h(\xi)\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{pmatrix}.

In this notation equation (
3.6
) can be rewritten as

\Gamma\left(\xi+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)=F\left(p_{\{\xi\}}\right)\Gamma(\xi)+H(\xi).

Iterating this formula we get

(3.7)

\Gamma\left(\xi+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)=F\left(p_{\{\xi\}}\right)F\left(p_{\{\xi-\frac{1}{\alpha}\}}\right)\Gamma\left(\xi-\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)+F\left(p_{\{\xi\}}\right)H\left(\xi-\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)+H(\xi).

and

\Gamma\left(\xi+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)=F\left(p_{\{\xi\}}\right)F\left(p_{\{\xi-\frac{1}{\alpha}\}}\right)\ldots F\left(p_{\{\xi-\frac{k}{\alpha}\}}\right)\Gamma\left(\xi-\frac{k}{\alpha}\right)+

\sum\limits_{s=0}^{k}F\left(p_{\{\xi\}}\right)F\left(p_{\{\xi-\frac{1}{\alpha}\}}\right)\ldots F\left(p_{\{\xi-\frac{s-1}{\alpha}\}}\right)H\left(\xi-\frac{s}{\alpha}\right).

By Lemma
3.1
the coefficients in front of
\Gamma(\xi-\frac{k}{\alpha})
and
H(\xi-\frac{s}{\alpha})
decay exponentially. Therefore, we can pass to the limit and get

\Gamma\left(\xi+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)=\sum\limits_{s=0}^{\infty}F\left(p_{\{\xi\}}\right)F\left(p_{\{\xi-\frac{1}{\alpha}\}}\right)\ldots F\left(p_{\{\xi-\frac{s-1}{\alpha}\}}\right)H\left(\xi-\frac{s}{\alpha}\right).

Thus, we get
||\Gamma||_{2}\leq C||H||_{2}=||h||_{2}
. Hence,
||G||_{2}\leq C^{\prime}||h||_{2}=C^{\prime}||PG||_{2}
. That is,
||PG||_{2}\gtrsim||G||_{2}
and, subsequently,
||LG||_{2}\gtrsim||G||_{2}
which is the desired estimate (
3.4
).

\square

3.5.
Proof of Lemma
3.1
. Preliminaries

We are going to construct a norm
||v||_{*}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that for all
p\in\mathcal{P}
we have
||F(p)||_{*}\leq c<1
. This implies by induction that
||F(p_{1})\ldots F(p_{m})||_{*}\leq c^{m}
. Since all norms on the finite-dimensional space are equivalent we get the result.

We will actually construct a norm in which
F(p)^{T}
is contractive uniformly for all
p\in\mathcal{P}
. If we are able to do so, then matrices
F(p)
will be contractive in the dual norm.

The proof consists of two steps. In the first step we show that it is enough to consider only polynomials

p
such that
\lambda_{k}
’s is either
\mu_{k}
or
\mu_{k+1}
for all
k
. Moreover, we will show that among them we can actually study only those for which all
\lambda_{k}
are distinct, that is polynomials
p_{l}(x)=\prod_{k\neq l}(x-\mu_{k})
,
l=1,\ldots,n+1
. In the second step we will construct a norm in which all matrices
F(p_{l})^{T}
are uniformly contractive.

3.6.
Step 1. Reduction to
n+1
matrices.

Let
p(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\lambda_{k})
be an arbitrary polynomial in
\mathcal{P}
. Assume that
\lambda_{k}\neq\mu_{k},\mu_{k+1}
for some
k
. Since
\mu_{k}>\lambda_{k}>\mu_{k+1}
we can find positive numbers
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
with
a+b=1
such that
(x-\lambda_{k})=a(x-\mu_{k})+b(x-\mu_{k+1})
. Denoting
q(x)=\frac{x-\mu_{k}}{x-\lambda_{k}}p(x)
and
r(x)=\frac{x-\mu_{k+1}}{x-\lambda_{k}}p(x)
we get
p(x)=aq(x)+br(x)
and therefore

F(p)^{T}=aF(q)^{T}+bF(r)^{T}
. Repeating this procedure with
q
and
r
we can express
F(p)^{T}
as a convex combination of matrices of the form
F(\rho)^{T}
where
\rho\in\mathcal{P}
and all its roots are from

\{\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n+1}\}
. By the triangle inequality if
F(\rho)^{T}
are contractive for all such polynomials
\rho
then
F(p)^{T}
is contractive as well.

Now we show that it is enough to consider only the polynomials
p_{l}
. Let us consider all
2^{n}
polynomials
p\in\mathcal{P}
with
\lambda_{k}=\mu_{k}
or
\lambda_{k}=\mu_{k+1}
and denote by
K
the convex hull of the corresponding matrices
F(p)^{T}
. Since it is a convex hull of finitely many points it is a polytope. It is well-known that any polytope is a convex hull of its vertices which are exactly the points that can not be written as a convex combination of other points from this polytope.

Let
p(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x-\lambda_{k})
be such that
\lambda_{k}=\mu_{k}
or
\lambda_{k}=\mu_{k+1}
for all
k
and moreover
\lambda_{l}=\lambda_{l+1}=\mu_{l+1}
for some
l
. There exist positive numbers
a,b
such that
a+b=1
and
(x-\mu_{l+1})=a(x-\mu_{l})+b(x-\mu_{l+2})
. Denoting
q(x)=\frac{x-\mu_{l}}{x-\mu_{l+1}}p(x)
and
r(x)=\frac{x-\mu_{l+2}}{x-\mu_{l+1}}p(x)
we get
p(x)=aq(x)+br(x)
and therefore
F(p)^{T}=aF(q)^{T}+bF(r)^{T}
. On the other hand we have
F(q)^{T},F(r)^{T}\in K
and both of them are not equal to
F(p)^{T}
. That is, we decomposed
F(p)^{T}
as a convex combination of other points from
K
. Thus, it is not a vertex of
K
.

Therefore the only possible candidates for the vertices of
K
corresponds to polynomials with distinct roots, that is
p_{l}
’s. That is, all points from
K
are convex combinations of
F(p_{l})^{T}
’s and so if
F(p_{l})^{T}
’s are contractive for all
l
then all other matrices from
K
are contractive as well.

Remark 3.2
.

Instead of appealing to the theory of polytopes one can more carefully decompose
p
into the sum of two other
polynomials such that at each step the number of repeated roots decreases and then continue the process until there are none.

3.7.
Step 2. Construction of the contractive norm for
F(p_{l})^{T}
’s.

Let us identify
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the space
\mathcal{P}_{n-1}
of all polynomials of degree less than
n
in a way that

\alpha=(\alpha_{n-1},\ \ldots\ ,\alpha_{0})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
corresponds to the polynomial

q_{\alpha}(z)=\alpha_{n-1}z^{n-1}+\ldots+\alpha_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{n-1}
. One can see that the action of
F(p)^{T}
on the polynomial

q\in\mathcal{P}_{n-1}

corresponds to the operation

{q(z)\mapsto zq(z)({\rm mod}\ p(z))}.

For each
l=1,2,\ \ldots\ ,n+1
consider the linear functional which sends the polynomial
q\in\mathcal{P}_{n-1}
to
q(\mu_{l})
.
Since these are
n+1
linear functionals on the
n
-dimensional vector space there is a linear dependence between them:

a_{1}q(\mu_{1})+a_{2}q(\mu_{2})+\ldots+a_{n+1}q(\mu_{n+1})=0

for all polynomials
q\in\mathcal{P}_{n-1}
.
Moreover, since the values of
q
at any
n
different points uniquely determine
q
none of
a_{k}
’s vanishes.
Put

||q||_{\mu}=|a_{1}q(\mu_{1})|+\ldots+|a_{n+1}q(\mu_{n+1})|.

Since
q
is uniquely determined by
q(\mu_{1}),\ldots,q(\mu_{n+1})
, this is a norm on
\mathcal{P}_{n-1}
. We show that
||F(p_{l})^{T}q||_{\mu}\leq\mu_{1}||q||_{\mu}
for all
l
. Since
\mu_{1}<1
this implies the result.

Put
r(x)=(F(p_{l})^{T}q)(x)
. We have

r(\mu_{k})=\mu_{k}q(\mu_{k})\text{ for all }k\neq l

and

a_{l}r(\mu_{l})=-\sum_{k\neq l}a_{k}r(\mu_{k}).

Therefore

(3.8)

||r||_{\mu}=\sum\limits_{k\neq l}|\mu_{k}a_{k}q(\mu_{k})|+|a_{l}r(\mu_{l})|=\sum\limits_{k\neq l}|\mu_{k}a_{k}q(\mu_{k})|+|\sum\limits_{k\neq l}a_{k}\mu_{k}q(\mu_{k})|=\\
\mu_{1}\left(\sum\limits_{k\neq l}\left|\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{1}}a_{k}q(\mu_{k})\right|+\left|\sum\limits_{k\neq l}a_{k}\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{1}}q(\mu_{k})\right|\right).

For
s_{k}\in[0,1]
,
x_{k}\in\mathbb{R},k\neq l
we have

\left|\sum_{k\neq l}s_{k}x_{k}\right|-\left|\sum_{k\neq l}x_{k}\right|\leq\left|\sum_{k\neq l}x_{k}(1-s_{k})\right|\leq\sum_{k\neq l}|x_{k}|(1-s_{k}).

Hence,

\sum_{k\neq l}|s_{k}x_{k}|+\left|\sum_{k\neq l}s_{k}x_{k}\right|\leq\sum_{k\neq l}|x_{l}|+\left|\sum_{k\neq l}x_{k}\right|.

Setting
x_{k}=a_{k}q(\mu_{k})
,
s_{k}=\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{1}}
we get

(3.9)

||r||_{\mu}\leq\mu_{1}||q||_{\mu},

as required.

\square

Remark 3.3
.

It is easy to see from the proof that we can choose

. But if two

’s approach each other then constant

may blow up. But one can check carefully analysing the proof of the above lemma that for say

we can choose

depending only on
\mu_{1}
and

.

|m_{s}(\xi)|
can be made smaller than any
\varepsilon>0
if
\delta
is small enough.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

Note that the similar result can be proved for the sum of arbitrary many kernels as long as
\alpha
and
\xi
are close enough to
1
so that we don’t have to worry about additional conditions coming from
\xi\in(1,\frac{1}{\alpha})
.

For fixed
\alpha,w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}
conditions (
5.3
) are three homogenious linear equations in
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
.
For this system to have a nontrivial solution corresponding
3\times 3
determinant has to be zero. We will construct numbers
\alpha,w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}
such that this determinant is zero and
\Re w_{k}
are pairwise different.
Moreover, in our construction we would have
w_{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi}
,
w_{3}=-\frac{1}{2\pi}
and arbitrary
\alpha
(rational or irrational) with
|\alpha-\frac{6}{7}|<\frac{1}{1000}
.

Let us begin with explicitly writing down the matrix corresponding to (
5.3
)

\begin{pmatrix}e^{2\pi w_{1}(\xi_{0}-\frac{2}{\alpha}+2)}&e^{2\pi w_{2}(\xi_{0}-\frac{2}{\alpha}+2)}&e^{2\pi w_{3}(\xi_{0}-\frac{2}{\alpha}+2)}\\
e^{2\pi w_{1}(\xi_{0}-\frac{1}{\alpha}+1)}(e^{\frac{2\pi}{\alpha}w_{2}}+e^{\frac{2\pi}{\alpha}w_{3}})&e^{2\pi w_{2}(\xi_{0}-\frac{1}{\alpha}+1)}(e^{\frac{2\pi}{\alpha}w_{1}}+e^{\frac{2\pi}{\alpha}w_{3}})&e^{2\pi w_{3}(\xi_{0}-\frac{1}{\alpha}+1)}(e^{\frac{2\pi}{\alpha}w_{1}}+e^{\frac{2\pi}{\alpha}w_{2}})\\
e^{2\pi w_{1}\xi_{0}+\frac{2\pi}{\alpha}w_{2}+\frac{2\pi}{\alpha}w_{3}}&e^{2\pi w_{2}\xi_{0}+\frac{2\pi}{\alpha}w_{1}+\frac{2\pi}{\alpha}w_{3}}&e^{2\pi w_{3}\xi_{0}+\frac{2\pi}{\alpha}w_{1}+\frac{2\pi}{\alpha}w_{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

First of all we note that whether this determinant is zero or not does not depend on
\xi_{0}
(in particular it is irrelevant if
0.99<\xi_{0}<1
or not).
Therefore, without loss of generality we can assume that
\xi_{0}=\frac{1}{\alpha}-1
. Expanding the determinant (which we view as a function of
w_{1}
) and dividing it by
e^{\frac{2\pi}{\alpha}(w_{1}+w_{2}+w_{3})}
we get

F(w_{1})=e^{2\pi w_{1}}(e^{-2\pi w_{2}}-e^{-2\pi w_{3}})-e^{-2\pi w_{1}}(e^{2\pi w_{2}}-e^{2\pi w_{3}})-e^{\varepsilon 2\pi w_{1}}(e^{-\varepsilon 2\pi w_{2}}

-e^{-\varepsilon 2\pi w_{3}})+e^{-\varepsilon 2\pi w_{1}}(e^{\varepsilon 2\pi w_{2}}-e^{\varepsilon 2\pi w_{3}})+C,

where
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{\alpha}-1
and
C
is a constant such that
F(w_{2})=F(w_{3})=0
.

Let us first put
\alpha=\frac{6}{7}
. In that case
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{6}
and thus
e^{2\pi w_{1}}=(e^{\varepsilon 2\pi w_{1}})^{6}
. Denoting
e^{\varepsilon 2\pi w_{1}}=z
and recalling that
w_{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi}
,
w_{3}=-\frac{1}{2\pi}
the equation is rewritten as

(5.6)

(e-e^{-1})(z^{6}+z^{-6})-(e^{1/6}-e^{-1/6})(z+z^{-1})-e^{2}+e^{-2}+e^{1/3}-e^{-1/3}=0.

This equation has solutions
z_{1}=e^{1/6}\approx 1.18,z_{2}=e^{-1/6}\approx 0.85
. But one can numerically verify that it also has negative solutions
z_{3}\approx-1.12
,
z_{4}\approx-0.89
.
They correspond for example to
w_{1}=0.108+3i
and
w_{1}=-0.111+3i
respectively. For any
\alpha
close to
\frac{6}{7}
we can find a close solution by the argument principle.

So, we proved the following theorem.

Theorem 5.7
.

There exists a rational window
g
of degree
3
and irrational number
\alpha<1
such that

\mathfrak{G}(g;\alpha,1)
is not a frame.

6.
Sampling in shift-invariant spaces

The results on Gabor frames allow us to obtain new theorems on sampling in shift-invaraint spaces,
generated by rational windows. We follow mainly the pattern of
[
10
]
, which in turns relies on the Janssen’s version of duality theory.

First, we remind the basic definitions. Given a function
g
which belongs to the Wiener amalgam space
W_{0}=W(\ell^{1},\mathbb{C})
i.e.
g
is continuous
and

(6.1)

\|g\|_{W_{0}}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\max_{x\in[k,k+1]}|g(x)|<\infty.

Consider the shift-invariant space
V^{2}(g)
which consists of the functions of the form

(6.2)

f(t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{k}g(t-k),\quad\{c_{k}\}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}).

Clearly,
V^{2}(g)\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and

(6.3)

\|f\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|g\|_{W_{0}}\|\{c_{k}\}\|_{2}.

We will assume the following stability of the generator
g
:

Proposition 6.1

(see e.g.
[
20
]
)
.

The following properties are equivalent

(i)

There exists
C>0
such that

(6.4)

\|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{k}g(t-k)\|\geq C\|\{c_{k}\}\|_{2};

(ii)

(6.5)

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\hat{g}(\xi-k)|^{2}>0\quad\text{ for all }\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

We say that a sequence
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}
is called
separated
if for some
\delta>0
,
|\lambda-\mu|>\delta
,
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda,\lambda\neq\mu
.

A separated sequence
\Lambda
is called
sampling
for
V^{2}(g)
if there exist constants
A,B>0
such that

(6.6)

A\|f\|^{2}_{2}\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|f(\lambda)|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2}_{2},\quad f\in V^{2}(g).

In what follows we apply these definitions to the sequences
\alpha\mathbb{Z}
,
\alpha<1
and spaces
V^{2}(g)
generated by the rational function
g
.

The relation between sampling and frame properties is given by the following statement.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
g\in W_{0}
has stable integer shifts. The following are equivalent:

(i)

The family
\mathfrak{G}(g;\alpha,1)
is a frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
;

(ii)

There exists
A,B>0
such that for each
x\in[0,1]
and
f
of the form (
6.2
)

(6.7)

A\|\{c_{k}\}\|^{2}_{2}\leq\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}|f(x-\alpha m)|^{2}\leq B\|\{c_{k}\}\|^{2}_{2}.

We refer the reader to (now) classical article
[
11
]
for the proof of this proposition
and also to
[
10
]
for more general sequences.

We combine Proposition
6.2
with Theorems
1.3
and
1.2
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let the function
g
have the form (
1.1
) and, in addition
g\in W_{0}

and
m_{0}(\xi)\neq 0
,
\xi>0
. Then for each
\alpha\not\in\mathbb{Q}
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
there exist
A_{\alpha},B_{\alpha}>0

such that

(6.8)

A_{\alpha}\|\{c_{k}\}\|^{2}_{2}\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|f(\alpha k)|^{2}\leq B_{\alpha}\|\{c_{k}\}\|^{2}_{2}

for each function
f
of the form
f(t)=\sum_{k}c_{k}g(t-k)
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that
g
admits stable sampling, for example check the inequality (
6.5
). We have

\hat{g}(\xi)=\begin{cases}\sum_{w_{k}<0}a_{k}e^{2\pi\xi w_{k}},\quad\xi>0\\
-\sum_{w_{k}>0}a_{k}e^{2\pi\xi w_{k}},\quad\xi<0\end{cases}.

each sum in the right hand-side has the leading term as
|\xi|\rightarrow\infty
(it corresponds to the smallest
|w_{k}|
).
Thus, we have (
6.5
).
∎

Theorem 6.4
.

Let the function
g
have the form (
1.1
) and, in addition,
g\in W_{0}
and
m_{0}(\xi)\neq 0
,
\xi>0
.
Then, for each
\alpha\in(0,1)
the set
\alpha\mathbb{Z}
is a sampling for
V^{2}(g)
i.e. there exist
A_{\alpha},B_{\alpha}>0

such that

(6.9)

A_{\alpha}\|\{c_{k}\}\|^{2}_{2}\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|f(\alpha k)|^{2}\leq B_{\alpha}\|\{c_{k}\}\|^{2}_{2},\quad f\in V^{2}(g).

Proof.

The left-hand side inequality is a direct consequemce of (
6.8
) and (
6.3
). The proof of the right-hand side
inequality follows the classical Plancherel-Polya pattern. Each function
f\in V^{2}(g)
has the form

(6.10)

f(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{c_{n}}{t-(n+iw_{k})}

and thus admits analytic continuation to
\mathbb{C}\setminus\cup_{k=1}^{N}(\mathbb{Z}+iw_{k})
.
Denote

q(x)=1-e^{2\pi iz},\quad Q(z)=\prod_{k=1}^{N}q(z-iw_{k})

and consider an entire function of exponential type which in addition belongs to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

(6.11)

F(z)=Q(z)f(z).

Fucntion
F
belongs to Paley-Wiener space
\mathcal{PW}_{a}
for some
a
and, hence, satisfies Plancherel-Polya inequality

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|F(\alpha k)|^{2}\lesssim\|F\|^{2}_{2}.

∎

Remark 6.5
.

It follows from
[
18
]
that condition
\alpha\not\in\mathbb{Q}
cannot be omitted in general. On the other hand the right inequality
in (
6.9
) is not related to irrationality of
\alpha
.

Remark 6.6
.

Relation (
6.8
) can be viewed as a statement on sampling by linear combination of values of functions in the Paley-Wiener space. Problems of such type appear in study of eigenfunction expansions of operator pencils, see e.g.
[
17
]
.

Indeed, given
f
of the form (
6.10
) we denote

H(z)=q(z)\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{c_{n}}{z-n}.

We than have

H\in\mathcal{PW}_{[0,1]},\quad\|H\|_{2}\asymp\|\{c_{k}\}\|_{\ell^{2}},

and (
6.9
) can be read as

\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}|\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}q(\alpha j-iw_{k})^{-1}H(\alpha j-iw_{k})|^{2}\asymp\|H\|^{2}_{2}.

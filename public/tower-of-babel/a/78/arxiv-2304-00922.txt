Abstract




Abstract.
We study nowhere-zero integer eigenvectors of the block graphs of Steiner triple systems and the Johnson graphs. For the first eigenvalue we obtain the minimums
of the
L_{\infty}
norm for several infinite series of Johnson graphs, including
J(n,3)
for all
n\geq 63
, as well as general upper and lower bounds.
The minimization of the
L_{\infty}
norm for nowhere-zero integer eigenvectors with the second eigenvalue of the block graph of a Steiner triple system
S
is equivalent to finding the minimum nowhere-zero flow for Steiner triple system
S
. For the all Assmuss-Mattson Steiner
triple systems of the orders greater or equal to
99
we prove that the minimum flow is bounded above by
5
.

Keywords:
Steiner triple system, flow, strongly regular graph, Johnson graph, Grassmann graph, eigenvalue

Definitions and notations

2.1.
Johnson, Grassmann graphs,
q
-ary Steiner triple systems and their block graphs

A regular graph of diameter
d
is called

if there is an array of integers

\{\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{d-1};\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d}\},

such that for any vertices

and

at distance

\beta_{i}
neighbors of

from

at distance

from
x
. The array of integers
\{\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{d-1};\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d}\}

is called the
intersection array
of the distance-regular graph
\Gamma
.
We say that a nonzero vector

is a
\theta(\Gamma)
-
eigenvector
if
A_{\Gamma}v=\theta(\Gamma)v
, where
A_{\Gamma}
is the adjacency matrix of
\Gamma
. In this case

is called an

of
\Gamma
.
It is well known that any distance-regular graph of diameter

has exactly

distinct eigenvalues, which we index
in descending order:
\theta_{0}(\Gamma)>\theta_{1}(\Gamma)>\ldots>\theta_{d}(\Gamma)
. Note that
\theta_{0}(\Gamma)
is the valency of the graph
\Gamma
.

The vertices of
the Grassmann graph

are

-subspaces of the finite vector space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
over the field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and the edges are the pairs of subspaces meeting in

-subspace.
We also include a limit case of

as we define

(also denoted by

-subsets of

and edges being pairs of subsets meeting in
(k-1)
set.
Let
[^{n}_{k}]_{q}
be the Gaussian binomial coefficient and
[^{n}_{k}]_{1}
be the ordinary binomial coefficient.
The

are as follows:

\theta_{i}(J_{q}(n,k))=q^{k+1}[^{k-i}_{1}]_{q}[^{n-k-i}_{1}]_{q}-[^{i}_{1}]_{q},0\leq i\leq k.

By a
q
-ary
Steiner triple system
(briefly, STS)

we mean a collection of
3
-subspaces (called blocks) of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
such that any

-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is in exactly one subspace in

.
By letting

we include Steiner triple system in the traditional sense in the definition above, i.e. a collection of
3
-subsets of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
(called blocks or triples) such that any

-subset is in exactly one block.
A
1
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
(
1
-subset of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
) is called
a point
of
S
.
By

of
S
, we mean the dimension

of the ambient space
F_{q}^{n}
(the number of points for
q=1
).
A Steiner triple system of order
n
(in the classical sense) is called

if its blocks are parted into

, i.e. collections of pairwise nonintersecting blocks with the size of each class equal to
\frac{n}{3}
.

The incidence matrix
of a
q
-ary Steiner triple system

is the matrix

(W_{S})_{i,T}=\begin{cases}1,&i\subseteq T\\

Similarly, we define the point-block incidence matrix

of

with the rows indexed by the points (
1

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}

The

]
and has the following eigenvalues:

\theta_{1}(\Gamma_{S})=\frac{[^{n}_{1}]_{q}-1}{[^{3}_{1}]_{q}-1}-[^{3}_{1}]_{q}-1,

The blocks of any

-ary STS,

could be treated as a set of vertices of

2

2.2.
Completely regular codes

Given

C_{0}=C,\ldots,C_{\rho}

C_{i}=\{x:d(x,C)=i\}.

The maximum of all

of

A subset

is called a

\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{\rho}

\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{\rho-1}

\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{\rho}

\beta_{i}

The array

of the completely
regular code

The following tridiagonal
(\rho+1)\times(\rho+1)

\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}\alpha_{0}&\beta_{0}&0&0&\ldots&0\\
\gamma_{1}&\alpha_{1}&\beta_{1}&0&\ldots&0\\
.&.&.&.&.&.\\
.&.&.&.&.&.\\
0&.&0&\gamma_{\rho-1}&\alpha_{\rho-1}&\beta_{\rho-1}\\
0&.&0&0&\gamma_{\rho}&\alpha_{\rho}\end{array}\right),

is called the

of the completely regular code
C

The eigenvalues of this matrix are

of the completely regular code

Given a set of vertices

its characteristic vector in the vertex set of the graph.

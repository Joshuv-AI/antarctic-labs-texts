S ​ O ​ ( 4 ) SO(4) Symmetry in Hydrogen Atom with Spin
Xing-Yan Fan, Xiang-Ru Xie, Sheng-Ming Li, Jing-Ling Chen

Abstract
As the simplest atom in nature, the hydrogen atom has been explored thoroughly from the perspective of non-relativistic quantum mechanics to relativistic quantum mechanics. Among the research on hydrogen atom, its energy level is the most basic, which can be obtained more conveniently predicated on the S ​ O ​ ( 4 ) SO(4) symmetry than the wave-equation resolution. Moreover, “spin” is another indispensable topic in quantum mechanics, appearing as an intrinsic degree of freedom. In this work, we generalize the quantum Runge-Lenz vector to a spin-dependent one, and then extract a novel Hamiltonian of hydrogen atom with spin based on the requirement of S ​ O ​ ( 4 ) SO(4) symmetry. Furthermore, the energy spectrum of hydrogen atom with spin potentials is also determined by the remarkable approach of S ​ O ​ ( 4 ) SO(4) symmetry. Our findings extend the ground of hydrogen atom, and may contribute to other complicated models based on hydrogen atom.

II
Brief Review of
SO(4)
Symmetry in Hydrogen Atom

The Hamiltonian of HA is given by
[
1
]

H=\dfrac{\vec{p}^{\,2}}{2M}-\dfrac{\kappa}{r},

(1)

where
\vec{p}=(p_{x},p_{y},p_{z})
is the linear momentum operator;
M=[m_{\rm e}m_{\rm p}/(m_{\rm e}+m_{\rm p})]
is the reduced mass with
m_{\rm e}
and
m_{\rm p}
representing the mass of electron and proton, respectively;
\kappa=Ze^{2}
, and for HA,
Z=1
;
e
depicts the absolute value of the charge involving an electron;
r=|\vec{r}|
indicates the norm of coordinate operator
\vec{r}=(r_{x},r_{y},r_{z})
regarding the relative motion between electron and proton.

One may define the orbital angular momentum operator as

\vec{\ell}=\vec{r}\times\vec{p},

(2)

which satisfies the definition of angular momentum

\vec{\ell}\times\vec{\ell}={\rm i}\hbar\,\vec{\ell},

(3)

or in a commutator form as

\left[{\ell}_{u},{\ell}_{v}\right]={\rm i}\hbar\,\epsilon_{uvw}{\ell}_{w},

(4)

with
\epsilon_{uvw}
being the Levi-Civita symbol, and
\epsilon_{xyz}=\epsilon_{yzx}=\epsilon_{zxy}=+1
,
\epsilon_{zyx}=\epsilon_{yxz}=\epsilon_{xzy}=-1
.

Due to

\displaystyle\left[{\ell}_{u},r_{v}\right]={\rm i}\hbar\,\epsilon_{uvw}r_{w},\;\;\;\;\left[{\ell}_{u},p_{v}\right]={\rm i}\hbar\,\epsilon_{uvw}p_{w},

(5)

it is easy to have

\left[\vec{\ell},\,r\right]=0,\;\;\;\left[\vec{\ell},\,\vec{p}^{\,2}\right]=0,

(6)

and then one proves that

\left[H,\,\vec{\ell}\,\right]=0.

(7)

Notably, Eq. (
4
) implies that a HA system possesses
SO(3)
symmetry.

However, the HA can have a hidden symmetry of
SO(4)
owing to the existence of the quantum Runge-Lenz (QRL) vector, defined by
[
6
,
7
]

\displaystyle\vec{R}=\dfrac{1}{2M}\left(\vec{p}\times\vec{\ell}-\vec{\ell}\times\vec{p}\right)-\kappa\,\dfrac{\vec{r}}{r},

(8)

which satisfies

\displaystyle\vec{R}\times\vec{R}=\dfrac{-2H}{M}{\rm i}\hbar\,\vec{\ell},

(9)

and

\displaystyle\left[H,\vec{R}\right]=0,

(10a)

\displaystyle\vec{\ell}\cdot\vec{R}=\vec{R}\cdot\vec{\ell}=0,

(10b)

\displaystyle\vec{R}^{\,2}=\dfrac{2H}{M}\left(\vec{\ell}^{\,2}+\hbar^{2}\right)+\kappa^{2},

(10c)

\displaystyle\left[{\ell}_{u},\,{R}_{v}\right]={\rm i}\hbar\,\epsilon_{uvw}R_{w}.

(10d)

Since
\{\vec{\ell},\vec{R}\}
satisfy the following
SO(4)
algebraic relations (up to a re-scaling factor
\sqrt{M/(-2H)}
multiplied together
\vec{R}
)

\displaystyle\left[{\ell}_{u},{\ell}_{v}\right]={\rm i}\hbar\,\epsilon_{uvw}{\ell}_{w},

(11a)

\displaystyle\left[{\ell}_{u},{R}_{v}\right]={\rm i}\hbar\,\epsilon_{uvw}{R}_{w},

(11b)

\displaystyle\left[{R}_{u},{R}_{v}\right]=\frac{-2H}{M}{\rm i}\hbar\,\epsilon_{uvw}{\ell}_{w},

(11c)

and thus the HA system possesses
SO(4)
symmetry.

III
An Inverse Problem

A HA is a real physical system existing in nature. The Hamiltonian (
1
) of a HA can be recast to

H=\dfrac{\vec{p}^{\,2}}{2M}+V_{\rm cp}(r),

(12)

where

V_{\rm cp}(r)=-\dfrac{\kappa}{r}

(13)

represents the well-known Coulomb potential (CP). In the previous section, we have considered a
forward problem
: Given the Hamiltonian of a HA, can one determine its underlying symmetry? The answer is positive. The underlying symmetry is just
SO(4)
symmetry, as shown in Eqs. (
11a
)-(
11c
).

In this section, let us consider an
inverse problem
: Given the underlying
SO(4)
symmetry, can one construct the Hamiltonian of a HA (or derive the CP)? The answer is still positive. The key point is the QRL vector; if one focuses on Eq. (
9
), he will find that the Hamiltonian
H
can be extracted from
\vec{R}\times\vec{R}
by requiring the result proportional to
H\,\vec{\ell}
. The following is the procedure of construction.

Without lose of generality, let us define the QRL vector in the following form:

\displaystyle\vec{R}=\dfrac{1}{2M}\left(\vec{p}\times\vec{\ell}-\vec{\ell}\times\vec{p}\right)+f(r)\,\vec{r},

(14)

where
f(r)
is a certain function depending on
r
, and we only require

\displaystyle[\vec{\ell},f(r)]=0.

(15)

If one compares Eq. (
8
) and Eq. (
14
), one may notice that

\displaystyle f(r)=-\dfrac{\kappa}{r}.

(16)

However, suppose we do not know it in the beginning, thus temporarily treating
f(r)
as an unknown function.

By direct calculation, we easily have

\displaystyle[{\ell}_{u},\ {\ell}_{v}]={\rm i}\hbar\,\epsilon_{uvw}\,{\ell}_{w},

(17)

\displaystyle[{\ell}_{u},\ {R}_{v}]={\rm i}\hbar\,\epsilon_{uvw}\,{R}_{w}.

(18)

The underlying
SO(4)
symmetry requires the following commutative relation

\displaystyle[{R}_{u},\ {R}_{v}]\propto{\rm i}\hbar\,\epsilon_{uvw}\,{\ell}_{w},

(19)

i.e.,
[{R}_{u},\ {R}_{v}]
is required to be proportional to
\ell_{w}
, and the proportional coefficient is related to the Hamiltonian
H
of a HA. To reach this purpose, let us compute the commutator
[{R}_{x},\ {R}_{y}]
, or
\vec{R}\times\vec{R}
.

The result gives

\displaystyle\vec{R}\times\vec{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\rm i}\hbar\dfrac{2}{M}\biggl(\dfrac{\vec{p}^{\,2}}{2\,M}+\dfrac{1}{2}\Bigl\{\big[\vec{\nabla}f(r)\big]\cdot\vec{r}+3\,f(r)\Bigr\}\biggr)\vec{\ell}

(20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{-2H}{M}{\rm i}\hbar\,\vec{\ell},

based on which we can immediately extract a Hamiltonian as

\displaystyle H=\dfrac{\vec{p}^{\,2}}{2M}+{\cal V}(r),

(21)

with the potential function

\displaystyle{\cal V}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\Bigl\{\big[\vec{\nabla}f(r)\big]\cdot\vec{r}+3\,f(r)\Bigr\}

(22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\Bigl\{rf^{\prime}(r)+3\,f(r)\Bigr\}.

It is easy to prove that

\displaystyle\left[\vec{\ell},H\right]=0.

(23)

Furthermore, we use the following condition

\displaystyle\left[\vec{R},H\right]=0

(24)

to determine the function
f(r)
, for
\vec{R}
is the conservation quantity of the Hamiltonian system. One can have

\displaystyle[\vec{R},H]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{-{\rm i}\hbar}{M}\left[\vec{\nabla}\left[{\cal V}(r)-f(r)\right]\cdot\vec{p}\right]\vec{r}

(25)

\displaystyle+\dfrac{{\rm i}\hbar}{M}\left[\left(\vec{\nabla}{\cal V}(r)\right)\cdot\vec{r}+f(r)\right]\vec{p}

\displaystyle+\dfrac{\hbar^{2}}{2M}\left(\vec{\nabla}^{2}f(r)\right)\vec{r},

and thus

\displaystyle\vec{\nabla}\left[{\cal V}(r)-f(r)\right]=0,

(26a)

\displaystyle\left(\vec{\nabla}{\cal V}(r)\right)\cdot\vec{r}+f(r)=0,

(26b)

\displaystyle\vec{\nabla}^{2}f(r)=0,

(26c)

which lead to

\displaystyle{\cal V}(r)=f(r)=\dfrac{C}{r}.

(27)

Consequently, by choosing
C=-\kappa
we have successfully constructed the Hamiltonian of a HA by starting from the QRL vector.

This significant finding of the inverse problem is very helpful for studying a more complicated Hamiltonian under the constraint of
SO(4)
symmetry. In the next section, based on
SO(4)
symmetry we will construct the Hamiltonian of HA with spin.

IV
Constructing the Hamiltonian of Hydrogen Atom with Spin

To construct the Hamiltonian of HA with spin, we need to generalize the orbital angular momentum
\vec{\ell}
and the spin-independent QPL vector
\vec{R}
to the spin-dependent ones, namely,
\vec{\ell}\Rightarrow\vec{J}
and
\vec{R}\Rightarrow\vec{\mathcal{R}}
, where
\vec{J}
and
\vec{\mathcal{R}}
depend on the spin angular momentum operator
\vec{S}
, which satisfies the definition of angular momentum

\vec{S}\times\vec{S}={\rm i}\hbar\,\vec{S},

(28)

or in a commutator form as

\left[{S}_{u},{S}_{v}\right]={\rm i}\hbar\,\epsilon_{uvw}{S}_{w}.

(29)

Our main result is the following theorem:

Theorem 1
.

Let

\displaystyle\vec{J}=\vec{\ell}+\mu\vec{S},

(30a)

\displaystyle\vec{\cal R}=\dfrac{1}{2\,M}\big(\vec{\Pi}\times\vec{J}-\vec{J}\times\vec{\Pi}\big)+h(r)\,\vec{r},

(30b)

and then they satisfy the
SO(4)
commutation relations (up to a re-scaling factor multiplied together
\vec{\cal R}
)

\displaystyle[J_{u},\ J_{v}]={\rm i}\hbar\,\epsilon_{uvw}J_{w},

(31a)

\displaystyle[J_{u},\ {\cal R}_{v}]={\rm i}\hbar\,\epsilon_{uvw}{\cal R}_{w},

(31b)

\displaystyle[{\cal R}_{u},\ {\cal R}_{v}]\propto{\rm i}\hbar\,\epsilon_{uvw}{\cal H}\,J_{w},

(31c)

and

\displaystyle[\vec{J},\,{\cal H}]=0,

(32a)

\displaystyle[\vec{\cal R},\,{\cal H}]=0.

(32b)

Here the Hamiltonian of HA with spin reads

\displaystyle{\cal H}=\dfrac{\vec{\Pi}^{2}}{2\,M}+{\cal V}(r),

(33)

with

\displaystyle\vec{\Pi}=\vec{p}-\vec{A},

(34a)

\displaystyle\vec{A}=\mu\dfrac{(\vec{r}\times\vec{S})}{r^{2}},

(34b)

\displaystyle h(r)=\dfrac{k_{1}}{r}+k_{2}\,\mu\,\dfrac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{S}}{r^{2}},

(34c)

\displaystyle{\cal V}(r)=h(r)+\mu\dfrac{1}{2\,M}\dfrac{(\vec{r}\cdot\vec{S})^{2}}{r^{4}},

(34d)

k_{1}
and
k_{2}
are two constants, and
\mu=0,1
.

Proof.

We divide five steps to complete the proof.

Step 1: Construction of
\vec{J}
and Proving Eq. (
31a
).
—Since the orbital angular momentum
\vec{\ell}
and the spin operator
\vec{S}
are defined in the external and internal spaces respectively, they are mutually commutative. If we define the “total” angular momentum operator as

\displaystyle\vec{J}=\vec{\ell}+\vec{S},

(35)

then one immediately proves Eq. (
31a
). Here we have designed
\vec{J}
in the form of Eq. (
30a
) with the parameter
\mu
, to treat the HAs with and without spin in a unified way; namely,
\vec{J}
reduces to
\vec{\ell}
if
\mu=0
, and becomes Eq. (
35
) if
\mu=1
.

Step 2: Construction of
\vec{\cal R}
and Proving Eq. (
31b
).
—Similar to Ref.
[
18
]
, one can recast
\vec{J}
to the following form

\displaystyle\vec{J}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\vec{\ell}+\mu\vec{S}

(36)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\vec{r}\times(\vec{p}-\vec{A})+\mu\,\vec{e}_{r}S_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\vec{r}\times\vec{\Pi}+\mu\,\vec{e}_{r}S_{r};

then one extracts “canonical” momentum
\vec{\Pi}
of the electron in HA as in Eq. (
34a
), and the spin vector potential
\vec{A}
induced by spin operator
\vec{S}
of the proton in HA as Eq. (
34b
). Here the operator

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{r}=\hat{r}\cdot\vec{S},

(37)

and
\hat{r}=\vec{e}_{r}=\vec{r}/r
.

After performing the following replacement

\displaystyle\vec{p}\Rightarrow\vec{\Pi},\quad\vec{\ell}\Rightarrow\vec{J},\quad f(r)\Rightarrow h(r),

(38)

the QPL vector
\vec{R}
is turned into the generalized QPL vector
\vec{\cal R}
as shown in Eq. (
30b
), with
h(r)
being a function of
r
and
(\vec{r}\cdot\vec{S})
satisfying the commutation relation

\displaystyle[\vec{J},\,h(r)]=0.

(39)

Due to the relations

\displaystyle[J_{u},\ J_{v}]={\rm i}\hbar\,\epsilon_{uvw}J_{w},

(40a)

\displaystyle[J_{u},\ \Pi_{v}]={\rm i}\hbar\,\epsilon_{uvw}\Pi_{w},

(40b)

\displaystyle[J_{u},\ (\vec{\Pi}\times\vec{J})_{v}]={\rm i}\hbar\,\epsilon_{uvw}(\vec{\Pi}\times\vec{J})_{w},

(40c)

\displaystyle[J_{u},\ (\vec{J}\times\vec{\Pi})_{v}]={\rm i}\hbar\,\epsilon_{uvw}(\vec{J}\times\vec{\Pi})_{w},

(40d)

\displaystyle[J_{u},\ r_{v}]={\rm i}\hbar\,\epsilon_{uvw}r_{w},

(40e)

one directly proves Eq. (
31b
).

Step 3: Proving Eq. (
31c
).
—After careful deductions, one has

\displaystyle\vec{\cal R}\times\vec{\cal R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm i}\hbar\,\dfrac{-2}{M}\Bigg\{\dfrac{\vec{\Pi}^{2}}{2\,M}+\dfrac{1}{2}\bigg[-\dfrac{1}{{\rm i}\hbar}\big[\vec{\Pi},\ h(r)\big]\cdot\vec{r}+3\,h(r)+\dfrac{\mu}{M}\dfrac{(\vec{r}\cdot\vec{S})^{2}}{r^{4}}\bigg]\Bigg\}\vec{J}-\dfrac{\mu}{M}\biggl\{\big[\vec{\Pi},\ h(r)\big]-{\rm i}\hbar\dfrac{\vec{r}}{r^{2}}h(r)\biggr\}(\vec{r}\cdot\vec{S}).

(41)

To fulfill the requirement of Eq. (
31c
), one must have

\displaystyle{\cal H}=\dfrac{\vec{\Pi}^{2}}{2\,M}+{\cal V}(r),

(42a)

\displaystyle{\cal V}(r)=\dfrac{1}{2}\bigg[-\dfrac{1}{{\rm i}\hbar}\big[\vec{\Pi},\ h(r)\big]\cdot\vec{r}+3\,h(r)+\dfrac{\mu}{M}\dfrac{(\vec{r}\cdot\vec{S})^{2}}{r^{4}}\bigg],

(42b)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mu}{M}\biggl\{\big[\vec{\Pi},\ h(r)\big]-{\rm i}\hbar\dfrac{\vec{r}}{r^{2}}h(r)\biggr\}(\vec{r}\cdot\vec{S})=0.

(42c)

For
\mu=0
, Eq. (
41
) reduces to Eq. (
20
), and thus Eq. (
31c
) is proved.
For
\mu=1
, from Eq. (
42c
) we obtain

\displaystyle\big[\vec{\Pi},\ h(r)\big]-{\rm i}\hbar\dfrac{\vec{r}}{r^{2}}h(r)=0.

(43)

By setting

\displaystyle h(r)=\xi_{1}(r)+\xi_{2}(r)\left(\vec{r}\cdot\vec{S}\right),

(44)

one obtains the solution of
h(r)
as shown in Eq. (
34c
) with
\xi_{1}(r)=k_{1}/r
and
\xi_{2}(r)=\mu k_{2}/r^{2}
, and hence Eq. (
31c
) is proved.
After substituting Eq. (
42c
) into Eq. (
42b
), one has the potential function
{\cal V}
as in Eq. (
34d
).

Step 4: Proving Eq. (
32a
).
—
Due to the relations

\displaystyle[\vec{J},\,\vec{\Pi}^{2}]=0,

(45a)

\displaystyle[\vec{J},\,r]=0,

(45b)

\displaystyle[\vec{J},\,\vec{r}\cdot\vec{S}]=0,

(45c)

one directly proves Eq. (
32a
).

Step 5: Proving Eq. (
32b
).
—
Due to Eq. (
40a
)-Eq. (
40e
), Eq. (
45a
)-Eq. (
45c
), and the following relations

\displaystyle[\Pi_{u},\ r_{v}]=-{\rm i}\hbar\,\delta_{uv},

(46a)

\displaystyle\bigg[\vec{\Pi},\ \dfrac{1}{r}\bigg]={\rm i}\hbar\dfrac{\vec{r}}{r^{3}},

(46b)

\displaystyle\bigg[\vec{\Pi},\ \dfrac{1}{r^{2}}\bigg]={\rm i}\hbar\dfrac{2\,\vec{r}}{r^{4}},

(46c)

\displaystyle[\Pi_{u},\,\Pi_{v}]={\rm i}\hbar\,\epsilon_{uvw}{\cal B}_{w},

(46d)

\displaystyle\vec{\cal B}={\rm i}\hbar\,\mu(\mu-2)\dfrac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{S}}{r^{4}}\vec{r},

(46e)

\displaystyle[\vec{\Pi},\,\vec{\Pi}^{2}]={\rm i}\hbar\big[(\vec{\Pi}\times\vec{\cal B})-(\vec{\cal B}\times\vec{\Pi})\big],

(46f)

\displaystyle\vec{\Pi}\times\vec{J}+\vec{J}\times\vec{\Pi}=2{\rm i}\hbar\,\vec{\Pi},

(46g)

\displaystyle\bigg[\vec{\Pi},\ \dfrac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{S}}{r^{2}}\bigg]={\rm i}\hbar(\mu-1)\dfrac{\vec{S}}{r^{2}}+{\rm i}\hbar(2-\mu)\frac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{S}}{r^{4}}\vec{r},

(46h)

with
\delta_{uv}
denoting the Kronecker delta symbol, one can directly prove Eq. (
32b
).
∎

V
Energy Spectrum of Hydrogen Atom with Spin

In this section, we come to solve the energy spectrum of HA with spin based on the approach of
SO(4)
symmetry. We restrict our problem to the Hilbert space of Hamiltonian
{\cal H}
’s eigenstates, where we can replace
{\cal H}
by its eigenvalue
E
, and then Eq. (
31c
) becomes

\displaystyle[{\cal R}_{u},{\cal R}_{v}]=\dfrac{-2E}{M}{\rm i}\hbar\,\epsilon_{uvw}\,J_{w}.

(47)

If we define a re-scaling operator as

\displaystyle\vec{\cal R}^{\prime}=\sqrt{\dfrac{M}{-2E}}\,\vec{\cal R},

(48)

then
\vec{J}
and
\vec{\cal R}^{\prime}
fit the standard SO(4) algebra.
Let us further define two new quantities as

\displaystyle\vec{W}=\dfrac{1}{2}(\vec{J}+\vec{\cal R}^{\prime}),

(49a)

\displaystyle\vec{K}=\dfrac{1}{2}(\vec{J}-\vec{\cal R}^{\prime}).

(49b)

Then they satisfy the following commutators

\displaystyle[W_{u},\ W_{v}]={\rm i}\hbar\,\epsilon_{uvw}W_{w},

(50a)

\displaystyle[K_{u},\ K_{v}]={\rm i}\hbar\,\epsilon_{uvw}K_{w},

(50b)

\displaystyle[W_{u},\ K_{v}]=0,

(50c)

which implies that
\vec{W}
and
\vec{K}
construct two independent
SU(2)
algebras
[
7
]
; hence the eigenvalues are given as
\vec{W}^{2}=w(w+1)\hbar^{2}
, and
\vec{K}^{2}=k(k+1)\hbar^{2}
, with
k,w=0,1/2,1,3/2,\dots
.

We can have

\displaystyle\vec{W}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{4}(\vec{J}^{2}+\vec{{\cal R}^{\prime}}^{2}+2\vec{J}\cdot\vec{{\cal R}^{\prime}}),

(51a)

\displaystyle\vec{K}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{4}(\vec{J}^{2}+\vec{{\cal R}^{\prime}}^{2}-2\vec{J}\cdot\vec{{\cal R}^{\prime}}).

(51b)

Afterwards, by using

\displaystyle\vec{J}\cdot\vec{{\cal R}^{\prime}}=\vec{{\cal R}^{\prime}}\cdot\vec{J}=\mu\sqrt{\dfrac{M}{-2E}}\,h(r)(\vec{r}\cdot\vec{S}),

(52a)

\displaystyle\vec{{\cal R}^{\prime}}^{2}=\left[\mu\dfrac{(\vec{r}\cdot\vec{S})^{2}}{r^{2}}-\vec{J}^{\,2}-\hbar^{2}\right]-\dfrac{M}{2E}\big[h(r)\big]^{2}r^{2},

(52b)

we get

\displaystyle\vec{W}^{2}+\vec{K}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\bigg[{\mu}\dfrac{(\vec{r}\cdot\vec{S})^{2}}{r^{2}}-\hbar^{2}-\dfrac{M}{2E}\big[h(r)\big]^{2}r^{2}\bigg],

(53a)

\displaystyle\vec{W}^{2}-\vec{K}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\sqrt{\dfrac{M}{-2E}}\,h(r)\,(\vec{r}\cdot\vec{S}),

(53b)

Furthermore, one can verify that

\displaystyle\left[\hat{S}_{r},\ {\cal H}\right]=0,

(54)

and therefore
\hat{S}_{r}
is also a conserved quantity for the Hamiltonian system, and we denote its eigenvalue as
s_{r}\hbar
. In the case of

\displaystyle\vec{S}=\frac{\hbar}{2}\vec{\sigma}

(55)

i.e., the spin-
1/2
operator, one has
S_{r}^{2}=(\hbar^{2}/4)\openone
, where
\vec{\sigma}
and
\openone
express the vector of Pauli matrices and identity matrix respectively, which means that
s_{r}=\pm 1/2
.

After replacing the operators by their corresponding eigenvalues, Eq. (
53a
) and Eq. (
53b
) become

\displaystyle w(w+1)\hbar^{2}+k(k+1)\hbar^{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}\bigg[{\mu}(s_{r}\hbar)^{2}-\hbar^{2}-\dfrac{M}{2E}(k_{1}+{\mu}\,k_{2}s_{r}\hbar)^{2}\bigg],

(56a)

\displaystyle w(w+1)\hbar^{2}-k(k+1)\hbar^{2}=\mu\sqrt{\dfrac{M}{-2E}}\,\big(k_{1}+{\mu}\,k_{2}s_{r}\hbar\big)s_{r}\hbar.

(56b)

There is no solution for the energy
E
if
k_{1}+{\mu}\,k_{2}s_{r}\hbar=0
. For

k_{1}+k_{2}s_{r}\hbar\neq 0
, one has the energy spectrum of HA with spin as

\displaystyle E\equiv E_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{M}{2\hbar^{2}}\dfrac{(k_{1}+\mu\,k_{2}s_{r}\hbar)^{2}}{\big(n\pm\mu\,s_{r}\big)^{2}},

(57)

with
n=2\,w+1=1,2,3,....
For
\mu=0
,

\displaystyle E_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{Mk_{1}^{2}}{2\hbar^{2}}\frac{1}{n^{2}},

(58)

which reduces to the energy spectrum of the usual HA.

VI
Conclusion and Discussion

To sum up, we have written down the spin-dependent QRL vector, constructed the Hamiltonian of HA with spin under the requirement of
SO(4)
symmetry. The energy spectrum of the novel hamiltonian system is also determined by the approach of
SO(4)
symmetry, which reduces to the usual HA without considering spin. Our computations extend the investigation of HA from the perspective of spin potential, which may be applied to HA-based models in atomic physics and condensed matter physics.

Considering the relativistic treatments for HA with
SO(4)
approach have been fully studied, such as

) under

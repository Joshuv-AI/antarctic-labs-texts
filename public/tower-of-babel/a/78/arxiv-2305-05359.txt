Abstract
We provide a mathematical framework to analyze the limits of Hybrid Automatic Repeat reQuest (HARQ) and derive analytical expressions for the most powerful test for estimating the decodability under maximum-likelihood decoding and t t -error decoding. Furthermore, we numerically approximate the most powerful test for sum-product decoding. We compare the performance of previously studied HARQ prediction schemes and show that none of the state-of-the-art HARQ prediction is most powerful to estimate the decodability of a partially received signal vector under maximum-likelihood decoding and sum-product decoding. Furthermore, we demonstrate that decoding in general is suboptimal for predicting the decodability.



I
Introduction

Hybrid Automatic Repeat reQuest (HARQ) is a widely used physical layer retransmission mechanism to ensure high reliability, while not sacrificing too much spectral efficiency. Generally, HARQ can be regarded as a special case of Variable-Length Feedback (VLF) codes. In
[
1
]
, Polyanskiy
et al.
show that VLF codes improve the achievable rate significantly. Particularly, stop-feedback codes attract particular interest, due to their simple stop feedback mechanism. For these codes, Polyanskiy
et al.
provide a random-coding bound on the achievable performance, assuming a noiseless and zero-latency feedback channel. This analysis is refined by Östman
et al.
in
[
2
]
by considering the latter two constraints.

In particular, the feedback latency becomes an issue with regards to Ultra-Reliable Low Latency Communication (URLLC) use cases, where for 6G, end-to-end latencies of down to sub-milliseconds are foreseen
[
3
]
. To address the latency issue, HARQ prediction mechanisms have been studied in the literature
[

. HARQ prediction provides the feedback ahead of the end of the transmission, such that the feedback is available at the transmitter when it has to decide whether more redundancy is required or not. In particular, the literature can be divided into schemes that rely on Channel State Information (CSI) for the decodability estimation and schemes that use the partially received signal vector. The first class of schemes is well understood also in theoretic terms. In
[
4
]
, Makki et. al. derive closed-form expressions for the message decoding probabilities and the performance in quasi-static Rician and Rayleigh fading channels. In
[

However, these schemes cannot be transfered to memoryless channels, where CSI knowledge cannot be determined at the receiver. In contrast to that, Log-Likelihood Ratio (LLR) and subcode-based schemes that use the partially received signal vector can be applied to memoryless channels. In fading channels, multiple works have shown a superior performance of these schemes

. Nevertheless, the limits of these schemes are not well understood and no theoretical analysis exists to date.

Contributions:
In this work, we provide a mathematical framework to analyze the limits of HARQ prediction. In contrast to previous theoretic works that try to design VLF coding schemes, we consider the channel code and the associated decoder as a given. We derive the most powerful tests for arbitrary deterministic codes under Maximum Likelihood (ML) decoders, particularizing for the Additive White Gaussian Noise (AWGN) channel, and
t
-error correcting codes under hard decision decoding. Furthermore, we numerically explore the performance of the most powerful test for a

Finally, we compare the performance of other state-of-the-art decodability prediction schemes to the most powerful test and show that none of these achieves the attainable power.

Analytical results

As Th.
1

\alpha

.

A decoder

\displaystyle Z_{D_{p}}(\mathbf{y}_{p}):=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }D_{p}(\mathbf{y}_{p})\in\mathcal{M}\\

is designated as

-based prediction.

IV-A

Maximum-Likelihood Decoding

ML decoding is a widely considered decoding scheme, whose decoding complexity scales exponentially with the information length in general. From theoretic perspective, ML decoding can be well analyzed and is given by

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{ML}}(\mathbf{y}_{n}):=\argmax_{i}i_{n}(\mathbf{c}_{i};\mathbf{y}_{n})\,.

(11)

The following theorem gives the conditional correct decoding probability. By applying it to Th.
1

Theorem 2
.

For an arbitrary code

\displaystyle P_{D|Y^{p}X^{n}}

\displaystyle(i|\mathbf{y}_{p},\mathbf{c}_{i})=\sum_{l=0}^{|\mathcal{M}_{i}|}\frac{1}{l+1}\sum_{\mathcal{G}\in{\mathcal{M}_{i}\choose l}}

\displaystyle P_{Y^{r}|X^{n}=\mathbf{c}_{i}}

\displaystyle\big[\{{\cap_{j\in\mathcal{M}_{i}\setminus\mathcal{G}}\{l(\mathbf{c}_{i},\mathbf{c}_{j};\mathbf{y}_{p})>l(\mathbf{c}_{j},\mathbf{c}_{i};Y^{r})\}}\}

\displaystyle\bigcap

\displaystyle\{\cap_{j\in\mathcal{G}}\{l(\mathbf{c}_{i},\mathbf{c}_{j};\mathbf{y}_{p})=l(\mathbf{c}_{j},\mathbf{c}_{i};Y^{r})\}\}\big]\,,

(12)

where
\mathcal{M}_{i}:=\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{x}}\setminus\{i\}

Corollary 1

For an AWGN channel, the decodability distribution of ML decoding reduces to

\displaystyle P_{D|Y^{p}X^{n}}(i|\mathbf{y}_{p},\mathbf{c}_{i})=\Phi_{|\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{x}}|}\Big[\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{B}_{p|i}(\mathbf{y}_{p}-\mathbf{c}_{i})\Big]\,,

IV-B

t-error correcting codes under hard-decision decoding

Algebraic block codes, such as Reed-Solomon codes, are usually decoded using hard-decision decoding. Hard-decision decoding involves quantizing the received signal to discrete values. The following proposition gives us the conditional correct decoding probability for
t
-error correcting codes.

Theorem 3
.

For an arbitrary
t
-error-correcting code under a corresponding hard-decision decoder, and a symbol-wise quantization function
\mathbf{Q}
with equal elements
Q:\mathcal{B}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
, the decoding probability yields

\displaystyle P_{D|Y^{p}X^{n}}(i|\mathbf{y}_{p},\mathbf{c}_{i})=\sum_{u=0}^{t-d_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{c}_{i},\mathbf{Q}(\mathbf{y}_{p}))}{n-p\choose u}v^{u}(1-v)^{n-p-u}\,,

(15)

where
v
is the associated quantization bit error probability and
d_{\mathrm{H}}
is the Hamming distance.

Proof.

See App.
F
.
∎

We note that the conditional correct decoding probability only depends on the previously received signal vector in terms of the already "occured" bit errors. This allows us to derive a different representation for linear codes. Linear codes can further be represented by their parity-check matrix. In this case, we can further establish a link to the syndrome coset of the code.

Theorem 4
.

Suppose that
\forall i\in\mathcal{M}:P_{D|X^{n}}(i|\mathbf{c}_{i})=P_{\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{A}}}

\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{NP}}(\mathbf{y}_{p})=\frac{1}{P_{\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{A}}}}{\sum_{\mathbf{e}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{s})}P_{E^{p}|Y^{p}}(\mathbf{e}|\mathbf{y}_{p})P_{\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{A}}|E^{p}=\mathbf{e}}}\,,

(16)

where
\mathbf{s}:=\mathbf{H}_{p}\mathbf{Q}(\mathbf{y}_{p})
is the syndrome and
\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{s})
is the coset associated with syndrome
\mathbf{s}
.

Proof.

See App.
G
.
∎

proposed in
[

Proposition 3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}^{n}=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
and suppose that the distribution of LLRs is i.i.d. Then,

is most powerful for any achievable

Th.

allows us to interpret the LLR test
Z_{E}
as the most powerful test for a
0

Although the theorem requires that the LLRs are distributed according to a Dirac delta distribution, this property is also approximately fulfilled for very small bit error probabilities i.e. high Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) in an Binary-Input Additive White Gaussian Noise (BI-AWGN) channel.

IV-C

Sum-product decoder

The sum-product decoder is an iterative decoder, which is known to be the bitwise Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) decoder on cycle-free tree graphs. However, modern LDPC codes contain cycles, which makes an exact analytical analysis of the performance on these codes a hard problem. In particular, the decoding probability
P_{D|Y^{p}X^{n}}
cannot be stated in a closed form. Hence, we approximate
dP_{Y^{p}|\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{A}}}
numerically by using a kernel density estimation with normal kernels. However, with increasing
p
also the dimensionality of the approximated probability density increases, which requires significantly larger sample sizes
[
23
]
. On top of that,
dP_{Y^{p}|\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{A}}}
is a mixed distribution with
|\mathcal{M}|
components. This increases the difficulty of applying a kernel density estimation even further. Hence, we define a measurable function
\mathbf{f}_{i}:\mathbf{y}_{p}\mapsto(\mathbf{y}_{p}-\mathbf{c}_{i}+\mathbf{c}_{1})
. Under the assumption of symmetry with respect to
P_{Y^{p}|X^{p}}
, which clearly is given for an BI-AWGN channel, the probability density becomes

\displaystyle dP_{Y^{p}|\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{A}}}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{M}}\frac{dP_{Y^{p}|X^{n}}(\mathbf{f}_{i}(\mathbf{y}_{p})|\mathbf{c}_{1})P_{D|Y^{p}X^{n}}(1|\mathbf{f}_{i}(\mathbf{y}_{p}),\mathbf{c}_{1})}{|\mathcal{M}|P_{D|X^{n}}(i|\mathbf{c}_{i})}\,.

(17)

Hence, the estimation task is reduced to estimating only a single component instead of the whole mixed distribution.

V
Numerical results

In this section, we present our results for a very short blocklength of
n=24

VI
Conclusions

In this work, we have derived the Neyman-Pearson tests for maximum-likelihood decoding and
t
-error codes. We particularized and applied the Neyman-Pearson test to the BI-AWGN channel. Furthermore, we showed in numerical evaluations that none of the previously studied prediction schemes is most powerful for maximum-likelihood decoding and sum-product decoding. In particular, we demonstrate that decoding and estimating the decodability, i.e. HARQ prediction, are different problems in general.

Proof.

Because we assume a memoryless channel, the information density can be split into an already determined and a random part with
\mathbf{y}_{p}\in\mathcal{B}^{p}
and
\mathbf{y}_{r}\in\mathcal{B}^{n-p}

where

with equiprobable codewords.

Proof of Theorem 4

Proof.

\mathbf{H}_{p}
be the parity-check matrix of the subcode, such that
\forall i\in\mathcal{M}:\mathbf{H}_{p}\mathbf{c}_{i}^{p}=0

\mathbf{H}_{p}
does not only include the parity-check constraints of the error correction code itself but also the check constraints of the error detection code, i.e. CRC. The syndrome of the received signal vector
\mathbf{y}_{p}

\mathbf{s}:=\mathbf{H}_{p}\mathbf{Q}(\mathbf{y}_{p})

\mathbf{s}
be the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{s}):=\{\mathbf{b}_{p}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{p}:\mathbf{H}_{p}\mathbf{b}_{p}=\mathbf{s}\}\,.

(37)

Furthermore, the following equality holds for all valid codewords

\displaystyle\forall i\in\mathcal{M}:\mathbf{s}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{H}_{p}\mathbf{Q}(\mathbf{y}_{p})=\mathbf{H}_{p}\mathbf{c}_{i}+\mathbf{H}_{p}(\mathbf{Q}(\mathbf{y}_{p})\oplus\mathbf{c}_{i})

(38)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{H}_{p}(\mathbf{e}:=\mathbf{Q}(\mathbf{y}_{p})\oplus\mathbf{c}_{i})\,,

(39)

where
\mathbf{Q}(\mathbf{y}_{p})\oplus\mathbf{c}_{i}
is the element-wise addition on
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Hence, we can conider the error pattern
\mathbf{e}
instead of the particular codewords.
Furthermore, the decoding probability in (
15

\mathbf{y}_{p}
in terms of the Hamming distance. Hence, by replacing the Hamming distance
d_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{c}_{i},\mathbf{Q}(\mathbf{y}_{p}))=d_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{0})
by the Hamming distance of the error pattern to the all zeros vector, we obtain

\displaystyle P_{D|Y^{p}X^{n}}

\displaystyle(i|\mathbf{y}_{p},\mathbf{c}_{i})=P_{\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{A}}|E^{p}=\mathbf{e}}

(40)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u=0}^{t-d_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{0})}{n-p\choose u}v^{u}(1-v)^{n-p-u}\,.

(41)

In addition, the probability of receiving a certain signal vector
\mathbf{y}_{p}
is determined by the probability of the error pattern:

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathcal{M}}P_{Y^{p}|X^{p}}(\mathbf{y}_{p}|\mathbf{c}_{i})\frac{P_{D|Y^{p}X^{n}}(i|\mathbf{y}_{p},\mathbf{c}_{i})}{|\mathcal{M}|P_{D|X^{n}}(i|\mathbf{c}_{i})}

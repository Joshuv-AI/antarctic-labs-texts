The AFLT q q -Morris constant term identity
Yue Zhou

Abstract
. It is well-known that the Selberg integral is equivalent to the Morris constant term identity. More generally, Selberg type integrals can be turned into constant term identities for Laurent polynomials. In this paper, by extending the Gessel–Xin method of the Laurent series proof of constant term identities, we obtain an AFLT type q q -Morris constant term identity. That is a q q -Morris type constant term identity for a product of two Macdonald polynomials. Keywords: AFLT Selberg integral, Macdonald polynomials, constant term identities, q q -Morris identity, symmetric functions.

(3)

Determination of roots

Let

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=\{-ic-1,-ic-2,\dots,-ic-b\>|\>i=0,1,\dots,n-1\},

\displaystyle A_{2}

4.
Constant term evaluations using iterated Laurent series

In this section we present a basic lemma for extracting
constant terms from rational functions in the field of iterated Laurent series.
In this paper, we denote
K=\mathbb{C}(q)
and work in the field of iterated
Laurent series
K\langle\!\langle x_{n},x_{n-1},\dots,x_{0}\rangle\!\rangle=K(\!(x_{n})\!)(\!(x_{n-1})\!)\cdots(\!(x_{0})\!)
.
That is, elements of this field are regarded first as Laurent series in
x_{0}
, then as
Laurent series in
x_{1}
, and so on.
For a more detailed account of the properties of this field,
see
[
29
]
and
[
30
]
.
A crucial fact is that the field
K(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
of
rational functions forms a subfield of

K\langle\!\langle x_{n},x_{n-1},\dots,x_{0}\rangle\!\rangle
, so that every rational function is identified as its unique Laurent series expansion in the field of iterated Laurent series.

The following series expansion of
1/(1-cx_{i}/x_{j})

for
c\in K\setminus\{0\}
forms a key ingredient in our approach:

\frac{1}{1-cx_{i}/x_{j}}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{l\geq 0}c^{l}(x_{i}/x_{j})^{l}&\text{if $i<j$},\\[14.22636pt]
\displaystyle-\sum_{l<0}c^{l}(x_{i}/x_{j})^{l}&\text{if $i>j$}.\end{cases}

Thus, the constant term of
1/(1-cx_{i}/x_{j})
with respect to
x_{i}

is
1
if
i<j
and
0
if
i>j
.
That is,

(4.1)

\CT_{x_{i}}\frac{1}{1-cx_{i}/x_{j}}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $i<j$},\\
0&\text{if $i>j$},\\
\end{cases}

where, for
f\in K\langle\!\langle x_{n},x_{n-1},\dots,x_{0}\rangle\!\rangle
,
we use the notation
\CT\limits_{x_{i}}f
to denote taking the constant term
of
f
with respect to
x_{i}
.
An important property of the constant term operators defined this
way is their commutativity:

\CT_{x_{i}}\CT_{x_{j}}f=\CT_{x_{j}}\CT_{x_{i}}f.

This means that we take constant terms with respect to a set of variables.

extracting constant terms from rational functions. It first

i_{r}\neq k
,
and define

(4.2)

f=p(x_{k})/D,

where

is of the form

\prod_{r=1}^{m}(1-c_{r}x_{k}/x_{i_{r}}),

and
c_{1},\dots,c_{m}\in K\setminus\{0\}
such that
c_{r}\neq c_{s}
if

.
Then

(4.4)

\CT_{x_{k}}f=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\\[1.0pt]
i_{r}>k\end{subarray}}^{m}\big(f\,(1-c_{r}x_{k}/x_{i_{r}})\big)\Big|_{x_{k}=c_{r}^{-1}x_{i_{r}}}.

At the end of this section, we obtain an equivalence between two kinds of constant terms.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
f(x)
be a rational function invariant under any permutation of
x
. Then

(4.5)

\CT_{x}f(x)\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{i}/x_{j})_{c}(qx_{j}/x_{i})_{c}=\frac{1}{n!}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{1-q^{(i+1)c}}{1-q^{c}}\times\CT_{x}f(x)\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}(x_{i}/x_{j})_{c}.

Proof.

For
w:=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and any function
g(x)
, define

w\circ g(x):=g(x_{w_{1}},\dots,x_{w_{n}}).

Since
w
acts on any Laurent polynomial (or Laurent series) does not change the constant term, we have

(4.6)

\CT_{x}f(x)\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{i}/x_{j})_{c}(qx_{j}/x_{i})_{c}\\
\quad=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\CT_{x}w\circ\bigg(f(x)\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}(x_{i}/x_{j})_{c}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{1-q^{c}x_{j}/x_{i}}{1-x_{j}/x_{i}}\bigg).

Because
f(x)\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}(x_{i}/x_{j})_{c}
is invariant under the action of
w
, the right-hand side of (
4.6
) becomes

\frac{1}{n!}\CT_{x}f(x)\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}(x_{i}/x_{j})_{c}\cdot\sum_{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}w\circ\bigg(\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{1-q^{c}x_{j}/x_{i}}{1-x_{j}/x_{i}}\bigg),

which is the right-hand side of (
4.5
) by the next identity
[
22
, III, (1.4)]
.

(4.7)

\sum_{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}w\circ\bigg(\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{1-q^{c}x_{j}/x_{i}}{1-x_{j}/x_{i}}\bigg)=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{1-q^{(i+1)c}}{1-q^{c}}.\qed

5.
A family of vanishing constant terms

In this section, we find that a family of constant terms vanish using the splitting formula obtained by Cai
[

]
.

Let

be a parameter such that

, where

Then it is easy to see that

where

,

, and

denotes the monomial

.
Note that if

then
the constant term in the left-hand side of (

) vanishes by the homogeneity.

To obtain the main result in this section, we need the equal parameter case of
[

Proposition 5.1
.

We can write

(5.2)

\frac{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\big(z_{i}/z_{j}\big)_{c}\big(qz_{j}/z_{i}\big)_{c}}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(z_{i}/w)_{c}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{c-1}\frac{A_{ij}}{1-q^{j}z_{i}/w},

where

(5.3)

A_{ij}=\frac{q^{c\big((j+1)n-i-j\big)}}{(q^{-j})_{j}(q)_{c-j-1}}\prod_{l=1}^{i-1}\big(q^{1-c}z_{i}/z_{l}\big)_{j}\big(q^{j+1}z_{i}/z_{l}\big)_{c-j}\prod_{l=i+1}^{n}\big(q^{-c}z_{i}/z_{l}\big)_{j+1}\big(q^{j+1}z_{i}/z_{l}\big)_{c-j-1}\\
\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq u<v\leq n\\
u,v\neq i\end{subarray}}\big(z_{u}/z_{v}\big)_{c}\big(qz_{v}/z_{u}\big)_{c}.

Note that all the

are polynomials in

.

For a nonnegative integer
r
and an alphabet
X:=\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots\}
, define the

-th complete symmetric function

in terms of its generating function as

\sum_{r\geq 0}t^{r}h_{r}(X)=\prod_{i\geq 1}\frac{1}{1-tx_{i}}.

Using Proposition
5.1
, we find that a family of constant terms vanish.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\lambda
be a partition such that
|v|=|\lambda|
. For

\lambda_{1}>\max\{v_{i}\mid i=1,\dots,n\}
,

(5.5)

\CT_{z}\frac{1}{z^{v}}h_{\lambda}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1-q^{c}}{1-q}z_{i}\Big]\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\big(z_{i}/z_{j}\big)_{c}\big(qz_{j}/z_{i}\big)_{c}=0.

Note that we can write
h_{\lambda}\big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1-q^{c}}{1-q}z_{i}\big]=g_{\lambda}(z;q,q^{c})

for modified complete symmetric function by (
6.18
) below.

Proof.

By the generating function for complete symmetric functions (
5.4
),

h_{r}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1-q^{c}}{1-q}z_{i}\Big]=\CT_{w}\frac{w^{r}}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(z_{i}/w)_{c}}

for

a nonnegative integer.
Thus

(5.6)

\CT_{z}\frac{1}{z^{v}}h_{\lambda}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1-q^{c}}{1-q}z_{i}\Big]\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\big(z_{i}/z_{j}\big)_{c}\big(qz_{j}/z_{i}\big)_{c}\\
=\CT_{z,w}\frac{w^{\lambda_{1}}h_{\lambda^{(1)}}\big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1-q^{c}}{1-q}z_{i}\big]\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\big(z_{i}/z_{j}\big)_{c}\big(qz_{j}/z_{i}\big)_{c}}{z^{v}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(z_{i}/w)_{c}},

where
\lambda^{(1)}=(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\dots)
.
Using (
5.2
), we can write the right-hand side of (

) as

(5.7)

\CT_{z,w}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{c-1}\frac{w^{\lambda_{1}}h_{\lambda^{(1)}}\big[\sum_{t=1}^{n}\frac{1-q^{c}}{1-q}z_{t}\big]A_{ij}}{z^{v}(1-q^{j}z_{i}/w)}.

Taking the constant term with respect to

in (

) yields

(5.8)

\CT_{z}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{c-1}q^{j\lambda_{1}}z^{-v}z_{i}^{\lambda_{1}}h_{\lambda^{(1)}}\Big[\sum_{t=1}^{n}\frac{1-q^{c}}{1-q}z_{t}\Big]A_{ij}.

Since

and the

, every term in the sums of (

) is a polynomial in

with no constant term.
Thus, the right-hand side of (

) vanishes. Then the lemma follows.
∎

6.
Macdonald polynomials

In this section, we give several essential results for Macdonald polynomials.

Given a sequence
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots)
of nonnegative integers such that
|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\cdots
is finite.
Let
X=\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots\}
be an alphabet of countably many variables. Then the monomial symmetric function indexed by a partition
\lambda
is defined as

m_{\lambda}=m_{\lambda}(X):=\sum x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots,

where the sum is over all distinct permutations
\alpha
of
\lambda
.
Denote
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Q}(q,t)
.
Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}}\times\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}

be the
q,t
-Hall scalar product on
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}}
given by
[
22
, page 306]

\langle p_{\lambda},p_{\mu}\rangle:=\delta_{\lambda,\mu}z_{\lambda}\prod_{i\geq 1}\frac{1-q^{\lambda_{i}}}{1-t^{\lambda_{i}}},

where
z_{\lambda}:=\prod_{i\geq 1}i^{m_{i}}m_{i}!
if
m_{i}
is the number of parts
i
in
\lambda
,
and the Kronecker symbol
\delta_{\lambda,\mu}=1
if
\lambda=\mu
and 0 otherwise.
The Macdonald polynomials

P_{\lambda}=P_{\lambda}(q,t)=P_{\lambda}(X;q,t)
are the unique symmetric functions
[
22
, VI, (4.7)]
such that

\langle P_{\lambda},P_{\mu}\rangle=0\quad\text{if}\quad\lambda\neq\mu

and

(6.1)

P_{\lambda}=m_{\lambda}+\sum_{\mu<\lambda}u_{\lambda\mu}m_{\mu},\quad u_{\lambda\mu}\in\mathbb{F}.

Let

b_{\lambda}=b_{\lambda}(q,t)=\langle P_{\lambda},P_{\lambda}\rangle^{-1},

and

(6.2)

Q_{\lambda}=b_{\lambda}P_{\lambda},

so that

\langle P_{\lambda},Q_{\mu}\rangle=\delta_{\lambda\mu}

for all partitions
\lambda
and
\mu
. See
[
22
, VI, (4.11), (4.12)]
.
For any three partitions
\lambda,\mu,\nu
let

f_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}=f_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}(q,t)=\langle Q_{\lambda},P_{\mu}P_{\nu}\rangle\in\mathbb{F}.

The coefficient
f_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}
is called the
q,t
-Littlewood–Richardson coefficient.
Let
\lambda,\mu
be partitions and define skew functions

Q_{\lambda/\mu}\in\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}}

by

(6.3)

Q_{\lambda/\mu}=\sum_{\nu}f_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}Q_{\nu}

so that

(6.4)

\langle Q_{\lambda/\mu},P_{\nu}\rangle=\langle Q_{\lambda},P_{\mu}P_{\nu}\rangle.

Likewise we define
P_{\lambda/\mu}
by interchanging the
P
’s and
Q
’s in (
6.4
)

(6.5)

\langle P_{\lambda/\mu},Q_{\nu}\rangle=\langle P_{\lambda},Q_{\mu}Q_{\nu}\rangle.

Since
Q_{\lambda}=b_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}
, it follows that

(6.6)

Q_{\lambda/\mu}=b_{\lambda}b_{\mu}^{-1}P_{\lambda/\mu}.

See
[
22
, VI, (7.8)]
.
For
u,v\in\mathbb{F}
such that
v\neq\pm 1
, let
\omega_{u,v}
denote the
\mathbb{F}
-algebra
endomorphism of
\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}}
defined by

\omega_{u,v}(p_{r})=(-1)^{r-1}\frac{1-u^{r}}{1-v^{r}}p_{r}

for all
r\geq 1
, so that

\omega_{u,v}(p_{\lambda})=(-1)^{|\lambda|-\ell(\lambda)}p_{\lambda}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\frac{1-u^{\lambda_{i}}}{1-v^{\lambda_{i}}}.

Then we have the duality
[
22
, VI, (7.16)]

(6.7)

\omega_{q,t}P_{\lambda/\mu}(q,t)=Q_{\lambda^{\prime}/\mu^{\prime}}(t,q).

For any alphabet
X
, the endomorphism
\omega_{q,t}
can be interpreted plethystically.
That is

(6.8)

X\mapsto\epsilon X\frac{1-q}{t-1}.

For two alphabets
X
and
Y
,

(6.9)

P_{\lambda}[X+Y]=\sum_{\mu\subset\lambda}P_{\lambda/\mu}[X]P_{\mu}[Y].

See
[
22
, VI, (7.9’)]
.
If
n<\ell(\lambda)
then

(6.10)

P_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};q,t)=0.

See
[
22
, VI, (4.10)]
.
If
\lambda
is a partition of length
n
, then

(6.11)

P_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};q,t)=x_{1}\dots x_{n}P_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};q,t),

where
\mu=(\lambda_{1}-1,\dots,\lambda_{n}-1)
.
See
[
22
, VI, (4.17)]
.
For the skew functions

(6.12)

Q_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};q,t)=0

unless
0\leq\lambda^{\prime}_{i}-\mu^{\prime}_{i}\leq n
for each
i\geq 1
.
See
[
22
, VII, (7.15)]
.
A generalization of the
q
-factorial to partitions is given by

(a;q,t)_{\lambda}:=\prod_{i\geq 1}(at^{1-i})_{\lambda_{i}}.

The generalized hook polynomial is defined as

c_{\lambda}(q,t)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(t^{n-i+1})_{\lambda_{i}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{(t^{j-i})_{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}}}{(t^{j-i+1})_{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}}}

for
n\geq\ell(\lambda)
.
Then

(6.13)

P_{\lambda}\Big(\Big[\frac{1-a}{1-t}\Big];q,t\Big)=\frac{t^{n(\lambda)}(a;q,t)_{\lambda}}{c_{\lambda}(q,t)}.

As claimed in
[
2
]
, this specialization is equivalent to
[
22
, p. 338]
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\lambda
and
\mu
be partitions such that
\mu\subset\lambda
.
Then for

(6.14)

P_{\lambda/\mu}\Big(\Big[\frac{1-q}{t-1}\big(m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{l}\big)\Big];q,t\Big)=(-1)^{|\lambda|-|\mu|}Q_{\lambda^{\prime}/\mu^{\prime}}\Big(\big[m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{l}\big];t,q\Big),

Proof.

We prove by straightforward calculation.

\displaystyle Q_{\lambda^{\prime}/\mu^{\prime}}\Big(\big[m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{l}\big];t,q\Big)

\displaystyle\quad=\omega_{q,t}P_{\lambda/\mu}\Big(\big[m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{l}\big];q,t\Big)\quad

by (

)

\displaystyle\quad=P_{\lambda/\mu}\Big(\Big[\big(\epsilon\frac{1-q}{t-1}\big)\big(m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{l}\big)\Big];q,t\Big)\quad

by (

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{|\lambda|-|\mu|}P_{\lambda/\mu}\Big(\Big[\frac{1-q}{t-1}\big(m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{l}\big)\Big];q,t\Big)\quad

\displaystyle\text{by \eqref{e-Mac3}}.

Then we obtain (

).
∎

By Lemma

we obtain the next vanishing property for Macdonald polynomials.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition and
\lambda_{i}
be its nonzero part. Then

(6.15)

P_{\lambda}\Big(\Big[\frac{1-q}{t-1}\big(m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{\lambda_{i}-1}\big)+n_{1}+\cdots+n_{i-1}\Big];q,t\Big)=0,

where the
m_{i}
and the

are single-letter alphabets.

Proof.

By (
6.9
)

(6.16)

P_{\lambda}\Big(\Big[\frac{1-q}{t-1}\big(m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{\lambda_{i}-1}\big)+n_{1}+\cdots+n_{i-1}\Big];q,t\Big)\\
=\sum_{\mu\subset\lambda}P_{\lambda/\mu}\Big(\Big[\frac{1-q}{t-1}\big(m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{\lambda_{i}-1}\big)\Big];q,t\Big)P_{\mu}\big([n_{1}+\cdots+n_{i-1}];q,t\big).

By Lemma
6.1
, the summand in the sum of (
6.16
) can be written as

(-1)^{|\lambda|-|\mu|}Q_{\lambda^{\prime}/\mu^{\prime}}\Big(\big[m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{\lambda_{i}-1}\big];t,q\Big)P_{\mu}\big([n_{1}+\cdots+n_{i-1}];q,t\big).

If
\ell(\mu)\geq i
then
P_{\mu}\big([n_{1}+\cdots+n_{i-1}];q,t\big)=0
by (
6.10
).
On the other hand, if
\ell(\mu)<i
then
\mu_{i}=0
and

\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}=\lambda_{i}>\mathrm{Car}(m_{1}+\cdots+m_{\lambda_{i}-1})=\lambda_{i}-1,

where

means the cardinality of the alphabet

.
It follows that

Q_{\lambda^{\prime}/\mu^{\prime}}\Big(\big[m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{\lambda_{i}-1}\big];t,q\Big)=0

by (
6.12
) with
(q,t,\lambda,\mu)\mapsto(t,q,\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
.
∎

Define the modified complete symmetric function
g_{n}(X;q,t)
by its generating function

(6.17)

\prod_{i\geq 1}\frac{(tx_{i}y;q)_{\infty}}{(x_{i}y;q)_{\infty}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}g_{n}(X;q,t)y^{n},

and for any partition

g_{\lambda}=g_{\lambda}(X;q,t)=\prod_{i\geq 1}g_{\lambda_{i}}(X;q,t).

In fact

g_{n}(X;q,t)=h_{n}\Big[\frac{1-t}{1-q}X\Big].

In this paper, we only need the

and
g_{n}
in (

) and (

For
\lambda=(r)
,

(6.19)

P_{(r)}=\frac{(q)_{r}}{(t)_{r}}g_{r}.

See
[
22
, VI, (4.9)]
.

The next result is called the Pieri formula for Macdonald polynomials
[
22
, VI, (6.24)]

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\mu
be a partition. For
r
a positive integer,

(6.20)

P_{\mu}g_{r}=\sum_{\lambda}\varphi_{\lambda/\mu}P_{\lambda},

where the sum is over partitions
\lambda
such that
\lambda/\mu
is a horizontal
r
-strip.

The explicit expression for
\varphi_{\lambda/\mu}
is given in
[
22
, VI, (6.24)]
.
We do not need the explicit form of
\varphi_{\lambda/\mu}
in this paper.

The symmetric functions
g_{\lambda}
form a basis of

\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}}

[
22
, VI, (2.19)]
.
Then the Macdonald polynomials can be written as a linear combination of
the modified complete symmetric functions.
The formula is referred as the generalized Jacobi-Trudi expansions for Macdonald polynomials.
We state that in the next theorem.

Theorem 6.4
.

[
19
]

Let
\lambda
and
\mu
be partitions with length at most
n
. Then

(6.21)

P_{\lambda}=\sum_{\mu\geq\lambda}c_{\mu}g_{\mu}.

Note that the explicit expression for
c_{\mu}
is quite complicated, we refer the reader to

[
19
, Theorem 3]
. When
q=t
, (
6.21
) reduces to the Jacobi-Trudi identity

s_{\lambda}=\det(h_{\lambda_{i}-i+j})_{1\leq i,j\leq n},

where
s_{\lambda}
is the Schur function.

We can express the skew Macdonald polynomials as (usual) Madonald polynomials.

Proposition 6.5
.

For partitions
\lambda
and
\mu
such that
\mu\subset\lambda
and
\ell(\mu)<\ell(\lambda)
,
let

(6.22)

P_{\lambda/\mu}=\sum_{\nu}r_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}\cdot P_{\nu},

where
\nu
is over all partitions such that
|\nu|=|\lambda|-|\mu|
.
Then
r_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}=0
for
\nu_{1}<\lambda_{\ell(\mu)+1}
, or equivalently,

\nu_{1}\geq\lambda_{\ell(\mu)+1}
.

Proof.

Substituting (
6.2
) into (
6.3
) yields

Q_{\lambda/\mu}=\sum_{\nu}f_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}Q_{\nu}=\sum_{\nu}f_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}b_{\nu}P_{\nu}.

Together with
Q_{\lambda/\mu}=b_{\lambda}b_{\mu}^{-1}P_{\lambda/\mu}
in (
6.6
), we have

P_{\lambda/\mu}=\sum_{\nu}f_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}b_{\mu}b_{\lambda}^{-1}b_{\nu}P_{\nu}.

Comparing this with (
6.22
), we have

r_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}=b_{\mu}b_{\nu}b_{\lambda}^{-1}f_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}.

Hence, to prove the proposition it suffices to prove that a family of

-Littlewood–Richardson coefficients are zeros. That is

(6.23)

f_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}=\langle Q_{\lambda},P_{\mu}P_{\nu}\rangle=0

for
\nu_{1}<\lambda_{\ell(\mu)+1}
,
\mu\subset\lambda
and
\ell(\mu)<\ell(\lambda)
.

By (
6.21
), we can write

P_{\mu}=\sum_{\omega\geq\mu}c_{\omega}g_{\omega}.

Using this and by the linearity of the
q,t
-Hall scalar product, we have

(6.24)

f_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}=\langle Q_{\lambda},P_{\mu}P_{\nu}\rangle=\sum_{\omega\geq\mu}c_{\omega}\langle Q_{\lambda},P_{\nu}g_{\omega}\rangle.

We can get
\ell(\omega)\leq\ell(\mu)
by
\omega\geq\mu
.
Consequently,
\ell(\omega)<\ell(\lambda)
.
Then we prove (
6.23
) by showing that each term in the sum of (
6.24
)

(6.25)

\langle Q_{\lambda},P_{\nu}g_{\omega}\rangle=0

for
\nu_{1}<\lambda_{\ell(\mu)+1}
and
\ell(\omega)<\ell(\lambda)
.
Repeatedly using the Pieri formulas for Macdonald polynomials (Proposition
6.3
) for

\ell(\omega)
times, we have

P_{\nu}g_{\omega}=P_{\nu}\cdot g_{\omega_{1}}\cdot g_{\omega_{2}}\cdots=\sum_{\lambda^{*}}\overline{c}_{\lambda^{*}}P_{\lambda^{*}},

where
\overline{c}_{\lambda^{*}}
is the coefficient for
P_{\lambda^{*}}
.
Since
\nu_{1}<\lambda_{\ell(\mu)+1}
and
\ell(\omega)\leq\ell(\mu)<\ell(\lambda)
,
none of the partitions
\lambda^{*}
can be
\lambda
.
(It needs at least
(\ell(\mu)+1)
-th use of the Pieri formula).
It follows that

\langle Q_{\lambda},P_{\nu}g_{\omega}\rangle=\sum_{\lambda^{*}}\overline{c}_{\lambda^{*}}\langle Q_{\lambda},P_{\lambda^{*}}\rangle=0.\qed

By Lemma
5.2
, Theorem
6.4
and Proposition
6.5
,
we can obtain the next vanishing proposition for Macdonald polynomials.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let

\lambda
and
\mu
be partitions such that

,
\ell(\mu)<\ell(\lambda)
,

and

\lambda_{\ell(\mu)+1}>\max\{v_{i}\mid i=1,\dots,n\}
.
Then

(6.26)

\CT_{x}x^{-v}P_{\lambda/\mu}(x;q,q^{c})\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\Big(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}\Big)_{c}\Big(\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}q\Big)_{c}=0.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.5
,

P_{\lambda/\mu}=\sum_{\nu}r_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}\cdot P_{\nu},

where
\nu
is over all partitions such that
|\nu|=|\lambda|-|\mu|=|v|

and
\nu_{1}\geq\lambda_{\ell(\mu)+1}

=\CT_{x}x^{-v}h_{\omega}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1-q^{c}}{1-q}x_{i}\Big]\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\Big(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}\Big)_{c}\Big(\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}q\Big)_{c}=0

for
\omega
a partition such that

.
Here the conditions for

By Lemma

∎

For positive integers

and

Then we obtain the next proposition which concerns the degrees of the Macdonald polynomials.

.

By (

By Lemma

Proof.

By (

) and (

\sum_{\nu\subset\mu}P_{\nu}\big(\big[\frac{q^{c-b-1}-q^{a}}{1-q^{c}}\big];q,q^{c}\big)\CT_{x}x_{0}^{-|\lambda|-|\mu|+|\nu|}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x_{0}/x_{i})_{a}(qx_{i}/x_{0})_{b}\times L(x),

We write

\displaystyle P_{\nu}\big(\big[\frac{q^{c-b-1}-q^{a}}{1-q^{c}}\big];q,q^{c}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\geq\nu}c_{\lambda}g_{\lambda}\Big(\Big[\frac{q^{c-b-1}-q^{a}}{1-q^{c}}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big)\quad

by (

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\geq\nu}c_{\lambda}h_{\lambda}\Big[\frac{q^{c-b-1}-q^{a}}{1-q}\Big]\quad

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\geq\nu}c_{\lambda}q^{(c-b-1)|\lambda|}\prod_{i\geq 1}\frac{(q^{a+b+1-c})_{\lambda_{i}}}{(q)_{\lambda_{i}}}.

The right-most equality holds by

[

of degree at most

The following rationality result, which is implicitly due to

[

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that
|\alpha|=0
. Then

(7.3)

\displaystyle\CT_{x}x^{\alpha}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\Big(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}\Big)_{c}\Big(\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}q\Big)_{c}=\frac{(q)_{nc}}{(q)_{c}^{n}}\cdot R_{n}(q^{c};q,\alpha),

where

and

.

By Proposition

we have the next
straightforward consequence.

Corollary 7.4
.

Let

be a nonnegative integer independent of

and

be a homogeneous Laurent polynomial in

of degree 0. If an expression for

\CT_{x}H\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\Big(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}\Big)_{c}\Big(\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}q\Big)_{c}

By Proposition

\frac{(q)_{c}^{n}}{(q)_{nc}}\CT_{x}H\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\Big(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}\Big)_{c}\Big(\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}q\Big)_{c}=R_{n}(q^{c};q,H).

In this section, we present several results that are essential to determine the roots of

8.1.
The key Lemmas

In this section, we obtain two key lemmas. The first one — Lemma
8.1
blew
— is to discuss the distribution of some integers.
This kind of discussion in order to evaluate some constant terms may originate from

[
9
, Lemma 4.2]
. Each time when this lemma was extended, some further (maybe harder) constant terms can be evaluated. See
[
20
,
32
]
in dealing with the
q
-Dyson style constant terms for example. Lemma
8.1
is a general extension of
[
31
, Lemma 4.2]
.
The
t=1
case of Lemma
8.1
corresponds to
[
31
, Lemma 4.2]
.
We will introduce the second key lemma blew.

Lemma 8.1
.

For
s
a positive integer, let
k_{1},\dots,k_{s}
and
t
be nonnegative integers such that
1\leq k_{i}\leq(s-1)c+b+t
for
1\leq i\leq s
.
Then, at least one of the following holds:

(1)

1\leq k_{i}\leq b
for some
i
with
1\leq i\leq s
;

(2)

-c\leq k_{i}-k_{j}\leq c-1
for some
(i,j)
such that
1\leq i<j\leq s
;

(3)

there exists a permutation
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{s}
and nonnegative integers
t_{1},\dots,t_{s}

such that

(8.1a)

k_{w(1)}=b+t_{1},

and

(8.1b)

k_{w(j)}-k_{w(j-1)}=c+t_{j}\quad\text{for $2\leq j\leq s$.}

Here the
t_{j}
satisfy

(8.2)

1\leq\sum_{j=1}^{s}t_{j}\leq t,

w(0):=0
, and
t_{j}>0
if
w(j-1)<w(j)
for
1\leq j\leq s
.
In particular, if
t=1
then

(8.3)

k_{i}=(s-i)c+b+1

for
i=1,\dots,s
.

Proof.

We prove the lemma by showing that if (1) and (2) fail then (3) must hold.

Assume that (1) and (2) are both false.
Then we construct a weighted tournament
T
on the complete graph
on
s
vertices, labelled
1,\dots,s
, as follows.
For the edge
(i,j)
with
1\leq i<j\leq s
, we draw an arrow from
j
to
i
and attach a weight
c
if
k_{i}-k_{j}\geq c
.
If, on the other hand,
k_{i}-k_{j}\leq-c-1
then we draw an arrow
from
i
to
j
and attach the weight
c+1
.
Note that the weight of each edge of a tournament is nonnegative.

We call a directed edge from
i
to
j
ascending if
i<j
.
It is immediate from our construction that
(i) the weight of the edge
i\to j
is less than or equal
k_{j}-k_{i}
, and
(ii) the weight of an ascending edge is positive.

We will use (i) and (ii) to show that any of the above-constructed
tournaments is acyclic and hence transitive.
As consequence of (i), the weight of a directed path from
i
to
j
in
T
,
defined as the sum of the weights of its edges, is at most
k_{j}-k_{i}
.
Proceeding by contradiction, assume that
T
contains a cycle
C
.
By the above, the weight of
C
must be non-positive, and hence
0
.
Since
C
must have at least one ascending edge, which by (ii) has positive
weight, the weight of
C
is positive, a contradiction.

Since each
T
is transitive, there is exactly one directed Hamilton path
P
in
T
,
corresponding to a total order of the vertices.
Assume
P
is given by

P=w(1)\rightarrow w(2)\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow w(s-1)\rightarrow w(s),

where we have suppressed the edge weights.
Then

k_{w(s)}-k_{w(1)}\geq(s-1)c,

and thus

(8.4)

k_{w(s)}\geq k_{w(1)}+(s-1)c\geq b+1+(s-1)c.

Together with the assumption that
k_{w(s)}\leq(s-1)c+b+t

this implies that
P
has at most
t-1
ascending edges.
Let
t_{1},\dots,t_{s}
be nonnegative integers such that (
8.1
) holds.
When
j=1
this gives
k_{w(1)}=b+t_{1}
.
Since (1) does not hold,
k_{w(1)}\geq b+1
, so that
t_{1}>0
.
For
2\leq j\leq s
, if
w(j-1)\to w(j)
is an ascending edge, then
t_{j}
is a positive integer.
That is, for
2\leq j\leq s
if
w(j-1)<w(j)
then
t_{j}>0
.
Set
k_{0}:=0
.
Since

\sum_{j=1}^{s}(k_{w(j)}-k_{w(j-1)})=k_{w(s)}=b+(s-1)c+\sum_{j=1}^{s}t_{j}\leq b+(s-1)c+t,

we have
\sum_{j=1}^{s}t_{j}\leq t
.
Together with the fact that
t_{1}>0
yields (
8.2
).
This completes the proof of the assertion that (3) must hold if
both (1) and (2) fail.

If
t=1
, then none of the edges of
P
can be ascending. That is
P=s\rightarrow s-1\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow 1
.
Correspondingly,
k_{i}=(s-i)c+b+1
for
i=1,\dots,s
.
∎

Lemma
8.2
allows us to combine the plethystic substitutions in symmetric functions with
the theory of iterated Laurent series. This discovery first appeared in
[
32
]
.

Lemma 8.2
.

For
s,t
positive integers, let
k_{1},\dots,k_{s}
be nonnegative integers such that

1\leq k_{i}\leq(s-1)c+b+t
for
1\leq i\leq s
.
If the
k_{i}
are such that (3) of Lemma
8.1
holds, then

(8.5)

-\frac{q^{c-b-1}-q^{a}}{1-q}x_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{s}\frac{1-q^{c}}{1-q}x_{i}\bigg|_{\begin{subarray}{c}-a=(s-1)c+b+t,\\[1.0pt]
x_{i}=q^{k_{s}-k_{i}},\,0\leq i\leq s\end{subarray}}=q^{n_{1}}+\dots+q^{n_{t-1}},

where
\{n_{1},\dots,n_{t-1}\}
is a set of integers determined by
s,b,c
and the
k_{i}
,
and we set
k_{0}:=0
.
In particular, set
q^{n_{1}}+\dots+q^{n_{t-1}}|_{t=1}:=0
.

We remark that the set
\{n_{1},\dots,n_{t}\}
(
n_{i}\neq n_{j}
for
1\leq i<j\leq t
) can be explicitly determined.
However, since the precise values of the
n_{i}
are irrelavent in the following, we
have omitted them from the above statement.
Indeed, the important fact about the right-hand side is that, viewed as an
alphabet, has cardinality
t-1
.

Proof.

Denote the left-hand side of (
8.5
) by
L
.
Carrying out the substitutions

a\mapsto-(s-1)c-b-t\quad\text{and}\quad x_{i}\mapsto q^{k_{s}-k_{i}}\text{ for $0\leq i\leq s$}

in

-\frac{q^{c-b-1}-q^{a}}{1-q}x_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{s}\frac{1-q^{c}}{1-q}x_{i},

we obtain

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{k_{s}}}{1-q}\bigg(q^{-(s-1)c-b-t}-q^{c-b-1}-\sum_{i=1}^{s}\big(1-q^{c}\big)q^{-k_{i}}\bigg)

(8.6)

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{k_{s}}}{1-q}\bigg(q^{-(s-1)c-b-t}-q^{c-b-1}-\sum_{i=1}^{s}(1-q^{c})q^{-k_{w(i)}}\bigg),

where
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{s}
is any permutation such that (
8.1
) holds.
By rearranging the terms in (
8.6
), it can be written as

L=\frac{q^{k_{s}}}{1-q}\bigg(q^{c-k_{w(1)}}\big(1-q^{k_{w(1)}-b-1}\big)+q^{-(s-1)c-b-t}\big(1-q^{(s-1)c+b+t-k_{w(s)}}\big)\\
+\sum_{i=2}^{s}q^{c-k_{w(i)}}\big(1-q^{-c+k_{w(i)}-k_{w(i-1)}}\big)\bigg).

By (
8.1
) and
k_{i}\leq(s-1)c+b+t
, we have

(8.7a)

k_{w(1)}-b-1\in\mathbb{N},

(8.7b)

(s-1)c+b+t-k_{w(s)}\in\mathbb{N},

and

(8.7c)

-c+k_{w(i)}-k_{w(i-1)}\in\mathbb{N}\quad\text{for $i=2,3,\dots,s$},

where
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
.
Since
(1-q^{n})/(1-q)=1+\dots+q^{n-1}
for
n
a positive integer and is 0 for
n=0
, we may conclude
that
L=q^{n_{1}}+\dots+q^{n_{p}}
,
where

is given by

p=k_{w(1)}-b-1+(s-1)c+b+t-k_{w(s)}+\sum_{i=2}^{s}\big(-c+k_{w(i)}-k_{w(i-1)}\big)=t-1.

This completes the proof.
∎

8.2.
The rational function
Q(d\>|\>u;k)

In this section, we discuss a kind of vanishing and recursive properties of the rational function
Q(d\>|\>u;k)
defined in (
8.9

Let

(8.8)

Q(d)=x_{0}^{-|\lambda|-|\mu|}P_{\lambda}(x;q,q^{c})P_{\mu}\Big(\Big[\frac{q^{c-b-1}-q^{-d}}{1-q^{c}}x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big)\\

It is clear that

\CT_{x}Q(-a)=A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu).

Note that the above equation holds for all integers

by Corollary
7.2
.

For any rational function
F
of
x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and
s
an integer such that
1\leq s\leq n
, and for
sequences of integers

and

Set

Note that the product on the right hand side of (

)
cancels all the factors in the denominator of

The numerator of

x_{0}^{-|\lambda|-|\mu|}P_{\lambda}(x;q,q^{c})P_{\mu}\Big(\Big[\frac{q^{c-b-1}-q^{-d}}{1-q^{c}}x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big)\\

The denominator of

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\big(q^{-d}x_{0}/x_{i}\big)_{d}

\prod_{r=1}^{nd}(1-c_{r}x_{0}/x_{i_{r}})

, where all the

, and

.
Thus, for

) with respect to

. Then we can apply Lemma

(8.10)

\CT_{x_{0}}Q(d)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k_{1}\leq d\\

.

(8.11)

\CT_{x}Q(d)=\sum_{s\in T\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq u_{1}<\cdots<u_{s}\leq n\\

The following vanishing and recursive properties of

Proposition 8.3
.

For
s

1\leq s\leq n
, the rational functions

(1)

If

(2)

If

(8.12)

\CT_{x_{u_{s}}}Q(d\>|\>u;k)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{s}<u_{s+1}\leq n\\

Proof.

(1)
If
d\leq(s-1)c+b
, then
since

1\leq k_{i}\leq(s-1)c+b\quad\text{for $i=1,\dots,s$}.

By Lemma

for some

,

If

, then

(2)
We first show that

8.3.

In this section, we find that the rational function
Q(d\>|\>u;k)
can be written as
a Laurent polynomial under certain conditions.

We can write
Q(d\>|\>u;k)
as

(8.13)

Q(d\>|\>u;k)=H\times\prod_{i=1}^{s}(q^{k_{i}-d})^{-1}_{d-k_{i}}(q)^{-1}_{k_{i}-1}\times V\times L\\
\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
i,j\notin U\end{subarray}}\big(x_{i}/x_{j}\big)_{c}\big(qx_{j}/x_{i}\big)_{c},

where

H=E_{u,k}\bigg(x_{0}^{-|\lambda|-|\mu|}\times P_{\lambda}(x;q,q^{c})P_{\mu}\Big(\Big[\frac{q^{c-b-1}-q^{-d}}{1-q^{c}}x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big)\bigg),

V=\prod_{i=1}^{s}(q^{1-k_{i}})_{b}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq s}(q^{k_{j}-k_{i}})_{c}(q^{k_{i}-k_{j}+1})_{c},

and

(8.14)

L=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\notin U\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{(q^{1-k_{s}}x_{i}/x_{u_{s}})_{b}}{\big(q^{k_{s}-d}x_{u_{s}}/x_{i}\big)_{d}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\notin U\end{subarray}}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\big(q^{k_{j}-k_{s}+\chi(i>u_{j})}x_{i}/x_{u_{s}}\big)_{c}\big(q^{k_{s}-k_{j}+\chi(u_{j}>i)}x_{u_{s}}/x_{i}\big)_{c}.

Proposition 8.4
.

Let
1\leq d\leq sc+b

For
i=1,\dots,s
the

satisfy

, and
there exists a permutation
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{s}
and nonnegative integers

such that

(8.15)

k_{w(1)}=b+t_{1},

and

(8.16)

k_{w(j)}-k_{w(j-1)}=c+t_{j}\quad\text{for $2\leq j\leq s$.}

Here the
t_{j}
satisfy

(8.17)

1\leq\sum_{j=1}^{s}t_{j}\leq c,

w(0):=0
, and
t_{j}>0
if
w(j-1)<w(j)
for
1\leq j\leq s
.
Then the rational function

in (

) can be written as a Laurent polynomial of the form

(8.18)

x_{u_{s}}^{(n-s)(sc-d)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\notin U\end{subarray}}^{n}\Big(p_{i}(x_{i}/x_{u_{s}})x_{i}^{d-sc}\Big),

where the
p_{i}(z)
are polynomials in

.

Note that the conditions for the

in this proposition is just the case (3) of Lemma
8.1

with

.

Proof.

We can further write

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\notin U\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{(q^{1-k_{s}}x_{i}/x_{u_{s}})_{b}}{(-x_{u_{s}}/x_{i})^{d}q^{dk_{s}-\binom{d+1}{2}}\big(q^{1-k_{s}}x_{i}/x_{u_{s}}\big)_{d}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\notin U\end{subarray}}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{s}(-x_{u_{s}}/x_{i})^{c}q^{\big(k_{s}-k_{j}+\chi(u_{j}>i)\big)c+\binom{c}{2}}\big(q^{k_{j}-k_{s}+1-c-\chi(u_{j}>i)}x_{i}/x_{u_{s}}\big)_{2c}

\displaystyle=C\times(x_{u_{s}})^{(n-s)(sc-d)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\notin U\end{subarray}}^{n}x_{i}^{d-sc}\frac{(q^{1-k_{s}}x_{i}/x_{u_{s}})_{b}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\big(q^{k_{j}-k_{s}+1-c-\chi(u_{j}>i)}x_{i}/x_{u_{s}}\big)_{2c}}{\big(q^{1-k_{s}}x_{i}/x_{u_{s}}\big)_{d}},

where

Let

\displaystyle S_{0}:=

\displaystyle\{1-k_{s},2-k_{s},\dots,d-k_{s}\},\quad B:=\{1-k_{s},2-k_{2},\dots,b-k_{s}\},

\displaystyle S_{j}:=

\displaystyle\{k_{j}-k_{s}+1-c-\chi(u_{j}>i),\dots,k_{j}-k_{s}+c-\chi(u_{j}>i)\},\quad\text{for $j=1,\dots,s$}.

By the expression for

i\notin U\end{subarray}}^{n}\big(q^{1-k_{s}}x_{i}/x_{u_{s}}\big)_{d}

in the denominator can be cancelled by the numerator

(q^{1-k_{s}}x_{i}/x_{u_{s}})_{b}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\big(q^{k_{j}-k_{s}+1-c-\chi(u_{j}>i)}x_{i}/x_{u_{s}}\big)_{2c}.

Then

is a Laurent polynomial,
rather than a rational function.
To do this, it is equivalent to showing that

(8.20)

S_{0}\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{s}S_{j}\bigcup B.

Since
S_{j}\supseteq S^{\prime}_{j}:=\{k_{j}-k_{s}+1-c,\dots,k_{j}-k_{s}+c-1\}
for
j=1,\dots,s
,

) holds if we show that

S_{0}\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{s}S^{\prime}_{j}\bigcup B.

We write the ordering of the

using the permutation

in the proposition.
To obtain (

(1)

b-k_{s}\geq k_{w(1)}-k_{s}-c
, i.e.,
k_{w(1)}\leq b+c
;

(2)

k_{w(s)}-k_{s}+c-1\geq d-k_{s}
, i.e.,
k_{w(s)}\geq d-c+1
;

(3)

k_{w(j-1)}-k_{s}+c-1\geq k_{w(j)}-k_{s}-c
, i.e.,

k_{w(j)}-k_{w(j-1)}\leq 2c-1
for

To prove (1), we find the upper bound for
k_{w(1)}
.
Since
k_{w(1)}=b+t_{1}
and

, we have

k_{w(1)}\leq b+c
. Then (1) holds.

To prove (2), we find the lower bound for

.
Summing both sides of (

) for

gives

k_{w(s)}=k_{w(1)}+(s-1)c+\sum_{j=2}^{s}t_{j}.

By (

)

k_{w(s)}\geq(s-1)c+b+\sum_{j=1}^{s}t_{j}\geq(s-1)c+b+1.

Here in the last inequality we use (
8.17
).
Since

, we have

k_{w(s)}\geq(s-1)c+b+1=sc+b-c+1=d-c+1.

Thus (2) holds.

If (3) fails for some

, then

k_{w(i)}-k_{w(i-1)}\geq 2c.

Together with (
8.16
) gives

k_{w(s)}-k_{w(1)}=\sum_{j=2}^{s}\big(k_{w(j)}-k_{w(j-1)}\big)\geq sc+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=2\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{s}t_{j}\geq sc.

Since
k_{w(1)}=b+t_{1}\geq b+1
, we have

k_{w(s)}\geq sc+b+1=d+1.

This contradicts the assumption that all the
k_{i}\leq d
.
Then (3) holds.
∎

is a Laurent polynomial in
x_{u_{i-1}}
with degree at most
-\lambda_{i}-\cdots-\lambda_{\ell(\lambda)}<0
.
It follows that
\CT\limits_{x}Q\big((i-1)c\>|\>u^{(i-1)};k^{(i-1)}\big)=0
.

Now we discuss the cases when
(i-1)c-\lambda_{i}+1\leq d<(i-1)c
if
\lambda_{i}>1
. (For

\lambda_{i}=1
no integer
d
satisfies
(i-1)c-\lambda_{i}+1\leq d<(i-1)c
.)
By (
6.9
) and (
3.2
) we can write

(9.11)

E_{u^{(i-1)},k^{(i-1)}}\big(P_{\lambda}(x;q,q^{c})\big)=P_{\lambda}(x;q,q^{c})\Big|_{x_{u_{j}}=x_{u_{i-1}}q^{k_{i-1}-k_{j}},1\leq j\leq i-1}\\
=\sum_{\nu}P_{\lambda/\nu}\Big(\Big[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\notin U\end{subarray}}^{n}x_{j}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big)P_{\nu}\big([m_{1}+\cdots+m_{i-1}];q,q^{c}\big)x_{u_{i-1}}^{|\nu|},

where
\nu
is over all partitions such that
\nu\subset\lambda
,
and
m_{j}:=q^{k_{i-1}-k_{j}}
for
j=1,\dots,i-1
.
We can further obtain that
\ell(\nu)\leq i-1
,
otherwise
P_{\nu}\big([m_{1}+\cdots+m_{i-1}];q,q^{c}\big)=0
by (
6.10
).
By the expression for
Q(d\>|\>u^{(i-1)};k^{(i-1)})
in (
9.10
), expanding
E_{u^{(i-1)},k^{(i-1)}}\big(P_{\mu}\big(\big[\frac{q^{c-b-1}-q^{a}}{1-q^{c}}x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\big];q,q^{c}\big)\big)
as a sum of monomials, using (
9.11
) and taking the constant term with respect to
x_{u_{i-1}}
, we can write
\CT\limits_{x}Q(d\>|\>u^{(i-1)};k^{(i-1)})
as a finite sum of the form

(9.12)

\CT_{x}\prod_{j\notin U}x_{j}^{-v_{j}}P_{\lambda/\nu}\Big(\Big[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\notin U\end{subarray}}^{n}x_{j}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq u<v\leq n\\
u,v\notin U\end{subarray}}\big(x_{u}/x_{v}\big)_{c}\big(qx_{j}/x_{i}\big)_{c},

where the
v_{j}
are integers such that
\sum_{j\notin U}v_{j}=|\lambda|-|\nu|
and

\max\{v_{j}\}\leq(i-1)c-d\leq\lambda_{i}-1<\lambda_{\ell(\nu)+1}
for
\ell(\nu)<i
.
Then for
\lambda_{\ell(\nu)+1}>\max\{v_{j}\}
and
\lambda_{\ell(\nu)+1}\geq\lambda_{i}>1
, we can apply Proposition
6.6
and find that each constant term of the form
(
9.12
) vanishes. Therefore,
\CT\limits_{x}Q(d\>|\>u^{(i-1)};k^{(i-1)})=0
for
(i-1)c-\lambda_{i}+1\leq d<(i-1)c
.
∎

9.3.
Determination of the roots
A_{3}
for
A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
if
\ell(\mu)\leq n

In this subsection, we will determine the roots
A_{3}
defined in (
1.7
) for

A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
under the assumption
\ell(\mu)\leq n
.
Note that if
\ell(\mu)\leq n
then all the elements of
A_{3}
are negative integers.

Lemma 9.4
.

The constant term
A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
vanishes for
a\in A_{3}
if
\ell(\mu)\leq n
.

Proof.

We can write
A_{3}=\cup_{j=1}^{\ell(\mu)}A_{3,j}
,
where

A_{3,j}=\{-(n-j)c-b-1,-(n-j)c-b-2,\dots,-(n-j)c-b-\mu_{j}\}.

By (
9.1
) we prove this lemma by showing that

(9.13)

\CT_{x}Q(d)=0\quad\text{for $-d\in A_{3,j}$ if $1\leq j\leq\ell(\mu)\leq n$}.

Let
j\in\{1,2,\dots,\ell(\mu)\}
be a fixed integer.
For
-d\in A_{3,j}
, we have
(n-j)c+b+1\leq d\leq(n-j)c+b+\mu_{j}
.
Applying the Gessel–Xin operation to
\CT\limits_{x}Q(d)
gives

(9.14)

\CT_{x}Q(d)=\sum_{s\in T\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq u_{1}<\cdots<u_{s}\leq n\\
1\leq k_{1},\dots,k_{s}\leq d\end{subarray}}\CT_{x}Q(d\>|\>u_{1},\dots,u_{s};k_{1},\dots,k_{s}).

As the discussion in the proof of Lemma
9.3
,
it is not hard to show that the
s
in (
9.14
) can only be
n-j+1
.
Hence, (
9.14
) reduces to

(9.15)

\CT_{x}Q(d)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq u_{1}<\cdots<u_{n-j+1}\leq n\\
1\leq k_{1},\dots,k_{n-j+1}\leq d\end{subarray}}\CT_{x}Q(d\>|\>u^{(n-j+1)};k^{(n-j+1)}),

where
u^{(n-j+1)}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n-j+1})
and
k^{(n-j+1)}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n-j+1})
.
We prove that every summand
\CT\limits_{x}Q(d\>|\>u^{(n-j+1)};k^{(n-j+1)})
in (
9.15
) vanishes.

Since
(n-j)c+b+1\leq d\leq(n-j)c+b+\mu_{j}
, we can write
d=(n-j)c+b+t
for
t\in\{1,\dots,\mu_{j}\}
.
It follows that
1\leq k_{i}\leq(n-j)c+b+t
. By Lemma
8.1
with
s=n-j+1
, at least one of the following holds:

(i)

1\leq k_{i}\leq b
for some
i
with
1\leq i\leq n-j+1
;

(ii)

-c\leq k_{r}-k_{l}\leq c-1
for some
(r,l)
such that
1\leq r<l\leq n-j+1
;

(iii)

there exists a permutation
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n-j+1}
and nonnegative integers
t_{1},\dots,t_{n-j+1}
such that

(9.16)

k_{w(1)}=b+t_{1},

and

k_{w(i)}-k_{w(i-1)}=c+t_{i}\quad\text{for $2\leq i\leq n-j+1$.}

Here the
t_{i}
satisfy

\sum_{i=1}^{n-j+1}t_{i}\leq t,

w(0):=0
, and
t_{i}>0
if
w(i-1)<w(i)
for
1\leq i\leq n-j+1
.

If (i) holds, then
Q(d\>|\>u^{(n-j+1)};k^{(n-j+1)})=0
since it has a zero factor
(q^{1-k_{i}})_{b}
. If (ii) holds, then
Q(d\>|\>u^{(n-j+1)};k^{(n-j+1)})=0
since it has a zero factor

(q^{k_{l}-k_{r}-c})_{2c}
. We omit the details because it is the same as that in the proof of Lemma
9.3
.
If (iii) holds, we also show that
Q(d\>|\>u^{(n-j+1)};k^{(n-j+1)})
has a zero factor.
By Lemma
8.2
with
s=n-j+1

-\frac{q^{c-b-1}-q^{-d}}{1-q}x_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{n-j+1}\frac{1-q^{c}}{1-q}x_{u_{i}}\bigg|_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=(n-j)c+b+t,\\[1.0pt]
x_{u_{i}}=q^{k_{n-j+1}-k_{i}}x_{u_{n-j+1}},\,0\leq i\leq n-j+1\end{subarray}}\\
=(q^{n_{1}}+\dots+q^{n_{t-1}})x_{u_{n-j+1}},

where
\{n_{1},\dots,n_{t-1}\}
is a set of integers determined by
n,j,b,c
and the
k_{i}
.
It follows that

\frac{q^{c-b-1}-q^{-d}}{1-q^{c}}x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\bigg|_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=(n-j)c+b+t,\\[1.0pt]
x_{u_{i}}=q^{k_{n-j+1}-k_{i}}x_{u_{n-j+1}},\,0\leq i\leq n-j+1\end{subarray}}\\
=\frac{1-q}{q^{c}-1}\big(q^{n_{1}}+\dots+q^{n_{t-1}}\big)x_{u_{n-j+1}}+\sum_{i=1,i\notin U}^{n}x_{i},

where
U:=\{u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{n-j+1}\}
. Then
Q(d\>|\>u^{(n-j+1)};k^{(n-j+1)})
has the factor

E_{u^{(n-j+1)},k^{(n-j+1)}}\Big(P_{\mu}\big(\big[\frac{q^{c-b-1}-q^{-d}}{1-q^{c}}x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\big];q,q^{c}\big)\Big)\\
=P_{\mu}\Big(\Big[\frac{1-q}{q^{c}-1}\big(q^{n_{1}}+\dots+q^{n_{t-1}}\big)x_{u_{n-j+1}}+\sum_{i=1,i\notin U}^{n}x_{i}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big),

which is zero for
1\leq t\leq\mu_{j}
by Proposition
6.2
with

(i,\lambda_{i},t)\mapsto(j,\mu_{j},q^{c})
.
Hence, for
d\in\{(n-j)c+b+1,\dots,(n-j)c+b+\mu_{j}\}
,
we have
\CT\limits_{x}Q(d\>|\>u^{(n-j+1)};k^{(n-j+1)})=0
.
∎

9.4.
Determination of the roots
A_{3}
for
A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
if
\ell(\mu)>n

In this subsection, we will determine the roots
A_{3}
defined in (
1.7
) for

A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
under the assumption
\ell(\mu)>n
.

Recall that

A_{3}=\{-(n-j)c-b-1,-(n-j)c-b-2,\dots,-(n-j)c-b-\mu_{j}\>|\>j=1,\dots,\ell(\mu)\}.

Let

(9.17)

A_{31}:=\{-(n-j)c-b-1,-(n-j)c-b-2,\dots,-(n-j)c-b-\mu_{j}\>|\>j=1,\dots,n\}

and

(9.18)

A_{32}:=\{-(n-j)c-b-1,-(n-j)c-b-2,\dots,-(n-j)c-b-\mu_{j}\>|\>j=n+1,\dots,\ell(\mu)\}

if
\ell(\mu)>n
.
Then
A_{3}=A_{31}\cup A_{32}
.
Note that all the elements in
A_{31}
(resp.
A_{32}
) are negative (resp. positive) integers if
\ell(\mu)>n
and
c>b+\lambda_{1}+\mu_{1}
.
We can carry out the same discussion as Lemma
9.4
to determine the roots

a\in A_{31}
for
A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
. We state this result in the next lemma and omit the proof.

Lemma 9.5
.

If
\ell(\mu)>n
, then
the constant term
A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
vanishes for
a\in A_{31}
.

Then we prove
A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)=0
for
a\in A_{32}
.
That is the content of the next lemma.

Lemma 9.6
.

If
\ell(\mu)>n
, then the constant term
A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
vanishes for
a\in A_{32}
.

Proof.

For
a\in A_{32}
,
we can write
a=(j-n)c-b-t
, where
j\in\{n+1,\dots,\ell(\mu)\}
and
t\in\{1,\dots,\mu_{j}\}
for each
j
.
Note that
a>0
by the conditions
j\geq n+1
,
t\leq\mu_{j}
and
c>b+\lambda_{1}+\mu_{1}
.
Then

\displaystyle\frac{q^{c-b-1}-q^{a}}{1-q^{c}}\Big|_{a=(j-n)c-b-t}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{c-b-1}-q^{(j-n)c-b-t}}{1-q^{c}}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{c-b-1}-q^{(j-n)c-b-1}+q^{(j-n)c-b-1}-q^{(j-n)c-b-t}}{1-q^{c}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-q^{(j-n-1)c}}{1-q^{c}}q^{c-b-1}+\frac{1-q^{t-1}}{q^{c}-1}q^{(j-n)c-b-t}.

Using the above equation, we can write

(9.19)

P_{\mu}\Big(\Big[\frac{q^{c-b-1}-q^{a}}{1-q^{c}}x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big)\Big|_{a=(j-n)c-b-t}\\
=P_{\mu}\Big(\Big[\frac{1-q^{(j-n-1)c}}{1-q^{c}}q^{c-b-1}x_{0}+\frac{1-q^{t-1}}{q^{c}-1}q^{(j-n)c-b-t}x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big).

The right-hand side of (
9.19
) is in fact of the form

(9.20)

P_{\mu}\Big(\Big[\frac{1-q}{q^{c}-1}(m_{1}+\cdots+m_{t-1})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}+r_{1}+\cdots+r_{j-n-1}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big),

where
m_{i}=q^{(j-n)c-b-t+i-1}x_{0}
for
i=1,\dots,t-1
,
and
r_{i}=q^{ic-b-1}x_{0}
for
i=1,\dots,j-n-1
.
Then by Proposition
6.2
,
for
j\in\{n+1,\dots,\ell(\mu)\}
and
1\leq t\leq\mu_{j}
the symmetric function (
9.20
) vanishes.
It follows that if
\ell(\mu)>n
and
a\in A_{32}
we can conclude that

A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)=0

since its factor
P_{\mu}\big(\big[\frac{q^{c-b-1}-q^{a}}{1-q^{c}}x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\big];q,q^{c}\big)

vanishes by the above discussion.
∎

9.5.
The value of
A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
at an additional point

In this subsection, we characterize the expressions for
A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)

when
a=-b-1
if
\ell(\mu)<n
and
a=(\ell(\mu)-n+1)c-b-1
if
\ell(\mu)\geq n
respectively.

We obtain a recursion for
A_{n}(-b-1,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
when
\ell(\mu)<n
.

Proposition 9.7
.

For
\ell(\mu)<n

(9.21)

A_{n}(-b-1,b,c,\lambda,\mu)=(-1)^{b}q^{-\binom{b+1}{2}-(b+1)\lambda_{n}}\frac{1-q^{nc}}{1-q^{c}}A_{n-1}(c-b-1,b+c,c,\overline{\mu},\overline{\lambda}),

where
\overline{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{n},\dots,\lambda_{n-1}-\lambda_{n})

and
\overline{\mu}=(\mu_{1}+\lambda_{n},\dots,u_{n-1}+\lambda_{n})
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.2
, we have

(9.22)

A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)=\frac{1}{n!}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{1-q^{(i+1)c}}{1-q^{c}}A^{\prime}_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu),

where

A^{\prime}_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)=\CT_{x}x_{0}^{-|\lambda|-|\mu|}P_{\lambda}(x;q,q^{c})P_{\mu}\Big(\Big[\frac{q^{c-b-1}-q^{a}}{1-q^{c}}x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big)\\
\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{x_{0}}{x_{i}}\Big)_{a}\Big(\frac{qx_{i}}{x_{0}}\Big)_{b}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\Big(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}\Big)_{c}.

We obtain a recursion for
A^{\prime}_{n}(-b-1,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
.

We can write

A^{\prime}_{n}(-b-1,b,c,\lambda,\mu)=\CT_{x}N/D,

where

N=x_{0}^{-|\lambda|-|\mu|}P_{\lambda}(x;q,q^{c})P_{\mu}\Big(\Big[\frac{q^{c-b-1}-q^{-b-1}}{1-q^{c}}x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big)\\
\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}(qx_{i}/x_{0})_{b}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}(x_{i}/x_{j})_{c}

and

D=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(q^{-b-1}x_{0}/x_{i})_{b+1}.

We find that
N
is a Laurent polynomial in
x_{0}
with degree at most
-|\lambda|
, and
D
is of the form
(
4.3
). Then, applying Lemma
4.1
to
N/D
with respect to
x_{0}
gives

(9.23)

A^{\prime}_{n}(-b-1,b,c,\lambda,\mu)=\sum_{u_{1}=1}^{n}\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{b+1}\CT_{x}P_{\lambda}(x;q,q^{c})P_{\mu}\Big(\Big[-q^{-b-1}x_{u_{1}}q^{k_{1}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big)\\
\times\frac{(q^{1-k_{1}})_{b}}{(q^{k_{1}-b-1})_{b+1-k_{1}}(q)_{k_{1}-1}(x_{u_{1}}q^{k_{1}})^{|\lambda|+|\mu|}}\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq u_{1}\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{\big(q^{1-k_{1}}x_{i}/x_{u_{1}}\big)_{b}}{(q^{k_{1}-b-1}x_{u_{1}}/x_{i})_{b+1}}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}(x_{i}/x_{j})_{c}.

Since the factor
(q^{1-k_{1}})_{b}=0
for
1\leq k_{1}\leq b
, the summand in the right-hand side of (
9.23
) does not vanish only when
k_{1}=b+1
.
Therefore,
A^{\prime}_{n}(-b-1,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
reduces to

A^{\prime}_{n}(-b-1,b,c,\lambda,\mu)=C_{1}\sum_{u_{1}=1}^{n}\CT_{x}(x_{u_{1}})^{-|\lambda|-|\mu|}P_{\lambda}(x;q,q^{c})P_{\mu}\Big(\Big[-x_{u_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big)\\
\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq u_{1}\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{\big(q^{-b}x_{i}/x_{u_{1}}\big)_{b}}{(x_{u_{1}}/x_{i})_{b+1}}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}(x_{i}/x_{j})_{c},

where
C_{1}=(-1)^{b}q^{-\binom{b+1}{2}-(b+1)(|\lambda|+|\mu|)}
.
It is clear that the summand is symmetric for
u_{i}=1,\dots,n
. Thus, we can further write

A^{\prime}_{n}(-b-1,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
as

(9.24)

\displaystyle nC_{1}\times\CT_{x}x_{n}^{-|\lambda|-|\mu|}P_{\lambda}(x;q,q^{c})P_{\mu}\Big(\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{i}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big)\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{\big(q^{-b}x_{i}/x_{n}\big)_{b}}{(x_{n}/x_{i})_{b+1}}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}(x_{i}/x_{j})_{c}

\displaystyle\quad=nC_{1}\times\CT_{x}x_{n}^{-|\lambda|-|\mu|}P_{\lambda}(x;q,q^{c})P_{\mu}\Big(\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{i}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(q^{b+1}x_{n}/x_{i})_{c-b-1}\big(q^{-b}x_{i}/x_{n}\big)_{b+c}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n-1}(x_{i}/x_{j})_{c}.

Here we use the fact that
(x_{n}/x_{i})_{b+1}
is a factor of
(x_{n}/x_{i})_{c}
for
c>b+\lambda_{1}+\mu_{1}\geq b
.

Taking
x_{n}q^{b+1}\mapsto x_{0}
in the right-hand side of (
9.24
), we obtain

A^{\prime}_{n}(-b-1,b,c,\lambda,\mu)=(-1)^{b}nq^{-\binom{b+1}{2}}\CT_{x}P_{\mu}\Big(\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{i}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big)P_{\lambda}\Big(\Big[q^{-b-1}x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{i}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big)\\
\times x_{0}^{-|\lambda|-|\mu|}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(x_{0}/x_{i})_{c-b-1}\big(qx_{i}/x_{0}\big)_{b+c}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n-1}(x_{i}/x_{j})_{c}.

Note that the substitution
x_{n}q^{b+1}\mapsto x_{0}
in the above does not change the constant term.
Using Proposition
4.2
again, we have

(9.25)

A^{\prime}_{n}(-b-1,b,c,\lambda,\mu)=(-1)^{b}n!q^{-\binom{b+1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-2}\frac{1-q^{c}}{1-q^{(i+1)c}}\CT_{x}x_{0}^{-|\lambda|-|\mu|}P_{\mu}\Big(\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{i}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big)\\
\times P_{\lambda}\Big(\Big[q^{-b-1}x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{i}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big)\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(x_{0}/x_{i})_{c-b-1}\big(qx_{i}/x_{0}\big)_{b+c}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n-1}\Big(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}\Big)_{c}\Big(\frac{qx_{j}}{x_{i}}\Big)_{c}.

From (
6.11
),

P_{\mu}\Big(\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{i}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big)P_{\lambda}\Big(\Big[q^{-b-1}x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{i}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big)\\
=(q^{-(b+1)}x_{0})^{\lambda_{n}}P_{\overline{\mu}}\Big(\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{i}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big)P_{\overline{\lambda}}\Big(\Big[q^{-b-1}x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{i}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big),

where
\overline{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{n},\dots,\lambda_{n-1}-\lambda_{n})

and
\overline{\mu}=(\mu_{1}+\lambda_{n},\dots,u_{n-1}+\lambda_{n})
.
Substituting this into (
9.25
) gives

(9.26)

A^{\prime}_{n}(-b-1,b,c,\lambda,\mu)=(-1)^{b}n!q^{-\binom{b+1}{2}-(b+1)\lambda_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-2}\frac{1-q^{c}}{1-q^{(i+1)c}}\CT_{x}\frac{P_{\overline{\mu}}\Big(\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{i}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big)}{x_{0}^{|\lambda|+|\mu|-\lambda_{n}}}\\
\times P_{\overline{\lambda}}\Big(\Big[q^{-b-1}x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{i}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big)\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(x_{0}/x_{i})_{c-b-1}\big(qx_{i}/x_{0}\big)_{b+c}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n-1}\Big(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}\Big)_{c}\Big(\frac{qx_{j}}{x_{i}}\Big)_{c}.

The constant term in the right-hand side of (
9.26
) is
A_{n-1}(c-b-1,b+c,c,\overline{\mu},\overline{\lambda})
.
Together with (
9.22
) (with
a\mapsto-b-1
) gives (
9.21
).
∎

If
\ell(\mu)=n
, then
A_{n}(-b-1,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
vanishes by Lemma
9.4
.
It also vanishes for
\ell(\mu)>n
by Lemma
9.5
.
Hence, we can not use this value as an additional value to determine

A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
if
\ell(\mu)\geq n
. For
\ell(\mu)\geq n
, we choose
a=(\ell(\mu)-n+1)c-b-1
as an additional point to determine
A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
.

Proposition 9.8
.

For
\ell(\mu):=l\geq n
,

(9.27)

A_{n}\big((l-n+1)c-b-1,b,c,\lambda,\mu\big)=q^{t}A_{n}\big(\big(l-n+1\big)c-b-1,b,c,\widetilde{\lambda},\widetilde{\mu}\big),

where
t=\Big(\binom{l-n+1}{2}c-(b+1)(l-n)\Big)\mu_{l}
,
\widetilde{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1}+\mu_{l},\dots,\lambda_{n}+\mu_{l})

and
\widetilde{\mu}=(\mu_{1}-\mu_{l},\dots,\mu_{l-1}-\mu_{l})
.

Proof.

If
l\geq n
,
then
(l-n+1)c-b-1
is a nonnegative integer by the assumption
c>b+\lambda_{1}+\mu_{1}
.
Since

\frac{q^{c-b-1}-q^{(l-n+1)c-b-1}}{1-q^{c}}=q^{c-b-1}\frac{1-q^{(l-n)c}}{1-q^{c}}=\sum_{i=1}^{l-n}q^{ic-b-1},

we write

(9.28)

A_{n}\big((l-n+1)c-b-1,b,c,\lambda,\mu\big)=\CT_{x}x_{0}^{-|\lambda|-|\mu|}P_{\lambda}(x;q,q^{c})P_{\mu}\Big(\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{l-n}q^{ic-b-1}x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big)\\
\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{x_{0}}{x_{i}}\Big)_{(l-n+1)c-b-1}\Big(\frac{qx_{i}}{x_{0}}\Big)_{b}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\Big(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}\Big)_{c}\Big(\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}q\Big)_{c}.

By (
6.11
), the right-hand side of (
9.28
) can be written as

(9.29)

q^{t}\CT_{x}x_{0}^{-|\lambda|-|\mu|+(l-n)\mu_{l}}P_{\widetilde{\lambda}}(x;q,q^{c})P_{\widetilde{\mu}}\Big(\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{l-n}q^{ic-b-1}x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\Big];q,q^{c}\Big)\\
\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{x_{0}}{x_{i}}\Big)_{\big(l-n+1\big)c-b-1}\Big(\frac{qx_{i}}{x_{0}}\Big)_{b}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\Big(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}\Big)_{c}\Big(\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}q\Big)_{c},

where
t
,
\widetilde{\lambda}
and
\widetilde{\mu}
are defined in the proposition.
The constant term in (
9.29
) is in fact
A_{n}\big(\big(l-n+1\big)c-b-1,b,c,\widetilde{\lambda},\widetilde{\mu}\big)
.
∎

9.6.
A recursion for
A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
and the proof of Theorem
1.1

Since we have characterized the additional value of
A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
in Proposition
9.7
and Proposition
9.8
, and we have determined all the roots of
A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
in Lemmas
9.1
–
9.6
, we can determine
A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
as a polynomial in
q^{a}
. In this subsection,
we obtain a recursion for
A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
. By the recursion, we give a proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We give a recursion for
A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
in the next theorem.

Theorem 9.9
.

For
\ell(\mu)\leq n
,

(9.30)

\displaystyle A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{b}q^{-\binom{b+1}{2}-(b+1)(\lambda_{n}+\mu_{n})}\frac{(1-q^{nc})}{(1-q^{c})}\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{(q^{a+ic+1})_{b}}{(q^{ic-b})_{b}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(q^{a+(i-1)c-\lambda_{i}+1})_{\lambda_{i}}(q^{ic-b-\lambda_{i}-\mu_{n}})_{\mu_{n}}}{(q^{(i-1)c-b-\lambda_{i}-\mu_{n}})_{\lambda_{i}+\mu_{n}}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(q^{a+(n-i)c+b+1})_{\mu_{i}}}{(q^{(n-i)c})_{\mu_{i}-\mu_{n}}(q^{(n-i+1)c+\mu_{i}-\mu_{n}})_{\mu_{n}}}A_{n-1}(c-b-1,b+c,c,\overline{\mu},\overline{\lambda}),

where
\overline{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{n},\dots,\lambda_{n-1}-\lambda_{n})

and
\overline{\mu}=(\mu_{1}+\lambda_{n},\dots,u_{n-1}+\lambda_{n})
.

Note that
\ell(\overline{\lambda})\leq n-1
and
\ell(\overline{\mu})\leq n-1
. Then we can iterate this theorem to obtain a closed form expression for
A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
if
\ell(\mu)\leq n
.

Proof.

Since
A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
is a polynomial in
q^{a}
with degree
nc+|\lambda|+|\mu|
by Corollary
7.2
, we can explicitly determine it if we find all its roots and one additional value.
By Proposition
9.7
and Lemmas
9.1
–
9.6
, it is straightforward to
obtain the following recursion for
A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
if
\ell(\mu)<n
:

(9.31)

\displaystyle A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{(q^{a+ic+1})_{b}}{(q^{ic-b})_{b}}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\frac{(q^{a+(i-1)c-\lambda_{i}+1})_{\lambda_{i}}}{(q^{(i-1)c-\lambda_{i}-b})_{\lambda_{i}}}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\mu)}\frac{(q^{a+(n-i)c+b+1})_{\mu_{i}}}{(q^{(n-i)c})_{\mu_{i}}}

\displaystyle\quad\times(-1)^{b}q^{-\binom{b+1}{2}-(b+1)\lambda_{n}}\frac{1-q^{nc}}{1-q^{c}}A_{n-1}(c-b-1,b+c,c,\overline{\mu},\overline{\lambda}),

where
\overline{\lambda}
and
\overline{\mu}
are defined in the theorem.

If
\ell(\mu)=n
then by Proposition
9.8
we have

A_{n}(c-b-1,b,c,\lambda,\mu)=A_{n}(c-b-1,b,c,\lambda^{*},\mu^{*}),

where
\lambda^{*}=(\lambda_{1}+\mu_{n},\dots,\lambda_{n}+\mu_{n})

and
\mu^{*}=(\mu_{1}-\mu_{n},\dots,\mu_{n-1}-\mu_{n})
. Then
\ell(\mu^{*})=n-1
. Applying (
9.31
) to
A_{n}(c-b-1,b,c,\lambda^{*},\mu^{*})
yields

\displaystyle A_{n}(c-b-1,b,c,\lambda,\mu)

\displaystyle=A_{n}(c-b-1,b,c,\lambda^{*},\mu^{*})

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{(q^{(i+1)c-b})_{b}}{(q^{ic-b})_{b}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(q^{ic-b-\lambda_{i}-\mu_{n}})_{\lambda_{i}+\mu_{n}}}{(q^{(i-1)c-b-\lambda_{i}-\mu_{n}})_{\lambda_{i}+\mu_{n}}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{(q^{(n-i+1)c})_{\mu_{i}-\mu_{n}}}{(q^{(n-i)c})_{\mu_{i}-\mu_{n}}}

\displaystyle\times(-1)^{b}q^{-\binom{b+1}{2}-(b+1)(\lambda_{n}+\mu_{n})}\frac{1-q^{nc}}{1-q^{c}}A_{n-1}(c-b-1,b+c,c,\overline{\mu},\overline{\lambda}).

Together with the fact that
A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
is a polynomial in
q^{a}
with degree
nc+|\lambda|+|\mu|
and we have determined all its roots by Lemmas
9.1
–
9.6
, we obtain

(9.32)

\displaystyle A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{(q^{a+ic+1})_{b}}{(q^{(i+1)c-b})_{b}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(q^{a+(i-1)c-\lambda_{i}+1})_{\lambda_{i}}}{(q^{ic-\lambda_{i}-b})_{\lambda_{i}}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(q^{a+(n-i)c+b+1})_{\mu_{i}}}{(q^{(n-i+1)c})_{\mu_{i}}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{(q^{(i+1)c-b})_{b}}{(q^{ic-b})_{b}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(q^{ic-b-\lambda_{i}-\mu_{n}})_{\lambda_{i}+\mu_{n}}}{(q^{(i-1)c-b-\lambda_{i}-\mu_{n}})_{\lambda_{i}+\mu_{n}}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{(q^{(n-i+1)c})_{\mu_{i}-\mu_{n}}}{(q^{(n-i)c})_{\mu_{i}-\mu_{n}}}

\displaystyle\quad\times(-1)^{b}q^{-\binom{b+1}{2}-(b+1)(\lambda_{n}+\mu_{n})}\frac{1-q^{nc}}{1-q^{c}}A_{n-1}(c-b-1,b+c,c,\overline{\mu},\overline{\lambda})

for
\ell(\mu)=n
. Substituting

\frac{(q^{ic-b-\lambda_{i}-\mu_{n}})_{\lambda_{i}+\mu_{n}}}{(q^{ic-b-\lambda_{i}})_{\lambda_{i}}}=(q^{ic-b-\lambda_{i}-\mu_{n}})_{\mu_{n}}

and

\frac{(q^{(n-i+1)c})_{\mu_{i}-\mu_{n}}}{(q^{(n-i+1)c})_{\mu_{i}}}=\frac{1}{(q^{(n-i+1)c+\mu_{i}-\mu_{n}})_{\mu_{n}}}

into (
9.32
), we obtain (
9.30
).
∎

Now we can complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

If
\ell(\mu)\leq n
, then
A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
is determined by its recursion (
9.30
)
and the initial value
A_{0}(a,b,c,0,0)=1
. Hence, to prove the theorem for
\ell(\mu)\leq n
,
it is straightforward to verify that the right-hand side of (
1.5
) satisfies the same recursion and initial condition. To carry out this verification, one may need (
6.13
).

For
\ell(\mu)=l>n
, we can obtain a formula for
A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
by all its roots (Lemmas
9.1
–
9.6
) and the value
A_{n}\big((l-n+1)c-b-1,b,c,\lambda,\mu\big)
.
This additional value
A_{n}\big((l-n+1)c-b-1,b,c,\lambda,\mu\big)
is determined by (
9.27
)
and the recursion for
A_{n}(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu)
for
\ell(\mu)\leq n
.

For example, we can get a recursion for
A_{n}\big(2c-b-1,b,c,\lambda,\mu\big)
if
\ell(\mu)=n+1
.
By (
9.27
) we have

A_{n}\big(2c-b-1,b,c,\lambda,\mu\big)=q^{(c-b-1)\mu_{n+1}}A_{n}(2c-b-1,b,c,\widetilde{\lambda},\widetilde{\mu}),

where
\widetilde{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1}+\mu_{n+1},\dots,\lambda_{n}+\mu_{n+1})

and
\widetilde{\mu}=(\mu_{1}-\mu_{n+1},\dots,\mu_{n}-\mu_{n+1})
. Then
\ell(\widetilde{\mu})=n
and we can apply
(
9.30
) to
A_{n}(2c-b-1,b,c,\widetilde{\lambda},\widetilde{\mu})
to obtain a recursion for

A_{n}\big(2c-b-1,b,c,\lambda,\mu\big)
. This leads to a recursion for
A_{n}\big(a,b,c,\lambda,\mu\big)
for
\ell(\mu)=n+1
.

By the above argument, to prove the theorem for the
\ell(\mu)>n
case, it suffices to check that
the right-hand side of (
1.5
) satisfies the same recursion as (
9.30
) for
\ell(\mu)=n
and
the same inductive relation as (
9.27
) for
\ell(\mu)>n
and
a=\big(\ell(\mu)-n+1\big)c-b-1
.
These verifications are straightforward.
∎

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate a data-driven framework to solve Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) problems when the dynamics is unknown, with the additional challenge of providing stability certificates for the overall learning and control scheme. Specifically, in the proposed on-policy learning framework, the control input is applied to the actual (unknown) linear system while iteratively optimized. We propose a learning and control procedure, termed Relearn LQR, that combines a recursive least squares method with a direct policy search based on the gradient method. The resulting scheme is analyzed by modeling it as a feedback-interconnected nonlinear dynamical system. A Lyapunov-based approach, exploiting averaging and timescale separation theories for nonlinear systems, allows us to provide formal stability guarantees for the whole interconnected scheme. The effectiveness of the proposed strategy is corroborated by numerical simulations, where Relearn LQR is deployed on an aircraft control problem, with both static and drifting parameters.

Introduction

The massive availability of data across automation and robotics applications pushed the control community to revise the traditional model-based optimal control approaches toward a learning-driven scenario.
In these solutions, the control policy is iteratively updated without an explicit knowledge of the underlying dynamical system, relying solely on the collected data.
Consequently, the fundamental distinction between off-policy and on-policy methods arises from the interconnection between the collected data and the current policy.
More in detail, off-policy algorithms pursue a value iteration approach, and data are, in general, independent of the current policy.
Conversely, on-policy algorithms employ a policy iteration framework and evaluate the performance of the current policy using data originated by the system under the same (under evaluation) policy.

The main contribution of this paper is the development of a data-driven
on-policy control scheme with stability certificates in the context of
LQR for unknown systems.
Specifically, the estimated control policy is applied to the actual
(unknown) linear system, while it is concurrently refined toward the
optimal solution of the LQR problem.
The proposed method, termed
Relearn
LQR, short for REcurvise LEARNing policy gradient for LQR, relies on the so-called
direct policy
search
reformulation of the LQR problem, which is an optimization
problem with the control policy gain
K
being the decision
variable.
This optimization problem, with cost function parametrized by the
system matrices
({A}_{\star},{B}_{\star})
, is addressed via a gradient-based method
combined with an estimation procedure to deal with the missing
knowledge of
({A}_{\star},{B}_{\star})
.
In particular, the system matrices are progressively reconstructed via
a Recursive Least Squares (RLS) mechanism that iteratively elaborates
the state-input samples obtained from the actual, closed-loop system.
The on-policy nature of
Relearn
LQR stems from the fact that each
state-input sample is gathered by actuating the (yet non-optimal)
state feedback.
To ensure persistency of excitation, a probing dithering signal is
also fed into the (running) closed-loop dynamics.
The stability certificates for the learning and control closed-loop
system are proved by resorting to Lyapunov arguments and averaging
theory for two-time-scale systems.
Specifically, for the whole closed-loop system consisting of the
gradient update on the gain
K
, the RLS scheme, and the system
dynamics, we show the exponential stability of a properly defined
steady state, in which: (i) the feedback policy is the optimal
solution of the LQR problem; (ii) the estimates of the unknown
matrices are exact; and (iii) the system state oscillates about the
origin with an amplitude arbitrarily tunable by setting the dither
magnitude.

The key strength of the proposed architecture (and analysis) lies in its inherent robustness.
The stability properties we prove ensure that the algorithm remains effective even in non-nominal conditions, where system and cost matrices change over time and/or disturbances affect the plant and the measurements.
In such scenarios, the closed-loop system adapts dynamically, restoring optimality without requiring any restart of either the optimization or the learning process.

To the best of the authors’ knowledge, our attempt is the first one (in discrete-time LQR) that provides a stability certificate for the overall closed-loop system that simultaneously addresses optimization, learning, and control tasks.
More in detail, existing data-driven approaches (see, e.g.,
[
14
]
) are not on-policy, namely, they are characterized by two distinct phases in which system samples are collected and then used to find the optimal gain.
An alternative, popular approach (see, e.g.,
[
31
,
7
]
) is based on improving the tentative policy by performing so-called experiments of the actuated system to evaluate it.
The main drawback of these policy-iteration approaches is that they are not online, namely, the real plant needs to be repeatedly initialized and actuated for a number of samples at each algorithm iteration.
Another branch of literature relies on the certainty equivalence principle, see, e.g.,
[
24
]
, which relates model and gain matrices accuracy and, thus, treats the optimization task as a mere corollary of the learning phase.
However, to the best of the authors’ knowledge, also this class of works do not focus on stability aspects of the overall closed-loop dynamics that tries to concurrently optimize the gain, learn the system matrices, and control the real plant.

2.2
On-Policy Data-Driven LQR: Problem Setup

In this paper, we focus on the LQR problem

\displaystyle\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{x}_{1},\mathrm{x}_{2},\ldots,\\
\mathrm{u}_{0},\mathrm{u}_{1},\ldots\end{subarray}}\>\>

\displaystyle\>\mathbb{E}\bigg[\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\Big({\mathrm{\mathrm{x}}_{t}}^{\top}Q\mathrm{\mathrm{x}}_{t}+{\mathrm{u}_{t}}^{\top}R\mathrm{u}_{t}\Big)\bigg]

(5a)

subj. to

\displaystyle\>\mathrm{\mathrm{x}}_{{t+1}}={A}_{\star}\mathrm{\mathrm{x}}_{t}+{B}_{\star}\mathrm{u}_{t},\qquad\qquad\mathrm{x}_{0}\sim\mathcal{X}_{0},

(5b)

where
\mathrm{\mathrm{x}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathrm{u}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
denote, respectively, the state and the input of the system at time
t\in\mathbb{N}
, while
{A}_{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{{n}\times{n}}
and
{B}_{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{{n}\times{m}}
represent the state and the input matrices.
The cost matrices
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{{n}\times{n}}
and
R\in\mathbb{R}^{{m}\times{m}}
are both symmetric and positive definite, i.e.,
Q=Q^{\top}>0
and
R=R^{\top}>0
.
As for the initial condition
\mathrm{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we assume that it is drawn from a (known) probability distribution
\mathcal{X}_{0}
.
Hence, the operator
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
denotes the expected value with respect to
\mathcal{X}_{0}
.
Importantly, we enforce the following properties on
({A}_{\star},{B}_{\star})
.

Assumption 2.7
(Unknown System Properties)

The pair
({A}_{\star},{B}_{\star})
is unknown and controllable.

\blacksquare

Notice that the positive definiteness of
Q
can be relaxed to mere positive semidefiniteness, provided that the pair
({A}_{\star},Q_{0})
is detectable with
Q:=Q_{0}^{\top}Q_{0}
.

As it will be useful later, we collect the pair
({A}_{\star},{B}_{\star})
in a single variable
\theta^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{(n+m)\times n}
defined as

\displaystyle\theta^{\star}:=\begin{bmatrix}{A}_{\star}&{B}_{\star}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}.

(6)

It is worth noting that, in light of Assumption
2.7
, by continuity, there exists
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{(n+m)\times n}
such that
[{A}_{\star}\hskip 1.42271pt{B}_{\star}]^{\top}\in\Theta
and
(A,B)
is controllable for all
[A\hskip 1.42271ptB]^{\top}\in\Theta
.
We denote by
\Theta_{b}\subseteq\Theta
the largest ball contained in
\Theta
and centered in
\theta^{\star}
.
It is well-known that, when
({A}_{\star},{B}_{\star})
are known the optimal solution to problem (
5
) is given by
a linear time-invariant policy
\mathrm{u}_{t}=K^{\star}\mathrm{\mathrm{x}}_{t}
with
K^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{{m}\times{n}}
given by

\displaystyle K^{\star}=-(R+{B}_{\star}^{\top}P^{\star}{B}_{\star})^{-1}{B}_{\star}^{\top}P^{\star}{A}_{\star},

where

solves the Discrete-time Algebraic Riccati Equation associated to problem (

), see
[
46
]
.

In this paper, we are interested in devising a data-driven on-policy strategy to design a state-feedback controller solution of (
5
).
Hence, the problem we address can be posed as follows.

Stability-certified on-policy LQR:

design a learning and control scheme that is capable of

(i)

learning the optimal policy solution to problem (
5
),

(ii)

estimating the unknown system matrices,

(iii)

actuating the (real) system with the currently available state-feedback policy,

while ensuring asymptotic stability of the closed-loop learning and control system.

As shown in Figure
1
, the proposed scheme aims to iteratively compute a tentative policy, say
\pi_{t}(\cdot)
, to be actuated on the real, though unknown, system.
One single sample of the system evolution is then collected and fed into a learning mechanism that refines the system matrices, which, in turn, are used to improve the quality of the previously actuated policy.
The distinctive feature of the proposed approach is that the aforementioned steps are interwoven rather than temporally separate, as is typically done in the literature.

2.3
Preliminaries: Model-based Gradient Method for LQR

Next, we recall the key ingredients for devising a model-based gradient method to address problem (
5
).

2.3.1
Model-based reduced problem formulation

First of all, we recall an equivalent (unconstrained) formulation of problem (
5
) that explicitly imposes the linear feedback structure to the optimal input and is amenable for gradient-based algorithmic solutions.
Problem (
5
) is rewritten by substituting in the dynamics and in the cost function the input in linear feedback form
\mathrm{u}_{t}=K\mathrm{\mathrm{x}}_{t}
,
where
K\in\mathbb{R}^{{m}\times{n}}
is to be computed.
First of all, given any gain
K
, the original (open-loop) dynamics (
5b
) admits the closed-loop formulation
\mathrm{\mathrm{x}}_{{t+1}}=({A}_{\star}+{B}_{\star}K)\mathrm{\mathrm{x}}_{t}
.
So that, for all
t\geq 0
, the state is uniquely determined as

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathrm{x}}_{t}=({A}_{\star}+{B}_{\star}K)^{t}\mathrm{x}_{0},\qquad\mathrm{x}_{0}\sim\mathcal{X}_{0}.

(7)

Hence, leveraging on (
7
), assuming,
without loss of generality
, that
\mathcal{X}_{0}
is a uniform distribution on the unit sphere, and taking the expected value on the initial condition, problem (
5
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\min_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\>

\displaystyle\>J(K,\theta^{\star}),

(8)

where
\theta^{\star}
is defined in (
6
), while, given the set of stabilizing gains
\mathcal{K}:=\{K\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\mid{A}_{\star}+{B}_{\star}K\text{ is Schur}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, we introduced
J:\mathcal{K}\times\mathbb{R}^{(n+m)\times n}\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

\displaystyle J(K,\theta^{\star})=

(9)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\Tr\left(\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}({A}_{\star}+{B}_{\star}K)^{t,\top}(Q+K^{\top}RK)({A}_{\star}+{B}_{\star}K)^{t}\right).

This formulation highlights that (i) the overall problem actually depends on the gain
K
only, and, (ii) the optimal gain
K^{\star}
does not depend on the initial condition
\mathrm{x}_{0}
.

Remark 2.8

Due to the linearity of the expected value operator and the properties of the trace, the formulation in (
9
) holds up to a constant scaling factor for any probability distribution
\mathcal{X}_{0}
with a well-defined second moment.

2.3.2
Model-based gradient method for problem (
8
)

The set of stabilizing gains
\mathcal{K}
is open
[

, Lemma IV.3]
and connected
[

Moreover, the cost function

is coercive

Had the pair

been known, the gradient descent method could have been used to solve problem (

Namely, at each iteration

, an estimate

is maintained and iteratively updated according to

\displaystyle K_{{t+1}}=K_{t}-\gamma G(K_{t},\theta^{\star}),

(10)

where

is the stepsize, while

is the gradient of

with respect to

evaluated at
(K_{t},\theta^{\star})
, when

is equipped with the Frobenius inner product.
It is possible to show that, by initializing
K_{0}\in\mathcal{K}
and selecting a proper stepsize

, the optimal gain
K^{\star}
is an exponentially stable equilibrium of the dynamical system (
10
), see
[
30
, Theorem 4.6]
.
The procedure to compute and evaluate the gradient
G(K_{t},\theta^{\star})
reads as follows:

(i)

solve for
W^{c}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and

the equations

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\hskip-21.52771pt({A}_{\star}+{B}_{\star}K_{t})W^{c}_{t}({A}_{\star}+{B}_{\star}K_{t})^{\top}-W^{c}_{t}=-I_{n}\\
&\hskip-21.52771pt({A}_{\star}+{B}_{\star}K_{t})^{\top}P_{t}({A}_{\star}+{B}_{\star}K_{t})-P_{t}=-(Q+{K_{t}}^{\top}RK_{t})\end{split}

(ii)

compute the gradient
G(K_{t},\theta^{\star})
as

\displaystyle G(K_{t},\theta^{\star})\!=\!\left(RK_{t}+{B}_{\star}^{\top}P_{t}({A}_{\star}+{B}_{\star}K_{t})\right)W^{c}_{t}.\!\!

(11)

Notice that our goal is to address the problem setup described in Section
2.2
without knowing
\theta^{\star}
.
Hence, in our framework, it is not possible to implement update (
10
).

3
On-policy LQR for Unknown Systems: Concurrent Learning and Optimization

In this section, we formally present
Relearn
LQR, a concurrent learning and optimization algorithm developed to solve the stability-certified on-policy LQR setup described in Section

Learning process

The adopted learning strategy to compute an estimate of

Specifically, with the measurements (

In particular, the estimate

\displaystyle\theta_{{t+1}}

\displaystyle=\theta_{t}-\gamma\left(\sum_{\tau=0}^{t}\lambda^{t-\tau}\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{x}_{\tau},\mathrm{u}_{\tau})\right)^{\dagger}\left(\sum_{\tau=0}^{t}\lambda^{t-\tau}\left(\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{x}_{\tau},\mathrm{u}_{\tau})\theta_{t}-\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{x}_{\tau},\mathrm{u}_{\tau},\mathrm{y}_{\tau})\right)\right),

Remark 3.4

We point out that Theorem

represents a local result.
In fact, as for the the initial pair
(A_{0},B_{0})\in\Theta_{b}
, it must belong to the largest ball centered at
({A}_{\star},{B}_{\star})
of controllable matrices
(A,B)
.
Also, an initial controller

that stabilizes both
(A_{0},B_{0})
and

is required.
The proof of Theorem

then exploits system theory tools based on averaging theory for two-time-scale systems (cf. Theorem

in Section

) and, therefore, introduces an auxiliary system called the

.
Such auxiliary system involves modified averaged dynamics of

and

(see Section

) and is shown to have an exponentially stable equilibrium in its origin. Moreover,
we are also able to show that

remains a stabilizing gain for

at all

(see Appendix

Such stabilizing property combined with closeness between trajectories of the averaged and original systems, imposed through
\gamma

[
45

), allows us to conclude that

, generated by (

({A}_{\star},{B}_{\star})
for all

\blacksquare

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we addressed infinite-horizon LQR problems with unknown state-input matrices.
Specifically, we propose a procedure mixing the identification phase of the unknown matrices with the optimization of the feedback policy.
We design an iterative algorithm combining a Recursive Least Squares (RLS) scheme (elaborating samples from the closed-loop system persistently excited by a dithering signal) with the gradient method.
We proved exponential convergence of the overall procedure to the optimal steady-state associated to the optimal gain and the exact matrices by using tools from Lyapunov-based analysis tools in combination with averaging theory for nonlinear systems.

Although our strategy is analyzed with a time-invariant plant without disturbances, the procedure we developed is inherently applicable to more challenging scenarios where, for instance, the system and cost matrices vary over time and the plant is affected by disturbances.
Thus, our work lays the foundation for obtaining stability certificates also in such complex settings.

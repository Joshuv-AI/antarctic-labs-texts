Abstract
. We give necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of a BMO solution to the quasilinear equation − Δ p ​ u = μ -\Delta_{p}u=\mu in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , u ≥ 0 u\geq 0 , where μ \mu is a locally finite Radon measure, and Δ p u = div ( | ∇ u | p − 2 ∇ u ) \Delta_{p}u=\text{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u) is the p p -Laplacian ( p > 1 p>1 ). We also characterize BMO solutions to equations − Δ p ​ u = σ ​ u q + μ -\Delta_{p}u=\sigma u^{q}+\mu in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , u ≥ 0 u\geq 0 , with q > 0 q>0 , where both μ \mu and σ \sigma are locally finite Radon measures. Our main results hold for a class of more general quasilinear operators div ( 𝒜 ( x , ∇ ⋅ ) ) {\rm div}(\mathcal{A}(x,\nabla\cdot)) in place of Δ p \Delta_{p} .

1.
Introduction

Let
M^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the class of all (locally finite) positive Radon measures in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. Let
\mu\in M^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In this paper, the following quasilinear equation with measure data is considered :

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta_{p}u=\mu,\quad u\geq 0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\displaystyle{\liminf_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}}\,u=0.\end{array}\right.

(1)

Here
\Delta_{p}u
is the
p
-Laplacian of
u
defined by
\Delta_{p}u:={\rm div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u
). All solutions to (
1
) are understood to be
p
-superharmonic solutions, or equivalently local renormalized solutions (see
[
9
]
,
[
11
]
). Since nontrivial
p
-superharmonic
functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
do not exist for
p\geq n
, it will be our standing assumption
that solutions to (
1
) are considered for
1<p<n
.

It is known that a necessary and sufficient condition for (
1
) to
admit a solution is the finiteness condition (see, e.g.,
[
24
]
)

\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\mu(B(0,\rho))}{\rho^{n-p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{d\rho}{\rho}<+\infty.

(2)

This is equivalent to the condition
{\bf W}_{p}\mu(x)<+\infty
for some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
(or equivalently
quasi-everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to the
p
-capacity),
where

{\bf W}_{p}\mu(x):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\mu(B(x,\rho))}{\rho^{n-p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{d\rho}{\rho}

is the Havin–Maz’ya–Wolff potential of
\mu
, often called the Wolff potential
(see
[
8
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
17
]
).

By the important result of Kilpeläinen and Malý
[
13
]
, any solution
u
to
(
1
) satisfies the pointwise estimates

C_{1}{\bf W}_{p}\mu(x)\leq u(x)\leq C_{2}{\bf W}_{p}\mu(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(3)

where
C_{1},C_{2}
are positive constants that depend only on
p
and
n
.

The
p
-capacity
{\rm cap}_{p}(\cdot)
is a natural capacity associated with the
p
-Laplace operator defined for each compact set
K
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

{\rm cap}_{p}(K)=\inf\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla h|^{p}dx:\,\,h\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,\,h\geq 1\text{ on }K\right\}.

In
[
28
, Lemma 3.1]
, it is shown that, for
1<p<n
, if (
1
) has a solution
u\in{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
then (
2
) holds along with the following bound for
\mu
:

\mu(B(x,R))\leq CR^{n-p},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,R>0.

(4)

Here
{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the space of functions
u
of bounded mean oscillation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e.,
u\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|u-\bar{u}_{B}|dx\leq C,

for all balls
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\bar{u}_{B}=\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}u\,dx
.

It is also known that, conversely, if
\mu
satisfies (
2
) and (
4
), then (
1
) has a solution
u\in{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
provided
2-\frac{1}{n}<p<n
(see
[
28
]
). A local version of this result was established in
[
18
]
for
p>2
. The linear case
p=2
is due to
D. Adams
[
1
]
.

One of the main goals of this paper is to extend this existence criterion to the full range

1<p<n
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mu\in M^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
1<p<n
. Then equation (
1
) has a solution
u\in{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

if and only if
\mu
satisfies conditions (
2
) and (
4
).

Moreover, any solution
u
to (
1
) lies in
{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

if and only if
\mu
satisfies (
4
).

Remark 1.2
.

If

\mu
satisfies (
4
), then actually any solution

u
to (
6
) satisfies the Morrey condition

\int_{B(x,R)}|\nabla u|^{s}dy\leq C\,R^{n-s},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,\,R>0,

provided
0<s<p
, which yields
u\in{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
s\geq 1

by Poincaré’s inequality.

A sharper local estimate for the end-point weak
L^{p}
norm
||\nabla u||_{L^{p,\infty}(B(x,R))}
in place of
Morrey’s norm is obtained in Theorem
2.8
below.

We observe that Theorem
1.1
can be regarded as the end-point case
\alpha=0
of the corresponding criterion for
u\in C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(
0\leq\alpha<1
), where

C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the Campanato space
of functions
u\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|u-\bar{u}_{B}|dx\leq C\,|B|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}},

for all balls
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then
u\in C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
u\in{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
\alpha=0
, and
u
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous when
\alpha\in(0,1]
(see
[
6
, Sec. 2.3]
).

A local version of the following result was obtained in
[
13
, Theorem 4.18]
and

[
14
, Theorem 1.14]
:

Let
0<\alpha<1
. A solution
u
to (
1
) is in
C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

if and only if
\mu
satisfies the condition

\mu(B(x,R))\leq CR^{n-p+\alpha(p-1)},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,R>0.

(5)

Notice that condition (
5
) combined with (
2
) is necessary and sufficient for the existence of a solution
u\in C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

to (
1
). The proof is similar to the
proof of Theorem
1.1
given below for
\alpha=0
.

Using Theorem
1.1
, we obtain criteria for the existence of BMO solutions to the equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta_{p}u=\sigma u^{q}+\mu,\quad u\geq 0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\displaystyle{\liminf_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}}\,u=0,\end{array}\right.

(6)

where
q>0
and
\mu,\sigma\in M^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(
\sigma\not=0
). Here we assume
without loss of generality that
\sigma\not=0
, since the case
\sigma=0
is covered
by Theorem
1.1
.

The corresponding results
are more complicated due to
the possible interaction between the datum
\mu
and
the source term
\sigma u^{q}
on the right-hand side, as well as competition with

-\Delta_{p}u
on the left-hand side.

We first consider the super-natural growth case
q>p-1
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mu,\sigma\in M^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(
\sigma\not=0
).
Let
q>p-1

and
1<p<n
. Then equation (
6
) has a solution
u\in{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

if
\mu
satisfies (
4
), and

\displaystyle(a)\quad{\bf W}_{p}[({\bf W}_{p}\mu)^{q}d\sigma](x)\leq c\,{\bf W}_{p}\mu(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

\displaystyle(b)\quad\sigma(B(x,R))\left[\int_{R}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{r^{n-p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{dr}{r}\right]^{q}\leq C\,R^{n-p},\,\,\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,R>0,

where
c
,
C
are positive constants, and
c=c(p,q,n)
is sufficiently small.

Conversely, if there exists a solution
u\in{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to (
6
),
then (
4
), and conditions (a), (b)
hold for some positive constants
c
,
C
.

In the sub-natural growth case
0<q<p-1
,
we denote by
\kappa
the least constant in the weighted norm inequality for Wolff potentials (see
[
5
]
),

||{\bf W}_{p}\nu||_{L^{q}(d\sigma)}\leq\kappa\,||\nu||^{\frac{1}{p-1}},\quad\forall\nu\in M^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(7)

where
||\nu||=\nu(\mathbb{R}^{n})
stands for
the total variation of
\nu
.

Using (
3
), it is easy to see that
\kappa
is equivalent to the least constant

\varkappa
in the inequality

||\phi||_{L^{q}(d\sigma)}\leq\varkappa\,||\Delta_{p}\phi||^{\frac{1}{p-1}},

(8)

for all positive test functions
\phi
that are
p
-superharmonic in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

such that
\displaystyle{\liminf_{|x|\to\infty}}\,\phi(x)=0
.

For
\sigma\in M^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and a ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
\sigma_{B}=\sigma|_{B}

be the restriction of
\sigma
to
B
. We denote by
\kappa(B)
the least constant in the
localized
version of (
7
), namely,

||{\bf W}_{p}\nu||_{L^{q}(d\sigma_{B})}\leq{\bf{\bf\kappa}}(B)\,||\nu||^{\frac{1}{p-1}},\quad\forall\nu\in M_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(9)

As mentioned above, equivalent constants
\varkappa({B})
, associated with
\sigma_{B}
in place of
\sigma
in (
8
),
can be used in place of
\kappa({B})
. Various lower and upper estimates of
\kappa({B})
can be found in
[
5
]
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

As mentioned above, the “only if”
part of Theorem
1.1
is proved in
[
28
, Lemma 3.1]
.

To prove the “if” part, we construct a solution
u\in{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to (
1
) under conditions (
2
) and (
4
) on
\mu
. Our construction below is based on an a priori estimate of
[
4
]
. Alternatively, it is possible to use the gradient estimates of
[
21
]
,
[
20
]
for the construction, but that will not be implemented in this paper.

We first observe that under (
4
),

\left\|{\bf I}_{1}\mu\right\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-1},\infty}(B(x,R))}\leq CMR^{\frac{(n-p)(p-1)}{p}},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,R>0,

(11)

where
C
depends only on
p
and
n
, and

M=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},R>0}\frac{\mu(B(x,R))}{R^{n-p}}.

A proof of (
11
) can be found in
[
1
]
. Here
{\bf I}_{\alpha}
,

\alpha\in(0,n)
, is the Riesz potential of order
\alpha
, defined for a measure
\mu\in M^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

{\bf I}_{\alpha}\mu(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}d\mu(y),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(12)

Also, the space
L^{q,\infty}(B(x,R))
,
q>0
, is the weak
L^{q}
space over the ball
B(x,R)
with

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{q,\infty}(B(x,R))}:=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda|\{y\in B(x,R):|f(y)|>\lambda\}|^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Let
\mu_{k}
(
k=1,2,\dots
) be a standard regularization of
\mu
by the convolution

\mu_{k}(x)=\rho_{k}*\mu(x),\quad\rho_{k}(\cdot)=k^{n}\rho(k\cdot),

where
0\leq\rho\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B(0,1))
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho dx=1
, and
\rho
is radial. Then it follows from Fubini’s Theorem and (
4
) that

\mu_{k}(B(x,R))\leq CR^{n-p},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,R>0,

where
C
is independent of
k
. Thus, we also have

\left\|{\bf I}_{1}\mu_{k}\right\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-1},\infty}(B(x,R))}\leq CR^{\frac{(n-p)(p-1)}{p}},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,R>0,

(13)

for a constant
C
independent of
k
.

Next, for each positive integer
N
we let
\mu_{B(0,N)}
be the restriction of the measure
\mu
to the open ball
B(0,N)
.
Consider now the unique
p
-superharmonic solution
u_{N,k}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(B(0,N))\subset W^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to the equation

-\Delta_{p}u_{N,k}=\rho_{k}*\mu_{B(0,N)}\quad\text{in }B(0,N).

(14)

Note that
\rho_{k}*\mu_{B(0,N)}\leq\mu_{k}
and we can write
\rho_{k}*\mu_{B(0,N)}=-{\rm div}\,\nabla v_{N,k}
in the sense of distributions in
B(0,N)
, where

v_{N,k}(x)=\int_{B(0,N)}G_{N}(x,y)[\rho_{k}*\mu_{B(0,N)}(y)]dy,\qquad x\in B(0,N),

with
G_{N}(x,y)
being the Green function associated with
-\Delta
in
B(0,N)
. Moreover, we have

|\nabla v_{N,k}|\leq C\,{\bf I}_{1}(\rho_{k}*\mu_{B(0,N)})\leq C\,{\bf I}_{1}\mu_{k}.

(15)

Now using (
13
), (
15
) and applying
[
4
, Theorem 1.2]
we find that

\left\|\nabla u_{N,k}\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B(x,R))}\leq CR^{\frac{n-p}{p}}

(16)

for all
x\in B(0,N)
and
0<R\leq 2N
. The constant
C
is independent of
x,R,N
, and
k
. Since
|\nabla u_{N,k}|=0
outside
B(0,N)
, it is obvious that (
16
)
also holds for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
R>0
.

When restricted to the ball
B(0,N+1)
,
\mu_{B(0,N)}
is a nonnegative finite measure and thus we can write

\mu_{B(0,N)}=f-{\rm div}F+\mu_{s}

as distributions in
B(0,N+1)
(see, e.g.,
[
16
]
). Here
f\in L^{1}(B(0,N+1))
,
F\in L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(B(0,N+1),\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
\mu_{s}
is a nonnegative measure
concentrated on a set of zero
p
-capacity in
B(0,N)
.

For any
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B(0,N))
and
k\geq 1
we have
\rho_{k}*\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B(0,N+1))
. Thus it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,N)}\rho_{k}*\mu_{B(0,N)}\varphi dx=\int_{B(0,N+1)}\rho_{k}*\varphi d\mu_{B(0,N)}

\displaystyle=\int_{B(0,N+1)}f\rho_{k}*\varphi dx+\int_{B(0,N+1)}F\cdot\nabla(\rho_{k}*\varphi)dx+\int_{B(0,N+1)}\rho_{k}*\varphi d\mu_{s}

\displaystyle=\int_{B(0,N)}\rho_{k}*f\varphi dx+\int_{B(0,N)}\rho_{k}*F\cdot\nabla\varphi dx+\int_{B(0,N)}\rho_{k}*\mu_{s}\varphi dx.

That is,

\rho_{k}*\mu_{B(0,N)}=\rho_{k}*f-{\rm div}(\rho_{k}*F)+\rho_{k}*\mu_{s}

pointwise everywhere and as distributions in
B(0,N)
.

As
\mu_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(0,N))=0
, by the Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem,
we see that
\rho_{k}*\mu_{s}\rightarrow\mu_{s}
in the narrow topology of measures in
B(0,N)
(see
[
16
, Definition 2.2]
). Moreover, this and the above equality yield

\int_{B(0,N)}|{\rm div}(\rho_{k}*F)|dx\leq M,

where
M
is independent of
k
.

At this point, in view of (
14
), we apply
[
16
, Theorem 3.2]
(see also
[
23
, Remark 6.6]
) to find a subsequence
\{u_{N,k_{j}}\}
of
\{u_{N,k}\}
and a
p
-superharmonic function
u_{N}
in
B(0,N)
such that

u_{N,k_{j}}\rightarrow u_{N}
a.e.,
\nabla u_{N,k_{j}}\rightarrow\nabla u_{N}
a.e. as
j\rightarrow\infty
, and
u_{N}
solves
the equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta_{p}u_{N}&=\mu_{B(0,N)}\quad\text{in }B(0,N),\\
\quad\quad u_{N}&=0\quad\text{on }\partial B(0,N),\end{array}\right.

in the renormalized sense (see
[
16
]
for the notion of renormalized solutions).

Moreover, by (
16
) we have
u_{N}\in W^{1,s}_{0}(B(0,N))\subset W^{1,s}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
1\leq s<p
and
Fatou’s Lemma yields that

\left\|\nabla u_{N}\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B(x,R))}\leq CR^{\frac{n-p}{p}},\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,R>0.

(17)

By
[
23
, Theorem 2.1]
we have

u_{N}(x)\leq C\,{\bf W}_{p}\mu(x),\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(18)

By
[
12
, Theorem 1.17]
we can find a subsequence
\{u_{N_{j}}\}
of
\{u_{N}\}
and
a
p
-superharmonic function
u
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
u_{N_{j}}\rightarrow u
a.e. and
\nabla u_{N_{j}}\rightarrow\nabla u
a.e. as
j\rightarrow\infty
.
Note that by condition (
2
) and (
18
),
u
must be finite a.e. (or q.e.) and

u(x)\leq C\,{\bf W}_{p}\mu(x),\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The weak continuity result of
[
26
]
yields that
u
is a
p
-superharmonic solution of (
1
).
That
\liminf_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}u=0
follows from the fact that
\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u=0
and the latter is a direct consequence of the pointwise bound (see
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
) :

u(x)\leq C\,{\bf W}_{p}\mu(x)\leq C[u(x)-\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u],\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Applying (
17
) with
\nabla u_{N_{j}}
in place of
\nabla u_{N}
, and
using Fatou’s Lemma as
N_{j}\to\infty
, we obtain

\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B(x,R))}\leq CR^{\frac{n-p}{p}},\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,R>0.

(19)

It is worth mentioning here that for
p\geq 2
estimate (
19
) can also be inferred from the work
[
19
]
.
Thus, for any ball
B=B(x,R)
, by Poincaré’s inequality and Hölder’s inequality we find

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\left|u(x)-\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}u(y)dy\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq CR\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|\nabla u|dx

\displaystyle\leq CR|B|^{-\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B)}

\displaystyle\leq C.

This shows that
u\in{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as desired.

It remains to show that any solution
u
to (
1
) lies in
{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
provided

\mu
satisfies condition (
4
). We will actually prove the
stronger estimate (
19
) (see Remark
1.2
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
u
be a nonnegative
p
-superharmonic solution of
-\Delta_{p}u=\mu
with
\mu
satisfying condition (
4
). Then
|\nabla u|\in L^{q}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
provided
0<q<p
. Moreover,
for any
0<q<p
,
0<\epsilon<p-1
and any ball
B(x,R)
we have

\Big(R^{q-n}\int_{B(x,R)}|\nabla u|^{q}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\,\left([\inf_{B(x,2R)}u]^{\frac{p-1-\epsilon}{p}}+\inf_{B(x,2R)}u\right),

(20)

where the constant
C
depends on
p,q,\epsilon,n
and the constant in condition (
4
).
In particular,

\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}R^{q-n}\int_{B(0,R)}|\nabla u|^{q}dy=0.

(21)

Proof 2.2
.

Let
u_{k}=\min\{u,k\}
,
k=1,2,\dots
Then
u_{k}\in W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a supersolution in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and hence the weak Harnack
inequality
[
25
]
implies that

\Big(\frac{1}{|B(x,R)|}\int_{B(x,R)}u_{k}^{s}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{s}}\leq C\,\inf_{B(x,R)}u_{k}\leq C\,\inf_{B(x,R)}u

for
0<s<\frac{n(p-1)}{n-p}
. Thus letting
k\rightarrow\infty
we obtain

\Big(\frac{1}{|B(x,R)|}\int_{B(x,R)}u^{s}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{s}}\leq C\,\inf_{B(x,R)}u.

(22)

To continue, we recall the following result for
p
-supersolutions from
[
9
, Lemma 3.57]
:

For any nonnegative
p
-supersolution
v
in an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, any
\epsilon>0
, and any
function
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, it holds that

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p}v^{-1-\epsilon}|\varphi|^{p}dx\leq(p/\epsilon)^{p}\int_{\Omega}v^{p-1-\epsilon}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}dx.

(23)

Now let
0<q<p
and fix an
\epsilon
such that
0<\epsilon<p-1
. Applying Hölder’s inequality, and then using (
23
) with
v=u_{k}
and an appropriate cut-off function
\varphi

supported in
B(x,2R)

such that
\varphi=1
on
B(x,R)
and
|\nabla\varphi|\leq CR^{-1}
,
we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,R)}|\nabla u_{k}|^{q}dy=\int_{B(x,R)}|\nabla u_{k}|^{q}u_{k}^{-(1+\epsilon)q/p}u_{k}^{(1+\epsilon)q/p}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{B(x,R)}|\nabla u_{k}|^{p}u_{k}^{-1-\epsilon}dy\Big)^{q/p}\Big(\int_{B(x,R)}u_{k}^{(1+\epsilon)q/(p-q)}dy\Big)^{(p-q)/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CR^{-q}\Big(\int_{B(x,2R)}u^{p-1-\epsilon}dy\Big)^{q/p}\Big(\int_{B(x,R)}u^{(1+\epsilon)q/(p-q)}dy\Big)^{(p-q)/p}.

Thus it follows from (
22
) that

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,R)}|\nabla u_{k}|^{q}dy

\displaystyle\leq CR^{-q}\Big(R^{n}[\inf_{B(x,2R)}u]^{p-1-\epsilon}\Big)^{q/p}\times

(24)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\Big(\int_{B(x,R)}u^{(1+\epsilon)q/(p-q)}dy\Big)^{(p-q)/p}.

On the other hand, by
[
13
, Theorem 1.6]
we have

\displaystyle u(y)

\displaystyle\leq C\inf_{B(y,3R)}u+C\int_{0}^{6R}\left(\frac{\mu(B(y,t))}{t^{n-p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq C\inf_{B(x,2R)}u+C\int_{0}^{6R}\left(\frac{\mu(B(y,t))}{t^{n-p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{dt}{t},

provided
y\in B(x,R)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,R)}

\displaystyle u^{(1+\epsilon)q/(p-q)}dy\leq CR^{n}[\inf_{B(x,2R)}u]^{(1+\epsilon)q/(p-q)}

(25)

\displaystyle+\,C\int_{B(x,R)}\left[\int_{0}^{6R}\left(\frac{\mu(B(y,t))}{t^{n-p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{dt}{t}\right]^{(1+\epsilon)q/(p-q)}dy.

Note that for
y\in B(x,R)
by a Hedberg type inequality (see
[
2
, Section 3.1]
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{6R}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mu(B(y,t))}{t^{n-p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{dt}{t}=\int_{0}^{6R}\left(\frac{\mu_{B(x,7R)}(B(y,t))}{t^{n-p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq C\mu(B(x,7R))^{\frac{p-\alpha}{(n-\alpha)(p-1)}}\,{\bf M}_{\alpha}(\mu_{B(x,7R)})^{\frac{n-p}{(n-\alpha)(p-1)}}

\displaystyle\leq C\mu(B(x,7R))^{\frac{p-\alpha}{(n-\alpha)(p-1)}}\,{\bf I}_{\alpha}(\mu_{B(x,7R)})^{\frac{n-p}{(n-\alpha)(p-1)}},

provided
0<\alpha<p
. Here
{\bf M}_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(0,n)
, is the fractional maximal function of order
\alpha
defined for a measure

\nu\in M^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

{\bf M}_{\alpha}\nu(x):=\sup_{r>0}\frac{\nu(B(x,r))}{r^{n-\alpha}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We now set
\theta=(1+\epsilon)q/(p-q)
. Then the above bound and (
4
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,R)}

\displaystyle\left[\int_{0}^{6R}\left(\frac{\mu(B(y,t))}{t^{n-p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{dt}{t}\right]^{\theta}dy

\displaystyle\leq CR^{\frac{(n-p)(p-\alpha)\theta}{(n-\alpha)(p-1)}}\int_{B(x,R)}{\bf I}_{\alpha}(\mu)^{\frac{\theta(n-p)}{(n-\alpha)(p-1)}}dy.

We next choose
0<\alpha<p
such that

\frac{p}{p-\alpha}>\frac{\theta(n-p)}{(n-\alpha)(p-1)},

and apply Hölder’s inequality to get

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,R)}

\displaystyle\left[\int_{0}^{6R}\left(\frac{\mu(B(y,t))}{t^{n-p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{dt}{t}\right]^{\theta}dy

(26)

\displaystyle\leq CR^{\frac{(n-p)(p-\alpha)\theta}{(n-\alpha)(p-1)}}R^{n-\frac{n\theta(n-p)(p-\alpha)}{p(n-\alpha)(p-1)}}\left\|{\bf I}_{\alpha}\mu\right\|^{\frac{\theta(n-p)}{(p-1)(n-\alpha)}}_{L^{\frac{p}{p-\alpha},\infty}(B(x,R))}

\displaystyle\leq CR^{n},

where in the last bound we used a result of
[
1
]
:

\left\|{\bf I}_{\alpha}\mu\right\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-\alpha},\infty}(B(x,R))}\leq CR^{(n-p)\frac{p-\alpha}{p}}.

At this point, we plug estimate (
26
) into (
25
) to obtain

\int_{B(x,R)}u^{(1+\epsilon)q/(p-q)}dy\leq CR^{n}(1+[\inf_{B(x,2R)}u]^{(1+\epsilon)q/(p-q)}).

In view of (
24
), this yields

R^{q-n}\int_{B(x,R)}|\nabla u_{k}|^{q}dy\leq C[\inf_{B(x,2R)}u]^{(p-1-\epsilon)q/p}\left(1+[\inf_{B(x,2R)}u]^{(1+\epsilon)q/p}\right).

Now letting
k\rightarrow\infty
we obtain estimate (
20
).

Finally, to obtain the decay (
21
), we observe that estimate (
20
) also holds if
u
is replaced by
\tilde{u}:=u-\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u
and that

\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tilde{u}=0
.

Remark 2.3
.

For
0<q<\frac{n(p-1)}{n-1}
, Lemma
2.1
holds without assuming condition (
4
) on
\mu
(see
[
9
, Theorem 7.46]
). Moreover,
the first term on the right-hand side of (

) can be dropped in this case.

Lemma 2.4
.

There exist constants
A=A(n,p)>1
sufficiently large and
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(n,p)\in(0,p-1)
sufficiently small such that the following
holds for any
T>1
,
\lambda>0
, and
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
. Fix a ball
B_{0}=B(z_{0},R_{0})
and let
u
be a solution of
-\Delta_{p}u={\rm div}\,(|{\bf f}|^{p-2}{\bf f})
in
2B_{0}
. Assume that for some
ball

with
\rho\leq R_{0}/8

\displaystyle B(y,\rho)\cap B_{0}\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:{\bf M}(\chi_{2B_{0}}

\displaystyle|\nabla u|^{p-\delta})^{\frac{1}{p-\delta}}(x)\leq\lambda\}\cap

\displaystyle\{{\bf M}(\chi_{2B_{0}}|{\bf f}|^{p-\delta})^{\frac{1}{p-\delta}}\leq\epsilon(T)\lambda\}\neq\emptyset,

where

\displaystyle H=H(T,\delta)=T^{-(p+\delta_{0})}+\delta^{(p-\delta)\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{p-1}\right\}}.

With this, we can now apply
[
4
, Lemma 4.1]
and Lemma
2.4
above to get the following result. Its proof is similar to that
of
[
4
, Lemma 4.6]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
A=A(n,p)
and
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(n,p)
be as in Lemma
2.4
.
The following holds for any
T>2
and
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
.
Fix a ball
B_{0}=B(z_{0},R_{0})
and let
u
be a solution of
-\Delta_{p}u={\rm div}\,(|{\bf f}|^{p-2}{\bf f})
in
2B_{0}
. Suppose that there
exists
N>0
such that

|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:{\bf M}(\chi_{2B_{0}}|\nabla u|^{p-\delta})^{\frac{1}{p-\delta}}(x)>N\}|<H|\tfrac{1}{8}B_{0}|.

Then for any integer
k\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle|\{x\in B_{0}:{\bf M}(\chi_{2B_{0}}|\nabla u|^{p-\delta})^{\frac{1}{p-\delta}}(x)>N(AT)^{k+1}\}|

\displaystyle\leq c(n)\,H\,|\{x\in B_{0}:{\bf M}(\chi_{2B_{0}}|\nabla u|^{p-\delta})^{\frac{1}{p-\delta}}(x)>N(AT)^{k}\}|

\displaystyle+\,c(n)\,|\{x\in B_{0}:{\bf M}(\chi_{2B_{0}}|{\bf f}|^{p-\delta})^{\frac{1}{p-\delta}}(x)>\epsilon(T)N(AT)^{k}\}|.

Here
\epsilon(T)
and
H=H(T,\delta)
are as defined in Lemma
2.4
.

We are now in a position to obtain a local
L^{p,\infty}
estimate for the gradient.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\mu\in M^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and let
u
be a solution of
-\Delta_{p}u=\mu
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then for any ball
B_{0}=B(z_{0},R_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,|B_{0}|^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{1}{|2B_{0}|}\int_{2B_{0}}|\nabla u|^{p-\delta}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p-\delta}}

(27)

\displaystyle+\,C\,\left\|[{\rm\bf I}_{1}(\chi_{2B_{0}}\mu)]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{0})},

with a constant
C=C(n,p)>0
and a constant
\delta=\delta(n,p)\in(0,p-1)
.

Proof 2.7
.

Let
B_{0}=B(z_{0},R_{0})
and
\delta_{0}
be as in Lemma
2.4
.
For
T>2
and
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
to be determined, we claim that there exists
N>0
such that

|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:{\rm\bf M}(\chi_{2B_{0}}|\nabla u|^{p-\delta})^{\frac{1}{2-\delta}}(x)>N\}|<H\,|\tfrac{1}{8}B_{0}|,

where
H=H(T,\delta)=T^{-(p-\delta_{0})}+\delta^{(p-\delta)\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{p-1}\right\}}
(as in Lemma
2.4
).

This can be done by using the weak type
(1,1)
estimate for the maximal function and choosing
N>0
such that

\frac{C(n)}{N^{p-\delta}}\int_{2B_{0}}|\nabla u|^{p-\delta}dx=H|\tfrac{1}{8}B_{0}|

(28)

provided the integral above is non-zero, which we may assume.

Let
A>1
and
\epsilon(T)>0
be as in Lemma
2.4
. Set

L=\sup_{k\geq 1}(AT)^{k}|\{x\in B_{0}:{\rm\bf M}(\chi_{2B_{0}}|\nabla u|^{p-\delta})^{\frac{1}{p-\delta}}(x)>N(AT)^{k}\}|^{\frac{1}{p}}.

We have

\left\|{\rm\bf M}(\chi_{2B_{0}}|\nabla u/N|^{p-\delta})^{\frac{1}{p-\delta}}\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{0})}\leq AT\,(|B_{0}|^{\frac{1}{p}}+L).

(29)

We now set, for
m=1,2,\dots
,

L_{m}=\sup_{1\leq k\leq m}(AT)^{k}|\{x\in B_{0}:{\rm\bf M}(\chi_{2B_{0}}|\nabla u|^{p-\delta})^{\frac{1}{p-\delta}}(x)>N(AT)^{k}\}|^{\frac{1}{p}},

and note that

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}L_{m}=L.

(30)

For any vector field
{\bf f}
such that
{\rm div}\,(|{\bf f}|^{p-2}{\bf f})=\mu
in
2B_{0}
, by Lemma
2.5
we find

\displaystyle L_{m}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sup_{1\leq k\leq m}\,(AT)^{k}H(T,\delta)^{\frac{1}{p}}\times

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times|\{x\in B_{0}:{\rm\bf M}(\chi_{2B_{0}}|\nabla u|^{p-\delta})^{\frac{1}{p-\delta}}(x)>N(AT)^{k-1}\}|^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle+\,C\sup_{1\leq k\leq m}\,(AT)^{k}\times

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times|\{x\in B_{0}:[{\rm\bf M}(\chi_{2B_{0}}|{\bf f}|^{p-\delta})]^{\frac{1}{p-\delta}}>\epsilon(T)N(AT)^{k-1}\}|^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,(AT)H(T,\delta)^{\frac{1}{p}}(L_{m}+|B_{0}|^{\frac{1}{p}})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,C_{1}(T,\delta)\,\left\|[{\rm\bf M}(\chi_{2B_{0}}(|{\bf f}|/N)^{p-\delta})]^{\frac{1}{p-\delta}}\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{0})}.

By the boundedness property of
{\bf M}
, this yields

L_{m}\leq C\,(AT)H(T,\delta)^{\frac{1}{p}}(L_{m}+|B_{0}|^{\frac{1}{p}})+CC_{1}(T,\delta)\left\|{\bf f}/N\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}(2B_{0})}.

We next choose
T
sufficiently large and
\delta
sufficiently small so that

C\,(AT)H(T,\delta)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq 1/2

and thus deduce from the above bound and (
30
) that

\tfrac{1}{2}L\leq\tfrac{1}{2}|B_{0}|^{\frac{1}{p}}+C\left\|{\bf f}/N\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}(2B_{0})}.

In view of (
29
) and (
28
) this gives

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{0})}\leq C|B_{0}|^{\frac{1}{p}}N+C\left\|{\bf f}\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}(2B_{0})}

(31)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|B_{0}|^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{1}{|2B_{0}|}\int_{2B_{0}}|\nabla u|^{p-\delta}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p-\delta}}+C\left\|{\bf f}\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}(2B_{0})}.

Finally, we write
\mu={\rm div}\,{\bf g}
in
2B_{0}
, where

{\bf g}=-\nabla\int_{2B_{0}}G(x,y)d\mu(y)

and
G(x,y)
is the Green function associated with
-\Delta
in
2B_{0}
. Note then that

|{\bf g}|\leq C\,{\bf I}_{1}(\chi_{2B_{0}}\mu)

and with
{\bf f}={\bf g}|{\bf g}|^{\frac{2-p}{p-1}}
we have
|{\bf f}|^{p-2}{\bf f}={\bf g}
. Thus
{\rm div}\,(|{\bf f}|^{p-2}{\bf f})=\mu

in
2B_{0}
and

|{\bf f}|\leq C\,[{\bf I}_{1}(\chi_{2B_{0}}\mu)]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

(32)

By (
31
), this completes the proof of the theorem.

We next prove a gradient estimate for solutions of (
1
) under condition (

).

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
1<p<n
, and let
u
be a nonnegative
p
-superharmonic solution of
-\Delta_{p}u=\mu
, where
\mu
satisfies condition (
4
). Then we have

\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B(x,R))}\leq CM^{\frac{1}{p-1}}R^{\frac{n-p}{p}},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,R>0,

where

M=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,R>0}\frac{\mu(B(x,R))}{R^{n-p}}.

(33)

Proof 2.9
.

Let
B_{0}=B(z_{0},R_{0})
be any fixed ball. By Theorem
2.6
we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,|B_{0}|^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{1}{|2B_{0}|}\int_{2B_{0}}|\nabla u|^{p-\delta}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p-\delta}}

(34)

\displaystyle+\,C\,\left\|[{\rm\bf I}_{1}(\chi_{2B_{0}}\mu)]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{0})}

for a constant
\delta=\delta(n,p)\in(0,p-1)
and we may assume that
\delta
is sufficiently small.
For any
r_{0}>4R_{0}+|z_{0}|
and any
r\in(0,r_{0}]
, let
w\in u+W^{1,\,p-\delta}_{0}(B(z_{0},r))
solve

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Delta_{p}w&=&0\quad\text{in}\penalty\ B(z_{0},r),\\
w&=&u\quad\text{on}\penalty\ \partial B(z_{0},r).\end{array}\right.

By
[
4
, Lemma 2.7]
for any
0<\rho\leq r
we have

\displaystyle\int_{B(z_{0},\rho)}|\nabla w|^{p-\delta}\,dy\leq C(\rho/r)^{n+(p-\delta)(\beta_{0}-1)}\int_{B(z_{0},r)}|\nabla w|^{p-\delta}\,dy,

for some
\beta_{0}=\beta_{0}(n,p)\in(0,1/2]
.
Then by using (
32
) and arguing as in the proof of

[
4
, Equation (5.4)]
we have

\displaystyle\phi(\rho)\leq C

\displaystyle\bigg[\left(\frac{\rho}{r}\right)^{n+(p-\delta)(\beta_{0}-1)}+\delta^{(p-\delta)\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{p-1}\right\}}+\epsilon\bigg]\phi(r)

(35)

\displaystyle+\,C(\epsilon)\,\int_{B(z_{0},r)}{\bf I}_{1}(\chi_{B(z_{0},r_{0})}\mu)^{\frac{p-\delta}{p-1}}\,dx,

which holds for all
\epsilon>0
and
\rho\in(0,r]
. In (
35
), we set

\phi(\rho)=\int_{B(z_{0},\rho)}|\nabla u|^{p-\delta}\,dx.

Now by Hölder’s inequality and (
11
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{B(z_{0},r)}{\bf I}_{1}(\chi_{B(z_{0},r_{0})}\mu)^{\frac{p-\delta}{p-1}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\bf I}_{1}\mu\right\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-1},\infty}(B(z_{0},r))}^{\frac{p-\delta}{p-1}}r^{\frac{n\delta}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq CM^{\frac{p-\delta}{p-1}}r^{\frac{(n-p)(p-\delta)+n\delta}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq CM^{\frac{p-\delta}{p-1}}r^{n-p+\delta},

where
M
is defined in (
33
). Thus it follows from (
35
) that

\displaystyle\phi(\rho)\leq C

\displaystyle\bigg[\left(\frac{\rho}{r}\right)^{n+(p-\delta)(\beta_{0}-1)}+\delta^{(p-\delta)\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{p-1}\right\}}+\epsilon\bigg]\phi(r)

\displaystyle+\,C(\epsilon)\,M^{\frac{p-\delta}{p-1}}r^{n-p+\delta},

which holds for all
\epsilon>0
and
\rho\in(0,r]
. As
n-p+\delta<n+(p-\delta)(\beta_{0}-1)
,
we can apply
[
7
, Lemma 3.4]
to obtain

\displaystyle\phi(\rho)\leq C\left(\frac{\rho}{r}\right)^{n-p+\delta}\phi(r)+CM^{\frac{p-\delta}{p-1}}\rho^{n-p+\delta}

provided
\delta
is sufficiently small. Since this estimate holds for all
0<\rho\leq r\leq r_{0}
, we may choose

\rho=2R_{0}
and
r=r_{0}
to deduce

\displaystyle\int_{B(z_{0},2R_{0})}|\nabla u|^{p-\delta}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{R_{0}}{r_{0}}\right)^{n-p+\delta}\int_{B(z_{0},r_{0})}|\nabla u|^{p-\delta}\,dx

(36)

\displaystyle\qquad+\,CM^{\frac{p-\delta}{p-1}}R_{0}^{n-p+\delta}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{R_{0}}{r_{0}}\right)^{n-p+\delta}\int_{B(0,2r_{0})}|\nabla u|^{p-\delta}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad+\,CM^{\frac{p-\delta}{p-1}}R_{0}^{n-p+\delta},

where we used that
B(z_{0},r_{0})\subset B(0,2r_{0})
.

At this point we combine (
34
), (

{\bf W}_{p}\mu
,

{\bf W}^{d}_{p}\mu(x)=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}}\left(\frac{\mu(Q)}{\ell(Q)^{n-p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\chi_{Q}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\mathcal{D}=\{Q\}
is the family of all dyadic cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and

\ell(Q)
stands for the side length of
Q
.

For
Q\in\mathcal{D}
, we denote by

Q^{*}
the concentric cube with side length
\ell(Q^{*})=3\,\ell(Q)
.
Clearly, the family of cubes
\{Q^{*}\}_{Q\in\mathcal{D}}
has the finite intersection property

\sum_{\ell(Q)=2^{k}}\chi_{Q^{*}}(x)\leq\beta(n),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,\,k\in\mathbb{Z},

(61)

where
\beta(n)
is a constant which depends only on
n
. We will actually need
a modified version of
{\bf W}^{d}_{p}
defined by

\widetilde{{\bf W}}^{d}_{p}\mu=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}}\left(\frac{\mu(Q^{*})}{\ell(Q)^{n-p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\chi_{Q}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

It is easy to verify (see
[
8
, p. 170]
) that

a\,{\bf W}^{d}_{p}\mu\leq{\bf W}_{p}\mu\leq A\,\widetilde{{\bf W}}^{d}_{p}\mu,

(62)

where the constants
a,A
depend only on
p
and
n
.

In view of (
62
),
it is enough to prove the following version of (
60
),

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\widetilde{{\bf W}}^{d}_{p}\mu)^{p-1}d\sigma\leq C\,c^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}({\bf W}_{p}\sigma)\,d\mu.

(63)

Since
p>2
, we can use duality to rewrite (
63
) in the equivalent form

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\widetilde{{\bf W}}^{d}_{p}\mu)\,g\,d\sigma\leq C\,c^{\frac{p-2}{(p-1)^{2}}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}({\bf W}_{p}\sigma)\,d\mu\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}},

(64)

for all
g\in L^{\frac{p-1}{p-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\sigma)
such that
||g||_{L^{\frac{p-1}{p-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\sigma)}\leq 1
. Interchanging the order of integration and summation
on the left-hand side of (
64
), we see that
(
63
) is equivalent to

I=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}}\left(\frac{\mu(Q^{*})}{\ell(Q)^{n-p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\int_{Q}g\,d\sigma\leq C\,c^{\frac{p-2}{(p-1)^{2}}}\,\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}({\bf W}_{p}\sigma)\,d\mu\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Using Hölder’s inequality with exponents
p-1
and
\frac{p-1}{p-2}
, we estimate

\displaystyle I\leq

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}}\left(\frac{\sigma(Q)}{\ell(Q)^{n-p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\mu(Q^{*})\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

(65)

\displaystyle\times\left[\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}}\frac{\sigma(Q)^{p^{\prime}}}{\ell(Q)^{\frac{n-p}{p-1}}}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma(Q)}\int_{Q}g\,d\sigma\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p-2}}\right]^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}},

where
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
. Notice that

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}}\left(\frac{\sigma(Q)}{\ell(Q)^{n-p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\mu(Q^{*})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}}\left(\frac{\sigma(Q)}{\ell(Q)^{n-p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\chi_{Q^{*}}\,d\mu.

If
x\in Q^{*}
, then obviously
Q\subset B(x,\alpha(n)\,\ell(Q))
, where
\alpha(n)
is a constant which depends only on
n
. We estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}}\left(\frac{\sigma(Q)}{\ell(Q)^{n-p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\chi_{Q^{*}}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\ell(Q)=2^{k}}\left(\frac{\sigma(Q)}{\ell(Q)^{n-p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\chi_{Q^{*}}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\,\left(\frac{\sigma(B(x,\alpha(n)2^{k}))}{2^{k(n-p)}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\sum_{\ell(Q)=2^{k}}\chi_{Q}^{*}(x).

Clearly,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\,\left(\frac{\sigma(B(x,\alpha(n)2^{k}))}{2^{k(n-p)}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\sigma(B(x,\alpha(n)r)}{r^{n-p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{dr}{r}

\displaystyle=C\,\alpha(n)^{\frac{n-p}{p-1}}{\bf W}_{p}\sigma(x),

where
C
depends only on
p
and
n
. Hence, by the finite intersection property
(
61
),

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}}\left(\frac{\sigma(Q)}{\ell(Q)^{n-p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\chi_{Q^{*}}(x)\leq C\,\alpha(n)^{\frac{n-p}{p-1}}\,\beta(n){\bf W}_{p}\sigma(x).

Integration both sides of the preceding inequality with respect to
d\mu
gives

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}}\left(\frac{\sigma(Q)}{\ell(Q)^{n-p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\mu(Q^{*})\leq C\,\alpha(n)^{\frac{n-p}{p-1}}\,\beta(n)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}({\bf W}_{p}\sigma)\,d\mu.

We estimate the second factor in (
65
) using the dyadic Carleson measure theorem. We observe that assumption (a) yields
the capacity condition
(
56
). It
is known (see
[
10
, Theorem 3.9]
) that (
56
) is equivalent to the dyadic Carleson measure
condition

\sum_{Q\subseteq P}\frac{\sigma(Q)^{p^{\prime}}}{\ell(Q)^{\frac{n-p}{p-1}}}\leq C\,c^{p^{\prime}-1}\sigma(P),

for all dyadic cubes
P
, where
c
is the constant
in (
56
), and

C
depends only on
p,n
. Hence by the dyadic Carleson measure theorem,

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}}\frac{\sigma(Q)^{p^{\prime}}}{\ell(Q)^{\frac{n-p}{p-1}}}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma(Q)}\int_{Q}g\,d\sigma\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p-2}}\leq C\,c^{p^{\prime}-1}\,||g||^{\frac{p-1}{p-2}}_{L^{\frac{p-1}{p-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\sigma)}\leq C\,c^{p^{\prime}-1},

since
||g||_{L^{\frac{p-1}{p-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\sigma)}\leq 1
. Combining the preceding estimates proves (
60
).

Applying Lemma
5.4
with
\mu_{2B}
and
\sigma_{B}
in place of

\mu
and
\sigma
, respectively, we obtain

\int_{B}({\bf W}_{p}\mu_{2B})^{p-1}d\sigma\leq C\,c^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\int_{2B}({\bf W}_{p}\sigma_{B})d\mu.

Invoking assumption (a) and condition (
4
) yields

\int_{2B}({\bf W}_{p}\sigma_{B})d\mu\leq C\,C_{1}\,c^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\mu(2B)\leq C\,|B|^{\frac{n-p}{n}}.

Thus, (
59
) holds for all
1<p<n
, and consequently

u\in{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Non-weight modules over the mirror Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
Dongfang Gao, Yao Ma, Kaiming Zhao

Abstract
. In this paper, we study irreducible non-weight modules over the mirror Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra 𝒟 \mathcal{D} , including Whittaker modules, 𝒰 ⁡ ( ℂ ​ d 0 ) \mathcal{U}(\mathbb{C}d_{0}) -free modules, and their tensor products. More precisely, we give the necessary and sufficient conditions for the Whittaker modules to be irreducible. We determine all 𝒟 \mathcal{D} -module structures on 𝒰 ⁡ ( ℂ ​ d 0 ) \mathcal{U}(\mathbb{C}d_{0}) , and find the necessary and sufficient conditions for these modules to be irreducible. At last we determine the necessary and sufficient conditions for the tensor products of Whittaker modules and 𝒰 ⁡ ( ℂ ​ d 0 ) \mathcal{U}(\mathbb{C}d_{0}) -free modules to be irreducible, and obtain that any two such tensor products are isomorphic if and only if the corresponding Whittaker modules and 𝒰 ⁡ ( ℂ ​ d 0 ) \mathcal{U}(\mathbb{C}d_{0}) -free modules are isomorphic. These lead to many new irreducible non-weight modules over 𝒟 \mathcal{D} .

Harish-Chandra modules and weight modules with infinite-dimensional weight spaces

2.
Notations and Preliminaries

For convenience, in this section we recall some notations and collect some known results.

Definition 2.1
.

The
mirror Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra

\mathcal{D}
is a Lie algebra with basis

\{d_{m},h_{r},{\bf{c}},{\bf{l}}~|~m\in\mathbb{Z},r\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}\}
subject to the following commutation relations

\begin{split}&[d_{m},d_{n}]=(m-n)d_{m+n}+\frac{m^{3}-m}{12}{\delta}_{m+n,0}{{\bf{c}}},\\
&[d_{m},h_{r}]=-rh_{m+r},\\
&[h_{r},h_{s}]=r{\delta}_{r+s,0}{\bf{l}},\\
&[{\bf{c}},\mathcal{D}]=[{\bf{l}},\mathcal{D}]=0,\end{split}

for
m,n\in\mathbb{Z},r,s\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}
.

The Lie subalgebra spanned by
\{d_{m},{\bf{c}}~|~m\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is the Virasoro algebra
\mathcal{V}
,
and the Lie subalgebra spanned by
\{h_{r},{\bf{l}}~|~r\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}\}

is the twisted Heisenberg algebra
\mathcal{H}
.
Moreover,
\mathcal{H}
is an ideal of
\mathcal{D}

and
\mathcal{D}
is the semi-direct product of
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{H}
.
It is clear that
\mathcal{D},\mathcal{V},\mathcal{H}
are all
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie algebras.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{G}}_{i}
(resp.
\mathcal{G}=\oplus_{i\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{G}}_{i}
) be a
\mathbb{Z}
(resp.
\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
)-graded Lie algebra.
A
\mathcal{G}
-module
V
is called the
\bf{restricted}
module if for any
v\in V
there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{G}_{i}v=0
,
for
i>n
(resp.
i>\frac{1}{2}n
). The category of restricted modules over
\mathcal{G}
will be denoted as
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{G}}
.

Note that if
V
is a
\mathcal{V}
-module,
then
V
can be easily viewed as a
\mathcal{D}
-module by defining
\mathcal{H}V=0
,
the resulting module is denoted by
V^{\mathcal{D}}
.
Thanks to
[
18
]
, for any
H\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{H}}
with the action of
\bf l
as a nonzero scalar
l
, we can give
H
a
\mathcal{D}
-module structure
denoted by
H^{{\mathcal{D}}}
via the following map

\displaystyle d_{n}

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{1}{2l}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}h_{n-k}h_{k},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{Z},n\neq 0,

(2.1)

\displaystyle d_{0}

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{1}{2l}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}h_{-|k|}h_{|k|}+\frac{1}{16},

(2.2)

\displaystyle h_{r}

\displaystyle\mapsto h_{r},\quad\forall r\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z},\quad{\bf{c}}\mapsto 1,\quad{\bf{l}}\mapsto l.

(2.3)

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a Lie algebra. Suppose
f
is an automorphism of
\mathcal{G}
and
V
is a
\mathcal{G}
-module.
The following actions

x\cdot v=f(x)v,\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{G},v\in V

give

a new
\mathcal{G}
-module structure, denoted by

Then

Remark 2.4
.

It is easy to see that equivalent modules over the Lie algebra have the same irreducibility.

For convenience, we define the following subalgebras. For any
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, set

\begin{split}&\mathcal{D}^{(m,-n)}=\sum_{i\geq m}\mathbb{C}d_{i}\oplus\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\mathbb{C}h_{-n+i+\frac{1}{2}}\oplus\mathbb{C}{\bf c}\oplus\mathbb{C}{\bf l},\\
&\mathcal{D}^{(m,-\infty)}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\mathbb{C}d_{m+i}\oplus\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}h_{i+\frac{1}{2}}+\mathbb{C}{\bf c}+\mathbb{C}{\bf l},\\

3.
Whittaker modules over
\mathcal{D}

In this section, we will determine the necessary and sufficient conditions for the Whittaker modules over
\mathcal{D}

to be irreducible.

Assume that
\phi:\mathcal{H}^{(0)}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a Whittaker function,
and that
\mathbb{C}w_{\phi}
is the one-dimensional module over
\mathcal{H}^{(0)}

defined by
x\cdot w_{\phi}=\phi(x)w_{\phi}
, for
x\in\mathcal{H}^{(0)}
.
Then for the Whittaker
\mathcal{H}
-module

W_{\phi}=\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathcal{H}^{(0)}}^{\mathcal{H}}\mathbb{C}w_{\phi},

we have the following result.

Lemma 3.1
.

The Whittaker
\mathcal{H}
-module
W_{\phi}
is irreducible if and only if
\phi({\bf l})\neq 0
.

Proof.

We first assume that
\phi({\bf l})\neq 0
.
For any nonzero
v\in W_{\phi}
,
v
is a linear combination of vectors in the form

h_{-r-\frac{1}{2}}^{i_{r}}\cdots h_{-1-\frac{1}{2}}^{i_{1}}h_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{i_{0}}w_{\phi},

where
i_{0},i_{1},\cdots,i_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
by PBW Theorem.
Let
\langle v\rangle
denote the submodule generated by
v
of
W_{\phi}
.
It is not hard to see
w_{\phi}\in\langle v\rangle
. Thus
\langle v\rangle=W_{\phi}
, which implies that
W_{\phi}
is irreducible.

Now we assume that
\phi({\bf l})=0
. Then it is easy to see that

h_{-\frac{1}{2}}w_{\phi}
is a Whittaker vector, which implies that
h_{-\frac{1}{2}}w_{\phi}
generates a nonzero proper submodule of
W_{\phi}
. So
W_{\phi}
is not irreducible.
∎

Now for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, let
\varphi_{m}:\mathcal{D}^{(m,0)}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

be a Whittaker function, and
\mathbb{C}w_{\varphi_{m}}
be the one-dimensional
module over
\mathcal{D}^{(m,0)}
defined by
x\cdot w_{\varphi_{m}}=\varphi_{m}(x)w_{\varphi_{m}}
, for
x\in\mathcal{D}^{(m,0)}
.
In the rest of this section, we will determine the irreducibility of the Whittaker
\mathcal{D}
-module

W_{\varphi_{m}}=\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathcal{D}^{(m,0)}}^{\mathcal{D}}\mathbb{C}w_{\varphi_{m}}.

Each irreducible quotient of
W_{\varphi_{m}}
(if it exists) is called the irreducible Whittaker
\mathcal{D}
-module with respect to
\varphi_{m}
.
It is clear that

\varphi_{m}(d_{2m+j})=\varphi_{m}(h_{m+j-\frac{1}{2}})=0,\,\,\forall j\in\mathbb{N},

since
\varphi_{m}([\mathcal{D}^{(m,0)},\mathcal{D}^{(m,0)}])=0
.
In particular, if
m=0
, then
W_{\varphi_{m}}
is a Verma module,
which has been studied in
[
28
]
.

First we consider
W_{\varphi_{m}}
with
\varphi_{m}({\bf l})=l\neq 0
. We define a new Whittaker function

\varphi^{\prime}_{m}:\mathcal{V}^{(m)}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
as follows

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}_{m}({\bf c})=\varphi_{m}({\bf c})-1,

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}_{m}(d_{k})=0,\quad\forall k\geq 2m+1,

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}_{m}(d_{k})=\varphi_{m}(d_{k})-\frac{1}{2l}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\varphi_{m}(h_{i+\frac{1}{2}})\varphi_{m}(h_{k-i-\frac{1}{2}})-\delta_{0,k}\frac{1}{16},\,\,\forall m\leq k\leq 2m.

Then we have the Whittaker
\mathcal{V}
-module

{W}_{\varphi^{\prime}_{m}}=\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathcal{V}^{(m)}}^{\mathcal{V}}\mathbb{C}w_{\varphi^{\prime}_{m}},

where
\mathbb{C}w_{\varphi^{\prime}_{m}}
is the one-dimensional module over
\mathcal{V}^{(m)}
defined by
x\cdot w_{\varphi^{\prime}_{m}}=\varphi^{\prime}_{m}(x)w_{\varphi^{\prime}_{m}}
, for
x\in\mathcal{V}^{(m)}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose that
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, and
\varphi_{m},\varphi^{\prime}_{m}
are given as above with
\varphi_{m}({\bf l})\neq 0
.
Let
H=U(\mathcal{H})w_{\varphi_{m}}
be in
W_{\varphi_{m}}
.
The following observations hold.

(1).

W_{\varphi_{m}}\cong H^{\mathcal{D}}\otimes W_{\varphi^{\prime}_{m}}^{\mathcal{D}}
.
In particular, this agrees with
[
28
, Proposition 5.1]
if
m=0
.

(2).

Suppose
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
W_{\varphi_{m}}
is an irreducible
\mathcal{D}
-module if and only if

\varphi_{m}(d_{2m})\neq 0\,\,{\text{or}}\,\,2\varphi_{m}({\bf l})\varphi_{m}(d_{2m-1})-\varphi_{m}(h_{m-\frac{1}{2}})^{2}\neq 0.

(3).

Each irreducible Whittaker module over
\mathcal{D}
with respect to
\varphi_{m}

is isomorphic to
H^{\mathcal{D}}\otimes Q^{\mathcal{D}}
, where
Q
is an irreducible quotient of
\mathcal{V}
-module
W_{\varphi^{\prime}_{m}}
.

Proof.

(1) For
\mathcal{V}^{(m)}
-module
\mathbb{C}w_{\varphi^{\prime}_{m}}
,
we can easily give
\mathbb{C}w_{\varphi^{\prime}_{m}}
a
\mathcal{D}^{(m,-\infty)}
-module structure by defining
\mathcal{H}w_{\varphi^{\prime}_{m}}=0
.
The resulting module is denoted by
\mathbb{C}_{\varphi^{\prime}_{m}}^{\mathcal{D}^{(m,-\infty)}}
.
By PBW Theorem, we can define the linear map :

\displaystyle\pi:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathcal{D}^{(m,0)}}^{\mathcal{D}^{(m,-\infty)}}\mathbb{C}w_{\varphi_{m}}\rightarrow H^{\mathcal{D}}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\varphi^{\prime}_{m}}^{\mathcal{D}^{(m,-\infty)}},

\displaystyle h_{-r-\frac{1}{2}}^{q_{r}}\cdots h_{-1-\frac{1}{2}}^{q_{1}}h_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{q_{0}}w_{\varphi_{m}}\mapsto h_{-r-\frac{1}{2}}^{q_{r}}\cdots h_{-1-\frac{1}{2}}^{q_{1}}h_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{q_{0}}w_{\varphi_{m}}\otimes w_{\varphi^{\prime}_{m}}.

It is clear that
\pi
is a bijection.

Claim 1
.

\pi(hw_{\varphi_{m}})=hw_{\varphi_{m}}\otimes w_{\varphi^{\prime}_{m}}

for
h\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
.

For
h\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
,
h
can be written as a linear combination of vectors in the form

h_{-r_{1}-\frac{1}{2}}^{q_{r_{1}}}\cdots h_{-1-\frac{1}{2}}^{q_{1}}h_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{q_{0}}{\bf l}^{t}h_{\frac{1}{2}}^{p_{0}}h_{1+\frac{1}{2}}^{p_{1}}\cdots h_{s_{1}+\frac{1}{2}}^{p_{s_{1}}}

by PBW Theorem.
Without loss of generality, we can assume

h=h_{-r_{1}-\frac{1}{2}}^{q_{r_{1}}}\cdots h_{-1-\frac{1}{2}}^{q_{1}}h_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{q_{0}}{\bf l}^{t}h_{\frac{1}{2}}^{p_{0}}h_{1+\frac{1}{2}}^{p_{1}}\cdots h_{s_{1}+\frac{1}{2}}^{p_{s_{1}}},

where
q_{i},p_{j},t,r_{1},s_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},
for
0\leq i\leq r_{1},0\leq j\leq s_{1}
. We compute

\begin{split}\pi(hw_{\varphi_{m}})=&\pi(h_{-r_{1}-\frac{1}{2}}^{q_{r_{1}}}\cdots h_{-1-\frac{1}{2}}^{q_{1}}h_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{q_{0}}{\bf l}^{t}h_{\frac{1}{2}}^{p_{0}}h_{1+\frac{1}{2}}^{p_{1}}\cdots h_{s_{1}+\frac{1}{2}}^{p_{s_{1}}}w_{\varphi_{m}})\\
=&\varphi_{m}({\bf l})^{t}\varphi_{m}(h_{\frac{1}{2}})^{p_{0}}\varphi_{m}(h_{1+\frac{1}{2}})^{p_{1}}\cdots\varphi_{m}(h_{s_{1}+\frac{1}{2}})^{p_{s_{1}}}\pi(h_{-r_{1}-\frac{1}{2}}^{q_{r_{1}}}\cdots h_{-1-\frac{1}{2}}^{q_{1}}h_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{q_{0}}w_{\varphi_{m}})\\

Theorem 4.3

\beta\neq 0
, then we get that

\beta^{-1}\lambda^{-r}h_{r}w=\sum_{i=0}^{s}(t+r)^{i}\otimes v_{i}\in M,

for
r\geq N
. We can write the right hand side in the form

\sum_{i=0}^{s}r^{i}w_{i}\in M,\forall r>N,

where
w_{i}\in\Omega(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)\otimes V
are independent of
r
. In particular,
w_{s}=1\otimes v_{s}
.
Taking
r=\frac{1}{2}+N,\frac{1}{2}+(N+1),\cdots,\frac{1}{2}+(N+s)
, we see that the coefficient matrix of
w_{i}
is a Vandermonde matrix.
Thus each
w_{i}\in M
. In particular,
w_{s}=1\otimes v_{s}\in M
.

If
\beta=0
, then
\alpha\neq 0
. Similarly, using the action of
d_{m}w,m>N
we can show that Claim
5
holds as well.

Claim 6
.

M=\Omega(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)\otimes V
.

From Claim
5
, we have that
1\otimes v\in M
for some nonzero
v\in V
.
Note that

\displaystyle d_{m}(t^{j}\otimes v)=\lambda^{m}(t+m\alpha)(t+m)^{j}\otimes v=\lambda^{m}t(t+m)^{j}\otimes v+\lambda^{m}m\alpha(t+m)^{j}\otimes v,

(5.1)

for any
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},m\geq N(v)
.
Thus by induction on
j
, we deduce that
t^{j}\otimes v\in M
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
i.e.,
\Omega(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)\otimes v\in M
.
Define

W=\{w\in V:\Omega(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)\otimes w\subseteq M\}.

It is clear that
W\neq 0
since
v\in W
. We need to prove that
W
is
\mathcal{D}
-submodule of
V
.
For any
w\in W,m\in\mathbb{Z},r\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}
, we compute that

\displaystyle d_{m}(\Omega(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)\otimes w)=d_{m}\Omega(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)\otimes w+\Omega(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)\otimes d_{m}w\subseteq M,

\displaystyle h_{r}(\Omega(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)\otimes w)=h_{r}\Omega(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)\otimes w+\Omega(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)\otimes h_{r}w\subseteq M.

Thus

\Omega(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)\otimes d_{m}w,\Omega(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)\otimes h_{r}w\subseteq M
, i.e.,
d_{m}w,h_{r}w\in W
.
These show that
W
is a nonzero submodule of
V
. Thus
W=V
since
V
is irreducible,
which implies
M=\Omega(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)\otimes V
. We complete the proof.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\lambda,\lambda_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{*},\alpha,\alpha_{1},\beta,\beta_{1}\in\mathbb{C}
.
Assume that
V
and
V_{1}
are irreducible restricted modules over
\mathcal{D}
.
Then
\Omega(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)\otimes V
and
\Omega(\lambda_{1},\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})\otimes V_{1}
are isomorphic as
\mathcal{D}
-modules
if and only if
(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)=(\lambda_{1},\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})
and
V\cong V_{1}
as
\mathcal{D}
-modules.

Proof.

It is clear that “if part” is trivial. We only need to show “only if part”.
Let
\psi:\Omega(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)\otimes V\to\Omega(\lambda_{1},\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})\otimes V_{1}
be a module isomorphism.
For any nonzero element
1\otimes v\in\Omega(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)\otimes V
, suppose

\psi(1\otimes v)=\sum_{i=0}^{p}t^{i}\otimes w_{i},

where
w_{i}\in V_{1}
and
w_{p}\neq 0
. Let
N_{v}=max\{N{(v)},N{(w_{0})},N{(w_{1})},\cdots,N{(w_{p})}\}
.
Then

d_{m}v=d_{m}(w_{i})=h_{r}v=h_{r}(w_{i})=0,

for
m,r\geq N_{v},i=0,1,\cdots,p
.

Claim 7
.

\lambda=\lambda_{1},\alpha=\alpha_{1},\beta=\beta_{1}
.

Take
m,m_{1}\geq N_{v}
, we have

(\lambda^{-m}d_{m}-\lambda^{-m_{1}}d_{m_{1}})(1\otimes v)=\alpha(m-m_{1})(1\otimes v).

By
(\lambda^{-m}d_{m}-\lambda^{-m_{1}}d_{m_{1}})\psi(1\otimes v)=\alpha(m-m_{1})\psi(1\otimes v)
,
we deduce that

\displaystyle\alpha(m-m_{1})\sum_{i=0}^{p}t^{i}\otimes w_{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{p}\{(\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda})^{m}(t+m\alpha_{1})(t+m)^{i}-(\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda})^{m_{1}}(t+m_{1}\alpha_{1})(t+m_{1})^{i}\}\otimes w_{i}.

(5.2)

Thus
((\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda})^{m}-(\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda})^{m_{1}})t^{p+1}\otimes w_{p}=0
for
m,m_{1}\geq N_{v}
.
So,
\lambda=\lambda_{1}
. Therefore, the equation (
5.2
) becomes

\alpha(m-m_{1})\sum_{i=0}^{p}t^{i}\otimes w_{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{p}t\{(t+m)^{i}-(t+m_{1})^{i}\}\otimes w_{i}+\sum_{i=0}^{p}\alpha_{1}\{m(t+m)^{i}-m_{1}(t+m_{1})^{i}\}\otimes w_{i},

for
m,m_{1}\geq N_{v}
. Then the coefficient of
t^{p}\otimes w_{p}
in the right side is
(\alpha_{1}+p)(m-m_{1})

which implies
\alpha=p+\alpha_{1}
, i.e.,
\alpha-\alpha_{1}=p\geq 0
.
Similar, using
\psi^{-1}
we can get
\alpha-\alpha_{1}\geq 0
.
So
\alpha=\alpha_{1}
and
p=0
. Thus

\psi(1\otimes v)=1\otimes w_{0}.

Note that for any
r\geq N_{v}
, we have

\displaystyle\beta\lambda^{r}(1\otimes w_{0})=\beta\lambda^{r}\psi(1\otimes v)=\psi(h_{r}(1\otimes v))=h_{r}(1\otimes w_{0})=\beta_{1}\lambda_{1}^{r}(1\otimes w_{0}).

Thus
\beta=\beta_{1}
.
Hence, Claim
7
is proved.

Now we define the linear map

\xi:V\rightarrow V_{1},

such that
\psi(1\otimes v)=1\otimes\xi(v)

for
v\in V
. It is clear that
\xi
is injective.
Note that for any
r\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}
,

\begin{split}\beta\lambda^{r}(1\otimes\xi(v))+1\otimes\xi(h_{r}(v))&=\psi(h_{r}(1\otimes v))=h_{r}(\psi(1\otimes v))\\
&=h_{r}(1\otimes\xi(v))=\beta\lambda^{r}(1\otimes\xi(v))+1\otimes h_{r}(\xi(v)),\end{split}

which implies

\displaystyle\xi(h_{r}(v))=h_{r}(\xi(v)),\forall v\in V,r\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}.

(5.3)

Next, we consider the equation

\psi(d_{m}(1\otimes v))=d_{m}(\psi(1\otimes v))

for
m\geq N_{v}
. We deduce

\displaystyle\lambda^{m}\psi(t\otimes v)+m\alpha\lambda^{m}(1\otimes\xi(v))=\lambda^{m}t\otimes\xi(v)+m\alpha\lambda^{m}(1\otimes\xi(v)).

So,
\psi(t\otimes v)=t\otimes\xi(v)
.
Hence

\psi(d_{n}(1)\otimes v)=d_{n}(1)\otimes\xi(v),

for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Using the equation
\psi(d_{n}(1\otimes v))=d_{n}\psi(1\otimes v)
for
n\in\mathbb{Z},v\in V
,
we deduce

1\otimes\xi(d_{n}(v))=1\otimes d_{n}(\xi(v)).

That shows

\displaystyle\xi(d_{n}(v))=d_{n}(\xi(v)),\forall v\in V,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

(5.4)

It is clear that

\psi({\bf c}(1\otimes v))={\bf c}\psi(1\otimes v){\rm\ and\ }\psi({\bf l}(1\otimes v))={\bf l}\psi(1\otimes v),

imply that

\displaystyle\xi({\bf c}(v))={\bf c}\xi(v){\rm\ and\ }\xi({\bf l}(v))={\bf l}\xi(v).

(5.5)

Therefore,
\xi
is a
\mathcal{D}
-module homomorphism by equations (
5.3
), (
5.4
), and (
5.5
).
Since
\xi(V)\neq 0
and
V_{1}
is irreducible, so
\xi(V)=V_{1}
.
Thus
\xi
is a
\mathcal{D}
-module isomorphism from
V
to
V_{1}
.
In conclusion, we complete the proof.
∎

It is clear that Whittaker
\mathcal{D}
-modules
W_{\varphi_{m}}
are restricted modules over
\mathcal{D}
.
So we have the following two main theorems from Theorems
3.4
,
4.3

and Propositions
5.1
,
5.2
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N},\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{*},\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
, and
\varphi_{m}:\mathcal{D}^{(m,0)}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a Whittaker function.

(1).

If
\varphi_{m}({\bf l})\neq 0
, then
\Omega(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)\otimes W_{\varphi_{m}}
is an irreducible
\mathcal{D}
-module
if and only if

(\varphi_{m}(d_{2m}),2\varphi_{m}({\bf l})\varphi_{m}(d_{2m-1})-\varphi_{m}(h_{m-\frac{1}{2}})^{2})\neq(0,0){\rm\ and\ }(\alpha,\beta)\neq(0,0).

(2).

If
\varphi_{m}({\bf l})=0
, then
\Omega(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)\otimes W_{\varphi_{m}}
is an irreducible
\mathcal{D}
-module
if and only if

\varphi_{m}(h_{m-\frac{1}{2}})\neq 0{\rm\ and\ }(\alpha,\beta)\neq(0,0).

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
m,m_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},\lambda,\lambda_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{*},\alpha,\alpha_{1},\beta,\beta_{1}\in\mathbb{C}.

Suppose that
\varphi_{m}:\mathcal{D}^{(m,0)}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
\varphi_{m_{1}}:\mathcal{D}^{(m_{1},0)}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be the Whittaker functions such that the corresponding
Whittaker
\mathcal{D}
-modules
W_{\varphi_{m_{1}}}
and
W_{\varphi_{m_{1}}}
are all irreducible.
The following results hold.

(1).

\Omega(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)\cong\Omega(\lambda_{1},\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})
if and only if

(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)=(\lambda_{1},\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})
.

(2).

\Omega(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)\otimes W_{\varphi_{m}}\cong\Omega(\lambda_{1},\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})\otimes W_{\varphi_{m_{1}}}

if and only if
(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)=(\lambda_{1},\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})
and
W_{\varphi_{m}}\cong W_{\varphi_{m_{1}}}
.

Remark 5.5
.

Using Theorems
3.4
,
4.3
and
5.3
we can obtain many new irreducible non-weight modules over
\mathcal{D}
.

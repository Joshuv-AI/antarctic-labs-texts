Abstract
. We study the spaces of embeddings S m ↪ ℝ n S^{m}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n} and those of long embeddings ℝ m ↪ ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{m}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n} , i.e. embeddings of a fixed behavior outside a compact set. More precisely we look at the homotopy fiber of the inclusion of these spaces to the spaces of immersions. We find a natural fiber sequence relating these spaces. We also compare the L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebras of diagrams that encode their rational homotopy type, when the codimension n − m ≥ 3 n-m\geq 3 .



1.
Introduction

In this paper we study a relation between the following two spaces:

(1)

\displaystyle\Embbar(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n}):=\mathrm{hofiber}\left(\Emb(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to\Imm(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\right);

(2)

\displaystyle\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n}):=\mathrm{hofiber}\left(\Emb_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to\Imm_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\right),

where
\Emb(-,-)
and
\Imm(-,-)
always refer to spaces of smooth embeddings and immersions, respectively. The homotopy fiber is taken over
the inclusions
i_{1}\colon S^{m}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m+1}\times 0^{n-m-1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
i_{2}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{m}={\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times 0^{n-m}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. The subscript

\partial
means that the embeddings and immersions must coincide with the inclusion
i_{2}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
outside a compact subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. The spaces (
1
) and (
2
) are called

spaces of embeddings modulo immersions
.

The spaces
\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
have been objects of active study
[

,

,

,

,

,

E_{m+1}
-algebras
[

,

,
28
]
equivalent to
(m+1)
-loop spaces
[
3
,
10
,
28
]
when
n-m\geq 3
.

To compare their homotopy type to that of
\Embbar(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
let us begin with a few observations.
Given an embedding
\psi\in\Embbar(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, we can define an inclusion

(3)

\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow\Embbar(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

The idea of this map is to perturb
\psi
near some point
p\in S^{m}
. By standard fibration and transversality arguments it is easy to show that for
n-m\geq 3
,
\pi_{*}\Emb_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\simeq\pi_{*}\Emb(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
\pi_{*}\Imm_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\simeq\pi_{*}\Imm(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
for
*\leq 1
. This implies that the inclusion (
3
) induces a bijection of the sets of connected components
\pi_{0}\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\simeq\pi_{0}\Embbar(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. It is also not hard to show that the inclusion (
3
) can be enhanced to an
\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
-action on

\Embbar(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
1
1

1

To carefully define this action the spaces
\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
\Embbar(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
need to be replaced by the homotopy equivalent spaces
\Embbar{}^{fr}_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
\Embbar{}^{fr}(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
of framed embeddings modulo framed immersions.
Our main result now states that the homotopy quotient of
\Embbar(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
by the action of
\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is the sphere
S^{n{-}m{-}1}
, or equivalently that
\Embbar(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is homotopy equivalent to a principal
\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
-bundle on the sphere.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
n-m\geq 3
, one has an equivalence

(4)

\Embbar(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\simeq\mathrm{hofiber}\left(S^{n-m-1}\xrightarrow{}B\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\right).

This in particular implies that all connected components of
\Embbar(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
have the same homotopy type and that
\pi_{0}\Embbar(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\pi_{0}\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

For an explicit definition of the classifying map
S^{n-m-1}\xrightarrow{}B\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
appearing in this Theorem we refer to Section
2
.

As a consequence we will in particular be able to express the rational homotopy types of
\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
\Embbar(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
through each other, see Corollaries
2.5
,
2.6
below. It is furthermore well known that the rational homotopy type of
\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
n-m\geq 3
, may be expressed through hairy graph-complexes.
More precisely, in
[
2
]
hairy graph-complexes were introduced, denoted by
{\mathrm{HGC}}_{\bar{A}_{m},n}
in this paper, which were proved
to compute the rational homotopy groups

(5)

H_{*}({\mathrm{HGC}}_{\bar{A}_{m},n})\simeq{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\pi_{*}\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})

for
n\geq 2m+2

[

(m+1)
-st delooping of
\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
n-m\geq 3

In particular

-loop space its set of connected components forms an abelian group (in fact finitely generated). The cited theorem proves the isomorphism (
5

can have non-trivial homology in negative degrees, that has to be ignored.

2.
Spherical and long embeddings

In this section we describe how the homotopy type of
\Embbar(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is compared to that of
\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, and in particular prove Theorem
1.1
. Throughout Sections
2
and
3
we assume
n-m\geq 3
.

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

The second statement of the theorem holds because the sphere
S^{n-m-1}
is simply connected.
By the Smale-Hirsch theorem
[
19
,
29
]
,
\Imm_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\simeq\Omega^{m}V_{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, where
V_{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\mathrm{SO}(n)/\mathrm{SO}(n{-}m)
is the Stiefel manifold
of orthogonal
m
-frames in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Thus

(6)

\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\simeq\mathrm{hofiber}\Bigl(\Emb_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\xrightarrow{D}\Omega^{m}V_{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\Bigr).

The space
\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is an
(m+1)
-loop space
[
3
,
10
,
28
]
. We denote by
B\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
its classifying space
and by
g
the map

(7)

g\colon\Omega_{*}^{m}V_{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\simeq B\Omega^{m+1}V_{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to B\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})

(where
\Omega_{*}
stays for the loop space component of the constant map) obtained by applying the classifying space functor
B
to the inclusion
\Omega^{m+1}V_{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

Consider also the map

(8)

h\colon S^{n-m-1}\to\Omega^{m}V_{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

adjoint to the composition

(9)

\Sigma^{m}S^{n-m-1}=S^{n-1}\xrightarrow{h_{0}}\mathrm{SO}(n)\to\mathrm{SO}(n)/\mathrm{SO}(n{-}m)=V_{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),

where
h_{0}
is the transition map for the tangent bundle of
S^{n}=D^{n}_{+}\cup_{S^{n-1}}D^{n}_{-}
relating trivializations over the upper and lower discs

D^{n}_{+}
and
D^{n}_{-}
. Note that since we assume
n-m\geq 3
,
the sphere
S^{n-m-1}
is connected and
h(S^{n-m-1})\subset\Omega_{*}^{m}V_{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

To show Theorem
1.1
we will check explicitly that for
n-m\geq 3
one has an equivalence

(10)

\Embbar(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\simeq\mathrm{hofiber}\left(S^{n-m-1}\xrightarrow{g\circ h}B\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\right).

In other words, the space
\Embbar(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is equivalent to a principal
\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
-bundle over
S^{n-m-1}
with the structure subgroup

\Omega^{m+1}V_{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\subset\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

The equivalence (
10
) and hence Theorem
1.1
can be shown using the following two propositions.

Proposition 2.1
.

For
n-m\geq 3
, one has an equivalence

\Embbar(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\simeq\mathrm{hofiber}\left(\Emb_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\times S^{n-m-1}\xrightarrow{m\circ(D\times h)}\Omega^{m}V_{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\right),

where
m\colon\Omega^{m}V_{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\times\Omega^{m}V_{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to\Omega^{m}V_{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is a loop product.

To recall
D
and
h
denote the maps from (
6
) and (
8
).
Proposition
2.1
is related to and in fact is a consequence of Budney-Cohen’s

[
4
, Proposition 4.4]
.
For completeness of exposition we present its full proof below.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
Y\xrightarrow{f}X
be a map of pointed spaces,
Z\xrightarrow{h}\Omega X
be any map. Let also

\Omega X\xrightarrow{i}\mathrm{hofiber}(Y\xrightarrow{f}X)
denote the natural inclusion and
m\colon\Omega X\times\Omega X\to\Omega X
denote the loop product. One has an equivalence

(11)

\mathrm{hofiber}\left(Z\xrightarrow{i\circ h}\mathrm{hofiber}(Y\xrightarrow{f}X)\right)\simeq\mathrm{hofiber}\left(\Omega Y\times Z\xrightarrow{m\circ(\Omega f\times h)}\Omega X\right).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We apply Proposition
2.1
and Proposition
2.2
to
the case
Y\xrightarrow{f}X
being
B\Emb_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\xrightarrow{BD}\Omega_{*}^{m-1}V_{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, and

Z\xrightarrow{h}\Omega X
being
S^{n-m-1}\xrightarrow{h}\Omega^{m}V_{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
One has,
\Omega B\Emb_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\simeq\Emb_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
because
\pi_{0}\Emb_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
n-m\geq 3
, is a group
[
16
,
17
]
. (Explicit
deloopings of
\Emb_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
n-m\geq 3
, were obtained in
[
3
,
10
,
28
]
.) Note that

(12)

\mathrm{hofiber}\left(B\Emb_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\xrightarrow{BD}\Omega_{*}^{m-1}V_{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\right)\simeq\Omega^{m}V_{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\sslash\Emb_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

The sphere
S^{n-m-1}
is connected and each connected component of (
12
) is equivalent to
B\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, which immediately yields (
10
).

∎

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Denote by
\Emb_{*}(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
\Imm_{*}(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
the spaces of embeddings and immersions, respectively, with a fixed behavior near
the basepoint
*\in S^{m}
. One can easily see that the space

\Embbar_{*}(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n}):=\mathrm{hofiber}\left(\Emb_{*}(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\xhookrightarrow{I}\Imm_{*}(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\right)

is weakly equivalent to
\Embbar(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Moreover, we claim that
\Emb_{*}(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\simeq\Emb_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\times S^{n-m-1}
and

\Imm_{*}(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\simeq\Omega^{m}V_{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with the map
I
of the homotopy type of
m\circ(D\times h)
.

We decompose
S^{m}=D^{m}_{+}\cup_{S^{m-1}}D^{m}_{-}
, where
D^{m}_{-}
is a small closed disc neighborhood of the basepoint
*\in S^{m}
, and
D^{m}_{+}
is its
complementary disc. We identify
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}=S^{n}\setminus\{N\}
as a sphere without its north pole. Similarly we decompose
S^{n}\setminus\{N\}=\left(D^{n}_{+}\setminus\{N\}\right)\cup_{S^{n-1}}D^{n}_{-}
. One has

(13)

\Emb_{*}(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\cong\Emb_{\partial}(D^{m}_{+},D^{n}_{+}\setminus\{N\}),

(14)

\Imm_{*}(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\cong\Imm_{\partial}(D^{m}_{+},S^{n}\setminus\{N\})\simeq\Omega^{m}V_{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

The last equivalence in (
14
) is by the Smale-Hirsch theorem, as the target manifold
S^{n}\setminus\{N\}={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is contractible.

Remark 2.3
.

The transition map between the coordinate framing on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}=S^{n}\setminus\{N\}
and the local coordinates framing near
N
, when restricted on a small
(n-1)
-sphere around
N
, is given by the map
h_{0}
from equation (
9
).

\Emb_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\sqcup\{*\},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})

H({\mathrm{HGC}}_{A_{m},n}^{\prime})\cong H({\mathrm{HGC}}_{A^{\prime}_{m},n}^{\prime})\cong H({\mathrm{HGC}}_{\bar{A}_{m},n}^{\prime})\oplus{\mathbb{Q}}T,

T=0

{\mathrm{HGC}}_{A_{m},n}^{\prime}

{\mathrm{HGC}}_{A_{m},n}
is a direct sum of three complexes

(23)

{\mathrm{HGC}}_{A_{m},n}=W_{0}\oplus{\mathrm{HGC}}_{A_{m},n}^{\prime}\oplus\bigl({\mathbb{Q}}L\oplus{\mathbb{Q}}D),

with
W_{0}

4.
Codimension
n-m\leq 2

4.1.
Codimension one

Proposition 4.1
.

For
n\geq 2
, one has an equivalence

\Embbar(S^{n-1},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\simeq\begin{cases}S^{0}\times\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1},{\mathbb{R}}^{n}),&n=3\text{ or }7;\\
\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1},{\mathbb{R}}^{n}),&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

It is easy to see that the statement of Proposition
2.1
holds for
n-m=1
. The crucial fact is that the complement of any long knot
{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is homotopy equivalent
to
S^{0}
, which follows from the generalized Schoenflies theorem
[
24
]
. Any codimension one long knot is
regularly homotopic to the trivial one
[
20
, Theorem 2]
, which means that the induced map

D_{*}\colon\pi_{0}\Emb_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to\pi_{0}\Omega^{n-1}V_{n-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\pi_{n-1}\mathrm{SO}(n)

is zero. On the other hand, the map

h_{*}\colon\pi_{0}S^{0}\to\pi_{n-1}\mathrm{SO}(n)

is trivial iff
S^{n-1}
can be eversed in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, i.e., iff
n=3
or 7
[
29
,
20
]
. The result follows.
∎

It is known that
\pi_{0}\Emb_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\theta_{n}
,
n\neq 4
, – the group of
n
-spheres,
see
[
5
, Section 5]
and references within. (For
n=4
, the question whether the space is connected is
equivalent to the smooth Schoenflies problem – does a smoothly embedded
S^{3}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
always bound the standard
D^{4}
, which is still open in this dimension.) By the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
,
one gets

\Embbar(S^{n-1},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\simeq\mathrm{hofiber}\left(S^{0}\to B\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\times\pi_{n-1}\mathrm{SO}(n)\right),\,\,n\neq 4,

since
\Omega^{n-1}\mathrm{SO}(n)\sslash\Emb_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\simeq B\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\times\pi_{n-1}\mathrm{SO}(n)
. We conclude that the main statement of Theorem
1.1

holds for
n=m+1
iff
n=3
or 7.

4.2.
Codimension two

The main statement of Theorem
1.1
always fails in codimension
n-m=2
. There are two reasons for this.
Firstly, for
n\geq 3
, neither
\Emb_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-2},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, nor
\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-2},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
are loop spaces

[
5
, Proposition 5.11]
8
8

8

It was pointed out to us by R. Budney that
the proof of this proposition has a little mistake that can easily be corrected. Contrary to what is said, there are codimension two long knots
f
with exterior

C_{f}\nsimeq S^{1}
and
\pi_{1}C_{f}={\mathbb{Z}}
. Such knots are studied in
[
18
]
. However, only
the trivial non-parametrized knot
S^{n-2}\hookrightarrow S^{n}
is invertible according to
[
30
,
31
,
23
]
.
.
Secondly, the complement
C_{f}
of a long knot
f\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{n-2}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
almost always
is not weakly equivalent to
S^{1}

[
30
]
. Thus, Proposition
2.1
does not hold for
n=m+2
.
Indeed, the space
\Emb_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-2},{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
is weakly homotopy
equivalent to
\Emb_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-2}\sqcup\{*\},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
(by the same argument as in Lemma
2.4
),
but the latter space is a possibly non-trivial fiber bundle over
\Emb_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-2},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with fiber
C_{f}
.

4.3.
Goodwillie-Weiss calculus and graph-complexes

Given a (formally) immersed manifold
M
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, one can consider the functor
\Embbar(-,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})

and its objectwise rationalization
\Embbar(-,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{\mathbb{Q}}
on the poset of open sets of
M
.
Goodwillie-Weiss calculus
[
15
,
33
]
produces Taylor towers of approximations to
these two functors:

(24)

\Embbar(M,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to T_{\infty}\Embbar(M,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to T_{\infty}\Embbar(M,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{\mathbb{Q}}.

In case codimension is
\geq 3
, the first map is an equivalence
[
14
,
13
]
, and the second
map is finite-to-one on
\pi_{0}
and a rational equivalence on connected components
[
11
, Section 4.2]
.
Even when the codimension condition is not satisfied, it can still be interesting to know what is the right-hand
side space of (
24
) as it can provide interesting invariants or more generally cohomology classes
of the embedding space in question. In
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
, B. Fresse and the authors computed

T_{\infty}\Embbar(M,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{\mathbb{Q}}
expressing it as the simplicial set of Maurer-Cartan elements of associated

L_{\infty}
-algebra of hairy graph-complexes, provided
M
is immersible or formally immersible in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n-2}
. In
particular, one has

(25)

\displaystyle T_{\infty}\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{\mathbb{Q}}

\displaystyle\simeq\mathsf{MC}_{\bullet}({\mathrm{HGC}}_{\bar{A}_{m},n}),\,\,n-m\geq 2;

(26)

\displaystyle T_{\infty}\Embbar(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{\mathbb{Q}}

\displaystyle\simeq\mathsf{MC}_{\bullet}({\mathrm{HGC}}_{A_{m},n}),\,\,n-m\geq 3\text{ or }n=m+2=3,5\text{ or }9.

(One needs
S^{n-2}
to be parallelizable to be formally immersible in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n-2}
, which is only true for

S^{1}
,
S^{3}
, and
S^{7}

[
20
,
29
]
.) When the codimension
n-m=2
, the hairy graph-complexes are no more of finite type and their
elements are infinite series of graphs. The graph-complexes in question are considered as completed pronilpotent
L_{\infty}
-algebras, the completion being taken with
respect to the complexity filtration, see Proof of Lemma
3.4
.
Since the
L_{\infty}
-structure of
{\mathrm{HGC}}_{\bar{A}_{m},n}
is abelian,
each space (
25
) is a product of Eilenberg-MacLane spaces

T_{\infty}\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}K\left(H_{i}({\mathrm{HGC}}_{\bar{A}_{m},n}),\,i\,\right),\,\,n-m\geq 2.

In particular this means that for
n-m\geq 2
,

\pi_{0}T_{\infty}\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{\mathbb{Q}}=\mathsf{MC}({\mathrm{HGC}}_{\bar{A}_{m},n})/{\sim}=H_{0}({\mathrm{HGC}}_{\bar{A}_{m},n}).

The statements of Theorem
3.1
and Corollary
3.3
hold for any
m
and
n
, in particular they
are also true in codimension
n-m=2
. The inclusion
U^{t}\oplus{\mathrm{HGC}}_{\bar{A}_{m},n}\subset{\mathrm{HGC}}_{A_{m},n}
is a quasi-isomorphism
of filtered (by complexity) completed
L_{\infty}
-algebras, which induces a quasi-isomorphism of associated graded complexes. By the generalized
Goldman-Millson theorem
[
9
]
, this inclusion induces an equivalence of simplicial sets

\mathsf{MC}_{\bullet}(U^{t}\oplus{\mathrm{HGC}}_{\bar{A}_{m},n})\simeq\mathsf{MC}_{\bullet}\left({\mathrm{HGC}}_{A_{m},n}\right).

As a consequence, in the range of equivalence (
26
), one has

\pi_{0}T_{\infty}\Embbar(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{\mathbb{Q}}=\mathsf{MC}({\mathrm{HGC}}_{A_{m},n})/{\sim}=\mathsf{MC}(U^{t}\oplus{\mathrm{HGC}}_{\bar{A}_{m},n})/{\sim}=\\
\mathsf{MC}({\mathrm{HGC}}_{\bar{A}_{m},n})/{\sim}=H_{0}({\mathrm{HGC}}_{\bar{A}_{m},n})=H_{0}({\mathrm{HGC}}_{A_{m},n}).

Indeed, for
n-m\geq 3
the third and last equalities are true by degree reasons, see
[
12
, Corollary 5.2]
, while for
n-m=2
and
n
odd,
U^{t}
is
a direct summand one-dimensional
L_{\infty}
-subalgebra of
U^{t}\oplus{\mathrm{HGC}}_{\bar{A}_{m},n}
(spanned by
L
of degree
1
). Thus, one has

T_{\infty}\Embbar(S^{n-2},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq K({\mathbb{Q}},1)\times T_{\infty}\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-2},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{\mathbb{Q}},\,\,n=3,\,5\text{ or }9.

One does not know yet how to express algebraically
T_{\infty}\Embbar(S^{m},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{\mathbb{Q}}
beyond the range of (
26
).
On the other hand, the equivalence (
25
) had been proved earlier by B. Fresse
and the authors in
[
11
, Theorem 1]
. In
[
11
, Corollaries 5 and 8]
, we similarly expressed
T_{\infty}\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{\mathbb{Q}}

and
T_{\infty}\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{\mathbb{Q}}
using the same, up to a degree one shift, graph-complex
\mathrm{GC}_{n}
,
the usual Kontsevich graph-complex of bald (no hairs) graphs endowed in both cases with the abelian
L_{\infty}
-structure.
The reason we get a smaller
complex for
n-m=1
is the relative non-formality of the little discs operads in codimension one
[
32
]
.
As a consequence, we obtain
[
11
, equation (14)]

T_{\infty}\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq\Omega T_{\infty}\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{\mathbb{Q}}.

Note that one also has

\Diff_{\partial}(D^{n})\simeq\Omega\Emb_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1},{\mathbb{R}}^{n}),

see
[
8
, Appendix, Section 5, Proposition 5]
,
[
5
, Proposition 5.3]
, which implies

\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},{\mathbb{R}}^{n}):=\mathrm{hofiber}\left(\Diff_{\partial}(D^{n})\to\Omega^{n}\mathrm{SO}(n)\right)\simeq\Omega\Embbar_{\partial}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1},{\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

This means that,
even though the Goodwillie-Weiss calculus is only applicable in codimensions
\geq 3
, it can still detect at least
rationally the codimension one versus codimension zero rigidity of embeddings.

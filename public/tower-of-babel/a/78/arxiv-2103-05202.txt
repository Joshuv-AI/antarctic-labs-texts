Introduction

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite, simple and undirected graph. A set of vertices in
G
is called
independent
if it does not contain an edge of
G
. We call a set
S

s
-set
if
|S|=s
. Now we cite the definition of
rainbow set
given in
[
1
]
.

Definition 1
[
1
]
Let
{\cal F}=(F_{1},\ldots,F_{m})
be a collection of (not necessarily distinct) sets. A
(partial) rainbow set
for
{\cal F}
is a set of the form

R=\{x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},\ldots,x_{i_{k}}\}
, where
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq m
, and

x_{i_{j}}\in F_{i_{j}}
(
j\leq k
). Here
R
is a set, namely that the
elements
x_{i_{j}}
are distinct.

When all sets in
{\cal F}
are matchings of
G
, then
R
is a (partial) rainbow matching for
{\cal F}
.
There are some results about rainbow matching when
G
is a bipartite graph. Drisko
[
4
]
showed that every family of
2n-1
matchings of size
n
in a bipartite graph where one side has size
n
contains
a rainbow matching of size
n
. Aharoni and Berger
[
3
]
showed that
this is true for all bipartite graphs. A well-known conjecture given by Ryser
[
6
]
was
that for odd
n
, every family of edge-disjoint
n
perfect matchings of

K_{n,n}
has a rainbow matching of size
n
. In
[
2
]
, some results about rainbow matchings for general graphs were given.

Given
a family
{\cal F}
of independent sets in a graph, a
rainbow independent set
is an
independent set whose vertices are contained in distinct independent sets in

{\cal F}
. For the line graph
H
of a graph
G
, rainbow matchings in
G
correspond to
rainbow independent sets in
H
. So the results on rainbow matchings of a graph
G
can be changed into rainbow independent sets of the line
graph of
G
. In this paper, we consider the rainbow independent sets.
We first introduce parameters given in
[
1
]
. For a graph
G
and integers
m\leq n
, let
f_{G}(n,m)
be the minimal
number
k
such that every
k
independent
n
-sets in
G
have a partial
rainbow independent
m
-set. For a class
{\cal C}
of graphs, let
f_{\cal C}(n,m)=\sup\{f_{G}(n,m)|G\in{\cal C}\}
. This can be
\infty
. In the same paper, Aharoni, Briggs, Kim and Kim
[
1
]
established finiteness or infiniteness of
f_{\cal C}(n,m)
for
many graph classes. In
[
5
]
, Kim, Kim and Kwon added two dense graph classes such that
f_{\cal C}(n,m)
is finiteness.
In
[
1
]
, Aharoni, Briggs, Kim and Kim presented some conjectures. We will pay attention to the case
{\cal C}=\{C_{2s+1}\}
and consider the following conjecture (Conjecture 2.9 in
[
1
]
).

Conjecture 1
[
1
]

If
s<\frac{t}{2}
, then
f_{C_{t}}(s,s)=s
.

Note that
f_{C_{t}}(s,s)\geq s
. In this paper, we will show that Conjecture 1 holds if
t=2s+1
, that is, we have the following result.

Theorem 1

Let
{\cal I}=(I_{1},I_{2},\ldots,I_{s})
be a set of independent sets of size
s
in
C_{2s+1}
(not necessarily distinct). Then there is a rainbow independent set of size
s
in
C_{2s+1}
, that is,
f_{C_{2s+1}}(s,s)=s
.

Let
V(C_{2s+1})=\mathbb{Z}_{2s+1}=\{1,2,\ldots,2s+1\}
with
(i,i+1)\in E(C_{2s+1})
(modulo
2s+1
) and
{\cal I}=(I_{1},I_{2},\ldots,I_{s})
be a set of independent sets of size
s
in
C_{2s+1}
. We define a bijection
f:\mathbb{Z}_{2s+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2s+1}
such that
f(j)=2j-1
for
1\leq j\leq 2s+1
(modulo
2s+1
). Then
f^{-1}(I_{i})
consists of
s
consecutive vertices on
C_{2s+1}
for all
1\leq i\leq s
. Thus Theorem 1 is equivalent to the following theorem.

Theorem 2

Let
{\cal F}=(V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{s})
, where
V_{i}
consists of
s
successive vertices on the cycle
C_{2s+1}
for
1\leq i\leq s
. Then there is a set of the form

\{x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},\ldots,x_{i_{s}}\}
such that

x_{i_{j}}\in V_{j}
(
1\leq j\leq s
) and
x_{i_{j}}x_{i_{j+1}}\in E(C_{2s+1})
for all
1\leq j\leq s-1
.

In Section 2, we will prove Theorem 2.

2. Proof of Theorem 2

Let
V(C_{2s+1})=\mathbb{Z}_{2s+1}=\{1,2,\ldots,2s+1\}
with
(i,i+1)\in E(C_{2s+1})
(modulo
2s+1
). Let
V_{i}=\{a_{i},a_{i}+1,\ldots,a_{i}+s-1\}
with
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2s+1}=V(C_{2s+1})
for
1\leq i\leq s
. Note that if there is
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,s\}
and
1\leq j\leq s-1
such that
a_{i}+j>2s+1
, then
a_{i}+j
is the vertex
(a_{i}+j)-(2s+1)
on
C_{2s+1}
. Assume, without loss of generality, that
1=a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\ldots\leq a_{s}\leq 2s+1
. By Pigeonhole Principle, there is
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,2s\}
such that
k\not\equiv a_{i}-1-i\pmod{2s+1}
and
k\not\equiv a_{i}+s-i\pmod{2s+1}
for all
1\leq i\leq s
. We have the following claim.

Claim 1

(1) If
k>a_{i}-1-i
, then
k>a_{j}-1-j
for all
1\leq j\leq i
.

(2) If
k>a_{i}+s-i
, then
k>a_{j}+s-j
for all
1\leq j\leq i
.

(3) If
k<a_{i}-1-i
, then
k<a_{j}-1-j
for all
i\leq j\leq s
.

(4) If
k<a_{i}+s-i
, then
k<a_{j}+s-j
for all
i\leq j\leq s
.

Proof of Claim 1
We just prove (1) and the other results can be proved by the same way.

Since
k>a_{i}-1-i
and
a_{i}\geq a_{i-1}
, we have
k>a_{i-1}-1-i
which implies
k\geq a_{i-1}-1-(i-1)
. Note that
k\not\equiv a_{i}-1-i\pmod{2s+1}
for all
1\leq i\leq s
. Thus we have
k>a_{i-1}-1-(i-1)
. By repeating this process, we have (1) holds.

Now we complete the proof by considering the following two cases.

Case 1

k<a_{1}+s-1
.

Since
a_{1}=1
, we have
k<s
. By Claim 1(4), we have
k<a_{j}+s-j
for all
1\leq j\leq s
. Let
r=\max\{i~|~k>a_{i}-1-i\}
. Then
r\geq 1
. By Claim 1(3), we have
k<a_{i}-1-i
for all
r+1\leq i\leq s
if
r\leq s-1
.

When
1\leq i\leq r
, we have
a_{i}-1<k+i<a_{i}+s
which implies
k+i\in V_{i}
.

When
r+1\leq i\leq s
, we have
k+i-s<a_{i}+s-(2s+1)
from
k<a_{i}-1-i
. We will prove that
a_{i}-1-(2s+1)<k+i-s
by induction.

When
i=s
, we have
a_{s}-1-(2s+1)<k
from
a_{s}\leq 2s+1
and
k\geq 0
. Suppose
a_{i}-1-(2s+1)<k+i-s
. That is
a_{i}+s-i\leq k+(2s+1)
. Since
k\not\equiv a_{i}+s-i\pmod{2s+1}
for all
1\leq i\leq s
, we have
a_{i}+s-i<k+(2s+1)
. Since
a_{i-1}\leq a_{i}
, we have
a_{i-1}+s-i<k+(2s+1)
, that is,
a_{i-1}-1-(2s+1)<k+(i-1)-s
.

Now we have
a_{i}-1-(2s+1)<k+i-s<a_{i}+s-(2s+1)
for all
r+1\leq i\leq s
. Then
k+i-s
is a vertex in
V_{i}
for all
r+1\leq i\leq s
. Thus

\{k+i-s~|~r+1\leq i\leq s\}\cup\{k+i~|~1\leq i\leq r\}

consists of
s
successive vertices on the cycle
C_{2s+1}
.

Case 2

k>a_{1}+s-1
.

In this case,
k>s
. Let
r=\max\{i~|~k>a_{i}+s-i\}
. Then
r\geq 1
and
k<a_{i}+s-i
for all
r+1\leq i\leq s
if
r\leq s-1
. By Claim 1(2),
k>a_{i}+s-i
for all
1\leq i\leq r
. Since
a_{s}\leq 2s+1
and
k>s
, we have
k>a_{s}-1-s
. By Claim 1(1),
k>a_{i}-1-i
for all
1\leq i\leq s
.

When
r+1\leq i\leq s
, we have
a_{i}-1<k+i<a_{i}+s
which implies
k+i\in V_{i}
.

When
1\leq i\leq r
, we have
k>a_{i}+s-i
which implies
k+s+i>a_{i}-1+(2s+1)
. We will show that
k+s+i<a_{i}+s+(2s+1)
by induction.

When
i=1
, we have
k+s+1<a_{1}+s+(2s+1)
by
k<2s+1
. Suppose
k+s+i<a_{i}+s+(2s+1)
. Then
k-(2s+1)\leq a_{i}-1-i
. Since
k\not\equiv a_{i}-1-i\pmod{2s+1}
for all
1\leq i\leq s
,
k-(2s+1)<a_{i}-1-i
. Since
a_{i}\leq a_{i+1}
,
k-(2s+1)<a_{i+1}-1-i
, that is,
k+s+(i+1)<a_{i+1}+s+(2s+1)
.

Now we have
a_{i}-1+(2s+1)<k+s+i<a_{i}+s+(2s+1)
for all
1\leq i\leq r
. Then
k+s+i
is a vertex in
V_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq r
. Thus

\{k+i~|~r+1\leq i\leq s\}\cup\{k+s+i~|~1\leq i\leq r\}

consists of
s
successive vertices on the cycle
C_{2s+1}
.

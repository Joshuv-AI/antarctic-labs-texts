Abstract
. In this paper, we study a class of first-order mean-field games (MFGs) that model price formation. Using Poincaré Lemma, we eliminate one of the equations and obtain a variational problem for a single function. This variational problem offers an alternative approach for the numerical solution of the original MFGs system. We show a correspondence between solutions of the MFGs system and the variational problem. Moreover, we address the existence of solutions for the variational problem using the direct method in the calculus of variations. We end the paper with numerical results for a linear-quadratic model.



1.
Introduction

Here, we consider the numerical solution of the first-order mean-field games (MFGs) system introduced in
[
31
]
to model price formation. The solution to this system determines the price
\varpi
of a commodity with supply
Q
when a large group of rational agents trades that commodity. The original price problem reads as follows:

Problem
1.

Suppose that
m_{0}\in\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}})
,
H\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, and
Q
,
V
, and
u_{T}
are continuous. Assume further that
H
is uniformly convex. Find
u,m:[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and
\varpi:[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying
m\geqslant 0
,

(1.1)

\begin{cases}-u_{t}+H(\varpi+u_{x})+V(x)=0&[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}},\\
m_{t}-\left(H^{\prime}(\varpi+u_{x})m\right)_{x}=0&[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}},\\
-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}H^{\prime}(\varpi+u_{x})m{\rm d}x=Q(t)&[0,T],\end{cases}

and

(1.2)

\begin{cases}m(0,x)=m_{0}(x)&\\
u(T,x)=u_{T}(x)&\end{cases}x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

The existence of solutions
(u,m,\varpi)
to the previous problem was proved in
[
31
]
. The first equation is solved in the viscosity sense by the value function of a typical player
u\in C([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}})
. The second equation is solved in the distributional sense by the probability distribution of the agents,
m\in C([0,T],{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}))
. The price
\varpi
is a continuous function on
[0,T]
.

Price formation models offer a load-adaptive pricing strategy relevant in energy markets. For instance,
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
modeled intraday electricity markets, obtaining a price from the solution of forward-backward equations. In
[
24
]
and
[
28
]
authors studied the effects of a major player in the market. The latest paper considered
N
-agent setting. A deterministic
N
-agent price model was studied in
[
8
]
. A MFG model of homogeneous agents for the electricity markets was considered in
[
25
]
.
In
[
4
]
, the price equilibrium is obtained for a finite number of agents who optimally control their production and trading rates in order to satisfy a demand subjected to common noise. Stackelberg games for price formation under revenue optimization were proposed in
[
13
]
and
[
40
]
, and Cournot models in
[
21
]
. A MFG of optimal switching was presented in
[
5
]
to model the transition to renewable energies. Other works incorporating market-clearing conditions are
[
41
]
and
[
27
]
, the former specialized to Solar Renewable Energy Certificate Markets and the latter in exchange markets. The stochastic supply case was studied in
[
29
]
, where authors obtained a price from a Lagrange multiplier rule for the balance constraint.

The standard MFG system exhibits a coupling of two partial differential equations with initial and terminal conditions (see for example
[
16
]
). Several numerical methods have been proposed to solve these MFG systems. Finite differences schemes and Newton-based methods were introduced in
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
. A recent survey can be found in
[
3
]
. Optimization methods and Fourier series approximations were proposed in
[
35
]
. Machine learning methods have been studied in
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
39
]
, and
[
34
]
. However, the MFG system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) not only couples a forward equation for
m
with a backward equation for
u
but also determines the coupling term
\varpi
through an integral constraint, which is the third equation in (
1.1
). Therefore, the numerical approximation of the solution
(u,m,\varpi)
of Problem
1
is challenging, and the main application of our methods is a novel numerical scheme for Problem
1
.

The word Potential in MFGs is used in two unrelated contexts. Potential MFGs (
[
33
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
36
]
) are MFG systems given by the first-order optimality conditions of a minimization problem. Previously, standard optimization techniques were used for its numerical solution (
[
14
]
). In contrast, our potential approach relies on the structure of the continuity equation and Poincaré lemma (
[
19
]
, Theorem 1.22). We introduce a potential functional that integrates the transport equation in (
1.1
).

Poincaré lemma was used for the continuity equation in
[
12
]
for the MFG planning problem. The authors obtained a variational problem for a potential function by eliminating one of the equations in the MFG system. Moreover, the solution
(u,m)
of the planning MFG can be recovered using only the solution of the variational problem. The structure of the MFG planning problem differs from that in Problem
1
in two critical aspects: the initial-terminal conditions and the way the constraint couples the equations.

In Section
2
, we use the existence result for Problem
1
provided in
[
31
]
to formally obtain a potential function,
\varphi
. We show that (
1.1
) corresponds to the Euler-Lagrange equation of a constrained variational problem depending on
\varphi
. To introduce this problem, let
F
be the Legendre transform of
H
; that is,

(1.3)

F(y)=\sup_{p\in{\mathbb{R}}}\left[py-H(p)\right],\quad y\in{\mathbb{R}},

and let
L:{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{0}^{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{0}^{+}
be given by

(1.4)

L(z,y)=\begin{cases}F\left(\frac{z}{y}\right)y,&(z,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+},\\
+\infty,&z\neq 0,\,y=0,\\
0,&z=0,\,y=0.\end{cases}

The constrained variational problem is

Problem
2.

Suppose that
m_{0}\in\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}})
,
H
is uniformly convex, and
Q
,
V
, and
u_{T}
are continuous. Find
\varphi:[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
that minimizes the functional

\varphi\mapsto\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}L(\varphi_{t},\varphi_{x})-V(x)\varphi_{x}-u^{\prime}_{T}(x)\varphi_{t}~{\rm d}x{\rm d}t,

over the set of functions such that
\varphi_{x}(t,\cdot)
is a probability density on
{\mathbb{R}}
for
t\in[0,T]
,
\varphi_{x}(0,\cdot)=m_{0}(\cdot)
, and satisfying

(1.5)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi(t,x)-M_{0}(x){\rm d}x=-\int_{0}^{t}Q(s){\rm d}s,\quad t\in[0,T].

We work under assumptions similar to those in
[
31
]
used to prove the existence and uniqueness of solutions to Problem
1
. The precise statement of our assumptions is presented in Section
3
. We rigorously study Problem
2
in Section
4
, where we show that its formulation is independent of the solution
(u,m,\varpi)
of Problem
1
, and relies only on problem data. In Section
5
, we obtain the existence of a price
\varpi
in (
1.1
) as a Lagrange multiplier, and we establish the following connection between solutions of Problems
1
and
2
:

Theorem
1.1.

Suppose that
\varphi\in C^{2}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}})
solves Problem
2
. Then, the solution
(u,m,\varpi)
of Problem
1
admits the representation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u(t,x)=u_{T}(x)-\int_{t}^{T}H\left(F^{\prime}\left(\frac{\varphi_{t}(s,x)}{\varphi_{x}(s,x)}\right)\right){\rm d}s-(T-t)V(x),\quad&(t,x)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}\\
m(t,x)=\varphi_{x}(t,x),\quad&(t,x)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}\\
\varpi(t)=w_{T}-\int_{t}^{T}w(s){\rm d}s,\quad&t\in[0,T],\end{cases}

where
w_{T}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}(L^{*}_{z}(T,y)-u^{\prime}_{T}(y))\varphi_{x}(T,y){\rm d}y
,

w(s)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\bigg(\left(L^{*}_{z}(s,y)\right)_{t}+\left(L^{*}_{y}(s,y)-V(y)\right)_{x}\bigg)\varphi_{x}(s,y){\rm d}y,\quad s\in[0,T],

and
L^{*}_{z}
and
L^{*}_{y}
are defined in (
5.10
) and (
5.11
), respectively.

Because Problem
2
is a convex minimization problem, we approximate its solution
\varphi
by using standard optimization methods. Furthermore, using the approximations for
\varphi
and Theorem
1.1
, we obtain efficient approximation methods for the solution to Problem
1
. In Section
6
, we illustrate the implementation of our approach for the linear-quadratic setting, for which explicit formulas are provided in
[
31
]
that can be used as benchmarks. For all these benchmarks, our numerical method provides accurate approximations.

2.
Derivation of the variational problem

In this section, we present a formal derivation of the variational problem for the potential function using the solution of the MFGs system. The precise assumptions we work with are stated in Section
3
. The rigorous statement of the variational problem is given in Section
4
, where we no longer rely on the solution of the MFGs system.

Let
(u,m,\varpi)
solve Problem
1
with
m>0
.
Then, the second equation in (
1.1
) can be
written as

\text{div}_{(t,x)}\left(m,-H^{\prime}(\varpi+u_{x})m\right)=0,\quad[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}.

The previous equation combined with Poincaré lemma (see
[
19
]
, Theorem 1.22) gives the existence of a function (the potential)
\varphi:[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that

(2.1)

\begin{cases}m=\varphi_{x},\\
H^{\prime}(\varpi+u_{x})m=\varphi_{t}.\end{cases}

Because
H
is uniformly convex,
H^{\prime}
is strictly monotone. Therefore, by (
1.3
), we have

(2.2)

F^{\prime}(y)=\left(H^{\prime}\right)^{-1}(y).

Hence, from the second equation in (
2.1
), we deduce that

u_{x}=F^{\prime}\left(\frac{\varphi_{t}}{\varphi_{x}}\right)-\varpi.

If
V\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, and
u
is twice differentiable, we differentiate the Hamilton-Jacobi equation in (
1.1
) with respect to
x
to obtain

-(u_{x})_{t}+\left(H(\varpi+u_{x})\right)_{x}+V^{\prime}=0.

Thus, the system (
1.1
) in terms of
\varphi
is reduced to the following two equations

(2.3)

\begin{cases}-\left(F^{\prime}\left(\frac{\varphi_{t}}{\varphi_{x}}\right)-\varpi\right)_{t}+\left(H\left(F^{\prime}\left(\frac{\varphi_{t}}{\varphi_{x}}\right)\right)\right)_{x}+V^{\prime}=0&[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}},\\
-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi_{t}+Q\varphi_{x}~{\rm d}x=0&t\in[0,T],\end{cases}

with initial condition,
\varphi(0,x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}m_{0}(y){\rm d}y\,\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and terminal condition

(2.4)

F^{\prime}\left(\frac{\varphi_{t}(T,x)}{\varphi_{x}(T,x)}\right)-\varpi(T)=u^{\prime}_{T}(x)\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Remark
2.1.

Notice that the initial condition
implies that
\varphi_{x}(0,x)=m_{0}(x)
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, which is the first equation in (
1.2
). Moreover, we have the following explicit formula for
\varphi
in terms of the solution
(u,m,\varpi)
of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
)

(2.5)

\varphi(t,x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}m_{0}(y){\rm d}y+\int_{0}^{t}H^{\prime}(\varpi(s)+u_{x}(s,x))m(s,x){\rm d}s,\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}.

Therefore, the potential function
\varphi
, which in principle has a closed formula arising from the solution of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), can be characterized using the initial condition with
m_{0}
, (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), which depend only, up to
\varpi
, on problem data.

Remark
2.2.

Notice that the first equation in (
2.3
) shows that the expression

-\left(F^{\prime}\left(\frac{\varphi_{t}}{\varphi_{x}}\right)\right)_{t}+\left(H\left(F^{\prime}\left(\frac{\varphi_{t}}{\varphi_{x}}\right)\right)\right)_{x}+V^{\prime}

is independent of
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, so it is a function of time only and equal to
\dot{\varpi}
. Similarly, (
2.4
) shows that

F^{\prime}\left(\frac{\varphi_{t}(T,x)}{\varphi_{x}(T,x)}\right)-u^{\prime}_{T}(x)

is independent of
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and equal to the constant
\varpi(T)
. Because any numerical method to compute
\varphi
provides an approximation of the value
\varphi(t,x)
, we can not expect the numerical approximation to be independent of
x
in (
2.3
) and (
2.4
). Therefore, we can not rely on these formulas to recover
\varpi
using an approximation of
\varphi
. In Section
5
, we provide a formula approximating
\varpi
that averages the dependence on
x
, and thus, can be implemented with any approximation of the potential.

Next, consider the functional

(2.6)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}L(\varphi_{t},\varphi_{x})-V\varphi_{x}{\rm d}x{\rm d}t-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}u^{\prime}_{T}(x)(\varphi(T,x)-\varphi(0,x))~{\rm d}x\end{split}

subject to
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}-(\varphi_{t}+Q\varphi_{x}){\rm d}x=0
on
[0,T]
, and with initial condition
\varphi(0,x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}m_{0}(y){\rm d}y
. Using the augmented functional associated with the constraint
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}-(\varphi_{t}+Q\varphi_{x}){\rm d}x=0
, we show that (
2.3
) is an Euler-Lagrange equation. Thus, we introduce a Lagrange multiplier
\varpi:[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}
for the integral constraint, and we define

(2.7)

\begin{split}\tilde{I}[\varphi,\varpi]:=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}L(\varphi_{t},\varphi_{x})-\varpi\left(\varphi_{t}+Q\varphi_{x}~\right)-V\varphi_{x}-u^{\prime}_{T}(x)\varphi_{t}~{\rm d}x{\rm d}t\end{split}

with initial condition
\varphi(0,x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}m_{0}(y){\rm d}y
. By considering critical points
(\varphi,\varpi)
of the previous functional, we obtain that (
2.3
) is the corresponding Euler-Lagrange equation, with the natural boundary condition (
2.4
).

Proposition
2.3.

Let
(\varphi,\varpi)
be a critical point of the functional (
2.7
) over
C^{2}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}})\times C^{1}([0,T])
satisfying
\varphi(0,x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}m_{0}(y){\rm d}y
. Assume further that
\varphi_{x}>0
. Then, the corresponding Euler-Lagrange equation is equivalent to (
2.3
)-(
2.4
).

Proof.

Let
(\varphi,\varpi)
be a critical point of (
2.7
). Taking
(\beta^{1},\beta^{2})\in C^{1}_{c}((0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}})\times C([0,T])
, we have

(2.8)

\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\tilde{I}[(\varphi,\varpi)+\varepsilon(\beta^{1},\beta^{2})]\right|_{\varepsilon=0}=0.

The previous identity implies that

(2.9)

-\left(L_{z}(\varphi_{t},\varphi_{x})-\varpi\right)_{t}-\left(L_{y}(\varphi_{t},\varphi_{x})\right)_{x}+V^{\prime}=0,

and

(2.10)

L_{z}(\varphi_{t}(T,x),\varphi_{x}(T,x))-\varpi(T)-u^{\prime}_{T}(x)=0

on
[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}
. Because
\varphi_{x}>0
, (
1.4
) gives

(2.11)

\begin{split}&L_{z}(\varphi_{t},\varphi_{x})=F^{\prime}\left(\frac{\varphi_{t}}{\varphi_{x}}\right),\\
&\left(L_{y}(\varphi_{t},\varphi_{x})\right)_{x}=\left(-\frac{\varphi_{t}}{\varphi_{x}}F^{\prime}\left(\frac{\varphi_{t}}{\varphi_{x}}\right)+F\left(\frac{\varphi_{t}}{\varphi_{x}}\right)\right)_{x}=-\frac{\varphi_{t}}{\varphi_{x}}\left(F^{\prime}\left(\frac{\varphi_{t}}{\varphi_{x}}\right)\right)_{x}.\end{split}

Notice that, by (
2.2
), we have

(2.12)

\left(H\left(F^{\prime}\left(\frac{\varphi_{t}}{\varphi_{x}}\right)\right)\right)_{x}=\frac{\varphi_{t}}{\varphi_{x}}\left(F^{\prime}\left(\frac{\varphi_{t}}{\varphi_{x}}\right)\right)_{x}.

Combining the identities in (
2.11
) with (
2.12
) and using (
2.9
), we deduce the first equation in (
2.3
). Using the first identity of (
2.11
) in (
2.10
), we obtain (
2.4
).

Finally, taking
\beta^{1}\equiv 0
in (
2.8
), we obtain

\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}-(\varphi_{t}+Q\varphi_{x}){\rm d}x\right)\beta^{2}~{\rm d}t=0,

where
\beta^{2}
is arbitrary. Thus, the continuity of the map
t\mapsto\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}-(\varphi_{t}+Q\varphi_{x}){\rm d}x
implies the second equation in (
2.3
). ∎

In Section
5
, we address the existence of the price
\varpi
as a Lagrange multiplier associated with a minimizer of (
2.6
).

3.
Assumptions

In this section, we state the assumptions to prove the existence of minimizers of the functional (
2.6
). This set of assumptions is similar to the ones introduced in
[
31
]
to guarantee the existence and uniqueness of
(u,m,\varpi)
solving (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).

The following two assumptions require standard growth and convexity properties for
H
.

Assumption
1.

There exist constants,
c>0
and
p>1
, such that the Legendre-Fenchel transform of
H
, the function
F
in (
1.3
), satisfies

F(v)\geqslant c|v|^{p}.

Assumption
2.

For all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the map
p\mapsto H(p)
is
uniformly convex; that is, there exists a constant
\kappa>0
such that
H^{\prime\prime}(p)\geqslant\kappa
for all
p\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Moreover, there exists a positive constant,
C
, such that
|H^{\prime\prime\prime}|\leqslant C
.

For the supply, to simplify, we assume it is a smooth function of time.

Assumption
3.

The supply function,
Q
, is
C^{\infty}([0,T])
.

The following assumption is technical and was used in
[
31
]
to get bounds for the price.

Assumption
4.

The potential
V
, the terminal cost
u_{T}
, the initial density function
m_{0}
are
C^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
functions and
V
,
u_{T}
are globally Lipschitz. Furthermore, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

|V^{\prime\prime}|\leqslant C,\quad|u_{T}^{\prime\prime}|\leqslant C,\quad|m_{0}^{\prime\prime}|\leqslant C.

The following condition guarantees the uniqueness of solutions of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).

Assumption
5.

The potential
V
and the terminal cost
u_{T}
are convex.

Finally, because we are interested in problems where agent’s assets are bounded, we require the following assumption on
m_{0}
. This assumption further simplifies some technical points in the presentation.

Assumption
6.

The initial density function
m_{0}
has compact support; that is, there exists
R_{0}>0
such that
\operatorname{supp}(m_{0})\subset[-R_{0},R_{0}]
.

4.
The variational approach

Here, we examine a variational problem associated with the MFG system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) continuing the formal derivation in Section
2
. This problem is obtained by minimizing the functional (
2.6
) in a suitable class of admissible functions. We study the existence and uniqueness of solutions to this variational problem. In Section
5
, we establish a formula representing the solution to the MFG system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), in terms of the solution to this variational problem.

First, we recall that, under Assumptions
1
-
5
, Theorem 1 in
[
31
]
gives existence and uniqueness of solutions
(u,m,\varpi)
to Problem
1
, where
m\in\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}})\cap C([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}})
. Moreover,
u
is a viscosity solution to the first equation in (
1.1
), Lipschitz continuous and semi-concave in
x
, and
u_{x}
,
u_{xx}
,
m
are bounded. Furthermore, by the results in
[
10
]
,
\varpi
is Lipschitz continuous.

4.1.
Preliminary results for the continuity equation

Before we formulate our variational problem, we prove a general result for the continuity equation (the second equation in (
1.1
)) that motivates the choice of the function spaces. We recall the following result from
[
15
]
about the existence and uniqueness of solutions to the continuity equation. Let
\mu_{0}\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
,
b\in L^{1}([0,T];W_{loc}^{1,1}({\mathbb{R}}))\cap C([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}})
and
b_{x}\in L^{\infty}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}})
. Then, the continuity equation

(4.1)

\begin{cases}\mu_{t}-(b\mu)_{x}=0&[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}},\\
\mu(0,x)=\mu_{0}(x)&x\in{\mathbb{R}}\end{cases}

has a unique solution
\mu\in L^{\infty}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}})
in distributional sense. The existence result follows from Theorem 1.1 in
[
15
]
, which addresses the existence and uniqueness of distributional solutions to the continuity equation (
4.1
) for a vector field
b
satisfying weaker conditions.

Now, we prove that if the initial condition
\mu_{0}
of the continuity equation is compactly supported, the solution
\mu
is also compactly supported.

Proposition
4.1.

Let
\mu_{0}\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
,
b\in L^{1}([0,T];W_{loc}^{1,1}({\mathbb{R}}))\cap C([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}})
and
b_{x}\in L^{\infty}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}})
. Assume further that
\mu_{0}\in C_{c}^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
.
Then, the unique solution to the continuity equation (
4.1
) has compact support; that is,
\mu\in L_{c}^{\infty}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}})
.

Proof.

From the results in
[
15
]
, it follows that there exists a unique,
\mu\in L^{\infty}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}})
solving (
4.1
) in the distributional sense.
Let
b^{\varepsilon}
be a sequence of functions in
C^{\infty}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}})
satisfying:

•

b^{\varepsilon}
is Lipschitz continuous w.r.t.
x
, and its Lipschitz constant satisfies
\mbox{Lip}(b^{\varepsilon})\leqslant\mbox{Lip}(b)
,

•

b^{\varepsilon}\to b
uniformly on every compact set of
[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}
.

We can obtain such sequence
b^{\varepsilon}
by considering the convolution with standard mollifiers in
x
and a partition of unity construction in
t
.
Next, we consider the continuity equation with the vector field
b^{\varepsilon}

(4.2)

\begin{cases}\mu^{\varepsilon}_{t}-(b^{\varepsilon}\mu^{\varepsilon})_{x}=0&[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}},\\
\mu^{\varepsilon}(0,x)=\mu_{0}(x)&x\in{\mathbb{R}}.\end{cases}

Because
b^{\varepsilon},\,b_{x}^{\varepsilon}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}})
, by Theorem 6.3 in
[
38
]
, (
4.2
) has a unique solution
\mu^{\varepsilon}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}})
given by

(4.3)

\mu^{\varepsilon}(t,X^{\varepsilon}(t;y))J(t;y)=\mu_{0}(y),\quad(t,y)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}},

where

(4.4)

J(t;y)=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}b^{\varepsilon}_{x}(s,X^{\varepsilon}(s;y))~{\rm d}s\right),

and
X^{\varepsilon}
solves the following initial value problem

(4.5)

\begin{cases}\dot{X}^{\varepsilon}(t;y)=b^{\varepsilon}(t,X^{\varepsilon}(t;y))&\quad(t,y)\in(0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}},\\
X^{\varepsilon}(0;y)=y&\quad y\in{\mathbb{R}}.\end{cases}

Because
b^{\varepsilon}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}})
, the map
y\mapsto X^{\varepsilon}(t;y)
is a diffeomorphism (see
[
37
]
, Chapter 3). Moreover, because
b^{\varepsilon}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}})
is Lipschitz continuous w.r.t.
x
, we have
|b^{\varepsilon}(t,x)|\leqslant C_{\varepsilon}\left(1+|x|\right)
, where, by the uniform convergence of
b^{\varepsilon}
to
b
on compact sets,

(4.6)

C_{\varepsilon}\leqslant\max\{1+|b(t,0)|,\mbox{Lip}(b)\}.

Applying Grönwall’s inequality to (
4.5
), provides

(4.7)

|X^{\varepsilon}(t;y)|\leqslant\left(|y|+C_{\varepsilon}T\right)\left(1+C_{\varepsilon}Te^{C_{\varepsilon}T}\right).

Because
|y|>R_{0}
implies
\mu_{0}(y)=0
for some
R_{0}>0
, (
4.3
) shows that
\mu^{\varepsilon}
may have non-zero values only for those
y
satisfying
|y|\leqslant R_{0}
, for which (
4.7
) implies

|X^{\varepsilon}(t;y)|\leqslant\left(R_{0}+C_{\varepsilon}T\right)\left(1+C_{\varepsilon}Te^{C_{\varepsilon}T}\right)=:R_{\varepsilon}.

Thus,
\mbox{supp}(\mu^{\varepsilon})\subset[0,T]\times[-R_{\varepsilon},R_{\varepsilon}]
, which, by (
4.6
), provides the existence of
R>0
, depending on
T
and
\mbox{Lip}(b)
, such that
\operatorname{supp}\mu^{\varepsilon}\subset[-R,R]
for every
1\gg\varepsilon>0
.
Furthermore, (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) imply that there exists
C\geqslant 0
, depending on
T
,
\mbox{Lip}(b)
and
\mu_{0}
, such that

||\mu^{\varepsilon}||_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}})}\leqslant C
for all
1\gg\varepsilon>0
.
Hence, by Banach-Alaoglu theorem, there exists
\bar{\mu}\in L^{\infty}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}})
such that

\mu^{\varepsilon}\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}\bar{\mu}\quad\mbox{as}\quad\varepsilon\to 0\quad\text{in}\quad L^{\infty}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}).

Consequently,
\operatorname{supp}\bar{\mu}\subset[-R,R]
as well. On the other hand,
\mu^{\varepsilon}
also solves (
4.2
) in the sense of distributions; that is,

(4.8)

-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mu^{\varepsilon}\left(\phi_{t}-b^{\varepsilon}\phi_{x}\right){\rm d}x{\rm d}t=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mu_{0}\phi~{\rm d}x,

for any
\phi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}})
.

Thus, given
\phi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}})
, we write

(4.9)

\begin{split}&-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\bar{\mu}\left(\phi_{t}-b\phi_{x}\right){\rm d}x{\rm d}t\\
&=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mu^{\varepsilon}\left(\phi_{t}-b^{\varepsilon}\phi_{x}\right)+\left(\mu^{\varepsilon}-\bar{\mu}\right)\left(\phi_{t}-b\phi_{x}\right)+\mu^{\varepsilon}\phi_{x}\left(b-b^{\varepsilon}\right){\rm d}x{\rm d}t.\end{split}

Because
\phi_{t}
,
\phi_{x}
,
b\phi_{x}\in L^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}})
, the second term on the right-hand side of (
4.9
) vanishes as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Furthermore, using the uniform bound for
\mu^{\varepsilon}
, and because
b^{\varepsilon}
converges uniformly to
b
in the compact support of
\phi
, we obtain that the third term on the right-hand side of (
4.9
) also vanishes as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Thus, using (
4.8
), we get

-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\bar{\mu}\left(\phi_{t}-b\phi_{x}\right){\rm d}x{\rm d}t=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mu_{0}\phi~{\rm d}x,

and since
\phi
is arbitrary, we conclude that
\bar{\mu}
is a solution to (
4.1
) in the distributional sense.

To conclude the proof, it is enough to recall that the results in
[
15
]
provide uniqueness for the initial value problem in (
4.1
), in the sense of the distributions.
∎

Applying the previous result to the MFG system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), we obtain the following.

Corollary
4.2.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
-
5
hold. Let
(u,m,\varpi)
be the solution to (
1.1
).
Assume further that Assumption
6
holds with
R_{0}>0
.
Then,
m
is compactly supported; that is, there exists a constant
R_{m}\geqslant R_{0}
, such that
\operatorname{supp}m(t,\cdot)\subseteq[-R_{m},R_{m}]
for
t\in[0,T]
. Moreover,
R_{m}
is bounded by a constant that depends only on the problem data.

Proof.

Let
b(t,x)=H^{\prime}(\varpi(t)+u_{x}(t,x))
denote the vector field of the continuity equation in (
1.1
). By Proposition 8 in
[
31
]
,
|u_{xx}|\leqslant C(T,V,u_{T})
, which implies that
u_{x}
is Lipschitz w.r.t.
x
.
By Assumptions
1
and
2
, for
p>2
,
|H^{\prime\prime}|\leqslant C(F)
, for some
C(F)>0
. Thus,
b(t,\cdot)\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
is Lipschitz continuous in
{\mathbb{R}}
uniformly with respect to
t
. Furthermore, the Lipchitz constant satisfies

\mbox{Lip}(b(t,\cdot))\leqslant C_{0},

where
C_{0}=C_{0}(T,V,F,u_{T})
. Therefore, Proposition
4.1
implies the first part of the result. Moreover, (
4.7
) shows that

(4.10)

R_{m}\leqslant\left(R_{0}+C_{0}T\right)\left(1+C_{0}Te^{C_{0}T}\right),

which concludes the proof.
∎

Remark
4.3.

Consider the potential
\varphi
associated with the solution
(u,m,\varpi)
of the MFG system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), as given by (
2.1
). By Corollary
4.2
, we deduce that the gradient of the potential
\varphi
has compact support; that is,
\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{t}(t,\cdot)),\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{x}(t,\cdot))\subseteq[-R_{m},R_{m}]
for all
t\in[0,T]
. Thus, (
4.10
) shows that, by selecting

R>\left(R_{0}+C_{0}T\right)\left(1+C_{0}Te^{C_{0}T}\right),

we obtain a compact set
[-R,R]
that depends only on problem data, and which contains the support of the gradient of
\varphi
when (
2.5
) holds. Thus, using this compact set, we can formulate our variational problem independently of the solution
(u,m,\varpi)
of the MFGs system (
1.1
) and (
1.2
). Notice that (
2.5
) already suggests a candidate for a minimizer. However, we study the existence of solutions to the variational problem independently of solutions to the MFGs system. Moreover, if uniqueness holds and we have existence for both problems, then (
2.5
) is the unique minimizer.

4.2.
Statement of the variational problem

In this subsection, we present our variational approach rigorously using only problem data. We start with the notations and the definition of admissible functions. Then, we formulate and study the variational problem.

Let
R_{0}
be given by Assumption
6
and let

(4.11)

R>\max\left\{\left(R_{0}+C_{0}T\right)\left(1+C_{0}Te^{C_{0}T}\right),R_{0}+\|Q\|_{L^{1}([0,T])}\right\}.

Notice that, by (
4.10
),
R
is an upper bound for
R_{m}
, as required, according to Remark
4.3
. The additional requirement
R>R_{0}+\|Q\|_{L^{1}([0,T])}
guarantees that the set of admissible functions that we define below is not empty. Set

\Omega_{R}=[0,T]\times[-R,R],\quad\Omega=[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}.

We denote by
\mathcal{M}(\Omega_{R})
(
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
) the set of Radon measures on
\Omega_{R}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
(
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
) and by
BV(\Omega_{R})
(
BV(\Omega)
) the set of functions with bounded variation on
\Omega_{R}
(
\Omega
) (see
[
23
]
,
[
9
]
).

To define the admissible set for our variational problem, we rewrite the balance condition, the second equation in (
2.3
). Recall that
\operatorname{supp}(m_{0})\subset[-R_{0},R_{0}]
and
R>R_{0}
.
Let

(4.12)

M_{0}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}m_{0}(y){\rm d}y=\int_{-R}^{x}m_{0}(y){\rm d}y,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}},

be the cumulative density function of
m_{0}
. Note that after integrating the balance condition over
[0,t]
, and requiring that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi_{x}(t,x){\rm d}x=1
for
t\in[0,T]
(which follows in case that (
2.1
) holds), we get

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi_{t}~{\rm d}x{\rm d}s=-\int_{0}^{t}Q(s){\rm d}s,\quad t\in[0,T].

Therefore, we write the balance condition as

(4.13)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi(t,x)-M_{0}(x)~{\rm d}x=-\int_{0}^{t}Q(s){\rm d}s,\quad t\in[0,T].

Relying on (
4.13
) and taking into account the discussion in Remark
4.3
, for any set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
satisfying
[0,T]\times[-R,R]\subseteq A
, we denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{R}(A)=

\displaystyle\left\{(\varphi-M_{0})\in W^{1,1}(A):~\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{t}(t,\cdot)),\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{x}(t,\cdot))\subseteq(-R,R),~t\in[0,T]\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(A)=

\displaystyle\left\{\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{R}(A):~\varphi_{x}\geqslant 0,~\varphi(0,x)=\int_{-R}^{x}m_{0}(y){\rm d}y,~x\in{\mathbb{R}}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi(t,x)-M_{0}(x)~{\rm d}x=-\int_{0}^{t}Q(s)~{\rm d}s,~\int_{-R}^{R}\varphi_{x}(t,x){\rm d}x=1,~t\in[0,T]\right\},

which are convex sets. Before proceeding, we prove a crucial property of the set
\mathcal{B}(\Omega)
.

Proposition
4.4.

For any function
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega)
, we have, for
t\in[0,T]
,

\varphi(t,x)=\begin{cases}0\quad&x\in(-\infty,-R],\\
\varphi(t,x)\quad&x\in(-R,R),\\
1\quad&x\in[R,+\infty).\end{cases}

Proof.

Because
(\varphi-M_{0})\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)
and
\lim\limits_{x\to+\infty}M_{0}(x)=1
, for each
t\in[0,T]
, there exists a sequence
{x_{k}}
such that
x_{k}\to\infty
and
\lim\limits_{k\to+\infty}\varphi(t,x_{k})=1
. On the other hand, recalling that

\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{t}(t,\cdot)),\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{x}(t,\cdot))\subset(-R,R),

we have that
\varphi
is constant on
\Omega\setminus[0,T]\times(-R,R)
. Consequently,
\varphi(t,x)=1
for
x\in[R,+\infty)
. Similarly, we can prove that
\varphi(t,x)=0
,
x\in(-\infty,-R]
.
∎

Finally, the set of admissible functions for our variational problem is given by

(4.14)

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}(\Omega_{R})=\left\{\varphi\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{R}):~\varphi(t,-R)=0\right\}.\end{split}

As a result of Proposition
4.4
, we obtain the following relation between the admissible set
\mathcal{A}(\Omega_{R})
and the set
\mathcal{B}(\Omega)
.

Corollary
4.5.

For any function
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega)
there exist a function
\tilde{\varphi}\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega_{R})
such that

\varphi\equiv\tilde{\varphi}
in
\Omega_{R}
. The opposite is also true.

Under Assumption
4
, we have

(4.15)

\left|\int_{\Omega_{R}}u^{\prime}_{T}(x)\varphi_{t}~{\rm d}x{\rm d}t\right|\leqslant\mbox{Lip}{(u_{T})}\int_{\Omega_{R}}|\varphi_{t}|~{\rm d}x{\rm d}t,

where
\mbox{Lip}{(u_{T})}
is the Lipschitz constant of
u_{T}
. Relying on the previous inequality, we consider the following variational problem

\begin{split}\inf_{\varphi\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega_{R}}L(\varphi_{t},\varphi_{x})-V\varphi_{x}-u^{\prime}_{T}(x)\varphi_{t}~{\rm d}x{\rm d}t,\end{split}

which, by Corollary
4.5
, coincides with the following (see (
2.6
))

(4.16)

\inf_{\varphi\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega_{R})}I[\varphi],

where

I[\varphi]:=\int_{\Omega_{R}}L(\varphi_{t},\varphi_{x})-V\varphi_{x}-u^{\prime}_{T}(x)\varphi_{t}~{\rm d}x{\rm d}t.

As anticipated in Remark
4.3
, (
4.11
) guarantees that the previous variational problem does not rely on the solution
(u,m,\varpi)
to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) but only on the data of Problem
1
.
Moreover, the infimum in (
4.16
) can be attained by at most one function, as we show next.

Proposition
4.6.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
-
6
hold. Then, at most, one function attains the infimum in (
4.16
).

Proof.

Let
\varphi^{1}
and
\varphi^{2}
attain the infimum in (
4.16
).
By Proposition
4.7
, we denote

\displaystyle\ell=\min\limits_{(\varphi)\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega_{R})}I[\varphi]\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Thus,
I[\varphi^{1}]=I[\varphi^{2}]=\mathcal{\ell}
. Setting
\bar{\varphi}=\frac{1}{2}(\varphi^{1}+\varphi^{2})
, and using the convexity of
L
, we obtain

(4.17)

\ell\leqslant I[\bar{\varphi}]\leqslant\tfrac{1}{2}I[\varphi^{1}]+\tfrac{1}{2}I[\varphi^{2}]=\ell.

Hence,
\bar{\varphi}
is also minimizer of (
4.16
).
Let
\tilde{\varphi}=\frac{\varphi^{1}+\bar{\varphi}}{2}
and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{U}}_{1}=\{(t,x)\in\Omega_{R}:{\varphi}_{x}^{1}>0\},\quad{\mathcal{U}}_{2}=\{(t,x)\in\Omega_{R}:{\varphi}_{x}^{2}>0\},

\displaystyle\tilde{{\mathcal{U}}}=\{(t,x)\in\Omega_{R}:\tilde{\varphi}_{x}>0\}=\bar{{\mathcal{U}}}=\{(t,x)\in\Omega_{R}:\bar{\varphi}_{x}>0\}={\mathcal{U}}_{1}\cup{\mathcal{U}}_{2}.

Arguing as in (
4.17
), we have

(4.18)

\ell\leqslant I[\tilde{\varphi}]\leqslant\tfrac{1}{2}I[\varphi^{1}]+\tfrac{1}{2}I[\bar{\varphi}]=\ell.

This with (
4.17
), yields that

\displaystyle L(\varphi^{1}_{t},\varphi^{1}_{x}),\,L(\varphi^{2}_{t},\varphi^{2}_{x}),\,L(\bar{\varphi}_{t},\bar{\varphi}_{x}),\,L(\tilde{\varphi}_{t},\tilde{\varphi}_{x})<+\infty\kern 5.0pt\hbox{a.e.~in }\Omega_{R}.

Therefore,

(4.19)

\displaystyle\varphi^{1}_{t}=0\kern 5.0pt\text{ a.e.~in }\Omega_{R}\setminus{\mathcal{U}}_{1},

\displaystyle\varphi^{2}_{t}=0\kern 5.0pt\text{ a.e.~in }\Omega_{R}\setminus{\mathcal{U}}_{2},

\displaystyle\bar{\varphi}_{t}=0\kern 5.0pt\text{ a.e.~in }\Omega_{R}\setminus\bar{{\mathcal{U}}}

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}_{t}=0\kern 5.0pt\text{ a.e.~in }\Omega_{R}\setminus\tilde{{\mathcal{U}}}.

Furthermore,
(
4.18
) implies

(4.20)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{R}}\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}L(\varphi^{1}_{t},\varphi^{1}_{x})+&\tfrac{1}{2}L(\bar{\varphi}_{t},\bar{\varphi}_{x})-L(\tilde{\varphi}_{t},\tilde{\varphi}_{x})\Big)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}t=0.\end{split}

The convexity of
L
and (
4.20
) implies

\tfrac{1}{2}L(\varphi^{1}_{t},\varphi^{1}_{x})+\tfrac{1}{2}L(\bar{\varphi}_{t},\bar{\varphi}_{x})-L(\tilde{\varphi}_{t},\tilde{\varphi}_{x})=0,\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega_{R}.

Consequently, the following also holds

(4.21)

\tfrac{1}{2}L(\varphi^{1}_{t},\varphi^{1}_{x})+\tfrac{1}{2}L(\bar{\varphi}_{t},\bar{\varphi}_{x})-L(\tilde{\varphi}_{t},\tilde{\varphi}_{x})=0,\quad\text{a.e. in }\quad{\mathcal{U}}_{1}\cap\bar{{\mathcal{U}}}\cap\tilde{{\mathcal{U}}}.

Because
L
is strictly convex in
{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
and
{\mathcal{U}}_{1}\cap\bar{{\mathcal{U}}}\cap\tilde{{\mathcal{U}}}={\mathcal{U}}_{1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
, we obtain from (
4.21
) that

\begin{cases}\varphi^{1}_{t}=\bar{\varphi}_{t}\\
\varphi^{1}_{x}=\bar{\varphi}_{x}.\end{cases}\quad\text{a.e. in}\quad{\mathcal{U}}_{1}

Hence,

(4.22)

\begin{cases}\varphi^{1}_{t}=\varphi^{2}_{t}\\
\varphi^{1}_{x}=\varphi^{2}_{x}.\end{cases}\quad\text{a.e. in}\quad{\mathcal{U}}_{1}

Taking
\varphi^{2}
instead of
\varphi^{1}
in (
4.18
) and arguing as before, we obtain

(4.23)

\begin{cases}\varphi^{1}_{t}=\varphi^{2}_{t}\\
\varphi^{1}_{x}=\varphi^{2}_{x}.\end{cases}\quad\text{a.e. in}\quad{\mathcal{U}}_{2}

Combing (
4.19
), (
4.22
) and (
4.23
), we conclude that
\varphi^{1}=\varphi^{2}
.
∎

Next, we prove that the infimum in (
4.16
) is bounded.

Proposition
4.7.

Assume that Assumptions
1
-
6
hold.
Then, there exist positive constants,
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, depending only on the problem data such that

(4.24)

-C_{2}\leqslant\inf_{\varphi\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega_{R})}I[\varphi]\leqslant C_{1}.

Furthermore, there exists a positive constant,
C
, depending only on problem data, such that for every minimizing sequence
\{\varphi^{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of the variational problem (
4.16
), we have

(4.25)

\int_{\Omega_{R}}\tfrac{\left|\varphi_{t}^{n}\right|^{p}}{(\varphi^{n}_{x})^{p-1}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}t,\quad\|\varphi^{n}_{t}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{R})}\leqslant C.

Proof.

First, we prove the upper bound in (
4.24
).
Let

\varphi^{0}(t,x)=M_{0}(x-q(t)),

where
M_{0}
is defined by (
4.12
) and
q(t)=\int_{0}^{t}Q(\tau)~d\tau
.
Therefore, since
q(0)=0
, we have

(4.26)

\varphi^{0}(0,x)=\int_{-R}^{x}m_{0}(y){\rm d}y,\quad\varphi^{0}_{x}=m_{0}(x-q(t)),\quad\varphi^{0}_{t}=-m_{0}(x-q(t))Q(t).

Thus,

(4.27)

\varphi^{0}\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega_{R}).

Taking into account (
4.26
), we have

(4.28)

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathcal{A}(\Omega)}I[\varphi]\leqslant I[\varphi^{0}]

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\Omega_{R}}L(\varphi^{0}_{t},\varphi^{0}_{x})+||V||_{L^{\infty}([-R,R])}\varphi_{x}^{0}+||u_{T}^{\prime}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{R})}|\varphi_{t}^{0}|~{\rm d}x{\rm d}t=:C_{1}.

Next, relying on this bound, we prove (
4.25
), implying the lower bound in (
4.24
).
By Assumption
1
and
2.2
,
L\geqslant 0
. Thus, for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega_{R})
, we have

(4.29)

-\int_{\Omega_{R}}V\varphi_{x}+u^{\prime}_{T}\varphi_{t}{\rm d}x{\rm d}t\leqslant I[\varphi].

Recalling that
V
is continuous and taking into account Assumption
4
by (
4.15
) and (
4.29
), we get

(4.30)

-C(u_{T},R,V)-\mbox{Lip}(u_{T})\int_{\Omega_{R}}\left|\varphi_{t}(t,x)\right|{\rm d}x{\rm d}t\leqslant I[\varphi].

From (
4.28
) follows that for any minimizing sequence
\{\varphi^{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, there exists
N
such that
n\geqslant N
implies
I[\varphi^{n}]\leqslant C_{1}+1
.
Consequently, recalling the definition of
L
in (
1.4
), by Assumption
1
and (
4.15
), we deduce that

(4.31)

\begin{split}c\int_{\Omega_{R}}\frac{\left|\varphi_{t}^{n}\right|^{p}}{(\varphi^{n}_{x})^{p-1}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}t\leqslant&C_{1}+1+||V||_{L^{\infty}([-R,R])}\int_{\Omega_{R}}\varphi^{n}_{x}{\rm d}x{\rm d}t+\mbox{Lip}(u_{T})\int_{\Omega_{R}}\left|\varphi^{n}_{t}(t,x)\right|{\rm d}x{\rm d}t\\
\leqslant&C+\mbox{Lip}(u_{T})\int_{\Omega_{R}}\left|\varphi^{n}_{t}(t,x)\right|{\rm d}x{\rm d}t,\end{split}

for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. On the other hand, by Young’s inequality, we have

(4.32)

\int_{\Omega_{R}}\left|\varphi_{t}^{n}\right|{\rm d}x{\rm d}t=\int_{\Omega_{R}}\frac{\left|\varphi_{t}^{n}\right|}{(\varphi^{n}_{x})^{\frac{p-1}{p}}}(\varphi^{n}_{x})^{\frac{p-1}{p}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}t\leqslant\varepsilon\int_{\Omega_{R}}\frac{\left|\varphi_{t}^{n}\right|^{p}}{(\varphi^{n}_{x})^{p-1}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}t+C(\varepsilon)\int_{\Omega_{R}}\varphi_{x}^{n}{\rm d}x{\rm d}t,

where
\varepsilon=\frac{c}{2\mbox{Lip}(u_{T})}
.
Recalling that
\int_{-R}^{R}\varphi_{x}(\cdot,x){\rm d}x=1
, the preceding inequality and (
4.31
) imply (
4.25
).
Finally, (
4.25
) and (
4.30
) yield the lower bound in (
4.24
).
∎

Thus, for all minimizing sequences of the variational problem (
4.16
), we obtain uniform bounds in
W^{1,1}(\Omega_{R})
. Therefore, any minimizing sequence has a weakly convergent sub-sequence in
BV(\Omega_{R})
(
[
23
]
, Chapter 5).
However, it is not guaranteed that the infimum in (
4.16
) is attained in
{\mathcal{A}}(\Omega_{R})
. Therefore, we enlarge the set of admissible functions by relaxing the conditions defining
\mathcal{A}(\Omega_{R})
, as we present in the next section.

4.3.
Relaxed variational problem

Here, we relax the variational problem (
4.16
) to ensure the existence of minimizers in the set of admissible functions.

First, we extend the functional in (
4.16
) to the convex set

BV_{0}^{+}(\Omega_{R})=\{\psi\in BV(\Omega_{R}):\psi_{x}\geqslant 0\}.

For that, let
W:BV_{0}^{+}(\Omega_{R})\to{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{+\infty\}
be given by

W[\varphi]=\begin{cases}\int_{\Omega_{R}}L(\varphi_{t},\varphi_{x})-V\varphi_{x}-u^{\prime}_{T}(x)\varphi_{t}~{\rm d}x{\rm d}t,~\varphi\in W^{1,1}(\Omega_{R})\cap BV_{0}^{+}(\Omega_{R})\\
+\infty,\quad\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

In
BV(\Omega_{R})
we consider the intermediate convergence; that is,
(\varphi_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset BV(\Omega_{R})
converges to
\varphi\in BV(\Omega_{R})
in the intermediate (or strict) sense if

\varphi_{k}\to\varphi\mbox{ in }L^{1}(\Omega_{R})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|D\varphi_{k}\|(\Omega_{R})\to\|D\varphi\|(\Omega_{R}),

where
\|D\varphi\|(\Omega_{R})
is the total variation of the measure
D\varphi
on
\Omega_{R}
(see
[
9
]
). We recall that
W^{1,1}(\Omega_{R})
is dense in
BV(\Omega_{R})
with respect to the intermediate convergence (see Theorem 10.1.2 in
[
11
]
). We aim to define a functional
{\mathcal{W}}
, the sequential lower semicontinuous envelope of
W
w.r.t. intermediate convergence on
BV
(Chapter 3,
[
20
]
); that is

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}[\varphi]=

\displaystyle\sup\left\{G[\varphi]:~G\leqslant W,~G\mbox{ is sequentially lower semicontinuous on }BV(\Omega_{R})\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\left.\mbox{ w.r.t. intermediate convergence}\right\},

which is the greatest functional below

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}[\varphi]=

\displaystyle\inf\Biggl\{\Biggr.\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}I[\varphi^{n}]:\,\{\varphi^{n}\}\subset W^{1,1}(\Omega_{R})\cap BV_{0}^{+}(\Omega_{R}),

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\varphi^{n}\to\varphi\text{ in the sense of the intermediate convergence in }BV(\Omega_{R})\Biggl.\Biggr\}.

Assuming that Assumption

\bar{f}_{N}(z,y)=\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\frac{f_{N}((z,y)t+(w_{1},w_{2}))-f_{N}(w_{1},w_{2})}{t},

+\infty&z\neq 0,y=0,\\

*

Proposition

2

]
.

4

Theorem

Suppose that Assumptions
2
and
4
hold for some

\begin{split}{\mathcal{W}}[\varphi]=\mathcal{J}[\varphi]&=\int_{\Omega_{R}}f_{N}\left(\frac{d(D_{t,x}\varphi)}{d{\mathcal{L}}^{2}}(t,x)\right)~{\rm d}x{\rm d}t-\int_{\Omega_{R}}(V+c(2p-1))\varphi_{x}-\int_{\Omega_{R}}u^{\prime}_{T}(x)\varphi_{t}\\

Proof.

Taking into account (

, (

cp(|v_{1}|+v_{2})\leqslant f_{N}(v_{1},v_{2}),\quad(v_{1},v_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+}_{0}.

Next, relying on Theorem

, we state the relaxed variational problem.
We set

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{0}(\Omega_{R})=

\displaystyle\left\{\varphi\in BV_{0}^{+}(\Omega_{R}):~\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{t}(t,\cdot)),\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{x}(t,\cdot))\subseteq(-R,R)\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(\Omega_{R})=

\displaystyle\left\{\varphi\in\mathcal{K}_{0}(\Omega_{R}):~\varphi(0,x)=\int_{-R}^{x}m_{0}(y){\rm d}y,~\varphi(t,-R)=0,\right.

Theorem
4.11.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
-
5
hold.
Then, there exists
\varphi\in\mathcal{K}(\Omega_{R})
such that

{\mathcal{I}}[\varphi]=\min_{\psi\in\mathcal{K}(\Omega_{R})}{\mathcal{I}}[\psi].

Proof.

We recall that

is dense in

with respect to the intermediate convergence (see Theorem 10.1.2 in
[
11
]
). Accordingly, we can take a minimizing sequence,

, such that

\min_{\psi\in\mathcal{K}(\Omega_{R})}{\mathcal{I}}[\psi]=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{I}}[\varphi^{n}]=\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}I[\varphi^{n}],

where

,
{\mathcal{I}}
are defined by (
4.16
) and (

), respectively.
Note that because

\varphi^{n}_{x}\in{\mathcal{P}}(-R,R)\cap L^{1}(-R,R),

Combining these estimates with the argument in Proposition
4.7
, we deduce that

(4.37)

||\varphi^{n}_{t}||_{L^{1}(\Omega_{R})}\leqslant C.

Consequently, because
\Omega_{R}
is bounded, Prohorov lemma (see Theorem 2.29 in
[
32
]

\nu\in{\mathcal{M}}(\Omega_{R})

\min_{\psi\in\mathcal{K}(\Omega_{R})}{\mathcal{I}}[\psi]=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{I}}[\varphi^{n}]=\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}I[\varphi^{n}]\geqslant{\mathcal{I}}[\varphi]=\min_{\psi\in\mathcal{K}(\Omega_{R})}{\mathcal{I}}[\psi].\qed

Proof.

(5.2)

Computing the left-hand side of the previous identity, we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}x\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{x}(t,x){\rm d}x

\phi=\psi-\varphi
can be selected to be strictly positive or negative in any neighborhood of
(0,T)\times(-R,R)
. Therefore, we can infer the nullity of the functions in both integrals in (
5
) as follows. First, select
\psi
satisfying
\psi(T,\cdot)=\varphi(T,\cdot)
. Then, (
5
) shows that

(5.17)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-R}^{R}\bigg(\left(L^{*}_{z}(t,x)\right)_{t}+\left(L^{*}_{y}(t,x)-V(x)\right)_{x}-w(t)\bigg)\phi(t,x){\rm d}x{\rm d}t\geqslant 0.

The regularity of

(t,x)\mapsto\left(L^{*}_{z}(t,x)\right)_{t}+\left(L^{*}_{y}(t,x)-V(x)\right)_{x}-w(t)

allows the localization of the integral in (

(5.18)

\displaystyle\left(L^{*}_{z}(t,x)\right)_{t}+\left(L^{*}_{y}(t,x)-V(x)\right)_{x}-w(t)=0\quad\mbox{a.e. }(t,x)\in(0,T)\times(-R,R).

Then, (

\displaystyle\int_{-R}^{R}\bigg(L^{*}_{z}(T,x)-u^{\prime}_{T}(x)-w_{T}\bigg)\left(\psi(T,x)-\varphi(T,x)\right){\rm d}x\geqslant 0,

and we proceed as before by localizing the integral using
x\mapsto\phi(T,x)
to conclude that

(5.19)

\displaystyle L^{*}_{z}(T,x)-u^{\prime}_{T}(x)-w_{T}=0\quad\mbox{a.e. }x\in(-R,R).

\dot{\varpi}(t)=w(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\quad\varpi(T)=w_{T}.\qed

7.
Conclusions and further directions

In this paper, we presented a variational approach based on Poincaré Lemma, reducing one variable in the MFG price formation model. We studied the variational approach independently of the MFG problem. We obtained existence for a relaxed formulation using bounded variation functions, and we proved uniqueness of the potential function. We showed that price existence follows a Lagrange multiplier rule associated with the balance constrained, an integral equation for the MFG model depending on a supply function. For the price problem, the variational formulation allows an efficient computation without solving the backward-forward coupled problem with integral constraints. The convexity of the variational approach allows the use of standard optimization tools to solve its discrete formulation. Our numerical method shows promising results and good agreement with the explicit solutions. We consider we can apply a similar approach to the price formation model with common noise, which corresponds to the case of a stochastic supply function. One challenge is the dependence of the variational problem formulation on the supply, requiring the discretization of time, state variables, and the common noise. We plan to investigate this case in future works.

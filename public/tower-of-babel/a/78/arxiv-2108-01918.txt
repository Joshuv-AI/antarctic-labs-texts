Fundamental Theorem of Projective Geometry over Semirings
Ayush Kumar Tewari

Abstract
We state the fundamental theorem of projective geometry for semimodules over semirings, which is facilitated by recent work in the study of bases in semimodules defined over semirings. In the process we explore in detail the linear algebra setup over semirings. We also provide more explicit results to understand the implications of our main theorem on maps between tropical lines in the tropical plane. Along with this we also look at geometrical connections to the rich theory of tropical geometry.



1
Introduction

Maps between projective spaces and their underlying algebraic structures have been a classical area of study in algebraic geometry. This has been immensely enriched with the seminal work of Tits on the theory of buildings and the connections with the study of classical groups. One of the prominent results in this case is the well known fundamental theorem of projective geometry which is a structure theorem and elaborates on the structure of the collinearity preserving maps between projective spaces associated with vector spaces over fields. This in turn also helps in assessing the automorphism group of such spaces and the group of isomorphisms of the underlying field. The theorem has appeared in various forms throughout the literature in the 19th and 20th century, with various attributions attached to the different versions. The first occurrence of some versions of the statement can be dated back to the work of Darboux, Segre, Veblen and Von Staudt
[
VS47
]
,
[
Dar80
]
,
[
Seg90
]
.

Since then considerable work has been done to understand these maps and their outcomes in terms of the associated geometry. Various versions of the statement have been proved for linear systems over algebraic structures other than fields, for e.g. free modules over commutative rings
[
OS69
]
. Our goal in this article is to understand these results in the realm of semirings and semimodules over them. The notion of linear independence and basis elements is a bit nuanced in this setting. The one stark difference in this setting is that two basis of a semimodule over a semiring may not have the same number of elements. We use recent findings about the properties of basis over semimodules
[
Tan14
]
and develop a background to define the notion of semiring semi-linear maps and semiring collineations.

With the help of definitions discussed earlier, we now state a version of Fundamental Theorem of Projective Geometry over semirings as follows,

Theorem 3

(Fundamental Theorem of Projective Geometry over semirings)
.

Let
V
and
V^{\prime}
be free, finitely generated semimodules of equal dimensions
n\geq 3
over linear semirings
R
and
R^{\prime}
respectively. Let
\overline{V}
and
\overline{V^{\prime}}
be the corresponding associated projective spaces. Let
\sigma
be a one-to-one (onto) correspondence of the "points" of
\overline{V}
and "points" of
\overline{V^{\prime}}
which has the following property: whenever three distinct "points"
L_{1},L_{2}
and
L_{3}
are coaxial :
L_{1}\subset L_{2}\oplus L_{3}
, then there images are coaxial:
\sigma L_{1}\subset\sigma L_{2}\oplus\sigma L_{3}
. There exists an isomorphism
\mu
of
R
onto
R^{\prime}
and a semiring semi-linear map
\lambda
of
V
onto
V^{\prime}
(with respect to
\mu
) such that the semiring collineation which
\lambda
induces on
\overline{V}
agrees with
\sigma
on the points of
\overline{V}
. For any
\alpha\neq 0
the map
\lambda(\alpha X)
is semiring semi-linear and induces the same semiring collineation as
\lambda
.

Proof.

We begin the proof similar to the setup used in the classical case in
[
Art11
]
and
[
Put
]
. We divide the proof in various small results,

Lemma 3.0.1
.

Let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{p}\in V
and
\langle v_{i}\rangle=L_{i}
(for all
1\leq i\leq p)\in\overline{V}
. Also, let
v^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,v^{\prime}_{p}\in V^{\prime}
and
\langle v^{\prime}_{i}\rangle=L^{\prime}_{i}
(for all
1\leq i\leq p)\in\overline{V^{\prime}}
. Then

\sigma(\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{p}\rangle)=\langle v^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,v^{\prime}_{p}\rangle

Proof.

The subsemimodule
\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{p}\rangle
, by definition is the minimal subsemimodule of V containing all
\langle v_{i}\rangle=L_{i}
. Since
\sigma
is a map which preserves dimension and is one to one and onto, hence
\sigma(\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{p}\rangle)
is the minimal subsemimodule of
V^{\prime}
containing each
\sigma(\langle v_{i}\rangle)=\langle v^{\prime}_{i}\rangle=L^{\prime}_{i}
. Hence, the claim.
∎

Let
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
be a basis for
V
and we want to construct a basis
\{v^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,v^{\prime}_{n}\}
for
V^{\prime}
. Let
v^{\prime}_{1}\in V^{\prime}
, such that
\sigma(\langle v_{1}\rangle)=\langle v^{\prime}_{1}\rangle.
This choice of
v^{\prime}_{1}
will be our only arbitrary choice; everything else will be determined by it. We now construct
\{v^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,v^{\prime}_{n}\}
.

Lemma 3.0.2
.

For
2\leq i\leq n
, there exists a unique
v^{\prime}_{i}\in V^{\prime}
such that

\sigma(\langle v_{i}\rangle)=\langle v^{\prime}_{i}\rangle\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\sigma(\langle v_{1}+v_{i}\rangle)=\langle v^{\prime}_{1}+v^{\prime}_{i}\rangle

Moreover, the set
\{v^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,v^{\prime}_{n}\}
is a basis for
V^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Pick
w_{i}
s
\in V^{\prime}
, such that
\sigma(\langle v_{i}\rangle)=\langle w_{i}\rangle
. Using Lemma
3.0.1
, we have

\sigma(\langle v_{1}+v_{i}\rangle)\subset\sigma(\langle v_{1},v_{i}\rangle)=\langle v^{\prime}_{1},w_{i}\rangle

Since
\sigma(\langle v_{1}+v_{i}\rangle)\neq\langle w_{i}\rangle
, therefore it follows that there exists a unique
\gamma_{i}\in R^{\prime}
such that

\sigma(\langle v_{1}+v_{i}\rangle)=\langle v^{\prime}_{1}+\gamma_{i}w_{i}\rangle

therefore the desired vector
v^{\prime}_{i}=\gamma_{i}w_{i}
. To deduce that
\{v^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,v^{\prime}_{n}\}
forms a basis, we use Lemma
3.0.1
and the fact that
\sigma
is one to one and onto.
∎

We now try to construct the isomorphism map
\mu:R\longrightarrow R^{\prime}
,

Lemma 3.0.3
.

For
2\leq i\leq n
, there exists a unique set map
\mu:R\longrightarrow R^{\prime}
such that

\sigma(\langle v_{1}+cv_{i}\rangle)=\langle v^{\prime}_{1}+\mu_{i}(c)\>v^{\prime}_{i}\rangle

(
c\in R
)

Proof.

We define
\mu_{i}
as follows, consider
c\in R
. We apply Lemma
3.0.1
to get

\sigma(\langle v_{1}+cv_{i}\rangle)\subset\sigma(\langle v_{1},v_{i}\rangle)=\langle v^{\prime}_{1},v^{\prime}_{i}\rangle

Since
\sigma(\langle v_{1}+cv_{i}\rangle)\neq\langle v^{\prime}_{i}\rangle
, therefore there exists a unique
\mu_{i}(c)\in R^{\prime}
, such that

\sigma(\langle v_{1}+cv_{i}\rangle)=\langle v^{\prime}_{1}+\mu_{i}(c)\>v^{\prime}_{i}\rangle

The uniqueness of
\mu_{i}
follows from the fact that
\mu_{i}(0_{R})=0_{R^{\prime}}
and
\mu_{i}(1_{R})=1_{R^{\prime}}
, where
0_{R}
and
0_{R^{\prime}}
represents the additive identities of the semirings
R
and
R^{\prime}
respectively. Similarly,
1_{R}
and
1_{R^{\prime}}
represents the multiplicative identities of the semirings
R
and
R^{\prime}
respectively.

∎

Lemma 3.0.4
.

For distinct
2\leq i,j\leq n
, we have
\mu_{i}=\mu_{j}
.

Proof.

Consider a non-zero
c\in R
. We have

\langle v_{i}-v_{j}\rangle\subset\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\langle v_{i}-v_{j}\rangle\subset\langle v_{1}+cv_{i},v_{1}+cv_{j}\rangle

Applying Lemma
3.0.1
, we get

\sigma(\langle v_{i}-v_{j}\rangle)\subset\langle v^{\prime}_{i},v^{\prime}_{j}\rangle\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\sigma(\langle v_{i}-v_{j}\rangle)\subset\langle v^{\prime}_{1}+\mu_{i}(c)\>v^{\prime}_{i},v^{\prime}_{1}+\mu_{j}(c)\>v^{\prime}_{j}\rangle

We have

\langle v^{\prime}_{i},v^{\prime}_{j}\rangle\cap\langle v^{\prime}_{1}+\mu_{i}(c)\>v^{\prime}_{i},v^{\prime}_{1}+\mu_{j}(c)\>v^{\prime}_{j}\rangle=\langle\mu_{i}(c)\>v^{\prime}_{i}-\mu_{j}(c)\>v^{\prime}_{j}\rangle

Therefore,

\sigma(\langle v_{i}-v_{j}\rangle)=\langle\mu_{i}(c)v^{\prime}_{i}-\mu_{j}(c)v^{\prime}_{j}\rangle

since the left hand side is independent of
c
in this equation, therefore the right hand side should also be independent of
c
. Hence,

\langle v^{\prime}_{i}-v^{\prime}_{j}\rangle=\langle\mu_{i}(1_{R})v^{\prime}_{i}-\mu_{j}(1_{R})v^{\prime}_{j}\rangle=\langle\mu_{i}(c)v^{\prime}_{i}-\mu_{j}(c)v^{\prime}_{j}\rangle

Therefore,

\mu_{i}(c)=\mu_{j}(c),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall c\in R

∎

Lemma 3.0.5
.

For
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}\in V
, we have

\sigma(\langle v_{1}+c_{2}v_{2}+\ldots+c_{n}v_{n}\rangle)=\langle v^{\prime}_{1}+\mu(c_{2})\>v^{\prime}_{2}+\ldots+\mu(c_{n})\>v^{\prime}_{n}\rangle

Proof.

We assume,

\sigma(\langle v_{1}+\ldots+c_{p}v_{p}\rangle)=\langle v^{\prime}_{1}+\mu(c_{2})\>v^{\prime}_{2}+\ldots+\mu(c_{p})\>v^{\prime}_{p}\rangle

for all
2\leq p\leq n
, by using induction on
p
. The base case is
p=2
and it holds true as that is the defining property of
\mu
, so we consider
2<p\leq n
. Applying Lemma
3.0.1
and the induction hypothesis we get,

\sigma(\langle v_{1}+\ldots+c_{p}v_{p}\rangle)\subset\sigma(\langle v_{1}+\ldots+c_{p-1}v_{p-1},v_{p}\rangle)=\langle v^{\prime}_{1}+\mu(c_{2})\>v^{\prime}_{2}+\ldots+\mu(c_{p-1})\>v^{\prime}_{p-1},v^{\prime}_{p}\rangle

Moreover,
\sigma(\langle v_{1}+c_{2}v_{2}+\ldots c_{p}v_{p}\rangle)
is not
\langle v^{\prime}_{p}\rangle
, so we deduce that there exists some
d\in R^{\prime}
, such that

\sigma(\langle v_{1}+c_{2}v_{2}+\ldots+c_{p}v_{p}\rangle)=\langle v^{\prime}_{1}+\mu(c_{2})\>v^{\prime}_{2}+\ldots+\mu(c_{p-1})\>v^{\prime}_{p-1}+dv^{\prime}_{p}\rangle

Applying Lemma
3.0.1
and defining property of
\mu
, we see

\sigma(\langle v_{1}+c_{2}v_{2}+\ldots+c_{p}v_{p}\rangle)\subset\sigma(\langle v_{1}+c_{p}v_{p},v_{2},\ldots,v_{p-1}\rangle)=\langle v^{\prime}_{1}+\mu(c_{p})\>v^{\prime}_{p},v^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,v^{\prime}_{p-1}\rangle

Therefore,
d=\mu(c_{p})
.
∎

Lemma 3.0.6
.

For
c_{2},\ldots,c_{n}\in R
, we have

\sigma(\langle c_{2}v_{2}+\ldots+c_{n}v_{n}\rangle)=\langle\mu(c_{2})\>v^{\prime}_{2}+\ldots+\mu(c_{n})\>v^{\prime}_{n}\rangle

Proof.

By Lemma
3.0.1
, we have

\sigma(\langle c_{2}v_{2}+\ldots+c_{n}v_{n}\rangle)\subset\sigma(\langle v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}\rangle)=\langle v^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,v^{\prime}_{n}\rangle

also with Lemma
3.0.1
and Lemma
3.0.5
, we have

\sigma(\langle c_{2}v_{2}+\ldots+c_{n}v_{n}\rangle)\subset\sigma(\langle v_{1},v_{1}+c_{2}v_{2},+\ldots+c_{n}v_{n}\rangle)=\langle v^{\prime}_{1},v^{\prime}_{1}+\mu(c_{2})\>v^{\prime}_{2}+\ldots+\mu(c_{n})\>v^{\prime}_{n}\rangle

and we get,

\sigma(\langle c_{2}v_{2}+\ldots+c_{n}v_{n}\rangle)=\langle\mu(c_{2})\>v^{\prime}_{2}+\ldots+\mu(c_{n})\>v^{\prime}_{n}\rangle

∎

We now show that the map
\mu
is an isomorphism of semirings.

Lemma 3.0.7
.

For
c,d\in R
we have
\mu(c+d)=\mu(c)+\mu(d)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.0.5
, we have

\sigma(\langle v_{1}+(c+d)v_{2}+v_{3}\rangle)=\langle v^{\prime}_{1}+\mu(c+d)\>v^{\prime}_{2}+v^{\prime}_{3}\rangle

By combining Lemma
3.0.1
with Lemma
3.0.5
and Lemma
3.0.6
, we have

\sigma(\langle v_{1}+(c+d)v_{2}+v_{3}\rangle)\subset\sigma(\langle v_{1}+cv_{2},dv_{2}+v_{3}\rangle)=\langle v^{\prime}_{1}+\mu(c)\>v^{\prime}_{2},\mu(d)\>v^{\prime}_{2}+v^{\prime}_{3}\rangle

combining these equations we get

\langle v^{\prime}_{1}+\mu(c+d)v^{\prime}_{2}+v^{\prime}_{3}\rangle\subset\langle v^{\prime}_{1}+\mu(c)\>v^{\prime}_{2},\mu(d)\>v^{\prime}_{2}+v^{\prime}_{3}\rangle

this can only hold true if

\mu(c+d)=\mu(c)+\mu(d)

∎

Lemma 3.0.8
.

For
c,d\in R
, we have

\mu(cd)=\mu(c)\cdot\mu(d)

Proof.

By Lemma
3.0.5
, we have

\sigma(\langle v_{1}+cdv_{2}+cv_{3}\rangle)=\langle v^{\prime}_{1}+\mu(cd)\>v^{\prime}_{2}+\mu(c)\>v^{\prime}_{3}\rangle

combining Lemma
3.0.1
and Lemma
3.0.6
, we get

\sigma(\langle v_{1}+cdv_{2}+cv_{3}\rangle)\subset\sigma(\langle v_{1},dv_{2}+v_{3}\rangle)=\langle v^{\prime}_{1},\mu(d)\>v^{\prime}_{2}+v^{\prime}_{3}\rangle

combining these two equations we get

\langle v^{\prime}_{1}+\mu(cd)\>v^{\prime}_{2}+\mu(c)\>v^{\prime}_{3}\rangle\subset\langle v^{\prime}_{1},\mu(d)\>v^{\prime}_{2}+v^{\prime}_{3}\rangle

the only way the this holds is iff

\mu(cd)=\mu(c)\cdot\mu(d)

∎

Lemma 3.0.9
.

The map
\mu:R\longrightarrow R^{\prime}
is an isomorphism of semirings.

Proof.

We know that
\mu(0_{R})=\mu(0_{R^{\prime}})
and
\mu(1_{R})=\mu(1_{R^{\prime}})
, therefore
\mu
is a one-one onto map. Also, by Lemma
3.0.7
and Lemma
3.0.8
we conclude that
\mu
is a semiring homomorphism. Hence,
\mu
is an isomorphism of semirings.
∎

We now have constructed our basis
\{v^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,v^{\prime}_{n}\}
for
V^{\prime}
and the isomorphism
\mu:R\longrightarrow R^{\prime}
, so we can define semiring semi-linear map
\lambda:V\longrightarrow V^{\prime}
via the formula

\lambda(c_{1}v_{1}+\ldots+c_{n}v_{n})=\mu(c_{1})\>v^{\prime}_{1}+\ldots+\mu(c_{n})\>v^{\prime}_{n}\hskip 10.00002pt(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}\in R)

Lemma 3.0.10
.

The semiring semi-linear map
\lambda:V\longrightarrow V^{\prime}
induces the semiring collineation
\sigma
.

Proof.

Consider a subspace
U
of
V
. We can write
U=\langle u_{1},\ldots,u_{p}\rangle
, where each
u_{i}
is either of the form
v_{1}+c_{2}v_{2}+\ldots+c_{n}v_{n}
or of the from
c_{2}v_{2}+\ldots+c_{n}v_{n}
for some
c_{2},\ldots,c_{n}\in R
. Combining Lemma
3.0.1
, Lemma
3.0.5
and Lemma
3.0.6
, we see that
\sigma(U)=\langle\lambda(u_{1}),\ldots,\lambda(u_{p})\rangle
, as desired.
∎

With all the above ten lemmas we complete the proof of the main theorem.

5
Conclusions

We would like to elaborate on the various connections and possible extensions of our results presented here. As alluded to earlier, restricted to the case of the tropical semiring, study regarding affine buildings has been carried out in
[
JSY07
]
,
[
Wer11
]
. Since, the fundamental theorem of projective geometry is a structure theorem for classical groups, hence it plays an important role in the study of classical groups and associated buildings. Therefore, a natural successor of our results could be a possible definition of classical groups over semimodules and associated buildings.

We recall some basic definitions from
[
Jos22
]
and
[
JSY07
]
. In this case, the study focuses on the case when the underlying field is
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}((t))
, field of Laurent series with complex coefficients. It has a valuation ring which is local, with the residue field isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}
. The vector space
\mathbb{K}^{d}
is a module over the valuation ring and lattices over this ring are considered as submodules generated by
d
linearly independent vectors in
\mathbb{K}^{d}
. Two lattices
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\subset\mathbb{K}^{d}
are called
equivalent
if
c\cdot\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
, for some
c\in K^{x}
. Two equivalence class of lattices
\langle\lambda_{1}\rangle
and
\langle\lambda_{2}\rangle
are called
adjacent
if there are representatives
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
such that
t\cdot\lambda_{2}\subset\lambda_{1}\subset\lambda_{2}
.

The adjacency relation on equivalence classes of lattices in
\mathbb{K}_{d}
defines a graph,
and its
flag simplicial complex
is the
Bruhat–Tits building

\mathbb{B}_{d}(\mathbb{K})
. A point in the tropical projective space
\mathbb{TP}^{d-1}
is a
lattice point
if it is represented by a vector
x
in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. A
tropical lattice polytope
is the tropical convex hull of finitely many lattice points in
\mathbb{TP}^{d-1}
. Similar to the classical case, we can define adjacency between tropical lattice points with respect to the
natural metric
defined in
[
JSY07
]
. This defines a graph whose flag simplicial complex provides a
triangulation
of the affine space
\mathbb{TP}^{d-1}
.

We believe that similar to the case of modules over valuation rings, one can study lattices over semimodules over an appropriately chosen semiring, much in essence to the study which we carried out in this article. This would also help in defining buildings over semimodules, and study of matrix groups over semirings. Further details will be explored elsewhere.

We also would like to point the reader to the recent developments in the theory of
ordered blueprints

[
BL18
]
and possible connections to our work. We recall some basic definitions from
[
BL18
]
. An
ordered semiring
is a semiring
R
with a partial order
\leq
such that
x\leq y
and
z\leq t
implies
x+z\leq y+t
and
xz\leq yt
. An
ordered blueprint
is a triple
B=(B^{.},B^{+},\leq)
where
(B^{+},\leq)
is an ordered semiring and
B^{.}
is a multiplicative subset of
B^{+}
that generates
B^{+}
as a semiring and contains
0
and
1
. An
ordered bluefield
is an ordered blueprint
B
with
B^{.}=B^{\times}\cup\{0\}
. We suggest the reader to refer
[
BL18
]
for further elaborate descriptions of
ordered blue schemes
and the construction of the functor
Proj
, but we emphasize the main takeaway; the notion of a projective space over ordered blueprints, and examples of projective line and projective planes
[
BL18
, Example 4.13]
. This leads us to the following question,

Question 5.0.1
.

Can a version of fundamental theorem of projective geometry be established over matroids in the background of ordered blueprints and the associated projective space ?

We wish to explore this question further and connect with the existing notion of projective geometry over ordered blueprints.

We now discuss one of the most prominent aspects of classical projective geometry related to our results -
cross ratios
. Most of what we recall here is referenced from the book by Jürgen Richter-Gebert
[
RG11
]
. We recall the notation that a point
a\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is denoted as
a=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}\\
a_{2}\\
\end{pmatrix}

and

[a,b]=\text{det}\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}\\
a_{2}&b_{2}\end{pmatrix}

Definition 23
.

Let
a,b,c,d
be four nonzero vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The
cross-ratio

(a,b;c,d)
is the following quantity,

(a,b;c,d):=\frac{[a,c][b,d]}{[a,d][b,c]}

This definition extends naturally to complex numbers and also to arbitrary fields. An important feature of cross ratios is that it is invariant under projective transformations.

Lemma 5.0.2
.

For any real nonzero parameters
\lambda_{a},\lambda_{b},\lambda_{c},\lambda_{d}\in\mathbb{R}
we have

(a,b;c,d)=(\lambda_{a}a,\lambda_{b}b;\lambda_{c}c,\lambda_{d}d)

Lemma 5.0.3
.

Let
M
be a
2\times 2
matrix with nonvanishing determinant and let
a,b,c,d
be four vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then we have

(a,b;c,d)=(M\cdot a,M\cdot b;M\cdot c,M\cdot d)

This invariance can be seen in the following result regarding classical perspectivities in the plane,

Corollary 1

(Corollary 4.1
[
RG11
]
)
.

Let
o
be a point and let
l
and
l^{\prime}
be two lines not passing through
o
. If four points
a,b,c,d
are on
l
and are projected by the viewpoint

to four points
a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime},d^{\prime}
on
l^{\prime}
, then the cross ratios satisfy
(a,b;c,d)=(a^{\prime},b^{\prime};c^{\prime},d^{\prime})
.

We now explore the possibility of a tropical counterpart to the notion of a cross ratio. Firstly, we recall the definition of the
tropical determinant

[
MS09
]
. For a
n\times n
matrix
X=x_{ij}
, with entries from the tropical semiring, the tropical determinant is defined as follows

\begin{split}\text{tdet}(X)&=\bigoplus_{\sigma\in S_{n}}x_{1\sigma(1)}\odot x_{2\sigma(2)}\odot\ldots\odot x_{n\sigma(n)}\\
&=\max_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\ x_{1\sigma(1)}\odot x_{2\sigma(2)}\odot\ldots\odot x_{n\sigma(n)}\end{split}

where
\sigma
represents a cycle in
S_{n}
, the symmetric group on
n
elements. A square matrix
X
is said to be
tropically singular
if
\text{tdet(X)}=\infty
or the tropical polynomial tdet vanishes on
X

[
Jos22
]
.

We fix the following notation,

[a,b]^{tr}=\text{tdet}\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}\\
a_{2}&b_{2}\end{pmatrix}

Let
a,b,c,d
be four nonzero vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We consider the the following quantity,

(a,b;c,d)^{tr}:=([a,c]^{tr}\odot[b,d]^{tr})-([a,d]^{tr}\odot[b,c]^{tr})

We now try to see if
(a,b;c,d)^{tr}
can be a candidate for a
tropical cross-ratio
.
We show invariance of
(a,b;c,d)^{tr}
with respect to tropical scalar multiplication,

Lemma 5.0.4
.

For any real nonzero parameters
\lambda_{a},\lambda_{b},\lambda_{c},\lambda_{d}\in\mathbb{R}
we have

(a,b;c,d)^{tr}=(\lambda_{a}\odot a,\lambda_{b}\odot b;\lambda_{c}\odot c,\lambda_{d}\odot d)^{tr}

Proof.

\begin{split}(\lambda_{a}\odot a,\lambda_{b}\odot b;\lambda_{c}\odot c,\lambda_{d}\odot d)^{tr}&=\left(\text{tdet}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{a}\odot a_{1}&\lambda_{c}\odot c_{1}\\
\lambda_{a}\odot a_{2}&\lambda_{c}\odot c_{2}\end{pmatrix}\odot\text{tdet}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{b}\odot b_{1}&\lambda_{d}\odot d_{1}\\
\lambda_{b}\odot b_{2}&\lambda_{d}\odot d_{2}\end{pmatrix}\right)\\
&-\left(\text{tdet}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{a}\odot a_{1}&\lambda_{d}\odot d_{1}\\
\lambda_{a}\odot a_{2}&\lambda_{d}\odot d_{2}\end{pmatrix}\odot\text{tdet}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{b}\odot b_{1}&\lambda_{c}\odot c_{1}\\
\lambda_{b}\odot b_{2}&\lambda_{c}\odot c_{2}\end{pmatrix}\right)\\
&=\text{max}(\lambda_{a}\odot a_{1}+\lambda_{c}\odot c_{2},\lambda_{a}\odot a_{2}+\lambda_{c}\odot c_{1})+\text{max}(\lambda_{b}\odot b_{1}+\lambda_{d}\odot d_{2},\\
&\lambda_{b}\odot b_{2}+\lambda_{d}\odot d_{1})-(\text{max}(\lambda_{a}\odot a_{1}+\lambda_{d}\odot d_{2},\\
&\lambda_{a}\odot a_{2}+\lambda_{d}\odot d_{1})+\text{max}(\lambda_{b}\odot b_{1}+\lambda_{c}\odot c_{2},\lambda_{b}\odot b_{2}+\lambda_{c}\odot c_{1}))\\
&=\lambda_{a}+\lambda_{c}+\text{max}(a_{1}+c_{2},a_{2}+c_{1})+\lambda_{b}+\lambda_{d}+\text{max}(b_{1}+d_{2},b_{2}+d_{1})-\\
&(\lambda_{a}+\lambda_{d}+\text{max}(a_{1}+d_{2},a_{2}+d_{1})+\lambda_{b}+\lambda_{c}+\text{max}(b_{1}+c_{2},b_{2}+c_{1}))\\
&=(\lambda_{a}+\lambda_{b}+\lambda_{c}+\lambda_{d})+\text{max}(a_{1}+c_{2},a_{2}+c_{1})+\text{max}(b_{1}+d_{2},b_{2}+d_{1})\\
&-(\lambda_{a}+\lambda_{b}+\lambda_{c}+\lambda_{d})-\text{max}(a_{1}+d_{2},a_{2}+d_{1})-\text{max}(b_{1}+c_{2},b_{2}+c_{1}))\\
&=(a,b;c,d)^{tr}\end{split}

∎

However, we are not able to recover all classical results. For example, let us consider the case of Lemma
5.0.3
. We consider a tropically non-singular
2\times 2
matrix
M=\begin{pmatrix}m_{1}&m_{2}\\
m_{3}&m_{4}\end{pmatrix}
. Then for
a=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}\\
a_{2}\\
\end{pmatrix}
and

b_{2}\\
\end{pmatrix}
we get,

M\odot a=\begin{pmatrix}m_{1}\odot a_{1}\oplus m_{2}\odot a_{2}\\
m_{3}\odot a_{1}\oplus m_{4}\odot a_{2}\end{pmatrix}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptM\odot b=\begin{pmatrix}m_{1}\odot b_{1}\oplus m_{2}\odot b_{2}\\
m_{3}\odot b_{1}\oplus m_{4}\odot b_{2}\end{pmatrix}

\begin{split}[M\odot a,M\odot b]^{tr}&=\text{tdet}\begin{pmatrix}m_{1}\odot a_{1}\oplus m_{2}\odot a_{2}&m_{1}\odot b_{1}\oplus m_{2}\odot b_{2}\\
m_{3}\odot a_{1}\oplus m_{4}\odot a_{2}&m_{3}\odot b_{1}\oplus m_{4}\odot b_{2}\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\underline{m_{1}\odot m_{3}\odot a_{1}\odot b_{1}}\oplus m_{1}\odot m_{4}\odot a_{1}\odot b_{2}m_{2}\odot m_{3}a_{2}\odot b_{1}\oplus\underline{m_{2}\odot m_{4}\odot a_{2}\odot b_{2}}\\
&\oplus\underline{m_{1}\odot m_{3}\odot a_{1}\odot b_{1}}\oplus m_{3}\odot m_{2}\odot a_{1}\odot b_{2}\oplus m_{1}\odot m_{4}\odot a_{2}\odot b_{1}\oplus\underline{m_{2}\odot m_{4}\odot a_{2}\odot b_{2}}\\
&=a_{1}\odot b_{2}\odot(m_{1}\odot m_{4}\oplus m_{2}\odot m_{3})\oplus a_{2}\odot b_{1}\odot(m_{1}\odot m_{4}\oplus m_{2}\odot m_{3})\\
&\oplus m_{1}\odot m_{3}\odot a_{1}\odot b_{1}\oplus m_{2}\odot m_{4}\odot a_{2}\odot b_{2}\\
&=(m_{1}\odot m_{4}\oplus m_{2}\odot m_{3})\odot(a_{2}\odot b_{1}\oplus a_{1}\odot b_{2})\oplus m_{1}\odot m_{3}\odot a_{1}\odot b_{1}\oplus m_{2}\odot m_{4}\odot a_{2}\odot b_{2}\\
&=\text{tdet}(M)\cdot[a,b]^{tr}\oplus m_{1}\odot m_{3}\odot a_{1}\odot b_{1}\oplus m_{2}\odot m_{4}\odot a_{2}\odot b_{2}\end{split}

We see that in the tropical case, we have an additional term along with
\text{tdet}(M)\cdot[a,b]^{tr}
, which is due to the fact that there is no additive inverse tropically and the tropical determinant is a tropical sum whereas the classical determinant has a change of sign for terms which results in cancellations. Therefore, for a tropically non-singular
2\times 2
matrix
M
we cannot conclude that

(a,b;c,d)^{tr}=(M\cdot a,M\cdot b;M\cdot c,M\cdot d)^{tr}

We would like to explore other suitable variants of the definition of a tropical cross ratio, in the context of tropical projective geometry, which complement the definition of tropical perpsectivity and remain invariant under projective transformations, which is true in the classical case.

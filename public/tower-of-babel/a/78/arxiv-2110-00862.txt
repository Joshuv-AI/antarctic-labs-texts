On Bruhat intervals of small lengths for Weyl groups
Eugenia Akhmedova

Abstract
The paper considers the Bruhat order on Coxeter groups in general and Weyl groups in particular.We suggest an invariant on the Bruhat order intervals that allows checking whether two Bruhat intervals are isomorphic. This invariant is constructed inductively and allows us to compute short length intervals for Coxeter groups of types A and B. The results are presented in the appendices at the end of the paper.



Keywords: Bruhat interval, Bruhat order, Weyl groups

Introduction

The number of Bruhat intervals in Coxeter groups is finite, and for the first few lengths, the intervals were described up to an isomorphism by A. Hultman in
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
using the correspondence between Bruhat intervals and cell decompositions of a 2d sphere and straightforward computations.
The main purpose of this paper consists of a description of the intervals in higher dimensions, as the Hultman’s geometric method is hard to apply due to rapidly growing with length number of nonisomorphic intervals.
We construct an invariant on subintervals in the Bruhat graphs, using their specific properties. This gives us a method of comparing two Bruhat interval, that is faster than the general algorithm for checking if two graphs are isomorphic. This construction is inductive, and thus, can be easily applied for any interval length and Weyl group.

In section
1
, we recall some basic definitions and properties concerning Coxeter and Weyl groups and the Bruhat order. Subsection
1.5
gives a brief overview of previous results in Bruhat interval classification.
In section
2
, we construct a combinatorial invariant on Bruhat intervals and show how it can be used to classify the intervals.
We sketch the algorithm of the inductive classification of Bruhat intervals of given length based on the invariant we suggest.

1.1
Coxeter groups

Definition 1
.

A Coxeter system is a pair
(W,S)
made up of a group
W
and its generating set
S
such that
\forall s\in S\ s^{2}=e
and all relations in
W
are generated by the relations of the type
(s_{1}s_{2})^{m(s_{1},s_{2})}=e
,
s_{1},s_{2}\in S
.

Definition 2
.

Each element
w\in W
of the Coxeter group can be related to two sets
T_{L}(w)
and
T_{R}(w)
. The sets
T_{L}=\{t\in T\ |\ \ell(tw)<\ell(w)\}
and
T_{R}=\{t\in T\ |\ \ell(wt)<\ell(w)\}
are called the left and right sets of associated reflections. The sets
D_{L}=T_{L}\cap S
and
D_{R}=T_{R}\cap S
are called sets of left and right descents, respectively.

For any
w\in W
there exists an expression
w=s_{1}s_{2}\dots s_{k},\ s_{i}\in S
. The minimal length of such expression is called the length of the element
w
and is denoted by
\ell(v)
. If
s_{1}s_{2}\dots s_{k}
is of minimal length, it is called a reduced word for
w
.

Definition 3
.

A reflection set of the Coxeter system
(W,S)
is the set
T=\{wsw^{-1}|s\in S,w\in W\}
.

Proposition 1
.

Strong exchange property

Suppose
w\in W
,
w=s_{1}s_{2}\dots s_{k}
, and
t\in T
. If
\ell(tw)<\ell(w)
, then
tw=s_{1}s_{2}\dots\hat{s_{i}}\dots s_{k}
, where
\hat{s_{i}}
denotes removing
s_{i}
from the word.

Proposition 2
.

Deletion property

Suppose
w\in W
,
w=s_{1}s_{2}\dots s_{k}
and
\ell(w)<k
. Then there exist
1\leq i<j\leq k
such that
w=s_{1}s_{2}\dots\hat{s_{i}}\dots\hat{s_{j}}\dots s_{k}
.

Theorem 1
.

(proven by Dyer in
[
5
]
)

Suppose
(W,S)
is a Coxeter system,
W^{\prime}
is a subgroup in
W
, generated by a subset of the set of reflections
T
.
Let
S^{\prime}=\{t\in T|\ell(t^{\prime}t)>\ell(t)\ \forall t^{\prime}\in T\}
.
Then
(W^{\prime},S^{\prime})
is a Coxeter system.

Proposition 3
.

The number of nonisomorphic Coxeter systems with
n
generators is finite.

1.2
Bruhat order

Let us now introduce an order on a Coxeter group
W
.

Definition 4
.

Let

\displaystyle(1)\ u\rightarrow w\text{ means }\ell(u)<\ell(v)\text{ and }\exists\ t\in T\text{ means }w=ut,

\displaystyle(2)\ u\lhd w\text{ means }u\rightarrow w\text{ and }\ell(w)=\ell(u)+1,

\displaystyle(3)\ u\leq w\text{ means there exists a chain in }W,

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \text{ such that }u=u_{0}\rightarrow u_{1}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow u_{k-1}\rightarrow u_{k}=w.

Definition 5
.

A Bruhat graph is a graph with vertices labeled by elements of
W
with edges
(u,w)
such that

.

Definition 6
.

A subword of a reduced word
s_{1}s_{2}\dots s_{q}
is a word
s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\dots s_{i_{k}}
such that
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{k}\leq q

Lemma 1

Suppose

,

that satisfies the following conditions:

\displaystyle(1)\ v>u

\displaystyle(2)\ \ell(v)=\ell(u)+1

\displaystyle(3)\ \text{There exists a reduced word for }v,\text{ that is a subbword of }s_{1}s_{2}\dots s_{q}

Corollary 1
.

Subword property

Suppose
w=s_{1}s_{2}\dots s_{q}
is a reduced word. Then
u\leq w
if and only if there exist a reduced word for
u
that is a subword of
s_{1}s_{2}\dots s_{q}
.

Proposition 4
.

If
W
is finite, then there exists an element of maximal length

.

Proposition 5
.

The element of maximal length
w_{0}
is the maximal element with respect to the Bruhat order.

Corollary 2
.

The maps

and

Proposition 6

Chain property

Suppose
u<w

u=x_{0}<x_{1}<\dots<x_{k}=w
such that
\forall i

\ell(x_{i+1})=\ell(u)+i

2
Invariant on Bruhat intervals

In this section, we construct a correspondence between Bruhat intervals and special arrays consisting of graph isomorphisms and natural numbers. Let us first give a short informal description of the proposed construction:

The construction is inductive. Suppose that we have classified Bruhat intervals in
W
of length
n-1
and enumerated the equivalency classes. Then for an interval of length
n
, one can find a number of equivalency classes of its length
n-1
subintervals with the same smallest element as the bigger interval.
From these numbers and some additional information, one can "glue together" representatives of the respective equivalency classes and get an interval, isomorphic to the original. So the invariant for each Bruhat interval
[u,v]
of the length
n
consists of a set of Bruhat intervals of length
n-1
and an instruction how to make an interval, isomorphic to
[u,v]
out of them.
This set of numbers and the additional information that will be described below together make up the suggested invariant.

It turns out that the value of the suggested invariant is the same for all isomorphic Bruhat intervals, which, knowing all length
n-1
intervals up to an isomorhism, allows us to describe length
n
Bruhat intervals up to an isomorhism.

Suppose that for every

Let us introduce three maps

T([u,v])=T([u^{\prime},v^{\prime}])
follows
[u,v_{i}]\cong[u^{\prime},v_{i}^{\prime}]
. Therefore,
\Theta_{i}(u)=\Theta_{i}^{\prime}(u^{\prime})
,
\Theta_{i}(v_{i})=\Theta_{i}^{\prime}(v_{i}^{\prime})
. From

P_{\psi}([u,v])=P_{Id}([u^{\prime},v^{\prime}])
follows
\exists w^{\prime}\lhd v^{\prime}_{i},v^{\prime}_{j}
such that
\varphi_{i}(w)=\Theta_{i}^{\prime}(w^{\prime})
and

\Theta_{j}(w)=\Theta_{j}^{\prime}(w^{\prime})
.

Then from
A_{\psi}([u,v])=A_{Id}([u^{\prime},v^{\prime}])
follows
\tau_{(u,v),w}^{\psi}=\tau_{(u^{\prime},v^{\prime}),w^{\prime}}^{Id}
. Therefore,

\varphi^{-1}_{[\Theta_{i}(u),\Theta_{i}(v_{i})]}\tau_{(u,v),w}^{\psi}\varphi_{[\Theta_{j}(u),\Theta_{j}(v_{i})]}=\varphi^{-1}_{[\Theta_{i}^{\prime}(u^{\prime}),\Theta_{i}^{\prime}(v_{i}^{\prime})]}\tau_{(u^{\prime},v^{\prime}),w^{\prime}}^{Id}\varphi_{[\Theta_{j}^{\prime}(u^{\prime}),\Theta_{j}^{\prime}(v_{i}^{\prime})]},

This means that

\Theta_{i}|_{[u,w]}\circ\Theta_{j}^{-1}|_{\Theta_{j}([u,w])}=\Theta_{i}^{\prime}|_{[u^{\prime},w^{\prime}]}\circ\Theta_{j}^{\prime-1}|_{\Theta_{j}^{\prime}([u^{\prime},w^{\prime}])}
.

By multiplying by
\Theta_{i}|_{[u,w]}^{-1}
on the left and
\Theta_{j}^{\prime}|_{[u^{\prime},w^{\prime}]}
on the right, one can get

\Theta_{j}^{\prime-1}\circ\Theta_{j}|_{[u,w]}=\Theta_{i}^{\prime-1}\circ\Theta_{i}|_{[u,w]}
.
∎

Theorem 4
.

Suppose
[u,v]\cong[u^{\prime},v^{\prime}]
. Then there exists an order of elements in
\{w\in W|u<w\lhd v\}
, and
\{w\in W|u^{\prime}<w\lhd v^{\prime}\}
such that
TPA([u,v])=TPA([u^{\prime},v^{\prime}])
.

Proof.

Suppose that
f:[u,v]\to[u^{\prime},v^{\prime}]
is an isomorphism of Bruhat order and
\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}\}
is an enumeration of elements of
\{w\in W|u<w\lhd v\}
.
Let us consider an enumeration of elements of
\{w\in W|u^{\prime}<w\lhd v^{\prime}\}
such that for all
i
’s
v_{i}^{\prime}=f(v_{i})
. Denote the functions
\varphi_{[u^{\prime},v^{\prime}_{i}]}
by
\varphi_{i}^{\prime}
. Then
[u,v_{i}]\cong[f(u),f(v_{i})]=[u^{\prime},v^{\prime}_{i}]
, and, therefore,
T([u,v])=T([u^{\prime},v^{\prime}])
.

Since
w\lhd v_{i},v_{j}
, we have
f(w)\lhd v_{i}^{\prime},v_{j}^{\prime}
, therefore,
P([u,v])=P([u^{\prime},v^{\prime}])
. Because
f
is a Bruhat order isomorphism, there exists a set of automorphisms
\psi
such that
\varphi_{i}^{\prime}\circ\psi_{i}=\varphi_{i}\circ f^{-1}
. Therefore,
\varphi_{j}^{\prime}\circ\psi_{j}\circ(\varphi_{i}^{\prime}\circ\psi_{i})^{-1}=\varphi_{i}\circ\varphi_{j}^{-1}
, in other notation that is
\tau_{(u,v),w}^{Id}=\tau_{(u^{\prime},v^{\prime}),f(w)}^{\psi}
. Consequently
A([u,v])=A_{\psi}([u^{\prime},v^{\prime}])
. This gives the required equality
TPA([u,v])=TPA([u^{\prime},v^{\prime}])
.
∎

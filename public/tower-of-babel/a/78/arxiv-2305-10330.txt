Continuity in law for solutions of SPDEs with space-time homogeneous Gaussian noise
Raluca M. Balan, Xiao Liang

Abstract
In this article, we study the continuity in law of the solutions of two linear multiplicative SPDEs (the parabolic Anderson model and the hyperbolic Anderson model) with respect to the spatial parameter of the noise. The solution is interpreted in the Skorohod sense, using Malliavin calculus. We consider two cases: (i) the regular noise, whose spatial covariance is given by the Riesz kernel of order α ∈ ( 0 , d ) \alpha\in(0,d) , in spatial dimension d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 ; (ii) the rough noise, which is fractional in space with Hurst index H < 1 / 2 H<1/2 , in spatial dimension d = 1 d=1 . We assume that the noise is colored in time. The similar problem for the white noise in time was considered in [ 7 , 14 ] .

1
Introduction

Stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) are mathematical models for random phenomena which evolve in space and time. The study of SPDEs is a modern and active research field, with difficult and interesting results being discovered every day. Key developments in this area focus on equations driven by space-time homogeneous Gaussian noise, using the random field approach initiated in Walsh’ lecture notes
[
28
]
. Walsh’ theory was developed for equations driven by a Gaussian
space-time white noise. In higher dimensions, the white noise is too rough, so that the solution can exist only in the
sense of distributions. To avoid this problem, Dalang
introduced in
[
10
]
a new type of Gaussian noise that was smoother in space, but still
white in time. Dalang’s seminal article sparked much interest in the scientific community, providing a natural extension of the classical Itô theory, tailored for the study of SPDEs.
Together with Walsh’ lecture notes, this article had created a new school of thought which was embraced by many researchers.

At the same time, in the late 1990’s, a new process began to be used extensively in stochastic analysis as a more attractive model for the temporal structure of the noise than the Brownian motion. This process is the
fractional Brownian motion
(fBm), and is not a semi-martingale. One of the appealing feature of fBm is that it can be embedded into an isonormal Gaussian process for which one can use Malliavin calculus.
Placing the study of SPDEs in the context of Malliavin calculus can be traced back to
[
1
]
, and opened the door for deeper investigations about the probabilistic behaviour of the solutions, such as: Hölder continuity, Feynman-Kac representations, intermittency, existence and smoothness of density, exact asymptotic behavior of the moments, ergodicity, and Gaussian fluctuations for the spatial average. We refer the reader to
[
4
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
12
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
]

for a sample of relevant references.

In the present article, we examine another interesting property of solutions of SPDEs, namely the continuity in law with respect to the noise parameter. In the case equations driven by the white noise in time, the continuity in law of the solutions to the heat and wave equations driven by fBm of index
H\in(0,1)
has been studied in the recent article
[
14
]
. This was a continuation of article
[
13
]
, in which the authors considered the same problem for quasi-linear equations. The general problem of weak continuity with respect to
H
of the local time of fBm, or of various integrals with respect to fBm, was examined in
[
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
. The continuity in law of the solution of the heat equation with white noise in time and spatial covariance given by the Riesz kernel was studied in
[
7
]
.

The purpose of the present article is to extend the results of
[
7
,
14
]
to the colored noise in time, and therefore contribute to the advancement of the rapidly growing knowledge about SPDEs, leading to a deeper understanding of the behaviour of the solutions to these equations.

2
Framework and Main Results

In this article, we consider the stochastic heat equation with linear multiplicative noise:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\Delta u(t,x)+u(t,x)\dot{W}(t,x),\quad t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=1,\end{cases}

(1)

and the stochastic wave equation with linear multiplicative noise:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x)+u(t,x)\dot{W}(t,x),\quad t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=1,\ \dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}(0,x)=0.\end{cases}

(2)

Equations (
1
) and (
2
) are called in the literature the
parabolic Anderson model
, respectively the
hyperbolic Anderson model
.

We assume that
W
is a space-time homogeneous Gaussian noise. More precisely,
W=\{W(\varphi);\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\}
is a zero-mean Gaussian process, defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, with covariance:

\mathbb{E}[W(\varphi)W(\psi)]=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}\varphi(t,\cdot)(\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}\psi(s,\cdot)(\xi)}\gamma_{0}(t-s)dtds\mu(d\xi)=:\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},

(3)

where
\mathcal{F}\varphi(t,\cdot)(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}\varphi(t,x)dx
is the Fourier transform of the function
\varphi(t,\cdot)
,
\xi\cdot x
is the Euclidean product in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\gamma_{0}
is a non-negative and non-negative definite function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

The spatial covariance structure of the noise is specified by the spatial spectral measure
\mu
. In general, this is assumed to be tempered, non-negative and non-negative definite. The scope of the present article is to analyze the influence of
\mu
on the solutions of equations (
1
) and (
2
), using continuity in law. To this end, we will consider two cases:

\mu(d\xi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|\xi|^{-\alpha}d\xi&\mbox{with $\alpha\in(0,d)$ and $d\geq 1$ ({Case I: the regular case})}\\
c_{H}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi&\mbox{with $H\in(0,1/2)$ and $d=1$ ({Case II: the rough case})}\end{array}\right.

In Case II, we assume that the constant
c_{H}
is given by:

c_{H}=\frac{\Gamma(2H+1)\sin(\pi H)}{2\pi}.

With this choice,
W
behaves in space like a
fractional Brownian motion
(fBm) of index
H
, in a sense which will be specified below. Recall that the fBm of index
H\in(0,1)
is a zero-mean Gaussian process
B=(B_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}}
with covariance:

R_{H}(x,y):=\mathbb{E}[B_{x}B_{y}]=\frac{1}{2}(|x|^{2H}+|y|^{2H}-|x-y|^{2H})=c_{H}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}1_{[0,x]}(\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}1_{[0,y]}(\xi)}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi.

If
H>1/2
, we have the following representation: for any
x>0
,
y>0
,

R_{H}(x,y)=\alpha_{H}\int_{0}^{x}\int_{0}^{y}|u-v|^{2H-2}dudv,

where
\alpha_{H}=H(2H-1)
. If
H=1/2
,
B
is a Brownian motion.
The fBm has a modification whose sample paths are Hölder continuous of order less than
H
. These paths are rougher (i.e. less regular), or smoother (i.e. more regular) than the Brownian paths, depending on whether
H<1/2
or
H>1/2
. Case II with parametrization
\alpha=2H-1
and
d=1
corresponds, modulo a constant, to the case when the noise
W
behaves in space like a fBm of index
H>1/2
. This explains our terminology for the two cases.

We mention now few more details about the noise. We denote by
\mathcal{H}
the Hilbert space defined as the completion of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
with respect to the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
. Then
W
can be extended to an isonormal Gaussian process
\{W(\varphi);\varphi\in\mathcal{H}\}
, as defined in Malliavin calculus. We refer the reader to
[
25
]
for more details about Malliavin calculus.

The space
\mathcal{H}
may contain distributions, but it also contains some nice functions. Under certain conditions, it can be proved that
1_{[0,t]\times[0,x]}\in\mathcal{H}
, in which case we can define the random field
\{W(t,x):=W(1_{[0,t]\times[0,x]});t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
. In Case II, for any
t>0
fixed,
\{W(t,x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}
is, modulo a constant, a fBm of index
H
, since

\mathbb{E}[W(t,x)W(t,y)]=C_{t}R_{H}(x,y),\quad\mbox{with $C_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma_{0}(u-v)dudv$}.

The same thing is true in Case I with parametrization
\alpha=1-2H
and
d=1
.

In Case I, the noise and the solution depend on the parameter
\alpha
, and will be denoted by
W^{\alpha},u^{\alpha}
, respectively. The goal of this article is to prove the continuity in law of
u^{\alpha}
with respect to
\alpha
, in the space of continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Similarly, in Case II, the noise and the solution are denoted by
W^{H},u^{H}
, respectively, and we are interested in the continuity in law of
u^{H}
with respect to
H
. In both cases, we will provide constructions which will guarantee that all noise processes are defined on the same probability space, for all parameter values
\alpha
(or
H
).

By the Bochner-Schwartz theorem, there exists a tempered measure
\mu_{0}
on
\mathbb{R}
such that
\gamma_{0}
is the Fourier transform of
\mu_{0}
in the space
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
of tempered distributions on
\mathbb{R}
. Then,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\phi(t)\phi(s)\gamma_{0}(t-s)dtds=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{F}\phi(\tau)|^{2}\mu_{0}(d\tau),

(4)

for any
\phi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R})
, where
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R})
is the space of
\mathbb{C}
-valued rapidly decreasing
C^{\infty}
-functions on
\mathbb{R}
, and
\mathcal{F}\phi
is the Fourier transform of
\phi
, given by
\mathcal{F}\phi(\tau)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\tau t}\phi(t)dt
for all
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
.

To construct all noise processes
(W^{\alpha})_{\alpha\in(0,d)}
, respectively
(W^{H})_{H\in(0,1/2)}
, on the same probability space, we will assume that
there exists a function
g_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty]
such that

\mu_{0}(d\tau)=g_{0}(\tau)d\tau.

(5)

A basic example is:

\gamma_{0}(t)=\alpha_{H_{0}}|t|^{2H_{0}-2}\quad\mbox{for some $H_{0}\in(\frac{1}{2},1)$},

(6)

in which case,
g_{0}(\tau)=c_{H_{0}}|\tau|^{1-2H_{0}}
, with the same constants
\alpha_{H_{0}}
and
c_{H_{0}}
as above. In this case,
W
behaves in time like a fBm of index
H_{0}
, in the sense that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
fixed,

\{W(t,x)\}_{t\geq 0}
is, modulo a constant, a fBm of index
H_{0}
, since

\mathbb{E}[W(t,x)W(s,x)]=C_{x}^{\prime}R_{H_{0}}(t,s)\quad\mbox{with $C_{x}^{\prime}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{F}1_{[0,x]}(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)$}.

In what follows, we will need an extension of relation (
4
) to higher dimensions, namely:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})\phi(\boldsymbol{s_{n}})\prod_{j=1}^{n}\gamma_{0}(t_{j}-s_{j})d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}d\boldsymbol{s_{n}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\mathcal{F}\phi(\boldsymbol{\tau_{n}})|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}g_{0}(\tau_{j})d\boldsymbol{\tau_{n}},

(7)

for any
\phi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
\boldsymbol{t}_{n}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
,
\boldsymbol{s}_{n}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
,
\boldsymbol{\tau_{n}}=(\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n})
, and
\mathcal{F}\phi
is the Fourier transform of
\phi
, given by
\mathcal{F}\phi(\boldsymbol{\tau_{n}})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tau_{j}t_{j}}\phi(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}
for all
\boldsymbol{\tau_{n}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We denote by
G_{t}
the fundamental solution of the heat equation, respectively the wave equation, on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. More precisely, in the case of the heat equation,

G_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi t)^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2t}\right)

In the case of the wave equation, in dimension
d=1
or
d=2
,
G_{t}
is an integrable function:

\displaystyle G_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}1_{\{|x|<t\}}\quad\mbox{if}\ d=1

\displaystyle G_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}1_{\{|x|<t\}}\quad\mbox{if}\ d=2;

in dimension
d=3
,
G_{t}
is a measure given by
G_{t}=(4\pi t)^{-1}\sigma_{t}
where
\sigma_{t}
is the surface measure on the sphere
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3};|x|=t\}
; and in dimension
d\geq 4
,
G_{t}
is a distribution.

The Fourier transform of
G_{t}
plays an important role in this framework. We recall that in any dimension
d
, this is given by:

\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)=e^{-t|\xi|^{2}/2}\quad\mbox{in the case of the heat equation},

respectively

\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)=\frac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\quad\mbox{in the case of the wave equation}

We will analyze simultaneously both equations, but we will provide a special analysis for the case of each equation, when needed.

We say that
u
is a
Skorohod solution
of equation (
1
), respectively (
2
), if
u
is adapted with respect to the filtration
\mathcal{F}_{t}=\sigma\{W(1_{[0,t]}\varphi);\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\},t\geq 0
associated with
W
, and satisfies the integral equation:

u(t,x)=1+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t-s}(x-y)u(s,y)W(\delta s,\delta y),

where the stochastic integral is interpreted in the Skorohod sense, i.e. it is given by the divergence operator
\delta
with respect to
W
. We recall that
\delta
is the adjoint of the Malliavin derivative
D
, and we refer the reader to
[
25
]
for the definitions and basic properties of these operators.

We begin now to review some basic facts about the solution. Using the methodology introduced in
[
18
]
, we know that if it exists, the solutions of equations (
1
) and (
2
) have the series expansion:

u(t,x)=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}I_{n}\big(f_{t,x,n}\big)\quad\mbox{in}\quad L^{2}(\Omega),

(8)

the terms of these series being orthogonal in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Here
I_{n}
is the multiple integral of order
n
with respect to
W
, and
f_{t,x,n}
is given by:

f_{t,x,n}(t_{1},x_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{n})=G_{t-t_{n}}(x-x_{n})\ldots G_{t_{2}-t_{1}}(x_{2}-x_{1})1_{\{0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}<t\}}.

The multiple integral
I_{n}(f)
is a zero-mean random variable with finite variance, which is well-defined for all
f
in the
n
-th tensor product space
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
. Its variance is given by
\mathbb{E}|I_{n}(f)|^{2}=n!\|\widetilde{f}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2}
, where

\widetilde{f}
is the symmetrization of the function
f
, defined by:

\widetilde{f}(t_{1},x_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{n})=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\rho\in S_{n}}f(t_{\rho(1)},x_{\rho(1)},\ldots,t_{\rho(n)},x_{\rho(n)}),

and
S_{n}
is the set of permutations of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

The solution exists if and only if the series (
8
) converges in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, i.e.

\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}|I_{n}\big(f_{t,x,n}\big)|^{2}=\sum_{n\geq 1}n!\,\|\widetilde{f}_{t,x,n}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2}<\infty.

In Case I, for both heat and wave equations, a sufficient condition for the existence of the solution is
Dalang’s condition
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi)<\infty,

which is equivalent to

d-\alpha<2.

(9)

If in addition,
\gamma_{0}
is given by the fractional kernel (
6
), then
(
9
) is the necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of the Skorohod solution of the heat equation (
1
) (see
[
3
]
), while in the case of the wave equation (
2
), the necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of the Skorohod solution is: (see
[
8
]
)

d-\alpha<2H_{0}+1.

In the case of a rough noise in space (Case II) with temporal covariance
\gamma_{0}
given by (
6
), a sufficient condition for the existence of the solution is
H_{0}+H>3/4
for the heat equation (see
[
15
]
), respectively
H>1/4
for the wave equation (see
[
26
]
).
These results provide extensions of the some earlier results of
[
4
,
16
,
17
]
which were obtained for the white noise in time (corresponding to the case
H_{0}=1/2
).

The following two theorems are the main results of the present article.

Theorem 2.1

(The Regular Case)
.

Let
\{W^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in(0,d)}
be a family of zero-mean Gaussian processes with covariance (
3
) in which
\gamma_{0}
is chosen such that its corresponding measure
\mu_{0}
satisfies (
5
), and the measure
\mu
is given by:

\mu(d\xi)=|\xi|^{-\alpha}d\xi\quad\mbox{with $\alpha\in(0,d)$ and $d\geq 1$}.

(10)

For any
\alpha\in\big(\max(d-2,0),d\big)
, let
u^{\alpha}
be a continuous modification of the Skorohod solution of equation (
1
), respectively equation (
2
), with noise
W
replaced by
W^{\alpha}
.
If
\alpha_{n}\to\alpha^{*}\in(\max(d-2,0),d)
, then

u^{\alpha_{n}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}u^{\alpha^{*}}\quad\ \mbox{in $C([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})$},

where
C([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
is equipped with the topology of uniform convergence on compact sets.

Theorem 2.2

(The Rough Case)
.

Let
\{W^{H}\}_{H\in(0,1/2)}
be a family of zero-mean Gaussian processes with covariance (
3
) in which
\gamma_{0}
is given by (
6
) and the measure
\mu
is given by:

\mu(d\xi)=|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi\quad\mbox{with $H\in(0,1/2)$ and $d=1$.}

(11)

Fix
H_{0}\in(1/2,1)
. Define

\ell=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\max(3/4-H_{0},0)&\mbox{in the case of the heat equation}\\
1/4&\mbox{in the case of the wave equation}\end{array}\right.

(12)

For any
H\in(\ell,1/2)
, let
u^{H}
be a continuous modification of the Skorohod solution of equation (
1
), respectively (
2
), with noise
W
replaced by
W^{H}
.
If
H_{n}\to H^{*}\in(\ell,1/2)
, then

u^{H_{n}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}u^{H^{*}}\quad\ \mbox{in $C([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})$},

where
C([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
is equipped with the topology of uniform convergence on compact sets.

The proofs of Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
are presented in Section
3
, respectively Section
4
, and follow the classical method of finite-dimensional convergence, plus tightness. Instead of the finite-dimensional convergence, we show the (stronger)
L^{2}(\Omega)
-convergence for every
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
fixed. For this, we first prove the corresponding

L^{2}(\Omega)
-convergence of the
m
-th approximation
u_{m}^{\alpha_{n}}(t,x)
(respectively
u_{m}^{H_{n}}(t,x)
) of the solution
u^{\alpha^{*}}(t,x)
(respectively
u^{H^{*}}(t,x)
) for fixed
m\geq 1
, and then we let
n\to\infty
, by showing the uniform convergence with respect to the noise parameter (
\alpha
or
H
). For tightness, we apply Kolmogorov-Centsov theorem, which means that we need to estimate the increments of the solution. This study requires a special analysis of the constants involved, which guarantees that all bounds are uniform for parameter values in a compact set.

3
Regular Noise

In this section, we consider equations (
1
) and (
2
) driven by a Gaussian noise
W
with covariance (
3
) in which
\gamma_{0}
is chosen such that its corresponding measure
\mu_{0}
satisfies (
5
), and the measure
\mu
is given by (
10
).

To emphasize the dependence on the parameter
\alpha
, we denote the noise, the Hilbert space, and the solution, by
W^{\alpha},\mathcal{H}^{\alpha},u^{\alpha}
, respectively. The multiple integral of order
n
with respect to
W^{\alpha}
will be denoted by
I_{n}^{\alpha}
.
With this notation, the series expansion (
8
) becomes:

u^{\alpha}(t,x)=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}I_{n}^{\alpha}(f_{t,x,n}).

As in
[
14
]
, we begin with the construction of the family
\{W^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in(0,d)}
on the same probability space. For this, we consider a
\mathbb{C}
-valued Gaussian random measure
\widehat{W}=\{\widehat{W}(A);A\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})\}
defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, given by:

\widehat{W}(A)=W_{1}(A)+iW_{2}(A)

(15)

where
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
are independent space-time Gaussian white noise processes on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. Then for any sets
A,B\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
,

\mathbb{E}[\widehat{W}(A)\overline{\widehat{W}(B)}]={\rm Leb}(A\cap B)
, where
{\rm Leb}
denotes the Lebesgue measure.

We let
\widehat{W}(1_{A})=\widehat{W}(A)
. By linearity, we extend this definition to simple functions, and by approximation, we extend it further to functions
h\in L_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
. We denote this extension by

\widehat{W}(h)=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(\tau,\xi)\widehat{W}(d\tau,d\xi).

The following isometry property holds: for any function
h\in L_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
,

\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(\tau,\xi)\widehat{W}(d\tau,d\xi)\right|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|h(\tau,\xi)|^{2}d\tau d\xi.

(16)

Let
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the set of
\mathbb{C}
-valued rapidly decreasing
C^{\infty}
-functions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we define

W^{\alpha}(\varphi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}\varphi(\tau,\xi)\sqrt{g_{0}(\tau)}|\xi|^{-\alpha/2}\widehat{W}(d\tau,d\xi),

(17)

where
\mathcal{F}\varphi(\tau,\xi)
is the Fourier transform in both variables:

\mathcal{F}\varphi(\tau,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i\tau t-i\xi\cdot x}\varphi(t,x)dtdx.

Note that
W^{\alpha}(\varphi)
is real-valued. We use the notation
W^{\alpha}(\varphi)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(t,x)W^{\alpha}(dt,dx)
.

By the isometry property (
16
), it follows that

\mathbb{E}[W^{\alpha}(\varphi)W^{\alpha}(\psi)]=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}\varphi(\tau,\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}\psi(\tau,\xi)}g_{0}(\tau)|\xi|^{-\alpha}d\tau d\xi=\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}}.

This shows that the process
W^{\alpha}
has the desired covariance function (
3
). Moreover, all processes
(W^{\alpha})_{\alpha\in(0,d)}
are defined on the same probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
as
\widehat{W}
.

Although we do not have a complete description of the space
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
, there is a procedure (given by Theorem 2.6 of
[
6
]
) which allows us to identify some of its elements. We describe this below. More precisely, it can be proved that
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
contains all functions
t\mapsto\varphi(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for which the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}\varphi(t,\cdot)
is a function for any
t>0
, there exists a version
\phi_{\xi}(t)
of
\mathcal{F}\varphi(t,\cdot)(\xi)
such that

(t,\xi)\mapsto\phi_{\xi}(t)
is measurable on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\phi_{\xi}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}\phi_{\xi}
of
\phi_{\xi}
satisfies:

\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}_{t}\phi_{\xi}(\tau)|^{2}g_{0}(\tau)|\xi|^{-\alpha}d\xi d\tau<\infty.

For such a function
\varphi
, the stochastic integral with respect to
W^{\alpha}
can be represented as:

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(t,x)W^{\alpha}(dt,dx)=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}_{t}[\mathcal{F}_{x}\varphi(t,\cdot)(\xi)](\tau)\sqrt{g_{0}(\tau)}|\xi|^{-\alpha/2}\widehat{W}(d\tau,d\xi),

(18)

where
\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathcal{F}_{x}
denote the Fourier transforms in the time and space variables, respectively.
This representation can be extended to multiple integrals with respect to
W^{\alpha}
:

I_{k}^{\alpha}(\varphi)=\int_{(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k}}\mathcal{F}_{t}[\mathcal{F}_{x}\varphi(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\bullet)(\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}})](\boldsymbol{\tau_{k}})\prod_{j=1}^{k}\sqrt{g_{0}(\tau_{j})}|\xi_{j}|^{-\alpha/2}\widehat{W}(d\tau_{1},d\xi_{1})\ldots\widehat{W}(d\tau_{k},d\xi_{k}),

(19)

where
\mathcal{F}_{x}
is the Fourier transform in the space variables and
\mathcal{F}_{t}
is the Fourier transform in the time variables, i.e. the Fourier transform of the function
\boldsymbol{t_{k}}\mapsto\mathcal{F}_{x}\varphi(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\bullet)(\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}})
. Whenever there is no risk of confusion, we drop the lower indices
t,x
from the Fourier transform notation.

The following two lemmas will play an important role below.
The first one can be found for instance in
[
2
]
, while the second one is proved by elementary methods.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
\alpha\in\big(\max(d-2,0),d\big)
and
t>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)|^{2}|\xi-\eta|^{-\alpha}d\xi\leq K_{d,\alpha}\,t^{r_{\alpha}},

where

K_{d,\alpha}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}|\xi|^{-\alpha}d\xi\leq c_{d}\left(\frac{1}{d-\alpha}+\frac{1}{2-(d-\alpha)}\right),

(20)

with
c_{d}
is given by (
14
) and

r_{\alpha}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-(d-\alpha)/2&\mbox{for the heat equation}\\
2-(d-\alpha)&\mbox{for the wave equation}\end{array}\right.

(21)

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}>-1
,

\int_{T_{n}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{\beta_{j}}d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(\beta_{j}+1)\,t^{|\beta|+n}}{\Gamma(|\beta|+n+1)},

where
|\beta|=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{j}
and
t_{n+1}=t
.

The following result will be used in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, in order to show the finite dimensional convergence.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
\alpha_{n}\to\alpha^{*}\in\big(\max(d-2,0),d\big)
, then for any
t>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
k\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}|I_{k}^{\alpha_{n}}(f_{t,x,k})-I_{k}^{\alpha^{*}}(f_{t,x,k})|^{2}\to 0,\quad\mbox{as}\quad n\to\infty.

Proof.

By (
19
),

I_{k}^{\alpha_{n}}(f_{t,x,k})-I_{k}^{\alpha^{*}}(f_{t,x,k})=\int_{(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}H_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{\tau_{k}},\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}})\widehat{W}(d\tau_{1},d\xi_{1})\ldots\widehat{W}(d\tau_{n},d\xi_{n}),

where

H_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{\tau_{k}},\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}})=\mathcal{F}\phi_{\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}}(\boldsymbol{\tau_{k}})\prod_{j=1}^{k}\sqrt{g_{0}(\tau_{j})}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{-\alpha_{n}/2}-\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{-\alpha^{*}/2}\right)

and

\phi_{\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}}):=\mathcal{F}f_{t,x,k}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\cdot)(\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}})=e^{-i(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{k})\cdot x}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j})

(22)

with
t_{k+1}=t
. Here, we use convention (
13
). Hence,

Q_{n}:=\mathbb{E}|I_{k}^{\alpha_{n}}(f_{t,x,k})-I_{k}^{\alpha^{*}}(f_{t,x,k})|^{2}=k!\|\widetilde{H}_{k}^{(n)}\|_{L_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}((\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k})}^{2}\leq k!\|H_{k}^{(n)}\|_{L_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}((\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k})}^{2}

where
\widetilde{H}_{k}^{(n)}
is the symmetrization of
H_{k}^{(n)}
. Using (
7
), we have:

\displaystyle Q_{n}

\displaystyle\leq k!\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k}}\left|\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{-\alpha_{n}/2}-\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{-\alpha^{*}/2}\right|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}|\mathcal{F}\phi_{\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}}(\boldsymbol{\tau_{k}})|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}g_{0}(\tau_{j})d\boldsymbol{\tau_{k}}d\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}

\displaystyle=k!\int_{T_{k}(t)}\int_{T_{k}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\gamma_{0}(t_{j}-s_{j})A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{s_{k}})d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}d\boldsymbol{s_{k}}

(23)

where
T_{k}(t)=\{0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{k}<t\}
and

\displaystyle A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{s_{k}})=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k}}\phi_{\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})\phi_{\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}}(\boldsymbol{s_{k}})\left|\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{-\alpha_{n}/2}-\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{-\alpha^{*}/2}\right|^{2}d\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}.

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and inequality
ab\leq\frac{1}{2}(a^{2}+b^{2})
, we have:

A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{s_{k}})\leq A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{t_{k}})^{1/2}A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{s_{k}},\boldsymbol{s_{k}})^{1/2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\big(A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{t_{k}})+A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{s_{k}},\boldsymbol{s_{k}})\big).

(24)

Hence,

Q_{n}\leq k!\int_{T_{k}(t)}\int_{T_{k}(t)}A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{t_{k}})\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\gamma_{0}(t_{j}-s_{j})d\boldsymbol{s_{k}}\right)d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}\leq k!\Gamma_{0,t}^{k}\int_{T_{k}(t)}A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{t_{k}})d\boldsymbol{t_{k}},

where
\Gamma_{0,t}=\int_{-t}^{t}\gamma_{0}(s)ds
. It suffices to show that the integral appearing in the upper bound above converges to
0
as
n\to\infty
, i.e.

T_{n}:=\int_{T_{k}(t)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big|\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j})\big|^{2}\left|\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{-\alpha_{n}/2}-\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{-\alpha^{*}/2}\right|^{2}d\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}\to 0.

Note that the integrand converges pointwise to
0
on
T_{k}(t)\times(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k}
, as
n\to\infty
.
To apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem, we need to bound the integrand by an integrable function. In fact, it suffices to find an integrable bound for the term depending on
\alpha_{n}
:

g_{n}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}):=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big|\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j})\big|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{-\alpha_{n}}.

We have to find a function
g
on
T_{k}(t)\times(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k}
, such that

g_{n}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}})\leq g(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}})\quad\mbox{for all $n$},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{T_{k}(t)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k}}g(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}})d\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}<\infty.

Recall that
d-2<\alpha^{\ast}<d
. Fix numbers
a
and
b
such that
d-2<a<\alpha^{\ast}<b<d
. Since
\alpha_{n}\to\alpha^{\ast}
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

a\leq\alpha_{n}\leq b
for all
n\geq N
.
We take:

g(\boldsymbol{t}_{k},\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}):=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big|\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j})\big|^{2}\big(|\xi_{j}|^{-b}1_{\{|\xi_{j}|\leq 1\}}+|\xi_{j}|^{-a}1_{\{|\xi_{j}|>1\}}\big).

Note that, for any
j=1,\ldots,k
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j})|^{2}\big(|\xi_{j}|^{-b}1_{\{|\xi_{j}|\leq 1\}}+|\xi_{j}|^{-a}1_{\{|\xi_{j}|>1\}}\big)d\xi_{j}

\displaystyle\leq C_{t}\int_{|\xi_{j}|\leq 1}|\xi_{j}|^{-b}d\xi_{j}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j})|^{2}|\xi_{j}|^{-a}d\xi_{j},

where
C_{t}=1
for the heat equation, and
C_{t}=t^{2}
for the wave equation.
By Lemma
3.1
, the last integral is bounded by
K_{d,a}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{r_{a}}
, where
K_{d,a}
and
r_{a}
are given by (
20
), respectively (
21
). Let

C_{a,b,d}=\frac{c_{d}}{d-b}+K_{d,a}
, where
c_{d}
is the area of
\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d};|z|=1\}
. Then,

\int_{T_{k}(t)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k}}g(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{x_{k}})d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}d\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}\leq C_{a,b,d}^{k}\int_{T_{k}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\Big(C_{t}+(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{r_{a}}\Big)d\boldsymbol{t_{k}},

and the last integral is finite due to Lemma
3.2
, since
r_{a}>-1
.
∎

We now start the preparations for the proof of tightness.
We begin by recalling some path properties of the solution
u^{\alpha}
.
The next result is a restatement Theorem 3.2 of
[
5
]
(for heat equation), respectively Theorem 8.3 of
[
6
]
(for the wave equation), in the case when the spatial covariance kernel of the noise is the Riesz kernel
f(x)=C_{d,\alpha}|x|^{-(d-\alpha)}
. We include this result for the sake of completeness, but we cannot use it directly for the proof of tightness, since we need the explicit form of the constant
C
as a function of
\alpha
, to be able to bound it uniformly for all
\alpha
in a compact interval
[a,b]
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume that
\alpha\in\big(\max(d-2,0),d\big)
.
Let
p\geq 2
and
T>0
be arbitrary, and
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact set.
For any
\beta\in(\frac{d-\alpha}{2},1)
,
t,t^{\prime}\in[0,T]
, and
x,x^{\prime}\in K
, we have

\displaystyle\|u^{\alpha}(t,x)-u^{\alpha}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\|_{p}\leq C\big(|t-t^{\prime}|^{\gamma}+|x-x^{\prime}|^{1-\beta}\big),

(25)

where
\gamma=\frac{1-\beta}{2}
for the heat equation, and
\gamma=1-\beta
for the wave equation. Here

C
is a constant that depends on
(\alpha,d,p,T,K,\beta)
.

By Theorem
3.4
, the process
u^{\alpha}
has a continuous modification in
(t,x)
. We work with this modification, which we denote also by
u^{\alpha}
.

In the case of the heat equation, we will use the following constant:

k_{\alpha}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-t|\xi|^{2}}|\xi|^{-\alpha}d\xi=\frac{c_{d}}{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{d-\alpha}{2}\right)\,t^{-\frac{d-\alpha}{2}},

with
c_{d}
given by (
14
). We will need the following result.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\max(d-2,0)<a<b<d
and
\beta\in(\frac{d-a}{2},1)
be arbitrary. For any
\alpha\in[a,b]
,

(a) for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big|\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi+\eta)\big|^{2}|\xi|^{-\alpha}d\xi\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}k_{\alpha}(t)&\mbox{for the heat equation}\\
C_{T,d,a,b,\beta}t^{2(1-\beta)}&\mbox{for the wave equation}\end{array}\right.

(b) for any
t>0
,

\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big|\mathcal{F}\big(G_{t+h}-G_{t}\big)(\xi+\eta)\big|^{2}|\xi|^{-\alpha}d\xi\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C_{\beta}h^{1-\beta}t^{\beta-1}k_{\alpha}(t/2)&\mbox{for the heat equation}\\
C_{d,a,b,\beta}h^{2(1-\beta)}&\mbox{for the wave equation}\end{array}\right.

(c) for any compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for any
z\in K
,

\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|e^{i(\xi+\eta)\cdot z}-1|^{2}|\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi+\eta)|^{2}|\xi|^{-\alpha}d\xi\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C_{\beta}|z|^{2(1-\beta)}t^{\beta-1}k_{\alpha}(t/2)&\mbox{for the heat equation}\\
C_{T,K,d,a,b,\beta}|z|^{2(1-\beta)}&\mbox{for the wave equation}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

This is result is proved exactly as Proposition 3.1 of
[
5
]
for the heat equation, respectively Proposition 7.4 of
[
9
]
for the wave equation, except that here we give a uniform bound for the constants, for all
\alpha\in[a,b]
(which can be obtained after examining closely the proofs).
In these references, the results are proved for
fixed

\alpha\in\big(\max(d-2,0),d\big)
and the exponents are given in terms of an arbitrary
\beta>0
which satisfies the condition:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\right)^{\beta}|\xi|^{-\alpha}d\xi<\infty,

(26)

which is equivalent to the requirement
\beta\in\left(\frac{d-\alpha}{2},1\right)
.
In our case, since we want to obtain a
uniform
bound for all
\alpha\in[a,b]
, we fix an apriori value
\beta\in\left(\frac{d-a}{2},1\right)
, which guarantees that condition (
26
) holds for all
\alpha\in[a,b]
.
∎

The next result gives the desired upper bound for the increments of the solution (uniformly in
\alpha\in[a,b]
), which will be used below to show the tightness of
(u^{\alpha})_{\alpha\in[a,b]}
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Assume that
\max(d-2,0)<a<b<d
. Let
p\geq 2
,
T>0
be arbitrary and
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact set. For any
\beta\in\big(\frac{d-a}{2},1\big)
,
t,t^{\prime}\in[0,T]
and
x,x^{\prime}\in K
, we have:

\sup_{\alpha\in[a,b]}\|u^{\alpha}(t,x)-u^{\alpha}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\|_{p}\leq C\Big(|t-t^{\prime}|^{\gamma}+|x-x^{\prime}|^{1-\beta}\Big),

where
\gamma=\frac{1-\beta}{2}
for the heat equation, respectively
\gamma=1-\beta
for the wave equation.
Here
C
is a constant that depends on
(d,p,T,K,\beta)
.

Proof.

We follow the same argument as in the proof of Theorem 3.2 of
[
5
]
for the heat equation, respectively Theorem 8.3 of
[
6
]
for the wave equation.

Step 1.
We study the time increments. Let
t\in[0,T]
and
h>0
be such that
t+h\in[0,T]
. By hypercontractivity,

\displaystyle\big\|u^{\alpha}(t+h,x)-u^{\alpha}(t,x)\big\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}(p-1)^{n/2}\big\|I_{n}^{\alpha}\big(f_{t+h,x,n}-f_{t,x,n}\big)\big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}(p-1)^{n/2}\left(\frac{2}{n!}\Big(A_{n}^{\alpha}(t,h)+B_{n}^{\alpha}(t,h)\Big)\right)^{1/2},

where

A_{n}^{\alpha}(t,h)=(n!)^{2}\|\widetilde{f}_{t+h,x,n}1_{[0,t]^{n}}-\widetilde{f}_{t,x,n}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}^{\otimes n}}^{2}
and
B_{n}^{\alpha}(t,h)=(n!)^{2}\|\widetilde{f}_{t+h,x,n}1_{[0,t+h]^{n}-[0,t]^{n}}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}^{\otimes n}}^{2}
.

We examine
A_{n}^{\alpha}(t,h)
. Note that

A_{n}^{\alpha}(t,h)\leq\Gamma_{0,t}^{n}\int_{[0,t]^{n}}\psi_{t,h,n}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})d\boldsymbol{t_{n}},

where the function
\psi_{t,h,n}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})
is defined as follows: if
\boldsymbol{t_{n}}\in[0,t]^{n}
and
\rho
is the permutation of
1,\ldots,n
such that
t_{\rho(1)}<\ldots<t_{\rho(n)}
, we let
u_{j}=t_{\rho(j+1)}-t_{\rho(j)}
(with
t_{\rho(n+1)}=t
), and

\displaystyle\psi_{t,h,n}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})

\displaystyle:=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\big|\mathcal{F}G_{u_{j}}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j})\big|^{2}\big|\mathcal{F}(G_{u_{n}+h}-G_{u_{n}})(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{n})\big|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\xi_{j}|^{-\alpha}d\boldsymbol{\xi_{n}}.

To find an upper bound for
\psi_{t,h,n}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})
, we use Lemma
3.5
.(a,b). For the heat equation,

\psi_{t,h,n}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})\leq C_{\beta}h^{1-\beta}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}k_{\alpha}(u_{j})u_{n}^{\beta-1}k_{\alpha}(u_{n}/2),

(27)

and hence, using the form of
k_{\alpha}
and Lemma
3.2
, we have:

\displaystyle A_{n}^{\alpha}(t,h)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\beta}h^{1-\beta}n!\,\Gamma_{0,t}^{n}c_{d}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{d-\alpha}{2}\right)^{n}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{\frac{\alpha-d}{2}}(t-t_{n})^{\beta-1-\frac{d-\alpha}{2}}d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\beta}h^{1-\beta}n!\,\Gamma_{0,t}^{n}c_{d}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{d-\alpha}{2}\right)^{n}\frac{\Gamma(1-\frac{d-\alpha}{2})^{n-1}\,t^{(n-1)(1-\frac{d-\alpha}{2})}}{\Gamma\big((n-1)(1-\frac{d-\alpha}{2})+1\big)}\cdot\frac{t^{\beta-\frac{d-\alpha}{2}}}{\beta-\frac{d-\alpha}{2}}.

This expression can be bounded uniformly in
\alpha\in[a,b]
, using monotonicity properties of the
\Gamma
function. For the wave equation,

\psi_{t,h,n}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})\leq C^{n}h^{2(1-\beta)}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}u_{j}^{2(1-\beta)}

(28)

where
C
is a constant that depends on
T,d,a,b,\beta
, and hence

\displaystyle A_{n}^{\alpha}(t,h)

\displaystyle\leq C^{n}h^{2(1-\beta)}n!\,\Gamma_{0,t}^{n}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{2(1-\beta)}d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{n}h^{2(1-\beta)}n!\,\Gamma_{0,t}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(3-2\beta)^{n-1}}{\Gamma\big((n-1)(3-2\beta)+1\big)}t^{(n-1)(3-2\beta)+1}.

In summary, we obtain that:

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in[a,b]}\sum_{n\geq 1}(p-1)^{n/2}\left(\frac{1}{n!}A_{n}^{\alpha}(t,h)\right)^{1/2}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}Ch^{\frac{1-\beta}{2}}&\mbox{for the heat equation}\\
Ch^{1-\beta}&\mbox{for the wave equation}\end{array}\right.

where
C
is a constant that depends on
(p,d,a,b,T,\beta)
.

Next, we examine
B_{n}^{\alpha}(t,h)
. Let
D_{t,h}=[0,t+h]^{n}\verb 2\2[0,t]^{n}
. Then

\displaystyle B_{n}^{\alpha}(t,h)

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma_{0,t+h}^{n}\int_{[0,t+h]^{n}}\gamma_{t,h,n}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})1_{D_{t,h}}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})d\boldsymbol{t_{n}},

where

\gamma_{t,h,n}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}}):=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\big|\mathcal{F}G_{u_{j}}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j})\big|^{2}\big|\mathcal{F}G_{u_{n}+h}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{n})\big|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\xi_{j}|^{-\alpha}d\boldsymbol{\xi_{n}},

and we define, as above,
u_{j}=t_{\rho(j+1)}-t_{\rho(j)}
if
t_{\rho(1)}<\ldots<t_{\rho(n)}<t+h
and
t_{\rho(n+1)}=t
.

To find an upper bound for
\gamma_{t,h,n}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})
, we use Lemma
3.5
.(a). For the heat equation,

\gamma_{t,h,n}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})\leq\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}k_{\alpha}(u_{j})k_{\alpha}(u_{n}+h),

and hence, using the form of
k_{\alpha}
and Lemma
3.2
, we have:

\displaystyle B_{n}^{\alpha}(t,h)

\displaystyle\leq n!\,\Gamma_{0,T}^{n}c_{d}^{n}\Gamma\Big(\frac{d-\alpha}{2}\Big)^{n}\int_{t}^{t+h}\int_{T_{n-1}(t_{n})}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{-\frac{d-\alpha}{2}}(t+h-t_{n})^{-\frac{d-\alpha}{2}}d\boldsymbol{t_{n-1}}dt_{n}

\displaystyle\leq n!\,\Gamma_{0,T}^{n}c_{d}^{n}\Gamma\Big(\frac{d-\alpha}{2}\Big)^{n}\frac{\Gamma(1-\frac{d-\alpha}{2})^{n-1}\,t^{(n-1)(1-\frac{d-\alpha}{2})}}{\Gamma\big((n-1)(1-\frac{d-\alpha}{2})+1\big)}\cdot\frac{h^{1-\frac{d-\alpha}{2}}}{1-\frac{d-\alpha}{2}}.

It is clear that the previous expression can be bounded uniformly in
\alpha\in[a,b]
. We also note that
h^{1-\frac{d-\alpha}{2}}\leq C_{T}h^{1-\beta}
since
\beta>\frac{d-a}{2}>\frac{d-\alpha}{2}
.
For the wave equation,

\gamma_{t,h,n}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})\leq C^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}u_{j}^{2(1-\beta)}(u_{n}+h)^{2(1-\beta)},

where
C
is a constant that depends on
(T,d,a,b,\beta)
, and hence

\displaystyle B_{n}^{\alpha}(t,h)

\displaystyle\leq n!\Gamma_{0,T}^{n}C^{n}\int_{t}^{t+h}\int_{T_{n-1}(t_{n})}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{2(1-\beta)}(t+h-t_{n})^{2(1-\beta)}d\boldsymbol{t_{n-1}}dt_{n}

\displaystyle\leq n!\Gamma_{0,T}^{n}C^{n}\frac{\Gamma(3-2\beta)^{n-1}\,t^{(n-1)(3-2\beta)}}{\Gamma\big((n-1)(3-2\beta)+1\big)}\cdot\frac{h^{3-2\beta}}{3-2\beta}.

In summary, we have:

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in[a,b]}\sum_{n\geq 1}(p-1)^{n/2}\left(\frac{1}{n!}B_{n}^{\alpha}(t,h)\right)^{1/2}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}Ch^{\frac{1-\beta}{2}}&\mbox{for the heat equation}\\
Ch^{(3-2\beta)/2}&\mbox{for the wave equation}\end{array}\right.

Step 2.
We study the space increments. By hypercontractivity,

\|u^{\alpha}(t,x+z)-u^{\alpha}(t,x)\|_{p}\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}(p-1)^{n/2}\|I_{n}^{\alpha}(f_{t,x+z,n}-f_{t,x,n})\|_{p}\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}(p-1)^{n/2}\left(\frac{1}{n!}C_{n}^{\alpha}(t,z)\right)^{1/2},

where
C_{n}^{\alpha}(t,z)=(n!)^{2}\|\widetilde{f}_{t,x+z,n}-\widetilde{f}_{t,x,n}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}^{\otimes n}}^{2}
. Note that

\displaystyle C_{n}^{\alpha}(t,z)

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma_{0,t}^{n}\int_{[0,t]^{n}}\psi_{t,z,n}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})d\boldsymbol{t_{n}},

where

\psi_{t,z,n}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\mathcal{F}G_{u_{j}}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j})|^{2}|1-e^{-i(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{n})\cdot z}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\xi_{j}|^{-\alpha}d\boldsymbol{\xi_{n}},

and we define, as above,
u_{j}=t_{\rho(j+1)}-t_{\rho(j)}
if
t_{\rho(1)}<\ldots<t_{\rho(n)}<t+h
and
t_{\rho(n+1)}=t
.

To find an upper bound for
\psi_{t,z,n}^{(\alpha)}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})
, we use Lemma
3.5
.(a,c). For the heat equation,

\displaystyle\psi_{t,z,n}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})\leq C_{\beta}|z|^{2(1-\beta)}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}k_{\alpha}(u_{j})u_{n}^{\beta-1}k_{\alpha}(u_{n}/2),

which is similar to (
27
) above. For the wave equation,

\displaystyle\psi_{t,z,n}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})\leq C^{n}|z|^{2(1-\beta)}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}u_{j}^{2(1-\beta)},

with a constant
C>0
that depends on
(T,K,d,a,b)
, which is similar to (
28
) above. We conclude that for both heat and wave equations,

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in[a,b]}\sum_{n\geq 1}(p-1)^{n/2}\left(\frac{1}{n!}C_{n}^{\alpha}(t,h)\right)^{1/2}\leq C|z|^{1-\beta}.

∎

Note that, in the case of the white noise in time considered in

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces u^{\alpha_{n}}_{m}(t,x)}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|u_{m}^{\alpha_{n}}(t,x)-u_{m}^{\alpha^{*}}(t,x)|^{2}\leq m\sum_{k=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}|I_{k}^{\alpha_{n}}(f_{t,x,k})-I_{k}^{\alpha^{*}}(f_{t,x,k})|^{2}\quad\mbox{as $n\to\infty$}.

The convergence indicated by the right vertical arrow holds trivially since by construction,

in

It remains to prove the uniform convergence in

\max(d-2,0)<a<\alpha^{*}<b<d

for any

\sup_{\alpha\in[a,b]}\mathbb{E}|u_{m}^{\alpha}(t,x)-u^{\alpha}(t,x)|^{2}=\sup_{\alpha\in[a,b]}\sum_{k\geq m+1}\mathbb{E}|I_{k}^{\alpha}(f_{t,x,k})|^{2}\to 0\quad\mbox{as $m\to\infty$.}

(29)

Note that

\mathbb{E}|I_{k}^{\alpha}(f_{t,x,k})|^{2}=k!\|\widetilde{f}_{t,x,k}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}^{\otimes k}}^{2}\leq k!\Gamma_{0,t}^{k}\int_{[0,t]^{n}}A_{k}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})d\boldsymbol{t_{k}},

where

.

If
\boldsymbol{t_{k}}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})\in[0,t]^{k}
is arbitrary and
\rho
is a permutation of
1,\ldots,k
such that
t_{\rho(1)}<\ldots<t_{\rho(k)}
, with
t_{\rho(k+1)}=t
, then by Lemma
3.1
, we have:

\displaystyle A_{k}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k!)^{2}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big|\mathcal{F}G_{t_{\rho(j+1)}-t_{\rho(j)}}\big(\sum_{i=1}^{j}\xi_{i}\big)\big|^{2}\,|\xi_{j}|^{-\alpha}d\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}\leq\frac{K_{d,\alpha}^{k}}{(k!)^{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(t_{\rho(j+1)}-t_{\rho(j)})^{r_{\alpha}},

where

Using Lemma

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|I_{k}^{\alpha}(f_{t,x,k})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma_{0,t}^{k}K_{d,\alpha}^{k}\int_{T_{k}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{r_{\alpha}}d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}=\Gamma_{0,t}^{k}K_{d,\alpha}^{k}\frac{\Gamma(r_{\alpha}+1)^{k}\,t^{k(r_{\alpha}+1)}}{\Gamma\big(k(r_{\alpha}+1)+1\big)}.

\alpha\in[a,b]

First, note that

. Moreover,
recall that there exists a value

such that
\Gamma
is decreasing on

In the case of the heat equation,

, and therefore for any

,

(30)

In the case of the wave equation,

where

is chosen such that
m_{1}(3-d+a)>x_{0}
, and therefore for any

,

\sup_{\alpha\in[a,b]}\sum_{k\geq m+1}\mathbb{E}|I_{k}^{\alpha}(f_{t,x,k})|^{2}\leq\sum_{k\geq m+1}\Gamma_{0,t}^{k}K_{d,a,b}^{k}\frac{2^{k}\,(t\vee 1)^{k(3-d+b)}}{\Gamma\big(k(3-d+a))+1\big)}.

(31)

Relation (

4
Rough Noise

In this section, we consider the case when the measure
\mu
is given by:

\mu(d\xi)=c_{H}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi\quad\mbox{for some $H\in(0,1/2)$}

In addition, we assume that the temporal covariance function
\gamma_{0}
is given by (
6
).

To emphasize the dependence on the parameter
H
, we denote the noise, the Hilbert space, and the solution, by
W^{H},\mathcal{H}^{H},u^{H}
, respectively. The multiple integral of order
n
with respect to
W^{H}
will be denoted by
I_{n}^{H}
.
With this notation, the series expansion (
8
) becomes:

u^{H}(t,x)=1+\sum_{k\geq 1}I_{n}^{H}(f_{t,x,k}).

Similarly to Section
3
,
we begin with a construction of all Gaussian processes
(W^{H})_{H\in(0,1/2)}
on the same probability space.
Let
\widehat{W}
be the
\mathbb{C}
-valued Gaussian random measure given by (
15
). For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
, let

W^{H}(\varphi)=\sqrt{c_{H_{0}}c_{H}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathcal{F}\varphi(\tau,\xi)\tau^{\frac{1}{2}-H_{0}}|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}-H}\widehat{W}(d\tau,d\xi),

where
\mathcal{F}\varphi(\tau,\xi)
denotes the Fourier transform in both variables
(t,x)
. We will use the notation

W^{H}(\varphi)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t,x)W^{H}(dt,dx)
.

By the isometry property (
16
), it follows that

\mathbb{E}[W^{H}(\varphi)W^{H}(\psi)]=c_{H_{0}}c_{H}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}\varphi(\tau,\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}\psi(\tau,\xi)}|\tau|^{1-2H_{0}}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\tau d\xi=\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}.

This shows that
W^{H}
has the desired covariance function (
3
), and all processes
(W^{H})_{H\in(0,1/2)}
are defined on the same probability space. For any function
\varphi
for which the stochastic integral is well-defined, we have:

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t,x)W^{H}(dt,dx)=\sqrt{c_{H_{0}}c_{H}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{t}[\mathcal{F}_{x}\varphi(t,\cdot)(\xi)](\tau)|\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}-H_{0}}|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}-H}\widehat{W}(d\tau,d\xi).

This representation can be extended to multiple integrals with respect to
W^{H}
:

I_{k}^{H}(\varphi)=c_{H_{0}}^{k/2}c_{H}^{k/2}\int_{(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})^{k}}\mathcal{F}_{t}[\mathcal{F}_{x}\varphi(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\bullet)(\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}})](\boldsymbol{\tau_{k}})\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\tau_{j}|^{\frac{1}{2}-H_{0}}|\xi_{j}|^{\frac{1}{2}-H}\widehat{W}(d\tau_{1},d\xi_{1})\ldots\widehat{W}(d\tau_{k},d\xi_{k}).

(32)

We will use the following lemma, which can be found for instance in
[
4
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
t>0
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t|\xi|^{2}}|\xi|^{\alpha}d\xi=\Gamma\left(\frac{1+\alpha}{2}\right)t^{-\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}\quad\mbox{for any $\alpha>-1$}

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|^{2}}|\xi|^{\alpha}d\xi=2^{1-\alpha}\widetilde{C}_{\alpha}t^{1-\alpha}\quad\mbox{for any $\alpha\in(-1,1)$},

where

\widetilde{C}_{\alpha}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(1-\alpha)^{-1}\Gamma(\alpha)\sin(\pi\alpha/2)&\mbox{if $\alpha\in(0,1)$}\\
\alpha^{-1}(1-\alpha)^{-1}\Gamma(1+\alpha)\sin(\pi\alpha/2)&\mbox{if $\alpha\in(-1,0)$}\\
\pi/2&\mbox{if $\alpha=0$}\end{array}\right.

We will use a similar approximation technique as in Section
3
. More precisely, we study first the
L^{2}(\Omega)
-continuity in
H
of the multiple integral of
I_{k}^{H}(f_{t,x,k})
for fixed
k
.
This is achieved by the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\ell
be given by (
12
).
If
H_{n}\to H^{*}\in(\ell,1/2)
, then

Q_{n}:=\mathbb{E}|I_{k}^{H_{n}}(f_{t,x,k})-I_{k}^{H^{*}}(f_{t,x,k})|^{2}\to 0,\quad\mbox{as $n\to\infty$},

for any
t>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

Similarly to (
23
), we have:

Q_{n}\leq k!\alpha_{H_{0}}^{k}\int_{T_{k}(t)}\int_{T_{k}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|t_{j}-s_{j}|^{2H_{0}-2}A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t}_{k},\boldsymbol{s_{k}})d\boldsymbol{t}_{k}d\boldsymbol{s_{k}},

where

A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t}_{k},\boldsymbol{s_{k}})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\phi_{\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})\phi_{\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}}(\boldsymbol{s_{k}})\left|c_{H_{n}}^{k/2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{\frac{1}{2}-H_{n}}-c_{H^{*}}^{k/2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{\frac{1}{2}-H^{*}}\right|^{2}d\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}.

and
\phi_{\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})
is given by (
22
). We treat separately the heat and wave equation.

Case 1. (wave equation)
In this case, we reduce our problem to the white noise in time. More precisely, using the inequality (
24
), we have

\displaystyle Q_{n}

\displaystyle\leq k!\Gamma_{0,t}^{k}\int_{T_{k}(t)}A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{t_{k}})d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}

\displaystyle=k!\Gamma_{0,t}^{k}\int_{T_{k}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\sin^{2}((t_{j+1}-t_{j})|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}|)}{|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}|^{2}}\left|c_{H_{n}}^{k/2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{\frac{1}{2}-H_{n}}-c_{H^{*}}^{k/2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{\frac{1}{2}-H^{*}}\right|^{2}d\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}

where
\Gamma_{0,t}=2H_{0}t^{2H_{0}-1}
. The proof of Theorem 4.1 of
[
14
]
shows that the last integral converges to
0
as
n\to\infty
.

Case 2. (heat equation)
In this case, we still use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t}_{k},\boldsymbol{s_{k}})\leq A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t}_{k},\boldsymbol{t_{k}})^{1/2}A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{s}_{k},\boldsymbol{s_{k}})^{1/2}
,
but we combine it with the following inequality, which is a consequence of the Littlewood-Hardy inequality: for any
\varphi\in L^{1/H_{0}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k})
,

\alpha_{H_{0}}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|t_{j}-s_{j}|^{2H_{0}-2}\varphi(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})\varphi(\boldsymbol{s_{k}})d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}d\boldsymbol{s_{k}}\leq b_{H_{0}}^{k}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}}|\varphi(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})|^{1/H_{0}}d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}\right)^{2H_{0}},

(33)

where
b_{H_{0}}>0
depends only on
H_{0}
. Hence,

Q_{n}\leq k!\,b_{H_{0}}^{k}\left(\int_{T_{k}(t)}A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t}_{k},\boldsymbol{t_{k}})^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}\right)^{2H_{0}}.

Therefore, it suffices to show that:

\int_{T_{k}(t)}A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t}_{k},\boldsymbol{t_{k}})^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}\to 0,\quad\mbox{as $n\to\infty$}.

(34)

For this, we apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem. We will show that

A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t}_{k},\boldsymbol{t_{k}})\to 0\quad\mbox{as $n\to\infty$},

(35)

and there exists a function
h_{k}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})
such that

A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t}_{k},\boldsymbol{t_{k}})\leq h_{k}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})\quad\mbox{and}\int_{T_{k}(t)}h_{k}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}<\infty.

(36)

We first prove (
35
). By the change of variables
\eta_{j}=\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,n
,

\displaystyle A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t}_{k},\boldsymbol{t_{k}})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}e^{-(t_{j+1}-t_{j})|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}|^{2}}\left|c_{H_{n}}^{k/2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{\frac{1}{2}-H_{n}}-c_{H^{*}}^{k/2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{\frac{1}{2}-H^{*}}\right|^{2}d\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}e^{-(t_{j+1}-t_{j})|\eta_{j}|^{2}}\left|c_{H_{n}}^{k/2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\eta_{j}-\eta_{j-1}|^{\frac{1}{2}-H_{n}}-c_{H^{*}}^{k/2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\eta_{j}-\eta_{j-1}|^{\frac{1}{2}-H^{*}}\right|^{2}d\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}},

where
\eta_{0}=0
. We denote by
B^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})
the integrand in the last integral above. Clearly,
B^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. So relation (
35
) will follow by the Dominated Convergence Theorem, as long as we show that there exists a function
B(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})
such that

B^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})\leq B(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}B(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})d\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}}<\infty.

Note that
B^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})\leq 2\big(B_{1}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})+B_{2}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})\big)
, where

B_{1}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})=\prod_{j=1}^{k}e^{-(t_{j+1}-t_{j})|\eta_{j}|^{2}}c_{H_{n}}^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\eta_{j}-\eta_{j-1}|^{1-2H_{n}},

and
B_{2}^{(n)}
has a similar expression with
H_{n}
replaced by
H^{*}
. It suffices to consider
B_{1}^{(n)}
.

Fix numbers
a
and
b
such that

\ell<a<H^{*}<b<\frac{1}{2}.

(37)

Then
H_{n}\in[a,b]
for
n
large enough.
Since the constant
c_{H}
defines a continuous function of
H
,
c_{H_{n}}
is bounded by a constant
c
.
We use the inequality

\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\eta_{j}-\eta_{j-1}|^{1-2H}\leq\sum_{\boldsymbol{a}\in A_{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\eta_{j}|^{(1-2H)a_{j}},

(38)

where
A_{k}
is a set of multi-indices
\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})
such that

a_{1}\in\{1,2\}
,
a_{n}\in\{0,1\}
,
a_{j}\in\{0,1,2\}
for
j=1,\ldots,k-1
,
\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}=k
, and
{\rm card}(A_{k})=2^{k-1}
. It follows that:

B_{1}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})\leq c^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{k}e^{-(t_{j+1}-t_{j})|\eta_{j}|^{2}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{a}\in A_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\eta_{j}|^{(1-2H_{n})a_{j}}.

Moreover,

|\eta_{j}|^{(1-2H)a_{j}}\leq f_{a}(|\eta_{j}|)
for any
j=1,\ldots,n
, where
a
is the constant from (
37
) and the functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}
are defined as follows:
f_{0}(r)=1
for any
r>0
,

f_{1}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}r^{1-2a}&\mbox{if $r\geq 1$}\\
1&\mbox{if $r\in(0,1)$}\end{array}\right.\quad\mbox{and}\quad f_{2}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}r^{2(1-2a)}&\mbox{if $r\geq 1$}\\
1&\mbox{if $r\in(0,1)$}\end{array}\right.

Hence,

B_{1}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})\leq c^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{k}e^{-(t_{j+1}-t_{j})|\eta_{j}|^{2}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{a}\in A_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}f_{a_{j}}(|\eta_{j}|)=:F(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}}).

By the same argument,
B_{2}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})\leq F(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})
. Hence,
B^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})\leq 2F(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})
and so,

A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{t_{k}})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}B^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})d\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}}\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}F(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})d\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}}.

(39)

It remains to prove that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}F(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})d\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}}<\infty
. We give below an estimate for this integral which will be used for the proof of relation (
36
) below. Note that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}F(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})d\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{a}\in A_{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}I(a_{j})\quad\mbox{with}\quad I(a_{j})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-(t_{j+1}-t_{j})|\eta_{j}|^{2}}f_{a_{j}}(|\eta_{j}|)d\eta_{j}.

We treat separately the cases
a_{j}=0,1,2
. Using Lemma
4.1
, we have:

\displaystyle I(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-(t_{j+1}-t_{j})|\eta_{j}|^{2}}d\eta_{j}=\sqrt{\pi}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{-1/2}

\displaystyle I(1)

\displaystyle=\int_{|\eta_{j}|\leq 1}e^{-(t_{j+1}-t_{j})|\eta_{j}|^{2}}d\eta_{j}+\int_{|\eta_{j}|>1}e^{-(t_{j+1}-t_{j})|\eta_{j}|^{2}}|\eta_{j}|^{1-2a}d\eta_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\pi}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{-1/2}+\Gamma(1-a)(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{-(1-a)}

\displaystyle I(2)

\displaystyle=\int_{|\eta_{j}|\leq 1}e^{-(t_{j+1}-t_{j})|\eta_{j}|^{2}}d\eta_{j}+\int_{|\eta_{j}|>1}e^{-(t_{j+1}-t_{j})|\eta_{j}|^{2}}|\eta_{j}|^{2(1-2a)}d\eta_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\pi}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{-1/2}+\Gamma\left(\frac{3-4a}{2}\right)(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{-\frac{3-4a}{2}}.

Therefore, there exists a constant
c_{a}>0
depending on
a
such that

\displaystyle I(a_{j})

\displaystyle\leq c_{a}\left\{(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{-1/2}+(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{-(1-a)}+(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{-\frac{3-4a}{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq c_{a}(t^{1-2a}+t^{\frac{1}{2}-a}+1)(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{-\frac{3-4a}{2}}=:c_{a,t}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{-\frac{3-4a}{2}}.

Since
{\rm card}(A_{k})=2^{k-1}
, we obtain:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}F(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}})d\boldsymbol{\eta_{k}}\leq 2^{k-1}c_{a,t}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{-\frac{3-4a}{2}}.

(40)

This proves the integrability of
F(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\cdot)
and relation (
35
) follows by the Dominated Convergence Theorem.

Next, we prove (
36
). By (
39
) and (
40
), we have:

\displaystyle A_{k}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\boldsymbol{t_{k}})^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}\leq c_{a,t}^{\frac{k}{2H_{0}}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{-\frac{3-4a}{4H_{0}}}=:h_{k}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}}).

The function
h_{k}
is integrable on
T_{k}(t)
since
\frac{3-4a}{4H_{0}}<1
, due to our choice of
a
in (
37
). This finishes the proof of (
36
) and the justification of the application of the Dominated Convergence Theorem.
∎

We now prove some moment estimates for the increments of the solution
u^{H}
, uniform in
H
, which will be used for the proof of tightness of the family
(u^{H})_{H\in[a,b]}
below. In particular, Theorem
4.3
shows that the process
u^{H}
has a continuous modification. We will work with this modification.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\ell
be given by (
12
). Let
[a,b]
be a compact set in
[0,1]
such that:

\ell<a<b<\frac{1}{2}.

(41)

(a) Let
u^{H}
be the solution of the heat equation (
1
) with noise
W^{H}
. For any
p\geq 2
,
T>0
,
c_{0}\in(0,\frac{2H_{0}+a-1}{2H_{0}})
and

0<\delta<2H_{0}(1-c_{0})+a-1,

(42)

there exist a constant
C>0
such that for any
t,t^{\prime}\in[0,T]
and
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\sup_{H\in[a,b]}\mathbb{E}|u(t,x)-u(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})|^{p}\leq C\Big(|t-t^{\prime}|^{p\delta/2}+|x-x^{\prime}|^{p\delta}\Big).

(b) Let
u^{H}
be the solution of the wave equation (
2
) with noise
W^{H}
. For any
p\geq 2
,
T>0
,
there exist a constant
C>0
such that for any
\delta\in(0,a)
and
t,t^{\prime}\in[0,T]
,

\sup_{H\in[a,b]}\mathbb{E}|u(t,x)-u(t^{\prime},x)|^{p}\leq C|t-t^{\prime}|^{p\delta}.

For any
p\geq 2
,
T>0
and
c_{1}\in(0,2a)
,
there exist a constant
C>0
such that for any
\delta\in(\frac{c_{1}}{2},a)
and
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\sup_{H\in[a,b]}\mathbb{E}|u(t,x)-u(t,x^{\prime})|^{p}\leq C|x-x^{\prime}|^{p\delta}.

Proof.

We examine separately the time increments and the space increments.
We denote by
c
a constant that may depend on
p,T,H_{0},a,b
and
c_{0}
and may be different in each of its appearances.

Step 1 (time increments).
Let
t\in[0,T]
and
h>0
be such that
t+h\in[0,T]
.
As in the proof of Theorem
3.6
,

\displaystyle\big\|u^{H}(t+h,x)-u^{H}(t,x)\big\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}(p-1)^{n/2}\left(\frac{2}{n!}\Big(A_{n}^{H}(t,h)+B_{n}^{H}(t,h)\Big)\right)^{1/2},

where

A_{n}^{H}(t,h)=(n!)^{2}\|\widetilde{f}_{t+h,x,n}1_{[0,t]^{n}}-\widetilde{f}_{t,x,n}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2}
and
B_{n}^{H}(t,h)=(n!)^{2}\|\widetilde{f}_{t+h,x,n}1_{[0,t+h]^{n}-[0,t]^{n}}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2}
.

We study separately the two terms, for the heat equation and for the wave equation.

Study of
A_{n}^{H}(t,h)
(heat equation).
By the Littlewood-Hardy inequality (
33
),

A_{n}^{H}(t,h)\leq b_{H_{0}}^{n}\left(\int_{[0,t]^{n}}\psi_{t,h,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}\right)^{2H_{0}},

(43)

where the function
\psi_{t,h,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})
is defined as follows: if
\boldsymbol{t_{n}}\in[0,t]^{n}
and
\rho
is the permutation of
1,\ldots,n
such that
t_{\rho(1)}<\ldots<t_{\rho(n)}
, we let
u_{j}=t_{\rho(j+1)}-t_{\rho(j)}
(with
t_{\rho(n+1)}=t
), and

\displaystyle\psi_{t,h,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})

\displaystyle:=c_{H}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\big|\mathcal{F}G_{u_{j}}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j})\big|^{2}\big|\mathcal{F}(G_{u_{n}+h}-G_{u_{n}})(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\xi_{k})\big|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H}d\boldsymbol{\xi_{n}}.

Using the change of variables
\eta_{j}=\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,n
(with
\eta_{0}=0
) followed by the inequality (
38
), we see that

\displaystyle\psi_{t,h,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})=c_{H}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\big|\mathcal{F}G_{u_{j}}(\eta_{j})\big|^{2}\big|\mathcal{F}(G_{u_{n}+h}-G_{u_{n}})(\eta_{n})\big|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\eta_{j}-\eta_{j-1}|^{1-2H}d\boldsymbol{\eta_{n}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq c_{H}^{n}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{F}G_{u_{j}}(\eta_{j})|^{2}|\eta_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}d\eta_{j}\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\mathcal{F}(G_{u_{n}+h}-G_{u_{n}})(\eta_{n})\big|^{2}|\eta_{n}|^{\alpha_{n}}d\eta_{n}\right)

(44)

where
D_{n}^{(H)}
is the set of multi-indices
\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
with
\alpha_{j}=(1-2H)a_{j}
and
\boldsymbol{a_{n}}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in A_{n}
. We use Lemma
4.1
to compute the
d\eta_{j}
integrals with
j=1,\ldots,n-1
. (We use the notation
G
here since we use this relation for both heat and wave equations.)

For the
d\eta_{n}
integral, we use the inequality

1-e^{-x}\leq x^{\varepsilon}
for any
x>0
and
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
, and obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\mathcal{F}(G_{u_{n}+h}^{h}-G_{u_{n}}^{h})(\eta_{n})\big|^{2}|\eta_{n}|^{\alpha_{n}}d\eta_{n}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-u_{n}|\eta_{n}|^{2}}\left(1-e^{-h|\eta_{n}|^{2}/2}\right)^{2}|\eta_{n}|^{\alpha_{n}}d\eta_{n}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\leq h^{2\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-u_{n}|\eta_{n}|^{2}}|\eta_{n}|^{\alpha_{n}+4\varepsilon}d\eta_{n}=h^{2\varepsilon}\Gamma\left(\frac{1+\alpha_{n}+4\varepsilon}{2}\right)u_{n}^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{n}+4\varepsilon}{2}}.

(We used the notation
G^{h}
here to emphasize that this calculation is valid only for the heat equation.) Therefore,

\displaystyle\psi_{t,h,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})

\displaystyle\leq h^{2\varepsilon}c_{H}^{n}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{1+\alpha_{j}}{2}\right)u_{j}^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{j}}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{1+\alpha_{n}+4\varepsilon}{2}\right)u_{n}^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{n}+4\varepsilon}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq h^{2\varepsilon}c_{H}^{n}C_{H,1}^{n-1}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}u_{j}^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{j}}{2}}u_{n}^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{n}+4\varepsilon}{2}}

using the fact that
\Gamma\left(\frac{1+\alpha_{n}+4\varepsilon}{2}\right)\leq\Gamma(3)=2
and

\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{1+\alpha_{j}}{2}\right)\leq C_{H,1}^{n-1}
, where

C_{H,1}=\max\left\{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right),\Gamma(1-H),\Gamma\left(\frac{3-4H}{2}\right)\right\}.

(45)

Both
c_{H}
and
C_{H,1}
can be uniformly bounded for all
H\in[a,b]
. From here, we derive that

\displaystyle\int_{[0,t]^{n}}\left(\psi_{t,h,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})\right)^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}\leq c^{n-1}h^{\frac{\varepsilon}{H_{0}}}n!\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{j}}{4H_{0}}}(t-t_{n})^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{n}+2\delta}{4H_{0}}}d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}.

(46)

To compute the integral

{\cal I}(\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}):=\int_{T_{n}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{j}}{4H_{0}}}(t-t_{n})^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{n}+2\delta}{4H_{0}}}d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}

we use Lemma
3.2
with
\beta_{j}=-\frac{1+\alpha_{j}}{4H_{0}}
for
j=1,\ldots,n-1
and
\beta_{n}=-\frac{1+\alpha_{n}+2\varepsilon}{4H_{0}}
. To apply this lemma, we need to check that
\beta_{j}>-1
for all
j=1,\ldots,n
. When
\alpha_{j}=2(1-2H)
, we use the condition
4H_{0}+4H>3
. When
\alpha_{n}=1-2H
and
H\in[a,b]
is arbitrary, we encounter the condition

\varepsilon<\frac{2H_{0}+a-1}{2}.

(47)

Under this condition, we can apply Lemma
3.2
to deduce that:

\displaystyle{\cal I}(\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}})=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\Gamma(1-\frac{1+\alpha_{j}}{4H_{0}})\Gamma(1-\frac{1+\alpha_{n}+4\varepsilon}{4H_{0}})}{\Gamma(\frac{2H_{0}+H-1}{2H_{0}}n-\frac{\varepsilon}{H_{0}}+1)}t^{\frac{2H_{0}+H-1}{2H_{0}}n-\frac{\varepsilon}{H_{0}}}.

To bound these factors, we use some monotonicity properties of the Gamma function:
\Gamma
is decreasing on
(0,x_{0})
and increasing on
(x_{0},\infty)
, where
x_{0}\approx 1.4
. Therefore, for any
\alpha_{j}\in\{0,1-2H,2(1-2H)\}
and
H\in[a,b]
,
we have
\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\Gamma(1-\frac{1+\alpha_{j}}{4H_{0}})\leq\Gamma(1-\frac{3-4a}{4H_{0}})^{n-1}
.
Next, we observe that
1-\frac{1+\alpha_{n}+4\varepsilon}{4H_{0}}
takes values in the interval
[1-\frac{1-a+2\varepsilon}{2H_{0}},1-\frac{1}{4H_{0}}]
whose lower bound may be close to 0. Since
\lim_{x\to 0}\Gamma(x)=\infty
, we control this term by fixing an arbitrary value
c_{0}\in(0,\frac{2H_{0}+a-1}{2H_{0}})
, and then
choosing

\varepsilon=\delta/2\quad\mbox{for some}\quad 0<\delta<2H_{0}(1-c_{0})+a-1.

(48)

With this choice of
\varepsilon
, (
47
) holds, and
more importantly
1-\frac{1-a+2\varepsilon}{2H_{0}}>c_{0}
, so that for any
\alpha_{n}\in\{0,1-2H\}
and
H\in[a,b]
,

\Gamma\left(1-\frac{1+\alpha_{n}+4\varepsilon}{4H_{0}}\right)\leq\Gamma(c_{0}).

(49)

To bound the
\Gamma
-value appearing in the denominator, we pick an integer
m_{0}
such that
(m_{0}-1)\frac{2H_{0}+a-1}{2H_{0}}>x_{0}
. Then, for any
n\geq m_{0}
and for any
H\in[a,b]
,

\Gamma\left(\frac{2H_{0}+H-1}{2H_{0}}n-\frac{\varepsilon}{H_{0}}+1\right)>\Gamma\left(\frac{(n-1)(2H_{0}+a-1)}{2H_{0}}+1\right)>c^{n-1}[(n-1)!]^{\frac{2H_{0}+a-1}{2H_{0}}}.

Hence, for any
\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}
and for any
H\in[a,b]
,

\displaystyle{\cal I}(\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}})\leq\frac{c^{n-1}}{[(n-1)!]^{\frac{2H_{0}+a-1}{2H_{0}}}}(t\vee 1)^{\frac{n(2H_{0}+b-1)}{2H_{0}}}.

Returning to (
46
), we have:

\int_{[0,t]^{n}}\Big(\psi_{t,h,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})\Big)^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}\leq h^{\frac{\varepsilon}{H_{0}}}\frac{c^{n-1}}{[(n-1)!]^{\frac{a-1}{2H_{0}}}}(t\vee 1)^{\frac{n(2H_{0}+b-1)}{2H_{0}}}

Finally, coming back to (
43
), we obtain:

A_{n}^{H}(t,h)\leq h^{2\varepsilon}\frac{c^{n-1}}{[(n-1)!]^{a-1}}(t\vee 1)^{n(2H_{0}+b-1)}.

Consequently, for any
p\geq 2
,
t\in[0,T]
,
H\in[a,b]
and
\varepsilon
as in (
48
),

\sum_{n\geq 1}(p-1)^{n/2}\left(\frac{1}{n!}A_{n}^{H}(t,h)\right)^{1/2}\leq Ch^{\varepsilon}.

(50)

Study of
A_{n}^{H}(t,h)
(wave equation).
By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

A_{n}^{H}(t,h)\leq\Gamma_{0,t}^{n}\int_{[0,t]^{n}}\psi_{t,h,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}

(51)

with
\Gamma_{0,t}=2H_{0}t^{2H_{0}-1}
and the same function
\psi_{t,h,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})
as above.

To estimate
\psi_{t,h,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})
, we use again (
44
). The
d\eta_{j}
integrals for
j=1,\ldots,n-1
are computed using Lemma
4.1
. For the
d\eta_{n}
integral, we consider separately the cases
\alpha_{n}=0
and
\alpha_{n}=1-2H
.
If
\alpha_{n}=0
, by Plancherel theorem,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\mathcal{F}(G_{u_{n}+h}^{w}-G_{u_{n}}^{w})(\eta_{n})\big|^{2}d\eta_{n}=2\pi\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(1_{\{|x|<u_{n}+h\}}-1_{\{|x|<u_{n}\}}\right)^{2}d\eta_{n}=4\pi h,

(52)

and if
\alpha_{n}=1-2H
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\mathcal{F}(G_{u_{n}+h}^{w}-G_{u_{n}}^{w})(\eta_{n})\big|^{2}|\eta_{n}|^{1-2H}d\eta_{n}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\sin((u_{n}+h)|\eta_{n}|)-\sin(u_{n}|\eta_{n}|)|^{2}}{|\eta_{n}|^{2}}|\eta_{n}|^{1-2H}d\eta_{n}.

(We used the notation
G^{w}
here to emphasize that this calculation is valid only for the wave equation.)
We write the last integral as
I_{1}+I_{2}
, where
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
are the integrals over the the regions

|\eta_{n}|\leq 1
, respectively
|\eta_{n}|>1
. For
I_{1}
, we use that fact that

|\sin((u_{n}+h)|\eta_{n}|)-\sin(u_{n}|\eta_{n}|)|^{2}=4\sin^{2}\left(\frac{h|\eta_{n}|}{2}\right)\cos^{2}\left(\frac{(2u_{n}+h)|\eta_{n}|}{2}\right)\leq h^{2}|\eta_{n}|^{2}

and hence
I_{1}\leq h^{2}\int_{|\eta_{n}|\leq 1}|\eta_{n}|^{1-2H}d\eta_{n}=\frac{1}{1-H}h^{2}
. For
I_{2}
, writing
\sin(x)=\frac{e^{ix}-e^{-ix}}{2i}
we have

\displaystyle|\sin((u_{n}+h)|\eta_{n}|)-\sin(u_{n}|\eta_{n}|)|^{2}

\displaystyle=|e^{iu_{n}|\eta_{n}|}(e^{ih|\eta_{n}|}-1)-e^{-iu_{n}|\eta_{n}|}(e^{-ih|\eta_{n}|}-1)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(|(e^{ih|\eta_{n}|}-1|^{2}+|e^{-ih|\eta_{n}|}-1|^{2}\right)\leq 4h^{2\delta}|\eta_{n}|^{2\delta},

for any
\delta\in[0,1]
, using the fact that
|1-e^{ix}|^{2}\leq|x|^{2\delta}
for any
x>0
and
\delta\in[0,1]
.
Hence,
I_{2}\leq h^{2}\int_{|\eta_{n}|>1}|\eta_{n}|^{2\delta-2H-1}d\eta_{n}=\frac{1}{2H-2\delta}h^{2\delta}
,
for any
\delta\in(0,H)
. To ensure that this condition holds for all
H\in[a,b]
, we choose
\delta\in(0,a)
. We obtain that for any
H\in[a,b]

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\mathcal{F}(G_{u_{n}+h}^{w}-G_{u_{n}}^{w})(\eta_{n})\big|^{2}|\eta_{n}|^{1-2H}d\eta_{n}\leq\left(\frac{1}{1-b}+\frac{1}{2a-2\delta}\right)h^{2\delta}.

(53)

From (
52
) and (
53
), we obtain that for any
\alpha_{n}\in\{0,1-2H\}
,
H\in[a,b]
and
\delta\in(0,a)
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\mathcal{F}(G_{u_{n}+h}^{w}-G_{u_{n}}^{w})(\eta_{n})\big|^{2}|\eta_{n}|^{\alpha_{n}}d\eta_{n}\leq ch^{2\delta}.

Returning to (
44
), we obtain:

\psi_{t,h,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})\leq ch^{2\delta}c_{H}^{n}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}2^{1-\alpha_{j}}\widetilde{C}_{\alpha_{j}}u_{j}^{1-\alpha_{j}}.

Note that
\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}2^{1-\alpha_{j}}\widetilde{C}_{\alpha_{j}}\leq C_{H,1}^{k-1}
, where

C_{H,1}=\max\left\{\pi,\frac{\Gamma(1-2H)}{H},\frac{2\Gamma(2-4H)}{4H-1}\right\}.

(54)

Clearly,
c_{H}
and
C_{H,1}
can be bounded for all
H\in[a,b]
. Hence, recalling (
51
), we have:

\displaystyle A_{n}^{H}(t,h)\leq\int_{[0,t]^{n}}\psi_{t,h,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq h^{2\delta}c^{n-1}n!\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{1-\alpha_{j}}d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}

\displaystyle=h^{2\delta}c^{n-1}n!\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\Gamma(2-\alpha_{j})}{\Gamma(n(2H+1)+\alpha_{n})}\,t^{n(2H+1)+\alpha_{n}-1}

\displaystyle\leq h^{2\delta}c^{n-1}\frac{(t\vee 1)^{n(2b+1)-2a}}{(n!)^{2a}},

using the fact that
\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\Gamma(2-\alpha_{j})\leq 1
and

\Gamma(n(2H+1)+\alpha_{n})\geq\Gamma(n(2a+1))\geq c^{n}(n!)^{2a+1}
.

Consequently, for any
p\geq 2
,
t\in[0,T]
,
H\in[a,b]
and
\delta\in(0,a)
,

\sum_{n\geq 1}(p-1)^{n/2}\left(\frac{1}{n!}A_{n}^{H}(t,h)\right)^{1/2}\leq Ch^{\delta}.

Study of
B_{n}^{H}(t,h)
(heat equation).
By the Littlewood-Hardy inequality (
33
),

B_{n}^{H}(t,h)\leq b_{H_{0}}^{n}\left(\int_{[0,t+h]^{n}}\gamma_{t,h,n}^{(H)}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}1_{D_{t,h}}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})d\boldsymbol{t_{n}},\right)^{2H_{0}}

(55)

where
D_{t,h}=[0,t+h]^{n}\verb 2\2[0,t]^{n}
, and the function
\gamma_{t,h,n}^{(H)}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})
is defined as follows: if
\boldsymbol{t}_{n}\in[0,t]^{n}
and
\rho
is the permutation of
1,\ldots,n
such that
t_{\rho(1)}<\ldots<t_{\rho(n)}
and
t<t_{\rho(n)}<t+h
, we let

u_{j}=t_{\rho(j+1)}-t_{\rho(j)}
for all
j=1,\ldots,n-1

u_{n}=t-t_{\rho(n)}
, and

\displaystyle\gamma_{t,h,n}^{(H)}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})

\displaystyle:=c_{H}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}|\mathcal{F}G_{u_{j}}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j})|^{2}|\mathcal{F}G_{u_{n}+h}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{n})|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H}d\boldsymbol{\xi_{n}}.

Using the change of variables
\eta_{j}=\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,n
(with
\eta_{0}=0
) followed by inequality (
38
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\gamma_{t,h,n}^{(H)}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})

\displaystyle\leq c_{H}^{n}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{F}G_{u_{j}}(\eta_{j})|^{2}|\eta_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}d\eta_{j}\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{F}G_{u_{n}+h}(\eta_{j})|^{2}|\eta_{n}|^{\alpha_{n}}d\eta_{n}\right).

(56)

(We will the notation
G
here since we use this relation for both heat and wave equation.)

Using Lemma
4.1
, we obtain:

\gamma_{t,h,n}^{(H)}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})\leq c_{H}^{n}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{1+\alpha_{j}}{2}\right)u_{j}^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{j}}{2}}(u_{n}+h)^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{n}}{2}}.

As noted above,
\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{1+\alpha_{j}}{2}\right)\leq C_{H,1}^{n}
where
C_{H,1}
is given by (
45
) and is bounded for all
H\in[a,b]
. Taking power
\frac{1}{2H_{0}}
, we obtain:

\gamma_{t,h,n}^{(H)}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})\leq c^{n}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}u_{j}^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{j}}{4H_{0}}}(u_{n}+h)^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{n}}{4H_{0}}}.

Integrating over the set
D_{t,h}
, we obtain:

\int_{[0,t+h]^{n}}\gamma_{t,h,n}^{(H)}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}1_{D_{t,h}}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}\leq c^{n}n!\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}\int_{t}^{t+h}J^{h}(t_{n})(t+h-t_{n})^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{n}}{2}}dt_{n},

where

J^{h}(t_{n}):=\int_{T_{n-1}(t_{n})}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{n}}{4H_{0}}}d\boldsymbol{t_{n-1}}\leq\frac{c^{n-1}}{[(n-1)!]^{\frac{4H_{0}+2a-2}{4H_{0}}}}.

We conclude that

\int_{[0,t+h]^{n}}\gamma_{t,h,n}^{(H)}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}1_{D_{t,h}}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}\leq\frac{c^{n-1}}{[(n-1)!]^{\frac{2a-2}{4H_{0}}}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}\frac{1}{1-\frac{1+\alpha_{n}}{4H_{0}}}h^{1-\frac{1+\alpha_{n}}{4H_{0}}}.

Note that
(1-\frac{1+\alpha_{n}}{4H_{0}})^{-1}
is bounded by
\frac{2H_{0}}{2H_{0}+a-1}
for any
\alpha_{n}\in\{0,1-2H\}
and
H\in[a,b]
. Moreover, due to condition (
47
),

\frac{\varepsilon}{H_{0}}<\frac{2H_{0}+a-1}{2H_{0}}\leq 1-\frac{1+\alpha_{n}}{4H_{0}}\leq 1-\frac{1}{4H_{0}}.

Therefore,

\int_{[0,t+h]^{n}}\gamma_{t,h,n}^{(H)}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}1_{D_{t,h}}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}\leq h^{\frac{\varepsilon}{H_{0}}}\frac{c^{n-1}}{[(n-1)!]^{\frac{2a-2}{4H_{0}}}}.

Returning to (
55
), we infer that

B_{n}(t,h)\leq h^{2\varepsilon}\frac{c^{n-1}}{[(n-1)!]^{a-1}}
,
and hence, for any
p\geq 2
,
t\in[0,T]
and
\varepsilon
satisfying (
47
) (and in particular, for any
\varepsilon
chosen as in (
48
)),

\sum_{n\geq 1}(p-1)^{n/2}\left(\frac{1}{n!}B_{n}(t,h)\right)^{1/2}\leq Ch^{\varepsilon}.

Study of
B_{n}^{H}(t,h)
(wave equation).
By the Cauchy-Schwarz equation,

B_{n}^{H}(t,h)\leq\Gamma_{0,t}^{n}\int_{[0,t+h]^{n}}\gamma_{t,h,n}^{(H)}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})1_{D_{t,h}}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})d\boldsymbol{t_{n}},

(57)

where the function
\gamma_{t,h,n}^{(H)}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})

is defined as above. As mentioned above, to estimate
\gamma_{t,h,n}^{(H)}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})
we use (
56
), which holds also for the wave equation. Using Lemma
4.1
, we obtain:

\gamma_{t,h,n}^{(H)}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})\leq c_{H}^{n}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}2^{1-\alpha_{j}}\widetilde{C}_{\alpha_{j}}u^{1-\alpha_{j}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}u_{j}^{1-\alpha_{j}}(u_{n}+h)^{1-\alpha_{n}}.

As noticed above,
\prod_{j=1}^{n}2^{1-\alpha_{j}}\widetilde{C}_{\alpha_{j}}\leq C_{H,1}^{n}
, where
C_{H,1}
is given by (
45
), and the constants
c_{H}
and
C_{H,1}
are uniformly bounded in
H\in[a,b]
. Hence,

\int_{[0,t+h]^{n}}\gamma_{t,h,n}^{(H)}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})1_{D_{t,h}}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}\leq c^{n}n!\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}\int_{t}^{t+h}J^{w}(t_{n})(t+h-t_{n})^{1-\alpha_{n}}dt_{n},

where

J^{w}(t_{n}):=\int_{T_{n-1}(t_{n})}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{1-\alpha_{j}}d\boldsymbol{t_{n-1}}\leq\frac{c^{n-1}}{[(n-1)!]^{2a+1}}.

(58)

We conclude that

\int_{[0,t+h]^{n}}\gamma_{t,h,n}^{(H)}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})1_{D_{t,h}}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}\leq\frac{c^{n-1}}{[(n-1)!]^{2a}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}\frac{1}{2-\alpha_{n}}h^{2-\alpha_{n}}.

Note that
(2-\alpha_{n})^{-1}
is bounded by
2
for any
\alpha_{n}\in\{0,1-2H\}
and
H\in[a,b]
. Moreover, for any
h\in(0,T)
and
\delta\in(0,a)
,
h^{2-\alpha_{n}}\leq ch^{2a+1}\leq ch^{2\delta+1}\leq ch^{2\delta}
.
Therefore,

B_{n}^{H}(t,h)\leq\int_{[0,t+h]^{n}}\gamma_{t,h,n}^{(H)}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})1_{D_{t,h}}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}\leq h^{2\delta}\frac{c^{n-1}}{[(n-1)!]^{2a}}.

Consequently, for any
p\geq 2
,
t\in[0,T]
,
H\in[a,b]
and
\delta\in(0,a)
,

\sum_{n\geq 1}(p-1)^{n/2}\left(\frac{1}{n!}B_{n}^{H}(t,h)\right)^{1/2}\leq ch^{\delta}.

Step 2. (space increments)
Note that for any
x,z\in\mathbb{R}
,

\|u(t,x+z)-u(t,x)\|_{p}\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}(p-1)^{n/2}\left(\frac{1}{n!}C_{n}^{H}(t,z)\right)^{1/2},

where
C_{n}^{H}(t,z)=(n!)^{2}\|\widetilde{f}_{n}(\cdot,t,x+z)-\widetilde{f}_{n}(\cdot,t,x)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2}
.
We study separately
C_{n}^{H}(t,z)
, for the heat equation and for the wave equation.

Study of
C_{n}^{H}(t,z)
(heat equation).

By the Littlewood-Hardy inequality (
33
),

C_{n}^{H}(t,z)\leq b_{H_{0}}^{2}\left(\int_{[0,t]^{n}}\psi_{t,z,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}d\boldsymbol{t_{n}})\right)^{2H_{0}},

(59)

where the function
\psi_{t,z,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})
is defined as follows: if
\boldsymbol{t_{n}}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in[0,t]^{n}
and
\rho
is the permutation such that
t_{\rho(1)}<\ldots<t_{\rho(n)}
, we let
u_{j}=t_{\rho(j+1)}-t_{\rho(j)}
(with
t_{\rho(n+1)}=t
) and

\displaystyle\psi_{t,z,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})=c_{H}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\mathcal{F}G_{u_{j}}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j})|^{2}|1-e^{-i(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{n})z}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H}d\boldsymbol{\xi_{n}}.

Using the inequality
|1-e^{ix}|^{2}\leq|x|^{2\delta}
for any
\delta\in[0,1]
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, followed by the change of variables
\eta_{j}=\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,n
(with
\eta_{0}=0
) and inequality (
38
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\psi_{t,z,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})

\displaystyle\leq|z|^{2\delta}c_{H}^{n}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{F}G_{u_{j}}(\eta_{j})|^{2}|\eta_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}d\eta_{j}\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{F}G_{u_{n}}(\eta_{n})|^{2}|\eta_{n}|^{\alpha_{n}+2\delta}d\eta_{n}\right).

(60)

(We will use (
60
) for both heat and wave equations.) Using Lemma
4.1
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\psi_{t,z,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})

\displaystyle\leq|z|^{2\delta}c_{H}^{n}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{1+\alpha_{j}}{2}\right)u_{j}^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{j}}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{1+\alpha_{n}+2\delta}{2}\right)u_{n}^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{n}+2\delta}{2}}.

As noticed above,
\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{1+\alpha_{j}}{2}\right)\leq c^{n-1}
. Moreover, since
\frac{1+\alpha_{n}+2\delta}{2}\in(\frac{1}{2},H)
, we have
\Gamma\left(\frac{1+\alpha_{n}+2\delta}{2}\right)\leq\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)
. Hence,

\displaystyle\psi_{t,z,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})\leq|z|^{2\delta}c^{n}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{H}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(t_{\rho(j+1)}-t_{\rho(j)})^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{j}}{2}}(t-t_{\rho(n)})^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{n}+2\delta}{2}}.

Taking power
\frac{1}{2H_{0}}
and integrating over
[0,t]^{n}
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\int_{[0,t]^{n}}\psi_{t,z,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq|z|^{\frac{\delta}{H_{0}}}n!\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{H}}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{j}}{4H_{0}}}(t-t_{n})^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{n}+2\delta}{4H_{0}}}.

Note that the sum appearing in the previous estimate is the same as in (
46
) in which we replace
\varepsilon
by
\delta/2
in the exponent of
t-t_{n}
.
Exactly as (
50
), we deduce that

\sum_{n\geq 1}(p-1)^{n/2}\left(\frac{1}{n!}C_{n}^{H}(t,z)\right)^{1/2}\leq C|z|^{\delta}.

Study of
C_{n}^{H}(t,z)
(wave equation).
By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

C_{n}^{H}(t,z)\leq\Gamma_{0,t}^{n}\int_{[0,t]^{n}}\psi_{t,z,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})d\boldsymbol{t_{n}},

with
\Gamma_{0,t}=2H_{0}t^{2H_{0}-1}
and the same function
\psi_{t,z,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})
as above. To estimate
\psi_{t,z,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})
, we use (
60
) and Lemma
4.1
. We obtain:

\displaystyle\psi_{t,z,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})\leq|z|^{2\delta}c_{H}^{n}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(2^{1-\alpha_{j}}\widetilde{C}_{\alpha_{j}}u_{j}^{1-\alpha_{j}}\right)\cdot\left(2^{1-\alpha_{n}-2\delta}\widetilde{C}_{\alpha_{n}+2\delta}u_{n}^{1-\alpha_{n}-2\delta}.\right)

For the application of Lemma
4.1
, we need
\alpha_{n}+2\delta<1
, which introduces the restriction
0<\delta<H
when
\alpha_{n}=1-2H
. To ensure that this holds for all
H\in[a,b]
, we take
0<\delta<a
. As noticed above,

\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}2^{1-\alpha_{j}}\widetilde{C}_{\alpha_{j}}\leq c^{n-1}
. Moreover, since
\alpha_{n}+2\delta\in(0,1)
,

\widetilde{C}_{\alpha_{n}+2\delta}=\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{n}-2\delta}\Gamma(\alpha_{n}+2\delta)\sin\frac{\pi(\alpha_{n}+2\delta)}{2}.

We study the range of values of
\alpha_{n}+2\delta
. If
\alpha_{n}=1-2H
, then
\alpha_{n}+2\delta\geq 1-2H\geq 1-2b
and
\Gamma(\alpha_{n}+2\delta)\leq\Gamma(1-2b)
. The problem is when
\alpha_{n}=0
since in this case
\alpha_{n}+2\delta=2\delta
may be close to
0
. To avoid this problem, we fix an arbitrary value
c_{1}>0
, and we choose
\delta\in(\frac{c_{1}}{2},a)
. Then
\Gamma(\alpha_{n}+2\delta)\leq\max\{\Gamma(1-2b),\Gamma(c_{1})\}
and
\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{n}-2\delta}\leq\frac{1}{2a-c_{1}}
. Hence,

\psi_{t,z,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})\leq|z|^{2\delta}c^{n}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(t_{\rho(j+1)}-t_{\rho(j)})^{1-\alpha_{j}}(t-t_{\rho(n)})^{1-\alpha_{n}-2\delta}.

Integrating over
[0,t]^{n}
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\int_{[0,t]^{n}}\psi_{t,z,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq|z|^{2\delta}c^{n-1}n!\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{1-\alpha_{j}}(t-t_{n})^{1-\alpha_{n}-2\delta}d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}

\displaystyle=|z|^{2\delta}c^{n-1}n!\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha_{n}}\in D_{n}^{(H)}}\int_{0}^{t}J^{w}(t_{n})(t-t_{n})^{1-\alpha_{n}-2\delta}dt_{n},

where
J^{w}
is defined and estimated in (
58
). Using this estimate for
J^{w}
, we see that:

\int_{[0,t]^{n}}\psi_{t,z,n}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}})d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}\leq|z|^{2\delta}c^{n-1}\frac{1}{[(n-1)!]^{2a}}.

We conclude that

\sum_{n\geq 1}(p-1)^{n/2}\left(\frac{1}{n!}C_{n}^{H}(t,z)\right)^{1/2}\leq c|z|^{\delta}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
2.2
:

Step 1. (finite dimensional convergence)
In this step, we prove that:

\big(u^{H_{n}}(t_{1},x_{1}),\ldots,u^{H_{n}}(t_{k},x_{k})\big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}\big(u^{H^{*}}(t_{1},x_{1}),\ldots,u^{H^{*}}(t_{k},x_{k})\big),

for any
(t_{1},x_{1}),\ldots,(t_{k},x_{k})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
. It is enough to prove that for any
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
,
u^{H_{n}}(t,x)\to u^{H^{*}}(t,x)
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
n\to\infty
.

As in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we approximate
u^{H}(t,x)
by the partial sum:

u_{m}^{H}(t,x)=1+\sum_{k=1}^{m}I_{n}^{H}(f_{t,x,k}).

Then
u_{m}^{H}(t,x)\to u^{H}(t,x)\to 0
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
m\to\infty
. Moreover, by Lemma
4.2
, for any
m\geq 1
fixed,

u_{m}^{H_{n}}(t,x)\to u_{m}^{H*}(t,x)
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
n\to\infty
. So, it remains to prove that
\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}|u_{m}^{H_{n}}(t,x)-u^{H}(t,x)|^{2}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
. We choose values
a
and
b
such that (
37
) holds. Since
H_{n}\to H^{*}
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a<H_{n}<b
for all
n\geq N
. Therefore, it suffices to prove that:

\sup_{H\in[a,b]}\mathbb{E}|u_{m}^{H}(t,x)-u^{H}(t,x)|^{2}=\sup_{H\in[a,b]}\sum_{k\geq m+1}\mathbb{E}|I_{k}^{H}(f_{t,x,k})|^{2}\to 0\quad\mbox{as $m\to\infty$}.

(61)

To prove (
61
), we need to estimate
\mathbb{E}|I_{k}^{H}(f_{t,x,k})|^{2}
. Although these estimates exist in the literature, we revisit them here since we are interested in a uniform bound in
H
.
First, note that by Littlewood-Hardy inequality (
33
),

\mathbb{E}|I_{k}^{H}(f_{t,x,k})|^{2}=k!\,\|\widetilde{f}_{t,x,k}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{H}^{\otimes k}}^{2}\leq k!\,b_{0}^{k}\left(\int_{[0,t]^{k}}A_{k}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}\right)^{2H_{0}},

(62)

where
A_{k}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})=c_{H}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}|\mathcal{F}\widetilde{f}_{t,x,k}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}},\bullet)(\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}})|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H}d\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}
.

Next, we would like to find an upper bound for
A_{k}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})
.
The major difference compared with the regular case is that in the rough case, we cannot apply Lemma
3.1
. Instead, we will use the common technique which consists of the change of variables
\eta_{j}=\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,k
(with
\eta_{0}=0
), followed by inequality (
38
) for bounding a product with a sum. In the application of (
38
) below, we let
D_{k}^{(H)}
be the set of multi-indices
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})
with
\alpha_{j}=(1-2H)a_{j}
and
\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\in A_{k}
.
Hence, if
\boldsymbol{t_{k}}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})\in[0,t]^{k}
and
\rho
is a permutation of
1,\ldots,k
such that
t_{\rho(1)}<\ldots<t_{\rho(k)}
, with
t_{\rho(k+1)}=t
, then

\displaystyle A_{k}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{H}^{k}}{(k!)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\mathcal{F}G_{t_{\rho(j+1)}-t_{\rho(j)}}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j})|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{1-2H}d\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{H}^{k}}{(k!)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\mathcal{F}G_{t_{\rho(j+1)}-t_{\rho(j)}}(\eta_{j})|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\eta_{j}-\eta_{j-1}|^{1-2H}d\boldsymbol{\xi_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{H}^{k}}{(k!)^{2}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in D_{k}^{(H)}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{F}G_{t_{\rho(j+1)}-t_{\rho(j)}}(\eta_{j})|^{2}|\eta_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}d\eta_{j}\right).

The integrals above are evaluated using Lemma
4.1
. We obtain:

\displaystyle A_{k}^{H}(\boldsymbol{t_{k}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{H}^{k}}{(k!)^{2}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in D_{k}^{(H)}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}h(\alpha_{j})(t_{\rho(j+1)}-t_{\rho(j)})^{\varphi(\alpha_{j})},

where

h(\alpha_{j})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Gamma((1+\alpha_{j})/2)&\mbox{for heat equation}\\
2^{1-\alpha_{j}}\widetilde{C}_{\alpha_{j}}&\mbox{for wave equation}\end{array}\right.\quad\varphi(\alpha_{j})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-(1+\alpha_{j})/2&\mbox{for heat equation}\\
1-\alpha_{j}&\mbox{for wave equation}\end{array}\right.

Since
\alpha_{j}\in\{0,1-2H,2(1-2H)\}
,
h(\alpha_{j})\leq C_{H,1}
, where the constant
C_{H,1}
is given by
(
45
) for the heat equation, respectively (
54
) for the wave equation.
Coming back to (
62
), we obtain that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|I_{k}^{H}(f_{t,x,k})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq k!\frac{c_{H}^{k}}{(k!)^{2}}b_{H_{0}}^{k}C_{H,1}^{k}\left[k!\int_{T_{k}(t)}\left(\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in D_{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{\varphi(\alpha_{j})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2H_{0}}}d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}\right]^{2H_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq(k!)^{2H_{0}-1}c_{H}^{k}b_{H_{0}}^{k}C_{H,1}^{k}\left[\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in D_{k}}\int_{T_{k}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{\frac{\varphi(\alpha_{j})}{2H_{0}}}d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}\right]^{2H_{0}}.

The last integral is calculated using Lemma
3.2
: in the case of the heat equation, this integral is:

\int_{T_{k}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{-\frac{1+\alpha_{j}}{4H_{0}}}d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k}\Gamma(-\frac{1+\alpha_{j}}{4H_{0}}+1)}{\Gamma(k\frac{2H_{0}+H-1}{2H_{0}}+1)}t^{k\frac{2H_{0}+H-1}{2H_{0}}}\leq C_{H,2}^{k}\frac{t^{k\frac{2H_{0}+H-1}{2H_{0}}}}{\Gamma(k\frac{2H_{0}+H-1}{2H_{0}}+1)},

and in the case of the wave equation, the integral is:

\int_{T_{k}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{\frac{1-\alpha_{j}}{2H_{0}}}d\boldsymbol{t_{k}}=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k}\Gamma(\frac{1-\alpha_{j}}{2H_{0}}+1)}{\Gamma(k\frac{H_{0}+H}{H_{0}}+1)}t^{k\frac{H_{0}+H}{H_{0}}}\leq C_{H,2}^{k}\frac{t^{k\frac{H_{0}+H}{H_{0}}}}{\Gamma(k\frac{H_{0}+H}{H_{0}}+1)},

where the constant
C_{H,2}
is given by:

C_{H,2}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\max\{\Gamma(1-\frac{1}{4H_{0}}),\Gamma(1-\frac{1-H}{2H_{0}}),\Gamma(1-\frac{3-4H}{4H_{0}}))\}&\mbox{for heat equation}\\
\max\{\Gamma(1+\frac{1}{2H_{0}}),\Gamma(1+\frac{H}{H_{0}}),\Gamma(1+\frac{4H-1}{2H_{0}}))\}&\mbox{for wave equation}\end{array}\right.

It is not difficult to see that these expressions can be uniformly bounded for all
H\in[a,b]
. This concludes the proof of (
61
).

Step 2. (tightness)
The fact that
(u^{H_{n}})_{n\geq 1}

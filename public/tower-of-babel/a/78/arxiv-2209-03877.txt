Few-photon transport via a multimode nonlinear cavity: theory and applications
Yunkai Wang, Kejie Fang

Abstract
Few-photon transport via waveguide-coupled local quantum systems has attracted extensive theoretical and experimental studies. Most of the study has focused on atomic or atomic-like local quantum systems due to their strong light-matter interaction useful for quantum applications. Here, we study few-photon transport via a waveguide-coupled multimode optical cavity with second-order bulk nonlinearity. We develop a Feynman diagram approach and compute the scattering matrix of the one- and two-photon transport. Based on the calculated scattering matrix, we show highly nonclassical photonic effects, including photon blockade and π − \pi- conditional phase shift, are achievable in the waveguide-coupled multimode optical cavity system via quantum interference and linear response engineering. Our results might lead to significant applications of quantum photonic circuits in all-optical quantum information processing and quantum network protocols.



I
Introduction

The architecture of waveguide-coupled local quantum systems enables coupling flying photons with stationary qubits for light-matter interactions. A prime example is the waveguide quantum electrodynamics, with several types of local quantum systems demonstrated successfully, including trapped atoms
Tiecke et al. 2014
;
Shomroni et al. 2014
;
Volz et al. 2014
, solid state defects
Javadi et al. 2015
;
Sipahigil et al. 2016
, and superconducting qubits
Mirhosseini et al. 2018
;
Kannan et al. 2020
. This architecture is also relevant to quantum networks where optic fibers link local quantum nodes for routing, storage, and processing of quantum information encoded in individual photons
Simon 2017
. In parallel, a large body of theoretical work has been carried out to study few-photon transport in the setting of waveguide-coupled local quantum systems
Chang et al. 2007
;
Shen and Fan 2007
;
Liao et al. 2010
;
Zheng et al. 2010
;
Rephaeli and Fan 2012
;
Xu and Fan 2015
. Most of the theoretical and experimental work, though, has focused on qubit-like quantum entities, which are sought to provide strong light-matter interactions for quantum information tasks.

Here, we study few-photon transport via a waveguide-coupled multimode optical cavity with second-order bulk nonlinearity. This is a common device setup used for cavity-enhanced parametric nonlinear optical processes including second-harmonic generation and parametric down-conversion
Lake et al. 2016
;
Guo et al. 2017
;
Luo et al. 2018
;
Chang et al. 2019
;
Zhang et al. 2019
;
Lukin et al. 2020
;
Lu et al. 2021
;
Zhao and Fang 2021
. However, its property at the few-photon level has not been fully explored, because bulk nonlinearity is generally believed to be insubstantial to impact single photons. We challenge this perspective by investigating the non-parametric few-photon transport and its physical observations. Besides using a systematic method from Ref.
Xu and Fan 2015
to compute the scattering matrix (
S-
matrix) of few-photon transport, we develop a Feynman diagram approach which provides further physical insight into the obtained
S-
matrix while offering mathematical simplicity in its computation. This method is particularly suited for weak nonlinear systems where only leading order Feynman diagrams are of interest. Exploiting the result of the
S-
matrix, we then study observational effects of few-photon transport via the waveguide-coupled multimode cavity, including photon blockade, conditional phase shift, and non-classical two-mode correlations. Surprisingly, while these effects are commonly believed to be associated with strong light-matter interaction, we find they could become substantial for waveguide-coupled optical cavities with only weak nonlinearity.

These results reveal a general approach for creation and control of few-photon correlations via quantum interference and linear response engineering. The few-photon transport amplitude of waveguide-coupled local quantum systems is a superposition of the interaction-free, i.e., linear, and interaction-mediated amplitudes. When the linear transmission coefficient is comparable to the interaction-mediated amplitude, quantum interference between the two pathways leads to highly nonclassical correlations between the propagating photons. For weak nonlinear systems, this might need a tuned linear transmission which could be achieved, for example, in a one-port waveguide-cavity configuration. However, this approach is distinct from the post-selection method where photon-photon correlations are induced by the measurement. Our result might lead to significant applications in quantum information science using quantum photonic circuits beyond the parametric regime, including quantum nondemolition measurement of photons
Xia et al. 2016
, two-photon quantum logic gates
Langford et al. 2011
, and nonlinearity-assisted entanglement swapping
Sangouard et al. 2011
.

II
Scattering matrix of few-photon transport

The system under consideration is a waveguide-coupled trimodal optical cavity made from
\chi^{(2)}
materials. A typical realization of such an optical cavity is a microring resonator (Fig.
1
a) which supports traveling-wave resonances. These resonances couple via the
\chi^{(2)}
nonlinearity when frequency-matching and, if necessary, phase-matching conditions are satisfied. Photons couple in and out of these resonances via the waveguide and meanwhile dissipate in other loss channels. In the Heisenberg-Langevin framework, this open quantum system can be modeled by the following effective Hamiltonian (
\hbar=1
)

H_{\textrm{eff}}=(\omega_{1}-i\frac{\kappa_{1}}{2})a^{\dagger}_{1}a_{1}+(\omega_{2}-i\frac{\kappa_{2}}{2})a^{\dagger}_{2}a_{2}+(\omega_{3}-i\frac{\kappa_{3}}{2})a^{\dagger}_{3}a_{3}+g(a_{1}a_{2}a^{\dagger}_{3}+a^{\dagger}_{1}a^{\dagger}_{2}a_{3}),

(1)

where
\omega_{j}
and
\kappa_{j}
are the frequency and photon loss rate of the
j
-th resonance, respectively, and
g
is the trimodal coupling coefficient. For simplicity, we define a complex frequency
\alpha_{j}\equiv\omega_{j}-i\frac{\kappa_{j}}{2}
. For the trimodal interaction to be resonantly enhanced, the three modes need to satisfy the frequency-matching condition
\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}\approx\omega_{3}
. The cavity photon loss includes both leakage into the waveguide and intrinsic losses due to, for example, material absorption and surface scattering, i.e.,
\kappa_{j}=\kappa_{je}+\kappa_{ji}
, where
\kappa_{je}
is the cavity-waveguide coupling rate and
\kappa_{ji}
is the intrinsic photon loss rate. Depending on the configuration of the waveguide-cavity coupling, the relation between the states of the outgoing and incoming photons in the waveguide is determined by the input-output formalism
Walls and Milburn 2007
. In this paper, we specifically consider the case of a cavity side-coupled to a waveguide unidirectionally—typical for ring resonators—which has the following input-output relation of the operators,

a_{\textrm{out},j}(t)=a_{\textrm{in},j}(t)-i\sqrt{\kappa_{je}}a_{j}(t).

(2)

It turns out such one-port cavity-waveguide configuration is critical for realizing strong quantum correlations between propagating waveguide photons coupled via a weak nonlinear optical cavity. However, the computation method developed here can be straightforwardly generalized to other waveguide-cavity configurations. For the calculation below, we also assume a linear dispersion of the waveguide, which is justified given the linewidth of the cavity, and thus the operation bandwidth of the system, is much smaller than the cavity frequency
Fan et al. 2010
.

The goal of this section is to calculate the scattering matrix (
S
-matrix) of the one- and two-photon transport via the waveguide-coupled trimodal
\chi^{(2)}
cavity (Fig.
1
b-d). We first use a systematic method from Ref.
Xu and Fan 2015
and apply it to the present case of a multi-mode
\chi^{(2)}
cavity. We also develop a Feynman diagram approach which provides further physical insight of the photon transport while lessen the computation complexity associated with the non-perturbative method of Ref.
Xu and Fan 2015
, especially for weak nonlinear systems.

a_{1}
,
a_{2}
, and
a_{3}
modes/photons, respectively.

. Illustrations of few-photon transport processes.

denotes the single-photon state with momentum

II.1
One-to-one-photon transport

We first consider the transport of a single photon (Fig.
1
b). The amplitude of transport of an incoming photon at time
t^{\prime}
to an outgoing photon at time
t
is given by the time-domain
S-
matrix

S_{t;t^{\prime}}\equiv\bra{0}a_{\textrm{out},1}(t)a_{\textrm{in},1}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})\ket{0},

(3)

where the photon is assumed to be in resonant with the
a_{1}
mode.
As shown in Ref.
Xu and Fan 2015
, the time-domain
S-
matrix is related to the Green’s function of the local system, i.e., here the nonlinear optical cavity, by applying Eq.
2
and the quantum causality condition

\displaystyle\left[a(t),I\left(t^{\prime}\right)\right]=\left[a^{\dagger}(t),I\left(t^{\prime}\right)\right]=0,\quad\text{ for }\quad t\leqslant t^{\prime},

(4)

\displaystyle\left[a(t),O\left(t^{\prime}\right)\right]=\left[a^{\dagger}(t),O\left(t^{\prime}\right)\right]=0,\quad\text{ for }\quad t\geqslant t^{\prime},

(5)

where
I(O)\left(t^{\prime}\right)
is a shorthand notation for the input(output) operators that represent either
a_{\text{in(out) }}\left(t^{\prime}\right)
or
a_{\text{in(out)}}^{\dagger}\left(t^{\prime}\right)
. Thus, the single-photon
S
-matrix becomes

S_{t;t^{\prime}}=\delta(t-t^{\prime})-\kappa_{1e}G(t;t^{\prime}),

(6)

where
G(t;t^{\prime})=\bra{0}\hat{T}[a_{1}(t)a_{1}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})]\ket{0}
is the two-point Green’s function,
\hat{T}
is the time ordering operator and we have also used
[a_{\textrm{in(out)}}(t),a_{\textrm{in(out)}}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})]=\delta(t-t^{\prime})
in the derivation.

The Green’s function of the local quantum system is calculated using operators time-evolved according to the effective Hamiltonian of
1

Xu and Fan 2015
. Thus, we proceed

\displaystyle G(t;t^{\prime})

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bra{0}e^{iH_{\textrm{eff}}t}a_{1}e^{-iH_{\textrm{eff}}t}e^{iH_{\textrm{eff}}t^{\prime}}a^{\dagger}_{1}e^{-iH_{\textrm{eff}}t^{\prime}}\ket{0}\theta(t-t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i\alpha_{1}(t^{\prime}-t)}\theta(t-t^{\prime}),

where
\theta(t-t^{\prime})
is the Heaviside step function.

The frequency-domain
S
-matrix quantifying the transport amplitude between output and input photons with fixed frequency or momentum is given by the Fourier transform of the time-domain
S
-matrix. For the single-photon transport,

\displaystyle S_{p_{1};k_{1}}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathscr{F}\left\{S_{t;t^{\prime}}\right\}

(8)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{dt}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{ip_{1}t}\int\frac{dt^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{ik_{1}t^{\prime}}S_{t;t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\delta(p_{1}-k_{1})-\kappa_{1e}G(p_{1};k_{1}),

where
G(p_{1};k_{1})
is the Fourier transform of the Green’s function of
7
. This definition of Fourier transform leads to
\bra{k}\ket{k'}=\delta(k-k^{\prime})
given
\bra{t}\ket{t'}=\delta(t-t^{\prime})
, where
\ket{k}\equiv\mathscr{F}\left\{\ket{t}\right\}
. The momentum-space Green’s function thus is

G(p_{1};k_{1})=\frac{i}{k_{1}-\alpha_{1}}\delta(p_{1}-k_{1}).

(9)

As a result, we have

S_{p_{1};k_{1}}=\left[1-\frac{i\kappa_{1e}}{k_{1}-\alpha_{1}}\right]\delta(p_{1}-k_{1})\equiv t_{k_{1}}\delta(p_{1}-k_{1}),

(10)

where
t_{k_{1}}
is the single-photon transmission coefficient. The derivation above straightforwardly applies to transport of a single photon in resonant with other resonances. For the rest of the paper, we use the subscript
j
in the momentum to indicate photons in resonant with the
j
-th resonance.

II.2
Two-to-one-photon transport and vice versa

Next, we consider the transport of two
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
photons to one
a_{3}
photon enabled by the
\chi^{(2)}
cavity (Fig.
1
c). This corresponds to the sum-of-frequency process in nonlinear optics. Following a similar procedure, the time-domain
S
-matrix of this process is found to be

\displaystyle S_{t_{3};t_{1}^{\prime}t_{2}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\equiv\bra{0}a_{\textrm{out},3}(t_{3})a_{\textrm{in},1}^{\dagger}(t_{1}^{\prime})a_{\textrm{in},2}^{\dagger}(t_{2}^{\prime})\ket{0}

(11)

\displaystyle=i\sqrt{\kappa_{1e}\kappa_{2e}\kappa_{3e}}\bra{0}\hat{T}[a_{3}(t_{3})a_{1}^{\dagger}(t_{1}^{\prime})a_{2}^{\dagger}(t_{2}^{\prime})]\ket{0}

\displaystyle=i\sqrt{\kappa_{1e}\kappa_{2e}\kappa_{3e}}\left(\bra{0}a_{3}(t_{3})a^{\dagger}_{1}(t_{1}^{\prime})a^{\dagger}_{2}(t_{2}^{\prime})\ket{0}\theta(t_{3}-t_{1}^{\prime})\theta(t_{1}^{\prime}-t_{2}^{\prime})+\bra{0}a_{3}(t_{3})a^{\dagger}_{2}(t_{2}^{\prime})a^{\dagger}_{1}(t_{1}^{\prime})\ket{0}\theta(t_{3}-t_{2}^{\prime})\theta(t_{2}^{\prime}-t_{1}^{\prime})\right).

We introduce
\ket{mnr}
to denote the state with
m
,
n
, and
r
photons in mode
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
, and
a_{3}
, respectively. One key property of the
\chi^{2}
trimodal system is that
\{\ket{100}\}
,
\{\ket{010}\}
, and
\{\ket{110},\ket{001}\}
form three closed subspaces of the effective Hamiltonian of
1
. Based on this property, we proceed to compute Eq.
11
:

\displaystyle\bra{0}a_{3}(t_{3})a^{\dagger}_{1}(t_{1}^{\prime})a^{\dagger}_{2}(t_{2}^{\prime})\ket{0}

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bra{000}a_{3}(t_{3})\left(\ket{001}\bra{001}+\ket{110}\bra{110}\right)a_{1}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime}_{1})\ket{010}\bra{010}a_{2}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime}_{2})\ket{000}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\bra{001}e^{-iH_{\textrm{eff}}t_{3}}\ket{001}\bra{001}e^{iH_{\textrm{eff}}t_{1}^{\prime}}\ket{110}+\bra{001}e^{-iH_{\textrm{eff}}t_{3}}\ket{110}\bra{110}e^{iH_{\textrm{eff}}t_{1}^{\prime}}\ket{110}\right)e^{-i\alpha_{2}(t_{1}^{\prime}-t_{2}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g}{\sqrt{\Delta\alpha^{2}+4g^{2}}}e^{-i\alpha_{2}(t_{1}^{\prime}-t_{2}^{\prime})}(e^{i\lambda_{2}(t_{1}^{\prime}-t_{3})}-e^{i\lambda_{1}(t_{1}^{\prime}-t_{3})}),

where
\Delta\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{3}
and
\lambda_{1,2}=\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3})\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\Delta\alpha^{2}+4g^{2}}
are the eigenvalues of
H_{\textrm{eff}}
in the subspace spanned by
\ket{110}
and
\ket{001}
, i.e.,

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}&g\\
g&\alpha_{3}\end{array}\right].

(13)

The result of
\bra{0}a_{3}(t_{3})a^{\dagger}_{2}(t_{2}^{\prime})a^{\dagger}_{1}(t_{1}^{\prime})\ket{0}
is obtained by exchanging the subscripts in Eq.
12
. Finally, the momentum-space
S-
matrix,
S_{p_{3};k_{1}k_{2}}\equiv\mathscr{F}\left\{S_{t_{3};t_{1}^{\prime}t_{2}^{\prime}}\right\}
, is given by

S_{p_{3};k_{1}k_{2}}\equiv i\sqrt{\kappa_{1e}\kappa_{2e}\kappa_{3e}}G(p_{3};k_{1},k_{2})=-ig\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{1e}\kappa_{2e}\kappa_{3e}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{(k_{1}+k_{2}-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})}{(k_{1}-\alpha_{1})(k_{2}-\alpha_{2})(k_{1}+k_{2}-\lambda_{1})(k_{1}+k_{2}-\lambda_{2})}\delta(p_{3}-k_{1}-k_{2}),

(14)

where
G(p_{3};k_{1},k_{2})
is the momentum-space Green’s function.

For the degenerate case, i.e.,
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
modes are the same mode, the matrix form of
H_{\textrm{eff}}
in the subspace spanned by
\ket{110}
(i.e.,
\ket{20}
) and
\ket{001}
is given by

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}2\alpha_{1}&\sqrt{2}g\\
\sqrt{2}g&\alpha_{3}\end{array}\right].

(15)

After a similar derivation, we obtain

S_{p_{3};k_{1}k_{2}}=-2ig\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{1e}^{2}\kappa_{3e}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{(k_{1}+k_{2}-2\alpha_{1})}{(k_{1}-\alpha_{1})(k_{2}-\alpha_{1})(k_{1}+k_{2}-\lambda^{\prime}_{1})(k_{1}+k_{2}-\lambda^{\prime}_{2})}\delta(p_{3}-k_{1}-k_{2}),

(16)

where
\lambda^{\prime}_{1,2}=\frac{1}{2}(2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{3})\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(2\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3})^{2}+8g^{2}}
are the eigenvalues of the matrix of
15
.
It is worth pointing out that
\lambda_{1,2}
(
\lambda^{\prime}_{1,2}
) are functions of the trimodal coupling coefficient
g
, and thus Eqs.
14
and
16
are non-perturbation result in terms of
g
, contributed by all virtual processes. These processes involving creation and annihilation of virtual photons will be revealed using the Feynman diagram approach in Section
III
.

The reverse process of one-to-two-photon transport has an
S-
matrix

S_{t_{1}^{\prime}t_{2}^{\prime};t_{3}}\equiv\bra{0}a_{\textrm{out},1}(t_{1}^{\prime})a_{\textrm{out},2}(t_{2}^{\prime})a^{\dagger}_{\textrm{in},3}(t_{3})\ket{0},

(17)

whose Fourier transform satisfies

S_{k_{1}k_{2};p_{3}}\equiv\mathscr{F}\left\{S_{t_{1}^{\prime}t_{2}^{\prime};t_{3}}\right\}=S_{p_{3};k_{1}k_{2}}.

(18)

II.3
Two-to-two-photon transport

Last, we consider the process of transport of two
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
photons to two
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
photons, without conversion to
a_{3}
photons (Fig.
1
d). We again start from the time-domain
S
-matrix

\displaystyle S_{t_{1}t_{2};t^{\prime}_{1}t^{\prime}_{2}}

\displaystyle\equiv\bra{0}a_{\textrm{out},1}(t_{1})a_{\textrm{out},2}(t_{2})a_{\textrm{in},1}^{\dagger}(t_{1}^{\prime})a_{\textrm{in},2}^{\dagger}(t_{2}^{\prime})\ket{0}

(19)

\displaystyle=\delta(t_{1}-t_{1}^{\prime})\delta(t_{2}-t_{2}^{\prime})-\kappa_{1e}\bra{0}\hat{T}[a_{1}(t_{1})a_{1}^{\dagger}(t_{1}^{\prime})]\ket{0}\delta(t_{2}-t_{2}^{\prime})-\kappa_{2e}\bra{0}\hat{T}[a_{2}(t_{2})a_{2}^{\dagger}(t_{2}^{\prime})]\ket{0}\delta(t_{1}-t_{1}^{\prime})

\displaystyle+\kappa_{1e}\kappa_{2e}\bra{0}\hat{T}[a_{1}(t_{1})a_{1}(t_{2})a_{1}^{\dagger}(t_{1}^{\prime})a_{2}^{\dagger}(t_{2}^{\prime})]\ket{0}.

The second and third terms are the single-photon Green’s function which have been calculated (see Eq.
7
). The last term is new and involves six different time orderings,

\displaystyle\bra{0}\hat{T}\left[a_{2}(t_{2})a_{1}(t_{1})a_{1}^{\dagger}(t_{1}^{\prime})a_{2}^{\dagger}(t_{2}^{\prime})\right]\ket{0}=

\displaystyle\bra{0}a_{2}(t_{2})a_{1}(t_{1})a_{1}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime}_{1})a_{2}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime}_{2})\ket{0}\theta(t_{2}-t_{1})\theta(t_{1}-t_{1}^{\prime})\theta(t_{1}^{\prime}-t_{2})

(20)

\displaystyle+\bra{0}a_{2}(t_{2})a_{1}(t_{1})a_{2}^{\dagger}(t_{2}^{\prime})a_{1}^{\dagger}(t_{1}^{\prime})\ket{0}\theta(t_{2}-t_{1})\theta(t_{1}-t_{2}^{\prime})\theta(t_{2}^{\prime}-t_{1}^{\prime})

\displaystyle+\bra{0}a_{1}(t_{1})a_{1}^{\dagger}(t_{1}^{\prime})a_{2}(t_{2})a_{2}^{\dagger}(t_{2}^{\prime})\ket{0}\theta(t_{1}-t_{1}^{\prime})\theta(t_{1}^{\prime}-t_{2})\theta(t_{2}-t_{2}^{\prime})

\displaystyle+\bra{0}a_{1}(t_{1})a_{2}(t_{2})a_{2}^{\dagger}(t_{2}^{\prime})a_{1}^{\dagger}(t_{1}^{\prime})\ket{0}\theta(t_{1}-t_{2})\theta(t_{2}-t_{2}^{\prime})\theta(t_{2}^{\prime}-t_{1})

\displaystyle+\bra{0}a_{1}(t_{1})a_{2}(t_{2})a_{1}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime}_{1})a_{2}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime}_{2})\ket{0}\theta(t_{1}-t_{2})\theta(t_{2}-t_{1}^{\prime})\theta(t_{1}^{\prime}-t_{2}^{\prime})

\displaystyle+\bra{0}a_{2}(t_{2})a_{2}^{\dagger}(t_{2}^{\prime})a_{1}(t_{1})a_{1}^{\dagger}(t_{1}^{\prime})\ket{0}\theta(t_{2}-t_{2}^{\prime})\theta(t_{2}^{\prime}-t_{1})\theta(t_{1}-t_{1}^{\prime}).

We first calculate
\bra{0}a_{2}(t_{2})a_{1}(t_{1})a_{1}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime}_{1})a_{2}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime}_{2})\ket{0}
:

\displaystyle\bra{0}a_{2}(t_{2})a_{1}(t_{1})a_{1}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime}_{1})a_{2}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime}_{2})\ket{0}

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bra{000}a_{2}(t_{2})\ket{010}\bra{010}a_{1}(t_{1})\left(\ket{110}\bra{110}+\ket{001}\bra{001}\right)a_{1}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime}_{1})\ket{010}\bra{010}a_{2}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime}_{2})\ket{000}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i\alpha_{2}(-t_{2}+t^{\prime}_{2}+t_{1}-t^{\prime}_{1})}\big(\bra{110}e^{-iH_{\textrm{eff}}t_{1}}\ket{110}\bra{110}e^{iH_{\textrm{eff}}t^{\prime}_{1}}\ket{110}+\bra{110}e^{-iH_{\textrm{eff}}t_{1}}\ket{001}\bra{001}e^{iH_{\textrm{eff}}t^{\prime}_{1}}\ket{110}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i\alpha_{2}(-t_{2}+t^{\prime}_{2}+t_{1}-t^{\prime}_{1})}\bigg[\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Delta\alpha}{\sqrt{\Delta\alpha^{2}+4g^{2}}}\Big)e^{-i\lambda_{1}(t_{1}-t^{\prime}_{1})}+\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Delta\alpha}{\sqrt{\Delta\alpha^{2}+4g^{2}}}\Big)e^{-i\lambda_{2}(t_{1}-t^{\prime}_{1})}\bigg].

Similarly, the second and third terms in Eq.
20
are found to be

\bra{0}a_{2}(t_{2})a_{1}(t_{1})a_{2}^{\dagger}(t_{2}^{\prime})a_{1}^{\dagger}(t_{1}^{\prime})\ket{0}=e^{i\alpha_{2}(t_{1}-t_{2})}e^{i\alpha_{1}(t^{\prime}_{1}-t^{\prime}_{2})}\bigg[\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Delta\alpha}{\sqrt{\Delta\alpha^{2}+4g^{2}}}\Big)e^{-i\lambda_{1}(t_{1}-t^{\prime}_{2})}+\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Delta\alpha}{\sqrt{\Delta\alpha^{2}+4g^{2}}}\Big)e^{-i\lambda_{2}(t_{1}-t^{\prime}_{2})}\bigg],

(22)

\bra{0}a_{1}(t_{1})a_{1}^{\dagger}(t_{1}^{\prime})a_{2}(t_{2})a_{2}^{\dagger}(t_{2}^{\prime})\ket{0}=e^{i\alpha_{1}(t^{\prime}_{1}-t_{1})}e^{i\alpha_{2}(t^{\prime}_{2}-t_{2})}.

(23)

The rest three terms in Eq.
20
are obtained by simply exchanging the indices
1
and
2
in Eqs.
21
-
23
.

Finally, by Fourier transform of Eq.
20
, we obtain the momentum-space Green’s function

G(p_{1},p_{2};k_{1},k_{2})=-\frac{1}{(k_{1}-\alpha_{1})(k_{2}-\alpha_{2})}\delta(p_{1}-k_{1})\delta(p_{2}-k_{2})+M(p_{1},p_{2},k_{1},k_{2})\delta(p_{1}+p_{2}-k_{1}-k_{2}),

(24)

where

M(p_{1},p_{2},k_{1},k_{2})=-\frac{ig^{2}}{2\pi}\frac{k_{1}+k_{2}-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}}{(k_{1}-\alpha_{1})(k_{2}-\alpha_{2})(p_{1}-\alpha_{1})(p_{2}-\alpha_{2})(k_{1}+k_{2}-\lambda_{1})(k_{1}+k_{2}-\lambda_{2})},

(25)

and the momentum-space
S-
matrix

S_{p_{1}p_{2};k_{1}k_{2}}=t_{k_{1}}t_{k_{2}}\delta(p_{1}-k_{1})\delta(p_{2}-k_{2})+\kappa_{1e}\kappa_{2e}M(p_{1},p_{2},k_{1},k_{2})\delta(p_{1}+p_{2}-k_{1}-k_{2}).

(26)

For the case of degenerate
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
, after a similar derivation, we have

G(p_{1},p_{2};k_{1},k_{2})=-\frac{1}{(k_{1}-\alpha_{1})(k_{2}-\alpha_{1})}[\delta(p_{1}-k_{1})\delta(p_{2}-k_{2})+\delta(p_{1}-k_{2})\delta(p_{2}-k_{1})]+M(p_{1},p_{2},k_{1},k_{2})\delta(p_{1}+p_{2}-k_{1}-k_{2})

(27)

and

S_{p_{1}p_{2};k_{1}k_{2}}=t_{k_{1}}t_{k_{2}}[\delta(p_{1}-k_{1})\delta(p_{2}-k_{2})+\delta(p_{1}-k_{2})\delta(p_{2}-k_{1})]+\kappa_{1e}^{2}M(p_{1},p_{2},k_{1},k_{2})\delta(p_{1}+p_{2}-k_{1}-k_{2}),

(28)

where

M(p_{1},p_{2},k_{1},k_{2})=-\frac{2ig^{2}}{\pi}\frac{k_{1}+k_{2}-2\alpha_{1}}{(k_{1}-\alpha_{1})(k_{2}-\alpha_{1})(p_{1}-\alpha_{1})(p_{2}-\alpha_{1})(k_{1}+k_{2}-\lambda^{\prime}_{1})(k_{1}+k_{2}-\lambda^{\prime}_{2})}.

(29)

Eqs.
26
and
28
show that the
S-
matrix of two-to-two-photon transport consists of an interaction-free component, which is the product of two single-photon transmission coefficients, and an interaction-mediated component. The total transport amplitude is given by the superposition of the two components, representing the quantum interference between the two pathways. This leads to profound consequences of the photon-photon interaction via the waveguide-coupled nonlinear optical cavities and applications in quantum information science (see Section
IV
). The interaction-mediated component was also interpreted as the two-photon bound state previously
Shen and Fan 2007
.

Feynman diagram approach

In this section, we introduce a perturbation method based on Feynman diagrams to calculate the momentum-space Green’s function and
S-
matrix. The perturbation method, suited for weak nonlinear systems with
g/\kappa_{j}<1
, reveals the relevant physical processes contributing to the few-photon transport amplitude. It is also mathematically simplified, especially when only the leading order Feynman diagrams are of interest, compared to the previous method where careful cancellation of divergence is needed when performing the Fourier transform of the time-domain Green’s function involving the Heaviside step function. The Feynman diagram rules here resemble those developed in quantum filed theory (see, e.g., Ref.
Peskin 2018
). In essence, the
n-

n

1

•

Propagator:
\frac{i}{k-\alpha}
,

•

Vertex:
-ig
,

•

Impose energy conservation at each vertex:
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\delta(\sum k-\sum p)
,

•

Integrate undetermined momentum:
\int dk\int dp
.

•

Multiply the symmetry factor:
m!
for
m
propagators of the same mode connecting two vertices or connecting a vertex with external points.

The Feynman diagrams corresponding to the propagator and vertex are shown in Fig.

9

i\kappa_{j}
, the propagator does not diverge at the real resonance frequency, avoiding the addition of an infinitesimal imaginary term in the propagator for performing the momentum integrals.

The three- and four-point momentum-space Green’s functions related to Eqs.

where the term of

corresponds to the first diagram on the right hand side in Fig.

corresponds to the diagram with
n

The four-point Green’s function is found to be

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{(k_{1}-\alpha_{1})(k_{2}-\alpha_{2})}\delta(p_{1}-k_{1})\delta(p_{2}-k_{2})

IV
Observational effects of few-photon transport

In this Section, we discuss several observational effects associated with the few-photon transport via a waveguide-coupled
\chi^{(2)}
cavity. Throughout this Section, we consider input photons in a weak coherent state, which are commonly used for single-photon level experiments.

IV.1
Single-photon down-conversion

We first consider the down-conversion process of
a_{3}
photons to
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
photons. The input weak coherent state of monochromatic
a_{3}
photons can be written as

\ket{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}=\ket{0}+\alpha\ket{1_t}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\ket{1_t1_t}+O(\alpha^{3}),

(33)

where
\alpha
is the amplitude of the coherent state and
\ket{1_t}=\ket{1}e^{-ikt}
represents a monochromatic single-photon state with momentum
k
in the time domain. Note
\ket{1_t}\neq\ket{t}
. We are interested in the second-order correlation function of the output
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
photons:

\displaystyle

\displaystyle g^{(2)}(\tau)

(34)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\bra{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(t)a_{\textrm{out},2}^{\dagger}(t+\tau)a_{\textrm{out},2}(t+\tau)a_{\textrm{out},1}(t)\ket{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}}{\bra{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(t)a_{\textrm{out},1}(t)\ket{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}\bra{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}a_{\textrm{out},2}^{\dagger}(t)a_{\textrm{out},2}(t)\ket{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}},

which can be calculated using the momentum-space
S
-matrix. To do so, we first perform a Fourier transform of
\ket{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}
:

\mathscr{F}\left\{\ket{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}\right\}=\int\frac{dt}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{ik^{\prime}t}\ket{1}e^{-ikt}=\sqrt{2\pi}\ket{1}\delta(k^{\prime}-k).

(35)

Thus we define Fourier-transformed monochromatic single-photon state to be
\sqrt{2\pi}\ket{1_{k}}
.
Keeping the leading order term in
\ket{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}
, we have

\displaystyle\bra{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(t)a_{\textrm{out},2}^{\dagger}(t+\tau)a_{\textrm{out},2}(t+\tau)a_{\textrm{out},1}(t)\ket{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}

(36)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}\bra{1_t}a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(t)a_{\textrm{out},2}^{\dagger}(t+\tau)a_{\textrm{out},2}(t+\tau)a_{\textrm{out},1}(t)\ket{1_{t}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}\bra{1_t}a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(t)a_{\textrm{out},2}^{\dagger}(t+\tau)\ket{0}\bra{0}a_{\textrm{out},2}(t+\tau)a_{\textrm{out},1}(t)\ket{1_{t}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2\pi}\int dp_{1}dp_{2}\bra{1_{k}}a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(p_{1})a_{\textrm{out},2}^{\dagger}(p_{2})\ket{0}e^{ip_{1}t+ip_{2}(t+\tau)}

\displaystyle\times\int d\tilde{p}_{1}d\tilde{p}_{2}\bra{0}a_{\textrm{out},2}(\tilde{p}_{2})a_{\textrm{out},1}(\tilde{p}_{1})\ket{1_{k}}e^{-i\tilde{p}_{1}t-i\tilde{p}_{2}(t+\tau)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2\pi}\int dp_{1}dp_{2}S_{p_{1}p_{2};k}^{*}e^{ip_{1}t+ip_{2}(t+\tau)}

\displaystyle\times\int d\tilde{p}_{1}d\tilde{p}_{2}S_{\tilde{p}_{1}\tilde{p}_{2};k}e^{-i\tilde{p}_{1}t-i\tilde{p}_{2}(t+\tau)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{|\alpha|^{2}g^{2}\kappa_{1e}\kappa_{2e}\kappa_{3e}}{|(k-\lambda_{1})(k-\lambda_{2})|^{2}}e^{-\kappa_{2}\tau},\tau>0,\\
\frac{|\alpha|^{2}g^{2}\kappa_{1e}\kappa_{2e}\kappa_{3e}}{|(k-\lambda_{1})(k-\lambda_{2})|^{2}}e^{\kappa_{1}\tau},\tau<0,\end{cases}

where
S_{p_{1}p_{2};k}
is given by Eq.
18
, and

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\bra{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(t)a_{\textrm{out},1}(t)\ket{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}

(37)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}\bra{1_t}a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(t)a_{\textrm{out},1}(t)\ket{1_{t}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}\int dp_{1}d\tilde{p}_{1}\bra{1_{k}}a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(p_{1})a_{\textrm{out},1}(\tilde{p}_{1})\ket{1_{k}}e^{ip_{1}t-i\tilde{p}_{1}t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}\int dp_{1}d\tilde{p}_{1}\bra{1_{k}}a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(p_{1})\left(\int dp_{2}\ket{p_2}\bra{p_2}+\int dp_{2i}\ket{p_{2i}}\bra{p_{2i}}\right)a_{\textrm{out},1}(\tilde{p}_{1})\ket{1_{k}}e^{ip_{1}t-i\tilde{p}_{1}t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}\left(\int dp_{1}d\tilde{p}_{1}dp_{2}S_{p_{1}p_{2};k}S_{\tilde{p}_{1}p_{2};k}^{*}+\frac{\kappa_{2i}}{\kappa_{2e}}\int dp_{1}d\tilde{p}_{1}dp_{2i}S_{p_{1}p_{2i};k}S_{\tilde{p}_{1}p_{2i};k}^{*}\right)e^{ip_{1}t-i\tilde{p}_{1}t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{2e}}\int dp_{1}d\tilde{p}_{1}dp_{2}S_{p_{1}p_{2};k}S_{\tilde{p}_{1}p_{2};k}^{*}e^{ip_{1}t-i\tilde{p}_{1}t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}\frac{\kappa_{1e}}{\kappa_{1}}\frac{g^{2}(\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2})\kappa_{3e}}{|(k-\lambda_{1})(k-\lambda_{2})|^{2}},

where
\ket{p_{2i}}
represents the state of
a_{2}
photons in the intrinsic loss channel. Because the states in the waveguide and the intrinsic loss channel combined form a complete Hilbert space of the leaked photons from the cavity, we have the identity

\int dp_{2}\ket{p_2}\bra{p_2}+\int dp_{2i}\ket{p_{2i}}\bra{p_{2i}}=I.

(38)

In other words,
\bra{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(t)a_{\textrm{out},1}(t)\ket{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}
measures the total
a_{1}
photon flux, regardless of the place of
a_{2}
photons, which could either be in the waveguide or the intrinsic loss channel. Thus, we have to insert Eq.
38
into Eq.
37
. We have also used the fact that the
S-
matrix involving
\ket{p_{2i}}
will be proportional to
\sqrt{\kappa_{2i}}
, by replacing
\sqrt{\kappa_{2e}}
in Eq.
14
. Exchanging subscripts 1 and 2 in Eq.
37
yields the result of
\bra{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}a_{\textrm{out},2}^{\dagger}(t)a_{\textrm{out},2}(t)\ket{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}
. The intra-cavity photon-pair generation rate is inferred from Eq.
37
to be

R=|\alpha|^{2}\frac{g^{2}(\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2})\kappa_{3e}}{|(k-\lambda_{1})(k-\lambda_{2})|^{2}}.

(39)

Finally, we have

\displaystyle g^{(2)}(\tau)=

\displaystyle\frac{|(k-\lambda_{1})(k-\lambda_{2})|^{2}}{2|\alpha|^{2}g^{2}(\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2})\kappa_{3e}}\frac{2\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}}\times\begin{cases}e^{-\kappa_{2}\tau},\tau>0,\\
e^{\kappa_{1}\tau},\tau<0,\end{cases}

(40)

and in the limit
g\ll\kappa_{1,2,3}
,

\displaystyle g^{(2)}(\tau)\approx

\displaystyle\frac{\left((k-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})^{2}+(\frac{\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}}{2})^{2}\right)\left((k-\omega_{3})^{2}+(\frac{\kappa_{3}}{2})^{2}\right)}{2|\alpha|^{2}g^{2}(\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2})\kappa_{3e}}

(41)

\displaystyle\times\frac{2\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}}\times\begin{cases}e^{-\kappa_{2}\tau},\tau>0,\\
e^{\kappa_{1}\tau},\tau<0.\end{cases}

Interestingly, Eq.
41
is identical to the result of spontaneous parametric down-conversion derived via a semi-classical approach by assuming non-depleted classical pumps and using Gaussian moment factoring theorem
Clausen et al. 2014
;
Guo et al. 2017
.

IV.2
Photon blockade

Next, we consider transport of
a_{1}
photons via a cavity with degenerate
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
modes. For input of
a_{1}
photons in this case, there will be probability of up-conversion to output
a_{3}
photons; however, we focus on the process without such up-conversion, which could be separated apart from the up-converted
a_{3}
photons because
a_{1}
and
a_{3}
photons are disparate in frequency. Assuming monochromatic input
a_{1}
photons in a weak coherent state given by Eq.
33
, we compute the second-order self-correlation function of the transported
a_{1}
photons:

\displaystyle

\displaystyle g^{(2)}(\tau)

(42)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\bra{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(t)a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(t+\tau)a_{\textrm{out},1}(t+\tau)a_{\textrm{out},1}(t)\ket{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}}{\bra{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(t)a_{\textrm{out},1}(t)\ket{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}\bra{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(t)a_{\textrm{out},1}(t)\ket{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}}.

By keeping the leading order terms in
\ket{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}
, we have

\displaystyle\bra{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(t)a_{\textrm{out},1}(t)\ket{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}

(43)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}\bra{1_t}a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(t)a_{\textrm{out},1}(t)\ket{1_t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}\bra{1_t}a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(t)\ket{0}\bra{0}a_{\textrm{out},1}(t)\ket{1_t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}\int dpe^{ipt}\bra{1_k}a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(p)\ket{0}\int d\tilde{p}e^{-i\tilde{p}t}\bra{0}a_{\textrm{out},1}(\tilde{p})\ket{1_k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}\int dpe^{ipt}S_{p;k}^{*}\int d\tilde{p}e^{-i\tilde{p}t}S_{\tilde{p};k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}|t_{k}|^{2}

and

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\bra{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(t)a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(t+\tau)a_{\textrm{out},1}(t+\tau)a_{\textrm{out},1}(t)\ket{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}

(44)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\alpha|^{4}}{2}\bra{1_t1_t}a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(t)a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(t+\tau)a_{\textrm{out},1}(t+\tau)a_{\textrm{out},1}(t)\ket{1_t1_t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\alpha|^{4}}{2}\int dp_{1}dp_{2}\bra{1_k1_k}a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(p_{1})a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(p_{2})\ket{0}e^{ip_{1}t+ip_{2}(t+\tau)}

\displaystyle\times\int d\tilde{p}_{1}d\tilde{p}_{2}\bra{0}a_{\textrm{out},1}(\tilde{p}_{2})a_{\textrm{out},1}(\tilde{p}_{1})\ket{1_k1_k}e^{-i\tilde{p}_{1}t-i\tilde{p}_{2}(t+\tau)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\alpha|^{4}}{4}\int dp_{1}dp_{2}S_{p_{1}p_{2};kk}^{*}e^{ip_{1}t+ip_{2}(t+\tau)}

\displaystyle\times\int d\tilde{p}_{1}d\tilde{p}_{2}S_{\tilde{p}_{1}\tilde{p}_{2};kk}e^{-i\tilde{p}_{1}t-i\tilde{p}_{2}(t+\tau)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{4}|t_{k}^{2}+T(k,\tau)|^{2},

where the
S-
matrix is given by Eq.
28
and

\displaystyle T(k,\tau)=-\frac{2g^{2}\kappa_{1e}^{2}}{(2k-\lambda^{\prime}_{1})(2k-\lambda^{\prime}_{2})(k-\alpha_{1})^{2}}e^{-i|\tau|(\alpha_{1}-k)}.

(45)

Note the additional factor of 1/2 in the third equality of Eq.

comes from the definition of Fock state
\ket{1_k1_k}=\ket{2_k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}a_{\textrm{in}}^{\dagger 2}(k)\ket{0}
.
Finally, we have

\displaystyle g^{(2)}(\tau)=\frac{|t_{k}^{2}+T(k,\tau)|^{2}}{|t_{k}^{2}|^{2}}.

(46)

for various

This result is remarkable in that even when
|T(k,\tau)|
is small, which is the case for practical
\chi^{(2)}
nonlinear optical cavities with
g/\kappa\ll 1
, highly nonclassical correlations of transported photons, i.e.,
g^{(2)}(\tau)\neq 1
, could be achieved by making
|t_{k}^{2}|
close to
|T(k,\tau)|
. This is due to the quantum interference between the interaction-free and interaction-mediated amplitudes of the two-photon transport, as indicated by the

), especially when the two amplitudes are compatible. To illustrate this more explicitly, for the one-port, phase-matched cavity (
2\omega_{1}=\omega_{3}
) and on-resonance input photons (

),

(47)

and

(48)

Thus, by making
\kappa_{1i}
and
\kappa_{1e}
such that

, which indicates the photon blockade. Remarkably, this can be achieved even for

is important in cancellation with

; intuitively, this is because during the interaction-mediated process the state
\ket{20}
undergoes a Rabi flip with the virtual state
\ket{01}
and back again, which yields a Berry phase of
\pi
.

Moreover, the statistical properties of the output
a_{1}
photons can be altered, e.g., from anti-bunching to bunching (

), by controlling

or
\kappa_{1i}
. This is shown in Fig.

nonlinear photonic platform with

, which shows the averaging effect on the nonclassical correlation due to the finite spectral width of the wavepacket. However, this is not surprising because the cavity-based mechanism is expected to work only for wavepackets with spectral width much smaller than the cavity linewidth.

IV.3
Nonlinear phase shift

Figure 4:
Nonlinear phase shift for various
\kappa_{1e}/\kappa_{1i}

\kappa_{3i}=2\kappa_{1i}

The photon-photon interaction in the
\chi^{(2)}
cavity also induces a nonlinear phase shift of the transported photons. To compute the nonlinear phase shift, we point out the numerator of the second-order correlation function (Eq.
42
) actually gives the norm of the time-domain wavefunction of the transported two photons (see Appendix
A
). Thus, the wavefunction of the transported two photons can be written as

\bra{t,t+\tau}\ket{\psi_{\textrm{out}}}=e^{-ik(2t+\tau)}(t_{k}^{2}+T(k,\tau)).

(49)

The wavefunction of two photons without the interaction is

\bra{t,t+\tau}\ket{\psi_{\textrm{out}}^{(0)}}=e^{-ik(2t+\tau)}t_{k}^{2}.

(50)

The nonlinear phase shift due to the photon-photon interaction thus is

\displaystyle\varphi_{\textrm{n}}

\displaystyle\equiv\textrm{Arg}\left[\bra{t,t+\tau}\ket{\psi_{\textrm{out}}}\right]-\textrm{Arg}\left[\bra{t,t+\tau}\ket{\psi_{\textrm{out}}^{(0)}}\right]

(51)

\displaystyle=\textrm{Arg}\left[1+\frac{T(k,\tau)}{t_{k}^{2}}\right].

By making
|t_{k}^{2}|\approx|T(k,\tau)|
, large nonlinear phase shift can be achieved, which is possible even for the weak coupling regime
g\ll\kappa_{1}
. Such nonlinear phase shift is a key enabler for quantum nondemolition measurement of photons
Xia et al. 2016
and conditional quantum logic gates
Turchette et al. 1995
;
Langford et al. 2011
.

shows the calculated nonlinear phase shift (for
\tau=0
) using experimentally achievable parameters of a state-of-the-art
\chi^{(2)}
nonlinear photonic platform
Zhao and Fang 2021
. It is seen that large and even
\pi-
nonlinear phase shift can be achieved in practical devices. Actually,
\pi-
nonlinear phase shift is always achieved at
k=\omega_{1}
as long as
t_{\omega_{1}}^{2}<-T(\omega_{1},0)
.

a_{2}

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}=

\displaystyle\ket{\alpha}_{1}\ket{\beta}_{2}

(52)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\ket{0}+\alpha\ket{1_{t}}_{1}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\ket{1_{t}1_{t}}_{1}+O(\alpha^{3})\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\ket{0}+\beta\ket{1_{t}}_{2}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\ket{1_{t}1_{t}}_{2}+O(\beta^{3})\right).

The two-mode and single-mode correlations are, respectively,

\displaystyle G_{12}(\tau)

(53)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha\beta|^{2}\int dp_{1}dp_{2}S_{p_{1}p_{2};k_{1}k_{2}}^{*}e^{ip_{1}t+ip_{2}(t+\tau)}

\displaystyle\times\int d\tilde{p}_{1}d\tilde{p}_{2}S_{\tilde{p}_{1}\tilde{p}_{2};k_{1}k_{2}}e^{-i\tilde{p}_{1}t-i\tilde{p}_{2}(t+\tau)}

\displaystyle|\alpha\beta|^{2}|t_{k_{1}}t_{k_{2}}+\widetilde{T}(k_{1},k_{2},\tau)|^{2},

where the

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}(k_{1},k_{2},\tau)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-\frac{g^{2}\kappa_{1e}\kappa_{2e}}{(k_{1}+k_{2}-\lambda_{1})(k_{1}+k_{2}-\lambda_{2})(k_{1}-\alpha_{1})(k_{2}-\alpha_{2})}e^{-i\tau(\alpha_{2}-k_{2})},\tau>0,\\

and

\displaystyle G_{11}(\tau)

(55)

\displaystyle\bra{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(t)a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(t+\tau)a_{\textrm{out},1}(t+\tau)a_{\textrm{out},1}(t)\ket{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\alpha|^{4}}{2}\int dp_{1}dp_{2}\bra{1_{k_1}1_{k_1}}a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(p_{1})a_{\textrm{out},1}^{\dagger}(p_{2})\ket{0}e^{ip_{1}t+ip_{2}(t+\tau)}

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{4}|t_{k_{1}}|^{4},

and similarly,

G_{22}(\tau)=|\beta|^{4}|t_{k_{2}}|^{4}.

(56)

In Eq.

, we have used the fact that the transport of two
a_{1}
photons via a non-degenerate cavity is interaction-free because
2\omega_{1}\neq\omega_{3}
.

To measure the nonclassicality of the two-mode correlation, we define

\zeta(\tau)\equiv\frac{G_{12}(\tau)}{\sqrt{G_{11}(\tau)G_{22}(\tau)}}=|1+\frac{\widetilde{T}(k_{1},k_{2},\tau)}{t_{k_{1}}t_{k_{2}}}|^{2}.

(57)

\zeta(\tau)>1
leads to violation of the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and indicates nonclassical two-mode correlations
Mandel and Wolf 1995
.
Given the similarity of Eq.

can be achieved when

is sufficiently smaller than
|\widetilde{T}(k_{1},k_{2},\tau)|

V
Conclusion

In summary, we have theoretically studied few-photon transport via a waveguide-coupled, multimode
\chi^{(2)}
optical cavity. We used both non-perturbative method and a new perturbation method based on Feynman diagrams to compute the
S-
matrix associated with the few-photon transport. The Feynman diagram approach provides physical insight into the transport process and is mathematically convenient. The
S-
matrix shows that the two-photon transport involves quantum interference between linear transmission and interaction-mediated transport. This effect leads to rather unexpected result that strong quantum correlations could be realized in weak nonlinear systems by matching the linear transmission coefficient with the interaction-mediated amplitude. We numerically showed several pronounced quantum optical effects, including photon blockade and
\pi-
conditional phase shift, could be achieved in state-of-the-art nonlinear quantum photonic platforms, which might have a significant impact on using these systems for quantum information science applications.

Appendix A
The wavefunction of transported photons

Here we prove the second-order correlation actually gives the norm of the output two-photon wavefunction.

\displaystyle\bra{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}a_{\textrm{out}}^{\dagger}(t)a_{\textrm{out}}^{\dagger}(t+\tau)a_{\textrm{out}}(t+\tau)a_{\textrm{out}}(t)\ket{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}

(58)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int dp_{1}dp_{2}dp_{3}dp_{4}e^{i(p_{1}t+p_{2}(t+\tau)-p_{3}(t+\tau)-p_{4}t)}\bra{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}\hat{S}^{\dagger}\hat{S}a_{\textrm{out}}^{\dagger}(p_{1})\hat{S}^{\dagger}\hat{S}a_{\textrm{out}}^{\dagger}(p_{2})\hat{S}^{\dagger}\hat{S}a_{\textrm{out}}(p_{3})\hat{S}^{\dagger}\hat{S}a_{\textrm{out}}(p_{4})\hat{S}^{\dagger}\hat{S}\ket{\psi_{\textrm{in}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int dp_{1}dp_{2}dp_{3}dp_{4}e^{i(p_{1}t+p_{2}(t+\tau)-p_{3}(t+\tau)-p_{4}t)}\bra{\psi_{\textrm{out}}}a_{\textrm{in}}^{\dagger}(p_{1})a_{\textrm{in}}^{\dagger}(p_{2})a_{\textrm{in}}(p_{3})a_{\textrm{in}}(p_{4})\ket{\psi_{\textrm{out}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int dp_{1}dp_{2}dp_{3}dp_{4}e^{i(p_{1}t+p_{2}(t+\tau)-p_{3}(t+\tau)-p_{4}t)}\bra{\psi_{\textrm{out}}}a_{\textrm{in}}^{\dagger}(p_{1})a_{\textrm{in}}^{\dagger}(p_{2})\ket{0}\bra{0}a_{\textrm{in}}(p_{3})a_{\textrm{in}}(p_{4})\ket{\psi_{\textrm{out}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int dp_{1}dp_{2}e^{i(p_{1}t+p_{2}(t+\tau))}\bra{p_1,p_2}\ket{\psi_{\textrm{out}}}^{*}\int dp_{3}dp_{4}e^{-i(p_{3}(t+\tau)+p_{4}t)}\bra{p_3,p_4}\ket{\psi_{\textrm{out}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\bra{t,t+\tau}\ket{\psi_{\textrm{out}}}|^{2}

where
\ket{t,t+\tau}
represents the state of two photons at time
t
and
t+\tau
, respectively, and thus the end result gives the time-domain wavefunction of the output two photons. We have used

Extremal behavior of reduced type of one dimensional rings
Sarasij Maitra, Vivek Mukundan

Abstract
. Let R R be a domain that is a complete local 𝕜 \mathbb{k} algebra in dimension one. In an effort to address the Berger’s conjecture, a crucial invariant reduced type s ⁡ ( R ) s(R) was introduced in [ HMM21 ] . In this article, we study this invariant and its max/min values separately and relate it to the valuation semigroup of R R . We justify the need to study s ⁡ ( R ) s(R) in the context of numerical semigroup rings and consequently investigate the occurrence of the extreme values of s ⁡ ( R ) s(R) for the Gorenstein, almost Gorenstein, and far-flung Gorenstein complete numerical semigroup rings. Finally, we study the finiteness of the category CM ⁡ ( R ) \operatorname{CM}(R) of maximal Cohen Macaulay modules and the category Ref ⁡ ( R ) \operatorname{Ref}(R) of reflexive modules for rings which are of maximal/minimal reduced type and provide many classifications.

Introduction

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},\mathbb{k})
be a non-regular one dimensional complete local domain which is a
\mathbb{k}
-algebra. We assume that
\mathbb{k}
is algebraically closed of characteristic
0
. Assume
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{\alpha_{1}t^{a_{1}},\dots\alpha_{n}t^{a_{n}}}\rrbracket\cong R=\frac{\mathbb{k}\llbracket{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}}\rrbracket}{I}
,
I\subseteq(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})^{2}
. Here
\alpha_{i}
are units of
\overline{R}
. Additionally, we also arrange the
x_{i}
such that
a_{1}<a_{2}<\dots<a_{n}
. The
reduced type
of
R
is

\displaystyle s(R):=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\frac{\mathfrak{C}+x_{1}R}{x_{1}R},

where
\mathfrak{C}
is the conductor ideal of the ring
R
. Recall that the conductor
\mathfrak{C}=R:_{\operatorname{Quot}(R)}\overline{R}
is the largest common ideal between
R
and
\overline{R}
. This invariant was defined in
[
HMM21
]
as a means of constructing an over ring
S
of
R
. The authors also demonstrated that constructing sufficient torsion elements in the module of differentials
\Omega_{S}
helped to solve the Berger’s conjecture for
R
. This invariant was also used by the authors in
[
MM23
]
extensively to build classes of rings which attests to the positivity of the conjecture. The main objective of this article is to investigate the values attained by this invariant
s(R)
and its relationship to the Cohen-Macaulay type
\operatorname{type}(R)
. As the name suggests, the reduced type
s(R)
is indeed related to
\operatorname{type}(R)
. For,
\frac{\mathfrak{C}+x_{1}R}{x_{1}R}\subseteq\frac{x_{1}R:\mathfrak{m}}{x_{1}R}
and the
\mathbb{k}
-vector space dimension of the latter object is precisely
\operatorname{type}(R)
. Thus the maximum and minimum values of
s(R)
are captured in the inequality

1\leqslant s(R)\leqslant\operatorname{type}(R).

Following this observation, we say that
R
is of
minimal
(resp.
maximal
) reduced type when
s(R)=1
(resp.
s(R)=\operatorname{type}(R)
). Interestingly, when
R
is Gorenstein (equivalently
\operatorname{type}(R)=1
),
s(R)
equals one, but not conversely (see for example
Example

2.6
). In fact, Gorenstein rings are the only ones of both maximal and minimal reduced type.

Notice that under the above setup, the integral closure
\overline{R}=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t}\rrbracket
is a
DVR
(Discrete Valuation Ring) and hence one can define an order valuation
v(\cdot)
on the ring
R
induced from the one on
\overline{R}
, allowing us to define a valuation semigroup
v(R)
of
R
. There is a strong relationship between the singularities of
R
and the structural representations of the valuation semigroup
v(R)
. For example, Kunz
[
Kun70
]
showed that
R
is Gorenstein if and only if
v(R)
is symmetric. This has since then been generalized to almost Gorenstein rings too. Barucci-Froberg
[
BF97
]
showed that
R
is almost Gorenstein (equivalently
\ell(\overline{R}/R)=\ell(R/\mathfrak{C})+\operatorname{type}(R)-1
, where
\ell(M)
is the length of the module
M
) if and only if the valuation semigroup
v(R)
is almost symmetric and
\operatorname{type}(R)=\operatorname{type}(v(R))
. The Cohen-Macaulay type
\operatorname{type}(v(R))
can be easily computed as the cardinality of the pseudo-Frobenius set
\PF(v(R))
(see
Section

3
for a definition). In fact there are some nice characterizations to check when the valuation semigroup
v(R)
is almost symmetric (
[
Nar13
]
). We show that the reduced type
s(R)
is also the cardinality of the set
[c-a_{1},c-1]\backslash v(R)
where
\mathfrak{C}=(t^{c})\overline{R}
, the conductor of the ring
R
(see
Theorem

2.13
). The integer
c
is called the conductor valuation of
R
. For the ring
R
we can also define a complete numerical semigroup ring
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{b_{1}},\dots,t^{b_{m}}}\rrbracket
where
v(R)=\langle b_{1},\dots,b_{m}\rangle
. Along with
[
BDF97
, Proposition II.1.16]
the following set of inequalities become apparent.

s\left(\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket\right)=s(R)\leqslant\operatorname{type}(R)\leqslant|\PF(v(R))|=\operatorname{type}(\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket).

Using this set of inequalities we conclude:

Theorem A

(
Proposition

3.4
)
.

Let
R
be as described at the beginning of the article.

(1)

The ring
R
is of minimal reduced type if and only if
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket
is of minimal reduced type.

(2)

The ring
R
is of maximal reduced type if
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket
is of maximal reduced type. The converse holds if additionally
\operatorname{type}(R)=\operatorname{type}(\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket)
.

In fact, when
R
is of minimal multiplicity or when
R
is almost Gorenstein, then the statements in (2) are equivalent.

The above result indicates that the exact nature of the minimal/maximal reduced type of
R
can often be captured by studying the minimal/maximal reduced type of the complete numerical semigroup
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket
. As such, we focus our attention to complete numerical semi-group rings and provide explicit characterization (

3.7
and
Proposition

3.8
) of maximal/minimal reduced type rings in terms of the distribution of the pseudo-Frobenius numbers. For instance, the above study yields the following criteria.

Theorem B

(
Corollaries

3.10
and
3.11
)
.

Let

be a complete numerical semigroup ring of minimal multiplicity. Then
R
is of maximal (resp. minimal) reduced type if and only if
a_{2}\geqslant a_{n}-a_{1}+1
(resp.
a_{n-1}+a_{1}-1<a_{n}
).

When

is almost Gorenstein, the maximal and minimal reduced type property bounds the conductor valuation

of

(

). But when the type is 2, these statements are actually equivalent. These types of rings are called

.

(

3.19
)
.

Let

be a complete numerical semigroup ring which is almost Gorenstein of type 2 with conductor valuation

. Then

2.
Maximal and Minimal Reduced type for one dimensional complete local domains

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},\mathbb{k})
be a non-regular one dimensional complete local domain which is a
k
-algebra. We assume that
\mathbb{k}
is algebraically closed of characteristic
0
. The integral closure of
R
in its field of fractions
K
if denoted by
\overline{R}
. Notice that
\overline{R}=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t}\rrbracket
. Using this and the fact that
R=\frac{\mathbb{k}\llbracket{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}}\rrbracket}{I}
,
I\subseteq(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})^{2}
, we can write each of the images of
X_{i}
modulo
I
, denoted
x_{i}
, as
x_{i}=\alpha_{i}t^{a_{i}}
. Here
\alpha_{i}
are units of
\overline{R}
. We also arrange the
x_{i}
such that
a_{1}<a_{2}<\dots<a_{n}
. Here
n
is the embedding dimension of
R
, which is defined as the minimal number of generators of the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
, denoted
\operatorname{edim}(R)
. By our assumption,
n\geqslant 2
.

There is a valuation on
\overline{R}
(a DVR) which in turn induces a valuation on
R
given by

\displaystyle v\left(\sum_{i}c_{i}t^{i}\right)=\min\{i~|~c_{i}\neq 0\}\text{ where }c_{i}\in\mathbb{k}.

Also, we set
v(\alpha)=0
if
\alpha
is a unit in
\overline{R}
. This valuation extends to the valuation on
\operatorname{Frac}R
, the fraction field of
R
, given by
v(\frac{\alpha}{\beta})=v(\alpha)-v(\beta)
for
\alpha,\beta\in R
. For any
R
submodule
M
of
\operatorname{Frac}R
, we let
v(M)
to be the collection
\{v(\alpha)~|~\alpha\in M\}
. The set
v(R)
forms a semigroup called the valuation semigroup of
R
.

Let
\mathfrak{C}=R:_{K}\overline{R}
denote the conductor ideal of
R
in
\overline{R}
. Observe that
\mathfrak{C}=(t^{c+i})_{i\geqslant 0}
, where
c
is the positive integer such that
t^{c-1}\not\in R
but
t^{c+i}\in R
for all
i\geqslant 0
.

Remark 2.1
.

Notice that
\mathfrak{m}\overline{R}=x_{1}\overline{R}
and thus

\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}\overline{R}\cap R=x_{1}\overline{R}\cap R=\overline{x_{1}},

i.e.,
x_{1}
is a minimal reduction of
\mathfrak{m}
. Since
\mathfrak{m}\overline{R}=x_{1}\overline{R}
, we get that
\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{C}=x_{1}\mathfrak{C}
. Hence,
\mu(\mathfrak{C})=\ell(\mathfrak{C}/x_{1}\mathfrak{C})=e(x_{1};\mathfrak{C})=e(x_{1};R)\operatorname{rank}_{R}(\mathfrak{C})=\ell(R/x_{1}R)\operatorname{rank}_{R}(\mathfrak{C})=e(R)
. The last few equalities follow from well-known properties of the Hilbert-Samuel multiplicity, and also the fact that a non-zero ideal is a maximal Cohen-Macaulay module of rank one.

The following result which we will keep using in the sequel is due to Herzog and Kunz. Many of the length computations reduce to counting arguments in the valuation semigroup.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
HK71
, Proposition 2.9]

Suppose
M_{1}\subseteq M_{2}
are two
R
submodules of
\operatorname{Frac}R
with
M_{1}\neq 0
. Then
M_{2}/M_{1}
has finite length and its length
\ell(M_{2}/M_{1})
is the number of elements in
v(M_{2})
but not in
v(M_{1})
.

Definition 2.3
.

[
HMM21
]

We define the reduced type of
R
, denoted
s(R)
as follows:

s(R):=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\frac{\mathfrak{C}+x_{1}R}{x_{1}R}

Since
\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{C}=x_{1}\mathfrak{C}\subseteq x_{1}R
, the above definition makes sense. Also notice by the same inclusions, we get that
s(R)\leqslant\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\frac{x_{1}R:_{R}\mathfrak{m}}{x_{1}R}
. The latter quantity is precisely the type of
R
and this justifies the nomenclature. Further, it is well-known that
\mathfrak{C}
is not contained in any proper principal ideal. Thus, we get that

1\leqslant s(R)\leqslant\operatorname{type}(R).

Definition 2.4
.

We say
R
has maximal reduced type if
s(R)=\operatorname{type}(R)
. Analogously, we say that
R
has minimal reduced type if
s(R)=1
.

Question 2.5
.

Can we classify when
R
has maximal or minimal reduced type?

Notice that if
R
is Gorenstein, then
R
has maximal (and minimal) reduced type since
\operatorname{type}(R)=1
. However,
s(R)=1
, does not force
R
to have type one.

Example 2.6
.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{5},t^{8},t^{9},t^{11}}\rrbracket
. The conductor
\mathfrak{C}=(t^{13})\overline{R}
and the valuation semigroup is
\{5,8,9,10,11,13\rightarrow\}
. Thus the reduced type
s(R)=1
, but the
\operatorname{type}(R)=2
and hence not a Gorenstein ring.

Definition 2.7
.

We say that
R
is of
minimal multiplicity
if
\operatorname{edim}R=e(R)
.

In the rest of the article
\ell(M)
denotes the length of a module
M
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},\mathbb{k})
be as above. Let
\mathfrak{C}
denote the conductor and
x_{1}
denote the minimal reduction of
\mathfrak{m}
as in the definition of
s(R)
. Then
R
has maximal reduced type if any of the following holds.

(1)

\ell(R/\mathfrak{C})\leqslant 2
.

(2)

\ell(R/\mathfrak{C})=3
and
R
does not have minimal multiplicity.

Proof.

Notice that maximal reduced type implies that we have the equality

\mathfrak{C}+x_{1}R=x_{1}R:_{R}\mathfrak{m}.

Observe that we always have the following inclusions:

\mathfrak{C}\subseteq\mathfrak{C}+x_{1}R\subseteq x_{1}R:_{R}\mathfrak{m}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}.

If
\mathfrak{C}+x_{1}R=\mathfrak{C}
, then
x_{1}\in\mathfrak{C}
.

If
\ell(R/\mathfrak{C})=1
, then
\mathfrak{C}=\mathfrak{m}
and hence all the inclusions are equalities and we are done. So assume
\ell(R/\mathfrak{C})=2
. If
\mathfrak{C}+x_{1}R=\mathfrak{C}
, then
x_{1}\in\mathfrak{C}
. Thus,
\mathfrak{m}=\overline{x_{1}}\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{C}}=\mathfrak{C}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
. This shows that
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{C}
but this contradicts the fact, that
\ell(R/\mathfrak{C})=2
. Hence,
\mathfrak{C}\subsetneq\mathfrak{C}+x_{1}R
. Since
\ell(\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{C})=1
, this shows that
\mathfrak{C}+x_{1}R=x_{1}:_{R}\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}
and we are done with the proof of
(a)
.

For
(b)
, first we recall the well-known fact that
R
is of minimal multiplicity if and only if
\mathfrak{m}^{2}=x_{1}\mathfrak{m}
. We include a quick proof here. Since
\operatorname{edim}(R)=e(R)
, we have that
\ell(\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2})=\ell(R/x_{1}R)
. The LHS is
\ell(R/\mathfrak{m}^{2})-1
whereas the RHS is
\ell(R/x_{1}\mathfrak{m})-1
(using the inclusions
\mathfrak{m}^{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}\subseteq R
and
x_{1}\mathfrak{m}\subseteq x_{1}R\subseteq R
respectively). Thus,
\ell(R/x_{1}\mathfrak{m})=\ell(R/\mathfrak{m}^{2})
which shows that
\mathfrak{m}^{2}=x_{1}\mathfrak{m}
. The converse is clear as well as the arguments are reversible.

Since
x_{1}R
is a minimal reduction of
\mathfrak{m}
, we get that
x_{1}R\cap\mathfrak{m}^{2}=x_{1}\mathfrak{m}
. Hence,
\mathfrak{m}^{2}=x_{1}\mathfrak{m}
if and only if
x_{1}R:_{R}\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}
. Since by assumption,
R
is not of minimal multiplicity, we get the following chain of ideals:

\mathfrak{C}\subsetneq\mathfrak{C}+x_{1}R\subseteq x_{1}R:_{R}\mathfrak{m}\subsetneq\mathfrak{m}.

The conclusion now follows because
\ell(\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{C})=2
.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

The arguments in the first part show that
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{C}
if and only if
\mathfrak{C}=\mathfrak{C}+x_{1}R
. In fact, one can precisely describe
R
in the case
\ell(R/\mathfrak{C})=2
(see
[
DMS23
, Proposition 6.3 (2)]
).

The following example shows that the minimal multiplicity condition cannot be ignored. The computation of
\operatorname{type}(R)
in the following examples can be obtained by computing the cardinality of the pseudo-Frobenius set
\PF(v(R))
(we refer to the next section for a definition of pseudo-Frobenius set).

Example 2.10
.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{4},t^{6},t^{9},t^{11}}\rrbracket
. Here
\mathfrak{C}=(t^{8},t^{9},t^{10},t^{11})
. Thus,
\ell(R/\mathfrak{C})=3
(using
Proposition

2.2
, it is enough to notice that the valuations missing from the conductor are
0,4,6
). Also,
R
has minimal multiplicity. Finally note that
s(R)=2
whereas
\operatorname{type}(R)=3
.

For higher colength, the equality can fail as the following examples show.

Example 2.11
.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{4},t^{6},t^{11},t^{13}}\rrbracket
. Here,
\mathfrak{C}=(t^{10},t^{11},t^{12},t^{13})
and thus
\ell(R/\mathfrak{C})=4
. Also,
R
has minimal multiplicity. But
s(R)=2
whereas
\operatorname{type}(R)=3
.

Example 2.12
.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{5},t^{7},t^{8},t^{11}}\rrbracket
. The conductor is
\mathfrak{C}=(t^{10})\overline{R}
. We get that
\ell(R/\mathfrak{C})=4
,
s(R)=2,\operatorname{type}(R)=3
. Here
R
is not of minimal multiplicity.

Theorem 2.13
.

The quantity
s(R)
equals the number of integers in the interval
[c-a_{1},c-1]
that are missing from the value group of
R
.

Proof.

Notice that
s(R)=\ell(\mathfrak{C}/(\mathfrak{C}\cap x_{1}R))
. Hence, we need to count the valuations that are in
\mathfrak{C}
but missing from the valuation set coming from
\mathfrak{C}\cap x_{1}R
. Since
\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{C}=x_{1}\mathfrak{C}\subseteq\mathfrak{C}\cap x_{1}R
, the only relevant valuations must come from
\mathfrak{C}\setminus\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{C}
. Hence, we can focus on the following generating set of
\mathfrak{C}
:
t^{c},\dots,t^{c+a_{1}-1}
.

Suppose for some
1\leqslant i\leqslant a_{1}
,
t^{c+a_{1}-i}=x_{1}\alpha
for some
\alpha\in R
. Then we get that
c+a_{1}-i=a_{1}+k
for some
k
lying in the value group of
R
. Hence,
k=c-i\in[c-a_{1},c]
. Thus the missing valuations from
[c-a_{1},c-1]
precisely correspond to the elements in the generating set which cannot be written as a multiple of
x_{1}
.
∎

Corollary 2.14
.

We have
s(R)=s(\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket)
.

Proof.

The proof is clear from
Theorem

2.13
.

3.
Numerical Semigroup Rings

For a semigroup
H
minimally generated by
b_{1}<\cdots<b_{m}
, we define a complete numerical semigroup
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{b_{1}},\dots,t^{b_{m}}}\rrbracket
. So for the numerical semigroup
v(R)
appearing in the previous section, we can generate a complete numerical semigroup ring
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket
.

It is a natural question whether our study can be transferred to the study of this numerical semigroup ring. For that we need the interpretations of type and reduced type in terms of valuations. One of the simplest relationships between
R
and
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket
is that their conductor valuations remain the same.

It is well-known that the type of a complete numerical semigroup ring
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket
is given by the cardinality of the pseudo-Frobenius set, denoted
\operatorname{PF}(H)
. It is defined as follows:

\operatorname{PF}(H)=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus H\mid x+h\in H\text{~for all~}0\neq h\in H\}.

The largest element in this set is called the Frobenius number of
H
, denoted
F(H)
. In our notations,
F(H)=c-1
.

In the previous section we saw that the reduced type is invariant under the study of
R
versus
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket
. But the type of
R
is more sensitive through this change of rings. The next result
Theorem

3.2
which appears in
[
BDF97
, Proposition II.1.16]
, captures this behaviour. We provide a proof for the convenience of the reader. Also, in the following
|S|
refers to the cardinality of a set
S
.

Remark 3.1
.

We recall that for any two ideals of
R
, we can identify
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(I,J)
with
J:I=\{\alpha\in\operatorname{Frac}(R)\mid\alpha I\subseteq J\}
via the following: for any non-zero
x,y\in I
and
f:I\to J
, we have
\frac{f(x)}{x}=\frac{f(y)}{y}
using
R
-linearity of
f
. Hence, for
0\neq r\in I
, we get
f(r)=r\frac{f(rx)}{rx}=r\frac{f(x)}{x}
, a multiplication by
\frac{f(x)}{x}\in\operatorname{Frac}(R)
.

Theorem 3.2
.

We have the inequality

s(R)\leqslant\operatorname{type}(R)\leqslant|\PF(v(R))|=\operatorname{type}(\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket).

Proof.

The first inequality follows from our discussion of reduced type above. For the second one, we make the following observations. Let
\omega_{R}
be a canonical ideal of
R
. Then
\operatorname{type}(R)=\ell(\omega_{R}/\mathfrak{m}\omega_{R})=\ell(\operatorname{Hom}(\omega_{R}/\mathfrak{m}\omega_{R},E(\mathbb{k})))
, where
E(\mathbb{k})
is the injective hull of
\mathbb{k}
. The latter equality is due to
[
BH98
, Proposition 3.2.12(b)]
while the former is due to
[
BH98
, Theorem 3.3.11(c)]
. Applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\cdot,\omega_{R})
to the exact sequence

0\to\mathfrak{m}\omega_{R}\to\omega_{R}\to\omega_{R}/\mathfrak{m}\omega_{R}\to 0,

we obtain

0\to\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\omega_{R},\omega_{R})\to\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\mathfrak{m}\omega_{R},\omega_{R})\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(\omega_{R}/\mathfrak{m}\omega_{R},\omega_{R})\to 0

where we used the facts that
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N)=0
if
M
is torsion and
N
is torsion-free, and also that
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(\omega_{R},\omega_{R})=0
(
[
BH98
, Theorem 3.3.10(c)(iii)]
) Now using local duality
[
BH98
, Theorem 3.5.8]
, we get that
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(\omega_{R}/\mathfrak{m}\omega_{R},\omega_{R})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(\omega_{R}/\mathfrak{m}\omega_{R}),E(\mathbb{k}))=\operatorname{Hom}(\omega_{R}/\mathfrak{m}\omega_{R},E(\mathbb{k}))
. Thus

\operatorname{type}(R)=\ell\Big(\frac{\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\mathfrak{m}\omega_{R},\omega_{R})}{\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\omega_{R},\omega_{R})}\Big).

Using the identification with colons as discussed before the theorem, we get that

\displaystyle\operatorname{type}(R)

\displaystyle=\ell\Big(\frac{\omega_{R}:\mathfrak{m}\omega_{R}}{\omega_{R}:\omega_{R}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\ell\Big(\frac{(\omega_{R}:\omega_{R}):\mathfrak{m}}{\omega_{R}:\omega_{R}}\Big)=\ell\Big(\frac{R:\mathfrak{m}}{R}\Big).

The first equality follows from Remark
3.1
while the last equality follows from
[
BH98
, Proposition 3.3.11(c)(ii)]
. Then
\ell(R:\mathfrak{m}/R)=|\left\{v(R:\mathfrak{m})\setminus v(R)\right\}|
. Since
v(R:\mathfrak{m})\subseteq\{x\in\mathbb{Z}\mid x+h\in v(R)\text{~for all~}h\in v(R)\}
, we have finished the proof.
∎

Thus the proof above shows that the type of
R
and the type of
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket
need not be the same. In fact, here is a concrete example which reflects this.

Example 3.3
.

[
BDF97
, Example II.1.19]

Let
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{4},t^{6}+t^{7},t^{11}}\rrbracket
. Then

v(R)=\{0,4,6,8,10,11,12,13,14,....\}
(we used that
t^{10}=t^{4}(t^{6}+t^{7})-t^{11}\in R
and
t^{13}=(t^{6}+t^{7})^{2}-(t^{4})^{3}-t^{10}t^{4}\in R
). Also,
v(R:\mathfrak{m})=\{0,4,6,7,8,9,...\}
. Thus,
\operatorname{type}(R)=|v(R:\mathfrak{m})\setminus v(R)|=2
. However,
\operatorname{PF}(v(R))=\{2,7,9\}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

The following statements hold.

(1)

The ring
R
is of minimal reduced type if and only if
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket
is of minimal reduced type.

(2)

The ring
R
is of maximal reduced type if
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket
is of maximal reduced type. The converse holds if additionally
\operatorname{type}(R)=\operatorname{type}(\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket)
.

Proof.

The case of minimal reduced type (i.e., when
s(R)=1
) follows from
Corollary

2.14
. For (2), we use
Theorem

3.2
and
Corollary

2.14
.
∎

The converse of Statement (2) in
Proposition

3.4
need not hold without the added assumption. The following example illustrates this.

Example 3.5
.

Let
R
be as in
Example

3.3
. Then from the description of
v(R)
, we see that
c=10
. Thus from
Theorem

2.13
, we get that
s(R)=2
since
\{7,9\}=[10-4,10-1]\setminus v(R)
. Thus
R
is of maximal reduced type but
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket
is not since its type is
3
whereas reduced type is
2
.

Corollary 3.6
.

Assume
R
is of minimal multiplicity. Then
R
is of maximal (resp. minimal) reduced type if and only if
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket
is of maximal (resp. minimal) reduced type.

Proof.

It is well-known that for a Cohen-Macaulay local ring with
e(R)>1
,
e(R)\geqslant\operatorname{type}(R)+1
and
R
is of minimal multiplicity if and only if
e(R)=\operatorname{type}(R)+1

[
HKS21
, Fact 2.6]
.

Since
e(R)=e(\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket)
, we get that
e(\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket)=\operatorname{type}(R)+1\leqslant\operatorname{type}(\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket)+1
using
Theorem

3.2
. But
\operatorname{type}(\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket)+1\leqslant e(\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket)
as discussed before.

Thus,
\operatorname{type}(v(R))=\operatorname{type}(\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket)
and now
Proposition

3.4
finishes the proof.
∎

The above discussions show that the study of maximal or minimal reduced type to complete numerical semigroup rings gives us information on the maximal or minimal reduced type for rings
R
.

About the notation for
R
: In the sequel, we will be studying the maximal and minimal reduced type for complete numerical semigroup rings. Essentially, as the above results suggest, studying the properties for
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket
gives us information on the properties for
R
. Now notice that
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
may not be a generating set for
v(R)
even though
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{\alpha_{1}t^{a_{1}},\dots,\alpha_{n}t^{a_{n}}}\rrbracket
. For example, for the ring
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{4},t^{6}+t^{7},t^{11}}\rrbracket
, the complete numerical semigroup ring is
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{4},t^{6},t^{11},t^{13}}\rrbracket
as opposed to
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{4},t^{6},t^{11}}\rrbracket
. Thus to avoid any confusion, we will be using a more general notation
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket
for a complete numerical semigroup as opposed to
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket
.

Proposition 3.7
.

For a numerical semigroup
H
, the ring
S=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket
has maximal reduced type if and only if
\min_{x\in\operatorname{PF}(H)}x\geqslant c-b_{1}
.

Proof.

It is clear that
[c-b_{1},c-1]\setminus v(S)\subseteq\operatorname{PF(H)}
by the definition of the latter. (This is another way of seeing in this case that
s(S)\leqslant\operatorname{type}(S)
). Thus,
S
is of maximal reduced type if and only if
\operatorname{PF}(H)=[c-b_{1},c-1]\setminus v(S)
if and only if
\min_{x\in\operatorname{PF}(H)}x\geqslant c-b_{1}
.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

For a numerical semigroup
H
, the ring
S=k\llbracket{H}\rrbracket
is of minimal reduced type if and only if
\displaystyle\max_{x\in\{\PF(H)\setminus\{c-1\}}x<c-b_{1}
.

Proof.

It is clear that
s(S)=1
if and only if
c-1
is the only integer in the interval
[c-b_{1},c-1]
that should be missing from
v(S)
. Hence, all the other elements in
\PF(H)
must be strictly less than
c-b_{1}
.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

In fact, it is clear that if
h\in H
, then
c-1-h\not\in\PF(H)
. Hence, the above statement can also be changed to minimal reduced type if and only if
\displaystyle\max_{x\in\{\PF(H)\setminus\{c-1\}}x\leqslant c-b_{1}-2
.

Corollary 3.10
.

For a numerical semigroup
H
, the ring, suppose
S=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket
has minimal multiplicity. Then
S
is of maximal reduced type if and only if
b_{2}\geqslant c
.

Proof.

Since
S
has minimal multiplicity,

\operatorname{PF}(H)=\{b_{2}-b_{1},b_{3}-b_{1},\dots,b_{n}-b_{1}\};

this follows from
[
RGS09
, Proposition 2.20, Proposition 3.31]
Thus,
S
has maximal reduced type if and only if
b_{2}-b_{1}\geqslant c-b_{1}
which completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.11
.

Suppose
S=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket
has minimal multiplicity. Then
S
is of minimal reduced type if and only if
b_{n-1}+b_{1}-1<b_{n}
.

Proof.

Since
S
has minimal multiplicity,

\operatorname{PF}(H)=\{b_{2}-b_{1},b_{3}-b_{1},\dots,b_{n}-b_{1}\};

this follows from
[
RGS09
, Proposition 2.20, Proposition 3.31]
. Thus,
S
has minimal reduced type if and only if
b_{n-1}-b_{1}<c-b_{1}
. But notice that
c-1=b_{n}-b_{1}
or
c=b_{n}-b_{1}+1
. Thus
S
has minimal reduced type if and only if

\displaystyle b_{n-1}-b_{1}<c-b_{1}=b_{n}-b_{1}+1-b_{1}=b_{n}-2b_{1}+1.

∎

In general, getting a complete classification of numerical semi-group rings of maximal or minimal reduced type seems tricky. We however can describe the condition abstractly using the notion of Apéry sets as well.

Recall that given
h\in H
, the Apéry set of
h
in
H
is defined to be

\operatorname{Ap}(H,h):=\{s\in H\mid s-h\not\in H\}.

One defines an order relation over the set of integers as follows:
a\leqslant_{H}b
if
b-a\in H
. Let
M(h)=\operatorname{Maximal}_{\leqslant_{H}}\operatorname{Ap}(H,h)
.

Proposition 3.12
.

Suppose
S=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket
is a complete numerical semi-group ring as above. Then
S
is of maximal reduced type if and only if

\min_{w\in M(e(R))}w\geqslant(\max\operatorname{Ap}(H,e(R))-e(R)+1.

Proof.

By
[
RGS09
, Proposition 2.20]
, we get that
\operatorname{PF}(H)=\{w-e(R)\mid w\in M(e(R))\}
. Furthermore, by
[
RGS09
, Proposition 2.12]
, we have that
c=(\max\operatorname{Ap}(H,e(R))-e(R)+1
. The proof is now complete by
Proposition

3.7
.
∎

3.1.
Almost Gorenstein Rings

Definition 3.13
.

[
BF97
, Definition-Proposition 20]

The ring
R
is called almost Gorenstein if

\ell(\overline{R}/R)=\ell(R/\mathfrak{C})+\operatorname{type}(R)-1.

This class of rings has recently attracted a lot of attention. See for instance
[
GMP13
]
,
[
GTT15
]
,
[
HHS19
]
,
[
DKT21
]
, etc. among other sources.

The characterization of almost Gorenstein property for numerical semigroup rings due to Nari is given below.

Theorem 3.14
.

[
Nar13
, Theorem 2.4]

Suppose
H
is a numerical semigroup with pseudo-Frobenius set
\PF(H)=\{f_{1},\dots,f_{t}\}
and
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket
is a numerical semigroup ring. Then
H
is almost Gorenstein if and only if
f_{i}+f_{t-i}=c-1
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant c-1
.

Proposition 3.15
.

Assume
R
is almost Gorenstein. Then
R
is of maximal (resp. minimal) reduced type if and only if
k\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket
is of maximal (resp. minimal) reduced type.

Proof.

Since
R
is almost Gorenstein, we have
\operatorname{type}(R)=\operatorname{type}(\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket)
by
[
BF97
, Proposition 29]
. The proof is now complete by
Proposition

3.4
.
∎

Proposition 3.16
.

Suppose the numerical semi-group ring
S=k\llbracket{H}\rrbracket
is almost Gorenstein. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

If
S
is maximal reduced type then
c\leqslant 2b_{1}-1
,

(2)

If
S
is of minimal reduced type but not Gorenstein then
c\geqslant 2b_{1}+3
.

Proof.

Let
r=\operatorname{type}(R)
and
\PF(v(R))=\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{r-1},c-1\}
with
f_{i}<f_{i+1}
. From
Theorem

3.14
, we see that
R
is almost Gorenstein if and only if
f_{i}+f_{r-i}=c-1
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant r-1
.

For
(a)
, assume that
R
has maximal reduced type. Hence, by
Proposition

3.7
,
f_{i}\geqslant c-b_{1}
for all
i
. Thus,
c-1=f_{i}+f_{r-i}\geqslant 2c-2b_{1}
which upon simplification yields our desired conclusion.

For
(b)
, assume
R
is of minimal reduced type. This implies that
f_{r-1}\leqslant c-b_{1}-2
. Thus,
c-1=f_{1}+f_{r-1}\leqslant 2c-2b_{1}-4
. Simplifying this, we get
c\geqslant 2b_{1}+3
.
∎

The following example shows that the converse of the above statements do not hold in general.

Example 3.17
.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{8},t^{11},t^{12},t^{13},t^{15},t^{17},t^{18}}\rrbracket
. Here
\PF(H)=\{4,5,7,9,10,14\}
and hence
R
is almost Gorenstein with
\operatorname{type}(R)=6
. Note however that
s(R)=4
. Thus,
R
is not of maximal reduced type even though
c\leqslant 2b_{1}-1
(here,
c=15,b_{1}=8
).

Example 3.18
.

[
Eto17
, Example 3.9]

Let
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{34},t^{51},t^{53},t^{70}}\rrbracket
. This is almost Gorenstein of type
3
since
\PF(H)=\{17,814,831\}
. Here
c=832
and hence satisfies
c\geqslant 2b_{1}+3
. However, notice that
s(R)=2
.

However, in almost Gorenstein numerical semigroup rings of type 2, the situation is very clear as the following corollary shows. Such rings are called
pseudo-symmetric
numerical semi-group rings.

Corollary 3.19
.

Suppose
S=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket
is an almost Gorenstein numerical semi-group ring of type
2
. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

S
is maximal reduced type if and only if
c\leqslant 2b_{1}-1
,

(2)

S
is of minimal reduced type if and only if
c>2b_{1}-1
.

Proof.

One of the directions for
(1)
follows from
Proposition

3.16
. For the converse, we first observe that since
\operatorname{type}(S)=2
, in the notation of the proof of
Proposition

3.16
, we get that
2f_{1}=c-1
. Hence,
\PF(H)=\{\frac{c-1}{2},c-1\}
.
Since
c\leqslant 2b_{1}-1
, we get that
\frac{c-1}{2}\geqslant c-b_{1}
and hence
R
is of maximal reduced type by
Proposition

3.7
. This proves (1).

Since
\operatorname{type}(S)=2
, if the ring is not of maximal reduced type, then it must be that
s(S)=1
. Thus, (2) follows directly from (1).
∎

The following examples illustrate both the cases.

Example 3.20
.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{4},t^{9},t^{11}}\rrbracket
. Then
\PF(H)=\{7,14\}
and hence
R
is almost Gorenstein of type 2. Moreover,
s(R)=1
by direct computation as well as by
Corollary

3.19
.

Example 3.21
.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{5},t^{6},t^{7},t^{9}}\rrbracket
. Here
\PF(H)=\{4,8\}
and hence
R
is almost Gorenstein of type
2
. Moreover,
s(R)=2
by direct computation as well as by
Corollary

3.19
.

3.2.
Far Flung Gorenstein Rings

Recently the notion of far-flung Gorenstein rings were introduced in
[
HKS21
]
. These are rings where the trace ideal of the canonical module equals
\mathfrak{C}
. We do not go into the details of trace ideals as we will not be using the notion in this article. We refer the reader to
[
Lin17
]
,
[
GIK20
]
and
[
HHS19
]
for further details. These rings are (evidently) non-Gorenstein, and hence
\operatorname{type}(R)\geqslant 2
(thus,
a_{1}=e(R)\geqslant 3
).

Proposition 3.22
.

Suppose
S=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket
is a far-flung Gorenstein complete numerical semigroup ring. Then
s(S)\geqslant 2
.

Proof.

It is well-known that there is a canonical module
\displaystyle\omega_{S}=\sum_{\alpha\in\operatorname{PF}(H)}St^{c-1-\alpha}
where
c
is the conductor valuation. Notice that
c-1\in\operatorname{PF}(H)
always. Thus,
t^{0}=1
is a generator of
\omega_{S}
. Moreover, from
[
HKS21
, Theorem 5.1]
, we get that
t^{1}
is also a generator. Hence,
\alpha=c-2\in\operatorname{PF}(H)
as well. But notice that
c-2\in[c-b_{1},c-1]\setminus v(R)
. Hence,
s(S)\geqslant 2
.
∎

Theorem 3.23
.

Suppose
S=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket
is a far-flung Gorenstein complete numerical semi-group ring of
\operatorname{type}(S)\leq 3
, Then
S
has maximal reduced type.

Proof.

From
Proposition

3.22
, the case when
\operatorname{type}(S)=2
is clear.

Suppose
\operatorname{type}(S)=3
and
S
has minimal multiplicity. Then we know that
b_{1}=e(S)=4=\operatorname{edim}(S)
(
[
Sal80
, Proposition 3.1]
). As in the proof of
Corollary

3.10
, we get that

\operatorname{PF}(H)=\{b_{2}-4,b_{3}-4,b_{4}-4\}

and hence
c-1=b_{4}-4
or
b_{4}=c+3
. Using the proof of
Proposition

3.22
, we see that
c-2\in\operatorname{PF}(H)
and hence,
b_{3}=c+2
. By
[
HKS21
, Corollary 6.2]
, we know that

\{2b_{4}-3,\cdots,2b_{4}\}\subseteq\{b_{i}+b_{j},2\leqslant i,j\leqslant 4\}.

Notice that
2b_{4}=2c+6,2b_{4}-1=2c+5=b_{3}+b_{4},2b_{4}-2=2c+4=2b_{3}
. If
b_{2}<c
,
2b_{4}-3
cannot be generated by
b_{i}+b_{j}
. Hence
b_{2}\geqslant c
and so we are done by
Corollary

3.10
.

Finally, suppose
\operatorname{type}(S)=3
and
S
is not of minimal multiplicity. First, we notice that
b_{1}=e(S)=5
by
[
HKS21
, Corollary 5.3]
. Choosing
\omega_{S}=\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\operatorname{PF}(H)}St^{c-1-\alpha}
as in the proof of
Proposition

3.22
, we know that
t^{0},t^{1}
are generators of
\omega_{S}
, i.e.,
c-1,c-2\in\operatorname{PF}(H)
. Since
\operatorname{type}(S)=3
,
[
HKS21
, Proposition 6.1 (ii)]
gives us that
\omega_{S}=\langle 1,t,t^{2}\rangle
or
\omega_{S}=\langle 1,t,t^{3}\rangle
.

Assume that
\omega_{S}=\langle 1,t,t^{2}\rangle
. Then we know that

\operatorname{PF}(H)=\{c-3,c-2,c-1\}.

Since
c-3>c-5
, we are done by
Proposition

3.7
. Next assume that
\omega_{R}=\langle 1,t,t^{3}\rangle
. Then

\operatorname{PF}(H)=\{c-4,c-2,c-1\}

and again
Proposition

3.7
finishes the proof.
∎

However, even if
S
is far-flung Gorenstein numerical semigroup of minimal multiplicity with
\operatorname{type}(S)\geqslant 4
, it is not guaranteed that the ring will be of maximal reduced type. The following example illustrates this.

Example 3.24
.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{5},t^{11},t^{17},t^{18},t^{19}}\rrbracket
. This ring is of minimal multiplicity with
e(R)=5,\operatorname{type}(R)=4,n=5
. Also notice that
R
is far-flung Gorenstein by
[
HKS21
, Corollary 6.2]
. However,
a_{2}=11<c=15
and hence
R
is not of maximal reduced type by
Corollary

3.10
.

If the ring is not of minimal multiplicity, then far flung Gorenstein rings of type
4
indeed have maximal reduced type.

Theorem 3.25
.

Suppose
S
is a far flung Gorenstein complete numerical semi-group ring of
\operatorname{type}(S)=4
which has non minimal multiplicity. Then
S
has maximal reduced type.

Proof.

As in the previous proof, choose
\omega_{S}=\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\operatorname{PF}(H)}t^{c-1-\alpha}
. Since
\operatorname{type}(S)=4
, we have
\omega_{S}=\langle 1,t,t^{a},t^{b}\rangle
where
a<b
. Also, it follows that
6\leqslant e(S)\leqslant 9
(
[
HKS21
, Corollary 5.3]
). Since
S
is far flung Gorenstein, by
[
HKS21
, Proposition 6.1]
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\{0,1,2,3,\cdots,e(R)-1\}\subseteq\{(c-1-\alpha)+(c-1-\beta)\mid\alpha,\beta\in\operatorname{PF}(H)\}.

Thus we have two cases for the description of
\omega_{R}
:

(1)

\omega_{S}=\langle 1,t,t^{2},t^{b}\rangle

(2)

\omega_{S}=\langle 1,t,t^{3},t^{b}\rangle

Notice that
c-1-b
is a minimal element in
\PF(H)
, the pseudo Frobenius set. Thus
R
is of maximal reduced type, if and only if
c-1-b\geqslant c-b_{1}
or
e(S)=b_{1}\geqslant b+1
.

Suppose
e(S)\leqslant b
. Now if case (1) holds, then
B\cap[5,e(S)-1]=\emptyset
which contradicts (
3.2
). On the other hand, if case(2) holds, then
B\cap\{5\}=\emptyset
, again contradicsts (
3.2
). Thus we have
e(S)\geqslant b+1
and hence
S
is of maximal reduced type.
∎

When the type is more than
4
, we need more tools to show that it is of maximum reduced type. In
[
HKS21
, Corollary 5.3]
, the authors connected the the maximum bound for the multiplicity with the
Rohrbach number

\overline{n}(r)
where
r=\operatorname{type}(R)
. The Rohrbach number is defined as follows: Suppose
A
is a set of non negative integers with cardinality
r
. Let
n(A)
is the integer such that
A+A=\{a+b~|~a,b\in A\}
contains consecutive integers
0,1,\dots n(A)-1
but not
n(A)
. The Rohrbach number is the integer

\displaystyle\overline{n}(r)=\max\{n(A)~|~|A|=r\}.

Theorem 3.26
.

Suppose
S=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket
is a far-flung Gorenstein numerical semigroup ring of type
r
. If
e(S)>\overline{n}(r-1)+1
, then
S
has maximal reduced type.

Proof.

Since
S
is a far-flung Gorenstein numerical semigroup ring, by
[
HKS21
, Theorem 5.1]
, there exists a canonical module
C=\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\rangle
where
n_{i}=v(f_{i}),n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots<n_{r}
such that
\{0,1,\dots,e-1\}\subseteq A+A
where
A=\{n_{1},\dots,n_{r}\}
. Here
e=e(S)
.

Suppose
n_{r}>e-1
. Then
\{0,1,\dots,e-1\}\subseteq A^{\prime}+A^{\prime}
where
A^{\prime}=\{n_{1},\dots,n_{r-1}\}
. Thus the consecutive set of numbers generated by
A^{\prime}+A^{\prime}
is
0,1,\dots,n(A^{\prime})-1
. But by hypothesis,
\overline{n}(r-1)<e-1
and thus, by definition we have
n(A^{\prime})\leqslant\overline{n}(r-1)<e-1
. This contradiction forces
n_{r}\leqslant e-1
. Thus the pseudo Frobenius set
PF(H)=\{c-1-n_{1},\dots,c-1-n_{r}\}
. Since
n_{r}\leqslant e-1
, we get
c-1-n_{r}\geqslant c-b_{1}
. Thus by
Proposition

3.7
, we have
R
is of maximum reduced type.
∎

The bound
\overline{n}(r-1)<e-1
may not be sharp. In the following result,
r=5,\overline{n}(r)-r+1=9
.

Proposition 3.27
.

Suppose

is far flung Gorenstein complete numerical semigroup ring of

, then

Proof.

As in the previous proof, choose

(3.2)

\displaystyle\{0,1,2,3,\cdots,e(S)-1\}\subseteq\{(c-1-\alpha)+(c-1-\beta)\mid\alpha,\beta\in\operatorname{PF}(H)\}.

Thus we have two cases for the description of

(1)

(2)

3.3.
Gluing of Numerical Semigroups

Definition 3.32
.

Let
H_{1}=\langle a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}\rangle
and
H_{2}=\langle b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m}\rangle
be two
numerical semigroups. Take
y\in H_{1}\setminus\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}\}
and
x\in H_{2}\setminus\{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m}\}

such that
(x,y)=1
. We say that

H=\langle xH_{1},yH_{2}\rangle=\langle xa_{1},xa_{2},\ldots,xa_{n},yb_{1},yb_{2},\ldots,yb_{m}\rangle

is a gluing of
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
.

The following results are well-known.

Theorem 3.33
.

[
Nar13
, Proposition 6.6]
Let
x,y,H_{1},H_{2}
be as in
Definition

3.32
and let
H=\langle xH_{1},yH_{2}\rangle
be the gluing. Let
S=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket
and
S_{i}=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H_{i}}\rrbracket
be the corresponding semigroup rings with
c_{S},c_{S_{i}}
denoting the respective conductor valuations. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

\displaystyle\PF(H)=\{xf+yg+xy\mid f\in\PF(H_{1}),g\in\PF(H_{2})\}.

(2)

\displaystyle c_{S}-1=x(c_{S_{1}}-1)+y(c_{S_{2}}-1)+xy.

(3)

\displaystyle\operatorname{type}(S)=\operatorname{type}(S_{1})\operatorname{type}(S_{2})
.

It is natural to expect a similar statement for the reduced type of the glued numerical semigroup ring
S
. This is precisely the content of the next theorem. We first observe that the multiplicity of the glued numerical semigroup ring is given by

\min\{x{a_{1}},y{b_{1}}\}.

Theorem 3.34
.

Let
S,S_{i}
be as in
Theorem

3.33
. Then

s(S)\leqslant s(S_{1})s(S_{2}).

Proof.

Let
\PF(H_{1})=\{f_{1}<f_{2}<\cdots<f_{\operatorname{type}(S_{1})}\}
and
\PF(H_{2})=\{g_{1}<g_{2}<\cdots<g_{\operatorname{type}(S_{2})}\}
. Observe that
f_{\operatorname{type}(S_{1})}=c_{S_{1}}-1,g_{\operatorname{type}(S_{2})}=c_{S_{2}}-1
. Also, notice that the multiplicity of
S
is
\min\{x{a_{1}},y{b_{1}}\}
.

Let
z\in[c_{S}-\min\{x{a_{1}},y{b_{1}}\},c_{S}-1]\setminus v(S)
. By
Theorem

2.13
, we need to count all such
z
to determine
s(S)
. Since
z\in\PF(H)
, by
Theorem

3.33
(1), we get that
z=xf_{i}+yg_{j}+xy
for some
f_{i}\in\PF(H_{1}),g_{j}\in\PF(H_{2})
.

Suppose
g_{j}\not\in[c_{S_{2}}-b_{1},c_{S_{2}}-1]
. Then we can write
g_{j}=c_{S_{2}}-b_{1}-k
with
k\geqslant 2
(since
c_{S_{2}}-b_{1}-1\not\in\PF(H_{2})
. Thus, we get

z=xf_{i}+yg_{j}+xy=xf_{i}+y(c_{S_{2}}-b_{1}-k)+xy.

Since
z\geqslant c_{S}-\min\{xa_{1},yb_{1}\}
, using
Theorem

3.33
(2), we get that

\displaystyle xf_{i}+y(c_{S_{2}}-b_{1}-k)+xy

\displaystyle\geqslant x(c_{S_{1}}-1)+y(c_{S_{2}}-1)+xy+1-\min\{xa_{1},yb_{1}\}

which upon simplification gives

\min\{xa_{1},yb_{1}\}-1\geqslant x(c_{S_{1}}-1-f_{i})+y(b_{1}+k-1)\geqslant y(b_{1}+k-1)\geqslant y(b_{1}+1).

This gives

yb_{1}-1\geqslant\min\{xa_{1},yb_{1}\}-1\geqslant y(b_{1}+1)

but this is a contradiction since
y>a_{1}\geqslant 1
.
Hence,
g_{j}\in[c_{S_{2}}-b_{1},c_{S_{2}}-1]
.

A similar argument with
f_{i}
shows that
f_{i}\in[c_{S_{1}}-a_{1},c_{S_{1}}-1]
. Since this holds for any such
z
, we conclude that
s(S)\leqslant s(S_{1})s(S_{2}).

∎

Corollary 3.35
.

Let
S,S_{i}
be as in
Theorem

3.33
. If
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are of minimal reduced type, then so is
S
.

Proof.

The proof is immediate from
Theorem

3.34
.
∎

However, the converse of the above statement need not hold, i.e., if
s(S)=1
, then
S_{1},S_{2}
simultaneously need not have minimal reduced type. The following example illustrates this.

Example 3.36
.

Consider
S_{1}
to be the ring in
Example

3.20
and
S_{2}
as in
Example

3.21
. Let

Then

. Here
s(S_{1})s(S_{2})=1\times 2=2
. However,
s(S)=1
: the conductor valuation
c_{S}=10(14)+13(8)+10\times 13+1=375
using
Theorem

3.33
(2). The multiplicity of

is
40

3.33
(1),

3.20

We do have the following statement in the converse direction which is reflected in

Theorem 3.37
.

Let
S,S_{i}
be as in

3.33
. If
s(S)=1
, then either
s(S_{1})=1
or
s(S_{2})=1
.

Proof.

We will prove the statement by contradiction. Assume that
s(S_{i})\geqslant 2
for

.
Let

be chosen as in the proof of

3.34

Consider

(the last part is due to

3.33
(1)). Since

(2). Since

\displaystyle c_{S}-1+y(1-b_{1}+k_{1})\leqslant c_{S}-\min\{xa_{1},yb_{1}\}-2

\displaystyle\min\{xa_{1},yb_{1}\}+1\leqslant y(b_{1}-1-k_{1})\leqslant y(b_{1}-1)

or

.

3.4.
Dual of numerical semigroup

In this section, we study the behaviour of

\operatorname{End}_{S}(\mathfrak{n}):=\operatorname{Hom}_{S}(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n})

where
S=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket
is a complete numerical semigroup ring and
\mathfrak{n}
is the maximal ideal of
S
. We also make the following notation. We have a surjection

\displaystyle\mathbb{k}\llbracket{Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m}}\rrbracket\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket

which gives us that
S=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket\cong\frac{\mathbb{k}\llbracket{Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m}}\rrbracket}{J}
where
J\subseteq(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m})^{2}
. Also,
y_{i}
denote the image of
Y_{i}
in
S
. Thus we denote
\mathfrak{n}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})
. Here notice that
\overline{S}=\overline{R}=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t}\rrbracket
.

By identifying with colons, we see that the endomorphism ring is the same as
\displaystyle\mathfrak{n}:\mathfrak{n}=S:\mathfrak{n}
. Clearly,
H\cup\PF(H)\subseteq v(\mathfrak{n}:\mathfrak{n})
. As in the proof of
Theorem

3.2
, notice that

\displaystyle\ell\left(\frac{\mathfrak{n}:\mathfrak{n}}{S}\right)=\operatorname{type}(S)=|\PF(H)|

Since
\ell\left(\frac{\mathfrak{n}:\mathfrak{n}}{S}\right)
computes the number of valuations
v(\mathfrak{n}:\mathfrak{n})
, but not in
H
. Now the cardinality of
H\cup\PF(H)
and
v(\mathfrak{n}:\mathfrak{n})
are the same. Thus we have the equality
H\cup\PF(H)=v(\mathfrak{n}:\mathfrak{n})
.

Let
B=\mathfrak{n}:\mathfrak{n}
. Notice that the conductor
C_{B}
of
B
has the form
\frac{\mathfrak{C}}{y_{1}}
where
\mathfrak{C}
is the conductor of
S
. For,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{B}=B:\overline{S}=(S:\mathfrak{n}):\overline{S}=S:\mathfrak{n}\overline{S}=S:(y_{1})\overline{S}=\frac{1}{y_{1}}(S:\overline{S})=\frac{1}{y_{1}}(\mathfrak{C}).

where
\mathfrak{C}=(t^{c})\overline{S}
. Thus the conductor
\mathfrak{C}_{B}=(t^{c-b_{1}})\overline{S}
(see for example,
[
BDF97
]
or
[
Nar13
, Proposition 3.4]
). The above discussion is precisely the motivation of the discussion that follows.

Definition 3.41
.

Let
H=\langle b_{1},\dots,b_{m}\rangle
be a numerical semigroup. Let
M
denote the maximal ideal of
H
. Then the dual
H^{*}
of
H
is the numerical semigroup
M-M=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}~|~x+m\in H\text{ for all }m\in M\}

Remark 3.42
.

Notice that
H\subseteq H^{*},\PF(H)\subseteq H^{*}
. Now let
\alpha\in H^{*}\backslash H
, then
\alpha+m\in H
for all
m\in M
. By definition
\alpha\in\PF(H)
. Thus
H^{*}=H\cup\PF(H)
. Thus for a numerical semigroup ring
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket
, we have
H^{*}=v(\mathfrak{n}:\mathfrak{n})=v(B)
. Thus we can set
B=\operatorname{End}_{S}(\mathfrak{n})=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H^{*}}\rrbracket
.

Thus it is clear that the multiplicity can drop as we move from
H
to
H^{*}
. However, the following proposition gives two conditions which guarantee that the multiplicities of
H
and
H^{*}
stay the same.

Proposition 3.43
.

The following statements hold for a numerical semigroup ring
S=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket
with
H=\langle b_{1},\cdots,b_{m}\rangle
and conductor valuation
c
.

(1)

Suppose
S
is of minimal reduced type but not Gorenstein. Then the multiplicity of
H
and
H^{*}
are equal. Moreover, the conductor valuation
c\geqslant 2b_{1}+3
.

(2)

Suppose
S
is Gorenstein. Then multiplicity of
H^{*}
is strictly less than
b_{1}
if and only if
S=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{2},t^{3}}\rrbracket
.

Proof.

For (1), using
[
Nar13
, Lemma 6.4]
, we observe that
c-1-f_{t-1}\leqslant f_{1}
where
\PF(H)=\{f_{1}<\cdots<f_{t}\}
with
t=\operatorname{type}(S)
. Since
S
is of minimal reduced type,
f_{t-1}\leqslant c-b_{1}-2
(
c-b_{1}-1\not\in\PF(H)
). Such an
f_{t-1}
exists as
t\geqslant 2
by assumption. Thus
c-1-f_{t-1}\geqslant c-1+2+b_{1}-c\geqslant b_{1}+1
. Thus we have

\displaystyle b_{1}+1\leqslant c-1-f_{t-1}\leqslant f_{1}

which proves that the multiplicity of
H
and
H^{*}
are equal. The last statement now follows by using the above established inequalities:

c\geqslant b_{1}+1+f_{t-1}+1=b_{1}+2+f_{1}\geqslant 2b_{1}+3.

For
(2)
, first notice that if
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{2},t^{3}}\rrbracket
, then
\PF(H)=\{1\}
and hence
H^{*}=\langle 1\rangle
, in particular the multiplicity dropped strictly. Conversely, assume that the multiplicity of
H^{*}
is strictly less than
b_{1}
. Since
R
is Gorenstein,
\PF(H)=\{c-1\}
. Hence, by
Remark

3.42
, we must have
c-1\leqslant b_{1}-1
, i.e.,
c\leqslant b_{1}
. But
c\in H
and hence
c=b_{1}
. Thus,
\mathfrak{C}=\mathfrak{m}
which shows that
S
has minimal multiplicity by
Remark

2.1
. Moreover,
b_{2}=b_{1}+1
since
\mathfrak{C}=\mathfrak{m}
. Since
R
is Gorenstein,
[
HKS21
, Fact 2.6]
immediately yields that
b_{1}=2
. So,
b_{2}=3
and
\operatorname{edim}(S)=b_{1}=2
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.44
.

Let
S=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket
with
c\geqslant b_{1}+1
and and is of minimal reduced type. Then
e(\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H^{*}}\rrbracket)=e(S)=b_{1}
.

Proof.

If
S
is not Gorenstein, then the result follows from
Proposition

3.43
(1). So assume
S
is Gorenstein. Since
c\geqslant b_{1}+1
,
S
cannot be
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{2},t^{3}}\rrbracket
and hence the result follows from
Proposition

3.43
(2).
∎

Now a natural question is to study the behaviour of
s(S)
under the construction of the dual semigroup. The following proposition sheds light upon this study.

Theorem 3.45
.

Suppose
S=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket
. If
\operatorname{End}_{S}(\mathfrak{n})
is of minimal reduced type and
e(\operatorname{End}_{S}(\mathfrak{n}))=e(S)
, then
S
is of minimal reduced type.

Proof.

Using
Remark

3.42
, we see that
H^{*}=v(\operatorname{End}_{S}(\mathfrak{n}))
. Suppose
H^{*}
is of minimal reduced type and the multiplicity of
H
and
H^{*}
are equal. From the discussion at the beginning of this subsection,
\mathfrak{C}_{B}=(t^{c-b_{1}})\overline{S}
. Thus
c-b_{1}-1\in\PF(H^{*})
. Since
s(B)=1
, every element of
[c-2b_{1},c-b_{1}-2]
is in
H^{*}=H\cup\PF(H)
.

Let
c-b_{1}+k\in[c-b_{1},c-2]
. Then
c-2b_{1}+k\in[c-2b_{1},c-b_{1}-2]\subseteq H^{*}
. Thus either
c-2b_{1}+k\in H
or
c-2b_{1}+k\in\PF(H)
. If
c-2b_{1}+k\in H
, then
c-2b_{1}+k+b_{1}=c-b_{1}+k\in H
. If
c-2b_{1}+k\in\PF(H)
, then
c-2b_{1}+k+b_{1}=c-b_{1}+k\in H
. Thus every member of
[c-b_{1},c-2]
is a member of
H
and hence
S
is of minimal reduced type by
Proposition

3.8
.
∎

In general, the minimality of reduced type need not stay preserved as the following examples illustrate.

Example 3.46
.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{4},t^{5},t^{6}}\rrbracket
. Then
\PF(H)=\{7\}
which shows that
R
is Gorenstein (hence
s(R)=1
). However,
B=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H^{*}}\rrbracket=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{4},t^{5},t^{6},t^{7}}\rrbracket
which is of minimal multiplicity (hence
\operatorname{type}(B)=3
) with conductor valuation
4
. Thus
s(B)=3
by
Corollary

3.10
.

Example 3.47
.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{40},t^{65},t^{78},t^{90},t^{91},t^{110},t^{117}}\rrbracket
. From
Example

3.36
, we know that
s(R)=1
and
\PF(H)=\{252,304,322,374\}
(so
R
is not Gorenstein). We construct the dual semigroup ring

B=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{40},t^{65},t^{78},t^{90},t^{91},t^{110},t^{117},t^{252},t^{304},t^{322},t^{374}}\rrbracket

which has conductor valuation
c_{B}=335
. Moreover,

\PF(H^{*})=\{187,212,213,226,232,239,257,264,282,283,284,296,309,334\}.

Thus
s(B)=3
.

Unfortunately, an analogue of the previous theorem for maximal reduced type does not hold. The following example establishes this.

Example 3.48
.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{5},t^{9},t^{11},t^{12}}\rrbracket
. Notice that
c=14
and
\PF(H)=\{6,7,13\}
. Here

and

, thus

is not of maximal reduced type. However,

Example

3.21
.

The following examples show that the multiplicity can drop even when we start with a ring of maximal reduced type. Moreover, the maximality of reduced type need not stay preserved.

Example 3.49
.

[
DMS23
, Example 7.14]

Let
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{4},t^{6},t^{7},t^{9}}\rrbracket

and

Example 3.50
.

Let

. Here
s(R)=2
with
\PF(H)=\{11,13\}
and

is of maximal reduced type. Note that

9

3.10
.

4.
The categories
\operatorname{Ref}(R)
and
\operatorname{CM}(R)

An
R
-module
M
is said to be reflexive if the natural map
M\to\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,R),R)
is an isomorphism whereas
M
is Maximal Cohen-Macaulay (MCM) if
0pt(M)=\dim(R)
. Let
\operatorname{CM}(R)
denote the category of maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-modules and let

\operatorname{Ref}(R)
denote the category of reflexive
R
-modules. We say a category is of
finite
type
if it has only finitely many indecomposable objects up to isomorphism.

Since we restrict our attention to one dimensional non-regular complete local domains containing an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{k}
of characteristic
0
, we know that
\displaystyle\{\text{free $R$- modules}\}\subseteq\operatorname{Ref}(R)\subseteq\operatorname{CM}(R)
; moreover,
R
is Gorenstein if and only if
\displaystyle\operatorname{Ref}(R)=\operatorname{CM}(R)
(
[
DMS23
, Remark 3.1]
). Thus it follows that if
\operatorname{CM}(R)
is of finite type, then
\operatorname{Ref}(R)
is also of finite type.

The study of finiteness of
\operatorname{CM}(R)
and the corresponding classification question of
R
has been of interest for quite some time. For more details on these results along with discussions on the notions of
ADE
-singularities, we refer the reader to
[
LW12
, Chapter 4 §3, Chapters 9, 10]
. For the convenience of the reader we also list the possible ADE hypersurface singularities as in
[
LW12
, §3, Page 70,71]

(1)

(A_{n}):x^{2}+y^{n+1},n\geqslant 1

(2)

(D_{n}):x^{2}y+y^{n-1},n\geqslant 4

(3)

(E_{6}):x^{3}+y^{4}

(4)

(E_{7}):x^{3}+xy^{3}

(5)

(E_{8}):x^{3}+y^{5}

We first discuss Gorenstein rings which are precisely the rings of both maximal and minimal reduced types. The following result is well-known in the literature and provides a complete picture in the Gorenstein setup for all dimensions.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
LW12
, Corollary 10.7]

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be an excellent, Gorenstein ring containing a
field of characteristic different from
2,3,5
, and let
K
be an algebraic closure
of
k
. Assume
d=\dim R\geqslant 1
and that
k
is perfect. Then
\operatorname{CM}(R)
is of finite
type if and only if there is a non-zero non-unit
f\in k\llbracket{x_{0},\cdots,x_{d}}\rrbracket
such that
\widehat{R}\cong k\llbracket{x_{0},\cdots,x_{d}}\rrbracket/(f)
and
K\llbracket{x_{0},\cdots,x_{d}}\rrbracket/(f)
is a complete
ADE
hypersurface singularity.

Note that in our notations, we have
d=1
,
\mathbb{k}=k=K
and
\widehat{R}=R
, where
\widehat{R}
denotes the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion of
R
. Thus, due to
Theorem

4.1
, it is enough to focus on non-Gorenstein rings.

Here, in order to study
\operatorname{CM}(R)
, we need the following results.

Theorem 4.2
.

[
LW12
, Theorem 4.10]
Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be an
analytically unramified local ring of
dimension one. Then
\operatorname{CM}(R)
is of finite type if and only if
e(R)\leqslant 3
and
\mu(\frac{\mathfrak{m}\overline{R}+R}{R})\leqslant 1
.

Remark 4.3
.

So,
\operatorname{CM}(R)
is of finite type for
R
only if
e(R)\leqslant 3
which in turn shows that either
R
is of minimal reduced type or of maximal reduced type.

Theorem 4.4
.

[
DMS23
, Propositions 6.12, 7.7]

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a one-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring.
Let
B=\operatorname{End}_{R}(\mathfrak{m})
. If
\operatorname{CM}(B)
is of finite type, then
\operatorname{Ref}(R)
is of finite type. Moreover, if
R
is almost Gorenstein, then the converse also holds.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}}\rrbracket/I
be a one dimensional complete local domain with
k
an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
with
\operatorname{edim}(R)\geqslant 2
. Let the integral closure
\overline{R}=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t}\rrbracket
. If
\operatorname{CM}(R)
is of finite type, then
\operatorname{CM}(\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket)
is of finite type for any numerical semigroup
H
with
R\subseteq\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket\subseteq\overline{R}
.

Proof.

Notice that
R\subseteq\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket\subseteq\operatorname{Frac}(R)
for any such given numerical semigroup
H
. Now the result is immediate from
[
LW12
, 4.14(iv)]
.
∎

Notice that
H
need not be
v(R)
in the above proof. For example if
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{3}+t^{4},t^{5},t^{7}}\rrbracket
, then
v(R)=\langle 3,5,7\rangle
and hence
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{3},t^{5},t^{7}}\rrbracket
, but
R\not\subseteq\mathbb{k}\llbracket{v(R)}\rrbracket
. But it is however true that
R\subseteq\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{3},t^{4},t^{5}}\rrbracket
. Thus, studying
\operatorname{CM}(R)
for a numerical semigroup ring of maximal or minimal reduced type can help to some extent in understanding
\operatorname{CM}(R)
. The link between the category of reflexive modules however is not so clear unless we have
R
Gorenstein to begin with. Nevertheless, we restrict our attention to
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket
for the rest of this section.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
S=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket
be a numerical semigroup ring which is not Gorenstein. Then
\operatorname{CM}(S)
is of finite type if and only if one of the following holds:

(1)

H=\langle 3,4,5\rangle

(2)

H=\langle 3,5,7\rangle
.

Proof.

We use the same notation as in the beginning of
Section

3.4
. Assume
\operatorname{CM}(S)
is of finite type. Then
e(S)\leqslant 3
by
Theorem

4.2
and since
S
is not Gorenstein, we get that
\operatorname{type}(S)=2
, i.e.,
S
is of minimal multiplicity. Hence
H=\langle 3,b_{2},b_{3}\rangle
. Now again by
Theorem

4.2
, we must have
\mu_{S}(\mathfrak{n}\overline{S}+S/S)\leqslant 1
. From
Remark

2.1
, we get that
L:=\frac{\mathfrak{n}\overline{S}+S}{S}=\frac{\mathfrak{n}\overline{S}}{(\mathfrak{n}\overline{S}\cap S)}=\frac{y_{1}\overline{S}}{\mathfrak{n}}
. Hence

1\geqslant\mu(L)=\ell\Big(\frac{y_{1}\overline{S}}{\mathfrak{n}y_{1}\overline{S}+\mathfrak{n}}\Big)=\ell\Big(\frac{y_{1}\overline{S}}{y_{1}^{2}\overline{S}+\mathfrak{n}}\Big).

Now
v(y_{1}\overline{S})=\{3,4,5,\rightarrow\}
whereas
v(y_{1}^{2}\overline{S}+\mathfrak{n})=\{3,b_{2},b_{3}\}\cup\{6,7,8,\rightarrow\}
. Thus the only possibilities of
b_{2}
are
4,5
. Suppose
b_{2}=4
. Notice that the ring
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{3},t^{4}}\rrbracket
has the conductor
\mathfrak{C}=(t^{6})\overline{R}
. Thus any element
t^{i},i\geqslant 6
can be generated by
t^{3},t^{4}
. Now since
\operatorname{edim}S=3
, we must have
b_{3}=5
. Similarly, if
b_{2}=5
, then
b_{3}=7
(the conductor of
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{3},t^{5}}\rrbracket
is
(t^{8})\overline{S}
). This finishes one direction of the proof.

Conversely, if
H
is of the two forms as stated, then the same argument in the above paragraph shows that
L
is generated by at most one element in both cases. Since
e(S)=3
, we can now apply
Theorem

4.2
.
∎

Theorem 4.7
.

If
S=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket
is of minimal reduced type, then
\operatorname{CM}(S)
is of finite type if and only if
S
is a complete
ADE
hypersurface singularity of multiplicity
2
or
3
.

Proof.

If
S
is non-Gorenstein with
\operatorname{CM}(S)
of finite type, then
S
is of maximal reduced type using
Corollary

3.10
on the two forms of
H
we obtained in
Theorem

4.6
. So, if
S
is of minimal reduced type with
\operatorname{CM}(S)
of finite type, then
S
must be Gorenstein. The conclusion for the last part of the statement now follows from
Theorem

4.1
.
∎

Theorem 4.8
.

Suppose
S=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket

B=\operatorname{End}_{R}(\mathfrak{m})
. Assume that
e(S)=e(B)
. Then
\operatorname{CM}(B)
is of finite type if and only if one of the following holds:

(1)

H=\langle 3,7,8\rangle

(2)

H=\langle 3,8,10\rangle
.

In particular, if
S
is non-almost Gorenstein of minimal reduced type, then
\operatorname{CM}(B)
is not of finite type.

Proof.

Since
S
is not almost Gorenstein,
B
is not Gorenstein by
[
GMP13
, Theorem 5.1]
. So, by
Theorem

4.6
, we get that
B
is constructed either by

and

is not Gorenstein, it follows that

\PF(H)=\{b_{2}-3,b_{3}-3\}

shows that

. If

, then

and
b_{3}=8
. If

Conversely, given the forms of

as in the statement, it is clear that

is given by either
\langle 3,4,5\rangle
or
\langle 3,5,7\rangle

4.2
.

For the last part, we use
Proposition

3.43
(1) along with the observation that both the forms of

given in the statement of the above corollary are of maximal reduced type by
Corollary

3.10
.
∎

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
S=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H}\rrbracket
be an almost Gorenstein ring of minimal reduced type and
\operatorname{type}(S)\geqslant 2
. Then
\operatorname{Ref}(S)
is of finite type if and only if

(1)

\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{3},t^{7},t^{11}}\rrbracket

(2)

\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{3},t^{8},t^{13}}\rrbracket
.

Proof.

Throughout the proof, we set
B=\operatorname{End}_{S}(\mathfrak{n})=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{H^{*}}\rrbracket
. By
Theorem

4.4
, we know that
\operatorname{Ref}(S)
is of finite type if and only if
\operatorname{CM}(B)
is of finite type.

Assume
S
is of the forms (1) or (2). The type of
S
is 2 and the pseudo Frobenius set is
\{4,8\}
or
\{5,10\}
respectively. Thus
B=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{3},t^{4}}\rrbracket
or
B=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{3},t^{5}}\rrbracket
respectively. These rings have ADE hypersurface singularities and hence
\operatorname{CM}(B)
is of finite type by
Theorem

4.1
.

Conversely, assume that
\operatorname{Ref}(S)
is of finite type. So
\operatorname{CM}(B)
is of finite type and hence
e(B)\leqslant 3
by
Theorem

4.1
(1). Since
s(S)=1
, we get that
e(S)\leqslant 3
by
Proposition

3.43
(1). Since
\operatorname{type}(S)\geqslant 2
, it forces
e(S)=3
and
\operatorname{type}(S)=2
, i.e.,
S
has minimal multiplicity
3
. Hence,
\operatorname{edim}(S)=3
, Let
H=\langle 3,b_{2},b_{3}\rangle
. We can describe
S
completely using
[
RGS09
, Lemma 4.27]
. We include the proof here for convenience of the reader: since

. Thus, taking

, we get that

. Further, since

. Finally notice that

. This upon simplification gives
b_{3}\geqslant b_{2}+4

,

Question 4.10
.

Can we classify all numerical semigroup rings of minimal reduced type which are not almost Gorenstein but

Proposition 4.11

Theorem 4.12

,

(2)

\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{b_{1}},t^{b_{1}+2},t^{b_{1}+3},\ldots,t^{2b_{1}-1},t^{2b_{1}+1}}\rrbracket

,

By

4.9
and
4.2

S
is of minimal multiplicity, we see that
S=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{b_{1}},t^{b_{1}+3},t^{b_{1}+4},\ldots,t^{2b_{1}-1},t^{2b_{1}+1},t^{2b_{1}+2}}\rrbracket
with
b_{1}\geqslant 4
. However,
\PF(H)=\{3,4,\ldots,b_{1}-1,b_{1}+1,b_{1}+2\}
. This is not almost Gorenstein since
3+b_{1}+1\neq b_{1}+2
by
Theorem

3.14
.

Thus
e(B)=3=\operatorname{edim}(B)
, i.e.,
B
is of minimal multiplicity.
Then by
Theorem

4.6
,
B=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{3},t^{4},t^{5}}\rrbracket
or
B=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{3},t^{5},t^{7}}\rrbracket
. Moreover,
S
cannot be of minimal multiplicity and hence
b_{1}=e(S)\geqslant 4
(because
\operatorname{type}(S)\geqslant 2
). Since
t^{3}\in B
,
3\in\PF(H)
. Thus
b_{i}=b_{1}+3
for some
i\geqslant 2
. Since
b_{1}\geqslant 4
and
3\in\PF(H)
, we have
3\geqslant c-b_{1}
as
S
has maximal reduced type. If
3>c-b_{1}
, then
c-b_{1}
is in the valuation semigroup of
S
which is a contradiction as
1\leqslant c-b_{1}<3<b_{1}
. Notice that the first inequality is because of the fact that
S
is not of minimal multiplicity and hence
c>b_{1}
. Thus
c-b_{1}=3
or
c=b_{1}+3
and hence
c-1=b_{1}+2
is not a member of the valuation semigroup of
S
. Thus the only choice of
S
with these properties is
S=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{b_{1}},t^{b_{1}+1},t^{b_{1}+3},t^{b_{1}+4},\ldots,t^{2b_{1}-1}}\rrbracket,b_{1}\geqslant 4
. Moreover, notice that
\PF(H)=\{3,4,5,\ldots,b_{1}-1,b_{1}+2\}
. Hence
S
is almost Gorenstein by
Theorem

3.14
. In other words
v(B)=\langle 3,4,5\rangle
or
B=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{3},t^{4},t^{5}}\rrbracket
. Interestingly, notice that
B
cannot take the form
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{3},t^{5},t^{7}}\rrbracket
as mentioned in the beginning of this paragraph.

Conversely, if
S=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{b_{1}},t^{b_{1}+1},t^{b_{1}+3},t^{b_{1}+4},\ldots,t^{2b_{1}-1}}\rrbracket,b_{1}\geqslant 4
, then
v(B)=\langle 3,4,5\rangle
and hence
B=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{3},t^{4},t^{5}}\rrbracket
. Thus
\operatorname{CM}(B)
is of finite type (by
Theorem

4.6
), equivalently
\operatorname{Ref}(S)
is of finite type.
∎

Question 4.14
.

Can we classify all numerical semigroup rings of maximal reduced type which are not almost Gorenstein but
\operatorname{Ref}(S)
is of finite type?

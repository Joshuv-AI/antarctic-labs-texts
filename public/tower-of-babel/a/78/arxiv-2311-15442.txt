Dimension-free estimates on l 2 ​ ( ℤ d ) l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}) for discrete dyadic maximal function over l 1 l^{1} balls: small scales
Jakub Niksiński

Abstract
. We give a dimension-free bound on l p ​ ( ℤ d ) l^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}) for the discrete Hardy-Littlewood operator over the l 1 l^{1} balls in ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} with small dyadic radii, where p ∈ [ 2 , ∞ ] p\in[2,\infty] .

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a convex, bounded, closed symmetric subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with non-empty interior, we will call such
G
a symmetric convex body. Natural examples are the
l^{q}
balls:

B^{q}=\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|x\|_{q}=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}\leq 1\Big\}\ \text{for}\ q\in[1,\infty),

B^{\infty}=\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|x\|_{\infty}=\max_{1\leq i\leq d}|x_{i}|\leq 1\Big\}.

With each symmetric convex body one can associate the corresponding Hardy-Littlewood averaging operator. For any
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we define

M_{t}^{G}f(x)=\frac{1}{|t\cdot G|}\int_{t\cdot G}f(x-y)\ dy,

for a locally integrable function
f
, where
t\cdot G=\{tx:x\in G\}
and
|t\cdot G|
denotes its Lebesgue measure. Now for any
p
let
C_{p}(d,G)>0
be the smallest number such that the following inequality

\|\sup_{t>0}|M_{t}^{G}f|\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{p}(d,G)\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

holds for every
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. It is well known that
C_{p}(d,G)<\infty
for all
p\in(1,\infty]
and all symmetric convex bodies
G
.

In 1980s dependency of
C_{p}(d,G)
on dimension
d
has begun to be studied. Various results were obtained, but as of now the major conjecture in this topic is still open, namely that
C_{p}(d,G)
can be bounded from the above by a number independent of dimension
d
for each fixed
p\in(1,\infty]
. We recommend the survey article
[
3
]
for a detailed exposition of the subject, which contains results that we skipped mentioning.

Similar questions can be considered for discrete analogue of the operator
M_{t}^{G}
. For every
t>0
and every
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we define the discrete Hardy-Littlewood averaging operator

\mathcal{M}_{t}^{G}=\frac{1}{|(t\cdot G)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|}\sum_{y\in t\cdot G\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f(x-y),\ \ f\in l^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}),

where
|(t\cdot G)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|
is the number of elements of the set
(t\cdot G)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Similarly as before we define
\mathcal{C}_{p}(d,G)>0
to be the smallest number such that the following inequality

\|\sup_{t>0}|\mathcal{M}_{t}^{G}f|\|_{l^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq\mathcal{C}_{p}(d,G)\|f\|_{l^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}

holds for every
f\in l^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
. Using similar methods as in the continuous case one can show that for any
p\in(1,\infty]
and any symmetric convex body we have
\mathcal{C}_{p}(d,G)<\infty
.

What about dependency of
\mathcal{C}_{p}(d,G)
on the dimension
d
?
We can ask similar questions as in the continuous setup, yet it turns out that the situation is much more delicate. Indeed in
[
1
]
the authors constructed a family of ellipsoids
E(d)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with the property that for each
p\in(1,\infty]
there exists
C_{p}>0
such that for every
d\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\mathcal{C}_{p}(d,E(d))\geq C_{p}(\log d)^{1/p}.

This means that if we want to establish dimension-free estimates for
\mathcal{C}_{p}(d,G)
we need to restrict ourselves to specific sets
G
, which contain more symmetries; one of the simpler options are
B^{q}
balls.

Literature in the discrete setting is not as fruitful, nevertheless there are some papers and positive results in this regard, for example:

•

In
[
1
]
it was proved that for every
p\in(\frac{3}{2},\infty]
there exists a constant
C_{p}>0
such that for every
d\in\mathbb{N}
and every
f\in l^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
we have

\|\sup_{t>0}|\mathcal{M}_{t}^{B^{\infty}}f|\|_{l^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{l^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

This result is as strong as the ones in continuous case. Unfortunately in the case of sets
B^{q}
for
q\neq\infty
authors of papers
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
6
]
could only obtain way weaker conclusions.

•

In
[
2
]
it was proved that for every
p\in[2,\infty)
there exists
C_{p}>0
such that for every
d\in\mathbb{N}
and every
f\in l^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
we have

\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D}}|\mathcal{M}_{t}^{B^{2}}f|\|_{l^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{l^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})},

where
\mathbb{D}=\{2^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

•

In
[
6
]
by extending methods of
[
2
]
it was proved that for any
q\in(2,\infty)
and any
p\in[2,\infty)
there exists constant
C(p,q)>0

such that for every
d\in\mathbb{N}
and every
f\in l^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
we have

\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\geq d^{1/q}}|\mathcal{M}_{t}^{B^{q}}f|\|_{l^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq C(p,q)\|f\|_{l^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})},

where
\mathbb{D}=\{2^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}.
The paper
[
6
]
did not cover the range
t<d^{1/q}
nor
q<2
.

In this paper we will prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
p\in[2,\infty)
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
d\geq 4
and any
f\in l^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
we have

\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},\ t\leq\sqrt{d}}|\mathcal{M}_{t}^{B^{1}}f|\|_{l^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq 40^{2/p}\|f\|_{l^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})},

where
\mathbb{D}=\{2^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
is the set of dyadic integers.

As far as the author is aware the result above is new. It is a small step beyond the results of
[
6
]
in the case
q=1
. Additionally we give explicit values to various constants. In the proof of Theorem
1.1
we will rely on methods shown in the
[
2
, Section 3]
. Right at the beginning we will replace
t\cdot B^{1}
by the simpler set
S_{t}=\{x\in\{-1,0,1\}^{d}:\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}|=t\}
and then consider only
S_{t}
. Later on we merely use tools from
[
2
, Section 3]
.
This replacement by a simpler set is easy due to the fact that there exists a formula for the number of lattice points in
t\cdot B^{1}
. This is not the case for other sets of the form
t\cdot B^{q}
, where
q\in(1,\infty)
. We restrict ourselves to
t\leq\sqrt{d}
due to the fact that then
(t\cdot B^{1})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
consists mostly of the points from
S_{t}
(see Lemma
2.4
). This is not the case when
t
is sufficiently big in terms of
d
and we would need different argument in that range.
Throughout the rest of this paper we will consider
B^{q}
only for
q=1
.

1.1.
Notation

(1)

For
t>0
we define
B_{t}=\{(x_{1},...,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}|\leq t\}
.

(2)

\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,...\}
will denote the set of positive integers and
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
will denote the set of non-negative integers.
\mathbb{D}=\{2^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
is the set of dyadic integers. We define

\mathbb{N}_{N}=\{1,2,...,N\}
.

(3)

We define
e(x)=e^{2\pi ix}
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(4)

We have standard scalar product on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

x\cdot y=\sum_{k=1}^{d}x_{k}y_{k},

where
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(5)

For
f,g\in l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
we define
f\ast g\in l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
by the series

f\ast g(x)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f(y)g(x-y)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f(x-y)g(y),

which is absolutely convergent for each
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

(6)

If
f\in l^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
we define the discrete Fourier transform by the formula

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f(x)e(x\cdot\xi),\ \text{ for }\ \xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

One can extend the discrete Fourier transform to
f\in l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
. Then it turns out that
\widehat{f}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. We have the following Parseval identity:

\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=\|f\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

Moreover, for any
f,g\in l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}),

\widehat{f\ast g}(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi).

\mathcal{F}^{-1}
will denote the inverse of the discrete Fourier transform, that is

\mathcal{F}^{-1}(G)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}G(\xi)e(-x\cdot\xi)\ d\xi,

where
G\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.

(7)

We let
m_{t}
be the multiplier symbol

m_{t}(\xi)=\frac{1}{|B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|}\sum_{x\in B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e(x\cdot\xi).

(8)

\mathbb{T}^{d}
will denote the
d
-dimensional torus, which will be identified with the set
[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})^{d}.

(9)

Sym(d)
will denote the permutation group of
\{1,2,...,d\}
.

Definition 1.2
.

The discrete Hardy-Littlewood averaging operator of the
l^{1}
ball is defined for any function
f:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
by the formula

\mathcal{M}_{t}f(x)=\frac{1}{|B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|}\sum_{y\in B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f(x-y).

Our goal is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

For any
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
d\geq 4
and any
f\in l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
we have

\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},\ t\leq\sqrt{d}}|\mathcal{M}_{t}f|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq 40\|f\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

Using a complex interpolation argument one can show that Theorem
1.3
implies Theorem
1.1
.
Notice that the operator
\mathcal{M}_{t}
is given by convolution, that is

\mathcal{M}_{t}f(x)=f\ast K_{t}(x),

where

K_{t}(y)=\frac{1}{|B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|}\mathds{1}_{B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(y).

Since we are considering
l^{2}
norms, using Fourier theory we can reduce our problem to understanding pointwise bounds of the following function

m_{t}(\xi)=\widehat{K_{t}}(\xi)=\frac{1}{|B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|}\sum_{x\in B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e(x\cdot\xi).

This will be explained in detail in the next sections.

2.
Estimate for
|B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|
and its consequences.

The goal of this section is to approximate function
m_{t}(\xi)
by a simpler function; it will be obtained by approximating
|B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|
by the number of lattice points in a simpler set.

Definition 2.1
.

For
t\in\mathbb{N}
we define

S_{t}=\{x\in\{-1,0,1\}^{d}:\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}|=t\},

moreover we define function
s_{t}:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
by formula

s_{t}(\xi)=\frac{1}{|S_{t}|}\sum_{x\in S_{t}}e(x\cdot\xi),

next we introduce averaging operator
\mathcal{S}_{t}
related to the above multiplier by the formula

\mathcal{S}_{t}f(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(s_{t}\hat{f})(x),\ \text{for any}f\in l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}).

Notice that
S_{t}\subseteq B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
t\in\mathbb{N},t\leq d
the following formulas hold

(2.1)

|S_{t}|=2^{t}\binom{d}{t}

(2.2)

|B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|=\sum_{l=0}^{t}2^{l}\binom{d}{l}\binom{t}{l}.

These formulas are well known. We give a proof for completeness.

Proof.

For equality (
2.1
) we notice that there are
\binom{d}{t}
options to choose
t
coordinates out of
d
coordinates, on which we have value
-1
or
1
. Next we can choose each sign in
2
ways, thus we obtain
|S_{t}|=2^{t}\binom{d}{t}.

For equality (
2.2
) we consider the following disjoint decomposition

B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}=\bigcup_{k=0}^{t}A_{k},

where

A_{k}=\{(x_{1},...,x_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}|=k\}.

Now for each
k\in\{0,1,...,t\}
we will compute
|A_{k}|
. We have
|A_{0}|=1
. Consider
k\geq 1
. Then

A_{k}=\bigcup_{l=1}^{k}\{\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}|=k,\ \textbf{x}\text{ has $l$ exactly nonzero coordinates }\}.

In the union above each
l
-th individual set has size

2^{l}\binom{d}{l}\cdot|\{(y_{1},...,y_{l})\in\mathbb{N}^{l}:y_{1}+...+y_{l}=k\}|=2^{l}\binom{d}{l}\cdot\binom{k-1}{l-1}.

Indeed, we have
\binom{d}{l}
options of choosing nonzero coordinates and then
2^{l}
options of choosing signs. The last equality above is classical "stars and bars" problem from combinatorics, see
[
4
, p. 38]
. Hence for
k\geq 1

|A_{k}|=\sum_{l=1}^{k}2^{l}\binom{d}{l}\cdot\binom{k-1}{l-1},

from which it follows

|B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|=\sum_{k=0}^{t}|A_{k}|=1+\sum_{k=1}^{t}\sum_{l=1}^{k}2^{l}\binom{d}{l}\cdot\binom{k-1}{l-1}

=1+\sum_{l=1}^{t}2^{l}\binom{d}{l}\sum_{k=l}^{t}\binom{k-1}{l-1}=1+\sum_{l=1}^{t}2^{l}\binom{d}{l}\binom{t}{l}=\sum_{l=0}^{t}2^{l}\binom{d}{l}\binom{t}{l}.

In the penultimate equality above we used the following fact: for any
t\geq l\geq 1
we have

\binom{t}{l}=\sum_{k=l}^{t}\binom{k-1}{l-1}.

One can prove this equality by segregating
l
-element subsets of the set
\{1,...,t\}
based on biggest number which they contain. For each
k
there are
\binom{k-1}{l-1}
subsets of
\{1,...,t\}
with
l
elements, whose biggest number is
k
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Notice that
|S_{t}|
is equal to term corresponding to
k=t
in the sum
(
2.2
)
. When
t
is small in terms of
d
then
\binom{d}{k+1}
is roughly
d
times bigger than
\binom{d}{k}
for each
k\leq t
; because of that we expect
|B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|
to be similar in size to
|S_{t}|
when
t
is sufficiently small in terms of
d
. Lemma
2.4
quantifies our expectations.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
t,d\in\mathbb{N}
be numbers such that
d\geq 4
,
1\leq t\leq\sqrt{d}
. Then the following inequalities hold

\frac{t^{2}}{2d}\leq\frac{|B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|-|S_{t}|}{|S_{t}|}\leq e\frac{t^{2}}{d}.

Proof.

Assumptions
d\geq 4
and
t\leq\sqrt{d}
imply that
2t\leq d
.

Using formulas from Lemma
2.2
we get that

\frac{|B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|-|S_{t}|}{|S_{t}|}=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}2^{k}\binom{t}{k}\binom{d}{k}}{2^{t}\binom{d}{t}}=\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}2^{k-t}\binom{t}{k}\frac{t!}{k!}\frac{(d-t)!}{(d-k)!}

=\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}2^{k-t}\binom{t}{k}\frac{t!}{k!}\frac{1}{(d-k)(d-k-1)...(d-t+1)}<\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}2^{k-t}\binom{t}{k}\frac{t!}{k!}(d-t)^{k-t}

=\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}2^{k-t}\binom{t}{k}^{2}(t-k)!(d-t)^{k-t}=\sum_{u=1}^{t}(2(d-t))^{-u}\binom{t}{u}^{2}u!=\sum_{u=1}^{t}a_{u}.

In the penultimate equality above we changed summation index by substitution
u=t-k
and used the fact that
\binom{t}{k}=\binom{t}{t-k}
, in the last equality we denoted each term of the sum by
a_{u}
.

Then for each
1\leq u\leq t-1
we have that
a_{u+1}\leq\frac{t^{2}}{d}\frac{1}{u+1}a_{u}
. Indeed,

a_{u+1}=(2(d-t))^{-u-1}\binom{t}{u+1}^{2}(u+1)!=(2(d-t))^{-u-1}\frac{t!^{2}}{(u+1)!(t-u-1)!^{2}}

=(2(d-t))^{-u-1}\frac{t!^{2}}{u!(t-u)!^{2}}\cdot\frac{(t-u)^{2}}{u+1}=\frac{(t-u)^{2}}{2(d-t)(u+1)}\cdot(2(d-t))^{-u}\binom{t}{u}^{2}u!

=\frac{(t-u)^{2}}{2(d-t)(u+1)}a_{u}\leq\frac{t^{2}}{2(d-t)(u+1)}a_{u}\leq\frac{t^{2}}{d}\frac{1}{u+1}a_{u}.

Iterating the above estimate we get

a_{u+1}\leq\frac{t^{2}}{d}\frac{1}{u+1}a_{u}\leq\Big(\frac{t^{2}}{d}\Big)^{2}\frac{1}{(u+1)\cdot u}a_{u-1}\leq...

\leq\Big(\frac{t^{2}}{d}\Big)^{u}\cdot\frac{1}{(u+1)\cdot u\cdot...\cdot 2}a_{1}=\Big(\frac{t^{2}}{d}\Big)^{u}\cdot\frac{1}{(u+1)!}a_{1}.

However, using the fact that
2t\leq d
we obtain that

a_{1}=\frac{t^{2}}{2(d-t)}\leq\frac{t^{2}}{d},

which implies

a_{u+1}\leq\Big(\frac{t^{2}}{d}\Big)^{u+1}\cdot\frac{1}{(u+1)!}

for any
0\leq u\leq t-1
, hence by
t\leq\sqrt{d}
we get

\sum_{u=1}^{t}a_{u}\leq\sum_{u=1}^{t}\frac{\Big(\frac{t^{2}}{d}\Big)^{u}}{u!}<e\frac{t^{2}}{d}.

Thus we proved the upper bound.

On the other hand the lower bound follows from estimating undermentioned sum by the term
k=t-1
.

\frac{|B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|-|S_{t}|}{|S_{t}|}=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}2^{k}\binom{t}{k}\binom{d}{k}}{2^{t}\binom{d}{t}}=\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}2^{k-t}\binom{t}{k}\frac{t!}{k!}\frac{(d-t)!}{(d-k)!}

>\frac{1}{2}\binom{t}{t-1}\frac{t!}{(t-1)!}\frac{(d-t)!}{(d-t+1)!}=\frac{t^{2}}{2(d-t+1)}\geq\frac{t^{2}}{2d}.

∎

In the paper we will use only the upper bound of Lemma
2.4
. Its main application is given in the Corollary
2.5
below.

Corollary 2.5
.

For
d,t\in\mathbb{N}
such that
d\geq 4
,
1\leq t\leq\sqrt{d}
and any
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
the following inequality holds

|m_{t}(\xi)-s_{t}(\xi)|\leq 2e\frac{t^{2}}{d}.

Proof.

Using the disjoint decomposition
B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}=S_{t}\cup\Big(B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap S_{t}\Big)
and the upper bound from Lemma
2.4
we obtain that

|m_{t}(\xi)-s_{t}(\xi)|=\Big|\frac{1}{|B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|}\sum_{x\in B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e(x\cdot\xi)-\frac{1}{|S_{t}|}\sum_{x\in S_{t}}e(x\cdot\xi)\Big|

\leq\Big|\sum_{x\in S_{t}}e(x\cdot\xi)\Big|\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{|S_{t}|}-\frac{1}{|B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|}\Big)+\frac{1}{|B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|}\Big|\sum_{x\in B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus S_{t}}e(x\cdot\xi)\Big|

\leq 2\frac{|B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|-|S_{t}|}{|B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|}=2\frac{|S_{t}|}{|B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|}\cdot\frac{|B_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|-|S_{t}|}{|S_{t}|}\leq 2e\frac{t^{2}}{d}

∎

The simple bound above has consequences in terms of norm estimates for the corresponding maximal dyadic operator. Proof of the next lemma is based on Lemma
2.4
and a square function estimate.

Lemma 2.6
.

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
d\geq 4
and any
f\in l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
the following inequality holds

\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|(\mathcal{M}_{t}-\mathcal{S}_{t})f|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq 6\|f\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

Proof.

Take any
f\in l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, then we have

\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|(\mathcal{M}_{t}-\mathcal{S}_{t})f|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq\|\Big(\sum_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|(\mathcal{M}_{t}-\mathcal{S}_{t})f|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}

=\Big(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|(\mathcal{M}_{t}-\mathcal{S}_{t})f(x)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}=\Big(\sum_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}\sum_{\ x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|(\mathcal{M}_{t}-\mathcal{S}_{t})f(x)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

=\Big(\sum_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}\||(\mathcal{M}_{t}-\mathcal{S}_{t})f|\|^{2}_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\Big)^{1/2}=\Big(\sum_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|m_{t}(\xi)-s_{t}(\xi)|^{2}|\widehat{f}|^{2}d\xi\Big)^{1/2}

\leq 2e\Big(\sum_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}\frac{t^{4}}{d^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\widehat{f}|^{2}d\xi\Big)^{1/2}=\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\cdot\frac{2e}{d}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor\log_{2}(\sqrt{d})\rfloor}2^{4n}\Big)^{1/2}

=\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\cdot\frac{2e}{d}\Big(2^{4}\cdot\frac{2^{4\lfloor\log_{2}(\sqrt{d})\rfloor}-1}{2^{4}-1}\Big)^{1/2}\leq\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\cdot\frac{2e}{d}\Big(\frac{16}{15}\cdot d^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\leq 6\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=6\|f\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

The second inequality above comes from Corollary
2.5
.
In the above reasoning multiple times we made use of Parseval identity and the fact that
\mathcal{M}_{t}(f)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(m_{t}\cdot\widehat{f})
,
\mathcal{S}_{t}(f)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(s_{t}\cdot\widehat{f})
.
∎

3.
Estimates for the multiplier
s_{t}
.

In the proof of Lemma
2.6
we have seen how a pointwise bound of
|m_{t}(\xi)-s_{t}(\xi)|
impacted

\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|(\mathcal{M}_{t}-\mathcal{S}_{t})f|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}
. The bound in Corollary
2.5
is fairly strong and uniform in
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Unfortunately we will not be that lucky in the future.
Our goal for now is to obtain bounds on
|s_{t}(\xi)|
for big
\xi
(then we expect some decay in
\xi
) and
|s_{t}(\xi)-1|
for small
\xi
.

Proof of the next lemma exploits in a simple way invariance of the set
S_{t}
under both permutation of coordinates and sign changes of each coordinate.

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
d,t\in\mathbb{N}
,
t\leq d
and every
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
we have

|s_{t}(\xi)-1|\leq 2\frac{t}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{j}).

Proof.

Notice that for any
\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{d}
we have that

x\in S_{t}\iff\epsilon x\in S_{t},

where
\epsilon x=(\epsilon_{1}x_{1},...,\epsilon_{d}x_{d})
.
This implies that

s_{t}(\xi)=\frac{1}{|S_{t}|}\sum_{\epsilon x\in S_{t}}e(\epsilon x\cdot\xi)=\frac{1}{|S_{t}|}\sum_{x\in S_{t}}e(\epsilon x\cdot\xi).

Hence

s_{t}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{d}}s_{t}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{d}}\frac{1}{|S_{t}|}\sum_{x\in S_{t}}e(\epsilon x\cdot\xi)

=\frac{1}{|S_{t}|}\sum_{x\in S_{t}}\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{d}}e(\epsilon x\cdot\xi)=\frac{1}{|S_{t}|}\sum_{x\in S_{t}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\cos(2\pi x_{j}\xi_{j}).

Note that for any sequences of complex numbers
\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d},\{b_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d}
such that
\max_{1\leq j\leq d}|a_{j}|\leq 1
and
\max_{1\leq j\leq d}|b_{j}|\leq 1
we have

(3.1)

\Big|\prod_{j=1}^{d}a_{j}-\prod_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}\Big|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{d}|a_{j}-b_{j}|,

This follows from an easy induction argument. Using (
3.1
) and the formula
\cos(2x)=1-2\sin^{2}(x)
we obtain

|s_{t}(\xi)-1|\leq\frac{1}{|S_{t}|}\sum_{x\in S_{t}}\Big|\prod_{j=1}^{d}\cos(2\pi x_{j}\xi_{j})-1\Big|\leq\frac{1}{|S_{t}|}\sum_{x\in S_{t}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\cos(2\pi x_{j}\xi_{j})-1|

=\frac{2}{|S_{t}|}\sum_{x\in S_{t}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{j}\xi_{j})\leq\frac{2}{|S_{t}|}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})\cdot\sum_{x\in S_{t}}x_{j}^{2}.

In the last inequality we used the fact that
|\sin(xy)|\leq|x||\sin(y)|
for any
x\in\mathbb{Z},y\in\mathbb{R}
(this can be proved by only considering
x\in\mathbb{N}
and induction with respect to
x
using formula for
\sin(a+b)
).

Now to understand the sum
\sum_{x\in S_{t}}x_{j}^{2}
we make use of the fact that
S_{t}
is closed under permutations of coordinates, that is for any
\sigma\in Sym(d)
we have

x\in S_{t}\iff\sigma(x)\in S_{t},

where
\sigma(x)=(x_{\sigma(1)},x_{\sigma(2)},...,x_{\sigma(d)})
.
Using similar argument as in the beginning of the proof one can show that for any
j\in\{1,...,d\}
we have

\sum_{x\in S_{t}}x_{j}^{2}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{x\in S_{t}}\|x\|_{l^{2}}^{2}.

Thus

|s_{t}(\xi)-1|\leq\frac{2}{|S_{t}|}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})\cdot\sum_{x\in S_{t}}x_{j}^{2}=\frac{2}{|S_{t}|}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})\cdot\frac{1}{d}\sum_{x\in S_{t}}\|x\|_{l^{2}}^{2}

=\frac{2}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})\cdot\frac{1}{|S_{t}|}\sum_{x\in S_{t}}\|x\|_{l^{1}}=2\frac{t}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{j}).

Above we used the fact that any for any
x\in S_{t}
we have
\|x\|_{l^{2}}^{2}=\|x\|_{l^{1}}
(since
x
has coordinates in the set
\{-1,0,1\}
) and that
\|x\|_{l^{1}}=t
.
∎

3.1.
Krawtchouk polynomials

Definition 3.2
.

(Krawtchouk polynomial). For every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

k\in\{0,1,...,n\}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
we define
k
-th Krawtchouk polynomial by the formula

\mathds{kr}_{k}^{(n)}(x)=\frac{1}{\binom{n}{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{x}{j}\binom{n-x}{k-j},

if
x
is not integer, then
\binom{x}{j}=\frac{x(x-1)...(x-j+1)}{j!}
.

Next theorem describes important facts regarding Krawtchouk polynomials.

Theorem 3.3
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and integers
x,k\in[0,n]
we have

(1)

Symmetry:
\mathds{kr}_{k}^{(n)}(x)=\mathds{kr}_{x}^{(n)}(k)
.

(2)

Reflection symmetry:
\mathds{kr}^{(n)}_{k}(n-x)=(-1)^{k}\mathds{kr}_{k}^{(n)}(x)
.

(3)

Uniform bound: For any
x,k\leq n/2
we have

|\mathds{kr}^{(n)}_{k}(x)|\leq e^{-ckx/n},

where
c=-2\log(0.93)=0.14514...

The proof of the first two points is contained in
[
7
]
, the last point is
[
5
, Lemma 2.2]
. The value of the constant
c
is not explicitly given in
[
5
]
, however one can deduce it from its proof.
From now on we define

c=-2\log(0.93)=0.14514...

Lemma 3.4
.

For any
t,d\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
t\leq d/2
we have

(3.2)

|s_{t}(\xi)|\leq\exp(-\frac{ct}{2d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sin(\pi\xi_{i})^{2})+\exp(-\frac{ct}{2d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\cos(\pi\xi_{i})^{2}).

Proof.

We will proceed as in the later part of the proof of
[
2
, Proposition 3.3]
.
Using the same symmetry invariance arguments as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
we have the following equalities

s_{t}(\xi)=\frac{1}{|S_{t}|}\sum_{y\in S_{t}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\cos(2\pi y_{i}\xi_{i})=\frac{1}{|S_{t}|}\sum_{y\in S_{t}}\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\sigma\in Sym(d)}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\cos(2\pi y_{\sigma(i)}\xi_{i}).

It is sufficient to prove that for any
y\in S_{t}
we have that

\Big|\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\sigma\in Sym(d)}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\cos(2\pi y_{\sigma(i)}\xi_{i})\Big|

\leq\exp(-\frac{ct}{2d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sin(\pi\xi_{i})^{2})+\exp(-\frac{ct}{2d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\cos(\pi\xi_{i})^{2}).

Fix any
y\in S_{t}
, notice that we have following disjoint decomposition.

(3.3)

Sym(d)=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{d}\\
|J|=t\end{subarray}}\{\sigma\in Sym(d):|y_{\sigma(j)}|=1\iff j\in J\}.

Notice that each set under the union has size
(d-t)!\cdot t!
.
Since cosine is even, from (
3.3
) we obtain

\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\sigma\in Sym(d)}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\cos(2\pi y_{\sigma(i)}\xi_{i})=\frac{1}{\binom{d}{t}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{d}\\
|J|=t\end{subarray}}\prod_{j\in J}\cos(2\pi\xi_{j}).

For any
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{d}
we define

a_{S}(\xi)=\prod_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{d}\setminus S}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})\cdot\prod_{i\in S}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i}).

For any set
J\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{d}
such that
|J|=t
we define
\varepsilon(J)\in\{-1,1\}^{d}
to be vector, such that
\varepsilon(J)_{j}=-1
exactly when
j\in J
. Then we have

\prod_{j\in J}\cos(2\pi\xi_{j})=\prod_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{d}}\Big(\frac{1+\cos(2\pi\xi_{j})}{2}+\varepsilon(J)_{j}\frac{1-\cos(2\pi\xi_{J})}{2}\Big)

=\prod_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{d}}\big(\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})+\varepsilon(J)_{j}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})\big)=\sum_{S\subset\mathbb{N}_{d}}a_{S}(\xi)w_{S}(\varepsilon(J)),

where
w_{S}:\{-1,1\}^{d}\to\{-1,1\}
is defined by the formula
w_{S}(\varepsilon)=\prod_{j\in S}\varepsilon_{j}
. Now by changing the order of summation we obtain

\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\sigma\in Sym(d)}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\cos(2\pi y_{\sigma(i)}\xi_{i})=\sum_{S\subset\mathbb{N}_{d}}a_{S}(\xi)\frac{1}{\binom{d}{t}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{d}\\
|J|=t\end{subarray}}w_{S}(\varepsilon(J)).

Notice that for fixed
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{d}
the following equalities hold

\frac{1}{\binom{d}{t}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{d}\\
|J|=t\end{subarray}}w_{S}(\varepsilon(J))=\frac{1}{\binom{d}{t}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{d}\\
|J|=t\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|S\cap J|}=\frac{1}{\binom{d}{t}}\sum_{j=0}^{t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{d}\\
|J|=t\\
|S\cap J|=j\end{subarray}}(-1)^{j}

=\frac{1}{\binom{d}{t}}\sum_{j=0}^{t}(-1)^{j}\binom{|S|}{j}\binom{d-|S|}{t-j}=\mathds{kr}_{t}^{(d)}(|S|).

Using the above we get

\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\sigma\in Sym(d)}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\cos(2\pi y_{\sigma(i)}\xi_{i})=\sum_{S\subset\mathbb{N}_{d}}a_{S}(\xi)\mathds{kr}_{t}^{(d)}(|S|).

If
|S|\leq d/2
then by our assumption that
t\leq d/2
and point
3
of Theorem
3.3
we get

|\mathds{kr}_{t}^{d}(|S|)|\leq e^{-\frac{c|S|t}{d}},

otherwise if
|S|>d/2
then using reflection symmetry and point
3
of Theorem
3.3
we obtain

|\mathds{kr}_{t}^{d}(|S|)|=|\mathds{kr}_{t}^{d}(d-|S|)|\leq e^{-\frac{c(d-|S|)t}{d}}.

This gives us

\frac{1}{d!}\Big|\sum_{\sigma\in Sym(d)}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\cos(2\pi y_{\sigma(i)}\xi_{i})\Big|\leq\sum_{S\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{d}}a_{S}(\xi)e^{-\frac{c|S|t}{d}}+\sum_{S\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{d}}a_{S}(\xi)e^{-\frac{c(d-|S|)t}{d}}

=\sum_{S\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{d}}\prod_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{d}\setminus S}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})\cdot\prod_{j\in S}e^{-\frac{ct}{d}}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})+\sum_{S\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{d}}\prod_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{d}\setminus S}e^{-\frac{ct}{d}}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})\cdot\prod_{j\in S}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})

=\prod_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{d}}\big(\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})+e^{-\frac{ct}{d}}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})\big)+\prod_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{d}}\big(e^{-\frac{ct}{d}}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})+\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})\big)

=\prod_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{d}}\big(1-(1-e^{-\frac{ct}{d}})\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})\big)+\prod_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{d}}\big(1-(1-e^{-\frac{ct}{d}})\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})\big)

\leq\exp\Big(-(1-e^{-\frac{ct}{d}})\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})\Big)+\exp\Big(-(1-e^{-\frac{ct}{d}})\sum_{j=1}^{d}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})\Big)

\leq\exp\Big(-\frac{ct}{2d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})\Big)+\exp\Big(-\frac{ct}{2d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})\Big).

In the penultimate inequality we used inequality
1-x\leq e^{-x}
for
x\geq 0
. In the ultimate inequality we used
x/2\leq 1-e^{-x}
for
0\leq x\leq 1/2
. This concludes the proof of Lemma
3.4
. ∎

4.
Introduction of new multipliers

Now we will define a pair of Fourier multipliers, whose corresponding maximal operators are bounded independently of the dimension by a general theory. Then we will try to approximate
s_{t}
pointwise by these multipliers.

Definition 4.1
.

For any
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
and any
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0},t\leq d
we define

(4.1)

\lambda_{t}^{1}(\xi)=\exp\Big(-\frac{t}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi)\Big),

(4.2)

\lambda_{t}^{2}(\xi)=(-1)^{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{t}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi)\Big).

Proof of the following theorem is a consequence of the theory of symmetric diffusion semigroups, see
[
8
, p. 73]
and
[
2
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

For any
f\in l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
the following inequality holds

\|\sup_{t>0}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\lambda_{t}^{1}\widehat{f})|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq 2\|f\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

Notice that
\lambda_{t}^{2}(\xi)=(-1)^{t}\lambda_{t}^{1}(\xi+\frac{1}{2})
, where
\xi+\frac{1}{2}=(\xi_{1}+\frac{1}{2},...,\xi_{d}+\frac{1}{2})
. Because of that we have

\sup_{\|f\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}=1}\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{N},t\leq d}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\lambda_{t}^{2}\widehat{f})|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}=\sup_{\|f\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}=1}\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{N},t\leq d}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\lambda_{t}^{1}\widehat{f})|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

We will approximate
s_{t}(\xi)
by
\lambda_{t}^{1}(\xi)
or
\lambda_{t}^{2}(\xi)
depending on which term in the right hand side of inequality (
3.2
) we expect to be bigger. Define

\Xi_{1}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}:\sum_{i=1}^{d}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\geq d/2\},\ \ \Xi_{2}=\mathbb{T}^{d}\setminus\Xi_{1}.

Then we have the following crucial inequalities.

Lemma 4.3
.

For every
t,d\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
t\leq d/2
and every
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
we have

(1)

if
\xi\in\Xi_{1}
, then

(4.3)

|s_{t}(\xi)-\lambda_{t}^{1}(\xi)|\leq 3\min\Big\{\exp\Big(-\frac{ct}{2d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\Big),\frac{t}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\Big\},

(2)

if
\xi\in\Xi_{2}
, then

(4.4)

|s_{t}(\xi)-\lambda_{t}^{2}(\xi)|\leq 3\min\Big\{\exp\Big(-\frac{ct}{2d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\Big),\frac{t}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\Big\}.

Proof.

Take
\xi\in\Xi_{1}
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i}).

Using Lemma
3.4
and the above, we obtain

|s_{t}(\xi)|\leq\exp\Big(-\frac{ct}{2d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\Big)+\exp\Big(-\frac{ct}{2d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\Big)

\leq 2\exp\Big(-\frac{ct}{2d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\Big),

so that

|s_{t}(\xi)-\lambda_{t}^{1}(\xi)|\leq 3\exp\Big(-\frac{ct}{2d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\Big).

On the other hand using Lemma
3.1
and inequality
1-e^{-x}\leq x
we obtain

|s_{t}(\xi)-\lambda_{t}^{1}(\xi)|\leq|s_{t}(\xi)-1|+|1-\lambda_{t}^{1}(\xi)|\leq 3\frac{t}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i}).

This proves (
4.3
).

Now let
\xi\in\Xi_{1}
. Then
\xi+\frac{1}{2}=(\xi_{1}+\frac{1}{2},...,\xi_{d}+\frac{1}{2})\in\Xi_{2}
,

\lambda_{t}^{2}(\xi)=(-1)^{t}\lambda_{t}^{1}(\xi+\frac{1}{2})
and

s_{t}(\xi)=\frac{1}{|S_{t}|}\sum_{x\in S_{t}}e(x\cdot\xi)=\frac{1}{|S_{t}|}\sum_{x\in S_{t}}(-1)^{\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}}e(x\cdot(\xi+\frac{1}{2}))

=\frac{1}{|S_{t}|}\sum_{x\in S_{t}}(-1)^{\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}|}e(x\cdot(\xi+\frac{1}{2}))=(-1)^{t}s_{t}(\xi+\frac{1}{2}).

By (
4.3
) we get

|s_{t}(\xi)-\lambda_{t}^{2}(\xi)|=|s_{t}(\xi+\frac{1}{2})-\lambda_{t}^{1}(\xi+\frac{1}{2})|

\leq 3\min\Big\{\exp\Big(-\frac{ct}{2d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i}+\frac{\pi}{2})\Big),\frac{t}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i}+\frac{\pi}{2})\Big\}

=3\min\Big\{\exp\Big(-\frac{ct}{2d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\Big),\frac{t}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\Big\}.

∎

5.
Conclusions and proof of the main theorem

We are almost ready to prove the main theorem. The next lemma is simple, however we give a proof to calculate the explicit constant
\frac{5}{3}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
we have

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\min\{4^{k}x,(4^{k}x)^{-1}\}\leq\frac{5}{3}

Proof.

If
x>1
then

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\min\{4^{k}x,(4^{k}x)^{-1}\}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}4^{-k}x^{-1}=\frac{4}{3}x^{-1}<\frac{4}{3}.

If
x\leq 1
, then let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
be such that
4^{k}x=s\in(\frac{1}{4},1]
. This gives us

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\min\{4^{n}x,(4^{n}x)^{-1}\}=\sum_{n=0}^{k}4^{n}x+\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}(4^{n}x)^{-1}

=\sum_{n=0}^{k}4^{n-k}s+\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}(4s)^{-1}\cdot 4^{k+1-n}\leq(s+\frac{1}{4s})\cdot\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}4^{-n}=\frac{4}{3}(s+\frac{1}{4s})\leq\frac{5}{3},

above we used the fact that function
(\frac{1}{4},1]\ni s\mapsto s+\frac{1}{4s}
is bounded by
\frac{5}{4}
.
∎

We are finally ready to prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proof.

Take any
f\in l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
we have that

\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|\mathcal{M}_{t}f|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|(\mathcal{M}_{t}-\mathcal{S}_{t})f|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}+\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|\mathcal{S}_{t}f|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

By Lemma
2.6
the first term above is bounded by
6\|f\|_{l^{2}}
. For the second term let
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
with
\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)\mathds{1}_{\Xi_{1}}(\xi)
and

\widehat{f_{2}}(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)\mathds{1}_{\Xi_{2}}(\xi)
. Then we have

\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|\mathcal{S}_{t}f|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}=\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(s_{t}\widehat{f})|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}

\leq\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(s_{t}\widehat{f_{1}})|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}+\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(s_{t}\widehat{f_{2}})|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}

\leq\sum_{i=1}^{2}\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\lambda_{t}^{i}\widehat{f_{i}})|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big((s_{t}-\lambda_{t}^{i})\widehat{f_{i}}\big)|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

By Theorem
4.2
the first sum is bounded by

2\|f_{1}\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}+2\|f_{2}\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq 2\sqrt{2}\big(\|f_{1}\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}^{2}+\|f_{2}\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}^{2}\big)^{1/2}=2\sqrt{2}\|f\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

To finish the proof it remains to estimate for
i=1,2
the following term

\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big((s_{t}-\lambda_{t}^{i})\widehat{f_{i}}\big)|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

We will only bound the above for
i=1
, the proof for
i=2
is basically the same.
From Lemma
4.3
we have that if
\xi\in\Xi_{1}
, then

|s_{t}(\xi)-\lambda_{t}^{1}(\xi)|\leq 3\min\Big\{\exp\Big(-\frac{ct}{2d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\Big),\frac{t}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\Big\}

\leq 3\min\Big\{\Big(\frac{ect}{2d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\Big)^{-1},\frac{t}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\Big\}

\leq\frac{6}{ec}\min\Big\{\Big(\frac{t}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\Big)^{-1},\frac{t}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\Big\}.

Using Lemma
5.1
for any
\xi\in\Xi_{1}
we obtain

\sum_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|s_{t}(\xi)-\lambda_{t}^{1}(\xi)|^{2}\leq\Big(\frac{6}{ec}\Big)^{2}\cdot\sum_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}\min\Big\{t^{-2}\Big(\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\Big)^{-2},t^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\Big)^{2}\Big\}

\leq\Big(\frac{6}{ec}\Big)^{2}\cdot\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\min\Big\{4^{-k}\Big(\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\Big)^{-2},4^{k}\Big(\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\Big)^{2}\Big\}\leq\Big(\frac{6}{ec}\Big)^{2}\cdot\frac{5}{3}.

Finally the following is true

\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big((s_{t}-\lambda_{t}^{1})\widehat{f_{1}}\big)|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq\|\Big(\sum_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big((s_{t}-\lambda_{t}^{1})\widehat{f_{1}}\big)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}

=\Big(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big((s_{t}-\lambda_{t}^{1})\widehat{f_{1}}\big)(x)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}=\Big(\sum_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big((s_{t}-\lambda_{t}^{1})\widehat{f_{1}}\big)(x)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

=\Big(\sum_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}\||\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big((s_{t}-\lambda_{t}^{1})\widehat{f_{1}}\big)|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

=\Big(\sum_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|s_{t}(\xi)-\lambda_{t}^{1}(\xi)|^{2}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\mathds{1}_{\Xi_{1}}(\xi)d\xi\Big)^{1/2}

\leq\frac{6}{ec}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{5}{3}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\mathds{1}_{\Xi_{1}}(\xi)d\xi\Big)^{1/2}=\frac{6}{ec}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{5}{3}}\|\widehat{f_{1}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=\frac{6}{ec}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{5}{3}}\|f_{1}\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

Similarly we obtain

\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big((s_{t}-\lambda_{t}^{2})\widehat{f_{2}}\big)|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq\frac{6}{ec}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{5}{3}}\|f_{2}\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

Which gives us

\sum_{i=1}^{2}\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big((s_{t}-\lambda_{t}^{i})\widehat{f_{i}}\big)|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq

\frac{6}{ec}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{5}{3}}\cdot\Big(\|f_{1}\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}+\|f_{2}\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\Big)\leq\frac{6}{ec}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{10}{3}}\|f\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

Combining all of the estimates we finally get

\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|\mathcal{M}_{t}f|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|(\mathcal{M}_{t}-\mathcal{S}_{t})f|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}+\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|\mathcal{S}_{t}f|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}

\leq\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|(\mathcal{M}_{t}-\mathcal{S}_{t})f|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\lambda_{t}^{i}\widehat{f_{i}})|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}

+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq\sqrt{d}}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big((s_{t}-\lambda_{t}^{i})\widehat{f_{i}}\big)|\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}

\leq\|f\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\Big(6+2\sqrt{2}+\frac{6}{ec}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{10}{3}}\Big)\leq 40\|f\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

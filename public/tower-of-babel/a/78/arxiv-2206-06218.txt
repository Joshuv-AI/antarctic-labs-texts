Abstract




Abstract

A 3-graph
\mathcal{F}
is
U(s,2s+1)
if for any
s
edges
e_{1},...,e_{s}\in E(\mathcal{F})
,
|e_{1}\cup...\cup e_{s}|\leq 2s+1
. Frankl and Kupavskii (2020) proposed the following conjecture: For any
3
-graph
\mathcal{F}
with
n
vertices, if
\mathcal{F}
is
U(s,2s+1)
, then

e(\mathcal{F})\leq\max\left\{{n-1\choose 2},(n-s-1){s+1\choose 2}+{s+1\choose 3},{2s+1\choose 3}\right\}.

In this paper, we confirm Frankl and Kupavskii’s conjecture.

Keywords
: stability; matching; matching number

A

\mathcal{F}

V(\mathcal{F})
and an edge set

. A hypergraph
\mathcal{F}
is

k

k

k
-graph for short. In particular, 2-graph is called graph.
A
matching
in a hypergraph
\mathcal{F}
is a set of pairwise disjoint edges in
\mathcal{F}
.
A
perfect matching
in a
k
-graph is a matching that covers every vertex. A
maximum matching
in a
k
-graph is a matching of maximum size among all matchings in the graph.
For a
k
-graph
\mathcal{F}
, we use
\nu(\mathcal{F})
to
denote the size of a maximum matching in
\mathcal{F}
. Let

G_{1}=(V_{1},E_{1})
and

be two graphs such that

. The

of graphs

and

denoted by

is the graph with vertex set
V_{1}\cup V_{2}
and edge set
E_{1}\cup E_{2}
. The
join
of graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
denoted by
G_{1}\vee G_{2}
is the graph union
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
together with all the edges joining
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
. For a graph
G
, we denote
the complement of
G
by
\overline{G}
.

We often identify
E(\mathcal{F})
with
\mathcal{F}
when there is no confusion and in particular,
|\mathcal{F}|
denotes the number of
edges in
\mathcal{F}
.
Given
T\subseteq V(\mathcal{F})
, let
\mathcal{F}-T
denote the subgraph of

\mathcal{F}
with vertex set
V(\mathcal{F})\setminus T
and edge set
\{e\in\mathcal{F}\ :\ e\subseteq V(\mathcal{F})\setminus T\}
.
Given a vertex set
X
and integer

, let
{X\choose k}=\{S\subseteq X\ |\ |S|=k\}
. Let
n\geq 1
be an integer and let

. For a
k
-graph
\mathcal{F}
and

N_{\mathcal{F}}(v)=\{e\in{V({\mathcal{F}})\choose k-1}\ |\ e\cup\{v\}\in E(\mathcal{F})\}
. When there is no confusion, we use

denote the
(k-1)
-graph with vertex set

and edge set
N_{\mathcal{F}}(v)
.
Let
m,i
be two integers such that
1\leq i\leq m\leq(n-2)/2
. Let
K^{0}_{i}
,
K^{1}_{2m-2i+1}
and
K^{2}_{n-2m+i-1}

. Let
A^{0}_{n,m}:=K^{1}_{2m+1}\cup\overline{K^{2}_{n-2m-1}}
.

Let
p
,
r
be two positive integers such that
p\geq r
. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{p,r}:=\mathcal{A}(p,r,n,k):=\{A\in{[n]\choose k}:|A\cap[p]|\geq r\}.

Let

. A

\displaystyle|e_{1}\cup...\cup e_{s}|\leq q

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1}:=\mathcal{A}(1,1,n,3),\ \ \mathcal{F}_{2}:=\mathcal{A}(s+1,2,n,3),\ \ \mathcal{F}_{3}:=\mathcal{A}(2s+1,3,n,3),

we can calculate

from these definition,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{1}|={{n-1}\choose 2}=\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2},

(2)

(3)

Conjecture 1.1

Let

In this paper, we prove this conjecture.

Theorem 1.2

Let
n,s
be two integers such that
s\geq 2
and
n\geq 2s+2
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a 3-graph with vertex set
[n]
. If
\mathcal{F}
is
U(s,2s+1)
, then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\max\{|\mathcal{F}_{i}|\ |\ i\in[3]\}
.

Let us recall some fundamental results of extremal set theory. Erdős, Ko and Rado
[
4
]
proved that if
\mathcal{F}\subseteq{n\choose k}
satisfying
|F\cap F^{\prime}|\geq t
for any
F
,
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
, then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq{n-t\choose{k-t}}
for
n>n_{0}(k,t)
.
Moreover, they also proved
n_{0}(k,1)=2k
. We call the conclusion as EKR Theorem in the following for short.
The smallest
n_{0}(k,t)
of the EKR Theorem has been determined by Frankl
[
5
]
for

t\geq 15
and subsequently by Wilson
[
12
]
for all
t:n_{0}(k,t)=(k-t+1)(t+1)
. Some new generalizations and analogues of the EKR Theorem can be found
[
2
,
8
]
. Frankl
[
6
]
added a union condition
U(s,n-t)
to
\mathcal{F}
and gave a upper bound to
|\mathcal{F}|
when
t\leq{\frac{2^{k}k}{150}}
. Erdős
[
3
]
in 1965 made the following conjecture: If
\mathcal{F}\subseteq{n\choose k}
,
\nu(\mathcal{F})\leq s
, then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\max\{|\mathcal{A}_{s,1}|,|\mathcal{A}_{(s+1)k-1,k}|\}
. It was one of the favourite problems of Erdős and attracted a lot of attention.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

Several Technical Lemma

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer.
Consider two sets
F_{1}=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})
with
a_{1}<\cdots<a_{k}
and
F_{2}=(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{k})
with
b_{1}<\cdots<b_{k}
. Then
F_{1}\prec_{s}F_{2}
iff
a_{i}\leq b_{i}
for every
i\in[k]
. We say that a family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq{[n]\choose k}
is
stable
if
F_{2}\in\mathcal{F}
and
F_{1}\prec_{s}F_{2}
implies
F_{1}\in\mathcal{F}
.

Lemma 2.1

Let
G
be a stable graph with vertex set
[n]
and
\nu(G)=m
. Let
r:=\max\{i\ |\ \nu(G-\{0,\ldots,i\})=\nu(G)-i\}
. Then
G
is a subgraph of
A_{n,m}^{r}
.

Proof.
Let
\mathcal{M}=\{\{j,2m+3-j\}\ |\ 1\leq j\leq m+1\}
. Since
\nu(G)=m
, then
\mathcal{M}
is not a matching of
G
. So there exists
j\in[m+1]
such that
\{j,2m+3-j\}\notin G
. Since
G
is stable, for any edge
\{u,v\}\in G
with
u<v
, either
u<j
or
v<2m+3-j
. Thus

G
is a subgraph of
A_{n,m}^{j-1}
. We choose maximum
j
such that
G
is a subgraph of
A_{n,m}^{j-1}
.

Next we show that
r=j-1
. Note that

m+1-j=\nu(G)+1-j\leq\nu(G-[j-1])\leq\nu(A_{n,m}^{j-1}-[j-1])=m+1-j.

Thus we have
\nu(G-[j-1])=\nu(G)-j+1
. Thus by the definition of
r
, we have
r\geq j-1
.
We claim
\{\{i+1,2m+2-i\}|\ j\leq i\leq m\}
is a matching of
G
. Otherwise, there exists
t\in[m]-[j-1]
such that
\{t+1,2m+2-t\}\notin G
.
Hence for any
\{a,b\}\in G
with
a<b
, either
a\leq t
or
b\leq 2m+1-t
. So
G
is a subgraph of
A^{t}_{n,m}
, contradicting the choice of
j
since
j-1<t
. This completes the proof.
\Box

Lemma 3.2

Let
n,s
be two integer such that
s\geq 3
and
n\geq 2s+4
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a stable 3-graph with vertex set
[n]
. If
\mathcal{F}
is
U(s,2s+1)
,
\nu(\mathcal{F})\geq 2
and
\nu(\partial\mathcal{F})\geq s+2
, then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\max\{|\mathcal{F}_{i}|\ |\ i\in[3]\}
.

Proof.
Since
\mathcal{F}
is stable and
\nu(\mathcal{F})\geq 2
, we have
\{2,3,4\}\in\mathcal{F}
. We first prove the following claim.

Claim 1.

\nu(\partial\mathcal{F}-[4])\leq s-2
.

Otherwise, suppose that
\nu(\partial\mathcal{F}-[4])\geq s-1
. Then
\partial\mathcal{F}-[4]
has a matching of size
s-1
. Let
M
be a matching of size
s-1
in
\partial\mathcal{F}-[4]
. Write
M^{\prime}:=\{e\cup\{1\}\ |\ e\in M\}\cup\{\{2,3,4\}\}
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is stable, we have
M^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
. One can see that
|M^{\prime}|=s
and
|\cup_{e\in M^{\prime}}e|=2s+2
, a contradiction since
\mathcal{F}
is
U(s,2s+1)
. This completes the proof of Claim 1.

Since
\nu(\partial\mathcal{F})\geq s+2
, by Claim 1, we have
\nu(\partial\mathcal{F})=s+2
and
\nu(\partial\mathcal{F}-[4])=s-2
.

Claim 2.
If
M_{0}
is a matching of size
s
in
\partial\mathcal{F}-\{1,2\}
, then
N_{\mathcal{F}}(2)\cap M_{0}=\emptyset
.

Otherwise, suppose that
N_{\mathcal{F}}(2)\cap M_{0}\neq\emptyset
. Let
e\in M_{0}\cap N_{\mathcal{F}}(2)
and
M_{0}^{\prime}=\{f\cup\{1\}\ |\ f\in M_{0}\setminus e\}\cup\{e\cup\{2\}\}
. By the stability of
\mathcal{F}
, one can see that
M_{0}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
and
|V(M_{0}^{\prime})|=2s+2
. So
\mathcal{F}
is not
U(s,2s+1)
, a contradiction. This completes the proof of Claim 2.

Claim 3.

e=\{2,3,2s+2\}\notin\mathcal{F}
.

By contradiction. Suppose that
\{2,3,2s+2\}\in\mathcal{F}
. By Claim 1,
\partial\mathcal{F}-[3]
has a matching of size
s-1
. By Lemma
2.2
,
M_{1}:=\{\{i,2s+5-i\}\ |\ 4\leq i\leq s+2\}
is matching of size
s-1
in
\partial\mathcal{F}-[3]
.
Let
M_{1}^{\prime}=\{f\cup\{1\}\ |\ f\in M_{1}\}\cup\{\{2,3,2s+2\}\}
. Then we have
|M_{1}^{\prime}|=s
,
|V(M_{1}^{\prime})|=2s+2
. Since
M_{1}\subseteq N_{\mathcal{F}}(1)
, we have
M_{1}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
. So
\mathcal{F}
is not
U(s,2s+1)
, a contradiction. This completes the proof of Claim 3.

Let
r=\max\{i\ |\ \nu(\partial\mathcal{F}-[i])=\nu(\partial\mathcal{F})-i\}
. From Claim 1 we have
r\geq 4
.
By Lemma
2.1
,
\partial\mathcal{F}
is a subgraph of
K_{r}^{0}\vee(K_{2s-2r+5}^{1}\cup K_{n-2s+r-5}^{2})
.
By Lemma
2.2
,
\{\{i,2s+5-i\}\ |\ 3\leq i\leq s+2\}
is a matching of size
s
in
\partial\mathcal{F}-\{1,2\}
.
Note that
\{s+2,s+3\}\in\partial\mathcal{F}
and by Claim 3, we have
\{s+2,s+3,2\}\notin\mathcal{F}
. By the stability of
\mathcal{F}
, every edge of
\mathcal{F}
containing no
1
intersects
[s+1]
at least two vertices, i.e.,
\mathcal{F}-\{1\}
is a subgraph of
\mathcal{F}_{2}-\{1\}
.
Thus we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{2}|

\displaystyle=|N_{\mathcal{F}}(1)\setminus N_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}(1)|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left(\left({[n]-[1]\choose 2}\setminus{[n]-[r]\choose 2}\right)\cup{[2s-r+5]-[r]\choose 2}\right)\setminus\left({[n]-[1]\choose 2}\setminus{[n]-[1+s]\choose 2}\right)\right|

\displaystyle={s-r+4\choose 2}

\displaystyle\leq{s\choose 2}\quad\mbox{(since $r\geq 4$)}.

Note that
\mathcal{M}:=\{\{i+2,2s+3-i\}\ |\ 1\leq i\leq s\}
is a matching of size
s
in
\partial\mathcal{F}-[2]
. By Claim 2, we have
\mathcal{M}\cap N_{\mathcal{F}}(2)=\emptyset
.

Claim 4.
For any two distinct edges
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathcal{M}
,
{e_{1}\cup e_{2}\choose 3}\cap\mathcal{F}=\emptyset
.

Otherwise, suppose that
{e_{1}\cup e_{2}\choose 3}\cap\mathcal{F}\neq\emptyset
. Let
e\in{e_{1}\cup e_{2}\choose 3}\cap\mathcal{F}
and let

. Note that

. So we have

.
Let
M_{2}=\{e,\{1,2,v\}\}\cup\{f\cup\{1\}\ |\ f\in\mathcal{M}\backslash\{e_{1},e_{2}\}\}
. Then we have
|M_{2}|=s
and
|V(M_{2})|=2s+2
. By the stability, One can see that
M_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
, contradicting that
\mathcal{F}
is
U(s,2s+1)
. This completes the proof of Claim 4.

Write
A=\{\{s+2,s+3\},\{3,2s+2\}\}
. Recall that
\mathcal{F}
and
\partial\mathcal{F}
are stable. By Claims 2 and 4,

for any

Hence by Claim 4, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{2}\setminus\mathcal{F}|

\displaystyle\geq\left|{\{2,3\}\choose 1}\right|\left|\mathcal{M}\backslash A\right|+{s\choose 2}(n-2s-1)+{4\choose 3}\left|{\mathcal{M}\backslash A\choose 2}\right|

\displaystyle=2(s-2)+{s\choose 2}(n-2s-1)+4{s-2\choose 2}

\displaystyle=2(s-2)^{2}+{s\choose 2}(n-2s-1)

So we have

Lemma 3.3

Let
n,s
be two integer such that
s\geq 3
and
n\geq 2s+2
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a stable 3-graph with vertex set
[n]
. If
\mathcal{F}
is
U(s,2s+1)
and
\nu(\partial\mathcal{F})\leq s
, then
|{\mathcal{F}}|\leq\max\{|\mathcal{F}_{i}|\ |\ i\in[3]\}
.

Proof.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\nu(\partial\mathcal{F})=s
since we may add some edges containing
1
to
\mathcal{F}
such that
\nu(\partial\mathcal{F})=s
. Let
r:=\max\{i\ |\ \nu(\partial\mathcal{F}-\{0,\ldots,i\})=\nu(\partial\mathcal{F})-i\}
.

Since
\partial\mathcal{F}
is stable and
\nu(\partial\mathcal{F})=s
, by Lemma
2.1
,
\partial\mathcal{F}
is a subgraph of
A^{r}_{n,s}
.
So for every edge
e
of
\mathcal{F}
, we have
|e\cap[r]|=2
or
e\subseteq[2s-r+1]
. Thus we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|

\displaystyle\leq{r\choose 2}(n-2s+r-1)+{{2s-r+1}\choose 3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}r^{3}+(\frac{n}{2}-1)r^{2}-(\frac{n}{2}-s+2s^{2}-\frac{2}{3})r+\frac{4}{3}s^{3}-\frac{1}{3}s.

Let
f(r)=\frac{1}{3}r^{3}+(\frac{n}{2}-1)r^{2}-(\frac{n}{2}-s+2s^{2}-\frac{2}{3})r+\frac{4}{3}s^{3}-\frac{1}{3}s
. Then
f(r)
is a convex function on interval
[0,s]
since
f^{\prime\prime}(r)=2r+n-2\geq 0
for
0\leq r\leq s
. Thus

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|

\displaystyle\leq\max\{f(0),f(s)\}

\displaystyle=\max\{{2s+1\choose 3},{s\choose 2}(n-s)+{s\choose 3}\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{|\mathcal{F}_{2}|,|\mathcal{F}_{3}|\}.

This completes the proof.
\Box

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|

\displaystyle\leq{r\choose 2}(n-2s+r-3)+{{2s-r+3}\choose 3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}r^{3}+(\frac{n}{2}-1)r^{2}-(\frac{n}{2}+2s^{2}+3s+\frac{1}{3})r+\frac{4}{3}s^{3}+4s^{2}+\frac{11}{3}s+1,

Let
g(r):=\frac{1}{3}r^{3}+(\frac{n}{2}-1)r^{2}-(\frac{n}{2}+2s^{2}+3s+\frac{1}{3})r+\frac{4}{3}s^{3}+4s^{2}+\frac{11}{3}s+1.
Note that

g(r)
is a convex function on interval
[3,s+1]
since
g^{\prime\prime}(r)=2r+n-2\geq 0
for
3\leq r\leq s+1
. Thus

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|

\displaystyle\leq\max\{g(3),g(s+1)\}

\displaystyle=\max\{{2s\choose 3}+3(n-2s),{s+2\choose 3}+{s+1\choose 2}(n-s-2)\}

\displaystyle=\max\{{2s\choose 3}+3(n-2s),{s+1\choose 3}+{s+1\choose 2}(n-s-1)\}

\displaystyle=\max\{3n+\frac{4}{3}s^{3}-2s^{2}-\frac{16}{3}s,|\mathcal{F}_{2}|\}.

Next we discuss two subcases.

Subcase 1.1.
n\leq(2s^{2}+5s)/3
.

From (
4
) we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{3}|-g(3)

\displaystyle=-3n+2s^{2}+5s\geq 0.

Hence,
g(3)\leq|\mathcal{F}_{3}|
. On the other hand,
g(s+1)=|\mathcal{F}_{2}|
. So we have
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\max\{g(3),g(s+1)\}\leq\max\{|\mathcal{F}_{i}|\ |\ i\in[3]\}
.

Subcase 1.2.
n>(2s^{2}+5s)/3
.

Note that
n>(2s^{2}+5s)/3>10s/3
. Thus we have

\displaystyle g(s+1)-g(3)

\displaystyle=-\frac{5}{3}s^{3}+(\frac{n}{2}+1)s^{2}+(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{14}{3})s-3n

\displaystyle>-\frac{5}{3}s^{3}+(\frac{5}{3}s+1)s^{2}+(\frac{5}{3}s+\frac{14}{3})s-10s\quad\mbox{(since $s\geq 3$)}

\displaystyle=\frac{8s}{3}(s-2)>0.

Thus by (
3
), one can see that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|

\displaystyle\leq\max\{g(3),g(s+1)\}=g(s+1)=|\mathcal{F}_{2}|.

Case 2.

r=0
.

Then
\partial\mathcal{F}
is a subgraph of
K_{2s+3}
. Thus
\mathcal{F}\subseteq{[2s+3]\choose 3}
. From the definition of
r
we have
\nu(\partial\mathcal{F}-\{1\})=\nu(\partial\mathcal{F})=s+1
, by Lemma
2.2
,
\mathcal{M}:=\{\{i+1,2s+4-i\ |\ 1\leq i\leq s+1\}\}
is a matching of size
s+1
in
\partial\mathcal{F}
.

Claim 1.
For any
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathcal{M}
,
{e_{1}\cup e_{2}\choose 3}\cap\mathcal{F}=\emptyset
.

Otherwise, suppose that
{e_{1}\cup e_{2}\choose 3}\cap\mathcal{F}\neq\emptyset
. Let
f\in{e_{1}\cup e_{2}\choose 3}\cap\mathcal{F}
. Write
M=\{f\}\cup\{e\cup\{1\}\ |\ e\in\mathcal{M}-\{e_{1},e_{2}\}\}
. One can see that
M\subseteq\mathcal{F}
and
|V(M)|=2s+2
, contradicting that
\mathcal{F}
is
U(s,2s+1)
. This complete the proof of Claim 1.

Claim 2.
For any three distinct edges
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\in\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{F}[\cup_{i\in[3]}e_{i}\cup\{1\}]
contains no matchings of size two.

Otherwise, there exists
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\in\mathcal{M}
such that
\mathcal{F}[(\cup_{i\in[3]}e_{i})\cup\{1\}]
contains a matching of size two, say
\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
. Write
M:=\{f_{1},f_{2}\}\cup\{e\cup\{1\}\ |\ e\in\mathcal{M}-\{e_{i}\ |\ i\in[3]\}\}
. One can see that
M\subseteq\mathcal{F}
and
|V(M)|\geq 2s+2
, contradicting that
\mathcal{F}
is
U(s,2s+1)
. This complete the proof of Claim 2.

By Lemma
3.4
, Claims 1 and 2, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|-|\mathcal{F}_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq{2s+3\choose 3}-\left|{\mathcal{M}\choose 2}\right|{4\choose 3}-4\left|{\mathcal{M}\choose 3}\right|-{2s+1\choose 3}

\displaystyle={2s+2\choose 2}+{2s+1\choose 2}-4{s+1\choose 2}-4{s+1\choose 3}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{3}s^{3}+2s^{2}+\frac{8}{3}s

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{2}{3}s^{2}+\frac{8}{3}s\leq 0\quad\mbox{(when $s\geq 4$)},

i.e.,
|\mathcal{F}|\leq|\mathcal{F}_{3}|
.
Next we consider
s=3
. Then
\mathcal{M}=\{\{i+1,10-i\}\ |\ i\in[4]\}
. If
\{1,6,7\}\in\mathcal{F}
, by Claim 2,
\{2,4,5\}\notin\mathcal{F}
. By the stability of
\mathcal{F}
, we have
\{2,i,j\}\notin\mathcal{F}
,
i,j\in\{4,5,\ldots,9\}
, thus

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|

\displaystyle\leq{9\choose 3}-\left|{[9]-[2]\choose 3}\right|-\left|{[9]-[3]\choose 2}\right|=34<35=|\mathcal{F}_{3}|.

So we may assume that
\{1,6,7\}\notin\mathcal{F}
. By Claim 1,
\{2,5,6\},\{2,4,7\}\notin\mathcal{F}
, and so by stability, we have
\{2,5,7\},\{2,5,8\},\{2,4,8\}\notin\mathcal{F}
. Hence

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|

\displaystyle\leq{9\choose 3}-\left|{[5]\choose 1}\right|\left|{[9]-[5]\choose 2}\right|-2\left|{\mathcal{M}\choose 2}\right|-\left|{[9]-[5]\choose 3}\right|-3

\displaystyle=35=|\mathcal{F}_{3}|.

Case 3.

r=1
.

By Lemma
2.1
,
\partial\mathcal{F}
is a subgraph of
A_{n,s+1}^{1}
. So we have
\mathcal{F}\subseteq{[2s+2]\choose 3}
.
By Lemma
2.2
,
\mathcal{M}:=\{\{i,2s+3-i\}\ |\ 1\leq i\leq s+1\}
is a matching of size
s+1
of
\partial\mathcal{F}
. If
s\geq 4
, by Claim 2 and Lemma
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|-|\mathcal{F}_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq{2s+2\choose 3}-4\left|{\mathcal{M}\choose 3}\right|-{2s+1\choose 3}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{3}s^{3}+2s^{2}+\frac{5}{3}s

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{3}(2s^{2}-5s)\quad\mbox{(since $s\geq 4$)}

\displaystyle<0.

So we may assume that
s=3
.
Then
\mathcal{M}:=\{\{i,9-i\}\ |\ 1\leq i\leq 4\}
. If
\{1,2,8\}\notin\mathcal{F}
, then we have
\mathcal{F}\subseteq{[2s+1]\choose 3}
and so
|\mathcal{F}|\leq|\mathcal{F}_{3}|
. Hence we may assume
\{1,2,8\}\in\mathcal{F}
. By Claim 2 and the stability of
\mathcal{F}
, we have
\{4,5,7\},\{4,5,8\},\{3,6,7\},\{3,6,8\}\notin\mathcal{F}
. Moreover, one can see that
|\{\{1,7,8\},\{2,3,6\}\}\cap\mathcal{F}|=1
by Claim 2. Let
e\in\{\{1,7,8\},\{2,3,6\}\}
such that
e\notin\mathcal{F}
. Write

M=\{e,\{4,5,7\},\{4,5,8\},\{3,6,7\},\{3,6,8\}\}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|-|\mathcal{F}_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq{8\choose 3}-{7\choose 3}-4{|\mathcal{M}|\choose 3}-|M|=0,

and so
|\mathcal{F}|\leq|\mathcal{F}_{3}|
.

Case 4.

r=2
.

By Lemma
2.1
,
\partial\mathcal{F}
is a subgraph
A_{n,s+1}^{2}
. By the definition of
r
, we have
\partial\mathcal{F}-[3]
has a matching size
s-1
. By Lemma
2.2
,
\mathcal{M}:=\{\{i,2s+5-i\}\ |\ 4\leq i\leq s+2\}
is a matching of
\partial\mathcal{F}-[3]
. If
\{1,2,2s+2\}\notin\mathcal{F}
, then
\partial\mathcal{F}\subseteq{[2s+1]\choose 2}
and
\nu(\mathcal{F})\leq s
, a contradiction. So we may assume that
\{1,2,2s+2\}\in\mathcal{F}
.

Claim 3.

\mathcal{M}\cap N_{\mathcal{F}}(3)=\emptyset
. In particular,
\{3,s+2,s+3\}\notin\mathcal{F}
.

Otherwise, suppose that
e\in\mathcal{M}\cap N_{\mathcal{F}}(3)
. Let
M:=\{\{3\}\cup e,\{1,2,2s+2\}\}\cup\{f\cup\{1\}\ |\ f\in\mathcal{M}\setminus e\}
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is stable,
M\subseteq\mathcal{F}
. Moreover,
|M|=s
and
|V(M)|=2s+2
, contradicting that
\mathcal{F}
is
U(s,2s+1)
.

Write
A=\{3,\ldots,s+1\}
and
B=[2s]-[s+1]
. By Claim 3, we have
\{3,4,2s+1\}\notin\mathcal{F}
. So for any
\{a,b\}\in{A\choose 2}
,
\{a,b,2s+1\}\notin\mathcal{F}
.
Then by Claim 3, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|

\displaystyle\leq{2s+1\choose 3}+(n-2s-1)-{|A|\choose 1}{|B|\choose 2}-{|A\cup B|\choose 2}

\displaystyle={2s+1\choose 3}+(n-2s-1)-(s-1){s-1\choose 2}-{2s-2\choose 2}

\displaystyle={2s+1\choose 3}+n-3-\frac{1}{2}(s-1)(s^{2}+s),

i.e.,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|\leq{2s+1\choose 3}+n-3-\frac{1}{2}(s-1)(s^{2}+s).

(6)

Consider
n\leq\frac{1}{2}(s-1)(s^{2}+s)+3
. Then we have
|\mathcal{F}|\leq|\mathcal{F}_{3}|
. So we may assume that
n>\frac{1}{2}(s-1)(s^{2}+s)+3\geq 3s+6
as
s\geq 3
. Then by (
6
),

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|-|\mathcal{F}_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq{2s+1\choose 3}+n-\frac{1}{2}(s-1)(s^{2}+s)-4-{s+1\choose 2}(n-s-1)-{s+1\choose 3}

\displaystyle\leq{2s+1\choose 3}+3s+7-\frac{1}{2}(s-1)(s^{2}+s)-4-{s+1\choose 2}(2s+6)-{s+1\choose 3}\quad\mbox{(since $n\geq 3s+7$)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}s(4s^{2}-1)-\frac{1}{2}(s+1)(3s^{2}+5s-6)-\frac{1}{6}s(s^{2}-1)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{3}s(4s^{2}-1)-\frac{3}{2}s^{3}-\frac{1}{6}s(s^{2}-1)\quad\mbox{(since $s\geq 3$)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{3}s^{3}-\frac{1}{6}s<0,

i.e.,
|\mathcal{F}|\leq|\mathcal{F}_{2}|
. This completes the proof.
\Box

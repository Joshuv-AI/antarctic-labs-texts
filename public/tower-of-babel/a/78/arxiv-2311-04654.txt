Abstract
We compare precision e + ​ e − → μ + ​ μ − e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-} cross section measurements by BESIII in the E cm = 3.8 − 4.6 E_{\rm cm}=3.8-4.6 GeV range, to predictions based on measured R had R_{\rm had} data. The consistency is poor (p-value < 0.012 <0.012 ). Allowing for resonance contributions not seen in R had R_{\rm had} gives an excellent fit, with the state at 4421 MeV ( 4.6 ​ σ 4.6\sigma ) giving insight into the ψ ⁡ ( 4415 ) \psi(4415) and the 3.1 ​ σ 3.1\sigma structure at 4211 MeV, if confirmed, being a new, very narrow resonance. This analysis shows the power of precision e + ​ e − → μ + ​ μ − e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-} measurements to uncover or probe otherwise difficult to access states.



Certain types of final states in
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions would not have been detected in existing
e^{+}e^{-}
experiments, due to event acceptance requirements
Farrar 2022
. We call such final states “invisible” for the purposes of this Letter. In addition to truly invisible Beyond the Standard Model states, hadronic states in which less than
\approx
30% of the energy is in visible hadrons or leptons, or whose energy deposit is too asymmetric would generally have been rejected
Farrar 2022
. The production of invisible final states in
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation can be investigated in two ways:

\bullet
Measuring the total cross section inclusively, through inclusive Initial State Radiation (ISR); this approach is limited by the relatively poor resolution of the ISR photon energy and very challenging background.

\bullet
Measuring
\sigma_{e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}}
very accurately, since the
\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2})
correction to the Born contribution depends on the true total
e^{+}e^{-}
cross section through a dispersion relation
Dong et al. 2020
.

Besides the intrinsic interest in potentially overlooked states, another motivation for a search for states not detected in
e^{+}e^{-}\to{\rm hadrons}
is the discrepancy between the very precisely measured anomalous magnetic moment of the muon
Aguillard et al. 2023
and its value predicted using dispersion relations to obtain the hadronic vacuum polarization contribution from
e^{+}e^{-}\to{\rm hadrons}
data
Aoyama et al. 2020
;
Alexandrou et al. 2022
. This discrepancy may be resolved by revision of
R_{\rm had}\equiv\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow{\rm hadrons})/\sigma^{B}(e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
in the
\rho
region, as suggested by the new CMD-3 measurement
Ignatov et al. 2023a
;
Ignatov et al. 2023b
, where
\sigma^{B}(e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-})=\frac{4\pi\alpha^{2}}{3s}
and
\alpha
is the QED fine structure constant. Complementary approaches to this problem are nevertheless valuable. Furthermore, as we show here, resonances which in principle can be seen in hadronic final states of
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions given sufficiently accurate measurements, can in some cases be more precisely probed indirectly, through their virtual impact on
\sigma_{e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}}
.

In this paper, we compare precision data on
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
from BESIII
Ablikim et al. 2020
to predictions using
R_{\rm had}
data from the PDG
Workman et al. 2022
. We find that the measured
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
cross section shows structure in the 4.2-4.5 GeV range that is in significant conflict with predictions from
R_{\rm had}
data. We look for possible sources of energy dependent systematic uncertainties in the
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
measurements, but none appear capable of explaining the data. However allowing for contributions from resonances whose contributions to
R_{\rm had}
have gone undetected provides an excellent fit.

The systematic uncertainty of the measurement is estimated by BESIII to be about

We allow for this with a linear rescaling of the
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}

R_{\mu^{+}\mu^{-}}
as a function of energy, which supplies information about final states which have escaped detection in the direct measurement of the hadronic cross section. The precision of our numerical analysis is at the 0.1% level.

Theoretical framework

The total cross section
\sigma_{\mu^{+}\mu^{-}}
, for the exclusive process
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
, is related to the vacuum polarization (VP),
\Pi(s)
, by

\sigma_{\mu^{+}\mu^{-}}(s)=\frac{\sigma^{\rm B}_{\mu^{+}\mu^{-}}(s)}{|1-\Pi(s)|^{2}},

(1)

where
s
is the c.m. energy squared.
The charged lepton contribution to
\Pi(s)
can be calculated in perturbation theory and we omit it in subsequent formulae for clarity.
The contribution to
\Pi(s)
from virtual states other than leptons,
\Pi_{nl}(s)
, is related to the total cross section
\sigma_{nl}(s)
to not-leptons final states in the one-photon exchange approximation through a dispersion relation
Dong et al. 2020
:

\Pi_{nl}(s)=\frac{s}{4\pi^{2}\alpha}\int_{4m^{2}_{\pi}}^{\infty}\frac{\sigma_{nl}(s^{\prime})}{s-s^{\prime}+i\epsilon}ds^{\prime}~.

(2)

Using the identity

(x+i\epsilon)^{-1}=P(1/x)-i\pi\delta(x)~,

gives

\Pi_{nl}(s)=-\frac{s}{4\pi^{2}\alpha}\,P\!\int_{4m^{2}_{\pi}}^{\infty}\frac{\sigma_{nl}(s^{\prime})}{s^{\prime}-s}ds^{\prime}-i\frac{s}{4\pi\alpha}\sigma_{nl}(s)~,

(3)

where
P
represents principal value. Following
Dong et al. 2020
, the integration is performed analytically for narrow resonances
J\kern-1.49994pt/\kern-1.49994pt\psi\kern 1.49994pt
,
\psi(2S)
,
\Upsilon(1S)
,
\Upsilon(2S)
, and
\Upsilon(3S)
. The high energy part of the integration assumes that
R(s)=R(s_{1})
is constant above a certain value
s_{1}
(taken to be above the
\Upsilon
resonance region), and the integral between threshold and
s_{1}
is carried out numerically after separation of the principle value part. Thus

\rm{Re}\,\Pi_{nl}(s)=\frac{3s}{\alpha}\sum\limits_{j}\frac{\Gamma^{j}_{e^{+}e^{-}}}{M_{j}}\frac{s-M_{j}^{2}}{(s-M_{j}^{2})^{2}+M_{j}^{2}\Gamma_{j}^{2}}+\frac{\alpha}{3\pi}R(s_{1})\ln\left|\frac{s-s_{1}}{s_{1}}\right|-\frac{s}{4\pi^{2}\alpha}\int_{4m^{2}_{\pi}}^{s_{1}}\frac{\sigma_{\rm nr}(s^{\prime})-\sigma_{\rm nr}(s)}{s^{\prime}-s}\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}-\frac{s\sigma_{\rm nr}(s)}{4\pi^{2}\alpha}\ln\left|\frac{s_{1}-s}{4m^{2}_{\pi}-s}\right|,

(4)

where
\Gamma_{j}
,
\Gamma^{j}_{e^{+}e^{-}}
, and
M_{j}
denote the total width, partial width to
e^{+}e^{-}
, and mass of the resonance
j
, respectively. Here,
\sigma_{\rm nr}(s)
is the
\sigma_{nl}(s)
in (
3
) with the contributions from narrow resonances subtracted.

The measured cross section for
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
includes unobserved radiated photons, so before comparing to data we integrate the exclusive cross section
\sigma_{\mu^{+}\mu^{-}}(s)
calculated from Eq. (
1
), with the ISR function as discussed in
Dong et al. 2020
.

We model a possible undetected contribution to
\sigma_{nl}
, either unresolved due to gaps in the
R_{\rm had}
measurements or decaying dominantly into undetected final states, as being due to one or two resonances, R1 and R2. The generalization to more resonances is straightforward from this case, and allowing for two unseen states proves sufficient for our purposes. For this initial study, we assume R1 and R2 can be described by constant-width Breit-Wigner functions

A=\frac{\sqrt{12\pi\Gamma_{ee}\Gamma}}{s-m^{2}+im\Gamma}~,

(5)

with
m
,
\Gamma
, and
\Gamma_{ee}
being the mass, width, and electronic partial width of the resonance. We allow for the possibility that the more massive resonance (R2) can have common, interfering decay channels with R1.

The resonances R1 and R2 may or may not have final states contributing to the detected
R_{\rm had}
, but their undetected contributions simply add to
\sigma_{nl}
without interference since the two types of final states are inherently orthogonal. If there is only one resonance, R1, it can be treated using Eqs. (
3
) and (
4
), just like the known vector mesons. This fit entails 3 new parameters — the mass, width and electronic width of R1:
m_{1}
,
\Gamma_{1}
,
\Gamma_{ee,1}
. However in computing
|1-\Pi_{R\rm had}-\Pi_{R}|^{-2}
, where
\Pi_{R}
is the contribution from the new sector, one must take care about the proper treatment of possible interference terms within the new sector. When there are two resonances, we have two masses, widths and
\Gamma_{ee}
’s, and in addition the branching fraction
{\cal B}
and relative phase
\phi
of R2 decay into R1 final states, which we take to be energy independent constants. If some resonance has both visible and unseen channels, the values of
m
,
\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{ee}
should be compatible when proper account is taken of interference within each sector; for this, a more sophisticated treatment than the constant width BW used here could be warranted.

The contribution to
\sigma_{nl}
of the resonances R1 and R2 not already included via the measured
R_{\rm had}
is then, in this approximation:

\displaystyle\sigma_{R1+R2}=

\displaystyle\,|A_{R1}+e^{i\phi}\sqrt{{\cal B}}\,A_{R2}|^{2}+(1-{\cal B})|A_{R2}|^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\,\sigma_{R1}+\sigma_{R2}+\sigma_{R1R2}~,

(6)

where
A_{R1,R2}
are the appropriate BW functions, given in Eq. (
5
) for the constant width treatment, and
\sigma_{R1R2}
is merely the shorthand

\sigma_{R1R2}\equiv 2\sqrt{{\cal B}}\,{\rm Re}[A^{*}_{R1}A_{R2}\,e^{i\phi}]~.

(7)

The first two terms in Eq. (
6
) are just Breit-Wigner cross sections and their contributions to
\Pi_{nl}(s)
in Eq. (
3

6

\rm{Re}\,\Pi_{nl}
in (
3

Taking the BW’s for R1 and R2 to be momentum-independent as in Eq. (
5

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\,\Pi_{R1R2}(s)\equiv-\frac{s}{4\pi^{2}\alpha}\,P\!\int_{4m^{2}_{\pi}}^{\infty}\frac{2\sqrt{{\cal B}}\,{\rm Re}[A^{*}_{R1}A_{R2}\,e^{i\phi}](s^{\prime})}{(s^{\prime}-s)}ds^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,K\,{\rm Re}\left[\frac{e^{i\phi}}{(s-a)(s-b)(b-a)}\left(b\,\ln\frac{a}{s}-a\,\ln\frac{b}{s}+s\,\ln\frac{b}{a}\right)\right],

(8)

where
a=m_{1}(m_{1}+i\Gamma_{1})
,
b=m_{2}(m_{2}-i\Gamma_{2})
,
K=6s/(\pi\alpha)\sqrt{{\cal B}\,\Gamma_{ee,1}\,\Gamma_{1}\,\Gamma_{ee,2}\,\Gamma_{2}}
and ln(
|z|e^{i\theta})=\mathrm{ln}|z|+i\,\theta
. If the resonance contributions are not constant-width Breit-Wigners, the integral can be done numerically.

Implications for the vacuum polarization

An important element of the Standard Model prediction of the muon anomalous magnetic moment is the determination of the Hadronic Vacuum Polarization (HVP) contribution, from experimental measurements of
e^{+}e^{-}\to{\rm hadrons}
in the “R-ratio” method.
The HVP contribution to the muon g-2 is

a_{\mu}^{\rm HVP,LO}=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{3\pi^{2}}\int ds\frac{R(s)K(s)\Theta(s)}{s}~,

(10)

where
s
is the c.m. energy squared,
R(s)\equiv\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\to{\rm hadrons})/\sigma_{\rm B}(e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
and the function
K(s)
is given in
Aoyama et al. 2020
. The function
\Theta(s)\to 1
when calculating
a_{\mu}^{\rm HVP,LO}
, but we include it for more detailed comparison to lattice QCD calculations below. With a different weighting function an analogous calculation yields the running of the QED coupling to higher scales.

Modifying the vacuum polarization to include invisible or not-yet-detected contributions to the observed R-ratio, whether from unseen hadrons or new physics (HVP being a minor misnomer in the latter case), modifies the predicted value of g-2.
Reference
Aoyama et al. 2020
performed a comprehensive analysis of experiments on
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\mathit{hadrons}
and reported an HVP contribution
a_{\mu}\equiv\rm{(g-2)_{\rm had}}\times 10^{10}=693.1\pm 4.0
. This leads to a Standard Model prediction
5.0\sigma
less than the latest muon g-2 measurement
Aguillard et al. 2023
. However CMD-3
Ignatov et al. 2023a
;
Ignatov et al. 2023b
recently reported a new determination of
R_{\rm had}
in the
\rho
resonance region to much higher accuracy than previous experiments, with improved control of systematics and using more sophisticated radiative corrections. Adopting the CMD-3 results gives
a_{\mu}({\rm exp})-a_{\mu}({\rm pred})=4.9(5.5)\times 10^{-10}

Ignatov et al. 2023b
.

We calculate the contributions of the new resonances to
a_{\mu}
and
\Delta\alpha(Z)
, by sampling the fit parameters according to the covariance matrix. The contributions of resonances R1 and R2 to
a_{\mu}^{\rm HVP}
and
\Delta\alpha(Z)
are closely correlated to one another and make only a small change to the predictions: an increment of
\delta a_{\mu}=2.18^{+2.16}_{-0.10}\times 10^{-11}
and
\delta\Delta\alpha(Z)=0.66^{+0.32}_{-0.19}\times 10^{-4}
. For more direct comparison to the predictions of lattice QCD, we use window functions to calculate the R1 and R2 contributions to the intermediate and short distance window functions
Colangelo et al. 2022
, finding:
\delta a_{\mu}^{W}=0.314^{+0.279}_{-0.165}\times 10^{-11}
and
\delta a_{\mu}^{SD}=1.86^{+1.63}_{-0.98}\times 10^{-11}
.

Interpretation

The dependence of
\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
on energy seen in the high-precision measurements by the BESIII collaboration
Ablikim et al. 2020
, from
E_{\rm cm}=3.8
to
4.6
GeV, is poorly described using dispersion relations and the measured
\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\to{\rm hadrons})
. The shape of the spectrum has a confidence level of only
0.012
, after allowing for a linear, energy-dependent shift in absolute normalization within the systematic uncertainty estimate. Adding additional resonances, R1 and R2, results in a very good fit. The contributions of R1 and R2 to
R_{\rm had}
required to explain the
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
cross section are shown in the right panel of Fig.
2
, with the mean value in black and 10 random realizations from the covariance matrix in grey. The
R_{\rm had}
data in blue shows no such structure. This means that if the R1 and R2 signals in
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
are real, their final states somehow elude detection.

The upper state R2, whose statistical significance is
4.6\sigma
, has mass
4421.5\pm 3.4
MeV, width
15.9\pm 14.7
MeV and
\Gamma_{ee}=0.63\pm 0.31
keV. The mass is virtually identical with that of
\psi(4415)
whose PDG value is
4421\pm 4
MeV and the width is narrower than that of
\psi(4415)
by
46\pm 25
MeV, slightly less than
2\sigma
. A possible interpretation of the combined
R_{\mu^{+}\mu^{-}}
and
R_{\rm had}
data is that R2 is in fact the
\psi(4415)
, with a width
\lesssim 30
MeV to account for the structure in
R_{\mu^{+}\mu^{-}}
, and a substantial fraction of its final states not being detected in the
R_{\rm had}
measurements, for some reason. In this scenario, the
\Gamma_{ee}
of R2 should be interpreted as
\Gamma_{ee}\times{\cal B}(\psi(4415)\to{\rm invisible})
and that of the
\psi(4415)
from PDG (
0.58\pm 0.07
keV)
Workman et al. 2022
as
\Gamma_{ee}\times{\cal B}(\psi(4415)\to{\rm visible})
final states. Then
\Gamma_{ee}
of the
\psi(4415)
would be about 1.2 keV. It should be noted that interpretation of the measurements is quite model dependent
Mo et al. 2010
and that the resonance parameters of the
\psi(4415)
in PDG are estimates
Workman et al. 2022
.

The lower mass resonance, R1 at
4211.4\pm 2.6
MeV, with
\Gamma=0.14\pm 0.16
MeV and
\Gamma_{ee}=0.076\pm 0.045
keV has a statistical significance of
3.1\sigma
in the
R_{\mu^{+}\mu^{-}}
data. It is very different from the known states in this energy region, the
\psi(4160)
and
\psi(4230)
, in both mass and width
Workman et al. 2022
. Thus if R1 is not just a statistical or systematic artifact, it is unlikely to be the same state as either
\psi(4160)
or
\psi(4230)
. Its width from
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
is compatible with being so narrow it simply lies between energy scan points. If that were the case it could decay into conventional, visible final states and be detected directly by rescanning that energy range; the only puzzle would be what produces such a narrow resonance.

Summary and Conclusions

Following up on the remark that certain types of hadronic final states fail to meet the event selection criteria for
e^{+}e^{-}\to{\rm hadrons}

Farrar 2022
, we have performed the first search for evidence of missed states via their virtual effect in
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
. In the dispersive representation of the vacuum polarization, a narrow resonance in the hadronic cross-section produces a very characteristic dip-peak shape in the
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
cross section. This striking structure appears in two places in the BESIII energy range. The upper one has a statistical significance of
4.6\sigma
. If real, its position and leptonic width suggests it is due to the known resonance
\psi(4415)
, which however has a narrower width and higher peak value than apparent in the
R_{\rm had}
data, which could be due to a significant portion of its final states going undetected. There is also an indication of the same shape structure produced by a very narrow state at 4211 MeV, albeit with only
3.1\sigma
statistical significance. It cannot be excluded that these structures arise from systematic glitches in the data, but the agreement of fits using different datasets taken at different times and in both scan and hi-luminosity modes, argues for their robustness.

There are several paths to investigate and extend these results. Recorded but not-selected events in BESIII data can be re-examined, e.g., searching for pair-produced, undetected neutrals via interactions of their decay products in the detector or via asymmetric energy deposits which caused the event class to be rejected previously. An analysis of inclusive ISR data from BaBar, Belle, and Belle II would in principle be complementary to this approach, but given the present energy resolution of ISR photons such a search could not detect such narrow states as are accessible with our technique. To the extent that adequate
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
data were available at lower energy, they could be used as here to see if undetected final states contribute to the
3.8\sigma
discrepancy between lattice QCD and dispersive
R_{\rm had}
determinations of the intermediate-window-function-weighted HVP
Blum et al. 2023
.

BESIII is currently taking data up to 5.0 GeV and will be able to extend the energy coverage to 5.6 GeV, with improved peak luminosity, after the upgrade in 2024; these measurements should significantly improve the sensitivity to undetected states in
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation in the 3.6-6 GeV regime.

Geometric conditions to obtain Anosov geodesic flow in non-compact manifolds Thanks: Keywords : Anosov geodesic flow, Jacobi field, focal points, warped product. Thanks: Mathematics Subject Classification (2010) : 37D40, 53C20.
Alexander Cantoral, Sergio Romaña

Abstract
. Let ( M , g ) (M,g) be a complete Riemannian manifold without focal points and curvature bounded below. We prove that when the average of the sectional curvature in tangent planes along geodesics is negative and uniformly away from zero, then the geodesic flow is of Anosov type. We use this result to construct a non-compact manifold of non-positive curvature with geodesic flow of Anosov type.



1.
Introduction

Geodesic flows appear naturally when we have a Riemannian metric on a complete manifold. These flows describe the evolution of vectors tangent along geodesics and their properties are closely related to the geometry of the manifold. For example, the curvature of the manifold can affect the behavior of the geodesics and certain geometric properties of the manifold can be deduced from the behavior of the geodesic flow.

In
[
4
]
, Hedlund noted that geodesic flows in surfaces of constant negative curvature are chaotic. Years later, Hopf in
[
5
]
extended this result for compact surfaces of negative curvature. It was in the 1960s that Anosov, in
[
1
]
, generalized this result for the case of compact manifolds with negative curvature. These geodesic flows are called uniformly hyperbolic or simply “Anosov”. If the manifold is not compact, but its curvature is bounded between two negative constants, using the Rauch’s comparison theorem it is possible to prove that the geodesic flow is also Anosov (see for instance
[
7
]
).

We can think that negative curvature is a necessary condition to obtain a geodesic flow of Anosov type, but, as it is explained in the introduction of
[
9
]
, a manifold with Anosov geodesic flow may have regions where the curvature can be positive, negative or zero. However, Eberlein in
[
2
]
proved that negative curvature is always present in Anosov metrics, because all geodesics must have at least one point of negative curvature. Recently, in
[
9
]
, Melo and Romaña proved that when the geodesic flow is Anosov, the curvature negative is, on average, uniformly distributed along geodesics. We will see in section 5.1 that, in the non-compact case, the negative sign of the curvature does not imply that we have a geodesic flow of Anosov type.

The main goal of this work is to find sufficient geometric conditions for a geodesic flow to be Anosov in non-compact manifolds and, consequently, obtain a useful tool to construct a great diversity of examples of these types of geodesic flows.

Before enunciating the results of this work, we begin fixing some notations. Given
\theta=(x,v)
in the unit tangent bundle
SM
, we denote by
\gamma_{\theta}
the unique geodesic with initial conditions
\gamma_{\theta}(0)=x
and
\gamma^{\prime}_{\theta}(0)=v
. For any non-zero perpendicular vector field
V(t)
along
\gamma_{\theta}(t)
, we denote by
K(\gamma^{\prime}_{\theta}(t),V(t))
the sectional curvature of the tangent plane spanned by
\gamma^{\prime}_{\theta}(t)
and
V(t)
at the point
\gamma_{\theta}(t)
. In section 2.3, we define the subspaces stable
X^{s}(\theta)
and unstable
X^{u}(\theta)
of
T_{\theta}SM
from stable and unstable solutions of the Jacobi equation (

When the manifold

is compact or compactly homogeneous, Eberlein in
[
2
]
proved the following result:

Theorem
.

Assume that
M
has no focal points. If for every
\theta\in SM
and for any non-zero perpendicular parallel vector field

along
\gamma_{\theta}(t)
there is
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

, then the geodesic flow is of Anosov type.

Melo and Romaña showed in
[
9
]
a more general version of Eberlein’s result for non-compact surfaces. To be more specific, they proved that if the average of the curvature in tangent planes along geodesics is negative and uniformly away from zero then the geodesic flow is Anosov. Our result that we present below is a generalization for higher dimensions analyzing only the curvature of tangent planes generated by stable Jacobi fields and the geodesics.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
be a complete Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
, without focal points and curvature bounded below by
-c^{2}
, for some
c>0
. Assume that, there are two constants
B,t_{0}>0
such that, for every
\theta\in SM
and for an orthonormal basis
\left\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1}\right\}
of

we have that

(1)

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}K(\gamma^{\prime}_{\theta}(s),J_{i}(s))ds\leq-B,\hskip 14.22636pti=1,\ldots,n-1,

whenever
t>t_{0}
, where
J_{i}
is the stable Jacobi field along
\gamma_{\theta}
associated with
\xi_{i}
. Then the geodesic flow of
M
is Anosov.

In section 4.2, we use this theorem to construct a non-compact manifold with non-positive curvature and geodesic flow of Anosov type. It is worth emphasizing that, since the manifold is not compactly homogeneous, Eberlein’s result cannot be applied. For our purpose, we will consider a “warped product” of the real line

and the flat torus

to construct an Anosov metric on

Putting together Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.1 of
[

Corollary 1.2
.

Let

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}K(\gamma^{\prime}_{\theta}(s),J(s))ds\leq-B,

2.
Preliminaries and notation

Throughout this paper,
M=(M,g)
will denote a complete Riemannian manifold without boundary of dimension
n\geq 2
,
TM
is the tangent bundle,
SM
its unit tangent bundle and
\pi:TM\rightarrow M
will denote the canonical projection, that is,
\pi(x,v)=x
for
(x,v)\in TM
.

2.1.
Geodesic flow

Given
\theta=(x,v)\in TM
, we denote by
\gamma_{\theta}
the unique geodesic with initial conditions
\gamma_{\theta}(0)=x
and
\gamma^{\prime}_{\theta}(0)=v
. The geodesic flow is a family of diffeomorphisms
\phi^{t}:TM\rightarrow TM
, where
t\in\mathbb{R}
, given by

\displaystyle\phi^{t}(\theta)=(\gamma_{\theta}(t),\gamma^{\prime}_{\theta}(t)).

Since geodesics travel with constant speed, we have that
\phi^{t}
leaves
SM
invariant. The geodesic flow generates a vector field
G
on
TM
given by

\displaystyle G(\theta)=\left.\dfrac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\phi^{t}(\theta)=\left.\dfrac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\left(\gamma_{\theta}(t),\gamma^{\prime}_{\theta}(t)\right).

For each
\theta=(x,v)\in TM
, let
V
be the vertical subbundle of
TM
whose fiber at
\theta
is given by
V_{\theta}=\ker d_{\theta}\pi
. Let
\alpha:TTM\rightarrow TM
be the connection map induced by the Riemannian metric (see
[
11
]
) and denotes by
H
the horizontal subbundle of
TM
whose fiber at
\theta
is given by
H_{\theta}=\ker\alpha_{\theta}
.

The maps
\left.d_{\theta}\pi\right|_{H_{\theta}}:H_{\theta}\rightarrow T_{x}M
and
\left.\alpha_{\theta}\right|_{V_{\theta}}:V_{\theta}\rightarrow T_{x}M
are linear isomorphisms. This implies that
T_{\theta}TM=H_{\theta}\oplus V_{\theta}
and the map
j_{\theta}:T_{\theta}TM\rightarrow T_{x}M\times T_{x}M
given by

\displaystyle j_{\theta}(\xi)=(d_{\theta}\pi(\xi),\alpha_{\theta}(\xi))

is an linear isomorphism. Furthermore, we can identify every element
\xi\in T_{\theta}TM
with the pair
j_{\theta}(\xi)
.

Using the decomposition
T_{\theta}TM=H_{\theta}\oplus V_{\theta}
, we endow the tangent bundle
TM
with a special Riemannian metric that makes
H_{\theta}
and
V_{\theta}
orthogonal. This metric is called the Sasaki metric and it’s given by

\displaystyle\left\langle\xi,\eta\right\rangle_{\theta}=\left\langle d_{\theta}\pi(\xi),d_{\theta}\pi(\eta)\right\rangle_{x}+\left\langle\alpha_{\theta}(\xi),\alpha_{\theta}(\eta)\right\rangle_{x}.

From now on, we work with the Sasaki metric restricted to the unit tangent bundle
SM
. Consider the
1
-form
\omega
of
SM
defined as

\displaystyle\omega_{\theta}(\xi)=\left\langle\xi,G(\theta)\right\rangle_{\theta}=\left\langle d_{\theta}\pi(\xi),v\right\rangle_{x}

and define
S(\theta)
as the subspace of
T_{\theta}SM
given by
\ker\omega_{\theta}
. Equivalently,
S(\theta)
is the orthogonal complement with respect to the Sasaki metric of the one dimensional subspace spanned by
G(\theta)
. Moreover, since
\omega
is invariant by
\phi^{t}
, then
S(\theta)
is invariant by the geodesic flow, that is,
d_{\theta}\phi^{t}(S(\theta))=S(\phi^{t}(\theta))
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

The types of geodesic flows that we discuss in this paper are the Anosov geodesic flows, whose definition follows below.

We say that the geodesic flow
\phi^{t}:SM\rightarrow SM
is of Anosov type if
T(SM)
has a continuous splitting
T(SM)=E^{s}\oplus\left\langle G\right\rangle\oplus E^{u}
such that

\displaystyle d_{\theta}E^{s(u)}(\theta)

\displaystyle=E^{s(u)}(\phi^{t}(\theta)),

\displaystyle\left\|\left.d_{\theta}\phi^{t}\right|_{E^{s}(\theta)}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda^{t},

\displaystyle\left\|\left.d_{\theta}\phi^{-t}\right|_{E^{u}(\theta)}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda^{t},

for all
t\geq 0
with
C>0
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
, where
G
is the geodesic vector field. It’s known that if the geodesic flow is Anosov, then
E^{s}(\theta)\oplus E^{u}(\theta)=S(\theta)
and the subspaces
E^{s(u)}(\theta)
are Lagrangian for every
\theta\in SM
(see
[
11
]
for more details).

2.2.
Jacobi fields

To study the differential of the geodesic flow with geometric arguments, let us recall the definition of a Jacobi field. A vector field
J
along a geodesic
\gamma
of
M
is a Jacobi field if it satisfies the Jacobi equation

(2)

\displaystyle J^{\prime\prime}(t)+R(\gamma^{\prime}(t),J(t))\gamma^{\prime}(t)=0,

where
R
is the curvature tensor of
M
and
"^{\prime}"
denotes the covariant derivative along
\gamma
. A Jacobi field is determined by the initial values
J(t_{0})
and
J^{\prime}(t_{0})
, for any given
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, for
\theta\in SM
, the map
\xi\rightarrow J_{\xi}
defines an isomorphism between
S(\theta)
and the space of perpendicular Jacobi fields along
\gamma_{\theta}
, where
J_{\xi}(0)=d_{\theta}\pi(\xi)
and
J^{\prime}_{\xi}(0)=\alpha_{\theta}(\xi)
.
The behavior of the differential of the geodesic flow is determined by the behavior of the Jacobi fields and, therefore, by the curvature. More precisely, for
\theta\in SM
and
\xi\in T_{\theta}SM
we have

(3)

\displaystyle d_{\theta}\phi^{t}(\xi)=(J_{\xi}(t),J^{\prime}_{\xi}(t)),\hskip 14.22636ptt\in\mathbb{R}.

2.3.
No conjugate points

Let
\gamma
be a geodesic joining
p,q\in M
. We say that
p,q
are conjugate along
\gamma
if there exists a Jacobi field along
\gamma
vanishing at
p
and
q
but not identically zero. A manifold
M
has no conjugate point if every geodesic of
M
has no conjugate points. This is equivalent to the fact that the exponential map is non-singular at every point of
M
. A manifold
M
has no focal points if for any unit speed geodesic
\gamma
in
M
and for any Jacobi field
J
along
\gamma
such that
J(0)=0
and
J^{\prime}(0)\neq 0
we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}\left\langle J(t),J(t)\right\rangle>0,\hskip 8.5359ptt>0.

It is not difficult to verify that if a manifold
M
has non-positive curvature then
M
has no focal points, and therefore, has no conjugate points.

There are examples of manifolds without conjugate points obtained from the hyperbolic behavior of the geodesic flow. In
[
6
]
, Klingenberg proved that a compact Riemannian manifold with Anosov geodesic flow has no conjugate points. Years later, Mañé (see
[
8
]
) generalized this result to the case of manifolds of finite volume. In the case of infinite volume, Melo and Romaña in
[
10
]
extended the result of Mañé over the assumption of curvature bounded below. These results show the relationship that exists between the geometry and dynamic of Anosov geodesic flows.

From now on, we assume that
M
is a complete Riemannian manifold without conjugate points and its sectional curvatures are bounded below by
-c^{2}
, for some
c>0
. Given
\theta\in SM
, consider an orthonormal parallel frame along the geodesic
\gamma_{\theta}(t)

\left\{V_{0}(t)=\gamma^{\prime}_{\theta}(t),V_{1}(t),\ldots,V_{n-1}(t)\right\}

and the symmetric curvature matrix
K(\gamma_{\theta}(t))
given by

\displaystyle K(\gamma_{\theta}(t))_{ij}=\left<R(\gamma^{\prime}_{\theta}(t),V_{i}(t))\gamma^{\prime}_{\theta}(t),V_{j}(t)\right>,

where
R
is the curvature tensor. Using this frame we can write the Jacobi equation (
2
) as a second order ODE on
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}

(4)

\displaystyle Y^{\prime\prime}(t)+K(\gamma_{\theta}(t))Y(t)=0,

where derivatives are taken componentwise. If
Y(t)
is a solution of (
4
), then the curve
J(t)=Y(t)w
corresponds to a perpendicular Jacobi field along
\gamma_{\theta}(t)
for every
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. Given
r\in\mathbb{R}
, let
Y_{\theta,r}(t)
be a solution of (
4
) such that
Y_{\theta,r}(0)=I
and
Y_{\theta,r}(r)=0
. This solution is unique since
M
has no conjugate points. In
[
3
]
, Green proved that there exists the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}Y_{\theta,r}(t)=Y_{\theta}^{s}(t)

for every
\theta\in SM
. Moreover,
Y_{\theta}^{s}(t)
is also a solution of (
4
) and
\det Y_{\theta}^{s}(t)\neq 0
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Analogously, taking the limit when
r\rightarrow-\infty
, we define
Y_{\theta}^{u}(t)
.

For every
\theta\in SM
, let

\displaystyle X^{s}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\left\{\xi\in T_{\theta}SM:\left<\xi,G(\theta)\right>=0\text{ and }J_{\xi}(t)=Y_{\theta}^{s}(t)d_{\theta}\pi(\xi)\right\},

\displaystyle X^{u}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\left\{\xi\in T_{\theta}SM:\left<\xi,G(\theta)\right>=0\text{ and }J_{\xi}(t)=Y_{\theta}^{u}(t)d_{\theta}\pi(\xi)\right\}.

The subspaces
X^{s}(\theta)
and
X^{u}(\theta)
are called the stable and unstable subspaces of
T_{\theta}SM
. When
\xi\in X^{s}(\theta)
(respectively,
\xi\in X^{u}(\theta)
), the Jacobi field associated
J_{\xi}(t)
is called a stable (respectively, unstable) Jacobi field along
\gamma_{\theta}(t)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

[4]

The subspaces
X^{s}(\theta)
and
X^{u}(\theta)
have the following properties:

1.

X^{s}(\theta)
and
X^{u}(\theta)
are
(n-1)
-dimensional subspaces of
T_{\theta}SM
and they are invariant under the differential
d\phi^{t}
, that is,

\displaystyle d_{\theta}\phi^{t}(X^{s(u)}(\theta))=X^{s(u)}(\phi^{t}(\theta)),\hskip 14.22636ptt\in\mathbb{R}.

2.

\|\alpha_{\theta}(\xi)\|\leq c\|d_{\theta}\pi(\xi)\|
for every
\xi\in X^{s}(\theta)
or
\xi\in X^{u}(\theta)
, where
\alpha:TTM\rightarrow TM
denotes the connection map.

3.

If
\xi\in X^{s}(\theta)
or
\xi\in X^{u}(\theta)
, then
J_{\xi}(t)\neq 0
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

4.

If
M
has no focal points then for
\xi\in X^{s}(\theta)
(respectively,
\xi\in X^{u}(\theta)
), the function
t\rightarrow\|J_{\xi}(t)\|
is non-increasing (respectively, non-decreasing).

5.

If
M
has no focal points then
X^{s}(\theta)
and
X^{u}(\theta)
depend continuously on
\theta\in SM
.

It’s important to remark that when the geodesic flow is of Anosov type,
E^{s}(\theta)=X^{s}(\theta)
and
E^{u}(\theta)=X^{u}(\theta)
. Therefore, these subspaces are the candidates to be the stable and unstable subspaces required by the Anosov condition.

3.
Proof of theorem 1.1

In this section we will prove the Theorem 1.1. The idea of the proof is to show that the stable Jacobi fields have norm exponentially decreasing from a large positive time and therefore the derivative of the geodesic flow, restricted to stable subspace, will have the same behavior in norm. Then, we translate this information for the unstable case and finally, by a similar argument utilized by Melo and Romaña in
[
9
]
, we show the uniform contraction and expansion of the derivative of the geodesic flow in the stable and unstable subspaces, respectively.

For the final part of the proof, we use the following lemma, which was proven by Melo and Romaña in
[
9
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f:(0,+\infty)\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
be a bounded function such that

(i)

f(t+s)\leq f(t)\cdot f(s)
for every
s,t>0
;

(ii)

There is
r>0
such that
f(r)<1
.

Then, there are two constants
C>0
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle f(t)\leq C\lambda^{t},\hskip 14.22636ptt>0.

We are ready to prove the Theorem 1.1. Remember that, for
\theta\in SM
and any non-zero perpendicular Jacobi vector field
J(t)
along
\gamma_{\theta}
, we denote by
K(\gamma^{\prime}_{\theta}(t),J(t))
the sectional curvature of the tangent plane spanned by
\gamma^{\prime}_{\theta}(t)
and
J(t)
at
\gamma_{\theta}(t)
.

Proof of Theorem 1.1.
Fix
\theta\in SM
and consider an orthonormal basis
\left\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1}\right\}
of
X^{s}(\theta)
. For
i=1,\ldots,n-1
, denote by
J_{i}
the stable Jacobi field along
\gamma_{\theta}
associated with
\xi_{i}
by the isomorphism seen in section 2.2. Define the function
f_{i}(t)=\left\|J_{i}(t)\right\|^{2}
. We have that
f_{i}
is a non-increasing function because
M
has no focal points. Moreover,

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\left\langle J_{i}(t),J_{i}(t)\right\rangle

(5)

\displaystyle=-2K(\gamma^{\prime}_{\theta}(t),J_{i}(t))f_{i}(t)+2\left\|J^{\prime}_{i}(t)\right\|^{2}.

Since
\xi_{i}\in X^{s}(\theta)
, by Proposition 2.1 we have that
J_{i}(t)\neq 0
and therefore
f_{i}(t)\neq 0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Consider the function

\displaystyle z_{i}(t)=\dfrac{f^{\prime}_{i}(t)}{f_{i}(t)}\leq 0.

By (
3
), we have that
z_{i}
satisfies the Ricatti’s equation

(6)

\displaystyle z^{\prime}_{i}(t)+z_{i}^{2}(t)-\dfrac{2}{f_{i}(t)}\left\|J^{\prime}_{i}(t)\right\|^{2}+2K(\gamma^{\prime}_{\theta}(t),J_{i}(t))=0

and by Proposition 2.1 and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\displaystyle\left|f^{\prime}_{i}(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2c\left\|d_{\phi^{t}(\theta)}\pi(d_{\theta}\phi^{t}(\xi_{i}))\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=2cf_{i}(t).

It follows that
\sup_{t\geq 0}\left|z_{i}(t)\right|\leq 2c
. Integrating the equation (
6
) on the interval
[0,t]
we obtain

\displaystyle\dfrac{z_{i}(t)-z_{i}(0)}{t}+\dfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}z_{i}^{2}(s)ds

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2}{t}\int_{0}^{t}(f_{i}(s))^{-1}\left\|J^{\prime}_{i}(s)\right\|^{2}ds-\dfrac{2}{t}\int_{0}^{t}K(\gamma^{\prime}_{\theta}(s),J_{i}(s))ds

\displaystyle\geq-\dfrac{2}{t}\int_{0}^{t}K(\gamma^{\prime}_{\theta}(s),J_{i}(s))ds.

The hypothesis (
1
) implies that there exists
t_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}z^{2}_{i}(s)ds\geq B,\hskip 14.22636ptt>t_{1}.

Since
z_{i}
is a non-positive function and bounded by

, we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{B}{4c^{2}}\leq\dfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{z_{i}^{2}(s)}{4c^{2}}ds\leq-\dfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{z_{i}(s)}{2c}ds,\hskip 17.07182ptt>t_{1}.

It follows that

(7)

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}z_{i}(s)ds\leq-\dfrac{B}{2c},\hskip 14.22636ptt>t_{1}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}z_{i}(s)ds=\dfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{d}{ds}\left[\log(f_{i}(s))\right]ds=\dfrac{2}{t}\log\left(\dfrac{\left\|J_{i}(t)\right\|}{\left\|d_{\theta}\pi(\xi_{i})\right\|}\right).

Then (
7
) implies that for
i=1,\ldots,n-1

(8)

\displaystyle\left\|J_{i}(t)\right\|\leq e^{-\frac{B}{4c}t},\hskip 17.07182ptt>t_{1}.

We claim that every stable Jacobi field along
\gamma_{\theta}
has norm exponentially decreasing for
t>t_{1}
. In fact, set
\xi\in X^{s}(\theta)
. We can write

\displaystyle\xi=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\alpha_{i}\xi_{i}

where
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n-1}\in\mathbb{R}
. By the uniqueness of the Jacobi fields, we have that

J_{\xi}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\alpha_{i}J_{i}(t).

By (
8
), it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|J_{\xi}(t)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left|\alpha_{i}\right|\left\|J_{i}(t)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\frac{B}{4c}t}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left|\alpha_{i}\right|,\hskip 11.38092ptt>t_{1}

\displaystyle\leq(n-1)e^{-\frac{B}{4c}t}\left\|\xi\right\|,\hskip 17.07182ptt>t_{1}.

Therefore, by (
3
), for all
\theta\in SM

(9)

\displaystyle\left\|\left.d_{\theta}\phi^{t}\right|_{X^{s}(\theta)}\right\|\leq Ce^{-\frac{B}{4c}t},\hskip 17.07182ptt>t_{1}

where
C=(n-1)(1+c^{2})^{1/2}>0
.

For the unstable case, if
\eta\in X^{u}(\theta)
then
\xi=d_{\theta}S(\eta)\in X^{s}(S(\theta))
, where
S:SM\rightarrow SM
takes
(x,v)
into
(x,-v)
. Since
S
is an isometry of
SM
and
S\circ\phi^{t}=\phi^{-t}\circ S
for all

we have that

\displaystyle\left\|d_{\theta}\phi^{-t}(\eta)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|d_{\phi^{t}(S(\theta))}S\circ d_{S(\theta)}\phi^{t}\circ d_{\theta}S(\eta)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|d_{S(\theta)}\phi^{t}(\xi)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\frac{B}{4c}t}\left\|\xi\right\|,\hskip 22.76228ptt>t_{1}

\displaystyle=Ce^{-\frac{B}{4c}t}\left\|\eta\right\|,\hskip 22.76228ptt>t_{1}.

Therefore, for all
\theta\in SM

(10)

\displaystyle\left\|\left.d_{\theta}\phi^{-t}\right|_{X^{u}(\theta)}\right\|\leq Ce^{-\frac{B}{4c}t},\hskip 17.07182ptt>t_{1}.

Since
M
has no focal points, by Proposition 2.1 we have that the norm of stable Jacobi fields are non-increasing and the norm of unstable Jacobi fields are non-decreasing. Also, as the curvature is bounded below by
-c^{2}
, using the item 2 of Proposition 2.1 and the identification (
3
) we obtain a constant
G>0
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\left.d_{\theta}\phi^{t}\right|_{X^{s}(\theta)}\right\|\leq e^{G}\hskip 8.5359pt\text{and}\hskip 8.5359pt\left\|\left.d_{\theta}\phi^{-t}\right|_{X^{u}(\theta)}\right\|\leq e^{G},\hskip 14.22636ptt\in[0,t_{1}].

To conclude the proof, we consider the following functions defined for
t\geq 0

\displaystyle f_{s}(t)=\sup_{\theta\in SM}\left\|\left.d_{\theta}\phi^{t}\right|_{X^{s}(\theta)}\right\|\hskip 11.38092pt\text{and}\hskip 11.38092ptf_{u}(t)=\sup_{\theta\in SM}\left\|\left.d_{\theta}\phi^{-t}\right|_{X^{u}(\theta)}\right\|.

Observe that
f_{s}
and
f_{u}
are bounded functions by the inequalities above. Since
\phi^{t}
is a flow, these functions satisfy the condition (i) of Lemma 3.1. Moreover, by (
9
) and (
10
), there exists
r>0
such that
f_{s}(r)<1
and
f_{u}(r)<1
, that is,
f_{s}
and
f_{u}
satisfy the item (ii) of Lemma 3.1. Thus, there are
C_{s},C_{u}>0
and
\lambda_{s},\lambda_{u}\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle f_{s}(t)\leq C_{s}\lambda_{s}^{t}\hskip 8.5359pt\text{and}\hskip 8.5359ptf_{u}(t)\leq C_{u}\lambda_{u}^{t},\hskip 14.22636ptt\geq 0.

If
C=\max\left\{C_{s},C_{u}\right\}
and
\lambda=\max\left\{\lambda_{s},\lambda_{u}\right\}
, we have that for all
\theta\in SM

\displaystyle\left\|\left.d_{\theta}\phi^{t}\right|_{X^{s}(\theta)}\right\|\leq C\lambda^{t}\hskip 8.5359pt\text{and}\hskip 8.5359pt\left\|\left.d_{\theta}\phi^{-t}\right|_{X^{u}(\theta)}\right\|\leq C\lambda^{t},\hskip 14.22636ptt\geq 0.

This last result shows us that
X^{s}(\theta)
and
X^{u}(\theta)
are linearly independent. Furthermore, since
M
has no focal points, the subspaces
X^{s}(\theta)
and
X^{u}(\theta)
depend continuously on
\theta\in SM
by Proposition 2.1. Therefore, we conclude that the geodesic flow is Anosov.
\hfill\square

Remark:
Following the same argument of the previous proof, we can replace the orthonormal basis of the stable subspace in Theorem 1.1 by an orthonormal basis of the unstable subspace and obtain the same result.

4.
Example of Anosov geodesic flow on a non-compact manifold

In this section, we use the Theorem 1.1 to construct a non-compact manifold that is non-compactly homogeneous and has geodesic flow of Anosov type. The main tool to construct this manifold is the “
warped product
”. The idea is similar to
[
9
]
, where the authors consider a warped product metric in
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
. We apply the same techniques with some modifications, since the sectional curvature formula is slightly complicated in higher dimensions.

4.1.
Warped products

Let
(M,g_{M})
and
(N,g_{N})
be Riemannian manifolds and
f>0
a smooth function on
M
. The
warped product

M\times_{f}N
is the manifold
M\times N
furnished with the Riemannian metric

\displaystyle g=\pi^{*}_{M}(g_{M})+(f\circ\pi_{M})^{2}\pi^{*}_{N}(g_{N}),

where
\pi_{M}
and
\pi_{N}
are the projections of
M\times N
onto
M
and
N
, respectively.

Let
X
be a vector field on
M
. The horizontal lift of
X
to
M\times_{f}N
is the vector field
\overline{X}
such that
d_{(p,q)}\pi_{M}(\overline{X}(p,q))=X(p)
and
d_{(p,q)}\pi_{N}(\overline{X}(p,q))=0
. If
Y
is a vector field on
N
, the vertical lift of
Y
to
M\times_{f}N
is the vector field
\overline{Y}
such that
d_{(p,q)}\pi_{M}(\overline{Y}(p,q))=0
and
d_{(p,q)}\pi_{N}(\overline{Y}(p,q))=Y(q)
. The set of all such lifts are denoted, as usual, by
\mathcal{L}(M)
and
\mathcal{L}(N)
, respectively.

We denote by
R_{P},R
and
S
the curvature tensor on
P=M\times_{f}N
,
M
and
N
, respectively. The following lemma describes the relationship between the curvature tensor of
P
and that of
M
and
N
.

Lemma 4.1
.

[2]

On the manifold
P=M\times_{f}N
, if
\overline{X},\overline{Y},\overline{Z}\in\mathcal{L}(M)
and
\overline{U},\overline{V},\overline{W}\in\mathcal{L}(N)
, then

(1)

R_{P}(U,V)W=S(U,V)W-\left\|G\right\|^{2}\left[(U,V)V-(V,W)U\right]
, where
G
is the gradient of
f
and
(
,
)
is the Riemannian metric on
N
.

(2)

R_{P}(X,V)Y=-\dfrac{1}{f}\left\langle\nabla_{X}G,Y\right\rangle V
, where
G
is the gradient of
f
.

(3)

R_{P}(\overline{X},\overline{Y})\overline{V}=R_{P}(\overline{V},\overline{W})\overline{X}=0
.

(4)

R_{P}(\overline{X},\overline{V})\overline{W}=R_{P}(\overline{X},\overline{W})\overline{V}=f(V,W)\nabla_{X}G
, where
G
is the gradient of
f
.

(5)

R_{P}(\overline{X},\overline{Y})\overline{Z}=R(X,Y)Z

We denote by

and

the sectional curvature on

and
N
respectively. Using the Lemma 4.1 we can compute the sectional curvature as follows: Let
\Pi
be a plane tangent to

at
(p,q)
, and let vectors

be an orthonormal basis for
\Pi
, where
x,y
are horizontal parts and
v,w
are vertical parts given by the projections. The sectional curvature is given by the following formula:

\displaystyle K_{P}(\Pi)=

\displaystyle K(x,y)\left\|x\wedge y\right\|^{2}-f(p)\left\{(w,w)\nabla^{2}f(x,x)-2(v,w)\nabla^{2}f(x,y)\right.

(11)

\displaystyle\left.+(v,v)\nabla^{2}f(y,y)\right\}+f^{2}(p)\left[L(v,w)-\left\|G(p)\right\|^{2}\right](v\wedge w,v\wedge w).

4.2.
Example

For
n\geq 1
, consider the flat
n
-torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}=\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2n}
and the warped product
\mathbb{R}\times_{f}\mathbb{T}^{n}
, where
f(x)=e^{g(x)}
and
g(x)
is a smooth function such that

(A)

h(x)=g^{\prime\prime}(x)+\left(g^{\prime}(x)\right)^{2}\geq 0
for any
x
;

(B)

h
is a periodic function with period
T>0
;

(C)

There are positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that
\frac{C_{1}}{2}<g^{\prime}(x)<\frac{C_{2}}{2}
for any
x
.

It’s not difficult to find functions
g
that satisfy these conditions. For example, we can consider
g(x)=ax-\cos x+\sin x
, for some
a>0
.

Let
\Pi
be a tangent plane to
\mathbb{R}\times_{f}\mathbb{T}^{n}
at
(t,p)
, then
\Pi
has orthonormal basis
z+v,w
with
v,w
vertical parts and
z
horizontal part given by the projections. By the equation (
4.1
) we have

(12)

\displaystyle K(\Pi)=-\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}(t)}{f(t)}\left\|z\right\|^{2}-\left(\dfrac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}\right)^{2}\left\|v\right\|^{2}\leq 0

where
\left\|z\right\|^{2}+\left\|v\right\|^{2}=1
(warped product norm). In particular, it follows from (B) and (C) that the sectional curvature of
\mathbb{R}\times_{f}\mathbb{T}^{n}
is bounded below and the manifold has no focal points since the sectional curvature is non-positive. Throughout the rest of this section, we will show that
\mathbb{R}\times_{f}\mathbb{T}^{n}
satisfies the equation (
1
). Consider a geodesic
\gamma(t)
in
\mathbb{R}\times_{f}\mathbb{T}^{n}
with
\left|\gamma^{\prime}(t)\right|=1
and a parametrization of
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{n}

\displaystyle\varphi_{t_{0}}:

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69046pt\mathbb{R}\times(t_{0},t_{0}+2\pi)^{n}\hskip 5.69046pt\rightarrow\hskip 45.52458pt\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{n}

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\hskip 19.91684pt\rightarrow(x,\cos y_{1},\sin y_{1},\ldots,\cos y_{n},\sin y_{n})

such that
\varphi_{t_{0}}(\mathbb{R}\times(t_{0},t_{0}+2\pi)^{n})\cap\gamma(\mathbb{R})\neq\emptyset
. Let
(x(t),y_{1}(t),\ldots,y_{n}(t))
be the local expression of
\gamma(t)
. Using the formula to calculate the Christoffel symbols we obtain that

•

\Gamma_{ii}^{1}=-f^{\prime}\cdot f=-g^{\prime}e^{2g}
, for
i=2,\ldots,n
.

•

\Gamma_{1i}^{i}=\Gamma_{i1}^{i}=\dfrac{f^{\prime}}{f}=g^{\prime}
, for
i=2,\ldots,n
.

•

\Gamma^{k}_{ij}=0
in other case.

Since
\gamma
is a geodesic with
\left|\gamma^{\prime}(t)\right|=1
, the functions

satisfy the following equalities:

(a)

x^{\prime\prime}(t)-e^{2g(x(t))}g^{\prime}(x(t))\left[(y^{\prime}_{1}(t))^{2}+(y^{\prime}_{2}(t))^{2}+\ldots(y^{\prime}_{n}(t))^{2}\right]=0

(b)

y^{\prime\prime}_{i}(t)+2g^{\prime}(x(t))x^{\prime}(t)y^{\prime}_{i}(t)=0
, for
i=1,\ldots,n
.

(c)

(x^{\prime}(t))^{2}+e^{2g(x(t))}\left[(y^{\prime}_{1}(t))^{2}+(y^{\prime}_{2}(t))^{2}+\ldots(y^{\prime}_{n}(t))^{2}\right]=1
.

Combining (a) and (c) we have that

(13)

\displaystyle x^{\prime\prime}(t)=g^{\prime}(x(t))(1-(x^{\prime}(t))^{2}).

Moreover,
\left|x^{\prime}(t)\right|\leq 1
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, since the equality (
13
) does not depend of
t_{0}
. If there exists

such that
\left|x^{\prime}(a)\right|=1

\gamma(t)=(x(a)-a+t,y_{1}(a),\ldots,y_{n}(a))
or
\gamma(t)=(x(a)+a-t,y_{1}(a),\ldots,y_{n}(a))
. Now assume that
|x^{\prime}(t)|<1
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
and denote by
b(t)=x^{\prime}(t)
. From (
13

(14)

\displaystyle\dfrac{b^{\prime}(t)}{1-(b(t))^{2}}=g^{\prime}(x(t)),

that is,

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\log\left(\dfrac{1+b(t)}{1-b(t)}\right)\right)^{\prime}=g^{\prime}(x(t)).

Integrating from

\displaystyle\log\left(\dfrac{1+b(t)}{1-b(t)}\right)-\log\left(\dfrac{1+b(0)}{1-b(0)}\right)=2\int_{0}^{t}g^{\prime}(x(s))ds.

By property (C) of the function

\displaystyle B_{0}e^{C_{1}t}<\dfrac{1+b(t)}{1-b(t)}<B_{0}e^{C_{2}t},

(15)

\displaystyle 1-\dfrac{2}{B_{0}e^{C_{1}t}+1}<b(t)<1-\dfrac{2}{B_{0}e^{C_{2}t}+1}.

Let

t>4T+\max\left\{\dfrac{2}{C_{1}}\log 3+4T,\dfrac{4}{C_{1}}\log 3\right\}
then

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}K(\gamma^{\prime}(s),J(s))ds<\max\left\{-\dfrac{\eta}{64T},-\dfrac{\eta}{4(8T+2A)}\right\}<0,

for any geodesic
\gamma(t)
and any non-zero perpendicular Jacobi field
J(t)
along
\gamma(t)
. It follows from Theorem 1.1 that the geodesic flow of
\mathbb{R}\times_{f}\mathbb{T}^{n}
is of Anosov type.

5.
Negative curvature does not guarantee Anosov geodesic flow in non-compact manifolds

It’s known that for a manifold of negative pinched curvature, its geodesic flow is Anosov. However, in the non-compact case, the negative sign of the curvature does not imply that we have a geodesic flow of Anosov type, as we will see in the following example. Thus, similar to the example given in
[
9
]
we have,

5.1.
Example

For
n\geq 1
, consider the manifold
M=\mathbb{R}\times_{f}\mathbb{T}^{n}
, where
f(x)=\sqrt{1+x^{2}}
. It follows from (
12
) that
-2\leq K(\Pi)<0
for all tangent plane to
M
. Now consider a ray
\gamma:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow M
, where
\gamma(t)=(t,y_{0})
with
y_{0}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
and a non-zero perpendicular Jacobi field
J(t)
along
\gamma(t)
. Observe that

\displaystyle K(\gamma^{\prime}(t),J(t))=-\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}(t)}{f(t)}=-\dfrac{1}{(1+t^{2})^{2}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\dfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}K(\gamma^{\prime}(s),J(s))ds=0.

By Corollary 1.2, we have that the geodesic flow of
M
is not Anosov. This allows us to conclude that, from the point of view of the dynamics, a manifold of negative curvature and a manifold of pinched negative curvature are totally different.

The Phase Diagram of BPS Black Holes in AdS 5 Preprint: LCTP-21-22
Nizar Ezroura, Finn Larsen, Zhihan Liu, and Yangwenxiao Zeng

Abstract
Motivated by recent studies of supersymmetric black holes, we revisit the phase diagram of AdS black holes, whether BPS or not, with particular emphasis on the role of rotation. We develop BPS thermodynamics systematically and, in many explicit examples, we study its striking similarities with more familiar AdS black holes, as well as some differences. We highlight an important fugacity that preserves BPS saturation but is not captured by the supersymmetric index.

The phase diagram for BPS black holes is surprisingly elaborate. It has been known for a few years that, with
\varphi^{\prime}=0

Our work augments the BPS phase diagram and show that, in various ranges of the “primed” BPS potentials, the small black holes may exhibit a maximal temperature, their free energy at high temperature may asymptote to a nonzero value, or the small black hole branch may disappear altogether. The large black holes, on the other hand, exhibit simple scaling laws at high temperature. These structures are reminiscent of the phase diagram for non-supersymmetric AdS black holes but here they appear in the context of BPS thermodynamics, justifying hope for a precision understanding of these interesting features starting from microscopic principles.

2.2.2
Subcritical Electric Potential:

Starting from vanishing electric potential

The phase diagram remains qualitatively similar to the AdS-Schwarzschild case as the electric potential increases, but quantitative features are modified. Some of these changes are similar to an increase in angular velocity at fixed electric potential:

•

The free energy at a given temperature decreases, on both the small and the large black hole branches. This follows from the thermodynamic relation
\partial_{\Phi}G=-Q

•

The transition temperature
T_{\text{HP}}

The minimal temperature, attained at the cusp where the two branches meet, also decreases.

•

The free energy at the cusp

•

The transition temperature

(and so the minimal temperature

3.1
General Structure of BPS Thermodynamics

Suppose we have a general free energy
2
2

2

In this subsection we refer to a single electric potential
\Phi
and a single angular velocity
\Omega
, to keep terminology simple. However, one or both kinds of potentials can have multiple components, corresponding to several independent charges and/or angular momenta.

G=G(T,\Phi,\Omega)=M-TS-\Phi Q-\Omega J~,

(55)

and want to study the states that satisfy the BPS mass relation

M=\Phi^{*}Q+\Omega^{*}J~,

(56)

where
\Phi^{*}
and
\Omega^{*}
are given numbers that depend on the system we consider. The general free energy (
55
) satisfies the
first law

dG=-SdT-Qd\Phi-Jd\Omega~,

so it yields the mass

M=G+TS+\Phi Q+\Omega J=(1-T\partial_{T}-\Phi\partial_{\Phi}-\Omega\partial_{\Omega})G~.

The BPS mass relation (
56
) is satisfied if and only if

\Big[1-T\partial_{T}G-(\Phi-\Phi^{*})\partial_{\Phi}-(\Omega-\Omega^{*})\partial_{\Omega}\Phi\Big]G=0~.

This is the condition that the free energy is
homogeneous of degree
1
in the variables
T,\Phi-\Phi^{*},\Omega-\Omega^{*}
.

Considering any
G
that is homogeneous of degree
1
, we define the BPS free energy

W(\Phi^{\prime},\Omega^{\prime})=\frac{G}{T}=-S-\Phi^{\prime}Q-\Omega^{\prime}J~.

(57)

The second form used the BPS mass relation (
56
)
and introduced primed potentials

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Phi-\Phi^{*}}{T}~,

(58)

\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega-\Omega^{*}}{T}~.

(59)

The function
W inherits a homogeneity property from
G
and, because we have divided by
T
, it is in fact precisely homogeneous in the variables
T,\Phi-\Phi^{*},\Omega-\Omega^{*}
, it has “weight” zero. Therefore, it is possible to
interpret it as a function of the ratios
\Phi^{\prime},\Omega^{\prime}
, as we have indicated in our notation for
W
.
In the BPS limit we necessarily have temperature
T\rightarrow 0
so these “primed” variables can be identified as derivatives with respect to the temperature, evaluated at
T=0
. An alternative but equivalent interpretation is that the BPS surface is a projective manifold defined by ratios of thermodynamic variables.

The BPS free energy
W
satisfies the first law

dW=-Qd\Phi^{\prime}-Jd\Omega^{\prime}~,

(60)

so

\displaystyle Q=-\frac{\partial W}{\partial\Phi^{\prime}}~,

\displaystyle~~~~~~~J=-\frac{\partial W}{\partial\Omega^{\prime}}~.

(61)

It yields the entropy

S=(-1+\Phi^{\prime}\partial_{\Phi^{\prime}}+\Omega^{\prime}\partial_{\Omega^{\prime}})W~.

(62)

However, the BPS mass has been “integrated out” of the BPS thermodynamics, it is not encoded in
W
and only follows from
Q
and
J
via the BPS mass relation (
56
).

In statistical mechanics, the BPS free energy
W
arises when introducing a BPS partition function as a trace over all BPS states

Z_{\rm BPS}={\rm Tr}_{\rm BPS}\left[e^{\Phi^{\prime}Q+\Omega^{\prime}J}\right]~,

(63)

with no explicit reference to mass. Then the BPS free energy
W=-\ln Z_{\rm BPS}
.

3.2
Parameters for BPS Black Holes in AdS
5

In this paper we primarily study
\text{AdS}_{5}
black holes with a single charge and two independent angular momenta. We want to make the general considerations in the preceding subsection explicit in this specific case.

The BPS condition is

M=3Q+J_{a}+J_{b}~.

(64)

Comparing with the generic formula (
56
) we have

\Phi^{*}=3~,

(65)

and, for
a=b
,
\Omega^{*}=2
. In the general case where
a\neq b
there are two independent rotational velocities
\Omega_{a,b}
with BPS values

\Omega^{*}_{a}=\Omega^{*}_{b}=1~.

(66)

The general parametric expressions for the black hole quantum numbers
(M,Q,J_{a,b})
in (

-

M-3Q-(J_{a}+J_{b})=\frac{1}{2}N^{2}\frac{3+(a+b)-ab}{(1-a)(1+a)^{2}(1-b)(1+b)^{2}}\Big(m-q(1+a+b)\Big)~.

The prefactor on the right hand side is manifestly positive for all
a,b\in[0,1[
, so the BPS formula (
64
) yields the condition on the parameters
(m,q,a,b)
:

m=q(1+a+b)~.

(68)

We can therefore consider
m
a dependent variable and parametrize the BPS black holes by
(q,a,b)
.

Even when the parameter
m
satisfies (
68
), the equation (
9
) for the coordinate location of the event horizon is a cubic equation in
r^{2}
. Instead of proceeding by brute force, it is useful to recall that BPS black holes are extremal and so we expect a double root. With this clue, and the BPS
condition (
68
), it is manageable to find the solution

r_{*}^{2}=a+b+ab~,

(69)

for a specific value of the parameter
q
:

q^{*}=(1+a)(1+b)(a+b)~.

(70)

Whichever way this solution for particular values of
m,q
was arrived at, we can, for any
m,q
, recast the cubic equation (

) satisfied by the horizon location

as

(r^{2}-r^{2}_{*})^{2}r^{2}+\Big((q-q^{*})-(r^{2}-r^{2}_{*})(1+a+b)\Big)^{2}+2r^{2}(m-q(1+a+b))=0~.

(71)

When the BPS condition (
68
) is satisfied this expression is the sum of two squares so, for BPS black holes, it is manifest that a real solution exists
only
for
q=q^{*}
and for this value of
q
there is indeed a double root when
r^{2}=r^{2}_{*}
. We will refer to the starred variables as the BPS values.

When
m
satisfies the BPS condition (
68
) and
q
takes its BPS value (
70
), the parametric formulae (
5
-
8
) for the physical variables
(M,Q,J_{a,b})

simplify to:

\displaystyle M^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}N^{2}\frac{(a+b)(3-a^{2}+ab-b^{2}-ab(a+b))}{(1-a)^{2}(1-b)^{2}}~,

(72)

\displaystyle Q^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}N^{2}\frac{a+b}{(1-a)(1-b)}~,

(73)

\displaystyle J_{a}^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}N^{2}\frac{(a+b)(2a+b+ab)}{(1-a)^{2}(1-b)}~,

(74)

\displaystyle J_{b}^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}N^{2}\frac{(a+b)(a+2b+ab)}{(1-a)(1-b)^{2}}~.

(75)

The potential
\Phi
and the angular velocities
\Omega_{a,b}
given in (
11
-
13
) reduce to the BPS values (
65
-
66
), as they should.
The entropy

(

) simplifies to:

S^{*}=2\pi\frac{1}{2}N^{2}\frac{(a+b)\sqrt{a+b+ab}}{(1-a)(1-b)}~.

(76)

As we have stressed, once we impose the BPS mass formula (
64
), black holes exist only when the parameters
q,a,b
satisfy the relation (
70
). Thus the BPS mass cannot be reached for all black hole variables
(Q,J_{a},J_{b})
. Rather, according to (

), (

) and (
75

\left(Q^{*3}+\frac{1}{2}N^{2}J_{a}^{*}J_{b}^{*}\right)-\left(3Q^{*}+\frac{1}{2}N^{2}\right)\left(3Q^{*2}-\frac{1}{2}N^{2}(J^{*}_{a}+J^{*}_{b})\right)=0~.

(77)

It is surprising that there is this condition on the charges.

3.3
The BPS Free Energy

Usually, a good starting point for the study of thermodynamics is the free energy
G
, an extensive function of the intensive thermodynamic potentials
T,\Phi,\Omega_{a,b}
. However, for BPS black holes the free energy vanishes identically
G=0
and the “variables” it would ordinarily be a “function” of are fixed at
T=0,\Phi^{*}=3
, and
\Omega^{*}_{a,b}=1
.

The general structure of BPS thermodynamics discussed in subsection
3.1
addresses this obstacle.
A free energy
G
that is homogeneous of degree one in
T
,
\Phi-\Phi^{*}
,
\Omega_{a,b}-\Omega_{a,b}^{*}
depends on variables that nominally depart from their BPS values, but the homogeneity property shows that the mass is
exactly
the BPS mass. Therefore, a free energy of this form describes thermodynamics intrinsic to the BPS surface.

3.3.1
Derivation of the BPS Free Energy

In the AdS
5
example we focus on, the explicit free energy
G
(
20
) of a general AdS
5
black hole depends on the electric potential
\Phi
, as it should, but it is also a function of the parameters
r_{+},a,b
, rather than
T
,
\Omega_{a,b}
. Conveniently, the expression for
G
given
in (
20
) vanishes when, in addition to
\Phi=3
,
the auxiliary horizon position
r^{2}_{+}
take the
BPS value
r^{2}_{*}
given in (
69
). At linear order in
both
r^{2}_{+}-r^{2}_{*}
and
\Phi-3
we have

G=-\frac{N^{2}}{2}\frac{a+b}{(1-a)(1-b)}(r^{2}_{+}-r^{2}_{*})-\frac{N^{2}}{6}\frac{(a+b)(1-a-b-ab)}{(1-a)(1-b)}(\Phi-3)~.

The combination
r^{2}_{+}-r^{2}_{*}
has a geometrical interpretation in terms of horizon position, but it is not a traditional thermodynamic potential. It is related to the angular velocities through the two equations (
17
-
18
) which are straightforward
to expand at linear order in
\Phi-\Phi^{*}
,
\Omega_{a,b}-\Omega_{a,b}^{*}
. The result

r^{2}_{+}-r^{2}_{*}=-\frac{(a+b)(1+a)}{2(1-a)}(\Omega_{a}-1)-\frac{(a+b)(1+b)}{2(1-b)}(\Omega_{b}-1)+\frac{a+b+2ab}{6}(\Phi-3)~,

(78)

leads to the free energy

G=-\frac{N^{2}}{12}\frac{(a+b)(2-a-b)}{(1-a)(1-b)}(\Phi-3)+\frac{N^{2}}{4}\frac{(a+b)^{2}(1+a)}{(1-a)^{2}(1-b)}(\Omega_{a}-1)+\frac{N^{2}}{4}\frac{(a+b)^{2}(1+b)}{(1-a)(1-b)^{2}}(\Omega_{b}-1)~.

(79)

Moreover, the condition that the two equations
(
17
-
18
) give identical values for
r^{2}_{+}-r^{2}_{*}

yields the
constraint
on the potentials

\frac{(a+b)(1+a)}{1-a}(\Omega_{a}-1)-\frac{(a+b)(1+b)}{1-b}(\Omega_{b}-1)+\frac{1}{3}(a-b)(\Phi-3)=0~.

(80)

The formula for temperature (
19
), also expanded
at linear order in
\Phi-\Phi^{*}
,
\Omega_{a,b}-\Omega_{a,b}^{*}
,
imposes a second constraint on the potentials

T=-\frac{\sqrt{a+b+ab}}{\pi(1-a)}(\Omega_{a}-1)-\frac{\sqrt{a+b+ab}}{\pi(1-b)}(\Omega_{b}-1)-\frac{\sqrt{a+b+ab}}{3\pi(a+b)}(\Phi-3)~.

(81)

We can interpret the two constraints
(
80
-
81
) together, as definitions of the auxiliary parameters
a,b
. Since both constraints are invariant under simultaneous linear scaling of
T,\Phi-\Phi^{*}
,
\Omega_{a,b}-\Omega_{a,b}^{*}
,
the

defined implicitly this way are homogeneous functions of these potentials.

The free energy (
79
) transforms linearly under simultaneous linear scaling of
T,\Phi-\Phi^{*}
,
\Omega_{a,b}-\Omega_{a,b}^{*}
so, as a function of these variables, it is homogeneous of degree one. According to the discussion in subsection
3.1
, it follows that it describes BPS black holes. To reach this conclusion it is important that the parameters
a,b
are invariant under such rescalings.

To avoid possible confusion, we reiterate the reasoning. The free energy (
79
) and the constraints (
80
-
81
) were all derived by expanding to linear order in
\Phi-\Phi^{*}
,
\Omega_{a,b}-\Omega_{a,b}^{*}
. That could leave the impression that they are approximations that are valid near the BPS limit but in fact they describe the BPS black holes themselves, these equations contain no information beyond BPS. Once a free energy is presented as a homogeneous function, the additional step of taking the limit
T\rightarrow 0
is possible, but not required, doing so corresponds to a choice of coordinates in projective geometry.

To understand how this is possible, consider the mass excess over the BPS mass (

where

(83)

This mass excess
M-M_{*}
is
quadratic
in the small variables and so the
black hole is BPS because the equality
M=M_{*}
holds at linear order.

3.3.2
Simplifications of the Constraints

In general, it is unpractical to solve the constraints (
86
-
87
) and give
a,b
as explicit functions of the potentials
\Omega^{\prime}_{a,b}
and
\Phi^{\prime}
. However, we can do so in the regime where
1-a\ \sim\ 1-b\ \sim\ \epsilon
is
small and positive. This is a version of the Cardy-like limit
Choi:2018hmj
;
David:2020ems
.

In this limit, the constraint (
87
) shows that the
\Phi^{\prime}
term is dominated by the large
\Omega^{\prime}_{a,b}
terms. We can partially address this by tuning
\Omega^{\prime}_{a}\sim\Omega^{\prime}_{b}\sim\epsilon
but, because
a,b
are both near unity we have
(a-b)\sim\epsilon
, and so it is insufficient that the
\Omega^{\prime}_{a,b}
terms are order unity, they must nearly cancel. Thus:

\frac{\Omega^{\prime}_{a}}{1-a}=\frac{\Omega^{\prime}_{b}}{1-b}~,

(88)

up to terms of order
\epsilon
. With this equality, and using
a\sim b\sim 1
extensively, the other constraint (
86
) then yields

\frac{\Omega^{\prime}_{a}}{1-a}=\frac{\Omega^{\prime}_{b}}{1-b}=\frac{\Phi^{\prime}+2\pi\sqrt{3}}{12}~.

(89)

This equation is equivalent to presenting
a,b
as explicit functions of the BPS potentials
\Phi^{\prime},\Omega^{\prime}_{a,b}
, as we wanted to do. With this result it is straightforward to also express the
BPS free energy
W
in (
84

W_{\rm Cardy}=-\frac{N^{2}}{432}\frac{(\Phi^{\prime}+2\pi\sqrt{3})^{3}}{\Omega_{a}^{\prime}\Omega_{b}^{\prime}}~.

(90)

This is expression is completely explicit, but it applies only in the Cardy-like limit.

A complementary approach to the awkward constraints (
86
-
87
) that define
a,b
as functions of the primed potentials is to implement them using Lagrange multipliers. Introducing two
multipliers
\Lambda_{1,2}
, we have:

\begin{split}W&=\frac{1}{2}N^{2}\left[\frac{-(a+b)(2-(a+b))}{6(1-a)(1-b)}\Phi^{\prime}+\frac{(a+b)^{2}(1+a)}{2(1-a)^{2}(1-b)}\Omega_{a}^{\prime}+\frac{(a+b)^{2}(1+b)}{2(1-a)(1-b)^{2}}\Omega_{b}^{\prime}\right]\\
&+\Lambda_{1}\left(-\frac{\sqrt{a+b+ab}}{1-a}\Omega_{a}^{\prime}-\frac{\sqrt{a+b+ab}}{1-b}\Omega_{b}^{\prime}-\frac{\sqrt{a+b+ab}}{3(a+b)}\Phi^{\prime}-\pi\right)\\
&+\Lambda_{2}\left(\frac{(a+b)(1+a)}{1-a}\Omega_{a}^{\prime}-\frac{(a+b)(1+b)}{1-b}\Omega_{b}^{\prime}+\frac{a-b}{3}\Phi^{\prime}\right)~.\end{split}

(91)

Extremization with respect to
a,b
give conditions that are solved when:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}N^{2}\frac{2(a+b)\sqrt{a+b+ab}}{(1-a)(1-b)}~,

(92)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}N^{2}\frac{-(a-b)}{2(1-a)(1-b)}~,

(93)

without imposing any additional relation on
\Phi^{\prime}
and
\Omega_{a,b}^{\prime}
.
Substituting these equations into (
91
), we find:

\begin{split}W_{\text{ext}}=-\frac{1}{2}N^{2}&\Big[\frac{2\pi(a+b)\sqrt{a+b+ab}}{(1-a)(1-b)}-\frac{(a+b)(2a+b+ab)}{(1-a)^{2}(1-b)}\Omega^{\prime}_{a}\\
&-\frac{(a+b)(a+2b+ab)}{(1-a)(1-b)^{2}}\Omega^{\prime}_{b}-\frac{a+b}{(1-a)(1-b)}\Phi^{\prime}\Big]~.\end{split}

(94)

Importantly, in this equation
a,b
are auxiliary variables:
W_{\rm ext}
is defined only after extremizing
a,b
with primed potentials kept fixed.

At this point differentiation with respect to the primed potentials is trivial, there is no need to take implicit dependence via
a,b
into account, because these auxiliary variables are anyway evaluated at their extremum. Thus, in (
94
) the coefficient of each BPS potential
\Phi^{\prime}
,
\Omega^{\prime}_{a,b}
must reproduce its conjugate conserved charge (
73
,
74
,
75
), as indeed it does. Additionally, the BPS black hole entropy can be computed from the BPS potential through the formula (
62

W_{\rm ext}
given in (
94

14

The free energy (
94
) is thus completely general,
unlike the expression (
90
) in the Cardy-limit

a,b
must be extremized over, and the resulting conditions are precisely the constraints (

-
87

Thermodynamics of BPS Black Holes

In our study of BPS thermodynamics, we will facilitate comparisons with the literature by trading the BPS electric potential

4.1
Primed Potentials and Their Conjugate Charges

The general BPS black holes we consider in this section are parametrized by the three

\Omega^{\prime}_{b}

(141)

As we discussed in subsection

\tau
(

\varphi^{\prime}
(

\varphi^{\prime}=0

we introduce a third potential:

\mu=\Omega^{\prime}_{a}-\Omega^{\prime}_{b}~,

(142)

(\tau,\varphi^{\prime},\mu)
follow from the first law of BPS black hole thermodynamics in the form

dW=-\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}(J_{a}+J_{b}+2Q)d\tau-Qd\varphi^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}(J_{a}-J_{b})d\mu~.

(143)

For completeness, we also record the inverse transform from the variables we employ to discuss the phase diagram to the original physical BPS potentials:

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\tau}+\varphi^{\prime}~,

(144)

\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime}_{a}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\tau}+\frac{\mu}{2}~,

(145)

\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime}_{b}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\tau}-\frac{\mu}{2}~,

(146)

that are conjugate the physical charges
Q,J_{a},J_{b}
.

The changes of variables above are routine, at the face of it: BPS black holes are characterized by three charges charges
\{Q,J_{a},J_{b}\}
which, in the grand canonical ensemble, correspond to three BPS potentials
\{\Phi^{\prime},\Omega^{\prime}_{a},\Omega^{\prime}_{b}\}
. When presenting explicit phase diagrams we further change the basis among the potentials to
\{\tau,\varphi^{\prime},\mu\}

77

Our physical interpretation of this peculiar feature, discussed at length in the introduction, is that all classical BPS black holes correspond to thermal equilibrium along the direction in parameter space that corresponds to the BPS potential
\varphi^{\prime}
. The formula for the mass excess
M-M_{*}
(

as well as the potential
\varphi=0

5
Discussion

In this article, we studied the thermodynamics of AdS black holes via analysing their phase diagrams. Compared with previous studies
Chamblin:1999tk
;
Caldarelli:1999xj
, this work has a particular emphasis on the role of rotation. We pointed out that rotation tends to
destabilise
a black hole in the sense that rotation increases the maximal free energy.

We also developed BPS thermodynamics systematically and, in many explicit examples, we pointed out that the phase diagram of the BPS black hole exhibits the similar ”cusp” structure as the well-known AdS Schwarzschild black hole phase diagram. We emphasised the role of an important fugacity,
\varphi^{\prime}
, that preserves BPS saturation. This fugacity

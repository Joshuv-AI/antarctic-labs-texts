There is no “Weil-”cohomology theory with real coefficients for arithmetic curves
Christopher Deninger

Abstract




1
Weil cohomologies over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}

A well known argument by Serre shows that there is no Weil cohomology theory with real coefficients for smooth projective varieties over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. For such a cohomology theory and every elliptic curve
E
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
there would have to be an
{\mathbb{R}}
-algebra anti-homomorphism

1.1
.

(\mathrm{End}\,E)\otimes{\mathbb{R}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}\,H^{1}(E,{\mathbb{R}})\;,

However, if
E
is supersingular, then
(\mathrm{End}\,E)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
is a quaternion algebra over
{\mathbb{Q}}
which is non-split precisely at
p
and
\infty
. Thus
(\mathrm{End}\,E)\otimes{\mathbb{R}}
would be the Hamilton quaternions
\mathbb{H}
whereas
\mathrm{End}\,H^{1}(E,{\mathbb{R}})=M_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, and there is no anti-homomorphism of
{\mathbb{R}}
-algebras
\mathbb{H}\to M_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. The algebras
(\mathrm{End}\,E)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
for
l\neq p
and
(\mathrm{End}\,E)\otimes Q_{p}
are split, where
Q_{p}
is the quotient field of
W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
, the (
p
-typical) Witt vector ring of
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Hence Serre’s argument does not contradict the existence of Weil cohomology theories with values in
{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
resp.
Q_{p}
. Indeed
l
-adic and crystalline cohomology are such theories. In particular, embedding
{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
or
Q_{p}
into
{\mathbb{C}}
one sees that there is a Weil cohomology with values in
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector spaces. Of course such embeddings are highly uncanonical. However, if the standard conjectures on algebraic cycles hold, there is a Weil-cohomology theory with values in
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
. It would give a theory with complex coefficients after choosing an embedding
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
, which is a more reasonable process than embedding
{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
or
Q_{p}
into
{\mathbb{C}}
.

In Scholze
[
15
, Conjecture 9.5 ff]
the existence of a Weil cohomology theory
H^{n}_{Kt_{{\mathbb{Q}}}}(X)
for varieties
X
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
with values in Kottwitz’ category
\mathrm{Kt}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
is conjectured, and assuming well known conjectures also proved. As yet, we have no indication that such a refined category as (the
\mathrm{ind}
-category of
\mathrm{Kt}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
) may be the target of our conjectural “Weil-”cohomology
H^{n}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})
for arithmetic curves in the next section. However, the following much simpler
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear abelian
\otimes
-category
K_{{\mathbb{R}}}
may be a target category. Its objects are
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector spaces
V=V_{0}\oplus V_{1}
with an antilinear isomorphism
\sigma
respecting the grading and
\sigma^{2}=(-1)^{i}
on
V_{i}
. Morphisms are
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear maps which respect the grading and commute with
\sigma
. On
V_{0}
the involution
\sigma
defines the real structure
V^{\sigma=\mathrm{id}}_{0}
, i.e.
V_{0}=V^{\sigma=\mathrm{id}}_{0}\otimes_{{\mathbb{R}}}{\mathbb{C}}
. On
V_{1}
we obtain a quaternionic structure: The quaternions
\mathbb{H}={\mathbb{R}}\oplus{\mathbb{R}}i\oplus{\mathbb{R}}j\oplus{\mathbb{R}}k
act on
V_{1}
by
1,i,j=\sigma
and
k=i\sigma
, turning
V_{1}
into an
\mathbb{H}
-vector space. Thus Serre’s argument would not exclude a Weil cohomology theory
H^{n}
with values in
K_{{\mathbb{R}}}
if
H^{1}(E)=H^{1}(E)_{1}
for supersingular elliptic curves. In fact, as Milne pointed out to me, it follows directly from Grothendieck’s standard conjectures that there exists a Weil cohomology with values in
K_{{\mathbb{R}}}
- the conjectures imply that the motives over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
form a polarized Tannakian category, to which one can apply
[
1
, Theorem 4.29]
. He also remarked that this was likely known to Grothendieck in the 1960s. Note that there are canonical
\otimes
-functors
\mathrm{Kt}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\to\mathrm{Kt}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\to K_{{\mathbb{R}}}
, where Kottwitz’ category
\mathrm{Kt}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is the same as the category
V_{\infty}
in
[
11
]
, Example 1.7.

2
“Weil-”cohomologies over
\mathrm{spec}\,{\mathbb{Z}}

In several articles we discussed the possibility of a cohomology theory
H^{n}(\ ,{\mathcal{C}})
with values in
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector spaces which is defined for all arithmetic schemes over
\mathrm{spec}\,{\mathbb{Z}}
and their arithmetic compactifications. The theory should be equipped with functorial endomorphisms
\theta
whose eigenvalues would be the zeroes and poles of the corresponding zeta functions. In particular the cohomology groups can be infinite dimensional. For arithmetic schemes over
\mathrm{spec}\,{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
such a theory can be constructed by a formal process starting with
l
-adic cohomology, c.f.
[
2
, section 4]
. The question arises whether a theory of this type can have real coefficients. For arithmetic schemes which are not flat over spec
{\mathbb{Z}}
the arguments in
[
5
, 4.7]
make this improbable but for flat schemes over
\mathrm{spec}\,{\mathbb{Z}}
real coefficients remained a theoretical possibility. In the following, we will explain why no real-valued theory of the type conjectured in
[
4
]
,
[
6
]
can exist, even for spectra of number rings. On the other hand, a theory with values in
K_{{\mathbb{R}}}
refining the conjectural
{\mathbb{C}}
-theory seems plausible. We begin by recalling some of the discussion from
[
4
]
,
[
6
]
. Let
k/{\mathbb{Q}}
be a number field and let
\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}}
be the arithmetic compactification of
\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}
. The basic expected properties of
H^{n}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})
and its operator
\theta
are the following:

2.1
.

\theta
is a derivation with respect to cup-product.

2.2
.

H^{0}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})={\mathbb{C}}
with
\theta=0
.

2.3
.

H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})
is infinite dimensional with the eigenvalues of
\theta
being the non-trivial zeroes of
\zeta_{k}(s)
with their multiplicities. Here the eigenvalues
\alpha
are counted with their algebraic multiplicities
\dim H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})^{\theta\sim\alpha}
. Here

H^{\theta\sim\alpha}=\{h\in H\mid(\theta-\alpha)^{n}(h)=0\quad\text{for some}\;n\geq 1\}

is the generalized
\alpha
-eigenspace of
\theta
.

2.4
.

There is a
\theta
-equivariant trace isomorphism

\mathrm{tr}:H^{2}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathbb{C}}(-1)\quad\text{where}\;{\mathbb{C}}(-1)={\mathbb{C}}\;\text{with}\;\theta=\mathrm{id}\;.

By
2.1
and
2.4
, for
h_{1},h_{2}\in H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})
we would have

2.5
.

h_{1}\cup h_{2}=\theta(h_{1}\cup h_{2})=\theta h_{1}\cup h_{2}+h_{1}\cup\theta h_{2}\;.

For
h_{1}\in H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})^{\theta\sim\alpha}
and
h_{2}\in H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})^{\theta\sim\beta}
we have

h_{1}\cup h_{2}\in H^{2}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})^{\theta\sim\alpha+\beta}\;.

Hence
h_{1}\cup h_{2}\neq 0
can only happen by
2.4
if
\alpha+\beta=1
and we expect that:

2.6
.

the pairing

\cup:H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})^{\theta\sim\alpha}\times H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})^{\theta\sim 1-\alpha}\xrightarrow{\cup}H^{2}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})\overset{\mathrm{tr}}{\xrightarrow{\sim}}{\mathbb{C}}

is perfect. In particular,
\alpha
is an eigenvalue of
\theta
on
H^{1}
if and only if
1-\alpha
is an eigenvalue and the (algebraic) multiplicities are equal. Note that via
2.3
this is compatible with the functional equation of
\zeta_{k}(s)
.

Much more optimistically, one could hope for the following property.

2.7
.

There is a canononical anti-linear automorphism
\ast
of
H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})
with
\ast^{2}=-\mathrm{id},\ast\mbox{\scriptsize$\,\circ\,$}\theta=\theta\mbox{\scriptsize$\,\circ\,$}\ast
and such that we get a scalar product by setting

\langle h,h^{\prime}\rangle=\mathrm{tr}(h\cup\ast h^{\prime})\quad\text{on}\;H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})\;.

In particular
\alpha
is then an eigenvalue of
\theta
on
H^{1}
if and only if
\overline{\alpha}
is an eigenvalue. Note that
2.5
and
2.7
imply the relation

\langle h,h^{\prime}\rangle=\langle\theta h,h^{\prime}\rangle+\langle h,\theta h^{\prime}\rangle\;.

Hence
\theta-\frac{1}{2}
would be skew-symmetric on
H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})
and its eigenvalues therefore purely imaginary. Using
2.3
the Riemann hypotheses would follow. Of course this argument follows the lead of Serre
[
16
]
. Also note that if
2.7
is true, skew-symmetry of
\theta-\frac{1}{2}
would imply that the algebraic eigenspaces
H^{\theta\sim\alpha}
of
\theta
are the usual ones
H^{\theta=\alpha}
.

The following heuristic argument suggests that property
2.7
may be more than whishful thinking. In
[
8
]
we showed that higher dimensional analogues of
2.7
together with certain Abel-Jacobi isomorphisms would imply Beilinson’s conjectures on the positivity of height pairings. In particular, suitably normalized the Néron-Tate height pairing on
E({\mathbb{Q}})
for an elliptic curve
E
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
would be the scalar product as in
2.7
restricted to the
1
-eigenspace of
\theta
on the relevant cohomology group. We expect an Abel-Jacobi isomorphism to identify
E({\mathbb{Q}})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}
with this
1
-eigenspace. Thus the same scalar product on cohomology whose positivity would imply that all zeroes of
L(E,s)
lie on the line
\mathrm{Re}\,s=1
would also imply that the Néron-Tate height pairing on
E({\mathbb{Q}})
is positive definite, c.f.
[
8
]
for the details.

For
\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}}
there is no natural known candidate of a
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector space which might be isomorphic to
H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}})^{\theta\sim\frac{1}{2}}
via an Abel-Jacobi map. We will discuss this intriguing problem below.

As a particular case of
2.3
we would get the formula

2.8
.

\mathrm{ord}_{s=1/2}\,\zeta_{k}(s)=\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})^{\theta\sim\frac{1}{2}}\;.

Note that if an antilinear involution
\ast
on
H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})
exists with
\ast^{2}=-\mathrm{id}
and
\ast\mbox{\scriptsize$\,\circ\,$}\theta=\theta\mbox{\scriptsize$\,\circ\,$}\ast
then
V_{k}=H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})^{\theta\sim\frac{1}{2}}
carries a quaternionic structure and hence it has even
{\mathbb{C}}
-dimension. Since the order of
\zeta_{k}(s)
at
s=1/2
is even by the functional equation, this is compatible with (
2.8
). Alternatively, the existence of the perfect alternating pairing
2.6
for
\alpha=1/2

2.9
.

\cup:V_{k}\times V_{k}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}

would also imply that
\dim V_{k}
is even.

Let us now assume that
k/{\mathbb{Q}}
is Galois with group
G
. By contravariant functoriality of the cohomology ring with derivation
(H^{{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet\,}}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}}),\cup,\theta)
the group
G
would act on
H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})
and in particular on
V_{k}
. By the following argument kindly pointed out to me by Baptiste Morin, it is natural to assume that
G
acts trivially on
H^{2}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})\cong{\mathbb{C}}
, so that the pairings
2.6
and in particular (
2.9
) would be
G
-invariant. Namely one may expect a natural continuous
G
-equivariant homomorphism from the Arakelov Chow group:

cl:CH^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}})\longrightarrow H^{2}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})\;.

(1)

The map
cl
would send the compact kernel of the Arakelov degree map to zero since
{\mathbb{C}}
has no non-trivial compact subgroups. Hence
cl
would factor over the Arakelov degree map and this would lead to a copy of
{\mathbb{R}}
with trivial
G
-action inside of
H^{2}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})
. Hence
G
would act trivially on the entire group.

For an equivalence class
\pi
of irreducible representations of
G
over
{\mathbb{C}}
let
e_{\pi}\in{\mathbb{C}}[G]^{\mathrm{opp}}=\mathrm{End}\,_{G}{\mathbb{C}}[G]
be the corresponding idempotent projecting to the
\pi
-isotypical component of
{\mathbb{C}}[G]
. For any complex vector space
H
on which
G
acts,
e_{\pi}
defines a projector and we set
H(\pi)=e_{\pi}H
. For a realization
V_{\pi}
of
\pi
consider the
G
-equivariant isomorphism:

\mathrm{Hom}_{G}(V_{\pi},H)\otimes V_{\pi}\xrightarrow{\sim}H(\pi)\quad\text{with}\;f\otimes v\mapsto f(v)\;.

Since
\theta
commutes with the
G
-action, it respects both
H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})(\pi)
and the decomposition

H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})=\bigoplus_{\pi}H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})(\pi)\;.

Consider the
G
-isomorphism:

\mathrm{Hom}_{G}(V_{\pi},H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}}))\otimes V_{\pi}\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})(\pi)\;.

It is compatible with
\theta
if
\theta
acts on the left as
\theta(f\otimes v)=(\theta\mbox{\scriptsize$\,\circ\,$}f)\otimes v
. In accordance with the formula

\zeta_{k}(s)=\prod_{\pi}L({\mathbb{C}}[G](\pi),s)=\prod_{\pi}L(V_{\pi},s)^{\dim V_{\pi}}

we expect:

2.10
.

The eigenvalues
\alpha
of
\theta
on
\mathrm{Hom}_{G}(V_{\pi},H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}}))
counted with their algebraic multiplicities are the non-trivial zeroes of the Artin
L
-function
L(V_{\pi},s)
counted with multiplicity.

A corresponding assertion in the function field case is true, c.f.
[
10
, § 9]
.

In particular, we would get

2.11
.

\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}\mathrm{Hom}_{G}(V_{\pi},H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})^{\theta\sim\alpha})=\mathrm{ord}_{s=\alpha}L(V_{\pi},s)\;.

For
\alpha=1/2
this amounts to the formula

2.12
.

\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}\mathrm{Hom}_{G}(V_{\pi},V_{k})=\mathrm{ord}_{s=1/2}L(V_{\pi},s)\;.

Theorem 2.13
.

For a number field
k
there is no functorial real valued cohomology theory
H^{{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet\,}}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{R}})
with endomorphism
\theta_{{\mathbb{R}}}
such that
H^{{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet\,}}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}}):=H^{{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet\,}}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{R}})\otimes_{{\mathbb{R}}}{\mathbb{C}}
with
\theta:=\theta_{{\mathbb{R}}}\otimes\mathrm{id}
satisfies property
2.12
.

Proof.

Let
k
be a finite Galois extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
whose Galois group
G
has an irreducible symplectic representation
V_{\pi}
with root number
W(\pi)=-1
. For example, there exist quaternion fields i.e. normal extensions of
{\mathbb{Q}}
with Galois group the quaternion group
H_{8}
with
8
elements whose Artin
L
-function for the unique non-abelian representation (it is symplectic) has root number
-1
. See
[
12
]
for examples of such fields. From the functional equation of
L(V_{\pi},s)
it follows that
\mathrm{ord}_{s=1/2}L(V_{\pi},s)\geq 1
is odd. By property
2.12
the representation
V_{\pi}
occurs with odd multiplicity
\mathrm{Hom}_{G}(V_{\pi},V_{k})
in
V_{k}
. If a theory
H^{{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet\,}}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{R}})
as in the theorem exists set

V^{{\mathbb{R}}}_{k}=H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{R}})^{\theta\sim\frac{1}{2}}\;.

Then
V^{{\mathbb{R}}}_{k}
would be a
G
-module with
V_{k}=V^{{\mathbb{R}}}_{k}\otimes_{{\mathbb{R}}}{\mathbb{C}}
. and we could write
V^{{\mathbb{R}}}_{k}
as a sum of irreducible representations
W
of
G
over
{\mathbb{R}}
. According to
[
17
, 13.2]
there are three possibilities for
W
. If
W
has the form
W=\mathrm{Res}V
for an irreducible non-self dual irreducible representation
V
over
{\mathbb{C}}
, then we would have
W\otimes{\mathbb{C}}=V\oplus\overline{V}
and since both
V
and
\overline{V}
are not self-dual they cannot be symplectic. If
W
is orthogonal then
W\otimes{\mathbb{C}}
is an irreducible orthogonal
{\mathbb{C}}
-representation and hence not symplectic. If
W
is symplectic i.e.
W=\mathrm{Res}V
for a symplectic irreducible representation
V
over
{\mathbb{C}}
then
W\otimes{\mathbb{C}}=V\oplus V
. It follows that the symplectic representation
V_{\pi}
occurs with even multiplicity in
V_{k}
. This is a contradiction.
∎

I would like to thank one of the referees for suggesting this simplification of my original argument.

There is thus no real valued “Weil-”cohomology for arithmetic curves. On the other hand, the operator
\ast
in
2.7
would give a canonical quaternionic structure on
H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})
and hence we could view
(H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}}),\ast)
as an object of
K_{{\mathbb{R}}}
, equal to its degree
1
part. The groups
H^{0}
and
H^{2}
being isomorphic to
{\mathbb{C}}
by
2.2
and
2.4
must be equal to their degree zero parts if they actually take values in
K_{{\mathbb{R}}}
. In other words, they must have natural real structures. For
H^{2}
the real structure would be the image of the cycle class map (
1
) in Morin’s heuristic argument above. We noted that
cl
would have to factorize over the Arakelov degree map onto
{\mathbb{R}}
.

Hence, a “Weil-”cohomology theory with values in
K_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is a possibility for arithmetic curves.

In
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
we discussed analogies between the leafwise (or tangential) cohomology of certain foliated dynamical systems and cohomologies such as the hoped for
H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})
. The operator
\theta
corresponds to the infinitesimal generator of the induced
{\mathbb{R}}
-action by the flow on leafwise cohomology. Complex leafwise cohomology always has the real structure given by the cohomology of the real forms. Since such a real structure cannot exist on the cohomologies
H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})
as we have seen, it follows that the analogy is too simple. There has to be a twisted version of leafwise cohomology with complex coefficients that does not have a natural real structure. This can become relevant since in
[
9
]
we did construct foliated dynamical systems for arithmetic schemes which have some (but not all) the expected properties.

We will now state the expected properties of the spaces
V_{k}=H^{1}(\overline{\mathrm{spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_{k}},{\mathcal{C}})^{\theta\sim 1/2}
and suggest the problem to find a direct number theoretical construction of the
V_{k}
. This refines the discussion in
[
3
, § 7]
. We need the
following conjecture which is due to Serre (unpublished). Sarnak and Rubinstein also arrived at this conjecture in a wider context on p. 195 of
[
14
]
. Previously Chowla had conjectured that the Dirichlet
L
-series of quadratic characters do not vanish at
s=1/2
.

Vanishing Conjecture

For an irreducible complex representation of the Galois group of
{\mathbb{Q}}
the corresponding Artin
L
-function has a zero at
s=1/2
if and only if the representation is symplectic with root number
-1
. In this case the order of vanishing is one.

If the root number is
-1
, the functional equation implies that the Artin
L
-function vanishes at
s=1/2
. For orthogonal irreducible representations it is known that the root number is
+1
by
[
10
]
. Hence there is no a priori reason why their
L
-functions should vanish at
s=1/2
. For certain quaternion fields the vanishing conjecture has been verified numerically in
[
12
]
.

If
k/{\mathbb{Q}}
is Galois, it follows from
2.12
and the vanishing conjecture that precisely the symplectic
\pi
’s with
W(\pi)=-1
among the irreducible representations of
G
are realized in the
G
-module
V_{k}
and each of them with multiplicity one. Thus, as
{\mathbb{C}}[G]
-modules we should have

2.14
.

V_{k}=\bigoplus_{\pi\,\text{\rm sympl}\atop W(\pi)=-1}V_{k}(\pi)

and

V_{k}(\pi)\cong V_{\pi}
.

Since the
V_{k}(\pi)
are irreducible self dual and pairwise non-isomorphic, it would follow that the cup-product on
V_{k}
gives the (up to scalar) unique
G
-invariant symplectic pairing on
V_{k}(\pi)
.

All together we get the following suggestion:

2.15
.

There should be a covariant functor
k\mapsto(V_{k},\cup)
from the category of number fields
k
to the category of finite dimensional complex vector spaces
V_{k}
with a non-degenerate alternating form
\cup:V_{k}\times V_{k}\to{\mathbb{C}}
. If
k
is Galois over
{\mathbb{Q}}
with group
G
, the
{\mathbb{C}}[G]
-module structure on
V_{k}
resulting from functoriality should be the one described in
2.14
. In particular, we should have

\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}V_{k}=\sum_{\pi\,\text{sympl}\atop W(\pi)=-1}\dim V_{\pi}\;.

Moreover, we expect the symplectic vector spaces
(V_{k},\cup)
to be equipped with an antilinear isomorphism
\ast:V_{k}\to V_{k}
with
\ast^{2}=-1
such that we obtain a scalar product by setting

\langle v,w\rangle=v\cup\ast w\quad\text{for}\;v,w\in V_{k}\;.

Like height pairings, the symplectic form
\cup
should be a sum of local contributions.

In case a natural candidate for
V_{k}
can be found, one may also expect a formula for the leading coefficient of
\zeta_{k}(s)
at
s=1/2
in the style of the Birch Swinnerton-Dyer or Lichtenbaum Weil-étale conjectures. From the point of view of the Beilinson conjectures,
V_{k}
would be the
1/2
-eigenspace of the Adams operators on
K_{0}(k)
, or the
CH^{1/2}
group of
\mathrm{spec}\,k
or the group of extensions
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}({\mathbb{C}}(0),{\mathbb{C}}(1/2))
in some
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear category of “motives” over
k
where a
1/2
-twist of the Tate motive exists. Classically all these objects do not make sense in the number field case. In the function field case the
H^{1}
of a supersingular elliptic curve over a finite field can be used as a kind of
1/2
-Tate twist, c.f.
[
13
]
. In the Galois module theory of rings of integers in tamely ramified extensions
k/{\mathbb{Q}}
, symplectic representations with root number
-1
play a vital role. Also, exponential motives come to mind because they allow non-integral twists.

KIAS-P20068 AdS 3 gravity and the complex SYK models
Pankaj Chaturvedi, Ioannis Papadimitriou, Wei Song, Boyang Yu

Abstract
We provide a non-conformal generalization of the Compère-Song-Strominger (CSS) boundary conditions for AdS 3 gravity that breaks the u ^ ​ ( 1 ) \widehat{u}(1) Kac-Moody-Virasoro symmetry to two u ⁡ ( 1 ) u(1) s. The holographic dual specified by the new boundary conditions can be understood as an irrelevant deformation of a warped conformal field theory (WCFT). Upon consistent reduction to two dimensions, AdS 3 gravity results in a deformed Jackiw-Teitelboim dilaton gravity model coupled to a Maxwell field. We show that near extremality the boundary conditions inherited from generalized CSS boundary conditions in three dimensions give rise to an effective action exhibiting the same symmetry breaking pattern as the complex Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev models. Besides the Schwarzian term reflecting the breaking of conformal symmetry, the effective action contains an additional term that captures the breaking of the u ^ ​ ( 1 ) \widehat{u}(1) Kac-Moody symmetry.

3D gravity and generalized CSS boundary conditions

In this section we discus aspects of three dimensional (3D) Einstein gravity with a negative cosmological constant. In particular, we describe a framework within which one can classify all possible solutions and boundary conditions. We focus especially on the subspace of solutions corresponding to the BTZ black hole with Compère, Song and Strominger (CSS) boundary conditions, first discussed in

2.2
Unconstrained phase space and the variational principle

A well posed variational principle on infinite volume manifolds requires further boundary terms, besides the Gibbons-Hawking term. For asymptotically locally AdS manifolds, the additional terms ensure that the variational problem is formulated in terms of boundary conformal equivalence classes and coincide with the boundary counterterms that render the on-shell action finite

\delta I^{\rm ren}_{\rm 3D}=\int_{{\cal M}}d^{3}x\;\sqrt{-g}\,(e.o.m.)\,\delta g_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial{\cal M}}d^{2}x\;\sqrt{-g\phantom{}_{(0)}\phantom{}}\;{\cal T}_{ij}\;\delta g_{(0)}^{ij}.

2.2.2
Deformed Warped CFT phase space

As we have seen, the GWCFT space of solutions does not admit a non-degenerate symplectic form. We will see in the next section that it is also insufficient for describing the most general solutions of the 2D dilaton gravity obtained by a circle reduction of AdS

When

can interpolate between a WCFT and a regular CFT
2

Ricci scalar

The Ricci scalar of the metric (

Stress tensor

Similar to the discussion for the WCFT phase space, the constraints (

\displaystyle{\cal T}_{-+}=\;-{{\mu^{-}}}{{\overline{{\cal O}}}_{-}}-{{\mu^{+}}}{{\overline{{\cal O}}}_{+}}+\frac{c}{24\pi}\Big(\frac{1+3{{\mu^{+}}}{{\mu^{-}}}}{1-{{\mu^{+}}}{{\mu^{-}}}}\Big)\partial_{+}\mathscr{F},

\displaystyle{{\overline{{\cal O}}}_{-}}\equiv\;-{\cal L}_{-}(z^{-})+\frac{c}{48\pi}\mathscr{F}^{2},\qquad{{\overline{{\cal O}}}_{+}}\equiv\;-(\mu^{+})^{-2}\Big({\cal L}_{+}(z^{+})-\frac{c}{48\pi}\big(\mathscr{F}^{2}-2{{\mu^{+}}}\partial_{+}\mathscr{F}\big)\Big)\,,

{\cal L}_{-}

The physical meaning and significance of the variables

Note that in the limit

As we discuss in section 3, this condition is the near extremality condition for black holes and is also required for a running dilaton solution to flow to a constant dilaton solution in the two dimensional Einstein-Maxwell-Dilaton gravity. We refer to the phase space parameterized by the boundary metric (

2.2.3
Variational principle on the DWCFT phase space

The DWCFT phase space allows us to formulate the variational principle in terms of unconstrained variables. From the solution (
2.2.2
), we note that the following relations hold:

\displaystyle{\cal T}_{--}-{{\mu^{+}}}{\cal T}_{+-}=\;-(1-{{\mu^{-}}}{{\mu^{+}}}){{\overline{{\cal O}}}_{-}}+\frac{c}{12\pi}{{\mu^{+}}}\partial_{+}\mathscr{F},

\displaystyle{\cal T}_{++}-{{\mu^{-}}}{\cal T}_{+-}=\;-(1-{{\mu^{-}}}{{\mu^{+}}}){{\overline{{\cal O}}}_{+}}+\frac{c}{12\pi}{{\mu^{-}}}\partial_{+}\mathscr{F}.

Inserting these, together with the metric (
2.25

\delta I^{\rm ren}_{\rm 3D}=-\int_{\partial{\cal M}}d^{2}x\,\big({\cal L}_{-}\delta{{\mu^{-}}}+(\mu^{+})^{-2}{\cal L}_{+}\delta\mu^{+}\big)+\delta I{}_{\rm local},

(2.33)

where

is the local expression

(2.34)

This local part of the renormalized on-shell action is directly related with the conformal anomaly of the 2D CFT at the boundary of AdS
3

[
35
]

Another important observation regarding

\Omega=-\int_{\partial{\cal M}}d^{2}x\,\big(\delta{\cal L}_{-}\wedge\delta{{\mu^{-}}}+(\mu^{+})^{-2}\delta{\cal L}_{+}\wedge\delta{{\mu^{+}}}\big).

(2.35)

Since

is local,

is an exact phase space one-form and drops out of the symplectic form.

Although
I{}_{\rm local}
does not contribute to the symplectic form, it affects the variational principle. A general variation of

takes the from

\delta I{}_{\rm local}=\frac{c}{48\pi}\int d^{2}x\,\big(\mathscr{F}^{2}\delta{{\mu^{-}}}+(\mu^{+})^{-2}(\mathscr{F}^{2}-2{{\mu^{+}}}\partial_{+}\mathscr{F})\delta{{\mu^{+}}}-4\partial_{+}\mathscr{F}\delta\Phi\big).

This expression is consistent with the general result that in the presence of a conformal anomaly (corresponding to
{{\mu^{+}}}\neq 0
or
\mathscr{F}\neq 0

[
52
]

{{\mu^{+}}}\neq 0

in order to obtain a well posed variational problem.

Except for the term proportional to

can be absorbed by a redefinition of the canonical variables. In particular, the variational principle (

\delta I^{\rm ren}_{\rm 3D}=\int_{\partial{\cal M}}d^{2}x\,\Big({{\overline{{\cal O}}}_{-}}\delta{{\mu^{-}}}+{{\overline{{\cal O}}}_{+}}\delta{{\mu^{+}}}-\frac{c}{12\pi}\partial_{+}\mathscr{F}\delta\Phi\Big),

(2.37)

where
{{\overline{{\cal O}}}_{-}}
and
{{\overline{{\cal O}}}_{+}}
are the variables introduced in (

In terms of these variables, the symplectic form (

\Omega=\int_{\partial{\cal M}}d^{2}x\,\big(\delta{{\overline{{\cal O}}}_{-}}\wedge\delta{{\mu^{-}}}+\delta{{\overline{{\cal O}}}_{+}}\wedge\delta{{\mu^{+}}}\big),

(2.38)

since the difference between the original and new canonical variables is due to
I{}_{\rm local}
and so does not contribute. We have therefore arrived at a variational principle in terms of the unconstrained symplectic variables
({{\mu^{-}}},{{\overline{{\cal O}}}_{-}})
and
({{\mu^{+}}},{{\overline{{\cal O}}}_{+}})
that parameterize a well defined phase space with a non-degenerate symplectic form. This enables us to impose alternative boundary conditions for this class of solutions, as we discuss in the next subsection.

2.2.4
Spectral flow

The variational principle can be modified through a change of variables that preserves the symplectic form (
2.38
). Generic canonical transformations correspond to the addition of a further finite boundary term to the renormalized action (i.e. to a shift of the symplectic potential one-form by an exact form) and can be used in order to classify the admissible boundary conditions. However, there exists a class of canonical transformations that preserve not only the symplectic form, but also the variational principle, i.e the symplectic potential. In other words, there exist canonical transformations that whose corresponding boundary term is identically zero.

We start with the canonical variables

{\overline{{\cal J}}}^{-}\equiv{{\mu^{-}}},\qquad{{\overline{{\cal O}}}_{-}},\qquad{\overline{{\cal J}}}^{+}\equiv{{\mu^{+}}},\qquad{{\overline{{\cal O}}}_{+}},

A canonical transformation

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{j}\frac{\partial{\cal J}^{j}}{\partial{\overline{{\cal J}}}^{i}}=\overline{{\cal O}}_{i},\quad{{{\cal O}}_{j}}\frac{\partial{\cal J}^{j}}{\partial{{\overline{{\cal O}}}_{i}}}=0,

take the values

In the present context, we wish to treat
\mu^{+}
as a parameter and so we consider canonical transformations that preserve

{\cal J}^{+}=\mu^{+}.

(2.41)

In this case, the relations (

is independent of

and

2.3
Generalized CSS boundary conditions and symmetry algebra

In the previous subsection we constructed a non-degenerate symplectic space of solutions of the constraints (
2.6
), and obtained a well posed variational principle in terms of unconstrained symplectic data. Moreover, we saw that there exists a class of canonical transformations that preserve the variational principle. We now discuss canonical transformations that modify the variational principle, allowing for more general boundary conditions. We will see that the boundary conditions relevant for describing the CSYK model are a generalization of the CSS boundary conditions
[

Starting with the spectrally flowed variables (
2.43

\delta I^{\rm ren}_{\rm 3D}=\int_{\partial{\cal M}}d^{2}x\,\Big({{{\cal O}}_{-}}\delta{\cal J}^{-}+{{\cal O}}_{+}\delta{\cal J}^{+}-\frac{c}{12\pi}\partial_{+}\mathscr{F}\delta\Phi\Big).

(2.55)

This can be modified by adding an arbitrary boundary term

\mathscr{K}

may contain a finite number of derivatives with respect to the boundary coordinates,

\Phi
as well. The argument given earlier for

\mathscr{K}
does not affect the symplectic form (

\{{\cal J}^{i},\,{{\cal O}}_{i}\},\,i=-,+

[
53
]

[
51
]
. However, a generic
\mathscr{K}
preserves a subset of the DWCFT phase space symmetries, if any at all.

Under the canonical transformation generated by
\mathscr{K}
, the variational principle is modified as

\displaystyle\delta I^{\prime\rm ren}_{\rm 3D}=

\displaystyle\;\int_{\partial{\cal M}}d^{2}x\,({{\cal P}_{-}}\delta{{\cal Q}^{-}}+{{\cal P}_{+}}\delta{{\cal Q}^{+}})=\int_{\partial{\cal M}}d^{2}x\,\Big[\Big(\frac{\partial\mathscr{K}}{\partial{\cal J}^{i}}+{{{\cal O}}_{i}}\Big)\delta{\cal J}^{i}+\frac{\partial\mathscr{K}}{\partial{{{\cal O}}_{i}}}\delta{{{\cal O}}_{i}}\Big],

(2.57)

where the new symplectic variables,
({{\cal Q}^{i}},{{\cal P}_{i}})

({\cal J}^{i},{{{\cal O}}_{i}})
, as

{{\cal P}_{j}}\frac{\partial{{\cal Q}^{j}}}{\partial{\cal J}^{i}}=\frac{\partial\mathscr{K}}{\partial{\cal J}^{i}}+{{{\cal O}}_{i}},\qquad{{\cal P}_{j}}\frac{\partial{{\cal Q}^{j}}}{\partial{{{\cal O}}_{i}}}=\frac{\partial\mathscr{K}}{\partial{{{\cal O}}_{i}}}.

(2.58)

The spectral flow equations (
2.40

\mathscr{K}=0
.

Given arbitrary functions
{{\cal Q}^{i}}({\cal J}^{j},{{\cal O}}_{j})
and
{{\cal P}^{i}}({\cal J}^{j},{{\cal O}}_{j})

these relations uniquely determine the boundary term,
\mathscr{K}({\cal J}^{i},{{{\cal O}}_{i}})
, provided it exists. Existence of
\mathscr{K}
is equivalent to preserving the symplectic form and so, for any choice of new variables that preserve the symplectic form, there exists a unique boundary term
\mathscr{K}
. However, a given boundary term,
\mathscr{K}({\cal J}^{i},{{{\cal O}}_{i}})

{{\cal Q}^{i}}({\cal J}^{j},{{{\cal O}}_{j}})
and
{{\cal P}_{i}}({\cal J}^{j},{{{\cal O}}_{j}})
due to the existence of canonical transformations that preserve the variational principle. Nevertheless, additional conditions may be imposed by the requirement that the deformed theory preserves certain symmetries or that it has a well defined holographic dual. In the case of scalar fields, for example, the form of the new canonical variables is dictated by the requirement that the canonical transformation corresponds to a multitrace deformation of the dual field theory in the large-

[

2.3.2

A well defined variational principle on a non-degenerate symplectic space defines a symplectic potential,

To determine the asymptotic symmetries and conserved charges, one starts by identifying the local symmetries that leave the action invariant up to anomalies, i.e. up to terms that depend locally on the generalized coordinates,
{{\cal Q}}^{a}

{\mathscr{W}}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{I}}^{I}=0.

(2.66)

As in some of the examples we will consider, the parameters

3
2D dilaton gravity and the DWCFT phase space

In this section, we review the consistent Kaluza-Klein (KK) reduction of AdS
3
gravity to two dimensions, as well as the space of solutions of the resulting 2D dilaton gravity
[
35
]
. Moreover, we demonstrate that any 2D solution can be embedded in the DWCFT phase space of 3D gravity. The discussion in this section is independent of any boundary conditions.

3.1
Consistent reduction to 2D and the space of solutions

The starting point for the KK reduction of AdS
3
gravity is the ansatz

ds^{2}_{\rm 3D}=ds^{2}_{\rm 2D}+e^{-2\psi}(dw+A)^{2},

(3.1)

where
\psi
and
A=A_{\mu}dx^{\mu}
are respectively a scalar and a U(1) gauge field on the two dimensional base manifold with metric
g_{\mu\nu}
. The coordinate
w\sim w+2\pi R_{w}
parameterizes the KK circle. Inserting this ansatz in the AdS
3
gravity action (
2.1
) results in the Einstein-Maxwell-Dilaton theory
[
65
,
62
,
35
]

I_{\rm 2D}=\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}_{\rm 2D}}\Big[\int d^{2}x\sqrt{-g}\;e^{-\psi}\Big(R[g]+\frac{2}{\ell^{2}}-\frac{1}{4}e^{-2\psi}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\Big)+2\int dt\sqrt{-\gamma}\;e^{-\psi}K\Big]\,,

(3.2)

where
\kappa^{2}_{\rm 2D}=\kappa^{2}_{\rm 3D}/2\pi R_{w}={6\ell\over cR_{w}}
,
K
is the trace of the extrinsic curvature of the induced metric,
\gamma
, on the (regulated) one dimensional boundary, and
F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}
is the field strength of
A_{\mu}
. Crucially, the 2D theory (
3.2
) is a consistent truncation of AdS
3
gravity
[
35
]
. Namely, the equations of motion following from (
3.2
) imply those obtained from (
2.1
), which means that any solution of the 2D theory uplifts to a solution of AdS
3
gravity.

The field equations following from the 2D action (
3.2
) can be solved exactly. Starting with the Maxwell equation

\nabla_{\mu}(e^{-3\psi}F^{\mu\nu})=0\,,

(3.3)

its general solution can be expressed covariantly as

F_{\mu\nu}=2Qe^{3\psi}\epsilon_{\mu\nu},\qquad F^{2}=-8Q^{2}e^{6\psi}\,,

(3.4)

where the constant
Q
is the 2D electric charge and
\epsilon_{\mu\nu}
is the Levi-Civita tensor in two dimensions.

Using (
3.4
) and adopting (without loss of generality) the FG gauge

ds^{2}_{\rm 2D}=d\rho^{2}+\gamma_{tt}(\rho,t)dt^{2},\qquad A=A_{t}(\rho,t)dt,

(3.5)

the field equations take the form

\displaystyle(\partial^{2}_{\rho}-\ell^{-2})e^{-\psi}+Q^{2}e^{3\psi}=0\,,

\displaystyle\big(\partial^{2}_{\rho}-\ell^{-2}-3Q^{2}e^{4\psi}\big)\sqrt{-\gamma}=0\,,

\displaystyle\big(\square_{t}+K\partial_{\rho}-\ell^{-2}\big)e^{-\psi}+Q^{2}e^{3\psi}=0\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}\big(\partial_{t}e^{-\psi}/\sqrt{-\gamma}\big)=0\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}A_{t}=-2Qe^{3\psi}\sqrt{-\gamma}\,,

(3.6)

where
\sqrt{-\gamma}=\sqrt{-\gamma_{tt}}
,
K=\partial_{\rho}\log\sqrt{-\gamma}
is the extrinsic curvature of
\gamma_{tt}
,
\square_{t}
is the covariant Laplacian with respect to
\gamma_{tt}
, and in the last equation we have chosen the orientation of the two dimensional base such that
\epsilon_{t\rho}=\sqrt{-\gamma}
.

The general solution of the system of equations (
3.1
) can be obtained analytically
[
35
]
. The solution space consists of two distinct branches, depending on whether the dilaton,
\psi
, is constant or not. However, running dilaton solutions that satisfy a certain extremality condition flow to constant dilaton solutions in the IR.

Running dilaton solutions

Provided the dilaton,
\psi
, is not a constant, the general solution of the system of equations (
3.1
) takes the form

\displaystyle e^{-\psi}=

\displaystyle\;\beta(t)e^{\rho/\ell}\sqrt{\Big(1+\frac{m-\beta^{\prime 2}(t)/\alpha^{2}(t)}{4\beta^{2}(t)}\ell^{2}e^{-2\rho/\ell}\Big)^{2}-\frac{Q^{2}\ell^{2}}{4\beta^{4}(t)}e^{-4\rho/\ell}}\,,

\displaystyle\sqrt{-\gamma}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{\alpha(t)}{\beta^{\prime}(t)}\partial_{t}e^{-\psi}\,,

\displaystyle A_{t}=

\displaystyle\;\mu(t)+\frac{\alpha(t)}{2\beta^{\prime}(t)}\partial_{t}\log\Big(\frac{4\ell^{-2}e^{2\rho/\ell}\beta^{2}(t)+m-\beta^{\prime 2}(t)/\alpha^{2}(t)-2Q/\ell}{4\ell^{-2}e^{2\rho/\ell}\beta^{2}(t)+m-\beta^{\prime 2}(t)/\alpha^{2}(t)+2Q/\ell}\Big)\,,

(3.7)

where primes denote a derivative with respect to
t
,
\alpha(t),\beta(t)
and
\mu(t)
are arbitrary functions, and
m
is an integration constant. Notice that the asymptotic behavior of the solution as
\rho\to\infty
is determined by the functions
\alpha(t),\beta(t)
and
\mu(t)
, namely

\gamma_{tt}\sim-\alpha^{2}e^{2\rho/\ell}+{\cal O}(1),\qquad e^{-\psi}\sim\beta e^{\rho/\ell}+{\cal O}(1),\qquad A_{t}=\mu+{\cal O}(e^{-2\rho/\ell})\,.

(3.8)

The asymptotic behavior of
e^{-\psi}
and
\sqrt{-\gamma}\sim\alpha\,e^{\rho/\ell}
require that both
\alpha
and
\beta
are positive definite. As discussed in
[
35
]
, for certain ranges of the parameters
m
and
Q
, these solutions correspond extremal or non-extremal 2D black holes and can be uplifted to the BTZ black hole.

Constant dilaton solutions

The system (
3.1
) admits a second branch of solutions where the dilaton is constant and determined by the electric charge
[
66
,
62
,
5
]
. These solutions take the form

e^{-2\psi}=\ell|Q|,\quad\sqrt{-\gamma}={\widetilde{\alpha}}(t)e^{2\rho/\ell}+\widetilde{\beta}(t)e^{-2\rho/\ell},\quad A_{t}=\widetilde{\mu}(t)-\ell Qe^{3\psi}\big({\widetilde{\alpha}}(t)e^{2\rho/\ell}-\widetilde{\beta}(t)e^{-2\rho/\ell}\big),

(3.9)

where
\widetilde{\alpha}(t)>0
,
\widetilde{\beta}(t)
and
\widetilde{\mu}(t)
are again arbitrary functions. In this branch, the metric is locally AdS
2
with radius
\ell_{\rm 2D}=\ell/2
. In particular, in the absence of a horizon, the functions
\widetilde{\alpha}(t)
and
\widetilde{\beta}(t)
correspond to 1D metrics on the two boundaries of global AdS
2
. More generally, the constant dilaton solutions correspond to the Very Near Horizon region of near extremal black holes
[
67
]
and can also be uplifted to the BTZ black hole.

3.2
Extremality condition and interpolating flow

The fact that the running dilaton solutions are (in a suitable Weyl frame) asymptotically AdS
2
with radius
\ell
, while the constant dilaton ones are asymptotically AdS
2
with radius
\ell_{\rm 2D}=\ell/2
suggests that there should exist a holographic RG flow that interpolates between the former in the UV and the latter in the IR. As we now review, this is indeed the case, provided the parameters of the two solution branches satisfy certain (near) extremality conditions.

It is instructive to discuss this interpolating flow from two complementary viewpoints. Firstly, starting from the running dilaton solutions in the UV, we show that such solutions flow to the constant dilaton ones in the IR, provided the extremality conditions are fulfilled. In the second approach, we start from the constant dilaton solutions in the IR and consider linear perturbations around them, which take us into the running dilaton class of solutions. This procedure corresponds to conformal perturbation theory with an irrelevant deformation around the IR fixed point.

UV to IR

Starting with the running dilaton solutions (
3.1
), we are interested in extremal black holes which allow for an RG flow description from the UV at the asymptotic infinity
\rho\to\infty
to the IR near the horizon. By examining the zeros of
\gamma_{tt}
we observe that the extremality condition is
m-2|Q|/\ell=\beta^{\prime 2}/\alpha^{2}
, in which case the horizon is located at
\rho\to-\infty
. Requiring trivial holonomy at the horizon imposes the additional condition
\mu=-{\rm sgn}(Q)\alpha/\beta
. The solution (
3.1
) then becomes

e^{-\psi}=\sqrt{\ell|Q|+\beta^{2}(t)e^{2\rho/\ell}},\quad\sqrt{-\gamma}=\frac{\alpha(t)\beta(t)e^{2\rho/\ell}}{\sqrt{\ell|Q|+\beta^{2}(t)e^{2\rho/\ell}}},\quad A_{t}=-\frac{{\rm sgn}(Q)\alpha(t)\beta(t)e^{2\rho/\ell}}{\ell|Q|+\beta^{2}(t)e^{2\rho/\ell}}.

(3.10)

The asymptotic UV behavior of this solution as
\rho\to+\infty
is still given by (
3.8
). However, since
\beta^{2}
enters always with a factor of
e^{2\rho/\ell}
, the IR expansion as
\rho\to-\infty
is equivalent to the parameter limit
\beta\to 0
, keeping
\alpha\beta
fixed, which also sets
m=2|Q|/\ell
and
|\mu|\to\infty
.
As was shown explicitly in
[
35
]
,
the leading order terms in the near horizon expansion, which is equivalent to small
\beta
expansion, correspond to a constant dilaton solution of the form (
3.9
) with
\widetilde{\alpha}=\alpha\beta/\sqrt{\ell|Q|}
,
\widetilde{\beta}=0
,
\widetilde{\mu}=0
.

In order to get a constant dilaton solution with non-zero
\widetilde{\beta}
and
\widetilde{\mu}
in the IR, we need to generalize this limit to near extremal running dilaton solutions. In particular, setting
\mu=-{\rm sgn}(Q)\alpha/\beta+\widetilde{\mu}(t)
and
m-\beta^{\prime 2}/\alpha^{2}=2|Q|/\ell+8\beta^{2}\delta(t)/\ell^{2}
for some
\widetilde{\mu}(t)
and small
\delta(t)\geqslant 0
, the limit
\beta\to 0
of the solutions (
3.1
), while keeping
\alpha\beta
fixed and
0\leqslant\delta(t)e^{-2\rho/\ell}\approx\beta^{2}(t)e^{2\rho/\ell}
, takes the form

\displaystyle e^{-\psi}=

\displaystyle\;\sqrt{\ell|Q|}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2\sqrt{\ell|Q|}}e^{2\rho/\ell}+\sqrt{\ell|Q|}e^{-2\rho/\ell}\delta+{\cal O}\big(\beta^{4}e^{4\rho/\ell}\big)\,,

\displaystyle\sqrt{-\gamma}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{\alpha\beta}{\sqrt{\ell|Q|}}e^{2\rho/\ell}+\sqrt{\ell|Q|}e^{-2\rho/\ell}\frac{\alpha\delta^{\prime}}{\beta^{\prime}}+{\cal O}\big(\beta^{2}e^{4\rho/\ell}\big)\,,

\displaystyle A_{t}=

\displaystyle\;\widetilde{\mu}-\frac{\alpha\beta}{\ell Q}e^{2\rho/\ell}+{\rm sgn}(Q)\frac{\alpha\delta^{\prime}}{\beta^{\prime}}e^{-2\rho/\ell}+{\cal O}\big(\beta^{2}e^{4\rho/\ell}\big)\,.

(3.11)

Up to
{\cal O}\big(\beta^{2}e^{4\rho/\ell}\big)
terms, this is a constant dilaton solution of the form (
3.9
) with parameters

\boxed{\widetilde{\alpha}=\frac{\alpha\beta}{\sqrt{\ell|Q|}},\qquad\widetilde{\beta}=\sqrt{\ell|Q|}\frac{\alpha\delta^{\prime}}{\beta^{\prime}},\qquad\widetilde{\mu}=\mu+{\rm sgn}(Q)\frac{\alpha}{\beta},}

(3.12)

where

\delta(t)=\frac{\ell^{2}}{8\beta^{2}}\big(m-2|Q|/\ell-\beta^{\prime 2}/\alpha^{2}\big),

(3.13)

is small and positive. In particular, we identify
\widetilde{\beta}
or
\delta
with the near extremality parameter. It is worth mentioning that this RG flow can be uplifted to three dimensional gravity using the KK ansatz (
3.1
)-(
3.5
). In particular, the near extremal running dilaton solutions uplift to the near extremal BTZ black hole, with the limit
\rho\to-\infty
corresponding again to the near horizon limit.

It is important to realize that the relations (
3.12
) are highly non-trivial and describe a renormalization group flow in the dual quantum mechanics. It is this flow that will enable us to model the CSYK model in the subsequent sections. Notice that (
3.12
) relate the UV parameters of the running dilaton solutions to the IR variables of the constant dilaton ones. Such an explicit map is very rarely available in holographic dualities. Here it is made possible by the fact that the running dilaton solutions are known exactly. The map between UV and IR variables allows us to describe the RG flow in parameter space, i.e. directly in the dual quantum mechanics, without reference to the radial coordinate. As we will see below, this RG flow uplifts directly to the
\mu^{+}\to 0
limit of the DWCFT phase space of 3D gravity solutions.

IR to UV

The approximate solution (
3.2
) can be alternatively obtained by perturbation theory around the constant dilaton solutions. Although such an approach is redundant when the running dilaton solutions are known, it has the advantage that it can be used in cases where the running dilaton solutions are not known. Such an example arises in the 2D dilaton theory obtained from the consistent KK reduction of five dimensional AdS gravity
[
38
]
.

Starting with the constant dilaton solutions, we consider the linear perturbations

e^{-\psi}=e^{-\psi_{0}}+(e^{-\psi})^{(1)},\qquad\sqrt{-\gamma}=(\sqrt{-\gamma})^{(0)}+(\sqrt{-\gamma})^{(1)},\qquad A_{t}=A_{t}^{(0)}+A_{t}^{(1)},

(3.14)

where

\displaystyle(\partial^{2}_{\rho}-4\ell^{-2})(e^{-\psi})^{(1)}=0\,,

\displaystyle(\partial^{2}_{\rho}-4\ell^{-2})(\sqrt{-\gamma})^{(1)}+12Q^{2}e^{5\psi_{0}}(e^{-\psi})^{(1)}(\sqrt{-\gamma})^{(0)}=0\,,

\displaystyle\big(\square^{(0)}_{t}+K^{(0)}\partial_{\rho}-4\ell^{-2}\big)(e^{-\psi})^{(1)}=0\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}A_{t}^{(1)}=-2Q\big(e^{3\psi_{0}}(\sqrt{-\gamma})^{(1)}-3e^{4\psi_{0}}(\sqrt{-\gamma})^{(0)}(e^{-\psi})^{(1)}\big)\,.

(3.15)

and is given by

\displaystyle(e^{-\psi})^{(1)}=

\displaystyle\;\nu(t)e^{2\rho/\ell}-\frac{\ell^{2}}{8\widetilde{\alpha}}\Big(\Big({\nu^{\prime}\over\widetilde{\alpha}}\Big)^{\prime}+{8\widetilde{\beta}\nu\over\ell^{2}}\Big)e^{-2\rho/\ell},

\displaystyle(\sqrt{-\gamma})^{(1)}=

\displaystyle\;-e^{\psi_{0}}\Big((\sqrt{-\gamma})^{(0)}(e^{-\psi})^{(1)}+\frac{\ell^{2}}{2}\Big({\nu^{\prime}\over\widetilde{\alpha}}\Big)^{\prime}\Big),

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{2}Qe^{4\psi_{0}}\partial_{\rho}\big((\sqrt{-\gamma})^{(0)}(e^{-\psi})^{(1)}\big),

(3.16)

where
\nu(t)
satisfies the differential equation

\bigg(\Big({\nu^{\prime}\over\widetilde{\alpha}}\Big)^{2}-{2\nu\over\widetilde{\alpha}}\Big({\nu^{\prime}\over\widetilde{\alpha}}\Big)^{\prime}-{16\widetilde{\beta}\nu^{2}\over\ell^{2}\widetilde{\alpha}}\bigg)^{\prime}=0\,.

(3.17)

Given the background specified by
\widetilde{\alpha},\,\widetilde{\beta},\,\widetilde{\mu}
and
Q
, the linearized perturbation
\nu
can be obtained by solving the differential equation (
3.17
). However, since
\nu
describes the leading growth of the running dilaton, it is sometimes more convenient to treat
\nu(t)
as arbitrary and use (
3.17
) to determine
\widetilde{\beta}
through the relation

\widetilde{\beta}={\ell^{2}\over 16\nu}\bigg({\nu^{\prime 2}\over\nu\widetilde{\alpha}}-2\Big({\nu^{\prime}\over\widetilde{\alpha}}\Big)^{\prime}-{\Delta_{m}\widetilde{\alpha}\over\nu}\bigg)\,,

(3.18)

with
\Delta_{m}
an integration constant. Therefore, the IR fixed point and the perturbations around it can be described by the phase space parameters
\widetilde{\alpha},\,\widetilde{\mu},Q,\,\widetilde{\beta}
together with the solution of (
3.17
), or equivalently just
\widetilde{\alpha},\,\widetilde{\mu},Q,\Delta_{m},\,\nu
.

Comparing the solutions (
3.2
) with the IR expansion of the running dilaton solutions in (
3.2
), we identify

\nu=\frac{\beta^{2}}{2\sqrt{\ell|Q|}},\qquad\Delta_{m}=m-2Q/\ell.

(3.19)

This verifies that the perturbative analysis around the constant dilaton solutions reproduces the IR limit of the running dilaton ones. However, the identification between the IR and UV variables, i.e. the relations (
3.19
), would not be possible without knowledge of the full running dilaton solutions.

3.3
3D uplift of 2D solutions

Since the 2D dilaton gravity (

3.3.1
Embedding into the DWCFT phase space

To make contact with the DWCFT phase space in section

Recall that the DWCFT phase space is described by the variables

and

and

Running dilaton solutions

The running dilaton solutions (

Comparing this with (

\alpha^{2}=-e^{2\Phi}\mu^{+}\Big(\frac{1-\mu^{+}\mu^{-}}{2\mu^{+}}\Big)^{2},\qquad\beta^{2}=-e^{2\Phi}\mu^{+},\qquad\mu=\frac{1+\mu^{+}\mu^{-}}{2\mu^{+}}.

Notice that the uplift of the running dilaton solutions always has

Inverting these relations determines the variables
\Phi
,
{\mu^{\pm}}
in terms of
\alpha
,
\beta
and

\frac{1}{\mu^{+}}=\mu\pm\frac{\alpha}{\beta},\qquad\mu^{-}=\mu\mp\frac{\alpha}{\beta},\qquad e^{2\Phi}=-\beta^{2}\Big(\mu\pm\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\Big),

\frac{1}{\mu^{+}}=\mu-\frac{\alpha}{\beta},\qquad\mu^{-}=\mu+\frac{\alpha}{\beta},\qquad e^{2\Phi}=-\beta^{2}\Big(\mu-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\Big).

{\cal L}_{+}(z^{+})

Effective action and the complex SYK model

In section

4.1

A natural question is how one could motivate and understand the new boundary term (
2.61
) within the 2D dilaton theory. The technical answer to this question is that the boundary term (
2.61
) determines the class of consistent boundary conditions (parameterized by the spectral function
\mathscr{R}(\lambda)
) for the running dilaton solutions that flow to Neumann boundary conditions for the gauge field at the IR fixed point. Notice that these do not amount simply to Neumann boundary conditions for the gauge field in the running dilaton solutions, because the asymptotic behavior of the gauge field in the running and constant dilaton solutions is qualitatively different.

This difference makes the extrapolation of Neumann boundary conditions for the gauge field away from the IR fixed point a non-trivial problem. To leading order in the deformation
\nu
away from the IR fixed point, one could determine the correct boundary term using the variational principle obtained from holographic renormalization near the IR fixed point, which was developed in
[
35
,
38
]
. However, the fact that we have control of the UV completion in this case allows us to adopt a more powerful approach that determines the form of the boundary term throughout the RG flow.

In particular, we start from the renormalized variational principle (
B.1
) of the UV theory, which in 2D is formulated on the phase space of running dilaton solutions. As for 3D gravity, consistent boundary conditions are classified by finite boundary terms that generate canonical transformations on this phase space
[
51
]
. The procedure of determining the appropriate boundary term within the phase space of running dilaton solutions of 2D dilaton gravity mirrors the analysis of section
2
for the DWCFT phase space of 3D gravity. Using the map between 3D and 2D variables given in (
3.34
) and (
3.38
), one can follow the procedure in section
2
entirely within the phase space of 2D dilaton gravity, arriving directly at the boundary term (
2.61
).

A few remarks may help further elucidate the significance of the new boundary conditions of 2D dilaton gravity imposed by (
2.61
). Firstly, one may wonder if it is possible to express the boundary term (
2.61
) in terms of the fields of 2D dilaton gravity. It is possible to express any boundary term in terms of the canonical variables of a radial Hamiltonian formulation of the bulk dynamics
[
35
,
38
]
. However, these variables correspond to unrenormalized couplings and observables and do not have well defined transformations under the RG flow. Boundary conditions can be studied more efficiently using renormalized canonical variables, which correspond to symplectic conjugate pairs on the phase space of solutions. This is the approach we adopted here. Nevertheless, one may express the phase space variables, as well the boundary term (
2.61
), in terms of bulk fields and their conjugate momenta using the explicit form of the running dilaton solutions in (
3.1
).

Secondly, we should emphasize that the full non-linear boundary term (
2.61
) is consistent only for the specific 2D dilaton gravity theory obtained from 3D gravity, since it is intimately connected with the non-relativistic deformation of 2D CFTs discussed in section
2
. However, its perturbative form around the IR fixed point and the associated variational principle (
4.8
)-(
4.1
) are applicable to any 2D dilaton gravity with a U(1) gauge field, such as those arising from the consistent KK reduction of higher dimensional black holes
[
38
,
45
]
.

The universality of the perturbative result (
4.8
)-(
4.1
) is reflected in the fact that it does not depend on the choice of spectral function
\mathscr{R}(\lambda)
parameterizing the full boundary term (
2.61
), as long as it satisfies the two conditions (
2.42
) and (
2.86
). It is this universal perturbative result near extremality that can be compared with e.g.
[
69
]
and the more recent work
[
70
,
46
,
47
]
. Contrary to our setup, these earlier works adopt exclusively a 2D perspective to the dynamics of near extremal black holes, without reference to a possible UV completion. The fact that they obtain the same symmetry breaking pattern as that derived from the boundary term (
2.61
) in the next section indicates a possible connection between the perturbative form of the boundary conditions used here and in these earlier works. We further comment on some of these earlier works in section
4.4.1
.

4.2
Symmetries and conserved charges

The symmetries of the 2D theory can be determined from the symplectic potential which can be obtained from the variational principle (
4.8
). Under 2D PBH and residual gauge transformations

\displaystyle\xi=\widetilde{\varepsilon}(t)\partial_{t}+\sigma(t)\partial_{\rho},\qquad\delta A_{t}=\partial_{t}\big(\widetilde{\varphi}(t)+\widetilde{\varepsilon}(t)A_{t}\big),

The extremal limit

At the extremal limit,

Imposing the boundary condition (

Away from extremality

For small but non-vanishing

). For general time-dependent solutions, time reparameterizations are still a local symmetry, while the the Ward identities for the Weyl symmetry and U(1) guage symmetry are both anomalous. We are interested in the charged black hole solutions (

Thermodynamics at the saddle point

We are particularly interested in the saddle point

First of all, the temperature arising from the periodicity (

\displaystyle S_{0}

\displaystyle\equiv

In this paper we determined a non-conformal generalization of the CSS boundary conditions of AdS

Using a consistent KK reduction to two dimensions and evaluating the renormalized on-shell action on near extremal solutions subject to this new boundary condition, we have uncovered specific relations connecting AdS

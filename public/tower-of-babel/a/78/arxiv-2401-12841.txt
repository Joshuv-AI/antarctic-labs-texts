Abstract
We study the null curves and their motion in a 3 3 -dimensional flat space-time M 3 M_{3} . We show that when the motion of null curves forms two surfaces in M 3 M_{3} the integrability conditions lead to the well-known AKNS hierarchy. In this case we obtain all the geometrical quantities of the surfaces arising from the whole hierarchy but we particulary focus on the surfaces of the MKdV and KdV equations. We obtain one- and two-soliton surfaces associated to the MKdV equation and show that the Gauss and mean curvatures of these surfaces develop singularities in finite time. We show that the tetrad vectors on the curves satisfy the spin vector equation in the ferromagnetism model of Heisenberg.



Keywords.
Null curves, Integrable equations, AKNS hierarchy, Soliton surfaces, Heisenberg model

Introduction

There are two different ways of studying integrable equations and the surface theory in three dimensional spaces. One of them is to use the Lax equations of certain integrable equations to obtain the associated parametrization of two surfaces in three dimensional Euclidean or Minkowski spaces
[
1
]
-

[

3

M_{3}
and determine surfaces swept by such curves. From the integrability conditions we obtain the well-known Ablowitz-Kaup-Newell-Segur (AKNS) system
[

. Here we focus on Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) and modified Korteweg-de Vries (MKdV) reductions of the AKNS system and the corresponding two surfaces. In particular, for the MKdV equation we obtain one-soliton and two-soliton surfaces. We obtain the mean and Gauss curvatures of these surfaces and observe that they are singular for the two-soliton surfaces meaning that for some values of the constants these surfaces develop singularities in finite time. We observe that the tetrad vectors of the curves satisfy the equation of the spin vector
\vec{S}
in the Heisenberg theory of ferromagnetism and in each case the curvature and the torsion of the curves satisfy certain nonlinear evolution equations.

The layout of the paper is as follows: In Section 2 we give Serret-Frenet equations and motion of the null curves in
n
-dimensional Minkowskian geometry, then in Section 3 we focus on null curves in three dimensional spaces. From the integrability conditions we obtain the recursion operator of the well-known AKNS system and we define surfaces arising from each member of the AKNS hierarchy. In Section 4 we obtain all possible surfaces arising from the hierarchy of AKNS system and derive the mean and Gauss curvatures. In Sections. 5 and 6 we study the surfaces arising from the NLS and MKdV systems. MKdV system has two important reductions KdV and MKdV equations. Surfaces from these equations are very interesting. We present the one- and two-soliton surfaces of the MKdV equation. Finally in Section 7 we considered the spin vector of the Heisenberg ferromagnetism.

In an

1&0&0&0&\cdots&0\end{array}\right).

(2.1)

At each point of the curve

(2.2)

where

The standard form of the Serret-Frenet equations is given as follows:

First the matrix

\Lambda\,g=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}0&\kappa&0&\cdots&0&0\\
-\kappa&0&\tau_{1}&\cdots&0&0\\
0&-\tau_{1}&\cdot&\cdots&0&0\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0&\tau_{N-2}\\
0&0&0&\cdots&-\tau_{N-2}&0\end{array}\right).

This means that the Serret-Frenet equations are given as

\displaystyle\frac{d\vec{t}}{ds}=\kappa\,\vec{b}_{N-3},

(2.5)

\displaystyle\frac{d\vec{n}}{ds}=\tau_{1}\,\vec{b}_{N-4}-\kappa\,\vec{b}_{N-2},

(2.6)

\displaystyle\frac{d\vec{b}_{1}}{ds}=\tau_{2}\,\vec{b}_{N-5}-\tau_{1}\,\vec{b}_{N-3},

(2.7)

\displaystyle\cdots\cdots=\cdots\cdots\cdots,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{d\vec{b}_{n}}{ds}=\tau_{N-2}\,\vec{n}.

(2.9)

Motion of the curve is described by
\vec{x}(s,t)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\vec{e}\,^{a}}}{\partial s}=\Lambda^{a}\,_{b}\,\vec{e}\,^{b},

(2.10)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\vec{e}\,^{a}}}{\partial t}=M^{a}\,_{b}\,\vec{e}\,^{b},

(2.11)

where

Moving curves in three dimensions
N=3

We consider the Frenet frames on null curves in an
N

\vec{e}\,^{a}=(\vec{t},\vec{n},\vec{b})

\vec{e}\,^{a}\cdot\vec{e}\,^{b}=g^{ab}

g=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&1\\

(3.1)

Then

\Lambda=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\kappa&0\\

\end{array}\right),

(3.2)

\kappa

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\vec{t}}{\partial s}=\kappa\,\vec{n},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\vec{n}}{\partial s}=\tau\,\vec{t}-\kappa\,\vec{b},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\vec{b}}{\partial s}=-\tau\,\vec{n}.

(3.5)

The motion of the curve sweeps the surface
\vec{x}(s,t)
where the motion of the tetrad at each point is governed by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\vec{t}}{\partial t}=f\,\vec{t}+g\,\vec{n},

(3.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\vec{n}}{\partial t}=h\,\vec{t}-g\,\vec{b},

(3.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\vec{b}}{\partial t}=-h\,\vec{n}-f\,\vec{b},

(3.8)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\kappa}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial g}{\partial s}+\kappa\,f,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tau}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}-\tau\,f,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial s}=\kappa\,h-\tau\,g.

(3.11)

The last equation can be written as
f=D^{-1}\,(\kappa\,h-\tau\,f)
, where
D^{-1}\,=\int^{s}\,ds
. Then the above integrability conditions turn to be the following evolution equations in matrix form

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}={\mathcal{R}}\,v

where
u=(\kappa,\tau)^{T}
,

and

{\mathcal{R}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}D-\kappa\,D^{-1}\tau&-\kappa D^{-1}\kappa\\
\tau D^{-1}\,\tau&-D+\tau\,D^{-1}\,\kappa\\
\end{array}\right)

[
22
]

Let us choose

(3.14)

then

u_{t}={\mathcal{R}}^{n}\,u_{s},~~n=0,1,2,\cdots\,.

(3.15)

For
n=1
we get the system of NLS equations, for
n=2
we get the system of MKdV equations, for
n=3

They are respectively given as follows:

For
n=1

g=\kappa_{s}
,
h=-\tau_{s}
.

\displaystyle\kappa_{t}=\kappa_{ss}-\kappa^{2}\tau,

(3.16)

\displaystyle\tau_{t}=-\tau_{ss}+\tau^{2}\kappa.

(3.17)

For
n=2
we have the system of MKdV equations;
g=\kappa_{ss}-\kappa^{2}\tau
,
h=\tau_{ss}-\tau^{2}\kappa

\displaystyle\kappa_{t}=\kappa_{sss}-3\kappa\tau\kappa_{s},

(3.18)

\displaystyle\tau_{t}=\tau_{sss}-3\kappa\tau\tau_{s}.

(3.19)

For
n=3

g=\kappa_{sss}-3\kappa\tau\kappa_{s}

h=-\tau_{sss}+3\kappa\tau\tau_{s}
.

\displaystyle\kappa_{t}=\kappa_{ssss}-3\tau\kappa_{s}^{2}-2\kappa\kappa_{s}\tau_{s}-4\kappa\tau\kappa_{ss}-\kappa^{2}\tau_{ss}+\frac{3}{2}\kappa^{3}\tau^{2},

(3.20)

\displaystyle\tau_{t}=-\tau_{ssss}+3\kappa\tau_{s}^{2}+2\tau\kappa_{s}\tau_{s}+4\kappa\tau\tau_{ss}+\tau^{2}\kappa_{ss}-\frac{3}{2}\kappa^{2}\tau^{3}.

The AKNS hierarchy has a compatible bi-Hamiltonian structure where
{\mathcal{R}}=J_{2}\,J_{1}\,^{-1}

(3.25)

\displaystyle H_{3}=\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\,\left[\tau_{s}\,\kappa_{ss}-\kappa_{s}\,\tau_{ss}+\frac{3}{2}(\kappa\tau^{2}\kappa_{s}-\tau\kappa^{2}\tau_{s})\right]\,ds,

\displaystyle\vec{N}_{s}=\epsilon\,\left[\left(\frac{C_{s}}{\kappa C}\right)_{s}-\frac{\kappa}{C}\,A-\tau\right]\,\vec{x}_{s}+\epsilon\,\frac{\kappa}{C}\,\vec{x}_{t},

6.2
MKdV surfaces

MKdV surfaces correspond to
\tau=k_{0}\,\kappa
where
k_{0}
is an arbitrary constant, i.e.,

\kappa_{t}-\kappa_{sss}+3k_{0}\kappa^{2}\kappa_{s}=0.

(6.24)

We have
g=\kappa_{ss}-k_{0}\kappa^{3}
,
h=k_{0}\,g
,
f=0
, and
C=\frac{1}{2}\,\kappa^{2}+\alpha_{0}(t)
giving

A=k_{0}(-\frac{1}{2}\kappa^{2}+\alpha_{0}(t)),\quad B=\kappa_{s}.

(6.25)

Then we can easily calculate all other geometrical quantities

\displaystyle H=\frac{\epsilon}{(\kappa^{2}+2\alpha_{0}(t))^{2}}[2\kappa_{ss}(\kappa^{2}+2\alpha_{0}(t))-2\kappa\kappa_{s}^{2}-k_{0}\kappa(\kappa^{2}+2\alpha_{0}(t))^{2}],

(6.26)

\displaystyle K=\frac{4}{(\kappa^{2}+2\alpha_{0}(t))^{3}}[(\kappa^{2}+2\alpha_{0}(t))\kappa_{ss}^{2}-2\kappa\kappa_{s}^{2}\kappa_{ss}-k_{0}\kappa^{3}(\kappa^{2}+2\alpha_{0}(t))\kappa_{ss}

\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt-\kappa(\kappa^{2}+2\alpha_{0}(t))\kappa_{st}+2k_{0}\kappa^{4}\kappa_{s}^{2}+2\kappa^{2}\kappa_{s}\kappa_{t}+2\kappa\kappa_{s}\frac{d\alpha_{0}(t)}{dt}].

(6.27)

We now present some MKdV surfaces obtained from the soliton solutions of the MKdV equation.

One-soliton surfaces
:  Let
k_{0}=-8
and
\kappa=\frac{p_{s}r-pr_{s}}{p^{2}+r^{2}}
. The equation (
6.24
) can be written in Hirota bilinear form as

\displaystyle(D_{s}^{3}-D_{t})\{p\cdot r\}=0,

(6.28)

\displaystyle D_{s}^{2}\{p\cdot p+r\cdot r\}=0.

(6.29)

One-soliton solution of (
6.24
) is obtained by
p=e^{\theta_{1}},r=1
, where
\theta_{1}=k_{1}s+\omega_{1}t+\delta_{1}
for
k_{1},\omega_{1},\delta_{1}
constants.
The Hirota bilinear form (
6.28
) and (
6.29
) gives the dispersion relation
\omega_{1}=k_{1}^{3}
. Therefore one-soliton solution becomes

\displaystyle\kappa=\frac{k_{1}e^{\theta_{1}}}{1+e^{2\theta_{1}}},\quad\theta_{1}=k_{1}s+k_{1}^{3}t+\delta_{1}.

(6.30)

Under the above solution we have

\displaystyle A=\frac{4k_{1}^{2}e^{2\theta_{1}}}{(1+e^{2\theta_{1}})}-8\alpha_{0}(t),

(6.31)

\displaystyle B=\frac{k_{1}^{2}e^{\theta_{1}}(1-e^{2\theta_{1}})}{(1+e^{2\theta_{1}})^{2}},

(6.32)

\displaystyle C=\frac{[(k_{1}^{2}+4\alpha_{0}(t))+2\alpha_{0}(t)(1+e^{4\theta_{1}})]}{2(1+e^{2\theta_{1}})^{2}},

(6.33)

and mean and Gauss curvatures become

\displaystyle H=\frac{4\epsilon k_{1}\alpha_{0}(t)(k_{1}^{2}+8\alpha_{0}(t))e^{\theta_{1}}(1+e^{2\theta_{1}})^{3}}{[(k_{1}^{2}+4\alpha_{0}(t))e^{2\theta_{1}}+2\alpha_{0}(t)(1+e^{4\theta_{1}})]^{2}},

(6.34)

\displaystyle K=\frac{8k_{1}^{3}e^{2\theta_{1}}\frac{d\alpha_{0}(t)}{dt}[1+2e^{2\theta_{1}}-2e^{6\theta_{1}}-e^{8\theta_{1}}]}{[(k_{1}^{2}+4\alpha_{0}(t))e^{2\theta_{1}}+2\alpha_{0}(t)(1+e^{4\theta_{1}})]^{3}}.

(6.35)

Note that if
\alpha_{0}(t)=0
we have
H=K=0
. For
\alpha_{0}(t)=\mathrm{constant}\neq 0
we have
K=0
but
H\neq 0
. Consider the following example.

Example 1.
Using one-soliton solution for
\kappa
with
\alpha_{0}(t)=\mathrm{constant}
, say
\alpha_{0}(t)=1
, and taking
\epsilon=1,k_{1}=\frac{3}{2},\delta_{1}=0
give
K=0
and

H=\frac{123}{2}\frac{e^{\frac{3}{2}s+\frac{27}{8}t}(1+e^{3s+\frac{27}{4}t})^{3}}{(\frac{25}{4}e^{3s+\frac{27}{4}t}+2+2e^{6s+\frac{27}{2}t})^{2}}.

(6.36)

The graph of the above mean curvature is given in Figure 1.

Figure 1:
The graph of the mean curvature
H
of MKdV surface
with the parameters
\alpha_{0}(t)=1
,
\epsilon=1,k_{1}=\frac{3}{2},\delta_{1}=0
.

It is clear that the mean curvature has no singularities for all
s
and
t
.

Two-soliton surfaces
: Two-soliton solution of the MKdV equation (
6.24
) can also be obtained by the help of Hirota method. Take
p=e^{\theta_{1}}+e^{\theta_{2}}
and
r=1+A_{12}e^{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}}
in the Hirota bilinear form (
6.28
) and (
6.29
). Here
\theta_{j}=k_{j}s+\omega_{j}t+\delta_{j}
,
j=1,2
. We get

\omega_{j}=k_{j}^{3}
,
j=1,2
, and
A_{12}=-\frac{(k_{1}-k_{2})^{2}}{(k_{1}+k_{2})^{2}}
. Therefore two-soliton solution of (
6.24
) is

\displaystyle\kappa=\frac{k_{1}e^{\theta_{1}}+k_{2}e^{\theta_{2}}-A_{12}e^{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}}(k_{1}e^{\theta_{2}}+k_{2}e^{\theta_{1}})}{1+e^{2\theta_{1}}+e^{2\theta_{2}}+2e^{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}}(1+A_{12})+A_{12}^{2}e^{2\theta_{1}+2\theta_{2}}}.

(6.37)

If we use two-soliton solution for
\kappa
the expressions for
A,B,C,H,
and
K
become lengthy. But here we note that even if
\alpha_{0}(t)=0
, we have
H\neq 0
and
K\neq 0
. In the following example we present graphs of
H
and
K
for particular choice of solution parameters with
\alpha_{0}(t)=0
.

Using two-soliton solution for
\kappa
with
\alpha_{0}(t)=0
, and taking
\epsilon=1,k_{1}=\frac{1}{4},k_{2}=2,\delta_{1}=\delta_{2}=0
give the graphs of
H
and
K
in Figure 2 and Figure 3, respectively.

,

.

We observe that both the mean and Gauss curvatures develop singularities for finite values of the parameter
t
. Such singular behaviour comes from the singularities of the curvature
\kappa
given in (

\alpha_{0}(t)
in (

7
Spin vector of the Heisenberg model

When one studies motion of the tetrad on a curve it is custom to express the spin vector
\vec{S}
in the Heisenberg’s ferromagnetism model in terms of the tetrad vectors. In this model
\vec{S}
satisfies the vector differential equation (see for instance
[
20
]
,
[
39
]
)

\vec{S}_{t}=\vec{S}\times\vec{S}_{ss}

(7.1)

For this purpose we need the vector products of the tetrad vectors

\displaystyle\vec{t}\times\vec{n}=\vec{t},

(7.2)

\displaystyle\vec{t}\times\vec{b}=-\vec{n},

(7.3)

\displaystyle\vec{n}\times\vec{b}=\vec{b}.

(7.4)

Using the above vector products we have the following cases:

1
. Let
\vec{S}=\vec{t}
, then
f=\kappa_{s},~~B=0,~~g=\kappa^{2}
and
h\kappa=\tau g+f_{s}
. In this case the pair
(\kappa,\tau)
satisfies
the following evolution equations:

\displaystyle\kappa_{t}=3\kappa\kappa_{s},

(7.5)

\displaystyle\tau_{t}=\left(\frac{\kappa_{ss}}{\kappa}\right)_{s}+\kappa\tau_{s}.

(7.6)

When
\kappa
is a nonzero constant then the torsion of the curve
satisfies the linear equation
\tau_{t}=\kappa\,\tau_{s}
.

2
. Let
\vec{S}=\vec{n}
, then
h=-\tau_{s}
,
g=\kappa_{s}
, and
f_{s}=h\kappa-g\tau
.
Here the pair
(\kappa,\tau)
satisfies

\displaystyle\kappa_{t}=\kappa_{ss}-\kappa^{2}\tau+\kappa\alpha_{1}(t),

(7.7)

\displaystyle\tau_{t}=-\tau_{ss}+\kappa\tau^{2}-\tau\alpha_{1}(t).

(7.8)

If
\alpha_{1}(t)=0
, we have the NLS system.

3
. Let
\vec{S}=\vec{b}
, then
h=\tau^{2}
,
f=-\tau_{s}
, and
g\tau=h\kappa-f_{s}

In this case the functions
\kappa
and
\tau
satisfy

\displaystyle\kappa_{t}=\Big(\frac{\tau_{ss}}{\tau}\Big)_{s}+\tau\kappa_{s},

(7.9)

\displaystyle\tau_{t}=3\tau\tau_{s}.

(7.10)

8
Concluding Remarks

In this work we focused on moving null curves in a three dimensional Minkowski space
M_{3}
and considered the case they form two surfaces in
M_{3}
.
We showed that the integrability conditions lead to the AKNS hierarchy. This means that we obtain infinitely many surfaces corresponding to each member of the hierarchy. As examples we studied the surfaces arising from the KdV and MKdV equations. In particular we obtained one- and two-soliton surfaces of the MKdV equation. We observed that the tetrad vectors satisfy the spin equations in Heisenberg model of ferromagnetism. In all the possible cases the curvature and the torsion of the curves satisfy certain nonlinear partial differential equations. For the two cases the spin vectors are null but in the case where the spin vector is the normal vector the spin vector is a spacelike vector and curvature and the torsion pair satisfies the NLS.

Jacob’s ladders, Riemann’s oscillators, quotient of two oscillating multiforms and set of metamorphoses of this system
Jan Moser

Abstract
. In this paper we introduce complicated oscillating system, namely quotient of two multiforms based on Riemann-Siegel formula. We prove that there is an infinite set of metamorphoses of this system (=chrysalis) on critical line σ = 1 2 \sigma=\frac{1}{2} into a butterfly (=infinite series of Möbius functions in the region of absolute convergence σ > 1 \sigma>1 ). Spectral Formula

(3.1)

Proof.

We will make the following transformations of the Riemann-Siegel formula

(3.2)

Z(t)=2\sum_{n\leq\tau(t)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\cos\{\vartheta(t)-t\ln n\}+\mathcal{O}(t^{-1/4}).

(a)

Since

\begin{split}&\sum_{\tau(x_{r})<n\leq\tau(x_{r}+H)}1=\mathcal{O}\{\tau(x_{r}+H)-\tau(x_{r})\}=\\
&=\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{x_{r}+H}{2\pi}}-\sqrt{\frac{x_{r}}{2\pi}}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{H}{\sqrt{x_{r}}}\right),\end{split}

then

\begin{split}&\sum_{\tau(x_{r})<n\leq\tau(x_{r}+H)}\frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}\cos\{\vartheta(t)-t\ln n\}=\\
&=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{H}{\sqrt{x_{r}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau(x_{r})}}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{H}{x_{r}^{3/2}}\right)=\mathcal{O}(x_{r}^{-1/2}).\end{split}

Consequently, we have (see (
3.2
)) that

(3.3)

\begin{split}&Z(t)=2\sum_{n\leq\tau(x_{r})}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\cos\{\vartheta(t)-t\ln n\}+\mathcal{O}(x_{r}^{-1/4}),\\
&t\in[x_{r},x_{r}+H].\end{split}

(b)

Next, we use the following formula

(3.4)

\begin{split}&\vartheta(t)=\vartheta(x_{r})+\vartheta^{\prime}(x_{r})(t-x_{r})+\frac{1}{2}\vartheta^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{r})(t-x_{r})^{2},\\
&\xi_{r}\in(x_{r},x_{r}+H),\ t\in[x_{r},x_{r}+H].\end{split}

Since (see
[
8
]
, pp. 221, 329)

\begin{split}&\vartheta(t)=\frac{t}{2}\ln\frac{t}{2\pi}-\frac{t}{2}-\frac{\pi}{8}+\mathcal{O}(t^{-1}),\\
&\vartheta^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{t}{2\pi}+\mathcal{O}(t^{-1}),\\
&\vartheta^{\prime\prime}(t)\sim\frac{1}{2t},\end{split}

then

(3.5)

\vartheta(x_{r})=x_{r}\ln\tau(x_{r})-\frac{x_{r}}{2}-\frac{\pi}{8}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{x_{r}}\right),

and

(3.6)

\begin{split}&\vartheta^{\prime}(x_{r})(t-x_{r})=\left\{\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{x_{r}}{2\pi}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{x_{r}}\right)\right\}(t-x_{r})=\\
&=\left\{\ln\tau(x_{r})+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{x_{r}}\right)\right\}(t-x_{r})=\\
&=t\ln\tau(x_{r})-x_{r}\ln\tau(x_{r})+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{t-x_{r}}{x_{r}}\right)=\\
&=t\ln\tau(x_{r})-x_{r}\ln\tau(x_{r})+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{H}{x_{r}}\right).\end{split}

Hence (see (
3.3
)–(
3.6

(3.7)

\begin{split}&\vartheta(t)-t\ln n=t\ln\frac{\tau(x_{r})}{n}-\frac{x_{r}}{2}-\frac{\pi}{8}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1+H}{x_{r}}\right)=\\
&=t\ln\frac{\tau(x_{r})}{n}-\frac{x_{r}}{2}-\frac{\pi}{8}+\mathcal{O}(x_{r}^{-1/4}).\end{split}

Putting (

3.3

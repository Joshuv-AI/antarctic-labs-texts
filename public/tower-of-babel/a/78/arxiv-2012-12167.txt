Sensitivity analysis in the infinite dimensional Heston model
Fred Espen Benth, Giulia Di Nunno, Iben Cathrine Simonsen

Abstract
We consider the infinite dimensional Heston stochastic volatility model proposed in [ 5 ] . The price of a forward contract on a non-storable commodity is modelled by a generalized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process in the Filipović space with this volatility. We prove different representation formulas for the forward price. Then we consider prices of options written on these forward contracts and we study sensitivity analysis with computation of the Greeks with respect to different parameters in the model. Since these parameters are infinite dimensional, we need to reinterpret the meaning of the Greeks. For this we use infinite dimensional Malliavin calculus and a randomization technique.

60H07, 60H15, 91G20, 46N30

Keywords:
stochastic volatility, infinite dimensional Heston model, infinite dimensional Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes, electricity markets, forward prices, option pricing, sensitivity analysis, Malliavin calculus, Greeks

1
Introduction

Consider a forward contract on electricity or some other non-storable commodity.
The forward price is often represented as a random field
f:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, where
T<\infty
and
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
is a complete probability space equipped with a
\mathbb{P}
-augmented filtration
\mathbb{F}=\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq T}
. In the Musiela notation,
t\in[0,T]
is the time horizon and
x=T-t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
represents the time to maturity.
Clearly, we can regard the forward price as an infinite dimensional stochastic process
f:[0,T]\times\Omega\to H
, where
H
is a suitable space of real-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
. This is the approach taken to describe the dynamics of evolution of the forward prices.

In this paper we consider an infinite dimensional stochastic volatility model for the forward price.
Our interests are motivated by the power markets where there are clear signs of intertemporal correlation structures across time to maturity (see e.g.
[
4
]
) and also signs of non-Gaussianity (see e.g.
[
6
]
).
We choose to consider the Heston-type infinite dimensional volatility model proposed in
[
5
]
, and study both the pricing of options written on the forwards and provide tools for the sensitivity analysis.
For this last one, we have to introduce the adequate concepts in the infinite dimensional setting.
We resolve this by exploiting some ideas in the approach of
[
1
]
, using the interplay of functional derivatives and the Malliavin calculus via a form of randomization.
We remark that there is a structural difference in the application perspectives between our paper and
[
1
]
.
While we face an infinite dimensional problem all the way through, the authors in
[
1
]
consider a finite dimensional noise and path-dependent coefficients.

Our paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the forward price model.
In Section
3
we consider prices of options written on such forward contracts and study the sensitivity of these prices with respect to different model parameters.

2
Stochastic volatility forward price model

Let
H
be a separable Hilbert space with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H}
and associated norm
\|\cdot\|_{H}
.
The space of bounded linear operators from
H
into itself is denoted by
L(H)
.
It is a Banach space with the operator norm denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{\text{op}}
.
Furthermore, let
\mathcal{H}\subseteq L(H)
denote the space of Hilbert-Schmidt operators on
H
.
Recall that
\mathcal{H}
is a separable Hilbert space.
The inner product and associated norm in
\mathcal{H}
are denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}
, respectively.

We start by introducing the stochastic volatility model from
[
5
]
.
Let
W=\{W_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
and
B=\{B_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be independent
\mathbb{F}
-adapted Wiener processes in
H
with covariance operators
Q_{W}
and
Q_{B}
.
Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{C}
be densely defined operators on
H
which generate
C_{0}
-semigroups
\{\mathcal{U}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{\mathcal{S}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
respectively, and let
\eta\in L(H)
.
Let
Z=\{Z_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be an
\mathbb{F}
-adapted stochastic process in
H
satisfying
\|Z_{t}\|_{H}=1
for all
t\geq 0
.
The process
X=\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, which will later be used to model the forward price, is defined by the following equation:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &dX_{t}=\mathcal{C}X_{t}dt+\Gamma^{Z}_{t}dB_{t},&&\mspace{40mu}X_{0}=x_{0}\in H,\\
&dY_{t}=\mathcal{A}Y_{t}\,dt+\eta\,dW_{t},&&\mspace{40mu}Y_{0}=y_{0}\in H,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

where
\Gamma^{Z}_{t}:=Z_{t}\otimes Y_{t}
defines the volatility process
\Gamma_{Z}=\{\Gamma^{Z}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
which is
\mathbb{F}
-adapted and takes values in
\mathcal{H}
.
From Peszat and Zabczyk
[
13
, Sect. 9.4]
we know that (
2.1
) has a unique mild solution given by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &X_{t}=\mathcal{S}_{t}x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}_{t-s}\Gamma^{Z}_{s}\,dB_{s},\qquad&t\geq 0,\\
&Y_{t}=\mathcal{U}_{t}y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{U}_{t-s}\eta\,dW_{s},\qquad&t\geq 0,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.2)

since the stochastic integral
\int_{0}^{t}\Gamma^{Z}_{s}dB_{s}
is well-defined by
[
5
]
.
The stochastic integral
\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}_{t-s}\Gamma^{Z}_{s}\,dB_{s}
is also well-defined, since
\mathcal{S}_{t}\in L(H)
and its operator norm grows at most exponentially by the Hille-Yoshida Theorem, see Engel and Nagel
[
9
, Prop. I.5.5]
.

The variance process
\mathcal{V}=\{\mathcal{V}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is defined by
\mathcal{V}_{t}\coloneqq Y_{t}^{\otimes 2}=Y_{t}\otimes Y_{t}=\langle Y_{t},\,\cdot\,\rangle_{H}Y_{t}
,
t\geq 0
.
It is a process of symmetric and positive definite operators in
\mathcal{H}
, and hence it has a unique square root process, which is given by

\mathcal{V}^{1/2}_{t}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\|Y_{t}\|_{H}^{-1}\mathcal{V}_{t},&Y_{t}\neq 0\\
0,&Y_{t}=0.\end{array}\right.

The variance process can be decomposed as
\mathcal{V}_{t}=\Gamma^{Z}_{t}(\Gamma^{Z}_{t})^{*}
for all
t\geq 0
, where
(\Gamma^{Z}_{t})^{*}=Y_{t}\otimes Z_{t}
.
Clearly, several choices of
\{Z_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
are possible.
A simple choice of
\{Z_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is
Z_{t}=\gamma\in H
for all
t\geq 0
, where
\|\gamma\|_{H}=1
.
We could also define
\{Z_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
as
Z_{t}=Y_{t}/\|Y_{t}\|_{H}
when
Y_{t}\neq 0
and
Z_{t}=0
when
Y_{t}=0
to get
\Gamma^{Z}_{t}=\mathcal{V}^{1/2}_{t}
for all
t\geq 0
.

We have a full description of the characteristic functional of
X_{t}
for
t\geq 0
(result presented in
[
5
, Proposition 9]
):

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that
Z
is
\mathbb{F}^{Y}
-adapted, where
\mathbb{F}^{Y}
is the filtration generated by
Y
.
Then, for any
h\in H
and
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\varphi_{X_{t}}(h)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}\left<X_{t},h\right>_{H}}\right]

\displaystyle={\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}\left<\mathcal{S}_{t}x_{0},h\right>_{H}}\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|Q_{B}^{1/2}Z_{s}\|_{H}^{2}\mathcal{S}_{t-s}\mathcal{V}_{s}\mathcal{S}^{*}_{t-s}\,ds\right)h,h\right\rangle_{H}\right)\right],

where the integral above is a Bochner integral in
L(H)
.

From the proposition above, we see that for any
t\geq 0
,
X_{t}
, conditional on
\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}
, is a Gaussian
H
-valued random variable.
The expectation of
X_{t}
is
\mathbb{E}[X_{t}]=\mathcal{S}_{t}x_{0}
and its covariance operator
Q_{X_{t}}
is found in
[
5
]
:

Q_{X_{t}}=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}_{t-s}\mathbb{E}\left[\|Q^{1/2}_{B}Z_{s}\|_{H}^{2}\mathcal{V}_{s}\right]\mathcal{S}^{*}_{t-s}\,ds.

(2.3)

Hence
X
is an
H
-valued conditionally Gaussian process.
In the particular case when
Z\equiv\gamma\in H
with
\|\gamma\|_{H}=1
, we have that

Q_{X_{t}}=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}_{t-s}\left((\mathcal{U}_{s}y_{0})^{\otimes 2}+\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{U}_{u}\eta Q_{W}\eta^{*}\mathcal{U}^{*}_{u}\,du\right)\mathcal{S}^{*}_{t-s}\,ds,

which is obtained by applying Theorem 8.7(iv) of Peszat and Zabczyk
[
13
]
, see
[
5
]
.

2.1
The forward price model

We shall model the price
F(t,T)
at time
t
of a forward contract maturing at time
T
by choosing a specific Hilbert space
H_{w}
and evaluating
X_{t}
at the time to maturity.
The price
F(t,T)
will be called the forward price.
We will use the Musiela notation in which the forward price is written as
f(t,x)=F(t,t+x)
where
x=T-t
is the time to maturity.

The Filipović space
H_{w}
was first introduced in
[
10
]
and is a Hilbert space consisting of real-valued measurable functions.
Let
w:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow[1,\infty)
be a measurable and increasing weight function with
w(0)=1
and
\int_{0}^{\infty}w^{-1}(x)\,dx<\infty
.
Then
H_{w}
is defined as the space of functions
h\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
which possess a weak derivative
h^{\prime}\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
such that
\int_{0}^{\infty}|h^{\prime}(x)|^{2}w(x)\,dx<\infty
.
We know that every such function has an absolutely continuous version.
The norm on
H_{w}
is defined as

\|h\|_{w}^{2}\coloneqq|h(0)|^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}|h^{\prime}(x)|^{2}w(x)\,dx.

The evaluation functional
\delta_{x}:H_{w}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
defined by
\delta_{x}(h)\coloneqq h(x)
is a continuous linear functional on
H_{w}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, see
[
10
]
.
This means that
\delta_{x}\in H_{w}^{*}
, and hence by the Riesz representation theorem
\delta_{x}=\langle\cdot,h_{x}\rangle_{w}
for some element
h_{x}\in H_{w}
.
Let
\|\cdot\|_{*}
denote the norm on
H_{w}^{*}
.
From
[
10
, Lemma 3.11]
, we have

h_{x}(y)=1+\int_{0}^{x\wedge y}\frac{1}{w(s)}ds,\quad y\in\mathbb{R}_{+},

(2.4)

and we see that for
h\in H_{w}
,

\displaystyle\langle h,h_{x}\rangle_{w}

\displaystyle=h(0)h_{x}(0)+\int_{0}^{\infty}w(y)h^{\prime}(y)h_{x}^{\prime}(y)\,dy=h(0)+\int_{0}^{\infty}w(y)h^{\prime}(y)\frac{1}{w(y)}1_{\{y\leq x\}}\,dy

\displaystyle=h(0)+\int_{0}^{x}h^{\prime}(y)\,dy=h(x)=\delta_{x}(h).

The following lemma is part of a result in Benth and Krühner
[
2
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\delta_{x}
be the evaluation functional on
H_{w}
.
Then
\|\delta_{x}\|_{*}^{2}=h_{x}(x)
, where
h_{x}
is given by equation (
2.4
).

Consider now equation (
2.1
) with
H
being the Filipović space
H_{w}
and
\mathcal{C}
being the derivative operator
\partial/\partial x
.
The
C_{0}
-semigroup generated by the derivative operator on
H_{w}
is the semigroup of left-shift operators, i.e.
\mathcal{S}_{x}(h)=h(\cdot+x)
for
x\geq 0
.
It is shown in Filipović
[
10
, Equation (5.10)]
that for
x\geq 0
,
\|\mathcal{S}_{x}\|_{\text{op}}\leq C
for some constant
C
.
A value for the constant
C
is found in Benth and Krühner
[
3
, Lemma 3.4]
:

Lemma 2.3
.

For
x\geq 0
, it holds that
\|\mathcal{S}_{x}\|_{\text{op}}\leq\sqrt{2\max\left(1,\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{w(s)}\,ds\right)}
.

While on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
the adjoint of the left-shift operator is the right-shift operator, this is not the case on
H_{w}
.
The following lemma gives the adjoint of the left-shift operator on
H_{w}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
x\geq 0
, let
\mathcal{S}_{x}
denote the left-shift operator on
H_{w}
, defined by
\mathcal{S}_{x}h=h(\cdot+x)
.
The adjoint operator
\mathcal{S}_{x}^{*}
of
\mathcal{S}_{x}
, defined by the relation
\langle\mathcal{S}_{x}f,g\rangle_{w}=\langle f,\mathcal{S}_{x}^{*}g\rangle_{w}
for all
f,g\in H_{w}
, is given by

\mathcal{S}_{x}^{*}g(y)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &g(0)\left(1+\int_{0}^{y}\frac{1}{w(s)}\,ds\right),\qquad&0\leq y\leq x,\\
&g(0)\left(1+\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{w(s)}\,ds\right)+\int_{0}^{y-x}\frac{w(s)}{w(s+x)}g^{\prime}(s)\,ds,\qquad&y>x.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.5)

Proof.

From Proposition 3.8 in
[
3
]
, we have that for an operator
\mathcal{T}\in L(H_{w})
, the adjoint operator
\mathcal{T}^{*}
is given by

\mathcal{T}^{*}g(y)=g(0)\eta(y)+\int_{0}^{\infty}q(y,s)g^{\prime}(s)\,ds,

where

\eta(y)\coloneqq(\mathcal{T}h_{y})(0)

and

q(y,s)\coloneqq(\mathcal{T}h_{y})^{\prime}(s)w(s).

For the left-shift operator we find that

\eta(y)=(\mathcal{S}_{x}h_{y})(0)=h_{y}(x)=\int_{0}^{y\wedge x}\frac{1}{w(s)}\,ds,\qquad y\geq 0.

From
[
10
, p. 78]
we have that
(\mathcal{S}_{x}h)^{\prime}=\mathcal{S}_{x}h^{\prime}
.
Hence, for
y,s\geq 0
,

\displaystyle q(y,s)

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{S}_{x}h_{y})^{\prime}(s)w(s)=\mathcal{S}_{x}h_{y}^{\prime}(s)w(s)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{x}\left(\frac{1}{w(s)}1_{\{s\leq y\}}\right)w(s)=\frac{1}{w(s+x)}1_{\{s+x\leq y\}}w(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{w(s)}{w(s+x)}1_{\{s\leq y-x\}}.

This gives

\mathcal{S}_{x}^{*}g(y)=g(0)\left(1+\int_{0}^{y\wedge x}\frac{1}{w(s)}\,ds\right)+\int_{0}^{(y-x)\vee 0}\frac{w(s)}{w(s+x)}g^{\prime}(s)\,ds,

and the result follows.
∎

We define the forward price as

f(t,x)\coloneqq\delta_{x}(X_{t}),\qquad t\in[0,T],\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

(2.6)

Observe that the covariance between the prices of two forward contracts with different times to delivery follows from Proposition
2.1
, see
[
5
]
.

Corollary 2.5
.

For all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we have

\displaystyle\textnormal{Cov}\big(f(t,x),\,f(t,y)\big)=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\|Q_{B}^{1/2}Z_{s}\|_{w}^{2}\,\delta_{x+t-s}(Y_{s})\delta_{y+t-s}(Y_{s})\,ds\right].

In the special case when
Z_{t}=\gamma\in H_{w}
with
\|\gamma\|_{w}=1
, it is shown in
[
5
]

that

\displaystyle\textnormal{Cov}(f(t,x),f(t,y))

\displaystyle=\|Q_{B}^{1/2}\gamma\|_{w}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{y+t-s}(\mathcal{U}_{s}y_{0})^{\otimes 2}\delta_{x+t-s}^{*}(1)\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\|Q_{B}^{1/2}\gamma\|_{w}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{y+t-s}\left(\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{U}_{u}\eta Q_{W}\eta^{*}\mathcal{U}^{*}(u)\,du\right)\delta^{*}_{x+t-s}(1)\,ds,

by applying Theorem 8.7(iv) of Peszat and Zabczyk
[
13
]
.

2.2
Commodities with delivery period

In the case of commodities with delivery over a period of time
[T_{1},T_{2}]
, as for electricity, the forward price
G(t,T_{1},T_{2})
at time
t\leq T_{1}
is modelled by

G(t,T_{1},T_{2})\coloneqq\frac{1}{T_{2}-T_{1}}\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}F(t,T)\,dT.

Hereafter, we study different representations of the forward price.
For this we introduce two integration functionals,
\mathcal{I}_{d}
and
\mathcal{J}_{x,d}
.
For
d\geq 0
,
\mathcal{I}_{d}:H_{w}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined as

\mathcal{I}_{d}(h)\coloneqq\frac{1}{d}\int_{0}^{d}h(u)\,du,\qquad h\in H_{w}.

(2.7)

For
x,d\geq 0
,
\mathcal{J}_{x,d}:H_{w}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined as

\mathcal{J}_{x,d}(h)\coloneqq\frac{1}{d}\int_{0}^{d}\delta_{x+u}(h)\,du,\qquad h\in H_{w}.

(2.8)

We can see that
\mathcal{J}_{x,d}=\mathcal{I}_{d}\circ\mathcal{S}_{x}
, where
\mathcal{S}_{x}
is the left-shift operator on
H_{w}
.
Using the Musiela notation we let
x
denote the time to the start of the delivery period and
d
denote the length of the delivery period.
Then
[T_{1},T_{2}]=[t+x,t+x+d]
, and the forward price can be written as

g(t,x,d)\coloneqq\mathcal{J}_{x,d}(X_{t})=\frac{1}{d}\int_{0}^{d}f(t,x+u)\,du,\qquad t\in[0,T_{1}],\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{+},d\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

(2.9)

The covariance between the prices of two forward contracts with different times
x
and
y
to the start of the delivery period and different lengths
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
of the delivery period was found in
[
5
]
:

\textnormal{Cov}\big(g(t,x,d_{1}),\,g(t,y,d_{2})\big)=\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}}\int_{0}^{d_{1}}\int_{0}^{d_{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\|Q_{B}^{1/2}Z_{s}\|_{w}^{2}\,\delta_{x+t-s+u}(Y_{s})\delta_{y+t-s+v}(Y_{s})\right]\,ds\,dv\,du.

The next two results present some properties of the functionals
\mathcal{I}_{d}
and
\mathcal{J}_{x,d}
defined in (
2.7
) and (
2.8
).

Lemma 2.6
.

The integration functionals
\mathcal{I}_{d}
and
\mathcal{J}_{x,d}
defined in (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) are well-defined continuous linear functionals on
H_{w}
, i.e.
\mathcal{I}_{d},\mathcal{J}_{x,d}\in H_{w}^{*}
.

Proof.

Since
h\in H_{w}
is locally integrable,
\mathcal{I}_{d}
is a well-defined linear functional on
H_{w}
.
We show that it is bounded.
For
h\in H_{w}
,

|\mathcal{I}_{d}(h)|\leq\frac{1}{d}\int_{0}^{d}|\delta_{u}(h)|\,du\leq\frac{1}{d}\left(\int_{0}^{d}\|\delta_{u}\|_{*}\,du\right)\|h\|_{w}

holds.
Since
x\mapsto\|\delta_{x}\|_{*}
is locally integrable, it follows that
\mathcal{I}_{d}
is bounded, i.e.
\mathcal{I}_{d}\in H_{w}^{*}
.
Since
\mathcal{S}_{t}
is continuous, it follows that
\mathcal{J}_{x,d}\in H_{w}^{*}
.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

For any
h\in H_{w}
, we have that

\mathcal{J}_{x,d}(h)=\langle h,h_{x,d}\rangle_{w},

where

h_{x,d}(y)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1+\int_{0}^{y}\frac{1}{w(s)}\,ds,\qquad&0\leq y\leq x,\\
&1+\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{w(s)}\,ds+\int_{0}^{y-x}\frac{d-s}{dw(s+x)}\,ds,\qquad&x<y\leq x+d,\\
&1+\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{w(s)}\,ds+\int_{0}^{d}\frac{d-s}{dw(s+x)}\,ds,\qquad&y>x+d.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.10)

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{I}_{d}\in H_{w}^{*}
, we have that
\mathcal{I}_{d}=\langle\cdot,h_{d}^{\mathcal{I}}\rangle_{w}
for some element
h_{d}^{\mathcal{I}}\in H_{w}
.
From Filipović
[
10
, Lemma 3.11]
we have

h_{d}^{\mathcal{I}}(y)=1+\frac{1}{d}\int_{0}^{y}\frac{d-s\wedge d}{w(s)}\,ds.

(2.11)

Since also
\mathcal{J}_{x,d}\in H_{w}^{*}
, we have that
\mathcal{J}_{x,d}=\langle\cdot,h_{x,d}\rangle_{w}
for some
h_{x,d}\in H_{w}
.
We see that

\mathcal{J}_{x,d}(h)=\mathcal{I}_{d}(\mathcal{S}_{x}(h))=\left\langle\mathcal{S}_{x}(h),h_{d}^{\mathcal{I}}\right\rangle_{w}=\left\langle h,\mathcal{S}_{x}^{*}(h_{d}^{\mathcal{I}})\right\rangle_{w},

which gives
h_{x,d}=\mathcal{S}_{x}^{*}(h_{d}^{\mathcal{I}})
.
Since
h_{d}^{\mathcal{I}}(0)=1
and

(h_{d}^{\mathcal{I}})^{\prime}(y)=\frac{1}{d}\frac{d-y\wedge d}{w(y)}=\frac{d-y}{dw(y)}1_{\{0\leq y\leq d\}},

we get by (
2.5
) that

\displaystyle h_{x,d}(y)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{x}^{*}h_{d}^{\mathcal{I}}(y)

\displaystyle=h_{d}^{\mathcal{I}}(0)\left(1+\int_{0}^{y\wedge x}\frac{1}{w(s)}\,ds\right)+\int_{0}^{(y-x)\vee 0}\frac{w(s)}{w(s+x)}(h_{d}^{\mathcal{I}})^{\prime}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=1+\int_{0}^{y\wedge x}\frac{1}{w(s)}\,ds+\int_{0}^{(y-x)\vee 0}\frac{w(s)}{w(s+x)}\frac{d-s}{dw(s)}1_{\{0\leq s\leq d\}}\,ds

\displaystyle=1+\int_{0}^{y\wedge x}\frac{1}{w(s)}\,ds+\int_{0}^{((y-x)\vee 0)\wedge d}\frac{d-s}{dw(s+x)}\,ds.

∎

The following corollary gives a representation for the forward price as an inner product between
X_{t}
and an element of
H_{w}
.

Corollary 2.8
.

The forward price can be expressed as

g(t,x,d)=\langle X_{t},h_{x,d}\rangle_{w},

where the function
h_{x,d}
is given in (
2.10
).

3
Sensitivity analysis

Consider an option written on a forward contract with delivery period
[T_{1},T_{2}]
as described in the previous section.
Let
\tau
denote the exercise time of the option, where
0\leq\tau\leq T_{1}
.
Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
denote the payoff function, which is assumed to be measurable and of at most linear growth.
The price of the option at time
t\leq\tau
is represented as

\Pi_{t}=e^{-r(\tau-t)}\mathbb{E}\left[\Phi(G(\tau,T_{1},T_{2}))\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{t}\right],

where
r
is a constant instantaneous interest rate.
Note that we are taking the expectation under the market probability measure
\mathbb{P}
and not a risk-neutral probability measure.
The reason for this is that electricity is not storable, hence it is not a tradeable asset in the usual sense, see
[
4
]
.
The standard argument of using a risk-neutral probability measure to prevent arbitrage opportunities is therefore not valid in this case, and any equivalent martingale measure can be used for option pricing.

As before we adopt the Musiela notation with
x=T_{1}-\tau
and
d=T_{2}-T_{1}
. Recalling (
2.9
), the option price can then be written as

\Pi_{t}=e^{-r(\tau-t)}\mathbb{E}\left[\Phi(g(\tau,x,d))\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]=e^{-r(\tau-t)}\mathbb{E}\left[(\Phi\circ\mathcal{J}_{x,d})(X_{\tau})\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{t}\right],

(3.1)

where the functional
\mathcal{J}_{x,d}
is defined in (
2.8
).
The option price at time
t=0
is given by

\Pi_{0}={\mathrm{e}}^{-r\tau}\mathbb{E}\left[(\Phi\circ\mathcal{J}_{x,d})(X_{\tau})\right].

(3.2)

In sensitivity analysis we are interested in the derivatives of the option price with respect to different parameters of the underlying price model.
In finance, these derivatives are called
Greeks
since they are denoted by greek letters.
Examples are the delta, which is the derivative with respect to the initial value of the underlying asset, and the vega, which is the derivative with respect to the volatility.
Our framework presents ”parameters” in infinite dimensions, e.g. the initial forward price is a function in
H_{w}
.
Thus we have to reinterpret the meaning of the Greeks.
For the delta, a natural choice is to take inspiration from
[
1
]
and interpret it as a directional derivative.
For the vega however, there is no natural generalization to our framework.
We choose to compute the directional derivatives with respect to the ”parameters”
y_{0}
and
\eta
of the stochastic volatility model instead.

We now consider the option price at time
t=0
as a function of the initial value
x_{0}
of
X_{t}
, the initial value
y_{0}
of
Y_{t}
and the volatility
\eta
of
Y_{t}
.
In the previous sections we assumed that
\eta\in L(H_{w})
.
We recall that the space of bounded linear operators on a Hilbert space is not reflexive.
In fact, it contains a subspace (the diagonal operators with respect to a given orthonormal basis) which is isomorphic to
\ell^{\infty}
, and
\ell^{\infty}
is not reflexive, see
[
11
, Theorem 1.11.16, Proposition 1.11.18 and Example 1.11.23]
.
Since
L(H_{w})
is not reflexive, it is not a UMD Banach space
[
15
, p.5]
.
We therefore need to have stronger assumptions on
\eta
to be able to apply the Malliavin calculus in infinite dimensions and in particular to use the chain rule
[
15
, Proposition 3.8]
for the Malliavin derivative.
Hence, in this section, we assume that
\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{w}\coloneqq L_{HS}(H_{w})\subset L(H_{w})
.
The option price at time
t=0
is then a functional on
H_{w}\times H_{w}\times\mathcal{H}_{w}
which takes the form

\Pi_{0}(x_{0},y_{0},\eta)={\mathrm{e}}^{-r\tau}\mathbb{E}\left[(\Phi\circ\mathcal{J}_{x,d})(X_{\tau}(x_{0},y_{0},\eta))\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\Psi(X_{\tau}(x_{0},y_{0},\eta))\right],

(3.3)

where the notation
X_{\tau}(x_{0},y_{0},\eta)
means that we regard the random variable
X_{\tau}
as a function of
x_{0}
,
y_{0}
and
\eta
, and to ease the notation, we have introduced the functional
\Psi
on
H_{w}
as
\Psi\coloneqq{\mathrm{e}}^{-r\tau}(\Phi\circ\mathcal{J}_{x,d})
.

We are going to consider
\Pi_{0}
as a function of one of the parameters keeping the two others fixed.
For this purpose we will use the notation
\Pi_{0}^{y_{0},\eta}
,
\Pi_{0}^{x_{0},\eta}
and
\Pi_{0}^{x_{0},y_{0}}
, where the variables in superscript are the ones we keep fixed.
The directional derivative
\partial_{h}\Pi_{0}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0})
of
\Pi_{0}^{y_{0},\eta}
at
x_{0}\in H_{w}
in direction
h\in H_{w}
is defined as

\partial_{h}\Pi_{0}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0})\coloneqq\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Pi_{0}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}+\varepsilon h)\bigg|_{\varepsilon=0}=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{\Pi_{0}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}+\varepsilon h)-\Pi_{0}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0})}{\varepsilon},\quad h\in H_{w}.

(3.4)

This will be our interpretation of the Greek delta.
The directional derivative
\partial_{h}\Pi_{0}^{x_{0},\eta}(y_{0})
is defined similarly.
The directional derivative of
\Pi_{0}^{x_{0},y_{0}}
at
\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{w}
in direction
\zeta\in\mathcal{H}_{w}
is defined as

\partial_{\zeta}\Pi_{0}^{x_{0},y_{0}}(\eta)\coloneqq\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Pi_{0}^{x_{0},y_{0}}(\eta+\varepsilon\zeta)\bigg|_{\varepsilon=0}=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{\Pi_{0}^{x_{0},y_{0}}(\eta+\varepsilon\zeta)-\Pi_{0}^{x_{0},y_{0}}(\eta)}{\varepsilon},\quad\zeta\in\mathcal{H}_{w}.

(3.5)

Before proceeding further, we briefly review some elements of Malliavin calculus on Hilbert spaces.
We refer to
[
12
]
and
[
8
]
for an introduction to Malliavin calculus, and to
[
7
]
and
[
15
]
for more details on Malliavin calculus on Hilbert and Banach spaces.

3.1
Some elements of Malliavin calculus

Let us consider an isonormal process
\mathbb{W}
on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
and some Hilbert space
H
, where
\mathbb{F}=\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq T}
is the filtration generated by
\mathbb{W}
and
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{T}
.
For all
h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}\in H
,
\mathbb{W}(h_{1}),\ldots,\mathbb{W}(h_{n})
are jointly normally distributed real-valued random variables with mean zero and
\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{W}(h_{i})\mathbb{W}(h_{j})]=\langle h_{i},h_{j}\rangle_{H}
.

Let
E
be another Hilbert space, which is assumed to be separable.
An
E
-valued random variable
F
is called
smooth
if there exists
h_{1},\dots,h_{n}\in H
such that
F
can be written on the form
F=f(\mathbb{W}(h_{1}),\dots,\mathbb{W}(h_{n}))
, where
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow E
is infinitely differentiable and all derivatives are polynomially bounded.
The set of smooth
E
-valued random variables is dense in
L^{p}(\Omega;E)
for
1\leq p<\infty
.
The Malliavin derivative
\mathscr{D}F
of a smooth
E
-valued random variable
F
is a random variable taking values in
E\otimes H
, and it is defined as

\mathscr{D}F\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(\mathbb{W}(h_{1}),\ldots,\mathbb{W}(h_{n}))\otimes h_{i}.

The space
E\otimes H
can be identified with the space
L_{HS}(H,E)
of Hilbert-Schmidt operators from
H
to
E
.
In the special case when
E=\mathbb{R}
,
L_{HS}(H,\mathbb{R})
can be identified with
H
.
The Malliavin derivative of a real-valued random variable is therefore an
H
-valued random variable.

The Malliavin derivative is closable as an unbounded operator from
L^{p}(\Omega;E)
to
L^{p}(\Omega;E\otimes H)
, and the closure will also be denoted by
\mathscr{D}
.
The domain of the closure is denoted by
\mathbb{D}^{1,p}(E)
, and it becomes a Banach space if we endow it with the following norm:

\|F\|_{\mathbb{D}^{1,p}(E)}\coloneqq\left(\|F\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;E)}^{p}+\|\mathscr{D}F\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;E\otimes H)}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

The space
\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(E)
is a Hilbert space.

We will need the following chain rule. We state it here for Hilbert spaces, but it is also valid in the more general case of UMD Banach spaces, see
[
15
, Proposition 3.8]
.

Lemma 3.1

(Chain rule)
.

Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be Hilbert spaces, and suppose
\varphi:E_{1}\rightarrow E_{2}
is Fréchet differentiable with a continuous and bounded derivative. If
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,p}(E_{1})
, then
\varphi(F)\in\mathbb{D}^{1,p}(E_{2})
and

\mathscr{D}(\varphi(F))=D\varphi(F)\circ\mathscr{D}F.

Proof.

See
[
15
, Proposition 3.8]
.
∎

The adjoint operator of the Malliavin derivative is denoted by
\delta
and is often called the
Skorohod integral
.
We remark that this is not to be confused with the evaluation functional for which we used the same notation.
The domain of
\delta
is the set of random variables
u\in L^{2}(\Omega;E\otimes H)
for which there exists a constant
C\geq 0
such that

\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\mathscr{D}F,u\rangle_{E\otimes H}\right]\right|\leq C\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\|F\|_{E}^{2}\right]\right)^{1/2},

for all
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(E)
.
For all
u\in\text{dom}(\delta)
and
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(E)
, the following relation holds

\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\mathscr{D}F,u\rangle_{E\otimes H}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\langle F,\delta(u)\rangle_{E}\right].

(3.6)

The following lemmas are stated for the case
E=\mathbb{R}
, since we will only need them in this setting.
In this case,
\delta
is an unbounded operator from
L^{2}(\Omega;H)
to
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
The first lemma gives a formula for the Skorohod integral of the product of a real-valued random variable and an
H
-valued random variable.
For a more general formulation of this lemma, see
[
15
, Lemma 4.9]
.

Lemma 3.2

(Integration by parts)
.

Let
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R})
and
u\in\text{dom}\,\delta
such that
Fu\in L^{2}(\Omega;H)
. Then
Fu\in\text{dom}\,\delta
and the following holds:

\delta(Fu)=F\delta(u)-\langle\mathscr{D}F,u\rangle_{H}.

Proof.

See
[
12
, Proposition 1.3.3]
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
u\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(H)
. Then
u\in\text{dom}\,\delta
and the following estimate holds:

\|\delta(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\|u\|_{\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(H)}^{2}=\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;H)}^{2}+\|\mathscr{D}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;H\otimes H)}^{2}.

Proof.

See
[
12
, Proposition 1.3.1]
.
∎

3.2
Sensitivity of
\Pi_{0}
with respect to
x_{0}
,
y_{0}
and
\eta

We now return to the computation of the directional derivatives of
\Pi_{0}
defined in (
3.4
) and (
3.5
).
In the framework of the previous subsection, we choose
H
to be the Filipović space
H_{w}
, and as the Hilbert space
E
we will use either
\mathbb{R}
,
H_{w}
or
\mathcal{H}_{w}
, depending on the context.
Let
(\Omega^{B,W},\mathcal{F}^{B,W},\mathbb{P}^{B,W})
denote the probability space on which
B
and
W
are defined.
To apply the Malliavin calculus, we introduce an isonormal Gaussian process
\mathbb{W}
on a filtered probability space
(\Omega^{\mathbb{W}},\mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{W}},\mathbb{F}^{\mathbb{W}},\mathbb{P}^{\mathbb{W}})
and
H_{w}
, where
\mathbb{P}^{\mathbb{W}}
is independent of
\mathbb{P}^{B,W}
,
\mathbb{F}^{\mathbb{W}}=\{\mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq T}
is the filtration generated by
\mathbb{W}
,
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{W}}=\mathcal{F}_{T}^{\mathbb{W}}
.
Note that
\mathbb{W}
is independent of
B
and
W
.
For a random variable
F
, we can write
F(\omega)=F(\omega^{B,W},\omega^{\mathbb{W}})
.
When we talk about the Malliavin derivative of
F
, we mean the Malliavin derivative of
F
with respect to
\mathbb{W}
.

For fixed
\omega^{B,W}\in\Omega^{B,W}
and
\omega^{\mathbb{W}}\in\Omega^{\mathbb{W}}
, we consider
X_{\tau}(\omega^{B,W},\omega^{\mathbb{W}}):H_{w}\times H_{w}\times\mathcal{H}_{w}\rightarrow H_{w}
as a function of the initial value
x_{0}
of
X_{\tau}
, the initial value
y_{0}
of
Y_{t}
and the volatility
\eta
of
Y_{t}
.
Recall that

X_{\tau}(x_{0},y_{0},\eta)=\mathcal{S}_{\tau}x_{0}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{S}_{\tau-s}\left(Z_{s}\otimes\left(\mathcal{U}_{s}y_{0}+\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{U}_{s-u}\eta\,dW_{u}\right)\right)\,dB_{s}\quad\omega-a.e,

where
\mathcal{S}_{t}
is the right-shift operator.
Following the notation introduced previously, we let
X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}
,
X_{\tau}^{x_{0},\eta}
and
X_{\tau}^{x_{0},y_{0}}
denote
X_{\tau}
as a function of
x_{0}
,
y_{0}
and
\eta
respectively, keeping the two other variables fixed.
The Fréchet derivatives of
X_{\tau}
are given in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4

(Fréchet derivatives of
X_{\tau}
)
.

The Fréchet derivatives of
X_{\tau}
with respect to
x_{0}
,
y_{0}
and
\eta
are given by:

(i)

DX_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0})(h)=\mathcal{S}_{\tau}(h)
,
h\in H_{w}
.

(ii)

DX_{\tau}^{x_{0},\eta}(y_{0})(h)=\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{S}_{\tau-s}\left(Z_{s}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{s}h\right)\,dB_{s}
,
\omega-a.e.
,
h\in H_{w}
.

(iii)

DX_{\tau}^{x_{0},y_{0}}(\eta)(\zeta)=\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{S}_{\tau-s}\left(Z_{s}\otimes\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{U}_{s-u}\zeta\,dW_{u}\right)\,dB_{s}
,
\omega-a.e.
,
\zeta\in\mathcal{H}_{w}
.

Proof.

(i)

The Fréchet derivative
DX_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0})
of
X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}
at
x_{0}\in H_{w}
is defined as the bounded linear operator
L:H_{w}\rightarrow H_{w}
such that

\lim_{\|h\|_{w}\rightarrow 0}\frac{\|X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}+h)-X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0})-Lh\|_{w}}{\|h\|_{w}}=0\quad\omega-a.e.

Since
X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}+h)-X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0})=\mathcal{S}_{\tau}(h)
, the result follows.

(ii)

Since

X_{\tau}^{x_{0},\eta}(y_{0}+h)-X_{\tau}^{x_{0},\eta}(y_{0})=\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{S}_{\tau-s}\left(Z_{s}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{s}h\right)\,dB_{s}\quad\omega-a.e.,

for
y_{0},h\in H_{w}
, the result follows from the definition of the Fréchet derivative.

(iii)

Since

X_{\tau}^{x_{0},y_{0}}(\eta+\zeta)-X_{\tau}^{x_{0},y_{0}}(\eta)=\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{S}_{\tau-s}\left(Z_{s}\otimes\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{U}_{s-u}\zeta\,dW_{u}\right)\,dB_{s}\quad\omega-a.e.,

for
\eta,\zeta\in\mathcal{H}_{w}
, the result follows from the definition of the Fréchet derivative.

∎

Assuming that
\Psi
(see (
3.3
)) is Fréchet differentiable, the composition
\Psi\circ X_{\tau}(\omega):H_{w}\times H_{w}\times\mathcal{H}_{w}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is also Fréchet differentiable with respect to each of the variables.
For
h\in H_{w}
, it holds that

D(\Psi\circ X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta})(x_{0})(h)=\partial_{h}(\Psi\circ X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta})(x_{0})=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}+\varepsilon h))-\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}))}{\varepsilon}\quad\omega-a.e.

Taking expectations, we have that

\mathbb{E}\left[D(\Psi\circ X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta})(x_{0})(h)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}+\varepsilon h))-\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}))}{\varepsilon}\right].

From (
3.4
) we recall that

\partial_{h}\Pi_{0}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0})=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{\Pi_{0}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}+\varepsilon h)-\Pi_{0}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0})}{\varepsilon}=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}+\varepsilon h))-\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}))}{\varepsilon}\right].

The following lemma shows that we can move the limit inside the expectation, and hence express the directional derivatives of
\Pi_{0}
in terms of the Fréchet derivatives of
\Psi\circ X_{\tau}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume that
\Psi
is Fréchet differentiable. Then the following holds:

(i)

\partial_{h}\Pi_{0}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0})=\mathbb{E}\left[D(\Psi\circ X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta})(x_{0})(h)\right]
,
h\in H_{w}
.

(ii)

\partial_{h}\Pi_{0}^{x_{0},\eta}(y_{0})=\mathbb{E}\left[D(\Psi\circ X_{\tau}^{x_{0},\eta})(y_{0})(h)\right]
,
h\in H_{w}
.

(iii)

\partial_{\zeta}\Pi_{0}^{x_{0},y_{0}}(\eta)=\mathbb{E}\left[D(\Psi\circ X_{\tau}^{x_{0},y_{0}})(\eta)(\zeta)\right]
,
\zeta\in\mathcal{H}_{w}
.

Proof.

Let
L_{\Psi}
denote the Lipschitz constant of
\Psi
.

(i):
For each
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
and for almost all
\omega\in\Omega
, we have that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}+\varepsilon h))-\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}))}{\varepsilon}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L_{\Psi}\|X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}+\varepsilon h)-X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0})\|_{w}}{|\varepsilon|}

\displaystyle=\frac{L_{\Psi}\|\mathcal{S}_{\tau}(x_{0}+\varepsilon h)-\mathcal{S}_{\tau}(x_{0})\|_{w}}{|\varepsilon|}

\displaystyle=\frac{L_{\Psi}\|\mathcal{S}_{\tau}(\varepsilon h)\|_{w}}{|\varepsilon|}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\Psi}\|\mathcal{S}_{\tau}(h)\|_{w}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\Psi}\|\mathcal{S}_{\tau}\|_{\text{op}}\|h\|_{w}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\Psi}C\|h\|_{w},

where the constant
C
is given in Lemma
2.3
.
The desired result then follows by the bounded convergence theorem.

(ii):
For each
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
, we have that

\left|\frac{\Psi(X_{\tau}^{x_{0},\eta}(y_{0}+\varepsilon h))-\Psi(X_{\tau}^{x_{0},\eta}(y_{0}))}{\varepsilon}\right|\leq\frac{L_{\Psi}\|X_{\tau}^{x_{0},\eta}(y_{0}+\varepsilon h)-X_{\tau}^{x_{0},\eta}(y_{0})\|_{w}}{|\varepsilon|}\eqqcolon A_{\varepsilon},

which means that

-A_{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{\Psi(X_{\tau}^{x_{0},\eta}(y_{0}+\varepsilon h))-\Psi(X_{\tau}^{x_{0},\eta}(y_{0}))}{\varepsilon}\leq A_{\varepsilon}.

Since

DX_{\tau}^{x_{0},\eta}(y_{0})(h)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{X_{\tau}^{x_{0},\eta}(y_{0}+\varepsilon h)-X_{\tau}^{x_{0},\eta}(y_{0})}{\varepsilon}\quad\omega-a.e.,

it follows by the continuity of the norm
\|\cdot\|_{w}
that

\|DX_{\tau}^{x_{0},\eta}(y_{0})(h)\|_{w}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\|X_{\tau}^{x_{0},\eta}(y_{0}+\varepsilon h)-X_{\tau}^{x_{0},\eta}(y_{0})\|_{w}}{|\varepsilon|}.

This gives

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}A_{\varepsilon}=L_{\Psi}\|DX_{\tau}^{x_{0},\eta}(y_{0})(h)\|_{w}\eqqcolon A\quad\omega-a.e.

If we can show that
A_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow A
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
when
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, the desired result will follow by Pratt’s lemma, see
[
14
]
.
To show convergence in
L^{1}(\Omega)
we will use Vitali’s theorem.
We first show that
\|A_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq M
for all
\varepsilon>0
for some constant
M
.
By the Itô isometry and Lemma
2.3
we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|A_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{L_{\Psi}^{2}\|X_{\tau}^{x_{0},\eta}(y_{0}+\varepsilon h)-X_{\tau}^{x_{0},\eta}(y_{0})\|_{w}^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{L_{\Psi}^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\bigg\|\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{S}_{\tau-s}\left(Z_{s}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{s}(\varepsilon h)\right)\,dB_{s}\bigg\|_{w}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=L_{\Psi}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\bigg\|\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{S}_{\tau-s}\left(Z_{s}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{s}h\right)\,dB_{s}\bigg\|_{w}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=L_{\Psi}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\tau}\|\mathcal{S}_{\tau-s}\left(Z_{s}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{s}h\right)Q_{B}^{1/2}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{w}}^{2}\,ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq L_{\Psi}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\tau}\|\mathcal{S}_{\tau-s}\|_{\text{op}}^{2}\|\left(Z_{s}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{s}h\right)Q_{B}^{1/2}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{w}}^{2}\,ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq L_{\Psi}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\tau}C^{2}\|\left(Z_{s}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{s}h\right)Q_{B}^{1/2}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{w}}^{2}\,ds\right],

since
\|\mathcal{S}_{t}\|_{\text{op}}\leq C
where the constant
C
is given in Lemma
2.3
.
Let
\{e_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an orthonormal basis of
H_{w}
.
By Parseval’s identity we have that

\displaystyle\|\left(Z_{s}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{s}h\right)Q_{B}^{1/2}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{w}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|\left(Z_{s}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{s}h\right)Q_{B}^{1/2}e_{n}\|_{w}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|\langle Z_{s},Q_{B}^{1/2}e_{n}\rangle_{w}\,\mathcal{U}_{s}h\|_{w}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{U}_{s}h\|_{w}^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\langle Z_{s},Q_{B}^{1/2}e_{n}\rangle_{w}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{U}_{s}h\|_{w}^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\langle Q_{B}^{1/2}Z_{s},e_{n}\rangle_{w}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{U}_{s}h\|_{w}^{2}\|Q_{B}^{1/2}Z_{s}\|_{w}^{2}.

By the Hille-Yosida theorem we have that
\|\mathcal{U}_{t}\|_{\text{op}}\leq K{\mathrm{e}}^{kt}
for some constants
K
and
k
.
Hence

\|\mathcal{U}_{s}h\|_{w}^{2}\leq\|\mathcal{U}_{s}\|_{\text{op}}^{2}\|h\|_{w}^{2}\leq K^{2}{\mathrm{e}}^{2ks}\|h\|_{w}^{2}.

Let
\{v_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the orthonormal basis of
H_{w}
consisting of eigenvectors of
Q_{B}
with corresponding eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
We know that such a basis exists since
Q_{B}
is a symmetric, positive definite trace class operator.
By Parseval’s identity and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we find that

\displaystyle\|Q_{B}^{1/2}Z_{s}\|_{w}^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle Q_{B}^{1/2}Z_{s},Q_{B}^{1/2}Z_{s}\rangle_{w}=\langle Q_{B}Z_{s},Z_{s}\rangle_{w}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle Z_{s},Q_{B}v_{n}\rangle_{w}\langle Z_{s},v_{n}\rangle_{w}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\langle Z_{s},v_{n}\rangle_{w}^{2}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\|Z_{s}\|_{w}^{2}\|v_{n}\|_{w}^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}=\text{Tr}(Q_{B}).

Hence we have that

\mathbb{E}\left[|A_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right]\leq L_{\Psi}^{2}C^{2}K^{2}\text{Tr}(Q_{B})\|h\|_{w}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\mathrm{e}}^{2c(\tau-s)}e^{2ks}\,ds=:M.

For a fixed
\delta>0
, it then holds by Hölder’s inequality and Markov’s inequality that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{|\varepsilon|<\delta}\mathbb{E}\left[|A_{\varepsilon}|1_{\{|A_{\varepsilon}|>N\}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{|\varepsilon|<\delta}\mathbb{E}\left[|A_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right]^{1/2}\mathbb{E}\left[1_{\{|A_{\varepsilon}|>N\}}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{|\varepsilon|<\delta}\|A_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\sqrt{\mathbb{P}(|A_{\varepsilon}|>N)}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{|\varepsilon|<\delta}\|A_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\frac{\|A_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}{N}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{|\varepsilon|<\delta}\frac{\|A_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}{N}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{M^{2}}{N}

\displaystyle=0.

By Vitali’s theorem it follows that
\mathbb{E}[|A_{\varepsilon}-A|]\rightarrow 0
when
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, and by Pratt’s lemma it then follows that

\partial_{h}V_{2}(y_{0})=\mathbb{E}\left[D(\Psi\circ X_{\tau}^{x_{0},\eta})(y_{0})(h)\right].

(iii):
This proof follows the same approach as the proof of (ii).
For each
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
, we have that

\left|\frac{\Psi(X_{\tau}^{x_{0},y_{0}}(\eta+\varepsilon\zeta))-\Psi(X_{\tau}^{x_{0},y_{0}}(\eta))}{\varepsilon}\right|\leq\frac{L_{\Psi}\|X_{\tau}^{x_{0},y_{0}}(\eta+\varepsilon\zeta)-X_{\tau}^{x_{0},y_{0}}(\eta)\|_{w}}{|\varepsilon|}\eqqcolon A_{\varepsilon},

which gives

-A_{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{\Psi(X_{\tau}^{x_{0},y_{0}}(\eta+\varepsilon\zeta))-\Psi(X_{\tau}^{x_{0},y_{0}}(\eta))}{\varepsilon}\leq A_{\varepsilon}.

By the continuity of the norm
\|\cdot\|_{w}
it follows that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}A_{\varepsilon}=L_{\Psi}\|DX_{\tau}^{x_{0},y_{0}}(\eta)(\zeta)\|_{w}\eqqcolon A\quad\omega-a.e.

We show that
\|A_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq M
for all
\varepsilon>0
for some constant
M
.
By the Itô isometry, the Hille-Yosida thorem and Fubini we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|A_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{L_{\Psi}^{2}\|X_{\tau}^{x_{0},y_{0}}(\eta+\varepsilon\zeta)-X_{\tau}^{x_{0},y_{0}}(\eta)\|_{w}^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{L_{\Psi}^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\bigg\|\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{S}_{\tau-s}\left(Z_{s}\otimes\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{U}_{s-u}\varepsilon\zeta\,dW_{u}\right)\,dB_{s}\bigg\|_{w}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=L_{\Psi}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\bigg\|\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{S}_{\tau-s}\left(Z_{s}\otimes\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{U}_{s-u}\zeta\,dW_{u}\right)\,dB_{s}\bigg\|_{w}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=L_{\Psi}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\|\mathcal{S}_{\tau-s}\left(Z_{s}\otimes\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{U}_{s-u}\zeta\,dW_{u}\right)Q_{B}^{1/2}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{w}}^{2}\,ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq L_{\Psi}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\tau}\|\mathcal{S}_{\tau-s}\|_{\text{op}}^{2}\left\|\left(Z_{s}\otimes\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{U}_{s-u}\zeta\,dW_{u}\right)Q_{B}^{1/2}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{w}}^{2}\,ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq L_{\Psi}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\tau}C^{2}{\mathrm{e}}^{2c(\tau-s)}\left\|\left(Z_{s}\otimes\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{U}_{s-u}\zeta\,dW_{u}\right)Q_{B}^{1/2}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{w}}^{2}\,ds\right]

\displaystyle=L_{\Psi}^{2}C^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\mathrm{e}}^{2c(\tau-s)}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\left(Z_{s}\otimes\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{U}_{s-u}\zeta\,dW_{u}\right)Q_{B}^{1/2}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{w}}^{2}\right]\,ds.

Let
\{e_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an orthonormal basis of
H_{w}
.
By Parseval’s identity we have that

\displaystyle\left\|\left(Z_{s}\otimes\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{U}_{s-u}\zeta\,dW_{u}\right)Q_{B}^{1/2}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{w}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\|\left(Z_{s}\otimes\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{U}_{s-u}\zeta\,dW_{u}\right)Q_{B}^{1/2}e_{n}\right\|_{w}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\|\langle Z_{s},Q_{B}^{1/2}e_{n}\rangle_{w}\,\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{U}_{s-u}\zeta\,dW_{u}\right\|_{w}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{U}_{s-u}\zeta\,dW_{u}\right\|_{w}^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\langle Z_{s},Q_{B}^{1/2}e_{n}\rangle_{w}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{U}_{s-u}\zeta\,dW_{u}\right\|_{w}^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\langle Q_{B}^{1/2}Z_{s},e_{n}\rangle_{w}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{U}_{s-u}\zeta\,dW_{u}\right\|_{w}^{2}\left\|Q_{B}^{1/2}Z_{s}\right\|_{w}^{2}.

By the Itô isometry and the Hille-Yosida theorem we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{U}_{s-u}\zeta\,dW_{u}\right\|_{w}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{s}\left\|\mathcal{U}_{s-u}\zeta Q_{W}^{1/2}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{w}}^{2}du\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{s}\|\mathcal{U}_{s-u}\|_{\text{op}}^{2}\|\zeta\|_{\text{op}}^{2}\|Q_{W}^{1/2}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{w}}^{2}du\right]

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}(Q_{W})\|\zeta\|_{\text{op}}^{2}\int_{0}^{s}\|\mathcal{U}_{s-u}\|_{\text{op}}^{2}du

\displaystyle\leq\text{Tr}(Q_{W})\|\zeta\|_{\text{op}}^{2}K^{2}\int_{0}^{s}e^{2k(s-u)}du.

Since
\|Q_{B}^{1/2}Z_{s}\|_{w}^{2}\leq\text{Tr}(Q_{B})
, it follows that

\mathbb{E}\left[|A_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right]\leq L_{\Psi}^{2}C^{2}K^{2}\text{Tr}(Q_{B})\text{Tr}(Q_{W})\|\zeta\|_{\text{op}}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{0}^{s}{\mathrm{e}}^{2c(\tau-s)}e^{2k(s-u)}\,duds=:M.

We have now shown that there exists a constant
M
such that
\|A_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq M
for all
\varepsilon>0
.
By the same argument as in the proof of (ii), it then follows from Vitali’s theorem that
A_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow A
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
when
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.
The desired result then follows by Pratt’s lemma.
∎

The following lemma gives expressions for the expectation of the Fréchet derivatives of
\Psi\circ X_{\tau}
.
The trick we use to compute these Fréchet derivatives is to randomize the parameter that we want to differentiate with respect to with an
H_{w}
-valued noise independent of
B
and
W
.
By applying the chain rule we can then express the Malliavin derivative with respect to this noise in terms of the Fréchet derivative that we want to compute.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\xi
be a real-valued random variable on
(\Omega^{\mathbb{W}},\mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{W}},\mathbb{P}^{\mathbb{W}})
.
Assume that
\xi\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
and that
\mathscr{D}\xi(\omega,x)\neq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and almost all
\omega\in\Omega
.
Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, and assume that
\frac{\xi}{\mathscr{D}\xi(x)}h_{x}
is Skorohod integrable and that the evaluation of the Skorohod integral at
\lambda=\frac{1}{\xi}
is well defined.
Assume also that the Skorohod integrals below and their evaluations at
\lambda=\frac{1}{\xi}
are well defined.
Then it holds that

(i)

\mathbb{E}\left[D(\Psi\circ X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta})(x_{0})(h)\right]=-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\{\delta\left(\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}-h+\lambda\xi h))\frac{\xi}{\mathscr{D}\xi(x)}h_{x}\right)\right\}\bigg|_{\lambda=\frac{1}{\xi}}\right]
,

(ii)

\mathbb{E}\left[D(\Psi\circ X_{\tau}^{x_{0},\eta})(y_{0})(h)\right]=-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\{\delta\left(\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(y_{0}-h+\lambda\xi h))\frac{\xi}{\mathscr{D}\xi(x)}h_{x}\right)\right\}\bigg|_{\lambda=\frac{1}{\xi}}\right]
,

(iii)

\mathbb{E}\left[D(\Psi\circ X_{\tau}^{x_{0},y_{0}})(\eta)(\zeta)\right]=-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\{\delta\left(\Psi(X_{\tau}^{x_{0},y_{0}}(\eta-\zeta+\lambda\xi\zeta))\frac{\xi}{\mathscr{D}\xi(x)}h_{x}\right)\right\}\bigg|_{\lambda=\frac{1}{\xi}}\right]
.

Proof.

We only prove (i) here. The proofs of (ii) and (iii) follow the same approach.
For (iii), note that we can still use the chain rule in Theorem
3.1
since we assumed
\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{w}
.
For a general
\eta\in L(H_{w})
we can not use the chain rule
[
15
, Proposition 3.8]
since
L(H_{w})
is not reflexive and hence not a UMD Banach space.

Define first a function
\theta:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow H_{w}
by
\theta(y)=x_{0}-h+\lambda yh
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
h\in H_{w}
.
The randomized initial path is then defined as
X_{0}\coloneqq\theta(\xi)
.
The Fréchet derivative
D\theta(y)
of
\theta
at
y
is the bounded linear operator
D\theta(y):\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow H_{w}
given by
D\theta(y)(x)=\lambda xh
.
By the chain rule in Lemma
3.1
it holds that
\theta(\xi)\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(H_{w})
and

\mathscr{D}X_{0}=\mathscr{D}(\theta(\xi))=D\theta(\xi)\circ\mathscr{D}\xi\qquad\omega-a.e.

(3.7)

The Malliavin derivative
\mathscr{D}\xi
is an
H_{w}
-valued random variable.
For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
we have that

\mathscr{D}X_{0}(\omega,x)=D\theta(\xi(\omega))(\mathscr{D}\xi(\omega,x))=\lambda\mathscr{D}\xi(\omega,x)h\qquad\omega-a.e.

(3.8)

By the chain rule in Lemma
3.1
it holds that
X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(X_{0})\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(H_{w})
.
Since the Fréchet derivative of
\Psi
is also bounded and continuous by assumption,
we also have
\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(X_{0}))\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
by the chain rule.
The chain rule in Lemma
3.1
also gives that

\mathscr{D}(\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(X_{0})))=D\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(X_{0}))\circ DX_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(X_{0})\circ D\theta(\xi)\circ\mathscr{D}\xi\qquad\omega-a.e.,

(3.9)

and therefore for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and for almost all
\omega\in\Omega
it holds that

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}(\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(X_{0})))(\omega,x)

\displaystyle=D\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(X_{0}(\omega)))\big(DX_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(X_{0}(\omega))(D\theta(\xi(\omega))(\mathscr{D}\xi(\omega,x)))\big)

\displaystyle=D\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(X_{0}(\omega)))\big(DX_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(X_{0}(\omega))(\lambda\mathscr{D}\xi(\omega,x)h)\big)

\displaystyle=\lambda\mathscr{D}\xi(\omega,x)D\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(X_{0}(\omega)))\big(DX_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(X_{0}(\omega))(h)\big),

where we used (
3.8
) and then factored out the scalar
\lambda\mathscr{D}\xi(\omega,x)
since the Fréchet derivative is linear.
By assumption,
\mathscr{D}\xi(\omega,x)\neq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and almost all
\omega\in\Omega
.
Multiplying with
\frac{\xi(\omega)}{\mathscr{D}\xi(\omega,x)}

\frac{\xi(\omega)}{\mathscr{D}\xi(\omega,x)}\mathscr{D}(\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(X_{0})))(\omega,x)=\lambda\xi(\omega)D\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(X_{0}(\omega)))(DX_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(X_{0}(\omega))(h))\qquad\omega-a.e.

For a fixed pair
(\omega,x)

\lambda=\frac{1}{\xi(\omega)}

X_{0}=x_{0}-h+\lambda\xi h
.
It holds for almost all
\omega\in\Omega

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{\xi(\omega)}{\mathscr{D}\xi(\omega,x)}\mathscr{D}(\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(X_{0})))(\omega,x)\right\}\bigg|_{\lambda=\frac{1}{\xi(\omega)}}

\displaystyle=\lambda\xi(\omega)D\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}-h+\lambda\xi(\omega)h))(DX_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}-h+\lambda\xi(\omega)h)(h))\bigg|_{\lambda=\frac{1}{\xi(\omega)}}

\displaystyle=D\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}))(DX_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0})(h)).

3.10
) and the fact that evaluating a function in the Filipović space
H_{w}

corresponds to taking the inner product with the function

defined in (

\omega\in\Omega

\displaystyle D(\Psi\circ X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta})(x_{0})(h)

\displaystyle=D\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}))(DX_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0})(h))

\displaystyle=\left\{\frac{\xi}{\mathscr{D}\xi(x)}\mathscr{D}(\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(X_{0})))(x)\right\}\bigg|_{\lambda=\frac{1}{\xi}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\frac{\xi}{\mathscr{D}\xi(x)}\Big\langle\mathscr{D}(\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}-h+\lambda\xi h))),h_{x}\Big\rangle_{w}\right\}\bigg|_{\lambda=\frac{1}{\xi}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathscr{D}(\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}-h+\lambda\xi h))),\frac{\xi}{\mathscr{D}\xi(x)}h_{x}\right\rangle_{w}\bigg|_{\lambda=\frac{1}{\xi}}.

Finally we compute the expectation of the Fréchet derivative above. Note that since we have an evaluation in the expression above, we can not apply the duality formula (
3.6
) here.
We apply instead Lemma
3.2
with
F=\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(X_{0}))

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[D(\Psi\circ X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta})(x_{0})(h)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\mathscr{D}(\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}-h+\lambda\xi h))),\frac{\xi}{\mathscr{D}\xi(x)}h_{x}\right\rangle_{w}\bigg|_{\lambda=\frac{1}{\xi}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\{\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}-h+\lambda\xi h))\delta\left(\frac{\xi}{\mathscr{D}\xi(x)}h_{x}\right)-\delta\left(\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}-h+\lambda\xi h))\frac{\xi}{\mathscr{D}\xi(x)}h_{x}\right)\right\}\bigg|_{\lambda=\frac{1}{\xi}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}))\delta\left(\frac{\xi}{\mathscr{D}\xi(x)}h_{x}\right)-\left\{\delta\left(\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}-h+\lambda\xi h))\frac{\xi}{\mathscr{D}\xi(x)}h_{x}\right)\right\}\bigg|_{\lambda=\frac{1}{\xi}}\right]

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\{\delta\left(\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}-h+\lambda\xi h))\frac{\xi}{\mathscr{D}\xi(x)}h_{x}\right)\right\}\bigg|_{\lambda=\frac{1}{\xi}}\right],

where we in last equality used that
\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}))
and
\delta\left(\frac{\xi}{\mathscr{D}\xi(x)}h_{x}\right)

\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}))

\mathcal{F}^{B}
-measurable and

\mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{W}}

For all

\xi=\exp(\mathbb{W}(1_{[0,T]}))

Then
\xi\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{+})

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|\mathscr{D}\big(\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}-h+\lambda_{1}\xi h))-\Psi(X_{\tau}^{y_{0},\eta}(x_{0}-h+\lambda_{2}\xi h))\big)(y)\big|^{2}\Big]

, we then have

3.11
) is satisfied and the result follows by Lemma

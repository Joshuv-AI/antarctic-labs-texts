Unique positive solutions to q q -discrete equations associated with orthogonal polynomials
T. Lasic Latimer a

Abstract
Boelen et al. [ 3 ] deduced a q q -discrete Painlevé equation satisfied by the recurrence coefficients of orthogonal polynomials and conjectured that the equation had a unique positive solution. We prove their conjecture and discuss related work in the area, highlighting a potentially unifying methodology.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the initial value problem (IVP)

\begin{cases}&a_{2k}(a_{2k+1}+q^{1-2k-\kappa}a_{2k}+q^{2}a_{2k-1}+q^{-4k+3-\kappa}a_{2k+1}a_{2k}a_{2k-1})\\
&\phantom{a_{2k}(a_{2k+1}+}=(1-q^{2k})q^{\kappa+2k-1},\\
&a_{2k+1}(a_{2k+2}+q^{-2k}a_{2k+1}+q^{2}a_{2k}+q^{-4k+1-\kappa}a_{2k+2}a_{2k+1}a_{2k})\\
&\phantom{a_{2k}(a_{2k+1}+}=(q^{-\kappa}-q^{2k+1})q^{\kappa+2k},\\
&a_{k}=0\;\text{for}\;k<1,\end{cases}

(1)

where
\kappa\geq 0
,
0<q<1
and
a_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
. Boelen
et al.

[
3
]
showed that a solution
\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
arises as coefficients of a 3-term recurrence relation satisfied by
q
-orthogonal polynomials and conjectured that this IVP must have a unique positive solution. We prove this result in Theorem
3.5
of this paper.

Let
\{P_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be monic polynomials orthogonal with respect to a weight function
w(x)
on an interval
I
. Taking
P_{0}=1
, and assuming
P_{-k}\equiv 0
for positive integer
k
, these polynomials satisfy a 3-term recurrence relation

x\,P_{n}(x)=P_{n+1}+b_{n}\,P_{n}(x)+a_{n}\,P_{n-1}(x),\quad n\geq 0,

where
b_{n}
and
a_{n}
are independent of
x
. Classical cases give rise to explicit coefficients. For example,
b_{n}=0
,
a_{n}=n
for Hermite polynomials
He_{n}(x)
.

Orthogonal polynomials that lie outside the classical case have been extensively studied. The semi-classical case arises under certain generalisations of conditions on the weight function
[
10
,
7
]
. In such cases, the coefficient
a_{n}
turns out to satisfy nonlinear IVPs, which are known not to have explicit solutions. The search for unique positive solutions of such IVPs has been carried out for polynomials orthogonal with respect to continuous measures. In this paper, we consider polynomials orthogonal with respect to a
q
-discrete measure
|x|^{\kappa}(q^{4}x^{4};q^{4})_{\infty}d_{q}x
.

1.1
Brief Literature Overview

Although recurrence relations arise from orthogonal polynomials, methods for proving the existence of unique positive solutions need not originate from orthogonal polynomials. Such methods are often specific to the problem at hand and do not easily generalise, particularly to higher dimensional discrete equations. This is highlighted by Alsulami
et al.

[
1
]
who recently proved that

a_{n}(\sigma_{n,1}a_{n+1}+\sigma_{n,0}a_{n}+\sigma_{n,-1}a_{n-1})+\kappa_{n}a_{n}=l_{n}\,,

(2)

has a unique positive solution, under certain constraints on
l_{n}
,
\sigma_{n,i}
and
\kappa_{n}
. Equation (
2
) is a non-linear equation which does not arise from orthogonal polynomials. However, it contains

a_{n}(a_{n+1}+a_{n}+a_{n-1})+\rho a_{n}=\begin{cases}n+\kappa,&n\,\text{even},\\
n,&n\,\text{odd},\end{cases}

(3)

as a special case. Equation (
3
) is satisfied by the recurrence coefficients of polynomials orthogonal with respect to the weight
|x|^{\kappa}e^{-x^{4}/4-\rho x^{2}/2}dx
. The proof used is independent of orthogonal polynomial theory and allows for the general form of Equation (
2
) but does not easily generalise other types of discrete equations.

Similarly, Clarkson
et al.

[
5
]
recently prove that the recurrence relation satisfied by polynomials orthogonal with respect to an exponential cubic weight,

\displaystyle a_{n}+a_{n+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{n}^{2}-t\,,

(4a)

\displaystyle a_{n}(b_{n}+b_{n-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\,,

(4b)

has a unique positive solution. Their proof also does not recognise the discrete equation arises from orthogonal polynomials.

The method presented in this paper allows one to show the existence of a unique positive solution to a large class of recurrence relations, arising from both
q
-discrete and continuous measures. In this way we extend the results of Magnus
[
9
]
to the
q
-discrete setting. Magnus, adding to the earlier work of Bonan and Nevai
[
4
]
, proved that recurrence relations satisfied by polynomials orthogonal with respect to weights of the form
e^{P(x)}dx
, where
P(x)
is an even polynomial with negative leading order, have a unique positive solution. The proof was restricted to smooth weights and thus could not be extended to the
q
-discrete setting.

Remark 1

Unless otherwise stated, any discrete equation

Definition 2.1
.

The
q

D_{q}f(x)=\frac{f(x)-f(qx)}{x(1-q)}\,.

Note that

D_{q}(xf(x))=qxD_{q}f(x)+f(x)\,.

Definition 2.2

We define

[n]_{q}=\frac{1-q^{n}}{1-q}\,.

Note that

D_{q}x^{n}=[n]_{q}x^{n-1}\,.

Definition 2.3
.

The Pochammer symbol
(a;q)_{\infty}
is defined as

(a;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{j})\,.

Definition 2.4
.

The
q
integral of
f(x)
from -1 to 1 is defined as

\int_{-1}^{1}f(x)d_{q}x=(1-q)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(f(q^{k})+f(-q^{k}))q^{k}\,.

Shifting from
q
-algebra we now look to recall the famous Favard’s Theorem.

Theorem 2.5

(Favard’s Theorem
[
7
,
2
, Chapter 2 Theorem 1.5]
)
.

Suppose we are given a sequence of polynomials
\{P_{n}(x)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
and real numbers
\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
with
a_{n}>0
, which satisfy

x\,P_{n}=P_{n+1}+a_{n}P_{n-1},

where
P_{0}=1
, and

. Then there exists a positive measure
\mu
on the real line, such that these polynomials are orthogonal in
L^{2}(\mu)
.

We also recall a theorem on moment determinate measures and prove a consequential lemma.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\mu
be a positive measure on
\mathbb{R}^{+}:[0,\infty)
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}e^{t|x|}d\mu(x)<\infty,

for where
t>0
is a constant. Then
\mu
is uniquely determined by the sequence of its moments,
\mu_{k}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}x^{k}d\mu(x)
, for
k
in
\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}

[
8
, Theorem 2]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mu
be a positive measure on the real line such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{tx^{2k}}d\mu(x)<\infty,

for some
k
in
\mathbb{N}
, and constant
t>0
. Then
\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2}d\mu(x)
is determined by the sub-sequence of its moments given by

\mu_{2kj}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2kj}d\mu(x)\,\quad\text{for $j$ in $\mathbb{N}$}.

Proof.

The map
f:x\rightarrow u
is defined as

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{2k}\,.

Thus,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}e^{cu}d\mu_{f}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{cx^{2k}}d\mu(x)<\infty\,,

where
\mu_{f}(u)=\mu(f^{-1}(u))
. Applying Theorem
2.6

\mu_{f}
is uniquely determined by its moments,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}u^{j}d\mu_{f}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2kj}d\mu(x)=\mu_{2kj}\,.

It follows that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2}d\mu(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}u^{\frac{1}{k}}d\mu_{f}(u)\,,

is determined by
\mu_{2kj}
.

(1-q^{n+\kappa+1})\int x^{n}d\mu=\int x^{n+4}d\mu,\quad\mathrm{for}\,n\geq 0\,.

Re-arranging we arrive at

\int x^{n}(1-x^{4})d\mu=q^{n+\kappa+1}\int x^{n}d\mu\quad\mathrm{for}\,n\geq 0\,.

(18)

Letting
n=2k
be even and taking the limit as
k
approaches infinity gives

\lim_{k\to\infty}x^{2k}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }x\in(-1,1),\\
+\infty,&\text{if }x\notin[-1,1].\end{cases}

Thus, the left hand side of Equation (
18
) approaches negative infinity if
\mu
is supported outside of
[-1,1]
. However, as
\mu
is positive, the right hand side of Equation (
18
) is positive. Hence,
\mu
is supported in
[-1,1]
.

We can now combine Theorem
3.3
and Lemma
2.7
to prove the main result of this paper.

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose we have a sequence of positive numbers
\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
which satisfy the IVP Equation (
1
). Then

a_{1}=\int_{-1}^{1}x^{2}w(x)d_{q}x\,,

where
w(x)
is defined as

w(x)=\frac{((qx)^{4};q^{4})_{\infty}|x|^{\kappa}}{(1-q)^{\frac{\kappa}{4}}}\,.

(19)

Proof.

Boelen
et al.

[
3
]
prove that
w(x)d_{q}x
satisfies
Theorem
3.3
. Let
\mu
be another normalised measure which satisfies Theorem
3.3
, for an arbitrary positive solution,
\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, to Equation (
1
). By the monotone convergence theorem and Theorem
3.3
we have

\int e^{c(x^{4})}d\mu(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int\frac{c^{n}x^{4n}}{n!}d\mu(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int\frac{c^{n}x^{4n}}{n!}w(x)d_{q}x=\int e^{c(x^{4})}w(x)d_{q}x\,.

The support of
w(x)
is compact, therefore

\int e^{c(x^{2k})}d\mu(x)=\int e^{c(x^{2k})}w(x)d_{q}x<\infty\,.

Applying Lemma
2.7
we find

\int x^{2}d\mu(x)=\int x^{2}w(x)d_{q}x\,.

Taking the integral with respect to
\mu
of Equation (
13
), when
n=1
, we see that

\int x^{2}d\mu(x)=a_{1}\,.

Thus,
a_{1}=\int x^{2}w(x)d_{q}x
.
∎

4
Results and Discussion

The main result of this paper is Theorem
3.5
, the proof can be split into two parts.

1.

First, we show that if the recurrence coefficients of a set of monic polynomials,
\{P_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, satisfy Equation (
1
), then
\int x^{4j}d\mu
is independent of the measure
\mu
for all
j
in
\mathbb{N}
, where
\mu
is a positive measure such that
\int P_{i}P_{k}d\mu=h_{i}\delta_{i,k}
.

2.

Second, we show that if for some
p
in
\mathbb{N}
,
m_{j}=\int x^{2pj}d\mu
is known for all
j
and decays sufficiently fast, then
\int x^{2}d\mu
is uniquely determined by
\{m_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
.

The same method of proof applies to other non-linear discrete equations. For example, the method can be applied to continuous orthogonal polynomials. Equation (
3
) can be shown to have a unique positive solution by directly applying the theory presented in this paper to the continuous case. With a slight modification to the first step of the proof, the existence of a unique positive solution to higher order, continuous and
q
-discrete, non-linear discrete equations can also be shown. There is also the possibility of applying the presented proof to non-linear discrete equations which do not arise from orthogonal polynomials.

To enable an extension, an interesting direction would be the long open question, what subset of moments are needed to show a measure is unique? This would enable a proof of similar results for discrete equations satisfied by the recurrence coefficients of polynomials orthogonal to measures of the form
|x|^{\kappa}e^{P(x)}dx
, where
P(x)
is a polynomial of even degree with negative leading order. Likewise, it would enable a proof for the
q
-discrete analogue, and possibly the
d
-discrete case. In particular, such an extension would confirm a similar conjecture by Boelen
et al.

[
3
]
for a generalised form of Equation (
1
), which remains an open problem.

A boson-fermion correspondence in cohomological Donaldson–Thomas theory
Ben Davison

Abstract
. We introduce and study a fermionization procedure for the cohomological Hall algebra ℋ Π Q \mathcal{H}_{\Pi_{Q}} of representations of a preprojective algebra, that selectively switches the cohomological parity of the BPS Lie algebra from even to odd. We do so by determining the cohomological Donaldson–Thomas invariants of central extensions of preprojective algebras studied in the work of Etingof and Rains, via deformed dimensional reduction. Via the same techniques, we determine the Borel–Moore homology of the stack of representations of the μ \upmu -deformed preprojective algebra introduced by Crawley–Boevey and Holland, for all dimension vectors. This provides a common generalisation of the results of Crawley-Boevey and Van den Bergh on the cohomology of smooth moduli schemes of representations of deformed preprojective algebras, and my earlier results on the Borel–Moore homology of the stack of representations of the undeformed preprojective algebra.

1.3.
Main results

In the rest of the paper we prove that the above discussion regarding the (noncommutative) conifold forms part of a general procedure for (selectively) “fermionizing” the cohomological Hall algebras of preprojective algebras. This culminates in the following theorem:

Theorem A
.

Let
\upmu\in\mathbb{C}^{Q_{0}}
. Set
\tilde{W}^{\upmu}=\sum_{a\in Q_{1}}[a,a^{*}]\sum_{i\in Q_{0}}\omega_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in Q_{0}}\upmu_{i}\omega_{i}^{2}

\tilde{Q}
with potential
\tilde{W}^{\upmu}

\poin(\DT_{\tilde{Q},\tilde{W}^{\upmu},\mathbf{d}},q^{1/2})=\begin{cases}\mathtt{a}_{Q,\mathbf{d}}(q^{-1})&\textrm{if }\mathbf{d}\cdot\upmu\neq 0\\
q^{-1/2}\mathtt{a}_{Q,\mathbf{d}}(q^{-1})&\textrm{if }\mathbf{d}\cdot\upmu=0\end{cases}

where
\mathtt{a}_{Q,\mathbf{d}}(q)
are the Kac polynomials for
Q
. In addition, the natural mixed Hodge structure on the cohomological BPS invariants

2.1.
Some algebras from quivers

A quiver is determined by a set of vertices
Q_{0}
, a set of edges
Q_{1}
, and two morphisms
s,t\colon Q_{1}\rightarrow Q_{0}
taking an arrow to its source and target respectively. We always assume that
Q_{0}
and
Q_{1}
are finite. We define the Euler form

(18)

\displaystyle\upchi_{Q}(\cdot,\cdot)\colon

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}\times\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle(\mathbf{d},\mathbf{e})\mapsto\sum_{a\in Q_{1}}\mathbf{d}_{s(a)}\mathbf{d}_{t(a)}-\sum_{i\in Q_{0}}\mathbf{d}_{i}\mathbf{e}_{i}.

Where there is no possibility of confusion, we drop the quiver
Q
from the notation and just write
\upchi(\cdot,\cdot)
. We denote by
\overline{Q}
the doubled quiver of
Q
, obtained by adding an arrow
a^{*}
for every arrow
a\in Q_{1}
, where
a^{*}
has the opposite orientation to
a
. We denote by
\tilde{Q}
the tripled quiver, obtained from

by adding a loop
\omega_{i}
at each vertex

.

Given a ring
A
and a quiver
Q
we denote by
AQ
the free path algebra of
Q
with coefficients in

. We denote by

the semisimple subalgebra spanned by length zero paths, so we may identify

. We denote by

the preprojective algebra for

, defined to be the quotient of the free path algebra
\mathbb{C}\overline{Q}
by the two-sided ideal generated by the element
\sum_{a\in Q_{1}}[a,a^{*}]
. As in the introduction, we denote by
\Pi_{Q}[\omega]
the trivial extension obtained by adjoining a central element

to the algebra

, i.e.
\Pi_{Q}[\omega]=\Pi_{Q}\otimes\mathbb{C}[\omega]

Let
\upmu\in R
. We recall the central extension of

introduced by Etingof and Rains

\Pi_{Q}^{\upmu}=\mathbb{C}[\omega]\overline{Q}/\langle\sum_{a\in Q_{1}}[a,a^{*}]+\upmu\omega\rangle.

There is an obvious isomorphism

\Pi_{Q}^{0}\cong\Pi_{Q}[\omega]

and natural isomorphisms

\displaystyle\Pi_{Q,\upmu}\cong

\displaystyle\Pi_{Q}^{\upmu}/\langle\omega-1\rangle

\displaystyle\Pi_{Q}\cong

\displaystyle\Pi_{Q}^{\upmu}/\langle\omega\rangle,

and the deformed preprojective algebra

In this paper we will study Jacobi algebras obtained from the tripled QP

4.1.
Calculating the BPS sheaves

While our main theorem gives a way to calculate the BPS cohomology

Lemma 4.1
.

For a quiver

\displaystyle\supp(\mathcal{BPS}_{\Pi^{\upmu}_{Q},\mathbf{d}})\subset\begin{cases}\tilde{e}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{d}}(\Pi_{Q})\times\mathbb{A}^{1})&\textrm{if }\upmu\cdot\mathbf{d}=0\\

Proof.

[

, if

Even in the case

4.2.
The BPS algebra

Identifying the BPS cohomology of the Jacobi algebra
\mathbb{C}(\tilde{Q},\tilde{W}^{\upmu})
is only part of understanding its cohomological DT theory; it tells us the size of the graded pieces of

[
DM20
]
regarding general quivers with potential (e.g. the PBW theorem). At least for
\upmu=0
, the algebra

has been studied from various points of view (see e.g.
[

,
MO19

KV19
,

[

GY20

For general

\upmu

carries a Lie algebra structure (by Theorem

). Even in the case
\upmu=0
we do not yet fully understand this Lie algebra for general

\upmu
to the case

, via the following construction:

Fix

\upmu
, and define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{\Even}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}\;\lvert\;\mathbf{d}\cdot\upmu=0}\mathfrak{g}_{\tilde{Q},\tilde{W},\mathbf{d}}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{\Odd}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}\;\lvert\;\mathbf{d}\cdot\upmu\neq 0}\mathfrak{g}_{\tilde{Q},\tilde{W},\mathbf{d}}.

Then

, and
\mathfrak{g}_{\Odd}
is a Lie module for it, and we consider the extension

\mathfrak{g}^{\upmu}=\mathfrak{g}_{\Even}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\Odd}[-1],

with the Lie bracket

[(\upalpha,\upbeta),(\upalpha^{\prime},\upbeta^{\prime})]=([\upalpha,\upalpha^{\prime}],[\upalpha,\upbeta^{\prime}]-[\upalpha^{\prime},\upbeta]).

Question 4.3
.

Is there is an isomorphism of Lie algebras

\mathfrak{g}^{\upmu}\cong\mathfrak{g}_{\tilde{Q},\tilde{W}^{\upmu}}?

Proposition 4.4
.

The answer to Question

is yes in the case
\upmu=0

\upmu
.

Proof.

The case
\upmu=0
is trivial, since then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{\upmu}=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{\Even}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{\tilde{Q},\tilde{W}}

and

. In the case of generic

tells us that there is an isomorphism

\mathfrak{g}_{\tilde{Q},\tilde{W}^{\upmu}}\cong\mathfrak{g}_{\tilde{Q},\tilde{W}}[-1]

\mathfrak{g}_{\tilde{Q},\tilde{W}^{\upmu}}

∎

In this paper, we have ignored completely the question of whether there is a natural double to the Lie algebra

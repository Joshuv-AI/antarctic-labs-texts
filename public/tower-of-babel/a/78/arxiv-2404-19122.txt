Spin Covariance Fluctuations in the SK Model at High Temperature
Christian Brennecke, Adrien Schertzer, Chen Van Dam

Abstract
Based on [ 10 ] , it is well known that the rescaled two point correlation functions N ​ ⟨ σ i ; σ j ⟩ = N ​ ( ⟨ σ i ​ σ j ⟩ − ⟨ σ i ⟩ ​ ⟨ σ j ⟩ ) \sqrt{N}\langle\sigma_{i};\sigma_{j}\rangle=\sqrt{N}\big(\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle-\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle\langle\sigma_{j}\rangle\big) in the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick spin glass model with non-zero external field admit at sufficiently high temperature an explicit non-Gaussian distributional limit as N → ∞ N\to\infty . Inspired by recent results from [ 1 , 3 , 4 ] , we provide a novel proof of the distributional convergence which is based on expanding ⟨ σ i ; σ j ⟩ \langle\sigma_{i};\sigma_{j}\rangle into a sum over suitable weights of self-avoiding paths from vertex i i to j j . Compared to [ 10 ] , our key observation is that the path representation of ⟨ σ i ; σ j ⟩ \langle\sigma_{i};\sigma_{j}\rangle provides a direct explanation of the specific form of the limiting distribution of N ​ ⟨ σ i ; σ j ⟩ \sqrt{N}\langle\sigma_{i};\sigma_{j}\rangle .

Introduction

Consider
N
spins
\sigma_{i}
,
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, with values in
\{-1,1\}
whose interactions are described by the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick (SK)
[
13
]
Hamiltonian
H_{N}:\{-1,1\}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}

H_{N}(\sigma)=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}g_{ij}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}+h\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma_{i}.

(1.1)

Here, the
(g_{ij})_{1\leq i<j\leq N}
are i.i.d. centered Gaussian random variables with variance
tN^{-1}
for
i\neq j
and we set
g_{ii}=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,N
. The parameter
t\geq 0
tunes the interaction strength and
h\in\mathbb{R}
tunes the external field strength. We assume the
\{g_{ij}\}
to be realized in some probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
and we denote the corresponding expectation by
\mathbb{E}(\cdot)
. We denote the
L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
norms by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=(\mathbb{E}\,|\cdot|^{p})^{1/p}
. Expectations over other, independent random variables are typically denoted by
E(\cdot)
.

In this paper, we analyze the distributional behavior of the spin covariances at high temperature in the limit
N\to\infty
. The magnetization
m_{i}
of spin
\sigma_{i}
and the spin covariance
m_{ij}
between spins
\sigma_{i}
and
\sigma_{j}
are defined by

m_{i}=\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ and }\hskip 14.22636ptm_{ij}=\langle\sigma_{i};\sigma_{j}\rangle=\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle-\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle\langle\sigma_{j}\rangle,

where
\langle\cdot\rangle
denotes the Gibbs expectation induced by
H_{N}
. It is defined so that

\langle f\rangle=\frac{1}{Z_{N}}\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}}f(\sigma)\,e^{H_{N}(\sigma)},\hskip 14.22636ptZ_{N}=\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}}e^{H_{N}(\sigma)}

for every observable
f:\{-1,1\}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
. For
t\geq 0
sufficiently small, it is well known that several quantities of interest related to the SK model have Gaussian fluctuations in the limit
N\to\infty
. This includes for instance the free energy fluctuations
[
2
,
7
,
11
]
and the overlap fluctuations
[
14
, Section 1.10]
. A notable exception consists of the two point functions
m_{ij}
in case of a non-vanishing external field
h\neq 0
: as shown in
[
10
]
, in this case the fluctuations
\sqrt{N}m_{ij}
converge in distribution to an explicit non-Gaussian limit.

The goal of this paper is to provide a new proof of the distributional convergence of
\sqrt{N}m_{ij}
. To state our main result, let us denote by
q=q_{t,h}\in[0,1]
the solution to

q=E\tanh^{2}(h+\sqrt{tq}Z),

where
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
denotes a standard Gaussian random variable and where
E(.)
denotes the expectation w.r.t.
Z
(if
h=0
, we set
q_{t,h}=0
). Moreover, we denote by
\mu=\mu_{t,h}\in[0,1]
the quantity

\mu=E\,\text{sech}^{4}(h+\sqrt{tq}Z).

Theorem 1
.

Assume that
t\geq 0
is sufficiently small and denote by
Z_{1},Z_{2},Z_{3}
i.i.d. standard Gaussian random variables. Then, in the limit
N\to\infty
, we have that

\sqrt{N}m_{ij}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\rightarrow}\limits^{\mathcal{D}}}\sqrt{\frac{t}{1-t\mu}}\,Z_{1}\,\emph{sech}^{2}\left(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{2}\right)\emph{sech}^{2}\left(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{3}\right).

(1.2)

Remarks:

1)

The convergence in (
1.2
) was proved first in
[
10
]
. To be more precise,
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
shows the convergence of the moments of
\sqrt{N}m_{ij}
towards those of the random variable on the r.h.s. in (
1.2
), which implies (
1.2
) by standard arguments.

2)

It is clear that the convergence (
1.2
) can only hold true if the parameters
(t,h)
satisfy the de Almeida-Thouless condition
[
8
]

t\mu_{t,h}<1
. Recall that this parameter range corresponds to the expected replica symmetric region of the model. Whether (
1.2
) holds true for all
(t,h)
that satisfy
t\mu_{t,h}<1
remains a challenging open question.

As pointed out above, our main result Theorem
1
is not new, but was already proved in
[
10
]
. The proof in
[
10
]
computes the moments of
\sqrt{N}m_{ij}
explicitly by relating them to suitable overlap moments and applying interpolation arguments which are based on the cavity method (for more details on this, see e.g.
[
14
, Sections 1.6 to 1.11]
). As such, the proof does not, unfortunately, shed much light on how the specific structure of the limiting distribution on the r.h.s. in (
1.2
) emerges. In particular, as pointed out by Talagrand
[
14
, Research Problem 1.11.2]
, one wonders whether the specific form of the limiting distribution arises from some underlying structure. In this paper, we aim to clarify this point by giving a new proof of Theorem
1
which is inspired by recent results in
[
1
,
3
,
4
]
and which is based on the following heuristics:

Basic mean field theory suggests that the magnetizations
m_{i}
satisfy for
t\geq 0
sufficiently small the cavity equations

m_{i}\approx\tanh\Big(h+\sum_{k\neq i}g_{ik}m_{k}^{(i)}\Big),

(1.3)

where
m_{k}^{(i)}
denotes the
k
-th magnetization after spin
i
has been removed from the system (for more details on our notation, see Section
2
below). The validity of (
1.3
) for small
t\geq 0
can be proved with a variety of tools and is well understood
[
5
,
14
,
1
]
. By differentiating (
1.3
) w.r.t. the external field, one can derive analogous equations for higher order correlations functions
[
1
]
, and in particular that

\begin{split}m_{ij}&\approx m_{ii}\sum_{k\neq i}g_{ik}m_{kj}^{(i)}=m_{ii}\,g_{ij}m_{jj}^{(i)}+m_{ii}\sum_{k\neq i,j}g_{ik}m_{kj}^{(i)}.\end{split}

Note that the leading order (in
t
) term
m_{ii}m_{jj}^{(i)}g_{ij}=(1-m_{i}^{2})(1-(m_{j}^{(i)})^{2})g_{ij}
suggests that
m_{ij}
is typically of size
O(N^{-1/2})
and that the scaling limit
\lim_{N\to\infty}\sqrt{N}m_{ij}
can not expected to be Gaussian unless
h=0
, in which case
m_{i}=m_{j}^{(i)}=0
by symmetry.

In order to go a step further and extract the correct candidate for the limiting distribution, we iterate the previous identity and obtain the graphical representation

\begin{split}m_{ij}&\approx m_{ii}\,m_{jj}^{(i)}\Big(g_{ij}+\sum_{k\neq i,j}g_{ik}m_{kk}^{(i,j)}g_{kj}+\!\!\sum_{k,l\neq i,j;k\neq l}\!\!\!g_{ik}m_{kl}^{(i,j)}g_{lj}\Big)\approx m_{ii}\,m_{jj}^{(i)}\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n+1}^{ij}}w(\gamma).\end{split}

Here,
\Gamma_{n+1}^{ij}
denotes the set of self-avoiding paths
\gamma
among
N
vertices from vertex
i
to
j
of length
|\gamma|=n+1
and the weight
w(\gamma)
is defined by

w(\gamma)=g_{ik_{1}}m_{k_{1}k_{1}}^{(i,j)}g_{k_{1}k_{2}}m_{k_{2}k_{2}}^{(i,k_{1},j)}g_{k_{2}k_{3}}\ldots m_{k_{n}k_{n}}^{(i,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n-1},j)}g_{k_{n}j},

(1.4)

if
\gamma=\big(\{i,k_{1}\},\{k_{1},k_{2}\},\ldots,\{k_{n},j\}\big)\in\Gamma_{n+1}^{ij}
and
n\geq 1
(for
n=0
we set
w(\gamma)=g_{ij}
).

Now, in order to see how the distribution on the r.h.s. in (
1.2
) emerges from the path representation of
m_{ij}
, let us make two observations. First, based on the cavity equations (
1.3
) and the identity
m_{ii}=1-m_{i}^{2}
, we expect that

m_{ii}\approx\text{sech}^{2}\Big(h+\sum_{k\neq i}g_{ik}m_{k}^{(i)}\Big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}{{=}}\text{sech}^{2}\Big(h+\sqrt{tq_{N}^{(i)}}Z\Big)\approx\text{sech}^{2}\big(h+\sqrt{tq}Z\big)

for a Gaussian
Z=(q_{N}^{(i)})^{-1/2}\sum_{k\neq i}g_{ik}m_{k}^{(i)}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, which is independent of the disorder
(g_{kl})_{k,l\neq i}
, and where we approximated in the third step

q_{N}^{(i)}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k\neq i}(m_{k}^{(i)})^{2}\approx q,

which can also be justified based on (
1.3
). Proceeding similarly for
m_{jj}^{(i)}
, this suggests

\sqrt{N}m_{ij}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}{{\approx}}\Big(\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n+1}^{ij}}\sqrt{N}w(\gamma)\Big)\,\text{sech}^{2}\big(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{2}\big)\text{sech}^{2}\big(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{3}\big)

(1.5)

for two i.i.d. Gaussian random variables
Z_{2},Z_{3}
that are independent of the first factor on the r.h.s. in (
1.5
) (indeed, as explained below, the correlations between the three factors are negligible as
N\to\infty
). That is, the second and third factors on the r.h.s. in (
1.2
) emerge as a consequence of the vertex weights
m_{ii}
and
m_{jj}^{(i)}
, respectively, and they cause the non-Gaussian behavior of the limiting distribution of
\sqrt{N}m_{ij}
if
h\neq 0
.

On the other hand, motivated by the leading order term
\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}^{ij}}\sqrt{N}w(\gamma)=\sqrt{N}g_{ij}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}{{=}}\mathcal{N}(0,t)
and by the results of
[
2
,
3
]
, a few basic moment computations combined with similar arguments as above suggest that the random vector
\big(T_{n+1}^{ij}\big)_{n\geq 0}
, defined through

T_{n+1}^{ij}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n+1}^{ij}}\sqrt{N}w(\gamma),

(1.6)

is close in distribution to a centered Gaussian vector
\textbf{Y}=(Y_{k})_{k\geq 0}
with covariance

E\,Y_{k}Y_{l}=t(t\mu)^{k}\delta_{kl}=\begin{cases}t(t\mu)^{k}&:k=l,\\
0&:k\neq l.\end{cases}

By the additivity of the variance for a sum of independent Gaussians, we thus expect

\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n+1}^{ij}}\sqrt{N}w(\gamma)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}{{\approx}}\sum_{n\geq 0}Y_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}{{=}}\sqrt{ t\sum_{n\geq 0}(t\mu)^{n}}Z_{1}

for some standard normal variable
Z_{1}
(independent of
Z_{2},Z_{3}
). With (
1.5
), we arrive at

\sqrt{N}m_{ij}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}{{\approx}}\sqrt{\frac{t}{1-t\mu}}\,Z_{1}\,\text{sech}^{2}\big(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{2}\big)\text{sech}^{2}\big(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{3}\big).

It turns out that the simple heuristics sketched above can be made rigorous without too much effort, thus providing an alternative and, hopefully, more transparent proof of Theorem
1
compared to
[
10
]
. To carry this out, we proceed in two main steps. After introducing basic notation in the next Section
2
, we first extend the analysis of
[
1
]
to derive the following truncated version of the path representation of
m_{ij}
in Section
3
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
t\geq 0
be small enough. Then, there exists a constant
C>0
, which is independent of
M,N
and
t
, such that

\Big\|\sqrt{N}m_{ij}-m_{ii}m_{jj}^{(i)}\sum_{n=0}^{M}T_{n+1}^{ij}\Big\|_{2}\leq CN^{-1/2}+Ct^{M/2}.

In the second step, we need to compute the limiting distribution of the random variable
m_{ii}m_{jj}^{(i)}\sum_{n=0}^{M}T_{n+1}^{ij}
. Inspired by
[
2
]
, this could for instance be based on a direct moment analysis. This is carried out in
[
15
]
on which part of this manuscript is based. In this paper instead, we rather find it convenient to follow the above heuristics more closely and we determine the joint limiting distribution of the random vector

\textbf{X}^{(M)}=(X_{n})_{n=0}^{M}=m_{ii}m_{jj}^{(i)}\big(T_{n+1}^{ij}\big)_{n=0}^{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{M+1},

(1.7)

for fixed
M\in\mathbb{N}
. Based on Stein’s method, the following is proved in Section
4
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
t\geq 0
be small enough. Then, for every
M\in\mathbb{N}
, we have in the limit
N\to\infty
that

\textbf{X}^{(M)}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\rightarrow}\limits^{\mathcal{D}}}\textbf{Y}^{(M)}\emph{sech}^{2}\big(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{2}\big)\emph{sech}^{2}\big(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{3}\big)

for a centered Gaussian vector
\textbf{Y}^{(M)}=(Y_{n})_{n=0}^{M}
with covariance
E\,Y_{k}Y_{l}=\delta_{kl}t(t\mu)^{k}
and for two i.i.d. standard normal variables
Z_{2},Z_{3}
which are independent of
\textbf{Y}^{(M)}
.

Theorem
1
is now a direct consequence of Prop.
2
and Prop.
3
:

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We assume
t\geq 0
to be sufficiently small, in particular
t<1
. For
M\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\textbf{X}^{(M)}
and
\textbf{Y}^{(M)}
be defined as above and let us denote by
\textbf{U}^{(M)}
the vector

\textbf{U}^{(M)}=\textbf{Y}^{(M)}\text{sech}^{2}\big(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{2}\big)\text{sech}^{2}\big(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{3}\big).

We use the standard fact that a sequence
(\chi_{k})_{k\geq 0}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued random variables converges in distribution to
\chi
if
Ef(\chi_{k})\to Ef(\chi)
as
k\to\infty
for all bounded Lipschitz continuous
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
(see e.g.
[
9
, Theorem 11.3.3]
). Applying this criterion, note that Prop.
3
and standard properties of independent Gaussian random variables imply that

\sum_{n=0}^{M}X_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\rightarrow}\limits^{\mathcal{D}}}\sum_{n=0}^{M}U_{n}\hskip 14.22636pt\text{as}\hskip 14.22636ptN\to\infty

as well as

\begin{split}\sum_{n=0}^{M}U_{n}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}{{=}}\sqrt{t\sum_{n=0}^{M}(t\mu)^{n}}\,Z_{1}\,\text{sech}^{2}\big(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{2}\big)\text{sech}^{2}\big(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{3}\big)\\
&\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\rightarrow}\limits^{\mathcal{D}}}\sqrt{\frac{t}{1-t\mu}}\,Z_{1}\,\text{sech}^{2}\big(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{2}\big)\text{sech}^{2}\big(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{3}\big)\equiv V\hskip 14.22636pt\text{as}\hskip 14.22636ptM\to\infty.\end{split}

Now, let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be bounded and Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
L\geq 0
. By Prop.
2
, Cauchy-Schwarz and the previous observations, we find that

\begin{split}\big|\mathbb{E}f(\sqrt{N}m_{ij})-Ef(V)\big|\leq&\ L\Big\|\sqrt{N}m_{ij}-\sum_{n=0}^{M}X_{n}\Big\|_{2}+o_{N}(1)+o_{M}(1)\\
\leq&\,L(CN^{-1/2}+Ct^{M/2})+o_{N}(1)+o_{M}(1).\end{split}

Sending
N\to\infty
and then
M\to\infty
, we find that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}f(\sqrt{N}m_{ij})=Ef(V)
.
∎

Notation and Auxiliary Results

In this section, we introduce some notation and we recall a few results from
[
1
]
.

First of all, connected correlation functions are defined by

m_{i_{1}\dots i_{n}}=\big(\partial_{h_{i_{1}}}\ldots\partial_{h_{i_{n}}}\log Z_{N}\big)|_{h_{1},\dots,h_{N}=h}.

Below, we also consider expectations of observables conditionally on a given number of spins. To this end, suppose
A=\{j_{1},j_{2},\dots,j_{k}\}\subset\{1,\dots,N\}
is disjoint from
B\subset\{1,\dots,N\}
with
|B|=l
and let
\tau=(\tau_{j_{1}},\dots,\tau_{j_{k}})\in\{-1,1\}^{k}
be a
k

H_{N}^{[A,B]}=H_{N,(\tau_{j_{1}},\dots,\tau_{j_{k}})}^{[A,B]}:\{-1,1\}^{N-k-l}\to\mathbb{R}

H_{N}^{[A,B]}(\sigma)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq N\\
i,j\notin A\cup B\end{subarray}}g_{ij}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}+h\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq N\\
i\notin A\cup B\end{subarray}}^{N}\Big(h+\sum_{j\in A}g_{ij}\tau_{j}\Big)\sigma_{i}.

H_{N}^{[A,B]}(\sigma)
corresponds to the energy of the system, conditionally on
\sigma_{j}
for
j\in A
(s.t.
\sigma_{j}=\tau_{j}
) and with all spins labeled by
j\in B
being removed from the system. For disjoint subsets
A,B\subset\{1,\dots,N\}
,
\langle.\rangle^{[A,B]}
denotes the Gibbs measure induced by
H_{N}^{[A,B]}
. Notice that
\langle f\rangle^{[A,B]}
is equal to the conditional expectation of
f
, given the spins
\sigma_{j}
for
j\in A

We abbreviate
\langle.\rangle^{[A]}\equiv\langle.\rangle^{[A,\emptyset]}
,
\langle.\rangle^{(B)}\equiv\langle.\rangle^{[\emptyset,B]}
as well as
\langle.\rangle\equiv\langle.\rangle^{[\emptyset,\emptyset]}
. By slight abuse of notation, if
A=\{i\}

\langle.\rangle^{[i]}=\langle.\rangle^{[\{i\}]}
and
\langle.\rangle^{(i)}=\langle.\rangle^{(\{i\})}
.

Finally, for disjoint
A,B\subset\{1,\dots,N\}
, an index

\begin{split}\delta_{i}\langle f\rangle^{[A,B]}&=\frac{1}{2}\big(\langle f\rangle^{[A,B]}(\sigma_{i}=1)-\langle f\rangle^{[A,B]}(\sigma_{i}=-1)\big),\\
\epsilon_{i}\langle f\rangle^{[A,B]}&=\frac{1}{2}\big(\langle f\rangle^{[A,B]}(\sigma_{i}=1)+\langle f\rangle^{[A,B]}(\sigma_{i}=-1)\big).\end{split}

Let
t\geq 0

Derivation of the Path Representation

In this section, we derive the path representation of
\sqrt{N}m_{ij}
and we prove Prop.
2
.

The path representation follows by combining and iterating the key identities in (
2.1
). To make this precise, let us recall the definitions in (
1.4
), (
1.6
) and (
1.7
), and let us recall that
\Gamma^{ij}_{n+1}
denotes the set of self-avoiding paths among vertices
\{1,\ldots,N\}
from
i
to
j
of length
n+1
. Moreover, given
a cavity set
B\subset\{1,\ldots,N\}
, let us define

\begin{split}E_{ij}^{(B)}&=m_{ii}\bigg(\sum_{k\not\in B\cup\{i\}}\int_{0}^{t}\big(\epsilon_{i}m_{kj}^{[i,B]}-m_{kj}^{(B\cup\{i\})}\big)\text{d}g_{ik}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\not\in B\cup\{i\}}\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{i}\big(m_{kjj}^{\left[i,B\right]}\big)\left(s\right)\frac{\text{d}s}{N}\bigg),\end{split}

(3.1)

as well as

\begin{split}E_{0}&=E_{ij}^{(\emptyset)},\hskip 14.22636ptE_{1}=m_{ii}m_{jj}^{(i)}\sum_{k_{1}\neq i,j}g_{ik_{1}}E_{jk_{1}}^{\left(i\right)},\\
E_{n}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\geq 2}}{{=}}m_{ii}m_{jj}^{(i)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma=(ik_{1}\ldots k_{n}j)\in\Gamma^{ij}_{n+1}\end{subarray}}\frac{w(\gamma)\,E_{k_{n-1}k_{n}}^{(i,k_{1},\ldots,k_{n-2},j)}}{m_{k_{n-1}k_{n-1}}^{(i,k_{1},\ldots,k_{n-2})}g_{k_{n-1}k_{n}}m_{k_{n}k_{n}}^{(i,k_{1},\ldots,k_{n-1},j)}},\\
A_{n}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\geq 2}}{{=}}m_{ii}m_{jj}^{(i)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma=(ik_{1}\ldots k_{n}j)\in\Gamma^{ij}_{n+1}\end{subarray}}\frac{w(\gamma)\,m_{k_{n-1}k_{n}}^{(i,k_{1},\ldots,k_{n-2},j)}}{m_{k_{n-1}k_{n-1}}^{(i,k_{1},\ldots,k_{n-2})}g_{k_{n-1}k_{n}}m_{k_{n}k_{n}}^{(i,k_{1},\ldots,k_{n-1},j)}}.\end{split}

Here, we introduced the shorthand notation
\gamma=(ik_{1}k_{2}\ldots k_{n}j)\equiv(\{i,k_{1}\},\ldots,\{k_{n},j\})
. Then, for every
M\geq 2
, a straightforward induction argument shows that

\sqrt{N}m_{ij}=\sum_{n=0}^{M}X_{n}+\sum_{n=0}^{M}\sqrt{N}E_{n}+\sqrt{N}A_{M+1}.

(3.2)

Indeed, combining the two identities in (
2.1
), we have first of all that

m_{ij}=m_{ii}m_{jj}^{(i)}g_{ij}+E_{0}+m_{ii}\sum_{k_{1}\neq i,j}g_{ik_{1}}m_{k_{1}j}^{(i)}=N^{-1/2}X_{0}+E_{0}+m_{ii}\sum_{k_{1}\neq i,j}g_{ik_{1}}m_{k_{1}j}^{(i)}.

Now, we apply the two identities in (
2.1
) to
m_{k_{1}j}^{(i)}=m_{jk_{1}}^{(i)}
, extracting the factor
m_{jj}^{(i)}
and expanding through Itô’s lemma in the disorder
(g_{jk})_{k\neq j}
. This yields

\begin{split}m_{ij}&=N^{-1/2}X_{0}+m_{ii}m_{jj}^{\left(i\right)}\sum_{k_{1}\neq i,j}g_{ik_{1}}m_{k_{1}k_{1}}^{\left(i,j\right)}g_{k_{1}j}+E_{0}+m_{ii}m_{jj}^{(i)}\sum_{k_{1}\neq i,j}g_{ik_{1}}E_{jk_{1}}^{\left(i\right)}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+m_{ii}m_{jj}^{\left(i\right)}\sum_{k_{1}\neq i,j}\sum_{k_{2}\neq i,j,k_{1}}g_{ik_{1}}m_{k_{1}k_{2}}^{\left(i,j\right)}g_{k_{2}j}\\
&=N^{-1/2}X_{0}+N^{-1/2}X_{1}+E_{0}+E_{1}+A_{2}.\end{split}

Iterating this procedure by expanding the two point function
m_{k_{M-1}k_{M}}^{(i,k_{1},\ldots,k_{M-2},j)}
in the definition
A_{M}
w.r.t.
(g_{k_{M-1}l})_{l\neq i,k_{1},\ldots,k_{M-1},j}
, we conclude (
3.2
) for every
M\geq 2
.

Proof of Prop.
2
.

It is enough to provide suitable bounds on
\sum_{n=0}^{M}E_{n}
and
A_{M+1}
. We start to estimate the contributions
E_{n}
, for
n\geq 0
. To control
E_{0}
, we use that
0\leq m_{ii}\leq 1
and we consider first the martingale term in (
3.1
). Using that

\Big\|\sum_{k\neq i}\int_{0}^{t}\big(\epsilon_{i}m_{kj}^{\left[i\right]}-m_{kj}^{\left(i\right)}\big)\text{d}g_{ik}\Big\|_{2}=\bigg(\sum_{k\neq i}\int_{0}^{t}\big\|\big(\epsilon_{i}m_{kj}^{\left[i\right]}-m_{kj}^{\left(i\right)}\big)(s)\big\|_{2}^{2}\frac{\text{d}s}{N}\bigg)^{1/2},

an application of Itô’s lemma w.r.t.
(g_{ik})_{k\neq i}
combined with the triangle inequality, the basic identity
m_{kjll}^{\left[i\right]}=-2m_{lk}^{\left[i\right]}m_{lj}^{\left[i\right]}-2m_{l}^{\left[i\right]}m_{ljk}^{\left[i\right]}
and Prop.
4
implies for
k\neq j
that

\begin{split}\big\|\big(\epsilon_{i}m_{kj}^{\left[i\right]}-m_{kj}^{\left(i\right)}\big)(s)\big\|_{2}&=\Big\|\sum_{l\neq i}\int_{0}^{s}\delta_{i}m_{kjl}^{\left[i\right]}\text{d}g_{il}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l\neq i}\int_{0}^{s}\epsilon_{i}m_{kjll}^{\left[i\right]}\left(u\right)\frac{\text{d}u}{N}\Big\|_{2}\leq Cs^{1/2}N^{-1}\end{split}

and, similarly, for
k=j
that
\big\|\big(\epsilon_{i}m_{kj}^{\left[i\right]}-m_{kj}^{\left(i\right)}\big)(s)\big\|_{2}\leq Cs^{1/2}N^{-1/2}
. Hence, we get

\Big\|\sum_{k\neq i}\int_{0}^{t}\big(\epsilon_{i}m_{kj}^{\left[i\right]}-m_{kj}^{\left(i\right)}\big)\text{d}g_{ik}\Big\|_{2}\leq CN^{-1}t

and arguing similarly for the drift term in (
3.1
) yields
\|E_{0}\|_{2}\leq CN^{-1}t
. The same arguments imply
\|E_{k_{1}k_{2}}^{(B)}\|_{2}\leq CN^{-1}t
if
k_{1}\neq k_{2}
, for
C>0
independent of
N,t
and
B
.

Next, to estimate
E_{n}
for
n\geq 1
, we proceed iteratively. In case of
E_{1}
, we bound

\begin{split}\Big\|m_{ii}m_{jj}^{(i)}\!\!\sum_{k_{1}\neq i,j}g_{ik_{1}}E_{jk_{1}}^{\left(i\right)}\Big\|^{2}_{2}\leq\Big\|\sum_{k_{1}\neq i,j}g_{ik_{1}}E_{jk_{1}}^{\left(i\right)}\Big\|^{2}_{2}=\frac{t}{N}\sum_{k_{1}\neq i,j}\big\|E_{jk_{1}}^{\left(i\right)}\big\|^{2}_{2}\leq\big(CtN^{-1}\big)^{2},\end{split}

where, in the second step, we used the independence of the disorder
(g_{ik})_{k\neq i}
from
E_{jk_{1}}^{(i)}
. Repeating the independence argument and using the fact that
0\leq m_{kk}^{[A,B]}\leq 1

n
times in order to control
E_{n}
for
n\geq 2
, we arrive at

\begin{split}\big\|E_{n}\big\|_{2}^{2}&=\Big\|m_{ii}m_{jj}^{(i)}\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma=(ik_{1}\ldots k_{n}j)\\
\in\Gamma^{ij}_{n+1}\end{subarray}}g_{ik_{1}}m_{k_{1}k_{1}}^{(i,j)}g_{k_{1}k_{2}}m_{k_{2}k_{2}}^{(i,k_{1},j)}\ldots g_{k_{n-2}k_{n-1}}\,E_{k_{n-1}k_{n}}^{(i,k_{1},\ldots,k_{n-2},j)}g_{k_{n}j}\Big\|_{2}^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{t}{N}\sum_{k_{1}\neq i}\Big\|m_{k_{1}k_{1}}^{(i,j)}\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma=(k_{1}\ldots k_{n}j)\in\Gamma^{ij}_{n}\end{subarray}}g_{k_{1}k_{2}}m_{k_{2}k_{2}}^{(i,k_{1},j)}\ldots g_{k_{n-2}k_{n-1}}\,E_{k_{n-1}k_{n}}^{(i,k_{1},\ldots,k_{n-2},j)}g_{k_{n}j}\Big\|_{2}^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{t}{N}\sum_{k_{1}\neq i}\frac{t}{N}\sum_{k_{2}\neq i,k_{1}}\Big\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma=(k_{2}\ldots k_{n}j)\in\Gamma^{ij}_{n-1}\end{subarray}}m_{k_{2}k_{2}}^{(i,k_{1},j)}\ldots g_{k_{n-2}k_{n-1}}\,E_{k_{n-1}k_{n}}^{(i,k_{1},\ldots,k_{n-2},j)}g_{k_{n}j}\Big\|_{2}^{2}\\
&\leq t^{n}\sup_{k\neq l}\big\|E_{kl}^{(i,k_{1},\ldots,k_{n-2},j)}\big\|_{2}^{2}\leq Ct^{n}N^{-2}.\end{split}

Similarly, we proceed to control
A_{M+1}
in which case Prop.
4
implies

\|A_{M+1}\|_{2}\leq C\,t^{M/2}\sup_{k\neq l}\big\|m_{kl}^{(i,k_{1},\ldots,k_{n-2},j)}\big\|_{2}\leq CN^{-1/2}t^{M/2}

and collecting the above bounds, this concludes that

\Big\|\sqrt{N}m_{ij}-\sum_{n=0}^{M}X_{n}\Big\|_{2}\leq\sum_{n=0}^{M}\sqrt{N}\|E_{n}\|_{2}+\sqrt{N}\|A_{M+1}\|_{2}\leq CN^{-1/2}+Ct^{M/2}.\qed

4
Limiting Distribution of Path Vector

Before proving Prop.
3
, we begin with some preliminary observations. Given the disorder
\textbf{G}=(g_{ij})_{1\leq i<j\leq N}
, consider a monomial
g_{k_{1}l_{1}}^{a_{1}}\dots g_{k_{n}l_{n}}^{a_{n}}
for mutually distinct pairs
(k_{i},l_{i})
and
a_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Assuming
f:\mathbb{R}^{N(N-1)/2}\to\mathbb{R}
to be smooth and bounded with bounded derivatives, the following straightforward estimates are a direct consequence of integration by parts and induction: if all
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in 2\mathbb{N}
, then

\Big|\mathbb{E}\,g_{k_{1}l_{1}}^{a_{1}}\dots g_{k_{n}l_{n}}^{a_{n}}f({\bf G})-\mathbb{E}f({\bf G})\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{t}{N}\Big)^{\frac{a_{k}}{2}}(a_{k}-1)!!\Big|\leq C\Big(\frac{t}{N}\Big)^{1+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}}.

(4.1)

Otherwise, if
d
denotes the number of odd integers among
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
, we have that

\big|\mathbb{E}\,g_{k_{1}l_{1}}^{a_{1}}\dots g_{k_{n}l_{n}}^{a_{n}}f({\bf G})\big|\leq C\Big(\frac{t}{N}\Big)^{\frac{d}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}}.

(4.2)

The constant
C
in (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) depends on the norms
\|\partial_{\alpha}f\|_{\infty}
for multi-indices with degree
|\alpha|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}
and on the powers
a_{i}
, but it is independent of
N
. Below, we apply (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) in particular to estimate (slight modifications of) contributions of the form
\mathbb{E}\,w(\gamma)w(\gamma^{\prime})
, given two paths
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{n+1}^{ij}
with
n+1\leq M+1
for fixed
M\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 5
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and mutually distinct indices
k_{l}\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
for
l\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Moreover, let
b_{l}\in\mathbb{N}
,
B_{l}\subset\{1,\ldots,N\}
and suppose that
\max_{1\leq l\leq n}|b_{l}|
and
\max_{1\leq l\leq n}|B_{l}|
are bounded uniformly in
N
. Then, for
t\geq 0
small enough, we have that

\bigg|\mathbb{E}\,\prod_{l=1}^{n}\left(m_{k_{l}k_{l}}^{(B_{l})}\right)^{b_{l}}-\prod_{l=1}^{n}\,E\,\emph{sech}^{2b_{l}}\big(h+\sqrt{tq}Z\big)\bigg|\leq CN^{-1/4},

(4.3)

where
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
and
C=C_{t}
depends on
t
, but is independent of
N
.

Proof.

If
h=0
, so that
q=q_{t,h}=0
for
t
small, the l.h.s. in (
4.3
) is equal to zero, so let us assume that
h\neq 0
. In this case, the claim follows by slightly generalizing some of the arguments detailed in the proofs of
[
1
, Prop. 1.2 & Lemma 4.1]
.

We first recall that the creation of a cavity does not affect the local magnetization significantly. Indeed, using Itô’s formula to expand w.r.t.
(g_{jk})_{k=1}^{N}
, we have

m_{i}^{[\{j\},B]}-m_{i}^{(B\cup\{j\})}=\sigma_{j}\sum_{k\not\in B\cup\{j\}}\int_{0}^{t}m_{ik}^{[\{j\},B]}\text{d}g_{jk}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\neq j}\int_{0}^{t}m_{ikk}^{[\{j\},B]}(s)\frac{\text{d}s}{N}.

Jensen’s inequality and Prop.
4
imply that

\big\|m_{i}^{(B)}-m_{i}^{(B,j)}\big\|_{2}=\big\|\big\langle m_{i}^{[\{j\},B]}-m_{i}^{(B,j\})}\big\rangle\big\|_{2}\leq\big\langle\big\|m_{i}^{[j,B]}-m_{i}^{(B,j)}\big\|_{2}\big\rangle\leq CN^{-1/2}

for every
i\not\in B\cup\{j\}
. Iterating this argument, we thus conclude that

\big\|m_{i}-m_{i}^{(B)}\big\|_{2}\leq CN^{-1/2},\hskip 14.22636pt\big\|q_{N}-q_{N}^{(B)}\big\|_{2}\leq CN^{-1/2},

(4.4)

where we set for
B\subset\{1,\ldots,N\}

q_{N}^{(B)}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k\not\in B}\big(m_{k}^{(B)}\big)^{2}\hskip 14.22636pt\text{and}\hskip 14.22636ptq_{N}=q_{N}^{(\emptyset)}.

Similarly, proceeding as in
[
1
, Lemma 4.1]
, Itô’s formula and Prop.
4
imply

\Big\|m_{i}^{(B)}-\tanh\Big(h+\sqrt{tq_{N}^{(B\cup\{i\})}}Z_{i}^{(B)}\Big)\Big\|_{2}\leq CN^{-1/2},

where

Z_{i}^{(B)}=\big(tq_{N}^{(B\cup\{i\})}\big)^{-1/2}\sum_{j\notin B\cup\{i\}}g_{ij}m_{j}^{(B\cup\{i\})}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1).

(4.5)

Now, consider indices
k_{l}
, powers
b_{l}
and cavity sets
B_{l}
, for
l\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, as in the statement of the lemma. Then, a slight variation of the previous observations implies

\Big\|m_{k_{l}}^{(B_{l})}-\tanh\Big(h+\sqrt{tq_{N}^{(B)}}Z^{(B)}_{k_{l}}\Big)\Big\|_{2}\leq CN^{-1/2}

for the union
B=\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}\}\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}B_{j}
and a basic covariance computation shows

\Big(Z^{(B)}_{k_{l}}\Big)_{l=1}^{n}\sim\mathcal{N}\big(0,\textbf{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big).

Combining this with
a^{u}-b^{u}=(a-b)\sum_{k=0}^{u}a^{u-k}b^{k}
,
0\leq m_{k_{l}k_{l}}^{(B_{l})}=1-\big(m_{k_{l}}^{(B_{l})}\big)^{2}\leq 1
,
0\leq\text{sech}(\cdot)\leq 1
, Cauchy-Schwarz, (
4.4
) and
[
1
, Prop. 1.2]
, we thus arrive at

\begin{split}&\bigg|\mathbb{E}\,\prod_{l=1}^{n}\left(m_{k_{l}k_{l}}^{(B_{l})}\right)^{b_{l}}-\prod_{l=1}^{n}\,\mathbb{E}\,\text{sech}^{2b_{l}}\big(h+\sqrt{tq}Z\big)\bigg|\\
&\leq\bigg|\mathbb{E}\prod_{l=1}^{n}\text{sech}^{2b_{l}}\big(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{k_{l}}^{(B)}\big)-\mathbb{E}\prod_{l=1}^{n}\,\,\text{sech}^{2b_{l}}\big(h+\sqrt{tq_{N}^{(B)}}Z_{k_{l}}^{(B)}\big)\bigg|+CN^{-1/4}\\
&\leq C\,\mathbb{E}\,\big|q-q_{N}^{(B)}\big|+CN^{-1/4}\leq CN^{-1/4}.\qed\end{split}

Equipped with the previous two lemmas, we are now ready to prove Proposition
3
.

Proof of Prop.
3
.

Recall the definition of
\textbf{X}^{(M)}
in (
1.7
) and that
\textbf{U}^{(M)}
equals

\textbf{U}^{(M)}=\textbf{Y}^{(M)}\text{sech}^{2}\big(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{2}\big)\text{sech}^{2}\big(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{3}\big),

where
\textbf{Y}^{(M)}=(Y_{n})_{n=0}^{M}
is a centered Gaussian vector with covariance
E\,Y_{k}Y_{l}=\delta_{kl}t(t\mu)^{k}
and
Z_{2},Z_{3}
are i.i.d. standard normal independent of
\textbf{Y}^{(M)}
. Our goal is to prove that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}f(\textbf{X}^{(M)})=Ef(\textbf{U}^{(M)})

(4.6)

for all
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{M+1})
. Notice that the family
\textbf{U}^{(M)},(\textbf{X}^{(M)})_{N\geq 2}
of random variables is tight, because
\max_{n=0,\ldots,M}\|U_{n}\|_{2}\leq C,\max_{n=0,\ldots,M}\|X_{n}\|_{2}\leq C
for some
C>0
that is independent of
N
. Combined with an application of Stone-Weierstrass, (
4.6
) implies
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}f(\textbf{X}^{(M)})=Ef(\textbf{U}^{(M)})
for all
f\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{M+1})
so that
\textbf{X}^{(M)}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\rightarrow}\limits^{\mathcal{D}}}\textbf{U}^{(M)}
as
N\to\infty
.

We split the proof of (
4.6
) into two parts and define

\Sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}=\Big(\delta_{kl}\sqrt{t(t\mu)^{k}}\Big)_{k,l=0}^{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{M+1\times M+1}.

Moreover, denote by
\textbf{W}^{(M)}=(W_{n})_{n=0}^{M}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\textbf{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{M+1}})
a standard Gaussian vector s.t.

\begin{split}\textbf{U}^{(M)}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}{{=}}\Sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}\textbf{W}^{(M)}\text{sech}^{2}\big(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{2}\big)\text{sech}^{2}\big(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{3}\big)\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}{{=}}\Sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}\textbf{W}^{(M)}\text{sech}^{2}\big(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{i}^{(j)}\big)\text{sech}^{2}\big(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{j}^{(i)}\big)\end{split}

with
\big(Z_{i}^{(j)},Z_{j}^{(i)}\big)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\textbf{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}})
as defined in (
4.5
). Then, we bound

\begin{split}|\mathbb{E}f(\textbf{X}^{(M)})-Ef(\textbf{U}^{(M)})|&\leq|\mathbb{E}Ef(m_{ii}m_{jj}^{(i)}\Sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}\textbf{W}^{(M)})-Ef(\textbf{U}^{(M)})|\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+|\mathbb{E}f(\textbf{X}^{(M)})-\mathbb{E}Ef(m_{ii}m_{jj}^{(i)}\Sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}\textbf{W}^{(M)})|\end{split}

(4.7)

and we claim that the two terms on the r.h.s. in (
4.7
) vanish as
N\to\infty
. The first term can be controlled using the same ideas as in the proof of Lemma
5
: we find that

\begin{split}&|\mathbb{E}Ef(\Sigma^{1/2}\textbf{W}^{(M)}\text{sech}^{2}(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{i}^{(j)})\text{sech}^{2}(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{j}^{(i)}))-\mathbb{E}Ef(m_{ii}m_{jj}^{(i)}\Sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}\textbf{W}^{(M)})|\\
&\leq\||\nabla f|\|_{\infty}\||\Sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}\textbf{W}^{(M)}|\|_{2}\|m_{ii}m_{jj}^{(i)}-\text{sech}^{2}(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{i}^{(j)})\text{sech}^{2}(h+\sqrt{tq}Z_{j}^{(i)})\|_{2}\leq\frac{C}{N^{1/4}}\end{split}

for some
C=C_{M}>0
that is independent of
N
. Here, the last step uses
[
1
, Prop. 1.2]
.

Let us now focus on the second term on the r.h.s. in (
4.7
). Here, we apply a suitable variant of Stein’s method that takes into account the randomness of
m_{ii}m_{jj}^{(i)}
(similar arguments were used recently in
[
12
]
). For
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{M+1})
and
0<\mathcal{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{M+1\times M+1}
, set

V_{f,\mathcal{C}}(\textbf{x})=-\int_{0}^{\infty}du\,\Big[Ef\big(e^{-u}\textbf{x}+\sqrt{1-e^{-2u}}\,\mathcal{C}^{\frac{1}{2}}\textbf{W}^{(M)}\big)-Ef\big(\mathcal{C}^{\frac{1}{2}}\textbf{W}^{(M)}\big)\Big].

By the multivariate Stein equation (see e.g.
[
6
, Lemma 2.6]
), it follows that

f(\textbf{x})-Ef\big(\mathcal{C}^{\frac{1}{2}}\textbf{W}^{(M)}\big)=\text{tr}\,\big(\mathcal{C}[\nabla^{2}V_{f,\mathcal{C}}](\textbf{x})\big)-\textbf{x}\cdot[\nabla V_{f,\mathcal{C}}](\textbf{x})

and an elementary bound shows that
\|\partial_{\alpha}V_{f,\mathcal{C}}\|_{\infty}\leq|\alpha|^{-1}\|\partial_{\alpha}f\|_{\infty}
. Choosing
\textbf{x}=\textbf{X}^{(M)}
,
\mathcal{C}^{\frac{1}{2}}=m_{ii}m_{jj}^{(i)}\Sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}
and taking the expectation over all the random variables yields

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}f\big(\textbf{X}^{(M)}\big)-Ef\big(m_{ii}m_{jj}^{(i)}\Sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}\textbf{W}^{(M)}\big)\\
&=\sum_{n=0}^{M}\mathbb{E}\big(m_{ii}{m_{jj}^{(i)}}\big)^{2}t(t\mu)^{n}\big[\partial_{n}^{2}V\big]\big(\textbf{X}^{(M)}\big)-\sum_{n=0}^{M}\mathbb{E}\,X_{n}\big[\partial_{n}V\big]\big(\textbf{X}^{(M)}\big),\end{split}

(4.8)

where we write in the remainder
V=V_{f,\mathcal{C}}
for this choice of
\mathcal{C}
and fixed
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{M+1})
. Prop.
3
thus follows if we show that the r.h.s. in (
4.8
) vanishes in the limit
N\to\infty
.
Setting
w^{(i)}(\gamma)=w(\gamma)/g_{ik_{1}}
if
\gamma=(ik_{1}k_{2}\ldots k_{n}j)
, integration by parts in
g_{i\bullet}
implies

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}X_{n}\big[\partial_{n}V\big]\big(\textbf{X}^{(M)}\big)\\
&=\frac{t}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma={(ik_{1}\dots k_{n}j)}\in\Gamma_{n+1}^{ij}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\,(\partial_{g_{ik_{1}}}m_{ii})m_{jj}^{(i)}w^{(i)}(\gamma)\big[\partial_{n}V\big]\big(\textbf{X}^{(M)}\big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{t}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{l=0}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma={(ik_{1}\dots k_{n}j)}\in\Gamma_{n+1}^{ij}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\,m_{ii}(\partial_{g_{ik_{1}}}m_{ii})(m_{jj}^{(i)})^{2}w^{(i)}(\gamma)\big[\partial_{l}\partial_{n}\widetilde{V}\big]\big(\textbf{X}^{(M)}\big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+t\sum_{l=0}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma=(ik_{1}\ldots k_{n}j)\in\Gamma^{ij}_{n+1},\\
\gamma^{\prime}=(ik_{1}k_{2}^{\prime}\ldots k_{l}^{\prime}j)\in\Gamma^{ij}_{l+1}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\big(m_{ii}m_{jj}^{(i)}\big)^{2}w^{(i)}(\gamma)w^{(i)}(\gamma^{\prime})\big[\partial_{l}\partial_{n}V\big]\big(\textbf{X}^{(M)}\big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+t\sum_{l=0}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma=(ik_{1}\ldots k_{n}j)\in\Gamma^{ij}_{n+1},\\
\gamma^{\prime}=(ik_{1}^{\prime}k_{2}^{\prime}\ldots k_{l}^{\prime}j)\in\Gamma^{ij}_{l+1}\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\mathbb{E}\,m_{ii}(\partial_{g_{ik_{1}}}m_{ii})(m_{jj}^{(i)})^{2}w^{(i)}(\gamma)w(\gamma^{\prime})\big[\partial_{l}\partial_{n}V\big]\big(\textbf{X}^{(M)}\big)\\
&=\Sigma_{1}^{(n)}+\Sigma_{2}^{(n)}+\Sigma_{3}^{(n)}+\Sigma_{4}^{(n)},\end{split}

where
\Sigma_{j}^{(n)}
is defined as the contribution in the
j
-th line on the r.h.s. and where we set

\big[\partial_{l}\partial_{n}\widetilde{V}\big](\textbf{x})=-\int_{0}^{\infty}du\,e^{-u}\sqrt{1-e^{-2u}}E\big(\Sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}\textbf{W}^{(M)})_{l}[\partial_{l}\partial_{n}f]\big(e^{-u}\textbf{x}+\sqrt{1-e^{-2u}}\mathcal{C}^{\frac{1}{2}}\textbf{W}^{(M)}\big).

Notice that
\|\partial_{l}\partial_{n}\widetilde{V}\|_{\infty}\leq C\|\partial_{l}\partial_{n}f\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}du\,e^{-u}\leq C
for some
C=C_{f,t,M}
.

It now turns out that
\Sigma_{1}^{(n)},\Sigma_{2}^{(n)}
and
\Sigma_{4}^{(n)}
vanish in the limit
N\to\infty
and that
\Sigma_{3}^{(n)}
cancels, up to another error that is negligible, with the
n
-th summand in the first term on the r.h.s. in (
4.8
). To see this, using that
\|\partial_{n}V\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}
, Cauchy-Schwarz implies

\begin{split}|\Sigma_{1}^{(n)}|^{2}&\leq\frac{C}{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma={(ik_{1}\dots k_{n}j)}\in\Gamma_{n+1}^{ij},\\
\gamma^{\prime}={(ik_{1}^{\prime}\dots k_{n}^{\prime}j)}\in\Gamma_{n+1}^{ij}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}(\partial_{g_{ik_{1}}}m_{ii})(\partial_{g_{ik_{1}^{\prime}}}m_{ii})w^{(i)}(\gamma)w^{(i)}(\gamma^{\prime})\\
&=\frac{C}{N}\sum_{u=0}^{n-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma={(ik_{1}\dots k_{n}j)}\in\Gamma_{n+1}^{ij},\\
\gamma^{\prime}={(ik_{1}^{\prime}\dots k_{n}^{\prime}j)}\in\Gamma_{n+1}^{ij}:|\gamma\cap\gamma^{\prime}|=u\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}(\partial_{g_{ik_{1}}}m_{ii})(\partial_{g_{ik_{1}^{\prime}}}m_{ii})w^{(i)}(\gamma)w^{(i)}(\gamma^{\prime})\\
&+\frac{C}{N}\sum_{\gamma={(ik_{1}\dots k_{n}j)}\in\Gamma_{n+1}^{ij}}\mathbb{E}(\partial_{g_{ik_{1}}}m_{ii})^{2}w^{(i)}(\gamma)^{2}.\end{split}

Recalling the explicit form
w^{(i)}(\gamma)=m_{k_{1}k_{1}}^{(i,j)}g_{k_{1}k_{2}}m_{k_{2}k_{2}}^{(i,k_{1},j)}g_{k_{2}k_{3}}\ldots m_{k_{n}k_{n}}^{(i,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n-1},j)}g_{k_{n}j}
, for
\gamma={(ik_{1}\dots k_{n}j)}
, the number
|\gamma\cap\gamma^{\prime}|
of joint edges determines the size of each summand according to the bounds (
4.1
) and (
4.2
): The last term of the latter is straightforward and of order
O(N^{-1})
. As for the term on the second line, we obtain that for
|\gamma\cap\gamma^{\prime}|=u
,

\mathbb{E}(\partial_{g_{ik_{1}}}m_{ii})(\partial_{g_{ik_{1}^{\prime}}}m_{ii})w^{(i)}(\gamma)w^{(i)}(\gamma^{\prime})\leq CN^{u-2n}.

Since
|\Gamma_{n+1}^{ij}|\leq N^{n}
and
|\{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{n+1}^{ij}:|\gamma\cap\gamma^{\prime}|=u\}|\leq N^{n-u}
for fixed
\gamma\in\Gamma_{n+1}^{ij}
, we get

|\Sigma_{1}^{(n)}|^{2}\leq\sum_{u=0}^{n-1}CN^{u-2n-1}\big|\big\{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{n+1}^{ij}:|\gamma\cap\gamma^{\prime}|=u\big\}\big|+CN^{-1}\leq CN^{-1}

for some
C=C_{n}>0
that depends on
n\leq M
, but that is independent of
N
. Recalling that
\|\partial_{l}\partial_{n}\widetilde{V}\|_{\infty}\leq C
, the same argument shows that
|\Sigma_{2}^{(n)}|^{2}\leq CN^{-1}
.

Next, let us consider
\Sigma_{3}^{(n)}
and let us compare it with the
n
-th summand in the first term on the r.h.s. in (
4.8
): then, we have that

\begin{split}&\Sigma_{3}^{(n)}-\big(m_{ii}{m_{jj}^{(i)}}\big)^{2}t(t\mu)^{n}\big[\partial_{n}^{2}V\big]\big(\textbf{X}^{(M)}\big)\\
&=t\,\mathbb{E}\big(m_{ii}m_{jj}^{(i)}\big)^{2}\big[\partial_{n}^{2}V\big]\big(\textbf{X}^{(M)}\big)\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma^{ij}_{n+1}\end{subarray}}(w^{(i)}(\gamma))^{2}-(t\mu)^{n}\bigg)\\
&\hskip 5.69046pt+(1-\delta_{n0})\sum_{l=1}^{M}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma=(ik_{1}\ldots k_{n}j)\in\Gamma^{ij}_{n+1},\\
\gamma^{\prime}=(ik_{1}k_{2}^{\prime}\ldots k_{l}^{\prime}j)\in\Gamma^{ij}_{l+1}:\gamma\neq\gamma^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!t\,\mathbb{E}\big(m_{ii}m_{jj}^{(i)}\big)^{2}w^{(i)}(\gamma)w^{(i)}(\gamma^{\prime})\big[\partial_{l}\partial_{n}V\big]\big(\textbf{X}^{(M)}\big)=\Sigma_{31}^{(n)}\!+\!\Sigma_{32}^{(n)}.\end{split}

The error terms
\Sigma_{31}^{(n)}
and
\Sigma_{32}^{(n)}
can be controlled through simple second moment estimates as before. Recalling that
\|\partial_{s}\partial_{t}V\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{s}\partial_{t}f\|_{\infty}
, we have on the one hand that

\begin{split}|\Sigma_{31}^{(n)}|^{2}&\leq C\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{ij}_{n+1}\end{subarray}}(w^{(i)}(\gamma))^{2}(w^{(i)}(\gamma^{\prime}))^{2}-2(t\mu)^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma^{ij}_{n+1}\end{subarray}}(w^{(i)}(\gamma))^{2}+(t\mu)^{2n}\bigg).\end{split}

Applying (
4.1
) & (
4.2
), that
|\Gamma_{n+1}^{ij}|=N^{n}+O(N^{n-1})
and Lemma
5
, we find that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma^{ij}_{n+1}\end{subarray}}(w^{(i)}(\gamma))^{2}&=\bigg[\Big(\frac{t}{N}\Big)^{n}\Big(\big(E\,\text{sech}^{4}\big(h+\sqrt{tq}Z\big)\big)^{n}+O(N^{-1/4})\Big)+O(N^{-n-1})\bigg]|\Gamma_{n+1}^{ij}|\\
&=(t\mu)^{n}+O(N^{-1/4}).\end{split}

and, similarly, that

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{ij}_{n+1}\end{subarray}}(w^{(i)}(\gamma))^{2}(w^{(i)}(\gamma^{\prime}))^{2}\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{ij}_{n+1}:\\
\gamma\cap\gamma^{\prime}=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}(w^{(i)}(\gamma))^{2}(w^{(i)}(\gamma^{\prime}))^{2}+\sum_{u=1}^{n+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{ij}_{n+1}:\\
|\gamma\cap\gamma^{\prime}|=u\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}(w^{(i)}(\gamma))^{2}(w^{(i)}(\gamma^{\prime}))^{2}\\
&=(t\mu)^{2n}+O(N^{-1/4})+\sum_{u=1}^{n-1}O\big(N^{2n-u}\times N^{-2n}\big)=(t\mu)^{2n}+O(N^{-1/4}).\end{split}

Here, the scaling
O(N^{2n-u}\times N^{-2n})
of the
u
-th summand in the error is obtained by multiplying the number of paths
|\{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{ij}_{n+1}:|\gamma\cap\gamma^{\prime}|=u\}|=O(N^{2n-u})
with the leading order decay in
N
of
\mathbb{E}(w^{(i)}(\gamma))^{2}(w^{(i)}(\gamma^{\prime}))^{2}
: if
|\gamma\cap\gamma^{\prime}|=u
, at least
u-1
Gaussian edge weights in the product
(w^{(i)}(\gamma))^{2}(w^{(i)}(\gamma^{\prime}))^{2}
have multiplicity four in which case
2n-2(u-1)
edge weights have multiplicity two, leading to an overall
O(N^{-2(u-1)})\times O(N^{-2(n-u+1)})=O(N^{-2n})
decay. On the other hand, there can be at most
u
edge weights having multiplicity four in
(w^{(i)}(\gamma))^{2}(w^{(i)}(\gamma^{\prime}))^{2}
in which case one obtains similarly that
\mathbb{E}(w^{(i)}(\gamma))^{2}(w^{(i)}(\gamma^{\prime}))^{2}=O(N^{-2u})\times O(N^{-2(n-u)})=O(N^{-2n})
. Collecting the previous observations, we conclude that
|\Sigma_{31}^{(n)}|^{2}\leq CN^{-1/4}
.

Finally, let us switch to
\Sigma_{32}^{(n)}
and
\Sigma_{4}^{(n)}
; both terms can be controlled with similar arguments as before, so let us only provide a few details for
\Sigma_{32}^{(n)}
. Fixing
l\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
, it turns out useful to split the
l
-th summand in the definition of
\Sigma_{32}^{(n)}
into

\begin{split}\sum_{u=0}^{\min(n,l)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma=(ik_{1}\ldots k_{n}j)\in\Gamma^{ij}_{n+1},\\
\gamma^{\prime}=(ik_{1}k_{2}^{\prime}\ldots k_{l}^{\prime}j)\in\Gamma^{ij}_{l+1}:\\
\gamma\neq\gamma^{\prime},|\gamma\cap\gamma^{\prime}|=u\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathbb{E}\big(m_{ii}m_{jj}^{(i)}\big)^{2}w^{(i)}(\gamma)w^{(i)}(\gamma^{\prime})\big[\partial_{l}\partial_{n}V\big]=\sum_{u=0}^{\min(n,l)}\Sigma_{32u}^{(nl)}\end{split}

and controlling each summand separately; considering for concreteness
\Sigma_{320}^{(n)}
, for instance, we apply Cauchy-Schwarz and get

\begin{split}|\Sigma_{320}^{(nl)}|^{2}&\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma=(ik_{1}\ldots k_{n}j)\in\Gamma^{ij}_{n+1},\\
\gamma^{\prime}=(ik_{1}k_{2}^{\prime}\ldots k_{l}^{\prime}j)\in\Gamma^{ij}_{l+1}:\gamma\cap\gamma^{\prime}=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau=(ip_{1}\ldots p_{n}j)\in\Gamma^{ij}_{n+1},\\
\tau^{\prime}=(ip_{1}p_{2}^{\prime}\ldots p_{l}^{\prime}j)\in\Gamma^{ij}_{l+1}:\tau\cap\tau^{\prime}=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathbb{E}\,w^{(i)}(\gamma)w^{(i)}(\gamma^{\prime})w^{(i)}(\tau)w^{(i)}(\tau^{\prime})\\
&\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq N:\\
k\neq i,j\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma^{kj}_{n},\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{kj}_{l}:\gamma\cap\gamma^{\prime}=\emptyset,\\
\tau\in\Gamma^{kj}_{n},\tau^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{kj}_{l}:\tau\cap\tau^{\prime}=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\,w(\gamma)w(\gamma^{\prime})w(\tau)w(\tau^{\prime})\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\neq p\leq N:\\
k,p\neq i,j\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma^{kj}_{n},\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{kj}_{l}:\\
\gamma\cap\gamma^{\prime}=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in\Gamma^{pj}_{n},\tau^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{pj}_{l}:\\
\tau\cap\tau^{\prime}=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\,w(\gamma)w(\gamma^{\prime})w(\tau)w(\tau^{\prime}).\end{split}

Now, the bounds (
4.1
), (
4.2
), Lemma
5
and basic combinatorics as above imply that

\begin{split}&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq N:\\
k\neq i,j\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma^{kj}_{n},\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{kj}_{l}:\gamma\cap\gamma^{\prime}=\emptyset,\\
\tau\in\Gamma^{kj}_{n},\tau^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{kj}_{l}:\tau\cap\tau^{\prime}=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\,w(\gamma)w(\gamma^{\prime})w(\tau)w(\tau^{\prime})\\
&\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq N:\\
k\neq i,j\end{subarray}}\sum_{u=0}^{n+l}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma,\tau\in\Gamma^{kj}_{n};\gamma^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{kj}_{l}:\\
\gamma\cap\gamma^{\prime}=\emptyset,\tau\cap\tau^{\prime}=\emptyset,\\
|(\gamma\cup\gamma^{\prime})\cap(\tau\cup\tau^{\prime})|=u\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\,w(\gamma)w(\gamma^{\prime})w(\tau)w(\tau^{\prime})\\
&=(N-2)\sum_{u=0}^{n+l}O\big(N^{2(n+l-2)-(u-2)}\times N^{u-2(n+l)}\big)\leq CN^{-1}.\end{split}

The
N^{-(u-2)}
scaling is due to the fact that is enough to fix
n-1
edges to have
\gamma=\tau
(the same for
\gamma^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}
). Analogously, one shows that

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\neq p\leq N:\\
k,p\neq i,j\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma^{kj}_{n},\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{kj}_{l}:\\
\gamma\cap\gamma^{\prime}=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in\Gamma^{pj}_{n},\tau^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{pj}_{l}:\\
\tau\cap\tau^{\prime}=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\,w(\gamma)w(\gamma^{\prime})w(\tau)w(\tau^{\prime})\leq CN^{-1}\end{split}

such that
|\Sigma_{320}^{(nl)}|\leq CN^{-1/2}
. The same arguments imply that
|\Sigma_{32u}^{(nl)}|\leq CN^{-1/2}
for
u\geq 1
s.t.
|\Sigma_{32}^{(n)}|\leq CN^{-1/2}
; finally, as already mentioned, a similar analysis implies
|\Sigma_{4}^{(n)}|\leq CN^{-1/2}
.

Collecting the above estimates, we have proved that the contribution on r.h.s. in (
4.8
) is of the order
O(N^{-1/4})
, which concludes the proof.
∎

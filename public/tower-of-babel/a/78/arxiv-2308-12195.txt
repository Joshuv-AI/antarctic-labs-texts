A motivic Fundamental Lemma
Arthur Forey, François Loeser, Dimitri Wyss

Abstract
. In this paper we prove motivic versions of the Langlands-Shelstad Fundamental Lemma and Ngô’s Geometric Stabilization. To achieve this, we follow the strategy from the recent proof by Groechenig, Wyss and Ziegler which avoided the use of perverse sheaves using instead p p -adic integration and Tate duality. We make a key use of a construction of Denef and Loeser which assigns a virtual motive to any definable set in the theory of pseudo-finite fields.



1.
Introduction

1.1.

First introduced in 1979 as a technical device of combinatorial nature, the Langlands-Shelstad Fundamental Lemma was a conjecture which has long resisted the efforts of mathematicians until its full proof by Ngô in 2008
[
37
]
.
We shall start with a loose presentation for non-specialists of some of the motivations behind it.
A powerful tool in the Langlands program is
the Arthur-Selberg trace formula which is a far-reaching extension of the classical Poisson summation formula for an arbitrary reductive group
G
over a global field.
It is an equality between a spectral side involving traces of automorphic representations and a so-called geometric side which is a sum of adelic orbital integrals.
These adelic orbital integrals are products of orbital integrals over local fields, that is integrals over
G(F)
-conjugacy classes, with
F
a local field.
Langlands’s principle of functoriality predicts that automorphic representations for a given reductive group
G
should transfer to
automorphic representations for any another reductive group
G^{\prime}
, given a morphism between their Langlands duals.
An approach to prove it in some cases consists in comparing the geometric side of the trace formula for the two groups.
If
F
is, say, a local field, an obstacle that immediately arises before going further is that in general there is a difference between stable conjugacy classes,
that is
G(\overline{F})
-conjugacy classes with
\overline{F}
the algebraic closure of
F
, and
G(F)
-conjugacy classes.
Indeed, while it is often possible to associate stable conjugacy classes for
G^{\prime}
to stable conjugacy classes for
G

there is no natural way to do this for conjugacy classes over
F
.
The Fundamental Lemma is a way to overcome this difficulty by expressing orbital integrals for a reductive group
G
over a local field in terms
of stable orbital integrals (integrals over stable conjugacy classes) for different groups over the same local field.

1.2.

Let us now state the Fundamental Lemma in the form proved by Ngô in
[
37
]
.
We fix a non-archimedean local field
F
and a reductive group
G
over
F
.
We assume
G
is unramified, that is, that
G
is the generic fiber of a reductive group scheme over the valuation ring of
F
.
Let
\gamma_{G}
be a regular semi-simple
F
-point of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
with centralizer

I_{\gamma_{G}}
.
The orbital integral associated to these data is

O_{\gamma_{G}}=\int_{I_{\gamma_{G}}(F)\backslash G(F)}\mathbf{1}_{\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{O}_{F})}(g^{-1}\gamma_{G}g)dg,

with
dg
a suitably normalized Haar measure.
The set of
G(F)
-conjugacy classes
in the stable conjugacy class of
\gamma_{G}

can be
identified with the
set of elements of
H^{1}(F,I_{\gamma_{G}})
whose image in
H^{1}(F,G)
is trivial.
Under this identification the class of
\gamma_{G}
corresponds to the identity element.
By Fourier transformation on the finite abelian group

H^{1}(F,I_{\gamma_{G}})
, the orbital integral

O_{\gamma_{G}}
can be expressed as a linear combination of

\kappa
-orbital integrals
O_{\gamma_{G}}^{\kappa}

where,
for a character
\kappa:H^{1}(F,I_{\gamma_{G}})\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
,

O_{\gamma_{G}}^{\kappa}

is defined as

O_{\gamma_{G}}^{\kappa}=\sum_{[\gamma^{\prime}]}\kappa([\gamma^{\prime}])\,O_{\gamma^{\prime}},

with
[\gamma^{\prime}]
running over
the set of
G(F)
-conjugacy classes within the stable conjugacy class
of
\gamma_{G}
. When
\kappa
is the trivial character one gets the stable orbital integral

O^{\mathrm{stab}}_{\gamma_{G}}
. Thus, the problem of stabilization of orbital integrals can be reduced to expressing a

\kappa
-orbital integral for
G
in terms of a stable orbital integral on some different unramified reductive group
H
over
F
.

The group
H
is constructed via the theory of endoscopy out of the data
(G,I_{\gamma_{G}},\kappa)
. In particular, the set of roots of the endoscopic group
H
is a subset of the set of all roots of
G
. Furthermore,
to each
\gamma_{G}
as before corresponds a
F
-point
\gamma_{H}
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
H

which is well defined up to stable conjugacy and which will be assumed to be regular semi-simple.
In particular, the stable orbital integral

O^{\mathrm{stab}}_{\gamma_{H}}
depends only on
\gamma_{G}
and
H
.
On the other hand, the
\kappa
-orbital integral
O_{\gamma_{G}}^{\kappa}
depends on the conjugacy class of
\gamma_{G}
, another choice

\gamma^{\prime}_{G}
would multiply it by a factor
\kappa([\gamma^{\prime}_{G}])
.
When the characteristic of
F
is greater than twice the Coxeter number of
G
, one way to solve this indeterminacy is to use
the so-called Kostant section which provides
a canonical conjugacy class within
each stable conjugacy class of regular semisimple elements in
\mathfrak{g}(F)
.

The Fundamental Lemma, as proved by Ngô in
[
37
]
, relates
\kappa
-orbital integrals on the group
G

to stable orbital integrals on the endoscopic
group
H
in the following way:

Fundamental Lemma 1.2.1
.

With the above notation,
assume the characteristic of
F
is greater than twice the Coxeter number of
G
and that the conjugacy class of
\gamma_{G}
is the one provided by the Kostant section.
Then
we have the equality

(
\ast
)

O_{\gamma_{G}}^{\kappa}=\Delta(\gamma_{H},\gamma_{G})\,O^{\mathrm{stab}}_{\gamma_{H}},

with
\Delta(\gamma_{H},\gamma_{G})
of the form
q_{F}^{r}
, with
q_{F}
the cardinality of the residue field of
F
and
r
an explicit integer.

1.3.

Heuristically,
all relations between integrals should come, in a certain sense, from geometry as mere shadows of geometric relations.
A suggestive instance of this principle
is provided by the
Kontsevich-Zagier conjecture on algebraic relations between periods
[
32
]
. In our case an example of such a phenomenon already showed up in the proof of the Fundamental Lemma for
\mathrm{Sp}(4)
by Hales
[
26
]
.
Indeed, in this case, each side of the Fundamental Lemma can be expressed as the number points of an elliptic curve over a finite field, and
the crucial fact used in the proof is that these two elliptic curves are isogenous, hence have the same number of points, cf.
[
24
,
25
]
.
As isogenous elliptic curves have the same rational Chow motive, this leads to ask whether (

∗

‣
1.2.1
) could already hold at the level of rational Chow motives.
The main result of the present paper is that it is indeed possible to lift the identity
(

∗

‣
1.2.1
) to an identity between two objects in a Grothendieck ring of rational Chow motives.
More precisely we prove an equality in the Grothendieck ring of Chow motives that specializes to the Fundamental Lemma for
p
large enough.
Note that a statement of this type was conjectured by Hales in
[
27
]
.
It would be interesting to establish a non-virtual version of our result, similarly to what Hoskins and Pepin Lehalleur prove in
[
29
]
, a non-virtual analogue of the result of Loeser and Wyss
[
34
]
on topological Mirror Symmetry.

1.4.

One of the key novelty in Ngô’s proof
is to move from local geometry over a local non-archimedean field to
the global geometry over a curve, using the Hitchin fibration which is the global analogue of affine Springer fibers.
Ngô’s proof is of geometric nature,
and uses the decomposition theorem
to
control the
support of the perverse cohomology sheaves of the Hitchin fibration.
Recently, Groechenig, Wyss and Ziegler
gave a new proof of the Fundamental Lemma using
p
-adic integration along the Hitchin fibration
[
22
]
, inspired by their proof of the topological Mirror Symmetry conjecture of Hausel-Thaddeus
[
28
]
.
More precisely, they provide a new proof of Ngô’s
Geometric Stabilization which is known to imply
the Fundamental Lemma by Ngô’s work
[
37
]
.
An important feature of their proof is that it circumvents the use of perverse sheaves and the decomposition theorem, which seemed insuperable
obstacles to a motivic version. Instead a transition from
p
-adic to motivic arguments is often possible, for example in the case of topological Mirror Symmetry
[
34
]
.

1.5.

We use the general theory of motivic integration over fields of characteristic zero developed by Cluckers and Loeser in
[
9
]

which is based on the notion of definability in model theory. Indeed it was already proved by Cluckers, Hales and Loeser in
[
7
]
, that all the terms occurring in
the Fundamental Lemma
can be defined purely within a first order language for valued fields, namely
the Denef-Pas
language. Thus using the “Transfer Principle” from
[
10
]
, a generalization of the Ax-Kochen-Eršov theorem,
they deduce the Fundamental Lemma in mixed characteristic from the equicharacteristic case, recovering conceptually an earlier result of
Waldspurger
[
40
]
.

1.6.

In the theory of motivic integration developed in
[
9
]
, volumes are elements of the ring
\mathcal{C}(*_{k})
which is
a localization of the Grothendieck ring of definable sets over the residue field
k
. Such
definable sets correspond to equivalence
classes of
formulas in the first order ring language with coefficients in
k
, two formulas being equivalent if they are equivalent when interpreted in every field extension of
k
.
Since in this paper we are only concerned with local non-archimedean fields, which have finite residue fields, it is natural to consider only fields extensions
of
k
that satisfy the same first order properties as finite fields. Such fields were introduced by Ax in
[
2
]
under the name of pseudo-finite fields.
Recall that a field
L
is pseudo-finite
if and only if it
is perfect with a unique extension in every positive degree and every geometrically irreducible variety over
L
has an
L
-rational point.
We will consider a specialization of the theory
of
[
9
]
for which volumes no longer take place in the ring
\mathcal{C}(*_{k})
, but in a ring called

\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Psf}}(*_{k})
which is defined exactly as
\mathcal{C}(*_{k})
, except that two formulas will be considered to be equivalent if and only if they are equivalent when interpreted in every pseudo-finite field extension of
k
.

1.7.

In order to identify for example classes of dual abelian varieties we need to further specialize
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Psf}}(*_{k})
. By a result of Denef and Loeser
[
12
]
(see also
[
13
,
38
]
)
one may assign to any element
\alpha
of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Psf}}(*_{k})
a virtual Chow motive
\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}(\alpha)

such that
the number of points of
\alpha
interpreted in a finite field of sufficiently large characteristic can
be recovered from
\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}(\alpha)

by taking the trace of the Frobenius on its étale realization.
Combining the construction from
[
12
]
and the general theory of

[
9
]
one can work out a theory of motivic integration with values in localized Grothendieck rings of Chow motives, which specializes well to
p
-adic integration for
p
large enough.
This is the framework which is used in this paper. Note that the integration theory which was used
in

[
34
]
, for which two formulas were considered to be equivalent
if they are equivalent when interpreted algebraically closed field extensions of
k
, would not
suffice here, since it does not have the property that the volume of a definable set specializes to its
p
-adic volume.

1.9.

The structure of the paper is the following.
In Section
2
we develop a version
of motivic
integration which is suited for the purpose of this paper, using
the general framework from
[
9
]
.
This can be viewed as a relative version of
the integrals constructed in
[
12
]
,
providing volumes in Grothendieck groups of étale motives
to definable sets in the Denef-Pas language with pseudo-finite residue fields.
We show that the basic properties we need (change of variable, Fubini theorem, specialization to
p
-adic integration, formula for the orbifold volume)
still hold in this context.
In the short Section
3
, which is mostly borrowed from
Section 4 of
[
37
]
, we recall the preliminary material required in order to formulate the Geometric Stabilization theorem.
In Section
4
, we show how the various objects appearing in the statement of the Geometric Stabilization theorem can actually be encoded
using the Denef-Pas language as definable
objects in the theory of pseudo-finite fields.
In particular Galois cohomology is encoded by cocycles so that classical isomorphisms between
Galois cohomology groups of finite fields
lift to definable isomorphisms over pseudo-finite fields.
We also
use twists of definable sets by Galois cocycles to construct isotypical components of motives associated to definable sets.
It is in this framework that we formulate
our motivic version of Geometric Stabilization
as an equality between motivic integrals, Theorem
4.8.1
.

Section
5
is devoted to the proof of motivic
Geometric Stabilization, Theorem
5.1.1
.
Following
[
22
]
we use the formalism of orbifold measures to reduce it to an identity between motivic integrals on Hitchin fibers for Langlands dual groups,
Theorem
5.3.1
. By a Fubini theorem one may further reduce to an identity involving only generically smooth Hitchin fibers, which then follows from Tate duality.

Finally,
we explain in Section
6
how the strategy introduced by Ngô
to deduce the Fundamental Lemma from Geometric Stabilization can be adapted to the motivic setting, allowing us to prove our motivic version of the
Fundamental Lemma, Theorem
6.1.1
. In particular, it follows directly from
Theorem
6.1.1
that the Fundamental Lemma
1.2.1
holds for quasi-split reductive groups of large enough residue characteristic
(Corollary
6.1.4
).

2.
Motivic integration with pseudo-finite residue field

In this section we recall what we need about motivic integration, specialized to the case of pseudo-finite residue field. We moreover extend the construction for motivic integrals to take values in the Grothendieck groups of étale motives, and show that many compatibility properties continue to hold in this context, such as the Fubini theorem.

2.1.
Pseudo-finite fields

A pseudo-finite field is a field satisfying all first order sentences in the ring language that are true in every finite field.
More concretely, by Ax’s theorem
[
2
]
a field
K
is pseudo-finite if and only if it is perfect, admits a unique extension of degree
n
for each

K
-variety admits a

T_{\mathrm{Psf}}
the corresponding theory. For a field

T_{\mathrm{Psf},k}
the theory of pseudo-finite fields containing

\mathrm{RDef}_{k}
the category of definable sets in the theory of pseudo-finite fields containing

\mathrm{\bf K}(\mathrm{RDef}_{k})
be the corresponding Grothendieck ring

denotes their dimension.

(3)

For every
X\in\Sch_{S}^{G}
, the map

\alpha\in R(G)\longmapsto\chi_{S}(X,\alpha)\in\mathrm{\bf K}(\DAC(S,\Lambda))\otimes\mathbb{Q}

is a group morphism.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in G^{*}
be the character of an irreducible
\Lambda
-representation
V_{\alpha}
of
G
of dimension
n_{\alpha}
. View
V_{\alpha}
as an object of
\DAC(S,\Lambda)
endowed with a
G
-action, by identifying it with
\bigoplus_{1\leq i\leq n_{\alpha}}\mathds{1}_{S}
. Let
p\colon X\to S\in\Sch_{S}^{G}
. Define

\chi_{S}([X],\alpha)=[\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(V_{\alpha},p_{!}(\mathds{1}_{X}))^{G}],

where
\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}
is the internal Hom.

By additivity of
p_{!}
, as in the proof of
[
30

, this extends uniquely to a group morphism

\chi_{S}(-,\alpha):\mathrm{\bf K}(\Sch_{S}^{G})\longrightarrow\mathrm{\bf K}(\DAC(S,\Lambda)).

Property
(1)
now follows from the standard fact that
\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(V_{\alpha},M)^{G}\otimes V_{\alpha}
is isomorphic to

,
see
[

for details. Property

follows from the fact that

\chi_{S}(-,\alpha)
for a virtual character

∎

The following proposition collects a few useful functorialities.

Proposition 2.2.3
.

Let
G
and

be finite groups,

,

(1)

(2)

Assume that

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Proposition 3.1.2 in

2.3.

2.4.
Motives with definable parameters

We shall need to consider also definable sets with parameters, and to associate motives to those. Let
S\in\mathrm{RDef}_{k}
, say with
n
variables, and let
\mathrm{RDef}_{S}
be the category of definable sets with parameters in
S
,
i.e.
definable sets
X\subseteq{\mathbb{A}}^{m}\times S
together with the projection to
S
. Morphisms are definable bijections preserving the projection to
S
.

Fix
X\in\mathrm{RDef}_{S}
. Since
S\subseteq{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
, there is a canonical map
X\to{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
and we can consider the
motive of
X
with parameters

\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},{\mathbb{A}}^{n}}([X])\in\mathrm{\bf K}(\DAC({\mathbb{A}}^{n},\Lambda))\otimes\mathbb{Q}
.

Since the precise set of parameters is not important, and that we shall need to change it regularly, we adopt the following convention.

Definition 2.4.1
.

Let
S\subseteq{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
be a
definable set and
M
be an element of
\mathrm{\bf K}(\DAC({\mathbb{A}}^{n},\Lambda))
such that
M=M\cdot\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},{\mathbb{A}}^{n}}(S)
.
We call such an
M

a
motive with parameters in
S
, or a
relative motive
.

We define the ring of relative motives
\mathrm{\bf K}(\DAC(\mathrm{rel},\Lambda))
as the colimit of the groups
\mathrm{\bf K}(\DAC({\mathbb{A}}^{n},\Lambda))
under the closed immersions

{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}^{n+1}
given by
x\mapsto(x,0)
.

We adopt the same definition for the various tensor products of
\mathrm{\bf K}(\DAC(\mathrm{rel},\Lambda))
that we shall consider, such as
\mathrm{\bf K}(\DAC(\mathrm{rel},\Lambda))\otimes\mathbb{Q}
. We also extend the construction to motives over some base scheme
A
, which gives
\mathrm{\bf K}(\DAC(A,\mathrm{rel},\Lambda))
, with elements motives in some
\mathrm{\bf K}(\DAC(A\times{\mathbb{A}}^{n},\Lambda))
for some
n
.
Similarly, given a definable set
X\in\mathrm{RDef}_{S}
with parameters
S\subseteq{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
, we write
\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}([X])\in\mathrm{\bf K}(\DAC(\mathrm{rel},\Lambda))\otimes\mathbb{Q}
for
\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},{\mathbb{A}}^{n}}([X])
.

2.5.
Denef-Pas language and definable subassignments

We use in this paper the framework for motivic integration developed by Cluckers and Loeser in
[
9
]
. See also
[
7
]
for an introduction.

Fix a field
k
of characteristic zero. The theory takes place in the Denef-Pas 3-sorted language
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},k}
in the sense of first order logic. It has one sort for the valued field with the ring language, one sort for the residue field with ring language, and one sort for the value group, with Presburger arithmetic language (0,1,+,
\leq
,
(\equiv_{n})_{n\in\N})
. It also has two unary function symbols,
\ord
from the valued field sort to the value group, interpreted as the valuation, and
\ac
from the valued field sort to the residue field, interpreted as an angular component. It also has constant symbols for elements of
k\mathopen{(\!(}t\mathopen{)\!)}
.

Given an extension
K
of
k
, we view naturally the field
K\mathopen{(\!(}t\mathopen{)\!)}
as an
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},k}
-structure, as follows. The underlying sets are
(K\mathopen{(\!(}t\mathopen{)\!)},K,\mathbb{Z})
. The function symbol
\ord
is interpreted as the valuation,
\ac
as the angular component, which is the first non-zero coefficient of
x\in K\mathopen{(\!(}t\mathopen{)\!)}
if
x\neq 0
and 0 otherwise. The relation
\equiv_{n}
is interpreted as congruence modulo
n
.

One considers the category
\mathrm{Def}_{k}
of definable objects in such structures, defined as follows. Let
\varphi
be an
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},k}
-formula, with respectively
m
,
r
and
s
free variables in the three sorts. For every extension
K
of
k
, we consider the subset
h_{\varphi}(K)\subseteq(K\mathopen{(\!(}t\mathopen{)\!)})^{m}\times K^{r}\times\mathbb{Z}^{s}
consisting of points satisfying
\varphi
. An object
S\in\mathrm{Def}_{k}
, called a definable subassignment (or definable set), is a map from the set of extensions
K
of
k
to sets such that there is some
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},k}
-formula
\varphi
such that
S(K)=h_{\varphi}(K)
for every
K
. One also considers general definable subassignments
\mathrm{GDef}_{k}
, which objects are definable subassignments of algebraic varieties over
k
, defined similarly using affine charts.

In this paper, we specialize this situation to the case of pseudo-finite residue fields. Let
T_{\mathrm{Psf},k}
the theory of pseudo-finite fields containing
k
. We work in the category denoted by
\mathrm{Def}_{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP}},T_{\mathrm{Psf}})
in
[
9
, Section 16]
, that we shall denote by
\mathrm{Def}_{\mathrm{Psf},k}
. Let
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{Psf},k}
be the extension of the Denef-Pas language
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},k}
where we add symbols for Galois formulas in the residue field. An object
S\in\mathrm{Def}_{\mathrm{Psf},k}
is a map from the set of pseudo-finite fields
K
extending
k
to sets that are of the form
S(K)=h_{\varphi}(K)
for some
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{Psf},k}
-formula
\varphi
. Combining Denef-Pas elimination of valued fields quantifiers with Fried-Jarden quantifier elimination for pseudo-finite fields, we see that in the definition of
S\in\mathrm{Def}_{\mathrm{Psf},k}
, we can choose the
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{Psf},k}
-formula
\varphi
to be quantifier free. The category
\mathrm{GDef}_{\mathrm{Psf},k}
is defined similarly.

We also write
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Psf},k}(S)
or
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Psf}}(S)
for
\mathcal{C}(S,(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP}},T_{\mathrm{Psf},k}))
, the ring of motivic constructible functions on
S\in\mathrm{GDef}_{\mathrm{Psf},k}
, when there is no risk of confusion.

Let
*_{k}
the final object in
\mathrm{GDef}_{\mathrm{Psf},k}
, whose value at every pseudo-finite extension of
k
is the one point set.
By definition

\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Psf}}(*_{k})=\mathrm{\bf K}(\mathrm{RDef}_{\mathrm{Psf},k})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{L}]}\mathbb{A},

where
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{Z}\left[\mathbb{L},\mathbb{L}^{-1},\left(\frac{1}{1-\mathbb{L}^{-i}}\right)_{i>0}\right]
.

2.6.
Evaluation of functions

We fix a Grothendieck universe
\mathcal{U}
containing
k
. This is merely a matter of convenience,
it follows from standard arguments relying on the reflection principle, cf.
[
17
]
,
that our main results are in fact valid within
\mathrm{ZFC}
.
Let
S\in\mathrm{GDef}_{\mathrm{Psf},k}
. We define the set of points
\left\lvert S\right\rvert
as the set of pairs
(x,K)
with
K\in\mathcal{U}
a pseudo-finite field containing
k
and
x\in S(K)
. We sometimes write abusively
x\in\left\lvert S\right\rvert
.

For such an
x\in S(K)
, let
k(x)
be the definable closure of
x
intersected with the residue field
K
and consider the language
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{Psf},k,x}
, where we add constants for
k(x)
. Note that
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{Psf},k,x}
is smaller than
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{Psf},k(x)}
, since we do not add constants symbols for
k(x)\mathopen{(\!(}t\mathopen{)\!)}
in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{Psf},k,x}
.

Consider the category of pseudo-finite fields
K^{\prime}
containing
k(x)
, and the category
\mathrm{Def}_{\mathrm{Psf},k,x}
such that its objects
X\in\mathrm{Def}_{\mathrm{Psf},k,x}
consists of maps associating to
K^{\prime}
as above
h_{\varphi}(K^{\prime})
for
\varphi
an
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{Psf},k,x}
-formula. This new theory falls into the framework of
[
9
, Section 2.7]
, hence we define similarly
\mathrm{GDef}_{\mathrm{Psf},k,x}
,
\mathrm{RDef}_{\mathrm{Psf},k,x}
,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Psf},k,x}(X)
,
etc
. Note that for the one point definable subassignment
*
, we have

\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Psf},k,x}(*)=\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Psf},k(x)}(*_{k(x)})=\mathrm{\bf K}(\mathrm{RDef}_{\mathrm{Psf},k(x)})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{L}]}\mathbb{A}.

A constructible motivic function
\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Psf},k}(S)
can be evaluated at
(x,K)\in\left\lvert S\right\rvert
as follows.

Since
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{Psf},k,x}
is an expansion of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{Psf},k}
, by
[
9
, Proposition 16.1.1]
, every
\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Psf},k}(S)
induces a unique function in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Psf},k,x}(S)
, that we still denote abusively
\psi
. By cell decomposition, or more precisely
[
9
, Theorem 7.2.1]
, there exists an
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{Psf},k,x}
-definable subassignment
V_{x}\subseteq S
, such that
x\in V_{x}(K)
, a definable function
g\colon V_{x}\to*
and
\psi_{x}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Psf},k,x}(*)
such that
g^{*}(\psi_{x})=\psi
. We define the evaluation of
\psi
at
(x,K)
to be
\psi(x)=\psi_{x}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Psf},k,x}(*)=\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Psf},k(x)}(*_{k(x)})
.

Remark 2.6.1
.

Our notion of evaluation at a point differs from the one used by Cluckers and Halupczok in
[
8
]
. They instead define the evaluation at a point
(x,K)
by working in the complete diagram with parameters a tuple of constants for the tuple of coordinates of
x
. More precisely, let
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{Psf},k}(x)
be the expansion of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{Psf},k}
by a constant symbol for each coordinate of
x
. Then they consider fields
K^{\prime}
such that
(K^{\prime}\mathopen{(\!(}t\mathopen{)\!)},K^{\prime},\mathbb{Z})
is elementary equivalent to
(K\mathopen{(\!(}t\mathopen{)\!)},K,\mathbb{Z})
in the language
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{Psf},k}(x)
. Since this theory is an expansion of ours in the
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{Psf},k,x}
-language, our notion of evaluation will determine the notion of evaluation in
[
8
]
.

Hence the following proposition follows readily from
[
8
, Theorem 1]
:

Proposition 2.6.2
.

A constructible motivic function is determined by its evaluation at points. More precisely, let
S\in\mathrm{GDef}_{\mathrm{Psf},k}
and
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Psf},k}(S)
. Then
\varphi=\psi
if and only if, for every
(x,K)\in\left\lvert S\right\rvert
,

\varphi(x)=\psi(x)
in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Psf},k(x)}(*_{k(x)})
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Psf},k}(S)
such that for every
(x,K)\in\left\lvert S\right\rvert
,
\varphi(x)=\psi(x)\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Psf},k(x)}(*_{k(x)})
. The evaluation of a constructible motivic function
\psi
at a point
(x,K)\in\left\lvert S\right\rvert
in the sense of
[
8
]
is defined as
i_{x}^{*}(\psi)
, where
i_{x}\colon\left\{x\right\}\to S
is the inclusion, which is definable in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{Psf},k}(x)
. Using the notations
V_{x},g
in the definition of evaluation, since
x\in V_{x}(K)
, we have
i_{x}^{*}(\psi)=i_{x}^{*}(\psi_{\mid V_{x}})=i_{x}^{*}g^{*}\psi_{x}
. Hence the evaluations (in the sense of
[
8
]
) of
\varphi
and
\psi
at every point
(x,K)\in\left\lvert S\right\rvert
are equal, therefore by
[
8
, Theorem 1]
,
\varphi=\psi
in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Psf},k}(S)
.
∎

2.8.

We say that

\mathrm{Kdim}(S)=d

S\in\mathrm{Def}_{\mathrm{Psf},k}
of dimension

\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Psf}}(S)

(the presence of a denominator in the formula)

The theorem also follows from a more general orbifold formula for Artin stacks, that will appear in the authors’ upcoming work in preparation.

3.
Geometric setup

In this section we introduce the relevant constructions for the formulation of the Geometric Stabilization theorem

. The main reference is Section 4 of
loc. cit.

Throughout this section let

Since a first order sentence in the language of rings holds in every pseudo-finite field of characteristic zero if and only it holds for all finite fields of large enough characteristic, see
[

We fix

3.4.

Let

This is a summary of

4.
Definability of Geometric Stabilization

In this section we show that the various objects appearing in the statement of the Geometric Stabilization theorem are definable in the theory of pseudo-finite fields and that its statement is expressible as an equality between motivic integrals. Part of this discussion is similar to
[
7
]
(see also
[
20
]
), where it is proven that the statement of the Fundamental Lemma is expressible as an equality between motivic integrals.

4.1.
Field extensions

Let
K
be a pseudo-finite field. We shall need to express conditions related to finite Galois extensions
L
of
K
. The ring language does not allow to do this directly, however, as long as the degree
\deg(L/K)=r
is fixed (or at least bounded), we can emulate it within the ring language as follows.

We describe
L
by using the minimal polynomial
m
of an element
\alpha
generating
L/K
. The polynomial
m_{b}=x^{r}+b_{r-1}x^{r-1}+\dots+b_{0}
is described via its tuple of coefficients
b=(b_{0},\dots,b_{r-1})\in K^{r}
. The following conditions can be described with ring formulas with free parameter
b
. We can express the fact that
m_{b}
is irreducible in
K[x]
. We then use the explicit basis
\left\{1,x,\dots,x^{r-1}\right\}
of
K[x]/(m_{b})=L
to identify
L
with
K^{r}
. Since
K
is pseudo-finite,
L/K
is Galois, with Galois group cyclic of order
r
. The Galois group
\Gal(L/K)
can now be described as well, viewing its elements as linear automorphisms of
K^{r}
respecting the field structure. We write the set of elements of
\Gal(L/K)
as
\Gal(L/K)=\left\{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r}\right\}
.

Hence we find that there is a ring formula
\varphi(b,\tau)
such that for every pseudo-finite field
K
,
\varphi(b,\tau)
is satisfied exactly when
m_{b}
is irreducible in
K[x]
and
\tau
is a generator of the Galois group of

K_{b,r}=K[x]/m_{b}
over
K
.

Given a definable set
X
, there is a definable set
X_{r}
with parameters
b,\tau
such that the
K
-points of
X_{r}
are identified with
X(L)
for
L
a Galois extension of degree
r
.

4.2.
Galois cohomology

We can also encode part of Galois cohomology as follows. Let
K
be a pseudo-finite field and
L
a finite Galois extension of
K
. Let
A
be a definable
\Gal(L/K)
-module, by which we mean that
A
is definable and the
\Gal(L/K)
-action as well. For example
A=X(L)
, where
X
is definable. We represent the elements of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gal(L/K),A)
using 1-cocycles. We view a 1-cocycle as a tuple
(c_{1},\dots,c_{r})\in A^{r}
corresponding to elements
(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r})
of
\Gal(L/K)
. The cocycle condition is then expressed by the definable condition

\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=\sigma_{k}\implies c_{i}\sigma_{i}(c_{j})=c_{k}.

We encode the fact that two cocycles
c,c’
are cohomologous if there exists a 1-coboundary
d
such that
c=c’d
. Since this is a definable equivalence relation on 1-cocycles, this allows to consider
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gal(L/K),A)
as an imaginary set (with parameters
(b,\tau)
).

If
A\to B
is a definable map between
\Gal(L/K)
-modules, then we get a definable map between
1
-cocyles for
A
and
1
-cocycles for
B
.

Basic facts about Galois cohomology are now expressible in a first-order way.

Proposition 4.2.1
.

Let
G
be a finite commutative group of order
r
. There is a definable bijection with parameters
(b,\tau)
such that for every pseudo-finite field
K
,

(4.2.1)

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gal(K_{b,r}/K),G)\simeq G.

determined by the chosen generator
\tau
of
\Gal(K_{b,r}/K)
.

Proof.

The image of the generator
\tau
of
\Gal(K_{b,r}/K)
determines a definable map

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gal(K_{b,r}/K),G)\to G
.

To show that it is a bijection, it is enough to show that it is bijective for every finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In this case, the map becomes

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gal(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}),G)\longrightarrow G,

determined by the image of a generator of
\Gal(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Since
r
is the order of
G
, it is well-known that it is bijective.
∎

Proposition 4.2.2
.

Let
{\mathbf{P}}
be a definable commutative algebraic group. Assume that
{\mathbf{P}}
is connected. Then for every pseudo-finite field
K
and
r\geq 1
,

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gal(K_{b,r}/K),{\mathbf{P}}(K_{b,r}))\simeq\left\{0\right\}.

Proof.

Since for an algebraic group being connected is equivalent to being irreducible, it follows from the following Proposition
4.2.3
that
the condition for
{\mathbf{P}}
to be connected is definable.

As in the previous proposition, to show the result it is enough to show that for every finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gal(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}),{\mathbf{P}}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}))\simeq\left\{0\right\}.

Since
{\mathbf{P}}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})
is the set of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}
-points of a connected commutative algebraic group over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, this holds by Lang’s theorem.
∎

Proposition 4.2.3
.

Let
f:X\to\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
be a quasi-projective morphism. Then the locus of irreducible fibers is definable, namely, there exists a formula

\varphi(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m})
in the (first-order) language of rings such that, for any field
K
and
b=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{m})
in
K^{m}
,
the fiber
X_{b}
is irreducible if and only
\varphi(b_{1},\cdots,b_{m})
holds in
K
.

Proof.

By working in affine charts it is enough to consider the case where
f
is affine (of finite type). In this case the statement follows directly from statements (I) and (II) in the introduction of
[
39
]
, see page 79 of
[
39
]
for an explicit statement.
∎

Proposition 4.2.4
.

Let
{\mathbf{P}}
be a definable commutative algebraic group with constant group
\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}})
of connected components of order
r
. There is a definable bijection with parameters
(b,\tau)
such that for every pseudo-finite field
K

(4.2.2)

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gal(K_{b,r}/K),{\mathbf{P}}(K_{b,r}))\simeq\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gal(K_{b,r}/K),\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}})).

Proof.

We have a definable map

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gal(K_{b,r}/K),{\mathbf{P}}(K_{b,r}))\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gal(K_{b,r}/K),\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}})(K_{b,r})).

Since this map is surjective when
K
is replaced by a finite field, it is also surjective for pseudo-finite fields. It is injective by Proposition
4.2.2
.
∎

Combining Propositions
4.2.1
and
4.2.4
above, we finally get:

Proposition 4.2.5
.

Let
{\mathbf{P}}
be a definable commutative algebraic group, with constant group
\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}})
of connected components of order
r
. There are definable bijections with parameters
(b,\tau)
such that for every pseudo-finite field
K
,

(4.2.3)

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gal(K_{b,r}/K),{\mathbf{P}}(K_{b,r}))\simeq\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gal(K_{b,r}/K),\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}}))\simeq\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}}).

Those bijections are explicitly described at the level of cocycles. For
g\in\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}})
, one builds a 1-cocycle
f_{g}
by sending the generator
\tau
of
\Gal(K_{b,r}/K)
to
g
. Then this cocycle can be lifted to
{\mathbf{P}}(K_{b,r})
: there exists an element of
x\in{\mathbf{P}}(K_{b,r})
such that sending
\tau
to
x
determines a
1
-cocycle that is sent to
f_{g}
by the morphism
{\mathbf{P}}(K_{b,r})\to\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}})
.

4.3.
Twisted varieties

Let
{\mathbf{X}}
be a definable variety and
{\mathbf{P}}
a definable commutative group acting definably on
{\mathbf{X}}
.

Fix a pseudo-finite field
K
. Suppose we are given a
1
-cocycle

f\colon\Gal(K_{b,r}/K)\longrightarrow{\mathbf{P}}(K_{b,r}).

By composing this cocycle with the action of
{\mathbf{P}}(K_{b,r})
on
{\mathbf{X}}(K_{b,r})
, we get for each
\sigma\in\Gal(K_{b,r}/K)
an automorphism of
{\mathbf{X}}(K_{b,r})
. Define
{\mathbf{X}}^{f}(K)
as the set of fixed points of
{\mathbf{X}}(K_{b,r})
under this system of automorphisms. This gives a formula with parameters
b,\tau,f
for a definable set
{\mathbf{X}}^{f}
such that for every pseudo-finite field
K
,
{{\mathbf{X}}}^{f}(K)
is the set of fixed points of the system of automorphisms.

The system of automorphisms is a Galois descent datum for the variety corresponding to
{\mathbf{X}}
over
K
, hence
{{\mathbf{X}}}^{f}
is a definable algebraic variety.

4.4.
Isotypical components

Keep notations as above, with
{\mathbf{X}}
a definable algebraic variety with an action of a definable commutative algebraic group
{\mathbf{P}}
. Let
\alpha

\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}})(K_{b,r})=\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}})(K^{\mathrm{alg}})
. Consider the motivic version

of

Definition 4.4.1
.

The
\alpha

is

\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}({\mathbf{X}},\alpha):=\frac{1}{\left\lvert H\right\rvert}\sum_{t\in H}\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}({{\mathbf{X}}}^{t})\,\alpha_{\mathrm{mot}}(t)

in

Thanks to the following remark, it is consistent with the notion introduced in Proposition

Note that the multiplicities

appearing in Proposition

are here equal to 1 since

Remark 4.4.2
.

Assume that

is a constant finite commutative group. Let
\alpha
be a character of

, and

be a

-variety with action of

. Then Proposition

states that

\frac{1}{\left\lvert H\right\rvert}\sum_{g\in H}\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}(X^{g})\,\alpha_{\mathrm{mot}}(g)=\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}(X,\alpha)\,)\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}([S])

in
\mathrm{\bf K}(\DAC(\mathrm{rel},\Lambda))\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
, where

is the set of parameters.

We extend the definition to the category of definable sets with action of

over a base

.
When

.

Finally let us record the following lemma for later use.

Lemma 4.4.3
.

Let
{\mathbf{X}}
a definable algebraic variety with an action of a definable commutative algebraic group
{\mathbf{P}}
. Assume further that we have a subgroup
{\mathbf{P}}^{\square}\subseteq{\mathbf{P}}
consisting of a union of connected components of
{\mathbf{P}}
, that is
{\mathbf{P}}/{\mathbf{P}}^{\square}\cong\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}})/\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}}^{\square})
, and a subvariety
{\mathbf{X}}^{\square}\subseteq{\mathbf{X}}
such that
{\mathbf{X}}\cong{\mathbf{X}}^{\square}\times^{{\mathbf{P}}^{\square}}{\mathbf{P}}
as
{\mathbf{P}}
-varieties. Let
r
be a large enough integer such that
\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}})(K_{b,r})=\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}})(K^{\alg})
.

Then we have for any character
\alpha
of
H=\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gal(K_{b,r}/K),\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}})(K_{b,r}))
the equality

\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}({\mathbf{X}},\alpha)=\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}({\mathbf{X}}^{\square},\alpha_{|H^{\square}}),

where
H^{\square}=\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gal(K_{b,r}/K),\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}}^{\square})(K_{b,r}))
.

Proof.

The isomorphism
{\mathbf{X}}\cong{\mathbf{X}}^{\square}\times^{{\mathbf{P}}^{\square}}{\mathbf{P}}
implies that we have for any
t\in H
a definable morphism
{\mathbf{X}}^{t}\to\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}})^{t}/\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}}^{\square})
. The
\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}})/\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}}^{\square})
-torsor
\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}})^{t}/\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}}^{\square})
is trivial if and only if
t\in H^{\square}
and thus
{\mathbf{X}}^{t}
can only admit a
K
-rational point for
t\in H^{\square}
. This implies that
\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}({\mathbf{X}}^{t})=0
for all
t\notin H^{\square}
and

\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}({\mathbf{X}}^{t})=\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}({\mathbf{X}}^{\square,t}\times\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}})/\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}}^{\square}))=\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}({\mathbf{X}}^{\square,t})|\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}})/\pi_{0}({\mathbf{P}}^{\square})|.

Since the absolute Galois group
\Gal(K)=\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
, say with topological generator
\sigma
, we have for any finite commutative group scheme
\Gamma
an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\Gamma(K)\longrightarrow\Gamma(K^{\mathrm{alg}})\xrightarrow{\>1-\sigma\>}\Gamma(K^{\mathrm{alg}})\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(K,\Gamma)\longrightarrow 0,

and thus
|\Gamma(K)|=|\mathrm{H}^{1}(K,\Gamma)|
. Putting all this together we get

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}({\mathbf{X}},\alpha)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left\lvert H\right\rvert}\sum_{t\in H}\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}({{\mathbf{X}}}^{t})\alpha_{\mathrm{mot}}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left\lvert H\right\rvert}\sum_{t\in H^{\square}}\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}({{\mathbf{X}}}^{\square,t})|H/H^{\square}|\alpha_{\mathrm{mot}}(t)=\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}({\mathbf{X}}^{\square},\alpha_{|H^{\square}}).\qed

4.5.
Split reductive groups

Recall that the classification of split connected reductive groups is independent of the base field and that their isomorphism classes are in bijection with root data
\mathcal{D}=({\mathbb{X}}^{*},\Phi,{\mathbb{X}}_{*},\Phi^{\vee})
, which are the character group of a split torus, the set of roots, the cocharacter group, and the sets of coroots. Each split reductive group can be realized over
k
. Fix a faithful representation
\delta\colon{\mathbb{G}}\to\GL(V)
for a
k
-vector space
V
, and
{\mathbb{G}}
the group associated to the root datum
\mathcal{D}
. Fix also a basis of
V
. Then there exists a ring formula describing
\delta({\mathbb{G}})\subseteq\GL(V)
.

4.6.
Quasi-split groups

Let
X
be a smooth projective curve over
k
, viewed as a definable set. For later purposes, suppose that we are also given a rational point
\infty\in X(k)
. Let
D
be a line bundle of large enough even degree

on
X
, in particular such that

is ample. Hence a power of

defines a closed embedding of

into
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
for some

. We identify

with its image in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
. For later purposes, we also assume that

is a square, i.e. that there exists

such that
{D^{\prime}}^{\otimes 2}\simeq D
.

Let
\rho
be an
\Out({\mathbb{G}})
-torsor over
X
that is trivial over
\infty\in X
. By
[
22
, Lemma 4.8]
there is a finite étale cover

of

over which

becomes trivial, and we can moreover assume that

is Galois. We then view

as a morphism
\Aut(X^{\prime}/X)\to\Out({\mathbb{G}})
, which amounts to choose a finite number of points of
\Out({\mathbb{G}})
satisfying some conditions.
By Section
4.2
, if we fix an integer
r
we get a definable set
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Aut(X^{\prime}/X),\Out({\mathbb{G}}))
representing the subset of
\Out({\mathbb{G}})
-torsors on
X
parametrized by morphisms
\Aut(X^{\prime}/X)\to\Out({\mathbb{G}})
with
X^{\prime}
a finite étale Galois cover of
X
of degree
r
. Fix one such parameter
f
.

A quasi-split form of
{\mathbb{G}}
over
X
is by definition a group of the form
G={\mathbb{G}}\times^{\Out({\mathbb{G}})}\rho
. The group
G
can be viewed as a definable set as follows. Let
r
be the degree of
X^{\prime}\to X
. Let
K
be a pseudo-finite field containing
k
. Let
x\in X(K)
. The map
X^{\prime}\to X
induces a finite Galois extension

of degree at most
r
. The extension

can then be described as above, using a parameter
\tau
for a fixed generator of
\Gal(L_{x}/K)
.

The torsor
\rho
induces at

an element of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gal(L_{x}/K),\Out({\mathbb{G}}))
, and the choice of a generator
\tau\in\Gal(L_{x}/K)
identifies this group with
\Out({\mathbb{G}})
. Let
\rho_{x}\in\Out({\mathbb{G}})
be the automorphism defined by
\rho
at
x
.

The set
G(K)_{x}
of
K
-points of

above

is identified with the set of fixed points of
{\mathbb{G}}(L)
under the endomorphism
\rho_{x}\circ\tau
of
{\mathbb{G}}(L)
. Using the fixed representation
\delta
of
{\mathbb{G}}
and our conventions regarding encoding of

in Section
4.1
, the set

is therefore defined by a formula with parameters. Since this formula is uniform in

, we get a definable

with parameters

such that
{\mathbf{G}}_{x}(K)=G(K)_{x}
for every pseudo-finite field

and

.

Summarizing the above discussion, we can state:

Proposition 4.6.1
.

Fix a smooth projective curve
X
over
k
, a split reductive group
{\mathbb{G}}
determined by its root datum, a fixed faithful representation of
{\mathbb{G}}
, and an integer
r
. Consider an
\Out({\mathbb{G}})
-torsor over
X
that is trivialized over a finite étale cover of
X
of degree
r
, represented by a 1-cocycle

. Then there is a definable algebraic group
{\mathbf{G}}
with parameters
X,b,\tau,f
such that for every pseudo-finite field
K
extending
k
and

,
{\mathbf{G}}_{x}(K)=G(K)_{x}
, where
G
is the quasi-split form of
{\mathbb{G}}
over
X
determined by
f
.

The lattice of characters
{\mathbb{X}}^{*}={\mathbb{X}}^{*}({\mathbb{T}})
of
{\mathbb{G}}
is similarly endowed with a Galois action. Its sublattice of fixed points is the lattice of characters of

, the fixed (non-split) maximal torus of

. We identify
{\mathbb{X}}^{*}
with
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
by fixing a basis. Since the Galois action on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
is definable (in the sense that the matrix defining it in the chosen basis is definable), we get a finite definable set whose points form a basis of the sublattice
{\mathbb{X}}^{*}(T)
.

The fixed maximal torus is also represented by a definable algebraic group
{\mathbf{T}}
. The Lie algebras
{\mathbf{g}}
and

are also defined as a set of fixed points using the
\Out({\mathbb{G}})
torsor, hence definable as well.

Given a split reductive group

4.8.
Geometric Stabilization

We can now give the precise statement of the motivic version of Geometric Stabilization.

We fix a field
k
of characteristic zero, a smooth projective curve
X
of genus
g
over
k
with a rational point
\infty\in X(k)
, an even integer
d
, larger or equal to

and an integer

. We view

embedded in projective space as in Section

on

of degree

, given by trivializations on open charts. As in Section

, we consider a definable quasi-split reductive group
{\mathbf{G}}
over

, with parameters

.

We fix a definable coendoscopic datum of
\widehat{\mathbf{G}}
,

which determines a coendoscopic group
{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathcal{E}}
of

. We assume that

We consider the Hitchin fibrations

with its image in
\widetilde{{\mathbf{A}}}^{\mathrm{ani}}

The homomorphism

corresponds to an element in

. We consider
\kappa
as a character of
{\mathbb{X}}_{*}({\mathbb{T}})
by identifying
{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
with the roots of unity in

via

. If follows from
[

that

when restricted to

. Thus we obtain a well-defined

-isotypical component

in the sense of Section

, hence used to defined twisted definable sets as in Section

.

For

we then get a smooth DM-stack
\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}^{\square,t}_{G,U_{a}}
and a corresponding definable
\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}^{\square,t}_{G,B_{a}}
.

Recall from Section
2.13
that we have a definable evaluation morphism

(5.3.1)

e:\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}^{\square,t,\natural}_{G,B_{a}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{I}_{\widehat{\mu}}\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}^{\square,t}_{G},

and Theorem
5.2.3
is equivalent to

Theorem 5.3.1
.

Let
K
be a pseudo-finite field
K
. For every
a\in\widetilde{{\mathbf{A}}}^{{\mathrm{ani}}}(K)
, every

and every

, we have

\int^{\mathrm{mot}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}^{\square,t,\natural}_{G,B_{a}}}\chi_{s}\circ e\ \mu_{\mathrm{orb}}=\int^{\mathrm{mot}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}^{\square,s,\natural}_{\widehat{G},B_{a}}}\chi_{t}\circ e\ \mu_{\mathrm{orb}}.

Proof that Theorems
5.3.1
and
5.2.3
are equivalent.

This is a direct application of the orbifold formula, Theorem

, which implies that

\int^{\mathrm{mot}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}^{\square,t,\natural}_{G,B_{a}}}\chi_{s}\circ e\ \mu_{\mathrm{orb}}=\int^{\mathrm{mot}}_{I_{\hat{\mu}}\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}^{\square,t}_{G,a}}\mathbb{L}^{-w}\chi_{s}

and

\int^{\mathrm{mot}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}^{\square,s,\natural}_{\widehat{G},B_{a}}}\chi_{t}\circ e\ \mu_{\mathrm{orb}}=\int^{\mathrm{mot}}_{I_{\hat{\mu}}\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}^{\square,s}_{\widehat{G},a}}\mathbb{L}^{-w}\chi_{t}.

Hence Theorems

and

are equivalent.
∎

5.4.
Integration on generic fibers

A key insight from
[
22
]
is that using a Fubini theorem, Theorem
5.3.1
can be proved one generically smooth Hitchin fiber at a time.

More precisely we let
B_{a}^{\flat}
denote the definable assignment of points in
{\mathbf{A}}(K\mathopen{[\![}t\mathopen{]\!]})
that specialize to
a
and whose generic fiber lies in the smooth locus of the Hitchin fibration.

In the case of the Hitchin fibration, the measure
\mu_{\mathrm{orb}}
is given by a global volume form
\left\lvert\omega_{\mathrm{orb}}\right\rvert
, as explained in
[
22
, Lemma 6.13]
. For each
b\in B_{a}^{\flat}
, write
\omega_{b}
for the volume forms on the smooth fibers
\widetilde{M}^{\square,t}_{G,b}
and
\widetilde{M}^{\square,s}_{\widehat{G},b}
obtained as a quotient of
\omega_{\mathrm{orb}}
by a fixed volume form
\eta
on the Hitchin base
\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}}^{{\mathrm{ani}}}
. Proposition
2.9.4
, a version of Fubini’s theorem, shows that

\int_{\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}^{\square,t}_{G,B_{a}}}\chi_{s}\circ e\ \mu_{\mathrm{orb}}=\int_{b\in B_{a}^{\flat}}\left\lvert\eta\right\rvert\int_{\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}^{\square,t}_{G,b}}\chi_{s}\circ e|\omega_{b}|.

We have a similar equality for the dual group, hence Theorem
5.3.1
is implied by the following theorem.

Theorem 5.4.1
.

For any
b\in B_{a}^{\flat}
we have an equality

\int_{\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}^{\square,t}_{G,b}}\chi_{s}\circ e|\omega_{b}|=\int_{\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}^{\square,s}_{\widehat{G},b}}\chi_{t}\circ e|\omega_{b}|.

5.5.
Proof of Theorem
5.4.1

Certainly the integral over the definable set
\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}^{\square,t}_{G,b}
is zero if for every pseudofinite field
K^{\prime}
containing
k(b)
, the set
\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}^{\square,t}_{G,b}(K^{\prime})
is empty. By construction,
\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}^{\square,t}_{G,b}(K^{\prime})
is the set of
K^{\prime}
-points of the
\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square,t}_{G,b}
-torsor
\widetilde{M}^{\square,t}_{G,b}
.

Let
{\mathcal{N}}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b})
be the Néron model of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b}
and
\overline{{\mathcal{N}}}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b})
its special fiber. For fixed
b
, we get a corresponding definable set
{\mathcal{N}}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b})
, but not uniformly in
b
.

By the Néron mapping property we have a morphism
\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}_{G,a}\to\overline{{\mathcal{N}}}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b})
, which induces one on component groups

i:\pi_{0}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}_{G,a})\longrightarrow\phi_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b}}=\pi_{0}(\overline{{\mathcal{N}}}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b})).

By the following lemma, this map detects whether
\widetilde{M}^{\square,t}_{G,b}
has rational points.

Lemma 5.5.1
.

The
\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b}
-torsor
\widetilde{M}^{\square,t}_{G,b}
is trivial if and only if
i(t)=0
.

Proof.

By construction
\widetilde{M}^{\square,t}_{G,b}
is unramified, i.e. splits after a finite extension of the residue field
K^{\prime}
. By
[
5
, Cor. 6.5.3]
, this torsor extends uniquely to an
{\mathcal{N}}\left(\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b}\right)
-torsor
{\mathcal{T}}
.
Since the extension is unique and
{\mathcal{T}}/K^{\prime}\mathopen{[\![}t\mathopen{]\!]}
is smooth,
\widetilde{M}^{\square,t}_{G,b}
is trivial if and only if its restriction to its special fiber
{\mathcal{T}}_{K^{\prime}}
is. The latter is the unramified
\overline{{\mathcal{N}}}\left(\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b}\right)
-torsor corresponding to
i(t)
and thus trivial if and only if
i(t)=0
.
∎

Using Lemma
5.5.1
we will identify
\widetilde{M}^{\square,t}_{G,b}
with
\widetilde{M}^{\square}_{G,b}
whenever
i(t)=0
.

Lemma 5.5.2
.

Assume
i(t)=0
. Then the function
\chi_{s}\circ e
is constant on
\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}^{\square}_{G,b}
if and only if
i(s)=0
.

Proof.

From its definition,
\chi_{s}
takes a finite number of values, say indexed by a finite group
\mu
. Since it is a constructible function, there is a
b
-definable partition of
\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}^{\square}_{G,b}
into parts
(X_{g})_{g\in\mu}
such that on
X_{g}
, the function
\chi_{s}
is equal to
g
. The statement of the lemma is then equivalent to the truth of a first-order sentence
\phi(b)
with parameter
b
. We then need to show that
\phi(b)
holds for generic
b
. By Propositions
2.10.1
and
4.8.4
, the specialization
\phi_{L}(b)
of
\phi(b)
to a local field
L
of large enough residue characteristic is equivalent to the equivalent lemma for an instance of the
Geometric Stabilization theorem over a reductive group over
L
. For generic
b
, the fact that
\phi_{L}(b)
holds is the key ingredient in the proof of Lemma 6.14 in
[
22
]
. By Proposition
2.10.1
, we conclude that
\phi(b)
holds for generic
b
.
∎

Lemma 5.5.3
.

Assume
i(t)=0
. Identify
\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}^{\square}_{G,b}
with
\widetilde{{\mathbf{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b}
by means of the Kostant section. The function
\chi_{s}\circ e
is a motivic character on
\widetilde{{\mathbf{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b}
and factors through the group of connected components of the Néron model of
\widetilde{{\mathbf{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b}
.

Proof.

Similarly to Lemma
5.5.2
, the multiplicativity of
\chi_{s}\circ e
is implied by the multiplicativity of its specialization to local fields, where it follows from a reinterpretation
\chi_{s}\circ e
in terms of Tate duality, see
[
22
, Section 6.5]
. Since the Néron model is not in general definable in families, we cannot readily deduce the factorisation property. However, using multiplicativity, it suffices to show that
\chi_{s}\circ e
is constant to 1 on the neutral component of the Néron model. Assume not, let
x
in the neutral component of the Néron model be such that
\chi_{s}\circ e(x)\neq 1
. Since elements in the neutral component of a Néron model are divisible, up to replacing
K^{\prime}
by a finite extension
K^{\prime}_{N}
, we can assume that there exists
y\in\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b}(K^{\prime}_{N}\mathopen{(\!(}t\mathopen{)\!)})
such that
y^{N}=x
, where
N
is the order of the image of
\chi_{s}\circ e
, and the extension
K^{\prime}_{N}
is of degree depending only on
N
. Since this extension is pseudo-finite, the function
\chi_{s}\circ e
is defined as well on this extension, multiplicative, and compatible with the restriction to
K^{\prime}
. By compatibility of the Néron model with unramified base change,
x
lies in the neutral component of the Néron model of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b}(K^{\prime}_{N}\mathopen{(\!(}t\mathopen{)\!)})
. Hence we get a contradiction, since if
\chi_{s}\circ e(x)\neq 1
, then
\chi_{s}\circ e(y)
is of order more than
N
.
∎

Proposition 5.5.4
.

We have

\int^{\mathrm{mot}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}^{\square,t}_{G,b}}\chi_{s}\circ e\left\lvert\omega_{b}\right\rvert=\begin{cases}0\ \ \ \text{ if }i(t)\neq 0\text{ or }i(s)\neq 0\\
\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}(\overline{{\mathcal{N}}}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b})\mathbb{L}^{-\ord(\omega_{b})})\ \ \ \text{ if }i(t)=0\text{ and }i(s)=0.\end{cases}

Note that by Lemma
5.5.1
, the conditions
i(t)\neq 0
and
i(s)\neq 0
translate into definable conditions.

Proof.

Assume first that
i(t)=0
and
i(s)=0
. By Lemma
5.5.1
we have an isomorphism

\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}^{\square,t}_{G,b}(K^{\prime}\mathopen{(\!(}t\mathopen{)\!)})\cong\widetilde{{\mathbf{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b}(K^{\prime}\mathopen{(\!(}t\mathopen{)\!)}).

By Lemma
5.5.2
,
\chi_{s}\circ e
is constant on
\widetilde{{\mathbf{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b}(K^{\prime}\mathopen{(\!(}t\mathopen{)\!)})
, hence necessarily constant to 1 since it is a group morphism. Hence we have

\int^{\mathrm{mot}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}^{\square,t}_{G,b}}\chi_{s}\circ e\left\lvert\omega_{b}\right\rvert=\int^{\mathrm{mot}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}^{\square,t}_{G,b}}\left\lvert\omega_{b}\right\rvert=\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}(\overline{{\mathcal{N}}}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b})\mathbb{L}^{-\ord(\omega_{b})}).

The last equation follows since the volume of an abelian variety with respect to any volume form equals the class of the special fiber of the Néron model up to a shift by the order of vanishing of the volume form along the special fiber, see for example
[
33
]
or
[
34
, Remark 4.1.1]
.

For the other case, if
i(t)\neq 0
, then by Lemma
5.5.1
the integral is zero. Thus assume finally
i(t)=0
and
i(s)\neq 0
.

By Lemma
5.5.3
,
\chi_{s}\circ e
factors as

(5.5.1)

\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b}(K^{\prime}\mathopen{(\!(}t\mathopen{)\!)})\xrightarrow{r_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b}}}\phi_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b}}(K^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\langle\cdot,i(s)\rangle}{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}.

Each fiber of
r_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b}}

is a
{\mathcal{N}}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b})^{0}
-torsor over
K^{\prime}\mathopen{(\!(}t\mathopen{)\!)}
and thus trivial, since
K^{\prime}
is pseudo-finite. Again as in the first case, the integral of
\chi_{s}\circ e
over this fiber is equal to the constant value of
\chi_{s}\circ e
multiplied by
\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}(\overline{{\mathcal{N}}}^{0}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b})\mathbb{L}^{-\ord(\omega_{b})})
.

We are given the integral of a constant function on a subset of an abelian variety defined by the preimage of a constructible subset of the special fiber of its Néron model. By smoothness of the Néron model, on each fiber, the integral is equal to the constant value of
\chi_{s}\circ e
multiplied by
\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}(\overline{{\mathcal{N}}}^{0}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b})\mathbb{L}^{-\ord(\omega_{b})})
. Since the induced function on
\phi_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b}}
is non-trivial by Lemma
5.5.2
, the sum of all values is zero by Lemma
2.12.1
. This concludes the proof of the proposition.
∎

We can now finish the proof of Theorem
5.4.1
. By Proposition
5.5.4
, if either
i(t)\neq 0
or
i(s)\neq 0
, then both sides are zero hence the equality holds. In the case
i(t)=i(s)=0
, we need to prove that
\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}(\overline{{\mathcal{N}}}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b}))=\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}(\overline{{\mathcal{N}}}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{P}}}^{\square}_{\widehat{G},b}))
. By the main result of
[
15
]
,
\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}_{\widehat{G},b}
and
{\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}}_{G,b}
are dual abelian varieties. Since the Grothendieck pairing on Néron models is non-degenerate in residue-characteristic
0

[
23
, 11.3, Exposé IX]
and
K^{\prime}
is pseudo-finite we deduce

|\phi_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b}}(K^{\prime})|=|\phi_{{\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}}_{\widehat{G},b}}(K^{\prime})|,

as in the proof of
[
35
, Proposition 4.3]
. Finally, the isogeny between
\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b}
and
\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}_{\widehat{G},b}
induces an isogeny between their Néron models by
[
5
, Proposition 7.3.6]
. The motives of two isogenous connected commutative groups are equal by
[
1
, Theorem 3.3 (4)]
and
[

, Lemma 2.5.1]
. The motive
\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}(\overline{{\mathcal{N}}}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b}))
is
|\phi_{{\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\square}}_{G,b}}|
times the motive of the neutral component, hence the motives
\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}(\overline{{\mathcal{N}}}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{P}}}^{\square}_{G,b}))
and
\chi_{\mathrm{Psf},\mathrm{rel}}(\overline{{\mathcal{N}}}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{P}}}^{\square}_{\widehat{G},b}))
are equal, which concludes the proof of Theorem
5.4.1
.

6.
A motivic Fundamental lemma

We show in this section how to deduce from the Geometric Stabilization theorem
4.8.1
a motivic version of the Fundamental Lemma. This part is geometric in the sense that it does not rely on any cohomological methods, and follows closely Ngô’s original argument.

6.1.
Statement

We briefly review
[
7
]
, in which it is shown that the orbital integrals of the Fundamental Lemma can be encoded by motivic integrals. This is similar to the encoding of the Hitchin fibration of Section
4
.

Let
L
be a discretely valued field with pseudo-finite residue field
K
. We encode unramified extensions of
L
of fixed degree
r
as definable sets in the Denef-Pas language using parameters similarly to what we have done in Section
4.2
. We introduce a parameter
b=(b_{0},\dots,b_{r-1})
in
L^{r}
.
The conditions that the
b_{i}
belong to the valuation ring of
L
and that the reduction of the polynomial

m_{b}=x^{r}+b_{r-1}x^{r-1}+\dots b_{0}
modulo the maximal ideal is an irreducible polynomial over the residue field are definable in the Denef-Pas language.
The unramified extension
L_{r}=L[x]/m_{b}
of
L
defined by this polynomial is then viewed as a definable set with parameter
b
by identifying it with
L^{r}
. As in Section
4.2
, we also use a parameter
\tau
for a generator of the Galois group
\Gal(L_{r}/L)
.

Recall that an unramified reductive group
G
over a
L
is a quasi-split reductive group over
L
that splits over an unramified extension of
L
.

We fix a split reductive group
{\mathbb{G}}
by fixing its root datum, as well as a faithful representation of
{\mathbb{G}}
. We choose a quasi-split form of
{\mathbb{G}}
by fixing a element of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gal(L_{r}/L),\mathrm{Out}({\mathbb{G}}))
, where
r
is some fixed integer. The choice of
\tau
identifies this torsor with an endomorphism
\theta
of
{\mathbb{G}}(L_{r})
. Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be the definable set with parameters such that for every discretely valued field
L
with pseudo-finite residue field,
{\mathbf{G}}(L)
is the set of fixed points of
{\mathbb{G}}(L_{r})
under the endomorphism
\theta\circ\tau
.

We have similarly definable sets
{\mathbf{T}}
for the maximal torus of
{\mathbf{G}}
determined by the root datum,
{\mathbf{g}}
for the Lie algebra of
{\mathbf{G}}
.

We also have the definable set of
{\mathbf{G}}
-conjugacy classes in
{\mathbf{g}}
, the Chevalley base
{\mathbf{c}}
, together with a definable map
\chi\colon{\mathbf{g}}\to{\mathbf{c}}
.

Fix some
{\mathbf{G}}
-invariant and
{\mathbf{T}}
-invariant definable volume forms
\left\lvert\omega_{G}\right\rvert
and
\left\lvert\omega_{T}\right\rvert
. We normalize integrals by choosing
\left\lvert\omega_{G}\right\rvert
such that
\int_{{\mathbf{G}}(\mathcal{O})}\left\lvert\omega_{G}\right\rvert=1
.

For
\gamma\in{\mathbf{g}}
regular semi-simple, define the motivic orbital integral as

O_{\gamma}(\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbf{g}}(\mathcal{O})})=\int_{{\mathbf{T}}\backslash{\mathbf{G}}}^{\mathrm{mot}}\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbf{g}}(\mathcal{O})}(g^{-1}\gamma g)\frac{\left\lvert\omega_{G}\right\rvert}{\left\lvert\omega_{T}\right\rvert}.

We note that the integral depends on a choice of normalization for
\left\lvert\omega_{T}\right\rvert
, but we do not include it explicitly in the notation.

Let
I_{\gamma}
the centralizer of
\gamma
. Let
\gamma^{\prime}\in{\mathbf{g}}
which is stably conjugate to
\gamma
, that is, conjugate over
{\mathbf{G}}(L_{r})
. Using the choice of a generator
\tau
, we get a finite definable set
\mathcal{D}
of representatives of stable conjugacy classes of
\gamma
. This
\gamma^{\prime}
determines an invariant
\mathrm{inv}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(L,I_{\gamma})
, and this assignment is definable when we consider
\mathrm{H}^{1}(L,I_{\gamma})
as definable using cocycles as in
4.2
. In particular we may identify
\mathcal{D}
with a subgroup of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(L,I_{\gamma})
.

Let
\kappa
be a character of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(L,I_{\gamma})
. The Tate-Nakayama pairing
\langle\mathrm{inv}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}),\kappa\rangle
is a definable map by
[
7
]
, in the sense that its image lies in
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
for some
n
, and the level sets (for fixed
\kappa
and varying
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}
) are definable sets. Below we consider its motivic version, as in Section
2.12
.

Define the
\kappa
-orbital integral as

O_{\gamma}^{\kappa}(\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbf{g}}(\mathcal{O})})=\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}}\langle\mathrm{inv}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}),\kappa\rangle O_{\gamma^{\prime}}(\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbf{g}}(\mathcal{O})}).

When
\kappa
is the trivial character, we write
O_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{stab}}(\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbf{g}}(\mathcal{O})})
instead of
O_{\gamma}^{\kappa}(\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbf{g}}(\mathcal{O})})
.

The Kostant section
\varepsilon\colon{\mathbf{c}}\to{\mathbf{g}}
is definable as well, and we use it to define for
a\in{\mathbf{c}}
regular semi-simple

O_{a}^{\kappa}(\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbf{g}}(\mathcal{O})})=O_{\varepsilon(a)}^{\kappa}(\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbf{g}}(\mathcal{O})}).

Let
H
be an endoscopic group of
G
,
{\mathbf{H}}
the associated definable group and
{\mathbf{h}}
the Lie algebra. We add subscripts
H
to the various objects associated to
H
, such as
{\mathbf{c}}_{H}
. We have an inclusion
{\mathbf{c}}_{H}\to{\mathbf{c}}
. Let
a_{H}\in{\mathbf{c}}_{H}(L)
be regular semi-simple, and
a
its image in
{\mathbf{c}}(L)
. The group
H
determines a character
\kappa
of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(L,J_{a})
, where
J_{a}\cong I_{\varepsilon(a)}
denotes the regular centralizer of
a
, see
[
37
, Section 1.4]
. Notice that
J_{a}(L)=T(L)
by definition.

We can now state the motivic version of the Fundamental Lemma.

Theorem 6.1.1
.

Let
G,H,a_{H},a,\kappa
be as above. Then there exists some
A_{a}
, motive of a commutative algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}(a)
and the parameters, such that

A_{a}\,O_{a}^{\kappa}(\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbf{g}}(\mathcal{O})})=A_{a}\,\mathbb{L}^{r^{G}_{H}(a_{H})}\,O_{a_{H}}^{\mathrm{stab}}(\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbf{h}}(\mathcal{O})})

in
\mathrm{\bf K}(\DAC(\mathbb{Q}(a),\mathrm{rel},\Lambda))\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Remark 6.1.2
.

Even though Theorem
6.1.1
is a local statement, the proof uses the global geometry of the Hitchin system. This will imply that
A_{a}
will in general contain a factor of an abelian variety coming from the factor
\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}(({\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}_{a})^{0})
in the notation of Section
6.3
below. Furthermore since the dimensions of the Hitchin system depends on

, we do not expect

to behave as a motivic constructible function for varying

in
{\mathbf{c}}_{H}^{\mathrm{rss}}
.

If one specializes to local fields of large residue characteristic, as in Corollary
6.1.4
below, one can cancel the factor
A_{a}
on both sides. In particular
A_{a}
is not a zero-divisor of
O_{a}^{\kappa}(\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbf{g}}(\mathcal{O})})-\mathbb{L}^{r^{G}_{H}(a_{H})}\,O_{a_{H}}^{\mathrm{stab}}(\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbf{h}}(\mathcal{O})})
.

We recover a variant of the result of Cluckers-Hales-Loeser
[
7
, Section 9.2]
.

Proposition 6.1.3
.

Let
{\mathbb{G}}
be a split reductive group over a normal domain

of finite type over
{\mathbb{Z}}
and

an integer. There is a non-empty open subscheme of

such that for every closed point

of
U
, and local field
L
with a map

to a uniformizer of

and residue field

, the following holds.

Denote by
{\mathcal{O}}
the valuation ring of
L
. Let
G
be a quasi-split reductive group over
L
, with split form
{\mathbb{G}}
, Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and Chevalley base
\mathfrak{c}
. Assume that
G
splits over an unramified extension of degree

. Let
H
be an endoscopic group of
G
and
{\mathbf{H}}
a definable endoscopic group of
{\mathbf{G}}
that specializes to
H
. For
a\in\mathfrak{c}_{H}^{\mathrm{rss}}
regular semi-simple, let

the character of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(L,J_{a})
associated to it. Then up to some choice of parameters, the motivic constructible function
O_{a}^{\kappa}(\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbf{g}}(\mathcal{O})})
considered in
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathrm{mot}}({\mathbf{c}}_{H}^{\mathrm{rss}})
, when specialized via
\Tr\mathrm{Frob}_{x}
to a function on

, is the function

By construction of the motivic orbital integral, the locus of integration and the integrand specialize to their counterpart on

. Hence the result follows from Proposition

.
∎

Recall from Ngô
[
37
, Théorème 1]
the statement of the Fundamental Lemma of Langlands-Shelstad for Lie algebras.

Corollary 6.1.4
.

The Fundamental Lemma
1.2.1
holds for quasi-split reductive groups of large enough residue characteristic.

Proof.

We just have to combine Theorem
6.1.1
with Proposition
6.1.3
, noting that every
a\in\mathfrak{c}_{H}^{\mathrm{rss}}
is the specialization of some
b\in{\mathbf{c}}_{H}^{\mathrm{rss}}
. Dividing both sides of the specialization to a local field of the equality in Theorem
6.1.1
by
\Tr\mathrm{Frob}_{x}(A_{b})
, which is non-zero as the number of points of an algebraic group, yields the equality of the Fundamental Lemma.
∎

6.2.
From orbital integrals to affine Springer fibers

The relation between orbital integrals and affine Springer fibers first appeared in
[
21
]
. For any discrete and cocompact subgroup
{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\subseteq{\mathbf{T}}
defined by a finite set of generators, we deduce by Fubini
that

\vol({\mathbf{\Lambda}}\backslash{\mathbf{T}},\omega_{T})\,O_{a}(\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbf{g}}(\mathcal{O})})=\vol({\mathbf{\Lambda}}\backslash{\mathbf{T}},\omega_{T})\int_{{\mathbf{T}}\backslash{\mathbf{G}}}^{\mathrm{mot}}\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbf{g}}(\mathcal{O})}(g^{-1}\varepsilon(a)g)\frac{\left\lvert\omega_{G}\right\rvert}{\left\lvert\omega_{T}\right\rvert}\\
=\int_{{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\backslash{\mathbf{G}}}^{\mathrm{mot}}\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbf{g}}(\mathcal{O})}(g^{-1}\varepsilon(a)g)\left\lvert\omega_{G}\right\rvert.

Furthermore, there is a
{\mathbf{G}}(\Oc)
-fibration

\{g\in{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\backslash{\mathbf{G}}\ |\ g^{-1}\varepsilon(a)g\in{\mathbf{g}}(\mathcal{O})\}\longrightarrow\{g\in{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\backslash{\mathbf{G}}/{\mathbf{G}}(\Oc)\ |\ g^{-1}\varepsilon(a)g\in{\mathbf{g}}(\mathcal{O})\}.

Here the right hand side can be identified with the
K(a)
-points of
{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\backslash{\mathcal{N}}_{a}
, where
{\mathcal{N}}_{a}
denotes the affine Springer fiber associated with
a
. Since it is not of finite type,
{\mathcal{N}}_{a}
is not definable. However, by
[
31
]
, or more precisely
[
37
, Proposition 3.4.1]
, the quotient
{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\backslash{\mathcal{N}}_{a}
is a projective finite type scheme whose points can be encoded by a definable set. We thus get

(6.2.1)

\vol({\mathbf{\Lambda}}\backslash{\mathbf{T}},\omega_{T})\,O_{a}(\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbf{g}}(\mathcal{O})})=\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}({\mathbf{\Lambda}}\backslash{\mathcal{N}}_{a}).

Now let
\gamma^{\prime}\in{\mathbf{g}}
be stably conjugate to
\varepsilon(a)
and let
\delta=\inv(\varepsilon(a),\gamma^{\prime})\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(L,J_{a})
denote the corresponding class. By
[
37
, Lemme 8.2.4]
we have an isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{1}(L,J_{a})\cong\mathrm{H}^{1}(K,{\mathbb{P}}(J^{\prime}_{a})),

where
J^{\prime}_{a}\to J_{a}
is a group scheme with connected fibers generically isomorphic to
J_{a}
and
{\mathbb{P}}(J^{\prime}_{a})
denotes the Picard-stack of
J^{\prime}_{a}
-torsors with a generic trivialization. Since
J^{\prime}_{a}
is connected, the
K
-points of
{\mathbb{P}}(J^{\prime}_{a})
can be identified with
J_{a}(L)/J^{\prime}_{a}({\mathcal{O}}_{L})
, see
[
37
, Section 3.3]
. The lattice
{\mathbf{\Lambda}}
thus naturally embeds into
{\mathbb{P}}(J^{\prime}_{a})
and the quotient
{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\backslash{\mathbb{P}}(J^{\prime}_{a})
is an affine finite type group scheme
[
37
, Lemme 3.8.1]
. We write
{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\backslash{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbf{J}}^{\prime}_{a})
for the associated definable set and
\pi_{0}(a)
for its group of connected components. Since
{\mathbf{\Lambda}}
is discrete and torsion free we have by Lang’s theorem an inclusion

\mathrm{H}^{1}(K,{\mathbb{P}}(J^{\prime}_{a}))\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{1}(K,{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\backslash{\mathbb{P}}(J^{\prime}_{a}))=\mathrm{H}^{1}(K,\pi_{0}(a)).

Similarly to the case of Higgs bundles
{\mathbb{P}}(J_{a})
and thus also
{\mathbb{P}}(J^{\prime}_{a})
act naturally on
{\mathcal{N}}_{a}
and it follows from the discussion in
[
21
, Section 15.5]
that we have an isomorphism

{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\backslash{\mathcal{N}}_{\gamma^{\prime}}\cong({\mathbf{\Lambda}}\backslash{\mathcal{N}}_{a})^{\delta},

where
({\mathbf{\Lambda}}\backslash{\mathcal{N}}_{a})^{\delta}
denotes the twisted variety as in Section
4.3
. Since (
6.2.1
) also holds with
\varepsilon(a)
replaced by
\gamma^{\prime}
we finally get, for every
\kappa
,

(6.2.2)

\vol({\mathbf{\Lambda}}\backslash{\mathbf{T}},\omega_{T})\,O^{\kappa}_{a}(\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbf{g}}(\mathcal{O})})=\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}}\langle\mathrm{inv}(\varepsilon(a),\gamma^{\prime}),\kappa\rangle\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}(({\mathbf{\Lambda}}\backslash{\mathcal{N}}_{a})^{\delta})\\
=\sum_{\delta\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(K,{\mathbb{P}}(J^{0}_{a}))}\langle\delta,\kappa\rangle\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}(({\mathbf{\Lambda}}\backslash{\mathcal{N}}_{a})^{\delta})=|\mathrm{H}^{1}(K,\pi_{0}(a))|\ \chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}({\mathbf{\Lambda}}\backslash{\mathcal{N}}_{a},\kappa),

where the
\kappa
-isotypical component is defined as in Section
4.4
and
\mathcal{D}
is identified with a subgroup of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(L,J_{a})\cong\mathrm{H}^{1}(K,{\mathbb{P}}(J^{0}_{a}))
through
\inv(\varepsilon(a),\cdot)
as in Section
6.1
. Here we also used
[
37
, Lemme 8.2.6]
, which implies that
({\mathbf{\Lambda}}\backslash{\mathcal{N}}_{a})^{\delta}
has no
K
-rational point for
\delta\notin\mathcal{D}
and thus
\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}(({\mathbf{\Lambda}}\backslash{\mathcal{N}}_{a})^{\delta})=0
in this case.

We can further simplify (
6.2.2
) as follows. First, again by Fubini, we have

\vol({\mathbf{\Lambda}}\backslash{\mathbf{T}},\omega_{T})=\vol({\mathbf{J}}^{\prime}_{a}({\mathcal{O}}),\omega_{T})\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}({\mathbf{\Lambda}}\backslash{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbf{J}}^{\prime}_{a}))\\
=\vol({\mathbf{J}}^{\prime}_{a}({\mathcal{O}}),\omega_{T})|\pi_{0}(a)|\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}({\mathbb{P}}({\mathbf{J}}^{\prime}_{a})^{0}).

Secondly since
\Gal(K)=\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
, say with topological generator
\sigma
, we have an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\pi_{0}(a)(K)\longrightarrow\pi_{0}(a)(K^{\mathrm{alg}})\xrightarrow{\>1-\sigma\>}\pi_{0}(a)(K^{\mathrm{alg}})\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(K,\pi_{0}(a))\longrightarrow 0,

and thus
|\pi_{0}(a)(K)|=|\mathrm{H}^{1}(K,\pi_{0}(a))|
. Plugging this into (
6.2.2
) and dividing by
|\pi_{0}(a)(K)|=|\mathrm{H}^{1}(K,\pi_{0}(a))|
we get

(6.2.3)

\vol({\mathbf{J}}^{\prime}_{a}({\mathcal{O}}),\omega_{T})\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}\left({\mathbb{P}}({\mathbf{J}}^{\prime}_{a})^{0}\right)O^{\kappa}_{a}(\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbf{g}}(\mathcal{O})})=\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}\left({\mathbf{\Lambda}}\backslash{\mathcal{N}}_{a},\kappa\right),

which is the motivic analogue of
[
37
, Proposition 8.2.5]
. Notice the equality
\vol({\mathbf{J}}^{\prime}_{a}({\mathcal{O}}),\omega_{T})=\mathbb{L}^{\alpha}\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}(({\mathbf{J}}^{\prime}_{a})_{K})
, with
\alpha
depending on the choice of
\omega_{T}
, in particular
\vol({\mathbf{J}}^{\prime}_{a}({\mathcal{O}}),\omega_{T})
is the motive of a commutative algebraic group.

6.3.
From affine Springer to Hitchin fibers

The connection between Springer and Hitchin fibers is given by the product formula
[
37
, Proposition 4.15.1]
. Consider the geometric setup of
G
-Higgs bundles on a curve
X
in the generality of Section
4.7
. Let
a\in\A^{{\mathrm{ani}}}
be a point with pseudo-finite residue field. By definition we get a morphism
a:X\to\mathfrak{c}_{D}
and we denote by
U\subseteq X
the open dense preimage of
\mathfrak{c}_{D}^{rs}
under
a
. For any closed point
\nu\in X\setminus U
the completion of the local ring at
\nu
is a discretely valued field
L_{\nu}
with pseudo-finite residue field
K_{\nu}
. We write a subscript
\nu
for the restriction of
G
,
T
,
a
etc. to this formal neighbourhood after having fixed a trivialization of
D
. In particular
a_{\nu}\in\mathfrak{c}_{\nu}^{rs}(L_{\nu})
gives rise to an affine Springer fiber
{\mathcal{N}}_{a_{\nu}}
.

Using the Kostant section one can define a morphism

\prod_{\nu\in X\setminus U}{\mathcal{N}}_{a_{\nu}}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{M}}_{a},

and similarly for the symmetry groups
\prod_{\nu\in X\setminus U}{\mathbb{P}}(J_{a_{\nu}})\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{a}
. The product formula
[
37
, Proposition 4.15.1]
, see also
[
37
, Proposition 8.4.2]
, states that over
\overline{K}
we have a homeomorphism of proper DM-stacks

(6.3.1)

\prod_{\nu\in X\setminus U}{\mathcal{N}}_{a_{\nu}}\times^{\prod_{\nu\in X\setminus U}{\mathbb{P}}(J_{a_{\nu}})}{\mathbb{P}}_{a}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{M}}_{a},

compatible with the actions of
\Gal(\overline{K}/K)
and
{\mathbb{P}}_{a}
. Furthermore let
{\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}_{a}\to{\mathbb{P}}(J_{a})
be as in
[
37
, Section 8.4]
, in particular
{\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}_{a}
is an algebraic group locally of finite type. It follows essentially by definition that (
6.3.1
) continues to hold with
{\mathbb{P}}(J_{a_{\nu}})
and
{\mathbb{P}}_{a}
replaced by
{\mathbb{P}}(J^{\prime}_{a_{\nu}})
and
{\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}_{a}
, or more conveniently for us, we have a fibration

\prod_{\nu\in X\setminus U}{\mathcal{N}}_{a_{\nu}}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}_{a}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{M}}_{a},

whose fibers are
\prod_{\nu\in X\setminus U}{\mathbb{P}}(J^{\prime}_{a_{\nu}})
-torsors.

By an argument similar to the one in the previous section we obtain for each Galois-invariant character
\kappa
of
\pi_{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}_{a})
the relation

(6.3.2)

\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}(({\mathbf{P}}^{\prime}_{a})^{0})\prod_{\nu\in X\setminus U}\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}({\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{\nu}\backslash{\mathcal{N}}_{a_{\nu}},\kappa)=\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}({\mathbf{M}}_{a},\kappa)\prod_{\nu\in X\setminus U}\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}({\mathbb{P}}({\mathbf{J}}^{\prime}_{a_{\nu}})^{0}).

The formula is the motivic analogue of
[
37
, Corollaire 8.4.4]
.

6.4.
Back to orbital integrals

It is explained in
[
37
, Section 8.6]
how to construct from the initial data
G,H,a_{H},a,\kappa
of Theorem
6.1.1
a curve
X
over
K
, a line bundle
D
on
X
, a distinguished
K
-point
\nu_{0}
and all the auxiliary data to construct the Hitchin fibrations for
G
and
H
. Furthermore, by choosing the degree of
D
sufficiently large, the affine linear sub-space
\mathrm{Z}\subseteq\A_{H}
, consisting of elements
a^{\prime}\in\A_{H}
such that
a^{\prime}_{\nu_{0}}\equiv a_{H}\mod(T^{N})
for
N
as in
[
37
, Proposition 3.5.1]
, is non-empty and its pull-back
\widetilde{\mathrm{Z}}\subseteq\widetilde{\A}_{H}
is geometrically irreducible by the same argument as in
[
37
, Lemma 5.3.2]
. For dimension reasons also
\widetilde{\mathrm{Z}}\cap\widetilde{\A}^{{\mathrm{ani}}}_{H}
is non-empty and geometrically irreducible. Since
K
is pseudo-finite we deduce
\widetilde{\mathrm{Z}}\cap\widetilde{\A}^{{\mathrm{ani}}}_{H}(K)\neq\emptyset
. Thus we may find
a^{\prime}_{H}\in\A_{H}^{{\mathrm{ani}}}(K)
which admits a lift to
\widetilde{\A}^{{\mathrm{ani}}}_{H}
and such that

\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}({\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{\nu_{0}}\backslash{\mathcal{N}}_{a^{\prime}_{\nu_{0}}},\kappa)\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ \chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}({\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{\nu_{0}}\backslash{\mathcal{N}}_{a^{\prime}_{H,\nu_{0}}},\mathrm{stab})

compute the orbital integrals appearing in
6.1.1
via (
6.2.3
). Here we wrote
a^{\prime}
for the image of
a^{\prime}_{H}
in
\A^{{\mathrm{ani}}}
.

Since both
a^{\prime}_{H}
and
a^{\prime}
admit lifts to
\widetilde{\A}^{{\mathrm{ani}}}_{H}
and
\widetilde{\A}^{{\mathrm{ani}}}
we may identify the corresponding fibers of
{\mathbf{M}}_{H}
and
{\mathbf{M}}
with the ones of
\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}_{H}
and
\widetilde{{\mathbf{M}}}
. The Geometric Stabilization theorem
5.1.1
for
G
and
H
together with formula (
6.3.2
) then gives

(6.4.1)

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}(({\mathbf{P}}^{\prime}_{a^{\prime}})^{0})\prod_{\nu\in X\setminus U}\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}(

\displaystyle{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{\nu}\backslash{\mathcal{N}}_{a^{\prime}_{\nu}},\kappa)=

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}(({\mathbf{P}}^{\prime}_{a^{\prime}})^{0}){\mathbb{L}}^{r_{H}^{G}(D)}\prod_{\nu\in X\setminus U}\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}({\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{\nu}\backslash{\mathcal{N}}_{a^{\prime}_{H,\nu}},\mathrm{stab}).

One can further adjust the choice of
a^{\prime}_{H}
as in
[
37
, Section 8.6.6]
in such a way, that for all
\nu\in X\setminus U
different from
\nu_{0}
the Springer fibers are of a very simple form, which is analyzed in
[
37
, Lemme 8.5.7]
. In the first two cases in
loc. cit.
both Springer-fibers are
0
-dimensional,
r^{G}_{H}(a_{H,\nu})=0
and

\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}({\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{\nu}\backslash{\mathcal{N}}_{a^{\prime}_{\nu}},\kappa)=\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}({\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{\nu}\backslash{\mathcal{N}}_{a^{\prime}_{H,{\nu}}},\mathrm{stab})=1.

In the third case we still have
\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}({\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{\nu}\backslash{\mathcal{N}}_{a^{\prime}_{H,{\nu}}},\mathrm{stab})=1
but
r^{G}_{H}(a^{\prime}_{H,\nu})=1
. The geometry of
{\mathcal{N}}_{a^{\prime}_{\nu}}
is described in
[
37
, Section 8.3]
and reduced to the case when
{\mathcal{N}}_{a^{\prime}_{\nu},\overline{K}}
is an infinite chain of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
’s. The Kostant section provides a
K_{\nu}
-rational point for
{\mathcal{N}}_{a^{\prime}_{\nu}}
and hence
{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{\nu}\backslash{\mathcal{N}}_{a^{\prime}_{\nu}}
is isomorphic to the Weil restriction along
K_{\nu}/K
of either a nodal
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
with trivial
\pi_{0}(a^{\prime}_{\nu})
-action or two
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
’s meeting in two points and
\pi_{0}(a^{\prime}_{\nu})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
exchanging components and intersection points, see
[
37
, Example 8.1.15]
. It follows that in both cases
\chi_{\mathrm{Psf}}({\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{\nu}\backslash{\mathcal{N}}_{a^{\prime}_{\nu}},\kappa)={\mathbb{L}}^{\deg(K_{\nu}/K)}
. Since
r_{H}^{G}(D)=\sum_{\nu\in X\setminus U}\deg(K_{\nu}/K)r^{G}_{H}(a^{\prime}_{H,\nu})
, the combination of (
6.4.1
) with (
6.2.3
) finishes the proof of Theorem
6.1.1
.

Discrete two-generator subgroups of PSL 2 {\rm PSL_{2}} over non-archimedean local fields
Matthew J. Conder and Jeroen Schillewaert

Abstract
. Let K K be a non-archimedean local field with residue field of characteristic p p . We give necessary and sufficient conditions for a two-generator subgroup G G of PSL 2 ​ ( K ) {\rm PSL_{2}}(K) to be discrete, where either K = ℚ p K=\mathbb{Q}_{p} or G G contains no elements of order p p . We give a practical algorithm to decide whether such a subgroup G G is discrete. We also give practical algorithms to decide whether a two-generator subgroup of either SL 2 ​ ( ℝ ) {\rm SL_{2}}(\mathbb{R}) or SL 2 ​ ( K ) {\rm SL_{2}}(K) (where K K is a finite extension of ℚ p \mathbb{Q}_{p} ) is dense. A crucial ingredient for this work is a structure theorem for two-generator groups acting by isometries on a Λ \Lambda -tree.



MSC 2020 Classification: 20E08; 22E40.

Keywords: Discrete groups, non-archimedean local fields, Bruhat-Tits tree.

1.
Introduction

The problem of identifying discrete two-generator subgroups of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(\mathbb{R})
has been extensively studied in the literature. A complete classification of such groups, and practical algorithms to decide whether or not a two-generator subgroup of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(\mathbb{R})
is discrete, are given in
[
13
,
17
,
24
]
.

Here we prove analogous results for certain two-generator subgroups of
{\rm PSL_{2}}
over a non-archimedean local field
K
by studying the action on the associated Bruhat-Tits tree
[
26
]
. Our main classification result is the following; see
A’
in Section
4
for a more detailed statement.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a discrete two-generator subgroup of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
, where
K
is a non-archimedean local field with residue field of characteristic
p
. If
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, or
G
contains no elements of order
p
, then one of the following holds (where
n
and
m
are positive integers, parameterised by
p
as detailed in
A’
:

(a)

G
is finite, and either cyclic, dihedral, or isomorphic to
A_{4}
,
S_{4}
or
A_{5}
;

(b)

G
is discrete and free of rank two;

(c)

G\cong C_{n}*C_{m}
;

(d)

G\cong C_{n}*\mathbb{Z}
;

(e)

G\cong C_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}
, or
G\cong\mathbb{Z}
;

(f)

G
is an HNN extension of either
D_{2n+1}
or
A_{4}
;

(g)

G
is isomorphic to either
D_{2n+1}*_{C_{2}}D_{2}
,
A_{4}*_{C_{3}}D_{3}
,
S_{4}*_{C_{4}}D_{4}
,
A_{5}*_{C_{3}}D_{3}
or
A_{5}*_{C_{5}}D_{5}
.

Moreover, each of these possibilities can occur.

Throughout the paper,
K
will be used to denote a non-archimedean local field with finite residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of characteristic
p
. That is,
K
is either a finite extension of the
p
-adic numbers
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, or the field of formal Laurent series
\mathbb{F}_{q}((t))

[
25
]
. We will denote the valuation ring of
K
by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, and the uniformiser of
K
by
\pi
.

When
K=\mathbb{F}_{q}((t))
, every order
p
element in
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
is unipotent
[
18
, p. 964]
. Since cocompact discrete subgroups of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
do not contain unipotent elements
[
12
, p. 10]
, we immediately obtain the following.

Corollary 1.1
.

The conclusion of Theorem
A
holds for discrete and cocompact two-generator subgroups of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(\mathbb{F}_{q}((t)))
.

Remark 1.2
.

As detailed in Theorem
A’
(see Section
4
), cases
(f)
and
(g)
only occur for certain congruence classes of
q
.

Remark 1.3
.

All groups we obtain in case
(g)
are of the form
G_{1}*_{G_{3}}G_{2}
, where
G_{3}
is a maximal cyclic subgroup of both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
. These are also described in case
(2)
of
[
28
, Theorem 3.5]
, which gives a list of discrete finitely generated subgroups of
{\rm PGL}_{2}
over a
p
-adic field.

Remark 1.4
.

Our proof of Theorem
A
relies heavily on the fact that the set of points of the Bruhat-Tits tree fixed by a finite order element of
G
is preserved under non-trivial powers; see Corollary
3.5
. If
K\neq\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, then
G
may contain elements of order
p
with fixed point sets which are not necessarily preserved under non-trivial powers
[
19
, Theorem 4.2]
. We expect that significant further analysis will be required to complete an analogous classification for discrete two-generator subgroups of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
which contain elements of order
p
.

A
is a consequence of the following geometric theorem with corresponding labelling. Here, and throughout the paper, we will use
l(X)
to denote the translation length of an isometry
X
of a tree
T
. We will also use
{\rm Fix}(A)
to denote the fixed point set of an elliptic isometry
A
of
T
, and
{\rm Ax}(B)
to denote the axis of a hyperbolic isometry
B
of
T
. Given a finite subpath
P=[x,y]
of
{\rm Ax}(B)
, where
B
translates
x
towards
y
, we also refer to
x
(respectively
y
) as the initial (respectively terminal) vertex of
P
.

Theorem B
.

Let
G
be a two-generator subgroup of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
. If
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, or
G
contains no elements of order
p
, then
G
is discrete if and only if there exists a generating pair
(A,B)
for
G
such that one of the following holds:

(a)

A
and
B
are elliptic with
{\rm Fix}(A)\cap{\rm Fix}(B)\neq\varnothing
, and
G=\langle A,B\rangle
is finite;

(b)

A
and
B
are hyperbolic, and
{\rm Ax}(A)\cap{\rm Ax}(B)
is either empty or a path of length
\Delta<\min\{l(A),l(B)\}
;

(c)

A
and
B
are elliptic of finite order and
{\rm Fix}(A)\cap{\rm Fix}(B)=\varnothing
;

(d)

A
is elliptic of finite order,
B
is hyperbolic of minimal translation length among the elements
\{A^{i}B:i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, and
{\rm Fix}(A)\cap{\rm Ax}(B)
is either empty or a path of length
\Delta<l(B)
;

(e)

A
is elliptic of finite order,
B
is hyperbolic, and
A
and
B
commute;

(f)

A
is elliptic of finite order,
B
is hyperbolic and
{\rm Fix}(A)\cap{\rm Ax}(B)
is a path
P
of length
\Delta=l(B)
. The group
G_{0}=\langle A,BAB^{-1}\rangle
is finite and does not contain a reflection in
{\rm Ax}(B)
about the terminal vertex of
P
;

(g)

A
is elliptic of finite order,
B
is hyperbolic and
{\rm Fix}(A)\cap{\rm Ax}(B)
is a path
P
of length
\Delta=l(B)
. The group
G_{0}=\langle A,BAB^{-1}\rangle
is finite and contains a reflection in
{\rm Ax}(B)
about the terminal vertex of
P
.

Using Theorems
A
and
B
, we obtain a practical algorithm (
6.1
) to decide whether or not a two-generator subgroup
G
of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
(where either
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, or
G
contains no elements of order
p
) is discrete. If
G
is discrete, then the algorithm returns the isomorphism type of
G
according to
A
. Following a suggestion by Pierre-Emmanuel Caprace, we also obtain practical algorithms (Algorithms
6.8
and
6.11
) to decide whether or not a two-generator subgroup of either
{\rm SL_{2}}(\mathbb{R})
or
{\rm SL_{2}}(K)
(where
K
is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
) is dense. All three of these algorithms have been implemented in
Magma

[
2
]
over appropriate subfields
[
9
]
.

Outline of paper:
In Section
2
, we prove a structure theorem (Theorem
C
) for (not necessarily discrete) two-generator groups acting by isometries on
\Lambda
-trees, which hinges on Klein-Maskit combination theorems given in
[

. In Section

{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
acting on the corresponding Bruhat-Tits tree

of certain finite order elements of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)

. The rest of this section is devoted to a systematic investigation of Theorem
C
for two-generator subgroups of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)

4
, we prove Theorem

and use it to prove Theorem

. The latter proof relies on examples provided in Section
5
.
Finally, in Section
6
, we present the practical algorithms identified above and discuss their implementation.

2.
Two-generator groups acting on trees

The following theorem is fundamental to our proof of
A
. It is stated in the context of
\Lambda
-trees, a wide class of metric spaces which includes
\mathbb{R}
-trees and simplicial trees; see
[
6
]
. The theorem crucially relies on Klein-Maskit combination theorems given in
[
20
, VII]
, and it also generalises Lemmas 2.1 and 2.2 of
[
15
]
, which deal with cases
(3)
and
(4)
. By replacing a
\Lambda
-tree
T
by an appropriate subdivision if necessary, we may assume that every isometry of
T
acts without inversions and is hence either elliptic or hyperbolic; see Lemma 1.3 of
[
6
, Chapter 3]
.

Theorem C
.

Let
G=\langle A,B\rangle
be a group acting by isometries on a
\Lambda
-tree
T
. After interchanging the roles of
A
and
B
if necessary, precisely one of the following holds:

(1)

A
and
B
are elliptic,
{\rm Fix}(A)\cap{\rm Fix}(B)\neq\varnothing
, and
G
fixes a point of
T
.

(2)

A
and
B
are hyperbolic,
{\rm Ax}(A)\cap{\rm Ax}(B)
is either empty or a path of length
\Delta<\min\{l(A),l(B)\}
, and
G=\langle A\rangle*\langle B\rangle
. Moreover,
{\rm Stab}_{G}(y)=\{e\}
for every
y\in{\rm Ax}(A)\cup{\rm Ax}(B)
.

(3)

A
and
B
are elliptic,
{\rm Fix}(A)\cap{\rm Fix}(B)=\varnothing
, and one of the following holds:

(i)

G=\langle A\rangle*\langle B\rangle

{\rm Stab}_{G}(x)=\langle A\rangle
for every
x\in{\rm Fix}(A)
and
{\rm Stab}_{G}(y)=\langle B\rangle
for every
y\in{\rm Fix}(B)
;

(ii)

{\rm Fix}(A^{i})\cap{\rm Fix}(B^{j})\neq\varnothing
for some non-trivial powers
A^{i}
and
B^{j}
.

(4)

A
is elliptic,
B
is hyperbolic,
{\rm Fix}(A)\cap{\rm Ax}(B)
is empty or a path of length
\Delta<l(B)
, and one of the following holds:

(i)

G=\langle A\rangle*\langle B\rangle
and
{\rm Stab}_{G}(y)=\langle A\rangle
for all
y\in{\rm Fix}(A)
;

(ii)

A^{i}B
is elliptic for some non-trivial power
A^{i}
;

(iii)

(\bigcup_{i;A^{i}\neq e}{\rm Fix}(A^{i}))\cap{\rm Ax}(B)
is a path of length
\Delta^{\prime}\geq l(B)
.

(5)

A
is elliptic,
B
is hyperbolic,
{\rm Fix}(A)\cap{\rm Ax}(B)
contains a geodesic ray, and one of the following holds:

(i)

A
commutes with a power of

and
{\rm Ax}(B)\subseteq{\rm Fix}(A)
;

(ii)

{\rm Stab}_{G}(y)
is infinite for every

.

(6)

A
is elliptic,
B
is hyperbolic,
{\rm Fix}(A)\cap{\rm Ax}(B)
is a finite path of length
\Delta\geq l(B)
(with initial vertex
x
and terminal vertex
y
), and one of the following holds, where
G_{0}=\langle A,BAB^{-1},\dots,B^{k}AB^{-k}\rangle
and

:

(i)

is an HNN extension
G_{0}*_{B}
, and
G_{0}={\rm Stab}_{G}(y)
;

(ii)

contains a subgroup

[y,B^{k+1}x]
and properly contains the subgroup
\langle BAB^{-1},\dots,B^{k}AB^{-k}\rangle

(iii)

and there exists

such that

.

(7)

A
and
B
are hyperbolic,
{\rm Ax}(A)\cap{\rm Ax}(B)
is a path of length
\Delta\geq\min\{l(A),l(B)\}
, and
\min\{l(A),l(B)\}+\min\{l(AB),l(A^{-1}B)\}<l(A)+l(B)

l(A)\leq l(B)
. By Lemmas 3.4 and 3.5 of
[
6
, Chapter 3]
,
\min\{l(AB),l(A^{-1}B)\}\leq l(B)-l(A)
, from which the conclusion follows immediately.
∎

3.
Subgroups of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
and their actions on the Bruhat-Tits tree

In this section, we consider subgroups of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
acting on the corresponding Bruhat-Tits tree
T_{q}
, where
K
is a non-archimedean local field with finite residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of characteristic
p
. Note that we view
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
as a subgroup of the isometry group of
T_{q}
, so that
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
inherits the topology of pointwise convergence from the product topology on
T_{q}^{T_{q}}
.

As we will frequently refer to it throughout the paper, we state below a lemma of Kato describing necessary and sufficient conditions for discreteness of subgroups of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
16
, Lemma 4.4.1]

Let
G
be a subgroup of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
.

(1)

If
G
is discrete, then
{\rm Stab}_{G}(y)
is finite for every vertex
y
of
T_{q}
.

(2)

If
{\rm Stab}_{G}(y)
is finite for some vertex
y
of
T_{q}
, then
G
is discrete.

The translation length of each element of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
on
T_{q}
is given by the following formula, where
v
is the discrete valuation associated to
K
.

Proposition 3.2
.

[
21
, Proposition II.3.15]

The translation length of
A\in{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
on
T_{q}
is given by

l(A)=-2\min\{0,v(\mathrm{tr}(\bar{A}))\},

where
\bar{A}
is either of the two representatives of
A
in
{\rm SL_{2}}(K)
.

We recall the following well-known classification of finite subgroups of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
; we include a proof for completeness.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
G
be a non-trivial finite subgroup of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
, where either
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
or
G
contains no elements of order
p
. Precisely one of the following holds:

•

G\cong C_{n}
, where either
q\equiv\pm 1\mod 2n
, or
q
is even and
q\equiv\pm 1\mod n
, or
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
n=p\in\{2,3\}
;

•

G\cong D_{n}
, where either
q\equiv\pm 1\mod 2n
, or
K=\mathbb{Q}_{2}
and
n=3
;

•

G\cong A_{4}
and either
p>3
or
K=\mathbb{Q}_{3}
;

•

G\cong S_{4}
and
q\equiv\pm 1\mod 8
;

•

G\cong A_{5}
and
q\equiv\pm 1\mod 10
.

Proof.

Let
\bar{G}
be a pre-image of
G
in
{\rm SL_{2}}(K)
. Since
\bar{G}
is finite, it is conjugate into
{\rm SL_{2}}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
, and hence the kernel of the reduction map
\bar{G}\to{\rm SL_{2}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is a pro-
p
group
[
18
, p. 964]
.

If
K\notin\{\mathbb{Q}_{2},\mathbb{Q}_{3}\}
, then
\bar{G}
does not contain elements of order
p
, unless
p=2
and
-I\in\bar{G}

[
18
, p. 972]
.
Since
-I
is the unique involution in any special linear group, it follows that
G
is isomorphic to a finite subgroup of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and the classification follows from
[
10
, Section 260]
.

On the other hand, if
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
for
p\in\{2,3\}
, then the proof of
[
18
, Theorem 3.6]
shows that
G
is isomorphic to a finite subgroup of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. The result follows since
{\rm PSL_{2}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\cong D_{3}
and
{\rm PSL_{2}}(\mathbb{F}_{3})\cong A_{4}
.
∎

We also classify the fixed point sets of certain finite order elements of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
. In characteristic zero, this also follows from
[
19
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
A\in{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
be a non-trivial element of finite order
n
. If
p\nmid n
or
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, then precisely one of the following holds:

•

{\rm Fix}(A)
consists of two adjacent vertices,
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
n=p\in\{2,3\}
;

•

{\rm Fix}(A)
is a single vertex, and either
q\equiv-1\mod 2n
, or
q
is even and
q\equiv-1\mod n
;

•

{\rm Fix}(A)
is a bi-infinite ray, and either
q\equiv 1\mod 2n
, or
q
is even and
q\equiv 1\mod n
.

Proof.

Let
\bar{A}
be a representative of
A
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(K)
with minimal order (hence
\bar{A}
has order
n
if
n
is odd, and order
2n
if
n
is even).
Replacing
\bar{A}
by an appropriate conjugate, if necessary, we may assume that
\bar{A}\in{\rm SL_{2}}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
, so
A
fixes the vertex
v
of
T_{q}
corresponding to the standard lattice
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{2}
. By
[
1
, Proposition 11]
, the number of vertices in
{\rm Fix}(A)
at distance
k
from
v
is precisely the number of roots of the characteristic polynomial of
\bar{A}
over
\mathcal{O}_{K}/\pi^{k}\mathcal{O}_{K}\cong\mathbb{Z}/q^{k}\mathbb{Z}
.

First suppose that
p\mid n
. Thus
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and, by
3.3
,
n=p\in\{2,3\}
. If
p=2
, then
\bar{A}
has order four and its characteristic polynomial is
\lambda^{2}+1
. This polynomial has one repeated root in
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and no roots in
\mathbb{Z}/2^{k}\mathbb{Z}
for
k>1
, hence
{\rm Fix}(A)=\{v,w\}
for some vertex
w
adjacent to
v
. If
p=3
, then
\bar{A}
has order three, and its characteristic polynomial is
\lambda^{2}+\lambda+1
. This polynomial has one repeated root in
\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
and no roots in
\mathbb{Z}/3^{k}\mathbb{Z}
for
k>1
. Hence
{\rm Fix}(A)
also consists of two adjacent vertices in this case.

Now suppose that
p\nmid n
. By
3.3
, either
2n\mid q\pm 1
, or
q
is even and
n\mid q\pm 1
. Observe from Hensel’s Lemma that, for
m
coprime to
p
,
K
contains an
m
-th root of unity if and only if
m\mid q-1
. If
2n\mid q-1
(respectively
q
is even and
n\mid q-1
), then it follows that
\bar{A}
is diagonalisable over
K
, so for every positive integer
k
the characteristic polynomial of
\bar{A}
has exactly two roots in
\mathbb{Z}/q^{k}\mathbb{Z}
. Hence
{\rm Fix}(A)
is a bi-infinite ray through
v
.
On the other hand, if
2n\mid q+1
(respectively
q
is even and
n\mid q+1
), then for every positive integer
k
the characteristic polynomial of
\bar{A}
has no roots in
\mathbb{Z}/q^{k}\mathbb{Z}
and
{\rm Fix}(A)=\{v\}
.
∎

We deduce that finite order elements of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
with order coprime to
p
, and finite order elements of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, have the same fixed point set as their non-trivial powers.

Corollary 3.5
.

If
A\in{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
is a non-trivial element of finite order
n
, and either
p\nmid n
or
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, then
{\rm Fix}(A)={\rm Fix}(A^{i})
for every positive integer
i\not\equiv 0\mod n
.

Proof.

Let
i
be a positive integer not divisible by
n
. If
p\mid n
, then
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
n=p\in\{2,3\}
by
3.3
. The result follows immediately since
A
and
A^{-1}
fix precisely the same points.

Hence suppose that
p\nmid n
and let
m
denote the order of
A^{i}
. Since
m
is a divisor of
n
, every odd
q
lies in the same congruence class modulo
2m
as it does modulo
2n
, and every even
q
lies in the same congruence class modulo
m
as it does modulo
n
.
It follows from
3.4
that
{\rm Fix}(A)
and
{\rm Fix}(A^{i})
are either both
a single vertex or both a bi-infinite ray. Since
{\rm Fix}(A)\subseteq{\rm Fix}(A^{i})
, the two fixed point sets must coincide.
∎

We will frequently use the following special case of
3.5
.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
A\in{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
be a non-trivial element of finite order
n
, where either
p\nmid n
or
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. If
{\rm Fix}(A)\neq{\rm Fix}(A^{2})
, then
A
is an involution.

We also make the following observations.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
A,B\in{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
be such that
B
is hyperbolic and
AB
has finite order. Suppose that either
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, or the order of
AB
is coprime to
p
. If
A
is an involution which fixes a vertex
y\in{\rm Ax}(B)
, then
A
is a reflection in
{\rm Ax}(B)
about
y
if and only if
AB
is an involution.

Proof.

If
A
is a reflection in
{\rm Ax}(B)
about
y
, then
(AB)^{2}
fixes
y
and
AB
does not, so
AB
is an involution by
3.6
. On the other hand, if
AB
is an involution, then
A\cdot By=B^{-1}Ay=B^{-1}y
and
A\cdot B^{-1}y=A(ABA)y=By
. By induction,
A\cdot B^{k}y=B^{-k}y
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
A\in{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
be a non-trivial element of finite order such that either
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, or the order of
A
is coprime to
p
. If
B\in{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
is hyperbolic (respectively elliptic with
{\rm Fix}(B)
a bi-infinite ray), then
A
and
B
commute if and only if
{\rm Fix}(A)={\rm Ax}(B)
(respectively
{\rm Fix}(A)={\rm Fix}(B)
).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
A
and
B
are respectively represented by the following matrices in
{\rm SL_{2}}(K)
:

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right]\textup{ and }\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda&0\\
0&\lambda^{-1}\end{array}\right].

Hence
A
and
B
commute if and only if
b(\lambda-\lambda^{-1})=c(\lambda-\lambda^{-1})=0
. Since
B
is non-trivial, this occurs if and only if
b=c=0
, in which case
A
and
B
fix the same two boundary points of
T_{q}
. The result follows from
3.4
.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
G
be a finite subgroup of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
which fixes at least two vertices of
T_{q}
. If
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, or
G
contains no elements of order
p
, then
G
is either cyclic or isomorphic to the Klein four-group
D_{2}
.

Proof.

By
3.4
, each element of
G
fixes a bi-infinite ray, unless
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
for
p\in\{2,3\}
and
G
contains an element of order
p
which fixes two adjacent vertices (in which case,
G
fixes precisely those two vertices).

We first show that
G
does not contain a non-abelian dihedral group. Indeed, suppose for a contradiction that
G
contains a dihedral group
H
of order
2m
, where
m>2
. Let
A
and
B
be generators of
H
, where
A
has order 2,
B
has order
m
and
ABA^{-1}=B^{-1}
. Note that
A\cdot{\rm Fix}(B)={\rm Fix}(ABA^{-1})={\rm Fix}(B)
, and either
{\rm Fix}(B)
consists of two adjacent vertices,
K=\mathbb{Q}_{3}
and
m=3
, or
{\rm Fix}(B)
is a bi-infinite ray. In the former case,
{\rm Fix}(A)
is a single vertex by
3.4
, which is a contradiction. In the latter case, if
x\in{\rm Fix}(B)
then
Ax\in{\rm Fix}(B)
and, since
A
fixes at least two vertices of
{\rm Fix}(B)
, we obtain
x\in{\rm Fix}(A)
.
Hence
{\rm Fix}(B)\subseteq{\rm Fix}(A)
and thus
{\rm Fix}(A)={\rm Fix}(B)
, so
A
and
B
commute by
3.8
, which is also a contradiction.

Since
G
is one of the groups listed in
3.3
, it must therefore be cyclic, or isomorphic to either
D_{2}
or
A_{4}
. Suppose that
G\cong A_{4}
(in particular,
p\neq 2
). Let
A
and
B
be generators of
G
, where
A
has order 3 and
B
has order 2. Note that
ABA^{-1}=B^{\prime}
for some element
B^{\prime}\in G
of order 2 which commutes with
B
. Since
p\neq 2
,
{\rm Fix}(B)
is a bi-infinite ray and it follows from
3.8
that
A\cdot{\rm Fix}(B)={\rm Fix}(B^{\prime})={\rm Fix}(B)
. As before, we conclude that
{\rm Fix}(A)={\rm Fix}(B)
, which is a contradiction since
A
and
B
do not commute. Hence
G
is either cyclic or isomorphic to
D_{2}
.
∎

We now consider certain two-generator subgroups
G
of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
which satisfy case
(6)
of
C
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
G=\langle A,B\rangle
be a subgroup of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
such that
A
is elliptic,
B
is hyperbolic, and
{\rm Fix}(A)\cap{\rm Ax}(B)
is a finite path
P
of length
\Delta\geq l(B)
. Suppose that
G_{0}=\langle A,BAB^{-1},\dots,B^{k}AB^{-k}\rangle
is finite, where
k=\lfloor\frac{\Delta}{l(B)}\rfloor
. If either
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, or
G_{0}
contains no elements of order
p
, then
\Delta=l(B)
and the following hold:

•

G_{0}
is isomorphic to one of the following groups:

–

D_{2n+1}
, where
q\equiv 1\mod 4
and
q\equiv\pm 1\mod(4n+2)
;

–

A_{4}
, where
q\equiv 1\mod 6
;

–

S_{4}
, where
q\equiv 1\mod 8
;

–

A_{5}
, where
q\equiv 1,19\mod 30
or
q\equiv 1\mod 10
.

•

If
G_{0}
is isomorphic to
S_{4}
or
A_{5}
, then there exists
g\in G_{0}
such that
g,gB
and
gBA
are involutions.

•

There is no proper subgroup of
G_{0}
which fixes at least two vertices of
T_{q}
and properly contains
\langle A\rangle
or
\langle BAB^{-1}\rangle
.

Proof.

Let
{\rm Fix}(A)\cap{\rm Ax}(B)=[x,y]
, where
B
translates
x
towards
y
. By
3.4
,
{\rm Fix}(A)
is a bi-infinite ray and
q-1
is divisible by either twice the order of
A
(if
q
is odd) or the order of
A
(if
q
is even).

If
\Delta>l(B)
, then the subgroup
\langle A,BAB^{-1}\rangle
of
G_{0}
fixes the path
[Bx,y]
(which contains at least two vertices). By
3.9
, this subgroup is abelian. Thus
A
and
BAB^{-1}
commute, so
{\rm Fix}(A)={\rm Fix}(BAB^{-1})
by
3.8
, a contradiction since
A
fixes
x
but
BAB^{-1}
does not. Hence
\Delta=l(B)
and
G_{0}=\langle A,BAB^{-1}\rangle
must be one of the non-abelian groups listed in
3.3
. We consider possible generating pairs for each such group, where both generators have the same order.

G_{0}
is dihedral
: If
G_{0}
is dihedral of order
2m
, then
A
has order 2 and
ABAB^{-1}
has order
m
. Thus
q\equiv 1\mod 4
and, by
3.3
,
q\equiv\pm 1\mod 2m
. Moreover, if
m
is even, then
{\rm Fix}(ABAB^{-1})
is a bi-infinite ray by
3.4
. Since
A
commutes with
(ABAB^{-1})^{\frac{m}{2}}
,
3.5
and
3.8
show that
{\rm Fix}(A)={\rm Fix}((ABAB^{-1})^{\frac{m}{2}})={\rm Fix}(ABAB^{-1})
. This is again a contradiction (since
A
fixes
x
but
ABAB^{-1}
does not), so

is odd and the result follows.

For the remainder of the proof, we will fix a choice of representatives for
A
and
B
in
{\rm SL_{2}}(K)
, and (by slight abuse of notation) define the trace of elements of
G
using these representatives. We will include appropriate bracketing to indicate the use of the well-known trace identity for elements
X,Y\in{\rm SL_{2}}(K)
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(X)\mathrm{tr}(Y)=\mathrm{tr}(XY)+\mathrm{tr}(XY^{-1}).

In particular, we will frequently use the identity

(3.1)

\mathrm{tr}(ABAB^{-1})=\mathrm{tr}^{2}(A)-\mathrm{tr}(ABA^{-1}B^{-1}).

G_{0}\cong A_{4}
: The only pairs of elements which have the same order and generate
A_{4}
are elements of order 3. Hence
A
has order 3, and
q\equiv 1\mod 6
since
3.3
shows that
q
is odd.

G_{0}\cong S_{4}
: In this case, it can be easily verified that
A
has order 4, and hence
q\equiv 1\mod 8
.
Moreover, it can also be verified that
ABAB^{-1}
and
ABA^{-1}B^{-1}
both have order 3. Since
\mathrm{tr}(A)=\pm\sqrt{2}
, it follows from
Equation

3.1
that
\mathrm{tr}(ABAB^{-1})=\mathrm{tr}(ABA^{-1}B^{-1})=1
. If
g=ABA^{-1}B^{-1}A\in G_{0}
, then

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(g)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(ABA^{-1}B^{-1})\mathrm{tr}(A)-\mathrm{tr}(ABA^{-1}B^{-1}A^{-1})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(A)-\mathrm{tr}(A)=0,

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(gB)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(ABA^{-1}B^{-1})\mathrm{tr}(AB)-\mathrm{tr}(ABA^{-1}B^{-2}A^{-1})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(AB)-\mathrm{tr}(A^{-1}B^{-1})=0,\textup{ and}

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(gBA)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(ABA^{-1}B^{-1})\mathrm{tr}(ABA)-\mathrm{tr}(ABA^{-1}B^{-1}A^{-1}B^{-1}A^{-1})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(ABA)-\mathrm{tr}(A^{-1}B^{-1}A^{-1})=0.

Hence
g,gB
and
gBA
are involutions.

G_{0}\cong A_{5}
: It can be easily verified that

must have order 3 or 5. If
A
has order 3, then

by
3.3
. It can also be verified that

and
ABA^{-1}B^{-1}
both have order 5. Hence
Equation

3.1
shows that
t=\mathrm{tr}(ABA^{-1}B^{-1})=1-\mathrm{tr}(ABAB^{-1})=\frac{1\pm\sqrt{5}}{2}
.

Similarly, if
A
has order 5, then
q\equiv 1\mod 10
and
Equation

3.1
shows that one of

and

has trace

, and the other has trace

In both cases, if

, then

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(g)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}((ABA^{-1}B^{-1})^{2})\mathrm{tr}(A)-\mathrm{tr}((ABA^{-1}B^{-1})^{2}A^{-1})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(A)(t^{2}-2)-\mathrm{tr}(A^{-1}(B^{-1}ABA^{-1}))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(A)(t^{2}-t-2)+\mathrm{tr}(AB^{-1}A^{-1}BA^{-1})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(A)(t^{2}-t-1)=0.

Similarly, it can be shown that

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(gB)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(AB)(t^{2}-t-1)=0,\textup{ and}

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(gBA)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(ABA)(t^{2}-t-1)=0.

Thus

and

are involutions.

Finally, suppose that
H
is a proper subgroup of
G_{0}
which fixes at least two vertices of

and properly contains
\langle A\rangle
or
\langle BAB^{-1}\rangle
. By considering the isomorphism types of

listed above,

is either a non-abelian dihedral group or

, but this contradicts

.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
G=\langle A,B\rangle
be a subgroup of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
such that either
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, or
G
contains no elements of order

.
Suppose that
A
is elliptic,
B
is hyperbolic, and
{\rm Fix}(A)\cap{\rm Ax}(B)
is a path of length

with initial vertex

and terminal vertex

. If

is finite and contains an element which maps

to

, then there exists

such that
g,gB
and
gBA
are involutions.

Suppose that

is such that

. Observe that any element of the form

has order 2 by

, since
(gBA^{i})^{2}

but

In particular,

.

Since

, it is readily verified (using a computational algebra package such as

to

∎

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
G=\langle A,B\rangle
be a subgroup of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
such that
A
is elliptic of order

,
B
is hyperbolic, and
{\rm Fix}(A)\cap{\rm Ax}(B)
is a path
P
of length
\Delta=l(B)
with initial vertex
x
and terminal vertex
y
. Let
G_{0}=\langle A,BAB^{-1}\rangle
and suppose that the following two conditions hold:

•

There exists
g\in G_{0}
such that
g,gB
and
gBA
are involutions;

•

There is no proper subgroup
H
of
G_{0}
which fixes at least two vertices and properly contains

.

Then
G=G_{0}*_{\langle A\rangle}\langle A,gB\rangle\cong G_{0}*_{C_{m}}D_{m}
and
G_{0}={\rm Stab}_{G}(y)
.

Proof.

Let

Finally, we consider the case where
G_{0}\cong A_{5}
. We require that either
A
has order 5 and
q\equiv 1\mod 10
, or
A
has order 3 and
q\equiv 1,19\mod 30
.

In the former case, we may choose
t=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
.
Set
s=1
, so that
ABAB^{-1}
has order 3.
As in the previous case, observe that
\mathrm{tr}(A^{2}BAB^{-1})=0
and so von Dyck’s Theorem shows that
\langle A,BAB^{-1}\rangle
is a quotient of
\langle x,y\mid x^{5},y^{5},(xy)^{3},(x^{2}y)^{2}\rangle\cong A_{5}
. Since
A_{5}
is simple, this gives
\langle A,BAB^{-1}\rangle\cong A_{5}.
As in the proof of
3.10
, the element
g=(ABA^{-1}B^{-1})^{2}A\in G_{0}
is such that
g,gB
and
gBA
are involutions. Moreover, the only proper subgroup of
A_{5}
which properly contains
C_{5}
is
D_{10}
, so a similar argument to the
S_{4}
case shows that
G\cong A_{5}*_{C_{5}}D_{5}
.

In the latter case, we may choose
t=1
, so that
A
has order 3.
Set
s=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
, so that
ABAB^{-1}
has order 5. Observe that

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(A^{2}BAB^{-1}ABAB^{-1})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(A)\mathrm{tr}((ABAB^{-1})^{2})-\mathrm{tr}(BAB^{-1}ABAB^{-1})

\displaystyle=(s^{2}-2)-\mathrm{tr}(AB^{-1}ABA)

\displaystyle=(s^{2}-2)-\mathrm{tr}(A)\mathrm{tr}(ABAB^{-1})+\mathrm{tr}(A^{-1}B^{-1}ABA)

\displaystyle=s^{2}-s-1=0.

Since
A_{5}
is simple, von Dyck’s Theorem shows that
\langle A,BAB^{-1}\rangle\cong\langle x,y\mid x^{3},y^{3},(xy)^{5},(x^{2}yxy)^{2}\rangle\cong A_{5}
. Since

is not divisible by 2,3 or 5,
G_{0}
contains no elements of order
p
and thus Lemmas
3.10
and

show that
G\cong A_{5}*_{C_{3}}D_{3}
. In both cases,
{\rm Stab}_{G}(y)\cong A_{5}
for some vertex

of

, so

is discrete by
3.1
.

6.
Algorithms for discreteness and density

We now present an algorithm which takes as input two elements
A,B
of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
(where either
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, or
G=\langle A,B\rangle
contains no elements of order
p
) and decides whether or not the subgroup
G=\langle A,B\rangle
is discrete. If
G
is discrete, then the algorithm returns the isomorphism type of
G
according to
A
. The algorithm relies on computing translation lengths on
T_{q}
using
3.2
.

Algorithm 6.1
.

Input
: Two elements
A,B\in{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
, where either
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
or the subgroup
G=\langle A,B\rangle
of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
contains no elements of order
p
.

Output
:
true:case (*)
if
G=\langle A,B\rangle
is discrete and of the type described in case
(*)
of
A
, and
false
otherwise.

(1)

If
G=\langle A,B\rangle
is finite, then return
true:case (a)
.

(2)

Set
X=A
and
Y=B
.

(3)

If
l(X)>l(Y)
, then swap
X
and
Y
.

(4)

If
l(X)>0
, then compute
m=\min\{l(XY),l(X^{-1}Y)\}
.

(i)

If
m\leq l(Y)-l(X)
then replace
Y
by an element from
\{XY,X^{-1}Y\}
which has translation length
m
and return to
(3)
.

(ii)

If
m>l(Y)-l(X)
, then return
true:case (b)
.

(5)

If
X
has infinite order, then return
false
.

(6)

Let
n
be the order of
X
. If
l(X^{i}Y)<l(Y)
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
, then replace
Y
by
X^{i}Y
.

(7)

If
l(Y)=0
, then return
true:case (c)
if
Y
has finite order and
l(XY)>0
, and otherwise return
false
.

(8)

If
l([X,Y])>0
, then return
true:case (d)
.

(9)

If
[X,Y]
has infinite order, then return
false
.

(10)

If
[X,Y]
is trivial, then return
true:case (e)
.

(11)

If
l([X,Y^{2}])=0
, then return
false
.

(12)

Set
G_{0}=\langle X,YXY^{-1}\rangle
. If
G_{0}
is infinite, then return
false
.

(13)

If there is no element
g\in G_{0}
such that
g
and
gY
both have order 2, then return
true:case (f)
.

(14)

Return
true:case (g)
.

Remark 6.2
.

Algorithm
6.1
includes several steps which involve determining the order of a subgroup or an element of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
. By Proposition
3.3
, there is a short list of finite subgroups of
{\rm PSL_{2}}(K)
and hence a short list of possibilities to check at each of these steps.

Remark 6.3
.

It is straightforward to determine the precise isomorphism class of a group described by cases
(a)-(g)
of Theorem
A
, but we omit this step from the algorithm for brevity.

Theorem 6.4
.

Algorithm
6.1
terminates and produces the correct output.

Proof.

The algorithm will always terminate, since the only recursive step is
(4)(i)
and this strictly reduces the integer
l(X)+l(Y)
; see also
[
8
, Theorem 4.2]
. It remains to prove that the algorithm is correct. If the algorithm returns:

•

true:case (a)
, then
G
is finite and hence discrete.

•

true:case (b)
, then
G
is discrete and free by
[
8
, Corollary 3.6]
.

•

true:case (c)
, then
X
and
Y
are elliptic of finite order. Since
l(XY)>0
, their fixed point sets are disjoint by
[
22
, Proposition 1.8]
. Thus
G
is discrete by
B

(c)
and
A’

(c)
holds.

•

true:case (d)
, then
X
is elliptic of finite order and

is hyperbolic. Since
l([X,Y])>0
, the path
{\rm Fix}(X)\cap{\rm Ax}(Y)
is either empty or of length shorter than
l(Y)
by
[

, Proposition 1.8]
. Hence
G
is discrete by
B

(d)
and
A’

(d)
holds.

•

true:case (e)
, then
X
is elliptic of finite order (possibly trivial),
Y
is hyperbolic, and
X
and
Y
commute. Hence
G
is discrete by
B

(e)
and
A’

(e)
holds.

•

true:case (f)
, then
X
is elliptic of finite order,
Y
is hyperbolic, and
P={\rm Fix}(X)\cap{\rm Ax}(Y)
has length
l(B)\leq\Delta<2l(B)
by
[
22
, Proposition 1.8]
since
[X,Y]
is elliptic and
[X,Y^{2}]
is not. Also
G_{0}=\langle A,BAB^{-1}\rangle
is finite and hence
\Delta=l(Y)
by

. By
3.7
,
G_{0}
does not contain a reflection in
{\rm Ax}(Y)
about the terminal vertex of

, so
G
is discrete by
B

(f)
and
A’

(f)
holds.

•

true:case (g)
, then, by the same argument as above,
X
is elliptic of finite order,
Y
is hyperbolic,
G_{0}=\langle A,BAB^{-1}\rangle
is finite and
P={\rm Fix}(X)\cap{\rm Ax}(Y)
has length
l(Y)
. Moreover,
3.7
shows that
G_{0}
does contain a reflection in
{\rm Ax}(Y)
about the terminal vertex of
P
, hence
G
is discrete by
B

(g)
and
A’

(g)
holds.

On the other hand, if the algorithm returns
false
(at steps
(5),(7)
,
(9)

(11)
or

), then

is infinite (this follows from

in the case of step

). Since

fixes the terminal vertex of

.
∎

We conclude by presenting algorithms (Algorithms

and

respectively) to decide whether a two-generator subgroup of either
{\rm SL_{2}}(\mathbb{R})
or
{\rm SL_{2}}(K)
(where

) is dense. We will use the following results, where

is either
\mathbb{R}
,

, or a finite extension of

, and

is accordingly the hyperbolic plane, the Riemann sphere, or the corresponding Bruhat-Tits tree.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
G
be a subgroup of
{\rm SL_{2}}(F)
.

(i)

If
G
is not Zariski dense, then either
G
fixes a point of
X
, or
G
stabilises a set of one or two points on the boundary
\partial X
.

(ii)

If
G
fixes a point of
X
, then
G
is not dense.

(iii)

If
G
stabilises a set of one or two points of
\partial X
, then
G
is not Zariski dense.

Proof.

(i)
: A Zariski closed proper subgroup of
{\rm SL_{2}}(F)
does not contain a non-abelian free subgroup. If
G
is not Zariski dense, then it follows from
[
14
, Section 3.1]
that
G
either fixes a point of
X
or stabilises a set of one or two points of
\partial X
.

(ii)
: If
G
fixes a point
x\in X
, then its closure in
{\rm SL_{2}}(F)
also fixes
x
. Since
{\rm SL_{2}}(F)
has no fixed points in
X
, this implies that
G
is not dense.

(iii)
: Observe that if
G
stabilises one or two points of
\partial X
, then the Zariski closure of
G
is at most two-dimensional. Since
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
is 3-dimensional,
G
is not Zariski dense.
∎

Retaining the notation from the previous lemma, we also obtain:

Lemma 6.7
.

If a subgroup
G
of
{\rm SL_{2}}(F)
is Zariski dense, then either
G
is discrete,
G
is dense, or
G
fixes a point of
X
.

Proof.

Consider
{\rm SL_{2}}(F)
as a finite-dimensional Lie group over either
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, and let
H
denote the closure of
G
in
{\rm SL_{2}}(F)
. By Cartan’s closed subgroup theorem
[
4
, Chapter III, §8 Theorem 2]
,
H
is a Lie subgroup of
{\rm SL_{2}}(F)
. Since
G
is Zariski dense, the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
H
is invariant under the adjoint action of
{\rm SL_{2}}(F)
. Hence
\mathfrak{h}
is an ideal of the simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
corresponding to
{\rm SL_{2}}(F)
, so either
\mathfrak{h}=0
or
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
. By
[
4
, Chapter III, §4 Theorem 3]
, either
H
is discrete or
H
is open. Suppose that
G
is neither discrete nor dense, so that
H
is a proper open subgroup of
{\rm SL_{2}}(F)
. By a result of Tits
[
27
, 3.6.2]

H
is bounded and thus the Bruhat–Tits fixed point theorem
[
5
, Proposition 3.2.4]
shows that
G
fixes a point of
X
.
∎

If

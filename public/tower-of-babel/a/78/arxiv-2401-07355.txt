Exploring dark matter-gauge boson effective interactions at the current and future colliders
Yu Zhang, Yi-Wei Huang, Wei-Tao Zhang, Mao Song, Ran Ding

Abstract
In this work, we investigate the collider constraints on effective interactions between Dark Matter (DM) particles and electroweak gauge bosons in a systematic way. We consider the simplified models in which scalar or Dirac fermion DM candidates only couple to electroweak gauge bosons through high dimensional effective operators. Taking into account the induced DM-quarks and DM-gluons operators from the Renormalization Group Evolution (RGE) running effect, we present comprehensive constraints on effective energy scale Λ \Lambda and Wilson coefficients C B ​ ( Λ ) , C W ​ ( Λ ) C_{B}(\Lambda),\,C_{W}(\Lambda) from direct detection, indirect detection, and collider searches. In particular, we present the corresponding sensitivity from the Large Hadron Electron Collider (LHeC) and the Future Circular Collider in electron-proton mode (FCC-ep) for the first time, update the mono- j j and mono- γ \gamma search limits at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC), and derive the new limits at the Circular Electron Positron Collider (CEPC).



I
Introduction

Observations from astrophysics and cosmology have provided overwhelming evidence for the existence of DM
Aghanim:2018eyx
. However, the microscopic properties of DM are still poorly known since all of such observations are based on its gravitational effects. The canonical DM candidate is the Weakly Interacting Massive Particles (WIMPs). Such a scenario is easy to fulfill relic abundance through thermal freeze-out mechanism (the “WIMP miracle”)
Lee:1977ua
, and can be naturally embedded in many popular theoretical frameworks
Jungman:1995df
;
Bertone:2004pz
. Moreover, due to the property of weak interaction with the SM particles, it can be probed through three experimental prongs, i.e., collider, direct and indirect detections.

Typically, the interactions between DM and SM particles are model-dependent and it is a great challenge to explore the complete model landscape. Instead of exhausting all possible theoretical model parameter space, the Effective Field Theory (EFT) framework is extensively used, which allows us to capture some key features of the high-scale physics effects on the electroweak scale while significantly simplifying the analysis procedures. For EFT collider searches, various signal channels based on different SM portal effective operators have been extensively studied at the hadron and electron colliders

II
Framework of DM-gauge boson effective interactions

We consider the DM particle as a Dirac fermion (
\chi
) or scalar (
\phi
), which connects with SM sector through high-dimensional DM-gauge boson effective operators. At the lowest order, such operators allow DM couple to single gauge boson, which induce electromagnetic dipole and anapole interactions
Cotta:2012nj
;
Rajaraman:2012fu
. However, these interactions lead to monochromatic gamma-rays due to the unsuppressed cross-section of DM annihilating into the
\gamma\gamma
and
\gamma Z
modes, which are severely constrained by current indirect detection experiments
Chen:2013gya
. We thus start from DM-diboson operators

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{B}^{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi^{*}\phi B^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu},\qquad{\cal O}_{W}^{\phi}=\phi^{*}\phi W^{a,\mu\nu}W^{a}_{\mu\nu},

(1)

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{B}^{\chi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\chi}\chi B^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu},\qquad{\cal O}_{W}^{\chi}=\overline{\chi}\chi W^{a,\mu\nu}W^{a}_{\mu\nu}.

(2)

These operators are assumed to be generated at the energy scale
\Lambda
with other high energy resonances much heavier than
\Lambda
are all decoupled. Notice that in this scenario, the coupling between DM and the SM quarks can also be induced through the renormalization group evolution (RGE) from the
\Lambda
scale down to the electroweak scale
\mu_{\rm EW}
. As a consequence, two extra dimension-6 (7) operators

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{y}^{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y_{q}\phi^{*}\phi\bar{q}Hq,\qquad{\cal O}_{H}^{\phi}=\phi^{*}\phi(H^{\dagger}H)^{2},

(3)

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{y}^{\chi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y_{q}\bar{\chi}\chi\bar{q}Hq,\qquad{\cal O}_{H}^{\phi}=\bar{\chi}\chi(H^{\dagger}H)^{2}.

(4)

need to be taken into account. In above equation,
y_{q}=\sqrt{2}m_{q}/v
are the Yukawa couplings of SM quarks, and
v
is the vacuum expectation value (VEV) of the SM Higgs doublet
H
. Then the total effective Lagrangian at scale
\mu
(
\mu_{\rm EW}<\mu<\Lambda
) is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm eff}^{\phi,\chi}=\sum_{k=B,W,y,H}\frac{C^{\phi,\chi}_{k}(\mu)}{\Lambda^{2}}{\cal O}_{k}\,.

(5)

To the leading logarithmic (LL) order, the Wilson coefficients of the effective operators
{\cal O}_{y}
and
{\cal O}_{H}
at the scale
\mu_{\rm EW}
are
Crivellin:2014gpa

\displaystyle C_{y}(\mu_{\rm EW})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{6Y_{qL}Y_{qR}\alpha_{1}}{\pi}\ln\left(\frac{\mu_{\rm EW}}{\Lambda}\right),

(6)

\displaystyle C_{H}(\mu_{\rm EW})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle-9\alpha^{2}_{2}\ln\left(\frac{\mu_{\rm EW}}{\Lambda}\right)C_{W}(\Lambda),

(7)

where

denotes the hypercharges of the left-handed (right-handed) quarks

Y_{uL}=Y_{dL}=1/6,Y_{uR}=2/3
and
Y_{dR}=1/3

and
\alpha_{2}

and
SU(2)_{Y}

\mu_{\rm EW}\equiv m_{Z}
with
\alpha_{1}\simeq 1/98
and
\alpha_{2}\simeq 1/29
. After Electroweak symmetry breaking (EWSB), the gauge eigenstate fields
B_{\mu}
and
W_{\mu}^{3}
mix into the physical massless photon field
A_{\mu}
and massive gauge field
Z_{\mu}
. Then the effective DM-diboson and DM-quark operators are recast as

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{FF}^{\phi}

\displaystyle=\phi^{*}\phi F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu},\quad{\cal O}_{FF}^{\chi}=\overline{\chi}\chi F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu},

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{q}^{\phi}

\displaystyle=m_{q}\phi^{*}\phi\bar{q}q,\quad{\cal O}_{q}^{\chi}=m_{q}\overline{\chi}\chi\bar{q}q.

(8)

where
FF=AA,AZ,ZZ,WW
. The relevant matching conditions are

\displaystyle{C_{AA}(\mu_{\rm EW})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{B}(\mu_{\rm EW})\cos^{2}\theta_{W}+C_{W}(\mu_{\rm EW})\sin^{2}\theta_{W},

\displaystyle{C_{ZZ}(\mu_{\rm EW})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{W}(\mu_{\rm EW})\cos^{2}\theta_{W}+C_{B}(\mu_{\rm EW})\sin^{2}\theta_{W},

\displaystyle{C_{AZ}(\mu_{\rm EW})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(C_{W}(\mu_{\rm EW})-C_{B}(\mu_{\rm EW})\right)\sin 2\theta_{W},

\displaystyle{C_{WW}(\mu_{\rm EW})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{W}(\mu_{\rm EW}),

\displaystyle{C_{q}(\mu_{\rm EW})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{y}(\mu_{\rm EW})-\frac{v^{2}}{m_{h}^{2}}C_{H}(\mu_{\rm EW}),

(9)

with
\theta_{W}
is the Weinberg angle.

Integrating out the heavy top quarks, the effective DM-gluon operators

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{q}^{\phi}=\alpha_{s}\phi^{*}\phi G^{a,\mu\nu}G_{\mu\nu}^{a},\quad{\cal O}_{q}^{\chi}=\alpha_{s}\overline{\chi}\chi G^{a,\mu\nu}G_{\mu\nu}^{a}

(10)

can be generated, where
G_{\mu\nu}^{a}
denotes the gluon field strength. The matching condition at leading order is given by

\displaystyle C_{G}(m_{t})=-\frac{1}{12\pi}C_{t}(m_{t}).

(11)

When RGEs are evolved from the EW scale down to the hadronic scale
\mu_{\rm had}\simeq 1
GeV,
{\cal O}_{AA}
will contribute to
{\cal O}_{q}
through the exchanging of virtual photons.
For scale
\mu

m_{q}<\mu<\mu_{\rm EW}

{C_{q}(\mu)}\simeq C_{q}(\mu_{\rm EW})+\frac{6Q_{q}^{2}\alpha_{\rm em}}{\pi}\ln\left(\frac{\mu}{\mu_{\rm EW}}\right)C_{AA}(\mu_{\rm EW})

(12)

at LL order, where

\alpha_{\rm em}\simeq 1/137

At
m_{b}
and
m_{c}
threshold, one has to integrate out the corresponding heavy bottom and charm quarks
by taking into account Eq. (
11

\mu_{\rm had}\simeq 1

\displaystyle C_{q}\left(\mu_{\rm had}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{6Y_{q_{L}}Y_{q_{R}}\alpha_{1}}{\pi}C_{B}(\Lambda)+9\alpha_{2}^{2}\frac{v^{2}}{m_{h}^{2}}C_{W}(\Lambda)\right)\ln\left(\frac{\mu_{\rm EW}}{\Lambda}\right)

(13)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{6Q_{q}^{2}\alpha_{\rm em}}{\pi}C_{AA}(\mu_{\rm EW})\ln\left(\frac{\mu_{\rm had}}{\mu_{\rm EW}}\right),

\displaystyle C_{G}\left(\mu_{\rm had}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{12\pi}\Bigg\{\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\pi}C_{B}(\Lambda)+27\alpha_{2}^{2}\frac{v^{2}}{m_{h}^{2}}C_{W}(\Lambda)\right)\ln\left(\frac{\mu_{\rm EW}}{\Lambda}\right)

(14)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha_{\rm em}}{3\pi}C_{AA}(\mu_{\rm EW})\left[\ln\left(\frac{m_{b}}{\mu_{\rm EW}}\right)+4\ln\left(\frac{m_{c}}{\mu_{\rm EW}}\right)\right]\Bigg\},

III
Sensitivities at the LHeC and FCC-ep

In this section, we will investigate the sensitivity of the DM-gauge boson effective operators at future electron-proton colliders.

III.1
Signal channels and SM backgrounds

From the effective interactions in Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
), DM pairs can be produced at the electron-proton collider through both the Charged Current (CC) and the Neutral Current (NC), which respectively correspond to
W
boson mode and
\gamma/Z
mode of VBF processes. In CC production, DM can be generated through the process of
e^{-}p\to\nu_{e}j\chi\bar{\chi}(\phi\phi^{*})
via
WW
fusion, resulting in a mono-jet plus missing energy signature. It is important to note that the mono-jet signature coincidentally aligns with the background of CC deeply inelastic scattering. Due to the absence of kinematic handles in the final state, distinguishing this signal channel from its primary background poses a significant challenge. Consequently, our study is primarily focused on NC production - specifically the process
e^{-}p\to e^{-}j\chi\bar{\chi}~(\phi\phi^{*})
, as depicted in Fig.
1
. The corresponding signature is thereby characterized as the
{\not{E}}_{T}+e^{-}j
channel.

Figure 1:
Feynman diagrams for the processes
e^{-}p\to e^{-}j\chi\bar{\chi}(\phi\phi^{*})

For SM background estimation, we consider both reducible background (RB) and irreducible background (IB). The IB mainly is due to the processes
e^{-}p\to e^{-}j\nu_{\ell}\bar{\nu}_{\ell}~(\ell=e,~\mu,~\tau)
. Among them, both
W/Z
-boson bremsstrahlung processes
e^{-}p\to e^{-}jZ/\nu_{e}jW^{-}~(Z\to\nu_{e}\bar{\nu}_{e}/W^{-}\to e^{-}\bar{\nu}_{e})
contribute to
\nu_{e}
final state, while for
\nu_{\mu,\tau}
,
only bremsstrahlung process
e^{-}p\to e^{-}jZ~(Z\to\nu_{\mu,\tau}\bar{\nu}_{\mu,\tau})
is relevant. For RB, a major contribution comes from
e^{-}p\to e^{-}jW^{\pm}
with
W
’s leptonic decay
W^{\pm}\to\ell^{\pm}\nu_{\ell}
. There then two possibilities that need to be taken into account. One is that electron and muon final states run out of the detector acceptance and
\tau
final state fakes a hard jet in the detector. Another possibility is that the products of hadronic
\tau
decays are too soft to be tagged
Tang:2015uha
;
Han:2018rkz
. As a consequence, The SM background can be summarized as follows:

•

IB:
e^{-}p\to e^{-}j\nu_{\ell}\bar{\nu}_{\ell}
,

•

RB:
e^{-}p\to e^{-}j\ell^{\pm}\nu_{\ell}~(\bar{\nu}_{\ell})
,

where
\ell=e,\mu,\tau
.

at the

|E_{\gamma}-E_{\gamma}^{Z}|<5\Gamma_{\gamma}^{Z}.

In this section, we discuss the bounds on
\Lambda
from current direct and indirect search experiments.
The effective operators
{\cal O}_{B}
and
{\cal O}_{W}

lead to the non-relativistic cross-sections of DM annihilation
\chi\bar{\chi},\ \phi\phi^{*}\to\gamma\gamma/\gamma Z/ZZ/WW
as

\langle\sigma_{k}v\rangle=a_{k}+b_{k}v^{2}+{\cal O}(v^{4})

(21)

with
k=B,W
annihilation channels. The coefficients of
a
and
b
are
Chen:2013gya

\displaystyle\chi\left\{\begin{aligned} a_{B}&=a_{W}=0,\\
b_{B}&=\frac{\left|C_{B}(\Lambda)\right|^{2}}{\pi}\frac{m_{\chi}^{4}}{\Lambda^{6}}\Big[c_{w}^{4}+\frac{c_{w}^{2}s_{w}^{2}}{8}\beta_{\gamma Z}^{2}\left(x_{Z}-4\right)^{2}\Theta(M_{Z}<2m_{\chi})+\frac{s_{w}^{4}}{8}\beta_{ZZ}\left(3x_{Z}^{2}-8x_{Z}+8\right)\Theta(M_{Z}<m_{\chi})\Big],\\
b_{W}&=\frac{\left|C_{W}(\Lambda)\right|^{2}}{\pi}\frac{m_{\chi}^{4}}{\Lambda^{6}}\Big[s_{w}^{4}+\frac{c_{w}^{2}s_{w}^{2}}{8}\beta_{\gamma Z}^{2}\left(x_{Z}-4\right)^{2}\Theta(M_{Z}<2m_{\chi})+\frac{c_{w}^{4}}{8}\beta_{ZZ}\left(3x_{Z}^{2}-8x_{Z}+8\right)\Theta(M_{Z}<m_{\chi})\\
&+\frac{1}{4}\beta_{WW}\left(3x_{W}^{2}-8x_{W}+8\right)\Theta(M_{W}<m_{\chi})\Big],\end{aligned}\right.

(22)

\displaystyle\phi\left\{\begin{aligned} a_{B}&=\frac{\left|C_{B}(\Lambda)\right|^{2}}{\pi}\frac{2m_{\phi}^{2}}{\Lambda^{4}}\Big[c_{w}^{4}+\frac{c_{w}^{2}s_{w}^{2}}{8}\beta_{\gamma Z}^{2}\left(x_{Z}-4\right)^{2}\Theta(M_{Z}<2m_{\phi})+\frac{s_{w}^{4}}{8}\beta_{ZZ}\left(3x_{Z}^{2}-8x_{Z}+8\right)\Theta(M_{Z}<m_{\phi})\Big],\\

VIII
Summary and conclusion

In this work, we focus on the constraints on the dimension-6 (7) effective operators between scalar (Dirac fermion) DM and SM gauge bosons at future
ep
colliders LHeC and FCC-ep via
{\not{E}}_{T}+e^{-}j
signature for the first time. We find that SM irreducible background and reducible background can be effectively suppressed by imposing appropriate kinematic cuts.
We also consider the sensitivity at the future
e^{+}e^{-}
collider CEPC in three different modes with mono-photon signature and update the limits from the LHC with current mono-
j
and mono-
\gamma
searches. Besides the contribution from
{\not{E}_{T}+W/Z\ (\to{\rm hardons})}
channel due to the DM-diboson operators for mono-
j
at LHC, we also investigate the contribution from the loop induced effective DM-quarks and DM-glouns operators. We find that the contribution from DM-quarks and DM-gluons operators can be ignored.
We present the constraints on effective energy scale
\Lambda
as a function of DM mass with three typical cases of Wilson coefficients:
C_{B}^{6(7)}=C_{W}^{6(7)}=500
,
C_{B}^{6(7)}=0,C_{W}^{6(7)}=500
, and
C_{B}^{6(7)}=500,C_{W}^{6(7)}=0
. The FCC-ep presents better sensitivity than LHeC in all cases for scalar and Dirac fermion DM. It can be found that the collider searches can probe light DM touching the neutrino floor.

Entropy stable finite difference schemes for Chew, Goldberger & Low anisotropic plasma flow equations
Chetan Singh, Anshu Yadav, Deepak Bhoriya, Harish Kumar, Dinshaw S. Balsara

Abstract
In this article, we consider the Chew, Goldberger & Low (CGL) plasma flow equations, which is a set of nonlinear, non-conservative hyperbolic PDEs modelling anisotropic plasma flows. These equations incorporate the double adiabatic approximation for the evolution of the pressure, making them very valuable for plasma physics, space physics and astrophysical applications. We first present the entropy analysis for the weak solutions. We then propose entropy-stable finite-difference schemes for the CGL equations. The key idea is to rewrite the CGL equations such that the non-conservative terms do not contribute to the entropy equations. The conservative part of the rewritten equations is very similar to the magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) equations. We then symmetrize the conservative part by following Godunov’s symmetrization process for MHD. The resulting equations are then discretized by designing entropy conservative numerical flux and entropy diffusion operator based on the entropy scaled eigenvectors of the conservative part. We then prove the semi-discrete entropy stability of the schemes for CGL equations. The schemes are then tested using several test problems derived from the corresponding MHD test cases.

2
CGL equations for anisotropic plasma flows

Following
[
39
,
53
]
, the CGL equations can be written as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho\textbf{u})=0,

(1a)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho\textbf{u}}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\left[\rho\textbf{u}\textbf{u}+p_{\perp}\textbf{I}+(p_{\parallel}-p_{\perp})\boldsymbol{b}\boldsymbol{b}-\left(\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{B}-\frac{|\boldsymbol{B}|^{2}}{2}\textbf{I}\right)\right]=0,

(1b)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial p_{\parallel}}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(p_{\parallel}\textbf{u})=-2p_{\parallel}\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla\textbf{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{b},

(1c)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial e}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\left[\textbf{u}\left(e+p_{\perp}+\frac{|\boldsymbol{B}|^{2}}{2}\right)+\textbf{u}\cdot\left((p_{\parallel}-p_{\perp})\boldsymbol{b}\boldsymbol{b}-\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{B}\right)\right]=0,

(1d)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{B}}{\partial t}+\nabla\times[-(\textbf{u}\times\boldsymbol{B})]=0.

(1e)

Here,
\rho,\textbf{u}=(u_{x},u_{y},u_{z})^{\top}
, and
\boldsymbol{B}=(B_{x},B_{y},B_{z})^{\top}
are the density, velocity, and magnetic field, respectively. The vector
\boldsymbol{b}
is the unit vector in the direction of the magnetic field, defined as
\boldsymbol{b}=\boldsymbol{B}/|\boldsymbol{B}|
. One of the key shortcomings of the CGL system is that a physics-free study of the CGL equations seems to require magnetic field
\boldsymbol{B}
to be non-zero. Otherwise, the conventional wisdom has been that the magnetic field direction is not defined, and hence the equations are not valid. However, it is useful to mention that a recent paper by
[
9
]
shows that a physics-based approach to the CGL equations resolves this and many other issues associated with simulating the CGL equations. However, we will assume that the magnetic field is non-zero. Integration of the mathematics-based approach here with the physics-based approach in
[
9
]
will be the topic of a future paper. The pressure tensor
\boldsymbol{P}
, rotated by the magnetic field is described using scalars
p_{\parallel}
and
p_{\perp}
via relation,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{P}=p_{\parallel}\boldsymbol{b}\boldsymbol{b}+p_{\perp}(\textbf{I}-\boldsymbol{b}\boldsymbol{b})+\boldsymbol{\Pi}

\displaystyle=p_{\parallel}\begin{pmatrix}b_{x}b_{x}&b_{x}b_{y}&b_{x}b_{z}\\
b_{y}b_{x}&b_{y}b_{y}&b_{y}b_{z}\\
b_{z}b_{x}&b_{z}b_{y}&b_{z}b_{z}\end{pmatrix}+p_{\perp}\begin{pmatrix}1-b_{x}b_{x}&-b_{x}b_{y}&-b_{x}b_{z}\\
-b_{y}b_{x}&1-b_{y}b_{y}&-b_{y}b_{z}\\
-b_{z}b_{x}&-b_{z}b_{y}&1-b_{z}b_{z}\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}\Pi_{xx}&\Pi_{xy}&\Pi_{xz}\\
\Pi_{yx}&\Pi_{yy}&\Pi_{yz}\\
\Pi_{zx}&\Pi_{zy}&\Pi_{zz}\end{pmatrix}

where the scalars are given by
p_{\parallel}=\boldsymbol{P}:\boldsymbol{b}\boldsymbol{b}
and
p_{\perp}=\boldsymbol{P}:\textbf{I}-\boldsymbol{b}\boldsymbol{b}
, denoting parallel and perpendicular components of the pressure tensor, respectively. The CGL model is obtained by neglecting the Finite Larmor Radius pressure tensor, i.e., we assume
\boldsymbol{\Pi}=0
. The equation of state is given by,

\displaystyle e=\frac{\rho|\textbf{u}|^{2}}{2}+\frac{|\boldsymbol{B}|^{2}}{2}+\frac{3p}{2}

(2)

where,
p=\frac{2p_{\perp}+p_{\parallel}}{3}
is the average scalar pressure. Given the above notations, we define the vector of conservative variables as
\mathbf{U}=(\rho,\rho\textbf{u},p_{\parallel},e,\boldsymbol{B})^{\top}
. Equation (
1a
) represents the conservation of mass, followed by Equation (
1b
), representing momentum conservation. The parallel component of the pressure tensor is governed by (
1c
). This is the only equation that contains non-conservative terms. The energy conservation is given by equation (
1d
), followed by the induction equation (
1e
) for the magnetic field. In addition, we also have divergence-free conditions on the magnetic field given by,

\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{B}=0

(3)

However, this condition is an outcome of the induction equations (
1e
) and divergence free initial condition.

The eigenvalues of the CGL system
[
44
,
53
]
in the
x
-direction are given by,

\boldsymbol{\Lambda}=(u_{x},~u_{x},~0,~u_{x}\pm c_{a},~u_{x}\pm c_{f},~u_{x}\pm c_{s})

(4)

where,

\displaystyle c_{a}=\sqrt{{\frac{B_{x}^{2}}{\rho}-\frac{(p_{\parallel}-p_{\perp}){b}_{x}^{2}}{\rho}}},

\displaystyle c_{f}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\rho}}\big[|\boldsymbol{B}|^{2}+2p_{\bot}+b_{x}^{2}(2p_{\parallel}-p_{\perp})+\{(|\boldsymbol{B}|^{2}+2p_{\perp}+b_{x}^{2}(2p_{\parallel}-p_{\perp}))^{2}

\displaystyle~~~~~~+4(p_{\perp}^{2}b_{x}^{2}(1-b_{x}^{2})-3p_{\parallel}p_{\perp}b_{x}^{2}(2-b_{x}^{2})+3p_{\parallel}^{2}b_{x}^{4}-3B_{x}^{2}p_{\parallel})\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big]^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle c_{s}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\rho}}\big[|\boldsymbol{B}|^{2}+2p_{\perp}+b_{x}^{2}(2p_{\parallel}-p_{\perp})-\{(|\boldsymbol{B}|^{2}+2p_{\perp}+b_{x}^{2}(2p_{\parallel}-p_{\perp}))^{2}

\displaystyle~~~~~~+4(p_{\perp}^{2}b_{x}^{2}(1-b_{x}^{2})-3p_{\parallel}p_{\perp}b_{x}^{2}(2-b_{x}^{2})+3p_{\parallel}^{2}b_{x}^{4}-3B_{x}^{2}p_{\parallel})\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It was shown in
[
44
]
that eigenvalues of the system can become imaginary. Hence, some conditions on the admissible domain need to be imposed. Following
[
44
]
, we define,

p_{m}=\frac{p_{\bot}^{2}}{6p_{\bot}+3|\boldsymbol{B}|^{2}},\text{ and }p_{M}={|\boldsymbol{B}|^{2}}+p_{\bot}.

Then, the admissible domain for the CGL system is given by,

\Omega=\{\textbf{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{9}|~\rho>0,~p_{\parallel}>0,p_{\perp}>0,~p_{m}\leq p_{\parallel}\leq p_{M}\}.

(5)

In addition, it was observed in
[
44
]
, that the slow wave is not necessarily slower than the Alfvén wave, hence the domain
\Omega
has to be divided as follows:

1.

p_{m}\leq p_{\parallel}\leq\frac{p_{M}}{4},~~~~~~~~~~\text{if}~~~~~~~c_{s}\leq c_{a}\leq c_{f},

2.

\frac{p_{M}}{4}\leq p_{\parallel}\leq\frac{p_{M}}{4}+\frac{3p_{m}}{4},~~\text{if}~~~~~~c_{s}\leq c_{a}<c_{f},

3.

\frac{p_{M}}{4}+\frac{3p_{m}}{4}\leq p_{\parallel}\leq p_{M},~~\text{if}~~~~~~c_{a}\leq c_{s}<c_{f}.

2.1
Entropy analysis

Several hyperbolic systems consider pressure tensor as one of the variables. Following
[

,
11
,
60
,
8
,
64
]
, we define the entropy
\mathcal{E}
and the entropy flux

for the CGL system (

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=-\rho s,\qquad\mathcal{Q}_{x}=-\rho u_{x}s,\qquad s=\ln\left(\dfrac{\det\boldsymbol{P}}{\rho^{5}}\right)=\ln\left(\dfrac{p_{\parallel}p_{\perp}^{2}}{\rho^{5}}\right).

(6)

Lemma 1

If
U
is a smooth solution of the CGL system (
1a
)-(
1e
), then it satisfies,

\partial_{t}\mathcal{E}+\partial_{x}\mathcal{Q}_{x}=0.

(7)

Proof

By the chain rule,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(p_{\parallel}p_{\perp}^{2})=p_{\perp}^{2}\partial_{t}p_{\parallel}+2p_{\parallel}p_{\perp}\partial_{t}p_{\perp}.

Using (
1a
)-(
1e
) to derive the evolution of
p_{\perp}
, to get,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}p_{\perp}=

\displaystyle-\partial_{x}(p_{\perp}u_{x})-p_{\perp}\partial_{x}u_{x}+p_{\perp}b_{x}(b_{x}{\partial_{x}u_{x}}+b_{y}{\partial_{x}u_{y}}+b_{z}{\partial_{x}u_{z}})-{(\textbf{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{B})}{\partial_{x}B_{x}}.

(8)

Combing with (
1c
), we get,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(p_{\parallel}p_{\perp}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{\perp}^{2}\partial_{t}p_{\parallel}+2p_{\parallel}p_{\perp}\partial_{t}p_{\perp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-u_{x}\partial_{x}(det\boldsymbol{P})-5(det(\boldsymbol{P}))\partial_{x}u_{x}-2p_{\parallel}p_{\perp}(\textbf{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{B})\partial_{x}B_{x},

Using (
1a
), we have,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}s+u_{x}\partial_{x}s+2\frac{(\textbf{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{B})}{p_{\perp}}\partial_{x}B_{x}=0.

which immediately results in

\partial_{t}\mathcal{E}+\partial_{x}\mathcal{Q}_{x}+\frac{2\rho(\textbf{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{B})}{p_{\perp}}\partial_{x}B_{x}=0.

(9)

This combining with
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{B}=0
, gives (
7
).

Remark 1

In two dimensions, (
9
) is replaced with,

\partial_{t}\mathcal{E}+\partial_{x}\mathcal{Q}_{x}+\partial_{y}\mathcal{Q}_{y}+\frac{2\rho(\textbf{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{B})}{p_{\perp}}\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{B}=0,

(10)

with
\mathcal{Q}_{y}=-\rho u_{y}s
. Furthermore, the entropy equality (
7
), for the weak solutions results in,

\partial_{t}\mathcal{E}+\partial_{x}\mathcal{Q}_{x}+\partial_{y}\mathcal{Q}_{y}\leq 0.

(11)

We aim to design the entropy stable schemes so that (
11
) holds at the semi-discrete level.

4.2
Entropy stable schemes

\displaystyle{\hat{\textbf{F}}}_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}=\tilde{\textbf{F}}_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}-\frac{1}{2}\textbf{D}_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}[\![\textbf{V}]\!]_{i+\frac{1}{2},j},~{\hat{\textbf{F}}}_{y,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}=\tilde{\textbf{F}}_{y,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\textbf{D}_{y,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}[\![\textbf{V}]\!]_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}},

4.1

[

Theorem 4.3

The numerical scheme (
27

5.2
ARK IMEX schemes

The source term
S
contains the parameter
\tau
, which makes the source term stiff. To avoid the small time step restriction required for stability of the explicit scheme, treat the source term implicitly. In particular, we use the Additive Runge-Kutta (ARK) IMEX scheme. For the second-order discretization, we use the L-Stable second-order accurate ARK-IMEX scheme from
[
56
,
10
]
. The method has two implicit stages and is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{i,j}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{U}^{n}_{i,j}+\Delta t\ \big(\beta\textbf{S}(\mathbf{U}^{(1)}_{i,j}\big),

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}^{(2)}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{U}^{n}_{i,j}+\Delta t\ \big(\mathcal{L}_{i,j}(\mathbf{U}^{(1)})+(1-2\beta)\textbf{S}(\mathbf{U}^{(1)}_{i,j})+\beta\textbf{S}(\mathbf{U}^{(2)}_{i,j})\big),

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{i,j}^{(n+1)}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{U}^{n}_{i,j}+\Delta t\ \bigg(\dfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{i,j}(\mathbf{U}^{(1)})+\dfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{i,j}(\mathbf{U}^{(2)})+\dfrac{1}{2}\textbf{S}(\mathbf{U}^{(1)}_{i,j})+\dfrac{1}{2}\textbf{S}(\mathbf{U}^{(2)}_{i,j})\bigg).

where
\beta=1-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
.

For the third and fourth-order ARK scheme, we follow
[
45
]
. In the ARK-IMEX schemes, we need to solve a system of nonlinear equations to solve the Implicit source part. We use the Newton solver, where the convergence and the next direction search are based on the back-tracing line search framework. A detailed procedure is given in
[
21
]
.

in (

2.

O3ES-IMEX

S
is treated implicitly.

3.

7
Conclusion

In this article, we have proposed higher-order finite difference entropy stable numerical schemes for the CGL equations, which form a system of non-conservative hyperbolic equations. The proposed schemes are based on the novel reformulation of the equations, such that the non-conservative terms do not contribute to the entropy evolution. We then present the Godunov symmetrization of the conservative part. To design entropy stable schemes for the complete system, we first consider the entropy conservative numerical schemes for the conservative part and then extend the same to include non-conservative terms. To ensure entropy stability, we have considered the diffusion operator based on the entropy scaled right eigenvector of the symmetrized conservative part. The time discretization is performed using SSP-RK schemes for explicit schemes, which are used to calculate anisotropic solutions. For the isotropic case, we have considered a still source term. Hence, we have used ARK-IMEX schemes for time discretization. To test the entropy stability of the proposed numerical schemes, we have designed several test cases in one dimension by generalizing the one-dimensional MHD Riemann problems. We demonstrate that for a wide variety of test cases, the numerical entropy decay holds for each time step for both anisotropic and isotropic limits. For the isotropic limit, we show that solutions matched the MHD solution, as expected. For the two-dimensional test case, we have considered the Orzag-Tang vortex test case and present the isotropic case. We demonstrate that the schemes decay entropy only when discontinuities start to form in the solution. Furthermore, the solutions match the MHD solutions.

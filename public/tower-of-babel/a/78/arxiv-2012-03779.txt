A new characterization of silting subcategories in the stable category of a Frobenius extriangulated category
Yajun Ma, Nanqing Ding, Yafeng Zhang, Jiangsheng Hu

Abstract




Abstract

We give a new characterization of silting subcategories in the stable category of a Frobenius extriangulated category, generalizing the result of Di et al. (J. Algebra 525 (2019) 42-63) about the Auslander-Reiten type correspondence for silting subcategories over triangulated categories. More specifically, for any Frobenius extriangulated category
\mathcal{C}
, we establish a bijective correspondence
between silting subcategories of the stable category
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
and certain covariantly finite subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
. As a consequence, a characterization of
silting subcategories in the stable category of a Frobenius exact category is given. This result is applied to homotopy categories over abelian categories with enough projectives, derived categories over Grothendieck categories with enough projectives as well as to the stable category of Gorenstein projective modules over a ring
R
.

Keywords:
Frobenius extriangulated category; silting subcategory; covariantly finite; cotorsion pair.

2010 Mathematics Subject Classification:
18E30; 18E10; 18G25; 18G20.

1.
Introduction

Silting subcategories and objects in triangulated categories were introduced by Keller and Vossieck
[
19
]
and later studied by Aihara and Iyama
[
1
]
, as a way of overcoming a problem inherent to tilting objects, namely, that mutations are sometimes impossible to define.
According to
[
20
,
25
]
, silting subcategories (objects) are closely related to t-structures and co-t-structures in triangulated categories. Thus one can better understand the structure of a triangulated category from the viewpoint of the silting theory.
Recently, Di, Liu, Wang and Wei
[
10
]
established a bijective correspondence
between silting subcategories of a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
and certain covariantly finite subcategories of
\mathcal{T}
. The notion of covariantly finite subcategories was first introduced by Auslander and Smal
{\o}

[
4
]
(note that a subcategory is called covariantly finite precisely when it is preenveloping in the sense of Enochs and Jenda
[
11
]
). It should be noted that the bijective correspondence mentioned above is known as the “Auslander-Reiten type correspondence” for silting subcategories in
[
10
]
, which is inspired by the classical Auslander-Reiten type correspondence for tilting modules established by Auslander and Reiten in
[
3
]
.

Happel
[
15
]
showed that if
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{S})
is a Frobenius exact category, then its stable category
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
carries a triangulated structure. Beligiannis obtained a similar result
[
5
, Theorem 7.2]
by replacing
\mathcal{C}
with a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
and replacing
\mathcal{S}
with a proper class of triangles
\mathcal{E}
. Recently, Nakaoka and Palu unified the above results and proved
that the stable category of a Frobenius extriangulated category is a triangulated category
(see
[
24
, Corollary 7.4 and Remark 7.5]
). The notion of extriangulated categories was introduced by Nakaoka and Palu in
[
24
]
as a simultaneous generalization of
exact categories and triangulated categories. As typical examples we
have that Frobenius exact categories and triangulated categories are Frobenius extriangulated categories (see
[
24
, Example 7.2(1) and Corollary 7.6]
). However, there exist some examples of Frobenius extriangulated categories which are neither exact nor
triangulated (see
[
29
, Example 4.14]
).

The aim of this paper is to give a new characterization of silting subcategories in the stable category of a Frobenius extriangulated category. More precisely, for any Frobenius extriangulated category
\mathcal{C}
, we give a bijective correspondence
between silting subcategories of the stable category
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
and certain covariantly finite subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
.

In dealing with the above problem, the main technical problem we encounter is that one has to choose an appropriate covariantly finite subcategory in a Frobenius extriangulated category
\mathcal{C}
from a given silting subcategory in the stable category
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
. To circumvent this problem, by the bijective correspondence between silting subcategories and bounded co-t-structures in a triangulated category established by Mendoza Hern
\acute{\mathrm{a}}
ndez, S
\acute{\mathrm{a}}
enz, Santiago Vargas and Souto Salorio in
[
23
, Corollary 5.9]
, we first provide an explicit procedure to construct cotorsion pairs in the “new” extriangulated category induced by the certain subcategory in a given extriangulated category, and then demonstrate that these cotorsion pairs not only give rise to constructing bounded co-t-structures for silting subcategories in the stable category of a Frobenius extriangulated category, but also are helpful to choose certain covariantly finite subcategories in a Frobenius extriangulated category.

To state our main results more precisely, let us first introduce some definitions.

Now, our main result can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Frobenius extriangulated category. The assignments

\Psi:\underline{\mathcal{M}}\mapsto

{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}

and

\Phi:\mathcal{H}\mapsto\underline{\mathcal{H}\bigcap{{}^{\bot}\mathcal{H}}}

give mutually inverse bijections between the following classes:

(1)

Silting subcategories
\underline{\mathcal{M}}
of the stable category
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
with
Proj(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\mathcal{M}
.

(2)

Subcategories
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathcal{C}
, which are specially covariantly finite and coresolving in
\mathcal{C}
such that
\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}=\mathcal{C}
and for any
H\in\mathcal{H}
, there exists a positive integer
t\geq 1
making
\mathbb{E}^{i}(H,\mathcal{H})=0
for all
i\geq t
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is a triangulated category in Theorem
1.1
, then we recover the Auslander-Reiten type correspondence for silting subcategories over triangulated categories established by Di, Liu, Wang and Wei in
[
10
]
(see Corollary
4.15
). Now, specializing
\mathcal{C}
to a Frobenius exact category, we then get the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Frobenius exact category. The assignments

\underline{\mathcal{M}}\mapsto

{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}

and

\mathcal{H}\mapsto\underline{\mathcal{H}\bigcap{{}^{\bot}\mathcal{H}}}

give mutually inverse bijections between the following classes:

(1)

Silting subcategories
\underline{\mathcal{M}}
of
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
with
Proj(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\mathcal{M}
.

(2)

Subcategories
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathcal{C}
, which are specially covariantly finite and coresolving in
\mathcal{C}
such that
\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}=\mathcal{C}
and for any
H\in\mathcal{H}
, there exists a positive integer
t\geq 1
making
\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{C}}^{i}(H,\mathcal{H})=0
for all
i\geq t
.

We will apply Corollary
1.2
to homotopy categories over abelian categories with enough projectives, derived categories over Grothendieck categories with enough projectives as well as to the stable category of Gorenstein projective modules over a ring
R
.

The contents of this paper are outlined as follows. In Section
2
, we fix notations and recall some definitions and basic facts used throughout the paper. In Section

3

. In Section

1.1

for some different Frobenius exact categories, including Corollary

]

Lemma 4.12
.

[
9
, Corollary 3.10]

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Frobenius extriangulated category. Suppose that
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
are two subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
such that

. Then

is a cotorsion pair with the core
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{U}\bigcap\mathcal{V}

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Frobenius extriangulated category. Suppose that
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
are two subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
such that
Proj(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\mathcal{U}\bigcap\mathcal{V}
.
Then
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
is a hereditary cotorsion pair on
\mathcal{C}
if and only if
(\mathcal{\underline{U}},\mathcal{\underline{V}})
is a co-t-structure on
\underline{\mathcal{C}}.

Proof.

“
\Rightarrow
”. By Lemma
4.12
and the definition of co-t-structures, it suffices to check that
\mathcal{\underline{U}}
is closed under
[-1]
. Let

\textstyle{U_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\underline{x}}
\textstyle{U_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\underline{y}}
\textstyle{U_{3}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\underline{z}}
\textstyle{U_{1}[1]}

be a triangle in
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
with
U_{2}
and
U_{3}
in
\mathcal{\underline{U}}
. Then there exists a standard triangle
\textstyle{U_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\underline{x^{{}^{\prime}}}}
\textstyle{U_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\underline{y^{{}^{\prime}}}}
\textstyle{U_{3}^{{}^{\prime}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\underline{z^{{}^{\prime}}}}
\textstyle{U_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}[1]}

which is isomorphic to the above triangle. Therefore
U_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}\oplus P_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}\cong U_{i}\oplus P_{i}
by Lemma
4.8
, where
P_{i}
and
P_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}
are in
Proj(\mathcal{C}),\ i=1,2,3
.
Then
U_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}
and
U_{3}^{{}^{\prime}}

are in
\mathcal{U}
, it follows that
U_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}
is in
\mathcal{U}
as
\mathcal{U}
is resolving. So
U_{1}\in\mathcal{U}
. It follows that
\mathcal{\underline{U}}
is closed under cocones. Note that
\mathcal{\underline{U}}
is closed under extensions and cocones if and only if
\mathcal{\underline{U}}
is closed under extensions and
[-1]
. It follows that
\mathcal{\underline{U}}
is closed under
[-1]
. Hence

(\mathcal{\underline{U}},\mathcal{\underline{V}})
is a co-t-structure on
\underline{\mathcal{C}}.

“
\Leftarrow
”. Let
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{U_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{U_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
be
an
\mathbb{E}
-triangle with
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
in
\mathcal{U}
.
Then

\textstyle{U_{2}[-1]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{U_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{U_{2}}

is a distinguished triangle in
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
.
Since
(\mathcal{\underline{U}},\mathcal{\underline{V}})
is a co-t-structure on
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
, one has
U_{2}[-1]\in\underline{\mathcal{U}}
. Thus
X\in\mathcal{\underline{U}}
,
and hence there exists
Y\in\mathcal{U}
such that
X\oplus P\cong Y\oplus Q
, where
P
and
Q
are in
Proj(\mathcal{C})
. It follows that
X\in\mathcal{U}
. So
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
is a hereditary cotorsion pair on
\mathcal{C}
by Lemmas
4.5
and
4.12
.
∎

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Frobenius extriangulated category. Suppose that
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
are two subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
such that
Proj(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\mathcal{U}\bigcap\mathcal{V}
. Then
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
is a hereditary cotorsion pair on
\mathcal{C}
with
\widehat{\mathcal{U}}=\widecheck{\mathcal{V}}=\mathcal{C}
if and only if
(\mathcal{\underline{U}},\mathcal{\underline{V}})
is a bounded co-t-structure on
\underline{\mathcal{C}}.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.13
, one has that
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
is a hereditary cotorsion pair on
\mathcal{C}
if and only if
(\mathcal{\underline{U}},\mathcal{\underline{V}})
is a co-t-structure on
\underline{\mathcal{C}}.

On the one hand, if
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
is a hereditary cotorsion pair on
\mathcal{C}
with
\widehat{\mathcal{U}}=\widecheck{\mathcal{V}}=\mathcal{C}
, then
(\mathcal{\underline{U}},\mathcal{\underline{V}})
is a co-t-structure on
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
with

\widehat{(\underline{\mathcal{U}})}=\widecheck{(\underline{\mathcal{V}})}=\underline{\mathcal{C}}
. Hence
(\mathcal{\underline{U}},\mathcal{\underline{V}})
is a bounded co-t-structure on
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
by
[
23
, Remark 4.2]
.
On the other hand, if
(\mathcal{\underline{U}},\mathcal{\underline{V}})
is a bounded co-t-structure on
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
, then
\widehat{(\underline{\mathcal{U}})}=\widecheck{(\underline{\mathcal{V}})}=\underline{\mathcal{C}}
by
[
23
, Remark 4.2]
. We claim that
\widehat{\mathcal{U}}=\widecheck{\mathcal{V}}=\mathcal{C}
.

Take
C\in\mathcal{C}
. We proceed by induction on
n=\mathrm{coresdim}_{\underline{\mathcal{V}}}(C)
. If
n=0
, then
C\in\mathcal{V}
by Lemma
4.8
. Suppose
n>0
. There is a triangle

\textstyle{C\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\underline{a}}
\textstyle{V\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\underline{b}}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\underline{c}}
\textstyle{C[1]}

with
V\in\mathcal{V}
and
\mathrm{coresdim}_{\underline{\mathcal{V}}}(X)=n-1
in
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
.
Therefore, we have a standard triangle

\textstyle{C^{{}^{\prime}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\underline{a}^{{}^{\prime}}}
\textstyle{V^{{}^{\prime}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\underline{b}^{{}^{\prime}}}
\textstyle{X^{{}^{\prime}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\underline{c}^{{}^{\prime}}}
\textstyle{C^{{}^{\prime}}[1]}

which is isomorphic to the above triangle.
By the induction hypothesis,
X\in\widecheck{\mathcal{V}}
. Since there is an isomorphism
X\oplus P\cong X^{{}^{\prime}}\oplus P^{{}^{\prime}}
with
P,P^{{}^{\prime}}\in Proj(\mathcal{C})
by Lemma
4.8
,
X^{{}^{\prime}}
is in
\widecheck{\mathcal{V}}
by the proof that
\widecheck{\mathcal{V}}
is closed under direct summands in the proof of Proposition
4.3
. Hence
C^{{}^{\prime}}
is in
\widecheck{\mathcal{V}}
. Similarly, we have
C\in\widecheck{\mathcal{V}}
. So
\widecheck{\mathcal{V}}=\mathcal{C}
. Dually, we can show that
\widehat{\mathcal{U}}=\mathcal{C}
. This completes the proof.
∎

In the following, given a subcategory
\mathcal{M}
of
\mathcal{C}
with
Proj(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\mathcal{M}
, the stable category of
\mathcal{M}
respect to the subcategory
Proj(\mathcal{C})
will be denoted by
\underline{\mathcal{M}}
. Note that
\underline{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq\underline{\mathcal{C}}
, and we let

\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{\bot}=\{N\in\underline{\mathcal{C}}\mid\underline{\mathcal{C}}(\underline{\mathcal{M}},N[i])=0\ \text{for all~}i>0\}

and

{}^{\bot}\underline{\mathcal{M}}=\{N\in\underline{\mathcal{C}}\mid\underline{\mathcal{C}}(N,\underline{\mathcal{M}}[i])=0\ \text{for all~}i>0\}.

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
1.1
from the introduction.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Assume that
\mathcal{H}
is a subcategory satisfying the condition in
(2)
. Then one has
Proj(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\mathcal{H}\bigcap{{}^{\bot}\mathcal{H}}
. It follows from Theorem
4.7
that
(^{\perp}\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})
is a hereditary cotorsion pair on
\mathcal{C}
with
\widehat{(^{\perp}\mathcal{H})}=\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}=\mathcal{C}
. Since
\underline{\mathcal{H}}\bigcap\underline{{}^{\bot}\mathcal{H}}=\underline{\mathcal{H}\bigcap{{}^{\bot}\mathcal{H}}}
by Lemma
4.12
, we get that
\underline{\mathcal{H}\bigcap{{}^{\bot}\mathcal{H}}}
is a silting subcategory of
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
with
Proj(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\mathcal{H}\bigcap{{}^{\bot}\mathcal{H}}
by Proposition
4.14
and Theorem
4.11
. So
\Phi
is well defined.

Let
\underline{\mathcal{M}}
be a silting subcategory of
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
with
Proj(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\mathcal{M}
.
Then
(^{\bot}(\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{\bot}),\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{\bot})
is a bounded co-t-structure on
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
by Theorem
4.11
.
We claim that
(^{\bot}(\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{\bot}),\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{\bot})=(\underline{{}^{\bot}(\mathcal{M}^{\bot})},\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}).

Indeed, by Lemma
4.9
, one has
\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{\bot}\subseteq\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}
.
If
X\in\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}
, then there exists an object
Y
in
\mathcal{M}^{\bot}
with
X\oplus P\cong Y\oplus Q
by Lemma
4.8
, where
P
and
Q
are in
Proj(\mathcal{C})
.
Hence

\underline{\mathcal{C}}(\underline{\mathcal{M}},X[i])=\underline{\mathcal{C}}(\underline{\mathcal{M}},(X\oplus P)[i])=\underline{\mathcal{C}}(\underline{\mathcal{M}},(Y\oplus Q)[i])=\mathbb{E}^{i}(\mathcal{M},Y\oplus Q)=0,

where the third equality is due to Lemma
4.9
. Thus
X\in{\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{\bot}}
.
Next, we show that
{}^{\bot}(\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{\bot})=\underline{{}^{\bot}(\mathcal{M}^{\bot})}
.
For
C\in\underline{{}^{\bot}(\mathcal{M}^{\bot})}
, there exists an object
Y
in
{}^{\bot}(\mathcal{M}^{\bot})
such that
C\oplus P_{1}\cong Y\oplus Q_{1}
by Lemma
4.8
, where
P_{1}
and
Q_{1}
are in
Proj(\mathcal{C})
.
Hence one has

\underline{\mathcal{C}}(C,\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{\bot}[i])=\underline{\mathcal{C}}(C,\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}[i])=\underline{\mathcal{C}}(C\oplus P_{1},\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}[i])=\underline{\mathcal{C}}(Y\oplus Q_{1},\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}[i])=\mathbb{E}^{i}(Y\oplus Q_{1},\mathcal{M}^{\bot})=0

for all
i\geq 1
.
Thus
\underline{{}^{\bot}(\mathcal{M}^{\bot})}\subseteq{{}^{\bot}(\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{\bot})}
.
If
Y\in{{}^{\bot}(\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{\bot})}
, one has

\mathbb{E}^{i}(Y,\mathcal{M}^{\bot})=\underline{\mathcal{C}}(Y,\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}[i])=\underline{\mathcal{C}}(Y,\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{\bot}[i])=0.

Hence
Y\in\underline{{}^{\bot}(\mathcal{M}^{\bot})}
.
So
{}^{\bot}(\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{\bot})=\underline{{}^{\bot}(\mathcal{M}^{\bot})}
.
So
{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}
satisfies the condition of
(2)
by Theorem
4.7
and Proposition
4.14
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}
be two subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
with
Proj(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{1}\bigcap\mathcal{M}_{2}
.
Assume that
\underline{\mathcal{M}_{1}}=\underline{\mathcal{M}_{2}}
.
Indeed, for any
X\in\mathcal{M}_{1}
, we have
X\oplus P\cong Y\oplus Q
by Lemma
4.6
, where
Y
is in
\mathcal{M}_{2}
,
P
and
Q
are in
Proj(\mathcal{C})
. Thus
X^{\perp}=Y^{\perp}
in
\mathcal{C}
, and hence
{\mathcal{M}_{1}}^{\perp}={\mathcal{M}_{2}}^{\perp}
in
\mathcal{C}
.
So
\Psi
is well defined by the above argument.

Let
\underline{\mathcal{M}}
be a silting subcategory of
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
with
Proj(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\mathcal{M}
. Then
\Phi\Psi(\underline{\mathcal{M}})=\Phi(\mathcal{M}^{\bot})=\underline{{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}\bigcap{{}^{\bot}(\mathcal{M}^{\bot})}}
. By the above argument,

(^{\bot}(\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{\bot}),\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{\bot})=(\underline{{}^{\bot}(\mathcal{M}^{\bot})},\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}})
is a bounded co-t-structure.
Hence, one has

\underline{{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}\bigcap{{}^{\bot}(\mathcal{M}^{\bot})}}=\underline{{\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}}\bigcap\underline{{{}^{\bot}(\mathcal{M}^{\bot})}}=\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{\bot}\bigcap(^{\bot}(\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{\bot})=\underline{\mathcal{M}},

where the first equality is due to Lemma
4.12
, and the third equality is due to Theorem
4.11
. Thus
\Phi\Psi
is an identity. Assume that
\mathcal{H}
is a subcategory satisfying the condition in
(2)
. Then
\Psi\Phi(\mathcal{H})=\Psi(\underline{\mathcal{H}\bigcap{{}^{\bot}\mathcal{H}}})={(\mathcal{H}\bigcap{{}^{\bot}{\mathcal{H}})^{\bot}}}
.
Note that

by Theorem

. It is easy to check that

. Thus
{(\mathcal{H}\bigcap{{}^{\bot}\mathcal{H}})^{\bot}}={\mathcal{M}^{\bot}}={(\widecheck{\mathcal{M}})^{\bot}}=\mathcal{H}
, and hence

is an identity.

Curvature of the Gauss map for normally flat submanifolds in space forms
Javier Álvarez-Vizoso

Abstract
For a submanifold with flat normal bundle in a space form there is a normal orthonormal basis that simultaneously diagonalizes the corresponding Weingarten operators, and at which these operators satisfy a simple Codazzi symmetry. When the second fundamental form has zero index of relative nullity, the image of the Gauss map as defined by Obata is a submanifold of a generalized Grassmannian manifold, with induced metric given by the third fundamental form, corresponding to the sum of the squares of these operators. In this case, we show that the Riemann curvature tensor of the Gauss image is completely determined by the curvature and Weingarten operators of the original submanifold, in a form analogous to a theorema egregium that relates the intrinsic geometry of the Gauss map with the extrinsic geometry of the original embedding. For this, we derive the difference vector of the induced Levi-Civita connections and express the Riemann curvature tensor with a generalized Kulkarni-Nomizu product.

Introduction

Classically, the first fundamental form of a submanifold of Euclidean space is defined to be the restriction of the Euclidean scalar product to the submanifold tangent space. The second fundamental form of a hypersurface in turn is traditionally defined as the symmetric bilinear form that measures the tangent variation of the normal vector to the hypersurface when moving along tangent vectors. The corresponding Weingarten operator, or shape operator, is the linear map associated to this bilinear form with respect to the metric. The bilinear form defined by the square of this operator is the classical third fundamental form.

Considering the second fundamental form of a submanifold as a (pseudo-)Riemannian metric, there has been a series of studies,

2.1
Submanifolds with flat normal bundle

We shall assume standard notation on covariant derivatives, curvature tensors and the fundamental equations by Gauss, Codazzi-Mainardi and Ricci for submanifolds isometrically embedded in space forms. References for these results can be found, e.g., in
[
12
,
13
]
and
[

]

We consdier our space forms to be the simply connected
(n+m)
-dimensional manifolds
\overline{M}(k)^{n+m}
of constant sectional curvature such that
\overline{M}
is: the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
for
k=0
, the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n+m}
of radius
1/\sqrt{k}
for
k>0
, and the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+m}
of curvature
k<0

Let us review some of the essential properties of submanifolds which have flat normal bundle.

Definition 1
.

An isometric immersion
x:\mathcal{M}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{M}(k)^{n+m}
, of an
n
-dimensional manifold
\mathcal{M}
into an
(n+m)
-dimensional space form of constant sectional curvature

, is said to be normally flat, or to have flat normal connection, if the curvature tensor of the normal connection,
\nabla^{\perp}_{X}\xi=(\overline{\nabla}_{X}\xi)^{\perp}
, is zero for every
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M}),\,\xi\in\Gamma(N\mathcal{M})
, i.e.,

R^{\perp}(X,Y)\xi=\nabla_{X}^{\perp}\nabla_{Y}^{\perp}\xi-\nabla_{Y}^{\perp}\nabla_{X}^{\perp}\xi-\nabla^{\perp}_{[X,Y]}\xi=0.

Proposition 1
.

The normal connection
\nabla^{\perp}
of
x:\mathcal{M}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{M}(k)^{n+m}
is flat if and only if at every point
p\in\mathcal{M}
there is an orthonormal basis
\{\xi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
of
N_{p}\mathcal{M}
that is parallel in the normal bundle, i.e.,

\nabla_{X}^{\perp}\xi_{j}=0,\quad\forall X\in T_{p}\mathcal{M},\quad j=1,\dots,m.

Proof.

See
[
5
, Ch. 4, Prop. 1.1]
.
∎

Proposition 2
.

There is a normal orthonormal basis
\{\xi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
of
T_{p}\mathcal{M}
, such that the Weingarten operators
\{A_{\xi_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{m}
are simultaneously diagonalizable, if and only if the isometric immersion
x:\mathcal{M}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{M}(k)^{n+m}
has flat normal bundle.

Proof.

This immediately follows from the Ricci equation.
∎

From the simultaneous diagonalization of the shape operators, the tangent space decomposes orthogonally at every
p\in\mathcal{M}
as
T_{p}\mathcal{M}=E_{1}(p)\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{s}(p)
, for some
1\leq s(p)\leq n
, such that for every normal vector
\xi\in N_{p}\mathcal{M}
there are real numbers
\lambda_{i}(\xi),1\leq i\leq s
, called
principal curvatures
, such that

A_{\xi}|_{E_{i}(p)}=\lambda_{i}(\xi)\Id

(1)

and the maps
\xi\mapsto\lambda_{i}(\xi)
are pairwise distinct. These maps are linear so there exist unique pairwise distinct normal vectors
\eta_{i}(x)\in N_{x}\mathcal{M}
, the
principal normal
vectors, satisfying

\lambda_{i}(\xi)=\langle\,\eta_{i}(p),\xi\,\rangle,\quad 1\leq i\leq s.

(2)

This makes the Weingarten operators take the simple expression

A_{\xi}X=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\langle\,\xi,\eta_{i}(p)\,\rangle\proj_{i}X,\quad\forall X\in T_{p}\mathcal{M},\;\forall\xi\in N_{p}\mathcal{M}.

(3)

Remark 1
.

The canonical connection on
Hom^{2}(TM,NM;TM)
yields a covariant derivative for the shape operators

(\nabla_{X}A_{\xi})X=\nabla_{X}(A_{\xi}X)-A_{\xi}\nabla_{X}Y-A_{\nabla^{\perp}_{X}\xi}Y,

that for normally flat submanifolds, and
\xi
one of its parallel normal vectors, leads us to use the following result and notation for the rest of this paper

(\nabla_{X}A_{\xi})X=\nabla_{X}(A_{\xi}X)-A_{\xi}\nabla_{X}Y,

(4)

i.e., we can work with this expression, for expanding or collecting parentheses, as if a product rule is taking place.

Finally, all along the proofs of the paper we shall make extensive use of the Codazzi symmetries of this covariant derivative, which for our purpose are enough to be true when
\xi
is a parallel normal vector.

Proposition 3

(Codazzi symmetries)
.

The Weingarten operators of a normally flat submanifold in a space form satisfy:

\langle\,(\nabla_{X}A_{\xi})Y,\,Z\rangle=\langle\,(\nabla_{Y}A_{\xi})X,\,Z\rangle=\langle\,(\nabla_{X}A_{\xi})Z,\,Y\rangle,

(5)

for all
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and
\xi
a parallel vector in the normal connection.

Proof.

The first equality is the traditional Codazzi symmetry satisfied by submanifolds of a manifold of constant sectional curvature, see
[
7
, Eq. 1.16]
. The second equality is the self-adjointness of the shape operator covariant derivative, which follows straightforwardly from
A
being self-adjoint (e.g., see also Lemma
1
) below.
∎

2.2
The Gauss-Obata map and the third fundamental form

Definition 2
.

Let
x:\mathcal{M}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{n+m}
be a submanfiold of a Riemannian manifold, and let
\{A_{\xi_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{m}
be the Weingarten operators at an orthonormal basis
\{\xi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
of the normal bundle
N\mathcal{M}
. The
Obata operator
is defined to be

W=\sum_{j=1}^{m}A^{2}_{\xi_{j}}:T\mathcal{M}\rightarrow T\mathcal{M}.

(6)

Lemma 1
.

The Obata operator satisfies the following properties for any
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
:

1.

It is self-adjoint with respect to the first fundamental form:

\langle\,WX,Y\,\rangle=\langle\,X,WY\,\rangle.

(7)

2.

Its covariant derivative is self-adjoint:

\langle\,(\nabla_{X}W)Y,Z\,\rangle=\langle\,(\nabla_{X}W)Z,\,Y\rangle.

(8)

3.

Its component with respect to the first fundamental form are independent of the normal orthornomal basis chosen for the shape operators, i.e.:

\langle\,WX,Y\,\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\langle\,A_{\xi_{j}}X,\,A_{\xi_{j}}Y\,\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\langle\,A_{n_{j}}X,\,A_{n_{j}}Y\,\rangle

(9)

for any other orthonormal basis
\{n_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
of
N\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

The properties follow straightforwardly by direct computation. For example (ii) follows from
XI\!I\!I(Y,Z)=XI\!I\!I(Z,Y)
, since then

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{X}(WY),Z\rangle+\langle WY,\nabla_{X}Z\rangle=\langle\nabla_{X}(WZ),Y\rangle+\langle WZ,\nabla_{X}Y\rangle

which by expanding the derivative of the product gives

\displaystyle\langle(\nabla_{X}W)Y,Z\rangle+\langle W\nabla_{X}Y,Z\rangle+\langle WY,\nabla_{X}Z\rangle=\langle(\nabla_{X}W)Z,Y\rangle+\langle W\nabla_{X}Z,Y\rangle+\langle WZ,\nabla_{X}Y\rangle

and most of the terms cancel by self-adjointess.
∎

In view of properties
(i)
and
(iii)
the following is well-defined.

Definition 3
.

The
third fundamental form

I\!I\!I
of a Riemannian submanifold
x:\mathcal{M}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{n+m}
is the symmetric bilinear form associated to the Obata operator with respect to the induced metric:

I\!I\!I(X,Y)=\langle\,WX,Y\,\rangle,

(10)

for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
.

Theorem 2
.

For a submanifold with flat normal bundle in a space form,

1.

The difference of the Levi-Civita connections in

(14)

(Theorema Egregium)
The difference between the Riemann-Christoffel curvature tensor of the Gauss-Obata image

\displaystyle I\!I\!I(R^{(I\!I\!I)}(X,Y)Z-

\displaystyle R(X,Y)Z,V)

\displaystyle=I\!I\!I(P(X,Y)Z,V)+I\!I\!I(T(X,Z),\,T(Y,V))-I\!I\!I(T(X,V),\,T(Y,Z)),

and

is the right hand side of (

3.

The scalar curvature of

\mathcal{R}^{(I\!I\!I)}=k(n-1)\frac{\sigma_{n-1}(W)}{\det(W)}+\tr_{I\!I\!I}(A_{H})-n+||T||^{2}_{I\!I\!I}-||\tr_{I\!I\!I}T||^{2}_{I\!I\!I}+\tr_{I\!I\!I}\tr_{c}P.

is the mean curvature vector of

The curvature tensor of the Gauss map of a normally flat submanifold in a space form with null extrinsic zero index, admits a decomposition in terms of the original curvature, the curvature of the shape operators, and generalized Kulkarni-Nomizu products determined by these operators:

\boxed{R^{(I\!I\!I)}=R+L+\widehat{T\mathbin{\bigcirc\mspace{-15mu}\wedge\mspace{3mu}}_{I\!I\!I}T}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\widehat{J_{j}\mathbin{\bigcirc\mspace{-15mu}\wedge\mspace{3mu}}_{I\!I\!I}J_{j}}}

.

Formulas

, our results in terms of products of

so that the analogy to the decomposition of equation

Remark 4

Notice the following interpretation of each term: the vector-valued bilinear form

3.1
Connection difference in terms of the Weingarten operators

Definition 4
.

Let us denote by
T
the vector-valued symmetric bilinear form
T\in S^{2}(T^{*}\mathcal{M})\otimes T\mathcal{M}
measuring the difference between the Levi-Civita connections induced by the third and first fundamental forms:

T(X,Y)=\nabla^{(I\!I\!I)}_{X}Y-\nabla_{X}Y.

(19)

Proof of Theorem
2
(i)

Let us recall that both
\nabla
and
\nabla^{(I\!I\!I)}
are torsion-free so that

[X,Y]=\nabla^{(I\!I\!I)}_{X}Y-\nabla^{(I\!I\!I)}_{Y}X=\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla_{Y}X,

(20)

which implies that
T
is symmetric,

. Moreover, the connections are metric compatible, thus

\displaystyle X(I\!I\!I(Y,Z))=I\!I\!I(\nabla^{(I\!I\!I)}_{X}Y,Z)+I\!I\!I(\nabla^{(I\!I\!I)}_{X}Y,Z),\text{ and}

(21)

\displaystyle X\langle Y,Z\rangle=\langle\nabla_{X}Y,Z\rangle+\langle\nabla_{X}Y,Z\rangle.

(22)

Now, Koszul’s formula can be used to define the unique connection satisfying the previous properties, hence

\displaystyle 2I\!I\!I(\nabla^{(I\!I\!I)}_{X}Y,Z)

\displaystyle=XI\!I\!I(Y,Z)+YI\!I\!I(X,Z)-ZI\!I\!I(X,Y)-I\!I\!I([Y,X],Z)-I\!I\!I([X,Z],Y)-I\!I\!I([Y,Z],X),

which expanding the metric in terms of the Obata operator (for ease of notation, let us write
A_{j}=A_{\xi_{j}}

\displaystyle 2\sum_{j=1}^{m}\langle A_{j}^{2}(\nabla^{(I\!I\!I)}_{X}Y),Z\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(X\langle A^{2}_{j}Y,Z\rangle+Y\langle A^{2}_{j}X,Z\rangle-Z\langle A^{2}_{j}X,Y\rangle\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\left.-\langle A^{2}_{j}[Y,X],Z\rangle-\langle A^{2}_{j}[X,Z],Y\rangle-\langle A^{2}_{j}[Y,Z],X\rangle\right).

By expanding the the first three terms of the right hand side with eq.

\displaystyle 2\sum_{j=1}^{m}\langle A_{j}^{2}(\nabla^{(I\!I\!I)}_{X}Y),Z\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(\langle\nabla_{X}(A_{j}^{2}Y),Z\rangle+\langle\nabla_{Y}(A_{j}^{2}X),Z\rangle-\langle\nabla_{Z}(A_{j}^{2}X),Y\rangle\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\left.-\langle\nabla_{Y}X-\nabla_{X}Y,A^{2}_{j}Z\rangle+\langle\nabla_{Z}X,A_{j}^{2}Y\rangle\right),

\displaystyle 2I\!I\!I(\nabla^{(I\!I\!I)}_{X}Y,Z)

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{X}(WY),Z\rangle+\langle\nabla_{Y}(WX),Z\rangle-\langle\nabla_{Z}(WX),Y\rangle-\langle W(\nabla_{Y}X-\nabla_{X}Y),Z\rangle+\langle W\nabla_{Z}X,Y\rangle.

Expanding the first three terms of the right hand side using

\displaystyle 2I\!I\!I(\nabla^{(I\!I\!I)}_{X}Y,Z)

\displaystyle=2\langle W(\nabla_{X}Y),Z\rangle+\langle(\nabla_{X}W)Y,Z\rangle-\langle(\nabla_{Z}W)X,Y\rangle+\langle(\nabla_{Y}W)X,Z\rangle,

that is to say, by definition of

2\langle WT(X,Y),Z\rangle=\;\langle(\nabla_{X}W)Y,Z\rangle-\langle(\nabla_{Z}W)X,Y\rangle+\langle(\nabla_{Y}W)X,Z\rangle.

(23)

But, employing equation

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{Z}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}A_{j}^{2}\right)X,Y\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\langle\;\left((\nabla_{Z}A_{j})A_{j}+A_{j}(\nabla_{Z}A_{j})\right)X,Y\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\langle\;(\nabla_{Z}A_{j})Y,A_{j}X\rangle+\langle\;(\nabla_{X}A_{j})Z,A_{j}Y\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\langle\;(\nabla_{Y}A_{j})(A_{j}X),Z\rangle+\langle\;(\nabla_{X}A_{j})(A_{j}Y),Z\rangle.

Then, since substituting in equation

\langle\cdot,Z\rangle
, for all

\displaystyle 2WT(X,Y)

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{X}W)Y+(\nabla_{Y}W)X-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left[\;(\nabla_{Y}A_{j})(A_{j}X)+(\nabla_{X}A_{j})(A_{j}Y)\;\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left[\;A_{j}(\nabla_{X}A_{j})Y+A_{j}(\nabla_{Y}A_{j})X\;\right]

from the covariant derivative of

3.2

We shall express the covariant derivative of Weingarten operators at a normal vector

Lemma 2

The covariant derivatives of the Weingarten operators

Proof.

From equations

4
Theorema Egregium for the Gauss-Obata map

In
[
24
, Prop. 3.3]
a formula for
I\!I\!I(R^{(I\!I\!I)}(X,Y)Y,X)
is obtained for submanifolds of Euclidean space. In this section we compute the full Riemann-Christoffel curvature tensor
I\!I\!I(R^{(I\!I\!I)}(X,Y)Z,V)
for normally flat submanifolds in space forms, in order to compare the curvature of the two induced metrics on
\mathcal{M}
, one coming from the isometric embedding in the space form and the other from the Gauss-Obata map into a generalized Grassmannian manifold. We then show how a generalized Kulkarni-Nomizu product encapsulates the operations involved both in our case and in the classical Gauss equation.

4.1
Curvature in terms of the Weingarten operators

Lemma 3
.

The Riemann curvature tensor of
(f(\mathcal{M}),I\!I\!I)

By definition of the curvature tensor in terms of second order covariant derivatives, and using
\nabla^{(I\!I\!I)}_{X}=\nabla_{X}+T(X,\cdot)

\displaystyle R^{(I\!I\!I)}(X,Y)Z

\displaystyle=\nabla^{(I\!I\!I)}_{X}\nabla^{(I\!I\!I)}_{Y}Z-\nabla^{(I\!I\!I)}_{Y}\nabla^{(I\!I\!I)}_{X}Z-\nabla^{(I\!I\!I)}_{[X,Y]}Z

\displaystyle=\nabla^{(I\!I\!I)}_{X}(\nabla_{Y}Z+T(Y,Z))-\nabla^{(I\!I\!I)}_{Y}(\nabla_{X}Z+T(X,Z))-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z-T([X,Y],Z)

20

\displaystyle R^{(I\!I\!I)}(X,Y)Z-R(X,Y)Z=

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}(T(Y,Z))+T(X,T(Y,Z))-\nabla_{Y}(T(X,Z))-T(Y,T(X,Z))

\displaystyle\quad-T(Y,\nabla_{X}Z)+T(X,\nabla_{Y}Z)-T(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)+T(\nabla_{Y}X,Z),

which yields

(\nabla_{X}T)(Y,Z)=\nabla_{X}(T(Y,Z))-T(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)-T(Y,\nabla_{X}Z)

∎

The previous lemma realizes the curvature tensor of the Gauss image in terms of

shows that, for hypersurfaces with the second fundamental form as metric, the terms in

have zero

4.2
Generalized Kulkarni-Nomizu product

The usual Kulkarni-Nomizu product is a particularization of the product in the graded algebra
\bigoplus^{n}_{i=1}S^{2}(\Omega^{i}\mathcal{M})
given on simple elements by

(\alpha\cdot\beta)\mathbin{\bigcirc\mspace{-15mu}\wedge\mspace{3mu}}(\gamma\cdot\delta)=(\alpha\wedge\gamma)\odot(\beta\wedge\delta),

where
\odot
is the symmetric product. For
h
and
k
symmetric
(0,2)
-tensors, and tangent vectors
X_{1},X_{2},X_{3},X_{4}
, the Kulkarni-Nomizu product is:

(h\mathbin{\bigcirc\mspace{-15mu}\wedge\mspace{3mu}}k)(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3},X_{4})=\frac{1}{2}\left|\begin{array}[]{ll}h(X_{1},X_{3})&h(X_{1},X_{4})\\
k(X_{2},X_{3})&k(X_{2},X_{4})\end{array}\right|+\frac{1}{2}\left|\begin{array}[]{ll}k(X_{1},X_{3})&k(X_{1},X_{4})\\
h(X_{2},X_{3})&h(X_{2},X_{4})\end{array}\right|,

(38)

sometimes the normalization by
1/2
is not included. The Kulkarni-Nomizu product is of interest since it plays a role in the tensor decomposition of algebraic curvature tensors, see
[
4
]
.

The Gauss equation of a submanifold
\mathcal{M}
in some Riemannian manifold
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
relates the intrinsic Riemann curvature tensor of
\mathcal{M}
to that of the ambient manifold via a combination of products of the second fundamental form, a purely extrinsic object dependent on the embedding:

\langle\overline{R}(X,Y)Z,V\rangle=\langle R(X,Y)Z,V\rangle+\langle I\!I(X,Z),I\!I(Y,V)\rangle-\langle I\!I(X,V),I\!I(Y,Z)\rangle.

(39)

In the classical case of surfaces in space, this equation results in the expression of the scalar curvature appearing in the
theorema egregium
:

K=\frac{LN-M^{2}}{eg-f^{2}},

where
L,M,N
are the independent components of the second fundamental form and
e,f,g
those of the first fundamental form.
The combination of operations in
39
appears as well in our main formula
2
, and resembles the expression of
38
for
h=k
. If we extend the definition to bilinear forms valued in an inner product space
(E,g)
, so that the products of
38
are understood as scalar
g
-products of the vector-valued forms
h,k
, then the operations carry over and generalize.

Definition 5
.

The generalized Kulkarni-Nomizu product
\mathbin{\bigcirc\mspace{-15mu}\wedge\mspace{3mu}}_{g}
of two vector-valued symmetric bilinear forms
h,k\in Sym^{2}(T_{p}\mathcal{M}^{*})\otimes E
, where
(E,g)
is a finite-dimensional vector space with inner product
g
, is defined to be

\displaystyle(h\mathbin{\bigcirc\mspace{-15mu}\wedge\mspace{3mu}}_{g}k)(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3},X_{4})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[g(h(X_{1},X_{3}),k(X_{2},X_{4}))-g(h(X_{1},X_{4}),k(X_{2},X_{3}))\right.

(40)

\displaystyle+\left.g(h(X_{2},X_{4}),k(X_{1},X_{3}))-g(h(X_{2},X_{3}),k(X_{1},X_{4}))\right],

for any
X_{1},X_{2},X_{3},X_{4}\in T_{p}\mathcal{M}
.

Using this expression, the usual Kulkarni-Nomizu product is
\mathbin{\bigcirc\mspace{-15mu}\wedge\mspace{3mu}}_{g}
when
(E,g)
is the real field with the usual multiplication. Now, equation

\langle\overline{R}(X,Y)Z,V\rangle=\langle R(X,Y)Z,V\rangle+(I\!I\mathbin{\bigcirc\mspace{-15mu}\wedge\mspace{3mu}}_{I}I\!I)(X,Y,Z,V).

where the inner product space in this case is the normal space to the manifold with the scalar product induced from the ambient Riemannian manifold, (which we have also called

by abuse of notation to stress its relationship with the first fundamental form when restricted to the tangent space instead).

Proof of Corollary

In equation

and thus
\{V_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{n}

Now, by definition of Ricci contraction

5
Concluding remarks

In the present work we have established formulas for the intrinsic geometry of the Gauss map via the third fundamental form as generalized by Obata, for a submanifold with flat normal bundle in a space form satisfying general regularity conditions. We have found a relationship analogous to a
theorema egregium
that yields the difference of the intrinsic Riemann-Christoffel curvature tensors of the Gauss image and the original submanifold in terms of tensors solely constructed from the extrinsic Weingarten operators of the original submanifold. For this, the explicit dependency of the difference of the induced covariant derivatives on the shape operators was obtained, along with the introduction of a generalized Kulkarni-Nomizu product. The scalar curvature of the Gauss map in terms of extrinsic scalars was established as a consequence.
Once the geometry of the Gauss map has been established, applications to the study of normally flat submanifolds may be possible, along with the investigation of extending the results to other types of submanifolds. For example, a natural line of research would be the study of the relationship between the mean curvature of the Gauss image, as defined by normal deformations of the Gauss map, and the third fundamental form scalar curvature, in order to establish characterizations similar to the case of the classical ovaloids and extrinsic spheres.

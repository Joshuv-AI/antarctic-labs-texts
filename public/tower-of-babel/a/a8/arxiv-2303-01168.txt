Spectrum of multiplicative functions over powerful numbers
Tsz Ho Chan

Abstract
Roughly speaking, the spectrum of multiplicative functions is the set of all possible mean values. In this paper, we are interested in the spectra of multiplicative functions supported over powerful numbers. We prove that its real logarithmic spectrum takes values from − 2 / ( 4 + 2 ) = − 0.26160 … -\sqrt{2}/(4+\sqrt{2})=-0.26160... to 1 1 while it is known that the logarithmic spectrum of real multiplicative functions over all natural numbers takes values from 0 0 to 1 1 . In the course of this study, we correct a proof of Granville and Soundararajan concerning contribution of small primes in the study of mean value of multiplicative functions.



1
Introduction and Main Results

A function
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is
multiplicative
if
f(ab)=f(a)f(b)
for all relatively prime integers
a
and
b
. A function
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is
completely multiplicative
if
f(ab)=f(a)f(b)
for all integers
a
and
b
. Let
S
be a subset of the unit disc
\mathbb{U}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|\leq 1\}
. Let
\mathcal{F}(S)
and
\hat{\mathcal{F}}(S)
be the class of completely multiplicative functions and the class of multiplicative functions with
f(n)\in S
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
respectively. Understanding the average behavior of multiplicative functions is one central theme in number theory. For example, Delange
[
2
]
, Wirsing
[
13
]
and Halász
[
9
]
successively contributed to the study of average value of multiplicative functions.

Theorem 1

(Delange, Wirsing & Halász)
.

Let
f\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{U})
be a multiplicative function. If there exists some real number
\tau
such that the series

\sum_{p}\frac{1-\Re(f(p)p^{-i\tau})}{p}

(1)

converges, then

\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\leq x}f(n)=\frac{x^{i\tau}}{1+i\tau}\prod_{p\leq x}(1-p^{-1})\sum_{\nu=0}^{\infty}f(p^{\nu})p^{-\nu(1+i\tau)}+o(1)

as
x\rightarrow\infty
. If there is no real number
\tau
for which the series (
1
) converges, then

\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\leq x}f(n)=o(1).

One way to prove the divergent case of Theorem
1
is through an effective Halász’ theorem
[
10
]
which was further refined by Montgomery
[
12
]
. Recently, Granville, Harper and Soundararajan gave more intuitive and versatile arguments for Halász’ theorem in
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
together with some interesting applications.

People are also interested in the set of average values taken by (completely) multiplicative functions. Define

\Gamma_{N}(S):=\Bigl\{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n\leq N}f(n):f\in\mathcal{F}(S)\Bigr\},\;\;\hat{\Gamma}_{N}(S):=\Bigl\{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n\leq N}f(n):f\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}(S)\Bigr\},

\Gamma(S)=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\Gamma_{N}(S),\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\hat{\Gamma}(S)=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\hat{\Gamma}_{N}(S)

where
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}J_{N}=J
for a sequence of subsets
J_{N}\subset\mathbb{U}
means that
z\in J
if and only if there is a sequence of points
z_{N}\in J_{N}
with
z_{N}\rightarrow z
as
N\rightarrow\infty
. Here
\Gamma(S)
is called the
spectrum
of the set
S
. Granville and Soundararajan
[
6
]
gave a general study on properties and geometric structures of the spectrum. One of their main results concerns about the spectrum of the interval
[-1,1]
:

\Gamma([-1,1])=[\delta_{1},1]\;\;\text{ where }\;\;\delta_{1}=1-2\log(1+\sqrt{e})+4\int_{1}^{\sqrt{e}}\frac{\log t}{t+1}dt=-0.656999\ldots.

(2)

This means that

\sum_{n\leq x}f(n)\geq(\delta_{1}+o(1))x

for all real-valued completely multiplicative functions with
-1\leq f(n)\leq 1
. The proof of (
2
) is partly based on the following beautiful structure theorem.

Theorem 2

(Granville & Soundararajan)
.

For any closed subset
S
of
\mathbb{U}
with
1\in S
, we have

\Gamma(S)=\Gamma_{\Theta}(S)\times\Lambda(S)\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\hat{\Gamma}(S)=\hat{\Gamma}_{\Theta}(S)\times\Lambda(S)

(3)

where

\Gamma_{\Theta}(S):=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\Bigl\{\prod_{p\leq x}\Bigl(1+\frac{f(p)}{p}+\frac{f(p^{2})}{p^{2}}+\cdots\Bigr)\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p}\Bigr):f\in\mathcal{F}(S)\Bigr\}

and

\hat{\Gamma}_{\Theta}(S):=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\Bigl\{\prod_{p\leq x}\Bigl(1+\frac{f(p)}{p}+\frac{f(p^{2})}{p^{2}}+\cdots\Bigr)\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p}\Bigr):f\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}(S)\Bigr\}

are called the Euler product spectra, and
\Lambda(S)
is the set of values of functions
\sigma:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{U}
that satisfy a certain integral equation (see equation (
12
) or
[
6
]
for more details).

Granville and Soundararajan
[
6
]
also investigated other notions of spectrum such as

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\Bigl\{\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{f(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\Big/\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{1}{n^{\sigma}}:f\in\mathcal{F}(S)(\text{ or }\hat{\mathcal{F}}(S))\Bigr\}

for any fixed
\sigma>0
. These new notions are easier to study than
\Gamma(S)
and
\hat{\Gamma}(S)
with the most interesting case being
\sigma=1
, the
logarithmic
spectrum:

\Gamma_{0}(S):=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\Bigl\{\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{f(n)}{n}\Big/\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{1}{n}:f\in\mathcal{F}(S)\Bigr\}\;\text{ and }\;\hat{\Gamma}_{0}(S):=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\Bigl\{\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{f(n)}{n}\Big/\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{1}{n}:f\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}(S)\Bigr\}.

By
[
6
, Theorem 8.4 & Corollary 4]
, one has the following analogous structure result for logarithmic spectrum.

Theorem 3

(Granville & Soundararajan)
.

For any closed subset
S
of
\mathbb{U}
with
1\in S
,

\Gamma_{0}(S)=\Gamma_{\Theta}(S)\times\Lambda_{0}(S).

Morover, when
S=[-1,1]
,

\Gamma_{0}([-1,1])=[0,1].

(4)

Subsequently, Granville and Soundararajan
[
8
]
obtained more refined description of the lower endpoints for
\Gamma_{0}([-1,1])
and
\hat{\Gamma}_{0}([-1,1])
: For large
x
,

\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{f(n)}{n}\geq-\frac{1}{(\log\log x)^{3/5}}

(5)

for all

f\in\mathcal{F}([-1,1])\;\;\text{ or }\;\;f\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}([-1,1])\text{ satisfying }\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1+f(2^{k})}{2^{k}}\gg(\log x)^{-1/20};

and

\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{f(n)}{n}\geq\delta_{1}\log 2+o(1)=-0.4553...+o(1)\;\;\text{ for all }\;\;f\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}([-1,1])

(6)

where the lower bound can be achieved if and only if

\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1+f(2^{k})}{2^{k}}\Bigr)\log x+\sum_{3\leq p\leq x^{1/(1+\sqrt{e})}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-f(p^{k})}{p^{k}}+\sum_{x^{1/(1+\sqrt{e})}\leq p\leq x}\frac{1+f(p)}{p}=o(1).

In particular, the obvious set inclusion
\Gamma_{0}([-1,1])\subset\hat{\Gamma}_{0}([-1,1])
together with (
4
), (
5
) and (
6
) imply

\hat{\Gamma}_{0}([-1,1])=[0,1]

as
\sum_{n\leq x}1/n=\log x+O(1)
. It is also worth mentioning that inequality (
5
) has recently been improved by Kerr and Klurman
[
11
]
to

\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{f(n)}{n}\geq-\frac{1}{(\log\log x)^{1-\epsilon}}

(7)

for
f\in\mathcal{F}([-1,1])
and any
\epsilon>0
.

In this paper, we are interested in the spectrum of multiplicative functions supported over powerful numbers. A number
n
is
powerful
or
squarefull
if its prime factorization
n=p_{1}^{a_{1}}p_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots p_{r}^{a_{r}}
satisfies
a_{i}\geq 2
for all
1\leq i\leq r
. Similarly, a number
n
is
k
-
full
if the exponents
a_{i}\geq k
for all
1\leq i\leq r
. For example,
72=2^{3}\cdot 3^{2}
is squarefull and
243=3^{5}
is
5
-full. Let
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
be the set of all positive
k
-full numbers and
Q_{k}(x)
be its counting function. It is well known that

Q_{k}(x):=\mathop{\sum_{n\leq x}}_{n\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}}1=\prod_{p}\Bigl(1+\sum_{m=k+1}^{2k-1}\frac{1}{p^{m/k}}\Bigr)x^{1/k}+O(x^{1/(k+1)})

(8)

where the product is over all primes (see
[
3
]
or
[
1
]
for example).

Let
\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{k}(S)
denote the class of multiplicative functions with
f(n)\in S\subset\mathbb{U}
that are supported over
k
-full numbers (i.e.,
f(n)=0
for
n\not\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}
). Define the regular spectrum and
“logarithmic”
spectrum of multiplicative functions over powerful numbers by

\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{Q}_{2}}(S):=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\Bigl\{\sum_{n\leq x}f(n)\;\;\Big/\sum_{n\leq x,n\in\mathcal{Q}_{2}}1:f\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{2}(S)\Bigr\}

and

\hat{\Gamma}_{0,\mathcal{Q}_{2}}(S):=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\Bigl\{\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{f(n)}{n^{1/2}}\;\;\Big/\sum_{n\leq x,n\in\mathcal{Q}_{2}}\frac{1}{n^{1/2}}:f\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{2}(S)\Bigr\}

respectively. The reason for the exponent
1/2
is that
Q_{2}(x)=\prod_{p}(1+\frac{1}{p^{3/2}})\sqrt{x}+O(x^{1/3})
implies

\sum_{n\leq x,n\in\mathcal{Q}_{2}}\frac{1}{n^{1/2}}=\prod_{p}\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{p^{3/2}}\Bigr)\log\sqrt{x}+O(1)

(9)

via partial summation. Also, we do not consider completely multiplicative functions supported over powerful numbers because there is only one such function, namely
f(1)=1
and
f(n)=0
for all
n>1
, as
f(p)=0
for all prime
p
. Now, we are ready to state our main results.

Theorem 4
.

For any closed subset
S
of
\mathbb{U}
with
1\in S
,

\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{Q}_{2}}(S)=\hat{\Gamma}_{\Theta}(S)\times\Lambda(S)\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\hat{\Gamma}_{0,\mathcal{Q}_{2}}(S)=\hat{\Gamma}_{\Theta}(S)\times\Lambda_{0}(S)

where

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}_{\Theta}(S):=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\biggl\{

\displaystyle\prod_{p\leq x}\Bigl(1+\frac{f(p^{3})}{p^{3/2}}+\frac{f(p^{4})-f(p^{2})f(p^{2})}{p^{4/2}}+\frac{f(p^{5})-f(p^{3})f(p^{2})}{p^{5/2}}+\cdots\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{1-\frac{1}{p}}{1-\frac{f(p^{2})}{p}}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{p^{3/2}}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle:f\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{2}(S)\biggr\}

is some kind of modified Euler product spectrum.

Theorem 5
.

When
S=[-1,1]
, we have

\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{Q}_{2}}([-1,1])=[\delta_{1},1]\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\hat{\Gamma}_{0,\mathcal{Q}_{2}}([-1,1])=[\delta_{2},1]=[-0.26120...,1]

where
\delta_{2}=-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4+\sqrt{2}}
.

The conditions on (
5
) and (
6
) as well as the proof of Theorem
5
indicate that logarithmic spectra are greatly influenced by the values of the multiplicative functions on powers of
2
. Hence, we have the following result for multiplicative functions supported over odd powerful numbers which is similar to (
7
) and follows easily from applying the argument of Kerr and Klurman
[
11
]
to (
5
) and the observation (
6
).

Corollary 1
.

Suppose
f\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}([-1,1])
with
f(2^{k})=0
for all integer
k\geq 2
. For any
\epsilon>0
,

\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{f(n)}{\sqrt{n}}\geq-\frac{1}{(\log\log x)^{1-\epsilon}}

for large enough
x
.

One way to interpret Theorem
5
is as follows. The set of powerful numbers
\mathcal{Q}_{2}
is generated by the prime powers

2^{2},\;3^{2},\;5^{2}\;\ldots;\;2^{3},\;3^{3},\;5^{3},\;\ldots;\;2^{4},\;3^{4},\;5^{4},\;\ldots;\;\ldots

multiplicatively. In the “logarithm” spectrum, we take square root of
n
. Hence, we may think of multiplicative functions supported over powerful numbers as functions over the following new multiplicative building blocks

2,\;3,\;5\;\ldots;\;2^{3/2},\;3^{3/2},\;5^{3/2},\;\ldots;\;2^{2},\;3^{2},\;5^{2},\;\ldots;\;\ldots

(10)

in contrast to the usual prime powers building blocks

2,\;3,\;5\;\ldots;\;2^{2},\;3^{2},\;5^{2},\;\ldots;\;2^{3},\;3^{3},\;5^{3},\;\ldots;\;\ldots

for ordinary multiplicative functions. Thus, Theorem
5
roughly says that, by perturbing the set of prime powers to (
10
), the behavior of its logarithmic spectrum changes from
[0,1]
to
[-0.26120...,1]
while its regular spectrum stays unchanged.

The paper is organized as follows. First, we collect some detailed structure results on various spectra as well as a couple of slow variation results on multiplicative functions. Then, we give a corrected proof to a result of Granville and Soundararajan in
[
6
]
on separation of contribution of small primes from mean values of multiplicative functions. This proof is related to later constructions on multiplicative functions supported over powerful numbers. At the end, we use these and ideas from
[
6
]
and
[
8
]
to prove Theorems
4
and
5
.

Notation.
Throughout the paper,
p
stands for a prime number. The symbols
f(x)=O(g(x))
,
f(x)\ll g(x)
and
g(x)\gg f(x)
are equivalent to
|f(x)|\leq Cg(x)
for some constant
C>0
. The symbol
f(x)=o(g(x))
means that
\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}f(x)/g(x)=0
.

2
More detailed structure and slow variation results

First, we recall some structure results for the Euler product spectrum
\Gamma_{\Theta}(S)
. Define

\mathcal{E}(S):=\left\{e^{-k(1-\alpha)}:\;k\geq 0,\;\alpha\text{ is in the convex hull of }S\right\}.

Theorem 6

(Granville & Soundararajan)
.

For all closed subsets
S
of
\mathbb{U}
with
1\in S
,

\mathcal{E}(S)\subset\Gamma_{\Theta}(S)\times\mathcal{E}(S)=\Gamma_{\Theta}(S)\subset\mathcal{E}(S)\times[0,1].

Moreove, if the convex hull of
S
contains a real point other than
1
, one has

\Gamma_{\Theta}(S)=\mathcal{E}(S)=\mathcal{E}(S)\times[0,1].

This comes from
[
6
, Theorem 4]
and implies that
\Gamma_{\Theta}(S)
is starlike. In particular when
S=[-1,1]
, we have
\mathcal{E}([-1,1])=[0,1]
and

\Gamma_{\Theta}([-1,1])=[0,1].

(11)

We also need some structure results on the logarithmic spectrum
\Gamma_{0}(S)
. For a closed subset
S
of
\mathbb{U}
with
1\in S
, denote
S^{*}
to be the convex hull of
S
. Let
K(S)
denote the class of measurable functions
\chi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow S^{*}
with
\chi(t)=1
for
0\leq t\leq 1
. By
[
6
, Theorem 3.3]
, associated to each
\chi
there is a unique function
\sigma:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{U}
satisfying the following integral equation:

u\sigma(u)=\int_{0}^{u}\sigma(u-t)\chi(t)dt

(12)

for
u>1
and with the initial condition
\sigma(u)=1
for
0\leq u\leq 1
.

Define
\Lambda_{0}(S)
to be the set of values

\Bigl\{\frac{1}{u}\int_{0}^{u}\sigma(t)dt:u>0\text{ and }\sigma\text{ satisfies }\eqref{fcneq}\text{ for some }\chi\in K(S)\Bigr\}

Define
\mathcal{R}
to be the closure of the convex hull of the points
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1+s_{i}}{2}
, for all
n\geq 1
, and all choices of points
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}
lying in
S^{*}
. In addition to Theorem
3
, one has the following.

Theorem 7

(Granville & Soundararajan)
.

For any closed subset
S
of
\mathbb{U}
with
1\in S
,

\Lambda_{0}(S)=\Lambda_{0}(S)\times\mathcal{E}(S),\;\;\Lambda_{0}(S)\subset\Gamma_{0}(S)\subset\Lambda_{0}(S)\times[0,1],\;\;\text{
and }\;\;\Lambda_{0}(S)\subset\mathcal{R}.

This comes from the proof of Theorem 8 in
[
6
]
. One can check that
1\in\Lambda_{0}(S)
. Hence, when
S=[-1,1]
, we have
\mathcal{R}=[0,1]
and

[0,1]=\mathcal{E}([-1,1])\subset\Lambda_{0}([-1,1])\times\mathcal{E}([-1,1])=\Lambda_{0}([-1,1])\subset\mathcal{R}=[0,1].

In particular,

\Lambda_{0}([-1,1])=[0,1].

(13)

Finally, we recall the following slow variation results on multiplicative functions.

Proposition 1
.

Let
f
be a multiplicative function with
|f(n)|\leq 1
for all
n
. Let
x
be large, and suppose
1\leq y\leq x
. Then

\Big|\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\leq x}f(n)-\frac{1}{x/y}\sum_{n\leq x/y}f(n)\Big|\ll\frac{\log 2y}{\log x}\exp\Bigl(\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{|1-f(p)|}{p}\Bigr).

Proposition 2
.

Let
f
be a multiplicative function with
|f(n)|\leq 1
for all
n
. Then, for
1\leq w\leq x/10
, we have

\frac{1}{x}\Big|\sum_{n\leq x}f(n)\Big|-\frac{w}{x}\Big|\sum_{n\leq x/w}f(n)\Big|\ll\Bigl(\frac{\log 2w}{\log x}\Bigr)^{1-2/\pi}\log\Bigl(\frac{\log x}{\log 2w}\Bigr)+\frac{\log\log x}{(\log x)^{2-\sqrt{3}}}.

The above propositions come from Proposition 4.1 in
[
6
]
and Corollary 3 in
[
7
]
respectively.

3
A correction to Granville and Soundararajan’s proof

To obtain spectrum results on multiplicative functions, Granville and Soundararajan
[
6
]
derived the following proposition which was used to separate the contribution of small primes.

Proposition 3
.

For any multiplicative function
f
with
|f(p^{k})|\leq 1
for every prime power
p^{k}
, let

s(f,x):=\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{|1-f(p)|}{p}.

For any
1>\epsilon\geq\log 2/\log x
, let
g
be the completely multiplicative function with

g(p):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\text{ if }p\leq x^{\epsilon};\\
f(p),&\text{ if }p>x^{\epsilon}.\end{array}\right.

(14)

Then

\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\leq x}f(n)=\Theta(f,x^{\epsilon})\frac{1}{x}\sum_{m\leq x}g(m)+O\bigl(\epsilon\exp(s(f,x))\bigr),

where the implicit constant is absolute.

In
[
6
]
, Granville and Soundararajan defined a multiplicative function
h
by
h(p^{k})=f(p^{k})-f(p^{k-1})
if
p\leq x^{\epsilon}
, and
h(p^{k})=0
otherwise. They claimed that
f(n)=\sum_{m|n}h(n/m)g(m)
. However, this is only true when
f
is completely multiplicative but is false in general. For example, say

f(p)=1,\;\;f(p^{2})=-1\text{ for some prime }p>x^{\epsilon}.

Then,

\sum_{m|p^{2}}h\Bigl(\frac{p^{2}}{m}\Bigr)g(m)=h(p^{2})g(1)+h(p)g(p)+h(1)g(p^{2})=0\cdot g(1)+0\cdot g(p)+1\cdot f(p)^{2}=1\neq-1=f(p^{2}).

Thus, one needs to be more careful with its proof. It is worth mentioning that most of the results in
[
6
]
dealt with completely multiplicative functions and were not affected by the above fault. However, our modified proof below is needed for the structure result
\hat{\Gamma}(S)=\hat{\Gamma}_{\Theta}(S)\times\Lambda(S)
in (
3
). Also, subsequent work like
[
7
, Theorem 2]
required Proposition
3
for all multiplicative functions. Therefore, we shall give a completely accurate proof for Proposition
3
.

Proof of Proposition
3
.

We define a multiplicative function
\tilde{h}
and a completely multiplicative function
\tilde{g}
by

\tilde{h}(p^{k}):=f(p^{k})-f(p^{k-1})f(p),\;\text{ and }\;\;\tilde{f}(p^{k})=f(p)^{k}

respectively for all prime
p
and positive integer
k
. Then, one can check that
f(n)=\sum_{m|n}\tilde{h}(n/m)\tilde{f}(m)
as

\displaystyle\sum_{m|p^{k}}\tilde{h}\Bigl(\frac{p^{k}}{m}\Bigr)\tilde{f}(m)

\displaystyle=\tilde{h}(p^{k})\tilde{f}(1)+\tilde{h}(p^{k-1})\tilde{f}(p)+\cdots+\tilde{h}(p)\tilde{f}(p^{k-1})+\tilde{h}(1)\tilde{f}(p^{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(f(p^{k-i})-f(p^{k-i-1})f(p))f(p)^{i}+f(p)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}f(p^{k-i})f(p)^{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}f(p^{k-j})f(p)^{j}+f(p)^{k}=f(p^{k})

Hence, we have

\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\leq x}f(n)=\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\tilde{h}(n)}{n}\cdot\frac{n}{x}\sum_{m\leq x/n}\tilde{f}(m).

Now, with the completely multiplicative function
g
defined by (
14
) and the new multiplicative function

h(p^{k})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f(p)^{k}-f(p)^{k-1},&\text{ if }p\leq x^{\epsilon};\\
0,&\text{ otherwise},\end{array}\right.

one can check that
\tilde{f}(n)=\sum_{m|n}h(n/m)g(m)
as

\sum_{m|p^{k}}h\Bigl(\frac{p^{k}}{m}\Bigr)g(m)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(f(p)^{k}-f(p)^{k-1})+(f(p)^{k-1}-f(p)^{k-2})+\cdots+(f(p)-f(1))+1,&\text{ if }p\leq x^{\epsilon};\\
0+0+\cdots+0+f(p)^{k},&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

which equals to
f(p)^{k}=\tilde{f}(p^{k})
in all cases. Therefore, by Proposition
1
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\leq x}f(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\tilde{h}(n)}{n}\sum_{m\leq x/n}\frac{h(m)}{m}\cdot\frac{1}{x/(mn)}\sum_{k\leq x/(mn)}g(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\tilde{h}(n)}{n}\sum_{m\leq x/n}\frac{h(m)}{m}\cdot\Bigl[\frac{1}{x}\sum_{k\leq x}g(k)+O\Bigl(\frac{\log mn}{\log x}\exp(s(g,x))\Bigr)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{h_{0}(n)}{n}\cdot\frac{1}{x}\sum_{k\leq x}g(k)+O\Bigl(\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{|\tilde{h}(n)|}{n}\sum_{m\leq x/n}\frac{|h(m)|}{m}\frac{\log m+\log n}{\log x}\exp(s(g,x))\Bigr)

(15)

where
h_{0}(n)=\tilde{h}*h(n)=\sum_{m|n}\tilde{h}(n/m)h(m)
. From (
14
), we know that

s(g,x)=\sum_{x^{\epsilon}<p\leq x}\frac{|1-f(p)|}{p}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;s(f,x^{\epsilon})+s(g,x)=\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{|1-f(p)|}{p}.

(16)

Since
|h(p^{k})|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|f(p)-1|,&\text{ if }p\leq x^{\epsilon};\\
0,&\text{ if }p>x^{\epsilon}\end{array}\right.
, we have

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{|h(m)|}{m}\leq\prod_{p\leq x^{\epsilon}}\Bigl(1+\frac{|f(p)-1|}{p}+\frac{2}{p^{2}}+\frac{2}{p^{3}}+\cdots\Bigr)\ll\exp(s(f,x^{\epsilon})).

(17)

By
|h(p^{k})|\leq 2
for
p\leq x^{\epsilon}
and
|h(p^{k})|=0

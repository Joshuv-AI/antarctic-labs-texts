Non-commutative crepant resolutions, an overview Volume: ?


Abstract
Non-commutative crepant resolutions (NCCRs) are non-commutative analogues of the usual crepant resolutions that appear in algebraic geometry. In this paper we survey some results around NCCRs.

1.2
Crepant resolutions and derived equivalences

Let
X
be a normal algebraic variety with Gorenstein
singularities. A resolution of singularities
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
is said to

crepant
if
\pi^{\ast}\omega_{X}=\omega_{Y}
. In some sense
a crepant resolution is the tightest possible smooth approximation of an algebraic variety. Such crepant resolutions need not exist however.
For starters, their existence implies that

has rational singularities
[
KM98
, Corollary 5.24]
and this already strong restriction is far from sufficient. For example the three-dimensional
hypersurface singularities

x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}+w^{n}=0\qquad(n\geq 2)

(1.1)

have crepant resolutions if and only if

is even
[

, Corollary 1.16]
.
When crepant resolution do exist they are generally not unique. E.g.

(1.2)

which corresponds to

1.1
),
has two distinct crepant resolutions given by blowing up

and

Nonetheless experience has shown that such different crepant resolutions

1.3
Non-commutative rings

Most of the results below will be based on the interplay between algebraic geometry and non-commutative rings.
The relation between those subjects was first
observed by Beilinson
[
Bei78
]
. The connection is via tilting complexes.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
Y
be a Noetherian scheme. A
partial tilting complex

{\mathcal{T}}
on
Y
is a perfect complex such that
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{Y}({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{T}})=0
for
i\neq 0
.
A
tilting complex
is a partial tilting complex that
generates

D_{\operatorname{Qch}}(Y)
in the sense that its right orthogonal is zero, i.e.
\operatorname{RHom}_{Y}({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{F}})=0
implies
{\mathcal{F}}=0
.
A
(partial) tilting bundle
is a (partial) tilting complex which is a vector bundle.

Below we will also use tilting complexes in slightly more general contexts (e.g. DM-stacks).
Very general results concerning tilting complexes are
[
KK20
, Theorems 1,2 ]
.
For simplicity we state a slightly dumbed down version of them,
although below we will sometimes silently rely on the stronger results in loc. cit. See also
[
Ric89
,
Bon90
]
.

Theorem 1.7

(
[
KK20
, Theorems 1,2 ]
)
.

If
{\mathcal{T}}
is a tilting complex on a noetherian scheme
Y
then
\operatorname{RHom}_{Y}({\mathcal{T}},-)
defines
an equivalence of categories between
D_{\operatorname{Qch}}(Y)
and
D(\Lambda^{\circ})
for
\Lambda=\operatorname{End}_{Y}({\mathcal{T}})
. Moreover, if
Y
is regular
then
\Lambda
has finite global dimension. If furthermore
\Lambda
is right noetherian
then
\operatorname{RHom}_{Y}({\mathcal{T}},-)
restricts to an equivalence of categories
{\mathcal{D}}(Y)\cong{\mathcal{D}}(\Lambda^{\circ})
.

So a tilting complex reduces the homological algebra of

to the usually non-commutative ring
\Lambda=\operatorname{End}_{Y}({\mathcal{T}})
.
In the case of projective space

one can take
{\mathcal{T}}={\mathcal{O}}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}(1)\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathcal{O}}(n)

[

]

A

Remark 2.3

We will sometimes use the concepts introduced in

Remark 2.4

In the sequel we will be mostly concerned with NCCRs and thus the other definitions are mainly provided for context. Twisted NCCRs are natural generalizations of NCCRs, but the good properties
of NCCRs (sometimes conjectural) are usually not shared by twisted NCCRs. See e.g. Example

The definition of a (twisted) NCR is more tentative.
In particular the normality and reflexivity hypotheses do not seem very relevant. For example there is a nice theory of non-commutative resolutions of non-normal singularities in dimension one

. A similar
result is true for NCCRs.

Theorem 2.12

(
[
SVdB08
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let

be a normal finitely generated Gorenstein

The actual result proved in loc. cit. applies in a more general context and this has been further exploited in

,

]
(see also
[

, Corollary 1.7]
).

Remark 2.13
.

In order to deal with singularities with a singular minimal model Iyama and Wemyss generalize the definition of an NCCR

,
IW14b
,
Wem18
]

to certain rings, of possibly infinite global dimension, called

2.2
Relation with crepant categorical resolutions

We conjecture that non-commutative crepant resolutions are examples of “strongly crepant categorical resolutions” as introduced by Kuznetsov in
[
Kuz08b
]
.
However we can only prove this in special cases.

Let
X
be an algebraic variety. A
categorical
resolution
[
Kuz08b
]
of
{\mathcal{D}}(X)
is a “smooth” triangulated category
\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}}
together with
functors

\pi_{\ast}:\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}(X),\qquad\pi^{\ast}:\operatorname{Perf}(X)\rightarrow\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}}

which are adjoint (i.e.
\operatorname{Hom}_{\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}}}(\pi^{\ast}A,B)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{D}}(X)}(A,\pi_{\ast}B)
for
A\in\operatorname{Perf}(X)
,
B\in\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}}
) such that the natural transformation
{\operatorname{id}}_{\operatorname{Perf}(X)}\rightarrow\pi_{\ast}\pi^{\ast}
, obtained by putting
B=\pi^{\ast}A
, is an isomorphism. This implies in particular
that
\pi^{\ast}
is fully faithful.
There is some variation possible in the definition of smoothness. For us it means that
\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}}
is equivalent to the derived category of perfect modules over a smooth DG-algebra
[
KKP08
, Definition 2.23]
.

Remark 2.14
.

If
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
is a resolution of singularities of
X
then
({\mathcal{D}}(Y),R\pi_{\ast},L\pi^{\ast})
is a categorical resolution of
{\mathcal{D}}(X)
if and only
if
X
has rational singularities. Remarkably however, it has been shown in
[
KL15
]
that
{\mathcal{D}}(Y)
can be suitably enlarged to yield a categorical resolution. On the other hand, this result cannot be extended to more general dg-categories
[
Efi20
]
.

Following
[
Kuz08b
]
we say that a categorical resolution
(\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}},\pi_{\ast},\pi^{\ast})
of
{\mathcal{D}}(X)

is
weakly crepant
if
\pi^{\ast}
is both a left and a right adjoint to
\pi_{\ast}
.

There is also a notion of a
strongly crepant categorical
resolution for which we need the notion of a
relative Serre functor
.
To define this assume that
X
is Gorenstein and that
\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}
is a smooth triangulated category which is a
\operatorname{Perf}(X)
-module.
We will denote the action of
A\in\operatorname{Perf}(X)
on
B\in\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}
as
A\otimes_{X}B
and we assume that
-\otimes-
is exact in both arguments. We also assume that
the functor
\operatorname{Perf}(X)\rightarrow\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}:A\mapsto A\otimes_{X}B
has a right adjoint
\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}(X)
which we denote by
\operatorname{R\mathcal{H}\mathit{om}}_{\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}/X}(B,-)
. I.e. for
C\in\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}
we have functorial isomorphisms

\operatorname{Hom}_{\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}}(A\otimes_{X}B,C)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{X}(A,\operatorname{R\mathcal{H}\mathit{om}}_{\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}/X}(B,C)).

An autoequivalence
S_{\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}/X}:\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}\rightarrow\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}
is said to be a
relative Serre functor
for
\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}/X
if
there are functorial isomorphisms

\operatorname{R\mathcal{H}\mathit{om}}_{X}(\operatorname{R\mathcal{H}\mathit{om}}_{\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}/X}(B,C),{\mathcal{O}}_{X})\cong\operatorname{R\mathcal{H}\mathit{om}}_{\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}/X}(C,S_{\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}/X}B)

for
B,C\in\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}
. We say that
\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}/X
is
strongly crepant
if the identity functor
\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}\rightarrow\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}
is a relative Serre functor.

A
strongly crepant categorical resolution
of
X
is a quadruple
(\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}},\pi_{\ast},\pi^{\ast},\otimes_{X})
such that
(\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}},\pi_{\ast},\pi^{\ast})

is a categorical resolution of
X
,
-\otimes_{X}-
is a
\operatorname{Perf}(X)
-module structure on
\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}}
such that
\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}}/X
is strongly crepant
and
\pi^{\ast}
is

\otimes_{X}
-linear. The last condition means that for
A,B\in\operatorname{Perf}(X)
we have functorial isomorphisms
A\otimes_{X}\pi^{\ast}B\cong\pi^{\ast}(A\otimes_{X}B)
satisfying the appropriate compatibilities.

It is shown in
[
Kuz08b
, §3]
that a strongly crepant categorical resolution is weakly crepant, and moreover that if
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
is a crepant resolution in the usual sense
then
({\mathcal{D}}(Y),R\pi_{\ast},L\pi^{\ast},L\pi^{\ast}(-)\otimes_{Y}-)
is a strongly crepant categorical resolution of
X
.

The following easy lemma, which is an extension of
[
Lun10
, Example 5.3]
, shows that, under suitable conditions, rings of the form
\operatorname{End}_{R}(M)

form crepant categorical resolutions. If
M
is an
R
-module then
\operatorname{add}(M)
is the category spanned by modules which are direct summands of some
M^{\oplus n}
.

Lemma 2.2.1
.

Assume that
X=\operatorname{Spec}R
is an algebraic variety and let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module such that
\Lambda=\operatorname{End}_{R}(M)
has finite global dimension. Then
{\mathcal{D}}(\Lambda)
is smooth. Assume in addition that
R\in\operatorname{add}(M)
. Then

\operatorname{Perf}(R)\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}(\Lambda):N\mapsto M\overset{L}{\otimes}_{R}N

(2.1)

yields a categorical resolution of singularities of
X
(since
\operatorname{Perf}(R)\cong\operatorname{Perf}(X)
). Moreover, assuming
furthermore that
R
is normal Gorenstein:

1.

if
M
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay then this categorical resolution is weakly crepant;

2.

if
\Lambda
is an NCCR then this categorical resolution is strongly crepant.

Note that if (2) holds then
M
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay since we have assumed that
R\in\operatorname{add}(M)
.

The hypotheses of Lemma
2.2.1
are actually too strong. For example an NCCR is always a strongly crepant categorical
resolution in dimension
\leq 3
. This follows from
Proposition
2.15
below
which can be proved using the methods of
[
IR08
,
IW13
]
.

Proposition 2.15
.

Assume that
\Lambda=\operatorname{End}_{R}(M)
is an NCCR and

\dim R\leq 3
then

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{\Lambda}(M,M)=0\qquad\text{for $i>0$}.

(2.2)

Proof.

For the benefit of the reader we give a proof.
We may assume that
R
is local of dimension 3 (the case

\dim\leq 2
is easy). By the Auslander Buchsbaum formula
[
IR08
, Proposition 2.3]

\Lambda
has global dimension
3
.
Since
M
is reflexive, it has depth
\geq 2
, and hence, again by the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula, it has projective dimension
\leq 1
over
\Lambda
and moreover
it is projective over
\Lambda
in codimension
2
.

Hence we
have a projective resolution of
M
as
\Lambda
-module:

0\rightarrow P_{1}\rightarrow P_{0}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0.

Applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(-,M)
we get a long exact sequence of
R
-modules

0\rightarrow R\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(P_{0},M)\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(P_{1},M)\rightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(M,M)\rightarrow 0.

(2.3)

Assume
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(M,M)\neq 0
.
Since
M
is projective over
\Lambda
in codimension two,
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(M,M)
is finite
dimensional and hence it has depth 0 as
R
-module. On the other hand, since
\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(P_{i},M)
is reflexive as
R
-module, it has
\operatorname{depth}\geq 2
.
Finally
R
being maximal Cohen-Macaulay has depth 3. One may verify that these depth restrictions are incompatible with
(
2.3
).
∎

It seems too much to hope for that (
2.2
) would always be true, but lack of time has prevented us from seriously looking for a counterexample.
On the other hand we are sufficiently optimistic to make the following conjecture.

Conjecture 2.16
.

If
X=\operatorname{Spec}R
is a normal algebraic variety with Gorenstein singularities then an NCCR of
R
always yields a strongly crepant categorical resolution of
X
.

To prove this conjecture one would have to construct for an NCCR
\Lambda
of
R
a partial tiling complex
P^{\bullet}
of
\Lambda
-modules such that

\operatorname{RHom}_{\Lambda}(P^{\bullet},P^{\bullet})=R
.

Remark 2.17
.

The strongly crepantness of
{\mathcal{E}}/X
as defined above is independent of the resolution property. One may check that if
\Lambda/R

is a twisted NCCR then
{\mathcal{D}}(\Lambda)
is strongly crepant over
\operatorname{Spec}R
. But one may check that it is not a categorical resolution.

3
Constructions of non-commutative crepant resolutions

3.1
Quotient singularities

Here we will restrict ourselves to quotient singularities for finite groups.
Quotient singularities for (non-finite) reductive groups will be covered in §
4
.

If
G
is a finite group and
W
is a faithful finite dimensional unimodular (i.e.
\det W=k
) representation of
G
then the skew group ring

\operatorname{Sym}(W)\#G=\operatorname{End}_{\operatorname{Sym}(W)^{G}}(\operatorname{Sym}(W))
is
an NCCR for
R=\operatorname{Sym}(W)^{G}
(which is Gorenstein because of the unimodularity hypothesis).

In dimension
\leq 3
such quotient singularities always have a crepant resolution by the celebrated BKR-theorem
[
BKR01
]
. In higher dimension
this is not so. The simplest counterexample is given by
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
acting with weights
(-1,-1,-1,-1)
on
W=k^{4}
because in that case

R
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial and terminal. See e.g.
[
Mo11
]
.

3.2
Crepant resolutions with tilting complexes

In case
R
is a normal Gorenstein domain and
Y\rightarrow\operatorname{Spec}R
is a crepant resolution and
{\mathcal{T}}
is a tilting complex on
Y

then
\operatorname{\mathcal{E}\mathit{nd}}_{Y}({\mathcal{T}})
is an NCCR of
R
by Theorem
2.6
. Conversely, assuming a crepant resolution exists, any NCCR has to be of this form if we accept Conjecture
2.7
(
{\mathcal{T}}
is the dual of the image of
\Lambda
under the asserted derived equivalence
{\mathcal{D}}(\Lambda)\cong{\mathcal{D}}(Y)
).

This is a very general method for constructing NCCRs. Note however that even in dimension three there may be crepant resolutions without tilting complex.
See Example
2.11
. Furthermore as indicated in §
3.1
there are normal Gorenstein singularities that admit an NCCR but not a crepant resolution.

Example 3.1
.

A textbook example where this method works very well is the case of determinantal varieties
[
BLVdB10
,
BLVdB16
]
.
Let

and

Let

be the varieties of matrices in

It is a classical result that

is Gorenstein. It is also well known that

has a crepant Springer type resolution given by

where
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
sends

to the composition of

. If
{\mathcal{R}}
denotes the universal subbundle
on

\operatorname{Hom}(k^{n},{\mathcal{R}})
(i.e.

]

(see also
[
BLVdB15
]
[
Efi17
]

.
For other approaches to this example see
[

]
and Theorem

below.

Alas things are often more complicated. For determinantal varieties associated to symmetric of skew-symmetric matrices,
the Springer type resolutions are not crepant so a tilting bundle on them only gives an NCR (see
[
WZ12
]
). NCCRs of such generalized determinantal
varieties will be obtained in §
4
using a different approach.

3.3
Resolutions with partial tilting complexes

Assume
R
is a normal Gorenstein domain with rational singularities and

is a
resolution which is not crepant. A strengthening of Conjecture

\operatorname{\mathcal{E}\mathit{nd}}_{Y}({\mathcal{T}})
for a
partial tilting complex

Proposition 3.3

3.4
Three-dimensional affine toric varieties

In this case there is the following beautiful result by Broomhead

The asymptotic expansion of Krätzel’s integral and an integral related to an extension of the Whittaker function
R. B. Paris

Abstract

2. The expansion of
F_{p,\nu}(x)
when
p>0

Let us consider first the case
p>0
in (
1.1
). We make the change of variable
t=\tau(x/p)^{1/(p+1)}
to find

F_{p,\nu}(x)=(x/p)^{\nu/(p+1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau^{\nu-1}e^{-X\psi(\tau)}\,d\tau,

(2.1)

where

\psi(\tau):=\frac{\tau^{p}}{p}+\frac{1}{\tau},\qquad X:=p^{1/(p+1)}x^{p/(p+1)}.

(2.2)

The integral in (
2.1
) converges at both endpoints provided
\Re(X)>0
; that is, when
|\arg\,x|<(p+1)\pi/(2p)

and so represents the analytical continuation of (
1.1
) into this wider sector.

Saddle points of the exponential factor occur when
\psi^{\prime}(\tau)=0
; that is, when
\tau^{p-1}-1/\tau^{2}=0
whence

\tau_{sk}=e^{2\pi ik/(p+1)}\qquad(k=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots).

The saddle points are consequently situated on the unit circle in the
\tau
-plane, with the saddles corresponding to higher
k
values being situated on different Riemann surfaces. The only contributory saddle for
F_{p,\nu}(x)
is found to be that corresponding to
k=0
; see Fig. 1 for typical examples of the steepest descent path from the origin passing to infinity in the direction
\arg\,\tau=-\theta/(p+1)
, where
\theta=\arg\,x
.

Noting that
\psi(1)=(p+1)/p
, we introduce the new variable
w

\frac{1}{2}w^{2}=\psi(\tau)-\psi(1)=\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(1)}{2!}(\tau-1)^{2}+\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}(1)}{3!}(\tau-1)^{3}+\cdots

Inversion of the above expansion using the

\tau=1+\frac{1}{(p+1)^{1/2}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}A_{k}w^{k},

A_{k}
are

A_{1}=1,\quad A_{2}=\frac{4-p}{6(p+1)^{1/2}},\quad A_{2}=\frac{26-19p+2p^{2}}{72(p+1)},

A_{4}=\frac{1}{3456(p+1)^{2}}(1252-2956p+1881p^{2}-316p^{3}+4p^{4}).

Then we have

\frac{d\tau}{dw}=\frac{1}{(p+1)^{1/2}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}kA_{k}w^{k-1}.

(2.4)

Combining (
2.3
) and (

\tau^{\nu-1}\frac{d\tau}{dw}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle e}}{{=}}\frac{1}{(p+1)^{1/2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}B_{k}w^{2k},

B_{k}

B_{0}=1,\quad B_{1}=\frac{1}{24(p+1)}(2+12\nu^{2}-12\nu(p-1)-7p+2p^{2}),

B_{2}=\frac{1}{3456(p+1)^{2}}\biggl\{4+144\nu^{4}-480\nu^{3}(p-1)-172p+417p^{2}-172p^{3}+4p^{4}

\hskip 142.26378pt+120\nu^{2}(4-11p+4p^{2})-24\nu(-6+41p(1-p)+6p^{3})\biggr\}.

p
and
\nu

B_{k}
using the numerical inversion procedure described above.

Then

3. An alternative derivation of the expansion for
F_{p,\nu}(x)
when
p>0

In Kilbas
et al.

[
4
]
it was shown that

F_{p,\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi ip}\int_{c-\infty i}^{c+\infty i}\Gamma(s)\Gamma\biggl(\frac{s+\nu}{p}\biggr)x^{-s}ds\qquad c>\max\{0,-\Re(\nu)\}

(3.1)

valid in the sector
|\arg\,x|<(p+1)\pi/(2p)
. The integration path lies to the right of the poles of
\Gamma(s)
and
\Gamma((s+\nu)/p)
situated at
s=-k
and
s=-kp-\nu
(
k=0,1,2,\ldots
), respectively. This result can be easily established by making use of the Cahen-Mellin integral (see, for example,
[
9
, p. 89]
)

e^{-z}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-\infty i}^{c+\infty i}\Gamma(s)z^{-s}ds\qquad(c>0;\ |\arg\,z|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi)

(3.2)

with
z=x/t
followed by substitution in (
1.1
) and evaluation of the resulting integral over
t
as a gamma function.

Displacement of the integration path to the left over the poles produces (provided
kp+\nu
does not take on non-negative integer values)
[
4
]

F_{p,\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Gamma\biggl(\frac{\nu-k}{p}\biggr)\,\frac{(-x)^{k}}{k!}+x^{\nu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Gamma(-\nu-kp)\,\frac{(-x^{p})^{k}}{k!}

\ \ =\frac{1}{p}{}_{1}\Psi_{0}(-1/p,\nu/p;-x)+x^{\nu}{}_{1}\Psi_{0}(-p,-\nu:-x^{p}),

(3.3)

where the Wright function
{}_{1}\Psi_{0}(a,b;z)
is defined by

{}_{1}\Psi_{0}(a,b;z):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Gamma(ak+b)\,\frac{z^{k}}{k!}\qquad(0<a<1).

When
kp+\nu
assumes a non-negative integer value, double poles arise with the result that logarithmic terms will be present.

To determine the asymptotic expansion of the integral in (
3.1
) we may displace the integration path as far to the right as we please, since there are no poles of the integrand on the right of this path. On the displaced path, which we denote by
L
, it follows that
|s|
is everywhere large on
L
. If we define the parameters

\kappa=\frac{p+1}{p},\quad h=p^{1/p},\quad\vartheta=\frac{\nu}{p}-\frac{1}{2},\quad{\cal A}_{0}=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}-\vartheta}p^{-\vartheta},

we have the inverse factorial expansion
[
9
, p. 39, Lemma 2.2]

\Gamma(s)\Gamma\biggl(\frac{s+\nu}{p}\biggr)=2\pi{\cal A}_{0}(h\kappa^{\kappa})^{-s}\biggl\{\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}(-)^{j}c_{j}\Gamma(\kappa s+\vartheta-j)+\rho_{M}(s)\Gamma(\kappa s+\vartheta-M)\biggr\},

(3.4)

where
M
is a positive integer and
\rho_{M}(s)=O(1)
as
|s|\to\infty
in
|\arg\,s|<\pi
. The coefficient
c_{0}=1
and an algorithm for the determination of the higher coefficients
c_{j}
(
j\geq 1
) is given in
[
9
, §2.2.4]
,
[
8
, pp. 321–323]
.

Substitution of (
3.4
) into (
3.1
) taken over the displaced integration path
L
then yields

F_{p,\nu}(x)=\frac{2\pi{\cal A}_{0}}{p}\biggl\{\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}(-)^{j}c_{j}\,\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{L}(\kappa X)^{-\kappa s}\Gamma(\kappa s+\vartheta-j)\,ds+R_{M}(x)\biggr\},

where
X=(hx)^{1/\kappa}=p^{1/(p+1)}x^{p/(p+1)}
(see (
2.2
)) and the remainder term
R_{M}(x)
is

R_{M}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{L}\rho_{M}(s)(\kappa X)^{-\kappa s}\Gamma(\kappa s+\vartheta-M)\,ds.

Application of Lemma 2.7 in
[
9
, p. 71]
shows that

R_{M}(x)=O(X^{\vartheta-M}e^{-\kappa X})\qquad(|X|\to\infty,\ |\arg\,X|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi).

Hence, upon use of the Cahen-Mellin integral in (
3.2
), we obtain the expansion

F_{p,\nu}(x)=\frac{2\pi{\cal A}_{0}}{\kappa p}\,X^{\vartheta}e^{-\kappa X}\biggl\{\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}(-)^{j}c_{j}(\kappa X)^{-j}+O(X^{-M})\biggr\}

(3.5)

as
|X|\to\infty
in the sector
|\arg\,X|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi
. We note that this sector includes the sector
|\arg\,x|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi
.

Routine algebra shows that the leading term of this expansion agrees with the leading term of (
2.5
). Consequently we must have the coefficients
c_{j}
related to the
B_{j}
in (
2.5
) given by

c_{j}=(-)^{j}(2\kappa)^{j}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})_{j}B_{j}.

4. The expansion for
F_{p,\nu}(x)
when
p<0

When
p<0
the expansions of
F_{p,\nu}(x)
are different according as
-1\leq p<0
and
p\leq-1
; the situations pertaining to
p=0
and
p=-1
being given in (
1.2
). In both ranges of the parameter
p
we require
\Re(\nu)<0
for convergence of the integral in (
1.1
) at the upper limit.

From
[
4
, (3.13)]
, we have when
p<0

F_{p,\nu}(x)=-\frac{1}{2\pi ip}\int_{c-\infty i}^{c+\infty i}\Gamma(s)\Gamma\biggl(\frac{s+\nu}{p}\biggr)x^{-s}\,ds\qquad 0<c<-\Re(\nu),

where the integration path now separates the poles of
\Gamma(s)
at
s=-k
from those of
\Gamma((s+\nu)/p)
at
s=-kp-\nu
, (
k=0,1,2,\ldots
). When
-1\leq p<0
, displacement of the path to the right over the poles at
s=-kp-\nu
produces

[
4
, Theorem 4.2]

F_{p,\nu}(x)=x^{\nu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(-kp-\nu)}{k!}\,(-x^{p})^{k}\qquad(\Re(\nu)<0,\ -1\leq p<0).

(4.1)

This convergent series also yields the asymptotic expansion for
|x|\to\infty
valid in
|\arg\,x|<(p+1)\pi/(2p)
. Hence we have

F_{p,\nu}(x)=x^{\nu}\biggl\{\Gamma(-\nu)-\frac{\Gamma(-p-\nu)}{x^{-p}}+O(1/x^{-2p})\biggr\}\qquad(x\to\infty).

When
p\leq-1
, displacement of the path to the left over the poles at
s=-k
produces
[
4
, Theorem 4.3]

F_{p,\nu}(x)=-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Gamma\biggl(\frac{\nu-k}{p}\biggr)\,\frac{(-x)^{k}}{k!}\qquad(\Re(\nu)<0,\ p\leq-1).

(4.2)

The asymptotic expansion in this case is obtained by displacing the integration path to the right over the poles at
s=-kp-\nu
to yield

F_{p,\nu}(x)\sim x^{\nu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Gamma(-kp-\nu)\,\frac{(-x^{p})^{k}}{k!}\qquad(\Re(\nu)<0,\ p\leq-1)

(4.3)

as
|x|\to\infty
in
|\arg\,x|<(p+1)\pi/(2p)
.

It is easily verified that when
p=-1
the expansions (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) both reduce to

F_{-1,\nu}(x)=(1+x)^{\nu}\Gamma(-\nu)

as stated in (
1.2
).

5. The expansion of
F_{p,\nu}(x)
when
\nu
and
x
are large

Let
\nu=1+aX
, where
a>0
is fixed and
X=p^{1/(p+1)}x^{p/(p+1)}
with
p>0
. Then from (
2.1
) we find

F_{p,\nu}(x)=(x/p)^{\nu/(p+1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau^{aX}e^{-X(\tau^{p}/p+\tau^{-1})}d\tau=(x/p)^{\nu/(p+1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-X\phi(\tau)}d\tau,

(5.1)

where

\phi(\tau)=\frac{\tau^{p}}{p}+\frac{1}{\tau}-a\log\,\tau.

Saddle points of the exponential factor are given by solutions of the equation

\tau^{p+1}=1+a\tau.

(5.2)

Examination of the steepest paths shows that the path from the origin through the contributory saddle
\tau_{s}>1
on the positive real axis is similar to that shown in Fig. 1.
Making the change of variable

\frac{1}{2}w^{2}=\phi(\tau)-\phi(\tau_{s}),

we find upon inversion as described in Section 2, followed by differentiation,

\frac{d\tau}{dw}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle e}}{{=}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\phi_{s}^{\prime\prime}}}\,\sum_{k\geq 0}C_{k}w^{2k},

where we use the abbreviation
\phi_{s}=\phi(\tau_{s})
and

C_{0}=1,\qquad C_{1}=\frac{1}{24\phi_{s}^{\prime\prime 3}}(5\phi_{s}^{\prime\prime\prime 2}-3\phi_{s}^{\prime\prime}\phi_{s}^{iv}),

C_{2}=\frac{1}{3456\phi_{s}^{\prime\prime 6}}(385\phi_{s}^{\prime\prime\prime 4}-630\phi_{s}^{\prime\prime}\phi_{s}^{\prime\prime\prime 2}\phi_{s}^{iv}+105\phi_{s}^{\prime\prime 2}(\phi_{s}^{iv})^{2}+168\phi_{s}^{\prime\prime 2}\phi_{s}^{\prime\prime\prime}\phi_{s}^{v}-24\phi_{s}^{\prime\prime 3}\phi_{s}^{vi}).

Then

F_{p,\nu}(x)=(x/p)^{\nu/(p+1)}\,\frac{e^{-X\phi(\tau_{s})}}{\sqrt{\phi_{s}^{\prime\prime}}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-Xw^{2}/2}\,\frac{d\tau}{dw}\,dw

\sim(x/p)^{\nu/(p+1)}\,e^{-X\phi(\tau_{s})}\,\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{X\phi_{s}^{\prime\prime}}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})_{k}C_{k}}{(X/2)^{k}}

as
x\to\infty
.

Upon noting that

\phi(\tau_{s})=\frac{p+1}{p\tau_{s}}+\frac{a}{p}-a\log\,\tau_{s},\qquad\phi_{s}^{\prime\prime}=\frac{1}{\tau_{s}^{2}}\biggl(\frac{p+1}{\tau_{s}}+ap\biggr),

where use has been made of (
5.2
), we finally obtain the expansion

F_{p,\nu}(x)\sim\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{X\phi_{s}^{\prime\prime}}}\,\frac{e^{-\kappa X/\tau_{s}-aX/p}}{(p/x)^{\nu/(p+1)}}\,\tau_{s}^{aX}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})_{k}C_{k}}{(X/2)^{k}}

(5.3)

as
x\to\infty
when
\nu=1+aX
,
X=p^{1/(p+1)}x^{p/(p+1)}
and
\kappa=(p+1)/p
. It is found that the steepest descent path when
x
is complex is very similar to that shown in Fig. 1(b), with the real positive saddle
\tau_{s}
still being the only contributory saddle when
|\arg\,x|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi
. Hence the expansion (
5.3
) holds for complex
x
when
|\arg\,x|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi
.

In a recent paper

]
, an extension of the Whittaker function was introduced by

M_{\kappa,\mu}^{(p,\nu)}(z)=\frac{x^{\mu+\frac{1}{2}}e^{x/2}}{B(\mu-\kappa+\frac{1}{2},\mu+\kappa+\frac{1}{2})}\sqrt{\frac{2p}{\pi}}\int_{0}^{1}t^{\mu+\kappa-1}(1-t)^{\mu-\kappa-1}e^{zt}K_{\nu+\frac{1}{2}}\biggl(\frac{p}{t(1-t)}\biggr)\,dt,

where
K_{\nu}(z)
is the modified Bessel function and
B(a,b)=\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)/\Gamma(a+b)
is the beta function. This representation is valid for
p\geq 0
,
\nu\geq 0
and
2\mu\neq-1,-2,\ldots\
. In the case
p=\nu=0
, the extended function reduces to the usual Whittaker function
M_{\kappa,\mu}(x)
given by
[
7
, p. 337]

M_{\kappa,\mu}(z)=\frac{x^{\mu+\frac{1}{2}}e^{-z/2}}{B(\mu-\kappa+\frac{1}{2},\mu+\kappa+\frac{1}{2})}\int_{0}^{1}t^{\mu+\kappa-\frac{1}{2}}(1-t)^{\mu-\kappa-\frac{1}{2}}e^{zt}dt.

Our aim is to determine the asymptotic expansion of the related integral

I(a,b;z)=\sqrt{\frac{2p}{\pi}}\int_{0}^{1}t^{a-\frac{1}{2}}(1-t)^{b-\frac{1}{2}}e^{zt}K_{\nu}\biggl(\frac{p}{t(1-t)}\biggr)\,dt\qquad(\nu\geq-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$},\ p>0),

(6.1)

valid for arbitrary parameters
a
and
b
,
as
|z|\to\infty
. This integral corresponds to that appearing in the definition of
M_{\kappa,\mu}^{(p,\nu)}(x)
when
a=\mu+\kappa-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}
,
b=\mu-\kappa-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}
and
\nu\to\nu+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}
.
By making the change of variable
t\to 1-t
, it is easily seen that
I(a,b;z)
satisfies the following Kummer-type transformation

I(a,b;z)=e^{z}I(b,a;-z).

(6.2)

We first consider the expansion of
I(a,b;z)
for large
z
in the sector
|\arg(-z)|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi
. We set
z=-x
and note that
for large
x
the dominant behaviour of the integrand results from a neighbourhood of
t=0
. From
[
7
, (10.40.2)]
we have the expansion

K_{\nu}\biggl(\frac{p}{t(1-t)}\biggr)\sim\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2p}}\exp\,\biggl[-\frac{p}{t}-\frac{p}{1-t}\biggr]\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}(\nu)}{p^{k}}\,(t(1-t))^{k+\frac{1}{2}}

as
t\to 0
, where
a_{0}(\nu)=1
and

a_{k}(\nu)=\prod_{r=1}^{k}\frac{4\nu^{2}-(2r-1)^{2}}{k!\ 2^{3k}}=(-1)^{k}\frac{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}+\nu)_{k}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}-\nu)_{k}}{k!2^{k}}\quad(k\geq 1).

Substitution of this expansion into the integral (
6.1
) then leads to

I(a,b;-x)\sim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}(\nu)}{p^{k}}\int_{0}^{1}t^{a+k}(1-t)^{b+k}e^{-xt-p/t}\,e^{-p/(1-t)}dt

\hskip 31.2982pt=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}(\nu)}{p^{k}x^{a+k+1}}\int_{0}^{x}\tau^{a+k}e^{-(\tau+px/\tau)}g_{k}(\tau/x)\,d\tau,

where

g_{k}(w):=(1-w)^{b+k}e^{-p/(1-w)}=e^{-p}(1-w)^{b+k}e^{-pw/(1-w)}.

(6.3)

We can expand
g_{k}(w)
about
w=0
to obtain

g_{k}(w)=e^{-p}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{r}c_{r}(k)}{r!}w^{r}\qquad(|w|<1),

where the first few coefficients
c_{r}(k)
are given by

c_{0}(k)=1,\qquad c_{1}(k)=p+\beta,

c_{2}(k)=(p+\beta)^{2}-(p+\beta)-p,

c_{3}(k)=(p+\beta)^{3}-6(p+\beta)^{2}+(3b+2)(p+\beta)+4p,

(6.4)

with
\beta:=b+k
. Then

I(a,b;-x)\sim e^{-p}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}(\nu)}{p^{k}x^{a+k+1}}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{r}c_{r}(k)}{r!\,x^{r}}\int_{0}^{x}\tau^{a+k+r}e^{-(\tau+px/\tau)}d\tau.

We now extend the upper limit
x
to
\infty
, thereby introducing an error of
O(e^{-x})
when
|\arg\,x|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi
. Employing the integral representation of the modified Bessel function
[
7
, (10.32.10)]

2(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}z)^{-\nu}K_{\nu}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(\tau+z^{2}/(4\tau))}\,\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{\nu+1}}\qquad(|\arg\,z|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}$}\pi)

and the fact that
K_{-\nu}(z)=K_{\nu}(z)
, we find (with
\omega:=a+1+k+r
)

I(a,b;-x)\sim 2e^{-p}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}(\nu)}{p^{k}x^{a+k+1}}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{r}c_{r}(k)}{r!\,x^{r}}\,(px)^{\mu/2}K_{\omega}(2\sqrt{px})

\hskip 34.14322pt=2e^{-p}\biggl(\frac{p}{x}\biggr)^{(a+1)/2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}(\nu)\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{r}c_{r}(k)p^{r}}{r!\,(px)^{(k+r)/2}}\,K_{\omega}(2\sqrt{px}).

(6.5)

I(a,b;-x)\sim 2e^{-p}\biggl(\frac{p}{x}\biggr)^{(a+1)/2}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{D_{r}K_{a+1+r}(2\sqrt{px})}{(px)^{r/2}}

(6.6)

as
|x|\to\infty
in
|\arg\,x|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi
, where
D_{0}=1
and

D_{r}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{r}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}p^{\ell}}{\ell!}\,a_{r-\ell}(\nu)c_{\ell}(r-\ell)\qquad(r\geq 1).

The expansion valid in
|\arg\,z|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi
immediately follows from (
6.6
) and the Kummer-type transformation in (
6.2
).

I(a,b;-x)\sim 2e^{-p}\biggl(\frac{p}{x}\biggr)^{(a+1)/2}K_{a+1}(2\sqrt{px})\sim\frac{\sqrt{\pi}p^{a/2+1/4}}{x^{a/2+3/4}}\,e^{-p-2\sqrt{px}}

(6.7)

as
|x|\to\infty
in
|\arg\,x|<\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi
.

J(a,b;-x)\sim 2e^{-p}\biggl(\frac{p}{x}\biggr)^{(a+1)/2}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{r}p^{r}c_{r}(0)}{r!(px)^{r/2}}\,K_{a+1+r}(2\sqrt{px})

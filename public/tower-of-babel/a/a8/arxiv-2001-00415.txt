Combinatorial Game Distributions of Steiner Systems
Yuki Irie Research Alliance Center for Mathematical Sciences Tohoku University Miyagi, Japan yirie@tohoku.ac.jp

Abstract
The 𝒫 {\mathscr{P}} -position sets of some combinatorial games have special combinatorial structures. For example, the 𝒫 {\mathscr{P}} -position set of the hexad game, first investigated by Conway and Ryba, is the block set of the Steiner system S ⁡ ( 5 , 6 , 12 ) S(5,6,12) in the shuffle numbering, denoted by 𝒟 sh {\mathcal{D}}_{\text{sh}} . There were, however, few known games related to Steiner systems like the hexad game. For a given Steiner system, we construct a game whose 𝒫 {\mathscr{P}} -position set is its block set. By using constructed games, we obtain the following two results. First, we characterize 𝒟 sh {\mathcal{D}}_{\text{sh}} among the 5040 isomorphic S ⁡ ( 5 , 6 , 12 ) {S}(5,6,12) with point set { 0 , 1 , … , 11 } \set{0,1,\dotsc,11} . For each S ⁡ ( 5 , 6 , 12 ) {S}(5,6,12) , our construction produces a game whose 𝒫 {\mathscr{P}} -position set is its block set. From 𝒟 sh {\mathcal{D}}_{\text{sh}} , we obtain the hexad game, and this game is characterized as a unique game with the minimum number of positions among the obtained 5040 games. Second, we characterize projective Steiner triple systems by using game distributions. Here, the game distribution of a Steiner system 𝒟 {\mathcal{D}} is the frequency distribution of the numbers of positions in games obtained from Steiner systems isomorphic to 𝒟 {\mathcal{D}} . We find that the game distribution of an S ⁡ ( t , t + 1 , v ) {S}({t},{t}+1,{v}) can be decomposed into symmetric components and that a Steiner triple system is projective if and only if its game distribution has a unique symmetric component.

\mathop{\operator@font Orb}\nolimits^{\alpha}({\mathcal{D}})=\Set{{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}\in\mathop{\operator@font Orb}\nolimits({\mathcal{D}}):{a}_{0}({\mathcal{D}}^{\prime})+{a}_{{k}}({\mathcal{D}}^{\prime})=\alpha}

for
\alpha\in{s}({\mathcal{D}})
.
Define

{f}_{{\mathcal{D}}}^{\alpha}\left({n}\right)=\left|\Set{{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}\in\mathop{\operator@font Orb}\nolimits^{\alpha}({\mathcal{D}}):\bigl\lvert{\mathcal{P}}^{{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}}\bigr\rvert={n}}\right|

and

\displaystyle\overline{{f}_{{\mathcal{D}}}^{\alpha}}\left({n}\right)

\displaystyle=\left|\Set{{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}\in\mathop{\operator@font Orb}\nolimits^{\alpha}({\mathcal{D}}):\bigl\lvert\overline{{\mathcal{P}}^{{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}}}\bigr\rvert={n}}\right|

\displaystyle=\bigl\lvert\bigl\{\,{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}\in\mathop{\operator@font Orb}\nolimits^{\alpha}({\mathcal{D}}):{a}_{0}({\mathcal{D}}^{\prime})={n}\,\bigr\}\bigr\rvert.\hypertarget{p13-25}{}

By definition,

{f}_{{\mathcal{D}}}\left({n}\right)=\sum_{\alpha\in s({\mathcal{D}})}{f}_{{\mathcal{D}}}^{\alpha}\left({n}\right).

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be an
S({t},{t}+1,{v})
.
Then the following proposition says that
{f}_{{\mathcal{D}}}^{\alpha}\left({n}\right)
is symmetric.
From this, the function
{f}_{{\mathcal{D}}}^{\alpha}
is called a
symmetric component
of the game distribution of
{\mathcal{D}}
.

Proposition 20
.

If
{\mathcal{D}}
is an
S({t},{k},{v})
with
{k}={t}+1
, then

\overline{{f}_{{\mathcal{D}}}^{\alpha}}\left({n}\right)=\overline{{f}_{{\mathcal{D}}}^{\alpha}}\left(\alpha-{n}\right)

for
{n}\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular,

{f}_{{\mathcal{D}}}^{\alpha}\left({n}\right)={f}_{{\mathcal{D}}}^{\alpha}\left(2{{v}\choose{k}}-\alpha-{n}\right).

Proof.

It suffices to show the assertion for
\alpha={a}_{0}({\mathcal{D}})+{a}_{{k}}({\mathcal{D}})
,
since
\overline{{f}_{{\mathcal{D}}}^{\alpha}}\left({n}\right)=\overline{{f}_{{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}}^{\alpha}}\left({n}\right)
for
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}\in\mathop{\operator@font Orb}\nolimits({\mathcal{D}})
.
Let
{\mathcal{B}}={\mathcal{B}}_{{\mathcal{D}}}
and
{P}\in{[{v}]\choose{k}}\setminus{\mathcal{B}}
.
We first show that

\bigl\lvert{\mathcal{O}}({P})\cap{\mathcal{B}}\bigr\rvert+\bigl\lvert{\mathcal{I}}({P})\cap{\mathcal{B}}\bigr\rvert={k}.

(5.1)

Let
{p}\in{P}
.
Since
{k}={t}+1
, there is a unique
{p}^{\prime}\in[{v}]
such that

{P}^{({p}\ {p}^{\prime})}\in{\mathcal{B}}
.
Note that
\left|{\mathcal{O}}({P})\cap{\mathcal{B}}\right|
equals the number of
{p}\in{P}
with
{p}^{\prime}<{p}
.
Similarly,
\left|{\mathcal{I}}({P})\cap{\mathcal{B}}\right|
equals the number of
{p}\in{P}
with
{p}^{\prime}>{p}
.
Hence (
5.1
) holds.

Let
{\pi}
be the permutation
{i}\mapsto{v}-1-{i}
of
[{v}]
.
We next show that
{\mathcal{D}}^{\pi}\in\mathop{\operator@font Orb}\nolimits^{\alpha}({\mathcal{D}})
.
Since
{p}<{q}\iff{p}^{\pi}>{q}^{\pi}
for
{p},{q}\in[{v}]
, it follows that
\bigl\lvert{\mathcal{I}}({P})\cap{\mathcal{B}}\bigr\rvert=\bigl\lvert{\mathcal{O}}({P}^{\pi})\cap{\mathcal{B}}^{\pi}\bigr\rvert
.
By (
5.1
),

\bigl\lvert{\mathcal{O}}({P})\cap{\mathcal{B}}\bigr\rvert=i\iff\bigl\lvert{\mathcal{O}}({P}^{\pi})\cap{\mathcal{B}}^{\pi}\bigr\rvert={k}-{i}.

This implies that
{a}_{i}({\mathcal{D}})={a}_{{k}-i}({\mathcal{D}}^{\pi})
, and hence that

{a}_{0}({\mathcal{D}}^{\pi})+{a}_{{k}}({\mathcal{D}}^{\pi})={a}_{k}({\mathcal{D}})+{a}_{0}({\mathcal{D}})=\alpha
.
Thus
{\mathcal{D}}^{\pi}\in\mathop{\operator@font Orb}\nolimits^{\alpha}({\mathcal{D}})
.
We also see that

{a}_{0}({\mathcal{D}})={n}\iff{a}_{0}({\mathcal{D}}^{\pi})=\alpha-{n}.

Therefore
\overline{{f}_{{\mathcal{D}}}^{\alpha}}\left({n}\right)=\overline{{f}_{{\mathcal{D}}}^{\alpha}}\left(\alpha-{n}\right)
.
∎

A Characterization of Projective Steiner Triple Systems

Theorem 24
.

If
{\mathcal{D}}
is an
S(2,3,{v})
,
then
{\mathcal{D}}
is projective if and only if
its game distribution has a unique symmetric component, that is,
\left|{s}({\mathcal{D}})\right|=1
.

To prove Theorem
24
, we show two lemmas.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be an
S(2,3,{v})
.
The following lemma enables us to calculate
{a}_{0}({\mathcal{D}})+{a}_{3}({\mathcal{D}})
.
Recall that
\lambda_{i}
, defined in (
2.1
), is the number of blocks of
{\mathcal{D}}
containing
i
given points.

Lemma 25
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a
{t}\mathchar 45({v},{k},\lambda)\text{\ design}
with block set
{\mathcal{B}}
.

(1)

If
{t}\geqslant 1
and
{\mathcal{B}}
is an independent set in
\Gamma_{{v},{k}}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}i{a}_{i}({\mathcal{D}})=\frac{\lambda_{0}{k}({v}-{k})}{2}.

(2)

If
{\mathcal{D}}
is an
S(2,3,{v})
, then

{a}_{0}({\mathcal{D}})+{a}_{3}({\mathcal{D}})=\sum_{\set{B,C}\in{{\mathcal{B}}\choose 2}}{I}({B},{C})-\frac{{v}({v}-1)({v}-3)}{12},

where
{I}({B},{C})=\left|{\mathcal{I}}({B})\cap{\mathcal{I}}({C})\right|
.

Proof.

(1) Let

{\mathcal{A}}_{{i}}({\mathcal{D}})=\biggl\{{P}\in{[{v}]\choose{k}}\setminus{\mathcal{B}}:\bigl\lvert{\mathcal{O}}({P})\cap{\mathcal{B}}\bigr\rvert=i\biggr\}.\hypertarget{p16-6}{}

Then
{a}_{i}({\mathcal{D}})=\left|{\mathcal{A}}_{{i}}({\mathcal{D}})\right|
.
Since
{\mathcal{B}}
is an independent set,
we see that

\bigcup_{i=0}^{{k}}{\mathcal{A}}_{{i}}({\mathcal{D}})={[{v}]\choose{k}}\setminus{\mathcal{B}}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{{k}}i{a}_{i}({\mathcal{D}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{{k}}\Bigl\lvert\Bigl\{({P},{B}):{P}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{{i}}({\mathcal{D}}),\ {B}\in{\mathcal{O}}({P})\cap{\mathcal{B}}\Bigr\}\Bigr\rvert

\displaystyle=\biggl\lvert\biggl\{({P},{B}):{P}\in{[{v}]\choose{k}}\setminus{\mathcal{B}},\ {B}\in{\mathcal{O}}({P})\cap{\mathcal{B}}\biggr\}\biggr\rvert

\displaystyle=\Bigl\lvert\Bigl\{\rule[0.0pt]{0.0pt}{9.6739pt}({P},{B}):{B}\in{\mathcal{B}},\ {P}\in{\mathcal{I}}({B})\Bigr\}\Bigr\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{{B}\in{\mathcal{B}}}\bigl\lvert{\mathcal{I}}({B})\bigr\rvert.\hypertarget{p16-12}{}

Let
{B}\in{\mathcal{B}}
and write
{B}=\set{{b}_{1},\dotsc,{b}_{k}}
with
{b}_{1}<\dotsb<{b}_{k}
.
Then
{P}\in{\mathcal{I}}({B})
if and only if
{P}={B}^{({b}_{i}\ {p})}
for some
{b}_{i}
and
{p}\not\in{B}
with
{p}>{b}_{i}
.
The number of such
{p}
equals
{v}-1-{b}_{i}-({k}-i)
. Hence

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{I}}({B})\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\bigl({v}-1-{b}_{i}-({k}-i)\bigr)={k}({v}-1)-\sum_{{b}\in{B}}{b}-\frac{{k}({k}-1)}{2}.

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{{B}\in{\mathcal{B}}}\sum_{{b}\in{B}}{b}

\displaystyle=(0+1+\dotsb+{v}-1)\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{v(v-1)}{2}\frac{{v-1\choose t-1}}{{k-1\choose t-1}}\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{k(v-1)}{2}\frac{{v\choose t}}{{k\choose t}}\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{{k}({v}-1)\lambda_{0}}{2},

it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{{B}\in{\mathcal{B}}}\bigl\lvert{\mathcal{I}}({B})\bigr\rvert

\displaystyle=\lambda_{0}\left({k}({v}-1)-\frac{{k}({v}-1)}{2}-\frac{{k}({k}-1)}{2}\right)=\frac{\lambda_{0}{k}({v}-{k})}{2}.

(2) Let
{a}_{i}={a}_{i}({\mathcal{D}})
.
We first show that

\sum_{{i}=0}^{3}{i\choose 2}{a}_{i}={a}_{2}+3{a}_{3}=\sum_{\set{{B},{C}}\in{{\mathcal{B}}\choose 2}}{I}({B},{C}).

(6.1)

By the definition of
{a}_{i}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{{i}=0}^{3}{i\choose 2}{a}_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{{i}=0}^{3}\left|\Set{\bigl({P},\set{{B},{C}}\bigr):{P}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{{i}}({\mathcal{D}}),\ \set{{B},{C}}\in{{\mathcal{O}}({P})\cap{\mathcal{B}}\choose 2}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\Set{\bigl({P},\set{{B},{C}}\bigr):{P}\in{[{v}]\choose 3}\setminus{\mathcal{B}},\ \set{{B},{C}}\in{{\mathcal{O}}({P})\cap{\mathcal{B}}\choose 2}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\Set{\bigl({P},\set{{B},{C}}\bigr):\set{{B},{C}}\in{{\mathcal{B}}\choose 2},\ {P}\in{\mathcal{I}}({B})\cap{\mathcal{I}}({C})}\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\set{B,C}\in{{\mathcal{B}}\choose 2}}{I}({B},{C}).

We now calculate
{a}_{0}+{a}_{3}
.
Note that
\lambda_{0}={v}({v}-1)/6
.
By (1),

\sum_{{i}=0}^{3}{i}{a}_{i}={a}_{1}+2{a}_{2}+3{a}_{3}=\frac{\lambda_{0}3({v}-3)}{2}=\frac{{v}({v}-1)({v}-3)}{4}.

Since

\sum_{{i}=0}^{3}{a}_{i}={a}_{0}+{a}_{1}+{a}_{2}+{a}_{3}={{v}\choose 3}-\lambda_{0}=\frac{{v}({v}-1)({v}-3)}{6},

it follows that

\sum_{{i}=0}^{3}{i}{a}_{i}-\sum_{{i}=0}^{3}{a}_{i}=-{a}_{0}+{a}_{2}+2{a}_{3}=\frac{{v}({v}-1)({v}-3)}{12}.\hypertarget{p17-14}{}

Therefore

{a}_{0}+{a}_{3}+\frac{{v}({v}-1)({v}-3)}{12}={a}_{2}+3{a}_{3}=\sum_{\set{B,C}\in{{\mathcal{B}}\choose 2}}{I}({B},{C}).

∎

From Lemma
25
, to calculate
{a}_{0}+{a}_{3}
, it is enough to compute
\sum I(B,C)
.
To do this, the following easy lemma will be used.

Lemma 26
.

Let
{B}
and
{C}
be two distinct blocks of an
S(2,3,{v})
.

(1)

If
{I}({B},{C})\neq 0
, then
\left|{B}\cap{C}\right|=1
.

(2)

If
{B}=\set{{x},{b},{b}^{\prime}}
,
{C}^{\prime}=\set{{x},{c},{c}^{\prime}}
,
{b}<{b}^{\prime}
, and
{b}<{c}<{c}^{\prime}
, then

{I}({B},{C})=[{c}<{b}^{\prime}]+[{c}^{\prime}<{b}^{\prime}],

(6.2)

where
[\ ]
is the Iverson bracket notation, that is,
[{S}]=1
if a statement
{S}
holds, and
[{S}]=0
otherwise.

(3)

If

{I}({B},{C})\neq{I}({B}^{(i\ i+1)},{C}^{(i\ i+1)}),

then
\set{{B},{C}}=\bigl\{\set{{i},{x},{y}},\set{{i}+1,{x},{z}}\bigr\}
for some
{x},{y},{z}\in[{v}]
with
{y}\neq{z}
.

Proof.

(1) If
{B}\cap{C}=\emptyset
, then
{I}({B},{C})=0
.
If
\left|{B}\cap{C}\right|\geqslant 2
, then
{B}={C}
. Hence
\left|{B}\cap{C}\right|=1
.

(2) Since
{b}<{b}^{\prime}
and
{b}<{c}<{c}^{\prime}
, it follows that

{\mathcal{I}}({B})\cap{\mathcal{I}}({C})=\begin{cases}\emptyset&\text{\ \ {if} \ }{b}^{\prime}<{c},\\
\bigl\{\set{{x},{b}^{\prime},{c}^{\prime}}\bigr\}&\text{\ \ {if} \ }{c}<{b}^{\prime}<{c}^{\prime},\\
\bigl\{\set{{x},{b}^{\prime},{c}^{\prime}},\set{{x},{b}^{\prime},{c}}\bigr\}&\text{\ \ {if} \ }{c}^{\prime}<{b}^{\prime}.\end{cases}

Thus (
6.2
) holds.

(3) By (1), if
\left|{B}\cap{C}\right|\neq 1
then
{I}({B},{C})={I}\bigl({B}^{(i\ i+1)},{C}^{(i\ i+1)}\bigr)
.
Let
{B}=\set{{x},{b},{b}^{\prime}}
and
{C}=\set{{x},{c},{c}^{\prime}}
.
We may assume that
{b}<{b}^{\prime}
and
{b}<{c}<{c}^{\prime}
.
If

[{y}^{(i\ i+1)}<{z}^{(i\ i+1)}]\neq[{y}<{z}],\hypertarget{p18-8}{}

then
\set{{y},{z}}=\set{i,i+1}
.
It follows from (2) that
\set{i,i+1}\in\bigl\{\set{{b},{c}},\set{{b}^{\prime},{c}},\set{{b}^{\prime},{c}^{\prime}}\bigr\}
.
Hence
\set{{B},{C}}=\bigl\{\set{{x},{i},{y}},\set{{x},{i}+1,{z}}\bigr\}
for some
{y},{z}\in[{v}]
with
{y}\neq{z}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
24
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be an
S(2,3,{v})
with block set
{\mathcal{B}}
.

We first show that if
{\mathcal{D}}
is projective,
then
\left|{s}({\mathcal{D}})\right|=1
.
By Lemma
26
,

\sum_{\set{{B},{C}}\in{{\mathcal{B}}\choose 2}}{I}({B},{C})=\sum_{\set{{B},{C}}\in{{\mathcal{B}}\choose 2},\ \left|{B}\cap{C}\right|=1}\hskip-20.00003pt{I}({B},{C}).

Let
\set{{x},{b},{b}^{\prime}}
and
\set{{x},{c},{c}^{\prime}}
be two distinct blocks of
{\mathcal{D}}
.
We may assume that
{b}<{b}^{\prime}
and
{b}<{c}<{c}^{\prime}
.

Since
{\mathcal{D}}
is projective, there are
{y},{z}\in[{v}]
such that
\set{{y},{b},{c}}
,
\set{{y},{b}^{\prime},{c}^{\prime}}
,
\set{{z},{b},{c}^{\prime}}
, and
\set{{z},{b}^{\prime},{c}}
are blocks.
Thus we obtain the following triple of pairs of blocks:

\Bigl\{\,\bigl\{\set{{x},{b},{b}^{\prime}},\set{{x},{c},{c}^{\prime}}\bigr\},\quad\bigl\{\set{{y},{b},{c}},\set{{y},{b}^{\prime},{c}^{\prime}}\bigr\},\quad\bigl\{\set{{z},{b},{c}^{\prime}},\set{{z},{b}^{\prime},{c}}\bigr\}\,\Bigr\}.

By Lemma
26
,

\begin{split}{I}\bigl(\set{{x},{b},{b}^{\prime}}&,\set{{x},{c},{c}^{\prime}}\bigr)+{I}\bigl(\set{{y},{b},{c}},\set{{y},{b}^{\prime},{c}^{\prime}}\bigr)+{I}\bigl(\set{{z},{b},{c}^{\prime}},\set{{z},{b}^{\prime},{c}}\bigr)\\
&=[{c}<{b}^{\prime}]+[{c}^{\prime}<{b}^{\prime}]+[{b}^{\prime}<{c}]+[{c}^{\prime}<{c}]+[{b}^{\prime}<{c}^{\prime}]+[{c}<{c}^{\prime}]=3.\end{split}

(6.3)

Note that every pair
\set{{B},{C}}
of blocks with
\left|{B}\cap{C}\right|=1
belongs to exactly one such triple and there are
{v}{\lambda_{1}\choose 2}/3
triples.
Hence

\sum_{\set{{B},{C}}\in{{B}\choose 2},\ \left|{B}\cap{C}\right|=1}\hskip-20.00003pt{I}({B},{C})=\frac{{v}{\lambda_{1}\choose 2}}{3}{3}=\frac{{v}({v}-1)({v}-3)}{8}.

By Lemma
25
,

{a}_{0}({\mathcal{D}})+{a}_{3}({\mathcal{D}})=\sum_{\set{B,C}\in{{\mathcal{B}}\choose 2}}{I}({B},{C})-\frac{{v}({v}-1)({v}-3)}{12}=\frac{{v}({v}-1)({v}-3)}{24}.

Therefore
{s}({\mathcal{D}})=\set{{v}({v}-1)({v}-3)/24}
.

We next show that if
{\mathcal{D}}
is not projective,
then
\left|{s}({\mathcal{D}})\right|>1
.
Note that
{v}\geqslant 9
.
Since
{\mathcal{D}}
is not projective,
it follows from Remark
4
that we may assume that

\set{0,{v}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2},2},\set{1,{v}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2},3},\set{0,{v}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}3},3},\set{1,{v}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}3},4}\in{\mathcal{B}}

by replacing
{\mathcal{D}}
with some
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}\in\mathop{\operator@font Orb}\nolimits({\mathcal{D}})
if necessary (see Figure
6
).

Figure 6:
The four blocks of
{\mathcal{D}}
.

We may also assume that
\set{0,1,{v}-1}\in{\mathcal{B}}
.
We show that

\sum_{\set{{B},{C}}\in{{\mathcal{B}}\choose 2}}{I}({B},{C})-{I}({B},{C})^{(0\ 1)}\neq 0\quad\text{{\ }or \ }\sum_{\set{{B},{C}}\in{{\mathcal{B}}\choose 2}}{I}({B},{C})^{(2\ 3)}-{I}({B},{C})^{(2\ 3)(0\ 1)}\neq 0,

(6.4)

where
{I}({B},{C})^{(0\ 1)}={I}({B}^{(0\ 1)},{C}^{(0\ 1)})
.
If
\set{{B},{C}}\in{{\mathcal{B}}\choose 2}
and
{I}({B},{C})\neq{I}({B},{C})^{(0\ 1)}
,
then, by Lemma
26
,

\set{{B},{C}}=\bigl\{\set{0,{x},{y}},\set{1,{x},{z}}\bigr\}

for some
{x},{y},{z}\in[{v}]
with
{y}\neq{z}
;
note that
{x}\neq{v}-1
since
\set{0,1,{v}-1}\in{\mathcal{B}}
.
For
{x}\in\set{2,3,\dotsc,{v}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2}}
, let
{B}_{{x}}=\set{0,{x},{y}_{{x}}}\in{\mathcal{B}}
and
{C}_{x}=\set{1,{x},{z}_{{x}}}\in{\mathcal{B}}
.
By assumption,

\begin{split}&{B}_{{v}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2}}=\set{0,{v}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2},2},\ {C}_{{v}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2}}=\set{1,{v}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2},3},\\
&{B}_{{v}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}3}}=\set{0,{v}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}3},3},\ {C}_{{v}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}3}}=\set{1,{v}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}3},4}.\end{split}

(6.5)

We see that

\sum_{\set{{B},{C}}\in{{\mathcal{B}}\choose 2}}{I}({B},{C})-{I}({B},{C})^{(0\ 1)}=\sum_{{x}=2}^{{v}-2}{I}({B}_{x},{C}_{x})-{I}({B}_{x},{C}_{x})^{(0\ 1)}.

(6.6)

Similarly, by Lemma
26
, if
\set{{B},{C}}\in{{\mathcal{B}}\choose 2}
and
{I}({B},{C})^{(2\ 3)}\neq{I}({B},{C})^{(2\ 3)(0\ 1)}
, then

\bigl\{\,{B}^{(2\ 3)},{C}^{(2\ 3)}\,\bigr\}=\bigl\{\set{0,{x},{y}}^{(2\ 3)},\set{1,{x},{z}}^{(2\ 3)}\bigr\}

for some
{x},{y},{z}\in[{v}]
with
{y}\neq{z}
,
and hence

\set{{B},{C}}=\set{{B}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{x}}},{C}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{x}}}}
.
Therefore

\sum_{\set{{B},{C}}\in{{\mathcal{B}}\choose 2}}{I}({B},{C})^{(2\ 3)}-{I}({B},{C})^{(2\ 3)(0\ 1)}=\sum_{{x}=2}^{{v}-2}{I}({B}_{x},{C}_{x})^{(2\ 3)}-{I}({B}_{x},{C}_{x})^{(2\ 3)(0\ 1)}.

(6.7)

If
{I}({B}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{x}}},{C}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{x}}})\neq{I}({B}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{x}}},{C}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{x}}})^{{{}^{(2\ 3)}}}
,
then
\set{{B}_{{x}},{C}_{{x}}}=\bigl\{\set{2,{x},{u}},\set{3,{x},{w}}\bigr\}
for some
{u},{w}\in[{v}]
,
so
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{x}}={v}-2
by (
6.5
).
Similarly, if

{I}({B}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{x}}},{C}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{x}}})^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(0\ 1)}}\neq{I}({B}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{x}}},{C}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{x}}})^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(2\ 3)(0\ 1)}}
,
then
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{x}}={v}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2}
.
Let
{B}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}o}}={B}_{{v}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2}}
and
{C}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}o}}={C}_{{v}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2}}
.
By (
6.6
) and (
6.7
),

\displaystyle\sum_{\set{{B},{C}}\in{{\mathcal{B}}\choose 2}}{I}({B},{C})-{I}({B},{C})^{(0\ 1)}-\left({I}({B},{C})^{(2\ 3)}-{I}({B},{C})^{(2\ 3)(0\ 1)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{{x}=2}^{{v}-2}{I}({B}_{x},{C}_{x})-{I}({B}_{x},{C}_{x})^{(0\ 1)}-\left({I}({B}_{x},{C}_{x})^{(2\ 3)}-{I}({B}_{x},{C}_{x})^{(2\ 3)(0\ 1)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{{x}=2}^{{v}-2}{I}({B}_{x},{C}_{x})-{I}({B}_{x},{C}_{x})^{(2\ 3)}-\left({I}({B}_{x},{C}_{x})^{(0\ 1)}-{I}({B}_{x},{C}_{x})^{(2\ 3)(0\ 1)}\right)

\displaystyle={I}({B}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}o}},{C}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}o}})-{I}({B}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}o}},{C}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}o}})^{(2\ 3)}-\left({I}({B}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}o}},{C}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}o}})^{(0\ 1)}-{I}({B}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}o}},{C}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}o}})^{(2\ 3)(0\ 1)}\right).

Since

\displaystyle{I}({B}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}o}},{C}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}o}})

\displaystyle={I}(\set{0,{v}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2},2},\set{1,{v}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2},3})=1,

\displaystyle{I}({B}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}o}},{C}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}o}})^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(2\ 3)}}

\displaystyle={I}(\set{0,{v}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2},3},\set{1,{v}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2},2})=2,

\displaystyle{I}({B}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}o}},{C}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}o}})^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(0\ 1)}}

\displaystyle={I}(\set{1,{v}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2},2},\set{0,{v}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2},3})=2,

\displaystyle{I}({B}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}o}},{C}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}o}})^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(2\ 3)(0\ 1)}}

\displaystyle={I}(\set{1,{v}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2},3},\set{0,{v}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2},2})=1,

we see that

\sum_{\set{{B},{C}}\in{{\mathcal{B}}\choose 2}}{I}({B},{C})-{I}({B},{C})^{(0\ 1)}-\left({I}({B},{C})^{(2\ 3)}-{I}({B},{C})^{(2\ 3)(0\ 1)}\right)=1-2-(2-1)=-2.

Therefore (
6.4
) holds. It follows from Lemma
25
that
\left|{s}({\mathcal{D}})\right|>1
.
∎

On Abelianity Lines in Elliptic 𝑾 \boldsymbol{W} -Algebras This paper is a contribution to the Special Issue on Elliptic Integrable Systems, Special Functions and Quantum Field Theory. The full collection is available at https://www.emis.de/journals/SIGMA/elliptic-integrable-systems.html

Abstract
We present a systematic derivation of the abelianity conditions for the q q -deformed W W -algebras constructed from the elliptic quantum algebra 𝒜 q , p ​ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ^ ​ ( N ) c ) \mathcal{A}_{q,p}\big(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}(N)_{c}\big) . We identify two sets of conditions on a given critical surface yielding abelianity lines in the moduli space ( p , q , c p,q,c ). Each line is identified as an intersection of a countable number of critical surfaces obeying diophantine consistency conditions. The corresponding Poisson brackets structures are then computed for which some universal features are described.

1
Introduction

The construction of deformed
W_{N}
algebras as subalgebras of the elliptic quantum algebra
\mathcal{A}_{q,p}\big(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}(N)_{c}\big)
was proposed in
[
3
]
. The construction uses
as generating functionals quadratic and higher rank traces of the quantum Lax operators defining

\mathcal{A}_{q,p}\big(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}(N)_{c}\big)
.
The existence of such closed subalgebras of the enveloping algebra of
\mathcal{A}_{q,p}\big(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}(N)_{c}\big)
was conditioned by a so-called “critical” relation between the elliptic modulus or nome
p
, the quantum deformation parameter
q
and the central charge
c
. This critical relation is parametrized by two integers
(m,n)
, and defines surfaces
\mathscr{S}_{m,n}
in the
(p,q,c)
moduli space.
The structure functions were identified as particular ratios of elliptic functions.
Characterizing these structures as
q
-deformed
W
-algebras was made possible by first finding a second constraint on
p
,
q
,
c
, yielding now structure functions degenerating to
1
.
This second constraint may thus be consistently called “abelianity condition”
and defines a line on the surface
\mathscr{S}_{m,n}
.
The expansion of the structure functions around this constraint, by infinitesimally relaxing it, yields Poisson structures, which could then be compared to, and in some cases identified with, the original ones in
[
6
,
7
]
.
The full quantum structures could then be identified as natural quantizations of these Poisson structures.
The derivation of this second “abelianity constraint” however assumed a very specific pattern of cancellation inside the elliptic structure functions.
It was therefore a natural question whether more general cancellation patterns occur which may then lead to new abelianity conditions (and as a consequence new Poisson structures).
We will address this issue here, and determine the most general cancellation pattern of the structure functions, within a given “fundamental” scheme using the particular form of the structure functions as ratios of products of a single elliptic function with shifted/modified arguments, and a remarkable periodicity property of this component function.
Quite remarkably, it turns out that:

1.

All abelianity lines are identified as intersections of critical surfaces (generically a countable set of such surfaces).

2.

Intersections of critical surfaces yielding abelianity lines are characterized by a diophantine-type condition of integrity of a certain ratio of combinations of their integer parameters.

We shall now detail this derivation, starting with a reminder of the general frame of
[
3
]
and prepare some notations. The main result shall then be stated precisely, and its proof will be given in a detailed way.
We then compute explicit associated Poisson structures and compare them along different surfaces converging onto the same line.
These Poisson structures are realized by linear combinations of a few fundamental elliptic functions, identified as logarithmic derivatives of the short Jacobi theta function. The specific elliptic functions depend only on the abelianity line itself, whichever realization by an intersection is achieved.
Only the constant rational coefficients and the span of the linear sums depend on the critical surface along which the PB structure is expanded.

2
Quadratic subalgebras in
\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}_{q,p}\big(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}(N)_{c}\big)}}

The central object of our study are quadratic subalgebras
\mathcal{W}^{(m,n)}_{pqc}(N)
in the
quantum elliptic algebra
{\mathcal{A}_{q,p}\big(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}(N)_{c}\big)}
, parametrized by two integers
m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
(in addition to the parameters
p
,
q
,
c
of the quantum elliptic algebra). We refer to
[
1
,
3
]
for the full construction, and summarize the main points needed for our present study.

The
\mathcal{W}^{(m,n)}_{pqc}(N)
subalgebras are defined on surfaces in the three-dimensional parameter space spanned by
(q,p,c)

\mathscr{S}_{m,n}\colon\ \big({-}p^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{m}\big({-}p^{*\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{n}=q^{-N},

(2.1)

where
p^{*}=pq^{-2c}
. We introduce the operators
t_{m,n}^{(k)}(z)
,
1\leq k\leq N
, generating the subalgebra

\mathcal{W}^{(m,n)}_{pqc}(N)
, and
L(z)
the Lax operator of the
{\mathcal{A}_{q,p}\big(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}(N)_{c}\big)}
algebra.
The
\mathcal{W}^{(m,n)}_{pqc}(N)
algebra is defined by the following proposition, proved in
[
3
]
:

Proposition 2.1
.

On the surface
\mathscr{S}_{m,n}
, one has:

a)

The generators
t_{m,n}^{(k)}(z)
obey the following exchange relation with
L(w)
:

t_{m,n}^{(k)}(z)L(w)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\mathcal{F}_{-m}(z_{i}/w)}{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{n}(z_{i}/w)}L(w)t_{m,n}^{(k)}(z).

(2.2)

The function
\mathcal{F}_{a}(x)
is expressed in terms of the function
\mathcal{U}(x)
defined in (
2.5
) as

\mathcal{F}_{a}(x)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\prod_{\ell=0}^{a-1}\mathcal{U}\big(\big({-}p^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{\ell}x\big)&\text{for $a>0$},\\
1&\text{for $a=0$},\\
\displaystyle\prod_{\ell=1}^{|a|}\mathcal{U}\big(\big({-}p^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{-\ell}x\big)^{-1}&\text{for $a<0$},\end{cases}\qquad\mathcal{F}^{*}_{a}(x)=\mathcal{F}_{a}(x)\big|_{p\to p^{*}}.

b)

They realize quadratic subalgebras in
{\mathcal{A}_{q,p}\big(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}(N)_{c}\big)}
with quadratic exchange relations for
1\leq k,k^{\prime}\leq N
:

t_{m,n}^{(k)}(z)t_{m,n}^{(k^{\prime})}(w)=\prod_{i=(1-k)/2}^{(k-1)/2}\prod_{j=(1-k^{\prime})/2}^{(k^{\prime}-1)/2}\mathcal{Y}_{m,n}\big(q^{i-j}z/w\big)t_{m,n}^{(k^{\prime})}(w)t_{m,n}^{(k)}(z),

(2.3)

where the function
\mathcal{Y}_{m,n}(x)
is given by

\mathcal{Y}_{m,n}(x)=\frac{\mathcal{F}_{n}^{*}(x)\mathcal{F}_{-n}^{*}(x)}{\mathcal{F}_{m}(x)\mathcal{F}_{-m}(x)}=\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{\ell=1}^{|m|}\mathcal{U}\big(\big({-}p^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{-\ell}x\big)\prod_{\ell=1}^{|n|-1}\mathcal{U}\big(\big({-}p^{*\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{\ell}x\big)}{\displaystyle\prod_{\ell=1}^{|m|-1}\mathcal{U}\big(\big({-}p^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{\ell}x\big)\prod_{\ell=1}^{|n|}\mathcal{U}\big(\big({-}p^{*\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{-\ell}x\big)}.

(2.4)

The function
\mathcal{U}(z)
is defined using the short Jacobi
\theta
function

\mathcal{U}(z)=q^{\frac{2}{N}-2}\frac{\theta_{q^{2N}}\big(q^{2}z^{2}\big)\theta_{q^{2N}}\big(q^{2}z^{-2}\big)}{\theta_{q^{2N}}\big(z^{2}\big)\theta_{q^{2N}}\big(z^{-2}\big)}.

(2.5)

We remind that the short Jacobi
\theta
function is defined in terms of the infinite
q
-Pochhammer symbols
(z;a)_{\infty}=\prod\limits_{n\geq 0}(1-za^{n})
by

\theta_{a}(z)=(z;a)_{\infty}\big(az^{-1};a\big)_{\infty}.

It enjoys the following properties

\theta_{a^{2}}\big(a^{2}z\big)=\theta_{a^{2}}\big(z^{-1}\big)=-\frac{\theta_{a^{2}}(z)}{z}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\theta_{a^{2}}(az)=\theta_{a^{2}}\big(az^{-1}\big).

To ensure a proper definition of the elliptic quantum algebra
{\mathcal{A}_{q,p}\big(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}(N)_{c}\big)}
, and in particular the convergence of the short Jacobi
\theta
functions as infinite products, we have to suppose that
|p|<1
and
|q|<1
.

Remark 2.2
.

At this point, we need to elaborate the exact meaning of the exchange relation (
2.3
). It is to be understood as an equality of formal series expansion after a suitable Riemann–Hilbert splitting of the meromorphic function
\mathcal{Y}_{m,n}(x)
. It will then acquire supplementary terms (central extensions or higher spin operators) on poles or zeroes of this exchange function. We do not achieve this procedure here since we are only interested in the abelianity conditions, see below.

Line of abelianity.
We are interested in characterizing the situations where the subalgebra (
2.3
) in
{\mathcal{A}_{q,p}\big(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}(N)_{c}\big)}
becomes abelian. Demanding an abelian exchange relation between the generators
t_{m,n}^{(1)}(z)
imposes
\mathcal{Y}_{m,n}(x)=1
.
Once abelianity is obtained between these generators, the whole subalgebra (
2.3
) becomes abelian, because the exchange function in (
2.3
) for generic
k
,
k^{\prime}
is a product of the
\mathcal{Y}_{m,n}
function with shifted arguments. The conditions allowing
\mathcal{Y}_{m,n}(x)=1
will define a
line of abelianity
.

Since this strict abelian condition implies that
\mathcal{Y}_{m,n}(x)
has neither a pole nor a zero, the extra terms mentioned in Remark
2.2
are not expected to appear and abelianity is indeed exact.
They will nevertheless contribute to the Poisson brackets computed in the neighborhood of the abelianity line, see Section
6
.

In the following, it will be convenient to parametrize
p
and
c
(or equivalently
p
and
p^{*}
)
on the surface
\mathscr{S}_{m,n}
through the relation

-p^{\frac{1}{2}}=q^{-N\lambda/m}\qquad\text{and}\qquad-p^{*\frac{1}{2}}=q^{-N\lambda^{*}/n} .

(2.6)

The surface condition then reads
\lambda+\lambda^{*}=1
, and the central charge is given by

c=-N\left(\frac{\lambda}{m}-\frac{\lambda^{*}}{n}\right).

(2.7)

A first step in deriving abelianity conditions was performed in
[
1
,
3
]
, where the following result was shown:

Proposition 2.3
.

On the surface
\mathscr{S}_{m,n}
, the generators
t_{m,n}^{(k)}(z)
realize an abelian subalgebra in
\mathcal{A}_{q,p}\big(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}(N)_{c}\big)
when
\lambda
,
\lambda^{*}
take non-vanishing
integer
values.

Note that the notation
\lambda^{*}
corresponds to
\lambda^{\prime}
in
[
1
,
3
]
(it is
not
the complex conjugate of
\lambda
!).

3
Main results

In this section, we expose our main results concerning the classification of the lines of abelianity, and their realization as intersections of critical surfaces.

3.1
Generic abelianity lines as intersections

Lemma 3.1
.

Two different surfaces
\mathscr{S}_{m,n}
and
\mathscr{S}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}
have a non-empty intersection if and only if
m\neq m^{\prime}
,
n\neq n^{\prime}
and the determinant
\left|\begin{smallmatrix}m&m^{\prime}\\
n&n^{\prime}\end{smallmatrix}\right|\neq 0
.
In that case, the parameters
p
,
p^{*}
and
c
are given by

-p^{\frac{1}{2}}=q^{N{\textstyle{\frac{n^{\prime}-n}{m^{\prime}n-mn^{\prime}}}}},\qquad-p^{*\frac{1}{2}}=q^{N{\textstyle{\frac{m-m^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}n-mn^{\prime}}}}},

(3.1)

and

c=N\frac{m^{\prime}+n^{\prime}-m-n}{m^{\prime}n-mn^{\prime}}.

(3.2)

Proof.

If two surfaces
\mathscr{S}_{m,n}
and
\mathscr{S}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}
intersect, then
p
,
q
,
c
have to satisfy simultaneously the two surface conditions
\big({-}p^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{m}\big({-}p^{*\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{n}=q^{-N}
and
\big({-}p^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{m^{\prime}}\big({-}p^{*\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{n^{\prime}}=q^{-N}
.
This implies
\big({-}p^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{m^{\prime}-m}=\big({-}p^{*\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{n-n^{\prime}}
.
Thus
n=n^{\prime}
implies that
m=m^{\prime}
(and vice-versa). But then the
two surfaces coincide, which contradicts the hypothesis.
Hence, we must have
m\neq m^{\prime}
and
n\neq n^{\prime}
.
Given that
p^{*}=pq^{-2c}
, the surface conditions can be rewritten
as
\big({-}p^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{m+n}=q^{nc-N}

and
\big({-}p^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{m^{\prime}+n^{\prime}}=q^{n^{\prime}c-N}
, which leads to

\displaystyle\big({-}p^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{m^{\prime}n-mn^{\prime}}=q^{N(n^{\prime}-n)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\big({-}p^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{m^{\prime}+n^{\prime}-m-n}=q^{c(n^{\prime}-n)}.

(3.3)

Then, since
q
is not a root of unity, the first equation in (
3.3
) has a solution if and only if
n^{\prime}m-m^{\prime}n\neq 0
.
In that case, (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) follow immediately.
Note that these relations are invariant in the exchange
(m,n)\leftrightarrow(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Recalling the parametrization (
2.6
), one sees that on the intersection of two surfaces

\mathscr{S}_{m,n}
and
\mathscr{S}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}
, one has the following values for the line viewed on
\mathscr{S}_{m,n}

\lambda=\frac{m(n-n^{\prime})}{m^{\prime}n-mn^{\prime}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lambda^{*}=\frac{n(m^{\prime}-m)}{m^{\prime}n-mn^{\prime}}.

(3.4)

Note that the surface condition
\lambda+\lambda^{*}=1
is then automatically satisfied.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathscr{S}_{m,n}
and
\mathscr{S}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}
be two surfaces with a non empty
intersection, defining a line in the moduli space. There are a countable number of
surfaces
\mathscr{S}_{m^{\prime\prime},n^{\prime\prime}}
intersecting on this line. They are uniquely determined by the relation

\frac{m^{\prime}-m}{n^{\prime}-n}=\frac{m^{\prime\prime}-m^{\prime}}{n^{\prime\prime}-n^{\prime}}.

(3.5)

Proof.

Suppose there is a third surface
\mathscr{S}_{m^{\prime\prime},n^{\prime\prime}}
intersecting on

\mathscr{S}_{m,n}\cap\mathscr{S}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}
. Then equations (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) hold for any choice of two pairs in
\{(m,n),(m^{\prime},n^{\prime}),(m^{\prime\prime},n^{\prime\prime})\}
:

\displaystyle\frac{n^{\prime}-n}{m^{\prime}n-mn^{\prime}}=\frac{n^{\prime\prime}-n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime\prime}n^{\prime}-m^{\prime}n^{\prime\prime}}=\frac{n^{\prime\prime}-n}{m^{\prime\prime}n-mn^{\prime\prime}},

(3.6)

\displaystyle\frac{m^{\prime}+n^{\prime}-m-n}{m^{\prime}n-mn^{\prime}}=\frac{m^{\prime\prime}+n^{\prime\prime}-m^{\prime}-n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime\prime}n^{\prime}-m^{\prime}n^{\prime\prime}}=\frac{m^{\prime\prime}+n^{\prime\prime}-m-n}{m^{\prime\prime}n-mn^{\prime\prime}}.

(3.7)

Dividing term by term the second equation by the first one, one gets

\frac{m^{\prime}-m}{n^{\prime}-n}=\frac{m^{\prime\prime}-m^{\prime}}{n^{\prime\prime}-n^{\prime}}=\frac{m^{\prime\prime}-m}{n^{\prime\prime}-n}.

(3.8)

Thus, relation (
3.5
) is a necessary relation for the surface
\mathscr{S}_{m^{\prime\prime},n^{\prime\prime}}
to exist. Note that this relation implies the second equality in (
3.8
), i.e., assuming (
3.5
) implies all the equalities deduced by circular permutation of the pairs
(m,n)
,
(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})
,
(m^{\prime\prime},n^{\prime\prime})
.

Now writing (
3.5
) as
(m^{\prime}-m)(n^{\prime\prime}-n^{\prime})-(m^{\prime\prime}-m^{\prime})(n^{\prime}-n)=0
and multiplying it by
n^{\prime}
, one finds the first equality of (
3.6
). Multiplying instead by
m^{\prime}
, one finds the first equality of (
3.7
). The other two equalities are found by a circular permutation on the pairs
(m,n)
,
(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})
,
(m^{\prime\prime},n^{\prime\prime})
. Hence equation (
3.5
) is equivalent to (
3.6
)–(
3.7
). As such, it is a necessary and sufficient condition for
\mathscr{S}_{m^{\prime\prime},n^{\prime\prime}}
to exist.

Finally, choosing
m^{\prime\prime}=m^{\prime}+u(m-m^{\prime})
and
n^{\prime\prime}=n^{\prime}+u(n-n^{\prime})
with
u\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, one shows at the same time that (
3.5
) admits solutions, and that there are a countable number of them.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

When non-empty, the intersection of two surfaces
\mathscr{S}_{m,n}
and
\mathscr{S}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}
is a line of abelianity of
\mathscr{S}_{m,n}
if and only if one of the following conditions is satisfied:

\displaystyle(a)\quad\frac{m(n-n^{\prime})}{m^{\prime}n-mn^{\prime}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\qquad\text{or equivalently}\qquad\frac{n(m^{\prime}-m)}{m^{\prime}n-mn^{\prime}}\in{\mathbb{Z}},

(3.9)

\displaystyle(b)\quad\frac{m+n-m^{\prime}-n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}n-mn^{\prime}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad(m+n)(m^{\prime}+n^{\prime})\neq 0,

(3.10)

\displaystyle(c)\quad(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})=\pm(1,-1)\qquad\text{and}\qquad m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}},

\displaystyle(c^{\prime})\quad(m,n)=\pm(1,-1)\qquad\text{and}\qquad m^{\prime},n^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

In case
(a)
, the intersection might not be a line of abelianity of
\mathscr{S}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}
. In cases
(b)
,
(c)
and
(c^{\prime})
, the intersection is also a line of abelianity of
\mathscr{S}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Any line of abelianity on a surface
\mathscr{S}_{m,n}
can be identified with the intersection of a countable number of suitable surfaces
\mathscr{S}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}
.

The proof of these two theorems is postponed until Section
5
.

3.2
Enhanced abelianities

Theorems
3.4
and
3.5
describe generic lines of abelianity on critical surfaces
\mathscr{S}_{m,n}
, i.e., such that the sole characterizing commutation property be
\big[t^{(k)}_{m,n}(z),t^{(k^{\prime})}_{m,n}(w)\big]=0
,
1\leq k,k^{\prime}\leq N
. We have identified several critical surfaces on which stronger commutation properties prevail, which can be overall characterized as “enhanced abelianity”. They are described in the following propositions.

Proposition 3.6
.

(i)

The surface
\mathscr{S}_{1,-1}
corresponds to the critical level
c\!=\!-N
, for which the generators
t^{(k)}_{1,-1}(z)
lie in the extended center of
{\mathcal{A}_{q,p}\big(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}(N)_{c}\big)}
, without any further condition on
q
,
p
.

(ii)

The intersection of
\mathscr{S}_{1,-1}
with any surface

\mathscr{S}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}
, when non-empty, provides an abelianity line for
\mathscr{S}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}
. The set of such lines is dense on the surface
\mathscr{S}_{1,-1}
.

Proposition 3.7
.

(i)

The generators
t_{0,n}^{(k)}(z)
satisfy an abelian algebra on the whole surface
\mathscr{S}_{0,n}
.

(ii)

There are a countable number of surfaces
\mathscr{S}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}
such that the intersection of
\mathscr{S}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}
with
\mathscr{S}_{0,n}
is an abelianity line for
\mathscr{S}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}
. These lines of abelianity form a dense set of lines on the surface
\mathscr{S}_{0,n}
.

(iii)

There exist a countable number of surfaces
\mathscr{S}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}
such that the intersection of
\mathscr{S}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}
with
\mathscr{S}_{0,n}
is not an abelianity line for
\mathscr{S}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}
.

Proposition 3.8
.

(i)

The generators
t_{m,-m}^{(k)}(z)
commute with the Lax operator
L(w)

of the elliptic quantum algebra
{\mathcal{A}_{q,p}\big(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}(N)_{c}\big)}
on the surface
\mathscr{S}_{m,-m}

(
“localized extended center”
)
when
the following conditions hold, see (
2.6
):

1)

m
is odd;

2)

m
and
\lambda
are coprime integers;

3)

m
and
\beta^{\prime}_{0}+1
are coprime integers, where
\beta_{0}
and
\beta^{\prime}_{0}
are the Bézout coefficients such that
\beta_{0}m-\beta^{\prime}_{0}\lambda=1
with
1\leq\beta^{\prime}_{0}\leq m-1
.

Such a submanifold of the surface
\mathscr{S}_{m,-m}
will be called a “super-abelianity line”.

(ii)

The intersection
\mathscr{S}_{m,-m}\cap\mathscr{S}_{1,1}
with
m
odd is a super-abelianity line for
\mathscr{S}_{m,-m}
but it is never an abelianity line for
\mathscr{S}_{1,1}
.

The proof of these propositions is postponed to Section
5
.

Remark 3.9
.

The construction can be extended to the case of the algebra

-matrix

-deformed

-algebras introduced in
[

,

]
.

4
Abelianity lines

We first introduce the following lemma which classifies the different lines of abelianity. This lemma is essential in proving the results exposed in Section
3
.

Lemma 4.1
.

On the surface
\mathscr{S}_{m,n}
, the elliptic nomes
p
and
p^{*}
being parametrized as in (
2.6
) and the central charge given by (
2.7
),
the generators
t_{m,n}^{(k)}(z)
realize an abelian subalgebra in
\mathcal{A}_{q,p}\big(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}(N)_{c}\big)
if one of the following conditions is satisfied:

1.

The parameters
\lambda
and
\lambda^{*}
take integer values.

2.

The parameters
\lambda
and
\lambda^{*}
obey the relations

\frac{\lambda}{m}-\frac{\lambda^{*}}{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\frac{m+n}{d}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, where
d
is the denominator of the irreducible fraction of
\frac{\lambda}{m}

(
or equivalently
\frac{\lambda^{*}}{n})
.

When
N>2
, these conditions are necessary and sufficient conditions.

The proof of this lemma is given in Section
4.1
. Remark that the first part of condition 2 amounts to say that the central charge is an integer proportional to the critical value
-N
, see equation (
2.7
).
For completeness, we solved the condition
(2)
of Lemma
4.1
:

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
be Bézout coefficients satisfying
\ell m+\ell^{\prime}n=g
, where
g=\gcd(m,n)
.
Then, the parameters
\lambda
and
\lambda^{*}
solutions to the condition
(2)
of Lemma
4.1
are given by

\frac{\lambda}{m}=\gamma^{\prime}\ell+\frac{\gamma}{d}+\frac{kn}{g}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{\lambda^{*}}{n}=\gamma^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}+\frac{\gamma}{d}-\frac{km}{g},\qquad k\in{\mathbb{Z}},

where
d
is a divisor of
m+n
such that
\frac{m+n}{d}
is coprime with
g
, and

(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma)
are the Bézout coefficients solution of
\gamma^{\prime}g+\gamma\frac{m+n}{d}=1
. The integer
\gamma
has to be coprime with
d
and such that
0<\gamma<d
.

The proof of this lemma is given in Section
4.2
.

4.1
Proof of Lemma
4.1

Before going into the details, let us stress that we restrict ourselves to a framework where
cancellations between the numerator and the denominator of the exchange function

\mathcal{Y}_{m,n}
are done through the functions
\mathcal{U}
as a whole. Indeed, due to the explicit form of
\mathcal{U}
, it seems very hard to obtain cancellation in a different way, even when dealing with some “magic” simplifications among elliptic functions. Remark however that for
N=2
, these ’magic’ simplifications may occur, because the shift
q^{2}
in the definition of
\mathcal{U}
, see (
2.5
), coincide with
q^{N}
, the half-period of the
\theta
functions. Thus, the proof done here is only a sufficient condition when
N=2
.

We start with the expression (
2.4
) of the function
\mathcal{Y}_{m,n}
with the parametrization (
2.6
), where
\lambda
,
\lambda^{*}
at this stage are complex numbers. The conditions for which
\mathcal{Y}_{m,n}(x)=1
are determined by looking at the different ways the functions
\mathcal{U}
entering in (
2.4
) may simplify each other.
The simplification of the
\mathcal{U}
functions can be done essentially in two distinct ways:

(1)

The functions
\mathcal{U}\big(\big({-}p^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{\ell}x\big)
in the numerator cancel the functions
\mathcal{U}\big(\big({-}p^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{\ell^{\prime}}x\big)
in the denominator, and similarly for the functions
\mathcal{U}\big(\big({-}p^{*\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{\ell}x\big)
. In other words, one assumes that no “cross-cancellations” occur between
p
and
p^{*}
shifted-terms.
This case corresponds to the study done in
[
1
,
3
]
and reminded in Proposition
2.3
.

(2)

There exist at least one pair
(\ell,\ell^{\prime})
of indices such that the function
\mathcal{U}\big(\big({-}p^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{\ell}x\big)
simplifies with the function
\mathcal{U}\big(\big({-}p^{*\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{\ell^{\prime}}x\big)
. Taking into account the
q^{N}
-periodicity of the function
\mathcal{U}(x)
, this leads to

\frac{\lambda\ell}{m}-\frac{\lambda^{*}\ell^{\prime}}{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Since the surface condition reads
\lambda+\lambda^{*}=1
, this last equation implies
\lambda,\lambda^{*}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
.

We focus here on case (2), since case (1) was dealt with in
[
1
]
.
The parameters
\lambda
,
\lambda^{*}
being then rational numbers, one sets

\frac{\lambda}{m}=\frac{a}{d}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{\lambda^{*}}{n}=\frac{b}{d^{\prime}},

where
(a,d)
and
(b,d^{\prime})
are pairs of coprime numbers with
d,d^{\prime}>0
.

Step 1:
Writing
|m|=ds+\mu
and
|n|=d^{\prime}s^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime}
with
0<\mu<d
and
0<\mu^{\prime}<d^{\prime}
, allows one to obtain a first simplification of the function
\mathcal{Y}_{m,n}(x)
(note that the values
\mu=0
,
\mu^{\prime}=0
, lead to
\lambda,\lambda^{*}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
which returns to case 1).
Using the
q^{N}
-periodicity of the function
\mathcal{U}
, one gets

\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{\ell=1}^{|m|}\mathcal{U}\big(\big({-}p^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{-\ell}x\big)}{\displaystyle\prod_{\ell=1}^{|m|-1}\mathcal{U}\big(\big({-}p^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{\ell}x\big)}=\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{\mu}\mathcal{U}\big(q^{Naj/d}x\big)}{\displaystyle\prod_{j^{\prime}=d-\mu+1}^{d-1}\mathcal{U}\big(q^{Naj^{\prime}/d}x\big)}.

Depending whether
\mu\leq d-\mu
or
\mu\geq d-\mu
, additional simplications may occur. In any case, introducing
\bar{\mu}=\inf(\mu,d-\mu)\leq d/2
, one checks that

\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{\mu}\mathcal{U}\big(q^{Naj/d}x\big)}{\displaystyle\prod_{j^{\prime}=d-\mu+1}^{d-1}\mathcal{U}\big(q^{Naj^{\prime}/d}x\big)}=\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{\bar{\mu}}\mathcal{U}\big(q^{Naj/d}x\big)}{\displaystyle\prod_{j^{\prime}=d-\bar{\mu}+1}^{d-1}\mathcal{U}\big(q^{Naj^{\prime}/d}x\big)}.

(4.1)

Noting that the maximal value of
|j-j^{\prime}|
is
d-2
and the minimal value is 1 in the r.h.s. of equation (
4.1
),
a(j-j^{\prime})/d
cannot be an integer, hence no further simplification occurs in the r.h.s. of equation (
4.1
).

The product of the
\mathcal{U}\big(\big({-}p^{*\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{-\ell}x\big)
functions is processed in a similar way. Therefore, one obtains

\mathcal{Y}_{m,n}(x)=\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{\bar{\mu}}\mathcal{U}\big(q^{Naj/d}x\big)}{\displaystyle\prod_{j=d-\bar{\mu}+1}^{d-1}\mathcal{U}\big(q^{Naj/d}x\big)}\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{j^{\prime}=d-\bar{\mu}^{\prime}+1}^{d-1}\mathcal{U}\big(q^{Nbj^{\prime}/d^{\prime}}x\big)}{\displaystyle\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{\bar{\mu}^{\prime}}\mathcal{U}\big(q^{Nbj^{\prime}/d^{\prime}}x\big)}.

(4.2)

No further simplification can occur “vertically”, hence it is necessary, for the function
\mathcal{Y}_{m,n}(x)
to be equal to one, that the product
\prod\limits_{j=1}^{\bar{\mu}}\mathcal{U}\big(q^{Naj/d}x\big)
simplifies the product
\prod\limits_{j^{\prime}=1}^{\bar{\mu}^{\prime}}\mathcal{U}\big(q^{Nbj^{\prime}/d^{\prime}}x\big)
, which implies
\bar{\mu}=\bar{\mu}^{\prime}
.

Let
d=\alpha\delta
,
d^{\prime}=\alpha\delta^{\prime}
where
\delta,\delta^{\prime}
are coprimes numbers. The term
\mathcal{U}\big(q^{Na/d}x\big)
has to simplify some term
\mathcal{U}\big(q^{Nbk/d^{\prime}}x\big)
, one can write
z=a/d-bk/d^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for a certain
1\leq k\leq\bar{\mu}
, hence
a\delta^{\prime}=\delta(bk+\alpha z\delta^{\prime})
.
This last equation implies
\delta=1
since
(a,\delta)
and
(\delta^{\prime},\delta)
are pairs of coprimes numbers. Similarly,
z^{\prime}=b/d^{\prime}-ak^{\prime}/d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for a certain
1\leq k^{\prime}\leq\bar{\mu}
, which leads to
\delta^{\prime}=1
. One concludes that
d=d^{\prime}
.

Step 2:
We look now at the possible cross-simplifications in (
4.2
) (“matching condition”).
A simplification occurs whenever the argument of a
\mathcal{U}
function in the upper left product matches the argument of a
\mathcal{U}
function in the lower right product up to a power of
q^{N}
due to the
q^{N}
-periodicity of
\mathcal{U}
.
This amounts to determine the possible permutations
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}
such that

\frac{aj}{d}-\frac{b\sigma(j)}{d}\in{\mathbb{Z}},\qquad\forall j=1,\dots,\bar{\mu}.

(4.3)

Imposing (
4.3
) for
j=i
and
j=1
implies
(\sigma(i)-i\sigma(1))\frac{b}{d}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Hence, for all
i=2,\dots,\bar{\mu}
,
d
is a divisor of
\sigma(i)-i\sigma(1)
since
b
and
d
are coprime integers.
Suppose that, for some
i
,
\sigma(i)-i\sigma(1)=0
.
One has
1\leq\sigma(j)\leq\bar{\mu}
for all
j
, hence one gets
|\sigma(i+1)-(i+1)\sigma(1)|=|\sigma(i+1)-\sigma(i)-\sigma(1)|<2\bar{\mu}\leq d
.
Therefore
d
cannot divide
\sigma(i+1)-(i+1)\sigma(1)
unless
\sigma(i+1)-(i+1)\sigma(1)=0
.
Considering now the case
i=2
, one has
|\sigma(2)-2\sigma(1)|<2\bar{\mu}\leq d
, hence
d
cannot divide
\sigma(2)-2\sigma(1)
unless
\sigma(2)-2\sigma(1)=0
.
Finally, taking
i=\bar{\mu}
, the recurrence leads to
\sigma(\bar{\mu})-\bar{\mu}\sigma(1)=0
. Since
\sigma(\bar{\mu})\leq\bar{\mu}
, one has
\sigma(1)=1
.
It follows that the only possible choice of
\sigma
is
\sigma(j)=j
for all
j=1,\dots,\bar{\mu}
, i.e., the identity.

The only consistent matching condition is thus simply
\frac{a-b}{d}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
complementing the relations
d=d^{\prime}
and
\bar{\mu}=\bar{\mu}^{\prime}
found at step 1.

Let us now further our analysis of these abelianity conditions.
The results obtained in step 1 show that one has to deal with two cases, depending on the relative positions of
\mu
and
d-\mu
and of
\mu^{\prime}
and
d-\mu^{\prime}
on the one hand, and on the signs of the two integers
m
and
n
on the other hand, namely:

case (I)

\displaystyle\quad m=d\bar{s}\pm\bar{\mu}\qquad\text{and}\qquad n=d\bar{s}^{\prime}\mp\bar{\mu}(\bar{s},\bar{s}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}})\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac{m+n}{d}\in{\mathbb{Z}},

(4.4)

case (II)

\displaystyle\quad m=d\bar{s}\pm\bar{\mu}\qquad\text{and}\qquad n=d\bar{s}^{\prime}\pm\bar{\mu}(\bar{s},\bar{s}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}})\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac{m-n}{d}\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

(4.5)

From the matching condition, one can set
a=\bar{a}d+\gamma
and
b=\bar{b}d+\gamma
where
\bar{a},\bar{b}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
0<\gamma<d
and
(\gamma,d)
are coprime integers (the last two conditions ensure that
(a,d)
and
(b,d)
are pairs of coprime integers). The surface condition
\lambda+\lambda^{*}=1
then takes the form

\lambda+\lambda^{*}=\bar{a}m+\bar{b}n+\frac{\gamma}{d}(m+n)=1.

(4.6)

When
m+n=0
, equation (
4.6
) reads
(\bar{a}-\bar{b})m=1
, which cannot be satisfied except in the very particular cases

m=-n=\pm 1
. These cases are dealt with by Proposition
3.6
and correspond to an extended centrality condition.

We now consider
m+n\neq 0
. The above hypotheses imply that
d
is a divisor of
m+n
, irrespective to case (I) or (II).

In case (II),
d
is a divisor of both
m+n
and
m-n
, it is therefore a divisor of
2m
, hence
2\bar{\mu}/d
is an integer. The upper bound
\bar{\mu}\leq d
then implies
\bar{\mu}=d/2
, which shows that
d
should be even in that case. Then,
n=d\bar{s}^{\prime}\pm d/2
can be rewritten as
n=d(\bar{s}^{\prime}\pm 1)\mp d/2
, and the case (II) appears as a subcase of case (I).

Thus, we have proved that when
\lambda,\lambda^{*}
are not integers, the abelianity property is equivalent to condition 2. This ends the proof of Lemma
4.1
.

4.2
Proof of Lemma
4.2

Let us now work out the condition (2) given in Lemma
4.1
.

Let
g=\gcd(m,n)
,
m=\bar{m}g
,
n=\bar{n}g
, where
\bar{m},\bar{n}
are coprime numbers, and similarly, set
u=\gcd(g,d)
,
d=\bar{d}u
with
g=\bar{g}u
. Equation (
4.6
) then writes

\lambda+\lambda^{*}=\bar{g}\left(u\bar{a}\bar{m}+u\bar{b}\bar{n}+\frac{\gamma}{\bar{d}}(\bar{m}+\bar{n})\right)=1,

(4.7)

which implies
\bar{g}=1
(note that
\bar{d}
is a divisor of
\bar{m}+\bar{n}
since
d
is a divisor of
m+n
and
\bar{d}
,
\bar{g}
are coprime numbers).
Hence, recalling (
4.4
), equation (
4.7
) takes the form

g(\bar{a}\bar{m}+\bar{b}\bar{n})+\gamma(\bar{s}+\bar{s}^{\prime})\allowbreak=1
,
showing that
g
and
\bar{s}+\bar{s}^{\prime}
shall be coprime numbers with Bézout coefficients
\gamma^{\prime}=\bar{a}\bar{m}+\bar{b}\bar{n}
and
\gamma
.

The equation
\gamma^{\prime}g+\gamma(\bar{s}+\bar{s}^{\prime})=1
is therefore a constraint equation.
Different cases may arise:

i)

The integers
g=\gcd(m,n)
and
\bar{s}+\bar{s}^{\prime}=(m+n)/d
are
not
coprime numbers. There is no solution, in other words no “cross-cancellation” can occur.

ii)

The integers
g=\gcd(m,n)
and
\bar{s}+\bar{s}^{\prime}=(m+n)/d
are coprime numbers.

In that case, note that one can write in general

m+n=g\prod_{i}d_{i}\prod_{j}d^{\prime}_{j},

where the
d_{i}
’s and
d^{\prime}_{j}
’s are prime integers, the
d_{i}
’s are divisors of
g
, and the
d^{\prime}_{j}
’s are coprimes with
g
. Then the admissible divisors of
m+n
, i.e., the divisors
d
such that
g=\gcd(m,n)
and
(m+n)/d
are coprime numbers, are of the form

d=g\prod_{i}d_{i}\prod_{j^{\prime}}d^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}},

where the set of indices
j^{\prime}
is some subset of the set of indices
j
.

The conditions on
\gamma
and
d
given above must now be examined on a case-by-case basis:

–

Either there
does not
exist Bézout coefficients
\gamma
satisfying
0<\gamma<d
with
\gamma
,
d
coprime numbers. The same negative conclusion holds.

–

Or such Bézout coefficients exist, and some “cross-cancellations” occur.

Consider some
\gamma
with the required properties and denote by
\gamma^{\prime}
the other Bézout coefficient.
If
(\ell,\ell^{\prime})
are the Bézout coefficients of
\bar{m}
and
\bar{n}
,
\ell\bar{m}+\ell^{\prime}\bar{n}=1
, the solution of
\gamma^{\prime}=\bar{a}\bar{m}+\bar{b}\bar{n}
is then given by

\bar{a}=\gamma^{\prime}\ell+k\bar{n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\bar{b}=\gamma^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}-k\bar{m},

with
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Finally, one obtains

\frac{\lambda}{m}=\gamma^{\prime}\ell+\frac{\gamma}{d}+k\frac{n}{g}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{\lambda^{*}}{n}=\gamma^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}+\frac{\gamma}{d}-k\frac{m}{g}.

This ends the proof of Lemma
4.2
.

Remark that when
m
and
n
are coprimes (
g=1
), the relation
\gamma^{\prime}g+\gamma(\bar{s}+\bar{s}^{\prime})=1
always admits a solution. In fact, it allows to eliminate
\gamma^{\prime}
and simplifies the expression of
\lambda
.

Proof of Theorem

Proof of Proposition

7
Conclusion

The results we have obtained on abelianity lines, their characterization as intersection of critical surfaces, and their associated Poisson structures, suggest some further lines of investigation. Let us propose a few such directions.

First, since we are dealing with intersections of critical surfaces, we have several types of
\mathcal{W}^{(m,n)}_{pqc}(N)
algebras defined simultaneously on these lines. Since the abelianity condition is not always symmetric, it is clear that these algebras cannot be always identical. However, we have proved that each intersection corresponds to a countable number of surfaces, and thus a countable number of
\mathcal{W}^{(m,n)}_{pqc}(N)
algebras. Then, it is likely that some of them may coincide, and an analysis on the number of truly different algebras on each line is certainly worth completing. In the same way, when the intersection defines an abelianity line for both surfaces, the corresponding algebras are obviously isomorphic, but it would be interesting to look at the realizations in
{\mathcal{A}_{q,p}\big(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}(N)_{c}\big)}
, and see if the generators are indeed identical.

We have derived several sets of Poisson bracket structures, characterized as surface-dependent linear combinations of solely line-dependent elliptic functions. As is always the case
[
1
,
3
]
, this abstract derivation provides only the leading spin terms of the
q
-
W_{N}
Poisson algebra, and their consistent quantizations along the critical surfaces. Only an explicit realization, e.g., by vertex operators will provide the lower spin and central extension terms (see also
[
4
]
for a systematic resolution of coboundary conditions). It must be emphasized indeed that a number of realizations of DVA by
q
-bosonized vertex operators, derived from
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}(2))
generators, have been proposed. The earliest ones were constructed as soon as the DVA algebra itself
[
16
]
. Very recently some new constructions were achieved
[
5
]
. The question here is to find the suitable deformation of free boson algebra yielding as leading order of the exchange structure our abstract DVA and more generally
q
-
W_{N}
algebras. It is amusing to note that a realization of a distinct DVA algebra, conjectured in
[
9
]
was given directly
[
15
]
in terms of VO of the elliptic quantum algebra
{\mathcal{A}_{q,p}\big(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}(2)_{c}\big)}
for some particular values of
\ln p/\ln q
. A curious connection thus arises again between elliptic quantum algebras and DVA.

The meaning of the integer conditions in Theorem
3.4
remains to be investigated. The equation of the critical surface is identified, in terms of coordinates
\ln p
,
\ln p^{*}
,
2N\ln q
, with a condition of linearity with the directing vector
(m,n,1)
defining the orthogonal direction to the critical plane. This vector must be in fact understood as a projective object with suitable integer conditions; in particular it belongs to the subspace (or “manifold chart” if we were not dealing with integers) characterized by a non zero third component
x_{3}
. The conditions in Lemma
3.1
mean that two surfaces intersect iff the vector product of their respective directing vectors has three non-zero components. In particular, it belongs to the consistent chart
x_{3}\neq 0
of the projective 3d vector space. It remains to see whether a geometric interpretation along these lines may then exist for the abelianity conditions.

Still in the light of Theorem
3.4
, it would be interesting to understand the algebraic structure occurring on intersection of surfaces, when the abelianity conditions are not fulfilled. Obviously, the Poisson structure introduced in the abelian case cannot be reproduced outside abelianity. However, a more general structure may arise that would generalize the notion of symplectic structure.
One could think for instance of a trace brackets structure, or a Poisson vertex algebra.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
\alpha\geq 1
. Consider the following lattice minimization problem

\displaystyle\min_{L}\sum_{\mathbb{P}\in L,|L|=1}(|\mathbb{P}|^{2}-\frac{b}{\alpha})e^{-\pi\alpha|\mathbb{P}|^{2}}.

There exists

(

•

, the minimizer of the lattice energy functional is
e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}}
, which corresponds to the hexagonal lattice;

•

if
b>b_{c}
, the minimizer of the lattice energy functional does not exists.

Remark 1.2

\displaystyle

\displaystyle(1):\hbox{Differences of Gaussian potentials},\;f(r^{2})=e^{-\pi\alpha r^{2}}-be^{-\pi a\alpha r^{2}},\;\;\alpha\geq 1,a>1,b\leq\sqrt{a},\;\;

(1.8)

\displaystyle\hbox{the minimizer is hexagonal lattice};

\displaystyle(2):\hbox{Product of Polynomial and Gaussian},\;f(r^{2})=(r^{2}-\frac{b}{\alpha})e^{-\pi\alpha r^{2}},\;\;\alpha\geq 1,b\leq\frac{1}{2\pi},

\displaystyle\hbox{the minimizer is hexagonal lattice}.

Next we need some delicate analysis of the Jacobi theta function which is defined as

\displaystyle\vartheta_{J}(z;\tau):=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i\pi n^{2}\tau+2\pi inz}.

The classical one-dimensional theta function is given by

\displaystyle\vartheta(X;Y):=\vartheta_{J}(Y;iX)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\pi n^{2}X}e^{2n\pi iY}.

(2.5)

By the Poisson summation formula, it holds that

\displaystyle\vartheta(X;Y)=X^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\pi(n-Y)^{2}}{X}}.

(2.6)

To estimate bounds of quotients of derivatives of
\vartheta(X:Y)

\displaystyle\mu(X):=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n^{2}e^{-\pi(n^{2}-1)X},\;\;\nu(X):=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n^{4}e^{-\pi(n^{2}-1)X}.

(2.7)

We shall state a lemma which is variant of Lemmas

Lemma 2.3

•

\displaystyle|\frac{\vartheta_{Y}(X;kY)}{\vartheta_{Y}(X;Y)}|\leq k\cdot\frac{1+\mu(X)}{1-\mu(X)}.

•

X<\frac{\pi}{\pi+2}
and any
Y>0
,

\displaystyle|\frac{\vartheta_{Y}(X;kY)}{\vartheta_{Y}(X;Y)}|\leq k\cdot\frac{1}{\pi}e^{\frac{\pi}{4X}}.

•

Proof.

Lemma 2.7
.

\displaystyle|\frac{1}{\sin(2\pi Y)}\big(\frac{\sin(2n\pi Y)}{\sin(2\pi Y)}\big)^{\prime}|\leq C(n),\;\;\hbox{for}\;\;Y\in{\mathbb{R}},

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin(2n\pi Y)\cos(2\pi Y)+\cos(2n\pi Y)\sin(2\pi Y)}{\sin(2\pi Y)}

It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sin(2\pi Y)}\big(\frac{\sin(2(n+1)\pi Y)}{\sin(2\pi Y)}\big)^{\prime}=\cos(2\pi Y)\cdot\frac{1}{\sin(2\pi Y)}\big(\frac{\sin(2n\pi Y)}{\sin(2\pi Y)}\big)^{\prime}-2(n+1)\pi\frac{\sin(2n\pi Y)}{\sin(2\pi Y)}.

(2.26)

Here the inequality

is used.

\displaystyle a_{n}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(a_{k+1}-a_{k})+a_{1}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}2\pi(k+k^{2})=\frac{2\pi}{3}(n-1)n(n+1),

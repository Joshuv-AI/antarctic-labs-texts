Tropical computations for toric intersection theory in Macaulay2
Alessio Borzì

Abstract
We present the Macaulay2 package TropicalToric.m2 for toric intersection theory computations using tropical geometry.



1
Introduction

Toric varieties are ubiquitous in algebraic geometry. Their intersection theory was first studied in Fulton and Sturmfels
[
fulton1997toricintersection
]
, and it has many applications in different contexts, including: wonderful and tropical compactifications
[
tevelev2007compactification
,
deconcini1995wonderful
,
feichtner2004chow
]
, birational geometry
[
gibeny2010equations
,
gibeny2012bounds
,
castravet2020blown
]
, tropical geometry
[
katz2011realization
]
, tropical intersection theory
[
katz2009toolkit
,
osserman2013lifting
,
allerman2010tropicalintersection
,
katz2012tropicalfromtoric
,
shaw2013matroidal
]
and combinatorial Hodge theory
[
huh2012milnor
,
huhkatz
,
adiprasito2018hodge
]
.

In a certain way, intersection classes of a toric variety with fan
\Sigma
can be thought in terms of balanced subfans of
\Sigma
, also referred as
Minkowski weights
(see
[
fulton1997toricintersection
]
or
[
maclagan2015tropical
, Theorem 6.7.5]
). From the Structure Theorem of Tropical Geometry
[
maclagan2015tropical
, Theorem 3.3.5]
we know that the tropicalization of a subvariety of a torus
Y\subseteq T^{n}
is a balanced fan. A surprising connection between tropical and toric geometry is that the tropicalization of
Y
is the balanced fan corresponding to the intersection class of the closure of
Y
inside an “enough refined” toric variety (see Theorem
for a more precise statement). This fact allows us to compute toric intersection classes starting from the data of the tropicalization.

We present a new package
TropicalToric.m2
for Macaulay2
[
M2
]
available at the following link.

https://github.com/AlessioBorzi/TropicalToric

The package implements toric cycles and intersection products on simplicial toric varieties (Section
2
), and, following the ideas outlined above, allows us to compute the intersection class of an irreducible subvariety of a simplicial toric variety not contained in the toric boundary, from the data of its tropicalization (Section
). The tropicalization is performed with the use of the Macaulay2 package
Tropical.m2

[
amendola2017computing
]
. Further, we present some applications to the intersection theory of wonderful compactifications and the moduli space
\overline{M}_{0,n}
, and illustrate an example in a multiprojective space using a theorem of Huh and Katz
[
huhkatz
]
about characteristic polynomials of realizable matroids.

2
Toric intersection theory

In this section, we review the basics of toric intersection theory, for more information see
[
fulton1997toricintersection
]
,
[
cox2011toric
, Section 12.5]
or
[
maclagan2015tropical
, Section 6.7]
. In addition, we showcase how it is implemented in the package.

Let
X_{\Sigma}
be a smooth complete toric variety of dimension
n
with fan
\Sigma
. We denote by
\Sigma(k)
the cones of
\Sigma
of dimension
k
, with
Z^{k}(X_{\Sigma})=Z_{n-k}(X_{\Sigma})
the group of codimension-
k
cycles and with
A^{k}(X_{\Sigma})=A_{n-k}(X_{\Sigma})
the codimension-
k
Chow group, that is, the group of codimension-
k
cycles modulo rational equivalence. The codimension-
k
Chow group
A^{k}(X_{\Sigma})
is generated by the set
\{[V(\sigma)]:\sigma\in\Sigma(k)\}
of classes of orbit closures of codimension-
k
, and the relations in each Chow group can be described in an explicit way, see
[
fulton1997toricintersection
, Proposition 2.1]
.

We can show (see
[
fulton1998intersection
, Chapter 8]
) that there is an intersection product
A^{k}(X_{\Sigma})\times A^{r}(X_{\Sigma})\rightarrow A^{k+r}(X_{\Sigma})
that makes
A^{*}(X_{\Sigma})=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}A^{k}(X_{\Sigma})
into a graded ring, called the
Chow ring
of
X_{\Sigma}
. If we now assume that
X_{\Sigma}
is just complete and simplicial, then the intersection product can be defined on rational cycles, making
A^{*}(X_{\Sigma})_{\mathbb{Q}}=A^{*}(X_{\Sigma})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
into a graded ring. The structure of the Chow ring has an explicit description, see for instance
[
maclagan2015tropical
, Theorem 6.7.1]
.

Our Macaulay2 package implements toric cycles and the intersection product as in
[
cox2011toric
, Lemma 12.5.2]
.

\par\par\@@numbered@section{section}{toc}{Tropical computations}
\par In this section we describe and showcase the algorithm implemented in the main function of the package \verb|classFromTropical| that computes the intersection class of an irreducible subvariety of a simplicial toric variety from its tropicalization.
\par The algorithm is mainly based on the following result, that appears in various versions in the literature, see for instance {\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{katz2011realization}{}{}, Lemma 2.3]}, \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{katz2009toolkit}{}{}, Section 9]} or \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{maclagan2015tropical}{}{}, Theorem 6.7.7]}}.
\par\begin{theorem}Let $Y$ be a subvariety of the algebraic torus $T^{n}$ and let $\overline{Y}$ be its closure in a toric variety $X_{\Sigma}$ such that $|\Sigma|=\operatorname{trop}(Y)$ and $\Sigma$ is simplicial. Let $\Sigma^{\prime}$ be a completion of the fan $\Sigma$ and let $i:X_{\Sigma}\rightarrow X_{\Sigma^{\prime}}$ be the induced inclusion. Then, for every maximal cone $\sigma$ in $\Sigma$ we have
$$m(\sigma)=\deg\big([i_{*}(\overline{Y})]\cdot[V(\sigma)]\big),$$where $m(\sigma)$ is the multiplicity of $\sigma$ in $\operatorname{trop}(Y)$.
\end{theorem}
\par\par Now let $Y$ be an irreducible $k$-dimensional subvariety of an $n$-dimensional simplicial toric variety $X_{\Sigma}$, and suppose that $Y\cap T^{n}\neq\emptyset$. Note that in this setting we cannot directly apply Theorem \ref{thm:the theorem of the algorithm} since $\operatorname{trop}(Y)$ is not necessarily a subfan of $\Sigma$.
\par In order to compute the class of $Y$ in the Chow ring of $X_{\Sigma}$, we proceed as follows. First, let $\Sigma^{\prime}$ be a completion of $\Sigma$ and $i:X_{\Sigma}\rightarrow X_{\Sigma^{\prime}}$ be the induced inclusion. Now let $\widetilde{\Sigma}$ be a refinement of $\Sigma^{\prime}$ such that it contains a subfan with support the tropicalization of $Y\cap T^{n}$, and let $\pi:X_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}\rightarrow X_{\Sigma^{\prime}}$ be the induced toric map. From \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{fulton1997toricintersection}{}{}, Proposition 2.4]} we have an isomorphism $A_{k}(X_{\Sigma^{\prime}})\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(A^{k}(X_{\Sigma^{\prime}}),\mathbb{Z})$ mapping a class $[Z]$ to the homomorphism $[Z^{\prime}]\mapsto\deg([Z]\cdot[Z^{\prime}])$. Therefore, in order to compute the class $[Y]\in A_{k}(X_{\Sigma})$, it is enough to compute the intersection numbers $\deg([i_{*}(Y)]\cdot[V(\sigma)])$ for every $\sigma\in\Sigma^{\prime}(k)$, as the classes $[V(\sigma)]$ generate $A^{k}(X_{\Sigma^{\prime}})$. Let $Y^{\prime}$ be the strict transform of $Y$ in $X_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}$. From the projection formula \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{fulton1998intersection}{}{}, Proposition 2.3 (c)]}, we have
$$[i_{*}(Y)]\cdot[V(\sigma)]=\pi_{*}([Y^{\prime}])\cdot[V(\sigma)]=\pi_{*}\big([Y^{\prime}]\cdot\pi^{*}([V(\sigma)])\big)$$
from which it follows $\deg\big([i_{*}(Y)]\cdot[V(\sigma)]\big)=\deg\big([Y^{\prime}]\cdot\pi^{*}([V(\sigma)])\big)$. These last intersection numbers can be computed from the tropicalization of $Y\cap T^{n}$ by using Theorem \ref{thm:the theorem of the algorithm}, since $\deg\big([Y^{\prime}]\cdot[V(\sigma^{\prime})]\big)$ is the multiplicity of the cone $\sigma^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\Sigma}(k)$ in the tropicalization of $Y\cap T^{n}$.
\par The function \verb|classFromTropical| performs the above algorithm to compute a toric cycle rationally equivalent to a given irreducible subvariety $Y$ of a simplicial toric variety $X_{\Sigma}$. The input of the function consists of the toric variety $X_{\Sigma}$ and the ideal $I$ of $Y\cap T^{n}$ of the Laurent ring of $T^{n}$. Since Laurent rings are not implemented in Macaulay2, the actual input will be instead the saturation of $I$ with respect to the product of the variables in the polynomial ring:
\begin{verbatim}
i2 : X = toricProjectiveSpace 2;
i3 : R = QQ[x,y];
i4 : I = ideal(x+y+1);
i5 : classFromTropical(X,I)
o5 = X
{0}
o5 : ToricCycle on X
i6 : J = ideal(x*y + x + y);
i7 : classFromTropical(X,J)
o7 = 2*X
{0}
o7 : ToricCycle on X
Ψ
\end{verbatim}
\noindent The function \verb|classFromTropicalCox| allows us to input the ideal of $Y$ in the Cox ring of $X_{\Sigma}$:
\par\begin{verbatim}
i8 : R = ring X;
i9 : I = ideal(R_0+R_1+R_2);
i10 : classFromTropicalCox(X,I)
o10 = X
{0}
o10 : ToricCycle on X
Ψ
\end{verbatim}

\par\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Wonderful compactifications}
\par\par\par Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an essential hyperplane arrangement of $n+1$ hyperplanes in $\mathbb{P}^{d}$. The intersection lattice $\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A})$ of $\mathcal{A}$ is isomorphic to the lattice of flats of the underlying matroid $M$ of $\mathcal{A}$ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{stanley2004introduction}{}{}, Proposition 3.6]}. Fix a building set $\mathcal{G}$ of the lattice of flats of $M$ (see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{feichtner2004chow}{}{}, Section 2]}), let $\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}/\mathbb{R}\textbf{1}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{n}$ be the Bergman fan of $M$ with respect to $\mathcal{G}$ (see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{maclagan2015tropical}{}{}, Chapter 4]}) and let $X_{\Sigma}$ be its associated toric variety. From \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{maclagan2015tropical}{}{}, Proposition 4.1.1]} the hyperplane arrangement complement $Y=\mathbb{P}^{d}\setminus\cup\mathcal{A}$ is naturally isomorphic to a linear subspace of the algebraic torus $T^{n}$. Thus we can embed $Y$ inside the toric variety $X_{\Sigma}$ and consider its closure $\overline{Y}$. This compactification coincides with the so-called De Concini-Procesi \emph{wonderful compactification} \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{deconcini1995wonderful}{}{}]}, with respect to the building set $\mathcal{G}$ (see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{tevelev2007compactification}{}{}, Section 4]}). The next result follows from \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{deconcini1995wonderful}{}{}, Theorem 3.2]} (see also \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{feichtner2005deconciniprocesi}{}{}, Definition 2.3]}).\par\begin{proposition}Let $X_{1},\dots,X_{t}$ be a linear extension of the opposite order of $\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A})$. The wonderful compactification $\overline{Y}$ is the result of successively blowing up $\mathbb{P}^{d}$ at (the strict transforms of) $X_{1},\dots,X_{t}$.
\end{proposition}
\par In \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{feichtner2004chow}{}{}]} Feichtner and Yuzvinsky showed that the cohomology of $\overline{Y}$ agrees with that of $X_{\Sigma}$. Since both varieties are smooth, the Chow ring is the same object as the cohomology ring, and we obtain the following result.
\par\begin{theorem}Let $\overline{Y}$ be a wonderful compactification of a hyperplane arrangement $\mathcal{A}$ with respect to a building set $\mathcal{G}$, and let $\Sigma$ be the associated Bergman fan. Then
$$A^{*}(\overline{Y})\simeq A^{*}(X_{\Sigma}).$$\end{theorem}
\par The above theorem allows us to view the intersection classes of a wonderful compactification as intersection classes of the associated toric variety. Thus, we can use our package to perform intersection theory computations on wonderful compactifications.

Let $\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{L}(M)\setminus\{\emptyset\}$ be the maximal building set of $\mathcal{L}(M)$. Then, from Proposition \ref{prop:compactification from blow-up}, the wonderful compactification $\overline{Y}$ of the complement $Y=\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus\cup\mathcal{A}$ with respect to $\mathcal{G}$ is the blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^{2}$ at the points $P_{1},P_{2},P_{3},P_{4}$.
In particular $\overline{Y}$ is a smooth projective surface, all Weil divisors are Cartier \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{hartshorne1977algebraicgeometry}{}{}, Proposition II.6.11]}, and the class group is isomorphic to the Picard group \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{hartshorne1977algebraicgeometry}{}{}, Corollary II.6.16]}. From \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{hartshorne1977algebraicgeometry}{}{}, Proposition V.3.2]}, the Picard group of $\overline{Y}$ has a basis given by
\begin{equation}\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{Y})=\langle[H],[E_{1}],\dots,[E_{t}]\rangle,\end{equation}where $[H]$ is the class of the strict transform $H$ of a general line in $\mathbb{P}^{2}$, and $[E_{i}]$ is the class of the exceptional divisor $E_{i}$ of the blow-up at $P_{i}$.

\par\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{The moduli space $\overline{M}_{0,n}$}
\par The Deligne-Mumford compactification of the moduli space $M_{0,n}$ can be realized as a wonderful compactification (see for instance \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{maclagan2015tropical}{}{}, Example 6.7.16]}). Therefore, we can apply to $\overline{M}_{0,n}$ the machinery described in the previous section. As an application, we compute one of the $15$ Keel-Vermeire divisors of $\overline{M}_{0,6}$, using one of the equations listed in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{guillen2017presentation}{}{}, Table 2]}. These divisors, found independently by Keel and Vermeire \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{vermeire2002counterexample}{}{}]}, were the first example of an effective divisor of $\overline{M}_{0,n}$ which class lies outside the cone generated by the classes of the boundary divisors, answering in the negative to a conjecture of Fulton (see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{keel2013contractible}{}{}]}).
\par\begin{verbatim}
i2 : R = QQ[x_0..x_8];

i4 : X = normalToricVariety fan tropicalVariety I;
i5 : f = x_0*x_1-x_2*x_3;
i6 : D = classWonderfulCompactification(X,I,f);
i7 : D = toricDivisorFromCycle(D)
o7 = X - X - 2*X + X + 2*X + 2*X - X + 2*X + 2*X - X
2 5 6 7 9 10 11 13 14 17
o7 : ToricDivisor on X
Ψ
\end{verbatim}
Now fix the Picard basis of $X_{\Sigma}$ given by the boundary divisors associated to the rays of $\Sigma$ with first lattice points not equal to the standard vectors $e_{i}$. The complement of this Picard basis is indexed by the list $l=\{0,1,2,4,5,7,11,13,21\}$. Finally, we verify that the obtained divisor is outside the cone generated by the classes of boundary divisors. In order to do so, we interface with Polymake \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{polymake:2000}{}{}]} by using the function \verb|polymakeConeContains|:
\begin{verbatim}

\par In \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{hassett2002effective}{}{}]} it was proved, by using computational methods, that the boundary divisors and the Keel-Vermeire divisors generate the effective cone of $\overline{M}_{0,6}$. In \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{castravet2020blown}{}{}]} it was proved that the effective cone of $\overline{M}_{0,n}$ for $n\geq 10$ is not polyhedral. The problem of determining the effective cone of $\overline{M}_{0,n}$ for $n\in\{7,8,9\}$ is still open. Some examples of extremal effective divisors on $\overline{M}_{0,7}$ were found in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{castravet2013hypertree,opie2016extremal,doran2017simplicial}{}{}]}. We performed computations similar to those displayed above on $\overline{M}_{0,7}$ and found the mentioned examples with a brute-force approach. More recently in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{sikiric2022extreme}{}{}]} several thousands of extremal effective divisors on $\overline{M}_{0,7}$ were found.
\par\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Characteristic polynomials}
\par Our last application is an explicit verification of a theorem proved by Huh and Katz \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{huhkatz}{}{}]} about characteristic polynomials of realizable matroids.
\par Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an arrangement of $n+1$ hyperplanes on $\mathbb{P}^{d}$, let $M$ be its underlying matroid of rank $d+1$, and let $\mathcal{L}(M)$ be the lattice of flats of $M$. The \emph{characteristic polynomial} of $M$ is
$$\chi_{M}(q)=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{L}(M)}\mu(\emptyset,F)q^{d+1-r(F)}$$
where $\mu$ is the M\"{o}bius function of $\mathcal{L}(M)$ (see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{stanley2021enumerativeI}{}{}, Section 3.7]}). The \emph{reduced characteristic polynomial} of $M$ is $\overline{\chi}_{M}(q)=\chi_{M}(q)/(q-1)$.
\par Now we embed the complement $Y=\mathbb{P}^{d}\setminus\cup\mathcal{A}$ in $T^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n}$, as described in Section \ref{sec:wonderful}, and consider the Cremona map
$$\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{n}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n},\quad(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\mapsto(x_{0}^{-1},\dots,x_{n}^{-1}).$$
Finally, let $\overline{Z}$ be the closure in $\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}$ of the graph $Z$ of the restriction $\varphi_{|Y}$.
\par\begin{theorem}Define the integers $a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}$ by the formula
$$\overline{\chi}_{M}(q)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}(-1)^{i}a_{i}q^{d-i}.$$Then
$$[\overline{Z}]=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}[\mathbb{P}^{r-i}\times\mathbb{P}^{i}]\in A_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}).$$\end{theorem}

The characteristic polynomial of $M$ coincide with the chromatic polynomial of $G$. Let $\mathbb{C}[x_{0}^{\pm 1},x_{1}^{\pm 1},x_{2}^{\pm 1},x_{3}^{\pm 1}]$ be the Laurent ring of the torus
$$T^{4}=\{(1:x_{0}:x_{1}:x_{2}:x_{3}):x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{4}.$$Let $\mathcal{A}$ be the hyperplane arrangement realizing $M$. More explicitly, the normal vectors of its hyperplanes are the columns of the matrix $A$. The Laurent ideal of the hyperplane arrangement complement $Y=\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus\cup\mathcal{A}$ embedded in $T^{4}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{4}$ is given by $I=(-1+x_{0}+x_{2},-1+x_{1}+x_{3})$.
\par Now consider a copy of $T^{4}$ with Laurent ring $\mathbb{C}[x_{4}^{\pm 1},x_{5}^{\pm 1},x_{6}^{\pm 1},x_{7}^{\pm 1}]$. Let $Z\subseteq T^{4}\times T^{4}$ be the graph of the Cremona map $\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{4}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{4}$ restricted to $Y$. The ideal of $Z$ in the Laurent ring $\mathbb{C}[x_{0}^{\pm 1},\dots,x_{7}^{\pm 1}]$ of $T^{4}\times T^{4}$ is generated by $I$ and the polynomials $x_{i}x_{i+4}-1$ for $i\in\{0,1,2,3\}$.
\begin{verbatim}

{0,1,2,3,5,6} {0,1,2,5,6,7} {0,1,5,6,7,8}

We obtained $[\overline{Z}]=[\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{0}]+4[\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}]+4[\mathbb{P}^{0}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}]$. We now verify that the coefficients of this class are the same, up to sign, to those of the (reduced) chromatic polynomial of $G$:

Yonggeun Cho, Kiyeon Lee, Tohru Ozawa

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the Cauchy problem of 2d Dirac equation with Hartree type nonlinearity c ( | ⋅ | − γ ∗ ⟨ ψ , β ψ ⟩ ) β ψ c(|\cdot|^{-\gamma}*\langle\psi,\beta\psi\rangle)\beta\psi with c ∈ ℝ ∖ { 0 } c\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\} , 0 < γ < 2 0<\gamma<2 . Our aim is to show the small data global well-posedness and scattering in H s H^{s} for s > γ − 1 s>\gamma-1 and 1 < γ < 2 1<\gamma<2 . The difficulty stems from the singularity of the low-frequency part | ξ | − ( 2 − γ ) χ { | ξ | ≤ 1 } |\xi|^{-(2-\gamma)}\chi_{\{|\xi|\leq 1\}} of potential. To overcome it we adapt U p − V p U^{p}-V^{p} space argument and bilinear estimates of [ 28 , 26 ] arising from the null structure. We also provide nonexistence result for scattering in the long-range case 0 < γ ≤ 1 0<\gamma\leq 1 .



1.
Introduction

We consider the following Hartree type Dirac equation:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(-i\partial_{t}+{\alpha}\cdot D+m\beta)\psi=(V*\left<\psi,\beta\psi\right>)\beta\psi\;\;\mathrm{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}^{1+2},\\
\psi(0)=\psi_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\end{array}\right.

where
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{1+2}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
is the spinor field represented by a column vector
\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\\
\end{array}\right)
,
D=-i\nabla
, and
{\alpha}=({\alpha}^{1},{\alpha}^{2}),\beta
are the Dirac matrices defined by

\displaystyle\alpha^{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\;\;1\\
1&\;\;0\end{array}\right)\quad\alpha^{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}0&-i\\
i&\;\;0\end{array}\right),\quad\beta=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\;\,\,\,0\\
0&-1\end{array}\right).

The constant
m\geq 0
is a physical mass parameter and the symbol
*
denotes convolution in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The potential
V
is of Coulomb type and defined by
c|x|^{-\gamma}\;(0<\gamma<2,c\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
. Throughout the paper
\left<,\right>
denotes the inner product on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
and
\left<\psi,\phi\right>_{L_{x}^{2}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left<\psi,\phi\right>\,dx
. When
\gamma=1
, (
1
) can be regarded as a simplified model of Chern-Simons-Dirac system in Coulomb gauge
[
4
]
.

It is well-known that every smooth solution to (
1
) satisfies the mass conservation:
\|\psi(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}=\|\psi_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}
for any
t
within the existence time interval. For instance see
[
5
]
. In the massless case (
m=0
) the equation (
1
) has the scaling invariance structure in
\dot{H}^{\frac{{\gamma}-1}{2}}
. That is, the
\dot{H}^{\frac{{\gamma}-1}{2}}
-scaled function
\psi_{\lambda}
for
{\lambda}>0
defined by
\psi_{\lambda}(t,x)={\lambda}^{\frac{3-{\gamma}}{2}}\psi({\lambda}t,{\lambda}x)
is also the solution to the equation (
1
). By this reason, the (
1
) is said to be mass-critical (supercritical, subcritical) when
\gamma=(>,<)\;1
, respectively.

In this paper we use the representation of solution based on the massive Klein-Gordon equation. Thus we only consider the massive case (
m>0
) and for simplicity we assume that
m=1
.

Let us define the energy projection operators
\Pi_{\pm}(D)
by

\Pi_{\pm}(D):=\frac{1}{2}\left(I\pm\frac{1}{\left<D\right>}[\alpha\cdot D+\beta]\right),

where
\left<D\right>=(1-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Then we get

(1.4)

\displaystyle{\alpha}\cdot D+\beta=\left<D\right>\left(\Pi_{+}(D)-\Pi_{-}(D)\right),

and

(1.5)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\pm}(D)\Pi_{\pm}(D)=\Pi_{\pm}(D),\;\;\Pi_{\pm}(D)\Pi_{\mp}(D)=0.

We denote
\Pi_{\pm}(D)\psi
by
\psi_{\pm}
. Then the equation (
1
) becomes the following system of semi-relativistic Hartree equations:

(1.6)

\displaystyle(-i\partial_{t}\pm\left<D\right>)\psi_{\pm}=\Pi_{\pm}(D)[(V*\left<\psi,\beta\psi\right>)\beta\psi]

with initial data
\psi_{\pm}(0,\cdot)=\psi_{0,\pm}:=\Pi_{\pm}(D)\psi_{0}
. The free solutions of (
1.6
) are
e^{\mp it\left<D\right>}\psi_{0,\pm}
, respectively, where

e^{\mp it\langle D\rangle}f(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}e^{\mp it\langle\xi\rangle}\mathcal{F}f=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i(x\cdot\xi\mp t\langle\xi\rangle)}\widehat{f}(\xi)\,d\xi.

Here
\langle\xi\rangle:=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{-1}
are Fourier transform, its inverse, respectively.
Then by Duhamel’s principle the Cauchy problem (
1.6
) is equivalent to solving the integral equations:

(1.7)

\displaystyle\psi_{\pm}(t)=e^{\mp it\langle D\rangle}\psi_{0,\pm}+i\int_{0}^{t}e^{\mp i(t-t^{\prime})\langle D\rangle}\Pi_{\pm}(D)[(V*\langle\psi,\beta\psi\rangle)\beta\psi](t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}.

We say that the solution
\psi
scatters forward (or backward) in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
if there exist
\psi^{\ell}
, linear solutions to
(-i\partial_{t}+\alpha\cdot D+\beta)\psi=0
, such that

(1.8)

\displaystyle\|\psi(t)-\psi^{\ell}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\to 0\;\;\mbox{as}\;\;t\to+\infty\;(-\infty,\;\;\mbox{respectively}).

In most cases we may take
\mathcal{H}=H^{s}
.
Equivalently,
\psi
is said to scatter forward ( or backward) in
\mathcal{H}
if there exist
\psi_{\pm}^{\ell}:=e^{\mp it\left<D\right>}\varphi_{\pm}\;(\varphi_{\pm}\in\mathcal{H})
such that

(1.9)

\displaystyle\|\psi_{\pm}(t)-\psi_{\pm}^{\ell}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\to 0\;\;\mbox{as}\;\;t\to\pm\infty.

We are concerned with the low-regularity global well-posedness (GWP) and scattering with small initial data for (
1
) and we equivalently consider (
1.7
).
For these questions the Dirac equation with Yukawa potential was studied in
[
28
,
26
,
27
]
and
[
10
]
. Yang
[
28
]
and Tesfahun
[
27
]
showed independently scattering results on
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for
s>0
with mass
m\geq 0
. Tesfahun
[
26
]
established scattering in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
s>0
for the massive case. One can find results about power type nonlinearity in
[
1
,
2
]
. As related topics we refer the readers to
[
21
,
22
]
,
[
17
,
4
,
16
]
, and
[
7
,
8
,
14
,
24
]
for the massless Dirac equation with nonlinearity
(|x|^{-\gamma}*|\psi|^{p-1})\psi
, Chern-Simons-Dirac equation in Coulomb gauge, and massive semi-relativistic equation with Coulomb potential, respectively.

Until now not much has been known about the low-regularity issue and the corresponding scattering theory of Dirac equation (
1
) with Coulomb type potential. In this paper we suggest a 2d result on the mass-supercritical case as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1<\gamma<2
and
s>{\gamma}-1
. Then there exists
{\varepsilon}>0
such that for all
\psi_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
satisfying

\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{s}}<{\varepsilon},

there exists a unique global solution
\psi\in C(\mathbb{R};H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
to (
1
). In particular, the global solution
\psi
scatters both forward and backward in
H^{s}
.

For the proof of Theorem
1.1
we apply the frequency localization to potential
V
which is two-fold. One is to handle the high-frequency part of
V
by the null-form and modulation estimates of
[
26
]
. The other is to deal with the low-frequency part of
V
via localized bilinear estimates arising from null-structure described in
[
3
]
. Since the low-frequency part of Yukawa type potential is bounded, we do not need to handle it. However, the low-frequency part of Coulomb type potential contains significant singularity which is the main obstacle for GWP. To control it we establish the 2d localized bilinear estimates, which are adapted from 3d versions in
[
15
,
28
]
. For details see Section
3.2
below. Our argument can also be applied to the 3d problem in the same way. We leave it to the readers.

In view of the scaling structure there seems to be still a room for GWP and scattering when
\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\leq s\leq\gamma-1
. This issue would be worth pursuing as a future work.

Remark 1.2
.

The potential
V
can be generalized as follows.
\widehat{V}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\})
and there exist
\gamma_{0},\gamma
such that
1<\gamma_{0}\leq 2
,
1\leq\gamma<2
, and

\displaystyle\left||\xi|^{k}\partial^{k}\widehat{V}(\xi)\right|\lesssim|\xi|^{-(2-{{\gamma}_{0}}))}\chi_{\{|\xi|\leq 1\}}+|\xi|^{-(2-{\gamma})}\chi_{\{|\xi|>1\}}\;(0\leq k\leq 4).

The prototype of
V
with the property as above is
\mathcal{F}^{-1}(m_{0}^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{-\frac{{\gamma}}{2}}

(m_{0}\geq 0,1<{\gamma}\leq 2)
, which includes the Coulomb type potential
c|x|^{-{\gamma}}\,(m_{0}=0,1<{\gamma}<2)
and Yukawa type
\mathcal{F}^{-1}(m_{0}^{2}+|\xi|^{2})\;(m_{0}>0)
.
V
does not include two critical potentials: mass-critical case
c|x|^{-1}\;(m_{0}=0,\theta=1)
, 2d Coulomb potential
-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln|x|=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\xi|^{-2})\;(m_{0}=0)
. However, it allows a slight modification of low frequency part of the critical potentials
(\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\xi|^{-(1-\varepsilon)}(1+|\xi|)^{-\theta}),\;0<\varepsilon\ll 1,\theta=\varepsilon
or
1+\varepsilon)
. For this type potential we can get exactly the same conclusion as Theorem
1.1
. At this point, the low-frequency index
\gamma_{0}
is not involved in the regularity because the summation of low-frequency part is carried out on
|\xi|\leq 1
and results in a constant (see Section
5.1
below).

The mass-supercritical potential can be referred to as a short-range one in the context of scattering theory. In the mass-subcritical (long-range) regime it is usually expected that a number of nontrivial smooth solutions do not behave like a linear solution in the sense of (
1.8
) or (
1.9
) as time goes on. This turns out to be true for our problem with
0<\gamma\leq 1
. To be more precise, let

\mathcal{V}(\psi,\phi)(t)=\int\Big(|\cdot|^{-\gamma}*\left<\psi,\phi\right>\Big)(t,x)\left<\psi,\phi\right>(t,x)\;dx.

Then we have the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
0<\gamma<1
. Suppose that
\psi
is a smooth global solution to (
1
) which scatters forward in
L_{x}^{2}
to a smooth linear solution
\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}
.
Suppose that for some
0<\theta<1
and
t_{*}>0
the linear solution
\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}
satisfies that

(1.10)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{V}(\psi_{\infty}^{\ell},\beta\psi_{\infty}^{\ell})(t)|\geq\theta\mathcal{V}(\psi_{\infty}^{\ell},\psi_{\infty}^{\ell})(t)

for any
t>t_{*}
. Then
\psi,\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}=0
in
L_{x}^{2}
.
Furthermore, if
\gamma=1
and
\psi
is a smooth global solution to (
1
) which scatters forward in
H^{s,\delta}
for some
0<s,\delta\ll 1
to a smooth linear solution
\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}
satisfying (
1.10
), then
\psi,\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}=0
in
L_{x}^{2}
.

Here
H^{s,\delta}
denotes the space
\{f\in L_{x}^{2}:\|f\|_{H^{s,\delta}}:=\|f\|_{H^{s}}+\|(1+|x|)^{\delta}f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}<\infty\}
. As for the backward scattering, exactly the same statement holds with the only change
t<t_{*}<0
. If
\psi
scatters in
L_{x}^{2}
or
H^{s,\delta}
to
\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}
satisfying (
1.10
), then by Lemmas
6.2
and
6.4
below one can find
0<\theta^{\prime}<1
and
t_{**}>0
such that

(1.11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}(\psi,\beta\psi)(t)\geq\theta^{\prime}\mathcal{V}(\psi,\psi)(t)

for any
t>t_{**}
.

A sufficient condition for (
1.10
) is that there exists
0<\theta^{\prime\prime}<1
such that

\theta^{\prime\prime}|{\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}}_{,1}(t,x)|\geq|{\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}}_{,2}(t,x)|\;\;{\rm or}\;\;\theta^{\prime\prime}|{\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}}_{,2}(t,x)|\geq|{\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}}_{,1}(t,x)|

for any
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+2}
with
t>t_{*}
, where
\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}}_{,1}\\
{\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}}_{,2}\\
\end{array}\right)
.

In Section
6
, we prove Theorem
1.3
. Here we explain how the condition (
1.10
) and
H^{s,\delta}
assumption come into play. In the proof it is essential to handle the lower bound of functional
H(t)={\rm sgn}(c){\rm Im}\left<\psi(t),\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}(t)\right>_{L_{x}^{2}}
by
\|\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}(0)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}
. We will show

\left|\frac{d}{dt}H(t)\right|\geq|c||\mathcal{V}(\psi_{\infty}^{\ell},\beta\psi_{\infty}^{\ell})(t)|+o(t^{-\gamma}).

H^{s,\delta}
assumption is needed for the remainder term
o(t^{-\gamma})
when
\gamma=1
. Due to the matrix
\beta
the value of
\mathcal{V}(\psi_{\infty}^{\ell},\beta\psi_{\infty}^{\ell})
may vanish. To avoid this we assume (
1.10
) and hence obtain

|\frac{d}{dt}H(t)|\gtrsim t^{-\gamma}\|\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}(0)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}+o(t^{-\gamma})
for sufficiently large
t
.
Therefore, if
0<\gamma\leq 1
and
\|\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}(0)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}>0
, then the lower bound eventually will lead us to contradiction to the uniform boundedness of
H(t)
.

In the papers
[
5
,
23
]
, a class of initial data was considered for the nonlinear spinor interaction
\left<\psi,\beta\psi\right>
to be destructive and hence for the nonlinear term to vanish. This argument can be applied to the equation (
1
). In this case the solution to (
1
) is just linear and is referred to
scatter trivially
. As an example we can take the initial data
\psi_{0}
satisfying that
A\psi_{0}=\overline{\psi_{0}}
, where
A:=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}0&a_{1}\\
a_{2}&0\end{array}\right)
for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
with
|a_{1}|=|a_{2}|=1
. This is the reason why some condition such as (1.8) (consequently (
1.11
)) is necessary for the nonexsitence of scattering.

In view of Theorem
1.3
the mass-critical index
\gamma=1
is actually scattering-critical. Hence as in many scattering-critical problems we expect a modified scattering theory.

2.2.
U^{p}-V^{p}
and adapted function space

We explain concisely
U^{p}-V^{p}
spaces. For more details we refer the readers to
[
11
,
12
,
18
,
19
]
.
Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
\mathcal{I}
be the collection of finite partitions
\{t_{0},\cdots,t_{N}\}
satisfying
-\infty<t_{0}<\cdots<t_{N}\leq\infty
. If
t_{N}=\infty
, by convention,
u(t_{N}):=0
for any
u:\mathbb{R}\to L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
Let us define a
U^{p}
-atom by a step function
a:\mathbb{R}\to L_{x}^{2}
of the form

a(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\chi_{[t_{k-1},t_{k})}\phi_{k}(t)\;\;\mbox{with}\;\;\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\phi_{k}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{p}=1.

Then the
U^{p}
space is defined by

U^{p}=\left\{u=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{\lambda}_{j}a_{j}:\mbox{$a_{j}$ are $U^{p}$-atoms and $\{{\lambda}_{j}\}\in\ell^{1}$ },\|u\|_{U^{p}}<\infty\right\},

where the
U^{p}
-norm is defined by

\|u\|_{U^{p}}:=\inf_{\mbox{representation of $u$}}\;\;\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|{\lambda}_{j}|.

We next define
V^{p}
as the space of all right-continuous functions
v:\mathbb{R}\to L_{x}^{2}
satisfying that
\underset{t\to-\infty}{\lim}v(t)=0
and the norm

\|v\|_{V^{p}}:=\sup_{\{t_{k}\}\in\mathcal{I}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|v(t_{k})-v(t_{k-1})\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

is finite.

We introduce some properties of
U^{p}
and
V^{p}
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
11
]
)
.

Let
1\leq p<q<\infty
. Then the following holds.

U^{p}
and
V^{p}
are Banach spaces.

The embeddings
U^{p}\hookrightarrow V^{p}\hookrightarrow U^{q}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};L_{x}^{2})
are continuous.

These spaces have the useful duality property.

Lemma 2.2

(Corollary of
[
19
]
)
.

Let
u\in U^{p}
be absolutely continuous with
1<p<\infty
. Then

\|u\|_{U^{p}}=\sup\left\{\left|\int\left<u^{\prime},v\right>_{L_{x}^{2}}dt\right|:v\in C_{0}^{\infty},\;\;\|v\|_{V^{p^{\prime}}}=1\right\}.

Now let us define the adapted function spaces
U_{\pm}^{p},\;V_{\pm}^{p}
as follows:

\|u\|_{U_{\pm}^{p}}:=\|{e^{\pm it\left<D\right>}}u\|_{U^{p}}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\|u\|_{V_{\pm}^{p}}:=\|{e^{\pm it\left<D\right>}}u\|_{V^{p}}.

Lemma 2.3

(Logarithm interpolation, Lemma 4.12 of
[
19
]
)
.

Let
p>2
and
v\in V_{\pm}^{2}
. Then there exist a constant
\kappa(p)>0
and functions
u\in U_{\pm}^{2}
and
w\in U_{\pm}^{p}
such that

v=u+w

and for all
M\geq 1

\frac{\kappa}{M}\|u\|_{U_{\pm}^{2}}+e^{M}\|w\|_{U_{\pm}^{p}}\lesssim\|v\|_{V_{\pm}^{2}}.

Proposition 2.4

(Transfer principle, Proposition 2.19 of
[
11
]
)
.

Let

T:L_{x}^{2}\times L_{x}^{2}\times\cdots\times L_{x}^{2}\to L_{loc}^{1}

be a multilinear operator. If

\left\|T\left(e^{\pm_{1}it\left<D\right>}f_{1},e^{\pm_{2}it\left<D\right>}f_{2},\cdots,e^{\pm_{k}it\left<D\right>}f_{k}\right)\right\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k}\|f_{j}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

for some
1\leq q,r\leq\infty
, then we have

\|T(u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{k})\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k}\|u_{j}\|_{U_{\pm_{j}}^{q}},

where
u_{j}
are arbitrary functions in
U_{\pm_{j}}^{q}
.

3.
Null-form and bilinear estimates

The null structure of (
1
) was revealed concretely in
[
26
]
and can be described as follows:

\displaystyle\left<\Pi_{+}(D)\psi_{1},\beta\Pi_{\pm}(D)\psi_{2}\right>=\sum_{j=1}^{3}Q_{j}^{\pm}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})+\sum_{j=1}^{4}B_{j}^{\pm}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2}),

where the null forms
Q_{j}^{\pm}
and bilinear forms
B_{j}^{\pm}
are given by

\displaystyle Q_{j}^{\pm}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\iint_{\eta-\sigma=\xi}\left<q_{j}^{\pm}(\eta,\sigma)\widehat{\psi_{1}}(\eta),\beta\widehat{\psi_{2}}(\sigma)\right>\,d\eta d\sigma,

\displaystyle B_{j}^{\pm}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\iint_{\eta-\sigma=\xi}\left<b_{j}^{\pm}(\eta,\sigma)\widehat{\psi_{1}}(\eta),\beta\widehat{\psi_{2}}(\sigma)\right>\,d\eta d\sigma

for
j=1,2,3
, and

B_{4}^{\pm}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})=\pm\iint_{\eta-\sigma=\xi}\left<\eta\right>^{-1}\left<\widehat{\psi_{1}^{+}}(\eta),\widehat{\psi_{2}^{\pm}}(\sigma)\right>\,d\eta d\sigma,

where

\displaystyle q_{1}^{\pm}(\eta,\sigma)

\displaystyle:=(|\widehat{\eta}||\widehat{\sigma}|\pm\widehat{\eta}\cdot\widehat{\sigma})I,\qquad\qquad b_{1}^{\pm}(\eta,\sigma):=(1-|\widehat{\eta}||\widehat{\sigma}|)I,

\displaystyle q_{2}^{\pm}(\eta,\sigma)

\displaystyle:=-(\widehat{\eta}|\widehat{\sigma}|\pm\widehat{\sigma}|\widehat{\eta}|)\cdot{\alpha},\qquad\quad b_{2}^{\pm}(\eta,\sigma):=-\frac{[\eta(\left<\sigma\right>-|\sigma|)\pm\sigma(\left<\eta\right>-|\eta|)]\cdot{\alpha}}{\left<\eta\right>\left<\sigma\right>},

\displaystyle q_{3}^{\pm}(\eta,\sigma)

\displaystyle:=\pm(\widehat{\eta}_{1}\widehat{\sigma}_{2}-\widehat{\eta}_{2}\widehat{\sigma}_{1}){\alpha}^{1}{\alpha}^{2},\;\;\;\;\;\;b_{3}^{\pm}(\eta,\sigma):=\pm\frac{[(\eta-\sigma)\cdot{\alpha}]\beta-(\left<\eta\right>\pm\left<\sigma\right>)\beta+I}{\left<\eta\right>\left<\sigma\right>}.

Here we used the normalization
\widehat{\xi}=\frac{\xi}{\left<\xi\right>},\widehat{\xi}_{j}=\frac{\xi_{j}}{\left<\xi\right>}\;(j=1,2)
.

We now provide localized null-form estimates and bilinear estimates.

3.1.
High-frequency estimates

Lemma 3.1

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\mu,{\lambda}_{1},{\lambda}_{2}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
satisfy
\mu\ll{\lambda}_{1}\sim{\lambda}_{2}
and
{\lambda}_{1},{\lambda}_{2}\geq 1
. Suppose
\pm_{1}=\pm_{2}
. Assume that
\psi_{j,{\lambda}_{j}}=\Pi_{\pm_{j}}(D)\psi_{j,{\lambda}_{j}}\in V_{\pm_{j}}^{2}

\displaystyle\left\|P_{\mu}\left<\psi_{1,{\lambda}_{1}},\beta\psi_{2,{\lambda}_{2}}\right>\right\|\lesssim\mu\|\psi_{1,{\lambda}_{1}}\|_{V_{\pm_{1}}^{2}}\|\psi_{2,{\lambda}_{2}}\|_{V_{\pm_{2}}^{2}}.

Proof.

We decompose
P_{\mu}\left<\psi_{1,{\lambda}_{1}},\beta\psi_{2,{\lambda}_{2}}\right>
with respect to

,
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in\Omega_{\kappa}
with
{\kappa}\sim\mu^{-1}{\lambda}_{1}
. Only those

with
|n-n^{\prime}|\leq\mu
are chosen for each

\left|\pm_{1}\xi_{\kappa}^{\nu}-\pm_{2}\xi_{\kappa}^{\nu^{\prime}}\right|=|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|\sim{\kappa}^{-1}
. Then we estimate

\displaystyle\|P_{\mu}\left<\psi_{1,{\lambda}_{1}},\beta\psi_{2,{\lambda}_{2}}\right>\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\xi_{\kappa}^{\nu},\xi_{\kappa}^{\nu^{\prime}}\in\Omega_{\kappa}}\sum_{n,n^{\prime}\in B_{\mu}}\left\|P_{\mu}\left<K_{{\kappa}}^{\nu}{\Gamma}_{\mu,n}\psi_{1,{\lambda}_{1}},\beta K_{{\kappa}}^{\nu^{\prime}}{\Gamma}_{\mu,n}\psi_{2,{\lambda}_{2}}\right>\right\|

Therefore, using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality with

∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\mu,{\lambda}_{1},{\lambda}_{2}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
satisfy
\mu\ll{\lambda}_{1}\sim{\lambda}_{2}
and
{\lambda}_{1},{\lambda}_{2}\geq 1
. Assume that
f_{{\lambda}_{1}},g_{{\lambda}_{2}}\in L_{x}^{2}
. Then

(3.5)

\displaystyle\left\|P_{\mu}\left(e^{\pm_{1}it\left<D\right>}f_{{\lambda}_{1}}\cdot e^{\pm_{2}it\left<D\right>}g_{{\lambda}_{2}}\right)\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{{\lambda}_{1}}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|g_{{\lambda}_{2}}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

with
\pm_{1}=\pm_{2}
.

Proof.

We assume that
\pm_{1}=\pm_{2}=+
. Then

\displaystyle\left\|P_{\mu}\left(e^{it\left<D\right>}f_{{\lambda}_{1}}\cdot e^{it\left<D\right>}g_{{\lambda}_{2}}\right)\right\|\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{|}|n_{i}|\sim{\lambda}_{i}\\
|n_{1}+n_{2}|\sim\mu\end{subarray}}\|I_{n_{1},n_{2}}\|,

where

I_{n_{1},n_{2}}(t,x)=\left({\Gamma}_{\mu,n_{1}}e^{it\left<D\right>}f_{{\lambda}_{1}}\cdot{\Gamma}_{\mu,n_{2}}e^{it\left<D\right>}g_{{\lambda}_{2}}\right).

Then
I_{n_{1},n_{2}}
is written as

\displaystyle I_{n_{1},n_{2}}(t,x)=I_{n_{1},n_{2}}^{1}(t,x)+I_{n_{1},n_{2}}^{2}(t,x),

where

\displaystyle I_{n_{1},n_{2}}^{i}=\int_{A_{i}}\int e^{i(t,x)\cdot(\left<\eta\right>+\left<\xi\right>,\,\xi+\eta)}\left(\rho_{{\lambda}_{1}}(\xi){\gamma}_{\mu,n_{1}}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)\cdot\rho_{{\lambda}_{2}}(\eta){\gamma}_{\mu,n_{2}}(\eta)\widehat{g}(\xi)\right)d\eta d\xi\;\;\;\;(i=1,2).

Here we denote
A_{i}
by

A_{i}\subset\{\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|\xi|\sim|\xi_{i}|\}\;\mbox{for}\;i=1,2\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;A_{1}\cup A_{2}=\mathbb{R}^{2}.

We make the change of variables
(\xi_{i},\eta)\rightarrow(\left<\eta\right>+\left<\xi\right>,\xi+\eta)=\zeta=(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3})
with

(3.6)

\displaystyle d\xi_{i}d\eta=\left|\frac{\partial(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3})}{\partial(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\xi_{i})}\right|^{-1}d\zeta.

Since
|n_{1}+n_{2}|\sim\mu
,
\left|\frac{\partial(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3})}{\partial(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\xi_{i})}\right|\sim 1
. Now we fix
i=1
. The other cases can be estimated similarly. We may estimate by Minkowski’s inequality

\displaystyle\|I_{n_{1},n_{2}}^{1}\|\lesssim\int_{|\xi_{2}|\lesssim\mu}\left\|\iint e^{i(t,x)\cdot(\left<\eta\right>+\left<\xi\right>,\xi+\eta)}\left(\rho_{{\lambda}_{1}}(\xi){\gamma}_{\mu,n_{1}}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)\cdot\rho_{{\lambda}_{2}}(\eta){\gamma}_{\mu,n_{2}}(\eta)\widehat{g}(\xi)\right)d\eta d\xi_{1}\right\|d\xi_{2}.

Using Plancherel’s theorem with respect to
(t,x)
, the change of variables
\zeta\to(\xi_{1},\eta)
, and (
3.6
), we get

\|I_{n_{1},n_{2}}^{1}\|\lesssim\int_{|\xi_{2}|\lesssim\mu}\left\|\left(\rho_{{\lambda}_{1}}(\xi){\gamma}_{\mu,n_{1}}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)\cdot\rho_{{\lambda}_{2}}(\eta){\gamma}_{\mu,n_{2}}(\eta)\widehat{g}(\xi)\right)\right\|_{L_{\eta,\xi_{1}}^{2}}d\xi_{2}.

And also we have

\displaystyle\|I_{n_{1},n_{2}}^{2}\|\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\left(\rho_{{\lambda}_{1}}(\xi){\gamma}_{\mu,n_{1}}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)\cdot\rho_{{\lambda}_{2}}(\eta){\gamma}_{\mu,n_{2}}(\eta)\widehat{g}(\xi)\right)\right\|_{L_{\eta,\xi}^{2}}\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\Gamma}_{\mu,n_{1}}f_{{\lambda}_{1}}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|{\Gamma}_{\mu,n_{2}}g_{{\lambda}_{2}}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}.

These estimates yield that

\left\|P_{\mu}\left(e^{it\left<D\right>}f_{{\lambda}_{1}}\cdot e^{it\left<D\right>}g_{{\lambda}_{2}}\right)\right\|\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{|}|n_{i}|\sim{\lambda}_{i}\\
|n_{1}+n_{2}|\sim\mu\end{subarray}}\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\Gamma}_{\mu,n_{1}}f_{{\lambda}_{1}}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|{\Gamma}_{\mu,n_{2}}g_{{\lambda}_{2}}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{{\lambda}_{1}}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|g_{{\lambda}_{2}}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
3.8
.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
\mu,{\lambda}_{1},{\lambda}_{2}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
satisfy
\mu\ll{\lambda}_{1}\sim{\lambda}_{2}
and
{\lambda}_{1},{\lambda}_{2}\geq 1
. Suppose

Proof.

Since
{e^{\pm it\left<D\right>}}\psi=\overline{{e^{\mp it\left<D\right>}}\overline{\psi}}
,
\|\psi\|_{V_{\pm}^{2}}=\|\overline{\psi}\|_{V_{\mp}^{2}}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\left\|P_{\mu}\left(\psi_{1,{\lambda}_{1}}\cdot\psi_{2,{\lambda}_{2}}\right)\right\|\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\psi_{1,{\lambda}_{1}}\|_{V_{\pm_{1}}^{2}}\|\psi_{2,{\lambda}_{2}}\|_{V_{\pm_{2}}^{2}}.

for
\pm_{1}=\pm_{2}=+

\mu\lesssim{\lambda}_{1}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\|P_{\leq\mu}\left(P_{\sim{\lambda}_{1}}\psi\cdot P_{\sim{\lambda}_{1}}\psi\right)\|\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\psi\|_{V_{+}^{2}}^{2}

for real-valued function
\psi\in V_{+}^{2}
. To this end let

We define

n(f):=\|\omega_{\mu}*\left(P_{\sim{\lambda}_{1}}f\cdot P_{\sim{\lambda}_{1}}f\right)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}.

Then we get
n(f+g)\leq n(f)+n(g)

3.5

(3.10)

\displaystyle\|n(e^{-it\left<D\right>}f)\|_{L_{t}^{4}}\lesssim\left\|\omega_{\mu}*\left(e^{-it\left<D\right>}f\cdot e^{-it\left<D\right>}f\right)\right\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\|n(e^{-it\left<D\right>}a)\|_{L_{t}^{4}}

&\lesssim{\lambda}_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}+2{\varepsilon}}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\|\psi_{j}\|_{U_{\pm_{j}}^{2}}\|\psi_{4}\|_{U_{\pm_{4}}^{4}}.\end{aligned}

The logarithm interpolation(Lemma
2.3
) between (
5.3
) and (

) gives us that

\left|\iint V*Q(\psi_{1}^{h},\psi_{2})\cdot Q(\psi_{3},\psi_{4})dxdt\right|\lesssim{\lambda}_{2}^{{\delta}}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\|\psi_{j}\|_{U_{\pm_{j}}^{2}}\|\psi_{4}\|_{V_{\pm_{4}}^{2}}.

The case
\psi_{2}=\psi_{2}^{h}
can be estimated similarly.

We now treat the case
\psi_{3}=\psi_{3}^{h}
as follows.

\displaystyle\left|\iint V*Q(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})\cdot Q(\psi_{3}^{h},\psi_{4})dxdt\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\mu\geq 1}\left\|P_{\mu}\left(V*Q(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})\right)\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}}\left\|\widetilde{P_{\mu}}Q(\psi_{3}^{h},\psi_{4})\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\mu\geq 1}\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}+{\gamma}}{\lambda}_{3}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\|\psi_{j}\|_{U_{\pm_{j}}^{2}}\|\psi_{4}\|_{V_{\pm_{4}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\lambda}_{2}^{{\delta}}\sum_{\mu\geq 1}\mu^{-1+{\gamma}-{\delta}}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\|\psi_{j}\|_{U_{\pm_{j}}^{2}}\|\psi_{4}\|_{V_{\pm_{4}}^{2}}.

This completes the proof of (

6.
Nonexistence of scattering

In this section we prove Theorem
1.3
. Since the proof for backward scattering is similar to the forward scattering, we only consider the forward scattering. We proceed by contradiction, assuming that
\|\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}(0)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}>0
.

Let us define functionals
H(t),H_{\varepsilon}(t)(\varepsilon>0)
by

\displaystyle H(t)={\rm sgn}(c){\rm Im}\left<\psi,\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\right>_{L_{x}^{2}},\;\;H_{\varepsilon}(t)={\rm sgn}(c){\rm Im}\left<(1-\varepsilon\Delta)^{-1}\psi,(1-\varepsilon\Delta)^{-1}\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\right>_{L_{x}^{2}}.

It is clear that
H(t)
is uniformly bounded from the mass conservation and that for each
t>0
,
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}H_{\varepsilon}(t)=H(t)
.
Now by taking the time derivative and using the self-adjointness of Dirac operator we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}H_{\varepsilon}(t)

\displaystyle={\rm sgn}(c){\rm Im}\left(\left<\partial_{t}(1-\varepsilon\Delta)^{-1}\psi,(1-\varepsilon\Delta)^{-1}\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\right>_{L_{x}^{2}}+\left<(1-\varepsilon\Delta)^{-1}\psi,\partial_{t}(1-\varepsilon\Delta)^{-1}\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\right>_{L_{x}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle={\rm sgn}(c){\rm Im}\left(\left<-i({\alpha}\cdot D+\beta)\psi+iV*\left<\psi,\beta\psi\right>\beta\psi,(1-\varepsilon\Delta)^{-2}\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\right>_{L_{x}^{2}}+\left<\psi,-i({\alpha}\cdot D+\beta)(1-\varepsilon\Delta)^{-2}\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\right>_{L_{x}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle={\rm sgn}(c){\rm Im}\left(i\left<V*\left<\psi,\beta\psi\right>\beta\psi,(1-\varepsilon\Delta)^{-2}\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\right>_{L_{x}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=|c|{\rm Re}\left<|\cdot|^{-\gamma}*\left<\psi,\beta\psi\right>\beta\psi,(1-\varepsilon\Delta)^{-2}\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\right>_{L_{x}^{2}}.

Integrating over
[t_{*},t^{*}]
, we get

H_{\varepsilon}(t^{*})-H_{\varepsilon}(t_{*})=|c|{\rm Re}\int_{t_{*}}^{t^{*}}\left<|\cdot|^{-\gamma}*\left<\psi,\beta\psi\right>\beta\psi,(1-\varepsilon\Delta)^{-2}\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\right>_{L_{x}^{2}}\,dt,

If
\psi\in C(\mathbb{R};H^{\frac{\gamma}{2}})
, then Hardy-Sobolev inequality yields
|\cdot|^{-\gamma}*\left<\psi,\beta\psi\right>\beta\psi\in C([t_{*},t^{*}];L_{x}^{2})
and thus by taking the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
we finally get

H(t^{*})-H(t_{*})=\int_{t_{*}}^{t^{*}}Y(t)\,dt,

where
Y(t)=|c|{\rm Re}\left<|\cdot|^{-\gamma}*\left<\psi,\beta\psi\right>\beta\psi,\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\right>_{L_{x}^{2}}
.

We will show that

(6.1)

\displaystyle|Y(t)|\gtrsim t^{-\gamma}

for sufficiently large
t
when
\|\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}>0
.
Once (
6.1
) has been shown, since the sign of

is fixed for large time and

, (
6.1
) leads us to a contradiction to the fact that

is uniformly bounded on time and hence completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

From now on we focus on the proof of (
6.1

as follows:

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=Y_{1}+Y_{2}+Y_{3},

\displaystyle Y_{1}

\displaystyle=|c|\mathcal{V}(\psi_{\infty}^{\ell},\beta\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}),

\displaystyle Y_{2}

\displaystyle Y_{3}

We first deal with the

Let
0<{\gamma}<1
. Then for any
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions
u_{1}\in L_{x}^{2},\,u_{2}\in L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{x}^{\infty}

(6.6)

\displaystyle\||x|^{-{\gamma}}*(u_{1}\overline{u_{2}})\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|u_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{1-{\gamma}}\|u_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}^{{\gamma}}.

Proof.

Using Hölder’s inequality we get for any
r>2

\displaystyle\||x|^{-\gamma}*(u_{1}\overline{u_{2}})\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\int|x-y|^{-{\gamma}}|u_{1}(y)\overline{u_{2}}(y)|dy\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\||x|^{-{\gamma}}\|_{L_{x}^{2}(|x|\leq R)}\|u_{1}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|u_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}+\||x|^{-{\gamma}}\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{r}{\gamma}}(|x|\geq R)}\|u_{1}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|u_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2r}{r-2\gamma}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{1-{\gamma}}\|u_{1}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|u_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}+R^{\frac{\gamma(2-r)}{r}}\|u_{1}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|u_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{1-\frac{2\gamma}{r}}\|u_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}^{\frac{2\gamma}{r}}.

By taking
R=\|u_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|u_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}^{-1}
we get the desired result.
∎

If
u_{1}=u_{2}
, then we can cover the case
\gamma=1
as follows.

Lemma 6.3
.

For any
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions
u\in L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{x}^{\infty}
we have

(6.7)

\displaystyle\||x|^{-1}*|u|^{2}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|u\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}.

Proof.

For any
1<r_{1}<2
and
r_{2}>2
we have

\displaystyle\||x|^{-1}*|u|^{2}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\int|x-y|^{-{\gamma}}|u(y)|^{2}dy\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\||x|^{-1}\|_{L_{x}^{r_{1}}(|x|\leq R)}\|u\|_{L_{x}^{r_{1}^{\prime}}}\|u\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}+\||x|^{-1}\|_{L_{x}^{r_{2}}(|x|\geq R)}\|u\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|u\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2r_{2}}{r_{2}-2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{\frac{2}{r_{1}}-1}\|u\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2-\frac{2}{r_{1}}}\|u\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}^{\frac{2}{r_{2}}}+R^{\frac{2}{r_{2}}-1}\|u\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2-\frac{2\gamma}{r_{2}}}\|u\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}^{\frac{2}{r_{2}}}.

By taking
R=\|u_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|u_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}^{-1}
we finish the proof.
∎

If
u_{1}\neq u_{2}
, then we need a regularity and a space decay assumption. Then we have

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
0<s,\delta\ll 1
. Then for any
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions
u_{1}\in H^{s,\delta}
and
u_{2}\in L_{x}^{\infty}

(6.8)

\displaystyle\||x|^{-1}*(u_{1}\overline{u_{2}})\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s,\delta}}\|u_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}.

Proof.

By Hölder’s inequality and Sobolev embedding we see that

\displaystyle\||x|^{-1}*(u_{1}\overline{u_{2}})\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||x|^{-1}\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2}{1+s}}(|x|\leq 1)}\|u_{1}\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2}{1-s}}}\|u_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}+\||x|^{-1}\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2}{2-\frac{\delta}{2}}}(|x|\geq 1)}\|u_{1}\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2}{1+\frac{\delta}{2}}}(|x|\geq 1)}\|u_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s}}\|u_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}+\|(1+|x|)^{\delta}u_{1}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|u_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s,\delta}}\|u_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}.

∎

If
0<\gamma<1
, then from Lemmas
6.2
,
6.1
and mass conservation it follows that

\displaystyle|Y_{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\left<V*\Big(\left<\psi,\beta\psi\right>-\left<\psi_{\infty}^{\ell},\beta\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\right>\Big)\beta\psi_{\infty}^{\ell},\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\right>_{L_{x}^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\int\Big(\left<\psi,\beta\psi\right>-\left<\psi_{\infty}^{\ell},\beta\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\right>\Big)V*\left<\beta\psi_{\infty}^{\ell},\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\right>dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi-\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\left(\|\psi\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\right)\|\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{1-{\gamma}}\|\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}^{{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi-\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\left(\|\psi_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|\varphi_{\infty}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\right)\|\varphi_{\infty}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2-{\gamma}}t^{-{\gamma}}\left(\|{\varphi}_{+}\|_{B_{1,1}^{2}}+\|{\varphi}_{-}\|_{B_{1,1}^{2}}\right)^{{\gamma}}

and

\displaystyle|Y_{3}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\left<V*\left<\psi,\beta\psi\right>\beta(\psi-\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}),\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\right>_{L_{x}^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\int\left<\psi,\beta\psi\right>\left(V*\left<\beta(\psi-\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}),\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\right>\right)dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}\|\psi-\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{1-{\gamma}}\|\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}\|\psi-\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|\varphi_{\infty}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{1-{\gamma}}t^{-{\gamma}}\left(\|{\varphi}_{+}\|_{B_{1,1}^{2}}+\|{\varphi}_{-}\|_{B_{1,1}^{2}}\right)^{{\gamma}}.

Therefore we get

(6.9)

\displaystyle|Y_{j}|=o(t^{-{\gamma}})

for
j=1,2
. Then (
6.5
) and (
6.9
) conclude (
6.1
) for the case
0<\gamma<1
.

If
\gamma=1
, then by Lemma
6.3
we have

\displaystyle|Y_{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\int\Big(\left<\psi,\beta\psi\right>-\left<\psi_{\infty}^{\ell},\beta\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\right>\Big)|\cdot|^{-1}*\left<\beta\psi_{\infty}^{\ell},\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\right>dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi-\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\left(\|\psi\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\right)\|\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi-\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\left(\|\psi_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|\varphi_{\infty}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\right)\|\varphi_{\infty}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}t^{-1}\left(\|{\varphi}_{+}\|_{B_{1,1}^{2}}+\|{\varphi}_{-}\|_{B_{1,1}^{2}}\right)=o(t^{-1})

and by Lemma
6.4
and mass conservation

\displaystyle|Y_{3}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\left<|\cdot|^{-1}*\left<\psi,\beta\psi\right>\beta(\psi-\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}),\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\right>_{L_{x}^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\int\left<\psi,\beta\psi\right>\left(V*\left<\beta(\psi-\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}),\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\right>\right)dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}\|\psi-\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\|_{H^{s,\delta}}\|\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}\|\psi-\psi_{\infty}^{\ell}\|_{H^{s,\delta}}t^{-1}\left(\|{\varphi}_{+}\|_{B_{1,1}^{2}}+\|{\varphi}_{-}\|_{B_{1,1}^{2}}\right)=o(t^{-1}).

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

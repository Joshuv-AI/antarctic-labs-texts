1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a projective variety and let
f\colon X\rightarrow X
be a surjective self-map, both of which are defined over a number field
K
. A key component of understanding the dynamical system
(X,f)
is to identify the invariant closed subschemes
Y
of
X
; that is,
Y
satisfying
f(Y)\subseteq Y
. In the case of such subschemes, the iterated preimages
f^{-n}(Y)
of
Y
under
f
form a tower

Y\subseteq f^{-1}(Y)\subseteq f^{-2}(Y)\subseteq\cdots.

If
g:V\to V^{\prime}
is a surjective generically finite morphism of projective varieties then
g^{*}
induces an injective homomorphism from the canonical ring of
V^{\prime}
into the canonical ring of
V
and so the Kodaira dimension of
V
is at least as large as that of
V^{\prime}
(see also the general conjecture of Iitaka
[
9
]
); in the case of curves we have a similar inequality involving genera coming from the Riemann-Hurwitz formula. These invariants serve as coarse proxies for the geometric complexity of varieties, and so one typically expects the irreducible components of
f^{-n}(Y)\smallsetminus f^{-(n-1)}(Y)
to become increasingly geometrically complex as
n
increases.

At the same time, there is a general principle within arithmetic geometry—now well-entrenched—that the geometric structure of a variety should exert significant influence over its underlying arithmetic structure. The most famous manifestation of this principle is the celebrated theorem of Faltings
[
10
,
11
]
(see also
[
20
]
), which asserts that an irreducible projective curve of genus at least two defined over a number field
K
has only finitely many
K
-points. More generally, there is a sweeping conjectural extension formulated by Bombieri and Lang (see
[
8
, Conjecture F.5.2.1]
), which predicts that for an irreducible variety
X
of general type (Kodaira dimension equal to its dimension as a variety) that is defined over a number field
K
, its
K
-points should lie in a proper closed subset.

In light of the observation that the Kodaira dimensions of components in
f^{-n}(Y)
are each at least as large as the Kodaira dimension of some component of
f^{-(n-1)}(Y)
, one expects that newly generated components under repeated backwards iteration should eventually be of general type, unless there is some compelling geometric reason for this not to occur. Consequently, we expect the tower of
K
-points

\displaystyle Y(K)\subseteq(f^{-1}(Y))(K)\subseteq(f^{-2}(Y))(K)\subseteq\cdots

to eventually stabilize.

This expectation was made precise in the following question, originally asked in
[
15
, Question 8.4 (1)]
.

Question 1.1

(Preimages Question)
.

Let
f\colon X\longrightarrow X
be a surjective self-morphism of a projective variety
X
defined over a number field
K
.
Let
Y\subset X
be a closed subscheme such that
f|_{Y}
factors through
Y\subset X
.
Then there exists a non-negative integer
s_{0}
such that

(f^{-(s+1)}(Y)\smallsetminus f^{-s}(Y))(K)=\emptyset

for all
s\geq s_{0}
.
In other words, for
x\in X(K)
, if
f^{s}(x)\in Y(K)
for some
s\geq 0
, then
f^{s_{0}}(x)\in Y(K)
.

Strictly speaking, in
[
15
]
, the authors consider a stronger variant of this question involving points in
(f^{-(s+1)}(Y)\smallsetminus f^{-s}(Y))(L)
,
where
L
varies over finite extensions of
K
of bounded degree. We shall also consider this stronger version of the question in this paper. It should be noted that in the case of self-maps of
\mathbb{P}^{N}
, when
Y
is a closed point, one can use Northcott’s theorem and canonical heights (see
[
4
]
) to show the existence of an integer
s_{0}
in the statement of Question
1.1
. Hence Question
1.1
asks whether a type of uniform boundedness result should hold: can the integer
s_{0}
can be uniformly bounded as one ranges over points in a closed invariant subscheme?

Our first main result shows that the above question has an affirmative answer for étale self-maps.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
,
Y
,
f
, and
K
be as in the statement of Question
1.1
. Then the following hold:

(a)

if
f
is étale then there exists a non-negative integer
s_{0}
such that

(f^{-(s+1)}(Y)\smallsetminus f^{-s}(Y))(K)=\emptyset

for all
s\geq s_{0}
;

(b)

if, in addition,
f|_{Y}
is flat, then for each natural number
d
there exists
s=s(X,Y,f,d)
such that
(f^{-(s+1)}(Y)\smallsetminus f^{-s}(Y))(L)=\emptyset
for all field extensions
L
of
K
with
[L:K]\leq d
.

In particular, Question
1.1
has an affirmative answer whenever
X
is a smooth variety with nonnegative Kodaira dimension.

We note that Theorem
1.2
(b) answers the original question asked in
[
15
, Question 8.4 (1)]
under the additional hypotheses on the map
f
.

An important special case of the Preimages Question above arises when
g
is a surjective self-map of a projective variety
Z
defined over a number field
K
and we consider the morphism
f:=(g,g)
of
X:=Z\times Z
, along with the diagonal subvariety
Y=\{(x,x)\colon x\in X\}
. In this case, Question
1.1
has a particularly appealing formulation in terms of “cancellation”: it asks whether there exists a natural number
s
such that whenever
x,y\in X(K)
are such that
f^{n}(x)=f^{n}(y)
for some
n>s
then we in fact have
f^{s}(x)=f^{s}(y)
.

Even in this case when
X
is
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, one quickly encounters thorny Diophantine problems concerning
K
-points on plane curves of the form

(g(X)-g(Y))/(X-Y)=0.

In the polynomial case, the classification of which
g
yield such curves with a genus zero component was worked out by Avanzi and Zannier
[
1
]
; their work involves difficult estimates and on occasion requires a finer understanding of the genera of components of more general polynomial plane curves of the form
a(X)=b(Y)
, some of which relies on previous work of Bilu and Tichy
[
3
]
. In the case of general rational functions, however, it is a notoriously difficult problem to classify rational functions
g(x)
with the property that the curve
(g(X)-g(Y))/(X-Y)=0
has a component of genus at most one. In light of Faltings’ theorem, this would be a key step in understanding which such curves have infinitely many
K
-points.

We are nevertheless able to resolve the above cancellation question in the case of curves by applying
p
-adic uniformization techniques of Rivera-Letelier
[
19
]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
f\colon X\longrightarrow X
be a surjective self-morphism of a projective curve
X
defined over a number field
K
.
Then there exists a non-negative integer
s_{0}
such that
for all
x,y\in X(K)
, if
f^{s}(x)=f^{s}(y)
for some
s\geq 0
, then
f^{s_{0}}(x)=f^{s_{0}}(y)
.

In light of Theorem
1.2
and
1.3
, we expect that the following general result should hold.

Conjecture 1.4

(Cancellation Conjecture)
.

Let
f\colon X\longrightarrow X
be a surjective self-morphism of a projective variety
X
defined over a number field
K
.
Then there exists a non-negative integer
s_{0}
satisfying the following property:
for all
x,y\in X(K)
, if
f^{s}(x)=f^{s}(y)
for some
s\geq 0
, then
f^{s_{0}}(x)=f^{s_{0}}(y)
.

We note that the assumption that
X
be projective is necessary in the statement of Conjecture
1.4
(and hence also in the statement of Question
1.1
, if one wants an affirmative answer). Indeed, in Section
6.1
, we show that Conjecture
1.4
fails for
X={\mathbb{A}}^{2}
.

A subtle issue is that, in general, Conjecture
1.4
cannot be proved using
p
-adic methods, and hence one cannot expect to implement our proof strategy for Theorem
1.3
in higher dimensions. In particular, in Proposition
6.1
, we construct a morphism
f\colon{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}
where Conjecture
1.4
fails for
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
for all primes
p\leq\infty
.

In light of Theorem
1.2
, the major content of Theorem
1.3
is that Conjecture
1.4
holds for
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. As it was remarked earlier, significantly more is known in the case of polynomial self-maps of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, due in large part to classical work of Ritt
[
18
]
. In particular, the behavior of semigroups of polynomial maps is much better understood than the case of general rational maps. This additional knowledge allows us to prove a more general cancellation result which allows for multiple different polynomial self-maps
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{r}
of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
K
be a number field and let
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{r}
be polynomial maps on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{K}
of degree at least two.
Then there is a proper closed subset
Z\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{K}\times_{K}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{K}
with the following property:
for any points
a,b\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}(K)
, if

\displaystyle\varphi_{i_{n}}\circ\cdots\circ\varphi_{i_{1}}(a)=\varphi_{i_{n}}\circ\cdots\circ\varphi_{i_{1}}(b)

for some
n\geq 0
and
i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\in\{1,\dots,r\}
,
then
(a,b)\in Z(K)
.

From the viewpoint of cancellation conjecture, one might expect a statement of the form

\varphi_{i_{n}}\circ\cdots\circ\varphi_{i_{1}}(a)=\varphi_{i_{n}}\circ\cdots\circ\varphi_{i_{1}}(b)\Longrightarrow\varphi_{i_{n_{0}}}\circ\cdots\circ\varphi_{i_{1}}(a)=\varphi_{i_{n_{0}}}\circ\cdots\circ\varphi_{i_{1}}(b),

but this does not hold in general. Theorem
1.5
is, in some sense, the optimal result one can expect for polynomial maps.
As a simple example, consider the case where
\varphi_{1}(x)=x^{2}
and
\varphi_{2}(x)=x^{3}
. Then

\varphi_{1}\circ\varphi_{2}^{n}(a)=\varphi_{1}\circ\varphi_{2}^{n}(-a),\quad\varphi_{2}^{n}(a)\neq\varphi_{2}^{n}(-a)

for all
a\neq 0
and all
n\geq 0
.

The outline of this paper is as follows. In Section
2.1
, we prove Theorem
1.2
; we prove Theorem
1.3
in Section
3
.
In Section
4
, we collect several important estimates involving the genera of curves; these will then be applied in Section
5
, where we prove Theorem
1.5
. Finally, in Section
6.1
we provide examples showing that Conjecture
1.4
is false (and hence Question
1.1
has a negative answer) if the projectivity assumption on
X
is dropped or
K
is replaced with a local field.

The étale case

•

Our goal in this subsection is to prove Theorem

Proof of Theorem

as follows:

•

;

•

\displaystyle\Sigma_{m+1}=\left\{C\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{K}\times_{K}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{K}\middle|\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise-11.06952pt\vbox{\halign{\relax\hfil\txtline@@{#}\hfil\cr\hbox{{\ignorespaces $C\notin\bigcup_{i=0}^{m}\Sigma_{i},\#C(K)=\infty$,}\\\hbox{{\ignorespaces$f_{j}(C)\in\Sigma_{m}$ for some $j$}\crcr}}}}\right\}.}}

Note that all
\Sigma_{i}
are finite.

We claim that
\Sigma_{n}=\emptyset
for all large
n
.
Indeed, suppose
\Sigma_{n}\neq\emptyset
for all
n\geq 0
.
Then there exists an infinite sequence

\displaystyle\cdots\xrightarrow{f_{i_{n+1}}}C_{n}\xrightarrow{f_{i_{n}}}C_{n-1}\xrightarrow{f_{i_{n-1}}}\cdots\xrightarrow{f_{i_{2}}}C_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{i_{1}}}\Delta

such that
C_{i}\in\Sigma_{i}
. By Lemma

. This implies

is an infinite set, contradicting Fact

(

Now let
\Omega=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\Sigma_{n}
. This is a finite set of curves as we just proved.
Let

and define

\displaystyle T=\{x\in({\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1})(K)\mid\text{$x\notin Z_{0}$, $f_{j}(x)\in Z_{0}$ for some $j$}\}.

Then

is finite.

is either an element of

Let
\hat{h}_{f_{1}},\dots,\hat{h}_{f_{r}}
be canonical heights on

with respect to
{\mathcal{O}}(1,1)
.
Let

be a Weil height on
({\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1})(\overline{K})
with respect to
{\mathcal{O}}(1,1)
.
Pick constants

so that for all

,

\displaystyle|\hat{h}_{f_{i}}-h|<B_{0}

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{f_{i}}(x)<B_{1}\quad\text{for all $x\in T$.}

Set

\displaystyle S=\{y\in({\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1})(K)\mid h(y)<3B_{0}+B_{1}\}.

6.
Counter-examples

6.1.
Counter-example for non-projective varieties

We give an example demonstrating the necessity of the projective assumption in Conjecture
1.4
(and hence that Question
1.1
has a negative answer without this hypothesis). In our example,
X={\mathbb{A}}^{2}
and
Y=\{0\}
. Let
f\colon{\mathbb{A}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
be given by

f(x,y)=(xy,x^{2}+xy).

Let
\{F_{n}\}
be the sequence of Fibonacci numbers with
F_{0}=0
and
F_{1}=1
, and let

a_{n}=\begin{cases}((-1)^{n+1}F_{n},(-1)^{n}F_{n-1}),&n\geq 1\\
(0,1),&n=0\\
(0,0),&n=-1\end{cases}.

Then for
n\geq 0
, we have

f(a_{n})=(-1)^{n}F_{n}a_{n-1}

and hence

f^{n+1}(a_{n})=0\quad\textrm{and}\quad f^{n}(a_{n})\neq 0.

As a result, for all
n\neq 0
,

f^{n+1}(a_{n})=f^{n+1}(a_{n-1})\quad\textrm{and}\quad f^{n}(a_{n})\neq f^{n}(a_{n-1}).

6.2.
Counter-example over local fields

We show that Conjecture
1.4
is not true over local fields, and hence one cannot, in general, employ
p
-adic methods to solve the conjecture.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
f\colon{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}
be given by

f(x:y:z)=(x^{2}+yz:x^{2}+y^{2}+yz:z^{2}).

Then for all
p\leq\infty
, the analogue of Conjecture
1.4
fails for the base change
f_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}:=f\times_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, i.e., for all
p\leq\infty
and all
n\geq 1
, there exist
a_{n,p},b_{n,p}\in{\mathbb{P}}^{2}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
such that

f^{n}_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}(a_{n,p})=f^{n}_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}(b_{n,p})\quad\textrm{and}\quad f^{n-1}_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}(a_{n,p})\neq f^{n-1}_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}(b_{n,p}).

Proof.

Since
f
is finite and
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
is smooth,
f
is flat by
[
5
, Corollary 18.17]
. Letting
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
be the locus where
z\neq 0
, we see
f({\mathbb{A}}^{2})\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
. Let

g=(f|_{{\mathbb{A}}^{2}}\times f|_{{\mathbb{A}}^{2}})\colon{\mathbb{A}}^{4}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{4}.

We denote the coordinates on
{\mathbb{A}}^{4}
by
(x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
and let
\Delta=V(x-x^{\prime},y-y^{\prime})\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{4}
be the diagonal. It is enough to prove

\left(g^{-(n+1)}(\Delta)\smallsetminus g^{-n}(\Delta)\right)({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})

is infinite for all
n\geq 1
and
p\leq\infty
.

For ease of notation, given any polynomial
F(x,y)\in{\mathbb{Q}}[x,y]
, we let
F^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Q}}[x^{\prime},y^{\prime}]
be the polynomial
F^{\prime}:=F(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
. Let us write
f|_{{\mathbb{A}}^{2}}^{n}=(P_{n},Q_{n})
. Then

\displaystyle P_{n+1}=P_{n}^{2}+Q_{n}

\displaystyle Q_{n+1}=P_{n}^{2}+Q_{n}^{2}+Q_{n}.

We see

g^{-n}(\Delta)=V(P_{n}-P^{\prime}_{n},Q_{n}-Q^{\prime}_{n})

and so

\displaystyle g^{-(n+1)}(\Delta)

\displaystyle=V\!\left(P_{n}^{2}+Q_{n}-(P^{\prime}_{n})^{2}-Q^{\prime}_{n},\ \,Q_{n}^{2}-(Q^{\prime}_{n})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\supset V\!\left(P_{n}+P^{\prime}_{n},\ \,Q_{n}-Q^{\prime}_{n}\right)=:Z_{n+1}

Since the origin of
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
is a fixed point of
f
, we see
P_{n}(0,0)=Q_{n}(0,0)=0
; it follows that the origin
0\in{\mathbb{A}}^{4}
lies on
Z_{n+1}
.

Letting
\sigma
be the automorphism of
{\mathbb{A}}^{4}
defined by
\sigma(x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=(x,y,-x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
, we see
Z_{n+1}=(\sigma g^{n})^{-1}(\Delta)
. Since
g
is finite flat,
Z_{n+1}
is pure of dimension
2
. In particular, there is a
2
-dimensional irreducible component
W_{n+1}
of
Z_{n+1}
containing
0
. Next, notice that
Z_{n+1}\cap g^{-n}(\Delta)=V(P_{n},P^{\prime}_{n},Q_{n}-Q^{\prime}_{n})=g^{-n}(V(x,x^{\prime},y-y^{\prime}))
. Again by flatness of
g
, we see
Z_{n+1}\cap g^{-n}(\Delta)
is pure of dimension
1
, and in particular,
W_{n+1}\not\subset g^{-n}(\Delta)
.

We will show that
Z_{n+1}
is smooth at
0
and hence
W_{n+1}
is the unique irreducible component of
Z_{n+1}
passing through
0
. We see then that
0
defines a smooth
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-point of
W_{n+1}
, and so
(W_{n+1})({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
is dense in
W_{n+1}\times_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
by
[
17
, Proposition 3.5.75]
. In particular,
\left(g^{-(n+1)}(\Delta)\smallsetminus g^{-n}(\Delta)\right)({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
is infinite.

To prove
Z_{n+1}
is smooth at
0
, observe that by induction

\frac{\partial P_{n}}{\partial x}(0)=\frac{\partial Q_{n}}{\partial x}(0)=0\quad\textrm{and}\quad\frac{\partial P_{n}}{\partial y}(0)=\frac{\partial Q_{n}}{\partial y}(0)=1

for all
n\geq 1
. Setting

\Phi_{n}=P_{n}+P^{\prime}_{n}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\Psi_{n}=Q_{n}-Q^{\prime}_{n},

it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial(\Phi_{n},\Psi_{n})}{\partial(x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime})}(0)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&1\\
0&1&0&-1\end{pmatrix}

and so
Z_{n+1}
is smooth at
0
.
∎

1.
Introduction

This paper is devoted to the study of the
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity for viscosity solutions of the following degenerate/singular fully nonlinear parabolic equations

(1.1)

\displaystyle u_{t}=|Du|^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u)+f,

where
\gamma>-1
, and
f
is bounded and continuous. Here the operator
F
is uniformly elliptic with certain structural conditions (the hypotheses on
F
will be precisely stated in
Section

2
). The fully nonlinear parabolic equation (
1.1
) is motivated by the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equations of the time-dependent, two-player stochastic differential games. In other words, the viscosity solution of (
1.1
) can be realized as the value function of an associated stochastic control problem; see e.g.
[
13
,
24
,
25
,
31
]
.

Thanks to the reduction scheme presented in
[
5
,
6
]
, it suffices to concentrate on the following homogeneous equations

(1.2)

\displaystyle u_{t}=|Du|^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u)

instead of nonhomogeneous one (
1.1
). Therefore, our main theorem is concerned with
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity of viscosity solutions
u
of (
1.2
) with uniform estimates provided that
F
is convex.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
\gamma>-1
,
F
satisfies
(F1)
,
(F2)
, and
(F3)
, and
f
is bounded and continuous in
Q_{1}
. Let
u
be a viscosity solution of (
1.1
) in
Q_{1}
. Then there exists a constant
\alpha\in(0,1)
depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
,
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
, and
\|F\|_{C^{1,1}(\mathcal{S}^{n})}
such that
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{1/2}})
with an estimate

\displaystyle\|Du\|_{C^{\alpha}(\overline{Q_{1/2}})}\leq C,

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
,
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
, and
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
. Moreover, it holds that

|u(x,t)-u(x,s)|\leq C|t-s|^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2-\alpha\gamma}}\quad\text{for all }(x,t),(y,s)\in Q_{1/2}.

In order to prove
Theorem

1.1
, we investigate the solvability of the Dirichlet problem which is generated by the regularization of (
1.2
):

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}&=(1+|Du|^{2})^{\gamma/2}F(D^{2}u)&&\text{in $Q_{1}$}\\
u&=\varphi&&\text{on $\partial_{p}Q_{1}$}.\end{aligned}\right.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
\gamma>-2
,
F
satisfies
(F1)
,
(F2)
, and
(F3)
. Let
\varphi\in C^{2,\beta}(\overline{Q_{1}})
for some
\beta\in(1/2,1)
, and satisfy the compatibility condition:

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}=(1+|D\varphi|^{2})^{\gamma/2}F(D^{2}\varphi)\quad\text{on $\partial_{c}Q_{1}$},

where
\partial_{c}Q_{1}\coloneqq\partial B_{1}\times\{-1\}
. Then the Dirichlet problem (
1.3
) is uniquely solvable in
C^{2,\beta}(\overline{Q_{1}})
.

It is noteworthy that (
1.3
) can be understood as one of the fully nonlinear generalizations of the mean curvature equation

(1.4)

u_{t}=\textnormal{div}\,\frac{Du}{(1+|Du|^{2})^{1/2}}=(1+|Du|^{2})^{-1/2}a_{ij}(Du)D_{ij}u,

where
a_{ij}(p)\coloneqq\delta_{ij}-p_{i}p_{j}/(1+|p|^{2})
. Here we remark that

\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+K^{2}}\leq\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+|p|^{2}}\leq a_{ij}(p)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq|\xi|^{2}\quad\text{for $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$ and $|p|\leq K$.}

In other words, the ellipticity
\Lambda/\lambda
of mean curvature-type equations is finite provided that
Du
is bounded, but it is heavily influenced by
Du
.

Let us summarize the results of the preceding literature which deals with degenerate/singular equations in non-divergence form. We first collect the solvability results of Dirichlet problems in different settings. If we replace the first equation in (
1.3
) by quasilinear equations such as

(1.5)

u_{t}=(\varepsilon^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{\gamma/2}\left(\delta_{ij}+\frac{(p-2)D_{i}uD_{j}u}{\varepsilon^{2}+|Du|^{2}}\right)D_{ij}u

or
u_{t}=a_{ij}(x,t,u,Du)D_{ij}u+b(x,t,u,Du)
, then the existence, uniqueness, and smoothness of the solution were illustrated in the comprehensive books
[
33
,
34
]
and references therein. Moreover, for fully nonlinear parabolic operators
F=F(x,t,r,p,M)
enjoying several structural conditions, the well-posedness of corresponding Dirichlet problems was formulated in
[
15
,
16
,
18
,
32
]
. Nonetheless, in the aforementioned papers, the uniform Lipschitz continuity with respect to the gradient variable
p
is the main assumption on
F
, which is not satisfied by our operator
F(x,t,r,p,M)=(1+|p|^{2})^{\gamma/2}F(M)
. Finally, the existence, uniqueness, and global Hölder regularity results for viscosity solutions of the Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}&=|Du|^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u)&&\text{in $Q_{1}$}\\
u&=\varphi&&\text{on $\partial_{p}Q_{1}$}.\end{aligned}\right.

were demonstrated in
[
9
,
19
]
under appropriate structural conditions on
F
,
f
, and
\varphi
. However, to the best of our knowledge, the higher regularity than Hölder continuity of viscosity solutions
u
of (
1.1
) is unknown in literature.

Let us move on to the interior regularity results. For the quasilinear variant instead of
F
in (
1.1
), the
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity for a viscosity solution
u
of

(1.6)

u_{t}=|Du|^{\gamma}\Delta_{p}^{N}u\coloneqq|Du|^{\gamma}(\delta_{ij}+(p-2)|Du|^{-2}D_{i}uD_{j}u)D_{ij}u

was established in
[
27
,
30
]
when
p>1
and
\gamma>-1
. They suggested two alternatives to describe the oscillation of
Du
with uniform estimates for approximated solutions; we will present a more precise explanation of their analysis, and compare it with ours later. In
[
22
,
23
]
, they provided similar consequences for an extended class of quasilinear parabolic equations. As mentioned before,
[
5
,
6
]
verified that the interior
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity result for homogeneous equations can be transferred to the one for nonhomogeneous equations, in both fully nonlinear and quasilinear settings.

On the other hand, the elliptic analogue of (
1.1
) has been relatively widely studied in the last decade. To be precise, the local
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity result for

(1.7)

|Du|^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u)=f

was developed in
[
28
]
. Later, the optimality of the exponent
\alpha

[
3
]
and the global regularity result
[
10
]
were investigated for a similar class of elliptic operators. There are many recent papers on fully nonlinear elliptic equations with generalized degeneracy or singularity; we refer to
[
2
,
4
,
7
,
8
,
11
,
17
,
21
]
.

We now display various approaches to achieve the Hölder estimate of the gradient in literature, and then outline the strategy of proof of our main theorem. In the elliptic setting, Imbert and Silvestre
[
28
]
developed the interior
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity for a viscosity solution
u
of (
1.7
) with
\gamma\geq 0
and
f
is bounded and continuous. In short, they first approximated
u
by
u_{k}
which is a viscosity solution of some modified equations with source terms
\|f_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\to 0
. Then by taking a limit and applying ‘cutting lemma’, they discovered that the limit function
u_{\infty}
satisfies a homogeneous equation which guarantees the desired regularity. Nevertheless, the existence of the time derivative term
u_{t}
, which is not necessarily bounded in
L^{\infty}
-norm, prevents us from adopting the same idea to the parabolic equation (
1.2
).

In the parabolic setting, Imbert, Jin, and Silvestre
[
27
]
proved interior
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity for a viscosity solution
u
of degenerate/singular quasilinear parabolic equations (
1.6
) with
p>1
and
\gamma>-1
. The key ingredients of their proof were the existence of a smooth solution
u^{\varepsilon}
that satisfies (
1.5
) with prescribed boundary data, and the uniform
C^{1,\alpha}
-estimates for
u^{\varepsilon}
. Even though their approach for uniform estimates is also available for degenerate/singular fully nonlinear parabolic equations, the major challenge arises from the fact that the solvability of the Dirichlet problem (
1.3
) is still unknown.

In view of Schuader fixed point theorem, the solvability of Dirichlet problems essentially follows from a priori estimate for associated problems. In the quasilinear setting, the key step in Bernstein technique for a priori estimate can be described as follows: the quantity
v\coloneqq|Du|^{2}
can be regarded as a weak solution of a linear equation in divergence form, which leads to the weakened regularity assumption on
u
from
C^{3}
to
C^{2}
; see
[
26
,
33
,
34
]
for details. However, such weak formulation strongly exploits the quasilinear structure of corresponding equations, and so it cannot be applied to our problem (
1.3
) which exhibits the fully nonlinear character.

To overcome such difficulty, we develop gradient estimates by employing a modified version of Bernstein technique. To be precise, in order to avoid differentiating
u
three times, we approximate the quantity
v=|Du|^{2}
in terms of the difference quotient:

\displaystyle v^{h}(x,t)\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{u(x+he_{k},t)-u(x,t)}{h}\right)^{2}.

Then it turns out that
v^{h}
is a subsolution of certain parabolic equations in the classical sense, and so
v
becomes a subsolution of similar parabolic equations in the viscosity sense, by passing the limit together with the stability theorem. In this viscosity formulation, the stronger
C^{2,\beta}
-regularity assumption must be imposed on
u
rather than
C^{2}
, but it remains valid for our purpose to deduce the solvability of (
1.3
).

This paper is organized as follows.
Section

2
consists of several notations, definitions, and auxiliary results for our main theorem.
Section

3
is devoted to the proof of
Theorem

1.2
based on a priori estimates. In
Section

4
, we derive the uniform estimates for approximated solutions and then prove our main theorem,
Theorem

1.1
.

2.1.
Notations

We summarize some basic notations as follows.

(i)

Points: For

x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},~X=(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\text{ and }O=(0,\cdots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.

(ii)

Sets: For a point

\displaystyle Q_{r}(Y)

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x-y|<r\}\times(s-r^{2},s].

\displaystyle\partial_{b}Q_{r}(Y)

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x-y|<r\}\times\{t=s-r^{2}\},

\displaystyle\partial_{c}Q_{r}(Y)

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x-y|=r\}\times\{t=s-r^{2}\},

\displaystyle\partial_{s}Q_{r}(Y)

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x-y|=r\}\times(s-r^{2},s),

\displaystyle\partial_{p}Q_{r}(Y)

\displaystyle=\partial_{b}Q_{r}(y,s)\cup\partial_{c}Q_{r}(y,s)\cup\partial_{s}Q_{r}(y,s).

For convenience, we denote

.

(iii)

Pucci’s operators: Given ellipticity constants

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(M)=\mathcal{M}^{-}(M)

(iv)

Distance functions: Let

(v)

Partial derivatives:
We denote partial derivatives of

u_{t}=\partial_{t}u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial t},\quad D_{i}u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}},\quad\text{and}\quad D_{ij}u=\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}.

(vi)

Hölder spaces: Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be an open set and

.

•

In other words,

is
\frac{\alpha}{2}
-Hölder continuous in

and
\alpha
-Hölder continuous in

.

•

means that
u
is
\frac{\alpha+1}{2}
-Hölder continuous in
t
and
Du
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous in
x
.

•

u\in C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
means that
u_{t}
is
\frac{\alpha}{2}
-Hölder continuous in
t
and

is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous in

.

2.2.
Hypotheses on
F

We assume that the fully nonlinear operator
F:\mathcal{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the following conditions:

(F1)

F
is uniformly elliptic with
F(0)=0
; that is, there exist constants

such that for any

\mathcal{M}^{-}(M-N)\leq F(M)-F(N)\leq\mathcal{M}^{+}(M-N).

(F3)

.

Lemma 3.2

Suppose that

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}&=(1+|Dv|^{2})^{\gamma/2}F(D^{2}u)&&\text{in $Q_{1}$}\\

,

,

Proof.

Let
G(x,t,M)\coloneqq(1+|Dv(x,t)|^{2})^{\gamma/2}F(M)
. Since
v\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{1}})
, the operator
G
satisfies the assumption given in
[
15
, Theorem 4.5]
or
[
16
, Theorem 8.4]
. In other words, the standard Perron’s method guarantees the existence of a viscosity solution
u\in C(\overline{Q_{1}})
of (
3.1
). Furthermore, since
\varphi
satisfies the compatibility condition at corner points,
[
37
, Theorem 3.1 and Theorem 3.2]
yields that
u\in C^{2,\overline{\alpha}}(\overline{Q_{1}})
with a uniform estimate:

\|u\|_{C^{2,\overline{\alpha}}(\overline{Q_{1}})}\leq C,

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
,
\|\varphi\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{Q_{1}})}
, and
\|v\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{1}})}
.

Finally, the uniqueness follows from the comparison principle,
Theorem

2.5
.
∎

We are now ready to provide the reduction of the proof of

1.2
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, and
F
satisfies
(F1)
and
(F2)
. Let
\varphi\in C^{2,\beta}(\overline{Q_{1}})
for some
\beta\in(1/2,1)
, and
\varphi
satisfy the compatibility condition:

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}=(1+|D\varphi|^{2})^{\gamma/2}F(D^{2}\varphi)\quad\text{on $\partial_{c}Q_{1}$}.

Moreover, suppose that there exist constants
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
L>0
(which are independent of
u
and
\sigma
) such that every
C^{2,\beta}(\overline{Q_{1}})
solution
u
of the
\sigma
-Dirichlet problems,

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}&=(1+|Du|^{2})^{\gamma/2}F_{\sigma}(D^{2}u)&&\text{in $Q_{1}$}\\
u&=\sigma\varphi&&\text{on $\partial_{p}Q_{1}$}\end{aligned}\right.

for
F_{\sigma}(M)\coloneqq\sigma F(\sigma^{-1}M)
and
0\leq\sigma\leq 1
, satisfies

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{1}})}\leq L.

Then the Dirichlet problem (
1.3
) is uniquely solvable in
C^{2,\beta}(\overline{Q_{1}})
.

Proof.

The uniqueness follows from the comparison principle,
Theorem

2.5
. For the existence, we fix an exponent
\alpha\in(0,1)
and set a Banach space
\mathcal{B}=C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{1}})
. Then
Lemma

3.2
implies that, for all
v\in\mathcal{B}
, the Dirichlet problem (
3.1
) admits a unique classical solution
u\in\mathcal{B}
. Thus, we define a operator
T:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}
by the relation
u=Tv
. It is easy to check that
u=\sigma Tu
is corresponding to the
\sigma
-Dirichlet problems, (
3.2
).

To apply
Lemma

3.1
, we claim that
T
is continuous and compact. First of all, by the uniform estimate obtained in
Lemma

3.2
, we know that
T
maps a bounded set in
\mathcal{B}=C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{1}})
into a bounded set in
C^{2,\overline{\alpha}}(\overline{Q_{1}})
. By Arzela-Ascoli theorem,
C^{2,\overline{\alpha}}(\overline{Q_{1}})
is precompact in
C^{2}(\overline{Q_{1}})
and so in
\mathcal{B}
, which indicates the compactness of

. Moreover, suppose that a sequence of functions
\{v_{m}\}
converges to

in
\mathcal{B}
. Since

is precompact in

converges to a limit function
u_{0}
in
C^{2}(\overline{Q_{1}})
. By the definition of
T
, we note that

\left\{\begin{aligned} (Tv_{m})_{t}&=(1+|Dv_{m}|^{2})^{\gamma/2}F(D^{2}(Tv_{m}))&&\text{in $Q_{1}$}\\
Tv_{m}&=\varphi&&\text{on $\partial_{p}Q_{1}$}\end{aligned}\right.

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} (Tv)_{t}&=(1+|Dv|^{2})^{\gamma/2}F(D^{2}(Tv))&&\text{in $Q_{1}$}\\
Tv&=\varphi&&\text{on $\partial_{p}Q_{1}$}.\end{aligned}\right.

Letting

, we conclude from the comparison principle,
Theorem

2.5
, that
\lim_{m\to\infty}Tv_{m}=u_{0}=Tv
.
∎

Lemma 3.4

(
L^{\infty}
-estimate)
.

Suppose that
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
F
satisfies
(F1)
. Let
u\in C^{2}(\overline{Q_{1}})
be a classical solution of (
1.3
) with
\varphi\in C(\partial_{p}Q_{1})
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sup_{Q_{1}}|u|\leq\sup_{\partial_{p}Q_{1}}|\varphi|.

Proof.

Since
u\in C^{2}(\overline{Q_{1}})
, we observe that

\displaystyle u_{t}\geq\mathcal{M}^{-}_{\lambda^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime}}(D^{2}u)\quad\text{in $Q_{1}$},

for the ellipticity constants
0<\lambda^{\prime}\leq\Lambda^{\prime}
which depend only on
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
, and
\|Du\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
. Then by applying the Alexandroff-Bakelman-Pucci estimate (see
[
36
, Theorem 3.14]
), we have

\displaystyle u\geq-\sup_{\partial_{p}Q_{1}}|\varphi|\quad\text{in $Q_{1}$}.

A similar argument gives the upper bound for
u
.
∎

Lemma 3.5

(Boundary gradient estimates)
.

Suppose that
\gamma>-2
and
F
satisfies
(F1)
. Let
u\in C^{2}(Q_{1})\cap C^{1}(\overline{Q_{1}})
be a solution of (
1.3
) with
\varphi\in C^{2}(\overline{Q_{1}})
. Then we have

\sup_{\partial_{p}Q_{1}}|Du|\leq C,

where
C
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
,
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
, and
\|\varphi\|_{C^{2}(\overline{Q_{1}})}
.

Proof.

Since
Du=D\varphi
on
\partial_{b}Q_{1}
, it is enough to prove the gradient estimates on
\partial_{s}Q_{1}
. Let
X_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})\in\partial_{s}Q_{1}
. Then there exists a point
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
|x_{0}-y|=1
and
\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:|x-y|<1\}\cap Q_{1}
is empty.

Let
m=\sup_{Q_{1}}|u-\varphi|
and
\Omega=\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:1<|x-y|<1+B(e^{Am}-1)/A\}
. We define the barrier functions

w^{\pm}(x,t)\coloneqq\varphi(x,t)\pm f(d(x)),

where

\displaystyle f(r)\coloneqq\frac{1}{A}\log\left(1+\frac{A}{B}r\right)\quad\text{and}\quad d(x)\coloneqq|x-y|-1,

for constants
A,B>0
to be determined later. Then we have

D_{i}w^{\pm}(x,t)=D_{i}\varphi(x,t)\pm f^{\prime}(d(x))\cdot\frac{x_{i}-y_{i}}{|x-y|}.

We now choose
B^{-1}=2e^{(2+\frac{2}{\gamma+2})Am}\|\varphi\|_{C^{2}(\overline{Q_{1}})}
. Then since

f^{\prime}(r)=\frac{1}{B+Ar}>\frac{1}{Be^{Am}}\geq 2\sup_{Q_{1}}|D\varphi|\quad\text{for all }r\in\left(0,\frac{B}{A}(e^{Am}-1)\right),

we obtain

\displaystyle|Dw^{\pm}|

\displaystyle\leq|D\varphi|+f^{\prime}(d(x))\leq 2f^{\prime}(d(x))<\frac{2}{B}\quad\text{in }\Omega\cap Q_{1}.

Note that

D_{i}d(x)=\frac{x_{i}-y_{i}}{|x-y|},\qquad D_{ij}d(x)=\frac{\delta_{ij}}{|x-y|}-\frac{(x_{i}-y_{i})(x_{j}-y_{j})}{|x-y|^{3}},

and

D_{ij}w^{+}(x,t)=D_{ij}\varphi(x,t)+\frac{D_{ij}d(x)}{B+Ad(x)}-\frac{AD_{i}d(x)D_{j}d(x)}{(B+Ad(x))^{2}}.

Since the only nonzero eigenvalue of
e\otimes e
is
|e|^{2}
for
e\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{+}(Dd\otimes Dd)=\Lambda\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{M}^{-}(Dd\otimes Dd)=\lambda.

Here
x\otimes y
denotes the tensor product of
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then we can choose sufficiently large
A>0
depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
, and
\|\varphi\|_{C^{2}(\overline{Q_{1}})}
so that

\displaystyle F(D^{2}w^{+})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{M}^{+}(D^{2}\varphi)+\frac{1}{B+Ad(x)}\mathcal{M}^{+}(D^{2}d)-\frac{A}{(B+Ad(x))^{2}}\mathcal{M}^{-}(Dd\otimes Dd)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi\|_{C^{2}(\overline{Q_{1}})}+\frac{n\Lambda-\lambda}{B}-\frac{\lambda A}{B^{2}e^{2Am}}

\displaystyle<-3\lambda Ae^{(2+\frac{4}{\gamma+2})Am}\|\varphi\|_{C^{2}(\overline{Q_{1}})}^{2}

in
\Omega\cap Q_{1}
. If
\gamma\geq 0
, then
(1+|Dw^{+}|^{2})^{\gamma/2}\geq 1
and so we can choose
A>0
large enough to obtain

w_{t}^{+}-(1+|Dw^{+}|^{2})^{\gamma/2}F(D^{2}w^{+})\geq\varphi_{t}+3\lambda Ae^{(2+\frac{4}{\gamma+2})Am}\|\varphi\|_{C^{2}(\overline{Q_{1}})}^{2}>0\quad\text{in $\Omega\cap Q_{1}$.}

If
-2<\gamma<0
, then
(1+|Dw^{+}|^{2})^{\gamma/2}\geq 3^{\gamma}B^{-\gamma}
provided that
B\leq\sqrt{5}
. We again choose
A>0
large enough to obtain

\displaystyle w_{t}^{+}-(1+|Dw^{+}|^{2})^{\gamma/2}F(D^{2}w^{+})\geq\varphi_{t}+3\lambda 6^{\gamma}Ae^{(2\gamma+4)Am}\|\varphi\|_{C^{2}(\overline{Q_{1}})}^{2+\gamma}>0\quad\text{in $\Omega\cap Q_{1}$. }

Furthermore, the function
w^{+}
satisfies

\displaystyle w^{+}

\displaystyle\geq\varphi=u\quad\text{on }\Omega\cap\partial_{p}Q_{1},

\displaystyle w^{+}

\displaystyle=\varphi+m\geq u\quad\text{on }\partial_{p}\Omega\cap Q_{1},

\displaystyle w^{+}(X_{0})

\displaystyle=\varphi(X_{0})=u(X_{0}).

Then the comparison principle
Theorem

2.5
implies that

w^{+}\geq u
in
\Omega\cap Q_{1}.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle u(X)-u(X_{0})\leq w^{+}(X)-w^{+}(X_{0})\leq\|\varphi\|_{C^{1}(\overline{Q_{1}})}|X-X_{0}|+\frac{1}{B}d(x)\leq C|X-X_{0}|.

for all
X\in\Omega\cap Q_{1}
. The lower bound follows from a similar argument for
w^{-}
. Since
u\in C^{1}(\overline{Q_{1}})
, we conclude that
|Du|\leq C
on
\partial_{p}Q_{1}
.
∎

We now develop global gradient estimates based on the Bernstein technique in terms of difference quotient, as introduced in
Section

1
. We recall two quantities, namely,
v\coloneqq|Du|^{2}
and its difference quotient counterpart

\displaystyle v^{h}(x,t)\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{u(x+he_{k},t)-u(x,t)}{h}\right)^{2}.

Lemma 3.6

(Global gradient estimates)
.

Suppose that
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, and
F
satisfies
(F1)
and
(F3)
. Let
\beta\in(1/2,1)
and let
u\in C^{2,\beta}(\overline{Q_{1}})
satisfy

u_{t}=(1+|Du|^{2})^{\gamma/2}F(D^{2}u)\quad\text{in }Q_{1}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\sup_{Q_{1}}|Du|=\sup_{\partial_{p}Q_{1}}|Du|.

Proof.

Let
v\coloneqq|Du|^{2}
and

\displaystyle v^{h}(x,t)\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(u_{k}^{h}(x,t)\right)^{2},

where we write

\displaystyle u_{k}^{h}(x,t)\coloneqq\frac{u(x+he_{k},t)-u(x,t)}{h}\quad\text{for }k=1,\cdots,n.

(Step 1: An equation satisfied by
v^{h}
.)
Since
u\in C^{2,\beta}(\overline{Q_{1}})
, the following two equations hold in the classical sense:

\displaystyle u_{t}(x+he_{k},t)

\displaystyle=(1+|Du(x+he_{k},t)|^{2})^{\gamma/2}F(D^{2}u(x+he_{k},t)),

\displaystyle u_{t}(x,t)

\displaystyle=(1+|Du(x,t)|^{2})^{\gamma/2}F(D^{2}u(x,t)).

By combining two equalities, we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{k}^{h}

\displaystyle=(1+|Du(x,t)|^{2})^{\gamma/2}\frac{F(D^{2}u(x+he_{k},t))-F(D^{2}u(x,t))}{h}

\displaystyle\qquad+F(D^{2}u(x+he_{k},t))\frac{(1+|Du(x+he_{k},t)|^{2})^{\gamma/2}-(1+|Du(x,t)|^{2})^{\gamma/2}}{h}.

Since
F
satisfies
(F3)
, we can apply the fundamental theorem of calculus to derive

\displaystyle F(D^{2}u(x+he_{k},t))-F(D^{2}u(x,t))

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{ds}F(sD^{2}u(x+he_{k},t)+(1-s)D^{2}u(x,t))\,ds

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{0}^{1}F_{ij}(sD^{2}u(x+he_{k},t)+(1-s)D^{2}u(x,t))(D_{ij}u(x+he_{k},t)-D_{ij}u(x,t))\,ds

\displaystyle\quad=ha_{ij}^{kh}D_{ij}u_{k}^{h},

where

\displaystyle a_{ij}^{kh}(x,t)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}F_{ij}(sD^{2}u(x+he_{k},t)+(1-s)D^{2}u(x,t))\,ds.

Note that since
F
is uniformly elliptic with ellipticity constants
\lambda
and
\Lambda
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda|\xi|^{2}\leq F_{ij}(M)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq\Lambda|\xi|^{2}\quad\text{for any $M\in\mathcal{S}^{n}$ and $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$},

which implies that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\lambda|\xi|^{2}\leq a_{ij}^{kh}(x,t)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq\Lambda|\xi|^{2}\quad\text{for any $(x,t)\in Q_{1}$ and $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

In a similar way, we can calculate

\displaystyle(1+|Du(x+he_{k},t)|^{2})^{\gamma/2}-(1+|Du(x,t)|^{2})^{\gamma/2}

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{0}^{1}\gamma(1+|\chi^{kh}(s)|^{2})^{\gamma/2-1}\chi^{kh}(s)\cdot(Du(x+he_{k},t)-Du(x,t))\,ds

\displaystyle\quad=h\psi^{kh}\cdot Du_{k}^{h},

where

\displaystyle[\chi^{kh}(s)](x,t)\coloneqq sDu(x+he_{k},t)+(1-s)Du(x,t)

and

\displaystyle\psi^{kh}(x,t)\coloneqq\gamma\int_{0}^{1}(1+|\chi^{kh}(s)|^{2})^{\gamma/2-1}\chi^{kh}(s)\,ds.

Since
u\in C^{2,\beta}(\overline{Q_{1}})
, we have
|[\chi^{kh}(s)](x,t)|\leq 2\|Du\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
and so

(3.4)

\displaystyle|\psi^{kh}(x,t)|\leq\Psi.

Here note that the constant
\Psi>0
can depend only on
\gamma
and
\|Du\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
. By combining previous computations, we arrive at

(3.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{k}^{h}=\left(1+|Du(x,t)|^{2}\right)^{\gamma/2}a_{ij}^{kh}D_{ij}u_{k}^{h}+F(D^{2}u(x+he_{k},t))\psi^{kh}\cdot Du_{k}^{h}.

Multiplying both sides of (
3.5
) by
2u_{k}^{h}
and summing the resulting equations from
k=1
to
n
, we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v^{h}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(1+|Du(x,t)|^{2})^{\gamma/2}a_{ij}^{kh}D_{ij}(u_{k}^{h})^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}F(D^{2}u(x+he_{k},t))\psi^{kh}\cdot D(u_{k}^{h})^{2}

\displaystyle-2\sum_{k=1}^{n}(1+|Du(x,t)|^{2})^{\gamma/2}a_{ij}^{kh}D_{i}u_{k}^{h}D_{j}u_{k}^{h}.

(Step 2: An equation satisfied by
v
.)
In this step, we take a limit
h\to 0
and employ the standard stability argument to find an equation satisfied by
v
. For this purpose, we first observe that,
a_{ij}^{kh}
and
\psi^{kh}
can be approximated by
a_{ij}^{0}
and
\psi^{0}
which are independent of
h>0
; i.e.

\lim_{h\to 0}a_{ij}^{kh}=a_{ij}^{0}\coloneqq F_{ij}(D^{2}u(x,t))

and

\lim_{h\to 0}\psi^{kh}=\psi^{0}\coloneqq\gamma(1+|Du(x,t)|^{2})^{\gamma/2-1}Du(x,t).

To be precise, since
F
is
C^{1,1}
and
u\in C^{2,\beta}(\overline{Q_{1}})
, we have

\displaystyle|a_{ij}^{kh}-a_{ij}^{0}|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{1}F_{ij}(sD^{2}u(x+he_{k},t)+(1-s)D^{2}u(x,t))\,ds-F_{ij}(D^{2}u(x,t))\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\|F\|_{C^{1,1}(\mathcal{S}^{n})}|D^{2}u(x+he_{k},t)-D^{2}u(x,t)|s\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\|F\|_{C^{1,1}(\mathcal{S}^{n})}\|u\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{Q_{1}})}h^{\beta}.

In a similar way, we have

|\psi^{kh}-\psi^{0}|\leq C(\gamma)\|u\|_{C^{1,1}(\overline{Q_{1}})}h

and

|F(D^{2}u(x+he_{k},t))-F(D^{2}u(x,t))|\leq\|F\|_{C^{0,1}(\mathcal{S}^{n})}\|u\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{Q_{1}})}h^{\beta}.

Moreover, we can check that for sufficiently small
h>0
,

\displaystyle|D_{ij}(u_{k}^{h})^{2}|

\displaystyle\leq 2|u_{k}^{h}D_{ij}u_{k}^{h}|+2|D_{i}u_{k}^{h}D_{j}u_{k}^{h}|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|u\|_{C^{0,1}(\overline{Q_{1}})}\cdot\frac{|D_{ij}u(x+he_{k},t)-D_{ij}u(x,t)|}{h}+2\|u\|_{C^{1,1}(\overline{Q_{1}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\|u\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{Q_{1}})}^{2}h^{\beta-1}

and

\displaystyle|D(u_{k}^{h})^{2}|=2|u_{k}^{h}Du_{k}^{h}|\leq 2n\|u\|_{C^{2}(\overline{Q_{1}})}^{2}.

Therefore, recalling (
3.4
) and
\beta>1/2
, the equation (
3.6
) can be written as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v^{h}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}(1+|Du(x,t)|^{2})^{\gamma/2}a_{ij}^{0}D_{ij}(u_{k}^{h})^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{k=1}^{n}F(D^{2}u(x,t))\psi^{0}\cdot D(u_{k}^{h})^{2}-C|D^{2}u|^{2}+O(h^{2\beta-1})\quad\text{as }h\to 0

\displaystyle=(1+|Du(x,t)|^{2})^{\gamma/2}a_{ij}^{0}D_{ij}v^{h}

\displaystyle\qquad+F(D^{2}u(x,t))\psi^{0}\cdot Dv^{h}-C|D^{2}u|^{2}+O(h^{2\beta-1})\quad\text{as }h\to 0.

Here we note that, by the ellipticity given in (
3.3
),

\displaystyle a_{ij}^{kh}D_{i}u_{k}^{h}D_{j}u_{k}^{h}\geq\lambda|Du_{k}^{h}|^{2}=\lambda|D^{2}u|^{2}-o(h)\quad\text{as }h\to 0.

Hence, by applying the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v^{h}

\displaystyle\leq(1+|Du(x,t)|^{2})^{\gamma/2}a_{ij}^{0}D_{ij}v^{h}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\;\;\,+F(D^{2}u(x,t))\psi^{0}\cdot Dv^{h}-C|D^{2}u|^{2}+O(h^{2\beta-1})\quad\text{as }h\to 0

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{M}_{\lambda^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime}}^{+}(D^{2}v^{h})+C|D^{2}u||Dv^{h}|-C|D^{2}u|^{2}+O(h^{2\beta-1})\quad\text{as }h\to 0

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{M}_{\lambda^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime}}^{+}(D^{2}v^{h})+C|Dv^{h}|^{2}+O(h^{2\beta-1})\quad\text{as }h\to 0,

where the ellipticity constants
\lambda^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime}
depend only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
, and
\|Du\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
. Since
u\in C^{2,\beta}(\overline{Q_{1}})
implies that
Dv^{h}\to Dv
uniformly, letting
h\to 0
together with the stability theorem (see
[
12
, Proposition 2.9]
) yields that

\displaystyle v_{t}\leq\mathcal{M}_{\lambda^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime}}^{+}(D^{2}v)+C|Dv|^{2}\quad\text{in the viscosity sense},

where
C>0
depends only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
, and
\|Du\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
.

(Step 3: Global gradient estimates.)

Set
V_{0}\coloneqq\sup_{\partial_{p}Q_{1}}|Du|^{2}
. Then we can choose a universal constant
\mu>0
such that if we let

\displaystyle\overline{v}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\mu}\left(1-e^{\mu(v-V_{0})}\right),

then
\overline{v}
satisfies

\displaystyle\overline{v}_{t}\geq\mathcal{M}_{\lambda^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime}}^{-}(D^{2}\overline{v})\quad\text{in the viscosity sense}.

By the Alexandroff-Bakelman-Pucci estimate, we have

\displaystyle\overline{v}\geq\inf_{\partial_{p}Q_{1}}\overline{v}=0\quad\text{in $Q_{1}$}.

Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle|Du|^{2}=v\leq V_{0}=\sup_{\partial_{p}Q_{1}}|Du|^{2}\quad\text{in $Q_{1}$},

which finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.7

(Interior a priori Hölder estimate for the gradient)

satisfy

u_{t}=(1+|Du|^{2})^{\gamma/2}F(D^{2}u)\quad\text{in }Q_{1}.

Moreover, assume that
|u|+|Du|\leq K
in
Q_{1}
, for some
K>0
. Then there exists a constant
\alpha\in(0,1)
depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
, and

such that for any

, we have

\displaystyle[Du]_{C^{\alpha}(Q^{\prime})}\leq Cd^{-\alpha},

where

and

is a constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
, and
K
. In addition, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\osc_{Q_{r}(Y)}D_{k}u\leq C\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{\alpha}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\osc_{Q_{\rho}(Y)}D_{k}u\quad\text{for $0<r\leq\rho\leq\textnormal{dist}(Y,\partial_{p}Q_{1})$}.

Proof.

Let
w\coloneqq\delta D_{l}u+|Du|^{2}
for

, where
\delta>0
to be determined. As in

, we approximate

in sense of difference quotient:

\displaystyle w^{h}(x,t)\coloneqq\delta u_{l}^{h}(x,t)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(u_{k}^{h}(x,t)\right)^{2},

where we write

\displaystyle u_{k}^{h}(x,t)\coloneqq\frac{u(x+he_{k},t)-u(x,t)}{h}\quad\text{for }k=1,\cdots,n.

By following Step 1 and Step 2 in the proof of
Lemma

3.6
, we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w^{h}\leq\mathcal{M}_{\lambda^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime}}^{+}(D^{2}w^{h})+C|Dw^{h}|^{2}+O(h^{2\beta-1})\quad\text{as }h\to 0,

where the ellipticity constants
\lambda^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime}
depend only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
, and
K
. Since
u\in C^{2,\beta}(\overline{Q_{1}})
implies that
Dw^{h}\to Dw
uniformly, letting
h\to 0
together with the stability theorem yields that

\displaystyle w_{t}\leq\mathcal{M}_{\lambda^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime}}^{+}(D^{2}w)+C|Dw|^{2}\quad\text{in the viscosity sense},

where
C>0
depends only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
, and
K
.

We are now ready to derive uniform interior Hölder estimate for
Du
. We suppose that

u_{t}=(1+|Du|^{2})^{\gamma/2}F(D^{2}u)\quad\text{in }Q_{4r}(x_{0},t_{0}).

For brevity, we write
Q_{4r}=Q_{4r}(x_{0},t_{0})
and
Q_{r}=Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})
. We first choose the constant
\delta=10nK
and choose

so that

\displaystyle\osc_{Q_{4r}}D_{k}u\leq\osc_{Q_{4r}}D_{l}u\quad\text{for all $k\in\{1,..,n\}$}.

We define

\displaystyle w^{\pm}=w_{k}^{\pm}\coloneqq\pm\delta D_{k}u+|Du|^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad W^{\pm}_{k}\coloneqq\sup_{Q_{4r}}w_{k}^{\pm}.

Then it is easy to check that

\displaystyle\osc_{Q_{4r}}|Du|^{2}\leq 2nM\osc_{Q_{4r}}D_{l}u

and so

\displaystyle 8nM\osc_{Q_{4r}}D_{k}u\leq\osc_{Q_{4r}}w^{\pm}\leq 12nM\osc_{Q_{4r}}D_{k}u.

On the other hand, we note that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w^{\pm}\leq\mathcal{M}_{\lambda^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime}}^{+}(D^{2}w^{\pm})+C|Dw^{\pm}|^{2}\quad\text{in the viscosity sense}.

We can choose a universal constant
\mu>0
such that if we let

\displaystyle\overline{w}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\mu}\left(1-e^{\mu(w^{\pm}-W^{\pm})}\right),

then
\overline{w}

\displaystyle\overline{w}_{t}\geq\mathcal{M}_{\lambda^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime}}^{-}(D^{2}\overline{w})\quad\text{in the viscosity sense}.

Since

is a nonnegative supersolution, we can apply the weak Harnack inequality (see
[

, Theorem 6.18]
or
[
37

) to obtain

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{\Theta_{r}}\overline{w}^{\varepsilon_{0}}\,dx\,dt\right)^{1/\varepsilon_{0}}\leq c\,\inf_{Q_{r}}\overline{w}\quad\text{for some $\varepsilon_{0}>0$},

where
\Theta_{r}\coloneqq Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0}-4r^{2})
. Since

\displaystyle c_{1}\,(W^{\pm}-w^{\pm})\leq\overline{w}\leq W^{\pm}-w^{\pm},

we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{\Theta_{r}}(W^{\pm}-w^{\pm})^{\varepsilon_{0}}\,dx\,dt\right)^{1/\varepsilon_{0}}\leq c\,\inf_{Q_{r}}(W^{\pm}-w^{\pm}).

Here we observe that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\inf_{Q_{r}}(W^{\pm}-w^{\pm})=\sup_{Q_{4r}}w^{\pm}-\sup_{Q_{r}}w^{\pm}\leq\osc_{Q_{4r}}w^{\pm}-\osc_{Q_{r}}w^{\pm}.

Moreover, since
w_{k}^{+}+w_{k}^{-}=2|Du|^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle(W^{+}-w^{+})+(W^{-}-w^{-})

\displaystyle\geq\delta\osc_{Q_{4r}}D_{k}u+2\left(\inf_{Q_{4r}}|Du|^{2}-|Du|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 6nM\osc_{Q_{4r}}D_{k}u

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\osc_{Q_{4r}}w^{\pm}\quad\text{in $Q_{4r}(\supset\Theta_{r})$}.

Thus, the inequality

(3.9)

\displaystyle W^{\pm}-w^{\pm}\geq\frac{1}{4}\osc_{Q_{4r}}w^{\pm}

holds for either
w^{+}
or
w^{-}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume it holds for
w^{+}
. By combining the estimate (
3.7
), (
3.8
), and (
3.9
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\osc_{Q_{r}}w^{+}\leq\frac{4c-1}{4c}\osc_{Q_{4r}}w^{+}.

By following the standard iteration argument (see
[
26
, Lemma 13.5]
, for example), we conclude that there exists a constant
\alpha\in(0,1)
depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
, and
K
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\osc_{Q_{r}(Y)}D_{k}u\leq C\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{\alpha}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\osc_{Q_{\rho}(Y)}D_{k}u\quad\text{for $0<r\leq\rho\leq\textnormal{dist}(Y,\partial_{p}Q_{1})$}

and so

\displaystyle\osc_{Q_{r}}D_{k}u\leq Cr^{\alpha}\quad\text{for any $k=1,\cdots,n$},

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
, and

.
∎

4.
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity via approximations

In this section, we consider an approximated solution
u^{\varepsilon}
,
\varepsilon>0
, of a regularized equation which will be specified soon. We first regularize the operator
F
by standard mollification technique. To be precise, we extend the domain of
F
from
\mathcal{S}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}}
by considering
F(M)=F\left(\frac{M+M^{T}}{2}\right)
. We also let
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}})
be a standard mollifier satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}}}\psi\,dM=1
and
\mathrm{supp}\,\psi\subset\{M\in\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}}:\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}M_{ij}^{2}\leq 1\}
, and define
\psi_{\varepsilon}(M)=\varepsilon^{-n^{2}}\psi(M/\varepsilon)
. If we define
F^{\varepsilon}
as

\displaystyle F^{\varepsilon}(M)\coloneqq F\ast\psi_{\varepsilon}(M)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}}}F(M-N)\psi_{\varepsilon}(N)\,dN.

It is easy to check that
F^{\varepsilon}
is uniformly elliptic (with the same ellipticity constants
\lambda,\Lambda
) and smooth. Moreover,
F^{\varepsilon}
is convex whenever
F
is convex. Finally, since
F
is Lipschitz continuous,
F^{\varepsilon}
converges to
F
uniformly.

We now consider the following regularized problem:

(4.1)

\partial_{t}u^{\varepsilon}=(\varepsilon^{2}+|Du^{\varepsilon}|^{2})^{\gamma/2}F^{\varepsilon}(D^{2}u^{\varepsilon}).

In the remaining of this section, we suppose that
F
satisfies the hypotheses
(F1)
,
(F2)
, and
(F3)
. We first derive uniform
C^{1,\alpha}
-estimates of
u^{\varepsilon}
for some universal constant
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and then develop
C^{1,\alpha}
-estimate of a viscosity solution
u
of (
1.2
), which finishes the proof of
Theorem

1.1
. For simplicity, we may write
u
and
F
instead of
u^{\varepsilon}
and
F^{\varepsilon}
, if there is no confusion.

4.1.
Uniform Lipschitz estimates

We begin with uniform log-Lipschitz estimates, whose proof is based on the Ishii-Lions’ method
[
29
]
. It is noteworthy that for

,

, and

Lemma 4.1

(Uniform log-Lipschitz estimate)
.

Let
u
be a viscosity solution of (
4.1
) in
Q_{1}
with
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\gamma>-2
. Then there exist two positive constants
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
, and
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
such that for every
(x_{0},t_{0})\in Q_{3/4}
, we have

\displaystyle u(x,t)-u(y,t)\leq L_{1}|x-y||\log|x-y||+\frac{L_{2}}{2}|x-x_{0}|^{2}+\frac{L_{2}}{2}|y-x_{0}|^{2}+\frac{L_{2}}{2}(t-t_{0})^{2}

for all
t\in[t_{0}-1,t_{0}]
and
x,y\in B_{3/4}(x_{0})
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
(x_{0},t_{0})=(0,0)
. We claim that

(4.2)

\displaystyle m\coloneqq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\overline{B_{3/4}}\\
t\in[-1,0]\end{subarray}}\left[u(x,t)-u(y,t)-L_{1}\phi(|x-y|)-\frac{L_{2}}{2}|x|^{2}-\frac{L_{2}}{2}|y|^{2}-\frac{L_{2}}{2}t^{2}\right]\leq 0,

where

\displaystyle\phi(r)\coloneqq\begin{cases}-r\log r\quad&\text{for $r\in[0,e^{-1}]$}\\
e^{-1}\quad&\text{for $r\geq e^{-1}$}.\end{cases}

We prove (
4.2
) by contradiction: suppose that the positive maximum
m
is attained at
t\in[-1,0]
and
x,y\in\overline{B_{3/4}}
. It immediately follows that
x\neq y
and

\displaystyle L_{1}\phi(|x-y|)+\frac{L_{2}}{2}|x|^{2}+\frac{L_{2}}{2}|y|^{2}+\frac{L_{2}}{2}t^{2}\leq 2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}.

In particular,
|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}+|t|^{2}\leq 12\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}/L_{2}
and

\displaystyle\phi(\theta)\leq\frac{2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}}{L_{1}},\quad\text{where $\theta=|a|$ and $a=x-y$}.

We choose
L_{2}=L_{2}(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})})>0
large enough to ensure
t\in(-1,0]
and
x,y\in B_{3/4}
. Moreover, by choosing
L_{1}
sufficiently large so that
\theta\in(0,e^{-1})
is small enough, we obtain

(4.3)

\displaystyle\phi(\theta)\geq 2\theta,\quad\phi^{\prime}(\theta)\geq 1,\quad\text{and so}\quad\theta\leq\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}}{L_{1}}.

Furthermore, by applying the parabolic version of Jensen-Ishii’s lemma
[
14
, Theorem 8.3]
, we observe that, for every
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, there exist
M_{x},M_{y}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
such that

(i)

(a_{x},p_{x},M_{x})\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}^{2,+}u(x,t)\quad\text{and}\quad(-a_{y},p_{y},-M_{y})\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}^{2,-}u(y,t)
;

(ii)

\begin{pmatrix}M_{x}&0\\
0&M_{y}\end{pmatrix}\leq L_{1}\begin{pmatrix}N&-N\\
-N&N\end{pmatrix}+(2L_{2}+\varepsilon)\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
0&I\end{pmatrix};

(iii)

a_{x}+a_{y}=L_{2}t
,

where

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle\coloneqq L_{1}\phi^{\prime}(\theta)\hat{a},\quad p_{x}\coloneqq p+L_{2}x,\quad p_{y}\coloneqq p-L_{2}y,

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle\coloneqq\phi^{\prime\prime}(\theta)\hat{a}\otimes\hat{a}+\frac{\phi^{\prime}(\theta)}{\theta}(I-\hat{a}\otimes\hat{a})\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{a}\coloneqq\frac{a}{|a|}=\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}.

It follows from (i), (iii), and
Lemma

2.4
that

\displaystyle L_{2}t

\displaystyle\leq(\varepsilon^{2}+|p_{x}|^{2})^{\gamma/2}F(M_{x})-(\varepsilon^{2}+|p_{y}|^{2})^{\gamma/2}F(-M_{y})

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon^{2}+|p_{y}|^{2})^{\gamma/2}(F(M_{x})-F(-M_{y}))+\left((\varepsilon^{2}+|p_{x}|^{2})^{\gamma/2}-(\varepsilon^{2}+|p_{y}|^{2})^{\gamma/2}\right)F(M_{x})

\displaystyle\leq(\varepsilon^{2}+|p_{y}|^{2})^{\gamma/2}\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(M_{x}+M_{y})+\left|(\varepsilon^{2}+|p_{x}|^{2})^{\gamma/2}-(\varepsilon^{2}+|p_{y}|^{2})^{\gamma/2}\right|\|M_{x}\|=:T_{1}+T_{2}.

Before we estimate two terms
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
, we first provide appropriate
L^{\infty}
-bounds for
p
,
p_{x}
,
p_{y}
,
M_{x}
, and
M_{y}
. By choosing
L_{1}
large enough, we may assume
|p|\gg L_{2}
which implies that
|p|/2\leq|p_{x}|,|p_{y}|\leq 2|p|.

Since the matrix inequality (ii) yields that

\displaystyle M_{x},M_{y}\leq\left(L_{1}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(\theta)}{\theta}+3L_{2}\right)I,

we have

\displaystyle F(M_{x})\geq(\varepsilon^{2}+|p_{x}|^{2})^{-\gamma/2}L_{2}t+\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}+|p_{y}|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}+|p_{x}|^{2}}\right)^{\gamma/2}F(-M_{y})\geq-C\left(|p|^{-\gamma}+L_{1}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(\theta)}{\theta}+1\right),

where
C
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
, and
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
. Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|M_{x}\|,\|M_{y}\|\leq C\left(|p|^{-\gamma}+L_{1}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(\theta)}{\theta}+1\right).

We are now ready to estimate
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
. For
T_{2}
, an application of mean value theorem gives

\displaystyle T_{2}\leq C|p|^{\gamma-1}\|M_{x}\||x+y|\leq C\left(|p|^{-1}+\frac{|p|^{\gamma}}{\theta}+|p|^{\gamma-1}\right).

For
T_{1}
, we again employ the previous matrix inequality given in (ii). First, by evaluating a vector of the form
(\xi,\xi)
for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle(M_{x}+M_{y})\xi\cdot\xi\leq 6L_{2}|\xi|^{2},

which implies that any eigenvalues of
M_{x}+M_{y}
are less than
6L_{2}
. Next, by considering a special vector
(\hat{a},-\hat{a})
, we arrive at

\displaystyle(M_{x}+M_{y})\hat{a}\cdot\hat{a}\leq 4L_{1}\phi^{\prime\prime}(\theta)+6L_{2}.

In other words, at least one eigenvalue of
M_{x}+M_{y}
is less than
4L_{1}\phi^{\prime\prime}(\theta)+6L_{2}

Lemma 4.4

,

,

,
\Lambda
, and

Lemma 4.6

\Lambda

\displaystyle\leq u(\overline{x},-1)-u(\underline{x},-1)+\begin{cases}10nA(1+16A^{2})^{\gamma/2}\Lambda&\text{if }\gamma\geq 0\\

. If we let

\widetilde{u}(x,t)=\frac{1}{\tau^{m}(1-\delta)^{m}}u(\tau^{m}x,\tau^{2m}(1-\delta)^{-m\gamma}t),

then
\widetilde{u}
solves

\widetilde{u}_{t}=(|D\widetilde{u}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}(1-\delta)^{-2m})^{\gamma/2}\widetilde{F}(D^{2}\widetilde{u})\quad\text{in $Q_{1}$},

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}(M)\coloneqq\frac{\tau^{m}}{(1-\delta)^{m}}F\left(\frac{(1-\delta)^{m}}{\tau^{m}}M\right)\ \text{whose ellipticity constants are the same as $F$.}

Furthermore, since
\|D\widetilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\leq 1
, we observe that

\underset{x\in B_{1}}{\textnormal{osc}}\,\widetilde{u}(x,t)\leq 2\quad\text{for all }t\in[-1,0].

By
Lemma

4.6
, we have
{\textnormal{osc}}_{Q_{1}}\,\widetilde{u}\leq C
and hence

(4.15)

\underset{Q_{\tau^{m}}^{(1-\delta)^{m}}}{\textnormal{osc}}\,u\leq C\tau^{m}(1-\delta)^{m}.

(Case 1:
\bm{m=m_{1}}
.)
In this case, we have
1/4\leq\varepsilon^{2}(1-\delta)^{-2m}\leq 1
. Thus, we can apply
Theorem

1.2
,
Lemma

3.7
, and the Schauder estimate (see
[
37
]
) to guarantee the existence of
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|q|\leq 1
such that

|D\widetilde{u}(x,t)-q|\leq C(|x|+|t|^{1/2})\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in Q_{\tau}^{1-\delta}

and
|\widetilde{u}_{t}|\leq C
in
Q_{\tau}^{1-\delta}
. By scaling back, we have

(4.16)

|Du(x,t)-(1-\delta)^{m}q|\leq C(|x|+|t|^{1/2})\leq C(|x|^{\alpha}+|t|^{\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha\gamma}})\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in Q_{\tau^{m+1}}^{(1-\delta)^{m+1}}

and

(4.17)

|u(x,t)-u(x,0)|\leq C\tau^{-m}(1-\delta)^{m(1+\gamma)}|t|\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in Q_{\tau^{m+1}}^{(1-\delta)^{m+1}},

where we used that
\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha\gamma}<\frac{1}{2}
for all
\gamma>-1
. Combining (
4.14
) and (
4.16
) gives

|Du(x,t)-(1-\delta)^{m}q|\leq C(|x|^{\alpha}+|t|^{\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha\gamma}})

for all
(x,t)\in Q_{\tau^{m}}^{(1-\delta)^{m}}
which is extensible
Q_{1/2}
. Furthermore, it follows from (
4.17
) that

(4.18)

|u(0,t)-u(0,0)|\leq C\tau^{i}(1-\delta)^{i}\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in Q_{\tau^{i}}^{(1-\delta)^{i}},\,i>m.

Similarly, (
4.15
) and (
4.18
) implies that

|u(0,t)-u(0,0)|\leq C|t|^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2-\alpha\gamma}}\quad\text{for all }t\in(-1/4,0].

(Case 2:
\bm{m=m_{2}}
.)
Since
m
was the nonnegative integer such that (
4.12
) does not hold, we observe

(4.19)

|\{X\in Q_{\tau^{m}}^{(1-\delta)^{m}}:Du(X)\cdot e\leq l(1-\delta)^{m}\}|\leq\mu|Q_{\tau^{m}}^{(1-\delta)^{m}}|

for some unit vector
e\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By recalling the definition of
\widetilde{u}
, (
4.19
) can be written as

|\{X\in Q_{1}:D\widetilde{u}(X)\cdot e\leq l\}|\leq\mu|Q_{1}|.

For
l=1-\varepsilon_{0}^{2}/2
, we can see that

\{X\in Q_{1}:|D\widetilde{u}(X)-e|>\varepsilon_{0}\}\subset\{X\in Q_{1}:D\widetilde{u}(X)\cdot e\leq l\}

and hence if we take
\mu=\varepsilon_{1}/|Q_{1}|
, then we have

|\{X\in Q_{1}:|D\widetilde{u}(X)-e|>\varepsilon_{0}\}|\leq|\{X\in Q_{1}:D\widetilde{u}(X)\cdot e\leq l\}|\leq\varepsilon_{1}.

Since
l
and
\mu
were arbitrary, we can take
\varepsilon_{0}
and
\varepsilon_{1}
small enough. Thus, by
Lemma

4.8
, for a positive constant
\eta
, there exists a constant
a\in\mathbb{R}
such that

|\widetilde{u}(x,t)-a-e\cdot x|\leq\eta\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in Q_{1/2}.

By
Theorem

4.9
, there exists
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

|D\widetilde{u}(x,t)-q|\leq C(|x|+|t|^{1/2})\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in Q_{\tau}^{1-\delta}

and
|\widetilde{u}_{t}|\leq C
in
Q_{\tau}^{1-\delta}
. Finally, the desired conclusion is obtained in the same way as in the first case.
∎

The following lemma illustrates that a viscosity solution
u
of (
1.2
) can be approximated by a sequence of regularized solutions
\{u^{\varepsilon}\}
of (
4.1
).

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
\varphi\in C(\overline{Q_{1}})
. Then the Dirichlet problem (
1.3
) is uniquely solvable in
C(\overline{Q_{1}})\cap C^{\infty}(Q_{1})
. In particular, for any
\varepsilon>0
, the regularized Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u^{\varepsilon}&=(\varepsilon^{2}+|Du^{\varepsilon}|^{2})^{\gamma/2}F^{\varepsilon}(D^{2}u^{\varepsilon})&&\text{in $Q_{1}$}\\
u^{\varepsilon}&=u&&\text{on $\partial_{p}Q_{1}$},\end{aligned}\right.

is uniquely solvable in
C(\overline{Q_{1}})\cap C^{\infty}(Q_{1})
.

Proof.

Fix
\beta\in(1/2,1)
. By arguing as in
[
33
,
34
]
, choose a sequence of functions
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that
\varphi_{k}\in C^{2,\beta}(\overline{Q_{1}})
,
\|\varphi_{k}-\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\to 0
, and
\varphi_{k}
satisfies the compatibility condition at the corner points:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi_{k}=(1+|D\varphi_{k}|^{2})^{\gamma/2}F(D^{2}\varphi_{k})\quad\text{on $\partial_{c}Q_{1}$}.

By applying
Theorem

1.2
, there exists a unique solution
u_{k}\in C^{2,\beta}(\overline{Q_{1}})
of the Dirichlet problem (
1.3
). Moreover, an application of
Lemma

4.2
yields that, for
r\in(0,1)
, there exists a constant
C>0
which depends only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
,
\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
, and
r
such that

\displaystyle\|Du_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{(r+1)/2})}\leq C.

Then, by applying
Lemma

3.7
, there exist constants
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
C>0
which depend only on
n,\lambda,\Lambda,\gamma
,
\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
, and
r
such that

\displaystyle\|u_{k}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{r}})}\leq C.

Thus, by
Lemma

4.11
, Arzela-Ascoli theorem, and the standard diagonal argument, we can extract a subsequence
\{u_{k}\}
which converges to a limit function
u\in C(\overline{Q_{1}})
such that

(i)

u\in C^{1,\alpha_{r}}(\overline{Q_{r}})
for
r\in(0,1)
;

(ii)

u_{k}\to u
uniformly in
C^{1,\alpha^{\prime}_{r}}(\overline{Q_{r}})
for any
\alpha^{\prime}_{r}\in(0,\alpha_{r})
.

Since
u_{k}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u_{k}&=(1+|Du_{k}|^{2})^{\gamma/2}F(D^{2}u_{k})&&\text{in $Q_{1}$}\\
u_{k}&=\varphi_{k}&&\text{on $\partial_{p}Q_{1}$},\end{aligned}\right.

the stability theorem yields that
u
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}&=(1+|Du|^{2})^{\gamma/2}F(D^{2}u)&&\text{in $Q_{1}$}\\
u&=\varphi&&\text{on $\partial_{p}Q_{1}$}.\end{aligned}\right.

Finally, the Schauder theory and the bootstrap argument guarantee the smoothness of
u
, i.e.,

\displaystyle u\in C^{1,\alpha}\implies Du\in C^{\alpha}\implies u\in C^{2,\alpha}\implies Du\in C^{1,\alpha}\implies u\in C^{3,\alpha}\quad\cdots.

We refer to
[
12
,
26
,
34
,
37
]
for details.
∎

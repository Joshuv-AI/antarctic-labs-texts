Hiroyoshi TAMORI

Abstract
. We classify completely prime primitive ideals whose associated varieties are the closure of the minimal nilpotent orbit of 𝔤 = 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( n , ℂ ) \mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C}) , and classify irreducible ( 𝔤 , 𝔨 ) (\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k}) -modules which have those ideals as annihilators. 1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a simple real Lie group and
\mathfrak{g}_{0}:=\mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}\nolimits(G)
.
Fix a maximally compact subalgebra
\mathfrak{k}_{0}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
.
Assume that the complexification
\mathfrak{g}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is simple.
Joseph proved that if
\mathfrak{g}
is not of type A,
then the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
has a unique completely prime ideal
J_{0}
whose associated variety, which is a subset of
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}:=\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{C})
, is the closure of the minimal nilpotent coadjoint orbit
\mathcal{O}^{\min}

[
10
]
,
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
.
Minimal representations of
G
are defined as admissible irreducible representations where the annihilators of the associated
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})
-modules are equal to the Joseph ideal
J_{0}
.

Minimal representations are classified
and are known to be infinitesimally equivalent to unitary representations
[
20
, Corollary 5.1]
.
In the Kirillov-Kostant orbit philosophy,
they are considered to be attached to
G
-orbits in
\mathcal{O}^{\min}\cap\{X\in\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}\mid X(\mathfrak{g}_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}\}
, and are considered to be a part of building blocks of the unitary dual of
G
.

Moreover, minimal representations are of interest in physics.
For example, the oscillator representation of the metaplectic group (whose irreducible components are minimal) has a realization as the bound states of the quantum harmonic oscillator,
and the minimal representation of the indefinite orthogonal group
\mathrm{O}(p,2)(p\geq 6)
has a realization as some solution space of the wave equation in the Minkowski space (see
[
13
, Theorem 1.4]
for example).

On the other hand,
there exist representations called “minimal” even when
\mathfrak{g}
is simple of type
A
, that is, when the definition of the Joseph ideal (hence the one of minimality) is not given.
Here the vague term “minimal” means that there is an interest in physics as in the previous examples, the
\mathfrak{k}
-types are as simple as possible (called pencil, see Corollary
3.5
(
2
) for the definition),
or there is a relation with the minimal nilpotent orbit.
Such representations include
the ladder representation of
\mathrm{O}(2,4)
(which is locally isomorphic to
\mathrm{SU}(2,2)
)
expressing the bound states of the Hydrogen atom
[
12
, Remark 3.6.2 (3)]
and
the irreducible unitary representations of the double cover
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}}(3,\mathbb{R})
of
\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
given by Torasso (see
[
21
, Theoreme VII.1]
,
[
19
]
).

The aim of this paper is to define minimality in terms of annihilators of
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})
-modules so that the above representations are minimal, and to classify minimal representations for connected simple real Lie groups of type
A_{n-1}

(n\geq 2)
.
Hence this study can be regarded as a small step of classifying irreducible
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})
-modules associated with a fixed completely prime primitive ideal.

Our first claim is that the two-sided ideals in
U(\mathfrak{g})

whose associated graded ideals coincide with the ideal defined by
\mathcal{O}^{\min}
are parametrized by the complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
(see Theorem
2.8
).
If we consider a simple Lie algebra not of type
A
, that condition characterizes the Joseph ideal
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Let us write
J_{a}
for the ideal corresponding to
a\in\mathbb{C}
(see Definition
2.7
for the precise definition).
We say that an irreducible
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})
-module is
a
-minimal
if its annihilator equals
J_{a}
, and is
minimal
if it is
a
-minimal for some
a\in\mathbb{C}
(see Definition
3.1
).

The associated variety gives us a necessary condition for the existence of minimal
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})
-modules.
If a minimal
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})
-module exists, then
\mathcal{O}^{\min}\cap\{X\in\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}\mid X(\mathfrak{g}_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}\}
is not empty. Hence it suffices to consider the real forms
\mathfrak{su}(p,q)

(p,q>0)
and
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{R})
by
[
18
, Proposition 4.1]
.

The main theorems in this paper are Theorems
5.1
and
5.3
, which classify
a
-minimal

-modules for any

and
\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{su}(p,q)

(p,q>0)
,
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{R})
.

For

They are related with the induction from maximal (

From the viewpoint of the orbit method, the situation matches to the fact that

and semisimple orbits through nonzero characters of the parabolic subalgebras.

Our construction of a minimal

-module for

(p,q>0)
and the one for

are different.
We realize it as the irreducible quotient of a Verma module for

\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{R})
.
The latter construction for Torasso’s representation (

) was given in

, and the one for genuine

We give a general framework for the construction in Section

The proof of exhaustion uses the facts that highest weights of
\mathfrak{k}
-types of a minimal

-module lie in some specified lattice,

-minimal

-modules are isomorphic to each other if they have a common

-type (see Proposition

).
The argument is the same as the classification when
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is not of type

[

]
.
We also need to show the injectivity of some intertwining differential operator
for the nonexistence of minimal

-modules with some

).

2.
Primitive ideals and irreducible highest weight modules

Let
n\geq 2
and
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
.
In this section, we classify two-sided ideals
J
in the enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
whose associated graded ideals
\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits J
are the ideal
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{O}^{\min})
defined by the minimal nilpotent coadjoint orbit
\mathcal{O}^{\min}
of
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
.
Moreover, we obtain the classification of irreducible highest weight modules whose annihilators are equal to one of these ideals.

For
A\in\mathfrak{g}
and
1\leq i,j\leq n
, we write
A_{i,j}
for the
(i,j)
-th component of the matrix
A
.
We write
\mathfrak{h}
for the Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
consisting of diagonal matrices.
Let
e_{i}

(1\leq i\leq n)
be an element of the dual
\mathfrak{h}^{\vee}
defined by
e_{i}(H)=H_{i,i}
for
H\in\mathfrak{h}
.
Then the set of roots for
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
is written as
\Sigma=\Set{e_{i}-e_{j}}{1\leq i,j\leq n}
.
We fix a set of positive roots
\Sigma^{+}=\Set{e_{i}-e_{j}}{i<j}
.
For an element
\lambda
in
\mathfrak{h}^{\vee}
, we define
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{C}

(1\leq i\leq n)
by
\lambda=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}e_{i}
and
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}=0
. Let us write
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
for
\lambda
.

We first describe the irreducible decomposition of the second symmetric power
S^{2}(\mathfrak{g})
.
Let
I_{n}
be the unit
n
-by-
n
matrix,
E_{i,j}
the matrix whose
(i,j)
-th component is one and the others are zero.
Set

T_{i,j}:=E_{i,j}-\frac{\delta_{i,j}}{n}I_{n}.

Here
\delta_{i,j}
denotes the Kronecker delta.
We remark that
A=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}A_{i,j}T_{i,j}
holds for every
A\in\mathfrak{g}
.
We define
a nondegenerate invariant bilinear form
B
on
\mathfrak{g}
by

(2.1)

\displaystyle B(X,Y):=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits(XY)\quad(X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}).

Put

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Omega:=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}T_{i,j}T_{j,i}\in S^{2}(\mathfrak{g}).

Proposition 2.1
.

(1)

As a
\mathfrak{g}
-module, the second symmetric power
S^{2}(\mathfrak{g})
irreducibly decomposes to

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}F(2e_{1}-2e_{n})\oplus F(e_{1}+e_{2}-e_{n-1}-e_{n})\oplus F(e_{1}-e_{n})\oplus F(0)&\text{ if $n\geq 4$,}\\
F(2e_{1}-2e_{n})\oplus F(e_{1}-e_{n})\oplus F(0)&\text{ if $n=3$,}\\
F(2e_{1}-2e_{n})\oplus F(0)&\text{ if $n=2$.}\end{cases}

Here
F(\lambda)
denotes the irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule of
S^{2}(\mathfrak{g})
with highest weight
\lambda
and we have
F(0)=\mathbb{C}\Omega
.

(2)

For
n\geq 4
, the
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule
F(e_{1}+e_{2}-e_{n-1}-e_{n})\oplus F(e_{1}-e_{n})\oplus F(0)
is equal to

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\nolimits\Set{T_{i,j}T_{k,l}-T_{i,l}T_{k,j}}{1\leq i,j,k,l\leq n}.

(3)

For
n\geq 3
, the linear map

\mathfrak{g}\to F(e_{1}-e_{n});A\mapsto\sum_{i,j,k}A_{i,j}T_{i,k}T_{k,j}

is a
\mathfrak{g}
-isomorphism.

Proof.

By
[T_{r,s},T_{i,j}]=\delta_{i,s}T_{r,j}-\delta_{r,j}T_{i,s}
, we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle[T_{r,s},T_{i,j}T_{k,l}-T_{i,l}T_{k,j}]

\displaystyle=(\delta_{i,s}T_{r,j}-\delta_{r,j}T_{i,s})T_{k,l}+T_{i,j}(\delta_{k,s}T_{r,l}-\delta_{r,l}T_{k,s})

(2.6)

\displaystyle\quad-(\delta_{i,s}T_{r,l}-\delta_{r,l}T_{i,s})T_{k,j}-T_{i,l}(\delta_{k,s}T_{r,j}-\delta_{r,j}T_{k,s})

(2.7)

\displaystyle=\delta_{i,s}(T_{r,j}T_{k,l}-T_{r,l}T_{k,j})-\delta_{r,j}(T_{i,s}T_{k,l}-T_{i,l}T_{k,s})

(2.8)

\displaystyle\quad+\delta_{k,s}(T_{i,j}T_{r,l}-T_{i,l}T_{r,j})-\delta_{r,l}(T_{i,j}T_{k,s}-T_{i,s}T_{k,j}).

Therefore the subspace (
2.4
) is invariant under the action of
\mathfrak{g}
and has a highest weight vector
T_{1,n-1}T_{2,n}-T_{1,n}T_{2,n-1}
of weight
e_{1}+e_{2}-e_{n-1}-e_{n}
for
n\geq 2
.

Let us consider a vector
\sum_{k}T_{i,k}T_{k,j}=\sum_{k}(T_{i,k}T_{k,j}-T_{i,j}T_{k,k})
, which belongs to (
2.4
).
We see

(2.9)

\displaystyle[T_{r,s},\sum_{k}T_{i,k}T_{k,j}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\{(\delta_{i,s}T_{r,k}-\delta_{r,k}T_{i,s})T_{k,j}+T_{i,k}(\delta_{k,s}T_{r,j}-\delta_{r,j}T_{k,s})\}

(2.10)

\displaystyle=\delta_{i,s}\sum_{k}T_{r,k}T_{k,j}-\delta_{r,j}\sum_{k}T_{i,k}T_{k,s}.

Therefore
[\mathfrak{g},\Omega]=0
and the map in (
3
) is a
\mathfrak{g}
-homomorphism into
S^{2}(\mathfrak{g})
.
For
n\geq 3
, the submodule (
2.4
) has highest weight
e_{1}-e_{n}
since
\sum_{k}T_{1,k}T_{k,n}
is nonzero.

Furthermore, (
2.4
) has highest weight
0
as it contains
\Omega
, and it does not contain
a nonzero multiple of
T_{1,n}T_{1,n}
, which is a highest weight vector of weight
2e_{1}-2e_{n}
in
S^{2}(\mathfrak{g})
.

By the Weyl dimension formula, we see the dimension of (
2.3
) is equal to the square of
n(n-1)/2
,
which is the dimension of
S^{2}(\mathfrak{g})
.
Hence (
2.3
) equals the direct sum of
F(2e_{1}-2e_{n})
and (
2.4
), and our claim follows.
∎

For
1\leq i<j<k<l\leq n
, we put

\mathcal{M}_{i,j,k,l}:=\Set{a_{1}(T_{i,j}+T_{k,l})+a_{2}(T_{i,k}+T_{j,l})+a_{3}(T_{i,l}+T_{j,k})\in\mathfrak{g}}{\sum_{r=1}^{3}a_{r}=0}.

Define a injective linear map

T\colon\Set{A\in\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})}{A_{i,j}=0\text{ for $i\geq j$}}\to S^{2}(\mathfrak{g})_{0};A\mapsto\sum_{i,j}A_{i,j}(T_{i,i}T_{j,j}-T_{i,j}T_{j,i}).

Proposition 2.2
.

Assume
n\geq 4
. Then the zero weight space
F(e_{1}+e_{2}-e_{n-1}-e_{n})_{0}
is equal to

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\nolimits\Set{T(\mathcal{M}_{i,j,k,l})}{1\leq i<j<k<l\leq n}.

Proof.

Define an
\mathfrak{su}(n)
-invariant Hermitian inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
on
\mathfrak{g}
by
(X,Y):=B(X,{}^{t}\overline{Y})=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits(X{}^{t}\overline{Y})
(
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
), where
\overline{Y}
denotes the complex conjugate of
Y
.
It induces an
\mathfrak{su}(n)
-invariant Hermitian inner product on
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
and one on
S^{2}(\mathfrak{g})
via the symmetrization, which we write
(\cdot,\cdot)
by abuse of notation.
By the assumption
n\geq 4
and Proposition
2.1
and its proof, the zero weight space
F(e_{1}+e_{2}-e_{n-1}-e_{n})_{0}
is the orthogonal complement of
F(e_{1}-e_{n})_{0}+F(0)=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\nolimits\Set{\sum_{k}T_{i,k}T_{k,i}}{1\leq i\leq n}
in the zero weight space
\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\nolimits\Set{T_{i,i}T_{j,j}-T_{i,j}T_{j,i}}{i<j}
of (
2.4
).
In particular,
\dim F(e_{1}+e_{2}-e_{n-1}-e_{n})_{0}=n(n-1)/2-n=n(n-3)/2
.

Let us prove that
T(\mathcal{M}_{i,j,k,l})
is contained in
F(e_{1}+e_{2}-e_{n-1}-e_{n})_{0}
for any
i<j<k<l
.
By

(T_{i,j},T_{r,s})=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits\left(\left(E_{i,j}-\frac{\delta_{i,j}}{n}I_{n}\right)\left(E_{s,r}-\frac{\delta_{r,s}}{n}I_{n}\right)\right)=\delta_{i,r}\delta_{j,s}-\frac{\delta_{i,j}\delta_{r,s}}{n},

we have

(2.12)

\displaystyle 2\Bigl(T_{i,i}T_{j,j}-T_{i,j}T_{j,i},\sum_{s}T_{r,s}T_{s,r}\Bigr)

(2.13)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}\{(T_{i,i},T_{r,s})(T_{j,j},T_{s,r})+(T_{j,j},T_{r,s})(T_{i,i},T_{s,r})

(2.14)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-(T_{i,j},T_{r,s})(T_{j,i},T_{s,r})-(T_{j,i},T_{r,s})(T_{i,j},T_{s,r})\}

(2.15)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}\left\{2\left(\delta_{i,r}\delta_{i,s}-\frac{\delta_{r,s}}{n}\right)\left(\delta_{j,r}\delta_{j,s}-\frac{\delta_{r,s}}{n}\right)-\delta_{i,r}\delta_{j,s}-\delta_{j,r}\delta_{i,s}\right\}

(2.16)

\displaystyle=2\left(\delta_{i,r}-\frac{1}{n}\right)\left(\delta_{j,r}-\frac{1}{n}\right)-\delta_{i,r}-\delta_{j,r}

(2.17)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{n^{2}}-\left(\frac{2}{n}+1\right)(\delta_{i,r}+\delta_{j,r})

for any
i<j
and
1\leq r\leq n
.
Since
\sum_{r,s}A_{r,s}=0
and
\sum_{r}({A}_{r,r_{0}}+A_{r_{0},r})=0
for any
A\in\mathcal{M}_{i,j,k,l}
and
1\leq r_{0}\leq n
, it follows that
T(\mathcal{M}_{i,j,k,l})
is orthogonal to
F(e_{1}-e_{n})_{0}+F(0)
, and is included in
F(e_{1}+e_{2}-e_{3}-e_{4})_{0}
.

Let us prove that the linear map
\Pi
from
\sum_{i<j<k<l}\mathcal{M}_{i,j,k,l}
to
\bigoplus_{1\leq i<j\leq n-2}\mathbb{C}\oplus\bigoplus_{1\leq i\leq n-3}\mathbb{C}
defined by
\Pi(A):=((A_{i,j})_{(i,j)},(A_{i,n-1})_{i})
is bijective. If it holds, we obtain
\dim\sum_{i<j<k<l}\mathcal{M}_{i,j,k,l}=(n-2)(n-3)/2+n-3=n(n-3)/2=\dim F(e_{1}+e_{2}-e_{3}-e_{4})_{0}
, and the proof is complete.

For any
(a_{i,j})\in\bigoplus_{1\leq i<j\leq n-2}\mathbb{C}
and
(a_{i,n-1})\in\bigoplus_{1\leq i\leq n-3}\mathbb{C}
, the image of

\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n-2}a_{i,j}(T_{i,j}+T_{n-1,n}-T_{i,n}-T_{j,n-1})+\sum_{1\leq i\leq n-3}a_{i,n-1}(T_{i,n-1}+T_{n-2,n}-T_{i,n}-T_{n-2,n-1})

is equal to
((a_{i,j}),(a_{i,n-1}))
.
Hence
\Pi
is surjective.

What is left is to prove the injectivity of
\Pi
. Assume
A\in\sum_{i<j<k<l}\mathcal{M}_{i,j,k,l}
and
\Pi(A)=0
.
We see
\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}A_{j,i}+\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}A_{i,j}=0
for any
1\leq i\leq n
.
Hence for any
1\leq i\leq n-3
, we have
A_{i,n}=-\sum_{j=i+1}^{n-1}A_{i,j}-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}A_{j,i}=0
.
Moreover, the sum of any two of
A_{n-2,n-1},A_{n-2,n},A_{n-1,n}
is zero.
Therefore
A=0
, and
\Pi
is injective.
∎

\mathfrak{g}
-submodules of
\bigoplus_{0\leq i\leq 2}S^{i}(\mathfrak{g})
.
For

, we write

for the irreducible highest weight

-module with highest weight

.
Let

We remark that the infinitesimal character of

is the orbit of the Weyl group through

, where

is half the sum of positive roots for

.

For

, let

be the
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule of

.

For a positive integer

We regard

For

, let us define an element of

by

(2.19)

\displaystyle\lambda(i,a)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{n}\left(\left(-a-\frac{n}{2}\right)\mathbf{1}_{i-1},(n-1)a-\frac{n(n+1-2i)}{2},\left(-a+\frac{n}{2}\right)\mathbf{1}_{n-i}\right).

Let
\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits
be the symmetrization map, that is, the
\mathfrak{g}
-isomorphism defined by

\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits\colon S(\mathfrak{g})\to U(\mathfrak{g});\ A_{1}A_{2}\cdots A_{m}\mapsto\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{\eta\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}}A_{\eta(1)}A_{\eta(2)}\cdots A_{\eta(m)},

where
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
denotes the group of permutations of
\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
.

We use the following lemma to compute annihilators of highest weight modules:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{\vee}
, and
F
be a
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule of
U(\mathfrak{g})
with respect to the adjoint action.
Then
\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits(F)
is contained in the annihilator
\mathop{\mathrm{Ann}}\nolimits L(\lambda)
if and only if the zero weight space
\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits(F_{0})
annihilates
l_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show the “if” part.
Suppose
\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits(F_{0})l_{\lambda}=0
.
We write
\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}
for the character of the Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}
where
\mathfrak{h}
acts by
\lambda
and the nilradical acts trivially.
Then
L(\lambda)
is isomorphic to the quotient of the Verma module
M(\lambda):=U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{b})}\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}
by the maximal proper submodule. Let
m_{\lambda}\in M(\lambda)
be a nonzero highest weight vector of weight
\lambda
.
Let
\mathop{\mathrm{proj}}\nolimits_{2}
be the projection to the second component of the decomposition
U(\mathfrak{g})=U(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{n}\oplus U(\mathfrak{b}^{-})
.
Since
F
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module,
\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits(F)M(\lambda)
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule of
M(\lambda)
.
By the Poincaré-Birkoff-Witt theorem, we see

\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits(F)M(\lambda)=\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits(F)U(\mathfrak{n}^{-})m_{\lambda}=U(\mathfrak{n}^{-})\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits(F)m_{\lambda}=U(\mathfrak{n}^{-})\mathop{\mathrm{proj}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits(F))m_{\lambda}
.
Hence the
\lambda
weight space of
\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits(F)M_{\lambda}
is
\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits(F_{0})m_{\lambda}
, which is zero by our assumption.
Therefore the submodule
\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits(F)M(\lambda)
is proper, and
\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits(F)L(\lambda)
is zero.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{C},\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{\vee}
.
Then
\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits(F^{a})
annihilates the highest weight module
L(\lambda)
if and only if
\lambda=\lambda(i,a)
for some
1\leq i\leq n
.

For the proof of Proposition
2.4
, we use the following

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume

. The symmetrization
\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits(F(e_{1}+e_{2}-e_{n-1}-e_{n}))
annihilates
L(\lambda)
if and only if
\lambda=\lambda(i,a)
for some

and

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3

2.2
and the definition of
\mathcal{M}_{i<j<k<l}
, we see that
\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits(F(e_{1}+e_{2}-e_{n-1}-e_{n}))\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Ann}}\nolimits L(\lambda)
is equivalent to

\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits(T(\mathcal{M}_{i,j,k,l}))l_{\lambda}=0
for

, and to

(2.20)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits(T_{i,i}T_{j,j}-T_{i,j}T_{j,i}+T_{k,k}T_{l,l}-T_{k,l}T_{l,k})l_{\lambda}

(2.21)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits(T_{i,i}T_{k,k}-T_{i,k}T_{k,i}+T_{j,j}T_{l,l}-T_{j,l}T_{l,j})l_{\lambda}

(2.22)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits(T_{i,i}T_{l,l}-T_{i,l}T_{l,i}+T_{j,j}T_{k,k}-T_{j,k}T_{k,j})l_{\lambda}

for

Since we have

\displaystyle(\lambda_{i}+1/2)(\lambda_{j}-1/2)+(\lambda_{k}+1/2)(\lambda_{l}-1/2)

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{i}+1/2)(\lambda_{k}-1/2)+(\lambda_{j}+1/2)(\lambda_{l}-1/2)

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{i}+1/2)(\lambda_{l}-1/2)+(\lambda_{j}+1/2)(\lambda_{k}-1/2)

The equations

Proof of Proposition
2.4
.

By Lemma
2.3
,
\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits(F^{a})\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Ann}}\nolimits L(\lambda)
is equivalent to
\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits(F^{a}_{0})l_{\lambda}=0
.

We see
F(e_{1}-e_{n})^{a}_{0}=\{\sum_{i,k}A_{i}T_{i,k}T_{k,i}-a(n-2)/n\sum_{i}A_{i}T_{i,i}\mid\sum_{i}A_{i}=0\}
and

(2.26)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits\Bigl(\sum_{i,k}A_{i}T_{i,k}T_{k,i}-\frac{a(n-2)}{n}\sum_{i}A_{i}T_{i,i}\Bigr)l_{\lambda}

(2.27)

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\sum_{i}A_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i<k}A_{i}[T_{i,k},T_{k,i}]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i>k}A_{i}[T_{k,i},T_{i,k}]-\frac{a(n-2)}{n}\sum_{i}A_{i}\lambda_{i}\Bigr)l_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}A_{i}C_{i}l_{\lambda},

where we put

Therefore
\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits(F(e_{1}-e_{n})^{a}_{0})l_{\lambda}=0
if and only if
the scalar

is independent of

, that is,

(2.29)

\displaystyle(\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{i})\left(\lambda_{i+1}+\lambda_{i}+\frac{n}{2}-i-\frac{a(n-2)}{n}\right)=0\quad\text{ for $1\leq i\leq n-1$.}

When

, (

) holds and any element in

can be written as

for some

, and the assertion follows.
When

When

the definition of

\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits(F^{a}_{0})l_{\lambda}=0
if and only if

) for some

and

.
Since

satisfies (

∎

Remark 2.6
.

The infinitesimal character of

\displaystyle\|\lambda(n,a)+\rho\|^{2}-\|\rho\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-1)(2a+n)(2a-n)}{4n},

Definition 2.7

For

(1)

(2)

Assume

Proof.

(

We next prove (

Let

-module

By

-submodules of

-module and
\mathfrak{g}
.
Since the Joseph ideal and the ideals

\displaystyle 0=\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits\circ\Xi(H)v

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\nolimits\left(zH+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}([H,x_{i}]y_{i}-[H,y_{i}]x_{i})\right)v

4.
Covariant differentials

In this section, we construct an intertwining differential operator between parabolically induced representations where the infinite-dimensional composition factors of the kernel are all minimal.
When
\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{R})
, the
\mathfrak{k}
-finite vectors in the kernel will be determined in Section
5
for some specific cases.
Since the method will be applied to a new construction of many minimal representations in a subsequent paper, we do not restrict ourselves to simple Lie algebras of type
A
.

Let
G
be a connected simple Lie group with finite center, and fix an Iwasawa decomposition
G=KA_{\min}N_{\min}
.
Assume that the complexification
\mathfrak{g}
is simple and
\mathfrak{g}\not\cong\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
.
Write
\Sigma(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\min})
for the restricted root system for

-weights of
\mathfrak{n}_{\min}

Let

be a standard parabolic subgroup of

and its Langlands decomposition.
Take a maximally split Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathop{\mathrm{s}}\nolimits}_{0}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}

\mathfrak{a}_{\min,0}
.
Then the center
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{m})
of

\mathfrak{k}

Via this direct sum decomposition, we will regard
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{m})^{\vee}
as a subspace of
(\mathfrak{h}^{\mathop{\mathrm{s}}\nolimits})^{\vee}
.
Moreover, any element
\mu\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{m})^{\vee}

\mathfrak{m}
via

\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}_{\mathop{\mathrm{ss}}\nolimits}\oplus\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{m})
.
We use the same symbol

for the character.

Let
(\sigma,V)
be
a finite-dimensional irreducible representation of
M
and
\nu
an element in the dual
\mathfrak{a}^{\vee}

We further assume that the
\mathfrak{m}
-module

irreducibly decomposes to a multiple of a character. By abuse of notation, we write

Set

We use the same symbol for the character of
\mathfrak{q}

By letting

We write

The representation of

C^{\infty}(G,\sigma_{\nu})^{Q}:=\{f\in C^{\infty}(G,\sigma_{\nu})\mid f(gq)=\sigma_{\nu}(q^{-1})f(g)\text{ for $g\in G,q\in Q$}\},

where the action of

is induced by the left transition

(L(h)f)(g):=f(h^{-1}g)
for

We define a bilinear map
\Psi\colon U(\mathfrak{g})\times C^{\infty}(G,\sigma_{\nu})\to C^{\infty}(G,\sigma_{\nu})
by the right differentiation:

(4.1)

\displaystyle\Psi(X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{n},f)(g):=\left.\frac{d^{n}}{dt_{1}\cdots dt_{n}}\right|_{t_{1}=\cdots=t_{n}=0}f(ge^{t_{1}X_{1}}\cdots e^{t_{n}X_{n}})

for

We write

for the contragredient representation of

Then the left ideal generated by

(4.2)

\displaystyle I(\mathfrak{q},\lambda):=U(\mathfrak{g})\mathop{\mathrm{Ann}}\nolimits_{U(\mathfrak{q})}(\lambda).

We remark that

is stable under the adjoint action of

Let
\mathop{\mathrm{proj}}\nolimits

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{proj}}\nolimits\colon U(\mathfrak{g})\to U(\mathfrak{g})/I(\mathfrak{q},\lambda).

\Psi

\displaystyle U(\mathfrak{g})/I(\mathfrak{q},\lambda)\otimes C^{\infty}(G,\sigma_{\nu})^{Q}\to C^{\infty}(G,\sigma_{\nu})

Here the

(4.5)

\displaystyle(h,q)\mathop{\mathrm{proj}}\nolimits(X)=\mathop{\mathrm{proj}}\nolimits(\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits(q)(X)),\quad(h,q)f(g)=\sigma_{\nu}(q)f(h^{-1}gq)

for

and

Define
\iota
to be the anti-involution of

\iota(X):=-X\text{ for $X\in\mathfrak{g}$.}

Definition 4.1
.

We define a
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule

of

as follows.

•

When

is not of type

•

When

Define

From (

we obtain a

(4.6)

\displaystyle D=D(Q,\sigma_{\nu})\colon C^{\infty}(G,\sigma_{\nu})^{Q}\to C^{\infty}(G,W^{\vee}\otimes\sigma_{\nu})^{Q}

defined by
\langle w,Df(g)\rangle:=\Psi(w,f)(g)

for

Here

In the rest of this section, we study properties of the kernel of

The kernel of

Linear orderings of combinatorial cubes
Boris Bukh, Anish Sevekari

Abstract
We show that, for every linear ordering of [ 2 ] n [2]^{n} , there is a large subcube on which the ordering is lexicographic. We use this to deduce that every long sequence contains a long monotone subsequence supported on an affine cube. More generally, we prove an analogous result for linear orderings of [ k ] n [k]^{n} . We show that, for every such ordering, there is a large subcube on which the ordering agrees with one of approximately ( k − 1 ) ! 2 ​ ( ln ⁡ 2 ) k \frac{(k-1)!}{2(\ln 2)^{k}} orderings.

Monotone subsequences.

A classic result of Erdős and Szekeres

Our first result is such a strengthening. Before stating it,

Theorem 1

For every

In this result one cannot replace affine cubes by arithmetic progressions.
More precisely, we observe the following.

Proposition 2

There exist arbitrarily long sequences of distinct real numbers

We construct such sequences, which we dub

(a_{1},a_{1}+M,a_{2},a_{2}+M,\dotsc,a_{m},a_{m}+M)

Indeed, suppose

If the parities of

[2]^{n}

An extension of Ramsey’s theorem.

Interestingly, after proving the main result in this paper (

Theorem 5

For every

such that for every

[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]\approx[\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}]
.

Note that if
\mathord{\mathrel{\triangleleft}}=\mathord{\mathrel{\triangleleft}}_{T}
, then
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]\preccurlyeq[\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}]
holds
if and only if
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]_{T}\preccurlyeq_{T}[\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}]_{T}
holds.
Furthermore, if
\mathord{\mathrel{\triangleleft}}=\mathord{\mathrel{\triangleleft}}_{T}
, then
\preccurlyeq
satisfies the following three properties:

1.

Transitivity:
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]\preccurlyeq[\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}]
and

[\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}]\preccurlyeq[\mathbf{e},\mathbf{f}]
together imply

[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]\preccurlyeq[\mathbf{e},\mathbf{f}]
.

2.

Comparability:
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]\preccurlyeq[\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}]
or
[\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}]\preccurlyeq[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]
.

3.

Ultrametric property: if
\mathbf{a}<\mathbf{b}<\mathbf{c}
, then
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c}]\approx\max([\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}],[\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}])
.

Call any relation
\preccurlyeq
on nontrivial subintervals of
[k]

tree-like
if it satisfies these three properties.
Given a uniform linear ordering on
[k]^{n}
, we first show that
\preccurlyeq
is tree-like, and then use
\preccurlyeq
to build
a weakly decreasing Schröder tree. That is done in the next two lemmas. Then in
Section 3
, we show that the ordering
induced by the resulting tree (almost) coincides with the original ordering on
[k]^{n}
.

Lemma 9
.

Suppose
n\geq 3
. If a linear ordering
\mathrel{\triangleleft}
on
[k]^{n}
is uniform,
then
\preccurlyeq
is tree-like.

Proof.

Transitivity:
By the assumption we have
\mathbf{cb}\mathrel{\triangleleft}\mathbf{da}
and
\mathbf{ed}\mathrel{\triangleleft}\mathbf{fc}
.
From this it follows that
\mathbf{edb}\mathrel{\triangleleft}\mathbf{fcb}\mathrel{\triangleleft}\mathbf{fda}
, which implies that
\mathbf{eb}\mathrel{\triangleleft}\mathbf{fa}
by uniformity.

Comparability:
Suppose
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]\not\preccurlyeq[\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}]
, and so
\mathbf{da}\mathrel{\triangleleft}\mathbf{cb}
.
Also,
Section 2
tells us that
\{\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}\}^{n}
is ordered lexicographically, implying that
\mathbf{cbd}\mathrel{\triangleleft}\mathbf{dbc}
.
Hence,
\mathbf{dad}\mathrel{\triangleleft}\mathbf{cbd}\mathrel{\triangleleft}\mathbf{dbc}
, which is to say
\mathbf{ad}\mathrel{\triangleleft}\mathbf{bc}
.

Ultrametric property:
We first show that
\max([\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}],[\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}])\preccurlyeq[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c}]
.
We have
\mathbf{ab}\mathrel{\triangleleft}\mathbf{ac}\mathrel{\triangleleft}\mathbf{ca}
, and so
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]\preccurlyeq[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c}]
. Similarly,

\mathbf{ac}\mathrel{\triangleleft}\mathbf{bc}\mathrel{\triangleleft}\mathbf{cb}
, and so
[\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}]\preccurlyeq[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c}]
.

We next show that
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c}]\preccurlyeq\max([\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}],[\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}])
.
Suppose
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c}]\not\preccurlyeq[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]
. Then
\mathbf{ba}\mathrel{\triangleleft}\mathbf{ac}
, and so

\mathbf{bca}\mathrel{\triangleleft}\mathbf{acc}\mathrel{\triangleleft}\mathbf{caa}
, and hence
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c}]\preccurlyeq[\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}]
.
∎

For a relation
\preccurlyeq
on the nontrivial subintervals of
[k]
and a weakly decreasing Schröder tree

T
, we abuse notation and write
\mathord{\preccurlyeq}=\mathord{\preccurlyeq}_{T}
provided

[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]\preccurlyeq[\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}]
holds if and only if
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]_{T}\preccurlyeq_{T}[\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}]_{T}
holds.

Lemma 10
.

For every tree-like ordering
\preccurlyeq
on
\binom{[k]}{2}
there exists a
weakly decreasing Schröder tree
T
such that
\mathord{\preccurlyeq}=\mathord{\preccurlyeq}_{T}
.

In a weakly decreasing Schröder tree
T
we may have

[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]_{T}\approx_{T}[\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}]_{T}
for two reasons: either
because
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]_{T}=[\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}]_{T}
, or neither of
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]_{T}

and
[\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}]_{T}
is a descendant of one another, and they happen to be equal
in the
\preccurlyeq_{T}
preorder. Therefore, to build a tree out of a tree-like ordering,
we need to distinguish these two situations. We achieve this by identifying the node
with the widest interval that generates it.

Proof of
Section 3
.

For a nontrivial subinterval
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]

of
[k]
, let
\mathbf{a^{\prime}}
be the least element of
[k]
such that
[\mathbf{a^{\prime}},\mathbf{b}]\approx[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]
.
Likewise, let
\mathbf{b^{\prime}}
be the largest element of
[k]
such that
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b^{\prime}}]\approx[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]
.
Define
\overline{[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}[\mathbf{a^{\prime}},\mathbf{b^{\prime}}]
.

Let
\mathcal{N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}\{\overline{[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]}:\mathbf{a}\mathrel{\triangleleft}\mathbf{b}\}
. We shall take
\mathcal{N}

be the set of nodes of our tree. To show that we indeed obtain a tree, we must prove that
every two intervals from
\mathcal{N}
are either disjoint or one of them contains the other.
To do this we first show two basic properties of the map
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]\mapsto\overline{[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]}
.

Claim 1:

\overline{[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]}\approx[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]
for every
\{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\}\in\binom{[k]}{2}
.
Indeed, suppose
\overline{[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]}=[\mathbf{a^{\prime}},\mathbf{b^{\prime}}]
.
If either
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{a^{\prime}}
or
\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{b^{\prime}}
, then the claim follows. Say
\mathbf{a^{\prime}}<\mathbf{a}<\mathbf{b}<\mathbf{b^{\prime}}
.
From the ultrametric property for the triple
\mathbf{a^{\prime}}<\mathbf{a}<\mathbf{b^{\prime}}
we deduce that either

[\mathbf{a^{\prime}},\mathbf{b^{\prime}}]\approx[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b^{\prime}}]
or
[\mathbf{a^{\prime}},\mathbf{b^{\prime}}]\approx[\mathbf{a^{\prime}},\mathbf{a}]
. In the former case

[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]\approx[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b^{\prime}}]\approx[\mathbf{a^{\prime}},\mathbf{b^{\prime}}]=\overline{[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]}
, proving the claim.
In the latter case, two applications of the ultrametric property yield

[\mathbf{a}^{\prime},\mathbf{b^{\prime}}]\approx[\mathbf{a^{\prime}},\mathbf{a}]\preccurlyeq[\mathbf{a^{\prime}},\mathbf{b}]\preccurlyeq[\mathbf{a^{\prime}},\mathbf{b^{\prime}}]
,
and so
[\mathbf{a^{\prime}},\mathbf{b^{\prime}}]\approx[\mathbf{a^{\prime}},\mathbf{b}]\approx[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]
as well.

Claim 2:

\overline{\overline{[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]}}=\overline{[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]}
for every
\{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\}\in\binom{[k]}{2}
.

Indeed, suppose
\overline{[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]}=[\mathbf{a^{\prime}},\mathbf{b^{\prime}}]
and
\overline{\overline{[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]}}=[\mathbf{a^{\prime\prime}},\mathbf{b^{\prime\prime}}]
.
Then by two applications of Claim 1 it follows that
[\mathbf{a^{\prime\prime}},\mathbf{b}]\preccurlyeq[\mathbf{a^{\prime\prime}},\mathbf{b^{\prime\prime}}]\approx[\mathbf{a^{\prime}},\mathbf{b^{\prime}}]\approx[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]
,
which, by the minimality of
\mathbf{a^{\prime}}
, implies that
\mathbf{a^{\prime\prime}}=\mathbf{a^{\prime}}
. Similarly,
\mathbf{b^{\prime\prime}}=\mathbf{b^{\prime}}
.

We are now ready to prove that every pair of intervals in
\mathcal{N}
is either disjoint or comparable.
Let
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]
and
[\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}]
be any two intervals from
\mathcal{N}
,
and suppose that they are not disjoint. Say
\mathbf{c}\leq\mathbf{b}
(the case
\mathbf{a}\leq\mathbf{d}
is analogous, and can be
reduced to this case by swapping the roles of the two intervals). If we also have
\mathbf{c}\leq\mathbf{a}
, then the interval

[\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}]
contains
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]
. So, assume that
\mathbf{a}<\mathbf{c}
. By Claim 2 we may
assume that
\mathbf{a}
is the minimal
\mathbf{a^{\prime}}
such that
[\mathbf{a^{\prime}},\mathbf{b}]\approx[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]
,
and similarly for
\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}
. From the minimality of
\mathbf{c}
we infer that
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{d}]\not\approx[\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}]
.
By the ultrametric property applied to the triple
\mathbf{a}<\mathbf{c}<\mathbf{d}
, it follows that
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{d}]\approx[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c}]
.
By another application of the ultrametric property, this time to
\mathbf{a}<\mathbf{c}<\mathbf{b}
, we infer that

[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c}]\preccurlyeq[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]
, and so

[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{d}]\preccurlyeq[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]
. The ultrametric property of
\mathbf{a}<\mathbf{b}<\mathbf{d}
then implies
that
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{d}]\approx[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]
, and so
\mathbf{b}\geq\mathbf{d}
by the maximality of
\mathbf{b}
.
Hence,
[\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}]
is contained in
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]
.

It follows that intervals in
\mathcal{N}
naturally form a tree under the containment relation.
The tree is plane, with intervals ordered in the natural way. We add leaves to the tree by
declaring that leaf
\mathbf{a}
is a descendant of all intervals that contain
\mathbf{a}
.
For tree nodes
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}],[\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}]\in\mathcal{N}
, we order them
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]\preccurlyeq_{T}[\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}]

if and only if
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]\preccurlyeq[\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}]
.
The ultrametric property then ensures that every path from the root is decreasing.
Denote the resulting weakly decreasing Schröder tree by
T
.

Recall that
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]_{T}
is the bottommost node of
T
containing
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{b}
.
Since
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\overline{[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]}
, it follows that

[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]_{T}\subseteq\overline{[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]}
. On the other hand,

[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]\subseteq[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]_{T}
, which implies that

\overline{[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]}\subseteq\overline{[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]_{T}}=[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]_{T}
.
So,
[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]_{T}=\overline{[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}]}
for every
\{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\}\in\binom{[k]}{2}

from which
\mathord{\preccurlyeq}=\mathord{\preccurlyeq}_{T}
follows.
∎

Extension of Ramsey’s theorem

In this section, we prove

Let

Chow groups, pull back and class groups
Kalyan Banerjee and Azizul Hoque

Abstract
. Let S S be a certain affine algebraic surface over ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} such that it admits a regular map to 𝔸 2 / ℚ {\mathbb{A}}^{2}/{\mathbb{Q}} . We show that any non-trivial torsion element in the Chow group 𝐶𝐻 1 ​ ( S ) {\it{CH}}^{1}(S) can be pulled back to ideal classes of quadratic fields whose order can be made as large as possible. This gives an affirmative answer to a question analogous to one raised by Agboola and Pappas, in the case of certain affine algebraic surfaces. We also show by constructing an element of odd order n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 in the class group of certain imaginary quadratic fields that the Picard group of S P S_{P} has a subgroup isomorphic to ℤ / n ​ ℤ {\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}} .

This further implies

This leads to the following possibilities (since

We now recall the following result

Theorem IV
.

Let

Proposition 5.3
.

Let

be as in Theorem
5.1
and

. Then
\alpha=k+m\sqrt{-d}
is not an

power of an element in the ring of integers,
\mathcal{O}_{K_{k,p,n}}
of the field

for any prime divisor

.

Proof.

Let
q
be a prime number such that

. Then

is odd since

We first consider the case when

. Suppose
\alpha=\left(a+b\sqrt{-d}\right)^{\ell}
for some integers

and

. That is,

k+m\sqrt{-d}=(a+b\sqrt{-d})^{q}.

We now compare the real parts to get,

k=a^{q}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\binom{q}{2\ell}a^{q-2\ell}b^{2\ell}(-d)^{\ell}.

(5.6)

This shows that

If

k=\pm 1\pm\sum_{\ell=1}^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\binom{q}{2\ell}b^{2\ell}(-d)^{\ell}.

Reading this modulo

k\equiv\pm\pmod{d}
. This contradicts to (i).

When

) becomes

k=\pm t^{q}\pm\sum_{\ell=1}^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\binom{q}{2\ell}t^{q-2\ell}b^{2\ell}(-d)^{\ell}.

As in previous case, reading this modulo

We now consider the remaining case when

. We take the norm on both sides to get,

p^{n}=(k^{2}+b^{2}d)^{q}.

This implies

db^{2}+k^{2}=p^{n/q}.

(5.7)

Also we have,

(5.8)

Since

(|b|,n/q)

We now consider the case when

If

If both

Taking norm on both sides, one gets

Since

Since

Proof of Theorem

.

Let
m

\alpha=k+m\sqrt{-d}
.
We note that
\gcd(\alpha,~\bar{\alpha})=1

. Therefore, we can write
(\alpha)=\mathfrak{a}^{n}
for some ideal

\mathcal{O}_{K_{k,p,n}}

is the ideal class containing

in the class group of

. If the order of

is less than

, then we obtain an odd prime divisor

of

and an element

. Since

is square-free, so that the only units in

are

which contradicts to Proposition

For an odd positive integer
n
not divisible by
3
and a fixed psoitive integer
k
, the conditions (i) and (ii) in Theorem
5.1
hold very often. This can be proved using Siegel’s theorem on integral points on affine curves. More precisely, we prove the following result to show the infinitude of the imaginary quadratic fields of the form
K_{k,p,n}
whose class group has an element of order
n
. We apply a celebrated theorem of Siegel
[
27
]
to prove the following result.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
n\geq 5
be an odd integer not divisible by
3
. For each positive integer
k
, the class group of
K_{k,p,n}
has an element of order
n
for infinitely many odd primes
p
.

Remark 5.3
.

This is a generalization of
[
5
, Theorem 1.2]
which can be obtained by considering
k
as an odd prime in Theorem
5.4
. One can prove this theorem by a similar argument as in
[
5
, Theorem 1.2]
. However for the sake of completeness, we give a proof of it.

Proof.

Let
n\geq 2
be an odd integer not divisible by
3
, and
k
an arbitrary positive integer. Let
p
be an odd prime such that
\gcd(p,k)=1
. Then by Theorem
5.1
, the class group of
K_{k,p,n}
has an element of order
n
except for
t^{q}\equiv\pm k\pmod{d}
, where
t(\neq k)
is a divisor of
k
and
q
is a prime divisor of
n
. For
t^{q}\equiv\pm k\pmod{d}
, one gets
d\leq k+t^{q}
.
For any positive integer
D
, the curve

Dx^{2}+p^{2}=y^{n}

(5.11)

is an irreducible algebraic curve of genus bigger than
0
(see
[
26
]
). Thus by Siegel’s theorem (see
[
9
,
27
]
), it follows that there are only finitely many integral points
(x,y)
on the curve (
5.11
). Therefore, for each positive integer
d
, there are at most finitely many primes
p
such that

dm^{2}+k^{2}=p^{n}.

As
K_{k,p,n}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})
, it follows that there are infinitely many fields of the form
K_{k,p,n}
for each positive integer
k
. Furthermore, if
p
is sufficiently large, then for
k^{2}-p^{n}=-m^{2}d
, one gets
d>k+t^{q}
. Therefore by Theorem
5.1
, the class group of
K_{k,p,n}
has an element of order
n
for sufficiently large
p
.
∎

In Theorem

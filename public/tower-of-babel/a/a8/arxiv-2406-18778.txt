Bridging between überhomology and double homology
Luigi Caputi, Daniele Celoria, Carlo Collari

Abstract
. We establish an isomorphism between the 0 0 -degree überhomology and the double homology of finite simplicial complexes, using a Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequence argument. We clarify the correspondence between these theories by providing examples and some consequences; in particular, we show that überhomology groups detect the standard simplex, and that the double homology’s diagonal is related to the connected domination polynomial.



Introduction

Often times in mathematics, coincidences can reveal hidden patterns or connections, leading to new insights. The aim of this paper is precisely to explore such a coincidence, involving two independently defined homology theories.

The first of the two objects we wish to compare is related to moment-angle complexes; these are central objects in the study of toric topology – sitting at the intersection of topology, symplectic and algebraic geometry, and combinatorics, see for instance
[
Pan13
,
BP15
]
.

The
double homology
of the moment-angle complex
\mathcal{Z}_{K}
, associated to a simplicial complex
K
, was defined in
[
LPSS23
]
by introducing a differential on the ordinary (simplicial) homology of
\mathcal{Z}_{K}
.
The study of this homology is motivated in part by stability properties of the homology
\mathrm{H}_{*}(\mathcal{Z}_{K})
with respect to deformations of
K
. This recently sparked some interest in computing double homology groups for classes of simplicial complexes
[
Rui24
]
, as well as finding information about its structure and, in particular, about its rank
[
Rui24
,
Han23b
,
Zha24
]
.

The second algebraic object we are going to look at is called
überhomology
; this is a combinatorially defined, triply graded homology of simplicial complexes, introduced in
[
Cel23
]
.
It was later proved in
[
CCC23a
]
, that überhomology is a particular instance of
poset homology
, as given in
[
Cha19
,
CCDT24
]
.

Restricting the triple grading yields a doubly graded homology, called
0
-degree überhomology, denoted by
\ddot{\mathrm{B}}
.
Several properties of this latter homology are indeed paralleled in double homology; for instance, the überhomology of a triangulated manifold contains the fundamental class (
cf.

[
Cel23
, Thm. 7.10]
and
[
LPSS23
, Prop. 7.2]
), it is well-behaved under cones and suspensions (
cf
.
[
CCC23b
, Prop.s 5.7 and 5.8]
and
[
LPSS23
, Thm. 6.3]
), and a graph’s überhomology vanishes if it contains a leaf (
cf
.
[
CCC23a
, Prop. 5.2]
and
[
LPSS23
, Thm. 6.7]
).

Finally, überhomology is explicitly computable –albeit only for small simplicial complexes– using freely available Python code
[
Fra22
]
.

In this paper, we establish an isomorphism between the
0
-degree überhomology
\ddot{\mathrm{B}}_{*}^{*}
and the double homology
{\rm DH}_{*,*}
(denoted by
\mathrm{HH}
in
[
LPSS23
]
) of finite simplicial complexes.

Theorem 1
.

Let
K
be a finite and connected simplicial complex with
m
vertices. Then, there is an isomorphism

\ddot{\mathrm{B}}_{i}^{j}(K)\cong{\rm DH}_{i-j+1,2j}(\mathcal{Z}_{K})

for each
i\neq 0,-1
. Equivalently,

{\rm DH}_{-k,2l}(\mathcal{Z}_{K})\cong\ddot{\mathrm{B}}_{l-k-1}^{l}(K)\ ,

for each choice of
l
and
k
such that
l-k\neq 0,1
.

To prove Theorem
1
(see Theorem
3.6
), we observe that, up to a regrading, there is an identification between the double homology of
\mathcal{Z}_{K}
and the second page of a Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequence (Theorem
3.4
). Überhomology was also shown to be the second page of a Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequence
[
CCC23b
]
. Theorem
1
follows, since the spectral sequences at hand are associated to the same cover – the anti-star cover.

In
[
Han23a
, Rmk. 3.11]
it was noted that double homology is the second page of a spectral sequence.
Further, in
[
VRS25
, Section 4]
, seing double homology as a page of a spectral sequence was leveraged to prove a vanishing criterion.
A connection between double homology and the Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequence was used in
[
AB24
]
(with particular reference to Remark 3.11 and Section 6) to compute higher operations on the cohomology of
\mathcal{Z}_{K}
.

Finally, we observe that Theorem
1
can be effectively used in both directions. For instance, on one hand, it implies that überhomology detects the standard simplex (Corollary
3.13
). On the other hand, we use Theorem
1
to prove that the diagonal part of double homology is related to the connected domination polynomial of graphs (Corollary
3.11
).

With an eye on überhomology, we conclude with the following question:

Question
.

The isomorphism provided by Theorem
1
only involves überhomology’s lowest weighting degree. Is there a way to interpret the full überhomology groups in terms of properties of moment-angle complexes?

1.
0
-degree überhomology

We start by recalling the definition of überhomology, following
[
Cel23
,
CCC23a
]
.

Let
K
be a finite and connected simplicial complex on
m
vertices, say
V(K)=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
. The ordering of the vertices will not affect the following discussion.

A
bicolouring

\varepsilon
on
K
is a map
\varepsilon\colon V(K)\to\{0,1\}
. As a visual aid, we will sometimes identify
0
with white and
1
with black.
A
bicoloured simplicial complex
is a pair
(K,\varepsilon)
, consisting of a simplicial complex
K
equipped with the bicolouring
\varepsilon
.
Given a
n
-dimensional simplex
\sigma
in
(K,\varepsilon)
, define its
\varepsilon
-
weight
as

(1)

w_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)\coloneqq n+1-\sum_{v_{i}\in V(\sigma)}\varepsilon(v_{i})\ .

Equivalently,
w_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)
is the number of
0
-coloured vertices in
\sigma
.
For a fixed bicolouring
\varepsilon
, the weight in Equation (
1
) induces a filtration of the simplicial chain complex
C_{*}(K;\mathbb{Z})
associated to
K
.
More explicitly, set

\mathscr{F}_{j}(K,\varepsilon)\coloneqq\mathbb{Z}\langle\ \sigma\mid w_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)\leq j\ \rangle\subseteq C_{*}(K;\mathbb{Z})\ .

The simplicial differential
\partial
respects this filtration, and it can be decomposed as the sum of two differentials (
cf
.
[
Cel23
, Lemma 2.1]
); one which preserves the
w_{\varepsilon}
, denoted by
\partial_{h}
, and one which decreases it by one. Call
(C(K,\varepsilon),\partial_{h})
the bigraded chain complex, whose underlying module is
C(K;\mathbb{Z})
; the first degree is given by simplices’ dimensions, while the weight
w_{\varepsilon}
gives the second.
The
\varepsilon
-
horizontal homology

\mathrm{H}^{h}(K,\varepsilon)
of
(K,\varepsilon)
is the homology of the bigraded chain complex
(C(K,\varepsilon),\partial_{h})
. In other words,
\mathrm{H}^{h}(K,\varepsilon)
is the homology of the associated graded object for the filtration
\mathscr{F}_{j}(K,\varepsilon)
.

Bicolourings on
K
can be canonically identified with elements of the Boolean poset
B(m)
on a set with
m
elements (partially ordered by inclusion).
Let
\varepsilon
and
\varepsilon^{\prime}
be two bicolourings on
K
, differing only on the vertex
v_{i}
; assume further that
\varepsilon(v_{i})=0
and
\varepsilon^{\prime}(v_{i})=1
. Denote by
d_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}
the weight-preserving part of the identity map
\mathrm{Id}\colon\mathrm{H}^{h}(K,\varepsilon)\to\mathrm{H}^{h}(K,\varepsilon^{\prime})
. By slightly abusing the notation,
d_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}
can be seen to coincide with the linear extension of the map

d_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\sigma)=\begin{cases}(-1)^{s(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime})}\sigma&\text{if }w_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)=w_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\sigma)\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\ ,

where
s(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime})=\left|\{j\in V\;:\;j<i,\;\varepsilon(v_{j})=1\}\right|
– see
[
Cel23
, Section 6]
and
[
CCC23a
, Section 2]
.
Note that
d_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\sigma)=0
occurs if and only if
w_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)=w_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\sigma)-1
.

For a simplicial complex

For a given bicolouring
\varepsilon

K
, set
\ell(\varepsilon)\coloneqq\sum_{j}\varepsilon(v_{j})
. The
j
-th über chain module
is then defined as

(2)

\ddot{\mathrm{C}}^{j}(K;\mathbb{Z})=\bigoplus_{\ell(\varepsilon)=j}\mathrm{H}^{h}(K,\varepsilon;\mathbb{Z})\ .

By
[
Cel23
, Proposition 6.2]
, the map

(3)

\ddot{d}^{j}\coloneqq\sum_{\ell(\varepsilon)=j}d_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}\colon\ddot{\mathrm{C}}^{j}(K;\mathbb{Z})\to\ddot{\mathrm{C}}^{j+1}(K;\mathbb{Z})

is a differential of degree
1
, turning
\left(\ddot{\mathrm{C}}^{*}(K;\mathbb{Z}),\ddot{d}\right)
into a cochain complex.

Definition 1.1
.

The
überhomology

\ddot{\mathrm{H}}^{*}(K)
of a finite and connected simplicial complex
K
is the homology of the complex
\left(\ddot{\mathrm{C}}^{*}(K;\mathbb{Z}),\ddot{d}\right)
.

Überhomology groups can be endowed with two extra gradings, yielding a triply graded module. Indeed, the differential
\ddot{d}
preserves both the simplices’ weight and dimension.
The notation for the three gradings on the überhomology is as follows:
\ddot{\mathrm{H}}^{j}_{k,i}(K)
denotes the component of the homology generated by simplices of dimension
i
, with
k
vertices of colour
0
, and whose (über)homological degree is
j
.

Remark 1.2
.

It was observed in
[
CCC23a
]
that the definition of überhomology can be rephrased in terms of poset homology
[
Cha19
,
CCDT24
]
.
With this interpretation, the choice of the signs in the definition of
d_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}
is a sign assignment in the sense of
[
CCC23a
, Definition 2.9]
, and different sign assignments yield isomorphic homology groups – see
[
CCC23a
, Remark 2.10]
and
[
CCDT24
, Example 3.15]
. Moreover, Definition
1.1
was extended to general coefficients in
[
CCC23a
]
.

As mentioned above, the überdifferential
\ddot{d}
preserves the
(k,i)
-bidegree. In particular, specialising to the component of überhomology of weight
0
yields a bigraded homology.

Definition 1.3
.

For a simplicial complex
K
, define the
0
-degree überhomology to be the bigraded homology

\ddot{\mathrm{B}}^{j}_{i}(K)\coloneqq\ddot{\mathrm{H}}^{j}_{0,i}(K)\ .

In view of Remark
1.2
, we can provide an alternative description of
\ddot{\mathrm{B}}(K)
as poset homology:

Lemma 1.4
.

The bigraded homology
\ddot{\mathrm{B}}^{*}_{i}(K)
is the poset homology of the Boolean poset
B(m)
, with coefficients in the
i
-th simplicial homology functor.

Proof.

Given a subset
I\subseteq V(K)
, denote by
K[I]\subseteq K
the sub-complex induced by
I
.
We include the case
I=\emptyset
, and set
K[\emptyset]=\emptyset
to be the empty simplicial complex.
For
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
define
I_{\varepsilon}
as the
set of
1
-coloured vertices with respect to
\varepsilon
. Observe that the weight 0 part of the horizontal homology
\mathrm{H}^{h}(K,\varepsilon;\mathbb{Z})
is the simplicial homology of
K[I_{\varepsilon}]
. By Equation (
2
), the homology
\ddot{\mathrm{B}}^{*}_{i}(K)
is then obtained by decorating each vertex
\varepsilon
in the Boolean poset
B(m)
with the
i
-
th
homology
\mathrm{H}_{i}(K[I_{\varepsilon}])
of
K[I_{\varepsilon}]
;
the differentials associated to the cube’s edges are induced by inclusion, as in Equation (
3
). Therefore, if
m=|V(K)|
, we obtain a functor

\mathcal{H}_{i}\colon B(m)\to\mathbf{Ab}

which associates to each
\varepsilon
the homology
\mathrm{H}_{i}(K[I_{\varepsilon}])
.
By definition of poset homology – see also
[
CCC23a
, Proposition 2.14]
– we get that the überhomological construction computes the poset homology of the Boolean poset
B(m)
, with coefficients in
\mathcal{H}_{i}
. This proves the statement.
∎

2.
Double homology

In this section we recall the definition of double homology, following
[
LPSS23
]
.

Let
K
be a simplicial complex.
For each
I\subseteq V=V(K)
, consider the reduced simplicial homology
\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{*}(K[I];\mathbb{Z})
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
(when clear from the context, coefficients will be omitted). We adopt the convention that the group
\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{p}(K[\emptyset])=\mathbb{Z}
is non-trivial only for
p=-1
.
For each
j\in V(K)\setminus I
, the inclusion map
K[I]\to K[I\cup\{j\}]
induces a morphism
\phi_{I,j}
between the associated homology groups.
We denote by
\phi_{p,I,j}
the morphism induced on the homology groups in degree
p
. For each
p\geq-1
there exists a differential (
cf
.
[
LPSS23
, Lemma 3.1]
)

(4)

\partial^{\prime}_{p}\coloneqq(-1)^{p+1}\sum_{j\in V\setminus I}(-1)^{|\{i\in I\;:\;i<j\}|}\phi_{p,I,j}\ ,

defined on the sum
\bigoplus_{I\subseteq V}\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{p}(K[I];\mathbb{Z})
.

Remark 2.1
.

This construction associates to a subset of the vertices of
K
the homology of the induced simplicial complex. This can be equivalently described as a functor on the Boolean poset
B(m)
on
m\coloneqq|V|
vertices, where the poset
B(m)
is regarded as being a category. Each object
i\in B(m)
corresponds to a set
I_{i}\subseteq V
. Then, it is straightforward that
F\colon B(m)\to\mathbf{Ab}
, defined by setting
F(i)\coloneqq\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{p}(K[I_{i}];\mathbb{Z})
, yields a functor, and thus we can consider the homology of
B(m)
with coefficients in
F
. The differential
\partial^{\prime}
from Equation (
4
) is well-defined after the choice a sign assignment (in fact, any) on the cube
B(m)
(cf.
[
CCDT24
]
).

To a finite simplicial complex
K
, one can also associate the well-known
moment-angle complex

\mathcal{Z}_{K}

[
BP15
, Chapter 4]
. The (co)homology of
\mathcal{Z}_{K}
has been extensively investigated (see, for instance,
[
LPSS23
, Theorem 2.1]
and references therein).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
K
be a simplicial complex with vertices
v_{1},\dots,v_{m}
and let
I_{K}
be the ideal of
\mathbb{Z}[v_{1},\dots,v_{m}]
generated by monomials
\prod_{i\in I}v_{i}
for which
I\subseteq\{1,\dots,m\}
is not a simplex of
K
. Then, there are isomorphisms of bigraded algebras

\mathrm{H}^{*}(\mathcal{Z}_{K})\cong\Tor_{\mathbb{Z}[v_{1},\dots,v_{m}]}(\mathbb{Z}[K],\mathbb{Z})\cong\bigoplus_{I\subseteq\{1,\dots,m\}}\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{*}(K[I];\mathbb{Z})\ ,

where
\mathbb{Z}[K]\coloneqq\mathbb{Z}[v_{1},\dots,v_{m}]/I_{K}
.

We are interested in a certain homology theory associated to
\mathcal{Z}_{K}
, called
double homology
. Before recalling its definition, observe that there is a bigraded decomposition of the homology of
\mathcal{Z}_{K}
, given as follows:

(5)

\mathrm{H}_{p}(\mathcal{Z}_{K})\cong\bigoplus_{2l-k=p}\left(\bigoplus_{|I|=l}\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{l-k-1}(K[I])\right)\ .

The summand between parenthesis in Equation (
5
) is denoted by
\mathrm{H}_{-k,2l}(\mathcal{Z}_{K})
, see
[
LPSS23
]
.
With respect to this bigrading, the differential
\partial^{\prime}
of Equation (
4
) splits as:

\partial^{\prime}_{-k,2l}\colon\mathrm{H}_{-k,2l}(\mathcal{Z}_{K})\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}_{-k-1,2l+2}(\mathcal{Z}_{K})\ .

Definition 2.3
.

The
double homology
of the moment-angle complex is defined as the homology

{\rm DH}_{*,*}(\mathcal{Z}_{K})\coloneqq\mathrm{H}(\mathrm{H}_{*,*}(\mathcal{Z}_{K}),\partial^{\prime})

of the complex
(\mathrm{H}_{*,*}(\mathcal{Z}_{K}),\partial^{\prime})
.

In
[
LPSS23
]
, double homology groups are denoted as
\mathrm{H}\mathrm{H}_{*}
; here we use
{\rm DH}_{*}
instead to avoid ambiguity with Hochschild homology.

Example 2.4

(cf.
[
LPSS23
, Proposition 6.2]
)
.

Let
K
be the boundary of the
(m-1)
-simplex
\Delta^{m-1}
. Then,

{\rm DH}_{-k,2l}(\mathcal{Z}_{K};\mathbb{Z})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}&\text{for }(-k,2l)=(0,0),(-1,2m)\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

As a concrete example, for
K=\partial\Delta^{1}

3.
Comparison and applications

In this section we will show that the 0-degree of überhomology, and double homology are closely related. To prove this, we are going to use the augmented Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequence with respect to a specific cover, the
anti-star
. We begin by introducing the necessary notation and then provide the comparison result in Theorem
3.6
.

In the following and in the next section, unless otherwise specified, we let
K
be a simplicial complex which is not the standard simplex. We shall also assume that
K
has no ghost vertices, that is
K[I]=\emptyset
if and only if
I=\emptyset
.
For each vertex
v\in V(K)
, denote by
\mathrm{ast}_{K}(v)
the subcomplex of
K
spanned by the vertices in
V(K)\setminus\{v\}
; that is the complement of the (open) star of
v
. Denote by
\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{ast}}(K)=\{\mathrm{ast}_{K}(v)\}_{v\in V(K)}
the anti-star cover of
K
, and by
\Nerve(\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{ast}})
its nerve.

To a
p
-simplex
\sigma\in\Nerve(\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{ast}})
spanned by the vertices
v_{j_{0}},\dots,v_{j_{p}}
, we associate the subcomplex
U_{\sigma}\coloneqq\mathrm{ast}_{K}(v_{j_{0}})\cap\dots\cap\mathrm{ast}_{K}(v_{j_{p}})\subseteq K
.

Remark 3.1
.

Note that, for
v\in V(K)
a vertex, we have
\mathrm{ast}_{K}(v)=K[V(K)\setminus\{v\}]
. Analogously, if
\sigma
is spanned by the vertices
v_{j_{0}},\dots,v_{j_{p}}
, we have
U_{\sigma}=K[V(K)\setminus\{v_{j_{0}},\dots,v_{j_{p}}\}]
.

For each
q\in\mathbb{N}
and
\sigma\in\Nerve(\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{ast}})
, define

\mathcal{H}_{q}(\sigma)\coloneqq\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{q}(U_{\sigma})

as the reduced
q
-
th
homology group of the subcomplex
U_{\sigma}
of
K
. If
\tau
is a face of
\sigma
, then the inclusion
U_{\sigma}\subseteq U_{\tau}
induces a map between the associated
q
-homology groups.
Denote by
\delta_{h}
the differential induced by the simplicial differential of
\Nerve(\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{ast}})
. Then, if
\Nerve_{p}(\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{ast}})
denotes the set of
p

\Nerve(\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{ast}})

(6)

C_{p,q}^{0}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Nerve_{p}(\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{ast}})}C_{q}(U_{\sigma})

of simplicial chains

endowed with the horizontal differential
\delta_{h}
and the simplicial differential
\delta_{v}\colon C^{0}_{p,q}\to C^{0}_{p,q-1}
as vertical differential. Taking the filtration of this double chain complex (with respect to the vertical differential first) yields the so-called Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequence of
K
with respect to the cover
\Nerve(\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{ast}})

Recall now that the first page of the Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequence associated to a simplicial complex
K
and cover
\mathcal{U}

is

(7)

E^{1}_{p,q}=\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\mathrm{N}_{p}(\mathcal{U})}\mathrm{H}_{q}(U_{\sigma}),

with differential
\delta^{(1)}\colon E^{1}_{p,q}\to E^{1}_{p-1,q}
induced by

. This spectral sequence is called

if the empty intersection
U_{\emptyset}=K
is allowed – that is, in the augmented Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequence we have
{E}^{1}_{-1,q}={\mathrm{H}}_{q}(K)
. We also consider a reduced and augmented version of it; that is, we consider the spectral sequence whose first page is

(8)

\overline{E}^{1}_{p,q}=\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\mathrm{N}_{p}(\mathcal{U})}\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{q}(U_{\sigma})\ ,\quad p\geq 0,q\geq-1\ ,

with the convention that if
Y=\emptyset
then
\widetilde{H}_{-1}(Y)=\mathbb{Z}
and it is trivial otherwise.
That is, we use the reduced homology groups
en lieu
of the unreduced homology when taking the vertical differential of the corresponding (augmented) double chain complex.
Furthermore, we augment it by setting
\overline{E}^{1}_{*,-1}=\overline{E}^{1}_{m-1,-1}=\mathbb{Z}
and
\overline{E}^{1}_{-1,q}=\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{q}(K)
. If
\mathcal{U}
is the anti-star cover
\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{ast}}
, the former corresponds to the intersection of all the anti-stars, while the latter corresponds to the empty intersections of the anti-stars. We refer to
[

]
for more details on the construction of the augmented unreduced Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequence.

Remark 3.2
.

The ‘usual’ (non-augmented) Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequence, associated to a finite open covering of

[

, Section 3.1]

0
by
[

, Remark 4.2]

Similarly, we have the following:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
K
be a simplicial complex which is not a standard simplex. Then, the reduced augmented Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequence, with respect to the anti-star cover, converges to
0
.

Proof.

Consider the double chain complex

of Equation (
6
), augmented in degree

with the simplicial chains
C_{-1,q}^{0}=C_{q}(K)
of

. Recall that in the reduced version of the augmented Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequence, we also have an additional row for

corresponding to the augmentations of the chain complexes

, the rows corresponding to

∎

We note here that the augmented reduced Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequence can also be effectively used to provide alternative proof of other statements in
[
LPSS23
]
, and to provide computations in double homology. As an example,
[
LPSS23
, Theorem 6.8]
can be proved as follows:

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
K\neq\Delta^{m-1}
be the flag complex of a chordal simple graph on
m
vertices. Then

{\rm DH}_{-k,2l}(\mathcal{Z}_{K})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}&\text{for }(-k,2l)=(0,0),(-1,4)\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Proof.

Flag complexes of chordal graphs are contractible and
1
-Leray
[
DE09
, Lemma 3.1]
. This means that the homology of all its induced
subcomplexes is trivial for all
i\geq 1
. As a consequence, in the augmented unreduced Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequence, only the
0
-
th
row is non-trivial; further, the spectral sequence collapses at the third page. Also, the augmented reduced Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequence is non-trivial only in the
0
-
th
rows and in position
(m-1,-1)
, corresponding to the empty set. Moreover, in such case the
(-1)
-
th
column is completely trivial, by the contractibility of
K
.

By Proposition
3.8
, the
0
-
th
row of the (second page of the) augmented reduced Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequence has Euler characteristic equal to
(-1)^{m+1}
. Therefore, there must be at least one non-zero class. On the other hand, there can be at most one such class. In fact, the augmented reduced spectral sequence converges to
0
by Lemma
3.3
, and the second differential is the only differential that can kill such classes. Hence, the only non-trivial element is paired with the class corresponding to the empty set by the second differential; this implies that there is a rank
1
element in bidegree
(m-3,0)
. The computation follows.
∎

Similarly, one could prove an analogous statement for
1
-Leray complexes. In such case,
[
CCC23b
, Theorem 1.2]
implies that the spectral sequence has non-zero term in row
0
and column
-1
. Such classes are paired up by the anti-diagonal differentials, with a further class appearing at position
(m-3,0)
.

Many vertex-disjoint even cycles of fixed length in a graph
Jianfeng Hou, Caiyun Hu, Heng Li, Xizhi Liu, Caihong Yang, Yixiao Zhang

Abstract
For every integer k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 , we determine the extremal structure of an n n -vertex graph with at most t t vertex-disjoint copies of C 2 ​ k C_{2k} when n n is sufficiently large and t t lies in the interval [ ex ⁡ ( n , C 2 ​ k ) ε ​ n , ε ​ n ] \left[\frac{\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{2k})}{\varepsilon n},\varepsilon n\right] , where ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 is a constant depending only on k k . The question for k = 2 k=2 and t = o ⁡ ( ex ⁡ ( n , C 2 ​ k ) n ) t=o\left(\frac{\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{2k})}{n}\right) was explored in prior work [ 20 ] , revealing different extremal structures in these cases. Our result can be viewed as an extension of the theorems by Egawa [ 9 ] and Verstraëte [ 36 ] , where the focus was on the existence of many vertex-disjoint cycles of the same length without any length constraints. Keywords: even cycles, the Bondy–Simonovits Theorem, Turán problems, the Corrádi–Hajnal Theorem.



1
Intorduction

The classical Corrádi–Hajnal Theorem
[
8
]
asserts that for every
t\geq 2
, a graph with minimum degree at least
2t
on at least
3t
vertices contains
t
pairwise vertex-disjoint cycles.
Thomassen
[
34
]
extends this to graphs with minimum degree at least
3t+1
on at least
t^{2+o(1)}
vertices, showing they contain
t
pairwise vertex-disjoint cycles of the same length.
Thomassen’s result was further extended by
Egawa
[
9
]
to graphs with minimum degree at least
2t
on at least
(17+o(1))t
vertices when
t\geq 3
.
In
[
36
]
, Verstraëte provided a concise proof, showing that for every
t\geq 2
, a graph with minimum degree at least
2t
on at least
t^{7t}
vertices also contains
t
pairwise vertex-disjoint cycles of the same length.
In particular, the case
t=2
confirms a conjecture of Häggkvist
[
34
]
.

There are some other extensions of the Corrádi–Hajnal Theorem from the average degree perspective or by imposing different constraints on the length of cycles.
Notable instances include the work of Justesen
[
22
]
, who demonstrated that graphs with
n\geq 3t
vertices and at least
\max\left\{(2t-1)(n-t),\binom{3t-1}{2}+(n-3t+1)\right\}

t
pairwise vertex-disjoint cycles.
Chiba et al.
[
7
]
showed that every graph with minimum degree at least
2t
and with sufficiently large number of vertices contains
t
pairwise vertex-disjoint even cycles except in a few exceptional cases.
Wang
[
37
]
proved that for
t\geq 2
, every graph with minimum degree at least
2t
and at least
4t
vertices contains
t
pairwise vertex-disjoint cycles of length at least four, except in three exceptional cases.
Harvey and Wood
[

]
extended this to
t\geq 6
and

, demonstrating that every graph with average degree at least
4\ell t/3
contains
t
pairwise vertex-disjoint cycles of length at least
r
.

The problem of finding cycles of a fixed length in a graph also attracted considerable attention and stands as a central topic in Extremal Graph Theory.
An old theorem due to Mantel
[

states that every
n
-vertex graph with at least

For every

The tightness of the bound
\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor+1
is demonstrated by the balanced complete bipartite graph on
n
vertices in both cases.
Finding even cycles of fixed length is much more challenging.
Erdős stated (without a proof) in

The classical Bondy-Simonovits Theorem

In this note, we explore the following problem, which can be seen as a collective extension of the results by Egawa, Verstraëte, and Bondy–Simonovits:

What is the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex graph without
t+1
pairwise vertex-disjoint cycles of a fixed length?

This study is motivated by a remarkable result of Allen et al.
[
1
]
, where they determined, for large
n
, both the maximum number of edges and the structures of four distinct extremal constructions (corresponding to different values of
t
) in an
n
-vertex graph without
t+1
pairwise vertex-disjoint triangles for all integers
t
in the interval
[0,n/3]
.
Their result builds upon old theorems established by of Erdős
[
10
]
, Moon
[
30
]
, and Simonovits
[
33
]
, which specifically address this problem in cases where
t
is relatively small compared to
n
.

Let us introduce some definitions before presenting the main result.
Given a graph
F
, let
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
denote the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex
F
-free graph.
We expand this notation to
\mathrm{ex}(n,(t+1)F)
, representing the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex graph containing at most
t
pairwise vertex-disjoint copies of
F
.

The results of Mantel and Simonovits can be expressed as
\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{2k+1})=\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor
for
k\geq 1
when
n
is sufficiently large.
Similarly, the results of Erdős and Bondy–Simonovits can be written as
\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{2k})\leq 100kn^{1+\frac{1}{k}}
for all
k\geq 2
.
The exact value of
\mathrm{ex}(n,(t+1)C_{2k+1})
was established in
[
21
]
for large
n
and
t=o(n)
.
Additionally, the exact value of
\mathrm{ex}(n,(t+1)C_{2k})
was determined in
[
20
]
for large
n
, with
t
satisfying
t=o\left(\frac{\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{2k})}{n}\right)
or
\sqrt{n}\ll t\ll n
.
The following result builds upon the previous one, determining the exact value of
\mathrm{ex}(n,(t+1)C_{2k})
for
\frac{\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{2k})}{n}\ll t\ll n
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every integer
k\geq 3
there exists
\varepsilon_{k}>0
such that for sufficiently large
n
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}(n,(t+1)C_{2k})=\binom{n}{2}-\binom{n-k(t+1)+1}{2}+1

holds for all integers
t\in\left[\frac{\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{2k})}{\varepsilon_{k}n},\varepsilon_{k}n\right]
.

Define
S(m,n)
as the graph obtained from an
m
by
n-m
complete bipartite graph by adding all pairs in the part of size
m
as edges. Let
S^{+}(m,n)
be the graph obtained from
S(m,n)
by adding an edge into the part of size
n-m
.
Our proof shows that the unique extremal construction for Theorem
1.1
is simply
S^{+}(k(t+1)-1,n)
.
In contrast, the extreml construction for
\mathrm{ex}(n,(t+1)C_{2k})
when
t=o\left(\frac{\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{2k})}{n}\right)
is different, and refer the reader to
[
20
]
for more details.

Given a graph
F
and an integer
q\leq v(F)
, let

\displaystyle F[q]:=\left\{F[S]\colon S\subset V(F)\text{ and }|S|\geq q\right\}.

For an integer
q
satisfying
v(F)-\alpha(F)\leq q\leq v(F)
,
let

\displaystyle F^{\mathrm{ind}}[q]:=\left\{F[S]\colon S\subset V(F),\ |S|\geq q,\text{ and }V(F)\setminus S\text{ is independent}\right\}.

In other words,
F[q]
consists of induced subgraphs of
F
that can be obtained by removing at most
v(F)-q
vertices, while
F^{\mathrm{ind}}[q]
consists of induced subgraphs of
F
that can be obtained by removing an independent set of size at most
v(F)-q
.

Two examples of ways to remove an independent set of size three from

Recall the definition of the covering number
\tau(F)
of a graph
F
is

\displaystyle\tau(F):=\min\left\{|S|\colon\text{$S\subset V(F)$ and $S\cap e\neq\emptyset$ for all $e\in F$}\right\}.

A minor adjustment to the proof of Theorem
1.1
leads to the following more general result. For simplicity, we omit its details in this note.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
s_{2}\geq s_{1}\geq 2
be integers and
\mathbb{B}:=B_{s_{1},s_{2}}
be an
s_{1}
by
s_{2}
connected bipartite graph with
\tau(B)=s_{1}
.
Suppose that some member in
\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm{ind}}[s_{2}+1]
is the union of a matching with isolated vertices.
Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that for sufficiently large

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}(n,(t+1)\mathbb{B})=\binom{n}{2}-\binom{n-\tau(t+1)+1}{2}+\mathrm{ex}(n-\tau(t+1)+1,\mathbb{B}[s_{2}+1])

holds for all integers
t\in\left[\frac{\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathbb{B})}{\varepsilon n},\varepsilon n\right]

The extremal construction for Theorem
1.2
is the graph obtained from

by adding a maximum
\mathbb{B}[s_{2}+1]
-free graph to the part of size

.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
.
Our proof strategy begins by considering the collection
L\subset V(G)
of all vertices in a maximum
n
-vertex
(t+1)C_{2k}
-free graph
G
with degree
\Omega(n)
.
We will show that a significant proportion of vertices in
L
possess a degree near
n
.
Consequently, leveraging Lemma
2.7
, we can show that the size of
L
is less than
k(t+1)
.
Following this, a more detailed analysis of the structure of
G-L
will show that
G-L
is
\{K_{1,2},M_{2}\}
-free, and hence, contains at most one edge.
Here,
M_{2}
denotes the matching with two edges.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Fix an integer
k\geq 3
, and let
\mathbb{C}:=C_{2k}
for simplicity.
Let

\displaystyle\delta_{1}:=\frac{1}{32k^{2}},\quad\delta_{2}:=\frac{1}{4k},\quad\delta_{3}:=\frac{1}{2(k+1)},\quad\varepsilon:=\frac{\delta_{1}}{144k^{2}},\quad\theta:=\frac{20}{\delta_{1}k}\frac{\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathbb{C})}{n}.

Let
n
be sufficiently large and
t
be an integer satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{72k}{\delta_{3}}\theta\leq t\leq\varepsilon n.

(1)

Let
G
be a maximum
n
-vertex
(t+1)\mathbb{C}
-free graph with vertex set
V
.
Let

\displaystyle L_{1}:=\left\{v\in V\colon d_{G}(v)\geq(1-\delta_{1})n\right\},\quad L_{2}:=\left\{v\in V\setminus L_{1}\colon d_{G}(v)\geq\delta_{2}n\right\},

and

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle:=L_{1}\cup L_{2},\quad U:=V\setminus L,\quad\ell:=|L|,\quad\ell_{1}:=|L_{1}|,\quad\ell_{2}:=|L_{2}|.

In addition, let

\displaystyle G_{1}:=G-L_{1},\quad G_{2}:=G[U],\quad n_{1}:=n-\ell_{1},\quad\text{and}\quad t_{1}:=t-\left\lfloor\ell_{1}/k\right\rfloor.

Claim 3.1
.

We have
\ell_{1}<k(t+1)
and
G_{1}
is
(t_{1}+1)\mathbb{C}
-free.

Proof.

First, suppose to the contrary that
\ell_{1}\geq k(t+1)
.
We may assume that
\ell_{1}=k(t+1)
since otherwise we can replace
L_{1}
by a subset of size
k(t+1)
.
Let
H
be the collection of edges in
G
that have exactly one vertex in
L_{1}
.
Notice that
H
is bipartite, and for every
v\in L_{1}
it follows from the assumptions on
t
and
\delta_{1}
that

\displaystyle d_{H}(v)\geq d_{G}(v)-|L_{1}|\geq(1-\delta_{1})n-k(t+1)\geq(1-\delta_{1})n-2\varepsilon kn>\left(1-2\delta_{1}\right)n.

Since, in addition,
\frac{\left(1-2k\delta_{1}\right)k}{k}(n-\ell_{1})\geq n/4\geq\ell_{1}
(by (
1
)),
it follows from Lemma
2.6
that
(t+1)\mathbb{C}\subset(t+1)K_{k,k}\subset H
, a contradiction.

Now suppose to the contrary that there exists a collection
\mathcal{B}=\{\mathbb{C}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{C}_{t_{1}+1}\}
of
t_{1}+1
pairwise vertex-disjoint copies of
\mathbb{C}
in
G_{1}
.
Let
B:=\bigcup_{i\in[t_{1}+1]}V(\mathbb{C}_{i})
and
V^{\prime}:=V\setminus B
.
Let
H_{1}
be the collection of edges in

that contain exactly one vertex in

.
Similar to the argument above, we have

\displaystyle d_{H_{1}}(v)\geq d_{G}(v)-|L_{1}\cup B_{1}|\geq(1-\delta_{1})n-2k(t+1)>\left(1-2\delta_{1}\right)n.

Similarly, it follows from (

2.6
that
\lfloor\ell_{1}/k\rfloor\mathbb{C}\subset H_{1}
, which together with

implies that

, a contradiction.

By Claim
3.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle n_{1}=n-\ell_{1}\geq n-k(t+1)\geq(1-2\varepsilon k)n\geq\left(1-\frac{\delta_{1}}{2}\right)n.

Combined with the definition of

\displaystyle\Delta(G_{1})\leq(1-\delta_{1})n=(1-\delta_{1})\times\frac{n}{n_{1}}\times n_{1}\leq\left(1-\frac{\delta_{1}}{2}\right)n_{1}.

Notice from the definition of

\displaystyle\theta=\frac{20}{\delta_{1}k}\frac{\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathbb{C})}{n}\geq\frac{5}{\delta_{1}k/2}\left(\frac{\mathrm{ex}(n_{1},\mathbb{C})}{n_{1}-1}+\frac{72k}{\delta_{1}/2}\right).

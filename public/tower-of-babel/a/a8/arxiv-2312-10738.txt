Equivariant injectivity of crossed products
Joeri De Ro

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of a G \mathbb{G} -operator space ( X , α ) (X,\alpha) , which consists of an action α : X ↶ G \alpha:X\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G} of a locally compact quantum group G \mathbb{G} on an operator space X X , and we make a study of the notion of G \mathbb{G} -equivariant injectivity for such an operator space. Given a G \mathbb{G} -operator space ( X , α ) (X,\alpha) , we define a natural associated crossed product operator space X ⋊ α G X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G} , which has canonical actions X ⋊ α G ↶ G X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G} (the adjoint action) and X ⋊ α G ↶ G ˇ X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}\curvearrowleft\check{\mathbb{G}} (the dual action) where G ˇ \check{\mathbb{G}} is the dual quantum group. We then show that if X X is a G \mathbb{G} -operator system, then X ⋊ α G X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G} is G \mathbb{G} -injective if and only if X ⋊ α G X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G} is injective and G \mathbb{G} is amenable, and that (under a mild assumption) X ⋊ α G X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G} is G ˇ \check{\mathbb{G}} -injective if and only if X X is G \mathbb{G} -injective. We discuss how these results generalise and unify several recent results from the literature, and we give new applications.

In

2.1.
Operator spaces and operator systems

For the theory of operator spaces and operator systems, we refer the reader to the standard reference
[
ER00

of operator spaces, a completely contractive map
\phi:Y\to X
is called

if
\phi(x)=x
for all

.
Several kinds of tensor products will play an important role in this paper. We employ the following notations:

•

\odot
will denote the algebraic tensor product of vector spaces.

•

\hat{\otimes}
will denote the
projective tensor product
of operator spaces
[
ER00
, Chapter 7]
.

•

\otimes
will denote the
injective (or minimal) tensor product
of operator spaces
[
ER00
, Chapter 8]
.

•

\bar{\otimes}
will denote the
Fubini tensor product
of operator spaces.

The Fubini tensor product is not as well-known as the other operator space tensor products. Therefore, we give a brief overview of the relevant facts that we will be needing. The following discussion is taken from
[
DH24
, Section 2.1]
, with the difference that we will be needing operator spaces and completely bounded maps instead of operator systems and unital completely positive maps. Therefore, the discussion was slightly modified accordingly. For proofs of the facts that follow, we refer the reader to
[
Ham82
, I, Section 3]
. We note that Hamana’s proofs and statements are given in the context of operator systems, but the proofs are easily adapted to the more general operator space setting. See also
[
Ham91
, Section 1]
for more information.

Let
X\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}),Y\subseteq B(\mathcal{K})
be concretely represented operator spaces. We define the Fubini tensor product

X\bar{\otimes}Y=\{z\in B(\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{K}):(\mu\bar{\otimes}\id)(z)\in Y\text{\ and }(\id\bar{\otimes}\nu)(z)\in X\text{\ for\ all }\mu\in B(\mathcal{H})_{*},\nu\in B(\mathcal{K})_{*}\}

which is again an operator space. The Fubini tensor product obeys obvious commutativity, associativity and distributivity (with respect to
\ell^{\infty}
-direct sums of operator spaces) properties. If either
X
or
Y
is finite-dimensional, then
X\bar{\otimes}Y
coincides with the algebraic tensor product
X\odot Y
. If
X,Y
are both von Neumann algebras, then the Fubini tensor product is simply the von Neumann tensor product.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
Ham82
, I, Lemma 3.5]

Assume that
X_{j}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}_{j}),j=1,2
are operator spaces and
\phi:X_{1}\to X_{2}
is a completely bounded map. Fix an orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in I}
for
\mathcal{K}
and let
\{E_{ij}\}_{i,j\in I}\subseteq B(\mathcal{K})
be the associated matrix units. The assignment

\phi\otimes\id:X_{1}\bar{\otimes}B(\mathcal{K})\to X_{2}\bar{\otimes}B(\mathcal{K}):\sum_{(i,j)}x_{ij}\otimes E_{ij}\mapsto\sum_{(i,j)}\phi(x_{ij})\otimes E_{ij}

(with the sums converging in the strong topology) is a well-defined completely bounded map with
\|\phi\otimes\id\|_{\operatorname{cb}}=\|\phi\|_{\operatorname{cb}}.
Moreover, it satisfies the following properties:

(1)

\phi\otimes\id
extends
\phi\odot\id:X_{1}\odot B(\mathcal{K})\to X_{2}\odot B(\mathcal{K})
.

(2)

(\phi\otimes\id)((1\otimes a)z(1\otimes b))=(1\otimes a)(\phi\otimes\id)(z)(1\otimes b)
for all
a,b\in B(\mathcal{K})
and all
z\in X_{1}\bar{\otimes}B(\mathcal{K})
.

(3)

(\id\otimes\omega)(\phi\otimes\id)(z)=\phi((\id\otimes\omega)(z))
for all
z\in X_{1}\bar{\otimes}B(\mathcal{K})
and all
\omega\in B(\mathcal{K})_{*}
.

The properties
(1)
and
(2)
determine
\phi\otimes\id
uniquely in the sense that if
\psi:X_{1}\bar{\otimes}B(\mathcal{K})\to X_{2}\bar{\otimes}B(\mathcal{K})
is a linear map satisfying
(1)
and
(2)
, then
\psi=\phi\otimes\id
.

Of course, we have the analoguous result for
\id\otimes\phi:B(\mathcal{K})\bar{\otimes}X_{1}\to B(\mathcal{K})\bar{\otimes}X_{2}
as well. If
X\subseteq B(\mathcal{H})
is an operator space and if
f\in X^{*}
, then we can form the slice map
f\otimes\id:X\bar{\otimes}B(\mathcal{K})\to\mathbb{C}\bar{\otimes}B(\mathcal{K})\cong B(\mathcal{K})
and similarly for
\id\otimes f
.

To make sure that everything works well, we need to impose continuity assumptions. This is illustrated by the following results, which will be used all the time without further mention.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
Ham82
, I, Lemma 3.8]

Let
X_{j}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}_{j}),j=1,2
be operator spaces,
Y\subseteq B(\mathcal{K})
an operator space and
\phi:X_{1}\to X_{2}
a completely bounded map. We have
(\phi\otimes\id)(X_{1}\bar{\otimes}Y)\subseteq X_{2}\bar{\otimes}Y
in the following two cases:

(1)

Y
is
\sigma
-weakly closed.

(2)

X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are
\sigma
-weakly closed and
\phi:X_{1}\to X_{2}
is
\sigma
-weakly continuous

A similar result holds for
\id\otimes\phi
.

Proposition 2.3
.

[
Ham82
, I, Lemma 3.9]

Let
X_{j}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}_{j})
and
Y_{j}\subseteq B(\mathcal{K}_{j})
,
j=1,2
be operator spaces. Let
\phi:X_{1}\to X_{2}
and
\psi:Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
be completely bounded maps. If
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
are
\sigma
-weakly closed and
\psi:Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
is
\sigma
-weakly continuous, then for
z\in X_{1}\bar{\otimes}Y_{1}
, the elements

(\id_{B(\mathcal{H}_{2})}\otimes\psi)(\phi\otimes\id_{B(\mathcal{K}_{1})})(z),\quad(\phi\otimes\id_{B(\mathcal{K}_{2})})(\id_{B(\mathcal{H}_{1})}\otimes\psi)(z)

agree and belong to
X_{2}\bar{\otimes}Y_{2}
.
In this way, we obtain a canonical map
\phi\otimes\psi:X_{1}\bar{\otimes}Y_{1}\to X_{2}\bar{\otimes}Y_{2}
with
\|\phi\otimes\psi\|_{\operatorname{cb}}=\|\phi\|_{\operatorname{cb}}\|\psi\|_{\operatorname{cb}}
. If

.

Given a von Neumann algebra
M
and an operator space

, we can use the preceding result to make sense of the abstract operator space

without specifying concrete realisations as bounded operators on certain Hilbert spaces.

Let us also mention the following result:

Proposition 2.4
.

[

If

If one encounters a Fubini tensor product (possibly with more than two factors) in this paper, it will always be the case that all factors, except possibly the first one, are von Neumann algebras and if tensor product maps between Fubini tensor products are considered, then there will always be

If

\Phi:X\bar{\otimes}M\to CB(M_{*},X),\quad\Phi(z)(\omega)=(\id\otimes\omega)(z),\quad z\in X\bar{\otimes}M,\omega\in M_{*}

is a completely isometric isomorphism

where we used

X

A

A

, every
A

2.2.
Locally compact quantum groups.

We recall some basic results from the theory of locally compact quantum groups
[
KV00
,
KV03
,
VV03
]
. We will follow the same conventions and notations as e.g.
[
DD24
]
. Note that the conventions for quantum group duality differ from our previous work
[
DH24
]
.

A
Hopf-von Neumann algebra
is a pair
(M,\Delta)
where
M
is a von Neumann algebra and
\Delta:M\to M\bar{\otimes}M
a unital, normal, isometric
*
-homomorphism such that
(\Delta\otimes\id)\circ\Delta=(\id\otimes\Delta)\circ\Delta
.

A
locally compact quantum group

\mathbb{G}
is a Hopf-von Neumann algebra
(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}),\Delta)
for which there exist normal, semifinite, faithful weights
\Phi,\Psi:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})_{+}\to[0,\infty]
such that
(\id\otimes\Phi)\Delta(x)=\Phi(x)1
for all
x\in\mathscr{M}_{\Phi}^{+}
and
(\Psi\otimes\id)\Delta(x)=\Psi(x)1
for all
x\in\mathscr{M}_{\Psi}^{+}
. The von Neumann algebra predual
L^{1}(\mathbb{G}):=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})_{*}
is a completely contractive Banach algebra for the multiplication

\mu\star\nu:=(\mu\otimes\nu)\circ\Delta,\quad\mu,\nu\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G}).

Without any loss of generality, we may (and will) assume that
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
is faithfully represented in standard form on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
. With respect to the weights
\Phi,\Psi
, we then have GNS-maps

\Lambda_{\Phi}:\mathscr{N}_{\Phi}\to L^{2}(\mathbb{G}),\quad\Lambda_{\Psi}:\mathscr{N}_{\Psi}\to L^{2}(\mathbb{G}).

Fundamental to the theory of locally compact quantum groups are the unitaries

V,W\in B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))

called respectively
right
and
left
regular unitary representation. They are uniquely characterised by the identities

(\id\otimes\omega)(V)\Lambda_{\Psi}(x)=\Lambda_{\Psi}((\id\otimes\omega)\Delta(x)),\qquad\omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G}),x\in\mathscr{N}_{\Psi},

(\omega\otimes\id)(W^{*})\Lambda_{\Phi}(x)=\Lambda_{\Phi}((\omega\otimes\id)\Delta(x)),\qquad\omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G}),x\in\mathscr{N}_{\Phi}.

They are
multiplicative unitaries

[
BS93
]
meaning that

V_{12}V_{13}V_{23}=V_{23}V_{12},\qquad W_{12}W_{13}W_{23}=W_{23}W_{12}

and they implement the coproduct of
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
in the sense that

W^{*}(1\otimes x)W=\Delta(x)=V(x\otimes 1)V^{*},\qquad x\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}).

Moreover, we have

C_{0}(\mathbb{G}):=[(\omega\otimes\id)(V)\mid\omega\in B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))_{*}]=[(\id\otimes\omega)(W)\mid\omega\in B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))_{*}]

which is a
\sigma
-weakly dense C
∗
-subalgebra of
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
. Then
\Delta(C_{0}(\mathbb{G}))\subseteq M(C_{0}(\mathbb{G})\otimes C_{0}(\mathbb{G}))
.

We can also define the von Neumann algebra

L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})=[(\omega\otimes\id)(W)\mid\omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G})]^{\sigma\textrm{-weak}}

with coproduct

\hat{\Delta}(x)=\Sigma W(x\otimes 1)W^{*}\Sigma
where
x\in L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})
.
Then the pair
(L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}),\hat{\Delta})
defines the locally compact quantum group
\hat{\mathbb{G}}
, which is called the dual of
\mathbb{G}
. Similarly, we can also define the von Neumann algebra
L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}})=[(\id\otimes\omega)(V)\mid\omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G})]^{\sigma\textrm{-weak}}

with coproduct

\check{\Delta}(x)=V^{*}(1\otimes x)V
for
x\in L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}})
.
Then the pair
(L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}}),\check{\Delta})
defines a locally compact quantum group
\check{\mathbb{G}}
. We have
L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})=L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}})^{\prime}
. If
G
is a locally compact group, we have

L^{\infty}(\hat{G})=\mathscr{L}(G)
and
L^{\infty}(\check{G})=\mathscr{R}(G)
, the left resp. right group von Neumann algebra associated with
G
. Therefore,
\hat{\mathbb{G}}
and
\check{\mathbb{G}}
should be thought of as a left and a right version of the dual locally compact quantum group.

The left multiplicative unitary of
\check{\mathbb{G}}
will be denoted by
\check{W}
and the right multiplicative unitary of
\check{\mathbb{G}}
will be denoted by
\check{V}
. We have
\check{W}=V
and

V\in L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}),\quad W\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}),\quad\check{V}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})^{\prime}\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}}).

In fact, we have e.g.
V\in M(C_{0}(\check{\mathbb{G}})\otimes C_{0}(\mathbb{G}))
and similarly for
W
and
\check{V}
. For future use, we will also need the following notations:

\displaystyle\Delta_{l}:B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})):x\mapsto W^{*}(1\otimes x)W,

\displaystyle\Delta_{r}:B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\to B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}):x\mapsto V(x\otimes 1)V^{*},

\displaystyle\check{\Delta}_{r}:B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\to B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}}):x\mapsto\check{V}(x\otimes 1)\check{V}^{*}.

A locally compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
is called
compact
if
C_{0}(\mathbb{G})
is unital, and we then write
C_{0}(\mathbb{G})=C(\mathbb{G})
. In that case the left Haar weight
\Phi
is a normal state and we say that
\mathbb{G}
is of
Kac type
if the Haar state
\Phi:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\to\mathbb{C}
is tracial. A locally compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
is called
discrete
if
\check{\mathbb{G}}
is compact and we then write
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})=\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
and
C_{0}(\mathbb{G})=c_{0}(\mathbb{G})
. If
\mathbb{G}
is a discrete quantum group, there is a unique normal state
\epsilon\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{G}):=L^{1}(\mathbb{G})
, called
counit
, such that

(\epsilon\otimes\id)\Delta=\id=(\id\otimes\epsilon)\Delta
. We call a discrete quantum group
\mathbb{G}

unimodular
if
\check{\mathbb{G}}
is of Kac type. A locally compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
is called
co-amenable

[
BT03
]
if there exists a state
\epsilon\in C_{0}(\mathbb{G})^{*}
such that
(\id\otimes\epsilon)\Delta=\id_{C_{0}(\mathbb{G})}=(\epsilon\otimes\id)\Delta
, where we note that this makes sense since we have a canonical extension
\id\otimes\epsilon:M(C_{0}(\mathbb{G})\otimes C_{0}(\mathbb{G}))\to M(C_{0}(\mathbb{G}))
. Thus, it is clear that discrete quantum groups are always co-amenable. On the other hand, a locally compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
is called
amenable
if there exists a state
m:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\to\mathbb{C}
such that

(m\otimes\id)\Delta(x)=m(x)1,\quad x\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}).

If
\mathbb{G}
is co-amenable, then
\check{\mathbb{G}}
is amenable but the converse is still open for general locally compact quantum groups. We say that a locally compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
is
inner amenable

[
Cr19
]
if there exists a state
n:L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}})\to\mathbb{C}
such that

(n\otimes\id)(\Delta_{r}(x))=n(x)1,\quad x\in L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}}).

If
G
is a locally compact group, this is equivalent with the existence of a conjugation-invariant mean on
L^{\infty}(G)

[
CZ17
, Proposition 3.2]
.

We will need the following elementary lemma several times:

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a locally compact quantum group and
\mathcal{H}
a Hilbert space.
If
U\in B(\mathcal{H})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
is a unitary satisfying
(\id\otimes\Delta)(U)=U_{12}U_{13}
, then

U^{*}(1\otimes L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}}))U\subseteq B(\mathcal{H})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}}).

Proof.

By the assumption, we see that
V_{23}U_{12}V_{23}^{*}=U_{12}U_{13}
or equivalently

U_{12}^{*}V_{23}U_{12}=U_{13}V_{23}\in B(\mathcal{H})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}})\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})).

Therefore, if
\omega\in B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))_{*}
, we find

\displaystyle U^{*}(1\otimes(\id\otimes\omega)(V))U=(\id\otimes\id\otimes\omega)(U_{12}^{*}V_{23}U_{12})=(\id\otimes\id\otimes\omega)(U_{13}V_{23})\in B(\mathcal{H})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}}).

Recalling that
L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}})=[(\id\otimes\omega)(V):\omega\in B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))_{*}]^{\sigma\textrm{-weak}}
, the lemma follows.
∎

2.3.
Actions of locally compact quantum groups and crossed products.

A (right)
\mathbb{G}
-dynamical von Neumann algebra
is a pair
(M,\alpha)
such that
M
is a von Neumann algebra and
\alpha:M\to M\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
is an injective, unital, normal
*
-homomorphism satisfying the coaction property
(\alpha\otimes\id)\circ\alpha=(\id\otimes\Delta)\circ\alpha
. We sometimes denote this with
\alpha:M\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}
. We write

M^{\alpha}=\{x\in M:\alpha(x)=x\otimes 1\}

for the von Neumann subalgebra of fixed points of
(M,\alpha)
and the trivial
\mathbb{G}
-action on a von Neumann algebra

will always be denoted by
\tau
, i.e.
\tau(x)=x\otimes 1
for
x\in M
. Given a
\mathbb{G}
-dynamical von Neumann algebra
(M,\alpha)
, we define the

M\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}=[\alpha(M)(1\otimes L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}}))]^{\prime\prime}.

We have the alternative description

M\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}=\{z\in M\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})):(\alpha\otimes\id)(z)=(\id\otimes\Delta_{l})(z)\}.

It is easy to verify that we have maps

so that

becomes a
\mathbb{G}
-resp.
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-dynamical von Neumann algebra. We have that
(M\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})^{\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}}=\alpha(M)
. Given any von Neumann algebra
M
, we have
M\rtimes_{\tau}\mathbb{G}=M\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}})
. Given two
\mathbb{G}
-dynamical von Neumann algebras
(M,\alpha)
and
(N,\beta)
, a completely bounded map
\phi:M\to N
is called
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant if
(\phi\otimes\id)\circ\alpha=\beta\circ\phi.

There is a unique
*
-isomorphism
\kappa:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\rtimes_{\Delta}\mathbb{G}\to B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))
such that
\kappa(\Delta(x))=x
and
\kappa(1\otimes y)=y
for all
x\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
and all
y\in L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}})
. In fact,

It defines a
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant resp.
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-equivariant isomorphism

(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\rtimes_{\Delta}\mathbb{G},\id\otimes\Delta_{r})\cong(B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})),\Delta_{r}),\qquad(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\rtimes_{\Delta}\mathbb{G},\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r})\cong(B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})),\check{\Delta}_{r}).

If
(M,\alpha)
is a
\mathbb{G}
-dynamical von Neumann algebra where

is standardly represented on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, there exists a canonical unitary

such that
\alpha(m)=U_{\alpha}(m\otimes 1)U_{\alpha}^{*}
for all
m\in M

[
Va01
]
. We call

the
unitary implementation
of
\alpha
, and it satisfies
(\id\otimes\Delta)(U_{\alpha})=U_{\alpha,12}U_{\alpha,13}.

Following the terminology introduced in
[
DD24
]
, we say that
(M,\alpha)
is
\mathbb{G}
-amenable
if there exists a
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant unital completely positive conditional expectation
(M\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}),\id\otimes\Delta)\to(\alpha(M),\id\otimes\Delta)
and we call
(M,\alpha)

inner
\mathbb{G}
-amenable
if there exists a
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant unital completely positive conditional expectation

. It is not so hard to see that the notion of an amenable action of a locally compact group on a von Neumann algebra, as introduced in
[
AD79
]
, is equivalent to the quantum definition (in
[
AD79
]
, the author uses left shifts, whereas we must use right shifts in our conventions). We also mention the important fact that

is
\mathbb{G}
-amenable if and only if

is inner

-amenable and that
(M,\alpha)
is inner
\mathbb{G}

is

-amenable

.

Let us also mention the following well-known principle:

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
(M,\alpha)
be a
\mathbb{G}
-dynamical von Neumann algebra and
N
a von Neumann subalgebra of
M
with
\alpha(N)\subseteq N\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
, so that also
(N,\alpha)
is a
\mathbb{G}
-dynamical von Neumann algebra. The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

There exists a
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant unital completely positive conditional expectation
\phi:M\to N
.

(2)

There exists a
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-equivariant unital completely positive conditional expectation
\psi:M\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}\to N\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}
.

Proof.

(1)\implies(2)
We can take
\psi=\phi\otimes\id
.

(2)\implies(1)
Since
\psi
is
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-equivariant, it restricts to a map
\psi:\alpha(M)\to\alpha(N)
between the fixed points. Then
\phi:=\alpha^{-1}\circ\psi\circ\alpha:M\to N
is a unital completely positive conditional expectation. We have
\psi(1\otimes y)=1\otimes y
for all
y\in L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}})
. From this, it follows that
(\psi\otimes\id)(z)=z
for all
z\in M(1\otimes C_{0}(\check{\mathbb{G}})\otimes C_{0}(\mathbb{G}))
. In particular,
V_{23}
belongs the multiplicative domain of
\psi\otimes\id
. Therefore, if
z\in M\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}
, we have

\displaystyle(\psi\otimes\id)(\id\otimes\Delta_{r})(z)=(\psi\otimes\id)(V_{23}(z\otimes 1)V_{23}^{*})=V_{23}(\psi(z)\otimes 1)V_{23}=(\id\otimes\Delta_{r})\psi(z).

From this, it follows that the map
\phi
is
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant.
∎

4.
Crossed products

Once again, we fix a locally compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
. We introduce crossed products and investigate basic properties.

Definition 4.1
.

If
(X,\alpha)
is a
\mathbb{G}
-operator space, we define the Fubini crossed product as the operator space

X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}:=\{z\in X\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})):(\id\otimes\Delta_{l})(z)=(\alpha\otimes\id)(z)\}.

Obviously, if
(X,\alpha)
is a
\mathbb{G}
-operator system, then
X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}
is also an operator system. If
(X,\alpha)
is a
\mathbb{G}
-dynamical von Neumann algebra, then

is the usual
W^{*}
-crossed product and the notation is unambiguous. As expected, the Fubini crossed product carries canonical
\mathbb{G}
and
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-actions:

Proposition 4.2
.

We have

(\id\otimes\Delta_{r})(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})\subseteq(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}),\quad(\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r})(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})\subseteq(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}}).

Consequently,
(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G},\id\otimes\Delta_{r})
is a
\mathbb{G}
-operator space and
(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G},\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r})
is a
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-operator space.

Proof.

Similar as in
[
DH24
, Theorem 3.40, Proposition 3.43]
, making use of the commutation relations

(\Delta_{l}\otimes\id)\circ\Delta_{r}=(\id\otimes\Delta_{r})\circ\Delta_{l}
and
(\Delta_{l}\otimes\id)\circ\check{\Delta}_{r}=(\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r})\circ\Delta_{l}.

∎

If
(X,\alpha)
and
(Y,\beta)
are
\mathbb{G}
-operator spaces, and if
\phi:(X,\alpha)\to(Y,\beta)
is
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant, it is easily verified that

(\phi\otimes\id)(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})\subseteq Y\rtimes_{\beta}\mathbb{G}.
We denote the restricted map by
\phi\rtimes\mathbb{G}
and clearly

\phi\rtimes\mathbb{G}:(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G},\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r})\to(Y\rtimes_{\beta}\mathbb{G},\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r})

is
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-equivariant. Therefore, we may view the Fubini crossed product as a functor from the category of
\mathbb{G}
-operator spaces to the category of
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-operator spaces.

So far, the Fubini crossed product behaved exactly like the von Neumann crossed product. However, there are some pitfalls that one has to be careful with. Namely:

•

It may happen that
\alpha(X)\subsetneq(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})^{\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}}
.

•

The Takesaki-Takai duality
(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})\rtimes_{\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}}\check{\mathbb{G}}\cong X\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))
may not hold.

The notion of
\mathbb{G}
-completeness of a
\mathbb{G}
-operator space, originally introduced in
[

, Definition 5.1]
in the context of locally compact groups, lies at the heart of these issues. Hamana’s definition readily generalises to the context of locally compact quantum groups:

Definition 4.3
.

A
\mathbb{G}
-operator space

is said to be
\mathbb{G}
-complete if for every
\mathbb{G}
-extension
Y
of
X
and every

, the condition

implies that

.

Note that if
\mathbb{G}
is a discrete quantum group, then every

-operator space is

-complete. Indeed, with

the counit, we have

for all

. Not every
\mathbb{G}
-operator space is
\mathbb{G}
-complete: Indeed, Hamana observes that if
G
is a locally compact group and if
X
is
G
-complete, then
X
must be necessarily translation-invariant
[
Ham91
, Proposition 5.6 (vi)]
in the sense of
[
Ham91
, Definition 3.2]
. However, Hamana also provides a way to construct explicit examples of
G
-operator spaces that are not translation-invariant.

The following proposition contains all we need to know about
\mathbb{G}
-completeness for our purposes. In the proof, we will make use of the following simple fact: if
(X,\alpha)\subseteq{G}(Y,\beta)
are
\mathbb{G}
-operator spaces, then

X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}=(Y\rtimes_{\beta}\mathbb{G})\cap(X\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))).

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
(X,\alpha)
be a
\mathbb{G}
-operator space.

(1)

If
(X,\alpha)
is
\mathbb{G}
-injective, it is
\mathbb{G}
-complete.

(2)

(X,\alpha)
is
\mathbb{G}
-complete if and only if
(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})^{\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}}=\alpha(X)
.

(3)

If
(X,\alpha)
is
\mathbb{G}
-complete, the complete isometry

\Phi:X\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\to X\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})):z\mapsto V_{23}^{*}(\alpha\otimes\id)(z)V_{23}.

has image
\operatorname{\Phi}(X\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})))=(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})\rtimes_{\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}}\check{\mathbb{G}}
. In other words, the Takesaki-Takai duality holds.

Proof.

(1)
Assume that
X\subseteq{G}Y
. The
\mathbb{G}
-injectivity of
X
yields a
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant complete contraction
\varphi:Y\to X
such that
\varphi(x)=x
for all
x\in X
. Assume that
y\in Y
with
L^{1}(\mathbb{G})\rhd y\subseteq X
. If
\omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G})
, we have

\omega\rhd\varphi(y)=\varphi(\omega\rhd y)=\omega\rhd y

so that
y=\varphi(y)\in X
.

(2) Assume first that
(X,\alpha)
is
\mathbb{G}
-complete. Choose a
\mathbb{G}
-dynamical von Neumann algebra
(Y,\beta)
such that
(X,\alpha)\subseteq{G}(Y,\beta)
and fix
z\in(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})^{\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}}
. Clearly

(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})^{\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}}\subseteq(Y\rtimes_{\beta}\mathbb{G})^{\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}}=\beta(Y).

Thus, there is
y\in Y
with
z=\beta(y)
and we have
\omega\rhd y\in X
for all
\omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G})
. Therefore, the
\mathbb{G}
-completeness of
X
ensures that
y\in X
. Thus,
z=\beta(y)=\alpha(y)\in\alpha(X)
.

Conversely, assume that
(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})^{\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}}=\alpha(X)
. Consider any
\mathbb{G}
-extension
(X,\alpha)\subseteq{G}(Y,\beta)
. We observe that

\alpha(X)=(X\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}))\cap\beta(Y).

Indeed, if
z\in(X\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}))\cap\beta(Y)
, then trivially
(\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r})(z)=z\otimes 1
and also

z\in(X\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})))\cap(Y\rtimes_{\beta}\mathbb{G})=X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}.

Consequently,
z\in(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})^{\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}}=\alpha(X)
.
If
y\in Y
satisfies
L^{1}(\mathbb{G})\rhd y\subseteq X
, then
\beta(y)\in X\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
and thus
\beta(y)\in\alpha(X)=\beta(X)
. By injectivity of
\beta
, we conclude that
y\in X
. Since
(Y,\beta)
was arbitrary, we conclude that
(X,\alpha)
is
\mathbb{G}
-complete.

(3) The following proof is contained in
[
Ham91
, Proposition 5.7]
. We recall it for the convenience of the reader. Let
(Y,\beta)
be any
\mathbb{G}
-dynamical von Neumann algebra such that
(X,\alpha)\subseteq{G}(Y,\beta)
. Then we know that the map

\Phi:Y\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\to(Y\rtimes_{\beta}\mathbb{G})\rtimes_{\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}}\check{\mathbb{G}}:z\mapsto V_{23}^{*}(\beta\otimes\id)(z)V_{23}

is a
*
-isomorphism of von Neumann algebras (see e.g.
[
DD24
, Theorem 5.24]
or
[
Va01
, Theorem 2.6]
).
Note first that

\displaystyle(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})\rtimes_{\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}}\check{\mathbb{G}}

\displaystyle=[((X\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})))\cap(Y\rtimes_{\beta}\mathbb{G}))\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))]\cap[(Y\rtimes_{\beta}\mathbb{G})\rtimes_{\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}}\check{\mathbb{G}}]

\displaystyle=[X\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))]\cap[(Y\rtimes_{\beta}\mathbb{G})\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))]\cap[(Y\rtimes_{\beta}\mathbb{G})\rtimes_{\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}}\check{\mathbb{G}}]

\displaystyle=[X\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))]\cap[(Y\rtimes_{\beta}\mathbb{G})\rtimes_{\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}}\check{\mathbb{G}}].

Next, observe that the
\mathbb{G}
-completeness of
(X,\alpha)
ensures that

\alpha(X)=(X\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})))\cap\beta(Y).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})\rtimes_{\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}}{\check{\mathbb{G}}}

\displaystyle=[X\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))]\cap[(Y\rtimes_{\beta}\mathbb{G})\rtimes_{\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}}\check{\mathbb{G}}]

\displaystyle=[X\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))]\cap[V_{23}^{*}(\beta(Y)\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})))V_{23}]

\displaystyle=V_{23}^{*}[X\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))]\cap[\beta(Y)\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))]V_{23}

\displaystyle=V_{23}^{*}(\alpha(X)\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})))V_{23}

\displaystyle=V_{23}^{*}(\alpha\otimes\id)(X\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})))V_{23}

and the proof is finished. ∎

Remark 4.5
.

Suppose that
(M,\alpha)
is a
\mathbb{G}
-dynamical von Neumann algebra and that
X\subseteq M
is a
\sigma
-weakly closed subspace such that
\alpha(X)\subseteq X\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
. In this way, we obtain the
\mathbb{G}
-operator space
(X,\alpha)
. It is an interesting question if the fixed point condition
\alpha(X)=(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})^{\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}}
is automatically satisfied in this case. Let us prove that this is indeed the case if
\hat{\mathbb{G}}
has the approximation property (AP)
[
DKV24
]
(in particular, it holds when
\mathbb{G}
is co-amenable). It follows from
[
DKV24
, Theorem 4.4]
that there exists a net
\{\omega_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq L^{1}(\mathbb{G})
such that

(\id\otimes\omega_{i})\alpha(m)\to m
in the
\sigma
-weak topology for every
m\in M
.
Therefore, if
\alpha(m)\in X\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
, the fact that
X
is
\sigma
-weakly closed entails that
m\in X
, proving that

(X\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}))\cap\alpha(M)=\alpha(X).

From this, one easily concludes that the desired condition
(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})^{\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}}=\alpha(X)
holds.

Also the following result involving an iterated crossed product will be useful later:

Proposition 4.6
.

If
(X,\alpha)
is a

-operator space, then the map

-equivariant completely isometric isomorphism such that

on

Proof.

Let
(Y,\beta)
be a
\mathbb{G}
-dynamical von Neumann algebra such that
(X,\alpha)\subseteq{G}(Y,\beta)
. Then the map

\Phi:((Y\rtimes_{\beta}\mathbb{G})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}}),\id\bar{\otimes}\id\bar{\otimes}\check{\Delta})\to((Y\rtimes_{\beta}\mathbb{G})\rtimes_{\id\otimes\Delta_{r}}\mathbb{G},\id\bar{\otimes}\id\bar{\otimes}\check{\Delta}_{r}):z\mapsto V_{23}zV_{23}^{*}

is a
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-equivariant

-isomorphism such that
\Phi\circ(\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r})=\beta\otimes\id
on

. Indeed, that

Also the equality

\displaystyle=[(Y\rtimes_{\beta}\mathbb{G})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}})]\cap[X\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}})]

The result then easily follows.
∎

5.
Equivariant injectivity of crossed products

Once again, fix a locally compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
. In this section, we prove our main results.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(X,\alpha)
be a
\mathbb{G}
-operator system. The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

(X,\alpha)
is
\mathbb{G}
-injective.

(2)

(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G},\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r})
is
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-injective and
(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})^{\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}}=\alpha(X)
.

Proof.

(1)\implies(2)
Assume
X
is
\mathbb{G}
-injective. We may assume
X\subseteq B(\mathcal{H})
.
Since
X
is
\mathbb{G}
-injective, there is a
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant unital completely positive map

\phi:(B(\mathcal{H})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}),\id\otimes\Delta)\to(X,\alpha)

such that
\phi\circ\alpha=\id_{X}
. Then applying the crossed product functor
-\rtimes\mathbb{G}
, we obtain a
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-equivariant unital completely positive conditional expectation

\psi:(B(\mathcal{H})\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})),\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r})\cong((B(\mathcal{H})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}))\rtimes_{\id\otimes\Delta}\mathbb{G},\id\otimes\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r})\to(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G},\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}).

Therefore, injectivity of
B(\mathcal{H})\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))
implies injectivity of
X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}
as an operator system. It remains to argue that the action
X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}\curvearrowleft\check{\mathbb{G}}
is
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-amenable. To see this, note that
\mathbb{G}
-injectivity provides us with a
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant unital completely positive map

E:(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G},\id\otimes\Delta_{r})\to(X,\alpha),\quad E\circ\alpha=\id_{X}.

Using Proposition
4.6
, we can consider the
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-equivariant unital completely positive map

that satisfies

. In other words, the action
\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}:X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}\curvearrowleft\check{\mathbb{G}}
is
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-amenable. By Proposition
3.10
,
(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G},\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r})
is
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-injective. By Proposition
4.4
, the statement about the fixed points is clear.

(2)\implies(1)
Assume that
(Y,\beta)
and

\mathbb{G}
-operator systems,

\mathbb{G}

and

\check{\mathbb{G}}

such that the diagram on the right commutes:

.

Amenability and inner amenability of actions

Fix a locally compact quantum group

. As we have seen, the notion of equivariant injectivity is closely related to the notion of amenability of an action. Hence, in this section, we investigate the notion of
\mathbb{G}
-amenability, and the related notion of inner
\mathbb{G}
-amenability, in some more detail.

6.1.
Inner actions

We start with a study of inner actions. Let
(M,\alpha)
be a
\mathbb{G}
-dynamical von Neumann algebra. The action
\alpha:M\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}
is called

if there is a unitary

.

The following lemma shows that the crossed product with respect to an inner action is very easy to understand:

Lemma 6.1
.

Assume that
M
is a von Neumann algebra, and
U\in M\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
a unitary such that
(\id\otimes\Delta)(U)=U_{12}U_{13}
. Consider the
\mathbb{G}
-action

\alpha:M\to M\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}):m\mapsto U(m\otimes 1)U^{*}.

Then

M\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}=U(M\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}}))U^{*}.
Consequently, the map

\psi:(M\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G},\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r})\to(M\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}}),\id\otimes\check{\Delta}):z\mapsto U^{*}zU

is a
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-equivariant isomorphism.

Proof.

If
x\in L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}})
, we have

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\id)(U(1\otimes x)U^{*})

\displaystyle=U_{12}U_{13}U_{12}^{*}(1\otimes 1\otimes x)U_{12}U_{13}^{*}U_{12}^{*}

\displaystyle=(\id\otimes\Delta)(U)(1\otimes 1\otimes x)(\id\otimes\Delta)(U)^{*}

\displaystyle=(\id\otimes\Delta)(U)(\id\otimes\Delta_{l})(1\otimes x)(\id\otimes\Delta)(U^{*})

\displaystyle=(\id\otimes\Delta_{l})(U(1\otimes x)U^{*})

so that
U(1\otimes x)U^{*}\in M\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}
. We conclude that

U(M\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}}))U^{*}\subseteq M\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}.

The other inclusion follows immediately from Lemma
2.5
. The

is immediate from the fact that

and

commute.

6.3.
Application to the non-commutative Poisson boundary

We give an application of the above results to the non-commutative Poisson boundary
[

]
. We briefly recall the details of the construction. Let

be a
\mathbb{G}
-dynamical von Neumann algebra and consider a normal

-equivariant unital completely positive map

of unital completely positive maps

in the point
\sigma
-weak topology, we find a
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant unital completely positive conditional expectation

Of particular importance is the case where

. In that case, to any state

[
Kus01
]
, we can associate a

, Section 2]

\mathbb{G}
-equivariant, normal, unital completely positive map. In that case, we write

. Note that with

the counit, we have

and

.

From Theorem
6.4
and the existence of the map

, the following result is then clear:

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
(M,\alpha)
be a

-dynamical von Neumann algebra.

(1)

is an amenable action, then so is
\alpha_{P}:\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(M,P)\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}

(2)

(3)

is

-injective, then so is

-injective.

In particular, the following statements are equivalent:

(a)

\check{\mathbb{G}}
is inner amenable if and only if

6.4.

In this subsection, we investigate some situations where (inner) amenability of an action comes for free.

Proposition 6.9

Let

be a
\mathbb{G}
-dynamical von Neumann algebra.

(1)

(2)

-amenable.

\mathbb{G}

Let

be the counit and

a state extending

. Since

Then we have for

\displaystyle\alpha(E(\alpha(x)))=(\id\otimes\id\otimes\tilde{\epsilon})(\alpha\otimes\id)\alpha(x)=(\id\otimes\id\otimes\tilde{\epsilon})(\id\otimes\Delta)\alpha(x)=\alpha(x)

.

.

(2)

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}(\alpha(x))=F(E\otimes\id)(\alpha\otimes\id)\alpha(x)=F(\alpha(x))=x

(3)

7.
Equivariant injective envelopes of crossed products

In this section, we examine the question if the injective envelope construction is compatible with the crossed product functor. More specifically, in this section we prove that given a
\mathbb{G}
-operator system
(X,\alpha)
, we have the identification

I_{\check{\mathbb{G}}}^{1}(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})=I{G}^{1}(X)\rtimes\mathbb{G}

of
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-operator systems, where
\mathbb{G}
is either a compact or a discrete quantum group. We were not able to prove versions of this result for other classes of locally compact quantum groups. Even for arbitrary classical locally compact groups, this remains open.

Remark 7.1
.

The
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-operator systems
I_{\check{\mathbb{G}}}^{1}(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})
and
I{G}^{1}(X)\rtimes\mathbb{G}
are both injective as operator systems, so they become unital
C^{*}
-algebras via the Choi-Effros product. Then the identification
I_{\check{\mathbb{G}}}^{1}(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})=I{G}^{1}(X)\rtimes\mathbb{G}
becomes an identification of unital
C^{*}
-algebras.

7.1.
Discrete quantum groups

In this subsection, let
\mathbb{G}
be a discrete quantum group.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
(X,\alpha)
a
\mathbb{G}
-operator system. If
T:X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}\to X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}
is a
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-equivariant unital completely positive map such that

T(\alpha(x)(1\otimes y))=\alpha(x)(1\otimes y)

for all
x\in X
and
y\in L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}})
, then
T=\id_{X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}}
.

Proof.

Consider the completely isometric isomorphism
\Phi:X\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\to(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})\rtimes_{\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}}\check{\mathbb{G}}
from Proposition
4.4
(3). Define

S:X\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Phi}}{{\cong}}(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})\rtimes_{\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}}\check{\mathbb{G}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T\rtimes\check{\mathbb{G}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})\rtimes_{\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}}\check{\mathbb{G}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Phi^{-1}}}{{\cong}}X\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})).

Then we claim that

S(\alpha(x))=\alpha(x)
and
S(1\otimes y)=1\otimes y

for all
x\in X
and all
y\in B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))
. Indeed, fix
y\in B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))
. Then

\Phi(1\otimes y)=V_{23}^{*}(\alpha\otimes\id)(1\otimes y)V_{23}=1\otimes V^{*}(1\otimes y)V.

Since
V\in M(C(\check{\mathbb{G}})\otimes c_{0}(\mathbb{G}))
, a standard multiplicative domain argument shows that

(T\rtimes\check{\mathbb{G}})(1\otimes V^{*}(1\otimes y)V)=1\otimes V^{*}(1\otimes y)V

so it is clear that
S(1\otimes y)=1\otimes y
. Similarly, if
x\in X
, we have

\Phi(\alpha(x))=V_{23}^{*}(\alpha\otimes\id)\alpha(x)V_{23}=V_{23}^{*}(\id\otimes\Delta)\alpha(x)V_{23}=\alpha(x)\otimes 1

so that

S(\alpha(x))=\Phi^{-1}((T\rtimes\check{\mathbb{G}})(\alpha(x)\otimes 1))=\Phi^{-1}(\alpha(x)\otimes 1)=\alpha(x).

Let
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
and
\{E_{s,t}\}_{s,t\in I}\subseteq B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))
be the corresponding matrix units. Since the counit
\epsilon\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{G})
is a vector state, we may assume that there is
k\in I
such that
\epsilon=\omega_{e_{k},e_{k}}
.

Then we see that if

and

Therefore, by a multiplicative domain argument, for
x\in X
and
i,j\in I
, we find

S(x\otimes E_{i,j})=S((1\otimes E_{i,k})\alpha(x)(1\otimes E_{k,j}))=(1\otimes E_{i,k})\alpha(x)(1\otimes E_{k,j})=x\otimes E_{i,j}.

A standard multiplicative domain argument implies that
S=\id_{X\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))}
. It follows that also
T
is the identity map.
∎

.

The following statements are equivalent for
\mathbb{G}
-operator systems

\mathbb{G}

-rigid extension of

(1)\implies(2)

7.2.
Compact quantum groups

In this subsection, let
\mathbb{G}
be a compact quantum group with Haar state
\Phi
and associated GNS-map
\Lambda_{\Phi}:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
. A unitary finite-dimensional representation
\pi
of
\mathbb{G}
consists of a pair
(\mathcal{H}_{\pi},U_{\pi})
where
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
is a finite-dimensional Hilbert space and
U_{\pi}\in B(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})\otimes C(\mathbb{G})
a unitary such that
(\id\otimes\Delta)(U_{\pi})=U_{\pi,12}U_{\pi,13}
in
B(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})\otimes C(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\mathbb{G})
. Given
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
, we define the matrix coefficient

U_{\pi}(\xi,\eta):=(\omega_{\xi,\eta}\otimes\id)(U_{\pi})
and we will write
n_{\pi}:=\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
. If
\{e_{i}^{\pi}\}_{i=1}^{n_{\pi}}
is an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
, then

\Delta(U_{\pi}(\xi,\eta))=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\pi}}U_{\pi}(\xi,e_{i}^{\pi})\otimes U_{\pi}(e_{i}^{\pi},\eta).

If
\pi,\chi
are unitary finite-dimensional representations, then a linear map
T:\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\to\mathcal{H}_{\chi}
is called intertwiner if
(T\otimes 1)U_{\pi}=U_{\chi}(T\otimes 1)
and we write
\Mor(\pi,\chi)
for the set of such intertwiners. The representations
\pi,\chi
are called (unitarily) equivalent if there exists a unitary operator in
\Mor(\pi,\chi)
. A unitary finite-dimensional representation
\pi
of
\mathbb{G}
is called irreducible if
\Mor(\pi,\pi)
is one-dimensional. We will fix a maximal family
\Irr(\mathbb{G})
of pairwise non-equivalent irreducible representations.
The space linearly spanned by all matrix coefficients is denoted by
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
. In fact, the elements

\{U_{\pi}(e_{i}^{\pi},e_{j}^{\pi}):\pi\in\Irr(\mathbb{G}),1\leq i,j\leq n_{\pi}\}
form a Hamel basis for
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
. The space
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
is norm-dense in
C(\mathbb{G})
and
(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}),\Delta)
forms a Hopf
∗
-algebra. If
\pi\in\Irr(\mathbb{G})
, there exists a canonical positive invertible operator
Q_{\pi}\in B(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
such that
\Tr(Q_{\pi}^{1/2})=\Tr(Q_{\pi}^{-1/2})
. We write
\dim_{q}(\pi)
for the latter value and the following orthogonality relations hold:

\displaystyle\Phi(U_{\pi}(\xi,\eta)U_{\chi}(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})^{*})=\delta_{\pi,\chi}\frac{\langle\xi,\xi^{\prime}\rangle\langle\eta^{\prime},Q_{\pi}^{-1/2}\eta\rangle}{\dim_{q}(\pi)},

\displaystyle\Phi(U_{\pi}(\xi,\eta)^{*}U_{\chi}(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}))=\delta_{\pi,\chi}\frac{\langle\xi^{\prime},Q_{\pi}^{1/2}\xi\rangle\langle\eta,\eta^{\prime}\rangle}{\dim_{q}(\pi)}.

If
\pi\in\Irr(\mathbb{G})
, define
\chi_{\pi}:=\dim_{q}(\pi)\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\pi}}U_{\pi}(e_{i}^{\pi},Q_{\pi}^{1/2}e_{i}^{\pi})\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
. We write

c_{c}(\tilde{\mathbb{G}})=\{\Phi(-a):a\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})\}\subseteq\operatorname{Lin}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}),\mathbb{C})

which becomes a
∗
-algebra for

(\omega\chi)(x):=(\omega\odot\chi)\Delta(x),\quad\omega^{*}(x)=\overline{\omega(S(x)^{*})}

where
\omega,\chi\in c_{c}(\tilde{\mathbb{G}})
and
x\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
. We have a faithful
*
-representation

\rho_{\tilde{\mathbb{G}}}:c_{c}(\tilde{\mathbb{G}})\to\ell^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}})\subseteq B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})),\quad\rho_{\tilde{\mathbb{G}}}(\omega)\Lambda_{\Phi}(x)=\Lambda_{\Phi}((\id\odot\omega)\Delta(x))=\Lambda_{\Phi}(x_{(1)})\omega(x_{(2)}).

We use this
*
-representation to view
c_{c}(\tilde{\mathbb{G}})\subseteq\ell^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}})
.
We define the projections
p_{\pi}:=\rho_{\tilde{\mathbb{G}}}(\Phi(-\chi_{\pi}^{*}))
and we note that
\sum_{\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathbb{G})}p_{\pi}=1
with the sum converging strongly in
B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})).

Let
(X,\alpha)
be a
\mathbb{G}
-operator system. Given
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathbb{G})
, let
X_{\pi}
be the associated spectral subspace and let
\mathcal{X}:=\bigoplus_{\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathbb{G})}^{\operatorname{alg}}X_{\pi}
be the associated algebraic core
[
DH24
, Section 3]
. We define the vector space

\mathcal{X}\rtimes_{\alpha,\operatorname{alg}}\mathbb{G}:=\operatorname{span}\{\alpha(x)(1\otimes y):x\in\mathcal{X},y\in c_{c}(\tilde{\mathbb{G}})\}\subseteq X\odot B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})).

The following lemma is known, see e.g. the argument in
[
DC17
, Theorem 5.31]
in the
C^{*}
-context. We sketch the proof for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 7.7
.

Let
(M,\alpha)
be a
\mathbb{G}
-dynamical von Neumann algebra with algebraic core
\mathcal{M}
. The vector space
(1\otimes p_{\pi})(\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha,\operatorname{alg}}\mathbb{G})(1\otimes p_{\pi}^{\prime})
is
\sigma
-weakly closed in
M\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}
for all
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\Irr(\mathbb{G})
.

Proof.

As in the proof of
[
DC17
, Theorem 5.31]
, there is a finite subset
F\subseteq\Irr(\mathbb{G})
such that

(1\otimes p_{\pi})(\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha,\operatorname{alg}}\mathbb{G})(1\otimes p_{\pi^{\prime}})\subseteq\bigoplus_{\pi^{\prime\prime}\in F}M_{\pi^{\prime\prime}}\odot(p_{\pi}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))p_{\pi^{\prime}}).

Since
\bigoplus_{\pi^{\prime\prime}\in F}M_{\pi^{\prime\prime}}
is
\sigma
-weakly closed in
M
, we deduce that

\bigoplus_{\pi^{\prime\prime}\in F}M_{\pi^{\prime\prime}}\odot(p_{\pi}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))p_{\pi^{\prime}})

is
\sigma
-weakly closed in
M\bar{\otimes}p_{\pi}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))p_{\pi^{\prime}}
. So, if
z_{i}\in(1\otimes p_{\pi})(\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha,\operatorname{alg}}\mathbb{G})(1\otimes p_{\pi^{\prime}})
is a net that converges
\sigma
-weakly to
z\in M\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))
, then we deduce that

z\in\bigoplus_{\pi^{\prime\prime}\in F}M_{\pi^{\prime\prime}}\odot(p_{\pi}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))p_{\pi^{\prime}})\subseteq\mathcal{M}\odot p_{\pi}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))p_{\pi^{\prime}}.

Next, define a unitary
U\in B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))
by

U(\Lambda_{\Phi}(a)\otimes\Lambda_{\Phi}(b))=(\Lambda_{\Phi}\odot\Lambda_{\Phi})(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}(a)(1\otimes b))=\Lambda_{\Phi}(a_{(2)})\otimes\Lambda_{\Phi}(a_{(1)}b),\quad a,b\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})

and define the normal
*
-homomorphism

\beta:M\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\to M\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})):s\mapsto U_{23}^{*}\Sigma_{23}(\alpha\otimes\id)(s)\Sigma_{23}U_{23}.

By
[
DC17
, Lemma 5.30]
, we have

\{s\in\mathcal{M}\odot p_{\pi}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))p_{\pi^{\prime}}:\beta(s)=s\otimes 1\}=(1\otimes p_{\pi})(\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha,\operatorname{alg}}\mathbb{G})(1\otimes p_{\pi^{\prime}}).

Therefore, we find that

z\in(1\otimes p_{\pi})(\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha,\operatorname{alg}}\mathbb{G})(1\otimes p_{\pi^{\prime}})
.
∎

Lemma 7.8
.

Let
(X,\alpha)
be a
\mathbb{G}
-operator system and
z\in X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}
. Then for
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\Irr(\mathbb{G})
, we have

(1\otimes p_{\pi})z(1\otimes p_{\pi^{\prime}})\in\mathcal{X}\rtimes_{\alpha,\operatorname{alg}}\mathbb{G}.

Proof.

Let
(M,\beta)
be a
\mathbb{G}
-dynamical von Neumann algebra such that
(X,\alpha)\subseteq{G}(M,\beta).
Since
\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\beta,\operatorname{alg}}\mathbb{G}
is
\sigma
-weakly dense in
M\rtimes_{\beta}\mathbb{G}
, we see that
(1\otimes p_{\pi})(\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\beta,\operatorname{alg}}\mathbb{G})(1\otimes p_{\pi^{\prime}})
is
\sigma
-weakly dense in
(1\otimes p_{\pi})(M\rtimes_{\beta}\mathbb{G})(1\otimes p_{\pi^{\prime}})
. By the preceding lemma, we find

(1\otimes p_{\pi})(M\rtimes_{\beta}\mathbb{G})(1\otimes p_{\pi^{\prime}})=(1\otimes p_{\pi})(\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\beta,\operatorname{alg}}\mathbb{G})(1\otimes p_{\pi^{\prime}})\subseteq\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\beta,\operatorname{alg}}\mathbb{G}.

Thus, if
z\in X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}
, we see that

v:=(1\otimes p_{\pi})z(1\otimes p_{\pi^{\prime}})\in(\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\beta,\operatorname{alg}}\mathbb{G})\cap(X\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))).

We can therefore write

v=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta(m_{i})(1\otimes y_{i})

with
m_{i}\in\mathcal{M}
and
y_{i}\in c_{c}(\tilde{\mathbb{G}})
where
\{y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are linearly independent. We can further write

\beta(m_{i})=\sum_{\pi\in F}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n_{\pi}}(m_{i})_{jk}^{\pi}\otimes U_{\pi}(e_{j}^{\pi},e_{k}^{\pi})

for a finite subset
F\subseteq\operatorname{Irr}(\mathbb{G})
(not depending on
i
)
and then we have

v=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\pi\in F}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n_{\pi}}(m_{i})_{jk}^{\pi}\otimes U_{\pi}(e_{j}^{\pi},e_{k}^{\pi})y_{i}.

It is well-known that the multiplication map

\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})\odot c_{c}(\tilde{\mathbb{G}})\to B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))

is injective, so we conclude that
(m_{i})_{jk}^{\pi}\in X
for all
i=1,\dots,n
,
\pi\in F
and all
j,k=1,\dots,n_{\pi}
(by the Hahn-Banach theorem and the definition of the Fubini tensor product). Therefore,

m_{i}=(\id\odot\epsilon)\beta(m_{i})\in X
, so that
v\in\mathcal{X}\rtimes_{\alpha,\operatorname{alg}}\mathbb{G}.

∎

Lemma 7.9
.

Let
(X,\alpha)
a
\mathbb{G}
-operator system. If
T:X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}\to X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}
is a
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-equivariant unital completely positive map such that

T(\alpha(x)(1\otimes y))=\alpha(x)(1\otimes y)

for all
x\in X
and

, then

.

Proof.

Let
z\in X\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}
. If
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\Irr(\mathbb{G})
, the element
(1\otimes p_{\pi})z(1\otimes p_{\pi^{\prime}})\in\mathcal{X}\rtimes_{\alg}\mathbb{G}
, so we have by the assumption and a multiplicative domain argument that

(1\otimes p_{\pi})z(1\otimes p_{\pi^{\prime}})=T((1\otimes p_{\pi})z(1\otimes p_{\pi^{\prime}}))=(1\otimes p_{\pi})T(z)(1\otimes p_{\pi^{\prime}}).

Since the partial sums
\sum_{\pi\in F}p_{\pi}
where
F
ranges over the finite subsets of
\operatorname{Irr}(\mathbb{G})
converge strongly to the identity on
B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))

T(z)=z
.
∎

8.
Equivariant injectivity and inner amenability pass to closed quantum subgroups

Given a locally compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
, we have
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})=L^{2}(\check{\mathbb{G}})
and thus Tomita-Takesaki theory gives us a canonical anti-unitary modular operator

\check{J}=\check{J}{G}:L^{2}(\mathbb{G})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{G}).

Assume now that
\mathbb{H}
is a
Vaes-closed quantum subgroup
of
\mathbb{G}

[
DKSS12
]
, i.e.
\mathbb{H}
is a locally compact quantum group and there exists a unital, normal, faithful
*
-homomorphism
\check{\gamma}:L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{H}})\to L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}})
such that
(\check{\gamma}\otimes\check{\gamma})\circ\check{\Delta}^{\mathbb{H}}=\check{\Delta}^{\mathbb{G}}\circ\check{\gamma}.
We also define the faithful normal
*
-homomorphism

\hat{\gamma}:L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{H}})\to L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}):x\mapsto\check{J}{G}\check{\gamma}(\check{J}_{\mathbb{H}}x^{*}\check{J}_{\mathbb{H}})^{*}\check{J}{G}

satisfying
(\hat{\gamma}\otimes\hat{\gamma})\circ\hat{\Delta}^{\mathbb{H}}=\hat{\Delta}{G}\circ\hat{\gamma}.
Define the unitaries

V_{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}}:=(\check{\gamma}\otimes\id)(V{H})\in L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}),\quad W_{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}}:=(\id\otimes\hat{\gamma})(W_{\mathbb{H}})\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})

and the normal
*
-homomorphisms

\displaystyle\Delta_{r}^{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}}:B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\to B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}):x\mapsto V_{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}}(x\otimes 1)V_{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}}^{*}

\displaystyle\Delta_{l}^{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}}:B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})):x\mapsto W_{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}}^{*}(1\otimes x)W_{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}}.

The map
\Delta_{r}^{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}}
defines an action
B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\curvearrowleft\mathbb{H}
and the map
\Delta_{l}^{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}}
defines an action
\mathbb{H}\curvearrowright B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))
. These maps restrict to coactions

\Delta^{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}}:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}),\quad\Delta^{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}}:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}).

In what follows, we will make use of the following list of well-known identities:

\displaystyle(\Delta_{r}{G}\otimes\id)\circ\Delta_{r}^{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle=(\id\otimes\Delta_{r}^{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}})\circ\Delta_{r}{G},

(8.1)

\displaystyle(\id\otimes\Delta_{l}{G})\circ\Delta_{l}^{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}}

\displaystyle=(\Delta_{l}^{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}}\otimes\id)\circ\Delta_{l}{G},

(8.2)

\displaystyle(\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}})\circ\Delta{G}

\displaystyle=(\Delta^{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}}\otimes\id)\circ\Delta{G}.

(8.3)

If
(M,\alpha)
is a
\mathbb{G}
-dynamical von Neumann algebra, there exists a unique coaction
\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}:M\to M\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})
such that

(\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}})\circ\alpha=(\alpha\otimes\id)\circ\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}.

(8.4)

In this way, we obtain the
\mathbb{H}
-dynamical von Neumann algebra
(M,\alpha_{\mathbb{H}})
. We call
\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}
the
restriction of the action

\alpha
to the quantum subgroup
\mathbb{H}
. We also have the identity

(\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}})\circ\alpha=(\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}\otimes\id)\circ\alpha

(8.5)

since we have (making use of (
8.4
) and (
8.3
))

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\id\otimes\id)(\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}\otimes\id)\alpha

\displaystyle=(\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}}\otimes\id)(\alpha\otimes\id)\alpha

\displaystyle=(\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}}\otimes\id)(\id\otimes\Delta{G})\alpha

\displaystyle=(\id\otimes\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}})(\id\otimes\Delta{G})\alpha

\displaystyle=(\id\otimes\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}})(\alpha\otimes\id)\alpha=(\alpha\otimes\id\otimes\id)(\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}})\alpha.

We will need the following non-trivial result:

Proposition 8.1
.

There is a unique isometric normal
*
-homomorphism

\iota:M\rtimes_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}}\mathbb{H}\to M\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}

such that

\iota(1\otimes y)=1\otimes\check{\gamma}(y),\quad\iota(\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}(m))=\alpha(m),\qquad m\in M,y\in L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{H}}).

•

Consider the
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-action

\beta:=(\id\otimes\id\otimes\check{\gamma})\circ(\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}^{\mathbb{H}}):M\rtimes_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}}\mathbb{H}\to(M\rtimes_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}}\mathbb{H})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}}).

\iota:(M\rtimes_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}}\mathbb{H},\beta)\to(M\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G},\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}^{\mathbb{G}})

is
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-equivariant.

•

Consider the
\mathbb{H}
-action

\id\otimes\Delta_{r}^{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}}:M\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}\to(M\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}).

Then
\iota:(M\rtimes_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}}\mathbb{H},\id\otimes\Delta_{r}^{\mathbb{H}})\to(M\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G},\id\otimes\Delta_{r}^{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}})
is
\mathbb{H}
-equivariant.

Proof.

Assume first that
M
is represented on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
in standard position. Let
U_{\alpha}\in B(\mathcal{H})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
be the unitary implementation of
\alpha
and let
U_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}}
be the unitary implementation of
\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}
. In
[
DC09
, Section 6.5]
(see in particular equation
(6.2)
in
[
DC09
]
; note however that we need to make the switch from left to right coactions), it is shown that

V_{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H},23}U_{\alpha,12}V_{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H},23}^{*}=(\id\otimes\Delta_{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}})(U_{\alpha})=U_{\alpha,12}U_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}},13}

or equivalently

U_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}},13}V_{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H},23}=U_{\alpha,12}^{*}V_{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H},23}U_{\alpha,12}.

Note that by Lemma
2.5
, we know that

U_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}}^{*}(M\rtimes_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}}\mathbb{H})U_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{H}})

so it makes sense to define

\iota:M\rtimes_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}}\mathbb{H}\to B(\mathcal{H}\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{G})):z\mapsto U_{\alpha}(\id\otimes\check{\gamma})(U_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}}^{*}zU_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}})U_{\alpha}^{*}.

Note that

V_{\mathbb{H},23}U_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}},12}V_{\mathbb{H},23}^{*}=(\id\otimes\Delta_{\mathbb{H}})(U_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}})=U_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}},12}U_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}},13}

so that

U_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}},12}^{*}V_{\mathbb{H},23}U_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}},12}=U_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}},13}V_{\mathbb{H},23}.

Therefore, if
\omega\in B(L^{2}(\mathbb{H}))_{*}
, we have

\displaystyle(\id\otimes\check{\gamma})(U_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}}^{*}(1\otimes(\id\otimes\omega)(V_{\mathbb{H}}))U_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}})

\displaystyle=(\id\otimes\check{\gamma})(\id\otimes\id\otimes\omega)(U_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}},12}^{*}V_{\mathbb{H},23}U_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}},12})

\displaystyle=(\id\otimes\check{\gamma})(\id\otimes\id\otimes\omega)(U_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}},13}V_{\mathbb{H},23})

\displaystyle=(\id\otimes\id\otimes\omega)(U_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}},13}V_{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H},23})

\displaystyle=(\id\otimes\id\otimes\omega)(U_{\alpha,12}^{*}V_{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H},23}U_{\alpha,12})

\displaystyle=U_{\alpha}^{*}(1\otimes(\id\otimes\omega)(V_{\mathbb{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}}}))U_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=U_{\alpha}^{*}(1\otimes\check{\gamma}((\id\otimes\omega)(V_{\mathbb{H}})))U_{\alpha}

so that

(\id\otimes\check{\gamma})(U_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}}^{*}(1\otimes x))U_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}})=U_{\alpha}^{*}(1\otimes\check{\gamma}(x))U_{\alpha},\quad x\in L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{H}}).

Hence, we find for
x\in L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{H}})
that

\displaystyle\iota(1\otimes x)=U_{\alpha}(\id\otimes\check{\gamma})(U_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}}^{*}(1\otimes x)U_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}})U_{\alpha}^{*}=U_{\alpha}U_{\alpha}^{*}(1\otimes\check{\gamma}(x))U_{\alpha}U_{\alpha}^{*}=1\otimes\check{\gamma}(x)

and if
x\in M
, we have

\iota(\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}(x))=U_{\alpha}(x\otimes 1)U_{\alpha}^{*}=\alpha(x)

and thus a map
\iota:M\rtimes_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}}\mathbb{H}\to M\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}
as in the proposition exists.

In the general case, choose a
*
-isomorphism
\pi:M\to N
where
N
is standardly represented on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Define the coaction

\beta:=(\pi\otimes\id)\circ\alpha\circ\pi^{-1}:N\to N\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}).

Then note that also

\beta_{\mathbb{H}}=(\pi\otimes\id)\circ\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}\circ\pi^{-1}:N\to N\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})

since we have

\displaystyle(\beta\otimes\id)\circ\beta_{\mathbb{H}}=(\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}})\circ\beta

\displaystyle=(\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}})\circ(\pi\otimes\id)\circ\alpha\circ\pi^{-1}

\displaystyle=(\pi\otimes\id\otimes\id)\circ(\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}})\circ\alpha\circ\pi^{-1}

\displaystyle=(\pi\otimes\id\otimes\id)\circ(\alpha\otimes\id)\circ\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}\circ\pi^{-1}

\displaystyle=(\beta\otimes\id)\circ(\pi\otimes\id)\circ\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}\circ\pi^{-1}.

Therefore, the composition

M\rtimes_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}}\mathbb{H}\cong N\rtimes_{\beta_{\mathbb{H}}}\mathbb{H}\hookrightarrow N\rtimes_{\beta}\mathbb{G}\cong M\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}

is the desired map. Checking the
\check{\mathbb{G}}
and
\mathbb{H}
-equivariance of
\iota
is straightforwardly verified on the canonical generators of the crossed product.
∎

Given a
\mathbb{H}
-dynamical von Neumann algebra
(N,\beta)
, we define the cotensor product

N\overset{\mathbb{H}}{\square}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}):=\{z\in N\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}):(\beta\otimes\id)(z)=(\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}})(z)\}.

By a routine argument involving slice maps and making use of equation (
8.2
), we see that

(\id\otimes\Delta{G})(N\overset{\mathbb{H}}{\square}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}))\subseteq(N\overset{\mathbb{H}}{\square}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}))\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})

so that
N\overset{\mathbb{H}}{\square}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
becomes a
\mathbb{G}
-dynamical von Neumann algebra. In particular, starting from a
\mathbb{G}
-dynamical von Neumann algebra
(M,\alpha)
, we have the associated
\mathbb{H}
-dynamical von Neumann algebra
(M,\alpha_{\mathbb{H}})
and we can form the
\mathbb{G}
-dynamical von Neumann algebra

M\overset{\mathbb{H}}{\square}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})=\{z\in M\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}):(\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}\otimes\id)(z)=(\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}})(z)\}.

If
m\in M
, equation (
8.5
) shows that
\alpha(m)\in M\overset{\mathbb{H}}{\square}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
and therefore the map

\alpha:M\to M\overset{\mathbb{H}}{\square}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
is a
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant isometric
*
-homomorphism.

Note that

\mathbb{C}\overset{\mathbb{H}}{\square}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\cong\{x\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}):\Delta^{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}}(x)=1\otimes x\}=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G})
.
In view of this, the following result and its proof are a generalisation of
[
Cr17b
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
(M,\alpha)
be a
\mathbb{G}
-dynamial von Neumann algebra. The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

(M,\alpha)
is
\mathbb{G}
-injective.

(2)

(M,\alpha_{\mathbb{H}})
is
\mathbb{H}
-injective and there exists a
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant unital completely positive map
P:M\overset{\mathbb{H}}{\square}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\to M
such that
P\circ\alpha=\id_{M}
.

Proof.

(1)\implies(2)
Consider the
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-action

\beta=(\id\otimes\id\otimes\check{\gamma})\circ(\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}^{\mathbb{H}}):M\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{H}))\to(M\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{H})))\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}}).

By
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-injectivity of
M\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}
and
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-equivariance of the canonical map
\iota
, there is a
\check{\mathbb{G}}
-equivariant unital completely positive map

E:(M\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{H})),\beta)\to(M\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G},\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}{G}).

extending
\iota
. Then, we have for
z\in M\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{H}))
that

\displaystyle(E\otimes\id)(\id\otimes\Delta_{r}^{\mathbb{H}})(z)

\displaystyle=(E\otimes\id)(V_{\mathbb{H},23}(z\otimes 1)V_{\mathbb{H},23}^{*})=V_{\mathbb{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}},23}(E(z)\otimes 1)V_{\mathbb{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}},23}^{*}=(\id\otimes\Delta_{r}^{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}})E(z)

where we used that
V_{\mathbb{H},23}
is in the multiplicative domain of
E\otimes\id
and that
E(1\otimes y)=1\otimes\check{\gamma}(y)
for
y\in L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{H}})
. Further, if
z\in M\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})
, we find

\displaystyle(\id\otimes\check{\Delta}_{r}^{\mathbb{G}})E(z)

\displaystyle=(E\otimes\id)\beta(z)=(E\otimes\id)(z\otimes 1)=E(z)\otimes 1

where we used that
\check{V}_{\mathbb{H}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})^{\prime}\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{H}})
. Therefore,
E(z)\in\alpha(M)
. Thus, it makes sense to define

F:=\alpha^{-1}\circ E:M\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})\to M.

Note that if
a\in M
, we have

F(\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}(a))=\alpha^{-1}(\iota(\alpha{H}(a)))=\alpha^{-1}(\alpha(a))=a.

Further, we check that
F
is
\mathbb{H}
-equivariant:

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\id)(F\otimes\id)(\id\otimes\Delta_{\mathbb{H}})

\displaystyle=(E\otimes\id)(\id\otimes\Delta{H})=(\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}})\circ E=(\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}})\circ\alpha\circ F=(\alpha\otimes\id)\circ\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}\circ F

so by injectivity of
\alpha\otimes\id
we conclude that

(F\otimes\id)\circ(\id\otimes\Delta_{\mathbb{H}})=\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}\circ F.

Thus,
F
is a
\mathbb{H}
-equivariant completely positive map satisfying
F\circ\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}=\id_{M}
, and thus
\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}:M\curvearrowleft\mathbb{H}
is an amenable action. We conclude that
(M,\alpha{H})
is
\mathbb{H}
-injective.

(2)\implies(1)
Let
E_{\mathbb{H}}:(M\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}),\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{H}})\to(M,\alpha_{\mathbb{H}})
be a unital completely positive map such that
E_{\mathbb{H}}\circ\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}=\id_{M}
. Given
z\in M\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
, we have

\displaystyle(\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}\otimes\id)(E_{\mathbb{H}}\otimes\id)(\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}})(z)

\displaystyle=(E_{\mathbb{H}}\otimes\id\otimes\id)(\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{H}}\otimes\id)(\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}})(z)

\displaystyle=(E_{\mathbb{H}}\otimes\id\otimes\id)(\id\otimes\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}})(\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}})(z)

\displaystyle=(\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}})(E_{\mathbb{H}}\otimes\id)(\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}})(z).

Therefore, we can define the map

Q:M\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\to M\overset{\mathbb{H}}{\square}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}):z\mapsto(E_{\mathbb{H}}\otimes\id)(\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}})(z)

which is
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant since by (
8.2
) we have

\displaystyle(Q\otimes\id)(\id\otimes\Delta{G})(z)

\displaystyle=(E_{\mathbb{H}}\otimes\id\otimes\id)(\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}}\otimes\id)(\id\otimes\Delta{G})(z)

\displaystyle=(E_{\mathbb{H}}\otimes\id\otimes\id)(\id\otimes\id\otimes\Delta{G})(\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}})(z)=(\id\otimes\Delta{G})Q(z).

We define the
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant map

E{G}:=P\circ Q:M\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\to M.

If
m\in M
, we have

E{G}(\alpha(m))=P(Q(\alpha(m)))=P(E_{\mathbb{H}}\otimes\id)(\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}\otimes\id)\alpha(m)=P(\alpha(m))=m

so the map
E{G}
witnesses the amenability of the action
\alpha:M\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}
.
∎

Remark 8.3
.

It is in general not clear that if
(M,\alpha)
is
\mathbb{G}
-amenable and
\mathbb{H}
is a closed quantum subgroup of
\mathbb{G}
, then
(M,\alpha_{\mathbb{H}})
is
\mathbb{H}
-amenable. However, Theorem
8.2
shows that this is true if the von Neumann algebra
M
is injective. For classical locally compact groups, it was established in
[
AD82
, Proposition 2.6]
that amenability of actions passes to closed subgroups, but under an additional countability assumption on the locally compact group. In the case that the von Neumann algebra involved is injective, we can therefore dispose of these countability assumptions.

The situation for inner amenability of actions is much clearer, as the following proposition shows.

Proposition 8.4
.

Let
(M,\alpha)
be a
\mathbb{G}
-dynamical von Neumann algebra and
\mathbb{H}
a Vaes-closed quantum subgroup of
\mathbb{G}
. If
(M,\alpha)
is
\mathbb{G}
-inner amenable, then
(M,\alpha_{\mathbb{H}})
is inner
\mathbb{H}
-amenable.

Proof.

Choose a
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant unital completely positive map

E{G}:(M\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G},\id\otimes\Delta_{r}{G})\to(M,\alpha),\quad E_{\mathbb{G}}\circ\alpha=\id_{M}.

Define then the unital completely positive map

E_{\mathbb{H}}:=E{G}\circ\iota:M\rtimes_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}}\mathbb{H}\to M.

If
z\in M\rtimes_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}}\mathbb{H}
, we find (using equations (
8.1
), (
8.4
) and
\mathbb{H}
-equivariance of
\iota
) that

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\id)\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}(E_{\mathbb{H}}(z))

\displaystyle=(\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}})\alpha(E{G}(\iota(z)))

\displaystyle=(\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}})(E{G}\otimes\id)(\id\otimes\Delta_{r}{G})\iota(z)

\displaystyle=(E{G}\otimes\id\otimes\id)(\id\otimes\id\otimes\Delta^{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}})(\id\otimes\Delta_{r}^{\mathbb{G}})\iota(z)

\displaystyle=(E{G}\otimes\id\otimes\id)(\id\otimes\Delta_{r}{G}\otimes\id)(\id\otimes\Delta_{r}^{\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}})\iota(z)

\displaystyle=(E{G}\otimes\id\otimes\id)(\id\otimes\Delta_{r}{G}\otimes\id)(\iota\otimes\id)(\id\otimes\Delta_{r}^{\mathbb{H}})(z)

\displaystyle=(\alpha\otimes\id)(E_{\mathbb{H}}\otimes\id)(\id\otimes\Delta_{r}^{\mathbb{H}})(z)

so
E_{\mathbb{H}}:(M\rtimes_{\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}}\mathbb{H},\id\otimes\Delta_{r}^{\mathbb{H}})\to(M,\alpha_{\mathbb{H}})
is
\mathbb{H}
-equivariant. Trivially,
E_{\mathbb{H}}\circ\alpha_{\mathbb{H}}=\id_{M}
so
(M,\alpha_{\mathbb{H}})
is inner
\mathbb{H}
-amenable.∎

Remark 8.5
.

If we apply Proposition
8.4
to the trivial action
\mathbb{C}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}
, we recover the fact that inner amenability of a locally compact quantum group passes to Vaes-closed quantum subgroups
[
Cr19
, Proposition 3.10]
.

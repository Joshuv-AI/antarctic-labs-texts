Limiting distribution of dense orbits in a moduli space of rank m m discrete subgroups in ( m + 1 ) (m+1) -space
Michael Bersudsky, Hao Xing

Abstract
We study the limiting distribution of dense orbits of a lattice subgroup Γ ≤ SL ​ ( m + 1 , ℝ ) \Gamma\leq\text{SL}(m+1,\mathbb{R}) acting on H \ SL ​ ( m + 1 , ℝ ) H\backslash\text{SL}(m+1,\mathbb{R}) , with respect to a filtration of growing norm balls. The novelty of our work is that the groups H H we consider have infinitely many non-trivial connected components. For a specific such H H , the homogeneous space H \ G H\backslash G identifies with X m , m + 1 X_{m,m+1} , a moduli space of rank m m -discrete subgroups in ℝ m + 1 \mathbb{R}^{m+1} . This study is motivated by the work of Shapira-Sargent who studied random walks on X 2 , 3 X_{2,3} .



1
Introduction

A well studied topic in homogeneous dynamics concerns the limiting distributions of
dense
orbits of a lattice
\Gamma\leq G
acting on a homogeneous space
H\backslash G
.
An notable basic result in this line of research is the work of Ledrappier
[
Led99
]
which gives the limiting distribution of the
\Gamma\leq\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-dense orbits in
\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
\mathbb{R}&1\end{bmatrix}\backslash\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus\textbf{0}
. An important aspect of
[
Led99
]
is the method of reducing the question on the limiting distribution of
\Gamma
orbits in
H\backslash G
to a question on
H
orbits in
G/\Gamma
. This kind of idea, which we refer to as the duality principle, has appeared extensively in the literature, and we refer the reader to
[
GN14
]
for a detailed exposition. In this paper, we continue the above line of research, and the main novelty of our work is that the subgroup
H\leq\text{SL}(m+1,\mathbb{R})
we consider has
infinitely
many non-trivial connected components. In this regard, we note that general results for a connected
H
were obtained in
[
GW04
,
GN14
]
, and results for
H
being a lattice were given in
[
Oh05
,
GW04
]
. Our method will also exploit the duality principle through a general theorem of
[
GW04
]
.

We now proceed to describe our results, and then we will explain our motivation to consider this particular setting. In what follows,
G:=\text{SL}(m+1,\mathbb{R})
,
\Gamma\leq G
is a lattice, and

H:=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}t^{-\frac{1}{m}}q&0\\
v&t\end{bmatrix}:t>0,q\in\Delta,v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\right\},

(1.1)

where
\Delta\leq\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})
is a lattice. In this setting, we have the following observation:

Proposition 1.1
.

For each
x_{0}\in H\backslash G
, the orbit
x_{0}\Gamma
is dense.

This statement follows from our Theorem
1.2
, but we briefly sketch a simple proof – A
\Gamma
-orbit passing through
Hg
is dense in
H\backslash G
if and only if the “dual”
H
-orbit passing through
g\Gamma
is dense in
G/\Gamma
. It’s well known that each orbit of the connected component of
H
is dense, see e.g. Proposition 1.5 of
[
SR80
]
.

We will be considering the filtration of the dense orbits given by the Hilbert-Schmidt norm:

\|g\|=\sqrt{\text{Trace}(^{t}gg)}=\sqrt{\sum_{ij}g_{ij}^{2}},~g\in G.

Namely, we denote

\Gamma_{T}:=\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\|\gamma\|\leq T\},

(1.2)

and we consider the following probability measures on
H\backslash G
:

\mu_{T,x_{0}}:=\frac{1}{\#\Gamma_{T}}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{T}}\delta_{x_{0}.\gamma},~T>0,x_{0}\in H\backslash G.

We will prove that the above measures converge as
T\to\infty
to the explicit measure
\tilde{\nu}_{x_{0}}
which we describe now.
Consider the group
P
containing
H
, which is given by

P:=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}t^{-\frac{1}{m}}\eta&0\\
v&t\end{bmatrix}:t>0,\eta\in\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R}),v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\right\}.

The space
H\backslash G
is naturally a fiber-bundle over
P\backslash G
with respect to the natural map sending

\pi(Hg):=Pg,

and the fibers are isomorphic to
\Delta\backslash\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})
. We note that the base space
P\backslash G
is identified with
\mathbb{S}^{m}
the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
via the orbit map of the right action
w.g:=\frac{w(^{t}g^{-1})}{\|w(^{t}g^{-1})\|},~g\in G,~w\in\mathbb{S}^{m}
. Thus we may view
H\backslash G
as a fiber bundle over the sphere. Notice that
\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R})
acts transitively on
P\backslash G
(with respect to the natural action), and we denote by
\mu_{P.\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R})}
the right
\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R})
-invariant probability on
P\backslash G=P.\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R})
. Now we define measures on each fiber

\pi^{-1}(P\rho)=HP\rho,~\rho\in\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R}).

Using the Iwasawa decomposition, we consider

x_{0}=:H\begin{bmatrix}\text{g}_{0}&0\\
\textbf{v}_{0}&\nicefrac{{1}}{{\det(\text{g}_{0})}}\end{bmatrix}\rho_{0},

where
|\det(\text{g}_{0})|=1
and
\rho_{0}\in\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R})
, and we define the function

\Phi_{x_{0}}\left(H\begin{bmatrix}\eta&0\\
v&1\end{bmatrix}\rho\right)=\sum_{q\in\Delta}\frac{1}{\|\text{g}_{0}^{-1}q\eta\|^{m^{2}}}.

(1.3)

Here
\rho\in\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R})
and
\eta\in\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})
. The expression on the right is well-defined as the Hilbert-Schmidt norm is bi-
\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R})
invariant. The infinite sum is convergent due to Lemma
2.7
below. We naturally identify the “standard” fiber
\pi^{-1}(P)=H\backslash P
with
\Delta\backslash\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})
by mapping
\Delta\eta\mapsto H\begin{bmatrix}\eta&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}
, which allows to define the
\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})
-invariant measure
\mu_{H.\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})}
on
H\backslash P
with the choice of normalization such that the measure

\nu_{x_{0},\pi^{-1}(P)}(f):=\int_{\pi^{-1}(P)}f(y)\Phi_{x_{0}}(y)d\mu_{H.\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})}(y),~f\in C_{c}(H\backslash G)

(1.4)

is a probability measure. On any other fiber
\pi^{-1}(P\rho),~\rho\in\text{SO}(m+1,R)
, we define the pushed measure

\nu_{x_{0},\pi^{-1}(P\rho)}:=\rho_{*}\nu_{x_{0},\pi^{-1}(P)},

where the pushforward is via the right translation by
\rho.
Having defined the above measures on the base space and the fibers, we can now define our probability measure on
H\backslash G
to be

\tilde{\nu}_{x_{0}}(f)=\int_{P.\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R})}\nu_{x_{0},\pi^{-1}(b)}(f)d\mu_{P.\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R})}(b),~f\in C_{c}(H\backslash G).

(1.5)

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Gamma\leq G
be a lattice and fix
{x_{0}}\in H\backslash G
. Then,
\mu_{T,{x_{0}}}
converges in the weak-* topology to
\tilde{\nu}_{x_{0}}
as
T\to\infty
.

1.1
Main motivating example - unimodular
m
-lattices in
(m+1)
-space

The main motivation behind this project was to study the limiting distribution of orbits of a lattice
\Gamma\leq G=\text{SL}(m+1,\mathbb{R})
acting on a moduli space of discrete groups. Such a research direction was originally suggested by U. Shapira as natural continuation of the work
[
SS17
]
which considers random walks on the space of rank 2 discrete subgroups in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
(see also the more recent work in this direction
[
GLS22
]
which generalizes
[
SS17
]
). We now describe our result in this setting, which is a particular case of Theorem
1.2
with
H
as in (
1.1
) such that
\Delta=\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{Z})
.

In what follows,
m
is a natural number strictly larger than 1.
We say that
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
is an
m
-lattice if
\Lambda
is the
\mathbb{Z}
-Span of a tuple of linearly independent vectors
{v_{1},v_{2},...,v_{m}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
, that is,

\Lambda:=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\{v_{1},v_{2},...,v_{m}\}.

We define the covolume function:

\text{Cov}(\Lambda):=\sqrt{\det(\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle)},

which is the area of a fundamental parallelogram of
\Lambda
.
We call an
m
-lattice
\Lambda

unimodular
if
\text{Cov}(\Lambda)=1
, and we consider the moduli space of unimodular
m
-lattices in
(m+1)
-space with a marked orthogonal vector:

X_{m,m+1}:=\{(\Lambda,w):\text{Cov}(\Lambda)=1,w\in\mathbb{S}^{m},w\perp\Lambda\}.

Remark 1.3
.

Compared to the space of (non-marked) unimodular
m
-lattices

L_{m,m+1}:=\{\Lambda:\text{Cov}(\Lambda)=1\},

the space
X_{m,m+1}
adds only slightly more information – for each
\Lambda
there are only two choices of an orthogonal vector
w\in\mathbb{S}^{m}
. All the results below imply directly the analogous results of
L_{m,m+1}
.

We define a right
\text{SL}(m+1,\mathbb{R})
action on
X_{m,m+1}
by

(\Lambda,w).g:=\left(\frac{\Lambda g}{\sqrt{\text{Cov}(\Lambda g)}},\frac{w(^{t}g^{-1})}{\|w(^{t}g^{-1})\|}\right),~g\in\text{SL}(m+1,\mathbb{R}),~w\in\mathbb{S}^{m},

(1.6)

where
\Lambda g
and
w(^{t}g^{-1})
are defined using the usual matrix multiplication, and where
\|\cdot\|
is the usual Euclidean norm. This action is transitive, and the stabilizer subgroup of the base point
\left(\text{Span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{m}\},e_{m+1}\right)
is
H
as in (
1.1
) with
\Delta=\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{Z})
. This gives a natural identification
X_{m,m+1}\cong H\backslash G
and the corresponding action of
\Gamma
on
H\backslash G
is the natural one.
For

x_{0}:=(\Lambda_{0},w_{0})\in X_{m,m+1},

consider the probability measures

\mu_{T,x_{0}}:=\frac{1}{\#\Gamma_{T}}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{T}}\delta_{x_{0}.\gamma},~T>0,

where
\Gamma_{T}
is as in (
1.2
).

Remark 1.4
.

When
m=1
the space
X_{m,m+1}
is identified with
\mathbb{S}^{1}
the unit circle in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The limiting distribution of
\mu_{T,x_{0}}
in the case of
m=1
was obtained in
[
Gor03
]
,

Having the identification
X_{m,m+1}\cong H\backslash G
, we obtain via Theorem
1.2
that the limiting measure of the measures
\mu_{T,x_{0}}
as
T\to\infty
is
\tilde{\nu}_{x_{0}}
defined in (
1.5
). In the following we interpret this limiting measure in terms of the
m
-lattices. The projection to the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{m}
defined by

\pi_{\perp}(\Lambda,w):=w,

endows
X_{m,m+1}
with a fiber-bundle structure over
\mathbb{S}^{m}
, where the fibers are isomorphic to

X_{m}:=\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R}).

The fiber
\pi_{\perp}^{-1}(w)
may be thought of as the collection of unimodular
m
-lattices orthogonal to
w\in\mathbb{S}^{m}
.
We now give an interpretation for the measures on
\nu_{x_{0},w}
on those fibers. We first consider the function
\Phi_{x_{0}}
which was defined in (
1.3
). For an operator T from a hyperplane
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
to another hyperplane
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
, we define

\|\text{T}\|^{2}_{\text{HS}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|\text{T}u_{i}\|^{2},

where
\{u_{1},u_{2},...,u_{m}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
U
, and where the norm on the right hand side is the usual Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
. We note that this norm is independent of the choice of an orthonormal basis
\{u_{1},u_{2},...,u_{m}\}
.
For an ordered tuple
B=(u_{1},u_{2},...,u_{m})
of linearly independent vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
we define the linear map
\text{T}_{B}:\text{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_{1},e_{2},...,e_{m}\}\to\text{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{u_{1},u_{2},...,u_{m}\}
, by sending
e_{1}\mapsto u_{1},...,e_{m}\mapsto u_{m}
. Now fix unimodular
m
-lattice
\Lambda_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
, and let
\mathscr{B}_{0}
be an ordered tuple of linearly independent vectors forming a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis for
\Lambda_{0}
. Then for
x_{0}=(\Lambda_{0},w_{0})
the function
\Phi_{x_{0}}
in (
1.3
) becomes

\Phi_{x_{0}}(\Lambda,w):=\sum_{\text{Span}_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathscr{B}}=\Lambda}\frac{1}{\|\text{T}_{\mathscr{B}}\circ\text{T}_{\mathscr{B}_{0}}^{-1}\|_{\text{HS}}^{m^{2}}}.

By identifying
\pi_{\perp}^{-1}(e_{m+1})
with
\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})
, we obtain the
\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})
-invariant measure
\mu_{e_{m+1}}
supported on
\pi_{\perp}^{-1}(e_{m+1})
scaled such that the measure
\nu_{x_{0},e_{m+1}}
defined by

\nu_{x_{0},e_{m+1}}(f):=\int_{\pi^{-1}_{\perp}(e_{m+1})}f(\Lambda,e_{m+1})\Phi_{x_{0}}(\Lambda,e_{m+1})d\mu_{e_{m+1}}(\Lambda),~f\in C_{c}(X_{m,m+1}),

is a probability measure. Next, for any other
w\in\mathbb{S}^{m}
, we choose
\rho_{w}\in\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R})
such that
w=e_{m+1}\rho_{w}
, and we define,

\nu_{x_{0},w}:=(\rho_{w})_{*}\nu_{x_{0},e_{m+1}},

which is the push-forward of the right translation by
\rho_{w}
via the right action of
\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R})
on
X_{m,m+1}
defined in (
1.6
). Note that
\nu_{x_{0},w}
is independent of the choice of
\rho_{w}
. Finally, we define
\tilde{\nu}_{x_{0}}
by

\tilde{\nu}_{x_{0}}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}\nu_{x_{0},w}(f)d\mu_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}(w).

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\Gamma\leq\text{SL}(m+1,\mathbb{R})
be a lattice and fix
{x_{0}}\in X_{m,m+1}
. Then,
\mu_{T,{x_{0}}}
converges in the weak-* topology to
\tilde{\nu}_{x_{0}}
as
T\to\infty
. In other words,
for all
f\in C_{c}(X_{m,m+1})
, we have

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\#\Gamma_{T}}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{T}}f(x_{0}.\gamma)=\int_{X_{m,m+1}}f(x)d\tilde{\nu}_{x_{0}}(x).

An example for
\Gamma=\text{SL}(m+1,\mathbb{Z})
.
We would like to point out a classical result in number theory that is related to the orbit
x_{0}.\Gamma
where
\Gamma=\text{SL}(m+1,\mathbb{Z})
and
x_{0}=(\text{Span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\{e_{1},...,e_{m}\},e_{m+1})\in X_{m,m+1}
.
The collection of
m
-lattices

\mathbb{Z}_{m,m+1}:=\left\{\text{Span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\{e_{1},...,e_{m}\}\gamma:\gamma\in\text{SL}(m+1,\mathbb{Z})\right\},T>0

consists of all primitive (maximal) rank
m
-discrete subgroups of
\mathbb{Z}^{m+1}
. Notice that here the
m
-lattices
\text{Span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\{e_{1},...,e_{m}\}\gamma
are without the covolume normalization, and their covolume can be arbitrarily large. It’s natural to consider the equivalence relation saying that
m
-lattices are equivalent if they differ by a rotation or by a dilation. If
\Lambda\leq\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
is an
m
-lattice, then it’s equivelence class
[\Lambda]
can be naturally represented by a point in
\text{PGL}(m,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\text{PGL}(m,\mathbb{R})/\text{PO}(m,\mathbb{R})
, where
PGL
denotes the projective linear group and where
PO
denotes the projective orthogonal group. A particular consequence of a classical result of Schmidt
[
Sch98
]
, who generalized the works of Roelcke
[
Roe56
]
and Maass
[
Maa59
]
, states that the uniform probability measures on

\left\{[\Lambda]:\Lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{m,m+1}\text{ and Cov}(\Lambda)\leq T\right\}

converge to the natural measure on
\text{PGL}(m,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\text{PGL}(m,\mathbb{R})/\text{PO}(m,\mathbb{R})
as
T\to\infty

Using our Theorem
1.5
, we can deduce the limiting measure of the following different sequence of probability measures on
\mathbb{Z}_{m,m+1}
given by
\nu_{T}:=\frac{1}{\#\Gamma_{T}}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{T}}\delta_{[\Lambda_{0}\gamma]},
where
\Lambda_{0}=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\{e_{1},...,e_{m}\}.
To obtain the limiting measure, we note that the equivalence relation modulo rotations and dilations defines a continuous map
[\cdot]:X_{m,m+1}\to\text{PGL}(m,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\text{PGL}(m,\mathbb{R})/\text{PO}(m,\mathbb{R})
. Pushing forward the measure
\tilde{\nu}_{x_{0}}
from Theorem
1.5
via this map gives the limiting measure of
\nu_{T}
, as
T\to\infty.

1.2
Paper organization

As was mentioned above, to prove our main result we verify the conditions of a theorem due to Gorodnik and Weiss, see Section 2.5 in
[
GW04
]
. Those conditions are certain volume estimate for the left Haar measure of ”skew”-norm balls in
H
, and ergodic theorems of those
H
-balls acting on

. The organization of the paper is as follows: Section
2
states and proves the aforementioned volume estimates, and Section
3
states and proves the aforementioned ergodic theorems. Finally, Section
4
applies the theorem of Gorodnik-Weiss.

Notations and conventions

Throughout this paper, for functions
f
and
g
we say that as
x\to\alpha
where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
that
f(x)=O(g(x))
or

if there is some
C>0
such that
|f(x)|\leq C|g(x)|
for all
x

\alpha
; by

f(x)\ll g(x)

; by

2
Volume estimates of expanding skew balls in
H

We begin by describing the left invariant measure on
H
. We consider

U:=\begin{bmatrix}I_{m}&0\\
\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}^{m}&1\end{bmatrix},A=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}t^{-\frac{1}{m}}I_{m}&0\\
0&t\end{bmatrix}:t>0\right\},\tilde{\Delta}:=\begin{bmatrix}\Delta&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix},

(2.1)

where
\Delta\leq\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})
is a lattice, and we denote

u_{v}:=\begin{bmatrix}I_{m}&0\\
v&1\end{bmatrix},a_{t}:=\begin{bmatrix}t^{-\frac{1}{m}}I_{m}&0\\
0&t\end{bmatrix},\tilde{q}:=\begin{bmatrix}q&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix},

(2.2)

for
q\in\Delta,t>0,v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Any element in
x\in H
can be represented as
x=u_{v}a_{t}\tilde{q}
uniquely in
v\in\mathbb{R}^{m},t>0,q\in\Delta
, and we have

(u_{v_{1}}a_{t_{1}}\tilde{q}_{1})(u_{v_{2}}a_{t_{2}}\tilde{q}_{2})=\left(u_{v_{1}+t_{1}^{\frac{m+1}{m}}v_{2}q_{1}^{-1}}\right)a_{t_{1}t_{2}}\left(\tilde{q}_{1}\tilde{q}_{2}\right),~v_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},t_{i}>0,q_{i}\in\Delta.

Namely,
H
is the semi-direct product

H\cong\mathbb{R}^{m}\rtimes(\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\Delta),

and it follows that a left Haar measure on
H
is given by

\displaystyle\int_{H}f(x)d\mu(x)=

\displaystyle\sum_{q\in\Delta}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}f\left(u_{v}a_{t}\tilde{q}\right)dv\frac{1}{t^{m+2}}dt,

(2.3)

where the measures
dv
and
dt
denote the Lebesgue measures on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\mathbb{R}
correspondingly.

Throughout this paper,
\|M\|
for a matrix
M
denotes it’s Hilbert-Schmidt norm.
Following
[
GW04
]
, for a subgroup
L\leq G
,
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
and
T>0
, we define the so-called “skewed balls” as follows (defined slightly differently from the definition in
[
GW04
]
):

L_{T}[g_{1},g_{2}]:=\{h\in L:\|g_{1}^{-1}hg_{2}\|<T\},

and we denote

L_{T}:=L_{T}[e,e].

(2.4)

For
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
, by using Iwasawa decomposition, we denote
k_{1},k_{2}\in\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R})
,
\text{g}_{1},\text{g}_{2}\in\text{GL}(m,\mathbb{R})
, and
\text{v}_{1},\text{v}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
by

g_{1}^{-1}=:k_{1}\begin{bmatrix}\text{g}_{1}&0\\
\text{v}_{1}&\det(\text{g}_{1})^{-1}\end{bmatrix},~g_{2}=:\begin{bmatrix}\text{g}_{2}&0\\
\text{v}_{2}&\det(\text{g}_{2})^{-1}\end{bmatrix}k_{2}.

(2.5)

We note that the above

, are not unique, but due to
\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R})
-invariance, all their appearances below are well defined regardless of their choices.

The key volume estimates we prove in this section are given in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\Gamma(z)
be the classical Gamma function, and let

C(m,g_{1},g_{2}):=\frac{m\pi^{\frac{m}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{m^{2}}{2})}{2\Gamma(\frac{m^{2}}{2}+\frac{m}{2}+1)}\left|\frac{\det(\emph{\text{g}}_{1})^{m}}{\det(\emph{\text{g}}_{2})}\right|.

There exists
\kappa>0
such that for a fixed bounded subset
B\subseteq\text{SL}(m+1,\mathbb{R})
it holds for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in B
that

\mu(H_{T}[g_{1},g_{2}])=\left(C(m,g_{1},g_{2})\sum_{q\in\Delta}\frac{1}{\|\emph{\text{g}}_{1}q\emph{\text{g}}_{2}\|^{m^{2}}}\right)T^{m(m+1)}+O(T^{m(m+1)-\kappa}),

(2.6)

as
T\to\infty
. The implied constant depends on
B
only.

We note that the sum
\sum_{q\in\Delta}\frac{1}{\|\text{g}_{1}q\text{g}_{2}\|^{m^{2}}}
is convergent due to Lemma
2.7
below.

Remark 2.2
.

Statements of this form are known in great generality for a connected
H
, see e.g. Theorems 7.17–7.18 in
[
GN10
]
. Here, since our
H
has infinitely many connected components, the volume of the skew-ball is a sum over the volumes of skew-balls of the connected component, see (
2.10
) below. Our main challenge here is to quantify the dependence of the error term of the main term appearing of the volume of each skew-ball of the connected component, see Lemma
2.6
below. Proving such a result for more general norms is the main obstacle for generalizing our results for more general norms.

Corollary 2.3
.

(Uniform volume growth for skewed balls in

For any bounded subset

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\Delta\leq\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})
by a lattice, and fix an integer
m\geq 2
. Then, for
\sigma>-m(m-1)
it holds that

\sum_{q\in\Delta_{T}}\|q\|^{\sigma}\ll T^{m(m-1)+\sigma},~\text{as }T\to\infty,

and if
\sigma<-m(m-1)
,
the sum above converges with the following tail estimate:

\sum_{q\in\Delta_{T}}\|q\|^{\sigma}=I_{\sigma}+O(T^{m(m-1)+\sigma}),~\text{as }T\to\infty.

Proof.

By
[
DRS93
]
, there is
C>0
(an explicit constant), such that

\#\Delta_{s}=Cs^{m(m-1)}+o(s^{m(m-1)}),~\text{as }s\to\infty.

(2.11)

This proves the statement in case that
\sigma=0
. Now, for
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\sum_{q\in\Delta_{T}}\|q\|^{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\in\Delta_{T}}\int_{0}^{\|q\|^{\sigma}}1dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\in\Delta_{T}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{[t<\|q\|^{\sigma}]}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{q\in\Delta_{T}}\mathbf{1}_{[t<\|q\|^{\sigma}]}dt.

Consider the case that
\sigma>0
. We have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{q\in\Delta_{T}}\mathbf{1}_{[t<\|q\|^{\sigma}]}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T^{\sigma}}\#\{q\in\Delta:t<\|q\|^{\sigma}<T^{\sigma}\}dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T^{\sigma}}\#\{q\in\Delta:\|q\|<T\}dt

\displaystyle\underbrace{\ll}_{\eqref{eq:DRS asymp}}T^{m(m-1)+\sigma}.

Next, consider
-m(m-1)\neq\sigma<0
. We have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{q\in\Delta_{T}}\mathbf{1}_{[t<\|q\|^{\sigma}]}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\#\{q\in\Delta:\|q\|<\min(t^{\frac{1}{\sigma}},T)\}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{T^{\sigma}}^{\infty}\#\{q\in\Delta:\|q\|<t^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}\}dt+\int_{0}^{T^{\sigma}}\#\{q\in\Delta:\|q\|<T\}dt

\displaystyle\underbrace{=}_{\eqref{eq:DRS asymp}}\int_{T^{\sigma}}^{\infty}\#\{q\in\Delta:\|q\|<t^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}\}dt+CT^{m(m-1)+\sigma}+o(T^{m(m-1)+\sigma}),~\text{as }T\to\infty.

To prove the claim, it remains to treat the integral
\int_{T^{\sigma}}^{\infty}\#\{q\in\Delta:\|q\|<t^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}\}dt
. Since
\|g\|\geq 1
for all
g\in\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})
, we have

\int_{T^{\sigma}}^{\infty}\#\{q\in\Delta:\|q\|<t^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}\}dt=\int_{T^{\sigma}}^{1}\#\{q\in\Delta:\|q\|<t^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}\}dt.

By (
2.11
), there is
C^{\prime}>0
such that

\#\{q\in\Delta:\|q\|<t^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}\}\leq C^{\prime}t^{\frac{m(m-1)}{\sigma}},~\text{for all }t\in(0,1).

Suppose that
-m(m-1)<\sigma<0
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{T^{\sigma}}^{1}\#\{q\in\Delta:\|q\|<t^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}\}dt\leq

\displaystyle C^{\prime}\int_{T^{\sigma}}^{1}t^{\frac{m(m-1)}{\sigma}}dt

\displaystyle=C^{\prime}\frac{-1}{\frac{m(m-1)}{\sigma}+1}\left(T^{m(m-1)+\sigma}-1\right)

\displaystyle\ll T^{m(m-1)+\sigma}.

It’s left to treat the case that
\sigma<-m(m-1)
. Since
\int_{0}^{1}t^{\frac{m(m-1)}{\sigma}}dt
converges, we have by dominated convergence that

\int_{0}^{1}\#\{q\in\Delta:\|q\|<t^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}\}dt=I_{\sigma}

for some constant
=I_{\sigma}
. Finally,

\displaystyle\int_{T^{\sigma}}^{1}\#\{q\in\Delta:\|q\|<t^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}\}dt=

\displaystyle I_{\sigma}-\int^{T^{\sigma}}_{0}\#\{q\in\Delta:\|q\|<t^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}\}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle I_{\sigma}+C^{\prime}\left(\int^{T^{\sigma}}_{0}t^{\frac{m(m-1)}{\sigma}}dt\right)

\displaystyle=I_{\sigma}+O(T^{m(m-1)+\sigma}).

∎

For the following, we note the basic fact – when
g_{1},g_{2}
vary in a bounded set
B

such that

C^{-}\|q\|\leq\|\text{g}_{1}q\text{g}_{2}\|\leq C^{+}\|q\|,

(2.12)

where
\text{g}_{1},\text{g}_{2}
are given by (
2.5
).

.

We assume that

vary in a fixed bounded set

. We have:

\displaystyle\mu\left(H_{T}[g_{1},g_{2}]\right)=

\displaystyle\sum_{q\in\Delta}\mu\left(V_{q,T}[g_{1},g_{2}]\right)

\displaystyle~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\in\Delta,\\

\displaystyle\underbrace{=}_{\text{Lemma \ref{lem:main estimate for large V skewed balls } }}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\in\Delta,\\

\displaystyle\underbrace{=}_{\text{Lemma }\ref{lemma on the integral and summation in a ball}}\left(C(m,g_{1},g_{2})\sum_{q\in\Delta}\frac{1}{\|\text{g}_{1}q\text{g}_{2}\|^{m^{2}}}\right)T^{m(m+1)}

Finally, we estimate the terms in the big-O notation above by again applying Lemma

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\in\Delta,\\

The

Proposition 3.4

The following lemma will be needed:

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded continuous function. Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an ellipsoid with surface area
S
, then for

we have the estimate:

\left|\int_{E}[f(v)-f(y+v)]dv\right|\ll\|f\|_{\infty}\|y\|S,

where the implied constant depends on

only.

Proof.

An elementary geometric argument (thickening the boundary) implies that

\text{Vol}(E\triangle(E-y))\ll\|y\|S,

where
S
is the surface area of the ellipsoid, and Vol stands for the Lebesgue volume. Alternatively, see
[
Sch10
]
for a much more general theorem implying this fact. Now

\displaystyle\left|\int_{E}[f(v)-f(y+v)]dv\right|=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{E}f(v)dv-\int_{E}f(v)dv\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{E\triangle(E-y)}|f(v)|dv

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\infty}\|y\|S,

(
f
is bounded)

∎

In order to apply the results of Shah, we need to establish that vectors expand by the action of

in a representation of

\mathcal{V}_{G}
a representation of

\mathcal{V}_{G}=\mathcal{V}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{1},

where
\mathcal{V}_{0}
is the space of
\text{SL}(m+1,\mathbb{R})
-fixed vectors and
\mathcal{V}_{1}
is it’s invariant complement. Suppose that
\mathcal{V}_{1}
is not trivial, and let
\Pi
be the projection of
\mathcal{V}_{G}
onto
\mathcal{V}_{1}
with kernel
\mathcal{V}_{0}
. Our main result in this section is the following.

Lemma 3.6
.

Fix
g_{0}\in G
, let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a bounded set, and let
\chi\in(0,1)
. Then for any
\nu>0
, there exist
t_{0}>0
such that for any
0<t<t_{0}
, any
\xi\in K
and any
\textbf{x}\in\mathcal{V}_{G}
such that
\|\Pi(\textbf{x})\|\geq r
, it holds

\sup_{v\in\text{B}\left(t^{\frac{1-\chi}{m}}\right)+\xi}\|a_{t}^{-1}u_{v}^{-1}g_{0}.\textbf{x}\|>\nu,

(3.7)

where
\text{B}(\beta)
is the ball of radius
\beta
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
centered at the origin.

The idea behind the proof of the lemma to decompose the elements
a_{t}^{-1}u_{v}^{-1}
into
m

\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
. That’s useful since we can use the explicit description of irreducible representations of

to prove an expansion of the norm for each term in the decomposition. We note that a similar approach was used in
[
KW06
]
.

We now give the decomposition. Let
E_{i,j}
be the
(m+1)\times(m+1)
matrix where the
(i,j)

while all other entries are zero. Consider the following copy of

in

\text{SL}^{(j)}(2,\mathbb{R}):=\{I_{m+1}+(a-1)E_{jj}+bE_{m+1,j}+cE_{j,m+1}+(d-1)E_{m+1,m+1}:ad-bc=1\},

(3.8)

for

. We will denote

\pi^{(j)}\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}:=I_{m+1}+(a-1)E_{jj}+bE_{m+1,j}+cE_{j,m+1}+(d-1)E_{m+1,m+1}.

We have the following observation which we leave the reader to verify.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\sigma:\{1,2,\dots m\}\to\{1,2,\dots,m\}
be a permutation. Then it holds that

a_{t}^{-1}u_{v}^{-1}=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\pi^{(\sigma(j))}\left(\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{1}{m}}&0\\
0&t^{-\frac{1}{m}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
-t^{-\frac{m-j}{m}}v_{\sigma(j)}&1\end{bmatrix}\right),

(3.9)

for

as in (

).

The decomposition (

) reduces the proof of Lemma

y&1\end{bmatrix}

in representations of
\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})

3.2.1
Expansion in
\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-irreducible representations

We now briefly recall some basic facts on
\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-irreducible representations.

Recall that if
\pi
is an
(n+1)
-dimensional irreducible representation of
\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
and
\pi^{\prime}
is the induced Lie algebra representation, then there exists a basis
v_{0},v_{1},...,v_{n}
such that

\displaystyle\pi^{\prime}(H)(v_{i})=(n-2i)v_{i},i=0,1,...,n;

\displaystyle\pi^{\prime}(X)(v_{i})=i(n-i+1)v_{i-1},i=0,1,...,n;

\displaystyle\pi^{\prime}(Y)(v_{i})=v_{i+1},i=0,1,...,n~(v_{n+1}=0).

where

H=\begin{bmatrix}1&~~0\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix},X=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}
, and

Y=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}
form a generating set of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
.

Under the matrix Lie group-Lie algebra correspondence,

\pi\left(\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{1}{m}}&0\\
0&t^{-\frac{1}{m}}\end{bmatrix}\right)=\exp(\pi^{\prime}(\log(t^{\frac{1}{m}})H))

has the matrix representation

\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{n}{m}}&&&\\
&t^{\frac{n-2}{m}}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&{t}^{-\frac{n}{m}}\end{bmatrix}

(3.10)

and

\pi\left(\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
-y&1\end{bmatrix}\right)=\exp(\pi^{\prime}(-yY))

has the matrix representation

\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
p_{1}(y)&1&&\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\\
p_{n}(y)&\cdots&p_{1}(y)&1\end{bmatrix}

(3.11)

where
p_{l}(y)=\frac{(-y)^{l}}{l!},~l=1,...,n
.
Observe that the line

W:=\mathbb{R}v_{n}

is the subspace of fixed vectors of
\pi\left(\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
-y&1\end{bmatrix}\right),y\in\mathbb{R}.
This space is important for us since it’s the eigenspace of the matrices
\pi\left(\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{1}{m}}&0\\
0&t^{-\frac{1}{m}}\end{bmatrix}\right)
which corresponds to the largest eigenvalue as
t\to 0
.
We denote by
\text{pr}_{W}
the orthogonal projection on
W
with respect to some scalar product on
\mathcal{V}_{n}
.

The following lemma is a very special case of
[
Gor03
, Lemma 13]
(which is essentially
[
Sha96
, Lemma 5.1]
). In our setting the proof simplifies considerably and we prove it for completeness.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\pi:\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{V}_{n}
be the
(n+1)
-dimensional irreducible representation of
\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
. Fix a bounded interval
I
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
\beta\in(0,1)
,
\tau\in\emph{\text{I}}
and
\textbf{x}\in\mathcal{V}_{n}
,

\sup_{y\in([0,\beta]+\tau)}\left\|\text{pr}_{W}\left(\pi\left(\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
-y&1\end{bmatrix}\right)\textbf{x}\right)\right\|\geq C\beta^{n}\|\textbf{x}\|.

Proof.

First, note that it’s enough to prove that there exists
C_{0}>0
such that:

\sup_{y\in[0,\beta]}\left\|\text{pr}_{W}\left(\pi\left(\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
-y&1\end{bmatrix}\right)\textbf{x}\right)\right\|\geq C_{0}\|\textbf{x}\|.

(3.12)

In fact, by assuming (
3.12
), we get

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in([0,\beta]+\tau)}\left\|\text{pr}_{W}\left(\pi\left(\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
-y&1\end{bmatrix}\right)\textbf{x}\right)\right\|=

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in[0,\beta]}\left\|\text{pr}_{W}\left(\pi\left(\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
-y&1\end{bmatrix}\right)\pi\left(\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
-\tau&1\end{bmatrix}\right)\textbf{x}\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\geq C_{0}\left\|\pi\left(\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
-\tau&1\end{bmatrix}\right)\textbf{x}\right\|

\displaystyle\geq C_{0}C_{1}\|\textbf{x}\|,

where
C_{1}
depends on I.
Now, in the coordinates of the basis
\{v_{0},...,v_{n}\}
, upon recalling (
3.11
) we see

\displaystyle\text{pr}_{W}\left(\pi\left(\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
-y&1\end{bmatrix}\right)\textbf{x}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
p_{1}(y)&1&&\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\\
p_{n}(y)&\cdots&p_{1}(y)&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x_{0}\\
x_{1}\\
\vdots\\
x_{n}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\vdots\\
0\\
p_{\textbf{x}}(y)\end{bmatrix},

where
p_{\textbf{x}}(y)=c\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}x_{n-k}y^{k}
(here
c
is some constant depending on chosen scalar product for the orthogonal projection
\text{pr}_{W}.
) Note the general fact that there exists
C_{2}>0
such that for any polynomial
p(x)=a_{n}x^{n}+...+a_{0}
of degree at most
n

\sup_{s\in[0,1]}|p(s)|\geq C_{2}\|\textbf{a}\|,

where
a
is the vector whose entries are the coefficients of
p(x)
. Thus we may conclude:

\sup_{y\in[0,\beta]}|p_{\textbf{x}}(y)|=\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\beta^{k}x_{n-k}s^{k}\right|\gg\beta^{n}\|\textbf{x}\|,

which proves the lemma.
∎

The above lemma implies the following statement, which is an analogue of Lemma
3.6
for
\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
.

.

Suppose that
\pi:\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{V}
is a representation, fix an interval

and let

. Then, for all

the following holds.

for all

, where the implied constant depends on

2.

If
\pi

\tau\in\emph{\text{I}}

\sup_{y\in[0,t^{\frac{1-\chi}{m}}]+\tau}\left\|\pi\left(\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{1}{m}}&0\\

for all

Proof.

In the following, for convenience, we will omit the representation symbol

\mathcal{V}
is the

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C\left\|\text{pr}_{W}\left(\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{1}{m}}&0\\

It holds that

For a bounded set

by the definition of fundamental domain, and
h_{1}=h_{2}
.
∎

Next, let
P:=\left\{u_{v}a_{t}\tilde{\text{g}}:\text{g}\in\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R}),~v\in\mathbb{R}^{m},~t>0\right\},
where
u_{v},a_{t}
are given by (
2.2
), and
\tilde{\text{g}}:=\begin{bmatrix}\text{g}&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}
. On
P
we define the Haar measure
dp=dv\frac{dt}{t^{m+2}}d\text{g}
, where
d\text{g}
is the Haar measure on
\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})
defined through standard Iwasawa decomposition, under which
\text{SO}(m,\mathbb{R})
has volume
1
. We note the formula

\int_{\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})}f(\text{g})d\text{g}=\int_{\mathcal{F}_{m}}\sum_{q\in\Delta}\int_{\text{SO}(m,\mathbb{R})}f(q\text{g}\rho^{\prime})d\rho^{\prime}d\text{g}.

(4.1)

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
d\rho
the Haar measure probability measure on
\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R})
, and consider the measure
\nu_{Y}
on
Y
defined by
d\nu_{Y}:=d\rho d\text{g}
. Then

dg=d\mu d\nu_{Y},

(4.2)

where
\mu
is (
2.3
), and
dg
is the Haar measure on
G=\text{SL}(m+1,\mathbb{R})
such that
\text{Vol}(G/\Gamma)=1
.

Proof.

by using Theorem 8.32 of
[
Kna02
]
, we unfold a Haar measure on
G
as follows

\displaystyle\int_{G}f(g)dg

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R})}\int_{P}f(p\rho)dpd\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R})}\int_{\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}f(u_{v}a_{t}\tilde{\text{g}}\rho)dv\frac{dt}{t^{m+2}}d\text{g}d\rho

(by definition of
dp
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R})}\sum_{q\in\Delta}\int_{\mathcal{F}_{m}}\int_{\text{SO}(m,\mathbb{R})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}f(u_{v}a_{t}\tilde{q}\tilde{\text{g}}\tilde{\rho^{\prime}}\rho)dv\frac{dt}{t^{m+2}}d\text{g}d\rho^{\prime}d\rho

(by formula (
4.1
))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R})}\sum_{q\in\Delta}\int_{\mathcal{F}_{m}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}f(u_{v}a_{t}\tilde{q}\tilde{\text{g}}\rho)dv\frac{dt}{t^{m+2}}d\text{g}d\rho

(invariance of
d\rho
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R})}\sum_{q\in\Delta}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{\mathcal{F}_{m}}f(u_{v}a_{t}\tilde{q}\tilde{\text{g}}\rho)d\text{g}dv\frac{dt}{t^{m+2}}d\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{H}\int_{\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R})}\int_{\mathcal{F}_{m}}f(h\tilde{\text{g}}\rho)d\text{g}d\rho d\mu(h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{H}\left(\int_{\mathcal{F}_{m}}\int_{\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R})}f(h\tilde{\text{g}}\rho)d\rho d\text{g}\right)d\mu(h),

which proves the statement.
∎

Fix
Hg_{0}\in H\backslash G
. By identifying
H\backslash G
with
Y
, we define a measure on
H\backslash G
via

d\nu_{Hg_{0}}(Hg):=\omega(\sigma(Hg_{0}),\sigma(Hg))d\nu_{Y}(\sigma(Hg)).

(4.3)

where
\omega(\cdot,\cdot)
is given in (
2.7
).

In view of Theorem 2.2 and Corollary 2.4 in
[
GW04
]
, an immediate consequence of Theorem
3.1
is the following

Corollary 4.3
.

Fix
g_{0}\in G
. For any compactly supported
\varphi\in C_{c}(H\backslash G)
,

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\mu(H_{T})}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{T}}\varphi(Hg_{0}.\gamma)=\int_{H\backslash G}\varphi(Hg)d\nu_{Hg_{0}}(Hg).

Now we would like to replace the normalization factor
\mu(H_{T})
by
\#\Gamma_{T}
.
By
[
DRS93
]
,

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\text{Vol}(G_{T})}{\#\Gamma_{T}}=1.

By formula A.1.15 in
[
DRS93
]
and by Proposition
2.1
, the limit

L:=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\mu(H_{T})}{\text{Vol}(G_{T})}

exists. Thus we conclude

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\mu(H_{T})}{\#\Gamma_{T}}=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\mu(H_{T})}{\text{Vol}(G_{T})}\frac{\text{Vol}(G_{T})}{\#\Gamma_{T}}=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\mu(H_{T})}{\text{Vol}(G_{T})}=L,

which shows that

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\#\Gamma_{T}}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{T}}\varphi(Hg_{0}.\gamma)=L\int_{H\backslash G}\varphi(Hg)d\nu_{Hg_{0}}(Hg).

Our goal now will be to show that
L\nu_{Hg_{0}}(H\backslash G)=1
. We first have the following lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

It holds that
\nu_{H}(H\backslash G)<\infty
and
\nu_{Hg_{0}}(H\backslash G)=\nu_{H}(H\backslash G)
for all
Hg_{0}\in H\backslash G.

Proof.

For
g_{0},y\in Y
, we consider the decompositions

g_{0}:=\begin{bmatrix}\text{g}_{0}&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\rho_{0},~y:=\begin{bmatrix}\text{g}&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\rho,

(4.4)

where
\text{g}_{0},\text{g}\in\mathcal{F}_{m}
and
\rho_{0},\rho\in\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R}).
Hence
\alpha
in (
2.7
) takes the form:
\omega(g_{0},y)=\frac{\sum_{q\in\Delta}\frac{1}{\|\text{g}_{0}^{-1}q\text{g}\|^{m^{2}}}}{\sum_{q\in\Delta}\frac{1}{\|q\|^{m^{2}}}}.

Using the
\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R})
-invariance of the Hilbert-Schmidt norm, we compute

\displaystyle\nu_{Hg_{0}}(H\backslash G)=

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\omega(g_{0},y)d\nu_{Y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sum_{q\in\Delta}\frac{1}{\|q\|^{m^{2}}}}\int_{\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R})}\int_{\mathcal{F}_{m}}\sum_{q\in\Delta}\frac{1}{\|\text{g}_{0}^{-1}q\text{g}\|^{m^{2}}}d\text{g}d\rho

(definition of
d\nu_{Y}
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sum_{q\in\Delta}\frac{1}{\|q\|^{m^{2}}}}\int_{\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})}\frac{1}{\|\text{g}_{0}^{-1}\text{g}\|^{m^{2}}}d\text{g}

(by formula (
4.1
))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sum_{q\in\Delta}\frac{1}{\|q\|^{m^{2}}}}\int_{\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})}\frac{1}{\|\text{g}\|^{m^{2}}}d\text{g}

(invariance of
d\text{g}
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu_{H}(H\backslash G).

It remains to verify that
\int_{\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})}\frac{1}{\|\text{g}\|^{m^{2}}}d\text{g}<\infty.
By monotone convergence, it’s enough to prove that
\int_{\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})_{T}}\frac{1}{\|\text{g}\|^{m^{2}}}d\text{g}
converges as
T\to\infty
, where
\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})_{T}=\{\text{g}\in\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R}):\|\text{g}\|\leq T\}.
For that we use a Fubini trick, similarly to the proof of Lemma
2.7
:

\displaystyle\int_{\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})_{T}}\frac{1}{\|\text{g}\|^{m^{2}}}d\text{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})_{T}}\int_{0}^{\infty}1_{\{t\leq\|\text{g}\|^{-m^{2}}\}}dtd\text{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})_{T}}1_{\{t\leq\|\text{g}\|^{-m^{2}}\}}d\text{g}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\text{Vol}\{\text{g}:\|\text{g}\|\leq\text{min}(T,t^{-\frac{1}{m^{2}}})\}dt,

(using that
\|\text{g}\|\geq 1,\forall\text{g}
)

where
Vol
stands for the
\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})
-Haar volume. The convergence is established by using formula A.1.15 in
[
DRS93
]
which gives
\text{Vol}\{\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})_{s}\}\sim s^{m(m-1)}.

∎

Finally, we prove

Proposition 4.5
.

\nu_{Hg_{0}}(H\backslash G)=\nu_{H}(H\backslash G)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\text{Vol}(G_{T})}{\mu(H_{T})}=\frac{1}{L}
.

Proof.

Since
\nu_{H}
is a Radon measure, for any
\epsilon>0
, we can choose
f_{\epsilon}\in C_{c}(H\backslash G)
with support
B_{\epsilon}
such that

\int_{Y}|f_{\epsilon}(Hy)-1|\omega(e,y)d\nu_{Y}(y)=\int_{H\backslash G}|f_{\epsilon}(Hg)-1|d\nu_{H}(Hg)\leq\epsilon.

As in
[
GW04
]
, we observe that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(H_{T})}\int_{G_{T}}|f_{\epsilon}(Hg)-1|dg=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(H_{T})}\int_{Y}\int_{\{h:\|hy\|<T\}}|f_{\epsilon}(Hy)-1|d\mu(h)d\nu_{Y}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(H_{T})}\int_{Y}|f_{\epsilon}(Hy)-1|\mu(\{h:\|hy\|<T\})d\nu_{Y}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Y}|f_{\epsilon}(Hy)-1|\frac{\mu(H_{T}[e,y])}{\mu(H_{T})}d\nu_{Y}(y)

Recall that
\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\mu(H_{T}[e,y])}{\mu(H_{T})}=\omega(e,y).

We will use below the dominated convergence theorem, and for that we will now show that the integrand
\frac{\mu(H_{T}[e,y])}{\mu(H_{T})}
is bounded by a function in
L^{1}(Y)
for large
T
.
By (
2.26
) and by (
2.10
), we see

\frac{\mu(H_{T}[e,y])}{\mu(H_{T})}\ll\sum_{q\in\Delta}\frac{1}{\|q\text{g}\|^{m^{2}}},\text{ where $y=\begin{bmatrix}\text{g}&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\rho,~\text{g}\in\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R}),\rho\in\text{SO}(m+1,\mathbb{R})$}.

Since
\int_{\text{SL}(m,\mathbb{R})}\frac{1}{\|\text{g}\|^{m^{2}}}d\text{g}
converges, it follows that
\Psi(y):=\sum_{q\in\Delta}\frac{1}{\|q\text{g}\|^{m^{2}}}\in L^{1}(Y)
(more precisely, to see that this is a
L^{1}
function, use (
4.1
)).

By the dominant convergence theorem, the second term satisfies

\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_{Y}|f_{\epsilon}(Hy)-1|\frac{\mu(H_{T}[e,y])}{\mu(H_{T})}d\nu_{Y}(y)=\int_{Y}|f_{\epsilon}(Hy)-1|d\nu_{H}(y)\leq\epsilon.

Therefore, by triangular inequality

\displaystyle\limsup_{T\to\infty}\left|\frac{\text{Vol}(G_{T})}{\mu(H_{T})}-\nu_{H}(H\backslash G)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{T\to\infty}\left|\frac{\text{Vol}(G_{T})}{\mu(H_{T})}-\frac{1}{\mu(H_{T})}\int_{G_{T}}f_{\epsilon}(Hg)dg\right|

\displaystyle+\limsup_{T\to\infty}\left|\frac{1}{\mu(H_{T})}\int_{G_{T}}f_{\epsilon}(Hg)dg-\int_{H\backslash G}f_{\epsilon}(Hg)d\nu_{H}(g)\right|

\displaystyle+\limsup_{T\to\infty}\left|\int_{H\backslash G}f_{\epsilon}(Hg)dg-\nu_{H}(H\backslash G)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon+0+\epsilon

(the middle term vanishes because of Theorem 2.3,
[
GW04
]
)

Now let
\epsilon\to 0
, and this finishes the proof.
∎

Therefore,
\tilde{\nu}_{Hg_{0}}:=L\nu_{Hg_{0}}
is a probability measure, and we conclude

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\#\Gamma_{T}}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{T}}\varphi(Hg_{0}.\gamma)=\int_{H\backslash G}\varphi(Hg)d\tilde{\nu}_{Hg_{0}}(g).

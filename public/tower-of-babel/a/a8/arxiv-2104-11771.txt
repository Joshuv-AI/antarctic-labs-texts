Quantitative Gromov Compactness
Mohan Swaminathan

Abstract
We establish a quantitative version of the Gromov compactness theorem for closed genus 0 0 pseudoholomorphic curves in the setting of a tamed almost complex manifold with bounded geometry.



1
Introduction

Let
(X,\omega,J,g)
be a 4-tuple where
(X,g)
is a smooth Riemannian manifold,
\omega
is a symplectic form on
X
and
J
is an
\omega
-tame almost complex structure. We will assume that we have
C^{k}
bounds on the geometry of
(X,\omega,J,g)
for some integer
k\geq 3
. Gromov’s compactness theorem (
[
Gro85
, 1.5.B]
,
[
MS12
, Theorem 5.3.1]
,
[
Hum97
, Theorem V.1.2]
) then implies that if we fix integers
\gamma,\ell\geq 0
, a constant
A>0
and a compact subset
K\subset X
, then the moduli space

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\gamma,\ell}(X,J;K)^{\leq A}

(1.0.1)

of (closed) stable
\ell
-pointed
J
-holomorphic maps in
X
of genus
\gamma
with energy
\leq A
and image lying in
K
is compact. In this article, we will establish a quantitative version of this statement for
\gamma=0
.

1.1
Setup

We start by identifying
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,4}
by mapping
[u:v]
(with
u^{3}\neq v^{3}
) to the curve
\mathbb{P}^{1}
equipped with the four marked points
[1:1],[e^{2\pi i/3}:1],[e^{-2\pi i/3}:1],[u:v]
and extending continuously. This endows
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,4}
with a natural round metric of total area
4\pi
(see §
2.1
). Next, for any
m\geq 4
consider the map

\displaystyle\iota_{m}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,m}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,4}^{\binom{m}{4}}

(1.1.1)

given by mapping
[\Sigma,x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,m}
to the
\binom{m}{4}
-tuple, indexed by choices
1\leq i<j<k<l\leq m
, of 4-point stabilizations
[\Sigma,x_{i},x_{j},x_{k},x_{l}]^{\text{st}}\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,4}
. The map
\iota_{m}
is a closed holomorphic embedding
[
MS12
, Theorem D.4.2]
. We can use the natural forgetful map
\pi_{m}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,m+1}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,m}
, which is compatible with the embeddings
\iota_{m}
and
\iota_{m+1}
, and the combinatorial identity
\binom{m+1}{4}=\binom{m}{4}+\binom{m}{3}
to get the closed holomorphic embedding

\displaystyle j_{m+1}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,m+1}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,m}\times Z_{m}

(1.1.2)

which realizes
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,m+1}
as an algebraic family of closed subschemes of
Z_{m}:=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,4}^{\binom{m}{3}}
, parametrized by
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,m}
. This algebraic family yields a natural map

\displaystyle\hat{\iota}_{m}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,m}

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{K}(Z_{m})

(1.1.3)

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathcal{C}_{s}:=\pi_{m}^{-1}(s)\subset Z_{m}

(1.1.4)

from the moduli space of stable
m
-pointed genus
0
curves to the space of compact subsets of
Z_{m}
, endowed with the Hausdorff distance metric (Definition
B.1
). Here, we view
Z_{m}
as a metric space by equipping it with the distance function obtained by taking the maximum of the distance functions on each of the
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,4}
factors. It is easy to verify that
\hat{\iota}_{m}
is injective and continuous and thus, a closed topological embedding (since the domain is compact and the target is Hausdorff).

Definition 1.1
.

For any
L>0
and any integer
m\geq 3
, define the
L
-distance
on
Z_{m}\times X
to be the metric which is given by the maximum of the distance function on
Z_{m}
and
L^{-1}
times the distance function on
X
. For compact subsets
A,B\subset Z_{m}\times X
, let
d_{H,L}(A,B)
the Hausdorff
L
-distance between
A
and
B
. Given
p,q\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,m}
and continuous maps

f:\mathcal{C}_{p}\to X,g:\mathcal{C}_{q}\to X
, define the
L
-distance
between
f
and
g
to be

\displaystyle{\text{dist}}_{L}(f,g)=d_{H,L}(\Gamma_{f},\Gamma_{g})

(1.1.5)

where
\Gamma_{f}
(resp.
\Gamma_{g}
) is the graph of
f:\mathcal{C}_{p}\to X
(resp.
g:\mathcal{C}_{q}\to X
), viewed as a compact subset of
Z_{m}\times X
.

Definition 1.2
.

Given
A>0
, an integer
\ell\geq 0
, a stable
J
-holomorphic map
u=[\Sigma,x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell},f]\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\ell}(X,J;K)^{\leq A}
and a nonnegative integer
m\geq 3-\ell
, define an
m
-decoration
of
u
to be a choice a point
p\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\ell+m}
and a biholomorphism
\varphi:\Sigma\to\mathcal{C}_{p}
with
\varphi(x_{1}),\ldots,\varphi(x_{\ell})
being the first
\ell
points, in this order, on the
(m+\ell)
-pointed curve
\mathcal{C}_{p}
. An
m
-decoration
\varphi
yields a
J
-holomorphic map
u^{\varphi}:=u\circ\varphi^{-1}
which has a well-defined associated
\varepsilon
-local Lipschitz constant

\displaystyle\text{Lip}_{\varepsilon}(u^{\varphi})=\sup_{0<{\text{dist}}(q_{1},q_{2})\leq\varepsilon}\frac{{\text{dist}}(u^{\varphi}(q_{1}),u^{\varphi}(q_{2}))}{{\text{dist}}(q_{1},q_{2})}

(1.1.6)

for every
0<\varepsilon\leq\pi
. Here, we are using metric induced by the inclusion
\mathcal{C}_{p}\subset Z_{\ell+m}
on the domain. (One should think of a local Lipschitz estimate as similar to a local gradient bound.) Moreover, given any
\delta>0
, we can define the neighborhood

\displaystyle V_{L,\delta}(u;\varphi)\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\ell}(X,J;K)^{\leq A}

(1.1.7)

of
u
to consist of all those stable
J
-holomorphic maps
v\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\ell}(X,J;K)^{\leq A}
for which there exists an
m
-decoration
\psi
satisfying
{\text{dist}}_{L}(u^{\varphi},v^{\psi})\leq\delta
.

1.2
Main result

We can now state the main result, proved in §
5
. Much more precise versions of this statement are contained in Theorems
3.18
and
4.2
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume
(X,\omega,J,g)
has bounded geometry,
K\subset X
is a compact subset,
\ell\geq 0
in an integer and
A>0
is a real number. Then, there exists
\lambda_{0}>0
depending only on the bounds on the geometry, an integer
m\geq 0
and a real number
\Lambda>0
both depending explicitly on
\ell,A
, the local bounds on the geometry of
X
and not on
K
with the following significance.

(i)

Given any
u\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\ell}(X,J;K)^{\leq A}
, it has an
m
-decoration
\varphi
such that the associated map
u^{\varphi}
has
1
-local Lipschitz constant
\leq\Lambda\lambda_{0}
.

(ii)

Let
u\mapsto\varphi_{u}
denote an assignment of a
m
-decoration of
1
-local Lipschitz constant
\leq\Lambda\lambda_{0}
as in
(i)
to each
u\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\ell}(X,J;K)^{\leq A}
. Given any positive
0<\delta\leq 1
, we can then find an integer
N\geq 0
depending explicitly on
\ell,A
,
K\subset(X,\omega,J,g)
and
\delta
with the following property. There is a subset
I\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\ell}(X,J;K)^{\leq A}
with
\leq N
elements, such that for every
u\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\ell}(X,J;K)^{\leq A}
, we can find a corresponding
v\in I
such that we have

\displaystyle{\text{dist}}_{\lambda_{0}}(u^{\varphi_{u}},v^{\varphi_{v}})\leq 4\delta.

(1.2.1)

In particular, the neighborhoods
\{V_{\lambda_{0},4\delta}(v;\varphi_{v})\}_{v\in I}
cover
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\ell}(X,J;K)^{\leq A}
.

In fact, we can take
m
and
\Lambda
to be given by the explicit formulas

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=\lfloor c\cdot(\ell+A/\lambda^{2})\rfloor

(1.2.2)

\displaystyle\log\Lambda

\displaystyle=c\cdot(\ell+A/\lambda^{2})

(1.2.3)

where
\lambda
is a constant depending only on the local bounds of the geometry of
X
while
c
is a sufficiently large absolute constant. Having fixed
m
and
\Lambda
, we can take
N
to be given by the following explicit formula

\displaystyle N=\left(1+\sigma\delta^{-2k}\nu(K,\lambda_{0})\right)^{(8\pi\Lambda^{2}\delta^{-2})^{\binom{m+\ell}{3}}}.

(1.2.4)

where
\sigma
is a constant depending on the local bounds on the geometry of
X
,
\nu(K,\lambda_{0})
is the size of the smallest
\lambda_{0}
-net in the compact metric space
K
and
2k=\dim X
.

Remark 1.4
.

Part (i) of Theorem
1.3
(or, more precisely, Theorem
4.2
) is analogous to the result of Groman on thick-thin decompositions of holomorphic curves
[
Gro13
]
. Part (ii) provides a way of using part (i) to estimate the “size” or “complexity” of the moduli space, provided we can understand the “complexity” of each of the individual neighborhoods
V_{\lambda_{0},\delta}(v;\varphi_{v})
, each of which can be covered by an explicit Kuranishi chart if
\delta
is sufficiently small (how small should depend on
A
and the bounds on the geometry).

Remark 1.5
.

Theorem
1.3
(i) follows from a careful reworking of the usual proof of convergence modulo bubbling in
[
MS12
, Chapter 4]
for a sequence of maps with bounded energy. The new marked points are chosen so that they have the effect of magnifying regions where energy is concentrating and this brings down the Lipschitz constant. Theorem
1.3
(ii) follows by examining the proof of the Arzelà-Ascoli Theorem for Lipschitz maps and modifying it to deal with the case of variable domains (a precise form of this argument appears in Theorem
3.18
).

2
Preparation

Before beginning the main discussion, we set notations and recall some fundamental analytical properties of pseudoholomorphic curves.

2.1
Notation

For
r\geq 0
, an integer
n\geq 1
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, define
B^{n}(x,r)
to be the set of
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying
|y-x|\leq r
. For
x=0
, we will denote
B^{n}(x,r)
simply by
B^{n}(r)
. Denote the boundary spheres of
B^{n}(x,r)
and
B^{n}(r)
, by
S^{n-1}(x,r)
and
S^{n-1}(r)
respectively.

For a set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a point
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define the distance from
z
to
\Omega
to be the quantity

\displaystyle|z-\Omega|=\inf_{w\in\Omega}|z-w|.

(2.1.1)

We will encounter many constants in our estimates below and while their exact values are not important, we will find it useful to record their dependence on parameters (e.g. the bounds on the geometry of
X
and previously defined constants). We indicate that a constant
C
explicitly depends on the
C^{k}
bounds on the geometry of
X
and some other parameters
a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}
by writing

\displaystyle C=C(X_{k},a_{1},\ldots,a_{N})

(2.1.2)

when it is first defined.

Consider the complex projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with homogeneous coordinates
[x:y]
. The standard round metric on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
(with area
4\pi
) is the Kähler metric with associated Kähler form
\omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
given (in the standard complex affine coordinate
z=x/y
) by

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}=\frac{2i\cdot dz\wedge d\bar{z}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{2}}

(2.1.3)

Similarly, define the standard flat metric on
\mathbb{C}
to be the Kähler metric with Kähler form
\omega_{\mathbb{C}}
given (in the standard complex coordinate
z
) by

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathbb{C}}=\textstyle\frac{i}{2}\cdot dz\wedge d\bar{z}

(2.1.4)

We use the notation
\ell(\cdot)
to denote the
g
-length of paths in
X
. We use
\mathcal{A}(\cdot)
to denote the
\omega
-action of a loop in
X
(assumed to be sufficiently small so that the symplectic action is defined unambiguously). Finally, given a compact Riemann surface (or, more generally, a projective prestable curve)
\Sigma
and a smooth map
u:\Sigma\to X
, we denote its energy by

\displaystyle E(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}|du|^{2}_{h}\,\text{dvol}_{h}

(2.1.5)

where
h
is any choice of a conformal metric on
\Sigma
. For an open subset
U\subset\Sigma
, we sometimes write
E(u,U)
to denote the energy of the map
u|_{U}:U\to X
. For any two points
x,x^{\prime}\in X
, we use the notation
{\text{dist}}(x,x^{\prime})
to denote the
g
-distance between
x
and
x^{\prime}
. We will also use the symbol
{\text{dist}}(\cdot,\cdot)
to denote distances in any metric space when the choice of the metric is clear from the context.

Local

We note the following fundamental estimates for pseudoholomorphic maps into

Lemma 2.1

(Elliptic bootstrapping)
.

There exist positive constants
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(X_{1})
and
c_{k}=c_{k}(X_{k+1})\geq 1
for each integer
k\geq 2
, with the following significance. If
u:B^{2}(r)\to X
is a
J
-holomorphic map, then

\displaystyle|(\nabla^{k}u)(0)|\leq c_{k}\|du\|_{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{r}+\frac{\|du\|_{\infty}}{\lambda_{0}}\right)^{k-1}.

(2.2.1)

Proof.

See Appendix
A.1
for the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.2

(Mean value inequality)
.

There exist positive constants
C\geq 1
and
q=q(X_{2})
with the following significance. If
u:B^{2}(r)\to X
is a
J
-holomorphic map with

E(u)\leq q
, then we have

\displaystyle r^{2}|du(0)|^{2}\leq C\cdot E(u).

(2.2.2)

Proof.

This is just Lemma 4.3.1(i) from
[
MS12
]
. See Appendix
A.2
for an alternate proof.
∎

Lemma 2.3

(Long cylinders of small energy)
.

There exist positive constants
c=c(X_{2})
and
l=l(X_{2})\leq\lambda_{0}
, with the following significance. Suppose
u:[R_{-},R_{+}]\times S^{1}\to X
is a
J
-holomorphic map with
\|du\|_{\infty}\leq l
. For each
s\in[R_{-},R_{+}]
, let
\gamma_{s}:S^{1}\to X
be the loop

. Then, for all
R_{-}\leq a\leq b\leq R_{+}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{[a,b]\times S^{1}}u^{*}\omega=\mathcal{A}(\gamma_{a})-\mathcal{A}(\gamma_{b}).

(2.2.3)

For each
s\in[R_{-},R_{+}]
, we also have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}(\gamma_{s})|\leq c\cdot\ell(\gamma_{s})^{2}.

(2.2.4)

Moreover, there exist constants
K_{k}=K_{k}(X_{k+1})\geq 1
for each integer
k\geq 1
such that the estimate

\displaystyle|(\nabla^{k}u)(s,t)|\leq K_{k}\sqrt{E(u)}(e^{s-R_{+}}+e^{R_{-}-s})

(2.2.5)

holds for all
(s,t)\in[R_{-}+1,R_{+}-1]\times S^{1}
, provided that
R_{+}-R_{-}\geq 2
.

Proof.

This is a variant of previously known statements. See
[
FO99
, Lemma 11.2]
and
[
MS12
, Lemma 4.7.3]
for example. For the proof, see Appendix
A.4
.
∎

3
Compact families of curves and holomorphic maps

In this section, we study the compactness properties of certain explicit families of curves and stable maps defined on them. The curves in such a family will be modeled on a fixed marked rooted tree. Please consult Appendix
C.1
for the notation and relevant background on trees which will be assumed below.

3.1
Curves

Definition 3.1
.

Fix a rooted tree
\mathcal{T}=(V,E,\partial)
and let
e_{0}
be its root edge and
v_{0}
the corresponding root vertex (see Definitions
C.5
and
C.6

\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{T}}
to be the vector space

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{T}}=\mathbb{C}^{E_{\text{int}}}\oplus\bigoplus_{v\in V}(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{v^{+}}

(3.1.1)

where
E_{\text{int}}\subset E
is the set of full edges. Let the coordinate functions corresponding to the
\mathbb{C}^{E_{\text{int}}}
factor be denoted by
\{\gamma_{e}\}
while the coordinates corresponding to an edge
e\in v^{+}
for any
v\in V
(see Definition
C.3
and Lemma
C.7
) are denoted by
\{(z_{v,e},\rho_{v,e})\}
. Given a positive path (see Definition
C.4
)
\mathcal{P}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
between vertices

and
v=v_{n}
and an edge

\displaystyle z^{u}_{v,e}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\textstyle\prod_{1\leq j<k}\gamma_{e_{j}}\rho_{v_{j},e_{j}}\right)z_{v_{k},e_{k}}

where we define
e_{i}
be the edge with endpoints

and
v_{i+1}
(for

) and

. Observe that for any vertex

and any edge

.
Define
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\subset\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{T}}
to be the Zariski open subset where the polynomial function

, defined by

\displaystyle F_{\mathcal{T}}=\prod(z^{u}_{v,e}-z^{u}_{v^{\prime},e^{\prime}})\cdot\prod\rho_{u,e}

(with

and

being the nearest common ancestor of

(with

). Next, define the closed subscheme
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}

\subset\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\times(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{V}

which, for each edge

(with

and

\displaystyle(x_{u}-z_{u,e}y_{u})y_{v}=\gamma_{e}\rho_{u,e}x_{v}y_{u}.

(3.1.4)

Define the projection map
\pi_{\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}
in the obvious fashion by dropping the

factor. We define
\pi_{\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}
to be the

. For any point

to be the fibre of

over

. For each

.

Let
p^{*}=(\{\gamma_{e}^{*}\},\{(z_{v,e}^{*},\rho_{v,e}^{*})\})\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}
be any point. Then, the fibre

(i)

Proof.

Lemma 3.3
.

The map
\pi_{\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}
of Definition
3.1
and
\sigma_{\mathcal{T}}
of Remark
3.2
(v)
together define a proper, flat algebraic family of prestable genus
0
curves with
|E_{\text{ext}}|
marked points. This family is a versal deformation of each of its fibres. If
\mathcal{T}
is a stable tree, then the fibres of this family are stable curves.

Proof.

We have already shown that for any
p^{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}
, the fibre
\mathcal{C}_{p^{*},\mathcal{T}}
is a prestable genus
0
and that
\{\sigma_{\mathcal{T}}(e,p^{*})\}_{e\in E_{\text{ext}}}
is a collection of
|E_{\text{int}}|

\displaystyle{\text{dist}}(x,x^{\prime})=\lim_{i}{\text{dist}}(f_{i}(s_{i}),f_{i}(s_{i}^{\prime}))\leq\Lambda_{\hat{w}}\lambda_{0}\cdot\lim_{i}{\text{dist}}_{\hat{w}}(s_{i},s_{i}^{\prime})=\Lambda_{\hat{w}}\lambda_{0}\cdot{\text{dist}}_{\hat{w}}(q,q^{\prime}).

The last estimate has the following implications. First, if

, then we get

. Thus,

is the graph of a set function

is compact. Moreover, the same estimate shows that

∎

Now, we are just left to show that

is
J
-holomorphic and that it satisfies the energy bound (

). Fix any smooth point

. Find a holomorphic coordinate neighborhood

, where

. Convergence of

to

. Moreover, the

), we conclude that

in

is

-holomorphic near all of its smooth points. Applying this argument at the points

, with

∎

3.3
Effective compactness

We will now estimate the size of the minimal

) in

Lemma 3.17
.

Given

, we have

\displaystyle\nu(\mathbb{P}^{1},\gamma)\leq\frac{2}{1-\cos(\frac{\gamma}{2})}\leq\frac{8\pi}{\gamma^{2}}.

(3.3.1)

Proof.

Note that the area of a disk of radius
\delta\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is given by

, while the total area of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is
4\pi
. Let
\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{N}\}
be a maximal collection of points such that
{\text{dist}}(q_{i},q_{j})\geq\gamma
for all

. From maximality, it follows that
\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{N}\}
is a
\gamma
-net. Now, since the open

-balls centred at the

are pairwise disjoint, we have

\displaystyle\nu(\mathbb{P}^{1},\gamma)\leq N\leq\frac{4\pi}{2\pi(1-\cos(\textstyle\frac{\gamma}{2}))}=\frac{2}{1-\cos(\frac{\gamma}{2})}

(3.3.2)

as desired. We conclude using

which holds for all

.
∎

Theorem 3.18

4.1
Gradient bound modulo bubbling

We will first prove Proposition

, which is a quantitative analogue of

, Theorem 4.6.1]
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\epsilon
be the constant introduced in Remark
4.1
. A
bubble configuration
is simply a pair
(T,\rho)
such that
T\subset\mathbb{C}
is a finite set (of
bubble points
) and
\rho:T\to[0,\infty)
is a function (called the
radius function
). The bubble configuration
(T,\rho)
is said to be
of type
\epsilon
if it has the following properties.

(i)

We have
\sup_{z\in T}|z|\leq\epsilon
.

(ii)

We have
\rho(z)\leq 4\epsilon
for all
z\in T
.

(iii)

For all
x,y\in T
such that
x\neq y
, we have the estimate

\displaystyle\rho(x)+\rho(y)\leq\textstyle\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}|x-y|

(4.1.1)

If we additionally have
0\in T
and equality in (i), we say that the bubble configuration
(T,\rho)
is
standard
. Note that if
|T|\geq 2
, then condition (ii) is superfluous in view of (i) and (iii).

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
A\geq 0
be a real number,
\ell\geq 0
be an integer and let
(\Sigma,x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell},f)
be a stable
J
-holomorphic map with
\Sigma\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
E(f)\leq A
. There is then an isomorphism
\Phi:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\Sigma
and a standard bubble configuration
(S,r)
of type
\epsilon
with the following properties.

(i)

If
\ell\geq 1
, then we have
\Phi([1:0])=x_{1}
and we define
S_{0}\subset\mathbb{C}
to be the set of
z
such that
\Phi([z:1])=x_{i}
for some
1<i\leq\ell
. If
\ell=0
, we define
S_{0}=\varnothing
. In both cases, we have
S_{0}\subset S
and
r^{-1}(0)=S_{0}
.

(ii)

Defining
f_{\Phi}(z)=(f\circ\Phi)([z:1])
, we have the energy identity

\displaystyle E(f_{\Phi},B^{2}(x,r(x)))=\lambda^{2}

(4.1.2)

for each
x\in S\setminus S_{0}
. In particular,
|S|\leq|S_{0}|+\lfloor A/\lambda^{2}\rfloor
. When
\ell=0
, we also have the energy bound

\displaystyle E(f_{\Phi},\mathbb{C}\setminus B^{2}(r^{\prime}))\geq\textstyle\frac{1}{9}C^{-1}(\lambda/r^{\prime})^{2}

(4.1.3)

for all
r^{\prime}\geq 1
, where
C
, resp.
\lambda
, are the constants from Lemma
2.2
, resp. Remark
4.1
.

(iii)

For all

, we have the gradient bound

\displaystyle|df_{\Phi}(z)|\leq\frac{8\sqrt{C}}{\epsilon^{2}}\cdot\frac{\lambda}{|z-S|}

(4.1.4)

where the constants
C,\lambda
are as in
(ii)
above and we are using the standard metric on
\mathbb{C}
.

The proof is carried out in six steps that follow.

Step 1
(Intial choice of gauge) There exists an isomorphism
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\Sigma
such that the
J
-holomorphic map
g:\mathbb{C}\to X
given by
g(z)=(f\circ\varphi)([z:1])
satisfies the following properties.

(i)

We have the estimate
\|dg\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda
, with equality if
\ell=0
.

(ii)

If
\ell=0
, then for all
0\leq r\leq 1
, we have the lower bound
E(g,B^{2}(r))\geq C^{-1}(\lambda r)^{2}
. If
\ell\geq 1
, then we have
\varphi([0:1])=x_{1}
and
\varphi^{-1}(x_{j})=[1:z_{j}]
for some
|z_{j}|\leq\epsilon
for each

.

Here, we are using the standard metric on
\mathbb{C}
and the constants
C,\lambda
are as before.

Proof.

If
\ell\geq 1
, start with any isomorphism
\psi:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\Sigma
for which
\psi([0:1])=x_{1}
. Now, taking the isomorphism
\varphi([z:w])=\psi([z:Tw])

gives the desired result.

If
\ell=0
, then start with any isomorphism
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\Sigma
for which the maximum
M
of
|d(f\circ\psi)|_{\omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}
occurs at
[0:1]
. Define
\varphi([z:w])=\psi([\lambda z:2Mw])
and notice that we now have
\lambda=|dg(0)|=\|dg\|_{\infty}
. Using (

) we get

and thus, by Lemma

, we get the asserted energy lower bound.
∎

Define

by

. Choose

such that

for

. In view of Step 1(i), we have the estimate

.

Step 2
(Energy threshold radii) We skip this step if

is constant. Assuming

is non-constant, for each

\mathfrak{r}(w)
to be the smallest number

such that

. Then, the function
\mathfrak{r}:\mathbb{C}\to(0,\infty)

(i)

We have
\inf_{w\in\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{r}(w)>0
.

(ii)

\mathfrak{r}
is

. In particular, it is continuous.

(iii)

At any

, we have
\mathfrak{r}(w)\cdot|dh(w)|\leq\lambda\sqrt{C}
.

Proof.

Since

is non-constant, we conclude that

, where the second inequality comes from (

) and (

). Indeed, if not, then we could apply Lemma

to large balls with arbitrary centres to conclude

. This shows that

is well-defined and takes values in
(0,\infty)
.
The bound (iii) now follows immediately by applying Lemma

to

.

To show (ii), we argue by contradiction. Suppose

are such that
\mathfrak{r}(x)<\mathfrak{r}(y)-|x-y|
. Then,

must be properly contained in

and both of them carry

of the energy

, which is a contradiction.

Finally, to prove (i), start by observing that

\displaystyle E(h,B^{2}(w,\mathfrak{r}(w)))\leq\pi\mathfrak{r}(w)^{2}\left(\frac{\gamma\lambda}{\sqrt{\pi}}\right)^{2}<\lambda^{2}

which contradicts the defintion of

and, if

(b)

(c)

We have the bound

Proof.

If
h
is constant, then we can simply take

and

. Now, assume that

is non-constant. Consider the set of all bubble configurations
(T,\rho)
satisfying conditions (i), (iii) of Definition

Let

be the set of

such that

and let

be the set of points

for all

, there exists

and
\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}|z-x|<\mathfrak{r}(z)+\rho(x)

Obviously, the set of such

is partially ordered (by inclusion of

and

). Moreover, since

\displaystyle|T\setminus T_{0}|\leq\lfloor E(h)/\lambda^{2}\rfloor\leq\lfloor A/\lambda^{2}\rfloor.

Step 4
(Gradient bound)
T

\displaystyle|dh(w)|\leq\frac{8\sqrt{C}}{\epsilon^{2}}\cdot\frac{\lambda}{|w-T|}.

4.2

To proceed further in the proof of Theorem

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose
(Z,d)
is a finite metric space and assume
(a_{i})_{i\geq 0}
is a sequence of positive real numbers such that
a_{i+1}\leq\frac{1}{2}a_{i}
for all

containing

such that the following properties hold.

(i)

For

in

|x-y|>a_{|Z^{\prime}|-1}
.

(ii)

There is a retraction

such that

for all
x\in Z
.

Moreover, in this case, the map
R
is uniquely determined by
Z^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Note that the subset
\{s\}
satisfies (i). Pick a subset
s\in Z^{\prime}\subset Z
satisfying (i), which is maximal with respect to inclusion. If
Z^{\prime}
violates (ii), then there exists a point

such that

. But then

also satisfies (i), and this is a contradiction to the maximality of
Z^{\prime}
. Finally, the uniqueness of
R
follows from the triangle inequality and the fact that
2a_{k}\leq a_{k-1}
for all
k\geq 1
.
∎

A.1
Elliptic bootstrapping (proof of Lemma

Let

\leq

\displaystyle|(\nabla^{k}v)(0)|\leq c_{k}\lambda_{0}

Now, consider a

are
\leq
the bounds on the geometry of

. Define

. We then get

\displaystyle r^{k}|(\nabla^{k}u)(0)|=|(\nabla^{k}v_{r})(0)|=\textstyle\frac{r}{\rho}|(\nabla^{k}v_{r})(0)|^{\prime}\leq c_{k}\lambda_{0}(\frac{r}{\rho})

A.3

\displaystyle r^{2}|du(0)|^{2}\leq\frac{G^{2}}{r^{2}}\int_{B^{2}(r)}|{\text{dist}}(u(\cdot),x)|^{2}

By the scale invariance of the result, we may assume that

(from §

\leq

. The

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}u=T(u)\cdot\partial u

, the Darboux theorem and the bounds on the geomery of
X
. Next, note that we have the energy estimate

\displaystyle E(u)\leq C^{\prime}\int_{[R_{-},R_{+}]\times S^{1}}u^{*}\omega=C^{\prime}\left(\mathcal{A}(\gamma_{R_{-}})-\mathcal{A}(\gamma_{R_{+}})\right)\leq 2C^{\prime}c(2\pi l)^{2}

(A.4.2)

where
C^{\prime}=C^{\prime}(X_{0})
is a positive constant which quantitatively expresses the fact that
\omega
tames
J
. Now, arguing as in
[
MS12
, Lemma 4.7.3]
, we can find constants
K^{\prime}=K^{\prime}(X_{2})
and
\mu=\mu(X_{2})
such that we have

\displaystyle|du(s,t)|\leq K^{\prime}\sqrt{E(u)}\left(e^{\mu(s-R_{+})}+e^{\mu(R_{-}-s)}\right)

(A.4.3)

for all
(s,t)\in[R_{-}+\frac{1}{4},R_{+}-\frac{1}{4}]\times S^{1}
. For completeness, we outline this argument in our context. For
0\leq r<\frac{R_{+}-R_{-}}{2}
, define the function
f(r)=E(u|_{[R_{-}+r,R_{+}-r]\times S^{1}})
. Differentiate
f
to get

\displaystyle-4\pi f^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle\geq\ell(\gamma_{R_{-}+r})^{2}+\ell(\gamma_{R_{+}-r})^{2}

(A.4.4)

\displaystyle\geq c^{-1}\left(\mathcal{A}(\gamma_{R_{-}+r})-\mathcal{A}(\gamma_{R_{+}-r})\right)

(A.4.5)

\displaystyle=c^{-1}\int_{[R_{-}+r,R_{+}-r]\times S^{1}}u^{*}\omega

(A.4.6)

\displaystyle\geq(C^{\prime}c)^{-1}f(r)

(A.4.7)

for all
0\leq r<\frac{R_{+}-R_{-}}{2}
. Integrating this inequality for
f
, combining with Lemma
2.2
and requiring
2C^{\prime}c(2\pi\ell)^{2}\leq q
(where
q
is the constant from Lemma
2.2
) gives the estimate (
A.4.3
). Integrating the estimate for
|du(s,t)|
shows that
u|_{[R_{-}+\frac{1}{4},R_{+}-\frac{1}{4}]\times S^{1}}
maps into a coordinate ball in
X
where we have bounds on the matrices representing
\omega,J,g
(and their inverses). For simplicity of notation in what follows, let us assume that this is actually true for
u
on the whole of
[R_{-},R_{+}]\times S^{1}
. We can adjust the choice of the coordinate system a little more so that we have
J(0)=i
and

\displaystyle\langle u\rangle=\int_{S^{1}}u(\textstyle\frac{R_{-}+R_{+}}{2},\theta)\,d\theta=0

(A.4.8)

For the rest of the argument, we work exclusively in this coordinate system. Since shrinking
l
reduces the
L^{\infty}
bound for
u
in this coordinate system, it now suffices to prove the following statement. Lemma
2.1
will then give the higher derivative bounds, completing the proof of Lemma
2.3
.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
J
be an almost complex structure on
B^{2n}(1)\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
J(0)=i
. Let

\displaystyle v:[R_{-},R_{+}]\times S^{1}\to B^{2n}(1)

(A.4.9)

be a
J
-holomorphic map satisfying
\langle v\rangle=0
. There exists a constant
\eta
, depending only on the
C^{2}
bounds on
J
, and an absolute constant
H
with the following significance. If
\|v\|_{\infty}\leq\eta
and
\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}\leq\eta
then we have

\displaystyle|\nabla v(s,t)|\leq H\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}(e^{s-R_{+}}+e^{R_{-}-s})

(A.4.10)

for all
(s,t)\in[R_{-}+\frac{1}{2},R_{+}-\frac{1}{2}]\times S^{1}
.

Proof.

Set
R=\frac{1}{2}(R_{-}+R_{+})
and define, for
0\leq r<R
, the non-negative function
g
by

\displaystyle g(r)=\int_{[r+R_{-},R_{+}-r]\times S^{1}}|\partial_{t}v|^{2}\,dt\,ds

(A.4.11)

and note that we have

\displaystyle-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}g^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\int_{S^{1}}\left(|\partial_{t}v(R_{+}-r,t)|^{2}+|\partial_{t}v(r+R_{-},t)|^{2}\right)dt

(A.4.12)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\partial[r+R_{-},R_{+}-r]\times S^{1}}v^{*}\lambda_{\text{std}}

(A.4.13)

\displaystyle=\int_{[r+R_{-},R_{+}-r]\times S^{1}}v^{*}\omega_{\text{std}}

(A.4.14)

\displaystyle=\int_{[r+R_{-},R_{+}-r]\times S^{1}}\langle i\partial_{s}v,\partial_{t}v\rangle\,dt\,ds

(A.4.15)

\displaystyle\geq g(r)\cdot(1-c^{\prime}\|v\|_{\infty})

(A.4.16)

where
c^{\prime}=\|J\|_{C^{1}}
and the last estimate follows from
i\partial_{s}v=(1-i(J(v)-i))\partial_{t}v
. Taking
\eta\leq\frac{1}{2}c^{\prime-1}
, we get
g(r)\leq e^{-r}\|\partial_{t}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}
. Repeating the same computation with
\partial_{s}v
instead of
\partial_{t}v
and adding the results gives the energy decay estimate

\displaystyle\int_{[r+R_{-},R_{+}-r]\times S^{1}}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dt\,ds\leq e^{-r}\int_{[R_{-},R_{+}]\times S^{1}}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dt\,ds.

(A.4.17)

Using Lemma
2.2
and requiring
\eta^{2}\leq q
gives a decay estimate for
|\nabla v|
. Integrating this and combining with the assumption that
\langle v\rangle=0
gives the estimate

\displaystyle|v(s,t)|+|(\nabla v)(s,t)|\leq A(e^{\frac{1}{2}(s-R_{+})}+e^{\frac{1}{2}(R_{-}-s)})\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}

(A.4.18)

for all
(s,t)\in[R_{-}+\frac{1}{4},R_{+}-\frac{1}{4}]\times S^{1}
, where
A
is an absolute constant. Following arguments modeled on the proof of
[
HT09
, Lemma 2.3]
, we will improve this decay estimate. For convenience, let us assume that (
A.4.18
) holds on all of
[R_{-},R_{+}]\times S^{1}
. Fourier expand
v
into eigenfunctions of the operator
L=i\partial_{t}
on
S^{1}
to get

\displaystyle v(s,t)=\sum_{m}v_{m}(s)e^{-imt}.

(A.4.19)

Let
\Pi_{\pm}
and
\Pi_{0}
be the orthogonal projections from
L^{2}(S^{1})
onto the
\pm
and
0
eigenspaces of the operator
L
and define
v_{\alpha}=\Pi_{\alpha}v
for
\alpha\in\{+,-,0\}
. The Cauchy-Riemann equation for
v
translates to

\displaystyle\partial_{s}v+Lv=S(v)\cdot Lv

(A.4.20)

where
S(\cdot)=(J(\cdot)-i)i
and thus, satisfies
|S(v)|\leq c^{\prime}|v|
pointwise. Define the functions

\displaystyle f_{\pm}(s)=\pm\textstyle\frac{1}{2}\langle Lv_{\pm},v_{\pm}\rangle_{L^{2}(\{s\}\times S^{1})}

(A.4.21)

for
s\in[R_{-},R_{+}]
and note that applying
\Pi_{\pm}
to (
A.4.20
) and applying
\langle\cdot,Lv_{\pm}\rangle_{L^{2}(\{s\}\times S^{1})}
gives

\displaystyle f_{+}^{\prime}(s)+\|Lv_{+}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\hat{\varepsilon}(s)\left(\|Lv_{+}\|^{2}+\|Lv_{-}\|^{2}\right)

(A.4.22)

\displaystyle-f_{-}^{\prime}(s)+\|Lv_{-}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\hat{\varepsilon}(s)\left(\|Lv_{+}\|^{2}+\|Lv_{-}\|^{2}\right)

(A.4.23)

where
\hat{\varepsilon}(s)=c^{\prime}A\eta(e^{\frac{1}{2}(s-R_{+})}+e^{\frac{1}{2}(R_{-}-s)})\leq\frac{1}{3}
provided
\eta
is chosen small enough. Taking
\gamma(s)=\frac{\hat{\varepsilon}(s)}{1-\hat{\varepsilon}(s)}
, and adding (
A.4.22
) to
\gamma(s)
times (
A.4.23
) gives

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{+}-\gamma f^{\prime}_{-}+(1-\gamma)\|Lu_{+}\|^{2}\leq 0

(A.4.24)

Now, noting that
\|Lu_{+}\|^{2}\geq 2f_{+}
and that
|\gamma^{\prime}|\leq 2\gamma(1-\gamma)
for
s\in[R_{-},R_{+}]
, we get

\displaystyle(f_{+}-\gamma f_{-})^{\prime}+2(1-\gamma)(f_{+}-\gamma f_{-})\leq 0.

(A.4.25)

Observe that
\int_{R_{-}}^{R_{+}}\gamma(s)\,ds\leq c^{\prime\prime}
for a constant
c^{\prime\prime}
independent of
R_{\pm}
and so, we get

\displaystyle(f_{+}-\gamma f_{-})(s)

\displaystyle\leq e^{2(c^{\prime\prime}+R_{-}-s)}(f_{+}-\gamma f_{-})(R_{-})

(A.4.26)

\displaystyle(f_{-}-\gamma f_{+})(s)

\displaystyle\leq e^{2(c^{\prime\prime}+s-R_{+})}(f_{-}-\gamma f_{+})(R_{+})

(A.4.27)

by integrating the previous estimate. Rearranging the last two estimates, we get

\displaystyle f_{\pm}(s)\leq\frac{e^{2c^{\prime\prime}}}{1-\gamma(s)^{2}}\left(e^{\pm 2(R_{\mp}-s)}f_{\pm}(R_{\mp})+\gamma(s)e^{\pm 2(s-R_{\pm})}f_{\mp}(R_{\pm})\right).

(A.4.28)

Noting that
2f_{\pm}(R_{\mp})\leq\|Lv_{\pm}\|^{2}(R_{\mp})\leq 2\pi\cdot(2A\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}})^{2}
by (
A.4.18
), we get

\displaystyle\|v_{+}\|^{2}+\|v_{-}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2f_{+}+2f_{-}

(A.4.29)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e^{2c^{\prime\prime}}}{1-\gamma(s)}\cdot 8\pi A^{2}\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\cdot\left(e^{2(s-R_{+})}+e^{2(R_{-}-s)}\right)

(A.4.30)

\displaystyle\leq A^{\prime 2}\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\cdot\left(e^{2(s-R_{+})}+e^{2(R_{-}-s)}\right)

(A.4.31)

with
A^{\prime}
denoting an absolute constant which may become bigger with subsequent appearances. Next, applying
\Pi_{0}
to (
A.4.20
) gives
v_{0}^{\prime}(s)=\Pi_{0}(S(v)Lv)
, which implies

\displaystyle|v_{0}^{\prime}(s)|

\displaystyle\leq c^{\prime}|v|\cdot\|Lv\|

(A.4.32)

\displaystyle\leq 2\pi c^{\prime}A^{2}\eta\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}\left(e^{\frac{1}{2}(s-R_{+})}+e^{\frac{1}{2}(R_{-}-s)}\right)^{2}

(A.4.33)

\displaystyle\leq A^{\prime}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}\left(e^{s-R_{+}}+e^{R_{-}-s}\right)

(A.4.34)

and integrating this (recalling that
\langle v\rangle=0
), we get the estimate

\displaystyle|v_{0}(s)|^{2}\leq A^{\prime 2}\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\cdot\left(e^{2(s-R_{+})}+e^{2(R_{-}-s)}\right).

(A.4.35)

Now combining (
A.4.31
) and (
A.4.35
) we find upper bounds for
\int_{[s-\frac{1}{4},s+\frac{1}{4}]\times S^{1}}|v|^{2}\,ds\,dt
for
s\in[R_{-}+\frac{1}{2},R_{+}-\frac{1}{2}]
and applying Lemma
A.2
gives the desired bound for
|(\nabla v)(s,t)|
with a suitable absolute constant
H
as in the statement.
∎

Appendix B
Hausdorff distance

In this appendix, we recollect some properties of Hausdorff distances. Fix a complete metric space
(Z,d)
.

Definition B.1
.

The
Hausdorff distance
pseudo-metric
d_{H}
on the power set
2^{Z}
is defined as

\displaystyle d_{H}(A,B)=\max\left\{\sup_{x\in A}\inf_{y\in B}d(x,y),\sup_{y\in B}\inf_{x\in A}d(x,y)\right\}

(B.0.1)

for any two subsets
A,B\subset Z
. Define
\mathcal{K}(Z)
to the space of compact subsets of
Z
endowed with
d_{H}
. On the subset
\mathcal{K}(Z)\subset 2^{Z}
,
d_{H}
is a genuine metric.

Definition B.2
.

Given a constant
\gamma>0
and a subset
Y\subset Z
, we call
Y
an
\gamma
-net for

Z
if and only if

\displaystyle d_{H}(Y,Z)<\gamma.

(B.0.2)

Define
\nu(Z,\gamma)
to be the minimum possible cardinality
|Y|
of a
\gamma
-net
Y\subset Z
. The metric space
Z
is said to be totally bounded if
\nu(Z,\gamma)
is finite for every choice of
\gamma>0
.

Remark B.3
.

Recall that a metric space is compact if and only if it is complete and totally bounded.

Lemma B.4
.

Suppose
\{Z_{i}\subset Z\}_{i\in I}
is a cover of
Z
by finitely many subsets. Then, for any
\gamma>0
, we have

\displaystyle\nu(Z,\gamma)\leq\sum_{i\in I}\nu(Z_{i},\gamma)

(B.0.3)

Proof.

Given a
\gamma
-net
Y_{i}
for each
Z_{i}
, we define
Y=\bigcup_{i}Y_{i}
. Then,
Y
is a
\gamma
-net for
Z=\bigcup_{i}Z_{i}
.
∎

Lemma B.5
.

Let
\gamma>0
and
K\subset Z
. Then, given any
\gamma
-net
Y\subset Z
, there exists a subset
Y^{\prime}\subset Y
and a
2\gamma
-net
K^{\prime}
for
K
such that

\displaystyle|K^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq|Y^{\prime}|

(B.0.4)

\displaystyle d_{H}(K,Y^{\prime})

\displaystyle<\gamma

(B.0.5)

Proof.

Choose
d_{H}(Y,Z)<\gamma^{\prime}<\gamma
. Define
Y^{\prime}\subset Y
to be the subset of points
y\in Y
such that
d(y,K)<\gamma^{\prime}
. For each
y\in Y^{\prime}
, choose a point
x(y)\in K
such that
d(y,x(y))<\gamma^{\prime}
. Define
K^{\prime}=\{x(y)\,|\,y\in Y^{\prime}\}
. It is now immediate that
K^{\prime}\subset K
is a
2\gamma
-net and that
|K^{\prime}|\leq|Y^{\prime}|
. Moreover, we have
d_{H}(K,Y^{\prime})\leq\gamma^{\prime}<\gamma
.
∎

Corollary B.6
.

For any
\gamma>0
, we have the estimate

\displaystyle\nu(\mathcal{K}(Z),\gamma)\leq 2^{\nu(Z,\gamma)}

(B.0.6)

Proof.

Lemma
B.5
shows that if
Y
is a
\gamma
-net for
Z
, then the power set
2^{Y}
is a
\gamma
-net for
\mathcal{K}(Z)
.
∎

Corollary B.7
.

Let
\gamma>0
and
K\subset Z
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\nu(K,2\gamma)\leq\nu(Z,\gamma)

(B.0.7)

Proof.

We simply note that
|K^{\prime}|\leq|Y^{\prime}|\leq|Y|
in Lemma
B.5
.
∎

Lemma B.8
.

d_{H}
is a complete metric on
\mathcal{K}(Z)
.

Proof.

Let
\{K_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
is a
d_{H}
-Cauchy sequence of compact subsets of
Z
for which wish to construct a
d_{H}
-limit
K_{\infty}
. First, notice that the union
\hat{K}=\bigcup K_{i}
is totally bounded. Indeed, given any
\gamma>0
, first choose
N=N(\gamma)
such that
d_{H}(K_{i},K_{j})\leq\frac{1}{2}\gamma
for
i,j\geq N
. For each
1\leq i\leq N
, let
Y_{i}
be a finite
\frac{1}{2}\gamma
-net for
K_{i}
. Then,
Y_{1}\cup\cdots\cup Y_{N}
is a
\gamma
-net for
K
. Thus, replacing
Z
by the closure of
\hat{K}
(which is complete and totally bounded and thus, compact by Remark
B.3
), we may safely assume that
Z
is compact.

We can now ignore the fact that
\{K_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
is Cauchy and simply produce a convergent subsequence. Using Corollary
B.7
, we see that there exists an increasing sequence of positive integers
\{N_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
and sequences
\{x_{i,j}\}_{j\geq 1}\subset K_{i}
for each
i\geq 1
with the following significance: for each integer
n\geq 1
, the collection
\{x_{i,j}\}_{j\leq N_{n}}\subset K_{i}
forms a
\frac{1}{n}
-net. By a diagonal argument, we can now pass to a subsequence, still denoted
\{K_{i}\}
, and construct a collection
\{x_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}\subset Z
such that
x_{j}=\lim_{i\to\infty}x_{i,j}
for each
i\geq 1
. Defining
K_{\infty}\subset Z
to be the closure of
\{x_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
, it easily follows that
\lim_{i\to\infty}d_{H}(K_{i},K_{\infty})=0
.
∎

Definition B.9
.

Suppose
T,Z,W
are complete metric spaces and
\mathfrak{X}\subset T\times Z
is closed subset, proper over
T
. For any
t\in T
, define
\mathfrak{X}_{t}\subset Z
to be the inverse image of
\mathfrak{X}
under the map
z\mapsto(t,z)
. Given a number
\Lambda>0
, define
\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{X}/T,W)
to be the set of pairs
(t,f:\mathfrak{X}_{t}\to W)
with
t\in T
and
f
being a continuous map with Lipschitz constant
\leq\Lambda
. Define a metric on
\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{X}/T,W)
by setting

\displaystyle d((t,f),(t^{\prime}f^{\prime}))=\max\{d(t,t^{\prime}),d_{H}(\Gamma_{f},\Gamma_{f^{\prime}})\}

(B.0.8)

where
\Gamma_{f}
(resp.
\Gamma_{f^{\prime}}
) is the graph of
f
(resp.
f^{\prime}
), viewed as a subset
Z\times W
. Here, the metric we use on
Z\times W
is
d((z,w),(z^{\prime},w^{\prime}))=\max\{d(z,z^{\prime}),d(w,w^{\prime})\}
.

Lemma B.10
.

If
\Lambda\geq 1
, then given any
\delta>0
, we can cover
\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{X}/T,W)
by a collection
\{\mathcal{F}_{i}\}_{i\in I_{\delta}}
of subsets satisfying the conditions

\displaystyle\sup_{i\in I_{\delta}}\text{diam}(\mathcal{F}_{i})

\displaystyle<4\delta

(B.0.9)

\displaystyle|I_{\delta}|=\nu(T,\delta)\cdot(1

\displaystyle+\nu(W,\delta))^{\nu(Z,\delta/\Lambda)}

(B.0.10)

and thus, for any subset
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{X}/T,W)
, we have

\displaystyle\nu(\mathcal{F},4\delta)\leq|I_{\delta}|.

(B.0.11)

Proof.

Let
A\subset T
,
C\subset W
be
\delta
-nets and let
B\subset Z
be a
(\delta/\Lambda)
-net. Choose
0<\gamma<\delta
such that the previous sentence remains true when we replace
\delta
by
\gamma
. Define
D=C\sqcup\{*\}
. Now, given any pair
(a,h)\in A\times D^{B}
consisting of an element
a\in A
and a set map
h:B\to D
, define the subset

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{(a,h)}\subset\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{X}/T,W)

(B.0.12)

as follows. It consists of elements
(t,f:\mathfrak{X}_{t}\to W)
such that
d(t,a)\leq\gamma
and such that for each
b\in B
,

(i)

if
h(b)=*
, then we have
d(b,\mathfrak{X}_{t})>\gamma/\Lambda
and,

(ii)

if
h(b)=c\in C
, then there exists a point
b_{t}\in\mathfrak{X}_{t}
such that
d(b,b_{t})\leq\gamma/\Lambda
and
d(f(b_{t}),c)\leq\gamma
.

Since
A,B,C
are
\gamma
,
(\gamma/\Lambda)
,
\gamma
-nets in
T,Z,W
(resp.), it follows that the sets
\mathcal{F}_{(a,h)}
ranging over all possible
(a,h)
cover the space
\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{X}/T,W)
. We will therefore be done if we estimate the diameter of each of these sets. Indeed, let
(t,f)
and
(s,g)
both lie in
\mathcal{F}_{(a,h)}
. We note that

\displaystyle d(t,s)\leq d(t,a)+d(a,s)\leq 2\gamma

(B.0.13)

and thus, we are left to show that
d_{H}(\Gamma_{f},\Gamma_{g})\leq 4\gamma
. Since the situation is symmetric, it will be enough to show that for any
x\in\mathfrak{X}_{t}
, we can find
y\in\mathfrak{X}_{s}
such that
d(x,y)\leq 4\gamma
and
d(f(x),g(y))\leq 4\gamma
. Starting with
x\in\mathfrak{X}_{t}
, first choose
b\in B
such that
d(x,b)\leq\gamma/\Lambda
. This is possible since
B\subset Z
is a
(\gamma/\Lambda)
-net. It follows from (i) that
c=h(b)\in C
and that we can find
b_{t}\in\mathfrak{X}_{t}
as in (ii). Similarly, we can use (ii) to find
y\in\mathfrak{X}_{s}
such that
d(b,y)\leq\gamma/\Lambda
and
d(g(y),c)\leq\gamma
. Now, observe that

\displaystyle d(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq d(x,b)+d(b,y)\leq 2\gamma/\Lambda\leq 2\gamma

(B.0.14)

\displaystyle d(f(x),g(y))

\displaystyle\leq d(f(x),c)+d(c,g(y))

(B.0.15)

\displaystyle\leq d(f(x),f(b_{t}))+d(f(b_{t}),c)+\gamma

(B.0.16)

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\cdot d(x,b_{t})+2\gamma

(B.0.17)

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\cdot(d(x,b)+d(b,b_{t}))+2\gamma\leq 4\gamma

(B.0.18)

and this completes the proof.
∎

Appendix C
Trees and curves

In this appendix, we establish the notation and some basic properties of (stable, marked) rooted trees. We also supply the proofs of Lemmas from §
3
.

C.1
Definitions and basic properties

Definition C.1
.

A
graph
(
with half edges
) is a triple
(V,E,\partial)
where
V
is a finite set (with elements called
vertices
),
E
is a finite set (with elements called
edges
) and a
boundary map

\partial:E\to 2^{V}
such that for all
e\in E
, we have
1\leq|\partial(e)|\leq 2
. The elements of
\partial(e)
, for
e\in E
, are called the
endpoints
of
e
. An edge
e\in E
is called a
half
or
full
edge when it has
1
or
2
endpoints respectively. For a vertex
v\in V
, we define its
degree
by
\deg(v)

=|\{e\in E\,|\,v\in\partial(e)\}|
.

Definition C.2
.

Given two graphs
G=(V,E,\partial)
and
G^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime},\partial^{\prime})
, an
isomorphism

\varphi:G\to G^{\prime}
is a pair of bijections
\varphi_{V}:V\to V^{\prime}
and
\varphi_{E}:E\to E^{\prime}
such that
2^{\varphi_{V}}\circ\partial=\partial^{\prime}\circ\varphi_{E}
.

Definition C.3
.

Given a graph
G=(V,E,\partial)
and an edge
e\in E
, an
orientation on

e
is an injective map
\mathfrak{o}_{e}:\partial(e)\to\{-1,+1\}
. An endpoint
v
of
e
is called
positive
(resp.
negative
), written as
v=e^{+}
(resp.
v=e^{-}
), if
\mathfrak{o}_{e}(v)=+1
(resp.
-1
). An
orientation
of
G
is a choice
\mathfrak{o}=\{\mathfrak{o}_{e}\}_{e\in E}
of orientations for each the edges of
G
. When
G
is equipped with an orientation
\mathfrak{o}
, for any vertex
v
, we use
v^{+}
(resp.
v^{-}
) to denote the set of edges
e\in E
for which
v\in\partial(e)
and
\mathfrak{o}_{e}(v)=-1
(resp.
\mathfrak{o}_{e}(v)=+1
).

Definition C.4
.

A
path

Definition C.5

Lemma C.15
.

For an integer

, define

to be the number of stable rooted trees, up to isomorphism preserving the root edge, with

\displaystyle T_{n}\leq\frac{1}{4\sqrt{3}}\left(\frac{2^{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^{3}

For

, define

to be the number of maximal stable rooted trees, up to isomorphism preserving the root, with

\displaystyle T_{n}\leq 2^{n-2}T^{\prime}_{n}

which follows by observing that any stable rooted tree can be formed by contracting a subset of full edges in some maximal stable tree.

can alternatively be described as the number of ways of multiplying

(where the order of the

(
[

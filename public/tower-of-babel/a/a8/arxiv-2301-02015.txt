Scaling transition for singular linear random fields on ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} : spectral approach
Donatas Surgailis

Abstract
We study partial sums limits of linear random fields X X on ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} with spectral density f ⁡ ( 𝒙 ) f(\boldsymbol{x}) tending to ∞ , 0 \infty,\,0 or to both (along different subsequences) as 𝒙 → ( 0 , 0 ) \boldsymbol{x}\to(0,0) . The above behaviors are termed (spectrum) long-range dependence, negative dependence, and long-range negative dependence, respectively, and assume an anisotropic power-law form of f ⁡ ( 𝒙 ) f(\boldsymbol{x}) near the origin. The partial sums are taken over rectangles whose sides increase as λ \lambda and λ γ \lambda^{\gamma} , for any fixed γ > 0 \gamma>0 . We prove that for above X X the partial sums or scaling limits exist for any γ > 0 \gamma>0 and exhibit a scaling transition at some γ = γ 0 > 0 \gamma=\gamma_{0}>0 ; moreover, the ‘unbalanced’ scaling limits ( γ ≠ γ 0 \gamma\neq\gamma_{0} ) are Fractional Brownian Sheet with Hurst parameters taking values from [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] . The paper extends [ 24 , 19 , 28 ] to the above spectrum dependence conditions and/or more general values of Hurst parameters. Keywords: Linear random field, Gaussian random field, spectral density, long-range dependence, negative dependence, long-range negative dependence, hyperbolic dependence, self-similarity, anisotropic scaling, scaling transition, fractional Brownian sheet,

Introduction

A stationary random field (RF)
X=\{X(\boldsymbol{t});\boldsymbol{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}
with finite second moment and covariance
r(\boldsymbol{t})={\rm Cov}(X({\bf 0}),X(\boldsymbol{t}))
is said
(i) long-range dependent (LRD) if
\sum_{\boldsymbol{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|r(\boldsymbol{t})|=\infty
; (ii) short-range dependent (SRD)
if
\sum_{\boldsymbol{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|r(\boldsymbol{t})|<\infty,\

\sum_{\boldsymbol{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}r(\boldsymbol{t})\neq 0
, and (iii) negatively dependent (ND)
if
\sum_{\boldsymbol{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|r(\boldsymbol{t})|<\infty,\ \sum_{\boldsymbol{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}r(\boldsymbol{t})=0

(for RF indexed by continuous argument, the above concepts are analogously defined with sums replaced by integrals over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
).
The above classification, albeit not completely satisfactory, is important in limit theorems, see
[
13
,
16
,
28
]
. Related
but not equivalent definitions of LRD, SRD and ND are given in terms of spectrum or moving-average coefficients of RF. In the sequel we refer
to the above covariance or moving-average characterizations as
spatial domain
properties, to be distinguished from
frequency domain (spectrum)
characterizations used in the literature. A very general form of such frequency domain
concepts assumes the existence of spectral density
f(\boldsymbol{u}),\boldsymbol{u}\in\Pi^{d}:=[-\pi,\pi]^{d}
which is bounded outside of the origin and
such that (i’)
\lim_{\boldsymbol{u}\to{\bf 0}}f(\boldsymbol{u})=\infty
(spectrum LRD),
(ii’)
\lim_{\boldsymbol{u}\to{\bf 0}}f(\boldsymbol{u})>0
(spectrum SRD), and
(iii’)
\lim_{\boldsymbol{u}\to{\bf 0}}f(\boldsymbol{u})=0
(spectrum ND). Moreover, in cases (i’) and (iii’) it is often additionally
assumed that spectral density varies regularly as
\boldsymbol{u}\to 0

[
13
,
22
,
25
]
. It is clear that in dimensions
d\geq 2

a function can increase regularly to
\infty
along one direction and vanish along another one. Such a behavior is not
reflected in (i’)-(iii’), calling for a new concept
(iv’)
0=\liminf_{\boldsymbol{u}\to{\bf 0}}f(\boldsymbol{u})<\limsup_{\boldsymbol{u}\to{\bf 0}}f(\boldsymbol{u})=\infty
which we term

spectrum long-range negative dependence

(spectrum LRND).
Properties (i’), (iii’) and (iv’) can be jointly dubbed as
singular
behaviors, the singularity limited to the origin and including both infinite and/or zero limits.

The present paper studies anisotropic partial sums limits
for a class of stationary linear RFs
X
on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with spectrum
LRD/ND/LRND properties. The partial sums

S_{\lambda,\gamma}(\boldsymbol{x}):=\ \sum_{\boldsymbol{t}\in K_{[\lambda x_{1},\lambda^{\gamma}x_{2}]}}X(\boldsymbol{t}),\hskip 20.00003pt\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}

(1.1)

are taken over rectangles
K_{[\lambda x_{1},\lambda^{\gamma}x_{2}]}:=\{\boldsymbol{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:1\leq t_{1}\leq\lambda x_{1},1\leq t_{2}\leq\lambda^{\gamma}x_{2}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}

whose sides increase as
O(\lambda)
and
O(\lambda^{\gamma})
as

\lambda\to\infty
, for a fixed

\gamma>0
. Our main object

\end{array}\ V_{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{x}),\hskip 10.00002pt\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda\to\infty,

(1.2)

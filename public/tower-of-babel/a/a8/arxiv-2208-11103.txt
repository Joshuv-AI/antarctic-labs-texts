1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study a kind of
k
-Hessian type partial differential equations of the form,

(1.1)

S_{k}\left[D^{2}u-A(x,u,Du)\right]=B(x,u,Du),\quad{\rm in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n},

where
Du
and
D^{2}u
denote the gradient vector and the Hessian matrix of
u
,
A
is a
n\times n
symmetric matrix function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
B:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a scalar valued function.
For
k=1,\cdots,n
, the
k
-Hessian operator
S_{k}
denotes the
k
-th elementary symmetric function defined by

S_{k}[r]:=S_{k}\left(\lambda(r)\right)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}},

where
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
denote the eigenvalues of the matrix
r
.

The classical model of (
1.1
) with
A=0
has been investigated in the pioneering paper of Caffarelli, Nirenberg and Spruck
[
4
]
.
For extensive studies and outstanding results on the standard Hessian equation
S_{k}(D^{2}u)=B(x,u,Du)
, see, for example,
[
8
,
31
,
32
]
and the references therein.
If
A\neq 0
, for
k=n
, equation (
1.1
) corresponds to a class of Monge-Ampère type equations arising in optimal transportation
[
26
]
and geometrical optics problems
[
14
,
35
]
, which have been studied in
[
25
,
19
,
15
]
. For general
k
, if
A
depends on
x
, a class of
k
-Hessian type equations (
1.1
) on Riemannian manifolds are studied by Urbas
[
33
]
, Guan
[
13
,
12
]
, and others;
if
A
depends on
Du
, the
k
-Hessian type equations (
1.1
) is related to conformally invariant elliptic equations
[
24
]
and the
k
-Yamabe problems in conformal geometry
[
34
]
;
if
A
depends on
x
,
u
,
Du
, the global regularity for Dirichlet and oblique boundary value problems of augmented Hessian equation (
1.1
) are studied in
[
20
,
21
]
.

More specifically, when
k=1
,
A=0
and
B(x,u,Du)=f(u)
, (
1.1
) reduces to the classical Poisson equation

(1.2)

\Delta u=f(u),\quad{\rm in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n}.

It is worthwhile to point out that Keller
[
22
]
and Osserman
[
27
]
simultaneously and independently proved that when
f
is a positive, nondecreasing and continuous function, equation (
1.2
) admits a subsolution which exists in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if and only if

(1.3)

\int^{+\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}f(t)\,{\rm d}t\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,{\rm d}\tau=+\infty,

where and later we omit the lower limit of integral to admit an arbitrary positive number, and the definition of subsolutions will be introduced later in this section.
The growth condition on
f
at infinity (
1.3
) is the celebrated Keller-Osserman condition.
For general
k
, Ji and Bao
[
17
]
extended the classical Keller-Osserman condition (
1.3
) to the standard Hessian equation

(1.4)

S_{k}^{\frac{1}{k}}(D^{2}u)=f(u)\quad{\rm in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n},

where
S_{k}^{\frac{1}{k}}
denotes the
k
-th root of
S_{k}
.
Specifically, for
2\leq k<n
and
k=n
, the generalized Keller-Osserman conditions for the solvability of subsolutions to (
1.1
) with
A=-\alpha I
and
B=f^{k}(u)
, which include (
1.4
) as a very special case, are established in
[
10
,
11
]
respectively.
For the generalized Keller-Osserman condition on
k
-Yamabe type equation, one can see
[
3
]
.
In the fully nonlinear framework, if we replace the
k
-Hessian operator of (
1.1
) with more general second-order degenerate elliptic operators, analogous results have been more recently obtained in
[
5
,
7
]
.
Remarkably, condition (
1.3
) is often used to study the boundary blow-up problems, see for example
[
18
,
28
,
29
]
.

As far as
k
-Hessian type equations with gradient terms are concerned, in the case of
k=1
, some relevant research studied the following boundary blow-up problem

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta u\pm g(|Du|)=f(u),\quad{\rm in}\ \Omega,\\
&u\to+\infty,\quad\ {\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)\to 0,\end{aligned}\right.

which appears in stochastic control problems with state constraints.
Lasry and Lions
[
23
]
first considered the case of
\pm g(|Du|)=-|Du|^{p}
and
f(u)=\lambda u+h
, where
p>1
,
\lambda>0
and
h
is a given smooth function in
\Omega
.
In this case, the equation in (
1.5
) is a very particular case of the so-called Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equations.
Subsequently, for
g(|Du|)=|Du|^{q}
,
q>0
, problem (
1.5
) was considered by Bandle and Giarrusso in
[
2
]
.
For general case, the existence and nonexistence of nonnegative solutions to problem (
1.5
) are proved in
[
1
]
, depending on new integral conditions of Keller-Osserman type involving
f
and
g
.
More interesting to us is that based on the degenerate maximal Pucci operator
\mathcal{M}_{0,1}^{+}
and the
k
-partial Laplacian operator
\mathcal{P}_{k}^{+}
, Capuzzo Dolcetta, Leoni and Vitolo
[
6
]
studied the more general fully nonlinear degenerate elliptic inequality with gradient term

(1.6)

F(x,D^{2}u)\geq f(u)+g(u)|Du|^{q},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

for
q\in(0,2]
, and gave a necessary and sufficient condition for the solvability of (
1.6
) which extended the classical Keller-Osserman condition to semilinear equations.
We note that in recent research paper involving
k
-Hessian operator with gradient terms, Cui
[
9
]
proved the existence of entire
k
-convex radial solutions to the Dirichlet problem of
k
-Hessian system

\left\{\begin{aligned} &S_{k}(\lambda(D^{2}u+\mu|Du|I))=p(|x|)f_{1}(u)f_{2}(v),\quad x\in B_{1}(0),\\
&S_{l}(\lambda(D^{2}v+\mu|Dv|I))=q(|x|)g_{1}(u)g_{2}(v),\quad\ x\in B_{1}(0),\\
&u=v=0,\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ x\in\partial B_{1}(0),\end{aligned}\right.

by using the monotone iterative method. For related studies of
k
-Hessian systems with
\mu=0
, see, for instance,
[
37
,
36
,
16
]
.

In this paper, we are interested in studying the following
k
-Hessian type equation

(1.7)

S_{k}^{\frac{1}{k}}(D^{2}u+\mu|Du|I)=f(u),\quad{\rm in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\mu
is a constant,
|Du|
denotes the norm of gradient
vector of
u
,
I
is the
n\times n
identity matrix and
f
is a given function defined on
\mathbb{R}
.
When
\mu=0
, equation (
1.7
) is the standard
k
-Hessian equation (
1.4
),
and when
\mu<0
, equation (
1.7
) satisfies the well-known Ma-Trudinger-Wang condition in
[
26
]
at the points
Du\neq 0
. In fact, when
A
in (
1.1
) depends on
Du
, the Heinz-Lewy example in
[
30
]
shows that one can not expect
C^{1}
regularity for solution
u
in general. Even for the simpler equation (
1.7
), it is not a trivial problem towards the existence of smooth solutions. Therefore, we only focus on this simpler case and discuss the entire smooth subsolutions of (
1.7
) in this paper.

To work in the realm of elliptic equations, we let

\Gamma_{k}=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:S_{p}(\lambda)>0,\ \forall p=1,\cdots,k\right\}

be a convex cone and we always
assume that
\lambda(D^{2}u+\mu|Du|I)\in\Gamma_{k}
.
Here, a function
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
is called admissible in
\Omega
if
\lambda(D^{2}u+\mu|Du|I)\in\Gamma_{k}
for all
x\in\Omega
.
Let
\Phi_{\mu}^{k}
denote the class of admissible functions in
\Omega
, i.e.,

\Phi_{\mu}^{k}(\Omega):=\{u\in C^{2}(\Omega):\lambda(D^{2}u+\mu|Du|I)\in\Gamma_{k},\ \forall x\in\Omega\}.

A function
u\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is said to be an entire admissible subsolution of (
1.7
), if
u\in\Phi_{\mu}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
u
satisfies the following inequality

(1.8)

S_{k}^{\frac{1}{k}}(D^{2}u+\mu|Du|I)\geq f(u),\quad{\rm in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n}.

Remark 1.1
.

Specially, we note that if
k=1
, equation (
1.7
) reduces to the semilinear equation

\Delta u+n\mu|Du|=f(u),\quad{\rm in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n},

which is the special case of (
1.5
) with
\pm g(|Du|)=n\mu|Du|
and is also the special case of (
1.6
) with
F=\Delta
,
g(u)=-n\mu
and
q=1
.

In this paper, we extend the results in
[
17
]
to a kind of
k
-Hessian type equations with gradient terms. Letting

(1.9)

\mu_{0}:=\sqrt{\frac{k}{n(k+1)(C_{n}^{k})^{\frac{1}{k}}}},

then our main results are:

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
f\in C(\mathbb{R})
is a positive and monotone non-decreasing function.

(i)

(Existence) For either the case
k=1
,
\mu\in(-\infty,+\infty)
; or the case
2\leq k\leq n
,
\mu\in[0,+\infty)
, if
f
satisfies

(1.10)

\int^{+\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}f^{k}(t)\,{\rm d}t\right)^{-\frac{1}{k+1}}\,{\rm d}\tau=+\infty,

then equation (
1.7
) admits a subsolution
u\in\Phi_{\mu}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(ii)

(Nonexistence) For
1\leq k\leq n
,
\mu\in(-\infty,\mu_{0})
, if
f
satisfies

\int^{+\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}f^{k}(t)\,{\rm d}t\right)^{-\frac{1}{k+1}}\,{\rm d}\tau<+\infty,

then equation (
1.7
) admits no subsolution
u\in\Phi_{\mu}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
f\in C(\mathbb{R})
is a positive and monotone non-decreasing function.
For either the case
k=1
,
\mu\in(-\infty,\mu_{0})
; or the case
2\leq k\leq n
,
\mu\in[0,\mu_{0})
,
equation (
1.7
) admits a subsolution
u\in\Phi_{\mu}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if condition (
1.10
) holds.

Here, we obtain the existence and nonexistence results of entire subsolutions to equation (
1.7
) in Theorem
1.1
.
In Theorem
1.2
, after adding an upper bound
\mu_{0}
on
\mu
, we actually obtain a necessary and sufficient condition on the solvability for the subsolutions of equation (
1.7
), which can be regarded as a generalized Keller-Osserman condition.
This boundness condition on
\mu
comes from solving technical difficulties and plays an important role in the proof of the necessity.

Remark 1.2
.

We point out that
[0,\mu_{0})
can be regarded as an interval to guarantee the Keller-Osserman condition (
1.10
) to be a necessary and sufficient condition of the global solvability of inequality (
1.8
) when
k\geq 2
; and
(-\infty,\mu_{0})
can be regarded as an interval to guarantee the Keller-Osserman condition (
1.10
) to be a necessary and sufficient condition of the global solvability of inequality (
1.8
) when
k=1
. Namely that we have found the intervals for
\mu
where the Keller-Osserman condition (
1.10
) could be a necessary and sufficient condition of the global solvability of inequality (
1.8
).

Note that the subsolution in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
has no sign restriction.
However, we are also interested in the existence of the positive subsolutions.
If we extend the requirement of
f
to the degenerate case, then the result of the positive subsolutions to equation (
1.7
) can be formulated as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
f\in C(\mathbb{R})
satisfies

(1.11)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &f(t)>0\ \text{is monotonically non-decreasing in}\ (0,+\infty),\\
&f(t)=0\ in\ (-\infty,0].\end{aligned}\right.

For either the case
k=1
,
\mu\in(-\infty,\mu_{0})
; or the case
2\leq k\leq n
,
\mu\in[0,\mu_{0})
, equation (
1.7
) admits a positive subsolution
u\in\Phi_{\mu}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if condition (
1.10
) holds.

Comparing with the standard Hessian equation in
[
17
]
, we make effort to deal with the gradient term
\mu|Du|I
in this paper. We observe that the radial solution of equation (
1.7
) satisfies an ordinary differential equation with a divergence structure, see (
2.9
) for example. When
\mu<0
, different results are found in the semilinear case (
k=1
) and in the fully nonlinear case (
k\geq 2
), which is one contribution of this paper. Moreover, an upper bound
\mu_{0}
of
\mu
has been found so that
(-\infty,\mu_{0})
in the
k=1
case and
[0,\mu_{0})
in the
k\geq 2
case provide ranges where Keller-Osserman condition (
1.10
) becomes a necessary and sufficient condition towards the entire existence of inequality (
1.8
). This is another contribution of this paper.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we make some preliminary calculations of

radial solutions and relate the
k
-Hessian type equation (
1.7

The proofs of the main theorems are given in Section

In Section

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we state some preliminary results of the radial functions.
We first calculate the eigenvalues of
k
-Hessian type operator in the radial sense and obtain the expression of
k
-Hessian type operator
S_{k}(D^{2}u+\mu|Du|I)
in the radial sense in Lemma
2.1
.
Thus, we get an ordinary differential equation associated with equation (
1.7
), which is equipped with a divergence structure. By integrating the ordinary differential equation once and imposing an initial condition, we get a Cauchy problem (
2.10
).
Then in Lemma
2.2
, we prove the
a priori
properties of the solutions to the Cauchy problem.
Finally, the local existence result of the Cauchy problem is established in Lemma
2.3
.

Letting
r=|x|=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}}
, we formulate these preliminary lemmas as follows.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume
\varphi(r)\in C^{2}[0,+\infty)
is radially symmetric with
\varphi^{\prime}(0)=0
. Then for
u(x)=\varphi(r)
, we have that
u(x)\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the eigenvalues of
\lambda(D^{2}u+\mu|Du|I)
are

(2.1)

\lambda(D^{2}u+\mu|Du|I)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\left(\varphi^{\prime\prime}(r)+\mu\varphi^{\prime}(r),\frac{1+\mu r}{r}\varphi^{\prime}(r),\cdots,\frac{1+\mu r}{r}\varphi^{\prime}(r)\right),\quad r\in(0,+\infty),\\
&\left(\varphi^{\prime\prime}(0),\varphi^{\prime\prime}(0),\cdots,\varphi^{\prime\prime}(0)\right),\quad r=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Moreover,

(2.2)

S_{k}(D^{2}u+\mu|Du|I)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &C_{n-1}^{k-1}(\varphi^{\prime\prime}(r)+\mu\varphi^{\prime}(r))(\frac{1+\mu r}{r}\varphi^{\prime}(r))^{k-1}+C_{n-1}^{k}(\frac{1+\mu r}{r}\varphi^{\prime}(r))^{k},\quad r\in(0,+\infty),\\
&C_{n}^{k}\left(\varphi^{\prime\prime}(0)\right)^{k},\quad r=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

It is easy to see that for
x\neq 0
,
i,j=1,\cdots,n
,

(2.3)

\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}(x)=\varphi^{\prime}(r)\frac{x_{i}}{r},

and

(2.4)

\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}(x)=\left(\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}(r)}{r^{2}}-\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(r)}{r^{3}}\right)x_{i}x_{j}+\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(r)}{r}\delta_{ij}.

Using (
2.3
) and
\varphi^{\prime}(0)=0
, we have

\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}(x)=\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(r)-\varphi^{\prime}(0)}{r-0}x_{i}=\varphi^{\prime\prime}(0)\cdot 0=0.

Similarly, by (
2.4
), we have

(2.5)

\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}(x)=\lim_{x\to 0}\left(\left(\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}(r)}{r^{2}}-\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(r)}{r^{3}}\right)x_{i}x_{j}+\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(r)}{r}\delta_{ij}\right)=\varphi^{\prime\prime}(0)\delta_{ij}.

Then
u(x)\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is obvious by defining

\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}(0)=0,\quad{\rm and}\ \ \frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}(0)=\varphi^{\prime\prime}(0)\delta_{ij}.

Combining (
2.4
), (
2.5
) and

|Du|=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right)^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}=\varphi^{\prime}(r)\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{x_{i}}{r}\right)^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}=\varphi^{\prime}(r),\quad r\geq 0,

then
D^{2}u+\mu|Du|I
can be denoted as

(2.6)

D^{2}u+\mu|Du|I=ax^{T}x+bI,

where

a=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}(r)}{r^{2}}-\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(r)}{r^{3}},&r\in(0,+\infty),\\
&0,&r=0,\quad\quad\quad\end{aligned}\right.

b=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(r)}{r}+\mu\varphi^{\prime}(r),\quad r\in(0,+\infty),\\
&\varphi^{\prime\prime}(0)+\mu\varphi^{\prime}(r),\quad r=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Since the eigenvalues of matrix
x^{T}x
are
\lambda(x^{T}x)=(|x|^{2},0,\cdots,0)
, and so the eigenvalues of
ax^{T}x+bI
in (
2.6
) are
(a|x|^{2}+b,b,\cdots,b)
. Hence (
2.1
) is proved. The proof of (
2.2
) is obtained by the definition of
S_{k}
.
∎

From Lemma
2.1
, we know that
u(x)=\varphi(r)\in C^{2}
is a
radial solution of equation (
1.7
) if and only if
\varphi(r)

satisfies the ordinary differential equation

(2.7)

C_{n-1}^{k-1}\left(\varphi^{\prime\prime}(r)+\mu\varphi^{\prime}(r)\right)\left(\frac{1+\mu r}{r}\varphi^{\prime}(r)\right)^{k-1}+C_{n-1}^{k}\left(\frac{1+\mu r}{r}\varphi^{\prime}(r)\right)^{k}=f^{k}(\varphi(r))

for
r\in(0,+\infty)
. By a simple calculation of (
2.7
), we have

(2.8)

\begin{split}k\varphi^{\prime\prime}(r)\left(\varphi^{\prime}(r)\right)^{k-1}+\left(n\mu+\frac{n-k}{r}\right)\left(\varphi^{\prime}(r)\right)^{k}=\frac{k}{C_{n-1}^{k-1}}\left(\frac{r}{1+\mu r}\right)^{k-1}f^{k}(\varphi(r)).\end{split}

Constructing a function
\chi(r)
such that
\chi^{\prime}(r)=n\mu+\frac{n-k}{r}
, then we get
\chi(r)=n\mu r+(n-k)\ln r
.
Multiplying
e^{\chi(r)}
on both sides of (
2.8
), we obtain

(2.9)

\begin{split}\left[\left(\varphi^{\prime}(r)\right)^{k}e^{\chi(r)}\right]^{\prime}&=k\varphi^{\prime\prime}(r)\left(\varphi^{\prime}(r)\right)^{k-1}e^{\chi(r)}+\left(n\mu+\frac{n-k}{r}\right)\left(\varphi^{\prime}(r)\right)^{k}e^{\chi(r)}\\
&=\frac{k}{C_{n-1}^{k-1}}e^{\chi(r)}\left(\frac{r}{1+\mu r}\right)^{k-1}f^{k}(\varphi(r)).\end{split}

Integrating (
2.9
) from
0
to
r
gives

\left(\varphi^{\prime}(r)\right)^{k}e^{\chi(r)}=\int_{0}^{r}\frac{k}{C_{n-1}^{k-1}}e^{\chi(s)}\left(\frac{s}{1+\mu s}\right)^{k-1}f^{k}(\varphi(s))\,{\rm d}s

for
r>0
, where
\varphi^{\prime}(0)=0
is used. Then we get the following expression of the derivative of the radial solution:

\varphi^{\prime}(r)=\left[e^{-\chi(r)}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{k}{C_{n-1}^{k-1}}e^{\chi(s)}\left(\frac{s}{1+\mu s}\right)^{k-1}f^{k}(\varphi(s))\,{\rm d}s\right]^{\frac{1}{k}},

where
\chi(r):=n\mu r+(n-k)\ln r
.
For any given initial value
a
, we are going to consider the related Cauchy problem:

(2.10)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\varphi^{\prime}(r)=\left(\frac{r^{k-n}}{e^{n\mu r}}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{k}{C_{n-1}^{k-1}}\frac{e^{n\mu s}s^{n-1}}{(1+\mu s)^{k-1}}f^{k}(\varphi(s))\,{\rm d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{k}},\quad r>0,\\
&\varphi(0)=a.\end{aligned}\right.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f(t)>0
be a continuous and monotone non-decreasing function defined on
\mathbb{R}
.
For any constant
a
, we assume that there exists
\varphi(r)\in C^{0}[0,+\infty)\cap C^{1}(0,+\infty)
satisfying the Cauchy problem (
2.10
).
Then
\varphi(r)\in C^{2}[0,+\infty)
, and it satisfies equation (
2.7
) with
\varphi^{\prime}(0)=0
.
Furthermore, for either the case
k=1
,
\mu\in(-\infty,+\infty)
; or the case
2\leq k\leq n
,
\mu\in[0,+\infty)
, we have

(2.11)

\lambda_{r}:=\left(\varphi^{\prime\prime}(r)+\mu\varphi^{\prime}(r),\frac{1+\mu r}{r}\varphi^{\prime}(r),\cdots,\frac{1+\mu r}{r}\varphi^{\prime}(r)\right)\in\Gamma_{k},\quad r\in(0,+\infty).

Proof.

We first prove
\varphi(r)\in C^{2}[0,+\infty)
. It is easy to see that
\varphi(r)\in C^{2}(0,+\infty)

By using the L’Hospital rule, we have

Lemma 2.3

(Local existence)

Proof.

The proof is divided into three steps. We first define a Euler’s break line and then show that this Euler’s break line is an
\varepsilon
-approximation solution of (

Step 1: Definition of Euler’s break line

We define a functional

on

where

and

are small enough positive constants.
Then

(2.14)

\varphi^{\prime}(r)=F(r,\varphi)

and

for

Define a Euler’s break line on

as

(2.15)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\psi(0)=a,\\
&\psi(r)=\psi(r_{i-1})+F\left(r_{i-1},\psi(r_{i-1})\right)(r-r_{i-1}),\quad r_{i-1}<r<r_{i},\end{aligned}\right.

where

. We claim that

.
In fact, for any
(r,\varphi)\in\mathcal{R}
, the discussion on the following two cases will prove this claim.

For

,
\mu\geq 0

\begin{split}F(r,\varphi)&\leq\left(\frac{r^{k-n}}{e^{n\mu r}}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{k}{C_{n-1}^{k-1}}\frac{e^{n\mu s}s^{n-1}}{(1+\mu s)^{k-1}}\,{\rm d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}f(a+h)\\

&\leq\left(\frac{k}{C_{n-1}^{k-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}rf(a+h)\\
&\leq\left(\frac{k}{C_{n-1}^{k-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}lf(a+h),\end{split}

which implies that

M:=\max_{\mathcal{R}}F(r,\varphi)\leq\left(\frac{k}{C_{n-1}^{k-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}lf(a+h).

Thus for the break line
(r,\psi)
, we have

(2.17)

\begin{split}a-h<a\leq\psi(r)&\leq a+\sum_{i}F(r_{i-1},\psi(r_{i-1}))(r-r_{i-1})\\
&\leq a+Mr\\
&\leq a+\left(\frac{k}{C_{n-1}^{k-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}l^{2}f(a+h),\quad r\in[0,l].\end{split}

If

.

For

Letting

then for the break line
(r,\psi)
, we have

(2.19)

If
h
is fixed, we can choose

sufficiently small such that
\psi(r)\in(a-h,a+h)
for
r\in[0,l]
.

r
, we get

\begin{split}\left(\varphi^{\prime}(r)\right)^{2}>&\left(\varphi^{\prime}(r)\right)^{2}-\left(\varphi^{\prime}(\frac{1-n}{n\mu})\right)^{2}\\
>&2\int_{\frac{1-n}{n\mu}}^{r}f(\varphi(s))\varphi^{\prime}(s)\,{\rm d}s\\
=&2\int_{\varphi(\frac{1-n}{n\mu})}^{\varphi(r)}f(t)\,{\rm d}t.\end{split}

By a simple calculation, we have

\left(\int_{\varphi(\frac{1-n}{n\mu})}^{\varphi(r)}f(t)\,{\rm d}t\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,{\rm d}\varphi>\sqrt{2}\,{\rm d}r.

Then similar to (
3.21
) we get a contradiction, which completes the proof.

Step 4: Conclusion
. Combining Steps 2 and 3, we deduce that equation (
1.7
) has a subsolution
u(x)\in\Phi^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

if and only if the Cauchy problem (
2.10
) has a solution
\varphi(r)\in C^{2}[0,+\infty)
satisfying (
2.11
) for any initial value
a
, which is equivalent to the condition (
1.10
).
∎

Remark 3.1
.

It is worth noting that for the proof of the sufficient condition in the third step of Theorem
1.2
, we have no upper bound restriction on the parameter
\mu
. This effectively means that for either the case
k=1
,
\mu\in(-\infty,+\infty)
; or the case
2\leq k\leq n
,
\mu\in[0,+\infty)
, if the condition (
1.10
) holds, then there exists a solution to the Cauchy problem (
2.10
).

Next, we will prove Theorem
1.1
with the help of the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We first prove the existence result. For either the case
k=1
,
\mu\in(-\infty,+\infty)
; or the case
2\leq k\leq n
,
\mu\in[0,+\infty)
, if the condition (
1.10
) holds, by using the sufficient condition in the third step in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we obtain the existence of solutions to (
2.10
), see Remark
3.1
. Then with the aid of the second step in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we actually obtain the existence of subsolutions to (
1.7
).
Alternatively, for
2\leq k\leq n
,
\mu\in[0,+\infty)
, the existence of admissible subsolutions to (
1.7
) can be directly obtained by the existence result of the standard
k
-Hessian equation in
[
17
]
, that is if equation
S_{k}^{\frac{1}{k}}(D^{2}u)=f(u)
admits an entire admissible subsolution, then the existence of entire admissible subsolutions to equation (
1.7
) is also obtained. The key technique is the use of the inequality

S_{k}^{\frac{1}{k}}(D^{2}u+\mu|Du|I)\geq S_{k}^{\frac{1}{k}}(D^{2}u)

for
\mu\geq 0
.

Next, we prove the nonexistence result.
For either the case
k=1
,
\mu\in(-\infty,\mu_{0})
; or the case
2\leq k\leq n
,
\mu\in[0,\mu_{0})
, it follows from Theorem
1.2
that equation (
1.7
) admits an entire admissible subsolution if and only if (
1.10
) holds.
Therefore, if
f
satisfies

\int^{+\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}f^{k}(t)\,{\rm d}t\right)^{-\frac{1}{k+1}}\,{\rm d}\tau<+\infty,

then (
1.10
) fails, which shows that equation (
1.7
) admits no subsolution
u\in\Phi_{\mu}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
For the case
2\leq k\leq n
,
\mu\in(-\infty,0)
, it follows from Remark
2.1
that equation (
1.7
) admits no entire subsolution satisfying (
2.11
).
∎

To end this section, according to the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we give the proof of Theorem
1.3
. Note that
f(t)
is degenerate at
t=0
, we turn to seek positive solutions to avoid the degeneracy.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

For either
k=1
,
\mu\in(-\infty,\mu_{0})
, or
2\leq k\leq n
,
\mu\in[0,\mu_{0})
, if
f
satisfies (
1.11
), by (
2.10
), we have

\varphi^{\prime}(r)=\left(\frac{r^{k-n}}{e^{n\mu r}}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{k}{C_{n-1}^{k-1}}\frac{e^{n\mu s}s^{n-1}}{(1+\mu s)^{k-1}}f^{k}(\varphi(s))\,{\rm d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}\geq 0

for
r>0
, which shows that
\varphi
is monotonically non-decreasing.
In order to discuss the radial solutions of (
1.7
) in a convex cone, for any positive number
a
, we let the Cauchy problem (
2.10
) have initial value
\varphi(0)=a>0
.
Combining with the monotonically non-decreasing property of
\varphi
, we get

(3.24)

\varphi(r)\geq\varphi(0)=a>0,\quad r>0.

Since
f
satisfies (
1.11
), we obtain

f(\varphi(r))\geq f(a)>0,\quad r>0.

Then we have
\varphi^{\prime}(r)>0
for
r>0
.
Similar to Lemma
2.2
, for
1\leq p\leq k
, we have
S_{p}(D^{2}u+\mu|Du|I)>0
, which shows that (
2.11
) holds. Hence for this degenerate case, we restrict our discussion within the context of positive solutions.
We only need to replace the initial value of the Cauchy problem (
2.10
) with any positive number
a
and the rest of the proof is similar to the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

4.
Applications

In this section, we give two applications to Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
respectively, which provide the necessary and sufficient conditions on the existence and nonexistence of entire subsolutions to specific equation (
1.7
) with two kinds of nonlinearities.
We also give two examples to verify the existence results.

The applications of the global solvability are given in the following corollaries.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
\alpha\geq 0
and
f(t)=e^{\alpha t}
, then for either the case
k=1
,
\mu\in(-\infty,\mu_{0})
; or the case
2\leq k\leq n
,
\mu\in[0,\mu_{0})
, equation (
1.7
) admits a subsolution
u\in\Phi_{\mu}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
\alpha=0
. For
1\leq k\leq n
,
\mu\in(-\infty,\mu_{0})
, if
\alpha>0
, then there exists no entire admissible subsolution of (
1.7
).

Proof of Corollary
4.1
.

For either the case
k=1
,
\mu\in(-\infty,\mu_{0})
; or the case
2\leq k\leq n
,
\mu\in[0,\mu_{0})
, if
\alpha=0
, then
f(t)\equiv 1
, which satisfies the condition in Theorem
1.2
. Moreover, we have

\int^{+\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}f^{k}(t)\,{\rm d}t\right)^{-\frac{1}{k+1}}\,{\rm d}\tau=\int^{+\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}\,{\rm d}t\right)^{-\frac{1}{k+1}}\,{\rm d}\tau=+\infty.

Hence, by Theorem
1.2
, there exists a subsolution of (
1.7
) if and only if
\alpha=0
.

For either the case
k=1
,
\mu\in(-\infty,\mu_{0})
; or the case
2\leq k\leq n
,
\mu\in[0,\mu_{0})
, if
\alpha>0
, we have
f^{\prime}(t)=\alpha e^{\alpha t}>0
, which also satisfies the condition in Theorem
1.2
. Moreover, we have

\begin{split}\int^{+\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}f^{k}(t)\,{\rm d}t\right)^{-\frac{1}{k+1}}\,{\rm d}\tau=&\int^{+\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{k\alpha t}\,{\rm d}t\right)^{-\frac{1}{k+1}}\,{\rm d}\tau\\
=&\sqrt[k+1]{k\alpha}\int^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\left(e^{k\alpha\tau}-1\right)^{\frac{1}{k+1}}}\,{\rm d}\tau.\end{split}

Since

\lim_{\tau\to+\infty}\frac{\tau^{2}}{(e^{k\alpha\tau}-1)^{\frac{1}{k+1}}}=\lim_{\tau\to+\infty}\left(\frac{\tau^{2(k+1)}}{e^{k\alpha\tau}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{k+1}}=\left(\lim_{\tau\to+\infty}\frac{\tau^{2(k+1)}}{e^{k\alpha\tau}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{k+1}}=0,

by Cauchy’s criterion for the infinite integral with nonnegative integrand, then the integral

\int^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\left(e^{k\alpha\tau}-1\right)^{\frac{1}{k+1}}}\,{\rm d}\tau\ {\rm converges},

which implies that

\int^{+\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}f^{k}(t)\,{\rm d}t\right)^{-\frac{1}{k+1}}\,{\rm d}\tau<+\infty,

for
f(t)=e^{\alpha t}
with
\alpha>0
.
By Theorem
1.2
, we know that equation (
1.7
) admits no entire admissible subsolution.
For
2\leq k\leq n
,
\mu<0
, if
\alpha>0
, it follows from Remark
2.1
that there is no entire subsolution satisfying (
2.11
) to equation (
1.7
). To sum up, we get the desired result.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
q\geq 0
and

f(t)=\left\{\begin{split}&t^{q},&t>0,\\
&0,&t\leq 0,\end{split}\right.

then for either the case
k=1
,
\mu\in(-\infty,\mu_{0})
; or the case
2\leq k\leq n
,
\mu\in[0,\mu_{0})
, equation (
1.7
) admits a positive subsolution
u\in\Phi_{\mu}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
q\leq 1
.

Proof of Corollary
4.2
.

For either the case
k=1
,
\mu\in(-\infty,\mu_{0})
; or the case
2\leq k\leq n
,
\mu\in[0,\mu_{0})
, if
0\leq q\leq 1
, then we have

\begin{split}\int^{+\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}f^{k}(t)\,{\rm d}t\right)^{-\frac{1}{k+1}}\,{\rm d}\tau=&\int^{+\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{kq}\,{\rm d}t\right)^{-\frac{1}{k+1}}\,{\rm d}\tau\\
=&\sqrt[k+1]{kq+1}\int^{+\infty}\tau^{-\frac{kq+1}{k+1}}\,{\rm d}\tau.\end{split}

Since

\lim_{\tau\to+\infty}\tau^{\frac{kq+1}{k+1}}\tau^{-\frac{kq+1}{k+1}}=1

and
\frac{kq+1}{k+1}\leq 1
, by Cauchy’s criterion for the infinite integral with nonnegative integrand, then the infinite integral
\int^{+\infty}\tau^{-\frac{kq+1}{k+1}}\,{\rm d}\tau
diverges, therefore we have

\int^{+\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}f^{k}(t)\,{\rm d}t\right)^{-\frac{1}{k+1}}\,{\rm d}\tau=+\infty.

Besides, we have
\frac{kq+1}{k+1}>1
for
q>1
. Again by Cauchy’s criterion, the infinite integral
\int^{+\infty}\tau^{-\frac{kq+1}{k+1}}\,{\rm d}\tau
converges, that is,

\int^{+\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}f^{k}(t)\,{\rm d}t\right)^{-\frac{1}{k+1}}\,{\rm d}\tau<+\infty,

which does not satisfy the condition (
1.10
).
By Theorem
1.3
, we know that equation (
1.7
) admits a positive subsolution
u\in\Phi_{\mu}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
0\leq q\leq 1
.
∎

Moreover, we give an example to the existence result in Theorem
1.1
with a power-type nonlinearity.

Example 4.1
.

In
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for
k=1,\cdots,n
,
\mu\geq 0
, then the function
u(x):=e^{Ax^{2}}>0
with
A\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{C_{n}^{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}
is an entire subsolution to equation

(4.1)

S_{k}^{\frac{1}{k}}(D^{2}u+\mu|Du|I)=u^{\alpha}

with
\alpha\leq 1
.

Proof.

Letting
\omega(x)=e^{Ax^{2}}
,
then

\mu|D\omega|I=\mu 2Ae^{Ax^{2}}|x|\delta_{ij},

and

D^{2}\omega+\mu|D\omega|I=4A^{2}e^{Ax^{2}}\left[x_{i}x_{j}+\frac{1}{2A}\left(1+\mu|x|\right)\delta_{ij}\right].

It follows from (
2.6
) that
\lambda(D^{2}\omega+\mu|D\omega|I)
can be denoted as

\lambda(D^{2}\omega+\mu|D\omega|I)=\left(4A^{2}e^{Ax^{2}}\left(|x|^{2}+\frac{1}{2A}(1+\mu|x|)\right),2Ae^{Ax^{2}}(1+\mu|x|),\cdots,2Ae^{Ax^{2}}(1+\mu|x|)\right).

By the definition of
k
-Hessian operator, we have

\begin{split}&S_{k}\left(\lambda(D^{2}\omega+\mu|D\omega|I)\right)\\
=&C_{n-1}^{k-1}4A^{2}e^{Ax^{2}}\left(|x|^{2}+\frac{1}{2A}(1+\mu|x|)\right)\left[2Ae^{Ax^{2}}(1+\mu|x|)\right]^{k-1}+C_{n-1}^{k}\left[2Ae^{Ax^{2}}(1+\mu|x|)\right]^{k}\\
=&C_{n-1}^{k-1}4A^{2}e^{Ax^{2}}|x|^{2}\left[2Ae^{Ax^{2}}(1+\mu|x|)\right]^{k-1}+C_{n}^{k}\left[2Ae^{Ax^{2}}(1+\mu|x|)\right]^{k}\\
\geq&C_{n}^{k}\left[2Ae^{Ax^{2}}\right]^{k}\geq e^{kAx^{2}},\end{split}

provided
A\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{C_{n}^{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}
.
Therefore we have

S_{k}\left(\lambda(D^{2}\omega+\mu|D\omega|I)\right)\geq\omega^{k}

all
k=1,\cdots,n
.
For
\alpha\leq 1
, combining with
\omega=e^{Ax^{2}}>1
, we have

(4.2)

S_{k}\left(\lambda(D^{2}\omega+\mu|D\omega|I)\right)\geq\omega^{k}\geq\omega^{k\alpha},

which implies that
\omega
is a positive entire subsolution to equation (
4.1
) with
\alpha\leq 1
.
∎

Note that the index
\alpha
of power function here can be negative, whose range is larger than the existence condition in Theorem
1.1
. This means that when the nonlinearity is monotonically decreasing, the existence result takes effect as well.

In the end, we give an example showing that even though
\mu<0
for the case
k=1
, we can still explicitly construct an entire subsolution to (
1.7
).

Example 4.2
.

In
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for
k=1
,
\mu<0
, then the function
u(x):=e^{Ax^{2}}>0
with
A\geq\frac{1}{2n}+\frac{1}{8}n\mu^{2}
is an entire subsolution to equation

(4.3)

S_{1}(D^{2}u+\mu|Du|I)=u^{\alpha},

with
\alpha\leq 1
.

Proof.

Letting
\omega(x):=e^{Ax^{2}}
,
then
|D\omega|=2Ae^{Ax^{2}}|x|
and
\Delta\omega=4A^{2}e^{Ax^{2}}x^{2}+2Ane^{Ax^{2}}
.
By the definition of
k
-Hessian operator, we have

(4.4)

\begin{split}S_{1}\left(\lambda(D^{2}\omega+\mu|D\omega|I)\right)&=tr(D^{2}\omega+\mu|D\omega|I)\\
&=\Delta\omega+n\mu|D\omega|\\
&=e^{Ax^{2}}\left(4A^{2}x^{2}+2An+2An\mu|x|\right),\end{split}

where
tr(D^{2}\omega+\mu|D\omega|I)
denotes the trace of the matrix
D^{2}\omega+\mu|D\omega|I
.
By Cauchy’s inequality, we have

(4.5)

\begin{split}-2An\mu|x|=2(A|x|)(-n\mu)\leq 4A^{2}x^{2}+\frac{1}{4}n^{2}\mu^{2}\leq 4A^{2}x^{2}+(2An-1),\end{split}

provided
A>0
and
\mu<0
, where
A\geq\frac{1}{2n}+\frac{1}{8}n\mu^{2}
is used in the last inequality. From (
4.5
), we get

4A^{2}x^{2}+2An+2An\mu|x|\geq 1,

which leads to

(4.6)

e^{Ax^{2}}\left(4A^{2}x^{2}+2An+2An\mu|x|\right)\geq e^{Ax^{2}}.

Therefore, we have proved that

S_{1}\left(\lambda(D^{2}\omega+\mu|D\omega|I)\right)\geq\omega
.
Similar to (
4.2
), we have

S_{1}\left(\lambda(D^{2}\omega+\mu|D\omega|I)\right)\geq\omega\geq\omega^{\alpha},

for
\alpha\leq 1
.
∎

In fact, it is Example
4.2
that motivates us to consider the “
k=1,\mu<0
” case separately, throughout the paper.

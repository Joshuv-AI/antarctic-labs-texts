On complex contact manifolds and their null submanifolds
Samuel Ssekajja*, Ange Maloko**

Abstract
. In the present paper, we study the geometry of certain classes of null submanifolds of indefinite complex contact manifolds. In particular, we show that quaternion null submanifolds are always totally geodesic. We also present the geometry of distributions on screen real and screen transversal anti-invariant submanifolds.

Primary 53C25; Secondary 53C40, 53C50

1.
Introduction

In the year 1996, K. L. Duggal and A. Bejancu published their book
[
3
]
on the null geometry of submanifolds which filled an important missing part in the general theory of submanifolds. The book was later updated by K. L. Duggal and B. Sahin in
[
4
]
, by collecting most of the new discoveries in the area since the first publiccation. In Chapter 8 of
[
4
]
, the authors introduces the geometry of null submanifolds of indefinite quaternion Kaehler manifolds. The authors studied the geometry of real null hypersurfaces, the structure of null submanifolds, both, of indefinite quaternion Kaehler manifolds and show that a quaternion null submanifold is always totally geodesic. This result implies that the study of null submanifolds, other than quaternion null submanifolds, is interesting. Then, they dealt with the geometry of screen real submanifolds in detail. As a generalization of real null hypersurfaces of quaternion Kaehler manifolds, they introduced
QR
-null submanifolds. Furthermaore, they show that the class of
QR
-null submanifolds does not include quaternion null submanifolds and screen real submanifolds. They also introduced and studied the geometry of screen
QR
-null and screen
CR
-null submanifolds as generalizations of quaternion null submanifolds and screen real submanifolds, and provided examples for each class of null submanifolds of indefinite quaternion Kaehler manifolds. Based on these two books, many researchers have investigated the geometry of null subspaces of semi-Riemannian manifolds.

On the other hand, in about the same time as in the book
[
3
]
, D. N. Kupeli
[
7
]
introduced the theory of null geometry in a relatively different way. The main tool in his approach was the consideration of a factor bundle which is isomorphic to the screen distribution used by the authors in
[
3
]
. Despite all the above contributions, we remark that the null geometry of submanifolds of indefinite Sasakian 3-structure manifolds, as well as indefinite complex contact manifold have not yet been studied.

In
[
1
]
, the geometry of complex contact manifolds in the Riemannian sense is done in which the foundations on such manifiolds is given, from structures to their curvatures. In
[
5
]
, the geometry of null real hypersurfaces of indefinite complex contact manifold was introduced and many interesting results were proved. The main objective of this paper is to study the geometry of
r
-null real submanifolds of indefinite complex contact manifolds. We present quaternion, screen real and screen transversal anti-invariant submanifolds. Several characterisation results are proved. The paper is arranged as follows; In Section
2
, we quote some basic notions on complex contact manifolds as well as null submanifolds needed in the rest the paper. In Section
3
, we study quaternion submanifolds. We prove that these submanifolds are totally geodesic. In Section
4
, we introduce screen real submanifolds, and in Section
5
we study screen transversal anti-invariant submanifolds.

for all

. Then, replacing

with
JX
in (

\displaystyle h^{l}(JX,JY)=Jh^{l}(JX,Y)=J^{2}h^{l}(X,Y)=-h^{l}(X,Y),

which proves the first relation in (

in (

\displaystyle\overline{g}(h^{s}(X,JY),W)

\displaystyle-\overline{g}(Jh^{s}(X,Y),W)

\displaystyle=u(X)d\sigma(W,GY)+v(X)d\sigma(W,HY),

(3.13)

for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
. Setting
X=U
and
X=V
in (
3

) and (

\sigma(W,GY)=0
and

, for all

\displaystyle h^{s}(X,JY)=Jh^{s}(X,Y).

(3.14)

Replacing
X
with

\displaystyle h^{s}(JX,JY)=Jh^{s}(JX,Y)=J^{2}h^{s}(X,Y)=-h^{s}(X,Y),

which proves the second relation in (

Turning to (

\displaystyle\overline{g}(h^{l}(X,GY),\xi)

\displaystyle-\overline{g}(Gh^{l}(X,Y),\xi)=v(X)d\sigma(G\xi,GY),

(3.16)

for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
. Then, setting
X=V
in (

) and using (

\displaystyle h^{l}(X,GY)=Gh^{l}(X,Y),

(3.17)

for all

. Replacing
X
by

\displaystyle h^{l}(GX,GY)=Gh^{l}(GX,Y)=G^{2}h^{l}(X,Y)=-h^{l}(X,Y),

which proves the first relation in (

\displaystyle h^{s}(GX,GY)=Gh^{s}(GX,Y)=G^{2}h^{l}(X,Y)=-h^{s}(X,Y),

Finally, using (

\displaystyle h^{l}(HX,HY)=-h^{l}(X,Y)\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;h^{s}(HX,HY)=-h^{s}(X,Y),

∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Any quaternion null submanifold

Proof.

By (

\displaystyle h^{l}(JX,JY)

\displaystyle=h^{l}(HGX-u(X)V+v(X)U,HGY-u(Y)V+v(Y)U)

(3.21)

Considering (

\displaystyle h^{l}(JX,JY)

\displaystyle=h^{l}(HGX,HGY)=-h^{l}(GX,GY)=h^{l}(X,Y),

4.
Screen real null submanifolds

In this section, we define screen real null submanifold of an indefinite complex contact manifold. But first, we note that when the characteristic subbundle
\mathcal{V}=Span\{U,V\}
is a subbundle of the transversal bundle
\mathrm{tr}(TM)(=l\mathrm{tr}(TM)\perp S(TM^{\perp}))
, then it cannot be a subbundle of null transversal bundle
l\mathrm{tr}(TM)
entirely. This is due to the fact that
l\mathrm{tr}(TM)
is a null subbundle yet
\mathcal{V}
is a spacelike subbundle. Therefore, by
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathrm{tr}(TM)
we shall always mean
\mathcal{V}\subset S(TM^{\perp})
. This choice has the following consequence.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a null submanifold of a normal indefinite complex contact manifold
\overline{M}
. If
\mathcal{V}\subset S(TM^{\perp})
then
G(TM),H(TM)\subset TM^{\perp}=\mathrm{Rad}\,TM\perp S(TM^{\perp})
.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathcal{V}\subset S(TM^{\perp})
. Then, from (
2.14
), we have

\displaystyle-\overline{g}(GX,Y)=\overline{g}(\overline{\nabla}_{X}U,Y),

(4.1)

for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
. It then follows from (
4.1
) and (
2.4
) that

\displaystyle\overline{g}(GX,Y)=\overline{g}(U,\overline{\nabla}_{X}Y)=\overline{g}(U,h^{s}(X,Y)).

(4.2)

Note that the left hand side of (
4.2
) is skew-symmetric while the right hand side is symmetric. So
\overline{g}(GX,Y)=0
, for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
. Similarly,
\overline{g}(HX,Y)=0
. Hence,
G(TM)\subset TM^{\perp}
and
H(TM)\subset TM^{\perp}
, which completes the proof.
∎

Definition 4.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a null submanifold of an indefinite complex contact manifold
\overline{M}
, which is transversal to the characteristic subbundle
\mathcal{V}
, that is
\mathcal{V}\subset S(TM^{\perp})
. Then,
M
is called a screen real null submanifold if
J_{a}\mathrm{Rad}\,TM=\mathrm{Rad}\,TM
and
J_{a}S(TM)\subset S(TM^{\perp})
, for all
a=1,2,3
.

Remark 4.3
.

(1)

It follows immediately from Definition
4.2
that the global 1-forms
u
and
v
vanishes on
TM
. Moreover, it is easy to see that
l\mathrm{tr}(TM)
is also invariant with respect to
J_{a}
, for all
a=1,2,3
.

(2)

Another important point to note is that
A_{W}
, for all
W\in\Gamma(S(TM^{\perp}))
, is never a screen valued operator. In fact, using (
2.6
) and (
2.14
), with
W=U
, we have
-GX+\sigma(X)V=-A_{U}X+\nabla_{X}^{s}U+D^{l}(X,U)
, for any
X\in\Gamma(TM)
. It would then mean that
\overline{g}(GX,N)=\overline{g}(A_{U}X,N)
, for all
N\in\Gamma(l\mathrm{tr}(TM))
. Thus, if
A_{U}
is screen valued, we obtain
\overline{g}(GX,N)=0
. Now, replacing
N
with
GN
and using (
2.19
), we get
\overline{g}(X,N)=0
, for all
X\in\Gamma(TM)
, which is a contradiction. A similar conclusion is arrived at if one uses (
2.15
).

Next, for any
W\in\Gamma(S(TM^{\perp}))
we have

\displaystyle J_{a}W=B_{a}W+C_{a}W,\;\;\forall\,a=1,2,3,

(4.3)

where,
B_{a}W
and
C_{a}W
are the tangential and transversal parts of
J_{a}W
.

Lemma 4.4
.

On any screen real null submanifold
(M,g)
of a normal indefinite complex contact manifold
\overline{M}
, we have
\overline{g}((\overline{\nabla}_{X}J_{a})\xi,PY)=0
, for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
and
\xi\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Rad}\,TM)
.

Proof.

This follows directly from (
2.19
)–(
2.21
) and Remark
4.3
.
∎

Next, we prove the following result.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
(M,g)
be a screen real null submanifold of a normal indefinite complex contact manifold
\overline{M}
. The induced connection
\nabla
is a metric connection if and only if
h^{s}(X,\xi)
has no components in
J_{a}S(TM)
, for any
X\in\Gamma(TM)
and
\xi\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Rad}\,TM)

Proof.

Using Lemma
4.4
, (
2.4
), (
2.9
) and (
4.3
), we derive

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}J_{a}\xi-B_{a}h^{s}(X,\xi),PY)=0,

(4.4)

for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
. We then see from (
4.4
) and (
2.9
) that

\displaystyle g(A^{*}_{J_{a}\xi}X,PY)=-g(B_{a}h^{s}(X,\xi),PY).

(4.5)

We see that when
h^{s}(X,\xi)
has no components in
J_{a}S(TM)
, the right hand side of (
4.5
) vanishes and we end up with
g(A^{*}_{J_{a}\xi}X,PY)=0
. Thus, by the fact that
S(TM)
is non-degenerate, we get
A^{*}_{J_{a}\xi}X=0
, for any
X\in\Gamma(TM)
and
\xi\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Rad}\,TM)
, and the result follows from Theorem
2.1
, which completes the proof.
∎

A null submanifold
(M,g)
of a semi-Riemannian manifold
\overline{M}
is called irrotational
[
4
, Definition 4.4.7, p. 182]
if
\overline{\nabla}_{X}\xi\in\Gamma(TM)
, for all
X\in\Gamma(TM)
and
\xi\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Rad}\,TM)
. Note that, for a null submanifold, this is equivalent to
h^{l}(X,\xi)=h^{s}(X,\xi)=0
, for all
X\in\Gamma(TM)
and
\xi\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Rad}\,TM)
. Thus, we have the following result.

Corollary 4.6
.

Any irrotational screen real null submanifold of a normal indefinite complex contact manifold carries an induced metric connection.

Theorem 4.7
.

The radical distribution,
\mathrm{Rad}\,TM
, of any screen real null submanifold of a normal indefinite complex contact manifold is integrable if and only if
B_{a}h^{s}(\xi_{1},J_{a}\xi_{2})=B_{a}h^{s}(\xi_{2},J_{a}\xi_{1})
, for all
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Rad}\,TM)
.

Proof.

Replacing
\xi
with
J_{a}\xi_{1}
in Lemma
4.4
, and using (
2.4
), (
2.11
) and (
4.3
), we derive

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}\xi_{1},PY)=-g(B_{a}h^{s}(X,J_{a}\xi_{1}),PY),

(4.6)

for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
and
\xi_{1}\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Rad}\,TM)
. letting
X=\xi_{2}
, where
\xi_{2}\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Rad}\,TM)
, in (
4.6
), we get

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{\xi_{2}}\xi_{1},PY)=-g(B_{a}h^{s}(\xi_{2},J_{a}\xi_{1}),PY).

(4.7)

It follows from that

\displaystyle g([\xi_{1},\xi_{2}],PY)=g(B_{a}h^{s}(\xi_{2},J_{a}\xi_{1})-B_{a}h^{s}(\xi_{1},J_{a}\xi_{2}),PY),

from which our result follows.
∎

Theorem 4.8
.

The screen distribution,
S(TM)
, of a screen real null submanifold of a normal indefinite complex contact manifold is integrable if and only if
-A_{J_{a}X}Y+A_{J_{a}Y}X
has no components in
\mathrm{Rad}\,TM
, for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(S(TM))
.

Proof.

Using (
2.19
)–(
2.21
) and Remark
4.3
, we get

\displaystyle\overline{g}((\overline{\nabla}_{X}J_{a})Y,N)=0,\;\;a=1,2,3,

(4.8)

for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(S(TM))
and
N\in\Gamma(l\mathrm{tr}(TM))
. Then, applying (
2.4
), (
2.6
) and (
4.3
), we derive

\overline{g}(\nabla_{X}Y,J_{a}N)=\overline{g}(A_{J_{a}Y}X,N)
, from which we get

\displaystyle\overline{g}([X,Y],J_{a}N)=\overline{g}(A_{J_{a}Y}X-A_{J_{a}X}Y,N),

(4.9)

Then, our result follows from (
4.9
) and the fact that
l\mathrm{tr}(TM)
is invariant with respect to
J_{a}
, and the proof is complete.
∎

We also have the following result.

Corollary 4.9
.

The screen distribution,
S(TM)
, of a screen real null submanifold of a normal indefinite complex contact manifold is integrable if and only if
A_{J_{a}X}Y=A_{J_{a}Y}X
, for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(S(TM))
.

A null submanifold
(M,g)
, of a semi-Riemannian manifold
(\overline{M},\overline{g})
is said to be totally umbilic in
\overline{M}

[
4
]
if there is a smooth transversal vector field
\mathcal{H}\in\Gamma(\mathrm{tr}(TM))
, called the transversal curvature vector of
M
such that
h(X,Y)=\mathcal{H}g(X,Y)
, for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
. Moreover, it is easy to see that
M
is totally umbilic in
\overline{M}
if and only if on each coordinate neighborhood
\mathscr{U}
there exists smooth vector fields
H^{l}\in\Gamma(l\mathrm{tr}(TM))
and
H^{s}\in\Gamma(S(TM^{\perp}))
such that, for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
,

\displaystyle h^{l}(X,Y)=H^{l}g(X,Y),\;\;\;h^{s}(X,Y)=H^{s}g(X,Y).

(4.10)

Lemma 4.10
.

On any totally umbilic screen real null submanifold of a normal indefinite complex contact manifold, we have
\overline{g}(H^{s},U)=\overline{g}(H^{s},V)=0
.

Proof.

From (
2.6
) and (
2.14
), we have

\displaystyle-GX+\sigma(X)V=-A_{U}X+\nabla_{X}^{s}U+D^{l}(X,U),

(4.11)

for any
X\in\Gamma(TM)
. Taking the product of (
4.11
) with
PY
, where
Y\in\Gamma(TM)
, we get
\overline{g}(A_{U}PX,PY)=0
. It then follows from (
2.7
) that
\overline{g}(h^{s}(PX,PY),U)=0
. Since
M
is totally umbilic, we have
g(PX,PY)\overline{g}(H^{s},U)=0
. That is,
\overline{g}(H^{s},U)=0
since
S(TM)
is nondegenerate. Finally,
\overline{g}(H^{s},V)=0
follow from (
2.15
) and similar steps as before.
∎

For a totally umbilic null submanifold
M
, we see from (
4.10
) that
h^{l}(X,\xi)=h^{s}(X,\xi)=0
, for all
X\in\Gamma(TM)
and
\xi\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Rad}\,TM)
. Thus, using this fact and Theorem
4.5
, we state the following result.

Theorem 4.11
.

Any totally umbilic screen real null submanifold of a normal indefinite complex contact manifold has an induced metric connection. Moreover,

(1)

H^{s}\in\Gamma(S(TM^{\perp}))
.

(2)

M
is either totally geodesic or
\dim S(TM)=1
.

Proof.

The first part of theorem is obvious. However, for the two parts (1) and (2), we let
\omega
be the complementary vector bundle to
J_{a}S(TM)
in
S(TM^{\perp})
. Then we can write
S(TM^{\perp})=J_{a}S(TM)\perp\omega
, for all
a=1,2,3
. Since
J_{a}(S(TM))
and
S(TM^{\perp})
are nondegenerate, it follows that
\omega
is also nondegenerate. Moreover, it is easy to see that
\omega
is invariant. Then, it means that the characteristic subbundle
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\omega
. Next, from (
2.19
)–(
2.21
) and Lemma
2.2
, we see that
\overline{g}((\overline{\nabla}_{PX}J_{a})PY,J_{a}W)=0
, for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
and
W\in\Gamma(\omega)
. It then follows from (
2.4
) and (
2.6
) that

\displaystyle\overline{g}(h^{s}(PX,PY),J_{a}W)=-\overline{g}(C_{a}h^{s}(PX,PY),J_{a}W).

(4.12)

As
M
is totally umbilic, relation (
4.12
) reduce to

\displaystyle g(PX,PY)\overline{g}(H^{s},J_{a}W)=-g(PX,PY)\overline{g}(H^{s},W),

which give
\overline{g}(H^{s},J_{a}W)=-\overline{g}(H^{s},W)
, since
S(TM)
is nondegenerate. Hence, replacing
W
with
J_{a}W
and using Lemma
4.10
, we get
\overline{g}(H^{s},J_{a}W)=\overline{g}(H^{s},W)
. So,
\overline{g}(H^{s},W)=0
, and hence
H^{s}\in\Gamma(S(TM^{\perp}))
, which proves (1). For (2), we first note, from (
2.19
)–(
2.21
), that
\overline{g}((\overline{\nabla}_{X}J_{a})X,Y)=0
, for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(S(TM))
. It then follows from this last relation, together with (
2.4
) and (
2.6
) that

\displaystyle\overline{g}(h^{s}(X,X),J_{a}Y)=\overline{g}(h^{s}(X,Y),J_{a}X).

(4.13)

The rest of the proof follows easily as in
[
4
, Theorem 8.3.10, p. 372]
, starting from (
4.13
), which proves (2). This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Screen transversal anti-invariant submanifolds

Using similar language as in
[

, together with Lemma
4.1
, we define screen transversal anti-invariant submanifolds as follows.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a null submanifold of an indefinite complex contact manifold
\overline{M}
, which is transversal to the characteristic subbundle
\mathcal{V}
, that is
\mathcal{V}\subset S(TM^{\perp})
. Then,
M
is called a screen transversal anti-invariant null submanifold if
J_{a}\mathrm{Rad}\,TM\subset S(TM^{\perp})
and
J_{a}S(TM)\subset S(TM^{\perp})
, for all

.

It then follows from Definition
5.1
that
J_{a}l\mathrm{tr}(TM)\subset S(TM^{\perp})
. Moreover,
S(TM^{\perp})
decomposes as follows;

\displaystyle S(TM^{\perp})=\{J_{a}\mathrm{Rad}\,TM\oplus J_{a}l\mathrm{tr}(TM)\}\perp J_{a}S(TM)\perp D_{0},

where
D_{0}
is a nondegenerate and
J_{a}
-invariant distribution. Then, for any

, we have

\displaystyle J_{a}W=B_{a}W+C_{a}W,

(5.1)

where
B_{a}W
and
C_{a}W
are the tangemtial and transversal parts of
J_{a}W
.

Theorem 5.2
.

The induced connection
\nabla
on a screen transversal anti-invariant null submanifold of a normal indefinite complex contact manifold is a metric connection if and only if
\nabla_{X}^{s}J_{a}\xi
has no components in
J_{a}(S(TM))
, for all
X\in\Gamma(TM)
and
\xi\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Rad}\,TM)
.

Proof.

First note that, on any screen transversal anti-invariant null submanifold
M
, the 1-forms
u
and
v
vanishes on
TM
. Thus, letting
Y=J_{a}\xi
in (
2.19
)-(
2.21
), we get

\displaystyle\overline{g}((\overline{\nabla}_{X}J_{a})J_{a}\xi,PY)=0,

(5.2)

for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
. Applying (
2.11
), (
2.4
) and (
2.6
) to (

), we get

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}\xi-J_{a}A_{J_{a}\xi}X+J_{a}D^{l}(X,J_{a}\xi)+J_{a}\nabla^{s}_{X}J_{a}\xi,PY)=0.

(5.3)

Considering (
5.3

5.1
) and (
2.9

\displaystyle g(A^{*}_{\xi}X,PY)=g(B_{a}\nabla^{s}_{X}J_{a}\xi,PY).

(5.4)

Finally, our result follows from (
5.4
) and Theorem
2.1
.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

The radical distribution,
\mathrm{Rad}\,TM
, of any screen transversal anti-invariant null submanifold of a normal indefinite complex contact manifold is integrable if and only if
B_{a}\nabla^{s}_{\xi_{1}}J_{a}\xi_{2}=B_{a}\nabla^{s}_{\xi_{2}}J_{a}\xi_{1}

\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Rad}\,TM)

5.3

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}\xi,PY)=-\overline{g}(J_{a}\nabla_{X}^{s}J_{a}\xi,PY),

for all

. Then, from (

) and (

\displaystyle g([\xi_{1},\xi_{2}],PY)=\overline{g}(B_{a}\nabla_{\xi_{2}}^{s}J_{a}\xi_{1}-B_{a}\nabla_{\xi_{1}}^{s}J_{a}\xi_{2},PY),

form which our result follows, and the proof is completed.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

The screen distribution,
S(TM)
, of a screen transversal anti-invariant null submanifold of a normal indefinite complex contact manifold is integrable if and only if
\nabla_{X}^{s}J_{a}Y-\nabla_{Y}^{s}J_{a}X

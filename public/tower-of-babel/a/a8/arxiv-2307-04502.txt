Modular Completely Dirichlet forms as Squares of Derivations
Melchior Wirth

Abstract
. We prove that certain closable derivations on the GNS Hilbert space associated with a non-tracial weight on a von Neumann algebra give rise to GNS-symmetric semigroups of contractive completely positive maps on the von Neumann algebra.



Introduction

The interplay between derivations and symmetric semigroups of unital (or contractive) completely positive maps has proven fruitful for applications in quantum information theory
[
Bar17
,
CM17
,
GR22
]
, operator algebras
[
Pet09
,
Pet09a
,
DI16
,
Cas21
]
and beyond. Using the framework of completely Dirichlet forms, this connection is particularly well-understood in the case of tracially symmetric semigroups after the seminal work of Cipriani and Sauvageot
[
Sau89
,
Sau90
,
CS03
]
.

In many situations however one encounters non-tracial reference states or weights: In quantum statistical mechanics, the reference state is typically a Gibbs state, which is not a trace at finite temperature; in quantum probability in the study of Lévy processes on compact quantum groups, the natural reference state is the Haar state, which is only a trace for the class of compact quantum groups of Kac type; and in the structure theory of von Neumann algebras, one is faced with non-tracial states when the von Neumann algebra has a non-trivial type III summand.

In the non-tracial setting, the connection between derivations and symmetric semigroups of completely positive maps is much less understood. Recently, it was shown by the author that every GNS-symmetric semigroup of unital completely positive maps gives rise to a canonical derivation via its associated Dirichlet form
[
Wir22
]
. This result was (partially) extended to KMS-symmetric semigroups by Vernooij and the author
[
VW23
]
.

There has also been work in the opposite direction – starting with a derivation to construct a completely Dirichlet form
[
Cip97
,
Par00
,
BK02
,
CZ21
,
LM22
,
Wir22
]
. However, these results all rely on additional structural assumptions on the derivation, usually some form of (approximate) innerness. This means that natural examples like derivations arising from cocycles on non-unimodular groups or Voiculescu’s derivation in non-tracial free probability could not be treated in this framework.

In this article, we prove in a general context that closable derivations give rise to GNS-symmetric semigroups of completely bounded maps. More precisely, our main result is the following.

Theorem
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a Tomita algebra,
\mathscr{H}
a normal Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
and
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathscr{H}
a closable symmetric derivation. Let
\mathscr{E}
be the closure of the quadratic form
\mathscr{E}_{0}
given by
\operatorname{dom}(\mathscr{E}_{0})=\operatorname{dom}(\delta)
and
\mathscr{E}_{0}(a)=\lVert\delta(a)\rVert_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}
. Then the strongly continuous semigroup associated with
\mathscr{E}
is the GNS implementation of a GNS-symmetric semigroup of contractive completely positive maps on the left von Neumann algebra generated by
\mathfrak{A}
.

Here a normal Tomita bimodule is a bimodule over a Tomita algebra that additionally carries a complex one-parameter group
(\mathscr{U}_{z})
and an involution
\mathscr{J}
satisfying some compatibility conditions, and a symmetric derivation
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathscr{H}
is a map that intertwines the complex one-parameter groups and involutions on
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathscr{H}
and satisfies the product rule

\delta(ab)=a\delta(b)+\delta(a)b.

These objects were introduced in
[
Wir22
]
and appear to be the natural non-tracial analogs of the Hilbert bimodule and derivation occurring in the context of completely Dirichlet forms on tracial von Neumann algebras.

Combined with the results from
[
Wir22
]
, we thus obtain a comprehensive picture of GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups analogous to the result of Cipriani and Sauvageot for tracially symmetric semigroups. Among other potential applications, we hope that this result opens the gate for applications to non-tracial free probability and deformation/rigidity theory of type III von Neumann algebras similar to recent work in this direction in the tracial case.

One main difficulty when trying to prove that closable derivations generate completely Dirichlet forms (or semigroups of completely positive maps) is that the property defining derivations, the product rule, is an algebraic property, while Dirichlet forms are defined in terms of order properties, and the domain of a derivation is not necessarily closed under order operations. As such, the problem of properly dealing with domains is crucial. Note that it is unavoidable to allow for unbounded derivations as everywhere defined derivations yield norm continuous semigroups of completely positive maps, which is too restrictive for many applications.

In the tracial case, this difficulty can be overcome since order operations such as taking the positive part can be expressed in terms of functional calculus and as such can be approximated by polynomials. In the non-tracial case, the order operations can still be expressed in terms of functional analysis in the setup of Haagerup
L^{p}
spaces, but the product rule is formulated in terms of Hilbert algebra multiplication, which is different from the product of two operators in Haagerup
L^{2}
(which is only in
L^{2}
if it is zero). Therefore it is not clear how to connect the two.

Instead of trying to follow the proof in the tracial setting, our proof strategy instead relies on Haagerup reduction method, which allows to embed a von Neumann algebra as an expected subalgebra of a bigger von Neumann algebra that can be approximated by finite von Neumann algebras. As it turns out, this reduction method is well-suited to reduce the problem at hand to the known case of tracial von Neumann algebras. One key challenge are again domain issues: For the Haagerup construction one has to extend the derivation to a domain on a crossed product that is sufficiently big, but such that the extension still satisfies the product rule. The essential new technical ingredient to overcome this kind of domain problems lies in the introduction of a new locally convex topology on the domain of a derivation that allows to extend derivations to derivations on a completion.

As a final note, considering the results from
[
VW23
]
, it is a natural question whether the results from the present article can be extended to cover KMS-symmetric semigroups. For one, our methods crucially use commutation with the modular group, which fails for KMS-symmetric maps if they are not GNS-symmetric. But more severely, it seems like there are additional algebraic obstructions, already in finite dimensions: It is shown in
[
VW23
]
that if
\mathscr{E}
is a completely Dirichlet form on
L^{2}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi)
, then there exist self-adjoint matrices
v_{j}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
such that

\mathscr{E}(\rho^{1/4}x\rho^{1/4})=\sum_{j}\mathrm{tr}(\lvert\rho^{1/4}[v_{j},x]\rho^{1/4}\rvert^{2}),

where
\rho
is the density matrix inducing the state
\varphi
on
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. However, without further assumptions on the operators
v_{j}
, the quadratic form on the right side of the previous equation is not necessarily a completely Dirichlet form.

Outline of the article

In Section
1
we recall some basics regarding modular theory, completely Dirichlet forms on standard forms of von Neumann algebras and Tomita bimodules and derivations. In Section
2
we introduce a topology on the domain of a derivation, the
\delta
-topology, and show that derivations can be extended to derivations on the completion in the
\delta
-topology. In Section
3
we give a closability criterion for derivations in our setting. In Section
4
we discuss how derivations can be extended to crossed products and discuss how completely Dirichlet forms behave with respect to change of the reference weight. Then we state and prove the main result of this article, Theorem
4.4
, showing that the quadratic form associated with a closable derivation is a modular completely Dirichlet form. Finally, in Section
5
we discuss several classes of examples, including inner derivations, derivations arising in non-tracial free probability and derivation induced by cocycles on (possibly non-unimodular) locally compact groups.

1.
Basics

In this section we briefly recap some material concerning modular theory and in particular Hilbert and Tomita algebras, completely Dirichlet forms, Tomita bimodules and derivations that is used in the later sections.

1.1.
Modular theory

As our approach is formulated in the language of Hilbert and Tomita algebras, we summarize the relevant definitions here. Our treatment mostly follows
[
Tak03
, Chapters VI–VII]
.

An algebra
\mathfrak{A}
with involution
♯
(resp.
♭
) and inner product
\langle\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,\rangle
is called
left (resp. right) Hilbert algebra
if

•

for every
a\in\mathfrak{A}
the map
\pi_{l}(a)\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{A}
,
b\mapsto ab
(resp.
b\mapsto ba
) is bounded,

•

\langle ab,c\rangle=\langle b,a^{\sharp}c\rangle
(resp.
\langle ab,c\rangle=\langle b,ca^{\flat}\rangle
) for all
a,b,c\in\mathfrak{A}
,

•

the involution
♯
(resp.
♭
) is closable,

•

the linear span of all products
ab
with
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}
is dense in
\mathfrak{A}
.

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra and
\varphi
a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
M
. We write
\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
for the definition ideal
\{x\in M\mid\varphi(x^{\ast}x)<\infty\}
and
(\pi_{\varphi},L^{2}(M,\varphi),\Lambda_{\varphi})
for the associated semi-cyclic representation.

The prototypical example of a left Hilbert algebra is
\mathfrak{A}=\Lambda_{\varphi}(\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast})
with the product
\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)\Lambda_{\varphi}(y)=\Lambda_{\varphi}(xy)
, the involution
\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)^{\sharp}=\Lambda_{\varphi}(x^{\ast})
and the inner product inherited from
L_{2}(M,\varphi)
, that is,
\langle\Lambda_{\varphi}(x),\Lambda_{\varphi}(y)\rangle=\varphi(x^{\ast}y)
. In this case,
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}=\pi_{\varphi}(M)
. We write
\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
for this left Hilbert algebra.

Conversely, every left Hilbert algebra
\mathfrak{A}
gives rise to a von Neumann algebra
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
acting on the completion of
\mathfrak{A}
and a weight

\varphi\colon\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}_{+}\to[0,\infty],\,\varphi(x)=\begin{cases}\lVert\xi\rVert^{2}&\text{if }x^{1/2}=\pi_{l}(\xi),\\
\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

If
\mathfrak{A}
is a full left Hilbert algebra
[
Tak03
, Definition VI.1.16]
, then
\varphi
is a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
, and
\mathfrak{A}
is canonically isomorphic to
\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
.

Let
\mathfrak{H}
be the completion of the left Hilbert algebra
\mathfrak{A}
. Since the involution
♯
on
\mathfrak{A}
is closable, its closure
S
on
\mathfrak{H}
exists and has a polar decomposition
S=J\Delta^{1/2}
. The operator
\Delta
is a non-singular positive self-adjoint operator, called the
modular operator
, and
J
is an anti-unitary involution, called the
modular conjugation
. If
\mathfrak{A}
is the left Hilbert algebra associated with a weight
\varphi
, we write
\Delta_{\varphi}
and
J_{\varphi}
for the associated modular operator and modular conjugation. We write
\Lambda_{\varphi}^{\prime}\colon\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast}\to L_{2}(M,\varphi)
for the map
x\mapsto J_{\varphi}\Lambda_{\varphi}(x^{\ast})
.

If
\mathfrak{A}
is full, the modular conjugation
J
gives rise to the positive self-dual cone
P=\overline{\{\pi_{l}(a)Ja\mid a\in\mathfrak{A}\}}
and
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
is in standard form
[
Tak03
, Definition IX.1.13]
.

The modular operator
\Delta
gives rise to a point weak
∗
continuous group of automorphisms
x\mapsto\Delta^{it}x\Delta^{-it}
on
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
. If
\varphi
is a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
M
, the group
\sigma^{\varphi}
given by
\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x)=\pi_{\varphi}^{-1}(\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}\pi_{\varphi}(x)\Delta_{\varphi}^{-it})
is called the
modular group
associated with
\varphi
.

If
(\alpha_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a point weak
∗
continuous group of
\ast
-automorphisms on
M
, then an element
x\in M
is called
entire analytic
if the map
t\mapsto\alpha_{t}(x)
has an extension
z\mapsto\alpha_{z}(x)
to the complex plane such that
z\mapsto\omega(\alpha_{z}(x))
is analytic for every
\omega\in M_{\ast}
. The entire analytic elements form a weak
∗
dense
\ast
-subalgebra of
M
.

A
Tomita algebra
is a left Hilbert algebra
\mathfrak{A}
endowed with a complex one-parameter group
(U_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{C}}
of algebra automorphism such that

•

z\mapsto\langle a,U_{z}b\rangle
is analytic for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}
,

•

(U_{z}a)^{\sharp}=U_{\bar{z}}(a^{\sharp})
for all
a\in\mathfrak{A}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
,

•

\langle U_{z}a,b\rangle=\langle a,U_{-\bar{z}}b\rangle
for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
,

•

\langle a^{\sharp},b^{\sharp}\rangle=\langle U_{-i}b,a\rangle
for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}
.

Note that every Tomita algebra becomes a right Hilbert algebra when endowed with the involution

\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{A},a\mapsto a^{\flat}=U_{-i}(a^{\sharp}).

For a full left Hilbert algebra
\mathfrak{A}
let

\mathfrak{A}_{0}=\left\{\xi\in\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}D(\Delta^{n})\,\bigg|\,\Delta^{n}\xi\in\mathfrak{A}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

For every
\xi\in\mathfrak{A}_{0}
the map
t\mapsto\Delta^{it}\xi
has an entire analytic extension
z\mapsto U_{z}\xi
with
U_{z}\xi\in\mathfrak{A}_{0}
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
. This makes
\mathfrak{A}_{0}
into a Tomita algebra such that
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A}_{0})^{\prime\prime}=\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
.

In particular,

(\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi})_{0}=\{\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)\mid x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast},\,x\text{ entire analytic for }\sigma^{\varphi}\}.

1.2.
Completely Dirichlet forms

Completely Dirichlet forms in the non-tracial setting were introduced by Goldstein and Lindsay
[
GL95
,
GL99
]
in the language of GNS Hilbert spaces of states (or weights) and by Cipriani
[
Cip97
]
in the language of standard forms with a fixed cyclic vector. Our approach is somewhat different from both of these formulations in that we use left Hilbert algebras, but in view of the previous subsection it is equivalent to the formulation by Goldstein–Lindsay (and to that of Cipriani in case the left Hilbert algebra has a unit).

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a full left Hilbert algebra with completion
\mathfrak{H}
. Let
C
be the closure of
\{\Delta^{1/4}a\mid a\in\mathfrak{A},\,0\leq\pi_{l}(a)\leq 1\}
and let
P_{C}
be the metric projection onto
C
. We say that a closed densely defined quadratic form
\mathscr{E}
on
\mathfrak{H}
is a
Dirichlet form
with respect to
\mathfrak{A}
if
\mathscr{E}\circ J=\mathscr{E}
and
\mathscr{E}(P_{C}(a))\leq\mathscr{E}(a)
for all
a\in\mathfrak{H}
with
Ja=a
.

The Dirichlet form
\mathscr{E}
is called
completely Dirichlet form
if for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
the quadratic form

\mathscr{E}^{(n)}\colon\mathfrak{H}\otimes M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\to[0,\infty],\,\mathscr{E}^{(n)}([\xi_{ij}])=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\mathscr{E}(\xi_{ij})

is a Dirichlet form with respect to
\mathfrak{A}\odot M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. Here
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
carries the normalized Hilbert–Schmidt inner product and the multiplication and involution on
\mathfrak{A}\odot M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
are given by
[a_{ij}][b_{ij}]=[\sum_{k}a_{ik}b_{kj}]
,
[a_{ij}]^{\sharp}=[a_{ji}^{\sharp}]
.

A (completely) Dirichlet form with respect to
\mathfrak{A}
is called
modular
(or GNS-symmetric) if
\mathscr{E}\circ U_{t}=\mathscr{E}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Completely Dirichlet forms are of particular interest for their connection to semigroups of contractive completely positive maps on von Neumann algebras. Let us briefly sketch this correspondence. Proofs can be found in
[
GL99
, Theorems 4.9, 5.7]
for the wider class of KMS-symmetric semigroups. The result for GNS-symmetric semigroups follows from the fact that GNS symmetry is equivalent to KMS symmetry and commutation with the modular group (see
[
AC82
, Proposition 6.1]
for example).

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra. A
quantum dynamical semigroup
is a semigroup of normal contractive completely positive operators on
M
that is continuous in the point weak
∗
topology. If
\varphi
is a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
M
, a quantum dynamical semigroup
(P_{t})
is called
GNS-symmetric
with respect to
\varphi
if
\varphi\circ P_{t}\leq\varphi
for all
t\geq 0
and

\varphi(P_{t}(x)^{\ast}y)=\varphi(x^{\ast}P_{t}(y))

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
and
t\geq 0
.

Every GNS-symmetric quantum dynamical semigroup gives rise to a strongly continuous semigroup
(T_{t})
on
L^{2}(M,\varphi)
, its
GNS implementation
, acting by
T_{t}\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)=\Lambda_{\varphi}(P_{t}(x))
for
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
, and the associated quadratic form is a modular completely Dirichlet form with respect to
\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
. Vice versa, the strongly continuous semigroup associated with a modular completely Dirichlet form is the GNS implementation of a GNS-symmetric quantum dynamical semigroup.

We call a completely Dirichlet form a
quantum Dirichlet form
if the associated quantum dynamical semigroups consists of unital maps. A criterion in terms of the form itself is given in
[
Wir22
, Proposition 3.2]
.

1.3.
Tomita bimodules and derivations

Tomita bimodules were introduced in
[
Wir22
]
as codomains of the derivations associated with modular completely Dirichlet forms.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a Tomita algebra. A
Tomita bimodule
over
\mathfrak{A}
is an inner product space
\mathscr{H}
endowed with non-degenerate commuting left and right actions of
\mathfrak{A}
, an anti-isometric involution
\mathscr{J}\colon\mathscr{H}\to\mathscr{H}
and a complex one-parameter group
(\mathscr{U}_{z})
of isometries such that

•

\lVert a\xi b\rVert\leq\lVert\pi_{l}(a)\rVert\lVert\pi_{r}(b)\rVert\lVert\xi\rVert
for
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}
,
\xi\in\mathscr{H}
,

•

\langle a\xi b,\eta\rangle=\langle\xi,a^{\sharp}\eta b^{\flat}\rangle
for
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}
,
\xi,\eta\in\mathscr{H}
,

•

\mathscr{U}_{z}(a\xi b)=(U_{z}a)(\mathscr{U}_{z}\xi)(U_{z}b)
for
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}
,
\xi\in\mathscr{H}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
,

•

\mathscr{J}(a\xi b)=(Jb)(\mathscr{J}\xi)(Ja)
for
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}
,
\xi\in\mathscr{H}
,

•

\mathscr{U}_{z}\mathscr{J}=\mathscr{J}\mathscr{U}_{\bar{z}}
for
z\in\mathbb{C}
.

Let
\bar{\mathscr{H}}
be the completion of
\mathscr{H}
. The first two bullet points imply that
\pi_{l}(a)\mapsto(\xi\mapsto a\xi)
extends to a non-degenerate
\ast
-homorphism from
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})
to
B(\bar{\mathscr{H}})
. If this map can be extended to a normal
\ast
-homomorphism from
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
to
B(\bar{\mathscr{H}})
, then we say that
\mathscr{H}
is a
normal Tomita bimodule
. Requiring normality for the right action instead leads to the same notion of normal Tomita bimodule.

If
\mathfrak{A}
is a Tomita algebra and
\mathscr{H}
a bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
, we call a linear map
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathscr{H}
a
derivation
if it satisfies the product rule

\delta(ab)=a\delta(b)+\delta(a)b

for
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}
. If
\mathscr{H}
is a Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
, we say that a derivation
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathscr{H}
is
symmetric
if
\delta\circ J=\mathscr{J}\circ\delta
and
\delta\circ U_{z}=\mathscr{U}_{z}\circ\delta
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
.

If
\mathfrak{A}
is a full left Hilbert algebra and
\mathscr{E}
a modular quantum Dirichlet form with respect to
\mathfrak{A}
, it is shown in
[
Wir22
, Theorem 6.3]
that

\mathfrak{A}_{\mathscr{E}}=\{a\in\mathfrak{A}_{0}\mid U_{z}a\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathscr{E})\text{ for all }z\in\mathbb{C}\}

is a Tomita subalgebra of
\mathfrak{A}_{0}
and a core for
\mathscr{E}
. Moreover, by
[
Wir22
, Theorem 6.8]
there exists a Tomita bimodule
\mathscr{H}
over
\mathfrak{A}
and a symmetric derivation
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}_{\mathscr{E}}\to\mathscr{H}
such that

\mathscr{E}(a,b)=\langle\delta(a),\delta(b)\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}

for
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathscr{E}}
.

Under the minimality condition
\mathscr{H}=\mathrm{lin}\{\delta(a)b\mid a,b\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathscr{E}}\}
, the pair
(\mathscr{H},\delta)
is uniquely determined by
\mathscr{E}
up to isometric isomorphism preserving the Tomita bimodule structure and intertwining the derivations
[
Wir22
, Theorem 6.9]
. By a slight abuse of notation, any such pair
(\mathscr{H},\delta)
is called the
first-order differential structure associated with
\mathscr{E}
. If
\mathscr{H}
is a normal Tomita bimodule, the quantum Dirichlet form
\mathscr{E}
is called
\Gamma
-regular
. A characterization in terms of the carré du champ is given in
[
Wir22
, Theorem 7.2]
.

2.
\delta
-topology and completeness

In this section we introduce a locally convex topology on the domain of a closable symmetric derivation, called the
\delta
-topology. This topology is strong enough to ensure that the derivation extends to a derivation on the completion, which is a key technical ingredient in the proof of the main theorem later.

For the definition of the
\delta
-topology recall that the Mackey topology
\tau(M,M_{\ast})
on a von Neumann algebra
M
is the finest linear topology
\mathscr{T}
on
M
such that the topological dual of
(M,\mathscr{T})
is
M_{\ast}
. Equivalently, it is the finest locally convex topology on
M
that coincides with the strong
∗
topology on norm bounded sets
[
Coo87
, Proposition 2.11, Remark 2.12]
. It has the advantage over the other usual locally convex topologies on
M
of being complete, which is convenient for several of the following arguments.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a Tomita algebra with completion
\mathfrak{H}
, let
\mathscr{H}
be a normal Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
and
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathscr{H}
a symmetric derivation. We define the
\delta
-topology
\mathscr{T}_{\delta}
on
\mathfrak{A}
as the coarsest locally convex topology that makes the maps

\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{H}\oplus\bar{\mathscr{H}},\,a\mapsto(\Delta^{n}a,\delta(\Delta^{n}a))

continuous with respect to the norm topology on
\mathfrak{H}\oplus\bar{\mathscr{H}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and the maps

\mathfrak{A}\to B(\mathfrak{H}),\,a\mapsto\pi_{l}(\Delta^{n}a)

continuous for the Mackey topology on
B(\mathfrak{H})
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Clearly, the
\delta
-topology is stronger than the topology induced by the graph norm
(\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}+\lVert\delta(\cdot)\rVert_{\mathscr{H}}^{2})^{1/2}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
\mathscr{E}
is a
\Gamma
-regular modular quantum Dirichlet form and
(\mathscr{H},\delta)
the associated first-order differential structure, then
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathscr{E}}
is complete in the
\delta
-topology.

Proof.

Let
(a_{j})
be a Cauchy net in
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathscr{E}}
with respect to the
\delta
-topology. In particular,
(\Delta^{n}a_{j},\delta(\Delta^{n}a_{j}))_{j}
is Cauchy in
\mathfrak{H}\oplus\bar{\mathscr{H}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since
\Delta^{n}
and
\delta
are closable on
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathscr{E}}
, it follows that there exists
a\in\mathfrak{H}
such that
(\Delta^{n}a_{j},\delta(\Delta^{n}a_{j}))\to(\Delta^{n}a,\bar{\delta}(\Delta^{n}a))
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. In particular,
a\in\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{dom}(\Delta^{n})
and
\Delta^{n}a\in\operatorname{dom}(\bar{\delta})=\operatorname{dom}(\mathscr{E})
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Moreover, as the Mackey topology is complete, for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
there exists
x_{n}\in B(\mathfrak{H})
such that
\pi_{l}(\Delta^{n}a_{j})\to x_{n}
with respect to
\tau(B(\mathfrak{H}),B(\mathfrak{H})_{\ast})
. For
b\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathscr{E}}
we have

x_{n}b=\lim_{j}\pi_{l}(\Delta^{n}a_{j})b=\lim_{j}\pi_{r}(b)\Delta^{n}a_{j}=\pi_{r}(b)\Delta^{n}a.

Since
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathscr{E}}
is dense in
\mathfrak{H}
, it follows that
\Delta^{n}a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathscr{E}}^{\prime\prime}
and
\pi_{l}(\Delta^{n}a_{j})\to\pi_{l}(\Delta^{n}a)
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Altogether we conclude that
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathscr{E}}
and
a_{j}\to a
in the
\delta
-topology.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

If
\mathfrak{A}
is a Tomita algebra,
\mathscr{H}
a Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
and
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathscr{H}
a closable symmetric derivation, the inclusion of
\mathfrak{A}
into its completion
\mathfrak{H}
extends to an injective map from the completion of
\mathfrak{A}
in the
\delta
-topology to
\mathfrak{H}
.

Proof.

We have to show that if
(a_{j})
is a Cauchy net in
\mathfrak{A}
with respect to the
\delta
-topology and
a_{j}\to 0
in
\mathfrak{H}
, then
a_{j}\to 0
in the
\delta
-topology. Since
\Delta^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
\delta
are closable, we have
(\Delta^{n}a_{j},\delta(\Delta^{n}a_{j}))\to 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Furthermore, using the completeness of the Mackey topology and a similar argument as in the previous lemma, one sees that
\pi_{l}(\Delta^{n}a_{j})\to 0
in
\tau(B(\mathfrak{H}),B(\mathfrak{H})_{\ast})
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence
a_{j}\to 0
in the
\delta
-topology.
∎

Let
\widehat{\mathfrak{A}}^{\delta}
denote the set of all elements
a\in\mathfrak{H}
for which there exists a net
(a_{j})
in
\mathfrak{A}
such that
a_{j}\to a
in
\mathfrak{H}
and
(a_{j})
is Cauchy in the
\delta
-topology. By the previous lemma,

\mathfrak{A}
in the
\delta
-topology, and we call it simply the
\delta
-completion of
\mathfrak{A}
. It is not hard to see that

is a Tomita subalgebra of

and contained in
\operatorname{dom}(\bar{\delta})
.

Recall that if
\mathfrak{H}
is a normal Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
, we can continuously extend the left and right action of
\mathfrak{A}
and the maps
\mathscr{J}
and
\mathscr{U}_{t}

, to the Hilbert completion
\bar{\mathscr{H}}

If it exists, this entire extension is unique and will be denoted by
z\mapsto\bar{\mathscr{U}}_{z}\xi
. Clearly,
\mathscr{H}\subset\mathscr{H}^{\mathrm{a}}
and
\mathscr{U}_{z}\subset\bar{\mathscr{U}}_{z}
for all

.

Lemma 2.3
.

If we endow
\mathscr{H}^{\mathrm{a}}
with the coarsest locally convex topology that makes

continuous for all

is complete.

Proof.

If

is the unique non-singular positive self-adjoint operator in

such that
\bar{\mathscr{U}}_{t}=A^{it}
for

, then
\mathscr{H}^{\mathrm{a}}=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{dom}(A^{n})
and
\mathscr{H}^{\mathrm{a}}
is the projective limit of the Banach spaces
(\operatorname{dom}(A^{n})\cap\operatorname{dom}(A^{-n}),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\bar{\mathscr{H}}}+\lVert A^{n}\,\cdot\,\rVert_{\bar{\mathscr{H}}}+\lVert A^{-n}\,\cdot\,\rVert_{\bar{\mathscr{H}}})
in the topology described in the lemma. In particular,
\mathscr{H}^{\mathrm{a}}
is complete.
∎

Since
\mathscr{H}
is a
normal
Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
, the Hilbert completion
\bar{\mathscr{H}}
has a canonical structure of a
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
-
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
correspondence determined by

\pi_{l}(a)\cdot\xi\cdot J\pi_{r}(b)^{\ast}J=a\xi b

for

and

.

If

,

in the
\delta
-topology and

Thus, if

, then group

and the restriction of

into a Tomita bimodule over

.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
\mathfrak{A}
is a Tomita algebra,
\mathscr{H}
is a Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
and

is a closable symmetric derivation with closure

, then

and

If

is a net in
\mathfrak{A}
such that

in the

It follows that

∎

3.
Closability of derivations

In this section we give a simple criterion for the closability of derivations inspired by a well-known result (see
[
Voi98
, Section 4]
and
[
Nel17
, Lemma 3.9]
for the non-tracial case) on the closability of the derivation used in free probability.

If
\mathfrak{A}
is a Tomita algebra and
\mathscr{H}
is a Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
, we say that
\xi\in\mathscr{H}
is a
bounded vector
if there exists
C>0
such that
\lVert a\xi b\rVert\leq C\lVert a\rVert\lVert b\rVert
for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}
. In this case, the maps
a\mapsto a\xi
and
b\mapsto\xi b
extend to bounded linear operators from the completion
\mathfrak{H}
of
\mathfrak{A}
to
\mathscr{H}
, which we denote by
R(\xi)
and
L(\xi)
, respectively.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a Tomita algebra,
\mathscr{H}
a normal Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
and
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathscr{H}
a derivation. If
\delta(\mathfrak{A})
is contained in the space of bounded vectors, then
\operatorname{dom}(\delta^{\ast})
is a subbimodule of
\mathscr{H}
and

\delta^{\ast}(a\xi b)=a^{\ast}\delta^{\ast}(\xi)b-L(\delta(a^{\ast}))^{\ast}(\xi b)-R(\delta(b^{\ast}))^{\ast}(a\xi)

for
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}
and
\xi\in\operatorname{dom}(\delta^{\ast})
.

Proof.

Let
a,b,c\in\mathfrak{A}
and
\xi\in\operatorname{dom}(\delta^{\ast})
. By the product rule,

\displaystyle\langle a\xi b,\delta(c)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\xi,a^{\ast}\delta(c)b^{\ast}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\xi,\delta(a^{\ast}cb^{\ast})-\delta(a^{\ast})cb^{\ast}-a^{\ast}c\delta(b^{\ast})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle a\delta^{\ast}(\xi)b-L(\delta(a^{\ast}))^{\ast}(\xi b)-R(\delta(b^{\ast}))^{\ast}(a\xi),c\rangle.

Thus
a\xi b\in\operatorname{dom}(\delta^{\ast})
and the claimed identity for
\delta^{\ast}(a\xi b)
holds.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a Tomita algebra,
\mathscr{H}
a normal Tomita bimodule over
\mathscr{H}
and
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathscr{H}
a derivation. If
\delta(\mathfrak{A})
is contained in the space of bounded vectors and
\operatorname{dom}(\delta^{\ast})
is a cyclic subset, then
\delta
is closable.

Proof.

By the previous lemma,
\operatorname{dom}(\delta^{\ast})
is a subbimodule of
\mathscr{H}
. Hence, if
\operatorname{dom}(\delta^{\ast})
is cyclic, then it is dense in
\mathscr{H}
. Therefore,
\delta
is closable.
∎

4.
Completely Dirichlet forms associated with closable derivations

In this section we prove the main theorem of this article, Theorem
4.4
, showing that the closure of the quadratic form associated with a closable symmetric derivation is a modular completely Dirichlet form.

As mentioned in the introduction, we rely on Haagerup’s reduction method. To set up the stage for its use, we first discuss crossed products of Tomita algebras and Tomita bimodules. To extend closable symmetric derivations to a sufficiently large domains on the crossed product, we use the
\delta
-completion technique developed in Section
2
. Further, to reduce the problem to the tracial case, we need a “change of reference weight” argument and an analysis of approximation properties of completely Dirichlet forms. This will be dealt with in the following lemmas. Finally, in Proposition
4.6
we discuss the relation between the derivation we started with and the first-order differential structure of the associated completely Dirichlet form.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a Tomita algebra,
\mathscr{H}
a normal Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
and
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathscr{H}
a closable symmetric derivation. Throughout this section we endow
\mathfrak{A}
with the
\delta
-topology and
\mathscr{H}
with the projective topology induced by the maps
\mathscr{H}\to\bar{\mathscr{H}},\,\xi\mapsto\bar{\mathscr{U}}_{in}\xi
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and we assume that
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathscr{H}
are complete in these topologies. As discussed in Section
2
, this can always be achieved by passing to the completions.

Let
G
be a countable subgroup of
\mathbb{R}
, viewed as discrete group. The vector space
C_{c}(G;\mathfrak{A})\cong C_{c}(G)\odot\mathfrak{A}
can be made into a Tomita algebra by the operations

\displaystyle(a\ast b)(g)

\displaystyle=\sum_{h\in G}U_{-h}a(g-h)b(h),

\displaystyle a^{\sharp}(g)

\displaystyle=U_{-g}(a(-g)^{\sharp}),

\displaystyle(U_{z}a)(g)

\displaystyle=U_{z}a(g).

Moreover, the vector space
C_{c}(G;\mathscr{H})
becomes a normal Tomita bimodule over
C_{c}(G;\mathfrak{A})
with the operations

\displaystyle(a\xi)(g)

\displaystyle=\sum_{h\in G}U_{-h}a(g-h)\xi(h)

\displaystyle(\xi b)(g)

\displaystyle=\sum_{h\in G}\mathscr{U}_{-h}\xi(g-h)b(h)

\displaystyle(\tilde{\mathscr{J}}\xi)(g)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{U}_{-g}\mathscr{J}\xi(-g)

\displaystyle(\tilde{\mathscr{U}}_{z}\xi)(g)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{U}_{z}\xi(g).

Furthermore,
1_{C_{c}(G)}\odot\delta\colon C_{c}(G;\mathfrak{A})\to C_{c}(G;\mathscr{H})
is a closable symmetric derivation, whose closure we denote by
1\otimes\bar{\delta}
.

We write
\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}
for the
(1\odot\delta)
-completion of
C_{c}(G;\mathfrak{A})
,
\tilde{\mathscr{H}}
for
C_{c}(G;\mathscr{H})^{\mathrm{a}}
and
\tilde{\delta}
for the restriction of
1\otimes\bar{\delta}
to
\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}
. By Lemma
2.4
the map
\tilde{\delta}
is a (closable) symmetric derivation from
\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}
to
\tilde{\mathscr{H}}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
x\in L(G)\otimes\mathbb{C}1_{\bar{\mathscr{H}}}
and
a\in\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}
, then
xa,ax\in\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}
and
\tilde{\delta}(xa)=x\tilde{\delta}(a)
,
\tilde{\delta}(ax)=\tilde{\delta}(a)x
.

Proof.

Let
x=y\otimes 1
with
y\in L(G)
, let
(y_{i})
be a bounded net in
\mathbb{C}[G]
such that
y_{i}\to y
in the strong
∗
topology and let
x_{i}=y_{i}\otimes 1
. Clearly,
x_{i}\to x
in the Mackey topology.

If
a\in C_{c}(G;\mathfrak{A})
, then
x_{i}a\in C_{c}(G;\mathfrak{A})
and
\Delta^{n}(x_{i}a)=x_{i}\Delta^{n}a
,
(1\odot\delta)(x_{i}a)=x_{i}(1\odot\delta)(a)
,
\pi_{l}(\Delta^{n}(x_{i}a))=x_{i}\pi_{l}(\Delta^{n}a)
. It follows that

in
\ell^{2}(G;\mathfrak{H})
, the net
(x_{i}a)
is Cauchy in the
\tilde{\delta}
-topology and
(1\odot\delta)(x_{i}a)\to x(1\odot\delta)(a)
. Thus
xa\in\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}
and
\tilde{\delta}(xa)=x\tilde{\delta}(a)
.

A similar argument shows that if
(a_{j})
is a Cauchy net in
C_{c}(G;\mathfrak{A})
with respect to
\mathscr{T}_{\tilde{\delta}}
and
a_{j}\to a
in
\ell^{2}(G;\mathscr{H})
, then
(xa_{j})
is Cauchy with respect to
\mathscr{T}_{\tilde{\delta}}
and
xa_{j}\to xa
in
\ell^{2}(G;\mathscr{H})
. Hence if
a\in\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}
, then
xa\in\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}
and
\tilde{\delta}(xa)=x\tilde{\delta}(a)
. The statement for

can be proven analogously.
∎

For the next lemma recall that
\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}=\Lambda_{\varphi}(\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast})
is the full left Hilbert algebra induced by the weight
\varphi
, the cone
C_{\varphi}
is the closure of
\{\Delta_{\varphi}^{1/4}a\mid a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi},0\leq\pi_{l}(a)\leq 1\}
, and
M_{\varphi}
denotes the centralizer of
\varphi
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra,
\varphi
a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
M
and
x\in M_{\varphi}
be positive and invertible. Let
\psi=\varphi(x^{1/2}\cdot x^{1/2})
.

If
\mathscr{E}
is a modular (completely) Dirichlet form on

with respect to
\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
,
x\operatorname{dom}(\mathscr{E})\subset\operatorname{dom}(\mathscr{E})
and
\mathscr{E}(xa,b)=\mathscr{E}(a,xb)
for all
a,b\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathscr{E})
, then
\mathscr{E}
is also a modular (completely) Dirichlet from with respect to
\mathfrak{A}_{\psi}
.

Proof.

Since
x
is invertible, the weight
\psi
is faithful and
J_{\psi}=J_{\varphi}
, and since
x
commutes with
(\Delta_{\varphi}^{it})
, we have
\Delta_{\psi}^{it}=x^{it}\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}(\cdot)x^{-it}=\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}(x^{it}\cdot x^{-it})
.

Let
A
be the positive self-adjoint operator associated with
\mathscr{E}
and
T_{t}=e^{-tA}
. The commutation relation
x\operatorname{dom}(\mathscr{E})\subset\operatorname{dom}(\mathscr{E})
and
\mathscr{E}(xa,b)=\mathscr{E}(a,xb)
for
a,b\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathscr{E})
implies that
x
commutes strongly with
A^{1/2}
. Hence
T_{t}(xa)=xT_{t}(a)
for all
a\in H
and
t\geq 0
. Since
T_{t}
commutes with
J_{\varphi}
, we also have
T_{t}(ax)=T_{t}(a)x
for
a\in H
,
t\geq 0
. In particular,
(T_{t})
and
(\Delta_{\psi}^{is})
commute.

Moreover,
C_{\psi}=x^{1/4}C_{\varphi}x^{1/4}
. Indeed, a direct computation shows that
\mathfrak{n}_{\psi}=\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
and
\Lambda_{\psi}(y)=\Lambda_{\varphi}(y)x^{1/2}
for
y\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
. Hence, if
y\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
with

, then

\Delta_{\psi}^{1/4}\Lambda_{\psi}(y)=x^{1/4}\Delta_{\varphi}^{1/4}\Delta_{\varphi}(y)x^{1/4}\in x^{1/4}C_{\varphi}x^{1/4}.

The converse inclusion follows by swapping the roles of
\varphi
and
\psi
.

Therefore, if
a\in C_{\psi}
, then

T_{t}(a)=x^{1/4}T_{t}(x^{-1/4}ax^{-1/4})x^{1/4}\in C_{\psi}.

Thus
\mathscr{E}
is a Dirichlet form with respect to

by
[
GL99

. The result for completely Dirichlet forms follows easily by applying the same argument to the forms
\mathscr{E}^{(n)}
on

.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra and
\varphi
a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
M
. Let
(M_{n})
be an increasing sequence of von Neumann subalgebras with weak
∗
dense union and assume that
M_{n}
is the range of a
\varphi
-preserving conditional expectation
E_{n}
on
M
. Let
H_{n}
denote the closure of
\Lambda_{\varphi}(\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\cap M_{n})
and let
P_{n}
denote the orthogonal projection from
H
to
H_{n}
.

If
\mathscr{E}
is a closed densely defined quadratic form on
H
such that for every

the quadratic form
\mathscr{E}|_{H_{n}}
is a Dirichlet form with respect to
\Lambda_{\varphi}(\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast}\cap M_{n})
and
\mathscr{E}\circ P_{n}\leq\mathscr{E}
, then
\mathscr{E}
is a Dirichlet form with respect to
\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
.

Proof.

Let
(T_{t})
be the strongly continuous semigroup associated with
\mathscr{E}
. Since
\mathscr{E}\circ P_{n}\leq\mathscr{E}
, we have
T_{t}(H_{n})\subset H_{n}
by Ouhabaz’ theorem
[
Ouh96
, Corollary 2.4]
. Thus
T_{t}
commutes with
P_{n}
, and it is easy to see that
(T_{t}P_{n})
is the semigroup associated with
\mathscr{E}|_{H_{n}}
, viewed as semigroup on
H
. In particular,
T_{t}P_{n}J_{\varphi}=J_{\varphi}T_{t}P_{n}
. In the limit we obtain
T_{t}J_{\varphi}=J_{\varphi}T_{t}
.

It remains to show that
T_{t}(C_{\varphi})\subset C_{\varphi}
for all
t\geq 0
. A direct computation shows that
E_{n}
and
P_{n}
are related by
P_{n}\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)=\Lambda_{\varphi}(E_{n}(x))
. Moreover,
E_{n}
is GNS-symmetric with respect to
\varphi
, which implies that
P_{n}
commutes with
(\Delta_{\varphi}^{it})
. Thus

is the closure of
\{\Delta_{\varphi}^{1/4}\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)\mid x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast}\cap M_{n},\,0\leq x\leq 1\}
. In particular,

.

Since
\mathscr{E}_{n}
is a Dirichlet form with respect to

and
(T_{t}P_{n})
is the associated semigroup, we have

. Moreover, since

is weak
∗
dense in

strongly by Kaplansky’s density theorem. Therefore,

.
∎

To prove that the quadratic form associated with a closable symmetric derivation is a completely Dirichlet form, we will reduce the problem to the tracially symmetric case by means of Haagerup’s reduction method. We only recall the necessary definitions here and refer to
[

]
for proofs in the case of states and to
[

]
for the extension to weights.

Let
M=\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
, let
\varphi
be the weight induced by the full left Hilbert algebra
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime\prime}
on
M
, let
G=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}2^{-n}\mathbb{Z}
, let
\tilde{M}=M\rtimes_{\sigma^{\varphi}}G=\pi_{l}(\tilde{\mathfrak{A}})^{\prime\prime}
and let
\tilde{\varphi}
be the dual weight of
\varphi
on
\tilde{M}
. Let
(a_{n})
be a sequence of self-adjoint elements of

,
\varphi_{n}=\varphi e^{-a_{n}}
,

and
\tau_{n}=\varphi_{n}|_{M_{n}}
. Here

denotes the centralizer of the weight

on

and
\mathscr{Z}(M)
is the center of the von Neumann algebra

By
[

, Theorem 8.1]
the sequence

can be chosen such that

•

is semi-finite with normal semi-finite faithful trace
\tau_{n}
,

•

for each

there exists a conditional expectation

from
M

such that

and
\sigma^{\tilde{\varphi}}_{t}\circ E_{n}=E_{n}\circ\sigma^{\tilde{\varphi}}_{t}

•

strongly
∗
for every

.

In the following we fix a sequence

with these properties. The concrete construction is irrelevant for our purposes.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a Tomita algebra with completion
\mathfrak{H}
, let
\mathscr{H}
be a normal Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
and
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathscr{H}
a closable symmetric derivation. The closure
\mathscr{E}
of the quadratic form

\mathfrak{H}\to[0,\infty],\,a\mapsto\begin{cases}\lVert\delta(a)\rVert_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}&\text{if }a\in\mathfrak{A},\\
\infty&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

is a modular completely Dirichlet form with respect to
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime\prime}
. If moreover
\mathfrak{A}
is unital, then
\mathscr{E}
is a modular quantum Dirichlet form.

Proof.

We continue to use the notation from the previous discussion. The derivation
\tilde{\delta}\colon\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}\to\tilde{\mathscr{H}}
is a restriction of
1\otimes\bar{\delta}
. Let
\tilde{\mathscr{E}}
denote the closure of the quadratic form

\ell^{2}(G;\mathfrak{H})\to[0,\infty],\,a\mapsto\begin{cases}\lVert\tilde{\delta}(a)\rVert_{\tilde{\mathscr{H}}}^{2}&\text{if }a\in\tilde{\mathfrak{A}},\\
\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

It is clear that
\tilde{\mathscr{E}}(a)=\lVert(1\otimes\bar{\delta})(a)\rVert_{\ell^{2}(G;\bar{\mathscr{H}})}^{2}
for
a\in\operatorname{dom}(\tilde{\mathscr{E}})
. Furthermore,
\operatorname{dom}(\tilde{\mathscr{E}})=\operatorname{dom}(1\otimes\bar{\delta})
and the strongly continuous semigroups
(T_{t})
and
(\tilde{T}_{t})
associated with
\mathscr{E}
and
\tilde{\mathscr{E}}
, respectively, are related by
\tilde{T}_{t}=\mathrm{id}_{\ell^{2}(G)}\otimes T_{t}
.

The map
\iota\colon\mathfrak{H}\to\ell^{2}(G;\mathfrak{H}),\,a\mapsto\mathds{1}_{0}\otimes a
is an isometric embedding such that
\iota(C_{\mathfrak{A}^{\prime\prime}})=C_{\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}^{\prime\prime}}\cap\iota(\mathfrak{H})
. Thus, if
\tilde{\mathscr{E}}
is a (completely) Dirichlet form with respect to
\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}^{\prime\prime}
, then
\mathscr{E}
is a (completely) Dirichlet form with respect to
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime\prime}
.

Since
M
is in standard form on
\mathfrak{H}
and
\tilde{M}
is in standard form on
\ell^{2}(G;\mathfrak{H})
, these spaces can be canonically identified with
L^{2}(M,\varphi)
and
L^{2}(\tilde{M},\tilde{\varphi})
, respectively, and we will tacitly do so in the following. Under these identifications,
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime\prime}=\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
,
\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}^{\prime\prime}=\mathfrak{A}_{\tilde{\varphi}}
and
\Delta_{\tilde{\varphi}}^{it}=\mathrm{id}_{\ell^{2}(G)}\otimes\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}
.

Let

\mathscr{A}_{n}=\{x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\tilde{\varphi}}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\tilde{\varphi}}^{\ast}\cap M_{n}\mid\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(xe^{-a_{n}/2})\in\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}\}.

Since
e^{a_{n}/2}\in\tilde{M}_{\tilde{\varphi}}
, if
x\in\mathscr{A}_{n}
, then

\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(x)=\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(xe^{-a_{n}/2})e^{a_{n}/2}\in\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}

by Lemma
4.1
. Reversing the roles of
e^{-a_{n}/2}
and
e^{a_{n}/2}
we get

\mathscr{A}_{n}=\{x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\tilde{\varphi}}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\tilde{\varphi}}^{\ast}\cap M_{n}\mid\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(x)\in\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}\}.

Since
\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}
is a Tomita algebra, it follows easily that
\mathscr{A}_{n}
is a
\ast
-algebra. Define an
\mathscr{A}_{n}
-
\mathscr{A}_{n}
-bimodule structure on
\ell^{2}(G;\bar{\mathscr{H}})
by

x\xi y=\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(x)\cdot\xi\cdot\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}^{\prime}(y).

Using that
\tilde{\mathscr{H}}
is a Tomita bimodule over
\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}
, it is not hard to see that this left and right action are contractive (anti-)
\ast
-homomorphisms. Moreover,
\tilde{\mathscr{J}}
extends to an anti-unitary involution on
\ell^{2}(G;\bar{\mathscr{H}})
intertwining the left and right action. We still denote this extension by
\tilde{\mathscr{J}}
.

Let

\partial_{n}\colon\mathscr{A}_{n}\to L^{2}(\tilde{M},\tilde{\varphi}),\,\partial_{n}(x)=\tilde{\delta}(\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(xe^{-a_{n}/2})).

Since
e^{-a_{n}/2}\in\tilde{M}_{\tilde{\varphi}}
and
x\in\tilde{M}_{\varphi_{n}}
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{n}(x^{\ast})

\displaystyle=\tilde{\delta}(\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(x^{\ast}e^{-a_{n}/2}))

\displaystyle=\tilde{\delta}(\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}^{\prime}(e^{-a_{n}/2}x^{\ast}))

\displaystyle=\tilde{\delta}(\tilde{J}\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(xe^{-a_{n}/2}))

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathscr{J}}\tilde{\delta}(\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(xe^{-a_{n}/2}))

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathscr{J}}\partial_{n}(x).

Moreover, it follows from Lemma
4.1
combined with
e^{-a_{n}/2}\in\tilde{M}_{\tilde{\varphi}}
and
x,y\in\tilde{M}_{\varphi_{n}}
that

\displaystyle\partial_{n}(xy)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\delta}(\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(xye^{-a_{n}/2}))

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(x)\cdot\tilde{\delta}(\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(ye^{-a_{n}/2}))+\tilde{\delta}(\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(x))\cdot\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(ye^{-a_{n}/2})

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(x)\cdot\tilde{\delta}(\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(ye^{-a_{n}/2}))+\tilde{\delta}(\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(xe^{-a_{n}/2}))\cdot\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(e^{a_{n}/2}ye^{-a_{n}/2})

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(x)\cdot\tilde{\delta}(\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(ye^{-a_{n}/2}))+\tilde{\delta}(\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(xe^{-a_{n}/2}))\cdot\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}^{\prime}(\sigma^{\varphi_{n}}_{-i/2}(y))

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(x)\cdot\tilde{\delta}(\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(ye^{-a_{n}/2}))+\tilde{\delta}(\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(xe^{-a_{n}/2}))\cdot\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}^{\prime}(y)

\displaystyle=x\partial_{n}(y)+\partial_{n}(x)y.

The operator
\partial_{n}
is closable when viewed as operator in
L^{2}(M_{n},\tau_{n})
since
\tilde{\delta}
is closable and the map
\Lambda_{\tau_{n}}(x)\mapsto\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(xe^{-a_{n}/2})
extends to an isometry
\iota_{n}
from
L^{2}(M_{n},\tau_{n})
to
L^{2}(\tilde{M},\tilde{\varphi})
.

Since
\tau_{n}
is a trace,
[
Cip08
, Theorem 4.5]
implies that the closure
Q_{n}
of the quadratic form

L^{2}(M_{n},\tau_{n})\to[0,\infty],\,a\mapsto\begin{cases}\lVert\partial_{n}(x)\rVert^{2}&\text{if }a=\Lambda_{\tau_{n}}(x),\,x\in\mathscr{A}_{n},\\
\infty&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

is a completely Dirichlet form.

Let
H_{n}=\overline{\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(\mathfrak{n}_{\tilde{\varphi}}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\tilde{\varphi}}^{\ast}\cap M_{n})}
and let
\mathscr{E}_{n}
be the closure of the quadratic form

H_{n}\to[0,\infty],\,a\mapsto\begin{cases}\lVert\tilde{\delta}(a)\rVert^{2}&\text{if }a\in\tilde{\mathfrak{A}},\\
\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

In other words,
\mathscr{E}_{n}=Q_{n}\circ\iota_{n}^{-1}
.

Note that
\iota_{n}
maps
\{\Lambda_{\tau_{n}}(x)\mid x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\tilde{\varphi}}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\tilde{\varphi}}^{\ast}\cap M_{n},0\leq x\leq 1\}
onto
\{\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(xe^{-a_{n}/2})\mid x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\tilde{\varphi}}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\tilde{\varphi}}^{\ast}\cap M_{n},0\leq x\leq 1\}
. Since
\varphi_{n}
is a trace on
M_{n}
, the latter set coincides with
\{\Lambda_{\varphi_{n}}(x)\mid x\in\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi_{n}}\cap H_{n},\,0\leq x\leq 1\}
. It follows that
\mathscr{E}_{n}
is a completely Dirichlet form with respect to
\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi_{n}}\cap H_{n}
.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{n}(\Delta_{\varphi_{n}}^{it}a)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{E}_{n}(e^{-ia_{n}/2t}(\Delta_{\tilde{\varphi}}^{it}a)e^{ia_{n}/2t})

\displaystyle=\lVert e^{-ia_{n}/2t}(\tilde{\mathscr{U}}_{t}\tilde{\delta}(a))e^{ia_{n}/2t}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\lVert\tilde{\delta}(a)\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{E}_{n}(a)

for
a\in\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}
. This can easily be extended to the closure so that
\mathscr{E}_{n}
is a modular completely Dirichlet form.

By Lemma
4.1
we have
e^{-a_{n}/2}\operatorname{dom}(\mathscr{E}_{n})\subset\operatorname{dom}(\mathscr{E}_{n})
and
\mathscr{E}_{n}(e^{a_{n}/2}a,b)=\mathscr{E}_{n}(a,e^{-a_{n}/2}b)
for
a,b\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathscr{E}_{n})
. Furthermore,
e^{-a_{n}/2}\in\tilde{M}_{\tilde{\varphi}}
. Hence
\mathscr{E}_{n}
is also a modular completely Dirichlet form with respect to
\mathfrak{A}_{\tilde{\varphi}}\cap H_{n}=\Lambda_{\tilde{\varphi}}(\mathfrak{n}_{\tilde{\varphi}}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\tilde{\varphi}}^{\ast}\cap M_{n})
by Lemma
4.2
.

Let
P_{n}
denote the orthogonal projection from
\ell^{2}(G;\mathfrak{H})
onto
H_{n}
. By definition,
\tilde{\mathscr{E}}|_{H_{n}}=\mathscr{E}_{n}
. To apply Lemma
4.3
, we have to check that
\mathscr{E}\circ P_{n}\leq\mathscr{E}
.

Let
(T_{t})
be the strongly continuous semigroup associated with
\mathscr{E}
. As discussed above,
(\mathrm{id}_{\ell^{2}(G)}\otimes T_{t})
is the strongly continuous semigroup associated with
\tilde{\mathscr{E}}
. The modular group of
\varphi_{n}
is given by
\Delta_{\varphi_{n}}^{it}=e^{-ita_{n}}(\mathrm{id}_{\ell^{2}(G)}\otimes\Delta_{\varphi}^{it})(\cdot)e^{ita_{n}}
. Since
(T_{t})
commutes with
(\Delta_{\varphi}^{it})
and
e^{a_{n}}\in L(G)\otimes\mathbb{C}1_{\mathfrak{H}}
, the semigroup
(\mathrm{id}_{\ell^{2}(G)}\otimes T_{t})
commutes with
(\Delta_{\varphi_{n}}^{it})
.

Since
M_{n}
is the centralizer of
\varphi_{n}
, the subspace
H_{n}
is the fixed-point set of
(\Delta_{\tilde{\varphi}_{n}}^{it})
. In particular,
(\mathrm{id}_{\ell^{2}(G)}\otimes T_{t})(H_{n})\subset H_{n}
. From Ouhabaz’s theorem
[
Ouh96
, Corollary 2.4]
we deduce
\mathscr{E}\circ P_{n}\leq\mathscr{E}
.

Now Lemma
4.3
shows that
\mathscr{E}
is a modular completely Dirichlet form with respect to
\mathfrak{A}_{\tilde{\varphi}}
.

If
\mathfrak{A}
is unital with unit
1_{\mathfrak{A}}
, then the left and right action of
\mathfrak{A}
on
\mathscr{H}
are unital since they are non-degenerate by definition. Thus

\delta(1_{\mathfrak{A}})=1_{\mathfrak{A}}\cdot\delta(1_{\mathfrak{A}})+\delta(1_{\mathfrak{A}})\cdot 1_{\mathfrak{A}}-\delta(1_{\mathfrak{A}})=0

and hence
\mathscr{E}(1_{\mathfrak{A}})=0
. Thus
T_{t}(1_{\mathfrak{A}})=0
, which implies that
\mathscr{E}
is a quantum Dirichlet form.
∎

In the situation of the previous theorem, we call
\mathscr{E}
the
completely Dirichlet form associated with
\delta
.

Remark 4.5
.

If
\mathfrak{A}
is not unital, the completely Dirichlet form associated with a derivation is not necessarily a quantum Dirichlet form, even in the commutative case. For example, this is the case for the standard Dirichlet energy
\mathscr{E}(f)=\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla f\rvert^{2}
with domain
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
if
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain.

Proposition 4.6
.

If
\mathfrak{A}
is a unital Tomita algebra,
\mathscr{H}
a normal Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
and
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathscr{H}
a closable symmetric derivation with associated completely Dirichlet form
\mathscr{E}
, then the first-order differential calculus associated with
\mathscr{E}
is a corestriction of
(\mathscr{H}^{\mathrm{a}},\bar{\delta}|_{\mathfrak{A}_{\mathscr{E}}})
. In particular,

\bar{\delta}(ab)=a\bar{\delta}(b)+\bar{\delta}(a)b

for
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathscr{E}}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{A}
is unital,
\mathscr{E}
is a modular quantum Dirichlet form by
4.4
. Let
(\mathscr{H}_{\mathscr{E}},\delta_{\mathscr{E}})
be a first-order differential calculus associated with
\mathscr{E}
. By definition,
\mathfrak{A}\subset\mathfrak{A}_{\mathscr{E}}\subset\mathfrak{A}^{\prime\prime}
and the graph norm of
\bar{\delta}
coincides with the graph norm of
\delta_{\mathscr{E}}
on
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathscr{E}}
. Thus
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
is strong
∗
dense in
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A}_{\mathscr{E}})^{\prime\prime}
and
\mathfrak{A}
is a core for
\delta_{\mathscr{E}}
. It follows that the linear hull of
\{\delta_{\mathscr{E}}(a)b\mid a,b\in\mathfrak{A}\}
is dense in
\mathscr{H}_{\mathscr{E}}
.

Let

U\colon\mathrm{lin}\{\delta_{\mathscr{E}}(a)b\mid a,b\in\mathfrak{A}\}\to\mathscr{H},\,U(\delta_{\mathscr{E}}(a)b)=\delta(a)b.

By
[
Wir22
, Lemma 5.9]
the map
U
is well-defined and extends to an isometric
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathscr{E}}
-bimodule map from
\bar{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathscr{E}}
to
\bar{\mathscr{H}}
such that
U(\delta_{\mathscr{E}}(a))=\delta(a)
for
a\in\mathfrak{A}
.

If
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathscr{E}}
, let
(a_{n})
be a sequence in
\mathfrak{A}
such that
\lVert a_{n}-a\rVert_{\bar{\delta}}\to 0
. As discussed above, this implies
\delta_{\mathscr{E}}(a_{n})\to\delta_{\mathscr{E}}(a)
. Hence
U(\delta_{\mathscr{E}}(a))=\bar{\delta}(a)
. If
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathscr{E}}
, then

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}(ab)

\displaystyle=U(\delta_{\mathscr{E}}(ab))

\displaystyle=U(a\delta_{\mathscr{E}}(b)+\delta_{\mathscr{E}}(a)b)

\displaystyle=aU(\delta_{\mathscr{E}}(b))+U(\delta_{\mathscr{E}}(a))b

\displaystyle=a\bar{\delta}(b)+\bar{\delta}(a)b.

Moreover,
\delta J=\mathscr{J}\delta
can be extended by continuity to
\bar{\delta}J=\mathscr{J}\bar{\delta}
, and

\mathbb{C}\to\bar{\mathscr{H}},\,z\mapsto\bar{\delta}(U_{z}a)

is an entire continuation of
t\mapsto\mathscr{U}_{t}\bar{\delta}(a)
for
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathscr{E}}
by
[
Wir22
, Lemma 6.6]
.

Thus
\bar{\delta}(\mathfrak{A}_{\mathscr{E}})\subset\mathscr{H}^{\mathrm{a}}
and
\bar{\delta}
is a symmetric derivation on
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathscr{E}}
. The statement now follows from the uniqueness of the first-order differential calculus associated with a modular completely Dirichlet form
[
Wir22
, Theorem 6.9]
.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

The previous result holds more generally with the same proof if
\mathfrak{A}
is not necessarily unital, but the completely Dirichlet form associated with
\delta
is still a quantum Dirichlet form.

Remark 4.8
.

In the light of Lemma
2.2
, one has
\widehat{\mathfrak{A}}^{\delta}\subset\mathfrak{A}_{\mathscr{E}}
in the situation of the previous proposition. It is an interesting question if one always has equality or if different derivations with
\delta
-complete domains can have the same associated completely Dirichlet form.

5.
Examples

In this section we present several classes of derivations that give rise to modular completely Dirichlet forms according to Theorem
4.4
. The first three classes of examples concern inner derivations, before we treat derivations arising in non-tracial free probability in Example
5.4
and derivations induced by cocycles on locally compact groups in Example
5.5
.

Example 5.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a Tomita algebra,
\mathscr{H}
a normal Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
and
\xi\in\mathscr{H}
be a bounded vector. Assume that there exists
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathscr{U}_{t}\xi=e^{i\omega t}\xi
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
The map

\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathscr{H}\oplus\mathscr{H},\,a\mapsto i(\xi a-a\xi,(\mathscr{J}\xi)a-a(\mathscr{J}\xi))

is a bounded derivation, and it is symmetric when
\mathscr{H}\oplus\mathscr{H}
is endowed with the involution
(\eta,\zeta)\mapsto(\mathscr{J}\zeta,\mathscr{J}\eta)
and the complex one-parameter group
(e^{-i\omega z}\mathscr{U}_{z},e^{i\omega z}\mathscr{U}_{z})
.

It follows that the closure of the quadratic form

\mathfrak{A}\to[0,\infty),\,a\mapsto\lVert\xi a-a\xi\rVert_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}+\lVert(\mathscr{J}\xi)a-a(\mathscr{J}\xi)\rVert_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}

is a (bounded) modular completely Dirichlet form with respect to
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime\prime}
. In the case
\mathscr{H}=\mathfrak{A}
, this was first proven by Cipriani
[
Cip97
, Proposition 5.3]
. See also
[
Wir22
, Proposition 5.5]
for arbitrary Tomita bimodules
\mathscr{H}
over
\mathfrak{A}
.

The next example is a (partial) extension of the previous example allowing for vectors implementing the inner derivation that are not necessarily bounded.

Example 5.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a Tomita algebra with Hilbert completion
\mathfrak{H}
. For
\xi\in\operatorname{dom}(\Delta^{1/2})
the operator

\pi_{l}^{0}(\xi)\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{H},\,a\mapsto\xi a

is closable since
\pi_{l}^{0}(\xi^{\sharp})\subset\pi_{l}^{0}(\xi)^{\ast}
. Likewise, if
\xi\in\operatorname{dom}(\Delta^{-1/2})
, then

\pi_{r}^{0}(\xi)\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{H},\,a\mapsto a\xi

is closable with
\pi_{r}^{0}(\xi^{\flat})\subset\pi_{r}^{0}(\xi)^{\ast}
. Hence if
\xi\in\operatorname{dom}(\Delta^{-1/2})\cap\operatorname{dom}(\Delta^{1/2})
, then
\pi_{l}^{0}(\xi)-\pi_{r}^{0}(\xi)
is closable with
\pi_{l}^{0}(\xi^{\sharp})-\pi_{r}^{0}(\xi^{\flat})\subset(\pi_{l}^{0}(\xi)-\pi_{r}^{0}(\xi))^{\ast}
.

Now assume that there exists
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\Delta^{it}\xi=e^{i\omega t}\xi
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. This implies in particular
\xi\in\operatorname{dom}(\Delta^{-1/2})\cap\operatorname{dom}(\Delta^{1/2})
.

Similar to the last example, one can turn
\mathfrak{A}\oplus\mathfrak{A}
into a Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
if one equips it with the usual bimodule structure, the involution
(\eta,\zeta)\mapsto(J\zeta,J\eta)
and the complex one-parameter group
(e^{-i\omega z}U_{z},e^{i\omega z}U_{z})
.Then the map

\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{A}\oplus\mathfrak{A},\,a\mapsto i(\xi a-a\xi,(J\xi)a-a(J\xi))

is a closable symmetric derivation.

Thus the closure of the quadratic form

\mathfrak{A}\to[0,\infty),\,a\mapsto\lVert\xi a-a\xi\rVert_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}+\lVert(J\xi)a-a(J\xi)\rVert_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}

is a modular completely Dirichlet form with respect to
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime\prime}
. This result has first been obtained by Cipriani and Zegarlinski
[
CZ21
, Theorem 2.5]
.

The previous examples require eigenvectors of the modular group to construct a symmetric derivation, which may be hard to find. In the following examples we show that in certain situations one can start with an arbitrary element if one “averages” the action of the modular group to ensure modularity.

Example 5.3
.

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra with separable predual. A normal semi-finite weight faithful weight
\varphi
on
M
is called
integrable

[
CT77
, Definition II.2.1]
if

\mathfrak{q}_{\varphi}=\left\{x\in M:\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x^{\ast}x)\,dt\text{ exists in the $\sigma$-strong topology}\right\}

is weak
∗
dense in
M
.

If
\varphi
is integrable, the set

\mathfrak{A}=\{\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)\mid x\in M\text{ analytic for }\sigma^{\varphi},\sigma^{\varphi}_{z}(x)\in\mathfrak{q}_{\varphi}\cap\mathfrak{q}_{\varphi}^{\ast}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast}\text{ for all }z\in\mathbb{C}\}

is a Tomita subalgebra of
(\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi})_{0}
with Hilbert completion
L^{2}(M)
and
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}=M
, as can be seen from
[
CT77
, Lemma II.2.3]
together with a standard mollifying argument.

Let
(V_{t})
be the translation group on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, that is,
V_{t}f(s)=f(s+t)
, and let
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})^{\mathrm{a}}
be the set of all entire analytic elements for
(V_{t})
. Endow
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})^{\mathrm{a}}\odot\mathfrak{A}
with the left and right action of
\mathfrak{A}
given by
a(f\otimes b)c=f\otimes abc
, the complex one-parameter group
(V_{z}\odot U_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{C}}
and the involution
f\otimes a\mapsto\bar{f}\otimes Ja
. It can be checked that this makes
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})^{\mathrm{a}}\odot\mathfrak{A}
into a normal Tomita bimodule, which we denote by
\mathscr{H}
.

Let
a\in\operatorname{dom}(\Delta_{\varphi}^{1/2})\cap\operatorname{dom}(\Delta_{\varphi}^{-1/2})
with
Ja=a
and define

\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R};L^{2}(M)),\,\delta(b)(s)=(U_{-s}a)b-b(U_{-s}a).

We have

\displaystyle\delta(U_{t}b)(s)

\displaystyle=(U_{-s}a)(U_{t}b)-(U_{t}b)(U_{-s}a)

\displaystyle=U_{t}((U_{-(s+t)}a)b-b(U_{-(s+t)}a))

\displaystyle=U_{t}\delta(b)(s+t).

Thus
\delta\circ U_{t}=(U_{t}\otimes V_{t})\circ\delta
. In particular,
\delta
maps into
\mathscr{H}^{\mathrm{a}}
.

It is not hard to check that
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathscr{H}^{\mathrm{a}}
is a symmetric derivation. To show closability, first note that for every fixed
s\in\mathbb{R}
the map
b\mapsto\delta(b)(s)
is closable as seen in the previous example. If
b_{n}\to 0
and
\delta(b_{n})\to\xi
, then there exists a subsequence such that
\delta(b_{n_{k}})(s)\to\xi(s)
for a.e.
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Closability of the map
b\mapsto\delta(b)(s)
implies
\xi(s)=0
for a.e.
s\in\mathbb{R}
, which proves the closability of
\delta
.

Thus the closure of the quadratic form

\mathfrak{A}\to[0,\infty),\,b\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lVert(U_{-s}a)b-b(U_{-s}a)\rVert^{2}\,ds

is a modular completely Dirichlet form.

If we drop the assumption
Ja=a
, a similar argument shows that the closure of the quadratic form

\mathfrak{A}\to[0,\infty),\,b\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\lVert(U_{-s}a)b-b(U_{-s}a)\rVert^{2}+\lVert(U_{-s}Ja)b+b(U_{-s}Ja)\rVert^{2})\,ds

is a modular completely Dirichlet form with respect to
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime\prime}
.

A similar construction is possible if one starts with a weight with periodic modular group instead of an integrable weight and integrates over a period of the modular group.

The following class of examples of derivations was introduced by Nelson
[
Nel17
]
in the context of non-tracial free probability.

Example 5.4
.

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra,
\varphi
a normal faithful state on
M
and
B\subset M
a
\ast
-subalgebra. Let
\partial\colon B\to M\overline{\otimes}M
be a linear map such that

\partial(xy)=(x\otimes 1)\cdot\partial(y)+\partial(x)\cdot(1\otimes y)

for
x,y\in B
. Note that Nelson works with
M\overline{\otimes}M^{\mathrm{op}}
instead, but under the identification
x\otimes y\mapsto x\otimes y^{\mathrm{op}}
, the
M
-bimodules
M\overline{\otimes}M
and
M\overline{\otimes}M^{\mathrm{op}}
(with the bimodule structure used in
[
Nel17
]
) are isomorphic.

Let
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
and write
M_{\infty}
for the set of entire analytic elements for
\sigma^{\varphi}
. Nelson
[
Nel17
, Definition 3.2]
calls the map
\partial
an
e^{\omega}
-modular derivation if
B\subset M_{\infty}
,
B
is invariant under
\sigma^{\varphi}_{z}
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
,
\partial(B)\subset M_{\infty}\odot M_{\infty}
and

\partial(\sigma^{\varphi}_{z}(x))=e^{i\omega z}(\sigma^{\varphi}_{z}\otimes\sigma^{\varphi}_{z})(\partial(x))

for all
x\in B
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
.

One example given by Nelson is the free difference quotient from free probability (see
[
Nel17
, Definition 3.4]
in the non-tracial case). Given a
\ast
-subalgebra
B
of
M
and an element
a\in M
that is algebraically free from
B
(and
a^{\ast}
is algebraically free from
a
if
a\neq a^{\ast})
, let

\partial_{a}\colon B[a]\to B[a]\odot B[a],\,\partial_{a}(a)=1\otimes 1,\,\delta|_{B}=0

(and
\delta_{a}(a^{\ast})=0
if
a^{\ast}\neq a
). If
a
is an eigenvector of
\Delta_{\varphi}
to the eigenvalue
e^{\omega}
, then
\partial_{a}
is an
e^{\omega}
-modular derivation.

Let us see how an
e^{\omega}
derivation gives rise to a symmetric derivation in our sense. For
x,y\in M
let
(x\otimes y)^{\dagger}=y^{\ast}\otimes x^{\ast}
. The conjugate derivation of
\partial
is the map

\hat{\partial}\colon B\to M_{\infty}\odot M_{\infty},\,\hat{\partial}(x)=\partial(x^{\ast})^{\dagger}.

Let
\mathfrak{A}=\Lambda_{\varphi}(B)
. Since
B
is consists of the analytic elements for
\sigma^{\varphi}
and is invariant under
\sigma^{\varphi}_{z}
for
z\in\mathbb{C}
, the set
\mathfrak{A}
is a Tomita subalgebra of
(\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi})_{0}=\Lambda_{\varphi}(M_{\infty})
.

Let
\mathscr{H}=(\Lambda_{\varphi}(M_{\infty})\odot\Lambda_{\varphi}(M_{\infty}))^{\oplus 2}
with left and right action of
\mathfrak{A}
given by

a(\xi_{1}\otimes\eta_{1},\xi_{2}\otimes\eta_{2})b=(a\xi_{1}\otimes\eta_{1}b,a\xi_{2}\otimes\eta_{2}b),

involution
\mathscr{J}
given by
(\xi_{1}\otimes\eta_{1},\xi_{2}\otimes\eta_{2})\mapsto(J\eta_{2}\otimes J\xi_{2},J\eta_{1}\otimes J\xi_{1})
, and complex one-parameter group
(\mathscr{U}_{z})=(e^{i\omega z}\Delta_{\varphi\otimes\varphi}^{iz},e^{-i\omega z}\Delta_{\varphi\otimes\varphi}^{iz})
. One can check that this makes
\mathscr{H}
into a normal Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
.

Let

\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathscr{H},\,\delta(\Lambda_{\varphi}(x))=(\Lambda_{\varphi\otimes\varphi}(\partial(x)),\Lambda_{\varphi\otimes\varphi}(\partial^{\dagger}(x))).

The product rule for
\partial
and
\hat{\partial}
translate to the product rule for
\delta
, the
e^{\omega}
modularity of
\partial
ensures
\delta\circ\Delta_{\varphi}^{iz}=\mathscr{U}_{z}\circ\delta
and the definition of
\hat{\partial}
and
\mathscr{J}
are tailored to guarantee
\delta\circ J_{\varphi}=\mathscr{J}\circ\delta
.

All of these properties follow by routine calculations, let us just show the product rule (for the first component of)
\delta
as illustration. Let
\delta_{1}(\Lambda_{\varphi}(x))=\Lambda_{\varphi\otimes\varphi}(\partial(x))
. By the product rule for
\partial
we have

\displaystyle\delta_{1}(\Lambda_{\varphi}(xy))

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{\varphi\otimes\varphi}((x\otimes 1)\partial(y)+\partial(x)(1\otimes y))

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{\varphi\otimes\varphi}((x\otimes 1)\Lambda_{\varphi\otimes\varphi}(\partial(y))+\partial(x))(1\otimes\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(y))

\displaystyle=(\pi_{l}(\Lambda_{\varphi}(x))\otimes 1)\delta_{1}(\Lambda_{\varphi}(y))+(1\otimes\pi_{r}(\Lambda_{\varphi}(y)))\delta_{1}(\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)).

Thus, if
\delta
is closable, the closure of the associated quadratic form is a completely Dirichlet form with respect to
\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
on the GNS Hilbert space
L^{2}(M,\varphi)
.

To compare that to the result of Nelson, he showed
[
Nel17
, Proposition 4.4]
that one gets a completely Dirichlet form on the GNS Hilbert space
L^{2}(M_{\varphi},\varphi)
of the
centralizer

M_{\varphi}
of
\varphi
, which is of course a tracial von Neumann algebra.

Our methods allow to extend this result to the “fully” non-tracial setting in that we obtain a modular completely Dirichlet form on the GNS Hilbert space of
M
on not just of the centralizer. Note however that Nelson’s definition of the map
\delta
between
L^{2}
spaces seems slightly different, owing to the use of
M\overline{\otimes}M^{\mathrm{op}}
instead of
M\overline{\otimes}M
.

The last example concerns group von Neumann algebras. The case of discrete groups was treated in
[
CS03
, Section 10.2]
, but to cover general locally compact groups, possibly non-unimodular, one needs the theory for non-tracial reference weights as developed here.

Example 5.5
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group with left Haar measure
\mu
and modular function
\Delta_{G}
. As discussed in
[
Tak03
, Proposition VII.3.1]
, the space
C_{c}(G)
of compactly supported continuous function on
G
with the
L^{2}
inner product, the convolution product, the involution
f^{\sharp}(g)=\Delta_{G}(g)^{-1}\overline{f(g^{-1})}
and the complex one-parameter group
U_{z}f(g)=\Delta_{G}(g)^{iz}f(g)
forms a Tomita algebra. We write
\lambda
and
\rho
for the associated left and right action of
C_{c}(G)
on
L^{2}(G)
and
\mathfrak{A}_{G}
for the associated full left Hilbert algebra.

Let
\pi
be a strongly continuous orthogonal representation of
G
on the real Hilbert space
H
. A continuous map
b\colon G\to H
is called
1
-cocycle if
b(gh)=b(g)+\pi(g)b(h)
for all
g,h\in G
. We extend
\pi
to a unitary representation of
G
on the complexification
H^{\mathbb{C}}
of
H
and write
\xi\mapsto\bar{\xi}
for the anti-unitary involution induced by
H\subset H^{\mathbb{C}}
.

On
C_{c}(G;H^{\mathbb{C}})
define a left and right action of
C_{c}(G)
by

\displaystyle(f\ast\xi)(g)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}f(h)\pi(h)\xi(h^{-1}g)\,d\mu(h)

\displaystyle(\xi\ast f)(g)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}f(h^{-1}g)\xi(h)\,d\mu(h),

an anti-unitary involution by
(\mathscr{J}\xi)(g)=-\Delta_{G}(g)^{-1/2}\pi(g)\overline{\xi(g^{-1})}
and a complex one-parameter group by
\mathscr{U}_{z}\xi(g)=\Delta_{G}(g)^{iz}\xi(g)
. One can check that
C_{c}(G;H^{\mathbb{C}})
with this operations is a Tomita bimodule over
C_{c}(G)
.

Let

\delta\colon C_{c}(G)\to C_{c}(G;H^{\mathbb{C}}),\,\delta(f)(g)=f(g)b(g).

Using the cocycle property of
b
, one gets

\displaystyle\delta(f_{1}\ast f_{2})(g)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}f_{1}(h)f_{2}(h^{-1}g)\,d\mu(h)\,b(g)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}f_{1}(h)f_{2}(h^{-1}g)(\pi(h)b(h^{-1}g)+b(h))\,d\mu(h)

\displaystyle=(f_{1}\ast\delta(f_{2}))(g)+(\delta(f_{1})\ast f_{2})(g).

It is readily verified that
\delta
also satisfies
\delta\circ J=\mathscr{J}\circ\delta
and
\delta\circ U_{z}=\mathscr{U}_{z}\circ\delta
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Hence
\delta
is a symmetric derivation. As a multiplication operator, it is clearly closable.

Therefore,

\mathscr{E}\colon L^{2}(G,\mu)\to[0,\infty],\,\mathscr{E}(f)=\int_{G}\lvert f(g)\rvert^{2}\lVert b(g)\rVert^{2}\,d\mu(g)

is a modular completely Dirichlet form with respect to
\mathfrak{A}_{G}
. The associated quantum dynamical semigroup on
L(G)
is given by

P_{t}\left(\int_{G}\hat{x}(g)\lambda(g)\,d\mu(g)\right)=\int_{G}e^{-t\lVert b(g)\rVert^{2}}\hat{x}(g)\lambda(g)\,d\mu(g).

In this case, complete positivity of
P_{t}
also follows directly from Schönberg’s theorem as
g\mapsto\lVert b(g)\rVert^{2}
is a conditionally negative definite function on
G
.

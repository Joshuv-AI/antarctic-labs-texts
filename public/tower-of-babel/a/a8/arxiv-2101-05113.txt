Beyond Procrustes: Balancing-Free Gradient Descent for Asymmetric Low-Rank Matrix Sensing
Cong Ma, Yuanxin Li, Yuejie Chi

Abstract
Low-rank matrix estimation plays a central role in various applications across science and engineering. Recently, nonconvex formulations based on matrix factorization are provably solved by simple gradient descent algorithms with strong computational and statistical guarantees. However, when the low-rank matrices are asymmetric, existing approaches rely on adding a regularization term to balance the scale of the two matrix factors which in practice can be removed safely without hurting the performance when initialized via the spectral method. In this paper, we provide a theoretical justification to this for the matrix sensing problem, which aims to recover a low-rank matrix from a small number of linear measurements. As long as the measurement ensemble satisfies the restricted isometry property, gradient descent—in conjunction with spectral initialization—converges linearly without the need of explicitly promoting balancedness of the factors; in fact, the factors stay balanced automatically throughout the execution of the algorithm. Our analysis is based on analyzing the evolution of a new distance metric that directly accounts for the ambiguity due to invertible transforms, and might be of independent interest.



Keywords:

asymmetric low-rank matrix sensing, nonconvex optimization, gradient descent

1.1
Why balancing is needed in prior work?

Before we investigate the possibility of a balancing-free procedure (i.e. vanilla gradient descent as in (
3

To handle the asymmetric factorization, it is common to stack the
two factors into one augmented factor

\bm{Y}_{\star}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{(n_{1}+n_{2})\times r}

loss function with respect to the lifted low-rank matrix:
\bm{Z}_{\star}\bm{Z}_{\star}^{\top}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{X}_{\star}\bm{X}_{\star}^{\top}&\bm{X}_{\star}\bm{Y}_{\star}^{\top}\\

the diagonal ones; correspondingly, the loss function is not (restricted)
strongly convex with respect to the augmented factor, unless we appropriately
regularize the diagonal blocks. This gives rise to the adoption of
the regularization term in (

1.2
This paper: balancing-free procedure?

The goal of this paper is to understand the effectiveness of vanilla
gradient descent (
3

Indeed, Figure

.

This paper justifies formally that even without explicit balancing
in asymmetric low-rank matrix sensing, gradient descent converges linearly
towards the global optimum, as long as the initialization is (nearly)
balanced and close to the optimum. As will be detailed later, our analysis is simple and
built on a novel distance metric that directly accounts for the ambiguity
due to invertible transformations—in contrast, the ambiguity set
reduces to orthonormal transforms when the balancing regularization
is present. Our key message is this:

As long as the factors are (nearly) balanced in a basin of attraction at the initialization,
they will stay approximately balanced throughout the trajectory of
gradient descent, and therefore no additional regularization is necessary.

2.1
Low-rank matrix sensing

Low-rank matrix sensing refers to the problem of recovering a low-rank matrix (i.e.
\bm{M}_{\star}
) from a small number of linear measurements. Specifically, we are given a set of
m
measurements as follows

y_{i}=\langle\bm{A}_{i},\bm{M}_{\star}\rangle=\langle\bm{A}_{i},\bm{X}_{\star}\bm{Y}_{\star}^{\top}\rangle,\qquad i=1,\cdots,m,

(7)

where
\bm{A}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}
is the
i
th sensing
matrix. For convenience, we define
\mathcal{A}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}

as an affine transformation from
\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}

to
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, such that
\mathcal{A}\left(\bm{M}\right)=\left\{\langle\bm{A}_{i},\bm{M}\rangle\right\}_{1\leq i\leq m}
.
Consequently, one can compactly write (
7
) as
\bm{y}=\mathcal{A}\left(\bm{M}_{\star}\right)
.
The adjoint operator
\mathcal{A}^{*}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}

is defined as
\mathcal{A}^{*}(\bm{y})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}\bm{A}_{i}
.

To recover the low-rank matrix, a natural choice is to minimize the
least-squares loss function

f\left(\bm{X},\bm{Y}\right)\triangleq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\bm{y}-\mathcal{A}(\bm{X}\bm{Y}^{\top})\right\|_{2}^{2}.

(8)

Algorithm
1

[

for minimizing (
8

Compared to the Procrustes Flow (PF) algorithm in
[

2.2
Theoretical guarantee for local linear convergence

To understand the performance of Algorithm
1
,
we adopt a standard assumption on the sensing operator
\mathcal{A}
,
namely the Restricted Isometry Property (RIP).

Definition 1

(RIP)
.

The operator
\mathcal{A}(\cdot)
is said to satisfy the rank-
r

RIP with a constant
\delta_{r}\in[0,1)
, if

(1-\delta_{r})\left\|\bm{M}\right\|_{{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}\leq\left\|\mathcal{A}(\bm{M})\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq(1+\delta_{r})\left\|\bm{M}\right\|_{{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}

holds for all matrices
\bm{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}
of
rank at most
r
.

It is well-known that many measurement ensembles satisfy the RIP property

[
13
]
. For example, under the Gaussian design where the entries of
\bm{A}_{i}
’s
are composed of i.i.d. Gaussian entries
\mathcal{N}(0,1/m)
,
the RIP is satisfied as long as
m
is on the order of
(n_{1}+n_{2})r/\delta_{r}^{2}
.

Armed with the RIP, we have the following theoretical guarantee for the
local convergence of Algorithm
1
.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{A}(\cdot)

satisfies the RIP with
\delta_{2r}\leq c
for some sufficiently
small constant
c
. Let
\bm{Z}_{0}\triangleq\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\bm{X}_{0}\\
\bm{Y}_{0}\end{array}\right]
be any initialization point that satisfies

\min_{\bm{R}\in\mathcal{O}^{r\times r}}\left\|\bm{Z}_{0}\bm{R}-\bm{Z}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq c_{0}\frac{1}{\kappa^{3/2}}\sigma_{\min}^{1/2},

(10)

for some small enough constant
c_{0}>0
. Then there exist some constant

c_{1}>0
such that at as long as
\eta_{t}=\eta=c_{1}
, the iterates
of unregularized gradient descent (cf. (
9
)) satisfy

\mathrm{dist}\left(\bm{Z}_{t},\bm{Z}_{\star}\right)\leq\left(1-\frac{\eta}{50\kappa}\right)^{t}\mathrm{dist}\left(\bm{Z}_{0},\bm{Z}_{\star}\right).

In words, Theorem
1
reveals that if the initialization

\bm{Z}_{0}
lands in a basin of attraction given by (
10
),
then Algorithm
1
converges linearly with
a constant step size. To reach
\epsilon
-accuracy, i.e.
\mathrm{dist}\left(\bm{Z}_{t},\bm{Z}_{\star}\right)\leq\epsilon
,
it takes an order of
\kappa\log(1/\epsilon)

order-wise equivalent to the regularized PF algorithm proposed in
[
7
]
.
Comparing to
[

]

1
only requires a weaker assumption

Achieving global convergence with a proper initialization

We are still in need of finding a good initialization that obeys (
10

In general, one could initialize with the balanced factors of the
output of projected gradient descent
(over the low-rank matrix), i.e.

\bm{M}_{\tau+1}=\mathcal{P}_{r}\left(\bm{M}_{\tau}-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\langle\bm{A}_{i},\bm{M}_{\tau}\rangle-y_{i}\right)\bm{A}_{i}\right),

where
\mathcal{P}_{r}(\cdot)
is the Euclidean projection operator
to the set of rank-
r
matrices. The spectral initialization specified
in Algorithm
1
can be regarded as the output
at the first iteration, initialized at zero
\bm{M}_{0}=\bm{0}
.
Based on
[
14
,
7
]
, the balanced factorization of
\bm{M}_{\tau}
, denoted by
\widetilde{\bm{Z}}_{\tau}
, satisfy

\min_{\bm{R}\in\mathcal{O}^{r\times r}}\big\|\widetilde{\bm{Z}}_{\tau}\bm{R}-\bm{Z}_{\star}\big\|_{{\mathrm{F}}}\leq c_{2}(2\delta_{4r})^{\tau}\frac{\left\|\bm{M}_{\star}\right\|_{{\mathrm{F}}}}{\sigma_{\min}^{1/2}}

(11)

for some constant
c_{2}
. Thus, to achieve the required initialization
condition (
10
) using the spectral method specified
in Algorithm
1
(which corresponds to setting
\bm{Z}_{0}=\widetilde{\bm{Z}}_{1}
with

\tau=1
in (

)), we need

\delta_{4r}\leq c_{2}\frac{1}{\kappa^{3/2}}\cdot\frac{\sigma_{\min}}{\left\|\bm{M}_{\star}\right\|_{{\mathrm{F}}}}.

In particular, under Gaussian design, where each measurement matrix
\bm{A}_{i}
has i.i.d.

entries, a total of

measurements suffice for the above requirement on

[
7
]
with the balancing regularizer, which is due to the restriction on the basin of attraction, as we have remarked earlier. Improving the dependence on

In order to alleviate the dependency on the condition number

\tau\geq c_{1}\log{\left(\kappa^{3/2}\frac{\left\|\bm{M}_{\star}\right\|_{{\mathrm{F}}}}{\sigma_{\min}}\right)}/\log{\left(\delta_{c}^{-1}\right)}

iterations of projected gradient descent for initialization, which order-wise
matches the requirement in
[
7
]
.

that achieves a faster convergence rate independent of the condition number
\kappa
without imposing the balancing regularization for a variety of low-rank matrix estimation problems, which are further extended in
[
45
]
to achieve robustness to adversarial outliers.

4
Proof of Theorem
1

In this section, we provide the proof of Theorem
1
.
We first discuss some basic properties of aligning two low-rank factors
via an invertible transformation. Then we prove a similar result for
a warm-up case of low-rank matrix factorization. In the end, viewing
matrix sensing as a perturbed version of low-rank matrix factorization
helps us finish the proof of Theorem
1
.

4.1
Alignment via invertible transformations

We begin with introducing the alignment matrix will play a key role in the subsequent analysis.

Definition 2
.

Fix a matrix
\bm{Z}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\bm{X}\\
\bm{Y}\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{(n_{1}+n_{2})\times r}
. We define the optimal alignment matrix
\bm{Q}
between
\bm{Z}

and
\bm{Z}_{\star}
as

\bm{Q}\triangleq\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}\\
\text{invertible}\end{subarray}}\left\|\bm{X}\bm{P}-\bm{X}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\left\|\bm{Y}\bm{P}^{-\top}-\bm{Y}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2},

whenever the minimum is attained.

As we will soon see, for the iterates

\{\bm{Z}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
generated by Algorithm
1
,
the optimal alignment matrix is always well-defined. Furthermore,
we call
\bm{Z}
and
\bm{Z}_{\star}
aligned if the corresponding
optimal alignment matrix is just the identity matrix
\bm{I}_{r}
.
Below we provide some basic understandings of this alignment matrix.

The following lemma provides a sufficient condition for the existence
of the optimal alignment matrix.

Lemma 1
.

Fix some matrix

\bm{Z}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\bm{X}\\
\bm{Y}\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{(n_{1}+n_{2})\times r}
. Suppose that there exists a matrix
\bm{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}

with
1/2\leq\sigma_{r}(\bm{P})\leq\sigma_{1}(\bm{P})\leq 3/2
such
that

\max\left\{\left\|\bm{X}\bm{P}-\bm{X}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}},\left\|\bm{Y}\bm{P}^{-\top}-\bm{Y}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\right\}\leq\delta\leq\frac{\sigma_{r}\left(\bm{X}_{\star}\right)}{80}.

(12)

Then the optimal alignment matrix
\bm{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}

between
\bm{Z}
and
\bm{Z}_{\star}
exists. In addition, the matrix

\bm{Q}
satisfies

\left\|\bm{P}-\bm{Q}\right\|\leq\left\|\bm{P}-\bm{Q}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\frac{5\delta}{\sigma_{r}\left(\bm{X}_{\star}\right)}.

Next, the lemma below presents a necessary condition for
\bm{Q}

to be the optimal alignment matrix between
\bm{Z}
and
\bm{Z}_{\star}
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\bm{Z}
and
\bm{Z}_{\star}

be any two matrices. Suppose that the optimal alignment matrix
\bm{Q}

between
\bm{Z}
and
\bm{Z}_{\star}
exists. Then we have

\widetilde{\bm{X}}^{\top}(\widetilde{\bm{X}}-\bm{X}_{\star})=(\widetilde{\bm{Y}}-\bm{Y}_{\star})^{\top}\widetilde{\bm{Y}},

where
\widetilde{\bm{X}}=\bm{X}\bm{\bm{Q}}
and
\widetilde{\bm{Y}}=\bm{\bm{Y}}\bm{\bm{Q}}^{-\top}

are two matrices after the alignment.

Both lemmas provide basic understandings of the solution to the alignment
problem with invertible transformations, which can be regarded as
a generalization of the classical orthogonal Procrustes problem that
only considers orthonormal transformations. Clearly, this generalized
problem is more involved and our work provides some basic
understandings.

4.2
A warm-up: low-rank matrix factorization

We consider the following minimization problem for low-rank matrix
factorization

f_{\mathrm{MF}}\left(\bm{X},\bm{Y}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\bm{X}\bm{Y}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2},

(13)

where
\bm{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times r}
and
\bm{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}\times r}
.
The gradient descent updates with an initialization
(\bm{X}_{0},\bm{Y}_{0})

can be written as

\displaystyle\bm{X}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\bm{X}_{t}-\frac{\eta}{\sigma_{\mathrm{\max}}}\nabla_{\bm{X}}f_{\mathrm{MF}}\left(\bm{X}_{t},\bm{Y}_{t}\right)=\bm{X}_{t}-\frac{\eta}{\sigma_{\mathrm{\max}}}(\bm{X}_{t}\bm{Y}_{t}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star})\bm{Y}_{t};

(14)

\displaystyle\bm{Y}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\bm{Y}_{t}-\frac{\eta}{\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}}\nabla_{\bm{Y}}f_{\mathrm{MF}}\left(\bm{X}_{t},\bm{Y}_{t}\right)=\bm{Y}_{t}-\frac{\eta}{\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}}(\bm{X}_{t}\bm{Y}_{t}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star})^{\top}\bm{X}_{t}.

Here,
\eta>0
stands for the step size. We have the following theorem
regarding the performance of (
14
), which parallels
Theorem
1
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\bm{Z}_{0}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\bm{X}_{0}\\
\bm{Y}_{0}\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{(n_{1}+n_{2})\times r}
be any initialization point that satisfies

\min_{\bm{R}\in\mathcal{O}^{r\times r}}\left\|\bm{Z}_{0}\bm{R}-\bm{Z}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq c_{0}\frac{1}{\kappa^{3/2}}\sigma_{\min}^{1/2}

(15)

for some sufficiently small constant
c_{0}>0
. Then setting the
step size
\eta>0
to be some sufficiently small constant, the iterates
of GD (cf. (
14
)) satisfy

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}\left(\bm{Z}_{t},\bm{Z}_{\star}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{\eta}{50\kappa}\right)^{t}\mathrm{dist}\left(\bm{Z}_{0},\bm{Z}_{\star}\right).

To prove Theorem
2
, we need the following
properties regarding the gradients of
f_{\mathrm{MF}}(\bm{X},\bm{Y})
; the proofs
are deferred to the appendix.

Lemma 3

(Gradient dominance)
.

Suppose that
\bm{Z}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\bm{X}\\
\bm{Y}\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{(n_{1}+n_{2})\times r}
is aligned with
\bm{Z}_{\star}
, i.e.

\bm{I}_{r}=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}\\
\text{invertible}\end{subarray}}\;\left\|\bm{X}\bm{P}-\bm{X}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\left\|\bm{Y}\bm{P}^{-\top}-\bm{X}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}.

Then we have

\left\langle\bm{X}-\bm{X}_{\star},\left(\bm{X}\bm{Y}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star}\right)\bm{Y}\right\rangle\geq\left\|\bm{Y}(\bm{X}-\bm{X}_{\star})^{\top}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\left\|\bm{X}-\bm{X}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{4},

and similarly,

\left\langle\bm{Y}-\bm{Y}_{\star},\left(\bm{X}\bm{Y}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star}\right)^{\top}\bm{X}\right\rangle\geq\left\|\bm{X}(\bm{Y}-\bm{Y}_{\star})^{\top}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\left\|\bm{Y}-\bm{Y}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{4}.

Lemma 4

(Smoothness)
.

Suppose that

\left\|\bm{Y}-\bm{Y}_{\star}\right\|\leq\sigma_{1}\left(\bm{Y}_{\star}\right)/4
,
then one has

\left\|\left(\bm{X}\bm{Y}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star}\right)\bm{Y}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\frac{3}{2}\sigma_{1}(\bm{Y}_{\star})\Big(\left\|(\bm{X}-\bm{X}_{\star})\bm{Y}^{\top}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}+\left\|\bm{X}(\bm{Y}-\bm{Y}_{\star})^{\top}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}+\left\|\bm{X}-\bm{X}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\left\|\bm{Y}-\bm{Y}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\Big).

Similarly, with the proviso that
\left\|\bm{X}-\bm{X}_{\star}\right\|\leq\sigma_{1}\left(\bm{X}_{\star}\right)/4
,
one has

\left\|\left(\bm{X}\bm{Y}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star}\right)^{\top}\bm{X}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\frac{3}{2}\sigma_{1}(\bm{X}_{\star})\Big(\left\|(\bm{X}-\bm{X}_{\star})\bm{Y}^{\top}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}+\left\|\bm{X}(\bm{Y}-\bm{Y}_{\star})^{\top}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}+\left\|\bm{X}-\bm{X}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\left\|\bm{Y}-\bm{Y}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\Big).

4.3
Proof of Theorem
2

With the help of Lemmas
1
–
4
,
we are in a position to establish Theorem
2
.
Denote by
\widehat{\bm{R}}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
the best rotation
matrix between
\bm{Z}_{0}
and
\bm{Z}_{\star}
, that is

\widehat{\bm{R}}\triangleq\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{\bm{R}\in\mathcal{O}^{r\times r}}\left\|\bm{Z}_{0}\bm{R}-\bm{Z}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}.

Combine the assumption of initialization (cf. (
15
))
and Lemma
1
to see that

\bm{Q}_{0}\triangleq\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}\\
\text{invertible}\end{subarray}}\left\|\bm{X}_{0}\bm{P}-\bm{X}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\left\|\bm{Y}_{0}\bm{P}^{-\top}-\bm{Y}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}

exists and in addition, one has

\|\bm{Q}_{0}-\widehat{\bm{R}}\|\leq\frac{5c_{0}}{\kappa^{3/2}}\leq\frac{1}{400\sqrt{\kappa}}

as long as
c_{0}>0
is sufficiently small.

The remaining proof is inductive in nature. In particular, we aim
at proving the following induction hypotheses.

1.

The optimal alignment matrix
\bm{Q}_{t}
between
\bm{Z}_{t}
and

\bm{Z}_{\star}
exists.

2.

The distance between
\bm{Z}_{t}
and
\bm{Z}_{\star}
obeys

\mathrm{dist}\left(\bm{Z}_{t},\bm{Z}_{\star}\right)\leq\left(1-\frac{\eta}{50\kappa}\right)^{t}\mathrm{dist}\left(\bm{Z}_{0},\bm{Z}_{\star}\right).

3.

The optimal alignment matrix
\bm{Q}_{t}
is nearly a rotation matrix
in the sense that

\|\bm{Q}_{t}-\widehat{\bm{R}}\|\leq\frac{1}{400\sqrt{\kappa}}.

It is straightforward to check that these three claims hold for
t=0
.
In what follows, we shall assume that the induction hypotheses hold
for all iterations up to the
t
th iteration and intend to establish
that they continue to hold for the
(t+1)
th iteration.

Verifying the first induction hypothesis

We begin with demonstrating the existence of
\bm{Q}_{t+1}
. In view
of the gradient update rule (
14
), we have

\displaystyle\bm{X}_{t+1}\bm{Q}_{t}

\displaystyle=\bm{X}_{t}\bm{Q}_{t}-\frac{\eta}{\sigma_{\mathrm{\max}}}\left(\bm{X}_{t}\bm{Y}_{t}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star}\right)\bm{Y}_{t}\bm{Q}_{t}=\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}-\frac{\eta}{\sigma_{\mathrm{\max}}}\left(\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star}\right)\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}\left(\bm{Q}_{t}^{\top}\bm{Q}_{t}\right),

\displaystyle\bm{Y}_{t+1}\bm{Q}_{t}^{-\top}

\displaystyle=\bm{Y}_{t}\bm{Q}_{t}^{-\top}-\frac{\eta}{\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}}\left(\bm{X}_{t}\bm{Y}_{t}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star}\right)^{\top}\bm{X}_{t}\bm{Q}_{t}^{-\top}=\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}-\frac{\eta}{\sigma_{\mathrm{\max}}}\left(\bm{X}_{t}\bm{Y}_{t}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star}\right)^{\top}\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}\left(\bm{Q}_{t}^{\top}\bm{Q}_{t}\right)^{-1},

where we denote

\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}\triangleq\bm{X}_{t}\bm{Q}_{t}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}\triangleq\bm{Y}_{t}\bm{Q}_{t}^{-\top}.

As a result, one has the following equality

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{X}_{t+1}\bm{Q}_{t}-\bm{X}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\left\|\bm{Y}_{t+1}\bm{Q}_{t}^{-\top}-\bm{Y}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left\|\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}-\bm{X}_{\star}-\frac{\eta}{\sigma_{\mathrm{\max}}}\left(\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star}\right)\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}\bm{\Lambda}_{t}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}}_{\eqqcolon\alpha_{1}}+\underbrace{\left\|\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}-\bm{Y}_{\star}-\frac{\eta}{\sigma_{\mathrm{\max}}}\left(\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star}\right)^{\top}\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}\bm{\Lambda}_{t}^{-1}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}}_{\eqqcolon\alpha_{2}},

where we have denoted
\bm{\Lambda}_{t}\triangleq\bm{Q}_{t}^{\top}\bm{Q}_{t}
.
By virtue of the third induction hypothesis, namely
\|\bm{Q}_{t}-\widehat{\bm{R}}\|\leq{1}/({400\sqrt{\kappa}})
,
it is easy to check that
\|\bm{\Lambda}_{t}-\bm{I}_{r}\|\leq{1}/({180\sqrt{\kappa}})\triangleq\zeta
. Let

\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{X}_{t}}\triangleq\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}-\bm{X}_{\star}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{Y}_{t}}\triangleq\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}-\bm{Y}_{\star}.

Expand
\alpha_{1}
to obtain

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}=

\displaystyle\big\|\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{X}_{t}}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\left(\frac{\eta}{\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}}\right)^{2}\underbrace{\left\|\left(\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star}\right)\widetilde{\bm{Y}_{t}}\bm{\Lambda}_{t}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}}_{\eqqcolon\beta_{1}}-2\frac{\eta}{\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}}\underbrace{\left\langle\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{X}_{t}},\left(\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star}\right)\widetilde{\bm{Y}_{t}}\bm{\Lambda}_{t}\right\rangle}_{\eqqcolon\gamma_{1}}.

Similarly, we can decompose
\alpha_{2}
into

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}=

\displaystyle\big\|\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{Y}_{t}}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\left(\frac{\eta}{\sigma_{\mathrm{\max}}}\right)^{2}\underbrace{\left\|\left(\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star}\right)^{\top}\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}\bm{\Lambda}_{t}^{-1}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}}_{\eqqcolon\beta_{2}}-2\frac{\eta}{\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}}\underbrace{\left\langle\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{Y}_{t}},\left(\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star}\right)^{\top}\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}\bm{\Lambda}_{t}^{-1}\right\rangle}_{\eqqcolon\gamma_{2}}.

We intend to apply Lemma
3
to lower

\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
and apply Lemma
4

to upper bound
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
. First, since
(\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t},\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t})

Lemma

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}

\displaystyle\geq\left\langle\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{X}_{t}},\left(\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star}\right)\widetilde{\bm{Y}_{t}}\right\rangle-\left|\left\langle\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{X}_{t}},\left(\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star}\right)\widetilde{\bm{Y}_{t}}\left(\bm{\Lambda}_{t}-\bm{I}_{r}\right)\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\geq\big\|\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{X}_{t}}^{\top}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\big\|\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{X}_{t}}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{4}-\|\bm{\Lambda}_{t}-\bm{I}_{r}\|\cdot\left\|\left(\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star}\right)\widetilde{\bm{Y}_{t}}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\big\|\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{X}_{t}}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}

\displaystyle\geq\big\|\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{X}_{t}}^{\top}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}-\frac{1}{400}\sigma_{\min}\big\|\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{X}_{t}}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}-\zeta\left\|\left(\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star}\right)\widetilde{\bm{Y}_{t}}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\big\|\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{X}_{t}}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}.

(16)

Here the last line follows from the bound

\big\|\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{X}_{t}}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\leq\mathrm{dist}^{2}\left(\bm{Z}_{t},\bm{Z}_{\star}\right)\leq\mathrm{dist}^{2}\left(\bm{Z}_{0},\bm{Z}_{\star}\right)\leq\frac{1}{100}\sigma_{\min}.

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3\zeta}{2}\sqrt{\sigma_{\max}}\Big(\big\|\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{X}_{t}}\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}^{\top}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}+\big\|\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{Y}_{t}}^{\top}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}+\big\|\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{X}_{t}}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}\big\|\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{Y}_{t}}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}\Big)\big\|\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{X}_{t}}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}

\displaystyle\beta_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 9\sigma_{\max}\Big(\big\|\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{X}_{t}}\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}^{\top}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}+\big\|\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{Y}_{t}}^{\top}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}+\big\|\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{X}_{t}}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}\big\|\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{Y}_{t}}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}\Big)^{2},

(17)

(a+b+c)^{2}\leq 3(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2})

\|\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{Y}_{t}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\leq\text{dist}^{2}(\bm{Z}_{0},\bm{Z}_{\star})\leq c_{0}^{2}\sigma_{\min}/\kappa^{3}
.
Similarly, one can bound

\displaystyle\beta_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 27\sigma_{\max}\left(\big\|\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{X}_{t}}\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}^{\top}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\big\|\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{Y}_{t}}^{\top}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\right)+27c_{0}^{2}\frac{\sigma_{\max}\sigma_{\min}}{\kappa^{3}}\big\|\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{Y}_{t}}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2},

Collect all the bounds on

\displaystyle\quad\leq\left(1+\frac{27c_{0}^{2}\eta^{2}}{\kappa^{4}}\right)\mathrm{dist}^{2}\left(\bm{Z}_{t},\bm{Z}_{\star}\right)+\left(\frac{54\eta^{2}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}}\right)\left(\big\|\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{X}_{t}}\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}^{\top}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\big\|\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{Y}_{t}}^{\top}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-2\frac{\eta}{\sigma_{\max}}\left[\frac{3}{4}\Big(\big\|\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{X}_{t}}\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}^{\top}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\big\|\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{Y}_{t}}^{\top}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\Big)-\frac{\sigma_{\min}}{7}\mathrm{dist}^{2}\left(\bm{Z}_{t},\bm{Z}_{\star}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\left(1+\frac{27c_{0}^{2}\eta^{2}}{\kappa^{4}}+\frac{\eta}{3.5\kappa}\right)\mathrm{dist}^{2}\left(\bm{Z}_{t},\bm{Z}_{\star}\right)+\left(\frac{54\eta^{2}}{\sigma_{\max}}-\frac{3\eta}{2\sigma_{\max}}\right)\cdot\left(\big\|\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{X}_{t}}\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t}^{\top}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\big\|\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{Y}_{t}}^{\top}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\left(1+\frac{\eta}{3\kappa}\right)\mathrm{dist}^{2}\left(\bm{Z}_{t},\bm{Z}_{\star}\right)-\frac{3\eta}{4\sigma_{\max}}\left(\sigma_{r}^{2}(\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t})\big\|\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{X}_{t}}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\sigma_{r}^{2}(\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t})\big\|\widetilde{\bm{E}}_{\bm{Y}_{t}}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\right),

where the last line follows as long as
\eta\leq 1/24
. Furthermore,
since
\sigma_{r}^{2}(\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_{t})\geq\sigma_{\min}/2
and

\sigma_{r}^{2}(\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t})\geq\sigma_{\min}/2
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{X}_{t+1}\bm{Q}_{t}-\bm{X}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\left\|\bm{Y}_{t+1}\bm{Q}_{t}^{-\top}-\bm{Y}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{\eta}{24\kappa}\right)\mathrm{dist}^{2}\left(\bm{Z}_{t},\bm{Z}_{\star}\right).

(18)

Lemma
1
then ensures the existence
of
\bm{Q}_{t+1}
.

Verifying the second induction hypothesis

The second induction hypothesis for the
(t+1)
th iteration follows
immediately from the above proof. Since
\bm{Q}_{t+1}
exists, by
definition, one has

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}\left(\bm{Z}_{t+1},\bm{Z}_{\star}\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\left\|\bm{X}_{t+1}\bm{Q}_{t+1}-\bm{X}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\left\|\bm{Y}_{t+1}\bm{Q}_{t+1}^{-\top}-\bm{X}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\left\|\bm{X}_{t+1}\bm{Q}_{t}-\bm{X}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\left\|\bm{Y}_{t+1}\bm{Q}_{t}^{-\top}-\bm{X}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{\eta}{50\kappa}\right)\mathrm{dist}\left(\bm{Z}_{t},\bm{Z}_{\star}\right).

Verifying the third induction hypothesis

It remains to show the last induction hypothesis, namely
\|\bm{Q}_{t+1}-\widehat{\bm{R}}\|\leq{1}/({400\sqrt{\kappa}})
. In view of (
18
), one has
\max\{\|\bm{X}_{t+1}\bm{Q}_{t}-\bm{X}_{\star}\|_{\mathrm{F}},\|\bm{Y}_{t+1}\bm{Q}_{t}^{-\top}-\bm{Y}_{\star}\|_{\mathrm{F}}\}\leq\mathrm{dist}\left(\bm{Z}_{t},\bm{Z}_{\star}\right)
.
Invoke Lemma
1
again to arrive
at

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{Q}_{t+1}-\bm{Q}_{t}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{5}{\sigma_{r}\left(\bm{X}_{\star}\right)}\mathrm{dist}\left(\bm{Z}_{t},\bm{Z}_{\star}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{5}{\sigma_{r}\left(\bm{X}_{\star}\right)}\left(1-\frac{\eta}{50\kappa}\right)^{t}c_{0}\frac{1}{\kappa^{3/2}}\sigma_{r}\left(\bm{X}_{\star}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 5c_{0}\left(1-\frac{\eta}{50\kappa}\right)^{t}\frac{1}{\kappa^{3/2}}.

Hence, by the triangle inequality and the telescoping sum, we obtain

\displaystyle\big\|\bm{Q}_{t+1}-\widehat{\bm{R}}\big\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s=0}^{t}\left\|\bm{Q}_{s+1}-\bm{Q}_{s}\right\|+\big\|\bm{Q}_{0}-\widehat{\bm{R}}\big\|

\displaystyle\leq 5c_{0}\sum_{s=0}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\eta}{50\kappa}\right)^{s}\frac{1}{\kappa^{3/2}}+5c_{0}\frac{1}{\kappa^{3/2}}

\displaystyle<5c_{0}\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{\eta}{50\kappa}\right)^{s}\frac{1}{\kappa^{3/2}}+5c_{0}\frac{1}{\kappa^{3/2}}

\displaystyle=5c_{0}\frac{50\kappa}{\eta}\frac{1}{\kappa^{3/2}}+5c_{0}\frac{1}{\kappa^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{400\sqrt{\kappa}},

as long as
c_{0}
is small enough and
\eta
is some constant.

Putting everything together, we finish the induction step and the proof is then completed.

.

A simple consequence is that for any

Similar to before, we denote
\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{t}=\bm{X}_{t}\bm{Q}_{t}

\displaystyle\bm{X}_{t+1}\bm{Q}_{t}

5
Conclusions

This paper establishes the local linear convergence of gradient descent
for asymmetric low-rank matrix sensing without explicit regularization
of factor balancedness under the standard RIP assumption, as long
as a balanced initialization is provided in the basin of attraction. Coupled with the standard spectral initialization, this leads to the global convergence guarantee of the balancing-free gradient descent algorithm for asymmetric low-rank matrix sensing.
Different from previous
work, we analyzed a new error metric that takes into account the ambiguity
due to invertible transforms, and showed that it contracts linearly
even without local restricted strong convexity. We believe that our technique
can be used for other low-rank matrix estimation problems. To conclude,
we outline a few future research directions.

•

Low-rank matrix completion
. We believe it is possible to
extend our analysis to study rectangular matrix completion without
regularization, by combining the leave-one-out technique in
[
26
,
34
]

to carefully bound the incoherence of the iterates for both factors
even without explicit balancing.

•

Improving dependence on
\kappa
and
r
. The current paper
does not try to optimize the dependence with respect to
\kappa

and
r
in terms of sample complexity and the size of the basin of
attraction, which are slightly worse than their regularized counterparts.
A finer analysis will likely lead to better dependencies, which we
leave to the future work.

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma
1

For notational convenience,
we define the following function

g\left(\bm{Q}\right)\triangleq\left\|\bm{X}\bm{Q}-\bm{X}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\left\|\bm{Y}\bm{Q}^{-\top}-\bm{Y}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}.

(22)

Clearly, the optimal alignment matrix, if exists, must be
\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}\,g(\bm{P})
.
With this notation in place, we consider the following constrained
minimization problem:

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}:\bm{Q}\text{ is invertible}}

\displaystyle\qquad g\left(\bm{Q}\right)

subject to

\displaystyle\qquad\left\|\bm{Q}-\bm{P}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\frac{5\delta}{\sigma_{\min}\left(\bm{X}_{\star}\right)}.

In view of Weyl’s inequality, we obtain that for any feasible
\bm{Q}
,

\sigma_{\min}\left(\bm{Q}\right)\geq\sigma_{\min}\left(\bm{P}\right)-\frac{5\delta}{\sigma_{\min}\left(\bm{X}_{\star}\right)}\geq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{4}=\frac{1}{4}

as long as
\delta\leq\sigma_{\min}(\bm{X}_{\star})/80
. As a result,
one sees that
g(\bm{Q})
is a continuous function over
\{\bm{Q}:\|\bm{Q}-\bm{P}\|\leq 5\delta/\sigma_{\min}(\bm{X}_{\star})\}
,
which is a compact set over invertible matrices. Applying the Weierstrass
extreme value theorem yields the claim that the minimizer of the constrained
problem exists. Denote this minimizer by
\bm{Q}_{1}
. In what follows,
we intend to show that
\bm{Q}_{1}
is also the minimizer of the
unconstrained problem. Letting
\bm{Q}
be an arbitrary matrix with

g(\bm{Q})\leq 2\delta^{2}
(the existence is assured since
g(\bm{Q}_{1})\leq g(\bm{P})\leq 2\delta^{2}
),
we have

\sqrt{2}\delta\geq\left\|\bm{X}\bm{Q}-\bm{X}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\geq\|\bm{X}\bm{Q}-\bm{X}\bm{P}\|_{\mathrm{F}}-\|\bm{X}\bm{P}-\bm{X}_{\star}\|_{\mathrm{F}},

which in conjunction with (
12
)
implies

(1+\sqrt{2})\delta\geq\|\bm{X}(\bm{Q}-\bm{P})\|_{\mathrm{F}}\geq\sigma_{\min}\left(\bm{X}\right)\left\|\bm{Q}_{1}-\bm{P}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}.

(23)

We now turn to investigating
\sigma_{\min}(\bm{X})
. Weyl’s inequality
tells us that

\displaystyle\left|\sigma_{\min}\left(\bm{X}\bm{P}\right)-\sigma_{\min}\left(\bm{X}_{\star}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\bm{X}\bm{P}-\bm{X}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\delta\leq\frac{1}{4}\sigma_{\min}\left(\bm{X}_{\star}\right),

which further implies

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\sigma_{\min}\left(\bm{X}_{\star}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sigma_{\min}\left(\bm{X}\bm{P}\right)\leq\sigma_{\min}\left(\bm{X}\right)\sigma_{\max}\left(\bm{P}\right)\leq\frac{3}{2}\sigma_{\min}\left(\bm{X}\right).

Therefore we arrive at
\sigma_{\min}(\bm{X})\geq\sigma_{\min}(\bm{X}_{\star})/2
.
Putting this back to (
23
) yields which finally gives

\left\|\bm{Q}-\bm{P}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq 2(1+\sqrt{2})\frac{\delta}{\sigma_{\min}(\bm{X}_{\star})}<\frac{5\delta}{\sigma_{\min}(\bm{X}_{\star})}.

In all, the above arguments reveal that any matrix
\bm{Q}
such
that
g(\bm{Q})\leq 2\delta^{2}
must obey the above bound. Therefore
the minimizer of the constrained problem and that of the unconstrained
one coincide with each other. This finished the proof.

Recall the function

\displaystyle\qquad-2\mathrm{Tr}\left(\bm{P}^{-1}\bm{Y}^{\top}\bm{Y}_{\star}\right)+\mathrm{Tr}\left(\bm{Y}_{\star}^{\top}\bm{Y}_{\star}\right).

The gradient is given by

\displaystyle\nabla

\displaystyle g\left(\bm{P}\right)=2\bm{X}^{\top}\bm{X}\bm{P}-2\bm{X}^{\top}\bm{X}_{\star}-2\left(\bm{P}\bm{P}^{\top}\right)^{-1}\bm{Y}^{\top}\bm{Y}\left(\bm{P}\bm{P}^{\top}\right)^{-1}\bm{P}+2\bm{P}^{-\top}\bm{Y}_{\star}^{\top}\bm{Y}\bm{P}^{-\top}.

Appendix C
Proof of Lemma
3

We prove the first part and the second part follows by symmetry. Denote

\bm{E}_{x}=\bm{X}-\bm{X}_{\star}
and
\bm{E}_{y}=\bm{Y}-\bm{Y}_{\star}
.
We have

\bm{X}\bm{Y}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star}=\bm{E}_{x}\bm{Y}^{\top}+\bm{X}_{\star}\bm{E}_{y}^{\top}.

Since
\bm{Z}
is aligned with
\bm{Z}_{\star}
, Lemma
2

tells us that

\bm{X}^{\top}\bm{E}_{x}=\bm{E}_{y}^{\top}{\bm{Y}}.

As a result, one has

\displaystyle\left\langle\bm{X}-\bm{X}_{\star},\left(\bm{X}\bm{Y}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star}\right)\bm{Y}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\bm{E}_{x}^{\top}\left(\bm{E}_{x}\bm{Y}^{\top}+\bm{X}_{\star}\bm{E}_{y}^{\top}\right)\bm{Y}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\bm{E}_{x}^{\top}\bm{E}_{x}\bm{Y}^{\top}\bm{Y}\right)+\mathrm{Tr}\left(\bm{E}_{x}^{\top}\bm{X}_{\star}\bm{E}_{y}^{\top}\bm{Y}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\|\bm{Y}\bm{E}_{x}^{\top}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\mathrm{Tr}\left(\bm{E}_{y}^{\top}\bm{Y}\bm{E}_{x}^{\top}\bm{X}\right)-\mathrm{Tr}\left(\bm{E}_{y}^{\top}\bm{Y}\bm{E}_{x}^{\top}\bm{E}_{x}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\|\bm{Y}\bm{E}_{x}^{\top}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\|\bm{X}^{\top}\bm{E}_{x}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}-\mathrm{Tr}\left(\bm{X}^{\top}\bm{E}_{x}\bm{E}_{x}^{\top}\bm{E}_{x}\right).

(24)

Complete the squares to see that

\|\bm{X}^{\top}\bm{E}_{x}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}-\mathrm{Tr}\left(\bm{X}^{\top}\bm{E}_{x}\bm{E}_{x}^{\top}\bm{E}_{x}\right)=\big\|\bm{E}_{x}^{\top}\bm{X}-\frac{1}{2}\bm{E}_{x}^{\top}\bm{E}_{x}\big\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\left\|\bm{E}_{x}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{4}.

Combine the previous two bounds to yield the desired result.

Appendix D
Proof of Lemma
4

Again, we demonstrate the claim on
\bm{X}
and the claim on
\bm{Y}

follows by symmetry. Given the decomposition

\displaystyle\bm{X}\bm{Y}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star}=(\bm{X}-\bm{X}_{\star})\bm{Y}^{\top}

\displaystyle+\bm{X}(\bm{Y}-\bm{Y}_{\star})^{\top}+(\bm{X}_{\star}-\bm{X})(\bm{Y}-\bm{Y}_{\star})^{\top},

we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\bm{X}\bm{Y}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star}\right)\bm{Y}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}

\displaystyle\leq\sigma_{1}(\bm{Y})\left\|\bm{X}\bm{Y}^{\top}-\bm{M}_{\star}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2}\sigma_{1}(\bm{Y}_{\star})\Big(\left\|(\bm{X}-\bm{X}_{\star})\bm{Y}^{\top}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}+\left\|\bm{X}(\bm{Y}-\bm{Y}_{\star})^{\top}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}+\left\|(\bm{X}_{\star}-\bm{X})(\bm{Y}-\bm{Y}_{\star})^{\top}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\Big),

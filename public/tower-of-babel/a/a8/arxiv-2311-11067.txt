The Weighted HOM-Problem over Fields
Andreea-Teodora Nász

Abstract
The HOM-problem , which asks whether the image of a regular tree language under a tree homomorphism is again regular, is known to be decidable. In this paper, we prove the weighted HOM-problem for all fields decidable, provided that the tree homomorphism is tetris-free (a condition that generalizes injectivity). To this end, we reduce the problem to a property of the device representing the homomorphic image in question; to prove this property decidable, we then derive a pumping lemma for such devices from the well-known pumping lemma for regular tree series over fields, proved by Berstel and Reutenauer in 1982.

Introduction

The well-known model of finite-state automata has seen various extensions over the past decades.
On the one hand, the qualitative evaluation of these acceptors was
generalized to a quantitative one, leading to

automata

[

,
10
,
27
]
.
Thereby, the favoured domains for weight calculations are often

[
16
,
18
]
,
as they are both quite general and computationally efficient due to their distributivity.

Another dimension of generalization for finite-state automata targets their input, allowing them to handle
more complex data structures such as infinite words
[
24
]
, trees
[
5
]
,
graphs
[
4
]
and pictures
[
26
]
. In particular,

finite-state tree automata
and the

they recognize were introduced
independently in
[
7
,
30
,
31
]
.
These devices find applications in a variety of areas like natural language processing
[
19
]
,
picture generation
[
8
]
and compiler construction
[
32
]
.
Unsurprisingly, combining both types of generalizations leads to intricate yet fruitful research areas, and so

(WTA) and the

they
recognize continue to be studied
[

]
.

Tree homomorphisms
are widely used in the context of term rewriting
[
13
]
and XML
types
[
29
]
. A tree homomorphism is a structure-preserving transformation on trees which
can duplicate subtrees, so the trees in the homomorphic image might have identical subtrees.
Unfortunately, tree automata have limited memory, so they cannot ensure that certain subtrees
are equal
[
12
]
(much like the classical (string) automata cannot ensure that the numbers of
a
’s
and of
b
’s in a word are equal). Therefore, unlike in the word case, regular tree languages are not closed
under tree homomorphisms. It was a long-standing open question if, given a regular tree language
\mathcal{L}

and a tree homomorphism
h
as input, it is decidable whether
h(\mathcal{L})
is again regular.
This
HOM-problem
was finally solved in
[
6
,
14
,
15
]
, with the
help of a well-studied extension called
tree automata with constraints
; these devices can explicitely
require certain subtrees to be equal, and can thus handle the duplications performed by
h
.

In the
weighted
HOM-problem, a regular tree series and a tree homomorphism
are given as input. By its nature, this question requires a customized investigation for different semirings.
Most recently, this problem was proved decidable for different scenarios
[
22
,
23
]
, but in both
cases, the semiring must be zero-sum free; this strong condition already excludes essential rings such as
\mathbb{Z}
.
In this paper, we decide the weighted HOM-problem for all fields (and thus, all subspaces of fields), provided that
the tree homomorphism is
tetris-free
, a property that generalizes injectivity.

The paper is structured as follows: In Section
2
we represent the homomorphic
image of the input tree series by a WTA with constraints (WTAh). In Section
4
we show that, if the input tree homomorphism is tetris-free, then the weighted
HOM-problem is equivalent to a certain decidable property of this WTAh.
Proving said decidability relies on a pumping lemma for
WTAh over fields, which we derive in Section
3
.
from the well-known pumping lemma for (regular) WTA over fields
[
1
]
.
Finally, we present an example that illustrates why the approach
is entirely unsuited for non-tetris-free tree homomorphisms.

•

Let

3
A Pumping Lemma over Fields

The weighted HOM-problem takes
a WTA
\mathcal{A}
and a nondeleting, nonerasing tree homomorphism
h
as input, and asks whether
h(\lVert\mathcal{A}\rVert)
is again regular. As mentioned earlier, the
\mathbb{N}
” variant of this problem was shown
to be decidable in
[
22
]
. The proof presented there makes two assumptions on the
semiring used for the weight calculations: First, it must be a subsemiring of a field, and second, it must
be zero-sum free; the only common semiring that satisfies both conditions is
\mathbb{N}
. Remarkably, the
strong condition of zero-sum freeness is only used to prove a pumping lemma for
h(\lVert\mathcal{A}\rVert)
.
In this section, we derive an alternative pumping lemma over fields, provided that
h
is
tetris-free. This way, we bypass the zero-sum freeness assumption, which allows us to lift the proof
of
[
22
]
to the HOM-problem over fields, for tetris-free tree homomorphisms.

We begin by establishing a notation for the tree fragment read by a rule.

, and a

Proof

4
The Tetris-Free Weighted HOM-Problem

In this section, we prove that the weighted HOM-problem over fields, restricted
to tetris-free homomorphisms, is decidable. Formally, we show the following result.

Theorem 4.1

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field,
\mathcal{A}
a WTA over
\mathbb{F}
and
\Sigma
, and
h\colon T_{\Sigma}\to T_{\Delta}
a tetris-free tree homomorphism. It is decidable whether
h(\lVert\mathcal{A}\rVert)
is regular.

The approach to prove this result is quite natural: Nonregularity of
h\big(\lVert\mathcal{A}\rVert\big)
is reduced to
the following decidable property of the WTAh
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
for
h(\lVert\mathcal{A}\rVert)
.

Definition 8
(see
[
22
, Definition 10]
)

Let
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=\big(Q\dot{\cup}\{\bot\},\Delta,F,R,\wt\big)
be the eq-restricted WTAh from
Lemma
1
for a WTA over a field
and a tetris-free tree homomorphism, and let
N
be the pumping constant of
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
. We say
that
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
has the
large duplication property (LDP)
if there exists
t\in\supp(\lVert\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\rVert)
with
an accepting run
\varrho
, a position
p\in\pos_{R}(\varrho)
where
\varrho(p)
has a nontrivial
constraint set
E
, and a position
p^{\prime}
that is constrained by
E
such that
\he(t|_{pp^{\prime}})\geq N
.

A constraint that only acts on finitely many trees is expendable, since we can process these particular trees
manually using additional states. If, however,
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
has the LDP, then by our pumping lemma we obtain
infinitely many trees to which a nontrivial constraint
E
applies, so we cannot bypass
E
. Thus, the LDP indicates that the constraints are indeed indispensable for
representing
\lVert\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\rVert
, and in turn these constraints cause nonregularity, as stated in Proposition
3
.

The decision procedure of
[
22
]
for input
\mathcal{A}
and
h
as above is now as follows.

1.

Construct an eq-restricted WTAh
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}

recognizing
h(\lVert\mathcal{A}\rVert)
via Lemma
1
.

2.

If
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
has the LDP, then
h\big(\lVert\mathcal{A}\rVert\big)

is not regular.

3.

If
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
does not have the LDP, then
h\big(\lVert\mathcal{A}\rVert\big)
is
regular.

For this procedure to be correct, the LDP must be (i) decidable and (ii) equivalent to the
nonregularity of
\lVert\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\rVert
. While proving (ii) only requires technical adaptations compared
to
[
22
]
, (i) presents new challenges since the pumping lemma for fields is
weaker. We prove (i) indirectly by examining the WTA
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
.

Proposition 2

(cf.
[
22
, Lemma 11]
)

Given as input a WTA
\mathcal{A}
over a field, and a tetris-free tree homomorphism
h
, it is
decidable whether the eq-restricted WTAh
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
for
h(\lVert\mathcal{A}\rVert)
from
Lemma
1
has the LDP.

Proof

Adopting the notation from Definition
8
, let
t_{0}=t|_{pp^{\prime}}
. We will not decide the existence of
a tree

in
\supp(\lVert\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\rVert)

counterpart

\supp(\lVert\widehat{\mathcal{A}}\rVert)

Consider thus the WTA
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
for
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
constructed in Definition

modify
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
by implementing a counter
into its state set, which ensures that only trees of height less than

are
attached to positions that are constrained in
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
. Then we check if any trees have been lost

\supp(\lVert\widehat{\mathcal{A}}\rVert)

Formally, let
q\neq\bot

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}

Consider now the WTA recognizing

union. Subtracting

removes all

It remains to decide whether

the WTA for it

2

3
]
and checking whether it has zero states. If indeed

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
has no tree that satisfies the condition of the LDP. If, however,

satisfies the LDP. ∎

Finally, we can complete the proof of Theorem

. For proving the final proposition, we apply the following version of

’s theorem
[
25
]
. For a set

by

Theorem 4.2

Let

be an integer and

such that

for some

.

Proposition 3

(cf.
[

•

\{p_{1},\dotsc,p_{k}\}=\bigcup_{p\in\pos_{\bot}(\ell)}[p]_{\equiv E}

\;p_{1},\dotsc,p_{k}\in\pos_{Q\dot{\cup}\{\bot\}}(\ell)

together with the pumping

First, we decompose

We now employ

’s theorem in the following way.
For

(t_{i_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}

for all
k,h\in\mathbb{N}

.
Finally, the mapping

\Theta_{kk}=\Theta_{=}
for all

and some set
\Theta_{=}
.
In the following, we show that
\Theta_{<}=\Theta_{>}=\emptyset

\Theta_{<}=\Theta_{>}\subseteq\Theta_{=}\,
; since
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
satisfies

3

all runs of

enforce equality for all positions in

Assume thus that
\Theta_{<}\neq\emptyset

(r_{1},p_{1})\dotsm(r_{m},p_{m})\in\Theta_{<}
with

. Moreover,

for
k,h\in\mathbb{N}
. We show that every constraint
from every
E_{i}
is satisfied on all
C_{kh}
with
k,h\geq 1
,
not just for
k<h
. More precisely, let
i\in[m]
,
(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in E_{i}
, and
(u,v)=(p_{i}u^{\prime},p_{i}v^{\prime})

C_{kh}|_{u}=C_{kh}|_{v}
for all
k,h\geq 1
. Note that by assumption,

C_{kh}|_{u}=C_{kh}|_{v}
is true for all
k,h\in\mathbb{N}
with

We show our statement by a case distinction depending on the

u
and

in relation to
\{w_{1},\dotsc,w_{r}\}=\pos_{\Box}(C)

1.

If both

Part 3.
Finally, we derive a representation of
\lVert\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\rVert
that allows us to prove
its nonregularity. For

we can assume a representation

for

to a specific state of a WTA recognizing

by the regularity assumption, and

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we have proved that the weighted HOM-problem over fields for tetris-free tree
homomorphisms is decidable. Formally, for a WTA
\mathcal{A}
over a field, and a tetris-free tree
homomorphism
h
as input, it is decidable whether
h(\lVert\mathcal{A}\rVert)
is again regular. A tree
homomorphism is tetris-free if its non-injective behaviour is located only at the symbol level, thus this
property generalizes injectivity.

Our proof strategy is similar to
[
22
]
: We have reduced the HOM-problem to
a decidable property of (the WTAh that recognizes)
h(\lVert\mathcal{A}\rVert)
. The
homomorphism
h
has the ability to duplicate subtrees of its input trees, and we have shown
that
h(\lVert\mathcal{A}\rVert)
is regular iff
h
duplicates only finitely many subtrees of trees accepted by
\mathcal{A}
.
This limited duplication is in turn decidable, and proving its decidability is our main contribution.
For this, we presented a pumping lemma for the WTAh recognizing
h(\lVert\mathcal{A}\rVert)
, by translating it
into a WTA and applying the pumping lemma for WTA over fields proved in
[
1
]
.

The analogous decision problem, without the tetris-free restriction, is also decidable for WTA
over
\mathbb{N}

[
22
]
. However, since fields allow zero-sums, the proof strategy fails without
the tetris-free restriction, as our last example illustrates.

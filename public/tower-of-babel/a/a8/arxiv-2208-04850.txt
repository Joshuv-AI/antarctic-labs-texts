Evolving finite elements for advection diffusion with an evolving interface
C.M. Elliott and T. Ranner and P. Stepanov

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to develop a numerical scheme to approximate evolving interface problems for parabolic equations based on the abstract evolving finite element framework proposed in [ 23 ] . An appropriate weak formulation of the problem is derived for the use of evolving finite elements designed to accommodate for a moving interface. Optimal order error bounds are proved for arbitrary order evolving isoparametric finite elements. The paper concludes with numerical results for a model problem verifying orders of convergence.



1.
Introduction

The model studied in the paper is the following, let
\Omega
be a stationary domain with a moving interface
\Gamma(t)
that encloses a subdomain
\Omega_{1}(t)
and let
\Omega_{2}(t)=\Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{1}(t)
.
We denote by
\nu_{\Gamma}
the outward pointing normal to
\Omega_{1}(t)
.
Let, for
i=1
and
2
,
\mathcal{A}_{i}
be a diffusion tensor field,
\mathcal{B}_{i}
be a vector field and
\mathcal{C}_{i}
be a scalar field, each continuous on
\Omega_{i}(t)
.
Let
f_{1},f_{2}
and
g
be time-dependent functions on
\Omega_{1}(t)
,
\Omega_{2}(t)
and
\Gamma(t)
respectively.
More precise definitions of the problem data are given in
2.16
.
We are interested in well posedness and a suitable finite element scheme for the solutions of the following problem:
Find scalar fields
u_{1}
on the subdomain
\Omega_{1}(t)
and
u_{2}
on the subdomain
\Omega_{2}(t)
, which satisfy:

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{i}-\nabla\cdot(\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x)\nabla u_{i})+\mathcal{B}_{i}(t;x)\cdot\nabla u_{i}+\mathcal{C}_{i}(t;x)u_{i}

\displaystyle=f_{i}(t;x)

\displaystyle\quad\text{in}\;\Omega_{i}(t),

(1.1b)

\displaystyle u_{2}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\quad\text{on}\;\partial\Omega,

(1.1c)

\displaystyle u_{1}-u_{2}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\quad\text{on}\;\Gamma(t),

(1.1d)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}(t;x)\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu_{\Gamma}}u_{1}\bigg|_{\Gamma(t)}-\mathcal{A}_{2}(t;x)\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu_{\Gamma}}u_{2}\bigg|_{\Gamma(t)}

\displaystyle=g(t;x)

\displaystyle\quad\text{on}\;\Gamma(t),

(1.1e)

\displaystyle u_{i}(0)

\displaystyle=u_{i}^{0}

\displaystyle\quad\text{on}\;\Omega_{i}(0).

Such equations can arise as subproblems when modelling the transport and diffusion of the concentration of a dissolved chemical species in evolving spatial domains.
In particular, we mention applications in fluid dynamics
[
50
,
12
,
2
]
, materials science
[
11
,
29
]
and cell biology
[
51
,
48
,
30
]
.

There are two main difficulties concerning this problem. The first of which is the evolution of the sub-domains and the second is the presence of a discontinuous jump across the interface. One common approach to moving domains is the
ALE
(Arbitrary Eulerian Lagrangian) method, see
[
33
,
49
,
39
]
. This involves having a parametrisation of the evolving region. The flow associated with this parametrisation could be physical or could be made to fit a specific purpose such as in
[
19
]
where the flow is chosen using knowledge of the surface velocity to construct a harmonic extension.
Another common method is to use a discontinuous or immersed Galerkin method
[
52
,
3
,
41
]
. In this paper we propose and analyse an ALE approach using evolving finite elements on an evolving fitted mesh allowing the use of isoparametric elements that accurately approximate the boundary and result in higher order error estimates. The underlying parametrisation is assumed given.

The key contributions of this work are:

•

We provide a functional analytic setting to show well posedness of the continuous problem,
eq.

1.1
.

•

We provide an ALE approach based on evolving isoparametric finite element spaces attached to evolving sub-domains. The evolving mesh is based on moving the Lagrange nodes with a given known smooth velocity. Achieving a higher order method requires a good initial mesh.

•

We provide a robust error bound which demonstrates the error in an
L^{2}
norm is bounded, up to a constant, by
h^{k+1}
, where
h
represents the mesh size and
k
is the degree of polynomials used both for the discretisation of the domain and the solution. This is the same order error as if we interpolated a known smooth solution.

•

Numerical results and the simulation code are provided both to demonstrate the results and to allow others to use the implementation.

The assumption is made that we are given a global, smooth velocity field
\mathbf{w}
. Furthermore, the velocity field is such that moving the nodes of the mesh with the
\mathbf{w}
preserves the regularity of the mesh over time. The velocity may be derived from physical considerations or otherwise an arbitrary velocity constructed in order to define a well behaved numerical scheme. We do not address how to achieve such a velocity in this work.
There are methods in the literature to prevent mesh deformation, which involve re-parametrising the flow responsible for the movement of the interface into a more suitable flow, see, for example,
[
10
,
20
,
21
]
.

Evolving space formulation and well posedness

In this paper,
c
will be used as a generic constant that depends on no quantity of particular importance. We use
(\cdot,\cdot)_{H}
for an inner product on a Hilbert space
H
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X}

X

X^{\prime}

2.1.
Evolving Hilbert Spaces

We set up the necessary tools from the theory of evolving Sobolev spaces which were introduced and developed in
[
5
,
6
,
4
]
. We will only concern ourselves with the Hilbert case. A more general theory is developed in
[
4
]
concerning general Banach spaces. Let
I=[0,T]
be a closed time interval and let
\{X(t)\}_{t\in I}
be a family of Hilbert spaces equipped with norm
\|\cdot\|_{X(t)}
. Assume that there exists a linear map
\phi_{t}:X(0)\to X(t)
satisfying the following properties:

B1

The map
\phi_{t}
is invertible for all
t\in I
with inverse denoted by
\phi_{-t}
and
\phi_{0}
being the identity.

B2

There exists a constant
C
independent of time such that
\|\phi_{t}\eta\|_{X(t)}\leq C\|\eta\|_{X(0)}
,
\|\phi_{-t}\widetilde{\eta}\|_{X(0)}\leq C\|\widetilde{\eta}\|_{X(t)}
, for all
\eta\in X(0)
and
\widetilde{\eta}\in X(t)
, for all
t\in I
.

B3

The map
t\mapsto\|\phi_{t}\eta\|_{X(t)}
is measurable for all
\eta\in X(0)
.

Here and elsewhere we use the notation
\phi_{t}\eta
to denote the map
\phi_{t}
applied to
\eta
.
If such a map
\phi_{t}
exists then we call it the
flow map
and the pair
(X(t),\phi_{t})_{t\in I}
a
compatible pair
. Given a compatible pair, define the
Hilbert moving spaces
as:

(2.1)

\displaystyle L^{2}_{X}:

\displaystyle=\bigg\{\eta:I\to\bigcup_{t\in I}X(t)\times\{t\},\quad t\mapsto(\widehat{\eta}(t),t)\,|\,\phi_{-t}\widehat{\eta}(t)\in L^{2}(I;X(0))\bigg\},

and the uniformly bounded equivalent:

(2.2)

\displaystyle L^{\infty}_{X}:

\displaystyle=\bigg\{\eta:I\to\bigcup_{t\in I}X(t)\times\{t\},\quad t\mapsto(\widehat{\eta}(t),t)\,|\,\phi_{-t}\widehat{\eta}(t)\in L^{\infty}(I;X(0))\bigg\}.

We identify
\eta(t)=(\widehat{\eta}(t),t)
with
\widehat{\eta}(t)
. The spaces
L^{p}_{X}
are equipped with the norm:

\|\eta\|_{L^{p}_{X}}:=\begin{cases}\left({\int_{0}^{T}\|\eta(t)\|_{X(t)}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}&\text{for }p=2,\\
\esssup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\eta(t)\|_{X(t)}&\text{for }p=\infty.\end{cases}

L^{2}_{X}
is indeed a Hilbert space, see
[
4
, Thm. 3.4]
.
The analogues of the spaces of continuous functions and of compactly supported smooth functions are defined as:

\displaystyle C^{k}_{X}

\displaystyle:=\bigg\{\eta:I\to\bigcup_{t\in I}X(t)\times\{t\},\quad t\to(\widehat{\eta}(t),t)\,|\,\phi_{-t}\widehat{\eta}(t)\in C^{k}(I;X(0))\bigg\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{X}

\displaystyle:=\bigg\{\eta:I\to\bigcup_{t\in I}X(t)\times\{t\},\quad t\to(\widehat{\eta}(t),t)\,|\,\phi_{-t}\widehat{\eta}(t)\in\mathcal{D}(I;X(0))\bigg\}.

Remark 2.1
.

(1)

The use of a Cartesian product
\times
inside the union in
eq.

2.1
rather then just taking the union of
\{X(t)\}_{t\in I}
by itself is in order to guarantee a disjoint union which is crucial to identify the function point-wise.

(2)

Note that the spaces
L^{p}_{X}
do not depend on the choice of the map
\phi_{t}
.

The
strong material
derivative in the evolving Hilbert space setting is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\bullet}\eta:=\phi_{t}\partial_{t}(\phi_{-t}\eta),\quad\eta\in C^{1}_{X}.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
4
]
, Thm. 2.4)
.

Given a compatible pair
(X(t),\phi_{t})_{t\in I}
, the maps
\phi_{t}:L^{2}(I;X(0))\to L^{2}_{X}
and
\phi_{-t}:L^{2}_{X}\to L^{2}(I;X(0))
define continuous linear isomorphism to their respective spaces.

Now assume
\{X(t)\}_{t\in I}
,
\{Y(t)\}_{t\in I}
and
\{X^{*}(t)\}_{t\in I}
are families of Hilbert spaces, with
X^{*}(t)
the dual of
X(t)
for all
t\in I
(crucially,
X(t)
and
X^{*}(t)
are not identified). Assume further that for all
t\in I
,
X(t)\subset Y(t)\cong Y^{*}(t)\subset X^{*}(t)
constitutes a Hilbert triple (in the sense that
X(t)
is densely and continuously embedded into
Y(t)
and
Y(t)
is identified with its dual via Riesz representation). It is also assumed that there exists a map
\phi_{t}:Y(0)\to Y(t)
with
\phi_{t}|_{X(0)}:X(0)\to X(t)
with adjoint flow
\phi_{-t}^{*}:X^{*}(0)\to X^{*}(t)
,

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{-t}^{*}f,v\rangle_{X(t)}:=\langle f,\phi_{-t}v\rangle_{X(0)},\quad f\in X^{*}(0),\,v\in X(t),

such that
(X(t),\phi_{t}|_{X(t)})_{t\in I}
,
(Y(t),\phi_{t})_{t\in I}
and
(X^{*}(t),\phi_{-t}^{*})_{t\in I}
all define compatible pairs and therefore we can define the spaces
L^{2}_{X},\;L^{2}_{Y},\;L^{2}_{X^{*}}
with their respective flows. In this case, just as for Bochner spaces, we have the have that
(L^{2}_{X})^{*}
is isometrically isomorphic to
L^{2}_{X^{*}}

[
4
, Thm. 3.7]
. Moreover, the Hilbert triple structure is preserved:
L^{2}_{X}\subset L^{2}_{Y}\subset L^{2}_{X^{*}}
. Note that
L^{2}_{Y}
remains a Hilbert space with a natural inner product structure, see
[
4
, Rem. 3.9]
. In order to generalise the concept of a “weak time derivative” to the evolving space, we first assume the following:

D1

The map
t\mapsto\langle\phi_{t}w_{0},\phi_{t}v_{0}\rangle_{X(t)}=(\phi_{t}w_{0},\phi_{t}v_{0})_{Y(t)}
is continuously differentiable for fixed
w_{0},v_{0}\in X_{0}
.

D2

For all
t\in I
, the map:

\displaystyle[w_{0},v_{0}]\mapsto\frac{d}{dt}(\phi_{t}w_{0},\phi_{t}v_{0})_{Y(t)},

for
[w_{0},v_{0}]\in X(0)\times X(0)
is continuous.

D3

There exists a constant
C_{\lambda}
independent of time such that, for almost all
t\in I
and
w_{0},v_{0}\in X(0)
, we have:

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{d}{dt}(\phi_{t}w_{0},\phi_{t}v_{0})_{Y(t)}\bigg|\leq C_{\lambda}\|w_{0}\|_{Y(0)}\|v_{0}\|_{Y(0)}.

Definition 2.3
.

Let Ass.

⁢
D
1

to

⁢
D
3

hold and label:

\displaystyle\lambda(t;w,v):=\bigg[\frac{d}{dt}(\phi_{t}w_{0},\phi_{t}v_{0})_{Y(t)}\bigg]\bigg|_{(w_{0},v_{0})=(\phi_{-t}w,\phi_{-t}v)},\quad w,v\in X(t).

Then
\lambda(t;\cdot,\cdot):Y(t)\times Y(t)\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous, symmetric, bounded and bilinear for almost all
t\in I
. We say
w\in L^{1}_{X}
has a weak material derivative if there exists
v\in L^{1}_{X^{*}}
such that:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}(w(t),\partial^{\bullet}_{t}\eta)_{Y(t)}\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\langle v(t),\eta\rangle_{X(t)}+\lambda(t;w,\eta)\,dt,

for all
\eta\in\mathcal{D}_{X}
. We label the weak material derivative
v=\partial_{t}^{\bullet}w
.

This definition follows all properties we expect from a weak derivative, such as being equivalent to the strong material derivative if the function is regular enough.

This allows us to define the equivalent of the Bochner solution space.

Definition 2.4
.

We define
W(X,Y):=\left\{v\in L^{2}_{X},\;\partial_{t}^{\bullet}v\in L^{2}_{Y}\right\}
with the norm:

\displaystyle\|v\|^{2}_{W(X,Y)}:=\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{X}}+\|\partial_{t}^{\bullet}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{Y}}

and the solution space
W(X,X^{*}):=\left\{v\in L^{2}_{X},\;\partial_{t}^{\bullet}v\in L^{2}_{X^{*}}\right\}
with the norm:

\displaystyle\|v\|^{2}_{W}:=\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{X}}+\|\partial_{t}^{\bullet}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{X^{*}}}.

Definition 2.5
.

The space
W(X,Y)
is said to satisfy moving space equivalence if:

\displaystyle v\in W(X,Y)\iff\phi_{-t}v\in\mathcal{W}^{2,2}(X(0),Y(0)),

where
\mathcal{W}^{2,2}(X(0),Y(0))=\{v_{0}\in L^{2}(I;X(0)),\;\partial_{t}v\in L^{2}(I;Y(0))\}.

Theorem 2.6

(The Transport Theorem)
.

Assume
v,w\in W(X,X^{*})
and the moving space equivalence is satisfied, then, the map
t\mapsto(v,w)_{Y(t)}
is uniformly continuous and for almost all
t\in I
, and the following holds:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(v,w)_{Y(t)}=\langle\partial_{t}^{\bullet}v,w\rangle_{X(t)}+\langle\partial_{t}^{\bullet}w,v\rangle_{X(t)}+\lambda(t;u,v).

Moreover,
C^{0}_{Y}\hookrightarrow W(X,X^{*})
.

See
[
4
, Sec. 4.5]
for proofs.

Lemma 2.7

(Characterisation of Material Derivative)
.

Let the moving space equivalence hold for
W(X,X)
, then for
v\in W(X,X)
, we have
v\in C_{X}^{0}
and there exists a function
v_{0}\in\mathcal{W}^{2,2}(X(0),X(0))
such that
v=\phi_{t}v_{0}
. Moreover,
C^{1}_{X}
is dense in
W(X,X)
.

This follows from
[
4
, Lem. 3.20]
. Importantly, this implies
\partial_{t}^{\bullet}v=0
if and only if
v=\phi_{t}v_{0}
for some
v_{0}\in X(0)
.

Remark 2.8
.

The assumption that the outer boundary is piecewise linear is made to avoid having to analyse perturbation of the domain for Dirichlet boundary conditions, however the presented method and analysis can easily be altered if one removes this assumption.

We label the outer normals of
\Omega_{1}(t)
and
\Omega_{2}(t)
by
\nu_{\Gamma(t)}
and
\nu_{\partial\Omega_{2}(t)}
respectively.
Let:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{i}:=\bigcup_{t\in I}\Omega_{i}(t)\times\{t\},\;\mathcal{Q}:=\Omega\times I.

Furthermore, we assume there exists a given, global velocity field
\mathbf{w}
transporting
\Omega_{1}(t)
and
\Omega_{2}(t)
, i.e
\mathbf{w}\cdot\nu_{\Gamma(t)}|_{\Gamma(t)}=V_{\Gamma}
where
V_{\Gamma}
is the normal velocity of
\Gamma(t)
,
\mathbf{w}\cdot\nu_{\partial\Omega_{2}(t)}|_{\partial\Gamma(t)}=-V_{\Gamma}
and
\mathbf{w}\cdot\nu_{\partial\Omega_{2}(t)}|_{\partial\Gamma}=0
. Throughout the paper this velocity is assumed to be of regularity
\mathbf{w}\in C(I;C(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d}))
with
\mathbf{w}_{i}(t;\cdot)\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega}_{i}(t);\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Let
\mathbf{\Phi}_{i}(t;x):\overline{\Omega}_{i}(0)\to\overline{\Omega}_{i}(t)
be the solution to the ordinary differential equations:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathbf{\Phi}_{i}(t;x)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{w}(t;\mathbf{\Phi}_{i}(t;x))\qquad x\in\Omega_{i}(0),

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Phi}_{i}(0;x)

\displaystyle=x.

We assume the solution exists and is of regularity
\mathbf{\Phi}_{i}\in C^{1}(\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{i};\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\mathbf{\Phi}_{i}(t;\cdot)\colon\overline{\Omega}_{i}(0)\to\overline{\Omega}_{i}(t)
and
\mathbf{\Phi}_{i}(t;\cdot)\in C^{2}(\Omega_{i}(0);\mathbb{R}^{d})
, see
[
47
, Thm. 1.45]
and
[
31
, Thm. II.1.1, Sec. V]
for the necessary additional conditions. Furthermore, both
\mathbf{\Phi}_{i}(t;\cdot)
are invertible diffeomorphisms for all
t\in I
with
\mathrm{Im}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{i}(t;\cdot))=\Omega_{i}(t)
.
We denote by
\Phi_{i}(-t,\cdot)
the inverse of
\Phi_{i}(t,\cdot)
.
Since we assumed
\mathbf{w}\in C(I;C(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, it follows that
\mathbf{\Phi}_{1}(t;x)|_{\Gamma(0)}=\mathbf{\Phi}_{2}(t;x)|_{\Gamma(0)}
.

Remark 2.9
.

For the abstract formulation of the problem, it is only required to assume that the velocity field
\mathbf{w}
is of sufficient regularity. However, for the purpose of evolving the mesh later, we will assume this velocity field is known explicitly.

Let

denote the determinant of Jacobian matrix,
J^{t}_{i}=\text{det}[\nabla\mathbf{\Phi}_{i}(t;x)]
. The prior assumptions imply
J^{t}_{i}(\cdot)\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}_{i}(0);\mathbb{R})
and there exists

independent of time and space such that:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{\Omega}}\leq|J_{i}^{t}|\leq C_{\Omega},

and

denotes its inverse.

Remark 2.10
.

Note that we have assumed the global parametric velocity
\mathbf{w}
is given.
The solution of the partial differential equation system is independent of
\mathbf{w}
apart from the requirements that
\mathbf{w}_{\Gamma}\cdot\nu_{\Gamma(t)}=V_{\Gamma}
and
\mathbf{w}\cdot\nu_{\partial\Omega}=0
. However the evolving mesh does depend on

hence the discrete solution depends on the full parametric velocity.

Let

be the signed distance function to

:

\displaystyle d_{\Gamma}(t;x)=\begin{cases}-\inf\{|x-y|:y\in\Gamma(t)\},\quad&\text{for}\,x\in\overline{\Omega}_{1}(t),\\
\inf\{|x-y|:y\in\Gamma(t)\},\quad&\text{for}\,x\in\Omega_{2}(t).\end{cases}

Then, since the interface is of class

, there exists a constant

such that if

, it can be uniquely decomposed as:

(2.4)

\displaystyle x=d_{\Gamma}(t;x)\nu_{\Gamma(t)}(\Pi_{t}(x))+\Pi_{t}(x),

where

is the nearest point on

, i.e,

(see

. Note that

2.3.
Realisation

For a given function
v
acting on
\Omega
, we decompose it as:

\displaystyle v_{1}:=v\chi_{\overline{\Omega}_{1}(t)},\quad v_{2}:=v\chi_{\overline{\Omega}_{2}(t)},

where
\chi_{\overline{\Omega}_{i}(t)}=1
if
x\in\overline{\Omega}_{i}(t)
and zero otherwise. A function
v
on
\Omega
will be identified as the pair
v=(v_{1},v_{2})
. The jump operator
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\Gamma(t)}:C(\overline{\Omega}_{1}(t);\mathbb{R})\times C(\overline{\Omega}_{1}(t);\mathbb{R})\to C(\Gamma(t);\mathbb{R})
as:

\displaystyle\llbracket v\rrbracket_{\Gamma(t)}:=[v_{1}-v_{2}]|_{\Gamma(t)}.

This functional has a natural extension on the Cartesian product of standard Sobolev spaces
H^{1}(\Omega_{1}(t))\times H^{1}(\Omega_{2}(t))
via use of the trace maps;
T_{i}(t):H^{1}(\Omega_{i}(t))\to H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega_{i}(t))
,
i\in\{1,2\}
, (see
[
46
, Sec. 7.2.5]
for an extensive definition of the trace map) as:

\displaystyle\llbracket v\rrbracket_{\Gamma(t)}:=[T_{1}(t)v_{1}-T_{2}(t)v_{2}]|_{\Gamma(t)}.

We define the following spaces:

\displaystyle H(t)

\displaystyle:=L^{2}(\Omega_{1}(t))\times L^{2}(\Omega_{2}(t)),\quad\|v\|_{H(t)}^{2}:=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\|v_{i}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i}(t))},

\displaystyle V(t)

\displaystyle:=\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega_{1}(t))\times H^{1}(\Omega_{2}(t)),\,\llbracket v\rrbracket_{\Gamma(t)}=0,\;v_{2}|_{\partial\Omega}=0\},\quad\|v\|_{V(t)}^{2}:=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\|v_{i}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega_{i}(t))}

\displaystyle Z_{k}(t)

\displaystyle:=\{w\in V(t)|\;w_{i}\in H^{1+k}(\Omega_{i}(t))\},\;\|w\|^{2}_{Z_{k}(t)}:=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\|w_{i}\|^{2}_{H^{1+k}(\Omega_{i}(t))}.

Theorem 2.15

.

Let

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{i}(t)}g_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{i}(t)}\partial_{t}g_{i}+\mathbf{w}\cdot\nabla g_{i}+g_{i}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}.

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega\setminus\Gamma(t)}g=\int_{\Omega\setminus\Gamma(t)}\partial_{t}g-\int_{\Gamma(t)}\llbracket g\rrbracket_{\Gamma(t)}V_{\Gamma}.

Note that here

, and:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{i}(t)}\mathbf{w}\cdot\nabla g_{i}+g_{i}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{i}(t)}\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{w}g_{i})

\displaystyle=-\int_{\partial\Omega}[\mathbf{w}g_{i}]\cdot\nu_{\partial\Omega}-\int_{\Gamma(t)}\llbracket\mathbf{w}g\rrbracket_{\Gamma(t)}\cdot\nu_{\Gamma(t)},

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Gamma(t)}\llbracket g\rrbracket_{\Gamma(t)}V_{\Gamma}.\qed

2.4.
The Weak Formulation

Taking the strong problem
eq.

1.1
, assuming there exists a regular enough solution

, we can rewrite the partial differential equation as:

(2.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\bullet}u_{i}+\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}u_{i}-\nabla\cdot(\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x)\nabla u_{i})+[\mathcal{B}_{i}(t;x)-\mathbf{w}]\cdot\nabla u_{i}+[\mathcal{C}_{i}(t;x)-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}]u_{i}=f_{i}.

Here the term

, corresponding to the previously identified bilinear form

,

2.6

and using the interface condition, we arrive at the following variational problem:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle\partial_{t}^{\bullet}u,v\rangle_{V(t)}\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\lambda(t;u,v)+\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{i}(t)}\mathcal{A}_{i}\nabla u_{i}\cdot\nabla v_{i}+[\mathcal{B}_{i}-\mathbf{w}]\cdot\nabla u_{i}\,v_{i}+[\mathcal{C}_{i}-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}]u_{i}\,v_{i}}_{=:a(t;u,v)}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\underbrace{\langle f,v\rangle_{V(t)}+\langle g,v\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(t)}}_{=:l(t;v)}\,dt.

Note that here the Hilbert triple structure is used for the duality pairings;
\langle f,v\rangle_{V(t)}=(f,v)_{H(t)}
and
\langle g,v\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(t)}=(g,v)_{\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(t)}

. This gives us the weak formulation:

(2.8)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle\partial_{t}^{\bullet}u,v\rangle_{V(t)}+a(t;u,v)+\lambda(t;u,v)\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}l(t;v)\,dt,

for all

. Moreover if, instead

, we get the equivalent formulation via the transport theorem (for notational convenience later on, we will label the inner product

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}m(t;u,v)+a(t;u,v)=m(t;u,\partial^{\bullet}_{t}v)+l(t;v).

If

, then via identification of the Hilbert triple, we have:

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}^{\bullet}u,v\rangle_{V(t)}=m(t;\partial_{t}^{\bullet}u,v),

and the problem can be restated abstractly in this case as

being the solution to:

(2.9)

\displaystyle m(t;\partial_{t}^{\bullet}u,v)+a(t;u,v)+\lambda(t;u,v)=l(t;v),

for almost all

Let

\displaystyle y(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{L_{K}}\frac{\mu_{j}(x)}{\widetilde{\mu}_{K}(x)}\widetilde{a}_{\widetilde{K}}^{j}.

\displaystyle[\tilde{I}^{h}\mathbf{\Psi}^{h}]|_{\widetilde{K}_{1}}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{j:\chi^{j}\in P^{k}\}}\Pi_{0}(\widetilde{\alpha}^{j}_{\widetilde{K}_{2}})\chi^{j}(x)=\sum_{\{j:\chi^{j}\in P^{k}\}}\mathbf{\Psi}^{h}(\widetilde{\alpha}_{\widetilde{K}_{2}}^{j})\chi^{j}(x)=[\tilde{I}^{h}\mathbf{\Psi}^{h}]|_{\widetilde{K}_{2}}(x).

For

is invertible on

\displaystyle E:=[I^{h}\mathbf{\Psi}^{h}]|_{\widetilde{K}_{1}}(\widetilde{E})=I^{h}\mathbf{\Psi}^{h}(\widetilde{K}_{1}\cap\widetilde{K}_{2})=I^{h}\mathbf{\Psi}^{h}(\widetilde{K}_{1})\cap I^{h}\mathbf{\Psi}^{h}(\widetilde{K}_{2})=K_{1}\cap K_{2}.

3.2.
Time Dependent Mesh

Define the flow:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Phi}^{h}_{i}(t;\cdot)|_{K_{i}(0)}:=I^{h}_{K_{i}(0)}[\mathbf{\Phi}_{i}(t;\mathbf{\Psi}^{h}\circ(\widetilde{I}^{h}\mathbf{\Psi}^{h})^{-1}(\cdot))].

\mathbf{\Phi}^{h}_{i}(t;\cdot)|_{K_{i}(0)}

Remark 3.3
.

The flow is defined this way such that it evolves the parametric meshes
\Omega_{i}^{h}(0)
. Indeed, decomposing
\mathbf{\Phi}^{h}_{i}(t;\cdot)
into its components:

\displaystyle(\widetilde{I}^{h}\mathbf{\Psi}^{h})^{-1}:\Omega^{h}_{i}(0)\to\widetilde{\Omega}^{h}_{i},\;\mathbf{\Psi}^{h}:\widetilde{\Omega}^{h}_{i}\to\overline{\Omega}_{i}(0),\,\mathbf{\Phi}_{i}(t;\cdot):\overline{\Omega}_{i}(0)\to\overline{\Omega}_{i}(t).

Hence, the flow
\mathbf{\Phi}^{h}_{i}(t;\cdot)
is a polynomial function approximating the evolution of the domains. Moreover, this does define a proper flow map, as, at

:

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Phi}^{h}_{i}(0;x)|_{K_{i}(0)}=I^{h}_{K_{i}(0)}[\mathbf{\Psi}^{h}\circ(\widetilde{I}^{h}\mathbf{\Psi}^{h})^{-1}(x)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\chi^{j}\in P^{k}}\mathbf{\Psi}^{h}\circ[(\widetilde{I}^{h}\mathbf{\Psi}^{h})^{-1}(\alpha^{j}_{K_{i}})]\chi^{j}(x),

\displaystyle=\sum_{\chi^{j}\in P^{k}}\mathbf{\Psi}^{h}(\widetilde{\alpha}^{j}_{K_{i}})\chi^{j}(x)=\sum_{\chi^{j}\in P^{k}}\alpha^{j}_{K_{i}}\chi^{j}(x)=x.

The composition property for
\mathbf{\Phi}^{h}_{i}(t+s;\cdot)=\mathbf{\Phi}^{h}_{i}(t;\mathbf{\Phi}^{h}_{i}(s;\cdot))
will be shown following the next lemma.

For
\widetilde{h}
small enough, this map is an invertible diffeomorphism on each element

Lemma 3.4
.

For
\widetilde{h}

\displaystyle P^{k}(t)

\displaystyle:=\{\chi\mapsto\sigma(\chi\circ F_{K(t)}):\,\sigma\in\widehat{\Sigma}^{k}\},

3.3.
The lift

The last mesh related concept needed is the
lift map
(see
[
23
, Sec. 8.6]
). Fix
t\in I
, if
K(t)\in\mathcal{J}^{h}(t)
is an interior element, then define the lift
\Lambda^{h}(t;\cdot)
as:

\displaystyle\Lambda^{h}(t;x)=x,\;\text{for}\;x\in K(t).

If instead
K(t)
is an interface element, we first pull-back the reference map to
\widehat{x}\in\widehat{K}_{\text{ref}}
such that
x=F_{K(t)}(\widehat{x})
, then decomposing
\widehat{x}
into barycentric coordinates with respect to the vertices
\widehat{a}_{K_{\text{ref}}}^{j}
of
\widehat{K}_{\text{ref}}
, we have:

\displaystyle\widehat{x}=\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}\mu_{j}(\widehat{x})\widehat{a}^{j}_{K_{\text{ref}}},

and once again, let
L_{K}
be the number of vertices on the interface and assume the vertices are ordered so that
\widehat{a}^{j}_{K_{\text{ref}}}
,
j=1,\ldots,L_{K}
, get mapped on to
\Gamma(t)
, then we introduce the interface distance and the singular set analogously:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mu}(\widehat{x})=\sum_{j=1}^{L_{K}}\mu_{j}(\widehat{x}),\;\sigma=\{\widehat{x}\in\widehat{K}_{\text{ref}}|\;\widetilde{\mu}(\widehat{x})=0\}.

The projection is now defined on the reference element:

\displaystyle\widehat{y}(\widehat{x})=\sum_{j=1}^{L_{K}}\frac{\mu_{i}(\widehat{x})}{\widetilde{\mu}(\widehat{x})},\;y(t;x):=F_{K(t)}(\widehat{y}(\widehat{x})).

Hence the lift operator can now be defined on interface elements as:

\displaystyle\Lambda^{h}(t;x)|_{K(t)}=\begin{cases}x+(\widetilde{\mu}(\widehat{x}))^{k+2}(\Pi_{t}(y(t;x))-y(t;x))\quad&\text{if}\,\widehat{x}\notin\sigma,\\
x\quad&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then, computing component wise, we see:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{h}\Lambda^{h}(t;x)|_{K(t)}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{w}^{h}(t;x)+\partial_{t}^{h}[(\widetilde{\mu}(\widehat{x}))^{k+2}(\Pi_{t}(y(t;x))-y(t;x))]\quad&\text{if}\,\widehat{x}\notin\sigma,\\
\mathbf{w}^{h}(t;x)\quad&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
z=\mathbf{\Phi}_{i}^{h}(-t;x)
(depending on whether
x\in\Omega_{i}^{h}(t)
, we also use the shorthand
\mathbf{\Phi}_{-t}(x)=\mathbf{\Phi}_{i}^{h}(-t;x)
and
\mathbf{\Phi}_{t}(z)=\mathbf{\Phi}_{i}^{h}(t;z)
, depending on whether
z\in\Omega^{h}_{i}(0)
):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{h}[(\widetilde{\mu}(\widehat{x}))^{k+2}(\Pi_{t}(y(t;x))-y(t;x))]

\displaystyle=\phi_{t}^{h}\frac{d}{dt}[(\widetilde{\mu}(F^{-1}_{\hat{K}}z))^{k+2}(\Pi_{t}(y(t;\mathbf{\Phi}^{h}_{t}(z))-y(t;\mathbf{\Phi}^{h}_{t}(z))],

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mu}(\hat{x})^{k+2}\phi_{t}^{h}\frac{d}{dt}[\Pi_{t}(y(t;\mathbf{\Phi}^{h}_{t}(z)))-y(t;\mathbf{\Phi}^{h}_{t}(z))],

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mu}(\hat{x})^{k+2}\left[\partial_{t}\Pi_{t}(y(t;x))+\partial^{h}_{t}y(t;x)\cdot[\nabla\Pi_{t}(y(t;x))]-\partial^{h}_{t}y(t;x)\right].

The formula for
\partial^{h}_{t}y(t;x)
can also be explicitly found:

\displaystyle\partial^{h}_{t}y(t;x)=\phi^{h}_{t}\frac{d}{dt}F_{K(t)}(\widetilde{y}(t;F^{-1}_{\hat{K}}z))=\phi^{h}_{t}\mathbf{w}^{h}(t;F_{K(t)}\widetilde{y}(t;F^{-1}_{\hat{K}}z))=\mathbf{w}^{h}(t;y(t;x)),

by use of
eq.

3.3
and the definition of
F_{K(t)}
. Hence:

(3.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{h}\Lambda^{h}(t;x)|_{K(t)}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\mathbf{w}^{h}(t;x)+\widetilde{\mu}(\hat{x})^{k+2}\left[\partial_{t}\Pi_{t}(y)+\mathbf{w}^{h}(t;y)\cdot[\nabla\Pi_{t}(y)]-\mathbf{w}^{h}(t;y)\right]\quad&\text{if}\,x\notin\sigma\\
\mathbf{w}^{h}(t;x)\quad&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\mathbf{w}^{h}(t;x)-\widetilde{\mu}(\hat{x})^{k+2}\left[\left(\mathbf{w}^{h}(t;y)-\mathbf{w}(t;\Pi_{t}(y))\right)\cdot\nu_{\Gamma}(\Pi_{t}(y))\nu_{\Gamma}(\Pi_{t}(y))+d_{\Gamma}(t;y)T(y)\right]\quad&\text{if}\,x\notin\sigma,\\
\mathbf{w}^{h}(t;x)\quad&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

with
T(x):=\partial_{t}[\nu_{\Gamma}(\Pi_{t}(x))]+\mathbf{w}^{h}(t;x)\cdot\nabla[\nu_{\Gamma}(\Pi_{t}(x))]
. We have used the tubular neighbourhood decomposition
eq.

4.
Evolving finite element method

4.1.
Scheme

For any
U^{h},\,\zeta^{h}\in V^{h}(t)
, let:

\displaystyle m^{h}(t;U^{h},\zeta^{h})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega^{h}_{i}(t)}U^{h}_{i}\zeta^{h}_{i},

\displaystyle a^{h}(t;U^{h},\zeta^{h})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega^{h}_{i}(t)}\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;\Lambda^{h}_{l}(t;x))\nabla U^{h}_{i}\cdot\nabla\zeta^{h}_{i}+[\mathcal{B}(t;\Lambda^{h}_{l}(t;x))-\mathbf{w}^{h}]\cdot\nabla U^{h}\,\zeta^{h}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+[\mathcal{C}(t;\Lambda^{h}_{l}(t;x))-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}^{h}(t;x)]U^{h}\,\zeta^{h},

\displaystyle l^{h}(t;\zeta^{h})

\displaystyle:=(f^{-l}J^{h},\zeta^{h})_{H^{h}(t)}+(g^{-l}\mu^{h},\zeta^{h})_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{h}(t))},

where
J^{h}
,
\mu^{h}
are the discrete Jacobians with respect to the lift maps
\Lambda^{h}(t;x)|_{\Gamma^{h}(t)}
, and by regularity of
\Lambda^{h}
, are of class
C^{k}(K_{i}(t);\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
C^{k}(E(t);\mathbb{R}^{d}),\;\forall K_{i}(t)\in\mathcal{J}^{h}_{i}(t),\;\forall E(t)\in\mathcal{J}^{h}_{\Gamma}(t)
, respectively.

The finite element method is to find
U^{h}(t)\in\mathcal{S}^{h}(t)
satisfying the discrete variational problem:

(4.1)

\displaystyle m^{h}(t;\partial^{h}_{t}U^{h},\zeta^{h})+a^{h}(t;U^{h},\zeta^{h})+\lambda^{h}(t;U^{h},\zeta^{h})

\displaystyle=l^{h}(t;\zeta^{h})\quad\forall\zeta^{h}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}},\forall t\in I,

\displaystyle U^{h}(0)=U^{h}_{0}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j=1}^{dim(\mathcal{S}^{h})}(u_{0},\chi_{j}^{h})_{H(0)}\chi_{j}^{h}.

Remark 4.1
.

It might not be practical to calculate
l^{h}(t;\cdot)
for an arbitrary pair
(f,g)\in L^{2}_{H}\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}}
as it would have to be calculated via numerical integration. We will assume for the rest of the paper that it is possible to calculate these integrals exactly. See
[
15
]
for numerical integration on curved domains.

This formulation can be rearranged to a more useful form via the transport theorem with respect to the form
m^{h}(t;\cdot,\cdot)
:

(4.2)

\frac{d}{dt}m^{h}(t;U^{h},\zeta^{h})-m^{h}(t;U^{h},\partial^{h}_{t}\zeta^{h})+a^{h}(t;U^{h},\zeta^{h})=l^{h}(t;\zeta^{h}),

for
\zeta^{h}\in C^{1}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}}
. Moreover, by construction of the
l^{h}(t;\cdot)
term, for a function
\eta^{h}\in H^{h}(t)
:

\displaystyle l(t;\eta^{h,l})=l^{h}(t;\eta^{h}).

4.2.
Well posedness of the finite element scheme

Theorem 4.2
.

There exists a unique solution to
eq.

4.1
with continuous bound:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in I}\|U^{h}\|^{2}_{H^{h}(t)}+\int_{0}^{T}\|U^{h}\|^{2}_{V^{h}(t)}\leq C(T)\bigg(\|U^{h}_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{h}(0)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}_{H}}+\|g\|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}^{*}}}\bigg).

Proof.

Substituting the Ansatz:

\displaystyle U^{h}(t;x)=\sum_{j=1}^{\text{dim}(\mathcal{S}_{h})}\alpha_{j}(t)\chi^{h}_{j}(t;x),

where
\{\chi^{h}_{j}(t;x)\}^{\text{dim}(\mathcal{S}_{h})}_{j=1}
are the basis functions of the evolving solution space
L^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}}
. We refer to
[
23
, Lem. 3.1]
for proof of the
transport property
:

\displaystyle\partial^{h}_{t}\chi^{h}_{j}=0\quad\forall j\in{1,....,N(k)}.

Then the problem can be restated as the finite dimensional problem:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\bigl(\mathbf{M}(t)\mathbf{\alpha}(t)\bigr)+\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{\alpha}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{L}(t)

\displaystyle\mathbf{\alpha}(0)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{\alpha}_{0}

where:

\displaystyle\mathbf{\alpha}(t)=(\alpha_{1}(t),\ldots,\alpha_{N(k)}(t)),\;

\displaystyle[\mathbf{M}(t)]_{j,k}=m^{h}(t;\chi^{h}_{j},\chi^{h}_{k}),\;

\displaystyle[\mathbf{A}(t)]_{j,k}=a^{h}(t;\chi_{j}^{h},\chi_{k}^{h}),\;

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}(t)=(l^{h}(t;\chi^{h}_{1}),....,l^{h}(t;\chi^{h}_{N(k)})).

Note that
\mathbf{M}(t)
is a Gram matrix (and hence invertible). Hence, by use of standard ODE theory (see
[
47
, Sec. 1.6]
), there exists a solution
\alpha(t)\in\mathcal{W}^{1,1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{N(k)})
. The uniform bound and uniqueness follows from testing with
U^{h}
and using the transport theorem.
∎

5.2.
Ritz Projection

We set

(5.7)

a_{\kappa}(t;w,v):=\kappa m(t;w,v)+a(t;w,v)

and observe that

a_{\kappa}(t;v,v)\geq\bigg(\gamma-\frac{\epsilon\|\mathcal{B}_{i}-\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{2}\bigg)\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{H(t)}\\
+\bigg(\kappa-\|\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}-\|\mathcal{C}_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}-\frac{\|\mathcal{B}_{i}-\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{2\epsilon}\bigg)\|v\|^{2}_{H(t)},

where
\gamma
is a lower bound for the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{A}_{i}

eq.

2.10
.
Thus taking
\epsilon
sufficiently small and
\kappa
sufficiently large, we may choose
\kappa
depending only on the data to ensure that the bounded bilinear form
a_{\kappa}(t;\cdot,\cdot)
is strictly coercive.

Similarly, we define

a^{h}_{\kappa}(t;w_{h},v_{h}):=\kappa m^{h}(t;w_{h},v_{h})+a^{h}(t;w_{h},v_{h}),

which is also coercive provided
\kappa
is large enough, independently of
h
, by the same argument.

The
Ritz projection

\Pi^{h}\colon V(t)\to\mathcal{S}^{h}(t)
is defined as the solution to:

(5.8)

\displaystyle a^{h}_{\kappa}(t;\Pi^{h}(\eta),v^{h})=a_{\kappa}(t;\eta,v^{h,l})\quad\forall v^{h}\in\mathcal{S}^{h}(t),

and
\pi^{h}\eta:=(\Pi^{h}\eta)^{l}
. By the coercivity and boundedness of
a^{h}_{\kappa}
, this gives us a uniformly bounded and linear operator
\Pi^{h}\colon V(t)\to\mathcal{S}^{h}(t)
. Moreover, it is further proven in
[
23
, Lem. 3.9]
, that
\Pi^{h}\eta\in C^{1}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}}
if
\eta\in C^{1}_{V}
and it follows by use of the same method that
\Pi^{h}\eta\in C^{0}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}}
if
\eta\in C^{0}_{V}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

The Ritz projection can be extended as a continuous linear operator
\Pi^{h}(\cdot):L^{2}_{V}\to L^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}}
. Moreover, if
\eta\in W(V,V)
, then
\Pi^{h}(\eta)\in W(\mathcal{S}_{h},\mathcal{S}_{h})=\{v^{h}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}},\;\partial_{t}^{h}v^{h}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}}\}
. In particular,
\Pi^{h}(\cdot):W(V,V)\to W(\mathcal{S}_{h},\mathcal{S}_{h})
is linear and bounded uniformly in
h
.

Proof.

Integrating
eq.

5.8
, we see, that for all
v^{h}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}}
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}a^{h}_{\kappa}(t;\Pi^{h}(\eta),v^{h})\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}a_{\kappa}(t;\eta,v^{h,l})\,dt.

By point-wise coercivity in time of
a_{\kappa}^{h}
, we also get the coercivity over
L^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}}
. Since
L^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}}
is a closed subspace of a Hilbert space, and
\int_{0}^{T}a_{\kappa}(t;\eta,(\cdot)^{l})\,dt
defines a bounded linear functional on
L^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}}
, via the standard use of Lax-Milgram, there exists a unique solution, labelled
\Pi^{h}(\eta)
and we achieve the bound:

(5.9)

\displaystyle\|\Pi^{h}(\eta)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}}}\leq\sup_{v^{h}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}},\,\|v^{h}\|=1}\int_{0}^{T}a_{\kappa}(t;\eta,v^{h,l})\,dt\leq c\|\eta\|_{L^{2}_{V}}.

Hence
\Pi^{h}(\cdot)
is continuous. Note that the bound in
eq.

5.9
can be taken to be independent of
h
, this is due to the fact that both the bilinear form
a_{\kappa}
and the lift map
(\cdot)^{l}
are both bounded independently of
h
.

To show the second claim, assume
\eta\in W(V,V)
and set
\zeta
as the solution to:

(5.10)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}a^{h}_{\kappa}(t;\zeta,v^{h})\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}a_{\kappa}(t;\partial_{t}^{l}\eta,v^{h,l})-b^{h}_{\kappa}(t;\Pi^{h}\eta,,v^{h})+b_{\kappa}^{l}(t;\eta,v^{h,l})\,dt,\;\forall v^{h}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}}.

The via the same argument as before, there exists a unique
\zeta\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}}
solving
eq.

5.10
with bound:

(5.11)

\displaystyle\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}}}\leq c\|\eta\|_{W(V,V)},

and similarly as before, the bound in equation
eq.

5.11
is independent of
h
. Since
\eta\in W(V,V)
, it is also in
C_{V}^{0}
by
lemma

2.7
,
\eta\in C_{V}^{0}
and therefore
\Pi(\eta)\in C^{0}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}}
.

Define:

\displaystyle w^{h}:=\phi^{h}_{t}\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{h}_{-s}\zeta(s)\,ds+\phi^{h}_{t}\Pi^{h}(\eta)(0).

Via the isomorphism lemma (
lemma

2.2
),
\phi^{h}_{-s}\zeta(s)\in L^{2}(I;\mathcal{S}^{h}(0))
, the standard Bochner space and therefore is Bochner integrable. Since
\mathcal{S}^{h}(0)
is a closed linear subspace, the definite Bochner integral of a function inside
\mathcal{S}^{h}(0)
remains in
\mathcal{S}^{h}(0)
for all
t\in I
. Using the isomorphism again, we see that
w^{h}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}}
.
We will show that
w^{h}=\Pi^{h}\eta
which will show the second claim.
Indeed,
w^{h}\in W(\mathcal{S}^{h},\mathcal{S}^{h})
with
\partial_{t}^{h}w^{h}=\zeta
. Substituting this back into
eq.

5.10
:

(5.12)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}a^{h}_{\kappa}(t;\partial_{t}^{h}w,v^{h})\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}a_{\kappa}(t;\partial_{t}^{l}\eta,v^{h,l})-b^{h}_{\kappa}(t;\Pi^{h}\eta,,v^{h})+b_{\kappa}^{l}(t;\eta,v^{h,l})\,dt,\;\forall v^{h}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}},

using the definition of
b^{h}_{\kappa}
, we see that, for
v^{h}\in\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{S}^{h},\mathcal{S}^{h})
with
v^{h}(0)=v^{h}(T)=0
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}a^{h}_{\kappa}(t;\partial_{t}^{h}w^{h},v^{h})\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}-b^{h}_{\kappa}(t;w^{h},v^{h})-a^{h}_{\kappa}(t;w^{h},\partial^{h}_{t}v^{h})\,dt,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}a_{\kappa}(t;\partial_{t}^{l}\eta,v^{h,l})\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}-b^{l}_{\kappa}(t;\eta,v^{h,l})-a_{\kappa}(t;\eta,[\partial_{t}^{h}v^{h}]^{l})\,dt,

using the commutation properties of the material derivatives
\partial^{h}_{t}
and
\partial_{t}^{l}
(see
eq.

3.8
). Substituting these expression back into
eq.

5.12
:

(5.13)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}a_{\kappa}(t;\eta,[\partial_{t}^{h}v^{h}]^{l})-a^{h}_{\kappa}(t;w^{h},\partial_{t}^{h}v^{h})\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}b^{h}_{\kappa}(t;w^{h}-\Pi^{h}\eta,v^{h})\,dt.

Using the definition or the Ritz projection
eq.

5.8
, we arrive at:

(5.14)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}a^{h}_{\kappa}(t;\Pi^{h}\eta-w^{h},\partial_{t}^{h}v^{h})\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}b^{h}_{\kappa}(t;w^{h}-\Pi^{h}\eta,v^{h})\,dt.

Testing this equation with
v^{h}(t)=\varphi(t)\psi^{h}(t)
with
\varphi(t)\in\mathcal{D}(I)
and
\psi^{h}(t)\in W(\mathcal{S}^{h},\mathcal{S}^{h})
, we see:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(t)a^{h}_{\kappa}(t;\Pi^{h}\eta-w^{h},\partial_{t}^{h}\psi^{h})+\varphi^{\prime}(t)a^{h}_{\kappa}(t;\Pi^{h}\eta-w^{h},\psi^{h})\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(t)b^{h}_{\kappa}(t;w^{h}-\Pi^{h}\eta,\psi^{h})\,dt.

Then, via the fundamental lemma of variational calculus, see
[
34
, Thm. 1.2.1 and Lem. 1.2.1]
, and the fact that
a^{h}_{\kappa}(t;\Pi^{h}\eta-w^{h},\psi^{h})
is continuous as
\Pi^{h}(\eta)-w^{h},\psi^{h}\in C^{0}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}}
, we get that for all
t\in I
:

(5.15)

\displaystyle a^{h}_{\kappa}(t;\Pi^{h}\eta-w^{h},\psi^{h})=\int_{0}^{t}b^{h}_{\kappa}(s;\Pi^{h}\eta-w^{h},\psi^{h})+a^{h}_{\kappa}(s;\Pi^{h}\eta-w^{h},\partial_{s}^{h}\psi^{h})\,ds

Fix
t\in I
and test with
\psi^{h}(s)=\phi^{h}_{s}\phi^{h}_{-t}(\Pi^{h}\eta-w^{h})
, we see that
\partial_{s}^{h}\psi^{h}(s)=0
and hence, via the coercivity of
a_{k}
and compatibility:

\displaystyle\|\Pi^{h}\eta-w^{h}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}(t)}

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{t}b^{h}_{\kappa}(s;\Pi^{h}\eta-w^{h},\psi^{h})\,ds,

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{t}\|\Pi^{h}\eta-w^{h}\|_{\mathcal{S}^{h}(s)}\|\Pi^{h}\eta-w^{h}\|_{\mathcal{S}^{h}(t)}\,ds.

By use of Young’s inequality:

\displaystyle\|\Pi^{h}\eta-w^{h}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}(t)}

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{t}\|\Pi^{h}\eta-w^{h}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}(s)}\,ds.

This holds for arbitrary point
t\in I
and hence can be repeated to see that this holds for all of
I
. By use of Grönwall’s inequality it must be that:

\displaystyle\|\Pi^{h}\eta-w^{h}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{h}(t)}=0,

and hence
\Pi^{h}\eta=w^{h}
and therefore
\Pi^{h}\eta\in W(\mathcal{S}^{h},\mathcal{S}^{h})
. As for the bound on
\Pi^{h}\eta
, we see that, since
\partial_{t}^{h}w^{h}=\partial_{t}^{h}\Pi^{h}(\eta)=\zeta
, using
eqs.

5.9
and
5.11
we see that
\|\Pi^{h}(\eta)\|_{W(\mathcal{S}^{h},\mathcal{S}^{h})}\leq c\|\eta\|_{W(V,V)}
independently of
h
.
∎

Note that

Define the dual solution operators

Lemma 5.6
.

Assuming additional regularity on the data:

\displaystyle\leq c\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(t)}.

Proof.

Writing

5.16
explicitly, we seek a solution

to:

\kappa

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}_{H}(v)\|_{V(t)}\leq c\|v\|_{H(t)}.

To show the additional regularity, rearranging

\displaystyle a_{\kappa}(t;w,\mathcal{R}_{H}(v))=a_{\kappa}(t;\mathcal{R}_{H}(v),w)+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{i}(t)}[\mathcal{B}_{i}-\mathbf{w}]\cdot([\nabla w_{i}]\mathcal{R}_{H}(v)-[\nabla\mathcal{R}_{H}(v)]w_{i}).

The remaining term of

\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{i}(t)}[\mathcal{B}_{i}-\mathbf{w}]\cdot([\nabla w_{i}]\mathcal{R}_{H}(v)-[\nabla\mathcal{R}_{H}(v)]w_{i})\\

Hence, the solution to

5.16

This solution exists by
2.16
. We seek to show that first

\mathcal{R}_{H}(v)=\mathcal{L}(v)
. By use of Theorem 1 in
[

]

and

, the solution to

5.23
is indeed in
Z_{1}(t)
since the data
(\mathcal{A}_{i},\mathcal{B}_{i},\mathcal{C}_{i})
is regular enough and moreover:

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}(v)\|_{Z_{1}(t)}\leq c(\|v\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(t)}+\|\mathcal{R}_{H}(v)\|_{V(t)})\leq c\|v\|_{H(t)}

To show

5.23

5.22

\displaystyle a_{\kappa}(t;\mathcal{R}_{H}(v)-\mathcal{L}(v),w)=0.

Lemma 5.7

5.6

Proof.

2.11

]

\displaystyle\qquad+|a_{\kappa}(t;w,I^{l}[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}}(\mathcal{T}\eta)]-\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}}(\mathcal{T}\eta))-a^{h}_{\kappa}(t;w^{-l},I^{h}[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}}(\mathcal{T}\eta)^{-l}]-\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}}(\mathcal{T}\eta)^{-l})|,

\displaystyle\leq ch^{k+1}\|w\|_{Z_{1}(t)}\|\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}}(\mathcal{T}\eta)\|_{V(t)}+ch^{k+1}\|w\|_{V(t)}\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}^{*}(t)}+ch^{k+1}\|w\|_{Z_{1}(t)}\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}^{*}(t)}

\displaystyle\leq ch^{k+1}\|w\|_{Z_{1}(t)}\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}^{*}(t)},

using
lemmas

3.10
and
5.8
, the regularity estimate,
eq.

5.27
, and
P1
,
P4
and
’
‣
5.3
.
Hence, we infer that

\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}^{*}(t)}\leq ch\|\eta\|_{V(t)}+ch^{k+1}\|w\|_{Z_{1}(t)}.

Substituting the final inequality here in
eq.

5.26
yields
eq.

5.25
.

Lemma 5.9
.

The error estimates described in
lemma

5.8
also hold for a.e
t\in I
for
w\in W(Z_{k},Z_{k})
.

Proof.

Via
lemma

2.7
,
C^{1}_{Z_{k}}
is dense within
W(Z_{k},Z_{k})
. For a
w\in W(Z_{k},Z_{k})
, take a sequence
w_{\epsilon}\in C^{1}_{Z_{k}}
such that
w_{\epsilon}\to w
in
W(Z_{k},Z_{k})
. This implies
\|w_{\epsilon}\|_{Z_{k}(t)}\to\|w\|_{Z_{k}(t)}
,
\|\partial_{t}^{\bullet}w_{\epsilon}\|_{Z_{k}(t)}\to\|\partial_{t}^{\bullet}w\|_{Z_{k}(t)}
,
\|\pi^{h}w_{\epsilon}\|_{V(t)}\to\|\pi^{h}w\|_{V(t)}
,
\|\partial_{t}^{l}\pi^{h}w_{\epsilon}\|_{V(t)}\to\|\partial_{t}^{l}\pi^{h}w\|_{V(t)}
and
\|\partial_{t}^{h}\Pi^{h}w_{\epsilon}\|_{V^{h}(t)}\to\|\partial_{t}^{h}\Pi^{h}w\|_{V^{h}(t)}
(by continuity of
\Pi^{h}
and
\pi^{l}
, see
remark

5.5
) in
L^{2}(I)
. We will only show the first inequality, but the same method can be used to obtained the two remaining ones. For arbitrary
\delta>0
, there exists an
\epsilon_{*}>0
such that when
\epsilon>\epsilon_{*}
, we have:

\displaystyle\int_{I}\left(\|\partial^{h}_{t}\Pi^{h}(w)\|_{V^{h}(t)}-\|\partial_{t}^{h}\Pi^{h}(w_{\epsilon})\|_{V^{h}(t)}\right)^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{2},

\displaystyle\int_{I}\left(\|\partial^{\bullet}_{t}w\|_{V(t)}-\|\partial^{\bullet}_{t}w_{\epsilon}\|_{V(t)}\right)^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{2},

\displaystyle\int_{I}\left(\|w\|_{V(t)}-\|w_{\epsilon}\|_{V(t)}\right)^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{2}.

We fix an arbitrary
t\in I
and by taking the mean integral in
[t-s,t+s]
,
s>0
(reflecting the functions for
t<0
, i.e
v(t)=v(-t)
), we see:

(5.28)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2s}\int_{t-s}^{t+s}\|\partial_{t}^{h}\Pi^{h}(w)\|_{V^{h}(\tau)}\,d\tau\leq\frac{1}{2s}\int_{t-s}^{t+s}\bigl|\|\partial^{h}_{t}\Pi^{h}(w)\|_{V^{h}(\tau)}-\|\partial_{t}^{h}\Pi^{h}(w_{\epsilon})\|_{V^{h}(\tau)}\bigr|+\|\partial_{t}^{h}\Pi^{h}(w_{\epsilon})\|_{V^{h}(\tau)}\,d\tau.

We note that via Young’s inequality,
eq.

5.28
can be bounded by:

\frac{1}{2s}\int_{t-s}^{t+s}\bigl|\|\partial^{h}_{t}\Pi^{h}(w)\|_{V^{h}(\tau)}-\|\partial_{t}^{h}\Pi^{h}(w_{\epsilon})\|_{V^{h}(\tau)}\bigr|\,d\tau\\
\leq s^{-1/2}\left(\int_{I}\left(\|\partial^{h}_{t}\Pi^{h}(w)\|_{V^{h}(t)}-\|\partial_{t}^{h}\Pi^{h}(w_{\epsilon})\|_{V^{h}(t)}\right)^{2}\,d\tau\right)^{1/2}\leq\delta s^{-1/2}.

Whereas for the second term, we use the bounds given by
lemma

5.8
and Hölder’s inequality once more:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2s}\int_{t-s}^{t+s}\|\partial_{t}^{h}\Pi^{h}(w_{\epsilon})\|_{V^{h}(\tau)}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{2s}\int_{t-s}^{t+s}\|w_{\epsilon}\|_{V(\tau)}+\|\partial_{t}^{\bullet}w_{\epsilon}\|_{V(\tau)}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{2s}\int_{t-s}^{t+s}\|w\|_{V(\tau)}+\|\partial_{t}^{\bullet}w\|_{V(\tau)}+\bigl|\|w_{\epsilon}\|_{V(\tau)}-\|w\|_{V(\tau)}\bigr|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\bigl|\|\partial_{t}^{\bullet}w_{\epsilon}\|_{V(\tau)}-\|\partial_{t}^{\bullet}w\|_{V(\tau)}\bigr|\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{2s}\int_{t-s}^{t+s}\|w\|_{V(\tau)}+\|\partial_{t}^{\bullet}w\|_{V(\tau)}\,d\tau+2\delta.

Combining both of these estimates and equation
eq.

5.28
:

(5.29)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2s}\int_{t-s}^{t+s}\|\partial^{h}_{t}\Pi^{h}(w)\|_{V^{h}(\tau)}\,d\tau\leq\frac{c}{2s}\int_{t-s}^{t+s}\|w\|_{V(\tau)}+\|\partial_{t}^{\bullet}w\|_{V(\tau)}\,d\tau+(2+s^{-1/2})\delta.

Since
\delta
is arbitrary, letting
\delta=o(s^{1/2})
, we see that by the limit as
s\to 0
, via the Lebesgue differentiation theorem, both sides of equation
eq.

5.29
converge to their point-wise values a.e, hence we obtain that for all
t\in I
:

\displaystyle\|\partial^{h}_{t}\Pi^{h}(w)\|_{V^{h}(t)}\leq c(\|w\|_{V(t)}+\|\partial_{t}^{\bullet}w\|_{V(t)})

where the constant
c
is the same as the constant in its equivalent estimate in
lemma

5.8
(and hence independent of both
h
and
t
).
∎

5.3.
Proof of error bound

Proof of
5.1
.

We have an additional right-hand side functional term that is not present in the original proof by
Elliott and Ranner 2021
. This requires a modification of the proof of
[
23
, Thm. 3.11]
.

To begin, we slightly modify the problem. For a test function
v\in W(V,H)
, we can rewrite the weak formulation of our problem as:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}m(t;u,v)-m(t;u,\partial^{\bullet}_{t}v)+a(t;u,v)=l(t;v).

Employing the standard parabolic rescaling
\check{u}=e^{-\kappa t}u
where
\kappa
is chosen as in the definition of the Ritz projection, the problem becomes:

(5.30)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}m(t;\check{u},v)-m(t;\check{u},\partial^{\bullet}_{t}v)+a_{\kappa}(t;\check{u},v)=\underbrace{e^{-\kappa t}l(v)}_{=:\widetilde{l}(v)}.

Performing the same transformation to the discrete analogue: define
\check{U}_{h}=e^{-\kappa t}U_{h}
which satisfies

(5.31)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}m^{h}(t;\check{U}^{h},v^{h})-m^{h}(t;\check{U},\partial^{h}_{t}v^{h})+a^{h}_{\kappa}(t;\check{U}^{h},v^{h})=\underbrace{e^{-\kappa t}l^{h}(v^{h})}_{=:\check{l}^{h}(v^{h})}.

Set
\theta:=\check{U}-\Pi^{h}\check{u}
, then using
eq.

5.30
and using the fact that
l^{h}(t;\cdot)
equals
l(t;(\cdot)^{l})
for functions in
H^{h}(t)
, for arbitrary
v^{h}\in W(\mathcal{S}^{h},\mathcal{S}^{h})
, we arrive at:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}m^{h}(t;\Pi^{h}\check{u},v^{h})+a^{h}_{\kappa}(t;\Pi^{h}\check{u},v^{h})-m^{h}(t;\Pi^{h}\check{u},\partial^{h}_{t}v^{h})-\widetilde{l}^{h}(v^{h}),

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}m^{h}(t;\Pi^{h}\check{u},v^{h})+a_{\kappa}(t;\check{u},v^{h,l})-m^{h}(t;\Pi^{h}\check{u},\partial^{h}_{t}v^{h})-\widetilde{l}^{h}(v^{h}),

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\bigg[m^{h}(t;\Pi^{h}\check{u},v^{h})-m(t;\check{u},v^{h,l})\bigg]-\bigg[m^{h}(t;\Pi^{h}\check{u},\partial^{h}_{t}v^{h})-m(t;\check{u},\partial^{\bullet}_{t}v^{h,l})\bigg],

\displaystyle=m^{h}(t;\partial_{t}^{h}\Pi^{h}\check{u},v^{h})-m(t;\partial^{l}_{t}\check{u},v^{h,l})+\lambda^{h}(t;\Pi^{h}\check{u},v^{h,l})-\lambda^{l}(t;\check{u},v^{h,l})+m(t;\check{u},\partial^{\bullet}_{t}v^{h,l}-\partial^{l}_{t}v^{h,l}).

Now subtracting this equation from
eq.

5.31
, and rearranging yields:

(5.32)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}m^{h}(t;\theta,v^{h})+a^{h}_{\kappa}(t;\theta,v^{h})-m^{h}(t;\theta,\partial^{h}_{t}v^{h})

\displaystyle=-\bigg[m^{h}(t;\partial_{t}^{h}\Pi^{h}\check{u},v^{h})-m(t;\partial^{l}_{t}\pi^{h}\check{u},v^{h,l})+m(t;\partial^{l}_{t}[\pi^{h}\check{u}-\check{u}],v^{h,l})+\lambda^{h}(t;\Pi^{h}\check{u},v^{h,l})-\lambda^{l}(t;\pi^{h}\check{u},v^{h,l})

\displaystyle\qquad+\lambda^{l}(t;[\pi^{h}\check{u}-\check{u}],v^{h,l})+m(t;\check{u},\partial^{\bullet}_{t}v^{h,l}-\partial^{l}_{t}v^{h,l})\bigg]=:-\Xi^{h}(\check{u},v^{h}).

Using the identity
\partial^{l}_{t}(v^{l,h})=(\partial^{h}_{t}v^{h})^{l}
and looking at
\Xi^{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
term by term, we see, for example:

\displaystyle|m^{h}(t;\partial_{t}^{h}\Pi^{h}\check{u},v^{h})-m(t;\partial^{l}_{t}\pi^{h}\check{u},v^{h,l})|

\displaystyle=|m^{h}(t;\partial_{t}^{h}\Pi^{h}\check{u},v^{h})-m(t;(\partial_{t}^{h}\Pi^{h}\check{u})^{l},v^{h,l})+m(t;(\partial_{t}^{h}\Pi^{h}\check{u})^{l}-\partial^{l}_{t}\pi^{h}\check{u},v^{h,l})|,

\displaystyle\leq ch^{k+1}(\|\check{u}\|_{Z_{k}(t)}+\|\partial^{\bullet}_{t}\check{u}\|_{Z_{k}(t)})\|v^{h}\|_{V^{h}(t)},

by
P1
and
lemma

5.9
. Similar rearrangement and the use of
lemma

3.10
with
P2
,
P3
,
P8
yields:

\displaystyle|\Xi^{h}(\check{u},v^{h})|\leq ch^{k+1}(\|\check{u}\|_{Z_{k}(t)}+\|\partial^{\bullet}_{t}\check{u}\|_{Z_{k}(t)})\|v^{h}\|_{V^{h}(t)}.

Using
eq.

5.32
and substituting
v^{h}=\theta
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}m^{h}(t;\theta,\theta)+a^{h}_{\kappa}(t;\theta,\theta)-m^{h}(t;\theta,\partial^{h}_{t}\theta)=\Xi^{h}(\theta,\theta).

Using the transport formula and the bound on
\Xi^{h}
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}m^{h}(t;\theta,\theta)+a^{h}_{\kappa}(t;\theta,\theta)\leq-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{h}(t;\theta,\theta)+ch^{k+1}(\|\check{u}\|_{Z_{k}(t)}+\|\partial^{\bullet}_{t}\check{u}\|_{Z_{k}(t)})\|\theta\|_{V^{h}(t)}.

Integrating over time and using Young’s and Grönwall’s inequality:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in I}\|\theta\|^{2}_{H^{h}(t)}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\theta\|^{2}_{V^{h}(t)}\leq c\|\theta\|^{2}_{H^{h}(0)}+c\int_{0}^{T}\|\theta\|^{2}_{H^{h}(t)}+ch^{2k+2}\int_{0}^{T}(\|\check{u}\|^{2}_{Z_{k}(t)}+\|\partial^{\bullet}_{t}\check{u}\|^{2}_{Z_{k}(t)}).

Finally, using the decomposition:

\displaystyle\check{u}-{\check{U}}^{h,l}=\check{u}-\pi^{h}\check{u}+\pi^{h}\check{u}-{\check{U}}^{h,l}.

Using the previous bound, the fact that the lift is a diffeomorphism and the bound on the Ritz map, we finally obtain:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in I}\|\check{u}-{\check{U}}^{h,l}\|^{2}_{H(t)}+h^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|\check{u}-{\check{U}}^{h,l}\|^{2}_{V(s)}\,ds

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\in I}\|\check{u}-\pi^{h}\check{u}+\theta^{l}\|^{2}_{H(t)}+h^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|\check{u}-\pi^{h}\check{u}+\theta^{l}\|^{2}_{V(s)}\,ds,

\displaystyle\leq c\|u_{0}-u_{0}^{h,l}\|_{H(0)}+h^{2k+2}c(C_{u}).

Undoing the scaling
u=e^{\kappa t}\check{u}
gives us the desired error bound.
∎

6.
Numerical results

All numerical results are computed using the
firedrake
package
[
8
,
9
,
16
,
45
]
.
Simulation code is available in
[
44
]
.
Results are computed on a sequence of meshes generated using
GMSH

[
26
]
rather than successive refinement of a single mesh.

The main challenges in implementing the numerical scheme are:

(1)

Computing the initial geometry:
We start with a piecewise linear geometry given by
GMSH
.
The initial isoparametric domain is computed through an explicit parametrisation applying directly the method from
section

3
efficiently using custom written
C
code.
The evolution of the mesh is carried out simply by moving the initial Lagrange nodes according to the smooth, given velocity field.

(2)

Labelling and tracking different parts of the domain:
Along side the geometry and topology of the mesh we must track labels which say which elements are in domain
\Omega^{h}_{1}(t)
or
\Omega^{h}_{2}(t)
and which facets are on
\Gamma^{h}(t)
. Once this is fixed for the initial domains
\widetilde{\Omega}^{h}_{1}
and
\widetilde{\Omega}^{h}_{2}
, this information is passed between different times.

GMSH
provides physical tags to each element and facets which can be used to identify the different domains.

Efficient and accurate quadrature rules are used to perform element-wise integrals.
Note that system matrices must be reassembled at each time step due to the evolution of the domain.

6.1.
Time discretisation of advection-diffusion problem

We start from the spatial discretisation from
section

4
. We will apply a backward difference formula (BDF) time discretisation of order
q
, see
[
36
]
for more including analysis of a similar surface only problem.
We take a partition of the time interval
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{M}=T
.
For simplicity we assume that each time interval is of the same length:
\tau:=t_{j}-t_{j-1}
for
j=1,2,\ldots,M
.

We use temporal interpolations of each domain at each time step to construct a sequence of triangulations
\mathcal{J}^{h}(t_{j})
each equipped with finite element spaces
\mathcal{S}^{h}(t_{j})
for
j=0,1,\ldots,M
.
We define the discrete velocity
W^{j}\in\mathcal{S}^{h}(t_{j})^{d}
by

(6.1)

W^{j}=\frac{1}{\tau}\sum_{l=0}^{q}\delta_{l}X^{j-l},

where
X^{j}
are the positions of the Lagrange nodes of the triangulation at time
t_{j}
and
\{\delta_{l}\}_{l=0}^{q}
are the backward difference formula weights of order
q
, determined from the relation:

(6.2)

\delta(\zeta)=\sum_{l=0}^{q}\delta_{l}\zeta^{q}=\sum_{l=1}^{q}\frac{1}{l}(1-\zeta)^{l}.

The fully discrete problem is the time discretisation
eq.

4.2
:
Given starting values
U^{0}\in\mathcal{S}^{h}(t_{0})
,
\ldots
,
U^{q-1}\in\mathcal{S}^{h}(t_{q-1})
, and data
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C}
and
l_{h}
, for
j=q,\ldots,M
, we wish to find
U^{j}\in\mathcal{S}^{h}(t_{j})
as the solution of

(6.3)

\frac{1}{\tau}\sum_{l=0}^{q}\delta_{l}m^{h}(t^{j-l};U^{j-l},\chi_{i}^{j-l})+a^{h}(t^{j};U^{j},\chi^{j}))=l_{h}(t^{j};\chi^{i}_{j})\qquad\mbox{ for all basis function }\chi_{i}^{j}\in\mathcal{S}^{h}(t_{j}),

where again
\delta_{l}
are the BDF weights
eq.

6.2
. Note that the first term on the left hand side is computed by summing over
q
different meshes to approximate the time derivative. Let
U^{h}(t_{j}):=U^{j}
and let
e^{h}(t):=u-U^{h,l}(t)
, then we assume that a similar estimate as in
[
36
, Thm. 5.3]
and
[
18
, Thm. 2.4]
holds towards the BDF scheme
eq.

6.3
in supplement to
5.1
:

(6.4)

\displaystyle\|e^{h}(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+h^{2}\tau\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\nabla e^{h}(t_{k})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c(\tau^{2q}+h^{2k+2}).

Remark 6.1
.

We recall that on the space
V(t)
, both the norms
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
and
\|\cdot\|_{H(t)}
are equivalent. Moreover, we expect the methods in
[
36
]
and in
[
18
]
are generalisable in our case in order to prove
eq.

6.4
.

6.2.
Numerical examples of advection-diffusion problem

For
d=2,3
, let
\Omega=[-1,1]^{d}
, for
t\in[0,T]
, we define the evolution of the domain through the flow map
\Phi_{t}
given by:

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}(x)=x+\frac{|x|^{1/3}\prod_{i=1}^{d}(1-x_{i}^{2})}{0.5\prod_{i=1}^{d}(1-4x_{i}^{2}/|x|)}\begin{cases}((\alpha(t)-1)x_{1},(\beta(t)-1)x_{2})&\mbox{ if }d=2,\\
((\alpha(t)-1)x_{1},(\beta(t)-1)x_{2},0)&\mbox{ if }d=3,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\phi(\cdot,t)=\begin{cases}\frac{x_{1}^{2}}{\alpha(t)^{2}}+\frac{x_{2}^{2}}{\beta(t)^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}&\mbox{ if }d=2\\
\frac{x_{1}^{2}}{\alpha(t)^{2}}+\frac{x_{2}^{2}}{\beta(t)^{2}}+x_{3}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}&\mbox{ if }d=3.\end{cases}

Appendix A
Proof of Regularity

In this section, we will show some results on the additional regularity of the smooth solution to
eq.

4.2
.

Lemma A.1

(The Trace Map)
.

There exists a bounded and continuous linear operator
\widetilde{\tau}_{(\cdot)}:L^{2}_{V}\to L^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}}
such that
\widetilde{\tau}_{t}\varphi(t)=\tau_{t}\varphi(t)

\forall\varphi\in C_{V}
, where
\tau_{t}:V(t)\to\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(t)
is the classical trace map.

Proof.

Let
\tau_{t}:V(t)\to\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(t)
be the classical trace map. It is proven in
[
7
]
that the following identity:
\tau_{t}(\phi_{t}w_{0})=\phi_{t}(\tau_{0}w_{0})
holds for all
t\in I
and
w_{0}\in V(0)
. Moreover, there exists a
c
independent of time such that:

\displaystyle\|\tau_{t}(\phi_{t}w_{0})\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(t)}\leq c\|\phi_{t}w_{0}\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(t)}.

Now, formally define
\widetilde{\tau}_{(\cdot)}
as:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\tau}_{(\cdot)}v(\cdot)=\phi_{(\cdot)}\tau_{0}(\phi_{-(\cdot)}v(\cdot)).

Then via
lemma

2.2
,
\phi_{-(\cdot)}v(\cdot)\in L^{2}(I;V(0))
and since
\tau_{0}
can also furthermore be uniquely identified as a linear map
\tau_{0}:L^{2}(I;V(0))\to L^{2}(I;\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(0))
, see
[
32
, Thm. 1.2.4]
, finally the push-forward
\phi_{t}:L^{2}(I;\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(0))\to L^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}}
maps back into the evolving space. Note that this map, by compatibility and it’s time independent bound is also bounded. Finally, if
\varphi\in C_{V}
, then, at time
t\in I
:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\tau}_{t}\varphi(t)=\phi_{t}\tau_{0}(\phi_{-t}\varphi(t))=\tau_{t}\varphi(t).\quad\qed

This allows us to formally identify the following pairing:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle g,v\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(t)}:=\int_{0}^{T}\langle g,\widetilde{\tau}_{t}v\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(t)},\;(g,v)\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\Gamma}}\times L^{2}_{V}.

Lemma A.2
.

Under the assumptions of

⁢
A
4

2.16
, for each
g\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}}
, there exists a unique solution
u_{g}\in L^{2}_{Z_{1}}
to:

(A.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{i}(t)}\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x)\nabla u_{g}\cdot\nabla v=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma(t)}gv\quad\text{for all}\quad v\in L^{2}_{V}.

Proof.

It follows from the regularity assumptions on the flow
\mathbf{\Phi}_{t}
that the pair
(Z_{1}(t),\phi_{t}|_{Z_{1}(t)})|_{t\in I}
is compatible.
For
g\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}}
, we can take a subset
I^{\prime}\subset I
of full measure such that
g(t)\in\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(t)
and
\|g(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(t)}<\infty
, for
t\in I^{\prime}
. Indeed via
lemma

2.2
, we can take the set of Lebesgue points of
\widetilde{g}(\cdot)=\phi_{-(\cdot)}g(\cdot)
to be
I^{\prime}
and push the function forwards,
g(t)=\phi_{t}\widetilde{g}(t)\in\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(t)
for all
t\in I^{\prime}
. Moreover, we see that, for
t\in I^{\prime}
, these remain Lebesgue Points of
\|g(\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(\cdot)}
in
L^{2}(I)
, indeed, via the reverse triangle inequality and compatibility:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\delta}\int_{t-\delta}^{t+\delta}|\|g\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(s)}-\|g\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(t)}|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{2\delta}\int_{t-\delta}^{t+\delta}|\|\widetilde{g}(s)\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(0)}-\|\widetilde{g}(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(0)}|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{2\delta}\int_{t-\delta}^{t+\delta}\|\widetilde{g}(s)-\widetilde{g}(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(0)}^{2}\,ds

Fix
t\in I^{\prime}
, set
u^{t}_{g}\in V(t)
to be the solution to:

(A.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{i}(t)}\mathcal{A}(t;x)\nabla u^{t}_{g}\cdot\nabla v=\int_{\Gamma(t)}gv\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall\penalty\ v\in V(t).

We will drop the distinction between
I^{\prime}
and
I
and just say for almost all
t\in I
, then via the Hilbert triple structure outlined in
section

2.3
,
g(t)\in\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(t)\subset\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(t)\subset\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\Gamma}(t)
. By
[
38
, Thm. 1]
we have that for almost all
t\in I
there exists a unique solution
u^{t}_{g}(\cdot)\in Z_{1}(t)
to
eq.

A.2
.

Set
u_{g}(t;\cdot):=u^{t}_{g}(\cdot)
, we show that this solution is in-fact in
L^{2}_{Z_{1}}
. We will proceed as follows:

(1)

First show that
u_{g}\in L^{2}_{V}
. By
[
5
, Lem. 2.14]
, it suffices to show first that
t\to(u_{g},w)_{V(t)}
is measurable for all
w\in L^{2}_{V}
and then that
\|u_{g}\|_{L^{2}_{V}}<\infty
.

(2)

We then reuse this method, showing that
t\to(u_{g},w)_{Z_{1}(t)}
is measurable for all
w\in L^{2}_{Z_{1}}
and
\|u_{g}\|_{L^{2}_{Z_{1}}}<\infty
, and hence
u_{g}\in L^{2}_{Z_{1}}
.

(3)

Finally, we show that
u_{g}
does indeed solve
eq.

A.1
.

To show the measurability, since the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x)
are bounded from both below and above independent of time, we can induce the equivalent inner product
(u,v)_{\widetilde{V}(t)}:=(\mathcal{A}(t;x)\nabla u,\nabla v)_{H(t)}
. Showing measurability then follows as:

\displaystyle(u_{g},v)_{\widetilde{V}(t)}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{i}(t)}\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x)\nabla u_{g}\cdot\nabla v=\int_{\Gamma(t)}gv=\int_{\Gamma(t)}\langle g,v\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(t)},

and since
(v,g)\in L^{2}_{V}\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}^{*}}
, by
[
5
, Lem. 2.14]
, the map
t\mapsto\langle g,v\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(t)}
is measurable and hence so is
(u_{g},v)_{\widetilde{V}(t)}
. For the uniform bound, testing the differential equation
eq.

A.2
with
v=u_{g}
and integrating in time, we have:

\displaystyle\|u_{g}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{V}}\leq C(\gamma)\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}^{*}}},

via Young’s and Poincaré’s inequalities, so
u_{g}\in L^{2}_{V}
. Before moving on, note that for any fixed
t\in I
, since
u_{g}(t;\cdot)\in Z_{1}(t)
, we can integrate by parts
eq.

A.2
, obtaining:

(A.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma(t)}\bigg\llbracket\mathcal{A}(t;x)\nabla u\cdot\nu_{\Gamma}\bigg\rrbracket v-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{i}(t)}\nabla\cdot(\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x)\nabla u_{g})v=\int_{\Gamma(t)}gv,

for
v\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
. For
v\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega_{1}(t))\times C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega_{2}(t))
, we see that
eq.

A.3
yields:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{i}(t)}\nabla\cdot(\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x)\nabla u_{g})v=0.

Since the space
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega_{1}(t))\times C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega_{2}(t))
is dense in
H(t)
, we get that:

(A.4)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot(\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x)\nabla u_{g})=0\;\text{a.e}.

By the Poincaré’s inequality, we can endow
Z_{1}(t)
with a more convenient equivalent inner product:

\displaystyle(v,w)_{Z_{1}(t)}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{i}(t)}\Delta v\cdot\Delta w+\nabla v\cdot\nabla w.

Since
\mathcal{A}(t;x)
is assumed to be differentiable, we introduce the equivalent inner product on
Z_{1}(t)
:

(\eta,v)_{\widetilde{Z}_{1}(t)}:=\\
\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{i}(t)}\nabla\cdot(\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x)\nabla\eta_{i})\nabla\cdot(\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x)\nabla v_{i})+(\nabla\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x))\cdot\nabla\eta_{i}(\nabla\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x))\cdot\nabla v_{i}+\nabla\eta_{i}\cdot\nabla v_{i}.

We will first show the following statement, let
\nabla^{2}
be the Hessian, then:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{i}(t)}|\mathcal{A}_{i}\nabla^{2}\eta_{i}|^{2}\geq\gamma\int_{\Omega_{i}(t)}|\Delta\eta_{i}|^{2},

where
\gamma>0
is the coercivity constant in
2.16
. For fixed
t\in I
, since
\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x)
is non-singular and symmetric, there exists orthogonal matrices
\mathcal{P}_{i}(t;x)
,
\mathcal{P}_{i}^{T}(t;x)
and diagonal matrix
\mathcal{D}_{i}(t;x)=[\lambda_{k}(t;x)\delta_{k,l}]_{k,l=1}^{n}
, the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x)
(note that eigenvalues are continuous for a continuous matrix) such that
\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x)=\mathcal{P}_{i}(t;x)\mathcal{D}_{i}(t;x)\mathcal{P}^{T}_{i}(t;x)
. Doing a change of coordinates,
x=\mathcal{P}_{i}y
and letting
\tilde{\eta}_{i}(x)=\eta_{i}(\mathcal{P}_{i}(x))
, we see:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x)\nabla^{2}(\eta_{i}(x))=\mathcal{D}_{i}(t;x)\Delta\tilde{\eta}_{i}(y)|_{y=\mathcal{P}_{i}^{T}x}.

Hence, since
\mathcal{P}
is orthogonal:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{i}(t)}|\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x)\nabla^{2}(\eta_{i}(x))|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{P}_{i}^{T}(\Omega_{i}(t))}|\mathcal{D}_{i}(t;\mathcal{P}_{i}(y))\Delta\tilde{\eta}_{i}(y)|^{2}\,dy,

\displaystyle\geq\min_{x\in\Omega_{i}(t)}\min_{k\in[1,n]}\lambda^{2}_{k}(t;\mathcal{P}_{i}^{T}(x))\int_{\mathcal{P}_{i}^{T}(\Omega_{i}(t))}|\Delta\tilde{\eta}_{i}(y)|^{2}\,dy,

\displaystyle\geq\gamma^{2}\int_{\mathcal{P}^{T}(\Omega_{i}(t))}|\Delta\tilde{\eta}_{i}(y)|^{2}\,dy=\gamma^{2}\int_{\Omega_{i}(t)}|\Delta\eta_{i}(x)|^{2}\,dx.

To show the equivalence of inner products
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\widetilde{Z}_{1}(t)}
and
(\cdot,\cdot)_{Z_{1}(t)}
, note:

\displaystyle\|\eta\|^{2}_{\widetilde{Z}_{1}(t)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{i}(t)}|\nabla\cdot(\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x)\nabla\eta_{i})|^{2}+|(\nabla\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x))\cdot\nabla\eta_{i}|^{2}+|\nabla\eta_{i}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{i}(t)}|\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x)\nabla^{2}\eta_{i}|^{2}+2\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x)\nabla^{2}\eta_{i}(\nabla\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x))\cdot\nabla\eta_{i}+2|(\nabla\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x))\cdot\nabla\eta_{i}|^{2}+|\nabla\eta_{i}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{i}(t)}\tfrac{1}{2}|\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x)\nabla^{2}\eta_{i}|^{2}+|\nabla\eta_{i}|^{2}\geq\min\left\{\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2},1\right\}\|\eta\|^{2}_{Z_{1}(t)}

and:

\displaystyle\|\eta\|^{2}_{\widetilde{Z}_{1}(t)}\leq c\left[|\mathcal{A}_{i}|_{C^{1}(\Omega_{i}(t))}\right](\|\nabla^{2}\eta_{i}\|^{2}_{H(t)}+\|\nabla\eta_{i}\|^{2}_{H(t)})\leq c\|\eta\|^{2}_{Z_{1}(t)}.

Hence,
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\tilde{Z}_{1}(t)}
and
(\cdot,\cdot)_{Z(t)}
are equivalent. Substituting
\eta=u_{g}
in the new inner product, using
eq.

A.4
, we arrive at:

\displaystyle(u_{g},v)_{\widetilde{Z}_{1}(t)}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{i}(t)}(\nabla\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x))\cdot\nabla u_{i}(\nabla\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x))\cdot\nabla v_{i}+\nabla u_{i}\cdot\nabla v_{i}.

Since we already know that
u_{g}\in L^{2}_{V}
, both
((\nabla\mathcal{A}(t;x))\cdot\nabla u,(\nabla\mathcal{A}(t;x))\cdot\nabla v)_{H(t)}
and
(\nabla u,\nabla v)_{H(t)}
are measurable and hence the map
t\to(u_{g},v)_{\widetilde{Z}_{1}(t)}
is measurable for all
v\in Z_{1}(t)
. For the bound,
[
38
, Thm. 1]
gives us a constant
C_{t}
(that depends on time) such that:

\displaystyle\|u_{g}\|_{Z_{1}(t)}\leq C_{t}\|g\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(t)}.

Using a similar method as
lemma

2.12
and changing the variables, it follows that there exists
C_{T}>C_{t}
,
C_{T}<\infty
. Hence:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|u_{g}\|_{Z_{1}(t)}^{2}\leq C_{T}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|g\|_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(t)}^{2}<\infty.

Hence
u_{g}\in L^{2}_{Z_{1}}
.

Finally, we have a solution to
u_{g}\in L^{2}_{Z_{1}}
solving
eq.

A.2
a.e in time. Since a function
v\in L^{2}_{V}
satisfies
v(t)\in V(t)
a.e in time, testing with such a
v(t)
and integrating both sides of
eq.

A.2
yields the desired solution. Uniqueness follows via coercivity.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

Under the assumption

⁢
A
4

from
2.16
, the solution of
eq.

A.1
posses a weak material derivative
\partial_{t}^{\bullet}u_{g}\in L^{2}_{V}
and satisfies the estimate:

\displaystyle\|u_{g}\|^{2}_{W(Z_{1},V)}\leq c\|g\|^{2}_{W(\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma},\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}^{*})}

Proof.

We use the same method as in the proof of
lemma

5.4
. Let:

\displaystyle k(t;v,w):=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{i}(t)}\mathcal{A}_{i}(t;x)\nabla v\cdot\nabla w

then we see, going back to
eq.

A.2
, we see that
u_{g}
solves:

\displaystyle k(t;u_{g},w)=(g,w)_{\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(t)},

a.e for all

(as before, we can take a subset of
I
of full measure such that
w(t)\in V(t)
, moreover, via the classical trace theorem, we can identify

by
(g,w)_{\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(t)}=(g,\tau_{t}w)_{\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(t)}
). The derivative of

,
\dot{k}(t;\cdot,\cdot)
, can be explicitly calculated to be:

(A.5)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}k(t;w,v)

\displaystyle=\dot{k}(t;w,v)+k(t;\partial_{t}^{\bullet}w,v)+k(t;w,\partial_{t}^{\bullet}v),\;\forall v,w\in W(V,V),

where

\displaystyle\dot{k}(t;w,v)=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{i}(t)}\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{A}}_{i}(\mathbf{w},\mathcal{A}_{i},w_{i},v_{i}),

and
\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
was defined in

2.12
. We set
\tilde{u}_{g}
to be the solution to:

(A.6)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}k(t;\tilde{u}_{g},\eta)\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\lambda_{\Gamma}(t;g,\eta)-\dot{k}(t;u_{g},\eta)+\langle\partial_{t}^{\bullet}g,\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(t)}\,dt\quad\forall\eta\in L^{2}_{V}.

The material derivative taken on the function
\partial_{t}^{\bullet}g
is the one with the triple
L^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}}\subset L^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}}\subset L^{2}_{V^{*}_{\Gamma}}
and the bilinear form
\lambda_{\Gamma}
is the corresponding form from
definition

2.3
satisfing the equation:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(v,w)_{\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(t)}=\langle\partial_{t}^{\bullet}v,w\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(t)}+\langle\partial_{t}^{\bullet}w,v\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}(t)}+\lambda_{\Gamma}(t;v,w).

See
[
6
, Sec. 5.4]
for an explicit form of
\lambda_{\Gamma}(t;\cdot,\cdot)
. Using the same method as the proof of
lemma

A.2
, we have that if
\partial^{\bullet}_{t}g\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}^{*}}
, there exists a unique
\widetilde{u}_{g}\in L^{2}_{V}
solving equation
eq.

A.6
.

Let
u_{g}
be the solution of
eq.

A.2
. Via isomorphism,
\phi_{-(\cdot)}\tilde{u}_{g}(\cdot),\phi_{-(\cdot)}u_{g}(\cdot)\in L^{2}(I;V(0))
, we pick a Lebesgue point
s_{*}\in I
of
\phi_{-(\cdot)}u_{g}(\cdot)
and set

\displaystyle w:=\phi_{t}\int_{s_{*}}^{t}\phi_{-\tau}\widetilde{u}_{g}(\tau)\,d\tau+\phi_{t}\phi_{-s_{*}}u_{g}(s_{*})=\phi_{t}\int_{s_{*}}^{t}\phi_{-\tau}\widetilde{u}_{g}(\tau)\,d\tau+z_{s_{*}}.

Thus
w\in W(V,V)
and
\partial_{t}^{\bullet}w=\tilde{u}_{g}
. We aim to show that
w=u_{g}
. To do so, note that by definition of
\dot{k}(t;\cdot,\cdot)

eq.

A.5
, testing with
\eta\in W(V,V)
with
\eta(0)=\eta(T)=0
:

(A.7)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}k(t;\partial_{t}^{\bullet}w,\eta)\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}-\dot{k}(t;w,\eta)-k(t;w,\partial_{t}^{\bullet}\eta)\,dt.

we see from comparing
eqs.

A.7
and
A.6
:

(A.8)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}k(t;w,\partial_{t}^{\bullet}\eta)\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\dot{k}(t;w-u_{g},\eta)-(\partial_{t}^{\bullet}\eta,g)_{\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(t)}\,dt,\quad\forall\eta\in W(V,V),\;\eta(0)=\eta(T)=0.

Comparing
eq.

A.8
with
eq.

A.2
, we infer:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}k(t;u_{g}-w,\partial_{t}^{\bullet}\eta)\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\dot{k}(t;w-u_{g},\eta)\,dt.

Letting
\eta(t)=\psi(t)v(t)
where
\psi(t)\in\mathcal{D}(I)
and
v(t)\in W(V,V)
, we see:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\psi(t)k(t;u_{g}-w,\partial_{t}^{\bullet}v)+\psi^{\prime}(t)k(t;u_{g}-w,v)\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\psi(t)\dot{k}(t;w-u_{g},v)\,dt.

Since this holds for arbitrary
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(I)
, then, by use of
[
34
, Lem. 1.2.1]
, there exists some
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that:

(A.9)

\displaystyle k(t;u_{g}-w,v)=\int_{s_{*}}^{t}\dot{k}(\tau;w-u_{g},v)+k(\tau;w-u_{g},\partial_{t}^{\bullet}v)\,d\tau+c,

a.e in time. Note that the right hand side of
eq.

A.9
is absolutely continuous in time and hence is the unique continuous representative of
k(t;u_{g}-w,v)
(since
k(\cdot;u_{g}-w,v)
is in
L^{1}(I)
as a function of time). It also follows from the fact that
k(t;\eta,v)
is continuous for
\eta,v\in C^{0}_{V}
that
s_{*}
is also a Lebesgue point of
k(t;u_{g}-w,v)
(by use of a standard density argument). Since the continuous representative equals its
L^{p}
counterpart on Lebesgue point (as Lebesgue points are also points of approximate continuity, see
[
24
, Sec. 1.7]
),
evaluating both sides of
eq.

A.9
at
t=s_{*}
, using the definition of

, yields
c=0
. Finally we can test with
\eta(s)=\phi_{s}\phi_{-t}(u_{g}-w)
and by use of the same argument as

we see that
u_{g}=w
and hence
u_{g}\in W(Z_{1},V)
.
∎

Lemma A.4
.

to

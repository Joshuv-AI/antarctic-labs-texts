τ \tau -tilting theory and silting theory of skew group algebra extensions
Yuta Kimura, Ryotaro Koshio, Yuta Kozakai, Hiroyuki Minamoto, Yuya Mizuno

Abstract
. Let Λ \Lambda be a finite dimensional algebra with an action by a finite group G G and A := Λ ∗ G A:=\Lambda*G the skew group algebra. One of our main results asserts that the canonical restriction-induction adjoint pair of the skew group algebra extension Λ ⊂ A \Lambda\subset A induces a poset isomorphism between the poset of G G -stable support τ \tau -tilting modules over Λ \Lambda and that of ( mod ⁡ G \operatorname{mod}G )-stable support τ \tau -tilting modules over A A . We also establish a similar poset isomorphism of posets of appropriate classes of silting complexes over Λ \Lambda and A A . These two results generalize and unify preceding results by Zhang-Huang, Breaz-Marcus-Modoi and the second and the third authors. Moreover, we give a practical condition under which τ \tau -tilting finiteness and silting discreteness of Λ \Lambda are inherited to those of A A . As applications we study τ \tau -tilting theory and silting theory of the (generalized) preprojective algebras and the folded mesh algebras. Among other things, we determine the posets of support τ \tau -tilting modules and of silting complexes over preprojective algebra Π ⁡ ( 𝕃 n ) \Pi({\mathbb{L}}_{n}) of type 𝕃 n {\mathbb{L}}_{n} .

The following lemma about the Jacobson radicals seems to be well-known.

Lemma 2.23

Let

(2)

For a rigid object

4.2.
The bijection

The aim of this section is to
prove that the induction-restriction adjoint pair
(F,R)
induces a bijection between
G
-stable silting objects and (
\operatorname{mod}G
)-stable silting complexes,
whose restriction maps give bijections of the sets of
2
-term silting complexes having appropriate stabilities, and the set of tilting objects having appropriate stabilities.
For this purpose, first we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

(1)

If
S\in{\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{b}}(\Lambda\operatorname{proj})
is a
G
-stable tilting (resp. silting,
2
-term silting) object of
{\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{b}}(\Lambda\operatorname{proj})
,
then

F(S)\in{\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{b}}(A\operatorname{proj})
is a tilting (resp. silting,
2
-term silting) object of
{\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{b}}(A\operatorname{proj})
.

(2)

If
T\in{\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{b}}(A\operatorname{proj})
is a
(
\operatorname{mod}G
)
-stable tilting (resp. silting,
2
-term silting) object of
{\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{b}}(A\operatorname{proj})
,
then

R(T)\in{\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{b}}(\Lambda\operatorname{proj})
is a tilting (resp. silting,
2
-term silting) object of
{\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{b}}(\Lambda\operatorname{proj})
.

Proof.

(1)
Since
\Lambda\in{\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{b}}(\Lambda\operatorname{proj})=\mathop{\mathsf{thick}}\nolimits S
, we have
A=F(\Lambda)\in\mathop{\mathsf{thick}}\nolimits F(S)
.
It follows that
\mathop{\mathsf{thick}}\nolimits F(S)={\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{b}}(A\operatorname{proj})
.

For
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have

\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{b}}(A\operatorname{proj})}(F(S),F(S)[n])\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{b}}(\Lambda\operatorname{proj})}(S,RF(S)[n]).

(4-7)

Since
S
is
G
-stable, we have
RF(S)\in\operatorname{add}S
by (a derived category version of) Corollary
2.14
.
It follows from the assumption that
S
is tilting (resp. silting) that

\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{b}}(A\operatorname{proj})}(F(S),F(S)[n])=0
for
n\neq 0
(resp.
n>0
).
Now we have shown that if
S
is a tilting (resp. silting) object, then so is
F(S)
.

Finally, since the functor
F
preserves projective modules, it follows that if
S
is a
2
-term silting complex, then so is
F(S)
.

(2) can be proved in a similar argument by using Proposition
4.1

and the fact that
R(A)
contains
\Lambda
as a direct summand.
∎

Let
\operatorname{silt}\Lambda
be the poset (of isomorphism classes) of basic silting complexes over
\Lambda
.
We denote by
(\operatorname{silt}\Lambda)^{G}
the subposet of
G
-stable basic silting complexes.
We also introduce

\operatorname{2-silt}\Lambda
and
(\operatorname{2-silt}\Lambda)^{G}
(resp.
\operatorname{tilt}\Lambda
and
(\operatorname{tilt}\Lambda)^{G}
) the poset of
2
-term silting complexes
(resp. tilting complexes) and the subposet of
G
-stable ones.

In a similar way, we define
(\operatorname{silt}A)^{\operatorname{mod}G},(\operatorname{2-silt}A)^{\operatorname{mod}G},(\operatorname{tilt}A)^{\operatorname{mod}G}

the poset of (
\operatorname{mod}G
)-stable silting complexes,
2
-term silting complexes and tilting complexes.

We remark that since we are not assuming that our algebras
\Lambda,A
are basic, strictly speaking
the silting complex
\Lambda
(resp.
A
) does not necessarily belong to
\operatorname{silt}\Lambda
(resp.
\operatorname{silt}A
).
However, to simplify the notation, we often denote
\Lambda
and
A
to denote the basic silting complexes
\Lambda^{\mathsf{basic}}

and
A^{\mathsf{basic}}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

The adjoint pair
(F=\operatorname{Ind}_{\Lambda}^{A},R=\operatorname{Res}_{\Lambda}^{A})
induces isomorphisms of posets

F=\operatorname{Ind}_{\Lambda}^{A}:(\operatorname{silt}\Lambda)^{G}\longleftrightarrow(\operatorname{silt}A)^{\operatorname{mod}G}:\operatorname{Res}_{\Lambda}^{A}=R.

Moreover, the restrictions of these maps give isomorphisms

F:(\operatorname{2-silt}\Lambda)^{G}\leftrightarrow(\operatorname{2-silt}A)^{\operatorname{mod}G}:R
and
F:(\operatorname{tilt}\Lambda)^{G}\leftrightarrow(\operatorname{tilt}A)^{\operatorname{mod}G}:R
.

Proof.

Thanks to Lemma
4.2
the map

F:(\operatorname{silt}\Lambda)^{G}\to(\operatorname{silt}A)^{\operatorname{mod}G},S\mapsto F(S)^{\mathsf{basic}}
and

R:(\operatorname{silt}A)^{G}\to(\operatorname{silt}\Lambda)^{G},T\mapsto R(T)^{\mathsf{basic}}
are well defined.
By (a derived category version of) Corollary
2.14
and Proposition
4.1
,
we see that these maps are inverse to each other.
It also follows from Lemma
4.2
that restrictions of these maps
give bijections
F:(\operatorname{2-silt}\Lambda)^{G}\leftrightarrow(\operatorname{2-silt}A)^{\operatorname{mod}G}:R
and
F:(\operatorname{tilt}\Lambda)^{G}\leftrightarrow(\operatorname{tilt}A)^{\operatorname{mod}G}:R
.

Recall that for two silting complexes
S_{1},S_{2}
, the inequality
S_{1}\geq S_{2}
is defined by the
equality
\operatorname{Hom}(S_{1},S_{2}[n])=0\ (\forall n>0)
.
Using adjoint isomorphisms as in (
4-7
) and (a derived category version of) Corollary
2.14
and Proposition
4.1
,
we can check that the above maps are poset morphisms.
∎

4.3.
Correspondence between
2
-term silting complexes and support
\tau
-tilting modules

Recall that taking the
0
-th chomology group
{\textup{H}}^{0}(S)
of a
2
-term silting complex
S
gives a bijection

{\textup{H}}^{0}:\operatorname{2-silt}\Lambda\to\textup{s}\tau\textup{-}\operatorname{tilt}\Lambda
.

Proposition 4.4
.

(1)

The bijection

{\textup{H}}^{0}:\operatorname{2-silt}\Lambda\to\textup{s}\tau\textup{-}\operatorname{tilt}\Lambda
restricts to a bijection

{\textup{H}}^{0}:(\operatorname{2-silt}\Lambda)^{G}\to(\textup{s}\tau\textup{-}\operatorname{tilt}\Lambda)^{G}
.

(2)

The bijection

{\textup{H}}^{0}:\operatorname{2-silt}A\to\textup{s}\tau\textup{-}\operatorname{tilt}A
restricts to a bijection

{\textup{H}}^{0}:(\operatorname{2-silt}A)^{\operatorname{mod}G}\to(\textup{s}\tau\textup{-}\operatorname{tilt}A)^{\operatorname{mod}G}
.

Proof.

(1)
We denote the inverse map of
{\textup{H}}^{0}:\operatorname{2-silt}\Lambda\to\textup{s}\tau\textup{-}\operatorname{tilt}\Lambda
by
{\textup{K}}:\textup{s}\tau\textup{-}\operatorname{tilt}\Lambda\to\operatorname{2-silt}\Lambda
.
It is clear that if
S
is a
G
-stable
2
-term silting complex, then
{\textup{H}}^{0}(S)
is
G
-stable.
Thus, we only have to show that if
M
is a
G
-stable support
\tau
-tilting module, then the
2
-term silting complex
{\textup{K}}(M)
is
G
-stable.

Recall
{\textup{K}}(M)
is constructed in the following way.
Let
P
be a complement projective module of
M
and
P^{-1}\to P^{0}\to M\to 0
a minimal projective presentation.
For simplicity we set
P_{M}:=[P^{-1}\to P^{0}]
.
Then
{\textup{K}}(M):=P_{M}\oplus P[1]
.

By Corollary
2.26
, the complex
RF(P_{M})
gives a minimal projective presentation of
RF(M)
.
Since
\operatorname{add}RF(M)=\operatorname{add}M
, we have
\operatorname{add}RF(P_{M})=\operatorname{add}P_{M}
in
{\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{b}}(\Lambda\operatorname{proj})
.
Next note that
RF(P)
is projective
\Lambda
-module.
Since the pairs
(R,F)
and
(F,R)
are adjoint pairs, we have
\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(RF(P),M)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}(P,RF(M))=0
.
It follows
RF(P)\in\operatorname{add}P
.
On the other hand, we have
P\in\operatorname{add}RF(P)
by Lemma
2.12
.
It follows that
\operatorname{add}RF(P)=\operatorname{add}P
.
Combining above two observations we conclude that
\operatorname{add}RF{\textup{K}}(M)=\operatorname{add}{\textup{K}}(M)
.

(2) is proved in a similar way by using Proposition
2.30
(2) and the fact that the canonical morphism
RF(Q)\to Q
is surjective.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

The equality
\operatorname{add}RF(P)=\operatorname{add}P
in the above proof immediately follows from
Propositions
3.14
and
3.19
.
However, these two propositions rely on Theorem
3.1
.
Therefore, if we use these two propositions in the proof, we can not use Proposition
4.4
to the second proof of the theorem given below.

The second proof of Theorem
3.1
.

(1) Let
M
be a
G
-stable support
\tau
-tilting
\Lambda
-module.
Then
{\textup{H}}^{0}F{\textup{K}}(M)
is a (
\operatorname{mod}G
)-stable support
\tau
-tilting
A
-module
by Theorem
4.3
and Proposition
4.4
.

(\textup{s}\tau\textup{-}\operatorname{tilt}\Lambda)^{G}\xrightarrow{\ {\textup{K}}\ }(\operatorname{2-silt}\Lambda)^{G}\xrightarrow{\ F\ }(\operatorname{2-silt}A)^{\operatorname{mod}G}\xrightarrow{\ {\textup{H}}^{0}\ }(\textup{s}\tau\textup{-}\operatorname{tilt}A)^{\operatorname{mod}G}.

On the other hand, it is straightforward to check that

.
This finishes the proof of (1).
The second statement is proved in a similar way.
∎

4.4.
Silting discreteness

We introduce the notion of
G
-stable silting discreteness.

Definition 4.6
.

An algebra
\Lambda
with a
G
-action is called

G
-stable silting discrete

if for any
n\geq 0
, there are only a finite number of
G
-stable silting complexes
S
over
\Lambda

such that
\Lambda[n]\leq S\leq\Lambda
.
In other words, we have
|(\operatorname{silt}\Lambda)^{G}\cap[\Lambda[n],\Lambda]|<\infty

where

[\Lambda[n],\Lambda]:=\{S\in\operatorname{silt}\Lambda\mid\Lambda[n]\leq S\leq\Lambda\}.

We provide a silting version of Theorem
3.11
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
\Lambda
an algebra with
G
-action that preserves idempotent elements and has local stabilizers.
Assume that
\Lambda
is
G
-stable silting discrete.
Then the following assertions hold.

(1)

A=\Lambda*G
is silting discrete.

(2)

All silting complexes over
A
are
(
\operatorname{mod}G
)
-stable.

(3)

The adjoint pair
(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Lambda}^{A},\operatorname{Res}_{\Lambda}^{A})
induces a bijection

\operatorname{Ind}_{\Lambda}^{A}:(\operatorname{silt}\Lambda)^{G}\longleftrightarrow\operatorname{silt}A:\operatorname{Res}_{\Lambda}^{A}.

Proof.

We can introduce the notion of ind-(
\operatorname{mod}G
)-stability of objects of
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}\Lambda)

as in the same way of Definition
3.12
.
Then we can prove by the same method of Lemma
3.13

that
every irreducible left or irreducible right silting mutation
\mu^{\operatorname{silt},\pm}_{i}(S)
of ind-(
\operatorname{mod}G
)-stable silting complexes over
A

is again ind-(
\operatorname{mod}G
)-stable.
On the other hand, as is mentioned in the proof of Theorem
3.11
, the
A
-module
A^{\mathsf{basic}}
is ind-(
\operatorname{mod}G
)-stable.
Therefore, if we denote by
\mu^{\operatorname{silt},{\mathbb{Z}}}_{\bullet}(A)
the set of all silting complexes over
A
obtained by silting mutations from
A^{\mathsf{basic}}
,
then
\mu^{\operatorname{silt},{\mathbb{Z}}}_{\bullet}(A)\subset(\textup{s}\tau\textup{-}\operatorname{tilt}A)^{\operatorname{mod}G}
.

Thus we only have to show that every silting complex
T
over
A
belongs to
\mu_{\bullet}^{\operatorname{silt},{\mathbb{Z}}}(A)
.
Shifting the cohomological grading, we may assume that
T\in[A[n],A]
for some
n\geq 0
.
Assume that
T\notin\mu_{\bullet}^{\operatorname{silt},{\mathbb{Z}}}(A)
.
Then
there exists an infinite sequence of irreducible left silting mutations starting from
A
that is bounded by
T
and hence by
A[n]
.

A^{\mathsf{basic}}\xrightarrow{\ \mu_{i_{1}}^{\operatorname{silt},-}\ }T_{1}\xrightarrow{\ \mu_{i_{2}}^{\operatorname{silt},-}\ }T_{2}\xrightarrow{\ \mu_{i_{3}}^{\operatorname{silt},-}\ }\cdots>T>A^{\mathsf{basic}}[n].

Thus the set

(\operatorname{silt}A)^{\operatorname{mod}G}\cap[A[n],A]
contains an infinite set
\{A,T_{1},T_{2},\dots\}
.
It follows from Theorem
4.3
that
|(\operatorname{silt}\Lambda)^{G}\cap[\Lambda[n],\Lambda]|=\infty
, a contradiction.
∎

4.5.
The case that
\Lambda
is Frobenius

In this section we assume that
\Lambda
is (finite dimensional) Frobenius.

4.5.1.
Preliminary

We always equip a Frobenius algebra
\Lambda
with a Nakayama automorphism
\nu_{\Lambda}

and a Nakayama form
\langle-,+\rangle_{\Lambda}:\Lambda\times\Lambda\to{\mathbf{k}}
, which now we recall.
A Nakayama automorphism
\nu_{\Lambda}:\Lambda\to\Lambda
is an algebra automorphism
and a Nakayama form
\langle-,+\rangle_{\Lambda}:\Lambda\times\Lambda\to{\mathbf{k}}
is a non-degenerate bilinear form
that satisfies the equations

\langle rs,t\rangle_{\Lambda}=\langle r,st\rangle_{\Lambda}=\langle s,t\nu_{\Lambda}(r)\rangle

for any
r,s,t\in\Lambda
.
We note that a Nakayama automorphism is unique up to inner automorphisms.
We also note that the map
\phi:\Lambda\to{\textup{D}}(\Lambda),\ \phi_{r}(s):=\langle r,s\rangle_{\Lambda}

gives an isomorphism
\phi:\Lambda\to{}_{\nu}{\textup{D}}(\Lambda)
of
\Lambda
-
\Lambda
-bimodules.

Following
[
6
, Definition 11]
,
we say that a group action
G\curvearrowright\Lambda
on a Frobenius algebra
\Lambda

preserves the Nakayama form

if we have
\langle g(r),g(s)\rangle_{\Lambda}=\langle r,s\rangle_{\Lambda}
for all
r,s\in\Lambda
and
g\in G
.

Proposition 4.8

(cf.
[
6
, Proposition 4.4, Corollary 4.5]
)
.

Let
\Lambda
be a Frobenius algebra with an action of
G
that preserves the Nakayama form.
Then the skew group algebra
A=\Lambda*G
is a Frobenius algebra
with a Nakayama automorphism
\nu_{A}
given by

\nu_{A}(r*g):=\nu_{\Lambda}(r)*g\ \ (r\in\Lambda,\ g\in G).

Proof.

For simplicity we write
\nu=\nu_{\Lambda},\ \langle-,+\rangle=\langle-,+\rangle_{\Lambda}
.
First we claim that
\nu g(r)=g\nu(r)
for all
r\in\Lambda
.
Indeed we can verify, as below, that the equation
\langle\nu^{-1}g^{-1}\nu g(r),s\rangle=\langle r,s\rangle
holds for any
s\in\Lambda
:

\begin{split}\langle\nu^{-1}g^{-1}\nu g(r),s\rangle=\langle s,g^{-1}\nu g(r)\rangle=\langle g(s),\nu g(r)\rangle=\langle g(r),g(s)\rangle=\langle r,s\rangle\end{split}

Now it is straightforward to check that the bilinear form

\langle-,+\rangle_{A}:A\times A\to{\mathbf{k}}

given by

\langle r*g,s*h\rangle_{A}:=\delta_{g,h^{-1}}\langle r,g(s)\rangle\ \ (r,s\in\Lambda,\ g,h\in G)

is a Nakayama form whose Nakayama automorphism is
\nu_{A}
.
∎

4.5.2.
Twist by Nakayama automorphism of finite order

Let
\Lambda
be a Frobenius algebra.
Now we assume that
\Lambda
has a Nakayama automorphism
\nu
of order
N<\infty
in the group
\operatorname{Aut}\Lambda
of algebra automorphisms of
\Lambda
.
Let
\langle\nu\rangle\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
be the subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}\Lambda
generated by
\nu
.
We consider the canonical action
\langle\nu\rangle\curvearrowright\Lambda
to be
\nu^{n}\cdot r:=\nu^{n}(r)
for
r\in\Lambda
and
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
.

We denote the posets of
\langle\nu\rangle
-stable objects

(\textup{s}\tau\textup{-}\operatorname{tilt}\Lambda)^{\nu}:=(\textup{s}\tau\textup{-}\operatorname{tilt}\Lambda)^{\langle\nu\rangle},

(\operatorname{silt}\Lambda)^{\nu}:=(\operatorname{silt}\Lambda)^{\langle\nu\rangle}
etc.

We point out the lemma below that follows from
[
3
, Theorem A.4.]
,
[
2
, Propositions 2.5, 2.6]
.

Lemma 4.9
.

We have the following equalities and an isomorphism of the posets

\operatorname{2-tilt}\Lambda=(\operatorname{2-silt}\Lambda)^{\nu}\cong(\textup{s}\tau\textup{-}\operatorname{tilt}\Lambda)^{\nu},\ \operatorname{tilt}\Lambda=(\operatorname{silt}\Lambda)^{\nu}.

The following corollary plays a key role in applications given in the next section.

Corollary 4.10
.

In the above setup, the skew group algebra
A:=\Lambda*\langle\nu\rangle
is symmetric
and we have the following equalities and an isomorphism of posets

\operatorname{2-tilt}A=\operatorname{2-silt}A\cong\textup{s}\tau\textup{-}\operatorname{tilt}A,\ \operatorname{tilt}A=\operatorname{silt}A.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.8
,
the automorphism
\nu_{A}
of
A
given by
\nu_{A}(r*\nu^{m}):=\nu_{\Lambda}(r)*\nu^{m}
is a Nakayama automorphism of
A
.
It is straightforward to check that
\nu_{A}
is the inner automorphism
\operatorname{ad}_{\epsilon}=\epsilon\cdot-\cdot\epsilon^{-1}
associated to the element
\epsilon:=1_{\Lambda}*\nu
.
It follows that
A
is symmetric.
Applying Lemma
4.9
to the algebra
A
, we obtain the desired equalities of posets.
∎

Applying Theorem
3.3
, Theorem
4.3
and Proposition
4.4
,
we obtain the following result.

Theorem 4.11
.

The adjoint pair
(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Lambda}^{A},\operatorname{Res}^{A}_{\Lambda})
induces the following isomorphisms of posets

\begin{split}&\operatorname{2-tilt}\Lambda\cong(\operatorname{2-tilt}A)^{\langle\nu\rangle\operatorname{mod}}\cong(\textup{s}\tau\textup{-}\operatorname{tilt}A)^{\langle\nu\rangle\operatorname{mod}},\ \ \ \operatorname{tilt}\Lambda\cong(\operatorname{silt}A)^{\langle\nu\rangle\operatorname{mod}}.\end{split}

Finally,
applying Theorem
3.11
and Theorem
4.7
, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 4.12
.

Assume that the action
\langle\nu\rangle\curvearrowright\Lambda
preserves idempotent elements and has local stabilizers and that

\Lambda
is tilting discrete.
Then the adjoint pair
(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Lambda}^{A},\operatorname{Res}^{A}_{\Lambda})
induces the following isomorphisms of posets

\begin{split}&\operatorname{2-tilt}\Lambda\cong\operatorname{2-tilt}A\cong\textup{s}\tau\textup{-}\operatorname{tilt}A,\ \ \ \operatorname{tilt}\Lambda\cong\operatorname{silt}A.\end{split}

Appendix B
A formalism by theory of Hopf algebras and tensor categories

In this section,
we discuss a formal aspect of the paper
including (
\operatorname{mod}G
)-action on

-modules given in Definition

by using theory of Hopf algebras and tensor categories.
For unexplained terminology, we refer
[

.

Since the group algebra

has a canonical structure of a Hopf algebra,
the module category
\operatorname{mod}G=\operatorname{mod}{\mathbf{k}}G
has a canonical structure of a symmetric monoidal category

(We remark that the tensor product of the monoidal category

A pair

\tilde{\Lambda}
of

It is well-known that the category

-module objects in

On the other hand,

-module category

It is straightforward to check that under the equivalence

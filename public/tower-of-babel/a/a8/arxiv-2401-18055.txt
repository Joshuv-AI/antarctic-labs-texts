1.
Introduction and statements of the results

Let
M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
and
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
denote the
{\mathbb{C}}
- vector space of modular forms and cusp forms respectively of integral weight
k
for the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(N)
with nebentypus
\chi
. Moreover, if the nebentypus is trivial, we denote the space of modular forms and cusp forms by
M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
and
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
, respectively. A cusp form
f\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
is said to be a Hecke eigenform (newform) if
f
is a common eigenform for all the Hecke operators and the Atkin-Lehner
W
-operators. The space of Hecke eigenforms (newforms) is denoted by
S^{new}_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
. The Fourier coefficients of a Hecke eigenform satisfy the famous Ramanujan-Petersson bound which follows from the work of Deligne
[
5
]
. With these notations, let
f\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
be a Hecke eigenform with the Fourier series expansion at the cusp
\infty
given by:

(1)

f(\tau)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(n)n^{\frac{k-1}{2}}q^{n},

where
q:=e^{2\pi i\tau}
and
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
.
f
is said to be normalised if
\lambda_{f}(1)=1
. The Fourier coefficients
\lambda_{f}(n)
are known as the normalised
n^{th}
-Fourier coefficients of
f
. The normalised Fourier coefficient
\lambda_{f}(n)
is a multiplicative function and satisfies the Hecke relation
[
8
, Eq. (6.83)]
:

(2)

\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)=\sum_{d|\gcd(m,n)}\lambda_{f}\left(\frac{mn}{d^{2}}\right),

for all positive integers
m
and
n
coprime to
N
. From Deligne’s estimate of Fourier coefficients of cusp forms, we have

(3)

|\lambda_{f}(n)|\leq\tau(n)\ll_{\epsilon}n^{\epsilon},

for any arbitrary small
\epsilon>0,
where
\tau(n)
denotes the number of positive divisors of
n
.

The arithmetic behaviour and distribution of values of arithmetical functions is the center of attraction in analytic number theory. The randomness in the behaviour of these arithmetical functions leads to many interesting results. In this context, the arithmetical function
n\mapsto\lambda_{f}(n)
(where
\lambda_{f}(n)
is the
n^{\rm th}
Fourier coefficients of a Hecke eigenform
f
) is one of the interesting host. The problems become more fascinating and harder if one considers the arithmetic behaviour and distribution over the sparse set of positive integers. Iwaniec and Kowalski
[
9
]
studied the distribution of
\{\lambda_{f}(p):p-{\rm prime}\}
and proved that there exist a positive constant
c
such that

\displaystyle{\sum_{p\leq X}}\lambda_{f}(p)\ll_{f}X\exp\left(-c\sqrt{\log X}\right),

which is an analogue of the prime number theorem for Hecke eigenforms. In this regard, a most popularly known problem is studied by Blomer
[
2
]
, where he considers the distribution of the sequence
\{\lambda_{f}(q(n)):n\in\mathbb{N}\},
where
q(x)
is a monic quadratic polynomial. More precisely, he proves that

\displaystyle{\sum_{n\leq X}}\lambda_{f}(q(n))\ll X^{\frac{6}{7}+\epsilon},

for any
\epsilon>0.
For a polynomial with more than one variable, the problem has been studied broadly for many arithmetic functions such as Von Mangoldt function (
n\mapsto\Lambda(n)
) and generalised divisor functions (
n\mapsto\tau_{k}(n)
) etc. In his remarkable work, Acharya
[
1
]
has studied a two variable analog of the sum studied in the work of Blomer
[
2
]
, where he studied the distribution of
\{\lambda_{f}(q(a,b))\}
where
q(x,y)=x^{2}+y^{2}
and obtained an estimate for the summatory function
\displaystyle{\sum_{k,l\in\mathbb{Z}\atop k^{2}+l^{2}\leq X}}\lambda_{f}(q(k,l)).
For details, we refer to
[
1
]
.

In this article, our motivation is to generalize the result of Acharya
[
1
]
by studying the distribution of
\{\lambda_{f}(\mathcal{Q}(\underline{x}))\}
where
\mathcal{Q}
is a primitive integral positive-definite binary quadratic forms of fixed discriminant
D<0
with the class number
h(D)=1.
In previous works, the second author study the higher power moment
\displaystyle{\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{n=\mathcal{Q}(\underline{x})\leq X\atop\gcd(n,N)=1}}\lambda_{f}^{j}(n)
for
j\leq 8
in
[
19

20
]
, and estimates for more general higher power moment has been carried in
[
16
]
. Before stating our result, we fix some notations.

Throughout the paper, Let
\mathcal{Q}(\underline{x})
be a primitive integral positive-definite binary quadratic (reduced) form given by;
\mathcal{Q}(\underline{x})=ax_{1}^{2}+bx_{1}x_{2}+cx_{2}^{2},
where
\underline{x}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,

with
\gcd(a,b,c)=1
and fixed discriminant

. Further, we assume that for such discriminant

, the class number

is
1
. For details, we refer to
[
4

.

We consider the following sum for an upper-bound estimate:

(4)

\begin{split}S(f,\mathcal{Q};X)&:=\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{n=\mathcal{Q}(\underline{x})\leq X\atop\gcd(n,N)=1}\lambda_{f}(n),\end{split}

where
\lambda_{f}(n)
denotes the
n^{\rm th}
Fourier coefficient of a Hecke eigenform

and symbol
\flat
means that the sum runs over all square-free integers.
We also produce the explicit dependency in terms of weight
k
and level

More precisely, we obtain the following estimate.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
be a normalised Hecke eigenform and
\mathcal{Q}
be a reduced form of discriminant
D
with the class number
h(D)=1
. For sufficiently large

and any arbitrary small

we have

\begin{split}S(f,\mathcal{Q};X)&=\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{n=\mathcal{Q}(\underline{x})\leq X\atop\gcd(n,N)=1}\lambda_{f}(n)\ll_{\epsilon}{(Nk^{2}|D|)}^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}X^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}.\\
\end{split}

where the implied constant is absolute.

Remark 1.1
.

If the class number
h(D)>1
, the same estimate (obtained in Theorem
1.1
) holds for the sum

(5)

\begin{split}S(f,D;X)&:=\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=\mathcal{Q}(\underline{x})\leq X\\
~\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{S}_{D},\underline{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\\
\gcd(n,N)=1\end{subarray}}\lambda_{f}(n),\end{split}

where
\mathcal{S}_{D}
denotes the set of in-equivalent primitive integral positive-definite binary quadratic (reduced) forms of fixed discriminant with the class number
h(D)=\#\mathcal{S}_{D}
.

A consequence of the above theorem is a result on the sign change of the Fourier coefficients. Below, we give a brief description of the sign change problem and state our second result. It is well-known that if the Fourier coefficients of a cusp form are real, it changes its sign infinitely often. The sequence of Fourier coefficients of Hecke eigenform always has a sign change in the interval of length
X,
for sufficiently large
X.
The problem of getting first ever sign change of
\lambda_{f}(n),
is quite hard. As mentioned in
[
10
]
, it is a
GL_{2}
-analogue of the
GL_{1}
problem which concerns about finding least quadratic non-residue. The problem is quite hard in the sense that
\lambda_{f}(n)
may take infinitely many values and it can be very small. We also determine the first sign change of the real sequence of Hecke eigenvalues over the integers which are represented by some function
\mathcal{U}
. In particular, if
\mathcal{U}
is an identity function on
\mathbb{N}
or characteristic function on the set of primes, the problem of first sign changes and counting the number of sign changes in short interval
[X,X+h],
for sufficiently large
X
and some
h<X,
has been considered by many authors.
Let
\mathcal{I}
denote the identity function on
\mathbb{N}
and
n_{f,\mathcal{I}}
denotes the first sign change of Fourier coefficients
\lambda_{f}(n)
of Hecke eignforms
f\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N)),
i.e., the least integer among all
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\lambda_{f}(n)<0
and
\gcd(n,N)=1
.
The problem of bounding
n_{f,\mathcal{I}}
in terms of analytic conductor has attracted many mathematicians. Kohnen and Sengupta
[
10
]
have proved that the bound of
n_{f,\mathcal{I}}
is given by

n_{f,\mathcal{I}}\ll kN(\log k)^{A}\exp\left(c_{2}\sqrt{\frac{\log(N+1)}{\log\log(N+2)}}\right),

for some constant
A,c_{2}>0
, using analytic properties of automorphic
L
-functions. Jointly with Iwaniec
[
7
]
, they improved the bound for
n_{f,\mathcal{I}}
and obtained that
n_{f,\mathcal{I}}\ll Q^{29/60},
where
Q
is the analytic conductor, of the
L
-function associated to
f
, given explicitly in terms of weight and level of the form
f.
Kowalski et al
[
11
]
has made an important observation that the Hecke relation (
2
) allows one to replace
\lambda_{f}(n)
by a step function when one wants to find the lower bound of partial sum of
\lambda_{f}(n).
This allowed to improve the exponent, i.e.,
n_{f,\mathcal{I}}\ll Q^{9/20}.
In the article
[
11
]
, they mentioned that the exponent
9/20
is not the limit of the method. This was further improved by Matom
\ddot{\rm a}
ki
[
14
]
to
3/8
by deep analysis of summatory function of
\lambda_{f}(n)
over square-free integers and this is best known bound for
n_{f,\mathcal{I}}
. The current known bound for
n_{f,\mathcal{I}}
is still far from the truth as it is known that
n_{f,\mathcal{I}}\ll(\log kN)^{2}
under generalised Riemann hypothesis.
The problem of getting the first-ever sign change becomes interesting if the sequence of integers is indexed by some function other than the identity function on
\mathbb{N}
. In particular, a result of this sort has been obtained by the first author in joint work with P. Tiwari
[
15
]
, where the sequence of integers is written as a sum of squares, i.e. by considering the function
Q:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
given by
Q({x,y})=x^{2}+y^{2}.
Following the argument of Kohnen and Sengupta
[
10
]
, Pandey and Tiwari
[
15
]
have proved that

n_{f,Q}\leq k^{8}N^{4}\log^{64}(kN)~\max\left(\!\!N^{4}\exp\left(\!\!c{\frac{\log(N+1)}{\log\log(N+2)}}\right)(\log\log(N+2))^{4}\psi^{4}_{k}(N),\log^{120}(kN)\!\!\right),

where
\psi_{k}(N)=\displaystyle{\prod_{p\mid N}\frac{\log(kN)}{\log p}}.

The method of the first author
[
15
]
can be generalised to any primitive integral positive-definite binary quadratic forms of fixed discriminant with the class number
1.
In this work adopting the method of Kowalski et al. and Matomaki, we improve the previous result of the first author
[
15
]
and generalise the result to any primitive integral positive-definite binary quadratic form of fixed discriminant with the class number
1
.

Now, we state our second result concerning the first-ever sign change of Hecke eigenvalues of a normalised Hecke eigenform at the integers represented by
\mathcal{Q}(\underline{x})

n_{f,\mathcal{Q}}
be the least integer among all

In order to obtain our first result, we need to study the following sum:

\begin{split}S(f,\mathcal{Q};X)&=\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{n=\mathcal{Q}(\underline{x})\leq X\atop\gcd(n,N)=1}\lambda_{f}(n).\end{split}

where symbol
\flat
means that the sum runs over all square-free integers. The function

partial sums defined in terms of known arithmetical functions modulo certain weight, i.e.,

(7)

\begin{split}S(f,\mathcal{Q};X)&=\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{n\leq X\atop\gcd(n,N)=1}\lambda_{f}(n)r_{\mathcal{Q}}(n)=w_{D}\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{n\leq X\atop\gcd(n,N)=1}\lambda_{f}(n)r^{*}_{\mathcal{Q}}(n),\end{split}

where

denotes the number of representations of

by the quadratic form
\mathcal{Q}(\underline{x})
,

is size of group of units and

for

associated to binary quadratic form

as follows:

\begin{split}\theta_{\mathcal{Q}}(\tau)&:=\displaystyle{\sum_{\underline{x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}q^{\mathcal{Q}(\underline{x})}}\quad=\quad\displaystyle{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}r_{\mathcal{Q}}(n)q^{n}},\qquad\quad~~~q=e^{2\pi i\tau}.\\
\end{split}

The generating function
\theta_{\mathcal{Q}}(\tau)\in M_{1}(\Gamma_{0}(|D|),\chi_{D})
(see
[
8
, Theorem 10.9]

\chi_{D}
is the Dirichlet character modulo

which is given by Jacobi symbol

We are considering the quadratic forms
\mathcal{Q}(\underline{x})
of discriminant

such that the class number
h(D)
is
1
and there is a well-known formula for

given by
[
8

(8)

r_{\mathcal{Q}}(n)=w_{D}\sum_{d|n}\chi_{D}(d),\ \ {\rm~where~}\ \ \ \ \ w_{D}=\begin{cases}6{\rm~~~~~if~~~~~~}D=-3,\\
4{\rm~~~~~if~~~~~~}D=-4,\\
2{\rm~~~~~if~~~~~~}D<-4.\end{cases}

The formula for

depends only on the discriminant

but not on the choice of

In order to obtain an upper bound for the sum
S(f,\mathcal{Q};X),
We consider the following Dirichlet series:

(9)

\begin{split}L(f,\mathcal{Q};s)&=\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{n\geq 1\atop\gcd(n,N)=1}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)r^{*}_{\mathcal{Q}}(n)}{n^{s}},\end{split}

where

The Dirichlet series

converges absolutely and uniformly for
\Re(s)>1.
To obtain an upper bound for the sum

we first decompose

L

L
-functions associated to a Hecke eigenform. The Hecke

(10)

\begin{split}L(s,f)&=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)}{n^{s}}=\prod_{p\mid N}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{f}(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1}\prod_{p\nmid N}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{f}(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{1}{p^{2s}}\right)^{-1},\qquad\Re(s)>1.\end{split}

The completed Hecke
L
-function is defined as follows:

(11)

\begin{split}\Lambda(s,f):=\left(\frac{\sqrt{N}}{2\pi}\right)^{s}\Gamma(s+k-1)~~L(s,f),\end{split}

which is analytically continued to the whole complex plane and satisfies a nice functional equation
(s\rightarrow 1-s).

For a given Dirichlet character
\chi

the twist of

The twisted Hecke

\end{split}

This also converges absolutely, uniformly and non-vanishing for
\Re(s)>1.
The completed twisted Hecke
L

(13)

\begin{split}\Lambda(s,f\otimes\chi):=\left(\frac{\sqrt{M}}{2\pi}\right)^{s}\Gamma(s+k-1)L(s,f\otimes\chi),\end{split}

which also has analytic continuation to whole

For details, see
[
8

The Dirichlet series

\end{split}

\begin{split}P(s)&=\prod_{p\mid N}\left[\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)\left(1-\frac{\chi_{D}(p)}{p^{s}}\right)\right]^{\eta}\prod_{p\nmid N}\left[\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)^{\eta}\left(1-\frac{\chi_{D}(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{\eta}\left(1+\frac{\eta(1+\chi_{D}(p))}{p^{s}}\right)\right].\\
\\
\end{split}

The last product converges absolutely and uniformly for

Proposition 2.3

Proof of the Proposition

n

m=1.
Now, we substitute the decomposition
L(f,\mathcal{Q};s)=L(s,f)L(s,f\otimes\chi_{D})G(s)
from Lemma
2.1
and by absolute convergence of
G(s)
for
\Re(s)>\frac{1}{2}
, we get

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(1/2+\epsilon)}\tilde{w}(s)L(f,\mathcal{Q};s)ds\\
&\ll\left(\int_{0}^{1}+\int_{1}^{T}\right)\frac{X^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}{|\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon+it|}|L(1/2+\epsilon+it,f)L(1/2+\epsilon+it,f\otimes\chi_{D})|dt+O(X^{-A})\\
&\ll(Nk^{2}|D|X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}+X^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}\int_{1}^{T}\frac{|L(1/2+\epsilon+it,f)L(1/2+\epsilon+it,f\otimes\chi_{D})|}{t}dt+O(X^{-A}).\\
\end{split}

We apply the dyadic division method and then Cauchy-Schwartz inequality to get

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(1/2+\epsilon)}\tilde{w}(s)L(f,\mathcal{Q};s)ds\\
&\ll\begin{cases}(Nk^{2}|D|X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}+O(X^{-A})\\
+~X^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}\underset{1\leq T_{1}\leq T}{\rm max}\!\!\left\{\frac{1}{T_{1}}\!\!\left(\int_{\frac{T_{1}}{2}}^{T_{1}}|L(1/2+\epsilon+it,f)|^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\!\left(\int_{\frac{T_{1}}{2}}^{T_{1}}L(1/2+\epsilon+it,f\otimes\chi_{D})|^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\}\\
\end{cases}\\
&\ll(Nk^{2}|D|X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}+X^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}\underset{1\leq T_{1}\leq T}{\rm max}\left(\frac{1}{T_{1}}(Nk^{2}T_{1}^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}+\epsilon}(Nk^{2}|D|^{2}T_{1}^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}+\epsilon}\right)+O(X^{-A})\\
&\ll(Nk^{2}|D|X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}.\end{split}

The last inequality is obtained by substituting
T=\frac{X^{1+\epsilon}}{Z}
and choosing
Z=X^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}
. Thus

(40)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(1/2+\epsilon)}\tilde{w}(s)L(f,\mathcal{Q};s)ds\ll(Nk^{2}|D|X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}.\\
\end{split}

From equations (
37
), (
38
), (
39
) and (
40
) and choosing
Z=X^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}
,
we have the required result.

Concerning about second result, one needs to obtain a lower estimate for the sum
S(f,\mathcal{Q};Y^{u_{0}})
under the certain assumption. Below, we sketch the proof to get a lower estimate for the sum
S(f,\mathcal{Q};Y^{u_{0}})
.

A lower bound for the sum
S(f,\mathcal{Q};Y^{u_{0}})
:
We recall that multiplicative function
h_{Y}
, supported on square-free positive integers, is given by:

\begin{split}&\qquad\quad h_{Y}(p)=\begin{cases}\alpha\left(\frac{\log p}{\log Y}\right),\qquad{\rm if}\qquad p\leq Y\quad{\rm and}\quad p\nmid N,\\
-2,\qquad\qquad\quad{\rm if}\qquad p>Y\quad{\rm and}\quad p\nmid N,\\
0,\qquad\qquad\qquad{\rm if}\qquad p\mid N,\end{cases}\\
\end{split}

where
\quad\alpha:[0,1]\rightarrow{[-2,2]}
is such that,
\alpha(0)=2
and
\alpha(t)=2\cos(\frac{\pi}{m+1})
if
\frac{1}{m+1}<t\leq\frac{1}{m}
for
m\in\mathbf{N}
.

Assume that
\lambda_{f}(n)\geq 0
for every square-free positive integer
n\leq Y,
which are represented by
\mathcal{Q}(\underline{x}).

Then, Hecke relation together with the assumption
\lambda_{f}(p)\geq 0
for every prime
p\leq Y^{1/m}
implies that
\lambda_{f}(p)\geq 2\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{m+1}\right).
This shows that
\lambda_{f}(p)\geq h_{Y}(p),
for all primes upto
Y.
Multiplicativity of these arithmetical functions shows that
\lambda_{f}(n)\geq h_{Y}(n),
for all
n\leq Y.
Similar to
[
11
]
, it is observed that for
X=Y^{u_{0}}
with
u_{0}=4/3
,

(41)

\begin{split}S(f,\mathcal{Q};X)&=\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{n\leq X\atop\gcd(n,N)=1}\lambda_{f}(n)r^{*}_{\mathcal{Q}}(n)\geq\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{n\leq X\atop\gcd(n,N)=1}h_{Y}(n)r^{*}_{\mathcal{Q}}(n)>0.\end{split}

Precisely, to see (
41
), let
g_{Q}
be a multiplicative function defined by convolution identity
\lambda^{*}_{f}(n)=(g_{Q}*h^{*}_{Y})(n)
,
where
\lambda^{*}_{f}(n)=\lambda_{f}(n)r^{*}_{\mathcal{Q}}(n)
and
h^{*}_{y}(n)=h_{Y}(n)r^{*}_{\mathcal{Q}}(n).
This gives that
g_{Q}(p)=\lambda_{f}(p)r^{*}_{\mathcal{Q}}(p)-h_{Y}(p)r^{*}_{\mathcal{Q}}(p).
If
p
is not represented by
\mathcal{Q}(\underline{x}),
then
g_{Q}(p)=0.
If
p
is represented by
\mathcal{Q}(\underline{x}),
then
g_{Q}(p)\geq 0
for all
p
not dividing
N
and upto
Y
by definition of
h_{Y}(p).
For
p>Y,
with
p\nmid N,
we have
h_{Y}(p)r^{*}_{\mathcal{Q}}(p)=-4
and
-2\leq\lambda_{f}(p)\leq 2.
Hence,
g_{Q}(p)\geq 4a+4\geq 0
as
-1\leq a\leq 1.
Here, we have written the value taken by
\lambda_{f}(p)r^{*}_{\mathcal{Q}}(p)
as
4a
. Thus, we have
g_{Q}(n)\geq 0
for all square-free positive integers
n.
Now, we see that

\begin{split}S(f,\mathcal{Q};X)&=\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{n\leq X\atop\gcd(n,N)=1}\lambda_{f}(n)r^{*}_{\mathcal{Q}}(n)=\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{n\leq X\atop\gcd(n,N)=1}\sum_{d\mid n}g_{Q}(d)h_{Y}(n/d)r^{*}_{\mathcal{Q}}(n/d)\\
&=\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{d\leq X\atop\gcd(d,N)=1}g_{Q}(d)\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{m\leq X/d\atop\gcd(m,N)=1}h_{Y}(m)r^{*}_{\mathcal{Q}}(m)\geq\sum_{n\leq X\atop\gcd(n,N)=1}h_{Y}(n)r^{*}_{\mathcal{Q}}(n)\end{split}

as
g_{Q}(1)=1
and
g_{Q}(n)\geq 0
for each square-free positive integer
n.
Hence, a positive lower bound for the sum
S(f,\mathcal{Q};X)
occurs and it is given by the sum
\displaystyle{\sum_{n\leq X^{u_{0}}\atop\gcd(n,N)=1}h_{Y}(n)r^{*}_{\mathcal{Q}}(n)}
for some
u_{0}<1
given in Proposition
2.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
:
Let
X=Y^{u}
with
u>1
.
By definition,
n_{f,\mathcal{Q}}
be the largest integer among all
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\lambda_{f}(n)\geq 0,
with
\gcd(n,N)=1
and
n=\mathcal{Q}(\underline{x})
for some
\underline{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Let us write
n_{f,\mathcal{Q}}=Y.
In other word,
\lambda_{f}(n)\geq 0,
for each
n\leq Y
with
\gcd(n,N)=1
and
n=\mathcal{Q}(\underline{x})
for some
\underline{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}.
A key idea to estimate
Y
is to compare lower and upper bound for the sum

\begin{split}S(f,\mathcal{Q};Y^{u})&=\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{n=\mathcal{Q}(\underline{x})\leq Y^{u}\atop\gcd(n,N)=1}\lambda_{f}(n)=\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{n\leq Y^{u}\atop\gcd(n,N)=1}\lambda_{f}(n)r_{Q}(n).\\
\end{split}

From Proposition
1.1
and (
33
), we have (for
u\leq u_{0}
)

\begin{split}0<\frac{P(1)L(1,\chi_{D})^{2}}{\Gamma(\alpha_{0})}Y^{u}\log Y^{u}\ll\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{n\leq Y^{u}\atop\gcd(n,N)=1}h_{Y}(n)r^{*}_{\mathcal{Q}}(n)\leq S(f,\mathcal{Q};Y^{u})\ll{(Nk^{2}|D|)}^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}Y^{\frac{u}{2}+\epsilon}.\end{split}

This shows that

\begin{split}Y^{u/2+\epsilon}\ll{(Nk^{2}|D|)}^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}L(1,\chi_{D})^{2}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad Y\ll{(Nk^{2})}^{\frac{1}{u}+\epsilon}|D|^{\frac{2}{u}+\epsilon}\end{split}

Now, we substitute
u=u_{0}=4/3
(as
u_{0}=4/3
such that
\sigma(u_{0})>0
) to get

Y\ll{(Nk^{2}|D|^{2})}^{\frac{3}{4}+\epsilon}.

This completes the proof.

An electromagnetic way to derive basic relativistic transformations
Congjun Wu

Abstract
We derive the relativistic velocity addition law, the transformations of electromagnetic fields and space-time intervals by examining the drift velocities in a crossed electromagnetic field configuration. The postulate of light velocity invariance is not taken as a priori, but is derived as the universal upper limit of drift velocities. The key is that a physical drift motion of either an electric charge or a magnetic charge remains a drift motion by any inertial reference frame transformations. Such a simple fact is incompatible with the Galilean velocity addition. This derivation provides a way to introduce relativity via elementary electromagnetism.

In this article, we provide a new pedagogical way to re-derive

without the Maxwell equations.
Even the simple phenomenon of the drift velocity in a crossed

The postulate of light velocity invariance is not taken for

Simply by examining the transformations of drift velocities

to the Galilean transform.
This would stimulate one to re-examine the space-time
coordinate transformation, which yields the

This is a logically natural way to start learning relativistic
physics.

Appendix A
Lorentz force for a magnetic monopole

We justify the formula of Lorentz force for a magnetic monopole
as shown in Eq.
3
.
Consider a dyon system consisting of a charge
q
and a monopole

g
.
Consider the situation that the monopole
g
is fixed at the
origin and the electron moves around.
The mechanical angular momentum
\mathbf{L}_{M,e}=\mathbf{r}\times m_{e}\mathbf{v}
is not conserved.

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathbf{L}_{M,e}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}\times m_{e}\frac{d\mathbf{v}}{dt}=\mathbf{r}\times\left(\frac{e}{c}\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}\right)

(52)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{eg}{c}\frac{\mathbf{r}}{r^{3}}\times\left(\frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt}\times\mathbf{r}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{eg}{c}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\mathbf{r}}{r}\right).

Hence, the right hand side can be viewed as as the time derivative
of the field contribution to angular momentum,

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{em}=-\frac{eg}{c}\left(\frac{\mathbf{r}}{r}\right).

(53)

In fact, it can be shown that

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{em}=\frac{1}{c}\int d^{3}\mathbf{r}~~\mathbf{r}\times\left(\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{r})\times\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r})\right).

(54)

Such that
\mathbf{L}_{tot}=\mathbf{L}_{M,e}+\mathbf{L}_{em}

is conserved.

Now let us consider the situation that the electric charge is
fixed at the origin while the monopole is moving around.
Again, we need to define the total angular momentum as

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}^{\prime}_{tot}=L^{\prime}_{M,g}+\mathbf{L}^{\prime}_{em}.

(55)

Since
\mathbf{r}
here is defined from the electric charge to
the monopole, which is opposite to the previous case, hence,
the field contribution of the angular momentum is

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}^{\prime}_{em}=\frac{eg}{c}\left(\frac{\mathbf{r}}{r}\right).

(56)

Then the time derivative of the mechanical orbital angular
momentum should satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathbf{L}_{M,g}=\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}_{L,g}=-\frac{d}{dt}\mathbf{L}^{\prime}_{em}=-\frac{eg}{c}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\mathbf{r}}{r}\right).

(57)

Hence, the above result is consistent with Eq.
52

if

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{L,g}=-\frac{eg}{c}\mathbf{v}\times\frac{\mathbf{r}}{r^{3}}.

(58)

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{L,g}=-g\frac{\mathbf{v}}{c}\times\mathbf{E}.

(59)

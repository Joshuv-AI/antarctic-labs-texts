Analysis and Design of Energy-Efficient Bus Encoding Schemes
Lorenzo Valentini, and Marco Chiani

Abstract
In computer system buses, most of the energy is spent to change the voltage of each line from high to low or vice versa. Bus encoding schemes aim to improve energy efficiency by reducing the average number of transitions between successive uses of the bus. We derive in closed form the performance of optimal and suboptimal low-weight line codes designed for this purpose, and propose new algorithms for their implementation. We then show that some low-complexity suboptimal schemes have a small performance loss with respect to the optimal ones. For example, by adding 8 8 lines to a 128 128 lines bus, we save 20.7 % 20.7\% of energy with the optimal scheme and 19.4 % 19.4\% with suitable suboptimal schemes.

II-B

Optimal Code: Minimum Achievable Average Distance

Let us analyze the minimum average distance (
1

Then, the line codewords are obtained by differential encoding as

6
]

We now analyze the performance of this optimal scheme for arbitrary

The probability that a codeword

is at distance

\mathbf{x}_{j-1}

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{opt}}(d)=\begin{cases}\binom{n}{d}\,2^{-k}&\text{if $0\leq d<d_{\mathrm{max}}$}\\
1-\sum_{i=0}^{d_{\mathrm{max}}-1}\binom{n}{i}\,2^{-k}&\text{if $d=d_{\mathrm{max}}$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(4)

where

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{max}}=\min\left\{d\in\mathbb{N}:\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{n}{i}\geq 2^{k}\right\}\,.

(5)

We then obtain the average distance of the optimal

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{opt}}(k,b)=d_{\mathrm{max}}-\sum_{i=0}^{d_{\mathrm{max}}-1}\frac{d_{\mathrm{max}}-i}{2^{k}}\,\binom{n}{i}\,.

(6)

Some optimal code implementations are discussed in the following section.

IV-A

Partitioned Inversion Scheme

In this section we analyze the average distance achieved by the
PI
(
PI
) scheme proposed in
[
7
]
.
The idea behind this scheme is to partition the information sequence of
k
bits into
b
block, and apply
DBI
to each block (Fig.
4
a).
Hence, each added bit indicates if the related partition has been obtained by inversion. Whenever
b
is not a multiple of
k
, we will assume the more uniform way to distribute
k
bits in
b
blocks. For example, with
b=3
and
k=16
the partitions will have lengths
6
,
5
, and
5
.

To derive the average distance for the
PI
scheme we observe that each block adopts
DBI
. Hence, it is simple to see that

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{PI}}(k,b)

\displaystyle=\Big[b-(k~\text{mod}~b)\Big]\,D_{\mathrm{DBI}}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{k}{b}\right\rfloor\right)

\displaystyle+(k~\text{mod}~b)\,D_{\mathrm{DBI}}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{k}{b}\right\rfloor+1\right)

(19)

where
D_{\mathrm{DBI}}(\cdot)
is given by (
8
).

IV-B

Random Scheme

In this section we analyze the
PR
(
PR
) scheme, which uses randomly pre-generated codewords.
More precisely, the encoder and the decoder share a set
\{\mathbf{r}^{(i)}\}_{i=0}^{L-1}
of
L=2^{b}
sequences of length
k
. This is similar to the scheme proposed in
[
28
]
with the difference that, since we assume a memoryless source with
i.i.d.
(
i.i.d.
) information bits, the
\mathbf{r}^{(i)}
are pre-generated randomly and stored by both the encoder and the decoder. Then, similarly to the
DBI
scheme, the encoder constructs temporary sequences and chose the closest to the previous on the bus. More precisely, the
i
-th temporary sequence
\mathbf{x}_{j}^{(i)}
is generated concatenating the binary representation of
i
using
b
bit,
\mathbf{a}^{(i)}
, with the bitwise XOR between
\mathbf{r}^{(i)}
and
\mathbf{u}_{j}
denoted by
\mathbf{u}^{(i)}_{j}

\mathbf{u}^{(i)}_{j}=\mathbf{u}_{j}\oplus\mathbf{r}^{(i)},\qquad\mathbf{x}_{j}^{(i)}=\mathbf{u}^{(i)}_{j}||\,\mathbf{a}^{(i)}\,.

(20)

The transmitted address is chosen as the closest to the previous

\mathbf{x}_{j}=\argmin_{\mathbf{x}_{j}^{(i)}}d_{\mathrm{H}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{j}^{(i)},\mathbf{x}_{j-1}\right)\,.

(21)

Note that (
21
) requires the computation of
2^{b}
Hamming distances. The encoding procedure can be visualized in Fig.
4
b.
The receiver will reconstruct
\mathbf{u}_{j}
by reading the redundancy bits
\mathbf{a}^{(i)}
, and then summing
\mathbf{r}^{(i)}
to the data part of
\mathbf{x}_{j}
.

We now evaluate the performance of this scheme in terms of average distance, averaged over all possible choices of the set
\{\mathbf{r}^{(i)}\}_{i=0}^{L-1}
.
Referring to Fig.
4
b, each temporary sequence is composed by a randomly generated part
\mathbf{u}^{(i)}_{j}
and the index part
\mathbf{a}^{(i)}
.
Let us define

\displaystyle\mathsfbr{Y}^{(w_{i})}=d_{\mathrm{H}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{j}^{(i)},\mathbf{x}_{j-1}\right)=\mathsfbr{D}^{(k)}+w_{i}

(22)

where
\mathsfbr{D}^{(k)}
denotes the distance between
\mathbf{u}^{(i)}_{j}
and the last
k
bits of the sequence
\mathbf{x}_{j-1}
, and
w_{i}
denotes the contribution due to the index part
\mathbf{a}^{(i)}
.
The
\mathsfbr{D}^{(k)}
is a random variable distributed according to (
2
).
About the contribution to the distance of the index part, whatever
\mathbf{x}_{j-1}
is, the
w_{i}
can assume
b+1
values. More precisely, there are
\binom{b}{w}
temporary codewords
\mathbf{x}_{j}^{(i)}
with values of the distance of the index part
w_{i}=w
.

Now, recalling that for a positive random variable
\mathsfbr{X}
it holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hskip-1.07639pt\left\{\mathsfbr{X}\right\}=\sum_{m\geqslant 0}\mathbb{P}\hskip-1.07639pt\left\{\mathsfbr{X}>m\right\}

(23)

and due to the fact that
\mathsfbr{D}^{(k)}
are
i.i.d.
, we can write

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{PR}}(k,b)

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbb{E}\hskip-1.07639pt\left\{\min_{i}\mathsfbr{Y}^{(w_{i})}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{k-1}\prod_{i=0}^{L-1}\mathbb{P}\hskip-1.07639pt\left\{\mathsfbr{D}^{(k)}>m-w_{i}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{k-1}\prod_{w=0}^{b}\Big[\mathbb{P}\hskip-1.07639pt\left\{\mathsfbr{D}^{(k)}>m-w\right\}\Big]^{\binom{b}{w}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{k-1}\prod_{w=0}^{b}\left[\,\sum_{\ell=m-w+1}^{k}\binom{k}{\ell}\,\frac{1}{2^{k}}\right]^{\binom{b}{w}}\,.

(24)

An approximation of (
IV-B
) can be obtained by considering the index parts as random and independent, so that the
L
candidates
\mathbf{x}_{j}^{(i)}
are approximated as composed of
i.i.d.
bits. The distances
d_{\mathrm{H}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{j}^{(i)},\mathbf{x}_{j-1}\right)
are therefore approximated as
i.i.d.
, with distribution given by (
2
) and parameter
n=k+b
. With this approximation we have

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{PR}}(k,b)

\displaystyle\simeq\sum_{m}\prod_{i=0}^{L-1}\mathbb{P}\hskip-1.07639pt\left\{\mathsfbr{D}^{(n)}>m\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\left[\,\sum_{\ell=m+1}^{n}\binom{n}{\ell}\,\frac{1}{2^{n}}\right]^{L}\,.

(25)

IV-C

Random & Inversion Scheme

In this section, we present an improvement of the
PR
scheme. The
RI
(
RI
) scheme proposed in the following replicates the
PR
idea, but with one of the
b
bits dedicated to sequence inversion as done in Section
III-A
.

More precisely, the encoder and the decoder share a set
\{\mathbf{r}^{(i)}\}_{i=0}^{L-1}
of
L=2^{b}
sequences of length
k
, the first half
\mathbf{r}^{(i)}
,
i=0,\ldots,L/2-1
randomly generated, and the other half obtained by their complements,
\mathbf{r}^{(i+L/2)}=\overline{\mathbf{r}}^{(i)}
. Then, using (
20
) and (
21
) we select the codeword with the minimum distance from the previous.

This scheme is expected to perform slightly better than
PR
for small
b
due to the fact that
\mathbf{r}^{(i)}
and its complement are at maximum distance, while for large
b
the randomness of
PR
is sufficient to create a varied set of temporary sequences.
To evaluate the performance in terms of average distance we introduce
L/2
random variables denoted by
\mathsfbr{Y}^{(w_{i})}
,
i=0,\ldots,L/2-1
, each of them accounting for the two candidates
\mathbf{x}_{j}^{(i)}=\left(\mathbf{u}_{j}\oplus\mathbf{r}^{(i)}\right)||\,\mathbf{a}^{(i)}
and
\mathbf{x}_{j}^{(i+L/2)}=\left(\mathbf{u}_{j}\oplus\overline{\mathbf{r}}^{(i)}\right)||\,\mathbf{a}^{(i+L/2)}
. Note that
\mathbf{a}^{(i)}
and
\mathbf{a}^{(i+L/2)}
differ only in the most significant bit.
We then define, for
i=0,\ldots,L/2-1

\displaystyle\mathsfbr{Y}^{(w_{i})}

\displaystyle=\min\left(d_{\mathrm{H}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{j}^{(i)},\mathbf{x}_{j-1}\right),d_{\mathrm{H}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{j}^{(i+L/2)},\mathbf{x}_{j-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\min\left(\mathsfbr{D}^{(k)}+w_{i},k+1-\mathsfbr{D}^{(k)}+w_{i}\right)

(26)

where
\mathsfbr{D}^{(k)}
is the distance contribution of the first
k
bits (information part), and
w_{i}
is the contribution due to the last
b
bits (index part).

From equation (
IV-C
) we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\hskip-1.07639pt\left\{\mathsfbr{Y}^{(w_{i})}=\ell\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\hskip-1.07639pt\left\{\mathsfbr{D}^{(k)}=\ell-w_{i}\right\}+\mathbb{P}\hskip-1.07639pt\left\{\mathsfbr{D}^{(k)}=k+1-\ell+w_{i}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{k}}\left[\binom{k}{\ell-w_{i}}+\binom{k}{\ell-w_{i}-1}\right]=\frac{1}{2^{k}}\binom{k+1}{\ell-w_{i}}

(27)

for
\ell<w_{i}+(k+1)/2
, and

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\hskip-1.07639pt\left\{\mathsfbr{Y}^{(w_{i})}=\ell\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\hskip-1.07639pt\left\{\mathsfbr{D}^{(k)}=\ell-w_{i}\right\}=\frac{1}{2^{k}}\binom{k}{\ell-w_{i}}

(28)

for
\ell=w_{i}+(k+1)/2
.

Considering that there are
\binom{b-1}{w}
indexes
\mathbf{a}^{(i)}
,
i<L/2
, with weight
w_{i}=w
,
and that the
\mathsfbr{Y}^{(w_{i})}
are independent, we can write

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{RI}}(k,b)

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbb{E}\hskip-1.07639pt\left\{\min_{0\leqslant i<L/2}\mathsfbr{Y}^{(w_{i})}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{k-1}\prod_{i=0}^{L/2-1}\mathbb{P}\hskip-1.07639pt\left\{\mathsfbr{Y}^{(w_{i})}>m\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{k-1}\prod_{w=0}^{b-1}\left[\mathbb{P}\hskip-1.07639pt\left\{\mathsfbr{Y}^{(w)}>m\right\}\right]^{\binom{b-1}{w}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{k-1}\prod_{w=0}^{b-1}\left[\sum_{\ell=m+1}^{\lfloor w+(k+1)/2\rfloor}\mathbb{P}\hskip-1.07639pt\left\{\mathsfbr{Y}^{(w)}=\ell\right\}\right]^{\binom{b-1}{w}}

(29)

which, together with (
IV-C
) and (
28
), gives the average distance for the
RI
scheme.

IV-D

Shift & Inversion Scheme

In this section we show an easy-to-implement scheme similar to the random scheme, but where the
\mathbf{u}^{(i)}_{j}
in (
20
), instead of being obtained by EXOR with randomly generated sequences, are obtained by the circular shift of
i
positions of
\mathbf{u}_{j}
.
A similar solution has been proposed in
[
24
]
and extended in
[
29
]
without bus inversion. We here combine the circular shift with bus inversion. So, we assume that there is one bit assigned to bit inversion, as in Section
III-A
, and
b-1
bits used for shifting purposes.

More precisely, the encoder and the decoder share a set
\{\mathbf{r}^{(i)}\}_{i=0}^{L-1}
of
L=2^{b}
sequences of length
k
, the first half
\mathbf{r}^{(i)}
,
i=0,\ldots,L/2-1
, obtained by the circular shift of the information bits of
i
positions,
\text{shift}(\mathbf{u}_{j},i)
, and the other half obtained by their complements,
\mathbf{r}^{(i+L/2)}=\overline{\mathbf{r}}^{(i)}
.

Formally, we compute for
i=0,\ldots,L/2-1

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}^{(i)}_{j}

\displaystyle=\text{shift}(\mathbf{u}_{j},i)

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{j}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{u}^{(i)}_{j}||\,\mathbf{a}^{(i)},\qquad\mathbf{x}_{j}^{(i+L/2)}=\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{j}^{(i)}||\,\mathbf{a}^{(i+L/2)}\,.

Then, using (
21
) we select the codeword with the minimum distance from the previous. We will refer to this scheme as
SI
(
SI
).

The circular shift of a sequence can be seen approximately as a realization of a random sequence, thus the performance is approximately those of
RI
. Note that, to make the
SI
scheme perform like the
RI
one, it is required that
2^{b-1}\leq k
. This is due to the fact that after
k
circular shifts we return to the initial sequence.
Therefore, the average distance for this scheme can be written as

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{SI}}(k,b)

\displaystyle\simeq\begin{cases}D_{\mathrm{RI}}(k,b)&\text{if $2^{b-1}\leq k$}\\
D_{\mathrm{RI}}(k,1+\lceil\log_{2}(k)\rceil)&\text{otherwise}\,.\\
\end{cases}

(30)

This approximation is confirmed by numerical simulation, as reported in the next section.

VI
Conclusions

We have shown what are the fundamental limits in energy saving by bus encoding and described several methods to implement the optimal encoding scheme to achieve these limits. Due to the complexity of optimal encoding for bus sizes of practical interest, we then studied suboptimal schemes, derived analytically their performance, and compared them with the fundamental limits.
For example, if we limit the number of additional bus lines to
16
, we see that we can reach an energy saving of around
35\%
and
28\%
for data size
k=64
and
k=128
, respectively. For comparison, the well-known partitioned bus inversion gives energy savings of around
22\%
and
18\%
for data size
k=64
and
k=128
, respectively.
Thus, when their implementation cost is small, suitable bus encoding techniques could be effective to reduce the energy spent to drive system buses.

Automorphism groups of ind-varieties of generalized flags
Mikhail Ignatev, Ivan Penkov

Abstract
We compute the group of automorphisms of an arbitrary ind-variety of (possibly isotropic) generalized flags. Such an ind-variety is a homogeneous ind-space for one of the ind-groups S ​ L ​ ( ∞ ) SL(\infty) , O ⁡ ( ∞ ) O(\infty) or S ​ p ​ ( ∞ ) Sp(\infty) . We show that the respective automorphism groups are much larger than S ​ L ​ ( ∞ ) SL(\infty) , O ⁡ ( ∞ ) O(\infty) or S ​ p ​ ( ∞ ) Sp(\infty) , and present the answer in terms of Mackey groups. The latter are groups of automorphisms of nondegenerate pairings of (in general infinite-dimensional) vector spaces. An explicit matrix form of the automorphism group of an arbitrary ind-variety of generalized flags is also given. The case of the Sato grassmannian is considered in detail, and its automorphism group is the projectivization of the connected component of unity in the group known as Japanese G ​ L ​ ( ∞ ) GL(\infty) . Keywords: ind-variety, ind-group, generalized flag, homogeneous space, automorphism group, Makey group, Sato grassmannian, Japanese G ​ L ​ ( ∞ ) GL(\infty) . AMS subject classification: 14L30, 14M15, 14M17, 14J50.



Introduciton

Given a homogenous space
X
, it is a natural problem to compute its automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}\,X
. In the case when
X
is a complex flag variety, that is,
X=G/P
for a connected reductive complex algebraic group and a parabolic subgroup
P\subset G
, the automorphism group of
X
is well known. Moreover, it is a classical result that here the connected component
\mathrm{Aut}^{0}\,X
of the identity equals the projectivized group
PG
, except in some special cases as described in
[
O
]
.

In this paper, we would like to pose and solve the problem of computing
\mathrm{Aut}\,X
for a class of homogeneous ind-varieties
X
. This is the class of ind-varieties of generalized flags introduced by that name in
[
DP
]
but also considered earlier in several works, see for instance
[
DPW
]
,
[
NRW
]
. These ind-varieties can be defined simply as
G/P
where
G
is one of the ind-groups
SL(\infty)=\varinjlim SL(n)
,
SO(\infty)=\varinjlim SO(n)
,
Sp(\infty)=\varinjlim Sp(2n)
and
P
is a splitting parabolic subgroup, i.e., a subgroup for which the intersections
P\cap SL(n)
,
P\cap SO(2n)
,
P\cap SO(2n+1)
,
P\cap Sp(2n)
are parabolic subgroups of
SL(n)
,
SO(2n)
,
SO(2n+1)
,
Sp(2n)
for all
n
, respectively. The definition from
[
DP
]
can be considered as a flag realization of the ind-varieties
G/P
as above, and is recalled in Section
Automorphism groups of ind-varieties of generalized flags
below. The main idea of that approach is that one designates certain chains of subspaces in the natural representation
V
of
SL(\infty)
as generalized flags, and then defines an ind-variety of generalized flags as the ind-variety of generalized flags which differ only “slightly” from a fixed generalized flag
W
in
V
. For the exact definition see Section
Automorphism groups of ind-varieties of generalized flags
. One then shows that the so obtained ind-variety is isomorphic to
G/P
for
G=SL(\infty)
and some splitting parabolic subgroup
P\subset G
.

An ind-grassmannian is an ind-variety of generalized flags for which the fixed generalized flag consists of a single proper subspace
W\subset V
. For
\dim W=\mathrm{codim}\,_{V}W=\infty
the ind-grassmannian is isomorphic to the Sato grassmannian. This has been pointed out for instance in
[
GS
]
.

In the cases of the groups
SO(\infty)
and
Sp(\infty)
we consider ind-varieties of isotropic generalized flags, as stated in Section
Automorphism groups of ind-varieties of generalized flags
.

Our main result is the explicit determination of the group
\mathrm{Aut}\,X
for an arbitrary ind-variety of, possibly isotropic, generalized flags. A notable feature is that the answer is very different from the ind-groups
PGL(\infty)
,
PO(\infty)
, or
PSp(\infty)
, and we present it in the language of Mackey groups. Such a group is defined in terms of a nondegenerate pairing of vector spaces
T\times R\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, and is a subgroup of the group of all linear operators
\varphi\colon T\rightarrow T
for which the dual operator
\varphi^{*}
determines a well-defined automorphism
\overline{\varphi}\colon R\rightarrow R
. This definition of Mackey group is inspired by G. Mackey’s dissertation
[
M
]
. If
T
and
R
are finite dimensional, then the Mackey group is nothing but
GL(T)\simeq GL(R)
. The group known as Japanese
GL(\infty)
is a Mackey group and plays a crusial role in our work. In the Appendix we discuss the structure of this group in detail.

The precise statement of our main result, Theorem
Automorphism groups of ind-varieties of generalized flags
, is presented in Section
Automorphism groups of ind-varieties of generalized flags
. The consideration of the isotropic case is postponed to Section
Automorphism groups of ind-varieties of generalized flags
. The proof of Theorem
Automorphism groups of ind-varieties of generalized flags
is divided into two parts: the case of an ind-grassmannian and the case of an arbitrary generalized flag. For the Sato grassmannian (which is the most interesting ind-grassmannian) our result implies that its automorphism group is isomorphic to the projectivization of the connected component of the identity in the group Japanese
GL(\infty)
. In Section
Automorphism groups of ind-varieties of generalized flags
we give a matrix realization of the group of automorphisms of an arbitrary ind-variety of generalized flags. In the isotropic case such a realization is given in Corollary
Automorphism groups of ind-varieties of generalized flags
.

We would like to point out that
\mathrm{Aut}\,X
depends essentially on the ind-variety
X
, despite the fact that all

are homogeneous spaces for the same group
SL(\infty)
(or, respectively,
SO(\infty)
,
Sp(\infty)
). This is in contrast with the finite-dimensional case in which the connected component of the identity in the automorphism group of a variety

(respectively,
SO(2n)/P
,
SO(2n+1)/P
or
Sp(2n)/P
) depends only on

and not on the choice of

. Further research should be carried out to compare the isomorphism classes of ind-varieties of generalized flags with the isomorphism classes of their automorphism groups.

Our possible application of the results of the present paper would be the study of locally reductive ind-groups

different from
G=SL(\infty)
,
SO(\infty)
,
Sp(\infty)
for which
G/P
is a homogeneous

In conclusion of this short introduction, we should mention that some particular cases of the automorphism groups of ind-varieties of generalized flags have been considered in

1. Brief background and statement of the main result

The ground field is
\mathbb{C}
. If
R
is a vector space, we set
GL(R)=\{\varphi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(R,R)\mid\varphi\text{ is invertible}\}
and
R^{*}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(R,\mathbb{C})
. We also use the superscript
\cdot^{*}
to denote the dual of a vector bundle, as well as the pullback functor for vector bundles along a morphism of varieties. In what follows we consider infinite matrices, in particular, infinite rows and columns. We call such matrices, rows or columns
finitary
if they have at most finitely many nonzero entries. By
\langle\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}
we denote the linear span over
\mathbb{C}
.

We fix a countable-dimensional vector space
V
. A
chain of subspaces

W=\{W_{\alpha}\}
is a set of subspaces
W_{\alpha}\subseteq V
, parameterized by some index set with elements
\alpha
such that for
\alpha\neq\alpha^{\prime}
we have
W_{\alpha}\varsubsetneq W_{\alpha^{\prime}}
or
W_{\alpha^{\prime}}\varsubsetneq W_{\alpha}
. The relation of inclusion induces a total order on the set of indices of a chain. A chain of subspaces
W=\{W_{\alpha}\}
is a
generalized flag in

V
if every index
\alpha
has either an immediate predecessor or an immediate successor, and every nonzero vector
v
of
V
is contained in some difference
W_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}\setminus W_{\alpha^{\prime}}
, where
\alpha^{\prime\prime}
is the immediate successor of
\alpha^{\prime}
. For a more detailed discussion of generalized flags, and for an introduction to ind-varieties of generalized flags, see, e.g.,
[
DP
]
,
[
IP
]
and
[
PT2
]
.

We say that a generalized flag
W
is
compatible with a basis

\widetilde{E}
of
V
(or that
W
is
\widetilde{E}
-
compatible
) if any space
W_{\alpha}
of
W
is spanned by elements of
\widetilde{E}
, i.e.,
W_{\alpha}=\langle W_{\alpha}\cap\widetilde{E}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}
for any
\alpha
. We set
E_{\alpha}=\widetilde{E}\cap W_{\alpha}
. Then
E_{\alpha}
is a basis of
W_{\alpha}
. By
(W_{\alpha})_{*}
we denote the span of the system of linear functions
\widetilde{E}^{*}
dual to the basis
E_{\alpha}
. We have
(W_{\alpha})_{*}\subseteq W^{*}_{\alpha}
. We also let
V_{*}
equal the span of the system of linear functions dual to the basis
\widetilde{E}
. The group
GL(\widetilde{E},V)
is the subgroup of
GL(V)
consisting of all invertible operators
\varphi\colon V\rightarrow V
each of which acts as the identity on all but finitely elements of
\widetilde{E}
.

In what follows, we fix a basis
\widetilde{E}
and a generalized flag
W
in
V
compatible with
\widetilde{E}
. The set
Fl(W,\widetilde{E},V)
is the set of all generalized flags

which are
\widetilde{E}
-commensurable
with

. This latter requirement spells out as the following three conditions for each element
W^{\prime}
of

:

•

the index set which parameterizes the generalized flag
W^{\prime}
is the same as the index set of
W
;

•

there exists a finite-dimensional subspace
Z\subsetneq V
depending on
W^{\prime}
, such that for any
\alpha

W^{\prime}_{\alpha}+Z=W_{\alpha}+Z
and
\dim(W_{\alpha}\cap Z)=\dim(W^{\prime}_{\alpha}\cap Z)
;

•

the generalized flag
W^{\prime}
is compatible with a basis
\widetilde{E}^{\prime}
of
V
, depending on
W^{\prime}
, such that
\widetilde{E}^{\prime}
differs from
\widetilde{E}
by finitely many vectors.

The set

has a natural structure of ind-variety. This is explained in detail in

]
(and in

]

[

arises as follows. Enumerate the basis

by the set
\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and put
V_{i}:=\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{i}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}
for

. Each intersection

is a flag in

of certain type

Fl(\underline{d}_{i},V_{i})\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow Fl(\underline{d}_{i+1},V_{i+1}),

(1)

called

Next we recall that if
T
and

are two (in general, infinite-dimensional) vector spaces endowed with a non-degenerate pairing

(2)

\{\psi\in GL(R)\mid\psi^{*}(T)=T\}

2. Examples

Before we embark on proving Theorem
Automorphism groups of ind-varieties of generalized flags
, we present five examples in which we compute the respective group
GL(\widetilde{E},V)\cdot St_{W}
from Theorem
Automorphism groups of ind-varieties of generalized flags

Automorphism groups of ind-varieties of generalized flags
below, which provides a matrix form of the group

in the general case.

2.1. The case of an ind-grassmannian.
Let’s consider the case where the generalized flag
W
has the form
0\subsetneq W\subsetneq V
, where
W
is a single proper subspace of

(we slightly abuse notation by using the same letter

, or
\dim W=\mathrm{codim}\,_{V}W=\infty
, or

, then

does not depend on the basis

are all subspaces of

of the same dimension as

depends as a set on the choice of the basis

depends only on
\mathrm{codim}\,_{V}W
. Moreover, as an ind-variety

is isomorphic to
Gr(\mathrm{codim}\,_{V}W,V)
. If
\dim W=\mathrm{codim}\,_{V}W=\infty
, the ind-variety

does not depend up to isomorphism on the choice of both

and
\widetilde{E}
. It is known, see for instance
[

]

is isomorphic to the Sato grassmannian introduced in
[

]

If

, then

and

P(GL(\widetilde{E},V)\cdot St_{W})\cong PGL(V).

(5)

Indeed, the action of

3. More background

We need to recall some facts about linear embeddings of finite-dimensional grassmannians and flag varieties. If
T
is a finite-dimensional space and
\underline{d}=\{d^{1},\ldots,d^{i}\}
is a vector of positive integers satisfying
d^{k}<d^{l}<\dim T
for
k<l
, then
Fl(\underline{d},T)
denotes the variety of all flags of subspaces
T_{1}\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq T_{i}\subsetneq T
, where
\dim T_{j}=d^{j}
. If
\underline{d}
consists of one integer

, we write simply
Gr(d,T)
. If
T
is endowed with a non-degenerate symmetric or antisymmetric (symplectic) form, we write respectively
FlO(\underline{d},T)
and
FlS(\underline{d},T)
for the varieties of isotropic flags in
T
with respect to the fixed form. We also write
GrO(d,T)
and
GrS(d,T)
. An isotropic flag has always length less or equal
\frac{\dim T}{2}
but, for convenience, in this paper by an
isotropic flag
we will mean a flag of the form

W_{1}\subset W_{2}\subset\ldots\subset W_{k}\subset W^{\perp}_{k}\subset\ldots\subset W^{\perp}_{1},

where the spaces
W_{1},~\ldots,~W_{k}
are isotropic and the spaces
W^{\perp}_{k},~\ldots,~W^{\perp}_{1}
are coisotropic. All flag varieties
FlO(\underline{d},T)
and
FlS(\underline{d},T)
are connected, except
GrO(d,T)
for
\dim T=2d
. In what follows, by
FlO(\underline{d},T)
or

The Picard group of any grassmannian or ind-grassmannian

is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
except in the case of

for

always denotes the ample generator of

. In the case of
GrO\left(\frac{\dim T}{2}-1,~T\right)
for

we have
\mathrm{Pic}\,GrO\left(\frac{\dim T}{2}-1,~T\right)\simeq\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
.

The automorphism groups of the flag varieties

,
FlO(\underline{d},~T)
,
FlS(\underline{d},~T)
have been known for long time. The fact that the automorphism group of the projective space

is

goes back to the nineteenth century. Wei-Liang Chow
[

]
extended this result to grassmannians in 1949. For a general flag variety

,

,

the connected component of the identity in the automorphism group

is the respective group

,

, or
Sp(T)
, except in several cases listed by A.L. Onishchik in
[

. These special cases are

, the five dimensional quadric

for

In all cases, see for instance
[

, the full automorphism group

is always a semidirect product of its connected component of unity

and a finite group of automorphisms of the Dynkin diagram of the Lie algebra

which keep fixed
the simple roots of the Lie algebra of the isotropy subgroup of a point on the respective flag variety. In the present paper we only consider classical groups of large enough rank, hence we can summarize the relevant part of this result as follows:

•

for

\mathrm{Aut}^{0}Gr(W,E,V)

satisfying (

We now check that the action of the image in

(17)

which vanish on

V_{n}

as linear functions on

In conclusion, the image of

which acts on

and
\widetilde{\varphi}=\varkappa\circ\widetilde{\varphi}_{W}
.

\widetilde{\varphi}(W)=W^{\prime}

, then
\widetilde{\varphi}=\varkappa^{-1}\circ\varkappa\circ\widetilde{\varphi}
where

.

\varphi=\varkappa^{-1}\varphi_{W}
for

, in other words, the image of
\varepsilon
lies in
P(GL(\widetilde{E},V)\cdot St_{W})
.

\varphi\in GL(\widetilde{E},V)\cdot St_{W}
determines a well-defined automorphism of
Gr(W,E,V)
. In the Appendix we introduce the degree

of an operator
\varphi
and show that the space

is
\widetilde{E}
-commensurable with
W
for any

. Denote by

the group of all operators
\varphi\in G(V_{E},V_{*E})
with

.

on

is by automorphisms of ind-varieties, and not merely by bijections of the set
Gr(W,E,V)
. Let

where
\widetilde{E}\cap U
spans
U
. Recall that our fixed nested finite-dimensional spaces

are spanned by elements of
\widetilde{E}
and

where

. The embeddings
X_{n}=Gr(d_{n},V_{n})\hookrightarrow Gr(d_{n+1},V_{n+1})=X_{n+1}
are strict standard extensions

F_{d_{n}}\longmapsto F_{d_{n}}\oplus W_{n|n+1},

where
W_{n+1}=W\cap V_{n+1}=W_{n}\oplus W_{n|n+1}
for

, and

. Therefore we have a decomposition

, where

is the
\widetilde{E}
-compatible direct complement of
V^{*}_{n}
within
W_{*}+V^{*}_{n}
and

is the dual of the
\widetilde{E}
-compatible direct complement

of

in
V
. Any invertible linear operator

induces a decomposition

and hence an operator

\zeta_{n}\colon V^{*}_{n}:=V_{*E}/({\overline{W}_{n}}_{*}\oplus U^{*}_{n})\longrightarrow V_{*E}/(\zeta({\overline{W}_{n}}_{*})\oplus\zeta(U^{*}_{n}))=\zeta_{n}(V^{*}_{n}).

Each linear operator

induces an isomorphism of grassmannians

and the varieties

form an ind-variety isomorphic to

. We conclude that, if
\varinjlim Gr(d_{n},\zeta_{n}(V^{*}_{n})^{*})=Gr(W,E,V)
then

induces an automorphism of the ind-variety
Gr(W,E,V)
. Set now
\zeta:=\overline{\varphi}^{-1}
for
\varphi\in G^{0}(V_{E},V_{*E})
. Then
\zeta_{n}=\left(\varphi^{*}_{n}\right)^{-1}
and
\zeta_{n}(V^{*}_{n})^{*}=\varphi_{n}(V_{n})
. By the above mentioned result from the Appendix, we know that
\varphi\cdot W^{\prime}\in Gr(W,E,V)
whenever
\varphi\in G^{0}(V_{E},V_{*E})
and
W^{\prime}\in Gr(W,E,V)
.
Therefore
\varinjlim Gr(d_{n},\varphi_{n}(V_{n}))=Gr(W,E,V)
,
and we have shown that
PG^{0}(V_{E},V_{*E})\subset\mathrm{im}\,\varepsilon
.

Finally, Theorem A.
Automorphism groups of ind-varieties of generalized flags
(i) from the Appendix implies that

, and hence that also

and

is a group. In particular,
GL(\widetilde{E},V)\cdot St_{W}=St_{W}\cdot GL(\widetilde{E},V)

\mathrm{im}\,\varepsilon=P(GL(\widetilde{E},V)\cdot St_{W}).
\square

5. Proof of Theorem

Step 1. Reduction to the case of an automorphism which preserves all inverse limits of dual tautological bundles.

of

Corollary 7.2.

Appendix: on the structure of the group
G(V_{E},V_{*E})

In this Appendix we have collected some basic statements about the Mackey group
G(V_{E},V_{*E})
. The isomorphism of this group a the group sometimes referred to as Japanese
GL(\infty)
is stated at the end.

A.1. A lemma from linear algebra.

Lemma A.1.
Let
A
be a countable-dimensional vector space
,
and
\mu\colon A^{*}\to A
,

\nu\colon A^{*}\to A
be linear maps such that
\alpha(\mu(\beta))=\beta(\nu(\alpha))
for all
\alpha,~\beta\in A^{*}
. Then the dimensions of the images of
\mu
and
\nu
are finite and equal.

Proof
. We first check that
\dim\mathrm{im}\,\mu<\infty
. This will imply that
\dim\mathrm{im}\,\nu
is also finite because the conditions on
\mu
and
\nu
are symmetric. Pick a basis
\{f_{1},~f_{2},~\ldots\}
of
A
, and let
\{f_{1}^{*},~f_{2}^{*},~\ldots\}
be the dual system in
A^{*}
. Assume that
\mathrm{im}\,\mu
is infinite dimensional. Then there exist linear functions
\lambda_{n}\in A^{*}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, such that
\dim\langle\mu(\lambda_{1}),~\ldots,~\mu(\lambda_{n})\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}=n
. It is easy to see that these linear functions can be chosen so that
\lambda_{n}(f_{i})=0
for
i<n
and
\lambda_{n}(f_{n})\neq 0
. Let
B:=\langle\lambda_{n}|n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}
. The formula
(a,b)\mapsto b(a)
for
a\in A
,
b\in B
defines a non-degenerate pairing between
A
and
B
. By a result of G. Mackey
[
M
, Lemma on p. 171]
, we may assume without loss of generality that
\lambda_{n}=f_{n}^{*}
for all
n
.

Since
\dim\mathrm{im}\,\mu=\infty
, for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
there exists
i_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
\mu(f_{i_{n}}^{*})
is not contained in
A_{n}:=\langle f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}
.
In other words, there exist two infinite sequences of integers
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots
and
2\leq k_{1}<k_{2}<\ldots
so that
\mu(f_{i_{n}}^{*})\in A_{k_{n}}\setminus A_{k_{n}-1}
for all
n\geq 1
.

Now we define a sequence
c_{1},~c_{2},~\ldots
of complex numbers inductively as follows. Set
c_{1}
to be an arbitrary nonzero scalar. For
n>1
we let
c_{n}
to be an arbitrary complex number such that

\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}f_{k_{j}}^{*}(\mu(f_{i_{n}}^{*}))=c_{n}f_{k_{n}}^{*}(\mu(f_{i_{n}}^{*}))+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}c_{j}f_{k_{j}}^{*}(\mu(f_{i_{n}}^{*}))\neq 0.

(Such a number exists because
f_{k_{n}}^{*}(\mu(f_{i_{n}}^{*}))\neq 0
.) Finally, we define
\alpha\in A^{*}
by the formula

\alpha(f_{j})=\begin{cases}c_{n}&\text{if }j=k_{n}\text{ for some }n\geq 1,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then

\begin{split}f_{i_{n}}^{*}(\nu(\alpha))=\alpha(\mu(f_{i_{n}}^{*}))=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}f_{k_{j}}^{*}(\mu(f_{i_{n}}^{*}))\neq 0\end{split}

for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. This means that
\nu(\alpha)\notin A_{i_{n}-1}
for all
n\geq 1
, i.e., that
\nu(\alpha)
can not be expressed as a finite linear combination of
f_{1},~f_{2},~\ldots
, and this is a contradiction.

Hence,
\dim\mathrm{im}\,\mu<\infty
, and also
\dim\mathrm{im}\,\nu<\infty
. It remains to check that
\dim\mathrm{im}\,\mu=\dim\mathrm{im}\,\nu
. Since
\dim\mathrm{im}\,\mu<\infty
, there exist
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and linear functions
\alpha_{1},~\ldots,~\alpha_{n}\in A^{**}
such that
\mu(a)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(a)f_{i}
for all
a\in A^{*}
. We note that in fact
\alpha_{i}\in A
for all
i
. Indeed,

f_{j}^{*}(\mu(a))=f_{j}^{*}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(a)f_{i}\right)=\alpha_{j}(a)=a(\nu(f_{j}^{*})),

so
\alpha_{j}=\nu(f_{j}^{*})\in A
for all
j
. It follows that there exist
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and scalars
\alpha_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}
so that
\alpha_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{ij}f_{i}
for all
1\leq j\leq n
. Clearly,
\dim\mathrm{im}\,\mu=\mathrm{rk}\,(\alpha_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{m,n}
. Similarly, there exist
n^{\prime},~m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, vectors
\beta_{j}\in A
, and scalars
\beta_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}
so that
\nu(b)=\sum_{j=1}^{n^{\prime}}\beta_{j}(b)f_{j}
and
\beta_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}\beta_{ij}f_{i}
for all
1\leq j\leq n^{\prime}
. Then
\dim\mathrm{im}\,\nu=\mathrm{rk}\,(\beta_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}
. However,
\alpha_{ij}=f_{i}^{*}(\alpha_{j})=f_{i}^{*}(\nu(f_{j}^{*}))=f_{j}^{*}(\mu(f_{i}^{*}))=f_{j}^{*}(\beta_{i})=\beta_{ji}
for all
i,~j
. This means that

,
n^{\prime}=m
, and the matrices
(\alpha_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{m,n}
and
(\beta_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n,m}
are transpose to each other. The result follows.
\square

, we have
\pi_{U^{*}}({\left.\kern-1.2pt\varphi\right|_{U^{*}}}(\gamma))(e_{i})=\varphi(\gamma)(e_{i})=\gamma(\overline{\varphi}(e_{i}))=\gamma(\pi_{U}(\overline{\varphi}(e_{i})))
. There exist linear functions
\beta_{j}\in U^{*}
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, such that
\pi_{U}(\overline{\varphi}(y))=(\beta_{1}(y),~\beta_{2}(y),~\ldots)
. Since
\pi_{U}(\overline{\varphi}(e_{i}))
belongs to
U
, there exists
k(i)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
\beta_{j}(e_{i})=0
for
j>k(i)
, i.e.,
\pi_{U}(\overline{\varphi}(e_{i}))=\sum_{j=1}^{k(i)}\beta_{j}(e_{i})e_{j}
. Thus,
\pi_{U^{*}}\circ({\left.\kern-1.2pt\varphi\right|_{U^{*}}})
can be represented as a matrix with finitary rows
A=(a_{-i,-j})_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}
, where
a_{-i,-j}=\beta_{j}(e_{i})
.

It follows that
\varphi\in G_{U}
can be represented by invertible matrices from
L
. This conclusion applies also to the inverse operator
\varphi^{-1}
by the same argument.

In order to verify the inclusion
\widetilde{G}_{U}\subset G_{U}
, let now
\psi
be a matrix from
L
such that
\psi^{-1}\in L
. Then the linear operator on
U^{*}\oplus U
with matrix
\overline{\psi}
is the restriction to
U^{*}\oplus U
of the operator dual to the operator defined by
\psi
. Indeed,

\begin{split}\langle\overline{\psi}(\gamma,y)^{T},(\gamma^{\prime},y^{\prime})^{T}\rangle&=\overline{\overline{\psi}(y,\gamma)^{T}}(\gamma^{\prime},y^{\prime})^{T}=\overline{(\gamma,y)^{T}}~\overline{\overline{\psi}}(\gamma^{\prime},y^{\prime})^{T}\\
&=\overline{(\gamma,y)^{T}}\psi(\gamma^{\prime},y^{\prime})^{T}=\langle(\gamma,y)^{T},\psi(\gamma^{\prime},y^{\prime})^{T}\rangle,\end{split}

(29)

where the three middle terms are products of two or respectively three matrices.
Moreover, the invertibility of
\overline{\psi}
follows from the equality
\overline{\psi}^{-1}=\overline{\psi^{-1}}
which is a consequence of the relation
\overline{\psi_{1}\psi_{2}}=\overline{\psi}_{2}\overline{\psi}_{1}
. This shows that
\psi
determines an operator from
G_{U}
.

\square

Remark A.9.
Finally, the reader will notice that if in the definition of
J
we replace the ordered set
\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}=\mathbb{Z}_{<0}\sqcup\mathbb{Z}_{>0}

by any ordered set which is the disjoint union
O_{1}\sqcup O_{2}
of two linearly ordered countable sets with the condition
r<s
for
r\in O_{1}
,
s\in O_{2}
, and define a group by imposing the conditions (
28
), we will obtain a group isomorphic to
G_{U}
. Of course, in such a setting the matrix of the operator
\overline{\varphi}=\varphi^{*}|_{U^{*}\oplus U}
, for a given
\varphi\in G_{U}
with matrix
\psi
, will not be
\overline{\psi}
as defined above, and its form will depend on the choice of
O_{1}
and
O_{2}
. Moreover, there will also be analogues of the operator
Sh
, and we leave it to the reader to define one.
\bigcirc

The above remark is used in the proof of Theorem
Automorphism groups of ind-varieties of generalized flags
presented in Section
Automorphism groups of ind-varieties of generalized flags
.

Finally, using the matrix form of the group
G_{U}
given by Theorem A.
Automorphism groups of ind-varieties of generalized flags
, it is straightforward to check that
G_{U}
is nothing but the group

Towards Races in Linear Logic
Wen Kokke \rsuper a, J. Garrett Morris \rsuper b, Philip Wadler \rsuper a

Abstract
. Process calculi based in logic, such as πDILL and CP, provide a foundation for deadlock-free concurrent programming, but exclude non-determinism and races. HCP is a reformulation of CP which addresses a fundamental shortcoming: the fundamental operator for parallel composition from the π-calculus does not correspond to any rule of linear logic, and therefore not to any term construct in CP. We introduce HCP ND \text{HCP}_{\text{ND}} , which extends HCP with a novel account of non-determinism. Our approach draws on bounded linear logic to provide a strongly-typed account of standard process calculus expressions of non-determinism. We show that our extension is expressive enough to capture many uses of non-determinism in untyped calculi, such as non-deterministic choice, while preserving HCP ’s meta-theoretic properties, including deadlock freedom.

2.
Non-determinism, Logic, and Session Types

Recent work extended πDILL and CP with operators for non-deterministic behaviour
[
ALM16
,
Cai14
,
CP17
]
. These extensions all implement an operator known as non-deterministic local choice. (This operator is written as
\textcolor
tmcolor
P+Q

[
MPW92
]

\textcolor{tmcolor}{P}\;\vdash\;\textcolor{tycolor}{\Gamma}

\textcolor{tmcolor}{Q}\;\vdash\;\textcolor{tycolor}{\Gamma}

\textcolor{tmcolor}{P+Q}\;\vdash\;\textcolor{tycolor}{\Gamma}

\begin{array}[]{c}\textcolor{tmcolor}{P+Q}\overset{}{\Longrightarrow}\textcolor{tmcolor}{P}\\
\textcolor{tmcolor}{P+Q}\overset{}{\Longrightarrow}\textcolor{tmcolor}{Q}\end{array}

Local choice introduces non-determinism explicitly, by listing all possible choices. This is unlike the π-calculus, where non-determinism arises due to multiple processes communicating on shared channels. We can easily implement local choice in the π-calculus, using a nullary communication:

\begin{array}[]{c}\textcolor{tmcolor}{(x\langle\rangle.0\parallel x().P\parallel x().Q)}\\[4.30554pt]
\rotatebox[origin]{270.0}{$\Longrightarrow^{\star}$}\\[4.30554pt]
\textcolor{tmcolor}{(P\parallel x().Q)}\quad\text{or}\quad\textcolor{tmcolor}{(x().P\parallel Q)}\end{array}

In this implementation, the process
\textcolor
tmcolor

\textcolor
tmcolor

or
\textcolor
tmcolor
Q

\textcolor{tmcolor}{(x\langle\rangle.0\parallel(x().P+x().Q))}

3.3.
Erratum for HCP

The typing rules for HCP presented here are more restrictive than those in earlier publications
[
KMP18
]
. Progress does not hold for the earlier version. For instance, the following process is stuck, yet typeable:

\textcolor{tmcolor}{0}\;\vdash\;\textcolor{tycolor}{\varnothing}

\textcolor{tmcolor}{y[].0}\;\vdash\;\textcolor{tycolor}{\textcolor{tmcolor}{y}\colon\!\textcolor{tycolor}{\mathbf{1}}}

\textcolor{tmcolor}{x[].y[].0}\;\vdash\;\textcolor{tycolor}{\textcolor{tmcolor}{x}\colon\!\textcolor{tycolor}{\mathbf{1}}\parallel\textcolor{tmcolor}{y}\colon\!\textcolor{tycolor}{\mathbf{1}}}

\textcolor{tmcolor}{x{\leftrightarrow}z}\;\vdash\;\textcolor{tycolor}{\textcolor{tmcolor}{x}\colon\!\textcolor{tycolor}{\bot},\textcolor{tmcolor}{z}\colon\!\textcolor{tycolor}{\mathbf{1}}}

\textcolor{tmcolor}{y().x{\leftrightarrow}z}\;\vdash\;\textcolor{tycolor}{\textcolor{tmcolor}{x}\colon\!\textcolor{tycolor}{\bot},\textcolor{tmcolor}{y}\colon\!\textcolor{tycolor}{\bot},\textcolor{tmcolor}{z}\colon\!\textcolor{tycolor}{\mathbf{1}}}

\textcolor{tmcolor}{x[].y[].0\parallel y().x{\leftrightarrow}z}\;\vdash\;\textcolor{tycolor}{\textcolor{tmcolor}{x}\colon\!\textcolor{tycolor}{\mathbf{1}}\parallel\textcolor{tmcolor}{y}\colon\!\textcolor{tycolor}{\mathbf{1}}\parallel\textcolor{tmcolor}{x}\colon\!\textcolor{tycolor}{\bot},\textcolor{tmcolor}{y}\colon\!\textcolor{tycolor}{\bot},\textcolor{tmcolor}{z}\colon\!\textcolor{tycolor}{\mathbf{1}}}

\textcolor{tmcolor}{(\nu{y})({x[].y[].0\parallel y().x{\leftrightarrow}z})}\;\vdash\;\textcolor{tycolor}{\textcolor{tmcolor}{x}\colon\!\textcolor{tycolor}{\mathbf{1}}\parallel\textcolor{tmcolor}{x}\colon\!\textcolor{tycolor}{\bot},\textcolor{tmcolor}{z}\colon\!\textcolor{tycolor}{\mathbf{1}}}

\textcolor{tmcolor}{(\nu{x})(\nu{y})({x[].y[].0\parallel y().x{\leftrightarrow}z})}\;\vdash\;\textcolor{tycolor}{\textcolor{tmcolor}{z}\colon\!\textcolor{tycolor}{\mathbf{1}}}

The earlier typing rules failed to guarantee a crucial property: each typing environment should correspond to one top-level action. The rules presented in this paper fixes the problem by disallowing hyper-environments in logical rules.

The move from channel names to endpoint names is not essential to the fix, but significantly streamlines the presentation. Otherwise, the type system must guarantee that each channel name occurs at most twice in the hypersequent, and if twice, then with dual types. Using endpoint names, it is sufficient to require that all names be distinct.

{defi}

So where does the non-determinism in
\text{HCP}_{\text{ND}}

\textcolor{tmcolor}{(\nu xx^{\prime})(({\star}{x}[y_{1}].P_{1}\parallel\dots\mid{\star}{x}[y_{n}].P_{n})\parallel{\star}{x^{\prime}}(y^{\prime}_{1}).\dots{\star}{x^{\prime}}(y^{\prime}_{n}).Q)}

As parallel composition is commutative and associative, we can rewrite this term to pair any client in the pool with the server before applying
E-Request

Does this mean that, for an arbitrary client pool
\textcolor{tmcolor}{P}

\textcolor{tmcolor}{(\nu xy)(P\parallel{\star}{y}(w).Q)}

\textcolor{tmcolor}{x}

\textcolor{tmcolor}{a}

\textcolor{tmcolor}{\begin{array}[]{lrl}(\nu xx^{\prime})&((&\;({\star}{x}[y_{1}].P_{1}\parallel\dots\mid{\star}{x}[y_{n}].P_{n})\\

&\mid&\;{\star}{x^{\prime}}(y^{\prime}_{1}).\dots{\star}{x^{\prime}}(y^{\prime}_{m}).Q\;)\end{array}}

If we reduce this term, then only the clients
\textcolor{tmcolor}{{\star}{x}[z_{1}].P_{1}}\dots\textcolor{tmcolor}{{\star}{x}[z_{n}].P_{n}}

\textcolor{tmcolor}{\begin{array}[]{lrl}(\nu xx^{\prime})&(&\;a().({\star}{x}[y_{n+1}].P_{n+1}\parallel\dots\mid{\star}{x}[y_{m}].P_{m})\\

Metatheory

\text{HCP}_{\text{ND}}
enjoys subject reduction, termination, and progress.

Lemma 6

(Preservation for
\equiv
)
.

By induction on the derivation of

\textcolor{tmcolor}{Q}\;\vdash\;\textcolor{tycolor}{\mathcal{G}}

By induction on the derivation of
\textcolor{tmcolor}{P}\overset{}{\Longrightarrow}\textcolor{tmcolor}{Q}
.

A process

\textcolor{tmcolor}{P}\equiv\textcolor{tmcolor}{(\nu x_{1}x^{\prime}_{1})\dots(\nu x_{n}x^{\prime}_{n})(P_{1}\mid\dots\mid P_{n+m+1})},

\textcolor{tmcolor}{x_{i}}

Lemma 8
.

If a well-typed process
\textcolor{tmcolor}{P}
is in canonical form, then it is blocked on
an external communication, i.e.,

\textcolor{tmcolor}{P}\equiv\textcolor{tmcolor}{(\nu x_{1}x^{\prime}_{1})\dots(\nu x_{n}x^{\prime}_{n})(P_{1}\mid\dots\mid P_{n+m+1})}

such that at least one process
\textcolor{tmcolor}{P_{i}}
acts on a free name.

Proof 4.3
.

We have

\textcolor{tmcolor}{P}\equiv\textcolor{tmcolor}{(\nu x_{1}x^{\prime}_{1})\dots(\nu x_{n}x^{\prime}_{n})(P_{1}\parallel\dots\parallel P_{n+m+1})},

such that no
\textcolor{tmcolor}{P_{i}}
is a cut or a link acting on a bound channel, and no two processes
\textcolor{tmcolor}{P_{i}}
and
\textcolor{tmcolor}{P_{j}}
are acting on the same bound channel with dual actions. The prefix of cuts and mixes introduces
n
channels. Each application of cut requires an application of mix, so the prefix introduces
n+m+1
processes. Each application of
\textsc{Cont}_{!}
requires an application of mix, so there are at most
m
clients acting on the same bound channel. Therefore, at least
one
of the processes
\textcolor{tmcolor}{P_{i}}
must be acting on a free channel, i.e., blocked on an external communication.

Theorem 9

(Progress)
.

If
\textcolor{tmcolor}{P}\;\vdash\;\textcolor{tycolor}{\mathcal{G}}
, then either
\textcolor{tmcolor}{P}
is in canonical form, or there exists a process
\textcolor{tmcolor}{Q}
such that
\textcolor{tmcolor}{P}\Longrightarrow\textcolor{tmcolor}{Q}
.

Proof 4.4
.

We consider the maximum prefix of cuts and mixes of
\textcolor{tmcolor}{P}
such that

\textcolor{tmcolor}{P}\equiv\textcolor{tmcolor}{(\nu x_{1}x^{\prime}_{1})\dots(\nu x_{n}x^{\prime}_{n})(P_{1}\parallel\dots\parallel P_{n+m+1})},

and no
\textcolor{tmcolor}{P_{i}}
is a cut. If any process
\textcolor{tmcolor}{P_{i}}
is a link, we reduce by
({\leftrightarrow})
. If any two processes
\textcolor{tmcolor}{P_{i}}
and
\textcolor{tmcolor}{P_{j}}
are acting dual endpoints
\textcolor{tmcolor}{x_{i}}
and
\textcolor{tmcolor}{x^{\prime}_{i}}
of the same channel, we rewrite by
\equiv
and reduce by the appropriate
\beta
-rule. Otherwise,
\textcolor{tmcolor}{P}
is in canonical form.

Theorem 10

(Termination)
.

If
\textcolor{tmcolor}{P}\;\vdash\;\textcolor{tycolor}{\mathcal{G}}
, then there are no infinite
\Longrightarrow
-reduction sequences.

Proof 4.5
.

Every reduction reduces a single cut to zero, one or two cuts. However, each of these cuts is smaller, measured in the size of the cut formula. Furthermore, each instance of the structural congruence preserves the size of the cut. Therefore, there cannot be an infinite
\Longrightarrow
-reduction sequence.

4.5.
\text{HCP}_{\text{ND}}
and Non-deterministic Local Choice

In
section

2
, we discussed the non-deterministic local choice operator, which is used in several extensions of πDILL and CP
[
ALM16
,
Cai14
,
CP17
]
. This operator is admissible in
\text{HCP}_{\text{ND}}
. We can derive the non-deterministic choice
\textcolor
tmcolor
P+Q
by constructing the following term:

\textcolor{tmcolor}{\begin{array}[]{lrlrl}(\nu xx^{\prime})&((&\;{\star}{x}[y].y\triangleleft\texttt{inl}.y[].0\\
&\mid&\;{\star}{x}[z].z\triangleleft\texttt{inr}.z[].0\;)\\
&\mid&\;{\star}{x^{\prime}}(y^{\prime}).{\star}{x^{\prime}}(z^{\prime}).y^{\prime}\>{\triangleright}\\
&&\quad\begin{array}[]{rl}\{&\texttt{inl}:\;(\nu ww^{\prime})(z^{\prime}\triangleright\{\texttt{inl}:z^{\prime}().w[].0;\texttt{inr}:z^{\prime}().w[].0\}\parallel w^{\prime}().P)\\
;&\texttt{inr}:\;(\nu ww^{\prime})(z^{\prime}\triangleright\{\texttt{inl}:z^{\prime}().w[].0;\texttt{inr}:z^{\prime}().w[].0\}\parallel w^{\prime}().Q)\;\})\end{array}\end{array}}

This term is a cut between two processes.

•

On the left-hand side, we have a pool of two processes,
\textcolor{tmcolor}{{\star}{x}[y].y\triangleleft\texttt{inl}.y[].0}
and
\textcolor{tmcolor}{{\star}{x}[z].z\triangleleft\texttt{inr}.z[].0}
. Each makes a choice: the first sends
\textcolor{tmcolor}{\texttt{inl}}
, and the second sends
\textcolor{tmcolor}{\texttt{inr}}
.

•

On the right-hand side, we have a server with both
\textcolor{tmcolor}{P}
and
\textcolor{tmcolor}{Q}
. This server has two channels on which a choice is offered,
\textcolor{tmcolor}{y^{\prime}}
and
\textcolor{tmcolor}{z^{\prime}}
. The choice on
\textcolor{tmcolor}{y^{\prime}}
selects between
\textcolor{tmcolor}{P}
and
\textcolor{tmcolor}{Q}
. The choice on
\textcolor{tmcolor}{z^{\prime}}
does not affect the outcome of the process at all. Instead, it is discarded.

When these clients and the server are put together, the choices offered by the server will be non-deterministically lined up with the clients which make choices, and either
\textcolor{tmcolor}{P}
or
\textcolor{tmcolor}{Q}
will run.

While there is a certain amount of overhead involved in this encoding, it scales linearly in terms of the number of processes. The reverse—encoding the non-determinism present in
\text{HCP}_{\text{ND}}
using non-deterministic local choice—scales exponentially, see,
e
.
g
., the examples in
section

2
.

5.
Cuts with Leftovers

So far, our account of a non-determinism in client/server interactions only allows for interactions between equal numbers of clients and server interactions. A natural question is whether or not we can deal with the scenario in which there are more client than server interactions or vice versa,
i
.
e
., whether or not the following rules are derivable:

\textcolor{tmcolor}{}\;\vdash\;\textcolor{tycolor}{\Gamma,\textcolor{tycolor}{{!}_{n+m}{A}}}

\textcolor{tmcolor}{}\;\vdash\;\textcolor{tycolor}{\Delta,\textcolor{tycolor}{{?}_{n}{A^{\bot}}}}

\textcolor{tmcolor}{}\;\vdash\;\textcolor{tycolor}{\Gamma,\Delta,\textcolor{tycolor}{{!}_{m}{A}}}

\textcolor{tmcolor}{}\;\vdash\;\textcolor{tycolor}{\Gamma,\textcolor{tycolor}{{!}_{n}{A}}}

\textcolor{tmcolor}{}\;\vdash\;\textcolor{tycolor}{\Delta,\textcolor{tycolor}{{?}_{n+m}{A^{\bot}}}}

\textcolor{tmcolor}{}\;\vdash\;\textcolor{tycolor}{\Gamma,\Delta,\textcolor{tycolor}{{?}_{m}{A^{\bot}}}}

These rules are derivable using a link. For instance, we can derive the rule for the case in which there are more clients than servers as follows:

\textcolor{tmcolor}{P}\;\vdash\;\textcolor{tycolor}{\Gamma,\textcolor{tmcolor}{x}\colon\!\textcolor{tycolor}{{!}_{n+m}{A}}}

\textcolor{tmcolor}{Q}\;\vdash\;\textcolor{tycolor}{\Delta,\textcolor{tmcolor}{x^{\prime}}\colon\!\textcolor{tycolor}{{?}_{n}{A^{\bot}}}}

\textcolor{tmcolor}{x^{\prime\prime}{\leftrightarrow}w}\;\vdash\;\textcolor{tycolor}{\textcolor{tmcolor}{x^{\prime\prime}}\colon\!\textcolor{tycolor}{{?}_{m}{A^{\bot}}},\textcolor{tmcolor}{w}\colon\!\textcolor{tycolor}{{!}_{m}{A}}}

6.
Relation to Manifest Sharing

In
section

2
, we mentioned related work which extends πDILL and CP with non-deterministic local choice
[
ALM16
,
Cai14
,
CP17
]
, and contrasted these approaches with ours.
In this section, we will contrast our work with the more recent work on manifest sharing
[
BP17
]
.

Manifest sharing extends the session-typed language
SILL
with two connectives,
\textcolor{tycolor}{{\uparrow^{S}_{L}}A}
and
\textcolor{tycolor}{{\downarrow^{S}_{L}}A}
, which represent the places in a protocol where a shared resource is aquired and released, respectively. In the resulting language,
\text{$\text{SILL}$ }_{S}
, we can define a type for,
e
.
g
., shared queues (using the notation for types introduced in this paper):

\textcolor{tycolor}{\text{queue}\;A}::=\textcolor{tycolor}{{\uparrow^{S}_{L}}(\;A^{\bot}\mathbin{\bindnasrepma}{\downarrow^{S}_{L}}(\text{queue}\;A)\;)\mathbin{\binampersand}(\;(A\oplus\bot)\mathbin{\bindnasrepma}{\downarrow^{S}_{L}}(\text{queue}\;A)\;)}

The type
\textcolor{tycolor}{\text{queue}\;A}
types a shared channel which, after we aqcuire exclusive access, gives us the choice between enqueuing a value (
\textcolor{tycolor}{A^{\bot}}
) and releasing the queue, or dequeuing a value if there is any (
\textcolor{tycolor}{A\oplus\bot}
) and releasing the queue.

The language
\text{$\text{SILL}$ }_{S}
is much more expressive than
\text{HCP}_{\text{ND}}
, as it has support for both shared channels and recursion.
In fact, Balzer, Pfenning, and Toninho
[
BPT18
]
show that
\text{$\text{SILL}$ }_{S}
is expressive enough to embed the untyped asynchronous π-calculus. This expressiveness comes with a cost, as
\text{$\text{SILL}$ }_{S}
processes are not guaranteed to be deadlock free, though recent work addresses this issue
[
BTP19
]
.

Despite the difference in expressiveness, there are some similarities between
\text{HCP}_{\text{ND}}
and
\text{$\text{SILL}$ }_{S}
. In the former, shared channels represent (length-indexed) streams of interactions of the same type. In the latter, it is necessary for type preservation that shared channels are always released at the same type at which they were acquired, meaning that shared channels also represent (possibly infinite) streams of interactions of the same type. In fact, in
\text{HCP}_{\text{ND}}
, the type for queues (with
n
interactions) can be written as
\textcolor{tycolor}{{!}_{n}{(A^{\bot}\mathbin{\binampersand}(A\oplus\bot))}}
.

One key difference between
\text{HCP}_{\text{ND}}
and
\text{$\text{SILL}$ }_{S}
is that in
\text{$\text{SILL}$ }_{S}
a server must finish interacting with one client before interacting with another, whereas in
\text{HCP}_{\text{ND}}
the server may interact with multiple clients simultaneously.

7.
Discussion and Future Work

We presented
\text{HCP}_{\text{ND}}
, an extension of
HCP
which permits non-deterministic communication without losing the strong connection to logic. We gave proofs for preservation, progress, and termination for the term reduction system of
\text{HCP}_{\text{ND}}
. We showed that we can define non-deterministic local choice in
\text{HCP}_{\text{ND}}
.

Our formalism so far has only captured servers that provide for a fixed number of clients. More realistically, we would want to define servers that provide for arbitrary numbers of clients. This poses two problems: how would we define arbitrarily-interacting stateful processes, and how would we extend the typing discipline of
\text{HCP}_{\text{ND}}
to account for them without losing its static guarantees.

One approach to defining server processes would be to combine
\text{HCP}_{\text{ND}}
with structural recursion and corecursion, following the
\mu\text{CP}
extension of Lindley and Morris
[
LM16
]
. Their approach can express processes which produce streams of
\textcolor
tycolor
A
channels. Such a process would expose a channel with the co-recursive type
\textcolor
tycolor
\nu X.A\mathbin{\bindnasrepma}(1\oplus X)
. Given such a process, it is possible to produce a channel of type
\textcolor
tycolor
A\mathbin{\bindnasrepma}A\mathbin{\bindnasrepma}\cdots\mathbin{\bindnasrepma}A
for any number of
\textcolor
tycolor
A
s, allowing us to satisfy the type
\textcolor
tycolor
{?}_{n}{A}
for an arbitrary
n
.

We would also need to extend the typing discipline to capture arbitrary use of shared channels. One approach would be to introduce resource variables and quantification. Following this approach, in addition to having types
\textcolor
tycolor
{?}_{n}{A}
and
\textcolor
tycolor
{!}_{n}{A}
for concrete
n
, we would also have types
\textcolor
tycolor
{?}_{x}{A}
and
\textcolor
tycolor
{!}_{x}{A}
for resource variables
x
. These variables would be introduced by quantifiers
\textcolor
tycolor
\forall xA
and
\textcolor
tycolor
\exists xA
. Defining terms corresponding to
\textcolor
tycolor
\forall xA
, and its relationship with structured recursion, presents an interesting area of further work.

Our account of
HCP
did not include the exponentials
\textcolor
tycolor
{?}{A}
and
\textcolor
tycolor
{!}{A}
. The type
\textcolor
tycolor
{!}{A}
denotes arbitrarily many independent instances of
\textcolor
tycolor
A
, while the type
\textcolor
tycolor
{?}{A}
denotes a concrete (if unspecified) number of potentially-dependent instances of
\textcolor
tycolor
A
. Existing interpretations of linear logic as session types have taken
\textcolor
tycolor
{!}{A}
to denote
\textcolor
tycolor
A
-servers, while
\textcolor
tycolor
{?}{A}
denotes
\textcolor
tycolor
A
-clients. However, the analogy is imperfect: while we expect servers to provide arbitrarily many instances of their behaviour, we also expect those instances to be interdependent.

With quantification over resource variables, we can give precise accounts of both CP’s exponentials and idealised servers and clients. CP exponentials could be embedded into this framework using the definitions
\textcolor{tycolor}{{!}{A}}::=\textcolor{tycolor}{\forall{n}{!}_{n}{A}}
and
\textcolor{tycolor}{{?}{A}}::=\textcolor{tycolor}{\exists{n}{{?}_{n}{A}}}
. We would also have types that precisely matched our intuitions for server and client behavior: an
\textcolor
tycolor
A
server is of type
\textcolor
tycolor
\forall{n}{{?}_{n}{A}}
, as it serves an unbounded number of requests with the requests being interdependent, while a collection of
\textcolor
tycolor
A
clients is of type
\textcolor
tycolor
\exists{n}{{!}_{n}{A}}
, as we have a specific number of clients with each client being independent.

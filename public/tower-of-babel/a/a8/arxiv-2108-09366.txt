Multi-boundary generalization of thermofield double states and their realization in critical quantum spin chains
Yijian Zou, Guifre Vidal

Abstract
We propose a multi-boundary generalization of thermofield double states (TFD) of a two-dimensional conformal field theory (CFT) and show, through a conformal map to the complex plane, that they are closely related to multi-point correlation functions. We then also describe how to approximately realize these multi-boundary TFD states numerically on the lattice, starting from a critical quantum spin chain Hamiltonian. In addition, finite size corrections on the lattice are seen to be significantly reduced by the use of smoothers – numerically optimized unitary transformations that locally re-arrange the quantum spin degrees of freedom. One merit of the spin chain realization is that it allows us to probe the properties of the proposed multi-boundary TFD states through numerical experiments, including the characterization of their entanglement structure. As an illustration, we explicitly construct generalized TFD states with three and four boundaries for the Ising CFT and compute entanglement quantities using novel free fermion techniques. We find ranges of parameters where their multipartite entanglement is significant or negligible.



Thermofield double (TFD) states play a distinguished role in several areas of quantum physics, including quantum many-body physics, quantum fields, and quantum gravity. They appear naturally e.g. in the study of quantum spin chains and quantum fields at finite temperature, as well as in the holographic description, in terms of a conformal field theory (CFT) of certain class of gravitational wormholes connecting two black holes
Maldacena 2003
;
Maldacena and Susskind 2013
;
Balasubramanian et al. 2014
;
Chapman et al. 2019
;
Yang et al. 2019
. Lately, the experimental realization of TFD states has been discussed
Cottrell et al. 2019
;
Wu and Hsieh 2019
;
Zhu et al. 2020
, including proposals involving quantum computers.

Consider a system with Hamiltonian
H
in a thermal state
\rho_{\beta}=e^{-\beta H}/Z
, where
\beta
is the inverse temperature and
Z(\beta)=\mbox{tr}(e^{-\beta H})
is a normalization constant. As further reviewed below, a TFD state
\ket{\psi_{\rho_{\beta}}^{(1,2)}}
can be understood as a symmetric purification of the thermal state
\rho_{\beta}
, which is an entangled pure state supported on two identical copies of the system, such that the thermal state in one copy (system 1) is recovered upon tracing out over the second copy (system 2), that is
\rho_{\beta}=\mbox{tr}_{(2)}(\ket{\psi_{\rho_{\beta}}^{(1,2)}}\bra{\psi_{\rho_{\beta}}^{(1,2)}})
. A TFD state can also be obtained by regarding a (suitably normalized) imaginary time evolution operator
e^{-(\beta/2)H}/Z(\beta/2)
as a wavefunction or, alternatively, by a closely related, euclidean path integral on an appropriate geometry. For instance, for a 1+1 dimensional quantum field theory on a circle, a TFD state on two copies of the circle is obtained by an Euclidean path integral on a cylinder. In particular, for a 1+1 CFT on the circle, the above TFD state has been proposed in AdS/CFT to be dual to a 2+1 dimensional quantum gravity geometry corresponding to a two-sided wormhole connecting two black holes
Maldacena 2003
;
Maldacena and Susskind 2013
;
Balasubramanian et al. 2014
;
Chapman et al. 2019
;
Yang et al. 2019
.

Generalizations of TFD states connecting more than two copies of the system have been proposed in the context of holography. Specifically, Ref.
Balasubramanian et al. 2014
investigated the entanglement properties of a family of so-called multi-boundary wormhole states, defined in terms of a CFT Euclidean path integral over a Riemann surface with
n
holes, which reduce to a TFD state for
n=2
. It was argued that in certain limit the corresponding
n
-partite wavefunction could be written in terms of the n-point functions of the CFT.

In this paper we also propose a family of generalized TFD states for 1+1 CFTs. As in Ref.
Balasubramanian et al. 2014
, our construction produces entangled states of n copies of a quantum system by means of an Euclidean path integral. However, the geometries with n boundaries that we use as a generalization of a cylinder differ from those in Ref.
Balasubramanian et al. 2014
.
We then present a number of results. Firstly, using a conformal map from the n-boundary geometry to the complex plane, we show that the resulting generalized TFD wavefunction encodes
n
-point correlation functions of the CFT. Next, we describe how to build a lattice version of the generalized TFD states, starting from a critical quantum spin chain Hamiltonian. On the lattice, the properties of the TFD states mimic those of the CFT in the continuum, but display strong finite-size corrections. In order to significantly reduce finite-size lattice corrections, we then introduce
smoothers
, numerically optimized unitary transformations that locally re-arrange the quantum spin degrees of freedom. As an illustration of our proposals, we construct the multi-boundary TFD state with three and four boundaries for the Ising CFT. We use novel free fermion techniques that allow us to compute the multi-point correlation functions from the lattice wavefunctions, and compare them to the CFT results.

A first merit of our construction is that, once we have built a generalized TFD state on the lattice, we can numerically study its properties, including its entanglement properties. (This was also possible in
Balasubramanian et al. 2014
for so-called holographic CFTs, whereas our results are valid for any CFT for which we can find a lattice realization). Here we compute bipartite and multi-partite entanglement quantities of the multi-boundary TFD states for the Ising CFT. Furthermore, we identify ranges of parameters where their multipartite entanglement is either significant or negligible.

A second merit of our proposal is that it allows us to numerically extract the operator product expansion (OPE) coefficients and multi-point correlation functions of a CFT from a critical spin chain that realizes the latter, by computing overlaps between wavefunctions on the lattice. In recent years, related proposals were made that required however computing lattice versions of the CFT primary operators
Zou et al. 2020
;
Zou and Vidal 2020
. Here, no such lattice scaling operators are needed: the OPE coefficients follows solely from the computation of wavefunction overlaps.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
I
we introduce the multi-boundary generalization of the TFD state and show how the wavefunction is related to the multi-point correlation functions. In Sec.
II
we describe how the state can be constructed on the lattice and the use of smoothers to significantly reduce finite-size corrections. In Sec.
III
we review the correlation matrix formalism of the free fermion and derive new techniques that are useful for the construction of multi-boundary TFD states. In Sec.
IV
we present numerical results for multi-boundary TFD states for the Ising CFT. We conclude with further discussions in Sec.
V
.

I
Multi-boundary wormhole state

In a CFT, a state can be prepared by a path integral on a manifold with one or more boundaries. In this section we define our proposed class of multi-boundary TFD states for 1+1 CFTs, which can be prepared by a path integral on a strip-like geometry with multiple boundaries. We start with the familiar thermofield double state prepared by the path integral on an open cylinder and then generalize it to three and four-sided TFD states. We then show that multi-point correlation functions of the CFT are encoded in these wavefunctions.

I.1
Thermofield double state

The TFD state can be prepared by a Euclidean path integral over an open cylinder. Denote the circumference of the cylinder as
L
and the length as
\tau_{0}
. The Hilbert space is supported on the circles that wind around the cylinder. Let
\mathcal{H}^{{\textsl{\tiny CFT}}}
be the Hilbert space of the CFT and
H^{{\textsl{\tiny CFT}}}
be the Hamiltonian of the CFT. The Hilbert space is spanned by the eigenstates
|\psi_{\alpha}\rangle
of
H^{{\textsl{\tiny CFT}}}
, which is in one-to-one correspondence with the scaling operators
\psi_{\alpha}
. The energy of
|\psi_{\alpha}\rangle
is given by

E_{\alpha}=\frac{2\pi}{L}\left(\Delta_{\alpha}-\frac{c}{12}\right),

(1)

where
\Delta_{\alpha}
is the scaling dimension and
c
is the central charge. For example,
\alpha=I
denotes the ground state of te CFT. Let
T
be an antiunitary operator (typically chosen as the time reversal operator) that commutes with the Hamiltonian. Then the thermofield double (TFD) state can be defined on a doubled Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{12}=\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}
where
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
are isomorphic to
\mathcal{H}^{{\textsl{\tiny CFT}}}
,

|\psi(\tau_{0})\rangle=\sum_{\alpha}e^{-\tau_{0}E_{\alpha}}|\psi^{1}_{\alpha}\rangle\otimes|\psi^{2*}_{\alpha}\rangle,

(2)

where we have used the short-hand notation

|\psi^{*}_{\alpha}\rangle=T|\psi_{\alpha}\rangle.

(3)

One may interpret the path integral as evolving in imaginary time from a maximal entangled state
|\Omega_{1,2}\rangle
on
\mathcal{H}_{12}
,

|\psi(\tau_{0})\rangle=e^{-\tau_{0}H_{1}/2}e^{-\tau_{0}H^{*}_{2}/2}|\Omega_{1,2}\rangle,

(4)

where
H_{1}=H\otimes I
and
H^{*}_{2}=I\otimes THT^{-1}
, and

|\Omega_{1,2}\rangle=\sum_{\alpha}|\psi^{1}_{\alpha}\rangle\otimes|\psi^{2*}_{\alpha}\rangle.

(5)

We may write the maximal entangled state in an alternative way

|\Omega_{1,2}\rangle=\sum_{\alpha\beta}A_{\alpha\beta}|\psi^{1}_{\alpha}\rangle\otimes|\psi^{2*}_{\beta}\rangle,

(6)

where

A_{\alpha\beta}=\langle\psi_{\alpha}|\psi_{\beta}\rangle=\delta_{\alpha\beta}.

(7)

Denoting the wavefunction as

\tilde{A}_{\alpha\beta}(\tau_{0})=\langle\psi^{1}_{\alpha}\psi^{2*}_{\beta}|\psi({\tau_{0}})\rangle,

(8)

we find that the scaling dimensions are encoded in the wavefunction as

\frac{\tilde{A}_{\alpha\beta}(\tau_{0})}{{\tilde{A}}_{II}(\tau_{0})}=e^{-\frac{2\pi\Delta_{\alpha}\tau_{0}}{L}}A_{\alpha\beta}.

(9)

I.2
Three-sided TFD state

Next, we generalize the TFD state to three boundaries. The wave-function can be produced by the CFT path integral over a pants-like geometry (Fig.
1
). The geometry is composed of three cylinders with circumsference
L_{1},L_{2}
and
L_{3}
, where
L_{1}=L_{2}+L_{3}
. At time
\tau=0
, the geometry encounters a sharp split from one cylinder to two disjoint cylinders. Strictly speaking, the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{1}
does not factorize into
\mathcal{H}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{3}
in a quantum field theory. However, as is well known in the study of the entanglement entropy in CFTs, the split is sensible if a UV cutoff is introduced
Calabrese and Cardy 2004
;
Calabrese and Cardy 2009
;
Cardy and Tonni 2016
. Alternatively, one may think of the geometry as the limit of a 2D square lattice where the boundary condition changes sharply at
\tau=0
and the lattice spacing goes to zero (see also Sec.
II
).

Figure 1:
Geometries of the path integrals for multi-boundary TFD states. (a) An open cylinder which corresponds to the TFD state Eq. (
4
). (b) The pants-like geometry which corresponds to the three-sided TFD state Eq. (
10
). (c) The combination of two pants-like geometry which corresponds to the four-sided TFD state Eq. (
17
).

The quantum state prepared by the path integral on the pants geometry can be represented by

|\psi(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3})\rangle=e^{-\tau_{1}H_{1}}e^{-\tau_{2}H^{*}_{2}}e^{-\tau_{3}H^{*}_{3}}|\Omega_{1,23}\rangle,

(10)

where
H_{i}
represents the CFT Hamiltonian on the
i
-th circle, and
\tau_{i}
is the length of the
i
-th cylinder. The state
|\Omega_{1,23}\rangle
is an entangled state on
\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{23}
,

|\Omega_{1,23}\rangle=\sum_{\alpha\beta\gamma}A_{\alpha\beta\gamma}|\psi^{1}_{\alpha}\rangle\otimes|\psi^{2*}_{\beta}\rangle\otimes|\psi^{3*}_{\gamma}\rangle

(11)

where

A_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=\langle\psi^{1}_{\alpha}|\psi^{2}_{\beta}\psi^{3}_{\gamma}\rangle

(12)

Again, the overlap only makes sense if one introduces a UV cutoff (such as a lattice) to the CFT. The coefficients
A_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
is completely determined by the conformal data. It can be shown, through a singular confomral map that maps the three-sided cylinder to the complex plane, that in the case of
L_{1}=2L_{2}=2L_{3}
and all indices representing primary states,

\frac{A_{\alpha\beta\gamma}}{A_{III}}=2^{\Delta_{\alpha}-2\Delta_{\beta}-2\Delta_{\gamma}}C_{\alpha\beta\gamma},

(13)

where
\Delta_{\alpha}
is the scaling dimension of the primary operator and
C_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
is the OPE coefficient. The coefficients involving descendant states can also determined by conformal invariance. A detailed proof is presented in the appendix.

Finally, the three-sided TFD state encodes the conformal data in the following way,

\frac{\tilde{A}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3})}{{\tilde{A}}_{III}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3})}=e^{-\frac{2\pi\Delta_{\alpha}\tau_{1}}{L_{1}}-\frac{2\pi\Delta_{\beta}\tau_{2}}{L_{2}}-\frac{2\pi\Delta_{\gamma}\tau_{3}}{L_{3}}}A_{\alpha\beta\gamma},

(14)

where

\tilde{A}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3})=\langle\psi^{1}_{\alpha}\psi^{2*}_{\beta}\psi^{3*}_{\gamma}|\psi(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3})\rangle

(15)

is the wavefunction. Below we will compare Eq. (
13
) with numerical results on the lattice. We also note that Eq. (
13
) as well as Eq. (
14
) leads to a way to compute the OPE coefficients from the wavefunctions,

\displaystyle C_{\alpha\beta\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{2\pi\Delta_{\alpha}\tau_{1}}{L_{1}}+\frac{2\pi\Delta_{\beta}\tau_{2}}{L_{2}}+\frac{2\pi\Delta_{\gamma}\tau_{3}}{L_{3}}}\times

(16)

\displaystyle 2^{-\Delta_{\alpha}+2\Delta_{\beta}+2\Delta_{\gamma}}\frac{\tilde{A}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3})}{{\tilde{A}}_{III}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3})}.

I.3
Four-sided TFD state

The four-sided TFD state is defined by the path integral in the geometry shown in Fig.
1
where two pants are connected through the cylinder in the middle with length
\tau_{0}
. Let the Hamiltonian on the four sides be
H_{1},H_{2},H_{3}
and
H_{4}
and the Hamiltonian on the cylinder in the middle be
H_{0}
. Then the four-sided TFD state is

\displaystyle|\psi(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3},\tau_{4};\tau_{0})\rangle

(17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\tau_{1}H_{1}}e^{-\tau_{2}H_{2}}e^{-\tau_{3}H^{*}_{3}}e^{-\tau_{4}H^{*}_{4}}|\Omega_{12,34}(\tau_{0})\rangle,

where

|\Omega_{12,34}(\tau_{0})\rangle=\sum_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}A_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}(\tau_{0})|\psi^{1}_{\alpha}\rangle\otimes|\psi^{2}_{\beta}\rangle\otimes|\psi^{3*}_{\gamma}\rangle\otimes|\psi^{4*}_{\delta}\rangle

(18)

and

A_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}(\tau_{0})=\langle\psi^{1}_{\alpha}\psi^{2}_{\beta}|e^{-\tau_{0}H_{0}}|\psi^{3}_{\gamma}\psi^{4}_{\delta}\rangle.

(19)

As in the three-sided TFD, the expression makes sense only if a UV cutoff (lattice spacing) is introduced. In the limit of the lattice spacing going to zero, it can be shown, through a conformal map to the complex plane, that

\displaystyle\frac{A_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}(\tau_{0})}{A_{IIII}(\tau_{0})}=2^{-\Delta_{\alpha}-\Delta_{\beta}}\left(\frac{e^{4\pi\tau_{0}/L_{0}}-1}{2e^{6\pi\tau_{0}/L_{0}}}\right)^{\Delta_{\gamma}+\Delta_{\delta}}\times

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{\alpha}(i)\phi_{\beta}(-i)\phi_{\gamma}(ie^{-2\pi\tau_{0}/L_{0}})\phi_{\delta}(-ie^{-2\pi\tau_{0}/L_{0}})\rangle_{pl},

(20)

which involves a four-point correlation function of the CFT on the plane. As is well known in CFT, the four-point correlation function can be decomposed into different fusion channels. The fusion channels clearly agree in Eq. (
20
) and the geometry in Fig.
1
. In the limit of
\tau_{0}\rightarrow 0
, the first point and the third point are close, which corresponds to the so-called
t
channel of fusion. In the opposite limit where
\tau_{0}\rightarrow\infty
, the last two points are close, which corresponds to the so-called
s
channel of fusion.

Finally, the four-sided TFD state encodes the scaling dimensions and OPE coefficients in the following way,

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{A}_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3},\tau_{4};\tau_{0})}{{\tilde{A}}_{IIII}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3},\tau_{4};\tau_{0})}

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{2\pi\Delta_{\alpha}\tau_{1}}{L_{1}}-\frac{2\pi\Delta_{\beta}\tau_{2}}{L_{2}}-\frac{2\pi\Delta_{\gamma}\tau_{3}}{L_{3}}-\frac{2\pi\Delta_{\delta}\tau_{4}}{L_{4}}}A_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}(\tau_{0}),

where

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3},\tau_{4};\tau_{0})

(22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\psi^{1}_{\alpha}\psi^{2}_{\beta}\psi^{3*}_{\gamma}\psi^{4*}_{\delta}|\psi(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3},\tau_{4};\tau_{0})\rangle

is the wavefunction.

II
Construction of multi-boundary wormhole states on the lattice

In the following we will construct the state
|\psi\rangle
on the lattice and compute several entanglement quantities. In general, there are finite-size corrections if we try to compute any CFT quantity using a critical lattice model with a finite number of sites. Additional finite-size correction is present here because of the singularity of the pants geometry. We will briefly discuss how to use a ”smoother” to reduce the finite-size corrections.

II.1
Setup

We will consider a critical quantum spin chain with Hamiltonian density
h_{i}
. The Hamiltonian on
N
sites is

H=\sum_{i=1}^{N}h_{i}.

(23)

We will use periodic boundary conditions throughout the paper. The low-energy eigenstates are in one to one correspondence with the CFT states. The energies correspond to the scaling dimensions of the CFT
Blöte et al. 1986
,

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{N}\left(\Delta_{\alpha}-\frac{c}{12}\right)+O(N^{-x}),

(24)

where
O(N^{-x})
with
x>1
denotes the finite-size correction. Note that Eq. (
24
) may require a particular normalization of the Hamiltonian density
h_{i}
. The normalization is such that the speed of light is one.

Next, we construct the three-sided wormhole state. Let the number of sites of the
i
-th side be
N_{i}
and
N_{1}=N_{2}+N_{3}
. The Hilbert spaces on the three sides satisfy
\mathcal{H}_{1}=\mathcal{H}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{3}
. The spins are labelled by
i=1,2\cdots 2N_{1}
, where the first side contains spins
1
to
N_{1}
, the second side contains spins
N_{1}+1
to
N_{1}+N_{2}
and the third side contains the rest of the spins. The Hamiltonians on the three sides are given by

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}h_{i}

(25)

\displaystyle H_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=N_{1}+1}^{N_{1}+N_{2}}h_{i}

(26)

\displaystyle H_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=N_{1}+N_{2}+1}^{2N_{1}}h_{i}.

(27)

Note that periodic boundary conditions are assumed for each of the subsystem, e.g. in
H_{1}
the spin 1 is identified with spin
N_{1}+1
, and in
H_{2}
the spin
N_{1}+1
is identified with the spin
N_{1}+N_{2}+1
, see Fig.
2
for an illustration.

The maximal entangled state is given by

|\Omega_{1,23}\rangle=\prod_{i=1}^{N_{1}}|\Psi_{i,N_{1}+i}\rangle

(28)

where

|\Psi_{i,j}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{m=1}^{d}|m_{i}m_{j}\rangle

(29)

is a maximal entangled state between two sites and
d
is the dimension of the Hilbert space on each lattice site (e.g.
d=2
for a spin-
1/2
chain discussed below). The three-sided wormhole state can then be constructed by using the imaginary time evolution,

|\psi_{3}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3})\rangle=e^{-\tau_{1}H_{1}-\tau_{2}H^{*}_{2}-\tau_{3}H^{*}_{3}}|\Omega_{1,23}\rangle.

(30)

As we will numerically show, this state is the lattice analogue of the three-sided wormhole state in the sense that the wavefunction

\tilde{A}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3})=\langle\psi_{\alpha}\psi^{*}_{\beta}\psi^{*}_{\gamma}|\psi_{3}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3})\rangle

(31)

satisfies Eq. (
14
) with finite-size corrections that decay with
N
.

Analogously, we may construct the four-sided wormhole state. Let the number of sites of the four sides be
N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}
and
N_{4}
, and
N_{1}+N_{2}=N_{3}+N_{4}=N_{0}
. The Hilbert spaces satisfy
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}=\mathcal{H}_{3}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{4}
. Then the Hamiltonian on the circles are

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}h_{i}

(32)

\displaystyle H_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=N_{1}+1}^{N_{0}}h_{i}

(33)

\displaystyle H_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=N_{0}+1}^{N_{0}+N_{3}}h_{i}

(34)

\displaystyle H_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=N_{0}+N_{3}+1}^{2N_{0}}h_{i}

(35)

\displaystyle H_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N_{0}}h_{i}.

(36)

One starts with the maximal entangled state

|\Omega_{12,34}(0)\rangle=\prod_{i=1}^{N_{0}}|\Psi_{i,N_{0}+i}\rangle

(37)

on
\mathcal{H}_{0}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{*}_{0}
, then a TFD state can be constructed by imaginary time evolution with
H_{0}
,

|\Omega_{12,34}(\tau_{0})\rangle=e^{-\tau_{0}H_{0}/2}e^{-\tau_{0}H^{*}_{0}/2}|\Omega_{12,34}(0)\rangle

(38)

The four-sided wormhole state can be obtained by further imaginary time evolution on four subsystems,

\displaystyle|\psi_{4}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3},\tau_{4};\tau_{0})\rangle

(39)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\tau_{1}H_{1}-\tau_{2}H_{2}-\tau_{3}H^{*}_{3}-\tau_{4}H^{*}_{4}}|\Omega_{12,34}(\tau_{0})\rangle.

Again, as we will show numerically, the lattice state is analogous to the continuous counterpart which satisfies Eq. (
21
) for primary states.

II.2
Finite-size corrections and the smoother

In this subsection we analyze the finite-size corrections in Eq. (
31
). The imaginary time evolution contributes a factor
e^{-\tau_{1}E_{\alpha}-\tau_{2}E_{\beta}-\tau_{3}E_{\gamma}}
to the wavefunction, since the states
|\psi_{\alpha}\rangle,|\psi^{*}_{\beta}\rangle
and
|\psi^{*}_{\gamma}\rangle
are eigenstates of
H_{1}
,
H_{2}
and
H^{*}_{3}
, respectively,

\tilde{A}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=e^{-\tau_{1}E_{\alpha}-\tau_{2}E_{\beta}-\tau_{3}E_{\gamma}}A_{\alpha\beta\gamma}

(40)

where

A_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=\langle\psi^{1}_{\alpha}\psi^{2*}_{\beta}\psi^{3*}_{\gamma}|\Omega_{1,23}\rangle.

(41)

Identifying the circumferences
L
’s with the number of spins
N
’s, the factor agrees with the prefactor in Eq. (
14
) up to the finite-size correction in the energies, Eq. (
24
).

Additional finite-size corrections come from
A_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
. It turns out that the finite-size corrections in
A_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
are much stronger than the finite-size corrections in the energies. We will make use of
smoothers
to reduce the finite-size corrections in
A_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
, analogous to those of Refs.
Milsted and Vidal 2018a
;
Milsted and Vidal 2018b
;
Milsted and Vidal 2018c
.

In order to obtain the smoother, first note that

A_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=\langle\psi^{1}_{\alpha}|\psi^{2}_{\beta}\psi^{3}_{\gamma}\rangle

(42)

by definition of the maximal entangled state. The strong finite-size correction is due to the sharp change of the boundary condition when we take the overlap. Let
U_{23}
be a local unitary operator of size
4l
which acts on spins on
\mathcal{H}_{2}
and
\mathcal{H}_{3}
near the boundary condition change, i.e., the spins labelled by
N_{1}+1
to
N_{1}+l
,
N_{1}+N_{2}-l+1
to
N_{1}+N_{2}+l
and
2N_{1}-l+1
to
2N_{1}
. See Fig.
2
for an illustration. We find
U_{23}
by an optimization

\max_{U}|\langle I^{1}|U_{23}|I^{2}I^{3}\rangle|^{2}.

(43)

The physical meaning of the unitary
U_{23}
is that it ”smoothens” the connection of the copy
2
and copy
3
such that the resulting state overlaps most with the low-energy subspace of the copy
1
. It is important to note that the unitary
U
is optimized only once with a fairly large system and the same unitary is applied for all system sizes. We will show numerically that the wavefunction
\tilde{A}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
satisfies Eq. (
14
) with a smaller finite-size correction.

II.3
The Ising model

The critical Ising model is defined on a spin-1/2 lattice,

H=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}X_{i+1}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}Z_{i}\right),

(44)

where periodic boundary conditions are assumed. The normalization constant
1/2
is such that Eq. (
24
) holds.

Using a Jordan-Wigner transformation one may map the spin model to the free fermion model on
2N
Majorana fermions
\gamma_{i}
,

\{\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j}\}=2\delta_{ij}.

(45)

The Hamiltonian is

H=-\frac{i}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2N}\gamma_{i}\gamma_{i+1}.

(46)

There are two possible boundary conditions, the NS boundary condition
\gamma_{2N+1}=-\gamma_{1}
or R boundary condition
\gamma_{2N+1}=\gamma_{1}
. The eigenstates of the spin Hamiltonian with even/odd
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry are mapped to eigenstates of the fermion model with NS/R boundary condition.

In the following we will use the free fermion model instead of the spin model to simplify numerical simulations. It should be noted that a numerical simulation of the spin model is also possible, by using tensor network techniques developed in Refs.
Zou et al. 2018
;
Milsted and Vidal 2018b
.

III
Free fermion techniques

In this section we outline the free fermion techniques that are used to construct the multi-boundary wormhole state for the free fermion model. We first review the correlation matrix formalism and then introduce two new techniques, the imaginary time evolution and the construction of the TFD state.

III.1
Review of the correlation matrix formalism

Here we work with Gaussian Majorana fermion states. A Gaussian state (either pure or mixed) is completely determined by the two-point correlation functions,

M_{ij}=\mathrm{Tr}(-i\rho(\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j}-\delta_{ij}))

(47)

The matrix
M
is known as the correlation matrix. It is a real, skew-symmetric,
2N\times 2N
matrix. It can be block-diagonalized by an orthogonal matrix,

M=O\Gamma O^{T},

(48)

where
O\in O(2N)
and

\Gamma=\oplus_{i=1}^{N}n_{i}(i\sigma^{y})\equiv\mathrm{diag}(n_{i})\otimes i\sigma^{y}

(49)

is block diagonal and
n_{i}\in[-1,1]
. A pure state has
n_{i}=\pm 1
and thus
M^{2}=-I
. The Von-Neumann entropy of the density matrix is given by
Peschel and Eisler 2009

S(\rho)\equiv-\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\log\rho)=H\left(\frac{1+n_{i}}{2}\right)+H\left(\frac{1-n_{i}}{2}\right),

(50)

where

H(x)=-\sum_{i}x_{i}\log x_{i}.

(51)

The expectation value of the fermion parity

P=(-i)^{N}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{2i-1}\gamma_{2i}

(52)

is given by

\mathrm{Tr}(\rho P)=\mathrm{Pf}(M)

(53)

as a result of the Wick theorem, where
\mathrm{Pf}
denotes the Pfaffian.

Given two states
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
with correlation matrices
M_{1},M_{2}
, if at least one state is pure, then their overlap is
Bravyi 2005
;
Bravyi and Gosset 2017

\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{1}\rho_{2})=2^{-N}|\mathrm{Pf}{(M_{1}+M_{2})}|.

(54)

This expression will be useful for computing the wavefunctions from the correlation matrix.

Let
H
be a quadratic Hamiltonian

H=-\frac{i}{4}\sum_{ij}h_{ij}\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j},

(55)

where
h
is a real, skew-symmetric,
2N\times 2N
matrix. The matrix
h
admits a Schur decomposition

h=O(\mathrm{diag}(\epsilon_{i})\otimes i\sigma^{y})O^{T}.

(56)

then the thermal state
\rho_{th}(\tau)\sim e^{-\tau H}
has correlation matrix

M_{th}(\tau)=O(\mathrm{diag}(-tanh{(\tau\epsilon_{i}/2)})\otimes i\sigma^{y})O^{T}

(57)

In the limit of
\tau\rightarrow+\infty
, we obtain the correlation matrix for the ground state

M_{gs}=O(\mathrm{diag}(-\mathrm{sgn}{(\epsilon_{i})})\otimes i\sigma^{y})O^{T}.

(58)

Every eigenstate of the Hamiltonian has a correlation matrix of the form

M(\{n_{i}\})=O(\mathrm{diag}(n_{i})\otimes i\sigma^{y})O^{T},

(59)

where each eigenstate corresponds to a choice of
n_{i}\in\{-1,1\}
for
i
from
1
to
N
. The energy of that eigenstate is given by

E(\{n_{i}\})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}n_{i}\epsilon_{i}/2.

(60)

The ground state corresponds to
n_{i}=-\mathrm{sgn}(\epsilon_{i})
.

Finally, if we have two Gaussian fermion states
|\psi_{1}\rangle
and
|\psi_{2}\rangle
with correlation matrices
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, then the state of composite system has the correlation matrix
M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
.

III.2
Imaginary time evolution

Let
H
be a quadratic Hamiltonian and
|\psi\rangle
be a pure state with correlation matrix
M
. Consider the imaginary time evolution

|\psi(\tau)\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{Z(\tau)}}e^{-\tau H}|\psi\rangle

(61)

where
Z(\tau)
is a normalization constant. The resulting state
|\psi(\tau)\rangle
is a Gaussian state with the correlation matrix

\displaystyle M(\tau)=M_{th}(2\tau)-G(\tau)(M+M_{th}(2\tau))^{-1}G(\tau),

(62)

where

G(\tau)=\sqrt{I+M^{2}_{th}(2\tau)}.

(63)

Note that at
\tau=0
,
M(0)=-M^{-1}=M
, as
M^{2}=-I
for a pure state. As
\tau\rightarrow\infty
, the thermal state goes into the ground state,
M_{th}(\tau)\rightarrow M_{gs}
and
M(\tau)\rightarrow M_{gs}
.

The normalization constant is

Z(\tau)=\prod_{i}\cosh(\tau\epsilon_{i})|\mathrm{Pf}(M+M_{th}(2\tau))|.

(64)

III.3
Construction of TFD state

Let
M
be the correlation matrix of a density matrix
\rho
on
2N
Majorana fermions, and the spectral decomposition of the density matrix be

\rho=\sum_{\alpha}\lambda^{2}_{\alpha}|\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha|.

(65)

The canonical purification is a pure state on
4N
Majorana fermions defined by

|\sqrt{\rho}\rangle\equiv\sum_{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}|\alpha\rangle\otimes|\alpha^{*}\rangle.

(66)

Note that if
\rho
is the thermal state of a local Hamiltonian, then the canonical purification coincides with the TFD state. As we show in the appendix, the correlation matrix of the canonical purification is

M_{\sqrt{\rho}}=\begin{bmatrix}M&\tilde{M}\\
-\tilde{M}&-M\end{bmatrix}

(67)

where

\tilde{M}=O\left(\mathrm{diag}\left({\sqrt{1-n^{2}_{i}}}\right)\otimes I_{2}\right)O^{T},

(68)

given the
n_{i}
and the matrix
O
in Eqs. (
48
) and (
49
). In particular, if the original state
\rho
is maximally mixed state with
M=0
, then the canonical purification is the maximal entangled state
|\Omega\rangle
. The correlation matrix of the state
|\Omega\rangle
is

M_{\Omega}=\begin{bmatrix}0_{2N}&I_{2N}\\
-I_{2N}&0_{2N}\end{bmatrix}

(69)

In order to compute the correlation matrix for the multi-boundary TFD state, we need to use the imaginary time evolution Eq. (
62
) and the correlation matrix Eq. (
69
) for the initial state. The generic expression Eq. (
67
) is also useful when we compute the multi-partite entanglement measure next section.

IV.2
Entanglement quantities

In the context of holography, entanglement properties of the CFT state on the boundary reflect geometric properties of the bulk
Ryu and Takayanagi 2006
;
Blanco et al. 2013
;
Takayanagi and Umemoto 2018
;
Dutta and Faulkner 2021
;
Nguyen et al. 2018
. For example, the Ryu-Takayanagi formula
Ryu and Takayanagi 2006
connects the entanglement entropy of the boundary CFT to the area of the minimal surface in the bulk. However, the entanglement entropy only characterizes bipartite entanglement and multi-partite entanglement plays an important role in many-body systems. There are vast number of multi-partite entanglement quantities, and we focus on the reflected entropy
S_{R}

Dutta and Faulkner 2021
. Given a pure state on three parties
1,2
and
3
, we first trace out subsystem
1
to obtain the reduced density matrix
\rho_{23}
. The reflected entropy is defined as

S_{R}(2:3)=S_{22^{*}}(|\sqrt{\rho}\rangle_{232^{*}3^{*}}),

(71)

where
|\sqrt{\rho}\rangle_{232^{*}3^{*}}
is known as the canonical purification, which is the square root of the density matrix regarded as a state in the doubled Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{23}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{*}_{23}
. The quantity is interesting because it is conjectured to be dual to the entanglement wedge cross-section in the context of holography
Dutta and Faulkner 2021
. Furthermore, it has been shown that the derived quantity

h(2:3)=S_{R}(2:3)-I(2:3),

(72)

represents the amount of multi-partite entanglement beyond the GHZ type
Zou et al. 2021
. We will use the quantity
h
as a measure of multi-partite entanglement.

The three-sided TFD state is naturally split into three subsystems. we will study how the bipartite and multi-partite entanglement quantities change with the parameters
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
and
\tau_{3}
. The lattice construction enables us to compute them numerically without referring to holographic conjectures.

Before going into the numerical results, it is useful to consider several limits as in Ref.
Balasubramanian et al. 2014
. If one of
\tau_{i}
’s goes to infinity, then that the copy of CFT is projected onto the ground state and there is no entanglement between this copy and the rest two copies. In the opposite limit, if
\tau_{1}\rightarrow 0
, then the reduced density matrix
\rho_{23}
becomes the tensor product of two thermal state
\rho_{23}\sim e^{-2\tau_{2}H_{2}}e^{-2\tau_{3}H_{3}}
and
S_{R}(2:3)=I(2:3)=0
. It is therefore expected that multi-partite entanglement is only significant when
\tau_{1}
is not too large or too small.

For simplicity we consider the case where
\tau_{2}=\tau_{3}
and we fix the boundary conditions of the three sides to be all NS. The entanglement quantities with respect to
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
is shown in Fig.

and Fig.

.

V
Conclusion

In this paper we have introduced a class of multi-boundary generalizations of TFD states. We have found how multi-point correlation functions of the CFT are encoded in the wavefunctions. Furthermore, we have proposed lattice constructions of the multi-boundary TFD state for critical quantum spin chains, and introduced smoothers that significantly reduce the finite-size corrections. Our lattice construction allows, on the one hand, for numerical explorations of the corresponding wavefunctions. They also lead to a scheme to numerically extract conformal data such as operator product expansion coefficients OPE of a CFT by computing wavefunction overlaps (as opposed to related techniques in Refs.
Zou et al. 2020
;
Zou and Vidal 2020
, which require computing a lattice version of the CFT primary operators). Finally, for illustrative purposes, we have numerically constructed the states for the Ising CFT using novel free fermion techniques and computed entanglement quantities.

The multi-boundary TFD states defined in this work are associated with path integral on surfaces that are flat except in a neighborhood around the singular points. This is mostly due to simplification in numerical techniques. More sophisticated tensor network techniques, such as a imaginary time evolution combined with MERA
Milsted and Vidal 2018a
;
Milsted and Vidal 2018c
;
Milsted and Vidal 2018b
can be used to obtain a lattice approximation to path integrals on more general, curved manifolds. In addition to correlation functions on the plane, one can also compute correlation functions on more complicated manifolds by combining several pants-like geometries. For example, the reduced density matrix of the first subsystem in the three-sided TFD state encodes two-point correlation functions on the torus.

Finally, the states considered in this paper may have holographic interpretations in the context of holographic CFTs. Our work enables a direct computation of entanglement quantities, which may be compared with holography qualitatively or even quantitatively.

\delta h
is a real antisymmetric matrix with a small norm. The cost function to the first order is

f(U)=|\langle\psi_{T}|\psi_{0}\rangle|^{2}+\delta h_{ij}G_{ij},

(109)

where

G_{ij}=\langle\psi_{T}|\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j}|\psi_{0}\rangle\langle\psi_{0}|\psi_{T}\rangle+c.c.

(110)

Now we use the following result from Ref.
Bravyi and Gosset 2017
:

\frac{\langle\psi_{T}|\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j}|\psi_{0}\rangle}{\langle\psi_{T}|\psi_{0}\rangle}=i\Delta_{ij},

(111)

where

\Delta=C_{T}-(I+iC_{T})(C_{0}+C_{T})^{-1}(I-iC_{T}).

(112)

Then

G_{ij}=|\mathrm{Pf}(C_{0}+C_{T})|\times i(\Delta_{ij}-\Delta^{*}_{ij})

(113)

In the gradient descent method we choose

\delta h_{ij}=\delta tG_{ij},

(114)

where
\delta t
is a tuning parameter that makes
f(U)
largest. In the next step we substitute
C_{0}
with

C_{0}\rightarrow e^{\delta h}C_{0}e^{-\delta h}

(115)

and repeat. The norm of gradient is
||G||
, and the optimization error is on the order of
||G||^{2}
. We stop the optimization at
||G||<10^{-3}
, where
U
is already sufficiently close to the optimum.

B.2
Imaginary time evolution

Let
H
be a quadratic Hamiltonian

H=-\frac{i}{4}\sum_{ij}\gamma_{i}h_{ij}\gamma_{j}

(116)

and
|\psi_{0}\rangle
be a pure state with correlation matrix
M_{0}
. The imaginary time evolution

|\psi(\tau)\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{Z(\tau)}}e^{-\tau H}|\psi_{0}\rangle

(117)

also reproduces a Gaussian state. The normalization constant equals

Z(\tau)=\langle\psi_{0}|e^{-2\tau H}|\psi_{0}\rangle.

(118)

To compute this, first we schur decompose the Hamiltonian matrix

h=O(\mathrm{diag}(\epsilon_{i})\otimes i\sigma^{y})O^{T}

(119)

Let

\rho_{th}=e^{-2\tau H}/Z_{th},

(120)

be the thermal state at inverse temperature
2\tau
, then

Z_{th}=2^{N}\prod_{i}cosh(\tau\epsilon_{i})

(121)

Let
\rho_{0}=|\psi_{0}\rangle\langle\psi_{0}|
, the normalization constant can be computed by

Z(\tau)=Z_{th}\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{0}\rho_{th})

(122)

and

\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{0}\rho_{th})=2^{-N}|\mathrm{Pf}(M_{0}+M_{th})|,

(123)

where

M_{th}=O(\mathrm{diag}(-tanh{\tau\epsilon_{i}})\otimes i\sigma^{y})O^{T}

(124)

is the correlation matrix of the thermal state. Combining together the above we obtain

Z(\tau)=\prod_{i}\cosh(\tau\epsilon_{i})|\mathrm{Pf}(M+M_{th})|.

(125)

Next, we derive the correlation matrix of the output state

M(\tau)_{ij}=\frac{1}{Z(\tau)}\mathrm{Tr}(-i(\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j}-\delta_{ij})e^{-\tau H}\rho_{0}e^{-\tau H})

(126)

Using the cyclic property of the trace, we obtain

M(\tau)_{ij}=\frac{\mathrm{Tr}(-i(e^{-\tau H}\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j}e^{\tau H}-\delta_{ij})\rho_{th}\rho_{0})}{\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{th}\rho_{0})}

(127)

Let
\Delta
be

\Delta_{ij}=\frac{\mathrm{Tr}(-i(\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j}-\delta_{ij})\rho_{th}\rho_{0})}{\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{th}\rho_{0})},

(128)

which equals
Bravyi and Gosset 2017

\Delta=M_{th}-(I+iM_{th})(M_{0}+M_{th})^{-1}(I-iM_{th}),

(129)

then

M(\tau)=e^{-i\tau h}\Delta e^{i\tau h}.

(130)

Using the factor that
M^{2}_{0}=-I
and with a bit matrix manipulations, we obtain

M(\tau)=M_{th}-\sqrt{I+M^{2}_{th}}(M_{0}+M_{th})^{-1}\sqrt{I+M^{2}_{th}}.

(131)

B.3
Canonical purification

Given a Hamiltonian
H
and an antiunitary operator
T
, the thermofield double can be defined by

|\beta\rangle=e^{-\frac{\beta}{4}(H_{L}+H_{R})}|\Omega\rangle,

(132)

where
H_{L}=H\otimes I
and
H_{R}=I\otimes THT^{-1}
,
|Omega\rangle
is the maximal entangled state in the doubled Hilbert space. In our convention, we choose the antiunitary operator
T
such that

T\gamma_{i}T^{-1}=\gamma_{i}.

(133)

This implies that

THT^{-1}=-H.

(134)

The maximal entangled state is the simultaneous eigenstate of
-i\gamma_{iL}\gamma_{iR}
with eigenvalue
1
. The correlation matrix us

M_{\Omega}=\begin{bmatrix}0&I\\
-I&0\end{bmatrix}

(135)

Applying the imaginary time evolution as in Eq. (
132
) and Eq. (
131
) we obtain the correlation matrix of
|\beta\rangle
,

M_{\beta}=\begin{bmatrix}M&\tilde{M}\\
-\tilde{M}&-M\end{bmatrix}

(136)

where

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O(\mathrm{diag}(n_{i})\otimes i\sigma^{y})O^{T}

(137)

\displaystyle\tilde{M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O(\mathrm{diag}(\sqrt{1-n^{2}_{i}})\otimes I)O^{T}

(138)

\displaystyle n_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-tanh(\beta\epsilon_{i}/2).

(139)

Note that the reduced density matrix of the left/right subsystem is a thermal state with inverse temperature
\beta
and Hamiltonian
H_{L}
/
H_{R}
.

Given a Gaussian reduced density matrix
\rho
with correlation matrix
M
, we may define the entanglement Hamiltonian by

\rho=e^{-H_{e}}.

(140)

The canonical purification is defined by

|\sqrt{\rho}\rangle=e^{-\frac{1}{4}(H_{eL}+H_{eR})}|\Omega\rangle,

(141)

which is the same as Eq. (
132
) except that
H
is substituted by the entanglement Hamiltonian
H_{e}
. Furthermore, the entanglement Hamiltonian of a Guassian state is always quadratic. Thus, repeating the same calculation that leads to Eq. (
136
) we obtain the same formula, except that the
O
and
n_{i}
in Eq. (
137
) come from the Schur decomposition of
M
rather than the Hamiltonian
h
.

Finally, in order to compute the reflected entropy, one starts with the correlation matrix
M_{\sqrt{\rho}}
of the canonical purification and compute the entanglement entropy of the given subsystem.

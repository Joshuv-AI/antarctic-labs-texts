Isotrivial Lagrangian fibrations of compact hyper-kähler manifolds
Yoon-Joo Kim, Radu Laza, Olivier Martin

Abstract
. This article initiates the study of isotrivial Lagrangian fibrations of compact hyper-Kähler manifolds. We present four foundational results that extend well-known facts about isotrivial elliptic fibrations of K3 surfaces. First, we prove that smooth fibers of an isotrivial Lagrangian fibration are isogenous to a power of an elliptic curve. Second, we exhibit a dichotomy between two types of isotrivial Lagrangian fibrations, which we call A and B. Third, we give a classification result for type A isotrivial Lagrangian fibrations. Namely, if a type A isotrivial Lagrangian fibration admits a rational section, then it is birational to one of two straightforward examples of isotrivial fibrations of hyper-Kähler manifolds of K3 [ n ] \text{K3}^{[n]} -type and Kum n \text{Kum}_{n} -type. Finally, we prove that a genericity assumption on the smooth fiber of an isotrivial Lagrangian fibration ensures that the fibration is of type A.



1.
Introduction

Hyper-Kähler manifolds are one of the three building blocks of compact Kähler manifolds with trivial canonical bundles. Due to their rich geometry and relation to other objects of interest, they have become the subject of intense study over the past decades. However, only few examples of such manifolds are known, namely two deformation types,
\text{K3}^{[n]}
and
\textup{Kum}_{n}
, in each even dimension
2n\geq 4
, and two sporadic deformation types, OG6 and OG10, in dimensions 6 and 10. A central question in the field is whether there are finitely many deformation types in each dimension.

One possible approach to this problem is via Lagrangian fibrations, as in
[
Saw16
]
. Specifically, it is expected that all deformations types of hyper-Kähler manifolds admit specializations to Lagrangian-fibered hyper-Kähler manifolds, thereby possibly reducing this boundedness problem to the study of families of abelian varieties. Bakker
[
Bak22
]
, improving earlier results of van Geemen–Voisin
[
vGV16
]
, showed that Lagrangian fibrations are either isotrivial or have maximal variation. Since isotrivial Lagrangian fibrations are relatively tractable they are particularly interesting objects of study in the search for new deformation types.

2.3.

In this subsection, we study the cohomology of the intermediate base

(

Let us first observe that the cohomology of

2.18

acts trivially on the cohomology of

\displaystyle H^{*}(Z,\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{*}(F\times\tilde{U},\mathbb{Q})^{\tilde{G}_{\tr}}

\displaystyle\cong\Big(H^{*}(F,\mathbb{Q})\otimes H^{*}(\tilde{U},\mathbb{Q})\Big)^{\tilde{G}_{\tr}}

\displaystyle\cong H^{*}(F,\mathbb{Q})\otimes H^{*}(\tilde{U},\mathbb{Q})^{\tilde{G}_{\tr}}\cong H^{*}(F,\mathbb{Q})\otimes H^{*}(U,\mathbb{Q}).

All these isomorphisms are obviously isomorphisms of mixed Hodge structures.

The following is the main result of this subsection, illustrating again the rigid cohomological behavior of the intermediate base

. It will play an important role in both

1.4
and

1.6
.

.

There exists a generically finite

-equivariant morphism

to a simple

-abelian torsor

If

If

Proof of

.

-equivariant Hodge substructure and determines a

-equivariant homomorphism

, where

-equivariant isogeny on the left. Its kernel

. We deduce that the composition

is defined over

. This implies that

is a multiple of

. Finally,

must be of the form

since it contains

with multiplicity one by

2.23
.
∎

In this section we discuss examples of isotrivial Lagrangian fibrations. In particular, we revisit

\textup{K3}^{[n]}
or
\textup{Kum}_{n}
-fibrations. As discussed in the introduction, we are neither able to produce nor to exclude an isotrivial fibration in the deformation type of LSV fibrations of manifolds of OG10 type

]
(see however

).

Furthermore, if

is not square free, hyper-Kähler manifolds of

[

-fibrations, these Lagrangian fibrations do not admit a rational section (even topologically). The first occurrence of such an additional fibration on a hyper-Kähler manifold of
\text{K3}^{[n]}
type is the case

admits a symplectic resolution which is a hyper-Kähler manifold of OG10 type, and the Lagrangian fibration

deforms to the LSV fibration associated to the intermediate Jacobian fibration for cubic fourfolds
3
3

3

can be interpreted as the LSV fibration associated to a period point belonging to the

. It is clear that

3.3.
The second isotrivial fibration

Another interesting byproduct of

is

3.12
.

The Lagrangian fibration
\pi:X\longrightarrow B
uniquely determines a second rational isotrivial Lagrangian fibration
\pi^{\prime}:X\dashrightarrow B^{\prime}
to a projective
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano variety

.

Proof.

As mentioned,
\pi^{\prime}
is constructed by taking the quotient of

in

. Let us first show that its general fiber is Lagrangian. Let

into

, where the first map
(p^{\prime},p)
is finite étale by

and the second map is the projection onto the first factor. The pullback homomorphism

2.18

is
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano by

2.15

∎

Proof of
Theorem

1.3
.

Corollary

2.12
shows that

is isogenous to a power of a simple abelian variety. It therefore suffices to show that

has an isogeny factor of dimension
1
. Let
\pi^{\prime}:X\dashrightarrow B^{\prime}
be the second fibration from

3.12
. We proved that the variety

\mathbb{Q}
-Fano. If a finite quotient

of an abelian torsor

For the sake of completeness, let us also present a simple argument which does not rely on

, Lem 4.2]
. Let

be the quotient map. Since

3.13

Let

.

Let

be a general fiber of the rational (isotrivial) Lagrangian fibration

above. Then one can prove that

4.
General singular fibers and the canonical bundle formula

So far, our analysis has been focused on the smooth fibration
\pi_{0}:X_{0}\longrightarrow B_{0}
obtained by restricting
\pi
to the complement of the singular fibers, and in particular on the cohomological properties of the general fiber
F
and of the base
U
of the intermediate trivialization. We now extend our study to a fibration
\pi_{1}:X_{1}\longrightarrow B_{1}
obtained by restricting
\pi
to the complement of a codimension two locus pulled back from the base
B
. Since
B
is normal, we may assume
B_{1}
is smooth. There are two general tools that apply to this situation. The first is Hwang and Oguiso’s description of general singular fibers using the notion of characteristic cycle
[
HO09
,
HO11
]
. The second is the canonical bundle formula of Kawamata
[
Kaw98
]
, Ambro
[
Amb04
,
Amb05
]
, and Fujino
[
Fuj03
]
, which is a higher dimensional generalization of Kodaira’s canonical bundle formula for elliptic fibrations. It takes the form

(4.1)

K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\pi^{*}(K_{B}+M_{B}+D_{\pi}),

and expresses the (
\mathbb{Q}
-linear equivalence class of the) canonical bundle of the total space of a relatively minimal
K
-trivial fibration
\pi:X\longrightarrow B
in terms of three
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors on the base
B
(see
[
Kol07
, Thm. 8.5.1]
): the canonical class of the base
K_{B}
, the moduli divisor
M_{B}
(which only depends on the restriction of the fibration to the smooth locus), and a discriminant divisor
D_{\pi}
which accounts for the singular fibers in codimension one (compare (
4.22
) below).

In the isotrivial case, both the local structure of the general singular fibers and the canonical bundle formula take a particularly simple form. Namely, the local monodromy groups are automatically finite, and in fact cyclic of order
m\in\{2,3,4,6\}
(
Corollary

4.12
,
[
BS23
, Lemma 3.6]
), and
m>2
is only possible if the elliptic curve
E
occurring in
Theorem

1.3
has CM by
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})
or
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
. Moreover, under the assumption that there are no multiple fibers in codimension
1
, we show that the only Hwang–Oguiso fiber types that occur for isotrivial fibrations are
II
,
III
,
IV
,
\text{I}_{0}^{*}
,
\text{II}^{*}
,
\text{III}^{*}
, and
\text{IV}^{*}
. Also, as in the case of elliptic fibrations, the type of singular fiber that can occur is controlled by the local monodromy. In particular, if the local mondromy is of order
2
, the only possible fiber type is
\text{I}_{0}^{*}
. Returning to the canonical bundle formula, in the isotrivial case, the moduli divisor contribution is trivial (compare
[
DB22
, Thm 3.3]
) and thus the only contribution to be determined is the discriminant divisor
D_{\pi}
, which is supported on the reduced discriminant
\Sigma
, and whose coefficients for the various components
D_{i}
of
\Sigma
depend on the Hwang–Oguiso singular fiber type at the generic point of
D_{i}
. These coefficients are familiar from the elliptic fibration setting (compare with
Appendix

A
), but to our knowledge the computations in this section (e.g.
Proposition

4.16
), refining Hwang–Oguiso’s work, are new in the hyper-Kähler setting. We also refer the reader to
Section

2.4.1
for a concrete application of these ideas in higher dimensions.

Note that most of the results in this section rely on the assumption that
every general singular fiber of
\pi:X\longrightarrow B
is a non-multiple fiber
, which explains this hypothesis in
Theorem

1.7
. This assumption is satisfied when
\pi
has a rational section.

4.1.
Compactification of the intermediate base

Let us start with the following general result about the existence of a preferred

-equivariant compactification

, which we call the

-compactification
.

4.2
.

Let

be a finite group and

(1)

(2)

Proof.

This is standard (e.g.
[

Recall that the intermediate base

is a Galois covering

of the Lagrangian fibration is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial

-Fano klt variety by

. Let

be the normal

-compactification whose quotient is

We write

where

is an an effective

-divisor on

4.4
.

Let

be the normal

-compactification of the intermediate base

(1)

If
\pi
is of type A, then
Y
is an abelian torsor. In particular,

compactifies to an abelian torsor.

(2)

If
\pi
is of type B, then

is ample.

Proof.

First, by
[
Mat99
, Thm 2]
, we have
\Pic B\cong\mathbb{Z}
. Hence a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
B
is either ample, anti-ample, or trivial. From the Hurwitz formula (
4.3
) and the fact that

is finite we deduce the second item, namely that the variety

is of general type if and only if
K_{Y}
is ample.

Similarly, if
\pi
is of type A then
K_{Y}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
. We have already shown that for type A fibrations,
U
(or
Y
) admits a birational map

f:Y\dashrightarrow A

to an abelian torsor
A
. Since
Y
is normal, the complement of its smooth locus
Y_{\reg}\subset Y
has codimension at least
2
. The restriction of
f
to
Y_{\reg}
is a morphism
f:Y_{\reg}\longrightarrow A
. As the canonical divisor of
Y_{\reg}
is trivial, this morphism is unramified and hence is an open immersion. It follows that the map

induced by
f
on the quotient does not contract any divisors.

We claim that
f
is an isomorphism in codimension
1
, namely that the complement of

has codimension at least
2
. Assume on the contrary that

contains a prime divisor

. Then the inverse birational map
f^{-1}:A\dashrightarrow Y
contracts

, and the quotient birational map
A/G\dashrightarrow Y/G
contracts the effective divisor
(G\cdot E)/G
. On the other hand, we have seen that the map
Y/G\dashrightarrow A/G
induced by
f
on the quotient does not contract any divisor. The conclusion is that
\rho(A/G)\geq\rho(Y/G)+1=\rho(B)+1=2
, which implies that
H^{2}(A,\mathbb{Q})^{G}
has dimension
\geq 2
. This violates
Corollary

2.20
, thereby proving that

is an isomorphism in codimension
1
. The conclusion follows from the following lemma. ∎

4.5
.

Let
Y
be a normal
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein projective variety with

and
A
an abelian torsor. A birational map

extends to an isomorphism.

Proof.

Since
K_{Y}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
and

is birational to

, it has canonical singularities. By
[
HM07
]
, the fibers of a resolution of indeterminacy of

are rationally connected. Since an abelian variety does not contain rational curves

extends to a morphism.
∎

4.6
.

In the type B case, we only have partial control on the singularities of the compactification

. We plan to return to this question in the future in connection to the study of type
B
isotrivial fibrations.

4.2.
Tubular neighborhood of general singular fibers

In this subsection, we will study the geometry of the general singular fibers of the isotrivial fibration

The following is a well-known equivalent definition of the local monodromy group and index. Fix a general point

4.3.
Log canonical thresholds and the canonical bundle formula

In this subsection we will discuss the canonical bundle formula and explain its relation with
Theorem

3.2
(
Proposition

4.24
). In short,
\pi
is of type
A
if and only if for every irreducible component
D_{i}\subset\Sigma
, the fiber of
\pi
over the generic point of
D_{i}
is of
*
-type, namely of type
\text{I}_{0}^{*},\text{II}^{*},\text{III}^{*},
or
\text{IV}^{*}
. When the endomorphism algebra of
E
is different from
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})
or
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
the local monodromy is
\mu_{2}
(
Corollary

4.12
) forcing the general singular fibers to be of type
\text{I}_{0}^{*}
.
Theorem

1.7
will follow easily.

The following
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
B
governs the behavior of the canonical divisor of
Y
via the Hurwitz formula
K_{Y}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}q^{*}\big(K_{B}+D_{q}\big)
(
4.3
):

(4.21)

D_{q}=\sum_{i}\big(1-\tfrac{1}{m_{i}}\big)D_{i}.

Let us introduce another
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
B
supported on the discriminant locus. Following
[
DB22
, §2.11]
, we define the
discriminant
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor

D_{\pi}
of the morphism
\pi:X\longrightarrow B
by

(4.22)

D_{\pi}=\sum_{i}\big(1-c_{i}\big)D_{i},\qquad c_{i}=\operatorname{lct}_{D_{i}}(X,X_{D_{i}})\ \in\mathbb{Q}.

Here

is the log canonical threshold of the pair
(X_{\eta_{i}},X_{D_{i},\eta_{i}})
, where

and

is the generic point of

.

The importance of these

[
Amb04
,
Amb05
]
, Kawamata
[
Kaw98
]
and Fujino
[

]
(see also Kollár
[

, Thm. 8.3.7]
). In the case of isotrivial Lagrangian fibrations this formula takes the following simple form:

4.23
[
DB22
, Thm 3.3]
.

There exists a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear equivalence of
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors

K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\pi^{*}\big(K_{B}+D_{\pi}\big).

In other words,
K_{B}+D_{\pi}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
on

.

Proposition

4.16
is describing the local structures around a general singular fiber of
\pi
and allows us to compute the log canonical thresholds

, and thus

.

4.24
.

Assume that all general singular fibers of
\pi
are non-multiple. Then we have

D_{q}\geq D_{\pi}.

Moreover, the following are equivalent.

.

(2)

\pi

(3)

\textup{I}_{0}^{*}
,
\textup{II}^{*}
,
\textup{III}^{*}
, or
\textup{IV}^{*}
.

Proof.

To show the inequality, we need to prove

for all irreducible components

.
The log canonical threshold of a pair is invariant under étale covering, so the étale covering

in

gives us the equality

where

its (singular) central fiber. The log canonical thresholds of the singular fibers of a minimal isotrivial elliptic fibration are listed in

In

holds exactly when the singular fiber

in the elliptic fibration is of Kodaira

5.
Classification of type A fibrations

In this section we prove
Theorem

1.6
, which describes type A.1 fibrations admiting a rational section. Recall from
Theorem

3.2
that the intermediate trivializiation
p:Z\longrightarrow U
is equivariantly birational to a morphism between

•

two
G
-abelian torsors (type A.1), or

•

a
G
-variety of Kodaira dimension
n
and a
G
-abelian torsor (type A.2), or

•

two

-varieties of Kodaira dimension
n
(type B).

In particular, if
\pi
is a type A.1 fibration then
X
is birational to the quotient of an abelian torsor of dimension

by the finite group
G
. As an intermediate step we introduce

, which does not rely on the existence of a rational section.

5.1
.

Let
\pi:X\longrightarrow B
be a type A.1 isotrivial fibration of a hyper-Kähler
2n
-fold, so that

and

are equivariantly birational to

and

(1)

T/G
is a primitive symplectic variety admitting a symplectic resolution by a hyper-Kähler manifold

.

(2)

There exists a commutative diagram

(5.2)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bar{F}/G=B^{\prime},}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bar{F}^{\prime}/G}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{bir}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

where

is an isotrivial fibration of a hyper-Kähler manifold

.

We will use the notion of a primitive symplectic variety following
[
BL22
, Def 3.1]
(see also
[
Sch20
]
). A
(projective) primitive symplectic variety
is a normal projective symplectic variety
(Y,\sigma)
such that
H^{0}(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{[1]})=0
and
H^{0}(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{[2]})=\mathbb{C}\cdot\sigma
, where

is the sheaf of reflexive

.

5.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group and

be a
G
-abelian variety of dimension
2n
. Suppose that there is a simple
G
-module

so that

Then

(1)

The quotient variety
T/G
is a symplectic variety.

(2)

If we further assume that

is not a symplectic
G
-module, then

is a primitive symplectic variety.

Proof.

The projective variety
\bar{X}=T/G
has quotient singularities so it is well behaved from the point of view of Hodge theory. In particular, we have
H^{1,0}(\bar{X})=H^{0}(\bar{X},\Omega_{\bar{X}}^{[1]})
and

, as well as the equalities

H^{1,0}(\bar{X})=H^{1,0}(T)^{G}=\big(V\oplus V^{\vee}\big)^{G}=0

and

H^{2,0}(\bar{X})=H^{2,0}(T)^{G}=\Big(\wedge^{2}V\oplus\wedge^{2}V^{\vee}\oplus\big(V\otimes V^{\vee}\big)\Big)^{G}.

Note that the last term is
(V\otimes V^{\vee})^{G}=\End_{G}(V)=\mathbb{C}\cdot\id
by Schur’s lemma. Hence there is a
G
-invariant holomorphic symplectic form

on

. It follows that
\bar{X}
is a symplectic variety by
[

.
∎

.

admits a partial resolution

by a

Proof of

.

The last ingredient we need to prove

is the following lemma, which is a consequence of a series of recent results of Auffarth, Lucchini Arteche, and Quezada providing a classification of smooth quotients of abelian varieties.

5.5
[
ALA20
,
ALAQ22
,
ALA22
]
.

Let
F
be a
G
-abelian torsor. Assume that (1)
G
acts on
F
faithfully and without any translations, (2)

is smooth, and (3)
H^{*}(F/G,\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathbb{Q})
. Then there exists a choice of an origin for
F
making it into a
G
-abelian variety satisfying:

(1)

F\cong E^{n}
for an elliptic curve
E
.

(2)

G
acts faithfully on
E^{n}
by fixing the origin and this action belongs to the following list:

(a)

\mathfrak{S}_{n+1}
acting on
\ker(E^{n+1}\longrightarrow E)
by permuting the factors.

(b)

\mu^{\times n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
acting on
E^{n}
in the obvious way, where
\mu=\mu_{2},\mu_{3},\mu_{4}
or
\mu_{6}
acts on
E
faithfully by fixing the origin.

(c)

(Only when
n=2
)
The Pauli group acting on
E^{2}
for
E=\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-1}]
.

(3)

F/G\cong\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

The
Pauli group
is the following non-abelian group of order
16
:

\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{-1}^{a}&0\\
0&\sqrt{-1}^{b}\end{pmatrix}:a\equiv b\mod 2\right\}\rtimes\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle\quad\subset\ \GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-1}]).

We refer the reader to
[
ALAQ22
, §3.2]
for the Pauli group action on
(E^{\prime})^{2}
appearing in the lemma.

Proof.

Consider a normal subgroup of
N\subset G
and its quotient

N=\{g\in G:F^{g}\neq\varnothing\},\qquad H=G/N.

By
[
ALA22
, Thm 1.1]
, there exists at least one point of
F
fixed by all of
N
, i.e.,
F^{N}\neq\varnothing
. We may thus choose an origin for
F
so that
N
acts on
F
by fixing the origin, i.e., so that
F
is an
N
-abelian variety. Moreover
F/N
is a smooth projective fiber bundle over an abelian variety

whose fiber is positive dimensional and of the form
\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{i}}
. Moreover,
H
acts freely on both
F/N
and
Z
so that the fiber bundle is
H
-equivariant. Taking the
H
-quotient, we obtain a fiber bundle

whose fiber is of the form
\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{i}}
.

The cohomology

can be computed in terms of the cohomology of the base
H^{*}(Z/H,\mathbb{Q})
by the Leray–Hirsch theorem:

H^{*}(F/G,\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{*}(Z/H,\mathbb{Q})\otimes H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{i}},\mathbb{Q}).

By assumption
H^{*}(F/G,\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathbb{Q})
, so this forces the base

to be a point and
F/G\cong\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Since

is an abelian variety and

is a finite group acting freely on

, this means that

is a point and
H
is trivial. That is,
F
is a
G
-abelian variety. The lemma now follows from the main results of
[
ALA20
,
ALAQ22
]
which provide a classification of
G
-abelian varieties
F
with smooth quotient
F/G
.
∎

6.
Additional remarks

In this closing section we collect some remarks about the locus of hyper-Kähler manifolds admitting an isotrivial Lagrangian fibration. Our main result is

is at least

6.1

Let

Proof.

For simplicity we assume that

is projective; the general case when

is proper can be dealt with by using the notion of relative automorphism scheme and torsor. Let
\mathcal{B}_{0}\subset\mathcal{B}
be a Zariski open subset over which

is smooth and proper and pick a large positive integer

. We can then find a finite étale cover

of

. We thus obtain a moduli map

\mathcal{A}
is a fine moduli space of abelian

-folds with level

Write

, so that

is the

is flat and

is closed in

Appendix B
Background on the Albanese morphism and fibrations

B
.1.
Albanese torsors and morphisms.

The aim of this section is to clarify the notions of Albanese varieties and morphisms used in this article.

Theorem-Definition B.1
.

We can associate to a smooth quasi-projective variety
Y
a triple
(\Alb_{Y},\Alb_{Y}^{\tor},\textup{alb}_{Y})
consisting of:

(1)

an abelian variety
\Alb_{Y}
called the Albanese variety of
Y
,

(2)

an
\Alb_{Y}
-torsor
\Alb_{Y}^{\tor}
called the Albanese torsor of
Y
, and

(3)

a morphism
\textup{alb}_{Y}:Y\longrightarrow\Alb_{Y}^{\tor}
called the Albanese morphism of
Y
.

These objects satisfy the following universal property: for any abelian variety
A
, an
A
-torsor
A^{\tor}
, and a morphism
Y\longrightarrow A^{\tor}
, there exists a unique homomorphism of abelian varieties
\Alb_{Y}\longrightarrow A
and equivariant morphism
\Alb_{Y}^{\tor}\longrightarrow A^{\tor}
giving a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Alb_{Y}^{\tor}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Alb_{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\tor}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A.}

B.2
.

Typically, in the literature, the Albanese variety of a smooth quasi-projective variety
Y
is a semi-abelian variety (e.g.,
[
Wit08
]
). Strictly speaking, we work with the Albanese variety of a smooth compactification of
Y
, which is a birational invariant of smooth quasi-projective varieties.

Given a smooth quasi-projective variety
Y
, there is a mixed Hodge structure on
H^{1}(Y,\mathbb{Q})
with weights
\geq 1
. We can obtain a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module by letting

W_{1}H^{1}(Y,\mathbb{Z}):=H^{1}(Y,\mathbb{Z})\cap W_{1}H^{1}(Y,\mathbb{Q}).

For any smooth projective compactification
j:Y\hookrightarrow\tilde{Y}
, the restriction map
j^{*}:H^{1}(\tilde{Y},\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow H^{1}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
is injective with image
W_{1}H^{1}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
.

B.3
.

Let
Y
be a smooth quasi-projective variety. Then there exist isomorphisms of Hodge structures (induced by the Albanese morphism)

H^{1}(\Alb_{Y},\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{1}(\Alb_{Y}^{\tor},\mathbb{Z})\cong W_{1}H^{1}(Y,\mathbb{Z}).

Moreover, if
Y
and
Y^{\prime}
are birational then their Albanese varieties, torsors, and morphisms are isomorphic. ∎

B.4
.

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve. Then its Albanese variety is
\Alb_{C}=\Pic^{0}_{C}
, its Albanese torsor is
\Alb_{C}^{\tor}=\Pic^{1}_{C}
, and its Albanese morphism is

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\;\;\Pic^{1}_{C}

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\longmapsto\big[\mathcal{O}_{C}(x)\big].

B.5
.

Let
X
and
Y
be smooth quasi-projective varieties,
A
an abelian variety and
f:X\times Y\longrightarrow A
a morphism. Then there are morphisms
g:X\longrightarrow A
and
h:Y\longrightarrow A
such that
f(x,y)=g(x)+h(y)
.

Proof.

The map
f
factorizes as
X\times Y\longrightarrow\Alb_{X}^{\tor}\times\Alb_{Y}^{\tor}\longrightarrow A
, where the latter map is a morphism between abelian torsors, and therefore is the sum of the two factors
\Alb_{X}^{\tor}\longrightarrow A
and
\Alb_{Y}^{\tor}\longrightarrow A
.
∎

B.6
.

Let
Y
be a smooth quasi-projective variety with a
G
-action. Then

(1)

There are
G
-actions on both
\Alb_{Y}
and
\Alb_{Y}^{\tor}
.

(2)

The Albanese morphism
Y\to\Alb_{Y}^{\tor}
is
G
-equivariant.

Proof.

The existence of the
G
-action on both
\Alb_{Y}
and
\Alb_{Y}^{\tor}
comes from the universal property of the Albanese morphism. For any
g\in G
, there exists a unique commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Alb_{Y}^{\tor}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Alb_{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Alb_{Y}^{\tor}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Alb_{Y}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

The
G
-actions on
A^{\tor}=\Alb_{Y}^{\tor}
and
A=\Alb_{Y}
are related by the following morphism

G\longrightarrow\Aut(A^{\tor})\cong A\rtimes\Aut_{0}(A)\twoheadrightarrow\Aut_{0}(A).\qed

B.7
.

Though
\Alb_{Y}
and
\Alb_{Y}^{\tor}
are isomorphic as varieties, they may not be isomorphic at
G
-varieties because some elements of
G
may act on
\Alb_{Y}^{\tor}
by translation. For example, this is the case if
Y
is an abelian variety and
G
acts on
Y
by translations. The Albanese morphism is then the (
G
-equivariant) identity map. However, the
G
-action on the Albanese variety
\Alb_{Y}
is trivial.

B
.2.
Rational fibrations.

A proper morphism
f:Y\longrightarrow Z
between irreducible normal varieties is called a fibration if it is surjective and has connected fibers. This is equivalent to the equality of coherent sheaves
\mathcal{O}_{Z}=f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
. One can generalize this notion to rational maps as follows.

B.8
[
Laz04
, Ex 2.1.12]
.

A rational map
f:Y\dashrightarrow Z
between irreducible normal varieties is called a
fibration
(or
rational fibration
to emphasize the fact that
f
is a rational map) if it is dominant and the subfield
f^{*}K(Z)\subset K(Y)
is algebraically closed.

B.9
.

If
f:Y\longrightarrow Z
is a proper morphism, then this definition coincides with the usual one by
[
Laz04
, Ex 2.1.12]
.

B.10
.

If a fibration
f:Y\longrightarrow Z
is not proper, then not all fibers of
f
need to be connected. For example, given a proper fibration
\bar{f}:\bar{Y}\longrightarrow Z
with a reducible fiber
F=F_{1}\cup F_{2}
over
z\in Z
, consider the composition of the inclusion
Y=\bar{Y}\setminus(F_{1}\cap F_{2})\subset\bar{Y}
with
\bar{f}
.

Appendix C
Equivariant versions of theorems of Ueno and Kawamata

This section is devoted to generalizing results of Ueno
[
Uen75
, Thm 10.9]

[
Mor87
, Thm 3.7]
and Kawamata
[
Kaw81
, Thm 13]
to the equivariant setting.

C.1
Equivariant version of Ueno’s theorem.

Let
G
be a finite group and
X\subset A^{\tor}
a closed irreducible
G
-subvariety of a
G
-abelian torsor
A^{\tor}
. Then there exists a
G
-abelian subvariety
B\subset A
, a closed
G
-subvariety
Y\subset A^{\tor}/B
, and a
G
-equivariant commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\tor}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\tor}/B}

with the following properties:

(1)

The diagram is cartesian, i.e.,
X\longrightarrow Y
is a
B
-torsor.

(2)

X\longrightarrow Y
is the Iitaka fibration of (the normalization of)
X
.

Proof.

Ueno’s theorem is a characterization of the Iitaka fibration of subvarieties of abelian varieties. We show that the proof works
G
-equivariantly. Ueno defines the abelian subvariety
B\subset A
as the identity component of

B^{\prime}=\{a\in A:a+X\subset X\},

and sets
Y:=X/B
. It thus suffices to show that
B
is a
G
-abelian subvariety of
A
. This follows from the fact that
B^{\prime}\subset A
is an algebraic subgroup closed under the
G
-action, and that the
G
-action fixes
0\in A
, thus taking the identity component of
B^{\prime}
to itself.
∎

C.2
Equivariant version of Kawamata’s theorem.

Consider a finite group
G
and a finite
G
-equivariant morphism
f:X\longrightarrow A^{\tor}
from a normal projective irreducible
G
-variety
X
to a
G
-abelian torsor
A^{\tor}
. Then there exists a
G
-abelian subvariety
B\subset A
, a finite
G
-equivariant morphism
h:Y\longrightarrow A^{\tor}/B
from a normal
G
-variety
Y
, and a
\,G
-equivariant commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\tor}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\tor}/B}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}

with the following properties.

(1)

X\longrightarrow Y
is generically
\tilde{B}
-isotrivial for an abelian variety
\tilde{B}
isogenous to
B
.

(2)

X\longrightarrow Y
is the Iitaka fibration of
X
.

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as Kawamata’s original proof from
[
Kaw81
, Thm 13]
. We provide details for the sake of completeness. Start with an Iitaka fibration
\phi:X\dashrightarrow Y^{\prime}
of
X
. Since the Iitaka fibration is defined up to birational equivalence, we may assume that
Y^{\prime}
is smooth projective. By realizing the Iitaka fibration as a stable image of the pluricanonical maps,
Y^{\prime}
admits a rational
G
-action and
\phi
is
G
-equivariant.

Let
F_{y}=\overline{\phi^{-1}(y)}
be the Zariski closure of a very general fiber of
\phi
. It is an irreducible subvariery of
X
with
\kappa(F_{y})=0
. The image
f(F_{y})\subset A^{\tor}
has Kodaira dimension
0
since it is a subvariety of
A^{\tor}
and is dominated by
F_{y}
. By Ueno’s theorem, it is isomorphic to an abelian torsor
B_{y}^{\tor}\subset A^{\tor}
for some abelian subvariety
B_{y}\subset A
. Since the set of abelian subvarieties of
A
is discrete,
B:=B_{y}
does not depend on the choice of a very general point
y\in Y^{\prime}
.

We claim that the abelian subvariety
B\subset A
is a
G
-abelian subvariety. For
y\in Y^{\prime}
very general, the automorphism
g:X\longrightarrow X
induces an isomorphism
g:B_{y}^{\tor}\to B_{g\cdot y}^{\tor}
. Since
B_{y}^{\tor},B_{g\cdot y}^{\tor}\subset A^{\tor}
are translations of one another, they are canonically isomorphic up to translation. Ignoring the translation part, we obtain an isomorphism
g:B\longrightarrow B
. This realizes
B
as a
G
-abelian subvariety of
A
.

Consider the quotient
p:A^{\tor}\longrightarrow A^{\tor}/B
. A very general fiber of
\phi
is sent to a point via

p\circ f:X\longrightarrow A^{\tor}\longrightarrow A^{\tor}/B.

By the rigidity lemma
[
Kaw81
, Lem 14]
, this induces an equivariant rational map
h^{\prime}:Y^{\prime}\dashrightarrow A^{\tor}/B
making the following diagram commutative:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\tor}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\tor}/B.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h^{\prime}}

As
Y^{\prime}
is smooth and
A^{\tor}/B
is an abelian torsor, the rational map
h^{\prime}
is defined everywhere. Denote by
\bar{X}
and
\bar{Y}
the image of
f
and
h^{\prime}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bar{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\tor}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bar{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\tor}/B.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h^{\prime}}

By construction
\bar{X}\longrightarrow\bar{Y}
is an equivariant and surjective morphism. We claim that it is in fact a
B
-torsor, i.e., that
\bar{X}=p^{-1}(\bar{Y})
. Since both
\bar{X}
and
p^{-1}(\bar{Y})
are projective and irreducible, the claim is equivalent to the dimension count
\dim\bar{X}=\dim\bar{Y}+\dim B
. This follows from a sequence of inequalities

\dim\bar{Y}+\dim B\leq\dim Y^{\prime}+\dim B=\dim X=\dim\bar{X}\leq\dim\bar{Y}+\dim B.

This shows that
\bar{X}\to\bar{Y}
is a
B
-torsor and that
\dim\bar{Y}=\dim Y^{\prime}=\kappa(X)
.

Finally, consider the Stein factorization
X\longrightarrow Y
of the composition
X\longrightarrow A^{\tor}\longrightarrow A^{\tor}/B
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bar{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\tor}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bar{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\tor}/B.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}

Since
\bar{X}\longrightarrow\bar{Y}
is a
B
-torsor, the Stein factorization
X\longrightarrow Y
is generically isotrivial with general fiber
\tilde{B}
, a finite étale cover of
B
, hence an abelian variety. It follows that a very general fiber of
X\longrightarrow Y
has Kodaira dimension
0
and
\dim Y=\kappa(X)
. These two properties characterize the Iitaka fibration (see
[
Mor87
, Def-Thm 1.11]
), so
X\longrightarrow Y
is again an Iitaka fibration of
X
. The diagram is
G
-equivariant.
∎

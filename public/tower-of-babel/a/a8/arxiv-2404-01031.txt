An 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -monoidal Grothendieck construction
Redi Haderi and Walker H. Stern

Abstract
Given an operad 𝒪 {\mathcal{O}} , we define a notion of weak 𝒪 {\mathcal{O}} -monoids — which we term 𝒪 {\mathcal{O}} -pseudomonoids – in a 2-category. In the special case with the 2-category in question is the 2-category 𝖢𝖺𝗍 {\mathsf{Cat}} of categories, this yields a notion of 𝒪 {\mathcal{O}} -monoidal category, which in the case of the associative and commutative operads retrieves unbiased notions of monoidal and symmetric monoidal categories, respectively. We carefully unpack the definition of 𝒪 {\mathcal{O}} -monoids in the 2-categories of discrete fibrations and of category-indexed sets. Using the classical Grothendieck construction, we thereby obtain an 𝒪 {\mathcal{O}} -monoidal Grothendieck construction relating lax 𝒪 {\mathcal{O}} -monoidal functors into 𝖲𝖾𝗍 {\mathsf{Set}} to strict 𝒪 {\mathcal{O}} -monoidal functors which are also discrete fibrations.

The Main theorem

With the main definitions established and unwound, the main theorem of this paper becomes relatively simple to state.

Theorem
.

For any operad
\mathcal{O}
, the classical Grothendieck construction
\int:{\mathsf{ISet}}\to{\mathsf{DFib}}
induces an equivalence of 2-categories

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\displaystyle\int^{\mathcal{O}}}:}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{ISet}}^{\mathcal{O},\operatorname{lax}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}\mathsf{Fib}.}

It is worth pointing out that we expect a number of generalizations of this theorem to hold. In particular, (1) we expect an analogous result to hold for indexed categories and Grothendieck fibrations, and (2) we expect a similar equivalence of
\infty

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x.}

Remark 4.5
.

The preceding definition alters Definition 3.1.1 of
[
9
]
in two ways. The first, purely notational, is that we use order-preserving maps of finite sets to encode what Leinster calls ‘double sequences’ in op. cit. The second, more important, is that we require

7
The
{\mathcal{O}}
-monoidal Grothendieck construction

Proposition 7.1
.

The 2-functors
\int
and
\operatorname{T}
are feline, and the natural transformations
\Phi
and
\Psi
of Theorem
2.5
are compatible with their feline structures.

Proof.

Since the functors
\int
and
\operatorname{T}
form a strict 2-equivalence, preservation of products is immediate. Given a set
X
, we view it as the discrete fibration
\iota_{{\mathsf{DFib}}}(X)
given by
{\operatorname{id}}:X\to X
and the indexed set
\iota_{{\mathsf{ISet}}}(X)
given by the functor
X\to{\mathsf{Set}}
which sends each element of
X
to the terminal set. Since
\operatorname{T}(\iota_{{\mathsf{DFib}}}(X))
is also a functor
X\to{\mathsf{Set}}
sending every element to a terminal set, there is a unique natural isomorphism
\mu_{X}
filling the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{Set}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{T}(\iota_{{\mathsf{DFib}}}(X))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{id}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{{\mathsf{ISet}}}(X)}

It is a simple matter to check that the components
({\operatorname{id}}_{X},\mu_{X})
define a natural isomorphism
T\circ\iota_{{\mathsf{DFib}}}\cong\iota_{{\mathsf{ISet}}}
, showing that
\operatorname{T}
is feline.

On the other hand, suppose that we note that
\int\iota_{{\mathsf{ISet}}}(X)
is the map
X\times\{\ast\}\to X
which sends
(x,\ast)\mapsto X
. Since the fibres are all singletons, there is a unique isomorphism

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\times\{\ast\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{pr}_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{pr}_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{id}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{id}}}

from
\int\iota_{{\mathsf{ISet}}}(X)
to
\iota_{{\mathsf{DFib}}}(X)
. It is again immediate that the components
(\operatorname{pr}_{1}^{X},{\operatorname{id}}_{X})
form a natural isomorphism
\int\circ\iota_{{\mathsf{ISet}}}\cong\iota_{{\mathsf{DFib}}}
.

It is an easy exercise to check that
\Phi
and
\Psi
are compatible with these feline structures.
∎

Theorem 7.2
.

For any operad
\mathcal{O}
, the classical Grothendieck construction
\int:{\mathsf{ISet}}\to{\mathsf{DFib}}
induces an equivalence of 2-categories

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\displaystyle\int^{\mathcal{O}}}:}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{ISet}}^{\mathcal{O},\operatorname{lax}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}\mathsf{Fib}.}

Proof.

Applying Corollary
3.15
and Theorem
2.5
, we see that
\int
induces a 2-equivalence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}\mathsf{Mon}({\mathsf{ISet}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}\mathsf{Mon}({\mathsf{DFib}}).}

However Theorem
5.2
identifies the target with
\mathcal{O}\mathsf{Fib}
and Theorem
6.2
identifies the source with
{\mathsf{ISet}}^{\mathcal{O},\operatorname{lax}}
, completing the proof.
∎

Corollary 7.3
.

For any operad
\mathcal{O}
and any
\mathcal{O}
-monoidal category
{\mathsf{I}}
, the Grothendieck construction induces an equivalence of 1-categories

{\operatorname{Fun}}^{\mathcal{O},\operatorname{lax}}({\mathsf{I}},{\mathsf{Set}})\simeq\mathcal{O}\mathsf{Fib}_{{\mathsf{I}}}.

Proof.

This is simply restricting each side of Theorem
7.2
to a single
\mathcal{O}
-monoidal base category
{\mathsf{I}}
.
∎

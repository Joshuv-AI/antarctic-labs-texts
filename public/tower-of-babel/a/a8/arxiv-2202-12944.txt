Domain wall between the Dirac sea and the ”anti-Dirac sea”
S.N. Vergeles

Abstract
It was shown in work Vergeles 2021 that in the theory of gravity coupled with the Dirac field, each state | λ ⟩ |\lambda\rangle has its own twin | λ ; P T ⟩ |\lambda;PT\rangle , which is obtained by a discrete PT transformation. If in the state | λ ⟩ |\lambda\rangle the Dirac sea is filled, then in the state | λ ; P T ⟩ |\lambda;PT\rangle there is an ”anti-Dirac” filling (in terms of the state | λ ⟩ |\lambda\rangle ). It is important that the energies of these states are the same. Therefore, there may be domains with different filling of the Dirac sea. Here we study a domain wall connecting two such adjacent domains.

I
Introduction

We study some properties of the theory of gravity coupled with the Dirac field. The theory is assumed to be lattice-regularized. The version of the lattice theory of gravity, which is used here, is discussed in detail in
Vergeles 2021
. In the naive long-wavelength limit, this lattice theory transforms into the considered continuum theory. It is convenient to represent the action of the theory of gravity in the form of Palatini, when the gravitational degrees of freedom are described by a tetrad and a connection.

It was shown in
Vergeles 2021
that in the lattice version of the theory (and thus in the long-wavelength limit) there is a global discrete symmetry, which is an analogue of the well-known
PT
-symmetry in field theory. We therefore call this discrete symmetry as
PT
-symmetry because Dirac fields are transformed at each vertex of the lattice (or point in space-time) according to the law of combined
P
and
T
transformation known in flat field theory. The difference between our proposed
PT
-transformation and the same in the flat field theory is that in the latter case the coordinates (which are global) change sign, while we propose to change the sign of the tetrad. In this case, the connection coefficients remain unchanged.
Such a rule for transforming variables in the case of
PT
-reflection is dictated by the lattice model of the theory. Indeed, there are no coordinates on the lattice at all, there are only dynamic variables assigned to the lattice elements. Therefore, the entire load of any transformation must be borne by dynamic variables.
To this it should be added that in the continual theory of gravity, the change in the sign of local coordinates seems to us not quite correct for the reason that different local coordinates mutually transform (generally speaking) according to a nonlinear law.

Since, as in the case of flat field theory, repeated application of the PT transformation gives the identical transformation, this means that there is
Z_{2}
-symmetry in the theory. This
Z_{2}
-transformation mutually replaces particles and antiparticles. To clarify the situation, it is useful to consider the case of Minkowski space, when it is correct to consider Dirac modes with a certain energy. As shown in (
20
), as a result of the
PT
-transformation, the mode energy changes sign. But one must take into account the fact that the tetrad also changes sign under the
PT
-transformation, and the Dirac Hamiltonian in curved space-time is a third-degree functional relative to the tetrad. Therefore, the energy of the transformed ground state (that is, the energy of the ”anti-Dirac sea”) remains negative, although from the point of view of an observer related with the initial ground state in Minkowski space-time, Dirac modes with positive energies in the transformed ground state are filled. Therefore, we call the transformed vacuum the ”anti-Dirac sea”.

To some extent, the described situation resembles the situation in the 2D Ising model
which also has
Z_{2}
-symmetry: this model is symmetric relative to the global reversal of the sign of all spins. At temperatures below critical the average spin value is not zero, but it can be positive or negative with equal Helmholtz free energy density. Therefore, in the Ising model, there can be adjacent domains with opposite sign values of the mean spin. A similar phenomenon takes place in the lattice theory of gravity considered here.
Here the slip of the tongue ”in the lattice theory of gravity” is not accidental.
The point is that in any lattice field theory the energies or quasi-energies of quasiparticles are limited in absolute value. Therefore, in the process of the Big Bang of the Universe, domains can be formed, the states of which differ in the indicated
PT
-transformation.

The subject of the proposed work is the study of a domain wall.

The work is organized as follows:

In Section II, the necessary notations are introduced, the studied model is described both in the continuum and on the lattice, and the global
PT
-transformation is determined. Further, some technical means are presented that are used in the calculations in the following sections.

Section III contains several solutions for a flat domain wall. Only the solution for a domain wall moving at the speed of light seems to be physically acceptable.

Section IV contains some speculation about the distribution of fermionic charge between domains. Although the total fermionic charge of the entire system is strictly conserved on the lattice and it can be zero, in one of the domains its density can be positive, and in the other it can be negative. This raises a fundamental question: is gravity a ”player” in the problem of the baryon asymmetry of the Universe?

The quantum theory of gravity is a non-renormalizable theory. In particular, this means that quantum fluctuations of gravitational dynamical variables (tetrads and connections) are large on ultra-small scales of the order of the Planck length. But on scales much larger than the Planck scale, these fluctuations decay rapidly (according to a power law). Therefore, we will assume that when considering physics on scales that are much larger than Planck’s, fluctuations of the gravitational degrees of freedom are insignificant, that is, these degrees of freedom are described by classical fields. In this case, the quantum fluctuations of the Dirac fields are taken into account by averaging over the Dirac vacuum of all operators constructed using the Dirac fields.
Thus, Dirac propagators calculated in an external classical gravitational field are introduced into the computational procedure.

is some scalar function. The fact that the relation
e^{a}_{\mu}\propto\Theta^{a}_{\mu}
takes place in the theory under consideration was previously contained in the works

Volovik 2021
;
Diakonov 2011
;
Vladimirov and Diakonov 2012
;
Vladimirov and
Diakonov 2014
.

In our case, the Einstein equation is convenient to use in the form

\displaystyle\delta{\mathop{{\mathfrak{A}}}\nolimits}/\delta e^{a}_{\mu}=0.

(29)

There is also an equation obtained by varying the action with respect to the connection
\omega^{ab}_{\mu}
:

\displaystyle\delta{\mathop{{\mathfrak{A}}}\nolimits}/\delta\omega^{ab}_{\mu}=0\longrightarrow\nabla_{\mu}e^{a}_{\nu}-\nabla_{\nu}e^{a}_{\mu}=\frac{il_{P}^{2}}{4}e^{b}_{\mu}e^{c}_{\nu}\overline{\Psi}\left(\gamma^{a}\sigma_{bc}+\sigma_{bc}\gamma^{a}\right)\Psi=0.

(30)

The last equality follows from the fact that, according to (
23
), the Dirac field propagator is linear in the
\gamma
-matrices, and also from the identity

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits\left[\gamma^{d}\left(\gamma^{a}\sigma^{bc}+\sigma^{bc}\gamma^{a}\right)\right]\equiv 0.

(31)

Let’s make an infinitesimal transformation of coordinates
x^{\mu}\longrightarrow x^{\mu}-\xi^{\mu}(x)
. As known, in this case, the metric tensor and the tetrad change according to the formulas

\displaystyle\delta g_{\mu\nu}=\nabla_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}+\nabla_{\nu}\xi_{\mu},\quad\delta e^{a}_{\mu}=\nabla_{\mu}\xi^{a},\quad\xi^{a}=e^{a}_{\mu}\xi^{\mu}.

(32)

The variation of the Dirac action (
3
) on the mass shell with respect to the variation (
32
) is equal to zero, and the direct calculation gives

\displaystyle\delta\mathop{{\mathfrak{A}}}\nolimits_{\Psi}=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{abcd}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}\int\langle 0|\Theta^{a}_{\mu}|0\rangle\,e^{b}_{\nu}\,e^{c}_{\lambda}\,\nabla_{\rho}\xi^{d}\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits x^{0}\wedge\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits x^{1}\wedge\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits x^{2}\wedge\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits x^{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{abcd}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}\int fe^{a}_{\mu}\,e^{b}_{\nu}\,e^{c}_{\lambda}\,\nabla_{\rho}\xi^{d}\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits x^{0}\wedge\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits x^{1}\wedge\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits x^{2}\wedge\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits x^{3}=0.

(33)

Here we have used the equalities (
28
) and (
32
).
Since the right side of Equation (
30
) is equal to zero, then the right-hand side (
33
) is rewritten as

\displaystyle\delta\mathop{{\mathfrak{A}}}\nolimits_{\Psi}=-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{abcd}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}\int\xi^{d}\left(\partial_{\rho}f\right)e^{a}_{\mu}\,e^{b}_{\nu}\,e^{c}_{\lambda}\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits x^{0}\wedge\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits x^{1}\wedge\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits x^{2}\wedge\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits x^{3}=0,

(34)

whence follows the equation

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{abcd}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}e^{a}_{\mu}\,e^{b}_{\nu}\,e^{c}_{\lambda}\partial_{\rho}f=0.

(35)

The equation (
35
) is in our case the equation of motion of matter, which in the general case has the form
\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}=0
, where
T^{\mu\nu}
is the energy-momentum tensor of matter.

Thus, to study the domain wall, we must solve the system of equations (
29
), (
30
) and (
35
) for the classical fields
e^{a}_{\mu}
,
\omega^{ab}_{\mu}
and
f
.

III
domain wall

We denote the coordinates on the space-like hyperplane
\Sigma
by
(x,\,y,\,z)
.
Suppose that the domain wall is flat and it
is parallel to the plane
z=0
in the hyperplane
\Sigma
.

It follows from the assumptions made that the metric tensor depends only on the coordinates
(t,\,z)
and is invariant relative to rotations around any perpendicular to the plane
(x,\,y)
. This means that all off-diagonal elements of the metric tensor, except for
g_{t\,z}
, are equal to zero. Then

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\big(g_{tt},\,g_{xx},\,g_{yy},\,g_{zz},\,g_{tz}\big)=\big(\rho^{2}(t,z),\,-\lambda^{2}(t,z),\,-\lambda^{2}(t,z),\,-\sigma^{2}(t,z),\,\tau(t,z)\big).

(36)

From the form of the metric (
36
) it follows that the tetrad can be chosen so that

e^{\alpha}_{i}\sim\delta^{\alpha}_{i}
for
\alpha=1,2
,
i=1,2
.
In this case, two of the four equations (
35
)
with
d=1,2
are satisfied identically. The other two equations with
d=0,3
are reduced to the form

\displaystyle\left(e^{3}_{3}\partial_{t}-e^{3}_{0}\partial_{z}\right)f=0,\quad\left(e^{0}_{3}\partial_{t}-e^{0}_{0}\partial_{z}\right)f=0.

(37)

A pair of equations (
37
) has a nonzero solution only if

\displaystyle e^{0}_{0}e^{3}_{3}-e^{0}_{3}e^{3}_{0}=0.

(38)

The last relation means that the metric is degenerate near the domain wall.

Let’s consider several options for solutions.

III.1
Stationary solution

A stationary solution to the system (
37
), when
f(z)
depends on
z
, but does not depend on
t
, is physically meaningless. Indeed, in this case, for the fulfillment of the equations
(
37
) requires
e^{0}_{0}=e^{3}_{0}=0
, and the metric takes the form

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits s^{2}=\left[\left(e^{0}_{3}\right)^{2}-\left(e^{3}_{3}\right)^{2}\right]\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits z^{2}-\lambda^{2}\left(\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits x^{2}+\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits y^{2}\right).

(39)

Thus, if the square bracket in (
39
) is less than zero, then the interval is always less than or equal to zero, and there is no proper time. Otherwise, the
z
coordinate becomes the time coordinate, which is also
meaningless.

III.2
The case
f=\mathop{\rm const}\nolimits

Let’s consider the simplest solution

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}f=0\longrightarrow f=\mathop{\rm const}\nolimits,

(40)

satisfying equations (
35
). In this case, for the existence a physically acceptable solution requires the condition

\displaystyle\tilde{f}\equiv f+\Lambda=0.

(41)

Indeed, from (
41
) it follows that only the term contributes to the Einstein equation (
29
)
\mathop{{\mathfrak{A}}}\nolimits_{g}
. According to (
2
), all components
\delta{\mathop{{\mathfrak{A}}}\nolimits}_{g}/\delta e^{a}_{\mu}
are proportional to curvature tensor
\mathop{{\mathfrak{R}}}\nolimits^{ab}
, which is bilinear with respect to the first derivatives and linear with respect to the second derivatives of the tetrad. For a physically acceptable solution, all derived tetrads must vanish far from the wall, that is, far from the wall, the equality
\delta{\mathop{{\mathfrak{A}}}\nolimits}_{g}/\delta e^{a}_{\mu}=0
is necessary. If we assume that the cosmological constant can only change on scales,
significantly larger scales of the domain wall, then the condition (
41
) must be satisfied at all points in space-time. Thus, in this case, the Einstein equation is reduced to the Einstein equation in emptiness.

Let us write down the set of Einstein’s equations.

It is natural to assume that in the case (
40
) the metric is nondegenerate. Then, by transforming the coordinates
(t,\,z)\longrightarrow(t^{\prime},\,z^{\prime})
, one can vanish the only nonzero off-diagonal component of the metric
g_{tz}
. As a result, the tetrad can be chosen in the form

\displaystyle e^{0}=\rho\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits t,\quad e^{1}=\lambda\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits x,\quad e^{2}=\lambda\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits y,\quad e^{3}=\sigma\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits z.

(42)

We will also assume that all geometric quantities depend only on
z
. Using equations
(
30
) find all components of the connection coefficients and then all components of the Riemann curvature tensor
\mathop{{\mathfrak{R}}}\nolimits^{ab}_{\mu\nu}
, whose nonzero components

\displaystyle\mathop{{\mathfrak{R}}}\nolimits^{01}_{01}=\mathop{{\mathfrak{R}}}\nolimits^{02}_{02}=\frac{\rho^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}}{\sigma^{2}},\quad\mathop{{\mathfrak{R}}}\nolimits^{03}_{03}=\left(\frac{\rho^{\prime\prime}}{\sigma}-\frac{\rho^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}{\sigma^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\mathop{{\mathfrak{R}}}\nolimits^{12}_{12}=\frac{\lambda^{\prime 2}}{\sigma^{2}},\quad\mathop{{\mathfrak{R}}}\nolimits^{13}_{13}=\mathop{{\mathfrak{R}}}\nolimits^{23}_{23}=\left(\frac{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}{\sigma}-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}{\sigma^{2}}\right).

(43)

Using the equations (
42
) and (
43
), we write out all independent and not identically zero Einstein’s equations (
29
) in the absence of matter:

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime 2}+2\lambda\lambda^{\prime\prime}-2\frac{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}{\sigma}=0,

\displaystyle\rho\lambda^{\prime\prime}+\rho^{\prime\prime}\lambda+\rho^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}-\frac{\sigma^{\prime}}{\sigma}\left(\rho\lambda\right)^{\prime}=0,

\displaystyle 2\rho^{\prime}\lambda+\rho\lambda^{\prime}=0.

(44)

The last equation has the integral
\rho^{2}\lambda=\mathop{\rm const}\nolimits
. Making the substitution
\rho=\mathop{\rm const}\nolimits\cdot\lambda^{-1/2}
in the second equation, we arrive at the first of the equations
(
44
) which is easy to integrate. Thus, we find:

\displaystyle\lambda=\rho^{-2},\quad\sigma=A\cdot\sqrt{\lambda}\lambda^{\prime}.

(45)

Here
A
is some constant. Two equations (
45
) for three functions admit many solutions, the selection of which is not clear.

Let domain I be located in the region
z>0
. Consider, for example, the following solution in domain I:

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{(a^{2}+z^{2})^{1/4}}{\sqrt{z}},\quad\lambda=\frac{z}{\sqrt{a^{2}+z^{2}}},\quad\sigma=A\frac{\sqrt{z}}{(a^{2}+z^{2})^{7/4}},\quad a=\mathop{\rm const}\nolimits.

(46)

Obviously, in the region
z<0
, that is, in domain II we have the following solution to the system of equations
(
44
):

\displaystyle-\rho(-z),\quad-\lambda(-z),\quad-\sigma(-z).

(47)

Thus, a tetrad is constructed that satisfies the Einstein equation everywhere except for the plane
z=0
and which is antisymmetric with respect to this plane. According to (
28
), together with the tetrad, the average
\Theta^{a}_{\mu}
changes its sign. In this case, the time part of the tetrad
\rho
jumps through infinity.

From the above formulas it follows that for a given solution the tetrad in the region
z<0
cannot be obtained from the tetrad in the region
z>0
by analytic continuation. If we take into account that we assume a lattice regularization of the theory, then this fact does not seem to be inadmissible.

We point out that the total action of the system remains finite near the domain wall. Indeed, we used the equations
\delta{\mathop{{\mathfrak{A}}}\nolimits}_{g}/\delta e^{a}_{\mu}=0
, from which it follows that the Ricci tensor is equal to zero. In this case, the gravitational part of the action also vanishes. We also have:

\displaystyle\mathop{{\mathfrak{A}}}\nolimits_{\Psi}+\mathop{{\mathfrak{A}}}\nolimits_{\Lambda}\sim e^{0}_{0}e^{1}_{1}e^{2}_{2}e^{3}_{3}=\rho\lambda^{2}\sigma\sim\frac{z^{2}}{(a^{2}+z^{2})^{5/2}}.

This quantity converges upon integration along the
z
axis. This implies the finiteness of the total action of the system for a unit of 4-volume including the final section of the domain wall.

As mentioned above, the author is unaware of any papers on domain walls in the theory of gravity that affect the structure of the Dirac sea. Therefore, generally speaking, there can be no direct comparison of our results with the results of other works on domain walls. Nevertheless, such a comparison can be made in this Subsection, since the condition (
41
) actually excludes the Dirac sea from consideration.
Thus, equations (
41
)-(
45
) also have a Kasner solution in domain I
(see
Vilenkin 1981
, Eq. (36)):

\displaystyle A=\frac{3}{2},\quad\rho=z^{-1/3},\quad\lambda=z^{2/3},\quad\sigma=1.

(48)

Apparently, this solution is even less suitable for describing the domain wall we need.

III.3
Time-dependent solution

Let us consider the case of a domain wall depending on one spatial coordinate and on time.
From a physical point of view, it seems reasonable to consider the case when the domain wall moves parallel to itself at a constant speed. Obviously, the meaningful case takes place if the wall moves along the
z
axis with the speed of light. Otherwise, the problem is reduced using the Lorentz transformation to the problem already considered in Section III A.
In other words, all variables must depend on the combination
\psi=(t\pm z)
. Consider the possibility
\psi=(t-z)
. Then the equations (
37
) take the form

\displaystyle e^{0}_{0}+e^{0}_{3}=0,\quad e^{3}_{0}+e^{3}_{3}=0.

(49)

Thus

\displaystyle e^{0}=\rho(\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits t-\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits z),\quad e^{1}=\lambda\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits x,\quad e^{2}=\lambda\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits y,\quad e^{3}=\sigma(\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits t-\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits z).

(50)

We assume that
|\sigma|<|\rho|
. The opposite case has no physical meaning, since then the metric would not have timelike intervals. With the help of the (local) Lorentz transformation, we vanish the component of the tetrad
e^{3}
, which is possible due to the inequality
|\sigma|<|\rho|
. Although the tetrad (
50
) is degenerate, it is easy to prove that in the used gauge the equation (
30
) has only one solution for the connection. Indeed, in the gauge
e^{3}=0
we have
\omega_{3a}=-\omega_{a3}=0
. Obviously, in the expansion
\omega_{ab}=\gamma_{abc}e^{c}
we can assume that
\gamma_{ab3}=0
. Thus,
\gamma_{abc}=0
if at least one of the indices
a,b,c
is 3. The remaining components
\gamma_{abc}
with indices
a,b,c=0,1,2
are determined unambiguously in the standard way using equations (
30
).

Bypassing intermediate calculations, we write out the nonzero components of the Riemann tensor
\mathop{{\mathfrak{R}}}\nolimits^{ab}_{\mu\nu}
:

\displaystyle\mathop{{\mathfrak{R}}}\nolimits^{01}_{01}=\mathop{{\mathfrak{R}}}\nolimits^{01}_{13}=\mathop{{\mathfrak{R}}}\nolimits^{02}_{02}=\mathop{{\mathfrak{R}}}\nolimits^{02}_{23}=-\frac{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}{\rho}+\frac{\lambda^{\prime}\rho^{\prime}}{\rho^{2}},\quad\mathop{{\mathfrak{R}}}\nolimits^{12}_{12}=-\frac{\lambda^{\prime 2}}{\rho^{2}}.

(51)

In this subsection, everywhere
F^{\prime}
means the derivative of the function
F
with respect to the variable
\psi=(t-z)
. All components of the Einstein equation are reduced to one equation:

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda\lambda^{\prime\prime}}{\rho}-\frac{\lambda^{\prime 2}}{2\rho}+\frac{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}\rho^{\prime}}{\rho^{2}}=\frac{3}{4}l_{P}^{2}\tilde{f}\rho\lambda^{2},

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(\psi)=\tilde{f}(-\psi),\quad|\tilde{f}|\longrightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad|\psi|\longrightarrow\infty.

(52)

We assume that in domain I the variable
\psi>0
, and in domain II
\psi<0
.

As above, Einstein’s equation does not fix the metric. Consider the following solution to the equation (
52
) in the region
\psi>0
:

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{\sqrt{\psi}}{(a^{2}+\psi^{2})^{1/4}},\quad\lambda=\frac{\psi}{\sqrt{a^{2}+\psi^{2}}},\quad a=\mathop{\rm const}\nolimits\gg l^{2}_{P}.

(53)

The solution in the region
\psi<0
is obtained from the solution (
53
) by reflection relative to the plane
\psi=0
according to (
47
) with the replacement of
z\longrightarrow\psi
. From equations (
52
) and (
53
) we find:

\displaystyle\tilde{f}=\frac{4a^{2}}{l^{2}_{P}|\psi|(a^{2}+\psi^{2})^{3/2}}.

(54)

IV
Discussion

Option C seems to be the most interesting. In coordinates
(t,\,x,\,y,\,z)
metric has the form

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits s^{2}=\rho^{2}(\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits t-\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits z)^{2}-\lambda^{2}\left(\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits x^{2}+\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits y^{2}\right).

(55)

This metric is degenerate.

We assume that in the region

\displaystyle\psi^{2}\gg a^{2}\gg l^{2}_{P}

(56)

the metric approaches non-degenerate and relaxes to its normal (quasi) flat form.

Consider a constant time surface
t=t_{0}
,
\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits t=0
,
\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits y=0
. The variable
\psi
becomes dependent only on the coordinate
z
:
\psi=\pm(z-t_{0})
. Then, according to (
53
) and (
55
), in the case of
\psi\longrightarrow 0
the solution to the equation
\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits s=0
has the form

\displaystyle z-t_{0}=\pm\frac{1}{4a}(x-x_{0})^{2}.

(57)

Thus, upon displacement near the domain wall along the curve
(
57
) we have
\int\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits s=0
, that is, the physical length of such an arc is zero.
It follows from this that in a model description of the domain wall, it can be considered infinitely thin.
But in this case it does not matter which solution of the equation
(
52
) is chosen because only the asymptotics for
|\psi|\longrightarrow\infty
are important.

Since the metric is degenerate near the domain wall, it is obvious that near the domain wall the action tends to zero, that is, the action is finite.

The text below is not a complete scientific result, but material for research, since some important aspects remain unresolved here. In particular, the author considers it necessary to thoroughly study the formulas (
63
)-(
65
) and the physics described by these formulas.

Consider a conserved Dirac current
J^{a}=\overline{\Psi}\gamma^{a}\Psi
near the domain wall. To calculate it, we use the general formula

\displaystyle J^{a}=\mbox{Re}\Big\{-i\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits\gamma^{a}S_{c}(x,y)|_{|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|=0,\,x^{0}-y^{0}\longrightarrow-0}\Big\}.

(58)

Hence, using the formulas (
23
) and (
24
) and the fact that here
\varepsilon\sim l^{2}_{P}
, we find

\displaystyle J^{a}=-\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\eta^{a\nu}\partial_{\nu}\frac{\varepsilon}{\xi^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\Big|_{|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|=0,\,x^{0}-y^{0}=-l_{P}},\quad\xi=-(x-y)^{2}+l^{2}_{P}.

(59)

Obviously, in (
59
) we can put
\xi=0
even before differentiation, and therefore

\displaystyle J^{a}(x)=-\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\eta^{a\nu}\partial_{\nu}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sim-\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\eta^{a\nu}\partial_{\nu}\frac{1}{l^{2}_{P}(\psi).}

(60)

According to (
27
) and (
28
)
f=2/(\pi^{2}l_{P}^{4})
. Therefore, for the current
(
60
) we get:

\displaystyle J^{a}=-\frac{1}{4}l_{P}^{2}\eta^{a\nu}\partial_{\nu}f=-\frac{1}{4}l_{P}^{2}\eta^{a\nu}\partial_{\nu}\tilde{f}.

(61)

Substituting here
\tilde{f}
from (
54
), we finally find the formula for the charge density of fermions
in domain I:

\displaystyle J^{0}(\psi)=\frac{a^{2}(a^{2}+4\psi^{2})}{|\psi|^{2}(a^{2}+\psi^{2})^{5/2}}>0.

(62)

According to (
62
)
J^{0}>0
, that is, near the domain wall, the density of particles (but not antiparticles) is finite.

The charge (number of particles)

\displaystyle{\cal N}=\frac{1}{6}\varepsilon_{abcd}\int_{\Sigma}J^{a}e^{b}\wedge e^{c}\wedge e^{d}

(63)

is conserved if
\nabla_{a}J^{a}=0
. The latter property takes place in the theory under consideration.
Here
\Sigma
there is a certain space-like hypersurface.
Note that the lattice analog of the number of particles is also strictly conserved. According to
(
9
)
(J^{PT})^{a}=J^{a}
. By definition, in domain I, the ground state is denoted

|0\rangle
, and in domain II, as
|0;PT\rangle
. Thus, we have:

\displaystyle\langle 0|J^{a}|0\rangle=\langle 0|(J^{PT})^{a}|0\rangle=\langle 0;PT|J^{a}|0;PT\rangle.

(64)

We split the integral (
63
) into 2 integrals: the integral over domain I and over domain II and denote the corresponding integrals as
{\cal N}_{I}
and
{\cal N}_{II}
. Then the average of the number of particles (
63
) is divided into 2 terms:

\displaystyle\langle{\cal N}\rangle=\langle{\cal N}_{I}\rangle+\langle PT|{\cal N}_{II}|PT\rangle=\mathop{\rm const}\nolimits.

(65)

According to (
62
) we have
\langle{\cal N}_{I}\rangle>0
, while according to
(
16
) and (
64
)
\langle PT|{\cal N}_{II}|PT\rangle<0
.
In this case, the sum (
65
) can be equal to zero, which is further assumed.

Consider the particle density in the region
\psi>\psi_{0}=t_{0}-z_{0}\gg a
. Let us introduce the particle distribution function
\delta n(\psi,{\bf p})
, so

\displaystyle J^{0}(\psi)=\int\frac{\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits^{(3)}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\delta n(\psi,{\bf p}).

(66)

It is very important here that the quasi-momentum of a quasiparticle on an irregular ”breathing” lattice

Vergeles 2021
cannot be strictly conserved. Therefore, it is natural to assume that
\delta n(\psi,{\bf p})>0
for any
{\bf p}
.
Let us write out the kinetic equation for this distribution function:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\delta n}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial\epsilon}{\partial{\bf p}}\cdot\frac{\partial\delta n}{\partial{\bf r}}-\frac{\partial\epsilon}{\partial{\bf r}}\cdot\frac{\partial\delta n}{\partial{\bf p}}=0.

(67)

We assume that
\epsilon({\bf p})=|{\bf p}|
, and omit the collision integral on the right-hand side
of kinetic equation. This means that we neglect dissipative phenomena in further reasoning, which does not devalue the qualitative conclusions. Since
\partial\epsilon/\partial{\bf r}=0
, the last term in the equation (
67
) is missing.

We have
\partial\epsilon/\partial{\bf p}={\bf p}/\epsilon={\bf n}
. For the momentum
{\bf p}_{-}=(0,0,-|{\bf p}|)
, directed in the opposite direction from the domain wall, equation (
67
)
takes the form

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\delta n}{\partial t}-\frac{\partial\delta n}{\partial z}=0.

(68)

This equation can be easily integrated: on the trajectory
t(s)=s+t_{0}
,
z(s)=-s+z_{0}
, the function
\delta n
remains constant:

\displaystyle\delta n(t=s+t_{0},\,z=-s+z_{0},\,{\bf p}_{-})=\mathop{\rm const}\nolimits>0.

(69)

On the same trajectory
\psi(s)=2s+\psi_{0}
. Of course the
s
-parameter in the solution (
69
) should remain small enough, since the collision integral was not taken into account when finding the solution.

Equation (
69
) shows that there is a dynamic tendency to the emergence of undamped positive particle density at any distance from the domain wall. It was said above that the total number of particles
(see Eq. (
65
)) is conserved. The question of where antiparticles accumulate remains open.

Although the presentation in this Section is very schematic, nevertheless it seems to us that the question ”is not gravity a source of baryon asymmetry in the Universe?” deserves consideration.

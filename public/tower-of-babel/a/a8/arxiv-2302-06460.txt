QCD equation of state at finite chemical potential from unbiased exponential resummation of the lattice QCD Taylor series
Sabarnya Mitra, Prasad Hegde

Abstract
Exponential resummation of the QCD finite-density Taylor series has been recently introduced as an alternative way of resumming the finite-density lattice QCD Taylor series. Unfortunately the usual exponential resummation formula suffers from stochastic bias which must be subtracted before identifying genuine higher-order contributions. In this paper, we present a new way of subtracting the stochastic bias at the level of each individual gauge configuration, up to a certain order of either the Taylor series or the cumulant expansion, by modifying the argument of the exponential. Retaining the exponential form of the resummation allows us to also calculate the phase factor of the fermion determinant on each gauge configuration. We present our results for the excess pressure, number density, and the average phase factor and show that the new results contain less stochastic bias and are in better agreement with the QCD Taylor series compared to the previous exponential resummation.

Stochastic bias is not an issue in the calculation of the Taylor coefficients, although such products also appear there, because there exist formulas for efficiently evaluating the unbiased estimate of

\displaystyle X^{Y}_{N}(T,\mu_{Y})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{D^{Y}_{n}(T)}{n!}\left(\frac{\mu_{Y}}{T}\right)^{n}.

The first four cumulants are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{1}(X^{Y}_{N})

\displaystyle=\langle X^{Y}_{N}\rangle,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{2}(X^{Y}_{N})

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{Y}_{2}=\overline{D^{Y}_{2}}+\left(\overline{(D^{Y}_{1})^{2}}-\left(\overline{D^{Y}_{1}}\right)^{2}\right),

\displaystyle\phantom{\mathcal{C}^{Y}_{4}}-12\left(\overline{D^{Y}_{2}D^{Y}_{1}}\;\overline{D^{Y}_{1}}-\overline{D^{Y}_{2}}\left(\overline{D^{Y}_{1}}\right)^{2}\right)+\overline{(D^{Y}_{1})^{4}}-4\,\,\overline{(D^{Y}_{1})^{3}}\;\overline{D^{Y}_{1}}+12\,\overline{(D^{Y}_{1})^{2}}\left(\overline{D^{Y}_{1}}\right)^{2}-6\left(\overline{D^{Y}_{1}}\right)^{4},\quad\text{etc.}

IV
Discussion and Outlook

Exponential resummation has been previously introduced as a new way of resumming the finite-density QCD Taylor series
Mondal:2021jxk
. The contribution of the the
n
th
\hat{\mu}_{Y}
derivative
D^{Y}_{n}(T)
of
\ln\det M(T,\mu_{Y})
, where
M(T,\mu_{Y})
is the fermion matrix, to all orders in
\mu_{Y}
is equal to
\exp(D^{Y}_{n}(T)\hat{\mu}_{Y}^{n}/n!)
. In this way, the contribution of the first
N
derivatives
D^{Y}_{1},\dots,D_{N}^{Y}
that are calculated during the
N
th order Taylor series calculation can be obtained to all orders in
\hat{\mu}_{Y}
. However as the
D^{Y}_{n}
are calculated stochastically, the exponential contains stochastic bias which needs to be subtracted before genuine higher-order contributions can be identified.

In this paper, we presented a new way of carrying out the exponential resummation in which the stochastic bias was subtracted, at the level of each individual gauge configuration, up to a finite order in
\hat{\mu}_{Y}
or the cumulant expansion. We applied our formalism to calculate the excess pressure and number density at finite isospin as well as finite baryon chemical potential. Our results were in good agreement with the Taylor series results, both for real as well as imaginary chemical potentials, up to
|\hat{\mu}_{I}^{2}|\leqslant 4
(up to
|\hat{\mu}_{B}^{2}|\leqslant 2
). We also calculated the average phase factor as a function of
\hat{\mu}_{B}
using both biased and unbiased resummations. As observed previously
Mondal:2021jxk
, the vanishing of the phase factor is accompanied by a breakdown of the calculation. The value
\hat{\mu}_{B}=\hat{\mu}_{B}^{c}
at which the breakdown occurs differs between the biased and unbiased resummations, with the differences increasing as the temperature is decreased. The breakdown of the calculation has physical significance as
\hat{\mu}_{B}^{c}
is the distance from the origin to the closest zero
\hat{\mu}_{B0}
of the QCD partition function
\mathcal{Z}(T,\mu_{B})
in the complex
\hat{\mu}_{B}
plane. Hence the vanishing of the phase factor could provide yet another way of locating the zeros of
\mathcal{Z}(T,\mu_{B})
(equivalently, the singularities of
\ln\mathcal{Z}(T,\mu_{B})
in the complex
\hat{\mu}_{B}
plane. Then it would be important to obtain an unbiased determination of
\hat{\mu}_{B}^{c}
, especially as the biased and unbiased estimates differ significantly at lower temperatures.

We also note that with exponential resummation, it is possible to calculate the QCD partition function
\mathcal{Z}(T,\mu_{B})
itself. By comparison, the QCD Taylor series is an expansion of
\ln\mathcal{Z}(T,\mu_{B})
. The finite Taylor series is analytic over the entire complex
\hat{\mu}_{B}
plane, whereas our resummation makes it possible to calculate the zeros of
\mathcal{Z}(T,\mu_{B})
and hence directly determine the location of poles or branch singularities that could correspond to the location of the much sought after QCD critical point
Stephanov:2006dn
;
Fukushima:2010bq
;
Mukherjee:2019eou
. This has been done previously
Mondal:2021jxk
;
Mukherjee:2021tyg
, but we hope to repeat these calculations in the future using our new formalism in order to obtain more reliable estimates of these important observables.

Appendix A
Proof of the Unbiasedness of Eq. (
13
) to
\mathcal{O}(\mu_{Y}^{4})

In Sec.
II
, we stated without proof that Eqs. (
13
) and (
14
) (with
N=4
) resum the first four derivatives
D^{Y}_{1},\dots,D^{Y}_{4}
in such a way that the resulting exponential as well as the excess pressure are both unbiased to
\mathcal{O}\left(\mu_{Y}^{4}\right)
where
Y\equiv B,I
. To see why this is so, we start by Taylor-expanding the exponential in Eq. (
13
). To
\mathcal{O}\left(\mu_{Y}^{4}\right)
, one obtains (with
\hat{\mu}_{Y}\equiv\mu_{Y}/T)
:

\displaystyle\exp\left[\sum_{n=1}^{4}\frac{\mathcal{C}^{Y}_{n}(T)}{n!}\left(\frac{\mu_{Y}}{T}\right)^{n}\right]=\;\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\,\frac{1}{k!}\,\left[\sum_{n=1}^{4}\frac{\mathcal{C}^{Y}_{n}(T)}{n!}\left(\frac{\mu_{Y}}{T}\right)^{n}\right]^{k}=1+\sum_{k=1}^{4}\,\mathcal{A}^{Y}_{k}(T)\,\frac{\hat{\mu}_{Y}^{\,k}}{k!}+\mathcal{O}\left(\hat{\mu}_{Y}^{5}\right),

(18)

where the
\mathcal{A}^{Y}_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,4
are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{Y}_{1}(T)

\displaystyle\;=\;\overline{D^{Y}_{1}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{Y}_{2}(T)

\displaystyle\;=\;\overline{D^{Y}_{2}}+\overline{(D^{Y}_{1})^{2}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{Y}_{3}(T)

\displaystyle\;=\;\overline{D^{Y}_{3}}+3\,\overline{D^{Y}_{2}D^{Y}_{1}}+\overline{(D^{Y}_{1})^{3}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{Y}_{4}(T)

\displaystyle\;=\;\overline{D^{Y}_{4}}+3\,\overline{(D^{Y}_{2})^{2}}+4\,\overline{D^{Y}_{3}D^{Y}_{1}}+6\,\overline{D^{Y}_{2}(D^{Y}_{1})^{2}}+\overline{(D^{Y}_{1})^{4}}.

(19)

We note that the
\mathcal{A}^{Y}_{k}
are just the derivatives of
\det\mathcal{M}
w.r.t.
\hat{\mu}_{Y}

Allton:2005gk

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{Y}_{k}(T)\equiv\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial\hat{\mu}_{Y}^{k}}\Big[\det\mathcal{M}(T,\mu_{Y})\Big]_{\mu_{Y}=0},

(20)

but with the terms appearing in the derivative evaluated in an unbiased manner. Now, as per Eq. (
13
), we need to extract the real part of the exponential. This means that the above series becomes an even series in
\hat{\mu}_{Y}
, since the coefficients of even (odd) powers of
\mu_{Y}
are purely real (imaginary). We therefore have:

\frac{\Delta P_{4}^{R(\text{unb})}}{T^{4}}=\frac{N_{\tau}^{3}}{N_{\sigma}^{3}}\,\ln\left\langle 1+\sum_{k=1}^{2}\,\mathcal{A}^{Y}_{2k}(T)\,\frac{\hat{\mu}_{Y}^{\,2k}}{(2k)!}+\mathcal{O}\left(\hat{\mu}_{Y}^{6}\right)\right\rangle.

(21)

We compute
\Delta P_{4}^{R\text{(unb)}(T,\mu_{Y})}/T^{4}
in the above equation by using the well-known formula for
\ln(1+x)
, namely

\ln(1+x)=x-\frac{x^{2}}{2}+\mathcal{O}(x^{3}).

(22)

Collecting coefficients upto
\mathcal{O}(\mu_{Y}^{4})
, we find the following:

\frac{\Delta P_{4}^{R(\text{unb})}}{T^{4}}=\frac{N_{\tau}^{3}}{N_{\sigma}^{3}}\left[\frac{\langle\mathcal{A}^{Y}_{2}\rangle}{2!}+\frac{\langle\mathcal{A}^{Y}_{4}\rangle-3\langle\mathcal{A}^{Y}_{2}\rangle^{2}}{4!}\right]+\mathcal{O}(\mu_{Y}^{6}).

(23)

This is just the Taylor series expansion
\Delta P^{T}_{4}(T,\mu_{B})
of the excess pressure to fourth order i.e.

\frac{\Delta P_{4}^{R(\text{unb})}}{T^{4}}=\frac{\chi_{2}^{Y}(T)}{2!}\left(\frac{\mu_{Y}}{T}\right)^{2}+\frac{\chi^{Y}_{4}(T)}{4!}\left(\frac{\mu_{Y}}{T}\right)^{4}+\mathcal{O}(\mu_{Y}^{6}),

(24)

with the Taylor coefficients given by the usual formulas
Allton:2005gk

\chi_{2}^{Y}=\frac{N_{\tau}^{3}}{N_{\sigma}^{3}}\,\big\langle\mathcal{A}^{Y}_{2}\big\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad\chi_{4}^{Y}=\frac{N_{\tau}^{3}}{N_{\sigma}^{3}}\bigg(\big\langle\mathcal{A}^{Y}_{4}\big\rangle-3\big\langle\mathcal{A}^{Y}_{2}\big\rangle^{2}\bigg).

(25)

Thus we find that Eq. (
13
) reproduces the usual Taylor series expansion of the excess pressure to
\mathcal{O}\left(\mu_{Y}^{4}\right)
. Since the Taylor coefficients are calculated in an unbiased manner, we conclude that the exponential in Eq. (
13
) too is unbiased to
\mathcal{O}\left(\mu_{Y}^{4}\right)
.

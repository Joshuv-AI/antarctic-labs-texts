Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem via random walks
Lior Bary-Soroker, Daniele Garzoni

Abstract
. Let G G be a connected linear algebraic group over a number field K K , let Γ \Gamma be a finitely generated Zariski dense subgroup of G ⁡ ( K ) G(K) and let Z ⊆ G ⁡ ( K ) Z\subseteq G(K) be a thin set, in the sense of Serre. We prove that, if G / R u ​ ( G ) G/\mathrm{R}_{u}(G) is semisimple and Z Z satisfies certain necessary conditions, then a long random walk on a Cayley graph of Γ \Gamma hits elements of Z Z with negligible probability. We deduce corollaries to Galois covers, characteristic polynomials, and fixed points in group actions. We also prove analogous results in the case where K K is a global function field.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem

This paper concerns generalizations of the celebrated Hilbert’s Irreducibility Theorem (HIT). In polynomial terms, HIT asserts that, for every
n\geqslant 1
, if
f(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},X)\in\mathbf{Q}(t_{1},...,t_{n})[X]
is irreducible, then there exists an
irreducible specialization
, that is to say,
(\underline{t_{1}},\ldots,\underline{t_{n}})\in\mathbf{Q}^{n}
such that
f(\underline{t_{1}},\ldots,\underline{t_{n}},X)\in\mathbf{Q}[X]
remains irreducible. Moreover, the Galois group of
f(\underline{t_{1}},\ldots,\underline{t_{n}},X)
over
\mathbf{Q}
is isomorphic to the Galois group of
f(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},X)
over
\mathbf{Q}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
. Hilbert’s original motivation came from the inverse Galois problem: In order to realize a finite group as a Galois group over
\mathbf{Q}
, given HIT it is sufficient to realize it over
\mathbf{Q}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
, where one has more tools.

HIT found numerous applications in number theory, algebraic geometry, and algebra, see
[
Ser89
,
Ser16
,
FJ08
,
MM18
]
.

HIT has an equivalent formulation in terms of rational points, an approach with traces going back at least to Hilbert’s works on the inverse Galois problem. It uses the notion of
thin sets
, introduced by Serre
[
Ser89
]
and Colliot-Thélène–Sansuc
[
CTS87
]
.
In this paper, a
K
-variety is a separated
K
-scheme of finite type.

Definition
.

(1)

Let
K
be a field and
V
be an irreducible
K
-variety.
A subset
Z
of
V(K)
is called
thin
if

(1)

Z\subseteq C(K)\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}\pi_{i}(V_{i}(K)),

where
C
is a proper closed subset of
V
,
t\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
, and for every
i=1,\ldots,t
,
V_{i}
is an irreducible
K
-variety,
\mathrm{dim}(V_{i})=\mathrm{dim}(V)
, and
\pi_{i}\colon V_{i}\to V
is a dominant separable morphism with
\deg(\pi_{i})\geqslant 2
.

(2)

We say that
V
has the
Hilbert property
(HP) if
V(K)
is not thin.

HIT is equivalent to the statement that
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\mathbf{Q}}
has the HP for every
n\geqslant 1
. Varieties having the HP satisfy an irreducibility specialization property, see
[
Ser16
, Proposition 3.3.5]
. Every connected linear algebraic group over a number field has the HP
[
San81
,
CTS87
,
BSFP14
]
.
See
[
CZ17
]
, where the HP is proven
for a geometrically non-rational variety for the first time, and see its extensions in

[
Dem20
]
.

For many
V
satisfying the HP, not only
V(K)
is not thin, but a thin set
Z
is small in
V(K)
in much stronger senses. For example, if
V=\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\mathbf{Q}}
, then
H=V(\mathbf{Q})\smallsetminus Z
is dense in the real topology, it contains arithmetic progressions, and
H
has asymptotic density
1
, with respect to counting either integral or rational points by height.

For a connected linear algebraic group
V=G
over a number field
K
, Liu
[
Liu21
]
and Corvaja
[
Cor07
]
, based on Zannier
[
Zan10
]
and Ferretti–Zannier
[
FZ07
]
, proved that thin sets
Z
are small with respect to the group structure. More precisely, let
\Gamma
be a Zariski dense subgroup of
G(K)

and let
Z
be a thin set of
G(K)
as in (
1
). For every
i
, assume that
V_{i}
is normal and
\pi_{i}\colon V_{i}\to G
is finite and
ramified
. Then,
\Gamma\nsubseteq Z
.

The ramification assumption is essential for the conclusion. Indeed, if
\pi\colon G_{1}\to G
is a nontrivial isogeny of algebraic groups; in particular,
\pi
is unramified, then
\Gamma:=\pi(G_{1}(K))
is a Zariski dense subgroup of
G(K)
, and also a thin set. We remark that the connection between the HP and ramification is made explicit in
[
CZ17
]
.

The objective of this paper is to obtain a quantitative version of this result. Loosely speaking, we will show that, under suitable conditions on
G
, “most” points of
\Gamma
do not belong to
Z
. We will measure this by performing long random walks on a Cayley graph of
\Gamma
. This direction is inspired by the works of Rivin
[
Riv08
]
, Jouve–Kowalski–Zywina
[
JKZ13
]
, Lubotzky–Meiri
[
LM12
]
, and Lubotzky–Rosenzweig
[
LR14
]
.

1.2.
Random walks

We fix notation for this subsection.

Notation list 1
.

\diamond

K
is a number field.

\diamond

G
is a connected linear algebraic
K
-group such that
G/\text{R}_{u}(G)
is either trivial or semisimple.

\diamond

\Gamma
is a finitely generated Zariski dense subgroup of
G(K)
.

\diamond

A
is a finite symmetric generating multiset of
\Gamma
, with
1\in A
.

\diamond

\omega_{n}
is the
n
-th step of a random walk on the Cayley graph
\mathrm{Cay}(\Gamma,A)
, starting at the identity.

\diamond

For every
G
, we define a function
\Xi\colon\mathbf{R}_{>0}\times\mathbf{Z}_{\geqslant 1}\to\mathbf{R}_{>0}
, as follows:

\Xi(C,n)=\begin{cases}e^{-n/C}&\mbox{if $G$ is semisimple}\\
Cn^{-1/(10\,\mathrm{dim}\,G)}&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Let us clarify the notation:
By
\mathrm{R}_{u}(G)
, we denote the unipotent radical of
G
.
By
A
symmetric, we mean that
g
and
g^{-1}
appear in
A
with the same multiplicity, for every
g\in\Gamma
. The Cayley graph
\mathrm{Cay}(\Gamma,A)
attached to
\Gamma
and
A
has
\Gamma
as the set of vertices and
\{(g,ga):a\in A,g\in\Gamma\}
as the multiset of edges. Then,
\omega_{n}
is the
n
-th step of the uniform random walk on
\mathrm{Cay}(\Gamma,A)
, starting at the identity.

Our main theorem asserts that, under our assumptions on
G
, the random walk
\omega_{n}
is asymptotically almost never in a thin set:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
Z
be a thin subset of
G(K)
as in (
1
) with the additional assumptions that
V_{i}
is normal and
\pi_{i}\colon V_{i}\to G
is finite and ramified, for each
i
. Then,
there exists
C>0
such that

\mathbf{P}(\omega_{n}\in Z)\leqslant\Xi(C,n).

In particular,
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{P}(\omega_{n}\in Z)=0
.

If
G
is semisimple, Theorem
1.1
gives exponential decay. When
G
is not semisimple, one cannot get exponential decay. Indeed, take
G=\mathbb{A}^{1}
,
\Gamma=\mathbf{Z}
,
A=\{0,1,-1\}
, and consider the thin set
Z=\{0\}
. Then,
\omega_{n}
is a classical one dimensional random walk, hence

(2)

\mathbf{P}(\omega_{n}\in Z)=\mathbf{P}(\omega_{n}=0)\geqslant cn^{-1/2}.

for some
c>0
.

If
G
is not semisimple, then
C
in Theorem
1.1
depends on
G
,
K
,
\Gamma
,
|A|
, and on
Z
. In fact, the dependence on
Z
is via the
complexity
, see Definition
2.1
. If
G
is semisimple,
C

depends also on the spectral gap of the Cayley graphs induced on the congruent quotients of
\Gamma
; see §
2
for further details. In all the theorems of the introduction,
C
will depend on these parameters.

As in Liu’s result, the assumption that the morphisms
\pi_{i}
are ramified is crucial: If
\pi\colon G_{1}\to G
is a nontrivial isogeny of algebraic groups, then
\mathbf{P}(\omega_{n}\in\pi(G_{1}(K)))\geqslant c>0
. See Remark
3.2
below for further details.

The assumption that the varieties
V_{i}
are normal and that the morphisms are finite is technical and can be removed. See Remark
3.3
and Lemma
3.1
.

It seems that Theorem
1.1
was known to experts in the case where
G
is semisimple and simply connected (
[
Kow10
, § 6.2]
).
We note that the estimates in
loc. cit.
are not valid for semisimple non-simply connected groups, as the example of a nontrivial isogeny shows.

We apply Theorem
1.1
to get irreducibility, in the spirit of the classical HIT formulation.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
V
be a normal irreducible
K
-variety, and
\pi\colon V\to G
be a finite surjective morphism. Assume that every non-scalar subcover of
\pi
is ramified.
Then, there exists
C>0
such that

\mathbf{P}(\pi^{-1}(\omega_{n})\mbox{ is not integral\,})\leqslant\Xi(C,n).

The original motivation of Hilbert came from Galois theory, where he used HIT to specialize Galois groups from
\mathbf{Q}(t)
to
\mathbf{Q}
. The next application is in this spirit.
If
\pi\colon V\to G
is a generically Galois cover of normal irreducible
K
-varieties with group
\mathcal{G}=\mathrm{Gal}(K(V)/K(G))
, then every
g\in G(K)
comes with the
specialized Galois group
, denoted
\mathcal{G}_{g}
, which is the automorphism group of the residue field extension at a point
x\in V
lying above
g
; this group is isomorphic to the decomposition group modulo the inertia group. Hence, the specialized Galois group is always a sub-quotient of
\mathcal{G}
. We show that typically it equals
\mathcal{G}
:

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
V
be a normal irreducible
K
-variety, and
\pi\colon V\to G
be a finite surjective morphism, which is generically Galois with group
\mathcal{G}
. Assume that every nontrivial geometrically irreducible subcover of
\pi
is ramified.
Then, there exists
C>0
such that

\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{G}_{\omega_{n}}\neq\mathcal{G})\leqslant\Xi(C,n).

A different sense for smallness of a thin set
Z
in an algebraic group
G
is the following: Take
\Gamma\subseteq G(K)
a finitely generated Zariski dense subgroup and prove that there exists a finite index coset that is disjoint from
Z
. As in our case, a ramification condition is necessary. Schinzel
[
Sch65
]
proved it for the additive group
\mathbb{G}_{a}
, Zannier
[
Zan10
]
for a torus or a power of a non-CM elliptic curve. Corvaja, Demeio, Javanpeykar, Lombardo, and Zannier
[
CDJ+20
]
generalized the latter to any abelian variety, and Liu
[
Liu21
]
and Corvaja
[
Cor07
]
generalized it to any connected linear algebraic group.

1.3.
Characteristic polynomial

We are still under Notation List
1
. The typical Galois group of the characteristic polynomial of
\omega_{n}
is well studied in the literature, see
[
Riv08
,
JKZ13
,
LR14
]
.

Our objective in this subsection is to show how these type of results follow from the general result on generic Galois groups (Corollary
1.3
). The advantage of our approach is that we do not use anything on the characteristic polynomial, except for the computation of the generic Galois group by Prasad and Rapinchuk. The original proofs use more specific information, see for instance
[
JKZ13
, § 4 and Proposition 4.1]
.

Fix an embedding
\rho\colon G\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{N}
. Then, we have the generic characteristic polynomial
\chi_{G,\rho}\in K(G)[X]
such that
\chi_{G,\rho}|_{g}=\mathrm{det}(X-g^{\rho})
for every
g\in G(K)
. Let
V
be the normalization of
G
in the splitting field
F
of
\chi_{G,\rho}
over
K(G)
, and let
\pi\colon V\to G
be the corresponding finite morphism, which is generically Galois.

It turns out that
F
is independent of
\rho
. It follows from a result of Prasad and Rapinchuk
[
PR03
]
that
\mathrm{Gal}(F/K(G))
is isomorphic to a certain extension of the Weyl group
W(G)
of
G
, that we denote by
\Pi(G)
. For instance, if
G
is split, then
\Pi(G)=W(G)
. See §
4.1
for the precise description of
\Pi(G)
.

It is easy to see that, in this problem, one can replace
G
by
G/\mathrm{R}_{u}(G)
. We will deduce from Corollary
1.3
the following:

Corollary 1.4
.

Assume that
G/\mathrm{R}_{u}(G)
is either trivial or semisimple, and put
\chi_{n}=\mathrm{det}(X-\omega_{n}^{\rho})
.
Then, there exists
C>0
such that

\mathbf{P}(\mathrm{Gal}(\chi_{n}/K)\not\cong\Pi(G))\leqslant e^{-n/C}.

A more general result was proved by Lubotzky and Rosenzweig
[
LR14
]
, who also considered non-connected groups.

In the case of arithmetic subgroups of
simple simply connected groups over a number field, Gorodnik and Nevo
[
GN11
]
considered the typical characteristic polynomial of elements by counting in archimedean balls, instead of using random walks. This is a substantially different problem. We remark that the methods in
[
GN11
]
can be used to deal with thin sets in the archimedean model.

1.4.
Fixed points

We are still under Notation list
1
. In this subsection, we deal with actions of
G
on varieties
X
. We show that typically
\omega_{n}
has no rational fixed points, unless generically every element has a fixed point. Our result is the random walk analogue of a theorem of Corvaja
[
Cor07
]
.

Let us be more precise. Assume that
G
acts, as a group scheme, on a
K
-variety
X
. We can then consider the variety of fixed points

V=\{(x,g)\mid xg=g\}\subseteq X\times G.

It is equipped with the natural projection morphism
\pi\colon V\to G
. Let
\accentset{\rule{5.7361pt}{0.8pt}}{K}
be an algebraic closure of
K
. In order to apply Theorem
1.1
, we need the existence of a Zariski dense subset of
G(\accentset{\rule{5.7361pt}{0.8pt}}{K})
consisting of elements fixing finitely many points on
X(\accentset{\rule{5.7361pt}{0.8pt}}{K})
. For technical reasons, we will also require that, letting
p\colon\widetilde{G}\to G
be the universal cover of
G
(which exists since
G/\mathrm{R}_{u}(G)
is semisimple), we have that
p^{-1}(\Gamma)\leqslant\widetilde{G}(K)
. In applications, this extra assumption can be obtained by replacing
K
by a finite extension.
We write
\theta\colon U\dashrightarrow W
for rational maps, i.e., defined on an open dense
U_{0}\subseteq U
.

Corollary 1.5
.

Assume that, with notation as above,
p^{-1}(\Gamma)\leqslant\widetilde{G}(K)
, and assume that there exists a Zariski dense subset of
G(\accentset{\rule{5.7361pt}{0.8pt}}{K})
consisting of elements fixing finitely many points on
X(\accentset{\rule{5.7361pt}{0.8pt}}{K})
. Then one of the following holds:

(i)

There exists a rational map
\theta\colon G\dashrightarrow X
such that

\theta(g)g=\theta(g)
for every
g
in the domain of
\theta
. Moreover, if
X
is projective, every element of
G(K)
fixes a point of
X(K)
.

(ii)

There exists
C>0
such that

\mathbf{P}(\omega_{n}\mbox{ has a fixed point in $X(K)$})\leqslant\Xi(C,n).

We have, therefore, a dichotomy: Either generically every element of
G
fixes a point on
X
, or almost surely a long random walk will hit elements with no rational fixed points.

To have an example,
one may take
X
to be a flag variety (Corollary
4.3
). For more examples, we refer the reader to
[
Cor07
]
.

1.5.
Function fields

The goal of this subsection is to extend our results to global fields of positive characteristic. The approach we take over number fields works for general global fields. However, the proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on the “strong approximation theorem”, which is more delicate in positive characteristic (see, e.g.,
[
LS12
, pp. 416–417]
). To overcome this obstacle, we add assumptions on
G
and
\Gamma
, in such a way that the setting remains quite general.

We start by modifying Notation list
1
:
K
denotes any global function field. We assume that

G
is simply connected and semisimple. (See Remark
2.10
for a more general setting to which our results apply.) In this case,
G=\prod_{i=1}^{t}G_{i}
, where
G_{i}
is simply connected and
K
-simple for each
i=1,\ldots,t
.

We consider more specific
\Gamma
. Let
S
be a finite set of places of
K
, let
\mathcal{O}_{S}
be the ring of
S
-integers of
K
and, for
\nu\in S
, let
K_{\nu}
be the completion of
K
with respect to
\nu
. Assume that
\prod_{\nu\in S}G_{i}(K_{\nu})
is non-compact for every
i=1\ldots,t
; in particular,
S
is non-empty. We then take

\Gamma=G(\mathcal{O}_{S})

to be the corresponding
S
-arithmetic subgroup, and we assume that
\Gamma
is finitely generated.
To have a concrete example in mind, we note that for the group
G=\mathrm{SL}_{N}
with
N\geqslant 3
, all these assumptions are satisfied for every non-empty
S
and for every
K
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
Z
be a thin set of
G(K)
. Then, there exists
C>0
such that

\mathbf{P}(\omega_{n}\in Z)\leqslant Cn^{-1/(10\,\mathrm{dim}\,G)}.

We extend Corollary
1.3
to function fields.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
V
be a normal irreducible
K
-variety, and

\pi\colon V\to G
be a finite surjective morphism,
which is generically Galois with group
\mathcal{G}
. Then, there exists
C>0
such that

\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{G}_{\omega_{n}}\neq\mathcal{G})\leqslant Cn^{-1/(10\,\mathrm{dim}\,G)}.

We also obtain a result on characteristic polynomials over function fields.

Corollary 1.8
.

Fix an embedding
\rho\colon G\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{N}
. Let
\Pi(G)
be the extension of the Weyl group of
G
, as explained in §
1.3
, and put
\chi_{n}=\mathrm{det}(X-\omega_{n}^{\rho})
. Then, there exists
C>0
such that

\mathbf{P}(\chi_{n}\mbox{ has an inseparable irreducible factor or }\mathrm{Gal}(\chi_{n}/K)\not\cong\Pi(G))\leqslant Cn^{-1/(10\,\mathrm{dim}\,G)}.

We remark that there exists a finite place
\nu
such that, if
\nu\in S
, then Theorem
1.6
, Corollary
1.7
and Corollary
1.8
hold with an exponential bound
e^{-n/C}
. See Remark
2.8
.

2.
Simply connected groups: Large sieve

In this section we will prove Theorem
1.1
in the case where
G
is simply connected, and we will prove Theorem
1.6
. We introduce the following technical notion.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
K
be a field,
V
be an irreducible
K
-variety, and
Z
be a thin set of
V(K)
. We define the
complexity
of
Z
to be the minimum of

\max\{t,\deg(V_{i}),\deg(\pi_{i}),N\mid i=0,\ldots,t\},

running over all
t\geqslant 0
,
N\geqslant 1
, subvarieties
V_{0},\ldots,V_{t}\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{K}^{N}
, and morphisms
\pi_{i}\colon V_{i}\to V
subject to the following conditions:
\mathrm{dim}(V_{0})<\mathrm{dim}(V)
; for every
i\geqslant 1
,
V_{i}
is irreducible,
\mathrm{dim}(V_{i})=\mathrm{dim}(V)
,
\pi_{i}
is dominant separable with
\deg(\pi_{i})\geqslant 2
;
Z\subseteq\bigcup_{i=0}^{t}\pi_{i}(V_{i}(K))
.

Let
K
be a global field and let
G
be a smooth connected linear algebraic
K
-group (any further requirement will be made explicit). Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated Zariski dense subgroup of
G(K)
, and
A
be a finite symmetric generating multiset of
\Gamma
, with
1\in A
.

If
\mathfrak{p}
is a nonzero prime of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
(the ring of integers of
K
) of sufficiently large norm, the equations defining

can be reduced modulo
\mathfrak{p}
, to give a smooth connected linear algebraic group over
K(\mathfrak{p}):=\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}
, that we still denote by
G
. Since
\Gamma
is finitely generated, if
\mathfrak{p}
is sufficiently large we get a well defined map
\Gamma\to G(K(\mathfrak{p}))
.

If
Q
is a finite quotient of
\Gamma
, we will consider the Cayley graph of
Q
with respect to the multiset induced by
A
. We will say that
Q
is a

\epsilon
-expander with respect to

if the second eigenvalue of the normalized adjacency operator of the corresponding Cayley graph is at most

.

Theorem 2.2
.

With notation as above, assume that there exists a set
\mathscr{P}
of primes of

of density one such that, for every distinct
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}\in\mathscr{P}
, the morphism
\Gamma\rightarrow G(k(\mathfrak{p}))\times G(k(\mathfrak{q}))
is well defined and surjective. Let

be a thin set of

of complexity
M
.

(1)

There exists
C_{1}>0
such that

\mathbf{P}(\omega_{n}\in Z)\leqslant C_{1}n^{-1/(10\,\mathrm{dim}\,G)},

where
C_{1}
depends on
G
,
K
,
|A|
,
M
.

(2)

Assume furthermore that, for every distinct
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}\in\mathscr{P}
, the finite groups
G(k(\mathfrak{p}))\times G(k(\mathfrak{q}))
are one-sided
\epsilon
-expanders with respect to
A
. Then,
there exists
C_{2}>0
such that

\mathbf{P}(\omega_{n}\in Z)\leqslant e^{-n/C_{2}},

where
C_{2}
depends on
G
,
K
,
|A|
,
M
,
\epsilon
.

2.1.
Group large sieve method

The proof of Theorem
2.2
is based on the “group large sieve method”, introduced by Kowalski
[
Kow08
, Chapter 7]
; revised and reformulated in a neat way by Lubotzky–Meiri
[
LM12
]
.

We first recall the main ideas of this method. Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group,
A
be a finite symmetric generating multiset of
\Gamma
, with
1\in A
, and
Z
be a subset of
\Gamma
. The group sieve method provides conditions for which a random walk on
\mathrm{Cay}(\Gamma,A)
hits elements of
Z
with probability tending to zero exponentially fast.

The method is based on looking at finite quotients of
\Gamma
. In order to get exponentially fast decay, the crux is that the Cayley graphs induced on these quotients should be uniform expanders, so that the corresponding random walks gets equidistributed in logarithmic time.

If the quotients are not expanders, we do not have equidistribution in logarithmic time, but still, we have equidistribution in polynomial time. This is sufficient in order to show that a random walk on
\mathrm{Cay}(\Gamma,A)
hits elements of
Z
with probability decaying as
n^{-c}
. We make this precise in Lemma
2.5
, below, which is the “polynomial” analogue of
[
LM12
, Theorem B]
; see also
[
Bre15
, Lemma 7.5]
. We first need a lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and let
A
be a finite symmetric generating multiset of
G
, with
1\in A
. Let
\omega_{n}
denote the
n
-th step of the uniform random walk on
\mathrm{Cay}(G,A)
, starting at the identity. Then, for every

\left|\mathbf{P}(\omega_{n}=g)-\frac{1}{|G|}\right|\leqslant|G|^{1/2}\cdot\left(1-\frac{1}{|A||G|^{2}}\right)^{n}.

Proof.

Let

be the eigenvalues of the normalized adjacency operator associated to
\mathrm{Cay}(G,A)
. Set
\pi_{*}:=\max\{\pi_{1},|\pi_{\ell-1}|\}
. By
[

, Lemma 2]
, we have that, for every

\left|\mathbf{P}(\omega_{n}=g)-\frac{1}{|G|}\right|\leqslant|G|^{1/2}\cdot\pi_{*}^{n}.

Now,
[

, Lemma 1 and Corollary 1]
show that

\pi_{*}\leqslant 1-\frac{1}{|A||G|^{2}}.

This concludes the proof.

We recall another lemma, which is an application of Chebyshev’s inequality.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

,

be events in a probability space. Assume that

(1)

\mathbf{P}(A_{i})\leqslant 1-\beta
for every

(2)

\mathbf{P}(A_{i}\cap A_{j})\leqslant\mathbf{P}(A_{i})\mathbf{P}(A_{j})+\delta
for every

.

Then,

\mathbf{P}\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{t}A_{i}\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\left(\delta+\frac{\beta}{t}\right).

Proof.

See
[

, Lemma 3.1]
. (In fact, that lemma is stated with

in the conclusion, but the proof gives the above, slightly stronger, statement.)
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group, let
A
be a finite symmetric generating multiset of
\Gamma
, with
1\in A
, and let
Z
be a subset of
\Gamma
. Let
N_{1},\ldots,N_{t}
be finite index normal subgroups of
\Gamma
. For every
i
, denote by
\pi_{i}:\Gamma\rightarrow\Gamma/N_{i}
the natural projection. Assume that there exist positive constants
C
,
D
and
0<\alpha<1
such that the following conditions are satisfied.

(1)

(CRT condition)
\Gamma/(N_{i}\cap N_{j})\cong\Gamma/{N_{i}}\times\Gamma/{N_{j}}
for every
i\neq j
.

(2)

(Quotients grow sub-polynomially)
|\pi_{i}(\Gamma)|\leqslant C\cdot t^{D}
for every
i
.

(3)

(Large sieve assumption)
|\pi_{i}(Z)|\leqslant(1-\alpha)|\pi_{i}(\Gamma)|
for every
i
.

Then, for every
n\geqslant 10|A|C^{5}t^{5D}/\alpha
,

\mathbf{P}(\omega_{n}\in Z)\leqslant\frac{3}{\alpha t}.

In particular, if
t\asymp n^{1/(5D)}
, we get
\mathbf{P}(\omega_{n}\in Z)\ll n^{-1/(5D)}
, where the implied constant depends polynomially on
1/\alpha,|A|,C
.

Proof.

For every
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
and for every
y\in\pi_{i}(\Gamma)
, by Lemma
2.3
and by assumption (2) we have

(3)

\displaystyle\left|\mathbf{P}(\pi_{i}(\omega_{n})=y)-\frac{1}{|\pi_{i}(\Gamma)|}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant|\pi_{i}(\Gamma)|^{1/2}\cdot\left(1-\frac{1}{|A||\pi_{i}(\Gamma)|^{2}}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant C^{1/2}t^{D/2}\cdot\left(1-\frac{1}{|A|C^{2}t^{2D}}\right)^{n}.

By the same reasoning and by assumption (1), for every
i\neq j
and
y_{i}\in\pi_{i}(\Gamma)
,
y_{j}\in\pi_{j}(\Gamma)
, we have

(4)

\displaystyle\left|\mathbf{P}(\pi_{i}(\omega_{n})=y_{i},\pi_{j}(\omega_{n})=y_{j})-\frac{1}{|\pi_{i}(\Gamma)||\pi_{j}(\Gamma)|}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant(|\pi_{i}(\Gamma)||\pi_{j}(\Gamma)|)^{1/2}\cdot\left(1-\frac{1}{|A|(|\pi_{i}(\Gamma)||\pi_{j}(\Gamma)|)^{2}}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant Ct^{D}\cdot\left(1-\frac{1}{|A|C^{4}t^{4D}}\right)^{n}.

Now, let
A_{i}
denote the event “
\pi_{i}(\omega_{n})\in\pi_{i}(Z)
”. Applying a union bound and three times the triangle inequality, we deduce from (
3
) and (
4
) that

\mathbf{P}(A_{i}\cap A_{j})\leqslant\mathbf{P}(A_{i})\mathbf{P}(A_{j})+3C^{3}t^{3D}\cdot\left(1-\frac{1}{|A|C^{4}t^{4D}}\right)^{n}.

Set now

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle:=3C^{3}t^{3D}\cdot\left(1-\frac{1}{|A|C^{4}t^{4D}}\right)^{n},

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle:=\alpha/2.

For every
n\geqslant 10|A|C^{5}t^{5D}/\alpha
, we have that
4\delta/\alpha^{2}\leqslant 1/\alpha t
.
Applying Lemma
2.4
to the events
A_{1},\ldots,A_{t}
, we get that

\mathbf{P}(\omega_{n}\in Z)\leqslant\mathbf{P}\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{t}A_{i}\right)\leqslant\frac{4}{\alpha^{2}}\left(\delta+\frac{\alpha}{2t}\right)\leqslant\frac{3}{\alpha t}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

). It is easy, then, to see that a random walk on a Cayley graph of
\Gamma
hits elements of

with positive probability, so that Theorem
1.1
fails.

(ii) The examples given in (i) cover essentially all the cases excluded in Theorem
1.1
. Indeed, in Theorem
1.1
, we can rephrase the assumption that the morphism
\pi_{i}\colon V_{i}\to G
is ramified, by requiring that the morphism
\pi_{i}\colon:(V_{i})_{E}\to G_{E}
is not an isogeny of algebraic groups; that is, there does not exist an isomorphism of
E
-varieties
\phi_{i}\colon(V_{i})_{E}\to G_{i}
, where
G_{i}
is an algebraic
E
-group, and an isogeny
f_{i}\colon G_{i}\to G_{E}
such that
f_{i}\circ\phi_{i}=\pi_{i}
.

Remark 3.3
.

In Theorem
1.1
, the assumption that the varieties are normal is not crucial. Some modification in the statement, however, is needed, because ramification can be resolved by the normalization map. See Lemma
3.1
for a reformulation.

We now deduce Corollaries
1.3
and
1.7
on generically Galois covers, and Corollary
1.2
on irreducibility of fibres.

Proof of Corollaries
1.3
and
1.7
.

We first prove Corollary
1.3
, so that
K
is a number field. Let
V
be a normal irreducible
K
-variety, and
\pi\colon V\to G
be finite, surjective and generically Galois, with group
\mathcal{G}
. We may assume
\deg(\pi)\geqslant 2
, otherwise the statement is trivial. Let
\Omega
be the set of maximal subgroups of
\mathcal{G}
. For
\mathcal{H}\in\Omega
, set
V_{\mathcal{H}}:=V/\mathcal{H}
, a normal variety,
and denote by
\pi_{\mathcal{H}}\colon V_{\mathcal{H}}\to G
the induced finite morphism.
Set

, and let
U
be an open dense subset of

such that the cover
\pi^{-1}(U)\to U
is étale. The set of elements

whose specialized Galois group

is not generic is contained in

(see, e.g.,
[

, Proposition 3.3.1]
). In view of Lemma

, it is then sufficient to bound
\mathbf{P}(\omega_{n}\in Z)
.

Note, now, that if
V_{\mathcal{H}}
is not geometrically irreducible, then
V_{\mathcal{H}}(K)=\varnothing
, and so can be removed from the definition of

. On the other hand, if
V_{\mathcal{H}}
if geometrically irreducible, then
\pi_{\mathcal{H}}
is ramified, by the assumptions of Corollary
1.3
. In particular,
Z
is a thin subset of
G(K)
that satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
; so Theorem
1.1
holds for

and Corollary
1.3
follows.

The proof of Corollary
1.7
is identical, using Theorems
1.6
(which has no ramification assumption) instead of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

We are under Notation list

. Let

K

4.1.
Corollaries
1.4
and
1.8
: Characteristic polynomial

Let
K
be a global field, and let
G
be as in Corollary
1.4
or
1.8
.

We will use that
G
has the HP over
K
, which, of course, follows from Theorems
1.1
and
1.6
. (In fact, this is known for more general
G
and
K
, see
[
BSFP14
,
CTS87
]
.)

Fix an embedding
\rho\colon G\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{N}
. Let
F
be the largest separable extension of
K(G)
contained in the splitting field of
\chi_{G,\rho}
over
K(G)
.
Let
V
be the normalization of
G
in
F
, and let
\pi\colon V\to G
be the corresponding finite surjective morphism, which is generically Galois with group
\mathcal{G}:=\mathrm{Gal}(F/K(G))
.

We now recall some preliminary facts from
[
JKZ13
, § 2]
or
[
PR03
]
. For an element
g\in G(K)
, let
K_{g}
the splitting field of
\mathrm{det}(X-g^{\rho})
over
K
. If
g
is semisimple or
K
is a number field, then
K_{g}
is a Galois extension of
K
, which is independent of the choice of the embedding
\rho
. For every
g\in G(K)
, let
\mathcal{G}_{g}
be the specialized group of
\pi
at
g
. The following holds:

(\star)

There exists an open dense subset
U_{0}
of
G
such that, for every
g\in U_{0}(K)
,
K_{g}/K
is Galois and
\mathcal{G}_{g}\cong\mathrm{Gal}(K_{g}/K)
.

Now, if
K
is a number field,
K_{g}=K_{g^{\phi}}
, where
\phi\colon G\to G/\mathrm{R}_{u}(G)
(see
[
JKZ13
, Lemma 2.3]
). In particular, if
K
is a number field we may replace
G
by
G^{\phi}
,
\Gamma
by
\Gamma^{\phi}
, and assume that
G
is semisimple. Note that this holds by assumption if
K
is a function field.

(\star\star)

From now on, therefore,
G
is a semisimple group.

Let
K^{s}
be a fixed separable closure of
K
. For a maximal
K
-torus
T
of
G
, we let
X(T):=\mathrm{Hom}(T_{K^{s}},(\mathbb{G}_{m})_{K^{s}})
, the character group of
T
, and let
K_{T}
be the splitting field of
T
, a Galois extension of
K
(
[
Mil17
, Theorem 12.18]
). Let
W(G,T)
denote the
K^{s}
-points of the group scheme
\text{N}_{G}(T)/T
. Then
W(G,T)
is a finite group, called the absolute Weyl group of
G
with respect to
T
. Now,
W(G,T)
and
\mathrm{Gal}(K_{T}/K)
can be viewed as subgroups of
\text{Aut}(X(T))
; define, then,
\Pi(G,T):=\langle W(G,T),\mathrm{Gal}(K_{T}/K)\rangle=W(G,T)\mathrm{Gal}(K_{T}/K)\leqslant\text{Aut}(X(T))
. This is a finite group and, if
T
is
K
-split,
\Pi(G,T)=W(G,T)
. It turns out that the isomorphism type of this group is independent of the choice of
T
, so that we can define
\Pi(G):=\Pi(G,T)
for any choice of
T
.

It follows from a result of Prasad and Rapinchuk
[
PR03
]
, and from the fact that
G
has the HP over
K
, that
\Pi(G)
is the generic Galois group of
\pi
:

Lemma 4.1
.

\mathcal{G}\cong\Pi(G)
.

Proof.

We divide the proof of the lemma in two parts.

(i) We show that
\mathcal{G}
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
\Pi(G)
.

There exists an open dense subset
U
of
G
such that, for every
g\in U(K)
,
g
is regular semisimple and, denoting by
T_{g}
the maximal torus of
g
,
K_{g}=K_{T_{g}}
, so that
\mathrm{Gal}(K_{g}/K)
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
\Pi(G)
(see, e.g.,
[
JKZ13
, Lemma 2.4]
, which is stated over number field but does not use this). Let
U_{0}
be as in
(\star)
. By the HP, we know that
\mathcal{G}_{g}\cong\mathcal{G}
for at least one
g\in U\cap U_{0}(K)
, and in particular,
\mathcal{G}
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
\Pi(G)
.

(ii) We show that
\mathcal{G}\cong\Pi(G)
.

In
[
PR03
, Theorem 3 and Remark 6]
(see also
[
PR14
, p. 242]
), it was shown that, if
G
is semisimple, there exists a Zariski dense subset
U^{\prime}
of
G(K)
such that, for every
g\in U^{\prime}
,
g
is regular semisimple and, with notation as in (i),
\mathrm{Gal}(K_{T_{g}}/K)=\Pi(G,T)
. In particular, taking
g\in U^{\prime}\cap U\cap U_{0}
, we get that
\mathcal{G}_{g}\cong\mathrm{Gal}(K_{g}/K)=\mathrm{Gal}(K_{T_{g}}/K)=\Pi(G,T)\cong\Pi(G)
. Hence
\Pi(G)
, as the Galois group of a specialization, is a sub-quotient of
\mathcal{G}
. From (i), we deduce that
\mathcal{G}\cong\Pi(G)
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

In fact, we used only a weak form of
[
PR03
]
, namely, the existence of
one
element
g\in G(K)
such that
\mathrm{Gal}(K_{g}/K)\cong\Pi(G)
. This already implies that
\Pi(G)
is the generic Galois group of
\pi
. In particular, by the HP, it follows automatically that the set of elements
g\in G(K)
such that
\mathrm{Gal}(K_{g}/K)\cong\Pi(G)
is Zariski dense in
G
.

Proof of Corollaries
1.4
and
1.8
.

By
(\star\star)
, we may assume that
G
is semisimple. Let
Z
be the set of elements
g
of
G(K)
such that
\mathrm{det}(X-g^{\rho})
has an inseparable irreducible factor or
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathrm{det}(X-g^{\rho})/K)
is not isomorphic to
\Pi(G)
. We want to show that
\mathbf{P}(\omega_{n}\in Z)\leqslant e^{-n/C}
. By Lemma
2.6
, it is sufficient to show that
\mathbf{P}(\omega_{n}\in Z^{\prime})\leqslant e^{-n/C}
, where
Z^{\prime}:=Z\cap U\cap U_{0}
, and
U_{0}
and
U
were defined in
(\star)
and in the proof of Lemma
4.1
, respectively. In particular, for
g\in Z^{\prime}
,
g
is regular semisimple, with maximal torus
T_{g}
,
K_{g}=K_{T_{g}}
, and
\mathcal{G}_{g}\cong\mathrm{Gal}(K_{g}/K)
.

Assume first that
G
is simply connected. Then the statement follows from Corollary
1.3
(if
K
is a number field) or Corollary
1.7
(if
K
is a function field), applied to the cover
\pi\colon V\to G
. In particular, from now on we may assume that
G
is not simply connected; hence,
K
is a number field. We use now Notation list
2
.

We have that
\mathbf{P}(\omega_{n}\in Z^{\prime})=\mathbf{P}(\widetilde{\omega_{n}}\in p^{-1}(Z^{\prime}\cap\Gamma)
), where
p^{-1}(Z^{\prime}\cap\Gamma)\leqslant\widetilde{G}_{E}(E)
. Now let
g\in Z^{\prime}
and, for ease of notation, set
T:=T_{g}
. Then
\mathrm{Gal}(K_{T}/K)\nsupseteq W(G,T)
. Let
\widetilde{g}\in p^{-1}(Z^{\prime}\cap\Gamma)
; note that
\widetilde{g}
is regular semisimple, and denote by
\widetilde{T}
the maximal
E
-torus of
\widetilde{G}_{E}
containing
\widetilde{g}
. Then
p(\widetilde{T})=T_{E}
and
E_{\widetilde{T}}=EK_{T}
, where
E_{\widetilde{T}}
denotes the splitting field of the torus
\widetilde{T}
. We see that
\mathrm{Gal}(E_{\widetilde{T}}/E)\nsupseteq W(\widetilde{G}_{E},\widetilde{T})\cong W(G,T)
, from which
\mathrm{Gal}(E_{\widetilde{g}}/E)\not\cong\Pi(\widetilde{G}_{E})
. Then, by the simply connected case, we deduce that
\mathbf{P}(\widetilde{\omega_{n}}\in p^{-1}(Z^{\prime}\cap\Gamma))\leqslant e^{-n/C}
, which concludes the proof.
∎

4.2.
Corollary
1.5
: Fixed points

Proof of Corollary
1.5
.

We use Notation list
2
. By assumption,
p^{-1}(\Gamma)\leqslant\widetilde{G}(K)
, hence we may take
E=K
.

Note, first, that the set of elements of
G(K)
fixing a point on
X(K)
is
\pi(V(K))
. Let
W
be an irreducible component of
V
. By assumption, there exists a Zariski dense subset of
G(\accentset{\rule{5.7361pt}{0.8pt}}{K})
consisting of elements fixing finitely many points on
X(\accentset{\rule{5.7361pt}{0.8pt}}{K})
. This implies that, if
\pi|_{W}
is dominant, then
\pi|_{W}
has finite degree. We can, therefore, apply Lemma
2.6
or Lemma
3.1
to each irreducible component
W
and to the set
Z=\pi(W(K))
.

We deduce that,
if conclusion (ii) of Corollary
1.5
does not hold, then there exists a rational map
\theta^{\prime}:\widetilde{G}\dashrightarrow V
such that
\pi\circ\theta^{\prime}=p
.

At this point,
[
Cor07
, Proposition 5.4]
shows that
\theta^{\prime}
is constant on the fibres of
p
. In particular, there exists a rational map
\theta^{\prime\prime}\colon G\dashrightarrow V
such that
\pi\circ\theta^{\prime\prime}=\text{id}
. Setting
\theta:=\pi_{X}\circ\theta^{\prime\prime}
, where
\pi_{X}\colon V\to X
is the natural projection, proves the first part of item (i) in the statement. Assume now that
X
is projective; let
U
be the domain of
\theta
and let
g\in G(K)\setminus U
. Let
C
be a smooth irreducible
K
-curve passing through
g
and having non-empty intersection with
U
. Since
X
is projective, the rational map
\theta|_{C}\colon C\dashrightarrow X
can be extended to a morphism
\accentset{\rule{5.7361pt}{0.8pt}}{\theta}\colon C\to X
. Then
\accentset{\rule{5.7361pt}{0.8pt}}{\theta}(g)g=\accentset{\rule{5.7361pt}{0.8pt}}{\theta}(g)
, which proves the last part of item (i). (This argument can be found in
[
Cor07
, p. 594]
.) This concludes the proof.
∎

acts naturally on the set of

-flags of

0<W_{1}<\cdots<W_{t}<V,

where

is a
K
-subspace of
V
of dimension

. Similarly,
G(\accentset{\rule{5.7361pt}{0.8pt}}{K})
acts on the set of

-flags of
\accentset{\rule{5.7361pt}{0.8pt}}{V}:=V\otimes_{K}\accentset{\rule{5.7361pt}{0.8pt}}{K}
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Assume that, with notation as above,

, and assume that there exists a Zariski dense subset of

.

(ii)

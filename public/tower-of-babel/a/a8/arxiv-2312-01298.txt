Noiselessly amplified thermal states and after multi-photon addition or subtraction
Xue-feng Zhan and Xue-xiang Xu †

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce a noiselessly amplified thermal state (ATS), by operating the noiseless amplification operator ( g n ^ g^{\hat{n}} ) on the thermal state (TS) with corresponding mean photon number (MPN) n ¯ \bar{n} . Actually, the ATS is an new TS with MPN N ¯ = g 2 ​ n ¯ / [ 1 − n ¯ ​ ( g 2 − 1 ) ] \bar{N}=g^{2}\bar{n}/[1-\bar{n}\left(g^{2}-1\right)] . Furthermore, we introduce photon-added-ATS (PAATS) and photon-subtracted-ATS (PSATS) by operating m m -photon addition ( a ^ † m \hat{a}^{{\dagger}m} ) and m m -photon subtraction ( a ^ m \hat{a}^{m} ) on the ATS, respectively. We study photon number distributions (PNDs), purities, and Wigner functions (WFs) for all these states. Keywords: noiseless amplification; photon addition; photon subtraction; Wigner function



I
Introduction

Quantum theory is originated from Planck’s discovery of the radiation law
1
;
2
. Often, those electromagnetic radiations emitted by a hot body
with a certain temperature were called as thermal lights
3
. One of
them, namely thermal state (TS), can be expressed in terms of Fock states

\rho_{th}\left(\bar{n}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\bar{n}^{n}}{(\bar{n}+1)^{n+1}}\left|n\right\rangle\left\langle n\right|,

(1)

where
\bar{n}
denotes its mean photon number (MPN)
4
. Usually, the
TS plays a significant role in statistical physics
5
. However, this TS
is an Gaussian state, which lack some desirable properties (e.g., Wigner
negativity) for quantum supremacy in various applications
6
;
7
.

Photon addition (by operating creation operator
\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}
) and
photon subtraction (by operating annihilation operator
\hat{a}
), as the
basic tools, have been used to generate non-Gaussian quantum states
8

with some desirable properties. Recently, Guerrini et al. called these
states as photon-varied quantum states
9
. Barnett et al. presented a
detailed analysis of the photon statistics for photon-subtracted and
photon-added states
10
. Based on the TS, the reseachers have
introduced many quantum states and done many theoretical and experimental
works
11
;
12
;
13
. Zavatta et al. demonstrated the bosonic communication
relation in experiment
14
by applying
\hat{a}\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}-e^{i\phi}\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}\hat{a}
on the TS. Zavatta et al. reported the generation
and the analysis of single-photon-added or single-photon-subtracted TSs
15
by applying
\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}
or
\hat{a}
on the TS. Many groups have
introduced and studied the multiphoton-subtracted TSs by using
\hat{a}^{m}

(
m
is an integer)
16
;
17
;
18
;
19
;
20
. Chatterjee compared the
nonclassicality of the added-then-subtraced and subtracted-then-added TS by
using
\hat{a}^{q}\hat{a}^{{\dagger}p}
or
\hat{a}^{{\dagger}p}\hat{a}^{q}
(
p,q

are integers)
21
. Deepak and Chatterjee reported a study related with
a coherent superposition TS by using
s\hat{a}\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}+t\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}\hat{a}

22
. Our group has introduced photon
annihilation-then-creation TS and photon creation-then-annihilation TS by
using
\left(\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}\hat{a}\right)^{m}
and
\left(\hat{a}\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}\right)^{m}
23
. Our group has also introduced a kind of
amplified TS by using
\left(g-1\right)\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}\hat{a}+1
24
,
where
g>1
is the amplification gain.

In recent decades, studies on the noiseless quantum amplifications have
attracted significant attention of researchers
25
;
26
;
27
;
28
;
29
;
30
. As
we know, noise is unavoidably added to the amplified signal in any
deterministic linear amplification. Observing the results in the
hypothetical noiseless amplification of coherent state (i.e.,
\left|\alpha\right\rangle\rightarrow\left|g\alpha\right\rangle
), one
can infer that it is not a deterministic physical operation (i.e., a
trace-preserving positive map)
31
. In order to overcome the limit
of deterministic amplification, one can resort to probabilistic amplification
32
, which can be approximate the conditional operation
g^{\hat{n}}
(
g>1
and
\hat{n}=\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}\hat{a}
). Thus, the input coherent state
\left|\alpha\right\rangle
can be mapped into an amplified coherent
state
\left|g\alpha\right\rangle
, i.e.,
g^{\hat{n}}\left|\alpha\right\rangle=e^{(g^{2}-1)\left|\alpha\right|^{2}/2}\left|g\alpha\right\rangle

33
. However, this operation
is still unphysical because no device realizing this transformation can be
implemented perfectly
34
. The idea on the heralded noiseless
amplification was theoretically proposed by Ralph and Lund
35
.
Afterwards, some amplifications were experimentally demonstrated by several
groups
36
;
37
;
38
.

In a recent work, we have studied multi-photon-addition amplified coherent
states by considering the effects of
g^{\hat{n}}
and
\hat{a}^{{\dagger}m}
39
. However, we have never seen any reports on the combined effect
of
g^{\hat{n}}
with
\hat{a}^{{\dagger}m}
or with
\hat{a}^{m}
on the TS. So
in this paper, we shall study this problem and introduce related quantum
states. The paper is structured as follows: we introduce the ATS in Sec.2,
and then introduce the PAATS and the PSATS in Sec.3. In Sec.4-6, we study
their photon number distribution (PND), purity, Wigner function (WF),
respectively. Our conclusions are given in the last section.

II
Introduction of amplified TS

As shown in Fig.1(a), we introduce another amplified TS (ATS) by
considering the effect of
g^{\hat{n}}
on the TS. Making simple analysis (see Appendix A), we prove that the operator
g^{\hat{n}}
can map
\rho_{th}\left(\bar{n}\right)
into a new TS
\rho_{th}\left(\bar{N}\right)
, i.e.

g^{\hat{n}}\text{: }\rho_{th}\left(\bar{n}\right)\Longrightarrow\rho_{th}\left(\bar{N}\right),

(2)

in the condition of
g^{2}\bar{n}/(\bar{n}+1)<1
and with new MPN
\bar{N}
.
Here, we name this new TS as noiselessly amplified TS (ATS) yielding

\rho_{th}\left(\bar{N}\right)=\frac{1}{N_{0}}g^{\hat{n}}\rho_{th}\left(\bar{n}\right)g^{\hat{n}},

(3)

with
N_{0}=[1-\bar{n}\left(g^{2}-1\right)]^{-1}
and
\bar{N}=N_{0}g^{2}\bar{n}
. Obviously, if
g=1
, leading to
\bar{N}=\bar{n}
, then
\rho_{th}\left(\bar{N}\right)
will reduce to
\rho_{th}\left(\bar{n}\right)
. But if
g^{2}\bar{n}/(\bar{n}+1)>1
, it is impossible to form a quantum
state.

It is necessary to emphasize that the parameters (
g
and
\bar{n}
) must
be chosen rationally because the operator
g^{\hat{n}}
is unbounded. In
Fig.1(b), we plot the possible regions with
\bar{N}>0
and
\bar{N}<0
in
the (
\bar{n}
,
g
) parameter space, where the boundary line is satisfied
g=\sqrt{(\bar{n}+1)/\bar{n}}
. We find that: (i) Only in the condition of
1<g<\sqrt{(\bar{n}+1)/\bar{n}}
, a physical state can be generated due to
\bar{N}>0
. (ii) But in the condition of
g>\sqrt{(\bar{n}+1)/\bar{n}}
, it
will be unphysical because of
\bar{N}<0
.

To further demonstrate Fig.1(b), we plot Fig.1(c) and Fig.1(d) by setting
several parameters. In Fig.1(c), we plot
\bar{N}
as a function of
g
with
\bar{n}=1.1
and
\bar{n}=1.5
. There is a critical value
g_{c}=\sqrt{(\bar{n}+1)/\bar{n}}
for each
\bar{n}
, with
\bar{N}>0
for
1<g<g_{c}

and
\bar{N}<0
for
g>g_{c}
. In the critical value
g=g_{c}
,
\bar{N}

will be limited to positive infinity or negative infinity. In Fig.1(d), we
plot
\bar{N}
as a function of
\bar{n}
with
g=1.06
and
\bar{n}=1.08
.
Similarly, there is a critical value
\bar{n}_{c}=1/\left(g^{2}-1\right)

for each
g
, with
\bar{N}>0
for
0<\bar{n}<\bar{n}_{c}
and
\bar{N}<0

for
\bar{n}>\bar{n}_{c}
. In the critical value
\bar{n}=\bar{n}_{c}
,
\bar{N}
will be limited to positive infinity or negative infinity.

III
Introduction of PAATS and PSATS

Based on the ATS, we further introduce photon-added ATS (PAATS) and
photon-subtracted ATS (PSATS) by considering the effects of
\hat{a}^{{\dagger}m}
and
\hat{a}^{m}
.

PAATS:
As shown in Fig.2(a), applying
\hat{a}^{{\dagger}m}
then
g^{\hat{n}}
(or
g^{\hat{n}}
then
\hat{a}^{{\dagger}m}
) on
\rho_{th}\left(\bar{n}\right)
yields the photon-added amplified TS (PAATS)

\displaystyle\rho_{m+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{m+}^{(1)}}g^{\hat{n}}\hat{a}^{{\dagger}m}\rho_{th}\left(\bar{n}\right)\hat{a}^{m}g^{\hat{n}}

(4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{m+}^{(2)}}\hat{a}^{{\dagger}m}g^{\hat{n}}\rho_{th}\left(\bar{n}\right)g^{\hat{n}}\hat{a}^{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{m+}}\hat{a}^{{\dagger}m}\rho_{th}\left(\bar{N}\right)\hat{a}^{m},

with the normalization coefficients
N_{m+}=m!\left(\bar{N}+1\right)^{m}
,

N_{m+}^{(1)}=g^{-2m}N_{0}N_{m+}
, and
N_{m+}^{(2)}=N_{0}N_{m+}
. In the
second step, we have used
g^{\hat{n}}\hat{a}^{{\dagger}m}=g^{m}\hat{a}^{{\dagger}m}g^{\hat{n}}
. In particularly,
\rho_{0+}
is just
\rho_{th}\left(\bar{N}\right)
.

PSATS:
As shown in Fig.2(b), applying
\hat{a}^{m}
then
g^{\hat{n}}
(or
g^{\hat{n}}
then
\hat{a}^{m}
) on
\rho_{th}\left(\bar{n}\right)
yields the photon-subtracted amplified TS (PSATS)

\displaystyle\rho_{m-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{m-}^{(1)}}g^{\hat{n}}\hat{a}^{m}\rho_{th}\left(\bar{n}\right)\hat{a}^{{\dagger}m}g^{\hat{n}}

(5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{m-}^{(2)}}\hat{a}^{m}g^{\hat{n}}\rho_{th}\left(\bar{n}\right)g^{\hat{n}}\hat{a}^{{\dagger}m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{m-}}\hat{a}^{m}\rho_{th}\left(\bar{N}\right)\hat{a}^{{\dagger}m},

with the normalization coefficients
N_{m-}=m!\bar{N}^{m}
,
N_{m-}^{(1)}=g^{2m}N_{0}N_{m-}
, and
N_{m-}^{(2)}=N_{0}N_{m-}
. In the
second step, we have used
g^{\hat{n}}\hat{a}^{m}=g^{-m}\hat{a}^{m}g^{\hat{n}}
. In particularly,
\rho_{0-}
is just
\rho_{th}\left(\bar{N}\right)
.

IV
Photon number distributions of considered states

After analyzing the density matrices for all above states (including TS,
ATS, PAATS and PSATS), we immediately know that their non-diagonal elements
are zero (i.e.,
\rho_{kl}=0
for
k\neq l
). So all these states can be
expanded into the following compound Poisson distribution

\rho=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\rho_{kk}\left|k\right\rangle\left\langle k\right|,

(6)

where the value of
\rho_{kk}
denotes the probability of finding the Fock
state
\left|k\right\rangle
in the state
\rho
. In addition, the
\rho_{kk}
s for
\rho_{th}\left(\bar{n}\right)
,
\rho_{th}\left(\bar{N}\right)
,
\rho_{m+}
, and
\rho_{m-}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\rho_{kk\left(\bar{n}\right)}^{\left(0\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{n}^{k}}{\left(\bar{n}+1\right)^{k+1}},

\displaystyle\rho_{kk\left(\bar{N}\right)}^{\left(0\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{N}^{k}}{\left(\bar{N}+1\right)^{k+1}},

\displaystyle\rho_{kk}^{\left(m+\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{N}^{\left(k-m\right)}k!}{\left(\bar{N}+1\right)^{k+1}m!\left(k-m\right)!},

\displaystyle\rho_{kk}^{\left(m-\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{N}^{k}\left(m+k\right)!}{\left(\bar{N}+1\right)^{k+m+1}m!k!},

(7)

respectively. Therefore, we just need to explore the photon number
distributions (PNDs) for these quantum states.

Taking
\bar{n}=1.5
with different
g=1.05
,
1.1
, and
1.2

g

V
Purities of considered states

The purity is defined as
P=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\rho^{2}\right)
for a
quantum state
\rho
. Thus, we can obtain the purity

P_{m+}=\frac{\bar{N}^{2m}}{\left(2\bar{N}+1\right)^{2m+1}}\left.{}_{2}F_{1}\right.(-m,-m;1,\frac{\left(\bar{N}+1\right)^{2}}{\bar{N}^{2}})

(8)

for
\rho_{m+}
and the purity

P_{m-}=\frac{\left(\bar{N}+1\right)^{2m}}{\left(2\bar{N}+1\right)^{2m+1}}\left.{}_{2}F_{1}\right.(-m,-m;1,\frac{\bar{N}^{2}}{\left(\bar{N}+1\right)^{2}})

(9)

for
\rho_{m-}
. Here
\left.{}_{2}F_{1}\right.(a,b;c,d)
denotes a
hypergeometric function
40
. In particularly, we have
P_{0+}=P_{0-}=1/\left(2\bar{n}+1\right)
for
\rho_{th}\left(\bar{n}\right)
and
P_{0+}=P_{0-}=1/\left(2\bar{N}+1\right)
for
\rho_{th}\left(\bar{N}\right)
.

Figure 5 depicts the purities (
P_{m+}
/
P_{m-}
) as functions of
\bar{N}
for different
m
. The bigger
m
is, the smaller the purities (
P_{m+}
/
P_{m-}
) are. It was surprising to see that the identity
P_{m+}\equiv P_{m-}
is hold for the same
\bar{N}
. Then the question is: why are these
two purities equal? Indeed, we can link
P
with
\rho_{kk}
with the rule

P=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\rho_{kk}^{2}.

(10)

for our considered states. That is to say, the purity for each of these state
is the sum of squares of all its diagonal elements
41
. Thus, we
immediately understand the reason that
P_{m+}
is always equal to
P_{m-}

for the same
m
.

VI
Wigner functions of considered states

Gaussian or non-Gaussian state can be judged from its WF. The Wigner
negativity (WN) is an indicator of nonclassicality of quantum state
42
. Gaussian state has Gaussian WF without WN. Non-Gaussian state has
non-Gaussian WF with or without WN. The WF for a quantum state
\rho
is
defined by

W_{\rho}\left(\beta\right)=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\rho O_{w}\left(\beta\right)\right)

(11)

with
O_{w}\left(\beta\right)=\frac{2}{\pi}:e^{-2\left(a^{{\dagger}}-\beta^{\ast}\right)\left(a-\beta\right)}:
and
\beta=(x+iy)/\sqrt{2}
43
. Corresponding to Fig.3 and Fig.4, we plot their WFs in Fig.6 and
Fig.7, respectively.

WFs for
\rho_{0+}
and
\rho_{0-}
(i.e.,
\rho_{th}\left(\bar{N}\right)
) have the common Gaussian form with

W_{\rho_{th}\left(\bar{N}\right)}\left(\beta\right)=\frac{2}{\pi\left(2\bar{N}+1\right)}e^{-\frac{2}{2\bar{N}+1}\left|\beta\right|^{2}}.

(12)

which is positive everywhere in the phase space and peaks at the center. As
the factor
g
increases, the distribution will become broader, which can
be shown in column 1 in Fig.6 and column 1 in Fig.7. Naturally, the
distribution of the ATS is broader than that of the TS.

WFs for
\rho_{m+}
and
\rho_{m-}
(
m>0
) have the respective
non-Gaussian forms with

\displaystyle W_{\rho_{m+}}\left(\beta\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{ng}^{(m+)}W_{\rho_{th}\left(\bar{N}\right)}\left(\beta\right),

\displaystyle W_{\rho_{m-}}\left(\beta\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{ng}^{(m-)}W_{\rho_{th}\left(\bar{N}\right)}\left(\beta\right),

(13)

with respective non-Gaussian terms
T_{ng}^{(m+)}
and
T_{ng}^{(m-)}
.
Obviously, the PAATS and the PSATS have ring-shaped non-Gaussian
distributions. These results demonstrate that the non-Gaussian operations (
\hat{a}^{{\dagger}m}
and
\hat{a}^{m}
) can render Gaussian states into
non-Gaussian states. From Fig.6, we see that the PAATSs are non-Gaussian
states with WNs. From Fig.7, we see that the PSATSs are non-Gaussian states
without WNs. For example, we have

\displaystyle T_{ng}^{(1+)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\left(\bar{N}+1\right)}{\left(2\bar{N}+1\right)^{2}}\left|\beta\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\bar{N}+1},

\displaystyle T_{ng}^{(1-)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\bar{N}}{\left(2\bar{N}+1\right)^{2}}\left|\beta\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\bar{N}+1}.

(14)

There exists WN for
\rho_{1+}
if
T_{ng}^{(1+)}<0
, i.e. in the condition
of
\left|\beta\right|<\sqrt{\left(2\bar{N}+1\right)/\left(\bar{N}+1\right)}/2
. But it is impossible to have WN for
\rho_{1-}

because of
T_{ng}^{(1-)}>0
for any
\beta
. Similar results are
applicable to cases
m\geq 2
. In order to see WN clearly in Fig.6, we plot
W\left(x,y=0\right)
for
\rho_{m+}
as a function of
x
in Fig.8. From
Fig.8, we see that: 1) the bigger the number
m
is, the smaller the WN is;
2) the bigger the proper
g
value is, the smaller the WN is. The minimum
WF values are on or below the order of
10^{-2}
,
10^{-3}
, and
10^{-4}

for cases
m=1,3,5
, respectively.

VII
Conclusion

In summary, we have introduced the ATS by acting
g^{\hat{n}}
on the TS and
found that the ATS only exist in a certain range of relative parameters
(including
g

Appendix A: Some information of the ATS

Operating
g^{\hat{n}}
on
\rho_{th}\left(\bar{n}\right)
and using
g^{\hat{n}}\left|n\right\rangle=g^{n}\left|n\right\rangle
, we
obtain the following relation

\frac{1}{N_{0}}g^{\hat{n}}\rho_{th}\left(\bar{n}\right)g^{\hat{n}}=\frac{1}{N_{0}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(g^{2}\bar{n}\right)^{n}}{(\bar{n}+1)^{n+1}}\left|n\right\rangle\left\langle n\right|

(A1)

with an undetermined coefficient
N_{0}
. In order to form a physical state,
we must ensure
\mathrm{Tr}[\frac{1}{N_{0}}g^{\hat{n}}\rho_{th}\left(\bar{n}\right)g^{\hat{n}}]=1
and then have

\frac{1}{N_{0}(\bar{n}+1)}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\frac{g^{2}\bar{n}}{\bar{n}+1})^{n}=1.

(A2)

Obviously, if and only if
g^{2}\bar{n}/(\bar{n}+1)<1
, the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\dfrac{g^{2}\bar{n}}{\bar{n}+1})^{n}
is convergent.
Thus, we can obtain
N_{0}=1/[1-\left(g^{2}-1\right)\bar{n}]
and
reconstruct Eq.(A1) as

\frac{1}{N_{0}}g^{\hat{n}}\rho_{th}\left(\bar{n}\right)g^{\hat{n}}=\frac{1}{\bar{N}+1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{\bar{N}^{n}}{(\bar{N}+1)^{n}}\left|n\right\rangle\left\langle n\right|

(A3)

with
\bar{N}=N_{0}g^{2}\bar{n}
. So the proof of Eq.(
3
) is completed.

On the other hand, it is impossible to form a quantum state if
g^{2}\bar{n}/(\bar{n}+1)>1
because the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\frac{g^{2}\bar{n}}{\bar{n}+1})^{n}
is divergent.

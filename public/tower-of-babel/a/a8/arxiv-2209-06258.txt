Cluster nature of quantum groups
Linhui Shen

Abstract
. We present a rigid cluster model to realize the quantum group 𝐔 q ​ ( 𝔤 ) {\bf U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}) for 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} of type ADE. That is, we prove that there is a natural Hopf algebra isomorphism from the quantum group 𝐔 q ​ ( 𝔤 ) {\bf U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}) to a quotient algebra of the Weyl group invariants of the Fock-Goncharov quantum cluster algebra 𝒪 q ​ ( 𝒫 𝖦 , ⊙ ) \mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},\odot}) . By applying the quantum duality of cluster algebras, we show that 𝐔 q ​ ( 𝔤 ) {\bf U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}) admits a natural basis 𝚯 ¯ \overline{\bf\Theta} whose structural coefficients are in ℕ ⁡ [ q 1 2 , q − 1 2 ] \mathbb{N}[q^{\frac{1}{2}},q^{-\frac{1}{2}}] . The basis 𝚯 ¯ \overline{\bf\Theta} satisfies an invariance property under Lusztig’s braid group action, the Dynkin automorphisms, and the star anti-involution.



1.
Introduction

The quantum group
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
, introduced by Drinfeld
[
Dri85
]
and Jimbo
[
Jim85
]
in 1985, continues to be a central topic in representation theory, knot theory, statistical physics, etc. Cluster algebras, introduced by Fomin and Zelevinsky
[
FZ02
]
, are a class of commutative algebras associated with integer skew-symmetrizable matrices and their mutations. Since its inception, the
rapid developments of cluster theory have found numerous exciting applications in representation theory, quantum geometry, contact topology, etc. The quantization of
cluster Poisson varieties
and their principal series representations has been systematically studied by Fock and Goncharov
[
FG09b
]
. One of the primary motivations for studying cluster algebras is understanding several remarkable features of quantum groups, including
total positivity
and
canonical bases
. We realize this goal in the present paper by providing a rigid cluster model for the quantum groups of type
ADE
.

Let
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{Z}[q^{\frac{1}{2}},q^{-\frac{1}{2}}]
be the ring of Laurent polynomials in the variable
q^{\frac{1}{2}}
with integer coefficients and let
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}(q^{\frac{1}{2}})
be its quotient field.
The quantum group
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
is a quantized universal enveloping algebra associated with a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. In this paper, we assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is of type ADE. The quantum group
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
carries an integral form
{\bf U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
, defined as the
\mathbb{L}
-linear span of a rescaled PBW basis (cf. Section
2
). The semiclassical limit of
{\bf U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
recovers the coordinate ring of the Poisson Lie dual group.

Let
\mathsf{G}
be a split algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}
such that
{\rm Lie}\,\mathsf{G}=\mathfrak{g}
. We assume that
\mathsf{G}
has trivial center and let
\tilde{\mathsf{G}}
be its universal cover.
Let
{\mathbb{S}}
be an oriented topological surface with punctures and marked boundary points.
In their seminal work
[
FG06b
]
, Fock and Goncharov introduced a pair
(\mathscr{A}_{\tilde{\mathsf{G}},{\mathbb{S}}},\mathscr{X}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}})
of moduli spaces, as an algebraic-geometric avatar of higher Teichmüller theory. In
loc.cit.
, they further show that the pair
(\mathscr{A}_{{\rm SL}_{n+1},{\mathbb{S}}},\mathscr{X}_{{\rm PGL}_{n+1},{\mathbb{S}}})
carries a natural cluster structure. The same result has been extended by Ian Le
[
Le19
]
to classical groups, based on a case by case construction. In
[
GS19
]
, Goncharov and the author give a uniform construction of the desired cluster structures for all semisimple groups.

The papers
[
GS15
,
GS19
]
introduce a decorated character variety
\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}}
as an enhanced version of Fock and Goncharov’s moduli space
\mathscr{X}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}}
by assigning a
pinning
to every boundary interval. The importance of the pinning is reflected at least in two aspects. First, when
{\mathbb{S}}
has boundary, the pinning data increases the dimension of
\mathscr{X}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}}
by adding new frozen cluster variables, so that the pair
(\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}},\mathscr{A}_{\widetilde{\mathsf{G}},{\mathbb{S}}})
forms a
cluster ensemble
.
Second, the pinnings allow us to amalgamate different surfaces along boundary intervals, which eventually makes the moduli spaces into a geometric avatar of TQFT. The coordinate ring of
\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}}
is a
cluster Poisson algebra
(
[
She21
, Th.1]
) and can be quantized to a Fock-Goncharov
quantum cluster algebra

\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}})
.

In this paper, we focus on the case when
{\mathbb{S}}=\odot
is a once-punctured disk with two marked points. As proved in
[
GS18
,
GS19
]
, the Weyl group acts on
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},\odot})
via cluster automorphisms. Denote by
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},\odot})^{W}
the subalgebra of
W
-invariants of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},\odot})
.
The
outer monodromies

\mathbb{O}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{O}_{r}
are particular
Casimir
elements in
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}})^{W}
that correspond to simple positive roots of
\mathsf{G}
. Let
\mathcal{I}
be the ideal generated by
\mathbb{O}_{i}-1
for
i=1,\ldots,r.

The quotient algebra
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},\odot})^{W}{/\penalty}\mathcal{I}
is naturally a Hopf algebra, obtained via amalgamation of punctured disks.

Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

There is a natural Hopf algebra isomorphism

\kappa:~{\bf U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},\odot})^{W}{\Big/\penalty}\mathcal{I}.

Theorem
1.1
is proven in Section
5.4
.
Below we include a few historical comments.

•

The idea that relates the quantum group
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
to the character variety of the marked once punctured disk is originally due to Fock and Goncharov in 2006.

•

Following a suggestion of Fock, Schrader and Shapiro
[
SS19
]
construct a concrete injective algebra homomorphism from the quantum group
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})
into a quantum torus algebra associated with the Fock-Goncharov quiver
\mathcal{D}_{n}
for
\mathscr{A}_{{\rm SL}_{n+1},\odot}
. With the help from the author, Schrader and Shapiro further show that the image of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})
is contained in the quantum cluster algebra for
\mathcal{D}_{n}
. In this way, they obtain, for the first time, an embedding
\kappa
of the quantum group
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})
into a quantum cluster algebra. However, their embedding is not surjective and the image of
\kappa
is not known. The paper
[
SS19
]
further obtains a description of the coproduct of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})
in terms of the corresponding quantum cluster algebra associated with the marked twice punctured disk, and shows a remarkable correspondence of the action of the
R
-matrix with the half-Dehn twist of the twice punctured disk.

•

For other simple Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
, Ivan Ip
[
Ip18
]
constructed an embedding of the quantum group
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
into a
quantum torus algebra
associated with a certain quiver
\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{g}}
. However, it is not clear whether the image of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
is in the quantum cluster algebra associated with
\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

•

The paper
[
GS15
]
introduces, for each marked point on
{\mathbb{S}}
, the potential functions
\mathcal{W}_{i}
and the
h
-distance functions
\mathcal{K}_{i}
on
\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}}
. When
{\mathbb{S}}
is a disk, the tropicalizations of
\mathcal{W}_{i}
and
\mathcal{K}_{i}
give rise to a natural parametrization of the Mirković-Vilonen basis for the tensor invariants of the representations of the Langlands dual group
\mathsf{G}^{L}
.

For each marked point on
{\mathbb{S}}
, the paper
[
GS19
]
defines an natural embedding of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
into
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}})
, by sending the generators
{\bf E}_{i},{\bf K}_{i}
to the quantum cluster promotion of
\mathcal{W}_{i}
and
\mathcal{K}_{i}
respectively.
When specializing to
\odot
, the paper
loc.cit.
gives a natural Hopf algebra embedding
\kappa
from the quantum group
{\bf U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
into
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},\odot})^{W}{\Big/\penalty}\mathcal{I}
, and further conjectures that the map
\kappa
is an isomorphism. For
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
, the surjectivity of
\kappa
can be verified by a direct calculation, since the underlying cluster structure is simply of type
A_{1}\times A_{1}
. For any other higher rank
\mathfrak{g}
beyond
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
, the surjectivity of
\kappa
was not known, and seems impossible to verify via a direct calculation, since the size of the underlying quiver increases tremendously. For example, the ice quiver for
\mathfrak{sl}_{4}
, as shown on Figure
5
, has 12 mutable vertices and 6 frozen vertices.

•

Our Theorem
1.1
proves the isomorphism of
\kappa
for
\mathfrak{g}
of type ADE, and therefore provides a
rigid
cluster realization of
{\bf U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
for the first time.
It is worth mentioning that the isomorphism
\kappa
is defined over
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{Z}[q^{\frac{1}{2}},q^{-\frac{1}{2}}]
. Therefore it is still valid when
q^{\frac{1}{2}}
is a root of unity. Its connection to the small quantum groups from the perspective of cluster theory is an interesting direction for future research.

As an application of Theorem
1.1
, we obtain a natural
\mathbb{L}
-linear basis of
{\bf U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
which satisfies many remarkable properties.

Theorem 1.2
.

The quantum group
{\bf U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
admits a natural linear basis
\overline{\bf\Theta}
satisfying the following properties.

a)

The structural coefficients of
\overline{\bf\Theta}
are in
\mathbb{N}[q^{\frac{1}{2}},q^{-\frac{1}{2}}]
.

b)

The basis
\overline{\bf\Theta}
, as a set, is preserved by Lusztig’s braid group action and the Dynkin automorphisms.

c)

Every element in
\overline{\bf\Theta}
is self-adjoint, i.e., invariant under the star anti-involution.

d)

The basis is naturally parametrized by the set
{\mathsf{U}}^{L}_{+}(\mathbb{Z}^{t})\times X^{*}(\mathsf{H})\times{\mathsf{U}}^{L}_{+}(\mathbb{Z}^{t})
, where
\mathsf{U}^{L}_{+}(\mathbb{Z}^{t})
is the set of Lusztig data, and
X^{*}(\mathsf{H})
is the weight lattice of
\mathsf{G}
.

We shall name
\overline{\bf\Theta}
the
cluster canonical bases
because they are constructed through the quantum cluster duality and our cluster model for
{\bf U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
as in Theorem
1.1
. Below we include a few more remarks on related results.

•

Lusztig introduced the algebra
\dot{\bf U}
as a modified form of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
, and further constructed a canonical basis
\dot{\bf B}
for
\dot{\bf U}
. Note that
\dot{\bf U}
is an algebra without unit. Therefore
\dot{\bf B}
is not a basis of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
. See
[
Lus94
, Part IV]
for more details.

•

Berenstein and Greenstein
[
BG17
]
constructed a basis
{\bf B}_{\mathfrak{g}}
, under the name of
double canonical basis
, for the quantum group
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
, via using a variant of Lusztig’s Lemma. They further established several nice properties about the
basis. However, it is not known whether
{\bf B}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is equivariant under Lusztig’s braid group action (cf. Conjecture 1.16 of
loc.cit.
). The structural coefficients of
{\bf B}_{\mathfrak{g}}
are shown to be in
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\frac{1}{2}},q^{-\frac{1}{2}}]
, but it is not clear whether they are in
\mathbb{N}[q^{\frac{1}{2}},q^{-\frac{1}{2}}]
.
For
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
, a direct calculation shows that
{\bf B}_{\bf g}
coincides with our basis
\overline{\bf\Theta}
. Its comparison for
\mathfrak{g}
beyond
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
remains mysterious, and is an interesting direction for future research.

•

The study of canonical bases for (quantum) cluster algebras is a central topic in cluster theory.
The Duality Conjecture of Fock and Goncharov
[
FG09a
]
predicts that every quantum cluster algebra admits a natural basis, parametrized by the tropicalization of its Langlands dual cluster
K_{2}
variety.

For the classical case when
q=1
, Gross, Hacking, Keel, and Kontsevich
[
GHKK18
]
constructed a family of formal power series, called
\theta
series, by counting broken lines in the associated scattering diagrams. When the Donaldson-Thomas transformation of the cluster variety is a cluster transformation, or equivalently when its underlying quiver admits a reddening sequence in the sense of
[
Kel13
]
, the
\theta
series provide an actual linear basis of the corresponding cluster Poisson algebra.

More recently, by combining the tools developed in
[
GHKK18
]
and Donaldson-Thomas theory, Davison and Mandel
[
DM21
]
prove the quantum cluster duality for skew-symmetric cases, under the same assumption on the existence of reddening sequences.

•

Theorem
1.1
allows us to apply the basis theory for quantum cluster algebras to the study of quantum groups. The proof of Theorem
1.2
crucially uses several tools developed in
[
GS18
,
GS19
]
.
First, the papers
loc.cit.
prove the clusterness of Donaldson-Thomas transformations for
\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}}
. Therefore one may apply the result of
[
DM21
]
to obtain an
\mathbb{L}
-linear basis for
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}})
.
Second, it should be noted that our basis
\overline{\bf\Theta}
is not exactly the theta basis of
[
DM21
]
.
Instead, in
[
GS18
,
GS19
]

we show that that Weyl group
W
acts on
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},\odot})
via quantum cluster automorphisms. As a consequence, one may take the sums of quantum theta functions of
[
DM21
]
along the
W
-orbits, whose projections to
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},\odot})^{W}{\Big/\penalty}\mathcal{I}
give rise to the linear basis
\overline{\bf\Theta}
for
{\bf U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
.

2.
Preliminaries on Quantum Groups

2.1.
Definitions

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a rank
r
complex Lie algebra of type ADE.
Associated with
\mathfrak{g}
is a
r\times r
Cartan matrix
(a_{ij})
.

We consider the unital associative
\mathbb{K}
-algebra
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
with the generators
{\bf E}_{i},{\bf F}_{i},{\bf K}_{i},\widetilde{\bf K}_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq r
) and the relations

(1)

{\bf K}_{i}{\bf K}_{j}={\bf K}_{j}{\bf K}_{i},\hskip 28.45274pt\widetilde{\bf K}_{i}\widetilde{\bf K}_{j}=\widetilde{\bf K}_{j}\widetilde{\bf K}_{i},\hskip 28.45274pt\widetilde{\bf K}_{i}{\bf K}_{j}={\bf K}_{j}\widetilde{\bf K}_{i},

(2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}{\bf K}_{i}{\bf E}_{j}=q^{a_{ij}}{\bf E}_{j}{\bf K}_{i},\quad\quad\widetilde{\bf K}_{i}{\bf E}_{j}=q^{-a_{ij}}{\bf E}_{j}\widetilde{\bf K}_{i},\\
{\bf K}_{i}{\bf F}_{j}=q^{-a_{ij}}{\bf F}_{j}{\bf K}_{i},\quad\quad\widetilde{\bf K}_{i}{\bf F}_{j}=q^{a_{ij}}{\bf F}_{j}\widetilde{\bf K}_{i},\end{array}\right.

(3)

{\bf E}_{i}{\bf F}_{j}-{\bf F}_{j}{\bf E}_{i}=\delta_{ij}(q-q^{-1})({\bf K}_{i}-\widetilde{\bf K}_{i}),

(4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}{\bf E}_{i}^{2}{\bf E}_{j}-(q+q^{-1}){\bf E}_{i}{\bf E}_{j}{\bf E}_{i}+{\bf E}_{j}{\bf E}_{i}^{2}=0\quad\mbox{if }a_{ij}=-1,\\
{\bf E}_{i}{\bf E}_{j}-{\bf E}_{j}{\bf E}_{i}=0\quad\mbox{if }a_{ij}=0,\end{array}\right.

(5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}{\bf F}_{i}^{2}{\bf F}_{j}-(q+q^{-1}){\bf F}_{i}{\bf F}_{j}{\bf F}_{i}+{\bf F}_{j}{\bf F}_{i}^{2}=0\quad\mbox{if }a_{ij}=-1,\\
{\bf F}_{i}{\bf F}_{j}-{\bf F}_{j}{\bf F}_{i}=0\quad\mbox{if }a_{ij}=0.\end{array}\right.

Note that
{\bf K}_{i}\widetilde{\bf K}_{i}
commutes with every generator. Hence,
{\bf K}_{i}\widetilde{\bf K}_{i}
is in the center of
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
.

The quantum group
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
is the quotient algebra of
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
modulo the
ideal generated by
{\bf K}_{i}\widetilde{\bf K}_{i}-1
for
1\leq i\leq r
. The generators
\{{\bf E}_{i},{\bf F}_{i},{\bf K}_{i}\}
are the rescaled version of the generators
\{E_{i},F_{i},K_{i}\}
used in
[
Lus94
]
:

(6)

{\bf E}_{i}=q^{-\frac{1}{2}}(q-q^{-1})E_{i},\qquad{\bf F}_{i}=q^{\frac{1}{2}}(q^{-1}-q)F_{i},\qquad{{\bf K}}_{i}=K_{i}.

We denote by

\mathcal{D}_{q}(\mathfrak{b}):=\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})[{\bf K}_{i}^{-1},\widetilde{\bf K}_{i}^{-1}]_{1\leq i\leq r}

the localization of
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
at the Cartan part. The algebra
\mathcal{D}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})
coincides with the quantized Drinfeld double of the Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}
.

2.2.
Braid group action and PBW basis

For
1\leq i\leq r
, let
T_{i}
be the
\mathbb{K}
-algebra automorphism of
\mathcal{D}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})
defined by

{\bf E}_{j}\mapsto q^{-1}{\bf K}_{j}^{-1}{\bf F}_{j},\quad{\bf F}_{j}\mapsto q{\bf E}_{j}\widetilde{\bf K}_{j}^{-1},\quad{\bf K}_{j}\mapsto{\bf K}_{j}^{-1},\quad\widetilde{\bf K}_{j}\mapsto\widetilde{\bf K}_{j}^{-1},\qquad\mbox{if }j=i;

{\bf E}_{j}\mapsto{\bf E}_{j},\quad{\bf F}_{j}\mapsto{\bf F}_{j},\quad{\bf K}_{j}\mapsto{\bf K}_{j},\quad\widetilde{\bf K}_{j}\mapsto\widetilde{\bf K}_{j},\qquad\mbox{if }a_{ij}=0;

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle{{\bf E}_{j}\mapsto\frac{q^{1/2}{\bf E}_{j}{\bf E}_{i}-q^{-1/2}{\bf E}_{i}{\bf E}_{j}}{q-q^{-1}},\quad{\bf F}_{j}\mapsto\frac{q^{1/2}{\bf F}_{j}{\bf F}_{i}-q^{-1/2}{\bf F}_{i}{\bf F}_{j}}{q-q^{-1}}},\\
{\bf K}_{j}\mapsto{\bf K}_{i}{\bf K}_{j},\quad\widetilde{\bf K}_{j}\mapsto\widetilde{\bf K}_{i}\widetilde{\bf K}_{j},\end{array}\right.\qquad\mbox{if }a_{ij}=-1.

The automorphisms
T_{i}
satisfy the braid relations:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}T_{i}T_{j}T_{i}=T_{j}T_{i}T_{j}&\mbox{if }a_{ij}=-1,\\
T_{i}T_{j}=T_{j}T_{i}&\mbox{if }a_{ij}=0.\end{array}\right.

Therefore, they give rise to a braid group action on
\mathcal{D}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})
.

Note that
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
is not closed under the braid group action. In particular,

T_{i}({\bf K}_{i})={\bf K}_{i}^{-1}\notin\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}).

Each
T_{i}
preserves the ideal generated by all
{\bf K}_{j}\widetilde{\bf K}_{j}-1
.
Therefore, the braid group action on
\mathcal{D}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})
descends to an action on
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
. After the normalization (
6
), this braid group action coincides with Lusztig’s braid group action on
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Fix a reduced word
{\bf i}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})
for the longest element
w_{0}
in the Weyl group associated with
\mathfrak{g}
. For
1\leq k\leq n
, we set

(7)

\begin{array}[]{c}{\bf E}_{{\bf i},k}:=T_{i_{1}}T_{i_{2}}\ldots T_{i_{k-1}}{\bf E}_{i_{k}},\\
{\bf F}_{{\bf i},k}:=T_{i_{1}}T_{i_{2}}\ldots T_{i_{k-1}}{\bf F}_{i_{k}}.\end{array}

Let
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
. Given two vectors
\vec{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
and
\vec{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
in
\mathbb{N}^{n}
, set

{\bf E}_{\bf i}(\vec{a})={\bf E}_{{\bf i},1}^{a_{1}}{\bf E}_{{\bf i},2}^{a_{2}}\cdots{\bf E}_{{\bf i},n}^{a_{n}},\quad\quad{\bf F}_{\bf i}(\vec{c})={\bf F}_{{\bf i},1}^{c_{1}}{\bf F}_{{\bf i},2}^{c_{2}}\cdots{\bf F}_{{\bf i},n}^{c_{n}}.

For
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{r})
and
\vec{d}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{r})
in
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
, we set

(8)

{\bf K}(\vec{b})={\bf K}_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots{\bf K}_{r}^{b_{r}},\quad\quad\widetilde{\bf K}(\vec{d})=\widetilde{\bf K}_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots\widetilde{\bf K}_{r}^{d_{r}}.

The set

(9)

\left\{{\bf E}_{\bf i}(\vec{a})\cdot{\bf K}(\vec{b})\cdot{\bf F}_{\bf i}(\vec{c})\cdot\widetilde{\bf K}(\vec{d})~\middle|~(\vec{a},\vec{b},\vec{c},\vec{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{2n+2r}\right\}

forms a
\mathbb{K}
-linear basis of
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
, called the Poincare-Birkhoff-Weil (PBW) basis.
We denote by
\widetilde{\bf U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
the
\mathbb{L}
-linear span of the PBW basis. Following Theorem
5.12
, we see that
\widetilde{\bf U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
is a
\mathbb{L}
-subalgebra inside
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
, and does not depend on the reduced word
{\bf i}
chosen.

Similarly, the algebra
\mathcal{D}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})
has a
\mathbb{K}
-linear basis

\left\{{\bf E}_{\bf i}(\vec{a})\cdot{\bf K}(\vec{b})\cdot{\bf F}_{\bf i}(\vec{c})\cdot\widetilde{\bf K}(\vec{d})~\middle|~\vec{a},\vec{c}\in\mathbb{N}^{n};\vec{b},\vec{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}\right\}.

Let
{\bf D}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})
be the
\mathbb{L}
-linear span of the above basis. Then
{\bf D}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})
is a
\mathbb{L}
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{D}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})
.

By imposing the conditions
{\bf K}_{i}\widetilde{\bf K}_{i}=1
, we obtain the quantum group
{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
with a basis

\left\{{\bf E}_{\bf i}(\vec{a})\cdot{\bf K}(\vec{b})\cdot{\bf F}_{\bf i}(\vec{c})~\middle|~\vec{a},\vec{c}\in\mathbb{N}^{n};\vec{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}\right\}.

Their
\mathbb{L}
-linear gives a
\mathbb{L}
-subalgebra
{\bf U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
inside
{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
.

2.3.
Dynkin automorphisms and stat anti-involution

A Dynkin automorphism
\sigma
of
\mathfrak{g}
is a permutation of the vertices of the Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{g}
that preserves the Cartan matrix:

a_{\sigma(i)\sigma(j)}=a_{ij}.

The set of Dynkin automorphisms forms a finite group and coincides with the outer automorphism group
{\rm Out}(\mathsf{G})
of
\mathsf{G}
.

Each
\sigma
includes an algebra automorphism of
{\bf D}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})
such that

{\bf E}_{i}\longmapsto{\bf E}_{\sigma(i)};\qquad{\bf F}_{i}\longmapsto{\bf F}_{\sigma(i)};\qquad{\bf K}_{i}\longmapsto{\bf K}_{\sigma(i)};\qquad\widetilde{\bf K}_{i}\longmapsto\widetilde{\bf K}_{\sigma(i)}.

The action
\sigma
further induces an action on
\widetilde{\bf U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
and on
{\bf U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
.

The
star anti-involution

\ast
is an anti-automorphism of
{\bf D}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})
that preserves the generators
{\bf E}_{i},{\bf F}_{i},{\bf K}_{i},\widetilde{\bf K}_{i}
, maps
q
to
q^{-1}
, and satisfies

({\bf fg})^{\ast}={\bf g}^{\ast}{\bf f}^{\ast},\qquad\forall{\bf f},{\bf g}\in{\bf D}_{q}(\mathfrak{b}).

The map
\ast
makes
{\bf D}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})
a
\ast

3.
Basics on Cluster Algebras

For the convenience of the reader, we briefly recall several basic definitions concerning the quantum cluster algebra of Fock and Goncharov, mainly following the conventions of
[
FG09a
]

It is worth mentioning that the Fock-Goncharov quantum cluster algebras are different from the Berenstein-Zelevinsky version of quantum cluster algebras in

3.2.
Fock-Goncharov quantum cluster algebras

Let
Q
be an ice quiver. The quantum torus algebra
{\bf T}_{Q}
is a
\mathbb{L}
-algebra generated by
X_{i}^{\pm 1}~(i\in V)
, satisfying

X_{i}X_{j}=q^{2\varepsilon_{ij}}X_{j}X_{i}.

The pair
\Sigma=(Q,{\bf T}_{Q})
is called a
quantum seed
.

Let
\Lambda
be a free abelian group with basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in V}
. Let us choose an order
e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}
of the basis of
\Lambda
, where
m
is the cardinality of
V
. Following the notation of
[
FG09a
, §3.1]
, for each vector
a=\sum_{i\in V}a_{i}e_{i}\in\Lambda
, we introduce the normalized monomial

(11)

X_{a}:=q^{-\sum_{i<j}a_{i}a_{j}\varepsilon_{ij}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}^{a_{i}}

for future use. Note that the monomial
X_{a}
does not depend on the order chosen. The set
\{X_{a}\}_{a\in\Lambda}
forms a
\mathbb{L}
-linear basis of
{\bf T}_{Q}
.

Let
\mathcal{F}:={\rm Frac}({\bf T}_{Q})
be the non-commutative fraction field of the quantum torus algebra
{\bf T}_{Q}
. For example, see the Appendix of
[
BZ05
]
for more details on the construction of
\mathcal{F}
. A mutation in the direction
k\in V-V_{0}
creates a new collections of variables
\{X_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i\in V}
, where

(12)

X_{i}^{\prime}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}X_{k}^{-1},&\mbox{if }i=k,\\
X_{i}\displaystyle{\prod_{r=1}^{|\varepsilon_{ik}|}(1+q^{2r-1}X_{k})},&\mbox{if }\varepsilon_{ik}<0,\\
X_{i}\displaystyle{\prod_{r=1}^{\varepsilon_{ik}}(1+q^{1-2r}X_{k}^{-1})},&\mbox{if }\varepsilon_{ik}\geq 0.\end{array}\right.

The new variables
X_{i}^{\prime}
satisfy the relation

X_{i}^{\prime}X_{j}^{\prime}=q^{2\varepsilon_{ij}^{\prime}}X_{j}^{\prime}X_{i}^{\prime},

where
\varepsilon_{ij}^{\prime}
is given by (
10
).

The quantum torus algebra
{\bf T}_{\mu_{k}(Q)}
is the
\mathbb{L}
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{F}
generated by
X_{i}^{\prime\pm 1}
,
i\in V
.
The pair
\mu_{k}(\Sigma)=(\mu_{k}Q,{\bf T}_{\mu_{k}Q})
is a mutated quantum seed in the direction
k
. We say that a quantum seed
\Sigma^{\prime}=(Q^{\prime},\mathbf{T}_{Q^{\prime}})
is mutation equivalent to
\Sigma
if it can be obtained from
\Sigma
by a sequence of quantum seed mutations. Denote by
|\Sigma|
the class of quantum seeds that are mutation equivalent to
\Sigma
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\Sigma=(Q,{\bf T}_{Q})
be a quantum seed. The quantum cluster algebra
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{X}_{|\Sigma|})
is the intersection of the quantum torus algebras
\mathbf{T}_{Q^{\prime}}
for all quantum seeds
(Q^{\prime},\mathbf{T}_{Q^{\prime}})\in|\Sigma|
:

\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{X}_{|\Sigma|}):=\bigcap_{(Q^{\prime},{\bf T}_{Q^{\prime}})\in|\Sigma|}{\bf T}_{Q^{\prime}}\subset\mathcal{F}.

We will frequently write
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{X}_{|\Sigma|})
as
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{X})
when there is no ambiguity.

Remark 3.3
.

The algebra
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{X}_{|\Sigma|})
has a natural
\ast
-algebra structure. Namely, there is an anti-automorphism
\ast
on
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{X}_{|\Sigma|})
that preserves all the generators
X_{i}
, maps
q
to
q^{-1}
. See
[
FG09b
]
on a beautiful construction on the
\ast
-representations of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{X}_{|\Sigma|})
.

Remark 3.4
.

The semiclassical limit of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{X}_{|\Sigma|})
as
q^{\frac{1}{2}}\rightarrow 1
gives rise to a cluster Poisson algebra
\mathcal{O}(\mathscr{X}_{|\Sigma|})
.
Geometrically, each seed in
|\Sigma|
corresponds to an algebraic torus
\left(\mathbb{G}_{m}\right)^{\#V}
. We may glue all the tori together via the classical version of the transition maps (
12
), obtaining a cluster Poisson variety
\mathscr{X}_{|\Sigma|}
.

For a quantum seed
\Sigma^{\prime}=(Q^{\prime},{\bf T}_{Q^{\prime}})\in|\Sigma|
, suppose
a=\sum_{i\in V}a_{i}e_{i}^{\prime}
is a vector such that

(13)

\sum_{i\in V}a_{i}\varepsilon_{ij}^{\prime}\geq 0,\qquad\forall j\in V-V_{0}.

The monomial
X_{a}^{\prime}
, defined as in (
11
), is a global function, i.e.,
X_{a}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{X}_{|\Sigma|})
. In this case, we call
X_{a}^{\prime}
a
global monomial
.

When
\sum_{i\in V}a_{i}\varepsilon_{ij}^{\prime}=0
for every
j\in V
, then the global monomial
X_{a}^{\prime}
belongs to the center of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{X}_{|\Sigma|})
. Furthermore, for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the power
X_{na}^{\prime}=(X_{a}^{\prime})^{n}
remains a monomial in every seed in
|\Sigma|
. Such a element
X_{a}^{\prime}
is called a
Casimir
.

3.3.
Cluster
K_{2}
varieties and tropicalization

Let
Q=(V,V_{0},\varepsilon)
be an ice quiver. Associated with
Q
is a split algebraic torus
\mathcal{A}_{Q}=(\mathbb{G}_{m})^{\#V}
with coordinates
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in V}
. The pair
\Sigma=(Q,\mathcal{A}_{Q})
is called a
cluster
K_{2}
seed
.

The cluster
K_{2}
mutation in the direction
k\in V-V_{0}
creates a new seed
(\mu_{k}Q,\mathcal{A}_{\mu_{kQ}})
, where
\mathcal{A}_{\mu_{k}Q}
is an algebraic torus with coordinates
\{A_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i\in V}
such that

(14)

A_{i}^{\prime}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}A_{i}&\mbox{if $i\neq k$};\\
{\displaystyle{\frac{\prod_{\varepsilon_{ki}\geq 0}A_{i}^{\varepsilon_{ki}}+\prod_{\varepsilon_{ki}\leq 0}A_{i}^{-\varepsilon_{ki}}}{A_{k}}}}&\mbox{if $i=k$}.\end{array}\right.

We recursively repeat the same procedure in the above new seeds in all the possible directions in
V-V_{0}
, obtaining possibly infinitely many cluster
K_{2}
seeds.
The
cluster
K_{2}
variety

\mathscr{A}_{Q}
is the union of all the obtained cluster tori
\mathcal{A}_{Q^{\prime}}
, glued together via sequences of the transition maps (
14
):

\mathscr{A}_{|\Sigma|}=\bigcup\mathcal{A}_{Q^{\prime}}.

By definition, the ring of regular functions on
\mathscr{A}_{|\Sigma|}
coincides with the upper cluster algebra of Bereinstein, Fomin, and Zelevinsky
[
BFZ05
]
. The set
\{A_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i\in V}
of cluster
K_{2}
variables for each
\mathcal{A}_{Q^{\prime}}
is called a
cluster chart
. The well-known Laurent phenomenon of cluster algebras asserts that
all the cluster variables
A_{i}^{\prime}
are regular functions on
\mathscr{A}_{|\Sigma|}
.
The variables
A_{i}
for
i\in V_{0}
are invariant under mutations and therefore are called
frozen cluster variables
.

The transition map (
14
) is subtraction free, and therefore gives rise to a positive structure on
\mathscr{A}_{|\Sigma|}
. Recall that a nonzero rational function
F
on
\mathscr{A}_{|\Sigma|}
is positive if it can be presented as a ratio of polynomials with non-negative integral coefficients in the cluster variables of one (and hence every) cluster chart. Denote by
\mathbb{Q}_{+}(\mathscr{A}_{|\Sigma|})
the set of all positive functions. The set
\mathbb{Q}_{+}(\mathscr{A}_{|\Sigma|})
forms a semifield, i.e., it is closed under addition, multiplication, and addition.

The tropical semifield
\mathbb{Z}^{t}
is a set of integers with the multiplication
\cdot_{t}
and the addition
+_{t}
given by

a\cdot_{t}b=a+b,\qquad a+_{t}b=\min\{a,b\}.

The tropicalization of
\mathscr{A}_{|\Sigma|}
is the set of semifield homomorphisms from
\mathbb{Q}_{+}(\mathscr{A}_{|\Sigma|})
to
\mathbb{Z}^{t}
:

\mathscr{A}_{|\Sigma|}(\mathbb{Z}^{t}):={\rm Hom}_{\mbox{\it semifield}}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{+}(\mathscr{A}_{|\Sigma|}),\mathbb{Z}^{t}\right).

Tautologically, for any positive function
F\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}(\mathscr{A}_{|\Sigma|})
, its tropicalization is a
\mathbb{Z}
-valued function on
\mathscr{A}_{|\Sigma|}(\mathbb{Z}^{t})
, defined as

F^{t}(l):=l(F),\qquad\forall l\in\mathscr{A}_{|\Sigma|}(\mathbb{Z}^{t}).

The variety
\mathscr{A}_{|\Sigma|}
is equipped with a canonical closed 2-form
\Omega
, which be expressed in a cluster chart as

\Omega=\sum\varepsilon_{ij}\frac{A_{i}}{A_{i}}\wedge\frac{A_{j}}{A_{j}}.

Following
[
Fra16
]
, a quasi-cluster automorphism of
\mathscr{A}_{|\Sigma|}
is an automorphism of
\mathscr{A}_{|\Sigma|}
that preserves the 2-form
\Omega
and can be presented by a sequence of cluster mutations as (
14
) followed by a renormalization by frozen cluster variables. Denote by
\mathcal{G}_{|\Sigma|}
the
quasi cluster modular group
that consists of all the quasicluster automorphisms. The group
\mathcal{G}_{|\Sigma|}
also acts on the quantum cluster algebra
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{X}_{|\Sigma|})
. See
[
GS19
, §19.2]
for more details.

\mathscr{A}_{|\Sigma|}(\mathbb{Z}^{t})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Theta\left(\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{X}_{|\Sigma|})\right),\qquad l\longmapsto\theta_{l}.

Let

If

4.
Decorated character variety

Let
\mathsf{G}
be a split semi-simple algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}
with trivial center. Let
\tilde{\mathsf{G}}
be the universal cover of
\mathsf{G}
. A decorated surface
{\mathbb{S}}
is an oriented topological surface with
punctures
inside, and a finite number of
marked points
on its boundary. The moduli space of
\mathsf{G}
-local systems over
{\mathbb{S}}
(a.k.a. the

\mathscr{L}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}}={\bf Hom}\left(\pi_{1}({\mathbb{S}}),\mathsf{G}\right)\Big/\mathsf{G}.

In their seminal work
[

(\mathscr{X}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}},\mathscr{A}_{\tilde{\mathsf{G}},{\mathbb{S}}})
of moduli spaces, which are varients of
\mathscr{L}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}}
by adding decorations of flags on punctures and the boundary marked points of
{\mathbb{S}}
. These spaces have found significant applications in representation theory
[
GS15
,
GS19
]
, higher Teichmüller theory
[
FG06b
]
, Donaldson-Thomas theory
[
GS18
]
, etc. One fundamental problem is constructing intrinsic cluster structures on the aforementioned pair of moduli spaces, which have been achieved by Fock and Goncharov
[
FG06b
]
for
\mathsf{G}={\rm PGL}_{m}
, and by Le
[
Le19
]
for classical groups based on a case-by-case study. The paper
[
GS19
]
presents a universal construction of the cluster structures for all semisimple groups and solves the problem in full generality.

The paper
[
GS19
]
further introduces a moduli space
\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}}
, which extends the space
\mathscr{X}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}}

by adding extra data called pinnings. The pair
(\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}},\mathscr{A}_{\tilde{\mathsf{G}},{\mathbb{S}}})
form a cluster ensemble under the framework of Fock and Goncharov
[
FG09a
]
. In particular, the coordinate ring of
\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}}
is naturally isomorphic to a cluster Poisson algebra (
[
She21
, Theorem 1]
), and hence admits a natural cluster quantization
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}})
.

In this Section, we briefly recall the definitions and several properties of
\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}}
and
\mathscr{A}_{\tilde{\mathsf{G}},{\mathbb{S}}}
, mainly following
[
GS19
]
.

4.1.
The moduli space
\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}}

Let
{\mathsf{B}}
and
{\mathsf{B}}^{\prime}
be a pair of Borel subgroups of
\mathsf{G}
such that
{\mathsf{B}}\cap{\mathsf{B}}^{\prime}
is abelian. In this case, we say
{\mathsf{B}}
and
{\mathsf{B}}^{\prime}
are of
generic position
. Correspondingly, we obtain a decomposition for the Lie algebra

(23)

\mathfrak{g}=Lie\mathsf{G}=\mathfrak{u}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{u}^{-},

such that
Lie{\mathsf{B}}=\mathfrak{u}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
and
Lie{\mathsf{B}}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{u}^{-}
. A
pinning

\pi
over the pair
({\mathsf{B}},{\mathsf{B}}^{\prime})
is equivalent to a choice of Chevalley basis that is compatible with the decomposition (
23
).

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the flag variety that parametrizes the Borel subgroups of
\mathsf{G}
. Let
\mathcal{L}\in{\bf Hom}(\pi_{1}({\mathbb{S}}),\mathsf{G})
be a
\mathsf{G}
-local system over
{\mathbb{S}}
. Consider the associated bundle
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}:=\mathcal{L}\times_{\mathsf{G}}\mathcal{B}
. The marked boundary points separate the boundary
\partial{\mathbb{S}}
into disconnected intervals.

Definition 4.1
.

The moduli space
\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}}
parametrizes the data
(\mathcal{L},\{{\mathsf{B}}_{x}\},\{{\mathsf{B}}_{p}\},\{\pi_{e}\})
, modulo the conjugation of
\mathsf{G}
, where

•

\mathcal{L}\in{\rm Hom}(\pi_{1}({\mathbb{S}}),\mathsf{G})
is a
\mathsf{G}
-local system;

•

for every marked boundary point
x
,
{\mathsf{B}}_{x}
is section of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}
over
x
;

•

for every puncture
p
,
{\mathsf{B}}_{p}
is a flat section of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}
over the circle
c_{p}
surrounding
p
;

•

for every boundary interval
e
with endpoints
a
and
b
, the associated pair
({\mathsf{B}}_{a},{\mathsf{B}}_{b})
is generic, and
\pi_{e}
is a pinning over
({\mathsf{B}}_{a},{\mathsf{B}}_{b})
.

As shown in
[
GS19
, §13]
, every puncture
p
corresponds to a birational Weyl group action on
\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}}
.
More concretely, let
m_{p}
be the monodromy of a
\mathsf{G}
-local system
\mathcal{L}
surrounding the puncture
p
.
Generically, the flags invariant under the monodromy
m_{p}
form a Weyl group torsor. The Weyl group acts on
\mathscr{P}_{\mathsf{G},{\mathbb{S}}}
by alternating the flat section
{\mathsf{B}}_{p}
and keeping the rest invariant.

4.2.
The moduli space
\mathscr{A}_{\tilde{\mathsf{G}},{\mathbb{S}}}

Consider the fiber bundle
T^{\prime}{\mathbb{S}}=T{\mathbb{S}}\backslash\{\mbox{0-section}\}
with fiber
\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\{0\}
, obtained from the tangent bundle
T{\mathbb{S}}
by deleting the 0-section. The projection from
T^{\prime}{\mathbb{S}}
to
{\mathbb{S}}
induces an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\pi_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\{0\})\longrightarrow\pi_{1}(T^{\prime}{\mathbb{S}})\longrightarrow\pi_{1}({\mathbb{S}})\longrightarrow 0.

The image of the generator of
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\{0\})=\mathbb{Z}
in
\pi_{1}(T^{\prime}{\mathbb{S}})
is denoted by
\mathfrak{o}
. It is known that
\mathfrak{o}
belongs to the center of
\pi_{1}(T^{\prime}{\mathbb{S}})
.

There is a natural set-theoretic lift
w\mapsto\overline{w}
from the Weyl group
W
to
\tilde{\mathsf{G}}
(cf.
[
FG06b
, §2]
). Set
s_{\tilde{\mathsf{G}}}:=\overline{w}_{0}^{2}
, where
w_{0}
is the longest element in the Weyl group. The element
s_{\tilde{\mathsf{G}}}
belongs to the center of
\tilde{\mathsf{G}}
, and
s_{\tilde{\mathsf{G}}}^{2}=1
.
A
twisted local system

\mathcal{L}
is an element of
{\bf Hom}(\pi_{1}(T^{\prime}{\mathbb{S}}),\tilde{\mathsf{G}})
such that
\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{o})=s_{\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}}
.
Every oriented loop
c_{p}\subset{\mathbb{S}}
surrounding a puncture
p
can be uniquely lift to an oriented loop
[c_{p}]\subset T^{\prime}{\mathbb{S}}
. We say a twisted local system
\mathcal{L}
is
unipotent
if the monodromy

u_{p}:=\mathcal{L}([c_{p}])

is a unipotent element in
\tilde{\mathsf{G}}
.

Consider the decorated flag variety
\mathcal{A}:=\tilde{\mathsf{G}}/\mathsf{U}
, where
\mathsf{U}
is a fixed maximal unipotent subgroup of
\tilde{\mathsf{G}}
. Let
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{L}\times_{\tilde{\mathsf{G}}}\mathcal{A}
be its corresponding associated bundle.
For every boundary marked point
x
, we fix a point
x^{\prime}
on the fiber
T^{\prime}_{x}{\mathbb{S}}
. A decoration of a twisted unipotent local system
\mathcal{L}
is an assignment of a flat section
{\mathsf{A}}_{x}
of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}
over every
x^{\prime}
, and a flat section
{\mathsf{A}}_{p}
over
[c_{p}]
for every puncture
p
. In particular,
{\mathsf{A}}_{p}
is invariant under the monodromy
u_{p}
.

Definition 4.2
.

The moduli space
\mathscr{A}_{\tilde{\mathsf{G}},{\mathbb{S}}}
parametrizes the data
(\mathcal{L},\{{\mathsf{A}}_{x}\}\cup\{{\mathsf{A}}_{p}\})
, modulo the conjugation action of
\tilde{\mathsf{G}}
, where
\mathcal{L}
is a twisted unipotent local system, and
\{{\mathsf{A}}_{x}\}\cup\{{\mathsf{A}}_{p}\}
is a decoration of
\mathcal{L}
.

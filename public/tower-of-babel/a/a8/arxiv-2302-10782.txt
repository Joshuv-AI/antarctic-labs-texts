DESY 23-028 Probing the weak mixing angle at high energies at the LHC and HL-LHC Journal: Physics Letters B
Simone Amoroso, Mauro Chiesa, Clara Lavinia Del Pio, Katerina Lipka, Fulvio Piccinini, Federico Vazzoler, Alessandro Vicini

Abstract
Measurements of neutral current Drell-Yan production at large invariant dilepton masses can be used to test the energy scale dependence (running) of the electroweak mixing angle. In this work, we make use of a novel implementation of the full next-to-leading order electroweak radiative corrections to the Drell-Yan process using the MS ¯ \overline{\mathrm{MS}} renormalization scheme for the electroweak mixing angle. The potential of future analyses using proton-proton collisions at s = 13.6 ​ TeV \sqrt{s}=13.6~\mathrm{TeV} in the Run 3 and High-Luminosity phases of the LHC is explored. In this way, the Standard Model predictions for the MS ¯ \overline{\mathrm{MS}} running at TeV \mathrm{TeV} scales can be probed.

Introduction

The electroweak mixing angle,
\theta_{W}
, is one of the fundamental parameters of the Standard Model (SM) of particle physics.
In the electroweak (EW) SM Lagrangian it is defined as

\sin^{2}\theta_{W}\equiv\frac{g_{1}^{2}}{g_{1}^{2}+g_{2}^{2}},

(1)

where
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are the
U(1)_{Y}
and
SU(2)_{L}
gauge couplings.
When considering electroweak (EW) radiative corrections, Eq.
1
gets modified, depending on the renormalization scheme and on the input parameter scheme used.
In particular, in the modified minimal-subtraction (
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
) renormalization scheme, the running
\sin^{2}\theta_{W}
, labeled in the following as
\sin^{2}\theta_{W}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(\mu)
, is defined as
Marciano:1979yg
,
Marciano:1980be
,
Sirlin:1989uf
,
Marciano:1991ix
,
Fanchiotti:1992tu
,
Sirlin:2012mh
,
Denner:2019vbn
,
Workman:2022ynf

\sin^{2}\theta_{W}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(\mu)\equiv\frac{4\pi\alpha_{\mathrm{EM}}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(\mu)}{{g_{2}^{2}}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(\mu)},

(2)

where
\alpha_{\mathrm{EM}}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(\mu)
is the running electromagnetic coupling.

The EW mixing angle at the scale of
\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{Z}}
has been measured at both lepton
ALEPH:2005ab
and hadron colliders
CDF:2018cnj
,
ATLAS:2015ihy
,
CMS:2011utm
,
ATLAS:2018gqq
,
CMS:2018ktx
,
LHCb:2015jyu
, with a precision at the sub-percent level.
Actually, the quantity determined with these measurements is the effective leptonic
\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\ell}
, which is defined at the
\mathrm{Z}
-boson mass peak through the ratio of vector and axial-vector couplings to the
\mathrm{Z}
.
It is conceptually different from
\sin^{2}\theta_{W}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(\mu)
, being the former flavour dependent and based on an on-shell definition.
Also other definitions of a running
\sin^{2}\theta_{W}(\mu)
can be given (see for instance
Czarnecki:1995fw
,
Czarnecki:1998xc
,
Czarnecki:2000ic
,
Ferroglia:2003wa
) different from the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
one.
In our study, we stick to the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
parameter.
Measurements of atomic parity violation, neutrino, and polarised electron scattering on fixed targets have been used to extract the EW mixing angle at lower energies
Kumar:2013yoa
,
Workman:2022ynf
.
First results for large space-like scales have been obtained in Ref.
ZEUS:2016vyd
,
H1:2018mkk
using DIS data.
The running at time-like scales above the
\mathrm{Z}
-boson mass, however, has yet to be probed experimentally.
The high energy regime is of particular interest since the renormalization group equation, which governs the evolution of
\sin^{2}\theta_{W}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(\mu)
, predicts a running with a steep positive slope
Erler:2004in
at high scales, as a result of the inclusion of the
\mathrm{W}
-boson contribution for scales larger than the
\mathrm{W}
-boson mass.
Probing the running of the weak mixing-angle at high energies is complementary to the corresponding studies at low-energies, where a negative slope is expected, and offers a far from trivial test of the consistency of the SM.
High energies can also indirectly probe new states carrying electroweak quantum numbers.
Indeed the effects of New Physics (NP) can be seen as a modification of the running of the electroweak gauge couplings, in a way independent of the particular decay channels
Georgi:1974yf
,
Marciano:1979yg
,
Einhorn:1981sx
.

A large sample of neutral current Drell-Yan (NCDY) events at large dilepton invariant masses (
\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}
) is expected to be produced in proton-proton collisions at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) at a center-of-mass energy of
\sqrt{s}=13.6~\mathrm{TeV}
.
In this work, we investigate the sensitivity to the weak mixing angle of the NCDY process at large dilepton invariant masses.
Several studies appeared in the literature on the potential of LHC and future hadronic machines to constrain NP models through the analysis of running couplings with DY processes
Rainwater:2007qa
,
Alves:2014cda
,
Farina:2016rws
,
Gross:2016ioi

DiLuzio:2018jwd

Torre:2020aiz

.
The existing analyses rely on leading order (LO) EW matrix elements, where the couplings are promoted to running couplings through leading logarithmic contributions to the beta functions.
For the first time, the possibility to probe directly the running of

e

\sin^{2}\theta_{W}
are renormalized in the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
scheme and the
\mathrm{Z}
-boson mass is renormalized in the on-shell scheme.
In the large leptonic invariant mass region the presence of the Sudakov logarithms

,
Ciafaloni:1998xg
,

,

the sensitivity determination.
The calculation has been developed and implemented in the framework of an upgraded version
Chiesa:in-prep

process

Theoretical predictions

We investigate the triple differential NCDY cross sections as a function of the invariant mass,
\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}
, rapidity,
y_{\ell\ell}
, of the dilepton system, and of the cosine of the angle between the incoming and outgoing fermions in the Collins-Soper reference frame,
\theta_{CS}

Collins:1977iv
.
At LO the triple differential NCDY cross section can be expressed as

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}\mathrm{d}y_{\ell\ell}\mathrm{d}\cos\theta_{CS}}=&\frac{\pi\alpha^{2}}{3\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}s}\biggr((1+\cos^{2}\theta_{CS})\sum_{q}S_{q}[f_{q}(x_{1},Q^{2})f_{\overline{q}}(x_{2},Q^{2})\\
&+f_{q}(x_{2},Q^{2})f_{\overline{q}}(x_{1},Q^{2})]+\cos\theta_{CS}\sum_{q}A_{q}\mathrm{sign}(y_{\ell\ell})\\
&\cdot[f_{q}(x_{1},Q^{2})f_{\overline{q}}(x_{2},Q^{2})-f_{q}(x_{2},Q^{2})f_{\overline{q}}(x_{1},Q^{2})]\biggr),\end{split}

(3)

where
\alpha
is the electromagnetic coupling,
\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}=\hat{s}=x_{1}x_{2}s
is the partonic center-of-mass energy and
s
is the hadronic one.
The
f_{q(\overline{q})}(x,Q^{2})
describe the momentum fraction
x
of the parton
q(\overline{q})
in the colliding protons, with the momentum transfer
Q^{2}
given by
\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}^{2}
.
The momentum fractions
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are related to
\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}
and
y_{\ell\ell}
as
x_{1,2}=\frac{\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}}{\sqrt{s}}e^{\pm y_{\ell\ell}}
.
The symmetric
S
and anti-symmetric
A
coupling combinations
Ball:2022qtp
are embedded into

\begin{split}&S_{q}=e^{2}_{\ell}e^{2}_{q}+P_{\gamma\mathrm{Z}}\cdot e_{\ell}v_{\ell}e_{q}v_{q}+P_{\mathrm{Z}\mathrm{Z}}\cdot(v^{2}_{\ell}+a^{2}_{\ell})(v^{2}_{q}+a^{2}_{q})\\
&A_{q}=P_{\gamma\mathrm{Z}}\cdot 2e_{\ell}a_{\ell}e_{q}a_{q}+P_{\mathrm{Z}\mathrm{Z}}\cdot 8v_{\ell}a_{\ell}v_{q}a_{q},\end{split}

(4)

expressed in terms of the electric charges
e_{i}
(in units of the positron charge) and the vector (axial-vector) couplings
v_{i}~(a_{i})
.
The propagator factors are given by

\begin{split}&P_{\gamma\mathrm{Z}}(\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell})=\frac{2\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}^{2}(\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}^{2}-\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{Z}}^{2})}{\sin^{2}\theta_{W}\cos^{2}\theta_{W}[(\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}^{2}-\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{Z}}^{2})^{2}+\Gamma^{2}_{\mathrm{Z}}\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{Z}}^{2}]}\\
&P_{\mathrm{Z}\mathrm{Z}}(\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell})=\frac{\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}^{4}}{\sin^{4}\theta_{W}\cos^{4}\theta_{W}[(\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}^{2}-\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{Z}}^{2})^{2}+\Gamma^{2}_{\mathrm{Z}}\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{Z}}^{2}]},\end{split}

(5)

where
\Gamma_{\mathrm{Z}}
represents the
\mathrm{Z}
width.
At the
\mathrm{Z}
peak, the EW mixing angle has been extracted by measuring the forward-backward asymmetry, which is defined as

A_{\mathrm{FB}}=\frac{\sigma(\cos\theta_{CS}>0)-\sigma(\cos\theta_{CS}<0)}{\sigma(\cos\theta_{CS}>0)+\sigma(\cos\theta_{CS}<0)}.

(6)

At high energy, however, the absolute differential cross section is a more suitable observable for the extraction of
\sin^{2}\theta_{W}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(\mu)
.
This can be seen by evaluating the logarithmic derivative w.r.t.
\sin^{2}\theta_{W}
, i.e. the relative variation under the change of
\sin^{2}\theta_{W}
, of the cross section and of
A_{\mathrm{FB}}
in the limit where
\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}
is much greater than
\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{Z}}

Gross:2016ioi
.
At the representative scale of
1~\mathrm{TeV}
, keeping the effect of finite
\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{Z}}
, the logarithmic derivative multiplied by
\sin^{2}\theta_{W}
is found to be
\sim 0.9
for the cross-sections and
\sim 0.3
for
A_{\mathrm{FB}}
.

In our study, NCDY production in proton-proton collisions at the LHC at
\sqrt{s}=13.6~\mathrm{TeV}
is considered.
We assume integrated luminosities of
300

\mathrm{fb}^{-1}
and
3000

\mathrm{fb}^{-1}
, expected at the end of the LHC Run 3 and High-Luminosity LHC (HL-LHC) phases
Dainese:2703572
, respectively.
We evaluate the triple differential NCDY cross section in six bins in
\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}
with boundaries
116
,
150
,
200
,
300
,
500
,
1500
,
5000~\mathrm{GeV}
, six bins in
|y_{\ell\ell}|
with boundaries

2.0

\cos\theta_{CS}

By considering the fully differential information we combine the sensitivity of the absolute cross-sections and the forward-backward asymmetry.
Fiducial selections, usually employed in ATLAS and CMS measurements (see for example
CMS:2018ktx
), are applied to the leptons.
The leading (sub-leading) lepton must have a transverse momentum of
p_{T}^{\ell}>40(30)~\mathrm{GeV}
and an absolute pseudorapidity of
|\eta_{\ell}|<2.5
.
The leptons are defined at Born level, i.e. prior to final-state photon radiation.

We compute our theoretical predictions using a hybrid EW scheme with
(\alpha_{\mathrm{EM}}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(\mu),\sin^{2}\theta_{W}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(\mu),\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{Z}})
as input parameters, where
\alpha_{\mathrm{EM}}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(\mu)
and
\sin^{2}\theta_{W}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(\mu)

\overline{\mathrm{MS}}

\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{Z}}

In the present study, the running parameters are calculated from the corresponding
\beta

\mathcal{O}(\alpha)
with decoupling of the
\mathrm{W}
-boson and top-quark for
\mu<\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{W}}

The

\mu=\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{W}}
is set to zero as it cancels the similar discontinuity appearing at the same perturbative order in the counterterms corresponding to the electric charge and the sine of the weak-mixing angle

3
Uncertainties

Several sources of experimental and theoretical uncertainties are considered in this study.
The statistical uncertainties in the pseudo-data are derived from the predicted number of events at reconstructed level in each bin.
The considered values of the systematic uncertainties in the lepton reconstruction and efficiencies are inspired by the ATLAS measurement of high-mass DY cross sections
ATLAS:2016gic
at
\sqrt{s}=8~\mathrm{TeV}
.
These are extrapolated to the Run 3 and HL-LHC scenarios, assuming a reduction factor of two or four, respectively.
They are propagated through the fit in a conservative way, assuming them to be uncorrelated in
\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}
,
|y_{\ell\ell}|
and
\cos\theta_{CS}
.
The uncertainty in the luminosity determination is taken as
1.5\%
for Run 3 and
1\%
for HL-LHC
Dainese:2703572
.
In addition, the theoretical uncertainties due to the knowledge of PDFs are estimated by propagating the
NNPDF31_nnlo_as_0118_hessian
eigenvectors.
The PDF variations are obtained using grids generated with
Madgraph_aMC@NLO
and
aMCfast

Bertone:2014zva
,
Alwall:2014hca
.

Missing higher-order terms typically represent an important source of systematic uncertainties for the absolute cross sections and an accurate evaluation of these uncertainties would be important in a real measurement.
As this study is only a sensitivity analysis, a simplified estimate is used.
The NCDY production cross section is known to N3LO in the strong coupling
Duhr:2021vwj
and with exact NNLO mixed QCD-EW corrections
Bonciani:2020tvf
,
Bonciani:2021zzf
,
Buccioni:2022kgy
.
We use the
n3loxs
code
Baglio:2022wzu
to compute cross-sections and 7-point variations of
\mu_{R}
and
\mu_{F}
as a function of
\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}
at N3LO in QCD, and we assume the results not to depend on
|y_{\ell\ell}|
and
\cos\theta_{CS}
.
The effect of the fiducial lepton selections is not included in
n3loxs
.
Since the fiducial acceptance is very large at high
\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}
their effect can be neglected.
The N3LO corrections to the cross section are small, of up to
2\%
.
The scale variations are found to be smaller than the expected statistical uncertainties at the HL-LHC in all but the first three
\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}
bins.
Their envelope is conservatively propagated to the fit by assuming them to be uncorrelated in
\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}
,
|y_{\ell\ell}|
and
\cos\theta_{CS}
.
The pure weak radiative corrections to
d\sigma/d\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}
range from few 0.1% around the
\mathrm{Z}
peak to
\mathcal{O}(10\%)
at the few
\mathrm{TeV}
scale, because of the Sudakov logarithms of the form
\alpha\log^{2}(\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}/\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{V}})
,
\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{V}}
being the mass of the gauge vector bosons.
While a refined treatment of electroweak corrections in the high energy regime should include a proper resummation of Sudakov logarithms, as proposed, for instance, in Ref.
Bauer:2017bnh
, our simulations are based on NLO fixed order contributions.
A rough estimate of the uncertainty associated with missing EW higher-order contributions can be obtained by taking the square of the size of the NLO weak correction.
This amounts to an uncertainty at the per cent level in the last
\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}
bin considered, dominated by the value at the lower edge of the bin.
Within the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
scheme, an alternative way to quantify the uncertainty due to missing higher orders can be given by analysing the dependence of the theoretical predictions on the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
renormalization scale, which by default is fixed to
\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}
.
Adopting a scale variation of a factor of two,
\mu=\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}/2
and
\mu=2\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}
, the cross sections change by about
0.1\%
at NLO, to be compared with
\mathcal{O}(\%)
variations at LO accuracy.
The overall uncertainty of pure weak origin can be considered below the level of the other uncertainties discussed in our present analysis.

Another source of uncertainty arises from the subtraction of background processes, such as diboson production or processes involving top quarks with subsequent leptonic decays. A comprehensive assessment of uncertainties pertaining to such backgrounds has not been explicitly addressed in our study. However, we note that these uncertainties are subdominant as compared to the other uncertainties and are typically derived from control regions utilizing empirical data, thereby their relevance is diminished with increasing statistics.

4
Fit strategy and results

The sensitivity to the running is assessed by extracting the expected value of
\sin^{2}\theta_{W}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(\mu)
and evaluating its uncertainty
\delta\sin^{2}\theta_{W}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(\mu)
as a function of
\hat{\mathrm{m}}_{\ell\ell}
, assuming SM running of
\alpha_{\mathrm{EM}}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(\mu)
.
The expected
\delta\sin^{2}\theta_{W}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(\mu)
values are obtained in a fit to the triple differential cross section pseudo-data in which independent parameters for
\delta\sin^{2}\theta_{W}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(\mu)
for each
\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}
bin are determined simultaneously.
The fit is performed by minimising a
\chi^{2}
function by using the
\mathrm{xFitter}
analysis tool
Alekhin:2014irh
.
The dependence of the cross-section on variations of
\sin^{2}\theta_{W}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(\mu)
in each bin is taken into account in the
\chi^{2}
calculation using a linear approximation, that has been verified to be valid within the range of variations considered.
The expected statistical and experimental systematic uncertainties, and the theoretical uncertainties from PDFs and missing higher orders are included as nuisance parameters in the
\chi^{2}
definition, such that they can be constrained in the fit.

The obtained values of
\delta\sin^{2}\theta_{W}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(\mu)
are presented in Fig.
2
and in Tab.
2
.
They range from about
1\%
(
1\%
) to
7\%
(
3\%
) for the LHC Run 3 (HL-LHC) scenario.
Due to the larger dataset and reduced experimental uncertainties, a significant improvement in sensitivity is expected in the HL-LHC scenario at high
\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}
.

5
Conclusions

In this work, the sensitivity of NCDY measurements at current and future LHC runs to the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
running of the electroweak mixing angle is investigated.
A simulation featuring QCD and EW NLO accuracy matched to QCD parton shower is used to generate NCDY events.
In particular, the NLO EW corrections are calculated using a hybrid EW scheme with
(\alpha_{\mathrm{EM}}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(\mu),\sin^{2}\theta_{W}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(\mu),\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{Z}})
as input parameters, where
\alpha_{\mathrm{EM}}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(\mu)
and
\sin^{2}\theta_{W}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(\mu)
are renormalized in the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
scheme and
\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{Z}}
in the on-shell one.
By using the triple differential NCDY cross section in six bins in
\mathrm{m}_{\ell\ell}
, six bins in
|y_{\ell\ell}|
and two bins in
\cos\theta_{CS}
for the forward and backward directions, it is shown that measurements of Drell-Yan production in the HL-LHC phase would result in the extraction of electroweak mixing angle with a precision at the percent level, under the assumption of SM running of the electromagnetic coupling constant.
We leave to a future study a more refined analysis, considering also additional observables.

Pole Inflation in Supergravity


Abstract
We show how we can implement, within Supergravity, chaotic inflation in the presence of a pole of order one or two in the kinetic mixing of the inflaton sector. This pole arises due to the selected logarithmic Kähler potentials K K , which parameterize hyperbolic manifolds with scalar curvature related to the coefficient ( − N ) < 0 (-N)<0 of a logarithmic term. The associated superpotential W W exhibits the same R R charge with the inflaton-accompanying superfield and includes all the allowed terms. The role of the inflaton can be played by a gauge singlet or non-singlet superfield. Models with one logarithmic term in K K for the inflaton, require N = 2 N=2 , some tuning – of the order of 10 − 5 10^{-5} – between the terms of W W and predict a tensor-to-scalar ratio r r at the level of 0.001 0.001 . The tuning can be totally eluded for more structured K K ’s, with N N values increasing with r r and spectral index close or even equal to its present central observational value.

1.1
Non-SUSY Set-up

The lagrangian of the homogenous inflaton field
\phi=\phi(t)
with
a kinetic mixing takes the form

{\cal L}=\sqrt{-\mathfrak{g}}\left(\frac{N_{p}}{2f_{p}^{2}}\dot{}\phi^{2}-V_{\rm I}(\phi)\right)~~\mbox{with}~~f_{p}=1-\phi^{p},~p>0~~\mbox{and}~~N_{p}>0.

(2)

Also,

Concentrating on integer

\frac{d\widehat{\phi}}{d\phi}=J=\frac{\sqrt{N_{p}}}{f_{p}}~~\Rightarrow~~\widehat{\phi}=\frac{\sqrt{N_{p}}}{p}B(\phi^{p};1/p,0),

(3)

\phi=\begin{cases}1-e^{-\widehat{\phi}/\sqrt{N_{1}}}&\mbox{for}~~p=1,\\
\tanh{\left(\frac{\widehat{\phi}}{\sqrt{N_{2}}}\right)}&\mbox{for}~~p=2\,.\end{cases}

(4)

As a consequence, Eq. (
2
) can be brought into the form

{\cal L}=\sqrt{-\mathfrak{g}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\dot{}\widehat{\phi}^{2}-V_{\rm I}(\phi(\widehat{\phi}))\right).

(5)

For
\widehat{\phi}\gg 1

V_{\rm I}(\widehat{\phi})
– expressed in terms of
\widehat{\phi}

[
5

6

(or
\alpha

[
7
]

[
8
,
6
]
for

2

2.1
General Set-up

The part of the SUGRA lagrangian including the (complex) scalar
fields
Z^{\alpha}
can be written as

{\cal L}=\sqrt{-\mathfrak{g}}\left(K_{{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}D_{\mu}Z^{\alpha}D^{\mu}Z^{*{\bar{\beta}}}-V_{\rm SUGRA}\right),

(20a)

where the kinetic mixing is controlled by the Kähler potential

K
and the relevant metric defined as

K_{{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}={K_{,Z^{\alpha}Z^{*{\bar{\beta}}}}}>0\>\>\>\mbox{with}\>\>\>K^{{\bar{\beta}}{\alpha}}K_{{\alpha}\bar{\gamma}}=\delta^{\bar{\beta}}_{\bar{\gamma}}.

(20b)

Also, the covariant derivatives for the scalar fields
Z^{\alpha}
are
given by

D_{\mu}Z^{\alpha}=\partial_{\mu}Z^{\alpha}+igA^{\rm a}_{\mu}T^{\rm a}_{{\alpha}{\beta}}Z^{\beta}

(20c)

with
A^{\rm a}_{\mu}
being the vector gauge fields,
g
the
(unified) gauge coupling constant and
T^{\rm a}
with
{\rm a}=1,...,\mbox{\sf\small dim}G_{\rm GUT}
the generators of a gauge
group
G_{\rm GUT}
. Here and henceforth, the scalar components of the
various superfields are denoted by the same superfield symbol.

The SUGRA scalar potential,

, is given in terms of

K
, and the superpotential,
W
, by

V_{\rm SUGRA}=V_{\rm F}+V_{\rm D}\>\>\>\mbox{with}\>\>\>V_{\rm F}=e^{K}\left(K^{{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}{\rm F}_{\alpha}{\rm F}^{*}_{\bar{\beta}}-3{|W|^{2}}\right)\>\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\>V_{\rm D}=g^{2}\sum_{\rm a}{\rm D}_{\rm a}{\rm D}_{\rm a}/2,

(20d)

where a trivial gauge kinetic function is adopted whereas the F-
and D-terms read

{\rm F}_{\alpha}=W_{,Z^{\alpha}}+K_{,Z^{\alpha}}W\>\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\>{\rm D}_{\rm a}=Z_{\alpha}\left(T_{\rm a}\right)^{\alpha}_{\beta}K^{\beta}\>\>\>\mbox{with}\>\>\>K^{{\alpha}}={K_{,Z^{\alpha}}}\,.

(20e)

Therefore, the models of PI in Sec.
1.1
can be supersymmetrized,
if we select conveniently the functions
K
and
W
so that
Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
) are reproduced.

We concentrate on PI driven by

If the inflaton is (the radial part of) a gauge singlet

Adopting a structured

In Sec.

It generates for

•

5
Conclusions

We reviewed the implementation of PI first in a non-SUSY and then
to a SUSY framework. In the former regime, we confined ourselves
to models displaying a kinetic mixing in the inflaton sector with
a pole of order one or two and verified their agreement with
observations. In the latter regime, we presented two classes of
models (CI and HI) depending on whether the inflaton is included
into a gauge singlet or non-singlet field. CI and HI are relied on
the superpotential in Eqs. (
24
) and (
35
) respectively which respects
an
R
symmetry and include an inflaton accompanying field which
facilitates the establishment of PI. In each class of models we
singled out three subclasses of models (
\delta
CI
,
CI2
and
CI4
) and
(
\delta
HI
,
HI4
and
HI8

\delta
CI
and
\delta
HI
are based on the
Kähler potentials in Eqs. (
26a
) and (
37a
) whereas (
CI2
,
CI4
) and (
HI4
,
HI8
) in
those shown in Eqs. (
27
) and (
38
). All those Kähler potentials parameterize
hyperbolic internal geometries with a kinetic pole of order one
for CI and two for HI. The Higgflaton in the last case implements
the breaking of a gauge
U(1)_{B-L}
symmetry at a scale which may
assume a value compatible with the MSSM unification.

All the models excellently match the observations by restricting
the free parameters to reasonably ample regions of values. In

Differentially fixed ideals in toric varieties
Lance Edward Miller, William D. Taylor, Janet Vassilev

Abstract
. This article concerns monomial ideals fixed by differential operators of affine semi-group rings over ℂ \mathbb{C} . We give a complete characterization of when this happens. Perhaps surprisingly, every monomial ideal is fixed by an infinite set of homogeneous differential operators and is in fact determined by them. This opens up a new tool for studying monomial ideals. We explore applications of this to (mixed) multiplier ideals and other variants as well as give examples of detecting ideal membership in integrally closed powers and symbolic powers of squarefree monomial ideals.



1.
Introduction

In this article, we give a complete answer to the following question. What proper monomial ideals in a complex normal affine semigroup ring are fixed by a fixed homogeneous differential operator? That is to say, when does
\delta(I)=I
for
\delta
a homogeneous differential operator and
I
a monomial ideal. This is inspired by a rough, but important, analogy between
p^{-e}
-linear maps which can be thought of as potential Frobenius splittings for rings of positive characteristic
p>0
and differential operators which of course can be considered in any characteristic, see for example
[
BJNB19
]
. We note the main theorem of
[
HSZ14
]
characterizes all ideals fixed by a fixed
p^{-e}
-linear map in the toric setting. In that case, there are finitely such fixed ideals
[
BB09
]
. In contrast, we show every proper monomial ideal
I
has infinitely many homogeneous differential operators fixing it. Moreover, the ideal
I
is completely determined by the differential operators fixing it. Our results are also distinct from but in similar spirit to
[
Ciu20
]
which where similar questions were addressed concerning ideals invariant under a fixed derivation.

Our approach is based on the Saito-Travis description of differential operators for such semigroup rings,
[
ST01
]
and the structures subsequently used in
[
BCK+21a
,
BCK+21b
]
. This gives an explicit description of homogeneous differential operators
\delta
of multidegree
\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
on a semigroup ring
S
defined by a cone of dimension
d
. Specifically, for each
\mathbf{d}
, a homogeneous differential operator
\delta
of degree
\mathbf{d}
has the form
\delta=x^{\mathbf{d}}f
where
x^{\mathbf{d}}
is a monomial in
d
variables and
f
is a polynomial differential operator of a prescribed form.

Before stating the main theorem, we establish a bit of notation. For such an operator, a key role in the characterization of fixed ideals is played by the monomial vanishing locus. Specifically, for
\delta=x^{\mathbf{d}}f
, as
f
is a polynomial differential operator one can look at the vanishing locus of the polynomial defining it. The set of lattice points on which
f
vanishes is the set
V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)=\{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\colon f(\mathbf{a})=\mathbf{0}\}
. The geometric information in
V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)
that plays an essential role are the points in
V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)
which are furthest along rays in the direction of
-\mathbf{d}
. To obtain these points, set
\nu(\mathbf{a}):=\inf\{t\in\mathbb{R}\colon\mathbf{a}+t\mathbf{d}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)\}
. For
\delta
to fix any ideals, we show it is necessary that
\nu(\mathbf{a})>-\infty
for all
\mathbf{a}\in S
. When this condition is satisfied, we pick out the furthest points along these rays via the quantity
\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{a}):=\max\{t\in[\nu(\mathbf{a}),\nu(\mathbf{a})+1)\colon\mathbf{a}+t\mathbf{d}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)\}
and consider the subset
V_{\textnormal{mon}}^{\prime}(f):=\{\mathbf{a}+\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{a})\mathbf{d}\colon\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\nu(\mathbf{a})>-\infty\}\subset V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f).
Utilizing this, we give a complete description of all ideals in
R
are
\delta
-fixed for some homogeneous
\delta
, and what ideals are fixed for a given
\delta
in terms of the exponent set
\Exp I=\{\mathbf{a}\colon x^{\mathbf{a}}\in I\}
. We state these characterization as follows.

Theorem

(cf. Theorem
3.6
and Corollary
4.7
)
.

Let
R=\mathbb{C}[S]
be an affine semigroup ring defined by a cone
\sigma^{\vee}
,
I\subseteq R
a proper monomial ideal, and
\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that
-\mathbf{d}\in S
. Set
\mathbf{e}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
primitive such that
\mathbf{d}=q\mathbf{e}
for some
q\in\mathbb{N}
.

•

There exists a homogeneous differential operator
\delta
of degree
\mathbf{d}
such that
I
is
\delta
-fixed.

•

If
-\mathbf{d}\in\inter\sigma^{\vee}
, then
\delta
fixes a monomial ideal
I
if and only if

(1)

\nu(\mathbf{a})>-\infty
for all
\mathbf{a}\in S
,

(2)

for all
\mathbf{a}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}^{\prime}(f)
and
i=0,\ldots,q-1
,
\mathbf{a}-i\mathbf{e}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)
, and

(3)

for every
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}^{\prime}(f)
,
\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}\notin S-\mathbf{e}
.

In this case,
\delta
fixes only one ideal, namely the ideal
I
with
\Exp I=\{\mathbf{a}\in S\mid\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{a})\geq 0\}
.

It is perhaps surprising but this characterization shows that every monomial ideal
I
is completely determined by the non-empty set of differential operators
D_{I}:=\{\delta\colon\delta(I)=I\}
. This opens up a new line of study for monomial ideals.

As an application, we consider a flexible but familiar setting where ideals are defined as the intersection of special polyhedral regions
P
. These regions simple from the point of view of combinatorial geometry. Specifically, any polyhedral region which is
\sigma^{\vee}
-closed
in that they are closed under additive translation by
\sigma^{\vee}
defines naturally an ideal and the usual correspondence between monomial ideals and their exponent sets shows all ideals are defined by such regions. We quite broadly work out explicit differential operators fixing such an ideal from the
face data
, i.e., hypersurfaces
\{g_{\tau}\}
defining
P
together with integers
M_{\tau}
which detect membership in
P
. This allows us to freely compute differential operations of various ideal theoretic constructions from their associated combinatioral geometric operations on polyhedra, a technique which is well-worn and pervades work on monomial ideals. In its most general form, the primary application is the following theorem.

Theorem
.

(cf. Theorem
5.3
) Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
be
\sigma^{\vee}
-closed polyhedra with face data
\{(g_{\tau},M_{\tau})\}_{\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
and
\mathbf{0}\neq\mathbf{d}\in-S
.

(1)

Let
I=\langle x^{\mathbf{a}}\mid\mathbf{a}\in\bigcup P_{i}\rangle
and
f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}(g_{\tau}(\theta),\lceil M_{\tau}-g_{\tau}(\mathbf{d})\rceil-1)!
. For any
h\in(H_{\mathbf{d}})\cap(f)
,
\delta=x^{\mathbf{d}}h(\theta)
fixes
I
, and
x^{\mathbf{a}}\in I
if and only if
\delta(x^{\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{d}})\neq 0
.

(2)

Let
J=\langle x^{\mathbf{a}}\mid\mathbf{a}\in\bigcup\inter P_{i}\rangle
and
f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}(g_{\tau}(\theta),\lfloor M_{\tau}-g_{\tau}(\mathbf{d})\rfloor)!
. For any
h\in(H_{\mathbf{d}})\cap(f)
,
\delta=x^{\mathbf{d}}h(\theta)
fixes
J
, and
x^{\mathbf{a}}\in J
if and only if
\delta(x^{\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{d}})\neq 0
.

We draw out explicit consequences of this as many important constructions in algebraic geometry and commutative algebra considered on monomial ideals come down to essentially lattice point membership in such a polyhedral region. Examples of this include the Blickle-Howald description of multiplier ideals. In particular, we characterize all jumping numbers using the differential operators fixing an a multiplier ideal.

Corollary
.

(cf. Corollary
5.18
) Let
c\geq 0
a real number and
I
a monomial ideal of an affine semigroup ring
R
. Set
\mathbf{w}=(1,1,\ldots,1)
,
X=\operatorname{Spec}R
, and
J(X,I^{c})=\langle x^{\mathbf{a}}\mid\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{w}\in c\cdot\inter\operatorname{Newt}I\rangle
the multiplier ideal of the pair
(X,I^{c})
. Set
(g_{\tau},M_{\tau})_{\tau\in\mathcal{F}}
a face data for
\operatorname{Newt}I
.

(1)

The differential operator
\delta_{c}=x^{-\mathbf{w}}H_{-\mathbf{w}}(\theta)\prod_{\tau\in\mathcal{F}}(g_{\tau}(\theta),\lfloor cM_{\tau}\rfloor)!
fixes
J_{c}
.

(2)

The jumping numbers of
I
are exactly
\left\{\min_{\tau\in\mathcal{F}}\left\{\frac{1}{M_{\tau}}g_{\tau}(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{w})\right\}\mid\mathbf{a}\in S\right\}
.

(3)

The log canonical threshold of
I
is
\min_{\tau\in\mathcal{F}}\left\{\frac{1}{M_{\tau}}g_{\tau}(\mathbf{w})\right\}
.

We provide similar descriptions for mixed multiplier ideals, as well as ideal membership problems on integral closures of powers and symbolic powers in Section 5. All of these are specializations of the singular combinatorial geometric principal in last theorem but we work out samples of specific applications as well as the explicit differential operators at play.

2.
Preliminaries and notation

Throughout,
x
will denote a list of variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
for
d\geq 1
. Thus we denote by
\mathbb{C}[x]:=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
for the polynomial ring with variables
x
. For a vector
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{d})
we denote by
x^{\mathbf{v}}
the monomial
x_{1}^{v_{1}}x_{2}^{v_{2}}\cdots x_{d}^{v_{d}}
.

We also fix
\sigma=\operatorname{cone}(\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{n})
a rational polyhedral cone for
\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and set
R=\mathbb{C}[S]
where
S=\sigma^{\vee}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
which identifies
R=\mathbb{C}[x^{\mathbf{v}_{1}},\ldots,x^{\mathbf{v}_{n}}]
as a normal affine semigroup ring. Unless otherwise stated
I\subset R
is a proper monomial ideal. We denote the exponent set by
\Exp I:=\{\mathbf{a}\in S\colon x^{\mathbf{a}}\in I\}
and by
\operatorname{int}S
the interior of
S
.

We denote by
\mathcal{D}
the differential operators of
R
, for which we utilize the following description. Setting
\theta_{x_{i}}=x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}
and
\mathbb{C}\langle\theta\rangle:=\mathbb{C}\langle\theta_{x_{1}},\ldots,\theta_{x_{d}}\rangle
, one may view
\mathcal{D}
as a subring of
\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]\langle\theta\rangle
. We also utilize the descending factorial notation introduced in
[
BCK+21b
,
BCK+21a
]
, namely for
g\in\mathbb{C}[x]
,

(g,n)!=\prod_{i=0}^{n}(g-i)=\begin{cases}g(g-1)(g-2)\cdots(g-n)&\text{if }n\geq 0\\
1&\text{if }n<0.\end{cases}.

Throughout, for
f\in\mathbb{C}[x]
we abuse notation, without comment, by substituting either elements of the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
or the tuple of variables
\theta
. Specifically, for
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d})
, we denote by
f(\mathbf{a}):=f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d})
. Similarly, we can evaluate such a function on
\theta
to obtain a differential operator
f(\theta)=f(\theta_{x_{1}},\ldots,\theta_{x_{d}})
. Note that there is potential confusion in this abuse, if
\delta
for example is a differential operator, then we also denote by
\delta(x^{\mathbf{a}})
for its action on a monomial. However, often we consider differential operators
\delta=x^{\mathbf{d}}f
, so we are forced to consider
f
both as its polynomial
\mathbb{C}[x]
and as the polynomial differential operator
f(\theta)\in\mathbb{C}\langle\theta\rangle
. For example, the notation
(f,n)!
is consistent if we view
f
as a polynomial or as a differential operator.

We also have need to consider the vanishing locus of a differential operator
f
defined as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

For
f\in\mathbb{C}[x]
, its
monomial vanishing locus
is the set

V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f):=\{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\colon f(\mathbf{a})=\mathbf{0}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

The facets
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{m}
of
\sigma^{\vee}
determine unique homogeneous linear forms
h_{i}\in\mathbb{C}[x]
via their primitive support functions. For
\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, the polynomials
H_{\mathbf{d}}
, where

H_{\mathbf{d}}=\prod_{i}(h_{i},h_{i}(-\mathbf{d})-1)!,

play an important role via the following theorem of Saito and Travis,
[
ST01
, Thm. 3.2.2]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

[Saito-Traves]
There is a graded decomposition

\mathcal{D}(R)=\bigoplus_{\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}x^{\mathbf{d}}(H_{\mathbf{d}}(\theta)).

Where the homogeneous elements are of the form
\delta=x^{\mathbf{d}}f(\theta)
, where
f\in\mathbb{C}[x]
,
H_{\mathbf{d}}
divides
f
, and for any monomial
x^{a}\in R
, we have that
\delta(x^{\mathbf{a}})=f(\mathbf{a})x^{\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{d}}
.

.

\displaystyle(2a-b)(2a-b-1)(2a-b-2)bx^{a}y^{b}

3.
Every monomial ideal is
\delta
-fixed

Throughout, the aim of the paper is to study monomial ideals and what differential operators fix them in the following sense.

Definition 3.1
.

For
\delta\in\mathcal{D}
, an ideal
I\subset R
is called
\delta
-fixed
if
\delta(I)=I
and
\delta
-compatible
if
\delta(I)\subset I
.

We characterize
\delta
-compatible ideals for homogeneous
\delta
in terms of the exponent set.

Theorem 3.2
.

The ideal
I
is
\delta
-compatible if and only if
f(\mathbf{a})=0
for all

\mathbf{a}\in\Exp I\setminus(\Exp I-\mathbf{d}).

Proof.

For any
\mathbf{a}\in\Exp I
, we have that
\delta(x^{\mathbf{a}})=f(\mathbf{a})x^{\mathbf{d}+\mathbf{a}}
. Therefore,
\delta(x^{\mathbf{a}})\in I
if and only if
\mathbf{d}+\mathbf{a}\in\Exp I
or
f(\mathbf{a})=0
. This is equivalent to the statement that if
\mathbf{a}\notin\Exp I-\mathbf{d}
, then
f(\mathbf{a})=0
.
∎

Next we turn to characterizing when a given ideal is
\delta
-fixed for which we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

The element
x^{\mathbf{a}}\in R
is in the image of
\delta
if and only if
f(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{d})\neq 0
.

Proof.

Since
\delta
is homogeneous we may restrict our attention to monomials. We have that
x^{\mathbf{a}}=\delta(\alpha x^{\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{d}})
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
if and only if

x^{\mathbf{a}}=\alpha f(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{d})x^{\mathbf{a}}.

This is possible precisely when
f(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{d})\neq 0
.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

The ideal
I
is
\delta
-fixed if and only if
-\mathbf{d}\in S
and

\Exp I\cap V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)=\Exp I\setminus(\Exp I-\mathbf{d}).

Proof.

By Theorem
3.2
,
I
is
\delta
-compatible if and only if

\Exp I\setminus(\Exp I-\mathbf{d})\subseteq V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f).

To show
I\subseteq\delta(I)
, it suffices to show that for every
\mathbf{a}\in\Exp I
,
\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{d}\in\Exp I
and
f(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{d})\neq 0
by Lemma
3.3
. If
-\mathbf{d}\notin S
, then for some
n\geq 1
,
\mathbf{a}-(n-1)\mathbf{d}\in\Exp I
but
\mathbf{a}-n\mathbf{d}\notin\Exp I
. Whence
x^{\mathbf{a}-(n-1)\mathbf{d}}\notin\delta(I)
. Thus,
-\mathbf{d}\in S
is necessary. It is also necessary that
f(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{d})\neq 0
for all
\mathbf{a}\in\Exp I
, or in other words,
f(\mathbf{a})\neq 0
for every
\mathbf{a}\in\Exp I-\mathbf{d}
. It follows these are also sufficient again by Lemma
3.3
. Therefore
I=\delta(I)
if and only if
-\mathbf{d}\in S
and
V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)\cap(\Exp I-\mathbf{d})=\varnothing
, that is,
\Exp I\cap V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)\subseteq\Exp I\setminus(\Exp I-\mathbf{d})
.
∎

Now we can show for fixed ideal
I
, there are many
\delta
which fix
I
.

Lemma 3.5
.

For
\mathbf{a}\in S
,
H_{\mathbf{d}}(\mathbf{a})>0
if and only if
\mathbf{a}\in-\mathbf{d}+S
and
H_{\mathbf{d}}(\mathbf{a})=0
otherwise.

Proof.

Recall
H_{\mathbf{d}}=\prod_{i}(h_{i},h_{i}(-\mathbf{d})-1)!
.
As
\mathbf{a}\in S
,
H_{\mathbf{d}}(\mathbf{a})>0
if and only if
h_{i}(\mathbf{a})-k>0
for all
0\leq k\leq h_{i}(-\mathbf{d})-1
. This happens if and only if
h_{i}(\mathbf{a})-h_{i}(-\mathbf{d})+1>0
which by linearity occurs if and only if
h_{i}(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{d})\geq 0
. Equivalently, this occurs when
\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{d}\in S
, or
\mathbf{a}\in-\mathbf{d}+S
.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Assume
-\mathbf{d}\in S
. There exists a homogeneous element
\delta\in\mathcal{D}(R)
of degree
\mathbf{d}
such that
I
is
\delta
-fixed.

Proof.

Let
I=(x^{\mathbf{a}_{1}},\ldots,x^{\mathbf{a}_{m}})
, so that
\Exp I=\bigcup_{i}(\mathbf{a}_{i}+S)
. Let
f=\sum_{i}H_{\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{a}_{i}}
, and note that
H_{\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{a}_{i}}(\mathbf{b})\geq 0
for all
i
and all
\mathbf{b}\in S
. Therefore, for any
\mathbf{b}\in\Exp I
, we have that
f(\mathbf{b})\neq 0
if and only if there exists

such that
H_{\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{a}_{i}}(\mathbf{b})>0
, which occurs exactly when
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbf{a}_{i}-\mathbf{d}+S
by Lemma

. Thus

if and only if

\mathbf{b}\in\bigcup_{i}(\mathbf{a}_{i}-\mathbf{d}+S)=-\mathbf{d}+\bigcup_{i}(\mathbf{a}_{i}+S)=-\mathbf{d}+\Exp I.

Thus, we have that
V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)\cap\Exp I=\Exp I\setminus(\Exp I-\mathbf{d})
. Furthermore, since
H_{\mathbf{d}}
divides
H_{\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{a}_{i}}
for each

, we have that
f\in(H_{\mathbf{d}})
, and so
\delta=x^{\mathbf{d}}f(\theta)\in\mathcal{D}(R)
, and

is
\delta
-fixed.
∎

\displaystyle=(h_{1}(\theta),-1)!\cdot(h_{2}(\theta),3)!\cdot(h_{3}(\theta),4)!\cdot(h_{4}(\theta),0)!

\displaystyle=(\theta_{x},3)!(\theta_{x}+\theta_{z},4)!(\theta_{y}+\theta_{z},0)!

\displaystyle H_{\mathbf{d}-(0,0,1)}(\theta)

\displaystyle=H_{(-2,0,-2)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{4}(h_{i}(\theta),h_{i}(2,0,2)-1)!

\displaystyle=(h_{1}(\theta),-1)!\cdot(h_{2}(\theta),1)!\cdot(h_{3}(\theta),3)!\cdot(h_{4}(\theta),1)!

\displaystyle=(\theta_{x},1)!(\theta_{x}+\theta_{z},3)!(\theta_{y}+\theta_{z},1)!

\displaystyle H_{\mathbf{d}-(1,2,-1)}(\theta)

\displaystyle=H_{(-3,-2,0)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{4}(h_{i}(\theta),h_{i}(3,2,0)-1)!

\displaystyle=(h_{1}(\theta),1)!\cdot(h_{2}(\theta),2)!\cdot(h_{3}(\theta),2)!\cdot(h_{4}(\theta),1)!

\displaystyle=(\theta_{y},1)!(\theta_{x},2)!(\theta_{x}+\theta_{z},2)!(\theta_{y}+\theta_{z},1)!

Therefore, a differential operator fixing
I
is given by

\delta=x^{-2}z^{-1}\left(H_{(-4,0,-1)}+H_{(-2,0,-2)}+H_{(-3,-2,0)}\right)

For example, we check that the generator
x^{2}
of
I
is in the image of
\delta
. The preimage of the generator
x^{2}
should be a multiple of
x^{4}z
. We have that

\delta(x^{4}z)=x^{-2}z^{-1}\left(H_{(-4,0,-1)}+H_{(-2,0,-2)}+H_{(-3,-2,0)}\right)(x^{4}z)

=x^{2}\left(H_{(-4,0,-1)}(4,0,1)+H_{(-2,0,-2)}(4,0,1)+H_{(-3,-2,0)}(4,0,1)\right)

Now, we have that

\displaystyle H_{(-4,0,-1)}(4,0,1)

\displaystyle=(4,3)!(5,4)!(1,0)!=4\cdot 3\cdot 2\cdot 1\cdot 5\cdot 4\cdot 3\cdot 2\cdot 1\cdot 1=2880

\displaystyle H_{(-2,0,-2)}(4,0,1)

\displaystyle=(4,1)!(5,3)!(1,1)!=4\cdot 3\cdot 5\cdot 4\cdot 3\cdot 2\cdot 1\cdot 0=0

\displaystyle H_{(-3,-2,0)}(4,0,1)

\displaystyle=(0,1)!(4,2)!(5,2)!(1,1)!=0\cdot-1\cdot 4\cdot 3\cdot 2\cdot 5\cdot 4\cdot 3\cdot 1\cdot 0=0

Therefore,

\delta(x^{4}z)=x^{2}\cdot(2880+0+0)=2880x^{2}

Hence
x^{2}=\delta\left(\frac{1}{2880}x^{4}z\right)

4.
The Fixed Ideals of a Differential Operator

We turn now to characterizing all ideals fixed by a given differential operator
\delta=x^{\mathbf{d}}f(\theta)
. To this end, we are interested in the points in
V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)
which are furthest along rays in the direction of
-\mathbf{d}
.

Definition 4.1
.

For every lattice point
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, set

\nu(\mathbf{a}):=\inf\{t\in\mathbb{R}\mid\mathbf{a}+t\mathbf{d}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)\}.

When
\nu(\mathbf{a})
is finite, set

\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{a}):=\sup\{t\in[\nu(\mathbf{a}),\nu(\mathbf{a})+1)\mid\mathbf{a}+t\mathbf{d}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)\}

and
\omega(\mathbf{a}):=\mathbf{a}+\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{a})\mathbf{d}
.

With the above notation, if
\nu(\mathbf{a})
is finite, then
\mathbf{a}+\nu(\mathbf{a})\mathbf{d}
is the point in
V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)
furthest along the ray through
\mathbf{a}
in the direction of
-\mathbf{d}
. Also in this case,
\omega(\mathbf{a})
is a point in
V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)
such that
\omega(\mathbf{a})-n\mathbf{d}\notin V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)
for any
n\geq 1
. Note that if
\mathbf{d}
is primitive, then
\nu(\mathbf{a})=\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{a})
, since
\mathbf{a}+t\mathbf{d}
will not be a lattice point if
t\notin\mathbb{Z}
.

Suppose
\delta=x^{\mathbf{d}}f(\theta)
is a differential operator on
R
with
\mathbf{0}\neq\mathbf{d}\in-S
. We have that
\nu(\mathbf{a})<\infty
for any
\mathbf{a}\in S
. To see this, note if
\nu(\mathbf{a})=\infty
, then
f(\mathbf{a}+t\mathbf{d})\neq 0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. If we take
i
such that
h_{i}(\mathbf{d})<0
, then for large enough
m\in\mathbb{N}
we have that
h_{i}(\mathbf{a}+m\mathbf{d})=h_{i}(\mathbf{a})+mh_{i}(\mathbf{d})<0
. Therefore
\mathbf{a}+m\mathbf{d}\notin S
. However, we also have that
x^{\mathbf{a}+m\mathbf{d}}
is a nonzero multiple of
\delta^{m}(x^{\mathbf{a}})
since
f(\mathbf{a}+t\mathbf{d})\neq 0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. This is a contradiction since
\delta
is an operator on
R
and
x^{\mathbf{a}+m\mathbf{d}}\notin R
.

We note a few more useful points about these functions. If
\nu(\mathbf{a})>0
, then
f(\mathbf{a})\neq 0
. For any
u\in\mathbb{R}
we have that

\displaystyle\nu(\mathbf{a}+u\mathbf{d})

\displaystyle=\inf\{t\in\mathbb{R}\mid\mathbf{a}+u\mathbf{d}+t\mathbf{d}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)\}

\displaystyle=\inf\{t+u\in\mathbb{R}\mid\mathbf{a}+(t+u)\mathbf{d}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)\}-u

\displaystyle=\nu(\mathbf{a})-u.

Furthermore, notice that if
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{b}+u\mathbf{d}
for some
u\in\mathbb{R}
, then
\omega(\mathbf{a})=\omega(\mathbf{b})
. By definition
\nu(\mathbf{a})\leq\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{a})<\nu(\mathbf{a})+1
, which means that for any
\mathbf{a}
, we have that

\nu(\omega(\mathbf{a}))=\nu(\mathbf{a}+\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{a})\mathbf{d})=\nu(\mathbf{a})-\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{a})

and so
\nu(\omega(\mathbf{a}))\in(-1,0]
. If
\mathbf{e}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is primitive such that
\mathbf{d}=q\mathbf{e}
, then
\nu(\mathbf{a}),\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{a})\in\frac{1}{q}\mathbb{Z}
for all
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

In the next theorem, our aim is to “cut out” parts of the semigroup
S
based on the values of the primitive support functions of the faces of
\sigma^{\vee}
. We have that a lattice point
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is in
S
if and only if
h_{i}(\mathbf{a})\geq 0
for all
i
. For any
\beta=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, we consider the subset of the lattice points in
S
whose values at the functions
h_{i}
are at least the values
\beta_{i}
. Let
W_{\beta}=\{\mathbf{a}\in S\mid\textrm{for all }i,h_{i}(\mathbf{a})\geq\beta_{i}\}
. Notice that the set
W_{\beta}
is closed under the semigroup action, in other words
W_{\beta}
is the exponent set of the ideal
I_{\beta}=(x^{\mathbf{a}}\mid\mathbf{a}\in W_{\beta})
.

We also consider for a collection of vectors
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{n}
,
W_{\mathcal{B}}:=\bigcup_{\beta\in\mathcal{B}}W_{\beta}
. Clearly
W_{\mathcal{B}}
is also closed under the semigroup action.

Definition 4.2
.

For a vector
\mathbf{d}\in-S
, we call a tuple
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{n}

compatible with
\mathbf{d}
if for all
i
, either
\beta_{i}=0
or
h_{i}(\mathbf{d})=0
.

A collection
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{n}
is called
compatible with
\mathbf{d}
if each
\beta\in\mathcal{B}
is compatible with
\mathbf{d}
.

The tuples compatible with
\mathbf{d}
will be part of the data defining the fixed ideals of a differential operator
\delta
of degree
\mathbf{d}
. Note that if
-\mathbf{d}\in\inter\sigma^{\vee}
, then the only tuple compatible with
\mathbf{d}
is the zero tuple.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
\delta
is a homogeneous operator of degree
\mathbf{d}
,
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{n}
is compatible with
\mathbf{d}
, and
I=(x^{\mathbf{a}}\mid\mathbf{a}\in W_{\mathcal{B}})
, then
I
is
\delta
-compatible. In particular, if
\mathbf{b}\in W_{\mathcal{B}}
and
\nu(\mathbf{b})
is finite, then
\omega(\mathbf{b})\in W_{\mathcal{B}}
.

Proof.

If
x^{\mathbf{a}}\in I
, then
\mathbf{a}\in\Exp I=W_{\mathcal{B}}
. Therefore there exists
\beta\in\mathcal{B}
such that for all
i
,
h_{i}(\mathbf{a})\geq\beta_{i}
. If
\delta(x^{\mathbf{a}})\neq 0
, then
\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{d}\in S
. For every
i
, if
h_{i}(\mathbf{d})\neq 0
, then
\beta_{i}=0
, and so
h_{i}(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{d})\geq\beta_{i}
. If
h_{i}(\mathbf{d})=0
, then
h_{i}(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{d})=h_{i}(\mathbf{a})\geq\beta_{i}
. Thus in either case
\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{d}\in W_{\beta}\subseteq W_{\mathcal{B}}=\Exp I
. Therefore
\delta(x^{\mathbf{a}})\in I
, and so
I
is
\delta
-compatible.

For the last statement, if
\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b})\leq 0
, then
\omega(\mathbf{b})=\mathbf{b}+(-\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b}))(-\mathbf{d})\in\mathbf{b}+S\subseteq W_{\mathcal{B}}
. If
\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b})>0
, then since
\nu(\omega(\mathbf{b}))>-1
, we have that
f(\omega(\mathbf{b})-m\mathbf{d})\neq 0
for all
m\geq 1
, and so
x^{\omega(\mathbf{b})}
is a nonzero multiple of
\delta^{m}(x^{\omega(\mathbf{b})-m\mathbf{d}})
for all
m\geq 1
. Taking
m=\lceil\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b})\rceil
, and noting that

\omega(\mathbf{b})-\lceil\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b})\rceil\mathbf{d}=\mathbf{b}+\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b})\mathbf{d}-\lceil\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b})\rceil\mathbf{d}=\mathbf{b}+(\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b})-\lceil\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b})\rceil)(-\mathbf{d})\in W_{\mathcal{B}}

we have that
x^{\omega(\mathbf{b})}
is a nonzero multiple of an element of
I
. Since
I
is
\delta
-compatible,
x^{\omega(\mathbf{b})}\in I
, and so
\omega(\mathbf{b})\in\Exp I=W_{\mathcal{B}}
.
∎

We now prove necessary and sufficient conditions for a differential operator
\delta=x^{\mathbf{d}}f(\theta)
to fix a monomial ideal. This is described using the following subset of
V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)
:

V_{\textnormal{mon}}^{\prime}(f):=\{\omega(\mathbf{a})\mid\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\nu(\mathbf{a})\text{ finite}\}.

Notation:
We also make the assumption for the rest of the section that
\mathbf{d}\neq\mathbf{0}
with
\mathbf{d}=q\mathbf{e}
with
\mathbf{e}
primitive.

Theorem 4.4
.

If there exists a
\mathbf{d}
-compatible subset
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{n}
such that

(1)

for all
\mathbf{a}\in W_{\mathcal{B}}
,
\nu(\mathbf{a})>-\infty

(2)

for all
\mathbf{a}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}^{\prime}(f)\cap W_{\mathcal{B}}
and
i=0,\ldots,q-1
,
\mathbf{a}-i\mathbf{e}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)
, and

(3)

for all
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}^{\prime}(f)\cap W_{\mathcal{B}}
,
\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}\notin S-\mathbf{e}
,

then
\delta
fixes the ideal
I=(x^{\mathbf{a}}\mid\mathbf{a}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}^{\prime}(f)\cap W_{\mathcal{B}})
. Furthermore
\Exp I=\{\mathbf{b}\in W_{\mathcal{B}}\mid\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b})\geq 0\}
.

Proof.

Let
I=(x^{\mathbf{a}}\mid\mathbf{a}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}^{\prime}(f)\cap W_{\mathcal{B}})
. We prove the last statement first. Let
\mathbf{b}\in\Exp I
and
\mathbf{a}\in V^{\prime}_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)\cap W_{\mathcal{B}}
such that
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbf{a}+S
. This implies that
\mathbf{b}\in W_{\mathcal{B}}
. By condition (1),
\omega(\mathbf{b})
exists and therefore
\omega(\mathbf{b})\in W_{\mathcal{B}}\cap V^{\prime}_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)
by Lemma
4.3
. If
\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b})<0
, then
\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b})\leq-\frac{1}{q}
, and so
\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b})\mathbf{d}+\mathbf{e}=(q\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b})+1)\mathbf{e}\in S
. Therefore we have that

\omega(\mathbf{b})-\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{b}+\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b})\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{a}=(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a})+(\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b})\mathbf{d}+\mathbf{e})-\mathbf{e}\in S-\mathbf{e},

a contradiction to condition (3). Therefore
\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b})\geq 0
. Hence
\Exp I\subseteq\{\mathbf{b}\in W_{\mathcal{B}}\mid\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b})\geq 0\}
.

On the other hand, If
\mathbf{b}\in W_{\mathcal{B}}
and
\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b})\geq 0
, then
\omega(\mathbf{b})\in W_{\mathcal{B}}\cap V^{\prime}_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)\subseteq\Exp I
and so

\mathbf{b}=\omega(\mathbf{b})+(-\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b}))(-\mathbf{d})\in\omega(\mathbf{b})+S\subseteq\Exp I.

Therefore
\{\mathbf{b}\in W_{\mathcal{B}}\mid\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b})\geq 0\}\subseteq\Exp I
, and so we have equality.

Let
\mathbf{b}\in\Exp I
. If
\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b})\geq 1
, then
\nu(\mathbf{b})>0
, and so
f(\mathbf{b})\neq 0
. On the other hand, if
0\leq\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b})<1
, then
\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b})=\frac{i}{q}
for some
i\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\}
. Therefore,

\mathbf{b}=\omega(\mathbf{b})-\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b})\mathbf{d}=\omega(\mathbf{a})-i\mathbf{e}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)

by condition (2), and so
f(\mathbf{b})=0
. Therefore
\mathbf{b}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)
if and only if
\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b})<1
, which occurs exactly when
\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{d})<0
, that is, when
\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{d}\notin\Exp I
. Therefore
\delta
fixes
I
by Theorem
3.4
.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

If the operator
\delta
fixes a nonzero monomial ideal
I
, then there exists a
\mathbf{d}
-compatible subset
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{n}
such that

(1)

for all
\mathbf{a}\in W_{\mathcal{B}}
,
\nu(\mathbf{a})>-\infty

(2)

for all
\mathbf{a}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}^{\prime}(f)\cap W_{\mathcal{B}}
and
i=0,\ldots,q-1
,
\mathbf{a}-i\mathbf{e}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)
,

(3)

for all
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}^{\prime}(f)\cap W_{\mathcal{B}}
,
\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}\notin S-\mathbf{e}
, and

(4)

I=(x^{\mathbf{a}}\mid\mathbf{a}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}^{\prime}(f)\cap W_{\mathcal{B}})
and
\Exp I=\{\mathbf{a}\in W_{\mathcal{B}}\mid\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{a})\geq 0\}

Proof.

Suppose that
\delta
fixes a monomial ideal
I=(x^{\mathbf{b}_{1}},\ldots,x^{\mathbf{b}_{m}})
. For
i=1,\ldots,n
and
j=1,\ldots,m
, let

\beta_{ij}=h_{i}(\mathbf{b}_{j})
if
h_{i}(\mathbf{d})=0
and
\beta_{ij}=0
otherwise. For
j=1,\ldots,m
, let

\beta_{j}=(\beta_{1j},\beta_{2j},\ldots,\beta_{nj})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, and then let
\mathcal{B}=\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m}\}
. By construction,
\mathcal{B}
is
\mathbf{d}
-compatible.

(1) Let
\mathbf{a}\in W_{\mathcal{B}}
, and choose
j
such that
\mathbf{a}\in W_{\beta_{j}}
. Fix
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. If
h_{i}(\mathbf{d})<0
, then for sufficiently large
s\in\mathbb{N}
we have that
h_{i}(\mathbf{a}-s\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{b}_{j})=h_{i}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}_{j})-sh_{i}(\mathbf{d})\geq 0
. If
h_{i}(\mathbf{d})=0
, then for all
s\in\mathbb{N}
we have that
h_{i}(\mathbf{a}-s\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{b}_{j})=h_{i}(\mathbf{a})-h_{i}(\mathbf{b}_{j})\geq\beta_{ij}-\beta_{ij}=0
. So, for some large
s
we have that
\mathbf{a}-s\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{b}_{j}\in S
, and so
\mathbf{a}-s\mathbf{d}\in\mathbf{b}_{j}+S\subseteq\Exp I
. Therefore, we have that for any
t\in\frac{1}{q}\mathbb{Z}
with
t\leq-s
,
\mathbf{a}+t\mathbf{d}=\mathbf{a}-s\mathbf{d}+(t+s)\mathbf{d}\in\Exp I
. Since
I
is
\delta
-fixed, this means that for all such
t
,
\mathbf{a}+(t-1)\mathbf{d}\notin V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)
. Therefore
\nu(\mathbf{a})\geq-s-1>-\infty
.

As a consequence to the last paragraph, if we take
\mathbf{a}\in W_{\mathcal{B}}
such that
\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{a})=0
, then the argument shows that there exists
s\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathbf{a}-s\mathbf{d}\in\Exp I
, and furthermore
f(\mathbf{a}-t\mathbf{d})\neq 0
for all
t\geq 1
. Therefore,
x^{\mathbf{a}}
is a nonzero multiple of
\delta^{s}(x^{\mathbf{a}-s\mathbf{d}})\in\delta^{s}(I)=I
. Therefore
V^{\prime}_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)\cap W_{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq\Exp I
.

(2) Let
\mathbf{a}\in V^{\prime}_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)\cap W_{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq\Exp I
. Suppose that for some
i\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\}
we have that
\mathbf{a}-i\mathbf{e}\notin V^{\prime}_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)
. Note this implies that
i\neq 0
. Therefore
\delta(x^{\mathbf{a}-i\mathbf{e}})\neq 0
, and so
\mathbf{a}-i\mathbf{e}+\mathbf{d}\in\Exp I
.
Therefore, for all
j=i,i+1,\ldots,q-1
, we have that
\mathbf{a}-j\mathbf{e}+\mathbf{d}\in\Exp I
, and therefore
\mathbf{a}-j\mathbf{e}\notin V^{\prime}_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)
since
I
is
\delta
-fixed. Hence
\mathbf{a}-t\mathbf{d}\notin V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)
for
t\geq\frac{i}{q}
, and so
\nu(\mathbf{a})\geq-\frac{i-1}{q}
. Therefore, for any
0<t<1-\frac{i-1}{q}=\frac{q-i+1}{q}
, we must have that
\mathbf{a}+t\mathbf{d}\notin V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)
since otherwise
\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{a})
would be greater than 0. Hence
\mathbf{a}-i\mathbf{e}+\mathbf{d}=\mathbf{a}+\frac{q-i}{q}\mathbf{d}\notin V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)
, and so
\mathbf{a}-i\mathbf{e}+2\mathbf{d}\in\Exp I
. However, this implies that
\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{d}\in\Exp I
, a contradiction since
\mathbf{a}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)
.

(3) Fix
j\in\{0,\ldots,m\}
and suppose that
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in V^{\prime}_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)\cap W_{\mathcal{B}}
, which implies that
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\Exp I
. If
\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}\in S-\mathbf{e}
, then
\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{e}\in\mathbf{b}+S
, and so
\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{e}\in\Exp I
. However, by condition (2),
\mathbf{a}-(q-1)\mathbf{e}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)
, and so
\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{a}-(q-1)\mathbf{e}+\mathbf{d}\notin\Exp I
, a contradiction.

(4) If
\mathbf{a}\in\Exp I
, then there exists
\mathbf{b}_{j}
such that
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbf{b}_{j}+S
, and so
\mathbf{a}\in W_{\beta_{j}}\subseteq W_{\mathcal{B}}
. Furthermore, if
\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{a})<0
, then
\omega(\mathbf{a})\in V^{\prime}_{\textnormal{mon}}\cap W_{\mathcal{B}}
and
\mathbf{a}-\lfloor\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{a})\rfloor\mathbf{d}=\omega(\mathbf{a})-i\mathbf{e}
for some
i\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\}
, and so
\mathbf{a}-\lfloor\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{a})\rfloor\mathbf{d}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)
. Therefore
x^{\mathbf{a}}
is not in the image of
\delta
, and so
\mathbf{a}\notin\Exp I
, a contradiction. Therefore
\Exp I\subseteq\{\mathbf{a}\in W_{\mathcal{B}}\mid\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{a})\geq 0\}
. Hence,

\{\mathbf{a}\in W_{\mathcal{B}}\mid\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{a})\geq 0\}\subseteq(V^{\prime}_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)\cap W_{\mathcal{B}})+S\subseteq\Exp I\subseteq\{\mathbf{a}\in W_{\mathcal{B}}\mid\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{a})\geq 0\}

and so we have equality throughout.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

Suppose
\mathbf{d}\neq 0
. The operator
\delta
fixes some nonzero monomial ideal if and only if there exists a
\mathbf{d}
-compatible subset
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{n}
such that

(1)

For all
\mathbf{a}\in W_{\mathcal{B}}
,
\nu(\mathbf{a})>-\infty

(2)

for all
\mathbf{a}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}^{\prime}(f)\cap W_{\mathcal{B}}
and
i=0,\ldots,q-1
,
\mathbf{a}-i\mathbf{e}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)
, and

(3)

for all
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}^{\prime}(f)\cap W_{\mathcal{B}}
,
\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}\notin S-\mathbf{e}
.

In this case,
\delta
fixes the ideal
I=(x^{\mathbf{a}}\mid\mathbf{a}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}^{\prime}(f)\cap W_{\mathcal{B}})
, and furthermore
\Exp I=\{\mathbf{a}\in W_{\mathcal{B}}\mid\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{a})\geq 0\}
.

Corollary 4.7
.

Suppose
-\mathbf{d}\in\inter\sigma^{\vee}
. The operator
\delta
fixes some nonzero monomial ideal if and only if

(1)

For all
\mathbf{a}\in S
,
\nu(\mathbf{a})>-\infty

(2)

for all
\mathbf{a}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}^{\prime}(f)
and
i=0,\ldots,q-1
,
\mathbf{a}-i\mathbf{e}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)
, and

(3)

for all
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}^{\prime}(f)
,
\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}\notin S-\mathbf{e}
.

In this case,
\delta
fixes only the ideal
I=(x^{\mathbf{a}}\mid\mathbf{a}\in V_{\textnormal{mon}}^{\prime}(f))
, and furthermore
\Exp I=\{\mathbf{a}\in S\mid\nu^{\prime}(\mathbf{a})\geq 0\}
.

Proof.

This corollary follows from Corollary
4.6
once we observe that if
-\mathbf{d}\in\inter\sigma^{\vee}
, then
h_{i}(\mathbf{d})<0
for all
i
. Therefore the only
\mathbf{d}
-compatible vector is
\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, and
W_{\mathbf{0}}=S
.
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

If
-\mathbf{d}\in\inter\sigma^{\vee}
, then
\delta
fixes at most one nonzero monomial ideal.

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{n}
be a
\mathbf{d}

Corollary 4.10
.

If
-\mathbf{d}\in S\setminus\inter\sigma^{\vee}
and

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{n}
be a
\mathbf{d}
-compatible subset such that
\delta
fixes the ideal

Each

5.
Applications

We now review applications, which are all derived from Theorem
5.3
and elementary combinatorial geometry. This is phrased in suitable abstraction to find interest beyond the applications we display.

First, we review a bit of the combinatorial geometry, for which one can see
[
Grü03
]
for a deeper introduction. Among other things this will establish definitions of basic objects which sometimes vary slightly from source to source. By a
polyhedron
we mean a region of a Euclidean space of finite dimension defined as the intersection of finitely many half-spaces. This is most similar to
H
-polyhedra, as opposed to a
V
-polyehdra where one utilizes a definition in terms of vertices, in many cases these descriptions are equivalent. We avoid the term ‘polytope’ which conflicts among standard sources.

Naturally, the
H
-description arises by preferring the
facets
, or maximal faces. We additionally find it convenient to use a related, slightly more flexible description as the model of computing or specifying a polyhedron
P
. We motivate this by the following natural set of data. Set
\mathcal{F}
the set of faces of
P
. Naturally associated to
\mathcal{F}
is a sequence
(g_{\tau},M_{\tau})_{\tau\in\mathcal{F}}
where
g_{\tau}\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
is a linear form and
M_{\tau}\in\mathbb{Q}
with the property that
\mathbf{v}\in P
if and only if
g_{\tau}(\mathbf{v})\geq M_{\tau}
for all
\tau\in\mathcal{F}
. We have need to consider a slight generalization of this description to account for inefficiencies.

Definition 5.1
.

Fix
P
a polyhedron.
A
face data
consists of a finite set
\mathcal{S}
and a sequence
\{(g_{\tau},M_{\tau})\}_{\tau\in\mathcal{S}}
with
g_{\tau}\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
is a linear form,
M_{\tau}\in\mathbb{Q}
and a point
\mathbf{v}\in P
if and only if
g_{\tau}(\mathbf{v})\geq M_{\tau}
for all
\tau
.

As described there is a canonical face data given the
H
-description of a polyhedron which motivates the name. However, as we have defined it there is not a unique face data describing a given polyhedron. In particular, a face data could have redundant information. Shortly, we will describe an algorithmic process for computing face data, but this will not be optimal in the sense that we do not guarantee it returns the canonical face data. However, in practice these redundancies cause no issue.

By our definition, polyhedra are not necessarily bounded nor convex. However, at times we will prefer to work with convex polyhedra. Convex or not, a unifying property we insist holds identifies those polyhedra whose intersection with the lattice defining the fixed semigroup ring under consideration in an ideal.

Definition 5.2
.

A polyhedron
P
is
\sigma^{\vee}
-closed
proved
\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{a}\in P
for all
\mathbf{p}\in P
and
\mathbf{a}\in\sigma^{\vee}
.

Notably,
\sigma^{\vee}
-closed polyhedra are never bounded. Any monomial ideal naturally has associated to it a canonical
\sigma^{\vee}
-closed polyhedron based on its exponent set. Also, by definition every
\sigma^{\vee}
-polyhedron defines an ideal. Obviously, this is not however a bijection as small perturbations in the faces of the polyhedron will often not change the associated ideal. Nonetheless, we refer to these structures as associated to each other.

Now we come to the general form of our primary application which articulates lattice point membership in a union of polyhedra in terms of a differential operator fixing its associated ideal.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
be
\sigma^{\vee}
-closed polyhedra with face data
\{(g_{\tau},M_{\tau})\}_{\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
and
\mathbf{0}\neq\mathbf{d}\in-S
.

(1)

Let
I=\langle x^{\mathbf{a}}\mid\mathbf{a}\in\bigcup P_{i}\rangle
and
f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}(g_{\tau}(\theta),\lceil M_{\tau}-g_{\tau}(\mathbf{d})-1\rceil)!
. For any
h\in(H_{\mathbf{d}})\cap(f)
,
\delta=x^{\mathbf{d}}h(\theta)
fixes
I
, and
x^{\mathbf{a}}\in I
if and only if
\delta(x^{\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{d}})\neq 0
.

(2)

Let
J=\langle x^{\mathbf{a}}\mid\mathbf{a}\in\bigcup\inter P_{i}\rangle
and
f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}(g_{\tau}(\theta),\lfloor M_{\tau}-g_{\tau}(\mathbf{d})\rfloor)!
. For any
h\in(H_{\mathbf{d}})\cap(f)
,
\delta=x^{\mathbf{d}}h(\theta)
fixes
J
, and
x^{\mathbf{a}}\in J
if and only if
\delta(x^{\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{d}})\neq 0
.

Proof.

As the proof of both statements are similar, we only provide the proof of the first statement. We claim that
g_{\tau}(\mathbf{a})\geq 0
for all
\tau\in\mathcal{F}
and
\mathbf{a}\in S
. Let
\mathbf{b}\in\Exp I
. For any
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbf{b}+m\mathbf{a}\in\Exp I\subseteq P
, and so
M_{\tau}\leq g_{\tau}(\mathbf{b}+m\mathbf{a})=g_{\tau}(\mathbf{b})+mg_{\tau}(\mathbf{a})
. Therefore
g_{\tau}(\mathbf{a})\geq(M_{\tau}-g_{\tau}(\mathbf{b}))/m
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and so
g_{\tau}(\mathbf{a})\geq 0
.

Therefore, for any face
\tau
, we have that
(g_{\tau}(\theta),\lceil M_{\tau}-g_{\tau}(\mathbf{d})\rceil-1)!(\mathbf{a})\geq 0
. Hence
f(\mathbf{a})\neq 0
if and only if there exists
1\leq i\leq n
such that
(g_{\tau}(\mathbf{a}),\lceil M_{\tau}-g_{\tau}(\mathbf{d})\rceil-1)!>0
for all
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{i}
. This occurs exactly when
g_{\tau}(\mathbf{a})>\lceil M_{\tau}-g_{\tau}(\mathbf{d})\rceil-1
, which is equivalent to
g_{\tau}(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{d})\geq M_{\tau}
. Thus,
f(\mathbf{a})\neq 0
if and only if there exists
i
such that
g_{\tau}(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{d})\in P_{i}
, i.e.
\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{d}\in\bigcup_{i}P_{i}=\Exp I
. Therefore
V_{\textnormal{mon}}(f)\cap\Exp I=\Exp I\setminus(\Exp I-\mathbf{d})
, and so
\delta
fixes
I
by Theorem
3.4
. Also, we have that
f(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{d})=0
for
\mathbf{a}\notin\Exp I
, which proves the last statement as well.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Theorem
3.6
is a special case of Theorem
5.3
. This comes from the fact that every monomial ideal
I
is of the form in the hypothesis of
5.3
. Precisely, if
I=(x^{\mathbf{a}_{1}},\ldots,x^{\mathbf{a}_{n}})
, we may set
P_{i}=\mathbf{a}_{i}+\sigma^{\vee}
for each
i
. Now each
P_{i}
is a polyhedron, and
I=(x^{\mathbf{a}}\mid\mathbf{a}\in\bigcup_{i}P_{i})
. The improvement in Theorem
5.3
is the ability to handle ideals which can be defined by polyhedra other than shifted cones, e.g. integrally closed ideals, symbolic powers, and multiplier ideals. See, for instance, Theorem
5.9
below.

The power in the above theorem comes from the ability to compute face data of polyhedra after fundamental operations. Specifically, for
\mathbf{d}
a vector,
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
polyhedra, four of the fundamental operations are the following operations.

Translating

P_{1}+\mathbf{d}

Scaling

cP_{1}

Intersection

P_{1}\cap P_{2}

Minkowski sum

P_{1}+P_{2}
.

Setting
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
the ideals associated to
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
, the combinatorial geometric operations on polyhedra of course produce a polyhedron whose associated ideal arises from expected ideal constructions, specifically, the ideal associated to
P_{1}+P_{2}
is the ideal product
I_{1}I_{2}
.

Remark 5.5
.

All fundamental operations described trivially preserve the
\sigma^{\vee}
-closed conditions.

One should consider unions as a fundamental operation, but as defined the union of polyhedra may fail to be a polyhedra and taking the convex hull is a lossy process in that working this way would not allow us to distinguish between ideals and their integral closures. As such, the statement of Theorem
5.3
assumes polyhedral input but works to describe the ideal associated to the union so this operation is not lost in our application.

Note, in total generality, computing face data of the output of some of these fundamental operations from the face data of its inputs can be computationally difficult, notably NP-hard even in more restrictive settings than our general polyhedra
[
Tiw08
]
. However, we quickly review some easy and rather explicit computations in some specialized settings sufficient for our applications.

Lemma 5.6
.

Fix
P_{1}
and
P_{2}

\sigma^{\vee}
-closed polyhedra with face data
\{(g_{\tau},M_{\tau})\}_{\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}
for
i=1,2
,
\mathbf{d}
a vector, and
c>0
.

(1)

P_{1}+\mathbf{d}
is defined by face data
\{(g_{\tau},M_{\tau}+g_{\tau}(\mathbf{d}))\}_{\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}

(2)

cP_{1}
is defined by face data
\{(g_{\tau},cM_{\tau})\}_{\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}

(3)

P_{1}\cap P_{2}
is defined by face data
\{(g_{\tau},M_{\tau})\}_{\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\cup\mathcal{S}_{2}}

(4)

P_{1}+P_{2}
is defined by face data
\{(g_{\tau},N_{\tau})\}_{\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\cup\mathcal{S}_{2}}
for some
N_{\tau}\in\mathbb{R}
. In the two-dimensional case, for each
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{i}
, we have that
N_{\tau}=M_{\tau}+g_{\tau}(\mathbf{a}_{\tau})
, where
\mathbf{a}_{\tau}
is a vertex of
P_{3-i}
such that line through
a_{\tau}
parallel to
\tau
does not intersect the interior of
P_{3-i}
.

Proof.

(
1
) We have that
\mathbf{v}\in P_{1}+\mathbf{d}
if and only if
\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{d}\in P_{1}
, which occurs exactly when
g_{\tau}(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{d})\geq M_{\tau}
for all
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
. Therefore
\mathbf{v}\in P_{1}+\mathbf{d}
if and only if
g_{\tau}(\mathbf{v})\geq M_{\tau}+g_{\tau}(\mathbf{d})
for all
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
.

(
2
) We have that
\mathbf{v}\in cP_{1}
if and only if
(1/c)\mathbf{v}\in P_{1}
, which occurs exactly when
g_{\tau}((1/c)\mathbf{v})\geq M_{\tau}
for all
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
. Therefore
\mathbf{v}\in cP_{1}
if and only if
g_{\tau}(\mathbf{v})\geq cM_{\tau}
for all
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
.

(
3
) We have that
\mathbf{v}\in P_{1}\cap P_{2}
if and only if
\mathbf{v}
is in both
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
, i.e. if
g_{\tau}(\mathbf{v})\geq M_{\tau}
for all
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\cup\mathcal{S}_{2}
.

(
4
) The result is well-known, and algorithms for computing the numbers
N_{\tau}
can be found in
[
dBCvKO08
, Ch. 13]
. However, there are some technical adaptations to derive the stated claim. Notably, we are working with
\sigma^{\vee}
-closed regions which are not compact and also any two polyhedra have parallel faces.

We note the selection criteria for which
\mathbf{a}_{\tau}
is chosen is simply done by linear programming. Thus, for each face
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
, there is a face
\tau^{\prime}
of
P_{1}+P_{2}
which is defined by
\tau^{\prime}=\tau+\mathbf{a}_{\tau}
, where
\mathbf{a}_{\tau}
is a vertex of
P_{2}
which minimizes
g_{\tau}
, and similarly for every
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
, there is a face
\tau^{\prime}=\tau+\mathbf{a}_{\tau}
, where
\mathbf{a}_{\tau}
is a vertex of
P_{1}
. A vector
\mathbf{v}
is in the half-plane defined by
\tau^{\prime}
if and only if
\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{a}_{\tau}
is in the half-plane defined by
\tau

g_{\tau}(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{a}_{\tau})\geq M_{\tau}
, implying
g_{\tau}(\mathbf{v})\geq M_{\tau}+g_{\tau}(\mathbf{a}_{\tau})

).

5.1.

We now turn out attention to sharper results in the case of ideal membership problems for the integral closure of powers

5.2.
Multiplier ideals and jumping numbers

We now turn to sharper applications of Theorem
5.3
towards multiplier ideals and their variants. In the general framework, if
X
is a normal variety,
\Delta
a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor so that
K_{X}+\Delta
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier where
K_{X}
is a canonical divisor,
\mathcal{I}
an ideal sheaf on
X
and
c\geq 0
a real number, the multiplier ideals
J(X,\Delta,\mathcal{I}^{c})
are defined in terms of a log resolution of this data. Famously, on toric varieties, so
X=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}[S]
, there is a canonical torus invariant choice for
K_{X}
. Moreover, choosing
\Delta
torus invariant then there is a multiple
r
and an exponent
\mathbf{u}
so that
\textrm{div }x^{\mathbf{u}}=r(K_{X}+\Delta)
, whence to each
\Delta
one can associate a vector
\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{u}/r
. The exponent associated to
\Delta=0
is
\mathbf{w}=(1,1,\ldots,1)
. Finally, for
\mathcal{I}
defined by a monomial ideal
I
, each
J(X,\Delta,\mathcal{I}^{c})
is a monomial ideal and we have the following explicit description of the multiplier ideals
[
Bli04
,
How01
]
.

Theorem 5.16

(Blickle-Howald)
.

The monomial ideal

J(X,\Delta,\mathcal{I}^{c})=\langle x^{\mathbf{a}}\in R\colon\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{w}\in c\cdot\operatorname{int}\operatorname{Newt}I\rangle

where
\operatorname{int}\operatorname{Newt}I
denotes the relative interior.

From here on, we’ll use the notation
J(X,\mathbf{w},I^{c})
instead of
J(X,\Delta,\mathcal{I}^{c})
for this ideal or even just
J(\mathbf{w},c)
when
X
and
I
are clear. As is well-known, as the value of
c
gets larger, the multiplier ideals

form a decreasing family of ideals and values where

for all
\epsilon>0
are called

for the triple
(X,\Delta,\mathfrak{a})
. The smallest jumping number is the log canonical threshold which plays a most critical role in the study of singularities. Detecting when a value

is the log canonical threshold therefore comes down to determining if the monomial ideal

is proper or not. Our applications will characterize the jumping numbers

Definition 5.20
.

Let
I_{1},\ldots,I_{n}
be monomial ideals,
\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}
,
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

is an ideal of the form

\displaystyle J_{I_{1},\ldots,I_{n}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{c})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\langle x^{\mathbf{a}}\mid\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{w}\in\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\operatorname{Newt}I_{j}\right\rangle\textrm{ or }

\displaystyle J^{\circ}_{I_{1},\ldots,I_{n}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{c})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\langle x^{\mathbf{a}}\mid\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{w}\in\sum_{j={1}}^{n}c_{j}\inter\operatorname{Newt}I_{j}\right\rangle.

.

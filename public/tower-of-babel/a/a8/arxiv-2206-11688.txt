Motivic stable cohomotopy and unimodular rows
Samuel Lerbet

Abstract
We relate the group structure of van der Kallen on orbit sets of unimodular rows ( [ Kal02 ] ) with values in a smooth algebra A A over a field k k with the motivic cohomotopy groups of X = Spec ⁡ A X=\Spec A with coefficients in 𝔸 n ∖ 0 \mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0 in the sense of [ AF22 ] . In the last section, we compare the motivic cohomotopy theory studied in this paper and defined by 𝔸 n + 1 ∖ 0 \mathbb{A}^{n+1}\setminus 0 or, equivalently, by an 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -weakly equivalent quadric Q 2 ​ n + 1 Q_{2n+1} to that considered in [ AF22 ] , defined by a quadric Q 2 ​ n Q_{2n} , by means of explicit morphisms Q 2 ​ n + 1 → Q 2 ​ n Q_{2n+1}\rightarrow Q_{2n} , Q 2 ​ n × 𝔾 m → Q 2 ​ n + 1 Q_{2n}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}\rightarrow Q_{2n+1} of quadrics.

1.  Introduction

Some history

Unimodular rows.

Let
n
be a non-negative integer. If
A
is a ring,
unimodular rows
of length
n
with values in
A
control surjective
A
-module homomorphisms
A^{n}\rightarrow A
and are thus of interest in algebraic
\mathrm{K}
-theory. They form a set
\mathrm{Um}_{n}(A)
which is endowed with a natural action of
\GL_{n}(A)
on the right so that we may consider the orbit set of the action of the subgroup
\mathrm{E}_{n}(A)
of elementary matrices. Building on earlier work of Vaserstein and Suslin in the case
n=2
(
[
VS76
, Section 5]
), van der Kallen introduced in
[
Kal89
]
a group structure on this orbit set under finiteness assumptions on the ring
A
. He uses the notion of
stable dimension

\sdim
of a ring
A
which is deduced from Bass’s
stable range
condition. This is in fact a set
(\mathrm{DS})_{r}
of conditions indexed by integers
r\geqslant 1
, defined on
[
Bas75
, p. 231]
, where
\mathrm{DS}
stand for the French
domaine stable
;
\mathrm{DS}(A)
is then the infinimum of the set of integers
r
such that
A
satisfies
(\mathrm{DS})_{r}
if this set is not empty, and
\mathrm{DS}(A)=\infty
if this set is empty. Finally, by definition,
\sdim(A)=\mathrm{DS}(A)-1
(
[
Kal89
, Introduction, 1.2]
). Then van der Kallen assumes that
\sdim(A)
satisfies the inequality
\sdim(A)\leqslant 2n-4
and he shows that one may then endow the orbit set
\mathrm{Um}_{n}(A)/\mathrm{E}_{n}(A)
with the structure of an abelian group functorially in
A
. This was accomplished in the early eighties and is stated in
[
Kal02
, Theorem 3.3]
: thanks to this theorem, the group structure may be easily understood in terms of the elementary Mennicke–Newman lemma. Definitions and results on this subject are recalled in Section
3
.

Stable cohomotopy.

The group law introduced by van der Kallen is an algebraic analogue of Borsuk’s construction of
cohomotopy groups
. Let
X
be a CW-complex. If
n
is an integer and if
X
is “small” relative to
n
—technically, we assume that the dimension
d
of
X
satisfies
d\leqslant 2n-2
—, then Borsuk equips the set
[X,\mathrm{S}^{n}]
of homotopy classes of continuous maps from
X
to the
n
-sphere
\mathrm{S}^{n}
with the structure of an abelian group denoted by
\pi^{n}(X)
, see
[
Bor36
]
. Roughly speaking, given continuous maps
f,g:X\rightarrow\mathrm{S}^{n}
, Borsuk lifts them into a map to the wedge sum
\mathrm{S}^{n}\vee\mathrm{S}^{n}
and composes with the fold map
\mathrm{S}^{n}\vee\mathrm{S}^{n}\rightarrow\mathrm{S}^{n}
to obtain a map
X\rightarrow\mathrm{S}^{n}
which is by definition the sum of
f
and
g
: in particular, it is easy to see that this group structure is functorial in
X
.

Remark
.

Here,
2n-2
should be thought of as
2(n-1)
and
n-1
as the connectedness of
\mathrm{S}^{n}
: more generally, if
Y
is a pointed space that is
m
-connected, then Borsuk’s construction yields a group structure on
[X,Y]
for any CW-complex
X
of dimension
d\leqslant 2m
which is functorial in
X

and

Y
.

The topological comparison theorem.

If
A
is an algebra over a field
k
, then unimodular rows of length
n
with values in
A
may be interpreted as
k
-morphisms from
\Spec A
to
\mathbb{A}_{k}^{n}\setminus 0=\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0
. Similarly, if
X
is a finite CW-complex of dimension
d
, then unimodular rows of length
n
with values in the ring
\mathrm{C}(X)
of real valued continuous functions on
X
may be viewed as continuous maps
X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. Composing with the standard homotopy equivalence
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow\mathrm{S}^{n-1}
allows one to think about unimodular rows with values in
\mathrm{C}(X)
in terms of the cohomotopy group
\pi^{n-1}(X)
. It is easy to see that elements of the same orbit under the action of
\mathrm{E}_{n}(\mathrm{C}(X))
induce continuous maps
X\rightarrow\mathrm{S}^{n-1}
which are homotopic, thus producing a map

\Phi:\mathrm{Um}_{n}(\mathrm{C}(X))/\mathrm{E}_{n}(\mathrm{C}(X))\rightarrow\pi^{n-1}(X).

It is then known that the ring
\mathrm{C}(X)
is of stable dimension
d
by work of Vaserstein
[
Vas71
]
, so that the source and the target of the map
\Phi
both have a natural group structure if
d\leqslant 2n-4
following van der Kallen and Borsuk respectively and in fact:

Theorem

(
[
Kal89
, Theorem 7.7]
)
.

If
X
is a finite CW-complex of dimension
d\leqslant 2n-4
, then the map
\Phi:\mathrm{Um}_{n}(\mathrm{C}(X))/\mathrm{E}_{n}(\mathrm{C}(X))\rightarrow\pi^{n-1}(X)
is a group isomorphism.

This theorem gave van der Kallen the intuition that one should be able to think about the group law on
\mathrm{Um}_{n}(A)/\mathrm{E}_{n}(A)
in terms of yet-to-be-defined cohomotopy groups for schemes which would shed light on the somewhat difficult-to-follow computations of
[
Kal89
]
. However, the technology to do homotopy theory in the category of schemes was not available at the time of van der Kallen’s work so that this remained a heuristic for some time.

Motivic homotopy theory.

The situation has changed in the last thirty years and we now have a well-established homotopy theory for schemes: the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy theory
initiated by Morel and Voevodsky in
[
MV99
]
. In particular, assuming one is working over a field
k
, one has (several) models of the topological sphere in
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy theory, one of those being the
k
-scheme
\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0
. This allows one to define cohomotopy groups
[X,\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0]_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
if
X
is of suitable (
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-cohomological) dimension: this is done by Asok and Fasel in
[
AF22
]
and we state the results that will be useful to us in Section
4
.

The motivic comparison theorem.

Now let
A
be a smooth affine
k
-algebra, that is, we assume that
A
is of finite type over
k
, and let
X
denote its spectrum. It is easy to see that unimodular rows in the same orbit under the action of
\mathrm{E}_{n}(A)
induce
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopic morphisms
X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0
so that we have a well-defined map

\Psi:\mathrm{Um}_{n}(A)/\mathrm{E}_{n}(A)\rightarrow[X,\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0]_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}.

Assume further that the Krull dimension
d
of
A
satisfies
d\leqslant 2n-4
. Then since
A
is Noetherian, we have the inequality
\sdim(A)\leqslant d
, see for instance
[
Bas75
, Théorème 1]
, hence the orbit set
\mathrm{Um}_{n}(A)/\mathrm{E}_{n}(A)
has a group structure following van der Kallen, while its definition and results of Nisnevich on the homonymous cohomological dimension show that the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-cohomological dimension of a scheme is bounded above by its Krull dimension, hence
[X,\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0]_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
also has a group structure following Asok and Fasel. The main result of the first part of this paper confirms van der Kallen’s intuition and may be stated as follows.

Theorem

(Theorem
5.1
)
.

Assume that the ring
A
is of Krull dimension
d\leqslant 2n-4
. Then the map
\Psi
is a group isomorphism.

Structure of the proof

The proof of Theorem
5.1
occupies Section
5
. It is divided in two subsections.

•

In the first subsection, we show that
\Psi
is a bijection. The proof is split in two parts. First, given a smooth
k
-algebra
A
, we compare the orbit set
\mathrm{Um}_{n}(A)/\mathrm{E}_{n}(A)
to the set of
naive

\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy classes of morphisms
X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0
: this is a particular case of
[
Fas11
, Theorem 2.1]
. Second, we appeal to a result of Asok, Hoyois and Wendt
[
AHW18
, Theorem 2.2.4]
, more precisely its consequence
[
AHW18
, Corollary 4.2.6]
, that states that
with source an affine scheme
and
with certain targets
, for example
\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0
, naive
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy classes and “true”
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy classes actually coincide. This phenomenon was already observed by Morel in
[
Mor12
, Remark 8.10]
for
k
perfect and
n\neq 2
in the particular case of target
\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0
.

•

In the second subsection, we prove that
\Psi
is a group homomorphism. We introduce an open subset
V_{2n}
of
(\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0)\times(\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0)
: this
k
-scheme allows us to produce an explicit, scheme-theoretic model of the fold map
(\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0)\vee(\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0)\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0
using the procedure of the Mennicke–Newman lemma. This model of the fold map is the link between cohomotopy groups and the group structure on the orbit set. To see that the datum of maps
u,v:X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0
for
X
small is equivalent (up to homotopy) to the datum of a map
X\rightarrow V_{2n}
, that is, that we can lift maps
X\rightarrow(\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0)\times(\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0)
to maps
X\rightarrow V_{2n}
(up to homotopy), we need to study the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connectedness of the map
V_{2n}\rightarrow(\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0)\times(\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0)
. This is done by using the motivic Blakers–Massey theorem to compare the fibre and the cofibre of this inclusion, the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connectedness of the cofibre being controlled by the purity theorem.

Comparing cohomotopy theories

After this investigation of the cohomotopy theory defined by unimodular rows or, equivalently, by
\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0
, we compare it to the cohomotopy theory studied in
[
AF22
]
. The latter is given by another model of sphere, the quadric

Q_{2n}=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n},z]/\langle x_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}y_{n}-z(1-z)\rangle.

This comparison is made more convenient by replacing
\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0
by the quadric

Q_{2n-1}=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]/\langle x_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}y_{n}-1\rangle

whose projection on the first
n
factors yields a morphism to
\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0
which is in fact an isomorphism in the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy category (slightly more can be said about this map, see our discussion of Jouanolou devices below): thus, this replacement is harmless from the point of view of cohomotopy theory.

With this comparison in view, we produce morphisms of
k
-schemes
\eta_{n}:Q_{2n+1}\rightarrow Q_{2n}
and
\mu_{n}:\mathbb{G}_{m}\times Q_{2n}\rightarrow Q_{2n+1}
for all
n>0
, where
\mathbb{G}_{m}
is the multiplicative group over
k
.

First, Morel’s computation of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy sheaves of motivic spheres gives an isomorphism
[Q_{2n+1},Q_{2n}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\simeq\mathrm{K}_{-1}^{\mathrm{MW}}(k)
where the left-hand side denotes the hom-set between
Q_{2n+1}
and
Q_{2n}
in the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy category (see below for more details on notations) and
\mathrm{K}_{*}^{\mathrm{MW}}(k)
denotes the Milnor–Witt
\mathrm{K}
-theory of
k
(
[
Mor12
, Definition 3.1]
):
\eta_{n}
corresponds to the generator
\eta
of Milnor–Witt
\mathrm{K}
-theory under this isomorphism, see Theorem
6.2
, which explains our interest in this map. Furthermore, in
[
BS00
, Section 7]
, given a Noetherian
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
A
of Krull dimension
d
where

an
even
integer, the authors construct a group homomorphism
\Psi
from
\mathrm{Um}_{d+1}(A)/\mathrm{E}_{d+1}(A)
to the
Euler class group

\mathrm{E}^{d}(A)
of

, the theory of these groups being the object of
[
BS00
]
. The map
\Psi
is constructed using the
Euler class
of the rank

\mathrm{Um}_{d+1}(A)
, namely the kernel of the corresponding surjective homomorphism of

[
BS00
, Section 4]
and the authors observe in the course of the proof of
[
BS00
, Proposition 7.5]
that it is trivial for modules of odd rank: thus, it was not clear how to define such a map for

odd. This was accomplished in
[
DZ15
]
where the authors construct a map
\phi:\mathrm{Um}_{d+1}(A)/\mathrm{E}_{d+1}(A)\rightarrow\mathrm{E}^{d}(A)
for
any

and for any Noetherian ring

by introducing a

, although a different strategy was used to prove that it is well-defined. The map

On the other hand, the morphism

2.  Some notations and facts of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy theory

Categorical considerations.

A
cogroup
object in a category
\mathsf{C}
is a group object in the opposite category
\mathsf{C}^{op}
; a
cocommutative
cogroup object in
\mathsf{C}
is a commutative group object in
\mathsf{C}^{op}
. By definition, if
X
is a (cocommutative) cogroup object in
\mathsf{C}
, then
\Hom_{\mathsf{C}}(X,Y)
has a natural (commutative) group structure for any object
Y
of
\mathsf{C}
. If
\mathsf{C}
has finite coproducts hence an initial object
0
, a (cocommutative) cogroup may be described as an object
X
of
\mathsf{C}
together with morphisms
c:X\rightarrow X\coprod X
(comultiplication or sometimes coaddition in the case of a cocommutative cogroup),
i:X\rightarrow X
(coinverse) and
e:X\rightarrow 0
(counit) such that, seen in
\mathsf{C}^{op}
,
(X,c,i,e)
is a (commutative) group object. In the context of homotopy theory, we will speak of (cocommutative)
h
-cogroups, that is, (cocommutative) cogroup objects in the homotopy category: these are discussed for general model categories in
[
Hov99
]
.

We have the following entirely formal lemma about functors and cogroups:

Lemma 2.1
.

If
\mathsf{C}
and
\mathsf{C}^{\prime}
have finite coproducts and if
F:\mathsf{C}\rightarrow\mathsf{C}^{\prime}
is a functor that commutes with finite coproducts, then
F
preserves cogroup objects in an obvious sense and given a cogroup object
X
in
\mathsf{C}
, the map
\Hom_{\mathsf{C}}(X,Y)\rightarrow\Hom_{\mathsf{C}^{\prime}}(F(X),F(Y))
is a group homomorphism for any object
Y
of
\mathsf{C}
.

Proof.

Let
(X,c,i,e)
be a cogroup in
\mathsf{C}
. By assumption on
F
, the map
\varphi:F(X)\coprod F(X)\rightarrow F(X\coprod X)
induced by application of
F
to the structure maps
X\rightarrow X\coprod X
is an isomorphism. Thus
c
induces a map
F(X)\xrightarrow{F(c)}F\left(X\coprod X\right)\xrightarrow{\varphi^{-1}}F(X)\coprod F(X)
. Similarly,
i
induces a map
F(i):F(X)\rightarrow F(X)
and
e
induces a map
F(X)\rightarrow F(0)
where
F(0)
is initial because
F
commutes with finite (hence empty) coproducts. These data endow
F(X)
with the structure of a cogroup by commutation of
F
with finite coproducts. Now let
Y
be an object of
\mathsf{C}
. We denote the group laws on
\Hom_{\mathsf{C}}(X,Y)
and
\Hom_{\mathsf{C}^{\prime}}(F(X),F(Y))
by
*
. Then we have
F(f)*F(g)=F(f)\coprod F(g)\circ(\varphi^{-1}\circ F(c))
since the comultiplication of
F(X)
is
\varphi^{-1}\circ F(c)
. By definition,
F(f)\coprod F(g)\circ\varphi^{-1}:F(X\coprod X)\rightarrow F(X)\coprod F(X)\rightarrow F(Y)
is equal to
F(f\coprod g)
. Thus
F(f)*F(g)=F(f\coprod g)\circ F(c)=F(f\coprod g\circ c)=F(f*g)
as required.
∎

Now let
\mathsf{A}
be an additive category with finite direct sums denoted using the symbol
\bigoplus
(thus we make no notational distinction between finite coproducts and finite products). Any object
X
of
\mathsf{A}
then has a natural cocommutative cogroup structure whose underlying coaddition
c^{add}:X\rightarrow X\oplus X
is the map induced by
(\Id_{X},\Id_{X})
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(X,c,i,e)
be a cogroup structure on the object
X
of the additive category
\mathsf{A}
. Then
c=c^{add}
.

In particular, for any object
Y
of
\mathsf{A}
, the group structure on the set
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(X,Y)
induced by
c
is precisely the group structure provided by the fact that
\mathsf{A}
is an additive category: there are no “exotic” cogroup objects in an additive category.

Proof.

We denote by
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}^{c}(X,X)
the hom-set of morphisms from
X
to
X
in the category
\mathsf{A}
endowed with the group structure induced by
c
and by
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(X,X)
the group of morphisms in the additive category
\mathsf{A}
(whose group structure is thus induced by
c^{add}
). By definition, the neutral element of
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}^{c}(X,X)
is the morphism
X\xrightarrow{e}0\rightarrow X
. Since
0
is a zero object of
\mathsf{A}
, it is also the neutral element of the group
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(X,X)
. Now denote by
*
the group law underlying
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}^{c}(X,X)
. Consider the structure morphisms
i_{1},i_{2}:X\rightarrow X\oplus X
and
p_{1},p_{2}:X\oplus X\rightarrow X
of the direct sums. Denoting by
*
the group law on
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(X,X)
. By definition of a cogroup,
*
is bilinear for composition, hence
p_{1}\circ(i_{1}*i_{2})=(p_{1}\circ i_{1})*(p_{1}\circ i_{2})=p_{1}\circ i_{1}=\Id_{X}
because
p_{1}\circ i_{2}
is the neutral element of
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(X,X)
hence of
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}^{c}(X,X)
as already observed. Similarly,
p_{2}\circ(i_{1}*i_{2})=\Id_{X}
and as a result, the equality
i_{1}*i_{2}=c^{add}=(\Id_{X},\Id_{X}):X\rightarrow X\oplus X
holds. On the other hand,
i_{1}*i_{2}=i_{1}\oplus i_{2}\circ c
where
i_{1}\oplus i_{2}:X\oplus X\rightarrow X\oplus X
is equal to
\Id_{X\oplus X}
by definition. Thus
c=i_{1}*i_{2}=c^{add}
.
∎

For instance, we may apply Lemma
2.1
with
\mathsf{C}^{\prime}
additive, in which case the target of the map of said lemma has an unambiguous group structure thanks to Lemma
2.2
.

.

We let

-schemes

, we denote by

. The

.

Let

-scheme. Then the datum of a quasi-coherent sheaf on

is equivalent to the datum of a

-module: we usually make no difference in notation between the two objects, denoting by

the quasi-coherent sheaf induced by the

-module

and vice versa, and we may loosely speak of

as a vector bundle on

-module of finite rank in view of the Serre–Swan correspondence between finite projective

,

]
, equivalence between (2) and (7)). We let

denote the

-scheme

, and similarly with

and

.

Given a morphism of rings

, we denote the

-module of Kähler differentials of

. If

,

]

its determinant, that is, the top exterior power

of

.

The
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy categories.

All the
k
-schemes that we consider are assumed to be of finite type. By

k
-scheme
, we mean a
k
-scheme that is smooth and separated (and of finite type). We let
\mathsf{Sm}_{k}

over

or
k

between

A

the simplicial circle, pointed as usual and viewed as a space using the associated constant presheaf, and

Example 2.3
.

Let

; there is a pointed

, §3, Example 2.20]

For

a pointed space, we set

Finally, we denote by

Jouanolou devices.

Let
X
be a topological space. Recall the notion of torsor under a sheaf of groups
[
Sta23
,
Tag 02FO
]
. If
X
is a scheme, by definition, the datum of the structure of a torsor under a sheaf of groups
\mathscr{G}
on a morphism
p:Y\rightarrow X
is the datum of the structure of a torsor under
\mathscr{G}
on the sheaf
U\mapsto\{s:U\rightarrow Y,p\circ s=\Id_{U}\}
of sections of
p
. We have the following lemma about the existence of affine models of smooth schemes.

Lemma 2.4

(Jouanolou–Thomason,
[
Wei89
, Proposition 4.4]
)
.

Let
X
be a separated
k
-scheme of finite type. Then there exists a morphism
\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
of
k
-schemes possessing the following properties.

•

The
k
-scheme
\widetilde{X}
is affine.

•

The morphism
\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
is a Zariski-locally trivial torsor under a vector bundle on
X
.

We call such an
X
-scheme
\widetilde{X}
a
Jouanolou device
for
X
.

Given a Jouanolou device
\widetilde{X}
of
X
,
\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
is Zariski-locally on
X
isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}_{X}^{n}\rightarrow X
; in particular, it is a smooth
X
-scheme. Hence if
X
is a smooth
k
-scheme, then
\widetilde{X}
is a smooth
k
-scheme and the structure map
\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
is an
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy equivalence. We make two additional observations about this lemma.

•

Base change does
not
preserve Jouanolou devices. That is, given a Jouanolou device
p:\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
of an object
X
of
\mathsf{Sm}_{k}
and a
k
-scheme morphism
f:Y\rightarrow X
where
Y
is an object of
\mathsf{Sm}_{k}
, form the following fibre product:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

Then
q:\widetilde{Y}\rightarrow Y
is not a Jouanolou device in general. In fact, the only obstacle is that
\widetilde{Y}
is not necessarily an affine
k
-scheme. Note however that if
f
is an affine morphism, then so is
g
by base change: since
\widetilde{X}
is affine, this implies that
\widetilde{Y}
is indeed affine in this case. We summarise this situation by saying that Jouanolou devices are preserved by base change by an affine morphism.

•

Assume
X
is a smooth affine
k
-scheme in Lemma
2.4
. Let
p:\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
be a Jouanolou device of
X
. Then
p
has a section: there exists a morphism
s:X\rightarrow\widetilde{X}
of
k
-schemes such that
p\circ s=\Id_{X}
. Indeed, since
X
is affine, given a quasi-coherent
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-module
\mathscr{E}
, any Zariski-locally torsor under
\mathscr{E}
is trivial by
[
Gro67
, Proposition 16.5.16]
(the crucial input is the vanishing of the Zariski sheaf cohomology group
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{Zar}}^{1}(X,\mathscr{E})
which follows from the fact that
X
is affine). Thus the sheaf of sections of
p
has a global section
s
as indicated previously (see
[
Sta23
,
Tag 02FP
]
).

As a result of combining these items, we obtain the following corollary:

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
Y
and
X
be smooth
k
-schemes such that
Y
is an affine
k
-scheme and let
\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
be a Jouanolou device. Then any morphism
Y\rightarrow X
of
k
-schemes factors through
\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
into a morphism
Y\rightarrow\widetilde{X}
. In other words, the map
\Hom_{\mathsf{Sm}_{k}}(Y,\widetilde{X})\rightarrow\Hom_{\mathsf{Sm}_{k}}(Y,X)
induced by the morphism
\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
is surjective.

Proof.

Note that the morphism
Y\rightarrow X
is automatically affine as
Y
is an affine
k
-scheme and
X
is a separated
k
-scheme (
[
Sta23
,
Tag 01SG
, (2)]
). Let
\widetilde{Y}
be the fibre product
\widetilde{Y}=Y\times_{X}\widetilde{X}
; then the morphism
\widetilde{Y}\rightarrow Y
is a Jouanolou device for
Y
and since
Y
is affine, it has a section
s:Y\rightarrow\widetilde{Y}
; composing with the structural morphism
\widetilde{Y}\rightarrow\widetilde{X}
of the fibre product yields a factorisation of the map
Y\rightarrow X
.
∎

Naive
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy.

Of particular interest to us, among
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopic morphisms, will be
naively

\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopic morphisms:

Definition 2.6

(naive
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy)
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
and
\mathscr{Y}
be
k
-spaces and let
f,g:\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
be
k
-morphisms. We say that
f
and
g
are
naively
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopic
when there exists a map
H:\mathscr{X}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
of
k
-spaces such that
H(\text{--},0)=f
and
H(\text{--},1)=g
; such an
H
is a naive
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy between
f
and
g
. The relation “being naively
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopic” generates an equivalence relation which we denote by
\sim_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
and the quotient of
\Hom_{\mathsf{Spc}_{k}}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y})
by
\sim_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
is denoted by
\Hom_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y})
; we then have a quotient map
\Hom_{\mathsf{Spc}_{k}}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y})\rightarrow\Hom_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y})
.

The relation
\sim_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
is compatible with composition in the sense that if
f\sim_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}f^{\prime}
, then
g\circ f\sim_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}g\circ f^{\prime}
and
f\circ h\sim_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}f^{\prime}\circ h
for any maps
g
and
h
. It follows that we have a category
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(k)
of
k
-spaces up to
naive

\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy where for any
k
-spaces
\mathscr{X}
and
\mathscr{Y}
,
\Hom_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(k)}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y})=\Hom_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y})
. There is an obvious full functor
\mathsf{Spc}_{k}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(k)
and any functor that sends naively
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-equivalent morphisms to equal morphisms factors uniquely through this functor.

Let
\mathscr{X}
and
\mathscr{Y}
be
k
-spaces. For
t\in k
, let
i_{t}:\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{X}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}
denote the inclusion of the
k
-rational point
t
in
\mathbb{A}^{1}
. Then a morphism
H:\mathscr{X}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
is a naive
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy between
f
and
g
if
f=i_{0}^{*}H=H\circ i_{0}
and
g=i_{1}^{*}H=H\circ i_{1}
. Letting
p:\mathscr{X}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}\rightarrow\mathscr{X}
denote the first projection, we have that
p\circ i_{0}=p\circ i_{1}
hence
i_{0}^{*}\circ p^{*}=i_{1}^{*}\circ p^{*}
as maps between hom-sets in
\mathcal{H}(k)
. On the other hand,
p
is an
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-weak equivalence by definition of
\mathcal{H}(k)
, hence
p^{*}
is a bijection. It follows that
i_{0}^{*}=i_{1}^{*}
, hence
f=g
in
\mathcal{H}(k)
. As a result, the obvious functor
\mathsf{Spc}_{k}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}(k)
factors (uniquely) through the functor
\mathsf{Spc}_{k}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(k)
. For general
\mathscr{X}
and
\mathscr{Y}
, the map
\Hom_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y})\rightarrow[\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
induced by the functor
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(k)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}(k)
is not injective nor surjective. For example, the main result of
[
Caz12
]
shows that this map is not surjective if
\mathscr{X}=\mathscr{Y}=\mathbb{P}^{1}
. An crucial difference between
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(k)
and
\mathcal{H}(k)
is that in the first category, any morphism from
\mathscr{X}
to
\mathscr{Y}
comes from a morphism of
spaces
from
\mathscr{X}
to
\mathscr{Y}
.

The quadrics
Q_{n}
.

We recall the definition of the quadrics alluded to above:

Definition 2.7
.

Let
n
be a non-negative integer. We set

\begin{array}[]{rcl}Q_{2n+1}&=&\displaystyle\Spec k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n+1}]/\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{i}y_{i}-1\right\rangle\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{2n+2}\\
Q_{2n}&=&\displaystyle\Spec k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n},z]/\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}-z(1-z)\right\rangle\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{2n+1}\end{array}

One readily checks that
Q_{n}
is a smooth affine integral
k
-scheme of Krull dimension
n
for all
n>0
.

Remark
.

In
[
ADF16
]
, a different quadric bears the name
Q_{2n}
, namely the quadric in
\mathbb{A}^{2n+1}=\Spec k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n},z]
defined by the equation
x_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}y_{n}=z(1+z)
. As noted in
[
AF22
]
, it is isomorphic to the quadric that we denote by

via the change of variables

and

. We use this isomorphism when appealing to the results of
[
ADF16
]
.

Let
R
be a
k
-algebra. We identify
Q_{2n}(R)
(respectively
Q_{2n+1}(R)
) with the set of ordered triples
(x,y,z)
(respectively ordered pairs
(x,y)
), where
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
(respectively
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})
and
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n+1})
) are row vectors in
R^{n}
(respectively in
R^{n+1}
) and

is an element of
R
, satisfying

(respectively

); see the first paragraph of
[
AF22
, Subsection 2.1]
(p. 2794). We also occasionally simply denote the elements of
Q_{2n}(R)
(respectively

) as
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n},z)\in R^{2n+1}
(respectively
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n+1})\in R^{2n+2}

(respectively

(respectively
Q_{2n+1}

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
n
be a non-negative integer. Then the map

-module and

.
∎

\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy sheaves and
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connectedness.

Recall that, in our context, an
h
-cogroup is a cogroup in the pointed
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy category
\mathcal{H}_{\bullet}(k)
. The simplicial spheres
\mathrm{S}^{i}
for
i\geqslant 1
have an
h
-cogroup structure in simplicial sets which yields an
h
-cogroup structure on
\mathrm{S}^{i}\wedge(\mathscr{U},*)
for any pointed space
(\mathscr{U},*)
; this
h
-cogroup is cocommutative if
i\geqslant 2
, owing to the corresponding fact for
\mathrm{S}^{i}
. Observe that by the loop-suspension adjunction, we deduce that
\Omega(\mathscr{X},x)
is an
h
-group for any pointed space
(\mathscr{X},x)
and
\Omega^{i}(\mathscr{X},x)
is a commutative
h
-group if
i\geqslant 2
. For more details on
h
-cogroups in the context of general model categories, we refer to
[
Hov99
]
.

In particular, given an integer
i\geqslant 1
and a pointed space
(\mathscr{X},x)
, we obtain a presheaf of groups on
\mathsf{Sm}_{k}
by the formula
U\mapsto[\mathrm{S}^{i}\wedge(U_{+}),(\mathscr{X},x)]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}
. By definition, the Nisnevich sheafification of this presheaf is the
i
-th
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy sheaf of
(\mathscr{X},x)
denoted by
\pi_{i}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathscr{X},x)
or
\pi_{i}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathscr{X})
if no confusion arises from this notation: it is a sheaf of groups for any
i\geqslant 1
and a sheaf of
abelian
groups if
i\geqslant 2
. We also denote by
\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathscr{X},x)
the Nisnevich sheaf associated with the presheaf
U\mapsto[\mathrm{S}^{0}\wedge(U_{+}),(\mathscr{X},x)]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}
of pointed sets and again, we simplify the notation to
\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathscr{X})
if no confusion arises from this notation.

Let
\mathscr{X}
and
\mathscr{Y}
be pointed spaces, let
f:\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
be a map of pointed spaces and let
n
be a non-negative integer. Then
\mathscr{X}
is said to be
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-
n
-connected
(respectively
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected
, respectively
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-simply connected
) if
\mathbf{\pi}_{i}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathscr{X})
is trivial for all
i\leqslant n
(respectively for
i=0
, respectively for all
i\leqslant 1
). Following
[
AF16
, p. 627]
(second-to-last paragraph before Theorem 4.1), we say that
f
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-
n
-connected (respectively
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected, respectively
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-simply connected) if
\pi_{i}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(f):\pi_{i}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathscr{X})\rightarrow\pi_{i}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathscr{Y})
is an isomorphism of sheaves for all
i\leqslant n
(respectively for
i=0
, respectively for all
i\leqslant 1
).

This last definition can also be phrased in terms of the homotopy fibre
\mathscr{F}
of
f
. The key property of homotopy fibres is that the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy sheaves of
\mathscr{F}
fit into a long exact sequence

\cdots\rightarrow\pi_{i+1}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathscr{Y})\rightarrow\pi_{i}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathscr{F})\rightarrow\pi_{i}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathscr{X})\rightarrow\pi_{i}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathscr{Y})\rightarrow\pi_{i-1}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathscr{F})\rightarrow\cdots

\cdots\rightarrow\pi_{1}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathscr{Y})\rightarrow\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathscr{F})\rightarrow\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathscr{X})\rightarrow\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathscr{Y})\rightarrow*

where exactness for sequences of sheaves of pointed sets is defined as usual: a sequence
A\xrightarrow{f}B\xrightarrow{g}C
of pointed maps between pointed sheaves is exact at
B
if
\operatorname{Im}f=g^{-1}(*)
where
*
is the point of
C
, and groups are pointed by their neutral elements. The following lemma is now clear:

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
f:\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
be a pointed map of pointed spaces with homotopy fibre
\mathscr{F}
. Let

\mathrm{K}_{*}^{\mathrm{MW}}(F,L,a)=\mathrm{K}_{*}^{\mathrm{MW}}(F)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[F^{\times}]}\mathbb{Z}[L^{0}].

This is the Milnor–Witt
\mathrm{K}
-theory of

twisted by
(L,a)
. Here it should be noted that the integer

is decorative in the

of twisted Milnor–Witt

-theory, though it is essential to keep track when using canonical isomorphisms such as switching

in the category of
graded
vector bundles (see the paragraph following
[
Fas20
, Definition 1.18]
). Observe also that the choice of

induces an isomorphism
\mathrm{K}_{*}^{\mathrm{MW}}(F)\rightarrow\mathrm{K}_{*}^{\mathrm{MW}}(F,L,a)
given by

. In particular, Milnor–Witt
\mathrm{K}
-theory twisted by the trivial graded line bundle is naturally isomorphic to untwisted Milnor–Witt
\mathrm{K}
-theory. We use this isomorphism to write generic elements of
\mathrm{K}_{*}^{\mathrm{MW}}(F,L,a)
as
\alpha\otimes l
where
\alpha\in\mathrm{K}_{*}^{\mathrm{MW}}(F)
and

.

There is also a global version of Milnor–Witt
\mathrm{K}
-theory: if

-scheme

to
\mathrm{Nis}(X)
if

\mathrm{K}

We make use of this map in the following lemma (with

\mathbb{G}_{m}

the pullback of the parameter

Lemma 2.23
.

Let
X
be a smooth

\mathrm{H}^{i}(X\times\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbf{K}_{j}^{\mathrm{MW}})=p_{X}^{*}\mathrm{H}^{i}(X,\mathbf{K}_{j}^{\mathrm{MW}})\oplus p_{X}^{*}\mathrm{H}^{i}(X,\mathbf{K}_{j-1}^{\mathrm{MW}})[t_{X}]

as a
\mathrm{K}_{0}^{\mathrm{MW}}(k)
-module. Moreover, the morphism
\iota:X\rightarrow X\times\mathbb{G}_{m}
given by the invertible global section

induces the splitting above.

.

Observe that

so that

is injective. Moreover, the last statement means that the map

satisfies

Proof.

This is

In particular, if

Now for any pair

Lemma 2.24
.

Let
i\geqslant 2
and let
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
X
be a pointed smooth
k
-scheme. The map
[X\wedge\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}\rightarrow[X\times\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}
factors through the inclusion of
p_{X}^{*}\mathrm{H}^{i}(X,\mathbf{K}_{j-1}^{\mathrm{MW}})[t_{X}]
into an isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{i}(X\wedge\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbf{K}_{j}^{\mathrm{MW}})\xrightarrow{\cong}p_{X}^{*}\mathrm{H}^{i}(X,\mathbf{K}_{j-1}^{\mathrm{MW}})[t_{X}]

of
\mathrm{K}_{0}^{\mathrm{MW}}(k)
-modules.

Proof.

Let
Y
be a pointed smooth
k
-scheme. The cofibre sequence
X\vee Y\rightarrow X\times Y\rightarrow X\wedge Y
induces an exact sequence

[\Sigma(X\times Y),\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}\rightarrow[\Sigma(X\vee Y),\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}\rightarrow[X\wedge Y,\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}\rightarrow[X\times Y,\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}\rightarrow[X\vee Y,\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}

of
\mathrm{K}_{0}^{\mathrm{MW}}(k)
-modules. We have a pointed
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-weak equivalence
\Omega\mathrm{K}_{i+1,j}\simeq\mathrm{K}_{i,j}
, hence the last three terms in the above sequence may be read as

[\Sigma(X\wedge Y),\mathrm{K}_{i+1,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}\rightarrow[\Sigma(X\times Y),\mathrm{K}_{i+1,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}\rightarrow[\Sigma(X\vee Y),\mathrm{K}_{i+1,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}

by adjunction. As noted previously when defining
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy sheaves, the suspension
\Sigma(X\times Y)
is an
h
-cogroup in a natural way. The projection morphisms
p_{Z}:X\times Y\rightarrow Z
and inclusions
i_{Z}:Z\rightarrow X\vee Y
for
Z=X
and
Z=Y
yield by composition and after suspension morphisms

\Sigma(X\times Y)\xrightarrow{\Sigma p_{Z}}\Sigma Z\xrightarrow{\Sigma i_{Z}}\Sigma(X\vee Y).

We may now use the
h
-cogroup structure on
\Sigma(X\times Y)
to multiply these morphisms, yielding a morphism
\Sigma(i_{X}\circ p_{X})\cdot\Sigma(i_{Y}\circ p_{Y}):\Sigma(X\times Y)\rightarrow\Sigma(X\vee Y)
. By construction, this map splits the suspension
\Sigma(X\vee Y)\rightarrow\Sigma(X\times Y)
of the inclusion
X\vee Y\rightarrow X\times Y
(see also
[
Mor12
, p. 72]
). Hence the morphism

[X\times Y,\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}\cong[\Sigma(X\times Y),\mathrm{K}_{i+1,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}\rightarrow[\Sigma(X\vee Y),\mathrm{K}_{i+1,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}\cong[X\vee Y,\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}

splits. Similarly and more directly, the morphism
[\Sigma(X\times Y),\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}\rightarrow[\Sigma(X\vee Y),\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}
splits. In particular, it is surjective hence the morphism
[\Sigma(X\vee Y),\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}\rightarrow[X\wedge Y,\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}
is the zero morphism so that the morphism
[X\wedge Y,\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}\rightarrow[X\times Y,\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}
is injective.

Assume now that
Y=\mathbb{G}_{m}
—in particular,
\mathrm{cd}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathbb{G}_{m})\leqslant\dim(\mathbb{G}_{m})=1
so that
\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbf{K}_{j+1}^{\mathrm{MW}})=0
. Then

[X\vee\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}\xrightarrow{\cong}[X,\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}\oplus[\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}

with
[\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}\cong\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbf{K}_{j}^{\mathrm{MW}})=0
by assumption and with the map
[X\vee\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}\rightarrow[X,\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}
induced by the structural map
X\rightarrow X\vee\mathbb{G}_{m}
whose composition with the cofibration
X\vee\mathbb{G}_{m}\rightarrow X\times\mathbb{G}_{m}
is the map
\iota
of Lemma
2.23
. Hence the sequence

0\rightarrow[X\wedge\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}\rightarrow[X\times\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}\xrightarrow{\iota^{*}}[X,\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}\rightarrow 0

is split-exact with
p_{X}^{*}:[X,\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}\rightarrow[X\times\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathrm{K}_{i,j}]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}
splitting
\iota_{X}^{*}
again by Lemma
2.23
. The lemma now follows directly from the cited lemma.
∎

The
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-Brouwer degree and computations.

Morel’s
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-Brouwer degree

[
Mor12
, Corollary 6.43]
may be viewed an isomorphism

\deg^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}:\Hom_{\mathcal{SH}(k)}(\Sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\infty}\mathrm{S}^{0},\Sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\infty}\mathrm{S}^{0})\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathrm{K}_{0}^{\mathrm{MW}}(k)

of rings. Thus if a pointed space
\mathscr{X}
is such that
\Sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\infty}\mathscr{X}\simeq\Sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\infty}\mathrm{S}^{0}
in
\mathcal{SH}(k)
—we say that
\mathscr{X}
has the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-stable
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy type of the unit object—, then the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-Brouwer degree of an element of
\Hom_{\mathcal{H}_{\bullet}(k)}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{X})
makes sense: by definition, it is the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-Brouwer degree of the image of this element in
\Hom_{\mathcal{SH}(k)}(\Sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\infty}\mathscr{X},\Sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\infty}\mathscr{X})\simeq\Hom_{\mathcal{SH}(k)}(\Sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\infty}\mathrm{S}^{0},\Sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\infty}\mathrm{S}^{0})
(the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-Brouwer degree does not depend on the choice of the isomorphism
\Sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\infty}\mathscr{X}\simeq\Sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\infty}\mathrm{S}^{0}
).

Let
n\geqslant 1
be an integer. The
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy type of
\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\setminus 0
was described in Example
2.3
shows that
\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\setminus 0
has the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-stable
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy type of the unit object so that the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-Brouwer degree of an endomorphism of
\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\setminus 0
makes sense. In fact,
[
Mor12
, Corollary 6.43]
yields an isomorphism
[\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\setminus 0,\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\setminus 0]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}\cong\mathrm{K}_{0}^{\mathrm{MW}}(k)
of rings: this isomorphism is induced by the functor
\Sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\infty}
, which provides a bijection
[\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\setminus 0,\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\setminus 0]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\Hom_{\mathcal{SH}(k)}(\Sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\infty}\mathrm{S}^{0},\Sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\infty}\mathrm{S}^{0})
, and by
\deg^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
. The next lemma gives a concrete representative of elements of the type
\langle a\rangle\in\mathrm{K}_{0}^{\mathrm{MW}}(k)
in
[\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\setminus 0,\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\setminus 0]_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\bullet}
.

Lemma 2.25
.

Let
n\geqslant 1
. Then the map of schemes
f_{a}^{n}:\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\setminus 0\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\setminus 0
defined by

f_{a}^{n}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n+1})=(ax_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n+1})

satisfies
\deg^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(f_{a}^{n})=\langle a\rangle
for any
a\in k^{\times}
.

Proof.

Note that
f_{a}^{n}
clearly defines a pointed endomorphism of
\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\setminus 0
. Consider the morphism
g:\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n+1}
sending
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n+1})
to
(ay_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n+1})
. It induces a finite morphism
u:\mathbb{P}^{n+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
, where
\mathbb{A}^{n+1}
is embedded into
\mathbb{P}^{n+1}=\Proj k[y_{0},\ldots,y_{n+1}]
by
D(y_{0})
, such that
u([y_{0}:y_{1}:\cdots:y_{n+1}])=[y_{0}:ay_{1}:y_{2}:\cdots:y_{n+1}]
. Thus by
[
KW19
, Proposition 15]
, the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-Brouwer degree of the morphism
\overline{g}:\mathbb{P}^{n+1}/\mathbb{P}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n+1}/\mathbb{P}^{n}
is the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-local Brouwer degree at
0
of the morphism
g
. Using for instance the formula for this
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-local Brouwer degree derived from
[
KW19
, Main theorem, Lemma 9]
, we see that
\deg^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\overline{g}=\langle a\rangle
(the matrix
(\frac{\partial g_{i}}{\partial x_{j}})_{i,j}
is diagonal with the first coefficient equal to
a
and the others to
1
).

On the other hand, there is a pushout square of spaces
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\setminus 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}^{n+1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}^{n+1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\setminus 0}

since the left vertical and top horizontal arrows are cofibrations. There is an induced
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-equivalence
\Sigma\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\setminus 0\sim\mathbb{A}^{n+1}/\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\setminus 0
. Moreover, the inclusion
\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
induces an
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-equivalence
\mathbb{A}^{n+1}/\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\setminus 0\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n+1}/(\mathbb{P}^{n+1}\setminus 0)
by the purity theorem
[
MV99
, Theorem 2.23, §3, p. 115]
. Moreover, the collapse map
\mathbb{P}^{n+1}/\mathbb{P}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n+1}\setminus(\mathbb{P}^{n+1}\setminus 0)
(collapsing
\mathbb{P}^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n+1}\setminus 0
to a point) is an isomorphism as is observed following the proof of
[
KW19
, Lemma 10]
. Thus there is an
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-weak equivalence
\Sigma\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\setminus 0\simeq\mathbb{P}^{n+1}/\mathbb{P}^{n}
and unwinding the definitions, we see that
\overline{g}=\Sigma f_{a}
. Since the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-Brouwer degree is invariant under simplicial suspension by definition, this establishes the claim.
∎

Similarly, given an integer

.

Let

. Then the endomorphism

Recollections on the Rost–Schmid complex.

We still use the notions and notations of
[
Fas20
]
, including the functor
D
introduced in
[
Fas20
, Subsection 1.3]
: this functor sends a vector bundle
V
over a scheme
X
to the pair
D(V)=(\det V,\rk V)
where
\det V
is the top exterior power
\bigwedge^{\rk V}V
of
V
.

Recall from
[
Fas20
, Subsection 2.1]
the Rost–Schmid complex which computes the Chow–Witt groups of a smooth
k
-scheme twisted by a (graded) line bundle. The fundamental building block is Milnor–Witt
\mathrm{K}
-theory twisted by a graded line bundle
\mathrm{K}_{*}^{\mathrm{MW}}(F,L,a)
. We then denote the cohomological Rost–Schmid complex associated with an object
X
of
\mathsf{Sm}_{k}
, a graded line bundle
(L,a)
over
X
and an integer
i
by
C_{\mathrm{RS}}(X,i,L,a)
: by definition,
\widetilde{\mathrm{CH}}^{i}(X,L,a)=\mathrm{H}^{i}(C_{\mathrm{RS}}(X,i,L,a))
. In degree
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,
C_{\mathrm{RS}}(X,i,L,a)^{j}
is given by

C_{\mathrm{RS}}(X,i,L,a)^{j}=\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(j)}}\mathrm{K}_{i-j}^{\mathrm{MW}}(k(x),\Lambda_{x}^{X}\otimes_{k(x)}(L(x),a)).

Here
X^{(j)}
denotes the set of points of codimension
j
of
X
,
\Lambda_{x}^{X}=D(\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2})^{-1}
for
x\in X^{(j)}
(
\Lambda_{x}^{X}
is thus graded by
j
) where
\mathfrak{m}_{x}
is the maximal ideal of the local ring
\mathscr{O}_{X,x}
, and
L(x)=L_{x}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X,x}}k(x)
. We omit
(L,a)
from the notation if
(L,a)
is the trivial graded line bundle; we do the same for
X
in
\Lambda_{x}^{X}
unless there is possible confusion.

The complex
C_{\mathrm{RS}}(X,i,L,a)
computes the cohomology of
X
with values in the sheaf
\mathbf{K}_{i}^{\mathrm{MW}}(X,L,a)
on
\mathrm{Nis}(X)
since it is a flasque resolution of this sheaf. Hence we may set

\mathrm{H}^{*}(X,\mathbf{K}_{i}^{\mathrm{MW}}(X,L,a))=\mathrm{H}^{*}(C_{\mathrm{RS}}(X,i,L,a)).

For instance,
\mathrm{H}^{n}(X,\mathbf{K}_{n}^{\mathrm{MW}}(X,L,a))=\widetilde{\mathrm{CH}}^{n}(X,L,a)
.

3.  Unimodular rows

Recall the definition of unimodular rows:

Definition 3.1

(unimodular row)
.

Let
A
be a ring and let
n
be a non-negative integer. A
unimodular row of length
n
with values in
A
is an
n
-tuple
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in A^{n}
such that the ideal generated by the
a_{i}
’s is
A
.

The set of unimodular rows of length
n
with values in
A
is denoted by
\mathrm{Um}_{n}(A)
; it defines a functor from the category of rings to the category of sets in an obvious way. Unimodular rows may be thought of in (at least) two ways.

•

If
A
is a ring, an
A
-morphism
f:A^{n}\rightarrow A
corresponds to the choice of an
n
-tuple
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
of elements of
A
; the morphism
f
is surjective if, and only if, the
n
-tuple
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
is a unimodular row.

•

If
A
is a
k
-algebra, an
n
-tuple of elements of
A
corresponds to the choice of a
k
-morphism
X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n}
where
X=\Spec A
; the
n
-tuple is a unimodular row if, and only if, the morphism
X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n}
factors through the open subset
\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0
.

Let
A
be a ring and let
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
be an element of
\mathrm{Um}_{n}(A)
. Then the
A
-module homomorphism
f:A^{n}\rightarrow A
naturally associated with
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
is surjective: since
A
is a projective
A
-module, this implies that
f
has a splitting, that is, there exists an
A
-module homomorphism
s:A\rightarrow A^{n}
with
f\circ s=\Id_{A}
. The image
s(1)
of

by

is a row
(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})
of elements of
A
such that
a_{1}\beta_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}\beta_{n}=f\circ s(1)=1
. This explains the following definition:

Definition 3.2
.

A
splitting
of
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
is a row
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})
of elements

such that
\sum a_{i}b_{i}=1
.

Remark
.

This notion of splitting allows one to produce a more explicit proof that the map
p_{*}
from Lemma

is surjective. Indeed, let
n
be a positive integer and let

be a morphism of

-schemes; we have to show that there exists a map

such that

. Then

corresponds to a unimodular row
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
: if
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})
is a splitting of

, we obtain an element
(a,b)
of

, thus a morphism

which by construction satisfies

.

Let
A
be a ring. The general linear group
\GL_{n}(A)
acts naturally on the right on
\mathrm{Um}_{n}(A)
, the action being induced by the natural action of
\GL_{n}(A)
on

; thus, any subgroup of

acts on

. It is in particular the case of the subgroup of

, denoted by
\mathrm{E}_{n}(A)
and defined as the subgroup of

generated by the set of matrices of the form

where
\mathrm{I}_{n}
is the identity matrix,

is an element of

and

(

) is the matrix whose entries are

except the entry in the

-th row and

-th column which is equal to

. There exists a group structure on the orbit set
\mathrm{Um}_{n}(A)/\mathrm{E}_{n}(A)
under suitable dimension hypotheses of

which van der Kallen described in terms of weak Mennicke symbols:

Definition 3.3

(
[
Kal89
, 3.2 Notations]
)
.

A
weak Mennicke symbol
on
\mathrm{Um}_{n}(A)
is a map
\phi:\mathrm{Um}_{n}(A)\rightarrow G
from
\mathrm{Um}_{n}(A)
to a group
G
(denoted multiplicatively) which satisfies the following identities:

1.

if
v
is a unimodular row of length
n
and if
E
is an elementary matrix, then
\phi(v)=\phi(vE)
;

2.

if
u=(q,a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})
and
v=(1+q,a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})
are unimodular rows of length
n
with values in
A
and if

mod
\langle a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}\rangle

.

There exists a universal weak Mennicke symbol
\mathrm{wms}:\mathrm{Um}_{n}(A)\rightarrow\mathrm{WMS}_{n}(A)
which induces a map

\overline{\mathrm{wms}_{n}}:\mathrm{Um}_{n}(A)/\mathrm{E}_{n}(A)\rightarrow\mathrm{WMS}_{n}(A)

from the set of
\mathrm{E}_{n}(A)
-orbits by virtue of the first item. The observation of van der Kallen is that

is a bijection if the stable dimension of

is sufficiently small. We briefly mentioned the notion of stable dimension in the introduction. In the sequel, we denote the stable

by

. Its definition is as follows: given a ring

, denote by

the following assertion:

: For any
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r+1})\in\mathrm{Um}_{r+1}(A)
, there exist
b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\in A
such that the row
(a_{1}+b_{1}a_{r+1},\ldots,a_{r}+b_{r}a_{r+1})
is unimodular.

Then
\sr(A)
is the smallest integer

such that

satisfies
(\mathrm{DS})_{r}
and
\infty
otherwise. It follows from
[
Bas75
, Proposition 1]
that if

, then

satisfies
(\mathrm{DS})_{r}
for any

. Finally,
\sdim(A)
is defined by the equality
\sr(A)=\sdim(A)+1
(
[

, Introduction, 1.2]
), hence

if

. The key property for us is the following:

Example 3.4
.

Let

be a Noetherian ring of Krull dimension

. Then the inequality

is satisfied according to
[
Bas75
, Théorème 1]
.

Theorem 3.5

(van der Kallen,
[
Kal89
, Theorem 3.6]
)
.

Let

be a ring of stable dimension

and let

be an integer such that

. Then the map
\overline{\mathrm{wms}_{n}}:\mathrm{Um}_{n}(A)/\mathrm{E}_{n}(A)\rightarrow\mathrm{WMS}_{n}(A)
is a bijection and the group

is abelian.

Let

. In
[
Kal02
]
, several relations that may be satisfied by a map

from

to a group

(MS1)

If

and

\phi(vE)=\phi(v)
.

(MS3)

If

If

\langle a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}\rangle

states that if

and that this group is abelian. On the other hand, (MS3) is closely linked to the well-known

Proposition 3.6

(Mennicke–Newman lemma)

Let

be a commutative ring of stable dimension

be an integer such that

and

.

This is a generalised version of

The proof is divided in three steps.

(1) Observe that since

(respectively of

(2) We can then write

\beta_{i}\in A
. Replacing

(respectively

(3) Finally assume that

For any commutative ring

, since the relation (MS4) is satisfied in

(

4.  Cohomotopy groups

The motivic Borsuk group law

We now turn to the cohomotopy group structure on
[U,\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus 0]_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
. Let
\mathscr{X}
be an pointed space. Let
\nabla:\mathscr{X}\vee\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{X}
be the fold map and let
\Delta_{U}:U\rightarrow U\times U
be the diagonal map. The idea of Borsuk is the following. Let
f
and
g
be morphisms from
U
to
\mathscr{X}
. Then we are in the following situation.
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{X}\vee\mathscr{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U\times U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{X}\times\mathscr{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nabla}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta_{U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f\times g}

If we wished to define a sum of
f
and

(f\times g)\circ\Delta_{U}
factors uniquely (up to homotopy) through
\mathscr{X}\vee\mathscr{X}

\overline{(f,g)}:U\rightarrow\mathscr{X}\vee\mathscr{X}
(up to homotopy) and we may want to define the sum of

and

as

. Unfortunately, in general, there does not exist a lift

and, if it exists, it is not necessarily unique (even up to homotopy). However, under suitable
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-cohomological dimension and
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connectedness assumptions on

and
\mathscr{X}
respectively (and a technical assumption on

which will be of no consequence to the material of this paper), there indeed exists a lift and it is unique.

Lemma 5.8
.

The map

In other words, the diagram

Proof.

By definition of the map

and
(0,\ldots,0,1)

Remark
.

The above result essentially sums up to the observation that the orbit

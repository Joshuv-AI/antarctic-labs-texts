Semiclassical resolvent bounds for short range L ∞ L^{\infty} potentials with singularities at the origin
Jacob Shapiro

Abstract
. We consider, for h , E > 0 h,E>0 , resolvent estimates for the semiclassical Schrödinger operator − h 2 ​ Δ + V − E -h^{2}\Delta+V-E . Near infinity, the potential takes the form V = V L + V S V=V_{L}+V_{S} , where V L V_{L} is a long range potential which is Lipschitz with respect to the radial variable, while V S = O ⁡ ( | x | − 1 ​ ( log ⁡ | x | ) − ρ ) V_{S}=O(|x|^{-1}(\log|x|)^{-\rho}) for some ρ > 1 \rho>1 . Near the origin, | V | |V| may behave like | x | − β |x|^{-\beta} , provided 0 ≤ β < 2 ​ ( 3 − 1 ) 0\leq\beta<2(\sqrt{3}-1) . We find that, for any ρ ~ > 1 \tilde{\rho}>1 , there are C , h 0 > 0 C,\,h_{0}>0 such that we have a resolvent bound of the form exp ⁡ ( C ​ h − 2 ​ ( log ⁡ ( h − 1 ) ) 1 + ρ ~ ) \exp(Ch^{-2}(\log(h^{-1}))^{1+\tilde{\rho}}) for all h ∈ ( 0 , h 0 ] h\in(0,h_{0}] . The h h -dependence of the bound improves if V S V_{S} decays at a faster rate toward infinity.



1.
Introduction and statement of results

Let
\Delta\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial^{2}_{j}\leq 0
be the Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. In this article, we study the semiclassical Schrödinger operator with real valued potential,

(1.1)

P=P(h)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=-h^{2}\Delta+V(x):L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\qquad h\in(0,1),\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We use
(r,\theta)=(|x|,x/|x|)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
to denote polar coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. For a function
f
defined on some subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we use the notation
f(r,\theta)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=f(r\theta)
and denote the derivative with respect to the radial variable by
f^{\prime}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\partial_{r}f.

We first describe the conditions we impose on the potential
V
. Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty);[0,1])
be such that
\chi=1
near
[0,1]
while
\chi=0
near
[2,\infty)
. We suppose that

(1.2)

V_{0}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\chi V\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\qquad\text{for some }p\geq 2,\,p>n/2,

has the bound

(1.3)

|V_{0}(r,\theta)|\leq c_{0}r^{-\beta},

for some
c_{0}>0
and some

(1.4)

0\leq\beta<2(\sqrt{3}-1)\approx 1.464.

On the other hand, we suppose
(1-\chi)V
may be decomposed as a
sum of long- and short-range terms:

(1.5)

(1-\chi)V=V_{L}+V_{S},\qquad V_{L},\,V_{S}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

The long-range term
V_{L}
must satisfy, for some
c_{L}>0
and some

(1.6)

y:[1,\infty)\to[0,1],\qquad\lim_{r\to\infty}y(r)=0,

that

(1.7)

V_{L}(r,\theta)\mathbf{1}_{r\geq 1}\leq c_{L}y(r),

where
\mathbf{1}_{r\geq 1}
denotes the characteristic function of
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|=r\geq 1\}
. We also require that there is a function
V^{\prime}_{L}\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
such that, for each
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, the function
(0,\infty)\ni r\mapsto V_{L}(r,\theta)
has distributional derivative equal to
r\mapsto V_{L}^{\prime}(r,\theta)
, and

(1.8)

V_{L}^{\prime}(r,\theta)\mathbf{1}_{r\geq 1}\leq c_{L}r^{-1}m_{L}(r).

where
m_{L}(r):[1,\infty)\to(0,1]
has the properties

(1.9)

\lim_{r\to\infty}m_{L}(r)=0,\qquad r^{-1}m_{L}(r)\in L^{1}[1,\infty).

A typical example of the function
m_{L}
is
m_{L}(r)=(\log r+1)^{-\rho}
for some
\rho>1.

As for the short-range term
V_{S}
, we require

(1.10)

|V_{S}(r,\theta)|\mathbf{1}_{r\geq 1}\leq c_{S}m_{S}(r)r^{-1-\delta},

for some
c_{S}>0
and
0\leq\delta\leq 1
. Depending on the value of
\delta
,
m_{S}:[1,\infty)\to[0,1]
should satisfy

(1.11)

\displaystyle r^{-1}m^{2}_{S}(r)\in L^{1}[1,\infty)

\displaystyle\delta=1,

\displaystyle m_{S}(r)=1

\displaystyle 0<\delta<1,

\displaystyle m_{S}(r)=(\log r+1)^{-\rho}\text{ for some $\rho>1$}

\displaystyle\delta=0.

The properties (
1.2
) and (
1.5
) imply
V\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
for some
p\geq 2,\,p>n/2
. Therefore, by
[
Ne64
, Theorem 8]
,
P
is self-adjoint
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when equipped with domain the Sobolev space
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Thus the resolvent
(P-z)^{-1}
is bounded
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
. Our main result is the following limiting absorption resolvent estimate.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
. Fix
s>1/2
and
[E_{\min},E_{\max}]\subseteq(0,\infty)
. Suppose
V
satisfies properties (
1.2
) through (
1.11
). Define

(1.12)

g^{\pm}_{s}(h,\varepsilon)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}(P(h)-E\pm i\varepsilon)^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\qquad\varepsilon,\,h>0,

where
\langle x\rangle=\langle r\rangle\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(1+r^{2})^{1/2}
.

If
\delta=1
, there exist
C>0
and
h_{\delta}\in(0,1)
independent of
\varepsilon
and
h
so that

(1.13)

g^{\pm}_{s}(h,\varepsilon)\leq\exp\big(Ch^{-\frac{4}{3}}\log(h^{-1})\big),\qquad E\in[E_{\min},E_{\max}],\,h\in(0,h_{\delta}],\,\varepsilon>0.

If
0<\delta<1
, then for any
\epsilon>0
, there exist
C>0
and
h_{\delta}\in(0,1)
independent of
\varepsilon
and
h
so that

(1.14)

g^{\pm}_{s}(h,\varepsilon)\leq\exp\big(Ch^{-\frac{2\delta+2}{2\delta+1}-\epsilon}),\qquad E\in[E_{\min},E_{\max}],\,h\in(0,h_{\delta}],\,\varepsilon>0.

Finally, if
\delta=0
, then for any
\tilde{\rho}\in(1,\rho]
, there exist
C>0
and
h_{\delta}\in(0,1)
independent of
\varepsilon
and
h
so that

(1.15)

g^{\pm}_{s}(h,\varepsilon)\leq\exp\big(Ch^{-2}(\log(h^{-1}))^{1+\tilde{\rho}}\big),\qquad E\in[E_{\min},E_{\max}],\,h\in(0,h_{\delta}],\,\varepsilon>0.

Remark 1.2
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
in fact establishes a more complicated but slightly improved version of (
1.14
). For any
\epsilon>0
, there exist
C>0
and
h_{\delta}\in(0,1)
independent of
\varepsilon
and
h
so that

g^{\pm}_{s}(h,\varepsilon)\leq\exp\big(Ch^{-\frac{4}{3}-\frac{2(1-\delta)+\lambda^{-1}}{3(1+2\delta-\lambda^{-1})}}(\log(h^{-1}))^{1+\epsilon}\big),\qquad E\in[E_{\min},E_{\max}],\,h\in(0,h_{\delta}],\,\varepsilon>0,

where
\lambda=\log(\log(h^{-1}))
.

Remark 1.3
.

The condition
\tilde{\rho}\in(1,\rho]
is needed for technical reasons in the proof of (
1.15
). However, it is clear that once (
1.15
) holds for some
\tilde{\rho}\in(1,\rho]
, it holds for all
\tilde{\rho}>\rho
too (with the same constants
C
and
h_{\delta}
).

Theorem
1.1
improves upon recent work on resolvent estimates in low regularity in several ways. When
\delta=1
, the bound (
1.13
) was previously proved in
[
GaSh22b
]
if
n\geq 3
,
V_{S}=O((r+1)^{-1}m_{S}(r))
, and
V_{L}=0
. Second, when
0<\delta<1
, it was established in
[
Vo20b
]
that, if
n\geq 3
,
V_{S}=O((r+1)^{-1-\delta})
, and
V_{L}^{\prime}(r,\theta)=O((r+1)^{-1-\gamma})
for some
\gamma>0
, then

g^{\pm}_{s}(h,\varepsilon)\leq\exp\big(Ch^{-\frac{2}{3}-\max\big\{\frac{7}{3\delta},\frac{4}{3\gamma}\big\}}(\log(h^{-1}))^{\max\big\{\frac{1}{\delta},\frac{1}{\gamma}\big\}}\big).

Thus, the novelties of Theorem
1.1
are that it gives resolvent bounds for more types of decay conditions on
V_{L}
and
V_{S}
, improves those bounds in several cases, allows
V
to be singular as
r\to 0
, and includes the dimension two case.

Furthermore, Theorem
1.1
warrants comparison with the resolvent bounds obtained in
[
Vo21
,
Vo22
]
for short-range,
radially symmetric

L^{\infty}
potentials
V
:

g^{\pm}_{s}(h,\varepsilon)\leq\begin{cases}\exp\big(Ch^{\frac{\delta+1}{\delta}}(\log(h^{-1}))^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\big)&V=O((r+1)^{-1-\delta}),\,\delta>3,\\
\exp\big(Ch^{-\frac{4}{3}}\big)&V=O((r+1)^{-1-\delta}),\,1<\delta\leq 3,\\
\exp\big(Ch^{-\frac{2\delta+2}{2\delta+1}}\big(\log(h^{-1})\big)^{\frac{\delta+2}{2\delta+1}}\big)&V=O((r+1)^{-1-\delta}),\,0<\delta\leq 1,\\
\exp\big(Ch^{-2}\big)&V=O((r+1)^{-1}\log(r+2)^{-\rho}),\,\rho>1.\end{cases}

Thus, another way to interpret how Theorem
1.1
extends the previous literature is that it shows arbitrary short-range potentials have resolvent bounds similar to those for short-range radial potentials, though additional losses remain.

Bounds on
g^{\pm}_{s}
are known to hold under various geometric, regularity, and decay assumptions. Burq
[
Bu98
,
Bu02
]
showed
g^{\pm}_{s}\leq e^{Ch^{-1}}
for
V
smooth and decaying sufficiently fast near infinity, and also for more general perturbations of the Laplacian. Cardoso and Vodev
[
CaVo02
]
extended Burq’s estimate to infinite volume Riemannian manifolds which may contain cusps. This exponential behavior is sharp in general, see
[
DDZ15
]
for exponential resolvent lower bounds. On
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
,
g^{\pm}_{s}\leq e^{Ch^{-1}}
still holds if
V
has long-range decay and Lipschitz regularity with respect to the radial variable
[
Da14
,
Sh19
,
Vo20c
,
GaSh22a
,
Ob23
]
. Potentials with singularities near zero are treated in
[
GaSh22a
,
Ob23
]
, and in particular
[
Ob23
]
requires
\partial_{r}V(r,\theta)\mathbf{1}_{r\leq 1}=O(r^{-j-\tilde{\beta}})
, for

(1.16)

0<\tilde{\beta}<4(\sqrt{2}-1)\approx 1.657

and
j=0,\,1
. In one dimension,
g^{\pm}_{s}\leq e^{Ch^{-1}}
if
V
is a finite Borel measure
[
LaSh23
]
.

In contrast, if
V:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
,
n\geq 2
, has purely
L^{\infty}
terms, it is an open problem to determine whether the bounds (
1.13
), (
1.14
), and (
1.15
) have optimal
h
-dependence. Further works on resolvent estimates with little regularity assumed are
[
Vo14
,
RoTa15
,
DadeH16
,
KlVo19
,
Vo19
,
DaSh20
,
Sh20
,
Vo20a
]
.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we establish a global Carleman estimate (
5.1
). This Carleman estimate is the byproduct of patching together what we call the away-from-origin estimate (
5.6
) and the near-origin estimate (
5.7
).

The away-from-origin estimate is an application of the so called energy method, a well established tool for proving semiclassical Carleman estimates. In particular, we combine and update the approaches from
[
GaSh22b
, Section 3]
and
[
Ob23
, Section 3]
, to construct the weight
w(r)
and phase
\varphi(r)
, which are key inputs to the energy method.

Near the origin,
w(r)
should vanish like
r^{2}
, to absorb the singular behavior of both
V
and, in dimension two, the so called effective potential
r^{-2}(n-1)(n-3)
(the latter arising after we separate variables in Section
2
). In our situation,
V_{0}
is only
L^{\infty}
near zero. In the proof of Proposition
4.1
below, this necessitates
8-4\beta-\beta^{2}>0
, which is a stronger requirement than what is needed if
V_{0}
has some radial regularity (see
[
Ob23
, Section 3]
). This is the source of the discrepancy between (
1.4
) and (
1.16
).

Away from the origin, roughly speaking,
w(r)>0
should increase and have
w^{\prime}(r)\sim\langle r\rangle^{-s}
, to furnish the weights appearing in (
1.12
). Meanwhile, the main task of
\varphi^{\prime}(r)>0
is control
V_{S}
without becoming too large, so as to keep
\varphi(r)
bounded. Since
V_{S}
may decay slowly toward infinity, this is a delicate balancing act, and the compromise we strike is that
\varphi^{\prime}(r)
have comparably slow decay for
r>h^{-M}
and suitable
M\gg 1
, see (
3.12
). Our choice of
M
, see (
3.7
), is inspired by
[
Vo20b
, Section 2]
and more refined compared to
[
GaSh22b
, Section 3]
. This is why we can handle decay slower than that treated in
[
GaSh22b
]
.

The near-origin estimate was proved by Obovu
[
Ob23
, Lemma 2.2]
using the Mellin transform, building on an earlier study of radial potentials
[
DGS23
]
. It makes up for the loss in the away-from-origin estimate stemming from the vanishing of
w(r)
as
r\to 0
. We emphasize that this vanishing of
w(r)
is essential in dimension two, even if
V_{0}
is not singular, because in that case the effective potential has an unfavorable sign.

Resolvent bounds like (
1.13
), (
1.14
), and (
1.15
) have application to local energy decay for the wave equation

(1.17)

\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}^{2}-c^{2}(x)\Delta)u(x,t)=0,&(x,t)\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\right)\times(0,\infty),\,n\geq 2,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\\
\partial_{t}u(x,0)=u_{1}(x),\\
u(t,x)=0,&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty),\end{cases}

where
\Omega
is a compact (possibly empty) obstacle with smooth boundary, and the initial data are compactly supported. A general logarithmic decay rate was first proved by Burq
[
Bu98
,
Bu02
]
for
c
smooth. Similar decay was subsequently established for
\Omega=\emptyset
and
c\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};(0,\infty))
bounded from above and below and identically one outside of a compact set
[
Sh18
, Theorem 2]
. See also
[
Be03
,
CaVo04
,
Bo11
,
Mo16
,
Ga19
]
. Since Theorem
1.1
allows the potential to be singular as
r\to 0
, we expect
[
Sh18
, Theorem 2]
extends to
c
which tends to
0
at a point. However, for such a
c
, the low frequency character of the solution to (
1.17
) still needs to be accounted for (see, e.g.,
[
Sh18
, Section 4]
). This question will be taken up elsewhere.

It’s worth mentioning that, in dimension
n\geq 3
, the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
hold for potentials
V
which are “Coulomb-like” near
r=0
, i.e., obeying
V=O(r^{-1})
as
r\to 0
. However, the assumption (
1.2
) does not capture such behavior in dimension two, because in that case
r^{-1}
is not in
L^{2}
near the origin. For Coulomb-like
V
in dimension two, one can use a quadratic form to show that
P=-h^{2}\Delta+V
is self-adjoint with respect to
\mathcal{D}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):Pu\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\}

[
Ch90
, Proposition 1.1]
. However, it seems difficult to use the method of this paper to prove resolvent estimates for
(P,\mathcal{D})
. This is because our Carleman estimate holds only for functions in
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. While it is well known that
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is dense in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
n\geq 2
, it is not evident from the standard result on essential self-adjointness for singular potentials
[
Si73
, Theorem 2]
that a similar class of smooth functions is dense in
(\mathcal{D},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{D}}),
where
\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(\|Pu\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}})^{1/2}
. This is a technical but nevertheless interesting issue that warrants further study.

2.
Preliminary calculations and overview of proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we set the stage for proving Theorem
1.1
by means of the energy method, which has proven to be a dependable tool for establishing resolvent estimates in low regularity (see, e.g.,
[
CaVo02
,
Da14
,
GaSh22b
,
Ob23
]
). Throughout this section, we take
P
as in (
1.1
), and assume the potential
V
obeys (
1.2
) through (
1.11
).

We work in polar coordinates, beginning from the well known identity

r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(-\Delta)r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}=-\partial^{2}_{r}+r^{-2}\Lambda,

where

(2.1)

\Lambda\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}+\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{4}\geq-\frac{1}{4},

and
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
denotes the negative Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Let
\varphi
be a soon-to-be-constructed phase function on
[0,\infty)
, which is locally absolutely continuous, and obeys
\varphi,\,\varphi^{\prime}\geq 0
and
\varphi(0)=0
. Using
\varphi
, we form the conjugated operator

(2.2)

\begin{split}P^{\pm}_{\varphi}(h)&\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=e^{\frac{\varphi}{h}}r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left(P(h)-E\pm i\varepsilon\right)r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{-\frac{\varphi}{h}}\\
&=-h^{2}\partial^{2}_{r}+2h\varphi^{\prime}\partial_{r}+h^{2}r^{-2}\Lambda+V-(\varphi^{\prime})^{2}+h\varphi^{\prime\prime}-E\pm i\varepsilon.\end{split}

For
u\in e^{\varphi/h}r^{(n-1)/2}C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, define a spherical energy functional,

(2.3)

F(r)=F[u](r)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\|hu^{\prime}(r,\cdot)\|^{2}-\langle(h^{2}r^{-2}\Lambda+V_{L}-(\varphi^{\prime})^{2}-E)u(r,\cdot),u(r,\cdot)\rangle,

where
\|\cdot\|
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denote the norm and inner product on
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}_{\theta}^{n-1})
, respectively. For a weight
w\in C^{0}[0,\infty)
that is piecewise
C^{1}
, the distribution
(wF)^{\prime}
on
(0,\infty)
is given by

(2.4)

\begin{split}(wF)^{\prime}&=w^{\prime}F+wF^{\prime}\\
&=w^{\prime}\|hu^{\prime}\|^{2}-w^{\prime}\langle(h^{2}r^{-2}\Lambda+V_{L}-(\varphi^{\prime})^{2}-E)u,u\rangle\\
&-2w\mathop{\rm Re}\langle P^{\pm}_{\varphi}(h)u,u^{\prime}\rangle+2wr^{-1}\langle h^{2}r^{-2}\Lambda u,u\rangle+w((\varphi^{\prime})^{2}-V_{L})^{\prime}\|u\|^{2}+4h^{-1}w\varphi^{\prime}\|hu^{\prime}\|^{2}\\
&\mp 2\varepsilon w\mathop{\rm Im}\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle+2w\mathop{\rm Re}\langle(V_{0}+V_{S}+h\varphi^{\prime\prime})u,u^{\prime}\rangle\\
&=-2\mathop{\rm Re}w\langle P^{\pm}_{\varphi}(h)u,u^{\prime}\rangle\mp 2\varepsilon w\mathop{\rm Im}\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle+wq\langle h^{2}r^{-2}\Lambda u,u\rangle\\
&+(4h^{-1}w\varphi^{\prime}+w^{\prime})\|hu^{\prime}\|^{2}+(w(E+(\varphi^{\prime})^{2}-V_{L}))^{\prime}\|u\|^{2}+2w\mathop{\rm Re}\langle(V_{0}+V_{S}+h\varphi^{\prime\prime})u,u^{\prime}\rangle.\end{split}

where we have put

(2.5)

q=q(r)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{2}{r}-\frac{w^{\prime}}{w}.

We shall construct
w
so that
w,\,w^{\prime}>0
and
q\geq 0
. Then using (
2.4
) and
2ab\geq-(\gamma a^{2}+\gamma^{-1}b^{2})
for all
\gamma>0
, we find

(2.6)

\displaystyle w^{\prime}F+wF^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{\gamma_{1}w^{2}}{h^{2}w^{\prime}}\|P^{\pm}_{\varphi}(h)u\|^{2}\mp 2\varepsilon w\mathop{\rm Im}\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle+(4(1-\gamma^{-1}_{2})h^{-1}w\varphi^{\prime}+(1-\gamma^{-1}_{1}-\gamma^{-1}_{2})w^{\prime})\|hu^{\prime}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\big((w(E+(\varphi^{\prime})^{2}-V_{L}))^{\prime}-\frac{h^{2}wq}{4r^{2}}-\frac{\gamma_{2}w^{2}|h^{-1}(V_{0}+V_{S})+\varphi^{\prime\prime}|^{2}}{w^{\prime}+4h^{-1}\varphi^{\prime}w}\big)\|u\|^{2},\qquad\gamma_{1},\,\gamma_{2}>0.

For
\gamma>0
, put
\gamma_{1}=2(1+\gamma)/\gamma,\,\gamma_{2}=(1+\gamma)
, yielding

(2.7)

\begin{split}(wF)^{\prime}&\geq-\frac{2(1+\gamma)w^{2}}{\gamma h^{2}w^{\prime}}\|P^{\pm}_{\varphi}(h)u\|^{2}\mp 2\varepsilon w\mathop{\rm Im}\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle+\frac{\gamma}{2(1+\gamma)}w^{\prime}\|hu^{\prime}\|^{2}\\
&+\big((w(E+(\varphi^{\prime})^{2}-V_{L}))^{\prime}-\frac{h^{2}wq}{4r^{2}}-\frac{(1+\gamma)w^{2}|h^{-1}(V_{0}+V_{S})+\varphi^{\prime\prime}|^{2}}{w^{\prime}+4h^{-1}\varphi^{\prime}w}\big)\|u\|^{2}.\end{split}

In Section
6
, we show how Theorem
1.1
follows from a certain global Carleman estimate, see Lemma
5.1
. An essential ingredient for this Carleman estimate is to specify
\varphi
and
w
as precisely as possible, in order that the second line of (
2.7
) has a good lower bound. More precisely, putting

(2.8)

A(r)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(w(E+(\varphi^{\prime})^{2}-V_{L}))^{\prime}-\frac{h^{2}wq}{4r^{2}},\qquad B(r)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{w^{2}|h^{-1}(V_{0}+V_{S})+\varphi^{\prime\prime}|^{2}}{w^{\prime}+4h^{-1}\varphi^{\prime}w},

we shall see that it suffices for
w
and
\varphi
to satisfy, for suitable
\gamma>0
,

(2.9)

A(r)-(1+\gamma)B(r)\geq\frac{E}{2}w^{\prime}(r),\qquad 0<h\ll 1.

To facilitate the proof of (
2.9
), we proceed, as in
[
GaSh22a
,
GaSh22b
,
Ob23
]
, to analyze
A
and
B

Our goal in this section is to prove Lemma

3.14
), hence

|r^{\frac{1}{2}-t_{0}}h\tilde{u}^{\prime}|^{2}=|r^{\frac{1}{2}-t_{0}}(e^{-\frac{\varphi}{h}}hu^{\prime}-\varphi^{\prime}e^{-\frac{\varphi}{h}}u)|^{2}\leq Cw^{\prime}(|u|^{2}+\max\varphi^{\prime}|hu^{\prime}|^{2})\leq e^{C/h}w^{\prime}(|u|^{2}+|hu^{\prime}|^{2}).

Consider now the second line of (
5.9
). We bound the first term appearing there using (
5.7
) and (
5.11
) through (
5.13
) (
\alpha\leq 1/2
for
h
small enough, see (
5.8
)). We bound the second and third terms using (
5.6
). Since negative powers of
\alpha
are bounded from above by
e^{C/h}
for
h
small, we conclude, for
E\in[E_{\min},E_{\max}],\,h\in(0,h_{\delta}],
and
0<\varepsilon\leq h
,

(5.14)

\begin{split}\|\langle r\rangle^{-\frac{1+\eta}{2}}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}&\leq e^{C/h}\big(\int_{r,\theta}|\langle r\rangle^{\frac{1+\eta}{2}}r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(P-E\pm i\varepsilon)v|^{2}+\int_{r,\theta}w^{\prime}(|u|^{2}+|hu^{\prime}|^{2})\\
&+e^{C/h}\int_{r,\theta}(r+1)^{1+\eta}|P^{\pm}_{\varphi}(h)u|^{2}+\varepsilon e^{C/h}\int_{r,\theta}|u|^{2}\\
&\leq e^{C/h}\|\langle r\rangle^{\frac{1+\eta}{2}}e^{\varphi/h}(P(h)-E\pm i\varepsilon)v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+e^{C/h}\varepsilon\|e^{\varphi/h}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\\
&+e^{C/h}\int_{r,\theta}w^{\prime}(|u|^{2}+|hu^{\prime}|^{2}),\end{split}

where we have used

2^{-\frac{1+\eta}{2}}\leq\left(\frac{\langle r\rangle}{r+1}\right)^{1+\eta}.

Employing (
5.6
) once more, to bound the last line of (
5.14
), we arrive at (
5.1
) as desired.

∎

6.
Resolvent estimates

In this section, we deduce Theorem
1.1
from the Carleman estimate (
5.1
). This same argument has been presented before, see, e.g.,
[
Da14
,
GaSh22a
,
GaSh22b
,
Ob23
]
. But we include it here for the sake of completeness. The constants
C>0
and
h_{\delta}\in(0,1)
may change between lines but stay independent of
E
,
\varepsilon
, and
h
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By the spectral theorem for self-adjoint operators, the bounds (
1.13
), (
1.14
), and (
1.15
) clearly hold for
\varepsilon>h
. Therefore, to prove Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to consider
0<\varepsilon\leq h
.

Since increasing
s
in (
1.12
) decreases the operator norm, to establish a certain estimate for (
1.12
) for fixed
s>1/2
independent
h
, it suffices to show the same estimate for
h
small enough and an
h
-dependent
s
of the form
(1+\eta)/2<1
. For the rest of the proof, we assume
s
has this form.

By Lemma
5.1
,

(6.1)

e^{-C_{\varphi}/h}\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq e^{C/h}\|\langle x\rangle^{s}(P(h)-E\pm i\varepsilon)v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon e^{C/h}\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

for all
E\in[E_{\min},E_{\max}]
,
h\in(0,h_{\delta}]
,
0\leq\varepsilon\leq h
, and
v\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and where
C_{\varphi}=C_{\varphi}(h)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=2\max\varphi
. Moreover, for any
\gamma>0
,

(6.2)

\begin{split}2\varepsilon\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}&=-2\mathop{\rm Im}\langle(P(h)-E\pm i\varepsilon)v,v\rangle_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\gamma^{-1}\|\langle x\rangle^{s}(P(h)-E\pm i\varepsilon)v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\gamma\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Setting
\gamma=e^{-(C+C_{\varphi})/h}
, and using (
6.2
) to estimate
\varepsilon\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}
from above in (
6.1
), we absorb the
\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}v\|_{L^{2}}
term that now appears on the right of (
6.1
) into the left side. Multiplying through by
2e^{C_{\varphi}/h}
, and applying (
4.3
), we find, for
E\in[E_{\min},E_{\max}]
,
h\in(0,h_{\delta}]
,
0\leq\varepsilon\leq h
, and
v\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

(6.3)

\begin{split}\|&\langle x\rangle^{-s}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\leq\begin{cases}\exp\big(Ch^{-4/3}(\log(h^{-1})\big)\|\langle x\rangle^{s}(P(h)-E\pm i\varepsilon)v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&\delta=1,\\
\exp\big(Ch^{-\frac{4}{3}-\frac{2(1-\delta)+\lambda^{-1}}{3(1+2\delta-\lambda^{-1})}}(\log(h^{-1}))^{1+\epsilon}\big)\|\langle x\rangle^{s}(P(h)-E\pm i\varepsilon)v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&0<\delta<1,\\
\exp\big(Ch^{-2}(\log(h^{-1}))^{1+\tilde{\rho}}\big)\|\langle x\rangle^{s}(P(h)-E\pm i\varepsilon)v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&\delta=0.\end{cases}\end{split}

The final task is to use (
6.3
) to show, for
E\in[E_{\min},E_{\max}]
,
h\in(0,h_{\delta}]
,
0<\varepsilon\leq h
, and
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

(6.4)

\begin{split}\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}(P(h)-E\pm&i\varepsilon)^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\begin{cases}\exp\big(Ch^{-4/3}(\log(h^{-1})\big)h)\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}&\delta=1,\\
\exp\big(Ch^{-\frac{4}{3}-\frac{2(1-\delta)+\lambda^{-1}}{3(1+2\delta-\lambda^{-1})}}(\log(h^{-1}))^{1+\epsilon}\big)\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}&0<\delta<1,\\
\exp\big(Ch^{-2}(\log(h^{-1}))^{1+\tilde{\rho}}\big)\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}&\delta=0.\end{cases}\end{split}

from which Theorem
1.1
follows. To establish (
6.4
), we prove a simple Sobolev space estimate and then apply a density argument that relies on (
6.3
).

The operator

[P(h),\langle x\rangle^{s}]\langle x\rangle^{-s}=\left(-h^{2}\Delta\langle x\rangle^{s}-2h^{2}(\nabla\langle x\rangle^{s})\cdot\nabla\right)\langle x\rangle^{-s}

is bounded
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. So, for
v\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\langle x\rangle^{s}v\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

(6.5)

\begin{split}\|\langle x\rangle^{s}(P(h)-E\pm i\varepsilon)v\|_{L^{2}}&\leq\|(P(h)-E\pm i\varepsilon)\langle x\rangle^{s}v\|_{L^{2}}+\|[P(h),\langle x\rangle^{s}]\langle x\rangle^{-s}\langle x\rangle^{s}v\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C_{E_{\max},\varepsilon,h}\|\langle x\rangle^{s}v\|_{H^{2}},\end{split}

for some constant
C_{E_{\max},\varepsilon,h}>0
depending on
E_{\max}
,
\varepsilon
and
h
.

Given
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the function
u=\langle x\rangle^{s}(P(h)-E\pm i\varepsilon)^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
because

u=(P(h)-E\pm i\varepsilon)^{-1}(f-w),\qquad w=\langle x\rangle^{s}[P(h),\langle x\rangle^{-s}]\langle x\rangle^{s}\langle x\rangle^{-s}u,

with
\langle x\rangle^{s}[P(h),\langle x\rangle^{-s}]\langle x\rangle^{s}
being bounded
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
since
s<1
.

Now, choose a sequence
v_{k}\in C_{0}^{\infty}
such that
v_{k}\to\langle x\rangle^{s}(P(h)-E\pm i\varepsilon)^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}f
in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Define
\tilde{v}_{k}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\langle x\rangle^{-s}v_{k}
. Then, as
k\to\infty
,

\begin{split}\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}\tilde{v}_{k}-\langle x\rangle^{-s}(&P(h)-E\pm i\varepsilon)^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}f\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\|v_{k}-\langle x\rangle^{s}(P(h)-E\pm i\varepsilon)^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}f\|_{H^{2}}\to 0.\end{split}

Also, applying (
6.5
),

\|\langle x\rangle^{s}(P(h)-E\pm i\varepsilon)\tilde{v}_{k}-f\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{E_{\max},\varepsilon,h}\|v_{k}-\langle x\rangle^{s}(P(h)-E\pm i\varepsilon)^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}f\|_{H^{2}}\to 0.

We then achieve (
6.4
) by replacing
v
by
\tilde{v}_{k}
in (
6.3
) and sending
k\to\infty
.

∎

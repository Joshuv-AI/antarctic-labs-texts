THE PITCH-CLASS INTEGER THEOREM
CALEB SCOTT ALONS

Abstract
. Mathematical music theory has assumed without proof that musical notes can be associated with the equivalence classes of ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} . We contest the triviality of this assertion, which we call the Pitch-class Integer Theorem (PCIT). Since the existing literature assumes the PCIT without proof, the mathematics to rigorously treat the PCIT does not yet exist. Thus, we construct an axiomatic proof of the PCIT to support the existing mathematical models of music theory.



1.
INTRODUCTION

Although musical harmony is well-understood and standardized from the musical perspective, this subject is comparatively undeveloped on the mathematical side. There does not currently exist a well-established and universally agreed upon canon of definitions and concepts, and most papers in this subject provide their own internal definitions for music theory concepts. Moreover, such a canon is far from achieving standardization in mathematical music theory. One particular concept, however, has gained traction and is universally accepted: the association of musical notes with the equivalence classes of finite groups of integers.

Fiore proposed an integer model of pitch, stating that we could translate pitch classes into numbers as follows
[
1
]
:

\displaystyle C=0,

\displaystyle C\sharp=D\flat=1,

\displaystyle D=2,

\displaystyle D\sharp=E\flat=3,

\displaystyle E=4,

\displaystyle F=5,

\displaystyle F\sharp=G\flat=6,

\displaystyle G=7,

\displaystyle G\sharp=A\flat=8,

\displaystyle A=9,

\displaystyle A\sharp=B\flat=10,

\displaystyle B=11.

Fiore assumed that these pitch classes were the elements of the group
\mathbb{Z}_{12}
. Fiore’s model was echoed later by Zhang, who postulated that pitches that are separated by an integer multiple of an octave or are enharmonically equivalent belong to the same pitch class
[
5
]
. Zhang’s definition of an ordered pitch-class interval assumes that pitch classes correspond to
\mathbb{Z}_{12}

Around the same time, Wright wrote the book
Mathematics and Music
to show how mathematics models music more broadly. In his chapter on harmony, he assumes that intervals are measured in semitones and that the set of intervals is identified with the set
\mathbb{Z}
of integers, with an integer
k
corresponding to the interval of
k
semitones
[
4
]
. Under this assertion, Wright claims that equivalence modulo
12
is octave identification and proposes the following pitch-class integer model: two intervals,
k
semitones and
l
semitones, are equivalent modulo octave if and only if
k\equiv l\mod{12}
. Kennedy wrote an undergraduate senior thesis that sought to use group theory to qualify
n
-note tonal systems. Her base assertion is to let the group
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
be the model for
n
-note tonal systems
[
2
]
. That is, Kennedy expanded Wright’s model to accommodate a tonal system that includes an arbitrary number of notes, rather than strictly
12
. We note that Zhang, Wright, and Kennedy do not cite Fiore’s initial paper, nor do they reference each other’s research.

Most recently, Tsok, Jelten, and Gamson published an article that cites the research done by Fiore and Zhang
[
3
]
. They reinforce the pitch-class integer model and assert that the musical notes form a group of integers modulo
12
such that

\mathbb{Z}_{12}=\{\textrm{C},\,\textrm{C}\sharp,\,\textrm{D},\,\textrm{D}\sharp,\,\textrm{E},\,\textrm{F},\,\textrm{F}\sharp,\,\textrm{G},\,\textrm{G}\sharp,\,\textrm{A},\,\textrm{A}\sharp,\,\textrm{B}\}.

We observe that pitch-class integer models have been independently developed by several authors and are currently assumed without proof. This paper seeks to formalize the association of musical notes with the equivalence classes of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, which we call the Pitch-class Integer Theorem (PCIT).

Definition 3.1

(Step, Step Set: Uniform, Non-uniform)
.

Suppose
\mathcal{T}_{n}(f)
is a tuning set. A
step
is a rule
\delta_{i}:\mathcal{T}_{n}(f)\to\mathcal{T}_{n}(f)
defined by

\nu_{k,i+1}=\nu_{k,i}\ast\delta_{i}\qquad\forall\nu_{k,i}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}(f).

A
step set
is the collection of steps
\Delta_{n}=\{\delta_{i}\}_{i\,=\,0}^{n-1}

\Delta_{n}
is
uniform
if
\delta_{i}=\delta

\forall i=0,\dots,n-1

\Delta_{n}
is
non-uniform.

The equation
\nu_{k,i+1}=\nu_{k,i}\ast\delta_{i}
says that the pitch that results from the rule
\delta_{i}
applied to
\nu_{k,i}
is
\nu_{k,i+1}
. If
\delta
belongs to a uniform step set
\Delta_{n}
, we denote

\nu_{k,i}\ast\delta^{j}=\nu_{k,i}\ast\underbrace{\delta\ast\cdots\ast\delta}_{j}.

We describe the relationship between two pitches in a tuning set using steps. We say that the pitches
\nu_{k,p},\nu_{k,q}
are
|p-q|

steps apart.
If
p>q
, we say that
\nu_{k,p}
is
p-q
steps above

\nu_{k,q}
, and if
p<q
, we say that
\nu_{k,p}
is
q-p
steps below

\nu_{k,q}
.

TUNING SPACES

Definition 4.1

(Tuning Space, Standard Pitch)
.

A
tuning space

\langle\mathcal{T}_{n}(\varsigma),\Delta_{n}\rangle
is a tuning set
\mathcal{T}_{n}(\varsigma)
together with a step set
\Delta_{n}
, where
\varsigma\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
is an arbitrarily selected pitch such that
\forall\nu_{k,i}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}(\varsigma)
,
\nu_{k,i}=2^{k}\varsigma\ast\delta_{0}\ast\cdots\ast\delta_{i-1}
. The pitch
\varsigma
is the
standard pitch
of the tuning space. We denote tuning spaces with
\mathcal{T}_{n}
, reserving the full notation for explicitly-defined tuning spaces.

Definition 4.2

(Chromatic, Non-chromatic)
.

A tuning space is
chromatic
if its step set is uniform. A
non-chromatic
tuning space is a tuning space that is not chromatic.

Definition 4.3

(
n
-tone Equal Temperament (
n
-TET))

Let
\mathcal{T}_{n}(\varsigma)
be a tuning set and
\Delta_{n}^{\textrm{ET}}

\delta_{\textrm{ET}}

. The tuning space
\langle\mathcal{T}_{n}(\varsigma),\Delta_{n}^{\textrm{ET}}\rangle
is
n
-tone equal temperament (
n
-TET) tuned to
\varsigma
.
The pitch equation for
n
-TET tuned to
\varsigma
is

\nu_{k,j}=2^{k}\varsigma\cdot\sqrt[n]{2^{j}}=\sqrt[n]{2^{j+kn}}\,\varsigma.

5.
OCTAVE EQUIVALENCE AND NOTES

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\nu_{a,p},\nu_{b,q}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
a tuning space; let
\sim
be a relation such that
\nu_{a,p}\sim\nu_{b,q}\Longleftrightarrow p=q
. Then
\sim
is an equivalence relation on
\mathcal{T}_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
\nu_{a,p},\nu_{b,q},\nu_{c,r}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
a tuning space; let
\sim
be a relation such that
\nu_{a,p}\sim\nu_{b,q}\Longleftrightarrow p=q
. Since
\nu_{a,p}\sim\nu_{a,p}\Rightarrow p=p
, then
\sim
is reflexive. Since
\nu_{a,p}\sim\nu_{b,q}\Rightarrow p=q\Rightarrow q=p\Rightarrow\nu_{b,q}\sim\nu_{a,p}
, then
\sim
is symmetric. Since
\nu_{a,p}\sim\nu_{b,q},\,\nu_{b,q}\sim\nu_{c,r}\Rightarrow p=q,\,q=r\Rightarrow p=r\Rightarrow\nu_{a,p}\sim\nu_{c,r}
, then
\sim
is transitive.

∎

Definition 5.2

(Octave Equivalence Relation)
.

The equivalence relation defined in Theorem
5.1
is the
octave equivalence relation.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\nu_{x,i}\leq\nu_{y,j}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
a tuning space. Then
\nu_{x,i},\nu_{y,j}
are octave equivalent
\Longleftrightarrow\nu_{y,j}=2^{y-x}\nu_{x,i}
.

Proof.

Let
\nu_{x,i}\leq\nu_{y,j}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
a tuning space; let
\sim
be octave equivalence; let
\nu_{x,i}\sim\nu_{y,j}
. Then
\nu_{y,j}=\nu_{y,i}
, and since
\nu_{x,i}\leq\nu_{y,j}
, we have that
x\leq y
. If
x=y
, then

\displaystyle\nu_{y,j}=2^{y-x}\nu_{x,i}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\nu_{y,i}=2^{y-y}\nu_{y,i}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\nu_{y,i}=\nu_{y,i}.

If
x<y
, we show that

\displaystyle 2^{y-x}\nu_{x,i}

\displaystyle=2^{x+(y-x)}\varsigma\ast\delta_{0}\ast\cdots\ast\delta_{i-1}

\displaystyle=2^{y}\varsigma\ast\delta_{0}\ast\cdots\ast\delta_{i-1}

\displaystyle=\nu_{y,i}

\displaystyle=\nu_{y,j}.

We now assume
\nu_{y,j}=2^{y-x}\nu_{x,i}
. We show that

2^{y}\varsigma\ast\delta_{0}\ast\cdots\ast\delta_{j-1}=2^{y-x}\cdot 2^{x}\varsigma\ast\delta_{0}\ast\cdots\ast\delta_{i-1}=2^{y}\varsigma\ast\delta_{0}\ast\cdots\ast\delta_{i-1}.

Then
i=j
, so
\nu_{x,i}\sim\nu_{y,j}
.

∎

Definition 5.4

(Note, Chromatic Note)
.

Let
\sim
be the octave equivalence relation on a tuning space
\mathcal{T}_{n}
. A
note
is an equivalence class
[\nu_{k,i}]=\{\nu_{k,x}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}\,|\,\nu_{k,x}\sim\nu_{k,i}\}
. The notes of a chromatic tuning space are called
chromatic notes.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{n}
be a tuning space with notes
[\nu_{k,i}]
,
i=0,\dots,n-1
. Then
[\nu_{k,n}]=[\nu_{k,0}]
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{n}
be a tuning space with notes
[\nu_{k,i}]
,
i=0,\dots,n-1
. Definition
2.4
gives that
[\nu_{k,n}]=[\nu_{k+1,0}]
, so we show that

\displaystyle[\nu_{k,n}]

\displaystyle=[\nu_{k+1,0}]

\displaystyle=\{\nu_{k+1,x}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}\,|\,\nu_{k+1,x}\sim\nu_{k+1,0}\}

\displaystyle=\{\dots,\nu_{-1,0},\nu_{0,0},\nu_{1,0},\nu_{2,0},\nu_{3,0},\dots\}

\displaystyle=\{\nu_{k,x}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}\,|\,\nu_{k,x}\sim\nu_{k,0}\}

\displaystyle=[\nu_{k,0}].

∎

6.
HARMONY GROUPS

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
[\nu_{k,i}]
be a note of
\mathcal{T}_{n}
a tuning space. Then
[\nu_{k,i}]\ast\delta_{i}=[\nu_{k,i+1}]
.

Proof.

Let
[\nu_{k,i}]=\{\nu_{k,x}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}\,|\,\nu_{k,x}\sim\nu_{k,i}\}
be a note of
\mathcal{T}_{n}
a tuning space. Then

[\nu_{k,i}]\ast\delta_{i}=\{\nu_{k,x}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}\,|\,\nu_{k,x}\sim(\nu_{k,i}\ast\delta_{i})\}=\{\nu_{k,x}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}\,|\,\nu_{k,x}\sim\nu_{k,i+1}\}=[\nu_{k,i+1}].

∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
[\nu_{k,x}]
be a note of
\mathcal{T}_{n}
a tuning space. Then
[\nu_{k,x}]=[\nu_{k,0}]\ast\delta_{0}\ast\cdots\ast\delta_{x-1}
.

Proof.

Let
[\nu_{k,x}]
be a note of
\mathcal{T}_{n}
a tuning space. If
x=0
, then trivially we have that
[\nu_{k,x}]=[\nu_{k,0}]
. If
0<x<n-1
, then

[\nu_{k,x}]=[\nu_{k,x-x}]\ast\delta_{x-x}\ast\cdots\ast\delta_{x-1}=[\nu_{k,0}]\ast\delta_{0}\ast\cdots\ast\delta_{x-1}.

∎

.

Definition 6.6

(Harmonic Addition)
.

The binary operation
+:\left(\mathcal{N}_{n}\times\mathcal{N}_{n}\right)\to\mathcal{N}_{n}
defined by
[\nu_{k,x}]+[\nu_{k,y}]=[\nu_{k,x+y}]
is
harmonic addition.

Lemma 6.7
.

Note sets are closed under harmonic addition.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{N}_{n}
be a note set; let
+
be harmonic addition. Suppose
[\nu_{k,x}],[\nu_{k,y}]\in\mathcal{N}_{n}
. If
0\leq x+y<n
, then
[\nu_{k,x+y}]\in\mathcal{N}_{n}
. If
n\leq x+y\leq 2n-2
, then
0\leq x+y-n<n-1
and

[\nu_{k,x+y}]=[\nu_{k,n+(x+y-n)}]=[\nu_{k,n}]+[\nu_{k,x+y-n}]=[\nu_{k,0}]+[\nu_{k,x+y-n}]=[\nu_{k,x+y-n}]\in\mathcal{N}_{n}.

∎

Theorem 6.8
.

A note set
\mathcal{N}_{n}
together with harmonic addition is a group.

Proof.

Let
[\nu_{k,a}],[\nu_{k,b}],[\nu_{k,c}]\in\mathcal{N}_{n}
a note set; let
+
be harmonic addition. We show that
+
is associative:

\displaystyle\left([\nu_{k,a}]+[\nu_{k,b}]\right)+[\nu_{k,c}]

\displaystyle=[\nu_{k,a+b}]+[\nu_{k,c}]

\displaystyle=[\nu_{k,(a+b)+c}]

\displaystyle=[\nu_{k,a+(b+c)}]

\displaystyle=[\nu_{k,a}]+[\nu_{k,b+c}]

\displaystyle=[\nu_{k,a}]+\left([\nu_{k,b}]+[\nu_{k,c}]\right).

Since
\forall\,[\nu_{k,x}]\in\mathcal{N}_{n}
,
[\nu_{k,0}]+[\nu_{k,x}]=[\nu_{k,x}]+[\nu_{k,0}]=[\nu_{k,x}]
, we see that
+
has identity
[\nu_{k,0}]\in\mathcal{N}_{n}
. We lastly show that
\forall\,[\nu_{k,a}]\in\mathcal{N}_{n}
,
\exists\,[\nu_{k,n-a}]\in\mathcal{N}_{n}
such that
[\nu_{k,a}]+[\nu_{k,n-a}]=[\nu_{k,n-a}]+[\nu_{k,a}]=[\nu_{k,n}]=[\nu_{k,0}]
.

∎

Definition 6.9

(Harmony Group)
.

A
harmony group
of
n
notes
\mathcal{H}_{n}
is a note set
\mathcal{N}_{n}
together with harmonic addition.

7.
THE PITCH-CLASS INTEGER THEOREM

Theorem 7.1

(Pitch-class Integer Theorem)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{n}
be a harmony group of
n
notes. Then
\mathcal{H}_{n}\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi:\mathcal{H}_{n}\to\mathbb{Z}_{n}
be a map defined by
\varphi([\nu_{k,x}])=x
. Since
\varphi([\nu_{k,x}])=\varphi([\nu_{k,y}])\Rightarrow x=y
, then
\varphi
is injective. We show that
\forall z\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
,
\exists\,[\nu_{k,z}]\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
such that
\varphi([\nu_{k,z}])=z
, so
\varphi
is surjective. Since
\varphi([\nu_{k,x}]+[\nu_{k,y}])=\varphi([\nu_{k,x+y}])=x+y=\varphi([\nu_{k,x}])+\varphi([\nu_{k,y}])
, we conclude that
\varphi
is a bijective homomorphism, so
\mathcal{H}_{n}\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
.

∎

Regularity of pullback attractors and equilibrium for non-autonomous stochastic FitzHugh-Nagumo system on unbounded domains
Wenqiang Zhao, Anhui Gu

Abstract
. A theory on bi-spatial random attractors developed recently by Li et al. is extended to study stochastic Fitzhugh-Nagumo system driven by a non-autonomous term as well as a general multiplicative noise. By using the so-called notions of uniform absorption and uniformly pullback asymptotic compactness, it is showed that every generated random cocycle has a pullback attractor in L l ​ ( ℝ N ) × L 2 ​ ( ℝ N ) L^{l}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}) with l ∈ ( 2 , p ] l\in(2,p] , and the family of obtained attractors is upper semi-continuous at any intensity of noise. Moreover, if some additional conditions are added, then the system possesses a unique equilibrium and is attracted by a single point.

(1.1)

\displaystyle d\tilde{v}+(\sigma\tilde{v}-\beta\tilde{u})dt=h(t,x)dt+\varepsilon\tilde{v}\circ d\omega(t),t>\tau,

(1.2)

with initial value numbers

\displaystyle\tilde{u}(x,\tau)=\tilde{u}_{\tau}(x),\ \ \ \ \ \ \tilde{v}(x,\tau)=\tilde{v}_{\tau}(x),

(\tilde{u}_{0},\tilde{v}_{0})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

Let

is the identity on

\sigma_{s+t}=\sigma_{s}\circ\sigma_{t}
for all

(\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})
-measurable mapping such that

is the identity on
\Omega

\vartheta_{s+t}=\vartheta_{s}\circ\vartheta_{t}
for all

and
\vartheta_{t}P=P
for all

. In particular, we call both
(Q,\{\sigma_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}})
and

be the collection of

X
.

Definition 2.1.

A measurable mapping
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times Q\times\Omega\times X\rightarrow X,(t,q,\omega,x)\mapsto\varphi(t,q,\omega,x)
is called
to be a random cocycle on

(Q,\{\sigma_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}})
and

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},{P},\{\vartheta_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}})
if for every
q\in Q,\omega\in\Omega
and
s,t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
the following statements are satisfied:

\displaystyle(i)\ \varphi(0,q,\omega,.)\ \mbox{is the identity on X},\ \ \ (ii)\ \varphi(t+s,q,\omega,.)=\varphi(t,\sigma_{s}q,\vartheta_{s}\omega,.)\circ\varphi(s,q,\omega,.).

An random cocycle is said to be continuous in
X
iff each operator
\varphi(t,q,\omega,.)
is continuous in
X
for each
q\in Q,\omega\in\Omega
and
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

In particular, it is pointed out that in this paper, we need further to assume that the random cocycle
\varphi
acting on
X
takes its values in the terminate space
Y
for for all
t>0
(except that
t=0
),
i.e.,

(H2)
For every
t>0,q\in Q,
and
\omega\in\Omega
,
\varphi(t,q,\omega,.):X\rightarrow Y
.

In the sequel, we use
\mathcal{D}
to denote a collection of some families of nonempty subsets of

which is parameterized by

:

\mathcal{D}=\{D=\{\emptyset\neq D(q,\omega)\in 2^{X};q\in Q,\omega\in\Omega\};f_{D}\ \mbox{satisfies some conditions}\}.

We further assume that
\mathcal{D}
is inclusion closed, that is, for each

,

\{\tilde{D}(q,\omega):\tilde{D}(q,\omega)\ \mbox{is a nonempty subset of}\ D(q,\omega),\forall\ q\in Q,\omega\in\Omega\}\in\mathcal{D}.

Given

if and only if
D_{1}(q,\omega)=D_{2}(q,\omega)
for each

and
\omega\in\Omega
.

Throughout this paper, all assertions about
\omega
are assumed to hold on a
\vartheta_{t}
-invariant set of full measure
(unless some exceptional cases needed).

Definition 2.2.
A set-valued mapping
K:Q\times\Omega\rightarrow 2^{X}
is called measurable in
X
with respect to
\mathcal{F}
in
\Omega
if
the mapping
\omega\in\Omega\mapsto\mbox{dist}_{X}(x,K(q,\omega))
is (
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}))
-measurable for every fixed

Definition 2.3.

Suppose
\varphi
is a random cocycle on
X
over
(Q,\{\sigma_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}})
and

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},{P},\{\vartheta_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}})
and takes its value in

. A set valued mappping

if

(i)
\mathcal{A}
is measurable in
X
(w.r.t the
P
-completion of
\mathcal{F}
in
\Omega
), and

\mathcal{A}(q,\omega)
is compact in

,

(ii)
\mathcal{A}
is invariant, that is, for every

\varphi(t,q,\omega,\mathcal{A}(q,\omega))=\mathcal{A}(\sigma_{t}q,\vartheta_{t}\omega),\forall\ t\geq 0,

(iii)

attracts every element

in

\mathop{\lim}\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\mbox{dist}_{Y}(\varphi(t,\sigma_{-t}q,\vartheta_{-t}\omega,D(\sigma_{-t}q,\vartheta_{-t}\omega)),\mathcal{A}(q,\omega))=0,

where

\varphi(t,q,\omega,D(q,\omega))=\{\varphi(t,q,\omega,x);x\in D(q,\omega)\}

If

-pullback attractors, which is first introduced in
[

. We also remark that the

is assumed in the initial space

Definition 2.4.
(see
[
15
]

Let both

In the sequel, we need to consider a family of random cocycles

with

, where

and
\varphi_{0}
is a deterministic cocycle over the parametric space

.

Definition 2.5.
(see

]

A family of random cocycles

is said to be convergent at point

in

and

,

\varphi_{\alpha}(t,q,\omega,x)\rightarrow\varphi_{\alpha_{0}}(t,q,\omega,x_{0})\ \mbox{in}\ X,

. A family of random cocycles

. We say a family of random cocycles

converges to a deterministic cocycle

if for each
q\in Q,
and

,

\varphi_{\alpha}(t,q,\omega,x)\rightarrow\varphi_{0}(t,q,x_{0})\ \mbox{in}\ X,

.

Definition 2.6.
(see
[
16
]
.)
A family of random cocycles
\{\varphi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}

if
each
\varphi_{\alpha}
has a closed and measurable pullback absorbing set

in

such that the closure
\overline{K}=\{\overline{\cup_{\alpha\in I}K_{\alpha}(q,\omega)};q\in Q,\omega\in\Omega\}\in\mathcal{D}

\limsup_{\alpha\rightarrow 0}\|K_{\alpha}(q,\omega)\|_{X}\leq c\ \ \mbox{for some deterministic constant}\ c>0.

Here a pullback absorbing set

means that for every

, there exists an absorbing time

Definition 2.7.

A family of random cocycles
\{\varphi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}
is said to be uniformly pullback asymptotically compact over

in
X
if for each
q\in Q,\omega\in\Omega
,
D\in\mathcal{D}
, the sequence

\displaystyle\{\varphi_{\alpha_{n}}(t_{n},\sigma_{-t_{n}}q,\vartheta_{-t_{n}}\omega,x_{n})\}\ \mbox{has a convergence subsequence in}\ X,

(2.1)

whenever
\alpha_{n}\in I,t_{n}\rightarrow\infty
, and

x_{n}\in D(\sigma_{-t_{n}}q,\vartheta_{-t_{n}}\omega)
. A family of random cocycles
\{\varphi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}
is uniformly pullback asymptotically compact in

if the convergence in (

) holds under
Y

\varphi_{\alpha_{0}}
is pullback asymptotically compact in

if (

.

Definition 2.8.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a collection of some families of nonempty subsets of

\displaystyle f(x,s)s\leq-\alpha_{1}|s|^{p}+\psi_{1}(x),

(3.1)

\displaystyle|f(x,s)|\leq\alpha_{2}|s|^{p-1}+\psi_{2}(x),

(3.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial s}f(x,s)\leq\alpha_{3},

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}f(x,s)\Big|\leq\psi_{3}(x).

(3.4)

In order to model the random noises in system (

),

), there exists a

-invarant set

Put

We are at the position to give the continuous random cocycle

\displaystyle+ce^{(b_{0}-b)t_{1}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{b_{0}s}z^{2}(s,\omega)(\|g(s+\tau,.)\|^{2}+\|h(s+\tau,.)\|^{2}+1)ds,

which finishes the proof.

Lemma 5.4.

Suppose that
g
and
h
satisfies (
3.5
),
f
satisfies (
3.1
) and (
3.3
) such that (
5.1
) holds. Then for
\tau\in\mathbb{R},\omega\in\Omega
, the element
u^{*}=u^{*}(\tau,\omega)
defined in Lemma 5.3 is a unique random equilibrium for the cocycle
\varphi
defined by (
3
) in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\varphi(t,\tau,\omega,u^{*}(\tau,\omega))=u^{*}(\tau+t,\vartheta_{t}\omega),\ \ \mbox{for every }\ t\geq 0,\ \tau\in\mathbb{R},\ \omega\in\Omega.

Furthermore, the random equilibrium
\{u^{*}(\tau,\omega),\tau\in\mathbb{R},\ \omega\in\Omega\}
is the unique element of the pullback attractor
\mathcal{A}=\{\mathcal{A}(\tau,\omega);\tau\in\mathbb{R},\ \omega\in\Omega\}
for the random cocycle
\varphi
, i.e., for every
\tau\in\mathbb{R},\omega\in\Omega
,

\mathcal{A}(\tau,\omega)=\{u^{*}(\tau,\omega)\}.

Proof
From the definition of random cocycle,

\varphi(t,\tau-t,\vartheta_{-t}\omega,(\tilde{u}_{\tau-t},\tilde{v}_{\tau-t}))=(\tilde{u}(\tau,\tau-t,\vartheta_{-\tau}\omega,\tilde{u}_{\tau-t}),\tilde{v\emph{}}(\tau,\tau-t,\vartheta_{-\tau}\omega,\tilde{v}_{\tau-t})),

then for for every
\tau\in\mathbb{R},\omega\in\Omega
, we see from Lemma 5.3 that

\displaystyle u^{*}(\tau,\omega)=\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\varphi(t,\tau-t,\vartheta_{-t}\omega,(\tilde{u}_{\tau-t},\tilde{v}_{\tau-t})),

(5.8)

where
(\tilde{u}_{\tau-t},\tilde{v}_{\tau-t})\in D(\tau-t,\vartheta_{-t}\omega)
. Thus by the continuity and the cocycle property of
\varphi
and (
5.8
), we find that for every
t\geq 0,\tau\in\mathbb{R},\omega\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle\varphi(t,\tau,\omega,u^{*}(\tau,\omega))

\displaystyle=\varphi(t,\tau,\omega,.)\circ\lim\limits_{s\rightarrow+\infty}\varphi(s,\tau-s,\vartheta_{-s}\omega,(\tilde{u}_{\tau-t},\tilde{v}_{\tau-t}))

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{s\rightarrow+\infty}\varphi(t,\tau,\omega,.)\circ\varphi(s,\tau-s,\vartheta_{-s}\omega,(\tilde{u}_{\tau-t},\tilde{v}_{\tau-t}))

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{s\rightarrow+\infty}\varphi(t+s,\tau-s,\vartheta_{-s}\omega,(\tilde{u}_{\tau-t},\tilde{v}_{\tau-t}))

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{s\rightarrow+\infty}\varphi(t+s,(\tau+t)-t-s,\vartheta_{-s-t}\vartheta_{t}\omega,(\tilde{u}_{\tau-t},\tilde{v}_{\tau-t}))=u^{*}(\tau+t,\vartheta_{t}\omega),

which also implies the invariance of
\mathcal{A}
, that is,
\varphi(t,\tau,\omega,\mathcal{A}(\tau,\omega))=\mathcal{A}(\tau+t,\vartheta_{t}\omega)
. The compactness of

\mathcal{A}(\tau,\omega)
is obvious and the attracting property follows from (
5.8
).

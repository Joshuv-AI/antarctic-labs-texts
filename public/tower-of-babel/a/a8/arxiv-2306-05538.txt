Geometric interpretation of valuated term (pre)orders
Netanel Friedenberg, Kalina Mincheva

Abstract
. Valuated term orders are studied for the purposes of Gröbner theory over fields with valuation. The points of a usual tropical variety correspond to certain valuated terms preorders. Generalizing both of these, the set of all “well-behaved” valuated term preorders is canonically in bijection with the points of a space introduced in our previous work on tropical adic geometry. In this paper we interpret these points geometrically by explicitly characterizing them in terms of classical polyhedral geometry. This characterization gives a bijection with equivalence classes of flags of polyhedra as well as a bijection with a class of prime filters on a lattice of polyhedral sets. The first of these also classifies valuated term orders. The second bijection is of the same flavor as the bijections from [ 14 ] in non-archimedean analytic geometry and indicates that the results of that paper may have analogues in tropical adic geometry.



1.
Introduction

Classical Gröbner theory uses a term order which compares two monomials
m=a\underline{x}^{\underline{u}}
and
m^{\prime}=a^{\prime}\underline{x}^{\underline{u^{\prime}}}
by only considering
\underline{u}
and
\underline{u^{\prime}}
. In
[
2
]
Chan and Maclagan build Gröbner theory in
k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, when
k
is a field with nontrivial valuation
v:k\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}=:\mathbb{T}
. Their valuated Gröbner theory takes into account the coefficients by picking a weight vector
\underline{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and comparing
v(a)+\underline{w}\cdot\underline{u}
with
v(a^{\prime})+\underline{w}\cdot\underline{u}^{\prime}
, and then using a term order to break any ties.

Valuated Gröbner theory has a natural connection to tropical geometry. More specifically, the tropicalization of a subvariety of the torus is the set of
\underline{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which each polynomial in its defining ideal has at least two monomials of maximal weight. In particular, tropical geometry studies valuated monomial preorders, rather than orders. A
valuated monomial preorder
is a multiplicative total preorder
\leq
on the set of monomials
a\underline{x}^{\underline{u}}
such that if
v(a)\leq v(b)
then
a\leq b
. Valuated monomial
orders
are those for which
a\underline{x}^{\underline{u}}\equiv b\underline{x}^{\underline{\mu}}
if and only if
v(a)=v(b)
and
u=\mu
. In light of this, we treat
a\underline{x}^{\underline{u}}
and
b\underline{x}^{\underline{u}}
as being the same if
v(a)=v(b)
.

When working with valuated monomial orders, one is usually only interested in those in which the valuation of the coefficients play a primary role. These are exactly the orders that arise by using a weight vector and term order as above. We call such orders Chan-Maclagan orders; they also occur in ongoing work of Vaccon and Verron
[
18
]
on universal Gröbner bases in Tate algebras.

Recent work of Amini and Iriarte
[
1
]
aimed at the study of Newton-Okounkov bodies investigates spaces of (quasi)monomial preorders. An understanding of valuated monomial preorders is needed to extend their theory from varieties to semistable models over a valuation ring.

We see from the above that a weight vector
\underline{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
provides a geometric interpretation for certain valuated monomial preorders.
In fact, if
v(k^{\times})\subseteq\mathbb{Q}
, then most points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
define a valuated monomial
order
. For if
\underline{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
has irrational entries which are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
, then distinct monomials will always have different weights.
This shows that certain valuated monomial orders have a geometric interpretation; however, not all of them arise this way.

The goal of the paper is to derive a geometric meaning for each of the following: (1) all valuated monomial preorders, (2) Chan-Maclagan orders, and (3) those valuated monomial preorders determined by a weight vector and a term
preorder
as in the construction of the Chan-Maclagan orders. We do this as an application of the theory proposed in
[
6
]
, thus verifying that
it
carries geometric information.

The theory from
[
6
]
applies more generally, and so we work with
k[M]
where
M
is a toric monoid. We are then able to quickly reduce to the torus case, where
k[M]=k[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
.

We now interpret the Chan-Maclagan preorders in the context of (additively idempotent) semirings, motivated by adic geometry.

The semiring analogue of a valuation is a homomorphism to a totally ordered semifield.
Just as equivalence classes of homomorphisms from a ring

2.
Preliminaries

Definition 2.1
.

By a
semiring

R
we mean a commutative semiring with multiplicative unit. That is,
R
is a set which is a commutative monoid with respect to each of two binary operations: an addition operation
+_{R}
, whose identity is denoted
0_{R}
, and a multiplication operation
\cdot_{R}
, whose identity is denoted
1_{R}
. Furthermore, we require that
0_{R}\neq 1_{R}
,
0_{R}
is multiplicatively absorbing, and multiplication distributes over addition. We omit the subscripts from the operations whenever this will not cause ambiguity. A
semifield
is a semiring in which all nonzero elements have a multiplicative inverse.

Definition 2.2
.

We call a semiring
R

additively idempotent
if
a+a=a
for all
a\in R
. We refer to additively idempotent semirings as just
idempotent

If
R
is an idempotent semiring, then the addition defines a canonical partial order on
R
in the following way:
a\geq b\Leftrightarrow a+b=a
.
With respect to this order,
a+b
is the least upper bound of
a
and
b
. When we consider a totally ordered idempotent semiring, we mean an idempotent semiring for which this canonical order is a total order.

Some of the idempotent semifields that we use throughout the paper are:

•

The
Boolean semifield

\mathbb{B}
is the semifield with two elements
\{1,0\}
, where
1
is the multiplicative identity,
0
is the additive identity and
1+1=1
.
\mathbb{B}
is the only finite additively idempotent semifield.

•

The
tropical semifield

\mathbb{T}
is defined on the set
\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
, by setting the
+
operation to be the usual maximum and the multiplication operation to be the usual addition, with
-\infty=0_{\mathbb{T}}
.

•

The semifield
\mathbb{Q}_{\max}
is a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
. As a set it is
\mathbb{Q}\cup\{-\infty\}
and the operations are the restrictions of the operations on
\mathbb{T}
. We also use the same notation when
\mathbb{Q}
is replaced by any other additive subgroup of
\mathbb{R}
. In fact, every sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
arises this way.

•

The semifield
\mathbb{T}^{(n)}
is defined on the set
\mathbb{R}^{n}\cup\{-\infty\}
, by setting the addition operation to be lexicographical maximum and the multiplication operation to be the usual pointwise addition.

Throughout this paper all monoids, rings and semirings are assumed to be commutative. All rings and semirings have a multiplicative unit. Whenever we use the word semiring without further qualification, we refer to an additively idempotent semiring.
Our focus will be on totally ordered semifields which are different from
\mathbb{B}
. Henceforth, when we refer to totally ordered semifields, we implicitly assume that they are not isomorphic to
\mathbb{B}
. In particular, whenever we refer to a sub-semifield of

In order to distinguish when we are considering a real number as being in

\dim_{S}S[M]=\operatorname{rk}\Lambda.

3.
Results

We provide a geometric interpretation of the points of
\operatorname{Cont}_{S}{S[M]}
with
S
a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
and
M
a toric monoid. We will denote by
\Gamma:=\log(S^{\times})
the subgroup of
\mathbb{R}
corresponding to
S
.

By
[
6
, Proposition 9.14]
, fixing the ideal-kernel of
P\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}{S[M]}
decomposes
\operatorname{Cont}_{S}{S[M]}
into toric strata, which are tori. Thus, we only need to deal with the torus case, i.e., the case where
M
is a finitely generated free abelian group.

The main results of this paper show that, in this case, there are explicit bijections between the points of
\operatorname{Cont}_{S}{S[M]}
, the set of prime filters on the lattice of
\Gamma
-rational polyhedral sets in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
where the filter contains a polytope, and certain equivalence classes of flags of polyhedra.

3.1.
Flags and prime congruences.

By a
cone
we will mean a strongly convex polyhedral cone which is not necessarily rational.
We note that there is a bijective correspondence between the set of non-empty strongly convex polyhedra
\mathcal{P}\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}
and the set of strongly convex cones
\sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
, not contained in
\{0\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
. Under this correspondence we map a polyhedron
\mathcal{P}
to the closed cone over it, denoted
c(\mathcal{P})
, and a cone
\mathcal{C}
to the restriction of
\mathcal{C}
to
\{1\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
, by which we mean the inverse image of
\mathcal{C}
under the map
N_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
given by
x\mapsto(1,x)
.

Additionally, we can think of a point
w=(r,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
as a homomorphism
w:\Gamma\times M\to\mathbb{R}
, mapping
(\gamma,u)\mapsto r\gamma+\left\langle\xi,u\right\rangle
. We also use
\left\langle\bullet,\bullet\right\rangle
to denote the resulting pairing
(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}})\times(\Gamma\times M)\to\mathbb{R}
.
Thus, for any
\mathfrak{S}\subseteq\Gamma\times M
we can consider
\mathfrak{S}^{\vee}=\{w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}\;:\;\left\langle w,\mathfrak{u}\right\rangle\leq 0\text{ for all }\mathfrak{u}\in\mathfrak{S}\}.

Definition 3.1
.

We denote by
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}=(\mathcal{P}_{0}\leq\mathcal{P}_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\mathcal{P}_{k})
a

flag of polyhedra
, where
\dim\mathcal{P}_{i}=i
. We assume that all polyhedra are strongly convex, non-empty and live in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Similarly, we let
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}=(\mathcal{C}_{0}\leq\mathcal{C}_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\mathcal{C}_{k})
denote a
flag of cones
in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq_{0}}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
with
\dim\mathcal{C}_{i}=i+1
. We say that
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
is
simplicial
if each
\mathcal{C}_{i}
is a simplicial cone.

Given a flag
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}
of polyhedra, the corresponding flag of cones is
c(\mathcal{P}_{\bullet})=(c(\mathcal{P}_{0})\leq c(\mathcal{P}_{1})\leq\cdots\leq c(\mathcal{P}_{k}))
. The flags of cones
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
which arise this way are exactly those for which
\mathcal{C}_{0}
is not contained in
\{0\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
. For such flags
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
, we write
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}|_{\{1\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}}
for the corresponding flag of polyhedra.

Consider the map taking a matrix
C
whose first column is lexicographically at least 0 to the prime it defines on
S[M]
. Here, because we have not fixed an identification
M\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
C
is of the form
C=\begin{pmatrix}r_{0}&\xi_{0}\\
r_{1}&\xi_{1}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
r_{k}&\xi_{k}\end{pmatrix}
where
r_{0},\ldots,r_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\xi_{0},\ldots,\xi_{k}\in N_{\mathbb{R}}
. We now show how this map factors through the set of simplicial flags of cones. Moreover, we show that if we restrict to matrices giving primes in
\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]
, the map factors through the corresponding set of flags of polyhedra.

Given a matrix
C
as above, we can use downward gaussian elimination as in Lemma
2.7
and removal of zero rows to get all of the entries in the first column non-negative and to get the rows linearly independent without changing the prime that
C
defines. Then we let
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}(C)
be the simplicial flag of cones defined by letting
\mathcal{C}_{i}(C)
be the cone generated by
(r_{0},\xi_{0}),\ldots,(r_{i},\xi_{i})
for each
0\leq i\leq k
.

For any simplicial flag of cones
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
, we get a prime congruence on
S[M]
as follows.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{-1}=\{0\}
and pick points
w_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{i}\backslash\mathcal{C}_{i-1}
for
0\leq i\leq k
. Since
w_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
, we can think of each
w_{i}
as a homomorphism
w_{i}:\Gamma\times M\to\mathbb{R}
. Thus, we can consider the group homomorphism

\boldsymbol{w}=(w_{0},\ldots,w_{k}):\Gamma\times M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k+1},

which preserves the order on
\Gamma
when we give
\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
the lexicographic order. Therefore,
\boldsymbol{w}
gives rise to a semiring homomorphism

\varphi_{\boldsymbol{w}}:S[M]\rightarrow\mathbb{T}^{(k+1)},\quad a\chi^{u}\mapsto(w_{0},\ldots,w_{k})(\log a,u).

We let
P_{\boldsymbol{w}}=\ker\varphi_{\boldsymbol{w}}
. That is,
P_{\boldsymbol{w}}
is the prime on
S[M]
defined by the matrix
\begin{pmatrix}w_{0}\\
\vdots\\
w_{k}\end{pmatrix}
.
By Proposition
2.8
,
P_{\boldsymbol{w}}
is in
\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]
if and only if the (1,1) entry of this matrix is positive. Since
w_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{0}\backslash\{0\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
, this happens exactly when
\mathcal{C}_{0}
is not contained in
\{0\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
, i.e., when
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}=c(\mathcal{P}_{\bullet})
for some flag
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}
of polyhedra.

In order for this construction to give a prime defined by
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
, we must show that
P_{\boldsymbol{w}}
is independent of the choice of
\boldsymbol{w}
. Set
v_{0}=w_{0}
. For
1\leq i\leq k
,
\mathcal{C}_{i}\backslash\mathcal{C}_{i-1}
contains a unique ray of
\mathcal{C}_{i}
. Fix a generator of this ray and call it
v_{i}
. Consider the vector
\boldsymbol{v}=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{k})
. Analogously to
\varphi_{\boldsymbol{w}}
and
P_{\boldsymbol{w}}
, we can define
\varphi_{\boldsymbol{v}}
and
P_{\boldsymbol{v}}
. The following proposition gives us that
P_{\boldsymbol{v}}
and
P_{\boldsymbol{w}}
are the same.

Proposition 3.2
.

There is an automorphism
\psi
of
\mathbb{T}^{(k+1)}
given by a linear transformation on
\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
such that
\varphi_{\boldsymbol{w}}=\psi\circ\varphi_{\boldsymbol{v}}
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
k
. The case
k=0
is trivial. Assume the statement is true for some
k=r
; we want to show that it is true for
k=r+1
.
Let
\tilde{\varphi}_{\boldsymbol{w}}
,
\tilde{\varphi}_{\boldsymbol{v}}
and
\tilde{\psi}
be the maps we obtain for the flag
(\mathcal{P}_{0}\leq\mathcal{P}_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\mathcal{P}_{r})
. Write

w_{r+1}=\sum_{i=0}^{r+1}\alpha_{i}v_{i},\text{ where }\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\text{ and }\alpha_{r+1}>0.

Thus, we have

\begin{pmatrix}w_{0}\\
w_{1}\\
\vdots\\
w_{r}\\
w_{r+1}\end{pmatrix}=\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}\tilde{\psi}&\begin{matrix}0\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{matrix}\\
\hline\cr\begin{matrix}\alpha_{0}&\alpha_{1}&\cdots&\alpha_{r}\end{matrix}&\alpha_{r+1}\end{array}\right)\begin{pmatrix}v_{0}\\
v_{1}\\
\vdots\\
v_{r}\\
v_{r+1}\end{pmatrix},

where we call the matrix in this product
\psi
. Note that it is invertible since
\tilde{\psi}
is and
\alpha_{r+1}>0
. Moreover,
\psi
is order preserving, i.e.,
\boldsymbol{h}>\boldsymbol{h}^{\prime}
implies that
\psi\boldsymbol{h}>\psi\boldsymbol{h^{\prime}}
. We can show this by induction; the base case is trivial. If
\tilde{\psi}
is order preserving and then
\psi
is, since
v_{r+1}>v_{r+1}^{\prime}
if and only if
\alpha_{r+1}v_{r+1}>\alpha_{r+1}v_{r+1}^{\prime}
and
\alpha_{r+1}>0
.
∎

Now consider
\boldsymbol{w}^{\prime}=(w^{\prime}_{0},\ldots,w^{\prime}_{k})
with
w^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{i}\backslash\mathcal{C}_{i-1}
. In the same way as we got
\boldsymbol{v}
from
\boldsymbol{w}
, we now get
\boldsymbol{v}^{\prime}
. Since
v_{i}
and
v^{\prime}_{i}
are generators of the same ray, they are positive multiples of each other. Thus,
P_{\boldsymbol{w}^{\prime}}=P_{\boldsymbol{v}^{\prime}}=P_{\boldsymbol{v}}=P_{\boldsymbol{w}}
. In light of this we can make the following definition.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
be a simplicial flag of cones. The
prime congruence
P_{\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}}
defined by
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
is
P_{\boldsymbol{w}}
for any choice of
\boldsymbol{w}=(w_{0},\ldots,w_{k})
with
w_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{i}\backslash\mathcal{C}_{i-1}
. If
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}=c(\mathcal{P}_{\bullet})
, then
P_{\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}}=P_{c(\mathcal{P}_{\bullet})}
is the
prime congruence defined by
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}
.

If
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}=\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}(C)
for some matrix
C
, then we can chose
w_{i}
to be the
(i+1)^{\text{st}}
row of
C
, so
C
is a defining matrix for
P_{\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}}
. In particular, for every prime congruence
P
on
S[M]
, there is a simplicial flag
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
of cones such that
P=P_{\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}}
. If
P\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]
, then we know that
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}=c(\mathcal{P}_{\bullet})
for some flag
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}
of polyhedra, so
P=P_{\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}}
.

3.2.
Equivalence of flags.

Recall that a
\Gamma
-rational polyhedron
is a polyhedron in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
which can be written as
\{x\in N_{\mathbb{R}}\ :\ \left\langle x,u_{i}\right\rangle\leq\gamma_{i}\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,q\}
for some
u_{1},\ldots,u_{q}\in M
and
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{q}\in\Gamma
. A
\Gamma
-rational polyhedral set
is a finite union of
\Gamma
-rational polyhedra.

A cone in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
is
\Gamma
-admissible
if it can be written as

\{(r,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}\ :\ r\gamma_{i}+\left\langle x,u_{i}\right\rangle\leq 0\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,q\}

for some
u_{1},\ldots,u_{q}\in M
and
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{q}\in\Gamma
.
A polyhedron
\mathcal{Q}
is
\Gamma
-rational if and only if
c(\mathcal{Q})
is
\Gamma
-admissible. A cone contained in
\{0\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
is
\Gamma
-admissible if and only if it is rational. We call a finite union of
\Gamma
-admissible cones a
\Gamma
-admissible fan support set
. Note that the set of
\Gamma
-rational polyhedral sets in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
and the set of
\Gamma
-admissible fan support sets each form a lattice when ordered by inclusion. In both of these lattices, meet and join are given by intersection and union, respectively.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}
be a flag of polyhedra and let
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
be a flag of cones. A
\Gamma
-rational neighborhood
of
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}
is a
\Gamma
-rational polyhedron
\mathcal{Q}
which meets the relative interior of each
\mathcal{P}_{i}
. A
\Gamma
-admissible neighborhood
of
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
is a
\Gamma
-admissible cone
\mathcal{D}
which meets the relative interior of each
\mathcal{C}_{i}
.

Definition 3.5
.

We say that two flags
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{\bullet}
of polyhedra are (
\Gamma
-)
locally equivalent
if the
\Gamma
-rational neighborhoods of
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}
are exactly the
\Gamma
-rational neighborhoods of
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{\bullet}
. Similarly, we say that a two flags
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\bullet}
are (
\Gamma
-)
locally equivalent
if they have the same
\Gamma
-admissible neighborhoods.

Note that
\mathcal{Q}
is a
\Gamma
-rational neighborhood of
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}
if and only if
c(\mathcal{Q})
is a
\Gamma
-admissible neighborhood of
c(\mathcal{P}_{\bullet})
. Thus
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{\bullet}
are locally equivalent if and only if
c(\mathcal{P}_{\bullet})
and
c(\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{\bullet})
are.

The following lemma provides an alternate characterization of neighborhoods of flags. To prove it we will use the fact that a point
x
is in the relative interior of a cone
\mathcal{C}
of dimension
k
if and only if
x
can be written as a positive linear combination of
k
linearly independent points in
\mathcal{C}
. See
[
19
, Lemmas 2.8 and 2.9]
for the proof of the corresponding fact about polytopes. The proof for cones is directly analogous.

Lemma 3.6
.

A
\Gamma
-admissible cone
\mathcal{D}
is a neighborhood of
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
if and only if
\mathcal{D}
meets
\mathcal{C}_{i}\backslash\mathcal{C}_{i-1}
for
0\leq i\leq k
. In particular,
a
\Gamma
-rational polyhedron
\mathcal{Q}
is a neighborhood of
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}=(\mathcal{P}_{0}\leq\mathcal{P}_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\mathcal{P}_{k})
if and only if
\mathcal{P}_{0}\in\mathcal{Q}
and
\mathcal{Q}
meets
\mathcal{P}_{i}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{i-1}
, for
1\leq i\leq k
.

Proof.

One direction follows from the fact that
\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathcal{C}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{i}\backslash\mathcal{C}_{i-1}
.
For the other direction, we will proceed by induction on
k
. The case
k=0
is trivial because
\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathcal{C}_{0}=\mathcal{C}_{0}\backslash\mathcal{C}_{-1}
. Assume the statement holds for
k-1
. By the inductive hypothesis,
\mathcal{D}
meets the relative interior of
\mathcal{C}_{i}
for
1\leq i<k
, so we only need to show that
\mathcal{D}
meets the relative interior of
\mathcal{C}_{k}
. Pick a point
x\in\mathcal{D}\cap\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathcal{C}_{k-1}
. So
x
is a positive linear combination of
k
linearly independent points in
\mathcal{C}_{k-1}
. Because
\mathcal{D}
meets
\mathcal{C}_{k}\backslash\mathcal{C}_{k-1}
, we can pick a point
y\in\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{C}_{k}\backslash\mathcal{C}_{k-1}
. Then
x+y
is a positive linear combination of
k+1
linearly independent points in
\mathcal{C}_{k}
, so
x+y\in\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathcal{C}_{k}
. Since
x,y\in\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}
is a cone, we get
x+y\in\mathcal{D}\cap\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathcal{C}_{k}
.

The claim about polyhedra follows by considering
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}=c(\mathcal{P}_{\bullet})
and
\mathcal{D}=c(\mathcal{Q})
and noting that, because
\mathcal{C}_{0}
is a ray,
\mathcal{C}_{0}\backslash\mathcal{C}_{-1}=\mathcal{C}_{0}\backslash\{0\}
meets the cone
\mathcal{D}
if and only if
\mathcal{C}_{0}\backslash\{0\}
is contained in
\mathcal{D}
.
∎

We now work towards showing that every flag of cones is locally equivalent to one that is simplicial. To do this, we will need a technical lemma.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a cone and let
\mathcal{B}
be a facet of
\mathcal{C}
. Let
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
be a subcone of
\mathcal{B}
with such that
\dim\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\dim\mathcal{B}
and let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
be a subcone of
\mathcal{C}
with
\dim\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\dim\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
a face of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. Suppose that
v\in\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
and
w\in\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathcal{C}
. Then there is a real number
\epsilon>0
such that
v+\epsilon w\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{B}
is a face of
\mathcal{C}
, there is some
u\in M_{\mathbb{R}}
such that such that
\mathcal{C}\subseteq u^{\vee}
and
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{C}\cap u^{\perp}
. In particular,
v\in u^{\perp}
and
w\in u^{\vee}
. Since
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\cap u^{\perp}
is a facet of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
and
v\in\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
, the star of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
at
v
is
u^{\vee}
. That is,
u^{\vee}=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}-v)
. Since
w\in u^{\vee}
is nonzero, this means that we can write
w=r(z-v)
for some
r>0
and
z\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. So, letting
\epsilon=\frac{1}{r}
, we have
v+\epsilon w=v+(z-v)=z\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.
∎

This lemma will help us with an inductive argument. The following definition will also facilitate this argument.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}=(\mathcal{C}_{0}\leq\mathcal{C}_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\mathcal{C}_{k})
be a flag of cones.
For any
j\leq k
, the
truncation
of
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
at
j
is
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}^{(j)}=(\mathcal{C}_{0}\leq\mathcal{C}_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\mathcal{C}_{j})
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Any flag
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
of cones is locally equivalent to a simplicial flag
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}^{\prime}
of cones. Any flag
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}
of polyhedra is locally equivalent to a flag
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}^{\prime}
of cones such that
c(\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}^{\prime})
is simplicial.

Proof.

Consider any flag of cones
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}=(\mathcal{C}_{0}\leq\cdots\leq\mathcal{C}_{k})
and, for each
0\leq i\leq k
pick a ray
\rho_{i}
of
\mathcal{C}_{i}
not contained in
\mathcal{C}_{i-1}
. Define a simplicial flag
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}^{\prime}
of cones by setting
\mathcal{C}_{i}^{\prime}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{i}\rho_{j}
. Note that
\mathcal{C}_{i}^{\prime}
is a subcone of
\mathcal{C}_{i}
with
\dim\mathcal{C}_{i}^{\prime}=i+1=\dim\mathcal{C}_{i}
and
\mathcal{C}_{i}^{\prime}\backslash\mathcal{C}_{i-1}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{i}\backslash\mathcal{C}_{i-1}
. In particular, any
\Gamma
-admissible neighborhood of
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}^{\prime}
is a
\Gamma
-admissible neighborhood of
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
.

We now prove that any
\Gamma
-admissible neighborhood of
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
is a
\Gamma
-admissible neighborhood of
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}^{\prime}
by induction on
k
. For the base of
k=0
, note that
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\prime}=\mathcal{C}_{0}
, so we are done. Now assume that
k\geq 1
and the result is true for
k-1
. Suppose that
\mathcal{D}
is a
\Gamma
-admissible neighborhood of
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
. Then
\mathcal{D}
is a neighborhood of
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}^{(k-1)}
so, by the inductive hypothesis,
\mathcal{D}
is a neighborhood of
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}^{\prime(k-1)}
. Thus
\mathcal{D}
meets the relative interior of
\mathcal{C}_{i}^{\prime}
for
i<k
, and it suffices to show that
\mathcal{D}
meets
\mathcal{C}_{k}^{\prime}\backslash\mathcal{C}_{k-1}^{\prime}
. We can pick
v\in(\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathcal{C}_{k-1}^{\prime})\cap\mathcal{D}
and
w\in(\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathcal{C}_{k})\cap\mathcal{D}
. By Lemma
3.7
, there is an
\epsilon>0
such that
v+\epsilon w\in\mathcal{C}_{k}^{\prime}
. Since
\epsilon w\in\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathcal{C}_{k}
and
\mathcal{C}_{k-1}
is a proper face of
\mathcal{C}_{k}
,
v\in\mathcal{C}_{k-1}
gives us that
v+\epsilon w\notin\mathcal{C}_{k-1}\supseteq\mathcal{C}_{k-1}^{\prime}
. Thus,
v+\epsilon w\in\mathcal{C}_{k}^{\prime}\backslash\mathcal{C}_{k-1}^{\prime}
. Since
v,w\in\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}
is a cone,
v+\epsilon w\in\mathcal{D}
.

Given any flag
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}
of polyhedra, consider the corresponding flag
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}=c(\mathcal{P}_{\bullet})
of cones and let
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}^{\prime}
be as above. Since
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\prime}=\mathcal{C}_{0}
is not contained in
\{0\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
, there is a flag
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}^{\prime}
of polyhedra such that
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}^{\prime}=c(\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}^{\prime})
. Since
c(\mathcal{P}_{\bullet})
and
c(\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}^{\prime})
are locally equivalent, so are
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}^{\prime}
.
∎

3.3.
The filter of a prime congruence.

Now we introduce the next key player in this paper. For
finitely many Laurent polynomials

f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}\in S[M]
we define the
rational set

R(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})
to be

R(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})=\{x\in N_{\mathbb{R}}\,:\,f_{0}(x)\geq f_{i}(x),\text{ for }1\leq i\leq n\}.

Here, when
f=\sum_{u\in M}f_{u}\chi^{u}\in S[M]
and
x\in N_{\mathbb{R}}
, we let
f(x)=\max_{u\in M}\left(\log(f_{u})+_{\mathbb{R}}\left\langle x,u\right\rangle\right)
.

For any
f\in S[M]
, we can form the homogenized function
\widetilde{f}
given by

\widetilde{f}(r,x)=\max_{u\in M}\left(r\log(f_{u})+_{\mathbb{R}}\left\langle x,u\right\rangle\right)

for any
(r,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
. We let

\widetilde{R}(f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})=\left\{w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}\,:\,\widetilde{f}_{0}(w)\geq\widetilde{f}_{i}(w)\text{ for }1\leq i\leq n\right\}.

Note that
R(f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
is the restriction of
\widetilde{R}(f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
to
\{1\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Say
f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}\in S[M]
and write
f_{i}=\sum_{u\in M}f_{i,u}\chi^{u}
. Then

(1)

Theorem 3.17

Proof.

We need to check that
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{P}
satisfies the conditions in the definition of prime filter on a lattice, namely, (1)
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
is not the whole lattice, (2)
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
is not empty, (3) if
\widetilde{U}_{1},\widetilde{U}_{2}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}

\widetilde{U}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
and each
\widetilde{U}_{i}
is a
\Gamma
-admissible fan support set, then some
\widetilde{U}_{i_{0}}
is in
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
.

(1) Write
P=P_{\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}}
for some simplicial flag
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
of cones. If
\widetilde{U}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}

(2) We have

(3) Say

and

(4) This is immediate from the definition of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
.

(5) Suppose
\widetilde{U}=\cup_{i=1}^{m}\widetilde{U}_{i}
is in
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
with each
\widetilde{U}_{i}

\Gamma
-admissible fan support set; we want to show that some
\widetilde{U}_{i}
is in
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
. By writing each

is a
\Gamma
-admissible cone. Let

. Note that we can write

for some increasing sequence of integers

and terms

.
Since
\widetilde{U}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
, there are terms

such that
\widetilde{U}\supseteq\cap_{j=1}^{q}\widetilde{R}(1_{S},c_{j}\chi^{\mu_{j}}),
and

. For

, set

a_{l}^{-1}\chi^{-u_{l}}&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}.

, so the cone

. Also,

To finish the proof it is enough to see the following claim:

or

is contained in some

.

The proof for

is analogous. ∎

Theorem 3.20
.

For any prime
P
on
S[M]
, let
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{P}
and pick a simplicial flag
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
of cones such that

. Then, for any
\Gamma
-admissible cone
\widetilde{U}
in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
,
\widetilde{U}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
if and only if
\widetilde{U}
is a neighborhood of
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
.

If
P\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}{S[M]}
then we can write

for some flag
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}
of polyhedra and consider

\Gamma
-rational polyhedron

Proof.

We first consider
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
.
Suppose that
\widetilde{U}
is a neighborhood of
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
, so for
0\leq i\leq k

\vdots\\

is
\Gamma

\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\vdots\\

. Thus,
\widetilde{U}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
.

For the other direction, suppose
\widetilde{U}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
. We proceed to prove that

is a neighborhood of

by induction on

. For the base case of

gives us that there are

such that

, so

is a neighborhood of
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}=(\mathcal{C}_{0})
. For the inductive step, suppose that

and the statement is true for

.
Fix
a_{1}\chi^{u_{1}},\ldots,a_{n}\chi^{u_{n}}\in S[M]
such that
\widetilde{U}\supseteq\displaystyle\bigcap_{l=1}^{n}\widetilde{R}(1_{S},a_{l}\chi^{u_{l}})
and

. In particular,
\mathfrak{P}:=\displaystyle\bigcap_{l=1}^{n}\widetilde{R}(1_{S},a_{l}\chi^{u_{l}})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
and it suffices to show that

is a neighborhood of
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
.
Consider the truncation
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}^{(k-1)}
of
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
, let

, and let
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}=\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{P^{\prime}}
. Lemma
3.19
tells us that

\mathfrak{P}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}

and so, by the inductive hypothesis,

\mathfrak{P}

is a neighborhood of
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}^{(k-1)}
. Since

\mathfrak{P}

is a neighborhood of
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
if and only if it is a neighborhood of
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}^{k-1}
and meets
\mathcal{C}_{k}\backslash\mathcal{C}_{k-1}
, it now suffices to show that

\mathfrak{P}

meets
\mathcal{C}_{k}\backslash\mathcal{C}_{k-1}
.

For each
1\leq l\leq n
,
1_{\kappa(P)}\geq|a_{l}\chi^{u_{l}}|_{P}
, so

satisfies (
3.15
.
i_{l}
) for some

. If

then

, so

\mathfrak{P}\cap(\mathcal{C}_{k}\backslash\mathcal{C}_{k-1})=\left(\displaystyle\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\
j\neq l\end{subarray}}\widetilde{R}(1_{S},a_{j}\chi^{u_{j}})\right)\cap(\mathcal{C}_{k}\backslash\mathcal{C}_{k-1}).

Hence,
it suffices to show that
\displaystyle\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\\

meets

for all

.

Since
\mathfrak{P}
is a neighborhood of
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}^{(k-1)}
and

.
Also,

meets
\mathcal{C}_{k}\backslash\mathcal{C}_{k-1}
, concluding the proof that
\widetilde{U}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}

\widetilde{U}
is a neighborhood of
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
.

Now suppose

and consider

and

\displaystyle U\in\mathcal{F}

Remark 3.26

\Gamma
-rational polyhedral sets. To see why this is not true, consider the following example:

\displaystyle N_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}^{2},\text{ and }\sigma=\{(0,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x\geq 0\}

contains a polytope.

for some flag
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}
of polyhedra. Let

be any full-dimensional
\Gamma
-rational polytope with the point

in its interior. Then

meets the relative interior of any polyhedron containing

,
so

Definition 3.28
.

A
\Gamma
-rational polytopal set
is a finite union of
\Gamma
-rational polytopes.

The set of
\Gamma
-rational polytopal sets in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
forms a lattice when ordered by inclusion. In this lattice, meet and join are given by intersection and union, respectively.

Proposition 3.29
.

There is a bijection between the set of prime filters on the lattice of
\Gamma
-rational polyhedral sets, where the filter contains some polytope and the set of prime filters on the lattice of
\Gamma
-rational polytopal sets.

Proof.

Given a prime filter
\mathcal{F}
on the lattice of
\Gamma
-rational polytopal sets, it generates a filter
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
on the lattice of
\Gamma
-rational polyhedral sets such that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
contains a polytope, and a polytopal set is in
\mathcal{F}
if and only if it is in
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is also prime. Indeed, say
U=U_{1}\cup U_{2}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
with
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
both
\Gamma
-rational. Letting
V\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
be a polytope we have
U\cap V=(U_{1}\cap V)\cup(U_{2}\cap V)
with
U\cap V
,
U_{1}\cap V
, and
U_{2}\cap V
polytopal and
U\cap V\in\mathcal{F}
. So either,
U_{1}\cap V\in\mathcal{F}
or
U_{2}\cap V\in\mathcal{F}
, giving us that
U_{1}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
or
U_{2}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
.

We will show that the above map
\mathcal{F}\mapsto\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is a bijection by showing that its inverse is given by
\mathcal{G}\mapsto\mathcal{G}|_{\square}
where, given a prime filter
\mathcal{G}
on the lattice of
\Gamma
-rational polyhedral sets such that
\mathcal{G}
contains a polytope,
\mathcal{G}|_{\square}
denotes the restriction of
\mathcal{G}
to the lattice of
\Gamma
-rational polytopal sets. We first note that, for any filter
\mathcal{F}
on the lattice of
\Gamma
-rational polytopal sets, we have
(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})|_{\square}=\mathcal{F}
and, similarly, for any filter
\mathcal{G}
on the lattice of
\Gamma
-rational polyhedral sets such that
\mathcal{G}
contains a polytope, we have
(\mathcal{G}|_{\square})^{\prime}=\mathcal{G}
.

It remains only to show that if
\mathcal{G}
is a prime filter on the lattice of
\Gamma
-rational polyhedral sets such that
\mathcal{G}
contains a polytope, then the restriction of
\mathcal{G}
to the lattice of
\Gamma
-rational polytopal sets is a prime filter
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{G}|_{\square}
. We check that each of the five conditions for a prime filter is satisfied.

(1) Since every
\Gamma
-rational polyhedron contains a
\Gamma
-rational polytope and
\mathcal{G}
is not all of the lattice of
\Gamma
-rational polyhedral sets,
\mathcal{F}
is not all of the lattice of
\Gamma
-rational polytopal sets.

(2) By hypothesis,
\mathcal{F}
is nonempty.

(3) If
U_{1},U_{2}\in\mathcal{F}
, then
U_{1},U_{2}\in\mathcal{G}
and
U_{1},U_{2}
are polytopal, so
U_{1}\cap U_{2}\in\mathcal{G}
and
U_{1}\cap U_{2}
is polytopal, implying that
U_{1}\cap U_{2}\in\mathcal{F}
.

(4) If
U\in\mathcal{F}
and
V
is a
\Gamma
-rational polytopal set which contains
U
, then
V\supseteq U\in\mathcal{G}
, implying that
V\in\mathcal{G}
. Since
V
is polytopal this gives
V\in\mathcal{F}
.

(5) If
U=\cup_{i=1}^{m}U_{i}\in\mathcal{F}
and each
U_{i}
is a
\Gamma
-rational polytopal set, then
U\in\mathcal{G}
with each
U_{i}
a
\Gamma
-rational polyhedral set, so some
U_{i_{0}}\in\mathcal{G}
. Since
U_{i_{0}}
is polytopal, we have
U_{i_{0}}\in\mathcal{F}
.
∎

Given a prime filter
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
on the lattice of
\Gamma
-admissible fan support sets, we define a relation
\leq_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}
on
\operatorname{Terms}(S[M])
by saying that
a\chi^{u}\leq_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}b\chi^{\lambda}
if there is some
\widetilde{U}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
such that
\left\langle w,\begin{pmatrix}\log(a)\\
u\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle\leq\left\langle w,\begin{pmatrix}\log(b)\\
\lambda\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle
for all

, i.e.,
\widetilde{U}\subseteq\widetilde{R}(b\chi^{\lambda},a\chi^{u})
. Similarly, if
\mathcal{F}
is a filter on the lattice of
\Gamma

on

Proposition 3.30
.

For any prime filter
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
on the lattice of
\Gamma
-admissible fan support sets,
\leq_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}
is
\leq_{P}
for some prime congruence
P
on
S[M]
.
For any prime filter
\mathcal{F}
on the lattice of
\Gamma
-rational polyhedral sets such that
\mathcal{F}
contains a polytope, the relation
\leq_{\mathcal{F}}
is
\leq_{P}
for some
P\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}{S[M]}
.

Proof.

We begin by verifying that
\leq_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}
satisfies conditions (1), (2), and (3) in Proposition
2.9
.

(1) (
\leq_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}
is a total preorder) The relation is clearly reflexive. We need to show transitivity. Suppose
a\chi^{u}\leq_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}b\chi^{\lambda}
and
b\chi^{\lambda}\leq_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}c\chi^{\mu}
, so there are
\widetilde{U}_{1},\widetilde{U}_{2}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
such that

\displaystyle\left\langle w,\begin{pmatrix}\log(a)\\
u\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle\leq\left\langle w,\begin{pmatrix}\log(b)\\
\lambda\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle\text{ for all }w\in\widetilde{U}_{1}\text{ and }

\displaystyle\left\langle w,\begin{pmatrix}\log(b)\\
\lambda\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle\leq\left\langle w,\begin{pmatrix}\log(c)\\
\mu\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle\text{ for all }w\in\widetilde{U}_{2}.

Then
\widetilde{U}_{1}\cap\widetilde{U}_{2}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
and

\displaystyle\left\langle w,\begin{pmatrix}\log(a)\\
u\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle\leq\left\langle w,\begin{pmatrix}\log(c)\\
\mu\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle\text{ for all }w\in\widetilde{U}_{1}\cap\widetilde{U}_{2}

so
a\chi^{u}\leq_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}c\chi^{\mu}
. Thus,
\leq_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}
is a preorder. To see that it is a total preorder, suppose that
a\chi^{u}\not\leq_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}b\chi^{\lambda}
. Then for every
\widetilde{U}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
,
\widetilde{U}\not\subseteq\widetilde{R}(b\chi^{\lambda},a\chi^{u})
. We now consider two cases: either there is some
\widetilde{U}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
such that
\widetilde{U}\subseteq\widetilde{R}(a\chi^{u},b\chi^{\lambda})
or, for all
\widetilde{U}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
both
\widetilde{U}\cap\widetilde{R}(a\chi^{u},b\chi^{\lambda})
and
\widetilde{U}\cap\widetilde{R}(b\chi^{\lambda},a\chi^{u})
are non-empty.
In the first case
a\chi^{u}\geq_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}b\chi^{\lambda}
and we are done. In the second case, fix any
\widetilde{U}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
. Since

\widetilde{U}=(\widetilde{U}\cap\widetilde{R}(a\chi^{u},b\chi^{\lambda}))\cup(\widetilde{U}\cap\widetilde{R}(b\chi^{\lambda},a\chi^{u})),

we get that either
\widetilde{U}\cap\widetilde{R}(a\chi^{u},b\chi^{\lambda})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
or
\widetilde{U}\cap\widetilde{R}(b\chi^{\lambda},a\chi^{u})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
, because
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
is prime. However, since all
\widetilde{V}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
are not contained in
\widetilde{R}(b\chi^{\lambda},a\chi^{u})
, we must have
\widetilde{U}\cap\widetilde{R}(a\chi^{u},b\chi^{\lambda})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
. Thus, we have
a\chi^{u}\geq_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}b\chi^{\lambda}
.

(2) (
\leq_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}
is multiplicative) Let
a\chi^{u},b\chi^{\lambda}
and
c\chi^{\mu}
be terms in
S[M]
, with
a\chi^{u}\leq_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}b\chi^{\lambda}
. Then there exists
\widetilde{U}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
such that
\widetilde{U}\subseteq\widetilde{R}(b\chi^{\lambda},a\chi^{u})=\widetilde{R}(b\chi^{\lambda}c\chi^{\mu},a\chi^{u}c\chi^{\mu})
, so
a\chi^{u}c\chi^{\mu}\leq_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}b\chi^{\lambda}c\chi^{\mu}
.

(3) (
\leq_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}
respects the order on
S
) Let
a,b\in S^{\times}
such that
a\leq b
. Then every
\widetilde{U}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
is in
\widetilde{R}(b,a)=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Thus,
\leq_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}
is
\leq_{P}
for some prime congruence
P
on
S[M]
.

Now consider
\leq_{\mathcal{F}}
. Applying the above to
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}=\{c(U)\;:\;U\in\mathcal{F}\}
, we get that
\leq_{\mathcal{F}}
, which is the same as
\leq_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}
, satisfies conditions (1), (2), and (3) in Proposition
2.9
. So it suffices to show that
\leq_{\mathcal{F}}
satisfies condition (4) from that proposition.

Consider a term
a\chi^{u}\in S[M]
. Fixing a polytope
U\in\mathcal{F}
, the set
\{\log(a)+\left<\xi,u\right>\;:\;\xi\in U\}
has a minimum value
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. Pick
\beta<\gamma
in
\Gamma
and set
b=t^{\beta}\in S^{\times}
. Then
b<_{\mathcal{F}}a\chi^{u}
.
∎

, and because a prime congruence

Lemma 3.32
.

Consider elements
a,a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}\in S^{\times}
and
u,u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n}\in M
, and suppose that
\displaystyle\bigcap_{l=1}^{n}R(1_{S},a_{l}\chi^{u_{l}})
is nonempty. Then
\displaystyle\bigcap_{l=1}^{n}R(1_{S},a_{l}\chi^{u_{l}})\subseteq R(1_{S},a\chi^{u})
if and only if there are

m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, and
b\in S

with
b\geq 1_{S}
such that
b(a\chi^{u})^{m}=\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{n}(a_{l}\chi^{u_{l}})^{m_{l}}
.

Proof.

Note that
b(a\chi^{u})^{m}=\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{n}(a_{l}\chi^{u_{l}})^{m_{l}}
is equivalent to having

\log b+m\log a=\sum_{l=1}^{n}m_{l}\log a_{l}\text{ and }mu=\sum_{l=1}^{n}m_{l}u_{l},

which is in turn equivalent to the existence of nonnegative rational numbers
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}
, such that

\log a\leq\sum_{l=1}^{n}r_{l}\log a_{l}\text{ and }u=\sum_{l=1}^{n}r_{l}u_{l}.

By
[
5
, Proposition 2.3]
applied with the extension of ordered fields
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q}
, this happens exactly if

\langle x,-u\rangle\geq\log a
is valid for all
x
in

\{x\in N_{\mathbb{R}}\ :\ \langle x,-u_{l}\rangle\geq\log a_{l}\text{, for }1\leq l\leq n\}=\displaystyle\bigcap_{l=1}^{n}R(1_{S},a_{l}\chi^{u_{l}}),

i.e., if

\displaystyle\bigcap_{l=1}^{n}R(1_{S},a_{l}\chi^{u_{l}})\subseteq R(1_{S},a\chi^{u})
.
∎

,

, the proof of the following proposition requires case-work.

Proposition 3.34
.

For any prime congruence
P
on
S[M]
,
\widetilde{R}(1_{S},a\chi^{u})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{P}
if and only if
1_{\kappa(P)}\geq|a\chi^{u}|_{P}
. If
P\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]
then
R(1_{S},a\chi^{u})\in\mathcal{F}_{P}
if and only if
1_{\kappa(P)}\geq|a\chi^{u}|_{P}
.

Proof.

The “if” directions in the above statements follow immediately from the definitions of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{p}
and
\mathcal{F}_{P}
. For the other directions, note that the statements are trivially true if
a=0_{S}
, so assume
a\neq 0_{S}
.

Suppose
P\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]
and
R(1_{S},a\chi^{u})\in\mathcal{F}_{P}
. So there are terms
a_{1}\chi^{u_{1}},\ldots,a_{n}\chi^{u_{n}}
such that
R(1_{S},a\chi^{u})\supseteq\cap_{l=1}^{n}R(1_{S},a_{l}\chi^{u_{l}})
and
|a_{l}\chi^{u_{l}}|_{P}\leq 1_{\kappa(P)}
for each
l
. Lemma
3.13
tells us that
\cap_{l=1}^{n}R(1_{S},a_{l}\chi^{u_{l}})
is nonempty. So, by Lemma
3.32
, there are
m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, and
b\in S
with
b\geq 1_{S}
such that
b(a\chi^{u})^{m}=\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{n}(a_{l}\chi^{u_{l}})^{m_{l}}
. Then
b^{-1}\leq 1_{S}
and

|a\chi^{u}|_{P}^{m}=|b^{-1}|_{P}\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{n}|a_{l}\chi^{u_{l}}|_{P}^{m_{l}}\leq 1_{\kappa(P)},

so
|a\chi^{u}|_{P}\leq 1_{\kappa(P)}
.

Now consider a general prime congruence
P
on
S[M]
, and suppose
\widetilde{R}(1_{S},a\chi^{u})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{P}
. There are terms
a_{1}\chi^{u_{1}},\ldots,a_{n}\chi^{u_{n}}
such that
\widetilde{R}(1_{S},a\chi^{u})\supseteq\cap_{l=1}^{n}\widetilde{R}(1_{S},a_{l}\chi^{u_{l}})
and
|a_{l}\chi^{u_{l}}|_{P}\leq 1_{\kappa(P)}
for each
l
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}=(\mathcal{C}_{0}\leq\cdots\leq\mathcal{C}_{k})
be a simplicial flag of cones such that
P=P_{\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}}
. Theorem
3.20
tells us that there is some
\widetilde{U}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{P}
with
\widetilde{U}\subseteq\{0\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
if and only if
\mathcal{C}_{k}\subseteq\{0\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
. We consider two cases, as to whether this happens or not.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}_{k}\not\subseteq\{0\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
. Since
\widetilde{R}(1_{S},a_{1}\chi^{u_{1}},\ldots,a_{n}\chi^{u_{n}})=\cap_{l=1}^{n}\widetilde{R}(1_{S},a_{l}\chi^{u_{l}})
and
\widetilde{R}(1_{S},a\chi^{u})
are in
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{P}
, neither of them is contained in
\{0\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
. Therefore,
\widetilde{R}(1_{S},a\chi^{u})=c(R(1_{S},a\chi^{u}))
and
\widetilde{R}(1_{S},a_{1}\chi^{u_{1}},\ldots,a_{n}\chi^{u_{n}})=c(R(1_{S},a_{1}\chi^{u_{1}},\ldots,a_{n}\chi^{u_{n}}))
. So

R(1_{S},a\chi^{u})\supseteq R(1_{S},a_{1}\chi^{u_{1}},\ldots,a_{n}\chi^{u_{n}})=\cap_{l=1}^{n}R(1_{S},a_{l}\chi^{u_{l}})

with
\cap_{l=1}^{n}R(1_{S},a_{l}\chi^{u_{l}})
nonempty. We can now apply Lemma
3.32
and, as in the previous case, conclude that
|a\chi^{u}|_{P}\leq 1_{\kappa(P)}
.

Finally, suppose that
\mathcal{C}_{k}\subseteq\{0\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
. Then, by the definition of
P_{\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}}
,
|b|_{P}=1_{\kappa(P)}
for any
b\in S^{\times}
. Note that
\widetilde{R}(1_{S},a\chi^{u})\cap(\{0\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}})=\{0\}\times u^{\vee}
, where
u^{\vee}=\{\zeta\in N_{\mathbb{R}}\;:\;\left\langle\zeta,u\right\rangle\leq 0_{\mathbb{R}}\}
. Similarly,
\cap_{l=1}^{n}\widetilde{R}(1_{S},a\chi^{u})\cap(\{0\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}})=\{0\}\times\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}^{\vee}
, so we have that
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}^{\vee}\subseteq u^{\vee}
. So, by the
\mathbb{Q}
-version of
[
19
, Proposition 1.9]
,
there are
m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
mu=\sum_{l=1}^{n}m_{l}u_{l}
. Thus,

|a\chi^{u}|_{P}^{m}=|\chi^{u}|_{P}^{m}=\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{n}|\chi^{u_{l}}|_{P}^{m_{l}}=\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{n}|a_{l}\chi^{u_{l}}|_{P}^{m_{l}}\leq 1_{\kappa(P)},

so
|a\chi^{u}|_{P}\leq 1_{\kappa(P)}
.
∎

Theorem 3.35
.

The map
P\mapsto\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{P}
gives a bijection from the set of prime congruences on
S[M]
to the set of prime filters on the lattice of
\Gamma
-admissible fan support sets. The inverse map is given by
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}\mapsto P_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}
.

The map
P\mapsto\mathcal{F}_{P}
gives a bijection from
\operatorname{Cont}_{S}{S[M]}
to the set of prime filters on the lattice of
\Gamma
-rational polyhedral sets where the filter contains some polytope. The inverse map is given by
\mathcal{F}\mapsto P_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Proof.

Note that, by Theorem
3.17
, Lemma
3.27
, and Definition
3.31
, all of these maps are well-defined.

We show that
P\mapsto\mathcal{F}_{P}
and
\mathcal{F}\mapsto P_{\mathcal{F}}
are inverses. The proof for
P\mapsto\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{P}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}\mapsto P_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}
is analogous.

To show that
P=P_{\mathcal{F}_{P}}
for any
P\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}S[M]
, it suffices to show that
a\chi^{u}\leq_{P}b\chi^{\lambda}
if and only if
a\chi^{u}\leq_{P_{\mathcal{F}_{P}}}b\chi^{\lambda}
for any terms
a\chi^{u},b\chi^{\lambda}\in S[M]
. Since
b\chi^{\lambda}
is a unit in
S[M]
, we can divide both inequalities by
b\chi^{\lambda}
, and so we may assume without loss of generality that
b\chi^{\lambda}=1_{S}
. By Proposition
3.34
, we know that
a\chi^{u}\leq_{P}1_{S}
if and only if
R(1_{S},a\chi^{u})\in\mathcal{F}_{P}
.
By the definitions of
P_{\mathcal{F}_{P}}
and
\leq_{\mathcal{F}_{P}}
,

R(1_{S},a\chi^{u})\in\mathcal{F}_{P}
if and only if
a\chi^{u}\leq_{\mathcal{F}_{P}}1_{S}
which, in turn, is the same as having
a\chi^{u}\leq_{P_{\mathcal{F}_{P}}}1_{S}
.

Now fix a prime filter
\mathcal{F}
on the lattice of
\Gamma
-rational polyhedral sets such that
\mathcal{F}
contains a polytope; we wish to show that
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{P_{\mathcal{F}}}
. Since
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}_{P_{\mathcal{F}}}
are prime filters on the lattice of
\Gamma
-rational polyhedral sets, they are determined by which
\Gamma
-rational polyhedra they contain. Using the fact that
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}_{P_{\mathcal{F}}}
are both filters, we then see that they are determined by which
\Gamma
-rational half-spaces they contain. But every
\Gamma
-rational half-space can be written as
R(1_{S},a\chi^{u})
for some term
a\chi^{u}\in S[M]
, so it suffices to show that
R(1_{S},a\chi^{u})\in\mathcal{F}
if and only if
R(1_{S},a\chi^{u})\in\mathcal{F}_{P_{\mathcal{F}}}
. By Proposition
3.34
,
R(1_{S},a\chi^{u})\in\mathcal{F}_{P_{\mathcal{F}}}
if and only if
a\chi^{u}\leq_{P_{\mathcal{F}}}1_{S}
. By the definitions of
P_{\mathcal{F}}
and
\leq_{\mathcal{F}}
, this happens if and only if
R(1_{S},a\chi^{u})\in\mathcal{F}
.
∎

Corollary 3.39

-rational polyhedral sets to the set of prime filters
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
on the lattice of
\Gamma
-admissible fan support sets such that every
\widetilde{U}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
is not contained in
\{0\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
. So it suffices to show that, if
P
is a prime congruence on
S[M]
, the map
S\to S[M]\to\kappa(P)
is injective if and only if every
\widetilde{U}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{P}
is not contained in
\{0\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Pick a simplicial flag
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}=(\mathcal{C}_{0}\leq\cdots\leq\mathcal{C}_{k})
of cones such that
P=P_{\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}}
and fix any
w_{i}\in\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathcal{C}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{i}\backslash\mathcal{C}_{i-1}
for
0\leq i\leq k
. Thus,
\begin{pmatrix}w_{0}\\
\vdots\\
w_{k}\end{pmatrix}
is a defining matrix for
P
, and so the map
S\to S[M]\to\kappa(P)
is injective if and only if the first column of this matrix is not all zero, i.e., if
w_{i}\notin\{0\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
for some
i
. Since
w_{i}\in\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathcal{C}_{i}
,
w_{i}\notin\{0\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
if and only if
\mathcal{C}_{i}\not\subseteq\{0\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
. So
S\to S[M]\to\kappa(P)
is injective if and only if
\mathcal{C}_{k}\not\subseteq\{0\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
.

But, by Theorem
3.20
,
\mathcal{C}_{k}\not\subseteq\{0\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
if and only if every
\widetilde{U}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{P}
is not contained in
\{0\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
.
∎

Remark 3.40

Under the bijection of Corollary
3.39
, the prime filter given in Remark

corresponds to the prime congruence on
\mathbb{T}[x^{\pm 1},y^{\pm 1}]
with defining matrix

1&0&0\end{pmatrix}

Corollary 3.41
.

Let
M\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
There are explicit bijections between

(1)

the set of all prime congruences on
\mathbb{B}[M]
,

(2)

the set of flags
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
of cones in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
modulo rational local equivalence,

(3)

the set of prime filters on the lattice of rational fan support sets in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
,

(4)

the set of all monomial preorders on a Laurent polynomial ring in
n
variables.

The bijections between (1), (2), and (3) are given by applying the bijections of Corollary
3.38
as follows. Given a prime congruence
P
on
\mathbb{B}[M]
, view it as a prime congruence on
\mathbb{T}[M]
by pulling back along the the map
\mathbb{T}[M]\to\mathbb{B}[M]
. Given a flag of cones in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
, consider it as a flag of cones in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
by adding a first coordinate
0
. Given a prime filter on the lattice of rational fan support sets in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
, push it forward to a filter base on the lattice of
\mathbb{R}
-admissible fan support sets in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
via the map
N_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
adjoining a first coordinate
0
, and let this filter base generate a filter. The map from (1) to (4) is given by sending a prime congruence
P
to the preorder
\leq_{P}
on
\operatorname{Terms}(\mathbb{B}[M])=M
.

Proof.

The primes on
\mathbb{T}[M]
which are pullbacks of primes on
\mathbb{B}[M]
are those that are given by matrices with first column zero. This shows that the primes on
\mathbb{B}[M]
are in bijection with local equivalence classes of flags of cones contained in
\{0\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
modulo local equivalence, i.e., flags of cones in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
modulo rational local equivalence. By Corollaries
3.38
and
3.39
, these are in bijection with the set of those prime filters on the lattice of
\mathbb{R}
-admissible fan support sets whose restriction to
\{1\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
is not a prime filter because this restriction contains the empty set. The only way this can occur is if the filter contains a set that is contained in
\{0\}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
. This, in turn, happens if and only if the filter is generated by the pushforward of a prime filter on the lattice of rational fan support sets in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
along the inclusion map
N_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
of the zero-slice.
∎

The following corollary is an application of the above results. It provides an explicit criterion for when two matrices define the same prime.

Corollary 3.42
.

Let
C
and
C^{\prime}
be real matrices of sizes
k\times n
and
k^{\prime}\times n
, respectively. Suppose that
C
and
C^{\prime}
define prime congruences on
S[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
, i.e., the first columns of
C
and
C^{\prime}
are lexicographically at least the zero vector. Use downward gaussian elimination and removal of rows of zeros on
C
and
C^{\prime}
to obtain matrices
\widetilde{C}
and
\widetilde{C}^{\prime}
, respectively, whose first columns have all entries non-negative and whose rows are linearly independent. Then
C
and
C^{\prime}
define the same prime congruence on
S[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
if and only if the flags
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}(\widetilde{C})
and
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}(\widetilde{C}^{\prime})
of cones are locally equivalent.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.7
, the prime congruences that
C
and
C^{\prime}
define are
P_{\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}(\widetilde{C})}
and
P_{\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}(\widetilde{C}^{\prime})}
, respectively. By Corollary
3.36
, these are equal if and only if
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}(\widetilde{C})
and
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}(\widetilde{C}^{\prime})
are locally equivalent.
∎

We now use Proposition
3.34
to show that the smallest dimension of a polyhedron in
\mathcal{F}_{P}
is an algebraic invariant of
P\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}{S[M]}
.

Proposition 3.43
.

For any
P\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}{S[M]}
,

\min\{\dim U:U\in\mathcal{F}_{P}\}=\operatorname{rk}(\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times})=\operatorname{rk}(M)-\operatorname{ht}(P)=\operatorname{tr.deg}(\kappa(P)/S).

Proof.

Lemma
2.13
gives us the second equality in the proposition. By
[
6
, Proposition B.11]
, we get the equality with
\operatorname{tr.deg}(\kappa(P)/S)
.

Note that
\min\{\dim U:U\in\mathcal{F}_{P}\}
is attained for some
\Gamma
-rational polyhedron
U\in\mathcal{F}_{P}
, for which
\dim U
is the dimension of the smallest
\Gamma
-rational affine space that contains
U
.
In particular, if we let
\mathcal{H}
be the subgroup of
\Gamma\times M
consisting of those
(\alpha,u)
such that
the hyperplane
\{\xi\in N_{\mathbb{R}}\;:\;\alpha+\left\langle\xi,u\right\rangle=0\}

is in
\mathcal{F}_{P}
, then
\min\{\dim U:U\in\mathcal{F}_{P}\}=\dim N_{\mathbb{R}}-\operatorname{rk}\mathcal{H}
.
Note that, for any term
a\chi^{u}\in S[M]
,
we have that
(\log(a),u)
is in
\mathcal{H}

exactly if
R(1_{S},a\chi^{u})
and
R(1_{S},a^{-1}\chi^{-u})
are both in
\mathcal{F}_{P}
. By Proposition
3.34
, this happens exactly if
1_{\kappa(P)}=|a\chi^{u}|_{P}
.

Choose a defining matrix for
P\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}{S[M]}
of the form

\begin{pmatrix}1&\xi_{0}\\
0&\xi_{1}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
0&\xi_{k}\end{pmatrix}

for some
\xi_{0},\ldots,\xi_{k}\in N_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then, looking at the conditions (
3.3
.0),…,(
3.3
.
k
) from Section
3.3
, we see that
1_{\kappa(P)}=|a\chi^{u}|_{P}
if and only if

log(a)+\left<\xi_{0},u\right>=\left<\xi_{1},u\right>=\cdots=\left<\xi_{k-1},u\right>=\left<\xi_{k},u\right>=0.

We now see that the projection
\Gamma\times M\rightarrow M
maps
\mathcal{H}
isomorphically onto its image. Indeed, the values of
\xi_{0}
and
u
uniquely determine
\log(a)
. So

\displaystyle\operatorname{rk}(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{rk}\left\{u\in M\;:\;\left<\xi_{0},u\right>\in\Gamma\text{ and }\left<\xi_{i},u\right>=0,\text{ for }1\leq i\leq k\right\}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{rk}(\ker(M\rightarrow\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times}))=\operatorname{rk}M-\operatorname{rk}(\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times}),

where the final equality holds because the map
M\to\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times}
is surjective. Thus,

\displaystyle\min\{\dim U\;:\;U\in\mathcal{F}_{P}\}

\displaystyle=\dim N_{\mathbb{R}}-\operatorname{rk}\mathcal{H}=\operatorname{rk}M-\operatorname{rk}(\ker(M\rightarrow\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times}))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{rk}\kappa(P)^{\times}/S^{\times}.

∎

The previous result finally allows us to prove the geometric criterion of when
\leq_{P}
is a valuated monomial
order
.
A flag
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}=(\mathcal{P}_{0}\leq\mathcal{P}_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\mathcal{P}_{k})
of polyhedra in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
is called
complete
if
k=\dim N_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proposition 3.44
.

For any
P\in\operatorname{Cont}_{S}{S[M]}
,
\leq_{P}
is an
order
on
\operatorname{Terms}(S[M])
if and only if we can write
P=P_{\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}}
with
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}
a complete flag in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}
is complete, then any
\Gamma
-rational neighborhood of
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}
must be full-dimensional because it contains
\dim N_{\mathbb{R}}+1
affinely independent points.
So
\dim_{N_{\mathbb{R}}}=\min\{\dim U:U\in\mathcal{F}_{P}\}=\operatorname{rk}(M)-\operatorname{ht}(P)
. Thus
\operatorname{ht}(P)=0
and so, by
[
6
, Corollary 9.15]

\leq_{P}
is an order on
\operatorname{Terms}(S[M])
.

For the other direction, suppose that
\leq_{P}
is an order on
\operatorname{Terms}(S[M])
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}=(\mathcal{P}_{0}\leq\mathcal{P}_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\mathcal{P}_{k})
be a flag of polyhedra in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
such that
P=P_{\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}}
and
k
is as large as possible. Now assume, for contradiction, that
k<\dim N_{\mathbb{R}}
.
For
0\leq i\leq k
pick
\xi_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{i}\backslash\mathcal{P}_{i-1}
, so

C=\begin{pmatrix}1&\xi_{0}\\
1&\xi_{1}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
1&\xi_{k}\end{pmatrix}

is a defining matrix for
P
. Since
k<\dim N_{\mathbb{R}}
, there is a point
\xi_{k+1}
which is affinely independent of
\xi_{0},\ldots,\xi_{k}
. So the matrix

C^{\prime}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lx@intercol\hfil\hskip-5.69054ptC\hfil\lx@intercol\\
\hline\cr 1&\xi_{k+1}\end{array}\right)

defines a flag
\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}^{\prime}=(\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\prime}\leq\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime}\leq\cdots\leq\mathcal{P}_{k+1}^{\prime})
of simplices in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.
For any two exponent vectors
\mathfrak{u}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{u}_{2}
we have
C^{\prime}\mathfrak{u}_{1}\leq_{\mathrm{lex}}C^{\prime}\mathfrak{u}_{2}
if and only if
C\mathfrak{u}_{1}\leq_{\mathrm{lex}}C\mathfrak{u}_{2}
because
\leq_{P}
is an order. Thus
P_{\mathcal{P}_{\bullet}^{\prime}}=P
, contradicting the maximality of
k
.
∎

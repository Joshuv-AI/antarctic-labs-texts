A serendipity fully discrete div-div complex on polygonal meshes
Michele Botti, Daniele A. Di Pietro, Marwa Salah

Abstract
In this work we address the reduction of face degrees of freedom (DOFs) for discrete elasticity complexes. Specifically, using serendipity techniques, we develop a reduced version of a recently introduced two-dimensional complex arising from traces of the three-dimensional elasticity complex. The keystone of the reduction process is a new estimate of symmetric tensor-valued polynomial fields in terms of boundary values, completed with suitable projections of internal values for higher degrees. We prove an extensive set of new results for the original complex and show that the reduced complex has the same homological and analytical properties as the original one. This paper also contains an appendix with proofs of general Poincaré–Korn-type inequalities for hybrid fields. Key words. Discrete de Rham method, serendipity, compatible discretisations, mixed formulation, div-div complex, biharmonic equation, Kirchhoff–Love plates MSC2010. 74K20, 74S05, 65N30

Introduction

The development of computationally viable discrete elasticity complexes is a long-standing problem in numerical analysis.
Finite element versions of the elasticity complex typically require a large number of degrees of freedom (DOFs) to deal with the symmetry constraint on tensor-valued fields

In this work, we study DOFs reduction through

Serendipity techniques exploit the information on the boundary to fix the values of (a subset of) internal DOFs while preserving polynomial consistency.
When working with discrete complexes, this reduction must be carefully designed in order to preserve key properties of the original complex.

Proposition 5

(Estimate of the

Let

and, for a given polynomial degree

Proof.

Let

Inserting
\overline{\varphi}_{E}

(3.27)

To estimate the first term we start with a Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality to write
\mathfrak{T}_{1}\lesssim h_{E}\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(E)}
, where
\varphi^{\prime}\coloneq\partial_{\boldsymbol{t}_{E}}\varphi
.
We then notice that, for all

inside the integral is justified by its definition after observing that
\psi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}^{m-2}(E)
and
\omega_{EV}
is the orientation of
V
relative to
E
.
Taking
\psi=\varphi^{\prime}
, using Cauchy–Schwarz and discrete trace inequalities in the right-hand side, simplifying and multiplying by
h_{E}
, we obtain

h_{E}\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(E)}\lesssim\|\pi_{\mathcal{P},E}^{m-2}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(E)}+h_{E}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}_{E}}|\varphi(\boldsymbol{x}_{V})|

\mathfrak{T}_{1}\lesssim\|\pi_{\mathcal{P},E}^{m-2}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(E)}+h_{E}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}_{E}}|\varphi(\boldsymbol{x}_{V})|.

Appendix A
Poincaré–Korn type inequalities in hybrid spaces

The proof of the functional inequality for hybrid vector fields that is used to establish point (i) of Lemma
3
(see Section
3.4.2
) is presented below. First, we introduce some additional notations concerning tensor calculus in three dimensions. For a bounded, Lipschitz domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
and for a sufficiently regular tensor field
\boldsymbol{P}:D\to\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
, we define

\TCURL\boldsymbol{P}\coloneq\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{2}P_{13}-\partial_{3}P_{12}&\partial_{3}P_{11}-\partial_{1}P_{13}&\partial_{1}P_{12}-\partial_{2}P_{11}\\
\partial_{2}P_{23}-\partial_{3}P_{22}&\partial_{3}P_{21}-\partial_{1}P_{23}&\partial_{1}P_{22}-\partial_{2}P_{21}\\
\partial_{2}P_{33}-\partial_{3}P_{32}&\partial_{3}P_{31}-\partial_{1}P_{33}&\partial_{1}P_{32}-\partial_{2}P_{31}\end{pmatrix}.

For later use, we also introduce the space
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{RM}}_{3}\coloneq\{\boldsymbol{a}\times\boldsymbol{x}+\boldsymbol{b}\,:\,\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\}
of three-dimensional rigid-body motions and the operator
\ANTI:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
given by

\ANTI\boldsymbol{a}\coloneq\begin{pmatrix}0&-a_{3}&a_{2}\\
a_{3}&0&-a_{1}\\
-a_{2}&a_{1}&0\end{pmatrix}\hskip 11.49994pt\forall\boldsymbol{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

The discrete functional inequalities below hinge on
[
21
, Theorem 3.3]
, which the authors refer to as
incompatible Korn type inequality for
L^{p}
-regular tensor fields
. For the sake of clarity, we recall the statement of this key result.

Lemma 24

(Incompatible Korn type inequality)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded, Lipschitz domain and let
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then, there exists
C_{\rm IK}>0
depending only on
D
and
p
such that, for all
\boldsymbol{P}\in L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})
,

\inf_{\boldsymbol{w}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{RM}}_{3}}\|\boldsymbol{P}-\ANTI\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}\leq C_{\rm IK}\left(\|\SYM\boldsymbol{P}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}+\|\SYM\TCURL\boldsymbol{P}\|_{\boldsymbol{W}^{-1,p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}\right).

(A.1)

It has been observed in
[
22
,
21
]
that the previous result can be seen as a generalisation of both the Poincaré–Wirtinger and Korn’s second inequalities. In the following Proposition, we apply Lemma
24
to some particular cases in which the tensor field
\boldsymbol{P}
is skew-symmetric and assuming
p=2
.

Proposition 25

(Poincaré–Korn inequalities for
L^{2}
-regular vector fields)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
n\in\{2,3\}
be a bounded, Lipschitz domain. Then, the following inequalities hold:

\displaystyle\inf_{\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{0}(D;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\boldsymbol{u}-\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(D;\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{\rm IK}\|\GRAD\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}(D;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})}

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{u}\in\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(D;\mathbb{R}^{d})\text{ with }1\leq d\leq n;

(A.2)

\displaystyle\inf_{\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{RT}}^{1}(D)}\|\boldsymbol{v}-\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(D;\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{\rm IK}\|\SYM\CURL\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}(D;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(D;\mathbb{R}^{2})\text{ with }n=2;

(A.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\inf_{\overline{\boldsymbol{w}}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{RM}}_{d}}\|\boldsymbol{w}-\overline{\boldsymbol{w}}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(D;\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{\rm IK}\|\SYM\GRAD\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}(D;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{w}\in\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(D;\mathbb{R}^{d})\text{ with }d=n.

Proof.

In order to establish (
A.2
) for
1\leq d\leq n\leq 3
, it suffices to consider the case

and

. Hence, we let

and apply Lemma
24
with
\boldsymbol{P}
such that

\SYM\boldsymbol{P}=\boldsymbol{0}

Moreover, it is observed that

\SYM\TCURL\boldsymbol{P}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&-\partial_{2}u&-\partial_{3}u\\

As a result, we get the conclusion.

We now proceed with the proof of (

\boldsymbol{P}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&w_{2}\\
0&0&-w_{1}\\
-w_{2}&w_{1}&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and }\;\boldsymbol{P}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-w_{3}&w_{2}\\
w_{3}&0&-w_{1}\\
-w_{2}&w_{1}&0\end{pmatrix},

for the case
n=d=2
and
n=d=3
, respectively.
∎

We are now ready to establish the main result of this Section. For the sake of simplicity, we detail the result only for the two dimensional case, but we refer to Remark
27
for some possible generalisations.

Proposition 26

(Poincaré–Korn inequalities for hybrid vector fields)
.

Let

\underline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}\coloneq\Big\{\begin{aligned} &\underline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}=\big((\boldsymbol{u}_{T})_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}},(\boldsymbol{u}_{E})_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}},\big)\,:\,\text{$\boldsymbol{u}_{T}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{k}(T;\mathbb{R}^{2})\ \forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}$},\;\text{$\boldsymbol{u}_{E}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{k}(E;\mathbb{R}^{2})\ \forall E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}$}\Big\}\end{aligned}

and, for all
\underline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}
, denote by
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
the piecewise polynomial field on
\mathcal{T}_{h}
such that
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})_{|T}\coloneq\boldsymbol{u}_{T}
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
Then, there is a constant
C_{\rm PK}>0
, only depending on
\Omega
and the mesh regularity parameter, such that

1.

For all
\underline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}
satisfying
\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}=\boldsymbol{0}
,

\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\leq C_{\rm PK}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\|\GRAD\boldsymbol{u}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}^{2}+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}h_{T}^{-1}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{T}-\boldsymbol{u}_{E}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(E;\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\right);

(A.5)

2.

For all
\underline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}
satisfying
\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{w}=0
for all
\boldsymbol{w}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{RT}}^{1}(\Omega)
,

\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\leq C_{\rm PK}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\|\SYM\CURL\boldsymbol{u}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}^{2}+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}h_{T}^{-1}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{T}-\boldsymbol{u}_{E}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(E;\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\right);

(A.6)

3.

For all
\underline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}
satisfying
\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{w}=0
for all
\boldsymbol{w}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{RM}}_{2}
,

\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\leq C_{\rm PK}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\|\SYM\GRAD\boldsymbol{u}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}^{2}+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}h_{T}^{-1}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{T}-\boldsymbol{u}_{E}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(E;\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\right).

(A.7)

Proof.

We only detail the proof of (
A.6
), which is used in the proof of Lemma
3
, since (
A.5
) and (
A.7
) can be obtained by reasoning in a similar way.
Let
\underline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}
and observe that the condition
\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{w}=0
for all
\boldsymbol{w}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{RT}}^{1}(\Omega)
implies

\inf_{\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{RT}}^{1}(\Omega)}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Therefore, applying the second inequality in Proposition
25
, it follows that

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\SYM\CURL\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}=\sup_{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}),\,\|\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}=1}\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\cdot\left(\VROT\boldsymbol{\eta}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}),\,\|\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}=1}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(-\int_{T}\CURL\boldsymbol{u}_{T}:\boldsymbol{\eta}+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}\omega_{TE}\int_{E}\boldsymbol{u}_{T}\cdot(\boldsymbol{\eta}\,\boldsymbol{t}_{E})\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}),\,\|\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}=1}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(-\int_{T}\SYM\CURL\boldsymbol{u}_{T}:\boldsymbol{\eta}+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}\omega_{TE}\int_{E}(\boldsymbol{u}_{T}-\boldsymbol{u}_{E})\cdot\boldsymbol{\eta}\,\boldsymbol{t}_{E}\right),

where we have integrated by parts element by element and used the fact that
\boldsymbol{\eta}
has continuous tangential traces across interedges and vanishing tangential traces on the boundary in order to insert
\boldsymbol{u}_{E}
into the boundary term.
Applying a Cauchy–Schwarz inequality on the integrals and invoking a discrete Cauchy–Schwarz inequality on the sum over

T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we infer that

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}),\,\|\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}=1}\left(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\SYM\CURL\boldsymbol{u}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}

\displaystyle+\left(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}h_{T}^{-1}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{T}-\boldsymbol{u}_{E}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(E;\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}h_{T}\|\boldsymbol{\eta}\,\boldsymbol{t}_{E}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(E;\mathbb{R}^{2})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left[\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\|\SYM\CURL\boldsymbol{u}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}^{2}+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}\hskip-2.84526pth_{T}^{-1}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{T}-\boldsymbol{u}_{E}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(E;\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\times\cancelto{1}{\sup_{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}),\,\|\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}=1}\|\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{S})}},

where, in the second inequality, we have used the continuous trace inequality
[
17
, Lemma 1.31]
.
∎

Remark 27

(Generalisations)
.

The results of Proposition
26
admit several extensions that we have decided not to include for the sake of brevity.
First, (
A.5
) and (
A.7
) can also be established in the three-dimensional case simply by replacing the interedges with interfaces.
Second, since the starting argument given by Lemma
24
holds for all Lebesgue indices
p\in(1,\infty)
, we can generalise the discrete Poincaré–Korn inequalities to the Banach setting.
The main modification required in the proof consists in replacing Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities with suitable versions of Hölder inequalities.
Finally, we notice that in the proof of Proposition
26
we are not using any inverse inequality requiring the hybrid vector fields to be polynomials. Thus, the previous Poincaré–Korn inequalities can be extended to vector fields with piecewise Sobolev regularity.

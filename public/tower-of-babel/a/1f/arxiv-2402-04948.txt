First Ruelle resonance for an Anosov flow with smooth potential
Tristan Humbert

Abstract
. We combine methods from microlocal analysis and dimension theory to study resonances with largest real part for an Anosov flow with smooth real valued potential. We show that the resonant states are closely related to special systems of measures supported on the stable manifolds introduced by Climenhaga in [ 7 ] . As a result, we relate the presence of the resonances on the critical axis to mixing properties of the flow with respect to certain equilibrium measures and show that these equilibrium measures can be reconstructed from the spectral theory of the Anosov flow.

1.3.
Ruelle zeta function.

The resolvents acting on
\mathscr{E}_{0}^{k}
are linked to the (weighted)
Ruelle zeta function
:

(1.10)

\zeta_{V}(\lambda):=\prod_{{\gamma\in\Gamma^{\sharp}}}\big(1-e^{-(\lambda+V_{\gamma})T_{\gamma}}\big),\ \ V_{\gamma}:=\frac{1}{T_{\gamma}}\int_{0}^{T_{\gamma}}V(\gamma(t))dt,

where
\Gamma^{\sharp}
is the set of primitive geodesics and
T_{\gamma}
denotes the period of the closed geodesic
\gamma
. This function can be shown to be convergent and holomorphic in a half plane
\{\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)\gg 1\}
. In a celebrated paper
[
17
]
, Giulietti, Liverani and Pollicott proved that the function
\zeta_{R}^{V}
admits a meromorphic extension to the whole complex plane. Another proof, using microlocal analysis, was given by Dyatlov and Zworski in
[
12
]
.

More precisely, let us introduce the
Poincaré
map
\mathcal{P}:\gamma\in\Gamma\mapsto\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}:=d\varphi_{-T_{\gamma}}|_{E_{s}\oplus E_{u}}
. The link between the resolvents on forms and the Ruelle zeta function is given by the
Guillemin trace formula
. In particular,
[
12
, Equation (2.5)]
gives

(1.11)

\zeta_{V}^{\prime}(\lambda)/\zeta_{V}(\lambda)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k+q}e^{-\lambda t_{0}}\tr^{\flat}\big(\varphi_{-t_{0}}^{*}\big((\mathbf{P})_{|\mathscr{E}_{0}^{k}}-\lambda)^{-1}\big)\big),

where the shift by a small time
t_{0}
is a technicality to ensure that the pullbacked resolvent
\varphi_{-t_{0}}^{*}(\mathbf{P}-\lambda)^{-1}
satisfies the wavefront set condition which makes its flat trace well defined (see
[
12
, Section 4]
). This shows that the meromorphic extension of
\zeta_{V}
follows from the extension of the resolvent acting on the space
\mathscr{E}_{0}^{k}
for any
k
(plus some additional arguments). Moreover, we get the poles of
\zeta_{V}
by studying poles of each resolvent.

The study of the first pole of the Ruelle zeta function can be found in
[
31
, Theorem 9.2]
.

Theorem

(Parry-Pollicott)
.

Let
V
be a Hölder continuous potential and suppose that the flow is Anosov and weakly topologically mixing. Then the Ruelle zeta function
\zeta_{V}
is non zero and analytic in the half plane
\{Re(\lambda)\geq P(V)\}
except for a simple pole at
\lambda=P(V)
, where
P(V)
is the topological pressure of the potential
V
.

As a consequence of our first two theorems, we get an analogue of the theorem of Parry and Pollicott on the first pole of the Ruelle zeta function.

Corollary 1

(Critical axis of the zeta function)
.

Suppose the flow to be topologically mixing, Anosov and let
V
be a smooth real-valued potential, then the Ruelle zeta function
\zeta_{V}^{R}
is analytic and non vanishing in the half plane
\{Re(\lambda)\geq P(V)\}
except for a simple pole at
\lambda=P(V)
.

We note that a consequence of this corollary and a standard Tauberian argument is the following asymptotic growth:

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma,T_{\gamma}\leq t}e^{V_{\gamma}T_{\gamma}}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma,T_{\gamma}\leq t}e^{\int_{0}^{T_{\gamma}}V(\gamma(t))dt}\sim\frac{e^{tP(V)}}{tP(V)}.

For
V=0
, this result is known as the Prime Orbit Theorem and can be found in
[
31
, Theorem 9.3]
.

2.1.

Definition 2.1

(Anosov)

The flow

There is a continuous splitting of the tangent space

The decomposition is flow-invariant, meaning that

\forall t\in\mathbb{R},\,\,E_{u}(\varphi_{t}(x))=(d\varphi_{t})_{x}(E_{u}(x)),\,\,E_{s}(\varphi_{t}(x))=(d\varphi_{t})_{x}(E_{s}(x)).

|(d\varphi_{t})_{x}(v_{s})|_{g}\leq Ce^{-\theta t}|v_{s}|_{g},\quad\quad\forall t\geq 0,\,\,\forall v_{s}\in E_{s}(x).

|(d\varphi_{t})_{x}(v_{u})|_{g}\leq Ce^{-\theta|t|}|v_{u}|_{g},\quad\quad\forall t\leq 0,\,\,\forall v_{u}\in E_{u}(x).

We will denote by

and

An important class of examples is given by geodesic flows on the unit tangent bundle

T^{*}_{x}M=E_{u}^{*}(x)\oplus E_{s}^{*}(x)\oplus E_{0}^{*}(x),

defined by

E_{s}^{*}(x)\big(E_{s}(x)\oplus\mathbb{R}{X}(x)\big)=0,\,\,E_{u}^{*}(x)\big(E_{u}(x)\oplus\mathbb{R}{X}(x)\big)=0,\,\,E_{0}^{*}(x)\big(E_{s}(x)\oplus E_{u}(x)\big)=0.

|(d\varphi_{-t})^{T}_{x}(\xi_{s})|\leq Ce^{-\theta t}|\xi_{s}|,\quad\quad\forall t\geq 0,\,\,\forall\xi_{s}\in E_{s}^{*}(x).

|(d\varphi_{-t})^{T}_{x}(\xi_{u})|\leq Ce^{-\theta|t|}|\xi_{u}|,\quad\quad\forall t\leq 0,\,\,\forall\xi_{u}\in E_{u}^{*}(x).

The following result, which is due to Faure, Roy, Sjöstrand in

There exists an order function

•

The order function

(

The escape function decreases along trajectories, that is

\exists R>0,|\xi|\geq R,\,\,\mathbf{X}(G_{m})(x,\xi)\leq 0.

We fix now an order function

We refer to

The symbol

For

In the following, the

The following properties hold:

•

•

•

If

(f\varphi,\psi)_{\mathcal{H}^{s}\times\mathcal{H}^{-s}}=(\varphi,\bar{f}\psi)_{\mathcal{H}^{s}\times\mathcal{H}^{-s}}

The transfer operator

We define the exponential growth in

-norm of

C_{0}(\mathbf{P})=C_{0}:=\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\|e^{t\mathbf{P}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}.

The fact that

is finite is a consequence of

being a semi-group.

We obtain the existence of the meromorphic extension of the resolvent to the whole complex plane and the fact that Ruelle resonances are contained in
\{\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)\leq C_{0}\}
. We refer to
[

for a proof of the following theorem.

Theorem 3

(Faure Sjöstrand)
.

There exists

such that for any

(2.12)

\mathbf{P}-\lambda:\mathrm{Dom}(\mathbf{P})\cap\mathcal{H}^{s}=\{u\in\mathcal{H}^{s}\mid\mathbf{P}u\in\mathcal{H}^{s}\}\to\mathcal{H}^{s}.

Moreover, the resolvent

(2.13)

R_{+}(\lambda)=(\mathbf{P}-\lambda)^{-1}=(-X+V-\lambda)^{-1}:\mathcal{H}^{s}\to\mathcal{H}^{s}

is well defined, bounded and holomorphic for

and has a meromorphic extension to

which is independent of any choice. Thus, the resolvent, viewed as an operator

, has a meromorphic extension to the whole complex plane. The poles of this extension are called the

, they are located in

. On

, one has

(2.14)

(\mathbf{P}-\lambda)^{-1}f=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{t(\mathbf{P}-\lambda)}fdt=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\!\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}V(\varphi_{-s}(x))ds\right)e^{-t({X}+\lambda)}fdt.

We sum up in the end of the subsection the characterization of generalized eigenfunctions: the

2.4.
Equilibrium states from dimension theory

In this section, we recall Climenhaga’s construction from
[
7
]
of leaf measures
m^{u}_{V}
and
m^{s}_{V}
using dimension theory.

There are two main ways equilibrium states are defined. The first one is through the use of
Markov partitions
and the second one is via the use of the
specification property
. A third approach is given by dimension theory: the goal is to generalize the idea of
Haussdorff dimension
and
Haussdorff measure
to a more dynamical setting. We recall the definition of the Haussdorff dimension for a metric space
(X,\delta)
. For
d\geq 0
and
\varepsilon>0
, define the
d
-dimensional Haussdorff measure by

H^{d}_{\varepsilon}(S):=\inf\{\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{diam}(U_{i})^{d}\mid S\subset\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}U_{i},\ \mathrm{diam}(U_{i})<\varepsilon\},

for any subset
S
and where the infimum is taken over all countable covering of sets
U_{i}
with diameter less than
\varepsilon
. We define an outer measure by taking

H^{d}(S)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to+\infty}H^{d}_{\varepsilon}(S)\in[0,+\infty].

We then define the Haussdorff dimension of
X
to be

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathrm{Hauss}}(X):=\inf\{d\geq 0\mid H^{d}(X)=+\infty\}=\sup\{d\geq 0\mid H^{d}(X)=0\}.

The idea of Climenhaga, Pesin and Zelerowicz (already present in essence in
[
23
]
and
[
25
]
, where the case of the measure of maximal entropy was treated) was to replace the sets with small diameters by more dynamical objects, namely, coverings should be made of
Bowen balls
(defined below) and we should let time
t\to+\infty
.

(2.17)

B_{t}(x,r):=\{y\in\mathcal{M}\mid\max_{s\in[0,t]}d(\varphi_{s}x,\varphi_{s}y)<r\}.

Let
\delta_{0}>0
be the size of the local (un)stable manifolds, then fix thereafter
\delta\in]0,\delta_{0}[
and define
\mathcal{W}^{\bullet}(x,\delta):=B(x,\delta)\cap\mathcal{W}^{\bullet}(x)
where
\bullet=u,s,ws,wu
. We will not always specify in the rest of the paper the dependance in
\delta
if it is not relevant to the argument.

Let
x\in\mathcal{M}
, consider
Z\subset\mathcal{W}^{u}(x,\delta)
. Define, for
T>0
,

\mathbb{E}(Z,T):=\{\mathscr{E}\subset M\times[T,+\infty[,\ Z\subset\bigcup_{(x,t)\in\mathscr{E}}B_{t}(x,r,\mathcal{W}^{u}(x,\delta))\}.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, then we define a measure
m^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{W}^{u}(x,\delta)}=m^{\alpha}
by putting

m^{\alpha}(Z)=\lim_{T\to+\infty}\inf_{\mathscr{E}\in\mathbb{E}(Z,T)}\sum_{(x,t)\in\mathscr{E}}e^{S_{t}V(x)-t\alpha}.

We then retrieve the Caratheodory dimension as a treshold just like in the case of the Haussdorff measure, this is a result due to Pesin, see
[
32
, Prop. 1.1 and 1.2]
. We have moreover, in this case that the measure for
\alpha=\alpha_{C}
defines a Borel measure, see
[
7
, Lemm. 2.14]
.

The measure
m^{\alpha}
defined above is an outer measure for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and

P(V):=\inf\{\alpha\mid m^{\alpha}(W(x,\delta))=+\infty\}=\sup\{\alpha\mid m^{\alpha}(\mathcal{W}^{u}(x,\delta))=0\}.

Moreover,
m^{P(V)}
is a Borel measure, denoted by
m^{u}_{x}
and called the leaf measure. It satisfies

(2.18)

\forall Z\subset\mathcal{W}^{u}(x_{1},\delta), \ m^{u}_{x}(Z)=\lim_{T\to+\infty}\inf_{\mathscr{E}\in\mathbb{E}(Z,T)}\sum_{(x,t)\in\mathscr{E}}e^{S_{t}V(x)-tP(V)}.

Up until now, we have defined a system of (unstable) leaf measures
\{m^{u}_{x}\mid x\in\mathcal{M}\}
satisfying the two following conditions:

•

Support: each measure
m^{u}_{x}
is supported in
\mathcal{W}^{u}(x,\delta)
.

•

Compatibility: if
Z\subset\mathcal{W}^{u}(x_{1},\delta)\cap\mathcal{W}^{u}(x_{2},\delta)
is a Borel set, then the two measures agree, i.e
m^{u}_{x_{1}}(Z)=m^{u}_{x_{2}}(Z)
.

The set of measures defined in (
2.18
) has actually two more important properties: it is
\varphi_{t}
-conformal and behaves naturally with holonomies (see
[
7
, Theo 3.4]
).

The system of (unstable) leaf measures
\{m^{u}_{x}\mid x\in\mathcal{M}\}
defined in (
2.18
) is
\varphi_{t}
-conformal, namely, for any
x\in\mathcal{M}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the measures
(\varphi_{t})_{*}m^{u}_{x}
and
m^{u}_{\varphi_{t}x}
are equivalent and more precisely:

(2.19)

m^{u}_{\varphi_{t}x}(\varphi_{t}Z)=\int_{Z}e^{tP(V)-S_{t}V(z)}dm^{u}_{x}(z).

In terms of Radon-Nikodym derivatives, we have

(2.20)

\frac{d((\varphi_{-t})_{*}m^{u}_{\varphi_{t}x})}{dm^{u}_{x}}(z)=e^{tP(V)-S_{t}V(z)},\quad\frac{d((\varphi_{t})_{*}m^{u}_{x})}{dm^{u}_{\varphi_{t}x}}(\varphi_{t}z)=e^{S_{t}V(z)-tP(V)}.

We define the notion of
holonomy
between (weak-un)stable leaves.

Given
\mathcal{W}^{u}(x_{1},\delta),\mathcal{W}^{u}(x_{2},\delta)
for
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{M}
, a
weak-stable
\delta
-holonomy
between
\mathcal{W}^{u}(x_{1},\delta)
and
\mathcal{W}^{u}(x_{2},\delta)
is a homeomorphism
\pi:\mathcal{W}^{u}(x_{1},\delta)\to\mathcal{W}^{u}(x_{2},\delta)
such that
\pi(z)\in\mathcal{W}^{ws}(z,\delta)
for all
z\in\mathcal{W}^{u}(x_{1},\delta)
.

To conclude, we give the change of variable formula for holonomies. We first introduce a useful function.
Let
\delta_{0}>0
be small enough, given
x\in\mathcal{M}
and
y\in\mathcal{W}^{ws}(x,\delta_{0})
, define

(2.21)

w_{V}^{+}(x,y)=S_{t(x,y)}V(x)+t(x,y)P(V)+\int_{0}^{+\infty}(V(\varphi_{t(x,y)+\tau}(x))-V(\varphi_{\tau}(y)))d\tau.

Here,
t(x,y)
is the Bowen time and the integral converges because
d(\varphi_{t+\tau}x,\varphi_{\tau}y)\to 0
exponentially fast and
V
is Hölder continuous.

We define for
x\in\mathcal{M}
and
y\in\mathcal{W}^{wu}(x,\delta_{0})
the quantity

w_{V}^{-}(x,y)=-S_{t(x,y)}V(x)+t(x,y)P(V)+\int_{0}^{+\infty}(V(\varphi_{t-\tau}x)-V(\varphi_{-\tau}y))d\tau.

We note that we have the special values:

\forall y\in\mathcal{W}^{s}(x),\quad w^{+}_{V}(x,y)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}(V(\varphi_{\tau}x)-V(\varphi_{\tau}y))d\tau,

as well as

(2.22)

w^{+}(x,\varphi_{t}x)=S_{t}V(x)-tP(V).

Finally, we have the cocycle relation

(2.23)

\forall y,z\in\mathcal{W}^{ws}(x,\delta_{0}/2),\quad w^{+}_{V}(x,y)=w^{+}_{V}(x,z)+w^{+}_{V}(z,y).

The holonomy we will mostly be interested in will be given by

\pi:\mathcal{W}^{u}(q,\delta)\to\mathcal{W}^{u}(p,\delta),\quad\pi(x):=[x,p]
and we can now state the second change of variable theorem, see
[
7
, Theorem 3.4]
for more details.

Consider the system of (unstable) leaf measures
\{m^{u}_{x}\mid x\in\mathcal{M}\}
defined in (
2.18
). Let
\mathcal{W}^{u}(x_{1},\delta),\mathcal{W}^{u}(x_{2},\delta)
for
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{M}
and let
\pi:\mathcal{W}^{u}(x_{1},\delta)\to\mathcal{W}^{u}(x_{2},\delta)
be a weak-stable
\delta_{0}
holonomy. Then the measures
\pi_{*}m_{x_{1}}^{u}
and
m_{x_{2}}^{u}
are equivalent, and we have

(2.24)

\frac{d(\pi_{*}m^{u}_{x_{1}})}{dm^{u}_{x_{2}}}(\pi(z))=e^{w^{+}_{V}(z,\pi z)}.

3.
Resolvent acting on functions

In this section we study the action of
\mathbf{P}
on functions. The goal is to locate the critical axis, show that it is given by
\{\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)=P(V+J^{u})\}
and to study the co-resonant states associated to resonances on this axis.

3.1.
Construction of the co-resonant state

We first prove that
P(V+J^{u})
is indeed a resonance, called the
first resonance
. In the case of a null potential (
V=0
), this is trivial as constant functions lie trivially in the kernel of the flow. In this case, the equilibrium state (which is usually referred to as the SRB measure) can be identified as the co-resonant state for the Ruelle resonance
0
.

Following Section

, we can define Ruelle resonances for the

\mathbf{P}^{*}=X+V-\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{vol}}(X)
. The resonant states for
\mathbf{P}^{*}
are referred to as

states for
\mathbf{P}
and their span has the same dimension as the the span of the resonant states. Equivalently, we can define the adjoint by duality, using relation (

(3.1)

\forall f,g\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}),\quad(\mathbf{P}f,g)_{\mathcal{H}^{s}\times\mathcal{H}^{-s}}=(f,\mathbf{P}^{*}g)_{\mathcal{H}^{s}\times\mathcal{H}^{-s}},

from which we can define an unbounded operator

(3.2)

\mathbf{P}^{*}:\mathrm{Dom}(\mathbf{P}^{*})\cap\mathcal{H}^{-s}=\{u\in\mathcal{H}^{-s}\mid\mathbf{P}^{*}u\in\mathcal{H}^{-s}\}\to\mathcal{H}^{-s}.

Now, a direct adaptation of
[
20
, lemma 5.3]
yields.

Lemma 3.1

(Co-resonant states)
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, then
\lambda
is a Ruelle resonance for
\mathbf{P}
if and only if
\overline{\lambda}
is a Ruelle resonance for
\mathbf{P}^{*}
. In this case, the space of resonant and co-resonant states have the same dimension
m
. If we consider
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{E_{u}^{*}}(\mathcal{M}):=\{u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\mid\mathrm{WF}(u)\subset E_{u}^{*}\}
and
v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{E_{s}^{*}}(\mathcal{M}):=\{u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\mid\mathrm{WF}(u)\subset E_{s}^{*}\}
two basis of the resonant states for
\mathbf{P}
and
\mathbf{P}^{*}
respectively satisfying
(u_{i},v_{j})=\delta_{i,j}
, then the projector
\Pi_{0}(\lambda)
on the space of resonant states for the resonance
\lambda
is given by

(3.3)

\Pi_{0}(\lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}u_{i}\otimes v_{i}.

In
[
7
, Theorem 3.10]
, Climenhaga gives a local product construction of the equilibrium state. For this, let’s first notice that Climenhaga’s construction is still valid when swapping the unstable and stable foliations.

We can thus define a system of stable leaf measures
\{m^{s}_{x}\mid x\in\mathcal{M}\}
which is compatible and supported in
\mathcal{W}^{s}(x)
. Moreover,
m^{s}_{x}
is defined using formula (

), but using backward Bowen-balls, see

, Section 3.3]
for details. The set
\{m^{s}_{x}\mid x\in\mathcal{M}\}
is
\varphi_{t}
-conformal in the sense of (

) and the change of variable by holonomy is the one explicited in (

) but with

replaced by

.

We can then extend the leaf measures to the weak (un)stable foliations

(3.4)

m^{ws}_{x}:=\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}m^{s}_{\varphi_{t}x}dt,\quad m^{wu}_{x}:=\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}m^{u}_{\varphi_{t}x}dt.

To state the main result of this subsection, we introduce further notations,

z=\Psi_{q}(x,y),\quad R_{q}^{u}(z)=\mathcal{W}^{u}(z,\delta)\cap R_{q},\ R_{q}^{ws}(z)=\mathcal{W}^{ws}(z,\delta)\cap R_{q},

where
R_{q}
and
\Psi_{q}
are defined in equation (

). Observe that

(3.5)

z=[x,y],\quad x=[z,q],\quad y=[q,z].

We adapt the product construction of Climenhaga (
[
7
, Theorem 3.10]
) to obtain the following local construction of the co-resonant state. In the following, we add an additional index
W
to denote the leaf measures constructed using (

) from the Hölder continuous potential
W
. For any

, we define three measures on

by putting, for a Borel set

,

(3.6)

m_{1}^{q}(Z):=\int_{Z}e^{w^{+}_{J^{u}+V}(z,[z,q])+w^{-}_{J^{u}}(z,[q,z])}d((\Psi_{q})_{*}(m^{u}_{q,J^{u}+V}\times m^{ws}_{q,J^{u}}))(z),

(3.7)

m_{2}^{q}(Z):=\int_{R^{ws}_{q}}\int_{Z\cap R^{u}_{q}(y)}e^{w^{-}_{J^{u}}(z,y)}dm^{u}_{y,J^{u}+V}(z)dm^{ws}_{q,J^{u}}(y),

(3.8)

m_{3}^{q}(Z):=\int_{R^{u}_{q}}\int_{Z\cap R^{ws}_{q}(x)}e^{w^{+}_{J^{u}+V}(z,x)}dm^{ws}_{x,J^{u}}(z)dm^{u}_{q,V+J^{u}}(x).

Theorem 4

(Construction of the co-resonant state)
.

For any
q\in\mathcal{M}
, the three measures
m_{1}^{q},m_{2}^{q}
and
m_{3}^{q}
coincide and there is a unique non-zero and finite Borel measure
\nu
on
\mathcal{M}
such that
\forall Z\subset R_{q}
one has
\nu(Z)=m_{1}(Z)
. This Borel measure satisfies

(3.9)

\mathbf{P}^{*}\nu=P(V+J^{u})\nu,\quad\mathrm{WF}(\nu)\subset E_{s}^{*}

and is therefore a co-resonant state. In other words,
P(V+J^{u})
is a Ruelle resonance.

Remark 3
.

Climenhaga’s original construction is obtained by taking the two potentials to be equal, in other words, all leaf measures are constructed using
V
and the resulting Borel measure is a non-zero multiple of the equilibrium state
\mu_{V}
. We observe that when
V=0
, both constructions coincide and we retrieve the known fact that the SRB measure is the co-resonant state in this case. Of course, by taking the adjoint, we obtain a construction of a resonant state
\eta
.

Proof.

We mostly follow the strategy of the proof of
[
7
, Theorem 10]
, while only changing the necessary details. We first prove that the three formulas indeed agree and define the same local measure. Starting from a measurable function

3.2.
Critical axis

In this subsection, we prove that the set of Ruelle resonances is contained in the half plane
\{\lambda\mid\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)\leq P(V+J^{u})\}
. The case
V=0
is enlightening; in this case, the critical axis is the imaginary axis. No resonance has positive real part, indeed, in the case of a volume preserving Anosov flow, this follows directly from Theorem
3
while in the general case, it follows from the general bound
\|e^{-Xt}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}
, see
[
20
, Corr. 4.16]
. We insist on the fact that the functional space
L^{\infty}
is not really suited for microlocal analysis and thus this part of the proof needs the introduction of some ”finer” measure theory. The goal of this subsection is thus to construct, using the co-resonant state
\nu
defined in Theorem

, a norm
\|.\|_{V}
such that

(3.12)

\forall f\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}),\quad\|e^{t\mathbf{P}}f\|_{V}\leq e^{tP(V+J^{u})}\|f\|_{V}.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\nu
be as in Theorem
4
, for

, define

\|f\|_{V}:=\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\nu)}=\int_{\mathcal{M}}|f|(z)d\nu(z).

Then this defines a norm on

such that

\forall f\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}),\quad\|e^{t\mathbf{P}}f\|_{V}\leq e^{tP(V+J^{u})}\|f\|_{V}.

We will denote by

the completion for

of
C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
.

Proof.

The fact that
\nu(|f|)\geq 0
is a consequence of
\nu
being a measure (i.e of
\nu
being a distribution which is non-negative on non-negative functions). The homogeneity and the triangle inequality are clear. Suppose now

, then use the formula (
3.6
) as well as (

) to see that the co-resonant state
\nu
gives positive measure to any non-empty open set of
\mathcal{M}
. Thus, if

is non-zero, then
|f|
is positive on a small open set which is a contradiction. The change of variable formula (
3.12
) is a direct consequence of (

) which was shown in the proof of Theorem

.
∎

As a direct consequence, we adapt
[
20
, Corr. 4.16]
to show that there is no Ruelle resonance in the half plane
\{\mathrm{Re}(z)>P(V+J^{u})\}

Lemma 3.4
.

The set of Ruelle resonances for the potential
V
is contained in the half plane
\{\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)\leq P(V+J^{u})\}

Proof.

Recall from the proof of Theorem

where the remainder is given by

where

is a cutoff supported in

Let

such that
\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)>P(V+J^{u})
. Let us show that it is not in the Ruelle spectrum. The projector
\Pi_{0}(\lambda)
on the eigenvalue

is given by the integral

(3.14)

\Pi_{0}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|z|=\varepsilon}(z\mathrm{Id}-F(\lambda))^{-1}dz,

for a radius

\displaystyle\|\tilde{R}(\lambda)f\|_{V}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{T}^{T+\varepsilon}|\chi^{\prime}(t)|e^{-t\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)}\times\|e^{t\mathbf{P}}f\|_{V}dt

if

∎

3.3.
Resonances on the critical axis

In this subsection, we investigate some properties of Ruelle resonances on the critical axis and prove the first part of Theorem
1
. The main results are: that Ruelle resonances on the critical axis have no Jordan block and that the first resonance is simple.

The following two lemmas justify that resonances on the critical axis have no Jordan block. The strategy is the same as in
[
20
, Lemma 5.1]
, where the space
L^{\infty}
is replaced by the new functional space

.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\lambda+P(V+J^{u})\in\{\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)=P(V+J^{u})\}
be on the critical axis. Then
\tilde{R}(\lambda)
has spectrum included in the closed unit disk.

Proof.

Consider
B\in\Psi^{0}(\mathcal{M})
such that its principal symbol is equal to
1
except in a conic neighborhood
V
of
E_{0}^{*}
. We can then write
\tilde{R}=\tilde{R}B+(\mathrm{Id}-B)\tilde{R}(\mathrm{Id}-B)+B\tilde{R}(\mathrm{Id}-B),
where
(\mathrm{Id}-B)\tilde{R}(\mathrm{Id}-B)
is smoothing thus compact (because
\mathrm{WF}(\tilde{R})
does not intersect
E_{0}^{*}
, see
[
28
, Theorem 9.11]
for a detailed computation of the wavefront set). Moreover, we have
\|\tilde{R}B+B\tilde{R}(\mathrm{Id}-B)\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}
if
T
is chosen large enough. This shows that the essential spectrum of
\tilde{R}
is contained in
B(0,1/2)
and that the spectrum outside this ball consists of isolated eigenvalues. Using the

boundedness of
\tilde{R}(\lambda)
, we see that for large values of

, we get the converging Neumann series (in

)

(3.15)

(z-\tilde{R}(\lambda))^{-1}=z^{-1}\sum_{k\geq 0}z^{-k}\tilde{R}(\lambda)^{k}.

Now, we use the bound (
3.12
) for

:

\displaystyle\|\tilde{R}(\lambda)f\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\nu)}\leq\int_{T}^{T+\varepsilon}e^{-tP(V+J^{u})}|\chi^{\prime}(t)|\times\|e^{t\mathbf{P}}f\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\nu)}

\displaystyle dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{T}^{T+\varepsilon}e^{-tP(V+J^{u})}|\chi^{\prime}(t)|e^{tP(V+J^{u})}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\nu)}dt

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\nu)},

to get that the above Neumann series actually converges for

in
\mathcal{L}(L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\nu))
and is analytic in
|z|>1
. By density of smooth functions in
\mathcal{H}^{s}
, this proves that the eigenvalues are contained in the closed unit disk.

∎

The next lemma, together with Remark
2
, justifies that resonances on the critical axis have no Jordan blocks.

Lemma 3.6
.

A complex number
\lambda_{0}\in P(V+J^{u})+i\mathbb{R}
is a Ruelle resonance, if and only if
\tilde{R}(\lambda_{0})
has an eigenvalue at
1
. Moreover, if
\tilde{R}(\lambda_{0})
has a eigenvalue on the unit circle, then it is equal to
1
and the eigenfunctions correspond to resonant states at the resonance
\lambda_{0}
. Finally, write
\Pi_{0}(\lambda_{0})
be the spectral projector of
\mathrm{Id}-\tilde{R}(\lambda_{0})
on the kernel
(

3.14
)
)
. Then we have the convergence, in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{s})

(3.16)

\Pi_{0}(\lambda_{0})=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\tilde{R}(\lambda_{0})^{k}.

Proof.

Suppose that
\tau\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
is an eigenvalue of
\tilde{R}(\lambda_{0})
. We first check that, for any smooth function

, one has
\mathbf{P}\tilde{R}(\lambda_{0})u=\tilde{R}(\lambda_{0})\mathbf{P}u.

In other words, the operator
\tilde{R}(\lambda_{0})
commutes with
\mathbf{P}
so that the space
\mathrm{Ran}(\Pi_{\tau}(\lambda_{0}))
associated to the eigenvalue

can be decomposed into generalized eigenspaces of
\mathbf{P}
. Suppose now that

is an eigenvector for
\mathbf{P}
for the eigenvalue

\tau u=\tilde{R}(\lambda_{0})u=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\chi^{\prime}(t)e^{-t\lambda_{0}+t\mu}u(x)dt=\widehat{\chi^{\prime}}(-i\lambda_{0}+i\mu)u.

This implies that
|\widehat{\chi^{\prime}}(-i\lambda_{0}+i\mu)|=1

This implies that

and that

is a resonant state for the Ruelle resonance

.

The previous discussion justifies that there exists an integer

\Pi_{0}(\lambda_{0}):=\{u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{E_{u}^{*}}(\mathcal{M})\mid(\mathbf{P}-\lambda_{0})^{N}u=0\}.

and equal to

3.5

To prove the first part of (

\displaystyle m^{u}_{V}(e^{t\mathbf{P}}\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{W}^{u}(q,\delta)}\left(\int_{R_{q}^{ws}(x)}e^{w_{V}^{+}(y,x)}e^{S_{t}V(\varphi_{-t}y)}e^{t\mathcal{L}_{{X}}}\big(\varphi\wedge\alpha\big)(y)\right)dm^{u}_{V}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{W}^{u}(q,\delta)}\left(\int_{R_{q}^{ws}(\varphi_{t}x)}e^{w_{V}^{+}(\varphi_{t}w,x)}e^{S_{t}V(w)}(\varphi\wedge\alpha)(w)\right)dm^{u}_{V}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{W}^{u}(q,\delta)}e^{2tP(V)}e^{-S_{t}V(x)}\left(\int_{R_{q}^{ws}(\varphi_{t}x)}e^{w_{V}^{+}(w,\varphi_{t}x)}(\varphi\wedge\alpha)(w)\right)dm^{u}_{V}(x)

Lemma 4.2

(Equilibrium state as a product)
.

There exists

m^{u}_{V}\wedge\alpha\wedge m^{s}_{V}=c\mu_{V}.

Similarly to the case of functions, we now prove that
\{\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)=P(V)\}
is the critical axis by using the ”

-norm” associated to the co-resonant state

. Nevertheless, because we work on forms, the following norm will only be well defined for section with values in

Lemma 4.3
.

We define a norm on
C^{0}(\mathcal{M};\Lambda_{0}^{d_{s}})
by posing

(4.4)

\forall\varphi\in C^{0}(\mathcal{M};\Lambda_{0}^{d_{s}}),\ \|\varphi\|_{V,0}:=m^{u}_{V}(|\varphi|).

This norm satisfies the bound

(4.5)

\forall\varphi\in C^{0}(\mathcal{M};\Lambda_{0}^{d_{s}}),\ \|e^{t\mathbf{P}}\varphi\|_{V,0}\leq e^{tP(V)}\|\varphi\|_{V,0}.

Proof.

Suppose
\varphi\in C^{0}(\mathcal{M};\Lambda^{d_{s}}_{0})
, the bundle is one dimensional and it thus makes sense to talk of
|\varphi|\wedge\alpha
as a
d_{s}+1
density. If
\varphi(q)\neq 0
then by continuity,
\varphi\neq 0
on a small open set. Then
\|\varphi\|_{V,0}>0
because
m^{u}_{V}
gives a positive measure to any open set. The bound (
4.5
) follows from the fact that
m^{u}_{V}
is a co-resonant state. The last point follows from the first two points by a direct adaptation of the proof of Lemma
3.4
.
∎

We now use this norm and a ”shift” to define inductively a norm on
C^{0}(\mathcal{M};\Lambda_{k}^{d_{s}})
for any
k
.
Consider the set of all finite covers by open sets which trivialize
E_{s}
and
E_{u}
:

\mathcal{C}:=\{\mathcal{U}:=(U_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}\mid\mathcal{M}=\cup_{j=1}^{n}U_{j},\ U_{j}\text{ open and }E_{s}\text{ and }E_{u}\text{ are trivial on }U_{j}\}.

For any
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}
, let
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{U})
be the set of partition of unity
\chi_{j}
associated to the cover
\mathcal{U}
. Finally, we define the set of (normalized) local trivialization of
E_{u}
:

\mathscr{V}^{u}(\mathcal{U}):=\{(X^{j}_{u,h})_{1\leq j\leq d_{u}}\in C^{0}(U_{h};E_{u})\mid(E_{u})_{|U_{h}}=\mathrm{Span}\{X^{j}_{u,h}\}_{1\leq j\leq d_{u}},\ \|X^{j}_{u,h}\|_{C^{0}}=1\},

and its dual conterpart

\mathscr{F}^{u}(\mathcal{U}):=\{(Y^{j}_{u,h})_{1\leq j\leq d_{s}}\in C^{0}(U_{h};E_{u}^{*})\mid(E_{u}^{*})_{|U_{h}}=\mathrm{Span}\{Y^{j}_{u,h}\}_{1\leq j\leq d_{s}},\ \|Y^{j}_{u,h}\|_{C^{0}}=1\}.

Proposition 4.4
.

We define a norm inductively on
C^{0}(\mathcal{M};\Lambda_{k}^{d_{s}}),k\geq 1
, by posing, for
f\in C^{0}(\mathcal{M};\Lambda_{k}^{d_{s}})

(4.6)

\|f\|_{V,k}:=\sup_{\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}}\sup_{(\chi_{j})\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{U})}\max_{h=1}^{n}\sup_{(X^{j}_{u,h})\in\mathscr{V}^{u}}\sup_{(Y^{i}_{u,h})\in\mathscr{F}^{u}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{s}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{u}}\|\chi_{h}\iota_{X_{u,h}^{j}}f\wedge Y_{u,h}^{i}\|_{V,k-1}.

This norm satisfies the bound

(4.7)

\forall\varphi\in C^{0}(\mathcal{M};\Lambda_{k}^{d_{s}}),\ \|e^{t\mathbf{P}}\varphi\|_{V,k}\leq Ce^{t(P(V)-k\eta)}\|\varphi\|_{V,k}

for some
C,\eta>0
. As a consequence, there are no Ruelle resonances in
\{\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)>P(V)\}
and if
\lambda
is a Ruelle resonance on the critical axis, then it has no Jordan block.

Proof.

Contracting with a vector in
E_{u}
and then wedging with a vector in
E_{u}^{*}
sends a section of
C^{0}(\mathcal{M};\Lambda_{k}^{d_{s}})
to
C^{0}(\mathcal{M};\Lambda_{k-1}^{d_{s}})
so the formula makes sense by induction. Let’s prove that the norm takes finite values. For this, we use the fact that
m^{u}_{V}
is of order zero and Lemma
4.3
to get first that for
f\in C^{0}(\mathcal{M};\Lambda_{0}^{d_{s}})
, one has
\|f\|_{V,0}\leq C\|f\|_{0}
. This implies, for any
f\in C^{0}(\mathcal{M};\Lambda_{1}^{d_{s}})

\sum_{i=1}^{d_{u}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{s}}\|\chi_{h}\iota_{X_{u,h}^{j}}f\wedge Y_{u,h}^{i}\|_{V,0}\leq C^{\prime}\sum_{i,j}\|X_{u,h}^{j}\|_{C^{0}}\|Y_{u,h}^{i}\|_{C^{0}}\|f\|_{0}\leq C^{\prime\prime}\|f\|_{0}

with a constant
C^{\prime\prime}
independent of the choice of cover or partition of unity. This shows that
\|.\|_{V,1}
takes finite values and a quick induction shows that it is also the case of
\|.\|_{V,k}
for any
k
. The triangle inequality, homogeneity and non-negativity of
\|.\|_{V,k}
follows from Lemma
4.3
and an induction. Suppose now that
f\in C^{0}(\mathcal{M};\Lambda_{k}^{d_{s}})
and
f\neq 0
. Then
f
is non zero on a small open set which is included in some
U_{h}
of the open cover. Now, because
(X_{s,h}^{j})
and
(Y_{u,h}^{i})
are local basis, then there is
(i_{0},j_{0})
such that
\chi_{h}\iota_{X_{s,h}^{j_{0}}}f\wedge Y_{u,h}^{i_{0}}
is continuous and non zero on an open set. By induction and the fact that
m^{u}_{V}
gives a positive measure to any non empty open set, we get that
\|f\|_{V,k}>0
and
\|.\|_{V,k}
thus defines a norm.

We now prove (
4.5
), for this, we consider
f\in C^{0}(\mathcal{M};\Lambda_{k}^{d_{s}})
, a cover
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}
, a partition of unity
(\chi_{h})
,
(X^{j}_{u,h})\in\mathscr{V}^{u}
and
(Y^{i}_{u,h})\in\mathscr{F}^{u}
. For any

, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d_{s}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{u}}\chi_{h}\iota_{X_{u,h}^{j}}e^{t\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{X}}}f\wedge Y_{u,h}^{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{s}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{u}}\chi_{h}(y)e^{t\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{X}}}\left(\iota_{(d\varphi_{-t})_{\varphi_{t}y}(X_{u,h}^{j})}f\wedge e^{t\mathcal{L}_{X}}Y_{u,h}^{i}\right)(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{s}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{u}}e^{t\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{X}}}\chi_{h}(\varphi_{t}y)\iota_{(d\varphi_{-t})_{\varphi_{t}y}(X_{u,h}^{j})(\varphi_{t}y)}f(y)\wedge e^{t\mathcal{L}_{X}}Y_{u,h}^{i}(y).

If we have
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}
, then using the invariance of the Anosov decomposition (
2.2
), we see that
\varphi_{-t}\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}
with an associated partition of unity given by
(\chi_{h}\circ\varphi_{t})_{1\leq h\leq n}
. Moreover, the same equation gives that

spans

for

and similarly

spans

. We compute

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d_{s}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{u}}e^{t\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{X}}}\chi_{h}(\varphi_{t}y)\iota_{(d\varphi_{-t})_{\varphi_{t}y}(X_{u,h}^{j})(\varphi_{t}y)}f(y)\wedge e^{t\mathcal{L}_{X}}Y_{u,h}^{i}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\|e^{t\mathcal{L}_{X}}Y_{u,h}^{i}\|\|(d\varphi_{-t})_{\varphi_{t}y}(X_{u,h}^{j})\|e^{t\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{X}}}\chi_{h}(\varphi_{t}y)\iota_{\tfrac{(d\varphi_{-t})_{\varphi_{t}y}(X_{u,h}^{j})}{\|(d\varphi_{-t})_{\varphi_{t}y}(X_{u,h}^{j})\|}}f(y)\wedge\frac{e^{t\mathcal{L}_{X}}Y_{u,h}^{i}}{\|e^{t\mathcal{L}_{X}}Y_{u,h}^{i}\|}.

We then have
\|e^{t\mathcal{L}_{X}}Y_{u,h}^{i}\|,\|(d\varphi_{-t})_{\varphi_{t}y}(X_{u,h}^{j})\|\leq Ce^{-t\eta}
for some (uniform)

by the Anosov property. Finally, we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{d_{s}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{u}}\|\chi_{h}\iota_{X_{u,h}^{j}}e^{t\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{X}}}f\wedge Y_{u,h}^{i}\|_{V,k-1}\leq Ce^{-2\eta t}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{s}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{u}}\big\|e^{t\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{X}}}\left(\chi_{h}\iota_{\tilde{X}_{u,h}^{j}}f\wedge\tilde{Y}_{u,h}^{i}\right)\big\|_{V,k-1}

for
\tilde{X}_{u,h}^{j}:=(d\varphi_{-t})_{\varphi_{t}y}(X_{u,h}^{j})/\|(d\varphi_{-t})_{\varphi_{t}y}(X_{u,h}^{j})\|
and
\tilde{Y}_{u,h}^{i}=e^{t\mathcal{L}_{X}}Y_{u,h}^{i}/\|e^{t\mathcal{L}_{X}}Y_{u,h}^{i}\|

Passing to the supremum thus gives

\|e^{t\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{X}}}f\|_{V,k-1}\leq Ce^{(P(V)-2(k-1)\eta)t}\|f\|_{V,k-1}\ \Rightarrow\ \|e^{t\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{X}}}f\|_{V,k}\leq Ce^{(P(V)-2k\eta)t}\|f\|_{V,k}.

To conclude the proof of (
4.7
), we only need to initialize the induction, and this is exactly the statement of Lemma
4.3
.

Consider
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M};\mathscr{E}_{d_{s}})
and consider its decomposition

We define a norm on

with the following bound when using the propagator:

\|f\|_{V}:=\sum_{k=0}^{d_{s}}\|\omega_{k}\|_{V,k},\quad\|e^{t\mathcal{L}_{X}}f\|_{V}\leq Ce^{tP(V)}\|f\|_{V}.

This is the only thing we need to mimick the argument of Lemma

and Lemma

and prove that no resonance exists in

and that resonances on the critical axis have no Jordan block.
∎

Proposition 4.5

n

\displaystyle\langle\omega,\Pi_{0}(\lambda)^{*}\theta\rangle

n

n

\omega

\theta_{x}(f):=(f[\mathcal{W}^{u}(x)],\theta)_{\mathcal{H}^{s}\times\mathcal{H}^{-s}}=(f\delta_{\mathcal{W}^{u}(x)},\mathrm{vol}_{\mathcal{W}^{u}(x)}\wedge\alpha\wedge\theta)_{\mathcal{H}^{s}\times\mathcal{H}^{-s}},

∎

Proof.

5.
Other critical axis and regularity of the pressure

In this section, we will prove the statement about critical axes of Theorem
1
as well as Corollary
2
.

Proof.

We start from the Guillemin trace formula

e^{\lambda t_{0}}\tr^{\flat}\big(\varphi_{-t_{0}}^{*}\big((\mathbf{P})_{\mathscr{E}_{0}^{k}}-\lambda\big)^{-1}\big)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{T_{\gamma^{\sharp}}e^{-(\lambda+V_{\gamma})T_{\gamma}}\tr(\Lambda^{k}\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})}{|\det(\mathrm{Id}-\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})|}.

We know by Lemma
4.4
that the critical axis of
(\mathbf{P})_{\mathscr{E}_{0}^{d_{s}}}
is
\{\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)=P(V)\}
. We will prove that the other functions are analytic in a half plane
\{\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)>P(V)-\varepsilon\}
for some
\varepsilon>0.
We list the eigenvalues of the Poincare map

(5.1)

e^{\lambda_{1}^{-}(\gamma)}\leq\ldots\leq e^{\lambda_{d_{u}}^{-}(\gamma)}\leq-e^{\eta T_{\gamma}}\leq e^{\eta T_{\gamma}}\leq e^{\lambda_{1}^{+}(\gamma)}\leq\ldots\leq e^{\lambda_{d_{s}}^{+}(\gamma)}

for some uniform constant
\eta>0
given by the Anosov property. Now we can compute

\tr(\Lambda^{k}\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}):=\sigma_{k}(e^{\lambda_{1}^{-}(\gamma)},\ldots,e^{\lambda_{d_{u}}^{-}(\gamma)},e^{\lambda_{1}^{+}(\gamma)},\ldots,e^{\lambda_{d_{s}}^{+}(\gamma)})

where
\sigma_{k}
is the
k
-th symmetric polynomial. We see that the maximum value of
\tr(\Lambda^{k}\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})
is attained at
k=d_{s}
where one can choose all eigenvalues larger than
1
without choosing any other eigenvalues. In particular, there is a constant
C>0
, independent of
\gamma
and
k
such that if
k\neq d_{s}
, then

|\tr(\Lambda^{k}\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})|\leq C\tr(\Lambda^{d_{s}}\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})e^{-\eta T_{\gamma}}.

But now, we know that for
k=d_{s}
, the trace is analytic and converges in
\{\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)>P(V)\}
. In particular, because all terms are positive, if
\varepsilon<\eta
, then for any
\lambda\in(P(V)-\varepsilon,P(V))
, one has

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{T_{\gamma^{\sharp}}e^{-(\lambda+V_{\gamma})T_{\gamma}}\tr(\Lambda^{k}\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})}{|\det(\mathrm{Id}-\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})|}\leq C\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{T_{\gamma^{\sharp}}e^{-(\lambda-\eta+V_{\gamma})T_{\gamma}}\tr(\Lambda^{d_{s}}\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})}{|\det(\mathrm{Id}-\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})|}<+\infty

and this shows that the left hand side is analytic in the region
\{\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)>P(V)-\varepsilon\}
as claimed.
∎

Now we prove Corollary
2
.

Proof.

From
[
19

C^{1}

\mathcal{U}
of

Proof.

\forall f\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}),\quad\|e^{t\mathbf{P}_{W}}f\|_{V}\leq e^{tP(V+J^{u})}\|f\|_{V},

\mathbf{P}_{W}:=-X+W=-X+V+iU

1.12
).

3.5

. In other words, the Ruelle resonances on the critical axis

Appendix A
Hölder continuity of a distributional product.

In this appendix, we fix a continuous function
\varphi
and an order zero distribution
\theta
such that
\mathrm{WF}(\theta)\subset E_{s}^{*}
. This means that the distributional product
(\delta_{\mathcal{W}^{u}(x)},\varphi\theta)
is well defined.
We will prove the following result.

Proposition A.1
.

There exists
C(\varphi)>0,\alpha>0
and
\varepsilon>0
such that if
d(x,x^{\prime})<\varepsilon
, then

(A.1)

|(\delta_{\mathcal{W}^{u}(x)},\varphi\theta)-(\delta_{\mathcal{W}^{u}(x^{\prime})},\varphi\theta)|\leq C(\varphi)d(x,x^{\prime})^{\alpha}.

Here, the exponent
\alpha
only depends on the unstable foliation.

Proof.

The proof is mostly contained in the proof of
[
39
, Proposition 6]
. Indeed, to get (
A.1
), one needs to prove superpolynomial decay of the Fourier transform of
\delta_{\mathcal{W}^{u}(x)}-\delta_{\mathcal{W}^{u}(x^{\prime})}
for
\xi\notin\Omega
where a small conic neighborhood of
E_{0}^{*}\oplus E_{u}^{*}
with a constant that is Hölder continuous. In other words,

\forall\xi\notin\Omega,\ |\widehat{\delta_{\mathcal{W}^{u}(x)}}(\xi)-\widehat{\delta_{\mathcal{W}^{u}(x^{\prime})}}(\xi)|\leq C_{N}d(x,x^{\prime})^{\alpha}\langle\xi\rangle^{-N}

where the Fourier transforms should be, strictly speaking, defined locally using charts. The super polynomial decay is proved in
[
39
, Proposition 6]
using integration by parts. Inspection of the proof shows that all
C^{k}
norms of the operator
L_{l}
which is used to integrate by parts, depend Hölder continuously on the base point
x
. Weich only uses continuity in the proof to get uniform constant
C_{N}
but using the Hölder continuity instead to bound the difference yields the refinement above.

On
\Omega
, we use the fact that the Fourier transform of
\theta
decays super polynomially and that
|\widehat{\delta_{\mathcal{W}^{u}(x)}}(\xi)-\widehat{\delta_{\mathcal{W}^{u}(x^{\prime})}}(\xi)|
has order zero. Actually, using the Hölder continuity of the foliation, the above difference is again controled by
d(x,x^{\prime})^{\alpha}
and using the definition of the distributional product yields (
A.1
).
∎

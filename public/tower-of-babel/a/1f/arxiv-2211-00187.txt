Introduction

We will come to these reformulations through the theory of semigroups. Recall a semigroup
S

[

[

The elements in the subsemigroup Orientable(

that are solutions to an
orientable
equation, a certain type of simple equation in one variable over
S

and

\sigma_{orient}

that if

did contain an identity element then the elements belonging to Orientable (
S
) could also be characterized as those elements that are
\sigma_{orient}
-congruent to
S
’s identity element.

Now in a certain sense, as somewhat indicated in the above paragraph, the subsemigroup Orientable(

) determines the congruence
\sigma_{orient}
on

[

serves as its identity element, in the case when Orientable(
S

that gives rise to a quotient group

that is a commutative group, the abelianization of

, and where the commutator subgroup
[G,G]

To further strengthen these resemblances it was also shown in

]

Main Results

Recall that for group

[G,G]
serves as its identity element. All of these claims can be found in most undergraduate abstract algebra textbooks, we cite two
[
Hu
,

]

we will use the notation

to denote its congruence class in the

, i.e.
[g]=\{h|h\in G\hskip 2.84544pt
and
\hskip 2.84544pth\sim g\}

If

Proof.

\Rightarrow

\in

\Leftarrow

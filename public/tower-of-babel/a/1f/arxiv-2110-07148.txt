Denominators in Lusztig’s asymptotic Hecke algebra via the Plancherel formula
Stefan Dawydiak

Abstract
Let W ~ \tilde{W} be an extended affine Weyl group, 𝐇 \mathbf{H} be the corresponding affine Hecke algebra over the ring ℂ ⁡ [ 𝐪 1 2 , 𝐪 − 1 2 ] \mathbb{C}[\mathbf{q}^{\frac{1}{2}},\mathbf{q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}] , and J J be Lusztig’s asymptotic Hecke algebra, viewed as a based ring with basis { t w } \{t_{w}\} . Viewing J J as a subalgebra of the ( 𝐪 − 1 2 ) (\mathbf{q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}) -adic completion of 𝐇 \mathbf{H} via Lusztig’s map ϕ \phi , we use Harish-Chandra’s Plancherel formula for p p -adic groups to show that the coefficient of T x T_{x} in t w t_{w} is a rational function of 𝐪 \mathbf{q} , with denominator depending only on the two-sided cell containing w w , and dividing a power of the Poincaré polynomial of the finite Weyl group. As an application, we conjecture that these denominators encode more detailed information about the failure of the Kazhdan-Lusztig classification at roots of the Poincaré polynomial than is currently known. Along the way, we show that upon specializing 𝐪 = q > 1 \mathbf{q}=q>1 , the map from J J to the Harish-Chandra Schwartz algebra is injective. As an application of injectivity, we give a novel criterion for an Iwahori-spherical representation to have fixed vectors under a larger parahoric subgroup in terms of its Kazhdan-Lusztig parameter.



Keywords—
Asymptotic Hecke algebra, Iwahori-Hecke algebra, Plancherel formula, parahoric subgroup

Introduction

Let
\tilde{W}

When

to the category of admissible representations with nonzero Iwahori-fixed vector of some
p

A form of local Langlands correspondence, the Deligne-Langlands conjecture, has been established
by Kazhdan and Lusztig in
[
23

using algebraic

[
12

must chose a complex number

by which

will act. Decomposing the

-theory of certain
subvarieties of Springer fibres into irreducible representations of a certain finite group yields

example, simple tempered representations, which play an essential role in the present paper, are of this form), but in
general simple modules are obtained as a certain unique nonzero quotient of standard modules. This quotient
exists when

is not a root of unity, but can be zero otherwise. Lusztig conjectured

[

[

Xi also showed that the classification fails in

\mathbf{H}|_{\mathbf{q}=q}
-modules attached to the lowest two-sided cell.
Our results in this paper explain that the lowest two-sided cell is, in a precise sense, maximally singular
with respect to the parameter
\mathbf{q}
.

One way Lusztig expressed the uniformity in
q
of the representation theory of the various algebras
\mathbf{H}|_{\mathbf{q}=q}
is via the asymptotic Hecke algebra
J
. This is a

-algebra (in fact, a

-algebra)

, equipped with an injection

-modules to

\mathbf{H}
-modules, and Lusztig has shown in
[
27

and
[
28

that when

induces a bijection between simple

\mathbf{H}|_{\mathbf{q}=q}
-modules and simple

\mathbb{C}

The map

and

In this way one can write a basis element

()^{\dagger}\circ\phi^{-1}(t_{w})=\sum_{x\in\tilde{W}}a_{x,w}T_{x},

In light of the above, it is natural

In Section

1.3
The affine Hecke algebra

Let
F
be a non-archimedean local field,
\mathcal{O}
its ring
of integers and
\varpi
be a uniformizer. Let
q
be the cardinality of the residue field. Then

q=p^{r}
is a prime power. We write
|\cdot|_{F}
for the
p
-adic absolute value on
F
; when necessary,

|\cdot|_{\infty}
will denote the archimedean absolute value on
\mathbb{C}
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive algebraic group defined and split over
F
,
\mathbf{A}
a maximal torus of
\mathbf{G}
, and

X_{*}=X_{*}(\mathbf{A})
the cocharacter lattice of
\mathbf{A}
. Let
\mathbf{N}
be unipotent radical of a chosen Borel subgroup
\mathbf{B}
, so that
\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{N}
. Let
W
be the finite Weyl group of
\mathbf{G}
, and
\tilde{W}=W\ltimes X_{*}(\mathbf{A})
be
the extended affine Weyl group. Write
S
for the set of simple
reflections in
\tilde{W}
. Let
\mathbf{G}^{\vee}
be the Langlands dual group of
\mathbf{G}
, taken over
\mathbb{C}
. We write
G=\mathbf{G}(F)
,
A=\mathbf{A}(F)
, etc. Where there is no danger of confusion, we also write

G^{\vee}
for
\mathbf{G}^{\vee}(\mathbb{C})
,

M^{\vee}
for
\mathbf{M}^{\vee}(\mathbb{C})
, etc. Let
K
be the maximal
compact subgroup
\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})
. Also let

be the Iwahori subgroup of

that is the preimage of

\mathbf{B}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. We sometimes write
P_{\mathbf{G}/\mathbf{B}}
for

, as this polynomial is also the Poincaré polynomial of the flag variety
(\mathbf{G}/\mathbf{B})(\mathbb{C})
.

We write
\mathbf{H}
for the affine Hecke algebra of
\tilde{W}
. It is a unital associative algebra over the ring

\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{q}^{\frac{1}{2}},\mathbf{q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}]
(in fact, it is defined over

\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{q}^{\frac{1}{2}},\mathbf{q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}]
but we will work over

to avoid having to introduce extra notation later), where
\mathbf{q}^{\frac{1}{2}}
is a formal variable. We will think of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
as

\Spec\mathcal{A}
. The algebra
\mathbf{H}
has the Coxeter presentation with standard basis

\{T_{w}\}_{w\in\tilde{W}}
with
T_{w}T_{w^{\prime}}=T_{ww^{\prime}}
if
\ell(ww^{\prime})=\ell(w)+\ell(w^{\prime})
and quadratic relation

(T_{s}+1)(T_{s}-\mathbf{q})=0
for

. We write
\theta_{\lambda}
for the generators of the Bernstein subalgebra.

Recall from
[
22
]
the two Kazhdan-Lusztig bases

and
\{C^{\prime}_{w}\}_{w\in\tilde{W}}
of
\mathbf{H}
and write

C_{x}C_{y}=\sum_{z\in\tilde{W}}h_{x,y,z}C_{z}
.
The
inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials

Q_{y,x}
are the unique family of polynomials
satisfying

T_{x}=\sum_{y\leq x}(-1)^{\ell(x)-\ell(y)}\mathbf{q}^{\frac{\ell(y)}{2}}Q_{y,x}(\mathbf{q})C^{\prime}_{y},

or equivalently,

\sum_{z\leq y\leq x}(-1)^{\ell(x)-\ell(y)}Q_{y,x}(\mathbf{q})P_{z,y}(\mathbf{q})=\delta_{z,x}

along with some restrictions on their degrees. For example, we shall use in Section

3.3.2
that

\deg Q_{y,x}\leq\frac{1}{2}(\ell(x)-\ell(y)-1)
. See
[
8
]
for further exposition.

It is well-known that there is an isomorphism of associative
\mathbb{C}
-algebras

\mathbf{H}|_{\mathbf{q}=q}:=\mathbf{H}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{C}\to C_{c}^{\infty}(G)^{I\times I}=:H,

where
\mathbf{q}
acts on
\mathbb{C}
by multiplication by

.

1.4
The asymptotic Hecke algebra

Definition 1
.

Lusztig’s a-function

a\colon\tilde{W}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is defined such that
a(w)
is the minimal value such that

\mathbf{q}^{\frac{a(w)}{2}}h_{x,y,w}\in\mathcal{A}^{+}=\Spec\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{q}^{1/2}]
for all
x,y\in\tilde{W}
.

The

-function is constant of two-sided cells of
\tilde{W}
. Obviously,
a(1)=1
,
and the
a
-function obtains its maximum, equal to the number of positive roots, on the two-sided cell
containing the longest word
w_{0}\in W
. In general, under the bijection between two-sided cells
\mathbf{c}
of

\tilde{W}
and unipotent conjugacy classes

in

of
[
28
]

a(\mathbf{c})=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{B}_{u}^{\vee}),

where
\mathcal{B}_{u}^{\vee}
is the Springer fibre of

. We have
a(w)\leq\ell(w)
for all

.

In
[
26
]
, Lusztig defined an associative algebra

J
over
\mathbb{C}
equipped with an injection
\phi\colon\mathbf{H}\mathrel{\hookrightarrow}J\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{A}

which becomes an isomorphism after taking a certain completion, to be recalled in Section

3.3.1
,
of both sides. As an abelian group,

is free with basis
\{t_{w}\}_{w\in\tilde{W}}
. The structure constants of
J
are obtained
from those of
\mathbf{H}
written in the
\{C_{w}\}_{w\in\tilde{W}}
-basis
under the following procedure: first, the integer
\gamma_{x,y,z}
is defined by the
condition

\mathbf{q}^{\frac{a(z)}{2}}h_{x,y,z^{-1}}-\gamma_{x,y,z}\in\mathbf{q}\mathcal{A}^{+}.

One then defines

t_{x}t_{y}=\sum_{z\in\tilde{W}}\gamma_{x,y,z}t_{z^{-1}}.

Surprisingly, this defines a unital associative algebra with unit

1_{J}=\sum_{d\in\mathcal{D}}t_{d},

where
\mathcal{D}
is the (finite) set of
distinguished involutions

[
26
]
. The elements

are orthogonal idempotents in

, which
decomposes as a direct sum indexed
by the two-sided cells of
\tilde{W}
in the sense of
[
25
]
. Each left cell, again in the sense of
op. cit.
, contains
a single distinguished involution which is the unit in the ring
t_{d}Jt_{d}
.
If
\mathbf{c}
is a two-sided cell, then
J_{\mathbf{c}}
is a
is a unital ring with unit

1_{J_{\mathbf{c}}}=\sum_{d\in\mathcal{D}\cap\mathbf{c}}t_{d}.

Lusztig further defined a map of algebras

\phi\colon\mathbf{H}\to J\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{A}

given by

\phi(C_{w})=\sum_{z\in W,~d\in\mathcal{D},~a(z)=a(d)}h_{x,d,z}t_{z}.

Write
\phi_{q}
for the specialization of this map when
\mathbf{q}=q
.
It is known
[
27
, Proposition 1.7]
that

is injective for all

1.4.1
Deformations of the group ring

Upon setting
\mathbf{q}=1
,
\mathbf{H}|_{\mathbf{q}=1}
is isomorphic to

, and so

is a deformation of
the group algebra of

.

Let, temporarily,

be any finite Coxeter group. Then one can define its Hecke algebra

, an algebra over

which deforms the group ring

. Let

. For
all but finitely-many values of

, all roots of unity, the algebras

are trivial deformations of

, and hence are all isomorphic.
However, this isomorphism requires choosing a square root of

. The affine Hecke algebra provides a canonical
isomorphism: away from finitely-many

is isomorphic to

is defined over

(although, as stated above, we will view it as a

-
algebra to unburden notation), see
[
30

Example 1
.

Let
W=\left\langle 1,s\,\middle|\,s^{2}=1\right\rangle
be the Weyl group of type
A_{1}
. The Kazhdan-Lusztig

-basis elements
are

and
C_{s}=\mathbf{q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}T_{s}-\mathbf{q}^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{1}
. There are two two-sided cells in

,
and one can easily check that

\phi\colon C_{1}\mapsto t_{1}+t_{s}

and

\phi\colon C_{s}\mapsto-\left(\mathbf{q}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathbf{q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)t_{s}.

Specializing
\mathbf{q}=q

becomes an isomorphism whenever

2.2
Harish-Chandra’s canonical measure

In this section, we recall the standard coordinates used in
[
4
]
, and
[
43
]
.
We follow both references closely. These conventions differ slightly from the original
[
20
]
.
Everything in this section is standard, but we include details because we do require explicit measures with which to compute.
We state the below for general
\mathbf{G}
, for application to each standard Levi subgroup of
\mathbf{G}

2.2.1
Unramified characters

Let
X(\mathbf{G})=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{G},{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}})
denote the rational characters of
\mathbf{G}
defined over
F
.
Let
\mathfrak{a}_{G}:=(X^{*}(\mathbf{A})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R})^{*}=(X^{*}(\mathbf{G})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R})^{*}
be the real Lie algebra of the maximal split central torus
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{A}_{G}
of
\mathbf{G}
and let
{\mathfrak{a}_{G}}_{\mathbb{C}}
be its complexification. We have a map

X(\mathbf{G})\to\mathrm{Hom}(G/G^{1},\mathbb{C}^{\times})=:\mathcal{X}(G)

given by
\chi\mapsto|\chi|_{F}
, where
|\chi|_{F}(g)=|\chi(g)|_{F}
and

G^{1}=\bigcap_{\chi\in X(\mathbf{G})}\ker|\chi|_{F}
.
This gives the unramified characters
\mathcal{X}(G)
a complex manifold structure under which

\mathcal{X}(G)\simeq(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathfrak{a}_{G}}
. For indeed, we have
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{a}_{G}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{X}(G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}

given by

\chi\otimes s\mapsto(g\mapsto|\chi(g)|_{F}^{s}=q^{-s\val(\chi(g))}).

The kernel is spanned by all
\chi\otimes s
such that
s\val(\chi(g))\in\frac{2\pi i}{\log q}\mathbb{Z}
for all

g\in G
. Hence the kernel is
\frac{2\pi i}{\log q}R
, where
R\subset X^{*}(\mathbf{A})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
is a
lattice. In this way the quotient
\mathcal{X}(G)
is a complex manifold.

2.2.2
Unitary unramified characters

Denote
\mathrm{Im}\,\mathcal{X}(G)
the set of unitary unramified characters taking values in
the unit circle
\mathbb{T}\subset\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. This notation is justified, as if
|\chi|_{F}=|\chi^{\prime}|_{F}
, then

\mathrm{Re}(\chi)=\mathrm{Re}(\chi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{a}_{G}
. Hence we can define
\mathrm{Im}\,\mathcal{X}(G)
to be the unramified
characters coming from pure imaginary elements of
{\mathfrak{a}_{G}}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

The surjection
\mathrm{Im}\,\mathcal{X}(G)\to\mathrm{Im}\,\mathcal{X}(A)
has finite kernel, and
\mathrm{Im}\,\mathcal{X}(A)
is
compact. We chose the Haar measure on it with volume one.

2.2.3
Action by twisting and the canonical measure

The group
\mathcal{X}(G)
acts on admissible representations of
G
by
twisting:
\omega\mapsto\omega\otimes\nu
for
\nu\in\mathcal{X}(G)
. This restricts to an action the unitary
unramified characters on
\mathcal{E}_{2}(G)
.

Pulling this action back to
i\mathfrak{a}_{G}
, and given a representation
\omega
, let
L^{*}
be its stabilizer in
i\mathfrak{a}_{G}
, so that the orbit
\mathfrak{o}
of
\omega
is identified as
i{\mathfrak{a}_{G}}/L^{*}
. This gives

\mathfrak{o}=\mathrm{Im}\,\mathcal{X}(G)\cdot\omega
the structure of a real submanifold of the larger orbit

\mathfrak{o}_{\mathbb{C}}={\mathfrak{a}_{G}}_{\mathbb{C}}/L^{*}=\mathcal{X}(G)\cdot\omega
.

Definition 9
.

Given an orbit
\mathfrak{o}\subset\mathcal{E}_{2}(G)
, the
Harish-Chandra
canonical measure
on
\mathfrak{o}
is the Euclidean measure on
\mathfrak{o}
whose pullback to

\mathrm{Im}\,\mathcal{X}(G)
agrees with the pullback of the Haar measure on
\mathrm{Im}\,\mathcal{X}(A)
.

Hence even though the construction of the canonical measure is slightly involved, in practice it will
be easy to recognize as being essentially the Haar measure on the compact torus
\mathrm{Im}\,\mathcal{X}(A)
.

Example 2
.

If
G=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)
and

is the diagonal torus, we have
\mathfrak{a}_{G}^{*}\simeq\mathbb{R}

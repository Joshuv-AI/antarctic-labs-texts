Orthonormal Strichartz estimate for dispersive equations with potentials
Akitoshi Hoshiya

Abstract
In this paper we prove the orthonormal Strichartz estimates for the higher order and fractional Schrödinger, wave, Klein-Gordon and Dirac equations with potentials. As in the case of the Schrödinger operator, the proofs are based on the smooth perturbation theory by T. Kato. However, for the Klein-Gordon and Dirac equations, we also use a method of the microlocal analysis in order to prove the estimates for wider range of admissible pairs. As applications we prove the global existence of a solution to the higher order or fractional Hartree equation with potentials which describes the dynamics of infinitely many particles. We also give a local existence result for the semi-relativistic Hartree equation with electromagnetic potentials. As another application, the refined Strichartz estimates are proved for higher order and fractional Schrödinger, wave and Klein-Gordon equations.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we prove the orthonormal Strichartz estimates for the higher order or fractional Schrödinger operators based on an abstract perturbation method and apply them to the Hartree equations and the refined Strichartz estimates. In Section 3, we prove the estimates for the wave, Klein-Gordon and Dirac equations by using the perturbation method and the microlocal analysis and apply them to the semi-relativistic Hartree equation and the refined Strichartz estimates. In Appendix A and B, we prove the abstract perturbation theorems for the ordinary Strichartz estimates for the wave and Klein-Gordon equations respectively including the endpoint case. We also give their applications to some operators including the magnetic Schrödinger operator, higher order or fractional Schrödinger operator.

3
Wave, Klein-Gordon and Dirac equations

In this section, we consider the orthonormal Strichartz estimates for wave, the Klein-Gordon and the Dirac equations. First, we consider wave and the Klein-Gordon equations. For some restricted pairs
(p,q)
, we can prove the estimate easily under less assumptions. However, it is difficult to prove the estimates for all the pairs since in some cases we need to handle derivatives. In such cases we use the method of microlocal analysis to deduce the estimates. By using these results, we prove the local existence of a solution to the semi-relativistic Hartree equation for infinitely many particles. Next we consider the Dirac equation. Concerning the orthonormal Strichartz estimates for the Dirac equation, there seems to be no result. So first, we deduce the estimates for the free operator based on that of the Klein-Gordon equation and the vector-valued duality principle. Then we consider the massive Dirac equation with potentials. In this way, we also use the construction of the parametrix for some fractional powers of elliptic operators based on the microlocal analysis. For wave and the Klein-Gordon equations, we also consider the refined Strichartz estimates.

3.1

]
. In
[

]
, the optimality of their estimates was also proved. However there seems to be no result for both wave and the Klein-Gordon equations with potentials. \displaystyle\|f\sin t\sqrt{H_{0}}H^{-1/4}_{0}(H^{-1/4}_{0}H^{1/4})(H^{1/4}H^{-1/4}_{0})\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2\alpha^{\prime}}_{x\rightarrow t,x}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2p^{\prime}}_{t}L^{2q^{\prime}}_{x}}

holds. For the last term, it suffices to show

\left\|f\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(t-s)\sqrt{H_{0}}}{\sqrt{H_{0}}}(Ve^{-is\sqrt{H}}H^{-1/4}_{0})ds\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2\alpha^{\prime}}_{x\rightarrow t,x}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2p^{\prime}}_{t}L^{2q^{\prime}}_{x}}

by Lemma
2.12
. By our assumption, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|f\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(t-s)\sqrt{H_{0}}}{\sqrt{H_{0}}}(Ve^{-is\sqrt{H}}H^{-1/4}_{0})ds\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2\alpha^{\prime}}_{x\rightarrow t,x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|f(t)\sin t\sqrt{H_{0}}H^{-1/4}_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos s\sqrt{H_{0}}H^{-1/4}_{0}Ve^{-is\sqrt{H}}H^{-1/4}_{0}ds\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2\alpha^{\prime}}_{x\rightarrow t,x}}

\displaystyle+\left\|f(t)\cos t\sqrt{H_{0}}H^{-1/4}_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sin s\sqrt{H_{0}}H^{-1/4}_{0}Ve^{-is\sqrt{H}}H^{-1/4}_{0}ds\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2\alpha^{\prime}}_{x\rightarrow t,x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2p^{\prime}}_{t}L^{2q^{\prime}}_{x}}\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos s\sqrt{H_{0}}H^{-1/4}_{0}Ve^{-is\sqrt{H}}H^{-1/4}_{0}ds\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2})}

\displaystyle+\|f\|_{L^{2p^{\prime}}_{t}L^{2q^{\prime}}_{x}}\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\sin s\sqrt{H_{0}}H^{-1/4}_{0}Ve^{-is\sqrt{H}}H^{-1/4}_{0}ds\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2})}.

For the first term, by Lemma
3.2
,

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos s\sqrt{H_{0}}H^{-1/4}_{0}Ve^{-is\sqrt{H}}H^{-1/4}_{0}uds,v\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\langle v_{2}e^{-is\sqrt{H}}H^{-1/4}_{0}u,v_{1}\cos s\sqrt{H_{0}}H^{-1/4}_{0}v\right\rangle ds\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v_{2}e^{-is\sqrt{H}}H^{-1/4}_{0}u\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\|v_{1}\cos s\sqrt{H_{0}}H^{-1/4}_{0}v\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|H^{1/4}H^{-1/4}_{0}u\|_{2}\|v\|_{2}\lesssim\|u\|_{2}\|v\|_{2}

holds. By the duality argument we obtain

\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos s\sqrt{H_{0}}H^{-1/4}_{0}Ve^{-is\sqrt{H}}H^{-1/4}_{0}ds\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2})}\lesssim 1.

By repeating similar calculations, we also obtain

\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\sin s\sqrt{H_{0}}H^{-1/4}_{0}Ve^{-is\sqrt{H}}H^{-1/4}_{0}ds\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2})}\lesssim 1.

Hence we have the desired estimates.
∎

Next we extend the orthonormal Strichartz estimates for more general

. In order to prove the estimate for

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
\mathcal{H}:=L^{2}(X)
for a
\sigma
-finite measure space
X
. Assume self-adjoint operators
H,H_{0}
and a densely defined closed operator
V
satisfy

H=H_{0}+V,\quad D(H)\cup D(H_{0})\subset D(V).

Furthermore we assume there exist
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, densely defined injective closed operators
w
and
W
which satisfy the following:

\displaystyle H+1,H_{0}+1\geq 0,\quad(H+1)^{\theta}(H_{0}+1)^{-\theta}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),

\displaystyle(H_{0}+1)^{\theta-1/2}(H+1)^{1/2}(H_{0}+1)^{-\theta}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),

\displaystyle w^{-1}(H+1)^{-\theta+1/4}V(H_{0}+1)^{\theta-1/4}W^{-1}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}).

Suppose
w
is
H
-smooth and
W
is
H_{0}
-smooth.
If we have

\left\|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\nu_{n}|e^{-it\sqrt{H_{0}+1}}f_{n}|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}}\lesssim\|\nu_{n}\|_{\ell^{\alpha}}

for some
p,q\in[1,\infty]
,
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
and all
\{f_{n}\}
: orthonormal systems in
D((H_{0}+1)^{\theta})
, then

\left\|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\nu_{n}|e^{-it\sqrt{H+1}}f_{n}|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}}\lesssim\|\nu_{n}\|_{\ell^{\alpha}}

holds for the same
p,q,\alpha,\theta,\{f_{n}\}
.

Proof.

By the Duhamel formula, we obtain

\displaystyle fe^{-it\sqrt{H+1}}(H_{0}+1)^{-\theta}=

\displaystyle f\cos t\sqrt{H_{0}+1}(H_{0}+1)^{-\theta}

\displaystyle-if\sin t\sqrt{H_{0}+1}(H_{0}+1)^{-\theta}(H_{0}+1)^{\theta-1/2}\sqrt{H+1}(H_{0}+1)^{-\theta}

\displaystyle-f\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin(t-s)\sqrt{H_{0}+1}}{\sqrt{H_{0}+1}}Ve^{-is\sqrt{H+1}}(H_{0}+1)^{-\theta}ds.

By the assumptions, we estimate the first and second term as

\displaystyle\|f\cos t\sqrt{H_{0}+1}(H_{0}+1)^{-\theta}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2\alpha^{\prime}}_{x\rightarrow t,x}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2p^{\prime}}_{t}L^{2q^{\prime}}_{x}},

\displaystyle\|f\sin t\sqrt{H_{0}+1}(H_{0}+1)^{-\theta}(H_{0}+1)^{\theta-1/2}\sqrt{H+1}(H_{0}+1)^{-\theta}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2\alpha^{\prime}}_{x\rightarrow t,x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2p^{\prime}}_{t}L^{2q^{\prime}}_{x}}\|(H_{0}+1)^{\theta-1/2}\sqrt{H+1}(H_{0}+1)^{-\theta}\|_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2p^{\prime}}_{t}L^{2q^{\prime}}_{x}}.

For the third term, by Lemma
2.12
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|f\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin(t-s)\sqrt{H_{0}+1}}{\sqrt{H_{0}+1}}Ve^{-is\sqrt{H+1}}(H_{0}+1)^{-\theta}ds\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2\alpha^{\prime}}_{x\rightarrow t,x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|f\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(t-s)\sqrt{H_{0}+1}}{\sqrt{H_{0}+1}}Ve^{-is\sqrt{H+1}}(H_{0}+1)^{-\theta}ds\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2\alpha^{\prime}}_{x\rightarrow t,x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2p^{\prime}}_{t}L^{2q^{\prime}}_{x}}\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}(H_{0}+1)^{\theta-1/2}\cos s\sqrt{H_{0}+1}Ve^{-is\sqrt{H+1}}(H_{0}+1)^{-\theta}ds\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}

\displaystyle+\|f\|_{L^{2p^{\prime}}_{t}L^{2q^{\prime}}_{x}}\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}(H_{0}+1)^{\theta-1/2}\sin s\sqrt{H_{0}+1}Ve^{-is\sqrt{H+1}}(H_{0}+1)^{-\theta}ds\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}.

Concerning the second factor of the first term in the last inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}(H_{0}+1)^{\theta-1/2}\cos s\sqrt{H_{0}+1}Ve^{-is\sqrt{H+1}}(H_{0}+1)^{-\theta}ds\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}(H_{0}+1)^{-1/4}\cos s\sqrt{H_{0}+1}WW^{-1}(H_{0}+1)^{\theta-1/4}V\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad\quad\quad\cdot(H+1)^{-\theta+1/4}w^{-1}we^{-is\sqrt{H+1}}(H+1)^{\theta-1/4}(H_{0}+1)^{-\theta}ds\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|W\cos t\sqrt{H_{0}+1}(H_{0}+1)^{-1/4}\|_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\rightarrow L^{2}_{t}\mathcal{H})}\cdot\|w^{-1}(H+1)^{-\theta+1/4}V(H_{0}+1)^{\theta-1/4}W^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\cdot\|we^{-it\sqrt{H+1}}(H+1)^{-1/4}\|_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\rightarrow L^{2}_{t}\mathcal{H})}\cdot\|(H+1)^{\theta}(H_{0}+1)^{-\theta}\|_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1

by our assumptions. By similar calculations, we also obtain

\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}(H_{0}+1)^{\theta-1/2}\sin s\sqrt{H_{0}+1}Ve^{-is\sqrt{H+1}}(H_{0}+1)^{-\theta}ds\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}\lesssim 1

Hence we have

\|fe^{-it\sqrt{H+1}}(H_{0}+1)^{-\theta}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2\alpha^{\prime}}_{x\rightarrow t,x}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2p^{\prime}}_{t}L^{2q^{\prime}}_{x}}

and by Lemma
2.10
, we obtain the desired estimates.
∎

3.2

In this subsection, we prove the local existence of a solution to the semi-relativistic Hartree equation with electromagnetic potentials for infinitely many particles. The ordinary semi-relativistic Hartree equations are given by

[

]

[

]

]

for an operator-valued function

Before stating our result, we define the Sobolev-type Schatten spaces. For

Theorem 3.17
.

Assume

,

are as in Lemma
3.16
and

Let

We define

\displaystyle\Phi(\gamma,\rho)=(\Phi_{1}(\gamma,\rho),\rho[\Phi_{1}(\gamma,\rho)]),

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}(\gamma,\rho)(t)=e^{-it\sqrt{H+1}}\gamma_{0}e^{it\sqrt{H+1}}-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-s)\sqrt{H+1}}[w*\rho,\gamma]e^{i(t-s)\sqrt{H+1}}ds.

3.16

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{1}(\gamma,\rho)\|_{C_{T}\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}^{\beta,s}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\gamma_{0}\|_{\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}^{\beta,s}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|[w*\rho,\gamma]\|_{\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}^{\beta,s}}ds

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sigma_{k}\\

Definition 3.19

for some

\mathcal{H}
by considering it as a matrix-valued multiplication operator:

f=\begin{pmatrix}f_{1}&&&\text{\huge{0}}\\
&f_{2}\\
&&f_{3}\\
{\text{\huge{0}}}&&&f_{4}\\
\end{pmatrix}

Note that density functions are uniquely determined by
\gamma
if they exist by the fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations. They are also denoted by
\rho(\gamma)
. Next we prove the vector-valued duality principle.

Lemma 3.20
.

Assume

\|fe^{-it(\mathcal{D}+\beta+V)}\{(\mathcal{D}+\beta)^{2}\}^{s}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2\alpha^{\prime}}_{x\rightarrow t,x}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2p^{\prime}}_{t}L^{2q^{\prime}}_{x}}

for

and

.

In the above lemma, since

are well-defined for all

Proof.

[

\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}\{|f_{1}|^{2},|f_{2}|^{2},|f_{3}|^{2},|f_{4}|^{2}\}
for

not on

∎

Since we obtain

\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}(f|u\rangle\langle u|)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sum_{j}f_{j}(x)|u_{j}(x)|^{2}dx

for all

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\nu_{n}|e^{-it(\mathcal{D}+\beta+V)}g_{n}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}}\lesssim\|\{\nu_{n}\}\|_{\ell^{\alpha}}

for

, which is an orthonormal system in
H^{-2s}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{C}^{4})
. By using the above argument and the orthonormal Strichartz estimates for the Klein-Gordon equation, we prove the orthonormal Strichartz estimates for the free massive Dirac equation.

Theorem 3.21
.

Let
q,r,s,\beta
be as in Corollary
1.9
with

. Then we have

\left\|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\nu_{n}|e^{-it(\mathcal{D}+\beta)}g_{n}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{q/2}_{t}L^{r/2}_{x}}\lesssim\|\{\nu_{n}\}\|_{\ell^{\beta}}

for all orthonormal systems
\{g_{n}\}
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{C}^{4})

Proof.

As in

e^{-it(\mathcal{D}+\beta)}=I_{4}\cos t\sqrt{1-\Delta}-i(\mathcal{D}+\beta)\frac{\sin t\sqrt{1-\Delta}}{\sqrt{1-\Delta}}

\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle g=(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},g_{4})\mapsto(0,\dots,\underbrace{\cos t\sqrt{1-\Delta}g_{j}}_{j},\cdots,0),

\displaystyle g=(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},g_{4})\mapsto(0,\dots,\underbrace{\psi(D)\sin t\sqrt{1-\Delta}g_{j}}_{k},\cdots,0).

Here
j,k\in\{1,2,3,4\},\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and it satisfies
|\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi}\psi(\xi)|\lesssim\langle\xi\rangle^{-|\alpha|}
. Since the former term is easier to treat, we only focus on the latter term. In order to use Lemma
3.20

\displaystyle f\cdot[g=(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},g_{4})\mapsto(0,\dots,\underbrace{\psi(D)\sin t\sqrt{1-\Delta}g_{j}}_{k},\cdots,0)]\cdot\{(\mathcal{D}+\beta)^{2}\}^{-s/2}

\displaystyle=[g\mapsto(0,\dots,\underbrace{f_{k}\sin t\sqrt{1-\Delta}\{(\mathcal{D}+\beta)^{2}\}^{-s/2}\psi(D)g_{j}}_{k},\dots,0)]

\displaystyle=[v\mapsto(0,\dots,\underbrace{v}_{k},\dots,0)]\circ[u\mapsto f_{k}\sin t\sqrt{1-\Delta}(1-\Delta)^{-s/2}u]\circ[g_{j}\mapsto\psi(D)g_{j}]\circ[g\mapsto g_{j}]

\displaystyle=T_{1}\circ T_{2}\circ T_{3}\circ T_{4}.

Here
T_{1}:L^{2}_{t}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{C})\rightarrow L^{2}_{t}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{C}^{4})

T_{3}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{C})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{C})

\|T_{1}\circ T_{2}\circ T_{3}\circ T_{4}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2\beta^{\prime}}(\mathcal{H}\rightarrow L^{2}_{t}\mathcal{H})}\lesssim\|f_{k}\|_{L^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}_{t}L^{2(r/2)^{\prime}}_{x}}

By taking a summation for

Then by Lemma
3.20

∎

If

\displaystyle H^{-s/2}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}z^{-s/2}(z-H)^{-1}dz

,

[

]

\displaystyle\langle x\rangle^{|\alpha|}\langle\xi\rangle^{|\beta|}|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}b^{jj}_{n}(x,\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-s/2}h^{n}(m+\lambda)^{-1}d\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{n}m^{-1}\int_{0}^{m}\lambda^{-s/2}d\lambda+h^{n}\int_{m}^{\infty}\lambda^{-1-s/2}d\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim m^{-s/2}h^{n}

holds. Now we define

a^{jj}_{-s/2}\sim\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b^{jj}_{n}\in S(m^{-s/2},g).

\displaystyle(H-z)\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}\{(p^{jj}_{0})^{W}(x,D_{x},z)+\dots+(p^{jj}_{n})^{W}(x,D_{x},z)\}=I+R_{n}(z)+S_{n}(z),

\displaystyle R_{n}(z)=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}\{(r^{jj}_{n})^{W}(x,D_{x},z)\},S_{n}(z)\in OPS(\langle\xi\rangle(m+\lambda)^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-2},g)

denotes the diagonal matrix whose

. Hence we obtain

\displaystyle(H-z)^{-1}=

holds. By substituting (

\displaystyle H^{-s/2}=

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}\{(a^{jj}_{-s/2})^{W}(x,D_{x})\}+\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}\{(c^{jj}_{n})^{W}(x,D_{x})\}

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}z^{-s/2}(z-H)^{-1}(R^{*}_{n}(\bar{z})+S^{*}_{n}(\bar{z}))dz

for some

]

]

Theorem A.1

(non endpoint case)
.

Suppose
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space and self-adjoint operators
H,H_{0}
and densely defined closed operators
v_{1},v_{2}
satisfy

D(H)\cup D(H_{0})\subset D(v_{1})\cap D(v_{2})\quad\text{and}\quad\langle Hu,v\rangle=\langle H_{0}u,v\rangle+\langle v_{2}u,v_{1}v\rangle

for all

H
and
H_{0}
are injective,
v_{1}
is
H_{0}
-smooth,
v_{2}P_{\mathrm{ac}}(H)
is
H
-smooth,

\mathcal{A}

[

]

By the Duhamel formula we have

Example A.12

Lemma B.5

(Low energy estimate)
.

For all
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
m\geq 2
and
\frac{d}{2}
admissible pairs
(p,q)
, we have

\left\||D|^{\frac{2(m-1)}{p}}e^{-itP_{m}(D)}\chi_{\{|D|\lesssim 1\}}u\right\|_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q,2}_{x}}\lesssim\|u\|_{2}

Proof.

We follow the argument in
[
MY1
]
. By the Littlewood-Paley theorem, the Minkowski inequality and the real interpolation, we have

\displaystyle\|\chi_{\{|D|\lesssim 1\}}u\|_{L^{q,2}}\lesssim\left(\sum_{j\leq 1}\|\phi_{j}(D)\chi_{\{|D|\lesssim 1\}}u\|^{2}_{L^{q,2}}\right)^{1/2},

(B.4)

where
\phi_{j}
denotes a homogeneous Littlewood-Paley decomposition. Since we have

|\nabla P_{m}(\xi)|\approx|\xi|^{2m-1},\quad|\mathop{\mathrm{Hess}}P_{m}(\xi)|\approx|\xi|^{d(2m-2)}

on
\{|\xi|\lesssim 1\}
, by Theorem 3.1 in
[
HHZ
]
, we obtain

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{ix\xi-itP_{m}(\xi)}|\xi|^{d(m-1)}\chi_{\{|\xi|\lesssim 1\}}d\xi\right|\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}.

Hence we have the dispersive estimate
\||D|^{d(m-1)}e^{-itP_{m}(D)}\chi_{\{|D|\lesssim 1\}}\|_{1\rightarrow\infty}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}
. Since
\||2^{-j}D|^{d(1-m)}\phi_{j}(D)\|_{\infty\rightarrow\infty}\lesssim 1
holds, we obtain
\|e^{-itP_{m}(D)}\phi_{j}(D)\|_{1\rightarrow\infty}\lesssim 2^{-jd(m-1)}|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}
for
j\leq 1
. Thus

\|\phi_{j}(D)e^{-i2^{-2j(m-1)}(t-s)P_{m}(D)}\phi_{j}(D)\|_{1\rightarrow\infty}\lesssim|t-s|^{-\frac{d}{2}}

holds and by the Keel-Tao theorem (
[
KT
]
), we obtain

\|e^{-i2^{-2j(m-1)}tP_{m}(D)}\phi_{j}(D)u\|_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q,2}_{x}}\lesssim\|u\|_{2}.

Then, by changing variables, (
B.4
) and the Minkowski inequality, we obtain the desired estimates.
∎

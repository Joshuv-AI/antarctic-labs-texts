Abstract
. We consider the relativistic Vlasov-Maxwell system in three dimensions and study the limiting asymptotic behavior as t → ∞ t\to\infty of solutions launched by small, compactly supported initial data. In particular, we prove that such solutions scatter to a modification of the free-streaming asymptotic profile. More specifically, we show that the spatial average of the particle distribution function converges to a smooth, compactly-supported limit and establish the precise, self-similar asymptotic behavior of the electric and magnetic fields, as well as, the macroscopic densities and their derivatives in terms of this limiting function. Upon constructing the limiting fields, a modified L ∞ L^{\infty} scattering result for the particle distribution function along the associated trajectories of free transport corrected by the limiting Lorentz force is then obtained. When the limiting charge density does not vanish, our estimates are sharp up to a logarithmic correction. However, when this quantity is identically zero in the limit, the limiting current density and fields may also vanish, which gives rise to decay rates that are faster than those attributed to the dispersive mechanisms in the system.



1.
Introduction

The motion of a collisionless plasma, namely a completely ionized gas that is sufficiently dilute to neglect collisional effects and sufficiently hot to consider relativistic velocities, is often modeled by the relativistic Vlasov-Maxwell system. Within this system charged particles interact with one another via self-consistent electromagnetic forces induced by the charges. In particular, the plasma consists of
N\in\mathbb{N}
different species, e.g. ions and electrons, each with associated masses
m_{\alpha}>0
and charges
e_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}
for
\alpha=1,...,N
. Additionally, each particle species is described in phase space by a distribution function
f^{\alpha}(t,x,p)
, which represents a number density at time
t\geq 0
, position
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and momentum
p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
The motion of particles is then governed by the Vlasov equation

(RVM
1
)

\partial_{t}f^{\alpha}+v_{\alpha}(p)\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{\alpha}+e_{\alpha}\left(E+\frac{v_{\alpha}(p)}{c}\times B\right)\cdot\nabla_{p}f^{\alpha}=0,

where
c
is the speed of light and the relativistic velocity of the
\alpha
th species is given by

(RVM
2
)

v_{\alpha}(p)=\frac{p}{\sqrt{m_{\alpha}^{2}+|p|^{2}/c^{2}}}.

The distribution function gives rise to charge and current densities, defined by

(RVM
3
)

\rho(t,x)=4\pi\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}e_{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f^{\alpha}(t,x,p)\,dp\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad j(t,x)=4\pi\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}e_{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v_{\alpha}(p)f^{\alpha}(t,x,p)\ dp,

and these densities generate electromagnetic fields
E(t,x)
and
B(t,x)
according to Maxwell’s equations

(RVM
4
)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}E-c\nabla\times B=-j,\hskip 21.62393pt\nabla\cdot E=\rho

\displaystyle\partial_{t}B+c\nabla\times E=0,\hskip 28.45274pt\nabla\cdot B=0.

The equations (
RVM1
)-(
RVM4
), which we will group together and denote by (
RVM
) are then supplemented by the initial conditions
f(0,x,p)=f_{0}(x,p)
,
E(0,x)=E_{0}(x)
and
B(0,x)=B_{0}(x)
,
which must further satisfy the compatibility conditions

(1.1)

\nabla\cdot E_{0}(x)=\rho(0,x),\qquad\nabla\cdot B_{0}(x)=0,\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho(0,x)\ dx=0.

For each species, the particle number is conserved, namely

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f^{\alpha}(t,x,p)\ dpdx=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f^{\alpha}_{0}(x,p)\ dpdx=:\mathcal{M}^{\alpha}

for all
t\geq 0
, with the overall net charge of the system given by

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{net}}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}e_{\alpha}\mathcal{M}^{\alpha}=0

due to the global neutrality assumption within (
1.1
).
Additionally, as our methods will require precise estimates on the growth of the characteristics associated to (
RVM
), we define the functions
\mathcal{X}^{\alpha}(t,\tau,x,p)
and
\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}(t,\tau,x,p)
for all
t,\tau\geq 0
and
x,p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
as solutions of the system

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{\mathcal{X}^{\alpha}}(t,\tau,x,p)=v_{\alpha}\left(\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}(t,\tau,x,p)\right)\\
&\dot{\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}}(t,\tau,x,p)=K^{\alpha}\left(t,\mathcal{X}^{\alpha}(t,\tau,x,p),\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}(t,\tau,x,p)\right)\end{aligned}\right.

with initial conditions

\mathcal{X}^{\alpha}(\tau,\tau,x,p)=x
and

\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}(\tau,\tau,x,p)=p,

where the Lorentz force on the
\alpha
th species is defined by

K^{\alpha}(t,x,p)=e_{\alpha}\left(E(t,x)+\frac{v_{\alpha}(p)}{c}\times B(t,x)\right).

Though the global-in-time existence of classical solutions to (
RVM
) for arbitrary data remains a crucial unsolved problem in the field, a variety of small data solutions are known to exist, ranging from more classical results
[
16
,
18
,
31
]
to newer theorems
[
6
,
33
]
that utilize Fourier or vector field methods to remove the assumption that the particle distribution functions be compactly supported. We also mention
[
34
]
, which utilized methods similar to those of Glassey and Strauss
[
18
]
to establish a global existence result for small initial data without compact support, but without the requirement that the initial electromagnetic fields be small.
We further note that some recent progress has been made on the global existence problem for arbitrary size data, as well, and in particular the development of new continuation criteria that ensure the extension of a local solution
[
22
,
23
,
24
,
28
]
.
For additional background, we refer the reader to
[
11
]
as a general reference concerning (
RVM
) and associated kinetic equations.

In addition to a lack of global-in-time well-posedness of large data, classical solutions to (
RVM
), the time asymptotic behavior of small data solutions is also not completely understood. Partial results concerning the asymptotic growth or decay of quantities for (
RVM
) or its classical limit, the Vlasov-Poisson system, are known in some situations, including small data
[
1
,
6
,
20
,
21
,
32
]
, monocharged and spherically-symmetric data
[
3
,
19
,
25
]
, and lower-dimensional settings
[
2
,
5
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
30
]
.
These results generally provide either time asymptotic growth estimates of characteristics or decay estimates of the electric field and charge density.
One typically expects that, inside the light cone, the fields and macroscopic densities tend to zero as
t\to\infty
like
t^{-2}
and
t^{-3}
, respectively, for all smooth solutions of (
RVM
) due to the dispersive properties induced within the system by the transport operator
\partial_{t}+v_{\alpha}(p)\cdot\nabla_{x}
. In fact, it is known that the Cauchy problem does not possess smooth steady states (cf.,
[
15
]
).
That being said, it remains a longstanding open problem to demonstrate that smooth solutions of (
RVM
) satisfy these decay properties or scatter to a profile along the trajectories generated by the (possibly modified) free transport operator as
t\to\infty
.
Hence, the goal of the current work is to establish the precise large-time behavior of solutions to (
RVM
) and construct small data solutions that display exactly this asymptotic dynamic.

Prior to submitting the current paper, we were made aware of a recent and similar work in this direction due to Bigorgne
[
6
]
, concerning the asymptotic behavior of small data solutions to (
RVM
).
Though our results are similar, we only assume
C^{2}
data for the particle distribution, we demonstrate improved decay rates in the case of a limiting charge density that vanishes, and we do not employ vector field methods to achieve the theorems that follow.
Instead, we use more classical
a priori
estimates (in the spirit of Glassey-Strauss
[
18
]
), which significantly shortens the proof and makes the result more accessible to the PDE community, especially for those
who are unfamiliar with some of the geometric and vector field methods utilized in
[
6
]
.

For brevity, we will use the small data solutions previously constructed by Glassey and Strauss as a starting point; however, our methods can be applied to other solutions with compact momentum support, such as the nearly-neutral small-data solutions constructed by Glassey and Schaeffer
[
16
]
.
Additionally, it may be possible to remove the support assumption completely, as some recent studies have done just this for similar kinetic equations
[
7
,
9
,
21
]
.
It must be noted, however, that removal of the compact momentum support assumption for the distribution function would allow for some particles to travel at speeds arbitrarily close to the speed of light and thus, enable resonant particles to travel along the boundary of the light cone.
The additional decay of the electromagnetic fields and their derivatives inside of the light cone may be lost, and this would create a significant challenge to establishing sharp estimates of the limiting behavior of solutions.

As the small data solutions we will construct remain bounded on any finite time interval, we are essentially concerned only with large time estimates, and thus we use the notation

A(t)\lesssim B(t)

to represent the statement that there is
C>0
, independent of
t
,
such that

A(t)\leq CB(t)

for
t
sufficiently large.
When a specific constant is desired, for instance when there is
\gamma>0
such that
|x|\leq\gamma t
for
t
sufficiently large, we will include the constant in this notation, namely

(1.3)

|x|\lesssim\gamma t.

Additionally,
C
will denote a positive constant (independent of the solution) that may depend upon initial data and can change from line to line.
As
B
represents a magnetic field, throughout we will denote the balls of radius
r>0
(open and closed, respectively) by

\Gamma_{r}=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|u|<r\}\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\overline{\Gamma}_{r}=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|u|\leq r\}.

1.1.
Main Results

For
t\geq 0
and
\alpha=1,...,N
define the support of
f^{\alpha}(t)
by

\mathcal{S}_{f^{\alpha}}(t)=\overline{\left\{(x,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{6}:f^{\alpha}(t,x,p)\neq 0\right\}}.

Next, for every
p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
denote the gradient of the velocity function by

\mathbb{A}_{\alpha}(p):=\nabla v_{\alpha}(p)

and its associated inverse determinant by

\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(p)=\left|\det\mathbb{A}_{\alpha}(p)\right|^{-1}

for
\alpha=1,...,N
.
Furthermore, for every every
q\in\Gamma_{1}
let

v_{\alpha}^{-1}(q)=\frac{m_{\alpha}q}{\sqrt{1-|q|^{2}/c^{2}}},

be the inverse function of
v_{\alpha}(p)

and

\mathbb{B}_{\alpha}(q):=\nabla v_{\alpha}^{-1}(q)

be its associated gradient for
\alpha=1,...,N
.

Our main results can now be stated precisely, and, for brevity, we normalize the speed of light
c=1
throughout.
First, we establish a refined global existence theorem for small initial data that augments the previous result of
[

]

solutions from

The improved regularity of solutions will be necessary to construct suitable limits for the electric and magnetic fields in the sequel.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any

Let

Let

).
If the initial data satisfy

then there exists a unique classical solution of (
RVM
) for all

and
t\geq 0
such that

f^{\alpha}(t,x,p)=0\qquad\mathrm{for}\qquad|p|\geq\beta

for all
\alpha=1,...,N
,

, and

.
Furthermore,

|E(t,x)|+|B(t,x)|=0

and we have the estimates

,

and

.

Next, we establish precise estimates on the large time asymptotic behavior of these small data solutions.

Theorem 1.2
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
1.1
, the small data solutions satisfy

(a)

For every
\alpha=1,...,N
,
\tau\geq 0

(b)

For every

Let
\gamma:=\max\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{2\beta}{\sqrt{1+4\beta^{2}}}\right\}<1

.

Remark 1.4
.

Though we do not prove such a refinement here, we note that the improved decay estimates on
\rho
,

, and their derivatives in Theorem
1.3
, also induce faster decay of the derivatives of the electric and magnetic fields.
Indeed, following the iteration argument of Glassey and Strauss
[

]

\sup_{|x|\lesssim\gamma t}\left(\left|\nabla_{x}E(t,x)\right|+\left|\nabla_{x}B(t,x)\right|\right)\lesssim t^{-4}\ln^{8}(t).

This faster rate uniformly bounds derivatives of the translated distribution function via Lemma
3.5
(as
\mathcal{K}_{1}(t)\lesssim t^{-4}\ln^{8}(t)
; see Section
2
) and induces faster convergence of the spatial averages via Lemma
3.6
.
These results can then be iteratively utilized with our tools (see also
[
26
]
) to improve the rates on the charge and current density, as well as other quantities in the system, and ultimately obtain the subsequent estimates

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}(t,\tau,x,p)-\mathcal{P}_{\infty}^{\alpha}(\tau,x,p)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-2},

\displaystyle\|F^{\alpha}(t)-F^{\alpha}_{\infty}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-2},

\displaystyle\|\rho(t)\|_{\infty}+\|j(t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-4},

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{x}\rho(t)\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{x}j(t)\|_{\infty}+\|\partial_{t}j(t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-5},

as well as

\sup_{|x|\lesssim\gamma t}\left(\left|E(t,x)\right|+\left|B(t,x)\right|\right)\lesssim t^{-3},

and

\sup_{|x|\lesssim\gamma t}\left(\left|\nabla_{x}E(t,x)\right|+\left|\nabla_{x}B(t,x)\right|\right)\lesssim t^{-4}.

Finally, these faster rates remove the need to modify the scattering trajectories and yield the improved convergence of the particle distributions, namely

\sup_{(x,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|f^{\alpha}(t,x+v_{\alpha}(p)t,p)-f^{\alpha}_{\infty}(x,p)\right|\lesssim t^{-1}

for any
\alpha=1,...,N
.
As previously mentioned, we do not present a proof of these improvements here, and as such, do not include these statements in the above theorem.

1.2.
Idea of the Proof

To establish the scattering result, we will show that the time derivative of the distribution function decays faster than
t^{-1}
in
L^{\infty}
.
Dropping the
\alpha
notation, as well as all masses and charges, we note that this cannot hold if the particle distribution is evaluated along
(x,p)
, as the transport term
v(p)\cdot\nabla_{x}f
cannot be expected to decay in time.
However, evaluating this function along the translated curves in phase space
(x+v(p)t,p)
by defining

g(t,x,p)=f(t,x+v(p)t,p)

transforms the Vlasov equation for
g
into

(1.5)

\partial_{t}g-t\mathbb{A}(p)K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\cdot\nabla_{x}g+K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\cdot\nabla_{p}g=0.

Because the spatial support of
g
stays strictly inside the light cone, one generally expects the Lorentz force to decay like
t^{-2}
on this set (see Theorem
2.1
).
As we will demonstrate later (Lemma
3.5
), the
p
-derivatives of
g
satisfy
\|\nabla_{p}g(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\ln^{2}(t)
thereby making the last term in (
1.5
) integrable in time.
Additionally,
\|\nabla_{x}g(t)\|_{\infty}\sim 1
, which means that the second term in the transformed Vlasov equation (
1.5
) will decay at best like
t^{-1}
.
Hence, in order to induce a faster decay rate, the trajectories must be augmented by a limiting Lorentz force that is generated by the limiting electric and magnetic fields.
In particular, we further define

h(t,x,p)=g(t,x-\ln(t)\mathbb{A}(p)K_{\infty}(p),p)

so that

\partial_{t}h=t^{-1}\mathbb{A}(p)\left(t^{2}K(t,\mathcal{W}(t,x,p),p)-K_{\infty}(p)\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}g-K(t,\mathcal{W}(t,x,p),p)\cdot\nabla_{p}g

where

\mathcal{W}(t,x,p)=x+v(p)t-\ln(t)\mathbb{A}(p)K_{\infty}(p).

Upon establishing an estimate on the convergence rate of the Lorentz force inside the light cone so that
t^{2}K(t,\mathcal{W}(t,x,p),p)
tends to
K_{\infty}(p)
at some algebraic order, say
t^{-\epsilon}
for some
\epsilon>0
, we will arrive at

\|\partial_{t}h(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim t^{-1-\epsilon},

which is integrable and guarantees the existence of a limiting particle distribution along such trajectories in phase space.
Of course, this requires an understanding of the higher-order behavior of the electromagnetic fields and their limits along these same trajectories.
In turn, one must identify the higher-order convergence of the macroscopic densities and their derivatives in order to establish the limiting field behavior.
Moreover, a crucial result (Lemma
5.2
) is needed to pass from the asymptotic behavior of the densities and their derivatives to the fields, which satisfy inhomogeneous wave equations with derivatives of these density terms as sources.
Within
[
26
]
, the electrostatic scattering problem was considered, and this merely required one to deduce the asymptotic behavior of the electric field as a solution to Poisson’s equation, given the known asymptotic behavior of the charge density.
Herein, the full electromagnetic description of the system is imposed, and this requires the significantly more challenging task of preserving the asymptotic behavior of the source terms in passing to the fields via inversion of wave equations.

In the next section, we establish some preliminary results that will be used throughout the paper.
Section
3
then contains estimates on the electric and magnetic fields, characteristics, and derivatives of the translated particle distribution function, as well as, the convergence of the spatial average and charge and current densities.
Section
4
assumes an estimate on second derivatives of the fields that will be provided by Theorem
1.1
and establishes the asymptotic behavior of derivatives of the densities and the spatial average.
Estimates that provide the analogous asymptotic behavior for the electric and magnetic fields are contained within Section
5
, as is the modified scattering result for the distribution functions. The proofs of Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
, and
1.3
are provided in Section
6
. Finally, in Section
7
we briefly discuss how these results can be adapted to small data solutions of the non-relativistic Vlasov-Maxwell system.

Though our results pertain directly to multiple species, for the majority of this article we will derive estimates for the single species system with charge
e_{\alpha}=1
. Hence, the number of species

m_{\alpha}

e_{\alpha}

In Section

With this, the relativistic velocity function defined by (

Remark 2.3
.

Because of the smoothness and boundedness of the velocity map
v(p)
and its inverse, we will often neglect their contribution to future estimates, as well as those of their derivatives.

In order to establish decay of the electric and magnetic fields and their derivatives both along characteristics and along translated curves in phase space, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in[0,t]\\
(x,p)\in\mathcal{S}_{f}(\tau)\end{subarray}}\biggl(\left|\nabla^{\ell}_{x}E(t,\mathcal{X}(t,\tau,x,p))\right|+\left|\nabla^{\ell}_{x}B(t,\mathcal{X}(t,\tau,x,p))\right|\biggr)

\displaystyle\quad+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|\lesssim\ln(t)\\
|p|\leq\beta\end{subarray}}\biggl(\left|\nabla^{\ell}_{x}E(t,x+v(p)t)\right|+\left|\nabla^{\ell}_{x}B(t,x+v(p)t)\right|\biggr),

for
\ell=0,1,2
.
The next result guarantees that the fields and their derivatives decay quickly on the spatial support of the particle distribution function, which stays firmly inside of the light cone.
The growth of the spatial support of
f
will also be crucial to proving both the small data theorem and the large time behavior.
As
f
has compact support, we will estimate only the growth of those characteristics along which
f\neq 0
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Define
\zeta=\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{1+\beta^{2}}}
and
\gamma=\max\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{2\beta}{\sqrt{1+4\beta^{2}}}\right\}

where
\beta>0
is given by Theorem
2.1
, so that
0<\zeta<\gamma<1
.
Then, we have

|\mathcal{X}(t,\tau,x,p)|\lesssim\gamma t

for any
\tau\in[0,t]
,
(x,p)\in\mathcal{S}_{f}(\tau)
,
and

|\mathcal{X}(t,\tau,x,p)|\leq L+\zeta t

for all
t\geq 0,\tau\in[0,t]
,
(x,p)\in\mathcal{S}_{f}(\tau)
.
Furthermore, for
|x|\lesssim\ln(t)
and
|p|\leq\beta

we have

t-|x+v(p)t|+2L\gtrsim t.

Finally, the fields and their derivatives satisfy

\mathcal{K}_{0}(t)\lesssim t^{-2}

and

\mathcal{K}_{1}(t)\lesssim t^{-3}\ln(t).

Proof.

To begin, the first results are obtained using the compact momentum support of
f(t)
and the characteristic equations, namely

|\mathcal{X}(t,\tau,x,p)|\leq|x|+\int_{0}^{t}v\left(\mathcal{P}(s,\tau,x,p)\right)ds\leq L+\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{1+\beta^{2}}}t=L+\zeta t\lesssim\frac{2\beta}{\sqrt{1+4\beta^{2}}}t\leq\gamma t.

Next, we establish the spatial inequality on translated curves.
The growth assumptions of
x
and
p
imply

|x+v(p)t|\lesssim\ln(t)+\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{1+\beta^{2}}}t\lesssim\gamma t,

and thus

t-|x+v(p)t|+2L\gtrsim\left(1-\gamma\right)t+2L\gtrsim t.

The field estimates then follow from Theorem
2.1
.
In particular, we use the estimates on the fields and spatial characteristics to find

\left|E(t,\mathcal{X}(t,\tau,x,p))\right|+\left|B(t,\mathcal{X}(t,\tau,x,p))\right|\leq\frac{C}{(t+1)\left(t-|\mathcal{X}(t)|+2L\right)}\leq\frac{C}{(t+L)^{2}}\lesssim t^{-2}

for all
\tau\in[0,t]
,
(x,p)\in\mathcal{S}_{f}(\tau)
.
Similarly, we have

\left|E(t,x+v(p)t))\right|+\left|B(t,x+v(p)t))\right|\leq\frac{C}{(t+1)\left(t-|x+v(p)t|+2L\right)}\lesssim t^{-2}

for
|x|\lesssim\ln(t)
and
|p|\leq\beta
, which, when combined with the above field estimate, implies the decay of
\mathcal{K}_{0}(t).

The estimate on derivatives of the fields similarly yields

|\nabla_{x}E(t,\mathcal{X}(t,\tau,x,p))|+|\nabla_{x}B(t,\mathcal{X}(t,\tau,x,p))|\leq\frac{C\ln(t+2)}{(t+1)\left(t-|\mathcal{X}(t)|+2L\right)^{2}}\leq\frac{C\ln(t+2)}{(t+L)^{3}}\lesssim t^{-3}\ln(t)

for all
\tau\in[0,t]
,
(x,p)\in\mathcal{S}_{f}(\tau)
.
Additionally, we have

|\nabla_{x}E(t,x+v(p)t)|+|\nabla_{x}B(t,x+v(p)t)|\leq\frac{C\ln(t+2)}{(t+1)\left(t-|x+v(p)t|+2L\right)^{2}}\lesssim t^{-3}\ln(t)

for
|x|\lesssim\ln(t)
and
|p|\leq\beta
, which gives the stated decay of
\mathcal{K}_{1}(t)
.
∎

Finally, we will need one lemma that will be used repeatedly to establish the convergence of a variety of quantities as
t\to\infty
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{H}:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{y}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p}
be a continuous, bounded function that is continuously differentiable in its third argument with bounded derivatives.
Then, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\mathcal{R}(t)>0
satisfying
x+\Gamma_{\mathcal{R}(t)}\subset\Gamma_{t}
for
t
sufficiently large,
we have

\int_{|y|\lesssim\mathcal{R}(t)}\left|\mathcal{H}\left(t,y,v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)\right)-\mathcal{H}\left(t,y,v^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right)\right|\ dy\lesssim t^{-1}\mathcal{R}(t)^{4}\|\nabla_{p}\mathcal{H}(t)\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

As
v^{-1}(q)
and its derivatives are uniformly bounded for
|q|<1
, we omit their contribution below.
Estimating the given integral, we find

\displaystyle\int_{|y|\lesssim\mathcal{R}(t)}\left|\mathcal{H}\left(t,y,v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)\right)-\mathcal{H}\left(t,y,v^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right)\right|\ dy

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{|y|\lesssim\mathcal{R}(t)}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{d\theta}\left[\mathcal{H}\left(t,y,v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-\theta y}{t}\right)\right)\right]d\theta\right|dy

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim t^{-1}\|\nabla_{p}\mathcal{H}(t)\|_{\infty}\int_{|y|\lesssim\mathcal{R}(t)}|y|\ dy

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim t^{-1}\mathcal{R}(t)^{4}\|\nabla_{p}\mathcal{H}(t)\|_{\infty},

and the proof is complete.
∎

3.1.
Characteristics

Lemma 3.1
.

For
\tau
sufficiently large,
t\geq\tau
, and
(x,p)\in\mathcal{S}_{f}(\tau)
, we have

\left|\frac{\partial\mathcal{X}}{\partial p}(t,\tau,x,p)\right|\lesssim t\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\left|\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}}{\partial p}(t,\tau,x,p)\right|\lesssim 1.

Proof.

Taking a
p

) yields

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial\dot{\mathcal{X}}}{\partial p}(t)=\mathbb{A}(\mathcal{P}(t))\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}}{\partial p}(t),\\
&\frac{\partial\dot{\mathcal{P}}}{\partial p}(t)=\nabla_{x}K(t,\mathcal{X}(t),\mathcal{P}(t))\frac{\partial\mathcal{X}}{\partial p}(t)+\nabla_{p}K(t,\mathcal{X}(t),\mathcal{P}(t))\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}}{\partial p}(t),\\
&\frac{\partial\mathcal{X}}{\partial p}(\tau)=0,\qquad\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}}{\partial p}(\tau)=\mathbb{I}.\end{aligned}\right.

Upon integrating, we can rewrite the latter ODE as

\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}}{\partial p}(t)=\mathbb{I}+\int_{\tau}^{t}\left(\nabla_{x}K(s,\mathcal{X}(s),\mathcal{P}(s))\frac{\partial\mathcal{X}}{\partial p}(s)+\nabla_{p}K(s,\mathcal{X}(s),\mathcal{P}(s))\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}}{\partial p}(s)\right)ds

so that by Lemmas
2.2
and
2.4
we have

\left|\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}}{\partial p}(t)\right|\leq 1+\int_{\tau}^{t}\left(\mathcal{K}_{1}(s)\left|\frac{\partial\mathcal{X}}{\partial p}(s)\right|+\mathcal{K}_{0}(s)\left|\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}}{\partial p}(s)\right|\right)ds\leq 1+C\int_{\tau}^{t}\left(s^{-a}\left|\frac{\partial\mathcal{X}}{\partial p}(s)\right|+s^{-2}\left|\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}}{\partial p}(s)\right|\right)ds

for any
a\in(2,3)
.
Now, we fix some
\delta\in[4,6]
and define

T_{0}(\tau)=\sup\left\{T\geq\tau:\left|\frac{\partial\mathcal{X}}{\partial p}(s)\right|\leq\delta(s-\tau)\ \mathrm{and}\ \left|\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}}{\partial p}(s)\right|\leq\delta\ \mathrm{for\ all}\ s\in[\tau,T]\right\}.

Note that
T_{0}>\tau
due to the initial conditions.
Then, estimating for
t\in[\tau,T_{0})
, we have

\left|\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}}{\partial p}(t)\right|\leq 1+C\delta\int_{\tau}^{t}\left[(s-\tau)s^{-a}+s^{-2}\right]ds\leq 1+C\delta\left(\tau^{2-a}+\tau^{-1}\right)\leq 2<\frac{1}{2}\delta

for
\tau
sufficiently large.
Similarly, we integrate the first ODE to find

\left|\frac{\partial\mathcal{X}}{\partial p}(t)\right|\leq\int_{\tau}^{t}\left|\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}}{\partial p}(s)\right|\ ds<\frac{1}{2}\delta(t-\tau)

for
\tau
sufficiently large.
Hence, we find
T_{0}=\infty
and the estimate on
p
-derivatives follows.
∎

Because the fields decay rapidly in time inside the light cone, we can immediately establish the limiting behavior of the momentum characteristics.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
\tau\geq 0
and
(x,p)\in\mathcal{S}_{f}(\tau)

For any

\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(\tau,x,p)=p+\int_{\tau}^{\infty}K(s,\mathcal{X}(s,\tau,x,p),\mathcal{P}(s,\tau,x,p))ds

Lemma 3.4

For every

\left|\mathcal{Y}(t,\tau,x,p)\right|\lesssim\ln(t)\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\mathcal{R}(t)\lesssim\ln(t).

Using (
3.4
) we immediately find

\left|\dot{\mathcal{Y}}(t)\right|\leq t|\mathbb{A}(v(\mathcal{P}(t)))||K(t,\mathcal{X}(t),\mathcal{P}(t))|\lesssim t\mathcal{K}_{0}(t),

and thus

\left|\mathcal{Y}(t,\tau,x,p)-(x-v(p)\tau)\right|\leq\int_{\tau}^{t}\left|\dot{\mathcal{Y}}(s,\tau,x,p)\right|\ ds\lesssim\int_{\tau}^{t}s\mathcal{K}_{0}(s)\ ds

for fixed
\tau\geq 0
and
(x,p)\in\mathcal{S}_{g}(\tau)
.
Furthermore, this implies

\left|\mathcal{Y}(t,0,x,p)\right|\lesssim|x|+\int_{0}^{t}s|K(s,\mathcal{X}(s),\mathcal{P}(s))|\ ds\lesssim 1+\int_{0}^{t}s\mathcal{K}_{0}(s)\ ds

for
(x,p)\in\mathcal{S}_{g}(0)
.
The estimate on the spatial radius then follows as

\mathcal{R}(t)=\sup_{(x,p)\in\mathcal{S}_{g}(0)}\left|\mathcal{Y}(t,0,x,p)\right|\lesssim 1+\int_{0}^{t}s\mathcal{K}_{0}(s)\ ds,

which by Lemma
2.4
implies the stated estimate.
∎

This growth estimate of the spatial support of
g
will prove useful later on when determining the asymptotic behavior of the Lorentz force, as we need only consider spatial values within the support of
g
, which satisfy
|x|\lesssim\ln(t)
, to derive such estimates.
Next, we show that momentum derivatives of
g
grow more slowly than those of
f
, which are established by Theorem
2.1
.

Lemma 3.5
.

We have

\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)\lesssim 1+\int_{1}^{t}\left(s\mathcal{K}_{0}(s)+s^{2}\mathcal{K}_{1}(s)\right)\ ds,

and thus by Lemma
2.4

\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)\lesssim\ln^{2}(t).

Proof.

To establish the result we estimate

\partial_{p_{k}}g(t,x,p)=\left(t\mathbb{A}_{jk}\partial_{x_{j}}f+\partial_{p_{k}}f\right)(t,x+v(p)t,p).

Applying the Vlasov operator

\mathcal{V}:=\partial_{t}+v(p)\cdot\nabla_{x}+K(t,x,p)\cdot\nabla_{p}

to the untranslated version of this quantity yields

\mathcal{V}\biggl(t\mathbb{A}_{jk}(p)\partial_{x_{j}}f(t,x,p)+\partial_{p_{k}}f(t,x,p)\biggr)=-t\mathbb{A}_{jk}\partial_{x_{j}}K\cdot\nabla_{p}f-\partial_{p_{k}}K\cdot\nabla_{p}f+t\partial_{x_{j}}fK\cdot\nabla_{p}\mathbb{A}_{jk},

and inverting gives

\displaystyle\left(t\mathbb{A}_{jk}\partial_{x_{j}}f+\partial_{p_{k}}f\right)(t,x,p)

\displaystyle=\partial_{p_{k}}f_{0}(\mathcal{X}(0),\mathcal{P}(0))-\int_{0}^{t}s\mathbb{A}_{jk}\left(\mathcal{P}(s)\right)\partial_{x_{j}}K(s,\mathcal{X}(s),\mathcal{P}(s))\cdot\nabla_{p}f(s,\mathcal{X}(s),\mathcal{P}(s))ds

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{p_{k}}K(s,\mathcal{X}(s),\mathcal{P}(s))\cdot\nabla_{p}f(s,\mathcal{X}(s),\mathcal{P}(s))ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}s\partial_{x_{j}}f(s,\mathcal{X}(s),\mathcal{P}(s))K(s,\mathcal{X}(s),\mathcal{P}(s))\cdot\nabla_{p}\mathbb{A}_{jk}\left(\mathcal{P}(s)\right)ds

for
j,k=1,2,3
.
From the estimates on the derivatives of
f
within Theorem
2.1
, as well as, those of the fields and their derivatives within Lemma
2.4
, this implies

\displaystyle\left|t\mathbb{A}_{jk}(p)\partial_{x_{k}}f(t,x,p)+\partial_{p_{k}}f(t,x,p)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\partial_{p_{k}}f_{0}\|_{\infty}+\int_{0}^{t}s\mathcal{K}_{1}(s)\|\nabla_{p}f(s)\|_{\infty}\ ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}|B(s,\mathcal{X}(s)|\|\nabla_{p}f(s)\|_{\infty}ds+\int_{0}^{t}s\mathcal{K}_{0}(s)\|\nabla_{x}f(s)\|_{\infty}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 1+\int_{0}^{t}s^{2}\mathcal{K}_{1}(s)ds+\int_{0}^{t}s\mathcal{K}_{0}(s)ds

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 1+\int_{1}^{t}s^{-1}\left(1+\ln(s)\right)\ ds

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\ln^{2}(t)

for
j,k=1,2,3
. Thus, we conclude

\|\nabla_{p}g(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\ln^{2}(t),

which completes the lemma.
∎

With the behavior of momentum derivatives along the translated characteristics well-understood as
t\to\infty
, we can establish the limiting behavior of the spatial average.

Lemma 3.6
.

There exists
F_{\infty}\in C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
\mathrm{supp}(F_{\infty})=\Omega_{p}
such that

F(t,p)=\int f(t,x,p)\ dx=\int g(t,x,p)\ dx

satisfies
F(t,p)\to F_{\infty}(p)
uniformly as
t\to\infty

with

\|F(t)-F_{\infty}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\int_{1}^{t}\left(\mathcal{K}_{0}(s)\mathcal{R}(s)^{3}\mathcal{G}_{1}(s)+s\mathcal{K}_{1}(s)\right)\ ds\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{5}(t).

In particular, the limit preserves particle number, i.e.

\int F_{\infty}(p)\ dp=\mathcal{M}.

Proof.

Upon integrating the Vlasov equation of (
1.5
) in
x
and integrating by parts, we find

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{t}\int g(t,x,p)\ dx\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\cdot(t\mathbb{A}(p)\nabla_{x}-\nabla_{p})g(t,x,p)\ dx\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|t\int\mathrm{tr}\left[\mathbb{A}(p)\nabla_{x}K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\right]g(t,x,p)\ dx+\int K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\cdot\nabla_{p}g(t,x,p)\ dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t\mathcal{K}_{1}(t)F(t,p)+\mathcal{K}_{0}(t)\mathcal{R}(t)^{3}\mathcal{G}_{1}(t).

Thus, we use Lemmas
2.4
,
3.4
, and
3.5
to find

\left|\partial_{t}F(t,p)\right|\lesssim t^{-2}\ln(t)F(t,p)+t^{-2}\ln^{5}(t).

As
F(0)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and the latter term above is integrable in time, we find

F(t,p)\leq F(0,p)+\int_{0}^{t}\left|\partial_{t}F(s,p)\right|\ ds\lesssim 1+\int_{1}^{t}s^{-2}\ln(s)F(s,p)\ ds,

and after taking the supremum and invoking Grönwall’s inequality, this yields

(3.5)

\|F(t)\|_{\infty}\leq\exp\left(\int_{1}^{t}s^{-2}\ln(s)\ ds\right)\lesssim 1.

Returning to the estimate of
\partial_{t}F
, we use the uniform bound on
\|F(t)\|_{\infty}
to find

\left|\partial_{t}F(t,p)\right|\lesssim t^{-2}\ln^{5}(t),

which implies that
\|\partial_{t}F(t)\|_{\infty}
is integrable.
This bound then establishes the estimate for
s\geq t

\|F(t)-F(s)\|_{\infty}=\left\|\int_{s}^{t}\partial_{t}F(\tau)\ d\tau\right\|_{\infty}\leq\int_{t}^{s}\|\partial_{t}F(\tau)\|_{\infty}\ d\tau\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{5}(t),

and taking
s\to\infty
establishes the limit. More precisely, as
F(t,p)
is continuous and the limit is uniform in
p
, there is
F_{\infty}\in C(\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that

\|F(t)-F_{\infty}\|_{\infty}\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{5}(t).

Next, we verify the properties of the limiting spatial average.
Due to the uniform convergence, we further conclude weak convergence of
F(t,p)
as a measure, namely

(3.6)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\int\psi(p)F(t,p)dp=\int\psi(p)F_{\infty}(p)dp

for every
\psi\in C_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{3}\right)
.
In this direction, let
\psi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be given and fix any
T\geq T_{1}
from Lemma
3.3
.
Then, we apply the measure-preserving change of variables
(\tilde{x},\tilde{p})=(\mathcal{X}(T,t,x,p),\mathcal{P}(T,t,x,p))
, so that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\int\psi(p)F(t,p)\ dp

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\iint\psi(p)\ f(t,x,p)\ dpdx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\iint\limits_{\mathcal{S}_{f}(t)}\psi(p)\ f(T,\mathcal{X}(T,t,x,p),\mathcal{P}(T,t,x,p))\ dpdx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\iint\limits_{\mathcal{S}_{f}(T)}\psi(\mathcal{P}(t,T,\tilde{x},\tilde{p}))\ f(T,\tilde{x},\tilde{p})\ d\tilde{p}d\tilde{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint\limits_{\mathcal{S}_{f}(T)}\psi(\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(T,\tilde{x},\tilde{p}))f(T,\tilde{x},\tilde{p})\ d\tilde{p}d\tilde{x}

by Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem. Now, by Lemma
3.3
for any
(\tilde{x},\tilde{p})\in\mathcal{S}_{f}(T)
, the mapping
\tilde{p}\mapsto\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(T,\tilde{x},\tilde{p})
is
C^{2}
with

\left|\det\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}_{\infty}}{\partial p}(T,\tilde{x},\tilde{p})\right)\right|\geq\frac{1}{2},

and thus bijective onto
\Omega_{p}
.
Hence, letting
u=\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(T,\tilde{x},\tilde{p})
, we perform a change of variables and drop the tilde notation to find

\lim_{t\to\infty}\int\psi(p)F(t,p)dp=\iint\psi(u)f(T,x,W(u))\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{p}}(u)}{\left|\det\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}_{\infty}}{\partial p}(T,x,W(u))\right)\right|}\ dudx

where for fixed
x
, the
C^{2}
function
W
defined on
\Omega_{p}
is given by

W(u)=\left(\mathcal{P}_{\infty}\right)^{-1}(T,x,u).

Therefore, by uniqueness of the weak limit we find from (
3.6
)

(3.7)

F_{\infty}(u)=\int f(T,x,W(u))\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{p}}(u)\left|\det\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}_{\infty}}{\partial p}(T,x,W(u))\right)\right|^{-1}\ dx

for any
u\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and thus
F_{\infty}\in C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with supp
(F_{\infty})=\Omega_{p}
.
Due to the invariance (
3.2
) of
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(\tau,x,p)
with respect to the time variable,
F_{\infty}
is also independent of the choice of
T
and thus, well defined.
Finally, the conservation of particle number is obtained by merely choosing
\psi(p)=1
within (
3.6
) and using the time-independence of this quantity.
∎

Now that we have shown the convergence of
F(t,p)
, we establish the precise asymptotic profiles of the charge and current densities.
First, recall that

\mathcal{D}(p)=\left|\det(\mathbb{A}(p))\right|^{-1}=\left(1+|p|^{2}\right)^{5/2}
due to (
2.2
).
From the limiting density
F_{\infty}(p)
, we define the induced charge density by

\rho_{\infty}(q)=F_{\infty}\left(v^{-1}(q)\right)\mathcal{D}\left(v^{-1}(q)\right)

for
q\in\Gamma_{1}
and smoothly extend it to
0
for all
q\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\Gamma_{1}
.
The induced current density is then defined by

j_{\infty}(q)=q\rho_{\infty}\left(v^{-1}(q)\right)

for every
q\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Of course, in the case of multiple species the quantities
F(t,p)
and
F_{\infty}(p)
are replaced by sums over
\alpha
of
F^{\alpha}(t,p)
and
F^{\alpha}_{\infty}(p)
, respectively, within the remaining lemmas.
Then, we obtain the limiting behavior of the charge and current densities as follows.

Lemma 3.7
.

The scaled charge density converges to a self-similar limiting function.
In particular, we have

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|t^{3}\rho(t,x)-\rho_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|\lesssim\|F(t)-F_{\infty}\|_{\infty}+t^{-1}\mathcal{R}(t)^{4}\mathcal{G}_{1}(t).

Thus, in view of Lemmas
3.4
,
3.5
, and
3.6
, we find

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|t^{3}\rho(t,x)-\rho_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{6}(t).

Proof.

Due to the growth of the spatial support of
f(t,x,p)
established in Lemma
2.4
, it suffices to consider only
|x|\lesssim\gamma t
. To prove the result, we first conduct a change of variables

y=x-v(p)t

with

dy=t^{3}\left|\det(\mathbb{A}(p))\right|dp

in the integral of
\rho(t,x)
.
Hence, this transforms

\rho(t,x)=\int f(t,x,p)\ dp=\int g(t,x-v(p)t,p)\ dp

into

(3.8)

\rho(t,x)=\frac{1}{t^{3}}\int g\left(t,y,v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)\right)\mathcal{D}\left(v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)\right)dy.

Letting

\mathcal{H}(t,y,p)=\mathcal{D}(p)g(t,y,p),

this can be expressed as

\rho(t,x)=t^{-3}\int\mathcal{H}\left(t,y,v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)\right)\ dy.

Therefore, as
\rho_{\infty}(q)=F_{\infty}\left(v^{-1}(q)\right)\mathcal{D}\left(v^{-1}(q)\right)
and
F(t,p)=\int g(t,y,p)\ dy
, the difference of the charge densities can be split into

\left|t^{3}\rho(t,x)-\rho_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|\leq I+II

where

I=\int\left|\mathcal{H}\left(t,y,v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)\right)-\mathcal{H}\left(t,y,v^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right)\right|dy

and

II=\left|F\left(t,v^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right)-F_{\infty}\left(v^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right)\right|\mathcal{D}\left(v^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right).

We estimate
I
by noting that
\mathcal{H}(t,y,p)
is supported on
|y|\lesssim\ln(t)
and invoking Lemma
2.5
to find

I\lesssim t^{-1}\mathcal{R}(t)^{4}\|\nabla_{p}\mathcal{H}(t)\|_{\infty}.

As
\mathcal{D}(p)
, its derivatives, and
\|g(t)\|_{\infty}
are all uniformly bounded, we have

\|\nabla_{p}\mathcal{H}(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)
,
and combining this with the estimates of Lemmas
3.4
and
3.5
gives

I\lesssim t^{-1}\mathcal{R}(t)^{4}\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{6}(t).

The estimate for
II
uses Lemma
3.6
, the compact support of
F(t)
and
F_{\infty}
, and
\left|\frac{x}{t}\right|\lesssim\gamma<1
so that
\mathcal{D}(p)
is bounded on the supports of these functions and

II\lesssim\|F(t)-F_{\infty}\|_{\infty}\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{5}(t).

Combining these estimates then yields the stated result.
∎

Next, we estimate the current density in a similar fashion.

Lemma 3.8
.

The scaled current density converges to a self-similar limiting function.
In particular, we have

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|t^{3}j(t,x)-j_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|\lesssim\|F(t)-F_{\infty}\|_{\infty}+t^{-1}\mathcal{R}(t)^{4}\mathcal{G}_{1}(t).

Thus, in view of Lemmas
3.4
,
3.5
, and
3.6
, we find

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|t^{3}j(t,x)-j_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{6}(t).

Proof.

Similar to the previous lemma, it suffices to consider only
|x|\lesssim\gamma t
.
We again prove the result by first conducting a change of variables, namely

y=x-v(p)t

in order to transform

j(t,x)=\int v(p)g(t,x-v(p)t,p)\ dp

into

j(t,x)=\frac{1}{t^{3}}\int\frac{x-y}{t}g\left(t,y,v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)\right)\mathcal{D}\left(v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)\right)dy

or

(3.9)

j(t,x)=\frac{1}{t^{3}}\int v(r)\mathcal{D}(r)g\left(t,y,r\right)\biggr|_{r=v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)}dy.

Letting

\mathcal{H}(t,y,p)=v(p)\mathcal{D}(p)g(t,y,p),

this can be expressed more succinctly as

j(t,x)=t^{-3}\int\mathcal{H}\left(t,y,v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)\right)dy.

Recall that

j_{\infty}(q)=q\mathcal{D}\left(v^{-1}\left(q\right)\right)F_{\infty}\left(v^{-1}\left(q\right)\right).

With this, the difference of the current densities can be split into two terms as

\displaystyle\left|t^{3}j(t,x)-j_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int\left|\mathcal{H}\left(t,y,v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)\right)-\mathcal{H}\left(t,y,v^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right)\right|dy

\displaystyle+\left|\frac{x}{t}\right|\mathcal{D}\left(v^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right)\left|F\left(t,v^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right)-F_{\infty}\left(v^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I+II.

As in the previous lemma, we estimate the first portion by invoking
Lemma
2.5
.
As the product
v(p)\mathcal{D}(p)
and all of its derivatives are uniformly bounded, we again deduce

\|\nabla_{p}\mathcal{H}(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)
.
Combining this with the estimates of Lemmas
3.4
and
3.5
then gives

I\lesssim t^{-1}\mathcal{R}(t)^{4}\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{6}(t).

The latter term uses
\left|\frac{x}{t}\right|\lesssim\gamma<1
, Lemma
3.6
, and the compact support of
F(t)
and
F_{\infty}
so that
\mathcal{D}(p)
is again bounded on the supports of these functions to find

II\lesssim\|F(t)-F_{\infty}\|_{\infty}\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{5}(t).

Combining the estimates of
I
and
II
then yields the stated result.
∎

4.
Higher Regularity Estimates

With the estimates on lower order derivatives of
g,E
, and
B
established, we turn our attention to second-order derivatives, which have not been previously obtained for (
RVM

Single

) and are crucial to constructing the time asymptotic limits and scattering profile that we wish to study.
The lemmas of this section will also be needed to prove the small data theorem.
Throughout this section we will assume that the second derivatives of the fields satisfy the estimate

(4.1)

\mathcal{K}_{2}(t)\lesssim t^{-4}\ln(t).

First, we estimate second-order derivatives of the translated distribution function; in particular, establishing a nearly sharp estimate on their growth using the decay of the second derivative of the fields.

Lemma 4.1
.

We have

\mathcal{G}_{2}(t)\lesssim 1+\int_{1}^{t}\left(s^{3}\mathcal{K}_{2}(s)+s^{2}\ln^{2}(s)\mathcal{K}_{1}(s)+s\ln^{2}(s)\mathcal{K}_{0}(s)\right)\,ds,

and thus by Lemma
2.4
and (

)

\mathcal{G}_{2}(t)\lesssim\ln^{4}(t).

Furthermore,

\|\nabla^{2}_{x}g(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim 1\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\|\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}g(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\ln^{2}(t).

Remark 4.2
.

If, in addition to the stated assumptions, we have
\|f_{0}\|_{C^{2}}\leq\epsilon_{0}
, then the estimate on spatial derivatives becomes

\|\nabla^{2}_{x}g(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\epsilon_{0}.

Proof.

On bounded time intervals, e.g.
t\in[0,1]
, uniform-in-time estimates can be obtained via straightforward methods (see
[
11
]
). Hence, we take
t>1
throughout and focus on large time estimates only.
Additionally, we suppress the argument of
\mathbb{A}(v(p))
and merely denote this term by
\mathbb{A}
.
To begin, we note that spatial derivatives of
g
are merely spatial derivatives of
f
evaluated along translated curves in phase space, and thus from Theorem
2.1
we find

(4.2)

\|\nabla_{x}g(t)\|_{\infty}=\|\nabla_{x}f(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim 1.

With this, we let
\mathcal{V}_{g}
denote the translated Vlasov operator appearing in (

(4.3)

\begin{split}\mathcal{V}_{g}[\partial_{m}\partial_{n}g](t,x,p)=\ &t\partial_{m}\left\{\partial_{n}\left[\mathbb{A}K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\right]\cdot\nabla_{x}g\right\}(t,x,p)\\
&+t\partial_{n}\left\{\partial_{m}\left[\mathbb{A}K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\right]\cdot\nabla_{x}g\right\}(t,x,p)\\
&-t\partial_{m}\partial_{n}\left[\mathbb{A}K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\right]\cdot\nabla_{x}g(t,x,p)\\
&-\partial_{m}\left\{\partial_{n}\left[K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\right]\cdot\nabla_{p}g\right\}(t,x,p)\\
&-\partial_{n}\left\{\partial_{m}\left[K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\right]\cdot\nabla_{p}g\right\}(t,x,p)\\

where

. Recall that all derivatives of

and

are bounded and that derivatives of

\displaystyle\partial_{p_{m}}\left[K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\right]=\

\displaystyle t\partial_{x_{i}}K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\mathbb{A}_{mi}+\partial_{p_{m}}(v(p))\times B(t,x+v(p)t),

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{n}}\partial_{p_{m}}\left[K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\right]=\

\displaystyle t\partial_{x_{n}}\partial_{x_{i}}K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\mathbb{A}_{mi}+\partial_{p_{m}}(v(p))\times\partial_{x_{n}}B(t,x+v(p)t),

\displaystyle\partial_{p_{m}}\partial_{p_{n}}\left[K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\right]=\

\displaystyle{t\partial_{p_{m}}\left[\partial_{x_{i}}K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\mathbb{A}_{ni}\right]}+{\partial_{p_{m}}\left[\partial_{p_{n}}(v(p))\times B(t,x+v(p)t)\right]}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle{t^{2}\partial_{x_{j}}\partial_{x_{i}}K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\mathbb{A}_{ni}\mathbb{A}_{mj}}+{t\partial_{x_{i}}K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\partial_{p_{m}}\mathbb{A}_{ni}}

\displaystyle+{t\partial_{p_{m}}(v(p))\times\partial_{x_{i}}B(t,x+v(p)t)\mathbb{A}_{ni}}+{\partial_{p_{m}}\partial_{p_{n}}(v(p))\times B(t,x+v(p)t)}

\displaystyle+{t\partial_{p_{n}}(v(p))\times\partial_{x_{i}}B(t,x+v(p)t)\mathbb{A}_{mi}}.

Due to the estimates of Lemma
2.4
and (
4.1
), this implies

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{p_{m}}\left[K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim t\mathcal{K}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{K}_{0}(t)\lesssim t^{-2}\ln(t),

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{x_{n}}\partial_{p_{m}}\left[K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim t\mathcal{K}_{2}(t)+\mathcal{K}_{1}(t)\lesssim t^{-3}\ln(t),

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{p_{m}}\partial_{p_{n}}\left[K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{2}\mathcal{K}_{2}(t)+t\mathcal{K}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{K}_{0}(t)\lesssim t^{-2}\ln(t).

Next, for
t>1
we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{2}(t):=\|\nabla_{x}^{2}g(t)\|_{\infty}+t^{-1}\|\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}g(t)\|_{\infty}+t^{-2}\|\nabla_{p}^{2}g(t)\|_{\infty}

which we shall bound uniformly in time.
Integrating (
4.3
) with
\partial_{m}\partial_{n}=\partial_{p_{m}}\partial_{p_{n}}
along the characteristics of
\mathcal{V}_{g}
gives

(4.4)

\begin{split}\|\nabla_{p}^{2}g(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\ &1+\int_{1}^{t}\biggl[s\left(s^{2}\mathcal{K}_{2}(s)+s\mathcal{K}_{1}(s)+\mathcal{K}_{0}(s)\right)\|\nabla_{x}g(s)\|_{\infty}\\
&+s\left(s\mathcal{K}_{1}(s)+\mathcal{K}_{0}(s)\right)\|\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}g(s)\|_{\infty}+\left(s^{2}\mathcal{K}_{2}(s)+s\mathcal{K}_{1}(s)+\mathcal{K}_{0}(s)\right)\|\nabla_{p}g(s)\|_{\infty}\\
&+\left(s\mathcal{K}_{1}(s)+\mathcal{K}_{0}(s)\right)\|\nabla_{p}^{2}g(s)\|_{\infty}\biggr]\,ds.\end{split}

Then, multiplying by
t^{-2}
and using (
4.2
) and Lemma
3.5
yields

(4.5)

\begin{split}t^{-2}\|\nabla_{p}^{2}g(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\ &t^{-2}+t^{-2}\int_{1}^{t}s\left(s^{-2}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{x}g(s)\|_{\infty}+s^{-2}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}g(s)\|_{\infty}\right)\,ds\\
&+t^{-2}\int_{1}^{t}\left(s^{-2}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{p}g(s)\|_{\infty}+s^{-2}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{p}^{2}g(s)\|_{\infty}\right)\,ds\\
\lesssim\ &1+t^{-2}\int_{1}^{t}s^{-1}\ln(s)\,ds+\int_{1}^{t}s^{-3}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}g(s)\|_{\infty}\,ds\\
&+t^{-2}\int_{1}^{t}s^{-2}\ln^{3}(s)\,ds+\int_{1}^{t}s^{-4}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{p}^{2}g(s)\|_{\infty}\,ds\\
\lesssim\ &1+\int_{1}^{t}s^{-2}\ln(s)\left(s^{-1}\|\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}g(s)\|_{\infty}+s^{-2}\|\nabla_{p}^{2}g(s)\|_{\infty}\right)\,ds\\
\lesssim\ &1+\int_{1}^{t}s^{-2}\ln(s)\mathcal{D}_{2}(s)\,ds.\end{split}

Integrating (
4.3
) with
\partial_{m}\partial_{n}=\partial_{p_{m}}\partial_{x_{n}}
along the characteristics of
\mathcal{V}_{g}
and using the field estimates gives

(4.6)

\begin{split}t^{-1}\|&\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}g(t)\|_{\infty}\\
\lesssim\ &t^{-1}+t^{-1}\int_{1}^{t}s\left(s^{-3}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{x}g(s)\|_{\infty}+s^{-3}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}g(s)\|_{\infty}+s^{-2}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{x}^{2}g(s)\|_{\infty}\right)\,ds\\
&+t^{-1}\int_{1}^{t}\left(s^{-3}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{p}g(s)\|_{\infty}+s^{-2}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}g(s)\|_{\infty}+s^{-3}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{p}^{2}g(s)\|_{\infty}\right)\,ds\\
\lesssim\ &1+t^{-1}\int_{1}^{t}s^{-2}\ln(s)\,ds+\int_{1}^{t}\left(s^{-3}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}g(s)\|_{\infty}+s^{-2}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{x}^{2}g(s)\|_{\infty}\right)\,ds\\
&+t^{-1}\int_{1}^{t}s^{-3}\ln^{3}(s)\,ds+\int_{1}^{t}\left(s^{-3}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}g(s)\|_{\infty}+s^{-4}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{p}^{2}g(s)\|_{\infty}\right)\,ds\\
\lesssim\ &1+\int_{1}^{t}s^{-2}\ln(s)\left(\|\nabla_{x}^{2}g(s)\|_{\infty}+s^{-1}\|\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}g(s)\|_{\infty}+s^{-2}\|\nabla_{p}^{2}g(s)\|_{\infty}\right)\,ds\\
\lesssim\ &1+\int_{1}^{t}s^{-2}\ln(s)\mathcal{D}_{2}(s)\,ds.\end{split}

Integrating (
4.3
) with
\partial_{m}\partial_{n}=\partial_{x_{m}}\partial_{x_{n}}
along the characteristics of
\mathcal{V}_{g}
and estimating, we find

(4.7)

\begin{split}\|\nabla_{x}^{2}g(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\ &1+\int_{1}^{t}s\left(s^{-4}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{x}g(s)\|_{\infty}+s^{-3}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{x}^{2}g(s)\|_{\infty}\right)\,ds\\
&+\int_{1}^{t}\left(s^{-4}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{p}g(s)\|_{\infty}+s^{-3}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}g(s)\|_{\infty}\right)\,ds\\
\lesssim\ &1+\int_{1}^{t}s\left(s^{-4}\ln(s)+s^{-3}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{x}^{2}g(s)\|_{\infty}\right)\,ds\\
&+\int_{1}^{t}\left(s^{-4}\ln^{3}(s)+s^{-3}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}g(s)\|_{\infty}\right)\,ds\\
\lesssim\ &1+\int_{1}^{t}s^{-2}\ln(s)\left(\|\nabla_{x}^{2}g(s)\|_{\infty}+s^{-1}\|\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}g(s)\|_{\infty}\right)\,ds\\
\lesssim\ &1+\int_{1}^{t}s^{-2}\ln(s)\mathcal{D}_{2}(s)\,ds.\end{split}

Adding the estimates (
4.5
), (
4.6
), and (
4.7
) yields

\mathcal{D}_{2}(t)\lesssim 1+\int_{1}^{t}s^{-2}\ln(s)\mathcal{D}_{2}(s)\,ds,

and upon using Grönwall’s inequality, we conclude

\mathcal{D}_{2}(t)\lesssim 1.

This further implies

\|\nabla_{x}^{2}g(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim 1,\qquad\|\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}g(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim t,\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\|\nabla_{p}^{2}g(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim t^{2}.

We use these bounds to improve the estimate (
4.6
). Indeed, returning to this inequality and inserting the above estimates gives

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}g(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\

\displaystyle 1+\int_{1}^{t}s\left(s^{-3}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{x}g(s)\|_{\infty}+s^{-3}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}g(s)\|_{\infty}+s^{-2}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{x}^{2}g(s)\|_{\infty}\right)\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{1}^{t}\left(s^{-3}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{p}g(s)\|_{\infty}+s^{-2}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}g(s)\|_{\infty}+s^{-3}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{p}^{2}g(s)\|_{\infty}\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\

\displaystyle 1+\int_{1}^{t}\left(s^{-2}\ln(s)+s^{-2}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}g(s)\|_{\infty}+s^{-1}\ln(s)\right)\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{1}^{t}\left(s^{-3}\ln^{3}(s)+s^{-2}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}g(s)\|_{\infty}+s^{-1}\ln(s)\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\

\displaystyle\ln^{2}(t)+\int_{1}^{t}s^{-2}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}g(s)\|_{\infty}\,ds.

Using Grönwall’s inequality again yields

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}g(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\ln^{2}(t)\ \exp\left(\int_{1}^{t}s^{-2}\ln(s)\,ds\right)\lesssim\ln^{2}(t).

Using this estimate of the mixed partial derivatives within (
4.4
), we have the improved estimate

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{p}^{2}g(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\

\displaystyle 1+\int_{1}^{t}\left[\left(s^{3}\mathcal{K}_{2}(s)+s^{2}\mathcal{K}_{1}(s)+s\mathcal{K}_{0}(s)\right)\|\nabla_{x}g(s)\|_{\infty}+\left(s^{2}\mathcal{K}_{1}(s)+s\mathcal{K}_{0}(s)\right)\|\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}g(s)\|_{\infty}\right]\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{1}^{t}\left[\left(s^{2}\mathcal{K}_{2}(s)+s\mathcal{K}_{1}(s)+\mathcal{K}_{0}(s)\right)\|\nabla_{p}g(s)\|_{\infty}+\left(s\mathcal{K}_{1}(s)+\mathcal{K}_{0}(s)\right)\|\nabla_{p}^{2}g(s)\|_{\infty}\right]\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\

\displaystyle 1+\int_{1}^{t}\left(s^{3}\mathcal{K}_{2}(s)+s^{2}\ln^{2}(s)\mathcal{K}_{1}(s)+s\ln^{2}(s)\mathcal{K}_{0}(s)\right)\,ds+\int_{1}^{t}s^{-2}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{p}^{2}g(s)\|_{\infty}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\

\displaystyle\ln^{4}(t)+\int_{1}^{t}s^{-2}\ln(s)\|\nabla_{p}^{2}g(s)\|_{\infty}\,ds,

which leads to the final application of Grönwall’s inequality, namely

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{p}^{2}g(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\ln^{4}(t)\ \exp\left(\int_{1}^{t}s^{-2}\ln(s)\,ds\right)\lesssim\ln^{4}(t).

Hence, we find

\mathcal{G}_{2}(t)\lesssim\ln^{4}(t),

and the proof is complete.
∎

Next, we use the estimate of
\mathcal{G}_{2}(t)
to establish the convergence rate of derivatives of the spatial average as
t\to\infty
.

Lemma 4.3
.

The spatial average further satisfies

\nabla_{p}F(t,p)\to\nabla_{p}F_{\infty}(p)
uniformly as
t\to\infty

with

\|\nabla_{p}F(t)-\nabla_{p}F_{\infty}\|_{\infty}\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{7}(t).

Proof.

To prove the derivative estimate, we proceed in a similar fashion to the estimate of
\partial_{t}F
in Lemma
3.6
.
Upon taking
\partial_{p_{i}}
within (
1.5
), integrating in
x
, and integrating by parts, we find

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int\partial_{p_{i}}g(t,x,p)\ dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t\int\partial_{p_{i}}\left(\mathrm{tr}\left[\mathbb{A}(p)\nabla_{x}K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\right]g(t,x,p)\right)dx

\displaystyle+\int\partial_{p_{i}}\left(K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\cdot\nabla_{p}g(t,x,p)\right)dx.

Hence, using the estimates of Lemmas
2.4
,
3.4
,
3.5
, and
4.1
yields

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{t}\partial_{p_{i}}F(t,p)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t\left(\mathcal{K}_{1}(t)+t\mathcal{K}_{2}(t)\right)F(t,p)+\left(\mathcal{K}_{0}(t)+t\mathcal{K}_{1}(t)\right)\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)\mathcal{R}(t)^{3}+\mathcal{K}_{0}(t)\mathcal{G}_{2}(t)\mathcal{R}(t)^{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t^{-2}\ln(t)F(t,p)+t^{-2}\ln^{6}(t)+t^{-2}\ln^{7}(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t^{-2}\ln^{7}(t).

Of course, this implies that
\|\partial_{t}\nabla_{p}F(t)\|_{\infty}
is integrable,
and as before, establishes the existence of a limit
\mathfrak{F}
with the estimate

\|\nabla_{p}F(t)-\mathfrak{F}\|_{\infty}\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{7}(t).

Finally, we find
\mathfrak{F}(p)=\nabla_{p}F_{\infty}(p)
by the uniqueness of the uniform limit.
∎

Now, estimates of the large time behavior of density derivatives are needed in order to obtain a more refined understanding of the field behavior,
as spatial derivatives of
\rho
and both spatial and time derivatives of
j
appear as source terms in the inhomogeneous wave equations for
E
and
B
induced by Maxwell’s equations.
As
F_{\infty}\in C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we can define the limiting derivatives of
\rho
and
j
in terms of derivatives of
F_{\infty}
.
More specifically, we have for
q\in\Gamma_{1}

\partial_{q_{k}}\rho_{\infty}(q)=\partial_{q_{k}}\left[\mathcal{D}\left(v^{-1}(q)\right)F_{\infty}\left(v^{-1}(q)\right)\right]

and

\partial_{q_{k}}j^{\ell}_{\infty}(q)=\partial_{q_{k}}\left[q^{\ell}\mathcal{D}\left(v^{-1}(q)\right)F_{\infty}\left(v^{-1}(q)\right)\right]

for every
k,\ell=1,2,3
,
respectively.
Additionally, due to the compact support of
F_{\infty}
, these quantities are smoothly extended by 0 to
q\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
As within the previous section, in the case of multiple species the quantities
F(t,p)
and
F_{\infty}(p)
are replaced by sums over
\alpha
of
F^{\alpha}(t,p)
and
F^{\alpha}_{\infty}(p)
, and their respective derivatives, within the following lemmas.
Then, we have the following convergence estimates on derivatives.

Lemma 4.4
.

The spatial derivatives of the charge density satisfy

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|t^{4}\partial_{x_{i}}\rho(t,x)-\partial_{q_{i}}\rho_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|\lesssim\|F(t)-F_{\infty}\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{p}F(t)-\nabla_{p}F_{\infty}\|_{\infty}+t^{-1}\mathcal{R}(t)^{4}\mathcal{G}_{2}(t)

for every
i=1,2,3
.
Furthermore, in view of the previous lemmas, we have

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|t^{4}\partial_{x_{i}}\rho(t,x)-\partial_{q_{i}}\rho_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{8}(t)

for every
i=1,2,3
.

Proof.

Where necessary, we use the Einstein summation convention. As in previous lemmas, it suffices to consider only
|x|\lesssim\gamma t
due to the growth of the spatial support of
f
.
We begin by taking a derivative with respect to
x_{i}
within (
3.8
) to find

\partial_{x_{i}}\rho(t,x)=t^{-4}\int\mathbb{B}_{ik}(v(r))\partial_{r_{k}}\left[\mathcal{D}\left(r\right)g\left(t,y,r\right)\right]\biggr|_{r=v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)}dy.

Furthermore, we have

\partial_{q_{i}}\rho_{\infty}(q)=\partial_{q_{i}}\left[\mathcal{D}\left(v^{-1}(q)\right)F_{\infty}\left(v^{-1}(q)\right)\right]=\mathbb{B}_{ik}(v(r))\partial_{r_{k}}\left[\mathcal{D}(r)F_{\infty}(r)\right]\biggr|_{r=v^{-1}(q)}.

Letting

\mathcal{H}(t,y,p)=\mathbb{B}_{ik}(v(p))\partial_{p_{k}}\left[\mathcal{D}(p)g(t,y,p)\right]

and subtracting the above expressions for the charge density and its expected limit we find

t^{4}\partial_{x_{i}}\rho(t,x)-\partial_{q_{i}}\rho_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)=\int\mathcal{H}\left(t,y,v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)\right)\ dy-\mathbb{B}_{ik}(v(p))\partial_{p_{k}}\left[\mathcal{D}(p)F_{\infty}(p)\right]\biggr|_{p=v^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)}.

We split this into two pieces so that

\left|t^{4}\partial_{x_{i}}\rho(t,x)-\partial_{q_{i}}\rho_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|\leq I+II

where

I=\int\left|\mathcal{H}\left(t,y,v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)\right)-\mathcal{H}\left(t,y,v^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right)\right|\ dy

and

II=\left|\mathbb{B}_{ik}(v(p))\right|\ \left|\partial_{p_{k}}\left[\mathcal{D}(p)\left(F(t,p)-F_{\infty}(p)\right)\right]\right|\biggr|_{p=v^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)}

as
\int g(t,y,p)\ dy=F(t,p)
.
We estimate
I
using Lemma
2.5
.
As the time-independent portions of
\mathcal{H}(t,y,p)
and their derivatives are uniformly bounded, we find

\|\nabla_{p}\mathcal{H}(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\mathcal{G}_{2}(t)
.
Combining this with the estimates of Lemmas
3.4
and
4.1
then gives

I\lesssim t^{-1}\mathcal{R}(t)^{4}\mathcal{G}_{2}(t)\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{8}(t).

The estimate of
II
uses
\left|\frac{x}{t}\right|\lesssim\gamma<1
, the compact support of
F(t)
and
F_{\infty}
, and Lemmas
3.6
and
4.3
, to conclude

II\lesssim\|F(t)-F_{\infty}\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{p}F(t)-\nabla_{p}F_{\infty}\|_{\infty}\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{7}(t).

Combining these estimates then yields the result.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

The spatial derivatives of the current density satisfy

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|t^{4}\partial_{x_{i}}j(t,x)-\partial_{q_{i}}j_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|\lesssim\|F(t)-F_{\infty}\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{p}F(t)-\nabla_{p}F_{\infty}\|_{\infty}+t^{-1}\mathcal{R}(t)^{4}\mathcal{G}_{2}(t)

for every
i=1,2,3
.
Furthermore, in view of the previous lemmas, we have

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|t^{4}\partial_{x_{i}}j(t,x)-\partial_{q_{i}}j_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{8}(t)

for every
i=1,2,3
.
Additionally, the time derivative of the current density satisfies

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|t^{4}\partial_{t}j(t,x)+\left[3j_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)+\frac{x}{t}\cdot\nabla_{q}j_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right]\right|\lesssim\|F(t)-F_{\infty}\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{p}F(t)-\nabla_{p}F_{\infty}\|_{\infty}+t^{-1}\mathcal{R}(t)^{4}\mathcal{G}_{2}(t).

and in view of the previous lemmas, we have

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|t^{4}\partial_{t}j(t,x)+\left[3j_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)+\frac{x}{t}\cdot\nabla_{q}j_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right]\right|\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{8}(t).

Proof.

Where necessary, we use the Einstein summation convention.
As before, we need only consider
|x|\lesssim\gamma t
due to the growth of the support of
f
.
To prove the first result, we begin by rewriting the
\ell
th component of (
3.9
) as

j^{\ell}(t,x)=\frac{1}{t^{3}}\int v^{\ell}(r)\mathcal{D}(r)g\left(t,y,r\right)\biggr|_{r=v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)}dy.

Then, taking an
x_{i}
derivative within this expression yields

(4.8)

\partial_{x_{i}}j^{\ell}(t,x)=t^{-4}\int\mathbb{B}_{ik}\left(v(r)\right)\partial_{r_{k}}\left[v^{\ell}(r)\mathcal{D}(r)g\left(t,y,r\right)\right]\biggr|_{r=v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)}dy.

Furthermore, we have

\partial_{q_{i}}j^{\ell}_{\infty}(q)=\partial_{q_{i}}\left[v^{\ell}\left(v^{-1}(q)\right)\mathcal{D}\left(v^{-1}(q)\right)F_{\infty}\left(v^{-1}(q)\right)\right]=\bigg(\mathbb{B}_{ik}(v(r))\partial_{r_{k}}\left[v^{\ell}(r)\mathcal{D}(r)F_{\infty}(r)\right]\bigg)\biggr|_{r=v^{-1}(q)}.

Next, we let

\mathcal{H}(t,y,p)=\mathbb{B}_{ik}(v(p))\partial_{p_{k}}\left[v^{\ell}(p)\mathcal{D}(p)g(t,y,p)\right],

and subtract the derivative terms to find

t^{4}\partial_{x_{i}}j^{\ell}(t,x)-\partial_{q_{i}}j^{\ell}_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)=\int\mathcal{H}\left(t,y,v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)\right)dy-\mathbb{B}_{ik}(v(p))\partial_{p_{k}}\left[v^{\ell}(p)\mathcal{D}(p)F_{\infty}(p)\right]\biggr|_{p=v^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)}.

Similar to the previous lemma, we split this into two pieces so that

\left|t^{4}\partial_{x_{i}}j^{\ell}(t,x)-\partial_{q_{i}}j^{\ell}_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|\leq I+II

where

I=\int\left|\mathcal{H}\left(t,y,v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)\right)-\mathcal{H}\left(t,y,v^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right)\right|\ dy

II=\left|\mathbb{B}_{ik}\left(v(p)\right)\right|\ \left|\partial_{p_{k}}\left[v^{\ell}(p)\mathcal{D}(p)\left(F(t,p)-F_{\infty}(p)\right)\right]\right|\biggr|_{p=v^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)}

as
\int g(t,y,p)\ dy=F(t,p)
.
As in previous lemmas, we estimate
I
using Lemma
2.5
.
Because the time-independent portions of
\mathcal{H}(t,y,p)
and their derivatives are uniformly bounded, this
yields the estimate

\|\nabla_{p}\mathcal{H}(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\mathcal{G}_{2}(t)
.
Combining this with the estimates of Lemmas
2.5
,
3.4
, and
4.1
then gives

I\lesssim t^{-1}\mathcal{R}(t)^{4}\mathcal{G}_{2}(t)\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{8}(t)

as

,
\mathcal{D}(p)

The term
II
uses
\left|\frac{x}{t}\right|\lesssim\gamma<1
, the compact support of

and
F_{\infty}

3.6
and
4.3

The combination of these estimates yields the first result.

Turning to the second result, we perform a similar computation and invoke the first result where needed.
As before, we write the
\ell
th component of the current density as

j^{\ell}(t,x)=\frac{1}{t^{3}}\int v^{\ell}(r)\mathcal{D}(r)g\left(t,y,r\right)\biggr|_{r=v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)}dy.

Taking a
t

\displaystyle\partial_{t}j^{\ell}(t,x)

\displaystyle=-3t^{-1}j^{\ell}(t,x)-t^{-4}\int\frac{x_{i}-y_{i}}{t}\mathbb{B}_{ik}\left(v(r)\right)\partial_{r_{k}}\left[v^{\ell}(r)\mathcal{D}(r)g\left(t,y,r\right)\right]\biggr|_{r=v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)}dy

\displaystyle\qquad+t^{-3}\int v^{\ell}(r)\mathcal{D}(r)\partial_{t}g\left(t,y,r\right)\biggr|_{r=v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)}dy

\displaystyle=-3t^{-1}j^{\ell}(t,x)-\frac{x}{t}\cdot\nabla_{x}j^{\ell}(t,x)

\displaystyle\qquad+t^{-4}\int\frac{y_{i}}{t}\mathbb{B}_{ik}\left(v(r)\right)\partial_{r_{k}}\left[v^{\ell}(r)\mathcal{D}(r)g\left(t,y,r\right)\right]\biggr|_{r=v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)}dy

\displaystyle\qquad+t^{-3}\int v^{\ell}(r)\mathcal{D}(r)\partial_{t}g\left(t,y,r\right)\biggr|_{r=v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)}dy

due to (

).
Multiplying by

, we expect the first term on the right side to converge to its previously established limit, while the second term will converge to a limit involving the gradient of

, and the last two terms are higher order.
Indeed, the difference we wish to estimate becomes

\displaystyle\left|t^{4}\partial_{t}j^{\ell}(t,x)+3j_{\infty}^{\ell}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)+\frac{x}{t}\cdot\nabla_{q}j^{\ell}_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 3\left|t^{3}j^{\ell}(t,x)-j_{\infty}^{\ell}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|+\left|\frac{x}{t}\cdot\left(t^{4}\nabla_{x}j^{\ell}(t,x)-\nabla_{q}j^{\ell}_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left|\int\frac{y_{i}}{t}\mathbb{B}_{ik}\left(v(r)\right)\partial_{r_{k}}\left[v^{\ell}(r)\mathcal{D}(r)g\left(t,y,r\right)\right]\biggr|_{r=v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)}dy\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+t\left|\int v^{\ell}(r)\mathcal{D}(r)\partial_{t}g\left(t,y,r\right)\biggr|_{r=v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)}dy\right|

\displaystyle\qquad=:I-IV.

To estimate
I
, we use Lemma
3.8
to find

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|t^{3}j(t,x)-j_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|F(t)-F_{\infty}\|_{\infty}+t^{-1}\mathcal{R}(t)^{4}\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t^{-1}\ln^{6}(t).

Next, we use the first result of the current lemma for
II
, yielding

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\gamma\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|t^{4}\nabla_{x}j^{\ell}(t,x)-\nabla_{q}j^{\ell}_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|F(t)-F_{\infty}\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{p}F(t)-\nabla_{p}F_{\infty}\|_{\infty}+t^{-1}\mathcal{R}(t)^{4}\mathcal{G}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t^{-1}\ln^{8}(t).

Within
III
, the derivatives appearing in the integrand are all dominated by
\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)
, and thus

III\lesssim t^{-1}\mathcal{R}(t)^{4}\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{6}(t).

Finally,
IV
is the most challenging term.
We estimate the integral and use (
1.5
) to write

\partial_{t}g(t,x,p)=t\mathbb{A}(p)K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\cdot\nabla_{x}g(t,x,p)-K(t,x+v(p)t,p)\cdot\nabla_{p}g(t,x,p).

This expression is inserted into the integral but evaluated at the point
(t,y,r)
where
r=v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)
so that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g(t,y,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t\mathbb{A}(r)K(t,y+v(r)t,r)\cdot\nabla_{x}g(t,y,r)-K(t,y+v(r)t,r)\cdot\nabla_{p}g(t,y,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t\mathbb{A}(r)K(t,x,r)\cdot\nabla_{x}g(t,y,r)-K(t,x,r)\cdot\nabla_{p}g(t,y,r).

We will integrate by parts in the
y
variable, thus we compute

\partial_{y_{i}}\left[g(t,y,r)\right]=\partial_{x_{i}}g(t,y,r)-t^{-1}\mathbb{B}_{ik}(v(r))\partial_{p_{k}}g(t,y,r)

so that

\partial_{x_{i}}g(t,y,r)=\partial_{y_{i}}\left[g(t,y,r)\right]+t^{-1}\mathbb{B}_{ik}(v(r))\partial_{p_{k}}g(t,y,r).

Replacing
\partial_{t}g(t,y,r)
, we see that
IV
can be split into

\displaystyle IV

\displaystyle\leq t^{2}\left|\int v^{\ell}(r)\mathcal{D}(r)\mathbb{A}(r)K(t,x,r)\cdot\nabla_{x}g(t,y,r)\biggr|_{r=v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)}dy\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+t\left|\int v^{\ell}(r)\mathcal{D}(r)K(t,x,r)\cdot\nabla_{p}g(t,y,r)\biggr|_{r=v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)}dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq t^{2}\left|\int v^{\ell}(r)\mathcal{D}(r)\mathbb{A}_{ij}(r)K^{j}(t,x,r)\partial_{y_{i}}\left[g(t,y,r)\right]\biggr|_{r=v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)}dy\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+t\left|\int v^{\ell}(r)\mathcal{D}(r)\mathbb{A}_{ij}(r)K^{j}(t,x,r)\mathbb{B}_{ik}(v(r))\partial_{p_{k}}g(t,y,r)\biggr|_{r=v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)}dy\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+t\left|\int v^{\ell}(r)\mathcal{D}(r)K(t,x,r)\cdot\nabla_{p}g(t,y,r)\biggr|_{r=v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)}dy\right|

\displaystyle=:IV_{A}+IV_{B}+IV_{C}.

Within
IV_{A}
, we integrate by parts in
y
, and as every
y_{i}
derivative of
r=v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)
provides an additional factor of
t^{-1}
, Lemma
2.4
produces the estimate

\displaystyle IV_{A}

\displaystyle=t^{2}\left|\int\partial_{y_{i}}\left[v^{\ell}(r)\mathcal{D}(r)\mathbb{A}_{ij}(r)K^{j}(t,x,r)\biggr|_{r=v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)}\right]g\left(t,y,v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)\right)dy\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim t\int_{|y|\lesssim\mathcal{R}(t)}\left|K(t,x,r)\right|g(t,y,r)\biggr|_{r=v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{3}(t).

The term
IV_{B}
is more straightforward as the boundedness of the functions of
r
within the integrand and Lemmas
2.4
and
3.5
give

IV_{B}\lesssim t\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)\int_{|y|\lesssim\mathcal{R}(t)}\left|K\left(t,x,v^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)\right)\right|dy\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{5}(t).

Finally, the estimate for
IV_{C}
is nearly identical to that of
IV_{B}
, and we similarly find

IV_{C}\lesssim t\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)\mathcal{R}(t)^{3}\mathcal{K}_{0}(t)\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{5}(t).

Therefore, we have

IV\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{5}(t),

and further collecting the estimates for
I
,
II
, and
III
yields the second result.
∎

Finally, we present a pivotal lemma that will be used to estimate kernel terms appearing in second order field derivatives within the proof of the small data theorem.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
m:\partial\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
be bounded on the support of
f
and continuously differentiable in its second argument with bounded derivatives. Define

M(t,\omega,y)=\int m(\omega,v(p))\partial_{x_{k}}f(t,y,p)\ dp

for any of
k=1,2,3
.
Then, for all
|\omega|=1
and
|y|\lesssim\gamma t
we have

|M(t,\omega,y)|\lesssim t^{-4}.

Remark 4.7
.

For the proof of the small data theorem, we will apply this result to the iterates.
Hence, if in addition to the stated assumptions we have
\|f_{0}\|_{C^{1}}\leq\epsilon_{0}
, then the estimate becomes

|M(t,\omega,y)|\leq C\epsilon_{0}(1+t)^{-4}\leq C\epsilon_{0}(t+|y|+2L)^{-4}

for all
t\geq 0
,
|\omega|=1
, and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
due to the bound on the support of
f
and
\partial_{x_{k}}f
.

Proof.

Again, we use the Einstein summation convention where necessary.
We first rewrite the integral as

M(t,\omega,y)=\int m(\omega,v(p))\partial_{x_{k}}g(t,y-v(p)t,p)\ dp

and conduct a change of variables

z=y-v(p)t

with

dz=t^{3}\left|\det(\mathbb{A}(p))\right|dp.

Recall that

\mathcal{D}(p)=\left|\det(\mathbb{A}(p))\right|^{-1}=\left(1+|p|^{2}\right)^{5/2}
due to (
2.2
).
Hence, we have

M(t,\omega,y)=t^{-3}\int m\left(\omega,v(r)\right)\partial_{x_{k}}g\left(t,z,r\right)\mathcal{D}\left(r\right)\biggr|_{r=v^{-1}\left(\frac{y-z}{t}\right)}\ dz.

Next, we note that

\partial_{x_{k}}g\left(t,z,v^{-1}\left(\frac{y-z}{t}\right)\right)=\frac{d}{dz_{k}}\left[g\left(t,z,v^{-1}\left(\frac{y-z}{t}\right)\right)\right]+t^{-1}\mathbb{B}_{k\ell}\left(\frac{y-z}{t}\right)\partial_{p_{\ell}}g\left(t,z,v^{-1}\left(\frac{y-z}{t}\right)\right).

Hence, replacing the
x_{k}
-derivative of
g
within the above integral splits it into two terms, and we find

\displaystyle M(t,\omega,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t^{-3}\int\frac{d}{dz_{k}}\left[g\left(t,z,v^{-1}\left(\frac{y-z}{t}\right)\right)\right]m\left(\omega,\frac{y-z}{t}\right)\mathcal{D}\left(v^{-1}\left(\frac{y-z}{t}\right)\right)\ dz

\displaystyle+\ t^{-4}\int\mathbb{B}_{k\ell}\left(v(r)\right)\partial_{p_{\ell}}g\left(t,z,r\right)m\left(\omega,v(r)\right)\mathcal{D}\left(r\right)\biggr|_{r=v^{-1}\left(\frac{y-z}{t}\right)}\ dz.

Integrating by parts in the first term yields

\displaystyle\int\frac{d}{dz_{k}}\left[g\left(t,z,v^{-1}\left(\frac{y-z}{t}\right)\right)\right]m\left(\omega,\frac{y-z}{t}\right)\mathcal{D}\left(v^{-1}\left(\frac{y-z}{t}\right)\right)\ dz

\displaystyle=t^{-1}\int g\left(t,z,r\right)\left[\partial_{v_{k}}m\left(\omega,v(r)\right)+\mathbb{B}_{k\ell}\left(v(r)\right)\partial_{p_{\ell}}\mathcal{D}\left(r\right)\right]\biggr|_{r=v^{-1}\left(\frac{y-z}{t}\right)}\ dz.

Noting that

\partial_{p_{\ell}}\left[m(\omega,v(p))\right]=\partial_{v_{k}}m\left(\omega,v(p)\right)\mathbb{A}_{k\ell}(p),

using the symmetry of
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
along with the identity (
2.1
),
and assembling this with the remaining term in the representation for
M(t,\omega,y)
gives

M(t,\omega,y)=t^{-4}\int\mathbb{B}_{k\ell}(v(r))\partial_{r_{\ell}}\left[gm\mathcal{D}\right]\left(t,\omega,z,r\right)\biggr|_{r=v^{-1}\left(\frac{y-z}{t}\right)}\ dz.

Therefore, we define

M_{\infty}(\omega,p)=\mathbb{B}_{k\ell}(v(p))\partial_{p_{\ell}}\left[F_{\infty}(p)m\left(\omega,v(p)\right)\mathcal{D}\left(p\right)\right]

and note that this quantity is uniformly bounded due to the compact support of
F_{\infty}
.
Then, we denote

\mathcal{H}(t,\omega,z,p)=\mathbb{B}_{k\ell}(v(p))\partial_{p_{\ell}}\left[g\left(t,z,p\right)m\left(\omega,v(p)\right)\mathcal{D}\left(p\right)\right]

and estimate the difference

\left|t^{4}M(t,\omega,y)-M_{\infty}\left(\omega,\frac{y}{t}\right)\right|=\biggl|\int\mathcal{H}\left(t,\omega,z,v^{-1}\left(\frac{y-z}{t}\right)\right)\ dz-\left(\mathbb{B}_{k\ell}\partial_{p_{\ell}}\left[m\mathcal{D}F_{\infty}\right]\right)\left(\omega,\frac{y}{t}\right)\biggr|.

This can be rewritten as

\left|t^{4}M(t,\omega,y)-M_{\infty}\left(\omega,\frac{y}{t}\right)\right|\leq I+II

where

I=\int\left|\mathcal{H}\left(t,\omega,z,v^{-1}\left(\frac{y-z}{t}\right)\right)-\mathcal{H}\left(t,\omega,z,v^{-1}\left(\frac{y}{t}\right)\right)\right|\ dz.

and

II=\left|\left(\mathbb{B}_{k\ell}\partial_{p_{\ell}}\left[m\mathcal{D}\left(F-F_{\infty}\right)\right]\right)\left(t,\omega,v^{-1}\left(\frac{y}{t}\right)\right)\right|

as

\int\mathcal{H}\left(t,\omega,z,p\right)\ dz=\mathbb{B}_{k\ell}(v(p))\partial_{p_{\ell}}\left[F\left(t,p\right)m\left(\omega,v(p)\right)\mathcal{D}\left(p\right)\right].

We estimate
I
merely by fixing
\omega\in S^{1}
and invoking Lemma
2.5
to find

I\lesssim t^{-1}\mathcal{R}(t)^{4}\|\nabla_{p}\mathcal{H}(t)\|_{\infty}.

As
m(\omega,v(p))
,
\mathcal{D}(p)
, their
p
-derivatives, and
\|g(t)\|_{\infty}
are all uniformly bounded, we have

\|\nabla_{p}\mathcal{H}(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\mathcal{G}_{2}(t),

and combining this with the estimates of Lemmas
3.4
and
3.5
gives

I\lesssim t^{-1}\mathcal{R}(t)^{4}\mathcal{G}_{2}(t)\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{7}(t).

The estimate of
II
uses Lemmas
3.6
and
4.3
and the compact support of
F(t)
and
F_{\infty}
to find

II\lesssim\|F(t)-F_{\infty}\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{p}F(t)-\nabla_{p}F_{\infty}\|_{\infty}\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{8}(t).

Combining these estimates then yields

\left|t^{4}M(t,\omega,y)-M_{\infty}\left(\omega,\frac{y}{t}\right)\right|\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{8}(t).

Finally, using this inequality and the boundedness of
M_{\infty}(\omega,p)
gives

\left|M(t,\omega,y)\right|\lesssim t^{-4}\left|M_{\infty}\left(\omega,\frac{y}{t}\right)\right|+\left|M(t,\omega,y)-t^{-4}M_{\infty}\left(\omega,\frac{y}{t}\right)\right|\lesssim t^{-4}\left|M_{\infty}\left(\omega,\frac{y}{t}\right)\right|+t^{-5}\ln^{8}(t)\lesssim t^{-4},

and yields the stated result.
∎

5.
Field Limits and Modified Scattering

5.1.
Convergence of the Electric and Magnetic Fields

With the large time asymptotic behavior established for the charge density, current density, and their derivatives, we can further generate the induced electric and magnetic fields as
t\to\infty
via self-similar solutions of inhomogeneous wave equations. In order to accomplish this, we will prove a lemma relating the asymptotic behavior of solutions to the inhomogeneous wave equation in terms of the associated forcing function.

First, we state a change of variables lemma from
[
11
]
that will allow us to estimate complicated integrals, both in the next lemma and within the next section, in which Theorem
1.1
is proved.

Lemma 5.1
.

[
11
, Lemma 6.5.2 (p. 176)]

For any continuous function
\Phi(\tau,\lambda)
of two real variables, and
\Psi(\sigma)
of one real variable, we have

\int_{|y-z|\leq s}\Phi(s-|y-z|,|z|)\Psi(|y-z|)\ dz=\frac{2\pi}{|y|}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{|s-|y|-\tau|}^{s+|y|-\tau}\Phi(\tau,\lambda)\lambda d\lambda(s-\tau)\Psi(s-\tau)d\tau,

where the integration on the left is over a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

With this, we can establish the asymptotic behavior of solutions to the inhomogeneous wave equation.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\eta\in C^{1}\left([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\right)
be supported on
\{(t,x):|x|\leq\zeta t+L\}

where
\zeta
is defined within Lemma
2.4
.
Assume that for
|x|\lesssim\gamma t
, the function
\eta
satisfies the estimate

\left|t^{4}\eta(t,x)-\eta_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{8}(t)

where
\eta_{\infty}\in C^{1}(\Gamma_{1})
has compact support.
Let
\psi(t,x)
be the unique solution of

(5.1)

\Box_{t,x}\psi=\eta(t,x)

with initial conditions

\psi(0,x)=\psi_{0}(x)

\partial_{t}\psi(0,x)=\psi_{1}(x),

both of which are supported in
\{|x|\leq L\}
.
Then, there exists
\psi_{\infty}\in C^{2,\delta}(\Gamma_{1})
for any
\delta\in(0,1)
such that

\left|t^{2}\psi(t,x)-\psi_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{8}(t)

for all
|x|\lesssim\gamma t
.
Additionally, if
\eta_{\infty}\equiv 0
, then
\psi_{\infty}\equiv 0
.

Proof.

We begin by constructing the limiting solution of the wave equation,
\psi_{\infty}
.
In particular, note that by assumption, the limiting profile of
\eta
(denoted
\eta_{\infty}
) possesses a self-similar structure.
Therefore, we will find a solution of the wave equation with this same property.
In this direction, we fix
T>0
such that for
t\geq T
, we have

\zeta t+L\leq\gamma t
and thus

\eta(t,x)
is supported on
\{(t,x):|x|\leq\gamma t\}
for
t\geq T
(see Figure
2
).

Figure 2.
Comparison of the sets enclosed by
L+\zeta t
and
\gamma t
where
0<\zeta<\gamma<1
.

Define the linear operator

\mathcal{L}u:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\left(y_{i}y_{j}-\delta_{ij}\right)\partial_{y_{i}y_{j}}u+6y\cdot\nabla_{y}u+6u

and note that for
|y|<\gamma<1

-\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\left(y_{i}y_{j}-\delta_{ij}\right)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}=|\xi|^{2}-|y\cdot\xi|^{2}>(1-\gamma^{2})|\xi|^{2}=C|\xi|^{2}

where
C>0
.
Thus, the operator is uniformly elliptic for sufficiently smooth functions defined on
\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|y|<\gamma\}
.
Further, as
\eta_{\infty}\in C^{1}
, we define
\psi_{\infty}\in C^{2,\delta}
for any
\delta\in(0,1)
to be the unique solution of the uniformly elliptic boundary-value problem
[
10
]

\mathcal{L}\psi_{\infty}(y)=\eta_{\infty}(y)

for
|y|<\gamma
with the boundary condition

\psi_{\infty}(y)=0\quad\mathrm{for}\ |y|=\gamma.

Due to the boundary condition, we merely extend
\psi_{\infty}(y)=0
for
|y|>\gamma
to preserve continuity and define the function for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Further,

\psi_{\infty}
will remain
C^{2,\delta}
on the interior of the sphere of radius
|y|=\gamma
.
Note that
\eta_{\infty}\equiv 0
implies
\psi_{\infty}\equiv 0
due to uniqueness.
Then, a brief calculation shows

(5.2)

\Box_{t,x}\left[t^{-2}\psi_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right]=t^{-4}\left(\mathcal{L}\psi_{\infty}\right)\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)=t^{-4}\eta_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)

for
|x|<\gamma t
.

With this, we let
\psi(t,x)
be the unique classical solution of (
5.1
) with the given initial conditions
\psi_{0}
and
\psi_{1}
at
t=0
. As we are interested in the values of this solution within the region
|x|\leq\gamma t
for
t\geq T
, we recast this as a forward-shifted initial-value problem for
t\geq T
with “initial” data at
t=T
and estimate within this region of space-time.
Using superposition, we may decompose the solution as

\psi(t,x)=\psi_{H}(t,x)+\psi_{I}(t,x)+\psi_{II}(t,x)

for
|x|<\gamma t

where (i)
\psi_{H}
is a homogeneous solution satisfying

\displaystyle\Box_{t,x}\psi_{H}=0

\displaystyle\mathrm{for}\ t>T,x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

\displaystyle\psi_{H}(T,x)=\psi(T,x)-T^{-2}\psi_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{T}\right)=:\widetilde{\psi}_{0}(x)

\displaystyle\mathrm{for}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi_{H}(T,x)=\partial_{t}\psi(T,x)-\partial_{t}\left(t^{-2}\psi_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right)\biggr|_{t=T}=:\widetilde{\psi}_{1}(x)\quad

\displaystyle\mathrm{for}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

(ii) the function
\psi_{I}
satisfies

\displaystyle\Box_{t,x}\psi_{I}=t^{-4}\eta_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)

\displaystyle\mathrm{for}\ t>T,x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

\displaystyle\psi_{I}(T,x)=T^{-2}\psi_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{T}\right)

\displaystyle\mathrm{for}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi_{I}(T,x)=\partial_{t}\left(t^{-2}\psi_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right)\biggr|_{t=T}\quad

\displaystyle\mathrm{for}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

and (iii)
\psi_{II}
satisfies

\displaystyle\Box_{t,x}\psi_{II}=\eta(t,x)-t^{-4}\eta_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\quad

\displaystyle\mathrm{for}\ t>T,x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

\displaystyle\psi_{II}(T,x)=0

\displaystyle\mathrm{for}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi_{II}(T,x)=0

\displaystyle\mathrm{for}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

With these functions well-defined, we consider only
t\geq T
and
|x|\leq\gamma t
and estimate
\psi_{H}
,
\psi_{I}
, and
\psi_{II}
separately.
We will further take
t
larger, as needed below, and in this case use the notation
|x|\lesssim\gamma t
.
First, we turn our attention to
\psi_{I}
. Note that by (
5.2
) and uniqueness, we must have

(5.3)

\psi_{I}(t,x)=t^{-2}\psi_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)

for all
t\geq T
and
|x|\leq\gamma t
, as these functions satisfy the same inhomogeneous wave equation with identical “initial” conditions at
t=T
.
As we will show, the leading contribution to
\psi(t,x)
will be the
\psi_{I}(t,x)
term, which is merely
t^{-2}\psi_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)
due to (
5.3
),

Next, we show that the homogeneous term
\psi_{H}
vanishes for sufficiently large
t
.
In particular, using the Kirchoff formula
[
8
]
we express the homogeneous solution
\psi_{H}(t,x)
for
t\geq T
and
|x|\leq\gamma t
exactly as

(5.4)

\psi_{H}(t,x)=\frac{1}{4\pi(t-T)^{2}}\left[\int_{|x-z|=t-T}\left(\widetilde{\psi}_{0}(z)+\nabla\widetilde{\psi}_{0}(z)\cdot(z-x)\right)dS_{z}+(t-T)\int_{|x-z|=t-T}\widetilde{\psi}_{1}(z)dS_{z}\right].

As
\psi_{0}(x)
and
\psi_{1}(x)
are supported on the set
\{|x|\leq L\}
, the solution
\psi(T,x)
of (
5.1
) is supported on
\{|x|\leq T+L\}
due to the finite speed of propagation of solutions to the wave equation and the support assumption on the source term
\eta
.
In particular, we note that the portion of
\psi(T,x)
that stems from the source term is exactly

\psi_{\text{inhom}}(T,x)=\int_{|x-z|\leq T}\eta(T-|x-z|,z)\frac{dz}{|x-z|},

and a brief calculation shows that
|x|>T+L
implies

|z|>\left(T-|x-z|\right)+L>\zeta\left(T-|x-z|\right)+L

for any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfying
|x-z|\leq T
.
As
\eta(t,x)
is supported on the set
\{(t,x):|x|\leq\zeta t+L\}
, such values lie outside of the support of this function when evaluated at the point
(T-|x-z|,z)
.
Hence,
\eta(T-|x-z|,z)=0
for
|x|>T+L
and any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfying
|x-z|\leq T
,
and we conclude that
\psi(T,x)
is supported on
\{|x|\leq T+L\}
.

Furthermore, as
\psi_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)=0
for
|x|\geq\gamma t
, we see that

\psi_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)
is supported on
\{|x|\leq\gamma t\}
, and this is a subset of
\{|x|\leq t+L\}
.
Thus, the new “data” functions
\widetilde{\psi}_{0}(x)
and
\widetilde{\psi}_{1}(x)
are also supported on
\{|x|\leq T+L\}
.
Recall that for
t\geq T
we have

|x|\leq\gamma t.

Therefore, we further take
t>\frac{2T+L}{1-\gamma}
so that for
z
in the support of
\widetilde{\psi}_{0}
and
\widetilde{\psi}_{1}
, we find

(5.5)

|x-z|\leq\gamma t+|z|\leq\gamma t+T+L<t-T

where
|x|\leq\gamma t
.
However, the integrals in (
5.4
) are taken over
|x-z|=t-T
, and as shown by (
5.5
), this set lies outside of the support of the integrands. Therefore, we have

(5.6)

\psi_{H}(t,x)=0

for
|x|\lesssim\gamma t
.

Finally, estimating
\psi_{II}
requires more care than the previous terms. We first denote

\mathcal{N}(t,x)=\left[\eta(t,x)-t^{-4}\eta_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right]\mathbbm{1}_{|x|\leq\gamma t}.

With this, we can express the solution of the associated inhomogeneous wave equation with zero “data” at
t=T
as

\psi_{II}(t,x)=\int_{|x-z|\leq t-T}\mathcal{N}(t-T-|x-z|,z)\frac{dz}{|x-z|}

for
t\geq T
.
For ease of notation, we let
s=t-T
and rewrite this as

\psi_{II}(s+T,x)=\int_{|x-z|\leq s}\mathcal{N}(s-|x-z|,z)\frac{dz}{|x-z|}

for
s\geq 0
.
Upon multiplying by
t^{-4}
, the assumed estimate on the integrand gives

\left|\eta(t,x)-t^{-4}\eta_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|\lesssim t^{-5}\ln^{8}(t).

In addition, as
\{|x|\leq\gamma t\}\subset\{|x|\leq t+L\}
, we can use the support of
\mathcal{N}
within this estimate to find

|\mathcal{N}(t,x)|\lesssim\left(t+|x|+2L\right)^{-5}\ln^{8}\left(t+|x|+2L\right)\mathbbm{1}_{|x|\leq t+L}.

This implies

|\psi_{II}(s+T,x)|\lesssim\int_{|x-z|\leq s}(s-|x-z|+|z|+2L)^{-5}\ln^{8}(s-|x-z|+|z|+2L)|x-z|^{-1}\mathbbm{1}_{|z|\leq s-|x-z|+L}dz.

To estimate the remaining integral, we rely upon Lemma
5.1
.
More specifically, we define a cutoff function
\phi_{L}(\lambda)
to be zero for
\lambda>\tau+L
and then choose

\Phi(\tau,\lambda)=(\tau+\lambda+2L)^{-5}\ln^{8}(\tau+\lambda+2L)\phi_{L}(\lambda)\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\Psi(\sigma)=\sigma^{-1}

in invoking Lemma
5.1
.
Thus, the integral of interest becomes

|\psi_{II}(s+T,x)|\lesssim r^{-1}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{a}^{b}(\tau+\lambda+2L)^{-5}\ln^{8}(\tau+\lambda+2L)\lambda\phi_{L}(\lambda)\ d\lambda d\tau

where

r=|x|,\qquad a=|s-r-\tau|,\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad b=s+r-\tau.

Using the inequalities

\tau+\lambda+2L\leq s+r+2L,\qquad\tau+\lambda+2L\geq s-r+2L,\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\lambda\leq\tau+\lambda+2L

simplifies the estimate to

|\psi_{II}(s+T,x)|\lesssim r^{-1}\ln^{8}(s+r+2L)(s-r+2L)^{-1}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{a}^{b}(\tau+\lambda+2L)^{-3}\ d\lambda d\tau.

Then, upon integrating in
\lambda
we find

(5.7)

|\psi_{II}(s+T,x)|\lesssim r^{-1}\ln^{8}(s+r+2L)(s-r+2L)^{-1}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{(b-a)(b+a+2\tau+4L)}{(\tau+a+2L)^{2}(\tau+b+2L)^{2}}\ d\tau.

To estimate the remaining integral, we first define the function

\mu(x)=\begin{cases}x,&x\geq 0,\\
0,&x<0\end{cases}

and split this into two pieces, namely

\int_{0}^{s}\frac{(b-a)(b+a+2\tau+4L)}{(\tau+a+2L)^{2}(\tau+b+2L)^{2}}d\tau=I+II

where

I:=\int_{0}^{\mu(s-r)}\frac{(b-a)(b+a+2\tau+4L)}{(\tau+a+2L)^{2}(\tau+b+2L)^{2}}\ d\tau

and

II:=\int_{\mu(s-r)}^{s}\frac{(b-a)(b+a+2\tau+4L)}{(\tau+a+2L)^{2}(\tau+b+2L)^{2}}\ d\tau.

To estimate
I
, we note that
\tau\leq s-r
on this interval so that

b-a=2\min\{r,s-\tau\}=2r\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad b+a=2\max\{r,s-\tau\}=2(s-\tau).

Furthermore, this implies
\tau+a=s-r
and
\tau+b=s+r
, and we find

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4r\int_{0}^{\mu(s-r)}\frac{s+2L}{(s-r+2L)^{2}(s+r+2L)^{2}}\ d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle r(s-r+2L)^{-2}(s+r+2L)^{-2}(s-r)(s+2L)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle r(s+r+2L)^{-1}(s-r+2L)^{-1}

Contrastingly, we estimate
II
, by first noting that
\tau\geq s-r
on this interval so that

b-a=2(s-\tau)\leq 2r\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad b+a=2r.

Furthermore, because
\tau+a=2\tau-s+r
and
\tau+b=s+r
, we find

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4r\int_{\mu(s-r)}^{s}\frac{\tau+r+2L}{(2\tau-s+r+2L)^{2}(s+r+2L)^{2}}\ d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle r(s+r+2L)^{-1}\int_{\mu(s-r)}^{\infty}(\tau+2L)^{-2}\ d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle r(s+r+2L)^{-1}(s-r+2L)^{-1}

as
\mu(s-r)\geq s-r
.
Now, as
I
and
II
satisfy the same estimate, we insert these within (
5.7
) to find

|\psi_{II}(s+T,x)|\lesssim(s+r+2L)^{-1}(s-r+2L)^{-2}\ln^{8}(s+r+2L).

Changing back to the
t
and
T
variables and removing
r=|x|>0
where possible yields

|\psi_{II}(t,x)|\lesssim(t-T+2L)^{-1}(t-T-|x|+2L)^{-2}\ln^{8}(t-T+|x|+2L).

Therefore, for
t
sufficiently large and
|x|\leq\gamma t
, we find

\displaystyle|\psi_{II}(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(t-T+2L)^{-1}((1-\gamma)t-T+2L)^{-2}\ln^{8}((1+\gamma)t-T+2L)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t^{-3}\ln^{8}(t).

Finally, assembling the estimate of
\psi_{II}
along with (
5.3
) and (
5.6
) gives

\displaystyle\left|\psi(t,x)-t^{-2}\psi_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\psi_{I}(t,x)-t^{-2}\psi_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|+|\psi_{H}(t,x)|+|\psi_{II}(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t^{-3}\ln^{8}(t),

for
|x|\lesssim\gamma t
.
Upon multiplying by
t^{2}
, the stated estimate follows, and the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

There exist

such that

{\sup_{|x|\lesssim\gamma t}}\left|t^{2}E(t,x)-E_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{8}(t),

and

{\sup_{|x|\lesssim\gamma t}}\left|t^{2}B(t,x)-B_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{8}(t).

such that

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|\lesssim\ln(t)\\
|p|\leq\beta\end{subarray}}\left|t^{2}K(t,x+v(p)t,p)-K_{\infty}\left(p\right)\right|\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{8}(t).

Proof.

Due to Lemma
2.4
, the supports of
\rho(t,y)
,

, and their derivatives all lie within

.

\left|t^{2}E^{i}(t,x)-E^{i}_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{8}(t)

for every

with
|x|\lesssim\gamma t
.

Similarly a wave equation for each component of the magnetic field can be derived from Maxwell’s equations resulting in

Thus, using the spatial derivative estimate on

from Lemma

, namely

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|t^{4}\partial_{x_{i}}j(t,x)-\partial_{q_{i}}j_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{8}(t)

,

\left|t^{2}B^{i}(t,x)-B^{i}_{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{8}(t)

|x|\lesssim\gamma t
.
Finally, assembling the convergence estimates (

) and (

) and defining

and

componentwise, we use these to construct the limiting Lorentz force given by

K_{\infty}(p):=E_{\infty}(v(p))+v(p)\times B_{\infty}(v(p))

for every

Further, considering

and

, and using the boundedness of

then directly yields

\displaystyle\left|t^{2}K(t,x+v(p)t,p)-K_{\infty}\left(p\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|t^{2}E(t,x+v(p)t)-E_{\infty}\left(v(p)\right)\right|+\left|t^{2}B(t,x+v(p)t)-B_{\infty}\left(v(p)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|t^{2}E(t,x+v(p)t)-E_{\infty}\left(\frac{x+v(p)t}{t}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|E_{\infty}\left(v(p)+\frac{x}{t}\right)-E_{\infty}\left(v(p)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|t^{2}B(t,x+v(p)t)-B_{\infty}\left(\frac{x+v(p)t}{t}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|B_{\infty}\left(v(p)+\frac{x}{t}\right)-B_{\infty}\left(v(p)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t^{-1}\ln^{8}(t)+\left(\|\nabla_{q}E\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{q}B\|_{\infty}\right)\frac{|x|}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t^{-1}\ln^{8}(t).

∎

In the next section, we will use this limiting Lorentz force and the associated convergence estimate to obtain a modified scattering result for solutions.

5.2.
Modified Scattering

With the field estimates solidified, we prove that the distribution function scatters to a limiting value as

along a specific trajectory in phase space that may differ from its linear profile, and this is known as “modified scattering”.
Many of the ideas in this direction arise from
[
21
,
26
,
9
]

First, we remind the reader that using the field estimates of Lemma

in Lemma

produces

|\mathcal{Y}(t,0,x,p)|\lesssim\ln(t)

where the implicit constant in this inequality may depend upon fixed

Thus, the spatial characteristics of

converges as

Lemma 5.4
.

There exists
f_{\infty}\in C(\mathbb{R}^{6})
such that

Moreover, we have the convergence estimate

\sup_{(x,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|f\biggl(t,x+v(p)t-\ln(t)\mathbb{A}(p)K_{\infty}(p),p\biggr)-f_{\infty}(x,p)\right|\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{8}(t).

Proof.

Let

\mathcal{W}(t,x,p)=x-\ln(t)\mathbb{A}(p)K_{\infty}(p)

for every
t\geq 1
and
x,p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
so that

h(t,x,p):=f\biggl(t,x+v(p)t-\ln(t)\mathbb{A}(p)K_{\infty}(p),p\biggr)=g(t,\mathcal{W}(t,x,p),p).

We will further take
t
to be sufficiently large as necessary.
Because of the compact momentum support of
g
, we note that
g(t,\mathcal{W}(t,x,p),p)=0
for
|p|>\beta
.
Additionally, there is
C_{0}>0

such that
|x|\geq C_{0}\ln(t)
implies
|\mathcal{W}(t,x,p)|\geq C_{1}\ln(t)
for some
C_{1}>0
, and thus
g(t,\mathcal{W}(t,x,p),p)=0
due to (
5.10
).
Hence,
g(t,\mathcal{W}(t,x,p),p)=0
whenever
|x|\geq C\ln(t)
or
|p|>\beta
, and it suffices to take
|x|\lesssim\ln(t)
and
|p|\leq\beta
throughout the proof. This further implies
|\mathcal{W}(t,x,p)|\lesssim\ln(t)
.
Because
g
satisfies (
1.5
), we deduce that

satisfies

\partial_{t}h=t^{-1}\left(t^{2}K(t,\mathcal{W}+v(p)t,p)-K_{\infty}(p)\right)\cdot\mathbb{A}(p)\nabla_{x}g(t,\mathcal{W},p)-K(t,\mathcal{W}+v(p)t)\cdot\nabla_{p}g(t,\mathcal{W},p).

Similar to the proof of Lemma
3.6
, we wish to show that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}h(t)\|_{\infty}
is integrable in order to establish the existence of a limiting function in this norm.

To this end, we decompose
\partial_{t}h
so that

\left|\partial_{t}h(t,x,p)\right|\lesssim I+II

where

I\leq t^{-1}\left|t^{2}K(t,\mathcal{W}+v(p)t,p)-K_{\infty}(p)\right|\ \left|\nabla_{x}g(t,\mathcal{W},p)\right|

and

2.4
and
3.5

(5.11)

|p|\leq\beta\end{subarray}}|K(t,x+v(p)t,p)|\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)\lesssim\mathcal{K}_{0}(t)\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)\lesssim t^{-2}\ln^{2}(t).

Using Lemma
2.4
yields

\|\nabla_{x}g(t)\|_{\infty}=\|\nabla_{x}f(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim 1.

Thus, the latter term in

I\lesssim t^{-1}\left|t^{2}K(t,\mathcal{W}+v(p)t,p)-K_{\infty}(p)\right|.

Because
\left|\mathcal{W}(t,x,p)\right|\lesssim\ln(t)
and
|p|\leq\beta
, we invoke Lemma
5.3
to conclude

Combining (

) and (

\|\partial_{t}h(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim t^{-2}\ln^{8}(t).

As this bound is integrable in time, there is
f_{\infty}\in C(\mathbb{R}^{6})
such that

\|h(t)-f_{\infty}\|_{\infty}\lesssim t^{-1}\ln^{8}(t),

which completes the proof.
∎

We first use Lemma
4.6
(more specifically, Remark
4.7
) and the boundedness of
d(\omega,v(p))
to obtain

(6.5)

\left|A_{\omega}\right|\leq\int_{|\omega|=1}\left|\int d(\omega,v(p))\partial_{x_{\ell}}f\ dp\right|d\omega\lesssim\epsilon_{0}t^{-4}.

2. Estimate of
A_{TT}
.

Following the strategy of Glassey-Strauss (see, e.g.
[
11
, pp. 178-179]
), we split this term into two parts in order to deal with the singularity as
|x-y|\to 0
.
In particular, we take
t
sufficiently large and write

\displaystyle A_{TT}=\iint_{|x-y|\leq\frac{1}{t}}a(\omega,v(p))\partial_{x_{\ell}}f|x-y|^{-3}\ dpdy+\iint_{\frac{1}{t}\leq|x-y|\leq t}a(\omega,v(p))\partial_{x_{\ell}}f|x-y|^{-3}\ dpdy=:I+II.

For
I
we use the fact that
\int_{|\omega|=1}a(\omega,v(p))\ d\omega=0
to introduce a term that makes no contribution but allows us to write

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\iint_{|x-y|\leq\frac{1}{t}}a(\omega,v(p))\partial_{x_{\ell}}f(t-|x-y|,y,p)|x-y|^{-3}\ dpdy

\displaystyle=\iint_{|x-y|\leq\frac{1}{t}}a(\omega,v(p))\partial_{x_{\ell}}\bigg(f(t-|x-y|,y,p)-f(t-|x-y|,x,p)\bigg)|x-y|^{-3}\ dpdy.

By the Mean-Value Theorem and Lemma
4.1
(more specifically, Remark
4.2
), we bound the difference by

\frac{\partial_{x_{\ell}}(f(t-|x-y|,y,p)-f(t-|x-y|,x,p))}{|y-x|}\leq\left\|\nabla^{2}_{x}f(t-|x-y|)\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|\nabla^{2}_{x}g(t-|x-y|)\right\|_{\infty}\lesssim\epsilon_{0}.

Moreover, the integration in the
p
variable is only over the set

\left\{p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\ :\ |\mathcal{X}(0,t-|x-y|,z,p)|\leq L\text{ for all }z\in[x,y]\right\}

where
[x,y]
is the line segment connecting
x
and
y
. From Lemma
3.1
(see also
[
11
, Lemma 6.3.3]
) we know that the diameter of this set is of order
(1+t-|x-y|)^{-1}
. Hence, we find

\displaystyle|I|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla^{2}_{x}g\|_{\infty}\|a\|_{\infty}\int_{|x-y|\leq\frac{1}{t}}(1+t-|x-y|)^{-3}|x-y|^{-2}\ dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon_{0}t^{-3}\int_{|x-y|\leq\frac{1}{t}}\left(\frac{1}{t}+1-\frac{|x-y|}{t}\right)^{-3}|x-y|^{-2}\ dy.

For
|x-y|\leq\frac{1}{t}
, we have
\left(\frac{1}{t}+1-\frac{|x-y|}{t}\right)^{-3}\lesssim 1
and thus conclude the estimate

(6.6)

|I|\lesssim\epsilon_{0}t^{-3}\int_{|x-y|\leq\frac{1}{t}}|x-y|^{-2}\ dy\lesssim\epsilon_{0}t^{-4}.

Turning to the estimate of
II
, we use Lemma
4.6
, (
6.3
), and Lemma
5.1
to find

\displaystyle|II|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\frac{1}{t}\leq|x-y|\leq t}\left|\int a(\omega,v(p))\partial_{x_{\ell}}f(t-|x-y|,y,p)\ dp\right||x-y|^{-3}\ dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon_{0}\int_{\frac{1}{t}\leq|x-y|\leq t}\left(t-|x-y|+|y|+2L\right)^{-4}|x-y|^{-3}\ dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{r}\int_{0}^{t-\frac{1}{t}}\int^{t+r-\tau}_{|t-r-\tau|}\left(\tau+\lambda+2L\right)^{-4}(t-\tau)^{-2}\ \lambda\ d\lambda d\tau

where
r=|x|
.
Next, we take
t
sufficiently large so that
1/t<t/2
and split the
\tau
-integral into

II=\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{r}\left(II_{A}+II_{B}\right)

where

II_{A}=\int_{0}^{t/2}\int^{t+r-\tau}_{|t-r-\tau|}\left(\tau+\lambda+2L\right)^{-4}(t-\tau)^{-2}\ \lambda\ d\lambda d\tau

and

II_{B}=\int_{t/2}^{t-\frac{1}{t}}\int^{t+r-\tau}_{|t-r-\tau|}\left(\tau+\lambda+2L\right)^{-4}(t-\tau)^{-2}\ \lambda\ d\lambda d\tau.

For brevity, we rename the lower and upper bounds of the
\lambda
integral
a
and
b
, respectively, so that

a=|t-r-\tau|\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad b=t+r-\tau.

As
\lambda,\tau,L>0
we have
\lambda\left(\tau+\lambda+2L\right)^{-4}\leq\left(\tau+\lambda+2L\right)^{-3}
. Then, using the relationships

(6.7)

t-r\leq\tau+a\leq\tau+\lambda\leq\tau+b=t+r

so that
\left(\tau+\lambda+2L\right)^{-1}\leq(t-r+2L)^{-1}

and performing the integration in
\lambda
, we find

\displaystyle II_{A}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{t^{2}(t-r+2L)}\int_{0}^{t/2}\int^{b}_{a}\left(\tau+\lambda+2L\right)^{-2}\ d\lambda d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{t^{2}(t-r+2L)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{b-a}{\left(a+\tau+2L\right)\left(b+\tau+2L\right)}\ d\lambda d\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{t^{2}(t-r+2L)(t+r+2L)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{b-a}{a+\tau+2L}\ d\lambda d\tau.

Now, we distinguish between the cases where
t-r-\tau
changes sign. Define the function

\mu(x)=\begin{cases}x,&x\geq 0,\\
0,&x<0.\end{cases}

We split the remaining integral at
\tau=\mu(t-r)
and use both
b-a=2\min\{r,t-\tau\}
and
\mu(t-r)\geq t-r
to find

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{b-a}{a+\tau+2L}\ d\lambda d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\mu(t-r)}\frac{2r}{t-r+2L}\ d\tau+\int_{\mu(t-r)}^{t}\frac{2(t-\tau)}{r-t+2\tau+2L}\ d\tau

\displaystyle\leq 2r\frac{\mu(t-r)}{t-r+2L}+\frac{1}{t-r+2L}\int_{\mu(t-r)}^{t}2(t-\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\left(r+\frac{r^{2}}{t-r+2L}\right)

\displaystyle\leq Cr\left(1+\frac{t+r+2L}{t-r+2L}\right)

\displaystyle\leq Cr\left(t+r+2L\right)\left(t-r+2L\right)^{-1}.

Using this within the estimate for
II_{A}
then yields

II_{A}\leq\frac{Cr}{t^{2}(t-r+2L)^{2}}\lesssim r(t+r+2L)^{-1}(t-r+2L)^{-3}.

Next, we estimate
II_{B}
. Because
\tau\geq t/2
,
\lambda\geq 0
, and
b^{2}-a^{2}=4r(t-\tau)
, we have

\displaystyle II_{B}

\displaystyle=\int_{t/2}^{t-\frac{1}{t}}\int^{b}_{a}\left(\tau+\lambda+2L\right)^{-4}(t-\tau)^{-2}\ \lambda\ d\lambda d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\left(t/2+2L\right)^{-4}\int_{t/2}^{t-\frac{1}{t}}(t-\tau)^{-2}\int^{b}_{a}\lambda\ d\lambda d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\left(t+2L\right)^{-4}\int_{t/2}^{t-\frac{1}{t}}(t-\tau)^{-2}(b^{2}-a^{2})\ d\tau

\displaystyle\leq Cr\left(t+2L\right)^{-4}\int_{t/2}^{t-\frac{1}{t}}(t-\tau)^{-1}\ d\tau

\displaystyle\leq Cr\left(t+2L\right)^{-4}\ln(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim r\ln(t+r+2L)(t+r+2L)^{-1}(t-r+2L)^{-3}.

Combining the estimates for
II_{A}
and
II_{B}
then provides

\displaystyle|II|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\epsilon_{0}\ln(t+r+2L)}{(t-r+2L)^{3}(t+r+2L)}.

Finally, assembling the estimates for
I
and
II
within
A_{TT}
, we have

(6.8)

\left|A_{TT}\right|\lesssim\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{t^{4}}+\frac{\epsilon_{0}\ln(t+r+2L)}{(t-r+2L)^{3}(t+r+2L)}\\
\lesssim\epsilon_{0}\ln(t+r+2L)(t-r+2L)^{-3}(t+r+2L)^{-1}.

3. Estimate of
A_{ST}+A_{TS}
.

First, we introduce a cutoff function
\varphi_{L}=\varphi_{L}(|y|)
that is
0
outside the support of
f(t-|x-y|,y,p)
; that is,
\varphi_{L}(|y|)=0
for all
y
such that
|y|\geq\gamma\left(t-|x-y|\right)+L
due to Lemma
2.4
. Then, we estimate this term as

\displaystyle A_{ST}+A_{TS}=

\displaystyle\ \iint_{|x-y|\leq t}\nabla_{p}b(\omega,v(p))\cdot\left(f\partial_{x_{\ell}}K+K\partial_{x_{\ell}}f\right)|x-y|^{-2}\ dpdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \left|\iint_{|x-y|\leq t}\nabla_{p}b\cdot\left(f\partial_{x_{\ell}}K\right)|x-y|^{-2}\ dpdy\right|+\left|\iint_{|x-y|\leq t}\nabla_{p}b\cdot\left(K\partial_{x_{\ell}}f\right)|x-y|^{-2}\ dpdy\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \int_{|x-y|\leq t}\left|\int(\nabla_{p}b)f(t-|x-y|,y,p)\ dp\right|\left|\nabla_{x}K(t-|x-y|,y)\right|\ |x-y|^{-2}\varphi_{L}(|y|)\ dy

\displaystyle+\int_{|x-y|\leq t}\left|\int(\nabla_{p}b)\partial_{x_{\ell}}f(t-|x-y|,y,p)\ dp\right|\left|K(t-|x-y|,y)\right|\ |x-y|^{-2}\varphi_{L}(|y|)\ dy

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\ I+II.

The two resulting terms are then estimated using (
6.4
) as well as, Lemmas
4.6
(more specifically, Remark
4.7
) and
5.1
to find

\displaystyle I\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \epsilon_{0}\|K\|_{1}\int_{|x-y|\leq t}\frac{\ln(t-|x-y|+|y|+2L)}{(t-|x-y|-|y|+2L)^{2}(t-|x-y|+|y|+2L)^{4}|x-y|^{2}}\ \varphi_{L}(|y|)\ dy

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \frac{\epsilon_{0}}{r}\|K\|_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\ln(\tau+\lambda+2L)}{(\tau-\lambda+2L)^{2}(\tau+\lambda+2L)^{4}(t-\tau)}\ \varphi_{L}(\lambda)\lambda\ d\lambda\ d\tau

and

\displaystyle II\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \epsilon_{0}\|K\|_{0}\int_{|x-y|\leq t}\frac{1}{(t-|x-y|-|y|+2L)(t-|x-y|+|y|+2L)^{5}|x-y|^{2}}\ \varphi_{L}(|y|)\ dy

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \frac{\epsilon_{0}}{r}\|K\|_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{1}{(\tau-\lambda+2L)(\tau+\lambda+2L)^{5}(t-\tau)}\ \varphi_{L}(\lambda)\lambda\ d\lambda\ d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \frac{\epsilon_{0}}{r}\|K\|_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{1}{(\tau-\lambda+2L)^{2}(\tau+\lambda+2L)^{4}(t-\tau)}\ \varphi_{L}(\lambda)\lambda\ d\lambda\ d\tau.

Upon reducing the three-dimensional integrals to a two-dimensional representation via Lemma
5.1
, we have
\varphi_{L}(\lambda)=0
for all
\lambda\geq\gamma\tau+L
where
\gamma\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)
.
Hence, combining these terms yields

(6.9)

|A_{ST}+A_{TS}|\lesssim\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{r}\|K\|\mathcal{A}(t,r)

where

(6.10)

\mathcal{A}(t,r):=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\ln(\tau+\lambda+2L)}{(\tau-\lambda+2L)^{2}(\tau+\lambda+2L)^{4}(t-\tau)}\ \varphi_{L}(\lambda)\lambda\ d\lambda\ d\tau.

As we’ll see in the next section, this term will appear again in the estimate of
A_{SS}
. To conclude this section, though, we merely estimate
\mathcal{A}(t,r)
.
We first use the upper bound on
\tau
to estimate the logarithmic term and then employ
\tau+\lambda+2L\geq\tau+a+2L\geq t-r+2L
to find

(6.11)

\mathcal{A}(t,r)\lesssim\frac{\ln(t+r+2L)}{t-r+2L}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{1}{(\tau-\lambda+2L)^{2}(\tau+\lambda+2L)^{3}(t-\tau)}\ \varphi_{L}(\lambda)\lambda\ d\lambda\ d\tau.

With this, we focus on estimating the remaining integral near and far from the singularity at
\tau=t
.
In particular, we break the integral at
\tau=\mu(t-r)
into

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{1}{(\tau-\lambda+2L)^{2}(\tau+\lambda+2L)^{3}(t-\tau)}\varphi_{L}(\lambda)\lambda\ d\lambda\ d\tau=I_{A}+I_{B}

where

I_{A}=\int_{0}^{\mu(t-r)}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{1}{(\tau-\lambda+2L)^{2}(\tau+\lambda+2L)^{3}(t-\tau)}\varphi_{L}(\lambda)\lambda\ d\lambda\ d\tau

and

I_{B}=\int_{\mu(t-r)}^{t}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{1}{(\tau-\lambda+2L)^{2}(\tau+\lambda+2L)^{3}(t-\tau)}\varphi_{L}(\lambda)\lambda\ d\lambda\ d\tau.

To estimate
I_{A}
, we consider
\tau\leq\mu(t-r)
. Then,
\mu(t-r)=0
implies
\tau=0
. Hence, for
\tau\neq 0
we have
\mu(t-r)=t-r
, and
\tau\leq\mu(t-r)=t-r
yields
r\leq t-\tau
.
Thus, we conclude

\lambda\leq b=r+t-\tau\leq 2(t-\tau).

Similarly, as
\mu(t-r)\leq t
we find

I_{A}\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{1}{(\tau-\lambda+2L)^{2}(\tau+\lambda+2L)^{3}}\varphi_{L}(\lambda)\ d\lambda\ d\tau.

Next, we change variables via
\alpha=\tau+\lambda
and
\beta=\tau-\lambda
, using
\tau+b=t+r
and
\tau+a\geq|t-r|
, as well as,

\tau-\lambda\geq\tau-(L+\gamma\tau)=(1-\gamma)\tau-L\geq-L,

to give the correct limits of integration so that

I_{A}\lesssim\int_{|t-r|}^{t+r}\frac{d\alpha}{(\alpha+2L)^{3}}\int_{-L}^{\infty}\frac{d\beta}{(\beta+2L)^{2}}\lesssim\int_{|t-r|}^{t+r}\frac{d\alpha}{(\alpha+2L)^{3}}.

Finally, we perform the integration in
\alpha
and use
|t-r|\geq t-r
to find

\displaystyle I_{A}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(|t-r|+2L\right)^{-2}-\left(t+r+2L\right)^{-2}

\displaystyle\lesssim r\left(t-r+2L\right)^{-2}\left(t+r+2L\right)^{-2}(t+2L)

\displaystyle\lesssim r\left(t-r+2L\right)^{-2}\left(t+r+2L\right)^{-1}.

Next, we estimate
I_{B}
by considering
\tau\geq\mu(t-r)
. Then, using
\lambda\leq L+\gamma\tau
and
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\geq 2
, we find

(\tau-\lambda+2L)^{-2}\leq\left[(1-\gamma)\tau+L\right]^{-2}=(1-\gamma)^{-2}\left[\tau+\frac{1}{1-\gamma}L\right]^{-2}\leq C(\tau+2L)^{-2}.

With this, we further employ the inequalities

\lambda\leq\gamma\tau+L\leq\tau+2L

and
(\tau+\lambda+2L)^{-3}\leq(\tau+2L)^{-3}

to yield

\displaystyle I_{B}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mu(t-r)}^{t}(\tau+2L)^{-1}(t-\tau)^{-1}\int_{a}^{b}(\tau+\lambda+2L)^{-3}\varphi_{L}(\lambda)\ d\lambda\ d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mu(t-r)}^{t}(\tau+2L)^{-4}(t-\tau)^{-1}(b-a)\ d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mu(t-r)}^{t}(\tau+2L)^{-4}\ d\tau

as
b-a=2\min\{r,t-\tau\}\leq 2(t-\tau)
.
Finally, removing two powers, evaluating the remaining integral, and using
\mu(t-r)\geq t-r
yields

\displaystyle I_{B}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\mu(t-r)+2L)^{-2}\int_{\mu(t-r)}^{t}(\tau+2L)^{-2}\ d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim(\mu(t-r)+2L)^{-2}\left[(\mu(t-r)+2L)^{-1}-(t+2L)^{-1}\right]

\displaystyle=(\mu(t-r)+2L)^{-3}(t+2L)^{-1}\left[t-\mu(t-r)\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim r(t-r+2L)^{-3}(t+r+2L)^{-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim r(t-r+2L)^{-2}(t+r+2L)^{-1}

as
t-r+2L\geq L
is implied by the field support condition
r\leq t+L
.

Combining the estimates for
I_{A}
and
I_{B}
ultimately gives

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{1}{(\tau-\lambda+2L)^{2}(\tau+\lambda+2L)^{3}(t-\tau)}\varphi_{L}(\lambda)\lambda\ d\lambda\ d\tau\lesssim r(t-r+2L)^{-2}(t+r+2L)^{-1},

and inserting this within (
6.11
) yields

(6.12)

\mathcal{A}(t,r)\lesssim r\ln(t+r+2L)(t-r+2L)^{-3}(t+r+2L)^{-1}.

Finally, using (
6.9
), we obtain

(6.13)

|A_{ST}+A_{TS}|\lesssim\epsilon_{0}\|K\|\ln(t+r+2L)(t-r+2L)^{-3}(t+r+2L)^{-1}.

4. Estimate of
A_{SS}
.

We follow the strategy of
[
11
]
where this term is broken up into several pieces, relying on the following representations of the expression
S(Kf)
and the operator
S^{2}
(see
[
11
, pp. 150-151]
)

\displaystyle S(Kf)

\displaystyle=-K\nabla_{p}\cdot(Kf)+fSK,

\displaystyle S^{2}f

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{p}\cdot[S(Kf)]+\sum_{j,k=1}^{3}\frac{\delta_{jk}-v^{j}(p)\cdot v^{k}(p)}{p_{0}}(f\partial_{x_{j}}K^{k}+K^{k}\partial_{x_{j}}f),

\displaystyle=\nabla_{p}\cdot\left[K\nabla_{p}\cdot(Kf)-fSK\right]+\sum_{j,k=1}^{3}\tilde{c}_{jk}(p)(f\partial_{x_{j}}K^{k}+K^{k}\partial_{x_{j}}f),

where we recall
p_{0}=\sqrt{1+|p|^{2}}
and have denoted
\tilde{c}_{jk}(p):=\frac{\delta_{jk}-v^{j}(p)v^{k}(p)}{p_{0}}
. We therefore have

\displaystyle A_{SS}=

\displaystyle\iint_{|x-y|\leq t}c(\omega,v(p))S^{2}(\partial_{x_{\ell}}f)|x-y|^{-1}\ dpdy=\iint_{|x-y|\leq t}c(\omega,v(p))\partial_{x_{\ell}}(S^{2}f)|x-y|^{-1}\ dpdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint_{|x-y|\leq t}c(\omega,v(p))\partial_{x_{\ell}}\left(\nabla_{p}\cdot[K\nabla_{p}\cdot(Kf)-fSK]+\sum_{j,k=1}^{3}\tilde{c}_{jk}(p)(f\partial_{x_{j}}K^{k}+K^{k}\partial_{x_{j}}f)\right)|x-y|^{-1}\ dpdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint_{|x-y|\leq t}c(\omega,v(p))\partial_{x_{\ell}}\left(\nabla_{p}\cdot[K\nabla_{p}\cdot(Kf)]\right)|x-y|^{-1}\ dpdy

\displaystyle+\iint_{|x-y|\leq t}c(\omega,v(p))\partial_{x_{\ell}}\left(\nabla_{p}\cdot[-fSK]\right)|x-y|^{-1}\ dpdy

\displaystyle+\iint_{|x-y|\leq t}c(\omega,v(p))\partial_{x_{\ell}}\left(\sum_{j,k=1}^{3}\tilde{c}_{jk}(p)(f\partial_{x_{j}}K^{k})\right)|x-y|^{-1}\ dpdy

\displaystyle+\iint_{|x-y|\leq t}c(\omega,v(p))\partial_{x_{\ell}}\left(\sum_{j,k=1}^{3}\tilde{c}_{jk}(p)(K^{k}\partial_{x_{j}}f)\right)|x-y|^{-1}\ dpdy=:I+II+III+IV.

We begin with
I
and integrate by parts to find

\displaystyle|I|=

\displaystyle\ \left|\iint_{|x-y|\leq t}c\ \partial_{x_{\ell}}\left(\nabla_{p}\cdot[K\nabla_{p}\cdot(Kf)]\right)|x-y|^{-1}\ dpdy\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \left|\iint_{|x-y|\leq t}\Big[\nabla_{p}(\nabla_{p}c\cdot\left(\partial_{x_{\ell}}K\right))\cdot(Kf)+\nabla_{p}(\nabla_{p}c\cdot K)\cdot\partial_{x_{\ell}}(Kf)\Big]|x-y|^{-1}\ dpdy\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \left|\iint_{|x-y|\leq t}\Big[\nabla_{p}(\nabla_{p}c\cdot\left(\partial_{x_{\ell}}K\right))\cdot(Kf)\Big]|x-y|^{-1}\ dpdy\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\iint_{|x-y|\leq t}\Big[\nabla_{p}(\nabla_{p}c\cdot K)\cdot\partial_{x_{\ell}}(Kf)\Big]|x-y|^{-1}\ dpdy\right|=:I_{A}+I_{B}.

Estimating
I_{A}
and using (
6.4
) and Lemma
5.1
we find

\displaystyle I_{A}

\displaystyle\leq(\|\nabla_{p}c\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{p}^{2}c\|_{\infty})\int_{|x-y|\leq t}\left(\int f\ dp\right)|\partial_{x_{\ell}}K||K|\ |x-y|^{-1}\ dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{r}\left(\|K\|_{0}^{2}+\|K\|_{1}^{2}\right)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\ln(\tau+\lambda+2L)}{(\tau+\lambda+2L)^{5}(\tau-\lambda+2L)^{3}}\varphi_{L}(\lambda)\lambda\ d\lambda d\tau

as
\|K\|_{0}\|K\|_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\|K\|_{0}^{2}+\|K\|_{1}^{2}\right)
.
Similarly, we use (
6.4
) and Lemmas
4.6
and
5.1
to estimate
I_{B}
as

\displaystyle I_{B}

\displaystyle\leq(\|\nabla_{p}c\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{p}^{2}c\|_{\infty})\int_{|x-y|\leq t}|K|\left[\left(\int f\ dp\right)|\partial_{x_{\ell}}K|+\left|\int\partial_{x_{\ell}}f\ dp\right||K|\right]|x-y|^{-1}\ dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{r}\left(\|K\|_{0}^{2}+\|K\|_{1}^{2}\right)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\lambda}{(\tau+\lambda+2L)^{2}}\left[\frac{\ln(\tau+\lambda+2L)}{(\tau+\lambda+2L)^{3}(\tau-\lambda+2L)^{3}}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.+\frac{1}{(\tau+\lambda+2L)^{4}(\tau-\lambda+2L)^{2}}\right]\varphi_{L}(\lambda)\ d\lambda d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{r}\left(\|K\|_{0}^{2}+\|K\|_{1}^{2}\right)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\ln(\tau+\lambda+2L)}{(\tau+\lambda+2L)^{5}(\tau-\lambda+2L)^{3}}\varphi_{L}(\lambda)\lambda\ d\lambda d\tau.

Hence,
I_{A}
and
I_{B}
satisfy the same estimate, allowing us to conclude

\displaystyle|I|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{r}\left(\|K\|_{0}^{2}+\|K\|_{1}^{2}\right)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\ln(\tau+\lambda+2L)}{(\tau+\lambda+2L)^{5}(\tau-\lambda+2L)^{3}}\varphi_{L}(\lambda)\lambda\ d\lambda d\tau,

and thus

|I|\lesssim\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{r}\left(\|K\|_{0}^{2}+\|K\|_{1}^{2}\right)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\ln(\tau+\lambda+2L)}{(\tau+\lambda+2L)^{4}(\tau-\lambda+2L)^{3}}\varphi_{L}(\lambda)\lambda\ d\lambda d\tau.

We define the remaining integral on the right side of the above estimate to be

(6.14)

\mathcal{B}(t,r):=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\ln(\tau+\lambda+2L)}{(\tau+\lambda+2L)^{4}(\tau-\lambda+2L)^{3}}\varphi_{L}(\lambda)\lambda\ d\lambda d\tau

so that

(6.15)

|I|\lesssim\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{r}\left(\|K\|_{0}^{2}+\|K\|_{1}^{2}\right)\mathcal{B}(t,r).

As we shall see below, all other terms
II-IV
will satisfy the same estimate as
I
, namely they will be bounded above by the right side of (
6.15
). Once we demonstrate this, we will then bound
\mathcal{B}(t,r)
at the end of this section to complete the estimate for
A_{SS}
.
We therefore turn our attention to
II
. We note that this term includes expressions involving the operator
S=\partial_{t}+v(p)\cdot\nabla_{x}
acting on the fields. The second part of this operator, namely the spatial derivative, is straightforward.
To estimate time derivatives we use Maxwell’s equations. Indeed, this yields

\|\partial_{t}E(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|\nabla_{x}\times B(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|\nabla_{x}K(t)\|_{\infty}

and from Lemma
3.8

Symmetric skew braces and brace systems
Valeriy G. Bardakov, Mikhail V. Neshchadim, Manoj K. Yadav

Abstract
. For a skew left brace ( G , ⋅ , ∘ ) (G,\cdot,\circ) , the map λ : ( G , ∘ ) → Aut ⁡ ( G , ⋅ ) , a ↦ λ a , \lambda:(G,\circ)\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot),~~a\mapsto\lambda_{a}, where λ a ​ ( b ) = a − 1 ⋅ ( a ∘ b ) \lambda_{a}(b)=a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ b) for all a , b ∈ G a,b\in G , is a group homomorphism. Then λ \lambda can also be viewed as a map from ( G , ⋅ ) (G,\cdot) to Aut ⁡ ( G , ⋅ ) \operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot) , which, in general, may not be a homomorphism. A skew left brace will be called λ \lambda -anti-homomorphic ( λ \lambda -homomorphic) if λ : ( G , ⋅ ) → Aut ⁡ ( G , ⋅ ) \lambda:(G,\cdot)\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot) is an anti-homomorphism (a homomorphism). We mainly study such skew left braces. We device a method for constructing a class of binary operations on a given set so that the set with any two such operations constitute a λ \lambda -homomorphic symmetric skew brace. Most of the constructions of symmetric skew braces dealt with in the literature fall in the framework of our construction. We then carry out various such constructions on specific infinite groups.

1.
Introduction

A triple
(G,\cdot,\circ)
, where
(G,\cdot)
and
(G,\circ)
are groups, is said to be a
skew left brace
if

(1.1)

a\circ(b\cdot c)=(a\circ b)\cdot a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ c)

for all
a,b,c\in G
, where
a^{-1}
denotes the inverse of
a
in
(G,\cdot)
. We call
(G,\cdot)
the
additive group
and
(G,\circ)
the
multiplicative group
of the skew left brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
. A skew left brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is said to be a
left brace
if
(G,\cdot)
is an abelian group. By analogy one can define a skew right brace and right brace. A triple
(G,\cdot,\circ)
, where
(G,\cdot)
and
(G,\circ)
are groups, is said to be a
skew right brace
if

(1.2)

(a\cdot b)\circ c=(a\circ c)\cdot c^{-1}\cdot(b\circ c)

for all
a,b,c\in G
. A skew right brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is said to be a
right brace
if
(G,\cdot)
is an abelian group. A skew left brace which is also a skew right brace is said to be
two-sided skew brace
.

In this article we mainly consider skew left braces. So, we’ll mostly suppress the word ‘left’ and only say skew brace(s).

The concept of braces was introduced by Rump
[
31
]
in 2007 in connection with non-degenerate involutive set theoretic solutions of the quantum Yang-Baxter equation.
We remark that, in fact, braces were considered by Kurosh in his lectures
[
27

of 1969–1970 academic year. The subject received a tremendous attention of the mathematical community after the work of Rump; see
[
2
,
13
,
21
,
26
,
32
,
34
]
and the references therein. Interest in the study of set theoretic solutions of the quantum Yang-Baxter equations was intrigued by the paper
[
17
]
of Drinfeld, published in 1992. Actually, set-theoretic solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation were studied before Drinfeld formulated his question. It was
Joyce
[
22
]
and Matveev
[
29
]
who introduced quandles as invariants of knots and links. Every quandle gives a set-theoretic solution of the Yang-Baxter equation.

The concept of skew braces was introduced by Guarnieri and Vendramin
[
19
]
in 2017 in connection with non-involutive non-degenerate set theoretic solutions of the quantum Yang-Baxter equation. For a skew left brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
, it was proved in
[
19
]
that the map

\lambda:(G,\circ)\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot),~~a\mapsto\lambda_{a}

is a group homomorphism, where
\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
denotes the automorphism group of
(G,\cdot)
and
\lambda_{a}
is given by
\lambda_{a}(b)=a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ b)
for all
a,b\in G
.
Obviously,
\lambda
can also be viewed as a map from
(G,\cdot)
to
\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
, which, in general, may not be a homomorphism.
We say that

is a
\lambda
-
anti-homomorphic skew left brace
(
\lambda
-
homomorphic skew left brace)
if the map
\lambda:(G,\cdot)\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
is an anti-homomorphism (a homomorphism). A
\lambda
-homomorphic skew left brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is said to be a
\lambda
-
cyclic skew left brace
if the image
\operatorname{Im}\lambda
is a cyclic subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
.
\lambda
-homomorphic skew braces of finite order were studied in
[
8
]
and that of infinite order in
[
4
]
.

It is a big challenge to describe all skew braces of a given order. For example, there are 51 groups of order 32, but there are 1223061 skew braces and 25281 left braces of order 32 (see
[
3
]
). It is not even easy to describe all skew braces with a given additive group. So the construction of certain specific classes of skew braces is highly desirable. In the present article our main focus is on the study of the following problem: For a given group
(G,\cdot)
, define binary operations ‘

’,

such that

,

,

,

2.
Brace systems

The concept of a brace block was introduced in
[
24
]
and further studied in
[
11
]
. Analogous concept of a multi-brace was introduced in
[
6
]
. In this section we intend to introduce a concept, which we will be calling a brace system, to generalize the concepts of brace block and multi-brace.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
and
V
be non-empty sets,
E\subseteq V\times V
.
A brace system on
G
associated with the pair
(V,E)
is an algebraic system

\mathcal{B}_{V,E}(G):=(G,\{\circ_{v}\}_{v\in V}),

where
\circ_{v}:G\times G\to G
is a binary operation for all
v\in V
, satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

(G,\circ_{v})
is a group for all
v\in V
,

(2)

(G,\circ_{u},\circ_{v})
is a skew left brace for all pairs
(u,v)\in E
.

For any non-empty set
V
and
E\subseteq V\times V
, we can associate an oriented graph
\Gamma=(V,E^{\prime})
with the set of vertices
V
and there is an oriented edge
e_{uv}
between
u,v\in V
if
(u,v)\in E
. More precisely
E^{\prime}=\{e_{uv}=(u,v)\in E\}
. Because of this association, the brace system
\mathcal{B}_{V,E}(G)
can also be denoted as
\mathcal{B}_{\Gamma}(G)
.

Notice that it is a two way passage. For any brace system
\mathcal{B}_{V,E}(G)
one can construct
an oriented graph
\Gamma=(V,E^{\prime})
with the set of vertices
V
and there is an edge between
u,v\in V
if
(G,\circ_{u},\circ_{v})
is a skew left brace. The preceding definition shows that each pair
(u,v)\in E
is an oriented edge. To distinguish, we denote

E^{\prime}=\{e_{uv}=(u,v)\in E\}.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{\Gamma}(G)
be a brace system on
G
associated to the graph
\Gamma=(V,E)
. Then
\mathcal{B}_{\Gamma}(G)
is said to be a
symmetric brace system
if for each pair
(u,v)\in V\times V
,
e_{uv}\in E
if and only if
e_{vu}\in E
. A symmetric brace system is said to be a
full symmetric brace system
on
G

if for any pair
(u,v)\in V\times V
, there exists an edge
e_{uv}\in E
.

One can see that if
\mathcal{B}_{\Gamma}(G)
is a symmetric brace system, then it defines a non-oriented graph since any edge has an opposite edge.
If
\mathcal{B}_{\Gamma}(G)
is a full symmetric brace system, then it defines a full (or complete) non-oriented graph.

Definition 2.3
.

(i) If
V=I
is a linearly ordered set and
E=\{(i,j)\in I\times I~|~i<j\},
then
the brace system
\mathcal{B}_{\Gamma}(G)
is said to be a
linear brace system
and is denoted by
\mathcal{B}_{I}(G)
.

(ii) If
\Gamma=(V,E)
is a rooted tree, meaning, there exists a vertex
v_{0}\in V
such that
for any
v\in V
there exists an oriented path from
v_{0}
to
v
, then the brace system

\mathcal{B}_{\Gamma}(G)
is said to be a
rooted brace system
.

Examples of linear and rooted brace system will be given in upcoming sections.

3.
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew braces

In this section we introduce the notion of
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew braces and see that these objects characterize symmetric skew braces. For any skew left brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
, it was proved in
[
19
]
that the map

\lambda:(G,\circ)\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot),~~a\mapsto\lambda_{a},

where
\lambda_{a}(b)=a^{-1}(a\circ b)
, is a group homomorphism.
Notice that
\lambda
can also be viewed as a set map from
(G,\cdot)
into
\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
. Not much is known about the properties of this map.
The map
\lambda
together with its domain, the additive group, characterizes the skew brace as a linear
q
-cycle set
[
33
]
.
We here take up the case when it is an anti-homomorphism, and introduce the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

A skew brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is said to be a
\lambda
-
anti-homomorphic skew brace
if the map
\lambda:(G,\cdot)\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
, defined by
\lambda_{a}(b)=a^{-1}(a\circ b)
,
a,b\in(G,\cdot)
, is an anti-homomorphism.

The following interesting property follows directly from the definition.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(G,\cdot,\circ)
be a
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew brace. Then for
a\in G
,
\bar{a}=a^{-1}u_{a}
for some
u_{a}\in\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda
, where
\bar{a}
denotes the inverse of
a
in
(G,\circ)
.

Proof.

As we know
\lambda:(G,\circ)\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
,
a\mapsto\lambda_{a}
, is a group homomorphism. So by the given hypothesis, for any
a\in G
, we have

\lambda_{\bar{a}}=\lambda^{-1}_{a}=\lambda_{a^{-1}},

which proves the required assertion.
∎

We remark that the skew braces
(G,\cdot,\circ)
in which the map
\lambda
is a homomorphism from
(G,\cdot)
into
\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
have been investigated earlier by some authors
[
8
]
,
[
4
]
. We’ll come back to it little later. Before we proceed, let us see an example.

Example 3.3
.

Let
G=(G,\cdot)
be any group. Define an operation ‘
\circ
’ on the set
G
by the formula

a\circ b=a\lambda_{a}(b)=ba

for all
a,b\in G
. Then it can be readily verified that
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is a skew brace. Now the map
\lambda:G\to\operatorname{Aut}\,G
,
a\mapsto\lambda_{a}
, where

\lambda_{a}(b)=a^{-1}(a\circ b)=a^{-1}ba\;\;\mbox{for all $b\in G$},

is an anti-homomorphism. Hence
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is a
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew brace.

Since the skew braces of the preceding example can be naturally defined for any group
G
, we would like to give those some name.

Definition 3.4
.

A skew brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is said to be
natural
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew brace
if
\circ=\cdot^{op}
.

We’ll show below that
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew braces can be characterised in terms of natural
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew brace.

Let
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is a
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew brace. Then for all
a,b,c\in G
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{a\circ b}(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\circ b)^{-1}(a\circ b\circ c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\circ b)^{-1}(a\circ(b\lambda_{b}(c)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{-1}a\lambda_{a}(\lambda_{b}(c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}(\lambda_{b}(c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{b\cdot a}(c)\;\;\mbox{ (by $\lambda$-anti-homomorphic property)}.

Hence

(3.5)

\lambda_{a\circ b}=\lambda_{b\cdot a}\;\;\mbox{ for all $a,b\in G$}.

Now we recall the definition of a symmetric skew brace, which is also known by the name bi-skew brace.

Definition 3.6
.

A skew brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is said to be a
symmetric skew brace
if
(G,\circ,\cdot)
is also a skew brace.

The necessary and sufficient condition for a skew brace to become symmetric skew brace is given in the following result
[
4
, Proposition 5.2, Remark 5.5]
.

Proposition 3.7
.

A skew brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is a symmetric skew brace if and only if

(3.8)

\displaystyle\lambda_{a\circ b}=\lambda_{b\cdot a}\;\;\mbox{for all $a,b\in G$}.

Let
(G,\cdot,\circ)
be a symmetric skew brace. To differentiate the maps
\lambda
, we write

\lambda^{\circ}
for the map
\lambda:(G,\circ)\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
and
\lambda^{\cdot}
for
\lambda:(G,\cdot)\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\circ)
. Rewriting (
3.8
) with the new notation, we get

(3.9)

\lambda^{\circ}_{a\circ b}=\lambda^{\circ}_{b\cdot a}\;\;\mbox{for all $a,b\in G$}.

Analogously we can also show that

(3.10)

\lambda^{\cdot}_{a\cdot b}=\lambda^{\cdot}_{b\circ a}\;\;\mbox{for all $a,b\in G$}.

We can now prove

Proposition 3.11
.

Every
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew brace is symmetric. On the other hand if
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is a symmetric skew brace, then both
(G,\cdot,\circ)
and
(G,\circ,\cdot)
are
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew braces.

Proof.

The first assertion follows from Proposition
3.7
using (
3.5
). Now assume that
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is a symmetric skew brace. Then (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) hold true. So by (
3.9
) we have

\lambda^{\circ}_{a\cdot b}=\lambda^{\circ}_{b\circ a}=\lambda^{\circ}_{b}\lambda^{\circ}_{a}\;\;\mbox{for all $a,b\in G$}.

This shows that
\lambda^{\circ}:(G,\cdot)\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
,
a\mapsto\lambda^{\circ}_{a}
, is an anti-homomorphism. Hence
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is a
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew brace. That
(G,\circ,\cdot)
is a
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew brace can be seen analogously using (
3.10
).
∎

Remark 3.12
.

The preceding proposition characterizes symmetric skew braces in terms of
\lambda
-anti-homorphic skew braces.

We remark that a similar characterization of symmetric skew braces (under the name bi-skew braces) in terms of
\gamma

Let us see another example, obtained through exact factorization (
[
4

The factorization is said to be exact if

Let a group

(a_{1}b_{1})\circ(a_{2}b_{2})=a_{1}a_{2}b_{2}b_{1}.

It is proved in

is isomorphic to the direct product

If

Let

The skew brace

We now device a method for constructing a
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew brace from a given anti-homomorphism of a group into its automorphism group. Let
G=(G,\cdot)
be a group and
\lambda:G\to\operatorname{Aut}\,G

a\lambda_{a}(b)a^{-1}b^{-1}\in\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda

. Then we can define a group
(G,\circ)

a\circ b=a\lambda_{a}(b).

Notice that
\bar{a}

\circ

\lambda^{-1}_{a}(a^{-1})
, where, as usual,
\lambda^{-1}_{a}

\lambda_{a}

is a
\lambda

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
G=(G,\cdot)
be a group and
\lambda:G\to\operatorname{Aut}\,G
be an anti-homomorphism such that
a\lambda_{a}(b)a^{-1}b^{-1}\in\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda
for all
a,b\in G
. Then
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is a
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew brace, where
a\circ b=a\lambda_{a}(b)
for all
a,b\in G
.

It now follows that
\lambda:(G,\circ)\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
,
a\mapsto\lambda_{a}
, is a homomorphism, where

\lambda_{a}(b)=a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ b)
for all
a,b\in(G,\circ)
. We first show that
\lambda(G)
is a subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\circ)
. To establish this, it is sufficient to show that
\lambda_{a}(b\circ c)=\lambda_{a}(b)\circ\lambda_{a}(c)
for all
a,b,c\in(G,\circ)
, which can be easily proved by a direct computation using the fact that
\lambda

\lambda_{a^{-1}}=(\lambda_{a})^{-1}
for all

It now follows that
\lambda:(G,\circ)\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\circ)

a\mapsto\lambda_{a}

\lambda:(G,\cdot)\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
.

{\bar{\lambda}}:(G,\circ)\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\circ)

{\bar{\lambda}}_{a}:=\lambda_{\bar{a}}=\lambda^{-1}_{a}\;\;\mbox{ for all $a\in(G,\circ)$}.

Then it easily follows that

is an anti-homomorphism and

\displaystyle{\bar{\lambda}}(a\circ{\bar{\lambda}}_{a}(b)\circ{\bar{a}}\circ{\bar{b}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{\overline{a\circ{\bar{\lambda}}_{a}(b)\circ{\bar{a}}\circ{\bar{b}}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{b\circ a\circ\overline{{\bar{\lambda}}_{a}(b)}\circ{\bar{a}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{a^{-1}\lambda_{a^{-1}}(b^{-1})ab}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Id},

Notice that for

a\circ_{1}b=a\circ{\bar{\lambda}}_{a}(b)=a\cdot\lambda({\bar{\lambda}}_{a}(b))=a\cdot b.

Hence

. We remark that further iteration will be a repetition, and will give no new skew brace.

In the following result we find a criterion to obtain a (symmetric) skew brace from two
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew braces with a common binary operation. For two subgroups
H

K

G

Lemma 3.16

Let
(G,\cdot,\circ)
and
(G,\cdot,\star)
be two
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew braces such that
[\lambda^{\circ}(G,\circ),\lambda^{\star}(G,\star)]=\operatorname{Id}

(G,\circ,\star)
is a skew brace if and only if (i)
[(G,\cdot),\lambda^{\star}(G,\star)]\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda^{\circ}
. Moreover,
(G,\circ,\star)

[(G,\cdot),\lambda^{\circ}(G,\circ)]\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda^{\star}

Proof.

Both
(G,\circ)
and
(G,\star)
are groups. For

\bar{a}
denote the inverse of
a
in
(G,\circ)
and
\hat{a}

(G,\star)

(G,\circ,\star)
is a skew brace if and only if

a\star(b\circ c)=(a\star b)\circ{\bar{a}}\circ(a\star c)\;\;\mbox{for all $a,b,c\in G$}.

Compute both sides separately in the skew braces
(G,\cdot,\circ)
and
(G,\cdot,\star)
simultaneously. First

\displaystyle a\star(b\circ c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\star(b\cdot\lambda^{\circ}_{b}(c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\cdot\lambda^{\star}_{a}(b\cdot\lambda^{\circ}_{b}(c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\cdot\lambda^{\star}_{a}(b)\cdot\lambda^{\star}_{a}(\lambda^{\circ}_{b}(c)).

Next

\displaystyle(a\star b)\circ{\bar{a}}\circ(a\star c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\cdot\lambda^{\star}_{a}(b))\circ{\bar{a}}\circ(a\cdot\lambda^{\circ}_{a}(c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\cdot\lambda^{\star}_{a}(b))\circ{\bar{a}}^{-1}\cdot(\bar{a}\circ\lambda^{\circ}_{a}(c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\cdot\lambda^{\star}_{a}(b))\circ\lambda^{\circ}_{\bar{a}}(\lambda^{\star}_{a}(c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\cdot\lambda^{\star}_{a}(b)\cdot\lambda^{\circ}_{a\cdot\lambda^{\star}_{a}(b)}(\lambda^{\circ}_{\bar{a}}(\lambda^{\star}_{a}(c)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\cdot\lambda^{\star}_{a}(b)\cdot\lambda^{\circ}_{\lambda^{\star}_{a}(b)}(\lambda^{\star}_{a}(c)).

Now
(G,\circ,\star)
is a skew brace if and only if
\lambda^{\star}_{a}(\lambda^{\circ}_{b}(c))=\lambda^{\circ}_{\lambda^{\star}_{a}(b)}(\lambda^{\star}_{a}(c))
if and only if
\lambda^{\circ}_{b^{-1}}(\lambda^{\star}_{a^{-1}}(\lambda^{\circ}_{\lambda^{\star}_{a}(b)}(\lambda^{\star}_{a}(c))))=c
, for all
a,b,c\in G
. By the given hypothesis this is equivalent to
\lambda^{\circ}_{\lambda^{\star}_{a}(b)b^{-1}}=\operatorname{Id}
for all
a,b\in G
, which is further equivalent to
[(G,\cdot),\lambda^{\star}(G,\star)]\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda^{\circ}
. This proves the first assertion. The second assertion holds by interchanging
\lambda^{\star}_{a}
and
\lambda^{\circ}_{a}
for
a\in G
in the above procedure.
∎

Let us see an example.

Example 3.17
.

Let
G=(G,\cdot)
be any group of nilpotency class
3
. Let
(G,\cdot,\circ)
be the
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew brace of Example
3.3
. Obviously
\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda^{\circ}=\operatorname{Z}(G)
. Now we construct another such skew brace. Let
\beta_{x}
be an inner automorphism induced by a fixed element
x\in G
, i.e.,
\beta_{x}(a)=x^{-1}ax
for all
a\in G
. Define
\lambda^{\star}:G\to\operatorname{Aut}\,G
,
a\mapsto\lambda^{\star}_{a}
, where

\lambda^{\star}_{a}(b)=(a^{-1}\beta_{x}(a))^{-1}ba^{-1}\beta_{x}(a)\;\;\mbox{for all $b\in G$}.

An easy computation shows that
\lambda^{\star}
is an anti-homomorphism.
Notice that for all
a,b\in G
, we have

a\lambda^{\star}_{a}(b)a^{-1}b^{-1}=[a,b][b,a^{-1}\beta_{x}(a)]\in\gamma_{2}(G).

Also notice that
u^{-1}\beta_{x}(u)\in\gamma_{3}(G)\leq\operatorname{Z}(G)
for all
u\in\gamma_{2}(G)
. It now follows that
a\lambda^{\star}_{a}(b)a^{-1}b^{-1}\in\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda^{\star}

(G,\cdot,\star)
is a
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew brace such that
\gamma_{2}(G)\leq\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda^{\star}

are satisfied, and hence
(G,\circ,\star)
is a symmetric skew brace.

We now restrict our attention to a special case of
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew braces
(G,\cdot,\circ)
in which
\lambda(G,\cdot)
is an abelian subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
. In this case

becomes a
\lambda
-homomorphic skew brace, a concept introduced and studied in
[

]
.
We take up this study in the next section.

4.
Brace systems through
\lambda
-homomorphic skew braces

In this section, we first recall the definition of a
\lambda
-homomorphic skew brace and its connection with regular subgroups of the holomorph of its additive group. We’ll then carry out detailed investigation of such skew braces with
\lambda(G)
abelian subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}\,G
.

Definition 4.1
.

A skew brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is said to be a
\lambda
-
homomorphic skew brace
if the map
\lambda:(G,\cdot)\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
, defined by
\lambda_{a}(b)=a^{-1}(a\circ b)
,
a,b\in(G,\cdot)
, is a homomorphism.

Let
G
be a group. The holomorph of
G
is the group
\operatorname{Hol}G:=\operatorname{Aut}G\ltimes G
, in which the product is given by

(f,a)(g,b)=(fg,af(b))

for all
a,b\in G
and
f,g\in\operatorname{Aut}G
.
Any subgroup
H
of
\operatorname{Hol}G
acts on
G
as follows

(f,a)\cdot b=af(b),\quad a,b\in G,\,\,f\in\operatorname{Aut}G.

A subgroup
H
of
\operatorname{Hol}G
is said to be
regular
if the action of
H
on
G
is free and transitive. It is equivalent to the fact that for each
a\in G
there exists a unique
(f,x)\in H
such that
xf(1)=a
.
Let
\pi_{2}:\operatorname{Hol}G\to G
denote the projection map from
\operatorname{Hol}G
onto the second component of
G
. Notice that
\pi_{2}|_{H}
, the restriction of
\pi_{2}
to
H
, is a bijection.

The following theorem provides a connection between skew braces and regular subgroups.

Theorem 4.2
.

[
19
, Theorem 4.2]

Let
(G,\cdot,\circ)
be a skew brace. Then
\left\{\,(\lambda_{a},a)\mid a\in G\,\right\}

is a regular subgroup of
\operatorname{Hol}G
, where
\lambda_{a}(b)=a^{-1}(a\circ b)
for all
b\in G
.

Conversely, if
(G,\cdot)
is a group and
H
is a regular subgroup of
\operatorname{Hol}\,(G,\cdot)
, then
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is a skew brace such that
(G,\circ)\cong H
, where
a\circ b=af(b)

with
(\pi_{2}|_{H})^{-1}(a)=(f,a)\in H
.

A sufficient condition, in view of Theorem
4.2
, on a group
G=(G,\cdot)
so that one can get a
\lambda
-homomorphic skew brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is given in the following result (
[
4
, Theorem 2.3]
):

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
G
be a group,
\lambda:G\to\mathrm{Aut}\,G
,
a\mapsto\lambda_{a}
, be a homomorphism of
G
into the group of its automorphisms. The set

H_{\lambda}:=\left\{\,(\lambda_{a},a)\,|\,a\in G\,\right\}

is a subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}\,G=\mathrm{Aut}\,G\ltimes G

if and only if

(4.4)

[G,\lambda(G)]:=\{\,b^{-1}\lambda_{a}(b)\mid a,b\in G\,\}\subseteq\mathrm{Ker}\,\lambda.

Moreover, if
H_{\lambda}
is a subgroup, then it is regular, and therefore by Theorem
4.2
we get a skew brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
, where ‘
\circ
’ is defined by
a\circ b=a\lambda_{a}(b)
.

We remark that this construction can also be carried out directly on the lines of the construction of
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew braces. More precisely, without getting into the regular subgroup business, we can directly prove

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
G=(G,\cdot)
be a group and
\lambda:G\to\operatorname{Aut}\,G
be a homomorphism such that
[G,\lambda(G)]\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda
. Then
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is a
\lambda
-homomorphic skew brace, where
a\circ b=a\lambda_{a}(b)
for all
a,b\in G
.

As a consequence we get

Corollary 4.6
.

If
\lambda(G)
is an abelian subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}\,G
, then the skew brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
constructed in the preceding theorem is symmetric. More precisely, any
\lambda
-homomorphic skew brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
with
\lambda(G)
abelian is symmetric.

Proof.

Since
\lambda(G)
is an abelian,
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is a
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew brace. Assertion now follows from Proposition
3.11
.
∎

We take a pause to present examples of a rooted brace system.

Example 4.7
.

With the preceding setup we can have the following constructions:

(1)

Let
(G,\cdot)
be a group and
\mathcal{H}
the set of regular subgroups of
\operatorname{Hol}\,G
. Then for any regular subgroup
H\in\mathcal{H}
there exists the operation
\circ_{H}
such that
(G,\cdot,\circ_{H})
is a skew left brace. Hence we have a rooted brace system
\mathcal{B}_{\Gamma}(G)
with the graph
\Gamma=(V,E)
, where
V=\{v_{0}\}\cup\{\circ_{H}\}_{H\in\mathcal{H}}
and
E=\{v_{0}\}\times\{\circ_{H}\}_{H\in\mathcal{H}}
.

(2)

By applying the construction as in (1) to the group
(G,\circ_{H})
for all
H\in\mathcal{H}
and continuing this process,
we can construct another rooted brace system.

Coming back, notice that the skew braces constructed through Theorem
4.3
are all
\lambda
-homomorphic. A variety of examples of such skew braces were constructed in
[
4
]
. Let
(G,\cdot,\circ)
be a
\lambda
-homomorphic skew brace. Then
\lambda:(G,\cdot)\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
,
a\mapsto\lambda_{a}
, is also a homomorphism. Hence for all
a,b,c\in G
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{a\circ b}(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}(\lambda_{b}(c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{a\cdot b}(c)\;\;\mbox{ (by $\lambda$-homomorphic property)}.

Hence

(4.8)

\lambda_{a\circ b}=\lambda_{a\cdot b}\;\;\mbox{ for all $a,b\in G$}.

As a consequence, we immediately get

[(G,\cdot),\lambda(G,\cdot)]\subseteq\mathrm{Ker}\,\lambda.

Now onwards we concentrate on the case when
\lambda(G,\cdot)
is an abelian subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
. We remark that in this special situation the concepts of
\lambda
-homomorphic and
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew braces coincide. The following lemma is the base for a recursive process of constructing skew braces.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
(G,\cdot,\circ)
be a
\lambda
-homomorphic skew brace such that
\lambda(G,\cdot)
is an abelian subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
. Then the homomorphism
\lambda
is also a homomorphism from
(G,\circ)
to
\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\circ)
. Moreover
\lambda(G,\circ)
is an abelian subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\circ)
and
[(G,\circ),\lambda(G,\circ)]\subseteq\mathrm{Ker}\,\lambda
.

Proof.

Since
\lambda(G,\cdot)
is an abelian subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
, obviously
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is also a
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew brace. It now follows from the preceding section (discussion after Theorem
3.15
) that
\lambda(G)
is a subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\circ)
.
Thus the homomorphism
\lambda:(G,\circ)\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
is also a homomorphism from
(G,\circ)
to
\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\circ)
with the image
\lambda(G,\circ)
.

Obviously
\lambda(G,\circ)
is abelian. Now using (
4.8
) and the given hypothesis, we get

\lambda({\bar{b}}\circ\lambda_{a}(b))=\lambda_{{\bar{b}}\circ\lambda_{a}(b)}=\lambda^{-1}_{b}\lambda_{\lambda_{a}(b)}=\lambda_{b^{-1}\lambda_{a}(b)}=\operatorname{Id}

for all
a,b\in(G,\circ)
. Hence
[(G,\circ),\lambda(G,\circ)]\subseteq\mathrm{Ker}\,\lambda
,
which completes the proof.
∎

We are now going to construct a symmetric linear brace system starting with a
\lambda
-homomorphic skew brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
such that
\lambda(G,\cdot)
is an abelian subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
, and iteratively applying Lemma
4.9
.
Let us denote
\cdot
and
\circ
by
\circ_{0}
and
\circ_{1}
respectively. Notice that
(G,\circ_{0},\circ_{1})
is a symmetric skew brace (Proposition
3.11
). By Lemma
4.9
we know that
\lambda:(G,\circ_{1})\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\circ_{1})
is a homomorphism,
\lambda(G,\circ_{1})
is abelian and
[(G,\circ_{1}),\lambda(G,\circ_{1})]\subseteq\mathrm{Ker}\,\lambda
. Then by Theorem
4.3
we get a
\lambda
-homomorphic skew brace
(G,\circ_{1},\circ_{2})
such that for
a,b\in G
,

a\circ_{2}b:=a\circ_{1}\lambda_{a}(b).

Again using Proposition
3.11
, we contend that it is symmetric.
By this iterative process, we can construct, for any integer
i\geq 1
, a symmetric
\lambda
-homomorphic skew brace
(G,\circ_{i},\circ_{i+1})
such that for
a,b\in G
,

a\circ_{i+1}b:=a\circ_{i}\lambda_{a}(b).

We now prove

Lemma 4.10
.

In the above construction
\lambda
is a homomorphism and
\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda
, as a subset of
G
, is the same at all levels. More precisely,
\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda
in
(G,\circ_{i})
is equal to
\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda
in
(G,\circ_{0})
for all
i
. Also
\lambda(G,\circ_{i})=\lambda(G,\circ_{0})
for all
i
.

Proof.

It is pertinent, by the very construction, that the following diagram is commutative.
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(G,\circ_{0})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\circ_{0})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(G,\circ_{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\circ_{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(G,\circ_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\circ_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(G,\circ_{3})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\circ_{3})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\vdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\vdots,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda^{\circ_{1}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda^{\circ_{2}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda^{\circ_{3}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda}

where
\operatorname{Id}
is the identity map on the level of permutations on
G
and
\lambda
is a homomorphism at each level.
We start with the fact that
\lambda
with respect to
\circ_{0}
and
\lambda^{\circ_{1}}
are equal at element level. Hence

\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda^{\circ_{1}}=\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda
. Now by the commutativity of the uppermost triangle, we have that the homomorphisms
\lambda^{\circ_{1}}
and
\lambda:(G,\circ_{1})\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\circ_{1})
are equal. Thus the kernels of
\lambda:(G,\circ_{0})\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\circ_{0})
and
\lambda:(G,\circ_{1})\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\circ_{1})
are the same as subsets of

. It also follows that
\lambda(G,\circ_{1})=\lambda(G,\circ_{0})
. A straightforward iterative argument now completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.11
.

If the exponent of
\lambda(G,\circ_{0})
is
n
, then
\circ_{n}=\circ_{0}
.

Proof.

For
a,b\in G
, we have

\displaystyle a\circ_{n}b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\circ_{n-1}\lambda_{a}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\circ_{n-2}\lambda^{2}_{a}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\circ_{0}\lambda^{n}_{a}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\circ_{0}b.

Since this is true for all
a,b\in G
, the assertion holds.
∎

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
0\leq i<j
be integers. Then
(G,\circ_{i},\circ_{j})
is a
\lambda
-homomorphic skew brace with
\lambda(G,\circ_{j})
is abelian. Hence
(G,\circ_{i},\circ_{j})
is a symmetric skew brace.

Proof.

For
a,b\in(G,\circ_{j})
, we have

\displaystyle a\circ_{j}b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\circ_{j-1}\lambda_{a}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\circ_{j-2}\lambda^{2}_{a}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\circ_{i}\lambda^{j-i}_{a}(b).

As we have seen above
\lambda:(G,\circ_{i})\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\circ_{i})
is a homomorphism which is equal, at the element level, to the homomorphism
\lambda:(G,\circ_{0})\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\circ_{0})
. Set
j-i=k
. Now consider the map
\lambda^{k}:(G,\circ_{i})\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\circ_{i})
,
a\mapsto\lambda^{k}_{a}
, where
\lambda^{k}_{a}=(\lambda_{a})^{k}
. Since
\lambda(G,\circ_{i})
is abelian, it follows that
\lambda^{k}
is a homomorphism. We claim that
[(G,\circ_{i}),\lambda^{k}(G,\circ_{i})]\leq\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda^{k}
.
We already know that
[(G,\circ_{i}),\lambda(G,\circ_{i})]\leq\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda
. So, for
a,b\in G
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{b^{\circ_{i}(-1)}\circ_{i}\lambda^{k}_{a}(b)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{b^{\circ_{i}(-1)}\circ_{i}\lambda^{k-1}_{a}(b)\circ_{i}\big((\lambda^{k-1}_{a}(b))^{\circ_{i}(-1)}\circ_{i}\lambda_{a}(\lambda^{k-1}_{a}(b))\big)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{b^{\circ_{i}(-1)}\circ_{i}\lambda^{k-1}_{a}(b)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{b^{\circ_{i}(-1)}\circ_{i}\lambda^{1}_{a}(b)}=\lambda_{b^{\circ_{i}(-1)}\circ_{i}\lambda_{a}(b)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Id}.

Hence
\lambda^{k}_{b^{\circ_{i}(-1)}\circ_{i}\lambda^{k}_{a}(b)}=(\lambda_{b^{\circ_{i}(-1)}\circ_{i}\lambda^{k}_{a}(b)})^{k}=\operatorname{Id}
. The proof now follows by first applying Theorem
4.3
and then Proposition
3.11
.
∎

Remark 4.13
.

Let
(G,\cdot,\circ)
be a
\lambda
-homomorphic skew brace such that
\lambda(G,\cdot)
is an abelian subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
. Then
(G,\circ,\cdot)
is a skew brace. Let
\xi:(G,\cdot)\to\operatorname{Aut}(G,\circ)
,
a\mapsto\xi_{a}
, be the corresponding map, where
\xi_{a}(b)={\bar{a}}\circ(a\cdot b)
for all
b\in G
. Notice that
\xi_{a}=\lambda^{-1}_{a}
for all
a\in G
. Hence
\xi
is a homomorphism and therefore,
(G,\circ,\cdot)
is a
\xi
-homomorphic skew brace with
\xi(G,\circ)
abelian in
\operatorname{Aut}(G,\circ)
. In the above notation
(G,\circ,\cdot)=(G,\circ_{1},\circ_{0})
. Define a binary operation
\circ_{-1}
on
G
as follows:

a\circ_{-1}b=a\circ_{0}\xi_{a}(b)\;\;\;(=a\circ_{0}\lambda^{-1}_{a}(b))

for all
a,b\in G
.
Then it follows that
(G,\circ_{0},\circ_{-1})
is a symmetric skew brace. For any integer

, iteratively define

a\circ_{-n}b=a\circ_{-n-1}\xi_{a}(b)

for all

. As above, we see that
(G,\circ_{-i},\circ_{-j})
is a symmetric skew brace for all

.

We have proved

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
G
be a group,
\lambda:G\to\operatorname{Aut}\,G
,
a\mapsto\lambda_{a}
, be a homomorphism such that
\lambda(G)
is abelian and
[G,\lambda(G)]\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda
.
Then there exists a symmetric linear brace system
\mathcal{B}_{I}(G):=(G,\{\circ_{i}\}_{i\in I})
on
G
,
I=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
, if the exponent of
\lambda(G)
is
n
. Otherwise
\mathcal{B}_{I}(G):=(G,\{\circ_{i}\}_{i\in I})
,
I=\mathbb{Z}
, is a symmetric linear brace system on
G
of infinite size.

Since a
\lambda
-homomorphic skew brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
with abelian
\lambda(G,\cdot)
is also a
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew brace, we can state Proposition
3.16
as follows.

Proposition 4.15
.

Let
(G,\cdot,\circ)
and
(G,\cdot,\star)
be two
\lambda
-homomorphic skew braces with abelian
\lambda^{\circ}(G,\circ)
and
\lambda^{\star}(G,\star)
such that
[\lambda^{\circ}(G,\circ),\lambda^{\star}(G,\star)]=\operatorname{Id}
in
\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
. Then
(G,\circ,\star)
is a skew brace if and only if (i)
[(G,\cdot),\lambda^{\star}(G,\star)]\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda^{\circ}
. Moreover,
(G,\circ,\star)
is a symmetric skew brace if and only if in addition to (i) we also have (ii)
[(G,\cdot),\lambda^{\circ}(G,\circ)]\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda^{\star}
.

Let us now see connection between different symmetric brace system constructed through Theorem
4.14
. When the group
G
and the linearly ordered set
I
are clear from the context, for simplicity, we only write
\mathcal{B}^{\circ}
for the symmetric linear brace system
\mathcal{B}_{I}(G):=(G,\{\circ_{i}\}_{i\in I})
.

Theorem 4.16
.

Let
\mathcal{B}^{\circ}:=(G,\{\circ_{i}\}_{i\in I})
and
\mathcal{B}^{\star}:=(G,\{\star_{i}\}_{i\in I})
be symmetric linear brace systems on
G
built on homomorphisms
\lambda^{\circ}:(G,\circ_{0})\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\circ_{0})
and
\lambda^{\star}:(G,\star_{0})\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\star_{0})
respectively through Theorem
4.14
, where
G=(G,\circ_{0})=(G,\star_{0})
. Let the hypotheses of Proposition
4.15
be satisfied for the skew braces
(G,\circ_{0},\circ_{1})
and
(G,\star_{0},\star_{1})
. Then
\mathcal{B}^{\circ}\cup\mathcal{B}^{\star}
is a symmetric brace system.

Proof.

For given
i,j\geq 1
, consider the groups
(G,\circ_{i})
and
(G,\star_{j})
. We know that
(G,\circ_{0},\circ_{i})
and
(G,\star_{0},\star_{j})
are symmetric skew braces. Let
\bar{\lambda}_{i}
and
\tilde{\lambda}_{j}
denote the homomorphisms

and
(G,\star_{j})\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\star_{0})
respectively. We know that
\bar{\lambda}_{i}(a)=(\lambda^{\circ}_{a})^{i}
and
\tilde{\lambda}_{j}(a)=(\lambda^{\star}_{a})^{j}
for all
a\in G
. It is now clear, using the given hypotheses, that
\bar{\lambda}_{i}:(G,\circ_{0})\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\circ_{0})
,
\tilde{\lambda}_{j}:(G,\star_{0})\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\star_{0})
are homomorphisms and the hypotheses of Proposition
4.15
are satisfied for the braces
(G,\circ_{0},\circ_{i})
and
(G,\star_{0},\star_{j})
. Hence
(G,\circ_{i},\star_{j})
is a symmetric skew brace. Since
i,j\geq 1
are arbitrary, it follows that
\mathcal{B}^{\circ}\cup\mathcal{B}^{\star}
is a symmetric brace system.
∎

We now present a generalization of Proposition
4.15
. Let
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
be two linearly ordered set and
I^{*}_{k}=I_{k}\cup\{0\}
,

. Let
\mathcal{B}_{I^{*}_{1}}(G)=(G,\{\circ_{i}\}_{i\in I^{*}_{1}})
and
\mathcal{B}_{I^{*}_{2}}(G)=(G,\{\star_{i}\}_{i\in I^{*}_{2}})
be two linear brace systems on
G
. Let
(G,\cdot,\circ_{i})
and
(G,\cdot,\star_{j})
for some
i\in I_{1}
,
j\in I_{2}
be two skew braces, where
\cdot=\circ_{0}=\star_{0}
. Let
\lambda:(G,\circ_{i})\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
and
\mu:(G,\star_{j})\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
be the corresponding homomorphisms. With this setup, we have

Lemma 4.17
.

The algebraic system
(G,\circ_{i},\circ_{j})
is a skew brace if

\mu_{a}(\lambda_{b}(c))=\lambda_{a\mu_{a}(b)}(\lambda_{a}^{-1}(a^{-1}))\left(\lambda_{a\mu_{a}(b)\lambda_{a\mu_{a}(b)}(\lambda_{a}^{-1}(a^{-1}))}(a\mu_{a}(c))\right)

for any
a,b,c\in G
.

Proof.

Obviously
(G,\circ_{i})
and
(G,\circ_{j})
are groups. We only weed to check the compatibility condition of the skew brace:

a\circ_{j}(b\circ_{i}c)=(a\circ_{j}b)\circ_{i}a^{\circ_{i}(-1)}\circ_{i}(a\circ_{j}c),~~~a,b,c\in G,

where
a^{\circ_{i}(-1)}
is the inverse to
a
in the group
(G,\circ_{i})
. Computing the left side, we get

a\circ_{j}(b\circ_{i}c)=a\mu_{a}(b\lambda_{b}(c))=a\mu_{a}(b)\mu_{a}(\lambda_{b}(c)),

where we used the fact that
\mu_{a}
is an automorphism of
(G,\cdot)
.

To compute the right side, notice that the inverse of
a
in
(G,\circ_{i})
is
a^{\circ_{i}(-1)}=\lambda_{a}^{-1}(a^{-1})
, where
a^{-1}
is the inverse of
a
in
(G,\cdot)
.
Then the right side is equal to

(a\circ_{j}b)\circ_{i}a^{\circ_{i}(-1)}\circ_{i}(a\circ_{j}c)=a\mu_{a}(b)\lambda_{a\mu_{a}(b)}(\lambda_{a}^{-1}(a^{-1}))\left(\lambda_{a\mu_{a}(b)\lambda_{a\mu_{a}(b)}(\lambda_{a}^{-1}(a^{-1}))}(a\mu_{a}(c))\right).

Comparing the left and the right sides, we get the required compatibility condition.
∎

5.
A unification for known symmetric brace systems

In this section we present a unification of most of the constructions of symmetric skew braces available in the literature. The concept was introduced by L.N. Childs in 2019 (
[
16
]
). Alan Koch
[
23
,
24
]
constructed symmetric brace systems on a given group
G
using abelian endomorphisms of
G
. This construction was generalized by A. Caranti and L. Stefanello
[
10
,
11
]
. We show that all theses symmetric skew braces are
\lambda
-homomorphic.

Let
G=(G,\cdot)
be a group. Let
\bar{A}
be an abelian subgroup of
\bar{G}:=G/\operatorname{Z}(G)
. The inverse image of
\bar{A}
in
G
is
A
. Let
\operatorname{Map}(G,A)
denote the set of all mappings from
G
into
A
. Define

\mathcal{A}:=\{f\in\operatorname{Map}(G,A)\mid f\;\;\mbox{induces an endomorphism from $\bar{G}$ into $\bar{A}$}\}.

We say that
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{A}
are related if both of these induce the same endomorphism
\bar{G}
into
\bar{A}
. This is an equivalence relation. Let
\bar{\mathcal{A}}
denote the set of all equivalence classes of the elements of
\mathcal{A}
. The equivalence class of an element
f\in\mathcal{A}
will be denoted by
[f]
. For a group
G:=(G,\cdot)
, we define

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}:=\{\alpha:G\times G\to\operatorname{Z}(G)\mid\alpha\mbox{ is bilinear and }\alpha(\operatorname{Z}(G),G)=\alpha(G,\operatorname{Z}(G))=e\},

where
e\in G
is the identity element. It follows from the definition that
\alpha(\gamma_{2}(G),G)=\alpha(G,\gamma_{2}(G))=e
.

Let
[f]\in\bar{\mathcal{A}}
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
. Define a map
\lambda:G\to\operatorname{Aut}\,G
,
a\mapsto\lambda_{a}
, where

\lambda_{a}(b)=f(a)^{-\epsilon}bf(a)^{\epsilon}\,\alpha(a,b)

with
\epsilon=\pm 1
. We claim that
\lambda
is a homomorphism and
\lambda(G)
is an abelian subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}\,G
. For
a,c\in G
, we know that
f(ac)=f(a)f(c)
modulo
\operatorname{Z}(G)
. Thus
f(ac)=f(a)f(c)z
for some
z\in\operatorname{Z}(G)
. Let
b\in G
. Since
f(G)
is abelian, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{ac}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(a)^{-\epsilon}f(c)^{-\epsilon}z^{-\epsilon}bz^{\epsilon}f(c)^{\epsilon}f(a)^{\epsilon}\,\alpha(ac,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(a)^{-\epsilon}f(c)^{-\epsilon}bf(c)^{\epsilon}\,\alpha(c,b)f(a)^{\epsilon}\,\alpha(a,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(a)^{-\epsilon}\lambda_{c}(b)f(a)^{\epsilon}\,\alpha(a,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(a)^{-\epsilon}\lambda_{c}(b)f(a)^{\epsilon}\,\alpha(a,\lambda_{c}(b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}(\lambda_{c}(b)).

This proves that
\lambda_{ac}=\lambda_{a}\lambda_{c}
. That
\lambda(G)
is abelian follows from the fact that
f(G)
is abelian and
\alpha(G,G)\subseteq\operatorname{Z}(G)
. This settles our claim.

Notice that
\gamma_{2}(G)\operatorname{Z}(G)\leq\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda
and
[G,\lambda(G)]\subseteq\gamma_{2}(G)\operatorname{Z}(G)
. Corollary
4.6
now gives

Proposition 5.1
.

(G,\cdot,\circ)
is a symmetric skew brace, where
a\circ b=a\lambda_{a}(b)
for all
a,b\in G
.

For each pair
([f],\alpha)\in(\bar{\mathcal{A}},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
, by Theorem
4.14
we get a symmetric linear brace system, which we denote by
\mathcal{B}_{(f,\alpha)}
. Since any two representatives of the class
[f]
give rise to the same inner automorphism
\lambda_{a}
for each
a\in G
, it follows that
\mathcal{B}_{(f,\alpha)}
is well defined. We now prove

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
G
be a group and
A^{\prime}
its subgroup such that
A^{\prime}\operatorname{Z}(G)/\operatorname{Z}(G)
is abelian. Let
A:=A^{\prime}\operatorname{Z}(G)
and the corresponding
\bar{\mathcal{A}}
be as defined above. Then

\bigcup_{([f],\alpha)\in(\bar{\mathcal{A}},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})}\mathcal{B}_{(f,\alpha)}

is a symmetric brace system.

Proof.

Let
[f_{1}],[f_{2}]\in\bar{\mathcal{A}}
be any two equivalence classes,
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{B}_{(f_{i},\alpha_{i})}
,
i=1,2
, be the corresponding symmetric brace systems. It suffices to show that
\mathcal{B}_{(f_{1},\alpha_{1})}\cup\mathcal{B}_{(f_{2},\alpha_{2})}
is a symmetric brace system. If
\mathcal{B}_{(f_{1},\alpha_{1})}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{(f_{2},\alpha_{2})}
or
\mathcal{B}_{(f_{2},\alpha_{2})}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{(f_{1},\alpha_{1})}
, then there is nothing to prove. So assume that the remaining case holds. To differentiate, assume that
{}_{(f_{i},\alpha_{i})}\lambda:G\to\operatorname{Aut}\,G
are the homomorphisms induced by
(f_{i},\alpha_{i})
,
i=1,2
. Let
(G,\cdot,\circ_{(f_{1},\alpha_{1})})
be the first skew brace in
\mathcal{B}_{(f_{1},\alpha_{1})}
and
(G,\cdot,\circ_{(f_{2},\alpha_{2})})
be the first skew brace in
\mathcal{B}_{(f_{2},\alpha_{2})}
. As we know that
\gamma_{2}(G)\leq\operatorname{Ker}\,_{(f_{i},\alpha_{i})}\lambda
and
[G,\,_{(f_{i},\alpha_{i})}\lambda(G)]\subseteq\gamma_{2}(G)
for
i=1,2
. It is now easy to see that the hypotheses of Theorem
4.16
are satisfied. Hence
\mathcal{B}_{(f_{1},\alpha_{1})}\cup\mathcal{B}_{(f_{2},\alpha_{2})}
is a symmetric brace system, and the proof is complete.
∎

6.
Further properties

In the present section we record various properties on the skew braces considered in previous sections. Let
(G,\cdot,\circ)
be a
\lambda
-homomorphic skew brace such that
b^{-1}\lambda_{a}(b)\in\operatorname{Z}(G,\cdot)
for
a,b\in G
. Then it easily follows that
\lambda_{a}(b)b^{-1}\in\operatorname{Z}(G,\cdot)
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
(G,\cdot,\circ)
be a
\lambda
-homomorphic skew brace with
\lambda(G)
abelian. If
[G,\lambda(G)]\subseteq\operatorname{Z}(G)
, then
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is a two-sided skew brace.

Proof.

Let
(G,\cdot,\circ)
be a skew braces as given in the hypothesis. We need to establish the relation

(a\cdot b)\circ c=(a\circ c)\cdot c^{-1}\circ(b\circ c)

for all
a,b,c\in G
. Since
G
is
\lambda
-homomorphic, we have

\displaystyle(a\cdot b)\circ c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\cdot b)\cdot\lambda_{a\cdot b}(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\cdot b)\cdot\lambda_{a}(\lambda_{b}(c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\cdot b)\cdot\lambda_{a}(c\cdot c^{-1}\cdot\lambda_{b}(c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\cdot b)\cdot\lambda_{a}(c)\cdot\lambda_{a}(c^{-1}\cdot\lambda_{b}(c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\cdot b)\cdot\lambda_{a}(c)\cdot c^{-1}\cdot\lambda_{b}(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\cdot b\cdot(\lambda_{a}(c)\cdot c^{-1})\cdot b^{-1}\cdot(b\cdot\lambda_{b}(c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\cdot\lambda_{a}(c))\cdot c^{-1}\cdot(b\cdot\lambda_{b}(c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\circ c)\cdot c^{-1}\cdot(b\circ c).

The proof is now complete.
∎

Let us see some examples. Let
(G,\cdot)
be a group of nilpotency class
2
and
(G,\cdot,\circ)
be any skew brace considered in Section
5
. Then
[(G,\cdot),\lambda(G,\cdot)]\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda\leq\operatorname{Z}(G)
. Hence all these skew braces are two-sided.
One can then construct radical rings from these two-sided skew braces
.

We now construct symmetric skew braces from
\lambda
-homomorphic skew braces. For that, we first recall the definition of opposite skew brace introduced in
[
33
, Section 3]
and further studied in
[
25
]
.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
(G,\cdot,\circ)
be a skew left brace. Define
\cdot^{op}:G\times G\to G
by
a\cdot^{op}b=b\cdot a
for all
a,b\in G
. Then
(G,\cdot^{op})
is a group and
(G,\cdot^{op},\circ)
is a skew left brace, which is called the
opposite skew left brace
of the given skew left brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
.

If
\circ=\cdot^{op}
, then
(G,\cdot^{op},\circ)
is a trivial skew brace. So we are mainly interested in non-trivial opposite skew braces;
\circ\neq\cdot^{op}
.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
(G,\cdot,\circ)
be a
\lambda
-homomorphic skew left brace. Then the opposite skew brace
(G,\cdot^{op},\circ)
is symmetric if and only if
\operatorname{Inn}\,(G,\cdot)
centralizes
\lambda\,(G,\cdot)
.

Proof.

Since
(G,\cdot^{op},\circ)
is a skew left brace, there exists a homomorphism
\lambda^{\prime}:(G,\circ)\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot^{op})
,
a\mapsto\lambda^{\prime}_{a}
, where
\lambda^{\prime}_{a}(b)=a^{-1}\cdot^{op}(a\circ b)
. We know that
\lambda:(G,\cdot)\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot)
,
a\mapsto\lambda_{a}
is a homomorphism, where
\lambda_{a}(b)=a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ b)
. Notice that

\lambda^{\prime}_{a}(b)=a^{-1}\cdot^{op}(a\circ b)=(a\circ b)\cdot a^{-1}=\iota_{a}(\lambda_{a}(b)),

where
\iota_{a}(x)=a\cdot x\cdot a^{-1}
for
x\in G
. Assume that
\operatorname{Inn}\,(G,\cdot)
centralizes
\lambda\,(G,\cdot)
. Now for
a,b,c\in(G,\cdot^{op})
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}_{a\cdot^{op}b}(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iota_{a\cdot^{op}b}(\lambda_{a\cdot^{op}b}(c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iota_{b\cdot a}(\lambda_{b\cdot a}(c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iota_{b}(\iota_{a}(\lambda_{b}(\lambda_{a}(c))))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iota_{b}(\lambda_{b}(\iota_{a}(\lambda_{a}(c))))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}_{b}(\lambda^{\prime}_{a}(c)).

This implies that
\lambda^{\prime}:(G,\cdot^{op})\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(G,\cdot^{op})
is an anti-homomorphism. Hence
(G,\cdot^{op},\circ)
is an anti-homomorphic skew brace, and therefore symmetric by Proposition
3.11
.

The converse part is left as an exercise for the reader.
∎

It is now not difficult to prove

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
(G,\cdot,\circ)
be a
\lambda
-homomorphic skew left brace such that
\lambda(G,\cdot)
is abelian and
\operatorname{Inn}\,(G,\cdot)
centralizes
\lambda(G,\cdot)
. Then
(G,\circ,\cdot^{op})
is a two-sided skew brace.

For a group
G
, let
\operatorname{Autcent}(G):=\operatorname{C}_{\operatorname{Aut}(G)}(\operatorname{Inn}(G))
be the group of all central automorphisms of
G
. Then any
\lambda
-homomorphic skew left brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
with
\lambda(G,\circ)\leq\operatorname{Autcent}(G,\cdot)
gives rise to a symmetric skew brace, namely
(G,\cdot^{op},\circ)
. In addition, if
\lambda(G,\circ)
is abelian, then
(G,\circ,\cdot^{op})
is a two-sided skew brace.
It is tempting to construct examples of such skew braces in which
\lambda(G,\circ)
is not contained in
\operatorname{Inn}(G,\cdot)
.

Let
(G,\{\circ_{i}\}_{i\in I})
be a symmetric linear brace system constructed from the
\lambda
-homomorphic skew brace
(G,\circ_{0},\circ_{1})
with
\lambda(G)
abelian by the procedure of Section 4. Then it follows from Lemma
4.10
that each group
(G,\circ_{i})
is an extension of
\operatorname{Ker}\lambda
by the abelian group
\lambda(G)
. It might very well happen that all
(G,\circ_{i})
constitute a single equivalence class of extensions. An example of such a symmetric linear brace system can be constructed from the
\lambda
-homomorphic brace
(\mathbb{Z}^{2},+,\circ_{1})
with
(\mathbb{Z}^{2},+)
a free abelian group with free basis
\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
and
a\circ_{1}b=a+\lambda_{a}(b)
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, where
\lambda_{x_{i}}
is the automorphism of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
defined on the basis elements as follows:

\lambda_{x_{i}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}x_{1}\mapsto(1+p)x_{1}-px_{2},\\
x_{2}\mapsto px_{1}+(1-p)x_{2}.\\
\end{array}\right.

It follows from
[
4
, Theorem 4.9 (2)]
that
(\mathbb{Z}^{2},\circ_{1})
is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}^{2},+)
. Using Lemma
4.9
and then Theorem
4.14
we can construct a symmetric linear brace system
\mathcal{B}_{I}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})=(\mathbb{Z}^{2},\{\circ_{i}\}_{i\in I})
such that
(\mathbb{Z}^{2},\circ_{i})
is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}^{2},+)
, where
I=\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
and
\circ_{0}=+
. One more such construction is carried out in the next section (Theorem
7.2
and its corollary). On the other hand, as we will see again in the next section (Corollaries
7.5
,
7.7
), there do exists a symmetric linear brace system which consists of infinitely many distinct equivalence classes of the extensions mentioned above.

We recall the definition of isomorphism of skew braces.

Definition 6.5
.

Let
(G,\cdot,\circ)
and
(H,\cdot,\circ)

be skew left braces. We say that they are isomorphic if there is a map

\varphi:G\rightarrow H
such that

\varphi:(G,\cdot)\rightarrow(H,\cdot)
,

\varphi:(G,\circ)\rightarrow(H,\circ)

are group isomorphisms.

For any
a,b,c\in G
, the following holds from the definition:

\varphi(a\circ(b\cdot c))=(\varphi(a)\circ\varphi(b))\cdot\varphi(a)^{-1}\cdot(\varphi(a)\circ\varphi(c)).

Definition 6.6
.

Let
(G,\cdot,\circ)=(H,\cdot,\circ)
, then any brace isomorphism from
(G,\cdot,\circ)
to
(H,\cdot,\circ)
is called a
brace automorphism
of
(G,\cdot,\circ)
. Let us denote the group of all brace automorphisms of
(G,\cdot,\circ)
by
\operatorname{Autb}\;G
.

From Lemma
4.9
we have

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
(G,\cdot,\circ)
be a
\lambda
-homomorphic skew brace with
\lambda(G)
is abelian. Then
\lambda(G)\leq\operatorname{Autb}\;G
.

In a given symmetric linear brace system
(G,\{\circ_{i}\}_{i\in I})
, it is not easy to specify whether two symmetric skew braces
(G,\circ_{i},\circ_{j})
and
(G,\circ_{i^{\prime}},\circ_{j^{\prime}})
are isomorphic or not.
We now establish a criterion for checking such an isomorphism. However, applying the same does not seem easy.

Suppose that we have two
\lambda
-homomorphic
skew left braces

(G,\cdot,\circ)
and
(H,\cdot,\circ)
which
are constructed by homomorphisms
\lambda:G\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}\,G
and

\mu:H\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}\,H
, that is, the operations are defined by the rules

a\circ b=a\lambda_{a}(b),\quad a,b\in G,

x\circ y=x\mu_{x}(y),\quad x,y\in H.

Let us find conditions under which these braces are isomorphic.

If
\varphi
is a brace isomorphism, then

\varphi(ab)=\varphi(a)\varphi(b),\quad\varphi(a\circ b)=\varphi(a)\circ\varphi(b),\quad a,b\in G.

The second equality has the form:

\varphi(a\lambda_{a}(b))=\varphi(a)\mu_{\varphi(a)}(\varphi(b))\Leftrightarrow\varphi(a)\varphi(\lambda_{a}(b))=\varphi(a)\mu_{\varphi(a)}(\varphi(b))\Leftrightarrow\varphi(\lambda_{a}(b))=\mu_{\varphi(a)}(\varphi(b)).

We can write the last equality in the form

\varphi\lambda_{a}\varphi^{-1}\varphi(b)=\mu_{\varphi(a)}(\varphi(b)).

Since
\varphi
is an isomorphism, we have

\varphi\lambda_{a}\varphi^{-1}=\mu_{\varphi(a)},\quad a\in G.

Hence, we have proved

Theorem 6.8
.

Two
\lambda
-homomorphic skew braces

(G,\cdot,\circ)
and
(H,\cdot,\circ)
,
which are constructed by homomorphisms
\lambda:G\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}\,G
and

\mu:H\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}\,H
, respectively, are isomorphic if and only if there is an isomorphism

\varphi:(G,\cdot)\rightarrow(H,\cdot)

such that

\varphi\lambda_{a}\varphi^{-1}=\mu_{\varphi(a)}\quad\mbox{for all }a\in G.

We conclude this section by generalizing the concept of meta-trivial skew brace introduced in
[
4
]
by defining a poly-trivial skew brace. Let us first recall the definition of an ideal of a skew brace (see
[
19
]
).
A non-empty subset
I
of a skew left brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is said to be an
ideal
of
G
if

1)
\lambda_{a}(I)\subseteq I
for all
a\in G
,

2)
I
is normal in
(G,\cdot)
,

3)
I
is normal in
(G,\circ)
.

For example, the kernel of any skew left brace homomorphism is an ideal.
The notion of ideal allows us to consider the quotient skew left brace
G/I
. Note that the condition 1) is equivalent to the equality
a\circ I=aI
for all
a\in G
. A skew left brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is said to be
meta-trivial
if it admits a ideal
I
such that both
I
and the quotient skew skew brace
G/I
are trivial. This concept can be generalised as follows:

Definition 6.9
.

A skew left brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is said to be a
poly-trivial skew brace
if there is a sequence of ideals of
G
:

\{e\}=I_{0}\leq I_{1}\leq\ldots\leq I_{n}=G,

such that
I_{i+1}/I_{i}
is a trivial skew brace for all
i=0,1,\ldots,n-1
. The minimal
n
with this property is called the
step of triviality
of
G
and is denoted by
st(G)
.

Notice that a meta-trivial skew brace is poly-trivial of step of triviality
2
. All
\lambda
-homomorphic skew braces are meta-trivial (
[
4
, Theorem 2.12]
). We now show that the converse of this statement (
[
4
, Question 2.13]
) is not true. For any non-trivial group
G:=(G,\cdot)
with abelian
\gamma_{2}(G)
and trivial
\operatorname{Z}(G)
, let
(G,\cdot,\circ)
, where
\circ=\cdot^{op}
, be
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew brace (see Example
3.3
). It follows from
[
15
, Proposition 2.3]
that
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is meta-trivial with
G^{2}=\gamma_{2}(G)
, where
G^{2}=\left<\lambda_{a}(b)b^{-1}\mid a,b\in G\right>
. Since
\lambda(G)\cong\operatorname{Inn}(G)
is not abelian,
(G,\cdot,\circ)
can not be
\lambda
-homomorphic. The simplest such example is
S_{3}
, the symmetric group on
3
letters, which was pointed out by L. Vendramin in the Zentralblatt and AMS review of
[
4
]
and also in
[
36
, Example 3.15]
.

We now discuss poly-triviality of
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic (symmetric) skew braces. We start by noting that any
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew brace with
\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda=1
is natural. Further we have

Proposition 6.10
.

Let
(G,\cdot,\circ)
be a
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew brace. Then either
(G,\cdot,\circ)
itself or
G/\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda
is a natural
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew brace.

Proof.

Suppose that
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is not a natural
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew brace. Notice that
\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda
is a normal subgroup of both
(G,\cdot)
and
(G,\circ)
. Also

\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda=\{a\in G\mid a\circ b=a\cdot b\mbox{ for all }b\in G\}.

For simplicity, let us denote this set by
G_{0}
. Obviously
G_{0}
is a trivial skew brace. Set
\bar{G}=G/G_{0}
. Then
(\bar{G},\cdot)
and
\bar{G},\circ)
are groups such that

G_{0}a\cdot G_{0}b=G_{0}a\cdot b\,\,\mbox{ and }\,\,G_{0}a\circ G_{0}b=G_{0}(a\circ b)

for all
a,b\in G
.

Since
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is a
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew brace, it follows that

a\lambda_{a}(b)a^{-1}b^{-1}\in\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda

for all
a,b\in G
. Hence

G_{0}a\circ G_{0}b=G_{0}(a\circ b)=G_{0}(a\lambda_{a}(b)a^{-1}b^{-1}ba=G_{0}ba=G_{0}b\cdot G_{0}a

for all
a,b\in G
. This proves that
G/\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda
is a natural
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew brace.
∎

As a consequence of the preceding result we conclude

Corollary 6.11
.

Any
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew brace is either natural or trivial-by-natural.

So, to characterise
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew braces, it only needs to study natural
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew braces. Note that the sub-skew brace
G_{0}=\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda
of a
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
as defined in the proof of Proposition
6.10
is an ideal of
(G,\cdot,\circ)
. For, since
a\lambda_{a}(b)a^{-1}b^{-1}\in\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda
, for any
a\in G
and
b\in G_{0}
,
\lambda_{a}(b)\in G_{0}
. So if
G/\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda
is poly-trivial with
st(G/\operatorname{Ker}\,\lambda)=n
, then
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is poly-trivial with
st(G)
at most
n+1
.

Let us now specialize to natural
\lambda
-anti-homomorphic skew braces. Suppose
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is such a skew brace with
\gamma_{2}(G,\cdot)
abelian. Then as discussed above

.

Now assume that

w=x_{1}^{m}w_{0},

where
m\in\mathbb{Z}
,
w_{0}\in\mathrm{Ker}\,\lambda
. Then

s^{-1}z_{j,k}s=w_{0}^{-1}z_{j,k-m-1}w_{0},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z},\quad j=2,\ldots,n.

Hence, the element
sw_{0}^{-1}
acts on the generators of
\mathrm{Ker}\,\lambda

by the shift of the indexes on
k
:

(sw_{0}^{-1})^{-1}z_{j,k}(sw_{0}^{-1})=z_{j,k-m-1},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z},\quad j=2,\ldots,n.

Thus

(F_{n},\circ)=H_{\lambda}=\mathrm{Ker}\,\lambda\rtimes\mathbb{Z}\cong F_{\infty}\rtimes\mathbb{Z},

where
F_{\infty}=\langle z_{j,k},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z},\quad j=2,\ldots,n\rangle

is a free group of infinite rank,
\mathbb{Z}=\left\langle s\right\rangle

is the infinite cyclic group and the action is defined by the rules

s^{-1}z_{j,k}s=z_{j,k-m-1},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z},\quad j=2,\ldots,n.

The proof is complete.
∎

As a direct consequence of Theorem
7.4
, we get

Corollary 7.5
.

There exists a symmetric linear brace system
\mathcal{B}_{I}(F_{n})=(F_{n},\{\circ_{i}\}_{i\in I})
,
n\geq 2
, where
I
is an infinite linearly ordered set.

Corollary 7.6
.

For any natural number
n\geq 2
there exists a homomorphism

\lambda:F_{2}\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}\,F_{2}
, such that for the skew left brace
(F_{2},\cdot,\circ)
the multiplicative group

(F_{2},\circ)
is isomorphic to
F_{n}
.

Let us define a subset of natural numbers consisting of

r_{0}=2,~~r_{k+1}=2r_{k}+1,~~k=0,1,\ldots

and define the graph
\Gamma
with the set of vertices
V=\{v_{k}~|~k=0,1,\ldots\}
, and set of edges

E=\{e_{k,k+1}=(k,k+1),e_{k+1,k}=(k+1,k)~|~k=0,1,\ldots\}.

From Theorem
7.4
for
m=1
it follows that we can construct brace system
\mathcal{B}_{\Gamma}(F_{2})
such that

(F_{2},\circ_{k},\circ_{k+1}),~~k=0,1,\ldots

is a symmetric skew brace such that
(F_{2},\circ_{k})\cong F_{r_{k}}
. Let
I=\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
. Hence, from this construction and Corollary
7.6
, we have

Corollary 7.7
.

There is a symmetric linear brace system
\mathcal{B}_{I}(G)=(G,\{\circ_{k}\}_{k\in I})
such that all groups
(G,\circ_{k})
are pairwise non-isomorphic.

8.
Rota–Baxter operators and brace systems

L. Guo, H. Lang, Y. Sheng
[
20
]
defined the notion of Rota–Baxter operator
on a group. A map
B\colon G\to G
is called a
Rota–Baxter operator
(of weight 1) if

B(g)B(h)=B(gB(g)hB(g)^{-1})

for all
g,h\in G
. A group
G
with a Rota–Baxter operator
B
is called a
Rota–Baxter group
.

A new binary operation
\circ\colon G\to G
on a Rota–Baxter group
(G,B)

was defined in
[
20
]
.

Proposition 8.1

(
[
20
]
)
.

Let
(G,\cdot,B)
be a Rota–Baxter group.

a) The pair
(G,\circ)
with the product

(8.2)

g\circ h=gB(g)hB(g)^{-1},

where
g,h\in G
, is also a group.

b) The operator
B
is a Rota–Baxter operator on the group
(G,\circ)
.

c) The map
B\colon(G,\circ)\to(G,\cdot)

is a homomorphism of Rota–Baxter groups.

As observed in
[
6
, Proposition 3.1]
, given a Rota–Baxter group
(G,\cdot,B)
, we get a skew left brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
, where ‘
\circ
’ is defined (
8.2
). Such a skew brace will be called a Rota–Baxter skew brace.
The following result provides a procedure for converting a word written in
(G,\circ)
to a word written in
G
.

Proposition 8.3

(
[
5
]
)
.

Let
(G,B)
be a Rota–Baxter group.
If
A
is a subset of
G
and

w=a_{i_{1}}^{\circ(k_{1})}\circ a_{i_{2}}^{\circ(k_{2})}\circ\ldots\circ a_{i_{s}}^{\circ(k_{s})},\quad a_{i_{j}}\in A,\ k_{j}\in\mathbb{Z},

is presented by a word under the operation
\circ
, then

w{=}(a_{i_{1}}B(a_{i_{1}}))^{k_{1}}(a_{i_{2}}B(a_{i_{2}}))^{k_{2}}\ldots(a_{i_{s}}B(a_{i_{s}}))^{k_{s}}B(a_{i_{s}})^{-k_{s}}B(a_{i_{s-1}})^{-k_{s-1}}\ldots B(a_{i_{1}})^{-k_{1}}.

In particular,

a^{\circ(-1)}=B(a)^{-1}a^{-1}B(a)
.

Suppose that
(G,\cdot,\circ_{B})
is a Rota–Baxter skew brace. The next proposition gives condition under which this skew brace is a symmetric skew brace.

Proposition 8.4
.

A Rota–Baxter skew brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is symmetric if and only if
B
is an anti-homomorphism modulo
Z(G^{(\cdot)})
, that is,

B(c)^{-1}B(a)^{-1}B(ca)\in Z(G^{(\cdot)})

for all
a,c\in G
. In particular, if
B
is an anti-homomorphism, then
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is a symmetric skew brace.

Proof.

As we know a skew brace is symmetric if and only if

\lambda_{a\circ c}(b)=\lambda_{c\cdot a}(b),~~~a,b,c\in G.

Rewrite the left side using part c) of Proposition
8.1
, we get

\lambda_{a\circ c}(b)=B(a\circ c)bB(a\circ c)^{-1}=B(a)B(c)bB(a)^{-1}B(c)^{-1}.

Rewriting the right side gives

\lambda_{c\cdot a}(b)=B(ca)bB(ca)^{-1}.

Hence,

[b,B(c)^{-1}B(a)^{-1}B(ca)]=1.

Since
b
is arbitrary element of
G
, one gets
B(c)^{-1}B(a)^{-1}B(ca)\in Z(G^{(\cdot)})
.

Converse part is left as an exercise.
∎

Corollary 8.5
.

If
B
is an endomorphism of
G
onto some abelian subgroup of
G
, then
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is a symmetric skew brace.

The next lemma follows from the definition of the Rota–Baxter operator

Lemma 8.6
.

If a Rota–Baxter operator
B:G\to G
is an anti-homomorphism, then

[B(b),B^{2}(a)B(a)]=1

for all
a,b\in G
.

Connection between Rota–Baxter skew braces
and
\lambda
-homomorphic skew left braces is given by

Proposition 8.7

(
[
6
]
)
.

A Rota–Baxter skew brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is
\lambda
-homomorphic
if and only if
B(ac)^{-1}B(a)B(c)\subseteq Z(G^{(\cdot)})

for all
a,c\in G
.

The following concept of a skew left multibrace was introduced in
[
6
]
.

Definition 8.8
.

Let
k
be a natural number.
By a
skew left
k
-brace
we mean a
(k+1)
-groupoid

(G,\circ_{0},\circ_{1},\ldots,\circ_{k})
, i. e.,
a non-empty set
G
with
k+1
binary algebraic operations, such that

1)
(G,\circ_{i})
is a group for all
i=0,1,\ldots,k
;

2) for
0<i\leq k

a\circ_{i}(b\circ_{i-1}c)=(a\circ_{i}b)\circ_{i-1}a^{\circ_{i-1}(-1)}\circ_{i-1}(a\circ_{i}c),

where
a^{\circ_{i-1}(-1)}
is the inverse to
a
in the group
(G,\circ_{i-1})
.

A skew left 1-brace is just the skew left brace.
By a
skew left multibrace
we mean a skew left
k
-brace for some
k>1
. In our terminology skew left multibrace is a brace system.

On a Rota–Baxter group one can construct multibrace using the following proposition.

Proposition 8.9

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
(G,\cdot,B)
be an RB-group and
k
be a natural number.
Define on the set
G
binary operations
\circ_{1},\circ_{2},\ldots,\circ_{k}
as follows,

x\circ_{i+1}y=x\circ_{i}B(x)\circ_{i}y\circ_{i}(B(x))^{\circ_{i}(-1)},

where
\circ_{0}=\cdot
. Then

(G,\cdot,\circ_{1},\circ_{2},\ldots,\circ_{k})
is a skew left
k
-brace.

If we take a skew brace
(G,\circ_{i},\circ_{i+1})
, then

\lambda^{(i)}_{x}(y)=B(x)\circ_{i}y\circ_{i}B(x)^{\circ_{i}(-1)}.

Just to demonstrate, let us rewrite first two new operations in terms of original operation
\cdot
. We have

x\circ y=x\circ_{1}y=x\cdot B(x)\cdot y\cdot(B(x))^{-1},

and

\lambda_{x}(y)=\lambda^{(0)}_{x}(y)=B(x)yB(x)^{-1}

Further,

x\circ_{2}y=x\circ_{1}B(x)\circ_{1}y\circ_{1}(B(x))^{\circ_{1}(-1)},

and by Proposition
8.3
, using the equality
(B(x))^{\circ_{1}(-1)}=(B^{2}(x))^{-1}B(x)^{-1}B^{2}(x)
, we get

x\circ_{2}y=x(B(x))^{2}B^{2}(x)yB(y)(B^{2}(x))^{-1}B(x)^{-1}B^{2}(x)B(y)^{-1}(B^{2}(x))^{-1}B(x)^{-1}.

In this case

\displaystyle\lambda^{(1)}_{x}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(x)\circ y\circ B(x)^{\circ(-1)}=B(x)\circ y\circ\left((B^{2}(x))^{-1}B(x)^{-1}B^{2}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(x)B^{2}(x)yB(y)(B^{2}(x))^{-1}B(x)^{-1}B^{2}(x)B(y)^{-1}(B^{2}(x))^{-1}.

Let
(G,\cdot)
be a group. Then the map
B(g)=g^{-1}
,
g\in G
, is a Rota–Baxter operator on
G
. We see that
B
is an anti-homomorphism. The skew brace constructed from the Rota–Baxter group
(G,\cdot,B)
is the one as in Example
3.3
. We conclude with another concrete example.

Example 8.10
.

Let
G=F_{2}(x,y)
be a free group of rank 2. The homomorphism
B:G\to\langle x\rangle
, which is defined on the generators by
B(x)=x
,
B(y)=x
is a Rota–Baxter operator on
G
. Let us construct multi-brace which it defines. For
a,b\in G
, we define

a\circ b=ax^{l(a)}bx^{-l(a)},

where
l(a)
is as defined in the preceding section. Inductively, for any integer
m\geq 1
, we can define binary operations

a\circ_{m}b=ax^{ml(a)}bx^{-ml(a)}.

It is not difficult to see that
(G,\cdot,\circ_{1},\circ_{2},\ldots)
is a multibrace with infinitely many binary operations.

Long-time Anderson Localization for the Nonlinear quasi-periodic Schrödinger Equation on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d}
Hongzi Cong, Yunfeng Shi, W.-M. Wang

Abstract
. Using a Birkhoff normal form transform to impede mode transfer in a finite “barrier”, we prove localization of arbitrary ℓ 2 \ell^{2} data for polynomially long time for the nonlinear quasi-periodic Schrödinger equation on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} .



1.
Introduction

We consider the nonlinear Schrödinger equation on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
:

(1.1)

{\rm{i}}\dot{q}_{\bm{j}}=V_{\bm{j}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\alpha})q_{\bm{j}}+\epsilon_{1}(\Delta q)_{\bm{j}}+\epsilon_{2}|q_{\bm{j}}|^{2}q_{\bm{j}},\,{\bm{j}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

where
\epsilon_{1}
and
\epsilon_{2}
are parameters in
[0,1]
, and

(\Delta q)_{\bm{j}}=\sum_{\bm{j}^{\prime},|\bm{j}^{\prime}-\bm{j}|_{1}=1}q_{\bm{j}^{\prime}},

is the usual discrete Laplacian,
|\bm{j}|_{1}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq d}|j_{i}|
and
the potential
V_{\bm{j}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\alpha})
is a trigonometric polynomial given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle V_{\bm{j}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\alpha})=\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\Gamma_{L}}v_{\bm{\ell}}\cos 2\pi\left(\bm{\ell}\cdot\left(\bm{\theta}+\bm{j}\bm{\alpha}\right)\right)\ {\rm with}\ \bm{\theta},\bm{\alpha}\in[0,1]^{d},

where

\displaystyle\bm{x}\bm{y}

\displaystyle:=(x_{1}y_{1},\cdots,x_{d}y_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\displaystyle\bm{x}\cdot\bm{y}

\displaystyle:=x_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+x_{d}y_{d}\in\mathbb{R},

for
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\Gamma_{L}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d},\ L\in\mathbb{N}
satisfies the following properties:

\displaystyle\text{(a) for each }\bm{\ell}=(\ell_{k})_{1\leq k\leq d}\in\Gamma_{L},\ell_{k}\neq 0\ {\rm for}\ \forall\ k=1,2,...,d;

\displaystyle\text{(b) for any two }\ \bm{\ell},\bm{\ell}^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{L},\,\bm{\ell}+\bm{\ell}^{\prime}\neq 0.

Note that finite multi-variable cosine series naturally satisfy conditions (a) and (b).

When
\epsilon_{2}=0
, it is known from the work of Bourgain
[
Bou07
]
that for analytic
V
, for any fixed
\bm{\theta}
, there
is a large set in
\bm{\alpha}
, so that the linear Schrödinger operator
H
:

H=V(\bm{\theta},\bm{\alpha})+\epsilon_{1}\Delta

has Anderson localization if
\epsilon_{1}
is sufficiently small. (See also
[
BGS02
]
for
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
; and
[
JLS20
]
for general long-range operators on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.)
Consequently using eigenfunction expansion, all
\ell^{2}
solutions which are initially localized about
the origin, remain localized for all time.

The main purpose of this paper is to prove an analogous, long time result, for the nonlinear equation (
1.1
), when
\epsilon_{2}\neq 0
.
Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Given any
\delta,\gamma>0,

M\gg 1
and for all initial datum
q(0)\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, let
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that

\sum_{|\bm{j}|>j_{0}}|q_{j}(0)|^{2}<\delta,

where
|\bm{j}|=\sqrt{\sum_{1\leq i\leq d}|j_{i}|^{2}}
.
Then there exists a constant
\varepsilon(\gamma,L,M,j_{0})>0
so that the following holds: for
0<\epsilon:=\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}<\varepsilon(\gamma,L,M,j_{0})
and
for all

|t|\leq\delta\cdot\epsilon^{-M}

one has

\sum_{|\bm{j}|>j_{0}+M^{2}}|q_{j}(t)|^{2}<2\delta,

on a set in
(\bm{\theta},\bm{\alpha})
of measure at least

\displaystyle 1-\gamma.

1.1.
Ideas of the proof

We use a Birkhoff normal form transform to prove the Theorem. This transform is related to that in
[
BW07
,
WZ09
,
CSZ21
]
in the random setting.
(For physical motivations of the problem, see e.g.,
[
FKS12
]
.)
Here we extend it to the quasi-periodic setting, and to arbitrary dimensions. Similar to
[
BW07
,
WZ09
,
CSZ21
]
, this normal form transform is effectuated in a finite neighborhood,
seen as a “barrier” to impede transfer to higher modes, higher indexed lattice sites.

Small divisor conditions are imposed, as usual, in the barrier for the normal form transform. The measure estimate of these small divisor conditions is more difficult
than that in the random case. We use the generalized Wronskian approach initiated in
[
SW23a
,
SW23b
]
. This is the main novelty here.

In
[
SW23a
]
, the Wronskian method was first used for the cosine potential
in one dimensional phase space, (i.e, the
\bm{\theta}
in (
1.1
) is one dimensional).
As it turns out, however, this method is general, and applicable to arbitrary trigonometric polynomials in arbitrary dimensional
phase space
[
SW23b
]
. Here we follow
[
SW23b
]
to make small divisor estimates and to prove that arbitrary
\ell^{2}
data remain localized, in the sense of the Theorem, for polynomially long time.

1.2.
Global in time solutions to quasi-periodic systems

It is natural to enquire whether nonlinear quasi-periodic systems such as equation (
1.1
) have
global
solutions. In
[
SW23a
]
, quasi-periodic in time
solutions were constructed for the nonlinear quasi-periodic wave equations (NLQPW) in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, for the cosine potential (in one dimensional phase space).
Related results for the nonlinear quasi-periodic Schrödinger equation on
\mathbb{Z}
were known before, see e.g.,
[
GYZ14
,
Yua02
]
. But the existence of quasi-periodic in time
solutions to NLQPW seemed to have remained open until
[
SW23a
]
. Finally, we would like to mention that the recent result in
[
SW23b
]
should pave the way toward
the existence of quasi-periodic in time solutions to (
1.1
), and also its NLQPW counterpart, in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, for arbitrary trigonometric polynomial potentials, thus further generalizing Bourgain’s work
[
Bou07
]
to a nonlinear setting.

1.3.
Some details about the small divisors

Before closing the section, let us give a bit more precision on the small divisors, which the reader may skip as a first reading. As mentioned earlier, the Birkhoff normal form transform is in a finite neighborhood, more precisely, in
an annulus neighborhood of the sphere
A(j_{0}):=\left\{\bm{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\left|\bm{j}\right|=j_{0}\right\}
,
which then acts as a “barrier” to impede mode transfer.
We choose “good”
(\bm{\theta},\bm{\alpha})
so that
V=(V_{\bm{j}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\alpha}))_{\bm{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
satisfies suitable
non-resonant conditions
.

More precisely, in the Birkhoff normal form transform, the values of the potential
V
at different sites
\bm{j}
,
V_{\bm{j}}
act as components of a frequency vector. Given
\gamma,L,M,j_{0}>0
, we say that a frequency vector
\omega=(\omega_{\bm{j}})_{\bm{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is
(\gamma,L,M,j_{0})
-nonresonant if for any
0\neq\bm{k}=(k_{\bm{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\bm{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}k_{\bm{j}}\omega_{\bm{j}}\right|\geq\left(\frac{\gamma}{\left(2LM^{2}j_{0}\right)^{2(d+1)}}\right)^{10L^{4}M^{4}}.

Since the normal form transform is in the barrier only, it suffices to realize the above non-resonant condition near
A(j_{0})
.
We remark that this is an essential point in the proof.

The structure of the paper is as follows. Some important facts on Hamiltonian dynamics, such as the Poisson bracket, symplectic transformation and non-resonant conditions
are presented in §2. The Birkhoff normal form type theorem and the main theorem are proved in §3. The measure estimates for the small divisors are in §4. Subsequently §5 concludes
the proof of some technical lemmas.

2.
Structure of the transformed Hamiltonian

We recast (
1.1
) as a Hamiltonian equation

\rm{i}\dot{q}_{\bm{j}}=2\frac{\partial H}{\partial\bar{q}_{\bm{j}}},

where

(2.1)

\displaystyle H(q,\bar{q})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{\bm{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}V_{\bm{j}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\alpha})\left|q_{\bm{j}}\right|^{2}+\epsilon_{1}\sum_{\bm{j},\bm{j}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\atop\left|\bm{j}-\bm{j}^{\prime}\right|=1}q_{\bm{j}}\bar{q}_{\bm{j}^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{2}\sum_{\bm{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left|q_{\bm{j}}\right|^{4}\right),

where
V_{\bm{j}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\alpha})
is defined by (
1.2
).
In order to prove the main result, we need to control the time derivative of the truncated sum of higher modes

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{|\bm{j}|>j_{0}}\left|q_{\bm{j}}(t)\right|^{2}.

In what follows, we will deal extensively with monomials in
q_{\bm{j}}
. Rewrite any monomial in the form

(2.3)

\displaystyle\prod_{\bm{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}q_{\bm{j}}^{n_{\bm{j}}}\bar{q}_{\bm{j}}^{n_{\bm{j}}^{\prime}}.

Let

\displaystyle\bm{n}=(\bm{n}_{\bm{j}},\bm{n}_{\bm{j}}^{\prime})_{\bm{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}.

We define

\displaystyle{\rm supp}\ \bm{n}

\displaystyle=\{\bm{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\ \bm{n}_{\bm{j}}\neq 0\ \mbox{or}\ \bm{n}_{\bm{j}}^{\prime}\neq 0\},

\displaystyle\Delta(\bm{n})

\displaystyle=\sup_{\bm{j},\bm{j}^{\prime}\in{\rm supp}\ \bm{n}}|\bm{j}-\bm{j}^{\prime}|,

\displaystyle|\bm{n}|_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\bm{n}_{\bm{j}}+\bm{n}_{\bm{j}}^{\prime}).

If
\bm{n}_{\bm{j}}=\bm{n}_{\bm{j}}^{\prime}
for all
\bm{j}\in\mbox{supp}\ \bm{n}
, then the monomial (
2.3
) is called resonant. Otherwise it is non-resonant. Note that non-resonant monomials contribute to the truncated sum in (
2.2
); while resonant ones do not. We define the (resonant) set as

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=\left\{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}:\ \bm{n}_{\bm{j}}=\bm{n}_{\bm{j}}^{\prime}\ {\rm for}\ \forall\ \bm{j}\right\}.

Given a large
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
N\ll j_{0}
, let

\displaystyle A(j_{0},N):=\left\{\bm{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\left|\left|\bm{j}\right|-j_{0}\right|\leq N\right\}.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a Hamiltonian

\displaystyle W(q,\bar{q})=\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}W(\bm{n})\prod_{{\rm supp}\ \bm{n}}q_{\bm{j}}^{\bm{n}_{\bm{j}}}\bar{q}_{\bm{j}}^{\bm{n}_{{\bm{j}}}^{\prime}},

for
j_{0},N\in\mathbb{N}
and
r>2
, we define

(2.5)

\displaystyle\left\|W\right\|_{j_{0},N,r}=\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\atop{{\rm supp}}\ \bm{n}\cap A(j_{0},N)\neq\emptyset}{\left|W(\bm{n})\right|\cdot|\bm{n}|_{1}\cdot r^{\Delta(\bm{n})+|\bm{n}|_{1}-1}}.

Remark 2.1
.

It is easy to see that

\left\|W\right\|_{j_{0},N_{1},r_{1}}\leq\left\|W\right\|_{j_{0},N_{2},r_{2}},

if

N_{1}\leq N_{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad r_{1}\leq r_{2}.

Definition 2.2
.

The Poisson bracket of
W
and
U
is defined as

\{W,U\}:={\rm{i}}\sum_{\bm{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}\left(\frac{\partial W}{\partial q_{\bm{j}}}\cdot\frac{\partial U}{\partial\bar{q}_{\bm{j}}}-\frac{\partial W}{\partial\bar{q}_{\bm{j}}}\cdot\frac{\partial U}{\partial q_{\bm{j}}}\right).

We have the following key estimate.

Proposition 2.3

(
Poisson Bracket
)
.

For
j_{0},N\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\displaystyle W(q,\bar{q})=\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\atop{\rm supp}\ \bm{n}\subset A(j_{0},N)}W(\bm{n})\prod_{{\rm supp}\ \bm{n}}q_{\bm{j}}^{\bm{n}_{\bm{j}}}\bar{q}_{\bm{j}}^{\bm{n}_{\bm{j}}^{\prime}},

and

U(q,\bar{q})=\sum_{\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}U(\bm{m})\prod_{{\rm supp}\ \bm{m}}q_{\bm{j}}^{\bm{m}_{\bm{j}}}\bar{q}_{\bm{j}}^{\bm{m}_{\bm{j}}^{\prime}}.

Then for any
0<\sigma<r/2
, we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle\left\|\left\{W,U\right\}\right\|_{j_{0},N,r-\sigma}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma}\left\|W\right\|_{j_{0},N,r}\cdot\left\|U\right\|_{j_{0},N,r}.

Proof.

First of all, we write

\displaystyle\{W,U\}=\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}\{W,U\}(\bm{l})\prod_{{\rm supp}\ \bm{l}}q_{\bm{j}}^{\bm{l}_{\bm{j}}}\bar{q}_{\bm{j}}^{\bm{l}_{\bm{j}}^{\prime}}.

Then one has

(2.7)

\displaystyle\{W,U\}(\bm{l})={\rm{i}}\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left(\sum_{\bm{n},\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}^{*}W(\bm{n})U(\bm{m})\left(\bm{n}_{\bm{k}}\bm{m}^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}-\bm{n}_{\bm{k}}^{\prime}\bm{m}_{\bm{k}}\right)\right)

and the sum
\sum\limits_{\bm{n},\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}^{*}
is taken as

\displaystyle\bm{l}_{\bm{j}}=\bm{n}_{\bm{j}}+\bm{m}_{\bm{j}}-1,\quad\bm{l}^{\prime}_{\bm{j}}=\bm{n}^{\prime}_{\bm{j}}+\bm{m}^{\prime}_{\bm{j}}-1\ {\rm for}\ \bm{j}=\bm{k},

\displaystyle\bm{l}_{\bm{j}}=\bm{n}_{\bm{j}}+\bm{m}_{\bm{j}},\quad\bm{l}^{\prime}_{\bm{j}}=\bm{n}^{\prime}_{\bm{j}}+\bm{m}^{\prime}_{\bm{j}}\ {\rm for}\ \bm{j}\neq\bm{k}.

Secondly, let

\displaystyle\widetilde{U}=\sum_{\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\atop{\rm supp}\ \bm{m}\cap A(j_{0},N)=\emptyset}U(\bm{m})\prod_{{\rm supp}\ \bm{m}}q_{\bm{j}}^{\bm{m}_{\bm{j}}}\bar{q}_{\bm{j}}^{\bm{m}^{\prime}_{\bm{j}}},

then one has
\left\{W,\widetilde{U}\right\}=0.

Hence, we can always assume that

(2.8)

\displaystyle U=\sum_{\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\atop{\rm supp}\ \bm{m}\cap A(j_{0},N)\neq\emptyset}U(\bm{m})\prod_{{\rm supp}\ \bm{m}}q_{\bm{j}}^{\bm{m}_{\bm{j}}}\bar{q}_{\bm{j}}^{\bm{m}^{\prime}_{\bm{j}}}.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
W
and
U
are homogeneous polynomials with degrees
n^{*}
and
m^{*}
respectively, i.e.,

\displaystyle W(q,\bar{q})=\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}},\left|\bm{n}\right|_{1}=n^{*}\atop{\rm supp}\ \bm{n}\subset A(j_{0},N)}W(\bm{n})\prod_{{\rm supp}\ \bm{n}}q_{\bm{j}}^{\bm{n}_{\bm{j}}}\bar{q}_{\bm{j}}^{\bm{n}_{\bm{j}}^{\prime}},

and

\displaystyle U(q,\bar{q})=\sum_{\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}},\left|\bm{m}\right|_{1}=m^{*}\atop{\rm supp}\ \bm{m}\cap A(j_{0},N)\neq\emptyset}U(\bm{m})\prod_{{\rm supp}\ \bm{m}}q_{\bm{j}}^{\bm{m}_{\bm{j}}}\bar{q}_{\bm{j}}^{\bm{m}_{\bm{j}}^{\prime}}.

Since
r>2
and
0<\sigma<r/2
, one has

(2.9)

\displaystyle 1<r-\sigma<r.

In view of (
2.9
) and

\displaystyle\Delta(\bm{l})\leq\Delta(\bm{n})+\Delta(\bm{m}),

one has

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}\left|\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{\bm{n},\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}^{*}W(\bm{n})U(\bm{m})\left(\bm{n}_{\bm{k}}\bm{m}^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}-\bm{n}_{\bm{k}}^{\prime}\bm{m}_{\bm{k}}\right)\right|\left(r-\sigma\right)^{\Delta(\bm{l})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{n},\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}\left|W(\bm{n})\right|\left|U(\bm{m})\right|\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left(\bm{n}_{\bm{k}}\bm{m}^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}+\bm{n}_{\bm{k}}^{\prime}\bm{m}_{\bm{k}}\right)\left(r-\sigma\right)^{\Delta(\bm{n})+\Delta(\bm{m})}

(2.10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}|W(\bm{n})|\cdot|\bm{n}|_{1}\cdot r^{\Delta(\bm{n})}\right)\left(\sum_{\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}|U(\bm{m})|\cdot|\bm{m}|_{1}\cdot r^{\Delta(\bm{m})}\right).

In view of (
2.7
), (
2.10
) and using

|\bm{l}|_{1}=|\bm{n}|_{1}+|\bm{m}|_{1}-2,
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left\{W,U\right\}\right\|_{j_{0},N,r-\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(|\bm{n}|_{1}+|\bm{m}|_{1}-2\right)(r-\sigma)^{|\bm{n}|_{1}+|\bm{m}|_{1}-3}

\displaystyle\times\left(\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}|W(\bm{n})|\cdot|\bm{n}|_{1}\cdot r^{\Delta(\bm{n})}\right)\left(\sum_{\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}|U(\bm{m})|\cdot|\bm{m}|_{1}\cdot r^{\Delta(\bm{m})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma}\left\|W\right\|_{j_{0},N,r}\cdot\left\|U\right\|_{j_{0},N,r},

which finishes the proof of (
2.6
), and where the last inequality is based on the following inequality:

\displaystyle(|\bm{n}|_{1}+|\bm{m}|_{1}-2)(r-\sigma)^{|\bm{n}|_{1}+|\bm{m}|_{1}-3}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma}r^{|\bm{n}|_{1}+|\bm{m}|_{1}-2}.

∎

Furthermore, one has

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
W
and
U
be given by Proposition
2.3
. Assume further that

(2.11)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{e}{\sigma}\right)\left\|W\right\|_{j_{0},N,r}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Then

\displaystyle\left\|U\circ X_{W}^{1}\right\|_{j_{0},N,r-\sigma}\leq 2\left\|U\right\|_{j_{0},N,r},

where
X_{W}^{1}
is the time-
1
map generated by the flow of
W
.

In general, we have

(2.12)

\displaystyle\left\|U\circ X_{W}^{1}-U\right\|_{j_{0},N,r-\sigma}\leq\frac{e}{\sigma}\cdot\left\|W\right\|_{j_{0},N,r}\cdot\left\|U\right\|_{j_{0},N,r},

and

(2.13)

\displaystyle\left\|U\circ X_{W}^{1}-U-\{U,W\}\right\|_{j_{0},N,r-\sigma}\leq\left(\frac{e}{\sigma}\right)^{2}\left\|W\right\|_{j_{0},N,r}^{2}\cdot\left\|U\right\|_{j_{0},N,r}.

3.
Analysis of the Symplectic Transformations

We now construct the symplectic transformation
\Gamma
by a finite step induction.

At the first step, i.e.,
s=1
(in view of (
2.1
)),

(3.1)

\displaystyle H_{1}=H=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{\bm{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}V_{\bm{j}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\alpha})|q_{\bm{j}}|^{2}+\epsilon_{1}\sum_{\bm{i},\bm{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\atop|\bm{i}-\bm{j}|_{1}=1}q_{\bm{i}}\bar{q}_{\bm{j}}+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{2}\sum_{\bm{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|q_{\bm{j}}|^{4}\right).

Then (
3.1
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=D+Z_{1}+R_{1},

where

D=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\bm{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}V_{\bm{j}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\alpha})|q_{\bm{j}}|^{2},

Z_{1}=\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{4}\sum_{\bm{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|q_{\bm{j}}|^{4},

and

R_{1}=\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{2}\sum_{\bm{i},\bm{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\atop|\bm{i}-\bm{j}|=1}q_{\bm{i}}\bar{q}_{\bm{j}}.

Let
M
be given as in Theorem
1.1
and fix any
r>2
. From (
2.5
), we have that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\left\|H_{1}-D\right\|_{j_{0},M^{2},r}\leq\epsilon^{0.99},

using
\epsilon=\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}
small enough.

3.1.
One step of Birkhoff normal form

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
V=(V_{\bm{j}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\alpha}))_{\bm{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
satisfy the
(\gamma,L,M,j_{0})
-nonresonant conditions (
1.3
).
Then there exists a change of variables
\Gamma_{1}:=X_{F_{1}}^{1}
such that

\displaystyle H_{2}

\displaystyle=H_{1}\circ X_{F_{1}}^{1}=D+Z_{2}+R_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\bm{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}V_{\bm{j}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\alpha})|q_{\bm{j}}|^{2}+\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\atop\bm{n}\in\mathcal{N}}Z_{2}(\bm{n})\prod_{{\rm supp}\ \bm{n}}\left|q_{\bm{j}}^{\bm{n}_{\bm{j}}}\right|^{2}+\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}R_{2}(\bm{n})\prod_{{\rm supp}\ \bm{n}}q_{\bm{j}}^{\bm{n}_{\bm{j}}}\bar{q}_{\bm{j}}^{\bm{n}_{\bm{j}}^{\prime}}.

Moreover, one has

(3.3)

\displaystyle\left\|F_{1}\right\|_{j_{0},M^{2},r}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{0.95},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\left\|Z_{2}\right\|_{j_{0},M^{2},r-\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{0.9}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{1}2^{-i}\right),

(3.5)

\displaystyle\left\|R_{2}\right\|_{j_{0},M^{2},r-\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{0.9}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{1}2^{-i}\right),

and

(3.6)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{R}_{2}\right\|_{j_{0},M^{2},r-\sigma}\leq\epsilon^{1.9},

where

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{2}=\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}R_{2}(\bm{n})\prod_{{\rm supp}\ \bm{n}\cap A(j_{0},M^{2}-40)\neq\emptyset}q_{\bm{j}}^{\bm{n}_{\bm{j}}}\bar{q}_{\bm{j}}^{\bm{n}_{\bm{j}}^{\prime}}.

Furthermore, for any
A\geq 3
the following estimate holds

(3.8)

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{\Delta(\bm{n})+|\bm{n}|=A}\left(|Z_{2}(\bm{n})|+|R_{2}(\bm{n})|\right)\prod_{{\rm supp}\ \bm{n}}q_{\bm{j}}^{\bm{n}_{\bm{j}}}\bar{q}_{\bm{j}}^{\bm{n}_{\bm{j}}^{\prime}}\right\|_{j_{0},M^{2},r-\sigma}\leq\epsilon^{1+0.9(A-3)}.

Proof.

By the Birkhoff normal form theory,
F_{1}
satisfies the homological equation

(3.9)

\displaystyle L_{V}F_{1}=\mathcal{R}_{1},

where the
Lie derivative
operator is defined by

R\times R
real matrix. Direct computations show

\displaystyle|\det W|=A_{1}\cdot A_{2}\cdot A_{3},

where

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{\bm{j},\bm{\ell}}|\cos 2\pi{\bm{\ell}}\cdot(\bm{\theta}+\bm{j}\bm{\alpha})|,

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{\bm{j},\bm{\ell}}(2\pi)^{2}((\bm{\ell}\bm{j})\cdot\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}+\bm{\ell}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\xi}})^{2},

and

\displaystyle A_{3}

\displaystyle=\prod_{(\bm{j},\bm{\ell})\neq(\bm{j}^{\prime},\bm{\ell}^{\prime})}(2\pi)^{2}\left((\bm{\ell}\bm{j})\cdot\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}+\bm{\ell}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\xi}})^{2}-((\bm{\ell}^{\prime}\bm{j}^{\prime})\cdot\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}+\bm{\ell}^{\prime}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\xi}})^{2}\right|.

If we choose a Diophantine vector
\bm{\xi}
, then we have

(4.4)

A_{2}\cdot A_{3}\geq\left(\frac{\gamma}{\left(2LM^{2}j_{0}\right)^{2(d+1)}}\right)^{10L^{3}M^{3}},

by using that
\Gamma_{L}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
satisfies Properties (a) and (b) (see (
1.2
) for the details).

We now estimate the lower bound of
A_{1}
. Firstly note that there are at least
R
terms in the product
\prod_{\bm{j},\bm{\ell}}
and
R\leq M(2L+1)
. Hence it suffices to estimate

|\cos 2\pi{\bm{\ell}}\cdot(\bm{\theta}+\bm{j}\bm{\alpha})|

for fixed
\bm{j}\in{\rm{supp}}\ {k},\ \bm{\ell}\in\Gamma_{L}
. Let
\left\|x\right\|_{{\mathbb{T}}/2}={\rm dist(x,\mathbb{Z}/2)}.

Using

4\left\|x-\frac{1}{4}\right\|_{{\mathbb{T}}/2}\leq\left|\cos 2\pi x\right|\leq 2\pi\left\|x-\frac{1}{4}\right\|_{{\mathbb{T}}/2},

there exists a subset
\Pi_{\bm{j},\bm{l}}
(the union of intervals) satisfying

\displaystyle{\rm meas}(\Pi_{\bm{j},\bm{l}})\leq\frac{\gamma}{\left(2LM^{2}j_{0}\right)^{2(d+1)}},

such that for any
(\bm{\theta},\bm{\alpha})\in[0,1]^{2d}\setminus\Pi_{\bm{j},\bm{l}},
one has

|\cos 2\pi{\bm{\ell}}\cdot(\bm{\theta}+\bm{j}\bm{\alpha})|\geq\frac{\gamma}{\left(2LM^{2}j_{0}\right)^{2(d+1)}}.

Hence for each
(\bm{\theta},\bm{\alpha})\in[0,1]^{2d}\setminus\bigcup_{\bm{j},\bm{l}}\Pi_{\bm{j},\bm{l}},
one has

(4.5)

\left|\det W\right|\geq\left(\frac{\gamma}{\left(2LM^{2}j_{0}\right)^{2(d+1)}}\right)^{20L^{3}M^{3}}.

By (
4.5
) and using Lemmas
5.1
and
5.3
in the Appendix, yields

\mbox{\rm meas}(\mathfrak{R}(\bm{k}))\leq\left(\frac{\gamma}{\left(2LM^{2}j_{0}\right)^{2(d+1)}}\right)^{10LM},

and further

\mbox{\rm meas}(\mathfrak{R}(\gamma,L,M,j_{0}))\leq\gamma,

which finishes the proof of (
4.3
).
∎

Lemma 5.2

(
[
KM98
]
)
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an interval of finite length (i.e.,
0<|I|<\infty
) and
k\geq 1.
If

satisfies

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in I}\left|\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}f(x)\right|\geq A>0,

\gamma>0,

\displaystyle{\rm meas}(\{x\in I:\ |f(x)|\leq\gamma\})\leq\zeta_{k}\left(\frac{\gamma}{A}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}},

where

\zeta_{k}=k(k+1)\left((k+1)!\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}.

Lemma 5.3

(
[
SW23b
]
)
.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
I=I_{\bm{a},\bm{b}}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}[a_{i},b_{i}]\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Assume that the function
f\in C^{k+1}(I;\mathbb{R})
satisfies for some

\displaystyle\inf_{\bm{x}\in I}\sup_{1\leq l\leq k}\left|{d_{\bm{\beta}}^{l}}f(\bm{x})\right|\geq A,

where

\displaystyle d_{\bm{\beta}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\beta_{i}{\partial}_{i},\ \partial_{i}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}.

with
{\bm{\beta}}=(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}

and

{d_{\bm{\beta}}^{l}}f(\bm{x})=d_{\bm{\beta}}(d_{\bm{\beta}}^{l-1}f(\bm{x}))

for

.
Let

\displaystyle\|f\|_{k+1}=\sup_{\bm{x}\in I}\sup_{1\leq|\bm{\gamma}|\leq k+1}\left|{\partial^{\bm{\gamma}}}f(\bm{x})\right|<\infty

\displaystyle\ \ \ {\rm meas}(\{\bm{x}\in I:\ |f(\bm{x})|\leq\varepsilon\})

\bm{\beta},k,d

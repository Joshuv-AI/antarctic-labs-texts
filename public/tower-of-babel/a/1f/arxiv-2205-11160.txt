Quantum state tomography of qudits via Hong-Ou-Mandel interference
Yoshiaki Tsujimoto, Rikizo Ikuta, Kentaro Wakui, Toshiki Kobayashi, Mikio Fujiwara

Abstract
We propose a method to perform the quantum state tomography (QST) of an n \mathit{n} -partite qudit state embedded in single photons using the Hong-Ou-Mandel (HOM) interference between the target state and probe state. This method requires only passive beam splitters for the HOM interference and removes all active optical devices in the target modes to control the measurement bases needed in conventional QST. Hence, it is applicable to various degree of freedom of the target state without altering the measurement setup. Moreover, a faithful estimation is realized even with classical probe light such as laser and thermal light. As a proof-of-principle, we performed the experimental demonstration using a polarization qubit. Regardless of the photon statistics of the probe light, the estimated results of state reconstruction are as accurate as those verified by conventional QST.



Introduction.-
The Hong-Ou-Mandel (HOM) interference is a bunching phenomenon based on the bosonic nature of photons
Hong1987
.
When two photons that are perfectly indistinguishable from each other are mixed using a half beam splitter (HBS), they always bunch and exhibit the absence of coincidence
PhysRevLett.100.133601
. This feature has been used in a variety of quantum information processing applications, including quantum computation
Knill2001
;
Kaltenbaek2010
;
Vigliar2021
and
quantum communication
Halder2007
;
Wang2015
;
Chen2017
;
RevModPhys.83.33
;
Yu2020
.
The decrease in coincidence reflects the degree of indistinguishability,
i.e., it provides information regarding the probability of overlap of the input photons. Thus, HOM interference enables the estimation of the unknown quantum state of a target photon under test using a probe photon with a known quantum state. Although this concept has been applied for estimating photon spectra
PhysRevLett.99.123601
;
Thiel:20
, spatial phase-amplitude structures
Chrapkiewicz2016
, and photon number distributions
PhysRevA.79.020101
;
PhysRevLett.113.070403
;
Tiedau_2018
,
there are no reported studies on the reconstruction of the complete density operators of photonic qubits.

In this Letter, we propose and demonstrate the quantum state tomography (QST) of photonic qubits based on HOM interference, which we refer to as HOM-QST. This concept is illustrated in Fig.
1
(a).
While the conventional QST
James2001

is performed by applying a set of projective measurements to the target photon, HOM-QST is performed by interference of the target photon with a set of probe photons.
HOM-QST offers the
following outstanding advantages over conventional QST: (i) Only passive optical HBSs are added to the photonic circuits, and there is no need for active control of the target photons under test. This feature enables a low-loss and compact circuit design and is hence ideal for integrated photonic quantum circuits
Najafi2015
;
Schuck2016
;
Gaggero:19
;
Gyger2021
, as shown in Fig.
1
(b). (ii) This method can be used to estimate the states of various degrees of freedom, such as polarization, time, frequency, and orbital angular momentum, without changing the measurement setup for the target photon. This is because the HBS allows for wide operation over these degrees of freedom
Harnchaiwat:20
;
Lingaraju:19
;
Nagali2009
.
To compensate for the above advantages, HOM-QST needs preparation of the probe light; nonetheless, HOM-QST has the following useful feature:
(iii) Except for the degree of freedom of interest for QST, there is no need to calibrate the probe photon information, such as the average photon number, photon statistics, and mode mismatch with the target photon. This feature mitigates the measurement cost of the proposed HOM-QST significantly.

Theory.-
We explain the HOM-QST of a
d
-dimensional qudit
in the Hilbert space spanned by
\{|m\rangle\}_{m=1,\ldots,d}

embedded in a single photon.
The polarization qubit corresponds to the case of
d=2
.
As shown in Fig.
1
(a), we consider the case wherein the target light under test is injected into the HBS from input port 1, and the probe light is injected from input port 2. The typical HOM interferogram (HOM dip) based on the two-fold coincidence probabilities between detectors
{\rm D}_{1}
and
{\rm D}_{2}
is shown in the inset graph.
The depth of the HOM dip reflects the degree of the bosonic bunching effect due to the indistinguishability of the input photons.
Here, we consider the HOM experiment with the probe light in state
k
which is a
superposition of the basis states. The depth of the dip is defined by

\Delta_{k}:=P_{\infty}-P_{k}
, where
P_{\infty}
and
P_{k}
are the two-fold coincidence probabilities with infinite and zero time delays of the input photons, respectively.

We assume that the detection efficiencies of the detectors are not dependent on
m
and
are significantly lower than 1,
such that the detection probabilities are proportional to the photon numbers
in the detected modes.
When we measure the photons without resolving the state
m
,
the coincidence probability,
P
, between
{\rm D}_{1}
and
{\rm D}_{2}
in Fig.
1
(a) is given by

P=\eta_{1}\eta_{2}\left\langle:\sum_{m}N_{1,m}^{\rm out}\sum_{m^{\prime}}N_{2,m^{\prime}}^{\rm out}:\right\rangle,

(1)

using the normal order representation, where
\hat{N}_{i,m}^{\rm out}
is the number operator of the photon in state
m
at the output port
i
of the HBS and

\eta_{i}
is a detection efficiency of
{\rm D}_{i}
.

When there is no mode mismatch between the target and probe lights,
the transformation of the HBS is given by

N_{1,m}^{\rm out}\rightarrow(a_{1,m}^{\dagger}+a_{2,m}^{\dagger})(a_{1,m}+a_{2,m})/2

and

N_{2,m^{\prime}}^{\rm out}\rightarrow(a_{1,m^{\prime}}^{\dagger}-a_{2,m^{\prime}}^{\dagger})(a_{1,m^{\prime}}-a_{2,m^{\prime}})/2
,
where
a_{i,m}
is the annihilation operator of the photon in state
m
at input port
i
of the HBS.
Assuming that
the target and probe lights have no phase correlations
and are statistically independent

;
Tsujimoto2018
,
P_{k}
is calculated as follows:

\displaystyle P_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{12}}{4}\left(\left\langle:(N_{1}+N_{2,k})^{2}:\right\rangle-2\left\langle N_{1,k}\right\rangle\left\langle N_{2,k}\right\rangle\right)

(2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{12}}{4}\left(n_{s}^{2}g^{(2)}_{s}+n_{p}^{2}g^{(2)}_{p}+2n_{s}n_{p}(1-\rho_{k})\right),

(3)

where
\eta_{12}:=\eta_{1}\eta_{2}
,
N_{i,m}:=a_{i,m}^{\dagger}a_{i,m}
,
N_{i}:=\sum_{m}N_{i,m}
, and
\rho_{k}:=\left\langle N_{1,k}\right\rangle/\left\langle N_{1}\right\rangle
.
We also used
\left\langle:N_{1}N_{2}:\right\rangle=\left\langle N_{1}\right\rangle\left\langle N_{2}\right\rangle
,

\left\langle N_{1}\right\rangle=n_{s}
,
\left\langle N_{2}\right\rangle=\left\langle N_{2,k}\right\rangle=n_{p}
,

\left\langle:N_{1}^{2}:\right\rangle=n_{s}^{2}g^{(2)}_{s}
, and
\left\langle:N_{2}^{2}:\right\rangle=n_{p}^{2}g^{(2)}_{p}
,
where
n_{s(p)}
and
g^{(2)}_{s(p)}
are an average photon number and intensity correlation function of the target (probe) light.
Here,
\rho_{k}
is the probability of the
k
-portion of the target photon that is to be estimated.
Since
P_{\infty}
is equal to the first term in Eq. (
2
),

\Delta_{k}
is given by

\Delta_{k}=\frac{\eta_{12}}{2}\left\langle N_{1,k}\right\rangle\left\langle N_{2,k}\right\rangle=\frac{\eta_{12}n_{s}n_{p}}{2}\rho_{k}.

(4)

As the coefficient of
\rho_{k}
is independent of
k
,
the target state
\hat{\rho}
can be reconstructed from the
HOM experiments with various settings of
k
.
Furthermore, we note that Eq. (
4

assuming that the contribution of the dark counts is the same between
P_{0}
and
P_{\infty}
.
When the target light is a polarization qubit,
\rho_{k}
corresponds to the probability obtained from projective measurements, as follows:

\rho_{k}=\langle k|\hat{\rho}|k\rangle,

(5)

where
|k\rangle
is the
k
-polarized single-photon state. By measuring
\Delta_{k}
for four independent polarization configurations, namely horizontal (
H
), vertical (
V
), diagonal (
D
), and right-circular (
R
) polarizations, we can reconstruct the target state. The feature of HOM-QST is that no prior knowledge of the probe light is needed, such as
n_{p}
and
g^{(2)}_{p}
in
P_{\infty}
and
P_{k}
; moreover, there is no theoretical requirement on these values.

Next, we consider a more practical situation wherein there exists a non-zero mode mismatch between the target and probe photons even when both are in state
k
.
We describe the operators for the target and probe photons that are mode-matched to the target by the same notations as those used above.
Moreover, we introduce operators for the probe light that express perfectly distinguishable mode mismatches with the target photon
by adding overlines to the related operators.
Eq. (
1
) is expanded to include the mode mismatch as

P=\eta_{12}\left\langle:\sum_{m}(N_{1,m}^{\rm out}+\overline{N}_{1,m}^{\rm out})\sum_{m^{\prime}}(N_{2,m^{\prime}}^{\rm out}+\overline{N}_{2,m^{\prime}}^{\rm out}):\right\rangle.

In input modes,

\left\langle N_{1}\right\rangle=n_{s}
and
\left\langle\overline{N}_{1}\right\rangle=0
hold for the target photon, and
\left\langle N_{2,k}+\overline{N}_{2,k}\right\rangle=n_{p}
and

\left\langle:(N_{2,k}+\overline{N}_{2,k})^{2}:\right\rangle=n_{p}^{2}g^{(2)}_{p}
hold for the probe light.
By defining the mode overlap probability between the target and probe light photons as
M:=\left\langle N_{2,k}\right\rangle/n_{p}
,
the coincidence probability
P_{k}

is described as

\displaystyle P_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{12}}{4}\left(\left\langle:(N_{1}+N_{2,k}+\overline{N}_{2,k})^{2}:\right\rangle-2\left\langle N_{1,k}\right\rangle\left\langle N_{2,k}\right\rangle\right)

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{12}}{4}\left(n_{s}^{2}g^{(2)}_{s}+n_{p}^{2}g^{(2)}_{p}+2n_{s}n_{p}(1-M\rho_{k})\right).

Note that the case of
M=1
in Eq. (
7

P_{k}
in Eq. (
3
), and the case of
M=0
corresponds with

\displaystyle P_{\infty}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{12}}{4}\left\langle:(N_{1}+N_{2,k}+\overline{N}_{2,k})^{2}:\right\rangle

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{12}}{4}\left(n_{s}^{2}g^{(2)}_{s}+n_{p}^{2}g^{(2)}_{p}+2n_{s}n_{p}\right).

(9)

The depth
\Delta_{k}
of the HOM dip is described as

\displaystyle\Delta_{k}=\frac{\eta_{12}n_{s}n_{p}M}{2}\rho_{k}.

(10)

Since
M
is independent of

without estimating

regardless of

Experiment.-

We experimentally demonstrate HOM-QST for polarization qubits. We prepared three types of probe lights, namely the heralded-single-photon-state (HSPS) (
g^{(2)}_{p}\ll 1
), thermal-state (
g^{(2)}_{p}\sim 2
), and coherent-state (
g^{(2)}_{p}\sim 1
) probes.
The sketch of the experimental setup is shown in Fig.
2
. A fiber-based mode-locked laser emitting
1550\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}

250\text{\,}\mathrm{M}\mathrm{H}\mathrm{z}

This setup consists of an electro-optical comb generator

Tsujimoto2021

10\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{m}
-long periodically poled lithium niobate waveguide (PPLN/W).
Then, the SHG light at
775\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}
is divided into two spatial modes using
a half waveplate (HWP) and polarization beam splitter (PBS) before being used to pump the subsequent PPLN/Ws for spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC).
One pump beam is used to prepare the target photon; this beam is coupled with a
34\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{m}
long PPLN/W (PPLN/W 1)
and generates a

The

The HSP at

The other pump at

Conclusion.-
In conclusion,
we proposed the QST of photonic qudits via HOM interference and demonstrated it successfully using polarization qubits as the proof-of-principle.
The proposed method shifts the complexity of the measurement system
to the probe light preparation system, which
then enables us to construct the optical circuit with only passive elements.
We theoretically showed that
the HOM-QST does not need information regarding the probe light,
such as intensity and photon statistics,
and allows for experimental imperfections,
such as mode mismatch between the photons
and use of an unbalanced BS.
We demonstrated the HOM-QST using the HSPS, thermal-state, and coherent-state probes. The estimated fidelities were of a similar order as those obtained
via conventional QST, which demonstrates the
precise estimation of the density operator regardless of
the intensity and photon statistics of the probe light.
In the demonstration, we applied HOM-QST to the polarization qubits.
However, our method exhibits its power for high-dimensional systems in which the measurement setup tends to be complex.
For example, in the case of frequency qudits, a line-by-line waveform shaping technique for frequency combs
Cundiff2010
can be used to prepare the probe light without requiring any nonlinear optical interactions in a measurement setup, e.g. as shown in Refs.
Kobayashi2016
;
Gil-Lopez:21
;
Kues2019
.
Our method serves as a new alternative to perform QST with a passive measurement setup and is expected to be applicable to integrated quantum photonics.

2i
, respectively.
We define
the set of serial numbers of the HOM interferometers as
X_{n}:=\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
We also define the power set of
X_{n}
as
{\mathfrak{P}}(X_{n})

to separate the interferometers into two groups: interferometers in
S\in{\mathfrak{P}}(X_{n})
satisfy the condition of zero time delay between the target and probe light, and those in
X_{n}\backslash S
satisfy the condition of large time delays.
We denote the list of probe states affecting the coincidence probabilities as
\kappa(S):=k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}
,
where

k_{i}
is the probe state of the
i
-th interferometer for
i\in S
,
and
k_{i}=\emptyset
for
i\in X_{n}\backslash S
.
Based on these definitions, the 2
n
-fold coincidence probability
P_{\kappa(S)}^{(n)}

is given by extending Eq. (
) in the main text as

\displaystyle P_{\kappa(S)}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta}{4^{n}}\left\langle:\left(\prod_{i\in X_{n}\backslash S}(N_{2i-1}+N_{2i,k_{i}}+\overline{N}_{2i,k_{i}})^{2}\right)\left(\prod_{j\in S}(N_{2j-1}+N_{2j,k_{j}}+\overline{N}_{2j,k_{j}})^{2}-2N_{2j-1,k_{j}}N_{2j,k_{j}}\right):\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{S^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{P}}(S)}(-1)^{|S^{\prime}|}\Delta_{\kappa(S^{\prime})}^{(n)},

(S1)

where
\eta
is the total detection efficiency from
2n
photon detectors, and

\displaystyle\Delta_{\kappa(S^{\prime})}^{(n)}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\eta}{4^{n}}\left\langle:\left(\prod_{i\in X_{n}\backslash S^{\prime}}(N_{2i-1}+N_{2i,k_{i}}+\overline{N}_{2i,k_{i}})^{2}\right)\left(2^{|S^{\prime}|}\prod_{j\in S^{\prime}}N_{2j-1,k_{j}}N_{2j,k_{j}}\right):\right\rangle,

(S2)

with
\prod_{i(j)\in\varnothing}\cdots:=1
.

P_{\kappa(\varnothing)}^{(n)}=\Delta_{\kappa(\varnothing)}^{(n)}
is
the probability obtained when the photons in all the interferometers are distinguishable corresponding to
P_{\infty}
in Eq. (
8
) in the main text for
n=1
.
We note that

\Delta_{\kappa(X_{n})}^{(n)}
, included in
the observable probability
P_{\kappa(X_{n})}^{(n)}
,
contains information pertaining to

\displaystyle\rho_{\kappa(X_{n})}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\langle\prod_{j\in X_{n}}N_{2j-1,k_{j}}\right\rangle/n_{s,n}

(S3)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\langle k_{1}\cdots k_{n}|\hat{\rho}|k_{1}\cdots k_{n}\rangle,

(S4)

which is to be determined,
where
n_{s,n}:=\left\langle\prod_{j\in X_{n}}N_{2j-1}\right\rangle

is the
n
-th order cross-correlation of the target state.

\Delta_{\kappa(X_{n})}^{(n)}
is given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{\kappa(X_{n})}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\eta}{4^{n}}\left\langle:\left(2^{|X_{n}|}\prod_{j\in X_{n}}N_{2j-1,k_{j}}N_{2j,k_{j}}\right):\right\rangle

(S5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\eta}{2^{n}}\left\langle\prod_{j\in X_{n}}N_{2j-1,k_{j}}\right\rangle\left\langle\prod_{j\in X_{n}}N_{2j,k_{j}}\right\rangle.

(S6)

Similar to the expansion of Eq. (
4
) to Eq. (
11
) in the main text for
n=1
,
by considering the mode mismatch effects, state dependency of the detection efficiency, and
BS imbalance, Eq. (
S6
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\Delta_{\kappa(X_{n})}^{(n)}=2^{n}T^{n}R^{n}\eta_{X_{n}}n_{s,n}n_{p,n}M_{X_{n}}\rho_{\kappa(X_{n})},

(S7)

where
n_{p,n}:=\left\langle\prod_{j\in X_{n}}(N_{2j,k_{j}}+\overline{N}_{2j,k_{j}})\right\rangle=g_{p,X_{n}}^{(n)}\prod_{j\in X_{n}}\left\langle N_{2j,k_{i}}+\overline{N}_{2j,k_{j}}\right\rangle

is the
n
-th order cross-correlation of the probe state,

\eta_{X_{n}}:=\prod_{j\in X_{n}}\eta_{k_{j}}\eta_{k_{j}}
is
the total state-dependent detection efficiency of the
2n
photon detectors,

M_{X_{n}}:=\prod_{j\in X_{n}}M_{j}
is the product of mode-matching
between the target and probe light in the
j
-th interferometer with

M_{j}:=\left\langle N_{2j,k_{j}}\right\rangle/\left\langle N_{2j,k_{j}}+\overline{N}_{2j,k_{j}}\right\rangle
,
and
g_{p,X_{n}}^{(n)}=n_{p,n}/\prod_{j\in X_{n}}\left\langle N_{2j,k_{j}}+\overline{N}_{2j,k_{j}}\right\rangle=\left\langle\prod_{j\in X_{n}}N_{2j,k_{j}}\right\rangle/\prod_{j\in X_{n}}\left\langle N_{2j,k_{j}}\right\rangle

is the
n
-th order cross-correlation function of the probe light.
The coefficient of
\rho_{\kappa(X_{n})}
in the equation does not depend on
\kappa(X_{n})
.
Thus, we can reconstruct
\hat{\rho}
from
(1+d^{2})^{n}
different experimental settings.

We present examples for the
n=1
and
n=2
cases, as follows.
From Eqs. (
S1
) and (
S2
),
for
n=1

P_{\kappa(X_{1})}^{(1)}=P_{k_{1}}^{(1)}=\Delta_{\kappa(\varnothing)}^{(1)}-\Delta_{k_{1}}^{(1)}

\Delta_{\kappa(\varnothing)}^{(1)}=\Delta_{\emptyset}^{(1)}=P_{\infty}

8

\Delta_{k_{1}}^{(1)}=\eta n_{s,1}n_{p,1}M_{1}\rho_{k_{1}}/2

For
n=2

P_{\kappa(X_{2})}^{(2)}=P_{k_{1},k_{2}}^{(2)}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle P_{k_{1},k_{2}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{\kappa(\varnothing)}^{(2)}-\Delta_{\kappa(\{1\})}^{(2)}-\Delta_{\kappa(\{2\})}^{(2)}+\Delta_{\kappa(\{1,2\})}^{(2)}

(S8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{\emptyset,\emptyset}^{(2)}+(P_{k_{1},\emptyset}^{(2)}-\Delta_{\emptyset,\emptyset}^{(2)})+(P_{\emptyset,k_{2}}^{(2)}-\Delta_{\emptyset,\emptyset}^{(2)})+\Delta_{k_{1},k_{2}}^{(2)}

(S9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{k_{1},\emptyset}^{(2)}+P_{\emptyset,k_{2}}^{(2)}-\Delta_{\emptyset,\emptyset}^{(2)}+4n_{s,2}n_{p,2}M_{1}M_{2}\rho_{k_{1},k_{2}}.

(S10)

\rho_{k_{1},k_{2}}

\Delta_{\emptyset,\emptyset}^{(2)}

and
P_{\emptyset,k_{2}}^{(2)}

We consider the HOM visibility between the
k

9
) and (

V_{\rm th}:=\frac{\Delta_{k}}{P_{\infty}}=M\frac{1}{1+\frac{\zeta g^{(2)}_{p}+1/\zeta g^{(2)}_{s}}{2}},

where
\zeta:=n_{p}/n_{s}
.
In Figs. 3(a), (c) and (d),

-polarized target photons and

-polarized probes. The experimental HOM visibilities defined by

,
0.311(29)
, and
0.575(16)

g^{(2)}_{s}=0.211(02)
, and

.

S11
),

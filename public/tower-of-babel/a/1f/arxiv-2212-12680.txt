One dimensional sharp discrete Hardy-Rellich inequalities
Xia Huang, Dong Ye

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish discrete Hardy-Rellich inequalities on ℕ \mathbb{N} with Δ ℓ 2 \Delta^{\frac{\ell}{2}} and optimal constants, for any ℓ ≥ 1 \ell\geq 1 . As far as we are aware, these sharp inequalities are new for ℓ ≥ 3 \ell\geq 3 . Our approach is to use weighted equalities to get some sharp Hardy inequalities using shifting weights, then to settle the higher order cases by iteration. We provide also a new Hardy-Leray type inequality on ℕ {\mathbb{N}} with the same constant as the continuous setting. Furthermore, the main ideas work also for general graphs or the ℓ p \ell^{p} setting.



2010 MSC: 26D10, 26D15, 35A23

1.
Introduction

In 1921, in a letter to Hardy, Landau
[
21
]
gave a proof of the following inequality with optimal constants: For any
p>1
and any non-negative real sequence
{\{a_{n}}\}_{n\geq 1}
, we have

(1.1)

\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{p}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}+...+a_{n}}{n}\right)^{p}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{p}.

Let
C_{c}({\mathbb{N}})
be the space of finitely supported real sequences indexed by the set of nonnegative integers
{\mathbb{N}}
, or equivalently, the space of finitely supported real functions on
{\mathbb{N}}
. With abuse of notations, we will write
u_{n}=u(n)=(u)_{n}
for any sequence
u
over
{\mathbb{N}}
. We denote also the discrete gradient over
{\mathbb{N}}
by
\nabla u_{n}:=(\nabla u)_{n}=u_{n}-u_{n-1}
for
n\geq 1
, then (
1.1
) is equivalent to

(1.2)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\nabla u_{n}|^{p}\geq\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{p}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|u_{n}|^{p}}{n^{p}},\quad\forall\;u\in C_{c}({\mathbb{N}}),\;u_{0}=0.

The most well-known case with
p=2
, was established by Hardy
[
13
]
when he proposed a simple proof for a Hilbert’s inequality.
Since one century, Hardy type inequalities have been enriched extensively and broadly, they play an important role in many branches of analysis and geometry. A huge literature exists, especially for the continuous setting, we refer to the classical or more recent books
[
2
,
9
,
22
,
23
,
25
]
for interested readers.

Although the history of Hardy inequalities found its origin somehow in the discrete setting, the discrete Hardy-Rellich inequalities are much less understood comparing to the continuous situation.
In 2018, Keller-Pinchover-Pogorzelski
[
17
,
18
]
proved the following improved discrete Hardy inequality of first order:

(1.3)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\geq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}w_{n}u_{n}^{2},\quad\forall\;u\in C_{c}({\mathbb{N}}),\;u_{0}=0

with
w_{n}=\frac{\Delta\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{n}}
, where
\Delta v_{n}=2v_{n}-v_{n+1}-v_{n-1}
denotes the discrete Laplacian over
{\mathbb{N}}
. More explicitly, for
n\geq 1
,

w_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\binom{4k}{2k}\frac{1}{2^{4k-1}(4k-1)n^{2k}}=\frac{1}{4n^{2}}+\frac{5}{64n^{4}}+\frac{21}{512n^{6}}+\ldots.

Inspired by
[
4
]
, they showed that the weights
(w_{n})
are critical in many aspects, in particular the inequality (
1.3
) cannot hold with any sequence
\gneq w_{n}
. See also
[
6
]
for similar improvement of (
1.2
) with general
p>1
.

A natural question is to handle the higher order cases, i.e. the Hardy-Rellich inequalities on
{\mathbb{N}}
with
\Delta^{\frac{\ell}{2}}
,
\ell\geq 2
. For odd integers
\ell\geq 1
, by
\Delta^{\frac{\ell}{2}}
we mean
\nabla\Delta^{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}
.

Recently, Gupta established in
[
11
]
the following Hardy-Rellich inequalities of general order over
C_{c}({\mathbb{N}})
: Let
\ell\geq 2
,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{2n\geq\ell}|\Delta^{\frac{\ell}{2}}u_{n}|^{2}\geq 2^{\ell-3}(\ell-1)!\sum_{n=\ell}^{\infty}\frac{u_{n}^{2}}{n^{2\ell}},\quad\forall\;u\in C_{c}({\mathbb{N}}),\;u_{k}=0,\;0\leq k\leq\ell-1.

In
[
11
, Theorem 2.5]
, the LHS of (
1.4
) was written as the sum on
n\geq 1
, we can see that under the assumptions
u_{k}=0
for
k\leq\ell-1
,
\Delta^{\frac{\ell}{2}}u_{n}=0
if
2n<\ell
.

However, the question of finding the best constants are widely open for
\ell\geq 2
. Very recently, the second order case was solved by Gerhat-Krejčiřík-Štampach
[
8
]
, they proved that for
\ell=2
, the constant in (
1.4
) can be raised to
\frac{9}{16}
, the sharp one expected for
\Delta
. More precisely, there holds

(1.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}|\Delta u_{n}|^{2}\geq\sum_{n\geq 2}\widetilde{w}_{n}u^{2}_{n},\quad\forall\;u\in C_{c}({\mathbb{N}}),\;u_{0}=u_{1}=0,

with

\widetilde{w}_{n}=6\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4^{k}-1}{4^{2k}}\frac{(4k)!}{(2k)!(2k+2)!}\frac{1}{n^{2k+2}}=\frac{9}{16n^{4}}+\frac{210}{256n^{6}}+\ldots

Their main idea is to use a factorization technique, but this approach seems to be difficultly adaptable for higher order situations.

Our main concern here is to provide the following Hardy-Rellich inequalities with optimal constants for any order
\ell\geq 2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\ell\geq 2
be integer. Given any
u\in C_{c}({\mathbb{N}})
with
u_{k}=0
for
0\leq k\leq\ell-1
, there holds

(1.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{2n\geq\ell}|\Delta^{\frac{\ell}{2}}u_{n}|^{2}\geq\Big[\frac{(2\ell)!}{4^{\ell}\ell!}\Big]^{2}\sum_{n=\ell}^{\infty}\frac{u_{n}^{2}}{n^{2\ell}}.

Notice that the constant in (
1.6
) coincides the sharp constant for Hardy-Rellich inequality in the continuum (see for instance
[
24
]
):

(1.7)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi^{(\ell)}(x)|^{2}dx\geq\Big[\frac{(2\ell)!}{4^{\ell}\ell!}\Big]^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{2}(x)}{x^{2\ell}}dx,\quad\forall\;\varphi\in C_{c}^{\ell}(0,\infty).

By establishing the link between discrete and continuous Hardy-Rellich inequalities, we will show the optimality of the constant in (
1.6
), see Proposition
4.1
below. We notice also that the assumptions
u_{k}=0
,
0\leq k\leq\ell-1
are crucially necessary to expect any Hardy-Rellich inequalities like (
1.6
), see Remark
4.3
below, this answers some question in
[
11
]
.

Furthermore, our approach can enable us a sequence of weights which are larger than that in (
1.6
), as in Keller-Pinchover-Pogorzelski or Gerhat-Krejčiřík-Štampach’s work with
\ell=1
and
2
respectively. In particular, for
\ell=2
, for any
u\in C_{c}({\mathbb{N}})
with
u_{0}=u_{1}=0
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}|\Delta u_{n}|^{2}\geq\sum_{n\geq 2}\left(\frac{9}{16n^{4}}+\frac{15}{16n^{5}}+\frac{213}{128n^{6}}+\cdot\cdot\cdot\right)u^{2}_{n}.

This improves asymptotically the weights given in
[
8
]
, see section 7.2.

We will make use of the following elementary observation: As
\nabla u_{n}=u_{n}-u_{n-1}
, the corresponding divergence operator in
C_{c}({\mathbb{N}})
is just
{\rm div}(u)_{n}=u_{n+1}-u_{n}
, in other words

{\rm div}=\tau_{1}\circ\nabla=\nabla\circ\tau_{1},

where
\tau_{k}
states for the shift operator
(\tau_{k}g)_{n}=g_{n+k}
for
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Readily
\Delta=-{\rm div}\circ\nabla
, so the Laplacian over
{\mathbb{N}}
is a combination of shift and gradient operators. Remark also that the three operators
\Delta
,
\nabla
and
{\rm div}
over
{\mathbb{N}}
commute with each other. Therefore, to get higher order Rellich inequalities, we need just to well understand the first order situation with suitable weights. Our main ideas are getting Hardy inequalities via equalities, combined with iteration and shift.

In fact, we establish the following first order Hardy inequalities with shifting weights.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(-\infty,0)
. Then

(1.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{\alpha}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\geq\frac{(\alpha-1)^{2}}{4}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n+1)^{\alpha-2}v_{n}^{2},\quad\forall\;v\in C_{c}({\mathbb{N}}),\;v_{0}=0,

or equivalently

(1.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n-1)^{\alpha}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\geq\frac{(\alpha-1)^{2}}{4}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n^{\alpha-2}u_{n}^{2},\quad\forall\;u\in C_{c}({\mathbb{N}}),\;u_{0}=u_{1}=0.

Our departure point is the following general equality, which can be called
weighted ground state transform
. Let
f_{n}>0
for
n\geq 1
and
f_{0}=0
, then for any weights
V=(V_{n})
and any
u\in C_{c}({\mathbb{N}})
with
u_{0}=0
, there holds

(1.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}V_{n}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm div}(V\nabla f)_{n}}{f_{n}}u^{2}_{n}=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}V_{n}\left|\sqrt{\frac{f_{n-1}}{f_{n}}}u_{n}-\sqrt{\frac{f_{n}}{f_{n-1}}}u_{n-1}\right|^{2}.

Indeed, let
n\geq 2
, direct calculation yields

\displaystyle\left|\sqrt{\frac{f_{n-1}}{f_{n}}}u_{n}-\sqrt{\frac{f_{n}}{f_{n-1}}}u_{n-1}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=(u_{n}-u_{n-1})^{2}-\frac{f_{n}-f_{n-1}}{f_{n}}u_{n}^{2}+\frac{f_{n}-f_{n-1}}{f_{n-1}}u_{n-1}^{2}

\displaystyle=|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}-\frac{\nabla f_{n}}{f_{n}}u_{n}^{2}+\frac{\nabla f_{n}}{f_{n-1}}u_{n-1}^{2}.

So we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}V_{n}\left|\sqrt{\frac{f_{n-1}}{f_{n}}}u_{n}-\sqrt{\frac{f_{n}}{f_{n-1}}}u_{n-1}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}V_{n}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}V_{n}\frac{\nabla f_{n}}{f_{n}}u_{n}^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}V_{n+1}\frac{\nabla f_{n+1}}{f_{n}}u_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}V_{n}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm div}(V\nabla f)_{n}}{f_{n}}u_{n}^{2}+V_{2}\frac{\nabla f_{2}}{f_{1}}u_{1}^{2}.

As
u_{0}=f_{0}=0
, then
\nabla u_{1}=u_{1}
and
{\rm div}(V_{1}\nabla f_{1})=V_{2}\nabla f_{2}-V_{1}\nabla f_{1},
this means that (
1.10
) holds.

The special case of (
1.10
) with
V_{n}\equiv 1
can be found in
[
20
,
19
]
. With
V_{n}\equiv 1
and
f_{n}=\sqrt{n}
for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, as the RHS of (
1.10
) is nonnegative, we get readily the Hardy inequality

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\geq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Delta\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{n}}u_{n}^{2},\quad\forall\;u\in C_{c}({\mathbb{N}}),u_{0}=0.

which is just the optimal estimate (
1.3
) found by Keller-Pinchover-Pogorzelski
[
18
]
.

As interesting application of (
1.10
), we obtain also the following sharp Hardy inequalities.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\alpha\geq 0
. Then

(1.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{\alpha}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\geq\frac{(\alpha-1)^{2}}{4}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{\alpha-2}u_{n}^{2},\quad\forall\;u\in C_{c}({\mathbb{N}}),\;u_{0}=0.

To study the Hardy-Rellich inequalities over
{\mathbb{N}}
, Gupta considered weighted Hardy inequalities by diverse methods. Firstly, based on the ground state transform, he showed in
[
10
]
that (
1.11
) is valid for
\alpha\in[0,1)\cup[5,\infty)
, and pointed out that this approach fails to work if
\alpha<0
or
\alpha\in(1,4)
, see
[
10
, Remark 1.1]
; then he proved (
1.11
) for
\alpha\in 2{\mathbb{N}}
by Fourier analysis in
[
11
]
. The estimate (
1.11
) with
\alpha\in[0,1)
follows also from
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
with
\kappa=2
and
c=2-\alpha
. Furthermore, when
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{3},1)
, Gupta gave an improved version by adding infinitely many positive lower order terms in RHS.

Here we present a unified approach to handle (
1.9
) and (
1.11
) for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
. It is worthy to mention that we do not need (
1.11
) to consider higher order Rellich inequalities (
1.6
).

Clearly, the estimate (
1.11
) is trivial with
\alpha=1
. In that case, we show a new Leray-type inequality over
{\mathbb{N}}
with an explicit constant. We can find similar result in
[
16
, Lemma 4.5]
with unknown constant.

Theorem 1.4
.

For any
u\in C_{c}({\mathbb{N}})
with
u_{0}=u_{1}=0
, there holds

(1.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{|u_{n}|^{2}}{n(\ln n)^{2}}.

In (
1.12
), we find again the sharp constant in the continuum, see
[
26
, Theorem 1]
.

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}x|\varphi^{\prime}(x)|^{2}dx\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{2}(x)}{x(\ln x)^{2}}dx,\quad\forall\;\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(1,\infty).

Indeed, our approach works for locally finite graphs. In section 2, we briefly describe some notions and provide some general higher order identities on locally finite graphs, see Theorems
2.3
-
2.4
. The key inequalities (
1.9
) and Theorem
1.1
will be established in section 3 and 4 respectively. The proof of (
1.11
) is given in section 5. Finally, we use similar ideas to consider the lattices
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
(
d\geq 2
) and the
\ell^{p}
setting (
p>1
).

2.
Weighted Hardy-Rellich equalities on graphs

Here we recall some standard notions for locally finite graphs
X
, i.e. each vertex
x\in X
has finite neighbors. A graph over a countable discrete set
X
is a symmetric function
b:X\times X\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
which vanishes at the diagonal.

We refer to the elements of
X
as vertices. We say that
x,y\in X
are adjacent or neighbors if
b(x,y)>0
, in which case we write
x\sim y
. Assume that
X
is equipped with the discrete topology and
b
is a graph over
X
. Moreover,
b
is said connected, if for every
x
and
y
in
X
there are
x_{0},...,x_{m}
in
X
such that
x_{0}=x
,
x_{m}=y
and
x_{i}\sim x_{i+1}
for
i=0,...,m-1
. Given a graph
b
over
X
, the associated Laplacian
\Delta
is defined as

\Delta u(x):=\sum_{y\sim x}b(x,y)\big[u(x)-u(y)\big].

We will use the formal inner product (when it makes sense) for
f,g\in C(X)
.

\langle f,g\rangle:=\sum_{x\in X}f(x)g(x).

Let
\nabla f(x,y):=f(x)-f(y)
be the discrete gradient defined on
X\times X
, and define

\langle\nabla f,\nabla g\rangle:=\sum_{x\in X}\langle\nabla f,\nabla g\rangle_{x}\quad\mbox{with }\;\langle\nabla f,\nabla g\rangle_{x}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\sim x}b(x,y)\big[f(x)-f(y)\big]\big[g(x)-g(y)\big].

Denote also

|\nabla f|^{2}(x):=\langle\nabla f,\nabla f\rangle_{x}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\sim x}b(x,y)\big[f(x)-f(y)\big]^{2}.

By direct calculation, we get some elementary equalities. Let
f\in C(X)
,
g\in C_{c}(X)
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\Delta f,g\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{x,y\in X}b(x,y)\big[f(x)-f(y)\big]g(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x,y\in X}b(x,y)\big[f(x)-f(y)\big]\big[g(x)-g(y)\big]+\sum_{x,y\in X}b(x,y)\big[f(x)-f(y)\big]g(y)

\displaystyle=2\langle\nabla f,\nabla g\rangle-\langle\Delta f,g\rangle.

Hence the following Green’s formula and Leibniz rule hold true: For any
f\in C(X)
,
g\in C_{c}(X)
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\langle\Delta f,g\rangle=\langle f,\Delta g\rangle=\langle\nabla f,\nabla g\rangle,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Delta(fg)(x)=\big[f\Delta g+g\Delta f\big](x)-2\langle\nabla f,\nabla g\rangle_{x}.

Consequently, for any
h\in C(X)
,
u\in C_{c}(X)
, we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\langle\Delta u,u\rangle=\sum_{x\in X}|\nabla u|^{2}(x)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\langle\Delta h,u^{2}\rangle=\langle h,\Delta(u^{2})\rangle=2\langle h,u\Delta u-|\nabla u|^{2}\rangle.

Let
f\in C(X)
be positive,
V\in C(X)
and
u\in C_{c}(X)
, we define

\displaystyle{\mathscr{R}}(V,f,u):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in X}b(x,y)V(x)\left(\sqrt{\frac{f(x)}{f(y)}}u(y)-\sqrt{\frac{f(y)}{f(x)}}u(x)\right)^{2}.

Based on above notations, there holds

\displaystyle{\mathscr{R}}(V,f,u)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in X}V(x)\left[2|\nabla u|^{2}(x)+\sum_{y\sim x}b(x,y)\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{f(y)}u^{2}(y)-\frac{\Delta f}{f}(x)u^{2}(x)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in X}V(x)|\nabla u|^{2}(x)+\sum_{y\sim x}\frac{\langle\nabla f,\nabla V\rangle_{y}}{f(y)}u^{2}(y)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\sim x}V(y)\frac{\Delta f}{f}(y)u^{2}(y)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in X}V(x)\frac{\Delta f}{f}(x)u^{2}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in X}\left[V(x)|\nabla u|^{2}(x)+\frac{\langle\nabla f,\nabla V\rangle_{x}}{f(x)}u^{2}(x)-\frac{V\Delta f}{f}(x)u^{2}(x)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in X}V(x)|\nabla u|^{2}(x)-\sum_{x\in X}\frac{{\mathscr{T}}(V,f)}{f}(x)u^{2}(x)

where

{\mathscr{T}}(V,f)(x):=V(x)\Delta f(x)-\langle\nabla f,\nabla V\rangle_{x},\quad\forall\;x\in X.

Hence we obtain a first order Hardy type equality

(2.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in X}V(x)|\nabla u|^{2}(x)-\sum_{x\in X}\frac{{\mathscr{T}}(V,f)}{f}(x)u^{2}(x)={\mathscr{R}}(V,f,u).

Let us define a divergence operator for
F\in C(X\times X)
as follows.

(2.5)

\displaystyle{\rm div}F(x):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\sim x}b(x,y)\big[F(y,x)-F(x,y)\big].

For
F,~G\in C(X\times X)
, we denote the formal inner product (when it makes sense) as follows.

\langle F,G\rangle:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in X}b(x,y)F(x,y)G(x,y).

By the symmetry of
b
, we check readily that

\displaystyle-\langle{\rm div}F,h\rangle=\langle F,\nabla h\rangle,\quad\forall\;F\in C(X\times X),\;h\in C_{c}(X).

An interesting fact is

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
V,f\in C(X)
and any
x\in X
,
{\mathscr{T}}(V,f)(x)=-{\rm div}(V\nabla f)(x)
. Here

(V\nabla f)(x,y):=V(x)\nabla f(x,y).

Proof
. Let
x\in X
, there holds

\displaystyle V(x)\Delta f(x)-\langle\nabla V,\nabla f\rangle_{x}

\displaystyle=V(x)\sum_{y\sim x}b(x,y)\nabla f(x,y)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\sim x}b(x,y)\nabla V(x,y)\nabla f(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\sim x}b(x,y)[V(x)+V(y)]\nabla f(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\sim x}b(x,y)\big[V(x)\nabla f(x,y)-V(y)\nabla f(y,x)\big]

\displaystyle=-{\rm div}(V\nabla f)(x),

where the last equality comes from the definition (
2.5
). ∎

Remark 2.2
.

As far as we are aware, we did not find a reference of divergence for general function
F\in C(X\times X)
. Clearly, when
F
is anti-symmetric as the gradient, it coincides with the definition of divergence for discrete differential forms over graphs, see for example
[
14
]
.

If
V\geq 0
, we get immediately from (
2.4
) a weighted Hardy inequality since
{\mathscr{R}}(V,f,u)\geq 0
.

(2.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in X}V(x)|\nabla u|^{2}(x)\geq\sum_{x\in X}\frac{-{\rm div}(V\nabla f)}{f}(x)u^{2}(x),\quad\forall\;u\in C_{c}(X).

Let
h\in C(X)
. Applying (
2.1
), (
2.3
), (
2.4
) and Lemma
2.1
with
Vh
, we get for any
u\in C_{c}(X)
,

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in X}V|\Delta u-hu|^{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in X}V|\Delta u|^{2}(x)-2\langle Vh,u\Delta u\rangle+\sum_{x\in X}Vh^{2}u^{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in X}V|\Delta u|^{2}(x)-2\langle Vh,|\nabla u|^{2}\rangle-\langle\Delta(Vh),u^{2}\rangle+\sum_{x\in X}Vh^{2}u^{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in X}V|\Delta u|^{2}(x)+\sum_{x\in X}\left[-\Delta(Vh)+Vh^{2}+2\frac{{\rm div}(Vh\nabla f)}{f}\right]u^{2}(x)

\displaystyle\quad-2{\mathscr{R}}(Vh,f,u).

Choose now
h=h_{0}=\frac{\Delta f}{f}=:L(f)
and using (
2.2
), for any
x\in X
, there holds

\displaystyle\quad f\left[-\Delta(Vh_{0})+Vh_{0}^{2}+2\frac{{\rm div}(Vh_{0}\nabla f)}{f}\right](x)

\displaystyle=\big[{-f\Delta(Vh_{0})+Vh_{0}^{2}f-2Vh_{0}\Delta f}\big](x)+2\langle\nabla f,\nabla(Vh_{0})\rangle_{x}

\displaystyle=-\big[f\Delta(Vh_{0})+Vh_{0}\Delta f\big](x)+2\langle\nabla f,\nabla(Vh_{0})\rangle_{x}

\displaystyle=-\Delta(Vh_{0}f)(x)

\displaystyle=-\Delta(V\Delta f)(x).

Combining the above two equalities, we obtain a second order Hardy-Rellich equality:

(2.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in X}V|\Delta u|^{2}(x)=\sum_{x\in X}\frac{\Delta(V\Delta f)}{f}(x)u^{2}(x)+2{\mathscr{R}}(VL(f),f,u)+\sum_{x\in X}V\left|\Delta u-L(f)u\right|^{2}(x).

Finally, using iteration, we arrive at

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
m\geq 1
,
V,f\in C(X)
satisfy
\Delta^{k}f>0
for all
0\leq k\leq m-1
. Then for any
u\in C_{c}(X)
, there holds

(2.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\sum_{x\in X}V|\Delta^{m}u|^{2}-\sum_{x\in X}\frac{\Delta^{m}(V\Delta^{m}f)}{f}u^{2}\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\sum_{x\in X}\frac{\Delta^{m-1-k}(V\Delta^{m}f)}{\Delta^{k+1}f}\left|\Delta^{k+1}u-L(\Delta^{k}f)\Delta^{k}u\right|^{2}(x)\\

Connecting the discrete Hardy inequality to the continuous setting, we claim the sharpness of (
1.6
).

Proposition 4.1
.

For any
\ell\geq 2
, the constant
\Big[\frac{(2\ell)!}{4^{\ell}\ell!}\Big]^{2}
is optimal for (
1.6
), equally we cannot replace it by any bigger constant such that the Hardy-Rellich inequality remains true for all

satisfying
u_{k}=0
,
0\leq k\leq\ell-1
.

Proof.
The key observation is that the discrete Hardy-Rellich estimate will deduce the corresponding inequality for the continuous setting. More precisely, we shall show that if

(4.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{2n\geq\ell}|\Delta^{\frac{\ell}{2}}u_{n}|^{2}\geq\lambda\sum_{n=\ell}^{\infty}\frac{u_{n}^{2}}{n^{2\ell}},\quad\forall\;u\in C_{c}({\mathbb{N}}),\;u_{k}=0,0\leq k\leq\ell-1;

with a constant
\lambda>0
, then there holds

(4.2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi^{(\ell)}(x)|^{2}dx\geq\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi(x)^{2}}{x^{2\ell}}dx,\quad\forall\;\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,\infty).

This connection is elementary, but as far as we are aware, we did not find an explicit statement of such flavor in the literature.

Since the best constant to (
4.2
) is
\Big[\frac{(2\ell)!}{4^{\ell}\ell!}\Big]^{2}
, we need only to make sure that (
4.1
) implies (
4.2
).

Consider just
\ell=2
, other cases can be proved by the same way. Assume that (
4.1
) is valid for
\ell=2
and some
\lambda>0
. Take
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,1)
. For any integer
M\geq 1
, consider
u_{n}=\varphi(n/M)
. Hence for
M
large,
u_{0}=u_{1}=0
. By Taylor-Lagrange formula,

\Delta u_{n}=2\varphi\Big(\frac{n}{M}\Big)-\varphi\Big(\frac{n-1}{M}\Big)-\varphi\Big(\frac{n+1}{M}\Big)=-\frac{1}{M^{2}}\varphi^{\prime\prime}\Big(\frac{n}{M}\Big)+r_{n}

where

. If we take

, then as

goes to infinity,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}(\Delta w_{n})^{2}=M^{3}\sum_{n=1}^{M}(\Delta u_{n})^{2}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{n=1}^{M}\varphi^{\prime\prime}\Big(\frac{n}{M}\Big)^{2}+O\left(M^{-1}\right)\quad\longrightarrow\quad\int_{0}^{1}|\varphi^{\prime\prime}(x)|^{2}dx.

On the other hand, as

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{w_{n}^{2}}{n^{4}}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{n=1}^{M}\Big(\frac{n}{M}\Big)^{-4}\varphi\Big(\frac{n}{M}\Big)^{2}\quad\longrightarrow\quad\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\varphi(x)^{2}}{x^{4}}dx.

Using (

Then (
1.10
) or (
3.1
) implies the desired Hardy inequalities. We decompose the study into three subcases.

5.1.
For
\alpha\in[0,1]
.

Take
f_{n}=n^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}
with
n\geq 0
, then for
n\geq 1
,

(5.2)

\displaystyle-\frac{{\rm div}(V\nabla f)_{n}}{f_{n}}=n^{\alpha}\left[1+\Big(1+\frac{1}{n}\Big)^{\alpha}-\Big(1+\frac{1}{n}\Big)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}-\Big(1-\frac{1}{n}\Big)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\right]=n^{\alpha}G_{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)

where

\displaystyle G_{\alpha}(x)=1+(1+x)^{\alpha}-(1+x)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}-(1-x)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}},\quad\mbox{for }\;0\leq x\leq 1.

Direct calculation gives
G_{\alpha}(0)=0
,
G^{\prime}_{\alpha}(0)=0
,
G^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha}(0)=\frac{(\alpha-1)^{2}}{2}
and

\displaystyle G^{\prime\prime\prime}_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\alpha(\alpha-1)(\alpha-2)(1+x)^{\alpha-3}+\frac{(1-\alpha^{2})(\alpha-3)}{8}(1+x)^{\frac{\alpha-5}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(1-\alpha^{2})(\alpha+3)}{8}(1-x)^{\frac{-5-\alpha}{2}}

\displaystyle=:\sum_{k=1}^{3}\widetilde{J}_{k}.

As
\alpha\in[0,1]
and
(1+x)^{\frac{\alpha-5}{2}}\leq 1\leq(1-x)^{\frac{-5-\alpha}{2}}
for
x\in[0,1]
, we get

\displaystyle\widetilde{J}_{2}+\widetilde{J}_{3}\geq\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha^{2})}{4}\geq 0.

Hence
G^{\prime\prime\prime}_{\alpha}(x)\geq 0
in
[0,1]
, which yields
G_{\alpha}(x)\geq\frac{(\alpha-1)^{2}}{4}x^{2}
in
[0,1]
. Seeing (
5.2
), we conclude that (
5.1
) holds true.

5.2.
For
\alpha\geq 3

In the following, we will choose the shifting sequence
f_{n}=(n+1)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}
for
n\geq 1
and
f_{0}=0
.

First,
2^{\alpha}>(\alpha-1)^{2}
for
\alpha\geq 3
, we have then

(5.3)

\displaystyle-\frac{{\rm div}(V\nabla f)_{1}}{f_{1}}=V_{1}+\Big(1-\frac{f_{2}}{f_{1}}\Big)V_{2}

\displaystyle=1+\Big[1-\Big(\frac{2}{3}\Big)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}\Big]2^{\alpha}\geq 1+\frac{2^{\alpha}}{3}>\frac{(\alpha-1)^{2}}{4}.

Let
n\geq 2
,

(5.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\frac{{\rm div}(V\nabla f)_{n}}{f_{n}}&=n^{\alpha}\left[\Big(1+\frac{1}{n}\Big)^{\alpha}-\Big(1+\frac{1}{n}\Big)^{\frac{3\alpha-1}{2}}\Big(1+\frac{2}{n}\Big)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}+1-\Big(1+\frac{1}{n}\Big)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}\right]\\
&=:n^{\alpha}F_{\alpha}\Big(1+\frac{1}{n}\Big)\end{split}

where

(5.5)

\displaystyle F_{\alpha}(x)=1+x^{\alpha}-x^{\frac{3\alpha-1}{2}}(2x-1)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}-x^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}=1+x^{\alpha}-x^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}-x^{\alpha}Y(x)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}.

So we are interested in
F_{\alpha}
over
I_{0}:=\big[1,\frac{3}{2}\big]
with

, we can check that

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime\prime}_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\alpha(\alpha-1)(\alpha-2)x^{\alpha-3}\Big[1-Y^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}\Big]-\frac{(\alpha-1)(\alpha-3)(\alpha-5)}{8}x^{\frac{\alpha-7}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{3(\alpha-2)(\alpha-1)}{4}x^{\alpha-4}Y^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}\Big[2(\alpha-1)-(\alpha+1)x^{-1}Y\Big]+\frac{(\alpha^{2}-1)(\alpha+3)}{8}x^{\alpha-6}Y^{\frac{\alpha+5}{2}}

\displaystyle=:\sum_{k=1}^{4}\widehat{J}_{k}.

Moreover,
x^{-1}Y=\frac{1}{2x-1}\in\big[\frac{1}{2},1\big]
and
xY=\frac{x^{2}}{2x-1}\geq 1
over
I_{0}
, there holds then

2(\alpha-1)-(\alpha+1)x^{-1}Y\geq\alpha-3,

so that

\displaystyle\widehat{J}_{3}\geq\frac{3(\alpha-1)(\alpha-2)(\alpha-3)}{4}x^{\alpha-4}Y^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{3(\alpha-1)(\alpha-2)(\alpha-3)}{4}x^{\frac{\alpha-7}{2}}(xY)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}Y

\displaystyle\geq\frac{9(\alpha-1)(\alpha-2)(\alpha-3)}{16}x^{\frac{\alpha-7}{2}}.

Therefore

\displaystyle(\widehat{J}_{3}+\widehat{J}_{2})x^{\frac{7-\alpha}{2}}\geq\frac{(\alpha-1)(\alpha-3)(7\alpha-8)}{16}\geq 0.

It means that
F^{\prime\prime\prime}_{\alpha}(x)\geq 0
in
I_{0}
for
\alpha\geq 3
, since
\widehat{J}_{1},\widehat{J}_{4}\geq 0
over
I_{0}
.

With
F_{\alpha}(1)=0
,
F^{\prime}_{\alpha}(1)=0
,
F^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha}(1)=\frac{(\alpha-1)^{2}}{2}
, we get
F_{\alpha}(x)\geq\frac{(\alpha-1)^{2}}{4}(x-1)^{2}
in
I_{0}
by Taylor expansion. Combining with (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), we get (
5.1
) for
\alpha\geq 3
.

5.3.
For
\alpha\in(1,3)

As above, we choose
f_{n}=(n+1)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}
for
n\geq 1
and
f_{0}=0
. For
\alpha\in(1,3)
,

(5.6)

\displaystyle-\frac{{\rm div}(V\nabla f)_{1}}{f_{1}}=1+\Big[1-\Big(\frac{2}{3}\Big)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}\Big]2^{\alpha}>1>\frac{(\alpha-1)^{2}}{4}.

Let
F_{\alpha}
be given by (
5.5
),
Y(x)
,
I_{0}
be also as in the previous case. Notice that
x^{-1}Y=2Y-1
, we rewrite

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime\prime}_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=(\alpha-1)x^{\alpha-3}\left[\alpha(\alpha-2)-\frac{(\alpha-3)(\alpha-5)}{8}x^{\frac{-1-\alpha}{2}}+L_{\alpha}(Y)\right]

\displaystyle=:(\alpha-1)x^{\alpha-3}K_{\alpha}(x)

with
Y^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}L_{\alpha}(Y)=

\displaystyle(\alpha+1)(\alpha+3)Y^{3}-\frac{3(3\alpha-1)(\alpha+1)}{2}Y^{2}+\frac{9(3\alpha-1)(\alpha-1)}{4}Y-\frac{3(3\alpha-1)(3\alpha-5)}{8}.

Consider
L_{\alpha}
as function of variable
Y
, direct computation gives
L^{\prime}_{\alpha}(Y)=Y^{\frac{\alpha-3}{2}}J_{\alpha}(Y)
,
where

\displaystyle J_{\alpha}(Y)

\displaystyle=\frac{(\alpha+1)(\alpha+3)(\alpha+5)}{2}Y^{3}-\frac{3(3\alpha-1)(\alpha+1)(\alpha+3)}{4}Y^{2}+\frac{9(3\alpha-1)(\alpha^{2}-1)}{8}Y

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{3(\alpha-1)(3\alpha-1)(3\alpha-5)}{16}.

For
Y\in I_{1}:=[\frac{3}{4},1]
and
\alpha\in(1,3)
, there holds

\displaystyle J^{\prime}_{\alpha}(Y)

\displaystyle=\frac{3(\alpha+1)}{8}Y\left[4(\alpha+3)(\alpha+5)Y-4(3\alpha-1)(\alpha+3)+3(3\alpha-1)(\alpha-1)Y^{-1}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3(\alpha+1)}{8}Y\Big[3(\alpha+3)(\alpha+5)-4(3\alpha-1)(\alpha+3)+3(3\alpha-1)(\alpha-1)\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{3(\alpha+1)Y}{8}\times 20(3-\alpha)>0.

Meanwhile, we have

\displaystyle J_{\alpha}\Big(\frac{3}{4}\Big)=\frac{3(-9\alpha^{3}+63\alpha^{2}-175\alpha+265)}{128}=:g(\alpha)

Elementary calculus shows that
g
is decreasing and positive in
[1,3]
, Hence
J_{\alpha}(Y)>0
for any
Y\in I_{1}
and
\alpha\in(1,3)
, so
L_{\alpha}
is increasing in
I_{1}
. Moreover, as
\alpha\in(1,3)
, using
xY\geq 1
,
Y\geq\frac{3}{4}
and
J_{\alpha}(Y)\geq J_{\alpha}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)
for
x\in I_{0}
, there holds

\displaystyle K^{\prime}_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{(\alpha+1)(\alpha-3)(\alpha-5)}{16}x^{-\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}+Y^{\frac{\alpha-3}{2}}J_{\alpha}(Y)Y^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=x^{-\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}\left[\frac{(\alpha+1)(\alpha-3)(\alpha-5)}{16}-(xY)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}YJ_{\alpha}(Y)\right]

\displaystyle\leq x^{-\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}\left[\frac{(\alpha+1)(\alpha-3)(\alpha-5)}{16}-\frac{3}{4}J_{\alpha}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=:x^{-\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}G(\alpha),

where

\displaystyle G(\alpha)=\frac{(\alpha+1)(\alpha-3)(\alpha-5)}{16}-\frac{3}{4}g(\alpha)=\frac{113\alpha^{3}-791\alpha^{2}+1799\alpha-1905}{512}.

We can check that
G(\alpha)<0
for
\alpha\in(1,3)
, which implies then
K^{\prime}_{\alpha}(x)<0
in
I_{0}
. Moreover,

K_{\alpha}(1)=\frac{3(\alpha^{2}-3\alpha+4)}{4}>0.

Recall that
F^{\prime\prime\prime}_{\alpha}(x)=(\alpha-1)x^{\alpha-3}K_{\alpha}(x)
, so
F^{\prime\prime\prime}_{\alpha}
changes eventually at most once its sign over
I_{0}
from positive value to negative value, hence

(5.7)

\displaystyle\min_{I_{0}}F_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}(x)=\min\Big\{F^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha}(1),~F^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)\Big\}.

Clearly
F^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha}(1)=\frac{(\alpha-1)^{2}}{2}>0
and

(5.8)

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)=(\alpha-1)\left[\alpha\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{\alpha-2}-(\alpha+1)\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{\frac{3\alpha-1}{2}}2^{\frac{-3-\alpha}{2}}-\frac{\alpha-3}{4}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha-5}{2}}\right]=:Q(\alpha)

We will check that

(5.9)

\displaystyle Q(\alpha)>\frac{(\alpha-1)^{2}}{2}\quad\mbox{for }~\alpha\in(1,3).

Indeed, we only need to prove
H(\alpha)>0
in
(1,3)
, where

H(\alpha):=\alpha-(\alpha+1)\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}-\frac{\alpha-3}{4}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}-\frac{\alpha-1}{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{2-\alpha}.

Directly calculus gives

\displaystyle H^{\prime}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=1-\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}-\frac{(\alpha+1)}{2}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}\ln\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}-\frac{\alpha-3}{8}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}\ln\frac{2}{3}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{2-\alpha}+\frac{\alpha-1}{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{2-\alpha}\ln\frac{3}{2}

and

\displaystyle H^{\prime\prime}(\alpha)=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{\frac{\alpha+3}{2}}\ln\frac{3}{4}-\frac{\alpha+1}{4}(\ln\frac{3}{4})^{2}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{\frac{\alpha+3}{2}}+\frac{1}{4}\ln\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha-3}{16}(\ln\frac{3}{2})^{2}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}+\ln\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{2-\alpha}-\frac{\alpha-1}{2}(\ln\frac{3}{2})^{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{2-\alpha}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle-\ln\frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{\frac{\alpha+3}{2}}\left(1+\ln\frac{3}{4}\right)+\ln\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{2-\alpha}\left(1-\ln\frac{3}{2}\right)>0.

Hence
H^{\prime}
is increasing. As
H^{\prime}(1.5)\approx-0.0198<0
,
H^{\prime}(1.55)\approx 0.0117>0
, there exists
1.5<\alpha_{0}<1.55
such that
H^{\prime}(\alpha_{0})=0
. The convexity of
H
implies

H(\alpha)\geq H(\alpha_{0})>1.5-2.55\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{\frac{9}{4}}+\frac{1.45}{4}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{\frac{2.55}{2}}-\frac{0.55}{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{0.5}\approx 0.0445>0.

Combining with (
5.7
)-(
5.9
), we see that
F^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha}(x)\geq\frac{(\alpha-1)^{2}}{2}
in
I_{0}
for
\alpha\in(1,3)
. Again, by Taylor expansion at
x_{0}=1
, we get
F_{\alpha}(x)\geq\frac{(\alpha-1)^{2}}{4}(x-1)^{2}
. Using (
5.6
), (
5.1
) is valid for
\alpha\in(1,3)
.

To conclude, for any
\alpha\geq 0
, the weighted Hardy inequality (
1.11
) holds true. ∎

6.
First order weighted Hardy inequality over
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

Here we attempt to apply our idea to consider the lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
with
d\geq 2
. By definition,
x\sim y
on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
if and only if
y=x\pm e_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq d
, and
b(x,y)=1
if
x\sim y
, here
\{e_{i}\}
means the canonical basis in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. We claim the following weighted Hardy inequality, where
|\cdot|
stands for the Euclidean norm in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\alpha>2-d
, there exists
A_{d,\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(6.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|x|^{\alpha}|\nabla u|^{2}(x)\geq\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}w(x)u^{2}(x),\quad\forall~u\in C_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus{\{0}\}),

where as
|x|\to+\infty

w(x)=\frac{(d-2+\alpha)^{2}}{4}|x|^{\alpha-2}+A_{d,\alpha}|x|^{\alpha-4}+O(|x|^{\alpha-6}).

Proof.
Take
f(x)=|x|^{2\gamma}
and
V=|x|^{\alpha}
for
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus{\{0}\}
, by the first order Hardy equality (
2.6
), the estimate (
6.1
) holds true with
w(x):=\frac{-{\rm div}(V\nabla f)}{f}
and
w(0)=0
. We will estimate
w(x)
then make a suitable choice of
\gamma
. To avoid confusion, we use
\Delta f_{x}
to denote the discrete Laplacian of
f
at
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta f_{x}}{f(x)}=\frac{1}{f(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}(f(x)-f(y))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left[2-\Big(1+\frac{2x_{i}}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}\Big)^{\gamma}-\Big(1-\frac{2x_{i}}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}\Big)^{\gamma}\right].

By Taylor expansion, as
|x|\to+\infty
, there holds

\displaystyle\Big(1+\frac{1+2x_{i}}{|x|^{2}}\Big)^{\gamma}+\Big(1+\frac{1-2x_{i}}{|x|^{2}}\Big)^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=2+\gamma\frac{2}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{\gamma(\gamma-1)}{2}\frac{2+8x_{i}^{2}}{|x|^{4}}+\frac{\gamma(\gamma-1)(\gamma-2)}{3}\frac{1+12x_{i}^{2}}{|x|^{6}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\gamma(\gamma-1)(\gamma-2)(\gamma-3)}{12}\frac{1+24x_{i}^{2}+16x_{i}^{4}}{|x|^{8}}+O(|x|^{-6}),

which yields

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta f_{x}}{f(x)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left[2-(1+\frac{2x_{i}}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{1}{|x|^{2}})^{\gamma}-(1-\frac{2x_{i}}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{1}{|x|^{2}})^{\gamma}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{-2d\gamma-4\gamma(\gamma-1)}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{\gamma(\gamma-1)[d+4(\gamma-2)]}{|x|^{4}}-\frac{4\gamma(\gamma-1)(\gamma-2)(\gamma-3)}{3}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{x_{i}^{4}}{|x|^{8}}

\displaystyle\quad+O(|x|^{-6}).

Meanwhile, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\nabla f,\nabla V\rangle_{x}}{f(x)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2f(x)}\sum_{x\sim y}\big[f(x)-f(y)\big]\big[V(x)-V(y)\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2f(x)}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Big[|x|^{2\gamma}-(|x|^{2}+2x_{i}+1)^{\gamma}\Big]\Big[|x|^{\alpha}-(|x|^{2}+2x_{i}+1)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2f(x)}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Big[|x|^{2\gamma}-(|x|^{2}-2x_{i}+1)^{\gamma}\Big]\Big[|x|^{\alpha}-(|x|^{2}-2x_{i}+1)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{|x|^{\alpha}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}(J_{+}+J_{-}),

where

\displaystyle J_{\pm}:=\Big[1-\Big(1\pm 2\frac{x_{i}}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}\Big)^{\gamma}\Big]\Big[1-\Big(1\pm 2\frac{x_{i}}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\Big].

As
|x|\to+\infty
, direct expansions yield

\displaystyle J_{\pm}=\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}\frac{(1\pm 2x_{i})^{2}}{|x|^{4}}+\frac{\alpha\gamma(\alpha+2\gamma-4)}{8}\frac{(1\pm 2x_{i})^{3}}{|x|^{6}}+K_{\alpha,\gamma}\frac{(1\pm 2x_{i})^{4}}{|x|^{8}}+O(|x|^{-6})

with

\displaystyle K_{\alpha,\gamma}=\frac{\alpha\gamma}{48}\Big[(\alpha-2)(\alpha-4)+3(\alpha-2)(\gamma-1)+4(\gamma-1)(\gamma-2)\Big].

Therefore, as
|x|
goes to infinity, there holds

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}(J_{+}+J_{-})=\frac{2\alpha\gamma}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{\alpha\gamma(d+3(\alpha+2\gamma-4))}{2|x|^{4}}+32K_{\alpha,\gamma}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{x_{i}^{4}}{|x|^{8}}+O(|x|^{-6}).

This implies as
|x|\to+\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{\omega(x)}{V(x)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta f_{x}}{f(x)}-\frac{\langle\nabla f,\nabla V\rangle_{x}}{f(x)V(x)}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2\gamma(d+\alpha-2+2\gamma)}{|x|^{2}}-\gamma\left[(\gamma-1)(d+4(\gamma-2))+\frac{d\alpha}{2}+\frac{3\alpha(\alpha+2\gamma-4)}{2}\right]\frac{1}{|x|^{4}}

\displaystyle\quad-\left[\frac{4\gamma(\gamma-1)(\gamma-2)(\gamma-3)}{3}+32K_{\alpha,\gamma}\right]\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{x_{i}^{4}}{|x|^{8}}+O(|x|^{-6}).

Now we choose
\gamma=\frac{2-d-\alpha}{4}<0
to optimize the coefficient of the main order term, then

\displaystyle\omega(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{(d-2+\alpha)^{2}}{4}|x|^{\alpha-2}+\frac{(d-2+\alpha)(3d+6+2\alpha^{2}-5\alpha)}{8}|x|^{\alpha-4}

\displaystyle\quad-C_{\alpha,d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{x_{i}^{4}}{|x|^{8-\alpha}}+O(|x|^{\alpha-6})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(d-2+\alpha)^{2}}{4}\frac{1}{|x|^{2-\alpha}}+A_{\alpha,d}\frac{1}{|x|^{4-\alpha}}+O(|x|^{\alpha-6}),

since
C_{\alpha,d}\sum x_{i}^{4}\leq\max(C_{\alpha,d},0)|x|^{4}
.∎

Remark 6.2
.

Let
d\geq 3
,
\alpha=0
, i.e
V\equiv 1
, it was shown in
[
18
, Theorem 7.2]
that (
6.1
) holds with

w(x)=\frac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}+O(|x|^{-3}),\quad\mbox{as }\;|x|\to\infty.

They used the asymptotic behavior of the Green’s function for discrete Laplacian over
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. We don’t know if their technique works for weighted situation with
\alpha\neq 0
, our approach seems to be more computable and provides a weight
w
with more precise behavior. In fact, for
\alpha=0
, we have

\displaystyle\omega(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{3(d^{2}-4)}{8}\frac{1}{|x|^{4}}-\frac{(d^{2}-4)(d+6)(d+10)}{192}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{x_{i}^{4}}{|x|^{8}}+O(|x|^{-6})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{(d^{2}-4)(d^{2}+16d-12)}{192}\frac{1}{|x|^{4}}+O(|x|^{-6}).

As for Theorem
1.4
, we can also get Leray type estimate in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. For simplicity, we consider just
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
case here, that is

(6.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}(x)\geq\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}w_{2}(x)u^{2}(x),\quad\forall\;u\in C_{c}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\cap{\{|x|\geq 2}\})

where

w_{2}(x)=\frac{1}{4|x|^{2}(\ln|x|)^{2}}+\frac{2(x_{1}^{4}+x_{2}^{4})}{|x|^{4}}-\frac{3}{2|x|^{4}\ln|x|}+O\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{4}\ln|x|^{2}}\right)\quad\mbox{as }|x|\to+\infty.

Let
f=(\ln|x|)^{\gamma}
with
|x|\geq 2
, and consider

h_{i}(t)=(\ln|x+te_{i}|)^{\gamma},\;\;i=1,2\quad\mbox{and}\quad g_{i}(t)=h_{i}(t)+h_{i}(-t)-2h_{i}(0).

We have then

g_{i}(0)=g^{\prime}_{i}(0)=g^{\prime\prime\prime}_{i}(0)=0,\quad g^{\prime\prime}_{i}(0)=2h^{\prime\prime}_{i}(0)=2\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}(x),

and
\Delta f_{x}=-g_{1}(1)-g_{2}(1)
. As before, Taylor expansion yields

\displaystyle\Delta f_{x}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}(x)-\frac{g_{1}^{(4)}(0)+g_{2}^{(4)}(0)}{24}-\frac{1}{120}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{5}g^{(6)}_{i}(s)ds

\displaystyle=\frac{-\gamma(\gamma-1)}{|x|^{2}(\ln|x|)^{2-\gamma}}-\frac{g_{1}^{(4)}(0)+g_{2}^{(4)}(0)}{24}+J.

Moreover, it’s not difficult to see that
|h^{(6)}_{i}(t)|=O(|x|^{-6}(\ln|x|)^{\gamma-1})
as
|x|\to+\infty
uniformly for
t\in[-1,1]
, which means

J=O(|x|^{-6}(\ln|x|)^{\gamma-1})
as
|x|\to+\infty
.

Set now
\gamma=\frac{1}{2}
, direct computation yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f(x)}\sum_{i=1}^{2}g^{(4)}_{i}(0)

\displaystyle=\left[36-48\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{x_{i}^{4}}{|x|^{4}}\right]\frac{1}{|x|^{4}\ln|x|}-\frac{1}{2}\left[26\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{x_{i}^{4}}{|x|^{4}}-21\right]\frac{1}{|x|^{4}(\ln|x|)^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{3}{4}\left[6-9\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{x_{i}^{4}}{|x|^{4}}\right]\frac{1}{|x|^{4}(\ln|x|)^{3}}-\frac{15}{8}\frac{1}{|x|^{4}(\ln|x|)^{4}}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{x_{i}^{4}}{|x|^{4}}

\displaystyle=\left[36-48\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{x_{i}^{4}}{|x|^{4}}\right]\frac{1}{|x|^{4}\ln|x|}+O(|x|^{-4}(\ln|x|)^{-2}).

Applying (
6.1
) with
V\equiv 1
, we get a Hardy inequality (
6.2
) with

\displaystyle w_{2}(x)=\frac{\Delta f_{x}}{f(x)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\frac{1}{|x|^{2}(\ln|x|)^{2}}+\frac{1}{24}\left[48\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{x_{i}^{4}}{|x|^{4}}-36\right]\frac{1}{|x|^{4}\ln|x|}+O(|x|^{-4}(\ln|x|)^{-2})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\frac{1}{|x|^{2}(\ln|x|)^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{|x|^{4}\ln|x|}+O(|x|^{-4}(\ln|x|)^{-2}).

7.
Further remarks

Using the weighted equality (
1.10
) or more generally (
2.4
), we establish some Hardy-Rellich inequalities with optimal constants in Theorems
1.1
to
1.3
. Our approach can be adapted to the
\ell^{p}
setting. There exist however many rooms for improvement for higher order Rellich inequalities over
{\mathbb{N}}
, and more mysteries exist for the lattice situation
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
with
d\geq 2
, even for the first order Hardy inequality.

7.1.

Firstly, we want to point out that the idea leading to the Hardy inequality (
2.6
) on graphs works also for general
\ell^{p}
setting. Let
(X,b)
be a locally finite graph. For
u\in C(X)
, denote

(7.1)

\displaystyle|\nabla u|^{p}_{p}(x):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\sim x}b(x,y)^{p-1}|\nabla u(x,y)|^{p},\quad\forall\;x\in X.

The above definition can be motivated as follows. Let
b(x,y)^{-1}
mean the direct distance between vertices
x,y
, then the unitary gradient of
u
is given by
b(x,y)\nabla u(x,y)
, therefore the accumulated pointwise
\ell^{p}
norm at
x
is given by
|\nabla u|^{p}_{p}(x)
.

For simplicity, we use the following notation

t^{\beta}=|t|^{\beta-1}t,\quad\forall\;\beta>0,\;t\in{\mathbb{R}}.

We define also

(7.2)

\displaystyle(\nabla u)^{p-1}_{b}(x,y):=b(x,y)^{p-2}\nabla u(x,y)^{p-1}{\mathbbm{1}}_{x\sim y},\quad\forall\;x,y\in X,u\in C(X).

Remark that
(\nabla u)^{p}
is anti-symmetric over
X\times X
and
{\rm div}\big[(\nabla u)^{p-1}_{b}\big]=\Delta_{p}u
.

Let
f\in C(X)
be a positive function. By the discrete Picone’s inequality (see
[
1
]
), there holds

\displaystyle|\nabla u(x,y)|^{p}\geq\nabla\left(\frac{u^{p}}{f^{p-1}}\right)(x,y)(\nabla f)^{p-1}(x,y),\quad\forall\;x,y\in X,u\in C(X).

Therefore, let
u\in C_{c}(X)
,

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in X}V(x)|\nabla u|^{p}_{p}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in X}V(x)b(x,y)^{p-1}|\nabla u(x,y)|^{p}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in X}b(x,y)^{p-1}V(x)\nabla\left(\frac{u^{p}}{f^{p-1}}\right)(x,y)\nabla f(x,y)^{p-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in X}b(x,y)^{p-1}\big[V(x)+V(y)\big]\frac{u^{p}}{f^{p-1}}(x)\nabla f(x,y)^{p-1}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{x\in X}\frac{{\rm div}\big[V(\nabla f)_{b}^{p-1}\big]}{f^{p-1}}(x)u^{p-1}(x).

For the last two lines above, we used the symmetry of
b
and the anti-symmetry of
(\nabla f)^{p-1}
. The divergence operator is that given in (
2.5
). Then for any
p>1
, we get the
\ell^{p}
- discrete Hardy inequality over
X
, which generalizes clearly (
2.6
) with
p=2
.

(7.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in X}V(x)|\nabla u|^{p}_{p}(x)\geq-\sum_{x\in X}\frac{{\rm div}\big[V(\nabla f)_{b}^{p-1}\big]}{f^{p-1}}(x)u^{p}(x),\quad\forall\;u\in C_{c}(X).

The proof of
[
6
, Proposition 3]
used somehow an equivalent form of (
7.3
) with
V\equiv 1
over
{\mathbb{N}}
, see also
[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
for locally summable graphs.

7.2.

As Keller-Pinchover-Pogorzelski’s result in (
1.3
) for
\ell=1
, we can obtain sharper weights by adding infinitely many positive remainder terms in (
1.6
). Consider for example
\ell=2
: Let
u\in C_{c}({\mathbb{N}})
with
u_{0}=u_{1}=0
,
seeing the proof of (
1.9
) for
\alpha=-2
, we arrive at

(7.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 2}(n-1)^{-2}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\geq\sum_{n\geq 2}A_{n}u_{n}^{2},\quad\forall\;u\in C_{c}({\mathbb{N}}),u_{0}=0,

where

\displaystyle A_{n}=H_{-2}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left[1+\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{-2}-\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}-\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\right]=\sum_{k\geq 2}\frac{a_{k}}{n^{k+2}},

with

\displaystyle a_{k}

\displaystyle=(k+1)-\frac{\binom{2k}{k}}{4^{k}}-(-1)^{k}\frac{3\binom{2k-4}{k-2}}{4^{k-1}k(k-1)}>0,\quad\forall\;k\geq 2.

Denote
v={\rm div}(u)
, so
v_{0}=0
. Applying (
1.3
) to
v
, there holds

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}|\Delta u_{n}|^{2}=\sum_{n\geq 1}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{n\geq 1}\omega_{n}v_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 2}\omega_{n-1}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}}{(n-1)^{2}}+\sum_{k=2}\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{\binom{4k}{2k}}{(4k-1)2^{4k-1}(n-1)^{2k}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n\geq 2}A_{n}u_{n}^{2}+\sum_{k=2}\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{(2k+1)^{2}\binom{4k}{2k}}{(4k-1)2^{4k+1}}\frac{u_{n}^{2}}{n^{2k+2}}.

We used (
7.4
) and (
1.9
) for the second inequality. More precisely, we have

(7.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}|\Delta u_{n}|^{2}\geq\sum_{n\geq 2}\left(\frac{9}{16n^{4}}+\frac{15}{16n^{5}}+\frac{213}{128n^{6}}+\ldots\right)u_{n}^{2},\quad\forall\;u\in C_{c}({\mathbb{N}}),u_{0}=u_{1}=0.

The above estimate could be still improved, an interesting question is to know whether the constant
\frac{15}{16}
is sharp.

A much more challenging question is to search optimal weights for
\ell=2
. Seeing (
2.9
), we could attempt to find suitable positive function
f\in C({\mathbb{N}})
and use the corresponding weights
w=\Delta^{2}f/f
, but we have no idea about the
right
choice of
f
.

In
[
8
]
, with the factorization method, Gerhat-Krejčiřík-Štampach obtained the weights
\widetilde{w}_{n}
in (
1.5
) by choosing
\widetilde{w}=(\Delta^{2}g)/g
with
g_{n}=n^{\frac{3}{2}}
. However, their result is not a direct consequence of (
2.9
) (or Theorem
2.3
with
V\equiv 1
and
f=g
), because
\Delta g_{n}<0
for
n\geq 1
.

7.3.

It’s worthy to mention that situations in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
(
d\geq 2
) have different nature comparing to the continuous situation
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
for
d\geq 2
, especially when
d
goes to infinity, even for the first order case. More precisely, consider the Hardy-Rellich inequalities

(7.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|\Delta^{\frac{\ell}{2}}u_{n}|^{2}\geq C_{\ell,d}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\frac{u_{n}^{2}}{n^{2\ell}},\quad\forall\;u\in C_{c}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}),u(0)=0.

In
[
12
]
, Gupta proved recently that for any
\ell\geq 1
, the sharp constant in (
7.6
), denoted by
C_{\ell,d}
, satisfies
C_{\ell,d}\sim d^{\ell}
as
d\to\infty
, that is

0<\liminf_{d\to\infty}\frac{C_{\ell,d}}{d^{\ell}}\leq\limsup_{d\to\infty}\frac{C_{\ell,d}}{d^{\ell}}<\infty.

Recall that in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we have the following sharp Hardy-Rellich inequalities (see
[
24
]
)

(7.7)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\Delta^{\frac{\ell}{2}}\varphi(x)|^{2}dx\geq 4^{-\ell}\prod_{k=1}^{\ell}(d-2k)^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{\varphi(x)^{2}}{|x|^{2\ell}}dx,\quad\forall\;\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\backslash\{0\}).

Therefore, the sharp constant has the asymptotic behavior as
4^{-\ell}d^{2\ell}
as
d
goes to infinity.

Abstract
. We prove bounds for the volume of neighborhoods of algebraic sets, in the euclidean space or the sphere, in terms of the degree of the defining polynomials, the number of variables and the dimension of the algebraic set, without any smoothness assumption. This generalizes previous work of Lotz [ 16 ] on smooth complete intersections in the euclidean space and of Bürgisser, Cucker and Lotz [ 7 ] on hypersurfaces in the sphere, and gives a complete solution to [ 5 , Problem 17] .

2.1.
Metric geometry

Notation 2.1
.

We will mostly be dealing with three metric spaces:

(A)

The euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the standard metric:
\mathrm{dist}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(a,b)=\|a-b\|\quad\forall a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(B)

The sphere
S^{n}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, with the riemannian metric induced by the ambient space. The distance between two points
a,b\in S^{n}
equals the length of the shortest geodesic on the sphere joining them:
\mathrm{dist}_{S^{n}}(a,b)=\arccos\langle a,b\rangle.
The diameter of the sphere for this metric is
\pi
.

(C)

Since the antipodal map
x\mapsto-x
is an isometry of the sphere, the riemannian metric on the sphere descends to a riemannian metric on
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}
. The distance
\mathrm{dist}_{\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}}([a],[b])
between two points
[a],[b]\in\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}
equals the length of the shortest geodesic on the the projective space joining them. The projective space is locally isometric to the sphere, but its diameter is
\frac{\pi}{2}
.

When the metric space
X
is clear from the context, we denote simply by
\mathrm{dist}(x,y)
the distance between two points

and, for

the closed ball of radius

around
x\in X
. In the above cases the metric comes from a riemannian structure on

. The riemannian structure induces a volume density
\omega_{X}
and we denote by
``\omega(\mathrm{dx})

we denote its volume by
\mathrm{vol}(A):=\int_{A}\omega_{X}(\mathrm{d}x).

Let

The next result is proved in

Theorem 3.1

(Lotz)
.

Let
V
be the zero set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of polynomials

of degree at most

. Assume that

is a smooth complete intersection of dimension

We use this result as a tool for proving next theorem, which deals with the case of algebraic sets in

, with no regularity assumption. Notice that the result has the same shape of (
3.1
), except for a doubling of the degree.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathbb{R}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
be a finite set of polynomials with degrees bounded by

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{dist}(x,V)\leq\varepsilon\right)\leq 4\sum_{i=0}^{m}{{n}\choose{n-m+i}}\left(\frac{4\delta\varepsilon}{\sigma}\right)^{n-m+i}\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{\sigma}\right)^{m-i}.

In particular:

(3.3)

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{dist}(x,V)\leq\varepsilon\right)\leq 4\left(\frac{4n\delta\varepsilon}{\sigma}\right)^{n-m}\left(1+\frac{(4\delta+1)\varepsilon}{\sigma}\right)^{m},

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{dist}(x,V)\leq\varepsilon\right)\leq 4e\left(\frac{4n\delta\varepsilon}{\sigma}\right)^{n-m}.

Let us first reduce to the situation in the hypothesis of Theorem

(this is just a technical step). Let
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}[X_{0},\ldots,X_{n}]
be the finite set of polynomials obtained by homogenizing the polynomials from

are still bounded by

. Denote by

and consider the affine chart

given by
\varphi([x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}])=(\frac{x_{1}}{x_{0}},\ldots,\frac{x_{n}}{x_{0}}).

Since
V=Z(\mathcal{P},\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}\setminus H)
has dimension at most
m
, we are in the position of applying part (A) of Theorem
2.10
: we get a one parameter family of real algebraic sets
\{V_{t}\}_{t>0}
and

such that for every

the set
V_{t}
is a nonsingular complete intersection in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

n-m

of degree bounded by
2\delta.

Fix
\varepsilon,\sigma>0
and pick

such that

The family

\lim_{t\to 0}\left(V_{t}\cap B(0,R)\right)=V\cap B(0,R).

For every

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{dist}(x,V)\leq\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{U}(V,\varepsilon)\cap B(p,\sigma))}{\mathrm{vol}(B(p,\sigma))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{U}(V_{t},\varepsilon+\tau)\cap B(p,\sigma))}{\mathrm{vol}(B(p,\sigma))}

\mathrm{vol}(\gamma(L))=\int_{L}\rho(y)\omega_{L}(\mathrm{d}y),

where the function

is computed as follows. For every point

we pick a basis
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell}\}
of

such that
|\omega_{L}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell})|=1

J_{y}\gamma:=[d_{y}\gamma v_{1},\ldots,d_{y}\gamma v_{\ell}]

(4.5)

\rho(y)=\sqrt{\det\left(J_{y}\gamma^{T}J_{y}\gamma\right)}.

Definition 4.2
.

be a smooth manifold of dimension

Definition 4.3
.

For every pair

Definition 4.5
.

Let

Remark 4.6
.

The function

play the analogues of polynomials for the spherical version of Weyl’s Tube Formula. More precisely, given a smooth compact manifold

, of dimension
m
and codimension

, possibly with boundary, and an open set

, there exists

such that for all

we have

]

(4.10)

\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{T}(A,\varepsilon))=\sum_{{i=0}}^{m}J_{n,c+i}(\varepsilon)\int_{N^{1}M}\psi_{i}(\nu)\omega_{N^{1}M}(\mathrm{d}\nu).

vanishes for

4.1.1.

We prove now a sequence of useful lemmas, relating the volume of tubes and the total absolute curvatures.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
M\hookrightarrow S^{n}
be a smooth manifold, possibly with boundary, of dimension
m
and codimension
c=n-m
. Let
A\subseteq M\setminus\partial M
be an open set. For every

(4.11)

\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{T}(A,\varepsilon))\leq\sum_{{i=0}}^{m}J_{n,c+i}(\varepsilon)|K_{i}|(A).

Proof.

This is simply an adaptation of
[
16
, Theorem 3.1]
to the spherical case. We prove the case

; the case
\varepsilon=\frac{\pi}{2}
follows easily by a limit argument.

Consider the density
r^{n-m-1}\mathrm{d}r\wedge\Theta
on
NM
and the map
\varphi:NM\simeq N^{1}M\times(0,\infty)\to S^{n}
defined by

(4.12)

\varphi(\nu,r):=\mathrm{exp}_{p(\nu)}(rv)=\frac{p(\nu)+r\nu}{(1+r^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

The Jacobian of the map
\varphi
is computed in
[
20
]
and can be used to compute the pull-back of the volume density
\omega_{S^{n}}
under
\varphi
:

(4.13)

\varphi^{*}(\omega_{S^{n}})=\frac{|\det(\mathbf{1}-rL_{x}(\nu))|}{(1+r^{2})^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}r^{n-m-1}dr\wedge\Theta.

The image of
\varphi|_{N^{1}A\times(0,\tan\varepsilon)}
contains
\mathcal{T}(A,\varepsilon)
and by the change of variables formula, since the set of critical values of
\varphi
has measure zero:

(4.14)

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{T}(A,\varepsilon))

\displaystyle\leq\int_{N^{1}A\times(0,\tan\varepsilon)}\frac{|\det(\mathbf{1}-rL_{x}(\nu))|}{(1+r^{2})^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}r^{n-m-1}dr\wedge\Theta(\mathrm{d}\nu)

(4.15)

\displaystyle=\int_{N^{1}A\times(0,\tan\varepsilon)}\frac{\left|\sum_{i=0}^{m}r^{i}\psi_{i}(\nu)\right|}{(1+r^{2})^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}r^{n-m-1}dr\wedge\Theta(\mathrm{d}\nu)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{N^{1}A\times(0,\tan\varepsilon)}\sum_{i=0}^{m}|\psi_{i}(\nu)|\wedge\Theta(\mathrm{d}\nu)

(4.17)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\left(\int_{0}^{\tan\varepsilon}\frac{r^{n-m-1+i}}{(1+r^{2})^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}dr\right)\left(\int_{N^{1}A}|\psi_{i}(\nu)|\omega_{N^{1}M}(\mathrm{d}\nu)\right)

(4.18)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{m}J_{n,c+i}(\varepsilon)|K_{i}|(A).

∎

6

The inequality in the statement follows from the identity:

(4.55)

{\alpha_{i}(n,m)}{\frac{2\Gamma\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n-m+i+1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{m-i}{2}\right)}}=\frac{\Gamma(n)}{\Gamma(m-i)\Gamma(n-m+i)}={{n-1}\choose{m-i}}(m-i).

∎

4.1.3.
The spherical algebraic case: degree estimates

Next lemma estimates the quantities
\beta_{i}(Z)
defined in Definition
4.13
in the case
Z\subset S^{n}
is a smooth complete intersection.

Lemma 4.16
.

Let
Z
be the zero set of homogeneous polynomials
P_{1},\ldots,P_{c}
of degree at most
d
in
S^{n}
. Assume that
Z
is a non-singular complete intersection of dimension
m=n-c
. Then for every
i=0,\ldots,m
we have:

(4.56)

\beta_{i}(Z)\leq 2(4d)^{n-m+i}.

Proof.

Let
X_{0},\ldots,X_{n}
be linear coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
such that

P_{1},\ldots,P_{c}\in\mathbb{R}[X_{0},\ldots,X_{n}]
.
Moreover, since we are interested in an upper bound on
\beta_{i}(Z)
, we can assume without loss of generality
that the polynomials
P_{1},\ldots,P_{c}
defining
Z
are of the same degree
d
.
The tangent bundle
TS^{n}
is embedded in the tangent bundle
T\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, which is trivial, and we denote by
Y_{0},\ldots,Y_{n}
, the coordinate functions corresponding to the basis
(\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{0}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{n}})
of
T_{0}\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

Following Definition
4.13
, for an element
g\in O_{Z,\pitchfork}(n+1)
, the set

Z\cap g\cdot S^{n-m+i}
can be described by (
4.61
) and

(4.57)

\displaystyle P_{1}=\cdots=P_{c}=

\displaystyle\ 0,

(4.58)

\displaystyle L_{0}=\cdots=L_{m-i-1}=

\displaystyle\ 0,

intersected with
S^{n}
,
where the
L_{i}
’s are generic linear forms in
X_{0},\ldots,X_{n}
.

By making a linear change in the
X_{i}
, we may assume that

L_{j}=X_{j},0\leq j\leq m-i-1
.

With this choice of coordinates,
the normal bundle

NZ\hookrightarrow TS^{n}\hookrightarrow T\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

can be described as projection to
T\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,
of the solutions to the set of equations,

(4.60)

\displaystyle X_{0}=\cdots=X_{m-i-1}=

\displaystyle\ 0,

(4.61)

\displaystyle P_{1}=\cdots=P_{c}=

\displaystyle\ 0,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{c}\lambda_{j}\frac{\partial P_{j}}{\partial X_{m-i}}=

\displaystyle\ Y_{m-i},

(4.62)

\displaystyle\vdots

(4.63)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{c}\lambda_{j}\frac{\partial P_{j}}{\partial X_{n}}=

\displaystyle\ Y_{n},

intersected with
S^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{c+n+1}
,
where
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{c}
are Lagrangian variables.
Notice that the above equations imply via the Euler identity that

X_{0}Y_{0}+\cdots+X_{n}Y_{n}=0,

and hence
Y_{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{0}}+\cdots+Y_{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{0}}
is constrained to belong
to the tangent space of
S^{n}
.

We have to bound the cardinality of
\gamma_{Z\cap g\cdot S^{n-m+i}}^{-1}(h\cdot S^{1})
for

h\in O_{g,\pitchfork}(n+1)
. Being zero–dimensional, we can assume (up to making another linear change of coordinates
X_{n-m},\ldots,X_{n}
) that

\gamma_{Z\cap g\cdot S^{n-m+i}}^{-1}(h\cdot S^{1})
has an empty intersection with
the hyperplane
X_{n}=0
.

Now, we can take
h\cdot S^{1}
to be the intersection of
S^{n}
, with
n-1
hyperplanes
defined by generic linear forms in
Y_{0},\ldots,Y_{n}
, which after another linear change in
coordinates we can assume to be
Y_{2},\ldots,Y_{n}
.

With the above assumptions,
using Definition
4.11
, the pull-back of
h\cdot S^{1}
under

\gamma_{Z\cap g\cdot S^{n-m+i}}
, for
h\in O_{g,\pitchfork}(n+1)
, can be described by
(
4.61
), (
4.62
) and

(4.64)

Y_{2}=\cdots=Y_{n}=0.

intersected with
S^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{c+n+1}
and projected to the first factor.

Homogenizing (
4.62
) with respect to
X_{n}
, we obtain the following system of
bi-homogeneous equations,

(4.65)

\displaystyle P_{1}=\cdots=P_{c}=

\displaystyle\ 0,

(4.66)

\displaystyle X_{0}=\cdots=X_{m-i-1}=

\displaystyle\ 0,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{c}\lambda_{j}\frac{\partial P_{j}}{\partial X_{m-i}}=

\displaystyle\ X_{n}^{d-1}Y_{m-i},

(4.67)

(4.68)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{c}\lambda_{j}\frac{\partial P_{j}}{\partial X_{n}}=

\displaystyle\ X_{n}^{d-1}Y_{n},

(4.69)

\displaystyle Y_{2}=\cdots=Y_{n}=

\displaystyle\ 0.

These equations are homogeneous of degree at most
d
in
(X_{0},\ldots,X_{n})
, and homogeneous
of degree at most
1

in
(Y_{0},\ldots,Y_{n},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{c})
. Also, using the fact

X_{0}=\cdots=X_{n-m-1}=Y_{2}=\cdots=Y_{n}=0
, the above equations define
a zero–dimensional subvariety of

\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{n-m+i}\times\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{c+1}
.

Using Kouchnirenko’s Theorem
[
14
]
the number of non-degenerate roots
of this system in
\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{n-m+i}\times\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{c+1}
is bounded by

(n-(m-i)+c+1)!\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{n-(m-i)+c+1}(d\cdot\Delta_{n-(m-i)}\times\Delta_{c+1}),

where for
p>0
,

\Delta_{p}
denotes the
p
-dimensional simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{p}
(i.e. with vertices the origin and
the standard basis vectors).
Noting that

\mathrm{vol}_{p}(\Delta_{p})=\frac{1}{p!},

we obtain that the number of non-degenerate roots
of this system in
\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{n-m+i}\times\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{c+1}

is bounded by

\displaystyle\frac{(n-(m-i)+c+1)!}{(n-(m-i))!(c+1)!}d^{n-m+i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{2c+i+1}{c+1}d^{n-m+i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{2c+i}d^{n-m+i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{2(c+i)}d^{c+i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(4d)^{c+i}.

This gives a bound for the number of real projective solutions of the system of equations in (
4.65
). The lemma follows after noting that
S^{n}
is a double covering of
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}
which gives the extra factor of
2
.
∎

4.1.4.
The spherical case: smooth complete intersections

Next theorem is the spherical analogue of Theorem
3.1
.

Theorem 4.17
.

Let
Z
be the zero set of homogeneous polynomials
P_{1},\ldots,P_{c}
in
S^{n}
of degree at most
d
. Assume that
Z
is a smooth complete intersection of dimension
m=n-c
. Let
x
be a uniformly distributed point in a ball
B(p,s)
of radius
0\leq s\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
around
p\in S^{n}
. Then, for every
0\leq r\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
:

(4.71)

\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{dist}(x,Z)\leq r)\leq 2\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{vol}(S^{n})}{2}\right)\left(\frac{4nd\sin r}{\sin s}\right)^{n-m}\left(1+(4nd+4d+1)\frac{\sin r}{\sin s}\right)^{m}.

In particular, if
\sin r\leq\frac{\sin s}{(4nd+4d+1)m}
,

(4.72)

\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{dist}(x,Z)\leq r)\leq 2e\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{vol}(S^{n})}{2}\right)\left(\frac{4nd\sin r}{\sin s}\right)^{n-m}.

Remark 4.18
.

The bounds from the statements that we put in the Introduction follow from:

(4.73)

\max_{n\geq 0}\frac{\mathrm{vol}(S^{n})}{2}=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(S^{6})}{2}=\frac{8}{15}\pi^{3}\leq 20.

Since
\mathrm{vol}(S^{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, for large
n
the reader might want to keep using the bound from the previous statement (similarly for Theorem
4.20
and Theorem
1.5
).

Proof.

Let
Z\hookrightarrow S^{n}
be a complete intersection defined by polynomials
P_{1},\ldots,P_{c}
whose degrees are bounded by
d
. Let
r,s>0
such that
Z\pitchfork B(p,s+r)
, denote by
Z^{\prime}=Z\cap B(p,s+r)
and define:

(4.74)

A_{0}=Z^{\prime}\setminus\partial Z^{\prime}\quad\textrm{and}\quad A_{1}=\partial Z^{\prime}.

With this choice we have

(4.75)

\mathcal{U}(Z,r)\cap B(p,s)\subseteq\mathcal{T}(A_{0},r)\cup\mathcal{T}(A_{1},r),

and consequently

(4.76)

\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{dist}(x,Z)\leq r)=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{U}(Z,r)\cap B(p,s))}{\mathrm{vol}(B(p,s))}\leq\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{T}(A_{0},r))}{\mathrm{vol}(B(p,s))}+\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{T}(A_{1},r))}{\mathrm{vol}(B(p,s))}.

We apply now Proposition
4.15
(which has no constraints on
\sigma>0
) for estimating both summands of (
4.76
), with the choice
\sigma=s+r
,
\varepsilon=r
and
A=A_{0},A_{1}.

For
j=0,1
, using the properties from Lemma
4.4
and setting
(r+s)^{\prime}:=\min\{r+s,\frac{\pi}{2}\}
, we get:

(4.77)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{T}(A_{j},r))}{\mathrm{vol}(B(p,s))}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{J_{n,n}(s)}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1-j}{{n-1}\choose{m-i-j}}\frac{(\sin r)^{n-m+i+j}}{n-m+i+j}(\sin(r+s)^{\prime})^{m-i-j}\beta_{i}(m-j,d)

(4.78)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{J_{n,n}(s)}\frac{\mathrm{vol}(S^{n})}{2}(\sin r)^{n}\beta_{m-j}(m-j,d).

Setting now
v_{n}=(1+\frac{\mathrm{vol}(S^{n})}{2})
, using (
4.78
) we get

(4.79)

\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{T}(A_{j},r))}{\mathrm{vol}(B(p,s))}\leq\frac{v_{n}}{J_{n,n}(s)}\sum_{i=0}^{m-j}{{n-1}\choose{m-i-j}}\frac{(\sin r)^{n-m+i+j}}{n-m+i+j}(\sin(r+s)^{\prime})^{m-i-j}\beta_{i}(m-j,d).

Let us write now:

(4.80)

{{n-1}\choose{m-i-j}}\frac{1}{n-m+i+j}={{m-j}\choose{i}}\frac{{{n-1}\choose{m-i-j}}\frac{1}{n-m+i+j}}{{{m-j}\choose{i}}}\leq{{m-j}\choose{i}}n^{n-m+j-1}

and, using Lemma
4.16
, let us estimate

(4.81)

\beta_{i}(m-j,d)\leq 2(4d)^{n-m+i+j}.

Using (
4.80
) and (
4.81
) into (
4.79
) we get

(4.82)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{T}(A_{j},r))}{\mathrm{vol}(B(p,s))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2v_{n}}{J_{n,n}(s)}\sum_{i=0}^{m-j}{{m-j}\choose{i}}n^{n-m+j-1}(\sin r)^{n-m+i+j}(\sin(r+s)^{\prime})^{m-i-j}(4d)^{n-m+i+j}

(4.83)

\displaystyle=\frac{2v_{n}}{J_{n,n}(s)}n^{n-m+j-1}(4d)^{n-m+j}(\sin r)^{n-m+j}\left(4d\sin r+\sin(r+s)^{\prime}\right)^{m-j}

(4.84)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2v_{n}}{(\sin s)^{n}}n^{n-m+j}(4d)^{n-m+j}(\sin r)^{n-m+j}\left(4d\sin r+\sin(r+s)^{\prime}\right)^{m-j},

where in the last inequality we have used Lemma
4.4
.

From this we see that

(4.85)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{T}(A_{0},r))}{\mathrm{vol}(B(p,s))}+\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{T}(A_{1},r))}{\mathrm{vol}(B(p,s))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2v_{n}n^{n-m}(4d)^{n-m}(\sin r)^{n-m}}{(\sin s)^{n}}\left(4d\sin r+\sin(r+s)^{\prime}\right)^{m-1}\cdot

(4.86)

\displaystyle\quad\cdot(4d\sin r+\sin(r+s)^{\prime}+n4d(\sin r))

(4.87)

\displaystyle=\frac{2v_{n}n^{n-m}(4d)^{n-m}(\sin r)^{n-m}}{(\sin s)^{n}}\left(4d\sin r+\sin(r+s)^{\prime}\right)^{m-1}\cdot

(4.88)

\displaystyle\quad\cdot((n+1)4d\sin r+\sin(r+s)^{\prime})

(4.89)

\displaystyle\leq 2v_{n}\left(\frac{4nd\sin r}{\sin s}\right)^{n-m}\left((n+1)4d\frac{\sin r}{\sin s}+\frac{\sin(r+s)^{\prime}}{\sin s}\right)^{m}=(*).

We observe now that for every
0\leq r,s\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
we have
\sin(r+s)^{\prime}\leq\sin r+\sin s
(Lemma
4.19
) and therefore:

(4.90)

\displaystyle(*)

\displaystyle=2v_{n}\left(\frac{n4d\sin r}{\sin s}\right)^{n-m}\left((n+1)4d\frac{\sin r}{\sin s}+\frac{\sin s+\sin r}{\sin s}\right)^{m}

(4.91)

\displaystyle\leq 2v_{n}\left(\frac{n4d\sin r}{\sin s}\right)^{n-m}\left(1+(4nd+4d+1)\frac{\sin r}{\sin s}\right)^{m}.

This proves the first part of the statement.

If now
\sin r\leq\frac{\sin s}{(4nd+4d+1)m}
, then

(4.92)

\left(1+(4nd+4d+1)\frac{\sin r}{\sin s}\right)^{m}\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{m}\right)^{m}\leq e

and the second part follows from (
4.91
).
∎

It remains to prove the lemma that we used in the proof.

Lemma 4.19
.

For every
0\leq r,s\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
we have
\sin\left(\min\{r+s,\frac{\pi}{2}\}\right)\leq\sin r+\sin s.

Proof.

If
r+s\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
, then

(4.93)

\sin\left(\min\left\{r+s,\frac{\pi}{2}\right\}\right)=\sin(r+s)=\sin r\cos s+\sin s\cos r\leq\sin r+\sin s.

If
r+s\geq\frac{\pi}{2}
, say
r\geq\frac{\pi}{2}-s
, then

(4.94)

\displaystyle\sin\left(\min\left\{r+s,\frac{\pi}{2}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)=1

(4.95)

\displaystyle=(\sin s)^{2}+(\cos s)^{2}\underset{0\leq\sin s,\cos s\leq 1}{\leq}\sin s+\cos s

(4.96)

\displaystyle=\sin s+\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-s\right)\leq\sin s+\sin r.

∎

4.2.
The general spherical case

We are now ready to give the proof of the bound for the general case in the sphere.

Theorem 4.20
.

Let
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}[X_{0},\ldots,X_{n}]
be a finite set of homogeneous polynomials of degree bounded by
\delta
and
Z\subset S^{n}
be their common zero set. Assume
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(Z)\leq m.
Given
p\in S^{n}
and
\sigma>0

be a uniformly distributed point. Then, for every

(4.97)

\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{dist}(x,Z)\leq\varepsilon)\leq 2\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{vol}(S^{n})}{2}\right)\left(\frac{8n\delta\sin\varepsilon}{\sin\sigma}\right)^{n-m}\left(1+(8n\delta+8\delta+1)\frac{\sin\varepsilon}{\sin\sigma}\right)^{m}.

In particular, if
\sin\varepsilon\leq\frac{\sin\sigma}{(8n\delta+8\delta+1)m}
,

(4.98)

\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{dist}(x,Z)\leq\varepsilon)\leq 2e\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{vol}(S^{n})}{2}\right)\left(\frac{8n\delta\sin\varepsilon}{\sin\sigma}\right)^{n-m}.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of Theorem
3.2
. Let
0\leq\varepsilon<\frac{\pi}{2}
(the case
\varepsilon=\frac{\pi}{2}
follows by a limit argument).

By part (B) of Theorem
2.10
there exists a one parameter family of real algebraic sets
\{Z_{t}\}_{t>0}
in the sphere
S^{n}
and
t_{0}>0
such that for all
0<t<t_{0}
the set
Z_{t}
is a complete intersection defined by homogeneous polynomials
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n-m}
of degree bounded by
2\delta
and such that:

(4.99)

\lim_{t\to 0}Z_{t}\supseteq Z.

Applying Theorem
2.3
with the choices
X=B=S^{n}
and
\{C_{t}\}_{t>0}=\{Z_{t}\}_{t>0}
, for every
0<\tau<\frac{\pi}{2}-\varepsilon

such that for all

(4.100)

\mathcal{U}(Z,\varepsilon)\cap B(p,\sigma)\subseteq\mathcal{U}(Z_{t},\varepsilon+\tau)\cap B(p,\sigma).

(4.101)

\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{dist}(x,Z)\leq\varepsilon)\leq 2\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{vol}(S^{n})}{2}\right)\left(\frac{8n\delta\sin(\varepsilon+\tau)}{\sin\sigma}\right)^{n-m}\left(1+(8n\delta+8\delta+1)\frac{\sin(\varepsilon+\tau)}{\sin\sigma}\right)^{m}.

).
∎

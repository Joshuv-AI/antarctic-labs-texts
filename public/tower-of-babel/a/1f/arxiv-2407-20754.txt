Cost-Based Semantics for Querying Inconsistent Weighted Knowledge Bases
Meghyn Bienvenu, Camille Bourgaux, Robin Jean

Abstract
In this paper, we explore a quantitative approach to querying inconsistent description logic knowledge bases. We consider weighted knowledge bases in which both axioms and assertions have (possibly infinite) weights, which are used to assign a cost to each interpretation based upon the axioms and assertions it violates. Two notions of certain and possible answer are defined by either considering interpretations whose cost does not exceed a given bound or restricting attention to optimal-cost interpretations. Our main contribution is a comprehensive analysis of the combined and data complexity of bounded cost satisfiability and certain and possible answer recognition, for description logics between ℰ ​ ℒ ⊥ \mathcal{EL}_{\bot} and 𝒜 ​ ℒ ​ 𝒞 ​ 𝒪 \mathcal{ALCO} .

Introduction

Ontology-mediated query answering (OMQA) is a framework for improving data access through the use of an ontology,
which has been extensively studied by the KR and database communities

[
2008
,
2015
,
2018
]
.
Much of the work on OMQA considers ontologies formulated in
description logics (DLs)
[
2017
]
. In the DL setting, OMQA consists in finding
the answers that are logically entailed from the knowledge base (KB),
consisting of the ABox (data) and TBox (ontology). Due to the use of classical
first-order semantics, whereby everything is entailed from a contradiction,
classical OMQA semantics fails to provide informative answers
when the KB is inconsistent.

The issue of handling inconsistencies, or more generally unwanted consequences,
in DL KBs has been explored from many angles. One solution is to modify the KB
in order to render it consistent, and there has been significant research on how to
aid users in the debugging process, e.g. by generating
justifications that pinpoint the sources of the inconsistency

[
2005
,
2020
]
.
This line of work mostly focuses on helping knowledge engineers to debug the
TBox before deployment in applications, but
some recent work specifically target ABoxes
[
2022
]
.
However, in an OMQA setting, where
the ABox can be very large and subject to frequent updates,
it is unrealistic to assume that we can always restore consistency.
This has motivated a substantial line of research on
inconsistency-tolerant semantics to obtain
meaningful answers from inconsistent KBs, surveyed in
[
2016
,
2020
]
.
Many of these semantics are based upon repairs, defined
as inclusion-maximal subsets of the ABox that are consistent w.r.t. the TBox.
Two of the most commonly considered repair semantics are the AR semantics
[
2010
]
,
which asks for those answers that hold in every repair, and the brave semantics
[
2013
]
,
which considers those answers that hold in at least one repair.
Note that the work on repair-based semantics typically assumes
that the TBox is reliable,
which is why repairs are subsets of the ABox, with the TBox left untouched.
A notable exception is the work of
?
(
?
), which considers generalized notions of repair for existential rule ontologies composed of hard and soft rules,
in which contradictions may be resolved by removing or minimally violating soft rules.

In this paper, we explore a novel quantitative approach to querying
inconsistent description logic KBs, which combines the idea of soft ontology axioms
from
[
2016
]
with a recent cost-based approach to repairing
databases w.r.t. soft constraints
[
2021
]
.
The idea is to associate with every TBox
axiom and ABox assertion a (possibly infinite) weight.
‘Hard’ axioms and assertions, which
must be satisfied, are assigned a weight of
\infty
, and
the remaining ‘soft’ axioms and assertions
are assigned weights based upon their reliability, with
higher weights indicating greater trust.
The cost of an interpretation is defined by
taking into account the number of violations of an axiom (assertions can be violated at most once)
and the weights of the violated axioms and assertions.
When determining the query answers, we shall use
the cost to select a set of interpretations,
either by considering all interpretations whose cost is below a given threshold, or considering only
those interpretations having an optimal (i.e. minimum) cost.
We shall then consider both the certain answers,
which hold in all of the selected interpretations,
and the possible answers, which hold w.r.t. at least one selected interpretation.
When restricted to consistent KBs, the optimal-cost certain and possible semantics coincide
with the classical certain and possible answer semantics, cf.
[
2024
]
.
By varying the cost bounds, we can identify answers that are robust (i.e. hold not
only in all optimal-cost interpretations but also ‘close-to-optimal’ ones) or rank
candidate answers based upon their incompatibility with the KB.

We perform a comprehensive analysis of the complexity of the main
decision problems in our setting, namely, bounded-cost satisfiability of weighted KBs and
recognition of certain and possible answers w.r.t. the
set of
k
-cost-bounded or optimal-cost interpretations.
Our study covers lightweight and expressive description logics,
ranging from
\mathcal{EL}_{\bot}
to
\mathcal{ALCO}
, and
queries given either as instance (IQs) or conjunctive queries (CQs).
We consider both the combined and data complexity measures,
as well as the impact of unary and binary encodings of the cost bound and weights.

3
Weighted Knowledge Bases

We consider a quantitative way of integrating the notion of soft constraints by giving weights to axioms and assertions. Intuitively, these weights represent penalties associated to each violation of the axioms or assertions. They will allow us to assign a cost to interpretations based upon the axioms and assertions they violate, and use this cost to select which interpretations to consider when answering queries.

Definition 1
.

A
weighted knowledge base
(WKB)
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}=(\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle,\omega)
consists of
a knowledge base
\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle

and a cost function
\omega:\mathcal{T}\cup\mathcal{A}\mapsto\mathbb{N}_{>0}\cup\{+\infty\}
.
We denote by
\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
(resp.
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
) the set of TBox axioms (resp. ABox assertions) that have an infinite cost and let
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}=\langle\mathcal{T}_{\infty},\mathcal{A}_{\infty}\rangle
. We sometimes use
\omega_{\chi}
as a shorthand for
\omega(\chi)
.

Example 1
.

Consider the following WKB about visa requirements to enter some country
c
:
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}=(\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle,\omega)
where
\mathcal{T}=\{\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3}\}
,
\mathcal{A}=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\}
and

\displaystyle\tau_{1}=

\displaystyle\mathsf{Visa}\sqcap\mathsf{NoVisa}\sqsubseteq\bot

\displaystyle\omega(\tau_{1})=\infty

\displaystyle\tau_{2}=

\displaystyle\exists\mathsf{hasNat}.\{c\}\sqcap\exists\mathsf{hasNat}.\{b\}\sqsubseteq\bot

\displaystyle\omega(\tau_{2})=\infty

\displaystyle\tau_{3}=

\displaystyle\forall\mathsf{hasNat}.\neg\{c\}\sqsubseteq\mathsf{Visa}

\displaystyle\omega(\tau_{3})=1

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}=

\displaystyle\mathsf{hasNat}(p,b)

\displaystyle\omega(\alpha_{1})=1

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}=

\displaystyle\mathsf{NoVisa}(p)

\displaystyle\omega(\alpha_{2})=2

Two ‘absolute’ constraints
\tau_{1}

To measure how far an interpretation is from being a model of the KB, we rely on the following sets of

Definition 2
.

The
set of violations of a concept inclusion

The

Definition 3
.

Let

The

We say that
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}

\mathcal{K}_{\omega}\models_{c}^{k}q
if

The preceding result shows, unsurprisingly, that

-cost-bounded semantics are only informative for

Increasing

leads to fewer and fewer queries being entailed under the
k
-cost-bounded certain semantics, which may be useful in identifying query answers that are robust in the sense that they continue to hold even if we consider a larger set of ‘close-to-optimal’ interpretations. By contrast, as
k

k
-cost-bounded possible semantics. Being quite permissive, the opt-cost and
k
-cost-bounded possible semantics will entail many queries, and thus are not suitable replacements for standard (certain answer) querying semantics.
Instead, non-entailment under these semantics can serve to eliminate or rank candidate tuples of individuals (or the corresponding instantiated Boolean queries) based upon how incompatible they are w.r.t. the expressed information.

Let

Complexity Measures

It is customary to consider
combined complexity
and
data complexity
when studying decision problems related to query answering over DL KBs. Data complexity considers only the size of the ABox while combined complexity takes into account the size of the whole input. In the case of WKBs, we consider the assertion weights as part of the ABox and inclusion weights as part of the TBox. We will use the following notation: given a WKB
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}=(\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle,\omega)
,
|\mathcal{A}|
(resp.
|\mathcal{T}|
,
|\mathcal{K}|
) is the length of the string representing
\mathcal{A}
(resp.
\mathcal{T}
,
\mathcal{K}
), where elements of
\mathsf{N_{C}}
,
\mathsf{N_{R}}
and
\mathsf{N_{I}}
are considered of length one, and
|\mathcal{A}_{\omega}|
(resp.
|\mathcal{T}_{\omega}|
,
|\mathcal{K}_{\omega}|
) is the length of the string representing the set
\{(\alpha,\omega(\alpha))\mid\alpha\in\mathcal{A}\}
(resp.
\{(\tau,\omega(\tau))\mid\tau\in\mathcal{T}\}
,
\{(\chi,\omega(\chi))\mid\chi\in\mathcal{T}\cup\mathcal{A}\}
), where elements of
\mathsf{N_{C}}
,
\mathsf{N_{R}}
and
\mathsf{N_{I}}
are considered of length one and weights are encoded either in unary or in binary. Note that if the TBox contains qualified number restrictions, the numbers can also be encoded in unary or binary. We will also make this encoding distinction for the integer
k
taken as input by some of the decision problems we consider. If
|k|
denotes the size of the encoding of
k
,
k=|k|
when encoded in unary and

when encoded in binary. Finally, for a BCQ

is the length of the string representing

where elements of
\mathsf{N_{C}}
,
\mathsf{N_{R}}
,
\mathsf{N_{I}}
and variables are considered of size one. Note that when we use
|\cdot|
over a set which is not a (weighted) ABox or TBox, we simply means the set cardinality.

Lemma 1

Let

Data Complexity

We now turn our attention to the data complexity of the decision problems we consider.
The second line of Table

gives an overview of the results.
Recall that data complexity takes only into account the size of the weighted ABox

.
In particular, for problems that have an integer

|\mathcal{A}_{\omega}|
and

at the end of the section.

5.1
Upper Bounds

To obtain the data complexity upper bounds, our general approach is to guess a ‘small’ interpretation of bounded or optimal cost, that satisfies or does not satisfy the query.
It follows from Proposition
5
that if
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
is satisfiable,
then
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
admits an interpretation of finite cost whose domain
is linearly bounded in the size of the ABox.
The following proposition shows that there is always such a bounded-cardinality interpretation which is also of optimal cost.

Proposition 6
.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}=(\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle,\omega)
be an
\mathcal{ALCO}
WKB and
k
an integer. If there exists an interpretation
\mathcal{I}
with
\mathit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{I})\leq k
, then there is
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
with
\mathit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime})\leq k
and
|\Delta^{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}|\leq|\mathsf{Ind}(\mathcal{K})|+2^{|\mathcal{T}|}
.

The upper data complexity bound for bounded cost satisfiability follows directly.

Theorem 8
.

BCS for
\mathcal{ALCO}
is in
NP
in data complexity (even with a binary encoding of weights).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}=(\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle,\omega)
be an
\mathcal{ALCO}
WKB and
k
an integer.
By Proposition
6
, guessing an interpretation
\mathcal{I}
of cardinality at most
|\mathsf{Ind}(\mathcal{K})|+2^{|\mathcal{T}|}
and checking if
\mathit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{I})\leq k
(which can be done in a polynomial time w.r.t
|\mathcal{A}_{\omega}|
) is an
NP
procedure to decide BCS.
∎

To obtain the results for query entailment, we need to refine Proposition
6
to preserve also query (non) entailment.

Proposition 7
.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}=(\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle,\omega)
be an
\mathcal{ALCO}
WKB,
k
an integer and
q
a BCQ.
If there exists an interpretation
\mathcal{I}
with
\mathit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{I})\leq k
and
\mathcal{I}\models q
, then there is one whose domain has cardinality at most
|\mathsf{Ind}(\mathcal{K})|+2^{|\mathcal{T}|}
.

Preserving query non-entailment is more complex.
Contrary to the proofs of Propositions
6
and
7
that build on the notion of filtration used to show the bounded model property of
\mathcal{ALC(O)}
,
the following proposition relies on a non-trivial adaptation of constructions introduced in the context of counting queries

[
2022
,
2022
]
. The latter work shows how to convert a (potentially infinite)
interpretation into a finite one while avoiding the introduction of new query matches.
In our case, we must further prevent new violations of soft TBox axioms.

Proposition 8
.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}=(\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle,\omega)
be an
\mathcal{ALCO}
WKB,
k
an integer, and
q
a BCQ. If there exists an interpretation
\mathcal{I}
with
\mathit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{I})\leq k
and

,
then there is one whose domain has cardinality that is bounded polynomially in

and

(with
|\mathcal{T}|
and
|q|
treated as constants).

We are now ready to prove the data complexity upper bounds for the
k
-cost-bounded semantics.

Theorem 9
.

CQA
{}^{b}_{p}
for
\mathcal{ALCO}

NP
in data complexity and CQA
{}^{b}_{c}
for
\mathcal{ALCO}
is in co

in data complexity (even if the weights are encoded in binary).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}=(\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle,\omega)
be an
\mathcal{ALCO}
WKB,
k
an integer, and
q
a BCQ.
For CQA
{}^{b}_{p}

that it suffices to guess an interpretation

whose domain has cardinality at most

and check that

We start by showing that bounded cost satisfiability is
NP

NP
-hardness of cardinality query answering

Theorem 12
.

BCS for
\mathcal{EL}_{\bot}
is
NP
-hard in data complexity (even with a unary encoding of weights).

Proof.

We reduce the 3-colorability problem to BCS. Let
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
be a graph, and define
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}=(\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle,\omega)
with:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}=

\displaystyle\{\exists R.C_{i}\sqcap\exists E.(\exists R.C_{i})\sqsubseteq B\mid 1\leq i\leq 3\}\cup

\displaystyle\{A\sqsubseteq\exists R.B,B\sqsubseteq\bot\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=

\displaystyle\{A(v)\mid v\in\mathcal{V}\}\cup\{E(v_{1},v_{2})\mid\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\in\mathcal{E}\}\cup

\displaystyle\{C_{1}(c_{1}),C_{2}(c_{2}),C_{3}(c_{3}),B(c_{1}),B(c_{2}),B(c_{3})\}

\omega(B\sqsubseteq\bot)=1
and
\omega(\chi)=\infty
for all other
\chi\in\mathcal{T}\cup\mathcal{A}
.

We show that
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
is 3-satisfiable iff
\mathcal{G}
is 3-colorable.

(
\Leftarrow
) If
\mathcal{G}
is 3-colorable, let
\rho:\mathcal{V}\mapsto\{c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\}
be a 3-coloring of
\mathcal{G}
and let
\mathcal{I}_{\rho}
be the interpretation that satisfies exactly the assertions of
\mathcal{A}\cup\{R(v,\rho(v))\mid v\in\mathcal{V}\}
. We show that
\mathcal{I}_{\rho}\models\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
, so that
\mathit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{I}_{\rho})=|B^{\mathcal{I}_{\rho}}|=3
.
By construction,
\mathcal{I}_{\rho}\models\mathcal{A}
and
since
A^{\mathcal{I}_{\rho}}=\mathcal{V}
, the
R(v,\rho(v))
assertions ensure that
\mathcal{I}_{\rho}\models A\sqsubseteq\exists R.B
. Moreover, by definition of
\rho
, there is no monochromatic edge, which ensures that
(\exists R.C_{i}\sqcap\exists E.(\exists R.C_{i}))^{\mathcal{I}_{\rho}}=\emptyset
for
i\in\{1,2,3\}
.

(
\Rightarrow
) If
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
is 3-satisfiable, since
\{c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\}\subseteq B^{\mathcal{I}}
for any
\mathcal{I}
of finite cost, then there exists
\mathcal{I}
such that
\mathit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{I})=3
and
B^{\mathcal{I}}=\{c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\}
.
For every
v\in\mathcal{V}
, since
\mathcal{I}\models A(v)
and
\mathcal{I}\models A\sqsubseteq\exists R.B
, then there exists
c\in\{c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\}
such that
(v,c)\in R^{\mathcal{I}}
.
Define a coloring
\rho:\mathcal{V}\mapsto\{c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\}
by arbitrarily selecting one such
c
per
v
:
(v,\rho(v))\in R^{\mathcal{I}}
for each
v\in\mathcal{V}
. We show that
\rho
is a 3-coloring of
\mathcal{G}
. Otherwise, if there was an edge
(v_{1},v_{2})
such that
\rho(v_{1})=\rho(v_{2})=c_{i}
, then
\mathcal{I}\models E(v_{1},v_{2})\wedge R(v_{1},c_{i})\wedge R(v_{2},c_{i})\wedge C_{i}(c_{i})
, i.e.,
v_{1}\in(\exists R.C_{i}\sqcap\exists E.(\exists R.C_{i}))^{\mathcal{I}}
. It would follow that
v_{1}\in B^{\mathcal{I}}
and
\mathit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{I})\geq 4
, contradicting
\mathit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{I})=3
.
∎

A direct adaptation of the last proof gives
NP
and co
NP
lower bounds for IQ (and thus CQ) answering under the
k
-cost-bounded semantics.

Theorem 13
.

IQA
{}^{b}_{p}
(resp. IQA
{}^{b}_{c}
) for
\mathcal{EL}_{\bot}
is
NP
-hard (resp. co
NP
-hard) in data complexity (even with a unary encoding of weights).

Proof.

Given a graph
\mathcal{G}
, consider the WKB
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
defined in the proof of Theorem

6
Related Work

Our cost-based framework for reasoning on inconsistent KBs shares features
with a number of existing formalisms.
Our axioms with finite weights
can be seen as quantitative versions of the soft rules considered in
[
2016
]
.
More generally, the idea of allowing TBox axioms to be (exceptionally) violated
shares high-level similarities with defeasible axioms, minimized concepts, and typicality operators
considered in non-monotonic extensions of DLs, cf.
[
2009
,
2013
,
2021
]
.
Although we differ in adopting
a quantitative
semantics for axiom violations,
we were nevertheless able to
import some
techniques from
circumscription
[
2023
]

and reasoning with closed predicates
[
2016
]
.

Our approach is related to existing quantitative notions of repair. Indeed, our
assignment of costs to interpretations
can be seen as adapting the database repairs for soft constraints from
[
2021
]

to the DL setting.
As detailed in Section
3
,
our approach is also
related to weight-based ABox repairs:
when TBox axioms have weight
\infty
,
our opt-cost certain semantics coincides with the
\leq_{\omega}
-AR semantics from
[
2014
]
.

Our proofs rely upon techniques devised for other forms of quantitative reasoning in DLs.
We exploit results on counting and cardinality queries
[
2022
,
2022
]

and reduce some of our problems to reasoning in DLs with number restrictions.
We could also have obtained some of our combined complexity upper bounds
by using results on
cardinality constraints

\leq nC
(which enforce that
|C^{\mathcal{I}}|\leq n
),
previously studied in
[
2000
]
and expressible in the
much more expressive quantitative logics in
[
2020
]
.
We expect that the techniques from these works
may prove useful in future studies of WKBs.

7
Conclusion

We have introduced a new cost-based framework to querying inconsistent DL KBs,
in which both TBox axioms and ABox assertions may be violated and notions of
possible and certain query answers
are defined w.r.t. the bounded or minimal-cost interpretations.
By exploiting connections to other DL reasoning tasks,
we were able to establish
an almost complete picture of the complexity of query answering in our
framework, for DLs between
\mathcal{EL}_{\bot}
and
\mathcal{ALCO}
.

The main aim of future work will be to extend our results to cover DLs involving other common constructs, like
inverse roles (
\mathcal{I}
), role inclusions (
\mathcal{H}
), functional roles (
\mathcal{F}
), and number restrictions (
\mathcal{Q}
).
We will focus first on the DL-Lite family due to its widespread use in OMQA.
While it is not difficult to adapt the definitions of violations and WKBs (although there may be several ways to count violations of a functionality axiom, as one could count as a single violation a domain element that has multiple successors or instead count every pair of two distinct successors), the techniques underlying our upper bounds do not readily transfer.
For example, violations of role inclusions cannot be encoded as concepts (at least in standard DLs),
and adding functionality or number restrictions may lead to
every optimal-cost interpretation having an infinite domain.
Inverse roles (present in even the simplest DL-Lite logics)
are also non-trivial to handle,
though we expect that
the techniques
employed for our data complexity upper bounds can be suitably adapted. Finally, another important but challenging direction is to devise practical algorithms that are amenable to implementation.

\displaystyle\mathrm{\top}\sqsubseteq\mathrm{D}\qquad\mathrm{A}\sqsubseteq\mathrm{D}\qquad\mathrm{\{a\}}\sqsubseteq\mathrm{A}\qquad\mathrm{A_{1}\sqcap A_{2}}\sqsubseteq\mathrm{A}

\displaystyle\mathrm{\exists\mathrm{R.B}}\sqsubseteq\mathrm{A}\qquad\mathrm{A}\sqsubseteq\mathrm{\exists\mathrm{R.B}}\qquad\mathrm{\lnot B}\sqsubseteq\mathrm{A}\qquad\mathrm{A}\sqsubseteq\mathrm{\lnot B}

where
A,A_{1},A_{2},B\in\mathsf{N_{C}}
,
D\in\mathsf{N_{C}}\cup\{\{a\}\mid\mathsf{N_{I}}\}
, and
R\in\mathsf{N_{R}}
.
It is well known that an arbitrary
\mathcal{ALCO}
TBox can be transformed (possibly through introduction of new concept names)
into an
\mathcal{ALCO}
TBox in normal form. As the transformation may possibly introduce new concept names,
the new TBox may not be equivalent to the original one, but it will be a conservative extension,
which is sufficient for our purposes.

We now proceed as follows to convert our initial WKB into a KB in normal form:

1.

For every

\tau=C\sqsubseteq D\in\mathcal{T}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
,
let
A_{\tau}
be a fresh concept name (i.e. occurring neither in
\mathcal{K}
nor
q
), and set

\mathcal{T}_{\tau}=\{A_{\tau}\sqsubseteq C\sqcap\neg D,C\sqcap\neg D\sqsubseteq A_{\tau}\}

2.

Apply the normalization procedure to the TBox

\mathcal{T}_{\infty}^{vio}=\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\cup\bigcup_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\infty}}\mathcal{T}_{\tau}

and let
\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}
denote the resulting TBox. We can assume w.l.o.g. that all concept names in
\mathsf{sig}(\mathcal{T}^{\dagger})\setminus\mathsf{sig}(\mathcal{T}_{\infty}^{vio})

occur neither in
q
nor
\mathcal{K}
.

We set
\mathcal{K}^{\dagger}=\langle\mathcal{T}^{\dagger},\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}\rangle
, where

\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}=\{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}\mid\mathcal{I}\models\alpha\}\cup\{A_{\top}(a)\mid a\in\mathsf{Ind}(\mathcal{A})\}

with
A_{\top}
a fresh concept, not in
\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}
nor
q
.
Note that the assertions
A_{\top}(a)
simply serve to ensure that no individuals from
\mathcal{K}

are lost during the translation (which will be convenient for the later constructions and proofs).

The following lemma summarizes the properties of
\mathcal{K}^{\dagger}
. Items (ii)-(iv) basically correspond to

\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}
being a conservative extension of
\mathcal{T}_{\infty}^{vio}
, while (v) is a direct consequence
of (iii) and the definition of
\mathcal{T}_{\infty}^{vio}
.

Lemma 5
.

The KB
\mathcal{K}^{\dagger}
satisfies the following:

(i)

\mathsf{Ind}(\mathcal{K})=\mathsf{Ind}(\mathcal{K}^{\dagger})

(ii)

\mathsf{sig}(\mathcal{T})\subseteq\mathsf{sig}(\mathcal{T}_{\infty}^{vio})\subseteq\mathsf{sig}(\mathcal{T}^{\dagger})
;

(iii)

every model of
\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}
is a model of
\mathcal{T}_{\infty}^{vio}
;

(iv)

for every model
\mathcal{I}_{1}
of
\mathcal{T}_{\infty}^{vio}
, there exists a model
\mathcal{I}_{2}
of
\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}

with
\Delta^{\mathcal{I}_{1}}=\Delta^{\mathcal{I}_{2}}
such that

\cdot^{\mathcal{I}_{2}}
and
\cdot^{\mathcal{I}_{1}}
coincide on all concept and role names except those in
\mathsf{sig}(\mathcal{T}^{\dagger})\setminus\mathsf{sig}(\mathcal{T}_{\infty}^{vio})
;

(v)

for every
\tau\in\mathcal{T}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
and every model
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{K}^{\dagger}
,

vio_{\tau}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime})=A_{\tau}^{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}
.

Using the preceding lemma, we can modify our original interpretation
\mathcal{I}

to get a model of the KB
\mathcal{K}^{\dagger}
:

Lemma 6
.

There exists a model
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}
of
\mathcal{K}^{\dagger}
with
\Delta^{\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}}=\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}

such that
\cdot^{\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}}
and
\cdot^{\mathcal{I}}
coincide on
\mathsf{sig}(\mathcal{K})\cup\mathsf{sig}(q)
.
In particular, this means that:

•

vio_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{I}^{\dagger})=vio_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{I})

•

for every
\tau\in\mathcal{T}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
:
vio_{\tau}(\mathcal{I}^{\dagger})=vio_{\tau}(\mathcal{I})

•

\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}\not\models q

Proof.

We first create an interpretation
\mathcal{I}^{\diamond}
which is the same as
\mathcal{I}
except for the
concept names
A_{\tau}\in\mathsf{sig}[\mathcal{T}_{\infty}^{vio}]\setminus\mathsf{sig}(\mathcal{T})
, for which we
set
(A_{\tau})^{\mathcal{I}^{\diamond}}=(C\sqcap\neg D)^{\mathcal{I}}
, and the concept name
A_{\top}

which we interpret as
\mathsf{Ind}(\mathcal{A})
(note that we may assume
\mathsf{Ind}(\mathcal{A})\subseteq\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}
due to the weak SNA).
This ensures that
\mathcal{I}^{\diamond}
satisfies the axioms in
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}
and the new assertions in
\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}
,
and since we have not modified the interpretation of any other symbols,

\mathcal{I}^{\diamond}
will be a model of both
\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}
.
Thus, the interpretation
\mathcal{I}^{\diamond}

is a model of
\langle\mathcal{T}_{\infty}^{vio},\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}\rangle
.
Hence, by Lemma
5
(iv), there exists a model
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}
of
\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}

which can be obtained from
\mathcal{I}^{\diamond}
solely by changing the interpretation of concept names in

\mathsf{sig}(\mathcal{T}^{\dagger})\setminus\mathsf{sig}(\mathcal{T}_{\infty}^{vio})
. In particular,
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}
is a model of
\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}

and hence of the KB
\mathcal{K}^{\dagger}
. It follows from the construction of
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}
that

\Delta^{\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}}=\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}
and that
\cdot^{\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}}
and
\cdot^{\mathcal{I}}
coincide on
\mathsf{sig}(\mathcal{K})\cup\mathsf{sig}(q)
.
The first and third items follow immediately (for the third item, recall that
\mathcal{I}\not\models q
), and
the second item holds due to
(A_{\tau})^{\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}}=(A_{\tau})^{\mathcal{I}^{\diamond}}=(C\sqcap\neg D)^{\mathcal{I}}
.
∎

As the model
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}
of
\mathcal{K}^{\dagger}
given in Lemma
6

satisfies the same key properties as
\mathcal{I}
(i.e. not satisfying
q
and having cost
\leq k
),
it can thus be used in place of
\mathcal{I}
in the following constructions.

Interlacing construction to regularize the interpretation

The second step will be to apply the interlacing construction from
[
2022
]

to our interpretation
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}
to obtain a more well-structured interpretation
that retains the essential properties of
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}
, i.e. it does not entail
q
and its cost does not exceed
k
.

The interlacing construction starts with the definition of the existential
extraction, which is a tree-shaped domain. The definition, which we recall next,
uses the alphabet
\Omega
consisting of all
\mathrm{R.A}
such that
\exists\mathrm{R.A}
is the RHS of an axiom in
the considered TBox (
\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}
in our case),
and for every
\mathrm{R.A}\in\Omega
, a function
\mathsf{succ}^{\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}}_{\mathrm{R.A}}

that maps every element
e\in(\exists\mathrm{R.A})^{\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}}
to an element
e^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}}
such that
(e,e^{\prime})\in\mathrm{R}^{\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}}
and
e^{\prime}\in\mathrm{A}^{\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}}
.

Definition 7

(Existential extraction)
.

Build the following mapping inductively over the set
\mathsf{Ind}(\mathcal{K}^{\dagger})\cdot\Omega^{*}
:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}f:\mathsf{Ind}(\mathcal{K}^{\dagger})\cdot\Omega^{*}&\rightarrow&\Delta^{\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}}\cup\{\uparrow\}\\
a&\mapsto&a\\
\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{rcl}w\cdot\mathrm{R.A}&\mapsto&\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\uparrow&\textrm{if }f(w)=\;\uparrow\\
&\textrm{ or }f(w)\notin(\exists\mathrm{R.A})^{\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}}\\
\mathsf{succ}^{\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}}_{\mathrm{R.A}}(f(w))&\textrm{otherwise}\end{array}\right.\end{array}

where
\uparrow
is a fresh symbol witnessing the absence of a proper image for an element of
\mathsf{Ind}(\mathcal{K}^{\dagger})\cdot\Omega^{*}
.
The
existential extraction
of
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}
is
\Delta^{\circ}:=\{w\mid w\in\mathsf{Ind}(\mathcal{K}^{\dagger})\cdot\Omega^{*},f(w)\neq\;\uparrow\}
.

The preceding definition only differs from the one in
[
2022
]
in one way: it uses the set
\mathsf{Ind}(\mathcal{K}^{\dagger})
of individuals in the considered KB rather than the set of individuals in the considered ABox. This is because we work with
\mathcal{ALCO}
(and thus individuals may appear in the TBox) whereas the original
construction was formulated for
\mathcal{ALCHI}
. Fortunately, although nominals were not considered in
[
2022
]
,
they are properly handled by the constructions and do not require any notable modifications.

One part of the construction that we will need to modify to suit our setting is
the domain of interest
\Delta^{*}
, which intuitively is the part of
\mathcal{I}
we wish to remain untouched:

\Delta^{*}:=\mathsf{Ind}(\mathcal{K}^{\dagger})\,\cup\,\bigcup_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\infty}}vio_{\tau}(\mathcal{I}^{\dagger})

Whereas the original definition of
\Delta^{*}
takes the set of ABox individuals together with all elements occurring
in a match of the input (counting) CQ, we must consider nominals from the TBox and all elements that occur in a violation of
an axiom in
\mathcal{T}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
. Note however that since in our case we start from an interpretation
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}
that does not
contain any matches for
q
, our set
\Delta^{*}
in fact is a superset of the one considered in
[
2022
]
.

We can now recall the function
f^{*}
, defined exactly as in
[
2022
]
but using the modified version of
\Delta^{*}
:

\begin{array}[]{cccl}f^{*}:&\Delta^{\circ}&\rightarrow&\Delta^{*}\uplus(\Delta^{\circ}\setminus\Delta^{*})\\[3.0pt]
&w&\mapsto&\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f(w)&\textrm{if }f(w)\in\Delta^{*}\\
w&\textrm{otherwise}\end{array}\right.\end{array}

With these notions in hand, we can now recall that definition of the interlacing of
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}
w.r.t. the function
f^{*}
:

Definition 8

(
f^{*}
-interlacing)
.

The
f^{*}
-interlacing

{\mathcal{I}}^{*}
of
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}
is the interpretation whose domain is

\Delta^{{\mathcal{I}}^{*}}:=f^{*}(\Delta^{\circ})
and which interprets concept and role names as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{A}^{{\mathcal{I}}^{*}}~:=~

\displaystyle\{f^{*}(u)\mid u\in\Delta^{\circ},f(u)\in\mathrm{A}^{\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}}\}

\displaystyle\mathrm{R}^{{\mathcal{I}}^{*}}~:=~

\displaystyle\{(a,b)\mid a,b\in\mathsf{Ind}(\mathcal{K}^{\dagger})\wedge\mathcal{K}^{\dagger}\models\mathrm{R}(a,b)\}

\displaystyle\cup~\{(f^{*}(u),f^{*}(u\cdot\mathrm{R.B}))\mid u,u\cdot\mathrm{R.B}\in\Delta^{\circ}\}

The preceding definition is a simplified version of Definition 20 from
[
2022
]
as we are not considering role inclusions nor inverse roles.
We also make a slight modification – using
\mathsf{Ind}(\mathcal{K}^{\dagger})
rather than only individuals from the ABox – to make it compatible with nominals.

We have phrased the definition directly in terms of our desired function
f^{*}
, but other functions
f^{\prime}
with domain
\Delta^{\circ}
can be used instead.
Depending on which
f^{\prime}
is used to define the interlacing,
the resulting interpretation
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
may or may not be a model of the considered KB.
It was shown in
[
2022
]
that if
f^{\prime}
is pseudo-injective, then the
f^{\prime}
-interleaving is a model
and moreover maps homomorphically into the starting interpretation.
This property (stated in Lemma
7
below, phrased for our KB
\mathcal{K}^{\dagger}
) was proven for
\mathcal{ALCHI}
KBs in normal form,
but is easily shown to also hold for

\mathcal{ALCO}
KBs in normal form.

Definition 9
.

A function
f^{\prime}:\Delta^{\circ}\rightarrow E
is
pseudo-injective
if: for all
u,v\in\Delta^{\circ}
, if
f^{\prime}(u)=f^{\prime}(v)
, then
f(u)=f(v)
.

Lemma 7
.

If
f^{\prime}:\Delta^{\circ}\rightarrow E
is pseudo-injective, then the
f^{\prime}
-interlacing
{\mathcal{I}}^{\prime}
is a model of
\mathcal{K}^{\dagger}
and the following mapping is a homomorphism from
{\mathcal{I}}^{\prime}
to
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}
:

\begin{array}[]{cccl}\sigma:&\Delta^{{\mathcal{I}}^{\prime}}&\rightarrow&\Delta^{\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}}\\
&f^{\prime}(u)&\mapsto&f(u)\end{array}

As
f^{\prime}
is pseudo-injective,
\sigma
is well defined.

It was proven in
[
2022
]
that
f^{*}
is pseudo-injective,
and the same arguments apply to
our modified
f^{*}
.

Lemma 8
.

f^{*}
is pseudo-injective.

It follows from the preceding lemmas that
{\mathcal{I}}^{*}
is a model of
\mathcal{K}^{\dagger}
which maps
homomorphically into
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}
via the mapping
\sigma
.
In fact, due to the way
f^{*}
is defined, we can be more precise about the homomorphism
\sigma
:

Lemma 9
.

The homomorphism
\sigma
from
{\mathcal{I}}^{*}
to
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}

is such that
\sigma(a)=a
for all
a\in\mathsf{Ind}(\mathcal{K}^{\dagger})
.

We are now ready to show that
{\mathcal{I}}^{*}
retains the desired properties of
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}
(and
\mathcal{I}
).

Lemma 10
.

{\mathcal{I}}^{*}\not\models q
.

Proof.

We could obtain the result by examining the proof of Lemma 5 in
[
2022
]
, which shows that
the query matches in
{\mathcal{I}}^{*}
(there denoted
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
and defined w.r.t. the original definition of
\Delta^{*}
) are injectively mapped into
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}

(and hence there cannot be any additional matches in
{\mathcal{I}}^{*}
). However, it turns out that
the argument is much simpler in our case, since we are start from an interpretation
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}
with
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}\not\models q
.
The existence of the homomorphism
\sigma
from
{\mathcal{I}}^{*}

to
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}
that is the identify on
\mathsf{Ind}(\mathcal{K}^{\dagger})
(see Lemmas
7
and
9
),
hence also on
\mathsf{Ind}(q)
,
means that any match of
q
in
{\mathcal{I}}^{*}
can be reproduced in
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}
.
Therefore, from
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}\not\models q
, we immediately obtain
{\mathcal{I}}^{*}\not\models q
.
∎

Lemma 11
.

The cost of
{\mathcal{I}}^{*}
does not exceed that of
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}
:

\mathit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}({\mathcal{I}}^{*})\leq\mathit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{I}^{\dagger})
. In particular, we have that

vio_{\tau}({\mathcal{I}}^{*})\subseteq vio_{\tau}(\mathcal{I}^{\dagger})\subseteq\Delta^{*}
for every
\tau\in\mathcal{T}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
.

Proof.

First note that since
{\mathcal{I}}^{*}
is a model of
\mathcal{K}^{\dagger}
, it is a model of
\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}
and hence satisfies all assertions in

\{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}\mid\mathcal{I}\models\alpha\}=\{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}\mid\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}\models\alpha\}
.
Since
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}\not\models\alpha
for
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}\setminus\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}
, it follows that

vio_{\mathcal{A}}({\mathcal{I}}^{*})\subseteq vio_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{I}^{\dagger})

Let us now consider violations of TBox axioms. First note that since

{\mathcal{I}}^{*}
is a model of
\mathcal{K}^{\dagger}
, it is a model of
\mathcal{T}_{\infty}^{vio}
(Lemma
5
, item (iii)).
It follows that
{\mathcal{I}}^{*}\models\tau
for every
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
.
Now consider some
\tau\in\mathcal{T}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
and suppose that
e\in vio_{\tau}({\mathcal{I}}^{*})
.
By Lemma
5
, item (v),
vio_{\tau}(\mathcal{I}^{*})=A_{\tau}^{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
, so
e\in A_{\tau}^{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
.
From the definition of
{\mathcal{I}}^{*}
, we know that there exists
w\in\Delta^{\circ}

such that
e=f^{*}(w)
.
As
\sigma
is a homomorphism from
{\mathcal{I}}^{*}
to
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}
(Lemma
7
) and
e=f^{*}(w)\in A_{\tau}^{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
, we must also have
f(w)\in A_{\tau}^{\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}}
.
As
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}
is a model of
\mathcal{K}^{\dagger}
, we can again apply Lemma
5
item (v)
to get
vio_{\tau}(\mathcal{I}^{\dagger})=A_{\tau}^{\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}}
. We thus obtain
f(w)\in vio_{\tau}(\mathcal{I}^{\dagger})
,
and thus
f(w)\in\Delta^{*}
(due to our definition of
\Delta^{*}
).
But
f(w)\in\Delta^{*}
implies that
e=f^{*}(w)=f(w)
, and hence that
e\in A_{\tau}^{\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}}
.
We have thus shown that

vio_{\tau}({\mathcal{I}}^{*})\subseteq vio_{\tau}(\mathcal{I}^{\dagger})
.

As every violation of an axiom or assertion from
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
in the interpretation
{\mathcal{I}}^{*}

is also present in
\mathcal{I}^{\dagger}
, we can conclude that

\mathit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}({\mathcal{I}}^{*})\leq\mathit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{I}^{\dagger})
.
∎

Quotient construction to get bounded-size interpretation

It now remains to ‘shrink’ the interpretation
{\mathcal{I}}^{*}
to obtain an interpretation
with the same properties but having the required size.
To do so, we can proceed exactly as in
Chapter 3.4 of
[
2022
]
, by defining a suitable equivalence relation,
and then considering the quotient interpretation obtained by merging elements from the same equivalence class.

We will now describe how the equivalence relation
\sim_{n}
is defined, but without giving every detail as
the definition is rather involved and we will not be modifying it except by
using our own definition of
\Delta^{*}
.
The definition of
\sim_{n}
involves the notion of the
n
-neighbourhood of an element
d
in
\mathcal{I}^{*}
relative to
\Delta^{*}
, denoted
\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{I}^{*},\Delta^{*}}_{n}(d)
,
consisting of the elements in
\Delta^{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
that can be reached by taking at most
n
‘steps’ along role edges, starting at
d

and stopping whenever an element of
\Delta^{*}
is reached.
If
d\in\Delta^{*}
, then
d
itself is the only element in its
n
-neighbourhood (for any
n
).
However, when
d\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\setminus\Delta^{*}
,
we know that
d=aw
for some
a\in\mathsf{Ind}(\mathcal{K}^{\dagger})
and
w\in\Omega^{*}
.
Using the tree-shaped structure of the domain
\Delta^{\circ}
,
we can identify a unique ‘root’ prefix
r_{n,d}
of
d=aw

such that
f^{*}(r_{n,d})\in\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{I}^{*},\Delta^{*}}_{n}(d)
and
for every
d^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{I}^{*},\Delta^{*}}_{n}(d)
, there is a unique word

w^{d^{\prime}}_{n,d}\in\Omega^{*}
such that
d^{\prime}=f^{*}(r_{n,d}\cdot w_{n,d}^{d^{\prime}})
and
|w^{d^{\prime}}_{n,d}|\leq 2n
.
With this uniform way of refer to the elements in neighbourhood of a considered element
d
,
we can define a function
\chi_{n,d}
whose output tells us for each word
w\in\Omega^{*}
with
|w|\leq 2n

whether there is an element in the neighbourhood whose word is
w
,
and if so, whether that element belongs to
\Delta^{*}
, and if not, which concept names it satisfies in
\mathcal{I}^{*}
.
The equivalence relation
\sim_{n}
then groups together those elements
d,e\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\setminus\Delta^{*}

which have the same associated word (i.e.
w^{d}_{n,d}=w^{e}_{n,e}
) and same associated function (
\chi_{n,d}=\chi_{n,e}
),
plus an additional condition on the length of
d
and
e
(namely,
|d|=|e|\textrm{ mod }2|q|+3
).

With the equivalence relation
\sim_{n}
at hand, it now
suffices to merge elements which are equivalent w.r.t.
\sim_{|q|+1}
.
Formally, we consider the quotient interpretation
\mathcal{J}:=\mathcal{I}^{*}/\sim_{|q|+1}

whose domain is
\Delta^{\mathcal{J}}=\{[e]_{\sim_{|q|+1}}\mid e\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\}

and whose interpretation function

\cdot^{\mathcal{J}}
is as follows:

•

A^{\mathcal{J}}=\{[e]_{\sim_{|q|+1}}\mid e\in A^{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\}

•

R^{\mathcal{J}}=\{([d]_{\sim_{|q|+1}},[e]_{\sim_{|q|+1}})\mid(d,e)\in R^{\mathcal{I}^{*}}\}

•

a^{\mathcal{J}}=[a^{\mathcal{I}^{*}}]_{\sim_{|q|+1}}

where
[e]_{\sim_{|q|+1}}
is the equivalence class of
e
w.r.t.
\sim_{|q|+1}
.

It has been shown that
\mathcal{J}
remains a model of the considered KB and does not contain
any additional query matches. Given that the interpretation
\mathcal{I}^{*}
we consider here is built in exactly
the same way as the interleaving
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
considered in
[
2022
]
, except that we work
with a larger domain of interest
\Delta^{*}
(which includes the one used to build
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
),
exactly the same arguments can be used to show Lemma
12
below.
We point out that the presence of nominals in
\mathcal{K}^{\dagger}
is not problematic
as all of its individuals (whether present in the ABox or TBox) are included in
\Delta^{*}
and are therefore left
untouched by the construction.

Lemma 12
.

The interpretation
\mathcal{J}
is a model of
\mathcal{K}^{\dagger}
such that
\mathcal{J}\not\models q
.

It remains to show that the quotient operation does not increase the cost.

Lemma 13
.

The cost of
\mathcal{J}
is no higher than the cost of
\mathcal{I}^{*}
:
\mathit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{J})\leq\mathit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}({\mathcal{I}}^{*})
.

Proof.

First note that since
\mathcal{J}
is a model of
\mathcal{K}^{\dagger}
, it satisfies all assertions in
\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}
.
It follows that
vio_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{J})\subseteq vio_{\mathcal{A}}({\mathcal{I}}^{*})
.
The fact that
\mathcal{J}
is a model of
\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}
means that it satisfies every
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
.
Consider now an axiom
\tau\in\mathcal{T}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
, and suppose that

e\in vio_{\tau}(\mathcal{J})
. Since
\mathcal{J}
is a model of
\mathcal{K}^{\dagger}
, we have

e\in A_{\tau}^{\mathcal{J}}
(recall Lemma
5
, item (v)).
It follows from the definition of
\mathcal{J}
that there exists

e^{*}
such that
e=[e^{*}]_{\sim_{|q|+1}}
and
e^{*}\in A_{\tau}^{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
.
However, since
\mathcal{I}^{*}
is also a model of
\mathcal{K}^{\dagger}
, we can infer from

e^{*}\in A_{\tau}^{\mathcal{I}^{*}}
that
e^{*}\in vio_{\tau}({\mathcal{I}}^{*})
.
By Lemma
11
,
we know that
vio_{\tau}({\mathcal{I}}^{*})\subseteq\Delta^{*}
.
As
e^{*}\in\Delta^{*}
, we have
[e^{*}]_{\sim_{|q|+1}}=\{e^{*}\}
.
It follows that
vio_{\tau}(\mathcal{J})\subseteq\{\{e^{*}\}\mid e^{*}\in vio_{\tau}({\mathcal{I}}^{*})\}
,
and hence
|vio_{\tau}(\mathcal{J})|\leq|vio_{\tau}({\mathcal{I}}^{*})|
.
Putting everything together, we can therefore conclude that

\mathit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{J})\leq\mathit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}({\mathcal{I}}^{*})
.
∎

Proof.

The proof is by reduction from the following
\Delta^{P}_{2}
-hard problem: given a satisfiable boolean formula
\varphi
over variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
such that
\varphi
is a set of clauses
c_{1},\dots,c_{m}
where each clause has exactly two positive and two negative literals and any of the four positions in a clause can be filled instead by one of the truth constants true and false, and given
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, decide whether the lexicographically maximum truth assignment
\nu_{max}
satisfying
\varphi
with respect to
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
fulfills
\nu_{max}(x_{k})=1
. It follows from
[
1988
]
and from the reductions from SAT to 3SAT and from 3SAT to 2+2SAT
[
1993
]
that this problem is
\Delta^{P}_{2}
-hard.

Let
\varphi
be an instance of the problem as previously defined. We define
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}=(\langle\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}\rangle,\omega)
an
\mathcal{EL}_{\bot}
WKB as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=

\displaystyle\{S(\varphi,c_{j})\mid 1\leq j\leq m\}\cup

\displaystyle\{P_{\ell}(c_{k},x_{j})\mid\ell\in\{1,2\},x_{j}\text{ is the }\ell^{th}\text{ pos. lit. of }c_{k}\}\cup

\displaystyle\{N_{\ell}(c_{k},x_{j})\mid\ell\in\{1,2\},\neg x_{j}\text{ is the }\ell^{th}\text{ neg. lit. of }c_{k}\}\cup

\displaystyle\{F(x_{i}),T(x_{i}),T^{\prime}(x_{i})\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}\cup\{F(f),T(t)\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}=

\displaystyle\{F\sqcap T\sqsubseteq\bot,F\sqcap T^{\prime}\sqsubseteq\bot\}\cup

\displaystyle\{\exists S.(\exists P_{1}.F\sqcap\exists P_{2}.F\sqcap\exists N_{1}.T\sqcap\exists N_{2}.T)\sqsubseteq\bot\}

We set
\omega(\tau)=\infty
for every
\tau\in\mathcal{T}
, and
\omega(\alpha)=\infty
for every assertion
\alpha
built on
P_{\ell}
and
N_{\ell}
as well as for
T(t)
and
F(f)
. For the remaining assertions, we define the weights through the following prioritization (see Lemma 6.2.5 in
[
2016
]
):

•

L_{1}=\{T(x_{i}),F(x_{i})\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}

•

L_{2}=\{S(\varphi,c_{j})\mid 1\leq j\leq m\}

•

L_{3}=\{T^{\prime}(x_{1})\}
,
L_{4}=\{T^{\prime}(x_{2})\}
,
\dots
,
L_{n+2}=\{T^{\prime}(x_{n})\}

Let
u=max(2n,m)+1
and define
\omega(\alpha)=u^{n+2-i}
for every
\alpha\in L_{i}
.

We show that
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}\models_{c}^{opt}T^{\prime}(x_{k})
iff
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}\models_{p}^{opt}T^{\prime}(x_{k})
iff
\nu_{max}(x_{k})=1
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an interpretation with optimal cost. By definition of
\omega
,
\mathcal{I}\models\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{I}
satisfies all
P_{\ell}
/
N_{\ell}
assertions,
T(t)
and
F(f)
.
First note that for every
1\leq i\leq n
,
\mathcal{I}
satisfies exactly one of the assertions
T(x_{i}),F(x_{i})
. Indeed, it cannot contain both since
\mathcal{I}\models F\sqcap T\sqsubseteq\bot
and if there was
i
such that
\mathcal{I}\not\models T(x_{i})
and
\mathcal{I}\not\models F(x_{i})
, then
\mathcal{J}
obtained from
\mathcal{I}
by setting
T^{\mathcal{J}}=T^{\mathcal{I}}\cup\{x_{i}^{\mathcal{I}}\}
,
S^{\mathcal{J}}=\emptyset
and
X^{\mathcal{J}}=X^{\mathcal{I}}
for every other concept or role name
X
would be such that
\mathit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{J})<\mathit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{I})
since
m\times u^{n}<u^{n+1}
because
m<u
.
Moreover, it is easy to see that
\mathcal{I}\models T^{\prime}(x_{i})
exactly when
\mathcal{I}\not\models F(x_{i})
, i.e. when
\mathcal{I}\models T(x_{i})
.

Let
\nu
be the valuation of
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
such that
\nu(x_{i})=1
iff
\mathcal{I}\models T(x_{i})
(iff
\mathcal{I}\models T^{\prime}(x_{i})
iff
\mathcal{I}\not\models F(x_{i})
).
Assume for a contradiction that
\nu
does not satisfy
\varphi
. There exists a clause
c_{j}
of
\varphi
such that for every positive literal
x_{i}
of
c_{j}
,
\nu(x_{i})=0
, i.e.
\mathcal{I}\models F(x_{i})
, and for every negative literal
\neg x_{i}
of
c_{j}
,
\nu(x_{i})=1
, i.e.
\mathcal{I}\models T(x_{i})
. It follows that
\mathcal{I}\not\models S(\varphi,c_{j})
(otherwise
\varphi^{\mathcal{I}}
would be in
(\exists S.(\exists P_{1}.F\sqcap\exists P_{2}.F\sqcap\exists N_{1}.T\sqcap\exists N_{2}.T))^{\mathcal{I}}
and the cost of
\mathcal{I}
would be infinite.
Since
\varphi
is satisfiable, there exists
\nu^{\prime}
that satisfies
\varphi
. Let
\mathcal{J}
be the interpretation such that
P_{\ell}^{\mathcal{J}}=P_{\ell}^{\mathcal{I}}
and
N_{\ell}^{\mathcal{J}}=N_{\ell}^{\mathcal{I}}
for
1\leq\ell\leq 2
,
T^{\mathcal{J}}=\{t^{\mathcal{I}}\}\cup\{x_{i}^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\nu^{\prime}(x_{i})=1\}
,
F^{\mathcal{J}}=\{f^{\mathcal{I}}\}\cup\{x_{i}^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\nu^{\prime}(x_{i})=0\}
, and
S^{\mathcal{J}}=\{(\varphi,c_{j})\mid 1\leq j\leq m\}
. Then
\mathit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{J})<\mathit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{I})
.
Indeed,
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
both violate the same number of
T
/
F
-assertions and the cost
u^{n}
of violating the single assertion
S(\varphi,c_{j})
is greater than the cost of violating all
T^{\prime}
-assertions
\Sigma_{i=3}^{n+2}u^{n+2-i}=\Sigma_{i=0}^{n-1}u^{i}=\frac{u^{n}-1}{u-1}
.
Hence,
\nu
satisfies
\varphi
.

Finally, we show that
\nu=\nu_{max}
. Define
\mathcal{J}
by
P_{\ell}^{\mathcal{J}}=P_{\ell}^{\mathcal{I}}
and
N_{\ell}^{\mathcal{J}}=N_{\ell}^{\mathcal{I}}
for
1\leq\ell\leq 2
,
T^{\mathcal{J}}={T^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{J}}=\{t^{\mathcal{I}}\}\cup\{x_{i}^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\nu_{max}(x_{i})=1\}
,
F^{\mathcal{J}}=\{f^{\mathcal{I}}\}\cup\{x_{i}^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\nu_{max}(x_{i})=0\}
,
S^{\mathcal{J}}=\{(\varphi,c_{j})\mid 1\leq j\leq m\}
. If there exists
l
such that
\nu
and
\nu_{max}
coincide on
x_{i}
for
i<l
but not on
x_{l}
, i.e.
\nu(x_{l})=0
and
\nu_{max}(x_{l})=1
(since
\nu_{max}
is the lexicographically maximum truth assignment satisfying
\varphi
), then
\mathit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{J})<\mathit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{I})
. Indeed, the cost
u^{n-l}
of violating
T^{\prime}(x_{l})
is greater than the cost
\Sigma_{i=l+3}^{n+2}u^{n+2-i}=\Sigma_{i=0}^{n-l-1}u^{i}=\frac{u^{n-l}-1}{u-1}
of violating all
T^{\prime}(x_{i})
for
i>l
.
Hence
\nu
and
\nu_{max}
coincide on every
x_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n
.

We have shown that for every interpretation with optimal cost
\mathcal{I}
,
\mathcal{I}\models T^{\prime}(x_{k})
iff
\nu_{max}(x_{k})=1
. Hence
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}\models_{c}^{opt}T^{\prime}(x_{k})
iff
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}\models_{p}^{opt}T^{\prime}(x_{k})
iff
\nu_{max}(x_{k})=1
.

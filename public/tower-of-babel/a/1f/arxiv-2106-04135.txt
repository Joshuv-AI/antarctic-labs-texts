Regularity of the free boundary for a parabolic cooperative system
G. Aleksanyan, M. Fotouhi, H. Shahgholian, G. S. Weiss

Abstract
. In this paper we study the following parabolic system Δ u − ∂ t u = | u | q − 1 u χ { | u | > 0 } , u = ( u 1 , ⋯ , u m ) , \Delta{\textbf{u}}-\partial_{t}{\textbf{u}}=|{\textbf{u}}|^{q-1}{\textbf{u}}\,\chi_{\{|{\textbf{u}}|>0\}},\qquad{\textbf{u}}=(u^{1},\cdots,u^{m})\ , with free boundary ∂ { | u | > 0 } \partial\{|{\textbf{u}}|>0\} . For 0 ≤ q < 1 0\leq q<1 , we prove optimal growth rate for solutions u to the above system near free boundary points, and show that in a uniform neighbourhood of any a priori well-behaved free boundary point the free boundary is C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} in space directions and half-Lipschitz in the time direction.



Keywords:
Semilinear parabolic equation, Free boundary, Regularity.

Mathematics Subject Classification:
35B65, 35R35.

Introduction

1.1.
Background

In this paper we shall study for
0\leq q<1
the parabolic (free boundary) system

(1.1)

\begin{array}[]{l}\Delta{\textbf{u}}-\partial_{t}{\textbf{u}}=f({\textbf{u}}):=|{\textbf{u}}|^{q-1}{\textbf{u}}\,\chi_{\left\{|{\textbf{u}}|>0\right\}},\qquad{\textbf{u}}=(u^{1},\cdots,u^{m})\ ,\end{array}

System (

accelerates the extinction/reaction of all species/reactants.
The special choice of our reaction kinetics would assure
a constant decay/reaction rate in the case that

i=1,\cdots,m
are

A diverse scalar parabolic free boundary problem has been subject of intense studies in more than half-century. On the other hand there are very few results for problems that involve systems (see
[
1
,
5
,
9
]
), and probably no results for the system related to equation (

The elliptic case of the above system is studied in
[
1
,
9
]
or in the scalar case, when
m=1
in
[
8
]
, where they prove optimal growth rate for the solutions as well as
C^{1,\alpha}

In this paper we shall study the parabolic system (
1.1
) from a regularity point of view. The analysis of the above parabolic system introduces several serious obstruction, and hence a straightforward generalization of the ideas and techniques of its elliptic counterpart is far from being obvious. Due to its technical nature, and the need for notations and definitions, we shall explain these difficulties below, during the course of developing the tools and ideas.

1.2.
Main results and plan of the paper

Our results concern two main questions: Optimal growth of the solution

u
at free boundary points (Theorem
3.3
), and the regularity of the free boundary (Theorem
6.5
) at
well-behaved
points.
1
1

1

Later we shall call them regular points.

To prove our results we use the regularity theory for the elliptic case, see
[
1
,
9
]
and follow the ideas that have been used to treat parabolic free boundary problems,
as in
[
4
]
that was used for the no-sign one phase scalar case. In doing so we encounter several technical problems, that we need to circumvent by enhancing the previous techniques.
The first problem we encounter is the use of the balanced-energy monotonicity formula for proving quadratic growth estimates from the free boundary points.
In parabolic setting, and specially in system case, the combination of balanced energy and Almgren’s frequency is more delicate than the elliptic case done in
[
1
]
.

The second problem we encounter concerns the regularity of the free boundary, where we are forced to use the epiperimetric inequality in elliptic setting. In order to do this we need to prove that
\partial_{t}{\textbf{u}}
, the time derivative of
u
, is Hölder regular for
q=0
. When
q>0
we need some modification (see Section 5). This, however, can be proved at the so-called regular points. Indeed, since the set of regular points is open (in relative topology) we can use indirect argument to show that
\partial_{t}{\textbf{u}}
tends to zero at free boundary points close to a regular point. From here one can bootstrap a Hölder regularity theory for
|\partial_{t}{\textbf{u}}|
. Once this is done we can invoke the epiperimetric inequality for equations with Hölder right hand side and deduce (in a standard way) the regularity of the free boundary in space. The Hölder regularity in time then follows by blow-up techniques, and indirect argument.

2.1.

Theorem 2.1

(Monotonicity formula)

\displaystyle 2\int_{-4}^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\nabla{\textbf{u}}_{r}:\nabla\frac{d{\textbf{u}}_{r}}{dr}+\frac{\kappa{\textbf{u}}_{r}}{2t}\frac{d{\textbf{u}}_{r}}{dr}+{\textbf{u}}_{r}|{\textbf{u}}_{r}|^{q-1}\frac{d{\textbf{u}}_{r}}{dr}\right)Gdxdt

\displaystyle\int_{-4r^{2}}^{-r^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{1}{-t}|w_{r_{m}}|^{2}Gdxdt\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{-4r^{2}}^{-r^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}(2\sqrt{3})^{n}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{3-t}}\frac{1}{-t}G(x,t)dxdt\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{-4}^{-3}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{-s}|w_{r_{m}}(y,s)|^{2}G(y,s)dyds\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{-4r^{2}}^{-r^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{\pi}{3}\left(\frac{3}{-\pi t}\right)^{n/2+1}\exp\left(\frac{|x|^{2}}{3-t}+\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}\right)dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{-4r^{2}}^{-r^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{\pi}{3}\left(\frac{3}{-\pi t}\right)^{n/2+1}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{8t}}dxdt\rightarrow 0\text{\ \ as }R\rightarrow\infty.

The proof of (
2.3
) is the same if we apply again Lemma
7.2
for the caloric function
\nabla w_{r_{m}}
.
∎

2.2.
Nondegeneracy

Proposition 2.5
.

(Nondegeneracy)
Let
u
be a solution of (
1.1
) with
0\leq q<1
. Then there is a positive constant
c=c(q,n)
such that
if
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\overline{\{|\emph{{\textbf{u}}}|>0\}}
, and
Q^{-}_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})\subset Q_{1}
, then

\sup_{Q^{-}_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})}|\emph{{\textbf{u}}}|\geq cr^{\kappa}.

Proof.

Let
U(x,t):=|{\textbf{u}}(x,t)|^{1-q}
. The proof follows in a standard way using

\Delta U-\partial_{t}U=(1-q)+(1-q)\frac{|\nabla{\textbf{u}}|^{2}}{U^{\kappa-1}}-\frac{1+q}{1-q}\frac{|\nabla U|^{2}}{U},\qquad\text{ in }\{U>0\}.

For any
(y,s)\in\{|{\textbf{u}}|>0\}
, (close to
(x_{0},t_{0})
), set
w(x,t)=c(|x-y|^{2}+(s-t))
for small constant
c>0
to be specified later.
Then
h=U-w
satisfies in
Q_{r}^{-}(y,s)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}h-\partial_{t}h

\displaystyle:=\Delta h-\partial_{t}h+\frac{1+q}{1-q}\left(\frac{\nabla(U+w)}{U}\cdot\nabla h-\frac{4c}{U}h\right)

\displaystyle=(1-q)-4c(\frac{2n+1}{4}+\frac{1+q}{1-q})+(1-q)\frac{|\nabla{\textbf{u}}|^{2}}{U^{\kappa-1}}+4c^{2}\frac{1+q}{1-q}\frac{s-t}{U}\geq 0,

provided that
c
is small enough. In particular
h
cannot attain a local maximum in
Q_{r}^{-}(y,s)\cap\{|{\textbf{u}}|>0\}
according to the maximum principle for
\mathcal{L}-\partial_{t}
.
On the other hand
h<0
on
\partial\{|{\textbf{u}}|>0\}
and hence the positive maximum of
h
is attained on
\partial_{p}Q_{r}^{-}(y,s)
, and we conclude that

\sup_{\partial_{p}Q_{r}^{-}(y,s)}(U-w)\geq U(y,s)>0,

which amounts to

\sup_{\partial_{p}Q_{r}^{-}(y,s)}U\geq cr^{2}.

Letting
(y,s)\rightarrow(x_{0},t_{0})
, we arrive at the statement of the proposition.
∎

3.
Regularity of solutions

In this section we study the regularity of solutions (to equation (
1.1
)), which
according to the parabolic regularity theory, are known to be
C_{x}^{1,\beta}\cap C_{t}^{0,(1+\beta)/2}
for
q=0
and
C_{x}^{2,\beta}\cap C_{t}^{1,\beta/2}
for
q>0
.
Here we will show the optimal growth for solutions from points where
u
vanish to the highest order for our problem.
In order to study the optimal growth (regularity) of solution, we start with the following definition; see also
[
10
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

The vanishing order of
u
at point
X_{0}
is defined to be the largest value
\mathcal{V}(X_{0})
which satisfies

\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\lVert\emph{{\textbf{u}}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r}^{-}(X_{0}))}}{r^{\mathcal{V}(X_{0})}}<+\infty.

One of main tools in studying a sublinear equation is Lemma
7.5
, which is the dual of

[
3
, Lemma 1.1]
for the elliptic case. For the convenience of reader we put the proof in the Appendix.
One of the useful result of Lemma
7.5
is that if
u
is a solution of (
1.1
) and
X_{0}\in\Gamma({\textbf{u}})
, then

\mathcal{V}(X_{0})\in\{1,2,3,\cdots,\lfloor\kappa\rfloor,\kappa\}
.
Moreover, we can find out easily that if
\mathcal{V}(X_{0})=s<\kappa
, then
\partial_{t}^{i}\partial_{x}^{\mu}{\textbf{u}}(X_{0})
exists and vanishes for
2i+|\mu|<s
. Indeed, there is a self-similar vectorial polynomial
P
of degree
s
such that
|{\textbf{u}}(X)-P(X)|\leq C|X-X_{0}|^{s}
.
Our main result for case
q>0
is that if
X_{0}\in\Gamma^{\kappa}({\textbf{u}})=\{X_{0}\in\Gamma({\textbf{u}}):\partial_{t}^{i}\partial_{x}^{\mu}{\textbf{u}}(X_{0})=0\text{ for all }2i+|\mu|<\kappa\}
, then
\mathcal{V}(X_{0})=\kappa
.

We start with the following lemma which is essential to obtain our result.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
u
solving (
1.1
) in
Q_{2}
, and satisfying the doubling

(3.1)

\lVert\emph{{\textbf{u}}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{2}^{-})}\leq 2^{\kappa}\lVert\emph{{\textbf{u}}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{-})},

we have

\lVert\emph{{\textbf{u}}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{-})}\leq\max\left\{1,C\lVert\emph{{\textbf{u}}}G^{1/(1+q)}\rVert_{L^{1+q}(Q_{1}^{-})}\right\},

where
C
is independent of
u
.

Proof.

Suppose the statement of the lemma fails. Then there is a sequence
{\textbf{u}}_{j}
satisfying the hypothesis of the lemma with

(3.2)

\lVert{\textbf{u}}_{j}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{-})}\geq 1,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lVert{\textbf{u}}_{j}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{-})}\geq j\lVert{\textbf{u}}_{j}G^{1/(1+q)}\rVert_{L^{1+q}(Q_{1}^{-})}.

Define
\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{j}={\textbf{u}}_{j}/\lVert{\textbf{u}}_{j}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{-})}
, and insert in (
3.2
), to arrive at

(3.3)

\frac{1}{j}\geq\lVert\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{j}G^{1/(1+q)}\rVert_{L^{1+q}(Q_{1}^{-})}.

Since
\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{j}
satisfies the doubling (
3.1
), then it yields

\lVert H(\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{j})\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{2}^{-})}\leq 2^{\kappa q}\lVert{\textbf{u}}_{j}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{-})}^{q-1}\leq 2^{\kappa q}.

Therefore we have a subsequence of
\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{j}
which converges to a limit function
{\textbf{u}}_{0}
satisfying

\lVert\tilde{{\textbf{u}}}_{0}G^{1/(1+q)}\rVert_{L^{1+q}(Q_{1}^{-})}=0,\qquad\lVert\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{-})}=1,

which is obviously a contradiction.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

For
u
a solution to (
1.1
), with
(0,0)\in\Gamma^{\kappa}(\emph{{\textbf{u}}})
, there exists a constant
C
such that

\sup_{Q^{-}_{r}}|\emph{{\textbf{u}}}|\leq Cr^{\kappa},\qquad\forall\ 0<r<1/2.

Proof.

Case
\kappa\notin\mathbb{N}
:
The proof in this case follows by standard blow-up and the use of Liouville’s theorem, and the only subtle point would be to prove the blow-up solution will vanish at the origin, of order
\kappa
; the latter is taken care of in Appendix. here is how it works out. If the statement of the theorem fails, then there exists a sequence
r_{j}\rightarrow 0
such that

\sup_{Q_{r}^{-}}|{\textbf{u}}|\leq jr^{\kappa},\qquad\forall r\geq r_{j},\qquad\sup_{Q_{r_{j}}^{-}}|{\textbf{u}}|=jr_{j}^{\kappa}.

In particular the function
\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{j}(x,t)=\frac{{\textbf{u}}(r_{j}x,r_{j}^{2}t)}{jr_{j}^{\kappa}}
satisfies

\sup_{Q_{R}^{-}}|\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{j}|\leq R^{\kappa},\qquad\text{ for }1\leq R\leq\frac{1}{r_{j}},

with equality for
R=1
, along with

H\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{j}=\frac{f(\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{j})}{j^{1-q}}\longrightarrow 0\ \text{ uniformly in }Q_{R}^{-}.

From this we conclude that there is a convergent subsequence, tending to a caloric function
{\textbf{u}}_{0}
with growth
\kappa
, i.e.

(3.4)

\sup_{Q_{R}^{-}}|{\textbf{u}}_{0}|\leq R^{\kappa},\quad\forall R\geq 1,\quad\sup_{Q_{1}^{-}}|{\textbf{u}}_{0}|=1,\quad H{\textbf{u}}_{0}=0,

and furthermore,
|\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{j}(X)|\leq C_{0}|X|
in
Q_{1}^{-}
uniformly for some constant
C_{0}>0
and all
j
.
Thus
|H\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{j}|\leq|\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{j}|^{q}\leq C_{0}^{q}|X|^{q}
in
Q_{1}^{-}
.
Now if we apply Lemma
7.5
for each component of
\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{j}=(\tilde{u}_{j}^{1},\cdots,\tilde{u}_{j}^{m})
, we obtain a caloric polynomial
P_{j}^{i}
of degree at most
\lfloor 2+q\rfloor=2
so that
|\tilde{u}_{j}^{i}(X)-P_{j}^{i}(X)|\leq C_{1}C_{0}|X|^{2+q}
in
Q_{1}^{-}
and the constant
C_{1}
depends only on
n,q
and an upper bound on
\lVert\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{j}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{-})}
.
Since
(0,0)\in\Gamma^{\kappa}({\textbf{u}})
, so
P_{j}^{i}\equiv 0
and then
|\tilde{u}_{j}^{i}(X)|\leq C_{1}C_{0}|X|^{2+q}
.
By a bootstrap argument we find out the uniform estimate
|\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{j}(X)|\leq C_{\epsilon}|X|^{\kappa-\epsilon}
for every
\epsilon>0
. Therefore, we get

(3.5)

{\textbf{u}}_{0}(0,0)=\partial_{t}^{i}\partial_{x}^{\mu}{\textbf{u}}_{0}(0,0)=0,\quad\text{ for all }2i+|\mu|<\kappa.

Obviously (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), along with the fact that
\kappa\notin\mathbb{N}
, violates Liouville’s theorem and we have a contradiction.

Case
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}
:

Consider the function
{\textbf{v}}=\eta{\textbf{u}}
where
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{3/4})
satisfies
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
and
\eta=1
in
B_{1/2}
.
Fix
0<r<\frac{1}{2}
, let
\rho_{i}:=2^{-i}r,i=0,1,2,...
, and define
{\textbf{v}}_{\rho_{i}}(x,t)={\textbf{v}}(\rho_{i}x,\rho_{i}^{2}t)/\rho_{i}^{\kappa}
, then

\displaystyle\int_{-r^{2}}^{0}\int_{B_{r}}|{\textbf{u}}|^{1+q}Gdxdt=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{-\rho_{i-1}^{2}}^{-\rho_{i}^{2}}\int_{B_{r}}|{\textbf{v}}|^{1+q}Gdxdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\rho_{i}^{2\kappa}\int_{-4}^{-1}\int_{B_{2^{i}}}|{\textbf{v}}_{\rho_{i}}|^{1+q}Gdxdt\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\rho_{i}^{2\kappa}\int_{-4}^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|{\textbf{v}}_{\rho_{i}}|^{1+q}Gdxdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1+q}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\rho_{i}^{2\kappa}\left({\mathbb{W}}({\textbf{v}}_{\rho_{i}},1)-\int_{-4}^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(|\nabla{\textbf{v}}_{\rho_{i}}|^{2}+\frac{\kappa|{\textbf{v}}_{\rho_{i}}|^{2}}{2t}\right)Gdxdt\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1+q}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\rho_{i}^{2\kappa}\left({\mathbb{W}}({\textbf{v}},\rho_{i})-\int_{-4}^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(|\nabla({\textbf{v}}_{\rho_{i}}-{\textbf{p}})|^{2}+\frac{\kappa|{\textbf{v}}_{\rho_{i}}-{\textbf{p}}|^{2}}{2t}\right)Gdxdt\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1+q}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\rho_{i}^{2\kappa}\left({\mathbb{W}}({\textbf{v}},1)+F(1)+\int_{-4}^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{\kappa|{\textbf{v}}_{\rho_{i}}-{\textbf{p}}|^{2}}{-2t}Gdxdt\right)

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cr^{2\kappa}\left(1+\int_{-1}^{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{\kappa|{\textbf{v}}_{r}-{\textbf{p}}|^{2}}{-2t}Gdxdt\right),

where we have used Lemma
7.4
,
F
is the function defined in Theorem
2.2
and
{\textbf{p}}\in\mathcal{H}
, the space of all
\kappa
-backward self-similar caloric vector-functions.
We now let
{\textbf{p}}=\pi_{r}
, where

\pi_{r}={\rm argmin}_{{\textbf{q}}\in\mathcal{H}}\int_{-1}^{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{|{\textbf{v}}_{r}-{\textbf{q}}|^{2}}{-t}Gdxdt,

and observe that

(3.7)

\int_{-1}^{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{({\textbf{v}}_{r}-\pi_{r})\cdot{\textbf{p}}}{-t}Gdxdt=0,\qquad\text{for every }{\textbf{p}}\in\mathcal{H}.

Now suppose, towards a contradiction, that there is a sequence
r_{k}\rightarrow 0
, such that

\sup_{Q_{r}^{-}}|{\textbf{u}}|\leq kr^{\kappa},\qquad\forall r\geq r_{k},\qquad\sup_{Q_{r_{k}}^{-}}|{\textbf{u}}|=kr_{k}^{\kappa}.

Consider the scaling
{\textbf{u}}_{r}(x,t)={\textbf{u}}(rx,r^{2}t)/r^{\kappa}
, where the sequence
{\textbf{u}}_{r_{k}}
satisfies the doubling condition (
3.1
) because

\lVert{\textbf{u}}_{r_{k}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{2}^{-})}=2^{\kappa}\lVert{\textbf{u}}_{2r_{k}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{-})}\leq 2^{\kappa}k=2^{\kappa}\lVert{\textbf{u}}_{r_{k}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{-})}.

Therefore Lemma
3.2
and (
3
) implies that

M_{k}=\left(\int_{-1}^{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{|{\textbf{v}}_{r_{k}}-\pi_{r_{k}}|^{2}}{-t}Gdxdt\right)^{1/2}\longrightarrow\infty.

For
{\textbf{w}}_{k}=\frac{{\textbf{v}}_{r_{k}}-\pi_{r_{k}}}{M_{k}}
, we have

(3.8)

\int_{-1}^{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{|{\textbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}}{-t}Gdxdt=1.

Furthermore, we can show that
\{\nabla{\textbf{w}}_{k}G^{1/2}\}
is bounded in
L^{2}(-1,0;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))
.
In order to show this, we can write

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(|\nabla{\textbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}+\frac{\kappa|{\textbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}}{2t}\right)Gdxdt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M_{k}^{2}}\int_{-1}^{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(|\nabla{\textbf{v}}_{r_{k}}|^{2}+\frac{\kappa|{\textbf{v}}_{r_{k}}|^{2}}{2t}\right)Gdxdt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M_{k}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{-2i\kappa}\int_{-4}^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(|\nabla{\textbf{v}}_{2^{-i}r_{k}}|^{2}+\frac{\kappa|{\textbf{v}}_{2^{-i}r_{k}}|^{2}}{2t}\right)Gdxdt

(3.9)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{M_{k}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{-2i\kappa}{\mathbb{W}}({\textbf{v}},2^{-i}r_{k})\longrightarrow 0,

which together with (
3.8
) implies

(3.10)

\int_{-1}^{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\nabla{\textbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}Gdxdt=O(1).

On the other hand, we have

(3.11)

H{\textbf{w}}_{k}=\frac{1}{M_{k}}H{\textbf{v}}_{r_{k}}=\frac{1}{M_{k}}\left(r_{k}^{2}{\textbf{u}}_{r_{k}}\Delta\eta(r_{k}x)+\eta(r_{k}x)^{1-q}f({\textbf{v}}_{r_{k}})+2r_{k}\nabla{\textbf{u}}_{r_{k}}\cdot\nabla\eta(r_{k}x)\right),

and also,

\displaystyle\frac{2}{1+q}\int_{-1}^{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|{\textbf{v}}_{r_{k}}|^{1+q}Gdxdt

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{1+q}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{-2i\kappa}\int_{-4}^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|{\textbf{v}}_{2^{-i}r_{k}}|^{1+q}Gdxdt

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{-2i\kappa}\left({\mathbb{W}}({\textbf{v}}_{2^{-i}r_{k}},1)-\int_{-4}^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(|\nabla{\textbf{v}}_{2^{-i}r_{k}}|^{2}+\frac{\kappa|{\textbf{v}}_{2^{-i}r_{k}}|^{2}}{2t}\right)Gdxdt\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{-2i\kappa}\left({\mathbb{W}}({\textbf{v}},1)+F(1)+\int_{-4}^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{\kappa|{\textbf{v}}_{2^{-i}r_{k}}-\pi_{r_{k}}|^{2}}{-2t}Gdxdt\right)

\displaystyle=O(1)+\int_{-1}^{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{\kappa|{\textbf{v}}_{r_{k}}-\pi_{r_{k}}|^{2}}{-2t}Gdxdt

\displaystyle=O(1)+\frac{M_{k}^{2}}{1-q}.

Therefore by (
3.11
), we get for
q>0

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|H{\textbf{w}}_{k}|^{(1+q)/q}Gdxdt\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M_{k}^{(1+q)/q}}\left(C(\eta,\lVert{\textbf{u}}\rVert_{H^{1}(B_{1},{\mathbb{R}}^{m})})+\int_{-1}^{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|{\textbf{v}}_{r_{k}}|^{1+q}Gdxdt\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M_{k}^{(1+q)/q}}\left(C(\eta,\lVert{\textbf{u}}\rVert_{H^{1}(B_{1},{\mathbb{R}}^{m})},n,q)+\frac{M_{k}^{2}}{1-q}\right)\longrightarrow 0,

and
\lVert H{\textbf{w}}_{k}\rVert_{\infty}\rightarrow 0
for
q=0
.
Hence
\{{\textbf{w}}_{k}\}
is bounded in
W^{1,p}(-1,\frac{1}{R};W^{2,p}(B_{R}))
for all fixed
R>0
and by diagonalization technique there is a weakly convergent subsequence with limit
{\textbf{w}}_{0}
in
L^{2}(-1,0;W^{2,p}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))
, satisfying
H{\textbf{w}}_{0}=0
.
We claim next that the strong convergence

(3.12)

\frac{{\textbf{w}}_{k}}{(-t)^{1/2}}G^{1/2}\rightarrow\frac{{\textbf{w}}_{0}}{(-t)^{1/2}}G^{1/2},\qquad\text{ in }L^{2}(-1,0;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})),

holds, which follows if we prove the uniform convergence in
k

(3.13)

\int_{-1}^{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{|{\textbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}}{-t}Gdxdt\rightarrow 0,\qquad\text{ as }R\rightarrow\infty.

This can obviously be obtained by applying Lemma
7.3
and relations (
3.8
) and (
3.10
)

\displaystyle R^{2}\int_{-1}^{-0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{|{\textbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}}{-t}Gdxdt\leq\int_{-1}^{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|{\textbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}\frac{|x|^{2}}{-t}Gdxdt=O(1).

Therefore we get

\int_{-1}^{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{|{\textbf{w}}_{0}|^{2}}{-t}Gdxdt=1,

and also by (
3.7
),

(3.14)

\int_{-1}^{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{{\textbf{w}}_{0}\cdot{\textbf{p}}}{-t}Gdxdt=0,\qquad\text{for every }{\textbf{p}}\in\mathcal{H}.

Moreover, from (
3
), (
3.12
) and weakly lower semicontinuity of norm we obtain for every
R>0

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{0}\int_{B_{R}}\left(|\nabla{\textbf{w}}_{0}|^{2}+\frac{\kappa|{\textbf{w}}_{0}|^{2}}{2t}\right)Gdxdt\leq\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{-1}^{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(|\nabla{\textbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}+\frac{\kappa|{\textbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}}{2t}\right)Gdxdt\leq 0.

It implies that

(3.15)

0\geq\int_{-1}^{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(|\nabla{\textbf{w}}_{0}|^{2}+\frac{\kappa|{\textbf{w}}_{0}|^{2}}{2t}\right)Gdxdt=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{-2i\kappa}\int_{-4}^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(|\nabla{\textbf{w}}_{0,2^{-i}}|^{2}+\frac{\kappa|{\textbf{w}}_{0,2^{-i}}|^{2}}{2t}\right)Gdxdt.

If we further have

(3.16)

|\partial_{t}^{\ell}\partial_{x}^{\mu}w_{0}^{j}(0,0)|=0,\qquad\text{for }2\ell+|\mu|\leq\kappa-1,

then by Lemma
2.4
, each component
w_{0}^{j}
of
{\textbf{w}}_{0,2^{-i}}
must satisfy

\int_{-4}^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(|\nabla w_{0}^{j}|^{2}+\frac{\kappa|w_{0}^{j}|^{2}}{2t}\right)Gdxdt\geq 0.

Summing over
j
and comparing with (
3.15
), implies that
w_{0}^{j}
is a
\kappa
-backward self-similar caloric function.
But (
3.14
) implies that
{\textbf{w}}_{0}=0
which contradicts (
3.13
).

To close the argument, we need to prove (
3.16
). This can be shown by invoking Lemma
7.5
to obtain uniform estimate
\lVert{\textbf{w}}_{k}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r}^{-})}=o(r^{\kappa-1})
.
To apply Lemma
7.5

it is necessary to show the uniform estimate
\lVert H{\textbf{w}}_{k}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r}^{-})}=o(r^{\kappa-2-1/2})
.
Since we have assumed
\lVert{\textbf{u}}_{r_{k}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{-})}\rightarrow\infty
by contradiction, the scaled sequence
\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{k}:={\textbf{u}}_{r_{k}}/\lVert{\textbf{u}}_{r_{k}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{-})}
satisfies
H\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{k}=f(\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{k})/\lVert{\textbf{u}}_{r_{k}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{-})}^{1-q}\rightarrow 0
and converges to a caloric function
\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{0}
as a subsequence.
Moreover,
|\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{k}(X)|\leq C_{0}|X|
for a constant
C_{0}>0
and all
k
. Now apply Lemma
7.5
repeatedly to obtain the uniform estimate
|\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{k}(X)|\leq C_{\epsilon}|X|^{\kappa-\epsilon}
for a small value
\epsilon
.
So,
|{\textbf{u}}_{r_{k}}(X)|\leq C_{\epsilon}\lVert{\textbf{u}}_{r_{k}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{-})}|X|^{\kappa-\epsilon}
and by Lemma
3.2
as well as (
3
)

|H{\textbf{w}}_{k}(X)|\leq\frac{1}{M_{k}}|f({\textbf{u}}_{r_{k}}(X))|\leq\frac{C_{\epsilon}^{q}}{M_{k}}(1+M_{k}^{2})^{q/(1+q)}|X|^{\kappa-2-\epsilon q}\leq C|X|^{\kappa-2-\epsilon q}.

∎

Remark 3.4
.

Although Theorem
3.3
shows the backward regularity, we can see obviously the regularity in forward problem.
A line of proof can be considered toward a contradiction and assuming the sequence
r_{j}\rightarrow 0
such that

\sup_{Q_{r}^{+}}|\emph{{\textbf{u}}}|\leq jr^{\kappa},\qquad\forall r\geq r_{j},\qquad\sup_{Q_{r_{j}}^{+}}|\emph{{\textbf{u}}}|=jr_{j}^{\kappa}.

Then
\emph{{\textbf{u}}}_{j}(X)=\emph{{\textbf{u}}}(r_{j}X)/(jr_{j}^{\kappa})
converges to a caloric function
\emph{{\textbf{u}}}_{0}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}
with polynomial growth,
\lVert\emph{{\textbf{u}}}_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}=1
and
\emph{{\textbf{u}}}_{0}\equiv 0
for
t\leq 0
(we also apply here Theorem
3.3
). This contradicts the uniqueness of heat equation solution with polynomial growth in forward problem.

4.
Homogeneous global solutions

In this section we perform energy classification of regular free boundary points, that will be needed later in order to establish the Hölder regularity of the time derivative
\partial_{t}{\textbf{u}}
in the next section. Indeed the main goal is to show that half-space solutions are isolated within certain topologies.
The proofs for the case
q=0
and
q>0
differs to some extent and hence we are forced to consider them separately. For the case
q=0
we need to consider two lemmas (Lemmas
4.1
, and
4.2

5.

Our way of approach, as mentioned in the introduction, is to use elliptic regularity theory for the free boundary problems. This approach is based on using the epiperimetric inequality for the elliptic systems as done in

Now we prove that the time derivative vanishes continuously

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
g
be the function defined in Lemma

and suppose

\lim_{(x,t)\rightarrow(x_{0},t_{0})}g(x,t)=0.

Proof.

Let

Let

Now using a standard iterative argument one can prove the Hölder regularity
of the time derivative.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
g
be the function defined in Lemma

and suppose

Proof.

We consider the following local (fixed time) version of balanced energy;

\displaystyle{W}_{t_{0}}({\textbf{u}},r,x_{0}):=\frac{1}{r^{n+2\kappa-2}}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|\nabla{\textbf{u}}(x,t_{0})|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{1+q}{|{\textbf{u}}(x,t_{0})|^{1+q}}dx

\displaystyle-\frac{\kappa}{r^{n+2\kappa-1}}\int_{\partial B_{r}(x_{0})}|{\textbf{u}}(x,t_{0})|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Gamma^{\kappa}
be a regular free boundary point, then
there exist constants
C>0
and
0<\beta<1

\left|{W}_{t_{0}}(\emph{{\textbf{u}}},r_{2},x_{0})-{W}_{t_{0}}(\emph{{\textbf{u}}},r_{1},x_{0})-2\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}r\int_{\partial B_{1}(0)}\left|\frac{d}{dr}\emph{{\textbf{u}}}_{r,t_{0}}\right|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}dr\right|\leq C|r_{2}^{\beta}-r_{1}^{\beta}|.

Proof.

Let us denote by
{\textbf{u}}_{r,t_{0}}:=\frac{{\textbf{u}}(rx+x_{0},t_{0})}{r^{\kappa}}
, then

{W}_{t_{0}}({\textbf{u}},r,x_{0})=\int_{B_{1}(0)}|\nabla{\textbf{u}}_{r,t_{0}}|^{2}+\frac{2}{1+q}|{\textbf{u}}_{r,t_{0}}|^{1+q}dx-\kappa\int_{\partial B_{1}(0)}|{\textbf{u}}_{r,t_{0}}|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Hence

(6.1)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}{W}_{t_{0}}({\textbf{u}},r,x_{0})

\displaystyle=2\int_{B_{1}(0)}\nabla{\textbf{u}}_{r,t_{0}}\nabla\frac{d}{dr}{\textbf{u}}_{r,t_{0}}+\frac{{\textbf{u}}_{r,t_{0}}}{|{\textbf{u}}_{r,t_{0}}|^{1-q}}\frac{d}{dr}{\textbf{u}}_{r,t_{0}}dx-2\kappa\int_{\partial B_{1}(0)}{\textbf{u}}_{r,t_{0}}\frac{d}{dr}{\textbf{u}}_{r,t_{0}}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=2\int_{B_{1}(0)}{-\partial_{t}{\textbf{u}}(rx+x_{0},t_{0})}\frac{d}{dr}{\textbf{u}}_{r,t_{0}}dx+2r\int_{\partial B_{1}(0)}\left|\frac{d}{dr}{\textbf{u}}_{r,t_{0}}\right|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Letting
r_{2}>r_{1}>0
and integrating (
6.1
) in the interval
(r_{1},r_{2})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big|{W}_{t_{0}}({\textbf{u}},r_{2},x_{0})-{W}_{t_{0}}({\textbf{u}},r_{1},x_{0})-

\displaystyle 2\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}r\int_{\partial B_{1}(0)}\left|\frac{d}{dr}{\textbf{u}}_{r,t_{0}}\right|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}dr\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\int_{B_{1}(0)}{|\partial_{t}{\textbf{u}}(rx+x_{0},t_{0})|}\left|\frac{d}{dr}{\textbf{u}}_{r,t_{0}}\right|dxdr

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{|\partial_{t}{\textbf{u}}(rx+x_{0},t_{0})|}{r}dxdr

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}r^{\beta-1}dr=\frac{C_{1}}{\beta}(r_{2}^{\beta}-r_{1}^{\beta}).

∎

Theorem 6.2

.

There exists

Proposition 6.3
.

(Energy decay, uniqueness of blow-up limits)
Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Gamma
be a regular point, and
\emph{{\textbf{u}}}_{0}
be any blow-up of
u
at
(x_{0},t_{0})
.
Suppose that the epiperimetric inequality holds with
0<\varepsilon<1
for each

\emph{{\textbf{c}}}_{r}(x,t_{0}):=|x|^{\kappa}\emph{{\textbf{u}}}_{r,t_{0}}\left(x/|x|,t_{0}\right)=\frac{|x|^{\kappa}}{r^{\kappa}}\emph{{\textbf{u}}}\left(x_{0}+rx/|x|,t_{0}\right)

and for all

Then there exists

and

such that

(6.2)

\left|{W}_{t_{0}}(\emph{{\textbf{u}}},r,x_{0})-{W}_{t_{0}}(\emph{{\textbf{u}}},0+,x_{0})\right|\leq Cr^{\gamma},\textrm{ for small }r>0,

and

(6.3)

\int_{\partial B_{1}(0)}\left|\frac{\emph{{\textbf{u}}}(rx+x_{0},t_{0})}{r^{\kappa}}-\emph{{\textbf{u}}}_{0}(x)\right|d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq Cr^{\gamma/2},

therefore
\emph{{\textbf{u}}}_{0}
is the unique blow-up limit of
u
at the point
(x_{0},t_{0})

Proof.

Let
e(r):={W}_{t_{0}}({\textbf{u}},r,x_{0})-{W}_{t_{0}}({\textbf{u}},0+,x_{0})

\displaystyle e^{\prime}(r)=

\displaystyle-\frac{n+2\kappa-2}{r^{n+2\kappa-1}}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|\nabla{\textbf{u}}(x,t_{0})|^{2}+\frac{2}{1+q}{|{\textbf{u}}(x,t_{0})|^{1+q}}dx+\frac{\kappa(n+2\kappa-1)}{r^{n+2\kappa}}\int_{\partial B_{r}(x_{0})}|{\textbf{u}}(x,t_{0})|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{r^{n+2\kappa-2}}\int_{\partial B_{r}(x_{0})}|\nabla{\textbf{u}}(x,t_{0})|^{2}+2{|{\textbf{u}}(x,t_{0})|}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle-\frac{\kappa}{r^{n+2\kappa-1}}\left(2\int_{\partial B_{r}(x_{0})}(\nabla{\textbf{u}}(x,t_{0})\cdot\nu)\cdot{\textbf{u}}(x,t_{0})d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\frac{n-1}{r}\int_{\partial B_{r}(x_{0})}|{\textbf{u}}(x,t_{0})|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{n+2\kappa-2}{r}\left(e(r)+{W}_{t_{0}}({\textbf{u}},0+,x_{0})\right)+\frac{1}{r}\int_{\partial B_{1}(x_{0})}|\nabla{\textbf{u}}_{r}(x,t_{0})|^{2}+\frac{2}{1+q}{|{\textbf{u}}_{r}(x,t_{0})|^{1+q}}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle-\frac{2\kappa}{r}\int_{\partial B_{1}(x_{0})}(\nabla{\textbf{u}}_{r}(x,t_{0})\cdot\nu)\cdot{\textbf{u}}_{r}(x,t_{0})d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\frac{\kappa(n-2)}{r}\int_{\partial B_{1}(x_{0})}|{\textbf{u}}_{r}(x,t_{0})|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\frac{n+2\kappa-2}{r}\left(e(r)+{W}_{t_{0}}({\textbf{u}},0+)\right)+\frac{1}{r}\int_{\partial B_{1}(0)}\left(|\nabla_{\theta}{\textbf{u}}_{r}|^{2}+\frac{2}{1+q}|{\textbf{u}}_{r}|^{1+q}-(\kappa(n-2)+\kappa^{2})|{\textbf{u}}_{r}|^{2}\right)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{n+2\kappa-2}{r}\left(e(r)+{W}_{t_{0}}({\textbf{u}},0+)\right)+\frac{1}{r}\int_{\partial B_{1}(0)}\left(|\nabla_{\theta}{\textbf{c}}_{r}|^{2}+\frac{2}{1+q}|{\textbf{c}}_{r}|^{1+q}-(\kappa(n-2)+\kappa^{2})|{\textbf{c}}_{r}|^{2}\right)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n+2\kappa-2}{r}\left(M({\textbf{c}}_{r})-e(r)-{W}_{t_{0}}({\textbf{u}},0+,x_{0})\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n+2\kappa-2}{r}\left(\frac{M({\textbf{v}})-{W}_{t_{0}}({\textbf{u}},0+,x_{0})}{1-\varepsilon}-e(r)\right),

where we employed the epiperimetric inequality in the last step. Now let us observe that

{\textbf{u}}_{r,t_{0}}
minimises the following energy

where

on
\partial B_{1}(0)
.
Hence

\displaystyle M({\textbf{v}})=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla{\textbf{v}}|^{2}+\frac{2}{1+q}|{\textbf{v}}|^{1+q}dx-\kappa\int_{\partial B_{1}}|{\textbf{v}}|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J({\textbf{v}})-2r^{2-\kappa}\int_{B_{1}}{\textbf{v}}\cdot\partial_{t}{\textbf{u}}(x_{0}+rx,t_{0})dx-\kappa\int_{\partial B_{1}}|{\textbf{u}}_{r,t_{0}}|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle M({\textbf{u}}_{r,t_{0}})+2r^{2-\kappa}\int_{B_{1}}({\textbf{u}}_{r,t_{0}}-{\textbf{v}})\cdot\partial_{t}{\textbf{u}}(x_{0}+rx,t_{0})dx.

(6.4)

\displaystyle e^{\prime}(r)\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n+2\kappa-2}{r(1-\varepsilon)}\left({M({\textbf{u}}_{r,t_{0}})-{W}_{t_{0}}({\textbf{u}},0+,x_{0})}+2r^{2-\kappa}\int_{B_{1}}({\textbf{u}}_{r,t_{0}}-{\textbf{v}})\cdot\partial_{t}{\textbf{u}}(x_{0}+rx,t_{0})dx\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{(n+2\kappa-2)e(r)}{r}=\frac{(n+2\kappa-2)e(r)}{r(1-\varepsilon)}-\frac{(n+2\kappa-2)e(r)}{r}

\displaystyle+2\frac{(n+2\kappa-2)}{(1-\varepsilon)}r^{1-\kappa}\int_{B_{1}}({\textbf{u}}_{r,t_{0}}-{\textbf{v}})\cdot\partial_{t}{\textbf{u}}(x_{0}+rx,t_{0})dx

by Corollary

Lemma 7.1

(Uniqueness of forward problem)
.

be global solutions of (

which have polynomial growth. If
\emph{{\textbf{u}}}(\cdot,s)=\emph{{\textbf{v}}}(\cdot,s)

Proof.

Multiply

by

and integrate

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{s}^{\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(f({\textbf{u}})-f({\textbf{v}}))\cdot({\textbf{u}}-{\textbf{v}})Gdxdt=-\int_{s}^{\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}

\displaystyle\Big[\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}|{\textbf{u}}-{\textbf{v}}|^{2}+|\nabla({\textbf{u}}-{\textbf{v}})|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\nabla({\textbf{u}}-{\textbf{v}})\cdot\frac{x}{2t}\right)\cdot({\textbf{u}}-{\textbf{v}})\Big]Gdxdt.

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{n}{-4t}|{\textbf{w}}|^{2}Gdxdt=:\phi(\tau).

Therefore,
-\frac{2\tau}{n}\phi^{\prime}(\tau)\leq\phi(\tau)
and so
\frac{d}{d\tau}\left[(-\tau)^{n/2}\phi(\tau)\right]\leq 0
for
s<\tau<0
. From
\phi(s)=0
, we conclude that
\phi(\tau)\equiv 0
.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
h
be a caloric function in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times(-4,0]
. Then for
s<t\leq 0
we have the following estimate

e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{t+s}}|h(x,t)|^{2}\leq\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}s}{s-t}\right)^{n}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|h(y,s)|^{2}G(y,s)dy.

Proof.

By the representation of the caloric function, we have

\displaystyle h(x,t)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}h(y,s)G(x-y,s-t)dy.

Then

(7.1)

\displaystyle|h(x,t)|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|h(y,s)|^{2}G(y,s)dy\right)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{\left(G(x-y,s-t)\right)^{2}}{G(y,s)}dy\right)

On the other hand, we can write

\displaystyle\frac{\left(G(x-y,s-t)\right)^{2}}{G(y,s)}=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{-s}{4\pi(t-s)^{2}}\right)^{n/2}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2(t-s)}-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4s}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{-s}{4\pi(t-s)^{2}}\right)^{n/2}\exp\left(-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2(t-s)}+\frac{x\cdot y}{t-s}-\frac{(t+s)|y|^{2}}{4s(t-s)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{-s}{4\pi(t-s)^{2}}\right)^{n/2}\exp\left(\left(-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon\right)\frac{|x|^{2}}{t-s}+\left(\frac{1}{4\epsilon}-\frac{t+s}{4s}\right)\frac{|y|^{2}}{t-s}\right).

For every
\epsilon>\frac{s}{t+s}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\exp\left(\left(\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon\right)\frac{|x|^{2}}{t-s}\right)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{\left(G(x-y,s-t)\right)^{2}}{G(y,s)}dy\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{-s}{4\pi(t-s)^{2}}\right)^{n/2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\exp\left(\left(\frac{1}{4\epsilon}-\frac{t+s}{4s}\right)\frac{|y|^{2}}{t-s}\right)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{-s}{(t-s)\left(\frac{t+s}{s}-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)}\right)^{n/2}.

Now let
\epsilon=\frac{3s-t}{2(t+s)}
, so by (
7.1
) the proof will be done.
∎

Lemma 7.3
.

Assume that
\emph{{\textbf{w}}}\in L^{2}(Q_{4})
has polynomial growth and
t<0
fixed, then

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\emph{{\textbf{w}}}(x,t)|^{2}\frac{|x|^{2}}{-t}G(x,t)dx\leq 4\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(n|\emph{{\textbf{w}}}(x,t)|^{2}-4t|\nabla\emph{{\textbf{w}}}(x,t)|^{2}\right)G(x,t)dx.

Proof.

Using the relation
\nabla G(x,t)=\frac{x}{2t}G(x,t)
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|{\textbf{w}}(x,t)|^{2}\frac{|x|^{2}}{-t}G(x,t)dx

\displaystyle=-2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|{\textbf{w}}|^{2}(x\cdot\nabla G)dx=2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\rm div}(|{\textbf{w}}|^{2}x)Gdx

\displaystyle=2n\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|{\textbf{w}}|^{2}Gdx+4\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\textbf{w}}\cdot(\nabla{\textbf{w}}\cdot x)Gdx

\displaystyle\leq 2n\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|{\textbf{w}}|^{2}Gdx+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|{\textbf{w}}|^{2}\frac{|x|^{2}}{-2t}Gdx+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(-8t)|\nabla{\textbf{w}}|^{2}Gdx.

Now we can easily prove the lemma.
∎

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
u
be a function defined in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times[-R,0)
(for some
a,R>0
) with polynomial growth, and
p
be a
\kappa
-backward self-similar caloric vector-function.
Then for
-R<t_{1}<t_{2}<0

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(|\nabla(\emph{{\textbf{p}}}-\emph{{\textbf{u}}})|^{2}+\frac{\kappa|\emph{{\textbf{p}}}-\emph{{\textbf{u}}}|^{2}}{2t}\right)Gdxdt=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(|\nabla\emph{{\textbf{u}}}|^{2}+\frac{\kappa|\emph{{\textbf{u}}}|^{2}}{2t}\right)Gdxdt.

Proof.

Since
\nabla G(x,t)=\frac{x}{2t}G(x,t)
, we have

(\nabla{\textbf{u}}:\nabla{\textbf{v}})G=\nabla{\textbf{u}}:\nabla({\textbf{v}}G)-(\nabla{\textbf{u}}\cdot x)\cdot\frac{{\textbf{v}}G}{2t}.

Obviously,
|\nabla({\textbf{p}}-{\textbf{u}})|^{2}=|\nabla{\textbf{u}}|^{2}-2\nabla{\textbf{p}}:\nabla{\textbf{u}}+|\nabla{\textbf{p}}|^{2}
, hence

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(|\nabla({\textbf{p}}-{\textbf{u}})|^{2}+\frac{\kappa|{\textbf{p}}-{\textbf{u}}|^{2}}{2t}\right)Gdxdt=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(|\nabla{\textbf{u}}|^{2}+\frac{\kappa|{\textbf{u}}|^{2}}{2t}\right)Gdxdt

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\nabla{\textbf{p}}:(\nabla{\textbf{p}}-2\nabla{\textbf{u}})Gdxdt

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\kappa\frac{{\textbf{p}}\cdot({\textbf{p}}-2{\textbf{u}})}{2t}Gdxdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(|\nabla{\textbf{u}}|^{2}+\frac{\kappa|{\textbf{u}}|^{2}}{2t}\right)Gdxdt

\displaystyle-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(\Delta{\textbf{p}}+\frac{1}{2t}x\cdot\nabla{\textbf{p}}-\frac{\kappa{\textbf{p}}}{2t}\right)\cdot({\textbf{p}}-2{\textbf{u}})Gdxdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(|\nabla{\textbf{u}}|^{2}+\frac{\kappa|{\textbf{u}}|^{2}}{2t}\right)Gdxdt

where we used integration by parts and that

\Delta{\textbf{p}}+\frac{1}{2t}x\cdot\nabla{\textbf{p}}-\frac{\kappa{\textbf{p}}}{2t}=\partial_{t}{\textbf{p}}+\frac{1}{2t}x\cdot\nabla{\textbf{p}}-\frac{\kappa}{2}\frac{{\textbf{p}}}{t}=\frac{1}{2t}L{\textbf{p}}=0.\qed

Lemma 7.5
.

Consider
\beta>0
to be noninteger, and let
u(x,t)
be a function satisfying

|H(u)(X)|\leq C_{*}|X|^{\beta}.

Then there is a caloric polynomial
P
of degree at most
\lfloor\beta\rfloor+2
such that

\lVert u-P\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r}^{-})}\leq CC_{*}r^{\beta+2},\qquad\text{for }r\in(0,1),

where constant
C
depends only on
n,\beta
and
\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{-})}
.

Proof.

We can assume that
\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{-})}\leq 1
and
C_{*}\leq\delta
, where
\delta
is small enough and will be determined later. (Replace
u
by
u(R^{-1}x,R^{-2}t)
for a large fixed constant
R
to find
|H(u)|\leq\delta|X|^{\beta}
)
The proof of lemma is based on the following claim.

Claim:
There exists
0<\rho<1
and a sequence of caloric polynomials
P_{k}
such that

\lVert u-P_{k}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\rho^{k}}^{-})}\leq\rho^{k(\beta+2)},

and

|\partial_{x}^{\mu}\partial_{t}^{\ell}(P_{k}-P_{k-1})(0,0)|\leq C_{0}\rho^{(k-1)(\beta+2-|\mu|-2\ell)},\quad\text{if }|\mu|+2\ell<\beta+2.

A straight forward implication of this claim is that the sequence
\{P_{k}\}
converges uniformly in
Q_{1}
to a polynomial
P
of degree at most
\lfloor\beta\rfloor+2
which clearly satisfies

\displaystyle\lVert u-P\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\rho^{k}}^{-})}\leq

\displaystyle\lVert u-P_{k}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\rho^{k}}^{-})}+\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}\lVert P_{i}-P_{i-1}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\rho^{k}}^{-})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\rho^{k(\beta+2)}+\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\mu|+2\ell<\beta+2}C_{0}\rho^{(i-1)(\beta+2-|\mu|-2\ell)}\rho^{k(|\mu|+2\ell)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\rho^{k(\beta+2)}+\sum_{|\mu|+2\ell<\beta+2}C_{0}\rho^{k(\beta+2)}\leq C_{n,\beta}C_{0}\rho^{k(\beta+2)}.

Therefore, the lemma will be proved for
C:=\frac{1}{\delta}C_{n,\beta}C_{0}\rho^{-(\beta+2)}
.

Now we prove the claim. It is obviously true for
k=0
(just take
P_{0}\equiv P_{-1}\equiv 0
). We now assume that it holds for
k
and we prove it for
k+1
. Define

v(X):=\frac{u(\rho^{k}x,\rho^{2k}t)-P_{k}(\rho^{k}x,\rho^{2k}t)}{\rho^{k(\beta+2)}}.

Then by inductive hypothesis
|v|\leq 1
in
Q_{1}^{-}
. In addition,

|H(v)|=\left|\frac{H(u)(\rho^{k}x,\rho^{2k}t)}{\rho^{k\beta}}\right|\leq C_{*}\leq\delta.

If we apply Lemma 6.1 in
[
7
]
, there exist
\delta=\delta(\epsilon)
and function
w
satisfying

|v-w|\leq\epsilon,\quad\text{ in }Q_{1/2}^{-},

and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}H(w)=0&\text{in }Q_{1/2}^{-},\\[8.0pt]
w=v&\text{on }\partial_{p}Q_{1/2}^{-}.\end{array}\right.

Now consider a polynomial
\hat{P}
of degree at most
\lfloor\beta\rfloor+2
such that

\partial_{x}^{\mu}\partial_{t}^{\ell}\hat{P}(0,0)=\partial_{x}^{\mu}\partial_{t}^{\ell}w(0,0)
for
|\mu|+\ell<\beta+2
.
Since
\lVert w\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1/2}^{-})}\leq\lVert v\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{-})}\leq 1
, by estimates on derivatives for caloric functions
|\partial_{x}^{\mu}\partial_{t}^{\ell}\hat{P}(0,0)|\leq C_{0}
for a universal constant
C_{0}
.
Obviously,
\hat{P}
is caloric and

\lVert w-\hat{P}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\rho}^{-})}\leq C_{0}\rho^{\lfloor\beta\rfloor+3}.

In particular, if we choose
\rho
sufficiently small so that
C_{0}\rho^{\lfloor\beta\rfloor+3}\leq\frac{1}{2}\rho^{\beta+2}
and then choose
\epsilon
such that
\epsilon\leq\frac{1}{2}\rho^{\beta+2}
, we arrive at

\lVert v-\hat{P}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\rho}^{-})}\leq\rho^{\beta+2},

or equivalently

\lVert u-P_{k+1}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\rho^{k+1}}^{-})}\leq\rho^{(k+1)(\beta+2)},\quad P_{k+1}(X):=P_{k}(X)+\rho^{k(\beta+2)}\hat{P}(\rho^{-k}x,\rho^{-2k}t).

We also have

|\partial_{x}^{\mu}\partial_{t}^{\ell}(P_{k+1}-P_{k})(0,0)|\leq\rho^{k(\beta+2-|\mu|-\ell)}|\partial_{x}^{\mu}\partial_{t}^{\ell}\hat{P}(0,0)|\leq C_{0}\rho^{k(\beta+2-|\mu|-\ell)}.

∎

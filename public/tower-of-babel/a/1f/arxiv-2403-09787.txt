Into Multiplier Hopf ( ∗ * -)Graph Algebras
Farrokh Razavinia, Ghorbanali Haghighatdoost

Abstract
This paper is concerned with the structures introduced recently by the authors of the current paper concerning the multiplier Hopf ∗ * -graph algebras and also the Cuntz-Krieger algebras and their relations with the C ∗ C^{*} -graph algebras, and once again by using the C ∗ C^{*} -graph algebra constructions associated to our toy example, to initiate our first example concerning the multiplier Hopf graph algebras. MSC Numbers (2020) : Primary 46L05; Secondary 46L67, 81P40, 46L55, 17B81. Keywords : multiplier Hopf algebra; magic unitary; quantum graph; graph algebra; C ∗ C^{*} -algebra; Cuntz-Krieger algebra; quantum permutation group.

Going back to the mid-eighties, there is the fascinating constructive definition and the only exact and fulfilled definition of a category of quantum groups, known as the compact (matrix) quantum groups studied and developed by Woronowicz

A compact quantum group is a pair

Our concern is more about the compact quantum groups arising from the (semi-)group algebras. For the compact group

Following the above discussion, and in order to provide an answer to a question by Connes, asking if “there are quantum permutation groups, and what would they look like?

Cuntz-Krieger Algebras

Here in this paper, our developments are mostly concerned with the finite directed graphs. Talking about the Cuntz-Krieger algebras, we need to take some steps backwards and talk about the Cuntz algebras were introduced by Cuntz in 1977 as a class of simple purely infinite
C^{*}
-algebras generated by isometries (
S
is an isometry if
S^{*}S=\id

[
4
]

Definition 1.5
.

Let
n\geq 2
. A universal
C^{*}
-algebra generated by isometries
S_{1},S_{2},\cdots,S_{n}
satisfying in the following relations

S_{1}^{*}S_{1}=S_{2}^{*}S_{2}=\cdots=S_{n}^{*}S_{n}=\id,\qquad\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}S_{i}^{*}=\id,

will be called a Cuntz algebra and will be denoted by
\mathcal{O}_{n}
.

Following the above definition, to a directed graph
\Gamma

C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(\Gamma^{0},\Gamma^{1}):=C^{*}(\Gamma)
by associating to its set of edges
\Gamma^{1}

\Gamma^{0}
a set of pairwise orthogonal projections (Hilbert spaces) satisfying in some specific relations, studied first by Cuntz and Krieger in 1980
[
5
]
, as a generalization of the Cuntz algebras.

By a directed graph
\Gamma=(\Gamma^{0},\Gamma^{1})
we mean a set
\Gamma^{0}
of vertices and a set
\Gamma^{1}
of edges alongside with source and range maps
r,s:\Gamma^{1}\to\Gamma^{0}
, and accordingly a vertex

will be called a sink if and only if

is empty, meaning that when
v
emits no edges, and a source if and only if
r^{-1}(v)
is empty, meaning that when
v
receives no edges.

Remark 1.6
.

1.

The graph
\Gamma
will be called simple if the map
\Gamma^{1}\to\Gamma^{0}\times\Gamma^{0}
taking

(s(e),r(e))
is injective. Meaning that it will be called simple if consists of no multiple edges.

2.

The directed graph

with
L\Gamma^{0}=\Gamma^{1}
and
L\Gamma^{1}=\{(e_{1},e_{2})|r(e_{1})=s(e_{2})\}
as a subset of
\Gamma^{1}\times\Gamma^{1}
will be called the line graph of
\Gamma
.

3.

Any line graph is simple.

4.

The
\Gamma^{0}\times\Gamma^{0}
-matrix
A_{\Gamma}(e)=\begin{cases}1\quad e\in\Gamma^{1},\\
0\quad e\notin\Gamma^{1},\end{cases},
will be called the adjacency matrix of
\Gamma=(\Gamma^{0},\Gamma^{1})
.

5.

The
\Gamma^{1}\times\Gamma^{1}
-matrix

0\quad else,\end{cases},
will be called the edge matrix of
\Gamma=(\Gamma^{0},\Gamma^{1})
.

6.

The edge matrix

of

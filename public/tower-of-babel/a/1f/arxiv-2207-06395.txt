Rough homogenization for Langevin dynamics on fluctuating Helfrich surfaces
Ana Djurdjevac, Helena Kremp, Nicolas Perkowski, Ana Djurdjevac, Helena Kremp, Nicolas Perkowski

Abstract
In this paper, we study different scaling rough path limit regimes in space and time for the Langevin dynamics on a quasi-planar fluctuating Helfrich surfaces. The convergence results of the processes were already proven in [ DEPS15 ] . We extend this work by proving the convergence of the Itô and Stratonovich rough path lift. For the rough path limit, there appears, typically, an area correction term to the Itô iterated integrals, and in certain regimes to the Stratonovich iterated integrals. This yields additional information on the homogenization limit and enables to conclude on homogenization results for diffusions driven by the Brownian motion on the membrane using the continuity of the Itô-Lyons map in rough paths topology. Keywords: Brownian motion on a random hypersurface, lateral diffusion, Laplace-Beltrami operator, Helfrich membrane, rough stochastic homogenization MSC2020: 35B27, 37A50, 60F05, 60H30, 60J55, 60L20

Introduction

The lateral diffusion of particles is crucial
for cellular processes, including signal transmission, cellular organization and the
transport of matter (cf.
[
MP11
,
MHS14
,
LP13
,
AV95
]
). Motivated by these applications, i.e. diffusion on cell membranes, we consider the diffusion on a curved domain - a hypersurface
\mathcal{S}
, cf
[
Sei97
]
. The Brownian motion on the surface, whose generator in local coordinates is the Laplace-Beltrami operator, is a simple example of a diffusing particle on a biological surface, which is also known from physics as the overdamped Langevin dynamics on a Helfrich membrane (cf.
[
NB07
]
).

We will restrict our considerations to the classical situation of the so-called “essentially
flat surfaces”
\mathcal{S}
. The standard way of representing the essentially flat surface is the Monge-gauge parametrization, where we specify the height
H
of the hypersurface as a function of the coordinates from the flat base, namely over
[0,L]^{2}
or, since we consider the periodic setting, over
\mathbb{T}^{2}
(such that we take
L=1
).

Moreover, these membranes are fluctuating both in time and space due to the spatial micro-structure and thermal fluctuations or active proteins.
The analysis of the macroscopic behavior of a laterally diffusive process on surfaces possessing microscopic space and time scales was derived in
[
Dun13
,
DEPS15
]
. Based on classical methods from homogenization theory, they prove that under the assumption of scale separation between the characteristic length and time scales of the
membrane fluctuations and the characteristic scale of the diffusing particle, the lateral
diffusion process can be well approximated by a Brownian motion on the plane with
constant diffusion tensor
D
. In particular, they show that
D
depends in a highly nonlinear way on the detailed properties of the surface.

Since this work is motivated by the Helfrich elasticity membrane model, we briefly describe it here in order to better understand understand the form of the considered system of SDEs.

The classical description of fluid membranes
\mathcal{S}
on the level of continuum elasticity is based on the Canham
[
Can70
]
- Helfrich
[
Hel73
]

\mathcal{E}[\mathcal{S}]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{[0,L]^{2}}\kappa K^{2}(x)dx\sqrt{|G|(x)}dx,

where

is the metric tensor of
\mathcal{S}
in local coordinates,

is the mean and the constant

[
Des15
]

Utilizing the standard approach (see
[
DEPS15
,
DE88
]
) one can derive the dynamics of the surface fluctuations that are described by the stochastic partial differential equation (SPDE) of the type:

\frac{dH(t)}{dt}=-RAH(t)+\xi(t)

(1.1)

where

(

. Moreover,

[

,

is the viscosity of the surrounding medium). The last term

is a Gaussian field, that is white in time and with spatial fluctuations having mean zero and covariance operator
2(k_{B}T)R
,
k_{B}
being the Boltzmann constant. Next, we consider the ansazt for the height function as the truncated Galerkin-projection on the Fourier spanned space given by

\displaystyle H(x,t)=h(x,\eta_{t})=\sum_{\lvert k\rvert\leqslant\tilde{K}}\eta_{t}^{k}e_{k}(x),\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},

(1.2)

for the Fourier basis
(e_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
on the torus

and fixed cut-off
\tilde{K}\in\mathbb{N}
. Substituting the
Equation

\eta=(\eta^{k})_{\lvert k\rvert\leqslant K}
with
K:=\#\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\mid 0<\lvert k\rvert\leqslant\tilde{K}\}
are independent Ornstein Uhlenbeck processes given by

\displaystyle d\eta^{k}_{t}=-\frac{\kappa\lvert 2\pi k\rvert^{3}+\sigma\lvert 2\pi k\rvert}{4\lambda}\eta^{k}_{t}dt+\sqrt{\frac{k_{B}T}{2\lambda\lvert 2\pi k\rvert}}dW^{k}_{t},

where
(W^{k})_{k}
are independent complex-valued standard Brownian motions with constraint
\overline{W^{k}}=W^{-k}
, i.e.
W^{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(Re(W^{k})+iIm(W^{k}))
with
Re(W^{k}),Im(W^{k})
independent
\mathbb{R}^{K}
-valued Brownian motions. Moreover, using
[
Stu10
, Lemma 6.25]
, we notice that without the ultra-violett cutoff

, the surface

is almost surely Hölder-continuous with exponent
\alpha<1
(in

Next, we consider a particle on the surface

with the molecular diffusion

. All together,

, section 2.2]

\displaystyle dX^{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle=F(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{t})dt+\sqrt{\Sigma(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{t})}dB(t)

(1.3)

\displaystyle d\eta^{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Gamma\eta^{\varepsilon}_{t}dt+\sqrt{\frac{2\Gamma\Pi}{\varepsilon}}dW(t),

(1.4)

where

. The coefficients

Based on this particular setting, in
[

]
the authors generalize this model (

Motivated by the recent link between stochastic homogenization and rough paths from the works
[
KM16
,
FGL15
,
CFK+19
,
DOP21
]
,
we prove a rough homogenization result for a Brownian particle on a fluctuating Gaussian hypersurface with covariance given by (the ultra-violet cutoff of) the Helfrich energy. Specifically, we extend previously mentioned results with proving the convergence towards a particular lift of the homogenization limit in rough path topology for different scaling regimes
(\alpha,\beta)
. Interestingly, in some regimes, the rough path lift of
(X^{\varepsilon})
converges to a non-trivial lift of the limiting Brownian motion

, in the sense that, additionally, an area correction to the iterated integrals appears. This phenomenon was already observed by
[
LL05
]
and
[
FGL15
]
in different situations. Considering the scaling limits in the rough path topology yields more information on the homogenization limit and enables us to conclude homogenization results for diffusions driven by the Brownian motion on the membrane using the continuity of the Itô-Lyons map in rough path topology.

As in the works by
[
Dun13
,
DEPS15
,
DOP21
]
, we utilize martingale methods for additive functionals of Markov processes (cf. also
[
KLO12
]
). That is, we identify a stationary, ergodic Markov process and exploit the solution of the associated Poisson equation for the generator of that Markov process to rewrite the (in general unbounded) drift term of
X^{\varepsilon}
. We consider both Itô and Stratonovich rough path lifts. As already observed in
[
DOP21
]
, for the Stratonovich lift we expect an area correction to appear if and only if the underlying Markov process is non-reversible. Indeed, in the regime
(\alpha,\beta)=(1,1)
, we have that the underlying Markov process
Y^{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon^{-1}X^{\varepsilon}
is reversible (under its invariant measure, for each fixed, stationary realization of
\eta
), and also the Stratonovich limit is the usual Stratonovich lift of the Brownian motion
X
. Contrary to that, in the
(\alpha,\beta)=(1,2)
regime, an area correction for the Stratonovich lift appears (which we expect is truly non-vanishing). In the regime
(\alpha,\beta)=(0,1)
the limit is obtained by averaging over the invariant measure of the Ornstein Uhlenbeck process
\eta
; and also the rough limit is given by the canonical lift of the Brownian motion, which follows as in this case the uniform controlled variation (UCV) condition is satisfied for
(X^{\varepsilon})
.

In the regime
(\alpha,\beta)=(1,-\infty)
and
\eta_{0}
being deterministic (i.e.
H
being a non-random periodic surface), we have that
X
is a diffusion with periodic coefficients and the equality in law
X^{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}(\varepsilon X_{\varepsilon^{-2}t})_{t}
holds. Note that this case corresponds to the quenched regime, i.e. we consider the surface to be time-independent (or fix a stationary realization
\eta_{0}
). Due to the functional central limit theorem for the Itô rough path lift of
X^{\varepsilon}
proven in
[
DOP21
, section 4.3]
the rough path limit is given by a nontrivial lift of the limiting Brownian motion
X
.

Rough homogenization results are useful in combination with the continuity of the Itô-Lyons map to obtain rough homogenization results for particles whose velocity is governed by the velocity of the particle
X
.
For example, processes like fusion are initiated by the velocities of particles itself. However, note that in this case the underdamped Langevin dynamics would better describe the system, and in our work we consider the overdamped Langevin dynamics. Hence, our results could be seen as a starting point for considering these more complicated systems.

The paper is structured as follows.
Section

2

. We also collect knowledge about the generator of the Markov process
(X,\eta)
and the growth conditions on the coefficients, as well as recall the definition of the space of

Section

Section

Preliminaries

In this section we will fix the definition of the time dependent random hypersurface
H(x,t)
and the diffusion
X
on
H
. For more details, we refer to
[
Dun13
,
DEPS15
]
.

We will consider fluctuating hypersurfaces that can be represented as a graph of a sufficiently smooth random field
H:[0,L]^{d}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
, i.e. it is represented via the so-called Monge gauge parametrization. More precisely, we assume that for each

t>0
,
H(x;t)
is smooth in
x
and
H(x;t)
is periodic in
x
with period
L_{H}
for each
t>0
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
L_{H}=1
.
Furthermore, we assume the existence of a characteristic timescale
T_{H}=T
, which models the observation time of the system. The hypersurface
\mathcal{S}(t)
is parametrized over
[0,1]^{d}
by
J:[0,1]^{d}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
as

J(x,t)=(x,H(x,t)).

(2.1)

The metric tensor of
\mathcal{S}(t)
in local coordinates
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is given by

G(x,t)=I+\nabla H(x,t)\otimes\nabla H(x,t)

and we define

|G|(x,t):=\det G(x,t)=1+|\nabla H(x,t)|^{2}.

Due to the physical application of the hypersurface, we restrict the dimension to
d=2
, but mathematically all results below also apply for general dimension
d
.
The dimension would become relevant if one considered taking the cutoff away, as the surface becomes rougher with increasing dimension.

Motivated by the Helfrich elastic fluctuating membrane model presented in the introduction (
1.1
), we assume that the random field
H(x,t)
is Gaussian and it can be written as
H(x,t)=h(x,\eta_{t})
via Fourier expansion with ultra-violet cutoff (for fixed
\tilde{K}\in\mathbb{N}
). More precisely, we define

\displaystyle H(x,t):=h(x,\eta_{t}):=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\,0<\lvert k\rvert\leqslant\tilde{K}}\eta^{k}_{t}e_{k}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}

(2.2)

(without the
k=0
Fourier mode) where
(e_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
is the Fourier basis on the two-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, i.e.
e_{k}(x)=\exp(2\pi ik\cdot x)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
and
\mathbb{T}^{2}
is identified with
[0,1]^{2}
. The cutoff is needed to ensure differentiability of the surface. The equation (
1.1
) for
H
then yields the dynamics of
\eta=(\eta^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:0<\lvert k\rvert\leqslant\tilde{K}}
, which is given by the complex-valued Ornstein Uhlenbeck processes with

\displaystyle d\eta_{t}=-\Gamma\eta_{t}dt+\sqrt{2\Gamma\Pi}dW_{t}

for a
K
-dimensional standard complex-valued Brownian motion
W
with complex conjugate
\overline{W^{k}_{t}}=W^{-k}_{t}
, where
K:=\#\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\mid 0<\lvert k\rvert\leqslant\tilde{K}\}
and where
\Gamma,\Pi
are real matrices, such that
\overline{\eta^{k}}=\eta^{-k}
, which implies that
H
is real-valued as
\overline{H}=H
.
Since we work in the real-valued setting, we
identify
\eta
with
(Re(\eta),Im(\eta))
, which is a
2K
-dimensional real-valued Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with the above dynamics for a
2K
-dimensional real-valued standard Brownian motion
W
and with the property that
Re(\eta^{k})=Re(\eta^{-k})
and
Im(\eta^{k})=-Im(\eta^{-k})
. The drift matrix
\Gamma
is symmetric and positive definite, defined by
\Gamma:=diag(\Gamma_{k})
with

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}=\frac{\kappa^{\ast}\lvert 2\pi k\rvert^{4}+\sigma^{\ast}\lvert 2\pi k\rvert^{2}}{\lvert 2\pi k\rvert},

where
\kappa^{\ast}=\kappa/(k_{B}T),\sigma^{\ast}=\sigma/(k_{B}T)
are positive constants that depend on the geometry of
\mathcal{S}
.
The diffusion matrix
\Pi
is also symmetric and positive definite and defined as
\Pi:=diag(\Pi_{k})
with

\displaystyle\Pi_{k}=\frac{1}{\kappa^{\ast}\lvert 2\pi k\rvert^{4}+\sigma^{\ast}\lvert 2\pi k\rvert^{2}}.

Since the symmetric, positive-definite matrices
\Gamma,\Pi
commute, the normal distribution

N(0,\Pi)=:\rho_{\eta}

(2.3)

is the invariant measure for the Ornstein Uhlenbeck process
\eta
. Then we have

\rho_{\eta}(d\eta)=\rho_{\eta}d\eta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^{2}\lvert\Pi\rvert}}\exp(-\frac{1}{2}\eta\cdot\Pi^{-1}\cdot\eta)d\eta,

(2.4)

with
\lvert\Pi\rvert:=\det(\Pi)
(note that we use the same notation
\rho_{\eta}
for the measure and its density).
The generator
\mathcal{L}_{\eta}
of
\eta
is given by

\mathcal{L}_{\eta}=-\Gamma\eta\cdot\nabla_{\eta}+\Pi\Gamma:\nabla_{\eta}\nabla_{\eta}.

(2.5)

Here and later on, we will use the notation

A:\nabla_{x}\nabla_{x}f(x):=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}A_{i,j}\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}f(x)\quad\text{for }A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n},f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}),n\in\mathbb{N}.

The generator
\mathcal{L}_{\eta}
is a closed, unbounded operator on
L^{2}(\rho_{\eta})
with domain
dom(\mathcal{L}_{\eta})=\{f\in L^{2}(\rho_{\eta})\mid\mathcal{L}_{\eta}f\in L^{2}(\rho_{\eta})\}
. Observe that, since
\mathcal{L}_{\eta}^{\ast}\rho_{\eta}=0
for the Lebesgue adjoint
\mathcal{L}_{\eta}^{\ast}
, the invariance of
\rho_{\eta}
, i.e.

\langle\mathcal{L}_{\eta}f\rangle_{\rho_{\eta}}=\int\mathcal{L}_{\eta}f(x)\rho_{\eta}(x)dx=0,\quad\text{for all }f\in dom(\mathcal{L}_{\eta}),

can be checked easily. Let us define the space

H^{1}(\rho_{\eta}):=\{f\in dom(\mathcal{L}_{\eta})\mid\langle(-\mathcal{L}_{\eta})f,f\rangle_{\rho_{\eta}}<\infty\}.

(2.6)

Furthermore, notice that for the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck generator
\mathcal{L}_{\eta}
, spectral gap estimates hold true, that is, there exists a constant
C>0
, such that

\displaystyle\lVert f\rVert_{H^{1}(\rho_{\eta})}^{2}=\langle(-\mathcal{L}_{\eta})f,f\rangle_{\rho_{\eta}}\geqslant C\lVert f-\langle f\rangle_{\rho_{\eta}}\rVert_{\rho_{\eta}}^{2}

(2.7)

for any

\langle f\rangle_{\rho_{\eta}}:=\int f(\eta)d\rho_{\eta}
. Indeed, from a simple calculation using the invariance for

, it follows that
\langle(-\mathcal{L}_{\eta})f,f\rangle_{\rho_{\eta}}=\langle\Pi\Gamma\nabla_{\eta}f,\nabla_{\eta}f\rangle_{\rho_{\eta}}

\min_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\rho_{\eta}>0
and the Poincaré-inequality for the Laplacian on

In particular,
\rho_{\eta}
is an ergodic measure for the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process
\eta
. Furthermore, if

is centered under
\rho_{\eta}
, then

is centered by invariance and thus (

of the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process:

\displaystyle\lVert P_{t}^{\eta}f-\langle f\rangle_{\rho_{\eta}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\rho_{\eta})}\leqslant e^{-Ct}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\rho_{\eta})}

for all

]
, cf. the rough central limit theorem for diffusions with periodic coefficients in
[
DOP21
, section 4.3]
.

The convergence results will be proven in
\gamma
-Hölder rough path topology. Here we briefly recall the definition of a
\gamma
-Hölder rough path and for more details we refer the reader to
[
FH20
]
. We will write
X_{s,t}:=X_{t}-X_{s}
and we define

\Delta_{T}:=\{(s,t)\in[0,T]^{2}\mid s\leqslant t\}.

Definition 2.2
.

[
FH20
, Def. 2.1]

For
\gamma\in(1/3,1/2]
we call
(X,\mathbb{X})\in C([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})\times C(\Delta_{T},\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
a
\gamma
-Hölder rough path if:

i)

Chen’s relation holds, that is

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}_{r,t}-\mathbb{X}_{r,s}-\mathbb{X}_{s,t}=X_{s,u}\otimes X_{u,t}

for all
0\leqslant r\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T
with
\mathbb{X}_{t,t}=0
, where
X_{s,t}:=X_{t}-X_{s}
,

ii)

the (inhomogeneous)
\gamma
-Hölder norms are finite, that is

\displaystyle\lVert(X,\mathbb{X})\rVert_{\gamma}:=\lVert X\rVert_{\gamma,T}+\lVert\mathbb{X}\rVert_{2\gamma,T}:=\sup_{0\leqslant s<t\leqslant T}\frac{\lvert X_{s,t}\rvert}{\lvert t-s\rvert^{\gamma}}+\sup_{0\leqslant s<t\leqslant T}\frac{\lvert\mathbb{X}_{s,t}\rvert}{\lvert t-s\rvert^{2\gamma}}<\infty.

We denote the nonlinear space of all such
\gamma
-Hölder rough paths by
C_{\gamma,T}
equipped with the distance

\displaystyle\lVert(X^{1},\mathbb{X}^{1});(X^{2},\mathbb{X}^{2})\rVert_{\gamma}=\lVert X^{1}-X^{2}\rVert_{\gamma,T}+\lVert\mathbb{X}^{1}-\mathbb{X}^{2}\rVert_{2\gamma,T}.

Remark 2.3
.

For a two-dimensional Brownian motion
B
, the Itô lift
(B,\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{Ito}})
, where
\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{Ito}}(s,t):=\int_{s}^{t}B_{s,r}\otimes dB_{r}
are Itô-integrals, as well as the Stratonovich lift
(B,\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{Strato}})
for
\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{Strato}}(s,t):=\int_{s}^{t}B_{s,r}\otimes\circ dB_{r}
being Stratonovich integrals, are for any
\varepsilon>0
almost surely
\gamma
-Hölder rough path for
\gamma=1/2-\varepsilon
, cf.
[
FH20
, Ch. 3]
. But also
(B,(s,t)\mapsto\mathbb{B}_{s,t}+A(t-s))
is a
\gamma
-rough path, where
A
is a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
and
\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{Ito}}
or
\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{Strato}}
. The latter will be the lift of the Brownian motion that we encounter below.

We finalize this section by recalling the concept of uniform controlled variations by Kurtz and Protter (
[
KP91
, Definition 7.3]
; here for continuous semimartingales without the need of stopping times).

Definition 2.4
.

A sequence
(X^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued continuous semi-martingales on
[0,T]
decomposed as the sum
X^{\varepsilon}=M^{\varepsilon}+A^{\varepsilon}
, where
M^{\varepsilon}
is a local martingale and
A^{\varepsilon}
is of finite variation, satisfies the UCV (Uniformly Controlled Variations) condition if and only if

(\left<M^{\varepsilon,i}\right>_{T})_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{and}\quad(\mathrm{Var}_{1,[0,T]}(A^{\varepsilon}))_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{are tight in }\mathbb{R},

(2.16)

for
i=1,...,d
and
\mathrm{Var}_{1,[0,T]}(f):=\lim_{\lvert\pi\rvert\to 0}\sum_{s,t\in\pi}\lvert f_{t}-f_{s}\rvert
denotes the one-variation of a function
f:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the limit is taken over all finite partitions
\pi
of
[0,T]
with mesh size
\lvert\pi\rvert=\max_{s,t\in\pi}\lvert t-s\rvert\to 0
.

Remark 2.5
.

The tightness (
2.16
) follows from

\displaystyle\max_{i=1,\dots,d}\sup_{\varepsilon}\big(\mathds{E}[\langle M^{\varepsilon,i}\rangle_{T}]+\mathds{E}[\mathrm{Var}_{1,[0,T]}(A^{\varepsilon})]\big)<\infty,

(2.17)

which is the bound, as we will verify when showing that a semimartingale satisfies the UCV condition.

We state a version of
[
KP91
, Definition 7.1, Theorem 7.4]
, that will be repeatedly exploited in the sequel in order to prove the distributional convergence of certain Itô integrals. For the proof see
[
KP96
, Thm. 7.4]
and
[
KP91
, Thm. 2.2]
.

Proposition 2.6
.

[
KP96
, Thm. 7.4]

A sequence
(X^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued continuous semi-martingales on
[0,T]
satisfies the UCV condition if and only if for all sequences
(Y^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
with
(Y^{\varepsilon},X^{\varepsilon})\Rightarrow(Y,X)
jointly in distribution in
C([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and with
Y^{\varepsilon}
integrable against
X^{\varepsilon}
and
Y
against
X
in the Itô sense, it follows that
(Y^{\varepsilon},X^{\varepsilon},\int_{0}^{\cdot}Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}\otimes dX^{\varepsilon}_{s})\Rightarrow(Y,X,\int_{0}^{\cdot}Y_{s}\otimes dX_{s})
in distribution in
C([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{2d+d\times d})
.

Let us furthermore state a version of the Kolmogorov criterion for rough paths,
[
FH20
, Theorem 3.1]
, that we use in the sequel.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
(X^{\varepsilon},\mathbb{X}^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
be a family of
\gamma
-Hölder rough path,
\gamma<1/2
. Assume that for any
p>2
, there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that:

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon}\mathds{E}[\lvert X^{\varepsilon,i}_{t}-X^{\varepsilon,i}_{s}\rvert^{p}]\leqslant C_{1}\lvert t-s\rvert^{p/2},\quad\forall s,t\in[0,T]

(2.18)

and

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon}\mathds{E}\bigg[\bigg\lvert\int_{s}^{t}X^{\varepsilon,i}_{s,r}dX^{\varepsilon,j}_{r}\bigg\rvert^{p/2}\bigg]\leqslant C_{2}\lvert t-s\rvert^{p/2},\quad\forall s,t\in\Delta_{T}

(2.19)

for
i,j\in\{1,...,d\}
.
Then for any

,

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon}\mathds{E}[\lVert(X^{\varepsilon},\mathbb{X}^{\varepsilon})\rVert_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{p}]<\infty.

If furthermore
\sup_{\varepsilon}\mathds{E}[\lvert X_{0}^{\varepsilon}\rvert]<\infty
(i.e. tightness of
(X_{0}^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
) holds true, then it follows that
(X^{\varepsilon},\mathbb{X}^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is tight in
C_{\gamma,T}
.

Proof.

The proof follows from the proof of
[
FH20
, Theorem 3.1]
applied to
\beta=1/2
and
\gamma^{\prime}=\beta-1/q-\varepsilon
,
\varepsilon>0
, using the fact that, according to assumption (

), (
2.19
) holds for any
p>2
.
Tightness in

then follows utilizing the compact embedding
C_{\gamma^{\prime},T}\hookrightarrow C_{\gamma,T}
for

.
∎

3
Membrane with purely temporal fluctuations

In this section, we consider the scaling regime
\alpha=0
,
\beta=1
in (
2.14
) and thus obtain the slow-fast system:

\displaystyle dX^{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle=F(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{t})dt+\sqrt{2\Sigma(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{t})}dB(t)

(3.1)

\displaystyle d\eta^{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Gamma\eta^{\varepsilon}_{t}dt+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\Gamma\Pi}dW_{t},\quad\eta^{\varepsilon}_{0}\sim\rho_{\eta},

(3.2)

where
B
and
W
are independent Brownian motions.

From classical stochastic averaging, see also
[
DEPS15
, Theorem 4]
, we know that
X^{\varepsilon}\Rightarrow X
in distribution in
C([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{2})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. The limit
X
is the solution of the averaged system

dX_{t}=\overline{F}(X_{t})dt+\sqrt{2\overline{\Sigma}(X_{t})}dB_{t},

with

\displaystyle\overline{F}(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2K}}F(x,\eta)\rho_{\eta}(d\eta),

(3.3)

\displaystyle\overline{\Sigma}(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2K}}\Sigma(x,\eta)\rho_{\eta}(d\eta),

(3.4)

where
\rho_{\eta}(d\eta)
is the invariant measure of
\eta
given by (
2.4
).
Utilizing the linear growth of
F
in
\eta
uniformly in
x
, cf. (
2.13
), boundedness of all moments of
\eta^{\varepsilon}_{t}
in
\varepsilon,t
and boundedness of
\Sigma
, cf. (
2.12
), we can conclude that
(X^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the UCV condition (
2.17
); see below for the detailed proof. Thus, according to Proposition
2.6
, the iterated Itô integrals of
X^{\varepsilon}
will converge to the iterated Itô integrals of the limit
X
. More precisely, the following theorem holds:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\gamma<1/2
and
X^{\varepsilon}
and
X
be as above. Let
\mathbb{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s,t}:=\int_{s}^{t}(X^{\varepsilon}_{r}-X^{\varepsilon}_{s})\otimes dX^{\varepsilon}_{r}
and
\mathbb{X}_{s,t}:=\int_{s}^{t}(X_{r}-X_{s})\otimes dX_{r}
, where the stochastic integrals are understood in the Itô sense. Then it follows that

\displaystyle(X^{\varepsilon},\mathbb{X}^{\varepsilon})\Rightarrow(X,\mathbb{X})

(3.5)

in distribution in
\gamma
-Hölder rough path topology.

Proof.

We first prove the weak convergence of the iterated integrals
(\mathbb{X}^{\varepsilon}_{0,t})
in
\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
for any
t\geqslant 0
and then show tightness of
(X^{\varepsilon},\mathbb{X}^{\varepsilon})
in
\gamma
-Hölder rough path topology.

The first aim is to apply
Proposition

2.6
and show that the sequence
(X^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the UCV condition. We have that
X^{\varepsilon}=A^{\varepsilon}+M^{\varepsilon}
for

\displaystyle A^{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}F(X^{\varepsilon}_{s},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{s})ds

\displaystyle M^{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{2\Sigma(X^{\varepsilon}_{s},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{s})}dB_{s},

where
A^{\varepsilon}
is of finite variation and
M^{\varepsilon}
is a martingale. For
(X^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
to satisfy the UCV condition, we thus have to show the bound (
2.17
).

By boundedness of
\Sigma
it is immediate that the expected quadratic variation of
M^{\varepsilon}
is also uniformly bounded in
\varepsilon
. For the bound on the total variation of
A^{\varepsilon}
we use that (
2.13
) holds uniformly in
x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
, such that:

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon}\mathds{E}\big[\mathrm{Var}_{1,[0,T]}(A^{\varepsilon})\big]

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\varepsilon}\mathds{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}|F(X^{\varepsilon}_{s},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{s})|ds\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{\varepsilon}\mathds{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}(1+|\eta^{\varepsilon}_{s}|)ds\bigg]

\displaystyle=C(1+\mathds{E}[\lvert\eta_{0}\rvert])T,

where in the last equality, we used the stationarity of
\eta^{\varepsilon}
. As we have weak convergence of
(X^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
to
X
in
C([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{2})
, this implies, according to
Proposition

2.6
, that we also have weak convergence of the Itô integrals

\mathbb{X}^{\varepsilon}:=\int X^{\varepsilon}\otimes dX^{\varepsilon}
to
\mathbb{X}:=\int X\otimes dX
in
C(\Delta_{T},\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
.

To prove tightness in
\gamma
-Hölder rough path topology for
\gamma<1/2
, we utilize
Lemma

2.7
.
Here (
2.18
) follows immediately from the linear growth of
F
in
\eta
and bounded moments of
\eta^{\varepsilon}_{t}
in
\varepsilon,t
by stationarity, as well as Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality for the martingale part and boundedness of
\Sigma
. Moreover, (
2.19
) follows from (
2.18
) and the estimate

\displaystyle\mathds{E}\bigg[\bigg\lvert\int_{s}^{t}X^{\varepsilon,i}_{s,r}dX^{\varepsilon,j}_{r}\bigg\rvert^{p/2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathds{E}\bigg[\bigg\lvert\int_{s}^{t}X^{\varepsilon,i}_{s,r}dM^{\varepsilon,j}_{r}\bigg\rvert^{p/2}\bigg]+\mathds{E}\bigg[\bigg\lvert\int_{s}^{t}X^{\varepsilon,i}_{s,r}dA^{\varepsilon,j}_{r}\bigg\rvert^{p/2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathds{E}\bigg[\bigg(\int_{s}^{t}\lvert X^{\varepsilon,i}_{s,r}\rvert^{2}dr\bigg)^{p/4}\bigg]+\mathds{E}\bigg[\bigg(\int_{s}^{t}\lvert X^{\varepsilon,i}_{s,r}F^{j}(X^{\varepsilon}_{r},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{r})\rvert dr\bigg)^{p/2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int_{s}^{t}\mathds{E}[\lvert X_{s,r}^{\varepsilon,i}\rvert^{p/2}]^{4/p}dr\bigg)^{p/4}+\bigg(\int_{s}^{t}\mathds{E}[\lvert X_{s,r}^{\varepsilon,i}F^{j}(X_{r}^{\varepsilon},\eta_{r}^{\varepsilon})\rvert^{p/2}]^{2/p}dr\bigg)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert t-s\rvert^{(2\times\frac{1}{2}+1)\times\frac{p}{4}}+\bigg(\int_{s}^{t}\mathds{E}[\lvert X_{s,r}^{\varepsilon,i}\rvert^{p}]^{1/p}\mathds{E}[\lvert F^{j}(X_{r}^{\varepsilon},\eta_{r}^{\varepsilon})\rvert^{p}]^{1/p}dr\bigg)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert t-s\rvert^{p/2}+\bigg(\int_{s}^{t}\mathds{E}[\lvert X_{s,r}^{\varepsilon,i}\rvert^{p}]^{1/p}\mathds{E}[(1+\lvert\eta_{r}^{\varepsilon}\rvert)^{p}]^{1/p}dr\bigg)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert t-s\rvert^{p/2}+\bigg(\int_{s}^{t}\mathds{E}[\lvert X_{s,r}^{\varepsilon,i}\rvert^{p}]^{1/p}dr\bigg)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert t-s\rvert^{p/2}+\lvert t-s\rvert^{p/4+p/2}\lesssim_{T}\lvert t-s\rvert^{p/2}

using the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality for the martingale part, boundedness of
\Sigma
in the second line and the generalized Minkowski’s inequality for integrals for both summands in the third line (and the linear growth of
F
and stationarity of
\eta
).

Combining distributional convergence of
(X^{\varepsilon},\mathbb{X}^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
to
(X,\mathbb{X})
in
C([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})\times C(\Delta_{T},\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and tightness in
C_{\gamma,T}
, we conclude on distributional convergence in
\gamma
-Hölder rough path topology for
\gamma<1/2
.
∎

4
Membrane with temporal fluctuations twice as fast as spatial fluctuations

In this section, we consider the scaling regime
\alpha=1
,
\beta=2
in (
2.14
), that is, temporal fluctuations occur twice as fast as spatial ones. We introduce the fast process

\displaystyle Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}:=\frac{X^{\varepsilon}_{t}}{\varepsilon}\mod\mathbb{T}^{2}.

Then the general SDE system can be written as

\displaystyle dX^{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}F(Y^{\varepsilon}_{t},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{t})dt+\sqrt{2\Sigma(Y^{\varepsilon}_{t},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{t})}dB_{t},

(4.1a)

\displaystyle dY^{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}F(Y^{\varepsilon}_{t},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{t})dt+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}\Sigma(Y^{\varepsilon}_{t},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{t})}dB_{t},

(4.1b)

\displaystyle d\eta^{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\Gamma\eta^{\varepsilon}_{t}dt+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}\Gamma\Pi}dW_{t},

(4.1c)

for independent Brownian motions
B
and
W
, where
B
is a two-dimensional and
W
is a
2K
-dimensional standard Brownian motion.

Utilizing Itô’s formula, one can easily check that on smooth, compactly supported functions
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2K},\mathbb{R})
, the infinitesimal generator of the fast process
(Y^{\varepsilon},\eta^{\varepsilon})
is
\varepsilon^{-2}\mathcal{G}
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{L}_{0}+\mathcal{L}_{\eta}

(4.2)

for

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}f(y,\eta)=F(y,\eta)\cdot\nabla_{y}f(y,\eta)+\Sigma(y,\eta):\nabla_{y}\nabla_{y}f(y,\eta),

(4.3)

which is the generator of
Y
(for fixed
\eta
) and
\mathcal{L}_{\eta}

is the generator of the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process
\eta
, given by (
2.5
), cf. also
[
Dun13
, section 5.3]
. We also write
\mathcal{L}_{0}(\eta)
to denote the operator
\mathcal{L}_{0}
acting on functions
f:\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
, stressing the dependence on fixed
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{2K}
. The reason for introducing the fast process
Y^{\varepsilon}
is that the drift term of
X^{\varepsilon}
is given as an (unbounded) additive functional of the Markov process
(Y^{\varepsilon},\eta^{\varepsilon})
. Moreover, we have the equality in law,
(Y_{t},\eta_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\varepsilon^{2}t},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{\varepsilon^{2}t})_{t\geqslant 0}
, where
(Y,\eta)
is the Markov process with generator
\mathcal{G}
.

One can show (cf.
[
Dun13
, Prop. 5.3.1]
) that there exists a unique invariant measure
\rho
for the Markov process
(Y,\eta)
, whose density is the unique, normalized solution of

\mathcal{G}^{*}\rho=0,

(4.4)

\mathcal{G}^{\ast}
being the adjoint operator of
\mathcal{G}
with respect to
L^{2}(dyd\eta)
. As
\rho
is the unique invariant measure, it is in particular ergodic for
(Y,\eta)
. Furthermore, we can extend the semigroup
(P^{(Y,\eta)}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
of the Markov process
(Y,\eta)
, with
P_{t}^{(Y,\eta)}f(y,\eta)=\mathds{E}[f(Y_{t},\eta_{t})\mid(Y_{0},\eta_{0})=(y,\eta)]
for
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{2K})
, uniquely to a strongly continuous contraction semigroup on
L^{2}(\rho)
(that is possible by invariance of
\rho
, cf.
[
Yos95
, Theorem 1, p. 381]
) and define the generator
\mathcal{G}:dom(\mathcal{G})\subset L^{2}(\rho)\to L^{2}(\rho)
with
dom(\mathcal{G})=\{u\in L^{2}(\rho)\mid\mathcal{G}u\in L^{2}(\rho)\}
and
\mathcal{G}u:=\lim_{t\to 0}t^{-1}(P_{t}^{(Y,\eta)}u-u)
with limit in
L^{2}(\rho)
.

Let us define

\displaystyle V(\eta):=1+\frac{1}{2}\lvert\eta\rvert^{2}.

Then, according to the proof of
[
Dun13
, Prop. 5.3.1]
,
V
is a Lyapunov function for the fast process
(Y^{\varepsilon},\eta^{\varepsilon})
and we have the pointwise spectral-gap-type estimates of the form

\lvert P_{t}^{(Y,\eta)}f(y,\eta)-\int fd\rho\rvert^{2}\leqslant Ke^{-ct}\lvert V(\eta)\rvert^{2}\quad\text{ for all }t\geqslant 0

(4.5)

for constants
K,c>0
(not depending on
f
)
and for all
f:\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2K}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\lvert f(y,\eta)\rvert\leqslant V(\eta)
,
(y,\eta)\in\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2K}
. If we integrate the pointwise inequality (
4.5
) over
(y,\eta)
with respect to
\rho
, we obtain the
L^{2}(\rho)
-spectral-gap-type estimates for all such
f
, assuming

,

\lVert P_{t}^{(Y,\eta)}f-\int fd\rho\rVert_{L^{2}(\rho)}^{2}\leqslant Ke^{-ct}\lVert V\rVert_{L^{2}(\rho)}^{2}\quad\text{ for all }t\geqslant 0.

(4.6)

We will in particular apply the spectral gap estimates to

, which satisfies (
2.13
).

Let us, similarly as in the previous section, define the

space with respect to the generator
\mathcal{G}
(notation:
\mathcal{G}^{S}:=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{G}+\mathcal{G}^{\star})

\displaystyle H^{1}(\rho):=\{u\in dom(\mathcal{G})\mid\langle(-\mathcal{G})u,u\rangle_{\rho}=\langle(-\mathcal{G}^{S})u,u\rangle_{\rho}<\infty\}.

The scalar product in
H^{1}(\rho)
is given by
\langle f,g\rangle_{H^{1}(\rho)}=\langle(-\mathcal{G}^{S})f,g\rangle_{H^{1}(\rho)}
.

Then, as a consequence of the spectral gap estimates, we can solve the Poisson equation

(-\mathcal{G})u=g

(4.7)

explicitly with right-hand side
g
that has mean zero under
\rho
,
\langle g\rangle_{\rho}=0
, and satisfies
\lvert g\rvert\leqslant V
with
V\in L^{2}(\rho)
. The unique solution
u\in H^{1}(\rho)
is given by
u=\int_{0}^{\infty}P_{t}^{(Y,\eta)}gdt\in L^{2}(\rho)
.
In fact, for our tightness arguments, we will need a stronger integrability condition on the solution

(and
\nabla_{\eta}u,\nabla_{y}u
), that is given by the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
p\geqslant 2
and let
g\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2K},\mathbb{R}^{2})
with

\displaystyle\lvert g(y,\eta)\rvert\leqslant V(\eta)

(4.8)

(i.p.

) and with
\langle g\rangle_{\rho}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2K}}g(y,\eta)\rho(d(y,\eta))=0
. Then the Poisson equation

\displaystyle(-\mathcal{G})u=g

u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2K},\mathbb{R}^{2})
with the property that
\langle u\rangle_{\rho}=0
. Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
, such that the solution satisfies
\lvert u(y,\eta)\rvert\leqslant CV(\eta)
and

\displaystyle\lvert\nabla_{(y,\eta)}u(y,\eta)\rvert\leqslant\lvert\nabla_{y}u(y,\eta)\rvert+\lvert\nabla_{\eta}u(y,\eta)\rvert\leqslant 2CV(\eta).

In particular, it follows that
u\in W^{1,p}(\rho)=\{u\in L^{p}(\rho)\mid\nabla_{(y,\eta)}u\in L^{p}(\rho)\}
.

Proof.

The solution
u=\int_{0}^{\infty}P^{(Y,\eta)}_{t}gdt
is smooth, as
g
is assumed to be smooth, cf. also
[
Dun13
, Proposition A.3.1]
. It satisfies an analogue growth bound as
g
by the pointwise spectral gap estimates (

) with constant
C=K\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-ct}dt\in(0,\infty)
, in particular
u\in L^{p}(\rho)
for any

. For the bound on the derivative, we proceed as in part (e) of the proof of
[
PV01
, Theorem 1]
applying Sobolev embedding and the estimate
(9.40)
from
[
GT01
]
and the bound on
g
and
u
, such that for

(notation:
B_{x,R}=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{2K}\mid\lvert z-x\rvert\leqslant R\}
)

\displaystyle\lvert\nabla_{(y,\eta)}u(y,\eta)\rvert\leqslant C(\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{(y,\eta),2})}+\lVert\mathcal{G}u\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{(y,\eta),2})})\leqslant 2CV(\eta).

Notice also that, compared to
[

]
in our situation, we have compactness in the

and
u,\nabla u
is uniform in

.
∎

In what follows, we will always assume the system (

(Y^{\varepsilon}_{0},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{0})\sim\rho
. In
[

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\rho
be the probability measure with the density denoted also by
\rho

be the unique solution, satisfying

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{0}^{\ast}+\mathcal{L}_{\eta})g_{\eta}(y)=0

(4.11)

\mathcal{L}_{0}=\mathcal{L}_{0}(\eta)

fulfills the disintegration formula

\displaystyle\rho(y,\eta)=g_{\eta}(y)f(\eta),

where

\displaystyle f(\eta)=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{\lvert\Pi\rvert}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\eta^{T}\Pi^{-1}\eta\right).

In particular, the marginal distribution of
\rho
in the
\eta
-variable is the normal distribution
N(0,\Pi)
.

Proof.

First we show that the form of the density
\rho
follows from the disintegration theorem from measure theory
(see for example
[
CM78
, chapter 3, 70 and 71]
) and the invariance of
\rho
. Let

be the projection and
\nu:=\rho\circ\pi^{-1}
the push-forward under
\rho
. Then the disintegration theorem implies that there exists a family of measures
(\mu_{\eta})_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{2K}}
on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, such that:

\bullet

\eta\mapsto\mu_{\eta}(A)
is Borel measurable for each Borel measurable set
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{T}^{2})

\bullet
for every Borel measurable function
h:\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2K}\to\mathbb{R}
,

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2K}}h(y,\eta)\rho(d(y,\eta))=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2K}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}h(y,\eta)\mu_{\eta}(dy)\nu(d\eta).

(4.12)

Since by assumption
\rho
has a density, that we also denote by
\rho
, it follows that
\nu
has a density given by

\eta\mapsto\int\rho(y,\eta)dy=:f(\eta).

Consequently, also
\mu_{\eta}
has a density, namely the conditional density

y\mapsto\mathbf{1}_{\{f>0\}}\rho(y,\eta)/f(\eta)=:g_{\eta}(y).

In order to prove that the marginal distribution
\nu
under
\rho
is the normal distribution
N(0,\Pi)
, consider

with

not depending on

. Then if

, we have for any

\mathds{E}[h(\eta_{t})]=\mathds{E}[h(Y_{t},\eta_{t})]=\int h(y,\eta)d\rho=\int h(\eta)f(\eta)d\eta.

Hence

is given by the density of the
N(0,\Pi)
distribution, as this is the unique invariant distribution for
(\eta_{t})_{t}
.

It is left to derive the equation (
4.11
) for the density
g_{\eta}(y)
. For that we use the invariance of
\rho
and write

\displaystyle 0=\mathcal{G}^{\ast}\rho=(\mathcal{L}_{0}^{\ast}+\mathcal{L}_{\eta}^{\ast})(g_{\eta}(y)f(\eta))=f(\eta)\mathcal{L}_{0}^{\ast}g_{\eta}(y)+\mathcal{L}_{\eta}^{\ast}(g_{\eta}(y)f(\eta))=f(\eta)\big(\mathcal{L}_{0}^{\ast}g_{\eta}(y)+\mathcal{L}_{\eta}g_{\eta}(y)\big),

where we used that for any
h\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2K},\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\eta}^{\ast}(h(\eta)f(\eta))

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\eta}\cdot(h(\eta)\Gamma\eta f(\eta))+\Gamma\Pi:\nabla_{\eta}\nabla_{\eta}(h(\eta)f(\eta))

\displaystyle=h(\eta)[\nabla_{\eta}\cdot(\Gamma\eta f(\eta))+\Gamma\Pi:\nabla_{\eta}\nabla_{\eta}f(\eta)]

\displaystyle\quad+f(\eta)[\Gamma\eta\cdot\nabla_{\eta}h(\eta)+\Gamma\Pi:\nabla_{\eta}\nabla_{\eta}h(\eta)]

\displaystyle\quad+2\nabla_{\eta}h(\eta)\cdot\Gamma\Pi\nabla_{\eta}f(\eta)

\displaystyle=h(\eta)\mathcal{L}_{\eta}^{\ast}f(\eta)+f(\eta)\mathcal{L}_{\eta}h(\eta)

\displaystyle\quad+2(\nabla_{\eta}h(\eta)\cdot\Gamma\Pi\nabla_{\eta}f(\eta)+f(\eta)\Gamma\eta\cdot\nabla_{\eta}h(\eta))

\displaystyle=h(\eta)\mathcal{L}_{\eta}^{\ast}f(\eta)+f(\eta)\mathcal{L}_{\eta}h(\eta)

\displaystyle=f(\eta)\mathcal{L}_{\eta}h(\eta),

where we added and subtracted the term

and then used that
\nabla_{\eta}f(\eta)=-f(\eta)\Pi^{-1}\eta
and
\mathcal{L}_{\eta}^{\ast}f=0
. As

, the previous implies the equation (

The uniqueness of the solution

in the class of probability densities in the

solving

be positive such that

and they solve

Then setting

for

, which implies

\displaystyle\quad-t\int(\nabla_{y}\chi^{i}+e_{i})\cdot 2\Sigma e_{j}d\rho+t\int e_{i}\cdot\Sigma e_{j}d\rho

\displaystyle=\mathbb{X}_{0,t}(i,j)+\frac{1}{2}\langle X^{i},X^{j}\rangle_{t}

\displaystyle\quad+t\bigg(\langle\chi^{i},(\mathcal{G}\chi)^{j}\rangle_{\rho}+\frac{1}{2}\langle X^{i},X^{j}\rangle_{1}-\int\nabla_{y}\chi^{i}\cdot 2\Sigma e_{j}d\rho-\int e_{i}\cdot\Sigma e_{j}d\rho\bigg)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathbb{X}}_{0,t}(i,j)+t\tilde{A}(i,j).

The area correction can furthermore be written as

\displaystyle\tilde{A}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\langle\chi^{i},\mathcal{G}\chi^{j}\rangle_{\rho}+D(i,j)-\int\nabla_{y}\chi^{i}\cdot 2\Sigma e_{j}d\rho-\int e_{i}\cdot\Sigma e_{j}d\rho

\displaystyle=\langle\chi^{i},\mathcal{G}^{A}\chi^{j}\rangle_{\rho}+\int e_{i}\cdot\Sigma\nabla_{y}\chi^{j}d\rho-\int\nabla_{y}\chi^{i}\cdot\Sigma e_{j}d\rho,

using that
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}^{A}+\mathcal{G}^{S}
for
\mathcal{G}^{S}:=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{G}+\mathcal{G}^{\star})
and
\mathcal{G}^{A}:=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{G}-\mathcal{G}^{\star})
, where
\mathcal{G}^{\star}
denotes the
L^{2}(\rho)
-adjoint, and that by
[
KLO12
, section 2.4]
, we have a correspondence between the quadratic variation of Dynkin’s martingale
\tilde{M}^{\varepsilon}
and the
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\rho)
-norm of
\chi
(utilizing also stationarity of
(Y,\eta)
and
(Y^{\varepsilon},\eta^{\varepsilon})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}(Y_{\varepsilon^{-2}\cdot},\eta_{\varepsilon^{-2}\cdot})
), such that

\displaystyle\langle\chi^{i},\mathcal{G}^{S}\chi^{j}\rangle_{\rho}=-\langle\chi^{i},\chi^{j}\rangle_{H^{1}(\rho)}=-\frac{1}{2}\mathds{E}[\langle\tilde{M}^{\varepsilon,i},\tilde{M}^{\varepsilon,j}\rangle_{1}]=-\int[\nabla_{y}\chi^{i}\cdot\Sigma\nabla_{y}\chi^{j}+\nabla_{\eta}\chi^{i}\cdot\Gamma\Pi\nabla_{\eta}\chi^{j}]d\rho.

For a base-point
s>0
we can do an analogue argument.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

We expect (without proof) that in fact
\tilde{A}(i,j)=\langle\chi^{i},\mathcal{G}^{A}\chi^{j}\rangle_{\rho}
holds true and that
\mathcal{G}^{A}
is a nontrivial operator, such that the Stratonovich area correction is truely non-vanishing. The problem to prove this is that, although we derived the form of the density
\rho
in
Lemma

4.3
, the density
g_{\eta}(y)
remains non-explicit.

Typically (cf.
[
DOP21
]
) the area correction in the Stratonovich case can be expressed in terms of the asymmetric part of the generator of the underlying Markov process, which is a non-trivial operator if the Markov process is non-reversible.

4.2
Tightness in
\gamma
-Hölder rough path topology

For the convergence in distribution of the lift
(X^{\varepsilon},\mathbb{X}^{\varepsilon})
to the respective lift of

it is left to prove tightness in the rough path space utilizing

. We verify the necessary moment bounds in the next proposition.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
(X^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon},\eta^{\varepsilon})
be as in
Proposition

4.4
. Then the following moment bounds hold true for any
p\geqslant 2

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon}\mathds{E}[\lvert X^{\varepsilon,i}_{t}-X^{\varepsilon,i}_{s}\rvert^{p}]\lesssim\lvert t-s\rvert^{p/2},\quad\forall s,t\in\Delta_{T}

(4.28)

and

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon}\mathds{E}\bigg[\bigg\lvert\int_{s}^{t}X^{\varepsilon,i}_{s,r}dX^{\varepsilon,j}_{r}\bigg\rvert^{p/2}\bigg]\lesssim\lvert t-s\rvert^{p/2},\quad\forall s,t\in\Delta_{T}

(4.29)

for
i,j\in\{1,2\}
.
In particular, tightness of
(X^{\varepsilon},\mathbb{X}^{\varepsilon})
in
C_{\gamma,T}
for
\gamma<1/2
follows.

Proof.

Let
p\geqslant 2
. First, utilizing the growth condition (
2.13
) on
F
, finiteness of moments of
\eta
, Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality and boundedness of
\Sigma
in (
2.12
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[\lvert X_{t}^{\varepsilon}-X^{\varepsilon}_{s}\rvert^{p}]

\displaystyle{\leqslant\mathds{E}\bigg[\bigg\lvert\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{s}^{t}F(Y^{\varepsilon}_{s},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{s})ds\bigg\rvert^{p}\bigg]+\mathds{E}\bigg[\bigg\lvert\int_{s}^{t}\sqrt{2\Sigma}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{s},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{s})dB_{s}\bigg\rvert^{p}\bigg]}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{-p}\lvert t-s\rvert^{{p}}+\lvert t-s\rvert^{p/2}.

(4.30)

Secondly, using the representation (
4.1
), we conclude

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[\lvert X^{\varepsilon,i}_{t}-X^{\varepsilon,i}_{s}\rvert^{p}]

\displaystyle\leqslant\varepsilon^{p}\mathds{E}[\lvert\chi^{i}(Y^{\varepsilon}(t),\eta^{\varepsilon}(t))-\chi^{i}(Y^{\varepsilon}(s),\eta^{\varepsilon}(s))\rvert^{p}]+\mathds{E}[\lvert M^{\varepsilon,i}(t)-M^{\varepsilon,i}(s)\rvert^{p}],

where the martingale is given by
M^{\varepsilon}=M_{1}^{\varepsilon}+M_{2}^{\varepsilon}
with

\displaystyle M_{1}^{\varepsilon,i}(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}(\nabla_{y}\chi^{i}+e_{i})\cdot\sqrt{2\Sigma}(Y^{\varepsilon}(s),\eta^{\varepsilon}(s))dB_{s}

\displaystyle M_{2}^{\varepsilon,i}(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{\eta}\chi^{i}(Y^{\varepsilon}(s),\eta^{\varepsilon}(s))\cdot\sqrt{2\Gamma\Pi}dW_{s}.

Burkholder-Davis-Gundy (BDG) and Minkowski inequality, together with the stationarity of
(Y^{\varepsilon},\eta^{\varepsilon})
yield

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[\lvert M^{\varepsilon}(t)-M^{\varepsilon}(s)\rvert^{p}]\lesssim\lvert t-s\rvert^{p/2}E_{\rho}[\lvert(\nabla_{y}\chi+I)^{T}\Sigma(\nabla_{y}\chi+I)+(\nabla_{\eta}\chi)^{T}\Gamma\Pi\nabla_{\eta}\chi\rvert^{p/2}].

Boundedness of
\Sigma
stated in (
2.12
) and
Proposition

4.1
, i.e.
\nabla_{y}\chi+\nabla_{\eta}\chi\in L^{p}(\rho)
for any
p\geqslant 2
, imply finiteness of the expectation on the right-hand-side.

Moreover, according to
Proposition

4.1
,
\chi
satisfies a growth condition in
\eta
, which we use to estimate the boundary term (as well as stationarity of
\eta^{\varepsilon}
and that
\eta_{0}
has all moments under
\rho
, as it is the normal distribution
N(0,\Pi)
), obtaining

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[\lvert X^{\varepsilon,i}_{t}-X^{\varepsilon,i}_{s}\rvert^{p}]

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{p}+\lvert t-s\rvert^{p/2}.

(4.31)

By combining the estimates (
4.2
) and (
4.31
), using the first one for
\varepsilon>\lvert t-s\rvert^{1/2}
and the second one for
\varepsilon<\lvert t-s\rvert^{1/2}
, we conclude

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon\in[0,1]}\mathds{E}[\lvert X^{\varepsilon,i}_{t}-X^{\varepsilon,i}_{s}\rvert^{p}]\lesssim\lvert t-s\rvert^{p/2}.

The estimate for the iterated integrals is then immediate by the estimate on the moments of
X^{\varepsilon,j}
and the decomposition of the iterated integral in (
4.1
), as well as the boundedness of the quadratic variation of the martingale
M^{\varepsilon,i}
in
L^{p/2}(\rho)
for any
p\geqslant 2
.

The conclusion on tightness in
C_{\gamma,T}
for
\gamma<1/2
follows from
Lemma

2.7
.
∎

5
Diffusion on a membrane with comparable spatial and temporal fluctuations

In this section we consider the space-time scaling regime when
\alpha=1,\beta=1
, which means that we observe spatial and temporal fluctuations of a membrane of comparable size. Then the general system becomes

\displaystyle dX^{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}F(Y^{\varepsilon}_{t},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{t})dt+\sqrt{2\Sigma(Y^{\varepsilon}_{t},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{t})}dB_{t},

\displaystyle dY^{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}F(Y^{\varepsilon}_{t},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{t})dt+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}\Sigma(Y^{\varepsilon}_{t},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{t})}dB_{t},

(5.1)

\displaystyle d\eta^{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Gamma\eta^{\varepsilon}_{t}dt+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\Gamma\Pi}dW_{t},

where again
B
and
W
are independent Brownian motions and
Y^{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon^{-1}X^{\varepsilon}\text{ mod }\mathbb{T}^{2}
.

To determine the limit in this regime, the difficulty is that
Y
and
\eta
now fluctuate at different scales, which means that, compared to the previous section, we no longer have the generator
\varepsilon^{-2}\mathcal{G}
for the joint Markov process
(Y^{\varepsilon},\eta^{\varepsilon})
, but the generator
\varepsilon^{-2}\mathcal{L}_{0}+\varepsilon^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{\eta}
for
\mathcal{L}_{0}=\mathcal{L}_{0}(\eta)
from (
4.3
) and
\mathcal{L}_{\eta}
from (
2.5
). The idea is to first deduce a quenched result for each fixed environment
\eta
and afterwards average over the invariant measure
\rho_{\eta}
of
\eta
.

Let for
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{2K}
,

\rho_{Y}(dy,\eta):=C^{-1}\sqrt{\lvert\Sigma^{-1}\rvert(y,\eta)}dy

be a probability measure on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, where
C:=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\sqrt{\lvert\Sigma^{-1}\rvert(y,\eta)}dy
is the normalizing constant. It is straightforward to check that
\rho_{Y}(\cdot,\eta)
is invariant for
\mathcal{L}_{0}(\eta)
, that is
\langle\mathcal{L}_{0}(\eta)f\rangle_{\rho_{Y}(\cdot,\eta)}=0
for all
f\in dom(\mathcal{L}_{0})\subset L^{2}(\rho_{Y}(\cdot,\eta))
, and that
\int F(y,\eta)\rho_{Y}(dy,\eta)=0
by the definition (
2.11
) of
F
.

Let now, by
[
Dun13
, Proposition A.2.2]
, for any
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
,
\chi(\cdot,\eta)\in C^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2})
be the unique solution of

\mathcal{L}_{0}(\eta)\chi(\cdot,\eta)=-F(\cdot,\eta),

(5.2)

with
\int\chi(y,\eta)\rho_{Y}(dy,\eta)=0
. Existence of
\chi
is based on the
L^{2}(\rho_{Y}(\cdot,\eta))
-spectral gap estimates for
\mathcal{L}_{0}(\eta)
from
[
Dun13
, Lemma A.2.1]
. That is, there exists a constant
\lambda(\eta)>0
such that for all
f\in L^{2}(\rho_{Y}(\cdot,\eta))
,

\displaystyle\lVert P_{t}^{0}f-\langle f\rangle_{\rho_{Y}(\cdot,\eta)}\rVert_{L^{2}(\rho_{Y}(\cdot,\eta))}\leqslant e^{-\lambda(\eta)t}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\rho_{Y}(\cdot,\eta))},

where
(P^{0}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}=(P^{0}_{t}(\eta))_{t\geqslant 0}
denotes the semigroup on
L^{2}(\rho_{Y}(\cdot,\eta))
associated to
\mathcal{L}_{0}(\eta)
. Furthermore, according to
[
Dun13
, Proposition A.2.2]
, the solution
\chi
will be smooth in the
\eta
-variable (as
F
is smooth) and satisfies
\lvert\nabla_{\eta}^{k}\chi(y,\eta)\rvert\leqslant C_{k}(1+\lvert\eta\rvert^{l_{k}})
for
k=0,1,2
and
l_{k}\geqslant 1
and a constant
C_{k}>0
(due to
F
satisfying such a growth condition with a possibly different constant
C_{k}
).

Then, we can decompose the drift part of
X^{\varepsilon}
with the help of that solution
\chi
, yielding

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}F^{i}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{s},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{s})ds

\displaystyle=\varepsilon({\chi^{i}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{0},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{0})-\chi^{i}(Y_{t}^{\varepsilon},\eta_{t}^{\varepsilon})})+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{\eta}\chi^{i}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{r},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{r})\cdot\sqrt{2\Gamma\Pi}dW_{r}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{y}\chi^{i}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{r},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{r})\cdot\sqrt{2\Sigma(Y^{\varepsilon}_{r},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{r})}dB_{r}

(5.3)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}(\mathcal{L}_{\eta}\chi)^{i}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{s},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{s})ds

for
i=1,2
.
Plugging (
5
) into the dynamics for
X^{\varepsilon}
, one can deduce that
(X^{\varepsilon})
converges in distribution to a Brownian motion with variance
2Dt
plus a constant drift
L
. This was proven in
[
Dun13
, Theorem 5.2.2]
, namely

(X^{\varepsilon}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}\Rightarrow(tL+\sqrt{2D}Z_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}

for a standard Brownian motion
Z
and where

\displaystyle D=\int\int(I+\nabla_{y}\chi)^{T}\Sigma(I+\nabla_{y}\chi)\rho_{Y}(dy,\eta)\rho_{\eta}(d\eta)

and

\displaystyle L=\int\int\mathcal{L}_{\eta}\chi(y,\eta)\rho_{Y}(dy,\eta)\rho_{\eta}(d\eta).

Indeed, the drift term
L
arises from the convergence of the last term in (
5
), that is

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}_{\eta}\chi(Y^{\varepsilon}_{s},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{s})ds\to t\int\int\mathcal{L}_{\eta}\chi(y,\eta)\rho_{Y}(dy,\eta)\rho_{\eta}(d\eta)=:tL

(5.4)

in probability, which motivates the ergodic theorem for
(Y^{\varepsilon},\eta^{\varepsilon})
,
Proposition

5.1
, below.

For completeness, we state the result here, its proof follows from the result from
[
Dun13
, Lemma A.2.3]
. One can also deduce its claim from decomposing the additive functional in terms of the solution
G(\cdot,\eta)
of the Poisson equation (analogously as in (

))

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}G(\cdot,\eta)=b(\cdot,\eta)-E_{\rho_{Y}(\cdot,\eta)}[b(\cdot,\eta)]

for fixed
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{2K}
(existence follows by the
L^{2}(\rho_{Y}(\cdot,\eta))
-spectral gap estimates on
\mathcal{L}_{0}(\eta)
,
[
Dun13
, Lemma A.2.1]
) and utilizing the ergodic theorem for the Ornstein Uhlenbeck process

started in
\rho_{\eta}
. The growth assumption on

in the following proposition is needed to obtain an analogue growth condition on

, such that the martingale term and the drift part involving

Proposition 5.1

Let

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}b(Y^{\varepsilon}_{s},\eta^{\varepsilon}_{s})ds\to t\int E_{\rho_{Y}(\cdot,\eta)}[b(\cdot,\eta)]_{\mid\eta=\tilde{\eta}}\rho_{\eta}(d\tilde{\eta}).

(5.6)

(Here

denotes integration with respect to a probability measure
\rho
on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, respectively
\mathbb{R}^{2K}
.)

.

In particular this applies for

Remark 5.6

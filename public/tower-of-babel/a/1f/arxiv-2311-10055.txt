The Minimum Clique Routing Problem on Cycles Corresponding author: ptolomei@fceia.unr.edu.ar
M. Escalante and P. Tolomei, M. Matamala and I. Rapaport, L.M. Torres

Abstract
In the Minimum Clique Routing Problem on Cycles MCRPC we are given a cycle together with a set of demands (weighted origin-destination pairs) and the goal is to route all the pairs minimizing the maximum weighted clique of the intersection graph induced by the routing. The vertices of this graph are the demands with their corresponding weights and two demands are adjacent when their routes share at least one arc. In this work we are not only interested in the MCRPC but also in two natural subproblems. First, we consider the situation where the demands are disjoint, in the sense that every two demands do not share any of their corresponding ends. Second, we analyze the subproblem where the weights of the routes are all equal. We first show that the problem is NP-complete even in the subproblem of disjoint demands. For the case of arbitrary weights, we exhibit a simple combinatorial 2-approximation algorithm and a 3 2 \frac{3}{2} -approximation algorithm based on rounding a solution of a relaxation of an integer linear programming formulation of our problem. Finally, we give a Fixed Parameter Tractable algorithm for the case of uniform weights, whose parameter is related to the maximum degree of the intersection graph induced by any routing. Keywords: combinatorial optimization, routing problem, ring networks, algorithms

Introduction

In the Minimum Clique Routing Problem
MCRP

[
15
]
, the input is an undirected graph and a set of weighted origin-destination pairs of nodes
\mathcal{D}
.
The goal is to
specify undirected paths in this graph (routes) connecting each origin node with the corresponding
destination node in such a way that the maximum weighted clique in the intersection graph induced by the routing is minimized, where two routes intersect if they have at least one common arc and the weight of a demand is associated to the corresponding route.

The
MCRP
is NP-hard in general, as deciding whether there is a disjoint set of routes connecting all pairs in
\mathcal{D}
corresponds to the Edge Disjoint Path Problem
EDPP
, a classical NP-complete problem
[
8
]
, which remains NP-complete even when restricted to planar graphs
[
11
]
and to series-parallel graphs
[
12
]
. On the other hand, the
EDPP
admits a Fixed Parameter Tractable (FPT) algorithm for parameter
|\mathcal{D}|
, the number of demands
[
9
,
14
]
. The optimization version of the
EDPP
, where the number of demands whose routes form a pairwise edge disjoint set is maximized, has an
O(\sqrt{n})
-approximation algorithm
[
1
]
.

The
MCRP
is closely related to the Minimum Load Routing Problem
MLRP
, where the goal is to find a routing which minimizes the load of the network, i.e., the maximum weight of routes sharing an edge. This problem has an approximation factor of
O(\log n/\log\log n)
which is matched by an
\Omega(\log n/\log\log n)
-hardness result
[
2
]
.

The
MLRP
restricted to cycles,
MLRPC
, was first discussed in
[
3
]
, where it was proved to be NP-hard and 2-approximable. This result was improved later in
[
10
]
, where the existence of a Polynomial Time Approximation Scheme was proved. It is also known that the problem can be solved in polynomial time when all the weights are the same
[
5
]
.

In this paper we focus on the MCRP where the input graph is a cycle. This problem, the Minimum Clique Routing Problem on Cycles
MCRPC
, was first studied in
[
15
]
. Note that, since any set of routes containing a common edge is a set of pairwise intersecting routes, the optimal value of the
MCRP
is an upper bound for the optimal value of the
MLRP
.

In
[
15
]
the authors construct families of instances with uniform demand weights for which a minimum load routing can result in a clique of size almost twice the optimum.

In the context of the routing in optical networks, instead of minimizing the maximum clique, one is often interested in minimizing the resulting chromatic number of the intersection graph induced by the routing. For instance, in the Routing and Wavelength Assignment Problem
RWAP
the task is to find, for each origin-destination pair, a route through the network and a wavelength (or color) so that routes with the same wavelength share no common edges, and the number of required wavelengths is minimized
[
13
]
.

Preliminaries

In order to state and prove our results, we introduce some definitions and notation. The graph over which the demands are routed is a cycle on
n

For any two distinct nodes

[i,j]

i<j

A demand is an origin-destination pair

If two demands have the same ends, we say that these demands are multiples.

The input of

A routing

3
NP-completeness with disjoint demands

Theorem 3.1
.

The
MCRPC
with disjoint demands is NP-complete.

Proof.

The decision problem associated with the
MCRPC
is in NP since, given a routing, one can check in polynomial time whether its maximum (weighted) clique
is upper bounded by
k

[
7
]
.

We prove the completeness by a polynomial reduction from the
Partition
Problem
[
6
]
.
Let
S=\{x_{2},x_{2+1}\ldots,x_{2+(r-1)}\}
be a multiset of
r
positive integers, an instance of the
Partition
Problem (in the reduction we are going to see the convenience of starting the indices from 2). In the
Partition
Problem we have to decide if there is
T\subseteq S
such that

\sum_{x\in T}x=\sum_{x\in S\setminus T}x.

We may assume, w.l.o.g., that
M=\sum_{i=2}^{r+1}x_{i}
is an even number.
In fact, the
Partition
Problem remains NP-complete with
M
even because one could multiply the numbers of the multiset by two, and then this new, modified problem is equivalent to the general one.

The reduction is as follows. First, the set of nodes in the cycle is
V=\{1,\ldots,2r+4\}
. The set of demands
\mathcal{D}
is composed by
r+2
demands divided into two groups (see Figure
3
).

The demands of the first group are the following two:
p_{1}=(1,2)
and
p_{r+2}=(1+(r+2),2+(r+2))
. We call these two demands
poles
.
The weights of the poles are given by

\mathbf{w}(p_{1})=\mathbf{w}(p_{r+2})=M
.

In the second group of
r
demands we code the multiset
S

given by the
Partition
Problem. More precisely,
the demands are (for
0<i<r-1
):

\displaystyle p_{2}

\displaystyle=(3+0,(3+0)+(r+2))

\displaystyle p_{2+i}

\displaystyle=(3+i,(3+i)+(r+2))

\displaystyle p_{2+(r-1)}

\displaystyle=(3+(r-1),3+(r-1)+(r+2))

The total weight assigned to these
r
demands is
M
, which is divided according to the values given by the instance
of the
Partition
Problem. More precisely,
\mathbf{w}(p_{i})=x_{i}
for all
2\leq i\leq 2+(r-1)
.

For any routing
R
, recalling that

R_{p}
is the route assigned to
p
, let

\mathcal{D}_{+}(R)=\{p\in\mathcal{D}:R_{p}=p^{+}\}

and

\mathcal{D}_{-}(R)=\{p\in\mathcal{D}:R_{p}=p^{-}\}.

Note that the demands in
\mathcal{D}_{+}(R)
are those that are routed in such a way that they contain the pole
p_{r+2}
while avoiding the pole
p_{1}
. The routing
R
does the opposite on the demands in
\mathcal{D}_{-}(R)
.

Also, in an optimum routing
R^{*}
, the two maximum cliques are “reached” in the poles of the associated circular-arc graph
H
.
In fact, in the optimal routing, both poles
p_{1}
and
p_{r+2}
are in
\mathcal{D}_{+}(R^{*})
and the intersection of the arcs associated with the two maximum cliques are the unitary
length arcs
[1,2]
and
[r+3,r+4]
. Finally, the weights
W
and
W^{\prime}
of the two cliques
are such that
M<W,W^{\prime}<2M
. Also:
W+W^{\prime}=3M

Let
k=\frac{3}{2}M
.
Now we are going to show that there exists a multiset

T\subseteq S
such that
\sum_{x\in T}x=\sum_{x\in S\setminus T}x=M/2
if and only if there exists a routing
R
in
H
such that
\omega(H_{R},\mathbf{w})=k
.
Suppose that there is some multiset
T
satisfying
\sum_{x\in T}x=\sum_{x\in S\setminus T}x=M/2
.
Then, the routing

R
given by
R_{p_{i}}=p_{i}^{+}
, for all
i
such that
x_{i}\in T
, and
R_{p_{i}}=p_{i}^{-}
otherwise, together with
R_{p_{1}}=p_{1}^{+}
and
R_{p_{r+2}}=p_{r+2}^{+}

gives
\omega(H_{R},\mathbf{w})=k
.

Conversely, let
R
be a routing in
H
such that
\omega(H_{R},\mathbf{w})=k
. It follows that
\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{D}_{+}(R))=\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{D}_{-}(R))=k
. This comes from the fact that the sum of the two polar cliques is
W+W^{\prime}=3M
.
It finally follows that the multiset

T=\{x_{i}:p_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{-}(R)\setminus\{p_{1}\}\}
must satisfy that

\sum_{x\in T}x=\sum_{x\in S\setminus T}x=M/2
.

4
Approximation algorithms

We first present a simple polynomial time combinatorial algorithm for the
MCRPC
. Given an instance
(\mathcal{D},\mathbf{w})
, it finds a routing
S
of
\mathcal{D}
such that

\omega(H_{S},\mathbf{w})\leq 2\omega(H_{R},\mathbf{w}),

for every routing
R
of
\mathcal{D}
.
Thus, this algorithm is a 2-approximation algorithm for the
MCRPC
.

Theorem 4.1
.

The
MCRPC
has a 2-approximation algorithm that runs in time
O(|\mathcal{D}|^{3})
.

Proof.

The algorithm
builds
a routing
S
for the set of
demands

\mathcal{D}
as follows: the demand
p
in
\mathcal{D}
is routed through
p^{+}
if
|V(p^{+})|\leq|V(p^{-})|
and through
p^{-}
, otherwise. Then, by using Hsu’s algorithm
[
7
]
it solves the weighted clique problem in the circular-arc graph
H_{S}
and output a clique
Q
achieving the maximum. This can be done in time
O(|\mathcal{D}|^{3})
.

Let
\mathcal{D}^{Q}
be the set of demands associated to the routes in
Q
.
In the example of Figure 3 the set of demands
\mathcal{D}
is given by

\mathcal{D}=\{(1,2),(3,9),(4,10),(5,11),(6,12),(7,8)\}.

Then,
S_{(i,j)}=(i,j)^{+}=[i,j]
, for each
(i,j)\in\mathcal{D}
, since

|V([i,j])|=j-i+1\leq|V([j,i])|=12-(j-i)+1,

as

j-i\leq 6
.
Hence,
H_{S}
is the disjoint union of a clique and an isolated node. In this case, the maximal clique
Q
has 5 routes and
\mathcal{D}^{Q}
is the set of demands
\mathcal{D}^{Q}=\{(3,9),(4,10),(5,11),(6,12),(7,8)\}
.

Thus,

\omega(H_{S},\mathbf{w})=\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{D}^{Q})

In order to prove that we have a 2-approximation we show that

\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{D}^{Q})\leq 2\omega(H_{R},\mathbf{w})
, for each routing
R
of
\mathcal{D}
.

Let
a
be an arc that appears the most in the routes of
Q
.
Let
b=(k,l)
be an arc such that the lengths of the routes
P_{a}=[j,k]
and
P_{b}=[l,i]
differ by at most one.
(it is unique when the cycle has an even number of nodes).

We prove that each demand in
\mathcal{D}^{Q}
has one end in
P_{a}
and one end in
P_{b}
. This is immediate for a such demand
p
for which either
a
or
b
belongs to
A(S_{p})
. So let us assume that neither
a
nor
b
belong to
A(S_{p})
. Then, by the definition of
P_{a}
and
P_{b}
,
S_{p}
is contained either in
P_{a}
or in
P_{b}
. Let
c
be the arc of
S_{p}
closest to
a
. Then, as
Q
is a clique of
H_{S}
, each route in
Q
that contains
a
must also contain
c
. Then, we get the contradiction that
c
appears in more routes of
Q
than
a
.

Let
R
be any routing of
\mathcal{D}
and let
p\in\mathcal{D}^{Q}
.
Since each demand in
\mathcal{D}^{Q}
has one end in
P_{a}
and one end in
P_{b}
,
the route
R_{p}
contains either
a
or
b
. Let
R^{a}
and
R^{b}
be the set of routes defined by
R
in
\mathcal{D}^{Q}
containing
a
and
b
, respectively. Then,
\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{D}^{Q})=\mathbf{w}(R^{a})+\mathbf{w}(R^{b}).

Both sets
R^{a}
and
R^{b}
form a clique in
H_{R}
and then
the weight of a maximum clique of
H_{R}
is at least
\max\{\mathbf{w}(R^{a}),\mathbf{w}(R^{b})\}
. We conclude that for each routing
R
,
\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{D}^{Q})=\mathbf{w}(R^{a})+\mathbf{w}(R^{b})\leq 2\omega(H_{R},\mathbf{w})
.
∎

Following ideas from
[
15
]
, we now present an LP-based polynomial time
3/2
-approximation algorithm for the
MCRPC
. Although we get a better approximation ratio than in Theorem
4.1
, the algorithm can be slower than the combinatorial one presented therein.

The algorithm proposed in
[
15
]
consists in rounding a solution of a relaxation of an integer linear programming formulation for
MCRPC
with uniform weights. Since the formulation has an exponential number of inequalities, the polynomiality of the algorithm depends on the ability to efficiently solve the corresponding separation problem. This latter problem consists in solving a clique problem in a circular-arc graph which is known to be polynomially solvable
[
7
]
.

The corresponding integer linear programming formulation for
MCRPC
with arbitrary weights also has an exponential number of inequalities. Moreover,
the separation problem consists in solving a weighted clique problem in a circular-arc graph. Luckily, this problem is also solvable in polynomial time
[
7
]
.
As a result, we can extend the algorithm for uniform weights to the general situation, as it is proved in the next theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

The
MCRPC
has a polynomial
\frac{3}{2}
-approximation algorithm.

Proof.

Following the notation of
[
15
]
, we introduce for each demand
p\in\mathcal{D}
two binary variables
x_{p}
and
y_{p}
, with
x_{p}+y_{p}=1
, to indicate whether
p
is routed over
p^{+}
(
x_{p}=1
) or over
p^{-}
(
y_{p}=1
).

Remember that
H
was defined as
the circular-arc graph obtained as the intersection graph of
all
possible demand routes, i.e., the intersection graph of the set of paths
\{p^{+},p^{-}\,:\,p\in\mathcal{D}\}
, and let
\mathcal{C}(H)
be the set of all cliques of
H
. Then the problem of finding a minimum-weight clique routing
for a set of demands
\mathcal{D}

can be formulated as:

\text{(MCR)}\left\{\begin{aligned} &\min K\\
&\text{s.t.}\\
&\sum_{p^{+}\in C}w_{p}x_{p}+\sum_{p^{-}\in C}w_{p}y_{p}\leq K,\quad\forall C\in\mathcal{C}(H),\\
&x_{p}+y_{p}=1,\quad\forall p\in\mathcal{D},\\
&x_{p},y_{p}\in\{0,1\},\quad\forall p\in\mathcal{D}.\end{aligned}\right.

Due to the equivalence between optimization and separation, the linear relaxation of this program can be solved in polynomial time. Indeed, given a fractional solution
(x^{*},y^{*})
the separation problem can be reduced to finding a maximum-weight clique on the circular-arc graph

H
with respect to the weights
w_{p}x^{*}_{p}
for
p^{+}
and
w_{p}y^{*}_{p}
for
p^{-}
, for all
p\in\mathcal{D}
.

5
A Fixed Parameter Tractable algorithm for uniform weights

In this section we assume that all weights are uniform, and w.l.o.g that all are equal to 1.
In this case the complexity of the
MCRPC
is not known. In fact, the subproblem we used to show that
MCRPC
is NP-hard for arbitrary weights, can be solved in polynomial time when the weights are all the same. We now present a structural property that we use later to show that
MCRPC
admits a Fixed Parameter Tractable (FPT) algorithm.

Given a routing
R
, two parallel demands
p
and
q

collide
in
R

if
A(R_{p})\cup A(R_{q})
is the set of arcs of the cycle and
A(R_{p})\cap A(R_{q})\neq\emptyset
. In this case, we call the pair
(p,q)
a
collision
in
R
.
Notice that two demands sharing the same endpoints (multiple demands) do not collide.

The existence of our Fixed Parameter Tractable algorithm for the
MCRPC
is based on the following structural property of its optimal solutions.

Theorem 5.1
.

The
MCRPC
for uniform weights has an optimal solution without collisions.

Proof.

By contradiction, let us assume that every optimal solution has collisions and take an optimal solution
R
with the minimum number of them. Let
(p,q)
be a collision in
R
such that
|A(R_{p})|+|A(R_{q})|
is as large as possible.

Consider the new solution
R^{\prime}
obtained from
R
by replacing
R_{p}
and
R_{q}
by
\overline{R_{p}}
and
\overline{R_{q}}
, respectively.
Then, in
H_{R^{\prime}}
the vertices
\hat{p}
and
\hat{q}
associated with the routes
R^{\prime}_{p}
and
R^{\prime}_{q}
are not adjacent. Moreover, any
neighbor of the vertex
\hat{p}
in
H_{R^{\prime}}
is also a neighbor of the vertex associated to
R_{q}
in
H_{R}
because
R^{\prime}_{p}\subseteq R_{q}
; similarly, any neighbor of the vertex
\hat{q}
in
H_{R^{\prime}}
is also a neighbor of the vertex associated to
R_{p}
in
H_{R}
.

Let
Q
be a set of pairwise adjacent vertices in
H_{R^{\prime}}
such that
|Q|=\omega(H_{R^{\prime}},\mathbf{1})
. We prove that
|Q|\leq\omega(H_{R},\mathbf{1})
.

If
Q
neither contain
\hat{p}
nor
\hat{q}
, then the vertices in
Q
are also pairwise adjacent in
H_{R}
. Whence,
|Q|\leq\omega(H_{R},\mathbf{1})
.
Otherwise, since
\hat{p}
and
\hat{q}
are not adjacent in
H_{R^{\prime}}
, we can assume that exactly one of them, let us say
\hat{p}
, belongs to
Q
. Hence, the vertices in
Q\setminus\{\hat{p}\}
are adjacent to the vertex associated to
R_{q}
in
H_{R}
thus defining a clique in
H_{R}
of size
|Q|
. This shows that
|Q|\leq\omega(H_{R},\mathbf{1})
.
Therefore,
\omega(H_{R^{\prime}},\mathbf{1})\leq\omega(H_{R},\mathbf{1})
.

In order to get a contradiction we observe that if
o\in\mathcal{D}
with
e(o)\neq e(p)
and
e(o)\subseteq V(\overline{R_{p}})
then,
R_{o}\subseteq\overline{R_{p}}
. Otherwise,
R_{p}\subseteq R_{o}
and
(o,q)
would be a collision, because
e(q)\subseteq R_{p}
. Since
|A(R_{p})|<|A(R_{o})|
, we would get a contradiction. Similarly, if
e(o)\neq e(q)
and
e(o)\subseteq V(\overline{R_{q}})
, then
R_{o}\subseteq\overline{R_{q}}
. This shows that neither
p
nor
q
are involved in any collision in
R^{\prime}
.
As any collision in
R^{\prime}
not involving neither
p
nor
q
is a collision in
R
we conclude that the number of collisions in
R^{\prime}
is smaller than the ones in
R
.
∎

Even though we do not known how to find an optimal routing for
all
the demands (in the general case), we can find an optimal routing when they are all parallel to some fixed demand. In order to present this result more precisely, we need some definitions.

For a demand
p\in\mathcal{D}
,
\mathcal{D}(p)
denotes the multiset of all demands
q
parallel to
p
such that
e(q)\neq e(p)
. Let
S^{p}
be the routing of
\mathcal{D}(p)
given by:

S^{p}_{q}\subseteq p^{+}
, when
e(q)\subseteq V(p^{+})
, and
S^{p}_{q}\subseteq p^{-}
, when
e(q)\subseteq V(p^{-})
.
Also, consider
c_{p}^{+}=|\{q:S^{p}_{q}\subseteq p^{+},q\neq p\}|
and
c_{p}^{-}=|\{q:S^{p}_{q}\subseteq p^{-},q\neq p\}|
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
p\in\mathcal{D}
be such that
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}(p)\cup\{p\}
. If
c_{p}^{+}\geq c_{p}^{-}
, there exists an optimal routing
R
without collisions such that
R_{p}=p^{-}
.

Proof.

From Theorem
5.1
we know that there is an optimal solution without collisions.
Suppose that
R^{\prime}
is an optimal routing without collisions such that
R^{\prime}_{p}=p^{+}
.
We know that for each
q
, with
S^{p}_{q}\subseteq p^{+}
, we have that
R^{\prime}_{q}=q^{+}
, in order to avoid collisions with
R^{\prime}_{p}
. Then,
\omega(H_{R^{\prime}},\mathbf{1})\geq c_{p}^{+}+1
.

Let
R
be the extension of
S^{p}
to a routing of
\mathcal{D}
given by
R_{p}=p^{-}
and

R_{q}=S^{p}_{q}
, for
q\neq p
. Then,
we get that
\omega(H_{R},\mathbf{1})=\max\{c_{p}^{+},c_{p}^{-}+1\}\leq c_{p}^{+}+1
, since
c_{p}^{+}\geq c_{p}^{-}
, by hypothesis. This shows that
\omega(H_{R},\mathbf{1})\leq\omega(H_{R^{\prime}},\mathbf{1})
. Therefore, since
S^{p}
has no collisions,

R
is an optimal routing without collisions.
∎

A demand
p
is
critical
for a routing
R
if for all
q\in\mathcal{D}(p)
,
R_{q}=S^{p}_{q}
.

It is easy to see that if
p
is such that
R_{p}
has the largest number of edges in a routing
R
without collisions, then
p
is critical for
R
. Notice that the number of routings for which a given demand
p
is critical is at most
2^{|\mathcal{D}\setminus\mathcal{D}(p)|}
. Thus, if
k=\max\{|\mathcal{D}\setminus\mathcal{D}(p)|:p\in\mathcal{D}\}
, we can derive an FPT algorithm parameterized by
k
since this number provides a bound on the possible routings having some critical demand in
\mathcal{D}
.
Notice also that when
\mathcal{D}
has no multiple demands, for each demand
p\in\mathcal{D}
, the value
|\mathcal{D}\setminus\mathcal{D}(p)|
is a lower bound for the degree of
p
in the graph
H_{R}
, for any routing
R
. Hence, in this case,
k
is a lower bound for the maximum degree of
H_{R}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

The
MCRPC
has an FPT algorithm with time complexity
O(2^{k}|\mathcal{D}|^{4})
.

Proof.

We define an algorithm that computes, for each demand
p
, a routing called
R^{p}
which minimizes
\omega(H_{R},\mathbf{1})
, over all routings
R
of
\mathcal{D}
for which
p
is critical for
R
.
The output of the algorithm is a routing
R^{*}
that minimizes
\omega(H_{R^{p}},\mathbf{1})
over all
p\in\mathcal{D}
.

The algorithm is correct since each routing without collisions has a critical demand and then, an optimal solution to our problem without collisions must be
R^{p}
, for some
p\in\mathcal{D}
.

For a given routing
R
, computing
\omega(H_{R},\mathbf{1})
takes time
O(|\mathcal{D}|^{3})
. Then, to determine
R^{p}
takes time
O(2^{k}|\mathcal{D}|^{3})
, since the set of routings for which
p
is critical has at most
2^{k}
elements. Therefore, the time complexity of this algorithm is
O(2^{k}|\mathcal{D}|^{4})
.
∎

6
Conclusions

We have proved that for arbitrary weights
MCRPC
is NP-hard and it has a 3/2-approximation algorithm.

We also have proposed an FPT algorithm for the case when all weights are equal to one.
Unfortunately, despite a great amount of effort, we have been unable to determine the complexity of
MCRPC
in this case. However, for some special cases (those appearing in the proof of the NP-hardness of
MCRPC
) we know that the unweighted case has a polynomial time algorithm whereas the weighted case is still NP-hard.

Surprisingly, even though we do not know how to get an optimal solution when all the weights are the same, we do know how to route each
extreme
demand, i.e., a demand
p
such that either
p^{+}
or
p^{-}
does not contain the two ends of another demand.
In fact, one can see that if
p^{+}
does not contain the two ends of another demand, then the vertex representing
p^{-}
in
H
is universal. Thus, if
R
routes
p
through
p^{-}
, then the routing
R^{\prime}
obtained from
R
by just changing the route of
p
from
p^{-}
to
p^{+}
defines a graph
H_{R^{\prime}}
whose maximum clique has at most the size of a maximum clique of
H_{R}
.

Notice that this idea allows us to reduce the original problem to a smaller one consisting only of demands which are not extreme. However, this new problem is a weighted problem. Even worse, the remaining demands
q
might have a different weights for
q^{+}
and
q^{-}
.

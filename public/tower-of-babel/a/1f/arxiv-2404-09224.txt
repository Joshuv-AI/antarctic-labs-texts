Quotient Modules of Finite Length and Their Relation to Fredholm Elements in Semiprime Rings
Niklas Ludwig

Abstract




1
Abstract

B. A. Barnes introduced so-called Fredholm elements in a semiprime ring whose definition is
inspired by Atkinson’s theorem
[
4
]
. Here the socle of a semiprime ring generalizes
the ideal of finite-rank operators on a Banach space.
There already exist many generalizations of Fredholm elements like the one discussed in
[
10
]
and
[
8
]
.
In this paper, we aim to see that the algebraic concept of the length of a module is
strongly related to that of Fredholm elements. This motivates another generalization of Fredholm elements
by requiring for an element
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
that the
{\mathcal{A}}
-modules of the form
{\mathcal{A}}/{\mathcal{A}}a
and
{\mathcal{A}}/a{\mathcal{A}}
are of finite length.
We are particularly interested in sufficient conditions for our generalized Fredholm elements to be Fredholm.
In a unital C
∗
-algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
we shall even see that an element
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
is Fredholm if and only if the
{\mathcal{A}}
-modules
{\mathcal{A}}/{\mathcal{A}}a
and
{\mathcal{A}}/a{\mathcal{A}}

both have finite length.

2
Preliminary

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a ring and
S\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
. Then we define the annihilators

\displaystyle\Lan(S)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{a\in{\mathcal{A}}:as=0\text{ for all }s\in S\}

\displaystyle\Ran(S)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{a\in{\mathcal{A}}:sa=0\text{ for all }s\in S\}.

Note that
\Lan(S)\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
is a left ideal and
\Ran(S)\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
is a right ideal.
A semiprime ring
{\mathcal{A}}
is called right annihilator ring if for every modular maximal left ideal
m\subsetneq{\mathcal{A}}
it holds that
\Ran(m)\neq 0
(see
[
2
]
).
Now assume
{\mathcal{A}}
is semiprime. Then, we write
\soc({\mathcal{A}})
for the socle of
{\mathcal{A}}
.
Moreover, we call a left (right) ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}
of finite order if it can be written as a finite sum of minimal left (right) ideals.
In this case, the minimal number of such minimal left (right) ideals needed is called the order of this left (right) ideal (see
[
3
]
).
If
{\mathcal{A}}
is unital, an element
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
is called Fredholm if the image
[a]

of the quotient map is invertible in
{\mathcal{A}}/\soc({\mathcal{A}})
(see
[
4
]
).
Similarly, we call an element
a
semi
+
-Fredholm if
[a]
is left invertible and
semi
-
-Fredholm if
[a]
is right invertible in
{\mathcal{A}}/\soc({\mathcal{A}})
.

Caution is advised as we always use the term semisimple in the sense of
{\mathcal{A}}
-modules and call a ring
J
-semisimple instead
if the intersection of its modular maximal left ideals is trivial.
Throughout the paper, we shall only consider Banach algebras and C
∗
-algebras over the complex numbers.

3
Fredholm Ideals

In the entire section
{\mathcal{A}}
denotes a semiprime unital ring.
For us, it will be more convenient to study Fredholm elements by using
the theory of one-sided ideals.

Definition 3.1
.

We call a left ideal
L\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
Fredholm if it contains a Fredholm element.

The following is an important result due to Barnes:

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
F\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
is a left (right) ideal of finite order. Then there exists an idempotent
p\in\soc({\mathcal{A}})
satisfying
{\mathcal{A}}p=F
(
p{\mathcal{A}}=F
).

Proof.

(see
[
3
]
, Theorem 2.2)
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

A left ideal
L\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
is Fredholm if and only if there exists an idempotent
p\in\soc({\mathcal{A}})
such that
L={\mathcal{A}}(1-p)
.

Proof.

Suppose there is a Fredholm element
a\in L
. Then, there exists an idempotent
q\in\soc({\mathcal{A}})
such that

{\mathcal{A}}a={\mathcal{A}}(1-q)
holds (see
[
4
]
, Theorem 2.3). We set
L^{\prime}\coloneqq L\cap{\mathcal{A}}q
. As
L^{\prime}
is of finite order,
there exists an idempotent
p^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}
such that
L^{\prime}={\mathcal{A}}p^{\prime}
by Lemma
3.2
. Now, we define
p\coloneqq q-p^{\prime}
. Note that
p^{\prime}q=p^{\prime}
and hence
p^{\prime}p=0
.

In order to show
L\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}(1-p)
, let
x\in L
. Then
xq=x-x(1-q)\in L
and therefore
xq\in L^{\prime}
.
Thus, there exists
b\in{\mathcal{A}}
with
xq=bp^{\prime}
. We finally see that

xp=x(1-q)p+xqp=x(1-p-p^{\prime})p+bp^{\prime}p=0.

For the other inclusion, let
x\in{\mathcal{A}}
with
xp=0
. Again we write
x=x(1-q)+xq
and note that
x(1-q)\in L
.
As
xq=xp+xp^{\prime}=xp^{\prime}
, it finally follows that
L={\mathcal{A}}(1-p)
.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Then
a
is semi
+
-Fredholm if and only if the left ideal
{\mathcal{A}}a
is Fredholm.

Proposition 3.5
.

A finite intersection of Fredholm left ideals is again Fredholm.

Proof.

Suppose
L\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
and
L^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
both are Fredholm left ideals. By Proposition
3.3
there exist
p,q\in\soc({\mathcal{A}})

with
L={\mathcal{A}}(1-p)
and
L^{\prime}={\mathcal{A}}(1-q)
. As
p{\mathcal{A}}+q{\mathcal{A}}
is of finite order, Lemma
3.2
implies that there exists
r\in\soc({\mathcal{A}})
such that

p{\mathcal{A}}+q{\mathcal{A}}=r{\mathcal{A}}.

We show that
L\cap L^{\prime}={\mathcal{A}}(1-r)
: Indeed let
x\in L\cap L^{\prime}
. As
xp=xq=0

and
r\in p{\mathcal{A}}+q{\mathcal{A}}
, we obtain that
xr=0
. Conversely, pick
x\in{\mathcal{A}}
with
xr=0
.
Clearly
p,q\in r{\mathcal{A}}
. This already implies
xp=xq=0
and thus
x\in L\cap L^{\prime}
.
∎

We show the following statement in order to bring the concept of length into play.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
L\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
be a left ideal. Then
L
is of finite order if and only if
L
has finite length as an
{\mathcal{A}}
-module.
In this case, the order of
L
and
\len_{\mathcal{A}}(L)
coincide.

Proof.

Assume
L
is of finite order. Then, there exist minimal left ideals
m_{1},\dots,m_{n}\subseteq L
such that
L=\oplus_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}

and
n
is the order of
L
(see
[
3
]
, Theorem 2.2).
Clearly, we have
\len_{\mathcal{A}}(m_{j})=1
. As the length is additive, we get

\len_{\mathcal{A}}(L)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\len_{\mathcal{A}}(m_{j})=n.

Conversely, suppose
n\coloneqq\len_{\mathcal{A}}(L)<\infty
. We prove the statement by induction on
n
:

If
n=0
, we are done.

If
n>0
, the left ideal
L
contains a minimal left ideal
m\subseteq L
since
\len_{\mathcal{A}}(L)<\infty
. Now, there exists a minimal idempotent
p\in{\mathcal{A}}
such that
m={\mathcal{A}}p
.
Thus, we can write
{\mathcal{A}}p\oplus[L\cap{\mathcal{A}}(1-p)]=L
: We only show
L\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}p\oplus[L\cap{\mathcal{A}}(1-p)]
: Indeed for
x\in L
,
we have
x(1-p)=x-xp\in L
and hence
x(1-p)\in L\cap{\mathcal{A}}(1-p)
. This yields
x=xp+x(1-p)\in{\mathcal{A}}p\oplus[L\cap{\mathcal{A}}(1-p)]
.
Finally

\len_{\mathcal{A}}(L\cap{\mathcal{A}}(1-p))=n-1

and the claim follows by using the induction hypothesis.
∎

Definition 3.7
.

Let
L\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
be a left ideal. Then we set
\varrho(L)\coloneqq\len_{{\mathcal{A}}}(\Ran(L))
and

\xi(L)\coloneqq\len_{\mathcal{A}}({\mathcal{A}}/L)
. If
L={\mathcal{A}}a
for some
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
, we write
\varrho_{l}(a)\coloneqq\varrho(L)
and

\xi_{l}(a)\coloneqq\xi(L)
. The corresponding definition can be made for right ideals. In particular for
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
,
we write
\varrho_{r}(a)\coloneqq\len_{{\mathcal{A}}}(\Lan(a))
and
\xi_{r}(a)\coloneqq\len_{{\mathcal{A}}}({\mathcal{A}}/a{\mathcal{A}})
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
a\in\soc({\mathcal{A}})
. Then both
a{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{A}}a
are of the same order.

Proof.

Let
\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}a
be a maximal orthogonal set of minimal idempotents.
Then
{\mathcal{A}}a={\mathcal{A}}p
where
p\coloneqq p_{1}+\dots+p_{n}
(see
[
3
]
, Theorem 2.2) and
n
coincides with the order of
{\mathcal{A}}a
.
Therefore, there exists
b\in{\mathcal{A}}
satisfying
a=bp
and the map
p{\mathcal{A}}\to a{\mathcal{A}},x\mapsto bx
is surjective. Thus, we conclude that the order of

a{\mathcal{A}}
is bounded by
n
. The other inequality is shown analogously.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
L\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
be a left ideal. Then
L
is Fredholm if and only if
\xi(L)\leq\varrho(L)<\infty
.
In this case,
\xi(L)=\varrho(L)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
L
is Fredholm. By Proposition
3.3
, there exists an idempotent
p\in\soc({\mathcal{A}})
such that
L={\mathcal{A}}(1-p)
.
As the one-sided ideals
p{\mathcal{A}}=\Ran(L)
and
{\mathcal{A}}p
are of the same order and
{\mathcal{A}}/L\cong{\mathcal{A}}p
, Lemma
3.6
yields
\len_{\mathcal{A}}{{\mathcal{A}}/L}=\varrho(L)<\infty
.

Conversely, assume
\len_{\mathcal{A}}{{\mathcal{A}}/L}\leq\varrho(L)<\infty
. Lemma
3.6
implies that
\Ran(L)
is of finite order and thus
there exists an idempotent
p\in\soc({\mathcal{A}})
satisfying
p{\mathcal{A}}=\Ran(L)
by Lemma
3.2
.
We set
L^{\prime}\coloneqq{\mathcal{A}}(1-p)
and we already know that
\len_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}/L^{\prime}=\varrho(L^{\prime})=\varrho(L)
. Since
L\subseteq L^{\prime}
, we can
consider the surjective map

\nu\colon{\mathcal{A}}/L\to{\mathcal{A}}/L^{\prime},[x]\mapsto[x].

As
\len_{\mathcal{A}}{{\mathcal{A}}/L}\leq\varrho(L)=\len_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}/L^{\prime}<\infty
, the map
\nu
is even an isomorphism. Hence,
L=L^{\prime}
and we are done.
∎

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
L\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
be a left ideal. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

L
is Fredholm,

(ii)

L=\Lan(\Ran(L))
and
\varrho(L)<\infty
,

(iii)

there exist
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and idempotent elements
(p_{j})_{j=1,\dots,n}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
such that for
j=1,\dots,n
the left ideals
{\mathcal{A}}p_{j}
are maximal
and
L=\bigcap_{j=1}^{n}{\mathcal{A}}p_{j}
.

Proof.

“
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
”: This is a direct consequence of Proposition
3.3
and Lemma
3.6
.

“
(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
”: By Lemma
3.6
, we know that
\Ran(L)
has finite order and thus there exist minimal idempotent elements
q_{1},\dots q_{n}\in{\mathcal{A}}

such that
\Ran(L)=q_{1}{\mathcal{A}}+\dots+q_{n}{\mathcal{A}}
. We set
p_{j}\coloneqq 1-q_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,n
and note that
{\mathcal{A}}p_{j}
are maximal left ideals. Finally, we can write

L=\Lan(\Ran(L))=\Lan(q_{1}{\mathcal{A}}+\dots+q_{n}{\mathcal{A}})=\bigcap_{j=1}^{n}{\mathcal{A}}p_{j}.

“
(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
”: Note that
1-p_{j}
are minimal idempotent elements for
j=1,\dots,n
and hence
{\mathcal{A}}p_{j}
are Fredholm left ideals by Proposition
3.3
.
The statement finally follows by Proposition
3.5
.
∎

Definition 3.11
.

We call a left ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}
semi-maximal if it can be written as an intersection of maximal left ideals.

Next, we repeat some standard results of non-commutative ring theory:

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
L\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
be a left ideal. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

the left
{\mathcal{A}}
-module
{\mathcal{A}}/L
is semisimple,

(ii)

L
is semi-maximal and
\xi(L)<\infty
,

(iii)

there exist
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and maximal ideals
m_{1},\dots,m_{n}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
such that
L=\bigcap_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}
.

Proof.

“
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
”: As
{\mathcal{A}}/L
is semisimple and
{\mathcal{A}}/L
is finitely generated (
[1]\in{\mathcal{A}}/L
), we see that
\len_{\mathcal{A}}{{\mathcal{A}}/L}<\infty
.
Further,
{\mathcal{A}}/L
is J-semisimple (see
[
7
]
, (4.14) Theorem) and thus
L

can be written as the intersection of all maximal left ideals that contain
L
.

“
(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
”: WLOG we have
L\subsetneq{\mathcal{A}}
. Then
L
is contained in a maximal left ideal
m_{1}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
and we set
L_{1}\coloneqq m_{1}
.
For
j\geq 2
, we choose
m_{j}
recursively and distinguish two cases:
If
L_{j-1}\subseteq m
for all maximal left ideals
L\subseteq m\subset{\mathcal{A}}
, we terminate. Otherwise we can pick a maximal left ideal
L\subseteq m_{j}\subset{\mathcal{A}}

with
L_{j-1}\nsubseteq m_{j}
and set
L_{j}\coloneqq L_{j-1}\cap m_{j}
. This procedure terminates after finitely many iterations as

L\subseteq\dots\subsetneq L_{2}\subsetneq L_{1}

and
\len_{\mathcal{A}}{{\mathcal{A}}/L}<\infty
. Let
n
be one less the number of iterations. Then, we obtain
L=L_{n}
since
L
is semi-maximal.

“
(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
”: We simply note that there is an injective
{\mathcal{A}}
-linear map
{\mathcal{A}}/L\to\oplus_{j=1}^{n}{\mathcal{A}}/m_{j}
.
∎

Corollary 3.13
.

If
L\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
is a Fredholm left ideal, then
{\mathcal{A}}/L
is a semisimple
{\mathcal{A}}
-module.

The following result due to B. A. Barnes states that a unital ring where all maximal left ideals are Fredholm is semisimple.
We shall later see that the situation can be very different for principal one-sided ideals.

Proposition 3.14
.

The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

{\mathcal{A}}
is a unital right annihilator ring,

(ii)

every left ideal is Fredholm,

(iii)

{\mathcal{A}}
is semisimple,

(iv)

every maximal left ideal is Fredholm.

Proof.

(see
[
2
]
, Theorem 4.3) and (see
[
4
]
, Theorem 2.4)
∎

4
Weak-Fredholm Elements

In the entire section,
{\mathcal{A}}
denotes a unital ring.
Note that the following definition is actually weaker than the definition of Fredholm elements.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Then we call
a
weak
+
-Fredholm if
\xi_{l}(a)<\infty
.
Similarly, we call
a
weak
-
-Fredholm if
\xi_{r}(a)<\infty
.
Moreover, we call an element weak-Fredholm if it is both weak
+
-Fredholm and weak
-
-Fredholm.

Example 4.2
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{A}}
is a commutative unital Banach algebra. Then an element
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
is weak-Fredholm if and only if
the multiplication operator
M_{a}\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}},\,x\mapsto xa
is semi
-
-Fredholm. Further,
\xi_{l}(a)=\xi_{r}(a)=\dim(\coker M_{a})
.

Proof.

Suppose
M_{a}
is semi
-
-Fredholm. Then, it is clear that
\xi_{l}(a)=\xi_{r}(a)<\infty
.

Conversely, assume that
a
is weak-Fredholm. Then, there exists a composition series

{\mathcal{A}}a=I_{0}\subsetneq I_{1}\dots\subsetneq I_{n}={\mathcal{A}}

such that the quotients
I_{j}/I_{j-1}
are simple
{\mathcal{A}}
-modules for
j=1,\dots,n
and
n=\xi_{l}(a)
. Hence, there exist maximal ideals
m_{j}\subset{\mathcal{A}}

satisfying
I_{j}/I_{j-1}\cong{\mathcal{A}}/m_{j}
. Since
{\mathcal{A}}
is a unital Banach algebra

I_{j}/I_{j-1}\cong{\mathcal{A}}/m_{j}\cong\mathbb{C}

for
j=1,\dots,n
and thus
n=\len_{\mathbb{C}}({\mathcal{A}}/{\mathcal{A}}a)=\dim(\coker M_{a})
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose
a,b\in{\mathcal{A}}
are weak
+
-Fredholm. Then
ab
is again weak
+
-Fredholm and
\xi_{l}(ab)\leq\xi_{l}(a)+\xi_{l}(b)
.

Proof.

Consider the map
\phi\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}/{\mathcal{A}}ab\,,x\mapsto[xb]
.
As
{\mathcal{A}}a\subseteq\ker\phi
, there exists an
{\mathcal{A}}
-linear map
\overline{\phi}\colon{\mathcal{A}}/{\mathcal{A}}a\to{\mathcal{A}}/{\mathcal{A}}ab

with
\im\overline{\phi}={\mathcal{A}}b/{\mathcal{A}}ab
. Thus, the following sequence is exact:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}}/{\mathcal{A}}a}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}}/{\mathcal{A}}ab}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}}/{\mathcal{A}}b}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\phi}}

As the length is additive in short exact sequences, the claim follows.
∎

In general, it is not always true that a weak
+
-Fredholm element
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
satisfies
\varrho_{l}(a)<\infty
.
In the following, we want to find sufficient conditions that ensure
\varrho(L)<\infty
for a left ideal
L\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
L\subsetneq{\mathcal{A}}
be a left ideal such that
{\mathcal{A}}/L
is a semisimple
{\mathcal{A}}
-module. Then, there exist
q\in{\mathcal{A}}\setminus L
such that

L+{\mathcal{A}}(1-q)
is a maximal left ideal and
Lq\subseteq L
.

Proof.

{\mathcal{A}}/L
contains a minimal submodule
N\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}/L
. As
{\mathcal{A}}/L
is semisimple there exists a submodule
M\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}/L
such that
N\oplus M={\mathcal{A}}/L
.
Thus, we can write
[1]=[q]+[m]
where
[q]\in N
and
[m]\in M
. Clearly,
[q]\neq 0
and hence
q\in{\mathcal{A}}\setminus L
.
For
x\in L
we have
[0]=[x]=[xq]+[xm]
. Since the sum is direct, this yields
[xq]=0
and thus
xq\in L
. This shows
Lq\subseteq L
.
In order to show that
L+{\mathcal{A}}(1-q)
is a maximal left ideal, we prove

M=\{a[1-q]:a\in{\mathcal{A}}\}.

Evidently,
[1-q]=[m]\in M
. Conversely, let
[x]\in M
. Then
[xq]=[x]-[xm]\in M
and therefore
[xq]=0
. Thus
[x]=[xm]=x[1-q]
.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a J-semisimple unital ring and
L\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
be a left ideal such that
{\mathcal{A}}/L
is a semisimple
{\mathcal{A}}
-module. Then
\varrho(L)<\infty
.

Proof.

We by proof the statement by induction on
n\coloneqq\len_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}}/L
. The case
n=0
is trivial.

If
n=1
, then
L
is a maximal left ideal. Let
0\neq R\subseteq\Ran(L)
be a right ideal and choose
0\neq x\in R
.
As
{\mathcal{A}}
is J-semisimple, there exists
b\in{\mathcal{A}}
such that
1-xb\notin{\mathcal{A}}^{\times}
. Clearly, we have

L\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}(1-xb)\subsetneq{\mathcal{A}}.

By the maximality of
L
, we get
L={\mathcal{A}}(1-xb)
and thus
1-xb\in L
. This yields
R=\Ran(L)
and we obtain
\len(\Ran(L))\leq 1
.

Now, assume
n\geq 2
. Lemma
4.4
yields the existence of an element
q\in{\mathcal{A}}
as described above. In order to get

\Ran(L)=[\Ran(L)\cap(1-q){\mathcal{A}}]+[\Ran(L)\cap q{\mathcal{A}}]=\Ran(L+{\mathcal{A}}q)+\Ran(L+{\mathcal{A}}(1-q)),

we only need to show
\Ran(L)\subseteq[\Ran(L)\cap(1-q){\mathcal{A}}]+[\Ran(L)\cap q{\mathcal{A}}]
. Indeed, let
x\in\Ran(L)
. We can write

x=(1-q)x+qx
. Since
aq\in L
for all
a\in L
, we obtain
(1-q)x,qx\in\Ran(L)
and thus the inclusion holds.
Now, apply the induction hypothesis.
∎

Definition 4.6
.

We say a semiprime ring
{\mathcal{A}}
has essential socle if
I\cap\soc({\mathcal{A}})=0
already implies
I=0
for all ideals
I\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
.

Let us recall some basic characterizations of semiprime rings with essential socle:

Proposition 4.7
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{A}}
is semiprime. Then, the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

the ring
{\mathcal{A}}
has essential socle,

(ii)

for every
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
with
a\soc({\mathcal{A}})=0
, it already holds that
a=0
,

(iii)

for every
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
with
\soc({\mathcal{A}})a=0
, it already holds that
a=0
.

Proof.

“
(i)\Rightarrow(iii)
”: Assume
\soc({\mathcal{A}})a=0
and let
I\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
be the ideal generated by
a
.
We want to show that
I\cap\soc({\mathcal{A}})=0
: Indeed, let
x\in I\cap\soc({\mathcal{A}})
.
Lemma
3.2
yields that there exists an idempotent
p\in\soc({\mathcal{A}})
such that
{\mathcal{A}}x={\mathcal{A}}p
.
Thus
p\in I
and there exist elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in{\mathcal{A}}
and
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\in{\mathcal{A}}
such that

p=p^{2}=p\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}ay_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(px_{j})ay_{j}=0

as
px_{j}\in\soc({\mathcal{A}})
for
j=1,\dots,n
. We hence see that
I\cap\soc({\mathcal{A}})=0
and as
{\mathcal{A}}
has essential socle we conclude
a=0
.

“
(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
”: Let
I\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
be an ideal with
I\cap\soc({\mathcal{A}})=0
. Then we pick an arbitrary
a\in I
and note that

\soc({\mathcal{A}})a\subseteq I\cap\soc({\mathcal{A}})=0
. Then, we already get

.

“
(i)\iff(ii)
”: This is done analogously.

Lemma 4.8
.

Assume
{\mathcal{A}}
has essential socle and let
R\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
be a right ideal. If there exists an upper bound
M\geq 0

such that for all idempotent elements
p\in\soc({\mathcal{A}})\cap R
we have
\len_{\mathcal{A}}(p{\mathcal{A}})\leq M
, then
\len_{\mathcal{A}}(R)\leq M
.

Proof.

Choose an idempotent
p\in\soc({\mathcal{A}})\cap R
such that
\len_{\mathcal{A}}(p{\mathcal{A}})
is maximal and suppose
p{\mathcal{A}}\subsetneq R
.
Then, there exists
r\in R\setminus p{\mathcal{A}}
. We write
r=pr+(1-p)r
and deduce that
0\neq(1-p)r\in R
.
Proposition
4.7
yields that there exists
0\neq x\in(1-p)r\soc({\mathcal{A}})\subseteq R
. By Lemma
3.2
, there exists an idempotent

q\in\soc({\mathcal{A}})
such that
q{\mathcal{A}}=x{\mathcal{A}}+p{\mathcal{A}}
. As
q\in R

and
p{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq q{\mathcal{A}}
, we obtain
p{\mathcal{A}}=q{\mathcal{A}}
by the maximality of
\len_{\mathcal{A}}(p{\mathcal{A}})
. But then
x\in p{\mathcal{A}}\cap(1-p){\mathcal{A}}=0

and we reach a contradiction!
∎

Theorem 4.9
.

Assume
{\mathcal{A}}
has essential socle. If
L\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
is a left ideal such that
\xi(L)<\infty
, then
\varrho(L)\leq\xi(L)<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
p\in\soc({\mathcal{A}})\cap\Ran(L)
be an idempotent. We show that
\len_{\mathcal{A}}(p{\mathcal{A}})\leq\xi(L)
:
Indeed, we have
L\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}(1-p)
. We then get by using Proposition
3.9

\len_{\mathcal{A}}(p{\mathcal{A}})=\varrho_{l}(1-p)=\xi_{l}(1-p)\leq\xi(L).

The claim finally follows by Lemma
4.8
.
∎

Suppose

(ii)

Let

a

Suppose
{\mathcal{A}}

+

Suppose

In order to simplify proofs, we need a notion of Fredholm maps between modules:

Definition 4.13
.

Let
M_{1},M_{2}
be left
{\mathcal{A}}
-modules and
\varphi\colon M_{1}\to M_{2}
be an
{\mathcal{A}}
-linear map. Then we call
\varphi
Fredholm if
\len_{\mathcal{A}}(\ker\varphi)<\infty

and
\len_{\mathcal{A}}(\coker\varphi)<\infty
. In this case, we set

\ind(\varphi)\coloneqq\len_{\mathcal{A}}(\ker\varphi)-\len_{\mathcal{A}}(\coker\varphi).

The following lemma is just a generalized version of the usual additivity of the index in the vector space case:

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
M_{1},M_{2},M_{3}
be left
{\mathcal{A}}
-modules and
\varphi\colon M_{1}\to M_{2}
as well as
\psi\colon M_{2}\to M_{3}
be
{\mathcal{A}}
-linear maps
that are Fredholm. Then

\ind(\psi\circ\varphi)=\ind(\psi)+\ind(\varphi).

Proof.

(see
[
11
]
, Theorem 4)
∎

Theorem 4.15
.

Suppose
a,b\in{\mathcal{A}}
are weak-Fredholm elements. Then

\zeta_{l}(ab)+\zeta_{r}(ab)=\zeta_{l}(a)+\zeta_{r}(a)+\zeta_{l}(b)+\zeta_{r}(b).

Proof.

Consider the maps
\varphi\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}\,,x\mapsto xa
and
\psi\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}\,,x\mapsto xb
.
Then
\varphi
and
\psi
are Fredholm maps and Lemma
4.14
yields that:

\displaystyle\varrho_{r}(ab)-\xi_{l}(ab)=\ind(\psi\circ\varphi)=\ind(\psi)+\ind(\varphi)=\varrho_{r}(a)-\xi_{l}(a)+\varrho_{r}(b)-\xi_{l}(b)

Similarly, we obtain
\varrho_{l}(ab)-\xi_{r}(ab)=\varrho_{l}(a)-\xi_{r}(a)+\varrho_{l}(b)-\xi_{r}(b)
.
By adding up both equations, the claim follows.
∎

Corollary 4.16
.

Assume
{\mathcal{A}}
is semiprime and let

is Fredholm or
b

Let

Theorem 5.3

-module

Now, we can see that

By the Neumann series, we obtain

.
In particular, we see that

is generated by an idempotent and that

The statement finally follows by Theorem

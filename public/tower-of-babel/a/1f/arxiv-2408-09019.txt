On Pólya groups of some non-Galois number fields
Abbas Maarefparvar

Abstract
. We prove two conjectures raised in Chabert and Halberstadt’s paper [ 4 ] concerning Pólya groups of S 4 S_{4} -fields and D 4 D_{4} -fields. More generally, the latter will be proved for D n D_{n} -fields with n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 an even integer. Further, generalizing a result of Zantema [ 17 ] , we also prove that the pre-Pólya group of a non-Galois field of a prime degree, e.g. an A 5 A_{5} -field, coincides with its Pólya group.

1.
Introduction

Let
K
be a number field and denote its ring of integers by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. The ring of integer-valued polynomials on
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is defined as

\displaystyle\Int(\mathcal{O}_{K})=\{f\in K[x]\mid f(\mathcal{O}_{K})\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}\}.

It has been proved that
\Int(\mathcal{O}_{K})
is a free
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-module, however finding an explicit
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-basis for
\Int(\mathcal{O}_{K})
might be a challenging task
[
2
, Remark II.3.7]
. Pólya
[
15
]
was interested in those fields
K
for which Int(
\mathcal{O}_{K}
) has a
regular basis
, i.e., an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
basis
\{f_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
such that
deg(f_{n})=n
, for every
n
. As suggested by Zantema
[
17
]
, such field
K
is called a
Pólya field
.

We observe that
K
is a Pólya field if and only if for every prime power
q
, the ideal

(1.1)

\displaystyle\Pi_{q}(K)=:\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{m}\in\mathbb{P}_{K}\\
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{m})=q\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{m}

is principal (By convention, if
K
has no ideal with norm
q
, set
\Pi_{q}(K)=\mathcal{O}_{K}
). This criterion is given by Ostrowski
[
14
]
, and so, we call
\Pi_{q}(K)
an
Ostrowski ideal
.

In a more modern approach, in 1997, the notion of
Pólya group
was introduced by Cahen and Chabert
[
2
, Chapter II,
\lx@sectionsign
3]
.

Definition 1.1
.

The Pólya group of a number field
K
, denoted by
\Po(K)
, is the subgroup of
\Cl(K)
generated by the classes of all the Ostrowski ideals (
1.1
), i.e.,

(1.2)

\Po(K)=\langle\left[\Pi_{p^{f}}(K)\right]\,:\,f\in\mathbb{N},\,\text{$p$ is a prime number}\rangle.

Pólya fields, i.e., number fields with trivial Pólya groups, include various ranges of number fields, such as class number one fields, cyclotomic fields
[
17
]
, and Hilbert class fields
[
9
]
.

Investigating Pólya fields and Pólya groups can be divided into Galois and non-Galois cases. If
K/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois, then all the Ostrowski ideals above
unramified
primes are principal
[
14
, Page 121]
. Consequently, the Pólya group
\Po(K)
would be generated by the classes
\Pi_{q}(K)
where
q
is a power of a
ramified
prime
p
in
K/\mathbb{Q}
. Most of the results in the literature for Pólya fields and Pólya groups concern Galois number fields, see, e.g.,
[
3
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
. In contrast, the situation for the non-Galois case is more complicated because, in this case, one also needs to consider the Ostrowski ideals above unramified primes. As the first tool for investigating the Pólya-ness of non-Galois fields, the notion of
pre-Pólya field
was introduced by Zantema.

Definition 1.2
.

[
17
, Definition 6.1]

A number field
K
is called a pre-Pólya field if for every unramified prime
p
in
K/\mathbb{Q}
and each
f\in\mathbb{N}
, the Ostrowski ideal
\Pi_{p^{f}}(K)
(
1.1
) is principal.

Remark 1.3
.

Note that if
K/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois, then
K
is a pre-Pólya field. Because in this case, as mentioned before, all the Ostrowski ideals
\Pi_{q}(K)
above unramified primes are principal.

Let
[K:\mathbb{Q}]=n\geq 3
. Recall that for a transitive subgroup
G
of
S_{n}
,
K
is called a
G
-field
if the Galois closure of
K
(over
\mathbb{Q}
) has the Galois group isomorphic to
G
. Zantema showed that for a ‘vast range’ of non-Galois
G
-fields, pre-Pólya-ness is equivalent to having class number one.

Theorem 1.4

(
Zantema
)
.

Let
K
be a number field of degree
n\geq 3
. The following assertions hold:

(a)

[
17
, Theorem 6.9]
If
K
is a
S_{n}
-field (
n\neq 4
), or an
A_{n}
-field (
n\neq 3,5
), or a
G
-field for some Frobenius group
G
, then

\text{$K$ is a pre-P\'{o}lya field}\iff\text{$K$ is a P\'{o}lya field}\iff h_{K}=1.

(b)

[
18
, Theorem 5.5]
If
K
is a
S_{4}
-field, then

\text{$K$ is a P\'{o}lya field}\iff h_{K}=1.

(c)

[
17
, Section 8]
If
K
is an
A_{5}
-field, then

\text{$K$ is a pre-P\'{o}lya field}\iff\text{$K$ is a P\'{o}lya field}.

Remark 1.5
.

Zantema gave an example of a pre-Pólya
S_{4}
-fields which is not Pólya, see
[
17
, Section 6]
. Also, he presented an example of a Pólya
A_{5}
-field with class number three, see
[
17
, Section 8]
.

Generalizing the notion of the pre-Pólya field, Chabert and Halberstadt
[
4
]
introduced the notion of
pre-Pólya group
.

Definition 1.6
.

[
4
, Section 1]

For a number field
K
, the
pre-Pólya group
of
K
, denoted by
\Po(K)_{nr}
, is the subgroup of
\Cl(K)
generated by the classes of all the Ostrowski ideals above unramified primes, i.e.,

(1.3)

\Po(K)_{nr}=\langle\left[\Pi_{p^{f}}(K)\right]\,:\,f\in\mathbb{N},\,\text{$p$ is an unramified prime in $K/\mathbb{Q}$}\rangle.

Part (a) of Theorem
1.4
has been generalized by Chabert and Halberstadt as follows.

Theorem 1.7
.

[
4
, Theorem 4.1]

Let
K
be a number field of degree
n\geq 3
. If
K
is a
G
-field, where
G=S_{n}
(
n\neq 4
), or
G=A_{n}
(
n\neq 3,5
), or
G
is a Frobenius group, then

\Po(K)_{nr1}=\Po(K)_{nr}=\Po(K)=\Cl(K),

where

(1.4)

\Po(K)_{nr1}=\langle\left[\Pi_{p}(K)\right]\,:\,\text{$p$ is an unramified prime in $K/\mathbb{Q}$}\rangle.

Inspired by part (b) of Theorem
1.4
,
Chabert and Halberstadt
[
4
, Section 6]
presented the following conjecture for
S_{4}
-fields.

Conjecture 1.8

(
Chabert-Halberstadt
)
.

Let
K
be a
S_{4}
-field. Then

\Po(K)=\Cl(K).

Similarly, part (c) of Theorem
1.4
leads us to the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.9
.

Let
K
be an
A_{5}
-field. Then
\Po(K)_{nr}=\Po(K)
.

Now let
K
be a
D_{n}
-field (Recall that
D_{n}
denotes the dihedral group of order
2n
). Chabert and Halberstadt proved that
\Po(K)_{nr1}=\Po(K)_{nr}
, see
[
4
, Proposition 5.1]
. Then, investigating
\Po(K)_{nr}
in the special case
[K:\mathbb{Q}]=4
, they proposed the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.10

(
Chabert-Halberstadt
)
.

[
4
, Section 6]

Let
K
be a
D_{4}
-field. If
h_{K}=2
, then
\Po(K)_{nr}=0
.

The main goal of this paper is to prove the above three conjectures. For proving Conjectures
1.8
and
1.9
, we employ the Main Theorem (Theorem
2.10
) in which we give a general tool that facilitates investigating Pólya groups of non-Galois number fields.

Main Theorem (Informal Version).

Let
K
be a non-Galois number field of degree
n\geq 3
. Let
H_{0}=\Gal(N_{K}/K)
, where
N_{K}
denotes the Galois closure of
K
(over
\mathbb{Q}
). Then, there exists an explicit group homomorphism

(1.5)

\Phi:\Po(K)\rightarrow\frac{H_{0}}{\langle H_{0}^{\prime}\rangle},

with
\Ker(\Phi)\subseteq\Po(K)_{nr1}
, where
H_{0}^{\prime}
denotes the derived subgroup of
H
.

The homomorphism in
\Phi
(
1.5
) is the key tool in proving Conjectures
1.8
and
1.9
. More precisely, in Section
3
, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem

(
Theorem
3.10
)
.

Let
K
be a
S_{4}
-field. Then
\Po(K)=\Cl(K)
. In other words, Conjecture
1.8
is true.

As another application of Main Theorem, in Section
4
, we obtain the following result in which
\PSL(d,q)
denotes the group of
d\times d
matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of determinant
1
modulo scalars. Also,
\mathrm{P}\Sigma\mathrm{L}(d,q)
denotes the permutation group on the points of
\mathbb{P}^{d-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
generated by
\text{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
\PSL(d,q)
, where
\text{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
denotes the group of field automorphisms of
\mathbb{F}_{q}

[
17
, Section 7]
.

Theorem

(
Theorem
4.5
)
.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a non-Galois extension of prime degree
\ell\geq 5
. Denote by
N_{K}
the Galois closure of
K
(over
\mathbb{Q}
), and let
G_{0}=\Gal(N_{K}/\mathbb{Q})
be unsolvable. Then

\Po(K)_{nr1}=\Po(K)_{nr}=\Po(K)=\Cl(K),

unless

\PSL(2,q)\subseteq G_{0}\subseteq\mathrm{P}\Sigma\mathrm{L}(2,q),

for some prime power

. In this case,

\ell
is a Fermat prime,
\frac{\Cl(K)}{\Po(K)_{nr1}}
is a cyclic group whose order dividing
\ell-2
, and

(1.6)

\Po(K)_{nr1}=\Po(K)_{nr}=\Po(K).

In particular, for a
G_{0}
-field

with
G_{0}=A_{5}\simeq\PSL(2,4)
, the equalities (

) hold. Consequently, Conjecture
1.9
is true.

Finally, in Section
5
, using a different approach, namely using the decomposition form of primes in even dihedral number fields, we prove that Conjecture
1.10
holds more generally for all

-fields with
n\geq 4
an even integer.

Theorem

(
Theorem
5.2
)
.

Let
K
be a
D_{n}
-field, where
n\geq 4
is an even integer. If

, then
\Po(K)_{nr}=0
. In particular, Conjecture
1.10
is true.

2.
Image of Pólya group under the Artin isomorphism

Zantema
[
17
, Section 6]
gave a group theoretical interpretation of pre-Pólyaness condition for number fields
K
. Using this approach, Chabert and Halberstadt
[
4
, Proposition 2.3]
determined the Artin symbols corresponding to the Ostrowski ideals
\Pi_{p^{f}}(K)
above non-ramified primes
p
. In this section, we use Zantema’s approach
[
17
, Section 8]
, to describe the image of the Pólya group
\Po(K)
under the Artin isomorphism, in which we also consider the Ostrowski ideals above ramified primes. Then we present the main result of this paper, namely Theorem
2.10
, which relates
\Po(K)
to some quotient factor of the Galois group of the Galois closure of
K
(over
\mathbb{Q}
).

From now on, we fix the following notations:

N_{K}
: the Galois closure of
K
over
\mathbb{Q}
;

G_{0}=\Gal(N_{K}/\mathbb{Q})
;

H_{0}=\Gal(N_{K}/K)
;

H_{K}
: the Hilbert class field of
K
;

N_{H_{K}}
: the Galois closure of
H_{K}
over
\mathbb{Q}
;

G=\Gal(N_{H_{K}}/\mathbb{Q})
;

H=\Gal(N_{H_{K}}/K)
;

H_{1}=\Gal(N_{H_{K}}/H_{K})
;

H_{2}=\Gal(N_{H_{K}}/N_{K})
.

Remark 2.1
.

Note that
H_{2}=\cap_{g\in G}gH^{-1}g
,
G_{0}=G/H_{2}
, and
H_{0}=H/H_{2}
.

Let
p
be a prime number, and fix a prime ideal
\mathfrak{P}
of
N_{H}
above
p
. Let
Z(\mathfrak{P}/p)
and
T(\mathfrak{P}/p)
be the decomposition group and the inertia group of
N_{H}/\mathbb{Q}
at
\mathfrak{P}
, respectively. The group
G
acts on the set of
n
cosets
Hs
(
s\in G
) as

(Hs)^{g}:=Hsg^{-1},\quad\forall g\in G,

see
[
17
, Section 8]
. Under this action, let
g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{t}
be the lengths of the orbits
Z(\mathfrak{P}/p)
. Since
T(\mathfrak{P}/p)
is a normal subgroup of
Z(\mathfrak{P}/p)
, each orbit of
Z(\mathfrak{P}/p)
, say the
i
-th orbit of length
g_{i}
, splits into
f_{i}
orbits of
T(\mathfrak{P}/p)
of equal length, say
e_{i}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
17
, Section 8]

For a prime number
p
, keep the notations as above. For every
i=1,2,\dots,t
, choose
s_{i}\in G
such that
Hs_{i}
is in the
i
-th orbit of
Z(\mathfrak{P}/p)
. Then the following assertions hold:

(1)

We have

p\mathcal{O}_{K}=\prod_{i=1}^{t}(s_{i}(\mathfrak{P})\cap K)^{e_{i}}

is the decomposition form of
p
in
K/\mathbb{Q}
, where
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(s_{i}(\mathfrak{P})\cap K)=p^{f_{i}}
.

(2)

Let
\frac{Z(\mathfrak{P}/p}{T(\mathfrak{P}/p)}=\langle g_{\mathfrak{P}}\rangle
for some
g_{\mathfrak{P}}\in G
. Then for every
i=1,2,\dots,t
, there exists
a_{i}\in T(\mathfrak{P}/p)
such that

s_{i}g_{\mathfrak{P}}^{f_{i}}a_{i}s_{i}^{-1}\in H.

Moreover, for an integer
f\geq 1
, the ideal
\Pi_{p^{f}}(K)
is principal if and only if

\prod_{\{i|f_{i}=f\}}s_{i}g_{\mathfrak{P}}^{f_{i}}a_{i}s_{i}^{-1}\in H_{1}.

Remark 2.3
.

If
p
is unramified in
K/\mathbb{Q}
, then
T(\mathfrak{P}/p)
is trivial. In this case, Proposition
2.2
coincides with
[
4
, Proposition 2.3]
.

Using Proposition
2.2
and by an argument similar to
[
4
, Proof of Proposition 2.3]
, one can describe the Ostrowski ideals
\Pi_{p^{f}}(K)
in terms of the Artin symbols.

Corollary 2.4
.

With the notations of this section, for every prime number
p
and every integer
f\geq 1
, one has

\left(\frac{H_{K}/K}{\Pi_{p^{f}}(K)}\right)=\prod_{\{i|f_{i}=f\}}s_{i}g_{\mathfrak{P}}^{f_{i}}a_{i}s_{i}^{-1}|_{H_{K}}.

In other words, under the Artin isomorphism
\Cl(K)\mathrel{{\mathop{\simeq}\limits}}H/H_{1}
, for every prime number
p
and every integer
f\geq 1
, we have

(2.1)

\alpha_{K}\left([\Pi_{p^{f}}(K)]\right)=\prod_{\{i|f_{i}=f\}}s_{i}g_{\mathfrak{P}}^{f_{i}}a_{i}s_{i}^{-1}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,H_{1}).

Zantema
[
17
, Section 6]
showed that the Galois closures of “some” non-Galois pre-Pólya fields coincide with the Galois closures of their Hilbert class fields.

Theorem 2.5
.

[
17
, Theorem 6.4]

For a number field
K
of degree
n\geq 3
, let
G
,
H
and
H_{1}
be defined as in this section. Under the action of
G
on the set of
n
cosets
Hs
(
s\in G
), defined as
(Hs)^{g}:=Hsg^{-1}
, let

(2.2)

T=\{h\in H\,:\,\forall s\in G\backslash H,\,Hsh\neq Hs\}.

Assume that
T\neq\emptyset
and
K
is a pre-Pólya field. Then

T\subseteq H_{1}
, and the Hilbert class field of
K
is contained in the Galois closure of
K
, i.e.,

H_{K}\subseteq N_{K}
.

The above theorem, has been generalized by Chabert and Halberstadt (Recall that
\Po(K)_{nr1}
, as defined in (
1.4
), is the subgroup of
\Cl(K)
generated by all the classes of
\Pi_{p}(K)
above unramified primes
p
).

Proposition 2.6
.

[
4
, Section 3]

Let
K
be a number field of degree
n\geq 3
. With the notations of this section, the following assertions hold:

(1)

Under the Artin isomorphism
\Cl(K)\mathrel{{\mathop{\simeq}\limits}}H/H_{1}
, we have

\alpha_{K}^{-1}\left(T\,(\mathrm{mod}\,H_{1})\right)\subseteq\Po(K)_{nr1},

where
T
is the set defined in (
2.2
).
In particular, if
\Po(K)_{nr1}=0
, then
T\subseteq H_{1}
.

(2)

If
T\neq\emptyset
, then
H_{2}\subseteq\langle T\rangle
.

(3)

The factor group
\frac{\Cl(K)}{\Po(K)_{nr1}}
is isomorphic to a quotient group of
\frac{H}{\langle H_{1},T\rangle}
.

As we can see, the set “
T
” is a key object in Theorem
2.5
and Proposition
2.6
, which is related to the action of
G
on the cosets
Hs
.
However, Chabert and Halberstadt
[
4
]
showed that in some cases, one may consider the action of
G_{0}
on the cosets
H_{0}s
, which makes the situation easier to investigate
\Po(K)
.

Theorem 2.7
.

[
4
, Theorem 3.11 and Remark 3.12]

With the notations of this section, denote by
\Omega_{0}
the set formed by the right cosets of
H_{0}
in
G_{0}
. Define the action of
G_{0}
on
\Omega_{0}
as

(H_{0}s)^{g}:=H_{0}sg^{-1},\quad\forall g\in G_{0}.

Let
T_{0}
be the set formed by the elements of
H_{0}
, which have only
H_{0}
as a fixed point. Then

T_{0}\neq\emptyset\iff T\neq\emptyset,

where
T
is the set defined in (
2.2
). Moreover, if
T_{0}\neq\emptyset
, then

\frac{H}{\langle H_{1},T\rangle}
is a quotient group of
\frac{H_{0}}{\langle T_{0},H_{0}^{\prime}\rangle}
, where
H_{0}^{\prime}
denotes the derived subgroup of
H_{0}
. Consequently,
\frac{\Cl(K)}{\Po(K)_{nr1}}
is isomorphic to a quotient group of
\frac{H_{0}}{\langle T_{0},H_{0}^{\prime}\rangle}
.

There are some applications of the above theorem for number fields of varying degrees, along with several examples for small degrees
[
4
, Sections 4–6]
.

Example 2.8
.

[
4
, Section 6]

For an
A_{5}
-field
K
we have

G_{0}=A_{5},\quad H_{0}=A_{4},\quad H_{0}^{\prime}=\{1,(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)\},\quad T_{0}=H_{0}^{\prime}\backslash\{1\},

see
[
4
, proof of Proposition 6.8]
. By Theorem
2.7
,
|\frac{\Cl(K)}{\Po(K)_{nr1}}|
divides
3
.

Remark 2.9
.

On one hand, by Theorem
2.7
,
\frac{H}{\langle H_{1},T\rangle}
is a quotient group of
\frac{H_{0}}{\langle T_{0},H_{0}^{\prime}\rangle}
. On the other hand,
\frac{H_{0}}{\langle T_{0},H_{0}^{\prime}\rangle}
is an abelian group. Hence
\frac{H}{\langle H_{1},T\rangle}
would be isomorphic to a subgroup of
\frac{H_{0}}{\langle T_{0},H_{0}^{\prime}\rangle}
. Consequently, there exists an injection

(2.3)

\lambda:\frac{H}{\langle H_{1},T\rangle}\hookrightarrow\frac{H_{0}}{\langle T_{0},H_{0}^{\prime}\rangle}.

Inspired by the above results, we obtain the main theorem of this paper as follows.

Theorem 2.10

(
Main Theorem
)
.

Let
K
be a number field of degree
n\geq 3
. With the notations of this section, under the action of
G
(resp.
G_{0}
) on the right cosets of
H
in
G
(resp.
H_{0}
in
G_{0}
), let

(resp.

) be the set formed by the elements of

(resp.
H_{0}
) which have only

(resp.

) as fixed point. Consider the composite map

(2.4)

\Po(K)\xrightarrow{\alpha_{K}}\frac{H}{H_{1}}\xrightarrow{\theta}\frac{H}{\langle H_{1},T\rangle}\xrightarrow{\lambda}\frac{H_{0}}{\langle T_{0},H_{0}^{\prime}\rangle},

where
\alpha_{K}
denotes the Artin isomorphism,
\theta
is the canonical surjection, and
\lambda
is the embedding map (

). Define the map

(2.5)

\Phi:\Po(K)\rightarrow\frac{H_{0}}{\langle T_{0},H_{0}^{\prime}\rangle}

\Phi:=\lambda\circ\theta\circ\alpha_{K}.

Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

For a prime number
p
, fix a prime ideal
\mathfrak{P}
of

above

. Let

and
T(\mathfrak{P}/p)
be the decomposition group and the inertia group of

\mathfrak{P}
, respectively. Under the action of
G
on the right cosets of
H
in
G
, let
t
be the number of the orbits of
Z(\mathfrak{P}/p)
, and let the
i
-th orbit splits into

orbits of
T(\mathfrak{P}/p)
. For every

, choose
s_{i}\in G
such that
Hs_{i}
is in the
i
-th orbit of
Z(\mathfrak{P}/p)
, and pick

a_{i}\in T(\mathfrak{P}/p)
such that

s_{i}g_{\mathfrak{P}}^{f_{i}}a_{i}s_{i}^{-1}\in H.

Then

\left(s_{i}g_{\mathfrak{P}}^{f_{i}}a_{i}s_{i}^{-1}\right)|_{N_{K}}=\hat{s}_{i}g_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}^{f_{i}}\hat{a}_{i}\hat{s}_{i}^{-1}\in H_{0},\quad\text{for}\,\,i=1,\dots,t,

where
\hat{s}_{i}=s_{i}|_{N_{K}}
,
\hat{a}_{i}=a_{i}|_{N_{K}}
, and
g_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}
is the Frobenius element of
N_{K}/\mathbb{Q}
at
\mathfrak{P}_{0}:=\mathfrak{P}\cap N_{K}
.
Furthermore,

\Phi\left([\Pi_{p^{f}}(K)]\right)=\prod_{\{i|f_{i}=f\}}\hat{s}_{i}g_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}^{f_{i}}\hat{a}_{i}\hat{s}_{i}^{-1}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\langle T_{0},H_{0}^{\prime}\rangle),\quad\forall f\in\mathbb{N}.

(ii)

We have
\Ker(\Phi)\subseteq\Po(K)_{nr1}
.

Proof.

(1) Let
g_{\mathfrak{P}}\in G
be the generator of
\frac{Z(\mathfrak{P}/p)}{T(\mathfrak{P}/p)}
. Using part (2) of Proposition
2.2
, we have

(2.6)

\Phi\left([\Pi_{p^{f}}(K)]\right)=\lambda\left(\prod_{\{i|f_{i}=f\}}s_{i}g_{\mathfrak{P}}^{f_{i}}a_{i}s_{i}^{-1}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\langle H_{1},T\rangle)\right).

On the other hand, the injection map
\lambda
is the composite map

\frac{H}{\langle H_{1},T\rangle}\hookrightarrow\frac{H}{\langle T,V\rangle}\simeq\frac{H/H_{2}}{\langle T,V\rangle/H_{2}}\simeq\frac{H_{0}}{\langle T_{0},V_{0}\rangle}\hookrightarrow\frac{H_{0}}{\langle T_{0},H_{0}^{\prime}\rangle},

where
V
(resp.
V_{0}
) denotes the image of the
transfer map

\Ver_{G\rightarrow H}
(resp.
\Ver_{G_{0}\rightarrow H_{0}}
), see
[
4
, Proof of Theorem 3.11]
. Since
H_{2}=\Gal(N_{H}/N_{K})
, considering the class of an element
s\in H
modulo
H_{2}
is equivalent to considering the restriction of
s
to
N_{K}
. In particular,

\left(s_{i}g_{\mathfrak{P}}^{f_{i}}a_{i}s_{i}^{-1}\right)|_{N_{K}}\in H/H_{2}=H_{0},\quad\text{for}\,\,i=1,\dots,t,

and (
2.6
) is equivalent to

\prod_{\{i|f_{i}=f\}}\left(s_{i}g_{\mathfrak{P}}^{f_{i}}a_{i}s_{i}^{-1}\right)|_{N_{K}}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\langle T_{0},H_{0}^{\prime}\rangle).

Finally, since
g_{\mathfrak{P}}
is the generator of
\frac{Z(\mathfrak{P}/p)}{T(\mathfrak{P}/p)}
, we have

g_{\mathfrak{P}}(x)\equiv x^{p}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathfrak{P}),\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{O}_{N_{H}}.

Thus for
\mathfrak{P}_{0}=\mathfrak{P}\cap N_{K}
, one has

g_{\mathfrak{P}}(x)\equiv x^{p}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathfrak{P}_{0}),\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{O}_{N_{K}},

which implies that
g_{\mathfrak{P}}|_{N_{K}}
is the Frobenius element of
N_{K}/\mathbb{Q}
at
\mathfrak{P}_{0}
.

(2) Let
\left[\Pi_{p^{f}}(K)\right]\in\Ker(\Phi)
. Since
\lambda
is injective, we obtain

\left[\Pi_{p^{f}}(K)\right]\in\Ker\left(\theta\circ\alpha_{K}\right).

Thereby,

\alpha_{K}\left([\Pi_{p^{f}}(K)]\right)\in\langle T\,(\mathrm{mod}\,H_{1})\rangle.

By part (1) of Proposition
2.6
, we get

[\Pi_{p^{f}}(K)]\in\alpha_{K}^{-1}\left(\langle T\,(\mathrm{mod}\,H_{1})\rangle\right)\subseteq\Po(K)_{nr1}.

∎

3.
Application of the Main Theorem for
S_{4}
-fields

As mentioned in Section
1
, Chabert and Halberstadt
[
4
, Section 6]
conjectured that for a
S_{4}
-field
K
, we have
\Po(K)=\Cl(K)
, see Conjecture
1.8
.
In this section, we show that this conjecture is true. To accomplish this, in addition to Theorem
2.10
, we need several lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
K
be a number field of degree
n\geq 3
. Let
M_{1}/K
be the sub-extension of
H_{K}/K
corresponding to the subgroup
\Po(K)_{nr1}
, obtained via the Galois correspondence and the Artin isomorphism
\Cl(K)\mathrel{{\mathop{\simeq}\limits}}\Gal(H_{K}/K)
. If the set
T_{0}
, as defined in Theorem
2.7
, is non-empty, then

[M_{1}:K]\,|\,\#\frac{H_{0}}{\langle H_{0}^{\prime},T_{0}\rangle}.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.7
, the order of
\frac{\Cl(K)}{\Po(K)_{nr1}}
is a divisor of the order of
\frac{H_{0}}{\langle H_{0}^{\prime},T_{0}\rangle}
. Now, the assertion follows from the isomorphisms below

\frac{\Cl(K)}{\Po(K)_{nr1}}\simeq\frac{\Gal(H_{K}/K)}{\Gal(H_{K}/M_{1})}\simeq\Gal(M_{1}/K).

∎

Remark 3.2
.

Note that by the Galois correspondence,
\Po(K)_{nr1}=0
if and only if
M_{1}=H_{K}
. Also, since
M_{1}\subseteq H_{K}
,
M_{1}/K
is an unramified abelian extension.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
K
be a
S_{4}
-field. Then
\Ker(\Phi)=\Po(K)_{nr1}
, where

\Phi:\Po(K)\rightarrow\frac{H_{0}}{\langle H_{0}^{\prime},T_{0}\rangle}

is the homomorphism map defined in Theorem
2.10
.

Proof.

Let
p
be a prime which is not ramified in
K/\mathbb{Q}
. By Theorem
2.10
we have

\Phi\left([\Pi_{p^{1}}(K)]\right)=\prod_{\{i|f_{i}=1\}}\hat{s}_{i}g_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}\hat{s}_{i}^{-1}\in H_{0},

where
g_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}
is the Frobenius element of
N_{K}/K
at some prime
\mathfrak{P}_{0}
above
p
, and
\hat{s}_{i}=s_{i}\mid_{N_{K}}\in G_{0}
, for all
i
(The elements
s_{i}
’s and the integers
f_{i}
’s are defined in Theorem
2.10
). Following Chabert and Halberstadt’s method
[
4
, Section 6]
, since
\Gal(N_{K}/K)=H_{0}\simeq S_{3}
, it is enough to show that

\prod_{\{i|f_{i}=1\}}\hat{s}_{i}g_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}\hat{s}_{i}^{-1}\in H_{0}^{\prime}\simeq A_{3},

for
g_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}=(1\,2)
and
g_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}=(1\,2\,3)
. As suggested in
[
4
, Proof of Proposition 6.1]
, for
g_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}=(1\,2)
, we can take
f_{1}=f_{2}=1
,
s_{1}=1
, and
s_{2}=(3\,4)
. Then, with the notations of Theorem
2.10
, we have

\Phi\left([\Pi_{p^{1}}(K)]\right)=\lambda\circ\theta\circ\alpha_{K}(\Pi_{p^{1}}(K))=\lambda\circ\theta(\prod_{i=1}^{2}s_{i}g_{\mathfrak{P}}s_{i}^{-1})=\lambda\circ\theta\left(\underbrace{\left((1\,2)(3\,4)\right)^{2}}_{1}\right)=1.

For
g_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}=(1\,2\,3)
, we can take
f_{1}=1
, and
s_{1}=1
. Then

\Phi\left([\Pi_{p^{1}}(K)]\right)=\prod_{\{i|f_{i}=1\}}\hat{s}_{i}g_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}\hat{s}_{i}^{-1}=\hat{s}_{1}g_{\mathfrak{P}}\mid_{N_{K}}\hat{s}_{1}^{-1}=g_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}=(1\,2\,3)\in H_{0}^{\prime}.

Hence
\Po(K)_{nr1}\subseteq\Ker(\Phi)
. The opposite inclusion is part (ii) of Theorem
2.10
.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
K
be a
S_{4}
-field. Then
\alpha_{K}\left(\Po(K)_{nr1}\right)=\langle T\,(\mathrm{mod}\,H_{1})\rangle
.

Proof.

As we have seen in the proof of Theorem
2.10
, we have

\Ker(\Phi)\subseteq\alpha_{K}^{-1}\left(\langle T\,(\mathrm{mod}\,H_{1})\rangle\right)\subseteq\Po(K)_{nr1}.

Now, the assertion follows from Lemma
3.3
.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
K
be a
S_{4}
-field. Let
M_{1}/K
be the subextension of
H_{K}/K
corresponding to the subgroup
\Po(K)_{nr1}
. Then
for every
f\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\theta\circ\alpha_{K}\left(\left[\Pi_{p^{f}}(K)\right]\right)=\left(\frac{M_{1}/K}{\Pi_{p^{f}}(K)}\right)

where

\alpha_{K}:\Cl(K)\rightarrow\Gal(H_{K}/K)=\frac{H}{H_{1}}

denotes the Artin isomorphism, and

\theta:\frac{H}{H_{1}}\rightarrow\frac{H}{\langle T,H_{1}\rangle}

is the canonical surjection. Also,

\left(\frac{M_{1}/K}{\Pi_{p^{f}}(K)}\right)
denotes the Artin symbol of
M_{1}/K
at
\Pi_{p^{f}}(K)
.

Proof.

On one hand, by Corollary
2.4
, we have

\theta\left(\alpha_{K}\left([\Pi_{p^{f}}(K)]\right)\right)=\theta\left(\prod_{\{i|f_{i}=f\}}s_{i}g_{\mathfrak{P}}^{f_{i}}a_{i}s_{i}^{-1}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,H_{1})\right)=\prod_{\{i|f_{i}=f\}}s_{i}g_{\mathfrak{P}}^{f_{i}}a_{i}s_{i}^{-1}\,\left(\mathrm{mod}\,\langle T,H_{1}\rangle\right).

On the other hand, since
M_{1}\subseteq H_{K}
, by
[
5
, Proposition 1.6]
, we get

\left(\frac{M_{1}/K}{\Pi_{p^{f}}(K)}\right)=\left(\frac{H_{K}/K}{\Pi_{p^{f}}(K)}\right)|_{M_{1}}=\prod_{\{i|f_{i}=f\}}s_{i}g_{\mathfrak{P}}^{f_{i}}a_{i}s_{i}^{-1}|_{M_{1}}=\prod_{\{i|f_{i}=f\}}s_{i}g_{\mathfrak{P}}^{f_{i}}a_{i}s_{i}^{-1}\,\left(\mathrm{mod}\,\langle T,H_{1}\rangle\right).

Note that using Corollary
3.4
, the latter equality follows from

\Gal(M_{1}/K)=\frac{\Gal(H_{K}/K)}{\Gal(H_{K}/M_{1})}\simeq\frac{\alpha_{K}(\Cl(K))}{\alpha_{K}(\Po(K)_{nr1})}=\frac{H/H_{1}}{\langle T\,(\mathrm{mod}\,H_{1})\rangle}=\frac{H}{\langle T,H_{1}\rangle}.

∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
K
be a
S_{4}
-field. Let
M_{1}/K
be the sub-extension of
H_{K}/K
corresponding to the subgroup
\Po(K)_{nr1}
, obtained via the Galois correspondence and the Artin isomorphism
\Cl(K)\mathrel{{\mathop{\simeq}\limits}}\Gal(H_{K}/K)
. Then either
M_{1}=K
or
M_{1}=K(\sqrt{\disc(K)})
.

Proof.

It has been proved in
[
4
, Proposition 6.1]
that
\#\frac{H_{0}}{\langle H_{0}^{\prime},T_{0}\rangle}=2
. Hence by Lemma
3.1
,
[M_{1}:K]
divides
2
. Let
[M_{1}:K]=2
. Since
G_{0}=\Gal(N_{K}/\mathbb{Q})\simeq S_{4}
, we have

•

\sqrt{\disc(K)}\in N_{K}\backslash K
;

•

H_{0}=\Gal(N_{K}/K)\simeq S_{3}
;

•

T_{0}=\{(1\,2\,3),(1\,3\,2)\}\subseteq H_{0}^{\prime}\simeq A_{3}
,

see
[
4
, Proof of Proposition 6.1]
. Let
F=K(\sqrt{\disc(K)})
. Then
F/K
is the unique quadratic subextension of
N_{K}/K
, which is the fixed subfield of
N_{K}
by
H_{0}^{\prime}
. Since, by Lemma
3.3
,
\Ker(\Phi)=\Po(K)_{nr1}
, we have

\Phi(\Po(K)_{nr1})\subseteq\langle H_{0}^{\prime},T_{0}\rangle=H_{0}^{\prime}.

Hence
M_{1}
(corresponding to
\Po(K)_{nr1}
) would be isomorphic to a subfield of
F
. But
[M_{1}:K]=[F:K]=2
, which implies that

M_{1}\simeq F=K(\sqrt{\disc(K)}).

∎

Remark 3.7
.

Lemma
3.6
is a generalization of Chabert and Halberstadt’s result
[
4
, Proposition 6.1]
in which they proved that
\Po(K)_{nr}=0
if and only if either we have
H_{K}=K
or
H_{K}=K(\sqrt{\disc(K)})
. Because, as mentioned in Remark
3.2
,
\Po(K)_{nr}=0
if and only if
M_{1}=H_{K}
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
K
be a
S_{4}
field, and
N_{K}
be its Galois closure over
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
M_{1}/K
be the subextension of
H_{K}/K
corresponding to the subgroup
\Po(K)_{nr1}
. If
M_{1}=K(\sqrt{\disc(K)})
, then
N_{K}/K
is unramified at all finite primes.

Proof.

From the proof of Lemma
3.6
, we have

\Gal(N_{K})/K\simeq S_{3},

and
M_{1}/K=K(\sqrt{\disc(K)})/K
is the unique quadratic subextension of
N_{K}/K
. Let
L/K
be a cubic subextension of
N_{K}/K
. Then,
L/K
is non-Galois, so
\disc(L/K)
is not square of any ideal of
K
. More precisely, the discriminant of
L/K
can be written as

(3.1)

\disc(L/K)=\disc(M_{1}/K).f^{2},

where
f
is the conductor of
N_{K}
over
M_{1}
, see
[
6
, Proposition 10.1.28]
. Since
M_{1}/K
is unramified, see Remark
3.2
,
\disc(M_{1}/K)
is a unit in
K
. Because
\disc(L/K)
can not be a perfect square, by (
3.1
), the only possible case is that
f
be also unit. Hence,
L/K
is unramified at all finite primes of
K
. Since
N_{K}
is the Galois closure of
L
over
K
,
N_{K}/K
is also unramified at all finite primes, as claimed.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

[

Let F be a number field and

be its Galois closure over
\mathbb{Q}
. Let

We are ready to prove Conjecture

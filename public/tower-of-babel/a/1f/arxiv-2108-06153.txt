On Lipschitz regularity for bounded minimizers of functionals with ( p , q ) \displaystyle(p,q) -growth
Karthik Adimurthi, Vivek Tewary

Abstract
We obtain Lipschitz estimates for bounded minimizers of functionals with nonstandard ( p , q ) \displaystyle(p,q) -growth satisfying the dimension-independent restriction q < p + 2 \displaystyle q<p+2 with p ≥ 2 \displaystyle p\geq 2 . This relation improves existing restrictions when p ≤ N − 1 \displaystyle p\leq N-1 , moreover our result is sharp in the range N > p ⁡ ( 2 + p ) 2 + 1 \displaystyle N>\frac{p(2+p)}{2}+1 . The standard Lipschitz regularity takes the form W loc 1 , ∞ − W loc 1 , p \displaystyle W^{1,\infty}_{\loc}-W^{1,p}_{\loc} , whereas we obtain W loc 1 , ∞ − L loc ∞ \displaystyle W^{1,\infty}_{\loc}-L^{\infty}_{\loc} regularity estimate and then make use of existing sharp L loc ∞ \displaystyle L^{\infty}_{\loc} bounds to obtain the required conclusion.

Since we require bounded solutions, we see that for minimizers, Lipschitz regularity would then require
\displaystyle\frac{q}{p}<1+\min\left\{\frac{p}{N-1-p},\frac{2}{p}\right\}
. In particular, if
\displaystyle N>\frac{p(p+2)}{2}+1
, then
\displaystyle\frac{2}{p}>\frac{p}{N-1-p}
and thus Lipschitz regularity holds for any minimizer as they are automatically bounded. In particular, due to the sharpness of the condition
Eq.

1.4
, we automatically obtain sharpness of the Lipschitz regularity in this range.

\displaystyle q<p+1

We now briefly describe the method of proof, first, we begin with a regularization procedure following
[
5
]
where a quadratic term is added to
\displaystyle f
. The regularized solution is shown to be in
\displaystyle C^{1,\gamma}\cap W^{2,2}

\displaystyle W^{2,2}

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle\nabla U\in L^{s}_{\loc}

\displaystyle s\in(1,\infty)

\displaystyle\|\nabla U\|_{s}

\displaystyle\|U\|_{L^{\infty}}

Remark 1.4
.

After this paper was written, we became aware that this result has been proved previously by Bildhauer and Fuchs
[
4

in 2002. The methods are, for the most part, similar. Instead of De Giorgi iteration, we use a Moser iteration in the last step. Also, we use a quadratic term for the regularization and we use the notion of bounded slope condition to gain Lipschitz regularity for the regularized minimizer.

Preliminaries for Regularization

We list some of the preliminaries that are required in the subsequent sections. The regularization procedure relies on the addition of a quadratic term to the functional. Stampacchia
[

has proved some general theorems on the local Lipschitz regularity of minimizers to convex minimization problems posed on convex domains with boundary values satisfying the

Definition 2.1

(Uniformly Convex set)
.

A bounded, open set
\displaystyle B\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is said to be uniformly convex if there exists
\displaystyle\nu>0
such that for every boundary point
\displaystyle x_{0}\in\partial B
there exists a hyperplane
\displaystyle H_{x_{0}}
passing through that point satisfying

\displaystyle\displaystyle\dist(y,H_{x_{0}})\geq\nu|y-x_{0}|^{2}\quad\mbox{ for every $\displaystyle y\in\partial B$.}

Definition 2.2

(Bounded Slope Condition)
.

Let
\displaystyle K
be a positive real number and
\displaystyle B
an open bounded convex subset of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We say that a function
\displaystyle\phi:\partial B\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the bounded slope condition of rank
\displaystyle K
if for any
\displaystyle x_{0}\in\partial B
there exists vectors
\displaystyle l_{x_{0}}^{-}
and
\displaystyle l_{x_{0}}^{+}
satisfying
\displaystyle||l_{x_{0}}^{-}||\leq K
and
\displaystyle||l_{x_{0}}^{+}||\leq K
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\langle l_{x_{0}}^{-},x-x_{0}\rangle\leq\phi(x)-\phi(x_{0})\leq\langle l_{x_{0}}^{+},x-x_{0}\rangle\quad\mbox{ holds for every $\displaystyle x\in\partial B$.}

The following proposition gives a necessary and sufficient condition for a function to satisfy the bounded slope condition, see
[
23

and
[
34
]
for the details of the proof.

Proposition 2.3

Let
\displaystyle B\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}

\displaystyle\partial B\in C^{1,1}

\displaystyle\phi(x)

\displaystyle x\in\partial B

4

We shall prove the following Caccioppoli inequality for the gradient of
\displaystyle u
. Note that the proof is only formal and everything can be made rigorous using difference quotients and the a priori regularity from

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\alpha\geq 0
. Let
\displaystyle u
be the solution to
Eq.

3.3
. Then it holds that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{B}|\nabla u|^{p-2+\alpha}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}\eta^{2}\,dx\leq C(M,m)\biggr\{\int_{B}\left(|\nabla u|^{q+\alpha}+|\nabla u|^{2+\alpha}\right)|\nabla\eta|^{2}\,dx\biggr\}.

(4.1)

Proof.

The minimizer
\displaystyle u
of
Eq.

3.3
satisfies the following Euler-Lagrange equation:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{B}\langle Df_{\sigma}(\nabla u),\nabla\phi\rangle\,dx=0.

By choosing
\displaystyle\phi=\psi_{x_{j}}\in H^{1}_{0}(B)
and integrating by parts, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{B}\langle D^{2}f_{\sigma}(\nabla u)\nabla u_{x_{j}},\nabla\psi\rangle\,dx=0.

Now, for
\displaystyle\kappa>0
, we choose
\displaystyle\psi=u_{x_{j}}(\kappa+|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\eta^{2}
, where
\displaystyle\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B)
such that
\displaystyle 0\leq\eta\leq 1
in
\displaystyle B
, to get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\underbrace{\int_{B}\left\langle D^{2}f_{\sigma}(\nabla u)\nabla u_{x_{j}},\nabla u_{x_{j}}\right\rangle(\kappa+|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\eta^{2}\,dx}_{I}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\underbrace{\int_{B}\left\langle D^{2}f_{\sigma}(\nabla u)\nabla u_{x_{j}},\nabla\left((\kappa+|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)\right\rangle u_{x_{j}}\eta^{2}\,dx}_{II}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\underbrace{-2\int_{B}\left\langle D^{2}f_{\sigma}(\nabla u)\nabla u_{x_{j}},\nabla\eta\right\rangle u_{x_{j}}\eta(\kappa+|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\,dx}_{III}.

(4.2)

Now observe that due to the coercivity of
\displaystyle D^{2}f_{\sigma}(z)
, we can apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for positive definite Hermitian matrix
\displaystyle O
given by
\displaystyle\langle Ox,y\rangle\leq\langle Ox,x\rangle^{1/2}\langle Oy,y\rangle^{1/2}
along with Young’s inequality to get

\displaystyle\displaystyle III\leq\frac{1}{2}I+2\int_{B}\langle D^{2}f_{\sigma}(\nabla u)\nabla\eta,\nabla\eta\rangle u_{x_{j}}^{2}(\kappa+|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\,dx.

(4.3)

Substituting
Eq.

4.3
in
Section

4
and summing over
\displaystyle j\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{B}\left\langle D^{2}f_{\sigma}(\nabla u)\nabla u_{x_{j}},\nabla u_{x_{j}}\right\rangle(\kappa+|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\eta^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{B}\left\langle D^{2}f_{\sigma}(\nabla u)\nabla u_{x_{j}},\nabla\left((\kappa+|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)\right\rangle u_{x_{j}}\eta^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{B}\left\langle D^{2}f_{\sigma}(\nabla u)\nabla\eta,\nabla\eta\right\rangle u_{x_{j}}^{2}(\kappa+|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\,dx.

Observing that
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}u_{x_{j}}\nabla u_{x_{j}}=(\kappa+|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\left((\kappa+|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)
, we can rewrite the previous estimate as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{B}\big\langle D^{2}f_{\sigma}

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\nabla u)\nabla u_{x_{j}},\nabla u_{x_{j}}\big\rangle(\kappa+|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\eta^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\alpha\int_{B}\left\langle D^{2}f_{\sigma}(\nabla u)\nabla\left((\kappa+|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right),\nabla\left((\kappa+|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right\rangle(\kappa+|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\eta^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{B}\left\langle D^{2}f_{\sigma}(\nabla u)\nabla\eta,\nabla\eta\right\rangle u_{x_{j}}^{2}(\kappa+|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\,dx.

Now, we shall apply
Eq.

3.2c
to get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{m}{2}\int_{B}|\nabla u|^{p-2}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}(\kappa+|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\eta^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\alpha m\int_{B}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\left|\nabla\left((\kappa+|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right|^{2}(\kappa+|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\eta^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq 2(M+1)\int_{B}\left(|\nabla u|^{q}+|\nabla u|^{2}\right)(\kappa+|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\,dx.

We can derive the required inequality by dropping the second term on the left hand side and passing to the limit
\displaystyle\kappa\to 0
by an application of Dominated Convergence Theorem on the right hand side and Fatou’s lemma on the left hand side.
∎

5
Higher Integrability of gradient

In this section, we will prove that
\displaystyle\nabla u\in L^{s}_{\loc}(B)
for all
\displaystyle s\in(1,\infty)
provided
\displaystyle q<p+2
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume that
\displaystyle q<p+2
and let
\displaystyle u
be the unique solution of
Eq.

3.3
(recall
Remark

3.2
). For every
\displaystyle\beta\geq 2
, there exists a constant
\displaystyle C=C(N,p,q,\beta,M,m)>0
such that for every
\displaystyle 0<\sigma<1
,
\displaystyle 0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
and every pair of concentric balls
\displaystyle B_{r_{0}}\Subset B_{R_{0}}\Subset B
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{0}}}|\nabla u|^{p+\beta}\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle CR_{0}^{N}\left[\left(\frac{||U||_{L^{\infty}(2B_{R_{0}})}}{R_{0}-r_{0}}\right)^{\frac{2(\beta+p)}{p}}+\left(\frac{||U||_{L^{\infty}(2B_{R_{0}})}}{R_{0}-r_{0}}\right)^{\frac{2(\beta+p)}{2-q+p}}+\left(\frac{||U||_{L^{\infty}(2B_{R_{0}})}}{R_{0}-r_{0}}\right)^{p+\beta}\right].

(5.1)

Proof.

We begin with the integral

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{B}|\nabla u|^{p+\beta}\eta^{2}\,dx=\int_{B}\nabla u\cdot(|\nabla u|^{p+\beta-2}\,\nabla u\,\eta^{2})\,dx,

where
\displaystyle\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B)
to be eventually chosen appropriately.
After integrating by parts, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{B}|\nabla u|^{p+\beta}\eta^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\int_{B}u\nabla\cdot(|\nabla u|^{p+\beta-2}\,\nabla u\,\eta^{2})\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq||u||_{L^{\infty}(B)}(p+\beta-1)\underbrace{\int_{B}|\nabla u|^{p+\beta-2}|\nabla^{2}u|\eta^{2}\,dx}_{A_{1}}+2||u||_{L^{\infty}(B)}\underbrace{\int_{B}|\nabla u|^{p+\beta-1}|\eta||\nabla\eta|\,dx}_{A_{2}}.

Applying Young’s inequality, we get

\begin{array}[]{rcl}A_{1}&\leq&\tau\int_{B}|\nabla u|^{p+\beta}\eta^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{4\tau}\int_{B}|\nabla u|^{p+\beta-4}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}\eta^{2}\,dx,\\
A_{2}&\leq&\tau\int_{B}|\nabla u|^{p+\beta}\eta^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{4\tau}\int_{B}|\nabla u|^{p+\beta-2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\,dx.\end{array}

Choosing
\displaystyle\tau=\frac{1}{4||u||_{L^{\infty}(B)}(p+\beta-1)}
, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{B}|\nabla u|^{p+\beta}\eta^{2}\,dx\leq C(p,\beta)||u||^{2}_{L^{\infty}(B)}\left\{\int_{B}|\nabla u|^{p+\beta-4}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}\eta^{2}\,dx+\int_{B}|\nabla u|^{p+\beta-2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\,dx\right\}.

For the first integral on the RHS, we will apply
Proposition

4.1
to get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{B}|\nabla u|^{p+\beta}\eta^{2}\,dx\leq C(p,\beta,M,m)||u||^{2}_{L^{\infty}(B)}\left\{\int_{B}\left(|\nabla u|^{q+\beta-2}+|\nabla u|^{\beta}+|\nabla u|^{p+\beta-2}\right)|\nabla\eta|^{2}\,dx\right\}.

Let us now fix a pair of concentric balls
\displaystyle B_{r}\subset B_{R}\subset B
and choose the cut-off function
\displaystyle\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{R})
such that
\displaystyle\eta\equiv 1
on
\displaystyle B_{r}
and
\displaystyle|\nabla\eta|\leq\frac{C}{R-r}
, to get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla u|^{p+\beta}\,dx\leq C(p,\beta,M,m)\frac{||u||^{2}_{L^{\infty}(B)}}{(R-r)^{2}}\left\{\int_{B_{R}}\left(|\nabla u|^{q+\beta-2}+|\nabla u|^{\beta}+|\nabla u|^{p+\beta-2}\right)\,dx\right\}.

(5.2)

Now, for
\displaystyle\tau>0
to be chosen, we estimate each of the terms on the right hand side of
Eq.

5.2
as follows:

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\nabla u|^{p+\beta-2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\tau|\nabla u|^{p+\beta}+\frac{C(p,\beta)}{\tau^{\frac{p+\beta-2}{2}}},

(5.3a)

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\nabla u|^{\beta}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\tau|\nabla u|^{p+\beta}+\frac{C(\beta)}{\tau^{\frac{\beta}{p}}},

(5.3b)

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\nabla u|^{q+\beta-2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=|\nabla u|^{q+\beta-2}\frac{\tau^{\frac{q+\beta-2}{p+\beta}}}{\tau^{\frac{q+\beta-2}{p+\beta}}}\leq\tau|\nabla u|^{p+\beta}+\frac{C(p,q,\beta)}{\tau^{\frac{q+\beta-2}{2-q+p}}}.

(5.3c)

For the last inequality
Eq.

5.3c
, we apply Young’s inequality with exponents
\displaystyle\frac{p+\beta}{q+\beta-2}
and
\displaystyle\frac{p+\beta}{2+p-q}
, which requires the condition
\displaystyle q<p+2
. Substituting
Eqs.

5.3a
,
5.3b
and
5.3c
in
Eq.

5.2
, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla u|^{p+\beta}\,dx\leq\frac{3C||u||^{2}_{L^{\infty}(B)}}{(R-r)^{2}}\tau\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{p+\beta}\,dx+\frac{C||u||^{2}_{L^{\infty}(B)}}{(R-r)^{2}}\left\{\left[\frac{1}{\tau^{\frac{p+\beta-2}{2}}}+\frac{1}{\tau^{\frac{\beta}{p}}}+\frac{1}{\tau^{\frac{q+\beta-2}{2-q+p}}}\right]R^{N}\right\},

where
\displaystyle C=C(p,q,M,m,\beta)
. Now, we choose
\displaystyle\tau=\frac{(R-r)^{2}}{6C||u||_{L^{\infty}(B)}^{2}}
to get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla u|^{p+\beta}\,dx\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{p+\beta}\,dx+CR^{N}\left[\left(\frac{||u||_{L^{\infty}(B)}}{R-r}\right)^{p+\beta}+\left(\frac{||u||_{L^{\infty}(B)}}{R-r}\right)^{\frac{2(\beta+p)}{p+2-q}}+\left(\frac{||u||_{L^{\infty}(B)}}{R-r}\right)^{\frac{2(\beta+p)}{p}}\right].

Now, we fix
\displaystyle r_{0}<R_{0}
, then by the iteration
Lemma

2.9
for
\displaystyle r_{0}\leq r<R\leq R_{0}
and the maximum principle in
Lemma

3.1
, we obtain
Eq.

5.1
.
∎

6
Moser’s Iteration

Now, we are in a position to use Moser’s iteration for an unbalanced Caccioppoli inequality as in
Eq.

4.1
. The difference from a standard Moser’s iteration is that the starting point of our iteration must require an exponent of
\displaystyle|\nabla u|
higher than
\displaystyle p
. In order to do this, we follow the same scheme as laid out in
[
6
]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Assume that
\displaystyle q<p+2
and let
\displaystyle u
be the solution to
Eq.

3.3
. Let
\displaystyle R_{0}>0
be such that
\displaystyle B_{2R_{0}}\Subset B
and we fix the following two exponents:

2^{*}:=\begin{cases}\frac{2N}{N-2},&N>2\\
(2,\infty),&N=2.\end{cases}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\alpha_{0}:=\max\left\{\frac{2q-{2^{*}}p}{{2^{*}}-2},2\right\}.

Then, there exists
\displaystyle C=C(p,q,N,M,m,R_{0},||U||_{L^{\infty}(2B)})
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle||\nabla u||_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\frac{R_{0}}{2}}\right)}\leq C.

(6.1)

Proof.

We restate the Caccioppoli inequality from
Proposition

4.1
as:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{B}\left|\nabla\left(|\nabla u|^{\frac{p+\alpha}{2}}\right)\right|^{2}\eta^{2}\,dx\leq C(M,m)(p+\alpha)^{2}\left\{\int_{B}\left(|\nabla u|^{q+\alpha}+|\nabla u|^{2+\alpha}\right)|\nabla\eta|^{2}\,dx\right\},

which may be further revised to

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{B}\left|\nabla\left(|\nabla u|^{\frac{p+\alpha}{2}}\eta\right)\right|^{2}\,dx\leq C(M,m)(p+\alpha)^{2}\left\{\int_{B}\left(|\nabla u|^{q+\alpha}+1\right)|\nabla\eta|^{2}\,dx\right\}.

By Sobolev’s embedding, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\int_{B}|\nabla u|^{\frac{(p+\alpha){2^{*}}}{2}}\eta^{{2^{*}}}\,dx\right)^{{2}/{{2^{*}}}}\leq C(M,m)(p+\alpha)^{2}\left\{\int_{B}\left(|\nabla u|^{q+\alpha}+1\right)|\nabla\eta|^{2}\,dx\right\}.

Finally, for two concentric balls
\displaystyle B_{\rho}\subset B_{R}
and
\displaystyle\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{R})
satisfying
\displaystyle\eta\equiv 1
in
\displaystyle B_{\rho}
and
\displaystyle|\nabla\eta|\leq\frac{C}{R-r}
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{\rho}}|\nabla u|^{\frac{(p+\alpha){2^{*}}}{2}}\,dx\right)^{{2}/{{2^{*}}}}\leq\frac{C(M,m)(p+\alpha)^{2}}{(R-\rho)^{2}}\left\{\int_{B_{R}}\left(|\nabla u|^{q+\alpha}+1\right)\,dx\right\}.

(6.2)

Now, for
\displaystyle n=1,2,3,\ldots
, we define
\displaystyle\rho_{n}:=\frac{R_{0}}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{2^{n}}\right)
and choose
\displaystyle\alpha_{n}
to satisfy
\displaystyle(p+\alpha_{n})\frac{2^{*}}{2}=\alpha_{n+1}+q
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\alpha_{n}=\left(\frac{{2^{*}}}{2}\right)^{n}\alpha_{0}+\left(\frac{{2^{*}}p}{2}-q\right)\frac{\left(\frac{{2^{*}}}{2}\right)^{n}-1}{\frac{{2^{*}}}{2}-1}.

(6.3)

From
Eq.

6.3
, it is easy to see that if
\displaystyle\alpha_{0}+\frac{{2^{*}}p-2q}{{2^{*}}-2}>0
, then
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\alpha_{n}=\infty
. Thus, we can rewrite
Eq.

6.2
as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho_{n+1}}}|\nabla u|^{\alpha_{n+1}+q}\,dx\leq C^{n}\left\{\int_{B_{\rho_{n}}}|\nabla u|^{q+\alpha_{n}}\,dx+1\right\}^{{2^{*}}/2},

(6.4)

where
\displaystyle C=C(R_{0},\alpha_{0},N,M,m,p,q)
is independent of
\displaystyle n
. Defining
\displaystyle Y_{n}:=\int_{B_{\rho_{n}}}|\nabla u|^{\alpha_{n}+q}\,dx
, estimate
Eq.

6.4
becomes

\displaystyle\displaystyle Y_{n+1}\leq C^{n}(Y_{n}+1)^{\frac{{2^{*}}}{2\hfill}}.

(6.5)

By iterating
Eq.

6.5
, we get

\begin{array}[]{rcl}Y_{n+1}&\leq&C^{n}\left[Y_{n}+1\right]^{{2^{*}}/2}\\
&\leq&C^{n}\left\{C^{n-1}\left[Y_{n-1}+1\right]^{{2^{*}}/2}+1\right\}^{{2^{*}}/2}\\
&\leq&C^{n+(n-1)\frac{{2^{*}}}{2}}2^{\frac{2^{*}}{2}}\left[Y_{n-1}+1\right]^{\left({{2^{*}}}/{2}\right)^{2}}\\
&\vdots&\\
&\leq&C^{\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left({{2^{*}}}/{2}\right)^{j}(n-j)}2^{\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left({{2^{*}}}/{2}\right)^{j+1}}\left[Y_{0}+1\right]^{\left({{2^{*}}}/{2}\right)^{n+1}}.\end{array}

As a result, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle Y_{n}^{\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}+q}}\leq\left(C^{\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left(\frac{{2^{*}}}{2}\right)^{j}(n-j)}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}+q}}\left(2^{\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left(\frac{{2^{*}}}{2}\right)^{j+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}+q}}\left[Y_{0}+1\right]^{\frac{{(2^{*})}^{n}}{2^{n}(\alpha_{n}+q)}}.

(6.6)

We make the following three observations:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\frac{\left(\frac{{2^{*}}}{2}\right)^{n}}{\alpha_{n}+q}&=&\frac{\left(\frac{{2^{*}}}{2}\right)^{n}}{\left(\frac{{2^{*}}}{2}\right)^{n}\alpha_{0}+\left(\frac{{2^{*}}p-2q}{{2^{*}}-2}\right)\left(\left(\frac{{2^{*}}}{2}\right)^{n}-1\right)+q}\overset{n\nearrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}+\left(\frac{{2^{*}}p-2q}{{2^{*}}-2}\right)},\\
\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac{{2^{*}}}{2}\right)^{j}(n-j)}{\alpha_{n}+q}&\leq&\frac{\left(\frac{\frac{{2^{*}}}{2}}{\left(\frac{{2^{*}}}{2}-1\right)^{2}}\right)\left(\left(\frac{{2^{*}}}{2}\right)^{n}-1\right)}{\left(\frac{{2^{*}}}{2}\right)^{n}\alpha_{0}+\left(\frac{{2^{*}}p-2q}{{2^{*}}-2}\right)\left(\left(\frac{{2^{*}}}{2}\right)^{n}-1\right)+q}\leq C(N,\alpha_{0},p,q)<\infty,\\
\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac{{2^{*}}}{2}\right)^{j+1}}{\alpha_{n}+q}&\leq&\frac{\left(\frac{{2^{*}}}{2}\right)\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac{{2^{*}}}{2}\right)^{j}(n-j)}{\alpha_{n}+q}\leq C(\alpha_{0},N,p,q)<\infty.\end{array}

(6.7)

Hence, following the observations from
Eq.

6.7
and passing to the limit as
\displaystyle n\to\infty
in
Eq.

6.6
, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle||\nabla u||_{L^{\infty}(B_{R_{0}/2})}\leq C\left(\int_{B_{R_{0}}}|\nabla u|^{\alpha_{0}+q}\,dx+1\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}+\left(\frac{{2^{*}}p-2q}{{2^{*}}-2}\right)}},

(6.8)

where
\displaystyle C=C(R_{0},\alpha_{0},M,m,p,q,N)
.

Now, we make use of
Eq.

5.1
with the choice of
\displaystyle\beta=\alpha_{0}+q-p\geq 2
, estimate
Eq.

6.8
becomes

\displaystyle\displaystyle||\nabla u||_{L^{\infty}(B_{R_{0}/2})}\leq C\left(\Gamma_{1}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}+\left(\frac{{2^{*}}p-2q}{{2^{*}}-2}\right)}},

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}=R_{0}^{N}\left[\left(\frac{||U||_{L^{\infty}(2B)}}{R_{0}}\right)^{\frac{2(\alpha_{0}+q)}{p}}+\left(\frac{||U||_{L^{\infty}(2B)}}{R_{0}}\right)^{\frac{2(\alpha_{0}+q)}{2-q+p}}+\left(\frac{||U||_{L^{\infty}(2B)}}{R_{0}}\right)^{\alpha_{0}+q}\right].

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

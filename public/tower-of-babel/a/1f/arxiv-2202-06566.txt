On p p -adic L L -functions for GL ⁡ ( 2 ) × GL ⁡ ( 3 ) \mathrm{GL}(2)\times\mathrm{GL}(3) via pullbacks of Saito–Kurokawa lifts
Daniele Casazza, Carlos de Vera-Piquero

Abstract
. We build a one-variable p p -adic L L -function attached to two Hida families of ordinary p p -stabilised newforms 𝐟 {\mathbf{f}} , 𝐠 {\mathbf{g}} , interpolating the algebraic part of the central values of the complex L L -series L ⁡ ( f ⊗ Ad ⁡ ( g ) , s ) L(f\otimes{\mathrm{Ad}}(g),s) when f f and g g range over the classical specialisations of 𝐟 {\mathbf{f}} , 𝐠 {\mathbf{g}} on a suitable line of the weight space. The construction rests on two major results: an explicit formula for the relevant complex central L L -values, and the existence of non-trivial Λ \Lambda -adic Shintani liftings and Saito–Kurokawa liftings studied in a previous work by the authors. We also illustrate that, under an appropriate sign assumption, this p p -adic L L -function arises as a factor of a triple product p p -adic L L -function attached to 𝐟 {\mathbf{f}} , 𝐠 {\mathbf{g}} , and 𝐠 {\mathbf{g}} .

1.
Introduction

The first significant work towards the study of
p
-adic
L
-functions for
\mathrm{GL}(2)\times\mathrm{GL}(3)
was achieved by C.-G. Schmidt in
[
Sch93
]
. By using the so-called modular symbol method, extending ideas due to Kazhdan and Mazur, Schmidt studied algebraicity and
p
-adic interpolation properties of the twists of special values for the
L
-series associated with Rankin–Selberg convolution of two fixed automorphic representations for
\mathrm{GL}(2)
and
\mathrm{GL}(3)
. This lead to the construction of the corresponding
p
-adic
L
-function in the cyclotomic variable.

The algebraicity results and
p
-adic interpolation properties studied in op. cit. have been pursued and generalised to
\mathrm{GL}(n)\times\mathrm{GL}(n+1)
by several authors along the last years (see, for example,
[
KS13
,
RS08
,
Rag10
,
Sun17
,
Jan19
]
). Concerning the construction of
p
-adic
L
-functions for
\mathrm{GL}(n)\times\mathrm{GL}(n+1)
, we must mention the work of F. Januszewski in
[
Jan15
]
, and more recently in
[
Jan
]
, where
p
-adic
L
-functions for Hida families on
\mathrm{GL}(n)\times\mathrm{GL}(n+1)
are achieved. As a by-product, Januszewski obtains strong non-vanishing results for central
L
-values of twisted Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions.

Within the general framework provided by the above mentioned works, the aim of the present paper is to construct a
p
-adic
L
-function (of a single weight variable) for
\mathrm{GL}(2)\times\mathrm{GL}(3)
, using explicit Hida families of ordinary modular forms. Our construction relies on very explicit Ichino-like formulae for central values of degree
6

L
-series proven in the recent years, which are expressed in terms of periods involving Saito–Kurokawa liftings that can be
p
-adically interpolated when varying the weight. A disadvantage of this route is that we are forced to impose some technical assumptions that are inherent to the method and to the available formulae. However, it is expected that most of these assumptions could be removed by pushing further some previous works. In contrast, the advantage of our approach is that it permits a closer link to the arithmetic of classical modular forms and their special
L
-values (for example, via the study of appropriate special cycles in the relevant algebraic varieties). Therefore, our construction provides a good complement to the works cited in the previous paragraph.

In order to describe our main results, let
N\geq 1
be an odd, squarefree integer, and
k,\ell\geq 1
two odd integers. Let
f\in S_{2k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
and
g\in S_{\ell+1}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
be two normalised newforms of level
\Gamma_{0}(N)
and weights
2k
and
\ell+1
, respectively, and let
V_{f}
and
V_{g}
denote the compatible system of
p
-adic Galois representations associated with

and
g
, respectively. Let also
{\mathrm{Ad}}(V_{g}):={\mathrm{Ad}}^{0}(V_{g})
be the
adjoint
representation of
V_{g}
(which, since

has trivial nebentype character, is just a cyclotomic twist of the symmetric square representation
{\mathrm{Sym}}^{2}(V_{g})
). Associated with this data, we may consider the complex
L
-series
L(f\otimes{\mathrm{Ad}}(g),s)
attached to
V_{f}\otimes{\mathrm{Ad}}(V_{g})
, defined by an Euler product

L(f\otimes{\mathrm{Ad}}(g),s)=\prod_{q}L_{(q)}(f\otimes{\mathrm{Ad}}(g),q^{-s})^{-1}

for
\mathrm{Re}(s)\gg 0
. If
q
is a prime not dividing
N
, and we write
\alpha_{q}(f)
,
\beta_{q}(f)
and
\alpha_{q}(g)
,
\beta_{q}(g)
for the roots of the
q
-th Hecke polynomials

P_{f,q}(T):=T^{2}-a_{q}(f)T+q^{2k-1},\qquad P_{g,q}(T):=T^{2}-a_{q}(g)T+q^{\ell},

of
f
and
g
, respectively, then
L(f\otimes{\mathrm{Ad}}(g),T)_{(q)}
is the degree
6
Euler factor defined by the identity

(1)

\displaystyle L_{(q)}(f\otimes{\mathrm{Ad}}(g),T)=\bigl(1-

\displaystyle\alpha_{q}(f)\tfrac{\alpha_{q}(g)}{\beta_{q}(g)}T\bigr)\bigl(1-\alpha_{q}(f)T\bigr)\bigl(1-\alpha_{q}(f)\tfrac{\beta_{q}(g)}{\alpha_{q}(g)}T\bigr)\cdot

\displaystyle\bigl(1-\beta_{q}(f)\tfrac{\alpha_{q}(g)}{\beta_{q}(g)}T\bigr)\bigl(1-\beta_{q}(f)T\bigr)\bigl(1-\beta_{q}(f)\tfrac{\beta_{q}(g)}{\alpha_{q}(g)}T\bigr).

The completed
L
-function

\Lambda(f\otimes{\mathrm{Ad}}(g),s):=L_{\infty}(f\otimes{\mathrm{Ad}}(g),s)\cdot L(f\otimes{\mathrm{Ad}}(g),s),

where

L_{\infty}(f\otimes{\mathrm{Ad}}(g),s)=\begin{cases}\Gamma_{{\mathbf{C}}}(s)\Gamma_{{\mathbf{C}}}(s-2\ell)\Gamma_{{\mathbf{C}}}(s-\ell)^{2}&\text{if }\ell<k,\\
\Gamma_{{\mathbf{C}}}(s)\Gamma_{{\mathbf{C}}}(s+1-2k)\Gamma_{{\mathbf{C}}}(s-\ell)^{2}&\text{if }\ell\geq k,\end{cases}

with
\Gamma_{{\mathbf{C}}}(s):=2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)
, admits analytic continuation to the whole complex plane and satisfies a functional equation relating the values
\Lambda(f\otimes{\mathrm{Ad}}(g),2k-s)
and
\Lambda(f\otimes{\mathrm{Ad}}(g),s)
. The center of symmetry
s=k
is always a critical point in the sense of Deligne, and the global sign
\varepsilon=\{\pm 1\}
appearing in this functional equation can be written as a product of local signs, one for each rational place:

\varepsilon=\prod_{v}\varepsilon_{v},\qquad\varepsilon_{v}\in\{\pm 1\}.

The local signs
\varepsilon_{v}
are
+1
away from
N\infty
, and hence this is actually a finite product. In addition, our assumption that both
f
and
g
are newforms of the same level
N
, with
N
odd and squarefree, implies that
\varepsilon_{q}=+1
for all primes
q\mid N
. Therefore, the sign in the functional equation for
\Lambda(f\otimes{\mathrm{Ad}}(g),s)
is completely governed by the local sign at the archimedean place
v=\infty
, i.e.
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{\infty}
. Finally, one can check that

\varepsilon_{\infty}=\begin{cases}-1&\text{if }\ell<k,\\
+1&\text{if }\ell\geq k.\end{cases}

In the second case, one expects the central value
\Lambda(f\otimes{\mathrm{Ad}}(g),k)
to be generically non-zero, and it is natural to aim for a (two-variable)
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathcal{L}_{p}({\mathbf{f}},{\mathrm{Ad}}({\mathbf{g}}))
interpolating the central values
\Lambda(f\otimes{\mathrm{Ad}}(g),k)
, in the region
\ell\geq k
, when both
f
and
g
vary in Hida families
{\mathbf{f}}
and
{\mathbf{g}}
of ordinary
p
-stabilised newforms. With this in mind, the goal of this note is to construct a one-variable
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\circ}({\mathbf{f}},{\mathrm{Ad}}({\mathbf{g}}))
attached to Hida families
{\mathbf{f}}
,
{\mathbf{g}}
interpolating those central values along the line
\ell=k
, which shall provide what it is expected to be the restriction of the two-variable one.

Assume from now on that
\ell=k
. Our construction relies on an explicit central value formula for
\Lambda(f\otimes{\mathrm{Ad}}(g),k)
in terms of Petersson products involving Saito–Kurokawa lifts, firstly described by Ichino
[
Ich05
]
and recently generalised in
[
PdVP19
]
and
[
Che20
]
. For our construction, we will rely on the formulation in
[
PdVP19
]
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N\geq 1
be a squarefree odd integer,
k\geq 1
be an odd integer, and let
f\in S_{2k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
,
g\in S_{k+1}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
be two normalised newforms of level
N
and weights
2k
and
k+1
, respectively. Then

\Lambda(f\otimes\mathrm{Ad}(g),k)=C(f,g)\cdot\frac{\langle f,f\rangle}{\langle h,h\rangle}\cdot\frac{|\langle\varpi(F),g\times g\rangle|^{2}}{\langle g,g\rangle^{2}},

where
C(f,g)=2^{k+1}N^{-1}\prod_{q\mid N}(1+q)^{2}
, and:

•

h\in S_{k+1/2}(N)
is any half-integral weight modular form in Shimura–Shintani correspondence with
f
;

•

F\in S^{(2)}_{k+1}(N)
is the Saito–Kurokawa lift of
h
;

•

\varpi(F)\in S_{k+1}(N)\otimes S_{k+1}(N)
is the pullback of
F
to
\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
embedded diagonally into Siegel’s upper half space
\mathcal{H}_{2}
(see Section
4
for details).

The Shimura–Shintani correspondence and the Saito–Kurokawa lift are recalled in Section
3
. A corollary of the above central value formula is the algebraicity property of that value when divided by the corresponding period, as predicted by Deligne. Indeed, one has

(2)

\Lambda(f\otimes\mathrm{Ad}(g),k)^{\mathrm{alg}}:=\frac{\Lambda(f\otimes\mathrm{Ad}(g),k)}{\langle g,g\rangle^{2}\Omega_{f}^{-}}=C(f,g)\cdot\frac{\langle f,f\rangle}{\langle h,h\rangle\Omega_{f}^{-}}\cdot\frac{|\langle\varpi(F),g\times g\rangle|^{2}}{\langle g,g\rangle^{4}}\in{\mathbf{Q}}(f,g),

where
\Omega_{f}^{-}\in{\mathbf{C}}^{\times}
denotes a complex period attached to
f
by Shimura (see
[
GS93
, Theorem 4.8]
and equation (
14
) below) and
{\mathbf{Q}}(f,g)
denotes the number field obtained by adjoining to
{\mathbf{Q}}
the Fourier coefficients of
f
and
g
. This explicit expression for the algebraic part of the central value
\Lambda(f\otimes{\mathrm{Ad}}(g),k)
suggests the construction of the desired
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\circ}({\mathbf{f}},{\mathrm{Ad}}({\mathbf{g}}))
attached to two Hida families of ordinary
p
-stabilised newforms by directly interpolating the right hand side of (
2
) when
f
and
g
vary along the classical specialisations of
{\mathbf{f}}
and
{\mathbf{g}}
. This is the strategy that we follow in this article.

In order to build the desired

Remark 1.3
.

The reason for the ornament
\circ
in the notation
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\circ}({\mathbf{f}},{\mathrm{Ad}}({\mathbf{g}}))
is that this is expected to be a one-variable restriction of a more general two-variable

-adic

satisfying an analogous interpolation property on the subset of

determined by

(see

]

divides the expected Euler factor arising from the general conjectural description of the

-adic L-function of an arbitrary motive, but it is not equal to it.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume that
\varepsilon({\mathbf{f}})=+1

-adic L-functions:

\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\mathrm{bal},\circ}({\mathbf{f}},{\mathbf{g}},{\mathbf{g}})^{2}=\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\circ}({\mathbf{f}},{\mathrm{Ad}}({\mathbf{g}}))\cdot\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\circ}({\mathbf{f}})\pmod{\Lambda^{\times}}.

The proof of this result follows straightforward by comparing the interpolation properties of the three

-adic
L

More interesting and challenging is the case when
\varepsilon({\mathbf{f}})=-1

\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\circ}({\mathbf{f}},{\mathrm{Ad}}({\mathbf{g}}))
(and more generally,
\mathcal{L}_{p}({\mathbf{f}},{\mathrm{Ad}}({\mathbf{g}}))

-adic
L
-function is to be replaced by an
unbalanced
triple-product
p
-adic
L
-function. The proof of the expected factorisation will not follow by comparing interpolation properties, as the interpolation regions will now be disjoint, but rather by following a strategy inspired by Dasgupta’s and Gross’ factorisation results in
[
Das16
]
,
[
Gro80
]
.

Finally, let us close this introduction by explaining the motivation behind some of the choices that are made for the construction of
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{\circ}({\mathbf{f}},{\mathrm{Ad}}({\mathbf{g}}))
. One of the two main objects we use is a
\Lambda
-adic family
\boldsymbol{\Theta}\in\Lambda[[q]]
of half-integral weight modular forms. A version of it was first defined in
[
Ste94
]
, but we shall use here the version introduced in
[
CdVP
]
. The main reason for our choice is that while the first construction could be trivial, the latter can be guaranteed to be nonzero, which is fundamental for the construction of the
p
-adic
L
-function.

Another mild innovation of the present manuscript is the introduction of a completely explicit element
{\mathbf{SK}}\in\Lambda[[q_{1},q_{2},\zeta]]
interpolating the Saito–Kurokawa lifts, which is used to build the
\Lambda
-adic object interpolating the left hand side of equation (

). This object is interpolating the elements
F_{\alpha}
, which should correspond to a suitable normalisation of the semi-ordinary
p
-stabilisation of the classical Saito–Kurokawa lift described in
[
SU06
]
. An explicit
p
-adic interpolation of families of coefficients of Saito–Kurokawa lifts was also explored in
[
Gue00
]
and in
[
LN13
]
, but that approach was not useful for our purposes in this paper. Our construction is more reminiscent of the one in
[
Kaw
]
, which is done for the Duke–Imamoḡlu lifting in level
1

2.
Modular forms

2.1.
Classical modular forms (of integral weight)

Let
M,k\geq 1
be integers, and let
S_{2k}(M)
denote the space of classical modular cuspforms of weight
2k
and trivial nebentype character for the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(M)
of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbf{Z}})
. If
\phi\in S_{2k}(M)
, we write its
q
-expansion as usual,

\phi=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}(\phi)q^{n},\qquad\text{where }q=\exp(2\pi i\tau).

If
\phi,\psi\in S_{2k}(M)
, we normalize their Petersson product to be

\langle\phi,\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{[\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbf{Z}}):\Gamma_{0}(M)]}\int_{\Gamma_{0}(M)\backslash\mathcal{H}}\phi(z)\overline{\psi(z)}y^{2k-2}dxdy.

With this normalisation, the Petersson product of
\phi
and
\psi
does not depend on
M
, in the sense that it remains invariant if we replace
M
by a multiple of it and see
\phi
and
\psi
as forms of that level.

Recall that if
\gamma\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}({\mathbf{Q}})
, then the slash operator (of weight
2k
) is defined by

\phi|_{2k}\gamma(\tau)=\det(\gamma)^{2k-1}(c\tau+d)^{-2k}\phi(\gamma\tau),\quad\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix},

where
\det:\mathrm{GL}_{2}\to\mathbf{G}_{m}
(the determinant) is the similitude morphism or scale map.

Let
p
be a prime, and consider the double coset operator
\Gamma_{0}(M)u_{p}\Gamma_{0}(M)
associated with the element
u_{p}:=\mathrm{diag}(1,p)\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}({\mathbf{Q}})
. As it is customary, when
p\nmid M
this operator will be denoted by
T_{p}
, while if
p\mid M
we will call it
U_{p}
. The action of
U_{p}
can be described as

U_{p}\phi(\tau)=\sum_{b\in{\mathbf{Z}}/p{\mathbf{Z}}}\phi|_{2k}\alpha_{b}(\tau),\qquad\alpha_{b}=\begin{pmatrix}1&b\\
0&p\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}({\mathbf{Q}}),

where
b
runs over a set of representatives for
{\mathbf{Z}}/p{\mathbf{Z}}
. By making explicit the slash action of the elements
\alpha_{b}
this can also be written as

(6)

U_{p}\phi(\tau)=p^{-1}\sum_{b\in{\mathbf{Z}}/p{\mathbf{Z}}}\phi((\tau+b)/p).

This last expression also makes sense for cuspforms
\phi\in S_{2k}(M)
with
M
not divisible by
p
; in that case,
U_{p}\phi
belongs to
S_{2k}(Mp)
.

Continue to fix the prime
p
. Working on
q
-expansions, we can consider the operators
U
and
V
defined by

U\phi:=\sum_{n}a_{pn}(\phi)q^{n},\qquad V\phi:=\sum_{n}a_{n}(\phi)q^{pn}=\sum_{n}a_{n/p}(\phi)q^{n},

where in the latter expression we read
a_{n/p}(\phi)=0
if
p\nmid n
. It is an easy exercise to see that
UV=1
while
VU
does not, so that
V
provides a right inverse to
U
. One can check that the action of
U
is the same as that of the expression (
6
), so that
U\phi\in S_{2k}(\mathrm{lcm}(M,p))
, and
U
coincides with the Hecke operator
U_{p}
when
p\mid M
. We also remark that
V=V_{p}:=p^{1-2k}V_{p,2k-1}
, where
V_{p,2k-1}\phi=p^{2k-1}\phi(p\tau)
is given by the (weight
2k
) slash action of the matrix
\mathrm{diag}(p,1)
, so that
V\phi\in S_{2k}(Mp)
. This is why we will often write simply
U
and
V
and no confusion should arise.

Suppose that
p
does not divide
M
. Then one has the well-known relation

(7)

T_{p}=U+p^{2k-1}V

among the operators
T_{p}
,
U
, and
V
. If
\phi=\sum_{n}a_{n}(\phi)q^{n}\in S_{2k}(M)
is a normalised eigenform, and
\alpha=\alpha_{p}(\phi)
,
\beta=\beta_{p}(\phi)
denote the roots of the
p
-th Hecke polynomial of
\phi
, so that
\alpha+\beta=a_{p}(\phi)
and
\alpha\beta=p^{2k-1}
, we write

\phi_{\alpha}:=(1-\beta V)\phi,\qquad\phi_{\beta}:=(1-\alpha V)\phi

for its so-called
p
-stabilisations
. These forms belong to
S_{2k}(Mp)
, and
U
acts on
\phi_{\alpha}
(resp.
\phi_{\beta}
) as multiplication by
\alpha
(resp.
\beta
).

We will write
S_{2k}^{\mathrm{new}}(M)\subseteq S_{2k}(M)
for the subspace of newforms of level
M
. It is the subspace orthogonal to the subspace of
old
forms in
S_{2k}(M)
arising from lower levels, with respect to the Petersson product. When
\phi
is a normalised eigenform for all Hecke operators, and
M\mid M^{\prime}
, we shall write

S_{2k}(M^{\prime})[\phi]:=\{\phi^{\prime}\in S_{2k}(M^{\prime})\mid T_{p}\phi^{\prime}=a_{p}(\phi)\phi^{\prime},\,\forall p\nmid M^{\prime}\}.

We will write
e_{\phi}
for the projection onto the
\phi
-component
S_{2k}(M^{\prime})[\phi]
. By the strong multiplicity one theorem, if
\phi\in S_{2k}^{\mathrm{new}}(M)
is a newform then
S_{2k}(M)[\phi]=\langle\phi\rangle
. From equation (
7
) and the definition of the
p
-stabilisations, one can also check that

(8)

S_{2k}(Mp)[\phi]=\langle\phi,V\phi\rangle=\langle U\phi,\phi\rangle=\langle\phi_{\alpha},\phi_{\beta}\rangle.

2.2.
Half-integral weight modular forms

Let
M\geq 1
and
k\geq 1
be integers, and assume for simplicity now that
M
is
odd
and

\mathfrak{S}_{k+1/2}(M)
for the space of cuspforms of half-integral weight

for the so-called ‘Kohnen’s plus subspace’, which consists of those forms

having

-expansion

h=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1,\\
(-1)^{r}n\equiv 0,1\,(4)\end{subarray}}c(n)q^{n}.

That is to say, the
n
-th Fourier coefficient is required to vanish if
(-1)^{r}n
is not a discriminant
1
1

1

Very often, Kohnen’s plus subspace is denoted

As described in
[

and
[
Koh82

, there is a theory of Hecke operators similar to that of integral weight modular forms. For the purposes of this note, we just describe the analogues of the operators
T_{p}
,
U
, and

described above for integral weight. If
p
is an odd prime not dividing

T_{p^{2}}
acting on

where we read

if

p

acting on

-expansions reads

Finally, if
p
is any prime, then the operator

(analogue of

) defined on

V_{p^{2}}\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}c(n)q^{n}\right)=\sum_{n\geq 1}c(n/p^{2})q^{n}

maps

into
S_{k+1/2}(Mp^{2})
. This implies that

and

, with the notation of the previous section. Let us write
\varepsilon_{p}(n):=\left(\frac{n}{p}\right)
for the Legendre symbol. By the expression of the above operator one can rephrase the action of
T_{p^{2}}
as

(9)

T_{p^{2}}h=U_{p^{2}}h+p^{2k-1}V_{p^{2}}h+(-1)^{r(p-1)/2}h\otimes\varepsilon_{p},

where

has

\sum_{n\geq 1}\varepsilon_{p}(n)c(n)q^{n},

if

denotes the

-th Fourier coefficient of

. Note that the identity (

(see
[

for a more general result about twists).

Because of our assumption that

there is a well-behaved theory of oldforms and newforms (see
[

for the subspace of newforms. In particular, if

is the two-dimensional subspace
\langle h,U_{p^{2}}h\rangle
spanned by
h

U_{p^{2}}h
.

Using equation (
9

U_{p^{2}}(h\otimes\varepsilon_{p})=0

. Indeed, let
a_{p}(h)
be the eigenvalue of

\alpha+\beta=a_{p}(h)
and

h_{\alpha}:=\alpha^{-1}(U_{p^{2}}-\beta)h,\qquad h_{\beta}:=\beta^{-1}(U_{p^{2}}-\alpha)h.

We have the following equality:

h_{\alpha}=h-(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p^{-k}\beta h\otimes\varepsilon_{p}-\beta V^{2}h.

3.
Shimura–Shintani correspondence and Saito–Kurokawa lifts

Changing slightly the notation from the previous paragraphs, in this section we fix integers
N,k\geq 1
, and assume for simplicity that
N
is odd and squarefree.

3.1.
Shimura–Shintani correspondence and
\mathfrak{d}
-th Shintani liftings

One of the main reasons for the arithmetic interest of half-integral weight modular forms stems from the so-called Shimura–Shintani correspondence.

Theorem 3.1

(Shimura–Shintani correspondence)
.

Let
N,k\geq 1
be as above. There is a Hecke-equivariant linear isomorphism

(11)

S_{2k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)\,\mathrel{{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits}}\,S_{k+1/2}^{\mathrm{new}}(N).

In particular, given a normalised newform
f\in S_{2k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
, on which the Hecke operators
T_{\ell}
at primes
\ell\nmid N
act with eigenvalue
a_{\ell}
, there is a unique
h\in S_{k+1/2}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
, up to scalar, on which the Hecke operators
T_{\ell^{2}}
, for primes
\ell\nmid N
, act with eigenvalue
a_{\ell}
. If
h
is such a half-integral weight modular form, one says that
h
is in Shimura–Shintani correspondence with
f
, or that
h
and
f
are in Shimura–Shintani correspondence. An example of how half-integral weight modular forms carry over arithmetic information about integral weight modular forms via this correspondence is the following important formula due to Kohnen
[
Koh85
, Corollary 1]
, generalizing a previous formula of Kohnen–Zagier
[
KZ81
, Theorem 1]
for trivial level.

Theorem 3.2

(Kohnen’s formula)
.

Let
N
and
k
be as above. Let
h\in S_{k+1/2}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
be any non-zero cusp form in Shimura–Shintani correspondence with
f\in S_{2k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
, and let
D
be a fundamental discriminant such that
(-1)^{k}D>0
and
\left(\frac{D}{\ell}\right)=w_{\ell}(f)
for all primes
\ell\mid N
, where
w_{\ell}(f)=\pm 1
denotes the eigenvalue of the
\ell
-th Atkin–Lehner involution acting on
f
. Then one has

(12)

|c_{|D|}(h)|^{2}=2^{\nu(N)}\frac{(k-1)!}{\pi^{k}}|D|^{k-1/2}\cdot\frac{\langle h,h\rangle}{\langle f,f\rangle}\cdot L(f,D,k),

where
\nu(N)
is the number of primes dividing
N
.

The isomorphism in (
11
) can be made explicit by means of the so-called
\mathfrak{d}
-th Shintani liftings
. Indeed, associated with each fundamental discriminant
\mathfrak{d}
such that
(-1)^{k}\mathfrak{d}>0
, the
\mathfrak{d}
-th Shintani lifting is a Hecke-equivariant linear map

\theta_{k,N,\mathfrak{d}}:S_{2k}(N)\,\longrightarrow\,S_{k+1/2}(N)

from
S_{2k}(N)
to Kohnen’s plus subspace
S_{k+1/2}(N)\subseteq\mathfrak{S}_{k+1/2}(N)
. It is defined by means of certain geodesic cycle integrals on the complex upper-half plane, firstly studied by Shintani
[
Shi75
]
, and it is adjoint to the so-called
\mathfrak{d}
-th Shimura lifting with respect to the Petersson product. An appropriate combination of Shintani liftings, for various discriminants, provides a realisation of the isomorphism
S_{2k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)\simeq S_{k+1/2}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
as in (
11
) (see
[
Koh82
, Theorem 2]
). Also, the proof of Theorem
3.2
shows that the
|\mathfrak{d}|
-th Fourier coefficient of the
\mathfrak{d}
-th Shintani lifting is in fact a multiple of the twisted
L
-value
L(f,\mathfrak{d},k)
. Namely, one has (see
[
Koh85
, p. 243]
,
[
CdVP
, Eq. (15)]
)

(13)

c_{|\mathfrak{d}|}(\theta_{k,N,\mathfrak{d}}(f))=(-1)^{[k/2]}2^{\nu(N)+k}|\mathfrak{d}|^{k}(k-1)!\cdot\frac{L(f,\mathfrak{d},k)}{(2\pi i)^{k}\mathfrak{g}(\chi_{\mathfrak{d}})},

where
[x]
denotes the smallest integer such that
[x]\geq x
, and
\mathfrak{g}(\chi_{\mathfrak{d}})
is the Gauss sum of the quadratic character associated with
\mathfrak{d}
.

Another important feature of the
\mathfrak{d}
-th Shintani lifting, especially for the purposes of
p
-adic interpolation and hence for the topic of this note, is the fact that it can be made algebraic. Namely, suppose that
f\in S_{2k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
, and choose a fundamental discriminant
\mathfrak{d}
with
(-1)^{k}\mathfrak{d}>0
and
\theta_{k,N,\mathfrak{d}}(f)\neq 0
(such a choice is indeed possible: one can use the above formula (
13
) together with well-known results on the non-vanishing of (twisted) central values of
L
-series of modular forms, e.g. from
[
BFH90
]
). A cohomological description of the Shintani lifting in terms of modular symbols, due originally to Stevens
[
Ste94
]
, shows that if
f\in S_{2k}(N)
is a Hecke eigenform then (cf.
[
CdVP
, Section 5.1]
)

(14)

\theta_{k,N,\mathfrak{d}}^{\mathrm{alg}}(f):=\frac{1}{\Omega_{f}^{-}}\theta_{k,N,\mathfrak{d}}(f)\in S_{k+1/2}(N;\mathcal{O}_{f}),

where
\mathcal{O}_{f}
denotes the ring of integers in the Hecke field of
f
, and
\Omega_{f}^{-}\in{\mathbf{C}}^{\times}
is a complex period attached to
f
by Shimura. One can actually attach two complex periods
\Omega_{f}^{\pm}\in{\mathbf{C}}^{\times}
satisfying the algebraicity property spelled out in
[
GS93
, Theorem 4.8]
, and with the additional property that
\Omega_{f}^{+}\Omega_{f}^{-}=\langle f,f\rangle
. We do fix such a choice attached to each Hecke eigenform.

We end this paragraph by pointing out the following result.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
f\in S_{2k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
,
p\nmid 2N
be a prime, and let
\alpha
be a root of the Hecke polynomial of
f
at
p
. If
p\mid\mathfrak{d}
, then we have the following equality:

\theta_{k,Np,\mathfrak{d}}(f_{\alpha})=\theta_{k,N,\mathfrak{d}}(f)_{\alpha}.

Proof.

This essentially follows from the fact that
\theta_{k,Np,\mathfrak{d}}(f)=\theta_{k,N,\mathfrak{d}}(f)
, which is proven in
[
CdVP
]
.
∎

3.2.
Saito–Kurokawa lift

The theory of Saito–Kurokawa liftings for arbitrary level and character in classical terms was established in
[
Ibu12
]
, generalizing
[
EZ85
]
and
[
Maa79a
,
Maa79b
,
Maa79c
]
. For the purpose of this note, we restrict ouselves to the case of odd squarefree level and trivial character. Thus we continue to assume as above that
N\geq 1
is odd and squarefree, and suppose that
k\geq 1
is odd as well. Fix also an odd prime
p
not dividing
N
.

Let
f\in S_{2k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
be a normalised eigenform, and
h\in S_{k+1/2}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
be any non-zero form in Shimura–Shintani correspondence with
f
(for example, we can normalize
h
to be the
\mathfrak{d}
-th Shintani of
f
for a particular choice of
\mathfrak{d}
as above, but it will not be necessary to fix such a choice now). The
Saito–Kurokawa lift
attaches to
h
a Siegel modular form

F:={\mathrm{SK}}_{N}(h)\in S_{k+1}^{(2)}(N)

of weight
k+1
and level
N
. This is sometimes referred to as the Saito–Kurokawa lift of
f
as well (although it is only defined up to a scalar multiple). On
q
-expansions, it is defined by setting (see, e.g.,
[
Ibu12
]
)

(15)

F=\sum_{B>0}A(B)q^{B},\qquad\text{with }\,A(B)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<d\mid\gcd(B),\\
(d,N)=1\end{subarray}}d^{k}c(\det(2B)/d^{2}),

where
c(n)
denotes the
n
-th Fourier coefficient in the
q
-expansion of
h
. Here,
\gcd(B)
denotes the greatest common divisor of
m
,
r
,
n
if
B
has entries
m
,
r/2
,
n
with the usual notation. Observe in particular that
A(B)=c(\det(2B))
when
\gcd(B)=1
.

From its very definition it is clear that
F={\mathrm{SK}}_{N}(h)
depends heavily on
N
, meaning that if we consider
h
as an old form of level
Nt
for some integer
t>1
, then the Siegel forms
{\mathrm{SK}}_{Nt}(h)
and
{\mathrm{SK}}_{N}(h)
are a priori different in
S_{k+1}^{(2)}(Nt)
.
Let us consider the Saito–Kurokawa lift
F^{(p)}:={\mathrm{SK}}_{Np}(h)\in S_{k+1}^{(2)}(Np)
of
h
in level
Np
. By definition, its
q
-expansion is

F^{(p)}=\sum_{B>0}A^{(p)}(B)q^{B},\qquad A^{(p)}(B)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<d\mid\gcd(B),\\
(d,Np)=1\end{subarray}}d^{k}c(\det(2B)/d^{2}).

An easy computation shows that
A^{(p)}(B)=A(B)-p^{k}A(B/p)
, for all
B
, which implies the following equality:

F^{(p)}=(1-p^{k}V)F.

This shows, in particular, that
{\mathrm{SK}}_{N}(h)\neq{\mathrm{SK}}_{Np}(h)
(compare this with the identity
\theta_{k,Np,\mathfrak{d}}(f)=\theta_{k,N,\mathfrak{d}}(f)
mentioned in the proof of Proposition
3.3
).

Similarly, let
\alpha_{f}
,
\beta_{f}
be the roots of the
p
-th Hecke polynomial of
f
. We define

F_{\alpha}:={\mathrm{SK}}_{Np}(h_{\alpha})\in S_{k+1}^{(2)}(Np)

to be the Saito–Kurokawa lift of the
p
-stabilisation
h_{\alpha}\in S_{k+1/2}(Np)
of
h
corresponding to
\alpha_{f}
. This is a genus two Siegel form of weight
k+1
and level
Np
, whose
q
-expansion is given by:

F_{\alpha}=\sum_{B>0}A_{\alpha}(B)q^{B},\qquad A_{\alpha}(B)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<d\mid\gcd(B),\\
(d,Np)=1\end{subarray}}d^{k}c_{\alpha}(\det(2B)/d^{2}),

where
c_{\alpha}(n)
denotes the
n
-th Fourier coefficient of
h_{\alpha}
. Note that the expression for
A_{\alpha}(B)
reads exactly as the one for
A^{(p)}
, but with the
c(n)
’s replaced now by
c_{\alpha}(n)
’s. By definition, we know that
h_{\alpha}=\alpha_{f}^{-1}(U_{p^{2}}-\beta_{f})
and since the Saito–Kurokawa lift is Hecke equivariant (in particular,
{\mathrm{SK}}_{Np}\circ U_{p^{2}}=U_{p}\circ{\mathrm{SK}}_{Np}
), one deduces that

(16)

F_{\alpha}=\alpha_{f}^{-1}(U-\beta)F^{(p)}=\alpha_{f}^{-1}(U-\beta_{f})(1-p^{k}V)F.

Remark 3.4
.

One can similarly define the Siegel modular form

F^{\mathrm{so}}:=(U-\beta_{f})(U-p^{k})F\in S_{k+1}^{(2)}(Np).

When
\alpha_{f}
is a
p
-adic unit, the latter is sometimes referred to as the semi-ordinary
p
-stabilisation of
F
. One can easily check that
F_{\alpha}=\alpha_{f}^{-2}F^{\mathrm{so}}
, since
U
acts invertibly on
S^{(2)}_{k+1}(Np)[f]
. We refer the reader to
[
Kaw
]
for more details on the Siegel form
F^{\mathrm{so}}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

With the above notation, we have

F_{\alpha}=(1-\beta_{f}V)(1-p^{k}V)F-(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p^{-k}\beta_{f}F\otimes\varepsilon_{p},

where
F\otimes\varepsilon_{p}
is the Siegel form defined by the
q
-expansion

F\otimes\varepsilon_{p}=\sum_{B>0}\varepsilon_{p}(\det(2B))A(B)q^{B}.

Proof.

The equality follows from Proposition
2.1
. Indeed, we have
h_{\alpha}=h-\sigma p^{-k}\beta_{f}h\otimes\varepsilon_{p}-\beta_{f}V^{2}h
, where
\sigma=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}
. Applying
{\mathrm{SK}}_{Np}
we get

F_{\alpha}=F^{(p)}-\beta_{f}{\mathrm{SK}}_{Np}(\sigma p^{-k}h\otimes\varepsilon_{p}+V^{2}h).

If
c(n)
denotes the
n
-th Fourier coefficient of
h
, then the
n
-th Fourier coefficient of
\sigma p^{-k}h\otimes\varepsilon_{p}+V^{2}h
is
\sigma p^{-k}(\frac{n}{p})c(n)+c(n/p^{2})
, and it easily follows that

{\mathrm{SK}}_{Np}(\sigma p^{-k}h\otimes\varepsilon_{p}+V^{2}h)=\sigma p^{-k}F^{(p)}\otimes\varepsilon_{p}+VF^{(p)}.

Noting that
F^{(p)}\otimes\varepsilon_{p}=F\otimes\varepsilon_{p}
, the equality of the
q
-expansions follows. Since all the other terms in the identity are modular forms of level
Np^{2}
, we see that
F\otimes\varepsilon_{p}\in S_{k+1}^{(2)}(Np^{2})
as well.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

We have the following equality:

UF-(p^{k}+a_{p}(f))F+(p^{2k-1}+p^{k}a_{p}(f))VF-p^{3k-1}V^{2}F+(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p^{k-1}F\otimes\varepsilon_{p}=0.

Proof.

This follows straightforward from the above proposition, replacing
F^{(p)}
by
(1-p^{k}V)F
and using the relations
\alpha_{f}\beta_{f}=p^{2k-1}
,
\alpha_{f}+\beta_{f}=a_{p}(f)
. This result can also be directly derived by looking at the Fourier coefficients of
F
, using the properties of the Fourier coefficients of
h
.
∎

4.
On the pullback of Saito–Kurokawa lifts to the diagonal

This section is devoted to describe the pullback of Siegel forms to the diagonal
\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\subset\mathcal{H}_{2}
, where
\mathcal{H}
denotes Poincaré’s upper half-plane, and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
stands for Siegel’s upper half-space of genus two. In particular, we describe pullbacks of Saito–Kurokawa lifts as considered in the previous section.

Before focusing on the specific case of Saito–Kurokawa lifts, we may first consider some generalitites about the pullback of Siegel forms. To do so, let us fix integers
k,M\geq 1
, with
k
odd, and consider the spaces
S_{k+1}(M)
and
S_{k+1}^{(2)}(M)
of classical and Siegel cusp forms of weight
k+1
and level
M
, respectively. Recall that
\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
can be embedded
diagonally
in the Siegel upper half-space
\mathcal{H}_{2}
of genus two via the map

(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}\tau_{1}&0\\
0&\tau_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

This induces a linear map, referred to as
pullback to the diagonal
,

\displaystyle\varpi:S_{k+1}^{(2)}(M)\,

\displaystyle\rightarrow\,S_{k+1}(M)\otimes S_{k+1}(M)

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle\mapsto\varpi(\Phi)=\Phi|_{\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}},

provided by the restriction to
z=0
. In terms of
q
-expansions, and adopting the notation of the previous section, suppose that

\Phi=\sum_{B}A(B)q^{B},

where
B
runs over the half-integral positive definite symmetric two-by-two matrices, and write

A(n,r,m)=A\left(\begin{pmatrix}n&r/2\\
r/2&m\end{pmatrix}\right),\quad n,r,m\in{\mathbf{Z}},4nm-r^{2}>0.

Then the pullback map is described on
q
-expansions by

(17)

\varpi(\Phi)(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})=\sum_{n,m\geq 1}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in{\mathbf{Z}},\\
r^{2}<4nm\end{subarray}}A(n,r,m)\right)q_{1}^{n}q_{2}^{m},\qquad q_{j}=\mathrm{exp}(2\pi i\tau_{j}).

We may write
\alpha_{n,m}(\varpi(\Phi))
for the
(n,m)
-th Fourier coefficient in this expansion. Observe that given a basis
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i}
of
S_{k+1}(M)
we can write the pullback of
\Phi
as

\varpi(\Phi)=\sum_{i,j}\lambda_{i,j}\phi_{i}(\tau_{1})\times\phi_{j}(\tau_{2}),

where the scalars
\lambda_{i,j}
are uniquely determined by
\Phi
.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
p
is a prime, then

\varpi(V\Phi)=V\times V\varpi(\Phi).

Proof.

It is clear from the definitions that
\varpi(\Phi(pZ))=\varpi(\Phi)(p\tau_{1},p\tau_{2})
, hence the statement follows.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

More generally, consider the embedding
\iota:\mathrm{G}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}\times\mathrm{SL}_{2})\,\hookrightarrow\,\operatorname{GSp}_{4}
given by

\left(\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}\\
c_{1}&d_{1}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}a_{2}&b_{2}\\
c_{2}&d_{2}\end{pmatrix}\right)\,\longmapsto\,\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&0&b_{1}&0\\
0&a_{2}&0&b_{2}\\
c_{1}&0&d_{1}&0\\
0&c_{2}&0&d_{2}\end{pmatrix},

where
\mathrm{G}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}\times\mathrm{SL}_{2})=\{(g,h)\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}:\det(g)=\det(h)\}
. Then the product slash action on
S_{k+1}(M)\otimes S_{k+1}(M)
with elements in
\mathrm{G}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}\times\mathrm{SL}_{2})
can be compared with the slash action on
S_{k+1}^{(2)}(M)
of their image under
\iota
. For example, consider the element

(v_{p},v_{p})=\left(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}p&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}p&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right)\right)\in\operatorname{G}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbf{Q}})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbf{Q}})).

The slash action of this element on
S_{k+1}(M)\otimes S_{k+1}(M)
is the operator
V_{p,k+1}\times V_{p,k+1}=p^{2k}V\times V
, while its image under the above embedding is
\gamma_{p}=\mathrm{diag}(p,p,1,1)
, and corresponds with the operator
V_{p,k+1}=p^{2k-1}V
for
\mathrm{GSp}(4)
. Taking care of the powers of
p
arising in each case, the above lemma can be rewritten as a comparison between the slash actions with
\gamma_{p}
and
(v_{p},v_{p})
.

The analogue of Lemma
4.1
for the operators
U
and
U\times U
does not hold, and this will be important from now on. To proceed in understanding how the
U
-operator behaves with respect to pullback, we restrict ourselves to the case of Saito–Kurokawa lifts. We thus assume from now on that
N\geq 1
is odd and squarefree,
k\geq 1
is an odd integer, and
p
is an odd prime not dividing
N
. With this, let
F={\mathrm{SK}}(h)\in S_{k+1}^{(2)}(N)
be a Saito–Kurokawa lift as in the previous section. In this setting, recall that we have a well-defined twisted Siegel form
F\otimes\varepsilon_{p}\in S_{k+1}(Np^{2})
as in Proposition
3.5
. The main result of the section is the following:

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
F={\mathrm{SK}}_{N}(h)\in S_{k+1}^{(2)}(N)
be a Saito–Kurokawa lifting and let
\phi\in S_{k+1}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
be a normalised eigenform. Let
\varpi_{\phi}:=e_{\phi}\otimes e_{\phi}\circ\varpi
, where
e_{\phi}
is the projector onto the
\phi
-component as defined in Section
2.1
, and let
\lambda_{\phi}
be such that
\varpi_{\phi}(F)=\lambda_{\phi}\phi\times\phi
. Then

\varpi_{\phi}(UF)=\lambda_{\phi}\left(A\phi\times\phi+B\phi\times V\phi+CV\phi\times\phi+DV\phi\times V\phi\right),

where

A=\frac{(p^{k-1}(p-1)+a_{p})a_{p}(\phi)^{2}-p^{k}(p+1)(p^{k-1}(p+1)+a_{p})}{a_{p}(\phi)^{2}-p^{k-1}(p+1)^{2}},

and
B
,
C
,
D
are given by the formulae

B=C=\frac{pa_{p}(\phi)}{p+1}(p^{k}-p^{k-1}+a_{p}-A),\quad D=-p^{k+1}(p^{k}+a_{p}-A).

As a consequence of the above theorem, we have the following:

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\phi\in S_{k+1}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
be a normalised eigenform, and suppose that
\varpi_{\phi}(F)=\lambda_{\phi}\phi\times\phi
. If
F_{\alpha}={\mathrm{SK}}_{Np}(h_{\alpha})\in S_{k+1}^{(2)}(Np)
is a
p
-stabilisation of
F
as described in Section
2.3
, then we have

\varpi_{\phi}(F_{\alpha})=\lambda_{\phi}\left(1-\frac{\beta}{p^{k}}\right)\left(A_{\alpha}\phi\times\phi+B_{\alpha}\phi\times V\phi+C_{\alpha}V\phi\times\phi+D_{\alpha}V\phi\times V\phi\right),

where
\beta
is the other eigenvalue of the
U_{p^{2}}
-action on
S_{k+1/2}(Np)[h]
,

A_{\alpha}=1-\frac{(p+1)\left(1-\frac{\beta}{p^{k-1}}\right)}{\left(p-\frac{\alpha_{\phi}}{\beta_{\phi}}\right)\left(p-\frac{\beta_{\phi}}{\alpha_{\phi}}\right)}

and
B_{\alpha}
,
C_{\alpha}
,
D_{\alpha}
are given by the formulae

B_{\alpha}=C_{\alpha}=\frac{pa_{p}(\phi)}{p+1}(1-A_{\alpha}),\quad D_{\alpha}=p^{k+1}(A_{\alpha}-1)-p\beta.

Proof.

From equation (
16
), we know that

\alpha F_{\alpha}=(U-\beta)(1-p^{k}V)F=UF-(p^{k}+\beta)F+\beta p^{k}VF.

Therefore,

\alpha\varpi_{\phi}(F_{\alpha})=\varpi_{\phi}(UF)-(p^{k}+\beta)\varpi_{\phi}(F)+\beta p^{k}V\times V\varpi_{\phi}(F)

Letting
A
,
B
,
C
, and
D
be as in Theorem
4.3
, and using that
\alpha\beta=p^{2k-1}
, we deduce that

(\alpha-p^{k-1})A_{\alpha}=A-(p^{k}+\beta),\quad(\alpha-p^{k-1})B_{\alpha}=B,\quad(\alpha-p^{k-1})C_{\alpha}=C,\quad(\alpha-p^{k-1})D_{\alpha}=D+\beta p^{k}.

Substituting
A
,
B
,
C
, and
D
by their expression as in Theorem
4.3
, one eventually gets the claimed formulae (see Corollary
A.2
in the Appendix for details).
∎

The rest of this section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
4.3
. The key ingredient is the following simple and yet striking relation between the operators
U
and
U\times U
via pullback.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
F\in S_{k+1}^{(2)}(N)
be as above. Then we have the following equality:

(-1)^{(p-1)/2}U\times U\varpi(F\otimes\varepsilon_{p})=U\times U\varpi(F)-\varpi(UF).

Proof.

Using the
q
-expansion, let us focus our attention on the
(n,m)
-th coefficient of
\varpi(F\otimes\varepsilon_{p})
, which is

\alpha_{n,m}\bigl(\varpi(F\otimes\varepsilon_{p}))=\sum_{r^{2}<4mn}A(n,r,m)\varepsilon_{p}(4nm-r^{2}).

By applying
U\times U
, the
(n,m)
-th coefficient becomes

\displaystyle\alpha_{n,m}\bigl(U\times U\varpi(F\otimes\varepsilon_{p}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{r^{2}<4mnp^{2}}A(pn,r,pm)\varepsilon_{p}(-r^{2})=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r^{2}<4mnp^{2}\\
p\nmid r\end{subarray}}A(pn,r,pm)=

\displaystyle=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}\sum_{r^{2}<4mnp^{2}}A(pn,r,pm)-(-1)^{(p-1)/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r^{2}<4mnp^{2}\\
p\mid r\end{subarray}}A(pn,r,pm)=

\displaystyle=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}\sum_{r^{2}<4mnp^{2}}A(pn,r,pm)-(-1)^{(p-1)/2}\sum_{r^{2}<4mn}A(pn,pr,pm).

We identify in the last expression the
(n,m)
-th coefficients of
U\times U\varpi(F)
and of
\varpi(UF)
, respectively. Therefore, we deduce that

U\times U\varpi(F\otimes\varepsilon_{p})=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}U\times U\varpi(F)-(-1)^{(p-1)/2}\varpi(UF),

which multiplying by
(-1)^{(p-1)/2}
yields the result.
∎

An immediate consequence of this proposition is the following identity, which avoids the presence of
F\otimes\varepsilon_{p}
, and is better suited for determining
\varpi(UF)
.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
F={\mathrm{SK}}_{N}(h)\in S_{k+1}^{(2)}(N)
be a Saito–Kurokawa lift. Then we have

(U\times U-p^{k-1})\varpi(UF)=(p^{k}-p^{k-1}+a_{p})U\times U\varpi(F)-p^{k}(p^{k-1}+a_{p})\varpi(F)+p^{3k-1}V\times V\varpi(F),

where
a_{p}=a_{p}(f)
.

Proof.

This follows by combining Corollary
3.6
and Proposition
4.5
. Indeed, Corollary
3.6
tells us that

UF=(p^{k}+a_{p})F-(p^{2k-1}+p^{k}a_{p})VF+p^{3k-1}V^{2}F-(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p^{k-1}F\otimes\varepsilon_{p}.

Applying pullback and then
U\times U
yields the relation

U\times U\varpi(UF)=(p^{k}+a_{p})U\times U\varpi(F)-(p^{2k-1}+p^{k}a_{p})\varpi(F)+p^{3k-1}V\times V\varpi(F)-(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p^{k-1}U\times U\varpi(F\otimes\varepsilon_{p}),

where we have used
\varpi\circ V=V\times V\varpi
. And now, Proposition
4.5
tells us that

(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p^{k-1}U\times U\varpi(F\otimes\varepsilon_{p})=U\times Up^{k-1}\varpi(F)-p^{k-1}\varpi(UF),

and hence we obtain

(U\times U-p^{k-1})\varpi(UF)=(p^{k}-p^{k-1}+a_{p})U\times U\varpi(F)-p^{k}(p^{k-1}+a_{p})\varpi(F)+p^{3k-1}V\times V\varpi(F),

which is the claimed relation.
∎

Fix now a normalised newform
\phi\in S_{k+1}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
. The
\phi\times\phi
-component of
S_{k+1}(Np)\otimes S_{k+1}(Np)
is spanned by the forms
\phi\times\phi
,
\phi\times V\phi
,

, and
V\phi\times\phi
. The following lemma, describing the action of the operator

on such a component, will be of good use for our computation below.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\phi\in S_{k+1}(N)
be a normalised eigenform. The matrix of the operator
\Xi:=U\times U-p^{k-1}
acting on the
\phi\times\phi

S_{k+1}(Np)\otimes S_{k+1}(Np)

is given by:

M_{\Xi}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{p}(\phi)^{2}-p^{k-1}&-a_{p}(\phi)p^{k}&-a_{p}(\phi)p^{k}&p^{2k}\\
a_{p}(\phi)&-p^{k-1}&-p^{k}&0\\
a_{p}(\phi)&-p^{k}&-p^{k-1}&0\\
1&0&0&-p^{k-1}\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

We know that
UV\phi=\phi
and, from equation (
7
), that
U\phi=a_{p}(\phi)\phi-p^{k}V\phi
. From this, we can easily describe the action of

on the elements of the basis for

. The result follows using that
M_{\Xi}=M_{U\times U}-p^{k-1}\mathrm{Id}_{4}
.

∎

Proof of Theorem
4.3
.

First of all, it is clear from Corollary
4.6
that
\varpi(UF)=0
if
\varpi(F)=0
, which is coherent with the statement. Thus we may assume that

from now on, and write

in terms of unknown coefficients

,

,

The proof boils down to restricting the identity in Corollary

to the

. We have

(18)

\Xi\varpi_{\phi}(UF)=(p^{k}-p^{k-1}+a_{p})U\times U\varpi_{\phi}(F)-p^{k}(p^{k-1}+a_{p})\varpi_{\phi}(F)+p^{3k-1}V\times V\varpi_{\phi}(F),

where

\lambda_{\phi}(p^{k}-p^{k-1}+a_{p})\left(a_{p}(\phi)^{2}\phi\times\phi-a_{p}(\phi)p^{k}\phi\times V\phi-a_{p}(\phi)p^{k}V\phi\times\phi+p^{2k}V\phi\times V\phi\right)

5.
p
-adic families of modular forms

Let
p
be an odd prime number. Fix algebraic closures
{\bar{\mathbf{Q}}}

, and elements

is endowed with the analytic structure induced from the natural identification

\mathcal{W}\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\Gamma,{\mathbf{C}}_{p}^{\times})

between

under (

of

for the subset of all such points.

If

is a finite flat
\Lambda
-algebra, then we write

for the set of continuous

-algebra homomorphisms from

(via the structure morphism

At last, we want to remark an example that will be of particular interest for us: if

5.1.
Hida theory for
\mathrm{GL}_{2}

Let
N\geq 1
be an integer, assume that
p\nmid N
, and fix an integer
k\geq 2
. The action of the operator
U_{p}
on the space
S_{k}(Np,{\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}})
yields Hida’s
ordinary projector

(20)

which is an idempotent in
\mathrm{End}(S_{k}(Np,{\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}}))
. The subspace of ordinary cusp forms in

It is well-known that the dimension of

is finite and independent of

for

(cf.
[
Hid93
, §7.2]
).
If

is an eigenform for

, then either

or
e_{\mathrm{ord}}\varphi=0
. In the first case,

is either

•

new at

, which can only happen when

(cf.
[
Miy06
]
) and we will exclude this case from now on, or

•

the
ordinary
p
-stabilisation
of a normalised eigenform

: that is to say,
\varphi=\phi_{\alpha}=(q-\beta V)\phi
, where

and

are the roots of the
p
-th Hecke polynomial of

(see section

), labelled so that

, hence

is a
p
-adic unit. We will say that such a

is
ordinary
at

, or just ordinary.

If

is ordinary at

on the

is characterised by the fact that

and
e_{\mathrm{ord}}\phi_{\beta}=0
.

Let now
\mathcal{R}
be a finite flat
\Lambda
-algebra. We denote by
\mathbf{S}(N,\mathcal{R})
the space of
\Lambda
-adic cusp forms of tame level
\Gamma_{0}(N)
over
\mathcal{R}
, namely the
\mathcal{R}
-module of formal power series

\boldsymbol{\phi}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbf{a}_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})q^{n}\in\mathcal{R}[[q]]

such that
\boldsymbol{\phi}(\kappa)
is the
q
-expansion of a classical modular form in
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N)\cap\Gamma_{1}(p))
, which we still denote
\boldsymbol{\phi}(\kappa)
, for every classical point
\kappa\in\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{cl}}(\mathcal{R})
of weight
\mathrm{wt}(\kappa)=k-2\geq 0
. By restricting to classical points whose weights are all contained in a suitable residue class modulo
p-1
, we may assume that all the
\boldsymbol{\phi}(\kappa)\in S_{k}(Np)
.

A
\Lambda
-adic cusp form

is said to be
ordinary
if for every classical point
\kappa\in\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{cl}}(\mathcal{R})
the specialisation
\boldsymbol{\phi}(\kappa)
is an ordinary cusp form, i.e. belongs to

. Write
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{ord}}(N,\mathcal{R})
for the subspace of ordinary
\Lambda
-adic forms in

For the purpose of this note, we may restrict ourselves to the following definition of Hida family as a family of ordinary
p
-stabilised newforms:

Definition 5.1
.

A Hida family of tame level
N
is a quadruple

•

•

•

Remark 5.2

When

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathbf{g}
be a Hida family as above and let
\mathcal{K}_{\mathbf{g}}:=\mathrm{Frac}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{g}})
. There exists a unique element
\mathcal{J}_{\mathbf{g}}:\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{ord}}(N,\mathcal{R})\to\mathcal{R}\otimes_{\Lambda}\mathcal{K}_{\mathbf{g}}
such that for every

\mathcal{J}_{\mathbf{g}}(\boldsymbol{\phi})(\kappa,\kappa^{\prime})=\frac{\langle\boldsymbol{\phi}(\kappa),\mathbf{g}(\kappa^{\prime})\rangle}{\langle\mathbf{g}(\kappa^{\prime}),\mathbf{g}(\kappa^{\prime})\rangle}

for all
(\kappa,\kappa^{\prime})\in\mathcal{U}_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}^{\mathrm{cl}}\times_{\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{cl}}}\mathcal{U}_{\mathbf{g}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
.

Proof.

This is a standard argument. Indeed,
\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{ord}}(N,\mathcal{R})\otimes\mathcal{K}_{\mathbf{g}}
is a finite-dimensional vector space over

, and Hida theory provides an idempotent

of the
\Lambda

whose specialization at a classical weight

is the usual idempotent

associated with the classical cusp form

. Therefore, the desired element

is defined by requiring that

for each

(a detailed proof in a slightly more general setting can be found in
[
DR14
, Lemma 2.19]
).
∎

Remark 5.4
.

(Mazur, Kitagawa, Greenberg–Stevens)
.

Let

5.2.
Tensor products of Hida families

Let
N
and
p
be as before, and fix an integer

. Recall Hida’s ordinary projector

acting on
S_{k+1}(Np,{\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}})

S_{k+1}(Np,{\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}})\otimes S_{k+1}(Np,{\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}})
, whose Hecke algebra contains the elements of the form
T_{1}\otimes T_{2}
with
T_{1}
,
T_{2}
in the usual Hecke algebra of level
N
and weight
k+1
. In particular, we can form the limit of
(U\otimes U)^{n!}
, which yields the idempotent
e_{\mathrm{ord}}\otimes e_{\mathrm{ord}}
, and the corresponding projector

e_{\mathrm{ord}}\otimes e_{\mathrm{ord}}:S_{k+1}(Np,\chi)\otimes S_{k+1}(Np,\chi)\to S_{k+1}^{\mathrm{ord}}(Np)\otimes S_{k+1}^{\mathrm{ord}}(Np).

By a slight abuse of notation, we will write
e_{\mathrm{ord}}
for
e_{\mathrm{ord}}\otimes e_{\mathrm{ord}}
, and it will be clear from the context which projector we are using.

Let
\phi\in S_{k+1}(N,{\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}})
be a normalised ordinary eigenform, and let
\phi_{\alpha}
,
\phi_{\beta}
denote the
p
-stabilisations of
\phi
with respect to the roots
\alpha=\alpha_{\phi}
and
\beta=\beta_{\phi}
of its
p
-th Hecke polynomial. If
\alpha
is the unit root, similarly as in the
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
-case now the ordinary projector on the

-isotypic component

is characterised by the fact that

In particular,
e_{\mathrm{ord}}\left(\left(S_{k+1}(Np,{\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}})\otimes S_{k+1}(Np,{\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}})\right)[\phi]\right)=\langle\phi_{\alpha}\times\phi_{\alpha}\rangle.

Let now
\mathcal{R}
be a finite flat
\Lambda
-algebra, and
\mathbf{S}(N,\mathcal{R})
denote the space of
\Lambda
-adic cusp forms of level

over
\mathcal{R}
as above. We can then consider the

-module
\mathbf{S}(N,\mathcal{R})\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}\mathbf{S}(N,\mathcal{R})

.

5.3.

-adic families of half-integral weight modular forms: a

-th Shintani lifting

The theory of
p
-adic families of half-integral weight modular forms is not yet developed in full generality and most known results are based on the Shintani lifting construction. We will describe a construction in a context broad enough to suite our needs. For this reason, we fix a Hida family
(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{f}},\mathcal{U}_{\mathbf{f}},\mathcal{U}_{\mathbf{f}}^{\mathrm{cl}},\mathbf{f})
of tame level
N
(and trivial tame character) as above. Recall that by definition of Hida family, there exists an integer
k_{0}
(only determined modulo
(p-1)/2
) such that every classical point
\kappa\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathbf{f}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
has weight
2k-2
with
2k\equiv 2k_{0}\pmod{p-1}
. We define

\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{f}}:=\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{f}}\otimes_{\Lambda,\sigma}\Lambda,

where
\sigma:\Lambda\to\Lambda
is the
\mathcal{O}
-algebra isomorphism induced by
[t]\mapsto[t^{2}]
on
\Gamma=1+p{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}
, and write
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{{\mathbf{f}}}:=\mathcal{W}(\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{f}})

\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{f}}
with the structure of

\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{f}}\,\longrightarrow\,\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbf{f}},\quad\alpha\,\longmapsto\,\alpha\otimes 1

-algebras, but it is
not
a homomorphism of
\Lambda

has weight

.

Fix once and for all a solution

of the congruence

\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{\mathbf{f}}:=\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{U}_{\mathbf{f}})

. Observe that this is only

.

.

Fix a discriminant

with

denotes the algebraic

-th Shintani lifting as explained in (

-adic periods

Proof.

The collection of

]
.
∎

The element

One may note that the construction seems to heavily depend on the choice of

Proposition A.1
.

Let
\phi\in S_{k+1}(N)
be a normalised eigenform, and suppose that
\varpi_{\phi}(F)=\lambda_{\phi}\phi\times\phi
. Then

\varpi_{\phi}(UF)=\lambda_{\phi}\left(A\phi\times\phi+B\phi\times V\phi+CV\phi\times\phi+DV\phi\times V\phi\right),

where

A=\frac{(p^{k-1}(p-1)+a_{p})a_{p}(\phi)^{2}-p^{k}(p+1)(p^{k-1}(p+1)+a_{p})}{a_{p}(\phi)^{2}-p^{k-1}(p+1)^{2}},

and
B
,
C
,
D
are given by the formulae

B=C=\frac{pa_{p}(\phi)}{p+1}(p^{k}-p^{k-1}+a_{p}-A),\quad D=-p^{k+1}(p^{k}+a_{p}-A).

Proof.

As indicated in the proof of Theorem
4.3
, one has to solve the system of linear equations arising from the identity (
18
). One easily checks that
C=B
, and then the system to solve becomes

\begin{cases}(p^{k}-p^{k-1}+a_{p})a_{p}(\phi)^{2}-p^{k}(p^{k-1}+a_{p})=A(a_{p}(\phi)^{2}-p^{k-1})+2Ba_{p}(\phi)+D,\\
a_{p}(\phi)p(p^{k}-p^{k-1}+a_{p})=Aa_{p}(\phi)p+B(p+1),\\
p^{k+1}(p^{k}+a_{p})=Ap^{k+1}-D.\end{cases}

From the last equation, we get

D=-p^{k+1}\left(p^{k}+a_{p}-A\right),

and from the second one,

B=\frac{pa_{p}(\phi)}{p+1}\left(p^{k}-p^{k-1}+a_{p}-A\right).

Plugging these expressions into the first equation of the above system yields

\left(p^{k-1}(p-1)+a_{p}-\frac{2p^{k+1}-2p^{k}+2pa_{p}}{p+1}\right)a_{p}(\phi)^{2}-p^{k}(1-p)(p^{k-1}(p+1)+a_{p})=

=A\left(a_{p}(\phi)^{2}+p^{k-1}(p^{2}-1)-\frac{2a_{p}(\phi)^{2}p}{p+1}\right).

Multiplying by
p+1
and dividing by
1-p
both sides of the equality, one eventually finds

A=\frac{(p^{k-1}(p-1)+a_{p})a_{p}(\phi)^{2}-p^{k}(p+1)(p^{k-1}(p+1)+a_{p})}{a_{p}(\phi)^{2}-p^{k-1}(p+1)^{2}}.

∎

Corollary A.2

Let

\beta_{\phi}

From equation (

Therefore,

Writing

vanishes if

\varpi_{\phi}(F_{\alpha})=\lambda_{\phi}\left(1-\frac{\beta_{f}}{p^{k}}\right)\left(A_{\alpha}\phi\times\phi+B_{\alpha}\phi\times V\phi+C_{\alpha}V\phi\times\phi+D_{\alpha}V\phi\times V\phi\right)

for some coefficients

,

Corollary A.3

From the definitions of

\phi=\frac{\alpha_{\phi}\phi_{\alpha}-\beta_{\phi}\phi_{\beta}}{\alpha_{\phi}-\beta_{\phi}},\qquad V\phi=\frac{\phi_{\alpha}-\phi_{\beta}}{\alpha_{\phi}-\beta_{\phi}}.

In other words, the matrix

\frac{1}{\alpha_{\phi}-\beta_{\phi}}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{\phi}&1\\

\frac{1}{(\alpha_{\phi}-\beta_{\phi})^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{\phi}^{2}&\alpha_{\phi}&\alpha_{\phi}&1\\
-\alpha_{\phi}\beta_{\phi}&-\alpha_{\phi}&-\beta_{\phi}&-1\\
-\alpha_{\phi}\beta_{\phi}&-\beta_{\phi}&-\alpha_{\phi}&-1\\
\beta_{\phi}^{2}&\beta_{\phi}&\beta_{\phi}&1\end{pmatrix}

gives the change on

from the basis

to the basis
\{\phi_{\alpha}\times\phi_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha}\times\phi_{\beta},\phi_{\beta}\times\phi_{\alpha},\phi_{\beta}\times\phi_{\beta}\}

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}_{\mathrm{ord}}(\varpi_{\phi}(F_{\alpha}))=\frac{\lambda_{\phi}\left(1-\frac{\beta_{f}}{p^{k}}\right)}{\left(1-\frac{\beta_{\phi}}{\alpha_{\phi}}\right)^{2}}(A_{\alpha}+B_{\alpha}\alpha_{\phi}^{-1}+C_{\alpha}\alpha_{\phi}^{-1}+D_{\alpha}\alpha_{\phi}^{-2})\phi_{\alpha}\times\phi_{\alpha},

B_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{\phi}\left(1-\frac{\beta_{f}}{p^{k}}\right)}{\left(1-\frac{\beta_{\phi}}{\alpha_{\phi}}\right)^{2}}\left((A_{\alpha}-1)\left(1-\frac{2pa_{p}(\phi)}{\alpha_{\phi}(p+1)}+\frac{p\beta_{\phi}}{\alpha_{\phi}}\right)+1-\frac{p\beta_{f}}{\alpha^{2}}\right)\phi_{\alpha}\times\phi_{\alpha}=

Upper and lower bounds of convergence rates for strong solutions of the generalized Newtonian fluids with non-standard growth conditions
Jae-Myoung Kim and Seungchan Ko

Abstract
We consider the motion of an incompressible shear-thickening power-law-like non-Newtonian fluid in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} with a variable power-law index. This system of nonlinear partial differential equations arises in mathematical models of electrorheological fluids. The aim of this paper is to investigate the large-time behaviour of the difference 𝒖 − 𝒖 ~ \boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{\tilde{u}} where 𝒖 \boldsymbol{u} is a strong solution of the given equations with the initial data 𝒖 0 \boldsymbol{u}_{0} and 𝒖 ~ \boldsymbol{\tilde{u}} is the strong solution of the same equations with perturbed initial data 𝒖 0 + 𝒘 0 \boldsymbol{u}_{0}+\boldsymbol{w}_{0} . The initial perturbation 𝒘 0 \boldsymbol{w}_{0} is not required to be small, but is assumed to satisfy certain decay condition. In particular, we can show that ( 1 + t ) − γ 2 ≲ ‖ 𝒖 ~ ​ ( t ) − 𝒖 ⁡ ( t ) ‖ 2 ≲ ( 1 + t ) − γ 2 , (1+t)^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{\tilde{u}}(t)-\boldsymbol{u}(t)\|_{2}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}, for sufficiently large t > 0 t>0 , where γ ∈ ( 2 , 5 2 ) \gamma\in(2,\frac{5}{2}) . The proof is based on the observation that the solution of the linear heat equation describes the asymptotic behaviour of the solutions of the electrorheological fluids well for sufficiently large time t > 0 t>0 , and the generalized Fourier splitting method with an iterative argument. Furthermore, it will also be discussed that the argument used in the present paper can improve the previous results for the generalized Newtonian fluids with a constant power-law index.

Introduction

In this paper, we aim to investigate the large-time behaviour for solutions of a system of nonlinear partial differential equations (PDEs) describing the rheological response of electrorheological fluids. The electrorheological fluids, which have recently gained increasing attention, are a special type of smart fluids of high technological interest and are characterized by their ability to change their rheological properties when in presence of an surrounding electromagnetic field: When it is disposed to an electro-magnetic field, the viscosity exhibits a significant change. For example, there exist some types of electrorheological fluids whose viscosity varies by a factor of
1000
as a response to the application of an electromagnetic field within 1ms. This property can be exploited in a wide range of scientific and industrial applications,
including for instance, shock absorbers, clutches and actuators.

In the present paper, we shall consider the reduced model for the incompressible electrorheological fluids consisting of the following system of PDEs:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}+(\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}-{\rm{div}}\,\boldsymbol{S}(\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{u})

\displaystyle=-\nabla\pi+\boldsymbol{f}\qquad

\displaystyle{\rm{in}}\,\,Q_{T}=(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

(1.1)

\displaystyle{\rm{div}}\,\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle{\rm{in}}\,\,Q_{T}=(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

(1.2)

where the Cauchy stress tensor is of the form

\boldsymbol{S}(\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{u})=(1+|\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{u}|^{2})^{\frac{p(t,x)-2}{2}}\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{u}.

(1.3)

In fact, the exponent
p(\cdot)
depends on the magnitude of the electric field
|\boldsymbol{E}|
. Since the electric field itself is a solution to the quasi–static Maxwell equations and is not influenced by the motion of the fluids, we may consider
p(\cdot)

)-(

In the above system of equations,

,

\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{u}
is the symmetric velocity gradient, i.e.
\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{u}=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}+(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})^{T})
. Here we prescribe the initial condition

\boldsymbol{u}(0,x)=\boldsymbol{u}_{0}(x)\quad{\rm{in}}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(1.4)

Such electrorheological models were studied in
[
20
,
21
]
, where the mathematical modelling, existence theory and numerical approximation were discussed.

In this paper, we shall investigate the large-time behaviour of the solutions for the model (
1.1
)-(
1.3
). Because of its importance in mathematics and physics, the large-time behaviour of viscous incompressible fluids has attracted more attention and has been investigated in many works. For the classical Navier–Stokes equations, there has been an extensive literature; see, e.g.,

\displaystyle=0\qquad

Lemma 2.2

For any positive continuous functions

Suppose that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|f(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\,{\rm{d}}\xi.

Lemma 2.4
.

For any real number

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{0}(r\omega)|^{2}\,{\rm{d}}\omega=Cr^{2\gamma-d}+o(r^{2\gamma-d})\quad{\rm{as}}\,\,r\rightarrow 0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}-\Delta\boldsymbol{u}

Now, as we deal with the variable power-law index, we need to consider the variable-exponent Lebesgue space. We write a set of all measurable functions
p:\Omega\rightarrow[1,\infty)
by
\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
and we shall call
p\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
a variable exponent. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open set and
p\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
. We then define
p^{-}={\rm{ess}}\,\inf_{x\in\Omega}p(x)
and
p^{+}={\rm{ess}}\,\sup_{x\in\Omega}p(x).
Also, for each measurable functions
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we define the modular
|f|_{p(\cdot)}
of

by

|f|_{p(\cdot)}=\int_{\Omega}|f(x)|^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Then we define the following variable-exponent Lebesgue spaces

L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left\{u\in L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\Omega):|u|_{p(\cdot)}<\infty\right\},

with the corresponding Luxembourg norms

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\bigg|\frac{u(x)}{\lambda}\bigg|_{p(\cdot)}\leq 1\right\}

If we assume that
1<p^{-}\leq p^{+}<\infty
, it is well-known that the variable-exponent space
L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is a reflexive, separable Banach space. We assume that
1<p^{-}\leq p^{+}<\infty
throughout the present paper.

In order to exploit various results from the theory of classical Lebesgue space, some regularity of the exponent
p(\cdot)
is needed: log
-Hölder continuity
.

Definition 2.5
.

We say a function
p:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is

-Hölder continuous
on
\Omega
if there exists a positive constant
C_{1}>0
satisfying for all

|p(x)-p(y)|\leq\frac{C_{1}}{{\rm{log}}(e+1/|x-y|)}.

(2.2)

Furthermore, we call that
p
satisfies the log
-Hölder decay condition
if there exist some constants
p_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}
and
C_{2}>0
such that for all
x\in\Omega,

|p(x)-p_{\infty}|\leq\frac{C_{2}}{{\rm{log}}(e+|x|)}.

(2.3)

We call that
p
is
globally
log
-Hölder continuous
in
\Omega
if both (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) hold. We say

a log-
Hölder constant
of

.

Definition 2.6
.

We define the family of log-Hölder continuous variable-exponent functions:

\mathcal{P}^{\rm{log}}(\Omega)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left\{p\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega):\frac{1}{p}\,\,{\rm{is}}\,\,{\rm{globally}}\,\,{\textrm{log-H\"{o}lder}}\,\,{\rm{continuous}}\right\}.

If
\Omega
is unbounded, we define

by
\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{p(x)}
.

Here we note that, since

is a bilipschitz mapping from

[
3

. Then we have

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{S}(\boldsymbol{B})-\boldsymbol{S}(\boldsymbol{C})):(\boldsymbol{B}-\boldsymbol{C})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\left(\boldsymbol{S}(\boldsymbol{B})-\boldsymbol{S}(\boldsymbol{C})\right)_{ij}(\boldsymbol{B}-\boldsymbol{C})_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k,\ell}\int^{1}_{0}\partial_{k\ell}\left(\boldsymbol{M}(s)_{ij}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{B}-\boldsymbol{C})_{ij}(\boldsymbol{B}-\boldsymbol{C})_{k\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k,\ell}\int^{1}_{0}\left((p(x)-2)(1+|\boldsymbol{M}|^{2})^{\frac{p(x)-4}{2}}\boldsymbol{M}_{k\ell}\boldsymbol{M}_{ij}+(1+|\boldsymbol{M}|^{2})^{\frac{p(x)-2}{2}}\partial_{k\ell}\boldsymbol{M}_{ij}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{B}-\boldsymbol{C})_{ij}(\boldsymbol{B}-\boldsymbol{C})_{k\ell}

\displaystyle=\int^{1}_{0}\left((p(x)-2)(1+|\boldsymbol{M}|^{2})^{\frac{p(x)-4}{2}}|\boldsymbol{M}:(\boldsymbol{B}-\boldsymbol{C})|^{2}+(1+|\boldsymbol{M}|^{2})^{\frac{p(x)-2}{2}}|\boldsymbol{B}-\boldsymbol{C}|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\geq\int^{1}_{0}(1+|\boldsymbol{M}|^{2})^{\frac{p(x)-2}{2}}|\boldsymbol{B}-\boldsymbol{C}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\geq|\boldsymbol{B}-\boldsymbol{C}|^{2}.

Therefore, we conclude that for all
\boldsymbol{B},\boldsymbol{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm{sym}}
, we have

\left(\boldsymbol{G}(\boldsymbol{B})-\boldsymbol{G}(\boldsymbol{C})\right):(\boldsymbol{B}-\boldsymbol{C})=\left(\boldsymbol{S}(\boldsymbol{B})-\boldsymbol{S}(\boldsymbol{C})\right):(\boldsymbol{B}-\boldsymbol{C})-|\boldsymbol{B}-\boldsymbol{C}|^{2}\geq 0,

which implies the monotonicity of
\boldsymbol{G}
.
∎

Next, we shall derive an

. Though the condition required in our main theorem is

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
p^{-}\geq\frac{11}{5}
and
\boldsymbol{v}

and

such that the following inequality holds for all

•

(Case

)

p^{-}\geq 3

•

(Case

\frac{11}{5}\leq p^{-}<3

where

and
\beta=\frac{5p^{-}-11}{4}

Proof.

By observing the inequality
(1+s)^{\alpha}-1\lesssim s+s^{\alpha}
for
s\geq 0
and
\alpha>0
, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\boldsymbol{G}(\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{v})|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left((1+|\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{v}|)^{p(x)-2}-1\right)|\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{v}|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{u}|+|\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{u}|^{p(x)-2}\right)|\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{u}|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{v}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{v}|^{p(x)-1}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{I}_{p}(\boldsymbol{v})+\int_{\{|\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{v}|\geq 1\}}|\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{v}|^{p(x)-1}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\{|\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{v}|<1\}}|\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{v}|^{p(x)-1}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{I}_{p}(\boldsymbol{v})+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{v}|^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{v}|^{p^{-}-1}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{I}_{p}(\boldsymbol{v})+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{v}|^{p^{-}-1}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Now it is enough to estimate the second integral on the right-hand side. For the case
p^{-}\geq 3
, by (
2.5
) and the interpolation inequality,

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}(s)\|^{p^{-}-1}_{p^{-}-1}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{t}_{0}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}(s)\|_{2}^{\frac{2}{p^{-}-2}}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}(s)\|^{\frac{p^{-}(p^{-}-3)}{p^{-}-2}}_{p^{-}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{L^{2}((0,T);L^{2})}^{\frac{2}{p^{-}-2}}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{L^{p^{-}}((0,T);L^{p^{-}})}^{\frac{p^{-}(p^{-}-3)}{p^{-}-2}}<\infty.

For the case of
\frac{11}{5}\leq p^{-}<3
, by (
2.6
) and Gagliardo–Nirenberg interpolation inequality (see, for example,
[
11
]
)

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}(s)\|^{p^{-}-1}_{p^{-}-1}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{t}_{0}\|\boldsymbol{v}(s)\|_{2}^{\alpha}\|\nabla^{2}\boldsymbol{v}(s)\|_{2}^{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int^{t}_{0}\|\boldsymbol{v}(s)\|_{2}^{\frac{2\alpha}{2-\beta}}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{2-\beta}{2}}\left(\int^{t}_{0}\|\nabla^{2}\boldsymbol{v}(s)\|_{2}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int^{t}_{0}\|\boldsymbol{v}(s)\|_{2}^{\frac{2\alpha}{2-\beta}}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{2-\beta}{2}},

where
\alpha=\frac{7-p^{-}}{4}
and
\beta=\frac{5p^{-}-11}{4}
, which completes the proof.
∎

•

Proof.

Taking the Fourier transformation on (

\big|\widehat{\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{G}(\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{\tilde{u}})}(t,\xi)\big|=\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{G}(\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{\tilde{u}})\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg|\leq|\xi|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\boldsymbol{G}(\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{\tilde{u}})|\,\mathrm{d}x,

and similarly we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we have concerned with the upper and lower bounds of convergence rates for the strong solutions of the
3
D generalized Newtonian fluids with variable power-law index. This mathematical model describes the rheological behaviour of an incompressible electrorheological fluid. In specific, we have obtained the following decay estimates: if the initial perturbation satisfies (
2.1
) with
2<\gamma<\frac{5}{2}
, we have

(1+t)^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{\tilde{u}}(t)-\boldsymbol{u}(t)\|_{2}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}},

for sufficiently large
t>0
. These estimates can be regarded as optimal in the sense that they coincide with those of the linear heat equations. The ideas of the proof include the comparison of the convergence rate for the given model with the one of heat equations for
t>0
sufficiently large, together with the Fourier splitting methods and iterative process. In order to prove the main estimates we assumed that
p^{-}\geq\frac{17}{7}
which was required to ensure that the itereation process reached to the optimal rate. Note, however, we still have a possibility to relax this condition and obtain the similar result with the mild restriction
p^{-}\geq\frac{11}{5}
, as presented in
[
15
]
where optimal algebraic decay rate of the strong solutions of the model under consideration was obtained provided that
p^{-}\geq\frac{11}{5}
. We leave it as further consideration.

In fact, the argument used in the present paper can improve the existing results for the model with a constant-power-law index. For example, in
[
27
]
, the authors established the same result for the bipolar generalized Navier–Stokes equations with fixed power-law index provided that
p\geq 3
. If we apply the same method presented in this paper for the case of
\frac{17}{7}\leq p<3
, it is straightforward that we can extend the result in
[
27
]
to the case of
p\geq\frac{17}{7}
.

Another interesting future research direction is to derive the similar bounds of convergence rates for the derivative of the strong solutions. In
[
15
]
, the optimal
L^{2}
decay rate for the gradient of the strong solutions was also obtained. By employing suitable modifications of the arguments used in
[
15
]
, we expect to obtain the following upper and lower bounds of convergence rates:

(1+t)^{-\frac{1+\gamma}{2}}\lesssim\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\tilde{u}}(t)-\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(t)\|_{2}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{1+\gamma}{2}},

which will be addressed in the forthcoming paper.

Double diffusion structure of logarithmically damped wave equations with a small parameter Thanks: ikehatar@hiroshima-u.ac.jp
Alessandra Piske, Ruy Coimbra Charão

Abstract
We consider a wave equation with a nonlocal logarithmic damping depending on a small parameter 0 < θ < 1 2 0<\theta<\frac{1}{2} . This research is a counter part of that was initiated by Charão-D’Abbicco-Ikehata considered in [ 5 ] for the large parameter case θ > 1 2 \theta>\frac{1}{2} . We study the Cauchy problem for this model in 𝐑 n {\bf R}^{n} to the case θ ∈ ( 0 , 1 2 ) \theta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}) , and we obtain an asymptotic profile and optimal estimates in time of solutions as t → ∞ t\to\infty in L 2 L^{2} -sense. An important discovery in this research is that in the case when n = 1 n=1 , we can present a threshold θ ∗ = 1 4 \theta^{*}=\frac{1}{4} of the parameter θ ∈ ( 0 , 1 2 ) \theta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}) such that the solution of the Cauchy problem decays with some optimal rate for θ ∈ ( 0 , θ ∗ ) \theta\in(0,\theta^{*}) as t → ∞ t\to\infty , while the L 2 L^{2} -norm of the corresponding solution never decays for θ ∈ [ θ ∗ , 1 2 ) \theta\in[\theta^{*},\frac{1}{2}) , and in particular, in the case θ ∈ [ θ ∗ , 1 2 ) \theta\in[\theta^{*},\frac{1}{2}) it shows an infinite time L 2 L^{2} -blow up of the corresponding solutions. The former (i.e., θ ∈ ( 0 , θ ∗ ) \theta\in(0,\theta^{*}) case) indicates an usual diffusion phenomenon, while the latter (i.e., θ ∈ [ θ ∗ , 1 2 ) \theta\in[\theta^{*},\frac{1}{2}) case) implies, so to speak, a singular diffusion phenomenon. Such a singular diffusion in the one dimensional case is a quite novel phenomenon discovered through our new model produced by logarithmic damping with a small parameter θ \theta . It might be already prepared in the usual structural damping case such as ( − Δ ) θ ​ u t (-\Delta)^{\theta}u_{t} with θ ∈ ( 0 , 1 / 2 ) \theta\in(0,1/2) , however unfortunately nobody has ever just pointed out even in the structural damping case.

We consider in this work the dissipative wave equation based on an operator
L_{\theta}
, that combines the composition of logarithm function with the Laplace operator, as follows:

\displaystyle u_{tt}-\Delta u+L_{\theta}u_{t}=0,\ \ \ (t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\bf R}^{n},

(1.1)

\displaystyle u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\ \ \ x\in{\bf R}^{n},

(1.2)

where the linear operator

L_{\theta}:D(L)\subset L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})\to L^{2}({\bf R}^{n}),\quad\theta>0,

is defined as follows:

D(L_{\theta}):=\left\{f\in L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})\,\bigm|\,\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}(\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta}))^{2}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi<+\infty\right\},

and for
f\in D(L_{\theta})
,

(L_{\theta}f)(x):={\cal F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}\left(\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})\hat{f}(\xi)\right)(x).

Here, one has just denoted the Fourier transform
{\cal F}_{x\to\xi}(f)(\xi)
of
f(x)
by

{\cal F}_{x\to\xi}(f)(\xi)=\hat{f}(\xi):=\displaystyle{\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}f(x)dx,\quad\xi\in{\bf R}^{n},

as usual with
i:=\sqrt{-1}
, and
{\cal F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}
expresses its inverse Fourier transform.
Since the operator
L_{\theta}
is non-negative and self-adjoint in
L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
(see
[
6
]
), the square root

L_{\theta}^{1/2}:D(L_{\theta}^{1/2})\subset L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})\to L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})

can be defined, and is also nonnegative and self-adjoint with its domain

D(L_{\theta}^{1/2})=\left\{f\in L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})\,\bigm|\,\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi<+\infty\right\}.

Note that
D(L_{\theta}^{1/2})
becomes Hilbert space with its graph norm

\|v\|_{D(L_{\theta}^{1/2})}:=\left(\|v\|^{2}+\|L_{\theta}^{1/2}v\|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

where to simplify the notation we define the
L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
-norm by

\|\cdot\|:=\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})}.

We also note that

H^{s}({\bf R}^{n})\hookrightarrow D(L_{\theta}^{1/2})\hookrightarrow L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})

for any
s>0
. Symbolically writing, one can see

L_{\theta}=\log(I+(-\Delta)^{\theta}),

where
\Delta
is the usual Laplace operator defined on
H^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
.
Since
L_{\theta}
is constructed by a nonnegative-valued multiplication operator, it is nonnegative and self-adjoint in
L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
. Then, by a similar argument to
[
29
, Proposition 2.1]
based on the Lumer-Phillips Theorem one can find that the problem (1.1)-(1.2) has a unique mild solution

u\in C([0,\infty);H^{1}({\bf R}^{n}))\cap C^{1}([0,\infty);L^{2}({\bf R}^{n}))

for each
[u_{0},u_{1}]\in H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})\times L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
, and the associated energy identity holds

E_{u}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\|L_{\theta}^{1/2}u_{t}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds=E_{u}(0),\quad t>0,

(1.3)

where

E_{u}(t):=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

The identity (
1.3
) implies that the total energy is a non increasing function in time because of the existence of the dissipative term
L_{\theta}u_{t}
.

Here, we mention an answer to the question: why do we study the wave equation with a log-damping term in a series of our papers? This comes from an observation below.

At first, notice that the properties of the equation such that

u_{tt}-\Delta u+(-\Delta)^{\theta}u_{t}=0

(a)

(b)

1.8

By comparing the equations (

\varphi(t,\xi):=\frac{e^{-\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})}t}}{\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})}P_{1}-\frac{e^{-\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})t}}{\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})}P_{1}=:P_{1}\left(\varphi_{1}(t,\xi)-\varphi_{2}(t,\xi)\right),

Theorem 1.1

(I).

Let

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)-{\cal F}_{\xi\rightarrow x}^{-1}(\varphi(t,\xi))(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left\{\begin{matrix}C(\|u_{1}\|_{1}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{1,2\theta}})\left(t^{-\frac{1}{4(1-\theta)}}+\displaystyle{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\theta}}}t^{-\frac{1}{4\theta}}\right),&\text{ if }0<\theta\leq\frac{1}{6},\\

(II).

Let

3
Asymptotic profile

As mentioned in the introduction, our main interest in this work is to investigate the problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) for the case of
0<\theta<1/2
in order to compensate the research in
[
5
]
studying the case of
\theta>1/2
.

The associated problem to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) in Fourier space is the following

\displaystyle\hat{u}_{tt}+\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})\hat{u}_{t}+|\xi|^{2}\hat{u}=0,\ \ \ t>0,\quad\xi\in{\bf R}^{n},

(3.1)

\displaystyle\hat{u}(0,\xi)=0,\quad\hat{u}_{t}(0,\xi)=\hat{u}_{1}(\xi),\ \ \ \xi\in{\bf R}^{n},

(3.2)

where the associated characteristic polynomial is

\lambda^{2}+\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})\lambda+|\xi|^{2}=0.

The characteristics roots are expressed as

\lambda_{\pm}=\frac{-\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})\pm\sqrt{\log^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})-4|\xi|^{2}}}{2},\quad\xi\in{\bf R}^{n}.

(3.3)

Lemma 3.1

There exists
\delta=\delta(\theta)
,
0<\delta<1
such that

\displaystyle\log^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})-4|\xi|^{2}\geq 0\text{ for }|\xi|\leq\delta,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\log^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})-4|\xi|^{2}<0\text{ for }|\xi|>\delta.

(3.5)

Proof.
Working with
r=|\xi|
, we first observe that
\log(1+r^{2})<2r
for all
r>0
. Also,
r^{2\theta}\leq r^{2}
for
r\geq 1
, since
\theta\leq 1
. Therefore, in the case
\theta<\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}
, one has

\log(1+r^{2\theta})\leq\log(1+r^{2})<2r

(3.6)

for all
r\geq 1
. Thus we may conclude that the function
f(r):=\log(1+r^{2\theta})-2r
is negative for all
r\geq 1
. However, the similar phenomena does not happen near the origin. In fact, we first notice that

\lim_{r\rightarrow+0}\frac{\log(1+r^{2\theta})}{r}=\infty,

for
\theta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. Therefore, there exists
r_{0}=r_{0}(\theta)<1
such that

\frac{\log(1+r^{2\theta})}{r}>2

for all
r\in{\bf R}^{n}
satisfying
0<r<r_{0}
. Then,
f(r)=\log(1+r^{2\theta})-2r\geq 0
for
0\leq r<r_{0}
. Furthermore, for
r\geq 0
one can get

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\theta r^{2\theta-2}\left[(2\theta-1)(1+r^{2\theta})-2\theta r^{2\theta}\right]}{(1+r^{2\theta})^{2}}=\frac{2\theta r^{2\theta-2}\left[2\theta-1-r^{2\theta}\right]}{(1+r^{2\theta})^{2}}.

Since
0<\theta<\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}
, the function
f:[0,\infty)\rightarrow{\bf R}
satisfies
f^{\prime\prime}(r)<0
. Due to
f(0)=0
and (
3.6
) one can conclude that there exists a unique number
\delta=\delta(\theta)
,
0<\delta<1
such that
f(r)\geq 0
for all
0\leq r\leq\delta
and
f(r)\leq 0
for all
r\geq\delta
. Finally, one can write

\log^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})-4|\xi|^{2}=f(|\xi|)\left(\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})+2|\xi|\right).

Therefore, using the properties of the function
f(r)=f(|\xi|)
one can obtain the desired statement.

\Box

By Lemma
3.1
, we see that that the characteristics roots (
3.3
) are real-valued for
|\xi|\leq\delta
, and complex-valued for
|\xi|>\delta
. This is a crucial different point from that observed in the case of
1/2\leq\theta
.

3.1
Estimates on the region
|\xi|\leq\delta

First part of this section we analyze the behavior of the characteristics roots near the origin
\xi=0
. To do that we need some remarks and lemmas.

Remark 3.1

For
q\geq 0
it is easy to check the inequality
\frac{1}{2}r^{q}\leq\log(1+r^{q})\leq r^{q}
for
r\in[0,1]
.

In particular, for
0<\theta<\frac{1}{2}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|\xi|^{2\theta}\leq\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})\leq\frac{3}{2}|\xi|^{2\theta},

(3.7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|\xi|^{2}\leq\log(1+|\xi|^{2})\leq\frac{3}{2}|\xi|^{2},

(3.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|\xi|^{2-2\theta}\leq\log(1+|\xi|^{2-2\theta})\leq\frac{3}{2}|\xi|^{2-2\theta}

(3.9)

for
|\xi|\leq 1
.

We note that for
0\leq\theta<1/2
it holds that

\lim_{r\rightarrow+0}\frac{r^{4-4\theta}}{r^{2}}=0.

Thus, there exists
\delta_{1}=\delta_{1}(\theta)
,
0<\delta_{1}<1
that satisfies

\frac{|\xi|^{4-4\theta}}{|\xi|^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{25}

(3.10)

whenever
0<|\xi|\leq\delta_{1}
. Moreover, one can choose
\delta_{1}\in(0,1)
such as
\delta_{1}<\delta
. In fact, from (
3.10
) one has

25|\xi|^{2}\leq|\xi|^{4\theta}

for
0\leq|\xi|\leq\delta_{1}
. In this region it also holds
|\xi|^{4\theta}\leq 4\log^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})
, due to (
3.7
).
Thus

\log^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})\geq\frac{25}{4}|\xi|^{2}\geq\frac{16}{3}|\xi|^{2}\geq 4|\xi|^{2}

(3.11)

for
|\xi|\leq\delta_{1}.
Comparing (
3.11
) with (
3.4
), we may conclude that
\delta_{1}<\delta
. From (
3.11
) we also obtain

\log^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})\geq\frac{16}{3}|\xi|^{2}

whenever
|\xi|\leq\delta_{1}
.

Now we define a new number:

\eta:=\sup\{\alpha>0;\frac{|\xi|^{4-4\theta}}{|\xi|^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{25}\text{ for }0<|\xi|\leq\alpha\}.

(3.12)

We note that
\eta
is positive and is well defined, because the set
\{\alpha>0;\frac{|\xi|^{4-4\theta}}{|\xi|^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{25}\text{ for }|\xi|\leq\alpha\}
is not empty (
\delta_{1}
is a member of this set) and is bounded from above. In fact, for example,
1
is an upper bound for this set, and
\eta<\delta<1
with
\delta
defined in Lemma
3.1
. In particular, the following two properties are true for
|\xi|\leq\eta
:

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\log^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})\geq 4|\xi|^{2},

(3.13)

\displaystyle 25|\xi|^{4-4\theta}\leq|\xi|^{2}.

(3.14)

Lemma 3.2

Let
\eta
be the number defined by (
3.12
). Then, for
|\xi|\leq\eta
it holds that

(i).

\lambda_{+}-\lambda_{-}\approx\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta});

(ii).

\lambda_{+}\approx-\log(1+|\xi|^{2-2\theta})\approx-|\xi|^{2-2\theta};

(iii).

\lambda_{-}\approx-\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta}).

Proof.

(i) The upper estimate is simple because for
|\xi|\leq\eta<\delta
it holds that

\lambda_{+}-\lambda_{-}=\sqrt{\log^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})-4|\xi|^{2}}\leq\sqrt{\log^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})}=\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta}).

On the other hand, by (
3.13
) we have

\frac{1}{4}\log^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})\leq\log^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})-4|\xi|^{2},\quad|\xi|\leq\eta.

For this reason, in the zone
|\xi|\leq\eta
it holds that

\frac{1}{2}\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})\leq\sqrt{\log^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})-4|\xi|^{2}}.

(ii). The inequality (
3.14
) provides us to get

0\geq 25|\xi|^{4-4\theta}-5|\xi|^{2}+4|\xi|^{2}=25|\xi|^{4-4\theta}-5|\xi|^{2\theta}|\xi|^{2-2\theta}+4|\xi|^{2}.

(3.15)

The lower inequality in (
3.7
) implies that
-10\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})\leq-5|\xi|^{2\theta}
for
|\xi|\leq 1
and in particular for
|\xi|\leq\eta
. By combining this fact with (
3.15
), we obtain

25|\xi|^{4-4\theta}-10\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})|\xi|^{2-2\theta}+4|\xi|^{2}\leq 0,\quad|\xi|\leq\eta.

Adding
\log^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})
on both sides we may obtain

\left(\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})-5|\xi|^{2-2\theta}\right)^{2}=\log^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})-10\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})|\xi|^{2-2\theta}+25|\xi|^{4-4\theta}\leq\log^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})-4|\xi|^{2}.

Hence, for
|\xi|\leq\eta
,
\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})-5|\xi|^{2-2\theta}\leq\sqrt{\log^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})-4|\xi|^{2}}
and

-\frac{5}{2}|\xi|^{2-2\theta}\leq\frac{-\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})+\sqrt{\log^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})-4|\xi|^{2}}}{2}=\lambda_{+}.

Furthermore, we also concludes that

-5\log(1+|\xi|^{2-2\theta})\leq-\frac{5}{2}|\xi|^{2-2\theta}\leq\lambda_{+}

(3.16)

on the zone
|\xi|\leq\eta
, due to (
3.9
).

In order to prove the upper estimate part of (ii) we first observe that

0\leq|\xi|^{2}+|\xi|^{4-4\theta}=4|\xi|^{2}+|\xi|^{4-4\theta}-3|\xi|^{2\theta}|\xi|^{2-2\theta}.

In the zone
|\xi|\leq\eta
it holds that
-3|\xi|^{2\theta}\leq-2\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})
by (
3.7
), which implies that

-3|\xi|^{2\theta}|\xi|^{2-2\theta}\leq-2\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})|\xi|^{2-2\theta}.

By using the inequality just above we may obtain that

0\leq 4|\xi|^{2}+|\xi|^{4-4\theta}-2\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})|\xi|^{2-2\theta}.

(3.17)

We add
\log^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})
in both side of (
3.17
) in order to get the following estimate:

\log^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})-4|\xi|^{2}\leq\log^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})-2\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})|\xi|^{2-2\theta}+|\xi|^{4-4\theta}=\left(\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})-|\xi|^{2-2\theta}\right)^{2}.

This implies

\lambda_{+}=\frac{-\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})+\sqrt{\log^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})-4|\xi|^{2}}}{2}\leq-\frac{1}{2}|\xi|^{2-2\theta}.

(3.18)

When one derives (
3.18
), one must check the fact that
\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})-|\xi|^{2-2\theta}\geq 0
on
|\xi|\leq\eta
. Indeed, this can be easily observed by a combination of (
3.13
) and (
3.14
).

Now, by combining inequalities (
3.18
) and (
3.9
) one obtain

\lambda_{+}\leq-\frac{1}{2}|\xi|^{2-2\theta}\leq-\frac{1}{3}\log(1+|\xi|^{2-2\theta})

because of
|\xi|\leq\eta
. The inequalities just above and (
3.16
) imply the desired statement of item (ii).

(iii). In the course of the proof of item (i) in the region
|\xi|\leq\eta
, we also have

-\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})\leq-\sqrt{\log^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})-4|\xi|^{2}}\leq-\frac{1}{2}\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta}).

Therefore, one can easily conclude that

-\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})\leq\frac{-\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})-\sqrt{\log^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})-4|\xi|^{2}}}{2}\leq-\frac{3}{4}\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta}),\quad|\xi|\leq\eta.

This implies the desired statement of item (iii).

3.1.1
Estimates on the low-frequency zone
|\xi|\leq\eta^{3}

Throughout this paper we assume the initial amplitude
u_{0}
satisfies
u_{0}=0
, without loss of generality in order to investigate the asymptotic profiles of solutions.

We first remember the number
\eta\in(0,\delta)
defined in (
3.7
)-(
3.14
). Also, since
0<\eta<1
, we have
\eta^{3}<\eta
. In the zone of low frequency
|\xi|\leq\eta^{3}<\eta
, the characteristics roots
\lambda_{\pm}
are real, and the solution of (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) is explicitly given by

\hat{u}(t,\xi)=\frac{e^{t\lambda_{+}}-e^{t\lambda_{-}}}{\lambda_{+}-\lambda_{-}}\hat{u}_{1}(\xi).

(3.19)

The purpose in this section is to get an asymptotic profile to the solution
\hat{u}(t,\xi)
, and in order to do that we need to obtain useful estimates. For this reason, we defined a function
g:[0,\delta]\rightarrow{\bf R}
inspired by an idea from
[

, as follows. A discovery of this function
g(s)
is one of decisive points in our proof.

\displaystyle g(s):=\left\{\begin{matrix}1+\sqrt{1-\frac{4s^{6}}{\log^{2}(1+s^{6\theta})}}&\text{ if }0<s\leq\delta\\
2&\text{ if }s=0.\end{matrix}\right.

(3.20)

Note that for
0<\theta<1/2
,

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{s^{6}}{\log^{2}(1+s^{6\theta})}=0.

Remark 3.2

Let
t>0
and
\xi\in{\bf R}^{n}
,
0<|\xi|\leq\eta
, be fixed. We recall that
\eta<\delta<1
. Let us consider the function
h(s)
defined on

as follows:

\displaystyle h(s):=e^{-\frac{t\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})}{2}g(s)}.

We see that
h(s)
is differentiable on
(0,\eta)
. Then, it should be noted that one can apply the mean value theorem in the interval
[0,s]
for each

to get

\displaystyle\frac{h(s)-h(0)}{s}=\frac{e^{-\frac{t\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})}{2}g(s)}-e^{-\frac{t\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})}{2}g(0)}}{s}=-\frac{t\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})}{2}e^{-\frac{t\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})}{2}g(\alpha s)}g^{\prime}(\alpha s)

(3.21)

with some
\alpha=\alpha(s,t,|\xi|)\in(0,1)
.

We observe that on the low frequency zone

it holds that

\lambda_{-}=-\frac{\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})}{2}g(\sqrt[3]{|\xi|}).

By applying (
3.21
) for

and

,
0<|\xi|\leq\eta^{3}
, we have

e^{t\lambda_{-}}=e^{-t\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})}-\frac{t}{2}\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})\sqrt[3]{|\xi|}e^{-\frac{t\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})}{2}g(\alpha\sqrt[3]{|\xi|})}g^{\prime}(\alpha\sqrt[3]{|\xi|})

(3.22)

with
\alpha:=\alpha(s,t,|\xi|)=\alpha(t,|\xi|)\in(0,1)
.

From the Chill-Haraux
[
12
]
idea, we also observe that

\lambda_{+}=-\frac{\lambda_{+}^{2}+|\xi|^{2}}{\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})},

so that one has

\displaystyle e^{t\lambda_{+}}=e^{-\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})}t}e^{-\frac{\lambda_{+}^{2}}{\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})}t}.

(3.23)

On the other hand, because of (
3.3
) we see that

\frac{1}{\lambda_{+}-\lambda_{-}}=\frac{1}{\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})}+R(|\xi|)

where

R(r)=\frac{4r^{2}}{\log^{3}(1+r^{2\theta})\sqrt{1-\frac{4r^{2}}{\log^{2}(1+r^{2\theta})}}\left(1+\sqrt{1-\frac{4r^{2}}{\log^{2}(1+r^{2\theta})}}\right)}.

(3.24)

By combining (
3.22
), (
3.23
) and (
3.24
) with the decomposition of initial data

\hat{u}_{1}(\xi)=A_{u_{1}}(\xi)-iB_{u_{1}}(\xi)+P_{u_{1}}=:A_{1}(\xi)-iB_{1}(\xi)+P_{1}

as in (
2.1
), we can write the solution of (

Lemma 3.3

There exists
\beta=\beta(\theta)

So, it suffices to check that

From Remark

) imply that

\displaystyle A_{1}(t,\theta)

\displaystyle=\omega_{1}P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\left(\frac{\alpha}{2t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\theta}}}e^{-\frac{2r^{2}}{\log(1+r^{2\theta})}t}\left(\frac{1-e^{-t\frac{\log^{2}(1+r^{2\theta})-r^{2}}{\log(1+r^{2\theta})}}}{\log(1+r^{2\theta})}\right)^{2}dr

\displaystyle\leq 36t^{2}\omega_{1}P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\left(\frac{\alpha}{2t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\theta}}}e^{-\frac{2r^{2}}{\log(1+r^{2\theta})}t}dr\leq 36t^{2}\omega_{1}P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\left(\frac{\alpha}{2t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\theta}}}dr

\displaystyle=36t^{2}\omega_{1}P_{1}^{2}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\theta}}=CP_{1}^{2}t^{2}t^{-\frac{1}{2\theta}}

\displaystyle=CP_{1}^{2}t^{\frac{4\theta-1}{2\theta}},\quad t\gg 1

(4.22)

with some generous constant
C>0
. Note that the estimate given by (
4
) is also holds for
\theta=\frac{1}{4}
.

In order to estimate
A_{2}(t,\theta)
, we use (
4.10
) for
\theta>1/4
to get the following estimate

\displaystyle A_{2}(t,\theta)

\displaystyle=\omega_{1}P_{1}^{2}\int_{\left(\frac{\alpha}{2t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\theta}}}^{\eta}e^{-\frac{2r^{2}}{\log(1+r^{2\theta})}t}\left(\frac{1-e^{-t\frac{\log^{2}(1+r^{2\theta})-r^{2}}{\log(1+r^{2\theta})}}}{\log(1+r^{2\theta})}\right)^{2}dr

\displaystyle\leq 4\omega_{1}P_{1}^{2}\int_{\left(\frac{\alpha}{2t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\theta}}}^{\eta}e^{-\frac{2r^{2}}{\log(1+r^{2\theta})}t}\left(\frac{1-e^{-2tr^{2\theta}}}{r^{2\theta}}\right)^{2}dr

\displaystyle\leq 4\omega_{1}P_{1}^{2}\int_{\left(\frac{\alpha}{2t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\theta}}}^{\eta}\frac{1}{r^{4\theta}}dr

\displaystyle=4\omega_{1}P_{1}^{2}\frac{1}{1-4\theta}\left(\eta^{1-4\theta}-\left(\frac{\alpha}{2t}\right)^{\frac{1-4\theta}{2\theta}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq CP_{1}^{2}\frac{1}{4\theta-1}t^{\frac{4\theta-1}{2\theta}},\quad t\geq t_{0}

(4.23)

with
C=4\omega_{1}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)^{\frac{1-4\theta}{2\theta}}
.
It is important to emphasize that the above estimate holds only for
\theta\neq\frac{1}{4}
and we have just used it for
1-4\theta<0
to obtain (
4.23
). The estimates for
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
prove the desired estimate from below of lemma.

\Box

As a special case one can introduce the following
\log
-order blowup result for the case of
\theta=\frac{1}{4}
.

Lemma 4.3

Let
n=1
and
\theta=\frac{1}{4}
. For
u_{1}\in L^{1}({\bf R})
the following optimal estimate holds.

C_{1}P_{1}^{2}\log t\leq\int_{{\bf R}}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq C_{2}P_{1}^{2}\log t,\quad t\gg 1,

with some constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
.

Proof.
We consider the functions
A_{1}(t,\frac{1}{4})
and
A_{2}(t,\frac{1}{4})
given by (
4.18
) and (
4.19
) with
\theta=\frac{1}{4}
. The estimate (
4
) also holds for
\theta=\frac{1}{4}
and it tells us the fact that

\displaystyle A_{1}(t,\frac{1}{4})\leq CP_{1}^{2},\quad t\gg 1.

(4.24)

While, by definition (
4.19
) and (
4.10
) we have

\displaystyle A_{2}(t,\frac{1}{4})

\displaystyle=\omega_{1}P_{1}^{2}\int_{\left(\frac{\alpha}{2t}\right)^{2}}^{\eta}e^{-\frac{2r^{2}}{\log(1+\sqrt{r})}t}\left(\frac{1-e^{-t\frac{\log^{2}(1+\sqrt{r})-r^{2}}{\log(1+\sqrt{r})}}}{\log(1+\sqrt{r})}\right)^{2}dr

\displaystyle\leq 4\omega_{1}P_{1}^{2}\int_{\left(\frac{\alpha}{2t}\right)^{2}}^{\eta}e^{-\frac{2r^{2}}{\log(1+\sqrt{r})}t}\left(\frac{1-e^{-2t\sqrt{r}}}{\sqrt{r}}\right)^{2}dr

\displaystyle\leq 4\omega_{1}P_{1}^{2}\int_{\left(\frac{\alpha}{2t}\right)^{2}}^{\eta}\frac{1}{r}dr

\displaystyle=4\omega_{1}P_{1}^{2}\left(\log\eta-\log\left(\frac{\alpha}{2t}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=4\omega_{1}P_{1}^{2}\Big(\log\eta-2\log\alpha+2\log 2+2\log t\Big)

\displaystyle\leq CP_{1}^{2}\log t,\quad t\gg 1.

(4.25)

The estimates (
4.24
) and (
4
) allow us to conclude the upper estimate

\displaystyle\int_{{\bf R}}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq C_{2}\log t,\quad t\gg 1,

(4.26)

with some constant
C_{2}>0
.

On the other hand, by (
4.1
) one can get

|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}|\varphi_{1}(t,\xi)|^{2}-|\varphi_{2}(t,\xi)|^{2},\quad t>0,\,\,\xi\in{\bf R}.

Thus, for
t>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{{\bf R}}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\int_{t^{-1}}^{t^{-\frac{2}{3}}}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{t^{-1}}^{t^{-\frac{2}{3}}}|\varphi_{1}(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi-\int_{t^{-1}}^{t^{-\frac{2}{3}}}|\varphi_{2}(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=:P_{1}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}K_{1}(t)-K_{2}(t)\right),

(4.27)

where

\displaystyle K_{1}(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{t^{-1}}^{t^{-\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{e^{-\frac{2|\xi|^{2}}{\log(1+\sqrt{|\xi|})}t}}{\log^{2}(1+\sqrt{|\xi|})}d\xi,

\displaystyle K_{2}(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{t^{-1}}^{t^{-\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{e^{-2\log(1+\sqrt{|\xi|})t}}{\log^{2}(1+\sqrt{|\xi|})}d\xi.

We remember that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{|\xi|}\leq\log(1+\sqrt{|\xi|})\leq\sqrt{|\xi|},\quad|\xi|\leq 1.

(4.28)

Thus by applying Remark 3.1 one has

\displaystyle K_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{t^{-1}}^{t^{-\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{e^{-\frac{2|\xi|^{2}}{\log(1+\sqrt{|\xi|})}t}}{\log^{2}(1+\sqrt{|\xi|})}d\xi\geq\int_{t^{-1}}^{t^{-\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{e^{-4|\xi|^{\frac{3}{2}}t}}{|\xi|}d\xi

\displaystyle=\omega_{1}\int_{t^{-1}}^{t^{-\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{e^{-4r^{\frac{3}{2}}t}}{r}dr\geq\omega_{1}e^{-4}\int_{t^{-1}}^{t^{-\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{1}{r}dr

\displaystyle=\omega_{1}e^{-4}\big(-\frac{2}{3}\log t+\log t\big)

\displaystyle=\omega_{1}\frac{e^{-4}}{3}\log t,\quad t\geq 1.

(4.29)

Similarly, in the case when large
t>1
such that
t^{-\frac{2}{3}}<1
it follows from (
4.28
) that

\displaystyle K_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{t^{-1}}^{t^{-\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{e^{-2\log(1+\sqrt{|\xi|})t}}{\log^{2}(1+\sqrt{|\xi|})}d\xi\leq 4\int_{t^{-1}}^{t^{-\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{e^{-\sqrt{|\xi|}t}}{|\xi|}d\xi

\displaystyle=4\omega_{1}\int_{t^{-1}}^{t^{-\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{e^{-\sqrt{r}t}}{r}dr\leq 4\omega_{1}e^{-\sqrt{t}}\int_{t^{-1}}^{t^{-\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{1}{r}dr

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\omega_{1}}{3}e^{-\sqrt{t}}\log t,\quad t\gg 1.

(4.30)

Therefore, from (
4
), (
4
) and (
4
) one has

\displaystyle\int_{{\bf R}}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\geq P_{1}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}K_{1}(t)-K_{2}(t)\right)

\displaystyle\geq P_{1}^{2}\omega_{1}\left(\frac{e^{-4}}{6}\log t-\frac{4}{3}e^{-\sqrt{t}}\log t\right)

\displaystyle=P_{1}^{2}\omega_{1}\log t\left(\frac{e^{-4}}{6}-\frac{4}{3}e^{-\sqrt{t}}\right),\quad t\gg 1

which implies the desired estimate from below to the case
\theta=1/4
and
n=1
:

\displaystyle\int_{{\bf R}}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\geq CP_{1}^{2}\log t,\quad t\gg 1

(4.31)

with some constant
C>0
. The estimates (
4.26
) and (
4.31
) complete the proof of lemma.

\Box

Now, let us prove Theorem
1.2
at a stroke.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.
One first observes that

\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|\hat{u}(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|\hat{u}(t,\xi)-\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi+\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi.

(4.32)

By combining Lemma
4.1
and Theorem
1.1
with (
4.32
), we have

\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|\hat{u}(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq C(P_{1}^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{1,2\theta}}^{2})\left(t^{-\frac{n-4\theta}{2(1-\theta)}}+\frac{1}{\theta}t^{-\frac{n-4\theta}{2\theta}}\right),\quad t\gg 1.

(4.33)

We can also observe that for
0<\theta<1/2
it holds that
2\theta\leq 2-2\theta
. Therefore
\frac{n-4\theta}{2-2\theta}\leq\frac{n-4\theta}{2\theta}
. Thus the decay rate
t^{-\frac{n-4\theta}{2\theta}}
is faster than
t^{-\frac{n-4\theta}{2-2\theta}}
. It results the following upper bound to the
L^{2}
-norm of the Fourier transformed solution
\hat{u}
such that

\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|\hat{u}(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq C(P_{1}^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{1,2\theta}}^{2})(1+\frac{1}{\theta})t^{-\frac{n-4\theta}{2(1-\theta)}},\quad t\gg 1.

By the Plancherel Theorem and from (
4.33
) the upper bound estimate of the statement of Theorem
1.2
follows with a generous constant
C>0
.

In order to obtain the lower bound, we observe that

|\varphi(t,\xi)|\leq|\hat{u}(t,\xi)-\varphi(t,\xi)|+|\hat{u}(t,\xi)|.

By Young’s inequality, we may obtain

|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}\leq 2|\hat{u}(t,\xi)-\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}+2|\hat{u}(t,\xi)|^{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\hat{u}(t,\xi)|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}-|\hat{u}(t,\xi)-\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2},\quad t\geq 0,\quad\xi\in{\bf R}^{n}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|\hat{u}(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi-\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|\hat{u}(t,\xi)-\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi

(4.34)

First, we consider the case
n\geq 1
and
0<\theta\leq\frac{1}{6}
. By combining (
4.34
) with the lower estimate of Lemma
4.1
and estimate of Theorem
1.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\hat{u}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C_{1}}{2}P_{1}^{2}t^{-\frac{n-4\theta}{2(1-\theta)}}-C_{2}(\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{1,2\theta}}^{2})\left(t^{-\frac{n}{2(1-\theta)}}+\frac{1}{\theta}t^{-\frac{n}{2\theta}}\right)

\displaystyle=t^{-\frac{n-4\theta}{2(1-\theta)}}\left(\frac{C_{1}}{2}P_{1}^{2}-2C_{2}(\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{1,2\theta}}^{2})\left(t^{-\frac{4\theta}{2(1-\theta)}}+\frac{1}{\theta}t^{-\frac{n+4\theta^{2}-2n\theta}{2\theta(1-\theta)}}\right)\right),

(4.35)

for
t\gg 1
and positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
. But for
0<\theta\leq\frac{1}{6}
we notice that
n+4\theta^{2}-2n\theta>0
, so that one can get

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{C_{1}}{2}P_{1}^{2}-2C_{2}(\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{1,2\theta}}^{2})\left(t^{-\frac{4\theta}{2(1-\theta)}}+\frac{1}{\theta}t^{-\frac{n+4\theta^{2}-2n\theta}{2\theta(1-\theta)}}\right)\right)=\frac{C_{1}}{2}P_{1}^{2}.

Therefore, there exists
t_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{C_{1}}{4}P_{1}^{2}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{2}P_{1}^{2}-2C_{2}(\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{1,2\theta}}^{2})\left(t^{-\frac{4\theta}{2(1-\theta)}}+\frac{1}{\theta}t^{-\frac{n+4\theta^{2}-2n\theta}{2\theta(1-\theta)}}\right)\leq C_{1}P_{1}^{2},\quad t\gg t_{1}.

From (
4
) it follows that

\displaystyle\|\hat{u}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C_{1}}{4}P_{1}^{2}t^{-\frac{n-4\theta}{2(1-\theta)}},

(4.36)

for
t\gg 1
.

The estimate (
4.36
) implies the desired estimate for lower bound in
t
in the case when
n\geq 1
and
0<\theta\leq\frac{1}{6}
.

Analogously, we may obtain the results for
n=1
with
\frac{1}{6}<\theta<\frac{1}{4}
, and for
n\geq 2
with
\frac{1}{6}<\theta\leq\frac{5}{12}
based on the results of Theorem
1.1
for these values of
\theta
and Lemma
4.1
.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

\Box

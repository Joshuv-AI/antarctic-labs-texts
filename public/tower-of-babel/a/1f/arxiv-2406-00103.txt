The Bispectrum in Lagrangian Perturbation Theory
Martin White

Abstract
We study the bispectrum in Lagrangian perturbation theory. Extending past results for the power spectrum, we describe a method to efficiently compute the bispectrum in LPT, focusing on the Zeldovich approximation, in which contributions due to linear displacements are captured to all orders in a manifestly infrared (IR) safe way. We then isolate the effects of these linear displacements on oscillatory components of the power spectrum like baryon acoustic oscillations or inflationary primordial features and show that the Eulerian perturbation theory (EPT) prescription wherein their effects are resummed by a Gaussian damping of the oscillations arise as a saddle-point approximation of our calculation. These two methods of IR resummation are in excellent agreement at 1-loop in the bispectrum. At tree level, resummed EPT does less well to capture the nonlinear damping of the oscillations, and the LPT calculation does not require an artificial split of the power spectrum into smooth and oscillatory components, making the latter particularly useful for modeling exotic features. We finish by extending our analysis of IR resummation in LPT to N-point functions of arbitrary order.

Introduction

The observed large-scale structure is thought to arise through the action of gravitational instability in a cold dark matter dominated, expanding Universe from (very nearly) Gaussian initial conditions
[
1
]
. The pattern of non-Gaussianity thus imparted is of a very particular form, and the tower of cumulants of the density and velocity fields on large scales can be systematically computed within perturbation theory
[
2
]
. While for a Gaussian field the power spectrum is a sufficient statistic, and thus contains all of the primordial information, once the field becomes non-Gaussian there is information to be gained about gravitational interactions, cosmological parameters and bias relations from measurements beyond the power spectrum. For a given set of galaxy bias parameters cosmological perturbation theory makes consistent predictions for all of these n-point functions, which can be combined with the power spectrum in data analyses to either improve power-spectrum-only constraints or access new physical signals not present in the 2-point function. Recent years have in particular seen rapid advances in the use of the 3-point bispectrum to measure primordial non-Gaussianity
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
and tighten constraints on cosmological parameters
[

]
. N-point functions are particularly appealing because, on scales where the non-Gaussianity is weak and well-controlled, the available cosmological signal is predominantly at low N such that the tower of cumulants can be truncated at some finite order. In fact, recent work suggests that measurement of the low-order cumulants contains effectively all of the perturbative information that is present in the galaxy density field if attention is restricted to the larger scales where the modeling is most robust, unless the contributions of certain modes is accidentally parametrically large compared to expectations at a given order in perturbation theory
[
5
]
.

The presence of a feature in the linear theory power spectrum due to baryon acoustic oscillations

In order to handle this it is necessary to sum a subset of the full perturbative terms to high order, a procedure that goes by the name of “IR resummation.” Since it fundamentally traces the advection of matter due to long-wavelength perturbations, IR resummation is very naturally handled within the Lagrangian formulation of perturbation theory (LPT;

Beyond the direct Lagrangian resummation of long-wavelength displacements it is also possible to specifically isolate the BAO feature using a “wiggle no-wiggle split”, estimate the effect of long-displacements on it, and resum only those particular effects. Since this procedure resums the linear displacements only in the wiggle component we will call it

[

Beyond the theoretical advantage of being able to compare different IR resummation schemes, the full Lagrangian calculation also has the advantage that it does not require that oscillatory features in the initial conditions be artificially separated. This separation is not inherent to the underlying physical theory and, while well-studied in the case of the BAO, will need to be done on a case-by-case basis if, for example, searching for oscillations in the power spectrum due to exotic inflationary models. The IR resummation of the wiggles can still be performed in this case

This will be particularly useful for upcoming spectroscopic surveys, which will enable us to place extremely tight bounds on these inflationary features

2
The Bispectrum in Lagrangian Perturbation Theory

Lagrangian perturbation theory (LPT;
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
) is by now well established and efficient codes for numerical computation of power spectra exist
[
23
]
. We will not repeat all of the details here, instead referring the reader to the above-cited articles; briefly LPT models the growth of structure by following the displacements of fluid elements from their initial, Lagrangian positions (
\mathbf{q}
) to their final, Eulerian positions (
\mathbf{x}
) via a displacement:
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{\Psi}(\mathbf{q})
. The displacement,
\mathbf{\Psi}
, is then systematically expanded order by order in the initial density perturbation. Biased tracers are modeled by introducing a functional,
F
, of the initial density, velocity and tidal shear and their low-order derivatives. Within this formalism the galaxy density
\delta_{g}(\textbf{x})
has a Fourier transform given by
[
12
,
13
]

\delta_{g}(\textbf{k})=\int d^{3}\textbf{q}\ e^{-i\textbf{k}\cdot\textbf{q}}\Big(F(\textbf{q})e^{-i\textbf{k}\cdot\Psi(\textbf{q})}-1\Big)\,.

(2.1)

where
F(\mathbf{q})
indicates the bias functional evaluated at the Lagrangian coordinate
\mathbf{q}
. For convenience we will ignore the
-1
piece below, since it is nonzero only for vanishing wavenumbers and is anyway trivial to evaluate. As cosmological perturbation theory is an
effective
theory modeling the large-scale fluid limit of structure formation, both the bias functional
F(\textbf{q})
and the perturbative expansion of the displacement
\Psi(\textbf{q})
have contributions whose sizes are free parameters, allowing us to marginalize over unknown small scale effects like galaxy formation (see e.g.
[
34
,
19
,
35
]
); since we focus on the role of long-wavelength linear displacements in this work we will not further discuss this aspect of LPT, and PT in genereal, except where relevant, leaving interested readers to consult the literature on this well-studied topic (see e.g. refs.
[
36
,
37
]
for thorough treatments of the matter bispectrum at 1- and 2-loops).

From the above the bispectrum can then be written as

\left\langle\delta(\textbf{k}_{1})\delta(\textbf{k}_{2})\delta(\textbf{k}_{3})\right\rangle=\int_{\textbf{q}_{1},\textbf{q}_{2},\textbf{q}_{3}}e^{-i\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{q}_{1}-i\textbf{k}_{2}\cdot\textbf{q}_{2}-i\textbf{k}_{3}\cdot\textbf{q}_{3}}\left\langle F_{1}F_{2}F_{3}\ e^{-i\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\Psi_{1}-i\textbf{k}_{2}\cdot\Psi_{2}-i\textbf{k}_{3}\cdot\Psi_{3}}\right\rangle\,,

where we have used the shorthand
f_{n}=f(\textbf{q}_{n}).
1
1

1

For clarity of presentation, we will adopt the shorthands

\int_{\textbf{q}_{n}}\equiv\int d^{3}\textbf{q}_{n}\,,\quad\int_{\textbf{p}_{n}}\equiv\int\frac{d^{3}\textbf{p}_{n}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,,

(2.2)

for configuration and Fourier space integrals, respectively. We also also label Lagrangian quantities by their coordinates, e.g.
F_{n}=F(\textbf{q}_{n})
.

This expression can be simplified by noting that the expectation value has to be translation invariant by statistical homogeneity, i.e. does not depend on all three Lagrangian coordinates
\textbf{q}_{1,2,3}
independently. Adopting the coordinate system
(\textbf{q},\textbf{r},\textbf{Q})=(\textbf{q}_{1}-\textbf{q}_{3},\textbf{q}_{2}-\textbf{q}_{3},\textbf{q}_{3})
, we can therefore eliminate the
Q
dependence in the bracketed mean and write

\displaystyle\left\langle\delta(\textbf{k}_{1})\delta(\textbf{k}_{2})\delta(\textbf{k}_{3})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\textbf{q},\textbf{r},\textbf{Q}}e^{-i\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{q}-i\textbf{k}_{2}\cdot\textbf{r}-i\textbf{k}_{123}\cdot\textbf{Q}}\left\langle F(\textbf{q})F(\textbf{r})F(\textbf{0})\ e^{-i\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\Psi(\textbf{q})-i\textbf{k}_{2}\cdot\Psi(\textbf{r})-i\textbf{k}_{3}\cdot\Psi(\textbf{0})}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{D}(\textbf{k}_{123})\ \int_{\textbf{q},\textbf{r}}e^{-i\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{q}-i\textbf{k}_{2}\cdot\textbf{r}}\left\langle F_{1}F_{2}F_{3}\ e^{-i\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\Psi_{1}-i\textbf{k}_{2}\cdot\Psi_{2}+i(\textbf{k}_{1}+\textbf{k}_{2})\cdot\Psi_{3}}\right\rangle\,,

where
\textbf{k}_{123}=\textbf{k}_{1}+\textbf{k}_{2}+\textbf{k}_{3}
, and in the last line, we have used that this correlator is zero unless
\textbf{k}_{123}=0
. The bispectrum is defined to be this correlator without the
\delta
-function, i.e.
[
33
]

B(\textbf{k}_{1},\textbf{k}_{2})=\int_{\textbf{q},\textbf{r}}e^{-i\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{q}-i\textbf{k}_{2}\cdot\textbf{r}}\left\langle F_{1}F_{2}F_{3}\ e^{-i\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\Delta_{13}-i\textbf{k}_{2}\cdot\Delta_{23}}\right\rangle_{(\textbf{q},\textbf{r})=(\textbf{q}_{1}-\textbf{q}_{3}\textbf{q}_{2}-\textbf{q}_{3})}\,,

(2.3)

where the pairwise displacement is
\Delta_{nm}=\Psi_{n}-\Psi_{m}
. Importantly, the right-hand side of Equation
2.3
is invariant under uniform shifts of the displacement, i.e. satisfies Galilean symmetry.

2.1
Matter Bispectrum in the Zeldovich Approximation

Let us start in the case of matter in the Zeldovich approximation
[
9
]
, i.e. lowest order LPT. Then
F_{n}=1
and
\Psi=\Psi^{(1)}
with
\Psi^{(1)}(\textbf{k})=i(\textbf{k}/k^{2})\delta_{\rm lin}
. In the Zeldovich approximation displacements are Gaussian, since they are first order in
\delta_{\rm lin}
, so the cumulant theorem allows us to write

\left\langle e^{-i\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\Delta_{13}-i\textbf{k}_{2}\cdot\Delta_{23}}\right\rangle=\exp\{-\frac{1}{2}\Big(k_{1,i}k_{1,j}\left\langle\Delta_{13}^{i}\Delta_{13}^{j}\right\rangle+k_{2,i}k_{2,j}\left\langle\Delta_{23}^{i}\Delta_{23}^{j}\right\rangle+2k_{1,i}k_{2,j}\left\langle\Delta_{13}^{i}\Delta_{23}^{j}\right\rangle\Big)\Big\}\,.

The moments of the pairwise displacements can be written as functions of the pairwise separations
q
,
r
by translation invariance
[
14
]

\left\langle\Delta_{13}^{i}\Delta_{13}^{j}\right\rangle=A_{ij}(\textbf{q})\,,\quad\left\langle\Delta_{23}^{i}\Delta_{23}^{j}\right\rangle=A_{ij}(\textbf{r})\,,

and can be decomposed into tensor comonents as
A_{ij}(\textbf{q})=X(q)\delta_{ij}+Y(q)\hat{q}_{i}\hat{q}_{j}

[
12
]
. The “mixed”, third term is slightly more complicated; it can be written as

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta_{13}^{i}\Delta_{23}^{j}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle(\Psi_{1}-\Psi_{3})^{i}(\Psi_{2}-\Psi_{3})^{j}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Psi_{1}^{i}\Psi_{2}^{j}\right\rangle-\left\langle\Psi_{1}^{i}\Psi_{3}^{j}\right\rangle-\left\langle\Psi_{3}^{i}\Psi_{2}^{j}\right\rangle+\left\langle\Psi_{3}^{i}\Psi_{3}^{j}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\left\langle\Psi_{1}^{i}\Psi_{2}^{j}\right\rangle-\left\langle\Psi^{i}\Psi^{j}\right\rangle\right)+\left(\left\langle\Psi^{i}\Psi^{j}\right\rangle-\left\langle\Psi_{1}^{i}\Psi_{3}^{j}\right\rangle\right)+\left(\left\langle\Psi^{i}\Psi^{j}\right\rangle-\left\langle\Psi_{3}^{i}\Psi_{2}^{j}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[A_{ij}(\textbf{r})+A_{ij}(\textbf{q})-A_{ij}(\textbf{q}_{12})\right];\quad\textbf{q}_{12}=\textbf{q}_{1}-\textbf{q}_{2}=\textbf{q}-\textbf{r}\,

(2.4)

where
\left\langle\Psi^{i}\Psi^{j}\right\rangle
is square displacement evaluated at a signle point. The full expectation value can thus be written as

\displaystyle\left\langle e^{-i\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\Delta_{13}-i\textbf{k}_{2}\cdot\Delta_{23}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\exp\{-\frac{1}{2}\Big(k_{1,i}k_{1,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q})+k_{2,i}k_{2,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{r})+k_{1,i}k_{2,j}\big(A_{ij}(\textbf{q})+A_{ij}(\textbf{r})-A_{ij}(\textbf{q}-\textbf{r})\big)\Big)\Big\}

\displaystyle=\exp\{\frac{1}{2}\Big(k_{1,i}k_{3,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q})+k_{2,i}k_{3,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{r})+k_{1,i}k_{2,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q}-\textbf{r})\Big)\Big\}\,,

(2.5)

where in the last line, we have used momentum conservation
\textbf{k}_{1}+\textbf{k}_{2}+\textbf{k}_{3}=0
to restore the symmetry between the sides of the triangle. Note that this form is symmetric in both the wavenumbers
\textbf{k}_{1,2,3}
and the three vertices of the triangle
\textbf{q}_{1,2,3}
.

2.2
Tree Level

It will be quite instructive to check equation
2.5
at the lowest order, i.e. tree level, in such a way as to make contact with the more familiar Eulerian perturbation theory (EPT) calculation. In the Zeldovich approximation, expanding order-by-order in the initial conditions, the density can be written as

\delta(\textbf{k})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{\textbf{p}_{1},...,\textbf{p}_{n}}\ Z_{n}(\textbf{p}_{1},...,\textbf{p}_{n})\ \delta_{0}(\textbf{p}_{1})...\delta_{0}(\textbf{p}_{n})\ (2\pi)^{3}\delta_{D}(\textbf{k}-\textbf{p}_{\rm tot})\,,

where
[
38
]

Z_{n}(\textbf{p}_{1},...,\textbf{p}_{n})=\frac{1}{n!}\left(\frac{\textbf{p}_{\textrm{tot}}\cdot\textbf{p}_{1}}{p_{1}^{2}}\right)\cdots\left(\frac{\textbf{p}_{\textrm{tot}}\cdot\textbf{p}_{n}}{p_{n}^{2}}\right)\,.

(2.6)

In particular, we have that

Z_{2}(\textbf{p}_{1},\textbf{p}_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\Big(1+\frac{\textbf{p}_{1}\cdot\textbf{p}_{2}}{p_{1}^{2}}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\textbf{p}_{1}\cdot\textbf{p}_{2}}{p_{2}^{2}}\Big)\,,

(2.7)

such that the tree-level bispectrum of the Zeldovich matter field is given by

B^{\rm tree}_{\rm Zel}(\textbf{k}_{1},\textbf{k}_{2},\textbf{k}_{3})=2\,Z_{2}(\textbf{k}_{1},\textbf{k}_{2})\,P_{\rm L}(k_{1})P_{\rm L}(k_{2})+2\,\textrm{cycle}\,.

(2.8)

Let us compare this to Equation
2.5
. To get a nonzero Fourier transform, we have to start at the second order term when Taylor expanding the exponential

\displaystyle B^{\rm tree}_{\rm Zel}=

\displaystyle\int_{\textbf{q},\textbf{r}}e^{-i\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{q}-i\textbf{k}_{2}\cdot\textbf{r}}\ \Big[\Big(\frac{1}{2}k_{1,i}k_{3,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q})\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{2}k_{2,i}k_{3,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{r})\Big)

(2.9)

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{1}{2}k_{1,i}k_{3,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q})\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{2}k_{1,i}k_{2,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q}-\textbf{r})\Big)+\Big(\frac{1}{2}k_{2,i}k_{3,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{r})\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{2}k_{1,i}k_{2,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q}-\textbf{r})\Big)\Big]\,.

The quadratic terms shown here are symmetric under permutations of the triangle coordinates in coordinate and Fourier space, so we need only look at the first term, which is the simplest due to being factorizable in the coordinates we have chosen with
\textbf{q}_{3}
‘at the origin’. In fact, this first piece gives

\int_{\textbf{q}}e^{-i\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{q}}\ \Big(\frac{1}{2}k_{1,i}k_{3,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q})\Big)\int_{\textbf{r}}e^{-i\textbf{k}_{2}\cdot\textbf{r}}\ \Big(\frac{1}{2}k_{2,i}k_{3,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{r})\Big)=2\,Z_{2}(\textbf{k}_{1},\textbf{k}_{2})\,P_{\rm L}(k_{1})P_{\rm L}(k_{2})\,,

(2.10)

where in obtaining this result we have used that
\textbf{k}_{3}=-(\textbf{k}_{1}+\textbf{k}_{2})
. Transforming the latter two pieces of
B^{\rm tree}_{\rm Zel}
give the cyclically permuted results so the full result is in agreement with equation
2.8
. Note that evaluating the Fourier transform above technically also incurs terms that are
linear
in the power spectrum, e.g.

\int_{\textbf{q},\textbf{r}}e^{-i\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{q}-i\textbf{k}_{2}\cdot\textbf{r}}\ \left(\frac{1}{2}k_{1,i,}k_{3,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q})\right)=\left(\frac{\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{k}_{3}}{k_{1}^{2}}\right)P_{\rm L}(k_{1})(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{D}(\textbf{k}_{2})\,,

as well as a zeroth order term equal to two
\delta
functions; this is because we have ignored the
-1
in Equation
2.3
which cancels out these disconnected pieces.

3
Direct IR-Safe Resummation of Zeldovich Displacements

While LPT and EPT agree order by order, as we showed in the previous section at tree level, the nonlinear structure of the exponentiated displacements LPT has physical consequences beyond this level, particularly for capturing the nonlinear damping of the BAO, which we will return to in §
4
. It is thus of great interest to develop efficient numerical techniques to evaluate the double integral in Equation
2.3
.

3.1
Preliminary Steps: Generalizing the Zeldovich Power Spectrum

Motivated by previously developed techniques to efficiently compute the power spectrum within LPT, we introduce

\mathcal{E}_{12}(\textbf{p})\equiv\mathcal{E}(\textbf{k}_{1},\textbf{k}_{2},\textbf{p})=\int_{\textbf{q}}e^{-i\textbf{p}\cdot\textbf{q}}\,e^{+\frac{1}{2}\textbf{k}_{1,i}\textbf{k}_{2,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q})}\,,~~{\rm i.e.}~~e^{+\frac{1}{2}\textbf{k}_{1,i}\textbf{k}_{2,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q})}=\int_{\textbf{p}}\,e^{i\textbf{p}\cdot\textbf{q}}\,\mathcal{E}_{12}(\textbf{p})\,,

(3.1)

which is equal to the Zeldovich power spectrum when
\textbf{p}=\textbf{k}_{1}=-\textbf{k}_{2}
(i.e. for
\textbf{k}_{1}+\textbf{k}_{2}=0
). Note the
+
sign in the exponent containing
A_{ij}
. The objects
\mathcal{E}_{nm}(\textbf{p})
are the Fourier transforms of the three exponentiated factors that make up the cumulant in Equation
2.5
. Techniques for evaluating this Fourier transform in that special case were developed in ref.
[
15
]
, and we extend them to allow for arbitrary
\textbf{k}_{1,2}
and
p
in Appedix
A
; roughly, while in the case of the power spectrum all three vectors
\textbf{k}_{1,2}
and
p
were co-linear, here they can form arbitrary spatial configurations, such that the result is a function of both the individual lengths
k_{1},k_{2},p
as well as the angles between the vectors specified by their dot products
\hat{k}_{1}\cdot\hat{k}_{2},\hat{k}_{1}\cdot\hat{p},\hat{k}_{2}\cdot\hat{p}
. We find that accounting for these new degrees of freedom, for a given bispectrum configuration specified by
\textbf{k}_{1,2}
, the integral can be performed for all
p
in terms of the product of two matrices, one consisting of radial Hankel transforms, which can be efficiently computed using the
FFTLog
algorithm
[
39
]
, and another depending solely on angular polynomial coefficients.

An important point is that since the integrand in Equation
3.1
does not asymptote to zero as
\textbf{q}\to\infty
,
\mathcal{E}
will have both a finite part and one proportional to a
\delta
-function, i.e.

\mathcal{E}_{12}(\textbf{p})=\mathcal{E}_{12}^{\rm fin}(\textbf{p})+(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{D}(\textbf{p})\ e^{\frac{1}{2}(\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{k}_{2})\Sigma^{2}}\,,

(3.2)

where
3\Sigma^{2}
is the mean pairwise displacement of two points separated by
\textbf{q}\rightarrow\infty
. While we focus on computing the numerically tricky finite piece in Appendix
A
, keeping track of both pieces is important for the stability of the integral, as we will discuss below. A particularly useful way to re-express Equation
3.2
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{12}(\textbf{p})=\mathcal{E}^{\rm fin}_{12}(\textbf{p})+\left(1-\int_{\textbf{p}^{\prime}}\mathcal{E}^{\rm fin}_{12}(\textbf{p}^{\prime})\right)(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{D}(\textbf{p})\,.

(3.3)

This can be seen by noting that, since
A_{ij}(\textbf{q}\rightarrow 0)=0
, the
\textbf{q}=0
Fourier transform of
\mathcal{E}
must be equal to unity and is again a consequence of Galilean invariance.

3.2
An IR-safe Integrand for the Bispectrum

Since the Zeldovich bispectrum is the Fourier transform of three exponentiated
A_{ij}
’s, it can be written using Equation
2.5
in terms of their Fourier transforms
\mathcal{E}
as

\displaystyle B(\textbf{k}_{1},\textbf{k}_{2})=\int_{\textbf{q}_{1},\textbf{q}_{2}}\,e^{-i\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{q}_{1}-i\textbf{k}_{2}\cdot\textbf{q}_{2}}\left\langle e^{-i\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\Delta_{1}-i\textbf{k}_{2}\cdot\Delta_{2}}\right\rangle=\int_{\textbf{p}}\,\mathcal{E}_{13}(\textbf{k}_{1}-\textbf{p})\mathcal{E}_{23}(\textbf{k}_{2}+\textbf{p})\mathcal{E}_{12}(\textbf{p})\,.

(3.4)

The configuration space integral above represents a double Fourier transform, which is difficult to evaluate numerically and thus has represented an obstacle in any practical implementation of bispectrum LPT results. Conversely, recasting it in the momentum space, the integral becomes a straightforward convolution amenable to direct integration, given the techniques to compute
\mathcal{E}
in the previous subsection. Indeed, by splitting up the finite and
\delta
-function pieces, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\textbf{p}}\,\mathcal{E}_{13}(\textbf{k}_{1}-\textbf{p})\mathcal{E}_{23}(\textbf{k}_{2}+\textbf{p})\mathcal{E}_{12}(\textbf{p})=\,

\displaystyle e^{\frac{1}{2}(\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{k}_{2})\Sigma^{2}}\mathcal{E}^{\rm fin}_{13}(\textbf{k}_{1})\mathcal{E}^{\rm fin}_{23}(\textbf{k}_{2})+2\,{\rm cycle}

\displaystyle+\int_{\textbf{p}}\,\mathcal{E}^{\rm fin}_{13}(\textbf{k}_{1}-\textbf{p})\mathcal{E}^{\rm fin}_{23}(\textbf{k}_{2}+\textbf{p})\mathcal{E}^{\rm fin}_{12}(\textbf{p})\,,

(3.5)

where we have dropped any disconnected pieces requiring one of the external momenta
\textbf{k}_{i}
to be zero. However, as we shall show now, it is possible to re-absorb these
\delta
-funtion contributions into the integral to obtain a numerically better-behaved integral.

The above integral, if implemented numerically as written, is rather sensitive to the behavior of the integrand terms when the argument of
\mathcal{E}
’s approaches zero, where they have a pole. As a particularly simple example, let us first look at the linearized result, where

\mathcal{E}^{\rm lin}_{12}(\textbf{p})=-\frac{(\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{p})(\textbf{k}_{2}\cdot\textbf{p})}{p^{4}}P_{\rm L}(p)+\left(1+\frac{1}{2}(\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{k}_{2})\Sigma^{2}\right)(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{D}(\textbf{p})\,,

(3.6)

Infrared Resummation of BAO and the Saddle-Point Approximation

4.1
Review: Resummation for the Power Spectrum

In the preceding sections we developed the formalism to compute the bispectrum of galaxies in Lagrangian perturbation theory with the linear displacements fully resummed. In practice, it is often desirable to look specifically at the effect of these displacements on the BAO feature, where their effects are most enhanced. In order to do so it is useful to isolate the BAO feature in the linear spectrum by performing a “wiggle no-wiggle split”
P_{\rm L}=P_{\rm L}^{nw}+f_{b}P_{\rm L}^{w}

[
20
]
where
f_{b}P_{\rm L}^{w}
is a purely oscillatory component carrying the BAO while
P_{\rm L}^{nw}
is the smooth component of the power spectrum. We have included the baryon fraction
f_{b}
as a counting parameter since we will be interested primarily in working at first order in the BAO, but we will keep it only implicitly below, with the understanding that each superscript
w
carries a factor of
f_{b}
. The definition of
P_{\rm L}^{nw}
is inexact and many algorithms to compute it exist in the literature with differing results (see e.g.
[
43
]
for a review of commonly used methods). Throughout the numerical portions of this work we will adopt the B-spline method in ref.
[
21
]
, which has the nice property that the total variance in linear displacements is preserved, though we note that the exact choice is somewhat a matter of taste and an inherent systematic of calculations involving this split.

The most prominent effect of the exponentiated linear displacements in the power spectrum is to produce a roughly Gaussian damping of the BAO feature, which can therefore be resummed by including this Gaussian factor whenever the wiggle piece of the power spectrum appears (
RWiggle
). This effect has long been known empirically in the large-scale structure literature
[
20
,
44
,
12
]
and was derived analytically in perturbation theory as an infrared resummation of long-wavelength modes enhanced by the BAO by refs.
[
17
,
18
,
21
,
22
]
. An equivalent derivation, presented in ref.
[
21
]
, shows that this form can be simply derived by performing a saddle-point approximation about the BAO in Lagrangian perturbation theory, and we briefly review it below.

Using the wiggle no-wiggle split we can separate
A_{ij}=A^{nw}_{ij}+A^{w}_{ij}
into two pieces due to the wiggle and no-wiggle power spectra

A_{ij}^{w,nw}(\textbf{q})=2\int_{\textbf{k}}\left(1-e^{i\textbf{k}\cdot\textbf{q}}\right)\left(\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{4}}\right)P_{\rm L}^{w,nw}(k)\,.

(4.1)

q=r_{s}

A_{ij}
without the BAO feature. Performing this split we have that the Zeldovich matter power spectrum for matter, at first order in the wiggles, is

\displaystyle P_{\rm Zel}^{w}(k)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}k_{i}k_{j}\int_{\textbf{q}}e^{i\textbf{k}\cdot\textbf{q}-\frac{1}{2}k_{i}k_{j}A^{nw}_{ij}(\textbf{q})}A^{w}_{ij}(\textbf{q})

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{1}{2}k_{i}k_{j}e^{-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}X_{s}}\int_{\textbf{q}}e^{i\textbf{k}\cdot\textbf{q}-\frac{1}{2}k_{i}k_{j}Y_{s}\hat{q}_{i}\hat{q}_{j}}A^{w}_{ij}(\textbf{q})

\displaystyle\approx e^{-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}\Sigma^{2}_{s}}P^{w}_{\rm L}(k),\quad kr_{s}\gg 1\,.

(4.2)

In going from the first to second lines, we have used the saddle-point

A_{ij}^{w}
expanded out, we have three distinct contributions in total proportional to
P_{w}(k_{1,2,3})
. In particular, we can use the same logic as in the previous section to write each as

\mathcal{E}^{w}(\textbf{k}_{1},\textbf{k}_{2},\textbf{p})=\int_{\textbf{q}}e^{i\textbf{p}\cdot\textbf{q}+\frac{1}{2}k_{1,i}k_{2,j}A^{nw}_{ij}(\textbf{q})}\left(\frac{1}{2}k_{1,i}k_{2,j}A^{w}_{ij}(\textbf{q})\right)\approx e^{\frac{1}{2}(\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{k}_{2})\Sigma^{2}_{s}}A(\textbf{k}_{1},\textbf{k}_{2},\textbf{p})P_{\rm L}^{w}(p)\,,

(4.8)

where we have defined the angular factor
A(\textbf{k}_{n},\textbf{k}_{m},\textbf{p})=-(\textbf{k}_{n}\cdot\textbf{p})(\textbf{k}_{m}\cdot\textbf{p})/p^{4}
. Note that the damping of the BAO in
\mathcal{E}
is itself IR safe, i.e. does not depend on displacements from modes longer than the BAO radius, since it is defined from pairwise displacements.

We can now proceed to consider BAO oscillations in the bispectrum itself. We will do this in two ways—first, by considering explicitly the effect of long-wavelength modes in (Lagrangian) configuration space and deriving the bispectrum version of
RWiggle
through a saddle-point calculation therein, and then by using an IR-safe integrand in Fourier space to extract the effect of all linear displacements and derive a convenient expression for evaluating the residual term.

4.2.1
Infrared Modes and Configuration-Space Saddle Point

We first consider the effect of purely infrared modes—that is, ones shorter than the wavenumbers entering a given bispectrum configuration, starting from Equation
3.11
. Specifically, in each of the three pieces of the integral we can extract contributions due to
p
with
p
smaller than
k_{1,2}/2
,
k_{1,3}/2
or
k_{2,3}/2
. These regions are naturally set by the angular bisectors defining the IR regions. Focusing on one of the three domains without lack of generality, we have in the first one

\displaystyle(\text{IR\ Region of }k_{1,2})

\displaystyle=\int_{|\textbf{p}|<k_{1,2}/2}\mathcal{E}_{12}^{\rm fin}(\textbf{p})\left(\mathcal{E}_{13}^{\rm fin}(\textbf{k}_{1}-\textbf{p})\mathcal{E}_{23}^{\rm fin}(\textbf{k}_{2}+\textbf{p})-\mathcal{E}_{13}^{\rm fin}(\textbf{k}_{1})\mathcal{E}_{23}^{\rm fin}(\textbf{k}_{2})\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\textbf{q}_{1,2}}\ e^{-i\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{q}_{1}-i\textbf{k}_{2}\cdot\textbf{q}_{2}}\ \left(\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm fin}_{12}(\textbf{q}_{1}-\textbf{q}_{2})-\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm fin}_{12}(\bf{0})\right)_{\rm IR}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm fin}_{13}(\textbf{q}_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm fin}_{23}(\textbf{q}_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{\textbf{q}_{1,2}}\ e^{-i\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{q}_{1}-i\textbf{k}_{2}\cdot\textbf{q}_{2}}\ \left(e^{\frac{1}{2}k_{1,i}k_{2,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q}_{1}-\textbf{q}_{2})}-1\right)_{\rm IR}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm fin}_{13}(\textbf{q}_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm fin}_{23}(\textbf{q}_{2})\,,

(4.9)

where the subscript denotes that all the non-IR modes are removed, and in going from the second to third lines, we have used the integral relation in Equation
3.3
. We can now add in the “tree-level” piece, which cancels the “minus one”:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\rm fin}_{13}(\textbf{k}_{1})\mathcal{E}^{\rm fin}_{23}(\textbf{k}_{2})

\displaystyle+(\text{IR\ Region of }k_{1,2})

\displaystyle=\int_{\textbf{q}_{1,2}}\ e^{-i\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{q}_{1}-i\textbf{k}_{2}\cdot\textbf{q}_{2}}\ \left(e^{\frac{1}{2}k_{1,i}k_{2,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q}_{1}-\textbf{q}_{2})}\right)_{\rm IR}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm fin}_{13}(\textbf{q}_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm fin}_{23}(\textbf{q}_{2})\,.

(4.10)

By construction, the BAO wiggle piece needs to come from the last two factors in the integrand and not the infrared first piece; splitting the two
\mathcal{E}
into a wiggle and no-wiggle piece yields the tree level power spectrum contributions

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{13}^{w,\rm fin}(\textbf{k}_{1})\mathcal{E}_{23}^{nw,\rm fin}(\textbf{k}_{2})+

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{13}^{nw,\rm fin}(\textbf{k}_{1})\mathcal{E}_{23}^{w,\rm fin}(\textbf{k}_{2})

\displaystyle=Z_{2}(\textbf{k}_{1},\textbf{k}_{2})\left(P^{w}_{\rm L}(k_{1})P^{nw}_{\rm L}(k_{2})+P^{nw}_{\rm L}(k_{1})P^{w}_{\rm L}(k_{2})\right)+\mathcal{O}(P_{\rm L}^{3})\,,

(4.11)

yielding six total such contributions when all three IR domains of Equation
3.11
are taken into account.

We now consider the BAO saddle-point of Equation
4.10
. Expanding to first order in the wiggles, we see that the oscillatory contribution from
\mathcal{E}^{w}_{13}(\textbf{q}_{1})
, which has support sharply peaked around
q_{1}=r_{d}
, picks out the pieces of the integral looking like the configuration shown in Figure
7
. In particular, while one edge of the triangle corresponding to the Lagrangian separation
\textbf{q}_{1}
has length fixed to the BAO scale, the length of the other two edges
\textbf{q}_{2}
and
\textbf{q}_{1}-\textbf{q}_{2}
are free. This corresponds to the three-point function configuration where two of three galaxies are separated by the BAO scale. Unlike in the power spectrum case, which only involved separations of two galaxies at a fixed distance, we have to contend with the exponential factor in Equation
4.10
having an extra degree of freedom in
\textbf{q}_{2}-\textbf{q}_{1}
, which will in general induce additional dynamical corrections to the observed BAO along
\textbf{k}_{1}
(
\textbf{q}_{1}
); nonetheless, if we fix
\textbf{q}_{2}=0
in the infrared piece, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{23}^{nw,\rm fin}(\textbf{k}_{2})\int_{\textbf{q}_{1}}\ e^{-i\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{q}_{1}}\left(e^{\frac{1}{2}k_{1,i}k_{2,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q}_{1})}\right)_{\rm IR}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{w,\rm fin}_{13}(\textbf{q}_{1})

\displaystyle\approx e^{\frac{1}{2}(\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{k}_{2})\Sigma^{2}_{s}}\mathcal{E}_{13}^{w,\rm fin}(\textbf{k}_{1})\mathcal{E}_{23}^{nw,\rm fin}(\textbf{k}_{2})

(4.12)

\displaystyle\approx e^{-\frac{1}{2}k_{1}^{2}\Sigma^{2}_{s}}A(\textbf{k}_{1},\textbf{k}_{2},\textbf{p})P_{\rm L}^{w}(p)\mathcal{E}_{23}^{nw,\rm fin}(\textbf{k}_{2})\,,

i.e. that the tree-level bispectrum is damped by IR displacements in a Gaussian way depending only on the wavenumber of the BAO wiggle itself.

The corrections due to this approximation come from the statistics of the other legs of the 3-point function triangle and can be computed as terms in a Taylor series

\int_{\textbf{q}_{2}}e^{-i\textbf{k}_{2}\cdot\textbf{q}_{2}}(\textbf{q}_{2,i_{1}}...\textbf{q}_{2,i_{n}})\ \tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{nw,\rm fin}_{23}(\textbf{q}_{2})\int_{\textbf{q}_{1}}\ e^{-i\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{q}_{1}}\ \left(\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial\textbf{q}_{1,i_{1}}...\partial\textbf{q}_{1,i_{n}}}\right)\left(e^{\frac{1}{2}k_{1,i}k_{2,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q}_{1})}\right)_{\rm IR}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{w,\rm fin}_{13}(\textbf{q}_{1})\,,

where the first factor is simply the
\textbf{k}_{2}
derivative of
\mathcal{E}^{nw,\rm fin}_{23}
. The second factor involves derivatives evaluated about
q_{1}=r_{d}
; these take the form

\left(\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial\textbf{q}_{1,i_{1}}...\partial\textbf{q}_{1,i_{n}}}\right)\left(e^{\frac{1}{2}k_{1,i}k_{2,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q}_{1})}\right)_{q_{1}=r_{d}}\sim\frac{1}{2}k_{1,i}k_{2,j}\int_{\textbf{p},\ p<1/r_{d}}\frac{p_{i}p_{j}p_{i_{1}}...p_{i_{n}}}{p^{4}}P_{\rm L}(p)\,.

(4.13)

In a power-law universe where
P_{\rm L}=Ap^{n_{s}}
it is easy to see that this integral scales as
r_{d}^{-n}
with
n
, such that the n
th
order contribution is suppressed by
(k_{2}r_{d})^{-n}
. We thus see that the damping can be captured by a simple damping of the input linear power spectrum, with perturbatively small corrections due to modes with wavelengths on the BAO scale.

4.2.2
Fourier Space Resummation of Linear Displacements

Here, we show that the equivalent result as derived above can be obtained from the Fourier space representation of the bispectrum, allowing us to relax the requirement of restricting to the modes smaller than
k_{1,2}/2
.
We thus start with the expression for the bispectrum (still working in Zeldovich approximation) given in Equation

.
Since we are free to shift the integration variable, we can recover any relevant momenta combination by considering only the case when the wiggle contribution of
\mathcal{E}^{\rm w}
appears as

\displaystyle\int_{\textbf{p}}\,\mathcal{E}^{\rm nw}_{13}(\textbf{k}_{1}-\textbf{p})\mathcal{E}^{\rm nw}_{23}(\textbf{k}_{2}+\textbf{p})\mathcal{E}^{\rm w}_{12}(\textbf{p})\,.

Given that the remaining two contributions can be recovered by transforming

\displaystyle=\int_{\textbf{p}}\,\mathcal{E}^{nw}_{13}(\textbf{k}_{1}-\textbf{p})\mathcal{E}^{nw}_{23}(\textbf{k}_{2}+\textbf{p})\mathcal{E}^{w}_{12}(\textbf{p})+(2~{\rm cycle})

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{1}{2}(\textbf{k}_{3}\cdot\textbf{k}_{1})\Sigma^{2}}\Big(\mathcal{E}^{nw,{\rm fin}}_{32}(\textbf{k}_{3})\mathcal{E}_{12}(\textbf{k}_{1})+\mathcal{E}^{w}_{32}(\textbf{k}_{3})\mathcal{E}^{nw,{\rm fin}}_{12}(\textbf{k}_{1})\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 128.0374pt+\int_{\textbf{p}}\,\mathcal{E}^{nw,{\rm fin}}_{12}(\textbf{k}_{1}-\textbf{p})\mathcal{E}^{nw,{\rm fin}}_{23}(\textbf{k}_{2}+\textbf{p})\mathcal{E}^{w}(\textbf{k}_{1},\textbf{k}_{2},\textbf{p})+(2~{\rm cycle})

\displaystyle\hskip 128.0374pt+\int_{\textbf{p},{\rm IR}}\,\mathcal{E}^{nw,{\rm fin}}_{13}(\textbf{k}_{1}-\textbf{p})\mathcal{E}^{nw,{\rm fin}}_{23}(\textbf{k}_{2}+\textbf{p})\mathcal{E}^{w}_{12}(\textbf{p})+(2~{\rm cycle})\,,

(4.15)

where we have again used that

\displaystyle\int_{\textbf{p},{\rm IR}}\,\mathcal{E}^{nw,{\rm fin}}_{13}(\textbf{k}_{1}-\textbf{p})\mathcal{E}^{nw,{\rm fin}}_{23}(\textbf{k}_{2}+\textbf{p})\mathcal{E}^{w}_{12}(\textbf{p})

\displaystyle~~\equiv\int_{\textbf{p}}\mathcal{E}^{nw,{\rm fin}}_{23}(\textbf{p})\Big(\mathcal{E}^{nw,{\rm fin}}_{31}(\textbf{k}_{3}+\textbf{p})\mathcal{E}^{w}_{21}(\textbf{k}_{2}-\textbf{p})\Theta\left(|\textbf{k}_{3}+\textbf{p}|-p\right)-\mathcal{E}^{nw,{\rm fin}}_{31}(\textbf{k}_{3})\mathcal{E}^{w}_{21}(\textbf{k}_{2})\Big)+(\textbf{k}_{1}\leftrightarrow\textbf{k}_{2})\,.

Furthermore, each contribution can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\int_{|\textbf{k}_{1}-\textbf{p}|<|\textbf{k}_{2}+\textbf{p}|}\mathcal{E}^{nw,{\rm fin}}_{13}(\textbf{k}_{1}-\textbf{p})

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{nw,{\rm fin}}_{23}(\textbf{k}_{2}+\textbf{p})\mathcal{E}^{w}_{12}(\textbf{p})

\displaystyle=\int_{p<|\textbf{k}_{3}+\textbf{p}|}\mathcal{E}^{nw,{\rm fin}}_{13}(\textbf{p})\mathcal{E}^{nw,{\rm fin}}_{32}(\textbf{k}_{3}+\textbf{p})\mathcal{E}^{w}_{12}(\textbf{k}_{1}-\textbf{p})\,.

\displaystyle(\textbf{k}_{1}-\textbf{p})\mathcal{E}^{nw,{\rm fin}}_{23}(\textbf{k}_{2}+\textbf{p})\mathcal{E}^{w}_{12}(\textbf{p})

\displaystyle=\int_{\textbf{p}}\mathcal{E}^{nw,{\rm fin}}_{23}(\textbf{p})\Big(\mathcal{E}^{nw,{\rm fin}}_{31}(\textbf{k}_{3}+\textbf{p})\Theta\left(|\textbf{k}_{3}+\textbf{p}|-p\right)-\mathcal{E}^{nw,{\rm fin}}_{31}(\textbf{k}_{3})\Big)\mathcal{E}^{\rm w}_{21}(\textbf{k}_{2}-\textbf{p})

\displaystyle~~~~+\mathcal{E}^{nw,{\rm fin}}_{31}(\textbf{k}_{3})\int_{\textbf{p}}\mathcal{E}^{nw,{\rm fin}}_{23}(\textbf{p})\Big(\mathcal{E}^{w}_{21}(\textbf{k}_{2}-\textbf{p})-\mathcal{E}^{w}_{21}(\textbf{k}_{2})\Big)+(\textbf{k}_{1}\leftrightarrow\textbf{k}_{2})

\displaystyle\int_{\textbf{p}}\mathcal{E}^{nw,{\rm fin}}_{23}(\textbf{p})\Big(

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{w}_{21}(\textbf{k}_{2}-\textbf{p})-\mathcal{E}^{w}_{21}(\textbf{k}_{2})\Big)

\displaystyle B^{w}

\displaystyle=-\Big(\mathcal{E}^{nw,{\rm fin}}_{32}(\textbf{k}_{3})(\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{k}_{2})+\mathcal{E}^{nw,{\rm fin}}_{23}(\textbf{k}_{2})(\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{k}_{3})\Big)e^{-\frac{1}{2}k_{1}^{2}\Sigma^{2}_{s}}P_{\rm L}^{w}(k_{1})/k_{1}^{2}

Further IR-Resummation Schemes

In addition to the direct Zeldovich calculation and

Ref.
[

constructed an IR resummation scheme of the two-point function with the goal of IR resummation operating on the usual EPT loop terms. This ensures that the IR resummed theory has approximately the same UV properties (counterterm structure and values) as the canonical, un-resummed, EPT. We follow the presentation from Appendix B of ref.
[

By defining
A_{ij}(\textbf{q})=\left\langle\Delta_{i}\Delta_{j}\right\rangle=\Sigma^{2}\delta^{\rm K}_{ij}-2\eta_{ij}(\textbf{q})
, we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\rm fin}_{12}(\textbf{p})

\displaystyle=\int_{\textbf{q}}\,e^{i\textbf{p}\cdot\textbf{q}}\left(e^{\frac{1}{2}k_{1i}k_{2j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q})}-e^{\frac{1}{2}k_{1i}k_{2j}A_{ij}(\infty)}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\textbf{q}}\,e^{i\textbf{p}\cdot\textbf{q}}\,e^{\frac{1}{2}k_{1i}k_{2j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q})}\left(1-e^{k_{1i}k_{2j}\eta_{ij}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\int_{\textbf{q}}\,e^{i\textbf{p}\cdot\textbf{q}}\,K_{12}(\textbf{q})\left(k_{1i}k_{2j}\eta_{ij}\right)^{n}\,,

(4.26)

where we introduce the Gaussian kernel

K_{12}(\textbf{q})=\exp\left\{\frac{1}{2}k_{1i}k_{2j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q})\right\}

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{12}^{1-{\rm loop}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big\{\left(k_{1i}k_{2j}\eta_{ij}\right)^{2}\Big\}_{\rm FT}-\frac{1}{2}\big(\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{k}_{2}\Sigma^{2}\big)\Big\{k_{1i}k_{2j}\eta_{ij}\Big\}_{\rm FT}\,.

Another related method to resum Zeldovich displacements was described in ref.

\tilde{O}(k)=\int d^{3}\textbf{q}\ e^{-i\textbf{k}\cdot(\textbf{q}+\Psi(\textbf{q}))}\ O(\textbf{q}).

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}\textbf{q}\ e^{-i\textbf{k}\cdot(\textbf{q}+\Psi)}\Big\{\delta(\textbf{q})+\frac{1}{2}\big(s^{2}(\textbf{q})-\frac{2}{3}\delta^{2}(\textbf{q})\Big)+\ldots\big\}\equiv\tilde{\delta}(\textbf{k})+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{2}(\textbf{k})+\ldots

While the numerical evaluation of the two alternative schemes discussed above is beyond the scope of this work, their mathematical structure is rather similar to the direct Zeldovich calculation presented in this work, and we outline the steps towards these extensions in the aforementioned Appendix.

5
IR Resummation for N-point Functions

We can also extend our analysis to the IR resummation of n-point functions beyond the bispectrum. To do so, we need to consider the generalised version of the cumulant in Equation
2.5

\left\langle e^{-i\sum_{n=1}^{N}k_{n,i}\Psi^{(1)}_{i}(\textbf{q}_{i})}\right\rangle_{\textbf{k}_{\rm tot}=0}=\exp\left\{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{m>n}k_{n,i}k_{m,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q}_{nm})\right\}\,.

(5.1)

Here, the sum runs over all
distinct
pairs
(n,m)
, and we have used the identity Equation
2.4
. In the above we have defined
\textbf{q}_{nm}=\textbf{q}_{n}-\textbf{q}_{m}
. As before it is useful to shift to a coordinate system
\textbf{q}_{i<N}\rightarrow\textbf{q}_{i<N}-\textbf{Q}
, with
\textbf{Q}=\textbf{q}_{N}
, such that the expectation values can be written independently of
Q
by translation invariance. The Fourier-space N-point function is then given by

C_{N}(\textbf{k}_{1},...,\textbf{k}_{N-1})=\left(\prod_{a=1}^{N-1}\int_{\textbf{q}_{a}}e^{-i\textbf{k}_{a}\cdot\textbf{q}_{a}}\right)\ \exp\left\{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\neq m}k_{n,i}k_{m,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q}_{nm})\right\}\ f_{N}(\textbf{q}_{1},...,\textbf{q}_{N-1})\,,

(5.2)

where we have used “
n\neq m
” as the shorthand for the sum over distinct pairs. Here,
f_{N}
captures terms due to galaxy bias and higher-order displacements and is equal to unity for matter in the Zeldovich approximation. We can equivalently express the above in terms of
\mathcal{E}
as

C(\textbf{k}_{1},...,\textbf{k}_{N-1})=\prod_{n\neq m}\int_{\textbf{p}_{nm}}\ \mathcal{E}(\textbf{k}_{n},\textbf{k}_{m},\textbf{p}_{nm})\prod_{a=1}^{N-1}(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{D}\big(\textbf{k}_{a}-\sum_{n>a}p_{an}+\sum_{n<a}p_{na}\big)\,,

(5.3)

where the momentum conservation comes from integrating over the vertices
\textbf{q}_{a}
, and we remind the reader that there is no sum over the N
th
vertex. Note that the bispectrum and power spectrum are special cases in that there is only one fewer vertex (2 and 1) than pairwise separations
\textbf{q}_{nm}
(3 and 2), leading to a reduction in the number of integrals in Fourier space. With the trispectrum, for example, one has to do an equal number of integrals in each space. For higher N-point functions there is a rapid scaling in the number of internal momenta
\textbf{p}_{nm}
with
N
, though the Fourier space integral still has the advantage of being smooth compared to the Fourier transforms required in configuration space.

Separating the contributions to the above integral via a wiggle-no wiggle split it is easy to work out the effect of long-wavelenght displacements from on BAO wiggles in the N-point functions. In particular, expanding the wiggles to linear order we will have contributions that are, schematically,

\displaystyle C_{w}\sim\left(\prod_{m=2}^{N-1}\int_{\textbf{q}_{m}}e^{-i\textbf{k}_{m}\cdot\textbf{q}_{m}}\right)

\displaystyle\exp\left\{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n,m>1}^{n\neq m}k_{n,i}k_{m,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q}_{nm})\right\}

\displaystyle\int_{\textbf{q}_{1}}e^{-i\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{q}_{1}}\exp\left\{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m\neq 1}k_{1,i}k_{m,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q}_{1m})\right\}\xi_{w}(\textbf{q}_{1})\,.

(5.4)

Here we have chosen a BAO features in the
\textbf{q}_{1}=\textbf{q}_{1N}
leg of the N-point function
f_{N}
in Lagrangian space but, since the final expression is invariant under permutations of the vertices we can do so without loss of generality. The top line of Equation
5.4
is smooth in
\textbf{q}_{1}
so we can ignore it. If we make the same approximation
\textbf{q}_{1m}\rightarrow\textbf{q}
as in the bispectrum in the previous section then the exponent in the second line becomes at the saddle point

\frac{1}{2}k_{1,i}\left(\sum_{m\neq n}k_{m,j}\right)A_{ij}(\textbf{q}_{s})=-\frac{1}{2}k_{1}^{2}\Sigma^{2}_{s}\,,

(5.5)

by momentum conservation. This implies that the effects of long-wavelength displacements on wiggles in N-point functions can be well-described, at leading order in the wiggles, by the substitution

P_{w}(k)\rightarrow e^{-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}\Sigma^{2}_{s}}P_{w}(k)\,.

(5.6)

This prescription is a consequence of translation invariance and momentum conservation; it is interesting to observe that this condition is also satisfied for N-point functions with collapsed legs, such that the loops can be computed with this substitution as well to approximate the effects of IR displacements.

6
Conclusions

Figure 12
:
Squeezed (left) and equilateral (right) Zeldovich bispectra when the linear power spectrum has logarithmic wiggles on top of our fiducial

The presence of sharp, localized features in the linear correlation function—or, oscillatory “wiggles” in the power spectrum—lead large-scale structure observables to be sensitive to the bulk motion of matter on the large-scales at which these features reside. In
\Lambda
CDM universes close to our own, the contributions of these infrared (IR) modes to galaxy N-point statistics can be numerically quite large, such that they need to be resummed in order for perturbative calculations to be well-behaved. Indeed, this is the case both for features that are the bread and butter of modern galaxy surveys, i.e. the baryon acoustic oscillations standard ruler, and proposed targets of future experiments, e.g. features in the primordial power spectrum that probe the particular shape of the inflaton potential. A natural way to formulate this procedure, known as IR resummation, is within the Lagrangian picture of fluid mechanics, wherein fluids are described via the displacements of individual fluid elements which give rise to these bulk flows on large scales. Within Lagrangian perturbation theory (LPT), the effect of the bulk displacements can be understood as the smearing of the correlation function due to
pairwise
displacements of fluid elements separated by the scale of the feature (e.g. the BAO radius
r_{d}
).

In this paper, we build on previous works on the galaxy 2-point function to study the galaxy 3-point function, or bispectrum, in Lagrangian perturbation theory. In the first part, we extend past techniques used to efficiently compute the resummed LPT power spectrum to the resummed bispectrum, focusing specifically on the case of the bispectrum within the Zeldovich approximation which contains all the long-wavelength displacements we wish to include. Our method involves computing the Fourier transform of the variance of the pairwise displacement, projected along arbitrary wavenumbers, and convolving the resulting kernels in Fourier space. These kernels contain an IR pole when the wavenumber
\textbf{p}=\textbf{0}
whose effects cancel in the full calculation, and in order to properly treat their convolution numerically we split the integral into Voronoi regions closest to each pole and re-organize the expression for the integrand in each such that IR divergences explicitly vanish. This is a generalization of the so-called IR-safe integrands in Eulerian perturbation theory, with the effects of bulk displacements now carried to arbitrary order. We make the code to perform these calculations,
triceratops
, publicly available.

In the second part of the paper, we focus on computing the effect of the resummed displacements on features in the linear 2-point function, taking the BAO peak as a particular example. In the 2-point function, the presence of such a feature allows for a simple saddle-point approximation of these displacements at the peak, leading to a simple Gaussian damping of the power spectrum wiggles in Fourier space. The situation in the 3-point function is not so simple: here, requiring that two points in a triangle be separated by the BAO radius nonetheless leaves substantial freedom for the remaining point and two legs of the triangle to move around. However, we can nonetheless perform a saddle point approximation of the Zeldovich displacements in the bispectrum—we show that this leads to the familiar form where the effect of the displacements is a Gaussian smoothing of the linear power spectrum in each leg
k
, which we call
RWiggle
, with corrections from the extra degrees of freedom due to perturbatively small density modes on the BAO scale, suppressed by inverse powers of
kr_{d}
. This conclusion holds for arbitrary
N
-point functions due to momentum conservation. We numerically compare the wiggle component computed in this approximation to that in the full Lagrangian calculation, finding close agreement, especially at 1-loop order after the IR resummation of the wiggle component. We also make contact with other IR resummation schemes in the literature, deriving an extension of the scheme developed for the power spectrum in ref.
[
17
]
from the Lagrangian calculation and showing how the bispctrum in the
shifted-operator
formalism developed for field-level analyses in ref.
[
45
]
can be computed uinsg the techniques in this paper.

We have thus established that resummed 1-loop perturbation theory very-well captures the effect of long-wavelength displacements on the BAO signal in cosmological N-point functions. However, it is worth noting that the direct Lagrangian treatment has distinct advantages over the
RWiggle
prescription, in that the wiggle (feature) component does not have to be distinctly treated. One advantage of this is that in the Lagrangian method one does not need to perform the wiggle/no-wiggle split, which is somewhat arbitrary, since there is no clear criterion that the smooth and wiggle components must satisfy, and indeed likely need to be adapted individually for each inflationary model treated. Moreover, features in the linear power spectrum beyond the BAO, e.g. inflationary signatures in the primordial spectrum, may have nonlinear dispersion relations not localized to any particular scale in configuration space—such a case is handled out-of-the-box in LPT but needs to be handled on a case-by-case basis in
RWiggle
, since each frequency in Fourier space corresponds to a distinct IR scale. This makes the Lagrangian treatment indispensable for future spectroscopic searches for exotic physics.

(6.1)

[

For current and future surveys, the galaxy bispectrum carries substantial promise as a probe of primordial features on a comparable footing with the power spectrum

Extending to redshift-space is a simple matter of coordinate transformations. Appendix A
Numerical Evaluation of
\mathcal{E}

In §
3
we introduced a generalized version of the Zeldovich power spectrum, the function
\mathcal{E}(\textbf{k}_{1},\textbf{k}_{2},\textbf{p})
, through which the effects of large linear displacements can be captured. The goal in this appendix is to develop numerical techniques to efficiently evaluate this function. In §
A.1
we outline one way to perform this calculation by performing an angular decomposition of the integral into parts that can be rapidly computed by Hankel transform. §
A.2
shows how the first few terms of this decomposition reduce to linear theory at leading order. Finally, §
A.3

D
.

A.1
Angular Decomposition and Fast Evaluation Using Hankel Transforms

Let us now examine
\mathcal{E}
in more detail. By symmetry, we have the angular dependence

\mathcal{E}(\textbf{k}_{1},\textbf{k}_{2},\textbf{p})=\mathcal{E}\left(k_{1},k_{2},p,\hat{k}_{1}\cdot\hat{k}_{2},\hat{k}_{1}\cdot\hat{p},\hat{k}_{2}\cdot\hat{p}\right)=\int d^{3}q\,e^{-i\textbf{p}\cdot\textbf{q}}e^{+\frac{1}{2}\textbf{k}_{1,i}\textbf{k}_{2,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q})}\,,

(A.1)

where
A_{ij}=X\delta^{K}_{ij}+Y\hat{q}_{i}\hat{q}_{j}

[
14
]
.
This gives us

\frac{1}{2}\textbf{k}_{1,i}\textbf{k}_{2,j}A_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}k_{1}k_{2}\left[\hat{k}_{1}\cdot\hat{k}_{2}X(q)+(\hat{k}_{1}\cdot\hat{q})(\hat{k}_{2}\cdot\hat{q})Y(q)\right]\,.

(A.2)

and thus

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle\left(k_{1},k_{2},p,\hat{k}_{1}\cdot\hat{k}_{2},\hat{k}_{1}\cdot\hat{p},\hat{k}_{2}\cdot\hat{p}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt=\int q^{2}dq\,e^{\frac{1}{2}(\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{k}_{2})\left[X(q)+Y(q)\right]}\mathcal{I}\left(qp,\tfrac{1}{2}k_{1}k_{2}Y(q),\hat{k}_{1}\cdot\hat{k}_{2},\hat{k}_{1}\cdot\hat{p},\hat{k}_{2}\cdot\hat{p}\right)\,.

(A.3)

We are thus faced with solving integrals of type

\mathcal{I}\left(\alpha,\beta,\hat{k}_{1}\cdot\hat{k}_{2},\hat{k}_{1}\cdot\hat{p},\hat{k}_{2}\cdot\hat{p}\right)=\int d\Omega_{\hat{q}}\,e^{-i\alpha\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q}+\beta\hat{k}_{1,i}\hat{k}_{2,j}\left(\hat{q}_{i}\hat{q}_{j}-\delta^{K}_{ij}\right)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\beta^{n}}{n!}I_{n}\,.

(A.4)

The key part to compute is the integral

I_{n}=\int d\Omega_{\hat{q}}\,\left[\hat{k}_{1,i}\hat{k}_{2,j}\left(\hat{q}_{i}\hat{q}_{j}-\delta^{\rm K}_{ij}\right)\right]^{n}e^{i\alpha\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q}}\,.

(A.5)

We can be a bit more general by replacing
\hat{p}
with
p
. We thus have

I_{n}=\int d\Omega_{\hat{q}}\,\left[\hat{k}_{1,i}\hat{k}_{2,j}\left(\hat{q}_{i}\hat{q}_{j}-\delta^{\rm K}_{ij}\right)\right]^{n}e^{i\alpha\textbf{p}\cdot\hat{q}}\,.

(A.6)

Notice that these integrals form the
recursion

I_{n+1}=-\hat{k}_{1,i}\hat{k}_{2,j}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial p_{i}\partial p_{j}}+\delta^{\rm K}_{ij}\right)I_{n}\,.

(A.7)

For
n=0
we have

\displaystyle I_{0}=4\pi j_{0}(\alpha p)\,,

(A.8)

and thus

I_{n}=4\pi\bigg[-\hat{k}_{1,i}\hat{k}_{2,j}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial p_{i}\partial p_{j}}+\delta^{\rm K}_{ij}\right)\bigg]^{n}j_{0}(\alpha p)\,.

(A.9)

We note that in case when
\hat{k}_{1}=-\hat{k}_{2}=\hat{p}
we have

I_{n}=4\pi\Big(1+\partial_{\alpha p}^{2}\Big)^{n}j_{0}(\alpha p)\,=4\pi~n!\left(\frac{2}{\alpha p}\right)^{n}j_{n}(\alpha p)\,,

(A.12)

\displaystyle\hskip 180.67517pt+2a(n+1)F_{n}+\left(2a-(\alpha p)^{2}(bc-a)\right)G_{n}\,,

\displaystyle G_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\left(2c\hat{O}_{2}(m)+2b\hat{O}_{1}(m)+\hat{O}_{12}(m)+2a\right)g_{n,m}

\displaystyle~~~+\left(-c\hat{O}_{2}(m)-b\hat{O}_{1}(m)+2a(n+1)\right)f_{n,m}-(2n+3)\hat{O}_{12}(m+1)f_{n,m+1}+(bc-a)g_{n,m-1}\,,

We can use the form above to factor out the

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}

where as a reminder

and

\displaystyle=4\pi\int dq\ q^{2}\ e^{\frac{1}{2}(\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{k}_{2})(X+Y)}\beta^{n}q^{2m-n}j_{n}(pq)

(A.19)

For any given triangle
\textbf{k}_{1,2,3}

A.2
Recovering Tree Level Results

The various kernels and infinite sums in the above being somewhat opaque, in this section we show how to recover the expected results at tree level by evaluating the first few summands at lowest order. We can begin by writing

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=4\pi\sum_{n}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\int dq\ q^{2}\Big(1

\displaystyle+\frac{\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{k}_{2}}{2}(X+Y)\Big)\Big(\frac{k_{1}k_{2}Y}{2}\Big)^{n}

\displaystyle(pq)^{-n}\Big(F_{n}-\frac{2n\Theta_{n>0}}{k_{1}k_{2}Y}(pq)^{2}G_{n-1}\Big)j_{n}(pq)+\mathcal{O}(P_{\rm L}^{2}).

(A.21)

Since each
X,Y
carries one power of
P_{\rm L}
, we can stop the sum at
n=2
in order to recover linear theory:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\rm lin}=4\pi\int

\displaystyle dq\ q^{2}\Bigg\{\Big(\frac{\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{k}_{2}}{2}\Big)(X+Y)\ F_{0}\ j_{0}(pq)

\displaystyle-(pq)^{-1}\Big(\frac{k_{1}k_{2}Y}{2}\Big)\ F_{1}\ j_{1}(pq)+(pq)^{-2}\Big(\frac{k_{1}k_{2}Y}{2}\Big)^{2}\Big(-\frac{4}{k_{1}k_{2}Y}G_{1}(pq)^{2}\Big)j_{2}(pq)\Bigg\}.

Using the definitions of
F_{n}
,
G_{n}
and
X
and
Y
as well as the identity
j_{1}(x)/x=(j_{0}+j_{2})/3
it is straightforward to recover

\mathcal{E}_{\rm lin}^{\rm fin}(\textbf{k}_{1},\textbf{k}_{2},\textbf{p})=-\frac{(\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{p})(\textbf{k}_{2}\cdot\textbf{p})}{p^{4}}P_{\rm L}(p).

(A.22)

It is interesting to note that unlike in the case of the Zeldovich power spectrum, where
\textbf{k}_{1}=-\textbf{k}_{2}
, recovering linear theory requires going to
n=2
. In general, in order to isolate contributions to
\mathcal{E}^{\rm fin}
at a given order we note that we can write

\Gamma^{(-)}_{n,m}=\sum_{r}\frac{4\pi}{r!}\int dq\ q^{2}\ \left(\frac{1}{2}(\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{k}_{2})(X+Y)\right)^{r}\beta^{n(-1)}q^{2m-n}j_{n}(pq)

such that the order-
N
contributions to
\Gamma^{(-1)}_{n,m}
come from
N=r+n(-1)
, and the corresponding contributions to
\mathcal{E}
come from setting all other terms in the sum to zero.

We can now proceed to obtain the tree-level bispectrum in our numerical implementation. Pluggin in the linear contribution to
\mathcal{E}
as derived above into Equation
3.11
we get to leading order

\displaystyle B^{\rm tree}(\textbf{k}_{1},\textbf{k}_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{\rm lin}^{\rm fin}(\textbf{k}_{1},\textbf{k}_{1}+\textbf{k}_{2},-\textbf{k}_{1}-\textbf{p})\mathcal{E}_{\rm lin}^{\rm fin}(\textbf{k}_{2},\textbf{k}_{1}+\textbf{k}_{2},-\textbf{k}_{2}-\textbf{p})+...

\displaystyle=\Big(1+\frac{\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{k}_{2}}{k_{1}^{2}}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{k}_{2}}{k_{2}^{2}}\Big)P_{\rm L}(\textbf{k}_{1})P_{\rm L}(\textbf{k}_{2})+\textrm{cycl.}

(A.23)

as expected.

A.3
Alternative Angular Decomposition

Figure 13
:
Convergence of the Hankel-transform series in Equation

compared to the fiducial method described in §

in the case of the Zeldovich power spectrum. The series both converge to the correct values as the number

of Hankel transforms increases, though Equation

It is also possible to perform the angular integral in
\mathcal{E}
by directly Taylor-expanding the angular dependence in
Y(q)
. In this case we have

\displaystyle\int d^{3}\textbf{q}\ e^{-i\textbf{p}\cdot\textbf{q}+\frac{1}{2}k_{1,i}k_{2,j}A_{ij}(\textbf{q})}

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}\textbf{q}\ e^{-i\textbf{p}\cdot\textbf{q}+\frac{1}{2}((\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{k}_{2})X(q)+(\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\hat{q})(\textbf{k}_{2}\cdot\hat{q})Y(q))}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\int d^{3}\textbf{q}\ e^{-i\textbf{p}\cdot\textbf{q}+\frac{1}{2}(\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{k}_{2})X(q)}k_{1,i_{1}}...k_{1,i_{n}}k_{2,j_{1}}...k_{2,j_{n}}\left(\frac{Y(q)}{2}\right)^{n}\hat{q}_{i_{1}}...\hat{q}_{j_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\int d^{3}\textbf{q}\ e^{-i\textbf{p}\cdot\textbf{q}+\frac{1}{2}(\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{k}_{2})X(q)}k_{1,i_{1}}...k_{1,i_{n}}k_{2,j_{1}}...k_{2,j_{n}}\left(\frac{Y(q)}{2}\right)^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n}C_{n,m}[\mathcal{L}_{2m}(\hat{q})]_{i_{1}...j_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{n}4\pi\left(k_{1,i_{1}}...k_{1,i_{n}}k_{2,j_{1}}...k_{2,j_{n}}[\mathcal{L}_{2m}(\hat{p})]_{i_{1}...j_{n}}\right)\frac{(-1)^{m}}{n!}\int dq\ q^{2}\ e^{\frac{1}{2}(\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{k}_{2})X(q)}C_{n,m}\left(\frac{Y(q)}{2}\right)^{n}j_{2m}(pq)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\frac{C_{n,m}}{n!}\left(k_{1,i_{1}}...k_{1,i_{n}}k_{2,j_{1}}...k_{2,j_{n}}[\mathcal{L}_{2m}(\hat{p})]_{i_{1}...j_{n}}\right)4\pi(-1)^{m}\int dq\ q^{2}\ e^{\frac{1}{2}(\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{k}_{2})X(q)}\left(\frac{Y(q)}{2}\right)^{n}j_{2m}(pq)

where we have defined the coefficients

\mu^{2n}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}C_{n,m}\mathcal{L}_{2m}(\mu).

(A.24)

As in the previous method the final expression is a series of Hankel transforms whose integrands are independent of
\hat{p}
and proportional to powers of
Y(q)
, multiplied by the angular coefficient

\mathcal{D}_{2m}(\hat{k}_{1},\hat{k}_{2},\hat{p})=\hat{k}_{1,i_{1}}...\hat{k}_{1,i_{n}}\hat{k}_{2,j_{1}}...\hat{k}_{2,j_{n}}[\mathcal{L}_{2m}(\hat{p})]_{i_{1}...j_{n}}.

(A.25)

Here
\mathcal{L}_{2m}
are the tensor equivalents of the Legendre polynomials (see e.g. ref.
[
49
]
). The
\mathcal{D}_{2m}
are polynomials in the dot products of the three unit vector arguments and can be worked out recursively using the recurrence relations of the Legendre polynomials.

Let us consider the special case of the power spectrum
\textbf{k}=\textbf{k}_{1}=-\textbf{k}_{2}=\textbf{p}
. In this case the angular contraction is

\mathcal{D}(\hat{k},-\hat{k},\hat{k})=(-1)^{n}\hat{k}_{i_{1}}...\hat{k}_{j_{n}}[\mathcal{L}_{2m}(\hat{k})]_{i_{1}...j_{n}}=(-1)^{n}

such that we can write

P_{\rm Zel}(k)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}4\pi(-1)^{m}\int dq\ q^{2}\ e^{-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}X(q)}j_{2m}(pq)F_{m}(q)

where

\displaystyle F_{m}(q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\frac{C_{n,m}}{n!}\left(-\frac{k^{2}Y(q)}{2}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(4m+1)\Gamma\left(m+\frac{3}{2}\right)\,}{(2m+1)\Gamma(2m+\frac{3}{2})}\ _{1}F_{1}\left(m+\frac{1}{2};2m+\frac{3}{2};-\frac{k^{2}Y(q)}{2}\right)\left(-\frac{k^{2}Y(q)}{2}\right)^{m}.

(A.26)

Note that
F_{m}
is proportional to
Y^{m}
to leading order, so this formula is a re-summation of the method presented above where terms with different powers were separated. Figure
13
shows the convergence of this sum as
m_{max}
is increased—it is in excellent agreement with the fiducial method and indeed exhibits significantly faster convergence at large wavenumbers.

B.5
Enhancement by the BAO

In the above cancellations we have implicitly used that we can approximate

P_{\rm L}(|\textbf{k}+\textbf{p}|)=P_{\rm L}(k)\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{p}{k}\right)\right).

(B.6)

P_{w}\sim\sin(r_s k)
we have that the corrections are instead of order powers of
r_{s}
times the signal, or enhanced by
r_{s}k
compared to expectations. In other words, a mode
p
which is in the infrared compared to
k
can still have a large effect due to the size of
r_{s}
.

In the 1-loop bispectrum we can consider this enhancement by looking at the contributions due to
B_{123}
and
B_{222}
for which we have the IR contribution

\displaystyle-2\left(k_{2,i}k_{3,j}+k_{2,i}k_{1,j}\right)

\displaystyle Z_{2}(\textbf{k}_{1},\textbf{k}_{2})\int_{\textbf{p}}^{k_{\rm IR}}\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p^{4}}P_{\rm L}(p)P_{\rm L}(|\textbf{k}_{2}-\textbf{p}|)P_{\rm L}(k_{1})

\displaystyle=2k_{2,i}k_{2,j}Z_{2}(\textbf{k}_{1},\textbf{k}_{2})\int_{\textbf{p}}^{k_{\rm IR}}\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p^{4}}P_{\rm L}(p)P_{\rm L}(|\textbf{k}_{2}-\textbf{p}|)P_{\rm L}(k_{1})

where we have dropped the
p
in
P_{\rm L}(|\textbf{k}_{1}-\textbf{p}|)
to focus on the effect on the BAO in

Appendix C
Corrections to Saddle-Point in Power Spectrum

Let us consider the integral

P_{w,\rm Zel}(k)=\int d^{3}\textbf{q}\ e^{i\textbf{k}\cdot\textbf{q}-\frac{1}{2}k_{i}k_{j}A_{ij}^{nw}(\textbf{q})}\ \xi_{w}(q).

(C.1)

To use the saddle-point approximation we make the Taylor expansion about
\textbf{q}=r_{d}\hat{q}+(q-r_{d})\hat{q}
:

A_{ij}^{nw}(\textbf{q})=A_{ij}^{nw}(r_{d}\hat{q})+(q-r_{d})\ \hat{q}_{a}\partial_{a}A_{ij}^{nw}(r_{d}\hat{q})+\ldots\ .

(C.2)

The first term gives us the leading order saddle-point result, i.e. exponential damping. The second term leads to a correction

-\frac{1}{2}k_{i}k_{j}\int d^{3}\textbf{q}\ e^{i\textbf{k}\cdot\textbf{q}-\frac{1}{2}k_{i}k_{j}A_{ij}^{nw}(\textbf{q})}\ (q-r_{d})\ \hat{q}_{a}\partial_{a}A_{ij}^{nw}(r_{d}\hat{q})\ \xi_{w}(q).

(C.3)

Defining

\xi^{\ell}_{n}(q)=\int\frac{dk\ k^{2+n}}{2\pi^{2}}P(k)j_{\ell}(kq)

(C.4)

we have that the derivative above is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial A^{nw}_{ij}(\textbf{q})}{\partial q_{k}}=\frac{2}{5}\xi^{1,nw}_{-1}(q)\hat{q}_{(i}\delta_{jk)}-\frac{4}{5}\xi^{3,nw}_{-1}(q)\left(\frac{5}{2}\hat{q}_{i}\hat{q}_{j}\hat{q}_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\hat{q}_{(i}\delta_{jk)}\right)

(C.5)

and, working to leading order we get that the correction is

-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}\Big[\frac{2}{3}\xi^{1}_{-1}(r_{d})P^{1}_{w,0}(k)-\frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{4}{5}\xi^{1}_{-1}(r_{d})-\frac{6}{5}\xi^{3}_{-1}(r_{d})\right)P^{1}_{w,2}(k)\Big].

In the above we have defined

P^{n}_{w,\ell}(k)=4\pi\int dq\ q^{2}\ (q-r_{d})^{n}\ \xi_{w}(q)\ j_{\ell}(kq).

(C.6)

Roughly speaking these should be order the width of the (linear) BAO
r_{D}^{n}
. The cumulative correction to the damping is thus of order
r_{D}r_{d}\sigma^{2}_{r_{d}}
, where we have used that
\xi^{1}_{-1}(q)=q\sigma^{2}_{q}/3
is related to the mean square density
\sigma^{2}_{q}
in spheres of radius
q
—in practice we find that the effect is sufficiently small to be subdominant even to differences due to the choice of the wiggle no-wiggle split for the BAO, though its relevance is proportional to the width of the feature in the correlation function which can vary for e.g. primordial features.

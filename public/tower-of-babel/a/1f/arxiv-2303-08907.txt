Master formulas for photon amplitudes in a combined constant and plane-wave background field
Christian Schubert, Rashid Shaisultanov

Abstract
The worldline formalism has previously been used for deriving compact master formulas for the QED N N - photon amplitudes in vacuum, in a constant field and in a plane-wave field. Here we carry this program one step further by deriving master formulas for the scalar and spinor QED N N -photon amplitudes in the background of the “parallel” special case of a combined constant and plane-wave field.

Clearly one would prefer to have a method that would avoid the segmentation of fermion loops and lines into
individual propagators. Such an approach, based on relativistic worldline path integrals, has been developed by Feynman

Although equivalent to the standard Feynman diagram approach, methodically it is quite different since each of the path integrals represents a whole fermion loop or line,
rather than individual propagators, and the emission or absorption of a photon by the fermion is effected through the insertion of a vertex operator, as in string theory.
As far as QED is concerned, the central formula of the formalism is the following master formula for the one-loop
N
-photon amplitude in scalar QED

[
35
,
31
,
32

:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm scal}(k_{1},\varepsilon_{1};\ldots;k_{N},\varepsilon_{N})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{(-ie)}^{N}{(2\pi)}^{D}\delta(\sum k_{i}){\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}}\frac{dT}{T}{(4\pi T)}^{-\frac{D}{2}}e^{-m^{2}T}

(1)

\displaystyle\hskip-150.0pt\times\prod_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}d\tau_{i}\exp\biggl\{\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\Bigl[\frac{1}{2}G_{ij}k_{i}\cdot k_{j}-i\dot{G}_{ij}\varepsilon_{i}\cdot k_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\ddot{G}_{ij}\varepsilon_{i}\cdot\varepsilon_{j}\Bigr]\biggr\}\Bigl|_{\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\ldots\varepsilon_{N}}\,.

Here we have abbreviated
G_{ij}\equiv G(\tau_{i},\tau_{j})
etc., where
G
is the “bosonic worldline Green’s function” defined by

\displaystyle G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\equiv\mid\tau-\tau^{\prime}\mid-\frac{(\tau-\tau^{\prime})^{2}}{T}

(2)

and a ‘dot’ denotes a derivative acting on the first variable,

\displaystyle\dot{G}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm sgn}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})-2\frac{(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}{T},\quad\ddot{G}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=2{\delta}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})-\frac{2}{T}\,.

(3)

The exponential must still be expanded and only the terms be retained that contain
each polarisation vector
\varepsilon_{i}
linearly:

\displaystyle\exp\bigl\{\cdot\bigr\}\bigl|_{\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\ldots\varepsilon_{N}}\quad\equiv{(-i)}^{N}P_{N}(\dot{G}_{ij},\ddot{G}_{ij})\exp\biggl[\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}G_{ij}k_{i}\cdot k_{j}\biggr]

(4)

with certain polynomials
P_{N}
.

The generalization to the spinor-loop case in the modern approach is done through the addition
of a Grassmann path integral, to be evaluated with the “fermionic worldline Green’s function”

\displaystyle{\cal G}_{B}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{T}{2{\cal Z}^{2}}\biggl({{\cal Z}\over{{\rm sin}{\cal Z}}}\,{\rm e}^{-i{\cal Z}\dot{G}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})}+i{\cal Z}\dot{G}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})-1\biggr)

(5)

\displaystyle{\cal G}_{F}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle G_{F}(\tau,\tau^{\prime}){{\rm e}^{-i{\cal Z}\dot{G}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})}\over{\rm cos}{\cal Z}}

(6)

where
{\cal Z}_{\mu\nu}\equiv eF_{\mu\nu}T

should be understood as power series in the Lorentz matrix

\displaystyle\hskip 20.0pt\times\exp\biggl\{\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\Bigl[\frac{1}{2}k_{i}\cdot{\cal G}_{Bij}\cdot k_{j}-i\varepsilon_{i}\cdot\dot{\cal G}_{Bij}\cdot k_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{i}\cdot\ddot{\cal G}_{Bij}\cdot\varepsilon_{j}\Bigr]\biggr\}\Big|_{\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\cdots\varepsilon_{N}}\,.

More recently, the worldline approach has also been applied to the plane-wave case, defined by a vector potential
A(x)
of the form

\displaystyle eA_{\mu}(x)=a_{\mu}(n\cdot x)

(8)

where
n^{\mu}
is a null vector,

n\cdot a=0

This case turned out to be less straightforward than the constant-field one, since the resulting path integrals are not
manifestly gaussian. Nevertheless, building on work by A. Ilderton and G. Torgrimsson on the vacuum-polarization case
[

it was shown by J.P. Edwards and one of the authors
[
47

how to use the special kinematics of the plane-wave case to
reduce the path integrals to gaussian ones, which then also made it straightforward to arrive at a master formula for the
N
-photon amplitudes
in such a background.

The gaussian property is equivalent to the exactness of the semiclassical approximation for the constant-field and plane-wave backgrounds,
and it is natural to ask whether it continues to hold for the combination of both backgrounds. While this is not the case in general, there
is a special case where it applies, namely when there exists a Lorentz frame where the electric and magnetic fields are parallel, and
the plane wave propagates in the same direction.
In such a field the Klein-Gordon and Dirac equations are still exactly solvable, as was first shown for the magnetic case by
P. J. Redmond
[
48

in 1965 and for the general case by I. A. Batalin and E. S. Fradkin
in 1970
[
49

(see also
[
50
,
2
]
). This fact has prompted studies of the scalar
[
51
]
and spinor
[
52
]
mass operators in such a field,
as well as of the spinor vacuum polarisation
[
53
]
.
Apart from its mathematical interest as the most general known field permitting the exact solution of the Klein-Gordon and Dirac equations,
it leads also to a number of interesting physical effects related to the existence of a cyclotron resonance
[
51
,
52
]
(see also
[
54
]
).
This resonance requires only the magnetic
field, and happens when the wave frequency coincides with the cyclotron frequency of a particle in the magnetic field (with the Doppler shift taken into account).

In this letter, we use the worldline formalism to derive master formulas for the
N
-photon amplitudes in this field configuration, working in parallel on scalar and

The starting point of
[
47
]
was the worldline path integral representation of the
N
-photon amplitude in scalar QED
in such a background
[

,

,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm scal}(\{{k_{i},\varepsilon_{i}\}};a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-ie)^{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dT}{T}\,\,{\rm e}^{-m^{2}T}\int Dx\,\,{\rm e}^{-\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\bigl[\frac{\dot{x}^{2}}{4}+i\dot{x}^{\mu}a_{\mu}(n\cdot x)\bigr]}

(9)

\displaystyle\times V^{\gamma}_{\rm scal}[k_{1},\varepsilon_{1}]\cdots V^{\gamma}_{\rm scal}[k_{N},\varepsilon_{N}]\,.

Here the path integral runs over all closed trajectories in (euclidean) spacetime obeying the
periodicity condition
x(T)=x(0)
in proper time, and each photon is represented by the following
photon vertex operator
:

\displaystyle V^{\gamma}_{\rm scal}[k,\varepsilon]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\,\varepsilon\cdot\dot{x}(\tau)\,{\,{\rm e}}^{ik\cdot x(\tau)}\,.

(10)

It is convenient to introduce (euclidean) light-cone coordinates
[
55
]
.
Setting
n^{\mu}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,0,1,i)
one denotes
x^{+}\equiv n\cdot x=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(x^{3}+ix^{4})
(“light-front time”),
x^{-}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(-x^{3}+ix^{4})
,
and
x^{\perp}\equiv(x^{1},x^{2})
.

Before proceeding with the functional integrals one has to address
the existence of a zero mode, the constant paths, which is done by separating off the average position
x_{0}^{\mu}
of the trajectory,
x^{\mu}(\tau)=x_{0}^{\mu}+q^{\mu}(\tau)
.
Defining also
k^{\pm}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\pm k^{3}+ik^{4})
,
and using the decomposition

\displaystyle k\cdot x=-k^{+}x^{-}-k^{-}x^{+}+k^{1}x^{1}+k^{2}x^{2}

(11)

allows one to integrate out
x_{0}^{\mu}
but for its
x_{0}^{+}
component:

\displaystyle\int Dx\,\,{\rm e}^{-\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\bigl[\frac{\dot{x}^{2}}{4}+i\dot{x}^{\mu}a_{\mu}(n\cdot x)\bigr]}V^{\gamma}_{\rm scal}[k_{1},\varepsilon_{1}]\cdots V^{\gamma}_{\rm scal}[k_{N},\varepsilon_{N}]

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}^{1}\bigr)\delta\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}^{2}\bigr)\delta\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}^{+}\bigr)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx_{0}^{+}\,{\rm e}^{-ix_{0}^{+}\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}^{-}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}d\tau_{i}

\displaystyle\times\int Dq\,{\rm e}^{-\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\bigl[\frac{\dot{q}^{2}}{4}+i\dot{q}^{\mu}a_{\mu}(x_{0}^{+}+n\cdot q(\tau))\bigr]}\,\,{\rm e}^{\sum_{i=1}^{N}(ik_{i}\cdot q_{i}+\varepsilon_{i}\cdot\dot{q}_{i})}\Big|_{\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\cdots\varepsilon_{N}}\,.

(12)

The functional integral might seem intractable, since the integration variable
q^{\mu}(\tau)
appears in the argument of the undetermined function
a_{\mu}
.
The upshot is that the vanishing relations

\displaystyle n^{2}=n\cdot a=0

(13)

can be used to replace the argument by a definite function of proper-time. This can be done in various ways
[
46
,
47
]
; here we will present a new approach
based on auxiliary functional integrals. Using the integral representation of the functional delta function, we can rewrite

\displaystyle\,{\rm e}^{-i\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\,\dot{q}^{\mu}a_{\mu}(x_{0}^{+}+n\cdot q(\tau))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int D\phi(\tau)\prod_{\tau}\delta(\phi(\tau)-n\cdot q(\tau))\,{\rm e}^{-i\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\,\dot{q}^{\mu}a_{\mu}(x_{0}^{+}+\phi(\tau))}

\displaystyle\hskip-70.0pt=\int D\phi(\tau)\int D\chi(\tau)\ \,{\rm e}^{i\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\chi(\tau)\bigl(\phi(\tau)-n\cdot q(\tau)\bigr)}\,{\rm e}^{-i\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\,\dot{q}^{\mu}a_{\mu}(x_{0}^{+}+\phi(\tau))}\,.

Employing this identity in (
12
), the functional integral over
q^{\mu}(\tau)
becomes gaussian,
and the usual “completing-the-square” procedure can be applied. This leads to

\displaystyle\int Dq\,{\rm e}^{-\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\bigl[\frac{\dot{q}^{2}}{4}+i\dot{q}\cdot a(\tau)\bigr]}\,\,{\rm e}^{\sum_{i=1}^{N}(ik_{i}\cdot q_{i}+\varepsilon_{i}\cdot\dot{q}_{i})}\,{\rm e}^{i\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\chi(\tau)\bigl(-n\cdot q(\tau)\bigr)}

\displaystyle={(4\pi T)}^{-{D\over 2}}\,{\rm e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\int_{0}^{T}d\tau^{\prime}\ddot{G}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})a(\tau)\cdot a(\tau^{\prime})+\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\bigl[\frac{1}{2}G_{ij}k_{i}\cdot k_{j}-i\dot{G}_{ij}\varepsilon_{i}\cdot k_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\ddot{G}_{ij}\varepsilon_{i}\cdot\varepsilon_{j}\bigr]}

\displaystyle\quad\times\,{\rm e}^{-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\bigl\{a(\tau)\cdot\bigl[\dot{G}(\tau,\tau_{i})k_{i}+i\ddot{G}(\tau,\tau_{i})\varepsilon_{i}\bigr]+\chi(\tau)n\cdot\bigl[G(\tau,\tau_{i})k_{i}+i\dot{G}(\tau,\tau_{i})\varepsilon_{i}\bigr]\bigr\}}

(15)

where
a_{\mu}(\tau)\equiv a_{\mu}(x_{0}^{+}+\phi(\tau))
and the vanishing relations (
13
)
have been used to eliminate some terms in the exponent, including one quadratic in
\chi(\tau)
.
As a consequence, the functional integral over
\chi(\tau)
becomes trivial and simply produces a

\prod_{\tau}\delta\bigl(\phi(\tau)+i\sum_{i=1}^{N}n\cdot\bigl[G(\tau,\tau_{i})k_{i}+i\dot{G}(\tau,\tau_{i})\varepsilon_{i}\bigr]\bigr)
,
which then can be used to eliminate the integral over
\phi(\tau)
.
The effect of all this is to change the argument of
a_{\mu}
to

\displaystyle a_{\mu}(\tau)=a_{\mu}\Bigl(x_{0}^{+}+n\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{N}[-iG(\tau,\tau_{i})k_{i}+\dot{G}(\tau,\tau_{i})\varepsilon_{i}]\Bigr)\,.

(16)

Further, introducing the worldline average of a general function
f

\displaystyle\langle\langle f\rangle\rangle\equiv\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}d\tau f(\tau)

(17)

and using (
3
) one can rewrite

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\int_{0}^{T}d\tau^{\prime}\ddot{G}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})a(\tau)\cdot a(\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\Bigl(\langle\langle a^{2}\rangle\rangle-\langle\langle a\rangle\rangle^{2}\Bigr)\,,

(18)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\,\ddot{G}(\tau,\tau_{i})a(\tau)\cdot\varepsilon_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Bigl(a(\tau_{i})-\langle\langle a\rangle\rangle\Bigr)\cdot\varepsilon_{i}\,.

(19)

For the integral involving
\dot{G}(\tau,\tau_{i})
, one can use integration-by-parts to rewrite

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\,\dot{G}(\tau,\tau_{i})a(\tau)\cdot k_{i}=-2\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}\cdot\bigl(I(\tau_{i})-\langle\langle I\rangle\rangle\bigr)

(20)

with the periodic integral function

\displaystyle I_{\mu}(\tau)\equiv\int_{0}^{\tau}d\tau^{\prime}\Bigl(a_{\mu}(\tau^{\prime})-\langle\langle a_{\mu}\rangle\rangle\Bigr)\,.

(21)

In this manner, one gets the following master formula for the scalar QED

N
-photon amplitude in a plane-wave background

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm scal}(\{{k_{i},\varepsilon_{i}\}};a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-ie)^{N}(2\pi)^{3}\delta\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}^{1}\bigr)\delta\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}^{2}\bigr)\delta\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}^{+}\bigr)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx_{0}^{+}\,{\rm e}^{-ix_{0}^{+}\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}^{-}}

\displaystyle\hskip-80.0pt\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dT}{T}\,{(4\pi T)}^{-{D\over 2}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}d\tau_{i}\,{\rm e}^{\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\bigl[\frac{1}{2}G_{ij}k_{i}\cdot k_{j}-i\dot{G}_{ij}\varepsilon_{i}\cdot k_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\ddot{G}_{ij}\varepsilon_{i}\cdot\varepsilon_{j}\bigr]}

\displaystyle\hskip-80.0pt\times\,{\rm e}^{-\bigl(m^{2}+\langle\langle a^{2}\rangle\rangle-\langle\langle a\rangle\rangle^{2}\bigr)T+2\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}\cdot\bigl(I(\tau_{i})-\langle\langle I\rangle\rangle\bigr)-2i\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(a(\tau_{i})-\langle\langle a\rangle\rangle\bigr)\cdot\varepsilon_{i}}\Bigl|_{\varepsilon_{1}\cdots\varepsilon_{N}}\,.

Further simplification can be achieved by choosing a gauge for the
\varepsilon_{i}
.
Imposing the condition

\displaystyle n\cdot\varepsilon_{i}=0\quad(i=1,\ldots,N)\,,

(23)

reduces (

) to

\displaystyle a_{\mu}(\tau)=a_{\mu}\Bigl(x_{0}^{+}-i\sum_{i=1}^{N}G(\tau,\tau_{i})k_{i}^{+}\Bigr)\,.

(24)

The master formula (
) can then be written more explicitly as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm scal}(\{{k_{i},\varepsilon_{i}\}};a)\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!(-e)^{N}(2\pi)^{3}\delta\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}^{1}\bigr)\delta\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}^{2}\bigr)\delta\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}^{+}\bigr)\!\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx_{0}^{+}\,{\rm e}^{-ix_{0}^{+}\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}^{-}}

(25)

\displaystyle\hskip-50.0pt\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dT}{T}\,{(4\pi T)}^{-{D\over 2}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}d\tau_{i}\,\,{\rm e}^{-\bigl(m^{2}+\langle\langle a^{2}\rangle\rangle-\langle\langle a\rangle\rangle^{2}\bigr)T}

\displaystyle\hskip-50.0pt\times\mathfrak{P}_{N}\,{\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}G_{ij}k_{i}\cdot k_{j}+2\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}\cdot\bigl(I(\tau_{i})-\langle\langle I\rangle\rangle\bigr)}

\displaystyle V^{\gamma}_{\rm spin}[k,\varepsilon]

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\,\Bigl[\varepsilon\cdot\dot{x}(\tau)-i\psi(\tau)\cdot f[k,\varepsilon]\cdot\psi(\tau)\Bigr]\,\,{\rm e}^{ik\cdot x(\tau)}

where

.

The same procedure that we applied above to eliminate

from the argument of
a_{\mu}
works

.
The Grassmann path-integral then becomes independent of the coordinate-space one.
It is already gaussian and can be performed by Wick contractions with the basic correlator

\displaystyle\langle\psi^{\mu}(\tau)\psi^{\nu}(\tau^{\prime})\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{G}_{F}^{\mu\nu}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

(29)

where the Green’s function
\mathfrak{G}_{F}
is the inverse of the operator

\displaystyle{\cal O}\equiv\frac{\delta_{\mu\nu}}{2}{d\over d\tau}+ia^{\prime}_{\mu}(\tau)n_{\nu}-in_{\mu}a^{\prime}_{\nu}(\tau)

\displaystyle J_{\mu}(\tau)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}d\bar{\tau}\Bigl(a^{\prime}_{\mu}(\bar{\tau})-\langle\langle a^{\prime}_{\mu}\rangle\rangle\Bigr)\,,

(31)

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{\mu}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle J_{\mu}(\tau)-J_{\mu}(\tau^{\prime})-\frac{T}{2}\dot{G}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\langle\langle a^{\prime}_{\mu}\rangle\rangle

\hskip-15.00002pt\mathfrak{G}_{F}^{\mu\nu}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=G_{F}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\biggl\{\delta^{\mu\nu}+2in^{\mu}{\cal J}^{\nu}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})+2i{\cal J}^{\mu}(\tau^{\prime},\tau)n^{\nu}+2\Bigl[{\cal J}^{2}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})-\frac{T^{2}}{4}\langle\langle a^{\prime}\rangle\rangle^{2}\Bigr]n^{\mu}n^{\nu}\biggr\}\,.

(33)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{{\rm spin},N}=\sum_{S=0}^{N}\Gamma_{NS}\,,\quad\Gamma_{NS}=\sum_{\{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{S}\}}\Gamma_{NS}^{\{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{S}\}}\,,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{NS}^{\{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{S}\}}

\displaystyle-2(-e)^{N}(2\pi)^{3}\delta\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}^{1}\bigr)\delta\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}^{2}\bigr)\delta\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}^{+}\bigr)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx_{0}^{+}\,{\rm e}^{-ix_{0}^{+}\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}^{-}}

(35)

\displaystyle\hskip-50.0pt\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dT}{T}\,{(4\pi T)}^{-{D\over 2}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}d\tau_{i}\,{\rm e}^{-\bigl(m^{2}+\langle\langle a^{2}\rangle\rangle-\langle\langle a\rangle\rangle^{2}\bigr)T}\,

\mathfrak{P}_{NS}
are defined by

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\big(-i\dot{G}_{ij}\varepsilon_{i}\cdot k_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\ddot{G}_{ij}\varepsilon_{i}\cdot\varepsilon_{j}\big)-2i\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(a(\tau_{i})-\langle\langle a\rangle\rangle\bigr)\cdot\varepsilon_{i}}\big|_{\varepsilon_{i_{1}}=\cdots=\varepsilon_{i_{S}}=0}\big|_{\varepsilon_{i_{S+1}}\cdots\varepsilon_{i_{N}}}

\displaystyle\equiv(-i)^{N-S}{\mathfrak{P}}_{NS}^{\{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{S}\}}\,,

6
Summary and Outlook

We have used the worldline formalism to derive master formulas for the
N
-photon amplitudes in a background field
that is a combination of a constant field with a plane-wave one, for the special “parallel” configuration that has been known
to be particularly amenable to an analytical treatment. As usual in QED applications, the formalism has allowed us to unify the scalar and spinor QED cases,
and to significantly reduce the algebraic complexity by avoiding the break-up of the scalar or fermion loop into individual propagators.
All the effects of the addition of the constant external field have been absorbed into generalizations of the various worldline Green’s functions,
global determinant factors and the single quantity
{\tilde{J}}^{\mu}(\tau)
defined in (
).
These advantages over the standard diagrammatic approach will naturally become increasingly more pronounced with increasing number of photons.

In a separate publication we will present the application of the master formula to the
N=2
case, that is, the vacuum polarization
and associated helicity flip in scalar and spinor QED.

Further generalization from the closed-loop to the open-line case should be feasible along the lines of
[
56
,
57
,
58
]
.

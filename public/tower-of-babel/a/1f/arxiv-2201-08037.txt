Abstract




Annotation.

We obtain some asymptotic formulae (with power savings in their error terms) for the number of quadruples in the Cartesian product of an arbitrary set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}
and for the number of quintuplets in
A\times A
for any subset
A
of the prime field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Also, we obtain some applications of our results to incidence problems in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

1
Introduction

Incidence Geometry (see, e.g.,
[
13
, section 8]
) deals with the incidences among different geometrical objects such as points, lines, curves, surfaces etc. A typical problem of this area is to estimate the quantity

\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{L}):=|\{(p,l)\in\mathcal{P}\times\mathcal{L}:\,p\in l\}|\,,

(1)

where the set of points
\mathcal{P}
and the set of lines
\mathcal{L}
belong to
\mathbb{F}\times\mathbb{F}
with
\mathbb{F}
be a field, say.
In our paper the set of points
\mathcal{P}
will be the Cartesian product
A\times B
for some sets
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{F}
. This particular choice of
{\cal P}
is very important for the applications see, e.g.,
[
2
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
13
]
because, basically, Cartesian products are naturally connected with arithmetic.
In this note we study collinear tuples in
A\times B
.
Namely, for any
k\geqslant 3

we define
{\mathcal{C}}_{k}(A,B)
to be the number of collinear
k
–tuples in
A\times B
. Let
{\mathcal{C}}_{k}(A)={\mathcal{C}}_{k}(A,A)
.
A consequence of the famous Szemerédi–Trotter Theorem
[
12
]
gives us that for any
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}
one has

{\mathcal{C}}_{3}(A)\ll|A|^{4}\log|A|

(2)

(a short proof of this result contains in
[
10
]
).
Actually, for
{\cal P}=A\times A
it is easy to see that bound (
2
) is
equivalent
to the Szemerédi–Trotter Theorem (up to logarithms).
In our paper
[
2
]
we have obtained an asymptotic formula for the number of collinear quadruples in the case of the prime field
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{p}

{\mathcal{C}}_{4}(A)=\frac{|A|^{8}}{p^{2}}+O(|A|^{5}\log|A|)\,.

(3)

The proof is based on Stevens–de Zeeuw’s Theorem
[
11
]
.
Both bounds (
2
), (
3
) are known to be tight up to some powers of logarithms as the case
A=\{1,\dots,n\}
shows, namely,
{\mathcal{C}}_{3}(A)\gg|A|^{4}\log^{c}|A|
,
c>0
for such
A
(see discussion in
[
2
, Pages 603, 633]
).
The problem of finding sharp asymptotic formula for the number of collinear triples in
A\times A
,
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
is a well–known important open question and at the moment the best result is (see
[
2
]
,
[
6
]
)

{\mathcal{C}}_{3}(A)=\frac{|A|^{6}}{p}+O(\min\{|A|^{9/2},p^{1/2}|A|^{7/2}\})\,.

(4)

As for
{\mathcal{C}}_{k}(A)
with large
k
, then one can easily obtain an analogues of formulae (
2
), (
3
) but, generally speaking, the error term must be at least
2|A|^{k+1}

(take
horizontal/vertical lines), and hence the consideration of large
k
does not
give anything new.

In this paper we make a further step and obtain a paucity result (see Theorems
22
,
29
below) for higher
{\mathcal{C}}_{k}(A)
.
As we said before, in general, one cannot obtain non–trivial bounds for such quantities and thus we need some restrictions, which we formulate in terms of
energies
.
For any two sets
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{F}
the
additive energy
of
A
and
B
is defined by

\mathsf{E}^{+}(A,B)=|\{(a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2})\in A\times A\times B\times B~:~a_{1}-b_{1}=a_{2}-b_{2}\}|\,.

If
A=B
, then we simply write
\mathsf{E}^{+}(A)
for
\mathsf{E}^{+}(A,A)
. Similarly, one can define the
multiplicative energy
of
A
and
B
. Finally, put
\overline{\mathsf{E}}^{\times}(A)=\max_{s\in\mathbb{F}}\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A-s)\leqslant|A|^{3}
.

Theorem 1

Let
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}
be sets.
Then

{\mathcal{C}}_{4}(A,B)-|B||A|^{4}-|A||B|^{4}\ll|B||A|^{3}+|A||B|^{3}+

(\mathsf{E}^{+}(A)\mathsf{E}^{+}(B))^{1/10}{\mathcal{C}}^{2/5}_{3}(A,B)|A|^{8/5}|B|^{6/5}\log^{2/5}|A|+(\overline{\mathsf{E}}^{\times}(A)\overline{\mathsf{E}}^{\times}(B))^{1/4}|A|^{2}|B|^{3/2}\log^{3/2}|A|\,.

(5)

Further let
p
be a prime number,
A\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Then

{\mathcal{C}}_{5}(A)-\frac{|A|^{10}}{p^{3}}-2|A|^{6}\lesssim\frac{|A|^{7}}{p}+\frac{|A|^{4}}{p^{2}}{\mathcal{C}}_{3}(A)+(\mathsf{E}^{+}(A))^{1/6}|A|^{11/2}+(\overline{\mathsf{E}}^{\times}(A))^{1/2}|A|^{9/2}\,.

(6)

Corollary 2

Let
A,B\subset{\mathbb{R}}
,
A=B
and
\mathsf{E}^{+}(A),\overline{\mathsf{E}}^{\times}(A)\leqslant|A|^{3-c}
, where
c>0
. Substituting estimate (
2
) to Theorem
6
, we get

{\mathcal{C}}_{4}(A)-2|A|^{5}\ll|A|^{5-c/5}\log^{4/5}|A|\,,

(7)

and in the case of the prime field and, say,
|A|\leqslant p^{2/3}
, we have

{\mathcal{C}}_{5}(A)-2|A|^{6}\lesssim|A|^{6-c/6}\log^{5/6}|A|\,.

(8)

Once again the condition
\mathsf{E}^{+}(A)\leqslant|A|^{3-c}
is required for (
7
), (
8
).
One can easily see that a random set
A
satisfies both assumptions

\mathsf{E}^{+}(A),\overline{\mathsf{E}}^{\times}(A)\leqslant|A|^{3-c}
, where
c>0
. Also, there are some concrete constructions of such sets, see section
3
, namely, one can take
A=B^{-1}+s
for any
s\in\mathbb{F}
and
B\subseteq\mathbb{F}
such that
|B+B|\ll|B|
and then
\mathsf{E}^{+}(A),\overline{\mathsf{E}}^{\times}(A)\leqslant|A|^{3-c}
.
It seems like that our Theorem
6
is the first paucity result in the area.
As for lower bounds on
{\mathcal{C}}_{k}(A)
we trivially have
\Omega(|A|^{k})
in the error term, counting
k
–tuples, which belong to the diagonal
(x,x)
, where
x
runs over
A
.

We obtain some applications of our paucity theorems in section
4
.
For example, let us formulate a consequence of our new incidence result in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
concerning points/lines incidences.
The basic regime here is the following: the sets
A
,
B
are large and the sets
X,Y
are small comparable to
p
.

Theorem 3

Let
k\geqslant 2
be an integer,
M\geqslant 1
be a real number,
A,B,C,X,Y\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
0\notin X
be sets,
|YC|\leqslant M|Y|
,
and
|C|\gtrsim_{k}M^{2^{k+1}}
,
|C|^{k-1}\geqslant(p/|Y|)^{2}
.
Suppose that

\max\{\mathsf{E}^{+}(B),\overline{\mathsf{E}}^{\times}(B)\}\leqslant|B|^{3-\delta}\,,

where
\delta>0
is a constant.
Then

|\{(a,b,x,y)\in A\times B\times X\times Y~:~y=bx+a\}|-\frac{|A||B||X||Y|}{p}\ll\sqrt{|A||B|}|X||Y|\cdot|B|^{-\frac{\delta}{35\cdot 2^{k}}}\,.

(9)

There are two main advantages of Theorem
9
. First of all, it is an asymptotic formula (and, again, the set
X\times Y
, which corresponds to the lines
\mathcal{L}
can be rather small, even of size
p^{\varepsilon}
) but not just upper bounds for
{\cal I}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{L})
as in
[
11
]
, say.
Some asymptotic formulae for the quantity
{\cal I}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{L})
were known before in the specific case of large sets (see
[
14
]
)
and in the case of Cartesian products but with large sets of lines, see
[
6
]
and
[
11
]
.
Such asymptotic formulae are important in the problem of estimating exponential sums (see, e.g.,
[
6
]
) and in the questions on mixing times of some Markov chains
[
9
]
, where one requires to have the set of points
A\times B
to be really large. The second advantage of Theorem
9
is that it works even for
|A|,|B|\gg p
(for size of the set
B
see the exact formulation of Theorem
32
below).

2
Definitions and preliminaries

By
{\mathbf{G}}
we denote an abelian group.
Sometimes we underline the group operation writing
+
or
\times
in the considered quantities (as the energy, the representation function and so on, see below).
Let
\mathbb{F}
be the field
{\mathbb{R}}
or
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{p}=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
for a prime
p
. Let
\mathbb{F}^{*}=\mathbb{F}\setminus\{0\}
.

We use the same capital letter to denote set
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}
and its characteristic function
A:\mathbb{F}\to\{0,1\}

and in the case of finite
\mathbb{F}
we write
f_{A}(x):=A(x)-|A|/|\mathbb{F}|
for the
balanced function
of
A
.
Given two sets
A,B\subset{\mathbf{G}}
, define
the
sumset

of
A
and
B
as

A+B:=\{a+b~:~a\in{A},\,b\in{B}\}\,.

In a similar way we define the
difference sets
and
higher sumsets
, e.g.,
2A-A
is
A+A-A
.
We write
\dotplus
for a direct sum, i.e.,
|A\dotplus B|=|A||B|
.
For an abelian group
{\mathbf{G}}

the Plünnecke–Ruzsa inequality (see, e.g.,
[
13
]
) holds stating

|nA-mA|\leqslant\left(\frac{|A+A|}{|A|}\right)^{n+m}\cdot|A|\,,

(10)

where
n,m
are any positive integers.
We use representation function notations like
r_{A+B}(x)
or
r_{A-B}(x)
and so on, which counts the number of ways
x\in{\mathbf{G}}
can be expressed as a sum
a+b
or
a-b
with
a\in A
,
b\in B
, respectively.
For example,
|A|=r_{A-A}(0)
.

For any two sets
A,B\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
the
additive energy
of
A
and
B
is defined by

\mathsf{E}(A,B)=\mathsf{E}^{+}(A,B)=|\{(a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2})\in A\times A\times B\times B~:~a_{1}-b_{1}=a_{2}-b_{2}\}|\,.

If
A=B
, then we simply write
\mathsf{E}(A)
for
\mathsf{E}(A,A)
.
More generally, for sets (real functions)
A_{1},\dots,A_{2k}
(
f_{1},\dots,f_{2k}
) belonging to an arbitrary (noncommutative) group
{\mathbf{G}}
and

k\geqslant 2
define the energy

\mathsf{T}_{k}(A_{1},\dots,A_{2k})
as

\mathsf{T}_{k}(A_{1},\dots,A_{2k})=

=|\{(a_{1},\dots,a_{2k})\in A_{1}\times\dots\times A_{2k}~:~a_{1}a^{-1}_{2}\dots a_{k-1}a^{-1}_{k}=a_{k+1}a^{-1}_{k+2}\dots a_{2k-1}a^{-1}_{2k}\}|\,,

(11)

and

\mathsf{T}_{k}(f_{1},\dots,f_{2k})=\sum_{a_{1}a^{-1}_{2}\dots a_{k-1}a^{-1}_{k}=a_{k+1}a^{-1}_{k+2}\dots a_{2k-1}a^{-1}_{2k}}f_{1}(a_{1})\dots f_{2k}(a_{2k})\,.

In the abelian case put for
k\geqslant 2

\mathsf{E}^{+}_{k}(A)=\sum_{x}r^{k}_{A-A}(x)=\sum_{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k-1}}|A\cap(A+\alpha_{1})\cap\dots\cap(A+\alpha_{k-1})|^{2}\,.

(12)

Clearly,
|A|^{k}\leqslant\mathsf{E}^{+}_{k}(A)\leqslant|A|^{k+1}
.
Having a function
f:\mathbb{F}\to\mathbb{C}
, we put
\overline{\mathsf{E}}^{\times}(f)=\max_{s\in\mathbb{F}}\mathsf{E}^{\times}(f-s)
.
In particular,
\overline{\mathsf{E}}^{\times}(A)\leqslant|A|^{3}
for any finite set
A
,
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}
.

The signs
\ll
and
\gg
are the usual Vinogradov symbols.
When the constants in the signs depend on a parameter
M
, we write
\ll_{M}
and
\gg_{M}
.
All logarithms are to base
2
.
If we have a set
A
, then we will write
a\lesssim b
or
b\gtrsim a
if
a=O(b\cdot\log^{c}|A|)
,
c>0
.
Let us denote by
[n]
the set
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.

Given an arbitrary line
l

denote by
i_{A,B}(l)=|l\cap(A\times B)|
hence in particular,
\mathcal{I}(A\times B,\mathcal{L})=\sum_{l\in L}i_{A,B}(l)
.
Clearly, for
k\geqslant 3
one has

{\mathcal{C}}_{k}(A,B)=\sum_{l~:~i_{A,B}(l)>1}i^{k}_{A,B}(l)+O_{k}(|A|^{2}|B|^{2}\cdot\left(\min\{|A|,|B|\}\right)^{k-3}\,.

(13)

One can think about the first term in (
13
) as another definition of the quantity
{\mathcal{C}}_{k}(A,B)
.

We need a result on the energy of an arbitrary set of affine transformations, see
[
3
, Theorem 2.1]
.
We consider any set of affine transformations
\mathcal{L}\subseteq{\rm Aff}(\mathbb{F})
as a subset of
\mathbb{F}^{*}\times\mathbb{F}
, i.e., we associate a map
x\to ax+b
with a point
(a,b)\in\mathbb{F}^{*}\times\mathbb{F}
.

Theorem 4

Let
\mathcal{L}\subseteq{\rm Aff}(\mathbb{F})
be a set of lines such that no line in
\mathbb{F}^{*}\times\mathbb{F}
contains more than
M(\mathcal{L})
points of
\mathcal{L}
, and no vertical line in
\mathbb{F}^{*}\times\mathbb{F}
contains more than
m(\mathcal{L})
points of
\mathcal{L}
.
Suppose that
m(\mathcal{L})|\mathcal{L}|\leqslant p^{2}
.
Then

\mathsf{E}(\mathcal{L})\ll m^{1/2}(\mathcal{L})|\mathcal{L}|^{5/2}+M(\mathcal{L})|\mathcal{L}|^{2}\,.

The following result is contained in
[
9
, Proposition 7]
.
Here we have considered the case of the prime field (which is more difficult) and the case of the real numbers can be treated in analogues way.

Proposition 5

Let
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{F}
be sets and
\mathcal{L}
be a set of affine transformations over
\mathbb{F}
.
Then for any positive integer
k
one has

{\cal I}(A\times B,\mathcal{L})-\frac{|A||B||\mathcal{L}|}{|\mathbb{F}|}\ll\sqrt{|A||B||\mathcal{L}|}\cdot(\mathsf{T}_{2^{k}}(\mathcal{L})|A|\log|A|)^{1/2^{k+2}}\,.

(14)

More precisely, if
\mathbb{F}={\mathbb{R}}
, then

{\cal I}(A\times B,\mathcal{L})\ll\sqrt{|A||B|}|\mathcal{L}|^{1/3}\cdot(\mathsf{T}_{2^{k}}(\mathcal{L})|A|\log|A|)^{1/3\cdot 2^{k}}\,.

(15)

We need the following structural result on the higher energies, see
[
8
, Theorem 4]
.

Theorem 6

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be an abelian group,
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a set,
\delta,\varepsilon\in(0,1]
be parameters,
\varepsilon\leqslant\delta
.

1)~
Then there is
k=k(\delta,\varepsilon)=\exp(O(\varepsilon^{-1}\log(1/\delta)))
such that either
\mathsf{E}_{k}(A)\leqslant|A|^{k+\delta}
or there is
H\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
,
|H|\gtrsim|A|^{\delta(1-\varepsilon)}
,
|H+H|\ll|A|^{\varepsilon}|H|

and there exists
Z\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
,
|Z||H|\ll|A|^{1+\varepsilon}

with

|(H\dotplus Z)\cap A|\gg|A|^{1-\varepsilon}\,.

2)~
Similarly, either there is a set
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
,
|A^{\prime}|\gg|A|^{1-\varepsilon}
and
P\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
,
|P|\gtrsim|A|^{\delta}
such that for all
x\in A^{\prime}
one has
r_{A-P}(x)\gg|P||A|^{-\varepsilon}
or
\mathsf{E}_{k}(A)\leqslant|A|^{k+\delta}
with
k\ll 1/\varepsilon
.

Thus either
\mathsf{E}_{k}(A)\leqslant|A|^{k+\delta}
, or
A
has some strong structural properties depending on the parameters
\delta
and
\varepsilon
. In the later case we say that
A
is
\mathsf{E}_{k}
–exceptional with
\delta,\varepsilon
.

We use a simplified version of

[
7
, Theorem 5]
, as well as
[
9
, Lemma 9]

in our last section.

Theorem 7

Let
k\geqslant 2
be an integer,
M\geqslant 1
be a real number,

A,B\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
be sets,
|AB|\leqslant M|A|
, and
|B|\gtrsim_{k}M^{2^{k+1}}
.
Then

\mathsf{T}^{+}_{2^{k}}(A)\lesssim_{k}M^{2^{k+1}}\left(\frac{|A|^{2^{k+1}}}{p}+|A|^{2^{k+1}-1}\cdot|B|^{-\frac{k-1}{2}}\right)\,.

(16)

Lemma 8

Let
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
be sets, and
\mathcal{L}=\{(a,b)~:~a\in A,\,b\in B\}\subseteq{\rm Aff}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
.
Then for any
k\geqslant 2
one has

\mathsf{T}_{k}(f_{\mathcal{L}})\leqslant|A|^{2k-1}\mathsf{T}^{+}_{k}(f_{B})\,.

(17)

3
The proof of the main result

First of all, combining Theorem
4
and Proposition
15
, we obtain a result on ”rich”  lines (i.e., lines having large intersections with
A\times B
) in terms of the quantities
m(\mathcal{L})
and
M(\mathcal{L})
.
We follow the method from
[
5
]
and
[
3
]
.
Given a set
\mathcal{L}
of affine transformations over
\mathbb{F}
and a real number
\tau\geqslant 2
put

\mathcal{L}_{\tau}=\mathcal{L}_{\tau}(A):=\{l\in\mathcal{L}~:~|l\cap(A\times B)|\geqslant\tau\}\,.

Formulae (
18
), (
19
) below can be treated as an alternative (to the Szemerédi–Trotter type estimates) upper bound for size of
|\mathcal{L}_{\tau}|
.

Corollary 9

Let
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{F}
be sets and
\mathcal{L}
be a set of affine transformations over
\mathbb{F}
.
Also, let
\tau\geqslant 1
be a real number,
\frac{2|A||B|}{|\mathbb{F}|}\leqslant\tau
and
m(\mathcal{L}_{\tau})|\mathcal{L}_{\tau}|\leqslant|\mathbb{F}|^{2}
.
Then

|\mathcal{L}_{\tau}|\ll\tau^{-16/3}m^{1/3}(\mathcal{L}_{\tau})|A|^{10/3}|B|^{8/3}\log^{2/3}|A|+\tau^{-4}M^{1/2}(\mathcal{L}_{\tau})|A|^{5/2}|B|^{2}\log^{1/2}|A|\,.

(18)

More precisely, if
\mathbb{F}={\mathbb{R}}
, then

|\mathcal{L}_{\tau}|\ll\tau^{-4}(m(\mathcal{L}_{\tau})|A|^{8}|B|^{6}\log^{2}|A|)^{1/3}+\tau^{-3}M^{1/2}(\mathcal{L}_{\tau})|A|^{2}|B|^{3/2}\log^{1/2}|A|\,.

(19)

P r o o f.

Let
m=m(\mathcal{L}_{\tau})
and
M=M(\mathcal{L}_{\tau})
.
By Proposition
15
with
k=1
, the definition of the set
\mathcal{L}_{\tau}
and our condition
\frac{2|A||B|}{|\mathbb{F}|}\leqslant\tau
, we get

\tau|\mathcal{L}_{\tau}|\ll\sqrt{|A||B||\mathcal{L}_{\tau}|}\cdot(\mathsf{E}(\mathcal{L}_{\tau})|A|\log|A|)^{1/8}\,.

We now apply Theorem
4
and obtain

\tau|\mathcal{L}_{\tau}|\ll\sqrt{|A||B||\mathcal{L}_{\tau}|}(|A|\log|A|)^{1/8}\cdot(m^{1/2}|\mathcal{L}_{\tau}|^{5/2}+M|\mathcal{L}_{\tau}|^{2})^{1/8}\,.

(20)

The last inequality is equivalent to (
18
).
To obtain estimate (
19
) we just use formula (
15
) instead of (
14
).
This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

Now let us give simple upper bounds for
the quantities
m(\mathcal{L}_{\tau})
,
M(\mathcal{L}_{\tau})
.

Lemma 10

Let
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{F}
be sets,
\tau\geqslant 2
and
\mathcal{L}
be a set of affine transformations over
\mathbb{F}
.
Then for any integer
k\geqslant 2
one has

m(\mathcal{L}_{\tau})\leqslant\tau^{-k}(\mathsf{E}^{+}_{k}(A)\mathsf{E}^{+}_{k}(B))^{1/2}\quad\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\quad M(\mathcal{L}_{\tau})\leqslant\tau^{-k}(\overline{\mathsf{E}}^{\times}_{k}(A)\overline{\mathsf{E}}^{\times}_{k}(B))^{1/2}\,.

P r o o f.

Let
m=m(\mathcal{L}_{\tau})
and
M=M(\mathcal{L}_{\tau})
.
By the definition of the quantity
m
we can find
m
lines in
\mathcal{L}_{\tau}
having the form
\alpha x+\beta
, where
\alpha\neq 0
is a fixed number and
\beta
runs over a set
\mathcal{B}
of cardinality
m
.
Using the definition of the set
\mathcal{L}_{\tau}
and
the Hölder inequality,
we obtain

\tau m\leqslant\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{B}}\,\sum_{x\in B}A(\alpha x+\beta)=\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{B}}r_{\alpha B-A}(\beta)\leqslant m^{1-1/k}(\mathsf{E}^{+}_{k}(\alpha B,A))^{1/k}\leqslant m^{1-1/k}(\mathsf{E}^{+}_{k}(A)\mathsf{E}^{+}_{k}(B))^{1/2k}\,.

Similarly, by the definition of the quantity
M
we find
M
lines concurrent at a certain point
(x_{0},y_{0})
.
We parameterise them by their slopes:
\beta=\frac{y-y_{0}}{x-x_{0}}
,
\beta\in\mathcal{B}
,
|\mathcal{B}|=M
. Hence as above

\tau M\leqslant\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{B}}\,r_{(A-y_{0})/(B-x_{0})}(\beta)\leqslant M^{1-1/k}(\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A-y_{0},B-x_{0}))^{1/2}\leqslant M^{1-1/k}(\overline{\mathsf{E}}^{\times}_{k}(A)\overline{\mathsf{E}}^{\times}_{k}(B))^{1/2k}\,.

This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

Now we are ready to obtain our main result in the case of the real field and a result on
{\mathcal{C}}_{5}(A,B)
for small
A
and
B
in the case of the prime field.

Theorem 11

Let
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}
be sets.
Then

{\mathcal{C}}_{4}(A,B)-|B||A|^{4}-|A||B|^{4}\ll|B||A|^{3}+|A||B|^{3}+

(\mathsf{E}^{+}(A)\mathsf{E}^{+}(B))^{1/10}{\mathcal{C}}^{2/5}_{3}(A,B)|A|^{8/5}|B|^{6/5}\log^{2/5}|A|+(\overline{\mathsf{E}}^{\times}(A)\overline{\mathsf{E}}^{\times}(B))^{1/4}|A|^{2}|B|^{3/2}\log^{3/2}|A|\,.

(21)

If
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}_{4}(A,B)\log|A|\leqslant p|A|^{5}|B|^{4}
and
|A|^{5}|B|^{6}\leqslant p^{9}
, then

{\mathcal{C}}_{5}(A,B)-|B||A|^{5}-|A||B|^{5}\ll|B||A|^{4}+|A||B|^{4}+{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{5}(A,B)\,,

where the error term
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{5}(A,B)
is at most

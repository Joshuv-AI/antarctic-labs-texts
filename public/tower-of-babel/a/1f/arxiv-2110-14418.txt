Harvesting of a Stochastic Population under a Mixed Regular-Singular Control Formulation
Ky Q. Tran, Bich T. N. Le, George Yin

Abstract
This work focuses on optimal harvesting-renewing for a stochastic population. A mixed regular-singular control formulation with a state constraint and regime-switching is introduced. The decision makers either harvest or renew with finite or infinite harvesting/renewing rates. The payoff functions depend on the harvesting/renewing rates. Several properties of the value functions are established. The limiting value function as the white noise intensity approaches infinity is identified. The Markov chain approximation method is used to find numerical approximation of the value function and optimal strategies. Key words. Harvesting problem, controlled diffusion, singular control, state constraint, Markovian switching

Introduction

This work focuses on harvesting and renewing strategies for stochastic ecosystems that are represented by controlled
stochastic differential equations. Mathematically, the problem we consider
belongs to a
class of singular stochastic control problems, namely, harvesting-type problems.
Such problems have been studied extensively in various settings for different domain of applications; see
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
19
,
24
]
. To mention just a few of the recent developments, we refer to
[
25
,
26
]

for single species and interacting population systems with regime switching.
The paper
[
6
]
focuses on sustainable harvesting policies under long-run average criteria, which are further studied in
[
18
]
; see also
[
21
]
for a related work on a predator-prey system and
[
15
]

for an ergodic two-sided singular control formulation. In
[
7
,
8
]
, the authors study ecosystems in which
both renewing and harvesting actions are included. Optimal exploitation problems of renewable natural resources, which are harvesting-type problems, are studied in
[
14
,
27
]
. Related works on a general one-dimensional diffusion that is reflected at zero can be found in
[
4
]
and references therein. Intensive treatment of stochastic population systems and hybrid diffusions can be found in
[
20
,
28
]
, while applications in various areas of singular stochastic control
can be found in
[
5
,
23
]
and many references therein.

In this work, we propose a generalized harvesting model for a stochastic population.
The controller can perform either a regular control or a singular control to harvest and renew the species.
Moreover, we also consider the control objective
associated with renewing and harvesting. It should be noted that in optimal harvesting formulations in
[
1
,
2
,
25
,
7
,
8
]
, the control cost is simply combined with the price functions.
As a result, it is frequently seen that when the manager decides to harvest (resp., renew), she should do that with the maximal possible harvesting rates (resp. renewing rates); see
[
1
,
7
,
8
]
. In
[
14
,
27
]
, interesting phenomena appear when the control objective
is taken into account. For instance, the maximal possible rates are no longer optimal for harvesting and renewing in certain cases.
With the generalized formulation proposed in this work, the distinctions are even more pronounced. In addition, as
a new twist,
state constraint is considered
in this work.
In particular, the time horizon of the control problem is
[0,\tau]
where
\tau
is the first time the population process is
below
a predetermined level. Another important
issue of interest is the impact of a white noise with large intensity. For Kolmogorov-type ecosystems, it is known that very large white noise make the species extinct, and have a major impact on harvesting actions; see
[
1
,
20
,
26
]
.
A novelty of the paper is the identification of
the limiting value function as the white noise intensity approaches infinity for a general stochastic population.

In contrast to the existing results, our new contributions in this
paper are as follows.
(i) We formulate a harvesting problem with renewing and the consideration of control objective,
a state constraint, and regime-switching. Both bounded and unbounded harvesting-renewing rates are allowed.
(ii) We establish the finiteness and the continuity of the value function. We show that in common cases, it is optimal to keep the population size in a compact set. (iii) We study the impact of a white noise with large intensity on harvesting.
(iv) Based on the Markov chain approximation method, we construct a controlled
Markov chain to approximate the given controlled population. It enables us to approximate the value function and near-optimal strategies.

The rest of our work is organized as follows. Section 2 begins with
the problem formulation. Section 3 focuses on properties of the value function and the impact of a white noise with large intensity. In Section 4, we construct a controlled Markov chain to approximate the given controlled population system.
Finally, the paper is concluded
with several numerical examples for illustration together with
additional remarks in the last section.

2
Formulation

We work with a complete filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},{\mathbb{P}},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\})
with the filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}
satisfying the usual condition
(i.e., it is right-continuous and
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
contains all the null sets). Let
\mathbb{R}_{+}=[0,\infty)
and
\mathbb{Z}_{+}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
.
For a real number
x
, we denote
x^{+}=\max\{x,0\}
and
x^{-}=\max\{-x,0\}
. Thus,
x=x^{+}-x^{-}
and
|x|=x^{+}+x^{-}
.
Suppose that the population size
\xi(t)
of a species at time
t
is given by

d\xi(t)=b\big(\xi(t),\Lambda(t)\big)dt+\sigma\big(\xi(t),\Lambda(t)\big)dw(t).

(2.1)

In the model,
w(\cdot)
is a
one-dimensional standard Brownian motion and
\Lambda(\cdot)
is a continuous time Markov chain. Moreover,
\Lambda(\cdot)
and
w(\cdot)
are independent and
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}
-adapted.
Suppose
\Lambda(\cdot)
takes values in
\mathcal{M}=\{1,2,\dots,m_{0}\}
with
generator
\Gamma=(\Gamma_{\alpha\ell})_{m_{0}\times m_{0}}
and
m_{0}
being a positive integer.
The coefficients
b(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\sigma(\cdot,\cdot)
are real-valued functions defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times{\cal{M}}
.
The transition probabilities of

\Lambda(\cdot)
is described by

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\mathbb{P}}\{\Lambda(t+\Delta t)=\ell|\Lambda(t)=\alpha\}=\begin{cases}\Gamma_{\alpha\ell}\Delta t+o(\Delta t)&\ \ \text{if}\ \ \alpha\not=\ell,\\
1+\Gamma_{\alpha\alpha}\Delta t+o(\Delta t)&\ \ \text{if}\ \ \alpha=\ell.\end{cases}\end{array}

(2.2)

Note that
\Gamma_{\alpha\ell}\geq 0
if
\alpha\neq\ell
and
\sum_{\ell\in{\cal{M}}}\Gamma_{\alpha\ell}=0
for any
\alpha\in{\cal{M}}
.
To illustrate, consider the stochastic logistic population growth model given by

dX(t)=X(t)\Big(\kappa_{1}(\Lambda(t))-\kappa_{2}(\Lambda(t))X(t)\Big)dt+\kappa_{3}(\Lambda(t))X(t)dw(t),

(2.3)

where
\kappa_{1}(\cdot),\kappa_{2}(\cdot),\kappa_{3}(\cdot)
are real-valued functions defined on the state space
{\cal{M}}
of the Markov chain
\Lambda(\cdot)
.
The switching component
\Lambda(\cdot)
is introduced to capture the major environmental shifts (daily or seasonal changes or catastrophes) leading to the changes in the carrying capacities and interactions in different environments.
To visualize the dynamics of
(
2.3
),
without loss of generality, assume that
\Lambda(0)=\alpha
. Then the
Markov chain rests in state
\alpha
for an exponentially distributed random duration,
in this time interval,
the model given by (
2.3
) obeys

dX(t)=X(t)\Big(\kappa_{1}(\alpha)-\kappa_{2}(\alpha)X(t)\Big)dt+\kappa_{3}(\alpha)X(t)dw(t),

until the Markov chain
\Lambda(\cdot)
jumps to another state
\ell
. Then the model (
2.3
) obeys

dX(t)=X(t)\Big(\kappa_{1}(\ell)-\kappa_{2}(\ell)X(t)\Big)dt+\kappa_{3}(\ell)X(t)dw(t)

for an exponentially distributed random
time until the Markov chain
\Lambda(\cdot)
jumps to a new state again
and so on.

To proceed, we introduce the generator of the process
\big(\xi(t),\Lambda(t)\big)
.
For a function
\Phi(\cdot,\cdot):\mathbb{R}\times{\cal{M}}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\Phi(\cdot,\alpha)\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for each
\alpha\in{\cal{M}}
, we define

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\cal L}\Phi(x,\alpha)&\!\!\!\displaystyle=b(x,\alpha)\Phi^{\prime}(x,\alpha)+\dfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}(x,\alpha)\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x,\alpha)+\sum\limits_{\ell\in{\cal{M}}}\Gamma_{\alpha\ell}\Phi(x,\ell),\end{array}

where
\Phi^{\prime}(\cdot,\alpha)
and
\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\cdot,\alpha)
denote the first and second derivatives
(w.r.t.
x
) of
\Phi(\cdot,\alpha)
, respectively.

Next, we suppose that the population can be instantaneously harvested,
instantaneously renewed,
harvested with bounded rates, or renewed with bounded rates. In order to harvest or renew instantaneously (that is, harvest or renew with infinite rates), the controller needs to exercise an impulsive control. Meanwhile, to
harvest or renew with bounded rates, the controller performs a regular control.
Specifically,
we
assume
the dynamics of the species is given by

\begin{array}[]{ll}X(t)&\!\!\!\displaystyle=x+\int_{0}^{t}b\big(X(s),\Lambda(s)\big)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma\big(X(s),\Lambda(s)\big)dw(s)\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\int_{0}^{t}f\big(X(s),C(s)\big)ds-Y(t)+Z(t),\end{array}

(2.4)

where
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

X(t)
is the population size at time
t\geq 0
,
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{U}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is the harvesting-renewing rate corresponding to the control
C(\cdot)
taking values in a nonempty compact set
\mathcal{U}
in
\mathbb{R}
, while
Y(\cdot)
and
Z(\cdot)
are impulsive controls.
In particular,

Y(t)
denotes the amount of the species that has been instantaneously harvested up to time
t
, while
Z(t)
denotes the amount of the species that has been instantaneously renewed up to time
t
.

Notation.
For each time
t
,

X(t-)
represents the state
before harvesting or renewing starts at time
t
, while
X(t)
is the state immediately after. We assume the initial population size
to be

X(0-)=x
and initial regime
to be

\Lambda(0)=\alpha
, respectively.
Hence
X(0)
may not be equal to
X(0-)
due to an impulsive harvesting
Y(0)
or an impulsive renewing
Z(0)
. Throughout the paper we use the convention that
Y(0-)=Z(0-)=0
.
The jump sizes of
Y(\cdot)
and
Z(\cdot)
at time
t
are denoted by
\Delta Y(t)=Y(t)-Y(t-)
and
\Delta Z(t)=Z(t)-Z(t-)
, respectively. Thus,

Y(t)=\sum\limits_{0\leq s\leq t}\Delta Y(s),\ \ Z(t)=\sum\limits_{0\leq s\leq t}\Delta Z(s).

Suppose
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and denote

\mathcal{S}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}:x\geq\lambda\}.

In this work,
we consider the harvesting problem on the time horizon
[0,\tau]
, where

\tau=\inf\{t\geq 0:X(t)\notin\mathcal{S}\}.

The price per unit of the species is a positive constant
a_{1}
.
The harvesting and renewing control is costly. Consider a function
g:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times{\cal{M}}\times\mathcal{U}\mapsto\mathbb{R}_{+}
. The accumulated cost of the regular control
C(\cdot)
is

\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\delta s}g\big(X(s),\Lambda(s),C(s)\big)ds,

where
\delta>0
is the discount factor.
Note that we
separate cost and income here. Later we will take into account
of both and set up the payoff.
Regarding the regular control
C(\cdot)
, the accumulated income of selling the harvested amount is
\int_{0}^{{\tau}}e^{-\delta s}a_{1}f^{+}\big(X(s),C(s)\big)ds
and the accumulated expense of the renewed amount is
\int_{0}^{{\tau}}e^{-\delta s}a_{1}f^{-}\big(X(s),C(s)\big)ds
.
For notational simplicity, we define the price-cost function
p:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times{\cal{M}}\times\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

p(x,\alpha,c)=a_{1}f(x,c)-g(x,\alpha,c)\ \ \text{for}\ \ (x,\alpha,c)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times{\cal{M}}\times\mathcal{U}.

(2.5)

Then the payoff functional associated with the regular control
C(\cdot)
is

{\mathbb{E}}_{x,\alpha}\Big[\int_{0}^{{\tau}}e^{-\delta s}p\big(X(s),\Lambda(s),C(s)\big)ds\Big],

where

{\mathbb{E}}_{x,\alpha}
denotes the expectation with

X(0-)=x
and
\Lambda(0)=\alpha
.
Let

a_{2}
and
a_{3}
be two positive constants. Suppose that the cost of instantaneous harvesting a unit of the species is
a_{2}
, while the cost of instantaneous renewing a unit of the species is
a_{3}
.
Then the accumulated cost of
exercising the impulsive control
Y(\cdot)
is
\int_{0}^{{\tau}}e^{-\delta s}a_{2}dY(s)
while the accumulated income of selling the harvested amount by
Y(\cdot)
is
\int_{0}^{{\tau}}e^{-\delta s}a_{1}dY(s)
.
Meanwhile,
the accumulated cost of exercising the impulsive control
Z(\cdot)
is
\int_{0}^{{\tau}}e^{-\delta s}a_{3}dZ(s)
and
the accumulated expense of the renewed amount by
Z(\cdot)
is
\int_{0}^{{\tau}}e^{-\delta s}a_{1}dZ(s)
.
Hence
the payoff functional associated with the impulsive controls
Y(\cdot)
and
Z(\cdot)
is

{\mathbb{E}}_{x,\alpha}\Big[\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\delta s}(a_{1}-a_{2})dY(s)-\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\delta s}(a_{1}+a_{3})dZ(s)\Big].

We define

q=a_{1}-a_{2},\ \ r=a_{1}+a_{3}.

Then, for a harvesting-renewing strategy
\Psi\equiv(C,Y,Z)
, we define the
payoff functional
as

\begin{array}[]{ll}J(x,\alpha,\Psi)={\mathbb{E}}_{x,\alpha}&\!\!\!\displaystyle\Big[\int_{0}^{{\tau}}e^{-\delta s}p\big(X(s),\Lambda(s),C(s)\big)ds\\
&\displaystyle\ +\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\delta s}qdY(s)-\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\delta s}rdZ(s)\Big].\end{array}

(2.6)

Control strategy.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{x,\alpha}
denote the collection of all admissible controls with initial condition
X(0-)=x
,
\Lambda(0)=\alpha
. A harvesting-renewing strategy
\Psi=(C,Y,Z)
will be in
\mathcal{A}_{x,\alpha}
if it satisfies
the following
conditions.

(a)

The processes
C(\cdot)
,
Y(\cdot)
, and
Z(\cdot)
are adapted to

\sigma\{w(s),\Lambda(s):0\leq s\leq t\}
;
C(\cdot)
takes values in
\mathcal{U}
,
Y(\cdot)
and
Z(\cdot)
are impulsive controls with non-decreasing, nonnegative, piecewise constant, and right-continuous sample paths;
\Delta Y(s)\Delta Z(s)=0
for any
s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
;
\Delta Y(s)=\Delta Z(s)=0
for any
s\geq\tau
;

(b)

System (
2.4
) has a unique solution
X(\cdot)
with
X(t)\in\mathcal{S}
for any
t\in[0,\tau]
;

(c)

0\leq J(x,\alpha,\Psi)<\infty
, where
J(\cdot)
is the functional defined in (
2.6
).

The problem we are
interested in is to maximize the
payoff functional and find an optimal strategy
\Psi^{*}=(C^{*},Y^{*},Z^{*})\in\mathcal{A}_{x,\alpha}
such that

J(x,\alpha,\Psi^{*})=V(x,\alpha):=\sup\limits_{\Psi\in\mathcal{A}_{x,\alpha}}J(x,\alpha,\Psi).

(2.7)

The function
V(\cdot)
is called the
value function
.

The standing assumptions are given below.

(A)

(a)

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, there exists a positive constant
K_{n}
such that
for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
|x|\leq n,|y|\leq n
and any
\alpha\in{\cal{M}}
,

|b(x,\alpha)-b(y,\alpha)|+|\sigma(x,\alpha)-\sigma(y,\alpha)|\leq K_{n}|x-y|.

Moreover, for each initial condition
(x,\alpha)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times{\cal{M}}
,
the population system (
2.1
) has a unique global solution.

(b)

The control set
\mathcal{U}
is a nonempty compact set of real numbers,
0\in\mathcal{U}
,
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
are positive constants and
a_{1}>a_{2}
.
The function
g(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
is continuous and bounded on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times{\cal{M}}\times\mathcal{U}
. The function
f(\cdot,\cdot)
is continuous and bounded on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{U}
and
f(\cdot,0)=0
.

Remark 2.1
.

For simplicity, we require the systems of equations having a global solution. In fact, the existence and uniqueness of global solutions for a large class of Kolmogorov systems are guaranteed under suitable conditions; see the recent work
[
22
]
and the references therein, and
also
[
20
]
for various sufficient conditions so that the population system (
2.1
) has a unique global solution.
Of course, here we are dealing with a controlled system so some modifications are needed. Nevertheless, in order to concentrate on our main task, we choose to simply assume this condition.

The mixed regular-singular control formulation together with the consideration of a state constraint, cost functions allow us to take into account various aspects of harvesting-type problems that have not been considered to date.
For instance, depending on the costs, one can choose to apply either an impulsive harvesting/renewing or harvest and renew through the regular control.

Remark 2.2
.

The consideration of
\lambda
is motivated by sustainability.
To the
best of our knowledge, the available literature focuses on the case
\lambda=0
in which the optimal or near-optimal harvesting strategies might drive the population process to a very low level or extinction; see
[
1
,
7
,
25
]
.
Because of the state constraint, one needs to
treat carefully control actions when the population size is close to
\lambda
.

The function
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{U}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is the harvesting-renewing rate corresponding to the control
C(\cdot)
.
In
[
8
]
, the authors studied
the case
f(x,c)=c
and
g(x,\alpha,c)=0
for any
(x,\alpha,c)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times{\cal{M}}\times\mathcal{U}
.
Motivated by the observations in
[
1
, Section 3]
and
[
14
]
, one can also take
f(x,c)=\min\{cx,\kappa\}
and
g(x,\alpha,c)=0
for any
(x,\alpha,c)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times{\cal{M}}\times\mathcal{U}
, where
\kappa
is a positive constant. By considering the general form
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{U}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
, our formulation is much more general than the aforementioned cases.

3
Properties of the Value Function

This section is devoted to several properties of the value function. We begin with a lemma that allows us to establish the finiteness and construct upper bounds of the value function.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(A)
be satisfied. Suppose that there exists a function
\Phi:\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}}\mapsto\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
\Phi(\cdot,\alpha)\in C^{2}({\mathcal{S}})
for each
\alpha\in{\cal{M}}
and

\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)
solves the following coupled system of quasi-variational inequalities

\max\bigg\{\mathcal{G}\Phi(x,\alpha),q-\Phi^{\prime}(x,\alpha),\Phi^{\prime}(x,\alpha)-r\bigg\}\leq 0\ \ \text{for}\ \ (x,\alpha)\in\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}},

(3.1)

where

\mathcal{G}\Phi(x,\alpha)=(\mathcal{L}-\delta)\Phi(x,\alpha)+\max\limits_{c\in\mathcal{U}}\Big[p(x,\alpha,c)-\Phi^{\prime}(x,\alpha)f(x,c)\Big].

Recall that
\delta
is the discount factor given in the payoff functional (
2.6
).
Then we have

V(x,\alpha)\leq\Phi(x,\alpha)\ \ \text{for}\ \ (x,\alpha)\in\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}}.

Proof.

For a fixed
(x,\alpha)\in\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}}
and
\Psi=(C,Y,Z)\in\mathcal{A}_{x,\alpha}
, let
X
denote the corresponding harvested process. Choose
N
sufficiently large so that
|x|<N
. Define

\tau_{N}=\inf\{t\geq 0:X(t)\geq N\},\ \ T_{N}=N\wedge\tau_{N}\wedge\tau.

Then

\tau_{N}\to\infty\ \ \text{and}\ \ T_{N}\to\tau\ \ \text{almost surely as }\ \ N\to\infty,

(3.2)

where
\tau=\inf\{t\geq 0:X(t)\notin\mathcal{S}\}.

Then Dynkin’s formula leads to

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\mathbb{E}}&\!\!\!\displaystyle\big[e^{-\delta T_{N}}\Phi\big(X(T_{N}),\Lambda(T_{N})\big)\big]=\Phi(x,\alpha)+{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T_{N}}e^{-\delta s}({\cal L}-\delta)\Phi\left(X(s),\Lambda(s)\right)ds\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T_{N}}e^{-\delta s}\Phi^{\prime}\left(X(s),\Lambda(s)\right)f\big(X(s),C(s)\big)ds\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+{\mathbb{E}}\sum\limits_{0\leq s\leq T_{N}}e^{-\delta s}\Big[\Phi\left(X(s),\Lambda(s-)\right)-\Phi\left(X(s-),\Lambda(s-)\right)\Big].\end{array}

(3.3)

It follows from (
3.1
) that

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\!\!\!\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T_{N}}e^{-\delta s}({\cal L}-\delta)\Phi\left(X(s),\Lambda(s)\right)ds-{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T_{N}}e^{-\delta s}\Phi^{\prime}\left(X(s),\Lambda(s)\right)f\big(X(s),C(s)\big)ds\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle\quad\leq-{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T_{N}}e^{-\delta s}p\left(X(s),C(s),\Lambda(s)\right)ds.\end{array}

(3.4)

For each
s\in[0,T_{N}]
,
by the mean value theorem, there exists a point
\widetilde{X}(s)
between
X(s)
and
X(s-)
such that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\Phi\left(X(s),\Lambda(s-)\right)-\Phi\big(X(s-),\Lambda(s-)\big)&\!\!\!\displaystyle=-\Delta Y(s)\Phi^{\prime}\big(\widetilde{X}(s),\Lambda(s-)\big)\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle\quad+\Delta Z(s)\Phi^{\prime}\big(\widetilde{X}(s),\Lambda(s-)\big).\end{array}

By (
3.1
), we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\Phi\big(X(s),\Lambda(s-)\big)-\Phi\big(X(s-),\Lambda(s-)\big)\leq-q\Delta Y(s)+r\Delta Z(s).\end{array}

(3.5)

It follows from
(
3.3
), (
3.4
), (
3.5
), and the nonnegativity of
\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)
that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\Phi(x,\alpha)&\!\!\!\displaystyle\geq{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T_{N}}e^{-\delta s}p\big(X(s),\Lambda(s),C(s)\big)ds\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle\qquad+{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T_{N}}e^{-\delta s}qdY(s)-{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T_{N}}e^{-\delta s}rdZ(s).\end{array}

Letting
N\to\infty
, it follows from (
3.2
) and the bounded convergence theorem that

\Phi(x,\alpha)\geq J(x,\alpha,\Psi).

Taking supremum over all
\Psi\in\mathcal{A}_{x,\alpha}
, we obtain
\Phi(x,\alpha)\geq V(x,\alpha)
. The conclusion follows.

\qquad\Box
\qquad\Box

Using Lemma
3.1
, we proceed to present an easily verifiable
condition for the finiteness of the value function.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(A)
be satisfied. Moreover, suppose that there is a positive constant
K
such that

b(x,\alpha)\leq\delta x+K\ \ \text{for}\ \ (x,\alpha)\in\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}}.

(3.6)

Then there exist a positive constant
M
such that

V(x,\alpha)\leq qx+M\ \ \text{for}\ \ (x,\alpha)\in\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}}.

Proof.

Define

\Phi(x,\alpha)=qx+\dfrac{Kq+\kappa_{0}}{\delta}\ \ \text{for}\ \ (x,\alpha)\in\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}},

where

\kappa_{0}=\sup\limits_{(y,\alpha,c)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times{\cal{M}}\times\mathcal{U}}\Big(p(y,\alpha,c)-qf(y,c)\Big).

By assumption (A)(b),
\kappa_{0}
is finite.
Since
q=a_{1}-a_{2}<r=a_{1}+a_{3}
and
\Phi^{\prime}(x,\alpha)=q
, it is clear that

q-\Phi^{\prime}(x,\alpha)=0,\ \ \Phi^{\prime}(x,\alpha)-r<0\ \ \text{for}\ \ (x,\alpha)\in\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}}.

(3.7)

By (
3.6
), we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\cal G}\Phi(x,\alpha)&\!\!\!\displaystyle=b(x,\alpha)q-\delta\Big(qx+\frac{Kq+\kappa_{0}}{\delta}\Big)+\max\limits_{c\in\mathcal{U}}\Big[p(x,\alpha,c)-qf(x,c)\Big]\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle\leq(\delta x+K)q-(\delta qx+Kq+\kappa_{0})+\kappa_{0}\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle=0\ \ \text{for}\ \ (x,\alpha)\in\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}}.\end{array}

(3.8)

It follows from (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) that

\max\bigg\{\mathcal{G}\Phi(x,\alpha),q-\Phi^{\prime}(x,\alpha),\Phi^{\prime}(x,\alpha)-r\bigg\}\leq 0\ \ \text{for}\ \ (x,\alpha)\in\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}}.

That is,
\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)
solves the system of inequalities (
3.1
). By virtue of Lemma
3.1
,
V(x,\alpha)\leq\Phi(x,\alpha)
for any
(x,\alpha)\in\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}}.

This completes the proof.

Next, we establish the continuity of the value function.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(A)
be satisfied.
Then the following assertions hold.

(a)

For any
x,y\in\mathcal{S}
and
\alpha\in{\cal{M}}
,

V(x,\alpha)\geq q(x-y)^{+}-r(y-x)^{+}+V(y,\alpha).

(3.9)

(b)

V(\cdot)
is Lipschitz continuous on
\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}}
.

Proof.

(a) Fix
\Psi=(C,Y,Z)\in\mathcal{A}_{y,\alpha}
. Define

\widetilde{C}(t)=C(t),\ \ \widetilde{Y}(t)=Y(t)+(x-y)^{+},\ \ \widetilde{Z}(t)=Z(t)+(y-x)^{+},\ \ t\geq 0,

and
\widetilde{\Psi}=(\widetilde{C},\widetilde{Y},\widetilde{Z})
.
Then
\widetilde{\Psi}\in\mathcal{A}_{x,\alpha}

and

J(x,\alpha,\widetilde{\Psi})=q(x-y)^{+}-r(y-x)^{+}+J(y,\alpha,\Psi).

Since
V(x,\alpha)\geq J(x,\alpha,\widetilde{\Psi})
, we have

V(x,\alpha)\geq q(x-y)^{+}-r(y-x)^{+}+J(y,\alpha,\Psi),

from which, (
3.9
) follows by taking supremum over
\Psi\in\mathcal{A}_{y,\alpha}
.

(b) Similar to (
3.9
), we have

V(y,\alpha)\geq q(y-x)^{+}-r(x-y)^{+}+V(x,\alpha).

(3.10)

In view of (
3.9
), (
3.10
), for any
x,y\in\mathcal{S}
and
\alpha\in{\cal{M}}
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}|V(x,\alpha)-V(y,\alpha)|&\!\!\!\displaystyle\leq\big(|q|+|r|\big)|x-y|.\end{array}

Thus,
V(\cdot)
is Lipschitz continuous on
\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}}
.

\qquad\Box
\qquad\Box

For population models given by diffusion processes, under optimal or near-optimal harvesting strategies, one should keep the population sizes in a bounded set. In other words, if the initial population is too high, an impulsive harvesting should be performed instantaneously; see
[
1
,
7
,
8
]
. We proceed to provide a proof of that result for a general setting of a controlled regime-switching diffusions with a mixed regular-singular control.

Theorem 3.4
.

Suppose that there exists a number
U>\lambda
such that

q\Big(b(x,\alpha)-\delta(x-U)\Big)+\sup\limits_{c\in\mathcal{U}}\Big(a_{2}f(x,c)-g(x,\alpha,c)\Big)<0,\ \ (x,\alpha)\in(U,\infty)\times{\cal{M}}.

(3.11)

Then for each
(x,\alpha)\in(U,\infty)\times{\cal{M}}
,

V(x,\alpha)=V(U,\alpha)+q(x-U).

Remark 3.5
.

By assumption (A)(b),

\kappa_{1}:=\sup\limits_{(x,\alpha,c)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times{\cal{M}}\times\mathcal{U}}\Big(a_{2}f(x,c)-g(x,\alpha,c)\Big)<\infty.

If
\limsup\limits_{x\to\infty}b(x,\alpha)<-\kappa_{1}/q

for each
\alpha\in{\cal{M}}
,
then we can take

U=\sup\big\{x>\lambda+1:\sup_{\alpha\in{\cal{M}}}b(x,\alpha)\geq-\kappa_{1}/q\big\}.

Proof.

Fix some
(x,\alpha)\in(U,\infty)\times{\cal{M}}
,

\Psi=(C,Y,Z)\in\mathcal{A}_{x,\alpha}
, and let
X

be the corresponding harvested process.
Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
be a constant and
define

\Phi(y,\alpha)=q(y-U)+\varepsilon\ \ \text{for}\ \ (y,\alpha)\in[U,\infty)\times{\cal{M}}.

(3.12)

We can extend
\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)
to the entire
\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}}
so that
\Phi(\cdot,\alpha)
is a
C^{2}
function for each
\alpha\in{\cal{M}}
, and
\Phi(y,\alpha)>0
for all
(y,\alpha)\in\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}}
.
By assumption (
3.11
), we can check that

(\mathcal{L}-\delta)\Phi(y,\alpha)+a_{2}f(y,c)-g(y,\alpha,c)<0,\ \ (y,\alpha,c)\times[U,\infty)\times{\cal{M}}\times\mathcal{U}.

Thus,

\begin{array}[]{ll}(\mathcal{L}-\delta)\Phi(y,\alpha)-qf(y,c)&\!\!\!\displaystyle<-\big[a_{2}f(y,c)-g(y,\alpha,c)\big]-qf(y,c)\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle=-\big[(a_{2}+q)f(y,c)-g(y,\alpha,c)\big]\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle=-p(y,\alpha,c),\ \ (y,\alpha,c)\times[U,\infty)\times{\cal{M}}\times\mathcal{U}.\end{array}

(3.13)

For an integer
N
satisfying
N>U
,
we define

\tau_{N}=\inf\{t\geq 0:X(t)\geq N\},\ \ \widetilde{\gamma}_{U}=\inf\{t\geq 0:X(t)\leq U\},\ \ T_{N}=N\wedge\tau_{N}\wedge\widetilde{\gamma}_{U}.

We have

T_{N}\to\widetilde{\gamma}_{U}
almost surely as
N\to\infty
.
Note that
\widetilde{\gamma}_{U}\leq\tau
. By Dynkin’s formula,

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\mathbb{E}}&\!\!\!\displaystyle\big[e^{-\delta T_{N}}\Phi\left(X(T_{N}),\Lambda(T_{N})\right)\big]-\Phi(x,\alpha)={\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T_{N}}e^{-\delta s}({\cal L}-\delta)\Phi\left(X(s),\Lambda(s)\right)ds\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle\qquad-{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T_{N}}e^{-\delta s}\Phi^{\prime}\left(X(s),\Lambda(s)\right)f\big(X(s),C(s)\big)ds\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle\qquad+{\mathbb{E}}\sum\limits_{0\leq s\leq T_{N}}e^{-\delta s}\Big[\Phi\left(X(s),\Lambda(s-)\right)-\Phi\left(X(s-),\Lambda(s-)\right)\Big].\end{array}

(3.14)

For each
s\in[0,T_{N}]
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\Phi\left(X(s),\Lambda(s-)\right)-\Phi\left(X(s-),\Lambda(s-)\right)&\!\!\!\displaystyle=-q\Delta Y(s)+q\Delta Z(s)\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle\leq-q\Delta Y(s)+r\Delta Z(s).\end{array}

(3.15)

We obtain from (
3.13
), (
3.14
), and (
3.15
) that

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\mathbb{E}}&\!\!\!\displaystyle\big[e^{-\delta T_{N}}\Phi\left(X(T_{N}),\Lambda(T_{N})\right)\big]-\Phi(x,\alpha)\leq-{\mathbb{E}}\int_{s_{0}}^{T_{N}}e^{-\delta s}p\big(X(s),\Lambda(s),C(s)\big)ds\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle\qquad-{\mathbb{E}}\sum\limits_{0\leq s\leq T_{N}}e^{-\delta s}q\Delta Y(s)+{\mathbb{E}}\sum\limits_{0\leq s\leq T_{N}}e^{-\delta s}r\Delta Z(s).\end{array}

(3.16)

Since
\Phi(y,\alpha)>0
for any
(y,\alpha)\in\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}}
, it follows from (
3.16
) that

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\!\!\!\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T_{N}}e^{-\delta s}p\big(X(s),\Lambda(s),C(s)\big)ds\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+{\mathbb{E}}\sum\limits_{0\leq s\leq T_{N}}e^{-\delta s}q\Delta Y(s)-{\mathbb{E}}\sum\limits_{0\leq s\leq T_{N}}e^{-\delta s}r\Delta Z(s)\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle\qquad\leq\Phi(x,\alpha).\end{array}

Letting
N\to\infty
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\!\!\!\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{\widetilde{\gamma}_{U}}e^{-\delta s}p\big(X(s),\Lambda(s),C(s)\big)ds\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+{\mathbb{E}}\sum\limits_{0\leq s\leq\widetilde{\gamma}_{U}}e^{-\delta s}q\Delta Y(s)-{\mathbb{E}}\sum\limits_{0\leq s\leq\widetilde{\gamma}_{U}}e^{-\delta s}r\Delta Z(s)\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle\qquad\leq\Phi(x,\alpha).\end{array}

(3.17)

As a result,

\begin{array}[]{ll}J(x,\alpha,\Psi)&\!\!\!\displaystyle\leq V(U,\alpha)+\Phi(x,\alpha)\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle=V(U,\alpha)+q(x-U)+\varepsilon.\end{array}

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
yields

\begin{array}[]{ll}J(x,\alpha,\Psi)&\!\!\!\displaystyle\leq V(U,\alpha)+q(x-U).\end{array}

(3.18)

Since (
3.18
) holds for any
\Psi\in\mathcal{A}_{x,\alpha}
,

V(x,\alpha)\leq V(U,\alpha)+q(x-U).

(3.19)

On the other hand, it is obvious (by harvesting instantaneously
x-U
at time
t=0
) that

V(x,\alpha)\geq V(U,\alpha)+q(x-U).

(3.20)

In view of (
3.19
) and (
3.20
),

V(x,\alpha)=V(U,\alpha)+q(x-U).

The conclusion follows.

We proceed to discuss the impact of large white noise. By using Lemma
3.1
, we construct an upper bound for the value function. Then we show that the value function approaches the upper bound as the white noise intensity approaches infinity. The following result is motivated by the presence of multiplicative noise.

Theorem 3.6
.

Suppose that
\lambda>0
.
Moreover,
there exist positive constants
\beta\in(0,1)
,
K
, and
N
such that

xb(x,\alpha)\leq K(1+x^{2}),\ \ b(x,\alpha)q-\delta q(x-\lambda)\leq K(x^{\beta}+1),

(3.21)

and

|\sigma(x,\alpha)|\geq Nx\ \ \text{for}\ \ (x,\alpha)\in\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}}.

Then

\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}V(x,\alpha)=q(x-\lambda),

uniformly on
[\lambda,M]\times{\cal{M}}
for any positive constant
M>\lambda
.

Proof.

Let
M>\lambda
.
For a fixed
\varepsilon\in(0,r-q)
,
let
K_{\varepsilon}>0
be sufficiently large such that

\dfrac{(M+1)^{\beta}}{K_{\varepsilon}}\leq\varepsilon,\ \ \dfrac{\beta}{K_{\varepsilon}}\leq r-q-\varepsilon.

Define

\Phi(x,\alpha)=q(x-\lambda)+\dfrac{(x+1)^{\beta}}{K_{\varepsilon}},\ \ (x,\alpha)\in\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}}.

Detailed computations lead to

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(x,\alpha)=q+\dfrac{\beta}{K_{\varepsilon}(x+1)^{1-\beta}},\\
&\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime\prime}(x,\alpha)=-\dfrac{\beta(1-\beta)}{K_{\varepsilon}(x+1)^{2-\beta}},\ \ (x,\alpha)\in\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}}.\end{array}

It is clear that

q-\Phi^{\prime}(x,\alpha)\leq 0,\ \ \Phi^{\prime}(x,\alpha)-r\leq 0\ \ \text{for}\ \ (x,\alpha)\in\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}}.

(3.22)

By assumption (A)(b),

\kappa_{2}:=\sup\limits_{(x,\alpha,c)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times{\cal{M}}\times\mathcal{U}}\Big[p(x,\alpha,c)-\Phi^{\prime}(x,\alpha)f(x,c)\Big]<\infty.

Hence,

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\cal G}\Phi(x,\alpha)&\!\!\!\displaystyle\leq b(x,\alpha)q+\dfrac{\beta}{K_{\varepsilon}(x+1)^{1-\beta}}b(x,\alpha)\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle-\dfrac{\beta(1-\beta)N^{2}x^{2}}{2K_{\varepsilon}(x+1)^{2-\beta}}-\delta q(x-\lambda)-\dfrac{\delta(x+1)^{\beta}}{K_{\varepsilon}}+\kappa_{2}.\\
\end{array}

(3.23)

For
x\geq\lambda>0
, by the first inequality in (
3.21
),

\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{\beta}{K_{\varepsilon}(x+1)^{1-\beta}}b(x,\alpha)&\!\!\!\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\beta}{K_{\varepsilon}x(x+1)^{1-\beta}}xb(x,\alpha)\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\beta K(1+x^{2})}{K_{\varepsilon}x^{2-\beta}}\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\beta K}{K_{\varepsilon}}\Big(x^{\beta}+\dfrac{1}{\lambda^{2-\beta}}\Big).\end{array}

It follows from (
3.21
) and (
3.23
) that for
x\geq\lambda
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\cal G}\Phi(x,\alpha)&\!\!\!\displaystyle\leq K(x^{\beta}+1)+\dfrac{\beta}{K_{\varepsilon}(x+1)^{1-\beta}}b(x,\alpha)-\dfrac{\beta(1-\beta)N^{2}x^{2}}{K_{\varepsilon}(x+1)^{2-\beta}}+\kappa_{2}\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle\leq\Big[K+\dfrac{\beta K}{K_{\varepsilon}}-\dfrac{\beta(1-\beta)N^{2}x^{2-\beta}}{2K_{\varepsilon}(x+1)^{2-\beta}}\Big]x^{\beta}+K+\dfrac{\beta K}{K_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{2-\beta}}+\kappa_{2}.\end{array}

Hence there exists a positive number
N_{0}
such that for any
N\geq N_{0}
,

{\cal G}\Phi(x,\alpha)\leq 0\ \ \text{for}\ \ (x,\alpha)\in\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}}.

(3.24)

By (
3.22
), (
3.24
), and Lemma
3.1
, we obtain

V(x,\alpha)\leq q(x-\lambda)+\dfrac{(x+1)^{\beta}}{K_{\varepsilon}},\ \ (x,\alpha)\in\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}}

provided that
N\geq N_{0}
.
In particular, since
\dfrac{(M+1)^{\beta}}{K_{\varepsilon}}\leq\varepsilon
, then for
N\geq N_{0}
, we have

V(x,\alpha)\leq q(x-\lambda)+\varepsilon\ \ \text{for}\ \ (x,\alpha)\in[\lambda,M]\times{\cal{M}}.

(3.25)

Let
\Psi=(C,Y,Z)\in\mathcal{A}_{x,\alpha}
given by

C(t)=0,\ \ Y(t)=x-\lambda,\ \ Z(t)=0\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\geq 0.

Then
J(x,\alpha,\Psi)=q(x-\lambda)
. It follows that

V(x,\alpha)\geq q(x-\lambda)
. This together with (
3.25
)
yields
that

0\leq V(x,\alpha)-q(x-\lambda)\leq\varepsilon\ \ \text{for}\ \ (x,\alpha)\in[\lambda,M]\times{\cal{M}},

which leads to the desired conclusion.

4
Numerical Approximation

Formally, the associated Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation of the underlying problem is given by

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\displaystyle\max\bigg\{\mathcal{G}\Phi(x,\alpha),q-\Phi^{\prime}(x,\alpha),\Phi^{\prime}(x,\alpha)-r\bigg\}=0\ \ \text{for}\ \ (x,\alpha)\in\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}},\\
&\displaystyle\Phi(x,\alpha)=0\ \ \text{for}\ \ (x,\alpha)\notin\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}},\end{array}

(4.1)

where

\mathcal{G}\Phi(x,\alpha)=(\mathcal{L}-\delta)\Phi(x,\alpha)+\max\limits_{c\in\mathcal{U}}\Big[p(x,\alpha,c)-\Phi^{\prime}(x,\alpha)f(x,c)\Big].

A closed-form solution to (
4.1
) is virtually impossible to obtain. Thus, we
proceed with
the Markov chain approximation method; see
[
11
,
16
,
17
]
. That is,
we
construct a discrete time, finite-state,
controlled Markov chain
to approximate the controlled switching diffusions.
In
[
11
]
, the authors applied that method to solve a harvesting-type problem for a regime-switching diffusion with a regular control and a singular control. In this paper, we need to adopt and modify the approximation to fit the combination of a two-sided singular control and regular control formulation.
In view of Theorem
3.4
,
we only need to choose a large positive integer
U
and compute the value function on
\mathcal{S}\cap[0,U]=[\lambda,U]
. With
x\in[\lambda,U]
, we can rewrite (
2.4
) as

\begin{array}[]{ll}X(t)&\!\!\!\displaystyle=x+\int_{0}^{t}\big(b(X(s),\Lambda(s)\big)-f\big(X(s),C(s)\big)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma\big(X(s),\Lambda(s)\big)dw(s)\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-Y(t)+Z(t).\end{array}

(4.2)

The payoff functional is

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\displaystyle J(x,\alpha,\Psi)={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\delta s}p(X(s),\Lambda(s),C(s))ds\\
&\!\!\!\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\delta s}qdY(s)-\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\delta s}rdZ(s)\bigg].\end{array}

(4.3)

4.1
Approximating Markov Chains

Let
h
be a discretization parameter for
X(\cdot)
.
Assume without loss of generality that
U
is a multiple of
h
.
Define

S_{h}:=[0,U]\cap\{x\in[\lambda-h,U]:x=kh,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}.

Let
\{(X^{h}_{n},\Lambda^{h}_{n}):n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}

be a discrete-time controlled Markov chain with state space
S_{h}\times{\cal{M}}
.
For each
n
, the increments of the chain
\Delta X^{h}_{n}=X^{h}_{n+1}-X^{h}_{n}
approximates exactly one of the following quantities dynamically.

•

Diffusion step:
\big(b(X(t),\Lambda(t))-f(X(t),C(t)\big)dt+\sigma(X(t),\Lambda(t))dw(t)
.

•

Impulsive harvesting step:
-dY(t)
.

•

Impulsive renewing step:
dZ(t)
.

We use
\pi^{h}_{n}
to denote the type of step
n
. We set
\pi^{h}_{n}=0
if step
n
is a diffusion step,
\pi^{h}_{n}=1
if step
n
is an impulsive harvesting step, and
\pi^{h}_{n}=-1
if step
n
is an impulsive renewing step. Each of these steps is described precisely in what follows.

(a) If
\pi^{h}_{n}=0
, a regular control
C^{h}_{n}
is exercised so that
\Delta X^{h}_{n}
is to behave like an increment of
\int(b-f)dt+\sigma dw
over a small time interval.
(b) If
\pi^{h}_{n}=1
, a regular control
C^{h}_{n}
and an impulsive harvesting
\Delta Y^{h}_{n}=h
are applied.
(c) If
\pi^{h}_{n}=-1
, a regular control
C^{h}_{n}
and an impulsive renewing
\Delta Z^{h}_{n}=h
are applied.

Thus, the amounts of impulsive harvesting and impulsive renewing at any step
n
are
\Delta Y^{h}_{n}=hI_{\{\pi^{h}_{n}=1\}}

and
\Delta Z^{h}_{n}=hI_{\{\pi^{h}_{n}=-1\}}
, respectively.
Let
C^{h}=\{C^{h}_{n}\}
be a sequence of regular controls. The space of regular controls is given by
\mathcal{U}
. Let
\pi^{h}=\{\pi_{n}^{h}\}_{n\geq 0}
denote the sequence of control types.
We denote by
p^{h}\left((x,\alpha),(y,\ell)|\pi,c\right)
the transition probability from state
(x,\alpha)
to another state
(y,\ell)
under the control type
\pi
and regular control
c
.
Denote

\mathcal{F}^{h}_{n}=\sigma\{X^{h}_{k},\Lambda^{h}_{k},\pi^{h}_{k},C^{h}_{k},k\leq n\}
.

The sequence
\{(\pi^{h}_{n},C^{h}_{n})\}

is said to be admissible if it satisfies the following conditions:

(a)

\pi^{h}_{n},C^{h}_{n}
are

\sigma\{X^{h}_{0},\dots,X^{h}_{n},\Lambda^{h}_{0},\dots,\Lambda^{h}_{n},\pi^{h}_{0},\dots,\pi^{h}_{n-1},C^{h}_{1},\dots,C^{h}_{n-1}\}-\text{adapted},

(b)

For any
(x,\alpha)\in S_{h}\times{\cal{M}}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\mathbb{P}}\{X^{h}_{n+1}=x,\Lambda^{h}_{n+1}=\alpha|\mathcal{F}^{h}_{n}\}&={\mathbb{P}}\{X^{h}_{n+1}=x,\Lambda^{h}_{n+1}=\alpha|X^{h}_{n},\Lambda^{h}_{n},\pi^{h}_{n},C^{h}_{n}\}\\
&=p^{h}\big(\big(X^{h}_{n},\Lambda^{h}_{n}),(x,\alpha)|\pi^{h}_{n},C^{h}_{n}\big),\end{array}

(c)

\pi^{h}_{n}\in\{0,\pm 1\},C^{h}_{n}\in\mathcal{U}
,
X^{h}_{n}\in S_{h},\Lambda^{h}_{n}\in{\cal{M}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

The class of all admissible control sequences
(\pi^{h},C^{h})
for initial state
(x,\alpha)
will be denoted by

\mathcal{A}^{h}_{x,\alpha}
.

For each

(x,\alpha,i,c)\in S_{h}\times{\cal{M}}\times\{0,\pm 1\}\times\mathcal{U}
,
we define
a family of the interpolation intervals
\Delta t^{h}(x,\alpha,i,c)
. The values of
\Delta t^{h}(x,\alpha,i,c)
will be specified later. Then we define

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\displaystyle t^{h}_{0}=0,\ \ \Delta t^{h}_{k}=\Delta t^{h}(X^{h}_{k},\Lambda^{h}_{k},\pi^{h}_{k},C^{h}_{k}),\ \ t^{h}_{n}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\Delta t^{h}_{k}.\\
\end{array}

(4.4)

For
(x,\alpha)\in S_{h}\times{\cal{M}}
and
(\pi^{h},C^{h})\in\mathcal{A}^{h}_{x,\alpha}
, the payoff functional for the controlled Markov chain is defined as

\begin{array}[]{ll}J^{h}(x,\alpha,\pi^{h},C^{h})&={\mathbb{E}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\eta_{h}-1}e^{-\delta t_{k}^{h}}\bigg\{p(X^{h}_{k},\Lambda^{h}_{k},C^{h}_{k})\Delta t^{h}_{k}+q\Delta Y^{h}_{k}-r\Delta Z^{h}_{k}\bigg\},\end{array}

(4.5)

with
\eta_{h}=\inf\{n\geq 0:X^{h}_{n}\notin\mathcal{S}\}
.
The value function of the controlled Markov chain is

V^{h}(x,\alpha)=\sup\limits_{(\pi^{h},C^{h})\in\mathcal{A}^{h}_{x,\alpha}}J^{h}(x,\alpha,\pi^{h},C^{h}).

(4.6)

The corresponding dynamic programming equation for the discrete approximation is given by

V^{h}(x,\alpha)=\max\{V^{h}_{i,c}(x,\alpha):i\in\{-1,0,1\},c\in\mathcal{U}\}

for any
(x,\alpha)\in S_{h}\times{\cal{M}}
, where

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\displaystyle V^{h}_{1,c}(x,\alpha)=\big(V^{h}(x-h,\alpha)+qh\big)I_{\{x>\lambda\}},\\
&\displaystyle V^{h}_{-1,c}(x,\alpha)=\big(V^{h}(x+h,\alpha)-rh\big)I_{\{x<U\}},\\
&\displaystyle V^{h}_{0,c}(x,\alpha)=e^{-\delta\Delta t^{h}(x,\alpha,0,c)}\sum\limits_{(y,\ell)\in S_{h}}V^{h}(y,\ell)p^{h}\big((x,\alpha),(y,\ell)|0,c\big)I_{\{x<U\}}\\
&\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+p(x,\alpha,c)\Delta t^{h}(x,\alpha,0,c)I_{\{x<U\}}.\end{array}

Note that
V^{h}(x,\alpha)=0
for
(x,\alpha)\notin S_{h}\times{\cal{M}}
.

4.2
Transition Probabilities

Let
{\mathbb{E}}^{h,\pi,c}_{x,\alpha,n}
,
{\mathbb{C}ov}^{h,\pi,c}_{x,\alpha,n}
denote the conditional expectation and covariance given by

\{X_{k}^{h},\Lambda_{k}^{h},\pi_{k}^{h},C^{h}_{k},k\leq n-1,X_{n}^{h}=x,\Lambda_{n}^{h}=\alpha,\pi^{h}_{n}=\pi,C^{h}_{n}=c\},

respectively. We proceed to define transition probabilities
p^{h}\big((x,\alpha),(y,\ell)|\pi,c\big)
so that the controlled Markov chain
\{(X^{h}_{n},\Lambda^{h}_{n})\}
is locally consistent with respect to
(X(\cdot),\Lambda(\cdot))

in the sense that the following hold.

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{h,0,c}_{x,\alpha,n}\Delta X_{n}^{h}={b}(x,\alpha)\Delta t^{h}(x,\alpha,0,c)+o(\Delta t^{h}(x,\alpha,0,c)),\\
&\displaystyle Cov^{h,0,c}_{x,\alpha,n}\Delta X_{n}^{h}=\sigma^{2}(x,\alpha)\Delta t^{h}(x,\alpha,0,c)+o(\Delta t^{h}(x,\alpha,0,c)),\\
&\displaystyle\sup\limits_{n,\ \omega}|\Delta X_{n}^{h}|\to 0\ \hbox{ as }\ h\to 0.\end{array}

(4.7)

To this end, motivated by the procedure in
[
11
,
17
]
,
we construct the transition probabilities below. For
(x,\alpha)\in S_{h}\times{\cal{M}}
,
\lambda\leq x<U
and
c\in\mathcal{U}
, we define

Q_{h}(x,\alpha,0,c)=\sigma^{2}(x,\alpha)+h|b(x,\alpha)-f(x,c)|-h^{2}\Gamma_{\alpha\alpha}+\zeta(h).

We set
\zeta(h)=h
. If, in addition,
\sigma^{2}(x,\alpha)>0
for any
(x,\alpha)\in\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}}
, we can simply take
\zeta(h)=0
. Then we define

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\displaystyle p^{h}\left((x,\alpha),(x+h,\alpha)|0,c\right)=\dfrac{\sigma^{2}(x,\alpha)/2+\big(b(x,\alpha)-f(x,c)\big)^{+}h}{Q_{h}(x,\alpha,0,c)},\\
&\displaystyle p^{h}\left((x,\alpha),(x-h,\alpha)|0,c\right)=\dfrac{\sigma^{2}(x,\alpha)/2+\big(b(x,\alpha)-f(x,c)\big)^{-}h}{Q_{h}(x,\alpha,0,c)},\\
&\displaystyle p^{h}\left((x,\alpha),(x,\ell)|0,c\right)=\dfrac{h^{2}\Gamma_{\alpha\ell}}{Q_{h}(x,\alpha,0,c)}\text{ for }\alpha\neq\ell,\\
&\displaystyle p^{h}\left((x,\alpha),(x,\alpha)|0,c\right)=\dfrac{\zeta(h)}{Q_{h}(x,\alpha,0,c)},\ \ \Delta t^{h}(x,\alpha,0,c)=\dfrac{h^{2}}{Q_{h}(x,\alpha,0,c)},\\
&\displaystyle p^{h}\left((x,\alpha),(y,\ell)|0,c\right)=0\ \ \text{for all not listed values of $(y,\ell)\in S_{h}\times{\cal{M}}$}.\end{array}

(4.8)

At impulsive harvesting and renewing steps, we define

\begin{array}[]{ll}&p^{h}\left((x,\alpha),(x-h,\alpha)|1,c\right)=1,\ \ \Delta t^{h}(x,\alpha,1,c)=0,\\
&p^{h}\left((x,\alpha),(x+h,\alpha)|-1,c\right)=1,\ \ \Delta t^{h}(x,\alpha,-1,c)=0.\end{array}

(4.9)

Thus,
p^{h}\left((x,\alpha),(y,\ell)|\pm 1,c\right)=0
for all nonlisted values of
(y,\ell)\in S_{h}\times{\cal{M}}
.
The definition of

p^{h}((x,\alpha),\cdot|i,c\big)
for
(x,\alpha)\notin\mathcal{S}\times{\cal{M}}
is not important since in the analysis of the control problem, the chain will be stopped at the first time
\{X^{h}_{n}\}
exits
\mathcal{S}
.
Using the above transition probabilities,
we can check that
the local
consistency
conditions of
\{(X^{h}_{n},\Lambda^{h}_{n})\}
in (
4.7
) are satisfied.

The convergence result is based on a continuous-time interpolation of the chain and relaxed control representations. In addition, a “stretched-out” time scale is introduced to overcome the possible non-tightness of the piecewise constant interpolations associated with
Y(\cdot)
and
Z(\cdot)
; see
[
11
]
and
[
16
]
. Using weak convergence methods, we can obtain the convergence of the value function. The main convergence result is given below. The proof is a modification of that in
[
11
]
, we omit it for brevity.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
V(x,\alpha)
and
V^{h}(x,\alpha)
be value functions defined in (
2.7
) and (
4.6
), respectively. Then
V^{h}(x,\alpha)\to V(x,\alpha)
as
h\to 0
.

Numerical Examples

We consider a stochastic population with harvesting and renewing given by

dX(t)=b(X(t),\Lambda(t))dt+\sigma(X(t),\Lambda(t))dw(t)-C(t)dt-dY(t)+dZ(t),

(5.1)

where

\begin{array}[]{ll}&b(x,\alpha)=x(\alpha-1.5x),\ \ \sigma(x,\alpha)=(\alpha/2)x.\end{array}

Suppose that
{\cal{M}}=\{1,2\}
and the generator
\Gamma=(\Gamma_{\alpha\ell})_{2\times 2}
of the Markov chain
\Lambda(\cdot)
is given by

\Gamma_{11}=-1,\ \ \Gamma_{12}=1,\ \ \Gamma_{21}=1.5,\ \ \Gamma_{22}=-1.5.

We also assume that

\begin{array}[]{ll}&q=1,\ \ r=2.25,\ \ \delta=0.05,\\
&p(x,\alpha,c)=\dfrac{3c}{2}-\dfrac{\alpha c^{2}}{8(1+x)}\ \ \text{for}\ \ (x,\alpha,c)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times{\cal{M}}\times\mathcal{U},\\
&f(x,c)=c\ \ \text{for}\ \ (x,c)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{U}.\\
\end{array}

The original price and costs are

a_{1}=1.5,a_{2}=0.5,a_{3}=0.75
and the regular control cost function is
g(x,\alpha,c)=\frac{\alpha c^{2}}{8(1+x)}
.
Thus, for each
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\alpha\in{\cal{M}}
,
g(x,\alpha,c)
has a quadratic form.
For a fixed regular control
c
and regime
\alpha\in{\cal{M}}
, the cost function is decreasing with respect to the population size. This is motivated by an observation in harvesting problems that when the species is rare, it is more difficult
to harvest
leading
to the higher harvesting cost; see
[
2

By the state constraint, the time horizon of the control problem is
[0,\tau]

\lambda
and the control set
\mathcal{U}
are to be determined.
Using the transition probabilities constructed in (
4.8
) and (
4.9
), we carry out the computation by using value iteration and policy iteration method. For each
(x,\alpha)\in S_{h}\times{\cal{M}}
, we use the following notations for the
n
th iteration:
C^{h,n}(x,\alpha)
is the regular control,
\pi^{h,n}(x,\alpha)
is the control type, and
V^{h,n}(x,\alpha)
is the value function. Initially, we take

C^{h,0}(x,\alpha)=0,\ \ \pi^{h,0}(x,\alpha)=1,\ \ V^{h,0}(x,\alpha)=q(x-\lambda)\ \ \text{for}\ \ (x,\alpha)\in S_{h}\times{\cal{M}}.

Note that
\big(\pi^{h,0}(x,\alpha),C^{h,0}(x,\alpha)\big)

corresponds to the control
\Psi=(C,Y,Z)\in\mathcal{A}_{x,\alpha}
given by

C(t)=0,\ \ Y(t)=x-\lambda,\ \ Z(t)=0\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\geq 0,

of the controlled switching diffusion (
2.4
).
Based on our computation of the
n
th iteration, we define

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\displaystyle V^{h,n+1}_{1,c}(x,\alpha)=\big(V^{h,n}(x-h,\alpha)+qh\big)I_{\{x>\lambda\}},\\
&\displaystyle V^{h,n+1}_{-1,c}(x,\alpha)=\big(V^{h,n}(x+h,\alpha)-rh\big)I_{\{x<U\}},\\
&\displaystyle V^{h,n+1}_{0,c}(x,\alpha)=e^{-\delta\Delta t^{h}(x,0,c,\alpha)}\big[V^{h,n}(x+h,\alpha)p^{h}\left((x,\alpha),(x+h,\alpha)|0,c\right)\\
&\displaystyle\qquad+V^{h,n}(x-h,\alpha)p^{h}\left((x,\alpha),(x-h,\alpha)|0,c\right)\\
&\displaystyle\qquad+V^{h,n}(x,\alpha)p^{h}\left((x,\alpha),(x,\alpha)|0,c\right)\big]I_{\{x<U\}}+p(x,\alpha,c)\Delta t^{h}(x,\alpha,i,c)I_{\{x<U\}}.\end{array}

We find an improved value
V^{h,n+1}(x,\alpha)

and record the corresponding controls by

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\displaystyle\left(\pi^{h,n+1}(x,\alpha),C^{h,n+1}(x,\alpha)\right)=\hbox{argmax}\left\{V^{h,n+1}_{i,c}(x,\alpha):i\in\{-1,0,1\},c\in\mathcal{U}\right\},\\
&\displaystyle V^{h,n+1}(x,\alpha)=\max\left\{V^{h,n+1}_{i,c}(x,\alpha):i\in\{-1,0,1\},c\in\mathcal{U}\right\}.\end{array}

The iterations stop as soon as the
increment

V^{h,n+1}(x,\alpha)-V^{h,n}(x,\alpha)

reaches a pre-specified
tolerance level. We set the error tolerance to be
10^{-6}
.

6
Further Remarks

This paper has been devoted to the study of a generalized harvesting problem for a stochastic population. We have established the finiteness and continuity of the value function. Moreover, we have shown that for common population systems, it is important to maintain the population size in a bounded set. We have also studied the impact of large white noise on harvesting.
A numerical algorithm has been constructed by the Markov chain approximation methods. The numerical experiments have revealed the effect of Markovian switching and control costs associated with harvesting/renewing activities. It has been observed that under a mixed singular-control formulation, the decision makers have more options to harvest or renew the species, which is much more beneficial than the known models with no renewing, or with only one control component. Moreover, the state constraint provides an effective strategy for sustainable purpose.

In this paper, we focus on single species. The development in this paper can be carried over to the multidimensional case.
As for single species models (Theorem
3.4
), it appears that the controller should keep the population sizes in a bounded set. However,
in order to identify such a bounded set,
one needs to be careful to handle the interaction between species.
Although multidimensional systems can be handled,
the
multidimensional structure added more difficulty to study the impact of large white noise. The computation of multidimensional ecosystems with many constraints and controls is another challenging task.
In addition, one can also consider the problem under other constraints, random prices, and random cost functions.

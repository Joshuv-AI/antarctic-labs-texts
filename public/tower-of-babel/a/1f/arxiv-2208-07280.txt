Abstract
. We present a geometrical construction of families of finite isospectral graphs labelled by different partitions of a natural number r r of given length s s (the number of summands). Isospectrality here refers to the discrete magnetic Laplacian with normalised weights (including standard weights). The construction begins with an arbitrary finite graph 𝑮 \bm{G} with normalised weight and magnetic potential as a building block from which we construct, in a first step, a family of so-called frame graphs ( 𝑭 a ) a ∈ ℕ (\bm{F}_{a})_{a\in\mathbb{N}} . A frame graph 𝑭 a \bm{F}_{a} is constructed contracting a a copies of G G along a subset of vertices V 0 V_{0} . In a second step, for any partition A = ( a 1 , … , a s ) A=(a_{1},\dots,a_{s}) of length s s of a natural number r r (i.e., r = a 1 + ⋯ + a s r=a_{1}+\dots+a_{s} ) we construct a new graph 𝑭 A \bm{F}_{A} contracting now the frames 𝑭 a 1 , … , 𝑭 a s \bm{F}_{a_{1}},\dots,\bm{F}_{a_{s}} selected by A A along a proper subset of vertices V 1 ⊂ V 0 V_{1}\subset V_{0} . All the graphs obtained by different s s -partitions of r ≥ 4 r\geq 4 (for any choice of V 0 V_{0} and V 1 V_{1} ) are isospectral and non-isomorphic. In particular, we obtain increasing finite families of graphs which are isospectral for given r r and s s for different types of magnetic Laplacians including the standard Laplacian, the signless standard Laplacian, certain kinds of signed Laplacians and, also, for the (unbounded) Kirchhoff Laplacian of the underlying equilateral metric graph. The spectrum of the isospectral graphs is determined by the spectrum of the Laplacian of the building block G G and the spectrum for the Laplacian with Dirichlet conditions on the set of vertices V 0 V_{0} and V 1 V_{1} with multiplicities determined by the numbers r r and s s of the partition.

Recall that two linear operators with discrete spectrum are called

same eigenvalues, answering negatively Kac’s original question.
Sunada’s method is based on certain quotients of a Riemannian normal
coverings and group theoretic properties of the covering groups. An
application to discrete graphs and examples that do not arise from
Sunada’s method can be found in
[

]
. We refer to
Section

metric graphs that are isospectral for the entire family are
isomorphic, cf.
[

). Additional references
including the relation between isospectral metric and discrete graphs
and the refinement of nodal or flip counts are, for

[

,

In

Structure of the article

The following section introduces the basic notions and results on
multidigraphs and discrete magnetic Laplacians needed in this
article. We also introduce here the two basic graph operations needed
in our construction: vertex
contraction
and vertex

virtualisation
leading to the corresponding Dirichlet
Laplacian. In Section
3
we present our construction first in
a family of examples with a concrete building block graph and
illustrate some of the key ideas of the general proof in these
prototypes. The following section focuses on the notion of a frame
obtained from a generic building block (magnetic graph) with some
distinguished subset of vertices
V_{0}
. We also calculate the
spectrum of a frame member and give a group theoretical explanation of
the different multiplicities that appear in the spectrum of the
isospectral graphs. In Section
5

present many families of isospectral graphs exploiting systematically

will be used throughout the article.

2.
Magnetic discrete graphs and their Laplacians

In this section, we introduce briefly the discrete structures and
operations needed for the construction for families of isospectral
graphs. We will consider discrete locally finite graphs having
normalised weights on vertices and edges. We will also consider a
magnetic potential on the graph in terms of an
\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
-valued
function
\alpha
on the edges. Finally we will specify the discrete normalised
magnetic Laplacian as a second order discrete analogue of the usual
Laplace operator on a Riemannian manifold with magnetic
potential.

2.1.
Discrete graphs

In this subsection, we fix the notation for discrete graphs following
the notation of Sunada (cf.
[
Sun13
, Chapter 3]
), see also our
previous articles
[
FCLP18
,
FCLP22c
]
, where certain aspects are
explained in more detail.

A
(discrete multidi-)graph

G=(V,E,\partial)
is given by two
disjoint and (in this article) finite sets
V
and
E
(the set of

vertices
and of
(directed) edges
or
arcs
)
together with an
incidence map

\partial\colon E\longrightarrow V\times V
,

\partial e=(\partial_{-}e,\partial_{+}e)
, where
\partial_{-}e
is the
initial
, and

\partial_{+}e
the
terminal
vertex of
e\in E
. The
order
of

G
is
\lvert G\rvert:=\lvert V(G)\rvert
.

An
inversion map

\overline{\cdot}\colon E\longrightarrow E
is a map fulfilling

\overline{\overline{e}}=e
,
\overline{e}\neq e
,
\partial_{+}(\overline{e})=\partial_{-}e
and

\partial_{-}(\overline{e})=\partial_{+}e
for all
e\in E
. The inversion map

\overline{\cdot}
introduces a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-action on
E
, the orbits

\{e,\overline{e}\}
are called
undirected
edges; in the figures, we
only plot the undirected edges; each comes with both directions
e

and
\overline{e}
.

A
loop
is an edge
e
such that
\partial_{+}e=\partial_{-}e
(note that a
loop also has two different directed edges
e
and
\overline{e}
). Two
edges
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
are called
parallel
if
e_{1}\neq e_{2}
with

\partial_{+}e_{1}=\partial_{+}e_{2}
and
\partial_{-}e_{1}=\partial_{-}e_{2}
. A graph is

simple
if it has no loops or parallel edges.

For a vertex
v\in V
, the set of directed edges with
origin
v
is denoted as
E_{v}(G)
or simply
E_{v}
, i.e.

E_{v}(G):=\{\,e\in E\,|\,\partial_{-}e=v\,\}.

The
degree
of a vertex
v
is the cardinality of
E_{v}
, denoted
by
\deg v:=\lvert E_{v}\rvert
. Observe that loops are counted twice in
each set
E_{v}
(as
e\neq\overline{e}
). We exclude
isolated

vertices and vertices with infinite degree, i.e., we assume that

0<\deg_{G}v<\infty
for all
v\in V
. A vertex of degree
1
is
called
pendant
. If the dependence of the graph
G
is
important, we write
V=V(G)
,
E=E(G)
or
\deg_{G}
etc. Let
G

be a graph with
n
vertices labelled
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}
. The
multiset

(2.1)

\deg G:=\lMset\deg v_{1},\deg v_{2},\dots,\deg v_{n}\rMset

is called the
degree multiset
of
G
(see Appendix
A.1

for more details on multisets). A graph
G
is
connected
if
for any two vertices
u,v
there exists a path from
u
to
v
, i.e.,
there is a sequence of distinct vertices
(u,v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1},v)
and
of directed edges
(e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n})
with

v_{i}=\partial_{+}e_{i}=\partial_{-}e_{i+1}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
and
\partial_{-}e_{1}=u

and
\partial_{+}e_{n}=v
.

A
graph homomorphism

\varphi\colon G\longrightarrow G^{\prime}
between the graphs

G=(V(G),E(G))
and
G^{\prime}=(V(G^{\prime}),E(G^{\prime}))
is given by two maps

\varphi\colon V(G)\longrightarrow V(G^{\prime})
and
\varphi\colon E(G)\longrightarrow E(G^{\prime})

respecting the incidence and inversion maps, i.e., they fulfil

\partial_{\pm}(\varphi(e))=\varphi(\partial_{\pm}e)
and

\overline{\varphi(e)}=\varphi(\overline{e})
for all
e\in E(G)
. The map
\varphi
is a

graph isomorphism
if
\varphi
is a bijection on the vertex and
edge sets; in this case,
G
and
G^{\prime}
are called
isomorphic
(discrete) graphs
. Note that the degree
multiset (
2.1
) is a graph invariant, i.e., if the degree
sequence or the degree multiset of two graphs differ, then the graphs
cannot be isomorphic.

Graphs with normalised weights and magnetic potentials

We briefly define here discrete graphs with normalised weights. As
before, details can be found in

FCLP22c
]
and references

is a function

such that
w_{\overline{e}}=w_{e}
for all

,
the associated
weighted degree
is defined by

(2.2)

\deg_{G}^{w}v=\sum_{e\in E_{v}}w_{e}.

The edge weight

and vertex weight
\deg_{G}^{w}
are called

[
FCLP18
,
FCLP22c
, Section 2.2]
. A particular case is
w_{e}=1

for all
e\in E
and hence
\deg^{w}_{G}v=\deg_{G}v
is the usual
degree of
v
; this weight is called
standard
.

3

Some
authors use the name

for the corresponding
Laplacian (see below) for we call
standard
here (see,

[
Ch97
,
BuG11
]
).
We extend

and

\deg^{w}_{G}
naturally as discrete measures on
E(G)
and

.

Next, we define magnetic potentials on discrete graphs
(see
[
FCLP18
,
FCLP22c
]
and references therein for details). A

is a function
\alpha\colon E\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
such

\alpha_{\overline{e}}=-\alpha_{e}
for all

. We call

For the standard weight and no magnetic potential (i.e.,

Two magnetic graphs

such that

for all

.

2.3.
Contraction of vertices

An important operation we need in this article is the

contraction of vertices
(also called
gluing
or

merging
of vertices in the literature). We introduce the
shrinking number
s
that quantifies the reduction of the number of
vertices in the quotient graph.

Definition 2.1

(Contracting vertices and shrinking number)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and
\sim
an equivalence relation on the
vertex set
V
. The graph
G/{\mathord{\sim}}=(V/{\mathord{\sim}},E)
obtained
from
G
by
contracting the vertices
(with respect to

\sim
) is given by
V/{\mathord{\sim}}
and incidence function

\partial(e)=([\partial_{-}(e)],[\partial_{+}(e)])
. The
shrinking number

s

of the equivalence relation
\sim
on
V(G)
is defined by

s:=\lvert G\rvert-\lvert G/{\mathord{\sim}}\rvert=\lvert V\rvert-\lvert V/{\mathord{\sim}}\rvert
.

Note that if we contract two
adjacent
vertices
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
,
all edges joining
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
become
loops
in

G/\mathord{\sim}
(in contrast to some conventions in combinatorics
where only simple graphs are allowed).

Given a discrete graph
G=(V,E)
with (edge) weight
w
, we choose

w
also as weight on the quotient graph

G/{\mathord{\sim}}=(V/{\mathord{\sim}},E)
(denoted by the same symbol
w
). For
the quotient, we have

E_{[v]}(G/{\mathord{\sim}})=\bigdcup_{v^{\prime}\in[v]}E_{v^{\prime}}(G),

where
\dcup
means that the union is
disjoint
. Hence the
weighted degree is

(2.3)

\deg_{G/{\mathord{\sim}}}^{w}[v]=w(E_{[v]}(G/{\mathord{\sim}}))=\sum_{e\in E_{[v]}(G/{\mathord{\sim}})}w_{e}=\sum_{v^{\prime}\in[v]}\sum_{e\in E_{v}}w_{e}=\sum_{v^{\prime}\in[v]}\deg_{G}^{w}v^{\prime}.

It follows that the quotient map
\pi\colon G\longrightarrow G/{\mathord{\sim}}
given by

v\mapsto[v]
is a measure preserving map, since the edge weights
are the same (the edges are actually not changed) and since

\displaystyle(\pi_{*}\deg_{G}^{w})(\widetilde{V})=\deg_{G}^{w}(\pi^{-1}(\widetilde{V}))=\sum_{v^{\prime}\in\pi^{-1}(\widetilde{V})}\deg_{G}^{w}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{[v]\in\widetilde{V}}\sum_{v^{\prime}\in[v]}\deg_{G}^{w}(v^{\prime})

(2.4)

\displaystyle=\sum_{[v]\in\widetilde{V}}\deg_{G/{\mathord{\sim}}}^{w}([v])=\deg_{G/{\mathord{\sim}}}^{w}(\widetilde{V})

Definition 2.2

Remark 2.3

Definition 2.4

(isospectral magnetic graphs)

Remark 2.5

(multiple edges versus weighted graphs without multiple edges)
.

A weighted graph
G
with multiple edges can be turned into a graph

\widetilde{G}
without multiple edges. On the other hand, a weighted
graph with integer (edge) weights can be turned into a graph with
multiple edges and standard weights. We discuss for simplicity only
the case of graphs without loops and magnetic potential.

It turns out that both graphs (with or without multiple edges) have
the same Laplacian. This leads us to the following definition: We
say that two weighted graphs
\bm{G}=(G,0,w)
and

\widetilde{\bm{G}}=(\widetilde{G},0,\widetilde{w})
on the same vertex set

V(\bm{G})=V(\widetilde{\bm{G}})

\deg^{w}_{\bm{G}}=\deg^{\widetilde{w}}_{\widetilde{\bm{G}}}
and

\Delta_{{\bm{G}}}f=\Delta_{{\widetilde{\bm{G}}}}f

, or, equivalently, if the matrix
representation of their Laplacians is the same.

Clearly, isomorphic magnetic graphs are isolaplacian, and
isolaplacian magnetic graphs are isospectral (as the corresponding
matrices are the same), but not vice versa.

(a)

Assume that
\bm{G}=(G,0,w)
is a weighted graph without magnetic
potential. If
G
has multiple edges, we define a weighted graph

\widetilde{G}=(\widetilde{G},0,\widetilde{w})
as follows: Denote by

(2.8)

E(v_{-},v_{+}):=\{\,e\in E(G)\,|\,\partial_{-}e=v_{-},\partial_{+}e=v_{+}\,\}.

Then

and terminating at
v_{+}
. We obtain a graph

without multiple edges by identifying

into a single edge

provided

Then the graphs
\bm{G}
and

are isolaplacian. We call

the
underlying simple weighted graph
. Note that
the graphs
\bm{G}
and

are not isomorphic (as a multiple

(b)

On the other hand, a weighted graph

4.
Frames constructed from a building block and their spectra

We construct formally in this section a family of magnetic graphs, called a

frame
, constructed from a given magnetic graph as a building
block and a subset of its vertex set along which we identify copies of
the given graph. In particular, the spectrum of this family depends
only on the given magnetic graph and the subset of vertices.

Given a magnetic graph
\bm{G}
and a subset
V_{0}
of its vertices, we
define a geometrical construction of graphs with a symmetric
structure:

Definition 4.1

(frame members and frames)
.

Let
\bm{G}=(G,\alpha,w)
be a magnetic graph (which we call
building block
),
V_{0}\subset V(G)
and
a\in\mathbb{N}
.

(a)

Define the
a
-th frame member
obtained from
G

identified along
V_{0}
by

F_{a}=F_{a}(G,V_{0}):=G^{a}/{\mathord{\sim_{V_{0}}}},\qquad\text{where}\qquad G^{a}:=\bigdcup_{j\in\{1,\dots,a\}}(G\times\{j\})

is the disjoint union of
a
copies of
G
with vertex set

V(G^{a})=V(G)\times\{1,\dots,a\}
and edge set

E(G^{a})=E(G)\times\{1,\dots,a\}
. The equivalence relation

\sim_{V_{0}}
on the vertex set
V(G^{a})
of the disjoint union is
given by

(v,i)\sim(v^{\prime},j)\qquad\text{if and only if}\qquad v=v^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad v\in V_{0},

and all other pairs are equivalent only to itself, i.e., we contract
each vertex
v\in V_{0}
of all copies to one vertex. Denote by

[(v,j)]
the corresponding class specifying a vertex in
F_{a}
.
Moreover, we call

\bm{F}_{a}(\bm{G},V_{0}):=(F_{a}(G,V_{0}),\alpha,w)

the
a
-th (
\bm{G}
-)frame member
obtained from the magnetic graph

\bm{G}
identified along
V_{0}
, where the weights and vector
potentials are the same on each copy (and also denoted by the same
symbol), i.e.,
w_{(e,j)}:=w_{e}
and
\alpha_{(e,j)}:=\alpha_{e}

for all
e\in E(G)
and
j\in\{1,\dots,a\}
.

(b)

The family
(\bm{F}_{a}(\bm{G},V_{0}))_{a\in\mathbb{N}}
is called a

(
\bm{G}
-)frame
identified along
V_{0}
.

If the dependence on
G
and
V_{0}
are clear from the context we
will denote a frame member and a frame simply by
\bm{F}_{a}
and

(\bm{F}_{a})_{a\in\mathbb{N}}
, respectively.

Remark 4.2
.

It is easily seen that for a simple graph
\bm{G}
and
a\geq 2
, the
frame
\bm{F}_{a}(\bm{G},V_{0})
is simple if and only if there are no
edges inside the identified vertex set
V_{0}
.

Remark 4.3

(frames with virtualised vertices)
.

We also allow that
\bm{G}
has

V_{1}\subset V(\bm{G})
, i.e., vertices on which we impose Dirichlet
boundary conditions (see Definition
2.8
). We write

(\bm{F}_{a})^{+}_{V_{1}}:=\bm{F}_{a}(\bm{G}^{+}_{V_{1}},V_{0})
for the

a
-th
\bm{G}^{+}_{V_{1}}
-frame member identified along
V_{0}
. Note
that the case
V_{1}=\emptyset
is just the case described in
Definition
4.1
.

The following result is a direct result of the preceding definitions.

Lemma 4.4

(order and degree multiset of frame members)
.

Let
\bm{G}^{+}_{V_{1}}
be a magnetic graph with virtualised vertices

V_{1}\subset V(G)
. Moreover, let
V_{0}
be a subset of vertices
such that
V_{1}\subset V_{0}\subset V

a
-th frame member

are given

\displaystyle\lvert(F_{a})^{+}_{V_{1}}\rvert=a(\lvert G\rvert-\lvert V_{0}\rvert)+\lvert V_{0}\rvert-\lvert V_{1}\rvert\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lvert E(F_{a})\rvert=\lvert E((F_{a})^{+}_{V_{1}})\rvert

\displaystyle=a\lvert E(G)\rvert.

Moreover, the degree multiset of
(F_{a})^{+}_{V_{1}}
is

\deg\bigl((F_{a})^{+}_{V_{1}}\bigr)=\biguplus_{v\in V(G)\setminus V_{0}}\lMset\deg_{G}v\rMset^{(a)}\uplus\biguplus_{v_{0}\in V_{0}\setminus V_{1}}\lMset a\deg_{G}v_{0}\rMset.

We prove next, that the spectrum of the magnetic graph

\bm{F}_{a}(\bm{G}^{+}_{V_{1}},V_{0})

identified along

and

Basically, we use the cyclic symmetry of the

-th frame member.

Proposition 4.5

(spectrum of frame members)
.

Let
\bm{G}^{+}_{V_{1}}
be a magnetic graph with virtualised vertices

V_{1}\subset V(G)
. Moreover, let
V_{0}
be a subset of vertices
such that
V_{1}\subset V_{0}\subset V
. Then the spectrum of the

a
-th
\bm{G}^{+}_{V_{1}}
-frame member

\bm{F}_{a}(\bm{G}^{+}_{V_{1}},V_{0})
identified along
V_{0}
(
a\in\mathbb{N}
)
is given by

\sigma_{\mathrm{}}\bigl(\bm{F}_{a}(\bm{G}^{+}_{V_{1}},V_{0})\bigr)=\sigma_{\mathrm{}}\bigl(\bm{G}^{+}_{V_{1}}\bigr)\uplus\sigma_{\mathrm{}}\bigl(\bm{G}^{+}_{V_{0}}\bigr)^{(a-1)}.

Proof.

We assume for ease of notation that
V_{1}=\emptyset
, i.e.,

\bm{G}^{+}_{V_{1}}=\bm{G}
, the general case can be treated exactly in the
same way.

Let
n=\lvert\bm{G}\rvert=\lvert V\rvert
and

\sigma_{\mathrm{}}(\bm{G})=\lMset\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\dots,\rho_{n}\rMset
be eigenvalues
of
\Delta_{{\bm{G}}}
with corresponding orthonormal eigenfunctions given
by
\{f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{n}\}
. For each
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we
extend first the eigenfunction
f_{k}
onto the disjoint union
\bm{G}^{a}

to a function
\widetilde{f}_{k}\colon V(\bm{G}^{a})\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
by

\widetilde{f}_{k}(v,j)=f_{k}(v)
for
j\in\{1,\dots,a\}
. With a little
abuse of notation we write by
\widetilde{f}_{k}
also the corresponding
function on the
a
-th frame member
\bm{F}_{a}=\bm{F}_{a}(\bm{G},V_{0})
,
which is naturally defined since the functions have the same value
on the contracted vertices
V_{0}
. The proof of the multiset
inclusion
\sigma_{\mathrm{}}\bigl(\bm{G}\bigr)\subset\sigma_{\mathrm{}}\bigl(\bm{F}_{a}(\bm{G},V_{0})\bigr)
is then
a special case of the first part of the proof in
Theorem
5.7
(choose
A=\lMset a\rMset
in the
construction of the symmetric functions).

To show that also the eigenvalues of
\bm{G}^{+}_{V_{0}}
with
Dirichlet conditions on
V_{0}
lift to eigenvalues on the frame
member
\bm{F}_{a}
put

n^{\prime}=\lvert\bm{G}^{+}_{V_{0}}\rvert=n-\lvert V_{0}\rvert
. Let

\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n^{\prime}}
be the eigenvalues of

\bm{G}^{+}_{V_{0}}
with corresponding orthonormal eigenfunctions

h_{1},h_{2},\dots,h_{n^{\prime}}
. Moreover, let
\eta\in\mathbb{C}
be a
non-trivial
a
-th root of unity, i.e.,
\eta\neq 1
and
\eta^{a}=1
.
For each such
\eta
we extend the eigenfunction
h_{k^{\prime}}
to an
eigenfunction
\widetilde{h}_{k^{\prime},\eta}
onto
\bm{F}_{a}
by

\widetilde{h}_{k^{\prime},\eta}([(v,j)])=\eta^{j}h_{k^{\prime}}(v).

Note that this choice is also well-defined on the quotient as

h_{k^{\prime}}(v)=0
for
v\in V_{0}
. We now show that
\widetilde{h}_{k^{\prime},\eta}

is an eigenfunction of
\Delta_{{\bm{F}_{a}}}
with eigenvalue

\lambda_{k^{\prime}}
: If
v\in V_{0}
, we have
h_{k^{\prime}}(v)=0
and

\displaystyle\bigl(\Delta_{{\bm{F}_{a}}}\widetilde{h}_{k^{\prime},\eta}\bigr)[(v,j)]

\displaystyle=0-\frac{1}{\deg_{G^{a}}^{w}[(v,j)]}\sum_{e\in E_{[(v,j)]}(F_{a})}w_{e}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha_{e}}\widetilde{h}_{k^{\prime},\eta}(\partial_{+}e)

\displaystyle=0-\frac{1}{\deg_{G}^{w}v}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{a}\eta^{j}\Bigr)\sum_{e\in E_{v}(G)}w_{e}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha_{e}}h_{k^{\prime}}(\partial_{+}e)=0=\lambda_{k^{\prime}}\widetilde{h}_{k^{\prime},\eta}[(v,j)]

as
\sum_{j=1}^{a}\eta^{j}=0
since
\eta\neq 1
.

If
v\in V\setminus V_{0}
, all equivalence classes

[(v,j)]
contain only one element, again denoted by
(v,j)
. In
this case, we have

\displaystyle\bigl(\Delta_{{\bm{F}_{a}}}\widetilde{h}_{k^{\prime},\eta}\bigr)(v,j)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{h}_{k^{\prime},\eta}(v,j)-\frac{1}{\deg_{G^{a}}^{w}[(v,j)]}\sum_{e\in E_{[(v,j)]}(F_{a})}w_{e}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha_{e}}\widetilde{h}_{k^{\prime},\eta}(\partial_{+}e)

\displaystyle=\eta^{j}\Bigl(h_{k^{\prime}}(v)-\frac{1}{\deg_{G}^{w}v}\sum_{e\in E_{v}(G)}w_{e}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha_{e}}h_{k^{\prime}}(\partial_{+}e)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\eta^{j}\lambda_{k^{\prime}}h_{k^{\prime}}(v)=\lambda_{k^{\prime}}\widetilde{h}_{k^{\prime},\eta}(v,j).

This shows that
\widetilde{h}_{k^{\prime},\eta}
is an
eigenfunction of
\Delta_{{\bm{F}_{a}}}
with eigenvalue
\lambda_{k^{\prime}}
as claimed.

Next we will show that the constructed eigenfunctions on the frame

\sum_{j=1}^{a}\eta^{j}=0
since
\eta\neq 1
. Therefore, we have

\bigl\langle{\widetilde{f}_{k}},{\widetilde{h}_{k^{\prime},\eta}}\bigr\rangle_{\ell_{2}({V(\bm{F}_{a}),\deg_{G^{a}}^{w}})}=\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{a}\overline{\eta}^{j}\Bigr)\bigl\langle{f_{k}},{h_{k^{\prime}}}\bigr\rangle_{\ell_{2}({V(\bm{G}),\deg_{G}^{w}})}=0.

Second, the eigenfunctions corresponding to different non-trivial

\bigl\langle{\widetilde{h}_{k^{\prime},\eta}},{\widetilde{h}_{k^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}}}\bigr\rangle_{\ell_{2}({V(\bm{F}_{a}),\deg_{G^{a}}^{w}})}=\sum_{j=1}^{a}\bigl\langle{\eta^{j}h_{k^{\prime}}},{(\eta^{\prime})^{j}h_{k^{\prime}}}\bigr\rangle_{\ell_{2}({V(\bm{G}),\deg_{G}^{w}})}=\sum_{j=1}^{a}(\eta\overline{\eta}^{\prime})^{j}=0\;

Here,
\eta\neq 1\neq\eta^{\prime}
and
\eta\neq\eta^{\prime}
, so that the product

\eta\eta^{\prime}
also determines a non-trivial root of unity. Therefore,
we have shown that the functions
\widetilde{f}_{k}
and
\widetilde{h}_{k^{\prime},\eta}

\sigma_{\mathrm{}}\bigl(\bm{G}^{+}_{V_{0}}\bigr)^{(a-1)}\subset\sigma_{\mathrm{}}\bigl(\bm{F}_{a}(\bm{G},V_{0})\bigr)

,

hence we have found all eigenvalues.

Remark 4.6

.

4.5
there

\mathbb{Z}_{a}

\mathbb{Z}_{a}
on the

\ell_{2}

U
of
\mathbb{Z}_{a}
. Since
\mathbb{Z}_{a}
is a finite Abelian group
the unitary dual satisfies
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{a}
and

which consist of multiplication with
a
-th root of unity (see

[

proof of Proposition
4.5
essentially show that the lifted

and

-eigenfunctions not only reduce the Laplacian but also
reduce the regular representation
U

5.

Disjoint frame unions

Symmetric eigenfunctions.

\displaystyle=\widetilde{f}_{k}(\widetilde{v})-\frac{1}{\deg_{F_{A,V_{1}}}^{w}(\widetilde{v})}\sum_{e\in E_{\widetilde{v}}(F_{A,V_{1}})}w_{e}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha_{e}}\widetilde{f}_{k}(\partial_{+}e)

and the inclusion of multisets

\sigma_{\mathrm{}}\bigl(\bm{G}^{+}_{V_{0}}\bigr)^{(a_{i}-1)}\subset\sigma_{\mathrm{}}\bigl(\bm{F}_{a_{i}}(\bm{G}^{+}_{V_{0}})\bigr)
follows. Since this inclusion holds for each

i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
and since
a_{1}+\dots+a_{s}=r
we conclude that

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathrm{}}\bigl(\bm{G}^{+}_{V_{0}}\bigr)^{(r-s)}\subset\sigma_{\mathrm{}}\bigl(\bm{F}_{A,V_{1}}\bigr).

As in the proof of Proposition
4.5
we also see that the functions

\widetilde{f}_{k}
and
\widetilde{h}_{k^{\prime\prime},\eta,i}
are mutually orthogonal since the
functions
\widetilde{h}
are supported on each frame member. Moreover, the
eigenfunctions
\widetilde{g}_{k^{\prime},\varepsilon}
constructed in the second step and

\widetilde{h}_{k^{\prime\prime},\eta,i}
turn out
to be also mutually orthogonal with a similar computation with the root of
unity as in the second step. This shows that

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathrm{}}\bigl(\bm{G}^{+}_{V_{1}}\bigr)^{(s-1)}\subset\sigma_{\mathrm{}}\bigl(\bm{F}_{A,V_{1}}\bigr).

Finally, we have to check that we identified all eigenvalues.
In fact, we have identified
\lvert G\rvert
symmetric eigenfunctions and

\sum_{a\in A}(a-1)(\lvert\bm{G}\rvert-\lvert V_{0}\rvert)=(r-s)(\lvert\bm{G}\rvert-\lvert V_{0}\rvert)

eigenfunctions from
\bm{G}^{+}_{V_{0}}
. Moreover, the remaining
eigenfunctions are
\widetilde{g}_{k^{\prime},\varepsilon}
for

k^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots,\lvert G\rvert-\lvert V_{1}\rvert\}
and
\varepsilon^{s}=1
with

\varepsilon\neq 1
determine
(s-1)(\lvert G\rvert-\lvert V_{1}\rvert)
additional eigenvalues.
Altogether, we have specified

\lvert G\rvert+(r-s)(\lvert\bm{G}\rvert-\lvert V_{0}\rvert)+(s-1)(\lvert G\rvert-\lvert V_{1}\rvert)=r(\lvert\bm{G}\rvert-\lvert V_{0}\rvert)+s(\lvert V_{0}\rvert-\lvert V_{1}\rvert)+\lvert V_{1}\rvert

eigenvalues corresponding to a mutually orthogonal set of
eigenfunctions. Since, according to Lemma
5.6
, the order
of
F_{A,V_{1}}
is precisely

r(\lvert\bm{G}\rvert-\lvert V_{0}\rvert)+s(\lvert V_{0}\rvert-\lvert V_{1}\rvert)+\lvert V_{1}\rvert

we conclude that we found all eigenvalues and the spectrum of the
magnetic Laplacian is determined.
∎

We can now formulate our main result on the construction of
isospectral, non-isomorphic and, now, connected graphs.

Theorem 5.8

(main theorem)
.

Let
\bm{G}=(G,\alpha,w)
be a magnetic graph,
V_{0}\subset V(G)
and
let
V_{1}\subset V_{0}
be a set of distinguished vertices. For any
different pair
A
and
B
of
s
-partitions of a natural number

r\geq 4
the graphs
\bm{F}_{A,V_{1}}
and
\bm{F}_{B,V_{1}}
(the

V_{1}
-contracted
A
- respectively
B
-union of

(\bm{F}_{a}(\bm{G},V_{0}))_{a\in\mathbb{N}}
, cf. Definition
5.5
) are
isospectral and not isomorphic.

Proof.

In the calculation of the spectrum in Theorem
5.7
,
only the natural number
r
and the length
s
of the partition are
relevant and not the concrete partitions
A
and
B
. Therefore
different
s
-partitions of
r
lead to isospectral graphs.
Moreover, the fact that two graphs determined by different
partitions are not isomorphic follows from Lemma
5.6
since
the corresponding degree multisets are different.
∎

Corollary 5.9

(isospectral equilateral metric graphs)
.

Let
G
be a discrete graph (with standard weights),

V_{0}\subset V(G)
and let
V_{1}\subset V_{0}
be a set of
distinguished vertices. For any different pair
A
and
B
of

s
-partitions of a natural number
r\geq 4
the corresponding
equilateral metric graphs
\overline{F}_{A,V_{1}}
and
\overline{F}_{B,V_{1}}

constructed according to the corresponding discrete graphs are
isospectral and not isomorphic.

Proof.

The proof follows from Theorem
5.8
and Proposition
2.9
, as in
our construction,
F_{A,V_{1}}
and
F_{B,V_{1}}
have the same
number of edges.
∎

7.
Conclusions

Sunada’s method
[
Sun85
]
as well as some of its
generalisations
[
BPBS09
,
PB10
,
BBJL17
]
use
representation theory as a key ingredient to construct the isospectral
manifolds or graphs which appear as quotient graphs. It is worth to point
out differences and similarities between the method presented here and
the one given, for example, in
[
BPBS09
]
even if we think that
both methods are different in nature. We do not exclude though that
both approaches may be combined.

In

Appendix A
Multisets and partitions

A.1.
Multisets

Multisets will be a convenient tool to deal with spectra and degree
lists of graphs.

•

A
multiset
is an ordered pair
(A,m)
where
A
is a set
and
m\colon A\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
is a map on
A
. We say that

x\in A
appears
m(x)
-times
in
A
. In order to simplify
notation, we set
m_{A}(x)=0
whenever
x\notin A
.

•

We often simply refer to
A
as a multiset without mentioning
the map
m
, and hence refer to the multiplicity map of
A
as

m_{A}
. Sets
A
can be considered as multisets by setting

m_{A}(x)=1
whenever
x\in A
and
0
otherwise.

•

The
cardinality
of the multiset
A
is the sum of the
multiplicities of all its elements, i.e.,

\lvert A\rvert=\sum_{x\in A}m_{A}(x)
.

•

When
A
is finite (i.e.,
\lvert A\rvert<\infty
) we write

A=\{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{s}\}
, and a multiset
(A,m)
will then also be
written as

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\lMset a_{1}^{(m(a_{1}))},a_{2}^{(m(a_{2}))},\dots,a_{s}^{(m(a_{s}))}\rMset\;=\;\lMset\underbrace{a_{1},\dots,a_{1}}_{m(a_{1})},\underbrace{a_{2},\dots,a_{2}}_{m(a_{2})},\dots,\underbrace{a_{s},\dots,a_{s}}_{m(a_{s})}\rMset.

Multisets with elements in
\mathbb{N}
or
\mathbb{R}
(or any other linearly
ordered set) can be also seen as ordered lists and sometimes it will
be convenient for the exposition to take this point of view.

•

The
sum
of two multisets
A
and
B
denoted as

A\uplus B
is the set
A\cup B
with multiplicity map

m_{A_{\uplus}B}(x):=m_{A}(x)+m_{B}(x)
for all
x\in A\cup B
.

•

The
difference
of two multisets, denoted by
A\ominus B

is the set
A\setminus B
with multiplicity map

m_{A\ominus B}(x):=\max\{m_{A}(x)-m_{B}(x),0\}
.

•

The
k
-th multiple
of a multiset
A
(with multiplicity
map
m_{A}
), denoted by
A^{(k)}
, is the multiset
A
with
multiplicity map
m_{A^{(k)}}(x)=km_{A}(x)
for all
x\in A
, i.e.,
the multiplicity of each element in
A^{(k)}
is multiplied by
k
.

A.2.
Partitions of a natural number

The notion of a partition of a natural number will be important for
our construction of isospectral graphs.

Definition A.1

(partition of a number)
.

A partition of a natural number
r
of length
s
(or an
s
-
partition

r
for short) is a multiset
A
of natural numbers with sum
r
, i.e.,

A=\lMset a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{s}\rMset\quad\text{such that}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}=r.

The number
s
is called the
length of the partition
.

We frequently use the following equations

(A.1)

\sum_{a\in A}1=s\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{a\in A}a=r,

where
A
is understood as a multiset.

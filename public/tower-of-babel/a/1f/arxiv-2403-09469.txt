Unexpected subgroup schemes of PGL 2 , k \operatorname{PGL}_{2,k} in characteristic 2 2
Bianca Gouthier, Dajano Tossici

Abstract
. If the characteristic of a field k k is odd any infinitesimal group scheme of PGL 2 , k \operatorname{PGL}_{2,k} lifts to SL 2 , k \operatorname{SL}_{2,k} . In this paper, we prove that this is not true in characteristic 2 2 and we give a complete description, up to isomorphism, of infinitesimal unipotent subgroup schemes of PGL 2 , k \operatorname{PGL}_{2,k} . Also, the infinitesimal trigonalizable case is considered.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are interested in finite subgroup schemes of
\operatorname{PGL}_{2,k}
.
In his paper
[
Bea10
]
, Beauville classified, up to conjugacy, all finite subgroups of
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(k)
of order coprime with the characteristic. Here we are interested in the opposite case, infinitesimal subgroup schemes.
It seems to the authors that it is quite an accepted fact that any infinitesimal subgroup scheme of
\operatorname{PGL}_{2,k}
lifts to
\operatorname{GL}_{2,k}
. In particular any unipotent infinitesimal sugbroup scheme of
\operatorname{PGL}_{2,k}
would be a subgroup scheme of
\mathbb{G}_{a,k}
, and so it would be isomorphic to
\alpha_{p^{n},k}
for some
n\geq 0
. In this paper, we prove that this is not true if the characteristic of the field is
2
. The result is instead true if the characteristic is odd and
we give a proof of it in the section §
2
.

We recall that, for any field
k
,
\operatorname{PGL}_{2,k}
represents the automorphism group functor of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
. So the study of subgroup schemes corresponds to faithful actions on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
.
Moreover
\operatorname{PGL}_{2,k}(k)
coincides with the Cremona group in dimension one, i.e. birational morphisms of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
, since any rational morphism from a projective nonsingular curve extends to the whole curve. In positive characteristic, the situation is completely different if we consider rational actions of infinitesimal group schemes. Most of the faithful infinitesimal actions of the affine line do not extend to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
. For instance, all the actions of
\alpha_{p}^{n}
, with
n\geq 4
, over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}
do not extend to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
, since
\operatorname{PGL}_{2,k}
has dimension
3
and the Lie algebra of
\alpha_{p}^{n}
has dimension
n
. See, for instance,
[
Bri22
, Lemma 3.6]
and
[
Gou24
, Corollary 6.9]
.

The main result of the paper is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
2
.

(1)

The infinitesimal unipotent subgroup schemes of
\operatorname{PGL}_{2,k}
are, up to isomorphism, all and only the subgroup schemes of the semi-direct product
\alpha_{2^{n},k}\rtimes\alpha_{2,k}
, with
n\geq 1
, where the action of
\alpha_{2,k}
on
\alpha_{2^{n},k}
is given by
a\cdot b=b+ab^{2}
.

(2)

If
k
is perfect,
any infinitesimal trigonalizable, not unipotent, subgroup scheme of
\operatorname{PGL}_{2,k}
is isomorphic to
\mu_{2^{l},k}
or to the semi-direct product of
\mu_{2^{l},k}
, for some
l\geq 1
, by one of the two unipotent group schemes

(i)

the semi-direct product
\alpha_{2^{n},k}\rtimes\alpha_{2}
, with
n\geq 1
, where the action of
\alpha_{2,k}
on
\alpha_{2^{n},k}
is given by
a\cdot b=b+ab^{2}

(ii)

\alpha_{2^{n},k}

for some nontrivial action of
\mu_{2^{l},k}
.

An explicit description of all these group schemes will be given in the section §
4
.
While the above Theorem gives a complete classification of infinitesimal unipotent subgroup schemes of
\operatorname{PGL}_{2,k}
, for trigonalizable group schemes
we do not know if, for any nontrivial action of
\mu_{2^{l},k}
over the unipotent group schemes in
(i)
, the associated semi-direct product acts faithfully on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
. In the section §
6
, we prove that there exists at least one action of
\mu_{2^{l},k}
over any unipotent group scheme which appears in
(i)
such that the associated semi-direct product acts faithfully on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
.
In the commutative case, we get a complete classification over an algebraically closed field.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
2
.
The list of infinitesimal commutative subgroup schemes of
\operatorname{PGL}_{2,k}
, up to isomorphism, is the following:

(1)

\alpha_{2^{n},k}
, for some
n\geq 0
,

(2)

\alpha_{2,k}\times\alpha_{2,k}
,

(3)

the
2
-torsion of a supersingular elliptic curve,

(4)

\mu_{2^{n}}
, for some
n>0
.

The corollary follows from the Theorem using the Lemma
4.2
.

2.
Infinitesimal subgroup schemes of
\operatorname{PGL}_{2,k}
in characteristic
p>2

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p
.
Let
G
be an infinitesimal subgroup scheme of
\operatorname{PGL}_{2,k}
. Since
\operatorname{SL}_{2,k}\to\operatorname{PGL}_{2,k}
is an étale covering then
G
lifts to
\operatorname{SL}_{2,k}
. This result is known and, for instance, it is mentioned in Fakhruddin
[
Fak20
]
.
We however report here the details of the proof, which are not present in the aforementioned paper. We have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Any extension of group schemes

1\to H\to G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\to}}Q\to 1,

such that
H
is étale and commutative and
Q
is infinitesimal,
is trivial and
G
is isomorphic to the direct product
H\times Q
.

Proof.

First of all, we observe that any action of
Q
on
H
is trivial.
Then the result follows from
[
DG70
, III §6, n.7]
.
We give here the proof in this simpler case.
Since
H
is étale and
Q
is infinitesimal then there is a schematic section of
\pi
. So all such extensions are classified by the Hochschild cohomology group
H_{0}^{2}(Q,H)

(see
[
DG70
, III §6, n.7]
). Since
Q
is infinitesimal and
H
is étale any scheme morphism from
Q^{2}\to H
is constant, so
H_{0}^{2}(Q,H)
is trivial.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

If
k
is a field of characteristic
p
and
p
does not divide
n
then any infinitesimal subgroup scheme of
\operatorname{PGL}_{n,k}
lifts to
\operatorname{SL}_{n,k}
.

Proof.

We consider the exact sequence

1\to\mu_{n,k}\to\operatorname{SL}_{n,k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\to}}\operatorname{PGL}_{n,k}\to 1.

Since
p
does not divide
n
then
\mu_{n,k}
is an étale group scheme. Let
G
be an infinitesimal subgroup scheme of
\operatorname{PGL}_{n,k}
. Then
\pi^{-1}G
is an extension of
G
by
\mu_{n,k}
. By the previous Lemma the extension is trivial, so
G
lifts to
\operatorname{SL}_{n,k}
.
∎

In particular the above Proposition applies when
n=2
and
p>2
.

3.
Infinitesimal unipotent subgroups schemes of
\operatorname{GL}_{2,k}

In this subsection, we give an explicit description of all infinitesimal unipotent subgroup schemes of
\operatorname{GL}_{2,k}
, where
k
is a field of positive characteristic
p
. The following result will be used in the proof of the Theorem.

Proposition 3.1
.

Any infinitesimal unipotent subgroup scheme of
\operatorname{GL}_{2,k}
is one of the following subgroup schemes of
\operatorname{SL}_{2,k}

H_{s_{1},s_{2},n}=\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x_{11}&x_{12}\\
x_{21}&x_{22}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\ker\operatorname{F}^{n}_{\operatorname{SL}_{2,k}}|s_{i}(x_{ii}-1)+s_{j}(x_{ij})=0,x_{22}=2-x_{11}\text{ for }(i,j)=(1,2),(2,1)\},

for some
[s_{1}:s_{2}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(k)
and
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

Any infinitesimal unipotent subgroup scheme of
\operatorname{GL}_{2,k}
is isomorphic to
\alpha_{p^{n},k}
, for some
n
, since, up to conjugation, it is contained in the subgroup of upper triangular unipotent matrices, which is isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{a,k}
.
Moreover any unipotent subgroup scheme
H
of
\operatorname{GL}_{2,k}
is contained in
\operatorname{SL}_{2,k}
since, over a field, any map from a unipotent group scheme to a diagonalizable group scheme is trivial, so the restriction of the determinant to
H
is trivial. We now observe that

\Hom_{k-gr}(\alpha_{p^{n},k},\operatorname{SL}_{2,k})\subseteq\operatorname{SL}_{2,k}(k[T]/(T^{p^{n}}))

and it consists of matrices

A(\overline{T})
such that
A(\overline{S+T})=A(\overline{S})A(\overline{T})\in\operatorname{SL}_{2,k}(k[S,T]/(S^{p^{n}},T^{p^{n}})
.
Since
A(0)=\Id
we have that

A(\overline{T})=\sum_{i=0}^{p^{n}-1}A_{i}\overline{T}^{i}

with
A_{i}\in M_{2}(k)
for any
0\leq i\leq p^{n}-1
and
A_{0}=\Id
.
Now

A(\overline{S+T})=\sum_{i=0}^{p^{n}-1}\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{i}{j}A_{i}\overline{S}^{j}\overline{T}^{i-j}

and

A(\overline{S})A(\overline{T})=\sum_{i,j=0}^{p^{n}-1}A_{i}A_{j}\overline{S}^{i}\overline{T}^{j}.

Therefore for any
0\leq i,j<p^{n}
we have that

\binom{i+j}{j}A_{i+j}=A_{i}A_{j}

where we set
A_{k}=0
if
k\geq p^{n}
.
Then

A_{i}^{p}=\binom{2i}{i}\cdots\binom{pi}{i}A_{pi}=\frac{(pi)!}{(i!)^{p}}A_{pi}

for any
0\leq i\leq p^{n}-1
.
Now

v_{p}\Big(\frac{(pi)!}{{(i!)}^{p}}\Big)=i-(p-1)v_{p}(i!)>0,

so

A_{i}^{p}=0.

Moreover
A_{i}
commutes with
A_{j}
for any
0\leq i,j\leq p^{n}-1
.
It is known that if two nilpotent matrices of rank
2
commute then one is a multiple of the other.
Therefore there exists a nilpotent matrix
B\in M_{2}(k)
and
f(\overline{T})\in k[T]/(T^{p^{n}})
such that

A(\overline{T})=\Id+f(\overline{T})B
. Now it is easy to verify that
f(\overline{T})
is additive.
Then the matrix
A(\overline{T})
belongs to
H_{s_{1},s_{2},n}(k[T]/(T^{p^{n}}))
, where
(s_{1},s_{2})\in\ker B\setminus\{(0,0)\}
.
So any infinitesimal unipotent subgroup scheme of
\operatorname{GL}_{2,k}
is contained in some
H_{s_{1},s_{2},n}
for some
[s_{1}:s_{2}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(k)
.
On the other hand, for any
[s_{1}:s_{2}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(k)
the matrix

A(\overline{T})=\Id+\overline{T}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}s_{1}s_{2}&-s_{1}^{2}\\
s_{2}^{2}&-s_{1}s_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)

gives an isomorphism between
\alpha_{p^{n},k}
and
H_{s_{1},s_{2},n}
.

∎

4.
Some noncommutative unipotent infinitesimal group schemes.

In this section we explicitly describe the group schemes which appear in the Theorem.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p
and consider the action of
\alpha_{p,k}
, as an automorphism of groups, on
\mathbb{G}_{a,k}
given by
a\cdot b=b+ab^{p}
.
We define the associated semi-direct product
\mathcal{E}=\mathbb{G}_{a,k}\rtimes\alpha_{p,k}
.

(i)

For any
n\geq 0
we define the subgroup scheme
\mathcal{D}_{n}
of
\mathcal{E}
induced by the closed immersion
\alpha_{p^{n},k}\to\mathbb{G}_{a,k}
. It is isomorphic to the induced semi-direct product
\alpha_{p^{n}}\rtimes\alpha_{p,k}
.

(ii)

For any
a\in k
and
n\geq 1
, we define
\mathcal{H}_{a,n}
as the kernel of the morphism

(-a\operatorname{F}^{n-1},i):\mathcal{D}_{n}\to\mathbb{G}_{a,k}

where
i
is the inclusion
i:\alpha_{p,k}\to\mathbb{G}_{a,k}
.

Explicitly we have that
\mathcal{D}_{n}
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{Spec}(k[S,T]/(S^{p},T^{p^{n}})

where the comultiplication is given by

\overline{S}\mapsto\overline{S}\otimes 1+1\otimes\overline{S}

and

\overline{T}\mapsto\overline{T}\otimes 1+1\otimes\overline{T}+\overline{S}\otimes\overline{T}^{p}.

We observe that, for
p=2
, this is the group that appears in the statement of the Theorem. Indeed the previous group scheme is equally isomorphic to
\operatorname{Spec}k[S,T]/(S^{p},T^{p^{n}})
, where the comultiplication is given by

\overline{S}\mapsto\overline{S}\otimes 1+1\otimes\overline{S}

and

\overline{T}\mapsto\overline{T}\otimes 1+1\otimes\overline{T}+\overline{T}^{p}\otimes\overline{S}.

The two group schemes are isomorphic via
\overline{S}\mapsto\overline{S},\overline{T}\mapsto\overline{T}+\overline{S}\overline{T}^{p}.
We will need this second presentation in the proof of the Theorem.

Moreover
\mathcal{H}_{a,n}
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{Spec}k[T]/(T^{p^{n}})
where the comultiplication is given by

\overline{T}\mapsto\overline{T}\otimes 1+1\otimes\overline{T}+a\overline{T}^{p^{n-1}}\otimes\overline{T}^{p}.

We collect some easy results that will be freely used in the rest of the paper.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p
.

(1)

\mathcal{D}_{0}
is isomorphic to
\alpha_{p,k}
and
\mathcal{D}_{1}
is isomorphic to
\alpha_{p,k}\times\alpha_{p,k}
.

(2)

For any
n\geq 2
,
\mathcal{H}_{0,1}
is the center of
\mathcal{D}_{n}
and
\mathcal{D}_{n}/\mathcal{H}_{0,1}
is isomorphic to
\alpha_{p^{n-1},k}\times\alpha_{p,k}
.

(3)

\mathcal{H}_{a,n}
is commutative if and only if
n\leq 2
or
a=0
.

(4)

\mathcal{H}_{0,n}
is isomorphic to
\alpha_{p^{n},k}
and
\mathcal{H}_{a,1}
is isomorphic to
\alpha_{p,k}
.

(5)

If
a\neq 0
,
\mathcal{H}_{a,2}
is isomorphic to
\alpha_{p^{2},k}
if
p>2
, and to a twisted form of the
2
-torsion of a supersingular elliptic curve if
p=2
.

(6)

If
n\geq l\geq 0
,
\mathcal{D}_{l}
is a closed subgroup scheme of
\mathcal{D}_{n}
, and if
l\geq 1
,
\mathcal{D}_{l}
corresponds to
\ker\operatorname{F}^{l}_{\mathcal{D}_{n}}
.

Proof.

All the proofs, except
(5)
, are straightforward.
If
char(k)>2
,
\mathcal{H}_{a,2}
is isomorphic to
\alpha_{p^{2},k}
via the isomorphism
\overline{T}\mapsto\overline{T}-a\frac{\overline{T}^{2p}}{2}
.
If
char(k)=2
and
k
algebraically closed,
\mathcal{H}_{a,2}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{H}_{1,2}
, via
\overline{T}\mapsto c\overline{T}
where
c
is a cubic root of
a
(see also Lemma
4.6
for more details). And
\mathcal{H}_{1,2}
is isomorphic to
\ker(\operatorname{F}+V:W_{2,k}\to W_{2,k})
, where
W_{2,k}
is the group scheme of Witt vectors of length
2
and
V
is the Verschiebung. This group scheme is known to be isomorphic to the
2
-torsion of a supersingular elliptic curve.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

If
n\geq 3
and
a\neq 0
,
\mathcal{H}_{a,n}
is a subgroup scheme of the nonabelian extension of
\mathbb{G}_{a}
by
\mathbb{G}_{a}
given by the cocycle
aT^{p}T^{\prime p^{n-1}}
(see
[
DG70
, II, §3, 4.6]
).

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
n\geq 0
. Any closed subgroup scheme of
\mathcal{D}_{n}
is equal to
\mathcal{D}_{l}
, with
0\leq l\leq n
,
or to
\mathcal{H}_{a,m}
, for some
a\in k
and
1\leq m\leq n
.

Proof.

The result is clear for
n\leq 1
. So we suppose
n\geq 2
. In particular,
\mathcal{D}_{n}
is not commutative.
Let
H
be a closed subgroup scheme of
\mathcal{D}_{n}
. If
H\subseteq\ker\operatorname{F}^{l}_{\mathcal{D}_{n}}
, for some
0\leq l<n
, then
H
is a closed subgroup scheme of
\mathcal{D}_{l}
. Up to take minimal
0\leq l\leq n
such that
\operatorname{F}^{l}_{H}=0
, we can suppose that
\operatorname{F}^{n-1}_{H}\neq 0
. In particular, if
H
is a proper subgroup of
\mathcal{D}_{n}
,
H
has order
p^{n}
, otherwise iterating
n-1
times the Frobenius, which has a kernel of order at least
p
, we will get the trivial morphism.
Suppose that
H
does not contain the center of
\mathcal{D}_{n}
. Then the natural map
H\times\mathcal{H}_{0,1}\to\mathcal{D}_{n}
is an isomorphism. Henceforth
H\simeq\mathcal{D}_{n}/\mathcal{H}_{0,1}\simeq\alpha_{p^{n-1},k}\times\alpha_{p,k}
, which would imply that
\mathcal{D}_{n}
is commutative. Therefore
H
contains the center of
\mathcal{D}_{n}
. As a consequence,
H/\mathcal{H}_{0,1}
is a closed subgroup scheme of
\mathcal{D}_{n}/\mathcal{H}_{0,1}\simeq\alpha_{p^{n-1},k}\times\alpha_{p,k}
. In particular,
H/\mathcal{H}_{0,1}
is a normal subgroup scheme of
\mathcal{D}_{n}/\mathcal{H}_{0,1}
, which implies that
H
is a normal subgroup scheme of
\mathcal{D}_{n}
.
So
H
is obtained as the kernel of a morphism from
\mathcal{D}_{n}
to
\alpha_{p}
. Any such a morphism is given by an element

P(\overline{S},\overline{T})=\sum_{0\leq i<p,0\leq j<p^{n}}a_{ij}\overline{S}^{i}\overline{T}^{j}\in k[S,T]/(S^{p},T^{p^{n}})

such that
P(\overline{S},\overline{T})^{p}=0
and

(4.1)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i<p\\
0\leq j<p^{n}\end{subarray}}a_{ij}(\overline{S}\otimes 1+1\otimes\overline{S})^{i}(\overline{T}\otimes 1+1\otimes\overline{T}+\overline{T}^{p}\otimes\overline{S})^{j}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i<p\\
0\leq j<p^{n}\end{subarray}}a_{ij}(\overline{S}^{i}\overline{T}^{j}\otimes 1+1\otimes\overline{S}^{i}\overline{T}^{j}).

in
k[S,T]/(S^{p},T^{p^{n}})\otimes k[S,T]/(S^{p},T^{p^{n}}).
We can suppose
a_{00}=0
.
If we reduce modulo
(\overline{T})\otimes(1)
we get

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i<p\\
0\leq j<p^{n}\end{subarray}}a_{ij}(\overline{S}\otimes 1+1\otimes\overline{S})^{i}(1\otimes\overline{T}^{j})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i<p\\
0<j<p^{n}\end{subarray}}a_{ij}(1\otimes\overline{S}^{i}\overline{T}^{j})+\sum_{0<i<p}a_{i0}(1\otimes\overline{S}^{i}+\overline{S}^{i}\otimes 1).

in
k[S,T]/(S^{p},T)\otimes k[S,T]/(S^{p},T^{p^{n}})
. Therefore
a_{ij}=0
if
1<i<p
or
i=1
and
j>0
. So

P(\overline{S},\overline{T})=a_{1,0}\overline{S}+Q(\overline{T}).

If we reduce (
4.1
) modulo
(1)\otimes(\overline{S})
we find that
Q(\overline{T})
should be additive. Since
P(\overline{S},\overline{T})^{p}=0
we get that
P(\overline{S},\overline{T})=a_{10}\overline{S}+a_{0p^{n-1}}\overline{T}^{p^{n-1}}
. Moreover,
a_{10}\neq 0
since we supposed that
\operatorname{F}^{n-1}_{H}\neq 0
.
So we have that
H
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{H}_{a,n}
, with
a=\frac{a_{0p^{n-1}}}{a_{10}}
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
G
be an infinitesimal unipotent group scheme with unidimensional Lie algebra over a field of characteristic
p
. An action, as an automorphism of groups, of an infinitesimal group scheme
H
of multiplicative type over
G
is faithful if and only if the induced action on
\ker\operatorname{F}_{G}
is faithful. And, if it happens,
H
has unidimensional Lie algebra.

Proof.

The ’if’ part is obvious. We prove the ’only if’ part.
We can suppose that
H
is of height
1
since the kernel of any action of an infinitesimal group scheme has a nontrivial intersection with the kernel of the Frobenius (see also
[
Gou24
, Proposition 6.1]
). We also remark that
\dim_{k}\Lie(H)=\dim_{k}\Lie(\ker{\operatorname{F}_{H}})
.
Therefore, by
[
DG70
, III,§6, Propoition 7.1]
,
H
is a subgroup scheme of
\mathcal{A}ut_{1}(G)=\ker(\mathcal{A}ut(G)\to\mathcal{A}ut(G/\ker\operatorname{F}_{G}))
.
Moreover,
\ker\operatorname{F}_{G}\simeq\alpha_{p}
is contained in the center since a unipotent group scheme has a nontrivial center, and, since
G
is infinitesimal unipotent, the intersection with the kernel of the Frobenius is nonempty.
Therefore the induced action of
G/\ker F_{G}
over
\ker F_{G}
is trivial, then, by
[
DG70
, III,§6, Proposition 7.4]
, we have an exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{H}om_{gr}(G/\ker\operatorname{F}_{G},\alpha_{p})\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}ut_{1}(G)\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}ut_{gr}(\ker\operatorname{F}_{G})\simeq\mathbb{G}_{m,k}.

But we remark that
\mathcal{H}om_{gr}(\alpha_{p},\alpha_{p})\simeq\mathbb{G}_{a,k}
, so, by dévissage, we get that
\mathcal{H}om_{gr}(G/\ker\operatorname{F}_{G},\alpha_{p})
is an unipotent group scheme.
Since
H
is of multiplicative type, if
H
acts faithfully on
G
then it acts faithfully on
\ker\operatorname{F}_{G}
. Therefore
H
is isomorphic to
\mu_{p,k}
and so its Lie algebra is unidimensional.

∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p
and
let
n>2
or
n=2
and
p=2
.

(1)

Any action of
\mu_{p,k}
over
\mathcal{H}_{0,n}\simeq\alpha_{p^{n},k}
, as automorphism of groups, is conjugate to
v\cdot t=v^{i}t
, for some
0\leq i\leq p-1
.
Therefore, for any nontrivial action of
\mu_{p,k}
over
\alpha_{p^{n},k}
, all semi-direct products
\alpha_{p^{n},k}\rtimes\mu_{p}
are isomorphic.

(2)

If
a,b\in k\setminus\{0\}
, then
\mathcal{H}_{a,n}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{H}_{b,n}
if and only if
b/a
is a
(p^{n}+p-1)
-th power.

(3)

There are no nontrivial actions, as automorphism of groups, of infinitesimal group schemes of multiplicative type over
\mathcal{H}_{a,n}
, for any
a\in k\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof.

(1)
. It is easy to see that the map
\mathcal{A}ut_{gr}(\mathbb{G}_{a})\to\mathcal{A}ut_{gr}(\alpha_{p^{n}})
admits a section. Therefore any action of
\mu_{p,k}
over
\alpha_{p^{n},k}
extends to an action over
\mathbb{G}_{a,k}
. Now, by
[
DG70
, III, §6, Corollaire 7.9]
, we have that any action of
\mu_{p,k}
on
\mathbb{G}_{a,k}
is given by

v\cdot x=v^{i}x+(v^{i}-1)\sum_{l=1}^{s}a_{l}x^{p^{l}}

for some

,
0\leq i\leq p-1
and
a_{i}\in k
for any
1\leq l\leq s
. Therefore any action of
\mu_{p,k}
on
\alpha_{p^{n},k}
is given by

v\cdot x=v^{i}x+(v^{i}-1)\sum_{l=1}^{p^{n}-1}a_{l}x^{p^{l}}

for some
a_{i}\in k
for any
1\leq l\leq p^{n-1}-1
and
0\leq i\leq p-1
. But this action is conjugated to the action
v\cdot x=v^{i}x
via the automorphism
x\mapsto x+\sum_{l=1}^{p^{n}-1}a_{l}x^{p^{l}}
. If the action is nontrivial, then
i>0
. Moreover,
v\mapsto v^{i}
is an automorphism of
\mu_{p,k}
, therefore all the associated semi-direct products are isomorphic.

(2),(3)
. We recall that the Hopf algebra of
\mathcal{H}_{a,n}
is isomorphic to
k[T]/(T^{p^{n}})
, where the comultiplication is given by
\overline{T}\otimes 1+1\otimes\overline{T}+a\overline{T}^{p^{n-1}}\otimes\overline{T}^{p}.
We now consider an element of
\mathcal{I}som(\mathcal{H}_{a,n},\mathcal{H}_{b,n})(R)
, with
R
a
k
-algebra.
An isomorphism from
\mathcal{H}_{a,n,R}
to
\mathcal{H}_{b,n,R}
is given by an element
P(\overline{T})=\sum_{i=1}^{p^{n}-1}a_{i}\overline{T}^{i}\in R[T]/(T^{p^{n}})
such that
a_{1}\in R
is invertible and

\sum_{i=1}^{p^{n}-1}a_{i}(\overline{T}^{i}\otimes 1+1\otimes\overline{T}^{i})+a(\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}a_{i}^{p^{n}}\overline{T}^{ip^{n-1}})\otimes(\sum_{i=1}^{p^{n-2}}a_{i}^{p}\overline{T}^{ip})=\sum_{i=1}^{p^{n}-1}a_{i}(\overline{T}\otimes 1+1\otimes\overline{T}+b\overline{T}^{p^{n-1}}\otimes\overline{T}^{p})^{i}.

Since it induces an isomorphism on the kernels of the
p^{n-1}
-th power of the Frobenius, which are isomorphic to
\alpha_{p^{n},R}
, we get that
a_{i}=0

if
1<i<p^{n-1}
and

i
is not a power of
p
.
Moreover,
a_{i}=0
if
i>p^{n-1}
and
i
is not divisible by
p
(you can see it deriving both sides).
So we get

\sum_{r=p^{n-2}+1}^{p^{n-1}-1}a_{pr}(\overline{T}^{pr}\otimes 1+1\otimes\overline{T}^{{pr}})+aa_{1}^{p^{n-1}}\overline{T}^{p^{n-1}}\otimes(\sum_{r=0}^{n-2}a_{p^{r}}^{p}\overline{T}^{p^{r+1}})=

\sum_{r=p^{n-2}+1}^{p^{n-1}-1}a_{pr}{(\overline{T}^{p}\otimes 1+1\otimes\overline{T}^{p})}^{r}+a_{1}b\overline{T}^{p^{n-1}}\otimes\overline{T}^{p}

If
n>2
and
a\neq 0
, comparing the coefficients of
\overline{T}^{p}\otimes\overline{T}^{p^{n-1}}
, we get
a_{p^{n-1}+p}=0
and, comparing the coefficients of
\overline{T}^{p^{n-1}}\otimes\overline{T}^{p}
,
we get
aa_{1}^{p^{n-1}+p}=a_{1}b
, which means that
a_{1}
is a
(p^{n-1}+p-1)
-th power of
b/a
.
If
n=2
and
p=2
the above equality reduces to

aa_{1}^{2+2}\overline{T}^{p}\otimes\overline{T}^{p}=a_{1}b\overline{T}^{p}\otimes\overline{T}^{p},

so
a_{1}
is a cubic root of
b/a
. If
R=k
this proves that
\mathcal{H}_{a,n}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{H}_{b,n}
if and only if
b/a
is a
(p^{n}+p-1)
-th power.
But this also proves that, if
a\neq 0
, the image of the map
\mathcal{A}ut(\mathcal{H}_{a,n})\to\mathcal{A}ut(\ker\operatorname{F}_{\mathcal{H}_{a,n}})
is contained in
\mu_{p^{n-1}+p-1,k}
.
This implies, by the previous Lemma, that any infinitesimal group scheme of multiplicative type acts trivially on
\mathcal{H}_{a,n}
if
a\neq 0
.
On the other hand, if
a_{1}\in k
is such that
a_{1}^{p^{n}+p-1}=b/a
then the polynomial
P(\overline{T})=a_{1}\overline{T}
gives an isomorphism for any
n\geq 1
and any prime
p
.

∎

5.
Proof of the Theorem

We start with a Lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
2
and
G
be a unipotent subgroup scheme of
\operatorname{PGL}_{2,k}
. Then

(1)

\ker\operatorname{F}_{G}
is isomorphic to
\alpha_{2,k}
or to
\alpha_{2,k}\times\alpha_{2,k}
;

(2)

\IM\operatorname{F}_{G}
is isomorphic to
\alpha_{2^{n},k}
, for some
n
.

Proof.

(1)

We observe that in characteristic
2
we have the following exact sequence of restricted
p
-algebras

0\to\Lie(\mathbb{G}_{a}^{2})\to\mathfrak{pgl}_{2}\to\Lie(\mathbb{G}_{m})\to 0.

Therefore
\Lie(\ker\operatorname{F}_{G})
is necessarily contained in
\Lie(\mathbb{G}_{a}^{2})
, which implies that

\ker F_{G}
is a subgroup scheme of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{2}
. So we are done.

(2)

Let
G
be a unipotent subgroup scheme of
\operatorname{PGL}_{2,k}
and let
\tilde{G}:=\pi^{-1}G
, where
\pi:\operatorname{SL}_{2,k}\to\operatorname{PGL}_{2,k}
is the projection. Then
we have an exact sequence

1\to\mu_{2,k}\to\tilde{G}\to G\to 0

which yields the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mu_{2,k}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{F}_{\mu_{2,k}}}
\textstyle{\tilde{G}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{F}_{\tilde{G}}}
\textstyle{G\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{0}

Since the Frobenius is trivial on

Since

Proposition 6.1

Deligne-Lusztig varieties and basic EKOR strata
Haining Wang

Abstract
. Under the axiom of He and Rapoport for the stratifications of Shimura varieties, we explain a result of Görtz, He and Nie that the EKOR strata contained in the basic loci can be described as a disjoint union of Deligne-Lusztig varieties. In the special case of Siegel modular varieties, we compare their descriptions to that of Görtz and Yu for the supersingular Kottwitz-Rapoport strata and to the descriptions of Harashita, Hoeve for the supersingular Ekedahl-Oort strata.

Primary 14G35, Secondary 11G18

Introduction

Let
\mathcal{A}_{g}
be the moduli space of principally polarized abelian varieties over
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. There are two interesting stratifications of this moduli space. The first one is the
Newton stratification
which stratify the space according to the Newton polygons of the abelian varieties. The other one is the
Ekedahl-Oort stratification
which stratify the space according to the isomorphism classes of the
p
-torsion subgroups of the abelian varieties. One of the most interesting strata in the Newton stratification is the basic stratum or the supersingular stratum. It is a classical question how the Ekedahl-Oort stratification interacts with the Newton stratification. In particular, one can ask how the Ekedahl-Oort stratification meets the basic Newton stratum. This question was answered in
[
Har10
]
by Harashita where he showed that certain unions of the supersingular Ekedahl-Oort strata are isomorphic to Deligne-Lusztig varieties. This result is refined by Hoeve in
[
Hoe10
]
and he showed that each individual supersingular Ekedahl-Oort stratum is isomorphic to a fine Deligne-Lusztig variety. For general Shimura varieties that have good integral model and stratification theory, one can still formulate the same questions. Vollaard and Wedhorn
[
Vo10
,
VW11
]
studied the case of unitary Shimura varieties of signature
(1,n-1)
at an innert prime and answered this question. Note in this case, the basic Newton stratum is very special in the sense that it can be covered entirely by Ekedahl-Oort strata and again each Ekedahl-Oort stratum is a union of Deligne-Lusztig varieties. Those Shimura varieties that satisfy the similar special properties have been classified by the work of Görtz, He and Nie, see
[
GH15
,
GHN19
]
. They termed these special Shimura varieties as the fully Hodge-Newton decomposable Shimura varieties. In fact they not only treated the case of Ekedahl-Oort stratifications on Shimura varieties with good reductions but also the general cases of Shimura varieties with parahoric level structures. Note in the case of Shimura varieties with parahoric level structures, one has to replace the Ekedahl-Oort strata by the so called EKOR (Ekedahl-Oort-Kottwitz-Rapoport) strata. This notion is introduced by He and Rapoport in
[
HR17
]
where they axiomatized the theory of stratifications on general Shimura varieties with parahoric level structures. This notion interpolates the Ekedahl-Oort stratification and the so-called Kottwitz-Rapoport stratification. The EKOR strata have been studied extensively in the recent preprint
[
SYZ19
]
in the cases of Hodge type and abelian type Shimura varieties. The Kottwitz-Rapoport stratification was first studied by Ngô and Genestier in the case of Siegel modular varieties with Iwahori level structure,
[
GN02
]
. It can be considered as a stratification by singularities as the semisimple traces of Frobenius on the nearby cycle sheaf is constant on each stratum. In
[
GY10
,
GY12
]
, Görtz and Yu studied those Kottwitz-Rapoport strata contained in the basic Newton strata on the Siegel modular varieties with Iwahori level structures. They showed again that those Kottwitz-Rapoport strata can be described as a union of Deligne-Lusztig varieties. Finally, we remark that the appearances of Deligne-Lusztig varieties in the basic Newton strata are not only beautiful in its own right but are also important in arithmetic applications. Notably, they play an important role in the study of arithmetic fundamental lemma
[
RTZ13
,
HLZ19
]
and they are also crucial in establishing geometric versions of Jacquet-Langlands correspondence
[
HTX17
,
XZ17
]
.

The first aim of the present note is to present a result of Görtz, He and Nie that generalizes the aforementioned results of Görtz and Yu as well as the results of Harashita and Hoeve. We formulate their results in the setting of Shimura varieties that satisfy the He-Rapoport axiom. Here we only state an informal version and the details can found in the proof of Theorem
4.7
.

Theorem 1

(Görtz-He-Nie)
.

Every EKOR stratum that is contained in the basic Newton stratum can be written as a disjoint union of classical Deligne-Lusztig varieties.

This theorem is not stated anywhere in the literature but can be deduced from
[
GH15
,
GHN19
]
. Here we give a quite self-contained presentation of this result. We also make an effort to bring this result in the frame work of He-Rapoport by introducing an additional axiom incorporating the Langlands-Rapoport conjecture, see axiom
4.1
. We hope that our presentation will be useful to workers in this trade, especially those who are not so familiar with
[
GH15
,
GHN19
]
. The salient feature of the above theorem is that once the group theoretic data giving rise to the Shimura varieties and their stratifications are at hand, the descriptions of the basic EKOR strata can be made explicit without reference to the Shimura varieties themselves. This is especially valuable when the Shimura variety does not admits a good moduli interpretation. We make the above descriptions of the EKOR strata explicit in the case of Siegel modular varieties both in the case of hyperspecial level and in the case of Iwahori level. In particular this allows us to compare the descriptions of the basic EKOR strata by Görtz-He-Nie with those obtained by Görtz-Yu and by Harashita, Hoeve.

Theorem 2
.

The descriptions of the basic EKOR strata of Görtz-He-Nie for Siegel modular varieties agree with

(1)

the descriptions of Görtz-Yu for the basic KR strata;

(2)

the decriptions of Hoeve for the basic Ekedahl-Oort strata.

The descriptions of the KR strata for Siegel modular varieties with Iwahori level will be discussed in §5.2 and the proof of
(1)
of the theorem above can be found in Theorem
5.4
. The descriptions of the Ekedahl-Oort strata for the Siegel modular varieties with hyperspecial level will be discussed in §5.3 and
(2)
is proved in Theorem
5.8
.

When there is no harm of confusion we will use the same symbol

]
gives us a bijection between

and the set of
\sigma
-
\breve{K}
-conjugacy classes of
\breve{K}_{1}\backslash G(\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})/\breve{K}_{1}
and author denotes this bijection by

(2.6)

tr:G(\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})/\breve{K}_{\sigma}(\breve{K}_{1}\times\breve{K}_{1})\rightarrow\mathcal{T}.

Now consider the map
\nu_{K}
in this setting, as we have known the image is contained in

\bigsqcup_{w\in\Adm(\{\mu\})^{K}\cap{{}^{K}\tilde{W}}}\breve{K}_{\sigma}(\breve{K}_{1}w\breve{K}_{1})/\breve{K}_{\sigma}(\breve{K}_{1}\times\breve{K}_{1})

and by the definition of

(

From the above discussions, we see that the EKOR stratification indeed interpolates the KR stratification and the Ekedahl-Oort stratification.

3.
Affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties

3.1.
Deligne-Lusztig varieties

Before we discuss affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties, we first introduce classical Deligne-Lusztig varieties. In this subsection, we will abuse the notations and denote by
G
a connected reductive algebraic group over
\mathbb{F}
which is an algebraic closure of a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
q
a power of
p
. Let
B
be a Borel subgroup of
G
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with a Levi decomposition
B=TU
which is defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
W=N_{T}(\mathbb{F})/T(\mathbb{F})
be the associated Weyl group with an action of
\sigma
. We denote by
\mathbb{S}
the set of simple reflections generating
W
. Let
w\in W
and we denote by
\mathrm{supp}(w)
the support of
w
which is the set of simple reflections that occur in some reduced expression of
w
. An element
w\in W
is called a
\sigma
-
Coxeter
element if
w
is a product of simple reflections, each of which belongs to a unique
\sigma
-orbit of
\mathbb{S}
.

Recall we have the Bruhat decomposition

G=\bigsqcup_{w\in W}BwB.

Then the
Deligne-Lusztig variety
associated to
w\in W
is defined to be

Y(w)=\{gB\in G/B:g^{-1}\sigma(g)\in BwB/B\}.

Here
\sigma
is the Frobenius acting on
G
and
X(w)
is considered as a locally closed subvariety of the flag variety
G/B
.

Let
J\subset\mathbb{S}
. Let
W_{J}
be the corresponding parabolic subgroup of
W
and
P_{J}
be corresponding parabolic subgroup of
G
. Recall that
{{}^{J}W}
is the set of minimal length representatives of
W_{J}\backslash W
. For any
w\in{{}^{J}W}
, we set

Y_{J}(w)=\{gP_{J}:g^{-1}\sigma(g)\in{P_{J}}_{\sigma}(BwB)\}.

This is called the
fine Deligne-Lusztig variety
.

3.2.
Affine Deligne-Lusztig varities

Now we move back to the set-up in §
1.1
. In particular, we assume for simplicity that
G
is a connected reductive group over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Let
b\in G(\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
be a representative of the
\sigma
-conjugacy class
[b]
. Let
K\subset\tilde{\mathbb{S}}
and
\breve{K}\subset G(\breve{\mathbb{Q}})
be the corresponding parahoric subgroup. Then the
affine Deligne Lusztig variety
is given by the set

X_{w}(b)_{K}=\{g\breve{K}:g^{-1}b\sigma(g)\in\breve{K}w\breve{K}\}.

This the
\mathbb{F}
-points of a locally closed subscheme of the
p
-adic partial affine flag variety
G(\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})/\breve{K}
,
[
BS17
,
Zhu17
]
. Note that the group of
\sigma
-centralizer

(3.1)

J_{b}=\{g\in G(\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}):g^{-1}b\sigma(g)=b\}

acts naturally on
X_{w}(b)_{K}
.

It is natural to consider the following variant of the above set which we will call the
generalized affine Deligne-Lusztig variety

X(\mu,b)_{K}=\{g\breve{K}:g^{-1}b\sigma(g)\in\bigcup_{w\in\Adm^{K}(\{\mu\})}\breve{K}w\breve{K}\}.

It is obvious we have a natural decomposition

(3.2)

X(\mu,b)_{K}=\bigcup_{w\in\Adm^{K}(\{\mu\})}X_{w}(b)_{K}.

We also need the notion of a
fine Deligne-Lusztig variety
. Let
w\in{{}^{K}\tilde{W}}
, we define

X_{K,w}(b)=\{g\breve{K}:g^{-1}b\sigma(g)\in\breve{K}_{\sigma}(\breve{I}w\breve{I})\}.

The decomposition in (
3.2
) can be made finer as in
[
GH15
, 3.4]
and we have

X(\mu,b)_{K}=\bigsqcup_{w\in{{}^{K}\tilde{W}}\cap\Adm^{K}(\{\mu\})}X_{K,w}(b).

In light of the EKOR stratifications of the Shimura varieties introduced in (
2.4
) and relation between the generalized affine Deligne-Lusztig variety and the Rapoport-Zink space that we will discuss later. We will refer to this decomposition of the generalized Deligne-Lusztig variety as the EKOR stratification of
X(\mu,b)_{K}
. The affine Deligne-Lusztig variety shows up in the stratification of Shimura varieties in the following explicit way,
[
GHN19
, 6.2]
. We will restrict the map
\gamma_{K}
to the Newton stratum
S_{K,[b]}=\delta^{-1}_{K}([b])
and obtain

(3.3)

\gamma_{K}(S_{k,[b]})=d^{-1}_{K}([b])\cap l^{-1}_{K}(\Adm^{K}(\{\mu\}))=J_{b}\backslash X(\mu,b)_{K}.

4.
The basic EKOR strata

4.1.
Basic Newton Stratum

Recall the Langlands-Rapoport conjecture
[
LR87
,
Rap05
]
asserts the following decomposition of the point of
Sh_{K}
valued in
\mathbb{F}

(4.1)

\bigsqcup_{\phi}I_{\phi}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash X_{p}(\phi)\times X^{p}(\phi)/K^{p}\xrightarrow{\cong}Sh_{K}(\mathbb{F}).

We will not recall the general form for this conjecture. But if the Shimura variety is a moduli space of abelian varieties, then
\phi
is supposed to run through the isogeny classes on the moduli space. The space
X_{p}(\phi)
is supposed to correspond to the part coming from the
p
-power isogenies and
X^{p}(\phi)
should correspond to the prime to
p
-part of the isogenies. In this case
I_{\phi}
is the algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}
corresponding to the automorphism group of an abelian variety in the isogeny class. Furthermore
X_{p}(\phi)
should be given by a suitable affine Deligne-Lusztig variety. Recall that we have a fixed element
\tau
in the basic class in
B(G,\mu)
. We consider now the associated basic Newton stratum
S_{K,[\tau]}
. In light of the above discussion on the Langlands-Rapoport conjecture, we postulate the following axiom in this note.

Axiom 4.1
.

The basic Newton stratum is uniformized by the affine Deligne-Lusztig variety under a uniformization map

(4.2)

\iota_{K}:X(\mu,\tau)_{K}\rightarrow S_{K,[\tau]}

which induces an isomorphism of the form

(4.3)

I_{\tau}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash X(\mu,\tau)_{K}\times X^{p}(\tau)/K^{p}\xrightarrow{\cong}S_{K,[\tau]}

where
X^{p}(\tau)
is a
G(\mathbb{A}^{p}_{f})
-torsor and
I_{\tau}
is an algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}
that acts on
X(\mu,\tau)_{K}
through a map
I_{\tau}(\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow J_{\tau}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. In addition, we require that the the composite of the map

\gamma_{K}\circ\iota_{K}:X(\mu,\tau)_{K}\rightarrow S_{K,[\tau]}\rightarrow J_{\tau}\backslash X(\mu,\tau)_{K}

is the natural map.

In the case for the Hodge type Shimura varieties, the uniformization map is constructed in
[
Kis17
]
in the unramified case and in
[
Zhou19
]
for the parahoric case. Another way to postulate this is to require that there is an isomorphism of perfect schemes

\mathcal{M}(G,\mu,\tau)^{per}_{K}\cong X(\mu,\tau)_{K}

where
\mathcal{M}(G,\mu,b)^{per}_{K}
is the perfection of the local Shimura variety (Rapoport-Zink space)
[
RV14
]
that could be used to uniformize the basic stratum in an analogues way as in (
4.3
), see
[
RV14
, (5.4)]
.

4.2.
The basic EKOR stratum

From here on, we will be concerned with the
basic EKOR strata
. In other words, we will be concerned with those
w\in\Adm(\{\mu\})\cap{{}^{K}\tilde{W}}
such that

EKOR_{K,w}\subset S_{K,[\tau]}.

For
w\in W_{a}
, we denote by
\mathrm{supp}(w)
the support of
w
, the set of
s_{i}\in\tilde{\mathbb{S}}
that appears in some reduced expression of
w
. We also define

\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w\tau)=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(\tau\sigma)^{n}(\mathrm{supp}(w)).

We have the following numerical criterion to characterize the basic EKOR strata.

Proposition 4.2

(
[
GHN19
, Proposition 5.6]
)
.

Let
w\in\Adm(\{\mu\})\cap{{}^{K}\tilde{W}}
, then
EKOR_{K,w}\subset S_{K,[\tau]}
if and only if
W_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)}
is finite.

Proof.

Note that
EKOR_{K,w}\subset S_{K,[\tau]}
if and only if
\breve{K}_{\sigma}(\breve{I}w\breve{I})\subset[\tau]
. By
[
GHN19
, Proposition 5.6]
, this happens if and only if
W_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)}
is finite and
\kappa_{\tilde{W}}(w)=\kappa_{G}(\tau)
. Since
w\in\Adm(\{\mu\})
, it follows that
w\leq t^{w_{0}(\mu)}
for some finite Weyl group element
w_{0}
and
\kappa_{\tilde{W}}(w)=\kappa_{\tilde{W}}(t^{w_{0}(\mu)})=\kappa_{\tilde{W}}(w_{0}t^{\mu}w^{-1}_{0})=\kappa_{G}(\tau)
.
∎

Let
w\in\tilde{W}
and
K\subset\tilde{\mathbb{S}}
. We write
\mathrm{Ad}(w)\sigma(K)=K
if for any
s_{k}\in K
there exists
s_{k^{\prime}}\in K
with
w\sigma(s_{k})w^{-1}=s_{k^{\prime}}
. The set of
\{K^{\prime}\subset K:\mathrm{Ad}(w)\sigma(K^{\prime})\subset K^{\prime}\}
contains a unique maximal element
[
GH15
, 3.1]
which we denote by

(4.4)

I(K,w,\sigma)=\mathrm{max}\{K^{\prime}\subset K:\mathrm{Ad}(w)\sigma(K^{\prime})\subset K^{\prime}\}.

Proposition 4.3

(
[
GHN19
, Proposition 5.7]
)
.

Let
K
be a
\sigma
-invariant subset of
\tilde{\mathbb{S}}
and
w\in{{}^{K}\tilde{W}}
. If
W_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)}
is finite, then
W_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)\cup I(K,w,\sigma)}
is also finite.

Let
w\in\Adm(\{\mu\})\cap{{}^{K}\tilde{W}}
be an element such that
\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)
is finite. Then
EKOR_{K,w}
and the basic Newton stratum
S_{K,[\tau]}
fits in the following diagram under the uniformizatiom map introduced in (
4.3
)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{K,w}(\tau)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces EKOR_{K,w}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J_{\tau}\backslash X_{K,w}(\tau)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X(\mu,\tau)_{K}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S_{K,[\tau]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J_{\tau}\backslash X(\mu,\tau)_{K}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{K}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{K}}

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
w\in\Adm(\{\mu\})\cap{{}^{K}\tilde{W}}
. The basic EKOR stratum
EKOR_{K,w}
can be uniformized by the fine Deligne-Lusztig variety
X_{K,w}(\tau)
in the sense of Axiom
4.1
that is

I_{\tau}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash X_{K,w}(\tau)\times X^{p}(\tau)/K^{p}\xrightarrow{\cong}EKOR_{K,w}.

Using the above corollary, we can restrict our attention to the fine affine Deligne-Lusztig variety
X_{K,w}(\tau)
in order to study the basic EKOR stratum
EKOR_{K,w}
. We recall the following results of Görtz and He
[
GH15
, Proposition 4.1.1, Theorem 4.1.2]
concerning the decompositions of fine affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties into fine Deligne-Lusztig varieties.

Theorem 4.5

(
[
GH15
, Theorem 4.1.1]
)
.

For
J\subset\tilde{\mathbb{S}}
and
w\in{{}^{J}\tilde{W}}
,

X_{J,w}(b)=\{g\breve{K}_{I(J,w,\sigma)}:g^{-1}b\sigma(g)\in\breve{K}_{I(J,w,\sigma)}w\breve{K}_{\sigma(I(J,w,\sigma))}\}.

Proposition 4.6

(
[
GH15
, Proposition 4.1.2]
)
.

Let
J\subset\tilde{\mathbb{S}}
and
w\in{{}^{J}\tilde{W}^{\sigma(J)}}\cap W_{a}\tau
such that
Ad(w)\sigma(J)\subset J
. If
W_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)\cup J}
is finite, then

\{g\breve{K}_{J}:g^{-1}\tau\sigma(g)\in\breve{K}_{J}w\breve{K}_{\sigma(J)}\}=\bigsqcup_{i\in J_{\tau}/J_{\tau}\cap\breve{K}_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)\cup J}}iY_{J}(w)

where
Y_{J}(w)=\{g\breve{K}_{J}\in\breve{K}_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)\cup J}/\breve{K}_{J}:g^{-1}\tau\sigma(g)\in\breve{K}_{J}w\breve{K}_{\sigma(J)}\}
is a classical Deligne-Lusztig variety.

Theorem 4.7

(Görtz-He-Nie)
.

Every basic EKOR stratum on
Sh_{K}
can be written as a disjoint union of classical Deligne-Lusztig varieties.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.5
,

X_{K,w}(\tau)=\{g\breve{K}_{I(K,w,\sigma)}:g^{-1}\tau\sigma(g)\in\breve{K}_{I(K,w,\sigma)}w\breve{K}_{\sigma(I(K,w,\sigma))}\}

where
I(K,w,\sigma)
is defined as in (
4.4
) and in particular
Ad(w)\sigma(I(K,w,\sigma))\subset I(K,w,\sigma)
. It follows from Proposition
4.6
that we have

\begin{split}X_{K,w}(\tau)=&\{g\breve{K}_{I(K,w,\sigma)}:g^{-1}\tau\sigma(g)\in\breve{K}_{I(K,w,\sigma)}w\breve{K}_{\sigma(I(K,w,\sigma))}\}\\
&=\bigsqcup_{i\in J_{\tau}/J_{\tau}\cap\breve{K}_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)\cup I(K,w,\sigma)}}iY_{I(K,w,\sigma)}(w).\\
\end{split}

∎

Remark 4.8
.

In the case
w
is a
\sigma
-Coxeter element in the finite group
W_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)}
, the Deligne-Lusztig variety
Y_{I(K,w,\sigma)}(w)
is isomorphic to the following fine Deligne-Lusztig variety by
[
GH15
, Corollary 4.6.2]

Y_{I(K,w,\sigma)}(w)=\{g\breve{K}_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)\cap K}\in\breve{K}_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)}/\breve{K}_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)\cap K}:g^{-1}\tau\sigma(g)\in{\breve{K}_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)\cap K\sigma}}\breve{I}w\breve{I}\}

and
Y_{I(K,w,\sigma)}(w)
is in turn isomorphic to the classical Deligne-Lusztig vareity

\{g\breve{I}\in\breve{K}_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)}/\breve{I}:g^{-1}\tau\sigma(g)\in\breve{I}w\breve{I}\}.

5.
Basic EKOR strata on Siegel modular varieties

5.1.
Siegel modular varieties

We first review the Siegel moduli spaces of abelian varieties with parahoric level structures. The algebraic group associated to this Shimura variety is
G=\mathrm{GSp}_{2g}
the group of symplectic similitudes. We consider the dominant miniscule coweight
\mu=(1^{(g)},0^{(g)})
as in Example

is generated by the simple reflections given by

The cocharacter group of

X_{*}(T)=\{(x_{1},\cdots,x_{2g})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2g}:x_{1}+x_{2g}=x_{2}+x_{2g-1}=\cdots=x_{g}+x_{g+1}\}.

The set of simple reflections

in the affine Weyl group is

We will use the notation

\eta

-dimensional abelian varieties. For simplicity, we will concentrate on these two cases in the following.

the Weyl group generated by the simple reflections in
\tilde{\mathbb{S}}-\{c,g-c\}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
w\in\Adm(\{\mu\})
. The Weyl group
W_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)}
is finite if and only if
w\in W_{\{c,g-c\}}\tau
for some
c\leq g/2
.

Proof.

One direction is clear that is if
w\in W_{\{c,g-c\}}\tau
, then
W_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)}
is obviously finite. On the other hand, if
W_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)}
is finite, then there is some
s_{c}
that is not in
\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)
. It follows then that
s_{g-c}\not\in\mathrm{supp}(w\tau^{-1})
. Therefore
w\in W_{\{c,g-c\}}\tau
.
∎

In
[
GY10
]
, the authors call a KR stratum
\mathcal{A}_{I,w}

superspecial
if there is an
0\leq c\leq[g/2]
such that for all
(A_{j},\eta)_{j\in I}\in\mathcal{A}_{I,w}
, the abelian varieites
A_{c}
and
A_{g-c}
are superspecial and that the isogeny
A_{c}\rightarrow A^{\vee}_{g-c}
is the Frobenius map. By
[
GY10
, Proposition 4.4]
, the KR stratum
\mathcal{A}_{I,w}
associated to
w
is superspecial if and only if
w\in W_{\{c,g-c\}}\tau
for some
0\leq c\leq[g/2]
. Therefore we have recovered the following theorem of Görtz and Yu.

Theorem 5.2

(
[
GY10
, Theorem 1.4]
)
.

All basic KR strata are superspecial.

Next we explain how to describe the basic KR stratum
\mathcal{A}_{I,w}
in terms of a disjoint union of Deligne-Lusztig varieties as in Theorem
4.7
. Note that the axioms of He-Rapoport is verified in
[
HR17
, §7]
and the axiom
4.3
is verified using Rapoport-Zink uniformization
[
RZ96
, Theorem 6.1]
. Therefore Corollary
4.4
takes the following form

I_{\tau}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash X_{I,w}(\tau)\times G(\mathbb{A}^{p}_{f})/K^{p}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{A}_{I,w}

where
I_{\tau}
is an inner form of
\mathrm{GSp}_{2g}
and

X_{I,w}(\tau)=\bigsqcup_{i\in J_{\tau}/J_{\tau}\cap\breve{K}_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)}}iY_{\sigma}(w)

where
Y_{\sigma}(w)=\{g\breve{I}\in\breve{K}_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)}/\breve{I}:g^{-1}\tau\sigma(g)\in\breve{I}w\breve{I}\}.

Let
J\subset I
be a
\tau\sigma
-stable subset. We make the following manipulation, since
\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)\subset J

\begin{split}X_{I,w}(\tau)&=\bigsqcup_{i\in J_{\tau}/J_{\tau}\cap\breve{K}_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)}}iY_{\sigma}(w)\\
&=\bigsqcup_{i\in J_{\tau}/J_{\tau}\cap\breve{K}_{J}}i\bigsqcup_{j\in J_{\tau}\cap\breve{K}_{J}/J_{\tau}\cap\breve{K}_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)}}jY_{\sigma}(w)\\
&=\bigsqcup_{i\in J_{\tau}/J_{\tau}\cap\breve{K}_{J}}iY(J,w)\\
\end{split}

where
Y(J,w)=\{g\breve{I}\in\breve{K}_{J}/\breve{I}:g^{-1}\tau\sigma(g)\in\breve{I}w\breve{I}\}
. Consider the stratum
\mathcal{A}_{J,\tau}
on
\mathcal{A}_{J}
. This is a finite set of points known as the
minimal EKOR stratum
. In fact we have the following lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

\mathcal{A}_{J,\tau}=I_{\tau}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash(J_{\tau}/J_{\tau}\cap\breve{K}_{J})\times G(\mathbb{A}^{p}_{f})/K^{p}.

Proof.

By Corollary
4.4
, we have

I_{\tau}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash X_{K_{J},\tau}(\tau)\times G(\mathbb{A}^{p}_{f})/K^{p}\xrightarrow{\cong}A_{J,\tau}.

Then we need to show that
(J_{\tau}/J_{\tau}\cap\breve{K}_{J})\cong X_{K_{J},\tau}(\tau)
. In the case
\breve{K}_{J}=\breve{I}
, this is shown in
[
GH15
, Theorem 2.1.1]
. The general case can be deduced from this by using the fact that
\mathcal{A}_{I,\tau}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{J,\tau}
is finite étale as shown in
[
SYZ19
]
. See also
[
GY10
, Proposition 6.1]
.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

The basic KR stratum
\mathcal{A}_{I,w}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{A}_{J,\tau}\times Y(J,w)
.

Proof.

This follows by summarizing the above discussions.
∎

Comparing this theorem with
[
GY10
, Corollary 6.5]
shows that the description of the basic KR stratum in Theorem
4.7
agrees with the description by Görtz and Yu in
[
GY10
, Corollary 6.5]
. Note that contravariant Dieudonné module is used in
[
GY10
]
and we are using covariant Dieudonné module here. This explains why we have
Y_{J}(w)
instead of
X(w^{-1})
in
[
GY10
, Corollary 6.5]
.

5.3.
Basic EO stratum

Consider the isomorphism class of the
p
-torsion subgroup
A[p]

together with its additional structures coming from a point
(A,\eta)
on
\mathcal{A}_{\{0\}}=\mathcal{A}_{g}
.
After passing to its Dieudonné module, we have a map

(5.1)

\nu_{K}:\mathcal{A}_{g}\rightarrow G(\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})/\breve{K}_{\sigma}(\breve{K}_{1}\times\breve{K}_{1}).

Let
{}^{g}W
be the minimal length representatives of
S_{g}\backslash W_{g}
. As explained in Example
2.4
, the image of the map (
5.1
) can be identified with
{}^{g}W\subset W_{g}
. Let
c\leq g
, the finite Weyl group
W_{c}
of
\mathrm{Sp}_{2c}
can be viewed as a subgroup of
W_{g}
naturally and we define
{{}^{c}W}
exactly as in the case for
{{}^{g}W}
. Explicitly, the reflection
s_{c+1-i}
in
W_{c}
will be mapped to
s_{g+1-i}
as a reflection in
W_{g}
.
We have the following criterion for identifying the basic EO stratum. Let
K=\tilde{\mathbb{S}}-\{s_{0}\}
which corresponds to the hyperspecial subgroup of
G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
w\in^{g}W
. Then the EO stratum
\mathcal{A}_{g,w}
is basic if and only if
w\in{{}^{c}W}
for some
c\leq g/2
.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.1
,
EO_{K,w}
is basic if and only if
w\in W_{\{c,g-c\}}\tau
considered as an element in
{}^{K}\tilde{W}
. Since

{}^{c}W=W_{\{c,g-c\}}\cap{{}^{g}W}

by
[
GH12
, Lemma 3.8]
,
EO_{K,w}
is basic if and only if
w\in{{}^{c}W}
as an element in
{{}^{g}W}
.
∎

This proposition rediscovers
[
Har10
, Remark 2.5.7]
and we now explain how to describe those basic EO strata as disjoint unions of Deligne-Lusztig varieties. Again Corollary
4.4
takes the following form

I_{\tau}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash X_{K,w}(\tau)\times G(\mathbb{A}^{p}_{f})/K^{p}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{A}_{g,w}

and by the proof of Theorem
4.7

X_{K,w}(\tau)=\bigsqcup_{i\in J_{\tau}/J_{\tau}\cap\breve{K}_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)\cup I(K,w,\sigma)}}iY_{I(K,w,\sigma)}(w)

where

\begin{split}Y_{I(K,w,\sigma)}(w)&=\{g\breve{K}_{I(K,w,\sigma)}\in\breve{K}_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)\cup I(K,w,\sigma)}/\breve{K}_{I(K,w,\sigma)}:g^{-1}\tau\sigma(g)\in\breve{K}_{I(K,w,\sigma)}w\breve{K}_{\sigma(I(K,w,\sigma))}\}\\
&=\{g\breve{K}_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)\cap K}\in\breve{K}_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)}/\breve{K}_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)\cap K}:g^{-1}\tau\sigma(g)\in{\breve{K}_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)\cap K\sigma}}\breve{I}w\breve{I}\}.\\
\end{split}

Lemma 5.6
.

Suppose that
w\in{{}^{c}W}
and
w\not\in{{}^{c-1}W}
, then

\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)=\{s_{0},\cdots,s_{c-1}\}\cup\{s_{g-c+1},\cdots,s_{g}\}.

Proof.

By the proof of
[
Hoe10
, Lemma 7.1]
, see also
[
GH12
, Lemma 3.8]
, the set of simple reflections in a reduced expression of
w
is equal to
\{s_{i},s_{i+1},\cdots,s_{g-1},s_{g}\}
with
i>g-c
. Since
w\not\in{{}^{c-1}W}
,
i=g-c+1
. Then by definition we have

\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)=\{s_{0},\cdots,s_{c-1}\}\cup\{s_{g-c+1},\cdots,s_{g}\}.

∎

Note the proof of the above lemma shows that those
w\in{{}^{c}W}
and
w\not\in{{}^{c-1}W}
are in fact
\sigma
-Coxeter elements in
W_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)}
. Therefore we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 5.7
.

The set
I(K,w,\sigma)
is equal to
\{s_{c+1},\cdots,s_{g-c-1}\}
.

Proof.

By
[
GH15
, Lemma 4.6.1]
,
I(K,w,\sigma)
is exactly the set of simple reflections that commute with

\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)=\{s_{0},\cdots,s_{c-1}\}\cup\{s_{g-c+1},\cdots,s_{g}\}.

The result is then clear.
∎

For
I(K,w,\sigma)=\{s_{c+1},\cdots,s_{g-c-1}\}
, we denote by
Y_{c}(w)
the fine Deligne-Lusztig variety given by

Y_{I(K,w,\sigma)}(w)=\{g\breve{K}_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)\cap K}\in\breve{K}_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)}/\breve{K}_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)\cap K}:g^{-1}\tau\sigma(g)\in{\breve{K}_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)\cap K\sigma}}\breve{I}w\breve{I}\}.

Since
K=\tilde{S}-\{s_{0}\}
,
K\cap\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)=\{s_{1},\cdots,s_{c-1}\}\cup\{s_{g-c+1},\cdots,s_{g}\}
. It follows that
Y_{c}(w)
is exactly the fine Deligne-variety denoted by
X_{c(w)}\{\tau(w)\}
in
[
Hoe10
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Theorem 5.8
.

For
w\in{{}^{c}W}
and
w\not\in{{}^{c-1}W}
, the basic EO stratum
\mathcal{A}_{g,w}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{A}_{\{c,g-c\},\tau}\times Y_{c}(w)

Proof.

We know

I_{\tau}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash X_{K,w}(\tau)\times G(\mathbb{A}^{p}_{f})/K^{p}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{A}_{g,w}

and

X_{K,w}(\tau)=\bigsqcup_{i\in J_{\tau}/J_{\tau}\cap\breve{K}_{\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)\cup I(K,w,\sigma)}}iY_{c}(w).

Notice that
\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)\cup I(K,w,\sigma)=\tilde{\mathbb{S}}-\{c,g-c\}
by Lemma
5.6
and Lemma
5.7
. The result follows from the fact that

I_{\tau}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash J_{\tau}/J_{\tau}\cap\breve{K}_{\tilde{\mathbb{S}}-\{c,g-c\}}\times G(\mathbb{A}^{p}_{f})/K^{p}.

is isomorphic to
\mathcal{A}_{\{c,g-c\},\tau}
as shown in Lemma
5.3
.
∎

This shows that the descriptions of Görtz-He-Nie in Theorem
4.7
for the basic EO stratum agrees with
the description of Hoeve as in
[
Hoe10
, Theorem 1.2]
. Moreover let
J=\tilde{\mathbb{S}}-\{c,g-c\}
in Theorem
5.4
and let
w\in{{}^{c}W}
as in Theorem
5.8
. Then we easily obtain the following commutative diagram combining Theorem
5.4
and Theorem
5.8

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}_{I,w}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}_{\{c,g-c\},\tau}\times Y(J,w)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}_{g,w}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}_{\{c,g-c\},\tau}\times Y_{c}(w)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

with the vertical arrows the natural projection maps. This recovers one of the main theorems of

.

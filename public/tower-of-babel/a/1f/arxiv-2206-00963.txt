Equivariant Self-homotopy equivalences of product spaces
Gopal Chandra Dutta, Debasis Sen, Ajay Singh Thakur

Abstract
. Let G G be a finite group. We study the group of G G -equivariant self-homotopy equivalences of product of G G -spaces. For a product of n n -spaces, we represent it as product of n n -subgroups under the assumption of equivariant reducibility. Further we describe each factor as a split short exact sequence. Also we obtain an another kind of factorisation, called L ​ U LU type decomposition, as product of two subgroups.

1.
Introduction

For a pointed space
X
, let
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
denote the set of homotopy classes of based self homotopy equivalences. This set is a group under composition of maps
[
1
,
15
]
. A natural approach for understanding
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
would be to start from some decomposition of
X
, for example as a product or wedge of simpler spaces, and then describe self-homotopy equivalences of
X
by in terms of those of the factors. The group
\operatorname{Aut}(X\times Y)
was studied in the past by many authors (cf.
[
3
,
4
,
9
,
10
,
12
,
14
]
). In particular Sawashita considered the group
\operatorname{Aut}(X\times Y)
when
X
and
Y
are spheres (
[
12
]
). We have projections
p_{X}:X\times Y\to X,~~p_{Y}:X\times Y\to Y
and inclusions
\iota_{X}:X\hookrightarrow X\times Y
and
\iota_{Y}:Y\hookrightarrow X\times Y
, determined by the base point. For a map
f:X\times Y\to X\times Y,
let

f_{XX}:=p_{X}\circ f\circ\iota_{X}:X\to X,~~f_{YY}:=p_{Y}\circ f\circ\iota_{Y}:Y\to Y.

A self homotopy equivalence
f
is called
reducible
if
f_{XX},f_{YY}
are self-homotopy equivalences. When self-homotopy equivalences of
X\times Y
are reducible, Heath and Booth obtained a split exact sequence

0\to[X,\operatorname{aut}_{1}(Y)]\to\operatorname{Aut}(X\times Y)\xrightarrow{}\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(Y)\to 0,

under the condition
[Y,\operatorname{aut}_{1}(X)]=\{\ast\}
(
[
4
,
6
]
). Here
\operatorname{aut}_{1}(X)
is the connected component of
\operatorname{Id}
of the space of based self homotopy equivalences of
X
. The group
\operatorname{Aut}(X\times Y)
has two natural subgroups
\operatorname{Aut}_{X}(X\times Y)
and
\operatorname{Aut}_{Y}(X\times Y)
, which are self-equivalences of
X\times Y
over
X
and
Y
respectively. Later Pavešić showed that under reducibility (without the condition
[Y,\operatorname{aut}_{1}(X)]=\{\ast\}
), the group
\operatorname{Aut}(X\times Y)
can be decomposed as product of the subgroups
\operatorname{Aut}_{X}(X\times Y)
and
\operatorname{Aut}_{Y}(X\times Y)
(see
[
9
,
10
]
),

(1)

\operatorname{Aut}(X\times Y)=\operatorname{Aut}_{X}(X\times Y)\cdot\operatorname{Aut}_{Y}(X\times Y).

Further each of the factors fits into split exact sequences

0\to[X,\operatorname{aut}_{1}(Y)]\to\operatorname{Aut}_{X}(X\times Y)\xrightarrow{}\operatorname{Aut}(Y)\to 0,

and

0\to[Y,\operatorname{aut}_{1}(X)]\to\operatorname{Aut}_{Y}(X\times Y)\xrightarrow{}\operatorname{Aut}(X)\to 0.

The result of Heath and Booth can be deduced as corollary of it. Moreover the decomposition of Equation
1
was generalised for
n
-product of spaces in terms of
n
-subgroups. Further a
LU
kind of decomposition was obtained for higher products (
[
11
,
10
]
).

In this paper, we consider equivariant self homotopy equivalences of product spaces and obtain equivariant versions of the results mentioned about. Let
G
be a finite group. A based
G
-space is a
G
-space with a point which is
G
-fixed.
The space of based
G
-equivariant maps is denoted by
\operatorname{map}^{G}(X,Y)
. Let
[X,Y]_{G}
denote the set of
G
-homotopy classes of based maps. For a based
G
-space
X
, we denote the space of based equivariant self-homotopy equivalences of
X
by
\operatorname{aut}^{G}(X)
. We denote by
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X)
the set of
G
-homotopy classes of elements of
\operatorname{aut}^{G}(X)
. The composition of maps induces a group operation on
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X)
. We call it the group of equivariant self-homotopy equivalences of
X
. This group has been studied by several authors (cf.
[
5
,
13
]
). Our goal here is to study the group
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
, where
X,Y
are based
G
-CW complexes and also for higher products. For our results we need the condition of
G
-reducibility.

1.1 Definition
.

With notations as above, a
G
-self-homotopy equivalence
f
of
X\times Y
is called
G
-reducible
if
f_{XX}\in\operatorname{aut}^{G}(X)
and
f_{YY}\in\operatorname{aut}^{G}(Y)
.

As in the non-equivariant case, the group
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
has two natural subgroups
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{X}(X\times Y)=\{f\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y):~~p_{X}\circ f=p_{X}\}
and
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{Y}(X\times Y)=\{f\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y):~~p_{Y}\circ f=p_{Y}\}
. We show that the product of these two subgroups is the whole group:

Theorem A
.

If all
G
-self-homotopy equivalences of
X\times Y
are
G
-reducible then

\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)=\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{X}(X\times Y)\cdot\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{Y}(X\times Y).

(See Theorem
2.7
below.)

Under the assumption of
G
-reducibility, we have a natural map
\phi:\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)\to\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(Y)
defined by
\phi(f)=(f_{XX},f_{YY})
. We show that the map
\phi
gives a split exact sequence. This is an equivariaint analogue of Heath and Booth
[
4
]
.

Theorem B
.

Let
X,Y
to be pointed connected
G
-CW complexes. If all
G
-self-homotopy equivalences of
X\times Y
are
G
-reducible and
[Y,\operatorname{aut}_{1}(X)]_{G}=\{\ast\}
, then we get a split short exact sequence

0\to[X,\operatorname{aut}_{1}(Y)]_{G}\to\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)\xrightarrow{\phi}\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(Y)\to 0.

(See Theorem
2.9
below.)

We use the above results of two products to generalize for the
n
-product of spaces
{\bf X}=X_{1}\times\ldots\times X_{n}
, where all
X_{i}
’s are
G
-spaces. The action of
G
on
{\bf X}
is the diagonal action. Let
\prod_{\hat{i}}:=X_{1}\times\ldots\times\hat{X_{i}}\times\ldots\times X_{n}
, i.e.
\prod_{\hat{i}}
denotes the subproduct of
{\bf X}
obtained by omitting
X_{i}
. We have a natural projection
{\bf X}\to\prod_{\hat{i}}
. The set of
G
-homotopy classes of
G
-self-equivalences of
{\bf X}
over
\prod_{\hat{i}}
is denoted by
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{\prod_{\hat{i}}}({\bf X})
. These are subgroups of
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}({\bf X})
.

Theorem C
.

If all
G
-self-homotopy equivalences of
{\bf X}
are
G
-reducible, then

\operatorname{Aut}^{G}({\bf X})=\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{\prod_{\hat{n}}}({\bf X})\ldots\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{\prod_{\hat{1}}}({\bf X}).

Moreover each
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{\prod_{\hat{i}}}({\bf X})
fits into a split exact sequence,

0\to\operatorname{Ker}(\phi_{i})\to\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{\prod_{\hat{i}}}({\bf X})\xrightarrow{\phi_{i}}\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X_{i})\to 0,~~~1\leq i\leq n.

(See Theorem
3.7
below.)

The group
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}({\bf X})
can be expressed as the product of its
n
-subgroups
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{\prod_{\hat{i}}}({\bf X})
(where
i=1,\ldots,n
), which is difficult from computational viewpoint. Therefore we develop a new factorization called LU decomposition, which is the following:

Theorem D
.

If all
G
-self-homotopy equivalences of
{\bf X}
are
G
-reducible, then

\operatorname{Aut}^{G}({\bf X})=L^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\cdot U^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}).

(See Theorem
4.2
below.) The factor
L^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
has the property that the induced maps on the homotopy groups can be represented by lower triangular matrices and similarly by upper triangular matrices for the other factor.

1.2
.

Organisation.

The rest of the paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
we consider product of two spaces. We give some sufficient conditions of
G
-reducibility and obtain Theorem A and Theorem B. In Section
3

n
-products of spaces and obtain Theorem C. In the last Section

G
will denote a finite group and all spaces are based

2.
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)

We begin this section by recalling some basic facts about
G
-CW complexes. We refer to
[
7
]
for details. Let
X
be a
G
-space. For a subgroup
H<G
, the
H
-fixed point set is defined as
X^{H}=\{x\in X:~~hx=x,~~\forall h\in H\}
. If
f:X\to Y
is a
G
-map then
f^{H}:=f|_{X^{H}}:X^{H}\to Y^{H}
. We know that an equivariant map
f:X\to Y
between
G
-CW complexes is
G
-homotopy equivalence if and only if
f^{H}:X^{H}\to Y^{H}
are ordinary homotopy equivalences for all subgroups
H
of
G
.

2.1
.

G
-reducibility.

For
G
-CW complexes
X,Y
, an equivariant self-homotopy equivalence
f
of
X\times Y
is
G
-reducible if and only if
f^{H}
is a reducible self-homotopy equivalence of
X^{H}\times Y^{H}
for all subgroups
H
of
G
.
The following are some examples of when
G
-self homotopy equivalences of
X\times Y
are
G
-reducible.

2.2 Examples
.

Let
X,Y
be two pointed
G
-CW complexes.

(a)

Assume that for every positive integer
n
and for every pair of
G
-maps
f:X\to Y,g:Y\to X
, at least one of the induced maps
\pi_{n}(f^{H}):\pi_{n}(X^{H})\to\pi_{n}(Y^{H}),\pi_{n}(g^{H}):\pi_{n}(Y^{H})\to\pi_{n}(X^{H})
is trivial for all
H\leq G
. Then all
G
-self equivalences of
X\times Y
are
G
-reducible.

To see this, let
h\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
. Therefore
h^{H}:X^{H}\times Y^{H}\to X^{H}\times Y^{H}
is an ordinary homotopy equivalence for all
H\leq G
.
By the given condition at least one of the induced homomorphisms on the homotopy groups of the maps
(h^{H})_{XY}:Y^{H}\to X^{H},~~(h^{H})_{YX}:X^{H}\to Y^{H}
is trivial.
Now from
[
9
, Proposition 2.1]
, we say that
(h^{H})_{XX},(h^{H})_{YY}
are self-homotopy equivalences of
X^{H}
and
Y^{H}
respectively for all
H\leq G
. Therefore
(h_{XX})^{H}
and
(h_{YY})^{H}
both are self-homotopy equivalences of
X^{H}
and
Y^{H}
respectively for all
H\leq G
(since
(h^{H})_{XX}=(p_{X})^{H}\circ h^{H}\circ(i_{X})^{H}=(p_{X}\circ h\circ i_{X})^{H}=(h_{XX})^{H}
. Consequently
h_{XX}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X),h_{YY}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(Y)
.

(b)

For every positive integer
n
, if at least one of the group
\pi_{n}(X^{H}),\pi_{n}(Y^{H})
is trivial for all
H\leq G
, then all
G
-self equivalences of
X\times Y
are
G
-reducible.

This follows from the above one.

(c)

If at least one of
\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_{n}(X^{H}),\pi_{n}(Y^{H})),\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_{n}(Y^{H}),\pi_{n}(X^{H}))
is trivial
\forall~H\leq G,\forall n>0
then all
G
-self equivalences of
X\times Y
are
G
-reducible.

This also immediately follows from the above.

(d)

Let
m,n\geq 2
. Assume that the group
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
acts on
S^{m}
and
S^{n}
by reflection about
m
and
n
dimensional hyperplane respectively. If
m\neq n
, then all
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-self equivalences of
S^{m}\times S^{n}
are
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-reducible. To see this, observe that,

(S^{m})^{H}=\begin{cases}S^{m-1},~~~if~H=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\\
S^{m},~~~~~~~if~~H=\{1\}.\end{cases}

If
m<n
, then
[(S^{m})^{H},(S^{n})^{H}]
is trivial. Therefore the induced maps between
\pi_{k}(S^{m})^{H}
and
\pi_{k}(S^{n})^{H}
are trivial, for all
H\leq\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and for all
k>0
. Similarly for
m>n
, we can say that the induced maps between
\pi_{k}(S^{n})^{H}
and
\pi_{k}(S^{m})^{H}
are trivial, for all
H\leq\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and for all
k>0
.
Hence all
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-self-homotopy equivalences of
S^{m}\times S^{n}
are
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-reducible.

Recall that, an endomorphism
\phi\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
of an additively written group
\Gamma
is called
quasi-regular
if the map
(\operatorname{Id}-\phi)\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
defined by
\gamma\mapsto\gamma-\phi(\gamma)
, is an automorphism. A self
G
-map
f\colon X\to X
is called
quasi-regular
if the endomorphism of groups
(f^{H})_{\#n}\colon\pi_{n}(X^{H})\to\pi_{n}(X^{H})
are quasi-regular for all
H\leq G
and all
n
. An endomorphism
\phi\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
is called
nilpotent
if
\phi^{k}=0
for some
k
. For a nilpotent endomorphism
\phi\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
such that
(\operatorname{Id}-\phi)
is a homomorphism, then
\phi
is quasi-regular (cf.
[
10
]
).

The following Proposition gives some sufficient conditions for
G
-reducibility. We denote
f_{XY}=p_{X}\circ f\circ\iota_{Y}:Y\to X
and
f_{YX}=p_{Y}\circ f\circ\iota_{X}:X\to Y.

2.3 Proposition
.

Let
X,Y
be based
G
-CW complexes.

(a)

Assume that
f_{YY}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(Y)
for all
f\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
. Then all
G
- self-homotopy equivalences of
X\times Y
are
G
-reducible.

(b)

Assume that for any
f\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
one of the maps
f_{XY}\circ\widetilde{f}_{YX}:X\to X
and
f_{YX}\circ\widetilde{f}_{XY}:Y\to Y
is quasi-regular. Then all
G
-self-homotopy equivalences of
X\times Y
are
G
-reducible. Here
\widetilde{f}
is the
G
-hmotopy inverse of
f
in
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
.

(c)

Suppose that every self
G
-map of
X
, which can be factored through
Y
(up to homotopy), induces a nilpotent endomorphism on
\pi_{n}(X^{H})
for all
n,~H<G
(i.e. if
X
and
Y
are homotopically distant), then
G
-self-homotopy equivalences of
X\times Y
are
G
-reducible.

Proof.

(a)

Let
f\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
. Then by assumption
f_{YY}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(Y)
. We have to show that
f_{XX}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X)
which is equivalent to show that
(f_{XX})^{H}\in\operatorname{Aut}(X^{H}),~~\forall H\leq G
. By definition
(f_{X})^{H}\circ(\iota_{X})^{H}=(f_{XX})^{H}
. Therefore by
[
9
, Proposition 2.3(b)]
, it is enough to show
((f_{X})^{H},(p_{Y})^{H})=(f_{X},p_{Y})^{H}\in\operatorname{Aut}(X^{H}\times Y^{H})
for all
H\leq G
.

Since
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
is a group,
f=({f}_{X},{f}_{Y})
has an inverse
\bar{f}=(\bar{f}_{X},\bar{f}_{Y})\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
. So
\bar{f}\circ f=\operatorname{Id}_{X\times Y}
.
Now observe that,

(p_{X},p_{Y})=\operatorname{Id}_{X\times Y}=\bar{f}\circ f=(\bar{f}_{X},\bar{f}_{Y})\circ(f_{X},f_{Y})=(\bar{f}_{X}\circ(f_{X},f_{Y}),\bar{f}_{Y}\circ(f_{X},f_{Y})).

So we get,
\bar{f}_{X}\circ(f_{X},f_{Y})=p_{X};~~\bar{f}_{Y}\circ(f_{X},f_{Y})=p_{Y}.
By the given condition, we can say that
\bar{f}_{YY}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(Y)
, as
\bar{f}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
. Hence
(\bar{f}_{YY})^{H}\in\operatorname{Aut}(Y^{H}),~\forall H\leq G
, i.e.
(\bar{f}_{Y})^{H}\circ(\iota_{Y})^{H}\in\operatorname{Aut}(Y^{H})
for all
H\leq G
. Therefore from
[
9
, Proposition 2.3(b)]
, we have
(p_{X},\bar{f}_{Y})^{H}=((p_{X})^{H},(\bar{f}_{Y})^{H})\in\operatorname{Aut}(X^{H}\times Y^{H}),~~\forall H\leq G
. Hence
(p_{X},\bar{f}_{Y})\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
.
Now,

(f_{X},p_{Y})=(f_{X},\bar{f}_{Y}\circ(f_{X},f_{Y}))=(p_{X},\bar{f}_{Y})\circ(f_{X},f_{Y})\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y).

Hence
(f_{X},p_{Y})^{H}\in\operatorname{Aut}(X^{H}\times Y^{H})
for all
H\leq G
.

(b)

For any map
f\colon X\times Y\to X\times Y
the induced endomorphism on homotopy groups
(f^{H})_{\#n}\colon{\pi}_{n}(X^{H})\times{\pi}_{n}(Y^{H})\to{\pi}_{n}(X^{H})\times{\pi}_{n}(Y^{H})
can be represented by the matrix form for all
H\leq G
,

M_{n}(f^{H}):=\begin{bmatrix}(f^{H}_{XX})_{\#n}&(f^{H}_{XY})_{\#n}\\
(f^{H}_{YX})_{\#n}&(f^{H}_{YY})_{\#n}\end{bmatrix}.

Without any ambiguity we can write
(f^{H})_{XX}=(f_{XX})^{H}=f^{H}_{XX}
and so on.

Therefore for any
f\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
with its inverse
\widetilde{f}
, we can say that

M_{n}(f^{H})\cdot M_{n}(\widetilde{f^{H}})=M_{n}(\operatorname{Id}_{X^{H}\times Y^{H}}).

This implies that
(f^{H}_{YX})_{\#n}\circ(\widetilde{f}^{H}_{XY})_{\#n}+(f^{H}_{YY})_{\#n}\circ(\widetilde{f}^{H}_{YY})_{\#n}=\operatorname{Id}_{\pi_{n}(Y^{H})}
. So we get,

(f^{H}_{YY})_{\#n}\circ(\widetilde{f}^{H}_{YY})_{\#n}=\operatorname{Id}_{\pi_{n}(Y^{H})}-(f^{H}_{YX})_{\#n}\circ(\widetilde{f}^{H}_{XY})_{\#n}=\operatorname{Id}_{\pi_{n}(Y^{H})}-(f_{YX}\circ\widetilde{f}_{XY})^{H}_{\#n}.

If
f_{YX}\circ\widetilde{f}_{XY}
is quasi-regular, then the maps
\operatorname{Id}_{\pi_{n}(Y^{H})}-(f_{YX}\circ f_{XY})^{H}_{\#n}
are automorphsims for all
n
and
H\leq G
. Consequently,
(f^{H}_{YY})_{\#n}\colon\pi_{n}(Y^{H})\to\pi_{n}(Y^{H})
are isomorphsim for all
n
and all
H\leq G
. By Whitehead theorem we get
f_{YY}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(Y)
. Using the part (a), we get that the
G
-self-homotopy equivalences of
X\times Y
are
G
-reducible.

Similarly, if we take
f_{XY}\circ\widetilde{f}_{YX}
is quasi-regular, then using the equality

(f^{H}_{XY})_{\#n}\circ(\widetilde{f}^{H}_{YX})_{\#n}+(f^{H}_{XX})_{\#n}\circ(\widetilde{f}^{H}_{XX})_{\#n}=\operatorname{Id}_{\pi_{n}(X^{H})},

we get the desire result.

(c)

Let
f\colon X\times Y\to X\times Y
be a self
G
-homotopy equivalence with
G
-homotopy inverse
\widetilde{f}\colon X\times Y\to X\times Y
. From part(b) its enough to show that
f_{XY}\circ\widetilde{f}_{YX}
is quasi-regular, which is equivalent to show that
\operatorname{Id}_{\pi_{n}(X^{H})}-(f_{XY}\circ\widetilde{f}_{YX})^{H}_{\#n}
are homomorphsims, whenever
(f_{XY}\circ\widetilde{f}_{YX})^{H}_{\#n}
are nilpotent endomorphism for all
n
and all
H\leq G
. Observe that,
\operatorname{Id}_{\pi_{n}(X^{H})}-(f_{XY}\circ\widetilde{f}_{YX})^{H}_{\#n}=(f_{XX})^{H}_{\#n}\circ(\widetilde{f}_{XX})^{H}_{\#n}
are homorphisms for all
n
and all
H\leq G
. By the given assumption,
(f_{XY}\circ\widetilde{f}_{YX})_{\#n}
are nilpotent natural transformation for all
n
. Therefore
(f_{XY}\circ\widetilde{f}_{YX})^{H}_{\#n}
are nilpotent endomorphism for all
n
and all
H\leq G
, so the natural transformation
(f_{XY}\circ\widetilde{f}_{YX})_{\#n}
are quasi-regular endomorphisms for all
n
. Hence the map
f_{XY}\circ\widetilde{f}_{YX}
is quasi-regular and we get the
G
-reducibility of the map
f
.

∎

Recall that we have natural projections
p_{X}:X\times Y\to X
and
p_{Y}:X\times Y\to Y
. A self-map of
X\times Y
such that
p_{X}\circ f=p_{X}
looks like
(p_{X},f_{Y})
. We define

\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{X}(X\times Y)=\{f\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)~|~~p_{X}\circ f=p_{X}\},

\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{Y}(X\times Y)=\{f\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)~|~~p_{Y}\circ f=p_{Y}\}.

We now show that these are subgroups
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
and obtain a short exact sequence. Recall that
\operatorname{aut}_{1}(X)
is the connected component of identity of
\operatorname{aut}(X)
. This has a
G
-action defined by
(g.f)(x)=gf(g^{-1}x).

2.4 Proposition
.

(a)

If
(p_{X},g):X\times Y\to X\times Y
is a
G
-self-homotopy equivalence, then there exists a
G
-map
\widetilde{g}:X\times Y\to Y
, such that
(p_{X},\widetilde{g})
is a
G
-equivariant homotopy inverse of
(p_{X},g)
.

(b)

The map
(p_{X},g):X\times Y\to X\times Y
is a
G
-homotopy equivalence if and only if
g\circ\iota_{Y}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(Y)
.

(c)

\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{X}(X\times Y)
is a subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
whose elements are of the form
(p_{X},g)
with
g\circ\iota_{Y}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(Y)
.

(d)

If
X
is a connected
G
-CW complex, then there exists a split exact sequence

0\to[X,\operatorname{aut}_{1}(Y)]_{G}\to\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{X}(X\times Y)\xrightarrow{\phi}\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(Y)\to 0,

where
\phi(p_{X},g)=g\circ\iota_{Y}
, for all
(p_{X},g)\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{X}(X\times Y).

Proof.

(a)

Let
(p_{X},g)\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
. Then it has an inverse in
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
, say
(f_{X},f_{Y})
.
So,

\displaystyle(p_{X},g)\circ(f_{X},f_{Y})=\operatorname{Id}_{X\times Y}=(p_{X},p_{Y})

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle~(f_{X},g\circ(f_{X},f_{Y}))=(p_{X},p_{Y})

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle~f_{X}=p_{X}.

Therefore
(p_{X},f_{Y})
is a
G
-equivariant homotopy inverse of
(p_{X},g)
. Consequently
(p_{X},\widetilde{g})
is a
G
-equivariant homotopy inverse of
(p_{X},g)
, where
\widetilde{g}=f_{Y}:X\times Y\to Y
.

(b)

We know that
(p_{X},g):X\times Y\to X\times Y
is a
G
-equivariant self-homotopy equivalence, if and only if
(p_{X},g)^{H}:X^{H}\times Y^{H}\to X^{H}\times Y^{H}
are self-homotopy equivalences for all
H\leq G
, i.e
((p_{X})^{H},g^{H})\in\operatorname{Aut}(X^{H}\times Y^{H})
for all
H\leq G
.
From
[
9
, Proposition 2.3]
, we say that
((p_{X})^{H},g^{H})\in\operatorname{Aut}(X^{H}\times Y^{H})
if and only if
g^{H}\circ(\iota_{Y})^{H}\in\operatorname{Aut}(Y^{H})
. Therefore
(p_{X},g):X\times Y\to X\times Y
is a
G
-equivariant homotopy equivalence, if and only if
(g\circ\iota_{Y})^{H}=g^{H}\circ(\iota_{Y})^{H}\in\operatorname{Aut}(Y^{H})
for all
H\leq G
i.e.
g\circ\iota_{Y}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(Y)
.

(c)

Follows from part (a) and (b).

(d)

First we prove that
\phi
is a homomorphism. Let
(p_{X},g),(p_{X},f)\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{X}(X\times Y)
, then

\phi((p_{X},g)\circ(p_{X},f))=\phi(p_{X},g\circ(p_{X},f))=g\circ(p_{X},f)\circ\iota_{Y}

=g\circ\iota_{Y}\circ f\circ\iota_{Y}=\phi(p_{X},g)\circ\phi(p_{X},f).

Let us define an another map

\psi:\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(Y)\to\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{X}(X\times Y),~~\psi(h)=(p_{X},h\circ p_{Y}),

for all
h\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(Y)
. Now
(\phi\circ\psi)(h)=\phi(p_{X},h\circ p_{Y})=h\circ p_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}=h
for all
h\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(Y)
, i.e.
\phi\circ\psi=\operatorname{Id}
. Therefore
\phi
is an onto map.

Now we determine the kernel of
\phi.
Clearly,
(p_{X},f)\in\ker(\phi)
if and only if
\phi(p_{X},f)=\operatorname{Id}_{Y}
, i.e
f\circ\iota_{Y}=\operatorname{Id}_{Y}
, where
f:X\times Y\to Y
is a
G
-map. Therefore
(p_{X},f)\in\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)
corresponds under the adjoint associativity to a base point preserving map
\widetilde{f}:X\to\operatorname{map}(Y,Y)
defined as
\widetilde{f}(x)(y)=f(x,y)
. Observe that
\widetilde{f}
sends the base point of
X
to
\operatorname{Id}_{Y}
. Since
X
is a connected, the image of
\widetilde{f}
is contained in the component of
\operatorname{map}(Y,Y)
that contains
\operatorname{Id}_{Y}
, denoted by
\operatorname{aut}_{1}(Y)
. Again
\widetilde{f}
is a
G
-equivariant map, because

(g\cdot\widetilde{f}(x))(y)=g\widetilde{f}(x)(g^{-1}y)=gf(x,g^{-1}y)=f(gx,y)=\widetilde{f}(gx)(y),

i.e.
\widetilde{f}(gx)=g\cdot\widetilde{f}(x)
. Therefore
\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)
can be identified with
[X,\operatorname{aut}_{1}(Y)]_{G}
, which proves the assertion.

∎

The following corollary follows easily from Proposition
2.4
(b).

2.5 Corollary
.

Assume that all
G
-self-homotopy equivalences of
X\times Y
are
G
-reducible. If
f=(f_{X},f_{Y})\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
, then
(p_{X},f_{Y})\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{X}(X\times Y)
, and
(f_{X},p_{Y})\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{Y}(X\times Y)
.

The following is a sufficient condition for an equivariant self-map
f
of
X\times Y
to be a
G
-homotopy equivalence.

2.6 Proposition
.

Let us assume all
G
-self-homotopy equivalences of
X\times Y
are G-reducible. For any
f\in\operatorname{map}^{G}(X\times Y,X\times Y)
, we have
f\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
if and only if
f_{XX}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X)
and
f_{YY}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(Y)
.

Proof.

The forward implication is trivial.

Conversely, let
f\in\operatorname{map}^{G}(X\times Y,X\times Y)
such that
f_{XX}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X),f_{YY}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(Y)
. By Proposition
2.4
[(a), (b)], we have
(f_{X},p_{Y})\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
with inverse
(\widetilde{f_{X}},p_{Y})\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
.
Observe that,

f=(f_{X},f_{Y})=[p_{X},f_{Y}\circ(f_{X},p_{Y})^{-1}]\circ(f_{X},p_{Y})=[p_{X},f_{Y}\circ(\widetilde{f_{X}},p_{Y})]\circ(f_{X},p_{Y}).

Moreover,

(p_{X},f_{Y})\circ(\widetilde{f_{X}},p_{Y})=[p_{X}\circ(\widetilde{f_{X}},p_{Y}),f_{Y}\circ(\widetilde{f_{X}},p_{Y})]\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y).

By applying the
G
-reducibility to the right-hand side, we say that
f_{Y}\circ(\widetilde{f_{X}},p_{Y})\circ\iota_{Y}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(Y)
. Therefore from Proposition
2.4
(b), we have
(p_{X},f_{Y}\circ(\widetilde{f_{X}},p_{Y}))\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
.
Consequently,
f=[p_{X},f_{Y}\circ(\widetilde{f_{X}},p_{Y})]\circ(f_{X},p_{Y})\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
.

∎

We have seen in Proposition
2.4
that
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{X}(X\times Y)
and
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{Y}(X\times Y)
are two subgroups of
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y).
We now show that the product of these subgroups is the whole group.

2.7 Theorem
.

Let us asssume all self-homotopy equivalences of
X\times Y
are
G
-reducible. Then

\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)=\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{X}(X\times Y)\cdot\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{Y}(X\times Y).

Proof.

Clearly,
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{X}(X\times Y)\cap\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{Y}(X\times Y)=(p_{X},p_{Y})
. Since the intersection of the two subgroups is trivial, therefore if the factorization of the element in
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
exists, it will be unique. We have the factorization,

f=(f_{X},f_{Y})=[p_{X},f_{Y}\circ(f_{X},p_{Y})^{-1}]\circ(f_{X},p_{Y})=[p_{X},f_{Y}\circ(\widetilde{f_{X}},p_{Y})]\circ(f_{X},p_{Y}).

From Corollary
2.5
we can say that the second factor
(f_{X},p_{Y})
in the decomposition of
f
is in
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{Y}(X\times Y)
. Also its inverse
(\widetilde{f}_{X},p_{Y})\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
. It is sufficient to show that the first factor is in
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{X}(X\times Y)
. Now

(f_{X},f_{Y})\circ(\widetilde{f}_{X},p_{Y})=[f_{X}\circ(\widetilde{f}_{X},p_{Y}),f_{Y}\circ(\widetilde{f}_{X},p_{Y})]\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y),

using
G
-reducibility we have,
f_{Y}\circ(\widetilde{f}_{X},p_{Y})\circ\iota_{Y}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(Y)
. Therefore by Proposition
2.4
(b), we can say that the first factor
[p_{X},f_{Y}\circ(\widetilde{f_{X}},p_{Y})]\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{X}(X\times Y)
.

∎

2.8 Lemma
.

Let Y be a connected
G
-CW complex such that
[Y,\operatorname{aut}_{1}(X)]_{G}=\{\ast\}
. For every
G
-map
h\colon X\times Y\to X
such that
h\circ\iota_{X}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X)
, then the relation
h\simeq h\circ\iota_{X}\circ p_{X}
holds.

Proof.

From
2.4
(d), we have a short exact sequence,

0\to[Y,\operatorname{aut}_{1}(X)]_{G}\to\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{Y}(X\times Y)\xrightarrow{\phi}\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X)\to 0,

defined as

\phi(f,p_{Y}):=f\circ\iota_{X}.

Since
[Y,\operatorname{aut}_{1}(X)]_{G}=\{\ast\}
, therefore
\phi
is an isomorphism.
Let the splitting map of
\phi
is
\psi\colon\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X)\to\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{Y}(X\times Y)
, defined as
\psi(g):=(g\circ p_{X},p_{Y})
.
Now for any
G
-map
h\colon X\times Y\to X
with
h\circ\iota_{X}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X)\Rightarrow(h,p_{Y})\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{Y}(X\times Y)
. Again

h\circ\iota_{X}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X)\Rightarrow\psi(h\circ i_{X})=(h\circ\iota_{X}\circ p_{X},p_{Y})\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{Y}(X\times Y).

Since
\phi\big(h,p_{Y}\big)=\phi\big(h\circ\iota_{X}\circ p_{X},p_{Y}\big)
, therefore
(h,p_{Y})\simeq(h\circ\iota_{X}\circ p_{X},p_{Y})
, otherwise
\phi
fails to be an isomorphism. Consequently we get the desired relation
h\simeq h\circ\iota_{X}\circ p_{X}
.

∎

We now obtain a description of
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
in terms of
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X)
and
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(Y)
, which is an equivariant version of Booth and Heath
[
4
, Theorem 2.7]
.

2.9 Theorem
.

Let
X
and
Y
be connected
G
-CW complexes and all self-homotopy equivalences of
X\times Y
are
G
-reducible. If
[Y,\operatorname{aut}_{1}(X)]_{G}=\{\ast\}
, then we have a split exact sequence

0\to[X,\operatorname{aut}_{1}(Y)]_{G}\to\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)\xrightarrow{\phi}\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(Y)\to 0.

where
\phi(f)=(f_{XX},f_{YY})
.

Proof.

First we show that
\phi
is a homomorphism. Let
f=(f_{X},f_{Y}),g=(g_{X},g_{Y})\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
, then

\displaystyle\phi(f\circ g)

\displaystyle=\phi\big(\big[f_{X}\circ(g_{X},g_{Y}),f_{Y}\circ(g_{X},g_{Y})\big]\big)

\displaystyle=\big[f_{X}\circ(g_{X},g_{Y})\circ\iota_{X},f_{Y}\circ(g_{X},g_{Y})\circ\iota_{Y}\big]

\displaystyle=\big[f_{X}\circ\iota_{X}\circ p_{X}\circ(g_{X},g_{Y})\circ\iota_{X},f_{Y}\circ(g_{X}\circ\iota_{X}\circ p_{X},g_{Y})\circ\iota_{Y}\big]~~~(\text{by Lemma }\ref{Lgred})

\displaystyle=(f_{X}\circ\iota_{X}\circ g_{X}\circ\iota_{X},f_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y}\circ g_{Y}\circ\iota_{Y})

\displaystyle=(f_{XX}\circ g_{XX},f_{YY}\circ g_{YY})

\displaystyle=\phi(f)\circ\phi(g).

Consider an another map
\psi:\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(Y)\to\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
, define as

\psi(h_{1},h_{2}):=(h_{1}\circ p_{X},h_{2}\circ p_{Y}),~~~~~~\forall(h_{1},h_{2})\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(Y).

From Proposition
2.6
, we can say that the map
\psi
is well-defined. Clearly
\phi\circ\psi=\operatorname{Id}_{\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(Y)}
, therefore
\phi
is onto homomorphsim.

Now we determine
\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)
. An element
f=(f_{X},f_{Y})\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X\times Y)
is in
\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)
if and only if
f_{XX}=\operatorname{Id}_{X},~~~f_{YY}=\operatorname{Id}_{Y}
. Therefore
f=(f_{X},f_{Y})\in\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)
corresponds under adjoint associativity to the base point preserving maps
\widetilde{f}_{X}:Y\to\operatorname{map}(X,X)
and
\widetilde{f}_{Y}:X\to\operatorname{map}(Y,Y)
, defined as
\widetilde{f}_{X}(y)(x):=f_{X}(x,y)
and
\widetilde{f}_{Y}(x)(y):=f_{Y}(x,y)
respectively. Since both
X
and
Y
are connected, therefore the images of
\widetilde{f}_{X}
and
\widetilde{f}_{Y}
are contained in the component of
\operatorname{map}(X,X)
and
\operatorname{map}(Y,Y)
which contains
\operatorname{Id}_{X}
and
\operatorname{Id}_{Y}
respectively. These components are denoted by
\operatorname{aut}_{1}(X)
and
\operatorname{aut}_{1}(Y)
. Again the maps
\widetilde{f}_{X}
and
\widetilde{f}_{Y}
are
G
-maps. Therefore the

can be identified with
[Y,\operatorname{aut}_{1}(X)]_{G}\times[X,\operatorname{aut}_{1}(Y)]_{G}
. Since
[Y,\operatorname{aut}_{1}(X)]_{G}=\{\ast\}

∎

\displaystyle(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n-1},f_{n})\circ(p_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{f}_{n-1},p_{n})=\Big[p_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{f}_{n-1},f_{n}\circ

\displaystyle(p_{1},....,\widetilde{f}_{n-1},p_{n})\Big]\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}({\bf X}).

Using
G
-reducibility we have
f_{n}\circ(p_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{f}_{n-1},p_{n})\circ\iota_{n}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X_{n})
. Therefore
\Big[p_{1},\ldots,p_{n-1},f_{n}\circ(p_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{f}_{n-1},p_{n})\Big]\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}({\bf X})
.
Consequently,

(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n-2},f_{n-1},f_{n})=\Big[p_{1},\ldots,p_{n-1},f_{n}\circ(p_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{f}_{n-1},p_{n})\Big]\circ(p_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1},p_{n}),

is in
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}({\bf X})
whenever
f_{n-1n-1}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X_{n-1})
and
f_{nn}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X_{n})
. This completes the proof for
k=n-1.

Now consider the case
k=n-2
. We have factorisation,

\displaystyle(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n-3},f_{n-2},f_{n-1},f_{n})=

\displaystyle\Big[p_{1},\ldots,p_{n-2},f_{n-1}\circ(p_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{f}_{n-2},p_{n-1},p_{n}),f_{n}\circ(p_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{f}_{n-2},p_{n-1},p_{n})\Big]\circ

\displaystyle(p_{1},\ldots,f_{n-2},p_{n-1},p_{n}).

Clearly, the second factor is in
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}({\bf X})
by Lemma
3.2
. To show the first factor is in
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}({\bf X})
, it is enough to show that (by the previous step
k=n-1
),

f_{n-1}\circ(p_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{f}_{n-2},p_{n-1},p_{n})\circ\iota_{n-1}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X_{n-1}),

and

f_{n}\circ(p_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{f}_{n-2},p_{n-1},p_{n})\circ\iota_{n}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X_{n}).

Since,

\displaystyle(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n-2},f_{n-1},f_{n})\circ(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n-3},\widetilde{f}_{n-2},p_{n-1},p_{n})=

\displaystyle\Big[p_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{f}_{n-2},f_{n-1}\circ(p_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{f}_{n-2},p_{n-1},p_{n}),f_{n}\circ(p_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{f}_{n-2},p_{n-1},p_{n})\Big]\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}({\bf X}).

Using
G
-reducibility we conclude that,

f_{n-1}\circ(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n-3},\widetilde{f}_{n-2},p_{n-1},p_{n})\circ\iota_{n-1}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X_{n-1}),

and

f_{n}\circ(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n-3},\widetilde{f}_{n-2},p_{n-1},p_{n})\circ\iota_{n}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X_{n}).

Therefore,
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n-3},f_{n-2},f_{n-1},f_{n})\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}({\bf X})
, whenever
f_{ii}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X_{i}),~~~~\forall i=n-2,n-1,n
.

Inductively, we can prove that
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k-1},f_{k},\ldots,f_{n})\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}({\bf X})
, whenever
f_{ii}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X_{i}),~~k\leq i\leq n
.

∎

.

Assume that all

3.4

.

(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})=\Big[p_{1},f_{2}\circ(\widetilde{f}_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{n}),\ldots,f_{n}\circ(\widetilde{f}_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{n})\Big]\circ(f_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{n}).

3.2
, we have
(f_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{n})\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}({\bf X})
with its inverse

-reducibility we can say that

3.4
we have,

.

∎

3.7 Theorem
.

Assume that all
G
-self-homotopy equivalences of
{\bf X}
are
G
-reducible. Then,

\operatorname{Aut}^{G}({\bf X})=\operatorname{Aut}_{\prod_{\hat{n}}}^{G}({\bf X})\ldots\operatorname{Aut}_{\prod_{\hat{1}}}^{G}({\bf X}).

Moreover, if each
X_{i}
are connected then
\operatorname{Aut}_{\prod_{\hat{i}}}^{G}({\bf X})
fits into a split exact sequence

0\to[\Pi_{\hat{i}},\operatorname{aut}_{1}(X_{i})]_{G}\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\prod_{\hat{i}}}^{G}({\bf X})\xrightarrow{\phi_{i}}\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X_{i})\to 0,

defined by

\phi_{i}\big(p_{1},\ldots,f_{i},\ldots,p_{n}\big)=f_{i}\circ\iota_{i},~~~\forall i=1,\ldots,n

2\leq k\leq n-1

Since
\operatorname{Aut}_{X_{1}\times\ldots\times X_{k}}^{G}({\bf X})\cap\operatorname{Aut}_{\prod_{\hat{k}}}^{G}({\bf X})=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
, therefore if the factorization exists, it must be unique.
Now,

\displaystyle(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k-1},f_{k},\ldots,f_{n})=

\displaystyle\Big[p_{1},\ldots,p_{k},f_{k+1}\circ(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k-1},\widetilde{f}_{k},p_{k+1},\ldots,p_{n}),\ldots,f_{n}\circ(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k-1},\widetilde{f}_{k},p_{k+1},\ldots,p_{n})\Big]\circ

\displaystyle(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k-1},f_{k},p_{k+1},\ldots,p_{n}).

Second factor
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k-1},f_{k},p_{k+1},\ldots,p_{n})\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\prod_{\hat{k}}}^{G}({\bf X})
by Lemma
3.2
.
Again,

\displaystyle(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k},f_{k+1},\ldots,f_{n})\circ(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k-1},\widetilde{f}_{k},p_{k+1},\ldots,p_{n})=

\displaystyle\Big[p_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{f}_{k},f_{k+1}\circ(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k-1},\widetilde{f}_{k},p_{k+1},\ldots,p_{n}),\ldots,f_{n}\circ(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k-1},\widetilde{f}_{k},p_{k+1},\ldots,p_{n})\Big]

\operatorname{Aut}^{G}({\bf X}).

\Big[p_{1},\ldots,p_{k},f_{k+1}\circ(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k-1},\widetilde{f}_{k},p_{k+1},\ldots,p_{n}),\ldots,f_{n}\circ(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k-1},\widetilde{f}_{k},p_{k+1},\ldots,p_{n})\Big]

(d) we can say that

is identified with

∎

4.
LU-factorization

In the previous section we have described a factorization of
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X_{1}\times\ldots\times X_{n})
which is symmetric. However, the number of factors in the decomposition increases with the number of factors in the topological product, which is inconvenient. In this section we give a different factorisation with always only two factors, called
LU
factorisation. It is closely related
LU
decomposition of matrices. We begin by introducing some notations. Define maps
u_{k}:{\bf X}\to{\bf X}
and
l_{k}:{\bf X}\to{\bf X}
on the product space
{\bf X}=X_{1}\times\ldots\times X_{n}
:

l_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}):=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},*,\ldots,*),~~u_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}):=(*,\ldots.*,x_{k},\ldots,x_{n}).

Note that

l_{j}\circ l_{k}=l_{k}\circ l_{j}=l_{\min\{j,k\}},~~u_{j}\circ u_{k}=u_{k}\circ u_{j}=u_{\max\{j,k\}}.

Let us define two subsets
L^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
and
U^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
by

L^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}):=\{f\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}({\bf X}):f_{k}=f_{k}\circ l_{k},~~1\leq k\leq n\},

U^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}):=\{f\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}({\bf X}):f_{k}=f_{k}\circ u_{k},~~f_{kk}=\operatorname{Id}_{X_{k}},~~1\leq k\leq n\}.

The induced endomorphism on homotopy
f_{\#i}^{H}\colon\pi_{i}(X_{1}^{H})\times\ldots\times\pi_{i}(X_{n}^{H})\to\pi_{i}(X_{1}^{H})\times\ldots\times\pi_{i}(X_{n}^{H})
can be represented as matrix form for all
H\leq G
,

M_{i}(f^{H}):=\begin{bmatrix}(f^{H}_{11})_{\#i}&(f^{H}_{12})_{\#i}&\ldots(f^{H}_{1n})_{\#i}\\
(f^{H}_{21})_{\#i}&(f^{H}_{22})_{\#i}&\ldots(f^{H}_{2n})_{\#i}\\
.\\
.\\
.\\
(f^{H}_{n1})_{\#i}&(f^{H}_{n2})_{\#i}&\ldots(f^{H}_{nn})_{\#i}\end{bmatrix}.

If
f\in L^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
, then

f_{kj}=f_{k}\circ\iota_{j}=f_{k}\circ l_{k}\circ\iota_{j}=0~~~~~\forall~~k<j~~~(\because l_{k}\circ\iota_{j}=0,\forall k<j).

Therefore the matrix can be written as:

M_{i}(f^{H})=\begin{bmatrix}(f^{H}_{11})_{\#i}&0&0&\ldots 0\\
(f^{H}_{21})_{\#i}&(f^{H}_{22})_{\#i}&0&\ldots 0\\
.\\
.\\
.\\
(f^{H}_{n1})_{\#i}&(f^{H}_{n2})_{\#i}&(f^{H}_{n3})_{\#i}&\ldots(f^{H}_{nn})_{\#i}\end{bmatrix}.

Hence the induced map of each element in
L^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
gives the lower triangular matrices on homotopy groups of every fixed point spaces.
Similarly, for
g\in U^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
we have,

g_{kj}=g_{k}\circ\iota_{j}=g_{k}\circ u_{k}\circ\iota_{j}=0~~~~~~\forall~~k>j,~~~~~(\because u_{k}\circ\iota_{j}=0,\forall~~k>j).

Therefore the matrix can be written as:

M_{i}(g^{H}):=\begin{bmatrix}1&(g^{H}_{12})_{\#i}&(g^{H}_{13})_{\#i}&\ldots(g^{H}_{1n})_{\#i}\\
0&1&(g^{H}_{23})_{\#i}&\ldots(g^{H}_{2n})_{\#i}\\
.\\
.\\
.\\
0&0&0&\ldots 1\end{bmatrix}.

Hence induced map of each element in
U^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
gives upper triangular matrices with identity automorphisms on the diagonal entries on homotopy groups of every fixed point spaces.

As in the non-equivariant case, we have the following chain of inclusions:

L^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\subseteq L^{G}(X_{1}\times X_{2},X_{3},\ldots,X_{n})\subseteq\ldots\subseteq L^{G}({\bf X})=\operatorname{Aut}^{G}({\bf X}),

\operatorname{Aut}^{G}({\bf X})\supseteq U^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\supseteq U^{G}(X_{1}\times X_{2},X_{3},\ldots,X_{n})\supseteq\ldots\supseteq U^{G}({\bf X})=\{1\}.

Now we show that these two subsets are actually the subgroups of
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}({\bf X})
.

4.1 Proposition
.

Assume that all
G
-self-homotopy equivalences of
{\bf X}
are
G
-reducible. Then
L^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
and
U^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
are subgroups of
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}({\bf X})
.

Proof.

(a) We first consider the case of
L^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
. Let
f,g\in L^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
. Then
p_{j}\circ f=f_{j}=f_{j}\circ l_{j};~p_{j}\circ g=g_{j}=g_{j}\circ l_{j}
.

For any
j\leq k
, we know that
l_{j}\circ l_{k}=l_{k}\circ l_{j}=l_{j}
.
Now,

\displaystyle l_{k}\circ g\circ l_{k}

\displaystyle=l_{k}\circ(g_{1},\ldots,g_{k},\ldots,g_{n})\circ l_{k}=l_{k}\circ(g_{1}\circ l_{k},\ldots,g_{k}\circ l_{k},\ldots,g_{n}\circ l_{k})

\displaystyle=(g_{1}\circ l_{k},\ldots,g_{k}\circ l_{k},*,\ldots,*)=(g_{1}\circ l_{1}\circ l_{k},\ldots,g_{k}\circ l_{k}\circ l_{k},*,\ldots,*)

\displaystyle=(g_{1}\circ l_{1},\ldots,g_{k}\circ l_{k},*,\ldots,*)=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{k},*,\ldots,*)=l_{k}\circ g.

\displaystyle(\because l_{j}\circ l_{k}=l_{\min\{j,k\}})

So,

p_{k}\circ(f\circ g)=p_{k}\circ f\circ l_{k}\circ g=p_{k}\circ f\circ l_{k}\circ g\circ l_{k}=p_{k}\circ f\circ g\circ l_{k}=p_{k}\circ(f\circ g)\circ l_{k}.

Hence
f,g\in L^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
implies
f\circ g\in L^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
.
To prove
L^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
is closed under formation of inverses, we proceed by induction. Let us first consider
L^{G}(X_{1},X_{2})
. We can decompose an element
f\in L^{G}(X_{1},X_{2})
as

f=(f_{11}\circ p_{1},p_{2})\circ[(f_{11}\circ p_{1},p_{2})^{-1}\circ f].

We split the proof into several claims.

Claim I:

(f_{11}\circ p_{1},p_{2})^{-1}=(f^{-1}_{11}\circ p_{1},p_{2})
.

Observe that,

\displaystyle(f_{11}\circ p_{1},p_{2})\circ(f^{-1}_{11}\circ p_{1},p_{2})

\displaystyle=[f_{11}\circ p_{1}\circ(f^{-1}_{11}\circ p_{1},p_{2}),p_{2}]=[f_{11}\circ p_{1}(f^{-1}_{11}\circ p_{1},p_{2}),p_{2}]

\displaystyle=(f_{11}\circ f^{-1}_{11}\circ p_{1},p_{2})=(p_{1},p_{2})=\operatorname{Id}_{X_{1}\times X_{2}}.

Similarly,
(f^{-1}_{11}\circ p_{1},p_{2})\circ(f_{11}\circ p_{1},p_{2})=\operatorname{Id}_{X_{1}\times X_{2}}
. Hence the claim is true.

Since
f\in L^{G}(X_{1},X_{2})
we have
(f_{11}\circ p_{1},p_{2})\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X_{1}\times X_{2})
, by using
G
-reducibility and Proposition
2.4
. Therefore

(f^{-1}_{11}\circ p_{1},p_{2})=(f_{11}\circ p_{1},p_{2})^{-1}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X_{1}\times X_{2}).

Again,
f^{-1}_{11}\circ p_{1}=f^{-1}_{11}\circ p_{1}\circ l_{1},~~~~p_{2}=p_{2}\circ l_{2}
.
Therefore
(f^{-1}_{11}\circ p_{1},p_{2})\in L^{G}(X_{1},X_{2}),
which imply again
(f^{-1}_{11}\circ p_{1},p_{2})\circ f\in L^{G}(X_{1},X_{2})
.
Since,

\displaystyle p_{1}\circ(f^{-1}_{11}\circ p_{1},p_{2})\circ f

\displaystyle=p_{1}\circ(f^{-1}_{11}\circ p_{1},p_{2})\circ(f_{1},f_{2})=p_{1}\circ[f^{-1}_{11}\circ p_{1}(f_{1},f_{2}),f_{2}]

\displaystyle=p_{1}\circ(f^{-1}_{11}\circ f_{1},f_{2})=f^{-1}_{11}\circ f_{1}=f^{-1}_{11}\circ f_{11}=p_{1}.

\displaystyle~~~~~~(\because f_{1}=f_{1}\circ l_{1}=f_{1}\circ\iota_{1}).

Therefore
(f^{-1}_{11}\circ p_{1},p_{2})\circ f\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{X_{1}}(X_{1}\times X_{2})\subseteq L^{G}(X_{1}\times X_{2})
.
Since
\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{X_{1}}(X_{1}\times X_{2})
is a group and
(f^{-1}_{11}\circ p_{1},p_{2})\circ f\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{X_{1}}(X_{1}\times X_{2}),
we have,

\displaystyle\Rightarrow f^{-1}\circ(f^{-1}_{11}\circ p_{1},p_{2})^{-1}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{G}_{X_{1}}(X_{1}\times X_{2})\subseteq L^{G}(X_{1},X_{2})

\displaystyle\Rightarrow f^{-1}\circ(f^{-1}_{11}\circ p_{1},p_{2})^{-1}\circ(f^{-1}_{11}\circ p_{1},p_{2})\in L^{G}(X_{1},X_{2})

\displaystyle\Rightarrow f^{-1}\in L^{G}(X_{1},X_{2}).

Hence
L^{G}(X_{1},X_{2})
becomes a group.

Now let
f\in L^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\subseteq L^{G}(X_{1}\times X_{2},\ldots,X_{n})
, we can assume inductively that
f^{-1}\in L^{G}(X_{1}\times X_{2},\ldots,X_{n})
.
Then we get the conditions

p_{j}\circ f^{-1}=p_{j}\circ f^{-1}\circ l_{j},~~~2\leq j\leq n.

So it remains to prove that
p_{1}\circ f^{-1}=p_{1}\circ f^{-1}\circ l_{1}
, which is equivalent to show that
p_{12}\circ f^{-1}\circ\iota_{12}\in L^{G}(X_{1},X_{2})
. This is because,

\displaystyle p^{\prime}_{1}\circ(p_{12}\circ f^{-1}\circ\iota_{12})=p^{\prime}_{1}\circ(p_{12}\circ f^{-1}\circ\iota_{12})\circ l^{\prime}_{1}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle p^{\prime}_{1}\circ p_{12}\circ f^{-1}\circ\iota_{12}=p_{1}\circ f^{-1}\circ l_{1}\circ\iota_{12}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle p^{\prime}_{1}\circ p_{12}\circ f^{-1}\circ\iota_{12}\circ p_{12}=p_{1}\circ f^{-1}\circ l_{1}\circ\iota_{12}\circ p_{12}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle p^{\prime}_{1}\circ p_{12}\circ f^{-1}\circ l_{12}=p_{1}\circ f^{-1}\circ l_{1}\circ l_{12}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle p^{\prime}_{1}\circ p_{12}\circ f^{-1}=p_{1}\circ f^{-1}\circ l_{1},~~~~~(\because f^{-1}\in L^{G}(X_{1}\times X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}))

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle p_{1}\circ f^{-1}=p_{1}\circ f^{-1}\circ l_{1}.

See the commutative diagrams:

\textstyle{X_{1}\times\ldots\times X_{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

f\mapsto(f_{11},\ldots,f_{nn}).

Now,

\displaystyle(f\circ g)_{jj}

\displaystyle=p_{j}\circ(f\circ g)\circ\iota_{j}

\displaystyle=p_{j}\circ f\circ(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})\circ\iota_{j}

\displaystyle=p_{j}\circ f\circ(g_{1}\circ l_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\circ l_{n})\circ\iota_{j}

\displaystyle=p_{j}\circ f\circ(g_{1}\circ l_{1}\circ i_{j},\ldots,g_{n}\circ l_{n}\circ\iota_{j})

\displaystyle=p_{j}\circ f\circ(*,\ldots,*,g_{j}\circ\iota_{j},\ldots,g_{n}\circ\iota_{j})~~~(\because l_{k}\circ\iota_{j}=*,\forall k<j)

\displaystyle=f_{j}\circ(*,\ldots,*,g_{j}\circ\iota_{j},\ldots,g_{n}\circ\iota_{j})

\displaystyle=f_{j}\circ l_{j}\circ(*,\ldots,*,g_{j}\circ\iota_{j},\ldots,g_{n}\circ\iota_{j})

\displaystyle=f_{j}\circ(*,\ldots,*,g_{j}\circ\iota_{j},*,\ldots,*)

\displaystyle=f_{j}\circ\iota_{j}\circ g_{jj}=f_{jj}\circ g_{jj}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\varphi(f\circ g)=((f\circ g)_{11},\ldots,(f\circ g)_{nn})=(f_{11}\circ g_{11},\ldots,f_{nn}\circ g_{nn})

\displaystyle=(f_{11},\ldots,f_{nn})\circ(g_{11},\ldots,g_{nn})=\varphi(f)\circ\varphi(g).

So,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ker}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\big\{f\in L^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})|~~f_{jj}=\operatorname{Id}_{X_{j}},~\forall~~1\leq j\leq n\big\}

\displaystyle=\bar{L}^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}).

\Psi:\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X_{1})\times\ldots\times\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X_{n})\to L^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})

\Psi(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}):=(g_{1}\circ p_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\circ p_{n}).

L^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\cong(\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X_{1})\times\ldots\times\operatorname{Aut}^{G}(X_{n}))\rtimes\bar{L}^{G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})

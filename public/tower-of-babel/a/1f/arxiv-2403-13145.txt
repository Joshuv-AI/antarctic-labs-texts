Loops in de Sitter space To Jacques Bros and Michel Gaudin. In loving memory
Sergio L. Cacciatori, Henri Epstein, Ugo Moschella

Abstract
We discuss general one and two-loops banana diagrams with arbitrary masses on the de Sitter spacetime by using direct methods of dS quantum field theory in the dimensional regularization approach. In the one-loop case we also compute the effective potential for an O ⁡ ( N ) O(N) model in d = 4 d=4 dimension as an explicit function of the cosmological constant Λ \Lambda , both exactly and perturbatively up to order Λ \Lambda . For the two-loop case we show that the calculation is made easy thanks to a remarkable Källén-Lehmann formula that has been in the literature for a while. We discuss the divergent cases at d = 3 d=3 using a contiguity formula for generalized hypergeometric functions and we extract the dominant term at d = 4 d=4 proving a general formula to deal with a divergent hypergeometric series.

Introduction

General Relativity and the Standard Model of Particle Physics are the greatest successes of theoretical physics and are among the highest achievements of mankind.
Starting from the researches of the pre-Socratic philosophers of the Greek world of the VI century B.C., it took almost three millennia to reach this level of comprehension of Nature
[
1

On its side, General Relativity offers the natural framework for gravity and cosmology, not only by providing equations that plausibly describe the evolution of the universe and of the objects that populate it, but also as a source of ideas and technical tools for improving and sometimes changing the way we observe and measure the sky;
examples are the already mature gravitational lensing and microlensing techniques
and the brand new multi-messenger astronomy.
On the other side, the Standard Model gives a deep understanding of the microscopic world and there is no need to recall its great successes here.

The mysterious fact remains that all attempts to construct a single coherent theory that includes both of them have consistently failed.
Nevertheless, cosmology, astrophysics and the physics of elementary particles are nowadays inextricably interconnected and even an incomplete understanding of the history and the dynamics of the universe requires some form of coexistence of the two theories. At the moment, the best thing that is available is Quantum Field Theory (QFT) on a curved background, possibly including a quantum linearized gravitational field and backreaction.

QFT in an inertial frame of flat Minkowski space is quite well understood, mainly at the perturbative level but non-perturbative methods have also been developed over the years. The necessity of improving our computational skills for a deeper understanding of the Standard Model has recently generated a great flurry of activity; new strategies for computing Feynman-like integrals in QFT and GR (especially for gravitational waves) are abundant in contemporary literature.

\displaystyle-(\nu+\mu)(\nu-\mu+1)(1-z^{2})^{\scriptstyle{1\over 2}}{\bf P}_{\nu}^{\mu-1}(z){\bf P}_{\sigma}^{\mu}(z)\Big]_{a}^{b}

(3.49)

and evaluate the r.h.s. of this equation under the

to evaluate the first term in the r.h.s. of (

\nu

Since
0<\mu<1

(\sigma+\mu)(\sigma-\mu+1)(1-z^{2})^{\scriptstyle{1\over 2}}{\bf P}_{\nu}^{\mu}(z){\bf P}_{\sigma}^{\mu-1}(z)\sim C(1-z)^{{\scriptstyle{1\over 2}}-{\mu\over 2}-{\mu\over 2}+{\scriptstyle{1\over 2}}}=C(1-z)^{1-\mu}\rightarrow 0\ .

(3.50)

As

z\rightarrow-1

(

\displaystyle(\sigma+\mu)(\sigma-\mu+1)(1-z^{2})^{\scriptstyle{1\over 2}}{\bf P}_{\nu}^{\mu}(z){\bf P}_{\sigma}^{\mu-1}(z)\rightarrow{2\pi^{-1}\sin(\pi\nu)\Gamma(\mu)\Gamma(1-\mu)\over\Gamma(1+\sigma-\mu)\Gamma(-\sigma-\mu)}\ .

(3.51)

Putting together the contributions of the two terms in the rhs of

\int_{-1}^{1}{\bf P}_{\nu}^{\mu}(z){\bf P}_{\sigma}^{\mu}(z)\,dz=2\pi^{-1}\Gamma(\mu)\Gamma(1-\mu)\Phi_{{\bf P}}(\nu,\sigma,\mu),

(3.52)

\Phi_{{\bf P}}(\nu,\sigma,\mu)={{\sin(\pi\sigma)\over\Gamma(1+\nu-\mu)\Gamma(-\nu-\mu)}-{\sin(\pi\nu)\over\Gamma(1+\sigma-\mu)\Gamma(-\sigma-\mu)}\over(\nu+\sigma+1)(\nu-\sigma)}.

(3.53)

\Phi_{{\bf P}}(\nu,\sigma,\mu)

it is symmetric in

\sigma

Equation (

Combining (

Here are a few consequences of the above result:

1.

(3.54)

2.

In odd spacetime dimension the formula becomes
very simple; for instance at

(3.55)

3.

At

gives

\displaystyle I(\lambda,\nu,4)=-\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}(d-4)}+\frac{1-\gamma+\log(4\pi)}{16\pi^{2}}

(3.56)

\displaystyle-\frac{\left(4\lambda^{2}+1\right)\left(\psi\left(\frac{3}{2}-i\lambda\right)+\psi\left(\frac{3}{2}+i\lambda\right)\right)-\left(4\nu^{2}+1\right)\left(\psi\left(\frac{3}{2}-i\nu\right)+\psi\left(\frac{3}{2}+i\nu\right)\right)}{64\pi^{2}\left(\lambda^{2}-\nu^{2}\right)}

(3.57)

\displaystyle+\ {\rm O}(d-4).

(3.58)

4

The
O(N)
model: a summary

Let
E\cong\mathbb{R}^{N}
be the
N
dimensional Euclidean vector space with the standard scalar product denoted by
\langle\cdot\mid\cdot\rangle
and
O(N)
the corresponding orthogonal group. Let us consider a scalar multi-component field

\displaystyle\phi:S_{d}\longrightarrow E,

(4.59)

where
S_{d}
is the (Euclidean) de Sitter sphere of radius
R
. The radius
R
and the cosmological constant

\Lambda=\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2R^{2}}

(4.60)

will reappear when necessary (see. Sect. (
6
)). For the moment we take
R=1
.

The action for
\phi
is the quartic
O(N)
-invariant action

\displaystyle S[\phi]=\int_{S_{d}}\Big[\Lambda_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\langle\partial_{\mu}\phi\mid\partial^{\mu}\phi\rangle+\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{2}\langle\phi\mid\phi\rangle+\frac{c_{0}}{4}\langle\phi\mid\phi\rangle^{2}\Big]\sqrt{g}\,d^{d}x,

(4.61)

where
m_{0}
and
c_{0}
are respectively the bare mass and bare self-coupling constant;
x^{\mu}
are local coordinates on the sphere. We included also an extra cosmological constant
\Lambda_{0}
for convenience when renormalizing.

We will compute the effective potential at
d=4
for the constant configuration

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}\equiv\varphi\,e_{0},

(4.62)

where
\varphi
is a real constant and
e_{0}
is a given vector of norm
1
in
E
. Of course a nonzero expectation value of
\phi
breaks the symmetry down to
O(N-1)
. After choosing any orthonormal basis
\{e_{j}\}_{j=0}^{N-1}
of
E
whose first element is
e_{0}
, we may write

\displaystyle\phi=(\varphi+\psi_{0})e_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\psi_{j}e_{j},

(4.63)

so that

\displaystyle S[\phi]=

\displaystyle\int_{S_{d}}\Big[\Lambda_{0}+\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{2}\varphi^{2}+\frac{c_{0}}{4}\varphi^{4}\Big]\sqrt{g}\,d^{d}x+\sum_{a=1}^{4}S_{a}[\psi;\varphi],

(4.64)

where

\displaystyle S_{1}[\psi;\varphi]=

\displaystyle\int_{S_{d}}\Big[{m_{0}^{2}}\varphi\psi_{0}+{c_{0}}\varphi^{3}\psi_{0}\Big]\sqrt{g}\,d^{d}x,

(4.65)

\displaystyle S_{2}[\psi;\varphi]=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{S_{d}}\Big[\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\partial_{\mu}\psi_{j}\partial^{\mu}\psi_{j}+(m_{0}^{2}+3c_{0}\varphi^{2})\psi_{0}^{2}+(m_{0}^{2}+c_{0}\varphi^{2})\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\psi_{j}^{2}\Big]\sqrt{g}\,d^{d}x,

(4.66)

\displaystyle S_{3}[\psi;\varphi]=

\displaystyle\int_{S_{d}}\Big[c_{0}\varphi\psi_{0}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\psi_{j}^{2}\Big]\,d^{d}x,

(4.67)

\displaystyle S_{4}[\psi;\varphi]=

\displaystyle\frac{c_{0}}{4}\int_{S_{d}}\sqrt{g}\Big[\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\psi_{j}^{2}\Big]^{2}\sqrt{g}\,d^{d}x.

(4.68)

The effective potential
{\cal V}(\varphi)
is defined as follows

\displaystyle\exp(-\Omega_{d}{\cal V}(\varphi))=\int[\prod_{j}D\psi_{j}]\exp(-S[\phi]);

(4.69)

here
\Omega_{d}
is the volume of the Euclidean de Sitter spacetime (which is finite) and
[\prod_{j}D\psi_{j}]
is the formal path integral measure.
By construction,
S_{1}
does not contribute to the effective potential; at 1-loop, we get

\displaystyle{\cal V}(\varphi)=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}+\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{2}\varphi^{2}+\frac{c_{0}}{4}\varphi^{4}-\frac{1}{\Omega_{d}}\log\int[\prod_{j}D\psi_{j}]\exp(-S_{2}[\phi]),

(4.70)

which we rewrite in the standard form:

\displaystyle{\cal V}(\varphi)=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}+\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{2}\varphi^{2}+\frac{c_{0}}{4}\varphi^{4}+{\cal V}_{0}(\varphi)+(N-1){\cal V}_{1}(\varphi),

(4.71)

where, for
a=0,1
,

\displaystyle{\cal V}_{a}(\varphi)=-\frac{1}{\Omega_{d}}\log\int[D\Phi]\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M_{d}}\sqrt{g}\Big[\partial_{\mu}\Phi\partial^{\mu}\Phi+M_{a}^{2}(\varphi)\Phi^{2}\Big]d^{d}x\right),

(4.72)

\displaystyle M_{0}^{2}(\varphi)=m_{0}^{2}+3c_{0}\varphi^{2},

(4.73)

\displaystyle M_{1}^{2}(\varphi)=m_{0}^{2}+c_{0}\varphi^{2}.

(4.74)

Several techniques can be used to compute
{\cal V}_{a}(\varphi)
.
Instead of looking at it as a function of
\varphi
, it is useful to see it as a function of
M_{a}^{2}\equiv z_{a}
.
By taking the first derivative w.r.t.
z
we get the tadpole integral which leads to the Lee-Sciaccaluga equation for determining the effective potential
[
16
]
.

When we differentiate twice, we get the one mass bubble integral; this is the strategy we want to adopt, since it will allow us to develop methods that will be helpful to compute the two-loop contribution (a research that will be considered elsewhere).
More precisely, if
J(m^{2}_{1},m^{2}_{2})
is the two-masses bubble, one has

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}{\cal V}(\varphi)=-\frac{1}{2}J(z,z),

(4.75)

from which we can recover the effective potential. As we will review in Sec.
6
, in the flat case and
d=4-2\epsilon
, we have

\displaystyle J(z,z)=\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\log\frac{z}{4\pi e^{\gamma}}\right)+O(\epsilon).

(4.76)

After integrating twice in
z
and renormalising, we get the standard result
[
17
,
9
]

\displaystyle{\cal V}(\varphi)=

\displaystyle\frac{m^{2}}{2}\varphi^{2}+\frac{c_{g}}{4}\varphi^{4}+\frac{(m^{2}+3c_{g}\varphi^{2})^{2}}{64\pi^{2}}\log\frac{m^{2}+3c_{g}\varphi^{2}}{\mu^{2}}

(4.77)

\displaystyle+(N-1)\frac{(m^{2}+c_{g}\varphi^{2})^{2}}{64\pi^{2}}\log\frac{m^{2}+c_{g}\varphi^{2}}{\mu^{2}},

(4.78)

where
m
and
c_{g}
are the renormalised mass and coupling constant, and
\mu^{2}
is a scale related to the precise renormalisation conditions. We will now extend this calculation to the de Sitter case.

Notice that

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int dy\ y^{2}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2n-1}{(n(n-1)+y)^{2}}-\frac{1}{y}\right),

where in the second line we have used the series expansion (

By using the standard Abel-Plana’s formula
[

]

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2n-1}{(n(n-1)+y)^{2}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2y^{2}}-4\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t}{e^{2\pi t}-1}\frac{(t^{2}-y)^{2}-y}{[(t^{2}-y)^{2}+t^{2}]^{2}},

(5.91)

\displaystyle B(y)=

\displaystyle\frac{y}{3}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{2t^{4}+t^{2}-1+y(4-2t^{2})}{(t^{2}-y)^{2}+t^{2}}\frac{t^{3}dt}{e^{2\pi t}-1}+6\int_{0}^{\infty}\arctan\left(t-\frac{y}{t}\right)\frac{t^{2}dt}{e^{2\pi t}-1}

(5.96)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{64\pi^{2}}\left[(m_{R}^{2}+3c_{g}\varphi_{R}^{2}-2)^{2}+(N-1)(m_{R}^{2}+c_{g}\varphi_{R}^{2}-2)^{2}\right]\log{\mu_{R}^{2}}.

(5.97)

\displaystyle\nu_{0}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{m_{R}^{2}+3c_{g}\varphi_{R}^{2}-\frac{9}{4}},

(5.98)

\displaystyle\nu_{1}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{m_{R}^{2}+c_{g}\varphi_{R}^{2}-\frac{9}{4}}.

6

Flat limit

Before discussing the flat limit let us recall that in flat space space the Schwinger function is proportional to a MacDonald function:

\displaystyle G_{m}(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int\frac{e^{-ipx}}{{p^{2}+m^{2}}}dp=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\left(\frac{r}{m}\right)^{1-\frac{d}{2}}K_{\frac{d}{2}-1}\left(mr\right),\ \ \ r=\sqrt{x^{2}}.

(6.103)

An easy computation in
x
-space
[
2
]
gives the textbook answer for the bubble:

\displaystyle\int G_{m_{1}}(x)G_{m_{2}}(x)dx=\frac{\Gamma\left(1-{\frac{d}{2}}\right)}{(4\pi)^{{\frac{d}{2}}}}\frac{m_{2}^{{d}-2}-m_{1}^{{d}-2}}{m_{1}^{2}-m_{2}^{2}}.

(6.104)

which, when
d=2
, reduces to

I(m_{1},m_{2},2)=\frac{\log(m_{1})-\log(m_{2})}{2\pi(m_{1}^{2}-m_{2}^{2})}.

(6.105)

To study the flat limit of Eq. (
3.45
), we have to restore a generic de Sitter radius
R
and rescale the masses accordingly. When
m>0
and
R\rightarrow+\infty
, by using the Stirling formula

\left|\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{d-1}{2}+iRm\right)}{\Gamma\left({\frac{1}{2}}+iRm\right)}\right|^{2}\sim(Rm)^{d-2}\ .

(6.106)

Hence,

I(Rm_{1},Rm_{2},d)\sim-R^{d-4}\frac{\Gamma\left(1-{\frac{d}{2}}\right)(m_{1}^{d-2}-m_{2}^{d-2})}{2^{d}\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}(m_{2}^{2}-m_{1}^{2})}\ \ \ (R\rightarrow+\infty)\ .

(6.107)

This fits exactly with (
6.104
).

Once more, we would like to draw the attention of the reader on the non triviality of this result as it expresses the flat limit of an integrated quantity as opposed to the asymptotic properties of the correlation functions which are merely local properties.

Even more interestingly, the same nontrivial result holds true for the effective potential. To show it, we need to compute the behaviour of
B(\nu)
for

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{k}dt}{e^{2\pi t}-1}=\frac{k!}{(2\pi)^{k+1}}\zeta(k+1).

(6.108)

Therefore,

\displaystyle B\left(\nu^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right)=\frac{1}{3}\nu^{2}-\frac{1}{15}\log\left(\nu^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right)+\frac{1}{12}-\frac{3\zeta(3)}{4\pi^{3}}+\ldots,

(6.109)

where the dots stay for terms vanishing for

On the other hand, for

where

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\nu^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right)^{2}\left(\psi\left(-\frac{1}{2}-i\nu\right)+\psi\left(-\frac{1}{2}+i\nu\right)\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\nu^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right)^{2}\log\left(\nu^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right)

(6.111)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{3}\nu^{2}+\frac{1}{20}+\ldots.

(6.112)

\displaystyle\frac{m_{R}^{2}}{2}\varphi_{R}^{2}+\frac{c_{g}}{4}\varphi_{R}^{4}+\frac{1}{32\pi^{2}}\Bigg[\frac{(m_{R}^{2}+3c_{g}\varphi_{R}^{2}-2)^{2}}{2}\log\frac{m_{R}^{2}+3c_{g}\varphi_{R}^{2}-2}{\mu_{R}^{2}}

(6.113)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{3}(m_{R}^{2}+3c_{g}\varphi_{R}^{2}-2)-\frac{1}{15}\log(m_{R}^{2}+3c_{g}\varphi_{R}^{2}-2)-\frac{1}{30}-\frac{3\zeta(3)}{4\pi^{3}}+\ldots\Bigg]

(6.114)

\displaystyle+\frac{N-1}{32\pi^{2}}\Bigg[\frac{(m_{R}^{2}+c_{g}\varphi_{R}^{2}-2)^{2}}{2}\log\frac{m_{R}^{2}+c_{g}\varphi_{R}^{2}-2}{\mu_{R}^{2}}

(6.115)

Conclusions

In a recent paper we have shown that calculating loop integrals in position space may be advantageous also in flat Minkowski space from several viewpoints. While we believe that that possibility in flat space deserves attention and is not just a luxury or a mathematical ornament, in curved space performing loop calculation in position space is compulsory. Here we have started this program in de Sitter space. Calculations are significantly more intricate than in flat space but can be performed successfully till the end. In a companion paper we present a study of the same diagrams in anti de Sitter space. We have found that in the AdS case things are a little simpler than in the present de Sitterian study because of the presence of a true spectral condition. The two papers together open a new way for precision calculations of QFT in the presence of a cosmological constant.

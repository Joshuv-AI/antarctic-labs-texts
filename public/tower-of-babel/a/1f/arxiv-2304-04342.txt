Unique continuation for Robin problems with non-smooth potentials
Zongyuan Li

Abstract
. In this work, we study the unique continuation properties of Robin boundary value problems with Robin potentials η ∈ L d − 1 + ε \eta\in L_{d-1+\varepsilon} . Our results generalize earlier ones in which η \eta was assumed to be either zero (Neumann problem) or differentiable.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
35J25, 35A02, 31B20

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the unique continuation properties of second-order divergence form elliptic equations with Robin boundary conditions

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}u:=D_{i}(a_{ij}D_{j}u+b_{i}u)+W_{i}D_{i}u+Vu=0\,\,

\displaystyle\text{in}\,\,\Omega\cap B_{1},

(1.1b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}u:=(a_{ij}D_{j}u+b_{i}u)\boldsymbol{n}_{i}=\eta u\,\,

\displaystyle\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega\cap B_{1}.

Here,
\boldsymbol{n}
is the unit outward normal direction. Throughout the paper, for variable coefficient operators, we assume

a_{ij}=a_{ji}\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda I_{d}\leq(a_{ij})_{d\times d}\leq\Lambda I_{d}

for some
0<\lambda<\Lambda<\infty
.
Our main result reads

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a
C^{1,1}
domain,
d\geq 2
. Suppose
u\in H^{1}(\Omega\cap B_{1})
is non-trivial and solves (
1.1a
)-(
1.1b
) with coefficients satisfying
a_{ij}\in C^{0,1}(\Omega)
and

(1.2)

\begin{split}&|b_{i}|+|W_{i}|\in L_{p,loc}(\Omega),\quad V\in L_{q,loc}(\Omega),\quad\text{and}\quad\eta\in L_{s,loc}(\partial\Omega)\\
&\text{with}\quad p>d,\quad q>d/2,\quad\text{and}\quad s>d-1.\end{split}

Then we have the following.

(a)

For every
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}\cap B_{1}
,
u
can at most vanish at a finite order, i.e., strong unique continuation property (SUCP) holds.

(b)

The set
\{u=0\}\cap\partial\Omega\cap B_{1}
is of Hausdorff dimension not exceeding
(d-2)
. When
d=2
, for each
\rho\in(0,1)
, the set
\{u=0\}\cap\partial\Omega\cap B_{\rho}
contains at most finitely many points.

As usual, we say that
u\in H^{1}_{loc}
solves (
1.1a
)-(
1.1b
) in the weak sense, if for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\overline{\Omega}\cap B_{1})
,

\int_{\Omega\cap B_{1}}-(a_{ij}D_{j}u+b_{i}u)D_{i}\varphi+(W_{i}D_{i}u+Vu)\varphi+\int_{\partial\Omega\cap B_{1}}\eta u\varphi=0.

By De Giorgi’s estimate (see the end of Section
2
), any
H^{1}_{loc}
weak solution is also Hölder continuous up to the boundary. Hence, it makes sense to discuss the Hausdorff dimension of the set
\{u=0\}\cap\partial\Omega
.

Remark 1.2
.

Our condition (
1.2
) is slightly scaling subcritical under
u\mapsto u_{\lambda}=u(\lambda\cdot)
, which we believe to be close to sharp. It would be interesting to construct the following counterexample: a non-trivial
u\in H^{1}_{loc}
that locally satisfies

\Delta u=0\,\,\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},\quad\partial u/\partial\boldsymbol{n}=\eta u\,\,\text{on}\,\,\partial\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}

with some
\eta\in L_{d-1-\varepsilon,loc}
. However, the set
\{u=0\}\cap\partial\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
has a positive surface measure. From the discussion in
[
1
]
, (SUCP) fails at every Lebesgue density point of
\{u=0\}\cap\partial\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
.

In application, the study of unique continuation properties of (
1.1a
) - (
1.1b
) is relevant to inverse problems that arise in non-destructive testing of materials and in modelling phenomena such as surface corrosion in metals. Typically corrosion on a surface is modeled by the Robin coefficient
\eta
— the ratio between the voltage (
u
) and the current (
\partial u/\partial\boldsymbol{n}
). Then, the goal in these applications is to recover Robin coefficients on a subset of the boundary by measuring the Cauchy data on the complementary subset. For examples of such studies, see
[
4
]
,
[
8
]
, and
[
22
]
.
After the discussions on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\eta\in L_{\infty}
in
[
4
]
, the following question was raised in
[
4
, Remark 4.1]
for higher dimensions:
let
u
be harmonic in
B_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 3
, satisfying

(1.3)

0\leq\frac{\partial u}{\partial\boldsymbol{n}}\big/u\leq C\,\,\text{on a relatively open subset}\,\,\Sigma\subset\partial B_{1}.

If the surface measure
\sigma(\{u=0\}\cap\Sigma)
is positive, can we conclude that
u\equiv 0
?
By defining
\eta=\frac{\partial u}{\partial\boldsymbol{n}}/u
on
\Sigma
, (
1.3
) can be rewritten in the form of (
1.1b
) with a non-negative and bounded
\eta
. Our Theorem
1.1
(b) answers this question in a more general manner — the Robin coefficient
\eta=\frac{\partial u}{\partial\boldsymbol{n}}\big/u
is allowed to be sign-changing and unbounded, and the set
\{u=0\}\cap\Sigma
is permitted to be of lower dimension.

Let us recall some history of studying unique continuation properties of second-order elliptic PDEs. Near an interior point, Koch and Tataru
[
14
]
proved the (SUCP) for (
1.1a
),
d\geq 3
, with
b_{i},W_{i}
in spaces slightly smaller than
L_{d}
and
V
in spaces slightly larger than
L_{d/2}
. Similar result when
d=2
was proved by Alessandrini in
[
3
]
. The sharpness of these requirements on the coefficients is demonstrated by several counterexamples.
At the boundary, besides (SUCP), there is also interest in Cauchy-type uniqueness problems — specifically, for a non-trivial solution
u
, researchers aim to determine the largest possible size of the zero Cauchy data set
\{u=\partial u/\partial\boldsymbol{n}=0\}\cap\partial\Omega
.
In the context of Dirichlet problem, i.e., (
1.1a
) with
u=0
on
\partial\Omega\cap B_{1}
, this is to bound the size of
E_{D}:=\{\partial u/\partial\boldsymbol{n}=0\}\cap\partial\Omega
. In
[
17
]
, Lin proved (SUCP) when
b_{i}=0
,
W_{i},V\in L_{\infty}
, and
\partial\Omega\in C^{1,1}
, and further demonstrated that
\dim(E_{D})\leq d-2
. Lin’s results were generalized to convex,
C^{1,\alpha}
, and
C^{1,Dini}
domains in
[
2
,
1
,
15
]
. More recently, Tolsa
[
23
]
established for Laplace equations that if
\partial\Omega
is Lipschitz with a small constant, then
\sigma(E_{D})=0
. Quantitative results were also obtained by Kenig-Zhao and Logunov et. al. in
[
13
,
19
]
. It is conjectured that for Laplace equations, even if
\partial\Omega
is Lipschitz with a possibly large constant,
\sigma(E)=0
should still hold.

For Neumann or Robin problems (
1.1a
)-(
1.1b
), Cauchy-type uniqueness problems are concerned with the largest possible size of
E_{N}:=\{u=0\}\cap\partial\Omega
.
Particularly for Neumann problems (
\eta=0
), it was demonstrated in
[
1
]
that (SUCP) holds on
C^{1,1}
domains. Without much effort, this result can be extended to situations where
\eta\in C^{0,1}
, and it further proves that
\dim(E_{N})\leq d-2
. Further generalization to semilinear problems were explored in
[
9
]
, allowing for
\eta\in W^{1,1}
under a point-wise growth condition. Quantative results were also obtained for
\eta=0
in
[
5
]
, for
\eta\in C^{0,1}
in
[
16
]
, and for Robin eigenfunctions (i.e., constant
\eta
) in
[
24
]
.
It is worth mentioning that, in all these studies,
\eta
is assumed to be at least differentiable. Such differentiability is crucial for deriving the major tool in these papers — certain generalization of Almgren’s monotonicity formulas.

The major novelty of the current work lies in combining two techniques to replace the use of monotonicity formulas found in earlier studies, therefore allowing us to handle rough coefficients.
The first technique involves our construction of an auxiliary function that simplifies Robin problems into homogeneous conormal problems. Our auxiliary function differs from those used in
[
22
]
and
[
24
]
and enables us to prove (SUCP) for non-differentiable, sign-changing
\eta
. The second technique is a robust method for blowup analysis detailed in Section
3
. This method, originating from
[
18
]
in the context of homogenization, has been adapted and further developed here for handling boundary value problems.
As a by product, our blowup analysis leads to the following results, which can be of independent interest.

2.
Strong unique continuation property

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
(a). Since our coefficients verify conditions for the interior (SUCP) in
[
3
,
14
]
, it suffices to prove it at boundary points.
Take

\widetilde{\eta}=\begin{cases}\eta\,\,&\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega\cap B_{1},\\
-|\partial\Omega\setminus B_{1}|^{-1}\int_{\partial\Omega\cap B_{1}}\eta\,\,&\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega\setminus B_{1}.\end{cases}

Since
\widetilde{\eta}\in L_{s}(\partial\Omega)
with
s>1
and
\fint_{\partial\Omega}\widetilde{\eta}=0
, by Lemma
B.1
, we can find a unique weak solution
\Psi
to

D_{i}(a_{ij}D_{j}\Psi)=0\,\,\text{in}\,\,\Omega,\quad a_{ij}D_{j}\Psi\boldsymbol{n}_{i}=\widetilde{\eta}\,\,\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega,

and
\Psi\in W^{1}_{\widetilde{s}}
for every
\widetilde{s}<ds/(d-1)
. A calculation shows that
v:=ue^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}-}\Psi}
solves

(2.1)

\begin{cases}D_{i}(a_{ij}D_{j}v+b_{i}v)+\widehat{W_{i}}D_{i}v+\widehat{V}v=0\quad\text{in}\,\,\Omega\cap B_{1},\\
(a_{ij}D_{j}v+b_{i}v)\boldsymbol{n}_{i}=0\quad\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega\cap B_{1},\end{cases}

where

\widehat{W_{i}}:=W_{i}+a_{ij}D_{j}\Psi,\,\,\widehat{V}:=V+a_{ij}D_{i}\Psi D_{j}\Psi+b_{i}D_{i}\Psi+W_{i}D_{i}\Psi.

From (
1.2

D\Psi\in L_{\widetilde{s}}

\widehat{W_{i}}\in L_{\widehat{p}},
and
\widehat{V}\in L_{\widehat{q}}

\widehat{q}=\min\{q,\widetilde{s}/2,p\widetilde{s}/(p+\widetilde{s})\}>d/2
if we choose
\widetilde{s}
to be sufficiently close to

. Next, we apply the flattening and reflection technique in
[
1
]
to the homogeneous conormal problem (
2.1

such that in new coordinates

2.1

\begin{cases}D_{i}(\widetilde{a_{ij}}D_{j}\widetilde{v}\widetilde{b_{i}}\widetilde{v})+\widetilde{W_{i}}D_{i}\widetilde{v}+\widetilde{V}\widetilde{v}=0\quad\text{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\cap B_{1},\\

(\widetilde{a_{ij}})_{d\times d}=\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{A}_{d-1\times d-1}&0\\

That is, the conormal vector

\widetilde{v}(x^{\prime},x_{d})=\widetilde{v}(x^{\prime},-x_{d}),

\widetilde{a_{ij}}(x^{\prime},x_{d})=\begin{cases}\widetilde{a_{ij}}(x^{\prime},-x_{d})\,\,\text{when either}\,\,i,j\neq d\,\,\text{or}\,\,i=j=d,\\

and
\widetilde{V}(x^{\prime},x_{d})=\widetilde{V}(x^{\prime},-x_{d})
.
It is not difficult to check that the extended

is still in

(2.2)

3.
Blowup analysis

In this section, we discuss asymptotics for (
1.1a
) - (
1.1b
) at
0\in\partial\Omega
. Weaker than the requirement
a_{ij}\in C^{0,1}
in Theorem
1.1
, in this section we only assume

(3.1)

\fint_{\Omega\cap B_{r}}|a_{ij}-a_{ij}(0)|^{2}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad r\rightarrow 0.

For
A(0)=(a_{ij}(0))_{d\times d}
, let
\Psi_{A}
be the linear transformation with the matrix

(3.2)

\Psi_{A}=(\Theta A(0)\Theta^{T})^{1/2}\Theta,\quad\text{where}\,\,\Theta=\begin{pmatrix}I_{(d-1)\times(d-1)}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&\begin{matrix}-a_{1d}(0)/a_{dd}(0)\\
\cdots\\
-a_{d-1,d}(0)/a_{dd}(0)\end{matrix}\\
\hline\cr 0&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&1\end{pmatrix}

Such transformation
\Psi_{A}
will take
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, and map the conormal direction

to normal direction.
In the following lemma, we prove that any solution to (

)-(
1.1b
) with “scaling subcritical” coefficients either vanishs at an infinite order or has a unique limiting homogeneity.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\cap B_{1})
satisfy (
1.1a
)-(
1.1b
) on
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}=\{x_{d}>0\}
, with coefficients satisfying (

) and (

).
Then, we must have, as
r\rightarrow 0
,

\begin{split}\log_{2}\left(\fint_{\Psi_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\cap B_{2r})}|u|^{2}\big/\fint_{\Psi_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\cap B_{r})}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}&\text{either goes to infinity,}\\
&\text{or converges to some $m\in\mathbb{N}$.}\end{split}

Lemma
3.1
plays the role of monotonicity formulas in previous works, and significantly relax the smoothness requirements on coefficients. In particular, we have the (subsequence) convergence of Almgren’s blowup, under very weak assumptions on

and coefficients.

3.1.
Proof of Lemma

The idea of Lemma

Here, we adapt the proof in

Some other differences include: First, in

Denote

and

From (

We first prove the following
Claim

\mu\notin\mathbb{N}

\fint_{B_{2r_{k}}^{+}}|u|^{2}\big/\fint_{B_{r_{k}}^{+}}|u|^{2}\leq 4^{\mu},\quad\fint_{B_{2\tau_{k}r_{k}}^{+}}|u|^{2}\big/\fint_{B_{\tau_{k}r_{k}}^{+}}|u|^{2}>4^{\mu}.

Passing to a subsequence, we may assume

v_{k}(y):=u(r_{k}y)\big/(\fint_{B_{r_{k}}^{+}}|u|^{2})^{1/2}.

Then

\mathcal{L}_{k}v_{k}=0\,\,\text{in}\,\,B_{2}^{+},\quad\mathcal{B}_{k}v_{k}=r_{k}\eta(r_{k}y)v_{k}\,\,\text{on}\,\,\Gamma_{2}

\text{with}\quad\fint_{B_{1}^{+}}|v_{k}|^{2}=1\quad\text{and}\quad\fint_{B_{2}^{+}}|v_{k}|^{2}\leq 4^{\mu},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}v=D_{i}(a_{ij}(r_{k}y)D_{j}v+r_{k}b(r_{k}y)v)+r_{k}W(r_{k}y)\cdot Dv+r_{k}^{2}V(r_{k}y)v,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{k}v=(a_{ij}(r_{k}y)D_{j}v+r_{k}b(r_{k}y))\boldsymbol{n}_{i}v.

By the Caccioppoli inequality, we have
\|v_{k}\|_{H^{1}(B_{2-\varepsilon}^{+})}\leq C\|v_{k}\|_{L_{2}(B_{2}^{+})}\leq C
for every every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and large enough

. Here, we have used the fact that all the exponents given in (

) are scaling subcritical.
See, for instance
[
20

where an interior Caccioppoli inequality is proved when
\|b\|_{L_{d}}+\|W\|_{L_{d}}+\|V\|_{L_{d/2}}
is small enough. Here, we can make such (scaling invariant) norms small by choosing

large enough. The Robin potential
\eta
can be treated in a similar way.
Now, by a Sobolev embedding, a trace theorem, and a diagonal argument, passing to a subsequence which is still denoted as

, we have

v_{k}\rightarrow v_{\infty}\,\,\begin{cases}\text{weakly in}\,\,L_{2}(B_{2}^{+}),H^{1}(B_{2-\varepsilon}^{+}),L_{2^{*}(B_{2-\varepsilon}^{+})},\,\,\text{and}\,\,L_{2^{**}(\Gamma_{2-\varepsilon})},\\
\text{strongly in}\,\,L_{2}(B_{2-\varepsilon}^{+})\,\,\text{and}\,\,L_{2}(\Gamma_{2-\varepsilon}),\end{cases}\forall\varepsilon\in(0,1).

Here,

Now we derive the equation for

\begin{split}\int_{B_{2}^{+}}D_{i}v_{k}D_{i}\varphi&=\int_{B_{2}^{+}}\left((\delta_{ij}-a_{ij}(r_{k}y))D_{j}\varphi+r_{k}W_{i}(r_{k}y)\varphi\right)D_{i}v_{k}\\
&\quad+\int_{B_{2}^{+}}\left(-r_{k}b_{i}(r_{k}y)D_{i}\varphi+r_{k}^{2}V(r_{k}y)\varphi\right)v_{k}+\int_{\Gamma_{2}}r_{k}\eta(r_{k}y)\varphi v_{k}.\end{split}

From (

) and (

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2}^{+}}\left|(\delta_{ij}-a_{ij}(r_{k}y))\right|^{2}+\left|r_{k}W_{i}(r_{k}y)\right|^{2}+\left|r_{k}b_{i}(r_{k}y)\right|^{2}\,dy

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{2}^{+}}\left|r_{k}^{2}V(r_{k}y)\right|^{2_{*}}\,dy+\int_{\Gamma_{2}}|r_{k}\eta(r_{k}y)|^{2_{**}}\,d\sigma_{y}\rightarrow 0.

Here,

and

Hence, a standard weak-strong convergence argument yields that the right-hand side of (

\Delta v_{\infty}=0\,\,\text{in}\,\,B_{2}^{+},\quad\partial v_{\infty}/\partial\boldsymbol{n}=0\,\,\text{on}\,\,\Gamma_{2}.

Now, from (

, we must have

. This is a contradiction since we have assumed

Back to the proof of the lemma. Clearly, we only need to prove when

In particular,

for some

. Let

be a small number satisfying

. Applying the claim iteratively with

, we can easily deduce that

\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0}\log_{2}\left(\fint_{B_{2r}^{+}}|u|^{2}\big/\fint_{B_{r}^{+}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq M+\varepsilon.

Since

can be arbitrarily small, we immediately have

We are left to show that

Dimension reduction

In this section, we prove Theorem

(b) by Federer’s dimension reduction argument. By the flattening map in the reflection technique in
[
1
]
(see Section
2
), without loss of generality, we may assume
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the set formed by all the subsets
E\subset\Gamma_{1}(=\partial\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\cap B_{1})
, verifying the following: there exists a function
w\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\cap B_{1})
which is not constantly zero and solves (
1.1a
)-(
1.1b
) with
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
and coefficients satisfying

and (
1.2
), such that

We call such

a defining function of

. By a De Giorgi estimate,

. Hence,
E
is always well-defined and is a relative closed subset of
\Gamma_{1}
. Meanwhile,
E
cannot be the whole

by the simple unique continuation property (since

We verify the following two properties of

.

P.1. (Closure under appropriate scaling and translation)
: If

: For

,
\{\lambda_{k}\}\downarrow 0
, and

, there exists a subsequence
\{\lambda_{k^{\prime}}\}
and

, such that

and

for each

. Here, for

, the convergence

means

\forall\varepsilon>0,\exists k(\varepsilon)\,\,\text{such that}\,\,\Gamma_{1-\varepsilon}\cap E^{(k)}\subset\{x\in\Gamma_{1}:\operatorname{dist}(E^{(\infty)},x)<\varepsilon\},\quad\forall k>k(\varepsilon).

Once
P.1
and
P.2
are verified, the conclusion in Theorem
1.1
(b) follows immediately from

Lemma 4.1

(Theorem A.4 in
[
21
]
, Lemma 2.2 in
[
17
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a collection of relatively closed proper subsets of the unit ball in

that verifies

and

. Then, the Hausdorff dimension of each set in
\mathcal{F}
is no greater than

. When

,
E\cap[-\rho,\rho](\subset\mathbb{R}^{1})
is finite for every

and every

.

We are left to verify

and
P.2
.

Verifying

is straightforward. For the set

given above, let

be a defining function. By (SUCP) in Theorem

(a),

cannot be identically zero on any open subset of

. Hence,

.

Appendix A
Monotonicity and rigidity property of Neumann problems

For a function
v\in H^{1}
, define the doubling index and the (averaged) Almgren’s frequency function as

N(r):=\fint_{B_{2r}^{+}}|v|^{2}\big/\fint_{B_{r}^{+}}|v|^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad F(r):=\fint_{B_{r}^{+}}|\nabla v|^{2}(r^{2}-|x|^{2})\big/\fint_{B_{r}^{+}}|v|^{2}.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
v
be a non-trivial solution to

(A.1)

\begin{cases}\Delta v=0\quad&\text{in}\,\,B_{2}^{+},\\
\partial v/\partial\boldsymbol{n}=0\quad&\text{on}\,\,\Gamma_{2}.\end{cases}

Then both
F
and
N
are non-decreasing for
r\in(0,2)
. Moreover, if either
F(t)=F(s)
or
N(t)=N(s)
for some
t>s
, then
v
must be homogeneous of degree
m(\in\mathbb{N})
,
F\equiv 2m
, and
N\equiv 4^{m}
.

Lemma
A.1
can be proved by an even extension and the interior result (cf.,
[
18
]
).
Here, we include a proof for completeness.

Proof.

By calculation, we obtain

(A.2)

\frac{d}{dr}\fint_{B_{r}^{+}}|v|^{2}=2r^{-1}\fint_{B_{r}^{+}}v(x\cdot\nabla v)=r^{-1}\fint_{B_{r}^{+}}|\nabla v|^{2}(r^{2}-|x|^{2}).

In other words,

(A.3)

\frac{d}{d\log r}\left(\log\fint_{B_{r}^{+}}|v|^{2}\right)=F(r).

Also,

(A.4)

\frac{d}{dr}F(r)=4(r\fint_{B_{r}^{+}}|v|^{2})^{-1}\left(\left(\fint_{B_{r}^{+}}|v|^{2}\right)\left(\fint_{B_{r}^{+}}|x\cdot\nabla v|^{2}\right)-\left(\fint_{B_{r}^{+}}v(x\cdot\nabla v)\right)^{2}\right).

The calculations for (
A.2
) and (
A.4
) are standard, based on integration by parts, (
A.1
), and
x\cdot\boldsymbol{n}=0
on
\Gamma_{2}
. Details can be found in, for instance,
[
1
,
9
,
10
]
.

From (
A.4
) and Hölder’s inequality,
F^{\prime}\geq 0
. Now, suppose
F(t)=F(s)
for some
t>s
. This implies
F^{\prime}(r)=0
for any
r\in(s,t)
. Hence, by the condition for achieving “=” in Hölder’s inequality, we must have
x\cdot\nabla v=C_{r}v
for any
|x|=r
and any
r\in(s,t)
. Taking the spherical harmonic expansion
v=\sum_{k}a_{k}r^{k}\psi_{k}(\theta)
, this implies
\sum_{k}(C_{r}-k)a_{k}r^{k}\psi_{k}(\theta)=0
which is only possible when merely one of
a_{k}
is non-zero, i.e.
v
itself is homogeneous.
Let
m(\in\mathbb{N})
be the homogeneity of
v
. Clearly,
N\equiv 4^{m}
. Moreover, combining
x\cdot\nabla v\equiv mv
, which comes from the homogeneity, and the second equality in (
A.2
), we obtain
F\equiv 2m
.

Baed on the discussion on
F
above, the corresponding results for
N
follows from the following formula

\displaystyle N(r)=\exp{\int_{r}^{2r}\frac{d}{ds}\log(\fint_{B_{s}^{+}}|v|^{2})\,ds}=\exp{\int_{\log_{2}r}^{1+\log_{2}r}F(2^{s})\,ds},

which can be derived from (
A.3
).
∎

Appendix B
A solvability result of Neumann problems

In this section, we state a solvability result for

(B.1)

\begin{cases}D_{i}(a_{ij}D_{j}u)=0\quad\text{in}\,\,\Omega,\\
a_{ij}D_{j}u\boldsymbol{n}_{i}=g\quad\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Lemma B.1
.

Suppose
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded
C^{1,1}
domain,
a_{ij}\in C^{0,1}
, and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then for every
g\in L_{p}(\partial\Omega)
with
\fint_{\partial\Omega}g=0
, there exists a unique weak solution
u
to (
B.1
). Moreover,
u\in W^{1}_{q}(\Omega)
for every
q<dp/(d-1)
.

Lemma
B.1
is a standard elliptic regularity result, for which we only sketch the proof. Note that since
a_{ij}\in C^{0,1}
, (
B.1
) has both divergence and non-divergence structures:
D_{i}(a_{ij}D_{j}u)=a_{ij}D_{i}D_{j}u+D_{i}(a_{ij})D_{j}u
. Hence, noting
\partial\Omega\in C^{1,1}
, we have both
W^{2}_{p}
and
W^{1}_{p}
estimates for every
p\in(1,\infty)
, with
g
coming from corresponding trace spaces. By interpolation, we reach the desired a priori estimate when
g\in L_{p}(\partial\Omega)
. The solvability follows from a standard density argument: For a sequence of
g_{k}\in C^{\alpha}(\partial\Omega)
with
g_{k}\rightarrow g
in
L_{p}(\partial\Omega)
, solve (
B.1
) for
u_{k}\in C^{1,\alpha}(\Omega)
by standard Schauder theory. The convergence of
u_{k}
to a solution
u
and the regularity of
u
follow from the aforementioned a priori estimate.

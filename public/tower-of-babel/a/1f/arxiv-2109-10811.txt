Revisiting rescattering contributions to 𝑩 ¯ ( 𝒔 ) → 𝑫 ( 𝒔 ) ( ∗ ) ​ 𝑴 \overline{B}_{(s)}\to D^{(*)}_{(s)}M decays Preprint: IPMU21–0057, KEK–TH–2348
Motoi Endo, Syuhei Iguro, and Satoshi Mishima

Abstract
Motivated by the reported discrepancies between experimental data and Standard Model predictions for the branching ratios of the color-allowed decays B ¯ 0 → D ( ∗ ) + ​ K − \overline{B}^{0}\to D^{(*)+}K^{-} and B ¯ s 0 → D s ( ∗ ) + ​ π − \overline{B}^{0}_{s}\to D^{(*)+}_{s}\pi^{-} , we study final-state rescattering effects on B ¯ ( s ) → D ( s ) ( ∗ ) ​ P \overline{B}_{(s)}\to D^{(*)}_{(s)}P and B ¯ ( s ) → D ( s ) ​ V \overline{B}_{(s)}\to D_{(s)}V , where P P ( V V ) is a light pseudoscalar (vector) meson. We consider quasi-elastic rescatterings in the framework of S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SU(3) and U ⁡ ( 3 ) U(3) flavor symmetries, and find that the effects cannot explain the measured branching ratios of the color-allowed and color-suppressed decays simultaneously. We also perform global fits to the B ¯ ( s ) → D ( s ) ( ∗ ) ​ P \overline{B}_{(s)}\to D^{(*)}_{(s)}P and B ¯ ( s ) → D ( s ) ​ V \overline{B}_{(s)}\to D_{(s)}V data, allowing for new physics contributions to the Wilson coefficient a 1 a_{1} associated with the color-allowed tree amplitudes. It is shown that the fits prefer a downward shift of 𝒪 ⁡ ( 10 % ) \mathcal{O}(10\%) in a 1 a_{1} even in the presence of the quasi-elastic rescattering contributions.

Introduction

Two-body hadronic
B
decays into a heavy-light final state

\overline{B}_{(s)}\to D_{(s)}^{(*)}M
, where
M
is a light meson,
have played an important role in testing the factorization
hypothesis
Bjorken:1988kk
.
In
\overline{B}_{(s)}^{0}\to D_{(s)}^{+}\pi^{-}
and

\overline{B}_{(s)}^{0}\to D_{(s)}^{+}K^{-}
decays, which are dominated by
the so-called color-allowed tree amplitude,
the hypothesis is expected to work well,
since contributions from a gluon exchange between the light meson
emitted from the weak vertex and the soft spectator quark in a heavy
meson are power suppressed in the heavy-quark limit. Moreover, they
have no penguin contribution.
For these decays the factorization theorem was proved
at the leading power in the heavy-quark expansion with the QCD
factorization (QCDF) approach
Beneke:2000ry

and with the soft-collinear effective theory (SCET)
Bauer:2001cu
.
There are great improvements in theoretical calculations of the
color-allowed tree amplitude in recent years, which enable us to study
possible contributions to these decays from new-physics (NP) beyond the Standard Model (SM).

The branching ratios of

\overline{B}_{(s)}^{0}\to D_{(s)}^{(*)+}\pi^{-}
and

\overline{B}_{(s)}^{0}\to D_{(s)}^{(*)+}K^{-}

are calculated in QCDF to next-to-next-to-leading order (NNLO)
accuracy
Huber:2016xod
. They have recently been updated
in ref.
Bordone:2020gao
with new determinations
of
\overline{B}_{(s)}\to D_{(s)}^{(*)}
form factors
in the heavy-quark expansion up to

\mathcal{O}(\Lambda^{2}_{\mathrm{QCD}}/m_{c}^{2})

Bordone:2019guc
.
The updated predictions for the branching ratios show a tension with
the experimental measurements with a significance of
the
4.4\hskip 0.80002pt\sigma
level
Bordone:2020gao
.
1
1

1

Similar results are obtained in ref.
Cai:2021mlt
with different inputs.

The tension requires a downward shift of 10% in the
b\to c\bar{u}q
amplitude
with
q
being
d
or
s
, which might be caused by NP.
2
2

2

Implication of CP-violating NP phases is studied in ref.
Fleischer:2021cct
.

It is shown in ref.
Iguro:2020ndk
that
the tension can be reduced partly by introducing a new vector boson
W^{\prime}

without conflicting with narrow resonance searches at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC).
3
3

3

A model-independent NP analysis has also been carried out in ref.
Cai:2021mlt
.

On the other hand, the experimental data for the branching ratios of

\overline{B}^{0}\to D^{(*)+}\pi^{-}
,

\overline{B}^{0}\to D^{(*)0}\pi^{0}
and

B^{-}\to D^{(*)0}\pi^{-}
indicate a failure of the naive
factorization
Cheng:2001sc
;
Neubert:2001sj
.
4
4

4

A different
conclusion was obtained in refs.
Xing:2001nj
;
Xing:2003fe
.

Here
\overline{B}^{0}\to D^{(*)0}\pi^{0}
is dominated by the
color-suppressed tree amplitude, while
B^{-}\to D^{(*)0}\pi^{-}
has both the color-allowed and color-suppressed tree amplitudes.
For
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
being the Wilson coefficients associated with
the color-allowed and color-suppressed tree amplitudes, respectively,
a fit to the data prefers a larger
|a_{2}|
compared to those extracted
from
\overline{B}\to J/\psi\overline{K}^{(*)}
and

\overline{B}\to\psi(2S)\overline{K}^{(*)}

with a sizable relative phase between
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
.
Similar results can also be obtained from other
\overline{B}_{(s)}\to D_{(s)}^{(*)}M

processes
Chiang:2002tv
;
Kim:2004hx
;
Chiang:2007bd
.
The relative phase between
a_{1}

and
a_{2}
implies that final-state interactions (FSI) are significant
in the color-suppressed modes.
5
5

5

In the PQCD approach based on
the
k_{T}
factorization, the larger
|a_{2}|
with the sizable phase
is explained by spectator-scattering contributions
Keum:2003js
;
Li:2008ts
.

It is however difficult to calculate the FSI effects in

\overline{B}_{(s)}\to D_{(s)}^{(*)}M
from first principles.
6
6

6

The FSI effects in heavy meson decays were explored first for the charmed meson decays in ref.
Blok:1986sn
using QCD sum rules.
.
In SCET, the FSI contributions in the color-suppressed decays are
factorized into soft factors
Mantry:2003uz
;
Blechman:2004vc
,
which have to be calculated in non-perturbative methods or extracted
from data. Non-factorizable contributions from a single soft-gluon
exchange are estimated with the light-cone QCD sum rules (LCSR)
for the color-allowed decays
Bordone:2020gao

and the color-suppressed decays
Halperin:1994hg
;
Cui:2004jc
,
and the size of them for the former (latter) is found to be
insignificant (significant) compared to the factorizable ones.
Also the rescattering of the final-state mesons are studied with various models
Donoghue:1996hz
;
Blok:1996uf
;
Suzuki:1999uc
;
Chua:2001br
;
Fayyazuddin:2002aa
;
Wolfenstein:2003pc
;
Calderon:2003vc
;
Fayyazuddin:2004ac
;
Wolfenstein:2004km
;
Cheng:2004ru
;
Chua:2005dt
;
Chua:2007qw
;
Suzuki:2007je
.

It is pointed out in ref.
Donoghue:1996hz
using the Regge approach that the final-state rescattering effects do not vanish in the large
b
-quark mass limit.
On the other hand, in the QCDF approach, the rescattering effects are found to be suppressed in the heavy-quark limit
Beneke:2000ry
.
Hence the suppression could be understood by a systematic cancellation among all intermediate hadronic states
Suzuki:1999uc
;
Beneke:2000ry
.
Nonetheless, the rescattering effects may remain sizable, since the
b
-quark mass is not infinite in reality.

In this paper we study the
quasi-elastic
rescattering contributions to the
\overline{B}_{(s)}\to D_{(s)}^{(*)}M
decays, following the framework developed in refs.
Chua:2001br
;
Chua:2005dt
;
Chua:2007qw
.
The elastic
D\pi\to D\pi
rescattering picture is extended to quasi-elastic
D^{(*)}P\to D^{(*)}P
and
DV\to DV
rescatterings by based on
SU(3)
and
U(3)
flavor symmetries,
where
P
and
V
represent the pseudoscalar
SU(3)
octet and the vector
U(3)
nonet, respectively.
We assume that inelastic-rescattering contributions are suppressed, and examine the question whether the quasi-elastic rescattering contributions can resolve the tensions between the theoretical predictions and the experimental data for the color-allowed decays.
The analyses in refs.
Chua:2001br
;
Chua:2005dt
;
Chua:2007qw
are updated with latest theoretical and experimental information.
In particular, the Wilson coefficient
a_{1}
at NNLO
Huber:2016xod
and the
\overline{B}_{(s)}\to D_{(s)}^{(*)}
form factors at

\mathcal{O}(\Lambda^{2}_{\mathrm{QCD}}/m_{c}^{2})

Iguro:2020cpg
are employed to make the calculation of the color-allowed tree amplitude more reliable.
7
7

7

It is assumed that the
SU(3)
flavor symmetry holds between the
\overline{B}\to D^{(*)}
and
\overline{B}_{s}\to D_{s}^{(*)}
form factors as suggested in the result of refs.
Kobach:2019kfb
;
Bordone:2019guc
.

This paper is organized as follows.
In section
2
the theoretical framework for calculating decay amplitudes is described under the presence of the quasi-elastic rescattering.
In section
3
numerical analysis of the rescattering effects in the color-allowed decays is presented.
Finally, the section
4
is devoted to conclusions and discussion.
Besides, appendix
A
contains some details of numerical results.

In this section the theoretical framework for calculating decay amplitudes is briefly introduced with quasi-elastic rescattering contributions.
The decays of
\overline{B}\to DP
,
\overline{B}\to D^{*}P
, and
\overline{B}\to DV
with
b\to c\bar{u}q
transitions are studied, where
q
is
d,s
, and
P
(
V
) stands for a light pseudoscalar (vector) meson.

8

8

We do not consider
\overline{B}\to D^{*}V
in this paper, since experimental uncertainties are large

2.1
Decay amplitudes

The weak effective Hamiltonian relevant for the current study consists only of the following tree operators:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{W}=\frac{4\hskip 0.80002ptG_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}\,\sum_{q=d,s}V_{cb}V_{uq}^{*}\big(C_{1}\mathcal{O}_{1}^{\hskip 0.32771ptq}+C_{2}\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\hskip 0.32771ptq}\big)+\mathrm{h.c.},

(1)

where
V_{ij}
are the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) matrix elements
Cabibbo:1963yz
;
Kobayashi:1973fv
.
The local operators are defined in the Chetyrkin-Misiak-Münz (CMM) basis
Chetyrkin:1996vx
;
Chetyrkin:1997gb
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{1}^{\hskip 0.32771ptq}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{c}\hskip 0.80002pt\gamma^{\mu}T^{a}P_{L}\hskip 0.39993ptb\big)\big(\bar{q}\hskip 0.39993pt\gamma_{\mu}T^{a}P_{L}u\big)\,,

(2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\hskip 0.32771ptq}

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{c}\hskip 0.80002pt\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}\hskip 0.39993ptb\big)\big(\bar{q}\hskip 0.39993pt\gamma_{\mu}P_{L}u\big)\,,

(3)

where
P_{L}=(1-\gamma_{5})/2
, and
T^{a}
is a generator of
SU(3)
.
Suppose that there is no CP-violating phase in
\mathcal{H}_{W}
9
9

9

Note that the CP-violating phase in the CKM matrix is irrelevant in the current study.
,
the time-reversal invariance of
\mathcal{H}_{W}
leads to

\displaystyle\langle i;\mathrm{out}|\mathcal{H}_{W}|\overline{B}\rangle^{*}=\sum_{j}S_{ji}^{*}\hskip 0.80002pt\langle j;\mathrm{out}|\mathcal{H}_{W}|\overline{B}\rangle\hskip 0.39993pt,

(4)

where the strong scattering
S
-matrix element
S_{ji}
is defined in terms of
in
and
out
states as

S_{ji}=\langle j;\mathrm{out}|i;\mathrm{in}\rangle
.
The solution of this relation is given by

\langle i;\mathrm{out}|\mathcal{H}_{W}|\overline{B}\rangle=\sum_{j}S_{ij}^{1/2}\mathcal{A}_{j}^{\hskip 0.32771pt0}
,
where
\mathcal{A}_{j}^{\hskip 0.32771pt0}
is real
Suzuki:1999uc
.
A decay amplitude with FSI, denoted as

\mathcal{A}_{i}^{\hskip 0.32771pt\mathrm{FSI}}=\langle i;\mathrm{out}|\mathcal{H}_{W}|\overline{B}\rangle
,
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{i}^{\hskip 0.32771pt\mathrm{FSI}}=\sum_{j}S_{ij}^{1/2}\mathcal{A}_{j}^{\hskip 0.32771pt0}\hskip 0.39993pt,

(5)

where
A_{j}^{0}
represents the corresponding decay amplitude without FSI.
Following the procedure described in ref.
Chua:2018ikx
, the scattering matrix is decomposed into a product of two matrices,

S_{ij}^{1/2}=\sum_{k}(S_{\mathrm{res}}^{1/2})_{ik}(S_{2}^{1/2})_{kj}
.
Then the amplitude is written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{i}^{\hskip 0.32771pt\mathrm{FSI}}=\sum_{k}(S_{\mathrm{res}}^{1/2})_{ik}\hskip 0.80002pt\mathcal{A}_{k}^{\hskip 0.65556pt\mathrm{fact}}\hskip 0.39993pt,\qquad\mathcal{A}_{k}^{\hskip 0.65556pt\mathrm{fact}}=\sum_{j}(S_{2}^{1/2})_{kj}\hskip 0.80002pt\mathcal{A}_{j}^{\hskip 0.32771pt0}\hskip 0.39993pt,

(6)

where
S_{\mathrm{res}}^{1/2}
is a rescattering matrix, and
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{\hskip 0.65556pt\mathrm{fact}}
is a decay amplitude which incorporates a part of FSI effects. For the color-allowed tree amplitude,
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{\hskip 0.65556pt\mathrm{fact}}
is assumed to correspond to the amplitude calculated in the QCDF approach.

The decay amplitude
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{\hskip 0.65556pt\mathrm{fact}}
can be expressed with topological amplitudes in the flavor-diagram approach based on the
SU(3)
flavor symmetry

;

;
Gronau:1995hm
;
Chiang:2002tv
;
Kim:2004hx
;

;
Chiang:2007bd
;
Fleischer:2010ca
.

D^{*}
or
V
.
The definitions of
the decay constants

f_{P}
,
f_{V}
and
f_{D^{(*)}}
and the transition form factors

f_{0}^{B\to D}
,

f_{0}^{B\to P}
,

f_{+}^{B\to P}
,

f_{+}^{B\to D}
,

A_{0}^{B\to D}
and

A_{0}^{B\to V}
are found in ref.
Gubernari:2018wyi
.
The coefficients
a_{1}(\mu)
and
a_{2}(\mu)
are evaluated at the
scale
\mu=m_{b}
, where the leading-order contributions are given by

a_{1}(\mu)=C_{2}(\mu)
and
a_{2}(\mu)=4\hskip 0.80002ptC_{1}(\mu)/9+C_{2}(\mu)/3

in terms of the Wilson coefficients in eq. (
1
).

The coefficient
a_{1}(\mu)
associated with the color-allowed tree amplitude is calculated for different processes in the framework of QCDF to the NNLO accuracy
Huber:2016xod
:

\displaystyle a_{1}(m_{b})=\begin{cases}\big(1.069^{+0.009}_{-0.012}\big)+\big(0.046^{+0.023}_{-0.015}\big)\hskip 0.80002pti&\mathrm{for}\ \overline{B}^{0}\to D^{+}K^{-},\\
\big(1.072^{+0.011}_{-0.013}\big)+\big(0.044^{+0.022}_{-0.014}\big)\hskip 0.80002pti&\mathrm{for}\ \overline{B}^{0}\to D^{+}\pi^{-},\\
\big(1.068^{+0.010}_{-0.012}\big)+\big(0.034^{+0.017}_{-0.011}\big)\hskip 0.80002pti&\mathrm{for}\ \overline{B}^{0}\to D^{*+}K^{-},\\
\big(1.071^{+0.012}_{-0.013}\big)+\big(0.032^{+0.016}_{-0.010}\big)\hskip 0.80002pti&\mathrm{for}\ \overline{B}^{0}\to D^{*+}\pi^{-}.\end{cases}

(14)

It is noticed that these are almost universal, and hence a common value of
a_{1}(m_{b})
is used in our numerical analysis:

\displaystyle a_{1}(m_{b})=1.070\pm 0.012\,.

(15)

We set
a_{1}
to be real by absorbing its phase in
a_{2}
and
E
, which are complex parameters in the following analysis.
On the other hand, it is hard to evaluate the color-suppressed tree amplitudes reliably in QCDF, because
subleading-power and non-factorizable contributions are expected to be significant.
In our analysis the effective coefficient
a_{2}^{\mathrm{eff}}
is considered to include these contributions and taken to be a free parameter.
Also,
SU(3)
breaking effects in the amplitudes are simply assumed to be described by the factorization form as in eqs. (
9
), (
11
) and (
13
).
Furthermore, the
W
-exchange amplitude
E
, which is power suppressed compared to the color-allowed amplitude
T
, also cannot be calculated in the QCDF approach
Beneke:2000ry
. Therefore
E
is taken as another free parameter under the assumption of the
SU(3)
flavor symmetry.

Finally, the partial decay widths for the
b\to c\bar{u}q
channels (
q=d,s
) are expressed with the decay amplitudes as

\displaystyle\Gamma=\frac{p_{\mathrm{cm}}}{8\hskip 0.39993pt\pi\hskip 0.39993ptm_{B}^{2}}\hskip 0.80002pt\big|V_{cb}V_{uq}^{*}\,\mathcal{A}\,\big|^{2},

(16)

where
p_{\mathrm{cm}}
is the magnitude of the three momentum of a final-state meson in the center-of-mass frame.

2.2
Quasi-elastic rescattering

The framework of the quasi-elastic rescattering in
\overline{B}_{(s)}\to D_{(s)}^{(*)}M
was developed in refs.
Chua:2001br
;
Chua:2005dt
;
Chua:2007qw
.
In the quasi-elastic picture, the rescattering occurs among different final states carrying the same quantum numbers.
Since QCD respects the
SU(3)
flavor symmetry to some extent, the rescattering is considered among the states that reside in the same multiplet under the symmetry.
In the case of
\overline{B}\to DV
, the
U(3)
symmetry instead of
SU(3)
can be applied, since there is no
U(1)
axial anomaly for the vector mesons.
Moreover, following the procedure in ref.
Chua:2018ikx
, we include a part of the
SU(3)
breaking effects through the meson decay constants.

Under the
SU(3)
flavor symmetry
the
D^{(*)}P
final states are decomposed into one
\bm{\overline{15}}
, one
\bm{6}
and two
\bm{\overline{3}}
representations, since
the light pseudo-scalar mesons transform as
{\bm{8}}
and
{\bm{1}}
, and the charm mesons
D^{(*)0}
,
D^{(*)+}
and
D_{s}^{(*)+}
form a
\bm{\overline{3}}
representation.
Therefore, the rescattering matrix in eq. (
6
) can be written in the
SU(3)
basis as
Chua:2001br

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{res}}^{1/2}=e^{i\hskip 0.32771pt\delta_{\bm{\overline{15}}}}\sum_{a=1}^{15}\big|{\bm{\overline{15}}};a\big\rangle\big\langle{\bm{\overline{15}}};a\big|+e^{i\hskip 0.32771pt\delta_{\bm{6}}}\sum_{b=1}^{6}\big|{\bm{6}};b\big\rangle\big\langle{\bm{6}};b\big|+\sum_{m,n={\bm{\bar{3}}},{\bm{\bar{3}^{\prime}}}}\sum_{c=1}^{3}\big|m;c\big\rangle\,\mathcal{U}^{1/2}_{mn}\,\big\langle n;c\big|\hskip 0.39993pt,

(17)

where
\mathcal{U}^{1/2}
is a
2\times 2
symmetric unitary matrix.
Since the overall phase is not physical, the phase difference,

\displaystyle\delta^{\prime}\equiv\delta_{\bm{6}}-\delta_{\bm{\overline{15}}}\hskip 0.39993pt,

(18)

is defined, and
\mathcal{U}^{1/2}
is parameterized
in terms of three parameters
\theta
,
\sigma
and
\tau
(see, e.g., ref.
Suzuki:2007je
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{\hskip 0.65556pt\prime}_{mn}\equiv\mathcal{U}^{1/2}e^{-i\hskip 0.32771pt\delta_{\bm{\overline{15}}}}=\left(\!\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos\tau&\sin\tau\\
-\sin\tau&\cos\tau\end{array}\!\right)\left(\!\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{i\hskip 0.32771pt\theta}&0\\
0&e^{i\hskip 0.32771pt\sigma}\end{array}\!\right)\left(\!\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos\tau&-\sin\tau\\
\sin\tau&\cos\tau\end{array}\!\right).

Thus the rescattering matrix is parameterized by the four parameters
\delta^{\prime}
,
\theta
,
\sigma
and
\tau
.
Note that the rescattering effects are turned off at
\delta^{\prime}=\theta=\sigma=\tau=0
.

The rescattering in the
D^{(*)}V
final states can be parameterized in a similar way.
The light vector mesons form a
U(3)
nonet instead of a
SU(3)
octet.
In this case the rescattering parameters have specific values as follows, which are called
“solution 1” and “solution 2” in ref.
Chua:2001br
:

\displaystyle\bullet\ \mathrm{Solution}~1:

\displaystyle\quad\delta^{\prime}=\theta,\quad\sigma=0\hskip 0.39993pt,\quad\sin\tau=\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}}\hskip 0.39993pt,\quad\cos\tau=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\hskip 0.39993pt,

(25)

\displaystyle\bullet\ \mathrm{Solution}~2:

\displaystyle\quad\delta^{\prime}=\theta=0,\quad\sin\tau=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}\hskip 0.39993pt,\quad\cos\tau=\frac{1}{3}\hskip 0.39993pt.

(26)

Hence only a single parameter is left free for each solution.

In the following the rescattering formulae are presented for the
\overline{B}\to DP
decays.
The corresponding formulae for
\overline{B}\to D^{*}P
and
\overline{B}\to DV
can be obtained by substituting the final-state mesons appropriately.

The final states are classified by the strangeness and the isospin.
There are three sets for the decays with the
b\to c\bar{u}d
transition:

\{S,I_{z}\}=\{0,-3/2\}
,
\{0,-1/2\}
and
\{1,-1\}
.
For
\overline{B}\to DP
,

\{S,I_{z}\}=\{0,-3/2\}
corresponds only to the single process
\overline{B}\to D^{0}\pi^{-}
.
Then the elastic rescattering causes only an unphysical overall phase shift,
and thus, does not alter the branching ratio of this channel.
For the other sets the decay amplitudes are assembled as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{d,0,-1/2}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}(\overline{B}^{0}\to D^{+}\pi^{-})\\[2.84526pt]
\mathcal{A}(\overline{B}^{0}\to D^{0}\pi^{0})\\[2.84526pt]
\mathcal{A}(\overline{B}^{0}\to D_{s}^{+}K^{-})\\[2.84526pt]
\mathcal{A}(\overline{B}^{0}\to D^{0}\eta_{8})\\[2.84526pt]
\mathcal{A}(\overline{B}^{0}\to D^{0}\eta_{1})\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathcal{A}_{d,1,-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}(\overline{B}_{s}\to D_{s}^{+}\pi^{-})\\[2.84526pt]
\mathcal{A}(\overline{B}_{s}\to D^{0}K^{0})\end{pmatrix},

(27)

where the subscripts denote the flavor of the down-type quark,
the strangeness and the isospin of the final states.
The physical states,
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
, are given in terms of the octet state
\eta_{8}
and
the singlet one
\eta_{1}
as

\eta=\eta_{8}\cos\theta_{\eta}-\eta_{1}\sin\theta_{\eta}
and

\eta^{\prime}=\eta_{8}\sin\theta_{\eta}+\eta_{1}\cos\theta_{\eta}

with
\theta_{\eta}=\phi_{\eta}-\mathrm{arctan}\sqrt{2}
.

Similarly, the final states in the processes with the
b\to c\bar{u}s
transition are classified into

\{S,I_{z}\}=\{-1,-1\}
,
\{-1,0\}
and
\{0,-1/2\}
. For
\{S,I_{z}\}=\{-1,-1\}
, there is only the single process
B^{-}\to D^{0}K^{-}
. The decay amplitudes for the others are written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{s,-1,0}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}(\overline{B}^{0}\to D^{+}K^{-})\\[2.84526pt]
\mathcal{A}(\overline{B}^{0}\to D^{0}\overline{K}^{0})\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathcal{A}_{s,0,-1/2}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}(\overline{B}_{s}\to D^{+}\pi^{-})\\[2.84526pt]
\mathcal{A}(\overline{B}_{s}\to D^{0}\pi^{0})\\[2.84526pt]
\mathcal{A}(\overline{B}_{s}\to D_{s}^{+}K^{-})\\[2.84526pt]
\mathcal{A}(\overline{B}_{s}\to D^{0}\eta_{8})\\[2.84526pt]
\mathcal{A}(\overline{B}_{s}\to D^{0}\eta_{1})\end{pmatrix}.

(28)

According to eq. (
6
), the rescattering is incorporated into the decay amplitudes as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{q,S,I_{z}}^{\hskip 0.32771pt\mathrm{FSI}}=V_{S,I_{z}}^{-1}\,\mathcal{S}_{S,I_{z}}^{1/2}\,V_{S,I_{z}}\,\mathcal{A}_{q,S,I_{z}}^{\hskip 0.65556pt\mathrm{fact}}\hskip 0.39993pt,

(29)

where
\mathcal{S}_{0,-1/2}^{1/2}
is the rescattering matrix, and
the matrix
V_{S,I_{z}}
represents the
SU(3)
breaking effects
Chua:2018ikx
.
It is noticed that the breaking effects are removed from

\mathcal{A}_{q,S,I_{z}}^{\hskip 0.65556pt\mathrm{fact}}
before the rescattering
by applying
V_{S,I_{z}}
, and then put them back after the rescattering by

V_{S,I_{z}}^{-1}

Chua:2018ikx
, since the rescattering is considered
to work under the
SU(3)
symmetry.

The explicit expressions of the symmetric matrices
\mathcal{S}_{S,I_{z}}^{1/2}

are given by
Chua:2001br
;
Chua:2005dt
;
Chua:2007qw

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1,-1}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{-1,0}^{1/2}=\frac{e^{i\hskip 0.32771pt\delta_{\bm{\overline{15}}}}}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1+e^{i\hskip 0.32771pt\delta^{\prime}}&1-e^{i\hskip 0.32771pt\delta^{\prime}}\\[2.84526pt]
&1+e^{i\hskip 0.32771pt\delta^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix},

(30)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{0,-1/2}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{i\hskip 0.32771pt\delta_{\bm{\overline{15}}}}}{8}\begin{pmatrix}3+u_{+}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big(5-u_{+}\big)&-1-u_{-}&\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\big(1-v_{-}\big)&\sqrt{6}\hskip 0.80002ptu^{\prime}\\[5.69054pt]

where

V_{-1,0}
is a unit matrix,

\displaystyle V_{1,-1}

\overline{B}_{s}\to D_{s}^{+}\pi^{-}
and
\overline{B}^{0}\to D^{+}K^{-}
become slightly larger than the previous results in Ref.

Also the uncertainty in

Consequently, the above results deviate from the measured values by

3.5\hskip 0.80002pt\sigma

4.5\hskip 0.80002pt\sigma
,
5.3\hskip 0.80002pt\sigma

The above predictions are very clean theoretically. There is neither penguin nor annihilation contribution to these processes. As a result, there is no chirally-enhanced hard-scattering contributions at
\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}/m_{B})

Moreover, power corrections at
\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}/m_{B})

twist-3 two-particle contributions of light-meson light-cone distribution amplitudes,

(or

B\to D
system and the light meson,
are expected to be less than a percent

Besides, the QCD
\times
QED factorization is studied recently in ref.
Beneke:2021jhp

QED contributions to the color-allowed tree amplitudes are found to reduce the total amplitudes by the sub-percent level, though ultrasoft photons may correct the measured decay rates up to a few percent.

4
Summary

Recent developments in the study of the
\overline{B}_{(s)}\to D_{(s)}
transition form factors have shed light on the discrepancies between the theoretical predictions and the experimental data for the color-allowed tree decays
B(\overline{B}^{0}\to D^{(*)+}K^{-})
and
B(\overline{B}^{0}_{s}\to D^{(*)+}_{s}\pi^{-})
.
The theoretical predictions for these branching ratios are universally larger than the measured values.
On the other hand, it has been known that the decays with the color-suppressed tree amplitude have smaller branching ratios in the factorization approaches than the experimental data.
This motivated us to revisit the final-state rescattering effects in the
\overline{B}\to DP
,
\overline{B}\to D^{*}P
and
\overline{B}\to DV
channels.

We have examined whether the discrepancies can be explained by the quasi-elastic rescattering contributions based on the
SU(3)
and
U(3)
flavor symmetries.
From the analysis of the color-allowed modes

\overline{B}^{0}\to D^{(*)+}K^{-}
and

\overline{B}^{0}_{s}\to D^{(*)+}_{s}\pi^{-}

and the color-suppressed modes

\overline{B}^{0}\to D^{(*)0}\overline{K}^{0}
and

\overline{B}^{0}_{s}\to D^{(*)0}K^{0}
,
we have concluded that the rescattering effects cannot account for the discrepancies.
Hence there should exist additional contributions such as inelastic rescattering contributions beyond the quasi-elastic picture, large power corrections in the QCDF approach and/or NP contributions to the short-distance Wilson coefficients.
We have studied the latter two possibilities by taking the coefficient
a_{1}(m_{b})
as a fit parameter.
The preferred range of
a_{1}(m_{b})
has been determined from the fits to all the available data for
\overline{B}_{(s)}\to D^{(*)}_{(s)}M
, where the fits have been performed separately for
\overline{B}\to DP
,
\overline{B}\to D^{*}P
and
\overline{B}\to DV
.
The results point to a downward shift of
\mathcal{O}(10\%)
in
a_{1}(m_{b})
compared to the QCDF prediction.
Remarkably, all the fits are consistent with each other.

As argued in detail in ref.
Bordone:2020gao
, it is hard to resolve the discrepancies by (unknown) higher-order or higher-power corrections to the decay amplitudes in the QCDF approach.
Effects of the inelastic rescattering might be significant, though their discussion is beyond the scope of this paper.
On the other hand, it is also challenging to construct viable NP models that realizes the NP contribution of
\mathcal{O}(10\%)
.
Therefore, further theoretical and experimental studies are needed to clarify this issue.

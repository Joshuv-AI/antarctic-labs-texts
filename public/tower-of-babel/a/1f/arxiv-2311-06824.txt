On the rate of change of Varentropy for Markov diffusion processes
Michele Pavon

Abstract
Relying on the reverse-time space-time harmonic property of the ratio of two solutions of the Fokker-Plank equation, we establish an explicit formula for derivative of the varentropy for a Markov diffusion process. The formula involves a nonlinear function of the local free energy ln ⁡ ( p t ​ ( x ) / p ¯ ​ ( x ) ) \ln(p_{t}(x)/\bar{p}(x)) .We then verify that our formula yields the correct result in the simple case of a scalar Gaussian diffusion. In the latter case, varentropy is exponentially decaying to zero.

1
Introduction

Entropy concentration phenomena have been long studied, see. e.g.
[
6
]
. In
[
3
]
, concentration of information for convex measures was studied through the variance of the
information content
, namely
-logp(X)
where
p
is the density of the probability distribution of the random vector
X
. In the
[
12
]
, the cutoff phenomenon was explicitly connected to an
entropy concentration principle
for non-negatively curved Markov chains. The central indicator of the entropy concentration property is the (worst-case)
Varentropy
, namely the variance of a relative information content with respect to the invariant measure.

We study here the evolution of varentropy in the simple case of a Markov diffusion process with constant diffusion coefficient. Using a reverse-time space-time harmonic property of the ratio of two solutions of the Fokker-Plank equation, we obtain an explicit formula for the derivative of the varentropy which involves a nonlinear function of the
local free energy

\ln(p_{t}(x)/\bar{p}(x))
,
\bar{p}
being the invariant density. We then show that the formula applied to the scalar, Gaussian case yields the correct result.

The paper is outlined as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some results by Föllmer dating back to the 1980’s. In Section
3
we recall some reverse-time properties of the ratio of two solutions of a Fokker-Planck equation. In Section
4
, we present our main result Theorem
2
on the time derivative of the Varentropy. In the final section, we show that the formula obtained in Section
4
yields the correct result when applied to a scalar, Gaussian diffusion.

2
Finite-energy diffusions

Let
{\cal W}_{x}
be Wiener measure starting at
x
on
\Omega=C([0,T],\mbox{\BB R}^{n})
. Let

{\cal W}=\int{\cal W}_{x}dx

be
stationary Wiener measure
. We denote by
\mathcal{D}
the family of probability measures on
\Omega
which are equivalent to stationary Wiener measure. Let
\mathcal{F}_{t}
and
\mathcal{G}_{t}
be the
\sigma
-algebras of events observable up to time
t
and from time
t
on, respectively. By Girsanov’s theorem, any
P\in\mathcal{D}
has a forward drift
\beta_{t}^{P}
measurable with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{t}
and a backward drift
\gamma_{t}^{P}
measurable with respect to
\mathcal{G}_{t}
. That means that, under
P
, the coordiante process admits for
0\leq s<t\leq T
the representations

\displaystyle X_{t}-X_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\beta_{\tau}^{P}d\tau+W_{t}-W_{s},

(1)

\displaystyle X_{t}-X_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\gamma_{\tau}^{P}d\tau+\bar{W}_{t}-\bar{W}_{s}.

(2)

Here
W
and
\bar{W}
are standard
n
-dimensional Wiener processes adapted to
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
and to
(\mathcal{G}_{t})
, respectively. Moreover, the two drifts satisfy the condition

P\left[\int_{0}^{T}|\beta^{P}_{t}|^{2}dt\right]=P\left[\int_{0}^{T}|\gamma^{P}_{t}|^{2}dt\right]=1,

see e.g
[
7
, Theorem 7.11]
. By the Girsanov theory, we have
P
a.s.

\displaystyle\frac{dQ}{dP}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q_{0}(X_{0})}{p_{0}(X_{0})}\exp\left[\int_{0}^{T}(\beta^{Q}_{t}-\beta^{P}_{t})dX_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\beta^{P}_{t}-\beta^{Q}_{t}\right)^{2}dt\right]

(3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q_{T}(X_{T})}{p_{T}(X_{T})}\exp\left[\int_{0}^{T}(\gamma^{Q}_{t}-\gamma^{P}_{t})dX_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\gamma^{P}_{t}-\gamma^{Q}_{t}\right)^{2}dt\right].

Proposition 1
.

Let
P,Q\in\mathcal{D}
have one-time density
p_{t}
and
q_{t}
,
t\in[0,T]
, respectively. If
\gamma^{P}_{t}=\gamma^{Q}_{t}
for
t\in[0,T]
,
\frac{q_{t}}{p_{t}}(X_{t})
is a
(P,\mathcal{F}_{t})
martingale on
[0,T]
. If
\beta^{P}_{t}=\beta^{Q}_{t}
for
t\in[0,T]
,
\frac{q_{t}}{p_{t}}(X_{t})
is a reverse-time
(P,\mathcal{G}_{t})
martingale on
[0,T]
.

Proof.

Suppose
\gamma^{P}_{t}=\gamma^{Q}_{t}
for
t\in[0,T]
. By (
3
), under
P
,

\mbox{\BB E}\left\{\frac{dQ}{dP}|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right\}=\frac{dQ_{0t}}{dP_{0t}}=\frac{q_{t}}{p_{t}}(X_{t}).

Being the conditional expectation of a fixed random variable with respect to an increasing family of
\sigma
-algebras,
\frac{q_{t}}{p_{t}}(X_{t})
is a supermantingale. Since it has constant expectation, it is actually a martingale. The other half is proven similarly.
∎

3
Markov diffusion processes

Let
\{X_{t};t\geq 0\}
be a
\mbox{\BB R}^{n}
-valued Markov diffusion process defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\cal F,\mbox{\BB P})
with forward It
\hat{o}
differential

dX_{t}=b_{+}(t,X_{t})dt+\sigma dW_{t},

(4)

where
W
is a standard
n
-dimensional Wiener process. Under rather weak assumptions
[
8
,
5
]
,
X
possesses on
t>0
also a reverse-time differential

dX_{t}=b_{-}(t,X_{t})dt+\sigma d\bar{W}_{t}.

(5)

Here
\bar{W}
is another
n
-dimensional standard Wiener process with
\bar{W}_{s}-\bar{W}_{t}
independent of
X_{\tau}
whenever
s<t\leq\tau
. Here and in the sequel
dt>0
so that, if
X
is a finite-energy diffusion, i.e.

\mbox{\BB E}\left\{\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\|b_{+}\|^{2}\right\}<\infty,

we get the two drifts as Nelson’s conditional derivatives, namely

\displaystyle b_{+}(t,X_{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{dt\searrow 0}E\left\{\frac{X_{t+dt}-X_{t}}{dt}|X_{\tau},t_{0}\leq\tau\leq t\right\},

\displaystyle b_{-}(t,X_{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{dt\searrow 0}E\left\{\frac{X_{t}-X_{t-dt}}{dt}|X_{\tau},t\leq\tau\leq t_{1}\right\},

the limits being in
L^{2}_{n}(\Omega,\cal f,\mbox{\BB P})
and
dt>0
for both formulas. Moreover, we have Nelson’s duality formula

b_{-}(t,X_{t})=b_{+}(t,X_{t})-\sigma^{2}\nabla\ln p_{t}(X_{t})\>{\rm a.s.}

(6)

where
p_{t}
is the probability density of
X_{t}
satisfying (at least weakly) the Fokker-Planck equation

\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(b_{+}p)-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta p=0.

(7)

From (
6
), we also have

\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(b_{-}p)+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta p=0.

(8)

There are two change of variables formulae related to (
4
) and (
5
).
Let
f:\mbox{\BB R}^{n}\times[t_{0},t_{1}]\rightarrow\mbox{\BB R}
be twice continuously differentiable with respect to the spatial variable
and once with respect to time.
Then:

\displaystyle f(X_{t},t)-f(X_{s},s)=\int_{s}^{t}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}+b_{+}(\tau,X_{\tau})\cdot\nabla+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta\right)f(X_{\tau},\tau)d\tau

(9)

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{t}\sigma\nabla f(X_{\tau},\tau)\cdot dW_{\tau},

(10)

\displaystyle f(X_{t},t)-f(X_{s},s)=\int_{s}^{t}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}+b_{-}(\tau,X_{\tau})\tau)\cdot\nabla-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta\right)f(X_{\tau},\tau)d\tau

(11)

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{t}\sigma\nabla f(X_{\tau})\cdot d\bar{W}_{\tau}.

(12)

The
stochastic integrals appearing in (
10
) and (
12
) are a (forward) Ito integral and a
backward Ito integral, respectively, see
[
9
]
for the details. Suppose that
b_{+}(t,x)=b_{+}(x)
is such that (
7
) has
\bar{p}
as invariant solution. Then, a direct calculation
[
10
]

shows that
\bar{p}/p_{t}
is reverse-time space time harmonic, namely

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}+b_{-}(\tau,x)\cdot\nabla-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta\right)\left(\frac{\bar{p}}{p_{t}}\right)=0.

(13)

It follows that
M_{t}:=\frac{\bar{p}}{p_{t}}(X_{t})
is a reverse-time local martingale with respect to the decreasing family
{\cal F}_{t}^{+}(X)=\sigma\left\{X_{\tau};\tau\geq t\right\}
. This follows also from Proposition
1
for diffusions with diffusion coefficient
\sigma^{2}
. In view of (
12
), the reverse-time Ito differential of
M_{t}
is

dM_{t}=\sigma\nabla\left(\frac{\bar{p}}{p_{t}}\right)(X_{t})d\bar{W}_{t}.

(14)

4
Varentropy

Define
-\ln p_{t}(x)
as the
local entropy
. We mention that
-\log p_{t}
, where
p_{t}
satisfies a Fokker-Planck equation, may be viewed as the value function of a stochastic control problem. In particular, in
[
10
, p.194]
,
-\log p(x,t)
was named
local entropy
and various of its properties were established. The corresponding optimal control is related to the so-called
score-function

\nabla\log p(x,t)
of generative models of machine learning based on flows. Local entropy was recently rediscovered in connection with an attempt to smooth the
energy landscape
of deep neural networks, see
[
11
]
and references therein.

Let
\psi(t,x)=\ln(p_{t}/\bar{p})
be the
local free energy
as in
[
10
]
. Then

\mbox{\BB E}\{\psi(t,X_{t})\}=\mbox{\BB D}(p_{t}\|\bar{p})=\int_{\mbox{\bb R}^{n}}\ln\frac{p_{t}}{\bar{p}}(x)p_{t}(x)dx,

the
relative entropy
or
divergence
or
Kullback-Leibler index
between
p_{t}
and
\bar{p}
.
Moreover, the free energy decays according to
[
4
]

\frac{d}{dt}\mbox{\BB E}\{\psi(t,X_{t})\}=\frac{d}{dt}\mbox{\BB D}(p_{t}\|\bar{p})=-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\int_{\mbox{\bb R}^{n}}\|\nabla\ln\frac{p_{t}}{\bar{p}}(x)\|^{2}p_{t}(x)dx.

(15)

Define the
Varentropy

{\cal V}_{KL}(p_{t}\|\bar{p})
as in
[
12
]

\displaystyle{\cal V}_{KL}(p_{t}\|\bar{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mbox{\bb R}^{n}}\left(\ln\frac{p_{t}(x)}{\bar{p}(x)}-\mbox{\BB D}(p_{t}\|\bar{p})\right)^{2}p_{t}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{\BB E}\left[\left(\psi(t,X_{t})-\mbox{\BB E}(\psi(t,X_{t})\right)^{2}\right]=\mbox{\BB E}\left\{\psi(t,X_{t})^{2}\right\}-\mbox{\BB E}\{\psi(t,X_{t})\}^{2}.

Hence, in view of (
15
),

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}{\cal V}_{KL}(p_{t}\|\bar{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mbox{\BB E}\left\{\psi(t,X_{t})^{2}\right\}-\frac{d}{dt}\left[\mbox{\BB D}(p_{t}\|\bar{p})^{2}\right]

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mbox{\BB E}\left\{\psi(t,X_{t})^{2}\right\}+\mbox{\BB D}(p_{t}\|\bar{p})\sigma^{2}\int_{\mbox{\bb R}^{n}}\|\nabla\ln\frac{p_{t}}{\bar{p}}(x)\|^{2}p_{t}(x)dx.

To understand the rate of change of the Varentropy, we need therefore to study

\frac{d}{dt}\mbox{\BB E}\left\{\psi(t,X_{t})^{2}\right\}=\frac{d}{dt}\mbox{\BB E}\left\{(-\ln M_{t})^{2}\right\},

where we recall that
M_{t}:=\frac{\bar{p}}{p_{t}}(X_{t})
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\psi(t,x)=\ln(p_{t}/\bar{p})
.The rate of change of the Varentropy is given by

\frac{d}{dt}{\cal V}_{KL}(p_{t}\|\bar{p})=\sigma^{2}\mbox{\BB E}\left\{\left[-\psi(t,X_{t})-1+\mbox{\BB E}\{\psi(t,X_{t})\}\right]\|\nabla\psi(t,X_{t})\|^{2}\right\}.

(17)

Proof.

We first compute the reverse-time differential of
N_{t}=(-\ln M_{t})^{2}=(\ln M_{t})^{2}=\left[\ln\frac{p_{t}}{\bar{p}}(X_{t})\right]^{2}=\psi(t,X_{t})^{2}
. Note that
f(x)=(\ln x)^{2}
is not convex nor concave on
x>0
and we have

f^{\prime}(x)=2\ln x\cdot\frac{1}{x},\quad f^{\prime\prime}(x)=\frac{2(1-\ln x)}{x^{2}}.

By (
14
) and the generalization of (
12
), we get

\displaystyle dN_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{df}{dx}(M_{t})dM_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d^{2}f}{dx^{2}}(M_{t})d\langle M\rangle_{t}=-\frac{1-\ln M_{t}}{M_{t}^{2}}\sigma^{2}\left\|\nabla\frac{p_{t}}{\bar{p}}\right\|^{2}dt+2\ln M_{t}\frac{1}{M_{t}}\nabla\frac{\bar{p}}{p_{t}}\sigma d\bar{W}_{t}

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}\left(-\ln\frac{p_{t}}{\bar{p}}(X_{t})-1\right)\|\nabla\ln\frac{p_{t}}{\bar{p}}(X_{t})\|^{2}dt+2\ln\frac{p_{t}}{\bar{p}}(X_{t})\nabla\ln\frac{p_{t}}{\bar{p}}(X_{t})\sigma d\bar{W}_{t}.

By a locatization argument, the stochastic integral

\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}2\ln\frac{p_{t}}{\bar{p}}(X_{t})\nabla\ln\frac{p_{t}}{\bar{p}}(X_{t})\sigma d\bar{W}_{t}

has zero expectation. Thus, in view of (
16
) and (
18
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}{\cal V}_{KL}(p_{t}\|\bar{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mbox{\BB E}\left[\left(\psi(t,X_{t})-\mbox{\BB E}(\psi(t,X_{t})\right)^{2}\right]

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mbox{\BB E}\left\{N_{t}\right\}+\mbox{\BB D}(p_{t}\|\bar{p})\sigma^{2}\int_{\mbox{\bb R}^{n}}\|\nabla\ln\frac{p_{t}}{\bar{p}}(x)\|^{2}p_{t}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}\mbox{\BB E}\left\{\left(-\ln\frac{p_{t}}{\bar{p}}(X_{t})-1\right)\|\nabla\ln\frac{p_{t}}{\bar{p}}(X_{t})\|^{2}\right\}+\mbox{\BB D}(p_{t}\|\bar{p})\sigma^{2}\int_{\mbox{\bb R}^{n}}\|\nabla\ln\frac{p_{t}}{\bar{p}}(x)\|^{2}p_{t}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}\mbox{\BB E}\left\{\left(-\ln\frac{p_{t}}{\bar{p}}(t,X_{t})-1+\mbox{\BB D}(p_{t}\|\bar{p})\right)\|\nabla\ln\frac{p_{t}}{\bar{p}}(X_{t})\|^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}\mbox{\BB E}\left\{\left[-\psi(t,X_{t})-1+\mbox{\BB E}\{\psi(t,X_{t})\}\right]\|\nabla\psi(t,X_{t})\|^{2}\right\}.

∎

5
The scalar Gaussian case

Consider the case of a scalar, zero-mean, Gaussian diffusion
\{X_{t};t\geq 0\}
with forward It
\hat{o}
differential

dX_{t}=-\frac{1}{2}X_{t}dt+dW_{t}

and initial condition
X_{0}
distributed according to

p_{0}(x)=\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left[-\frac{x^{2}}{\sigma_{0}^{2}}\right].

In this simple case, the time derivative of Varentropy can be computed explicitly.
The variance
\sigma_{t}^{2}
of
X_{t}
satisfies

\frac{d}{dt}(\sigma_{t}^{2})=-\sigma_{t}^{2}+1,\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sigma^{2}_{t}=\bar{\sigma}^{2}=1.

Thus the invariant density is simply the standard Normal

\bar{p}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left[-x^{2}\right].

Then

\psi(t,x)=\ln(p_{t}/\bar{p})(x)=\ln\left\{\frac{1}{\sigma_{t}}\exp\left[-\frac{x^{2}}{2}\left(-1+\frac{1}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}\right)\right]\right\}=-\ln\sigma_{t}-\frac{x^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{1-\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}\right)

It follows that

\mbox{\BB E}\{\psi(t,X_{t})\}=-\ln\sigma_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right).

Observe that

\dot{\sigma}_{t}=\frac{d}{dt}\left(\sqrt{\sigma_{t}^{2}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma_{t}^{2}}}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\sigma^{2}_{t}\right)=\frac{1}{2\sigma_{t}}\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right).

Then

\frac{d}{dt}\mbox{\BB E}\{\psi(t,X_{t})\}=-\frac{\dot{\sigma}_{t}}{\sigma_{t}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)=-\frac{1}{2\sigma_{t}^{2}}\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)^{2}}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}.

Recal that the Varentropy is

{\cal V}_{KL}(p_{t}\|\bar{p})=\mbox{\BB E}\left\{\psi(t,X_{t})^{2}\right\}-\mbox{\BB E}\{\psi(t,X_{t})\}^{2}.

We compute first
\mbox{\BB E}\left\{\psi(t,X_{t})^{2}\right\}
. We have

\psi(x,t)^{2}=\left[-\ln\sigma_{t}-\frac{x^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{1-\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}\right)\right]^{2}=\left(\ln\sigma_{t}\right)^{2}+\ln\sigma_{t}x^{2}\left(\frac{1-\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}\right)+\frac{x^{4}}{4}\left(\frac{1-\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}\right)^{2}.

As
X_{t}
ia a centered Gaussian
\mbox{\BB E}\{X_{t}^{4}\}=3\sigma_{t}^{4}
. Hence, we get

\mbox{\BB E}\left\{\psi(x,t)^{2}\right\}=\left(\ln\sigma_{t}\right)^{2}+\ln\sigma_{t}\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)+\frac{3}{4}\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)^{2}.

Thus,

\displaystyle{\cal V}_{KL}(p_{t}\|\bar{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\ln\sigma_{t}\right)^{2}+\ln\sigma_{t}\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)+\frac{3}{4}\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)^{2}-\left[\ln\sigma_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\ln\sigma_{t}\right)^{2}+\ln\sigma_{t}\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)+\frac{3}{4}\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)^{2}-\left(\ln\sigma_{t}\right)^{2}-\ln\sigma_{t}\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{4}\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)^{2}.

It follows that

\frac{d}{dt}{\cal V}_{KL}(p_{t}\|\bar{p})=-\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)\frac{d}{dt}\left(\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)=-\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)^{2}=-2{\cal V}_{KL}(p_{t}\|\bar{p}).

(20)

Hence, Varentropy is exponentially decaying according to

{\cal V}_{KL}(p_{t}\|\bar{p})={\cal V}_{KL}(p_{0}\|\bar{p})e^{-2t}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\sigma_{0}^{2}\right)^{2}e^{-2t}.

We now try to compute the Varentropy time derivative using formula (
19
)

\frac{d}{dt}{\cal V}_{KL}(p_{t}\|\bar{p})=\mbox{\BB E}\left\{\left[-\psi(t,X_{t})-1+\mbox{\BB E}\{\psi(t,X_{t})\}\right]\left\|\frac{\partial\psi(t,X_{t})}{\partial x}\right\|^{2}\right\}.

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}{\cal V}_{KL}(p_{t}\|\bar{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{\BB E}\left\{\left[\ln\sigma_{t}+\frac{X_{t}^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{1-\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}\right)-1-\ln\sigma_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)\right]\cdot\left[X_{t}^{2}\left(\frac{1-\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}\right)^{2}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{\BB E}\left\{\frac{X_{t}^{4}}{2}\left(\frac{1-\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}\right)^{3}\right\}-\mbox{\BB E}\left\{X_{t}^{2}\left(\frac{1-\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}\right)^{2}\right\}-\frac{1}{2}\mbox{\BB E}\left\{X_{t}^{2}\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)\left(\frac{1-\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}\right)^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3\sigma^{4}_{t}}{2}\left(\frac{1-\sigma_{t}^{2}}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}\right)^{3}-\frac{\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)^{2}}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)^{3}}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2\sigma_{t}^{2}}\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)^{3}-\frac{\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)^{2}}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)^{3}}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)^{3}}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}-\frac{\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)^{2}}{\sigma_{t}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(1-\sigma_{t}^{2}\right)^{2}

which coincides with (
20
).

6
Closing comments

Further study, possibly based on functional inequalities, is needed to establish if (
19
) implies that Varentropy
{\cal V}_{KL}(p_{t}\|\bar{p})
, in analogy to relative entropy
\mbox{\BB D}(p_{t}\|\bar{p})
, is always decreasing for Markov diffusions like in the Gaussian case. If not, there could be cases where Varentropy attains a maximum at some time
t_{M}
after which it decreases to zero in analogy to what is observed in some Markov chains models
[
12
]
.

On ordered Ramsey numbers of matchings versus triangles
Martin Balko, Marian Poljak

Abstract
For graphs G < G^{<} and H < H^{<} with linearly ordered vertex sets, the ordered Ramsey number r < ​ ( G < , H < ) r_{<}(G^{<},H^{<}) is the smallest positive integer N N such that any red-blue coloring of the edges of the complete ordered graph K N < K^{<}_{N} on N N vertices contains either a blue copy of G < G^{<} or a red copy of H < H^{<} . Motivated by a problem of Conlon, Fox, Lee, and Sudakov (2017), we study the numbers r < ​ ( M < , K 3 < ) r_{<}(M^{<},K^{<}_{3}) where M < M^{<} is an ordered matching on n n vertices. We prove that almost all n n -vertex ordered matchings M < M^{<} with interval chromatic number 2 satisfy r < ​ ( M < , K 3 < ) ∈ Ω ⁡ ( ( n / log ⁡ n ) 5 / 4 ) r_{<}(M^{<},K^{<}_{3})\in\Omega((n/\log n)^{5/4}) and r < ​ ( M < , K 3 < ) ∈ O ⁡ ( n 7 / 4 ) r_{<}(M^{<},K^{<}_{3})\in O(n^{7/4}) , improving a recent result by Rohatgi (2019). We also show that there are n n -vertex ordered matchings M < M^{<} with interval chromatic number at least 3 satisfying r < ​ ( M < , K 3 < ) ∈ Ω ⁡ ( ( n / log ⁡ n ) 4 / 3 ) r_{<}(M^{<},K^{<}_{3})\in\Omega((n/\log n)^{4/3}) , which asymptotically matches the best known lower bound on these off-diagonal ordered Ramsey numbers for general n n -vertex ordered matchings.

Introduction

For graphs
G
and
H
, their
Ramsey number

r(G,H)
is the smallest positive integer

such that any coloring of the edges of
K_{N}
with colors red and blue contains either
G
as a subgraph with all edges colored red or
H
as a subgraph with all edges colored blue.
The existence of these numbers was proved by Ramsey

and later independently by Erdős and Szekeres

.
The study of the growth rate of these numbers with respect to the number of vertices of
G
and
H
is a classical part of combinatorics and plays a central role in Ramsey theory.

Motivated by several applications from discrete geometry and extremal combinatorics, various researchers

;
bjv16
;
cp02
;
conlon
;
msw15
;

started investigating an ordered variant of Ramsey numbers.
An
ordered graph

is a graph

together with a linear ordering of its vertex set.
An ordered graph

is an
ordered subgraph
of another ordered graph
H^{<}
if
G
is a subgraph of
H
and the vertex ordering of
G
is a suborder of the vertex ordering of
H
.
The
ordered Ramsey number

r_{<}(G^{<},H^{<})
of ordered graphs
G^{<}
and
H^{<}
is the smallest positive integer
N
such that any coloring of the edges of the complete ordered graph
K^{<}_{N}
on
N
vertices with colors red and blue contains either
G^{<}
as an ordered subgraph with all edges red or
H^{<}
as an ordered subgraph with all edges blue.

It is known that the ordered Ramsey numbers always exist and that they can behave very differently from the unordered Ramsey numbers.
For example, there are ordered
matchings

M^{<}
(that is, 1-regular ordered graphs) on
n
vertices for which
r_{<}(M^{<},M^{<})
grows superpolynomially in

, in particular, we have
r_{<}(M^{<},M^{<})\in 2^{\Omega(\log^{2}{n}/\log{\log{n}})}

;

while

is linear for all graphs

with bounded maximum degrees

.
The superpolynomial bound obtained for ordered matchings is almost tight for sparse graphs as, for every fixed

, every

-degenerate ordered graph

on
n
vertices satisfies
r_{<}(G^{<},G^{<})\in 2^{O(\log^{2}{n})}

.

One of the most interesting cases for ordered Ramsey numbers is the study of the growth rate of

where

is an ordered matching on

Conlon, Fox, Lee, and Sudakov

.
On the other hand, they showed that there exists a positive constant

such that, for all even positive integers

, there is an ordered matching

on

vertices with

Conlon, Fox, Lee, and Sudakov expect that the upper bound

Problem 1
(

Does there exist an

such that for any ordered matching

on

Problem

is one of the most important questions in the theory of ordered Ramsey numbers as in order to get a subquadratic upper bound on

one has to be able to employ the sparsity of

since the bound

Being able to use the sparsity of

However, this is difficult as some ordered matchings

can be used to approximate the behavior of complete graphs, which is the reason why the numbers

Some partial progress on Problem

The interval chromatic number can be understood as an analogue of the chromatic number for ordered graphs as, for example, there is a variant of the Erdős–Stone–Simonovits theorem for ordered graphs

(

There is a constant

on

Motivated by Problem

We strengthen some bounds by Rohatgi

Proof of Theorem

Here, we show that for all positive integers

on

The proof is carried out using a similar probabilistic argument used by Conlon, Fox, Lee, and Sudakov

We use the following result about the edge-density between disjoint intervals in a random ordered matching with interval chromatic number 2.

Lemma 1

Let

be a uniform random ordered matching on

contains an edge between any two intervals

and

, respectively.

Proof

For sets

and

with

, the probability that

has no edge between

and

\left(\frac{n-t}{n}\right)\left(\frac{n-t-1}{n-1}\right)\cdots\left(\frac{n-2t+1}{n-t+1}\right)\leq\left(\frac{n-t}{n}\right)^{t}\leq e^{-t^{2}/n}

for every

and

for every

.
There are at most

between them is at most

This probability goes to 0 with increasing

Thus, it suffices to take

Now, consider disjoint subsets

Set

We show that asymptotically almost surely there are at most

between

and

This is trivial for

and

Thus, we assume

Then, the probability that there are

\displaystyle\binom{2\sqrt{n\log n}}{r}\left(\frac{s}{n}\right)\left(\frac{s-1}{n-1}\right)\cdots\left(\frac{s-r+1}{n-r+1}\right)

Lemma 2
(The Lovász local lemma)

Let
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}\}
be a finite set of events in a probability space.
A directed graph
D=(V,E)
is the
dependency graph
of
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}
if each event
A_{i}
is mutually independent of all the events from
\{A_{j}\colon(i,j)\notin E\}
.
Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
be real numbers such that
0\leq x_{i}<1
and
\Pr[A_{i}]\leq x_{i}\prod_{(i,j)\in E}(1-x_{j})
for every
i\in[n]
.
Then,

\Pr\left[\overline{A_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap\overline{A_{n}}\right]\geq\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-x_{i}).

In particular, the probability that none of the events
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}
hold is positive.

on

satisfies at least one of the following three claims:

(a)

\chi
contains a blue copy of

,

(b)

\chi
contains a red copy of

, or

\chi
contains a red copy of

between

Proof

Let
\chi

on
[2N]
.
Suppose for contradiction that
\chi
satisfies none of the three claims from the statement of the theorem.

Let

be the matrix

(i,j)\in[N]\times[N]
contains the color of the edge
\{i,N+j\}
in
\chi
.
We set
T=\sqrt{n/\ell}
and we run the multi-thread scanning for

in

with
T

For every
t\in\{0,1,\dots,T-1\}
, the blue entries from the thread
Th(t)
intersect each row of
A
in a set that we call

.
Let
S(t)
be the set of segments obtained from
Th(t)
.

Observe that each segment forms an interval of blue entries in a row of
A
.
Moreover, each segment has length less than
2n
as otherwise there is a vertex of
K_{2N}^{<}
incident to at least
2n
blue edges in
\chi
and, since there is no blue triangle in
\chi
, the neighborhood of such a vertex induces a red copy of
K^{<}_{2n}
.
This is impossible by our assumptions on
\chi
.

Claim

Fix
t
and
t^{\prime}
with
0\leq t^{\prime}<t\leq T
.
Assume that
k
segments in
S(t)
intersect with some segments from
S(t^{\prime})
.
Then,
L(\pi_{M^{<}})\geq k
.

Each segment from
S(t)
intersects at most one segment from
S(t^{\prime})
as no two segments from
S(t)
lie in the same row of
A
and the same claim is true for segments from
S(t^{\prime})
.
Moreover, if two segments intersect, then they are contained in the same row of
A
.
Let
s^{t}_{1}
and
s^{t}_{2}
be two segments from
S(t)
and let
s^{t^{\prime}}_{1}
and
s^{t^{\prime}}_{2}
be two segments from
S(t^{\prime})
and assume
s^{t}_{1}\cap s^{t^{\prime}}_{1}\neq\emptyset
and
s^{t}_{2}\cap s^{t^{\prime}}_{2}\neq\emptyset
.
Then, the columns of
A
intersected by
s^{t}_{1}
are to the left of the columns intersected by
s^{t}_{2}
if and only if the columns of
A
intersected by
s^{t^{\prime}}_{1}
are to the left of the columns intersected by
s^{t^{\prime}}_{2}
.
This is because no two segments from
S(t)
intersect the same column from
A
and the same claim is true for segments from
S(t^{\prime})
.
Altogether, the
k
segments from
S(t)
intersect exactly
k
segments in
S(t^{\prime})
and indices of their rows decreased by
t
and
t-t^{\prime}
, respectively, form a shift in
\pi_{M^{<}}
of size
k
.
The claim follows.

Consider some
t\in\{0,1,\dots,T-1\}
.
Since no thread succeeds in
\chi
, the thread
Th(t)
reveals at least
N-n
blue entries of
A
.
The claim and our assumption
L(\pi_{M^{<}})\leq\ell
imply that the segments from
S(t)
intersect at most
\ell
segments from
S(t^{\prime})
for every
t^{\prime}<t
.
Since each segment has length at most

, the thread
Th(t)
reveals at least

.
This is at least
N/2
by our assumption

and by the choice of

since

N-2n-2tn\ell\geq N-2n(T\ell+1)=N-2n(\sqrt{n\ell}+1)\geq N/2.

Thus, the total number of blue entries in

is at least

.
Since the multi-thread scanning visited
n+T

.

\frac{TN}{2(T+n)}\geq\frac{\sqrt{\frac{n}{\ell}}4n(\sqrt{n\ell}+1)}{2\left(\sqrt{\frac{n}{\ell}}+n\right)}=2n.

Thus, the blue neighborhood of the vertex

contains either a blue triangle or a red copy of

.
This contradicts our assumptions on
\chi
.
∎

For every
\varepsilon>0
, Theorem
6.1
immediately implies that

for every ordered matching with

Now, it suffices to show that Theorem

Proof
(Proof of Theorem

Derived p p -adic heights and the leading coefficient of the Bertolini–Darmon–Prasanna p p -adic L L -function
Francesc Castella, Chi-Yun Hsu, Debanjana Kundu, Yu-Sheng Lee, Zheng Liu

Abstract
. Let E / 𝐐 E/\mathbf{Q} be an elliptic curve and let p p be an odd prime of good reduction for E E . Let K K be an imaginary quadratic field satisfying the classical Heegner hypothesis and in which p p splits. The goal of this paper is two-fold: (1) We formulate a p p -adic BSD conjecture for the p p -adic L L -function L 𝔭 BDP L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\rm BDP} introduced by Bertolini–Darmon–Prasanna [ BDP13 ] . (2) For an algebraic analogue F 𝔭 ¯ BDP F_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\rm BDP} of L 𝔭 BDP L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\rm BDP} , we show that the “leading coefficient” part of our conjecture holds, and that the “order of vanishing” part follows from the expected “maximal non-degeneracy” of an anticyclotomic p p -adic height. In particular, when the Iwasawa–Greenberg Main Conjecture ( F 𝔭 ¯ BDP ) = ( L 𝔭 BDP ) (F_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\rm BDP})=(L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\rm BDP}) is known, our results determine the leading coefficient of L 𝔭 BDP L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\rm BDP} at T = 0 T=0 up to a p p -adic unit. Moreover, by adapting the approach of Burungale–Castella–Kim [ BCK21 ] , we prove the main conjecture for supersingular primes p p under mild hypotheses. In the p p -ordinary case, and under some additional hypotheses, similar results were obtained by Agboola–Castella [ AC21 ] , but our method is new and completely independent from theirs, and apply to all good primes.

1.2.
p
-adic analogue of BSD for
L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\rm BDP}

For the formulation of our conjecture, we assume that the triple
(E,K,p)
satisfies the following additional hypotheses:

(h0)

E(K)[p]=0,

and that for every
\mathfrak{q}\in\{\mathfrak{p},\overline{\mathfrak{p}}\}
the restriction map

(h1)

{\rm res}_{\mathfrak{q}}:\check{S}_{p}(E/K)\rightarrow E(K_{\mathfrak{q}})\otimes\mathbf{Z}_{p}

has non-torsion image, where
\check{S}_{p}(E/K)=\varprojlim_{k}{\rm Sel}_{p^{k}}(E/K)
is the usual compact Selmer group. (Note that (
h1
) is implied by the finiteness of
\Sha(E/K)[p^{\infty}]
, since by the
p
-parity conjecture
[
Nek01
,
Kim07
]
, hypothesis (
Heeg
) implies that the Selmer group
\check{S}_{p}(E/K)
has odd
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-rank.)

Denote by
E(K_{\mathfrak{q}})_{/{\rm tor}}
the quotient of
E(K_{\mathfrak{q}})
by its torsion submodule, and let
{\rm res}_{\mathfrak{q}/{\rm tor}}
be the composition of
{\rm res}_{\mathfrak{q}}
with the projection
E(K_{\mathfrak{q}})\otimes\mathbf{Z}_{p}\rightarrow E(K_{\mathfrak{q}})_{/{\rm tor}}\otimes\mathbf{Z}_{p}
.
Let

T=\varprojlim_{k}E[p^{k}]

be the
p
-adic Tate module of
E
,
and set
{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,T)={\rm ker}({\rm res}_{\mathfrak{q}/{\rm tor}})
.

In Section
3
, building on Howard’s theory of derived
p
-adic heights
[
How04
]
, we construct a filtration

{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,T)\supset\underleftarrow{\mathfrak{S}}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{(1)}\supset\underleftarrow{\mathfrak{S}}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{(2)}\supset\cdots\supset\underleftarrow{\mathfrak{S}}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{(i)}\supset\cdots,

with
\underleftarrow{\mathfrak{S}}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(i)}=\{0\}
for
i\gg 0
,
equipped with a sequence of “derived”
p
-adic height pairings

(1.1)

h_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(i)}:\underleftarrow{\mathfrak{S}}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(i)}\times\underleftarrow{\mathfrak{S}}_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}^{(i)}\rightarrow J^{i}/J^{i+1},

where
J\subset\Lambda
is the augmentation ideal.
Using these pairings, we define a
p
-adic regulator

{\rm Reg}_{\mathfrak{p},{\rm der}}\in\bigl((J^{\sigma}/J^{\sigma+1})\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}\mathbf{Q}_{p}\bigr)/\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times},

where
\sigma=\sum_{i\geq 1}i\cdot{\rm rank}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}(\underleftarrow{\mathfrak{S}}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(i)}/\underleftarrow{\mathfrak{S}}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(i+1)})
. By construction,
{\rm Reg}_{\mathfrak{p},{\rm der}}
is
always
nonzero.

Set

r:={\rm rank}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}\check{S}_{p}(E/K).

Under hypotheses (
h0
)-(
h1
), the Selmer groups
{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,T)\subset\check{S}_{p}(E/K)
are free
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-modules of rank
r-1
and
r
, respectively.
Let
(s_{1},\dots,s_{r-1})
be a
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-basis for
{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{p}}(K,T)
, and extend it to a
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-basis
(s_{1},\dots,s_{r-1},s_{\mathfrak{p}})
for
\check{S}_{p}(E/K)
.
In particular,
{\rm res}_{\mathfrak{p}/{\rm tor}}(s_{\mathfrak{p}})\neq 0
.

The following is our
p
-adic BSD conjecture for
L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\rm BDP}
.

Conjecture 1.1

(
p
-adic BSD conjecture for
L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\rm BDP}
)
.

Assume
(
h0
)
-
(
h1
)
.

(i)

(Leading Coefficient Formula)
Let

\varrho_{\rm an}={\rm ord}_{J}L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\rm BDP}:=\max\{i\geq 0\,\colon\,L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\rm BDP}\in J^{i}\},

and denote by
\overline{L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\rm BDP}}
the natural image of
L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\rm BDP}
in
J^{\varrho_{\rm an}}/J^{\varrho_{\rm an}+1}
.
Then, up to a
p
-adic unit

\overline{L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\rm BDP}}=\biggl(\frac{1-a_{p}(E)+p}{p}\biggr)^{2}\cdot{\rm log}_{\mathfrak{p}}(s_{\mathfrak{p}})^{2}\cdot{\rm Reg}_{\mathfrak{p},{\rm der}}\cdot\#\Sha_{\rm BK}(K,W)\cdot\prod_{\ell\mid N}c_{\ell}^{2}.

(ii)

(Order of Vanishing)

Let
r^{\pm}
denote the
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-rank of the
\pm
-eigenspace of
\check{S}_{p}(E/K)
under the action of complex conjugation.
Then,

\varrho_{\rm an}=2(\max\{r^{+},r^{-}\}-1).

Here

is the Tamagawa number of
E/\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}
,
W=E[p^{\infty}]
, and
\Sha_{\rm BK}(K,W)={\rm Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K)_{/{\rm div}}

is the Bloch–Kato Tate–Shafarevich group, i.e., the quotient of the
p^{\infty}
-Selmer group
{\rm Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K)
by its maximal divisible submodule.
Also,

{\rm log}_{\mathfrak{p}}:\check{S}_{p}(E/K)\rightarrow\mathbf{Z}_{p}

denotes the composition
{\rm log}_{\omega_{E}}\circ{\rm res}_{\mathfrak{p}/{\rm tor}}
.

Remark 1.2
.

In the
p
-ordinary case, a variant of Conjecture

was formulated in
[

]
,
with a regulator defined using the theory of derived

-adic heights by Bertolini–Darmon

. An important advantage of the present formulation in comparison with the formulation of

is that the latter requires a hypothesis on the derived

-adic heights amounting to the requirement that

for

for the definition of their derived

-adic regulator in [

Remark 1.3
.

Assuming
\#\Sha(E/K)[p^{\infty}]<\infty
for the usual Tate–Shafarevich group

, it is possible to remove the ambiguity by a

-adic unit in the formulation of Conjecture

(i).
Indeed, following an observation from
[

, this can be achieved as follows: in this case

\check{S}_{p}(E/K)\simeq E(K)\otimes\mathbf{Z}_{p}=(E(K)/E(K)_{\rm tor})\otimes\mathbf{Z}_{p}\simeq\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{r},

using (
h0
) for the middle equality.
Let
M
be an endomorphism of
\check{S}_{p}(E/K)
sending a
\mathbf{Z}
-basis
(P_{1},\dots,P_{r})
of

to
(s_{1},\dots,s_{r-1},s_{\mathfrak{p}})

Then it suffices to replace
{\rm Reg}_{\mathfrak{p},{\rm der}}
in the right-hand side of Conjecture

(i) by the modification

{\rm det}(M)^{-2}\cdot{\rm Reg}_{\mathfrak{p},{\rm der}},

which is a well-defined element in
(J^{\sigma}/J^{\sigma+1})\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}\mathbf{Q}_{p}
and is independent of the choice of

.

Remark 1.4
.

When
{\rm ord}_{s=1}L(E/K,s)=1

(ii) follows immediately from (

) and the work of Gross–Zagier and Kolyvagin.
In this case,

(i) is

(see Proposition

1.3.
Main results

By the Iwasawa–Greenberg Main Conjecture (see Conjecture
2.4
),
L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\rm BDP}
should generate the characteristic ideal of a
\Lambda
-adic Selmer group denoted
X_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}={\rm Sel}_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}(K_{\infty},W)^{\vee}

in the body of the paper.
This module is known to be
\Lambda
-torsion under hypothesis (
h0
) (see Proposition
2.5
).

The main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
F_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\rm BDP}\in\Lambda
be a generator of
{\rm char}_{\Lambda}(X_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}})
, and put

\varrho_{\rm alg}={\rm ord}_{J}F_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\rm BDP}:=\max\{i\geq 0\,\colon\,F_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\rm BDP}\in J^{i}\}.

(i)

Let
\overline{F_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\rm BDP}}
be the natural image of
F_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\rm BDP}
in
J^{\varrho_{\rm alg}}/J^{{\varrho_{\rm alg}}+1}
.
Then, up to a
p
-adic unit

\overline{F_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\rm BDP}}=\biggl(\frac{1-a_{p}(E)+p}{p}\biggr)^{2}\cdot{\rm log}_{\mathfrak{p}}(s_{\mathfrak{p}})^{2}\cdot{\rm Reg}_{\mathfrak{p},{\rm der}}\cdot\#\Sha_{\rm BK}(K,W)\cdot\prod_{\ell\mid N}c_{\ell}^{2}.

(ii)

Furthermore,

{\varrho_{\rm alg}}\geq 2(\max\{r^{+},r^{-}\}-1),

with equality if and only if
{\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}\underleftarrow{\mathfrak{S}}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2)}=\left|r^{+}-r^{-}\right|-1
and
\underleftarrow{\mathfrak{S}}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(i)}=0
for
i\geq 3
.

Set
h_{\mathfrak{p}}:=h_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(1)}
.
We say that
h_{\mathfrak{p}}
is
maximally non-degenerate
when the condition for equality in the last part of Theorem
1.5
holds.
In the
p
-ordinary case, conjectures due to Mazur and Bertolini–Darmon (
[
BD95
, §3]
)
imply that
h_{\mathfrak{p}}
is maximally non-degenerate (see Remark
6.14
), and we expect this condition to hold for all good primes
p>2
.
(For the second assertion in Theorem
1.5
(ii), note that hypothesis (
Heeg
) and the
p
-parity conjecture imply that
r^{+}
and
r^{-}
have different parities, and so
\left|r^{+}-r^{-}\right|-1\geq 0
.)

As a consequence, when the Iwasawa–Greenberg Main Conjecture

(1.2)

\bigl(F_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\rm BDP}\bigr)\overset{?}{=}\bigl(L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\rm BDP}\bigr)

is known, Theorem
1.5
implies Conjecture
1.1
(i) (up to a
p
-adic unit). For ordinary primes, a proof of the Main Conjecture (
1.2
) was obtained in
[
BCK21
]
. In Section
7
we extend their methods to the supersingular case, leading to the following result.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
p>2
be a prime of good supersingular reduction for
E
, and let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field satisfying (
Heeg
) and (
spl
). Assume in addition that:

(i)

N
is square-free.

(ii)

E[p]
is ramified at every prime
\ell\mid N^{+}
.

(iii)

E[p]
is ramified at every prime
\ell\mid N^{-}
with
\ell\equiv\pm{1}\pmod{p}
.

(iv)

Every prime above
p
is totally ramified in
K_{\infty}/K
.

Then
X_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}
is
\Lambda
-torsion, with

{\rm char}_{\Lambda}\bigl(X_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}\bigr)=\bigl(L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\rm BDP}\bigr).

In other words, the Iwasawa–Greenberg Main Conjecture (
1.2
) holds.

Hence (together with the proof of (
1.2
) in
[
BCK21
]
in the
p
-ordinary case, since Theorem
1.5
also applies to this case), our results in this paper determine (up to a
p
-adic unit) the leading coefficient of
L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\rm BDP}
at
T=0
for all primes
p>2
of good reduction, and reduce a full proof of Conjecture
1.1
to the expected maximal non-degeneracy of
h_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

1.4.
Relation to prior work

In the
p
-ordinary case, a version of Theorem
1.5
was obtained in
[
AC21
, Thm. 6.12]
under some additional hypotheses, including:

(i)

a_{p}(E)\not\equiv 1\pmod{p}
,

(ii)

p\nmid c_{\ell}
for all primes
\ell\mid N
,

(iii)

\#\Sha(E/K)[p^{\infty}]<\infty
.

As noted in [
op. cit.
, §6.2], their proof is largely based on an adaptation of the arguments from
[
BD95
]
, and hypotheses (i) and (ii) are essential to their method. Our proof, building on the approach to derived
p
-adic heights later developed by Howard
[
How04
]
, is completely different from and independent of
[
AC21
]
, and we are able to dispense with hypotheses (i)–(iii) (and obtain a result also in the
p
-supersingular case).

Back to the
p
-ordinary case, Sano
[
San23
]
has also given a new proof of
[
AC21
, Thm. 6.12]
removing hypotheses (i) and (ii)
by building on an extension of Nekovář’s descent formalism
[
Nek06
, §11.6]
using “derived” Bockstein maps; his methods are also different from ours.

Finally we note that for supersingular primes
p>2
, the results of Theorem
1.5
is new for all cases
\varrho_{\rm alg}>0
; on the other hand, the case
\varrho_{\rm alg}=0
of Theorem
1.5
(in which case
{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,T)=0
and
p
-adic heights make no appearance)
can be deduced from the “anticyclotomic control theorem” of Jetchev–Skinner–Wan
[
JSW17
]
,
but our results also give a new proof in this case.

1.5.
Method of proof

In a series of papers culminating in
[
PR92
]
(see also
[
Col00
]
), Perrin-Riou developed a method for computing the leading coefficient of algebraic
p
-adic
L
-functions in terms of arithmetic invariants.
In the setting of this paper, her methods allow one to determine a formula for
\varrho_{\rm alg}
and the image
\overline{F_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\rm BDP}}\in J^{\varrho_{\rm alg}}/J^{{\varrho_{\rm alg}}+1}
(up to a
p
-adic unit) precisely when the sequence

0\rightarrow\underleftarrow{\mathfrak{S}}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(\infty)}\otimes\mathbf{Q}_{p}\rightarrow{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{p}}(K,V)\xrightarrow{h_{\mathfrak{p}}}{\rm Hom}({\rm Sel}_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}(K,V),\mathbf{Q}_{p})\rightarrow{\rm Hom}\bigl(\underleftarrow{\mathfrak{S}}_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}^{(\infty)}\otimes\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathbf{Q}_{p}\bigr)\rightarrow 0

is exact, where
V=T\otimes\mathbf{Q}_{p}
is the rational
p
-adic Tate module,
\underleftarrow{\mathfrak{S}}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{(\infty)}\otimes\mathbf{Q}_{p}=\cap_{i\geq 1}\underleftarrow{\mathfrak{S}}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{(i)}\otimes\mathbf{Q}_{p}
is the space of universal norms in
{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,V)
,
and the middle arrow is defined by
the
p
-adic height pairing
h_{\mathfrak{p}}
(see
[
PR92
, Thm. 3.3.4]
).
However, in the setting of this paper one can show that

\underleftarrow{\mathfrak{S}}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{(\infty)}=0,

(see Proposition
2.5
and Corollary
3.11
(ii)), and therefore exactness of (
1.5
) amounts to the
non-degeneracy
of
h_{\mathfrak{p}}
, which in the anticyclotomic setting fails as long as
\left|r^{+}-r^{-}\right|>1
(see Proposition
6.11
).

The key technical innovation of this paper is the development of an extension of the results of
[
PR92
]
applicable to
X_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}
,
building on Howard’s theory of derived
p
-adic heights
[
How04
]
to account for the systematic degeneracies of
h_{\mathfrak{p}}
in the anticyclotomic setting.

3.1.
\Lambda_{k}
-adic Selmer groups

Fix a topological generator

Adopting the notations in

(3.1)

0\rightarrow\Lambda_{k}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}_{k}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{k}\rightarrow 0.

By Shapiro’s lemma, we have a canonical
\Lambda_{k}[G_{K,\Sigma}]
-module isomorphism

[

(

\gamma

Tensoring (
3.1
) over
\mathcal{O}_{k}

with
S^{(k)}
, we then obtain

(3.2)

0\rightarrow S_{\rm Iw}^{(k)}\rightarrow S_{\mathcal{K}}^{(k)}\rightarrow S_{\infty}^{(k)}\rightarrow 0,

dropping the subscripts in
{\mathcal{K}}_{k}
for the ease of notation.

For
\mathfrak{q}\in\{\mathfrak{p},\overline{\mathfrak{p}}\}

\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{q}}

{\rm H}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{q}}}(K_{w},S_{\mathcal{K}}^{(k)})=\begin{cases}{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm unr}(K_{w},S_{{\mathcal{K}}}^{(k)})&\textrm{if $w\nmid p\infty$,}\\

3.2.
Howard’s derived
p
-adic heights

For every

Theorem 3.2

(Howard)
.

For
i\geq 1
, there is a sequence of canonical symmetric
i
-th “derived” height pairings

such that the kernel on the left (resp. right) is
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathfrak{q},k}^{(i+1)}
(resp.
\mathfrak{S}_{\overline{\mathfrak{q}},k}^{(i+1)}
).

Proof.

By definition (
3.3
), the local conditions cutting out
\mathfrak{Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\mathcal{K}}^{(k)})
and
\mathfrak{Sel}_{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}}(K,S_{\mathcal{K}}^{(k)})

{\rm H}^{1}(K_{w},S_{\mathcal{K}}^{(k)})\times{\rm H}^{1}(K_{w},S_{\mathcal{K}}^{(k)})\rightarrow{\rm H}^{2}(K_{w},{\mathcal{K}}(1))\simeq{\mathcal{K}}

induced by the Weil pairing
S^{(k)}\times S^{(k)}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{k}(1)
.
Therefore, by
[
How04
, Thm. 1.11]
there is a canonical symmetric

height pairing

\tilde{h}_{\mathfrak{q},k}:\mathfrak{Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty}^{(k)})\times\mathfrak{Sel}_{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}}(K,S_{\infty}^{(k)})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{k}.

(As written here, the pairing
\tilde{h}_{\mathfrak{q},k}
depends on the choice of a topological generator
\gamma\in\Gamma
, but
the
J/J^{2}
-valued pairing
(\gamma-1)\cdot\tilde{h}_{\mathfrak{q},k}

is independent of
\gamma
, as one checks immediately from
[
How04
, Lem. 1.10(b)]

Note that
J^{i-1}\mathfrak{Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty}^{(k)})[J]
is the image of the injection

(3.5)

\phi_{i,\gamma}:\frac{\mathfrak{Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty}^{(k)})[J^{i}]}{\mathfrak{Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty}^{(k)})[J^{i-1}]}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty}^{(k)})[J]

given by multiplication by
(\gamma-1)^{i-1}
.
Thus we may define

\tilde{h}_{\mathfrak{q},k}^{(i)}:J^{i-1}\mathfrak{Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty}^{(k)})[J]\times J^{i-1}\mathfrak{Sel}_{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}}(K,S_{\infty}^{(k)})[J]\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{k}

\tilde{h}_{\mathfrak{q},k}^{(1)}
is the restriction of
\tilde{h}_{\mathfrak{q},k}
to

) restricts to an isomorphism

\mathfrak{S}_{\mathfrak{q},k}^{(i)}\simeq J^{i-1}\mathfrak{Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty}^{(k)})[J],

we can transfer

to

via this isomorphism.
Thus, we get a sequence of pairings

with the stated properties.

We now compare the Selmer groups

and

Let
{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty}^{(k)})
be the Selmer group cut out by the local conditions

{\rm H}^{1}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K_{w},S_{\infty}^{(k)})=\begin{cases}{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm unr}(K_{w},S_{\infty}^{(k)})&\textrm{if $w\nmid p\infty$,}\\
{\rm H}^{1}(K_{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}},S_{\infty}^{(k)})&\textrm{if $w=\overline{\mathfrak{q}}$,}\\

Putting
S_{\infty}=\varinjlim_{k}S_{\infty}^{(k)}

{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty}):=\varinjlim_{k}{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty}^{(k)}),\quad

where the limit is with respect to the maps induced by the inclusion

Lemma 3.3
.

For every
\mathfrak{q}\in\{\mathfrak{p},\overline{\mathfrak{p}}\}
there is a canonical
\Lambda
-module isomorphism

{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty})\simeq{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K_{\infty},W).

Proof.

By Shapiro’s lemma, we have
{\rm H}^{1}(G_{K,\Sigma},S_{\infty})\simeq{\rm H}^{1}(G_{K_{\infty},\Sigma},W)
. We check that under the above isomorphism, the Selmer groups in the statement are cut out by the same local conditions. For the primes
w
above
p
, this is clear. For the primes
w\in\Sigma_{f}\setminus\{\mathfrak{p},\overline{\mathfrak{p}}\}
, we need to check that
\varinjlim_{k}{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm unr}(K_{w},S_{\infty}^{(k)})=0
, but this follows from
[
How04
, Lem. 1.7]
, since by our assumption on
\Sigma

these primes are split in

, so they are finitely decomposed in
K_{\infty}/K
. ∎

Remark 3.4
.

In light of Lemma
3.3
,
we shall henceforth write
{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty})
and
{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K_{\infty},W)
interchangeably.

Directly from the definitions we have the inclusion
\mathfrak{Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty}^{(k)})\subset{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty}^{(k)})
.
On the other hand, the natural surjection

(3.6)

{\rm H}^{1}(G_{K,\Sigma},S_{\infty}^{(k)})\rightarrow{\rm H}^{1}(G_{K,\Sigma},S_{\infty})[p^{k}]

induces a map
\alpha_{\mathfrak{q}}:{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty}^{(k)})\rightarrow{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty})[p^{k}]
.

Proposition 3.5
.

For every
\mathfrak{q}\in\{\mathfrak{p},\overline{\mathfrak{p}}\}
, the composition

\mathfrak{Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty}^{(k)})\xrightarrow{\subset}{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty}^{(k)})\xrightarrow{\alpha_{\mathfrak{q}}}{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty})[p^{k}]

is injective with finite cokernel of bounded order as
k\to\infty
.

Proof.

By (
h0
), the map (
3.6
) is an injection, and therefore so is
\alpha_{\mathfrak{q}}
.
To bound the cokernel of the map in the statement, we bound the cokernel of each of the two maps in the composition.

From the definitions, we see that the quotient
{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty}^{(k)})/\mathfrak{Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty}^{(k)})
injects into

(3.7)

\frac{{\rm H}^{1}(K_{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}},S_{\infty}^{(k)})}{{\rm im}\bigl\{{\rm H}^{1}(K_{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}},S_{\mathcal{K}}^{(k)})\rightarrow{\rm H}^{1}(K_{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}},S_{\infty}^{(k)})\bigr\}}\simeq{\rm ker}\bigl\{{\rm H}^{2}(K_{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}},S_{\rm Iw}^{(k)})\rightarrow{\rm H}^{2}(K_{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}},S_{\mathcal{K}}^{(k)})\bigr\}.

By local duality, this is bounded by the size of

\bigoplus_{\eta\mid\overline{\mathfrak{q}}}E(K_{\infty,\eta})[p^{k}]
.
Since
\bigoplus_{\eta\mid\overline{\mathfrak{q}}}E(K_{\infty,\eta})[p^{\infty}]
is finite by
[
KO20
, Lem. 2.7]
, the above quotient has the desired bound.

On the other hand, for the primes
w\in\Sigma_{f}\setminus\{\mathfrak{p},\overline{\mathfrak{p}}\}
,
[
How04
, Lem. 1.7]
gives

{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm unr}(K_{w},S_{\infty}^{(k)})={\rm H}^{1}_{\rm unr}(K_{w},S_{\infty})=0.

Therefore, by the snake lemma we see that the cokernel of
\alpha_{\mathfrak{q}}
is bounded by the kernel of the restriction map

(3.8)

(r_{w})_{w}:\bigoplus_{w\in\Sigma_{f}\setminus\{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}\}}{\rm H}^{1}(K_{w},S_{\infty}^{(k)})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{w\in\Sigma_{f}\setminus\{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}\}}{\rm H}^{1}(K_{w},S_{\infty})[p^{k}].

From the cohomology long exact sequence associated with multiplication by
p^{k}
we see that
{\rm ker}(r_{w})={\rm H}^{0}(K_{w},S_{\infty})/p^{k}{\rm H}^{0}(K_{w},S_{\infty})
, and this is bounded by

(3.9)

\prod_{w\in\Sigma_{f}\setminus\{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}\}}\#((B_{w})_{/{\rm div}}),\quad\textrm{where $B_{w}:=\bigoplus_{\eta\mid w}E(K_{\infty,\eta})[p^{\infty}]$,}

which is clearly finite and independent of
k
. Thus
\#{\rm coker}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{q}})
has bounded order as
k\to\infty
, whence the result.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

With notation as above, the following equality of
\Lambda
-modules holds

\varinjlim_{k}\mathfrak{Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty}^{(k)})={\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty}).

Proof.

By Proposition
3.5
,
\varinjlim_{k}\mathfrak{Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty}^{(k)})
is contained in
{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty})
with finite index, so the result follows from Proposition
2.6
.
∎

The next result is a variant of Mazur’s control theorem for our
\mathfrak{q}
-strict Selmer groups.

Proposition 3.7
.

The map
W\rightarrow S_{\infty}
induces an injection

\beta_{\mathfrak{q}}:{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,W)\rightarrow{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty})[J]

with finite cokernel.

Proof.

The map
\beta_{\mathfrak{q}}
is the restriction of the natural map
{\rm H}^{1}(G_{K,\Sigma},W)\rightarrow{\rm H}^{1}(G_{K,\Sigma},S_{\infty})
induced by
W\rightarrow S_{\infty}
.
This natural map is part of the cohomology long exact sequence induced by

0\rightarrow W\rightarrow S_{\infty}\xrightarrow{\gamma-1}S_{\infty}\rightarrow 0,

and by (
h0
), it induces an isomorphism
{\rm H}^{1}(G_{K,\Sigma},W)\simeq{\rm H}^{1}(G_{K,\Sigma},S_{\infty})[J]
.
The injectivity of
\beta_{\mathfrak{q}}
follows from this.
On the other hand, from the definitions and a direct application of the snake lemma, we see that the cokernel of
\beta_{\mathfrak{q}}
is bounded by the kernel of the restriction map

(3.10)

(r_{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}},(r_{w})_{w}):\frac{{\rm H}^{1}(K_{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}},W)}{{\rm H}^{1}(K_{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}},W)_{\rm div}}\times\prod_{w\in\Sigma_{f}\setminus\{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}\}}{\rm H}^{1}(K_{w},W)\rightarrow\{0\}\times\prod_{w\in\Sigma_{f}\setminus\{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}\}}\prod_{\eta\mid w}{\rm H}^{1}(K_{\infty,\eta},W).

For
w=\overline{\mathfrak{q}}
, we compute

(3.11)

\displaystyle{\rm ker}(r_{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}})

\displaystyle={\rm H}^{1}(K_{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}},T)_{\rm tors}\quad\textrm{by local Tate duality}

\displaystyle={\rm ker}\{{\rm H}^{1}(K_{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}},T)\rightarrow{\rm H}^{1}(K_{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}},V)\}

\displaystyle={\rm coker}\{{\rm H}^{0}(K_{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}},V)\rightarrow{\rm H}^{0}(K_{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}},W)\},

where the last equality follows from the cohomology long exact sequence associated to
T\hookrightarrow V\twoheadrightarrow W
.
Since
{\rm H}^{0}(K_{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}},V)=0
, this shows that

(3.12)

\#{\rm ker}(r_{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}})=\#{\rm H}^{0}(K_{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}},W)=\#E(\mathbf{Q}_{p})[p^{\infty}].

For
w={\mathfrak{q}}
, we have

{\rm ker}(r_{{\mathfrak{q}}})=B_{{\mathfrak{q}}}/(\gamma-1)B_{{\mathfrak{q}}},

where
B_{\mathfrak{q}}
is as in (
3.9
), and therefore finite by
[
KO20
, Lem. 2.7]
.
Finally, for
w\in\Sigma_{f}\setminus\{\mathfrak{p},\overline{\mathfrak{p}}\}
we have
{\rm ker}(r_{w})=B_{w}/(\gamma-1)B_{w}
.
From the exact sequence

0\rightarrow E(K_{w})[p^{\infty}]\rightarrow B_{w}\xrightarrow{\gamma-1}B_{w}\rightarrow B_{w}/(\gamma-1)B_{w}\rightarrow 0

and the finiteness of
E(K_{w})[p^{\infty}]
, we see that

(B_{w})_{\rm div}\subset(\gamma-1)B_{w},

and so
\#{\rm ker}(r_{w})
is bounded by
[B_{w}:(B_{w})_{\rm div}]
.
Since all primes
w\in\Sigma_{f}
are finitely decomposed in
K_{\infty}/K
by our assumption on
\Sigma
, this concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

When
\#{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,W)<\infty

Corollary 3.9
.

The generalised Selmer group
\varprojlim_{k}\mathfrak{Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S_{\infty}^{(k)})[J]

Proof.

By (

), the natural surjection

Since the local conditions defining

∎

3.4.
The derived regulator

Note that by Corollary
3.9
and (

), the Selmer group

\varprojlim_{k}\mathfrak{Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,S^{(k)})=\underleftarrow{\mathfrak{S}}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{(1)}

Definition 3.13
.

For
i\geq 1
, define

\underleftarrow{\overline{\mathfrak{S}}}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{(i)}:=\bigl(\underleftarrow{\mathfrak{S}}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{(i)}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}\mathbf{Q}_{p}\bigr)\cap{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,T)

-adic saturation of
\underleftarrow{\mathfrak{S}}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{(i)}
in

In particular,
\underleftarrow{\overline{\mathfrak{S}}}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{(1)}={\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{q}}(K,T)

By linearity, the pairings (
3.16
) extend to
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
-valued pairings

(3.17)

{h}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{(i)}:\underleftarrow{\overline{\mathfrak{S}}}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{(i)}\times\underleftarrow{\overline{\mathfrak{S}}}_{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}}^{(i)}\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}_{p},

whose kernel on the left (resp. right) is
\underleftarrow{\overline{\mathfrak{S}}}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{(i+1)}
(resp.
\underleftarrow{\overline{\mathfrak{S}}}_{\overline{\mathfrak{q}}}^{(i+1)}

3.2

By definition, for every

the quotients
\underleftarrow{\overline{\mathfrak{S}}}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(i)}/\underleftarrow{\overline{\mathfrak{S}}}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(i+1)}

\underleftarrow{\overline{\mathfrak{S}}}_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}^{(i)}/\underleftarrow{\overline{\mathfrak{S}}}_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}^{(i+1)}

\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-ranks of these quotients are equal.
Hence for every

(3.18)

\underleftarrow{\overline{\mathfrak{S}}}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{(i)}/\underleftarrow{\overline{\mathfrak{S}}}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{(i+1)}\simeq\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{e_{i}}

for some integers

(the same for both
\mathfrak{p}
and
\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
).
By Corollary
3.11
(ii) and Proposition
2.5
we know that

for

.

Note that the
i
-th derived
p
-adic heights

depend on the choice of a topological generator
\gamma
, but the
(J^{i}/J^{i+1})\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}\mathbf{Q}_{p}
-valued pairings

are independent of this choice.
We record the dependence on
\gamma
in the following definition.

Definition 3.14
.

Let
(x_{1,1},\dots,x_{1,e_{1}};\cdots;x_{i,1},\dots,x_{i,e_{i}};\cdots)
be a
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-basis for
{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{p}}(K,T)
adapted to the filtration

{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{p}}(K,T)=\underleftarrow{\overline{\mathfrak{S}}}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(1)}\supset\underleftarrow{\overline{\mathfrak{S}}}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2)}\supset\cdots\supset\underleftarrow{\overline{\mathfrak{S}}}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(i)}\supset\cdots,

so that for every
i\geq 1
the elements
x_{i,1},\dots,x_{i,e_{i}}
project to a
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-basis for
\underleftarrow{\overline{\mathfrak{S}}}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(i)}/\underleftarrow{\overline{\mathfrak{S}}}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(i+1)}
.
Let
(y_{1,1},\dots,y_{1,e_{1}};\cdots;y_{i,1},\dots,y_{i,e_{i}};\cdots)
be a
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-basis for
{\rm Sel}_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}(K,T)
defined in the same manner.
Define the
i
-th partial regulator
by

R_{\mathfrak{p},\gamma}^{(i)}:=\det\bigl(h_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(i)}(x_{i,j},y_{i,j^{\prime}})\bigr)_{1\leq j,j^{\prime}\leq e_{i}},

and the

by
{\rm Reg}_{\mathfrak{p},{\rm der},\gamma}:=\prod_{i\geq 1}R_{\mathfrak{p},\gamma}^{(i)}
.

Remark 3.15
.

By definition, the partial regulators
R^{(i)}_{\mathfrak{p},\gamma}
are non-zero, and they are well-defined up to a
p
-adic unit.
So, we have

{\rm Reg}_{\mathfrak{p},{\rm der},\gamma}\in\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}/\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}.

Replacing
h_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(i)}
by
(\gamma-1)^{i}\cdot h_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(i)}
and writing

, the above definition gives a non-zero derived regulator

which is independent of
\gamma
.

4.
BSD conjecture for
L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\rm BDP}

In this section, we formulate our
p
-adic analogue of the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture for
L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\rm BDP}
, extending the formulation given in
[
AC21
]
in the
p
-ordinary case.

We keep the setting from Section
2
, and assume in addition that (
h0
) and (
h1
) hold.

By Lemma
2.2
, we have

{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{p}}(K,T)={\rm ker}({\rm res}_{\mathfrak{p}/{\rm tor}})\simeq\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{r-1},

where
r={\rm rank}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}\check{S}_{p}(E/K)
.
Let
(s_{1},\dots,s_{r-1})
be a
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-basis for
{\rm Sel}_{\mathfrak{p}}(K,T)
, and extend it to a
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-basis
(s_{1},\dots,s_{r-1},s_{\mathfrak{p}})
for
\check{S}_{p}(E/K)
.
In particular,
{\rm res}_{\mathfrak{p}}(s_{\mathfrak{p}})\in E(K_{\mathfrak{p}})\otimes\mathbf{Z}_{p}
is non-torsion.
Henceforth, we let

{\rm log}_{\mathfrak{p}}:\check{S}_{p}(E/K)\rightarrow\mathbf{Z}_{p}

be the composition of the map
{\rm res}_{\mathfrak{p}/{\rm tor}}
with the formal group logarithm
E(K_{\mathfrak{p}})_{/{\rm tor}}\otimes\mathbf{Z}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbf{Z}_{p}
associated with a Néron differential
\omega_{E}\in\Omega^{1}(E/\mathbf{Z}_{(p)})
.
Also, let
\Sha_{\rm BK}(K,W)={\rm Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K)_{/{\rm div}}
be the Bloch–Kato Tate–Shafarevich group, and for every prime
\ell\mid N
write
c_{\ell}
to denote the Tamagawa number of
E/\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}
.

In the following, we shall interchangeably view
L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\rm BDP}
as an element in
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}\llbracket{T}\rrbracket
via the identification
\Lambda_{\widehat{\mathcal{O}}}\simeq\widehat{\mathcal{O}}\llbracket{T}\rrbracket
defined by
T=\gamma-1
for our fixed topological generator
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Thus
T
corresponds to a generator of the augmentation ideal
J\subset\Lambda_{\widehat{\mathcal{O}}}
.

Conjecture 4.1

(
p
-adic BSD conjecture for
L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\rm BDP}
)
.

The following assertions hold:

(i)

(Leading Coefficient Formula)
Let

\varrho_{\rm an}:={\rm ord}_{J}L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\rm BDP}
.
Then, up to a
p
-adic unit,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varrho_{\rm an}!}\frac{d^{\varrho_{\rm an}}}{dT^{\varrho_{\rm an}}}L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\rm BDP}\Bigl|_{T=0}

\displaystyle=\biggl(\frac{1-a_{p}(E)+p}{p}\biggr)^{2}\cdot{\rm log}_{\mathfrak{p}}(s_{\mathfrak{p}})^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\times{\rm Reg}_{\mathfrak{p},{\rm der},\gamma}\cdot\#\Sha_{\rm BK}(K,W)\cdot\prod_{\ell\mid N}c_{\ell}^{2}.

(ii)

(Order of Vanishing)

Set
r^{\pm}
to denote the
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-corank of the
\pm
-eigenspace of
\check{S}_{p}(E/K)
under the action of complex conjugation.
Then

\varrho_{\rm an}=2(\max\{r^{+},r^{-}\}-1).

Remark 4.2
.

We observe that Conjecture
4.1
is a reformulation (depending on
\gamma
) of Conjecture
1.1
in the Introduction.

Remark 4.3
.

As noted in the Introduction (see Remark
1.2
), Conjecture
4.1
extends to all good primes

and with an unconditional definition of

the

-adic Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture formulated in

, Conj. 4.2]

-adic heights (deduced from the construction in

]
) agree with ours (deduced from the construction in

]

[

, §11]
and
[

, §10]
.

By the works of Kolyvagin, Gross–Zagier, and Bertolini–Darmon–Prasanna, Conjecture

enjoys the following compatibility with the classical Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer for

.

.

Assume
{\rm ord}_{s=1}L(E/K,s)=1
. Then:

(i)

4.1
(i) is equivalent to the

-part of the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyler formula for

(ii)

5.

\displaystyle\times{\rm Reg}_{\mathfrak{p},{\rm der},\gamma}\cdot\#\Sha_{\rm BK}(K,W)\cdot\prod_{\ell\mid N}c_{\ell}^{2}.

(ii)

The following inequality holds

{\varrho_{\rm alg}}\geq 2(\max\{r^{+},r^{-}\}-1).

Combined with progress towards the Iwasawa–Greenberg Main Conjecture (Conjecture

-adic Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture for

.

(1b)

\overline{\rho}
is ramified at every prime

.

(1c)

\overline{\rho}

(1d)

.

(1e)

.

6.1.
Step 1: Non-semisimple torsion
\Lambda
-modules and augmentation filtration

Let

be a
\Lambda
-module, where
\Lambda=\mathbf{Z}_{p}\llbracket{T}\rrbracket
. For every

, denote by

\beta_{X}^{(r)}:T^{r}X\rightarrow T^{r}X

the map given by multiplication by

, and put
h(\beta_{X}^{(r)})=\#{\rm coker}(\beta_{X}^{(r)})/\#{\rm ker}(\beta_{X}^{(r)})
whenever both terms in the right-hand side are finite.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
X
be a finitely generated torsion
\Lambda
-module, and let
F_{X}\in\Lambda
be a generator of
{\rm char}_{\Lambda}(X)
.
Put

\varrho_{X}:={\rm ord}_{T}F_{X}.

For any
r\geq\varrho_{X}
, the sub-quotients
T^{r}X/T^{r+1}X
and
T^{r}X[T]
are both finite.
In addition,

\frac{1}{\varrho_{X}!}\frac{d^{\varrho_{X}}}{dT^{\varrho_{X}}}F_{X}\Bigl|_{T=0}\;\sim_{p}\;h(\beta_{X}^{(r)})=\frac{\#\bigl(T^{r}X/T^{r+1}X\bigr)}{\#(T^{r}X[T])},

where
\sim_{p}
denotes equality up to a
p
-adic unit.

Proof.

Suppose first that
X
is an elementary module, in the sense that

X=\Lambda/(f),

where
f=a_{n}T^{n}+a_{n+1}T^{n+1}+\cdots\in\Lambda
with
a_{n}\neq 0
(so
n=\varrho_{X}
). Then for any
r\geq n
we find

\displaystyle X[T^{r}]

\displaystyle=(T^{-n}f)/(f),

\displaystyle T^{r}X

\displaystyle=(T^{r},f)/(f)\simeq(T^{r})/(T^{r})\cap(f)=(T^{r})/(T^{r-n}f),

Therefore,for any
r\geq\varrho_{X}
we have that
T^{r}X[T]\simeq X[T^{r+1}]/X[T^{r}]
is trivial and

T^{r}X/T^{r+1}X\simeq(T^{r})/(T^{r+1},T^{r-n}f)\simeq\mathbf{Z}_{p}/(a_{n})

so the result is true in this case.

In general, by the structure theorem
there exists a
\Lambda
-module homomorphism

\phi:X\rightarrow Y

with finite kernel and cokernel, where
Y
is a direct sum of elementary modules as above. Since
X
and
Y
have the same characteristic ideal and by the above argument the result is true for
Y
, it remains to show that
h(\beta^{(r)}_{X})=h(\beta^{(r)}_{Y})
for any
r\geq\varrho_{X}=\varrho_{Y}
.

For any
\Lambda
-module homomorphism
\beta:M\rightarrow M
with finite kernel and cokernel, put
h(\beta)=\#{\rm coker}(\beta)/\#{\rm ker}(\beta)
.
Note that if
0\rightarrow A\rightarrow B\rightarrow C\rightarrow 0
is a
\Lambda
-module exact sequence, and any two of the multiplication-by-
T
maps
T_{A}:A\rightarrow A
,
T_{B}:B\rightarrow B
,
T_{C}:C\rightarrow C
have finite kernel and cokernel, then from an easy application of the snake lemma we see that
h(T_{A})
,
h(T_{B})
,
h(T_{C})
are all defined, with

(6.2)

h(T_{B})=h(T_{A})\cdot h(T_{C}).

For any
r>0
,
\phi
induces maps
\phi_{r}:T^{r}X\rightarrow T^{r}Y
with finite kernel and cokernel.
Applying (
6.2
) to the tautological exact sequence
0\rightarrow{\rm im}(\phi_{r})\rightarrow T^{r}Y\rightarrow{\rm coker}(\phi_{r})\rightarrow 0
, we obtain

(6.3)

h(\beta_{Y}^{(r)})=h(T_{{\rm im}(\phi_{r})})\cdot h(T_{{\rm coker}(\phi_{r})})=h(T_{{\rm im}(\phi_{r})})

for any
r\geq\varrho_{X}
, using that
h(T_{{\rm coker}(\phi_{r})})=1
(since
\#{\rm coker}(\phi_{r})<\infty
) for the last equality. On the other hand, applied to
0\rightarrow{\rm ker}(\phi_{r})\rightarrow T^{r}X\rightarrow{\rm im}(\phi_{r})\rightarrow 0
, (
6.2
) similarly gives

(6.4)

h(\beta_{X}^{(r)})=h(T_{{\rm ker}(\phi_{r})})\cdot h(T_{{\rm im}(\phi_{r})})=h(T_{{\rm im}(\phi_{r})})

for any
r\geq\varrho_{X}
. Combining (
6.3
) and (
6.4
), the result follows.

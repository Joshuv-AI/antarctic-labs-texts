Abstract
. We study the collective synchronized behavior of the Kuramoto model with inertia and frustration effects on a connected and symmetric network. We aim to establish sufficient frameworks for achieving complete frequency synchronization, taking into account initial configuration, small inertia and frustration, and large coupling strength. More precisely, we first demonstrate that the phase diameter will be uniformly bounded by a small value after a finite time. Then we prove that the frequency diameter exhibits exponential decay to zero. Our approach relies on a careful construction of energy functionals, which effectively control the dissipation of phase and frequency diameters.

Introduction

The occurrence of collective synchronized behaviors in large oscillatory systems is ubiquitous in the real world. For instance, synchronous flashing of fireflies, heart beat generation, and the Josephson junction arrays

.
Due to their potential applications, synchronous phenomena have attracted significant interest in diverse scientific communities such as biology, physics, and engineering. The investigation of synchronization from a mathematical perspective was initially undertaken by Winfree and Kuramoto
[
23
,
34
]
, in which the Kuramoto model was introduced. This model has became a representative model for describing synchronization and has been extensively studied
[
3
,
11
,
17
,
19
]
. To better simulate the realistic systems, researchers have introduced additional physical effects into the original Kuramoto model, including the frustration, inertia, time delay, and network topology
[
10
,
13
,
14
,

,
18
,
20
,

,

,

,
31
]
.

In this paper, we aim to investigate the Kuramoto model with inertia and frustration on a locally coupled network. To fix the idea, we consider a symmetric and connected network, which can be registered by a weighted graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},E,A)
. Here
\mathcal{V}=\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
is the vertex set,
A=(a_{ij})
is an
N\times N
matrix whose element
a_{ij}
denotes the communication weight flowing from vertex
j
to
i
, and
E=\{(i,j):a_{ij}>0\}\subset\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}
is the edge set. For a given graph
\mathcal{G}
, we assume that the oscillators in the Kuramoto model are located at the vertices of the graph
\mathcal{G}
, with their interactions determined by the edges in
E
and the communication weights in
A
. Let
\theta_{i}=\theta_{i}(t)\in\mathbb{R}
be the phase of the
i
-th oscillator with natural frequency
\Omega_{i}
. Then, the dynamics of Kuramoto oscillators with inertia
m>0
and frustration
\alpha\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
is governed by the following second-order phase system:

m\ddot{\theta}_{i}(t)+\dot{\theta}_{i}(t)=\Omega_{i}+\frac{K}{N}\sum_{l=1}^{N}a_{il}\sin(\theta_{l}(t)-\theta_{i}(t)+\alpha),\quad t>0,\ i=1,2,\ldots,N,

(1.1)

subject to the initial data:

(\theta_{i},\omega_{i})(0)=(\theta_{i0},\omega_{i0}),

(1.2)

where
K>0
represents the coupling strength and
\omega_{i}(t)=\dot{\theta}_{i}(t)
is the instantaneous frequency.

In the absence of frustration (
\alpha=0
), the inertial Kuramoto model was initially proposed by Ermentrout
[
14
]
as an explanation for the slow adaptation to frequency synchronization in certain species of fireflies. Significant progress has been made in understanding the dynamics of this model under different network structures and initial conditions. For the special case of an all-to-all network, i.e.,
a_{il}=1
in (
1.1
), the emergence of exponential synchronization was proven when the oscillators are initially contained within a half circle
[
5
,
6
]
. Later on, the complete frequency synchronization was obtained under more general initial conditions
[
4
,
8
,
22
]
. In the case of a connected and symmetric network, the second-order gradient-like flow structure was employed to establish the emergence of synchronization
[
7
,
8
,
25
]
. We refer to
[
4
,
32
,
35
]
for synchronization with more general settings.
However, researches on the dynamics of the non-zero frustration case (
\alpha\neq 0
) in the Kuramoto model are relatively limited. Authors in
[
16
]
provided several sufficient frameworks leading to the exponential frequency synchronization for the model on an all-to-all network. They further studied the uniqueness and well-ordering property of emergent phase-locked states
[
24
]
. We refer to
[
9
,
12
]
for more information. To the best of our knowledge, the dynamics of the Kuramoto oscillators with inertia and frustration on a locally coupled network remain relatively unexplored from a theoretic standpoint.
At least, there are two basic and natural questions:

•

Will the emergence of frequency synchronization occur in (
1.1
)?

•

What is the convergence rate of the emergence of synchronization?

The problems outlined above can indeed pose significant challenges. The first major difficulty lies in deriving the dissipation of the phase diameter in (
1.1
). In the all-to-all case, one may show well ordering structure such as in
[
24
]
, so that the diameter
D(\theta(t))=\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}
for fixed
i,j
. This allows the diameter to be second-order smooth and its dynamics can be effectively described by a second-order equation as shown in
[
16
]
. However, establishing such a well-ordering structure for the model (
1.1
) on a network still remains an open problem. Consequently, the diameter is only Lipschitz continuous and can not be governed by a second order equation. The second main difficulty arises from the lack of a gradient-flow structure in the system (
1.1
) due to the presence of the frustration term. As a result, even if one can derive the boundedness of the phase diameter, it is impossible to employ the criteria in
[
7
,
8
,
25
]
to yield the convergence to a synchronous state, let alone exponential convergence.

Then, our contributions in this paper are two folds. Firstly, we establish a suitable energy functional that depends on the relative phases and frequencies of the system. This energy functional will play an important role in controlling the phase diameter (see (
2.2
)). Then, our analysis on the dissipation of this energy functional reveals that the phase diameter will decrease significantly within a finite time (see Lemma
3.5
). Secondly, we prove the frequency synchronization and yield the exponential convergence rate. To accomplish this, we combine together the dynamics of the frequencies and accelerations (see (
4.3
)) to obtain a Cucker-Smale type estimate, which eventually shows the exponential emergence of the frequency synchronization (see Theorem
2.1
).

The rest of paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce several basic concepts and estimates, and provide our sufficient frameworks and main result. In Section
3
, we show that the phase diameter of the ensemble of Kuramoto oscillators will be confined in a quarter circle after some finite time. In Section
4
, we present the complete frequency synchronization emerges at the exponential convergence rate. Section
5
is devoted to a brief summary of our main result and future discussion.

\displaystyle\theta(t):=(\theta_{1}(t),\ldots,\theta_{N}(t)),\quad\omega(t):=(\omega_{1}(t),\ldots,\omega_{N}(t)),\quad\Omega:=(\Omega_{1},\ldots,\Omega_{N}),

\displaystyle D(\theta(t)):=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}|\theta_{i}(t)-\theta_{j}(t)|,\quad D(\omega(t)):=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}|\omega_{i}(t)-\omega_{j}(t)|,

\displaystyle D_{\Omega}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\Omega_{i}-\Omega_{j}|^{2},\quad a_{u}:=\max_{(i,j)\in E}a_{ij},\quad a_{l}:=\min_{(i,j)\in E}a_{ij}.

2.
Preliminaries and main result

In this section, we first introduce some basic concepts and related estimates which will be used throughout the paper. Then, we give our assumptions and present our main result.

2.1.
Basic concepts

In this part, we provide some concepts such as connectivity and synchronization for the second-order Kuramoto system (
1.1
).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},E,A)
be a graph. Then

(1)

A path from
i
to
j
is a sequence
i_{0},i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}
with
i_{0}=i
and
i_{k}=j
such that each successive pair of vertices is an edge of graph
\mathcal{G}
.

(2)

If there exists a path from
i
to
j
, then vertex
j
is said to be reachable from vertex
i
.

(3)

A graph
\mathcal{G}
is said to be connected if each vertex can be reachable from any other vertex of
\mathcal{G}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\theta=\theta(t)
be a solution to Kuramoto model (
1.1
). The Kuramoto model exhibits asymptotic complete-frequency synchronization iff the relative frequency differences tend to zero asymptotically:

\lim_{t\to\infty}|\dot{\theta}_{i}(t)-\dot{\theta}_{j}(t)|=0,\quad\forall\ i\neq j.

2.2.
Preparatory estimates

Let’s consider a connected graph
\mathcal{G}
and we see that for any
(i,j)\in\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}
, there exists a shortest path from
i
to
j
, say

i=i_{0}\ \to\ i_{1}\ \to\ \cdots\ \to i_{r_{ij}}=j,\quad(i_{l+1},i_{l})\in E,\ l=0,1,\cdots,r_{ij}-1,

Here,
r_{ij}
denotes the distance between vertices
i
and
j
, i.e., the length of the shortest path from vertex
i
to
j
. We set

r:=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}r_{ij},\quad\Lambda_{1}:=\frac{1}{1+r|E^{c}|},

(2.1)

where
E^{c}
is the complement of edge set
E
in
\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}
and
|E^{c}|
denotes its cardinality.
As we know the graph
\mathcal{G}
may not be complete, we first provide an equivalence relation in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that the graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},E,A)
is connected, and let
\theta_{i}(t)
and
\omega_{i}(t)
be the phase and frequency of the Kuramoto oscillator located at the vertex
i
at time
t
, respectively. Then we have

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\theta_{i}(t)-\theta_{j}(t)|^{2}\leq\sum_{(i,j)\in E}|\theta_{i}(t)-\theta_{j}(t)|^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\theta_{i}(t)-\theta_{j}(t)|^{2},

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\omega_{i}(t)-\omega_{j}(t)|^{2}\leq\sum_{(i,j)\in E}|\omega_{i}(t)-\omega_{j}(t)|^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\omega_{i}(t)-\omega_{j}(t)|^{2},

where
\Lambda_{1}
is a positive constant defined in (
2.1
).

Proof.

We only prove the first inequality since the second one can be similarly dealt with. For the right part of the first inequality, it is obvious that

\sum_{(i,j)\in E}|\theta_{i}(t)-\theta_{j}(t)|^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\theta_{i}(t)-\theta_{j}(t)|^{2}.

We next verify the left part of the first inequality. In fact, for
(k,l)\in E^{c}
, due to the connectivity of the graph, we can find a shortest path from vertex
k
to
l
:

k=i_{0}\ \to\ i_{1}\ \to\ \cdots\ \to\ i_{p}=l,\qquad(i_{s+1},i_{s})\in E,\ s=0,1,\ldots,p-1,

where
p
is the length of the shortest path from
k
to
l
.
It is easy to see that

|\theta_{k}-\theta_{l}|\leq|\theta_{i_{0}}-\theta_{i_{1}}|+|\theta_{i_{1}}-\theta_{i_{2}}|+\cdots+|\theta_{i_{p-1}}-\theta_{i_{p}}|.

Then for
(k,l)\in E^{c}
, we have

\displaystyle|\theta_{k}(t)-\theta_{l}(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq p[|\theta_{i_{0}}(t)-\theta_{i_{1}}(t)|^{2}+|\theta_{i_{1}}(t)-\theta_{i_{2}}(t)|^{2}+\cdots+|\theta_{i_{p-1}}(t)-\theta_{i_{p}}(t)|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq r[|\theta_{i_{0}}(t)-\theta_{i_{1}}(t)|^{2}+|\theta_{i_{1}}(t)-\theta_{i_{2}}(t)|^{2}+\cdots+|\theta_{i_{p-1}}(t)-\theta_{i_{p}}(t)|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq r\sum_{(i,j)\in E}|\theta_{i}(t)-\theta_{j}(t)|^{2},

where the parameter
r
is defined in (
2.1
).
This yields

\sum_{(k,l)\in E^{c}}|\theta_{k}(t)-\theta_{l}(t)|^{2}\leq|E^{c}|r\sum_{(i,j)\in E}|\theta_{i}(t)-\theta_{j}(t)|^{2}.

Thus, it follows that

\sum_{(k,l)\in E^{c}}|\theta_{k}(t)-\theta_{l}(t)|^{2}+\sum_{(k,l)\in E}|\theta_{k}(t)-\theta_{l}(t)|^{2}\leq(1+|E^{c}|r)\sum_{(i,j)\in E}|\theta_{i}(t)-\theta_{j}(t)|^{2}.

Then, we derive that

\frac{1}{1+r|E^{c}|}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\theta_{i}(t)-\theta_{j}(t)|^{2}\leq\sum_{(i,j)\in E}|\theta_{i}(t)-\theta_{j}(t)|^{2},

which completes the proof.
∎

2.3.
Main result

We now present our main result which states that the frequency synchronization occurs exponentially fast under sufficient conditions. For simplicity, we set
D_{0}
and
D^{\infty}
to be positive constants and
\mathcal{E}_{1}(t)
to be an energy function below

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}(t)=\ 2m\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})(\omega_{i}-\omega_{j})+(1-m\sqrt{K})\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})^{2}+2m^{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}(\omega_{i}-\omega_{j})^{2}.

(2.2)

Then we make following assumptions:

Assumption
(\mathcal{A})
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}(0)<\frac{1}{8}D_{0}^{2},\quad D^{\infty}\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}),\quad D_{0}\in(0,\pi),\quad D^{\infty}<D_{0},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sin\alpha<\frac{a_{l}\cos\alpha\cos D^{\infty}}{12a_{u}(1+r|E^{c}|)\sin D^{\infty}},\quad m<1,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\sqrt{K}>\frac{(1+r|E^{c}|)}{a_{l}\cos\alpha}\max\left\{\frac{D_{0}}{\sin D_{0}},\frac{1}{\cos D^{\infty}}\right\},

(2.5)

\displaystyle m\sqrt{K}<\frac{1}{8},\quad mK<\frac{a_{l}}{a_{u}^{2}(1+r|E^{c}|)\cos\alpha}\min\left\{\frac{\sin D_{0}}{36D_{0}},\frac{\cos D^{\infty}}{24}\right\},

(2.6)

\displaystyle K\sin\alpha<\frac{1}{8a_{u}\sin D^{\infty}},

(2.7)

\displaystyle C_{1}=\left[\frac{3(1+|E^{c}|r)D_{0}}{K^{\frac{3}{2}}a_{l}\cos\alpha\sin D_{0}}+12\frac{m}{K^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right]D_{\Omega}+12N^{2}K^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{u}^{2}\sin^{2}\alpha\left[\frac{(1+|E^{c}|r)D_{0}}{a_{l}\cos\alpha\sin D_{0}}+4mK\right]<\frac{1}{16}(D^{\infty})^{2}.

(2.8)

Remark 2.1
.

We briefly comment on Assumption
(\mathcal{A})
.

(1)

The set of parameters satisfying Assumption
(\mathcal{A})
is nonempty. In fact, we may fix all the other parameters except for
m
,
K
and
\alpha
. Then we set
m=\alpha=K^{-2}
and let
K\gg 1
. Under this setting, Assumption
(\mathcal{A})
is obviously fulfilled.

(2)

If the oscillators are initially distributed in a half circle, i.e.,
D(\theta(0))
is close to
\pi
, our assumption
(\mathcal{A})
may not work. This is the restriction of our method.

Under the Assumption
(\mathcal{A})
, our main result on exponential frequency synchronization is presented as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose the graph
\mathcal{G}
is symmetric and connected, let
(\theta(t),\omega(t))
be a solution to system (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), and suppose the Assumption
(\mathcal{A})
is fulfilled. Then there exist a positive constant
C

and a finite time
t_{*}
such that

D(\omega(t))\leq Ce^{-\frac{\sqrt{K}}{2}(t-t_{*})},\quad\text{for all}\ \ t\geq t_{*}.

3.
Uniformly small boundedness of phase diameter

We will show that the Kuramoto oscillators (
1.1
) are going to be trapped into a small region confined in a quarter circle under Assumption
(\mathcal{A})
. More precisely, we first introduce some basic estimates on the energy functional
\mathcal{E}_{1}
. Then, we provide the first-order Gronwall-type inequality to derive the desired results.

3.1.
Estimates on the energy functional
\mathcal{E}_{1}(t)

Recall the energy functional defined in (
2.2
). The following lemma shows that the phase diameter can be bounded by
\mathcal{E}_{1}(t)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose the inertia and coupling strength satisfy

m\sqrt{K}<\frac{1}{8}.

(3.1)

Then for any
t\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}(t)\geq\frac{1}{8}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\theta_{i}(t)-\theta_{j}(t))^{2}+\frac{2}{3}m^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\omega_{i}(t)-\omega_{j}(t))^{2}.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle D(\theta(t))\leq\sqrt{8\mathcal{E}_{1}(t)},\quad t\geq 0.

Proof.

According to the formula

2|x||y|\leq x^{2}+y^{2},\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R},

(3.2)

we have

2m\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})(\omega_{i}-\omega_{j})\geq-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left[\frac{3}{4}(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})^{2}+\frac{4}{3}m^{2}(\omega_{i}-\omega_{j})^{2}\right].

Therefore, we see from (
2.2
) that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{1}{4}-m\sqrt{K}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})^{2}+\frac{2}{3}m^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\omega_{i}-\omega_{j})^{2}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{8}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})^{2}+\frac{2}{3}m^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\omega_{i}-\omega_{j})^{2},

which complete the proof of the first part.

Moreover, it follows from (
3.3
) that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}(t)\geq\frac{1}{8}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\theta_{i}(t)-\theta_{j}(t))^{2}.

This yields

\displaystyle(\theta_{i}(t)-\theta_{j}(t))^{2}\leq 8\mathcal{E}_{1}(t).

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle D(\theta(t))=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}|\theta_{i}(t)-\theta_{j}(t)|\leq\sqrt{8\mathcal{E}_{1}(t)}.

∎

3.2.
Differential inequality for
\mathcal{E}_{1}(t)

In this part, we derive a Gronwall-type differential inequality for
\mathcal{E}_{1}
along the flow (
1.1
). Before this, we provide some a priori estimates.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose the graph
\mathcal{G}
is symmetric and connected, and the phase configuration
\theta(t)=(\theta_{1}(t),\ldots,\theta_{N}(t))
to system (
1.1
) at time
t
satisfies

D(\theta(t))=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}|\theta_{i}(t)-\theta_{j}(t)|<D_{0}.

Then, we have the following estimates:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\Omega_{i}-\Omega_{j})\cdot 2(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})

(3.4)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3(1+|E^{c}|r)D_{0}}{Ka_{l}\cos\alpha\sin D_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\Omega_{i}-\Omega_{j})^{2}+\frac{1}{3}Ka_{l}\cos\alpha\frac{\sin D_{0}}{D_{0}}\frac{1}{1+|E^{c}|r}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})^{2},

\displaystyle\frac{K}{N}\sin\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{l=1}^{N}[a_{il}\cos(\theta_{l}-\theta_{i})-a_{jl}\cos(\theta_{l}-\theta_{j})]\cdot 2(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{12N^{2}Ka_{u}^{2}\sin^{2}\alpha(1+|E^{c}|r)D_{0}}{a_{l}\cos\alpha\sin D_{0}}+\frac{1}{3}Ka_{l}\cos\alpha\frac{\sin D_{0}}{D_{0}}\frac{1}{1+r|E^{c}|}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})^{2},

\displaystyle\frac{K}{N}\cos\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{l=1}^{N}[a_{il}\sin(\theta_{l}-\theta_{i})-a_{jl}\sin(\theta_{l}-\theta_{j})]\cdot 2(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq-2Ka_{l}\cos\alpha\frac{\sin D_{0}}{D_{0}}\frac{1}{1+r|E^{c}|}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}|^{2}.

Proof.

We will only show the proof of (
3.6
), since (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) are simply derived by applying (
3.2
). For convenience, we set

\mathcal{I}_{2}=\frac{K}{N}\cos\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{l=1}^{N}[a_{il}\sin(\theta_{l}-\theta_{i})-a_{jl}\sin(\theta_{l}-\theta_{j})]\cdot 2(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}).

Now for
\mathcal{I}_{2}
, applying the symmetric property of the network, we have the following reduction:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{K}{N}\cos\alpha\sum^{N}_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\sum_{l=1}^{N}[a_{il}\sin(\theta_{l}-\theta_{i})-a_{jl}\sin(\theta_{l}-\theta_{j})]\cdot 2(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})

(3.7)

\displaystyle=\frac{K}{N}\cos\alpha\left\{\sum^{N}_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\sum_{l=i,j}+\sum^{N}_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\sum^{N}_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq i,j\end{subarray}}\right\}[a_{il}\sin(\theta_{l}-\theta_{i})-a_{jl}\sin(\theta_{l}-\theta_{j})]\cdot 2(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})

\displaystyle=-\frac{2K}{N}\cos\alpha\sum^{N}_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}a_{ji}\sin(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})\cdot 2(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})

\displaystyle+\frac{K}{N}\cos\alpha\sum^{N}_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\sum^{N}_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq i,j\end{subarray}}[a_{il}\sin(\theta_{l}-\theta_{i})]\cdot 2(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})+\frac{K}{N}\cos\alpha\sum^{N}_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\sum^{N}_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq i,j\end{subarray}}[-a_{jl}\sin(\theta_{l}-\theta_{j})]\cdot 2(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}).

For the last two terms in above equality, we exchange the indices to see that

\displaystyle\frac{K}{N}\cos\alpha\sum^{N}_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\sum^{N}_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq i,j\end{subarray}}[a_{il}\sin(\theta_{l}-\theta_{i})]\cdot 2(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})=\frac{K}{N}\cos\alpha\sum^{N}_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,l=1\\
i\neq l\end{subarray}}\sum^{N}_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i,l\end{subarray}}[a_{ij}\sin(\theta_{j}-\theta_{i})]\cdot 2(\theta_{i}-\theta_{l}),

and

\displaystyle\frac{K}{N}\cos\alpha\sum^{N}_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\sum^{N}_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq i,j\end{subarray}}[-a_{jl}\sin(\theta_{l}-\theta_{j})]\cdot 2(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})=\frac{K}{N}\cos\alpha\sum^{N}_{\begin{subarray}{c}l,j=1\\
l\neq j\end{subarray}}\sum^{N}_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq l,j\end{subarray}}[-a_{ji}\sin(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})]\cdot 2(\theta_{l}-\theta_{j}).

This implies that

\displaystyle\frac{K}{N}\cos\alpha\sum^{N}_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\sum^{N}_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq i,j\end{subarray}}[a_{il}\sin(\theta_{l}-\theta_{i})]\cdot 2(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})+\frac{K}{N}\cos\alpha\sum^{N}_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\sum^{N}_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq i,j\end{subarray}}[-a_{jl}\sin(\theta_{l}-\theta_{j})]\cdot 2(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})

(3.8)

\displaystyle=-\frac{K}{N}\cos\alpha\sum^{N}_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,l=1\\

.

5.
Summary

We studied the dynamics of Kuramoto oscillators under the interplay of the effects of inertia and frustration under a connected and undirected graph. For this, we constructed two energy functionals which can bound the phase and frequency diameters, respectively. Under a sufficient regime in terms of initial data, small inertia, small frustration and large coupling, we first derived that the phase diameter will shrink to a small region after some finite time by the energy method. Next, we similarly applied the energy estimates and showed that the frequency diameter approaches to zero at the exponential rate. This ultimately yields the emergence of complete frequency synchronization. However, the interaction network we considered here is rather limited. In a general network, the energy functionals we constructed here become useless. In that case, we need to reconsider the dynamics of the diameters, and this will be our future work.

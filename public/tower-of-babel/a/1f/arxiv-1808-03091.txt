Breakdown of effective temperature, power law interactions and self-propulsion in a momentum conserving active fluid
Amit Singh Vishen, Jacques Prost, Madan Rao

Abstract
The simplest extensions of single particle dynamics in a momentum conserving active fluid - an active suspension of two colloidal particles or a single particle confined by a wall - exhibit strong departures from Boltzmann behavior, resulting in either a breakdown of an effective temperature description or a steady state with nonzero entropy production rate. This is a consequence of hydrodynamic interactions that introduce multiplicative noise in the stochastic description of particle positions. This results in fluctuation-induced interactions that depend on distance as a power law. We find that the dynamics of activated colloids in a passive fluid, with stochastic forcing localized on the particle, is different from that of passive colloids in an active fluctuating fluid.



I
Introduction

Fluctuations of a dilute active suspension (e.g., bacterial bath,
Wu and Libchaber 2000
;
Lau et al. 2003
) have often been described in terms of an equilibrium system with
a (large) effective temperature
Palacci et al. 2010
;
Takatori and Brady 2015
,
with theoretical rationalisations provided by studies of the dynamics of a single particle in an active fluctuating
fluid or a single active particle embedded in a passive fluid
Takatori and Brady 2015
;
Burkholder and Brady 2017
.

However, as we find here, even the simplest extensions - the stochastic dynamics of

two
particles embedded in an unbounded
isotropic active fluctuating gel or a particle in an isotropic active fluctuating gel bound within confining walls - do not allow for an effective temperature description, since the corresponding steady state probability
distribution shows strong departures from the Boltzmann form. This is a consequence of a drift that arises from hydrodynamic interactions, that introduces a multiplicative noise in the
stochastic description of particle positions. The form of the drift can only be decided
after solving the full hydrodynamics problem
.
This has important implications for current discussions on active contributions to pressure, osmotic pressure and surface tension in momentum conserving active fluids
Takatori and Brady 2015
;
Marini et al. 2015
;
Rodenburg et al. 2017
.

Indeed, deviations from Boltzmann behaviour and consequent
breakdown of an effective temperature description
have been systematically analysed in a
dry
system of active Ornstein-Uhlenbeck particles (AOUP)
Fodor et al. 2016
, where momentum is not conserved.
Within a systematic perturbation expansion in the active noise correlation time

\tau_{n}
, the nonequilibrium nature of the steady state distribution first shows up at order
\tau_{n}
(characterised by non-Boltzmann
probability distribution but zero entropy dissipation), while the full nonequilibrium aspect with nonzero entropy production shows up at order
\tau_{n}^{3/2}

Fodor et al. 2016
.
Moreover, there have been several studies on the form of the effective interaction between passive particles (both fixed and mobile) embedded in a bath of dry active particles. For instance, passive colloids in a suspension of self-propelled particles interact through a non-equilibrium analog of depletion forces
Angelani et al. 2011
;
Ray et al. 2014
;
Ni et al. 2015
. The range of these interactions depends on the shape of tracers - being short-range between passive spheres
Angelani et al. 2011
, and long-range between two parallel walls

Ni et al. 2015
. In addition, passive spheres can have large interactions due to density fluctuations of dry active particles
Baek et al. 2018
.

In this paper, we ask, what is the nature of effective interactions and departures from Boltzmann distribution, in momentum conserving active systems?
We find that the simplest extensions of single-particle dynamics, viz., that of colloid particle-wall and colloid particle-particle interactions embedded in a three dimensional active
fluctuating gel, exhibits a clear non-Boltzmann steady state distribution,
characterized by an effective attractive potential
(
\propto 1/r
, for particle-wall separated by
r
and
\propto 1/r^{4}
, for particle-particle).
This is a consequence of the active (fluctuation-dissipation relation violating)
fluctuations and
hydrodynamic interactions that introduce a multiplicative noise.
A dimer of unequal sized spherical particles embedded in an active fluctuating gel self-propels with a velocity proportional to the fluctuation amplitude.
Interestingly, for this momentum conserving active system, both the breakdown of the effective temperature description
and finite entropy production rate,
appear even in the limit
\tau_{n}\to 0
.
We next study the statistics of fluctuations of activated particles in a passive fluid, i.e., particles directly subject to a stochastic driving force. We find
that the dynamics of activated particles in a passive medium
is
not
the same as the dynamics of passive particles in an active
medium -
for instance, the effective particle-particle interaction is repulsive and long ranged (
\propto 1/r^{2}

III
Pressure

The contribution to the force on the wall due to the bare colloid-wall potential
\mathrm{U}(z)
is,

F_{p}=\int_{0}^{\infty}dzP(z)\partial_{z}\mathrm{U}(z)\propto\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{H}_{z}}}e^{-\mathrm{U}/\lambda}\partial_{z}\mathrm{U}(z).

(12)

The observation that the force on the wall depends on the form of wall-particle interaction, is directly related to the fact that the probability distribution is non-Boltzmann.
This is analogous to the situation in dry active particle systems
Solon et al. 2015
, where, apart from the kinetic contribution,
this would have sufficed to give the pressure.
However, in momentum conserving Stokesian fluid systems, the net force due to particle-wall interactions is
balanced
by the force due to
the embedding fluid.

Consider a suspension of
N
colloidal particles in a semi-infinite active fluid confined by a wall.
Assuming particles do not interact directly with each other via forces such as van der Waals, the total force on the
I^{th}

Force balance on the particle

(15)

Substituting Eq.

+

\Phi=\mathrm{U}(r)-\frac{1}{2}\lambda\left[\frac{15ab(a^{3}+b^{3})}{8(a+b)}\frac{1}{r^{4}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{r^{5}}\right)\right].

(38)

Note that while we can define an effective potential for the marginal dynamics of
r
, there is no
effective potential description in the full Fokker-Planck description that includes

\hat{\bf r}
and
{\bf R}
. If we
now assume that the dynamics of
r
is
fast
,
we can decompose the probability distribution as

P({\bf R,\hat{r}},r)=P(r)\int drr^{2}P({\bf R,\hat{r}},r)=P(r)\tilde{P}({\bf R,\hat{r}}),

(39)

where we have defined
\tilde{P}({\bf R,\hat{r}})\equiv\int drr^{2}P({\bf R,\hat{r}},r)
.

Decomposing the derivative as radial and rotational derivative

\nabla_{{\bf r}}={\bf\hat{r}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{1}{r}{\bf\hat{r}}\times\mathcal{R},

(40)

where
\mathcal{R}
is the rotation operator that rotates the
{\bf r}
keeping
r
fixed. In spherical co-ordinates it reads

{\bf\hat{r}}\times\mathcal{R}=\hat{\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+\hat{\phi}\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}.

(41)

Integrating out
r
from Eq.
25
we get
Illien et al. 2017

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{{\bf R}}\cdot\left[-v_{0}\,{\bf\hat{r}}+\lambda\left\langle\frac{{\sf M}_{12}}{r}\right\rangle\cdot{\bf\hat{r}}\times\mathcal{R}+\lambda\left\langle{\sf M}_{11}\right\rangle\cdot\nabla_{{\bf R}}\right]\tilde{P}

(42)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\lambda{\bf\hat{r}}\times\mathcal{R}\cdot\left[\left\langle\frac{{\sf M}_{12}}{r}\right\rangle\cdot\nabla_{{\bf R}}+\left\langle\frac{{\sf M}_{22}}{r^{2}}\right\rangle\cdot{\bf\hat{r}}\times\mathcal{R}\right]\tilde{P},

where the averages are over the distributions
P(r)
,
\langle\phi(r)\rangle=\int drr^{2}\phi(r)P(r)
, and the self-propelled velocity

v_{0}=\int r^{2}dr{\bf\hat{r}}\cdot\left({\sf M}_{12}\cdot{\bf\tilde{f}}_{2}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\nabla_{{\bf r}}\cdot{\sf M}_{12}-\lambda\sf{M}_{12}\cdot\nabla_{{\bf r}}\right)P(r).

(43)

Note that if the fluctuations are thermal, the middle term in Eq.
43
is not present,
the probability distribution has the Boltzmann form
P(r)\propto e^{-\mathrm{U}/\lambda}
, and the velocity vanishes as shown Appendix
C
.
To evaluate the average, we consider the bare inter-particle potential to be a stiff spring,
\mathrm{U}=k(r-l)^{2}/2
, and
l\gg a,b
. In this limit, the relaxation time
scale of
r
is set by
\sf{M_{22}}
,
k
, and
\lambda
;
kl^{2}/2\lambda\gg 1
ensures that the
r
-dynamics is fast
Illien et al. 2017
.
Using the Laplace approximation
Bender and Orszag 1999
, we obtain, to leading order in
1/l
, the propulsion velocity

v_{0}=\frac{5ab}{4\pi\eta}(b-a)\frac{\lambda}{l^{5}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{l^{6}}\right).

(44)

directed along
{\bf\hat{r}}
, if
b>a
.
Orientation decorrelation will lead to diffusion over times longer than the orientation correlation time of
{\bf\hat{r}}
(
\tau_{R}
). The enhancement of the diffusion constant due to this self-propulsion in units of diffusion of a sphere of radius
l
is
v_{0}^{2}\tau_{R}/D_{t}=(1-\delta)^{2}\delta^{2}\epsilon^{6}
, where
D_{t}=\lambda/6\pi\eta l
,
\tau_{R}\sim 3\eta l^{3}/\lambda
,
\delta=a/b
, and
\epsilon=b/l
. Since
\delta<1
and
\epsilon\ll 1
,
this enhancement is very small.

V
Activating the particle by a fluctuating force

Now consider two colloidal spheres of radii
a
and
b
, embedded in an unbounded passive fluid, each of which
experiences a stochastic force,
localized on the colloids
.
To make the discussion simple, we set the stress fluctuations of the embedding medium
\sigma^{n}_{ij}=0
;
the dynamics of the colloids is then given by Eq.
20
, with
{\bm{\vartheta}_{\beta}}=0
, and the applied force on the particles is a sum of deterministic and stochastic components,

{\bf f_{\beta}}\equiv{\bf f_{\beta}}+{\bm{\xi}_{\beta}}
. The fluctuating force on the two colloids is taken to be isotropic, zero mean Gaussian white with correlations,

\langle{\bf\xi}_{\alpha i}(t){\bf\xi}_{\beta j}(s)\rangle=2\Lambda\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta_{ij}\delta(t-s).

(45)

Since the fluctuation is external and the dissipation is in the fluid bath, the system is always active. Hence, for any choice of noise convention the fluctuation dissipation relation is not satisfied. For consistency with the approximation for passive fluid, we take the noise correlation time to be the slowest timescale leading to Stratonovich noise convention.
The corresponding Fokker-Planck equation (in Stratonovich convention) is now (see Appendix
A
),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}P=\nabla_{{\bf R}}\cdot\left(-{\sf M}_{11}\cdot{\bf\tilde{f}}_{1}-{\sf M}_{12}\cdot{\bf\tilde{f}}_{2}+\Lambda{\sf D}_{11}\cdot\nabla_{{\bf R}}\right)P

(46)

\displaystyle+\Lambda\nabla_{{\bf R}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{{\bf r}}\cdot{\sf D}_{12}+{\sf D}_{12}\cdot\nabla_{{\bf r}}\right)P+\nabla_{{\bf r}}\cdot\left(\Lambda{\sf D}_{12}\cdot\nabla_{{\bf R}}\right)P

\displaystyle+\nabla_{{\bf r}}\cdot\left(-{\sf M}_{12}\cdot{\bf\tilde{f}}_{1}-{\sf M}_{22}\cdot{\bf\tilde{f}}_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Lambda\nabla_{{\bf r}}\cdot{\sf D}_{22}+\Lambda{\sf D}_{22}\cdot\nabla_{{\bf r}}\right)P,

where,
{\sf M}
is given by Eq.
24
, and the diffusion matrix
{\sf D}_{\alpha\beta}
is
3\times 3
matrix given in terms of
{\sf H}_{\alpha\beta}
by
{\sf D}_{11}=\left({\sf H}_{11}+{\sf H}_{12}\right)^{2}+\left({\sf H}_{22}+{\sf H}_{12}\right)^{2}
,
{\sf D}_{12}={\sf D}_{21}={\sf H}_{22}^{2}-{\sf H}_{11}^{2}
, and
{\sf D}_{22}=\left({\sf H}_{11}-{\sf H}_{12}\right)^{2}+\left({\sf H}_{22}-{\sf H}_{12}\right)^{2}
.

Once again, Eq.
46
does not satisfy the potential condition given by Eq.
27
, and hence does not have a zero probability current steady state,

even when the spheres are of the same size
. This proves that the dynamics of particles in an active medium is fundamentally
different from the dynamics of activated particles.
In this context, we refer to recent experiments
Berut et al. 2014
;
Bérut et al. 2016
in which two spheres embedded in a fluid are held in two optical traps. A fluctuating force is applied on one sphere by
moving the position of its laser trap randomly. This has been modeled as a two temperature system
Hough and Ou-Yang 2002
;
Berut et al. 2014
;
Bérut et al. 2016
, where the static particle feels the bath temperature and the particle in the fluctuating trap, a higher temperature.
Our study demonstrates the inadequacy of such an effective temperature approach, and in principle (at least numerically) provides a full solution to the steady state
distribution.

We now consider the case when
\mathrm{U}
is a function of
r
alone implying
{\bf\tilde{f}}_{1}=0
and
{\bf\tilde{f}}_{2}=-\mathrm{U}{\bf\hat{r}}
. For this case Eq.
46
reduces to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}P

\displaystyle=\nabla_{{\bf R}}\cdot\left(-{\sf M}_{12}\cdot{\bf\tilde{f}}_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{{\bf r}}\cdot{\sf D}_{12}+\Lambda{\sf D}_{11}\cdot\nabla_{{\bf R}}\right)P

(47)

\displaystyle+\Lambda\nabla_{{\bf R}}\cdot\left({\sf D}_{12}\cdot\nabla_{{\bf r}}\right)P+\nabla_{{\bf r}}\cdot\left(\Lambda{\sf D}_{12}\cdot\nabla_{{\bf R}}\right)P

\displaystyle+\nabla_{{\bf r}}\cdot\left(-{\sf M}_{22}\cdot{\bf\tilde{f}}_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Lambda\nabla_{{\bf r}}\cdot{\sf D}_{22}+\Lambda{\sf D}_{22}\cdot\nabla_{{\bf r}}\right)P.\qquad

The steady state for the marginal distribution
P(r)
obtained by integrating Eq.
47
over
{\bf R}
, with no flux boundary condition gives

\nabla_{{\bf r}}\log P({\bf r})=\frac{1}{\Lambda}{\sf D}_{22}^{-1}\cdot{\sf M}_{22}\cdot{\bf\tilde{f}}_{2}-\frac{1}{2}{\sf D}_{22}^{-1}\cdot\nabla_{{\bf r}}\cdot{\sf D}_{22}.

(48)

The tensor
{\sf D}
decomposed as sum of projection along
\hat{\bf r}
(denoted by superscript
n
) and perpendicular to
\hat{\bf r}
(denoted by superscript
q
) is

{\sf D}_{\alpha\beta}=d_{\alpha\beta}^{q}(r)\left({\bf\mathrm{\sf I}}-{\bf\mathrm{\bf\hat{r}\hat{r}}}\right)+d_{\alpha\beta}^{n}(r){\bf\mathrm{\bf\hat{r}\hat{r}}}.

(49)

The inverse is

{\sf D}_{\alpha\beta}^{-1}=\frac{1}{d^{q}_{\alpha\beta}}\left({\sf I}-{\bf\hat{r}\hat{r}}\right)+\frac{1}{d^{n}_{\alpha\beta}}{\bf\hat{r}\hat{r}},

(50)

and the divergence is

\nabla_{\bf r}\cdot{\sf D}_{\alpha\beta}=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial r}d^{n}_{\alpha\beta}+2\frac{\left(d^{n}_{\alpha\beta}-d^{q}_{\alpha\beta}\right)}{r}\right){\bf\hat{r}}.

(51)

Using Eq.
50
, and Eq.
51
we get

{\sf D}^{-1}_{22}\cdot\left(\nabla_{\bf r}\cdot{\sf D}_{22}\right)=\frac{1}{d_{22}^{n}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial r}d^{n}_{22}+2\frac{\left(d^{n}_{22}-d^{q}_{22}\right)}{r}\right){\bf\hat{r}}.

(52)

Taking the diffusion tensor
\sf{D}
(see Appendix
D
) to the order
1/r
and substituting it in Eq.
52
gives

{\sf D}^{-1}_{22}\cdot\left(\nabla_{\bf r}\cdot{\sf D}_{22}\right)=-\frac{9(a^{2}b^{2})}{4(a^{2}+b^{2})}\frac{1}{r^{3}}\hat{\bf r},

(53)

and substituting the diffusion tensor in the first term on the right of Eq.
48
we obtain

{\sf D}_{22}^{-1}\cdot{\sf M}_{22}\cdot{\bf\tilde{f}}_{2}=-\frac{m_{22}^{n}}{d_{22}^{n}}{\sf U^{\prime}}(r)\hat{\bf r}

(54)

Substituting Eq.
53
and
54
in Eq.
48
gives

\nabla_{{\bf r}}\log P({\bf r})=-\frac{1}{\Lambda}\frac{m_{22}^{n}}{d_{22}^{n}}{\sf U^{\prime}}(r){\bf\hat{r}}+\frac{9(a^{2}b^{2})}{8(a^{2}+b^{2})}\frac{1}{r^{3}}\hat{\bf r}.

(55)

From this the effective potential
\Phi\equiv-\Lambda\log P
upon integration of Eq.
55
is

\Phi=\int drr^{2}\frac{m_{22}^{n}}{d_{22}^{n}}{\mathrm{U}^{\prime}}+\Lambda\frac{9(a^{2}b^{2})}{16(a^{2}+b^{2})}\frac{1}{r^{2}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{r^{3}}\right).

(56)

Note that in this case
m_{22}/d_{22}
depends on
r
, it is not possible to define an effective free energy, keeping the energy
\mathrm{U}
and a constant effective temperature.
As in the colloid-colloid interaction in an active fluid, the steady state distribution is non-Boltzmann
with a fluctuation-induced particle-particle interaction. However, in contrast, the interaction is repulsive, long-ranged, and depends on the form of the interaction potential
\mathrm{U}
.
This effect is similar to that of effective colloid-wall interactions due interplay between hydrodynamic and electrostatic interactions
Saha and Ramaswamy 2014
.

Integrating out
r
from Eq.
47
leads to the similar form of self-propulsion velocity as Eq.
43
.
To order
1/r^{3}

{\sf M}_{12}
is a constant and self-propulsion velocity is

v_{0}=-N\int drr^{2}\left(m^{n}_{12}\mathrm{U}^{\prime}-d^{n}_{12}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\Phi\right)e^{-\Phi/\Lambda}.

(57)

Using Eq.
56
we get

\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\Phi=\frac{m_{22}^{n}}{d_{22}^{n}}{\mathrm{U}^{\prime}}-\Lambda\frac{9(a^{2}b^{2})}{8(a^{2}+b^{2})}\frac{1}{r^{3}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{r^{4}}\right).

(58)

and

\frac{m_{22}^{n}}{d_{22}^{n}}=\frac{(6\pi\eta ba)(a+b)}{a^{2}+b^{2}}\left[1+\frac{6b^{2}a^{2}}{(a^{2}+b^{2})(a+b)}\frac{1}{r}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)\right].

(59)

Thus to leading order in

v_{0}=-N\int drr^{2}m^{n}_{12}\left(1-\frac{d^{n}_{12}m^{n}_{22}}{d^{n}_{22}m^{n}_{12}}\right){\mathrm{U}^{\prime}}e^{-{\mathrm{U}}/\Lambda^{\prime}},

(60)

where

As before, taking

The long time dynamics of the dimer is diffusive, thus resembling an active Brownian particle
Romanczuk et al. 2012

The enhancement in diffusion constant over the bare diffusion

\frac{v_{0}^{2}\tau_{R}}{D_{t}}\sim\frac{(1-\delta)^{2}}{(1+\delta^{2})^{2}\epsilon^{2}}\frac{\Lambda^{\prime 2}}{\mathrm{k_{B}T}^{2}}

where the rotational correlation time is

V.1
One-Dimensional example of self-propulsion

To obtain an intuitive understanding of the self-propulsion described above, consider a simple example in which two spherical colloids interacting via a harmonic potential are confined along the
x
-axis. The center of the spheres are positioned at
x_{1}(t)
and
x_{2}(t)
with
x_{1}<x_{2}
. This dimer is activated by an external force
f
acting on the colloid at
x_{1}
.
The one-dimensional Langevin dynamics for this dimer, with the hydrodynamic interaction tensor
Kim and Karilla 1991
(Appendix
B
) can be obtained from Eq.
20
,

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{6\pi\eta a}-\frac{1}{4\pi\eta r}\right)k(r-l)+\frac{f}{6\pi\eta a},

(62)

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{1}{6\pi\eta a}-\frac{1}{4\pi\eta r}\right)k(r-l)+\frac{f}{4\pi\eta a},

(63)

where
r=x_{2}-x_{1}
. In terms of the separation
r
and center of mass
R=x_{1}+x_{2}
we get

\displaystyle\dot{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{1}{6\pi\eta a}-\frac{1}{4\pi\eta r}\right)(2k(r-l)+f),

(64)

\displaystyle\dot{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{6\pi\eta a}+\frac{1}{4\pi\eta r}\right)f.

(65)

At steady state
r=l-f/2k
and the center of mass velocity is

\dot{R}=\frac{f}{6\pi\eta a}+\frac{f}{4\pi\eta\left(l-f/2k\right)}.

(66)

Thus we see that the magnitude of
\dot{R}
are not equal for
f\to-f
. For positive
f
the harmonic spring is compressed leading to a larger speed in comparison to the case when
f
is negative and the spring is stretched leading to a lower speed.
Now consider a periodic symmetric driving where
f
switches direction after fixed time intervals of duration
\Delta t
. The average force applied is zero, however, the dimer will have a net positive velocity given by

\langle\dot{R}\rangle=\frac{f^{2}/k}{8\pi\eta\left(l^{2}-(f/2k)^{2}\right)}.

(67)

This mechanism is similar to that proposed in ref.
Kumar et al. 2008
;
Baule et al. 2008
for the self-propulsion of an elastic dimer on a frictional substrate.
This setup can be experimentally realized by connecting a paramagnetic and a diamagnetic bead by a polymer and applying a magnetic field gradient which periodically switches direction.

VI
Discussion

We have seen that the interplay between
nonequilibrium fluctuations and hydrodynamics, even in the simplest extension of a single particle embedded in a momentum conserving fluid,
viz., two particles embedded in an unbounded active gel or a particle in an active gel bound within confining walls,
brings out the inadequacy of the effective temperature description, since the corresponding steady state probability distribution shows strong departures from the equilibrium Boltzmann form.
This leads to a non-equilibrium effective “Casimir-like” power law interaction

Kirkpatrick et al. 2013
;
Aminov et al. 2015
;
Cattuto et al. 2006
;
Bartolo et al. 2003
;
Parra-Rojas and Soto 2014
;
Brito et al. 2007
.
Furthermore, both in an active fluid or in a passive fluid activated by stochastic forcing, unequal size particles exhibit short time ballistic motion.
This shows that the effective equilibrium limits, that have been obtained for “dry” active systems, do not hold for “wet” active systems.
The effective interaction between activated particles can be experimentally verified by more precise measurements of the particle position in the optical trap setups used in the experiments reported in Ref.
Bérut et al. 2016
.

We expect the interplay between hydrodynamic interactions and active fluctuations will also lead to effective interaction between self-propelled particles in a passive fluid. Since the effective interactions are sensitive to the precise origin of the fluctuation, the form of effective interaction may be different for different models of self-propelled particles.
There are various extensions of this work that will be useful to explore.
So far in this work we only look at incompressible fluid, it would be of interest to analyze the behavior for compressible fluids.
The form of the effective interaction and self-propulsion velocity was computed in the far-field approximation of the hydrodynamic interaction. We expect the effective interaction and self-propulsion to exist in lubrication limit as well.

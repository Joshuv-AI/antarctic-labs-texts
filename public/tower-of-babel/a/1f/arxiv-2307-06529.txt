Wavelet-based Edge Multiscale Parareal Algorithm for subdiffusion equations with heterogeneous coefficients in a large time domain
Guanglian Li

Abstract
We present the Wavelet-based Edge Multiscale Parareal (WEMP) Algorithm, recently proposed in [Li and Hu, J. Comput. Phys. , 2021], for efficiently solving subdiffusion equations with heterogeneous coefficients in long time. This algorithm combines the benefits of multiscale methods, which can handle heterogeneity in the spatial domain, and the strength of parareal algorithms for speeding up time evolution problems when sufficient processors are available. Our algorithm overcomes the challenge posed by the nonlocality of the fractional derivative in previous parabolic problem work by constructing an auxiliary problem on each coarse temporal subdomain to completely uncouple the temporal variable. We prove the approximation properties of the correction operator and derive a new summation of exponential to generate a single-step time stepping scheme, with the number of terms of 𝒪 ⁡ ( | log ⁡ τ f | 2 ) \mathcal{O}(|\log{\tau_{f}}|^{2}) independent of the final time, where τ f \tau_{f} is the fine-scale time step size. We establish the convergence rate of our algorithm in terms of the mesh size in the spatial domain, the level parameter used in the multiscale method, the coarse-scale time step size, and the fine-scale time step size. Finally, we present several numerical tests that demonstrate the effectiveness of our algorithm and validate our theoretical results.



Keywords:

multiscale, heterogeneous, long time, edge, wavelets, parareal, time-fractional, diffusion

Introduction

We consider in this paper a new efficient multiscale parareal algorithm for time-fractional diffusion problems with heterogeneous coefficients, which reads to find
u\in V:=H^{1}_{0}(D)
, satisfying

\left\{\begin{aligned} {\partial^{\alpha}_{t}}u&=\nabla\cdot\left(\kappa\nabla u\right)+f&&\text{in }D,\quad t\in\left(0,T\right]\\
u&=0&&\text{on }\partial D,\;t\in\left(0,T\right]\\
u(\cdot,0)&=u_{0}&&\text{in }D.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

Here,
{\partial^{\alpha}_{t}}u
(
\alpha\in(0,1)
) refers to the left-sided Caputo fractional derivative of order
\alpha
of the function

, defined by (see, e.g.
[

or
[

{\partial^{\alpha}_{t}}u(\cdot,t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\alpha}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial s}(\cdot,s)\,\mathrm{ds}

(1.2)

with
\Gamma(\cdot)
being Euler’s Gamma function:
\Gamma(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{x-1}\mathrm{e}^{-s}\mathrm{d}s
for
x>0
.

Furthermore,

for
d=2,3
is bounded with certain smoothness and
\kappa
is piecewise smooth as described in Assumption
3.1
. The force term
f\in W^{2,\infty}([0,T];L^{2}(D))
, the initial data
u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{2\sigma}(D)\cap H^{1}_{0}(D)
with
\sigma\in(0,1]
. The subspace
\dot{H}^{2\sigma}(D)\subset L^{2}(D)
is a Hilbert space to be defined in Section
2
. To simplify the notation, let
I:=[0,T]
. The main challenges for solving Problem (
1.1
) numerically lie in: 1) the existence of multiple scales in the coefficient
\kappa
, since resolving the problem to the finest scale as mandatory for classical numerical methods would incur huge number of degrees of freedom in the spatial domain. 2) the nonlocality of the fractional derivative
{\partial^{\alpha}_{t}}
(
1.2
) makes the numerical computation and storage extremely costly especially for the case of long time simulation with a large final time
T
.

On the one hand, the multiscale nature of the solution in the spatial domain as a consequence of the heterogeneous coefficient
\kappa
makes the computation challenging and deteriorates the storage cost. Nevertheless, the accurate description of many important applications, e.g., composite materials, porous media, ground water modeling and reservoir simulation, inevitably involves many heterogeneous parameters with multiple scales. They can have both multiple inseparable scales and high-contrast in order to reflect the nature of the practical applications. Because of this, the classical numerical treatment becomes prohibitively expensive especially for the unsteady problem (
1.1
). Consequently, various multiscale model reduction techniques have been proposed in the literature over the past few decades. Among them are multiscale finite element methods (MsFEMs), heterogeneous multiscale methods (HMMs), variational multiscale methods, flux norm approach, generalized multiscale finite element methods (GMsFEMs) and localized orthogonal decomposition (LOD)
[

. Wavelet-based Edge Multiscale Finite Element Methods (WEMsFEMs) was proposed recently by the author, c.f. Algorithm

[
15
,
16
,
23
]
. This method utilizes wavelets as the basis functions over the coarse edges to define local multiscale basis functions, which facilitates transforming the approximate rate over the edges to the convergence rate in each local region. Then the Partition of Unity Method (PUM)
[
35
]
is applied to derive the global convergence rate. The motivation for using wavelets as the ansatz space is that the existence of heterogeneity causes the solution has a low regularity, and wavelets are known to be efficient in approximating functions with such unfavorable property.

One the other hand, the nonlocality of the fractional derivative makes long-time simulation prohibitive, which are essential in many applications such as the rare event sampling and molecular dynamics simulation. The L1 scheme is one of the well known time stepping schemes that uses piecewise linear approximation on each time step. High order schemes have been designed and analyzed in the literature to obtain a higher order convergence rate when the solution has sufficient temporal regularity. Furthermore, these time stepping schemes can be combined with approximation by exponential sums or the proper orthogonal decomposition method in the history part to reduce the storage costs
[

,
38

39

[

]
by using an iterative solver based on a cheap inaccurate sequential coarse-scale time stepping scheme and expensive accurate fine-scale time stepping scheme that can be performed in parallel. This algorithm was introduced by Lions, Mayday and Turinici
[
27
]
. Coupling of parareal algorithm and some other techniques has been developed in many literature, see

As a continuation of
[
24

Firstly, we derive a new model reduction in the temporal domain by means of summation of exponentials to handle long time situation with the number of terms independent of the final time. Secondly, the stability of the resulting time stepping scheme is rigorously analyzed, with a very mild requirement on the accuracy of the approximation by summation of exponentials. Thirdly, together with the multiscale model reduction in the spatial domain, we obtain a stable iterative scheme with a very fast convergence rate. Furthermore, the convergence analysis is rigorously justified.

To demonstrate the performance of our proposed algorithm we present several numerical tests to first investigate the influence of approximation by exponential sums on the accuracy of our method. Then a series of simulations are made to show the convergence of our method. Finally, we present several numerical results with large final time

Problem setting

We first present in Section
2.1
the full discretization of problem (

) by the standard conforming Galerkin piecewise linear element in the spatial domain under a tiny fitted mesh

, which is analogous to

\{(\lambda_{m},\phi_{m})\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
be the eigenpairs of the following eigenvalue problems with the eigenvalues arranged in a nondecreasing order,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\phi_{m}

\displaystyle:=-\nabla\cdot(\kappa\nabla\phi_{m})=\lambda_{m}\phi_{m}

\displaystyle\text{ in }D

\displaystyle\phi_{m}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ on }\partial D.

Note that the eigenfunctions

The associated norm in

The Hilbert space

\displaystyle\dot{H}^{s}(D)=\{v\in L^{2}(D):\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{m}^{s}|(v,\phi_{m})_{D}|^{2}<\infty\}

2.1
Galerkin-L1-scheme

To discretize problem (
1.1
), we first introduce fine and coarse discretization in the spatial domain
D
. Let
\mathcal{T}_{H}
be a regular partition of the domain
D
into finite elements (triangles, quadrilaterals, tetrahedral, etc.) with a mesh size
H
, which is determined by the desired accuracy of our numerical method. We refer to this partition and its elements as coarse mesh and the coarse elements. Then each coarse element is further partitioned into a union of connected fitted fine elements. This fine-scale partition is denoted by
\mathcal{T}_{h}
with
h
being its mesh size. Let
\mathcal{F}_{h}
(or
\mathcal{F}_{H}
) be the collection of all edges in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
(or
\mathcal{T}_{H}
). Over the fine mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, let
V_{h}
be the conforming piecewise linear finite element space:

V_{h}:=\{v\in V:V|_{E}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(E)\text{ for all }E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},

where
\mathcal{P}_{1}(E)
denotes the space of linear polynomials on the fine element
E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.

The time interval
I:=[0,T]
is decomposed into a sequence of uniform coarse subintervals
[T^{n},T^{n+1}]
for
n=0,1,\cdots,M_{c}
of size
\tau_{c}
with
\tau_{c}:=T/M_{c}
for some
M_{c}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
T^{0}:=0
. The total number of coarse steps
M_{c}
is governed by the available number of processors and the aimed speedup. Each coarse time interval
[T^{n},T^{n+1}]
is further uniformly discretized with a fine time step
\tau_{f}
controlled by the expected accuracy in the temporal discretization.
Let
t_{n}=n\times\tau_{f}
for
n=0,1,\cdots,M_{f}
with
M_{f}:=T\times\tau_{f}^{-1}
. Note that we assume
\tau_{c}\gg\tau_{f}
throughout of this paper.

The
L1
scheme
[
26
,
36
]
is one of the most popular and simplest discretization schemes for (
1.2
) the temporal domain
I
, which is based on the idea of using piecewise constant approximation to approximate the first derivative. The scheme reads

\displaystyle\tilde{\partial}_{\tau_{f}}^{\alpha}v(x,t_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}b_{j}\frac{v(x,t_{n+1-j})-v(x,t_{n-j})}{\tau_{f}^{\alpha}c_{\alpha}}

(2.2)

with
c_{\alpha}=\Gamma(2-\alpha)
and
b_{j}=(j+1)^{1-\alpha}-j^{1-\alpha}
for
j=0,1,\cdots,n
.

The next lemma provides a lower bound of the coefficient for the L1 scheme:

Lemma 2.1
.

The coefficient from (
2.2
) has the following lower bound,

\displaystyle b_{j-1}-b_{j}\geq\alpha(1-\alpha)(n+1)^{-\alpha-1}\text{ for all }j=1,\cdots,n.

Proof.

Let
r(x):=(x+1)^{1-\alpha}-x^{1-\alpha}
for
x>0
. Then
r(x)
is decreasing and convex. Consequently,

\displaystyle b_{j-1}-b_{j}\geq b_{n-1}-b_{n}\geq-r^{\prime}(n)=(1-\alpha)\left(n^{-\alpha}-(n+1)^{-\alpha}\right).

Note that
r_{1}(x)=x^{-\alpha}
for
x>0
is decreasing and convex, this yields,

\displaystyle b_{j-1}-b_{j}\geq(1-\alpha)\left(n^{-\alpha}-(n+1)^{-\alpha}\right)\geq\alpha(1-\alpha)(n+1)^{-\alpha-1}.

We have proved the desired assertion.
∎

We use conforming Galerkin method for the discretization in the spatial domain

\left\{\begin{aligned} (\tilde{\partial}_{\tau_{f}}^{\alpha}u^{n}_{h},v_{h})_{D}+a(u_{h}^{n},v_{h})&=(f(t_{n}),v_{h})_{D}\quad\text{ for all }v_{h}\in V_{h}\\
u_{h}^{0}&=I_{h}u_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.3)

Here, the bilinear form

a(v_{1},v_{2}):=\int_{D}\kappa\nabla v_{1}\cdot\nabla v_{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\text{ for all }v_{1},v_{2}\in V

The fine-scale solution

Parareal algorithm

We present in Algorithm

This section is concerned with the construction of the multiscale space by means of the Wavelet-based Edge Multiscale Finite Element Methods (WEMsFEMs)

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot(\kappa\nabla\chi_{i})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\quad\text{ in }\;\;K

\displaystyle\chi_{i}

\displaystyle=g_{i}

\displaystyle\quad\text{ on }\partial K.

Here,

is affine over

for all

. Recall that

.

The construction of the multiscale test space

is presented in Algorithm

4
Model reduction in the history part

Recall that the time fractional operator
{\partial^{\alpha}_{t}}
can be split into a summation of the local component and the history component

\displaystyle{\partial^{\alpha}_{t}}v(x,t_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t_{n+1}}\frac{1}{(t_{n+1}-s)^{\alpha}}v^{\prime}(x,s)\,\mathrm{ds}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\frac{1}{(t_{n+1}-s)^{\alpha}}v^{\prime}(x,s)\,\mathrm{ds}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\frac{1}{(t_{n+1}-s)^{\alpha}}v^{\prime}(x,s)\,\mathrm{ds}

\displaystyle=:{\partial^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{N}}}v(x,t_{n+1})+{\partial^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{F}}}v(x,t_{n+1}).

(4.1)

We will approximate the local component
{\partial^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{N}}}v(x,t_{n+1})
by the L1 scheme and the history component
{\partial^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{F}}}v(x,t_{n+1})
by a new exponential sums inspired by sinc approximation
[
7
,
34
]
in Section
4.1
. Based upon this, a new local time integrator is presented in Section
4.2
that can handle long time simulation more efficiently.

4.1
Approximation by exponential sums

In order to deal with long time situation, we will construct a new approximation by exponential sums inspired by

[
7
]
, which is essentially based upon Sinc quadrature to analytic, improperly integrable functions in
\mathbb{R}
, see
[
34
]
. Let
F(s)
be an analytic, improperly integrable functions defined on
\mathbb{R}
and let
\tau>0
be the step size and
N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, then we obtain by integration after Sinc interpolation to
F(s)
the following quadrature formula

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}F(s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\approx\tau\sum\limits_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}F(j\tau)

\displaystyle\approx\tau\sum\limits_{j=-N}^{N}F(j\tau).

Here, the second estimate is a direct truncation of the first estimate. The quadrature error can be estimated by the following theorem
[
34
, Theorem 3.2.1]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
F(s)
be holomorphic in the strip
\mathcal{D}_{\delta}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\Im z\in(-\delta,\delta)\}
with
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\mathcal{D}_{\delta}}|F(s)||ds|<\infty,\text{ and for some }c,\beta>0:|F(s)|<c\mathrm{e}^{-\beta|s|}\text{ for all }s\in\mathbb{R}.

Then taking
\tau:=\Big(\frac{2\pi\delta}{\beta N}\Big)^{1/2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}F(s)\mathrm{d}s-\tau\sum\limits_{j=-N}^{N}F(j\tau)\right|\lesssim\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{2\pi\delta\beta N}}.

Here, the hidden constant depends only on
F(s)
,
\delta
and
\beta
.

We will utilize Lemma
4.1
to derive a new exponential sums approximation to
\frac{1}{t^{1+\alpha}}
. To this end, we first rewrite it as an integral over
\mathbb{R}
of a analytic, improperly integrable function, which satisfies the conditions listed in Lemma
4.1
.

Theorem 4.1

(Approximation by exponential sums for long time)
.

For all
\alpha>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, there holds

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{t^{1+\alpha}}-\tau\sum\limits_{\ell=-N}^{N}F(\ell\tau;t)\right|\leq\left(\frac{(t-\alpha/\mathrm{e})^{-1}+1}{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\pi\sqrt{\beta N}}}+\frac{2}{\beta}\right)\mathrm{e}^{-\pi\sqrt{\beta N}}\text{ for all }t>\alpha.

(4.2)

Given
t>0
,
F(s;t):\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
as a function of
s\in\mathbb{R}
is

\displaystyle F(s;t)

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{e}^{-t\ln(1+\mathrm{e}^{s})}(\ln(1+\mathrm{e}^{s}))^{\alpha}\frac{1}{1+\mathrm{e}^{-s}}.

(4.3)

The step size
\tau
and the positive parameter
\beta
are

\left\{\begin{aligned} \tau&:=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\beta N}}\\
\beta&:=\min\{1,t-\alpha\}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.4)

Proof.

For any
t>0
, by the definition of Gamma function, we obtain

\displaystyle t^{-(1+\alpha)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-tx}x^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Then taking integration by parts and a change of variables yields,

\displaystyle t^{-(1+\alpha)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-t\ln(1+\mathrm{e}^{s})}(\ln(1+\mathrm{e}^{s}))^{\alpha}\frac{1}{1+\mathrm{e}^{-s}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}F(s;t)\mathrm{d}s.

Here,
F(s;t):\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
as a function of
s
parameterized by
t\in(0,\infty)
is defined in (
4.3
). In the following, we will verify that
F(s;t)
satisfies the conditions listed in Lemma
4.1
when
t>\alpha
.

On the one hand, it holds that

\displaystyle|F(s;t)|\lesssim\mathrm{e}^{-(t-\alpha)|\Re s|}.

(4.5)

Indeed, Definition (
4.3
) implies

|F(s;t)|=\left|\mathrm{e}^{-t\ln(1+\mathrm{e}^{s})}\left(\ln(1+\mathrm{e}^{s})\right)^{\alpha}\frac{1}{1+\mathrm{e}^{-s}}\right|\lesssim\mathrm{e}^{-t\Re s}(\Re s)^{\alpha}\text{ for all }\Re s>0.

This, together with the fact that

\ln x\leq\frac{x}{\mathrm{e}}\text{ for all }x>0,

leads to

|F(s;t)|\leq\mathrm{e}^{-(t-\alpha)\Re s}\text{ for all }\Re s>0.

One can observe that

\displaystyle|F(s;t)|\lesssim\mathrm{e}^{-|\Re s|}\text{ for all }\Re s<0.

Consequently, inequality (
4.5
) has been proved.

Note that
F(s;t)
is holomorphic in the strip
\mathcal{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\Im z\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)\}
, and one can prove from inequality (
4.5
),

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\mathcal{D}}|F(s;t)||ds|\lesssim\frac{1}{t-\alpha}+1.

Consequently, all the requirement for Lemma
4.1
is fulfilled and the assertion follows from Lemma
4.1
.
∎

Note that we attempt to use sum of exponentials to approximate the history component
{\partial^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{F}}}v(x,t_{n+1})
, and thus we demand an estimate (
4.2
) for
t>\tau_{f}
instead. This, nevertheless, can be derived by a scaling argument.

Corollary 4.1
.

For any
\epsilon>0
and
\tau_{f}\in(0,1)
, there exist
(\omega_{j},\lambda_{j})_{j=1}^{N_{\text{exp}}}
, s.t.,

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{1}{t^{1+\alpha}}-\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\text{exp}}}\omega_{j}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{j}t}\Big|\lesssim\epsilon\text{ for all }t\in(\tau_{f},T),

(4.6)

with

N_{\text{exp}}\approx|\log({\tau_{f}^{1+\alpha}}{\epsilon})|^{2}.

Proof.

Let
t>\tau_{f}
, then the new variable
s:=({\alpha}+1)\frac{t}{\tau_{f}}>{\alpha}+1
. In view of (
4.2
), we obtain for
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{1}{s^{1+\alpha}}-\tau\sum\limits_{\ell=-N}^{N}F(\ell\tau;s)\Big|\leq\Big(\frac{2}{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\pi\sqrt{N}}}+2\Big)\mathrm{e}^{-\pi\sqrt{N}}

with
\tau=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{N}}
.

Next, making a change of variable by
t=\frac{s\tau_{f}}{{\alpha}+1}
and using (
4.3
), lead to

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{1}{t^{1+\alpha}}-\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\text{exp}}}\omega_{j}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{j}t}\Big|\lesssim\tau_{f}^{-1-\alpha}\Big(\frac{2}{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\pi\sqrt{\frac{N_{\text{exp}}-1}{2}}}}+2\Big)\mathrm{e}^{-\pi\sqrt{\frac{N_{\text{exp}}-1}{2}}}\text{ for all }t\in(\tau_{f},\infty).

Here,

\displaystyle N_{\exp}

\displaystyle=2N+1,

\displaystyle\omega_{j}

\displaystyle:=\tau_{f}^{-1-\alpha}({\alpha}+1)^{1+\alpha}\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{N}}\Big(\ln(1+\mathrm{e}^{(j+N+1)\tau})\Big)^{\alpha}\frac{1}{1+\mathrm{e}^{-(j+N+1)\tau}},

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}

\displaystyle:=\tau_{f}^{-1}({\alpha}+1)\ln(1+\mathrm{e}^{(j+N+1)\tau}).

Consequently, to guarantee

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{1}{t^{1+\alpha}}-\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\text{exp}}}\omega_{j}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{j}t}\Big|\lesssim\epsilon\text{ for all }t\in(\tau_{f},\infty),

one only needs to choose
N_{\text{exp}}
sufficiently large, s.t.,

\displaystyle\tau_{f}^{-1-\alpha}\Big(\frac{2}{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\pi\sqrt{\frac{N_{\text{exp}}-1}{2}}}}+2\Big)\mathrm{e}^{-\pi\sqrt{\frac{N_{\text{exp}}-1}{2}}}\lesssim\epsilon,

i.e.,

\displaystyle N_{\text{exp}}\approx|\log({\tau_{f}^{1+\alpha}}{\epsilon})|^{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

In another word, a number of
\mathcal{O}(|\log({\tau_{f}^{1+\alpha}}{\epsilon})|^{2})
terms is required to have an approximation error below a tolerance
\epsilon>0
. Note that this result is independent of the final time. Further, one can observe that
\lambda_{j}=\mathcal{O}(\tau_{f}^{-1})
and
\lambda_{j}>0
for all
j=1,\cdots,N_{\text{exp}}
. Denote

\displaystyle\gamma:=\min_{j=1,\cdots,N_{\text{exp}}}\{\lambda_{j}\},

(4.7)

then
\gamma\gg 1
and
\gamma=\mathcal{O}(\tau_{f}^{-1})
.

Remark 4.1

( Alternative approximation by exponential sums)
.

Another approximation by exponential sums are provided in
[
19
, Theorem 2.5]
,
For any
\epsilon>0
, there exist
(\omega_{1,j},\lambda_{1,j})_{j=1}^{N_{1,\text{exp}}}
, s.t.,

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{1}{t^{1+\alpha}}-\sum_{j=1}^{N_{1,\text{exp}}}\omega_{1,j}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{1,j}t}\Big|\lesssim\epsilon\text{ for all }t\in(\tau_{f},T),

(4.8)

with

N_{1,\text{exp}}\approx|\ln\epsilon|\Bigg(\ln|\ln\epsilon|+\ln\frac{T}{\tau_{f}}\Bigg)+|\ln\tau_{f}|\Big(\ln|\ln\epsilon|+|\ln\tau_{f}|\Big).

Note that this approximation is only suitable for cases with a final time of reasonable size due to the term
\ln\frac{T}{\tau_{f}}
in the total number of terms
N_{1,\text{exp}}
.

4.2
Local time integrators

We construct in this section local time integrators based upon the approximation by exponential sums developed in Corollary
4.1
.
The accuracy requirement of this exponential sums approximation is derived in (
6.1
), which implies that
\epsilon=\mathcal{O}(T^{-1-\alpha})
. The fine scale time step
\tau_{f}
can be very tiny due to the possible singularity of the solution
u(\cdot,t)
at the original
t=0
due to, for example, insufficient regularity in the source term
f
or the initial data
u_{0}
. For this reason, we assume

\displaystyle\tau_{f}\ll\epsilon

(4.9)

for given tolerance
\epsilon>0
throughout this paper.

Applying the summation of exponentials (Corollary
4.1
) to the history component
{\partial^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{F}}}
after taking integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}_{\tau_{f}}^{\alpha}v(x,t_{n+1})

\displaystyle\approx\frac{v(x,t_{n+1})-v(x,t_{n})}{\tau_{f}^{\alpha}c_{\alpha}}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\Bigg(\frac{v(x,t_{n})}{\tau_{f}^{\alpha}}-\frac{v(x,t_{0})}{t_{n+1}^{\alpha}}-\alpha\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm{exp}}}\omega_{j}\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{j}(t_{n+1}-s)}v(x,s)\mathrm{d}s\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{v(x,t_{n+1})-v(x,t_{n})}{\tau_{f}^{\alpha}c_{\alpha}}}_{=:{\bar{\partial}^{\alpha}_{\tau_{f},\mathcal{N}}}v(x,t_{n+1})}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\Big(\frac{v(x,t_{n})}{\tau_{f}^{\alpha}}-\frac{v(x,t_{0})}{t_{n+1}^{\alpha}}-\alpha\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\text{exp}}}\omega_{j}{\psi}_{j}[n]\Big)}_{=:{\bar{\partial}^{\alpha}_{\tau_{f},\mathcal{F}}}v(x,t_{n+1})}.

(4.10)

Here, the parameters
{\psi}_{j}[n]
for
j=1,\cdots,N_{\text{exp}}
are defined by

\displaystyle{\psi}_{j}[n]:=\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{j}(t_{n+1}-s)}v(x,s)\mathrm{d}s.

which can be written as

\displaystyle{\psi}_{j}[n]=\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{j}(t_{n+1}-s)}v(x,s)\mathrm{d}s+\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{j}(t_{n+1}-s)}v(x,s)\mathrm{d}s.

This implies the following recurrence relation

\displaystyle\psi_{j}[n]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{j}(t_{n+1}-t_{n})}\psi_{j}[n-1]+\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{j}(t_{n+1}-s)}v(x,s)\mathrm{d}s\text{ for }n\geq 1

\displaystyle\psi_{j}[0]

\displaystyle=0.

Note that the integrals in the recurrence relation can be estimated by the L1 scheme, which yields

\displaystyle\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{j}(t_{n+1}-s)}v(x,s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\approx\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{j}(t_{n+1}-s)}\Big(\frac{t_{n}-s}{\tau_{f}}v(x,t_{n-1})+\frac{s-t_{n-1}}{\tau_{f}}v(x,t_{n})\Big)\mathrm{d}s

(4.11)

\displaystyle=:c_{1,j}^{\tau_{f}}v(x,t_{n-1})+c_{2,j}^{\tau_{f}}v(x,t_{n}),

with

\displaystyle c_{1,j}^{\tau_{f}}:=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{j}\tau_{f}}}{\lambda_{j}^{2}\tau_{f}}\Big(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{j}\tau_{f}}-\lambda_{j}\tau_{f}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{j}\tau_{f}}\Big)\text{ and }c_{2,j}^{\tau_{f}}:=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{j}\tau_{f}}}{\lambda_{j}^{2}\tau_{f}}\Big(-1+\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{j}\tau_{f}}+\lambda_{j}\tau_{f}\Big).

(4.12)

Note also that the parameters
c_{i,j}^{\tau_{f}}
for
i=1,2
and
j=1,2,\cdots,N_{\text{exp}}
rely only on
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{\text{exp}}}
and the parameter
\tau_{f}
. Analogously, we can define
c_{i,j}^{\tau_{c}}
with a coarse step size
\tau_{c}
.

We establish an estimate of those parameters in the next lemma that provides a property of the history information.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
c_{i,j}^{\tau}
for
i=1,2
and
j=1,2,\cdots,N_{\text{exp}}
be defined in (
4.12
) with
\tau=\tau_{f}
or
\tau=\tau_{c}
. Then it holds

\displaystyle\Bigg|\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm{exp}}}\omega_{j}c_{i,j}^{\tau}\Bigg|\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma\tau}\Big(\tau^{-\alpha}(1-\alpha)^{-1}+\epsilon\tau/2\Big).

Proof.

A combination of (
4.11
) and (
4.8
) lead to

\displaystyle\Bigg|\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm{exp}}}\omega_{j}c_{1,j}^{\tau}\Bigg|

\displaystyle=\Bigg|\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm{exp}}}\omega_{j}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{j}(t_{n+1}-s)}\frac{t_{n}-s}{\tau_{f}}\mathrm{d}s\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma\tau}\Bigg|\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm{exp}}}\omega_{j}\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{j}(\tau-s)}\frac{\tau-s}{\tau}\mathrm{d}s\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma\tau}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}(\tau-s)^{-1-\alpha}\frac{\tau-s}{\tau}\mathrm{d}s+\epsilon\int_{0}^{\tau}\frac{\tau-s}{\tau}\mathrm{d}s\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma\tau}(\tau^{-\alpha}(1-\alpha)^{-1}+\epsilon\tau/2).

Recall that
\gamma
is defined in (
4.7
). This has proved the case for
i=1
. The other case can be shown similarly.
∎

Remark 4.2

(Efficiency of the new time stepping scheme (
4.10
))
.

{\bar{\partial}^{\alpha}_{\tau_{c}}}
can be defined similarly by replacing the fine time steps with the coarse time steps.
One can observe from formulas (
4.10
) that each calculation of
{\bar{\partial}^{\alpha}_{\tau_{c}}}v(x,T^{n})
involves
N_{\text{exp}}
calculation and
N_{\text{exp}}
storage for
n=2,\cdots,M_{c}
. Note from (
4.2
) and (
4.9
) that
N_{\text{exp}}\ll M_{f}
and
N_{\text{exp}}
is independent of the final time
T
as
T\to\infty
. Comparing with the L1 scheme (
2.2
), the new time stepping scheme is much more efficient.

For the sake of conciseness, we will rewrite the time integrator as

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}_{\tau_{f}}^{\alpha}v(x,t_{n+1})

\displaystyle=:\frac{v(x,t_{n+1})-v(x,t_{n})}{\tau_{f}^{\alpha}c_{\alpha}}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\Big(\frac{v(x,t_{n})}{\tau_{f}^{\alpha}}-\frac{v(x,0)}{t_{n+1}^{\alpha}}-\alpha\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\text{exp}}}\omega_{j}{\psi}_{j}[n]\Big)

(4.13)

\displaystyle\psi_{j}[n+1]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}(\psi_{j}[n];\tau_{f},v(x,t_{n}),v(x,t_{n+1}))\text{ for }n\geq 0\text{ and }\psi_{j}[0]=0\text{ for all }j=1,\cdots,N_{\text{exp}}.

Here, the propagation operator of the history information is

In matrix form, it reads

\left\{\begin{aligned} U^{n+1}_{\text{ms},\ell}&=\Big(\frac{\tau_{c}^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)}M_{\text{ms},\ell}+A_{\text{ms},\ell}\Big)^{-1}M_{\text{ms},\ell}\Bigg(\tau_{c}^{-\alpha}\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)}U^{n}_{\text{ms},\ell}+\\

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Phi^{n}_{\text{ms},\ell}&:=[\psi_{1}[n],\psi_{2}[n],\cdots,\psi_{N_{\mathrm{exp}}}[n]]&&\\

be the dimension of the multiscale space

\displaystyle+\alpha(1-\alpha)\tau_{c}^{\alpha}\int_{D}e_{{\text{ms}}}^{n+1}(v_{1,\rm{his}}-v_{2,\rm{his}})\,\mathrm{d}x.

\displaystyle\left\|e_{{\text{ms}}}^{n+1}\right\|_{D}^{2}\leq\alpha\left\|v_{1}-v_{2}\right\|_{D}\left\|e_{{\text{ms}}}^{n+1}\right\|_{D}+\alpha(1-\alpha)\tau_{c}^{\alpha}\left\|v_{1,\rm{his}}-v_{2,\rm{his}}\right\|_{D}\left\|e_{{\text{ms}}}^{n+1}\right\|_{D}.

The a direct calculation proves the first assertion.

2. To prove the second assertion, let

\displaystyle e^{n+1}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{S}(T^{n},v_{1};\Phi_{1})-\mathcal{S}(T^{n},v_{2};\Phi_{2})

\displaystyle=\Big(\mathcal{F}^{{\text{ms},\ell}}_{f}(T^{n},v_{1};\Phi_{1})-\mathcal{F}^{{\text{ms},\ell}}_{f}(T^{n},v_{2};\Phi_{2})\Big)-\Big(\mathcal{G}^{{\text{ms},\ell}}(T^{n},v_{1};\Phi_{1})-\mathcal{G}^{{\text{ms},\ell}}(T^{n},v_{2};\Phi_{2})\Big)

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathcal{F}}^{{\text{ms},\ell}}_{f}(T^{n},v_{1}-v_{2};\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2})-\bar{\mathcal{G}}^{{\text{ms},\ell}}(T^{n},v_{1}-v_{2};\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2})

\displaystyle=\left(\bar{\mathcal{F}}^{{\text{ms},\ell}}_{f}(T^{n},v_{1}-v_{2};\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2})-\bar{\mathcal{F}}^{{\text{ms},\ell}}(T^{n},v_{1}-v_{2};\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2})\right)

\displaystyle-\left(\bar{\mathcal{G}}^{{\text{ms},\ell}}(T^{n},v_{1}-v_{2};\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2})-\bar{\mathcal{F}}^{{\text{ms},\ell}}(T^{n},v_{1}-v_{2};\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2})\right)

\displaystyle=:e^{n+1}_{f}-e^{n+1}_{c}.

To estimate
e^{n+1}
, we only need to derive the estimate for
e_{f}^{n+1}
and
e_{c}^{n+1}
, separately.

Firstly, we introduce the following semidiscrete Galerkin approximation problem in the spatial domain
D
: let
v:=\bar{\mathcal{F}}^{{\text{ms},\ell}}(T^{n},v_{1}-v_{2};\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2})_{1}\in L^{2}([T^{n},T^{n+1}];V_{\text{ms},\ell})
satisfy

\displaystyle\forall w_{\text{ms}}\in V_{\text{ms},\ell}:\int_{D}{\partial^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{N}}}v\;w_{\text{ms}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{D}\kappa\nabla v\cdot\nabla w_{\text{ms}}\,\mathrm{d}x=-\frac{1}{\tau_{c}^{\alpha}\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{D}(v_{1}-v_{2})w_{\text{ms}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{D}(v_{1,\rm{his}}-v_{2,\rm{his}})w_{\text{ms}}\,\mathrm{d}x

(6.3)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad v(\cdot,T^{n})=v_{1}-v_{2}.

Note that the error equation for
e^{n+1}_{c}
derived from a fully discretization problem with L1 scheme in the temporal domain is

\displaystyle\forall w_{\text{ms}}\in V_{\text{ms},\ell}:\int_{D}\bar{\partial}_{\tau_{c},\mathcal{N}}^{\alpha}e_{c}^{n+1}\;w_{\text{ms}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{D}\kappa\nabla e_{c}^{n+1}\cdot\nabla w_{\text{ms}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{D}w_{0}\cdot w_{\text{ms}}\,\mathrm{d}x

(6.4)

\displaystyle e_{c}^{n}

\displaystyle=0.

with
w_{0}
being the approximation error from L1 scheme,

\displaystyle w_{0}

\displaystyle:=-{\partial^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{N}}}v(\cdot,T^{n+1})+{\bar{\partial}^{\alpha}_{\tau_{c},\mathcal{N}}}{v(\cdot,T^{n+1})},

which can be estimated by Lemma
6.2
,

\left\|w_{0}\right\|_{D}\leq{\rm C}_{\mathrm{0}}\tau_{c}^{-(1-\sigma)\alpha}\Big({|v_{1}-v_{2}|_{2\sigma}}+\alpha{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\Big).

The stability estimate
[
20
, Theorem 3.5]
implies

\displaystyle\left\|e_{c}^{n+1}\right\|_{D}\leq{\rm C}_{\mathrm{0}}\tau_{c}^{\sigma\alpha}\Big(|v_{1}-v_{2}|_{2\sigma}+\alpha{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\Big).

Analogously, we can obtain the estimate for
e_{f}^{n+1}
, which reads

\displaystyle\left\|e_{{f}}^{n+1}\right\|_{D}\leq{\rm C}_{\mathrm{0}}\tau_{f}^{\alpha}\tau_{c}^{\sigma\alpha-\alpha}\Big(|{v_{1}-v_{2}}|_{2\sigma}+\alpha\tau_{f}^{\alpha}\tau_{c}^{-\alpha}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\Big).

Note that
\tau_{f}\ll\tau_{c}
, then a combination of the two estimates above with the triangle inequality, shows the second assertion. ∎

to eliminate the source term

Lemma 6.5

(Smoothing property of the SOE-scheme (
4.13
))
.

Let Assumption
3.1
hold. Assume that the source term
f\in W^{2,\infty}(I;L^{2}(D))
and initial data
u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{2\sigma}(D)
for some
0<\sigma\leq 1
. Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
be the level parameter. The coarse time step size and fine time step size are
\tau_{c}
and
\tau_{f}

) and let

be the solution from Algorithm
3
with iteration

.
Then for any

, it holds

\displaystyle|{U_{\text{ms},\ell}^{m}-U_{k}^{n}}|_{2\sigma}

\displaystyle\lesssim(T^{n})^{-\alpha\sigma}\left\|u_{\text{ms},\ell}^{m^{\prime}}-U_{k}^{{n-1}}\right\|_{D}.

(6.5)

Proof.

Note that Algorithm
3
is equivalent to seeking
U_{k}^{n}\in V_{\text{ms},\ell}
for

, satisfying

\left\{\begin{aligned} \bar{\partial}_{\tau_{c}}^{\alpha}(U_{k}^{n},v_{\text{ms}})_{D}&+a(U_{k}^{n},v_{\text{ms}})=(\mathcal{P}_{\ell}f(\cdot,T^{n}),v_{\text{ms}})_{D}+\bar{\partial}_{\tau_{c}}^{\alpha}(S_{k-1}^{n},v_{\text{ms}})_{D}+a(S_{k-1}^{n},v_{\text{ms}})\quad\text{for all}\,\,v_{\text{ms}}\in V_{\text{ms},\ell}\\
\Phi_{k,j}^{n}&=\mathcal{H}(\Phi_{k,j}^{n-1};\tau_{c},U_{k}^{n-1},U_{k}^{n})\text{ for }n\geq 0\text{ and }\Phi_{k,j}^{0}=0\text{ for all }j=1,\cdots,N_{\text{exp}}\\
U_{k}^{0}&=\mathcal{P}_{\ell}u_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

(6.6)

Here, the local correction
S_{k-1}^{n}:=\mathcal{S}(T^{n-1},U^{n-1}_{k-1};\Phi^{n-1}_{k-1})
. Note that for all
v_{\text{ms}}\in V_{\text{ms},\ell}
, it holds

\displaystyle(\bar{\partial}_{\tau_{c}}^{\alpha}S_{k-1}^{n},v_{\text{ms}})_{D}+a(S_{k-1}^{n},v_{\text{ms}})=(\bar{\partial}_{\tau_{c}}^{\alpha}-\bar{\partial}_{\tau_{f}}^{\alpha})\Big(\mathcal{F}^{\text{ms},\ell}_{f}(T^{n-1},U_{k-1}^{n-1};\Phi_{k-1}^{n-1})_{1},v_{\text{ms}}\Big)_{D}.

Let

\left\{\begin{aligned} \bar{\partial}_{\tau_{c}}^{\alpha}(e_{k}^{n},v_{\text{ms}})_{D}&+a(e_{k}^{n},v_{\text{ms}})=(R_{n},v_{\text{ms}})_{D}\quad\text{for all}\,\,v_{\text{ms}}\in V_{\text{ms},\ell}\\
f_{j,k}[n]&=\mathcal{H}(f_{j,k}[n-1];\tau_{c},e_{k}^{n-1},e_{k}^{n})\text{ for }n\geq 0\text{ and }f_{j,k}[0]=0\text{ for all }j=1,\cdots,N_{\text{exp}}\\
e_{k}^{0}&=0\end{aligned}\right.

(6.7)

with the source term

being

\displaystyle:=-(\bar{\partial}_{\tau_{c}}^{\alpha}-\bar{\partial}_{\tau_{f}}^{\alpha})U_{\text{ms},\ell}^{m}+(\bar{\partial}_{\tau_{c}}^{\alpha}-\bar{\partial}_{\tau_{f}}^{\alpha})\mathcal{F}^{\text{ms},\ell}_{f}(T^{n-1},U_{k-1}^{n-1};\Phi_{k-1}^{n-1})_{1}

\displaystyle=-(\bar{\partial}_{\tau_{c}}^{\alpha}-\bar{\partial}_{\tau_{f}}^{\alpha})\Big(U_{\text{ms},\ell}^{m}-\mathcal{F}^{\text{ms},\ell}_{f}(T^{n-1},U_{k-1}^{n-1};\Phi_{k-1}^{n-1})_{1}\Big)

\displaystyle=-(\bar{\partial}_{\tau_{c}}^{\alpha}-\bar{\partial}_{\tau_{f}}^{\alpha})\mathcal{F}^{\text{ms},\ell}_{f}(T^{n-1},U_{\text{ms},\ell}^{m^{\prime}}-U_{k-1}^{n-1};\Phi_{\text{ms},\ell}^{m^{\prime}}-\Phi_{k-1}^{n-1})_{1}.

\displaystyle\|R_{n}\|_{D}\leq C_{0}(n-1)^{-\alpha}\tau_{c}^{-\alpha}\Big(\|e_{k-1}^{n-1}\|_{D}+\alpha{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Phi_{\text{ms},\ell}^{m^{\prime}}-\Phi_{k-1}^{n-1}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\Big).

\displaystyle\tau_{c}^{\alpha\sigma}|e_{k}^{n}|_{2\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}\tau_{c}^{\alpha}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\|R_{n}\|_{D}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}n^{1-\alpha}\Big(\|e_{k-1}^{n-1}\|_{D}+\alpha{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Phi_{\text{ms},\ell}^{m^{\prime}}-\Phi_{k-1}^{n-1}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\Big).

Consequently, we obtain

\displaystyle|e_{k}^{n}|_{2\sigma}\leq C_{0}n^{1-\alpha}\times\tau_{c}^{-\alpha\sigma}\Big(\|e_{k-1}^{n-1}\|_{D}+\alpha{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Phi_{\text{ms},\ell}^{m^{\prime}}-\Phi_{k-1}^{n-1}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\Big).

Then one can follow the proof in
[
37
, Lemma 7.3]
and
[

the desired result, under the condition that

∎

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\tau_{c}\gamma}((1-\alpha)^{-1}+\epsilon\tau_{c}^{1+\alpha}/2)<\eta(\alpha,\theta):=\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}(\theta-\alpha)\text{ for given }\theta\in[\alpha,1).

\displaystyle+\left({\tau_{f}}^{\sigma\alpha}+\eta(H,\ell)|\log\eta(H,\ell)|(T^{n})^{-\alpha}+\Pi_{j=0}^{k}\Big(\frac{\tau_{c}}{T^{n-j}}\Big)^{\alpha\sigma}\right)|u_{0}|_{2\sigma}.

We next estimate the error induced by parareal algorithm in the multiscale method, i.e., the second term

\displaystyle e_{k}^{n}:=\left\|U_{\text{ms},\ell}^{m}-U_{k}^{n}\right\|_{D}\leq{\rm C}_{\mathrm{0}}\Pi_{j=0}^{k}\Big(\frac{\tau_{c}}{T^{n-j}}\Big)^{1-\alpha}|u_{0}|_{2\sigma}.

Obviously, the inequality (

k

k+1
. Before that, we first derive the error estimate for the history information. Let

\displaystyle d_{k}^{n}:={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Phi^{m}_{\text{ms},\ell}-\Phi^{n}_{k}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}

\displaystyle U_{\text{ms},\ell}^{m}-U_{k+1}^{n}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}(T^{n-1},U_{\text{ms},\ell}^{m^{\prime}{}};\Phi^{m^{\prime}}_{\text{ms},\ell})-\mathcal{S}(T^{n-1},U_{k}^{n-1};\Phi^{n-1}_{k})

6.4

\displaystyle e_{k+1}^{n}=\left\|U_{\text{ms},\ell}^{m}-U_{k+1}^{n}\right\|_{D}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\mathcal{S}(T^{n-1},U_{\text{ms},\ell}^{m^{\prime}{}};\Phi^{m^{\prime}}_{\text{ms},\ell})-\mathcal{S}(T^{n-1},U_{k}^{n-1};\Phi^{n-1}_{k})\right\|_{D}+\left\|\mathcal{G}^{{\text{ms},\ell}}(T^{n-1},u_{\text{ms},\ell}^{m^{\prime}{}};\Phi^{m^{\prime}}_{\text{ms},\ell})-\mathcal{G}^{{\text{ms},\ell}}(T^{n-1},U_{k+1}^{n-1};\Phi^{n-1}_{k+1})\right\|_{D}

be a positive parameter, then together with the inequality (

\displaystyle(1-\beta)e_{k+1}^{n}+\beta d_{k+1}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq{\rm C}_{\mathrm{0}}(1-\beta+\beta\eta(\alpha,\theta)(1+\alpha))\Big(\frac{\tau_{c}}{T^{n}}\Big)^{\alpha\sigma}\Big(e_{k}^{n-1}+\alpha d_{k}^{n-1}\Big)

and

\displaystyle(1-\beta)\alpha(1-\alpha)+\beta\eta(\alpha,\theta)(1+\alpha-\alpha^{2})\leq\theta\beta.

6.10
) and (
6.14
), lead to

\displaystyle(1-\beta)e_{k+1}^{n}+\beta d_{k+1}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq{\rm C}_{\mathrm{0}}^{2}\Bigg(1+\frac{2\alpha}{1-\eta(\alpha,\theta)}\Bigg)\Pi_{j=0}^{k+1}\Big(\frac{\tau_{c}}{T^{n-j}}\Big)^{\alpha\sigma}|u_{0}|_{2\sigma}

\displaystyle+\theta\Bigg((1-\beta)e_{k+1}^{n-1}+\beta d_{k+1}^{n-1}\Bigg).

\displaystyle(1-\beta)e_{k+1}^{n}+\beta d_{k+1}^{n}\leq{\rm C}_{\mathrm{0}}^{2}\Bigg(1+\frac{2\alpha}{1-\eta(\alpha,\theta)}\Bigg)\Pi_{j=0}^{k+1}\Big(\frac{\tau_{c}}{T^{n-j}}\Big)^{\alpha\sigma}|u_{0}|_{2\sigma}+\theta\Bigg((1-\beta)e_{k+1}^{n-1}+\beta d_{k+1}^{n-1}\Bigg).

(6.15)

\displaystyle(1-\beta)e_{k+1}^{n}+\beta d_{k+1}^{n}\leq\frac{1}{1-\theta}{\rm C}_{\mathrm{0}}^{2}\Bigg(1+\frac{2\alpha}{1-\eta(\alpha,\theta)}\Bigg)\Pi_{j=0}^{k+1}\Big(\frac{\tau_{c}}{T^{n-j}}\Big)^{\alpha\sigma}|u_{0}|_{2\sigma}\times A(n,k),

The last inequality above holds because
\alpha\sigma<1
. Notice that we have proved in
[
24
, Theorem 4.1]
that

is uniformly bounded with respect to

when

is small. Consequently, this proves the case for
k+1
, and completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\frac{\|u_{h}^{n}-\hat{u}_{h}^{n}\|_{D}}{\|u_{h}^{n}\|_{D}}\times 100\qquad\frac{\|u_{h}^{n}-\hat{u}_{h}^{n}\|_{H^{1}_{\kappa}(D)}}{\|u_{h}^{n}\|_{H^{1}_{\kappa}(D)}}\times 100,

7.1

and

Let

8
Conclusion

We propose in this paper a new efficient algorithm for time-fractional diffusion equations with heterogeneous coefficients. This algorithm is named as the Wavelet-based Edge Multiscale Parareal (WEMP) Algorithm, which can reduce computational cost and storage cost significantly. Moreover, it is suitable for long time simulation when multiple processors are available. We want to remark that the total number of terms in the exponential sums can be reduced hugely for a given accuracy requirement (
4.2
) if an adaptive representation instead of a uniform representation is utilized. Consequently, this will lead to a more efficient algorithm for more challenging applications, for example, for much longer time simulation.

1.
Introduction

Let
S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},...,x_{n}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring over a field
\mathrm{k}
. Let
I=I(G)
be the edge ideal of a simple graph
G
on the vertex set
[n]
. For each
s\geq 1
, we have the following containments of ideals
I^{s}\subseteq\overline{I^{s}}\subseteq I^{(s)}
where
\overline{I^{s}}
is the integral closure of
I^{s}
and
I^{(s)}
is the
s
-th symbolic powers of
I
(see Section
2
for the definition of integral closure and symbolic powers). In
[
MNPTV
]
, together with Nam, Phong, and Thuy, we laid out a general procedure for comparing regularity of monomial ideals and proved that
\reg I^{s}=\reg I^{(s)}
for
s=2,3
. Subsequently, in
[
MV1
,
MV2
]
, we proved a rigidity property of the regularity of intermediate ideals lying between
I^{s}
and
I^{(s)}
. More precisely, for monomial ideals
J\subseteq K
, we define
\Inter(J,K)
the set of monomial ideals
L
such that
L=J+(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})
where
f_{i}
are among minimal monomial generators of
K
. In this paper, we apply this procedure to prove that all ideals in
\Inter(I^{s},\overline{I^{s}})
have the same regularity for
s\leq 4
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
I
be the edge ideal of a simple graph
G
. For all
s\leq 4
and all intermediate ideals
J\in\Inter(I^{s},\overline{I^{s}})
, we have

\reg J=\reg\overline{I^{s}}=\reg(I^{s}).

The asymptotic property of regularity of integral closure of powers
\reg\overline{I^{s}}
was established in
[
CHT
]
alongside the asymptotic property of regularity of powers
\reg I^{s}
in
[
CHT
,
Kod
]
. Nonetheless, to our knowledge, there was no explicit calculation of regularity of integral closure of powers of edge ideals in the case where
I
is not normal. By
[
SVV
,
OH
]
,
I
is not normal, i.e.
\overline{I^{s}}\neq I^{s}
for some
s>1
if and only if
G
has an induced subgraph which is the disjoint union of two odd cycles. In the simplest case where
I
is not normal, i.e.
G
is an odd bicyclic graphs, Kumar and Kumar
[
KK
]
prove that
\reg I^{s}=\reg\overline{I^{s}}
for all
s
. The regularity of powers of edge ideals of bicyclic graphs is calculated in few cases
[
CJNP
,
G
]
; though, these edge ideals are normal. The inequality
\reg\overline{I^{s}}\leq\reg I^{s}
is known in some cases
[
BCDMS
]
.

We realize that the Stanley-Reisner ideal
I_{\Delta}
of a one-dimensional simplicial complex
\Delta
provides plenty of examples of non-normal squarefree monomial ideals. Indeed, if
\Delta
viewed as a graph, contains an induced subgraph that is isomorphic to the complete bipartite graph
K_{3,3}
, then
I_{\Delta}
is non-normal. Using the arguments in
[
MV2
]
, we deduce the following

Theorem
4.1
.
Let
I=I_{\Delta}
be the Stanley-Reisner ideal of a one-dimensional simplicial complex
\Delta
. Assume that
I
is non-normal. Then
\girth\Delta\leq 4
. Furthermore, for all
s\geq 1
, and all
J\in\Inter(I^{s},\overline{I^{s}})
, we have

\reg J=\reg I^{s}=\begin{cases}3s&\text{ if }\girth\Delta=3\\
2s+1&\text{ if }\girth\Delta=4.\end{cases}

This paper is a continuation of
[
MNPTV
]
where we analyse the degree complex
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)
via the radical ideal
\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
. When studying the degree complex
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I^{s})
with
\mathbf{a}
supports on two odd cycles, we discover the following example whose all powers have regularity depend on the characteristic of the base field.

Example 1.2
.

Let

\displaystyle I=(x_{1}y_{1},x_{2}y_{1},x_{3}y_{1},x_{7}y_{1},x_{9}y_{1},x_{1}y_{2},x_{2}y_{2},x_{4}y_{2},x_{6}y_{2},x_{10}y_{2},x_{1}y_{3},x_{3}y_{3},x_{5}y_{3},x_{6}y_{3},

\displaystyle x_{8}y_{3},x_{2}y_{4},x_{4}y_{4},x_{5}y_{4},x_{7}y_{4},x_{8}y_{4},x_{3}y_{5},x_{4}y_{5},x_{5}y_{5},x_{9}y_{5},x_{10}y_{5},x_{6}y_{6},x_{7}y_{6},x_{8}y_{6},x_{9}y_{6},

\displaystyle x_{10}y_{6},z_{1}z_{2},z_{2}z_{3},z_{1}z_{3},z_{4}z_{5},z_{5}z_{6},z_{4}z_{6})\subseteq k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{10},y_{1},\ldots,y_{6},z_{1},\ldots,z_{6}],

be the edge ideal of the disjoint union of two triangles and the graph in the Dalili and Kummini’s example
[
DK
]
. By Corollary
5.2
, for each
s\geq 1
, we have

\reg I^{s}=\reg\overline{I^{s}}=\reg I^{(s)}=\begin{cases}5+2s&\text{ when }\charr\mathrm{k}=2\\
4+2s&\text{ when }\charr\mathrm{k}\neq 2.\end{cases}

In particular,
\reg I^{s},\reg\overline{I^{s}},\reg I^{(s)},
and their asymptotic constants all depend on the characteristic of the base field.

The example shows that the asymptotic linearity constant of
\reg I^{s}
is not combinatorial. Note that, for edge ideals in the examples of Katzman
[
Kat
]
and Dalili and Kummini
[
DK
]
, the regularity of their second powers do not depend on the characteristic of the base field.

We now describe the idea of proof of Theorem
1.1
. For a monomial ideal
J
, an exponent
(\mathbf{a},i)\in\NN^{n}\times\NN
is called an extremal exponent of
J
if
\reg S/J=|\mathbf{a}|+i
and
\lk_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)}F
has a non-vanishing homology in degree
i-1
for some face
F
of
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)
such that
F\cap\supp\mathbf{a}=\emptyset
. The steps to prove Theorem
1.1
(
s=3
and
s=4
) are:

(1)

Let
J_{1}\subseteq J_{2}\in\Inter(I^{s},\overline{I^{s}})
be intermediate ideals. Let
(\mathbf{a},i)
be an extremal exponent of
J_{2}
. We prove that if
\sqrt{J_{2}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}\neq\sqrt{J_{1}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
, then there exists a variable
x_{t}
such that
x_{t}\in\sqrt{J_{2}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
and
x_{t}\notin\supp x^{\mathbf{a}}
. By induction, Lemma
2.19
, and Lemma
2.20
we deduce that
\reg J_{2}\leq\reg J_{1}.

(2)

For each form
x^{\mathbf{a}}\notin I^{s}
, for which
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I^{s})\neq\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(\overline{I^{s}})
, using Lemma
2.19
and induction, we reduce to the case where
G
is the disjoint union of a smaller subgraph and odd cycles. We then use
[
NV1
, Theorem 1.1]
to establish the reverse inequality
\reg I^{s}\leq\reg\overline{I^{s}}
.

Since we do not have a mixed sum type formula for the regularity of the integral closure of powers, we need to consider disconnected graphs in our arguments. The main obstruction to carry out this comparison for higher powers is that when
s\geq 5
, the difference between
\sqrt{\overline{I^{s}}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
and
\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
are hard to describe.

Now we explain the organization of the paper. In Section
2
, we recall some notation and basic facts about Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity, the integral closure, and the degree complexes of monomial ideals. In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.1
. In section
4
, we compute the regularity of intermediate ideals in
\Inter(I_{\Delta}^{s},\overline{I_{\Delta}^{s}})
for Stanley-Reisner ideals of one-dimensional simplicial complexes. In Section
5
, we provide examples of graphs whose all powers have regularity depend on the characteristic of the base field.

2.
Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity, integral closure, and degree complexes

In this section, we recall some definitions and properties concerning Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity, the degree complexes of monomial ideals, symbolic powers of squarefree monomial ideals, and integral closure of powers of edge ideals. See
[
BH
,
D
,
E
,
S
,
V
]
for more details.

Throughout the paper, let
S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},...,x_{n}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring over a field
\mathrm{k}
. For statements without proper citations, we refer to
[
MNPTV
]
for proofs.

2.1.
Graphs and their edge ideals

Let
G
denote a finite simple graph over the vertex set
V(G)=[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and the edge set
E(G)
. For a vertex
x\in V(G)
, let the neighbours of
x
be the subset
N_{G}(x)=\{y\in V(G)~|~\{x,y\}\in E(G)\}
, and set
N_{G}[x]=N_{G}(x)\cup\{x\}
. For a subset
U
of the vertices set
V(G)
,
N_{G}(U)
and
N_{G}[U]
are defined by
N_{G}(U)=\cup_{u\in U}N_{G}(u)
and
N_{G}[U]=\cup_{u\in U}N_{G}[u]
. If
G
is fixed, we shall use
N(U)
or
N[U]
for short.

An independent set in
G
is a set of pairwise non-adjacent vertices.

A subgraph
H
is called an induced subgraph of
G
if for any vertices
u,v\in V(H)\subseteq V(G)
then
\{u,v\}\in E(H)
if and only if
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
.

An induced matching is a subset of the edges that do not share any vertices and it is an induced subgraph. The induced matching number of
G
, denoted by
\mu(G)
, is the largest size of an induced matching in
G
.

A
m
-cycle in
G
is a sequence of
m
distinct vertices
1,\ldots,m\in V(G)
such that
\{1,2\},\ldots,\{m-1,m\},\{m,1\}
are edges of
G
. The girth of
G
, denoted
\girth(G)
is the size of a smallest induced cycle in
G
.

A clique in
G
is a complete subgraph of
G
. We also call a clique of size
3
a triangle.

A graph
G
is bipartite if
[n]
can be partition into two disjoint subsets
[n]=U\cup V
such that every edge connects a vertex in
U
to one in
V
.

The edge ideal of
G
is defined to be

I(G)=(x_{i}x_{j}~|~\{i,j\}\in E(G))\subseteq S.

For simplicity, we often write
i\in G
(resp.
ij\in G
) instead of
i\in V(G)
(resp.
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
). By abuse of notation, we also call
x_{i}x_{j}\in I(G)
an edge of
G
.

2.2.
Simplicial complexes and Stanley-Reisner correspondence

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
that is a collection of subsets of
[n]
closed under taking subsets. We put
\dim F=|F|-1
, where
|F|
is the cardinality of
F
. The dimension of
\Delta
is
\dim\Delta=\max\{\dim F\mid F\in\Delta\}
. The set of its maximal elements under inclusion, called by facets, is denoted by
\mathfrak{F}(\Delta)
.

A simplicial complex
\Delta
is called a cone over
x\in[n]
if
x\in B
for any
B\in\mathfrak{F}(\Delta)
. If
\Delta
is a cone, it is acyclic (i.e., has vanishing reduced homology).

For a face
F\in\Delta
, the link of
F
in
\Delta
is the subsimplicial complex of
\Delta
defined by

\lk_{\Delta}F=\{G\in\Delta\mid F\cup G\in\Delta,F\cap G=\emptyset\}.

For each subset
F
of
[n]
, let
x_{F}=\prod_{i\in F}x_{i}
be a squarefree monomial in
S
. We now recall the Stanley-Reisner correspondence

Definition 2.1
.

For a squarefree monomial ideal
I
, the Stanley-Reisner complex of
I
is defined by

\Delta(I)=\{F\subset[n]\mid x_{F}\notin I\}.

For a simplicial complex
\Delta
, the Stanley-Reisner ideal of
\Delta
is defined by

I_{\Delta}=(x_{F}\mid F\notin\Delta).

The Stanley-Reisner ring of
\Delta
is
\mathrm{k}[\Delta]=S/I_{\Delta}.

From the definition, it is easy to see the following:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
I,J
be squarefree monomial ideals of
S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Then

(1)

\Delta(I)
is a cone over
t\in[n]
if and only if
x_{t}
does not divide any minimal generator of
I
.

(2)

I\subseteq J
if and only if
\Delta(I)\supseteq\Delta(J)
.

(3)

\Delta(I+J)=\Delta(I)\cap\Delta(J).

(4)

\Delta(I\cap J)=\Delta(I)\cup\Delta(J).

2.3.
Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity

Let
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be the maximal homogeneous ideal of
S
. For a finitely generated graded
S
-module
L
, let

a_{i}(L)=\begin{cases}\max\{j\in\ZZ\mid H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(L)_{j}\neq 0\}&\text{ if $H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(L)\neq 0$}\\
-\infty&\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(L)
denotes the
i
-th local cohomology module of
L
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
. Then, the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity (or regularity for short) of
L
is defined to be

\reg(L)=\max\{a_{i}(L)+i\mid i=0,\ldots,\dim L\}.

For a non-zero and proper homogeneous ideal
J
of
S
we have
\reg(J)=\reg(S/J)+1
.

We frequently use the result on the regularity of powers of mixed sum
[
NV1
, Theorem 1.1]
in this paper, so we state a part of it here for ease of reference.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
I\subseteq R
and
J\subseteq S
be proper monomial ideals in standard graded polynomial rings over a field
\mathrm{k}
. Let
P
be the sum of the extensions of
I
and
J
to
R\otimes_{\mathrm{k}}S
. Then

\reg P^{s}=\max_{i\in[1,s-1],j\in[1,s]}\left(\reg I^{i}+\reg J^{s-i},\reg I^{j}+\reg J^{s-j+1}-1\right).

In particular,

\reg P^{s}\geq\reg I^{j}+\reg J^{s-j+1}-1,\text{ for }j\in[1,s].

2.4.
Integral closure of monomial ideals

Definition 2.4
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal of
S
. The exponent set of
I
is
E(I)=\{\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}|x^{\mathbf{a}}\in I\}
. The Newton polyhedron of
I
is

\NP(I)=\conv(\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}|x^{\mathbf{a}}\in I),

the convex hull of the exponent set of
I
in
\RR^{n}
.

The following characterization of the integral closure of
I
in term of the Newton polyhedron of
I
is well-known (see e.g.
[
E
]
).

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal of
S
. Then the integral closure of
I
is a monomial ideal with the exponent
set
E(\overline{I})=\NP(I)\cap\ZZ^{r}.

Definition 2.6
.

A monomial ideal
I
of
S
is said to be normal if
\overline{I^{s}}=I^{s}
for all
s\geq 1
.

For a monomial
f
in
S
, the support of
f
, denoted
\supp(f)
, is the set of all indices
i\in[n]
such that
x_{i}|f
. For a monomial ideal
J
of
S
and a subset
V
of
[n]
, the restriction of
J
to
V
, denoted
J_{V}
is:

J_{V}=(f\mid f\text{ is a minimal generator of J}\text{ such that }\supp f\subseteq V).

We have

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal. Then

\overline{(I_{V})}=(\overline{I})_{V}.

Proof.

Follows from Lemma
2.5
.
∎

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal. Let
J\in\Inter(I^{s},\overline{I^{s}})
be an intermediate ideal. Let
V\subseteq[n]
be a subset. Then
J_{V}\in\Inter(I_{V}^{s},\overline{I_{V}^{s}}).

Proof.

Follows from the definition and Corollary
2.7
.
∎

For a monomial
f
of
S
and
i\in[n]
,
\deg_{i}(f)=\max(t\mid x_{i}^{t}\text{ divides }f)
denotes the degree of
x_{i}
in
f
. For a monomial ideal
I
, the degree of
x_{i}
in
I
is defined by

\rho_{i}(I)=\max\{\deg_{i}(u)\mid u\text{ is a minimal monomial generator of }I\}.

We have the following property of minimal generators of integral closure.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal. Then
\rho_{j}(\overline{I})\leq\rho_{j}(I),
for all
j=1,\ldots,n
. In particular, if
I
is squarefree, then
\rho_{j}(\overline{I^{s}})\leq s
for all
j=1,\ldots,n
.

Proof.

Let
x^{\mathbf{a}}
be a minimal generator of
\overline{I}
. By Lemma
2.5
, there exists non-negative real numbers
c_{1},...,c_{m}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}=1
and minimal generators
x^{\mathbf{b}_{1}},...,x^{\mathbf{b}_{m}}
of
I
such that

a_{j}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}b_{ij}

for all
j
. Since
x^{\mathbf{a}}
is minimal, this implies that
a_{j}=\lceil\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}b_{ij}\rceil
for all
j
. Since
b_{ij}\leq\rho_{j}(I)
and
\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}=1
, this implies that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}b_{ij}\leq\rho_{j}(I)
. Thus
a_{j}\leq\rho_{j}(I)
. The conclusion follows.
∎

We now describe the minimal generators of the integral closure of powers of edge ideals of simple graphs.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
I=I(G)
be the edge ideal of a simple graph
G
. Let
f
be a minimal generator of
\overline{I(G)^{s}}
. Then there exist odd cycles
C_{1},...,C_{2a}
, and edges
e_{1},...,e_{b}
of
G
such that

f=C_{1}\cdots C_{2a}\cdot e_{1}\cdots e_{b},

where
(|C_{1}|+\cdots+|C_{2a}|)/2+b=s.
Furthermore, the odd cycles
C_{i}
can be chosen so that
N(C_{i})\cap C_{j}=\emptyset
for all
i\neq j\in\{1,...,2a\}
.

Proof.

For each pair
C_{1},C_{2}
of induced disjoint odd cycles, denote
m_{C_{1},C_{2}}=x_{C_{1}}\cdot x_{C_{2}}
. Denote
P
the set of all pairs of disjoint odd cycles in
G
. By
[
V
, Proposition 8.7.19]
, we have

\overline{\mathcal{R}(It)}=S[It,m_{C_{i},C_{j}}t^{m_{i,j}/2}\mid(C_{i},C_{j})\in P],

where
m_{i,j}=\deg m_{C_{i},C_{j}}
. The conclusion follows by taking the graded component of degree
s
and a remark that if
N(C_{i})\cap C_{j}\neq\emptyset
, then
x_{C_{i}}\cdot x_{C_{j}}\in I^{u}
with
u=m_{i,j}/2.

∎

2.5.
Symbolic powers

Let
I
be a non-zero and proper homogeneous ideal of
S
. Let
\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{r}\}
be the set of the minimal prime ideals of
I
. Given a positive integer
s
, the
s
-th symbolic power of
I
is defined by

I^{(s)}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}I^{s}S_{P_{i}}\cap S.

2.6.
Degree complexes

For a monomial ideal
I
in
S
, Takayama in
[
T
]
found a combinatorial formula for
\dim_{\mathrm{k}}H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(S/I)_{\mathbf{a}}
for all
\mathbf{a}\in\ZZ^{n}
in terms of certain simplicial complexes which are called degree complexes. For every
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\ZZ^{n}
we set
G_{\mathbf{a}}=\{i\mid\ a_{i}<0\}
and write
x^{\mathbf{a}}=\Pi_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{a_{j}}
. Thus,
G_{\mathbf{a}}=\emptyset
whenever
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
. The degree complex
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)
is the simplicial complex whose faces are
F\setminus G_{\mathbf{a}}
, where
G_{\mathbf{a}}\subseteq F\subseteq[n]
, so that for every minimal generator
x^{\mathbf{b}}
of
I
there exists an index
i\not\in F
with
a_{i}<b_{i}
. It is noted that
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)
may be either the empty complex or
\{\emptyset\}
and its vertex set may be a proper subset of
[n]
. The next lemma is useful to compute the regularity of a monomial ideal in terms of its degree complexes.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal in
S
. Then

\reg(S/I)=\max\{|\mathbf{a}|+i~|~\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n},i\geq 0,\widetilde{H}_{i-1}(\lk_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)}F;\mathrm{k})\neq 0\\
\text{ for some $F\in\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)$ with $F\cap\supp\mathbf{a}=\emptyset$}\}.

In particular, if
I=I_{\Delta}
is the Stanley-Reisner ideal of a simplicial complex
\Delta
then

\reg(\mathrm{k}[\Delta])=\max\{i\mid\widetilde{H}_{i-1}(\lk_{\Delta}F;\mathrm{k})\neq 0\text{ for some }F\in\Delta\}
.

Remark 2.12
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal in
S
and a vector
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
. In the proof of
[
T
, Theorem 1]
, the author showed that if there exists
j\in[n]
such that
a_{j}\geq\rho_{j}(I)
then
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)
is either a cone over
\{j\}
or the void complex.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal in
S
. A pair
(\mathbf{a},i)\in\NN^{n}\times\NN
is called
an extremal exponent of
I
if
\reg(S/I)=|\mathbf{a}|+i
as in Lemma
2.11
.

Remark 2.14
.

We sometime call
\mathbf{a}
instead of
(\mathbf{a},i)
an extremal exponent of
I
. Let
\mathbf{a}
be an extremal exponent of
I
. Then
x^{\mathbf{a}}\notin I
and
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)
is not a cone over
t
with
t\in\supp\mathbf{a}
. In particular, by Remark
2.12
,
\mathbf{a}
belongs to the finite set

\Gamma(I)=\{\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}~|~a_{j}<\rho_{j}\text{ for all }j=1,\ldots,n\}.

Furthermore, we have the following interpretation of the degree complex
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)
.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal in
S
and
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
. Then

I_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)}=\sqrt{I:x^{\mathbf{a}}}.

Remark 2.16
.

Let
\mathbf{a}
be an extremal exponent of
I
. By Lemma
2.2
, Lemma
2.15
, and Remark
2.14
, for each

, there exists a minimal generator

of

such that

.

We have the following inequality on the regularity of restriction of a monomial ideal.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal and

be a variable. Then

\reg(I,x)\leq\reg I.

Proof.

See
[

, Corollary 4.8]
or
[

, Lemma 2.14]

∎

Lemma 2.18
.

Let
J
be a monomial ideal in
S
and
V\subseteq[n]
. We have

\reg(J_{V})\leq\reg(J).

In particular, for a monomial ideal

then

\reg\overline{I_{V}^{s}}\leq\reg I^{s}.

Proof.

Let
\{t,\ldots,n\}=[n]\setminus V
. Then,
J_{V}+(x_{t},...,x_{n})=J+(x_{t},...,x_{n}).
The conclusion follows from Lemma

and the fact that

is a regular sequence with respect to

The last statement follows from the first statement and Lemma

The following lemma is essential to using the induction method in studying the regularity of a monomial ideal.

Lemma 2.19
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal and the pair
(\mathbf{a},i)\in\NN^{n}\times\NN
be its extremal exponent. If

is a variable that appears in
\sqrt{I:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
and
x\notin\supp\mathbf{a}
, then

\reg(I)=\reg(I,x).

For comparing regularity, we have the following useful lemma.

Lemma 2.20
.

Let
I,J
be proper monomial ideals of
S
. Let
(\mathbf{a},i)
be an extremal exponent of
I
. If
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)=\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)
, then
\reg I\leq\reg J
. In particular, if

and
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)=\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)
for all exponent
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
such that
x^{\mathbf{a}}\notin I
then
\reg I\leq\reg J.

For a monomial
f
in
S
, the radical of
f
is defined by
\sqrt{f}=\prod_{i\in\supp f}x_{i}
. We have the following simple observation on the generators of the radical of colon ideal.

Lemma 2.21
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal in
S
generated by the monomials
f_{1},...,f_{s}
and
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
. Then
\sqrt{I:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
is generated by
\sqrt{f_{1}/\gcd(f_{1},x^{\mathbf{a}})},...,\sqrt{f_{s}/\gcd(f_{s},x^{\mathbf{a}})}
.

Let
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
be an exponent. To study the radical ideal of the form
\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
, we introduce the notion of
I-\mathbf{a}
-radical power. For a monomial

, the

-radical power of

\rp_{\mathbf{a}}^{I}(f)=\max(t\mid f^{m}x^{\mathbf{a}}\in I^{t}\text{ for some }m>0).

If

then
\rp_{\mathbf{a}}^{I}(f)=\infty
. When fixing an ideal
I
, we omit
I
in the notation, and simply write
\rp_{\mathbf{a}}(f)
.

Assume now that
I=I(G)
is the edge ideal of a graph. The following description of generators of
\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
helps to simplify our arguments later on. The
I
-order of
f
is defined by

\ord_{I}(f)=\max(t\mid f\in I^{t}).

From the definition, it is clear that if
g|f
, then
\ord_{I}(g)\leq\ord_{I}(f)
.

Lemma 2.22
.

[
MV2
, Lemma 2.16]
Let
F
be an independent set of
G
, and
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
an exponent. Assume that

(2.1)

\sum_{j\in N(F)}a_{j}+\ord_{I}\left(\prod_{u\notin N[F]}x_{u}^{a_{u}}\right)\geq s,

then
x_{F}\in\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
. Conversely, if
x_{F}
is a minimal generator of
\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
then (
2.1
) holds.

We have the following property of the radical powers.

Lemma 2.23
.

Let
I=I(G)
be an edge ideal of a simple graph
G
. Let
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\NN^{n}
be exponents, and
f
a monomial in
S
. Then

(1)

\rp_{\mathbf{a}}(f)\geq\ord_{I}(x^{\mathbf{a}})
.

(2)

\rp_{\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}}(f)\geq\rp_{\mathbf{a}}(f)+\rp_{\mathbf{b}}(f)
.

(3)

If
\supp f\cup\supp\mathbf{a}\supseteq\supp C
, where
C
is an odd cycle of length
2\ell+1
, then
\rp_{\mathbf{a}}(f)\geq\ell
. Furthermore, if
\supp f\cap\supp C\neq\emptyset
then
\rp_{\mathbf{a}}(f)\geq\ell+1.

(4)

Let
C_{1},C_{2}
be disjoint odd cycles of length

respectively. If
\supp f\cup\supp\mathbf{a}\supseteq(\supp C_{1}\cup\supp C_{2})
, and
\supp f\cap(\supp C_{1}\cup\supp C_{2})\neq\emptyset
then
\rp_{\mathbf{a}}(f)\geq\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+1.

(5)

Let
F=\supp f
. Then

\rp_{\mathbf{a}}(f)\geq\sum_{j\in N(F)}a_{j}+\ord_{I}\left(\prod_{u\notin N[F]}x_{u}^{a_{u}}\right).

Proof.

Parts (1) and (2) follow from the definition. For part (3), it suffices to note that
\ord_{I}(x_{C})=\ell
and
\ord_{I}(x_{i}x_{C})=\ell+1
for any
i\in\supp C
. Part (4) follows from Part (2) and Part (3). Part (5) follows from Lemma
2.22
and a remark that if
F
is not an independent set then
\rp_{\mathbf{a}}(f)=\infty
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

Let
G
be a simple graph with vertex set
[n]
and edge set

. We assume that
G
has no isolated vertices;
G
might be disconnected. Let
I=I(G)
be the edge ideal of
G
. In this section, we will prove Theorem
1.1
. By Theorem
2.10
,
\overline{I}=I
and
\overline{I^{2}}=I^{2}
, we have two cases
s=3
and
s=4
. We start with a general formula for the radical of colon ideal in the case it contains a set of ’good variables’.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
I
be the edge ideal of a simple graph
G
. Let
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
be an exponent such that
x^{\mathbf{a}}\notin I^{s}
. Assume that
\mathbf{a}
has a decomposition
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{c}
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

x_{i}\in\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
for all
i\in N[\supp\mathbf{b}]
,

(2)

N[\supp\mathbf{b}]\cap\supp\mathbf{c}=\emptyset
.

Let
H
be the restriction of
G
to
[n]\setminus N[\supp\mathbf{b}]
. Denote
t=s-\ord_{I}(x^{\mathbf{b}})
. Then,

(3.1)

\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}=\left(x_{i}\mid i\in N[\supp\mathbf{b}]\right)+\sqrt{I(H)^{t}:x^{\mathbf{c}}}.

In particular, if
(\mathbf{a},i)
is an extremal exponent of
I^{s}
then

(3.2)

\reg(I^{s})\leq\reg I(H)^{t}+|\mathbf{b}|.

Proof.

First, we prove the equality (
3.1
). From the assumption, it is clear that the left hand side contains the right hand side. Let
V=[n]\setminus N[\supp\mathbf{b}]
. It suffices to prove that if
f
is a minimal generator of
\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
with
\supp f\subseteq V
, then
f\in\sqrt{I(H)^{t}:x^{\mathbf{c}}}
. If
f\in I
, then by definition,
f\in I(H)\subseteq\sqrt{I(H)^{t}:x^{\mathbf{c}}}
. Thus, we may assume that
F=\supp f
is an independent set. By Lemma
2.22
, we have

\sum_{j\in N(F)}a_{j}+\ord_{I}(h)\geq s,\text{ where }h:=\prod_{u\notin N[F]}x_{u}^{a_{u}}.

Since
N[\supp\mathbf{b}]\cap\supp\mathbf{c}=\emptyset
,
N[\supp\mathbf{c}]\cap\supp\mathbf{b}=\emptyset
. In particular,
N[F]\cap\supp\mathbf{b}=\emptyset
. Hence,

(3.3)

\sum_{j\in N(F)\cap V}c_{j}=\sum_{j\in N(F)}a_{j}.

Let

h_{1}:=\prod_{u\in V\setminus N[F]}x_{u}^{c_{u}}=\prod_{u\in V\setminus N[F]}x_{u}^{a_{u}}.

Hence,
h=x^{\mathbf{b}}h_{1}
. Since
N[\supp\mathbf{b}]\cap\supp h_{1}=\emptyset
,
\ord_{I}(h)=\ord_{I}(x^{\mathbf{b}})+\ord_{I}(h_{1})
. Thus

\sum_{j\in N(F)\cap V}c_{j}+\ord_{I}(h_{1})=\sum_{j\in N(F)}a_{j}+\ord_{I}(h)-\ord_{I}(b)\geq t.

By Lemma
2.22
,
f\in\sqrt{I(H)^{t}:x^{\mathbf{c}}}
as required.

We now prove (
3.2
). By (
3.1
),
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I^{s})=\Delta_{\mathbf{c}}(I(H)^{t})
. Since
(\mathbf{a},i)
is an extremal exponent of
I^{s}
,
x^{\mathbf{a}}\notin I^{s}
, hence
t\geq 1
and
x^{\mathbf{c}}\notin I^{t}
. By Lemma
2.11
,

\reg I^{s}=|\mathbf{a}|+i+1=|\mathbf{b}|+|\mathbf{c}|+i+1\leq|\mathbf{b}|+\reg I(H)^{t}.

The conclusions follow.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

A decomposition

In proving the inequality

of

with

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
I
be the edge ideal of a simple graph
G
. Fix a power
s\geq 2
. Let
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
be an extremal exponent of
I^{s}
. Assume that
\mathbf{a}
has a good decomposition
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{c}

be the restriction of

to

be the restriction of

\reg I^{s}\leq\reg\overline{I^{s}}

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
,
\reg I^{s}\leq\reg I(H)^{s}+|\mathbf{b}|

\reg L^{s}\geq\reg I(H)^{t}+\reg I(K)^{s-t+1}-1\geq\reg I(H)^{t}+|\mathbf{b}|\geq\reg I^{s}.

By assumption

\reg I^{s}\leq\reg L^{s}\leq\reg\overline{L^{s}}\leq\overline{I^{s}},

where the last inequality follows from Lemma

We now proceed to the proof of Theorem

for

.

4.
Integral closure of powers of Stanley-Reisner ideals of one-dimensional simplicial complexes

In this section, we compute the regularity of intermediate ideals in
\Inter(I_{\Delta}^{s},\overline{I_{\Delta}^{s}})
for a one-dimensional simplicial complex
\Delta
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
I=I_{\Delta}
be the Stanley-Reisner ideal of a one-dimensional simplicial complex
\Delta
. Assume that

is non-normal. Then
\girth\Delta\leq 4
. Furthermore, for all

, and all

, we have

\reg J=\reg I^{s}=\begin{cases}3s&\text{ if }\girth\Delta=3\\
2s+1&\text{ if }\girth\Delta=4.\end{cases}

Proof.

If
\girth\Delta\geq 4
then
I_{\Delta}
is the edge ideal of a graph
G
which is a complement of
\Delta
. By Theorem
2.10
,
I
is non-normal if and only if
G
has an induced subgraph consisting of two disjoint odd cycles. Since
\dim\Delta=1
, these two odd cycles must be triangles. Hence,
\girth\Delta\leq 4
. Let
(\mathbf{a},i)
be an extremal exponent of
J
. By Lemma
2.9
and Remark
2.14
,
a_{j}\leq s-1
for all
j\in[n]
. There are two cases.

Case 1.

\girth\Delta=3
. The arguments in
[
MV2
, Lemma 3.2]
carry over give us
\reg J=3s
.

Case 2.

\girth\Delta=4
. Then
I=I_{\Delta}
is the edge ideal of a graph
G
which is the complement of
\Delta
. The lower bound
\reg J\geq 2s+1
follows from Lemma
2.18
. Thus, it suffices to prove that
|\mathbf{a}|+i\leq 2s
. The argument in
[
MV2
, Lemma 3.3]
stills apply, and thus if
i=2
, then
|\mathbf{a}|\leq 2s-2
, hence
|\mathbf{a}|+i\leq 2s
. Now assume that
i\leq 1
, thus we may assume that
|\mathbf{a}|\geq 2s-1
. Since
I^{s}\subseteq J\subseteq\overline{I^{s}}\subseteq I^{(s)}
, we have

\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}\subseteq\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbf{a}}}\subseteq\sqrt{I^{(s)}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}=\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}

where the last equality follows from Lemma
2.11
and
[
MV2
, Lemma 3.5]
. By Lemma
2.11
,
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)=\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I^{s})
. By Lemma
2.20
and
[
MV2
, Theorem 1.1]
, we deduce that

\reg J\leq\reg I^{s}=2s+1.

That concludes the proof of the Theorem.
∎

5.

In this section, we provide an example of an ideal whose regularity of powers depends on the characteristic of the base field. We first give a general statement which is a simple consequence of

.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
H
be a graph whose edge ideal

satisfies the condition

\reg I(H)^{s}\leq\reg I(H)+2s-2\text{ for all }s\geq 1.

Let
T
be a graph whose edge ideal

satisfies the condition

\reg I(T)^{s}=\reg I(T)+2s-2\text{ for all }s\geq 1.

Let

be the disjoint union of

and

. Then for all
s\geq 1
we have

\reg I(G)^{s}=\reg I(H)+\reg I(T)+2s-3.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.3
, we have

\reg I(G)^{s}=\max_{i\in[1,s-1],j\in[1,s]}(\reg I(H)^{i}+\reg I(T)^{s-i},\reg I(H)^{j}+\reg I(T)^{s-j+1}-1).

For all
1\leq i\leq s-1
, we have

\displaystyle\reg I(H)^{i}+\reg I(T)^{s-i}

\displaystyle\leq\reg I(H)+2i-2+\reg I(T)+2(s-i)-2

\displaystyle<\reg I(H)+\reg I(T)+2s-3.

For all
2\leq j\leq s
, we have

\displaystyle\reg I(H)^{j}+\reg I(T)^{s-j+1}

\displaystyle\leq\reg I(H)+2j-2+\reg I(T)+2(s-j)

\displaystyle=\reg I(H)+\reg I(T)+2s-2.

Finally,

\reg I(H)+\reg I(T)^{s}=\reg I(H)+\reg I(T)+2s-2.

The conclusion follows.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
G=C_{1}\cup C_{2}\cup H
be the disjoint union of two triangles and a bipartite graph
H
whose
\reg I(H)
depends on the characteristic of the base field (e.g. the Dalili-Kummini’s example
[
DK
]
). Then for all
s\geq 1
, we have

\reg I^{s}=\reg I^{(s)}=\reg\overline{I^{s}}=\reg I(H)+2s.

In particular,
\reg I^{s},\reg\overline{I^{s}},\reg I^{(s)}
depend on the characteristic of the base field as
\reg H
does.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.1

\reg I^{s}=\reg I(H)+\reg I(C_{1}\cup C_{2})+2s-3=\reg I(H)+2s.

With similar argument as in the proof of Theorem
5.1
, by
[
HNTT
, Theorem 5.11]
, and the fact that
I(H)^{s}=I(H)^{(s)}
,
\reg I(C_{1})^{(s)}=\reg I(C_{2})^{(s)}=2s
for all
s\geq 1
, we deduce that

\reg I^{(s)}=\reg I(H)+\reg I(C_{1}\cup C_{2})+2s-3=\reg I(H)+2s.

By
[
KK
, Theorem 4.14]
, we have
\reg I^{s}=\reg\overline{I^{s}}
. The Corollary follows.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

Let

Let

in Corollary
5.2
with any edge ideal

Askey–Wilson braid algebra and centralizer of U q ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 ) U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
Nicolas Crampé, Loïc Poulain d’Andecy, Luc Vinet, Meri Zaimi

Abstract
A presentation of the centralizer of the three-fold tensor product of the spin s s representation of the quantum group U q ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 ) U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}) is provided. It is expressed as a quotient of the Askey–Wilson braid algebra. This newly defined algebra combines the Askey–Wilson relations with the braid group relations, on three strands, together with a characteristic equation of degree 2 ​ s + 1 2s+1 for the braid generators. Explicit bases are given for the centralizer.



1
Introduction

Numerous quotients of the braid group algebra have been studied previously and occur in different contexts. For example, they naturally appear when considering the centralizer of tensor products of quantum group representations, and are used in the computation of the invariants for knot theory
[
26
,
27
,
1
,
33
]
. Note that explicit algebraic presentations for these centralizers are known only in a few cases. The most basic example is the Hecke algebra, which corresponds to a quotient of the braid group algebra realized by demanding that the generators satisfy a polynomial equation of degree
2
. The centralizer of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
in the tensor product of its fundamental representation is in general isomorphic to a quotient of the Hecke algebra
[
25
,
37
]
. For
n=2
, this centralizer is the Temperley–Lieb algebra
[
38
]
.
A quotient of the braid group algebra by a polynomial equation of degree
3
and additional relations, called the Birman–Murakami–Wenzl algebra
[
1
,
33
]
, has also been studied intensively. It is related to the centralizer of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
in the spin
1
representation
[
30
]
. Generalizations of the Hecke algebra for any symmetric representation have been described recently in
[
10
]
.
Let us also mention that quotients of the braid group algebra by a polynomial equation of degree
k
for the braid generators appear in the world of complex reflection groups
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
31
]
.
In particular, the resulting algebra is finite-dimensional only when
\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{n}>\frac{1}{2}
where
n
is the number of strands. It turns out to be quite difficult to find presentations of finite-dimensional quotients of the braid group algebra, see
e.g.

[
32
]
for the quotients by a cubic characteristic relation for the generators.
The main result of this paper can be seen as a new family of explicit presentations for finite-dimensional quotients of the braid group algebra on three strands.
For each integer
k>1
, one member of this family has a characteristic equation of degree
k
, and the additional relations to make it finite-dimensional (and in fact, isomorphic to the centralizer) are obtained from the Askey–Wilson algebra.

A new point of view on the centralizers of
U(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
or
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
in tensor products of any three possibly different spin representations has been brought up in
[
11
,
13
]
.
Indeed, these centralizers are related to the Racah or Askey-Wilson algebras.
The Racah algebra has been introduced in
[
20
]
in connection with the
6j
-symbols intervening in the coupling of three angular momenta.
The
q
-deformation of this approach has been initiated in
[
40
]
where the Askey–Wilson algebra is introduced.
The Racah and Askey–Wilson algebras have subsequently found applications in different contexts such as algebraic combinatorics
[
18
,
39
]
, Kauffman bracket skein algebras
[
5
]

and symmetry of physical models
[
28
,
35
,
19
]
.
Their representation theory has been studied in
[
23
,
22
,
21
]
. A review on the Racah algebra and its generalizations as well as the connection with the universal diagonal centralizer of
U(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
can be found in
[
8
]
, and a review of the Askey–Wilson algebra is given in
[
6
]
.

The goal of this paper is to combine the braid relations with the Askey–Wilson relations in order to provide
a description in terms of generators and relations of the centralizer of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
in the tensor product of three identical spin
s
representations. Our focus on three-fold tensor products (the three-strands case) is justified by the expectation that
in general the
n
-fold centralizers will be defined by relations involving a limited number of strands (this is called locality of the presentation). In other words, we expect the centralizer for general
n
to be fully described once a finite number of small values of
n
are understood. The level
n=3
is the first non-trivial one, where for example the braid relation appears. This level is enough to define the Temperley–Lieb, Hecke and Birman–Murakami–Wenzl algebras. In our case we expect to have new relations appearing at higher level, but nevertheless a good understanding of the case
n=3
is necessary for the general case.

To achieve our goal, we start with the quotient of the three-strands braid group algebra by a characteristic polynomial equation of degree
2s+1
. This quotient is a generalization of the three-strands Hecke algebra, which is recovered when
s=1/2
.
A new algebra, called Askey–Wilson braid algebra
AW\!B_{3}^{s}(q)
, is then defined by adding the Askey–Wilson relations. This algebra can both be seen as a quotient of the three-strands braid group algebra and as a quotient of the usual Askey–Wilson algebra.
The remaining of the paper consists in the study of the quotient of the Askey–Wilson braid algebra by two supplementary relations which are enough to prove that it is isomorphic to the centralizer of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
in the tensor product of three spin
s
representations. Let us remark that the connection between the braid group and the Askey–Wilson algebra has already been exploited to give a simpler interpretation of some generators of the Askey–Wilson algebra
[
9
]
and to find representations of the braid relation in terms of the
q
-Racah polynomials
[
14
]
. The latter point is related to spin Leonard pairs
[
15
,
34
]
.

The plan of this paper is as follows. Section
2
provides the notations and contains definitions of different quotients of the braid group algebra on three strands.
In particular the quotient
AW\!B_{3}^{s}(q)
, the main object of interest of this paper, is given in Definition
2.1
.
Then, the isomorphism between a quotient of
AW\!B_{3}^{s}(q)
and the centralizer of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
in the tensor product of three identical representations is stated in Theorem
2.2
and the rest of the paper is mainly devoted to its proof.
The definition of the centralizer of interest in this paper is given in Section
3
.
Using previous results in the literature, the surjectivity is established.
Section
4
focuses on the proof of the injectivity. Two explicit bases of the quotient of
AW\!B_{3}^{s}(q)
are provided. The cases where
s=1/2
and
s=1
are specifically considered as examples, and the connections with the Temperley–Lieb and Birman–Murakami–Wenzl algebras are exhibited.
The paper concludes with some perspectives in Section
5
. Technical proofs are gathered in appendix
A
.

2
The Askey–Wilson braid algebra and its special quotient

Let
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}/2
be a strictly positive integer or half-integer and
q
be an indeterminate. Throughout the paper, we will use the
q
-commutator of two elements:

[A,B]_{q}=qAB-q^{-1}BA,

and the following notations:

q_{p}:=(-1)^{p}q^{p(p+1)}\quad\text{and}\quad\chi_{p}:=q^{2p+1}+q^{-2p-1}.

(2.1)

2.1
Preliminaries on Askey–Wilson algebras

We shall consider a specialization of the Askey–Wilson algebra
\mathfrak{\textbf{aw}}(3)
(see
[
6
]
for a review).
It is generated by three generators
A
,
B
and
C
and demanding that the following three elements

\frac{1}{q^{2}-q^{-2}}[A,B]_{q}+C,\qquad\frac{1}{q^{2}-q^{-2}}[C,A]_{q}+B,\qquad\frac{1}{q^{2}-q^{-2}}[B,C]_{q}+A,

(2.2)

are equal to the same central element written as follows for later convenience

\frac{\chi_{s}(\kappa+\chi_{s})}{q+{q^{-1}}}.

In this specialisation of
\mathfrak{\textbf{aw}}(3)
, the central element (computed in
[
40
]
) reads:

\Omega=\chi_{s}(\kappa+\chi_{s})(qA+q^{-1}B+qC)-q^{2}A^{2}-q^{-2}B^{2}-q^{2}C^{2}-qABC.

A quotient of
\mathfrak{\textbf{aw}}(3)
is obtained by fixing the values of this central element
\Omega
:

\Omega=\kappa(\kappa+\chi_{s}^{3})+3\chi_{s}^{2}-(q+q^{-1}).

This quotient, called special Askey–Wilson algebra, is important since it allows to provide a precise connection with the centralizers of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
representations (for more details, see
e.g.

[
6
]
).

2.2
A quotient of the braid group algebra

We define a quotient of the braid group algebra on three strands by imposing a characteristic equation of order
2s+1
for the braid generators.

The algebra
B^{s}_{3}(q)
is generated by
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
subject to the following defining relations:

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\prod_{p=0}^{2s}(\sigma_{i}-q_{p})=0,\quad\text{for }i=1,2.

(2.4)

In
B^{s}_{3}(q)
, we define the idempotents, for
r=0,1,\dots,2s
and
i=1,2
,

\displaystyle e_{i}^{(r)}:=\prod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{p=0}{p\neq r}}^{2s}\frac{\sigma_{i}-q_{p}}{q_{r}-q_{p}}

(2.5)

satisfying

\sum_{r=0}^{2s}e_{i}^{(r)}=1,\quad e_{i}^{(p)}e_{i}^{(r)}=\delta_{pr}e_{i}^{(r)}\quad\text{and}\quad e_{i}^{(r)}\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i}e_{i}^{(r)}=q_{r}e_{i}^{(r)}.

(2.6)

The generators
\sigma_{i}
can be written in terms of these idempotents as

\sigma_{i}=\sum_{r=0}^{2s}q_{r}e_{i}^{(r)}.

(2.7)

2.3
The Askey–Wilson braid algebra

We are ready to define an algebra combining the algebra
B^{s}_{3}(q)
and the Askey–Wilson relations. We define in
B^{s}_{3}(q)
the elements

g_{i}:=\sum_{r=0}^{2s}\chi_{r}e_{i}^{(r)},\quad\text{for}\ i=1,2,

(2.8)

which allows to give the following definition:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}/2
.
The Askey–Wilson braid algebra (on three strands)
AW\!B_{3}^{s}(q)
is the quotient of the algebra
B^{s}_{3}(q)
obtained by asking that
the triple
(g_{1},g_{2},\sigma_{1}g_{2}\sigma_{1}^{-1})
satisfies the relations (
2.2
) of the Askey–Wilson algebra, i.e. the three elements

\frac{[g_{1},g_{2}]_{q}}{q^{2}-q^{-2}}+\sigma_{1}g_{2}\sigma_{1}^{-1},\qquad\frac{[g_{2},\sigma_{1}g_{2}\sigma_{1}^{-1}]_{q}}{q^{2}-q^{-2}}+g_{1},\qquad\frac{[\sigma_{1}g_{2}\sigma_{1}^{-1},g_{1}]_{q}}{q^{2}-q^{-2}}+g_{2},

(2.9)

are equal to the same central element which will still be denoted by

\frac{\chi_{s}(\kappa+\chi_{s})}{q+{q^{-1}}}.

Since the relations of the Askey–Wilson algebra are satisfied in
AW\!B_{3}^{s}(q)
, the element

\Omega:=\chi_{s}(\kappa+\chi_{s})(qg_{1}+q^{-1}g_{2}+q\sigma_{1}g_{2}\sigma_{1}^{-1})-q^{2}g_{1}^{2}-q^{-2}g_{2}^{2}-q^{2}\sigma_{1}g_{2}^{2}\sigma_{1}^{-1}-qg_{1}g_{2}\sigma_{1}g_{2}\sigma_{1}^{-1}

(2.10)

is also central in
AW\!B_{3}^{s}(q)
.
The main motivation for the previous definition is the connection with the centralizer of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
given in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

The quotient
\mathcal{A}_{s}
of
AW\!B_{3}^{s}(q)
by relations

\displaystyle\Omega=\kappa(\kappa+\chi_{s}^{3})+3\chi_{s}^{2}-\chi_{0}^{2},

(2.11)

\displaystyle e_{1}^{(0)}g_{2}e_{1}^{(0)}=\frac{\chi_{s}^{2}}{\chi_{0}}e_{1}^{(0)},

(2.12)

is isomorphic to the centralizer of the diagonal action of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
in three copies of its spin
s
irreducible representation.

The proof of this theorem is given in Sections
3
and
4
.
One recognizes with (
2.11
) the known relation defining the special Askey–Wilson algebra, so it is not surprising to find it here. The quotient of
AW\!B_{3}^{s}(q)
only by (
2.11
)
could be called the special Askey–Wilson braid algebra.

Remark 2.3
.

It is an open question whether the Askey–Wilson braid algebra
AW\!B_{3}^{s}(q)
is finite-dimensional. What one can show is that, adapting slightly the proofs in Section
4
, the algebra becomes finite-dimensional after adding the relation (
2.11
). Note that we need the final relation (
2.12
) to prove the isomorphism with the centralizer but we conjecture that it is actually implied by the other relations. We were not able to prove this, but it seems to be true for low values of the spin
s
(
s=1/2,1,3/2,2
), see Remark
4.6
for additional comments on this.

Now a remarkable simplification occurs.
Indeed it is easy to check that the three elements appearing in (
2.9
) are conjugated by
\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}
in
B^{s}_{3}(q)
using

[\sigma_{i},g_{i}]=0,\ \qquad\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}g_{1}=g_{2}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2},\ \qquad\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}g_{2}=g_{1}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}.

(2.13)

Therefore, the statement that the three elements (
2.9
) are central can be replaced equivalently by demanding that the element

\kappa:=\frac{1}{(q-{q^{-1}})\chi_{s}}[g_{1},g_{2}]_{q}+\frac{q+{q^{-1}}}{\chi_{s}}\sigma_{1}g_{2}\sigma_{1}^{-1}-\chi_{s}\ \ \ \in\mathcal{B}^{s}_{3}(q)

(2.14)

is a central element. This allows to give an equivalent presentation of
AW\!B_{3}^{s}(q)
but also of its quotient
\mathcal{A}_{s}
as summarized in the following corollary:

Corollary 2.4
.

The algebra
\mathcal{A}_{s}
is isomorphic to the quotient of
\mathcal{B}^{s}_{3}(q)
by

\displaystyle\kappa\text{ is central,}

(2.15)

\displaystyle\Omega=\kappa(\kappa+\chi_{s}^{3})+3\chi_{s}^{2}-\chi_{0}^{2},

(2.16)

\displaystyle e_{1}^{(0)}g_{2}e_{1}^{(0)}=\frac{\chi_{s}^{2}}{\chi_{0}}e_{1}^{(0)}.

(2.17)

Let us remark that the algebra
AW\!B_{3}^{s}(q)
is also generated by
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
(instead of
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
). Indeed, these elements satisfy

e_{i}^{(r)}g_{i}=g_{i}e_{i}^{(r)}=\chi_{r}e_{i}^{(r)}

(2.18)

and the characteristic equation

\prod_{p=0}^{2s}(g_{i}-\chi_{p})=0.

(2.19)

As a consequence, the idempotents
e_{i}^{(r)}
can be expressed in terms of
g_{i}
as follows

e_{i}^{(r)}=\prod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{p=0}{p\neq r}}^{2s}\frac{g_{i}-\chi_{p}}{\chi_{r}-\chi_{p}},

(2.20)

which shows that
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
generate
AW\!B_{3}^{s}(q)
. Therefore,
AW\!B_{3}^{s}(q)
could equivalently be defined as a quotient of the Askey–Wilson algebra by the characteristic polynomial (
2.19
)
and the braid relation (
2.3
).

3
Centralizer of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})

In this section, we give an explicit surjective homomorphism from the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{s}
to the centralizer of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
defined more precisely below. In other words, we show that some elements of the centralizer of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
satisfy the relations of the Askey–Wilson braid algebra
AW\!B_{3}^{s}(q)
but also of its quotient
\mathcal{A}_{s}
. We start by recalling well-known results about
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
.

3.1
Tensor products of representations of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})

The quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
is a quasitriangular Hopf algebra that can be viewed as a
q
-deformation of the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
. Its definition by generators and relations can be found in
[
14
]
and will not be reproduced here. Let us recall that the deformation parameter
q
is taken to be generic in this paper.

For each non-negative integer or half-integer
s
referred to as the spin, the algebra
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
has a finite irreducible representation
V_{s}
of dimension
2s+1
. The tensor product of two such representations decomposes as follows

V_{s}^{\otimes 2}\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{2s}V_{j}.

(3.1)

Similarly, the tensor product of three copies of
V_{s}
decomposes as

V_{s}^{\otimes 3}\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{2s}V_{j}\otimes V_{s}\cong\bigoplus_{j=j_{\text{min}}}^{3s}V_{j}^{\oplus d_{j}},

(3.2)

where

j_{\text{min}}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if $s$ is integer},\\
\frac{1}{2},&\text{if $s$ is half-integer},\end{cases}

(3.3)

and the degeneracy
d_{j}
of
V_{j}
is given explicitly by

d_{j}=\min(2s,s+j)-|s-j|+1=\begin{cases}2j+1,&\text{if }j_{\min}\leq j\leq s,\\
3s-j+1,&\text{if }s<j\leq 3s.\\
\end{cases}

(3.4)

3.2
Centralizer

If
X\in U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
, we will denote by
X_{|_{V_{s}^{\otimes 3}}}
its representation in
\text{End}(V_{s}^{\otimes 3})
. The algebra of interest is the centralizer of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
in
\text{End}(V_{s}^{\otimes 3})
defined as

Z_{s}:=\text{End}_{U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})}(V_{s}^{\otimes 3})=\{M\in\text{End}(V_{s}^{\otimes 3})\ |\ MX_{|_{V_{s}^{\otimes 3}}}=X_{|_{V_{s}^{\otimes 3}}}M\quad\forall X\in U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})\}.

(3.5)

Because of the first isomorphism in (
3.2
), there is a vector space isomorphism

Z_{s}\cong\bigoplus_{a,b=0}^{2s}\text{Hom}_{U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})}\bigl(V_{a}\otimes V_{s}\,,\,V_{b}\otimes V_{s}\bigr),

(3.6)

where

\displaystyle\text{Hom}_{U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})}\bigl(V_{a}\otimes V_{s}\,,\,V_{b}\otimes V_{s}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\{M\in\text{Hom}\bigl(V_{a}\otimes V_{s}\,,\,V_{b}\otimes V_{s}\bigr)\ |\ MX_{|_{V_{a}\otimes V_{s}}}=X_{|_{V_{b}\otimes V_{s}}}M\quad\forall X\in U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})\}.

(3.7)

An explicit formula for the dimension of the centralizer
Z_{s}
for any choice of spin
s
can be obtained using the degeneracies
d_{j}
given in (
3.4
):

\dim(Z_{s})=\sum_{j=j_{\text{min}}}^{3s}d_{j}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}(2s+1)((2s+1)^{2}+1).

(3.8)

Another way to compute the dimension of the centralizer is to use the vector space direct sum decomposition (
3.7
). The dimension of the summand corresponding to
a,b
in this direct sum is

\min(a+b,2s)-|a-b|+1=\begin{cases}2\min(a,b)+1,&\text{if $a+b\leq 2s$},\\
2s-|a-b|+1,&\text{if $a+b>2s$}.\end{cases}

(3.9)

The dimension of the centralizer
Z_{s}
is then the sum of the previous dimensions over
a,b=0,1,\dots,2s
, which coincides with (
3.8
).

3.3
Elements and relations for the centralizer

There is a quadratic Casimir element in
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
that can be normalized (see
[
14
]
) such that it is represented in
\text{End}(V_{s})
by

\chi_{s}\text{Id}_{V_{s}},

(3.10)

where
\chi_{s}
is defined in (
2.1
) and
\text{Id}_{V_{s}}
denotes the identity acting on
V_{s}
. The normalization choice (
3.10
) implies that in the direct sum basis (
3.1
), the Casimir element of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
is represented by the following direct sum matrix in
\text{End}(V_{s}^{\otimes 2})

C=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{2s}\chi_{j}\text{Id}_{V_{j}}.

(3.11)

Since the Casimir element is central in
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
, its representation (
3.11
) belongs to the centralizer of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
in
\text{End}(V_{s}^{\otimes 2})
.

There is another element
\check{R}
in this centralizer that can be constructed using the universal
R
-matrix of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
. The matrix
\check{R}
takes the following form in the decomposed basis (
3.1
)

\check{R}=\alpha_{s}\bigoplus_{j=0}^{2s}q_{j}\text{Id}_{V_{j}},

(3.12)

where
q_{j}
is defined in (
2.1
) and
\alpha_{s}=(-1)^{2s}q^{-2s(s+1)}
is the normalization constant that appears when using the conventions of
[
14
]
(a detailed proof of this result can be found therein). Equations (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) imply that we can define the following orthogonal idempotent matrices for
r=0,1,\dots,2s

\displaystyle E^{(r)}:=\prod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{p=0}{p\neq r}}^{2s}\frac{\alpha_{s}^{-1}\check{R}-q_{p}\text{Id}_{V_{s}^{\otimes 2}}}{q_{r}-q_{p}}=\prod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{p=0}{p\neq r}}^{2s}\frac{C-\chi_{p}\text{Id}_{V_{s}^{\otimes 2}}}{\chi_{r}-\chi_{p}}

(3.13)

which satisfy

E^{(r)}\check{R}=\check{R}E^{(r)}=\alpha_{s}q_{r}E^{(r)},\quad E^{(r)}C=CE^{(r)}=\chi_{r}E^{(r)}.

(3.14)

In other words,
E^{(r)}
is the projector on the irreducible representation space
V_{r}
in the direct sum decomposition (
3.1
). We can write

C=\sum_{r=0}^{2s}\chi_{r}E^{(r)}.

(3.15)

Now we consider the three-fold tensor product
V_{s}^{\otimes 3}
. We denote
C_{12}:=C\otimes\text{Id}_{V_{s}}
and
C_{23}:=\text{Id}_{V_{s}}\otimes C
. The matrices
\check{R}_{12},\check{R}_{23}
and
E^{(r)}_{12},E^{(r)}_{23}
are understood
similarly using (
3.12
) and (
3.13
) respectively. It is then possible to define

C_{13}:=\check{R}_{12}C_{23}\check{R}_{12}^{-1}.

(3.16)

We also denote by
C_{123}
the matrix representing the action of the Casimir element of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
on
V_{s}^{\otimes 3}
which takes the following form in the fully decomposed basis of (
3.2
):

C_{123}=\bigoplus_{j=j_{\text{min}}}^{3s}\chi_{j}\text{Id}_{V_{j}}^{\oplus d_{j}}.

(3.17)

The triple of matrices
C_{12},C_{23},C_{13}
are representations of the so-called intermediate Casimir elements of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})^{\otimes 3}
in
\text{End}(V_{s}^{\otimes 3})
.
With the normalization (
3.10
), they satisfy the relations of the special Askey–Wilson algebra as defined in Section
2
, with the total Casimir element
C_{123}

corresponding to the central element
\kappa

[
40
,
9
]
. For example, one gets

\frac{[C_{12},C_{23}]_{q}}{q^{2}-q^{-2}}+\check{R}_{12}C_{23}\check{R}_{12}^{-1}=\frac{\chi_{s}}{\chi_{0}}(C_{123}+\chi_{s}\text{Id}_{V_{s}^{\otimes 3}}).

(3.18)

The matrices
C_{12},C_{23},C_{13},C_{123}
all belong to the centralizer
Z_{s}
,
as can be deduced from the discussions above
(for the element
C_{13}
, this fact is a direct consequence of the results of
[
9
]
). In
[
13
]
, it has been proven
that they generate the whole centralizer
Z_{s}
.
It is also apparent that the matrices
\check{R}_{12}
and
\check{R}_{23}
belong to
Z_{s}
and they generate the centralizer
Z_{s}

[
29
]
.
They are called braided
R
-matrices because they satisfy the braided Yang–Baxter equation

\check{R}_{12}\check{R}_{23}\check{R}_{12}=\check{R}_{23}\check{R}_{12}\check{R}_{23}.

(3.19)

The total Casimir element
C_{123}
has the property

E_{12}^{(0)}C_{123}E_{12}^{(0)}=C_{123}E_{12}^{(0)}=\chi_{s}E_{12}^{(0)}.

(3.20)

Indeed, recall that
E_{12}^{(0)}
is the projector on the space
V_{0}\otimes V_{s}
of the first decomposition in (
3.2
).
The matrix
E_{12}^{(0)}C_{123}E_{12}^{(0)}
hence acts as zero on all the summands of this decomposition except on
V_{0}\otimes V_{s}\cong V_{s}

where it acts as the constant
\chi_{s}
. This leads directly to equation (
3.20
).
Multiplying (
3.18

Proposition 3.1

The mapping given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}\mapsto\alpha_{s}^{-1}\check{R}_{12},\qquad\sigma_{2}\mapsto\alpha_{s}^{-1}\check{R}_{23},

to the centralizer

\displaystyle\ g_{1}\mapsto C_{12},\qquad g_{2}\mapsto C_{23},\qquad\sigma_{1}g_{2}\sigma_{1}^{-1}\mapsto C_{13},\qquad\kappa\mapsto C_{123}.

4
Isomorphism with the centralizer

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
2.2
which is the main result of the paper.
Since, we have just proven that there exists a surjective homomorphism from
\mathcal{A}_{s}
to
Z_{s}
, it remains to show that
\dim(\mathcal{A}_{s})\leq\dim(Z_{s})
to prove that this homomorphism is an isomorphism.
Our strategy will be to provide a generating family for
\mathcal{A}_{s}
with cardinality equal to the dimension of
Z_{s}
. This generating family will hence be a basis for the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{s}
.

4.1
Paths

We start by introducing a formalism that will be useful in the following.

Let
N
be a non-negative integer. A path
\gamma
of length
N
is a sequence of integers
(\gamma_{i})_{i=0}^{N}
with
\gamma_{i+1}\in\{\gamma_{i}-1,\gamma_{i},\gamma_{i}+1\}
for all
i=0,\dots,N-1
, and
0\leq\gamma_{i}\leq 2s
. The set of all possible paths
\gamma
going from
a
to
b
with length
N
will be denoted

\Gamma^{(a,b)}_{N}:=\{\gamma=(\gamma_{i})_{i=0}^{N}\ |\ \gamma_{0}=a,\gamma_{N}=b\}.

(4.1)

Note that
\Gamma^{(a,b)}_{N}
is empty if
N<|a-b|
. The index
N
will be dropped when referring to paths of all possible lengths,
i.e.

\Gamma^{(a,b)}:=\bigcup_{N\geq|a-b|}\Gamma^{(a,b)}_{N}.

(4.2)

We will illustrate a path
\gamma=(\gamma_{i})_{i=0}^{N}\in\Gamma^{(a,b)}_{N}
by a diagram of
N+1
connected dots, with the horizontal direction representing the values of the indices
i
and the vertical direction representing the values
\gamma_{i}
. The boundary values
a
and
b
will be explicitly indicated at the beginning and at the end of the path. An example is given in Figure
1
.

0
2

Figure 1
:
Diagrammatic representation of the path
\gamma=(0,1,2,1,1,2)\in\Gamma^{(0,2)}_{5}
.

Note that there is only one possible path of length
N=|a-b|
going from
a
to
b
: it is the strictly increasing path if
a<b
, the strictly decreasing path if
a>b
, or the zero length path if
a=b
. We will specifically denote it by
\overline{\gamma}(a,b)
. In the case where
a=b
, the path has zero length and it will be illustrated by a labeled dot
a
.

To each path
\gamma
, we associate the following element of
AW\!B_{3}^{s}(q)
(or of
\mathcal{A}_{s}
)

x_{\gamma}=e_{1}^{(\gamma_{0})}g_{2}e_{1}^{(\gamma_{1})}g_{2}e_{1}^{(\gamma_{2})}\dots e_{1}^{(\gamma_{N-1})}g_{2}e_{1}^{(\gamma_{N})}.

(4.3)

If
\gamma=(\gamma_{i})_{i=0}^{M}\in\Gamma_{M}^{(a,b)}
and
\gamma^{\prime}=(\gamma^{\prime}_{i})_{i=0}^{N}\in\Gamma_{N}^{(b,c)}
are two paths, then their horizontal concatenation is defined by

\gamma\cdot\gamma^{\prime}=(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{M-1},\gamma^{\prime}_{0},\gamma^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\gamma^{\prime}_{N})\in\Gamma_{M+N}^{(a,c)}.

(4.4)

In this case, we can write
x_{\gamma}x_{\gamma^{\prime}}=x_{\gamma\cdot\gamma^{\prime}}
.
If
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
are instead two paths with
\gamma_{M}\neq\gamma^{\prime}_{0}
, then the orthogonality property of the idempotents
e_{1}^{(r)}
implies that
x_{\gamma}x_{\gamma^{\prime}}=0
.

4.2
A basis for
\mathcal{A}_{s}

From the PBW basis of the Askey–Wilson algebra (see
e.g.

[
6
]
), it is seen that a generating family for
\mathcal{A}_{s}
is given by

\{g_{1}^{a}g_{2}^{b}(\sigma_{2}^{-1}g_{1}\sigma_{2})^{c}\kappa^{p}\ |\ a,b,c,p\in\mathbb{N}\}.

(4.5)

Due to the characteristic polynomials (
2.19
) for
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
, this generating family can be reduced to

\{g_{1}^{a}g_{2}^{b}\sigma_{2}^{-1}g_{1}^{c}\sigma_{2}\kappa^{p}\ |\ 0\leq a,b,c\leq 2s,\ p\in\mathbb{N}\}.

(4.6)

The elements
g_{1}^{a}
,
g_{2}^{b}\sigma_{2}^{-1}
and
g_{1}^{c}
can be written respectively in terms of linear combinations of
e_{1}^{(a)}
,
g_{2}^{b}
and
e_{1}^{(c)}
.
Using moreover the fact that
\sigma_{2}
is invertible and
\kappa
central, one deduces that the set

\{e_{1}^{(a)}g_{2}^{b}e_{1}^{(c)}\kappa^{p}\ |\ 0\leq a,b,c\leq 2s,\ p\in\mathbb{N}\}

(4.7)

is also a generating family. To reduce this set further, we need some additional properties of the quotient
\mathcal{A}_{s}
of the Askey–Wilson braid algebra.

Proposition 4.1
.

For any integers
0\leq a,c\leq 2s
and
b\geq 0
,

e_{1}^{(a)}g_{2}^{b}e_{1}^{(c)}=0\quad\text{if }|a-c|>b.

(4.8)

Proof.

The case
b=0
is obvious from the second relation in (
2.6
). To obtain the case
b=1
,
multiply the first Askey–Wilson relation in (
2.9
) by
e_{1}^{(a)}
on the left and by
e_{1}^{(c)}
on the right with
a\neq c
. Using the properties of the idempotents and the fact that
\kappa
is central, one finds

\left(\frac{q\chi_{a}-{q^{-1}}\chi_{c}}{q^{2}-q^{-2}}+q_{a}q_{c}^{-1}\right)e_{1}^{(a)}g_{2}e_{1}^{(c)}=0.

(4.9)

For
q
generic,
the multiplicative factor on the LHS of (
4.9
) does not vanish for
|a-c|>1
, which implies relation (
4.8
) with
b=1
. The other cases are then proved by induction on
b
. Indeed, suppose that (
4.8
) holds for some integer
0\leq b\leq 2s
. One can write

e_{1}^{(a)}g_{2}^{b+1}e_{1}^{(c)}=\sum_{r=0}^{2s}e_{1}^{(a)}g_{2}e_{1}^{(r)}g_{2}^{b}e_{1}^{(c)}=e_{1}^{(a)}g_{2}e_{1}^{(a-1)}g_{2}^{b}e_{1}^{(c)}+e_{1}^{(a)}g_{2}e_{1}^{(a)}g_{2}^{b}e_{1}^{(c)}+e_{1}^{(a)}g_{2}e_{1}^{(a+1)}g_{2}^{b}e_{1}^{(c)}.

(4.10)

By hypothesis, each term on the RHS of the previous equation is seen to be zero if
|a-c|>b+1
, which concludes the proof.
∎

5
Perspectives

A presentation in terms of generators and relations of the centralizer of the diagonal action of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
in three copies of its spin
s
irreducible representation was given in this paper.
It has been achieved by combining two well-known algebras: the braid group algebra and the Askey–Wilson one.
We focused on the case with three strands, since it is the first non-trivial case. This gives a good starting point for the general case of
n
-fold tensor products. We know that new relations will be needed for higher
n
(see for example
[
8
,
30
]
) to obtain the centralizer. Nevertheless, one can take our relations and define an algebra on
n
strands by plugging them on all triples of adjacent strands. This algebra will be larger than the centralizer, but one can still ask whether it is finite-dimensional and study its representation theory for example.
What seems natural for finding the new relations for general
n
is to bring in the higher rank Askey–Wilson algebra introduced in
[
36
,
16
,
17
]
. In these higher rank cases, the definition of the special quotient of the Askey–Wilson algebra
has not been given yet and deserves more investigation (see
[
5
]
though for
n=4
).

One may also wonder if results similar to those proven in this paper hold when
q
is a root of unity. It is well-known that the representation theory of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
is more complicated in this case and hence the study of the centralizer should be more involved. For instance, it is known that for
q
a root of unity there are some degeneracies in the characteristic
polynomial equation (
2.4
) for
\sigma_{i}
which makes the definition of the idempotents more complicated.
The limits
q\to\pm 1
are also of interest. Indeed, the case
q\to 1
would correspond to replacing the Askey–Wilson algebra by the Racah algebra.
In
[
11
]
, a quotient of the Racah algebra has been conjectured to be isomorphic to the centralizer of the diagonal action of
U(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
in any three irreducible representations.
As for
q\to-1
, a similar result has been obtained with the Bannai–Ito algebra and the centralizer of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
in
[
12
,
7
]
. The use of the permutation group may simplify the presentations given in these previous papers. We hope to return to some of these questions in the near future.

A classification of the division algebras that are isotopic to a cyclic Galois field extension
S. Pumplün

Abstract
. We classify all division algebras that are principal Albert isotopes of a cyclic Galois field extension of degree n > 2 n>2 up to isomorphisms. We achieve a “tight” classification when the cyclic Galois field extension is cubic. The classification is “tight” in the sense that the list of algebras has features that make it easy to distinguish non-isomorphic ones.

Primary: 17A35; Secondary: 17A60, 17A99

Introduction

Classifications of classes of nonassociative division algebras of dimension
n
over some base field
F
heavily depend on the choice of
F
and on
n
, take
n=3
as example: if
F
is an algebraically closed or a real closed field, then
there are no three-dimensional nonassociative division algebras over
F
.
Over finite fields, however, three-dimensional nonassociative division algebras exist.

The first examples of three-dimensional division algebras are due to Dickson who constructed three-dimensional unital commutative nonassociative algebras already in 1905
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
. Kaplansky then developed their structure theory in a more modern language
[
10
,
11
]
. Over finite fields of characteristic not two, every three-dimensional commutative division algebra is a field or one of these “Dickson” algebras; all are isotopic to generalized twisted fields

[
17
,
18
]
.

In this paper, we will investigate the
n
-dimensional algebras over a field
F
that are principal Albert isotopes of cyclic field extensions,
with a special emphasis on
n=3
.

Let
V
be an
n
-dimensional
F
-vector space and
{\rm Alg}(V)
be the set of non-unital algebra structures on
V
.
If
A\in{\rm Alg}(V)
is
regular
(i.e., the left and right multiplication by some element in
A
are invertible) then the orbit of
A
under

G_{0}(V)={\rm Gl}(V)\times{\rm Gl}(V)
contains a unital algebra. If this algebra is associative or an octonion algebra, then it is unique up to isomorphism (Theorem
1.4
). We call it the
unital heart
of
A
. As a first step we obtain
a rough “classification” of all those regular algebras which have different associative unital hearts, and which have different octonion algebras as their unital heart (Theorem
1.4
). We proceed to give necessary and sufficient criteria for two algebras to be isomorphic, which have the same commutative associative unital heart (Theorem
1.5
).

In this paper we focus on algebras whose unital heart is a cyclic Galois field extension
K/F
of dimension
n
. Nonassociative
n
-dimensional division algebras isotopic to cyclic field extensions are
understood up to isomorphism over those base fields, where we have a full understanding of the cyclic field extensions of degree
n
: Given a fixed cyclic field extension
K
of degree
n
over
F
, we can list all the types of division algebras over the field
F
whose unital heart is
K
, but only achieve a full classification for some subtypes (Theorem
5.2
). The situation improves slightly when
n
is prime (Theorem
5.3
).

The Classification Theorem for algebras with a cubic field extension as unital heart is “tight” in the sense of Petersson for several cases (Theorem
4.2
). We then give necessary and sufficient criteria
for any two algebras of dimension three with the same unital heart to be isomorphic.

The content of the paper is as follows:
After collecting the basic terminology (Section
1
), we describe the bijective linear maps for Galois
C_{n}
-algebras in Section
2
. We then consider regular algebras which have a Galois
C_{n}
-algebra as their unital heart (Section
3
), and
achieve a classification of the three-dimensional division algebras which have a cubic Galois field extension as unital heart in Section

With the same methology, we then investigate the algebras whose unital heart is a cyclic Galois field extension of degree
n
in Section
5
. In this generality, we are still able to list non-isomorphic classes of algebras, but are not able to parametrize the algebras to the extent we were able to in several subcases for the
n=3

There are a range of papers that attempt to classify certain subfamilies of nonassociative algebras of small dimension up to isomorphism, often over
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{R}

we have avoided them in our classification, as we believe the structure of the algebras becomes more visible this way.

Petersson

In this paper, we successfully generalized Petersson’s methodology to certain classes of

Regular

Singular (i.e., not regular) algebras can be partly investigated by a direct approach following

1.
Singular and regular algebras and the unital heart

1.1.
Isotopes and Kaplansky’s trick

Let

The monoid

We know that

An algebra

If

is right regular. It also follows easily that, if

for all
(f,g)\in G_{0}(V)
.

Let

be a field. From now on, let

Note that every division algebra over a field

The following extends

Lemma 1.1
.

For

Division algebras are principal Albert isotopes of unital division algebras, more generally so are regular algebras

(Kaplansky’s Trick) Let

Lemma 1.3
.

Let

(ii)

This is the generalization of

Let

Let

(ii)

.

(The Unital Heart)
For all

(i)

(ii) There exist a unital algebra

We can now “sort” nonassociative regular algebras over

Remark 1.9

The restriction to unital commutative associative algebras, respectively, to octonion algebras as unital hearts is clearly limiting the scope of the classification we can achieve with our method. Even in small dimensions, and over an algebraically closed field of characteristic not two, 19 of the 59 unital associative algebras of dimension 5 listed up to isomorphism in

Theorem 2.1
.

(cf.
[

for

Let

Then the map

\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}x_{i}t^{i}\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}L(x_{i})\tau^{i};

is an isomorphism of central simple algebras. In particular, we can write

{\rm End}_{F}(V)=L(K)\oplus L(K)\tau\oplus\dots\oplus L(K)\tau^{n-1}

as a direct sum of

\det(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}L(x_{i})\tau^{i})=N_{A_{0}/F}(x_{0}+x_{1}t+\cdots+x_{n-1}t^{n-1}).

Proof.

The map

\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}x_{i}t^{i}\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}L(x_{i})\tau^{i}

is a
homomorphism. This is an immediate consequence of the way multiplication in

A homomorphism between two central simple algebras of the same dimension is an isomorphism of central simple algebras.

The second statement is now

Proposition 2.2

L(w_{1})\tau+\dots+L(w_{n-1})\tau^{n-1}=\sigma(L(x_{1})\tau+\dots+L(x_{n-1})\tau^{n-1})\sigma^{-1}L(v)

=\sigma L(x_{1})\tau\sigma^{-1}L(v)+\sigma L(x_{2})\tau^{2}\sigma^{-1}L(v)+\dots+\sigma L(x_{2})\tau^{n-1}\sigma^{-1}L(v)

=L(\sigma(x_{1})\tau(v))\tau+L(\sigma(x_{2})\tau^{2}(v))\tau^{2}+\dots+L(\sigma(x_{2})\tau^{n-1}(v))\tau^{n-1}.

By Corollary
2.3
, this is equivalent to

w_{i}=\sigma(x_{i})\tau^{i}(v)
for all
i
.

(4)
Here, the critical relations reduce to
u=1
and

1+L(w_{1})\tau+\dots+L(w_{n-1})\tau^{n-1}=\sigma(1+L(x_{1})\tau+\dots+L(x_{n-1})\tau^{n-1})\sigma^{-1}L(v)

=L(v)+\sigma L(x_{1})\tau\sigma^{-1}L(v)+\dots+\sigma L(x_{n-1})\tau^{n-1}\sigma^{-1}L(v)

=L(v)+L(\sigma(x_{1})\tau(v))\tau+\dots+L(\sigma(x_{n-1})\tau^{n-1}(v))\tau^{n-1}.

By Corollary
2.3
, this is equivalent to

L(v)=1
and
w_{i}=\sigma(x_{i})\tau^{i}(v)
for all
i
.
This is equivalent to
v=1
and
w_{i}=\sigma(x_{i})\tau^{i}(v)=\sigma(x_{i})
for all
i
.

(5) Here, the critical relations cannot be simplified.

∎

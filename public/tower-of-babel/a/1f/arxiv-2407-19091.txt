Li-Yorke Chaotic Weighted Composition Operators


Abstract
. We establish complete characterizations of the notion of Li-Yorke chaos for weighted composition operators on C 0 ​ ( X ) C_{0}(X) spaces and on L p ​ ( μ ) L^{p}(\mu) spaces. As a consequence, we obtain simple characterizations of the Li-Yorke chaotic weighted shifts on c 0 c_{0} and on ℓ p \ell^{p} ( 1 ≤ p < ∞ 1\leq p<\infty ) that complement previously known results. We also investigate the notion of Li-Yorke chaos for weighted shifts on Fréchet sequence spaces. As applications, we obtain characterizations of the Li-Yorke chaotic weighted shifts on Köthe sequence spaces depending only on the entries of the Köthe matrix and the weights of the shift.

1.
Introduction

Chaos theory is a very popular branch of the area of dynamical systems.
Among the many notions of chaos studied so far, Li-Yorke chaos has a prominent place, not only because it was the first notion of chaos to appear explicitly in the mathematical literature, but also because it motivated a whole class of interesting notions of chaos based on the behaviors of pairs of points (dense chaos, generic chaos, distributional chaos of types 1, 2 and 3, and so on).
Moreover, it has interesting relationships with other fundamental notions in dynamics, including the notion of topological entropy.

Given a metric space
M
with metric
d
, recall that a continuous map
f:M\to M
is said to be
Li-Yorke chaotic
if there is an uncountable set
S\subset M
(a
scrambled set
for
f
) such that each pair
(x,y)
of distinct points in
S
is a
Li-Yorke pair
for
f
, in the sense that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}d(f^{n}(x),f^{n}(y))=0\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \limsup_{n\to\infty}d(f^{n}(x),f^{n}(y))>0.

On the other hand, the area of linear dynamics has witnessed a great development during the last few decades and many interesting connections to other branches of dynamics, such as topological dynamics, ergodic theory, differentiable dynamics and symbolic dynamics, have been established. Moreover, especially through the study of the dynamics of semigroups of operators, many applications to other areas of science have been found, including applications to biology, engineering and physics. For an overview of the area of linear dynamics, we refer the reader to the books
[
2
,
24
]
and to the more recent papers
[
3
,
8
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
19
,
22
]
,
where many additional references can be found.

In the setting of linear dynamics, an extensive study of Li-Yorke chaos was developed in
[
4
,
7
]
.
In particular, the following useful characterizations were obtained:

For any continuous linear operator
T
on any Fréchet space
X
, the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

T
is Li-Yorke chaotic;

(ii)

T
admits a semi-irregular vector, that is, a vector
x\in X
such that the sequence
(T^{n}x)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

does not converge to zero but has a subsequence converging to zero.

(iii)

T
admits an irregular vector, that is, a vector
x\in X
such that the sequence
(T^{n}x)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

is unbounded but has a subsequence converging to zero.

Composition operators appear naturally in many different contexts (analytic, measure-theoretic, topological, etc)
and so they actually constitute a family of classes of operators.
They play a very important role in operator theory and its applications.
The dynamics of such operators has been investigated by several authors in different settings
(see
[
1
,
5
,
6
,
14
,
15
,
16
,

,

]

In particular, a detailed study of Li-Yorke chaos for composition operators on
L^{p}(\mu)
spaces was developed in
[
9
]
.

In the present note we will investigate the notion of Li-Yorke chaos for weighted composition operators

C_{w,f}(\varphi)\!:=(\varphi\circ f)\cdot w

on
C_{0}(X)
spaces (Section
2
) and on
L^{p}(\mu)
spaces (Section
3
).
Our goal is to obtain complete characterizations of Li-Yorke chaos for these operators (Theorems
1
and
10
).
As applications, we will derive simple characterizations of the Li-Yorke chaotic weighted shifts (unilateral and bilateral) on the
classical Banach sequence spaces
c_{0}
and

that complement previously known results.
In the case of

[

in the unweighted case
and we were strongly motivated by this previous work.

In the final Section

we will study the notion of Li-Yorke chaos for weighted shifts on Fréchet sequence spaces.
As applications, we will obtain characterizations of the Li-Yorke chaotic weighted shifts on Köthe sequence spaces
that are of a computational character, that is, depend only on the entries of the Köthe matrix and the weights of the shift

2.
Weighted composition operators on
C_{0}(X)
spaces

In this section we fix a locally compact Hausdorff space
X
and let
C_{0}(X)
denote the Banach space over
\mathbb{K}
of all continuous maps

\varphi:X\to\mathbb{K}
that vanish at infinity endowed with the supremum norm

\|\varphi\|\!:=\sup_{x\in X}|\varphi(x)|.

More generally, for any subset
B
of
X
, we define

\|\varphi\|_{B}\!:=\sup_{x\in B}|\varphi(x)|,

where we consider this supremum to be
0
whenever
B=\varnothing
.
Given a map
\varphi:X\to\mathbb{K}
, we denote by
\operatorname{supp}\varphi
its
support
, that is,

\operatorname{supp}\varphi\!:=\overline{\{x\in X:\varphi(x)\neq 0\}}.

We also fix a
weight function

w:X\to\mathbb{K}
, that is, a continuous map such that

(1)

\varphi\cdot w\in C_{0}(X)\ \text{ for all }\varphi\in C_{0}(X).

It is not difficult to see that (
1
) holds if and only if
w
is bounded on
X
.
Given a continuous map
f:X\to X
, it is also not difficult to show that the weighted composition operator

C_{w,f}(\varphi)\!:=(\varphi\circ f)\cdot w

is a well-defined bounded linear operator on
C_{0}(X)
if and only if

(2)

f^{-1}(K)\cap X_{\varepsilon}\text{ is compact for every }\varepsilon>0\text{ and }K\subset X\text{ compact},

where
X_{\varepsilon}\!:=\{x\in X:|w(x)|\geq\varepsilon\}
. In particular, the (unweighted) composition operator

C_{f}(\varphi)\!:=\varphi\circ f

is a well-defined bounded linear operator on
C_{0}(X)
if and only if

f^{-1}(K)\text{ is compact for every }K\subset X\text{ compact},

that is,
f
is a proper mapping.
Moreover, if
w\in C_{0}(X)
then
C_{w,f}
is a well-defined bounded linear operator on
C_{0}(X)
for every continuous map
f:X\to X
.

For the remaining of this section, we fix a continuous map
f:X\to X
satisfying condition (
2
).
Hence, the weighted composition operator

C_{w,f}:\varphi\in C_{0}(X)\mapsto(\varphi\circ f)\cdot w\in C_{0}(X)

is a well-defined bounded linear operator. We associate to
w
and
f
the following sequence of continuous maps from
X
into
\mathbb{K}
:

w^{(1)}\!:=w\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ w^{(n)}\!:=(w\circ f^{n-1})\cdot\ldots\cdot(w\circ f)\cdot w\ \text{ for }n\geq 2.

If
w
is the constant function
1
, then so is
w^{(n)}
for all
n\geq 1
.

We now establish the main result of this section, a characterization of the weighted composition operators on
C_{0}(X)
that are Li-Yorke chaotic.

Theorem 1
.

The weighted composition operator
C_{w,f}
on
C_{0}(X)
is Li-Yorke chaotic if and only if there is a sequence
(B_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of relatively compact open subsets of
X
such that the following conditions hold:

(A)

There is an increasing sequence
(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive integers such that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\|w^{(n_{j})}\|_{f^{-n_{j}}(B_{i})}=0\ \ \text{ for all }i\in\mathbb{N}.

(B)

\displaystyle\sup\big\{\|w^{(n)}\|_{f^{-n}(B_{i})}:i,n\in\mathbb{N}\big\}=\infty
.

Proof.

Suppose that there is a sequence
(B_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with all the properties described in the statement of the theorem.
For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, let
Y_{i}\!:=\{\varphi\in C_{0}(X):\operatorname{supp}\varphi\subset B_{i}\}
, which is a vector subspace of
C_{0}(X)
.
Let
Y
be the closed linear span of
\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}Y_{i}
in
C_{0}(X)
.
By condition (A), for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
and each
\varphi\in Y_{i}
,

\|(C_{w,f})^{n_{j}}(\varphi)\|=\|(\varphi\circ f^{n_{j}})\cdot w^{(n_{j})}\|_{f^{-n_{j}}(B_{i})}\leq\|\varphi\|\|w^{(n_{j})}\|_{f^{-n_{j}}(B_{i})}\to 0\text{ as }j\to\infty.

Hence, the set of all
\varphi\in Y
whose orbit under
C_{w,f}
has a subsequence converging to
0
is dense in
Y
,
and so it is residual in
Y

[
7
, Proposition 3]
.
On the other hand, given
i,n\in\mathbb{N}
with
f^{-n}(B_{i})\neq\emptyset
, choose
a_{i,n}\in f^{-n}(B_{i})
such that

|w^{(n)}(a_{i,n})|\geq\|w^{(n)}\|_{f^{-n}(B_{i})}-1.

By Urysohn’s lemma, there is a continuous map
\phi_{i,n}:X\to[0,1]
such that

\operatorname{supp}\phi_{i,n}\subset B_{i}\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ \phi_{i,n}(f^{n}(a_{i,n}))=1.

Hence,
\phi_{i,n}\in Y_{i}\subset Y
,
\|\phi_{i,n}\|=1
and

\|(C_{w,f})^{n}(\phi_{i,n})\|\geq|\phi_{i,n}(f^{n}(a_{i,n}))w^{(n)}(a_{i,n})|\geq\|w^{(n)}\|_{f^{-n}(B_{i})}-1.

Thus, condition (B) implies that the sequence
(\|(C_{w,f})^{n}|_{Y}\|)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is unbounded, and so the Banach-Steinhaus theorem
[
30
, Theorem 2.5]
guarantees that the set of all
\varphi\in Y
whose orbit under
C_{w,f}
is unbounded is also residual in
Y
.
This shows the existence of an irregular vector for
C_{w,f}
, proving that
C_{w,f}
is Li-Yorke chaotic.

Conversely, suppose that the operator
C_{w,f}
is Li-Yorke chaotic. Then it admits an irregular vector
\psi\in C_{0}(X)
.
For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, let

B_{i}\!:=\{x\in X:|\psi(x)|>i^{-1}\},

which is a relatively compact open subset of
X
.
Since
\psi
is an irregular vector for
C_{w,f}
, there is an increasing sequence
(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive integers such that

\|(C_{w,f})^{n_{j}}(\psi)\|\to 0
as
j\to\infty
. Since, for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
,

\|(C_{w,f})^{n_{j}}(\psi)\|\geq\|(\psi\circ f^{n_{j}})\cdot w^{(n_{j})}\|_{f^{-n_{j}}(B_{i})}\geq i^{-1}\|w^{(n_{j})}\|_{f^{-n_{j}}(B_{i})},

we obtain condition (A). Now, if

\beta\!:=\sup\big\{\|w^{(n)}\|_{f^{-n}(B_{i})}:i,n\in\mathbb{N}\big\}<\infty,

then

\|(C_{w,f})^{n}(\psi)\|=\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\|(\psi\circ f^{n})\cdot w^{(n)}\|_{f^{-n}(B_{i})}\leq\beta\|\psi\|\ \text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N},

contradicting the fact that
\psi
is an irregular vector for
C_{w,f}
. This proves condition (B).
∎

Corollary 2
.

A composition operator
C_{f}
on
C_{0}(X)
is never Li-Yorke chaotic.

Remark 3
.

Note that the sequence
(B_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
constructed in the proof of the necessity of the conditions in Theorem
1

has the following additional property:

(C)

\overline{B_{i}}\subset B_{i+1}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

If we include this additional condition in the statement of Theorem
1
, then we can replace condition (A) by the following weaker condition:

(A’)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|w^{(n)}\|_{f^{-n}(B_{i})}=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

In other words:
The weighted composition operator
C_{w,f}
on
C_{0}(X)
is Li-Yorke chaotic if and only if there is a sequence

(B_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of relatively compact open subsets of
X
satisfying conditions (A’), (B) and (C).

Indeed, since the necessity is clear, let us prove the sufficiency. If

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|w^{(n)}\|_{f^{-n}(B_{j})}>0\ \ \text{ for a certain }j\in\mathbb{N},

take a continuous map
\psi:X\to[0,1]
with
\operatorname{supp}\psi\subset B_{j+1}
and
\psi=1\text{ on }\overline{B_{j}}
. Since

\|w^{(n)}\|_{f^{-n}(B_{j})}\leq\|(C_{w,f})^{n}(\psi)\|\leq\|w^{(n)}\|_{f^{-n}(B_{j+1})}\ \ \text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N},

we see that
\psi
is a semi-irregular vector for
C_{w,f}
, and so
C_{w,f}
is Li-Yorke chaotic. Assume

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|w^{(n)}\|_{f^{-n}(B_{i})}=0\ \ \text{ for all }i\in\mathbb{N}.

Then condition (A) holds with
(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\!:=(n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Since we are assuming that condition (B) holds,
C_{w,f}
is Li-Yorke chaotic by Theorem
1
.

Corollary 4
.

A unilateral weighted backward shift

B_{w}:(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\ldots)\in c_{0}(\mathbb{N})\mapsto(w_{1}x_{2},w_{2}x_{3},\ldots)\in c_{0}(\mathbb{N}),

where
w\!:=(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a bounded sequence of scalars, is Li-Yorke chaotic if and only if

(3)

\sup\{|w_{i}\cdots w_{j}|:i,j\in\mathbb{N},i\leq j\}=\infty.

Proof.

Consider
X\!:=\mathbb{N}
endowed with the discrete topology and
f:n\in\mathbb{N}\mapsto n+1\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then
C_{0}(X)=c_{0}(\mathbb{N})
and
C_{w,f}=B_{w}
.
Since, for each
B\subset X
finite,
f^{-n}(B)=\varnothing
for all sufficiently large
n
, we have that condition (A) is superfluous in the present case. If (
3
) fails, that is,
\beta\!:=\sup\{|w_{i}\cdots w_{j}|:i,j\in\mathbb{N},i\leq j\}<\infty
, then

\|w^{(n)}\|_{f^{-n}(A)}=\sup_{i\in A,i>n}|w^{(n)}(i-n)|=\sup_{i\in A,i>n}|w_{i-n}\cdots w_{i-1}|\leq\beta

for all
A\subset\mathbb{N}
, and so condition (B) fails no matter how we choose the sequence
(B_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.
On the other hand, if (
3
) is true, then condition (B) holds with
B_{i}\!:=\{i\}
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

The above corollary was established in
[
4
, Proposition 27]
in the case
w
is a bounded sequence of nonzero scalars.

Corollary 5
.

A bilateral weighted backward shift

B_{w}:(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in c_{0}(\mathbb{Z})\mapsto(w_{n}x_{n+1})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in c_{0}(\mathbb{Z}),

where
w\!:=(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a bounded sequence of nonzero scalars, is Li-Yorke chaotic if and only if the following conditions hold:

(a)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}|w_{-n}\cdots w_{-1}|=0
.

(b)

\sup\{|w_{i}\cdots w_{j}|:i,j\in\mathbb{Z},i\leq j\}=\infty
.

Proof.

Consider
X\!:=\mathbb{Z}
endowed with the discrete topology and
f:n\in\mathbb{Z}\mapsto n+1\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then
C_{0}(X)=c_{0}(\mathbb{Z})
and
C_{w,f}=B_{w}
. Suppose that (a) and (b) hold. If

\limsup_{n\to\infty}|w_{-n}\cdots w_{-1}|>0,

then
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{\{0\}}
is a semi-irregular vector for
C_{w,f}
, and so
C_{w,f}
is Li-Yorke chaotic. If

\lim_{n\to\infty}|w_{-n}\cdots w_{-1}|=0,

then conditions (A) and (B) hold with
(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\!:=(n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(B_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
an enumeration of the sets
\{k\}

for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, and so
C_{w,f}
is Li-Yorke chaotic by Theorem
1
.
Conversely, suppose that
C_{w,f}
is Li-Yorke chaotic and let
(B_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be as in the statement of Theorem
1
. By choosing
k\in B_{i}
for some
i\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that

|w_{-n_{j}+k}\cdots w_{-1+k}|=\|w^{(n_{j})}\|_{f^{-n_{j}}(\{k\})}\leq\|w^{(n_{j})}\|_{f^{-n_{j}}(B_{i})}\to 0\text{ as }j\to\infty,

which gives (a). If (b) is false, that is,
\beta\!:=\sup\{|w_{i}\cdots w_{j}|:i,j\in\mathbb{Z},i\leq j\}<\infty
, then

\|w^{(n)}\|_{f^{-n}(B_{i})}=\sup_{k\in B_{i}}|w_{-n+k}\cdots w_{-1+k}|\leq\beta\ \ \text{ for all }i\in\mathbb{N},

contradicting condition (B).
∎

To the best of the authors knowledge, a characterization of the Li-Yorke chaotic bilateral weighted shifts on
c_{0}(\mathbb{Z})
has not been given before.

Remark 6
.

We can remove the hypothesis that the weights
w_{n}
are
nonzero
in Corollary
5
provided we state the result as follows:

Consider a bilateral weighted backward shift

B_{w}:(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in c_{0}(\mathbb{Z})\mapsto(w_{n}x_{n+1})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in c_{0}(\mathbb{Z}),

where
w\!:=(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a bounded sequence of scalars. Let

\beta^{-}\!:=\sup\{|w_{i}\cdots w_{j}|:i\leq j\leq-1\}\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \beta^{+}\!:=\sup\{|w_{i}\cdots w_{j}|:0\leq i\leq j\}.

Then
B_{w}
is Li-Yorke chaotic if and only if one of the following conditions hold:

(I)

\beta^{+}=\infty
and
\,\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}|w_{-n}\cdots w_{-k}|=0
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(II)

\beta^{+}<\infty
,
\beta^{-}=\infty
and
\,\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}|w_{-n}\cdots w_{-k}|=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Indeed, let
X
and
f
be as in the proof of Corollary
5
. Suppose that
C_{w,f}
is Li-Yorke chaotic and let

(B_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be as in the statement of Theorem
1
. Let

\beta\!:=\sup\{|w_{i}\cdots w_{j}|:i,j\in\mathbb{Z},i\leq j\}\ \ \text{ and }\ \ B\!:=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}B_{i}.

We claim that

(4)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}|w_{-n}\cdots w_{-1+\ell}|=0\ \ \text{ for all }\ell\in B.

Indeed, take
\ell\in B_{i}
for some
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Since

|w_{-n_{j}+\ell}\cdots w_{-1+\ell}|=\|w^{(n_{j})}\|_{f^{-n_{j}}(\{\ell\})}\leq\|w^{(n_{j})}\|_{f^{-n_{j}}(B_{i})}\to 0\ \text{ as }j\to\infty,

we obtain (
4
). Moreover, we must have
\beta=\infty
. In fact, if
\beta<\infty
then

\|w^{(n)}\|_{f^{-n}(B_{i})}=\sup_{k\in B_{i}}|w_{-n+k}\cdots w_{-1+k}|\leq\beta\ \ \text{ for all }i\in\mathbb{N},

contradicting condition (B). If
\beta^{+}=\infty
, then (
4
) implies that condition (I) holds.
Assume
\beta^{+}<\infty
. Since
\beta\leq\max\{\beta^{-},\beta^{+},\beta^{-}\!\!\cdot\!\beta^{+}\}
, we must have
\beta^{-}=\infty
.
If
B
is unbounded below, then (
4
) implies that (II) holds. Assume
B
bounded below and let
m\!:=\min B
.
We claim that

(5)

w_{n}\neq 0\ \ \text{ for all }n<m.

Indeed, assume
w_{t}=0
for a certain
t<m
and let
C\!:=\sup\{|w_{i}\cdots w_{j}|:t<i\leq j\}
.
Since
\beta^{+}<\infty
, we have that
C<\infty
. Since
t<m=\min B
and
w_{t}=0
,

\|w^{(n)}\|_{f^{-n}(B_{i})}=\sup_{k\in B_{i}}|w_{-n+k}\cdots w_{-1+k}|\leq C\ \text{ for all }i\in\mathbb{N}.

This contradiction proves our claim. By (
4
),
\liminf_{n\to\infty}|w_{-n}\cdots w_{-1+m}|=0
.
Hence, (
5
) implies that (II) holds.

We now prove the converse. We can replace the “
\liminf
” in (I) and (II) by “
\lim
”, since

\liminf_{n\to\infty}|w_{-n}\cdots w_{-k}|=0\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \limsup_{n\to\infty}|w_{-n}\cdots w_{-k}|>0

imply that
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{\{-k+1\}}
is a semi-irregular vector for
C_{w,f}
, and so
C_{w,f}
is Li-Yorke chaotic.

If (I) holds, let
B_{i}\!:=\{-k+i\}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\lim_{n\to\infty}|w_{-n}\cdots w_{-k}|=0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|w^{(n)}\|_{f^{-n}(B_{i})}=\lim_{n\to\infty}|w_{-n-k+i}\cdots w_{-1-k+i}|=0\ \text{ for all }i\in\mathbb{N}.

Moreover, condition (B) holds because
\beta^{+}=\infty
. Thus,
C_{w,f}
is Li-Yorke chaotic.

If (II) holds, let
B_{i}\!:=\{-i\}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\lim_{n\to\infty}|w_{-n}\cdots w_{-k}|=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|w^{(n)}\|_{f^{-n}(B_{i})}=\lim_{n\to\infty}|w_{-n-i}\cdots w_{-1-i}|=0\ \text{ for all }i\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
\beta^{-}=\infty
, condition (B) holds. Thus,
C_{w,f}
is Li-Yorke chaotic.

3.
Weighted composition operators on
L^{p}(\mu)
spaces

In this section we fix
p\in[1,\infty)
and an arbitrary positive measure space
(X,\mathfrak{M},\mu)
.
As usual,
L^{p}(\mu)
denotes the Banach space over
\mathbb{K}
of (equivalence classes of)
\mathbb{K}
-valued
p
-integrable functions on
(X,\mathfrak{M},\mu)
endowed with the
p
-norm

\|\varphi\|_{p}\!:=\left(\int_{X}|\varphi|^{p}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

We also fix a
weight function

w:X\to\mathbb{K}
, which now means a measurable map such that

(6)

\varphi\cdot w\in L^{p}(\mu)\ \text{ for all }\varphi\in L^{p}(\mu).

The next result characterizes the weight functions in the case
\mu
is semifinite.
This fact is probably known, but we present it below for the sake of completeness.

Proposition 7
.

If the measure
\mu
is semifinite, that is, every set with infinite measure contains a set with positive finite measure
(in particular, if
\mu
is
\sigma
-finite), then (
6
) holds if and only if
w\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
.

Proof.

Since the sufficiency of the condition is clear, let us prove its necessity.
For this purpose, suppose that
w\not\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

A_{n}\!:=\{x\in X:n^{2}\leq|w(x)|<(n+1)^{2}\}\in\mathfrak{M}.

Let
I\!:=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\mu(A_{n})>0\}
. Since
w\not\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
,
I
is an infinite set.
For each
n\in I
, let
B_{n}\in\mathfrak{M}
be such that
B_{n}\subset A_{n}
and
0<\mu(B_{n})<\infty
. Define

\varphi\!:=\sum_{n\in I}\Big(\frac{1}{n^{2p}\mu(B_{n})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{B_{n}}.

Note that
\varphi\in L^{p}(\mu)
, since the above series is absolutely convergent:

\sum_{n\in I}\Big\|\Big(\frac{1}{n^{2p}\mu(B_{n})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{B_{n}}\Big\|_{p}=\sum_{n\in I}\Big(\int_{X}\frac{1}{n^{2p}\mu(B_{n})}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{B_{n}}d\mu\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\sum_{n\in I}\frac{1}{n^{2}}<\infty.

However,

\int_{X}|\varphi\cdot w|^{p}d\mu=\sum_{n\in I}\frac{1}{n^{2p}\mu(B_{n})}\int_{B_{n}}|w|^{p}d\mu\geq\sum_{n\in I}\frac{1}{n^{2p}\mu(B_{n})}\cdot n^{2p}\mu(B_{n})=\sum_{n\in I}1=\infty,

proving that
\varphi\cdot w\not\in L^{p}(\mu)
. This shows that (
6
) fails.
∎

Remark 8
.

The hypothesis that
\mu
is semifinite cannot be omitted in the previous proposition.
For instance, suppose that there is a sequence
(x_{n})
of pairwise distinct points in
X
such that

\{x_{n}\}\in\mathfrak{M}
and
\mu(\{x_{n}\})=\infty
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
Y\!:=X\backslash\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots\}
and define
w:X\to\mathbb{R}
by
w\!:=1
on
Y
and
w(x_{n})\!:=n
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then
w\not\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, but

\varphi\in L^{p}(\mu)\ \ \Rightarrow\ \ \varphi(x_{n})=0\ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}\ \ \Rightarrow\ \ \varphi\cdot w=\varphi\cdot{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{Y}\in L^{p}(\mu).

Given a bimeasurable map
f:X\to X
(i.e.,
f(A)\in\mathfrak{M}
and
f^{-1}(A)\in\mathfrak{M}
for all
A\in\mathfrak{M}
), it is easy to show that the weighted composition operator

C_{w,f}(\varphi)\!:=(\varphi\circ f)\cdot w

is a well-defined bounded linear operator on
L^{p}(\mu)
if and only if there exists a constant
c\in(0,\infty)
such that

(7)

\int_{A}|w|^{p}d\mu\leq c\,\mu(f(A))\ \textrm{ for every }A\in\mathfrak{M}.

Moreover, in this case,
\|C_{w,f}\|\leq c^{\frac{1}{p}}
.

For the remaining of this section, we fix a bimeasurable map
f:X\to X
satisfying condition (
7
).
Hence, the weighted composition operator

C_{w,f}:\varphi\in L^{p}(\mu)\mapsto(\varphi\circ f)\cdot w\in L^{p}(\mu)

is a well-defined bounded linear operator. If
w
is the constant function
1
, then we obtain the (unweighted) composition operator

C_{f}:\varphi\in L^{p}(\mu)\mapsto\varphi\circ f\in L^{p}(\mu).

We associate to
\mu
,
w
and
f
, the following sequence of positive measures on
(X,\mathfrak{M})
:

\mu_{1}(A)\!:=\int_{A}|w|^{p}d\mu,\ \ \mu_{n}(A)\!:=\int_{A}|w\circ f^{n-1}|^{p}\cdots|w\circ f|^{p}|w|^{p}d\mu\ \ (A\in\mathfrak{M},n\geq 2).

If
w
is the constant function
1
, then
\mu_{n}=\mu
for all
n\geq 1
.

Lemma 9
.

\mu_{n}(A)\leq c^{n}\mu(f^{n}(A))
for all
A\in\mathfrak{M}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

The case
n=1
is obvious. If
n\geq 2
and
A\in\mathfrak{M}
, then

\displaystyle\mu_{n}(A)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}|{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{f^{n}(A)}\circ f^{n}|^{p}|w\circ f^{n-1}|^{p}\cdots|w\circ f|^{p}|w|^{p}d\mu

\displaystyle=\|(C_{w,f})^{n}({\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{f^{n}(A)})\|_{p}^{p}\leq c^{n}\mu(f^{n}(A)),

as it was to be shown.
∎

We now establish the main result of this section, a characterization of the weighted composition operators on
L^{p}(\mu)
that are Li-Yorke chaotic.

Theorem 10
.

The weighted composition operator
C_{w,f}
on
L^{p}(\mu)
is Li-Yorke chaotic if and only if there is a nonempty countable family

(B_{i})_{i\in I}
of measurable sets of finite positive
\mu
-measure such that the following conditions hold:

(A)

There is an increasing sequence
(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive integers such that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\mu_{n_{j}}(f^{-n_{j}}(B_{i}))=0\ \ \text{ for all }i\in I.

(B)

\displaystyle\sup\Bigg\{\frac{\mu_{n}(f^{-n}(B_{i}))}{\mu(B_{i})}:i\in I,n\in\mathbb{N}\Bigg\}=\infty
.

Proof.

(
\Leftarrow
): Let
Y
be the closed linear span of
\{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{B_{i}}:i\in I\}
in
L^{p}(\mu)
. By condition (A), for each
i\in I
,

\displaystyle\|(C_{w,f})^{n_{j}}({\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{B_{i}})\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}|{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{B_{i}}\circ f^{n_{j}}|^{p}|w\circ f^{n_{j}-1}|^{p}\cdots|w\circ f|^{p}|w|^{p}d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{f^{-n_{j}}(B_{i})}|w\circ f^{n_{j}-1}|^{p}\cdots|w\circ f|^{p}|w|^{p}d\mu

\displaystyle=\mu_{n_{j}}\big(f^{-n_{j}}(B_{i})\big)\to 0\ \text{ as }j\to\infty.

Hence, the set of all
\varphi\in Y
whose orbit under
C_{w,f}
has a subsequence converging to
0
is dense in
Y
,
and so it is residual in
Y
by
[
7
, Proposition 3]
.
For each
i\in I
, let
\phi_{i}\!:=\mu(B_{i})^{-\frac{1}{p}}\cdot{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{B_{i}}\in Y
. Since

\|\phi_{i}\|_{p}=1\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \|(C_{w,f})^{n}(\phi_{i})\|_{p}^{p}=\frac{\mu_{n}\big(f^{-n}(B_{i})\big)}{\mu(B_{i})}\ \ \ \ (n\in\mathbb{N}),

it follows from condition (B) that the sequence
(\|(C_{w,f})^{n}|_{Y}\|)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is unbounded.
Hence, by the Banach-Steinhaus theorem
[
30
, Theorem 2.5]
,
the set of all
\varphi\in Y
whose orbit under
C_{w,f}
is unbounded is also residual in
Y
.
Thus,
C_{w,f}
admits an irregular vector, and so it is Li-Yorke chaotic.

(
\Rightarrow
): Since the operator
C_{w,f}
is Li-Yorke chaotic, it admits an irregular vector
\psi\in L^{p}(\mu)
. For each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, let

B_{i}\!:=\{x\in X:2^{i-1}\leq|\psi(x)|<2^{i}\}\in\mathfrak{M}.

Consider the countable set
I\!:=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:\mu(B_{i})>0\}
, which is nonempty because
\psi
is not the zero vector in
L^{p}(\mu)
.
Since

\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{(i-1)p}\mu(B_{i})\leq\int_{X}|\psi|^{p}d\mu<\infty,

we have that
\mu(B_{i})\in(0,\infty)
for all
i\in I
.
Since
\psi
is an irregular vector for
C_{w,f}
, there is an increasing sequence
(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive integers such that

\|(C_{w,f})^{n_{j}}(\psi)\|_{p}\to 0
as
j\to\infty
. Since, for each
i\in I
,

\displaystyle\|(C_{w,f})^{n_{j}}(\psi)\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}|\psi\circ f^{n_{j}}|^{p}|w\circ f^{n_{j}-1}|^{p}\cdots|w\circ f|^{p}|w|^{p}d\mu

\displaystyle\geq\int_{f^{-n_{j}}(B_{i})}|\psi\circ f^{n_{j}}|^{p}|w\circ f^{n_{j}-1}|^{p}\cdots|w\circ f|^{p}|w|^{p}d\mu

\displaystyle\geq 2^{(i-1)p}\mu_{n_{j}}\big(f^{-n_{j}}(B_{i})\big),

we obtain condition (A). If condition (B) is false, then there exists
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(8)

\mu_{n}\big(f^{-n}(B_{i})\big)\leq\beta\mu(B_{i})\ \text{ for all }i\in I\text{ and }n\in\mathbb{N}.

In view of Lemma
9
, we have that (
8
) holds for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\|(C_{w,f})^{n}(\psi)\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{f^{-n}(B_{i})}|\psi\circ f^{n}|^{p}|w\circ f^{n-1}|^{p}\cdots|w\circ f|^{p}|w|^{p}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{ip}\int_{f^{-n}(B_{i})}|w\circ f^{n-1}|^{p}\cdots|w\circ f|^{p}|w|^{p}d\mu

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{ip}\mu_{n}\big(f^{-n}(B_{i})\big)\leq 2^{p}\beta\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{(i-1)p}\mu(B_{i})\leq 2^{p}\beta\|\psi\|_{p}^{p},

which contradicts the fact that
\psi
is an irregular vector for
C_{w,f}
.
∎

Remark 11
.

Note that condition (A) in Theorem
10
can be replaced by the following weaker condition:

(A’)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(f^{-n}(B_{i}))=0
for all
i\in I
.

In fact, suppose that there is a nonempty countable family
(B_{i})_{i\in I}
of measurable sets of finite positive
\mu
-measure satisfying conditions (A’) and (B). We have to prove that
C_{w,f}
is Li-Yorke chaotic. If

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(f^{-n}(B_{k}))>0\ \ \text{ for some }k\in I,

then
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{B_{k}}
is a semi-irregular vector for
C_{w,f}
, and so
C_{w,f}
is Li-Yorke chaotic. Assume

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(f^{-n}(B_{i}))=0\ \ \text{ for all }i\in I.

This means that condition (A) holds with
(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\!:=(n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Since we are assuming that condition (B) holds,
C_{w,f}
is Li-Yorke chaotic by Theorem
10
.

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
10
, we obtain the following result, which is exactly Theorem 1.1 in
[
9
]
.

Corollary 12
.

The composition operator
C_{f}
on
L^{p}(\mu)
is Li-Yorke chaotic if and only if there is a nonempty countable family
(B_{i})_{i\in I}
of measurable sets of finite positive
\mu
-measure such that the following conditions hold:

•

There is an increasing sequence
(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive integers such that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\mu(f^{-n_{j}}(B_{i}))=0\ \ \text{ for all }i\in I.

•

\displaystyle\sup\Bigg\{\frac{\mu(f^{-n}(B_{i}))}{\mu(B_{i})}:i\in I,n\in\mathbb{N}\Bigg\}=\infty
.

Remark 13
.

In view of Remark
11
, we have that the first condition in the above corollary can be replaced by the following weaker condition:

•

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mu(f^{-n}(B_{i}))=0
for all
i\in I
.

Corollary 14
.

A unilateral weighted backward shift

B_{w}:(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\ldots)\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})\mapsto(w_{1}x_{2},w_{2}x_{3},\ldots)\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N}),

where
w\!:=(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a bounded sequence of scalars, is Li-Yorke chaotic if and only if

(9)

\sup\{|w_{i}\cdots w_{j}|:i,j\in\mathbb{N},i\leq j\}=\infty.

Proof.

Consider
X\!:=\mathbb{N}
,
\mathfrak{M}\!:=\mathcal{P}(X)
(the power set of
X
),
\mu
the counting measure on
\mathfrak{M}
, and
f:n\in\mathbb{N}\mapsto n+1\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then
L^{p}(\mu)=\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
and
C_{w,f}=B_{w}
. In the present case, condition (A) is superfluous.
If (
9
) fails, that is,
\beta\!:=\sup\{|w_{i}\cdots w_{j}|:i,j\in\mathbb{N},i\leq j\}<\infty
, then

\mu_{n}(A)=\sum_{i\in A}|w_{i}\cdots w_{i+n-1}|^{p}\leq\beta^{p}\mu(A)\ \ \text{ for all }A\in\mathfrak{M},

and so condition (B) fails no matter how we choose the family
(B_{i})_{i\in I}
.
On the other hand, if (
9
) is true, then condition (B) holds with
B_{i}\!:=\{i\}
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

The above corollary was established in
[
4
, Proposition 27]
in the case
w
is a bounded sequence of nonzero scalars.

Corollary 15
.

A bilateral weighted backward shift

B_{w}:(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})\mapsto(w_{n}x_{n+1})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}),

where
w\!:=(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a bounded sequence of nonzero scalars, is Li-Yorke chaotic if and only if the following conditions hold:

(a)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}|w_{-n}\cdots w_{-1}|=0
.

(b)

\sup\{|w_{i}\cdots w_{j}|:i,j\in\mathbb{Z},i\leq j\}=\infty
.

Proof.

Consider
X\!:=\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathfrak{M}\!:=\mathcal{P}(X)
,
\mu
the counting measure on
\mathfrak{M}
, and
f:n\in\mathbb{Z}\mapsto n+1\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then
L^{p}(\mu)=\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
and
C_{w,f}=B_{w}
. Suppose that (a) and (b) hold. If

\limsup_{n\to\infty}|w_{-n}\cdots w_{-1}|>0,

then
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{\{0\}}
is a semi-irregular vector for
C_{w,f}
, and so
C_{w,f}
is Li-Yorke chaotic. If

\lim_{n\to\infty}|w_{-n}\cdots w_{-1}|=0,

then conditions (A) and (B) hold with
(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\!:=(n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
B_{i}\!:=\{i\}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, and so
C_{w,f}
is Li-Yorke chaotic by Theorem
10
.
Conversely, suppose that
C_{w,f}
is Li-Yorke chaotic and let
(B_{i})_{i\in I}
and
(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be as in the statement of Theorem
10
.
By choosing
i\in I
and
k\in B_{i}
, we have that

|w_{-n_{j}+k}\cdots w_{-1+k}|^{p}=\mu_{n_{j}}(f^{-n_{j}}(\{k\}))\leq\mu_{n_{j}}(f^{-n_{j}}(B_{i}))\to 0\text{ as }j\to\infty,

which gives (a). If (b) is false, that is,
\beta\!:=\sup\{|w_{i}\cdots w_{j}|:i,j\in\mathbb{Z},i\leq j\}<\infty
, then

\mu_{n}(f^{-n}(B_{i}))=\sum_{k\in B_{i}}|w_{-n+k}\cdots w_{-1+k}|^{p}\leq\beta^{p}\mu(B_{i})\ \ \text{ for all }i\in I,

contradicting condition (B).
∎

The above corollary was established in
[
9
, Corollary 1.6]
in the case
w
is a bounded sequence of positive scalars.

Remark 16
.

We can remove the hypothesis that the weights
w_{n}
are
nonzero
in Corollary
15
provided we state the result as follows:

Consider a bilateral weighted backward shift

B_{w}:(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})\mapsto(w_{n}x_{n+1})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}),

where
w\!:=(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a bounded sequence of scalars. Let

\beta^{-}\!:=\sup\{|w_{i}\cdots w_{j}|:i\leq j\leq-1\}\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \beta^{+}\!:=\sup\{|w_{i}\cdots w_{j}|:0\leq i\leq j\}.

Then
B_{w}
is Li-Yorke chaotic if and only if one of the following conditions hold:

(I)

\beta^{+}=\infty
and
\,\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}|w_{-n}\cdots w_{-k}|=0
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(II)

\beta^{+}<\infty
,
\beta^{-}=\infty
and
\,\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}|w_{-n}\cdots w_{-k}|=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Indeed, let
X
,
\mathfrak{M}
,
\mu
and
f
be as in the proof of Corollary
15
.
Assume
C_{w,f}
Li-Yorke chaotic and let
(B_{i})_{i\in I}
and
(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be as in Theorem
10
.
Define

\beta\!:=\sup\{|w_{i}\cdots w_{j}|:i,j\in\mathbb{Z},i\leq j\}\ \ \text{ and }\ \ B\!:=\bigcup_{i\in I}B_{i}.

If
\ell\in B_{i}
for some
i\in I
, then

|w_{-n_{j}+\ell}\cdots w_{-1+\ell}|^{p}=\mu_{n_{j}}(f^{-n_{j}}(\{\ell\}))\leq\mu_{n_{j}}(f^{-n_{j}}(B_{i}))\to 0\ \text{ as }j\to\infty.

Therefore,

(10)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}|w_{-n}\cdots w_{-1+\ell}|=0\ \ \text{ for all }\ell\in B.

Since

\mu_{n}(f^{-n}(B_{i}))=\sum_{k\in B_{i}}|w_{-n+k}\cdots w_{-1+k}|^{p}\leq\beta^{p}\mu(B_{i})\ \ \ \ (i\in I,n\in\mathbb{N}),

condition (B) gives
\beta=\infty
.
If
\beta^{+}=\infty
, then (
10
) implies that (I) holds. Assume
\beta^{+}<\infty
.
Since
\beta\leq\max\{\beta^{-},\beta^{+},\beta^{-}\!\!\cdot\!\beta^{+}\}
, we must have
\beta^{-}=\infty
.
If
B
is unbounded below, (
10
) implies that (II) holds.
Assume
B
bounded below and let
m\!:=\min B
. We claim that

(11)

w_{n}\neq 0\ \ \text{ for all }n<m.

Indeed, suppose that
w_{t}=0
for a certain
t<m
. Let

C\!:=\sup\{|w_{i}\cdots w_{j}|:t<i\leq j\}.

Since
\beta^{+}<\infty
, we have that
C<\infty
. Since
t<m=\min B
and
w_{t}=0
,

\mu_{n}(f^{-n}(B_{i}))=\sum_{k\in B_{i}}|w_{-n+k}\cdots w_{-1+k}|^{p}\leq C^{p}\mu(B_{i})\ \ \ \ (i\in I,n\in\mathbb{N}).

This contradiction proves our claim. By (
10
),

\liminf_{n\to\infty}|w_{-n}\cdots w_{-1+m}|=0.

Hence, (
11
) implies that (II) holds.

Let us now prove the converse. If (I) holds, let
I\!:=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:i>-k\}
and
B_{i}\!:=\{i\}
for each
i\in I
.
Since
\liminf_{n\to\infty}|w_{-n}\cdots w_{-k}|=0
, we have that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(f^{-n}(B_{i}))=\liminf_{n\to\infty}|w_{-n+i}\cdots w_{-1+i}|^{p}=0\ \text{ for all }i\in I.

Moreover, condition (B) holds because
\beta^{+}=\infty
.
Thus, in view of Remark
11
, we have that
C_{w,f}
is Li-Yorke chaotic.

Finally, if (II) holds, consider
I\!:=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:i\leq-1\}
and
B_{i}\!:=\{i\}
for each
i\in I
.
Since
\liminf_{n\to\infty}|w_{-n}\cdots w_{-k}|=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(f^{-n}(B_{i}))=\liminf_{n\to\infty}|w_{-n+i}\cdots w_{-1+i}|^{p}=0\ \text{ for all }i\in I.

Since
\beta^{-}=\infty
, condition (B) holds. Thus,
C_{w,f}
is Li-Yorke chaotic by Remark
11
.

4.
Weighted shifts on Fréchet sequence spaces

In this section we complement the previous results on Li-Yorke chaos for weighted shifts on the classical Banach sequence spaces

c_{0}
and
\ell^{p}
by considering weighted shifts on the more general setting of abstract Fréchet sequence spaces.
Recall that a
Fréchet sequence space
is a Fréchet space
X
which is a vector subspace of the product space
\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{N}}

such that the inclusion map
X\to\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{N}}
is continuous, i.e., convergence in
X
implies coordinatewise convergence.
If
w\!:=(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of nonzero scalars, the closed graph theorem implies that the

unilateral weighted backward shift

B_{w}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\ldots)\!:=(w_{1}x_{2},w_{2}x_{3},w_{3}x_{4},\ldots)

is a continuous linear operator on
X
as soon as it maps
X
into itself.
The canonical vectors
e_{n}\!:=(\delta_{n,j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{N}}
(
n\in\mathbb{N}
) form a
basis
of
X
if they belong to
X
and

x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}e_{n}\ \ \text{ for all }x\!:=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in X.

Recall also that a
Fréchet sequence space over
\mathbb{Z}
is a Fréchet space
X
which is a vector subspace of the product space

\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{Z}}
such that the inclusion map
X\to\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is continuous. As before, if
w\!:=(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of nonzero scalars,
then the
bilateral weighted backward shift

B_{w}((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}})\!:=(w_{n}x_{n+1})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}

is a continuous linear operator on
X
as soon as it maps
X
into itself.
The canonical vectors
e_{n}\!:=(\delta_{n,j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{Z}}
(
n\in\mathbb{Z}
) form a
basis
of
X
if they belong to
X
and

x=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}x_{n}e_{n}\ \ \text{ for all }x\!:=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in X.

Finally, recall that a continuous map
f:M\to M
, where
M
is a metric space, is said to be
densely
(resp.
generically
)

Li-Yorke chaotic
if it admits a dense (resp. residual) scrambled set.

The following trichotomy complements the study of Li-Yorke chaos for linear operators developed in
[
7
]
.

Theorem 17
.

Let
T
be a continuous linear operator on a Fréchet space
X
. If

\operatorname{NS}(T)\!:=\{x\in X:(T^{n}x)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\text{ has a subsequence converging to zero}\},

then either

(a)

T
has a residual set of irregular vectors, or

(b)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}T^{n}x=0
for every
x\in X
, or

(c)

\operatorname{NS}(T)
is not dense in
X
.

Moreover:

(A)

If
\operatorname{NS}(T)
is not closed in
X
, then
T
is Li-Yorke chaotic.

(B)

If
X
is separable and
\operatorname{NS}(T)
is dense and not closed in
X
, then
T
is densely Li-Yorke chaotic.

Proof.

It is clear that the three properties (a), (b) and (c) are mutually exclusive.

We assume that
\operatorname{NS}(T)
is dense in
X
and prove that either (a) or (b) must be true.
If
T
has an irregular vector, then the Dense Li-Yorke Chaos Criterion
[
7
, Definition 16]
holds.
Hence, by
[
7
, Theorem 17]
(see also the last paragraph of
[
7
, Section 6]
), (a) is true.
If
T
does not have an irregular vector, then
[
7
, Theorem 8]
guarantees that
T
does not have a semi-irregular
vector either. Thus,

(12)

\operatorname{NS}(T)=\Big\{x\in X:\lim_{n\to\infty}T^{n}x=0\Big\},

which is a vector subspace of
X
. Since
\operatorname{NS}(T)
is residual in
X
, we must have
\operatorname{NS}(T)=X
.
Indeed, given
x\in X
, since
\operatorname{NS}(T)
and
x+\operatorname{NS}(T)
are both residual in
X
, the intersection
\operatorname{NS}(T)\cap(x+\operatorname{NS}(T))

is nonempty, and so
x\in\operatorname{NS}(T)+\operatorname{NS}(T)=\operatorname{NS}(T)
. Hence, (b) is true.

In order to prove (A), we assume that
T
is not Li-Yorke chaotic.
Let
M
be the set in the right-hand side of (
12
) and let
Y\!:=\overline{M}
.
Consider the operator
S:Y\to Y
obtained by restricting
T
to
Y
.
Since
T
is not Li-Yorke chaotic,
T
has no semi-irregular vector, and so
\operatorname{NS}(T)=M
.
Moreover, both (a) and (c) are false for the operator
S
.
Thus, (b) must hold for the operator
S
, which means that
M=Y=\overline{M}
.

Finally, assume the hypotheses of (B).
Since
\operatorname{NS}(T)
is dense in
X
, (c) does not hold.
Since
\operatorname{NS}(T)
is not closed in
X
, (A) ensures that
T
is Li-Yorke chaotic, and so (b) is also false.
By the above trichotomy, (a) must be true.
Since we are assuming that
X
is separable,
T
is densely Li-Yorke chaotic by
[
7
, Theorem 10]
.
∎

Remark 18
.

The converses of (A) and (B) are false in general. For instance, for every generically Li-Yorke chaotic operator
T
,
we have that
\operatorname{NS}(T)=X
by
[
7
, Theorem 34]
.

Corollary 19
.

Suppose that
X
is a Fréchet sequence space in which the sequence
(e_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of canonical vectors is a basis,

w\!:=(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of nonzero scalars, and the unilateral weighted backward shift
B_{w}
is a well-defined
operator on
X
. Then either

(a)

B_{w}
is densely Li-Yorke chaotic, or

(b)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}(B_{w})^{n}x=0
for every
x\in X
.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{NS}(B_{w})
is dense in
X
, the result follows from the above trichotomy.
∎

In other words, the unilateral weighted backward shift
B_{w}
on
X
is Li-Yorke chaotic
if and only if there is a vector
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in X
such that

(w_{1}\cdots w_{n}x_{n+1},w_{2}\cdots w_{n+1}x_{n+2},w_{3}\cdots w_{n+2}x_{n+3},\ldots)\not\to 0\text{ in }X\text{ as }n\to\infty.

In particular, the unilateral unweighted backward shift
B
on
X
is Li-Yorke chaotic (provided it is well-defined, of course)
if and only if there is a vector
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in X
such that

(x_{n+1},x_{n+2},x_{n+3},\ldots)\not\to 0\text{ in }X\text{ as }n\to\infty.

For example, this is not the case if
X=c_{0}(\mathbb{N})
or
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
for any
p\in[1,\infty)
, but it is true if
X=\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{N}}
.

Corollary 20
.

Suppose that
X
is a Fréchet sequence space over
\mathbb{Z}
in which the sequence
(e_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of canonical vectors is a basis,

w\!:=(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of nonzero scalars, and the bilateral weighted backward shift
B_{w}
is a well-defined
operator on
X
. If the sequence
(w_{-n}\cdots w_{-1}e_{-n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
has a subsequence converging to zero, then either

(a)

B_{w}
is densely Li-Yorke chaotic, or

(b)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}(B_{w})^{n}x=0
for every
x\in X
.

Proof.

The additional hypothesis on the weights means that there is an increasing sequence
(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive integers such that

(B_{w})^{n_{j}}e_{0}\to 0\ \text{ as }j\to\infty.

For each
k\geq 1
, there is a constant
a_{k}
such that

(B_{w})^{k}e_{k}=a_{k}e_{0}.

For each
k\leq-1
, there is a constant
b_{k}
such that

(B_{w})^{-k}e_{0}=b_{k}e_{k}.

Thus, for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(13)

(B_{w})^{n_{j}+k}e_{k}\to 0\ \text{ as }j\to\infty.

Given
m\geq 1
and
k\in\{-m,\ldots,m\}
, by applying
(B_{w})^{m-k}
in (
13
), we see that

(B_{w})^{n_{j}+m}e_{k}\to 0\ \text{ as }j\to\infty.

Thus,
(B_{w})^{n_{j}+m}x\to 0
as
j\to\infty
, for every
x\in\operatorname{span}(\{e_{-m},\ldots,e_{m}\})
.
This proves that the set
\operatorname{NS}(B_{w})
contains every bilateral sequence of finite support, and so it is dense in
X
.
Thus, the result follows from the previous trichotomy.
∎

Remark 21
.

The additional hypothesis on the weights is essential for the validity of the above corollary.
Indeed, for the unweighted backward shift on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, both (a) and (b) are false.

Theorem 22
.

Suppose that
X
is a Fréchet sequence space over
\mathbb{Z}
in which the sequence
(e_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of canonical vectors is a basis,

w\!:=(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of nonzero scalars, and the bilateral weighted backward shift
B_{w}
is a well-defined
operator on
X
. Then
B_{w}
is Li-Yorke chaotic if and only if the following conditions hold:

(a)

The sequence
(w_{-n}\cdots w_{-1}e_{-n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
has a subsequence converging to zero.

(b)

There is a vector
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in X
such that

(w_{j}\cdots w_{j+n-1}x_{j+n})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\not\to 0\text{ in }X\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Proof.

Suppose that
B_{w}
is Li-Yorke chaotic and let
x\!:=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in X
be a semi-irregular vector for
B_{w}
.
Since
(B_{w})^{n}(x)\not\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, (b) holds.
Choose
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
x_{k}\neq 0
and let
(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be an increasing sequence of positive integers such that

(B_{w})^{n_{j}}(x)\to 0
as
j\to\infty
.
Given a neighborhood
V
of
0
in
X
, the equicontinuity of the family of operators
y\in X\mapsto y_{n}e_{n}\in X
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
implies the existence of a neighborhood
U
of
0
in
X
such that

y\in U\ \Longrightarrow\ y_{n}e_{n}\in V\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{Z}.

There exists
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

j\geq j_{0}\ \Longrightarrow\ (B_{w})^{n_{j}}(x)\in U\ \Longrightarrow\ w_{k-n_{j}}\cdots w_{k-1}x_{k}e_{k-n_{j}}\in V.

This proves that
w_{k-n_{j}}\cdots w_{k-1}x_{k}e_{k-n_{j}}\to 0
as
j\to\infty
, which implies (a).

Conversely, if (a) and (b) hold, then
B_{w}
is (densely) Li-Yorke chaotic by the previous corollary.
∎

In particular, the bilateral unweighted backward shift
B
on
X
is Li-Yorke chaotic (provided it is well-defined, of course)
if and only if the sequence
(e_{-n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
has a subsequence converging to zero and there is a vector

(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in X
such that

(x_{j+n})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\not\to 0\text{ in }X\text{ as }n\to\infty.

For example, this is not the case if
X=c_{0}(\mathbb{Z})
or
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
for any
p\in[1,\infty)
, but it is true if
X=\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{Z}}
.

It is known that
B
is
hypercyclic
(i.e., it has a dense orbit) if and only if
there is an increasing sequence
(n_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive integers such that, for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

e_{k-n_{j}}\to 0\ \text{ and }\ e_{k+n_{j}}\to 0\ \text{ in }X\text{ as }j\to\infty

(see
[
24
, Theorem 4.12]
).
With these characterizations it is easy to exhibit such a shift
B
which is Li-Yorke chaotic but not hypercyclic
(although stronger and more sophisticated examples can be found in
[
28
]
).
As an illustration, consider the weight sequence
\nu\!:=(\nu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
, where
\nu_{-n}\!:=1/n
for all
n\geq 1
, and

(\nu_{n})_{n\geq 0}\!:=(1,2,1,2,2^{2},2,1,2,2^{2},2^{3},2^{2},2,1,\ldots,1,2,2^{2},\ldots,2^{n},\ldots,2^{2},2,1,\ldots),

a concatenation of blocks of the form
(1,2,2^{2},\ldots,2^{n},\ldots,2^{2},2)
for
n\geq 1
.
Fix
p\in[1,\infty)
and consider the weighted
\ell^{p}
space

\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z},\nu)\!:=\Big\{x\!:=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{Z}}:\|x\|_{p}^{p}\!:=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}|x_{n}|^{p}\nu_{n}<\infty\Big\}.

Clearly the bilateral backward shift
B
is a well-defined operator on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z},\nu)
.
It is not hypercyclic because
\|e_{n}\|_{p}\geq 1
for all
n\geq 0
.
In order to see that it is Li-Yorke chaotic, let
j_{n}\!:=n(n+1)
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and consider the vector

x\!:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-\frac{n}{p}}e_{j_{n}}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z},\nu).

Since
e_{-n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
and
\|B^{n}x\|_{p}\geq\|B^{n}(2^{-\frac{n}{p}}e_{j_{n}})\|_{p}=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
we have that
B
is Li-Yorke chaotic.

We shall now focus on a more concrete class of Fréchet sequence spaces known as Köthe sequence spaces.
Let
J\!:=\mathbb{N}
or
\mathbb{Z}
. Recall that a
Köthe matrix
(
on
J
) is a family of scalars of the form

A\!:=(a_{j,k})_{j\in J,k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that, for each
j\in J
, there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
a_{j,k}>0
,
and
0\leq a_{j,k}\leq a_{j,k+1}
for all
j\in J
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Given a real number
p\in\{0\}\cup[1,\infty)
and such a Köthe matrix
A
,
the associated
Köthe sequence space

\lambda_{p}(A,J)
is the Fréchet sequence space defined as follows:

•

If
p\in[1,\infty)
, then

\lambda_{p}(A,J)\!:=\Big\{(x_{j})_{j\in J}\in\mathbb{K}^{J}:\sum_{j\in J}|a_{j,k}x_{j}|^{p}<\infty\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}

endowed with the seminorms

\|x\|_{k}\!:=\Big(\sum_{j\in J}|a_{j,k}x_{j}|^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\ \text{ for }x\!:=(x_{j})_{j\in J}\in\lambda_{p}(A,J)\text{ and }k\in\mathbb{N}.

•

If
p=0
, then

\lambda_{p}(A,J)\!:=\Big\{(x_{j})_{j\in J}\in\mathbb{K}^{J}:\lim_{j\in J,|j|\to\infty}a_{j,k}x_{j}=0\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}

endowed with the seminorms

\|x\|_{k}\!:=\sup_{j\in J}|a_{j,k}x_{j}|\ \text{ for }x\!:=(x_{j})_{j\in J}\in\lambda_{p}(A,J)\text{ and }k\in\mathbb{N}.

Note that the sequence
(e_{j})_{j\in J}
of canonical vectors in
\mathbb{K}^{J}
is a basis of
\lambda_{p}(A,J)
for every
p\in\{0\}\cup[1,\infty)
.

Let us adopt the convention that
\frac{0}{0}=1
.
Given a sequence
w\!:=(w_{j})_{j\in J}
of nonzero scalars, it is well-known that the weighted backward shift

B_{w}((x_{j})_{j\in J})\!:=(w_{j}x_{j+1})_{j\in J}

is a well-defined operator on
\lambda_{p}(A,J)
if and only if, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that

a_{j,k}=0
whenever
a_{j+1,m}=0
(
j\in J
), and

(14)

\sup_{j\in J}\frac{a_{j,k}|w_{j}|}{a_{j+1,m}}<\infty.

We refer the reader to the books
[
27
,
29
]
for a detailed study of Köthe sequence spaces.

The next result characterizes Li-Yorke chaos for unilateral weighted backward shifts.

Theorem 23
.

Consider a Köthe sequence space
X\!:=\lambda_{p}(A,\mathbb{N})
,
where
A\!:=(a_{j,k})
is a Köthe matrix on
\mathbb{N}
with nonzero entries and
p\in\{0\}\cup[1,\infty)
.
Let
w\!:=(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of nonzero scalars such that
the unilateral weighted backward shift
B_{w}
is a well-defined operator on
X
.
Then
B_{w}
is Li-Yorke chaotic if and only if there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\sup\Big\{\frac{a_{i,k}|w_{i}\cdots w_{j}|}{a_{j+1,\ell}}:i,j\in\mathbb{N},i\leq j\Big\}=\infty\ \ \text{ for all }\ell\in\mathbb{N}.

Below is the corresponding result for bilateral weighted backward shifts.

Theorem 24
.

Consider a Köthe sequence space
X\!:=\lambda_{p}(A,\mathbb{Z})
,
where
A\!:=(a_{j,k})
is a Köthe matrix on
\mathbb{Z}
with nonzero entries and
p\in\{0\}\cup[1,\infty)
.
Let
w\!:=(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence of nonzero scalars such that
the bilateral weighted backward shift
B_{w}
is a well-defined operator on
X
.
Then
B_{w}
is Li-Yorke chaotic if and only if the following conditions hold:

(a)

There is an increasing sequence
(n_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive integers such that

\lim_{i\to\infty}a_{-n_{i},k}w_{-n_{i}}\cdots w_{-1}=0\ \ \text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{N}.

(b)

There exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\sup\Big\{\frac{a_{i,k}|w_{i}\cdots w_{j}|}{a_{j+1,\ell}}:i,j\in\mathbb{Z},i\leq j\Big\}=\infty\ \ \text{ for all }\ell\in\mathbb{N}.

Below we will prove Theorem
24
.
The proof of Theorem
23
is similar (but slightly simpler) and is left to the reader.

Proof.

Suppose that properties (a) and (b) hold.
We shall prove that
B_{w}
is Li-Yorke chaotic by applying Theorem
22
.
Since (a) is equivalent to property (a) in this theorem, it remains to show that property (b) in this theorem is also true.
For this purpose, fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
as in (b).
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the continuity of
B_{w}^{n}
implies the existence of a constant
C_{n}\in(0,\infty)
and an integer
r_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\|B_{w}^{n}(x)\|_{k}\leq C_{n}\|x\|_{r_{n}}\ \ \text{ for all }x\in X.

By applying this inequality to the canonical vectors
e_{j}
, we see that

(15)

\sup\Big\{\frac{a_{i,k}|w_{i}\cdots w_{j}|}{a_{j+1,r_{n}}}:i,j\in\mathbb{Z},j-i=n-1\Big\}\leq C_{n}\ \ \text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that the sequence
(r_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is strictly increasing.
We shall construct a sequence
(t_{s})_{s\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive integers and two sequences
(i_{s})_{s\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(j_{s})_{s\in\mathbb{N}}

of integers such that the following properties hold:

(
\alpha
)

The sequence
(t_{s})_{s\in\mathbb{N}}
is strictly increasing.

(
\beta
)

For every
s\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that
i_{s}\leq j_{s}
and
a_{i_{s},k}|w_{i_{s}}\cdots w_{j_{s}}|>2^{s}a_{j_{s}+1,t_{s}}
.

(
\gamma
)

The sequence
(n_{s})_{s\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
n_{s}\!:=j_{s}-i_{s}+1
, is strictly increasing.

(
\delta
)

j_{s}\neq j_{t}
whenever
s\neq t
.

We begin by putting
t_{1}\!:=r_{1}
and by choosing
i_{1},j_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

i_{1}\leq j_{1}\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \frac{a_{i_{1},k}|w_{i_{1}}\cdots w_{j_{1}}|}{a_{j_{1}+1,t_{1}}}>2.

The equality in (b) for
\ell\!:=t_{1}
guarantees the existence of such a pair
i_{1},j_{1}
.
Suppose that
t_{1},\ldots,t_{s}
,
i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}
and
j_{1},\ldots,j_{s}
have already been chosen with the desired properties.
Let

C\!:=\max\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{n_{s}+1}\}

and choose
t_{s+1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

t_{s+1}>\max\{t_{s},r_{n_{s}+1}\}.

It follows from (
15
) that

(16)

\sup\Big\{\frac{a_{i,k}|w_{i}\cdots w_{j}|}{a_{j+1,t_{s+1}}}:i,j\in\mathbb{Z},0\leq j-i\leq n_{s}\Big\}\leq C.

Now we have to consider two possibilities.

Case 1.
There exists
j_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

(17)

\sup\Big\{\frac{a_{i,k}|w_{i}\cdots w_{j_{0}}|}{a_{j_{0}+1,t_{s+1}}}:i\in\mathbb{Z},i\leq j_{0}\Big\}=\infty.

Then (
17
) holds with
j
instead of
j_{0}
for every
j\geq j_{0}
.
Hence, there exist
i_{s+1},j_{s+1}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

j_{s+1}>\max\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{s}\},\ \ i_{s+1}\leq j_{s+1}\ \text{ and }\ \frac{a_{i_{s+1},k}|w_{i_{s+1}}\cdots w_{j_{s+1}}|}{a_{j_{s+1}+1,t_{s+1}}}>\max\{C,2^{s+1}\}.

Thus, (
16
) implies that
n_{s+1}=j_{s+1}-i_{s+1}+1>n_{s}
.

Case 2.
For every
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(18)

K_{j}\!:=\sup\Big\{\frac{a_{i,k}|w_{i}\cdots w_{j}|}{a_{j+1,t_{s+1}}}:i\in\mathbb{Z},i\leq j\Big\}<\infty.

In this case, we apply the equality in (b) for
\ell\!:=t_{s+1}
to obtain a pair
i_{s+1},j_{s+1}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

i_{s+1}\leq j_{s+1}\ \text{ and }\ \frac{a_{i_{s+1},k}|w_{i_{s+1}}\cdots w_{j_{s+1}}|}{a_{j_{s+1}+1,t_{s+1}}}>\max\{C,K_{j_{1}},\ldots,K_{j_{s}},2^{s+1}\}.

Then
j_{s+1}\not\in\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{s}\}
and, as in Case 1,
n_{s+1}>n_{s}
.

This completes the construction of the sequences satisfying (
\alpha
)–(
\delta
). Now, define

x_{j_{s}+1}\!:=\frac{1}{2^{s}a_{j_{s}+1,t_{s}}}\ \ (s\in\mathbb{N})\ \ \text{ and }\ \ x_{j}\!:=0\ \text{ if }j\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{j_{s}+1:s\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Property (
\delta
) ensures that the sequence
x\!:=(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is well-defined.
Additionally, from its definition we see that
x
satisfies

\lim_{j\to\pm\infty}a_{j,\ell}\,x_{j}=0

for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, so that
x\in X
in case of
p=0
.
Given
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, property (
\alpha
) implies the existence of an integer
s_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
t_{s}\geq\ell
whenever
s>s_{0}
.
Therefore, if
p\in[1,\infty)
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|a_{j,\ell}\,x_{j}|^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}|a_{j_{s}+1,\ell}\,x_{j_{s}+1}|^{p}\leq\sum_{s=1}^{s_{0}}|a_{j_{s}+1,\ell}\,x_{j_{s}+1}|^{p}+\sum_{s=s_{0}+1}^{\infty}|a_{j_{s}+1,t_{s}}x_{j_{s}+1}|^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{s_{0}}|a_{j_{s}+1,\ell}\,x_{j_{s}+1}|^{p}+\sum_{s=s_{0}+1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{s}}\Big)^{p}<\infty.

Hence,
x\in X
no matter what is the value of
p
.
By property (
\beta
), for any
s\in\mathbb{N}
,

\|B_{w}^{n_{s}}(x)\|_{k}\geq|a_{i_{s},k}w_{i_{s}}\cdots w_{i_{s}+n_{s}-1}x_{i_{s}+n_{s}}|=|a_{i_{s},k}w_{i_{s}}\cdots w_{j_{s}}x_{j_{s}+1}|>1.

In view of property (
\gamma
), we conclude that
B_{w}^{n}(x)\not\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
Thus, by Theorem
22
,
B_{w}
is Li-Yorke chaotic.

Conversely, suppose that
B_{w}
is Li-Yorke chaotic.
We know that property (a) holds, since it is equivalent to property (a) in Theorem
22
.
Suppose, by contradiction, that property (b) is false.
Then, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\ell_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

C_{k}\!:=\sup\Big\{\frac{a_{i,k}|w_{i}\cdots w_{j}|}{a_{j+1,\ell_{k}}}:i,j\in\mathbb{Z},i\leq j\Big\}<\infty.

Take
x\!:=(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in X
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. If
p\in[1,\infty)
, then

\|B_{w}^{n}(x)\|_{k}^{p}=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}|a_{j,k}w_{j}\cdots w_{j+n-1}x_{j+n}|^{p}\leq\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}|C_{k}a_{j+n,\ell_{k}}x_{j+n}|^{p}=C_{k}^{p}\|x\|_{\ell_{k}}^{p}.

If
p=0
, then

\|B_{w}^{n}(x)\|_{k}=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|a_{j,k}w_{j}\cdots w_{j+n-1}x_{j+n}|\leq\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|C_{k}a_{j+n,\ell_{k}}x_{j+n}|=C_{k}\|x\|_{\ell_{k}}.

In any case, we see that the sequence
(B_{w}^{n}(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded for all
x\in X
.
Thus,
B_{w}
does not admit an irregular vector, which contradicts the fact that
B_{w}
is Li-Yorke chaotic.
∎

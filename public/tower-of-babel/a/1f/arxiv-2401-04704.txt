All Segal objects are monads in generalised spans
David Kern

Abstract
We extend Barwick’s and Haugseng’s construction of the double ∞ \infty -category of spans in a pullback-complete ∞ \infty -category ℭ \mathfrak{C} to more general shapes: for a large class of algebraic patterns 𝔓 \mathfrak{P} , we define a 𝔓 \mathfrak{P} -monoidal ∞ \infty -category of 𝔓 \mathfrak{P} -shaped spans in ℭ \mathfrak{C} , and we identify monads in it with Segal 𝔓 \mathfrak{P} -objects in ℭ \mathfrak{C} . For the cell pattern Θ op {\Theta}^{\mathrm{op}} , this recovers a homotopical reformulation of Batanin’s original definition of weak ω \omega -categories, and in general can be seen as a variant of the generalised multicategories of Burroni, Hermida, Leinster and Cruttwell–Shulman.



1
Introduction

1.1
Algebraic structures for higher categories

The various definitions of higher categories come in two families:
algebraic definitions specify the minimal amount of shape data (for

\ell
-categories, an
\ell
-graph, comprised only of elementary
cells) and add the structure of all the composition operations and
their higher coherences, while geometric definitions start from a
bigger shape containing all the possible pasting diagrams of cells and
simply impose conditions to ensure that they come from decompositions
into compatible elementary cells.

For example, the standard definition of an internal category, in a
category
\mathfrak{C}
admitting finite pullbacks, is as a

{{\mathchoice{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\displaystyle\Delta$}\put(0.42,0.7){\line{10}{-26}{0.25}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\textstyle\Delta$}\put(0.42,0.7){\line{10}{-26}{0.25}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\scriptstyle\Delta$}\put(0.42,0.7){\line{10}{-26}{0.25}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}\put(0.42,0.7){\line{10}{-26}{0.25}}\end{picture}}}}^{\mathrm{op}}
-shaped object
X_{\bullet}
of
\mathfrak{C}
— where

is the category of
1
-dimensional pasting diagrams, that
is sequences of composable arrows — satisfying the Segal
decomposition condition which expresses each value
X_{n}
on a
pasting of
n
consecutive arrows as

X_{1}\times_{X_{0}}\dots\times_{X_{0}}X_{1}
. This can be
reinterpreted in a more algebraic way as giving a graph

{}X_{\bullet}\rvert_{\{[0],[1]\}}
in
\mathfrak{C}
and a certain kind of
algebra structure on it, subject to the simplicial identities. To make
good sense of this algebra structure, it was noticed
by
[
Bén67
]
that a graph in
\mathfrak{C}
is nothing but
an endomorphism in the bicategory (or better, the double category) of
spans in
\mathfrak{C}
, and the required algebra structure is none other
than a structure of monad on this endomorphism.

For
strict
higher categories, the situation generalises
directly: on the one hand,
[
Joy97
]
introduced a
category
\Theta
of
\omega
-categorical pasting diagrams, so that
strict
\omega
-categories in any category with fibre products

\mathfrak{C}
are exactly
\mathfrak{C}
-valued presheaves on
\Theta

satisfying a Segal condition. On the other hand,

[
Bat98
]
constructed an internal
(strict)
\omega
-category in
\mathfrak{Cat}
(a globular object in

\mathfrak{Cat}
equipped with compositions)

\mathbb{Span}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{C})
of infinitely iterated spans in

\mathfrak{C}
, so that globular monads in it are exactly strict

\omega
-categories internal to
\mathfrak{C}
.

The key insight of
[
Bat98
]
is
then that, using the higher structure naturally present in globular
categories, one can refine the teminal globular operad to a suitable
contractible globular operad
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}^{\mathbb{G}}
,
which contains enough coherence data for

\mathcal{A}_{\infty}^{\mathbb{G}}
-algebras in

\mathbb{Span}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{C})
to be a good definition of weak

\omega
-categories in
\mathfrak{C}
.

While the presence of higher cells in globular sets allows one to make
sense of
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}^{\mathbb{G}}
as an algebraic
resolution of the terminal globular operad, eschewing any homotopical
machinery, formulating things in a setting of homotopy theory allows
many constructions to become simpler, and more widely
applicable. Indeed, the logic of using the higher cells to tame the
infinite towers of coherences needed for a resolution only works for
full
\omega
-categories, but breaks down if trying to define weak

\ell
-categories for some
\ell<\omega
. Nonetheless,

[
Hau21
]
showed that the situation for (weak)

1
-categories can be dealt with using
\infty
-categories: category
objects in an
(\infty,1)
-category
\mathfrak{C}
are identified with
homotopy-coherent monads in the double
(\infty,1)
-category of spans
in
\mathfrak{C}
.

In this note, we extend this result (as a direct application
of
5.7
) to a characterisation of

\ell
-category objects as monads in
\ell
-times iterated spans,
which both extends Batanin’s definition of weak
\omega
-categories to
one for weak
\ell
-categories for any
\ell\leq\omega
, and also
simplifies it by removing the need to resolve the terminal globular

\infty
-operad by a more complicated one.

1.2
Multicategories and algebraic patterns

In order to understand how to construct categories of generalised
spans, let us switch gears to another categorical structure that can
be defined in a similar way: multicategories, or coloured operads. It
was noticed
by
[
Bur71
,
Her04
,
Lei98
]

that multicategories can be defined as monads in a double category of
Kleisli
\mathcal{M\!}\,
-spans, where
\mathcal{M\!}\,
is the “free monoid”
monad on
\mathfrak{Set}
, fitting in a more general framework of

\mathcal{T\!}\,
-multicategories, for a cartesian monad
\mathcal{T\!}\,
, as
monads in a double category of Kleisli
\mathcal{T\!}\,
-spans, whose
morphisms are the spans twisted by
\mathcal{T\!}\,
on their source, and
whose composition uses
\mathcal{T\!}\,
’s monad structure. In particular,
Batanin’s globular operads can also be obtained in this way.

Unfortunately, this double category of Keisli
\mathcal{T\!}\,
-spans is not
characterised by a clear universal property (see
[
CS10
, Remark
4.2]
), which makes constructing it in the

\infty
-categorical world very difficult. Because of this, we will
instead use a different kind of structure to organise the generalised
spans.

To explain the idea, let us keep focusing of the example of
multicategories. An
\mathcal{M\!}\,
-span from a set
Y_{0}
to a set

X_{0}
is given by a span
\mathcal{M\!}\,Y_{0}\leftarrow X_{1}\to X_{0}
,
which we interpret as a multispan (as championed by
[
Baa19
]
for
the study of hyperstructures) of some arbitrary arity
a+1
, one of
whose legs (the root) goes to
X_{0}
and the
a
others (the leaves)
to
Y_{0}
. To compose it with an
\mathcal{M\!}\,
-span

\mathcal{M\!}\,Z_{0}\leftarrow Y_{1}\to Y_{0}
, one forms the

\mathcal{M\!}\,
-span

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M\!}\,Y_{1}\mathop{\times}\limits_{\mathcal{M\!}\,Y_{0}}X_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M\!}\,Y_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M\!}\,Z_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M\!}\,^{2}Z_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M\!}\,Y_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu}

(1)

expressing that one takes
a
copies of the multispan corresponding to

\mathcal{M\!}\,Z_{0}\leftarrow Y_{1}\to Y_{0}
and glues their distinguished

\mathcal{M\!}\,Y_{0}\leftarrow X_{1}\to X_{0}
.

As is usual in operad theory, one also, instead of blowing up the

; the composition operation
defined in this way, leaf by leaf, will no longer be a categorical

While there are many different approaches to operadic structures in
the
1
-categorical setting, in the
\infty
-categorical one a very
convenient and powerful framework is that of the algebraic patterns

[

]
, which extract the necessary data on a
category of shapes to speak of Segal decompositions (inert morphisms

of
\infty
-operadic structure, while remembering what is the
algebraic part. In the approach that we will follow in this note, the
choice of an algebraic pattern will play the role of the choice of the
cartesian monad
\mathcal{T\!}\,
in the story sketched above.

We will then construct in
section

, for any algebraic
pattern
\mathfrak{P}
(satisfying a condition we call global saturation
— that will be verified in all examples we know of, in particular
in

\omega
-categories) and any
complete enough
(\infty,1)
-category
\mathfrak{C}
, a Segal

\mathfrak{P}
-object in
\mathfrak{\mathnormal{(\infty,1)}\textnormal{-}Cat}
of
\mathfrak{P}
-shaped spans in
\mathfrak{C}
,
by adapting the construction of
[
Hau18
]
with the
ideas raised in
[
Str00
]
and expanded upon in
[
Web07

4.8]
. We will continue
in
section

\mathfrak{P}
-objects in
\mathfrak{C}
.

The

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}\to\mathfrak{P}
be a weak Segal fibration and
\mathfrak{C}
a

\mathfrak{P}
-complete
(\infty,1)
-category. There is an equivalence of

(\infty,1)
-categories

\mathfrak{Seg}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{C})\simeq\mathfrak{Alg}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{Span}(\mathfrak{C}))\text{.}

(26)

Proof.

Let

\mathcal{S\!}\,\colon\mathfrak{X}\to\overline{\mathfrak{Span}}_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathfrak{C})

be an
\infty
-functor over
\mathfrak{P}
that preserves cocartesian
morphisms over inert arrows (so corresponds to

\widetilde{\mathcal{S\!}\,}\colon\mathfrak{X}\to\mathfrak{C}
). It factors through

\mathfrak{Span}_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathfrak{C})
if and only if for every

X\in\mathfrak{X}
, the
\mathfrak{P}^{\text{{inrt}}}_{\mathcal{f\!}\,X/}
-object

\mathcal{S\!}\,(X)
in
\mathfrak{C}
is Segal.

By
[
Bar22
, Lemma 2.39]
, for any map of

and any
P\in\mathfrak{P}
, the

\mathfrak{O}\times_{\mathfrak{P}}\mathfrak{P}_{P/}\to\mathfrak{P}

\mathfrak{O}=\mathfrak{P}^{\text{{inrt}}}
and

(for any

equivalent to
\widetilde{\mathcal{S\!}\,}
being a Segal
\mathfrak{X}
-object.
∎

Remark 6.1

(Graphs and endomorphisms)
.

Since the Segal condition for a pattern

\mathfrak{P}
-endomorphisms in

Remark 6.5.2
.

The definition of operads, and more general multicategorical
structures, as monads in multispans is well-known: it dates
to
[
Bur71
]
, and was independently rediscovered by
both
[
Her04
]

and
[
Lei98
]
, and then further
systematised by
[
Lei04
]

and
[
CS10
]
. From a cartesian monad
\mathcal{T\!}\,
on a
category
\mathfrak{C}
, one constructs a double category of Kleisli

\mathcal{T\!}\,
-spans, whose objects are those of
\mathfrak{C}
and morphisms
from
C
to
D
are spans from
\mathcal{T\!}\,C
to
D
, composition of
spans using the monad structure.

For example, taking
\mathcal{T\!}\,
to be the monad

\mathcal{F\!}\,_{{{{\mathchoice{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\displaystyle\Gamma$}\put(0.47,0.025){\line{0}{1}{0.95}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\textstyle\Gamma$}\put(0.47,0.025){\line{0}{1}{0.95}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\scriptstyle\Gamma$}\put(0.47,0.025){\line{0}{1}{0.95}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\scriptscriptstyle\Gamma$}\put(0.47,0.025){\line{0}{1}{0.95}}\end{picture}}}}^{\mathrm{op}}}^{\flat}}
for free monoids, a Kleisli

\mathcal{T\!}\,
-span is a multispan of arbitrary arity, and monads (in
the double-categorical sense) are coloured operads. Generally
speaking, if
\mathcal{T\!}\,
is the monad
\mathcal{F\!}\,_{\mathfrak{P}}
for free
Segal
\mathfrak{P}
-objects on
\mathfrak{P}
-graphs for some appropriate
algebraic pattern
\mathfrak{P}
, we expect that monads in Kleisli

\mathcal{F\!}\,_{\mathfrak{P}}
-spans should be weak Segal

\mathfrak{P}
-fibrations, obtained as the Segal objects for a plus
construction
\mathfrak{P}^{+}
of
\mathfrak{P}
(as in
[
Ker23
, Proposition
3.2.10]
), so as
\mathfrak{P}^{+}
-monads in

\mathfrak{P}^{+}
-spans.

However, Kleisli
\mathcal{F\!}\,_{{{{\mathchoice{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\displaystyle\Gamma$}\put(0.47,0.025){\line{0}{1}{0.95}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\textstyle\Gamma$}\put(0.47,0.025){\line{0}{1}{0.95}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\scriptstyle\Gamma$}\put(0.47,0.025){\line{0}{1}{0.95}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\scriptscriptstyle\Gamma$}\put(0.47,0.025){\line{0}{1}{0.95}}\end{picture}}}}^{\mathrm{op}}}^{\flat}}
-spans and

{{{\mathchoice{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\displaystyle\Omega$}\put(0.2925,0.167){\line{0}{1}{0.72}}\put(0.6825,0.17){\line{0}{1}{0.72}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\textstyle\Omega$}\put(0.2925,0.167){\line{0}{1}{0.72}}\put(0.6825,0.17){\line{0}{1}{0.72}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\scriptstyle\Omega$}\put(0.2925,0.167){\line{0}{1}{0.72}}\put(0.6825,0.17){\line{0}{1}{0.72}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\scriptscriptstyle\Omega$}\put(0.2925,0.167){\line{0}{1}{0.72}}\put(0.6825,0.17){\line{0}{1}{0.72}}\end{picture}}}}^{\mathrm{op}}}^{\natural}
-spans, while both admitting a natural
interpretation as multispans, form markedly different structures. On
the one hand,

\mathbb{Span}_{{{{\mathchoice{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\displaystyle\Omega$}\put(0.2925,0.167){\line{0}{1}{0.72}}\put(0.6825,0.17){\line{0}{1}{0.72}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\textstyle\Omega$}\put(0.2925,0.167){\line{0}{1}{0.72}}\put(0.6825,0.17){\line{0}{1}{0.72}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\scriptstyle\Omega$}\put(0.2925,0.167){\line{0}{1}{0.72}}\put(0.6825,0.17){\line{0}{1}{0.72}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\scriptscriptstyle\Omega$}\put(0.2925,0.167){\line{0}{1}{0.72}}\put(0.6825,0.17){\line{0}{1}{0.72}}\end{picture}}}}^{\mathrm{op}}}^{\natural}}(\mathfrak{C})
is a
categorical
\infty
-operad, whose operadic composition is given
(leg by leg) by simple pullbacks. On the other hand, Keisli

\mathcal{F\!}\,_{{{{\mathchoice{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\displaystyle\Gamma$}\put(0.47,0.025){\line{0}{1}{0.95}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\textstyle\Gamma$}\put(0.47,0.025){\line{0}{1}{0.95}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\scriptstyle\Gamma$}\put(0.47,0.025){\line{0}{1}{0.95}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\scriptscriptstyle\Gamma$}\put(0.47,0.025){\line{0}{1}{0.95}}\end{picture}}}}^{\mathrm{op}}}^{\flat}}
-spans only form a double
category, but its composition is more complex and makes full use of
the monad structure on
\mathcal{F\!}\,_{{{{\mathchoice{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\displaystyle\Gamma$}\put(0.47,0.025){\line{0}{1}{0.95}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\textstyle\Gamma$}\put(0.47,0.025){\line{0}{1}{0.95}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\scriptstyle\Gamma$}\put(0.47,0.025){\line{0}{1}{0.95}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\scriptscriptstyle\Gamma$}\put(0.47,0.025){\line{0}{1}{0.95}}\end{picture}}}}^{\mathrm{op}}}^{\flat}}
. For a
general algebraic pattern
\mathfrak{P}
, the difference will be similar:
we think of it as moving the structure from the microcosm (on the
Keisli
\mathcal{F\!}\,_{\mathfrak{P}}
-spans side) to the macrocosm (on the

\mathfrak{P}^{+}
-spans side).

It is nonetheless unclear what the precise relation between the two
constructions is, if there even is one: the Keisli-type construction
can be abstracted away from a span setting by using general monads
acting on virtual double categories, but it is unlikely to be
able to handle non-directed structures such as the cyclic and
modular
\infty
-operads of
6.5.1
.

7
Conclusion: A fibrational perspective

Notation 7.1
.

In this
section
, we will identify

(\infty,1)
-categories with internal categories in
\mathfrak{\infty\textnormal{-}Grpd}
,
where internal categories are by definition Segal

{{{\mathchoice{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\displaystyle\Delta$}\put(0.42,0.7){\line{10}{-26}{0.25}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\textstyle\Delta$}\put(0.42,0.7){\line{10}{-26}{0.25}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\scriptstyle\Delta$}\put(0.42,0.7){\line{10}{-26}{0.25}}\end{picture}}{\begin{picture}\put(0.0,0.0){$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}\put(0.42,0.7){\line{10}{-26}{0.25}}\end{picture}}}}^{\mathrm{op}}}^{\natural}
-objects satisfying Rezk’s
univalence-completeness condition. It will also be convenient to see
Segal
\mathfrak{P}
-objects in
\mathfrak{\mathnormal{(\infty,1)}\textnormal{-}Cat}
— such as, in particular,
the
\mathfrak{P}
-
(\infty,1)
-categories of
\mathfrak{P}
-spans — as
internal categories in
\mathfrak{Seg}_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathfrak{\infty\textnormal{-}Grpd})
.

Recall that, for any regular cardinal
\kappa
, the

(\infty,1)
-category
\mathfrak{\infty\textnormal{-}Grpd}^{(\kappa)}\subset\mathfrak{\infty\textnormal{-}Grpd}
is the
base of the universal discrete cocartesian fibration with

\kappa
-small fibres

\mathfrak{\infty\textnormal{-}Grpd}^{(\kappa)}_{\bullet}\to\mathfrak{\infty\textnormal{-}Grpd}^{(\kappa)}
in the

(\infty,2)
-topos
\mathfrak{\mathnormal{(\infty,1)}\textnormal{-}Cat}
, just as

\mathfrak{Set}^{(\kappa)}\subset\mathfrak{Set}
is the universal
\kappa
-small
discrete cocartesian fibration in the
(2,2)
-topos
\mathfrak{Cat}
.
In
[
Web07
, Examples 4.7 and 4.8]
, it is explained
that, for an algebraic pattern
\mathfrak{P}^{\text{{el}}}
in which all objects
are elementary and all morphisms inert, the construction

\mathbb{Span}_{\mathfrak{P}^{\text{{el}}}}(-)
preserves classifying
discrete fibrations, so that the
2
-topos

\mathfrak{Cat}\bigl(\bigl\{\mathfrak{\mathfrak{P}^{\text{{el}}}},\mathfrak{Set}\bigr\}\bigr)
has a sufficient
family of classifying discrete cocartesian fibrations given by

\mathbb{Span}_{\mathfrak{P}^{\text{{el}}}}(\mathfrak{Set}_{\bullet}^{(\kappa)})\to\mathbb{Span}_{\mathfrak{P}^{\text{{el}}}}(\mathfrak{Set}^{(\kappa)})
(where
“sufficient” means that every discrete cocartesian fibration is
classified by one in the family).

In the
\infty
-categorical setting, the properties of universal (or
“classifying”) fibrations are captured by the notion of univalence,
which we restate from
[
GK16
]
(see also
[
Ras21a
, Theorem
4.4]
) in the internal setting.

Construction 7.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{C}
be a finitely complete
(\infty,1)
-category. Recall
that a
discrete cocartesian fibration
in
\mathfrak{C}
is an
internal functor
\mathcal{f\!}\,\colon\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{B}

such that

(d_{1},\mathcal{f\!}\,_{1})\colon\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{0}\times_{\mathbb{B}_{0}}\mathbb{B}_{1}

is an equivalence.

Lifting the construction of
[
GK16
, Theorem 2.10]
to
the cartesian closed
(\infty,2)
-category
\mathfrak{Cat}(\mathfrak{C})
, one
can construct for any discrete cocartesian fibration

\mathcal{f\!}\,\colon\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{B}
in
\mathfrak{C}
an
internal category

\mathbb{Eq}_{/\mathbb{B}\times\mathbb{B}}(\varpi_{1}^{\ast}\mathbb{E},\varpi_{2}^{\ast}\mathbb{E})

over
\mathbb{B}\times\mathbb{B}
(where

\varpi_{1},\varpi_{2}\colon\mathbb{B}\times\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{B}

are the two projections) characterising equivalences between fibres
of
\mathcal{f\!}\,
.

Definition 7.3

(Univalent fibration)
.

A discrete cocartesian fibration
\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{B}

internal to
\mathfrak{C}
is
univalent
if

\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{Eq}_{/\mathbb{B}\times\mathbb{B}}(\varpi_{1}^{\ast}\mathbb{E},\varpi_{2}^{\ast}\mathbb{E})

is an equivalence.

Example 7.4
.

It is shown in
[
Cis19
, Proposition
5.3.13]
that the universal
discrete cocartesian fibration
\mathfrak{\infty\textnormal{-}Grpd}_{\bullet}\to\mathfrak{\infty\textnormal{-}Grpd}
(in
the
(\infty,2)
-category
\mathfrak{\mathnormal{(\infty,1)}\textnormal{-}Cat}=\mathfrak{Cat}(\mathfrak{\infty\textnormal{-}Grpd})
) is
univalent.

Using this characterisation, one can show (though we omit the proof
here as this result is only used for motivation) that for any globally
saturated algebraic pattern
\mathfrak{P}^{\text{{inrt}}}
all of whose morphisms
are inert, the construction

\mathbb{Span}_{\mathfrak{P}^{\text{{inrt}}}}(-)\colon\mathfrak{\mathnormal{(\infty,1)}\textnormal{-}Cat}^{(\text{$\mathfrak{P}$-cplt})}\to\mathfrak{Cat}(\mathfrak{Seg}_{\mathfrak{P}^{\text{{inrt}}}}(\mathfrak{\infty\textnormal{-}Grpd}))
preserves classifying
discrete cocartesian fibrations:

Proposition 7.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{G}_{\bullet}\to\mathfrak{G}
be a univalent discrete
cocartesian fibration. Then

\mathbb{Span}_{\mathfrak{P}^{\text{{inrt}}}}(\mathfrak{G}_{\bullet})\to\mathbb{Span}_{\mathfrak{P}^{\text{{inrt}}}}(\mathfrak{G})
is a univalent
discrete cocartesian fibration internally to

\mathfrak{Seg}_{\mathfrak{P}^{\text{{inrt}}}}(\mathfrak{G})
. ∎

For an algebraic pattern with non-trivial active morphisms, the
situation becomes richer and goes beyond the
(\infty,2)
-topos theory
of internal categories in presheaf
(\infty,1)
-topoi. Indeed,

5.7
shows that, even when restricting
our attention as we are doing here from weak Segal fibrations to
strong ones, the morphisms of interest will be the lax morphisms, the
maps of underlying weak Segal fibrations. We will thus use a notion of
lax univalence, obtained by replacing strong morphisms by general lax
morphisms of categorical Segal
\mathfrak{P}
-
\infty
-groupoids in the
definition of univalence for fibrations in
\mathfrak{Seg}_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathfrak{\infty\textnormal{-}Grpd})
.

Conjecture 7.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{G}_{\bullet}\to\mathfrak{G}
be a univalent discrete
cocartesian fibration. Then

\mathbb{Span}_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathfrak{G}_{\bullet})\to\mathbb{Span}_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathfrak{G})
is a lax-univalent
discrete cocartesian fibration internally to

\mathfrak{Seg}_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathfrak{G})
.

This
section
states that

\mathbb{Span}_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathfrak{G}_{\bullet})\to\mathbb{Span}_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathfrak{G})
classifies a class of
discrete cocartesian fibrations. It remains to see that
every

such class is classified by a universal fibration of this form.

Conjecture 7.7
.

Suppose

\bigl(\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}_{\bullet}\to\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}\bigr)_{\kappa\in K}
is a sufficient family of univalent fibrations for

\mathfrak{\mathnormal{(\infty,1)}\textnormal{-}Cat}
. Then the family

\bigl(\mathbb{Span}_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}_{\bullet})\to\mathbb{Span}_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)})\bigr)_{\kappa\in K}
provides enough lax-univalent fibrations for

\mathfrak{Cat}(\mathfrak{Seg}_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathfrak{\infty\textnormal{-}Grpd}))
.

Corollary 7.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{P}
be a globally generated algebraic pattern and

\mathfrak{X}\to\mathfrak{P}
be a Segal
\mathfrak{P}
-fibration, and assume
that
7.6

and
7.7
hold. Then Segal

\mathfrak{X}
-objects in
\mathfrak{\infty\textnormal{-}Grpd}
are internal discrete cocartesian
fibrations over the straightening
\mathbb{X}
of
\mathfrak{X}
.

Proof.

The key point is that, by
[
GK16
, Proposition 3.8]
,
if
\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}_{\bullet}\to\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}
is univalent
then
\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}
is a full sub-
(\infty,1)
-category of

\mathfrak{\infty\textnormal{-}Grpd}
, from which it follows that lax morphisms

\mathfrak{X}\to\mathfrak{Span}_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)})
can be seen as
lax morphisms
\mathfrak{X}\to\mathfrak{Span}_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathfrak{\infty\textnormal{-}Grpd})
. By the
two conjectures, discrete cocartesian fibrations over

\mathbb{X}
are the same thing as lax morphisms

\mathfrak{X}\to\mathfrak{Span}_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathfrak{\infty\textnormal{-}Grpd})
(factoring through some

\mathfrak{Span}_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)})
). At the same time,
by
5.7
, the latter are the same thing
as Segal
\mathfrak{X}
-objects in
\mathfrak{\infty\textnormal{-}Grpd}
(or, to be precise, in
some
\mathfrak{G}^{(\kappa)}
), which proves the result.
∎

Example 7.9

(Double fibrations and Segal fibrations)
.

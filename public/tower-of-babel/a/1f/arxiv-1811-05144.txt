Sensitivity Analysis of a Stationary Point Set Map under Total Perturbations. Part 1: Lipschitzian Stability
Duong Thi Kim Huyen, Jen-Chih Yao, Nguyen Dong Yen

Abstract
By applying some theorems of Levy and Mordukhovich (Math Program 99: 311–327, 2004 ) and other related results, we estimate the Fréchet coderivative and the Mordukhovich coderivative of the stationary point set map of a smooth parametric optimization problem with one smooth functional constraint under total perturbations. From the obtained formulas we derive necessary and sufficient conditions for the local Lipschitz-like property of the stationary point set map. This leads us to new insights into the preceding deep investigations of Levy and Mordukhovich in the above-cited paper and of Qui (J Optim Theory Appl 161: 398–429, 2014 ; J Glob Optim 65: 615–635, 2016 ).

Introduction

Appeared at the early stage of optimization theory, smooth programming problems continue to attract common attention of the optimization community due to their importance and beauty. Polynomial optimization problems, including nonconvex quadratic programs, are typical examples of such problems.

The present paper investigates the Lipschitz-like property and the Robinson stability of the stationary point set map of a smooth parametric optimization problem with one smooth functional constraint under total perturbations. The aim is achieved by using some theorems of Levy and Mordukhovich
LeMo04
and other related results from
Yen_Yao_NA_2009
,
LeeYen11AA
, and
Qui_JOTA2014
.

Introduced by Aubin
(
Aubin_1984
, p. 98)
under the name pseudo-Lipschitz property, the
local Lipschitz-like property
of multifunctions is a fundamental concept in stability and sensitivity analysis of optimization and equilibrium problems. It is equivalent to the
classical metric regularity
of the inverse map (see
Borwein_Zhuang_1988
;
Penot_1989
and
B-M_TAMS_1993
). The local Lipschitz-like property guarantees the local convergence of some variants of Newton’s method for generalized equations
Célia_Alain_2005
;
Dontchev_1996
;
DR_2009
. In particular, from
(
DR_2009
, Theorem 6C.1, p. 328)
it follows that, if a mild approximation condition is satisfied and the solution map under right-hand-side perturbations is locally Lipschitz-like around a point in question, then there exists an iterative sequence
Q
-linearly converging to the solution. Moreover, as shown by Dontchev
(
Dontchev_1996
, Theorem 1)
, the Newton method applied to a generalized equation in a Banach space is locally convergent uniformly in the canonical parameter if and only if a certain map is locally Lipschitz-like around the reference point. The author also proved (see
(
Dontchev_1996
, Theorem 2)
) that the latter property implies the uniform
Q
-quadratic convergence, provided that the derivative of the base map is locally Lipschitz.

The
Robinson stability
of an implicit multifunction, which has been called the metric regularity in the sense of Robinson by several authors, was introduced by Robinson
Robinson_1976
. This property is a kind of uniform local error bounds and has numerous applications. Recently, Gfrerer and Mordukhovich
Gfrerer_Mor_SIOPT2016
have given first-order and second-order sufficient conditions for this stability property of a parametric constraint system and put it in the relationships with other properties, such as the classical metric regularity and the local Lipschitz-like property.

The coderivative analysis of composite constraint functions of Levy and Mordukhovich
LeMo04
is based on the rich generalized differentiation calculus in
(
Rock_Wets_1998
, Chapter 10)
. Among other things, it uses the properties of amenable functions and strongly amenable functions, and the extended chain rule for subdifferentials
(
Rock_Wets_1998
, Theorem 10.49)
. The analysis allows us to derive sharp upper estimates for the Mordukhovich coderivative of the stationary point set map, where the limiting second-order subdifferential is used.

To get lower estimates for the Fréchet and the Mordukhovich coderivatives of the stationary point set map, we combine the lower estimates of Lee and Yen
LeeYen11AA
with some results of Qui
Qui_JOTA2014
;
Qui_JoGO_2016
.

With the above upper and lower coderivative estimates, we can use the
Mordukhovich criterion
for the local Lipschitz-like property of locally closed multifunctions to obtain both
necessary and sufficient conditions
for this property. Here, we do not need an additional technical assumption of Qui. Besides, by invoking a result of
Yen_Yao_NA_2009
, we are able to show that these sufficient conditions also guarantee the Robinson stability of the stationary point set map.

Our conditions are easy to verify and can be effectively applied to nonconvex quadratic programming under a possibly nonconvex quadratic constraint. The results on quadratic programming in this paper extend the preceding ones of Lee and Yen
LeeYen14NA
and Qui and Yen
QuiYen_SIOPT2014
to a broader class of quadratic programs.

Optimization problems under total perturbations have been studied in
MNg_2014
;
MNg_2016
;
MNR_2015
;
MRS_2013
by different approaches and concepts. But, our results are very different from those of the cited works.

Solution stability of variational inequalities on fixed or linearly perturbed polyhedral convex sets, which is closely related to that of optimization problems under linear constraints, has been investigated intensively; see
Ban_Song_2016
;
DR_1996
;
Henrion_Mordukhovich_Nam_2010
;
Lu_Robinson_2008
;
Nam_2010
;
Qui_AMV_2011
;
Qui_NA_2011
;
Qui_JMAA_2011
;
Robinson_2007
;
Trang_2016
;
Yao_Yen_AMV_2009
;
Yao_Yen_PJM_2009
, and the references therein. The case of nonlinearly perturbed polyhedral convex sets has been considered in
Qui_JOTA_2012
.

It is well known that
calmness
is weaker than the local Lipschitz-like property. Calmness of the stationary point set map of a general parametric optimization problem has been considered, e.g., in
(
Guo_Lin_Ye_SIOPT2012
, Sect. 4)
.

The paper is divided into two parts. In this part, Sect.
2
recalls some basic concepts from variational analysis, formulates the problem studied herein, and presents a series of auxiliary results in a unified form. Sections
3
and
4
present new results on smooth parametric optimization problem with one smooth functional constraint under total perturbations. Namely, sensitivity analysis of the stationary point set at the interior points (resp., at the boundary points) of the progamming variable-parameter domain is given in Sect.
3
(resp., in Sect.
4
).

In Part 2, sufficient conditions for the Robinson stability of the stationary point set map will be established. This allows us to revisit and extend several stability theorems in indefinite quadratic programming. A comparison of our results with the ones which can be obtained via another approach will be also given.

Preliminaries

The scalar product and the norm in a finite-dimensional Euclidean space are denoted respectively by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and
\|\cdot\|
. The symbols
B(x,\rho)
and
\bar{B}(x,\rho)
stand for the open (resp., closed) ball centered at
x\in X
with radius
\rho>0
. The distance
\displaystyle\inf_{u\in A}\|x-u\|
from
x\in X
to a subset

is denoted by

.

We now recall several basic concepts from variational analysis
Rock_Wets_1998
;
B-M06
which will be used intensively later on.

The
Fréchet normal cone
(also called the
prenormal
cone
, or the
regular normal cone
) to a set

\Omega\subset I\!\!R^{s}
at
\bar{v}\in\Omega
is given by

\displaystyle\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{v})=\left\{v^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{s}\,\mid\,\displaystyle\limsup_{v\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{v}}\,\displaystyle\frac{\langle v^{\prime},v-\bar{v}\rangle}{\|v-\bar{v}\|}\leq 0\right\},

where

v\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{v}
means
v\to\bar{v}
with
v\in\Omega
. By
convention,
\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{v}):=\emptyset
when
\bar{v}\notin\Omega
.
Provided that
\Omega
is locally closed around
\bar{v}\in\Omega
, one calls

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rl}N_{\Omega}(\bar{v})&=\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{v\to\bar{v}}\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(v)\\
&:=\big\{v^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{s}\,\mid\,\exists\mbox{ sequences }v_{k}\to\bar{v},\ v_{k}^{\prime}\rightarrow v^{\prime},\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\ \mbox{with }v_{k}^{\prime}\in\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(v_{k})\,\mbox{ for all }\,k=1,2,\dots\big\}\end{array}

the
Mordukhovich
(or
limiting/basic
)
normal cone
to
\Omega
at
\bar{v}
.
If
\bar{v}\notin\Omega
, then one puts
N_{\Omega}(\bar{v})=\emptyset
.

A multifunction
\Phi:I\!\!R^{n}\rightrightarrows I\!\!R^{m}
is
said to be
locally closed
around a point
\bar{z}=(\bar{x},\bar{y})
from
\mbox{\rm gph}\,\Phi:=\{(x,y)\in I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R^{m}\,\mid\,y\in\Phi(x)\}
if
\mbox{\rm gph}\,\Phi
is locally closed around
\bar{z}
. Here, the product space

I\!\!R^{n+m}=I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R^{m}
is equipped with the topology generated by the sum norm
\|(x,y)\|=\|x\|+\|y\|
.

For any
\bar{z}=(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mbox{gph}\,\Phi
,

\displaystyle\widehat{D}^{*}\Phi(\bar{z})(y^{\prime}):=\big\{x^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{n}\,\mid\,(x^{\prime},-y^{\prime})\in\widehat{N}_{\mbox{gph}\,\Phi}(\bar{z})\big\}\quad(y^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{m})

are called the
Fréchet coderivative
values of
\Phi
at
\bar{z}
. Similarly, the
Mordukhovich coderivative
(limiting coderivative) values of
\Phi
at
\bar{z}
are defined by

\displaystyle D^{*}\Phi(\bar{z})(y^{\prime}):=\big\{x^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{n}\,\mid\,(x^{\prime},-y^{\prime})\in N_{\mbox{gph}\,\Phi}(\bar{z})\big\}\quad(y^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{m}).

Thus,
\widehat{D}^{*}\Phi(\bar{z})
and
D^{*}\Phi(\bar{z})
are multifuntions from
I\!\!R^{m}
to
I\!\!R^{n}
. By
(
B-M06
, Theorem 1.38)
, if
\Phi
is strictly Fréchet differentiable at
\bar{x}
, then

\widehat{D}^{*}\Phi(\bar{x})(y^{\prime})=D^{*}\Phi(\bar{x})(y^{\prime})=\{\nabla\Phi(\bar{x})^{*}(y^{\prime})\}

for any
y^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{m}
.

Suppose that
X
,
Y
, and
Z
are finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces. Consider a function
\psi:X\rightarrow\bar{I\!\!R}
with
|\psi(\bar{x})|<\infty
. The set

\partial\psi(\bar{x}):=\{x^{\prime}\in X^{*}\mid(x^{\prime},-1)\in N_{{\rm epi}\,\psi}(\bar{x},\psi(\bar{x}))\}

is the
Mordukhovich subdifferential
of
\psi
at
\bar{x}
. We put
\partial\psi(\bar{x})=\emptyset
if
|\psi(\bar{x})|=\infty
. The set

\partial^{\infty}\psi(\bar{x}):=\{x^{*}\in X^{*}\mid(x^{*},0)\in N_{{\rm epi}\,\psi}(\bar{x},\psi(\bar{x}))\}

is the
singular subdifferential
of
\psi
at
\bar{x}
. For a set
\Omega\subset X
and a point
\bar{x}\in\Omega
, we have

N_{\Omega}(\bar{x})=\partial\delta_{\Omega}(\bar{x})=\partial^{\infty}\delta_{\Omega}(\bar{x}),

where
\delta_{\Omega}(\bar{x})
is the indicator function of
\Omega
; see
(
B-M06
, Proposition 1.79)
. If
\psi
depends on two variables

and

, and
|\psi(\bar{x},\bar{y})|<\infty
, then
\partial_{x}\psi(\bar{x},\bar{y})
denotes the Mordukhovich subdifferential of
\psi(.,\bar{y})
at
\bar{x}
. For any
\bar{v}\in\partial\psi(\bar{x})
,

\partial^{2}\psi(\bar{x}|\bar{v})(u):=D^{*}(\partial\psi)(\bar{x}|\bar{v})(u)\quad(u\in X^{**}=X)

is the
limiting second-order subdifferential
(or the generalized Hessian).

A multifunction
G:Y\rightrightarrows X
is said to be
locally Lipschitz-like
around
(\bar{y},\bar{x})\in{\rm gph}\,G
if there exists a constant
\ell>0
and neighborhoods
U
of
\bar{x}
,
V
of
\bar{y}
such that

G(y^{\prime})\cap U\subset G(y)+\ell\|y^{\prime}-y\|\bar{B}_{X}\quad\forall y,y^{\prime}\in V,

where
\bar{B}_{X}
denotes the closed unit ball in
X
. When
G
is locally closed around
(\bar{y},\bar{x})
, the
Mordukhovich criterion
(see
B-M_TAMS_1993
,
(
Rock_Wets_1998
, Theorem 9.40)
, and
(
B-M06
, Theorem 4.10)
) says that
G
is locally Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{y},\bar{x})
if and only if

D^{*}G(\bar{y},\bar{x})(0)=\{0\}.

For a multifunction
F:X\times Y\rightrightarrows Z
and a pair
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in X\times Y
satisfying
0\in F(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, we say that the
implicit multifunction

G:Y\rightrightarrows X
given by

G(y)=\{x\in X\mid 0\in F(x,y)\}
has the
Robinson stability
at
\omega_{0}:=(\bar{x},\bar{y},0)
if there exist constants
r>0
,
\gamma>0
, and neighborhoods
U
of
\bar{x}
,
V
of
\bar{y}
such that

d(x,G(y))\leq rd(0,F(x,y))

for any
(x,y)\in U\times V
with
d(0,F(x,y))<\gamma
.
Note that the condition
d(0,F(x,y))<\gamma
can be omitted if
F
is
inner semicontinuous
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y},0)
; see
Huyen_Yen_2016
. Note that, in some cases, the Robinson stability of
G
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y},0)
implies its local Lipschitz-likeness around
(\bar{y},\bar{x})
; see, e.g., [8]. For the generalized linear constraint system studied in [11], these properties are equivalent. In the sequel, we will see that the regularity conditions in use guarantee for our stationary point set map to have both properties.

(-x^{\prime},w^{\prime})-\nabla^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}(v^{\prime}_{1},0)\in\Omega_{2}(v^{\prime}_{1}).

Although it is rather difficult to compute the set

Upper estimates
for the limiting coderivative values of

can be derived from a result of Levy and Mordukhovich
(

. But, a constraint qualification must be imposed to have these estimates (see

and

. Then,

S(w)=\{x\in I\!\!R^{n}\mid 0\in G(x,w)+M(x,w)\}.

(11)

Since
\bar{x}\in S(\bar{w})
,
\bar{\tau}:=(\bar{x},\bar{w},-\nabla_{x}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w}))
belongs to
{\rm gph}\,M
. Note that

is locally closed around
\bar{\tau}
. The following result combines the lower estimates with the upper estimates mentioned above.

Lemma 2.3

(see
(

, Theorem 3.4)
)

The lower estimates

\widehat{\Gamma}(x^{\prime})\subset\widehat{D}^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(x^{\prime})\subset D^{*}S(\bar{w}\mid\bar{x})(x^{\prime}),

where

\widehat{\Gamma}(x^{\prime}):=\displaystyle\bigcup_{v^{\prime}_{1}\in I\!\!R^{n}}\left\{w^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{d}\mid(-x^{\prime},w^{\prime})\in\nabla G(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}v^{\prime}_{1}+\widehat{D}^{*}M(\bar{\tau})(v^{\prime}_{1})\right\},

(12)

hold for any
x^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{n}
. If the constraint qualification

0\in\nabla G(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}v^{\prime}_{1}+D^{*}M(\bar{\tau})(v^{\prime}_{1})\ \;\Longrightarrow\ \;v^{\prime}_{1}=0\quad\quad

(
C1
)

is satisfied, then the upper estimate

D^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(x^{\prime})\subset\Gamma(x^{\prime}),

where

\Gamma(x^{\prime}):=\displaystyle\bigcup_{v^{\prime}_{1}\in I\!\!R^{n}}\left\{w^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{d}\mid(-x^{\prime},w^{\prime})\in\nabla G(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}v^{\prime}_{1}+D^{*}M(\bar{\tau})(v^{\prime}_{1})\right\},

is valid for any

. If, in addition, M is graphically regular at
\bar{\tau}
, then

\widehat{\Gamma}(x^{\prime})=\widehat{D}^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(x^{\prime})=D^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(x^{\prime})=\Gamma(x^{\prime}).

From Lemma
2.3
, for any

,
\widehat{\Gamma}(x^{\prime})\subset\widehat{D}^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(x^{\prime})
.
This implies that
\widehat{\Gamma}(0)\subset\widehat{D}^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(0)\subset D^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(0)
. If we put

\widehat{D}^{*}\widetilde{M}(\omega_{0})(v^{\prime}_{1})=\nabla G(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}v^{\prime}_{1}+\widehat{D}^{*}M(\bar{\tau})(v^{\prime}_{1})

for any
v^{\prime}_{1}\in I\!\!R^{n}
, where
\omega_{0}:=(\bar{x},\bar{w},0)\in{\rm gph}\,\widetilde{M}
. Therefore, we can write

\widehat{\Gamma}(x^{\prime})=\displaystyle\bigcup_{v^{\prime}_{1}\in I\!\!R^{n}}\left\{w^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{d}\mid(-x^{\prime},w^{\prime})\in\widehat{D}^{*}\widetilde{M}(\omega_{0})(v^{\prime}_{1})\right\}.

Note that
0\in\widehat{\Gamma}(0)
. According to the Mordukhovich criterion, if

is locally Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
, then
D^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(0)=\{0\}
and

as a result. In addition, if the constraint qualification (

for any

.
In particular,
D^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(0)\subset\Gamma(0)
. Hence, if (
C1
) is valid and
\Gamma(0)=\{0\}
, then

D^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(0)=\{0\}.

So, due to the Mordukhovich criterion,
S
is locally Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
. This idea has been presented in

and we will follow it throughout this paper.

Put
\mathcal{D}=\{(x,w)\in I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R^{d}\mid F(x,w)\leq 0\}

of

of

(\bar{x},\bar{w})
. Remind that
\bar{w}\in I\!\!R^{d}

3
Interior points

Suppose that
F(\bar{x},\bar{w})<0
, i.e.,
(\bar{x},\bar{w})
is an
interior point
of
\mathcal{D}
. A point
\bar{x}
belongs to

if and only if

0\in\nabla_{x}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})+N_{C(\bar{w})}(\bar{x}),

where

. Since

, the continuity of

\bar{x}\in{\rm int}\,C(\bar{w})

. Thus,

if and only if
\nabla_{x}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0

The inequality

\partial g(F(\bar{x},\bar{w}))=\{0\}

\bar{y}=0

\partial g(F(\bar{x},\bar{w}))

{\rm gph}\,\partial g=(I\!\!R_{-}\times\{0\})\cup(\{0\}\times I\!\!R_{+})

N_{{\rm gph}\,\partial g}(F(\bar{x},\bar{w}),\bar{y})=\{0\}\times I\!\!R.

Hence, for any
v^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R^{d}
,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial^{2}g(F(\bar{x},\bar{w})|\,\bar{y})(\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime})\\
&=D^{*}(\partial g(F(\bar{x},\bar{w})|\bar{y}))(\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime})\\
&=\{u^{\prime}\in I\!\!R\mid(u^{\prime},-\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime})\in N_{{\rm gph}\,\partial g}(F(\bar{x},\bar{w}),\bar{y})\}=\{0\}.\end{array}

Therefore,
\Omega_{1}(\bar{y},v^{\prime})=\{0\}
for any
v^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R^{d}
. So, from (
8
) it follows that

\partial^{2}f((\bar{x},\bar{w})|\bar{v})(v^{\prime})\subset\{0\}

for any
\bar{v}\in\partial f(\bar{x},\bar{w})
and
v^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R^{d}
. Since
\nabla_{x}f(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0
, invoking (
4
) and the fact that
\partial g(F(\bar{x},\bar{w}))=\{0\}
, we get

\Omega_{2}(v^{\prime}_{1})=\partial^{2}f((\bar{x},\bar{w})|0)(v^{\prime}_{1},0)\subset\{0\}

for any
v^{\prime}_{1}\in I\!\!R^{n}
.
Then, condition (
C0
) is fulfilled if

0\in\nabla^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}(v^{\prime}_{1},0)\ \;\Longrightarrow\ \;v^{\prime}_{1}=0.

By the symmetry of
\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})
and the equality
\nabla^{2}_{xw}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{T}=\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})
, this is equivalent to

\begin{cases}\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}=0\\
\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}=0\end{cases}\ \;\Longrightarrow\ \;v^{\prime}_{1}=0.

Clearly, the latter means that

{\rm ker}\,\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})\cap{\rm ker}\,\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})=\{0\}.

(14)

We now suppose that condition (
14
), which guarantees the validity of (
C0
), is satisfied. Then, by Lemma
2.2
,

D^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(x^{\prime})\subset\displaystyle\bigcup_{v^{\prime}_{1}\in I\!\!R^{n}}\{w^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{d}\mid(-x^{\prime},w^{\prime})-\nabla^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}(v^{\prime}_{1},0)\in\Omega_{2}(v^{\prime}_{1})\},

for any
x^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{n}
. Since
\Omega_{2}(v^{\prime}_{1})\subset\{0\}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{rl}&D^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(x^{\prime})\\
&\subset\Gamma_{1}(x^{\prime}):=\displaystyle\bigcup_{v^{\prime}_{1}\in I\!\!R^{n}}\left\{w^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{d}\mid\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}=-x^{\prime},\;w^{\prime}=\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}\right\}.\end{array}

Note that
0\in D^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(0)
. So, if
\Gamma_{1}(0)=\{0\}
, then
D^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(0)=\{0\}
; as a result,
S
is locally Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
by the Mordukhovich criterion. We have
\Gamma_{1}(0)=\{0\}
if and only if

\left\{w^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{d}\mid\exists v^{\prime}_{1}\in I\!\!R^{n}\ {\rm with}\ \nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}=0,\;w^{\prime}=\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}\right\}=\{0\}.

This can be rewritten equivalently as

{\rm ker}\,\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})\subset{\rm ker}\,\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w}).

(15)

It is easy to show that (
15
) and (
14
) hold simultaneously if and only if

{\rm ker}\,\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})=\{0\}.

(16)

In particular, (
16
) is a
sufficient condition
for

being locally Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
.

Example 3.1

Consider the problem
(P_{w})
with

, where

. Suppose that

with
\bar{w}:=(\bar{D},\bar{c},\bar{\rho})
and

Having the sufficient condition (

) for the Lipschitz-likeness of

around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
, we want to find a

for this property. We know that if

is locally Lipschitz-like around

\widehat{\Gamma}(0)=\{0\}
, where the sets have been defined in (

U
of
\bar{x}
and
W
of
\bar{w}

for any

for all

for
(x,w)
in a neighborhood of

S(w)\cap U=\{x\in I\!\!R^{n}\mid\nabla_{x}f_{0}(x,w)=0\}\cap U\quad(\forall w\in W).

(17)

Since
\widehat{D}^{*}M(\bar{\tau})(v^{\prime}_{1})=\{0\}
for any

, one has

\begin{array}[]{rl}\widehat{\Gamma}(x^{\prime})&=\displaystyle\bigcup_{v^{\prime}_{1}\in I\!\!R^{n}}\left\{w^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{d}\mid(-x^{\prime},w^{\prime})\in\nabla G(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}v^{\prime}_{1}\right\}\\

\widehat{\Gamma}(0)=\displaystyle\bigcup_{v^{\prime}_{1}\in I\!\!R^{n}}\left\{w^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{d}\mid(0,w^{\prime})\in(\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1},\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1})\right\}.

.

Example 3.2

Consider the problem
(P_{w})
with

In particular, for

The following theorem summarizes our results for the case of interior points.

Theorem 3.1

Suppose that
F(\bar{x},\bar{w})<0
. The following assertions are valid:

(a)

If
S
is locally Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
, then condition (
15
) holds;

(b)

If conditions (
14
) and (
15
) are simultaneously fulfilled, then
S
is locally Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
;

(c)

If condition (
14
) is satisfied, then
S
is locally Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
if and only if condition (
15
) holds.

Thus, if condition (
14
) fails we can assert nothing about the local Lipschitz-likeness of
S
around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
. The next example shows that
S
can be locally Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
when (
14
) is not satisfied,

Example 3.3

Consider
(P_{w})
with

f_{0}(x,w)=\frac{1}{3}x^{3}-w^{2}x
and

F(x,w)=x^{2}+w^{2}-1

for all
(x,w)\in I\!\!R\times I\!\!R
. Put
\bar{w}=0
. The point
\bar{x}=0
belongs to
S(\bar{w})
because
(\bar{x},\bar{w})\in{\rm int}\,D
and
\nabla_{x}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0
. Since
\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0
and

\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0,

condition (
14
) is invalid. We have known that
S
is locally defined by

\begin{array}[]{rl}S(w)&=\{x\in I\!\!R\mid\nabla_{x}f_{0}(x,w)=0\}.\\
&=\{x\in I\!\!R\mid|x|=|w|\}.\end{array}

Then, for any
x^{\prime}\in I\!\!R
,

\begin{array}[]{rl}D^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(x^{\prime})&=\{w^{\prime}\in I\!\!R\mid(w^{\prime},-x^{\prime})\in N_{{\rm gph}\,S}(0,0)\}.\\
&=\{w^{\prime}\in I\!\!R\mid|w^{\prime}|=|x^{\prime}|\}.\end{array}

It follows that
D^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(0)=\{0\}
. Thus,
S
is locally Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
.

around

Example 3.4

Consider
(P_{w})
with

f_{0}(x,w)=\frac{1}{4}x^{4}-w^{2}x
and

for all
(x,w)\in I\!\!R\times I\!\!R

\bar{w}=0

belongs to

Remark 3.2

The second assertion of Theorem
3.1

) and (

around
\bar{w}
for
\bar{x}

\bar{w}
. This means that there exist a neighborhood
W

\bar{w}

\bar{x}

Consider the extended stationary point set map

&&x^{\prime}=\nabla_{xx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})z^{\prime}\}.\end{array}

Hence,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}D^{*}\widetilde{S}((\bar{w},0),\bar{x})(0)=\{(w^{\prime},z^{\prime})\in I\!\!R^{d}\times I\!\!R^{n}&\mid&\nabla_{xx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})z^{\prime}=0\\
&&w^{\prime}=-\big(\nabla_{wx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})\big)^{T}z^{\prime}\}.\end{array}

Therefore,
D^{*}\widetilde{S}((\bar{w},0),\bar{x})(0)=\{0\}
if and only if
{\rm ker}\,\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})=\{0\}.
Thanks to Mordukhovich’s criterion, this shows that condition (
16
) is necessary and sufficient for the local Lipschitz-like
property of the the extended stationary point set map
\widetilde{S}
around
((\bar{w},0),\bar{x})
.

4
Boundary points

Suppose that
F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0
, i.e.,
(\bar{x},\bar{w})
is a boundary point of
\mathcal{D}
. To obtain a sufficient condition for the Lipschitz-like property of
S
around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
, we will follow the scheme which has been used in the case of interior points. We first need to find out a condition which guarantees the fulfillment of (
C0
). Note that
(MFCQ)
yields
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\neq 0
. Since
\partial g(F(\bar{x},\bar{w}))\subset I\!\!R
and

\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}\gamma=\gamma\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})

for any
\gamma\in I\!\!R
, there exists only one element
\lambda\in\partial g(F(\bar{x},\bar{w}))
satisfying

\nabla_{x}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})+\lambda\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0.

(20)

This element
\lambda
is the unique
Lagrange multiplier
for the stationary point
\bar{x}
of the minimization problem
\big(P_{\bar{w}}\big)
. Due to
\lambda\in\partial g(F(\bar{x},\bar{w}))
, we have
\lambda\geq 0
. Let us consider two possibilities of the Lagrange multiplier.

4.1 The nondegenerate case

Suppose that
\lambda>0
. Clearly, the equality
{\rm gph}\,\partial g=(I\!\!R_{-}\times\{0\})\cup(\{0\}\times I\!\!R_{+})
yields

N_{{\rm gph}\,\partial g}(F(\bar{x},\bar{w}),\lambda)=N_{{\rm gph}\,\partial g}(0,\lambda)=I\!\!R\times\{0\}.

By definition, for any
v^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R^{d}
,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial^{2}g(F(\bar{x},\bar{w})|\,\lambda)(\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime})\\
&=D^{*}(\partial g)(F(\bar{x},\bar{w})|\lambda)(\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime})\\
&=\{u^{\prime}\in I\!\!R\mid(u^{\prime},-\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime})\in N_{{\rm gph}\,\partial g}(F(\bar{x},\bar{w}),\lambda)\}.\end{array}

Hence

\partial^{2}g(F(\bar{x},\bar{w})|\,\lambda)(\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime})=\begin{cases}I\!\!R&\mbox{ if }\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}=0\\
\ \emptyset&\mbox{ if }\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}\neq 0.\end{cases}

So,

\Omega_{1}(\lambda,v^{\prime})=\begin{cases}\{\gamma\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\mid\gamma\in I\!\!R\}&\mbox{ if }\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}=0\\
\emptyset&\mbox{ if }\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}\neq 0.\end{cases}

For
\bar{v}:=\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}\lambda
, the conditions
\bar{y}\in\partial g(F(\bar{x},\bar{w}))
and
\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}\bar{y}=\bar{v}
force
\bar{y}=\lambda
. So, by (
8
) we get

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rl}\partial^{2}f((\bar{x},\bar{w})|\bar{v})(v^{\prime})&\subset\lambda\nabla^{2}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}+\Omega_{1}(\lambda,v^{\prime})\\
&=\lambda\nabla^{2}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}+\{\gamma\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\mid\gamma\in I\!\!R\}\end{array}

if
\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}=0
, and
\partial^{2}f((\bar{x},\bar{w})|\bar{v})(v^{\prime})=\emptyset
if
\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}\neq 0.
Since
\bar{y}=\lambda
is the unique element satisfying the conditions
\bar{y}\in\partial g(F(\bar{x},\bar{w}))
and

\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}\bar{y}=-\nabla_{x}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w}),

from (
10
) it follows that

\Omega_{2}(v^{\prime}_{1})\subset\left\{\lambda\nabla^{2}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})(v^{\prime}_{1},0)+\gamma\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\mid\gamma\in I\!\!R\right\},

(23)

for any
v^{\prime}_{1}
with
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}=0
. Besides, if
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}\neq 0
, then the set
\Omega_{2}(v^{\prime}_{1})
is empty. Thus, (
C0
) is fulfilled if the following is satisfied:
for any

v^{\prime}_{1}\in I\!\!R^{n}
,

if

\begin{cases}-\nabla^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}(v^{\prime}_{1},0)\in\left\{\lambda\nabla^{2}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})(v^{\prime}_{1},0)+\gamma\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\mid\gamma\in I\!\!R\right\}\\
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}=0,\end{cases}

then

v^{\prime}_{1}=0
.
Equivalently,
for any

v^{\prime}_{1}\in I\!\!R^{n}
,

if

\begin{cases}0=\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}+\lambda\nabla^{2}_{xx}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}+\gamma\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w}),\\
0=\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}+\lambda\nabla^{2}_{wx}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}+\gamma\nabla_{w}F(\bar{x},\bar{w}),\\
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}=0,\ \gamma\in I\!\!R,\end{cases}

then

v^{\prime}_{1}=0
.
Putting

A_{1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})+\lambda\nabla^{2}_{xx}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})&\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\end{array}\right]\in I\!\!R^{n\times(n+1)}

and

A_{2}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})+\lambda\nabla^{2}_{wx}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})&\nabla_{w}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\end{array}\right]\in I\!\!R^{d\times(n+1)},

where
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})
and

are interpreted as column vectors, we can rewrite the last condition equivalently as follows:

\begin{cases}A_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}v^{\prime}_{1}\\
\gamma\end{matrix}\right)=0,\quad A_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}v^{\prime}_{1}\\
\gamma\end{matrix}\right)=0\\
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}=0,\quad\gamma\in I\!\!R\end{cases}\quad\quad\Longrightarrow\ \;v^{\prime}_{1}=0.

Since
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\neq 0
, the latter is equivalent to saying that

{\rm ker}\,A_{1}\cap{\rm ker}\,A_{2}\cap\left({\rm ker}\,\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\times I\!\!R\right)=\{(0,0)\}.

(24)

, for any

Since

Theorem 4.1

Suppose that

and the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda
corresponding to the stationary point

Combining the conditions (

(\bar{w},\bar{x})
, that is

{\rm ker}\,A_{1}\cap\left({\rm ker}\,\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\times I\!\!R\right)=\{0\}.

Example 4.1

\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}>0,\end{cases}

or

\begin{cases}-x^{\prime}-\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}=0,\ \,w^{\prime}-\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}=0,\\
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}<0.\end{cases}

By putting

\Delta_{1}=\{(v^{\prime}_{1},\gamma)\in I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R\mid\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}>0,\,\gamma\geq 0\}

and

\Delta_{2}=\{v^{\prime}_{1}\in I\!\!R^{n}\mid\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}<0\},

we see that
\Gamma_{3}(x^{\prime})
consists of vectors
w^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{d}
such that

\begin{cases}w^{\prime}=\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}+\gamma\nabla_{w}F(\bar{x},\bar{w}),\\
\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}+\gamma\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=-x^{\prime},\\
(v^{\prime}_{1},\gamma)\in{\rm ker}\,\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\times I\!\!R,\end{cases}

or

\begin{cases}w^{\prime}=\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}+\gamma\nabla_{w}F(\bar{x},\bar{w}),\\
\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}+\gamma\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=-x^{\prime},\\
(v^{\prime}_{1},\gamma)\in\Delta_{1},\end{cases}

or

\begin{cases}w^{\prime}=\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1},\\
\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}=-x^{\prime},\\
v^{\prime}_{1}\in\Delta_{2}.\end{cases}

In particular, the set
\Gamma_{3}(0)
consists of vectors
w^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{d}
such that

\begin{cases}w^{\prime}=A^{\prime}_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}v^{\prime}_{1}\\
\gamma\end{matrix}\right),\ \,A^{\prime}_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}v^{\prime}_{1}\\
\gamma\end{matrix}\right)=0,\\
(v^{\prime}_{1},\gamma)\in{\rm ker}\,\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\times I\!\!R.\end{cases}

or

\begin{cases}w^{\prime}=A^{\prime}_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}v^{\prime}_{1}\\
\gamma\end{matrix}\right),\ \,A^{\prime}_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}v^{\prime}_{1}\\
\gamma\end{matrix}\right)=0,\\
(v^{\prime}_{1},\gamma)\in\Delta_{1}.\end{cases}

or

\begin{cases}w^{\prime}=\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1},\\
\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}=0,\,v^{\prime}_{1}\in\Delta_{2}.\end{cases}

Therefore,
\Gamma_{3}(0)=\{0\}
if and only if the following three conditions are simultaneously fulfilled

{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{1}\cap\left({\rm ker}\,\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\times I\!\!R\right)\subset{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{2},

(32)

{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{1}\cap\Delta_{1}\subset{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{2},

(33)

and

{\rm ker}\,\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})\cap\Delta_{2}\subset{\rm ker}\,\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w}).

(34)

Remember that under condition (
30
) we have the estimate (
31
). Therefore, the fulfillment of (
30
), (
32
), (
33
), and (
34
) implies
D^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(0)=\{0\}
. Applying the Mordukhovich criterion, we obtain the following
sufficient condition
for the local Lipschitz-likeness of
S
around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
:

\begin{cases}{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{1}\cap{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{2}=\{0\},\\
{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{1}\cap\left({\rm ker}\,\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\times I\!\!R\right)\subset{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{2},\\
{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{1}\cap\Delta_{1}\subset{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{2},\\
{\rm ker}\,\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})\cap\Delta_{2}\subset{\rm ker}\,\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w}).\end{cases}

(35)

Now, by the arguments of Qui
(
Qui_JOTA2014
, pp. 414–416)
, the Fréchet coderivative values of the multifunction
M
in (
11
) admit the lower estimate

\widehat{D}^{*}M(\bar{\tau})(v^{\prime}_{1})\supset\left\{\gamma\big(\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w}),\nabla_{w}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\big)\mid\gamma\geq 0\right\}

for any
v^{\prime}_{1}
with
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}\geq 0
. If
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}<0
, then
\widehat{D}^{*}M(\bar{\tau})(v^{\prime}_{1})=\emptyset
; see
(
Qui_JOTA2014
, p. 412)
. (It is important to stress that
we don’t need the condition
\nabla_{w}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\neq 0
here
.) So, by (
12
) one has

\widehat{\Gamma}(x^{\prime})\supset\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}(x^{\prime})
for any
x^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{n}
,
where

\begin{array}[]{rl}\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}(x^{\prime})&:=\displaystyle\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v^{\prime}_{1}\in I\!\!R^{n},\\
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\Big\{w^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{d}\mid(-x^{\prime},w^{\prime})\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\in\nabla G(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}v^{\prime}_{1}+\left\{\gamma\big(\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w}),\nabla_{w}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\big)\mid\gamma\geq 0\right\}\Big\}.\end{array}

Choosing
v^{\prime}_{1}=0
, we have
0\in\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}(0)
. In combination with the results in Lemma
2.3
, this yields

\{0\}\subset\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}(0)\subset\widehat{\Gamma}(0)\subset\widehat{D}^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(0)\subset D^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(0).

According to the Mordukhovich criterion, if
S
is locally Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
, then
D^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(0)=\{0\}
and
\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}(0)=\{0\}
as a result. Since

\nabla G(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}v^{\prime}_{1}=(\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1},\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}),

the latter means that

\begin{cases}w^{\prime}=\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}+\gamma\nabla_{w}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\\
\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}+\gamma\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0\qquad\quad\ \Longrightarrow\ \,w^{\prime}=0.\\
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}\geq 0,\ \,v^{\prime}_{1}\in I\!\!R^{n},\ \,\gamma\in I\!\!R_{+}\end{cases}

For
\Delta_{3}:=\{(v^{\prime}_{1},\gamma)\in I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R\mid\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}\geq 0,\,\gamma\geq 0\}
,
this condition becomes

\begin{cases}w^{\prime}=A^{\prime}_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}v^{\prime}_{1}\\
\gamma\end{matrix}\right)\\
A^{\prime}_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}v^{\prime}_{1}\\
\gamma\end{matrix}\right)=0\qquad\quad\ \Longrightarrow\ \,w^{\prime}=0.\\
\left(v^{\prime}_{1},\gamma\right)\in\Delta_{3}\end{cases}

Clearly, the last property is equivalent to

{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{1}\cap\Delta_{3}\subset{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{2}.

(36)

Thus, we have shown that (
36
) is a
necessary condition
for
S
being locally Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
.

Condition (
35
) implies (
36
). Indeed, suppose that (
35
) is fulfilled and
\left(v^{\prime}_{1},\gamma\right)\in{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{1}\cap\Delta_{3}
. If
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}=0
, then

\left(v^{\prime}_{1},\gamma\right)\in{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{1}\cap\left({\rm ker}\,\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\times I\!\!R\right);

so
\left(v^{\prime}_{1},\gamma\right)\in{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{2}
by the second condition in (
35
). If
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}>0
, then
\left(v^{\prime}_{1},\gamma\right)\in{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{1}\cap\Delta_{1}
; hence
\left(v^{\prime}_{1},\gamma\right)\in{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{2}
by the third condition in (
35
). We have thus proved that (
35
) yields (
36
).

The above elementary analysis clearly shows how the sufficient condition for the local Lipschitz-likeness of
S
around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
in the degenerate case is stronger than the necessary one.

We can summarize our results for the degenerate case as follows.

Theorem 4.2

Suppose that
F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0
and the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda
corresponding to the stationary point
\bar{x}\in S(\bar{w})
is null. The following assertions are true:

(a)

If
S
is locally Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
, then condition (
36
) holds;

(b)

If condition (
35
) is fulfilled, then
S
is locally Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
.

Let us consider a simple example to see how Theorem
4.2
works for concrete optimization problems.

Example 4.3

Let

f_{0}(x,w)=x^{2}(w-2)
and
F(x,w)=w(x-1)
for all
(x,w)\in I\!\!R\times I\!\!R
.
The stationary point set of
(P_{w})
is given by

S(w)=\{x\in I\!\!R\mid 0\in 2x(w-2)+\partial_{x}f(x,w)\},

with
f(x,w)=(g\circ F)(x,w)
and
g(y)=\delta_{I\!\!R_{-}}(y)
for all
y\in I\!\!R
. Let
\bar{w}=1
. Then, the point
\bar{x}=1
belongs to
S(\bar{w})
. Indeed, since
F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0
and
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=1
, condition
(MFCQ)
is valid. Hence, from (
5
) we have

\begin{array}[]{rl }\partial_{x}f(\bar{x},\bar{w})&=\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}N_{\mathbb{R_{-}}}(F(\bar{x},\bar{w}))\\
&=\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}\mathbb{R_{+}}=\mathbb{R_{+}}.\end{array}

Now it is easy to check that
\bar{x}\in S(\bar{w})
. Here we have

A^{\prime}_{1}=[\begin{array}[]{ccc}-4&1\end{array}]
,

A^{\prime}_{2}=[\begin{array}[]{ccc}2&0\end{array}]
, and

{\rm ker}\,\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=\{0\}
. Thus, condition (
35
) is fulfilled; as a result,
S
is locally Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
.

Remark 4.3

Looking back to Example
4.3
, we see that

\nabla_{w}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=\bar{x}-1=0.

So, the preceding results of Qui
(
Qui_JoGO_2016
, Theorem 4.2)
cannot be applied for the boundary point
(\bar{x},\bar{w})
of the set

\mathcal{D}=\{(x,w)\mid F(x,w)\leq 0\}=\{(x,w)\mid x\leq 1,\,w\geq 0\}\cup\{(x,w)\mid x\leq 1,\,w\geq 0\},

as it requires that
\nabla_{w}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\neq 0
.

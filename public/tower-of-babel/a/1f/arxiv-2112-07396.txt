Abstract
. The equality problem of the two-variable Bajraktarević means can be expressed as the functional equation ( f g ) − 1 ​ ( f ⁡ ( x ) + f ⁡ ( y ) g ⁡ ( x ) + g ⁡ ( y ) ) = ( h k ) − 1 ​ ( h ⁡ ( x ) + h ⁡ ( y ) k ⁡ ( x ) + k ⁡ ( y ) ) ( x , y ∈ I ) , \left(\frac{f}{g}\right)^{-1}\bigg(\frac{f(x)+f(y)}{g(x)+g(y)}\bigg)=\left(\frac{h}{k}\right)^{-1}\bigg(\frac{h(x)+h(y)}{k(x)+k(y)}\bigg)\qquad(x,y\in I), where I I is a nonempty open real interval, f , g , h , k : I → ℝ f,g,h,k:I\to\mathbb{R} are continuous functions, g g , k k are positive and f / g f/g , h / k h/k are strictly monotone. This functional equation, for the first time, was solved by Losonczi in 1999 under 6th-order continuous differentiability assumptions. Additional and new characterizations of this equality problem have been found recently by Losonczi, Páles and Zakaria under the same regularity assumptions in 2021. In this paper it is shown that the same conclusion can be obtained under substantially weaker regularity conditions, namely, assuming only first-order differentiability.

1.
Introduction

In this paper
I
will stand for a nonempty open interval and the classes of continuous strictly monotone and continuous positive functions defined on
I
will be denoted by
\mathscr{CM}(I)
and
\mathscr{CP}(I)
, respectively.

Given two functions
f,g:I\to\mathbb{R}
with
g\in\mathscr{CP}(I)
and
f/g\in\mathscr{CM}(I)
, the
two-variable Bajraktarević mean

\mathscr{B}_{f,g}:I^{2}\to I
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{f,g}(x,y):=\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)^{-1}\bigg(\frac{f(x)+f(y)}{g(x)+g(y)}\bigg)\qquad(x,y\in I).

The pair
(f,g)
is called the
generator of
\mathscr{B}_{f,g}
.
This notion was introduced, in a somewhat different form and for more than two variables, by Bajraktarević
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
in the late 50s. By taking
g\equiv 1
, we can see that this class of means includes quasiarithmetic means (cf.
[
5
]
).

One of the most basic questions related to this class of means is their
equality problem
, which is to find the solutions
f,g,h,k:I\to\mathbb{R}
of the functional equation

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{f,g}(x,y)=\mathscr{B}_{h,k}(x,y)\qquad(x,y\in I).

(1)

It is not difficult to prove that if there exist four real constants
a,b,c,d
with
ad-bc\neq 0
such that

\displaystyle h=af+bg\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad k=cf+dg

(2)

hold on
I
, then the means
\mathscr{B}_{f,g}
and
\mathscr{B}_{h.k}
are identical. Motivated by this observation, if the equalities (
2
) are satisfied by some constants
a,b,c,d
with
ad-bc\neq 0
, then we say that
the pair
(f,g)
is equivalent to
(h,k)
and we denote this property by
(f,g)\sim(h,k)
. For the equality of two Bajraktarević means with more than two variables the equivalence of their generators is not only sufficient but it is also necessary (cf.
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
). Surprisingly, in the setting of two-variable means, the situation is completely different. This case was investigated by Losonczi
[
6
]
,
[
10
]
who, assuming 6 times continuous differentiability, discovered that beyond the equality via equivalence of the generators, there are further 32 possibilities.

This result has been revisited by Losonczi, Páles, and Zakaria in
[
11
]
, where several new characterizations of the equality of two-variable Bajraktarević means (
1
) have been obtained. To recall this result, we need to introduce some further notations. If
f,g:I\to\mathbb{R}
are differentiable functions, then their Wronskian
W_{f,g}
is defined as

\displaystyle W_{f,g}:=f^{\prime}g-fg^{\prime}.

For a real parameter
\alpha
, let us define the functions
S_{\alpha},C_{\alpha}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
as follows:

\displaystyle S_{\alpha}(t)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{k}t^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\sin(\sqrt{-\alpha}t)}{\sqrt{-\alpha}}&\text{ if }\alpha<0,\\
t&\text{ if }\alpha=0,\\
\dfrac{\sinh(\sqrt{\alpha}t)}{\sqrt{\alpha}}&\text{ if }\alpha>0,\end{cases}

\displaystyle C_{\alpha}(t)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{k}t^{2k}}{(2k)!}=\begin{cases}\cos(\sqrt{-\alpha}t)&\text{ if }\alpha<0,\\[5.69054pt]
1&\text{ if }\alpha=0,\\[5.69054pt]
\cosh(\sqrt{\alpha}t)&\text{ if }\alpha>0.\end{cases}

We note that these functions form a fundamental system of solutions of the second-order homogeneous linear differential equation
Y^{\prime\prime}=\alpha Y
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
f,g,h,k:I\to\mathbb{R}
be 6 times continuously differentiable functions such that
g,k\in\mathscr{CP}(I)
and the derivatives
(f/g)^{\prime}
,
(h/k)^{\prime}
do not vanish in
I
. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

The means
\mathscr{B}_{f,g}
and
\mathscr{B}_{h.k}
satisfy the functional equation
(
1
)
.

(ii)

Either
(f,g)\sim(h,k)
or there exist real constants
\alpha
,
\beta
and
\gamma
such that

\displaystyle W_{f^{\prime},g^{\prime}}=\alpha\big(W_{f,g}\big)^{3},\qquad W_{h^{\prime},k^{\prime}}=\beta\big(W_{h,k}\big)^{3},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad W_{h,k}=\gamma W_{f,g}

hold on
I
.

(iii)

Either
(f,g)\sim(h,k)
or there exist real constants
a,b,c,A,B,C,\gamma
such that

\displaystyle af^{2}+bfg+cg^{2}=1,\qquad Ah^{2}+Bhk+Ck^{2}=1,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad W_{h,k}=\gamma W_{f,g}

hold on
I
.

(iv)

Either
(f,g)\sim(h,k)
or there exist two real polynomials
P
and
Q
of at most second degree which are positive on the ranges of
f/g
and
h/k
, respectively, and there exist real constants
\gamma
and
\delta
such that

\displaystyle g=\frac{1}{\sqrt{P}}\circ\frac{f}{g},\qquad k=\frac{1}{\sqrt{Q}}\circ\frac{h}{k},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\bigg(\int\frac{1}{Q}\bigg)\circ\frac{h}{k}=\gamma\bigg(\int\frac{1}{P}\bigg)\circ\frac{f}{g}+\delta.

(v)

Either
(f,g)\sim(h,k)
or there exist a continuous strictly monotone function
w:I\to\mathbb{R}
and real constants
\alpha
and
\beta
such that

\displaystyle(f,g)\sim(S_{\alpha}\circ w,C_{\alpha}\circ w)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad(h,k)\sim(S_{\beta}\circ w,C_{\beta}\circ w).

(vi)

Either
(f,g)\sim(h,k)
or
\mathscr{B}_{f,g}=\mathscr{B}_{w,1}=\mathscr{B}_{h.k}
holds on
I^{2}
with
w:=\int W_{f,g}
.

(vii)

Either
(f,g)\sim(h,k)
or there exists a continuous strictly monotone function
w:I\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathscr{B}_{f,g}=\mathscr{B}_{w,1}=\mathscr{B}_{h.k}
holds on
I^{2}
.

In fact, assertion (ii) of the above theorem is the corrected form of assertion (iv) of
[
11
, Theorem 15]
, where, unfortunately, the condition
(f,g)\sim(h,k)
was missing.

The equality problem of means in various classes of two-variable means has been solved. We refer here to Losonczi’s works
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
where the equality of two-variable means is characterized in several settings. A key idea in these papers, under appropriate order differentiability assumptions, is to calculate and then to compare the partial derivatives of the means at points of the form
(x,x)
, where
x\in I
. A similar problem, the mixed equality problem of quasiarithmetic and Lagrangian means was solved by Páles
[
13
]
. The equality problem of generalized Bajraktarević means was solved by Grünwald and Páles in
[
4
]
.

The main goal of this paper is to solve one of the open problems listed in the paper
[
12
]
. We show that conditions (i) and (iii)–(vii) of Theorem
1
are equivalent to each other under first-order continuous differentiability assumptions and thus, we substantially weaken the regularity requirements of Theorem
1
. The main difficulty to prove our result comes the fact that the validity of equality (
1
) does not imply the differentiability properties of the unknown functions
f,g,h,k
. Indeed, (
1
) can hold only if
(f,g)\sim(h,k)
. On the other hand, if (
1
) holds but
(f,g)\not\sim(h,k)
, then the functions
p,q,\varphi,\psi:J\to\mathbb{R}
which are constructed in Lemma
2
are infinitely many times differentiable.

The pairwise equivalence of conditions (i) and (iii)–(vii) suggests that statement of Theorem
1
(without assertion (ii) could be true even if we do not assume first-order continuous differentiability.

2.
Reduction of the equality problem and auxiliary results

In what follows, we reduce the equality problem of two-variable Bajraktarević means to the equality of a two-variable Bajraktarević mean and an arithmetic mean weighted by a weight function.

Lemma 2
.

Let
f,g,h,k:I\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous functions such that
g,k\in\mathscr{CP}(I)
,
f/g,h/k\in\mathscr{CM}(I)
. Then the equality
(
1
)
holds if and only if

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{q,p}(u,v)=\mathscr{B}_{\varphi,\psi}(u,v)\qquad(u,v\in J),

(3)

where

\displaystyle p:=k\circ\bigg(\frac{h}{k}\bigg)^{-1},\qquad q(u):=p(u)u,\qquad\varphi:=f\circ\bigg(\frac{h}{k}\bigg)^{-1},\qquad\psi:=g\circ\bigg(\frac{h}{k}\bigg)^{-1},\qquad J:=\big(\tfrac{h}{k}\big)(I).

Proof.

Equation (
1
) is equivalent to the functional equation

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{f}{g}\bigg)^{-1}\bigg(\frac{f(x)+f(y)}{g(x)+g(y)}\bigg)=\bigg(\frac{h}{k}\bigg)^{-1}\bigg(\frac{h(x)+h(y)}{k(x)+k(y)}\bigg)\qquad(x,y\in I).

(4)

Then, with the substitutions
x:=\big(\frac{h}{k}\big)^{-1}(u)
and
y:=\big(\frac{h}{k}\big)^{-1}(v)
, (
4
) implies

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\varphi}{\psi}\bigg)^{-1}\bigg(\frac{\varphi(u)+\varphi(v)}{\psi(u)+\psi(v)}\bigg)=\bigg(\frac{h}{k}\bigg)\circ\bigg(\frac{f}{g}\bigg)^{-1}\bigg(\frac{f(x)+f(y)}{g(x)+g(y)}\bigg)=\frac{h(x)+h(y)}{k(x)+k(y)}=\frac{p(u)u+p(v)v}{p(u)+p(v)}

(5)

for all
u,v\in J:=\big(\tfrac{h}{k}\big)(I)
. This proves that (
3
) is valid.

The proof of the reversed implication is similar.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varphi,\psi:J\to\mathbb{R}
be
n
-times continuously differentiable functions such that
\psi\in\mathscr{CP}(J)
and
\big(\frac{\varphi}{\psi}\big)^{\prime}
is nowhere vanishing. Then
\mathscr{B}_{\varphi,\psi}
is
n
-times continuously differentiable on
J^{2}
.

Proof.

Due to the assumptions, the function
\big(\frac{\varphi}{\psi}\big)^{-1}
is
n
-times continuously differentiable on
\frac{\varphi}{\psi}(J)
. By the chain rule, this implies that the mean
\mathscr{B}_{\varphi,\psi}
is
n
-times continuously differentiable on
J^{2}
.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varphi,\psi:J\to\mathbb{R}
be
n
-times continuously differentiable functions such that
\psi\in\mathscr{CP}(J)
and
\big(\frac{\varphi}{\psi}\big)^{\prime}
is nowhere vanishing. Let
p\in\mathscr{CP}(J)
and define
q:J\to\mathbb{R}
by
q(u):=p(u)u
. If
(
3
)
holds, then
p
is
n
-times continuously differentiable.

Proof.

To prove the
n
-times continuous differentiability of
p
at an arbitrarily fixed element
u_{0}\in J
, let
v_{0}\in J
be another fixed element which is distinct from
u_{0}
. Then, due to the strict mean property of Bajraktarević means, it follows that
\mathscr{B}_{\varphi,\psi}(u_{0},v_{0})\neq u_{0}
. Hence, for some neighborhood
U_{0}
of
u_{0}
, we have that
\mathscr{B}_{\varphi,\psi}(u,v_{0})\neq u
hold for all
u\in U_{0}
. By the equality (
3
), for
u\in U_{0}
, we get

\displaystyle p(u)=p(v_{0})\cdot\frac{v_{0}-\mathscr{B}_{\varphi,\psi}(u,v_{0})}{\mathscr{B}_{\varphi,\psi}(u,v_{0})-u}.

(6)

Using this formula, and applying Lemma
3
, we can see that
p
is
n
-times continuously differentiable on
U_{0}
, in particular, at
u_{0}
.
∎

3.
Main Results

First, we are going to establish various consequences of the equality (
3
).

Lemma 5
.

Let
p:J\to\mathbb{R}
and let
\varphi,\psi:J\to\mathbb{R}
be continuously differentiable functions such that
\psi,p\in\mathscr{CP}(J)
and
\big(\frac{\varphi}{\psi}\big)^{\prime}
is nowhere vanishing. Define
q:J\to\mathbb{R}
by
q(u):=p(u)u
. If
(
3
)
holds, then
p
is continuously differentiable and, for all
(u,v)\in J^{2}
,

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\Big(\varphi^{\prime}(u)(\psi(u)+\psi(v))-(\varphi(u)+\varphi(v))\psi^{\prime}(u)\Big)\Big(p^{\prime}(v)p(u)(v-u)+p(v)(p(u)+p(v))\Big)

(7)

\displaystyle=\Big(\varphi^{\prime}(v)(\psi(u)+\psi(v))-(\varphi(u)+\varphi(v))\psi^{\prime}(v)\Big)\Big(p^{\prime}(u)p(v)(u-v)+p(u)(p(u)+p(v))\Big).

Furthermore, there exists a nonzero constant
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle W_{\varphi,\psi}=\gamma p^{2}.

(8)

Proof.

By Lemma
3
and Lemma
4
,
p
and
\mathscr{B}_{\varphi,\psi}
are continuously differentiable on
J
and
J^{2}
, respectively. Differentiating the equation (
3
) with respect to the first and second variables, for all
(u,v)\in J^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\bigg(\bigg(\frac{\varphi}{\psi}\bigg)^{-1}\bigg)^{\prime}

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\varphi(u)+\varphi(v)}{\psi(u)+\psi(v)}\bigg)\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(u)(\psi(u)+\psi(v))-(\varphi(u)+\varphi(v))\psi^{\prime}(u)}{(\psi(u)+\psi(v))^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{\prime}(u)p(v)(u-v)+p(u)(p(u)+p(v))}{(p(u)+p(v))^{2}},

\displaystyle\bigg(\bigg(\frac{\varphi}{\psi}\bigg)^{-1}\bigg)^{\prime}

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\varphi(u)+\varphi(v)}{\psi(u)+\psi(v)}\bigg)\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(v)(\psi(u)+\psi(v))-(\varphi(u)+\varphi(v))\psi^{\prime}(v)}{(\psi(u)+\psi(v))^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{\prime}(v)p(u)(v-u)+p(v)(p(u)+p(v))}{(p(u)+p(v))^{2}}.

These equalities easily yield that (
7
) is true for all
(u,v)\in J^{2}
.

Now, using the definition of
W_{\varphi,\psi}
, the equality (
7
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\Big(2W_{\varphi,\psi}(u)+\varphi^{\prime}(u)(\psi(v)-\psi(u))-(\varphi(v)-\varphi(u))\psi^{\prime}(u)\Big)\Big(p^{\prime}(v)p(u)(v-u)+p(v)(p(u)+p(v))\Big)

\displaystyle=\Big(2W_{\varphi,\psi}(v)+\varphi^{\prime}(v)(\psi(u)-\psi(v))-(\varphi(u)-\varphi(v))\psi^{\prime}(v)\Big)\Big(p^{\prime}(u)p(v)(u-v)+p(u)(p(u)+p(v))\Big).

After rearranging this equality, we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{\big(W_{\varphi,\psi}/p\big)(v)-\big(W_{\varphi,\psi}/p\big)(u)}{v-u}

\displaystyle=W_{\varphi,\psi}(u)\frac{p^{\prime}(v)}{p(v)(p(u)+p(v))}+W_{\varphi,\psi}(v)\frac{p^{\prime}(u)}{p(u)(p(u)+p(v))}

\displaystyle-\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(v)(\psi(u)-\psi(v))-(\varphi(u)-\varphi(v))\psi^{\prime}(v)}{2(v-u)}\Big(\frac{p^{\prime}(u)(u-v)}{p(u)(p(u)+p(v))}+\frac{1}{p(v)}\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(u)(\psi(v)-\psi(u))-(\varphi(v)-\varphi(u))\psi^{\prime}(u)}{2(v-u)}\Big(\frac{p^{\prime}(v)(v-u)}{p(v)(p(u)+p(v))}+\frac{1}{p(u)}\Big).

Observe now that each term on the right hand side of this equality has a limit as
v
tends to
u
. Therefore, the limit of the left hand side must exist, showing that the function
w:=W_{\varphi,\psi}/p
is differentiable at
J
. Upon taking the limit as
v\to u
, we obtain

\displaystyle w^{\prime}(u)=\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}(u)w(u),

which implies that
w/p
is constant on
J
. Consequently, there exists
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
such that (
8
) holds on
J
. The constant
\gamma
cannot be zero, otherwise
W_{\varphi,\psi}
is identically zero, which contradicts that the derivative
\big(\frac{\varphi}{\psi}\big)^{\prime}
is nowhere vanishing. ∎

Lemma 6
.

Let
\varphi,\psi,p:J\to\mathbb{R}
be continuously differentiable functions such that
\psi,p\in\mathscr{CP}(J)
and
\big(\frac{\varphi}{\psi}\big)^{\prime}
is nowhere vanishing. Assume that the equality
(
7
)
is satisfied for all
(u,v)\in J^{2}
. Then there exists an open (possibly empty) subset
H\subseteq J

\varphi,\psi,p
are infinitely many times differentiable on
H

\displaystyle\gamma W_{q^{\prime},p^{\prime}}-W_{\varphi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}}=\gamma\frac{r^{\prime\prime}}{r}p^{2}

(9)

holds on
H

r

r^{\prime\prime}
vanishes on

r

u_{0}

u_{0}

Proof.

Assume that (
7

(u,v)\in J^{2}

\gamma\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\varphi}{\psi}\Big)^{\prime}=\frac{W_{\varphi,\psi}}{\psi^{2}}=\frac{\gamma p^{2}}{\psi^{2}}.

(10)

Thus, for all
(u,v)\in J^{2}

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(u)(\psi(u)+\psi(v))-(\varphi(u)+\varphi(v))\psi^{\prime}(u)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{\varphi}{\psi}\Big)^{\prime}(u)\psi(u)(\psi(u)+\psi(v))+\psi^{\prime}(u)\psi(v)\int_{v}^{u}\Big(\frac{\varphi}{\psi}\Big)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\gamma\bigg(\frac{p^{2}(u)(\psi(u)+\psi(v))}{\psi(u)}+\psi^{\prime}(u)\psi(v)\int_{v}^{u}\frac{p^{2}}{\psi^{2}}\bigg).

Using this formula, the equality (

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{p^{2}(u)(\psi(u)+\psi(v))}{\psi(u)}+\psi^{\prime}(u)\psi(v)\int_{v}^{u}\frac{p^{2}}{\psi^{2}}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{p^{\prime}(v)(v-u)}{p(v)}+\frac{p(u)+p(v)}{p(u)}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{p^{2}(v)(\psi(u)+\psi(v))}{\psi(v)}+\psi^{\prime}(v)\psi(u)\int_{u}^{v}\frac{p^{2}}{\psi^{2}}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{p^{\prime}(u)(u-v)}{p(u)}+\frac{p(u)+p(v)}{p(v)}\bigg).

Therefore, for all
(u,v)\in J^{2}
with
u\neq v
,
this is an equation of the following form:

\displaystyle A(u,v)\psi^{\prime}(u)+B(u,v)p^{\prime}(u)=C(u,v),

(11)

where

\displaystyle A(u,v)

\displaystyle:=\bigg(\frac{(p(u)+p(v))\psi(v)}{p(u)(u-v)}-\frac{p^{\prime}(v)\psi(v)}{p(v)}\bigg)\int_{u}^{v}\frac{p^{2}}{\psi^{2}},

\displaystyle B(u,v)

\displaystyle:=\frac{p^{2}(v)(\psi(u)+\psi(v))}{p(u)\psi(v)}+\frac{\psi(u)\psi^{\prime}(v)}{p(u)}\int_{u}^{v}\frac{p^{2}}{\psi^{2}},

\displaystyle C(u,v)

\displaystyle:=\bigg(\frac{p(u)}{\psi(u)}-\frac{p(v)}{\psi(v)}\bigg)\frac{(\psi(u)+\psi(v))(p(u)+p(v))}{u-v}

\displaystyle-\frac{p^{\prime}(v)p^{2}(u)(\psi(u)+\psi(v))}{p(v)\psi(u)}-\frac{(p(u)+p(v))\psi(u)\psi^{\prime}(v)}{p(v)(u-v)}\int_{u}^{v}\frac{p^{2}}{\psi^{2}}.

For a fixed
u\in J
, consider now the curve
\Gamma(u)
defined by

\displaystyle\Gamma(u):=\big\{(A(u,v),B(u,v))\colon v\in J\setminus\{u\}\big\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}

\displaystyle K:=\{u\in J\colon\dim\Gamma(u)=1\}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad H:=\{u\in J\colon\dim\Gamma(u)=2\}.

\displaystyle A(u_{0},v_{1})B(u_{0},v_{2})\neq A(u_{0},v_{2})B(u_{0},v_{1}).

\displaystyle A(u,v_{1})B(u,v_{2})\neq A(u,v_{2})B(u,v_{1}),

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}(u)=\frac{C(u,v_{1})B(u,v_{2})-C(u,v_{2})B(u,v_{1})}{A(u,v_{1})B(u,v_{2})-A(u,v_{2})B(u,v_{1})},\qquad p^{\prime}(u)=\frac{A(u,v_{1})C(u,v_{2})-A(u,v_{2})C(u,v_{1})}{A(u,v_{1})B(u,v_{2})-A(u,v_{2})B(u,v_{1})}

\displaystyle=q^{\prime}(u)p(u)-q(u)p^{\prime}(u)=(p(u)+up^{\prime}(u))p(u)-up(u)p^{\prime}(u)=p^{2}(u),

\displaystyle=q^{\prime\prime}(u)p^{\prime}(u)-q^{\prime}(u)p^{\prime\prime}(u)=(2p^{\prime}(u)+up^{\prime\prime}(u))p^{\prime}(u)-(p(u)+up^{\prime}(u))p^{\prime\prime}(u)=(2p^{\prime 2}-pp^{\prime\prime})(u).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\gamma p^{2}=W_{\varphi,\psi}=\varphi^{\prime}\psi-\varphi\psi^{\prime}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad 2\gamma p^{\prime}p=\big(W_{\varphi,\psi}\big)^{\prime}=\varphi^{\prime\prime}\psi-\varphi\psi^{\prime\prime}.

\displaystyle=\varphi^{\prime\prime}\psi^{\prime}-\varphi^{\prime}\psi^{\prime\prime}=\frac{2\gamma p^{\prime}p+\varphi\psi^{\prime\prime}}{\psi}\psi^{\prime}-\frac{\gamma p^{2}+\varphi\psi^{\prime}}{\psi}\psi^{\prime\prime}=\frac{2\gamma p^{\prime}p}{\psi}\psi^{\prime}-\frac{\gamma p^{2}}{\psi}\psi^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\lambda(u)\bigg(\frac{(p(u)+p(v))\psi(v)}{p(u)(u-v)}-\frac{p^{\prime}(v)\psi(v)}{p(v)}\bigg)\int_{u}^{v}\frac{p^{2}}{\psi^{2}}

\displaystyle+\mu(u)\bigg(\frac{p^{2}(v)(\psi(u)+\psi(v))}{p(u)\psi(v)}+\frac{\psi(u)\psi^{\prime}(v)}{p(u)}\int_{u}^{v}\frac{p^{2}}{\psi^{2}}\bigg)=0.

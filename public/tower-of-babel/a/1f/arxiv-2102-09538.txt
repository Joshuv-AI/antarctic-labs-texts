Ricci-Yang-Mills flow on surfaces and pluriclosed flow on elliptic fibrations Thanks: We gratefully acknowledge support from the NSF via DMS-1454854
Jeffrey Streets

Abstract
. We give a complete description of the global existence and convergence for the Ricci-Yang-Mills flow on T k T^{k} bundles over Riemann surfaces. These results equivalently describe solutions to generalized Ricci flow and pluriclosed flow with symmetry.



1.
Introduction

Suppose
\mathcal{G}\to M\to\Sigma
is the total space of a principal
\mathcal{G}
-bundle. Given a choice
h
of metric on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
\mathcal{G}
, a one-parameter family of metrics
g_{t}
on
\Sigma
and principal connections
\mu_{t}
satisfies the
Ricci-Yang-Mills flow (RYM flow)

[
39
,
43
]
if

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g=&\ -2\Rc_{g}+F_{\mu}^{2},\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mu=&\ -d^{*}_{g}F_{\mu},\end{split}

where
F_{\mu}
denotes the curvature of
\mu
, and
F_{\mu}^{2}=\tr_{h}\tr_{g^{-1}}F_{\mu}\otimes F_{\mu}
.
This system of equations has arisen in mathematical physics literature in the study of renormalization group flows. Also, this flow arises by modifying the Ricci flow of an invariant metric on a principal bundle to fix the metric on the fibers. This modification is a natural way to simplify the equation to understand the geometry of principal bundles, while of course the Ricci flow of invariant metrics is also natural in understanding collapsing limits and has been significantly studied (cf.
[
17
]
). Further results on RYM flow appear in
[
14
,
27
,
28
,
42
]
.

A second origin of these equations is as a symmetry reduction of the
generalized Ricci flow
(cf.
[
9
]
). Given a smooth manifold
M
, a one-parameter family of metrics
G_{t}
and closed three-forms
H_{t}
is a solution of generalized Ricci flow if

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}G=&\ -2\Rc_{G}+\tfrac{1}{2}H^{2},\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H=&\ \Delta_{d}H,\end{split}

where
H^{2}=\tr_{g^{-1}}\tr_{g^{-1}}H\otimes H
.
This system of equations also arises in the study of renormalization group flows
[
4
]
, coupling the Ricci flow for a metric with the natural heat flow for a closed three-form. As discussed in §
2.1
below, given a solution to RYM flow
(g_{t},\mu_{t})
over a Riemann surface, the pairs

\displaystyle G_{t}=\pi^{*}g_{t}+\tr_{h}\mu_{t}\otimes\mu_{t},\qquad H_{t}=\tr_{h}F_{\mu}\wedge\mu

satisfy generalized Ricci flow.

A third origin of Ricci-Yang-Mills flow is complex geometry, where it arises as a special case of
pluriclosed flow

[
36
]
. The pluriclosed flow is a natural evolution equation generalizing the Kähler-Ricci flow to complex, non-Kähler manifolds. Given
(M^{4},J)
a complex surface, a one-parameter family of Hermitian, pluriclosed metrics
\omega_{t}
satisfies pluriclosed flow if

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\omega=

\displaystyle\ -\rho_{B}^{1,1},

where
\rho_{B}
is the Ricci curvature of the Bismut connection. In
[
38
]
it was shown that after a certain gauge modification pluriclosed flow is in fact equivalent to generalized Ricci flow. Furthermore, it was shown in
[
35
]
that the pluriclosed flow of
T^{2}
-invariant metrics on complex surfaces reduces to the RYM flow. What is somewhat surprising is that the pluriclosed flow, defined in general with no symmetry considerations in mind, naturally fixes the metric on the Lie algebra and results in the RYM flow in this setting.

In this paper, building on prior results (cf.
[
28
]
), we give a complete description of the Ricci-Yang-Mills flow for
T^{k}
-bundles over Riemann surfaces. A complete description of the Ricci flow on Riemann surfaces was achieved by Hamilton/Chow
[
7
,
12
]
, with other approaches coming later (cf.
[
3
,
40
]
). Also, a complete description of Yang-Mills flow over Riemann surfaces was given by Råde
[
22
]
. We first state the main result describing the behavior of Ricci-Yang-Mills flow:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
T^{k}\to M\to\Sigma
denote a principal
T^{k}
-bundle over a Riemann surface
\Sigma
, and fix
h
a choice of metric on
\mathfrak{t}^{k}
. Let
g_{0}
denote a Riemannian metric on
\Sigma
and
\mu_{0}
a choice of principal connection. We let
(g_{t},\mu_{t})
denote the solution to Ricci-Yang-Mills flow with this initial data, and
G_{t}=\pi^{*}g_{t}+\tr_{h}\mu_{t}\otimes\mu_{t}
the associated one-parameter family of invariant metrics on
M
. The following hold:

(1)

If
\chi(\Sigma)<0
then
(g_{t},\mu_{t})
exists on
[0,\infty)
and
(M,\frac{G_{t}}{2t})
converges in the Gromov-Hausdorff topology to
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
, where
g_{\Sigma}
denotes the canonical metric of constant curvature
-1
.

(2)

If
\chi(\Sigma)=0
then
(g_{t},\mu_{t})
exists on
[0,\infty)
and
(M,\frac{G_{t}}{2t})
converges in the Gromov-Hausdorff topology to a point.

(3)

If
\chi(\Sigma)>0
and
c_{1}(M)=0
, then there exists
T<\infty
such that
(g_{t},\mu_{t})
exists on
[0,T)
, and
(M,\frac{1}{T-2t}G_{t})
converges in the
C^{\infty}
topology to
(\Sigma\times\mathbb{R},g_{\Sigma}\times g_{\mathbb{R}})
, where
g_{\Sigma}
denotes a metric of constant curvature
1
.

(4)

If
\chi(\Sigma)>0
, and
c_{1}(M)\neq 0
, then
(g_{t},\mu_{t})
exists on
[0,\infty)
and there are constants
\lambda_{i}
and a one-parameter family of diffeomorphisms
\phi_{t}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\phi_{t}^{*}g_{t}=

\displaystyle\ \lambda_{1}g_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\phi_{t}^{*}F_{\mu_{t}}=

\displaystyle\ \lambda_{2}\omega_{\Sigma},

where
g_{\Sigma}
denotes a metric of constant curvature
1
, and
\omega_{\Sigma}
denotes the associated area form.

Remark 1.2
.

Note that the qualitative behavior of the metric
g_{t}
in cases (1) and (2) agrees with the case of Ricci flow, regardless of the topology of the bundle. The cases (3) and (4), where
\chi(\Sigma)>0
, are more subtle. We recall that the Ricci flow on
S^{2}
with arbitrary initial data contracts in finite time to a round point
[
7
,
12
]
. In case the bundle is trivial, the RYM flow includes these Ricci flow lines as special cases, and case (3) shows that this behavior still holds in general, that is, the flow still converges to a round point, with
F_{\mu}\to 0
in the limit.

Considering homogeneous examples, one sees a basic qualitative difference in the case when the bundle is nontrivial, that is, when
F_{\mu}\neq 0
. Specifically, the
F_{\mu}^{2}
term in the evolution of
g
acts as a
restoring force
which doesn’t allow the sphere to collapse. Rather, along RYM flow the base will converge to a round sphere of fixed size depending on the Chern class of the bundle and the choice of
h
, without need for normalization, with
F_{\mu}
remaining fixed (note this indicates the necessity of the constants
\lambda_{i}
). Outside of the homogeneous setting one can hope that this behavior will still hold due to the topological nontriviality of the bundle. Case (4) of Theorem
1.1
confirms this behavior for all initial data.

Remark 1.3
.

The proofs of cases (1) and (2) follow from maximum principle arguments combined with monotonicity of a modified Liouville energy. On the other hand, the proofs of cases (3) and (4) are significantly more intricate. For case (3) we rely on a modified Perelman-type entropy functional to prove a
\kappa
-noncollapsing result for the metrics along the flow. Constructing a blowup limit at a singular time thus yields an ancient solution, which by maximum principle arguments can be shown to satisfy
F_{\mu}\equiv 0
, so that it is in fact a solution to Ricci flow. A result of Perelman
[
21
]
yields that this is then isometric to the shrinking sphere solution, yielding the claimed behavior. Case (4) requires studying a certain gauge-modified flow to allow for an application of Aubin’s improved Moser-Trudinger inequality. We furthermore use the topological hypothesis of nontriviality of the bundle to establish an a priori lower bound for the volume along the flow.

As discussed above, Ricci-Yang-Mills flow over Riemann surfaces is equivalently described by solutions to generalized Ricci flow, thus Theorem
1.1
has an immediate corollary:

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
T^{k}\to M\to\Sigma
denote a principal
T^{k}
bundle over a Riemann surface
\Sigma
, with
h
a choice of metric on
\mathfrak{t}^{k}
. Given
G_{0}=\pi^{*}g_{0}+\tr_{h}\mu_{0}\otimes\mu_{0}
an invariant metric on
M
, let
H_{0}=\tr_{h}F_{\mu_{0}}\wedge\mu_{0}
. Let
(G_{t},H_{t})
denote the unique solution to generalized Ricci flow on
M
with initial data
(G_{0},H_{0})
. Then
G_{t}=\pi^{*}g_{t}+\tr_{h}\mu_{t}\otimes\mu_{t},H_{t}=\tr_{h}F_{\mu_{t}}\wedge\mu_{t}
, where
(g_{t},\mu_{t})
is the solution to RYM flow with initial condition
(g_{0},\mu_{0})
. In particular, the existence and convergence properties are described as in Theorem
1.1
according to the topology of
M
.

Remark 1.5
.

As solutions to generalized Ricci flow with a torus symmetry, the flow lines in Corollary
1.4
are subject to the action of
T
-duality (cf.
[
8
,
9
,
31
]
). As an elementary example, homogeneous solutions on the unit tangent bundle over
\Sigma
are
T
-dual to solutions on the same bundle with
g
and
\mu
preserved, while the length of the circles is inverted.

Furthermore, due to the connection to pluriclosed flow described above, Theorem
1.1
has applications to complex geometry. Complex surfaces give the first examples of non-Kähler manifolds and among these, elliptic surfaces form a diverse and interesting class. Conjectures of the behavior of pluriclosed flow on these surfaces were described in
[
25
]
, and the next result confirms these conjectures in the case of invariant initial data on principal bundles, announced in
[
25
]
.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
(M,J)
be a compact complex surface which is the total space of a holomorphic
T^{2}
-principal bundle over a Riemann surface
\Sigma
.

(1)

Suppose
\chi(\Sigma)<0
. Given
\omega_{0}
an invariant pluriclosed metric on
(M,J)
, the solution to pluriclosed flow with initial data
\omega_{0}
exists on
[0,\infty)
, and
(M,\frac{\omega_{t}}{2t})
converges in the Gromov-Hausdorff topology to
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
, where
g_{\Sigma}
denotes the canonical metric of constant curvature
-1
.

(2)

Suppose
\chi(\Sigma)=0
. Given
\omega_{0}
an invariant pluriclosed metric on
(M,J)
, the solution to pluriclosed flow with initial data
\omega_{0}
exists on
[0,\infty)
, and
(M,\frac{\omega_{t}}{2t})
converges in the Gromov-Hausdorff topology to a point.

(3)

Suppose
(M,J)\cong S^{2}\times T^{2}
. Given
\omega_{0}
an invariant pluriclosed metric on
(M,J)
, let

\displaystyle T=(8\pi)^{-1}\Area_{\omega_{0}}(S^{2}).

The solution to pluriclosed flow with this initial data exists on
[0,T)
, and
(M,\frac{1}{T-2t}\omega_{t})
converges in the
C^{\infty}
topology to
(S^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\omega_{S^{2}}\times\omega_{\mathbb{R}^{2}})
.

(4)

Suppose
(M,J)
is a Hopf surface. Given
\omega_{0}
an invariant pluriclosed metric on
(M,J)
, the solution to pluriclosed flow with this initial data exists on
[0,\infty)
, and
(M,\omega_{t})
converges in the
C^{\infty}
topology to a multiple of
\omega_{\mbox{\tiny{Hopf}}}
, the standard Hopf metric.

Remark 1.7
.

(1)

This result confirms the natural maximal existence time conjecture for pluriclosed flow in this setting (
[
38
]
Conjecture 5.2)

(2)

Up to finite covers, all non-Kähler surfaces of Kodaira dimension
0
or
1
are total spaces of holomorphic
T^{2}
bundles, and admit invariant pluriclosed metrics and flow lines as described in items (1) and (2).

(3)

The surfaces in case (1) have Kodaira dimension
1
. The general behavior of the Kähler-Ricci flow on Kähler surfaces of Kodaira dimension
1
, analyzing the more subtle case of singular fibrations, is described in
[
24
]
.

(4)

The surfaces in case (2) have Kodaira dimension
0
, and include both Kähler and non-Kähler Kodaira surfaces.

(5)

The surfaces in cases (3) and (4) have Kodaira dimension
-\infty
. In fact, the only Hopf surfaces which are principal holomorphic
T^{2}
bundles are
standard
, described as
\mathbb{Z}
-quotients

\displaystyle S^{3}\times S^{1}\cong\mathbb{C}^{2}\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}/\left<(z_{1},z_{2})\to(\alpha z_{1},\beta z_{2})\right>,

where
\left|\alpha\right|=\left|\beta\right|<1
. These surfaces admit the Hermitian, pluriclosed, metric defined by the
\mathbb{Z}
-invariant Kähler form

\displaystyle\omega_{\mbox{\tiny{Hopf}}}=\rho^{-2}\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\rho^{2},

where
\rho=\sqrt{\left|z_{1}\right|^{2}+\left|z_{2}\right|^{2}}
is distance to the origin. The associated Riemannian metric is the standard cylindrical metric on
\mathbb{C}^{2}\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}\cong S^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
. We denote the metric on the quotient as
\omega_{\mbox{\tiny{Hopf}}}
, which is Bismut-flat.

Here is an outline of the rest of this paper. In §
2
we derive fundamental properties of RYM flow on surfaces, including a reduction of the metric component to a flow of conformal factors, a scalar reduction of the Yang-Mills flow component, a monotonicity formula for a modified Liouville energy, and the derivation of some evolution equations. We also recall a higher regularity estimate for the flow assuming certain uniform bounds on the base metric
g
. We also describe the symmetry reductions discussed above. Next in §
3
we record certain a priori estimates for the metric and Yang-Mills potential along the flow, adapted to each topological setting. In §
4
we give the proofs of the main theorems, case by case. The cases when
\chi\leq 0
are handled using the a priori estimates of §
3
. The case of
\chi(\Sigma)>0
and trivial bundle is handled by blowup argument, relying on a modification of Perelman’s entropy functional to this setting. Finally we address the case of
\chi(\Sigma)>0
and nontrivial bundle, relying on an a priori lower volume estimate and an estimate for the associated Sobolev constant.

2.
Background

2.1.
Setup and scalar reductions

Fix
T^{k}\to M\to\Sigma
a principal
T^{k}
bundle over a compact Riemann surface
\Sigma
. An invariant metric
G
on
M
is determined by a triple
(g,\mu,h)
, where
g
is a metric on
\Sigma
,
\mu
is a principal
T^{k}
-connection, and
h
is a family of metrics on
\mathfrak{t}^{k}
parameterized by
\Sigma
. Specifically,
G=\pi^{*}g+\tr_{h}\mu\otimes\mu
. We will make the further restriction that this family
h
is constant over
M
, thus determined by a choice of inner product on
\mathfrak{t}^{k}
. We will study a certain normalization of the Ricci-Yang-Mills flow system, namely we fix
\lambda\in\{-1,0,1\}
and consider

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g=&\ -2\Rc_{g}+F_{\mu}^{2}-\lambda g,\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mu=&\ -d^{*}_{g}F_{\mu},\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h=&\ -\lambda h.\end{split}

where
(F^{2}_{\mu})_{ij}=h_{IJ}g^{kl}F^{I}_{ik}F^{J}_{jl}
. We note that our normalized flow also scales the metric
h
. This is natural from the point of view of RYM flow as a symmetry reduction of flows on the total space of the bundle, and in particular it follows that the data described by (
2.1
) differs from the system by a rescaling of the associated metrics
G_{t}=\pi^{*}g_{t}+\tr_{h_{t}}\mu_{t}\otimes\mu_{t}
. To begin our analysis we first show that RYM flow over a Riemann surface can be described using a conformal factor on the base space and a
\mathfrak{t}^{k}
-valued function determining the principal connection.

Lemma 2.1
.

Given a solution to Ricci-Yang-Mills flow, one has
g_{t}=e^{u_{t}}g_{\Sigma}
, where

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u=

\displaystyle\ e^{-u}\left(\Delta_{g_{\Sigma}}u-R_{\Sigma}\right)+\tfrac{1}{2}\left|F_{\mu}\right|^{2}_{g,h}-\lambda.

Proof.

As
\Sigma
is a surface, it follows easily that

\displaystyle\Rc_{g}=\tfrac{1}{2}Rg,\qquad F_{\mu}^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\left|F_{\mu}\right|^{2}_{g,h}g.

It follows easily that the ansatz
g_{t}=e^{u_{t}}
is preserved, and furthermore

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u\right)g_{t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{t}=-\left(R-\tfrac{1}{2}\left|F_{\mu}\right|^{2}_{g,h}+\lambda\right)g_{t}.

Using the formula
R=e^{-u}\left(R_{\Sigma}-\Delta_{g_{\Sigma}}u\right)
, the result follows.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Given a solution to Ricci-Yang-Mills flow , and a background connection
\overline{\mu}
there exists a one-parameter family of
\mathfrak{t}^{k}
-valued functions
f_{t}
such that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mu_{t}=\overline{\mu}+d^{c}f,\qquad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}f=

\displaystyle\ \Delta_{g}f+\tr_{\omega}F_{\overline{\mu}}.

Proof.

Since
\mu_{0}
and
\overline{\mu}
are connections on the same bundle over a Riemann surface, using Hodge theory and a gauge transformation we can solve for a function
f_{0}
such that
\mu_{0}=\overline{\mu}+d^{c}f_{0}
. Given the solution to Ricci-Yang-Mills flow, we can solve for
f_{t}
as in the statement by the theory of linear parabolic equations, using initial data
f_{0}
. We then define
\widetilde{\mu}_{t}=\overline{\mu}+d^{c}f_{t}
and observe that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\widetilde{\mu}=

\displaystyle\ d^{c}\left(\Delta_{g}f+\tr_{\omega}F_{\overline{\mu}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ d^{c}\tr_{\omega}\left(F_{\overline{\mu}}+dd^{c}f\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ d^{c}\tr_{\omega}F_{\widetilde{\mu}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -d^{*}_{g}F_{\widetilde{\mu}}.

Thus
\widetilde{\mu}
satisfies the Yang-Mills flow with respect to the time-dependent metric
g
, and since
\widetilde{\mu}_{0}=\mu_{0}
and solutions to Yang-Mills flow are unique, it follows that
\mu_{t}=\widetilde{\mu}_{t}=\overline{\mu}+d^{c}f
, as required.
∎

Thus we have shown that the metric
G_{t}
is defined equivalently in terms of a conformal factor
u
on
\Sigma
and a
\mathfrak{t}^{k}
-valued function
f
, and this notation will be used throughout. Furthermore we will refer to the principal connection as
\mu_{f}:=\overline{\mu}+d^{c}f
, where
\overline{\mu}
is some background connection.

2.2.
Energy functional

We next exhibit a gradient formulation for Ricci-Yang-Mills flow in this setting. From
[
40
]
we know that the Ricci flow on surfaces is the gradient flow of the Liouville energy. A generalization of this to Ricci-Yang-Mills flow was shown in
[
28
]
, and below we give a minor modification of this to account for the scaling parameter
\lambda
.

Definition 2.3
.

Given data
(u,f,h)
as above, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(u,f,h)=

\displaystyle\ \int_{\Sigma}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\left|du\right|^{2}+e^{-u}\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|^{2}_{g_{\Sigma},h}\right)dV_{\Sigma}+R_{\Sigma}\int_{\Sigma}udV_{\Sigma}+\lambda\int_{\Sigma}e^{u}dV_{\Sigma}.

Proposition 2.4
.

Given a solution to Ricci-Yang-Mills flow, one has

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{F}(u_{t},f_{t},h_{t})=

\displaystyle\ -\int_{\Sigma}e^{u}\dot{u}^{2}dV_{\Sigma}-\lambda\int_{\Sigma}e^{-u}\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|^{2}_{g_{\Sigma},h}dV_{\Sigma}-2\int_{\Sigma}e^{-2u}\left<\nabla^{g}F_{\mu_{f}},\nabla^{g}F_{\mu_{f}}\right>_{g_{\Sigma},h}dV_{\Sigma}.

Proof.

First we compute

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Sigma}\tfrac{1}{2}\left|du\right|^{2}dV_{\Sigma}=

\displaystyle\ \int_{\Sigma}\left<d\dot{u},du\right>dV_{\Sigma}=-\int_{\Sigma}\dot{u}\Delta_{g_{\Sigma}}udV_{\Sigma}

Next

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Sigma}e^{-u}\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|^{2}_{g_{\Sigma},h}dV_{\Sigma}=

\displaystyle\ \int_{\Sigma}\left[\left(-\dot{u}-\lambda\right)e^{-u}\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|^{2}_{g_{\Sigma},h}+2e^{-u}\left<\Delta_{g}F_{\mu_{f}},F_{\mu_{f}}\right>_{g_{\Sigma},h}\right]dV_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \int_{\Sigma}\left[\left(\dot{u}+\lambda\right)\left(-e^{-u}\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|^{2}_{g_{\Sigma},h}\right)-2e^{-2u}\left<\nabla^{g}F_{\mu_{f}},\nabla^{g}F_{\mu_{f}}\right>_{g_{\Sigma},h}\right]dV_{\Sigma}.

Lastly

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(R_{\Sigma}\int_{\Sigma}udV_{\Sigma}+\lambda\int_{\Sigma}e^{u}dV_{\Sigma}\right)=\int_{\Sigma}\left(R_{\Sigma}\dot{u}+\lambda\dot{u}e^{u}\right)dV_{\Sigma}.

Combining these yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{F}(u_{t},f_{t})=

\displaystyle\ \int_{\Sigma}\dot{u}\left(-\Delta_{g_{\Sigma}}u+R_{\Sigma}-e^{-u}\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|^{2}_{g_{\Sigma},h}+\lambda e^{u}\right)dV_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle\ -\lambda\int_{\Sigma}e^{-u}\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|^{2}_{g_{\Sigma},h}dV_{\Sigma}-2\int_{\Sigma}e^{-2u}\left<\nabla^{g}F_{\mu_{f}},\nabla^{g}F_{\mu_{f}}\right>_{g_{\Sigma},h}dV_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -\int_{\Sigma}e^{u}\dot{u}^{2}dV_{\Sigma}-\lambda\int_{\Sigma}e^{-u}\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|^{2}_{g_{\Sigma},h}dV_{\Sigma}-2\int_{\Sigma}e^{-2u}\left<\nabla^{g}F_{\mu_{f}},\nabla^{g}F_{\mu_{f}}\right>_{g_{\Sigma},h}dV_{\Sigma},

as claimed.
∎

2.3.
Higher Regularity

One key application of the energy monotonicity of Proposition
2.4
is to obtain higher regularity estimates for the flow in the presence of certain bounds.

Proposition 2.5
.

Given a solution
(g_{t},\mu_{f_{t}},h_{t})
to (
2.1
), suppose there exists a constant
C>0
so that for all
t>0
,

(2.4)

\displaystyle C_{S}(g_{t})\leq C,\qquad C^{-1}\leq\Vol(g_{t})\leq C,\qquad\left|\left|du\right|\right|_{L^{2}}\leq C,\qquad\mathcal{F}(u_{t},f_{t},h_{t})\leq C.

There exists
\epsilon,A
depending on
C
so that if
[t_{0},t_{1}]
is a time interval such that

\displaystyle\left|t_{1}-t_{0}\right|+\mathcal{F}(u_{t_{0}},f_{t_{0}},h_{t_{0}})-\mathcal{F}(u_{t_{1}},f_{t_{1}},h_{t_{1}})\leq\epsilon,

then

\displaystyle\sup_{[t_{0},t_{1}]}\left(\left|\left|u\right|\right|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\left|\left|e^{u}\left|\nabla^{g}F_{\mu_{f}}\right|_{g}\right|\right|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leq A\left(\left|\left|u(t_{0})\right|\right|_{H^{2}}+\left|\left|e^{u}\left|\nabla^{g}F_{\mu_{f}}\right|_{g}\right|(t_{0})\right|_{L^{2}}^{2}+1\right).

Moreover, if the assumptions (
2.4
) hold on a finite time interval
[0,T)
, then the flow extends smoothly past time
T
.

Proof.

This is established in (
[
28
]
§4), and is easily modified to account for the normalizations we have chosen here.
∎

2.4.
Symmetry Reductions

In this subsection we record two ways in which the Ricci-Yang-Mills flow on Riemann surfaces arises via considering natural flow equations in higher dimensions with symmetries. First, certain generalized Ricci flow lines on a three-manifold with a principal
S^{1}
symmetry reduce to Ricci-Yang-Mills flow on the base space. Also, invariant solutions to pluriclosed flow on complex surfaces which are principal
T^{2}
bundles reduce to Ricci-Yang-Mills flow on the base space. These reductions show how to obtain Corollaries
1.4
and
1.6
from Theorem
1.1
, but are also instrumental in showing the behavior of the Ricci-Yang-Mills flow in the most difficult case of the sphere.

2.4.1.
T^{k}
-invariant generalized Ricci flow over Riemann surfaces

As described in the introduction, the
generalized Ricci flow
is the parabolic system for a Riemannian metric
G
and closed three-form
H
defined by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}G=

\displaystyle\ -2\Rc_{G}+\tfrac{1}{2}H^{2},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H=

\displaystyle\ \Delta_{d}H.

This is a parabolic system of equations and basic regularity and long-time existence obstructions have been established in
[
26
]
. A Perelman-type
\mathcal{F}
-functional for this system was found in
[
19
]
, and an expander entropy functional was found in
[
26
]
. Some recent results in the homogeneous setting have appeared
[
20
]
, as well as a stability result near Ricci-flat metrics
[
23
]
. In
[
9
]
it was shown that the equation reduces to Ricci-Yang-Mills flow in the case of a
U(1)
principal bundle over a Riemann surface. This extends to
T^{k}
bundles:

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
T^{k}\to M\to\Sigma
be a principal
T^{k}
-bundle over a Riemann surface, and suppose
(g_{t},\mu_{t})
is a solution of Ricci-Yang-Mills flow (
1.1
). Let

\displaystyle G_{t}=\pi^{*}g+\tr_{h}\mu_{t}\otimes\mu_{t},\quad H_{t}=\tr_{h}F_{\mu_{t}}\wedge\mu_{t}.

Then
(G_{t},{H}_{t})
is a solution of generalized Ricci flow.

Proof.

The proof is identical to (cf.
[
9
]
Prop 4.39), which is written for the case
k=1
but generalizes immediately to the case of arbitrary
k
.
∎

2.4.2.
T^{2}
-invariant pluriclosed flow on complex surfaces

Given a complex manifold
(M^{2n},J)
, a Hermitian metric
g
is
pluriclosed
if the associated Kähler form
\omega
satisfies
\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\omega=0
. For a pluriclosed metric we define
H=-d^{c}\omega=\sqrt{-1}(\partial-\overline{\partial})\omega
, noting
dH=0
. There is a Hermitian connection on
TM
, the
Bismut connection
, defined by
\nabla^{B}=\nabla+g^{-1}H
. This has an associated curvature tensor
\Omega^{B}
, and the Bismut-Ricci form is the natural contraction

\displaystyle\rho_{B}=\tfrac{1}{2}\tr\Omega^{B}J.

The
pluriclosed flow
is the equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\omega=

\displaystyle\ -\rho_{B}^{1,1}.

This is a parabolic equation
[
36
]
, which solves Kähler-Ricci flow if the initial metric is Kähler. Furthermore, after a gauge modification, the associated pairs
(g_{t},H_{t})
of metrics and three-forms are a solution of generalized Ricci flow
[
38
]
. Global existence and convergence results for pluriclosed flow have appeared in
[
15
,
29
,
30
]
. Pluriclosed flow also preserves generalized Kähler geometry
[
37
]
, and global existence and convergence results have been shown in this setting
[
1
,
34
,
33
,
32
]
, and specifically the results of
[
34
]
overlap partially with Corollary
1.6
.

Let us now restrict to the case where
(M^{4},J)
is a complex surface which is the total space of a holomorphic principal
T^{2}
bundle over a base manifold
\Sigma
. Let
Z,W
denote canonical vertical vector fields associated to a basis
\mathfrak{Z},\mathfrak{W}
for the torus action, such that
W=JZ
. Let
g
denote an invariant Hermitian metric on
J
, and let
\left<,\right>
denote a metric on
\mathfrak{t}^{2}
such that
\left<\mathfrak{Z},\mathfrak{W}\right>=0
. As explained in
[
35
]
, a choice of invariant Hermitian metric
G
is equivalent to a triple
(g,\mu,\psi)
, where

\displaystyle\psi=

\displaystyle\ G(Z,Z)=G(W,W),

\displaystyle\mu(X)=

\displaystyle\ \psi^{-1}g(X,Z)\mathfrak{Z}+\psi^{-1}g(X,W)\mathfrak{W},

\displaystyle g(X,Y)=

\displaystyle\ G(X,Y)-\psi\left<\mu(X),\mu(Y)\right>.

Furthermore, the metric is pluriclosed if and only if
\psi
is constant. Here
\psi\left<,\right>
is playing the role of the metric
h
as described above. By general principle the pluriclosed flow will preserve the
T^{2}
symmetry, and the fact that
\psi
is constant. As shown in (
[
35
]
Lemma 6.2), the natural Hermitian connection is determined by functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
where

\displaystyle\mu_{f}=\left(\mu^{\mathfrak{Z}}+d^{c}f_{1}+df_{2}\right)\otimes\mathfrak{Z}+\left(\mu^{\mathfrak{W}}+d^{c}f_{2}-df_{1}\right)\otimes W.

Furthermore, the symmetry reduced equations are gauge-equivalent to the Ricci-Yang-Mills flow:

Proposition 2.7
.

(cf.
[
35
]
Proposition 6.3) Given
(M^{4},J)
as above, a one-parameter family of
T^{2}
invariant metrics
G_{t}=\pi^{*}g_{t}+\tr_{h_{t}}\mu_{f}\otimes\mu_{f}
is a solution to normalized pluriclosed flow if and only if

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\partial g}{\partial t}=&\ -\left(R-\psi\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|^{2}+\lambda\right)g^{T},\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}f_{1}=&\ \tr_{\omega}F^{\mathfrak{Z}}_{\mu_{f}}=\Delta_{g}f_{1}+\tr_{\omega}F^{\mathfrak{Z}}_{\mu},\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}f_{2}=&\ \tr_{\omega}F^{\mathfrak{W}}_{\mu_{f}}=\Delta_{g}f_{2}+\tr_{\omega}F^{\mathfrak{W}}_{\mu},\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi=&\ -\lambda\psi.\end{split}

The proof in
[
35
]
is by direct computation, but a more conceptual proof for a more general case can be given as follows. Suppose
T^{k}\to M\to\Sigma
is a principal
T^{k}
bundle. By taking a product with a trivial
T^{k}
bundle we obtain a
T^{2k}
bundle
\widetilde{M}
over
\Sigma
, which admits a natural complex structure induced by a choice of complex structure on
\mathfrak{t}^{2k}
determined by the natural splitting. We can extend a choice of principal connection on
M
using a flat connection to determine a principal connection
\widetilde{\mu}
on
\widetilde{M}
. Letting
\xi^{i}
denote a basis for
T^{k}
, it follows that the
(1,1)
form

\displaystyle\omega=\pi^{*}\omega_{g}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu^{\xi_{i}}\wedge\mu^{J\xi_{i}}

is positive, and moreover
H=-d^{c}\omega=\sum_{i=1}^{k}F^{\xi_{i}}\wedge\mu^{\xi_{i}}
. Since the solution to pluriclosed flow is equivalent to generalized Ricci flow after a gauge transformation (
[
38
]
), it follows from Proposition
2.6
that, up to a gauge transformation, the solution to pluriclosed flow is the same as the associated solution to Ricci-Yang-Mills flow with initial data
(g,\mu)
on
\Sigma
.

3.
A priori estimates

In this section we establish a priori estimates building towards the global existence claims of Theorem
1.1
. In all cases we will choose a background conformal metric
g_{\Sigma}
with constant scalar curvature. With this choice we obtain a solution
u_{t}
to the flow of conformal factors (
2.2
) by Lemma
2.1
. Furthermore using Hodge theory we can choose a background connection
\overline{\mu}
such that

\displaystyle F_{\overline{\mu}}\equiv\omega_{\Sigma}\otimes\zeta\in\Lambda^{2}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{t}^{k}.

With this choice we apply Lemma
2.2
to obtain a solution
f_{t}
to the potential flow (
2.3
).

3.1.
Evolution equations

Lemma 3.1
.

Given a solution to Ricci-Yang-Mills flow, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)e^{-u}=

\displaystyle\ -\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}_{g_{t}}e^{-u}+R_{\Sigma}e^{-2u}-\tfrac{1}{2}\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|_{g_{t},h_{t}}^{2}e^{-u}+\lambda e^{-u}.

Proof.

We directly compute using Lemma
2.1

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}e^{-u}=

\displaystyle\ -e^{-u}\left(\Delta u-e^{-u}R_{\Sigma}+\tfrac{1}{2}\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{t}}-\lambda\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \Delta e^{-u}-\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}_{g_{t}}e^{-u}+R_{\Sigma}e^{-2u}-\tfrac{1}{2}\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|_{g_{t},h_{t}}^{2}e^{-u}+\lambda e^{-u}.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Given a solution to Ricci-Yang-Mills flow, we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}=&\ -2\left|\nabla^{2}f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}-\tfrac{1}{2}\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|_{g_{t},h_{t}}^{2}\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}+\lambda\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}\\
&\ +2\left<\nabla e^{-u}\otimes\tr_{\omega_{\Sigma}}F_{\overline{\mu}},\nabla f\right>_{g_{t},h_{0}}.\end{split}

Proof.

We directly compute using Lemma
2.1
, Lemma
2.2
, and the Bochner formula

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}=

\displaystyle\ \left(R-\tfrac{1}{2}\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|_{g_{t},h_{t}}^{2}+\lambda\right)\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}+2\left<\nabla\left(\Delta f+\tr_{\omega}F_{\overline{\mu}}\right),\nabla f\right>_{g_{t},h_{0}}

\displaystyle\ -2\left<\Delta\nabla f,\nabla f\right>_{g_{t},h_{0}}-2\left|\nabla^{2}f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -2\left|\nabla^{2}f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}-\tfrac{1}{2}\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|_{g_{t},h_{t}}^{2}\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}+\lambda\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}

\displaystyle\ +2\left<\nabla e^{-u}\otimes\tr_{\omega_{\Sigma}}F_{\overline{\mu}},\nabla f\right>_{g_{t},h_{0}},

as claimed.
∎

3.2.
The case
\chi(\Sigma)<0

Assume
\chi(\Sigma)<0
, so that by the uniformization theorem we may choose the background metric
g_{\Sigma}
so that
R_{g_{\Sigma}}=-1
. In this case we also set
\lambda=1
, and these choices hold throughout this subsection.

Proposition 3.3
.

Given a solution to Ricci-Yang-Mills flow with
\chi(\Sigma)<0
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{M\times\{t\}}\left(e^{-u}-1\right)\leq

\displaystyle\ e^{-t}\sup_{M\times\{0\}}\left(e^{-u}-1\right).

Proof.

Specializing Lemma
3.1
to the case
R_{\Sigma}=-1
,
\lambda=1
, and dropping negative terms yields

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\left(e^{-u}-1\right)\leq

\displaystyle\ -(e^{-u})^{2}+e^{-u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -\left(e^{-u}-1\right)-\left(e^{-u}-1\right)^{2}.

The result follows from the maximum principle.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Given a solution to Ricci-Yang-Mills flow with
\chi(\Sigma)<0
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{M\times\{t\}}\left|f\right|_{h_{0}}\leq C(1+t).

Proof.

Using the a priori estimate of Proposition
3.3
we see by Lemma
2.2
that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)f=

\displaystyle\ \tr_{\omega}F_{\overline{\mu}}\leq C.

The upper bound follows by the maximum principle, and the lower bound is similar.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Given a solution to Ricci-Yang-Mills flow with
\chi(\Sigma)<0
, there exists a constant
A>0
so that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)

\displaystyle\left(A(e^{-u}-1)+e^{-t}\left|\nabla f\right|_{g_{t},h_{0}}^{2}+e^{-t}\left|f\right|_{h_{0}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ -\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|_{g_{t},h_{t}}^{2}-\frac{C^{-1}A}{2}\left|\nabla e^{-u}\right|^{2}_{g_{t}}-e^{-t}\left|\nabla f\right|_{g_{t},h_{0}}^{2}-\frac{A}{2}e^{-2u}+2Ae^{-u}.

Proof.

Fix
A>0
and let
\Phi=A\left(e^{-u}-1\right)+e^{-t}\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}+e^{-t}|f|_{h_{0}}^{2}
. By combining Lemmas
2.2
,
3.1
, and
3.2
we obtain

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\Phi=&\ A\left\{-\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}_{g_{t}}e^{-u}-\tfrac{1}{2}\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|_{g_{t},h_{t}}^{2}e^{-u}-e^{-2u}+e^{-u}\right\}\\
&\ +e^{-t}\left\{-2\left|\nabla^{2}f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}-\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|_{g_{t},h_{t}}^{2}\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}+2\left<\nabla e^{-u}\otimes\tr_{\omega_{\Sigma}}F_{\mu},\nabla f\right>_{g_{t},h_{0}}\right\}\\
&\ -2e^{-t}\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}+2e^{-t}e^{-u}\left<\tr_{\omega_{\Sigma}}F_{\overline{\mu}},f\right>_{h_{0}}-e^{-t}|f|_{h_{0}}^{2}\\
\leq&\ -C^{-1}A\left|\nabla e^{-u}\right|^{2}_{g_{t}}+A\left(e^{-u}-e^{-2u}\right)\\
&\ -2e^{-t}\left|\nabla^{2}f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}+Ce^{-t}\left|\nabla e^{-u}\right|_{g_{t}}\left|\nabla f\right|_{g_{t},h_{0}}-2e^{-t}\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}\\
&\ +Ce^{-t}e^{-u}\left|f\right|_{h_{0}}-e^{-t}|f|_{h_{0}}^{2}\\
\leq&\ -2e^{-t}\left|\nabla^{2}f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}-\frac{C^{-1}A}{2}\left|\nabla e^{-u}\right|^{2}_{g_{t}}-e^{-t}\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}+A\left(e^{-u}-e^{-2u}\right)\\
&\ +C(1+t)e^{-t}e^{-u},\end{split}

where the second line follows from the estimate of Proposition
3.3
, and the third line follows by choosing
A
sufficiently large and applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and the estimate of Proposition
3.4
. Next we use the formula
F_{\mu_{f}}=F_{\overline{\mu}}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}f
, together with the fact that
h_{t}=e^{-t}h_{0}
to obtain the estimate

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\nabla^{2}f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}\geq&\ \left|\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}\\
=&\ \left|F_{\mu_{f}}-F_{\overline{\mu}}\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}\\
\geq&\ \tfrac{1}{2}\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}-4\left|F_{\overline{\mu}}\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}\\
\geq&\ \tfrac{1}{2}e^{t}\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|^{2}_{g,h}-Ce^{-2u}.\end{split}

Inserting this into (
3.3
) and choosing
A
sufficiently large yields the inequality

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\Phi\leq

\displaystyle\ -\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{t}}-\frac{C^{-1}A}{2}\left|\nabla e^{-u}\right|^{2}_{g_{t}}-e^{-t}\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}+A\left(e^{-u}-e^{-2u}\right)

\displaystyle\ +C(1+t)e^{-t}e^{-u}+C(e^{-u})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ -\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{t}}-\frac{C^{-1}A}{2}\left|\nabla e^{-u}\right|^{2}_{g_{t}}-e^{-t}\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}-\frac{A}{2}e^{-2u}+2Ae^{-u},

where the last line follows by choosing
A
sufficiently large.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Given a solution to Ricci-Yang-Mills flow with
\chi(\Sigma)<0
, there exists a constant
C>0
so that

\displaystyle\sup_{M\times\{t\}}e^{-t}\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}\leq

\displaystyle\ C.

Proof.

Defining
\Phi
as in Lemma
3.5
and returning to line (
3.3
) and using the result of Proposition
3.3
yields the differential inequality

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\Phi\leq

\displaystyle\ -e^{-t}\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}+C.

Using the estimates of Proposition
3.3
and
3.4
, at a sufficiently large maximum for
\Phi
it follows that
e^{-t}\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}
is also arbitrarily large, yielding a contradiction. Hence
\Phi
has an a priori upper bound and thus so does
e^{-t}\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}
.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Given a solution to Ricci-Yang-Mills flow with
\chi(\Sigma)<0
, there exists a constant
C>0
so that

\displaystyle\sup_{M\times\{t\}}u\leq C.

Proof.

Choose
A>0
so that the conclusion of Lemma
3.5
holds, and let

\displaystyle\Phi=u+A(e^{-u}-1)+e^{-t}\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}+e^{-t}\left|f\right|_{h_{0}}^{2}

Combining Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
3.5
we obtain, dropping some negative terms,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\Phi\leq&\ \tfrac{1}{2}\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{t}}+e^{-u}-1+\left\{-\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|_{g_{t},h_{t}}^{2}-\frac{A}{2}e^{-2u}+2Ae^{-u}\right\}\\
\leq&\ Ce^{-u}-1.\end{split}

Suppose there exists
(x_{0},t_{0})
such that

\displaystyle\Phi(x_{0},t_{0})=\sup_{M\times[0,t_{0}]}\Phi=B.

Using Propositions
3.3
,
3.4
, and
3.6
, if
B
is sufficiently large, it follows that

\displaystyle u(x_{0},t_{0})\geq\frac{B}{2}.

Rearranging this inequality we obtain

\displaystyle e^{-u}(x_{0},t_{0})\leq

\displaystyle\ e^{-\frac{B}{2}}.

Since
(x_{0},t_{0})
is a spacetime maximum for
\Phi
, returning to (
3.5
) and applying the maximum principle we obtain

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\ \left[\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\Phi\right](x_{0},t_{0})\leq Ce^{-\frac{B}{2}}-1,

a contradiction for
B
chosen sufficiently large. It follows that
\Phi
has a uniform upper bound, and the proposition follows.
∎

, and

3.4.

Lemma 3.12

Given a solution to Ricci-Yang-Mills flow with
\chi(\Sigma)>0
,

, one has

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}=

\displaystyle\ -2\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}-2\left|(\nabla^{2}f)^{2,0+0,2}\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}-\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|_{g_{t},h_{0}}^{2}\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}

Proof.

In the case

, Lemma
3.2
yields

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}=

\displaystyle\ -2\left|\nabla^{2}f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}-\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|_{g_{t},h_{0}}^{2}\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}.

Since

4.2.
The case
\chi(\Sigma)=0

This case requires a further technical lemma to obtain the limiting behavior, namely a bound on the Sobolev constant of the conformal metric. This lemma is also employed in obtaining estimates in the case
\Sigma=S^{2}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
is a compact Riemann surface. Fix
u\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}udV_{\Sigma}=0,\qquad\left|\left|du\right|\right|_{L^{2}}\leq A.

There exists a constant
C=C(A)
such that
C_{S}(e^{u}g_{\Sigma})\leq C
.

Proof.

Using the
L^{2}
gradient bound for
u
, it follows from the Moser-Trudinger inequality that for any
p>1
one has a constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}e^{p\left|u\right|}dV_{\Sigma}\leq C.

We note that
W^{1,2}
embeds into any
L^{p}
space, so we choose the constant
C_{S}(g_{\Sigma})
such that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Sigma}f^{8}dV_{\Sigma}\right)^{\tfrac{1}{4}}\leq C_{S}(g_{\Sigma})\left(\int_{\Sigma}\left|df\right|^{2}_{g_{\Sigma}}dV_{\Sigma}+\int_{\Sigma}f^{2}dV_{\Sigma}\right).

Using the above estimates we will bound the Sobolev constant for the metric
g=e^{u}g_{\Sigma}
, for the embedding
W^{1,2}\to L^{4}
. The argument modifies in on obvious way to estimate other Sobolev constants. To begin we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Sigma}f^{4}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=

\displaystyle\ \left(\int_{\Sigma}f^{4}e^{u}dV_{\Sigma}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \left(\int_{\Sigma}f^{8}dV_{\Sigma}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\int_{\Sigma}e^{2u}dV_{\Sigma}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ C\left(\int_{\Sigma}f^{8}dV_{\Sigma}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Using the Sobolev inequality for
g_{\Sigma}
and the conformal invariance of the Dirichlet energy we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Sigma}f^{4}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\int_{\Sigma}f^{8}dV_{\Sigma}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\leq

\displaystyle\ C\int_{\Sigma}\left(\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{\Sigma}}+f^{2}\right)dV_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ C\int_{\Sigma}\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g}dV_{g}+C\int_{\Sigma}f^{2}dV_{\Sigma}.

We furthermore choose
N>0
and observe that

\displaystyle\int_{e^{-u}\geq N}dV_{\Sigma}\leq\frac{1}{N}\int_{\Sigma}e^{\left|u\right|}dV_{\Sigma}\leq\frac{C}{N}.

Using this we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}f^{2}dV_{\Sigma}=

\displaystyle\ \int_{e^{-u}\leq N}f^{2}dV_{\Sigma}+\int_{e^{-u}\geq N}f^{2}dV_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ N\int_{\Sigma}f^{2}dV_{g}+\left(\int_{\Sigma}f^{8}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\int_{e^{-u}\geq N}dV_{\Sigma}\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ N\int_{\Sigma}f^{2}dV_{g}+CN^{-\frac{3}{4}}\left(\int_{\Sigma}f^{8}dV_{\Sigma}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Choosing
N
sufficiently large and using this above gives the claim.
∎

.

By combining the estimates of Propositions

To obtain the convergence, we first observe that by Proposition

.

Let

\displaystyle\ -\Delta f_{-}-R+\frac{1}{2}\left|F_{\mu}\right|^{2}_{g,h}+\frac{n}{2\tau}.

term). Using this, the construction of the blowup limit follows from the compactness theory for Ricci-Yang-Mills flow/generalized Ricci flow (
[
39
]
,
[
9
]
Ch. 5).
∎

Remark 4.10
.

The blowup limits constructed in Theorem
4.9
will necessarily be solutions to Ricci-Yang-Mills flow on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
-bundles, since the fiber metric is fixed along the flow, and thus blowing up along the rescaled sequence.

4.3.2.
Global existence and convergence

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
T^{k}\to\Sigma\times T^{k}\to\Sigma
denote the trivial principal
T^{k}
-bundle over a compact Riemann surface with
\chi(\Sigma)>0
. Given
G_{0}=\pi^{*}g_{0}+\tr_{h_{0}}\mu_{0}\otimes\mu_{0}
an invariant metric on
\Sigma\times T^{k}
, the solution to (
2.1
) with
\lambda=0
and initial condition
(g_{0},\mu_{0},h_{0})
exists on a finite time interval
[0,T)
, and satisfies

(1)

\lim_{t\to T}(T-t)\left|F_{\mu_{t}}\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}=0
,

(2)

\lim_{t\to T}(T-t)^{-1}g_{t}=g_{\Sigma}
,

g_{\Sigma}
denotes a metric of constant curvature
1
.

Proof.

By taking a double cover if necessary we can reduce to the case
\Sigma\cong S^{2}
. We first claim that the existence time is finite. We describe the proof in the case
k=1
for simplicity, the general case is directly analogous. As in the proof of Proposition
4.12
, we can extend the principal connection
\mu_{0}
using a flat connection to obtain a Hermitian connection
\underline{\mu}_{0}
on the trivial
T^{2}
bundle on
S^{2}\times T^{2}
. This defines a choice of complex structure on
T^{2}
, which then yields a complex structure on the product
S^{2}\times T^{2}
. Furthermore, by (
[
35
]
Proposition 5.3) the data
(g_{0},\underline{\mu}_{0},h_{0})
defines a Hermitian metric on
S^{2}\times T^{2}
, which is furthermore pluriclosed by (
[
35
]
Lemma 5.6). By (
[
35
]
Proposition 6.3, cf. also Remark 6.4) it follows that the pluriclosed flow with this initial data will reduce to Ricci-Yang-Mills flow. The
S^{2}
slices are all holomorphic curves in this complex manifold, and it follows from Stokes Theorem (cf.
[
36
]
Proposition 3.9) that for the solution
\omega_{t}
to pluriclosed flow one has

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{S^{2}}\omega_{t}=-8\pi.

It follows that the area goes to zero in finite time for any initial data, and thus the flow must go singular at a time
T<(8\pi)^{-1}\Area_{\omega_{0}}(S^{2})
.

Fix a solution
(g_{t},\mu_{t})
to Ricci-Yang-Mills flow as in the statement, with associated potential functions
f_{t}
. If the flow exists on a maximal time interval
[0,T)
,
T<\infty
, by an elementary point-picking argument we can choose a sequence
(x_{i},t_{i})
of points satisfying conditions (1) and (2) of Theorem
4.9
, and construct a corresponding blowup limit
(g^{\infty}_{t},\mu^{\infty}_{t},h^{\infty})
as described in Theorem
4.9
. We note that by construction, the sequence of functions
f^{i}(x,t)=f(x,t_{i}+\Lambda_{i}^{-1}t)
are potential functions for the rescaled flows, and the
f^{i}
will converge to a limit function
f^{\infty}
satisfying
F_{\mu^{\infty}_{t}}=dd^{c}f^{\infty}_{t}
. On the other hand, since
\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g_{t},h_{0}}
has a uniform upper bound by Proposition
3.13
, it follows by the scaling law that
\left|\nabla f^{\infty}\right|^{2}_{g^{\infty},h_{0}}\equiv 0
. Thus
f^{\infty}
is constant, and so
F_{\mu^{\infty}}\equiv 0
. It follows that
g^{\infty}_{t}
is an ancient
2
-dimensional
\kappa
-solution to Ricci flow, which by (
[
21
]
Corollary 11.3) is isometric to the standard round shrinking sphere solution. Using this blowup sequence it is straightforward to show that the whole flow line converges to a round point and satisfies the scale-invariant decay of curvature claimed in the statement.
∎

4.4.
The case
\chi(\Sigma)>0
, nontrivial bundle

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
T^{k}\to P\to\Sigma
denote a principal
T^{k}
-bundle over a compact Riemann surface with
\chi(\Sigma)>0
,
c_{1}(P)\neq 0
. Given
G_{0}=\pi^{*}g_{0}+\tr_{h_{0}}\mu_{0}\otimes\mu_{0}
an invariant metric on
P
, the solution to (
2.1
) with
\lambda=0
and initial condition
(g_{0},\mu_{0},h_{0})
exists on
[0,\infty)
, and there is a one-parameter family of diffeomorphisms
\phi_{t}
such that

(1)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\phi_{t}^{*}g_{t}=\gamma_{2}g_{\Sigma}
,

(2)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\phi_{t}^{*}F_{\mu_{t}}=\gamma_{1}\omega_{\Sigma}
,

where
g_{\Sigma}
denotes a metric of constant curvature
1
and
\omega_{\Sigma}
denotes the associated area form.

Proof.

First, by lifting to a double cover it suffices to consider the case
\Sigma=S^{2}
. We first employ a gauge-fixing procedure (cf.
[
40
]
) to account for the noncompact group of conformal diffeomorphisms of
S^{2}
. For such a diffeomorphism
\phi
we will set

\displaystyle\phi^{*}g=\phi^{*}\left(e^{u}g_{S^{2}}\right)=e^{u\circ\phi}\phi^{*}g_{\Sigma}=e^{v}g_{\Sigma}=\hat{g},

where

(4.5)

\displaystyle v=u\circ\phi+\log\det d\phi.

The condition we require, aiming at application of the sharp Sobolev inequality of Aubin
[
2
]
, is that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\int_{S^{2}}xdV_{\hat{g}}=0,

where
x
denotes the position vector in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. By (
[
5
]
Lemma 2) we can find a conformal transformation
\phi_{0}
so that (
4.6
) is satisfied at time
t=0
. To solve for the relevant gauge transformations we first set
\xi_{t}=\left(\phi_{t}^{-1}\right)_{*}\dot{\phi}
, which is a vector field on
S^{2}
. Differentiating equation (
4.5
) gives

\displaystyle\dot{v}=\dot{u}\circ\phi+\xi v+\divg_{g_{S^{2}}}\xi.

Differentiating the defining condition (
4.6
) yields (cf.
[
40
]
Lemma 6.2)

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\ \int_{S^{2}}\left[x\left(-2ve^{-v}+\tfrac{1}{2}\left|F_{\mu_{f}}\right|^{2}_{\hat{g},h}\right)-\xi\right]dV_{\hat{g}}.

As explained in (
[
40
]
§5.2) this condition suffices to solve for
\xi
. Thus we obtain the required family of diffeomorphisms
\phi_{t}
such that

\displaystyle\phi_{t}^{*}g_{t}=\phi_{t}^{*}(e^{u}g_{S^{2}})=e^{v_{t}}g_{S^{2}}=\hat{g}_{t},

where condition (
4.6
) is satisfied at all times. These diffeomorphisms define new curvature densities
\hat{F}_{t}=\phi_{t}^{*}F_{t}
, realized as the curvature of a family of connections
\hat{\mu}_{t}
satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\hat{\mu}_{t}=-d^{*}_{\hat{g}}F_{\hat{\mu}}+i_{\xi_{t}}F_{\hat{\mu}}.

Next we observe the key qualitative influence of the hypothesis that
c_{1}(P)\neq 0
, namely the a priori volume lower bound provided by Lemma
3.14
. It follows that
\hat{g}_{t}
also has a uniform lower volume bound. It follows from
[
2
]
that for any
\epsilon>0
we have
C=C(\epsilon)
such that

\displaystyle c\leq\fint_{S^{2}}e^{v}dV_{S^{2}}\leq C\exp\left[\left(\tfrac{1}{8}+\epsilon\right)\fint_{S^{2}}\left|dv\right|^{2}_{g_{S^{2}}}dV_{S^{2}}+\tfrac{1}{2}R_{\Sigma}\fint_{S^{2}}vdV_{S^{2}}\right].

Choosing for instance
\epsilon=\tfrac{1}{16}
, we can rearrange this to obtain the estimate

(4.7)

\displaystyle\frac{6}{16}\fint_{S^{2}}\left|dv\right|^{2}_{g_{S^{2}}}dV_{S^{2}}+R_{\Sigma}\fint_{S^{2}}vdV_{S^{2}}\geq-C.

We also note that the functional
\mathcal{F}
is bounded above along the ungauged flow by Proposition
2.4
. Since the Liouville energy is invariant under the action of conformal diffeomorphisms by (
[
5
]
Lemma 1), it follows that the Liouville energy of
v
is uniformly bounded above. Using this together with (
4.7
) gives

\displaystyle\left|\left|dv\right|\right|_{L^{2}}\leq C.

Using this, it follows that
e^{\left|v\right|}
is uniformly bounded in any
L^{p}
space, also also that
v
is bounded in
L^{1}
. Thus by Lemma
4.2
we obtain a uniform estimate of the Sobolev constant of
e^{v}g
. Applying Proposition
2.5
we obtain the long time existence.

To obtain the convergence we further analyze the monotonicity of
\mathcal{F}
. Using the Sobolev constant estimate for
\hat{g}
and the uniform
L^{2}(\hat{g})
estimate for
F_{\mu_{f}}
it follows that that

(4.8)

\displaystyle\left|\left|\left|F_{\hat{\mu}}\right|_{g,h}\right|\right|_{L^{4}(\hat{g})}^{2}\leq C\left(\left|\left|\nabla^{\hat{g}}F_{\hat{\mu}}\right|\right|_{L^{2}(\hat{g})}^{2}+\left|\left|F_{\hat{\mu}}\right|\right|_{L^{2}(\hat{g})}^{2}\right)\leq C\left(\left|\left|\nabla^{\hat{g}}F_{\hat{\mu}}\right|\right|_{L^{2}(\hat{g})}^{2}+1\right),

where the last line follows from the upper bound for
\mathcal{F}
.
In particular, since
\mathcal{F}
is bounded below along the flow, there is a sequence
t_{i}\to\infty
such that
\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{F}(v_{t},\hat{\mu}_{t},h)=0
, thus, for sufficiently large
i
, at any time
t_{i}
we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle\epsilon\geq\int_{\Sigma}\left(R_{\hat{g}}-\tfrac{1}{2}\left|F_{\hat{\mu}}\right|^{2}_{\hat{g},h}\right)^{2}dV_{\hat{g}}+2\int_{\Sigma}\left|\nabla^{\hat{g}}F_{\hat{\mu}}\right|^{2}_{{\hat{g}},h}dV_{\hat{g}}.

This implies that
\left|\left|\nabla^{\hat{g}}F_{\hat{\mu}}\right|\right|_{L^{2}(g,h)}
is bounded, thus applying (
4.8
) we obtain a uniform
L^{4}({\hat{g}})
estimate for
F_{\hat{\mu}}
, and it follows easily then from (
4.9
) that the Calabi energy of
\hat{g}
is bounded. Since
\int_{M}e^{2\left|v\right|}dV_{\Sigma}<C
, it follows that the curvature does not concentrate in
L^{1}
, and thus by (
[
40
]
Theorem 3.2, cf. also
[
6
]
),
v
is uniformly bounded in
H^{2}
. By following the arguments of Proposition
2.5
, we obtain a uniform
H^{2}
estimate for
v
on
[t_{i},t_{i}+1]
. Since there exists a relevant time
t_{i}
in every time interval of the form
[T,T+1]
for all sufficiently large
T
, it follows that there is a uniform
H^{2}
estimate for
v
. Using this and a bootstrapping argument as described in Proposition
4.1
, it follows that there are uniform
C^{k,\alpha}
estimates for
v
and
\hat{F}
. Returning to the monotonicity formula for
\mathcal{F}
, it follows that
\nabla^{\hat{g}}\hat{F}\to 0
, and
R_{\hat{g}}\to\mbox{const}
. Thus
\hat{g}
is converging to a round metric, and
\hat{F}
is converging to a multiple of the area form, finishing the proof.
∎

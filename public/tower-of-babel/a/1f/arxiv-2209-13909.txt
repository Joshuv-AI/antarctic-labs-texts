On semi shift invariant graph filters
Feng Ji, See Hian Lee, Wee Peng Tay

Abstract
In graph signal processing, one of the most important subject is the study of filters, i.e., linear transformations that capture relations between graph signals. One of the most important families of filters is the space of shift invariant filters, defined as transformations commute with a preferred graph shift operator. Shift invariant filters have a wide range of applications in graph signal processing and graph neural networks. A shift invariant filter can be interpreted geometrically as an information aggregation procedure (from local neighborhood), and can be computed easily using matrix multiplication. However, there are still drawbacks to use solely shift invariant filters in applications, such as being restrictively homogeneous. In this paper, we generalize shift invariant filters by introducing and study semi shift invariant filters. We give an application of semi shift invariant filters with a new signal processing framework, the subgraph signal processing. Moreover, we also demonstrate how semi shift invariant filters can be used in graph neural networks.

Suppose the size of a graph

G

M_{n}(\mathbb{R})

The full space of filters
M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is
n^{2}
dimensional. This means that if we want to find an appropriate filter for signal processing tasks, the search space can be oversized. Therefore, there are attempts to construct filter banks, subspaces of
M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, with smaller dimensions. One of the most important filter banks in GSP is the space of shift invariant filters. Under favorable condition, a shift invariant filter can be expressed as a polynomial in a chosen graph shift operator, such as the graph adjacency matrix or the Laplacian. Shift invariant operators are widely used as they enjoy a few important properties:

1.

A shift operator is usually interpretable as an aggregation mechanism of information from surrounding nodes of each node in the graph. It approximates well our understanding of many actual physical systems in real applications.

2.

The dimension of the space of shift invariant filters is at most
n
. It is much smaller than the dimension of
M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. This makes shift invariant filters preferred in many learning problems, as we do not want any candidate filter bank containing too many unrealistic filters.

3.

It is usually computationally efficient to handle shift invariant filters as only matrix multiplications are involved.

Many subspaces of the space of shift invariant filters, such as small degree shift invariant filters and band-pass filters, play prominent roles in many applications.

However, shift invariant filters have some drawbacks as well. For example, a shift invariant filter is homogeneous in the following sense. For the above mentioned graph shift operators, the way signals are gathered for each node from its neighboring nodes does not change from node to node. Simple calculations shows that the same observation holds for any polynomial, i.e., shift invariant filter, of the shift operator. This feature can be restrictive in applications as we can always encounter non-homogeneous situations. Moreover, the space of shift invariant filters can be insufficient in certain applications considering its relatively small dimension.

In this paper, we introduce the spaces of semi shift invariant filters, which are essentially mixtures of shift invariant filters restricted to different subgraphs of the ambient graph
G
. The concept subsumes the above mentioned filter banks such as shift invariant filter, the full filter space
M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
as special cases. Moreover, we shall demonstrate that the newly introduced filter banks can resolve certain drawbacks of the shift invariant filters.

Our main contributions are as follows:

•

We define semi shift invariant filters and discuss their properties. We compute dimensions of different families of semi shift invariant filters and discuss their includement relations.

•

We describe a new graph signal processing framework: subgraph signal processing. The purpose is to perform signal processing without observing signals at all the nodes or even without observing the full graph.

•

We demonstrate that semi shift invariant filters can also be applied to graph neural networks (GNN). We explain how the concept can be systematically synergized with existing GNN models.

II
Semi shift invariant graph filters

II-A

Graph signal processing preliminaries

In this subsection, we give a brief overview of graph signal processing (GSP). The main purpose is to introduce shift invariant (SI) filters and motivate subsequent subsections.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph of size
n
. A graph signal
f
assigns a number
f(v)
to each node
v\in V
, and the space of graph signals can be identified with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A
graph shift operator (GSO)

\mathbf{S}
is a linear transformation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In the literature, there are a few preferred candidates of
\mathbf{S}
such as the adjacency matrix
\mathbf{A}_{G}
of
G
, the Laplacian
\mathbf{L}_{G}
and their normalized versions
\widetilde{\mathbf{A}_{G}},\widetilde{\mathbf{L}_{G}}
. These choices all have the geometric interpretation of aggregating signals from
1
-hop neighborhood for each node. Therefore, graph information is contained in each of them. Moreover, such an
\mathbf{S}
is normal, which we assume holds true for any chosen GSO in the sequel. It implies that
\mathbf{S}
admits an orthonormal decomposition
\mathbf{U}\Lambda\mathbf{U}^{*}
with
\Lambda
a diagonal matrix consisting of eigenvalues of
\mathbf{S}
. The columns of
\mathbf{U}
forms the graph Fourier basis of the
frequency domain
associated with
\mathbf{S}
.
Graph Fourier transform
(GFT) is nothing but base change w.r.t. the Fourier basis, i.e., GFT of
f
is
\hat{f}=\mathbf{U}^{*}f
.

The operator
\mathbf{S}
is also a
graph filter
. In general, a graph filter is a linear transformation in
M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. One uses filters to describe relations between different signals. One of the main topics of GSP is the construction of filter banks. Associated with
\mathbf{S}
itself, there is the (vector) space of
shift invariant (SI) filters
as those filters
\mathbf{F}
commute with
\mathbf{S}
, i.e.,
\mathbf{F}\circ\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{S}\circ\mathbf{F}
. The notion is analogous to that of shift invariance in classical Fourier theory if
G
is taken as the direct cyclic graph and
\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{A}_{G}
. If
\mathbf{S}
does not have repeated eigenvalues, then each SI filter
\mathbf{F}
is a polynomial in
\mathbf{S}
. By the Cayley-Hamilton theorem, the dimension of space of SI filters is
n
. Consequently, a typical SI filter is a linear combination of monomials
\mathbf{S}^{d}
, which aggregates
d
-hop information. However, this loses flexibility as the same linear combination is used across the entire graph. In particular, this is not preferred if we only want to gather
d
-hop information for nodes in a subset
V_{0}
of
V
. In the next subsection, we introduce semi shift invariant filters that addresses this issue.

II-B

Semi shift invariant graph filters

In this subsection, we introduce the space of semi shift invariant filters as a generalization of the space of shift invariant filters.

Let
\mathbf{S}
be a fixed shift operator for
G
(of size
n
), whose matrix form we assume to be normal. This is a theoretical requirement. As we have seen, the shift operator
\mathbf{S}
dictates how information is being passed on
G
.

Definition 1
.

Consider
V_{0}\subset V
and
d\leq n-1
. Let
\mathbf{P}_{V_{0}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{|V_{0}|}
be the projection, i.e., for
v\in V_{0}
, the
v
-component of
\mathbf{P}_{V_{0}}f
is
f(v)
.

Let
\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{V_{0}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
f\mapsto\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{V_{0}}f
be such that
\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{V_{0}}f(v)=f(v)
for
v\in V_{0}
and
0
on
V\backslash V_{0}
. A
semi shift invariant filter
of degree
d
is a composition
\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{V_{0}}\circ Q_{d}(\mathbf{S})
for a polynomial
Q_{d}
of degree
d
.

From
1
, the output of a semi shift invariant filter is supported on
V_{0}
. More generally, we have the following.

Definition 2
.

For
k\geq 1
, let
C=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{k})
be a tuple of subsets of vertices in
V
and
D=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{k})
be a tuple of non-negative integers each smaller than
n
. The space of semi shift invariant filters
\mathscr{J}_{C,D}
on
C
of degree type
D
is the span of semi shift invariant filters supported on
V_{j}
of degree
d_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq k
, i.e., if
\mathbf{F}\in\mathscr{J}_{C,D}
, then

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{V_{j}}\circ Q_{d_{j}}(\mathbf{S})

for some polynomials
Q_{d_{1}},\ldots,Q_{d_{k}}
.

For any
C
and
D
,
\mathscr{J}_{C,D}
is a vector space, and we use
\dim\mathscr{J}_{C,D}
to denote its dimension. We now give some examples.

Example 1
.

1.

Suppose
\mathbf{S}
does not have repeated eigenvalues. If
C=V
and
D=n-1
, then
\mathscr{J}_{C,D}
is the space of all shift invariant filters.

2.

We work out a numerical example with the directed cycle graph shown in Fig.
1
. The directed adjacency matrix
\mathbf{A}_{G}
is used as the shift operator for the ambient graph
G
. Let
V_{0}=\{v_{0},v_{2},v_{2},v_{5}\}
,
V_{1}=\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
and
V_{2}=\{v_{0},v_{5}\}
. Set
C=(V_{1},V_{2})
and
D=(1,3)
. An example of a filter in the filter family
\mathscr{J}_{C,D}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}=\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{V_{1}}\circ\mathbf{A}_{G}+\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{V_{2}}\circ\mathbf{A}_{G}^{3}.

For any signal
f
, applying
\mathbf{F}
to
f
permutes its
V_{0}
components in the cyclic order according to their positions on the induced subgraph
H_{0}
. To see this, let
u\leadsto v
denote the permutation of the
u
component of a signal to the
v
component after applying an operator. In
\mathbf{F}
, the operator term
\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{V_{1}}\circ\mathbf{A}_{G}
achieves
v_{0}\leadsto v_{1}\leadsto v_{2}
. Here, we notice that
v_{2}\leadsto v_{3}
and so on due to
\mathbf{A}_{G}
but are nulled out by
\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{V_{1}}
. Similarly,
\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{V_{2}}\circ\mathbf{A}_{G}^{3}
achieves
v_{2}\leadsto v_{5}\leadsto v_{0}
. Therefore, in total,
\mathbf{F}
results in
v_{0}\leadsto v_{1}\leadsto v_{2}\leadsto v_{5}\leadsto v_{0}
.

Locality of semi shift invariants is explained by the following observation.

Lemma II.1
.

For
V_{0}\subset V
and
d\geq 0
, the
d
-hop neighborhood
B_{d}(V_{0})
of
V_{0}
is the union of vertices that are at most
d
hops away, in V, from some vertex of
V_{0}
. Suppose
\mathbf{L}_{G}
is the Laplacian of
G
and
\mathbf{L}_{V_{0},d}
is the Laplacian of the induced subgraph on
B_{d}(V_{0})
, extended by
0
to
V\backslash B_{d}(V_{0})
. Then a semi shift invariant filter
\mathbf{F}=\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{V_{0}}\circ Q_{d}(\mathbf{L}_{G})
of degree
d
is also given by
\mathbf{F}=\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{V_{0}}\circ Q_{d}(\mathbf{L}_{V_{0},d})
.

Proof:

Let
x
be any graph signal. As
Q_{d}
is of degree
d
, the value of
Q_{d}(\mathbf{L}_{G})(x)
at each vertex
v\in V
depends only on the signal values at vertices at most
d
hops away from
v
. The filters
Q_{d}(\mathbf{L}_{G})
and
Q_{d}(\mathbf{L}_{V_{0},d})
are thus the same on
V_{0}
. As the signal value of
\mathbf{F}(x)
is
0
outside
V_{0}
, we have the desired identity
\mathbf{F}=\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{V_{0}}\circ Q_{d}(\mathbf{L}_{V_{0},d})
.
∎

II-C

Relations among families of semi shift invariant filters

In filter design or estimation, we usually need to pre-define a filter bank from which the desired filter is chosen. The filter bank should have an appropriate size, measured by for example its dimension. We want the filter bank to be large enough to contain good approximation of the ground truth. On the other hand, a filter bank being too large can hinder effective learning.

In this paper, we propose to choose
\mathscr{J}_{C,D}
with appropriate
C
and
D
as filter bank candidate. The purpose of this subsection is to provide estimation of
\dim\mathscr{J}_{C,D}
and discuss relation among
\mathscr{J}_{C,D}
for different
C
and
D
.

To motivate our discussion on dimension, we give more examples. For convenience, if
C=\{v\}
and
D=d
for some vertex
v
and non-negative integer
d
, we write
\mathscr{J}_{C,D}
as
\mathscr{J}_{v,d}
.

Example II.1
.

1.

Suppose a symmetric shift operator
\mathbf{S}
does not have repeated eigenvalues, and no eigenvector of
\mathbf{S}
has zero components. For each
v\in V
and
0\leq d\leq n-1
, we have
\dim\mathscr{J}_{v,d}=d+1
. To see this, we note that the space
\mathscr{J}_{v,d}
is spanned by
\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{v}\circ\mathbf{S}^{i}
, for
0\leq i\leq d
. Therefore,
\dim\mathscr{J}_{v,d}\leq d+1
. We also have
\dim\mathscr{J}_{v,d+1}-\dim\mathscr{J}_{v,d}\leq 1
. Hence, it suffices to prove the case for
d=n-1
. Suppose
\sum_{0\leq i\leq n-1}a_{i}\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{v}\circ\mathbf{S}^{i}=0.
We write the orthonormal decomposition of
\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{U}\Lambda\mathbf{U}^{*}
. The diagonal entries of
\Lambda
are the distinct eigenvalues
\lambda_{0},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}
. Suppose the index of
v
is
j
. Unwrapping the above identity as a transformation in the frequency domain:

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq i\leq n-1}a_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{j,0}\lambda_{0}^{i},\ldots,\mathbf{U}_{j,n-1}\lambda_{n-1}^{i})=0.

As the row vectors
(\lambda_{0}^{i},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}^{i})
,
0\leq i\leq n-1
are linearly independent and the matrix entries
\mathbf{U}_{j0},\ldots,\mathbf{U}_{j,n-1}
are non-zero, the vectors
(\mathbf{U}_{j,0}\lambda_{0}^{i},\ldots,\mathbf{U}_{j,n-1}\lambda_{n-1}^{i})
,
0\leq i\leq n-1
are linearly independent. Consequently,
a_{i}=0
for
0\leq i\leq n-1
.

2.

Suppose
\mathbf{S}
does not have repeated eigenvalues, and no eigenvector of
\mathbf{S}
has zero components. If
C=(\{v\})_{v\in V}
and
D=(n-1,\ldots,n-1)
, then
\mathscr{J}_{C,D}
is the space of all filters on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. To see this, we note that the dimension of the space of all linear filters is
n^{2}
. For any vertices
u,v\in V
and polynomials
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
,
\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{u}\circ Q(\mathbf{S})
and
\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{v}\circ Q^{\prime}(\mathbf{S})
are supported on different vertices and are thus linearly independent in
\mathscr{J}_{C,D}
. It suffices to show that for each
v\in V
,
\dim\mathscr{J}_{v,n-1}=n
, but this follows from
1
in this example. More generally, if
D=(l,\ldots,l)
for some
l\leq n-1
, then
\mathscr{J}_{C,D}
is the space of
node-variant graph filters
up to degree
l
described in
[
17
]
.

While the trivial space is an extreme case,
II.1
2
is the other extreme. For our applications in the paper, we are also interested in other intermediate cases. The following discussion can be technical, and readers may skip until Fig.
3
upon first reading. The insight is that if
C^{\prime}
is “finer” than
C
and
D
is “smaller” than
D^{\prime}
, then
\mathscr{J}_{C,D}
is a subspace of
\mathscr{J}_{C^{\prime},D^{\prime}}
. The example shown in Fig.
3
illustrates the general picture.

To state the general result rigorously, we need to introduce more concepts.

Definition II.1
.

The tuple
C=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{k})
is called
essential
if for each
1\leq i\leq k
,
V_{i}\backslash\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq k,j\neq i}V_{j}\neq\emptyset,

i.e., none of
V_{i}
is completely contained in the union of the remaining sets of
C
.

Definition II.2
.

A tuple
C^{\prime}=(V_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,V_{l}^{\prime})
is said to be a
refinement
of
C
if the following holds (cf.
Fig.

2
):

1.

\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq k}V_{i}=\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq l}V_{j}^{\prime}
.

2.

Each
V_{j}^{\prime}
is contained in some
V_{i}
.

3.

If distinct
V_{j_{1}}^{\prime},V_{j_{2}}^{\prime}
are contained in the same
V_{i}
for some
i
, then
V_{j_{1}}^{\prime}\cap V_{j_{2}}^{\prime}=\emptyset
.

Fig. 2
:
In (a),
C=(V_{1},V_{2})
(blue) and
C^{\prime}=(V_{1}^{\prime},V_{2}^{\prime},V_{3}^{\prime})
(red).
C^{\prime}
is not a refinement of

because
V_{2}^{\prime}
is contained in neither
V_{1}
nor
V_{2}
. In (b),

We now state our main structural result on
\mathscr{J}_{C,D}
for various tuples
C
and
D
, whose proof is technical and can be found in

A
. We compute the dimension of
\mathscr{J}_{C,D}
based on conditions on
C
and
D
.

Proposition II.1
.

If
V_{1}\subset V_{2}\subset V
and
0\leq d_{1}\leq d_{2}\leq n-1
, then
\dim\mathscr{J}_{V_{1},d_{1}}\leq\dim\mathscr{J}_{V_{2},d_{2}}
. Furthermore, suppose
\mathbf{S}
does not have repeated eigenvalues, and no eigenvector of
\mathbf{S}
has zero components. Then, the following holds.

1.

If
C=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{k})
is essential and
D=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{k})
, then
\dim\mathscr{J}_{C,D}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}d_{i}+k
.

2.

Let
C=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{k})
,
D=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{k})
,
C^{\prime}=(V_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,V_{l}^{\prime})
and
D^{\prime}=(d_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,d_{l}^{\prime})
. Suppose
C^{\prime}
is a refinement of
C
. If
D
and
D^{\prime}
satisfy
d_{i}\leq d_{j}^{\prime}
whenever
V_{j}^{\prime}\subset V_{i}
, then
\mathscr{J}_{C,D}\subset\mathscr{J}_{C^{\prime},D^{\prime}}
. On the other hand, suppose that
\mathscr{J}_{C,D}\subset\mathscr{J}_{C^{\prime},D^{\prime}}
and
\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq l}V_{j}^{\prime}\subset\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq k}V_{j}
. If
D
and
D^{\prime}
are constant tuples with
d_{i}=d_{l}^{\prime}=d
for all
i,l
and the same
0\leq d<n
, and
C^{\prime}
is essential, then
C^{\prime}
is a refinement of
C
.

Discussions

With the above results, we gain more understanding of different families of semi shift invariant filters. For a chosen
\mathbf{S}
, it is possible to visualize the relations between different
\mathscr{J}_{C,D}
using a directed acyclic graph
G_{\mathbf{S}}
, by connecting two such filter banks if one is a maximal subspace of the other (cf. Fig.
3
). More specifically, we associate with each node of
G_{\mathbf{S}}
a filter space
\mathscr{J}_{C,D}
. Two distinct filter spaces
\mathscr{J}_{C_{1},D_{1}}
and
\mathscr{J}_{C_{2},D_{2}}
are connected by a directed edge in
G_{\mathbf{S}}
if and only if the following holds:

1.

\mathscr{J}_{C_{1},D_{1}}\subset\mathscr{J}_{C_{2},D_{2}}
, and

2.

if
\mathscr{J}_{C_{1},D_{1}}\subset\mathscr{J}_{C_{3},D_{3}}\subset\mathscr{J}_{C_{2},D_{2}}
, then either
\mathscr{J}_{C_{1},D_{1}}=\mathscr{J}_{C_{3},D_{3}}
or
\mathscr{J}_{C_{3},D_{3}}=\mathscr{J}_{C_{2},D_{2}}
.

In the graph
G_{\mathbf{S}}
, we have nodes associated with a large array of candidate filter banks that are between the extreme cases
M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
and the trivial space. We may also use concepts in lattice theory to enhance our understanding. For example,
\mathscr{J}_{C_{1},D_{1}}
is an upper bound of
\mathscr{J}_{C_{2},D_{2}}
if
\mathscr{J}_{C_{1},D_{1}}\supset\mathscr{J}_{C_{2},D_{2}}
and
\mathscr{J}_{C_{2},D_{2}}
is called a lower bound of
\mathscr{J}_{C_{1},D_{1}}
. The condition is equivalent to having a directed path in
G_{\mathbf{S}}
from
\mathscr{J}_{C_{2},D_{2}}
to
\mathscr{J}_{C_{1},D_{1}}
. The join of two nodes in
G_{\mathbf{S}}
is their least upper bound, and the meet is their largest lower bound. The difference of two filter banks can be inspected on
G_{\mathbf{S}}
by how far they are away from their meet and join.

Fig. 3
:
In the illustration, the graph
G
with
2

v_{1},v_{2}
are considered. For
\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{L}_{G}
, we list down all
\mathscr{J}_{C,D}
. Their relations are shown in the graph

\mathscr{J}_{C_{1},(1)}
is

, then a learning method using the shift invariant filter bank can be erroneous.

For a signal processing task, in additional to finding the correct graph shift operator
\mathbf{S}
, we also propose to choose an appropriate node in the graph
G_{\mathbf{S}}
such that the associated semi shift invariant filter bank is both economic and expressive enough for the problem.

In the following sections, we discuss applications with numerical experiments. Emphasis is put on how and why
C
and
D
are chosen.

III
Subgraph signal processing

One primary goal of introducing semi shift invariant filters is to develop the framework of
subgraph signal processing
, which we describe one aspect in this section.

Suppose
V_{0}
is a subset of
V
of size
n_{0}
and
f\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a signal on
V
. We want to perform signal processing on the
subgraph signal

f_{V_{0}}=\mathbf{P}_{V_{0}}f
, observed only at
V_{0}
. We call it
subgraph signal processing
. There can be scenarios where such a framework might be needed. It is possible that full observation of
f
is impossible, or even the full graph structure is unavailable. For example, in a sensor network, it is possible the overall sensor network is unknown or readings from some sensors are missing, due to reasons such as processing delay
[
18
]

IV
Graph neural networks

In this section, we discuss applications of semi shift invariant filters in graph neural networks (GNNs). We first give a brief overview of GNNs.

GNNs are neural network models that operate on graph structured data. By inputting a graph
G
and a matrix of node features
\mathbf{X}
to a GNN model, embeddings of graph components such as nodes and edges can be learned for downstream tasks through neighborhood aggregation process
[
26
]
. In particular, we want to study semi-supervised node classification problems. Each node of the graph has a class label such as article type in the citation network Citeseer. We want to estimate the class label of every node, assuming only the class labels of a small percentage of nodes are observed. We consider both homogeneous and heterogeneous graphs. A homogeneous graph is a graph with a single node type and a single edge type, while a heterogeneous graph has multiple node and/or edge types.
One of the most conventional GNN models is graph convolutional network (GCN)
[
7
]
. In the nutshell, GCN uses convolutional aggregations and is made up of multiple graph convolutional layers. Each graph convolutional layer is of the form:

\mathbf{H}^{(l+1)}=\sigma(\tilde{\mathbf{D}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\overline{\mathbf{A}}_{G}\tilde{\mathbf{D}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{H}^{(l)}\mathbf{W}^{(l)}).

(4)

In the expression,
\sigma
is an activation function, and
\overline{\mathbf{A}}_{G}=\mathbf{A}_{G}+\mathbf{I}_{n}
is the adjacency matrix with self-loops. The degree matrix
\tilde{\mathbf{D}}
of
\overline{\mathbf{A}}_{G}
contains information about the number of edges attached to each node, and
\mathbf{W}^{(l)}
is the trainable layer-specific weight matrix. The initial
\mathbf{H}^{(0)}
is taken to be
\mathbf{X}
.

Most of subsequent GNN models inherit the same philosophy of GCN that performing. Examples include the graph attention model GAT
[
27
]
that involves an additional the step of estimating edge weights and the hyperbolic model HGCN
[
28
]
that performs a hyperbolic version of convolutional aggregation. Moreover, heterogeneous GNN model HAN
[
29
]
that generates homogeneous graphs from heterogeneous graphs using the concept of meta-path. In the subsequent subsections, we propose to take a slightly different approach that performs aggregation that may change for different (cluster of) nodes, in the spirit of the paper.

IV-A
1
Node homophily

To motivate, we consider the concept of node homophily. Even though GNN models have achieved prominent results for graph learning tasks, the neighborhood aggregation mechanism has implicit graph homophily assumption, where “similar" nodes are connected to one another
[
30
]
. When the graph is heterophilous i.e. many neighboring nodes belonging to dissimilar classes, the models tend to perform poorly
[
31
]
. Many works then design various graph filters to address heterophily
[
31
,
32
]
. Nevertheless, they neglect the fact that a graph can have different extend of homophily/heterophily at different regions of the graph and a learned filter is still indifferently applied over the entire graph. In this subsection, we investigate the homophily of classes of nodes. In the next subsection, based on the observations, we modify GCN which learns a first order shift invariant filter expressed as the adjacency matrix
\mathbf{A}_{G}
to instead learn semi-shift invariant filters that are restricted to different subsets of vertices in the graph. The new model shall be called
SemiGCN
.

We want to investigate the extend of node homophily in different classes. Node homophily is typically defined based on similarity of connected node pairs. Nodes are deemed to be similar if they are of the same node label. We slightly modify the homophily metric introduced by
[
33
]
to be
\eta
class specific as follows:

\displaystyle H_{\eta}(G)=\frac{1}{|V_{\eta}|}\sum_{v\in V_{\eta}}\frac{\text{number of }v\text{'s neighbors of the same label}}{\text{number of }v\text{'s neighbors}}.

(5)

A large
H_{\eta}(G)
implies that the homophily of that specific class is strong. We plot the class specific homophily scores of four homogeneous graph datasets as seen in
Fig.

[

]
, the statistics of homophily score across the classes for the datasets are not necessarily uniform. Thus, it is not ideal to learn and utilise only a single filter across the entire graph.

Conclusions

In this paper, we generalize the notion of shift invariant filters in GSP by introducing semi shift invariant filters. We study their properties and demonstrate how they can be used in signal processing and machine learning applications.

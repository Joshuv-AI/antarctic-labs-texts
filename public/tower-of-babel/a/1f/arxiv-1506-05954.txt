Some effects of the noise intensity upon non-linear stochastic heat equations on [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1]
Bin Xie

Abstract
Various effects of the noise intensity upon the solution u ⁡ ( t , x ) u(t,x) of the stochastic heat equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions on [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] are investigated. We show that for small noise intensity, the p p -th moment of sup x ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] | u ⁡ ( t , x ) | \sup_{x\in[0,1]}|u(t,x)| is exponentially stable, however, for large one, it grows at least exponentially. We also prove that the noise excitation of the p p -th energy of u ⁡ ( t , x ) u(t,x) is 4 4 , as the noise intensity goes to infinity. We formulate a common method to investigate the lower bounds of the above two different behaviors for large noise intensity, which are hard parts in [ 8 ] , [ 10 ] and [ 16 ] .



Keywords:
stochastic heat equation, Dirichelt boundary condition,
space-time white noise, excitation index, exponential stability, growth rate

We are interested in various behaviors of the following stochastic heat equation relative to
\lambda

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)&=\frac{1}{2}\Delta u(t,x)+\lambda\sigma(u(t,x))\dot{w}(t,x),\ t>0,\ x\in(0,1),\\

where

\sigma

and the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (KPZ) equation

In this paper, two kinds of the behaviors of the solution relative to noise intensity

\displaystyle\|u_{\lambda}\|_{p,\beta}=\Big(\sup_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{E}[e^{\beta t}\|u_{\lambda}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p}]\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

2
Proof of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2

Since we consider the mild solution
u_{\lambda}(t,x)
of (
1.1
) with homegeneous Dirichlet boundary condition, most of our calculations depend on various estimates of
g_{D}(t,x,y)
. We will recall necessary properties of
g_{D}(t,x,y)
when they are required. Firstly, by the spectral theory, it is well-known that

\displaystyle g_{D}(t,x,y)=2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-(n\pi)^{2}t}\sin(n\pi x)\sin(n\pi y),\ x,y\in[0,1].

(2.1)

It is also easy to know that
t>0
and
x,y\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle 0\leq g_{D}(t,x,y)\leq g(t,x,y)

(2.2)

where
g(t,x,y)
denotes the transition probability density of some one-dimensional standard Brownian motion.

Different from the study of the long time behaviour of
\mathbb{E}[|u_{\lambda}(t,x)|^{p}]
in
[
10
]
, to study that of
\mathbb{E}[\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p}]
, the estimate of the derivative of
g_{D}(t,x,y)
is vital, that is

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}g_{D}(t,x,y)|\leq K_{1}t^{-1}e^{-K_{2}\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{t}}.

(2.3)

where
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are two generic positive constants.

Before we state the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we will give some lemmas.
Let us first formulate the famous Kolmogorov’s regularity theorem with its brief proof for the reader’s convenience and our purpose.

Lemma 2.1
.

(Kolmogorov’s regularity theorem)
Let
\{u(x)\}_{x\in[0,1]}
be a real valued stochastic process. If there exist
p\geq 1
and positive constants
K,\delta
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}]\leq K|x-y|^{1+\delta}.

(2.4)

then we have that for each
\epsilon\in(0,\min\{\delta,1\})
,
there exists a positive constant
\kappa
depending only on
p,\delta,\epsilon
such that

\displaystyle|u(x)-u(y)|\leq\kappa B^{1/p}|x-y|^{\frac{\delta-\epsilon}{p}},

(2.5)

where
B=B(\epsilon,\delta)
is the positive random variable defined by

\displaystyle B=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{2+\delta-\epsilon}}dxdy.

(2.6)

In particular, the stochastic process
\{u(x)\}_{x\in[0,1]}
has a
\frac{\delta-\epsilon}{p}
-Hölder continuous modification.

Proof.

This lemma is a modification of Corollary 1.2
[
22
]
. Similarly, we state its proof briefly using the celebrated analytic inequality introduced by Garsia, Rodemich and Rumsey, see the original research paper
[
12
]
, Theorem 1.1
[
22
]
or Theorem
6.1
in Appendix.
Let us consider
\Phi(x)=|x|^{p},\ x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\phi(x)=|x|^{\frac{2+\delta-\epsilon}{p}},\ x\in[0,1]
. It is clear that the two functions
\Phi(x)
and
\phi(x)
satisfy the conditions in Theorem
6.1
.
We can now rewrite the random variable
B
defined by (
2.6
) as

\displaystyle B=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\Phi\Big(\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{\phi(|x-y|)}\Big)dxdy.

Taking the expectation of
B
, and combining with the condition (
2.4
) on
u(x)
, we can easily arrive at

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[B]

\displaystyle=K\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}{|x-y|^{-1+\epsilon}}dxdy=\frac{2K}{\epsilon(1+\epsilon)}<\infty,

(2.7)

where
\epsilon\in(0,\min\{\delta,1\})
has been used.
Thus, we can apply the Garsia-Rodemich-Rumsey Theorem, see Theorem
6.1
. Using Theorem
6.1
and the integration by parts, we obtain that

\displaystyle|u(x)-u(y)|

\displaystyle\leq 8\int_{0}^{|x-y|}B^{\frac{1}{p}}u^{-\frac{2}{p}}d(u^{\frac{2+\delta-\epsilon}{p}})

\displaystyle=8B^{\frac{1}{p}}[|x-y|^{\frac{\delta-\epsilon}{p}}+\frac{2}{p}\int_{0}^{|x-y|}u^{\frac{\delta-\epsilon}{p}-1}du]

\displaystyle=8B^{\frac{1}{p}}(1+(\delta-\epsilon)^{-1})|x-y|^{\frac{\delta-\epsilon}{p}}.

Taking
\kappa=8(1+(\delta-\epsilon)^{-1})
, the estimate of (
2.5
) is proved. Finally, the existence of
\frac{\delta-\epsilon}{p}
-Hölder continuous modification for the stochastic process
\{u(x)\}_{x\in[0,1]}
is obvious from (
2.6
). Consequently, our proof is completed.
∎

Assume

D_{1}(t,\cdot)\in B_{p,\beta}
for
\beta\in(0,p\pi^{2})
.

Consequently, we proved that the solution
u_{\lambda}(t,x)\in B_{p,\beta}
for each
\beta\in(0,(2-\alpha)\pi^{2})
, which is equivalent to (
2.23
). Therefore, the proof is completed.
∎

3
Proof of Theorem
1.3

The proof of this theorem is essentially depends on the global behavior of the lower bound for the heat kernel
g_{D}(t,x,y)
. Such estimate is very important and has been studied actively. For large time and short time, we, for example, refer the reader to
[
6
]
and
[
24
]
respectively. For our aim, the global behavior for
g_{D}(t,x,y)
is needed, which is studied by many authors for different domains, please see
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
and
[
25
]
. Based on their research, we will state in the next corollary using our notation and omit its proof.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exist two strictly positive constants
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
such that for any
x,y\in[\gamma,1-\gamma],\gamma\in(0,1/4)

\displaystyle g_{D}(t,x)\geq\kappa_{1}\exp(-\pi^{2}t)\exp\big(-\kappa_{2}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}\big)(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}1_{(0,\gamma^{2}]}(t)+1_{(\gamma^{2},\infty)}(t)),\ t>0.

Remark 3.1
.

In the papers of
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
and
[
25
]
, sharp bounds of both sides of Dirichlet
heat kernel for the Laplacian on bounded domains with different conditions are discussed. To prove our result, the above lower bound of
g_{D}(t,x,y)
is enough. So we do not state the corresponding upper bound, see
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
and
[
25
]
and the references therein.

Let us now formulate the proof of Theorem
1.3
based on the above corollary.

Proof.

(
Proof of Theorem
1.3
)
Under our assumptions, by the positivity of
g_{D}(t,x,y)
and Lemma
3.1
, it is easy to see that for all
x\in[\gamma,1-\gamma]
,

\displaystyle D_{1}(t,x)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}g_{D}(t,x,y)u_{0}(y)dy

(3.1)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\inf_{x,y\in[\gamma,1-\gamma]}g_{D}(t,x,y)\int_{\gamma}^{1-\gamma}u_{0}(y)dy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C_{1}\inf_{x,y\in[\gamma,1-\gamma]}\exp(-\pi^{2}t)\exp\big(-\kappa_{2}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}\big)(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}1_{(0,\gamma^{2}]}(t)+1_{(\gamma^{2},\infty)}(t))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C_{1}\exp(-\pi^{2}t)\exp(-\kappa_{2}t^{-1})\Big(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}1_{(0,\gamma^{2}]}(t)+1_{(\gamma^{2},\infty)}(t)\Big),

where
C_{1}=\kappa_{1}\int_{\gamma}^{1-\gamma}u_{0}(y)dy>0
by the assumption
(A1)
.

Noting that
\inf_{t\in(0,\kappa^{2}]}t^{-\frac{1}{2}\exp(-\kappa_{2}t^{-1})}>0
and
\exp(-\kappa_{2}t^{-1})\geq\exp(-\kappa_{2}\gamma^{-2})
for
t\geq\gamma^{2}
, the above estimate (
3.1
), implies that there is a constant
C_{2}>0
such that for all
t\geq 0
and
x\in[\gamma,1-\gamma]
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}g_{D}(t,x,y)u_{0}(y)dy\geq

\displaystyle C_{2}\exp(-\pi^{2}t)

(3.2)

By Ito’s isometry and the assumption
|\sigma(u)|\geq K_{L}|u|,u\in\mathbb{R}
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|D_{2,\lambda}(t,x)|^{2}]=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}g_{D}^{2}(t-s,x,y)\mathbb{E}[|\lambda\sigma(u_{\lambda}(s,y))|^{2}]dyds

(3.3)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}K_{L}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}g_{D}^{2}(t-s,x,y)\mathbb{E}[|u_{\lambda}(s,y)|^{2}]dyds.

From now, let us deal with the term

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}g_{D}^{2}(t,x,y)\mathbb{E}[|u_{\lambda}(s,y)|^{2}]dyds

appeared in the last inequality.
For brevity, let us define
h(t)=\inf_{x\in[\gamma,1-\gamma]}\mathbb{E}[|u_{\lambda}(t,x)|^{2}]
. Using Lemma
3.1
, if
x\in[\gamma,1-\gamma]
, then we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}g_{D}^{2}(t,x,y)\mathbb{E}[|u_{\lambda}(s,y)|^{2}]dyds

(3.4)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\gamma}^{1-\gamma}g_{D}^{2}(t-s,x,y)h(s)dyds

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\gamma}^{1-\gamma}\kappa_{1}^{2}\exp(-2\pi^{2}(t-s))\exp\big(-2\kappa_{2}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t-s}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Big((t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}1_{(0,\gamma^{2}]}(t-s)+1_{(\gamma^{2},\infty)}(t-s)\Big)^{2}h(s)dyds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\gamma}^{1-\gamma}\kappa_{1}^{2}\exp(-2\pi^{2}(t-s))\exp\big(-2\kappa_{2}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t-s}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Big((t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}1_{(0,\gamma^{2}]}(t-s)+1_{(\gamma^{2},t)}(t-s)\Big)h(s)dyds

Set
A(x;s,t):=[\gamma,1-\gamma]\cap\{y:|y-x|\geq\sqrt{t-s}\},s\leq t
and then noting that
\gamma\in(0,1/4)
,
we can show that for any
x\in[\gamma,1-\gamma],

|A(x;s,t)|\geq\sqrt{t-s}
. Consequently, noting that for
y\in A(x;s,t)
,
|y-x|\leq\sqrt{t-s}
and the non-increasing of
e^{-x}
, for
s\in(t-\gamma^{2},t)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}^{1-\gamma}\exp(-2\pi^{2}(t-s))\exp\big(-2\kappa_{2}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t-s}\big)(t-s)^{-1}dy

(3.5)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{A(x;t,s)}\exp(-2\pi^{2}(t-s))\exp\big(-2\kappa_{2}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t-s}\big)(t-s)^{-1}dy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\exp(-2\kappa_{2})\exp(-2\pi^{2}(t-s))(t-s)^{-1}|A(x;t,s)|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\exp(-2\kappa_{2})\exp(-2\pi^{2}(t-s))(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

On the other hand, if
s\in(0,t-\gamma^{2}]
, then for any
x,y\in[\gamma,1-\gamma]
,

\exp\big(-2\kappa_{2}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t-s}\big)\geq\exp\big(-2\kappa_{2}\frac{(1-2\gamma)^{2}}{t-s}\big)\geq\exp(-2\kappa_{2}\gamma^{-2})>0.

Thus, it is easy to see that if
s\in(0,t-\gamma^{2}]
, then for any
x\in[\gamma,1-\gamma]

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}^{1-\gamma}\kappa_{1}^{2}\exp(-2\pi^{2}(t-s))\exp\big(-2\kappa_{2}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t-s}\big)dy

(3.6)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\exp(-2\kappa_{2}\gamma^{-2})\exp(-2\pi^{2}(t-s)).

Inserting (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) into (
3.4
), we see that there exists a constant
C_{3}>0
such that for all
t>0
and
x\in[\gamma,1-\gamma]

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}g_{D}^{2}(t,x,y)\mathbb{E}[|u_{\lambda}(s,y)|^{2}]dyds

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C_{3}\int_{0}^{t}\exp(-2\pi^{2}(t-s))\Big((t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}1_{(0,\gamma^{2}]}(t-s)+1_{(\gamma^{2},t]}(t-s)\Big)h(s)ds,

and then, noting that
1\geq\gamma(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
for
t-s\geq\gamma^{2}
,
we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}g_{D}^{2}(t,x,y)\mathbb{E}[|u_{\lambda}(s,y)|^{2}]dyds\geq C_{4}\int_{0}^{t}\exp(-2\pi^{2}(t-s))(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}h(s)ds

holds for some constant
C_{4}>0
.

Consequently, combining (
3.3
) with
the above estimate,
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|D_{2,\lambda}(t,x)|^{2}]\geq C_{4}\lambda^{2}K_{L}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\exp(-2\pi^{2}(t-s))(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}h(s)ds.

(3.7)

Noting that under our assumptions, Ito’s isometry gives that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|u_{\lambda}(t,x)|^{2}]=\left(\int_{0}^{1}g_{D}(t,x,y)u_{0}(y)dy\right)^{2}+\mathbb{E}[|D_{2,\lambda}(t,x)|^{2}].

Using (
3.2
) and (
3.7
), we have that for any
x\in[\gamma,1-\gamma]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|u_{\lambda}(t,x)|^{2}]\geq C_{2}^{2}\exp(-2\pi^{2}t)+C_{4}\lambda^{2}K_{L}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\exp(-2\pi^{2}(t-s))(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}h(s)ds,

which implies that for any
t>0

\displaystyle h(t)\geq C_{2}^{2}\exp(-2\pi^{2}t)+C_{4}\lambda^{2}K_{L}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\exp(-2\pi^{2}(t-s))(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}h(s)ds.

(3.8)

Let us now define
H(t)=\exp(2\pi^{2}t)h(t)
and then from (
3.8
), we get the following relation for
H(t)
:
for any
t>0

\displaystyle H(t)\geq C_{2}^{2}+C_{4}\lambda^{2}K_{L}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}H(s)dyds.

Finally, owing to Gronwall’s inequality, we can easily obtain that

\displaystyle H(t)\geq C_{2}^{2}\exp(C_{4}^{2}\lambda^{4}K_{L}^{4}t),\ t>0,

which completes our proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
and Corollary
1.5

The first aim of this part is to formulate the proof of Theorem
1.4
based on Theorem
1.3
and the second one is to prove Corollary
1.5

1.4
.

Proof.

(
Proof of Theorem
1.4
)

Owing to Theorem
1.1
and
|u_{\lambda}(t,x)|\leq\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}}
, it is sufficient for us to verify the lower bound, i.e., for any
\lambda\in(\lambda_{U},\infty)
and
x\in[\gamma,1-\gamma]

\displaystyle 0<\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mathbb{E}[|u_{\lambda}(t,x)|^{p}].

(4.1)

Jensen’s inequality tells us that for any
p>2
,

\mathbb{E}[|u_{\lambda}(t,x)|^{2}]^{1/2}\leq\mathbb{E}[|u_{\lambda}(t,x)|^{p}]^{1/p},

and thus it is enough for us to show that there exists a large enough
\lambda_{U}
, (
4.1
) is satisfied when
p=2
.
However, this is a quick result of Theorem
1.3
. In fact, since (
1.5
) holds for any
t>0
, we easily know that

\displaystyle\log\Big(\inf_{x\in[\gamma,1-\gamma]}\mathbb{E}[|u_{\lambda}(t,x)|^{2}]\Big)\geq\log\kappa_{1}+(\kappa_{2}\lambda^{4}K_{L}^{4}-2\pi^{2})t,\ t\geq 0.

Dividing both sides of the above inequality by
t
and taking the infimum limit as
t\to\infty
, we see that

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\Big(\inf_{x\in[\gamma,1-\gamma]}\mathbb{E}[|u_{\lambda}(t,x)|^{2}]\Big)\geq\kappa_{2}\lambda^{4}K_{L}^{4}-2\pi^{2}.

Let us take
\lambda_{U}=\Big(\frac{2\pi^{2}}{\kappa_{2}K_{L}^{4}}\Big)^{1/4}
. Then, from the above inequality, we have that for all
\lambda>\lambda_{U}
, (
4.1
) holds for
p=2
.
∎

Proof.

(Proof of Corollary
1.5
)

The first part comes immediately from Theorem
1.2
by noting that for any
p\geq 2
,
\mathbb{E}[\|u_{\lambda}(t)\|_{L^{p}}^{p}]\leq\mathbb{E}[\|u_{\lambda}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p}]
.

Let us now consider the proof of the second part.
By Fubini’s theorem and Jensen’s inequality, for any
p>2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{1}|u_{\lambda}(t,x)|^{p}dx\right]\geq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}[u_{\lambda}^{2}(t,x)]^{p/2}dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}^{1-\gamma}\inf_{x\in[\gamma,1-\gamma]}\mathbb{E}[u_{\lambda}^{2}(t,x)]^{p/2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-2\gamma)\inf_{x\in[\gamma,1-\gamma]}\mathbb{E}[u_{\lambda}^{2}(t,x)]^{p/2}.

Combining the above estimate with Theorem
1.3
, we deduce that for all
t>0

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{E}_{p}(t,\lambda)\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\log\left((1-2\gamma)\inf_{x\in[\gamma,1-\gamma]}\mathbb{E}[u_{\lambda}^{2}(t,x)])^{p/2}\right)

(4.2)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\log\big((1-2\gamma)\kappa_{1}^{p/2}\big)+\frac{1}{2}(\kappa_{2}\lambda^{4}K_{L}^{4}-2\pi^{2})t.

Finally, dividing both sides by
t
and taking the infimum limit as
t\to\infty
, we see that the lower bound holds for any
\lambda>\lambda_{U}
.
∎

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\Phi

f

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\Phi\Big(\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{\phi(|x-y|)}\Big)dxdy=B<\infty,

\displaystyle|f(x)-f(y)|\leq 8\int_{0}^{|x-y|}\Phi^{-1}\Big(\frac{B}{u}\Big)dp(u)\ a.e.\ x,y\in[0,1].

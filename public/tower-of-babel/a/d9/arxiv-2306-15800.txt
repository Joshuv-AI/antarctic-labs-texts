INR-TH-2023-009 Dark photon production via elastic proton bremsstrahlung with non-zero momentum transfer arXiv: 2306.15800
D. Gorbunov, E. Kriukova

Abstract
We explore hypothetical vector particles, dark photons γ ′ \gamma^{\prime} , which mix with the Standard Model photons and thus mediate interactions with charged particles into the hidden sector. We study the elastic proton bremsstrahlung of dark photons with masses 0.4–1.8 GeV, relevant for direct searches with proton accelerators. A key feature of our calculation is that it explicitly considers the non-zero momentum transfer between protons in the process p ​ p → p ​ p ​ γ ′ pp\rightarrow pp\gamma^{\prime} . We compare the obtained differential and full bremsstrahlung cross sections with the results of other authors. Our calculation agrees well (up to 3–9% corrections) with the Weizsacker-Williams approximation that confirms its applicability for proton beams. Then we refine predictions for the dark photon production with proton beams of energy 30 GeV, 70 GeV, 120 GeV and 400 GeV relevant for past, present and future experiments considered in literature.

2
Overview of the previously suggested methods

One of the first successful attempts to calculate the electron bremsstrahlung is given in
Kim:1973he
. There the modified Weizsacker-Williams approximation is formulated assuming the one-photon exchange in the process
e(a)p(P_{i})\rightarrow\gamma^{\prime}(b)e(c)p(P_{f})
. The photon propagator gives the contribution
1/t^{2}
to the square of matrix element, where
t\equiv-q^{2}
and
q
is the momentum of virtual photon. Then the main contribution comes from a phase space region near the minimal value of
t

\sqrt{t_{\text{min}}}=\frac{(a\cdot b)-m^{2}_{\gamma^{\prime}}/2}{a_{0}-b_{0}}.

(2)

In the lab frame this happens when the difference of 3-dimensional vectors

\vec{a}-\vec{b}
and vector
\vec{c}
are parallel,

\vec{a}-\vec{b}\parallel\vec{c}
. The differential bremsstrahlung cross section is approximated then by the differential cross section of the subprocess
e(a)\gamma(q)\rightarrow\gamma^{\prime}(b)e(c)
taken at
t=t_{\text{min}}

\left[\frac{\differential^{2}\sigma(ep\rightarrow\gamma^{\prime}ep)}{\differential(a\cdot b)\differential(b\cdot P_i)}\right]_{WW}=\left[\frac{\differential\sigma(e\gamma\rightarrow\gamma^{\prime}e)}{\differential(a\cdot b)}\right]_{t=t_{\text{min}}}\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\frac{\chi}{(c\cdot P_{i})},

(3)

where
\alpha
is the fine-structure constant,
\chi
is the flux of photons emitted by the target proton

\chi=\int\displaylimits_{t_{\text{min}}}^{t_{\text{max}}}\frac{t-t_{\text{min}}}{t^{2}}G_{2}(t)\,\differential t

(4)

and
G_{2}(t)
is the proton electric form factor depending on the energy transfer as presented in
Kim:1973he
. This approximation was used to calculate the dark photon production cross section in electron bremsstrahlung
Bjorken:2009mm
. The validity of Weizsacker-Williams approximation for dark photon production in electron bremsstrahlung was questioned in
Liu:2017htz
.

Recently the Weizsacker-Williams approximation has been applied to proton bremsstrahlung
p(p)p(P_{i})\rightarrow\gamma^{\prime}(k)p(p^{\prime})p(P_{f})
in the case of elastic proton scattering by Foroughi-Abari and Ritz

. In this approach the protons exchange virtual effective vector bosons, the so-called Donnachie-Landshoff pomerons

, which have effective propagators and proton-pomeron vertices obtained from fits to experimental data on elastic proton scattering
Donnachie:1992ny
;
Donnachie:2013xia

\left[\frac{\differential^{2}\sigma(pp\rightarrow\gamma^{\prime}pp)}{\differential z\differential k^{2}_{\perp}}\right]_{WW}=\frac{\epsilon^{2}\alpha z(1-z)}{16\pi^{2}H^{2}}A_{2\rightarrow 2}\chi_{\mathbb{P}}\absolutevalue{F_{\text{VMD}}}^{2}F_{\text{virt}}^{2}\,.

(5)

Here
z\equiv k_{z}/p_{z}
is the ratio of projections of dark photon 3-momentum and incident proton 3-momentum on the beam axis,
k_{\perp}
is the transverse momentum of dark photon,

H\equiv k^{2}_{\perp}+(1-z)m^{2}_{\gamma^{\prime}}+z^{2}M^{2},

(6)

M

In the limit
t\rightarrow 0
the squared and averaged matrix element of the subprocess

reads

A_{2\rightarrow 2}=2\left(\frac{1+\left(1-z\right)^{2}}{1-z}-2\left(2M^{2}+m^{2}_{\gamma^{\prime}}\right)\frac{k^{2}_{\perp}z^{2}}{H^{2}}\right).

The pomeron flux from the target proton is

\chi_{\mathbb{P}}=\int\displaylimits_{t_{\text{min}}}^{t_{\text{max}}}\left(t-t_{\text{min}}\right)\absolutevalue{\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{P}}}^{2}\differential t,

Another widely used approximation for the proton bremsstrahlung has been obtained by Blumlein and Brunner in
Blumlein:2013cua
. Although in the original paper the authors study the inelastic bremsstrahlung with inclusive cross section, we find that the similar result can be obtained in the elastic case along the same lines. The idea of the calculation is the following. Proton-proton interaction is considered as the exchange of hypothetical massless vector particles
b
(in full analogy with virtual photons in the electron bremsstrahlung
Kim:1973he

Foroughi-Abari:2021zbm

is extracted from the
pb\rightarrow\gamma^{\prime}p

and the hypothetical boson momentum

is set exactly to 0 revealing

\left[\frac{\differential^{2}\sigma(pp\rightarrow\gamma^{\prime}pp)}{\differential z\differential k^{2}_{\perp}}\right]_{BB}=w_{p\gamma^{\prime}}(z,k_{\perp}^{2})\sigma_{pp}(\bar{s}),

(11)

where the splitting function for
p\rightarrow\gamma^{\prime}p
is given by

\left.+2z(1-z)(1+(1-z)^{2})\frac{M^{2}m_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{2}}{H^{2}}+2z(1-z)^{2}\frac{m^{4}_{\gamma^{\prime}}}{H^{2}}\right],

(12)

5
Discussion

To summarize, we have obtained the elastic bremsstrahlung cross section considering the non-zero momentum transfer between incident and target protons. Our result agrees well with the Weizsacker-Williams approximation. That is the Weizsacker-Williams approximation is applicable not only to structureless fermions, but also to protons and can give reliable results. With our method we present the refined predictions for the dark photon production cross section to be used for the data analysis with proton beams of 30 GeV, 70 GeV, 120 GeV and 400 GeV.

Following the well-known study of the proton bremsstrahlung
Blumlein:2013cua
, in this paper we considered
elastic
proton scattering as the protons exchanging a hypothetical boson coupled to them like a massless vector, with the photon-type propagator. Generally speaking, the elastic proton scattering should be described as the exchange of effective particle, pomeron, with a completely different propagator dependence on 4-momentum squared
q^{2}
(see
Donnachie:2002en
for a review). The pomeron model, closest to our rough consideration, is the Donnachie-Landshoff (DL) model
Donnachie:1983hf
;
Donnachie:1985iz
;
Donnachie:1987gu
. The DL pomeron is a vector with the charge parity
C=+1
. For a long time the DL model was successfully used to describe the elastic proton scatterings as it was one of the first proposed models, being simple, easy to use and adequately describing the experimental data available at the time
Donnachie:2002en
. Later it has been criticised for poor grounding in QFT and has been argued to be ruled out by experiment
Britzger:2019lvc
. However, there are alternative pomeron models considering it as an effective spin-0 or spin-2 particle
Ewerz:2013kda
. The scalar nature of pomeron is argued to be excluded by the STAR experiment and tensor pomeron model is suggested to be the most suitable
Ewerz:2016onn
. Recent works
Lebiedowicz:2022nnn
;
Lebiedowicz:2023mhe
contain the calculations of the SM bremsstrahlung and the central-exclusive production cross sections within the tensor pomeron model. One of the crucial future tasks is to revisit the dark photon production cross section in the framework of contemporary pomeron models.

In order to estimate the dark photon production cross section in the bremsstrahlung taking place in a real fixed-target experiment, one needs to consider the process
pA\rightarrow pA\gamma^{\prime}
, where
A
is the target nuclei. Following
Watanabe:2023rgp
, we assume that the hypothetical vector particle considered in our study has equal coupling constants with proton and neutron (
Q_{b}
charge in our calculation) just like a pomeron. Then the roughest estimate for the cross section of proton bremsstrahlung on a target composed of atoms with the number of nucleons
A
is
\sigma\left(pA\rightarrow pA\gamma^{\prime}\right)=A\times\sigma\left(pp\rightarrow pp\gamma^{\prime}\right)
.

The cross section of the inelastic bremsstrahlung is expected to be an order of magnitude greater than the elastic bremsstrahlung cross section that we studied in this paper. It is the inelastic process that mostly contributes to dark photon production. Therefore it is also important to explore the inelastic dark photon production. From general considerations there should be the possibility to break the bremsstrahlung cross section into the proton (in)elastic scattering cross section and the splitting probability as it has been done by Blumlein and Brunner in
Blumlein:2013cua
. But then it is not clear why the result for elastic cross section obtained in this splitting approach is so different from the Weizsacker-Williams approximation and our non-zero momentum transfer calculation. One of the questions for future research is whether the inelastic bremsstrahlung cross section calculated taking into account non-zero momentum transfer indeed rapidly grows at small
z
as is stated in
Blumlein:2013cua
or not. The task to resolve the differences between existing approaches requires further consideration.

Appendix A
Elastic pp scattering

In this appendix we consider the elastic proton scattering
p\left(p^{\prime}+q\right)p\left(P_{i}\right)\rightarrow p\left(p^{\prime}\right)p\left(P_{f}\right)
. The fit of differential cross section to experimental data is usually parameterized as follows

\derivative{\sigma_{pp}}{q^2}=\frac{\absolutevalue{T_+(\bar{s}, q^2)+T_+^c(\bar{s}, q^2)}^{2}}{16\pi\bar{s}\left(\bar{s}-4M^{2}\right)},

(58)

where the Mandelstam variable is
\overline{s}\equiv\left(p^{\prime}+q+P_{i}\right)^{2}
, functions
T_{+}(\bar{s},q^{2})
and
T_{+}^{c}(\bar{s},q^{2})
are defined as in
ParticleDataGroup:2016lqr
and the numerator is the absolute square of matrix element averaged over initial and summed over final spins,

\overline{\absolutevalue{\mathcal{M}}^{2}}(\bar{s},q^{2})\equiv\absolutevalue{T_+(\bar{s}, q^2)+T_+^c(\bar{s}, q^2)}^{2}.

(59)

The matrix element constructed along the same lines as for the proton bremsstrahlung reads

i\mathcal{M}=\frac{iQ_{b}^{2}}{q^{2}}\bar{u}\left(p^{\prime}\right)\gamma_{\mu}u\left(p^{\prime}+q\right)J^{\mu},

(60)

where
J^{\mu}
is the target proton current. Then the averaged matrix element square is

\overline{\absolutevalue{\mathcal{M}}^{2}}=\frac{1}{4}\frac{Q_{b}^{4}}{\left(q^{2}\right)^{2}}\widetilde{L}_{\mu\nu}W^{\mu\nu},

(61)

with the hadronic tensor of incident proton

\widetilde{L}_{\mu\nu}=4\left(2p^{\prime}_{\mu}p^{\prime}_{\nu}+p^{\prime}_{\mu}q_{\nu}+p^{\prime}_{\nu}q_{\mu}-g_{\mu\nu}p^{\prime\lambda}q_{\lambda}\right)

(62)

and target proton hadronic tensor
W^{\mu\nu}
defined in the main text by eq. (
24
). Since the incident proton hadronic tensor is transversal,
q^{\mu}\widetilde{L}_{\mu\nu}=0
, for the product of hadronic tensors one obtains

\widetilde{L}_{\mu\nu}W^{\mu\nu}=-g^{\mu\nu}\widetilde{L}_{\mu\nu}W_{1}+\frac{1}{M^{2}}P_{i}^{\mu}P_{i}^{\nu}\widetilde{L}_{\mu\nu}W_{2}.

(63)

Let us assume that the functions
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
are of the same order of magnitude as in the case of structureless fermions. Then for typical values of
M
,
q^{2}
and
\bar{s}
the first term in (
63
) is numerically much less than the second one, and hence can be neglected, which yields from eqs. (
59
) and (

W_{2}\left(\bar{s},q^{2}\right)=\frac{2\left(q^{2}\right)^{2}M^{2}}{Q_{b}^{4}}\frac{\absolutevalue{T_+(\bar{s}, q^2) + T_+^c(\bar{s}, q^2)}^{2}}{\left(\bar{s}-2M^{2}\right)^{2}+q^{2}\left(\bar{s}-M^{2}\right)}\,.

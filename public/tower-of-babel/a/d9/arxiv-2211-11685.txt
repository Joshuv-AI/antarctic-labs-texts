Finite Model Properties for Residuated Semigroups
Szabolcs Mikulás

Abstract
. We have a quick look at various finite model properties for residuated semigroups. In particular, we solve Problem 19.17 from the monograph by Hirsch and Hodkinson [ HH02 ] .



1.
Introduction

Residuated semigroups have been investigated on their own right, but also in connection with susbstructural logics, like the
Lambek Calculus (LC) Lambek
[
La58
]
, and relevance logics Anderson et al.
[
ABD92
]
.
Indeed, relational semantics, i.e. models based on binary relations, have been proposed for these substructural logics, see
van Benthem
[
vB91
]
and Routley and Meyer
[
RM73
]
,
and the algebraic settings of these relational semantics give rise to residuated semigroups (and their expansions to larger similarity types).
For instance, representable residuated semigroups (see below) provide sound and complete semantics for LC,
see Andréka and Mikulás
[
AM94
]
.
The connection between (fragments of) relevance logics and (variants of) residuated semigroups have been investigated
in e.g. Bimbó et al.
[
BDM09
]
, Maddux
[
Ma10
]
, Hirsch and Mikulás
[
HM11
]
and Mikulás
[
Mi09
]
,
[
Mi15
]
.

In this note, we will concentrate on representable residuated semigroups with finite bases (see below) and show that they have smaller expressive power
than representable semigroups in general. In particular, we show that there is a finite representable residuated semigroup that is not isomorphic to any
representable residuated semigroup with a finite base, thus providing a solution to Problem 19.17 of Hirsch and Hodkinson
[
HH02
]
.

Next we define (representable) residuated semigroups.
Strictly speaking, these are not algebras but algebraic structures (because of the ordering
\leq
) but the reader should not have any problem with applying the usual algebraic techniques (like taking subalgebras) to algebraic structures.

Definition 1.1
.

A
residuated semigroup

{\mathcal{A}}=(A,\leq,\circ,\mathbin{\backslash},\mathbin{/})
is an algebra such that the following are satisfied:

•

\leq
is a reflexive ordering on
A
,

•

(A,\circ)
is a semigroup,

•

\circ
is monotonic w.r.t.
\leq
,

•

\mathbin{\backslash},\mathbin{/}
are the right and left residuals of
\circ
:

(1)

y\leq x\mathbin{\backslash}z\text{ iff }x\circ y\leq z\text{ iff }x\leq z\mathbin{/}y

for every
x,y,z
in
A
.

We denote the class of residuated semigroups by
{\sf RS}
.

Next we define subclasses of
{\sf RS}
that are isomorphic to algebras of binary relation and the relation
\leq
and operations
\circ,\mathbin{\backslash},\mathbin{/}
have “relational” interpretations.

Let

The subscript

Definition 1.3
.

let
\sf K
be a class of representable algebras (like
{\sf RRS}
or
{\sf RRS}^{\Box}
).

We say that
{\mathcal{A}}\in{\sf K}
is
representable on a finite base
, or
finitely representable
, if the base
U
can be chosen to be finite in Definition
1.2
.

The class
\sf K
has the
finite representation property
(frp) if every finite
{\mathcal{A}}\in{\sf K}
is finitely representable.

2.

3.
Conclusions

The above proof shows that
{\sf RRS}
does not have the
finite base property
(fbp) w.r.t. implications:
the implication

(9)

(a\leq a\circ a\land a\circ a\leq a)\to a\leq a\circ b\backslash b\circ a

is not
{\sf RRS}
-valid but cannot be refuted in any
{\sf RRS}
with finite base.

The solution to the following problem would answer the question of the
finite relational model property
(frmp) for the LC:
whether non-derivable sequents of LC are refutable in relational models with finite bases.

Problem 3.1
.

Does
{\sf RRS}
have the finite base property w.r.t. atomic formulas
t\leq s
(where
t,s
are
\{\circ,\backslash,/\}
-terms of variables).

We can ask the same problems for the square version
{\sf RRS}^{\Box}
of
{\sf RRS}
.
These problems are related to that version of the LC that allows the empty word and sequents with empty antecedents
[
La61
]
.
The corresponding
{\sf RRS}
is a class with reflexive units
W
, thus every
x\mathbin{\backslash}_{W}x
and
x\mathbin{/}_{W}x
subsume the identity relation,
see Andréka and Mikulás
[
AM94
]
.
Thus the proof of Theorem
2.1
does not work in this case.

Problem 3.2
.

Does
{\sf RRS}^{\Box}
have the finite representation and finite base properties?

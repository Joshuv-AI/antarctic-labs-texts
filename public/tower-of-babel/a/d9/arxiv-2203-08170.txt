On the General Position Number of Mycielskian Graphs
Elias John Thomas, Ullas Chandran S. V, James Tuite, Gabriele Di Stefano

Abstract
The general position problem for graphs was inspired by the no-three-in-line problem and the general position subset selection problem from discrete geometry. A set S S of vertices of a graph G G is a general position set if no shortest path between two vertices of S S contains a third element of S S ; the general position number of G G is the size of a largest general position set. In this note we investigate the general position numbers of the Mycielskian of graphs, which are of interest in chromatic graph theory. We give tight upper and lower bounds on the general position number of the Mycielskian of a graph G G and investigate the structure of the graphs meeting these bounds. We determine this number exactly for common classes of graphs including cubic graphs and a wide range of trees.

2
General position in the Mycielskian of complete graphs

To begin our study of the gp-numbers of Mycielskian graphs, we first give the following result concerning the existence of gp-sets in Mycielskian graphs that contain the root vertex
u^{*}
of
\mu(G)
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph with order
n\geq 3
and no isolated vertices. Then the root vertex
u^{*}
of
\mu(G)
belongs to every gp-set of
\mu(G)
if and only if
G
is isomorphic to
K_{n}
.

Proof.

First suppose that
G
is a complete graph. Then the set
V\cup\{u^{*}\}
is a general position set of
\mu(K_{n})
of size
n+1
, and so
{\rm gp}(\mu(K_{n}))\geq n+1
. Let
S
be a gp-set of
\mu(K_{n})
and assume for a contradiction that
u^{*}\not\in S
. If there is no vertex
u
of
G
such that both
u
and its twin
u^{\prime}
are contained in
S
, then we would have
|S|\leq n
, a contradiction; therefore assume that
\{u,u^{\prime}\}\subseteq S
. Let
v\in V\setminus\{u\}
. Then
u,v,u^{\prime}
is a geodesic in
S
and so
v\not\in S
; since
|S|\geq n+1
, we must have
S=V^{\prime}\cup\{u\}
. As
n\geq 3
, there are vertices
v,w\in V\setminus\{u\}
and
v^{\prime},u,w^{\prime}
is a geodesic in
\mu(G)
containing three points of
S
, which is impossible. Therefore we must have
u^{*}\in S
. If a vertex of
V^{\prime}
, say
u^{\prime}
, lies in
S
, then for any
v\not=u
the paths
v,u^{\prime},u^{*}
and
u^{\prime},u^{*},v^{\prime}
are geodesics, which would imply that
|S|\leq 3
, which is too small; therefore
V\cup\{u^{*}\}
is the unique gp-set of
\mu(K_{n})
.

Conversely, suppose that every gp-set of
\mu(G)
contains
u^{*}
and assume for a contradiction that
G
is non-complete. We must have
{\rm gp}(\mu(G))\geq n+1
, for otherwise
V^{\prime}
would be a gp-set not containing
u^{*}
. Let
S
be any gp-set of
\mu(G)
and suppose that there is a vertex
u
of
G
such that
u^{\prime}\in S
. As
u^{*}\in S
, no vertex of
V^{\prime}\setminus\{u^{\prime}\}
is contained in
S
. Furthermore, if
u\sim v
in
G
then
v,u^{\prime},u^{*}
is a geodesic in
\mu(G)
, so
S\cap N_{G}(u)=\emptyset
. Thus
n+1\leq|S|\leq n+2-d_{G}(u)
. There are no isolated vertices in
G
and hence we conclude that
|S|={\rm gp}(\mu(G))=n+1
and
u
is a leaf of
G
. Let the neighbour of
u
in
G
be
v
. All vertices of
V\setminus\{v\}
lie in
S
; in particular the subgraph of
G
induced by
V\setminus\{u,v\}
must be a clique. There is a path of length three from
u^{\prime}
to any vertex of
V\setminus\{u,v\}
via
u^{*}
. As any such path cannot be a geodesic and there is no edge from
u^{\prime}
to
V\setminus\{u,v\}
, there must be a path of length two from
u^{\prime}
to any vertex
w\in V\setminus\{u,v\}
, which implies that
v
is adjacent in
G
to every vertex of
V\setminus\{u,v\}
. Hence
G
is isomorphic to
K_{n-1}
with a pendent vertex
u
attached. However in this case
V^{\prime}\cup\{u\}
is a gp-set of size
n+1
not containing
u^{*}
, a contradiction. Therefore
S=V\cup\{u^{*}\}
. As every vertex of
V
is contained in
S
,

must be a complete graph.
∎

.

For any integer

{\rm gp}(\mu(K_{n}))=n+1

Notice that Mycielskian of

3
General position sets in Mycielskian graphs

Thanks to Lemma
1
, we can now confine our attention to non-complete graphs and assume that there is a gp-set of
\mu(G)
that does not contain the root
u^{*}
. In this section we present sharp upper and lower bounds on the general position number of the Mycielskian of a graph. First we observe the connection between shortest paths in the base graph
G
and the shortest paths in the Mycielskian
\mu(G)
.

Definition 3
.

The
projection
of a path
P=u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{\ell}
in
V(\mu(G))\setminus\{u^{*}\}
is the path with all vertices of
P
that lie in
V^{\prime}
replaced by the corresponding twin vertices in
V
. For example in Figure
1.1
the projection of the path
u_{0},u_{1}^{\prime},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4}^{\prime}
is
u_{0},u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4}
. If
Q
is a path in
V(G)
, then an
expansion
of
Q
is any path in
\mu(G)
with projection equal to
Q
.

Observation 4
.

If
u,v\in V(G)
and
d_{G}(u,v)\leq 3
, then a
u,v
-path in
\mu(G)
is a shortest path in
\mu(G)
if and only if it is an expansion of a shortest path in
G
. If
d_{G}(u,v)\geq 4
and
P
is a shortest
u,v
-path in
\mu(G)
, then either
d_{G}(u,v)=4
and
P
is an expansion of a
u,v
-path of length four in
G
, or else
P
has the form
u,w^{\prime}_{1},u^{*},w^{\prime}_{2},v
, where
u\sim w_{1}
and
v\sim w_{2}
in
G
. If
u\not=v
, then the
u,v^{\prime}
-geodesics in
\mu(G)
are either expansions of
u,v
-geodesics in
G
or have the form
u,w^{\prime},u^{*},v^{\prime}
, where
u\sim w
in
G
.

This observation concerning shortest paths in
\mu(G)
allows us to give a characterisation of the gp-sets in
\mu(G)
in terms of an associated partition of the vertex set of
G
. Lemma
1
shows that for any non-complete graph
G
there exists a gp-set
S
of
\mu(G)
that does not contain the root vertex
u^{*}
of
\mu(G)
; with any such gp-set we can associate a partition
\pi(G)=(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3},V_{4})
of
V(G)
into four (possibly empty) parts by setting
V_{1}=\{u\in V(G):u,u^{\prime}\in S\}
,
V_{2}=\{u\in V(G):u^{\prime}\in S,u\not\in S\}
,
V_{3}=\{u\in V(G):u^{\prime}\not\in S,u\in S\}
and
V_{4}=\{u\in V(G):u,u^{\prime}\not\in S\}
. If we let
n_{i}=|V_{i}|
for
i=1,2,3,4
, then we have
{\rm gp}(\mu(G))=n+n_{1}-n_{4}
. This motivates the following definition.

Definition 5
.

A partition
\pi(G)=(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3},V_{4})
of
V(G)
into four (possibly empty) sets is a
Mycielski partition
if and only if it satisfies the following conditions:

1.

if
e
is an edge in
\langle V_{1}\cup V_{3}\rangle
, then
e
has both endpoints in
V_{3}
,

2.

if a vertex
u
in
V_{1}\cup V_{3}
has a neighbour
v
in
V_{2}
, then
d(u,w)=2
for all
w\in(V_{1}\cup V_{2})\setminus\{u,v\}
and
d(u,w)\leq 3
for all
w\in V_{3}\setminus\{u\}
, and

3.

if
u,v\in V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup V_{3}
and
P
is a shortest
u,v
-path in
G
with length
\ell(P)\leq 4
and
P
passes through another point of
V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup V_{3}
, then either
u,v\in V_{2}
and
\ell(P)\geq 3
, or else either
P
or the reverse of
P
has one of the following forms: i)
u_{0},u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4}
, where
u_{0}\in V_{2}
and
u_{4}\in V_{1}\cup V_{3}
and
u_{3}\not\in V_{1}\cup V_{2}
, ii)
u_{0},u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}
, where
u_{0},u_{1}\in V_{2}
and
u_{3}\in V_{1}\cup V_{3}
or iii)
u_{0},u_{1},u_{2}
, where
u_{0},u_{1}\in V_{2}
.

We note the following three important consequences of the conditions in Definition
5
.

Lemma 6
.

If
(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3},V_{4})
is a Mycielski partition of
G
, then
V_{1}
is an independent set of
G
,
(V_{1},V_{3})=\emptyset
and
(V_{1},V_{2})
is a matching.

Proof.

That
V_{1}
is independent and
(V_{1},V_{3})=\emptyset
follows immediately from Condition 1 in Definition
5
. We now show that
(V_{1},V_{2})
is a matching. By Condition 2 of Definition
5
a vertex
u\in V_{1}
can be at distance one from at most one vertex of
V_{2}
, so each vertex of
V_{1}
has at most one neighbour in
V_{2}
. Suppose now that there is a vertex
w\in V_{2}
with distinct neighbours
u,v\in V_{1}
; as
V_{1}
is independent,
u,w,v
would be a shortest path in
G
of the form forbidden by Condition 3. It follows that the edges in
(V_{1},V_{2})
are independent.
∎

We now complete our characterisation of gp-sets of Mycielskians. We use the following convention: given a set
S
of vertices of a graph, we will call a geodesic containing at most two points of
S

sound
and a shortest path containing at least three points of
S

unsound
.

Theorem 7
.

Let
G
be a non-complete graph with order
n
. For
1\leq i\leq 4
set
n_{i}=|V_{i}|
. Then
{\rm gp}(\mu(G))
is the largest value of
n+n_{1}-n_{4}
over all Mycielski partitions of
G
.

Proof.

Let
\pi(G)=(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3},V_{4})
be a Mycielski partition of
G
and consider the set
S=V_{1}\cup V_{1}^{\prime}\cup V_{2}^{\prime}\cup V_{3}
; we will show that this set is in general position in
\mu(G)
so that
{\rm gp}(\mu(G))\geq n+n_{1}-n_{4}
. We need to verify that all geodesics
P(u,v)
in
\mu(G)
are sound.

First let
u,v\in V_{1}\cup V_{3}
. By Condition 3 of Definition
5
no expansion of a
u,v
-geodesic with length
\leq 4
of
G
contains a third point of
S
. Furthermore if
d_{G}(u,v)\geq 4
then by Observation
4
the only other geodesics to consider are the paths of the form
u,w^{\prime}_{1},u^{*},w^{\prime}_{2},v
, where
u\sim w_{1}
and
v\sim w_{2}
in
G
. Neither of the vertices
w_{1},w_{2}
can lie in
V_{2}
; if say
w_{1}\in V_{2}
, then by Condition 2 of Definition
5
we would have
d_{G}(u,v)\leq 3
, so that
u,w^{\prime}_{1},u^{*},w^{\prime}_{2},v
would not be a shortest path. Hence this path will only fail to be sound if either
w_{1}
or
w_{2}
is in
V_{1}
; however Condition 1 shows that this is not the case, so these paths are also sound.

Now suppose that
u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\in V_{1}^{\prime}\cup V_{2}^{\prime}
. A geodesic
P(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})
in
\mu(G)
has length two and
P
is unsound only if it passes through a common neighbour of
u
and
v
in
V_{1}\cup V_{3}
; by Condition 2 and Lemma
6
this is impossible. We can thus assume that any unsound geodesic
P
has one endpoint in
V_{1}\cup V_{3}
and the other in
V_{1}^{\prime}\cup V_{2}^{\prime}
. If
u\in V_{1}
, the
u,u^{\prime}
-geodesics in
\mu(G)
are the paths
u,w,u^{\prime}
, where
u\sim w
in
G
; by Lemma
6
the vertex
w
does not lie in
S
. Hence we can assume that the endpoints of
P
are not twins of each other; let us denote them by
u,v^{\prime}
, where
u\in V_{1}\cup V_{3}
and
v\in V_{1}\cup V_{2}
and
u\not=v
. By Condition 2 of Definition
5

P
cannot have length two, so by Observation
4
the
u,v^{\prime}
-geodesics in
\mu(G)
are either expansions of shortest paths of length three in
G
, or else have the form
u,w^{\prime},u^{*},v^{\prime}
, where
u\sim w
in
G
. By Condition 3 the former geodesics are sound and the latter are sound by Condition 2. This shows that
S
is in general position.

Conversely, suppose that
S
is in general position in
\mu(G)
and does not include the root vertex
u^{*}
and let
\pi(G)=(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3},V_{4})
be the partition associated with
S
as above; we claim that
\pi(G)
obeys the conditions of Definition
5
, so that
\pi(G)
is a Mycielski partition. To verify Condition 1, suppose that there is an edge
u\sim v
in
\langle V_{1}\cup V_{3}\rangle
, where
u
and
v
do not both lie in
V_{3}
. If
u,v\in V_{1}
, then the path
u,v,u^{\prime}
would be unsound. Similarly, if
u\in V_{1},v\in V_{3}
, then
u,v,v_{1}
is unsound.

To verify Condition 2, suppose that a vertex
u\in V_{1}
has a neighbour
v\in V_{2}
. By Condition 1,
u
has no neighbours in
V_{1}
. Furthermore if
u
had a second neighbour
v_{2}\not=v
in
V_{2}
, then the path
v^{\prime},u,v_{2}^{\prime}
would be unsound. Thus
d(u,w)\geq 2
for each
w\in(V_{1}\cup V_{2}\setminus\{u,v\}
. Suppose that there is a
w\in(V_{1}\cup V_{2})\setminus\{u,v\}
such that
d(u,w)\geq 3
; then
u,v^{\prime},u^{*},w^{\prime}
would be an unsound geodesic. Therefore
d(u,w)=2
for all
w\in(V_{1}\cup V_{2})\setminus\{u,v\}
. Also if
w\in V_{3}
is such that
d(u,w)\geq 4
, then
u,v^{\prime},u^{*},w_{1}^{\prime},w
would be an unsound path, where
w_{1}
is any neighbour of
w
in
G
. This proves our statement for edges in
(V_{1},V_{2})
; the argument for edges in
(V_{2},V_{3})
is very similar.

Finally we turn to Condition 3. Suppose that
P
is a geodesic
u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{\ell}
in
G
with length
\ell\leq 4
that has both endpoints and an internal vertex in
V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup V_{3}
. If
P
has both endpoints in
V_{1}\cup V_{3}
, then
P
has an unsound expansion that is a geodesic in
\mu(G)
, so we can assume that the initial vertex
u_{0}
of
P
is in
V_{2}
. Suppose firstly that
\ell=2
; then we must have
u_{1}\in V_{2}
, for if
u_{1}\in V_{1}\cup V_{3}
, then
u_{0}^{\prime},u_{1},u_{2}
is unsound if
u_{2}\in V_{1}\cup V_{3}
and
u_{0}^{\prime},u_{1},u_{2}^{\prime}
is unsound if
u_{2}\in V_{2}
. Now suppose that
P
has length
\ell\geq 3
and the terminal vertex
u_{\ell}
lies in
V_{1}\cup V_{3}
. Let
\ell=3
. As
u_{0}^{\prime},u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}
and
u_{0}^{\prime},u^{*},u_{2}^{\prime},u_{3}
are shortest paths in
\mu(G)
, we see that
u_{1},u_{2}\not\in V_{1}\cup V_{3}
and
u_{2}\not\in V_{2}
, so that
P
must have the form in variant ii) of Condition 3. Lastly, let
\ell=4
; then
u_{0},u^{*},u_{3}^{\prime},u_{4}
is a shortest path in
\mu(G)
, so that
u_{2}\not\in V_{1}\cup V_{2}
. This completes the proof of the claimed duality between general position sets of
\mu(G)
and Mycielski partitions of
G
.
∎

We now use Theorem
7
to derive tight upper and lower bounds for
{\rm gp}(\mu(G))
. We start with the lower bound.

Corollary 8
.

If
\alpha_{gp}(G)
is the size of the largest independent set of
G
that is also in general position in
G

{\rm gp}(\mu(G))\geq\max\{n,2\alpha_{gp}(G)\}.

(1)

Proof.

Now, let

It was indicated in the previous section that the regular graphs provide a rich source of meagre graphs. In this section we will see that there is a strong natural upper bound on

Theorem 13
.

If

{\rm gp}(\mu(G))\leq n+\frac{d-1}{2}+\frac{1}{d}.

Proof.

Let

V_{2}
must induce a triangle in
G
, so that
V_{3}=\emptyset
. Hence
G
is isomorphic to
G(3)
, completing the proof. ∎

Theorem 17
.

Any
d
-regular graph with order
\geq d^{3}-2d^{2}+2d+2
is meagre.

Proof.

Let
G
be an abundant
d
-regular graph. We can assume that
r=|(V_{1},V_{2})|\geq d>0
. Let
u_{1}\sim v_{1}
be an edge of the matching
(V_{1},V_{2})
, where
u_{1}\in V_{1}
,
v_{1}\in V_{2}
. Consider a breadth-first search tree
T
of depth three rooted at
u
. By Condition 2 in Definition
5
all vertices of
(V_{1}\cup V_{2})\setminus\{u,v\}
are contained in
N^{2}(u)
and
V_{3}
is contained in
N^{2}(u)\cup N^{3}(u)
. There are at most
d^{2}-d
vertices at distance two from
u_{1}
and at most
d(d-1)^{2}
vertices in
N^{3}(u_{1})
.

Moreover there are at least
2d-2
vertices of
V_{1}\cup V_{2}
in
N^{2}(u)
and no such vertex can have descendants at distance three from
u_{1}
in
V_{3}
by Condition 3 in Definition
5
. Therefore we see that

|V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup V_{3}|\leq 2+d^{2}-d+[d^{2}-d-(2d-2)](d-1)=d^{3}-3d^{2}+4d.

As there are at least
d
vertices of
V_{2}
and so as
G
is abundant we must have

n_{4}\leq n_{1}-1\leq 1+(d^{2}-d)-(d-1)-1=d^{2}-2d+1.

In total then the order of
G
is bounded above by
d^{3}-2d^{2}+2d+1
.
∎

In particular this shows that for any value of
d
there are only a finite number of abundant
d
-regular graphs.

5
The Mycielskian of trees and graphs with large girth

We will now investigate the general position number of the Mycielskian of trees and, as a by-product, give a new upper bound on this number for graphs without short cycles. Let
T
be any tree with order
n\geq 3
and
L
be the set of all
\ell(T)
leaves of
T
. Recall that
T
is
abundant
if
{\rm gp}(\mu(T))>\max\{n,2\alpha_{gp}(T)\}
and otherwise is
meagre
. It was shown in
[
6
]
that
{\rm gp}(T)=\ell(T)
; as
L
is an independent set in general position in
T
, it follows that
{\rm gp}(\mu(T))\geq\{n,2\ell(T)\}
and
T
is abundant if strict inequality holds. It turns out that many trees are abundant; we present an exact expression for
{\rm gp}(\mu(T))
for a wide class of trees.

A vertex of
T
is
next-to-terminal
, or
NT
for short, if it is adjacent to a leaf of
T
. We will denote the set of NT vertices of
T
by
W(T)=\{y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{w}\}
and the number of NT vertices of
T
by
w=|W(T)|
, whilst
K=\{z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{k}\}
is the set of vertices in
V(T)\setminus(L\cup W)
, so that
n=\ell+w+k
. We will associate with each NT vertex
y_{i}
the set
L_{i}
of leaves to which it is adjacent in
T
. Let
D_{T}=\min\{d_{T}(l,l^{\prime}):l\in L_{i},l^{\prime}\in L_{j},i\not=j\}
, where
d_{T}
represents the distance in
T
. If there is just one NT vertex in
T
, then
T
is a star and by Theorem
12
we have
{\rm gp}(\mu(T))=2\ell(T)
, so that
T
is meagre. Hence we can assume that
w\geq 2
and
D_{T}
is defined.

We begin with a lower bound on
{\rm gp}(\mu(T))
for trees with NT vertices at distance at least three from each other. We will then proceed to prove that this bound is sharp.

Lemma 18
.

If
T
has
\ell
leaves and
w
NT vertices and also
D_{T}\geq 5
, then the general position number of the Mycielskian
\mu(T)
satisfies

{\rm gp}(\mu(T))\geq n+\ell-w.

Proof.

Recall that
L
is the set of leaves of
T
(and
L^{\prime}
their twins in
\mu(T)
) and
K
is the subset of vertices of
T
that are neither leaves nor NT vertices. It follows from Theorem
7
that
S=L\cup L^{\prime}\cup K^{\prime}
is in general position in
\mu(T)
, implying that
{\rm gp}(\mu(T))\geq 2\ell+k=n+\ell-w
.
∎

In the following let
T
be an abundant tree and
S
be a gp-set of
\mu(T)
, which we can assume does not contain
u^{*}
by Lemma
1
. We now present an upper bound on
{\rm gp}(\mu(G))
for graphs with girth at least six; recall by our convention that the girth of a tree is set as
\infty
, so the following result applies in particular to trees.

Theorem 19
.

Let
G
be a graph with order
n\geq 4
, girth
g(G)\geq 6
and matching number
\nu(G)
. Then

{\rm gp}(\mu(G))\leq 2n-2\nu(G).

Also if
G
satisfies
{\rm gp}(\mu(G))>n
and
\pi(G)=(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3},V_{4})
is a Mycielski partition corresponding to a gp-set
S
of
\mu(G)
that does not contain
u^{*}
, then
(V_{1},V_{2})=(V_{2},V_{3})=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let
G
,
S
and
\pi(G)
be as described. Suppose that the matching
(V_{1},V_{2})
contains an edge
u\sim v
, where
u\in V_{1}
and
v\in V_{2}
. By Condition 2 of Definition
5
all vertices of
(V_{1}\cup V_{2})\setminus\{u,v\}
are contained in
N^{2}(u)
. Suppose that
n_{1}\geq 2
and let
w\in V_{1}\setminus\{u\}
. If
w\in N(v)
, then we cannot have
w\in N(u)
, or else
G
would contain a cycle of length
\leq 4
; therefore
u,v,w
is a shortest path, which is forbidden by Condition 3 of Definition
5
. Thus
G
contains a path
w,x,u,v
, where
x\in N(u)\setminus\{v\}
. Again by Condition 3 this cannot be a shortest path, so there must be a path of length
\leq 2
from
w
to
v
, implying the existence of a cycle of length
\leq 5
, a contradiction. Thus in this case
n_{1}=1
. Thus if
{\rm gp}(\mu(G))>n
, then
n_{4}=0
. As
u
has no neighbours in
V_{3}\cup V_{4}
and just one neighbour in
V_{2}
,
u
is a leaf. Therefore by Conditions 2 and 3 of Definition
5
we have
N(v)=\{u\}\cup(V_{2}\setminus\{v\})
and any vertices of
V_{3}
must lie in
N^{3}(u)
; however, if there is such a vertex
x\in V_{3}
the shortest path
u,x
path in
G
would violate Condition 3. As
G
is triangle-free, it follows that
G
is a star. However, by Corollary
11
the star
S_{n}
has
{\rm gp}(\mu(S_{n}))=2n-2
, whereas in the partition described above all but one vertex of
G
lies in
V_{2}
; since
S
is assumed to be a gp-set, this implies that
{\rm gp}(\mu(G))=n+1=2n-2
, yielding
n=3
.

Suppose now that there is an edge
v\sim w
in
G
, where
v\in V_{2}
and
w\in V_{3}
. By Condition 3 of Definition
5
all vertices of
V_{1}
lie in
N^{2}(v)
; as above, if
u\in V_{1}
, whether or not
u
lies in
N(v)
we either create a short cycle or a path forbidden by Condition 3; thus
n_{1}=0
and
{\rm gp}(\mu(G))=n
, a contradiction.

If
{\rm gp}(\mu(G))=n
, then the claimed bound certainly holds, so assume that
{\rm gp}(\mu(G))>n
. Let
M
be a maximum matching of
G
. If
x\sim y
is any edge in
M
, then it follows from
(V_{1},V_{2})=(V_{1},V_{3})=\emptyset
that the vertices
\{x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\}
of
\mu(G)
can contribute at most two to
{\rm gp}(\mu(G))
; summing over all edges of
M
, we see that the vertices saturated by
M
and their twins contribute at most
2\nu(G)
to
{\rm gp}(\mu(G))
. Assuming that every other vertex lies in
V_{1}
, we obtain the upper bound
{\rm gp}(\mu(G))\leq 2\nu(G)+2(n-2\nu(G))=2n-2\nu(G)
.
∎

Corollary 20
.

If
G
is a graph with order
n\geq 4
and girth
g(G)\geq 6
that has a perfect matching, then
{\rm gp}(\mu(G))=n
.

Corollary 21
.

If
T
is an abundant tree with order
n\geq 3
and matching number
\nu(T)
, then
{\rm gp}(\mu(T))\leq 2n-2\nu(T)
and
(V_{1},V_{2})
and
(V_{2},V_{3})
are empty.

Lemma 22
.

Let
T
be any tree with order
n\geq 3
and
\ell=w
, i.e. each NT vertex of
T
is adjacent to exactly one leaf. Then
{\rm gp}(\mu(T))=n
.

Proof.

We first verify the result for paths using induction. The result is true for the path
P_{4}
by Corollary
20
, as
P_{4}
has a perfect matching. Write the vertex set of
P_{5}
as
\{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\}
, where
v_{i}\sim v_{i+1}
for
i=0,1,2,3
. Suppose that
{\rm gp}(\mu(P_{5}))\geq 6
with corresponding Mycielski partition
(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3},V_{4})
; then first using Theorem
19
on the matching
v_{0}\sim v_{1},v_{2}\sim v_{3}
and then the matching
v_{1}\sim v_{2},v_{3}\sim v_{4}
, we see that
v_{0},v_{4}\in V_{1}
. By Corollary
21
we have
v_{1},v_{3}\in V_{4}
, so that
v_{2}\in V_{1}
; however
T
is now a path of length four containing three vertices of
V_{1}
, contradicting Condition 3 of Definition
5
. Consider now a path
P_{n}
with order
n\geq 6
and assume that the result is true for all paths with order
n^{\prime}
in the range
4\leq n^{\prime}<n
. Let
S
be a gp-set of
\mu(P_{n})
not containing
u^{*}
and let
x
be a leaf of
P_{n}
and
y
its neighbour. By Theorem
19
at most two of the vertices
\{x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\}
lie in
S
. The subtree
P_{n}\setminus\{x,y\}
has
{\rm gp}(\mu(P_{n}\setminus\{x,y\}))\leq n-2
by induction; as
\mu(P_{n}\setminus\{x,y\}))
is an isometric subgraph of
P_{n}
we have
{\rm gp}(\mu(P_{n}))\leq n
, establishing the result for paths.

We may now assume that
T
has order
n\geq 4
and contains a vertex
u
with degree
d(u)\geq 3
and that the result is true for all trees with order in the range
3\leq n^{\prime}<n
. Let
x
be an eccentric vertex of
u
; as
u
is adjacent to at most one leaf,
x
is a leaf at distance
\geq 2
from
u
. Let
P
be the
x,u
-path in
T
and
Q
be the longest subpath of
P
with initial vertex
x
such that for every vertex
v\in V(Q)
we have
d(v)=2
. Let
Q
have order
n_{2}
. As
\ell(T)=w(T)
the path
Q
has length at least one, i.e.
d(y)=2
. Deleting
Q
from
T
leaves a tree
T_{1}
with order
n_{1}\geq 4
and
\ell(T_{1})=w(T_{1})
; by induction
{\rm gp}(\mu(T_{1}))=n_{1}
. If
n_{2}\not=3
the previous result implies that
{\rm gp}(\mu(Q))=n_{2}
and so
{\rm gp}(\mu(T))\leq{\rm gp}(\mu(T_{1}))+{\rm gp}(\mu(Q))=n_{1}+n_{2}=n
.

Thus we can assume that
n_{2}=3
. Let the
x,u
-path in
T
be
x,u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},\dots,u
. As
Q
has length two, we must have
d(u_{3})\geq 3
. Hence when we consider the distance partition of
T
relative to
u
, the vertex
u_{3}
has a descendent
w
at distance
d(u,x)-2
from
u
apart from
u_{2}
. Let the subtree induced by
w
and its descendants be denoted by
Q_{2}
. By the foregoing argument
Q_{2}
is either isomorphic to
K_{1}
(i.e.
w
is a leaf) or to
P_{3}
. Deleting
P^{\prime}
will work in our induction unless there are four vertices of
\{x,u_{1},u_{2},x^{\prime},u_{1}^{\prime},u_{2}^{\prime}\}
contained in
S
, in which case by Corollary
21
we must have
x,u_{2}\in V_{1}
. If
Q\cong K_{1}
then our induction argument works unless
w\in V_{1}
and, as with
Q
, if
Q_{2}\cong P_{3}
then we can again assume that
w\in V_{1}
. However, this implies that
x,u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},w
is a path of length four in
T
containing three vertices of
V_{1}
, which is forbidden by Condition 3 of Definition
5
. This contradiction establishes the result.
∎

Corollary 23
.

Let
T
be a tree with order
n\geq 3
,
\ell
leaves and
w
NT vertices. Then
{\rm gp}(\mu(T))\leq n+\ell-w
.

Proof.

For each NT vertex of
T
deleted all but one leaf to form a tree
T_{1}
with order
n-\ell+w
. By Lemma
22
we have
{\rm gp}(\mu(T_{1}))=n-\ell+w
. The deleted
\ell-w
leaves make a contribution of at most
2(\ell-w)
to
{\rm gp}(\mu(T))
. It follows that
{\rm gp}(\mu(T))\leq(n-\ell+w)+2(\ell-w)=n+\ell-w
.
∎

Combined with the lower bound from Lemma
18
this yields an exact expression when all NT vertices of
T
are at distance at least three from each other.

Corollary 24
.

Let
T
be a tree with order
n\geq 3
,
\ell
leaves,
w
NT vertices and
D_{T}\geq 5
. Then
{\rm gp}(\mu(T))=n+\ell-w
.

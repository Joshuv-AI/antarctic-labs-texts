Linking coefficients and the Kontsevich integral
Jean-Baptiste Meilhan

Abstract
. It is well known how the linking number and framing can be extracted from the degree 1 1 part of the (framed) Kontsevich integral. This note gives a general formula expressing any product of powers of these two invariants as combination of coefficients in the Kontsevich integral. This allows in particular to express the sum of all coefficients of a given degree in terms of the linking coefficients. The proofs are purely combinatorial.



1.
Introduction

The
Kontsevich integral
is a strong invariant of framed oriented knots and links, which dominates all rational finite type invariants and all Witten–Reshetikhin–Turaev quantum invariants, in the sense that any other factors through it.
It takes values in a certain space of
chord diagrams
, which are copies of the oriented unit circle, endowed with a number of chords, which are pairings of pairwise disjoint points on the circles; chord diagrams naturally come with a degree, which is given by the number of chords.
Kontsevich defined this invariant in terms of iterated integrals, what can be seen as a far-reaching generalization of the Gauss integral for the linking number of two curves
[
6
]
. As a matter of fact, it is well-known that the linking number
appears as the simplest coefficient in the Kontsevich integral. Specifically, given a framed link
L
, denoting by
C_{L}[D]
the coefficient
1
1

1

of a chord diagram
D
in the Kontsevich integral of
L
, and denoting by
\ell_{ij}
the linking number of the
i
th and
j
th components,
we have

(
1_{1}
)

\ell_{ij}(L)=C_{L}[_{i}\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[height]{D12.pdf}}}\!\!\textrm{ }_{j}].

Denoting half the framing of the
i
th component of
L
by
\ell_{ii}(L)
, it is also well-known that

(
2_{1}
)

\ell_{ii}(L)=\frac{1}{2}fr_{i}(L)=C_{L}[\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[height]{D11.pdf}}}\!\textrm{ }_{i}].

Hence the degree
1
part of the Kontsevich integral of a link is fully characterized by the linking coefficients,
i.e.
the coefficients of the linking matrix.

The main result of this note is Theorem
1

which generalizes these two elementary results.
This provides a general (
i.e.
in all degrees) formula identifying certain combinations of coefficients in the Kontsevich integral in terms of the coefficients of the linking matrix.
A number of works investigate, in a similar way, how combinations of coefficients in the Kontsevich integral can be expressed in terms of classical invariants of knot theory, see for example
[

be the set of symmetric matrices of size

Given

Theorem 1

3.
Linking coefficients and the Kontsevich integral

The purpose of this section is to prove Theorem
1
. This will be done in Subsection
3.3
, after setting some notation and preliminary results in the next two subsections.

3.1.
Some notation

Let
A
be an element of
\mathcal{A}(m)
. Let
D
be a chord diagram on
m
circles.
We denote by
C[D](A)
the coefficient of
D
in
A
.
In particular, we set

C_{L}[D]:=C[D](Z(L)),

for a framed oriented link
L
, that is,
C_{L}[D]
denotes ‘the coefficient’  of diagram
D
in the Kontsevich integral of
L
.
This quantity is of course in general not well-defined since an element of
\mathcal{A}(m)
consists of diagrams subject to the
4T
relation, but taking an appropriate combination of such coefficients shall yield a link invariant, see Claim

3.1
below.

We denote by
C[D]
the assignment
A\mapsto C[D](A)
and, abusing notation, we still denote by
C[D]
the precomposition
L\mapsto C_{L}[D]
with the Kontsevich integral.

Let
S=(s_{ij})_{i,j}\in\mathcal{S}_{m}
be a symmetric matrix of size
m
with entries in
\mathbb{N}
.
We set

\mathcal{L}_{S}:=\sum_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{S}(m)}C[D],

where
\mathcal{D}_{S}(m)
is the set of all chord diagrams on
m
circles with exactly
s_{ij}
chords of type
(i,j)
for all
i\leq j
, as defined in the introduction.

Claim 3.1
.

This formula yields a well-defined map on
\mathcal{A}(m)
, and in particular defines an
m
-component link invariant.

This is straighforwardly checked using the following general invariance criterion:
given a collection
\mathcal{D}
of chord diagrams, the assignement
X:=\sum_{D\in\mathcal{D}}C[D]
defines a map on
\mathcal{A}(m)
if and only if
X
vanishes on any linear combination of chord diagrams arising from a
4T
relation. See for example
[
3
]
.

We also recall from the introduction the link invariant associated with the symmetric matrix
S
,

\ell_{S}=\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq m}\frac{1}{s_{ij}!}\ell_{ij}^{s_{ij}},

where
\ell_{ij}
is the linking number between components
i
and
j
if
i\neq j
, and
\ell_{ii}:=\frac{1}{2}fr_{i}
is half the framing of the
i
th component.

3.2.
Crossing change formula for the invariant
\mathcal{L}_{S}

Let us pick some indices
a,b
in
\{1,\cdots,m\}
(possibly with
a=b
).
Consider two
m
-component links
L_{+}
and
L_{-}
, that are identical away from a small
3

L_{+}=\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[height]{over.pdf}}}\quad\textrm{and}\quad L_{-}=\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[height]{under.pdf}}}.

We stress that this crossing change may involve two strands of either the same (
a=b
) or different (
a\neq b
) components.

Now let
S=(s_{ij})_{i,j}\in\mathcal{S}_{m}
.
By the mere definition of the Kontsevich integral at a crossing, we have

\mathcal{L}_{S}(L_{+})-\mathcal{L}_{S}(L_{-})=\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{1}{(2j+1)!2^{2j}}\mathcal{L}_{S}(D_{2j+1}),

where
D_{k}\in\mathcal{A}(m)
is obtained from the Kontsevich integral of
L_{\pm}

by replacing the local contribution of the crossing involved in the crossing change, as given in (
2.1
), by the local diagram

with exactly
k
parallel chords.

Set
s:=s_{ab}=s_{ba}
, the entry of the matrix
S
corresponding to our crossing change.
In order to slightly simplify our notation, for any
p
such that
s\geq p\geq 0
we denote by
\mathcal{L}_{p}
the invariant
\mathcal{L}_{S_{p}}
, where
S_{p}
is the matrix
S
with the coefficient
s

;
in particular we have
\mathcal{L}_{s}=\mathcal{L}_{S}
.

Clearly, we have
\mathcal{L}_{s}(D_{k})=0
for
k>s
, hence the variation formula

(3.1)

\mathcal{L}_{S}(L_{+})-\mathcal{L}_{S}(L_{-})=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{s+1}{2}\rfloor}\frac{1}{(2j+1)!2^{2j}}\mathcal{L}_{s}(D_{2j+1}).

When
k\leq s
, we have the following.

Claim 3.2
.

For all
k
such that
s\geq k\geq 0
, we have
\mathcal{L}_{s}(D_{k})=\mathcal{L}_{s-k}(D_{0})
.

Proof.

A degree
n
chord diagram on
m
circles that contributes to
\mathcal{L}_{s}(D_{k})
necessarily
contains
k
parallel chords of type
(a,b)
, as imposed by the definition of

.
The set of all diagrams contributing to
\mathcal{L}_{s}(D_{k})
is thus obtained by adding

chords, with exactly
s-k

(a,b)
, in all possible ways. But since these additional chords do not arise from the crossing change, they are attached outside a disk containing the
k
parallel chords of type
(a,b)
.
This is thus equivalent to taking the contribution of
all
chord diagrams in
\mathcal{D}_{S_{s-k}}
.
∎

By Claim
3.2
, computing the variation
\mathcal{L}_{S}(L_{+})-\mathcal{L}_{S}(L_{-})
reduces to computing
\mathcal{L}_{k}(D_{0})
for all
k
.
This is done in the next lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

We have

\mathcal{L}_{k}(D_{0})=\sum_{p=0}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{p}}{p!2^{p}}\mathcal{L}_{k-p}(L_{+})

for all
k
;
s\geq k\geq 0
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
k
.
The formula for
k=0
is clear:
the invariant
\mathcal{L}_{0}
vanishes on any diagram with a chord of type
(a,b)
,
so that
\mathcal{L}_{0}(D_{k})=0
for all
k\geq 1
,
and by definition of the Kontsevich integral at a positive crossing (
2.1
), we thus have

\mathcal{L}_{0}(L_{+})=\mathcal{L}_{0}(D_{0})
.
For the inductive step, again by definition of the Kontsevich integral at a positive crossing, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}(D_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}(L_{+})-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{j!2^{j}}\mathcal{L}_{k}(D_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}(L_{+})-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{j!2^{j}}\mathcal{L}_{k-j}(D_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}(L_{+})-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{j!2^{j}}\sum_{i=0}^{k-j}\frac{(-1)^{i}}{i!2^{i}}\mathcal{L}_{k-j-i}(L_{+}),

where the second equality follows from Claim
3.2
, while the third equality uses the induction hypothesis.
For each
p
such that
0\leq p\leq k
, the coefficient of
\mathcal{L}_{k-p}(L_{+})
in the above double sum is then given by

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{p}\frac{1}{j!2^{j}}\times\frac{(-1)^{p-j}}{(p-j)!2^{p-j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-1}{p!2^{p}}\sum_{j=1}^{p}(-1)^{p-j}\binom{p}{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-1}{p!2^{p}}\Big(\underbrace{\sum_{j=0}^{p}(-1)^{p-j}\binom{p}{j}}_{=0}-(-1)^{p}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{p}}{p!2^{p}}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1

We proceed by induction on the degree
|S|=\sum_{i\leq j}s_{ij}

The base case

1

) and (

Now, assume that the formula holds for all matrices of
\mathcal{S}_{m}
of degree

Let
S\in\mathcal{S}_{m}
be a degree
k

Choose some indices
a,b

is nonzero (possibly

Let

and

be two

Combining (

By the induction hypothesis, we have

\mathcal{L}_{s-2j-k-1}(L_{+})=\frac{1}{(s-2j-k-1)!}\ell_{ab}^{s-2j-k-1}\prod_{\{i,j\}\neq\{a,b\}}\frac{1}{s_{ij}!}\ell_{ij}^{s_{ij}}.

Setting
\ell_{0}:=\prod_{\{i,j\}\neq\{a,b\}}\frac{1}{s_{ij}!}\ell_{ij}^{s_{ij}}

\mathcal{L}_{S}(L_{+})-\mathcal{L}_{S}(L_{-})=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{s+1}{2}\rfloor}\frac{\ell_{0}}{(2j+1)!2^{2j}}\sum_{k=0}^{s-2j-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!(s-2j-k-1)!2^{k}}\ell_{ab}(L_{+})^{s-2j-k-1}.

The coefficient of
\ell_{ab}(L_{+})^{s-i}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{i+1}{2}\rfloor}\frac{\ell_{0}}{(2j+1)!2^{2j}}.\frac{(-1)^{i-2j-1}}{(i-2j-1)!(s-i)!2^{i-2j-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{i+1}\ell_{0}}{2^{i-1}(s-i)!}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{i+1}{2}\rfloor}\frac{1}{i!}\binom{i}{2j+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{i+1}\ell_{0}}{2^{i-1}i!(s-i)!}\underbrace{\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{i+1}{2}\rfloor}\binom{i-1}{2j}+\binom{i-1}{2j+1}}_{=2^{i-1}}.

This shows that

\mathcal{L}_{S}(L_{+})-\mathcal{L}_{S}(L_{-})=\ell_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{i+1}}{i!(s-i)!}\ell_{ab}^{s-i}.

Now, this formula coincides with the variation of the linking invariant
\ell_{S}

\ell_{S}(L_{+})-\ell_{S}(L_{-})=\ell_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{i+1}}{i!(s-i)!}\ell_{ab}^{s-i}.

This is easily verified using the binomial formula, noting that

Hence we showed that the invariants
\mathcal{L}_{S}

have the same variation formula under a crossing change.
By a sequence of such operations, any
m

Denote by

In the case where

Let us first compute

This can be used to check that

3.4.
An alternative proof of Theorem
1

We briefly sketch, in this final section, another argument for proving Theorem
1
, that was pointed out by the referee.

Recall that a
Jacobi diagram
on an oriented
1
-manifold
X
is a unitrivalent diagram, whose trivalent vertices are equipped with a cyclic ordering of the three incident edges and whose set of univalent vertices is embedded in
X
; each connected component is further assumed to contain at least one univalent vertex.
In particular, a chord diagram as defined in Section
2
is merely a Jacobi diagram without trivalent vertices.
The degree of a Jacobi diagram is defined as half its total number of vertices, what agrees with the number of chords for a chord diagram.
We denote by
\mathcal{J}(X)
the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space generated by all Jacobi diagrams on
X
, modulo the STU relation. We shall consider here the case where
X
is either the disjoint union
\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[height]{D10.pdf}}}^{\,m}
of
m
copies of the oriented circle, or the disjoint union
\uparrow^{\,m}
of
m
copies of the oriented interval.
Both
\mathcal{J}(\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[height]{D10.pdf}}}^{\,m})
and
\mathcal{J}(\uparrow^{\,m})
have a coalgebra structure, where the coproduct
\Delta(J)
of a diagram
J
is given by the sum of all ways of splitting
J
in a disjoint union of two Jacobi diagrams; this actually endows
\mathcal{J}(\uparrow^{\,m})
with a Hopf algebra structure, with product given by stacking intervals. Picking an oriented interval in each component of
\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[height]{D10.pdf}}}^{\,m}
yields a canonical map
\iota:\mathcal{J}(\uparrow^{\,m})\rightarrow\mathcal{J}(\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[height]{D10.pdf}}}^{\,m})
.
There is a natural isomorphism
\varphi:\mathcal{J}(\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[height]{D10.pdf}}}^{\,m})\rightarrow\mathcal{A}(m)
, given by expressing a Jacobi diagram as a linear combination of chord diagrams using the STU relation, see
[
1
, Thm. 6]
.

We also need the space
\mathcal{B}(m)
of labeled Jacobi diagrams, which is the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space spanned by unitrivalent diagrams with univalent vertices labeled by
\{1,\cdots,m\}
, modulo the AS and IHX relations, see
[
1
]
. This is a graded Hopf algebra, with product given by the disjoint union
\sqcup
, and graded by half the number of vertices.
As a diagrammatic analogue of the PBW isomorphim, we have a graded Hopf algebra isomorphism
\chi:\mathcal{B}(m)\rightarrow\mathcal{J}(\uparrow^{\,m})
, which acts by averaging all ways of attaching the
k
-labeled univalent vertices of a diagram along the
k
th oriented interval of
\uparrow^{\,m}
.

Given a framed link
L
in
S^{3}
, a fundamental property of the Kontsevich integral is that
Z(L)
is a
group-like
element in
\mathcal{J}(\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[height]{D10.pdf}}}^{\,m})
, see
[
8
, Thm. 3.7]
.
More precisely, we have that

Z(L)=\iota\circ\chi\left(\textrm{exp}_{\sqcup}\Bigg(\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq m}\ell_{ij}I_{ij}\Bigg)\sqcup T\right)\in\mathcal{J}(\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[height]{D10.pdf}}}^{\,m}),

where the
\ell_{ij}=\ell_{ij}(L)
are the linking coefficients as above,
I_{ij}
denotes the (dashed) interval with endpoints labeled by
i
and
j
, and where
T
is a linear combination of possibly disconnected labeled Jacobi diagrams, each having at least one trivalent vertex.
Now, the formula of Theorem
1
can be derived as follows (here we freely use of the notation of the Section
2
).
Given
S=(s_{ij})_{i,j}\in\mathcal{S}_{m}
, let
\mathcal{C}_{S}:\mathcal{J}(\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[height]{D10.pdf}}}^{\,m})\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}
be the map defined by
\mathcal{C}_{S}(J)=\sum_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{S}(m)}C_{\varphi(J)}[D]
; for a link
L
in
S^{3}
, note that
\mathcal{C}_{S}(Z(L))
is precisely the sum of coefficients
\sum_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{S}(m)}C_{L}[D]
involved in Theorem
1
.
As in the proof of Claim
3.4
, we have that
\mathcal{C}_{S}(T)=0
by the STU relation. Hence the above expression for
Z(L)
gives

\mathcal{C}_{S}(Z(L))=\mathcal{C}_{S}\circ\iota\circ\chi\left(\textrm{exp}_{\sqcup}\Bigg(\sum_{i\leq j}\ell_{ij}I_{ij}\Bigg)\right)=\sum_{n}\frac{1}{n!}\mathcal{C}_{S}\circ\iota\circ\chi\Bigg(\sum_{i\leq j}\ell_{ij}I_{ij}\Bigg)^{\sqcup n}.

All terms in the right-hand sum are zero, except when
n=|S|=\sum_{i\leq j}s_{ij}
is the degree of
S
(where we have the ‘right number’ of chords). For
n=|S|
, we have that

\frac{1}{|S|!}\Bigg(\sum_{i\leq j}\ell_{ij}I_{ij}\Bigg)^{\sqcup|S|}=\prod_{i\leq j}\frac{1}{s_{ij}!}\ell_{ij}^{s_{ij}}I_{ij}^{\,\sqcup s_{ij}}=\ell_{S}(L)I_{ij}^{\,\sqcup s_{ij}}.

Since
\mathcal{C}_{S}\circ\iota\circ\chi\left(I_{ij}^{\,\sqcup s_{ij}}\right)=1
as a direct consequence of the definitions, the conclusion follows.

Foldings of KLR algebras
Ying Ma, Toshiaki Shoji and Zhiping Zhou

Abstract
. Let 𝐔 q − \mathbf{U}_{q}^{-} be the negative half of the quantum group associated to the Kac-Moody algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} , and 𝐔 ¯ q − \underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-} the quantum group obtained by a folding of 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} . Let 𝐀 = 𝐙 ⁡ [ q , q − 1 ] \mathbf{A}=\mathbf{Z}[q,q^{-1}] . McNamara showed that 𝐔 ¯ q − \underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-} is categorified over a certain extension ring 𝐀 ~ \widetilde{\mathbf{A}} of 𝐀 \mathbf{A} , by using the folding theory of KLR algebras. He posed a question whether 𝐀 ~ \widetilde{\mathbf{A}} coincides with 𝐀 \mathbf{A} or not. In this paper, we give an affirmative answer for this problem.



Introduction

Let
X=(I,(\ ,\ ))
be a Cartan datum,
and
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
the negative half of the quantum group associated to
X
.
Let
Q=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbf{Z}\alpha_{i}
be the root lattice of
X
,
and set
Q_{+}=\sum_{i\in I}\mathbf{N}\alpha_{i}
.
Set
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
, and let
{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
be Lusztig’s integral
form of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
, which is an
\mathbf{A}
-subalgebra of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
.
In the case where
X
is of symmetric type,
Lusztig constructed in [L1] the canonical basis of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
, by using
the geometry of a quiver
\overrightarrow{Q}
associated to
X
. More precisely,
he constructed, for each
\beta\in Q_{+}
, a category
\mathcal{Q}_{V_{\beta}}
consisting of
certain semisimple
\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{l}
-complexes on the representation space
V_{\beta}
of
\overrightarrow{Q}

associated to
\beta
, and showed that the direct sum

K(\mathcal{Q})=\bigoplus_{\beta\in Q_{+}}K(\mathcal{Q}_{V_{\beta}})

of the Grothendieck group
K(\mathcal{Q}_{V_{\beta}})
of
\mathcal{Q}_{V_{\beta}}
has a structure of

A
-algebra, which
is isomorphic to
{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
.
The canonical basis of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is obtained from a natural basis of

K(\mathcal{Q}_{V_{\beta}})
coming from simple perverse sheaves in

\mathcal{Q}_{V_{\beta}}
.

Let
\sigma
be an admissible diagram automorphism on
X
(see 1.5), which
gives a bijection
\sigma:I\to I
.
Let
J
be the set of
\sigma
-orbits in
I
.
Then
\underline{X}=(J,(\ ,\ ))
gives a Cartan datum.
We denote by
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
the corresponding quantum group.
In [L2], Lusztig constructed the canonical basis of
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
,
by extending the discussion in the symmetric case.
By a suitable choice of the orientation,
\sigma
acts on the quiver
\overrightarrow{Q}
,
and it induces a periodic functor
\sigma^{*}
on
\mathcal{Q}_{V_{\beta}}
whenever
\beta
is
\sigma
-stable.
He defined a new category
\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{V_{\beta}}
attached to
\mathcal{Q}_{V_{\beta}}
and
\sigma^{*}
,
and a (modified) Grotehndieck group
K(\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{V_{\beta}})
.
Let
Q_{+}^{\sigma}
be the set of
\sigma
-stable elements in
Q_{+}
. Then the direct sum

K(\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}})=\bigoplus_{\beta\in Q_{+}^{\sigma}}K(\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{V_{\beta}})
has a structure of

\mathbf{Z}[\zeta_{\mathbf{n}},q,q^{-1}]
-algebra, where
\mathbf{n}
is the order of
\sigma
, and
\zeta_{\mathbf{n}}

is a primitive
\mathbf{n}
-th root of unity in
\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{l}
.
He proved that a certain
\mathbf{A}
-subalgebra
{}_{\mathbf{A}}K(\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}})
is isomorphic to

{}_{\mathbf{A}}\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
. In that case, a natural basis of
{}_{\mathbf{A}}K(\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}})
gives
the canonical basis of
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
, up to sign, i.e., gives the canonical signed basis.
The canonical basis of
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
is obtained from it by suitably fixing the signs, which
was done by an algebraic argument using Kashiwara’s tensor product rule.

K(\mathcal{Q})
(for symmetric
X
) gives a geometric categorification of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
.
On the other hand,
in [KL], [R1], Khovanov-Lauda, and Rouquier introduced the Khovanov-Lauda-Rouquier
algebra
R=\bigoplus_{\beta\in Q_{+}}R(\beta)
(KLR algebra in short) over an algebraically
closed field
\mathbf{k}
, and gave
a categorification of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
(for general
X
) in terms of KLR algebras.
For each
\beta\in Q_{+}
, let

R(\beta)\operatorname{-{\bf gp}}
be the category of finitely generated graded projective
R(\beta)
-modules,
and
K_{\operatorname{{\bf gp}}}(\beta)
the Grothendieck group of
R(\beta)\operatorname{-{\bf gp}}
. They proved that

K_{\operatorname{{\bf gp}}}=\bigoplus_{\beta\in Q_{+}}K_{\operatorname{{\bf gp}}}(\beta)
has a structure of
\mathbf{A}
-algebra, and
is isomorphic to
{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
.

It is natural to expect a similar discussion as in Lusztig’s case will hold
also for KLR algebras. McNamara [M] developed the theory of foldings for
KLR algebras. Let
X
and
\underline{X}
be as above (for
X
: general type),
and choose
\beta\in Q_{+}^{\sigma}
.
By a suitable choice of defining parameters of
R
,
\sigma
acts on
R(\beta)
, thus
we obtain a periodic functor
\sigma^{*}
on
R(\beta)\operatorname{-{\bf gp}}
. One can define a new category

\widetilde{R}(\beta)\operatorname{-{\bf gp}}=\mathscr{P}_{\beta}
, and a modified Grothendieck group
K(\mathscr{P}_{\beta})
.

K(\mathscr{P})=\bigoplus_{\beta\in Q_{+}^{\sigma}}K(\mathscr{P}_{\beta})
has a structure of

\mathbf{Z}[\zeta_{\mathbf{n}},q,q^{-1}]
-algebra. McNamara constructed a natural basis
\underline{\mathbf{B}}

of
K(\mathscr{P})
by using the theory of crystals. Define an extension
\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}
of
\mathbf{A}

as the smallest
subring
\mathbf{Z}[\zeta_{\mathbf{n}},q,q^{-1}]
containing the structure constants of

\underline{\mathbf{B}}
with respect to the product in
K(\mathscr{P})
. Hence the
\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}
-span
{}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}}K(\mathscr{P})

of
\underline{\mathbf{B}}
gives an
\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}
-algebra. He proved that
{}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}}K(\mathscr{P})
is
isomorphic to
{}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}}\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
, where
{}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}}\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
is the extension
of
{}_{\mathbf{A}}\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
to
\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}
, thus obtained a categorification of
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
.
He showed that
\mathbf{A}\subset\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}\subset\mathbf{Z}[\zeta_{\mathbf{n}}+\zeta_{\mathbf{n}}^{-1},q,q^{-1}]
,
and posed a question whether
\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}
coincides with
\mathbf{A}
or not ([M, Question 11.2]).

The aim of this paper is to show that this certainly holds (Theorem 3.16).
The main ingredient for the proof is the following result proved in [MSZ].
We consider the case where the order
\mathbf{n}
of
\sigma
is a power of a prime number
\ell
.
Let
\mathbf{F}=\mathbf{Z}/\ell\mathbf{Z}
be the finite field of
\ell
-elements. Let

\mathbf{A}^{\prime}=\mathbf{F}[q,q^{-1}]=\mathbf{A}/\ell\mathbf{A}
, and consider the
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
-algebras

{}_{\mathbf{A}^{\prime}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
and
{}_{\mathbf{A}^{\prime}}\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
, by changing the base ring from
\mathbf{A}

to
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
.
\sigma
acts naturally on
{}_{\mathbf{A}^{\prime}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
, and let
{}_{\mathbf{A}^{\prime}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-,\sigma}

be the fixed point subalgebra by
\sigma
. In [MSZ, Thm. 3.4, Thm. 4.27], it is proved
that
{}_{\mathbf{A}^{\prime}}\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
is realized as the quotient algebra of
{}_{\mathbf{A}^{\prime}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-,\sigma}
,
and that the canonical basis of
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
is obtained from the
\sigma
-stable canonical
basis of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
, up to sign, through the quotient map.
This gives an alternate approach for Lusztig’s geometric construction of canonical signed
basis of
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
. A similar argument also works for the KLR algebras. In this scheme,
the finite field
\mathbf{F}
enters in the theory, but
\zeta_{\mathbf{n}}
is not involved.
By comparing this with McNamara’s isomorphism, we obtain the required result.

In the case where
X
is symmetric, and
R
is a symmetric type KLR algebra (see 5.1)
over a field
\mathbf{k}
of characteristic zero, Varagnolo-Vasserot [VV] and Rouquier [R2] proved
that the natural basis of
K_{\operatorname{{\bf gp}}}
gives the canonical basis (which coincides with
Kashiwara’s lower global basis by [GL]) of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
, by using
the geometric interpretation of
R
in terms of the Ext algebra coming from Lusztig’s
category
\mathcal{Q}_{V_{\beta}}
. A similar result holds also for
K(\mathscr{P})
, namely,
the basis
\underline{\mathbf{B}}
corresponds, via McNamara’s isomorphism, to the canonical basis
of
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
, which coincides with the lower global basis. This is stated in
the last paragraph of [M], with a brief indication for the proof, which is based
on the geometric argument. In Theorem 5.13 and Theorem 5.16,
we give a simple proof of this fact, without using the geometry
(though assuming the results in [VV], [R2]).

1.
Quantum groups

1.1.

Let
X=(I,(\ ,\ ))
be a Cartan datum, where
I
is a finite set,
and
(\ ,\ )
is a symmetric bilinear form on the vector space

\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbf{Q}\alpha_{i}
with basis
\alpha_{i}
, satisfying the properties
that

(i)

(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})\in 2\mathbf{Z}_{>0}
for any
i\in I
,

(ii)

\frac{2(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i^{\prime}})}{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})}\in\mathbf{Z}_{\leq 0}

for any
i\neq i^{\prime}\in I
.

For
i,i^{\prime}\in I
, set
a_{ii^{\prime}}=2(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i^{\prime}})/(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})\in\mathbf{Z}
.

A=(a_{ii^{\prime}})
is called the Cartan matrix associated to
X
.
The Cartan datum is called symmetric if
(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})=2
for any
i\in I
.

Let
Q=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbf{Z}\alpha_{i}
be the root lattice of
X
.
We set
Q_{+}=\sum_{i\in I}\mathbf{N}\alpha_{i}
, and
Q_{-}=-Q_{+}
.

1.2.

Let
q
be an indeterminate, and for an integer
n
, a positive integer
m
,
set

[n]=\frac{q^{n}-q^{-n}}{q-q^{-1}},\quad[m]^{!}=[1][2]\cdots[m],\quad[0]^{!}=1.

For each
i\in I
, set
d_{i}=(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})/2\in\mathbf{N}
, and

q_{i}=q^{d_{i}}
.
We denote by
[n]_{i}
the element obtained from
[n]
by
replacing
q
by
q_{i}
.

For the Cartan datum
X
, let
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}=\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}(X)
be the negative
half of the quantum group associated to
X
.
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is an associative
algebra over
\mathbf{Q}(q)
generated by
f_{i}\ (i\in I)
satisfying the
q
-Serre relations

(1.2.1)

\sum_{k+k^{\prime}=1-a_{ii^{\prime}}}(-1)^{k}f_{i}^{(k)}f_{i^{\prime}}f_{i}^{(k^{\prime})}=0,\quad\text{ for $i\neq i^{\prime}\in I$,}

where
f_{i}^{(n)}=f_{i}^{n}/[n]_{i}^{!}
.

Set
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
, and let
{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
be Lusztig’s integral form
of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
, namely, the
\mathbf{A}
-subalgebra of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
generated by

f_{i}^{(n)}
for
i\in I,n\in\mathbf{N}
.

We define a
\mathbf{Q}
-algebra automorphism, called the bar-involution,

{}^{-}:\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\to\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
by
\overline{q}=q^{-1}
,
\overline{f}_{i}=f_{i}
for
i\in I
.
We define an anti-algebra automorphism
{}^{*}:\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\to\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}

by
f_{i}^{*}=f_{i}
for any
i\in I
.

1.3.

\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
has a weight space decomposition

\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}=\bigoplus_{\beta\in Q_{-}}(\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-})_{\beta}
, where

(\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-})_{\beta}
is a subspace of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
spanned by elements
f_{i_{1}}\cdots f_{i_{N}}

such that
\alpha_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\alpha_{i_{N}}=-\beta
.

x\in\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is called homogeneous with
\operatorname{wt}x=\beta
if
x\in(\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-})_{\beta}
.
We define a multiplication on
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\otimes\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
by

(1.3.1)

(x_{1}\otimes x_{2})\cdot(x_{1}^{\prime}\otimes x_{2}^{\prime})=q^{-(\operatorname{wt}x_{2},\operatorname{wt}x_{1}^{\prime})}x_{1}x_{1}^{\prime}\otimes x_{2}x_{2}^{\prime}

where
x_{1},x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2},x_{2}^{\prime}
are homogeneous in
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
.
Then
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\otimes\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
becomes an associative algebra with respect to
this twisted product. One can define a homomorphism

r:\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\to\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\otimes\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
by
r(f_{i})=f_{i}\otimes 1+1\otimes f_{i}

for each
i\in I
. It is known
that there exists a unique bilinear form

(\ ,\ )
on
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
satisfying the following properties;

(1,1)=1
and

(1.3.2)

\displaystyle(f_{i},f_{j})=\delta_{ij}(1-q_{i}^{2})^{-1},

\displaystyle(x,y^{\prime}y^{\prime\prime})=(r(x),y^{\prime}\otimes y^{\prime\prime}),

\displaystyle(x^{\prime}x^{\prime\prime},y)=(x^{\prime}\otimes x^{\prime\prime},r(y)),

where the bilinear form on
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\otimes\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is
defined by
(x_{1}\otimes x_{2},x_{1}^{\prime}\otimes x_{2}^{\prime})=(x_{1},x_{1}^{\prime})(x_{2},x_{2}^{\prime})
.
Thus defined bilinear form is symmetric and non-degenerate.

Following [L2, 1.2.13],
for each
i
, we define a
\mathbf{Q}(q)
-linear map
{}_{i}r:\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\to\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}

by the condition that

(1.3.3)

r(x)=f_{i}\otimes{}_{i}r(x)+\sum y\otimes z,

where
y
runs over homogeneous
elements such that
\operatorname{wt}y\neq-\alpha_{i}
.
Since
{}_{i}r
coincides with the operator
e_{i}^{\prime}
defined in
[K1, 3.4], (see [L2, Prop. 3.1.6] and [K1, Lemma 3.4.1]),
hereafter we write
{}_{i}r
as
e_{i}^{\prime}
.
Thus, if we define the action of
f_{i}
on
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
by the left
multiplication, then
e_{i}^{\prime},f_{i^{\prime}}
satisfies the
q
-boson relations, as operators
on
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
,

(1.3.4)

e_{i}^{\prime}f_{i^{\prime}}=q^{-(\alpha_{i},a_{i^{\prime}})}f_{i^{\prime}}e_{i}^{\prime}+\delta_{ii^{\prime}}.

1.4.

Let
V
be a
\mathbf{Q}(q)
-subspace of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
.
A basis
\mathcal{B}
of
V

(1.4.1)

(b,b^{\prime})\in\begin{cases}1+q\mathbf{Z}[[q]]\cap\mathbf{Q}(q)&\quad\text{ if }b=b^{\prime},\\
q\mathbf{Z}[[q]]\cap\mathbf{Q}(q)&\quad\text{ if }b\neq b^{\prime}.\end{cases}

Recall that
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
. Let
\mathbf{A}_{0}=\mathbf{Q}[[q]]\cap\mathbf{Q}(q)
.
Set

(1.4.2)

\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{Z}}(\infty)=\{x\in{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\mid(x,x)\in\mathbf{A}_{0}\}.

Then

is a
\mathbf{Z}[q]
-submodule of
{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
.
It is known that if
\mathcal{B}

\mathbf{A}
-basis of
{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}

We define a subset

If there exists a basis

Thus we can define

2.1.

Let

Assume that
\mathbf{k}

as follows. For each

choose a polynomial
Q_{i,i^{\prime}}(u,v)\in\mathbf{k}[u,v]

(i)

(ii)

(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})p+(\alpha_{i^{\prime}},\alpha_{i^{\prime}})q=-2(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i^{\prime}})\quad\text{ if $t_{ii^{\prime};pq}\neq 0$. }

Moreover,
t_{ii^{\prime};-a_{ii^{\prime}},0}\neq 0,t_{ii^{\prime};0,-a_{ii^{\prime}}}\neq 0

(iii)

Q_{i,i^{\prime}}(u,v)=Q_{i^{\prime},i}(v,u)

(iv)

Q_{\sigma(i),\sigma(i^{\prime})}(u,v)=Q_{i,i^{\prime}}(u,v)

For
\beta=\sum_{i\in I}n_{i}\alpha_{i}\in Q_{+}

|\beta|=\sum_{i}n_{i}
.
For
\beta\in Q_{+}
such that

The KLR algebra

\mathbf{k}
-algebra
defined by the generators,

(
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{n})\in I^{\beta}

x_{k}

(1\leq k\leq n)

\tau_{k}\ (1\leq k<n)

(1)

\displaystyle e(\nu)e(\nu^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\delta_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}e(\nu),\quad\sum_{\nu\in I^{\beta}}e(\nu)=1,

(2)

\displaystyle x_{k}x_{l}

\displaystyle=x_{l}x_{k},\quad x_{k}e(\nu)=e(\nu)x_{k},

(3)

\displaystyle\tau_{k}e(\nu)

\displaystyle=e(s_{k}\nu)\tau_{k},\quad\tau_{k}\tau_{l}=\tau_{l}\tau_{k},\quad\text{ if }|k-l|>1,

(4)

\displaystyle\tau_{k}^{2}e(\nu)

\displaystyle=Q_{\nu_{k},\nu_{k+1}}(x_{k},x_{k+1})e(\nu),

(5)

\displaystyle(\tau_{k}x_{l}

\displaystyle-x_{s_{k}l}\tau_{k})e(\nu)=\begin{cases}-e(\nu)&\quad\text{ if }l=k,\nu_{k}=\nu_{k+1},\\
e(\nu)&\quad\text{ if }l=k+1,\nu_{k}=\nu_{k+1},\\
0&\quad\text{ otherwise, }\end{cases}

(6)

\displaystyle(\tau_{k+1}

\displaystyle\tau_{k}\tau_{k+1}-\tau_{k}\tau_{k+1}\tau_{k})e(\nu)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{Q_{\nu_{k},\nu_{k+1}}(x_{k},x_{k+1})-Q_{\nu_{k},\nu_{k+1}}(x_{k+2},x_{k+1})}{x_{k}-x_{k+2}}e(\nu)&\quad\text{ if }\nu_{k}=\nu_{k+2},\\
0&\quad\text{ otherwise. }\end{cases}

(Here

(k,k+1)
in the symmetric group

3.

3.1.

First we recall the general theory of periodic functors due to
[L2, 11.1].
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a linear category over a field
\mathbf{k}
,
namely, a category where the space of morphisms between
any two objects has the structure of
\mathbf{k}
-vector space such that the composition
of morphisms is bilinear, and that finite direct sums exist. A functor between
two linear categories is said ro be linear if it preserves the
\mathbf{k}
-vector space structure.

A linear functor
\sigma^{*}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
is said to be periodic if there exists

\mathbf{n}\geq 1
such that
(\sigma^{*})^{\mathbf{n}}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{C}}
.
Assume that
\sigma^{*}
is a periodic functor on
\mathcal{C}
. We define a new category

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}
as follows. The objects of
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}
are the pairs
(A,\phi)
, where

A\in\mathcal{C}
, and
\phi:\sigma^{*}A\,\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\underrightarrow{\sim}$}\,A
is an isomorphism in
\mathcal{C}
such that the composition
satisfies the relation

(3.1.1)

\phi\circ\sigma^{*}\phi\circ\cdots\circ(\sigma^{*})^{\mathbf{n}-1}\phi=\operatorname{id}_{A}.

A morphism from
(A,\phi)
to
(A^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}
is a morphism

f:A\to A^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{C}
satisfying the following commutative diagram,

(3.1.2)

\textstyle{\sigma^{*}A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sigma^{*}f}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{\sigma^{*}A^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi^{\prime}}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{A^{\prime}.}

The category
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}
is a linear category, hence is an additive category.

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}

the category associated to the periodic functor
\sigma^{*}
on
\mathcal{C}

5.
KLR algebras and global bases

5.1.

In this section, we assume that
R=\bigoplus_{\beta\in Q_{+}}R(\beta)
is symmetric type,
namely, the Cartan matrix
A=(a_{ij})
is symmetric, and

Q_{i,j}(u,v)
is a polynomial in
u-v
. Furthermore, we assume that

\mathbf{k}
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0.
We consider the isomorphisms
\gamma:{}_{\mathbf{A}}\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}\,\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\underrightarrow{\sim}$}\,{}_{\mathbf{A}}K(\mathscr{P})

and
\gamma^{*}:{}_{\mathbf{A}}K(\mathscr{L})\,\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\underrightarrow{\sim}$}\,{}_{\mathbf{A}}\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
as in Corollary 4.15.
In the last paragraph of [M], he states a result that
\gamma^{-1}(\underline{\mathbf{B}})
coincides with
the canonical basis, and also coincides with the lower global basis of
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
,
with a brief indication for the proof, which relies on the geometric argument.
In this section we give a simple proof of this fact, without using the geometry.

Let
\widetilde{\varTheta}_{0}:{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\,\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\underrightarrow{\sim}$}\,K_{\operatorname{{\bf gp}}}
be the isomorphism of

\mathbf{A}
-algebras as given in Theorem 2.7.
Recall that
\mathbf{B}
is the basis of
K_{\operatorname{{\bf gp}}}
, and
\mathbf{B}^{*}
is the basis of
K_{\operatorname{{\bf gm}}}

given in 2.4.
Let
\mathcal{B}^{*}
be the upper global basis of
{}_{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-})^{*}
as given
in Theorem 1.10, and
\mathcal{B}
the lower global basis of
{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
.
Also let
\mathcal{B}^{\natural}
be the canonical basis of
{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
.
In the case where
X
is symmetric, it is known by Grojnowski-Lusztig [GL]
that

coincides with
\mathcal{B}^{\natural}
.

The following result was proved by Varagnolo-Vasserot [VV], and Rouquier [R2],
by using the Ext algebra obtained from Lusztig’s category

related to the geometry of quivers (see Introduction).

Theorem 5.2

([VV], [R2])
.

Under the setup in 5.1, the isomorphisms
\widetilde{\varTheta}_{0}:{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\,\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\underrightarrow{\sim}$}\,K_{\operatorname{{\bf gp}}}

sends
\mathcal{B}^{\natural}
to
\mathbf{B}
.

5.3.

Since
\mathcal{B}^{\natural}
is invariant under
*
,
\mathcal{B}^{\natural}
corresponds to
\mathbf{B}

under the isomorphism
\widetilde{\varTheta}:{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\,\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\underrightarrow{\sim}$}\,K_{\operatorname{{\bf gp}}}

in Proposition 2.9. Since the basis
\mathcal{B}^{\natural}
is almost orthonormal,

\mathbf{B}
is also almost orthonormal in the sense of 1.4, i.e.,
for
b,b^{\prime}\in\mathbf{B}
,

(5.3.1)

(b,b^{\prime})\in\begin{cases}1+q\mathbf{Z}[[q]]&\quad\text{ if $b=b^{\prime}$, }\\
q\mathbf{Z}[[q]]&\quad\text{ if $b\neq b^{\prime}$.}\end{cases}

We want to show that
\underline{\mathbf{B}}
has a similar property.

Proposition 5.4
.

Assume that
b\in\underline{\mathbf{B}}
. Then we have
\mathbf{D}b=b
, and

(5.4.1)

(b,b^{\prime})\in\begin{cases}1+q\mathbf{Z}[[q]]&\quad\text{ if $b=b^{\prime}$, }\\
q\mathbf{Z}[[q]]&\quad\text{ if $b\neq b^{\prime}$. }\end{cases}

Proof.

Take
b,b^{\prime}\in\underline{\mathbf{B}}
, and write it as
b=[P],b^{\prime}=[Q]
for

P=(P_{0},\phi),Q=(Q_{0},\phi^{\prime})\in\mathscr{P}_{\beta}
. The relation

\mathbf{D}b=b
is clear from the definition.
We show (5.4.1). By Lemma 4.6,
P_{0}
is an indecomposable projective

R(\beta)
-module. Since
(P_{0},\phi)
is self-dual,
P_{0}
is also self-dual.
Thus
[P_{0}]
corresponds to some element in
\mathbf{B}
. Hence by (5.3.1),

P_{0}
satisfies the relation
([P_{0}],[P_{0}])\in 1+q\mathbf{Z}[[q]]
.
We compare the inner product (2.5.3) for
K_{\operatorname{{\bf gp}}}(\beta)
and the inner product
(3.5.4) for
K(\mathscr{P}_{\beta})
. We show that

(5.4.2)

([P],[Q])\in\begin{cases}1+q\mathbf{Z}[\zeta_{\mathbf{n}}][[q]]&\quad\text{ if $[P]=[Q]$, }\\
q\mathbf{Z}[\zeta_{\mathbf{n}}][[q]]&\quad\text{ if $[P]\neq[Q]$. }\end{cases}

Since
\mathbf{D}P_{0}\simeq P_{0}
, by using the relation (3.5.2) (it also holds
if
M
is projective), in the definition of
the inner product on
K_{\operatorname{{\bf gp}}}(\beta)
and on
K(\mathscr{P}_{\beta})
, one can replace
the tensor product
P_{0}^{\psi}\otimes_{R(\beta)}P_{0}
by the Hom space

\operatorname{Hom}_{R(\beta)}(P_{0},P_{0})
. Then (5.3.1) shows that

\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\operatorname{Hom}_{R(\beta)}(P_{0},P_{0})_{0}=1
, and

\dim_{\mathbf{k}}\operatorname{Hom}_{R(\beta)}(P_{0},q^{k}P_{0})_{0}=0
for

k>0
. In particular, the grading preserving homomorphisms
P_{0}\to P_{0}
are
only the scalar multiplications.
The action of
\phi\otimes\phi
on
(P_{0}^{\psi}\otimes_{R(\beta)}P_{0})_{0}
corresponds
to the action
f\mapsto\phi^{-1}\circ f\circ\phi
for
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{R}}(P_{0},P_{0})_{0}
,
hence it induces the identity map on
\operatorname{Hom}_{R(\beta)}(P_{0},P_{0})_{0}
.
This shows the first formula in (5.4.2).
The second formula is obtained similarly from the relation

\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{R(\beta)}(P_{0},q^{k}Q_{0})=0
for
k>0
.

Now by Corollary 4.15 (i), we have an isometry

\gamma:{}_{\mathbf{A}}\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}\,\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\underrightarrow{\sim}$}\,{}_{\mathbf{A}}K(\mathscr{P})
.
Hence
([P],[Q])\in\mathbf{Q}((q))
. Combined with (5.4.2),
we obtain (5.4.1). The proposition is proved.
∎

5.6.

Take
j\in J
. Consider the operator

Since the convolution product is an exact functor,

For a simple object

We define crystal operators

\widetilde{\mathbf{F}}_{j}M\in\mathscr{L}_{\beta+\alpha_{j}}

by

(5.6.2)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{E}}_{j}M

\displaystyle=q_{j}^{1-\varepsilon_{j}(M)}\operatorname{soc}E_{j}M,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{F}}_{j}M

\displaystyle=q_{j}^{\varepsilon_{j}(M)}\operatorname{hd}F^{\prime}_{j}M.

If we use the restriction functor defined in 3.6,

is written as

E_{j}M\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{R(\alpha_{j})}(L(j),\operatorname{Res}_{\beta-\alpha_{j},\alpha_{j}}M).

Thus
\widetilde{\mathbf{E}}_{j}M
(resp.
\widetilde{\mathbf{F}}_{j}M

(resp.
\widetilde{f}_{j}^{*}M
)
given in [M, 9], where

\displaystyle\widetilde{e}^{*}_{j}M

\displaystyle=q_{j}^{1-\varepsilon_{j}(M)}\operatorname{soc}\operatorname{Hom}_{R(\alpha_{j})}(L(j),\operatorname{Res}_{\beta-\alpha_{j},\alpha_{j}}M),

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}^{*}_{j}M

\displaystyle=q_{j}^{\varepsilon_{j}(M)}\operatorname{hd}(M\circ L(j)).

(Note that we use

as in (5.6.1). This

in the notation of [M].)

The following result is immediate from [M, Lemma 8.3], though
this formula is not used in later discussions.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
M\in\mathscr{L}_{\beta}
be a simple object.
Then
\widetilde{\mathbf{F}}_{j}M
is a simple object such that

\varepsilon_{j}(\widetilde{\mathbf{F}}_{j}M)=\varepsilon_{j}(M)+1
, and
[F_{j}^{\prime}M]
is written
in the Grothendieck group
K(\mathscr{L}_{\beta+\alpha_{j}})
,

[F_{j}^{\prime}M]=q_{j}^{-\varepsilon_{j}(M)}[\widetilde{\mathbf{F}}_{j}M]+\sum_{k}[L_{k}],

where
L_{k}\in\mathscr{L}_{\beta+\alpha_{j}}
are simple objects such that

\varepsilon_{j}(L_{k})<\varepsilon_{j}(M)+1
.

In order to consider the action of
E_{j}
, we need a lemma,
which is obtained from Lemma 8.1 and Lemma 8.2 in [M].

Lemma 5.8

([M])
.

(i)

Let
N
be a simple object in
\mathscr{C}_{\beta}
such that
\varepsilon_{j}(N)=0
.
Set
M=N\circ L(j)^{(m)}
for any

.
Then
\operatorname{hd}M
is irreducible, and
\varepsilon_{j}(M)=m
.

(ii)

Let
N
be a simple object in
\mathscr{C}_{\beta}
such that
\varepsilon_{j}(N)=m
. Then
there exists a simple object
X
in
\mathscr{C}_{\beta-m\alpha_{j}}
with
\varepsilon_{j}(X)=0
such that

\operatorname{Res}_{\beta-m\alpha_{j},m\alpha_{j}}(N)\simeq X\otimes L(j)^{(m)}.

We show the following.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
M\in\mathscr{L}_{\beta}
be a simple object such that
\varepsilon_{j}(M)=m>0
.
Then
\widetilde{\mathbf{E}}_{j}M
is a self-dual simple object in
\mathscr{L}_{\beta-\alpha_{j}}
, and
in the Grothendieck group

K(\mathscr{L})
,
[E_{j}M]
is written as

(5.9.1)

[E_{j}M]=G(q)[\widetilde{\mathbf{E}}_{j}M]+\sum_{1\leq k\leq p}[L_{k}],

where
G(q)\in\mathbf{N}[q,q^{-1}]
,

\varepsilon_{j}(\widetilde{\mathbf{E}}_{j}M)=m-1
, and

L_{k}\in\mathscr{L}_{\beta-\alpha_{j}}
are simple objects such that
\varepsilon_{j}(L_{k})<m-1
.

Proof.

The proof is done by a similar argument as in the proof of [M, Lemma 9.4].
We give an outline of the proof below.
It is known by [M, Lemma 9.4],
\widetilde{\mathbf{E}}_{j}M
is a self-dual simple
object in
\mathscr{L}_{\beta-\alpha_{j}}
. We fix a composition series of
E_{j}M
, and let

N
be a simple object such that
\varepsilon_{j}(N)=m-1
, appearing as a composition
factor. We show

(5.9.2)
N
is isomorphic to
\widetilde{\mathbf{E}}_{j}M
, up to degree shift.

Note that
E_{j}M\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{R(\alpha_{j})}(L(j),\operatorname{Res}_{\beta-\alpha_{j},\alpha_{j}}M)
.
Let
\widetilde{N}
be a subobject of
E_{j}M
such that
N
is a simple quotient of
\widetilde{N}
.
By the adjunction isomorphism, we have

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}_{\beta}}(\widetilde{N}\circ L(j),M)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}_{\beta-\alpha_{j}}}(\widetilde{N},\operatorname{Hom}_{R(\alpha_{j})}(L(j),\operatorname{Res}_{\beta-\alpha_{j},\alpha_{j}}M)).

Since the right hand side of this equality is non-zero,
M
is a simple quotient
of
\widetilde{N}\circ L(j)
.
By Lemma 5.8 (ii), there exists a simple
X
such that

(5.9.3)

\operatorname{Res}_{\beta-m\alpha_{j},m\alpha_{j}}(M)\simeq X\otimes L(j)^{(m)}.

Since the restriction functor is exact, we have a surjective map

\operatorname{Res}_{\beta-m\alpha_{j},m\alpha_{j}}(\widetilde{N}\circ L(j))\to X\otimes L(j)^{(m)}.

We consider the Mackey filtration (see [M, Thm. 4.5]) for

\operatorname{Res}_{\beta-m\alpha_{j},m\alpha_{j}}(\widetilde{N}\circ L(j))=\operatorname{Res}_{\beta-m\alpha_{j},m\alpha_{j}}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\beta-\alpha_{j},\alpha_{j}}(\widetilde{N}\otimes L(j))

Since
\varepsilon_{j}(N)=m-1

which involves
\operatorname{Res}_{\beta-m\alpha_{j},(m-1)\alpha_{j}}N

By Lemma 5.8 (ii), there exists a simple object
Y
such that

(5.9.4)

\operatorname{Res}_{\beta-m\alpha_{j},(m-1)\alpha_{j}}N\simeq Y\otimes L(j)^{(m-1)}.

We have a surjective homomorphism
Y\otimes L(j)^{(m)}\to X\otimes L(j)^{(m)}
with
degree shift.
Since
X,Y
are simple, we have
X\simeq Y
, up to degree shift.

By considering the adjunction isomorphism in (5.9.4), we have a non-zero homomorphism

Y\circ L(j)^{(m-1)}\to N

Since
\varepsilon_{j}(N)=m-1
, we have
\varepsilon_{j}(Y)=0

\operatorname{hd}(Y\circ L(j)^{(m-1)})
is irreducible. It follows that

N\simeq\operatorname{hd}(Y\circ L(j)^{(m-1)})

By applying this argument to

we see that
\widetilde{\mathbf{E}}_{j}M\simeq\operatorname{hd}(X\circ L(j)^{(m-1)})

Hence

Since
\varepsilon_{j}(M)=m
, all the composition factors

E_{j}M
satisfy
the relation
\varepsilon_{j}(L)\leq m-1

, we have

(5.12.3)

e_{j}^{\prime}(b_{0})=G(q)\gamma^{*}(\widetilde{\mathbf{e}}_{j}b)+\sum_{b^{\prime}}a_{bb^{\prime}}\gamma^{*}(b^{\prime}).

Here by induction,
\gamma^{*}(\widetilde{\mathbf{e}}_{j}b),\gamma^{*}(b^{\prime})\in\underline{\mathcal{B}}^{*}_{-\beta+\alpha_{j}}
.
Moreover, since
\gamma^{*}
commutes with
e_{j}^{\prime}
,

\varepsilon_{j}(\gamma^{*}(\widetilde{\mathbf{e}}_{j}b))=\varepsilon_{j}(b_{0})-1
, and

\varepsilon_{j}(\gamma^{*}(b^{\prime}))<\varepsilon_{j}(b_{0})-1
.
Comparing (5.12.3) with Theorem 1.10 (iii) (applied for
{}_{\mathbf{A}}(\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-})^{*}
),
we see that
b_{0}=\gamma^{*}(b)\in\underline{\mathcal{B}}^{*}_{-\beta}
.
This proves (5.12.1). Hence (i) holds. (ii) follows from (i).
∎

Remarks 5.13.

(i) The quantum group of arbitrary type is obtained as
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
from
some
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
of symmetric type, through an admissible automorphism
\sigma
.
Hence our result shows that the global crystal basis of the quantum group of arbitrary
type is obtained from the KLR algebras through foldings.

(ii) In general, the natural basis
\mathbf{B}
of
K_{\operatorname{{\bf gp}}}
has the positivity property,
i.e., for
b,b^{\prime}\in\mathbf{B}
,
bb^{\prime}
is a linear combination of the basis with coefficients
in
\mathbf{N}[q,q^{-1}]
. This property does not hold for
the global crystal basis
\mathcal{B}
of non-symmetric type. Note that the basis
\underline{\mathbf{B}}

of
K(\mathscr{P})
not necessarily has the positivity property.

5.14.

By Theorem 5.2, the map
\widetilde{\varTheta}_{0}
sends the canonical basis
\mathcal{B}^{\natural}
to

\mathbf{B}
. By applying Theorem 5.12 for the case where
\sigma=\operatorname{id}
, we rediscover
the result of [GL] that
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}^{\natural}
. In the following,
we show that this also holds in the non-symmetric case.

For
b\in\underline{\mathbf{B}},b^{\prime}\in\underline{\mathbf{B}}^{*}
and
j\in J
, we set

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{j}(b)

\displaystyle=\max\{k\geq 0\mid b\in f_{j}^{k}K(\mathscr{P})\},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{j}(b^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\max\{k\geq 0\mid e_{j}^{\prime k}b^{\prime}\neq 0\}.

Note that the definition
\varepsilon_{j}(b^{\prime})
is compatible with the definition

\varepsilon_{j}(M)
in (5.6.1).

Take
b\in\underline{\mathbf{B}}
, and let
b_{*}\in\underline{\mathbf{B}}^{*}
be the basis dual to
b
.
Then we have

(5.14.1)

\varepsilon_{j}(b)=\varepsilon_{j}(b_{*}).

In fact,
assume that
b\in f_{j}^{n}K(\mathscr{P})
. Then

\operatorname{\operatorname{\langle}\!\!\operatorname{\langle}\,}f_{j}^{n}K(\mathscr{P}),b_{*}\operatorname{\operatorname{\rangle}\!\!\operatorname{\rangle}}\neq 0
, and there exists
b^{\prime}\in\underline{\mathbf{B}}
such that

\operatorname{\operatorname{\langle}\!\!\operatorname{\langle}\,}f_{j}^{n}b^{\prime},b_{*}\operatorname{\operatorname{\rangle}\!\!\operatorname{\rangle}}=\operatorname{\operatorname{\langle}\!\!\operatorname{\langle}\,}b^{\prime},e_{j}^{\prime n}b_{*}\operatorname{\operatorname{\rangle}\!\!\operatorname{\rangle}}\neq 0
, hence
e_{j}^{\prime n}b_{*}\neq 0
.
On the contrary, assume that
e_{j}^{\prime n}b_{*}\neq 0
. Then there exists
b^{\prime}\in\underline{\mathbf{B}}
such that

\operatorname{\operatorname{\langle}\!\!\operatorname{\langle}\,}b^{\prime},e_{j}^{\prime n}b_{*}\operatorname{\operatorname{\rangle}\!\!\operatorname{\rangle}}=\operatorname{\operatorname{\langle}\!\!\operatorname{\langle}\,}f_{j}^{n}b^{\prime},b_{*}\operatorname{\operatorname{\rangle}\!\!\operatorname{\rangle}}\neq 0
.
it is known ([L2, Thm. 14.3.2]) that a subset of
\mathcal{B}^{\natural}
gives a basis of

f_{j}^{n}\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
for each
n\geq 0
. Then by Corollary 5.5,
a subset of
\underline{\mathbf{B}}
gives a basis of
f_{j}^{n}K(\mathscr{P})
for each
n\geq 0
.
It follows that
b\in f_{j}^{n}K(\mathscr{P})
. Thus (5.14.1) holds.

Theorem 5.15
.

\gamma^{-1}
sends
\underline{\mathbf{B}}
to
\underline{\mathcal{B}}^{\natural}
. Hence
the canonical basis
\underline{\mathcal{B}}^{\natural}
and the global crystal basis
\underline{\mathcal{B}}
of

\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
coincide with.

Proof.

The proof is done just by applying some properties of
K(\mathscr{P})
and
K(\mathscr{L})
,
together with Theorem 5.12.
Below, we give an outline of the proof.

By Proposition 5.11, Theorem 5.12, together with Theorem 1.10 (iii),
we have, for
b\in\underline{\mathbf{B}}^{*}
such that
\varepsilon_{j}(b)=m>0
,

(5.15.1)

e_{j}^{\prime}b=[\varepsilon_{j}(b)]_{j}\widetilde{\mathbf{e}}_{j}b+\sum_{b^{\prime}\in\underline{\mathbf{B}}^{*}}a_{bb^{\prime}}b^{\prime},\qquad(a_{bb^{\prime}}\in\mathbf{A}),

where
\varepsilon_{j}(\widetilde{\mathbf{e}}_{j}b)=\varepsilon_{j}(b)-1
, and
a_{bb^{\prime}}=0
unless

\varepsilon_{j}(b^{\prime})<\varepsilon_{j}(b)-1
.

We define
\widetilde{\mathbf{f}}_{j}:\underline{\mathbf{B}}_{\beta-\alpha_{j}}\to\underline{\mathbf{B}}_{\beta}
as the transpose
of the map
\widetilde{\mathbf{e}}_{j}:\underline{\mathbf{B}}^{*}_{\beta}\to\underline{\mathbf{B}}^{*}_{\beta-\alpha_{j}}
, i.e.,

\widetilde{\mathbf{f}}_{j}(b)=b^{\prime}
if and only if
\widetilde{\mathbf{e}}_{j}((b^{\prime})_{*})=b_{*}
, where
b_{*},(b^{\prime})_{*}
are
dual of
b,b^{\prime}
, respectively.

\widetilde{\mathbf{f}}_{j}
gives a bijection
\underline{\mathbf{B}}_{\beta-\alpha_{j}}\,\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\underrightarrow{\sim}$}\,\underline{\mathbf{B}}_{\beta}
.
Since
\underline{\mathbf{B}}
is a dual basis of
\underline{\mathbf{B}}^{*}
, (5.15.1) and (5.14.1) implies,
for
b\in\underline{\mathbf{B}}
, that

(5.15.2)

f_{j}b=[\varepsilon_{j}(b)+1]_{j}\widetilde{\mathbf{f}}_{j}b+\sum_{b^{\prime}\in\underline{\mathbf{B}}}c_{bb^{\prime}}b^{\prime},\qquad(c_{bb^{\prime}}\in\mathbf{A}),

where
\varepsilon_{j}(\widetilde{\mathbf{f}}_{j}b)=\varepsilon_{j}(b)+1
, and
c_{bb^{\prime}}=0
unless

\varepsilon_{j}(b^{\prime})>\varepsilon_{j}(b)+1
.
By (5.15.2), we have the following.

(5.15.3) Assume that
b\in\underline{\mathbf{B}}
is such that
\varepsilon_{j}(b)=0
.
Then for any
n\geq 1
, there exists
b_{1}\in\underline{\mathbf{B}}
such that

f_{j}^{(n)}b=b_{1}+\sum_{b^{\prime}\in\underline{\mathbf{B}}}c_{bb^{\prime}}b^{\prime},\qquad(c_{bb^{\prime}}\in\mathbf{A}),

where
\varepsilon_{j}(b_{1})=n
, and
c_{bb^{\prime}}=0
unless
\varepsilon_{j}(b^{\prime})>n
.
Note that
c_{bb^{\prime}}\in\mathbf{A}
follows from Corollary 4.15.

For each
j\in J,n\in\mathbf{N}
, set

\underline{\mathbf{B}}_{n;j}=\{b\in\underline{\mathbf{B}}\mid\varepsilon_{j}(b)=n\}
.
We define a map
\pi_{n;j}:\underline{\mathbf{B}}_{0;j}\to\underline{\mathbf{B}}_{n;j}
by

b\mapsto b_{1}
in (5.15.3).
\pi_{n;j}
is a bijection since
\widetilde{\mathbf{f}}_{j}
is a bijection.

By using Proposition 5.11, we have

\bigcap_{j\in J}\{b\in\underline{\mathbf{B}}^{*}\mid\varepsilon_{j}(b)=0\}=[1]\in{}_{\mathbf{A}}K(\mathscr{L}_{0})

This implies, for each
\beta\in Q_{+}^{\sigma}
, that

(5.15.4)

\underline{\mathbf{B}}_{\beta}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J,n>0\\
\beta-n\alpha_{j}\in Q_{+}^{\sigma}\end{subarray}}\pi_{n;j}(\underline{\mathbf{B}}_{0;j}\cap\underline{\mathbf{B}}_{\beta-n\alpha_{j}}).

We now consider the isomorphism
\gamma^{-1}:{}_{\mathbf{A}}K(\mathscr{P})\,\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\underrightarrow{\sim}$}\,{}_{\mathbf{A}}\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
,
and act
\gamma^{-1}
on both sides of (5.15.4). Since
\gamma
commutes with the action of

f_{j}^{(n)}
, the right hand side of (5.15.4) corresponds exactly an inductive
construction of canonical basis (see [L2, 14.4.2, Thm. 14.4.3]).
Thus
\gamma^{-1}(\underline{\mathbf{B}})
coincides with the canonical basis
\underline{\mathcal{B}}^{\natural}

of
{}_{\mathbf{A}}\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
.
The theorem now follows from Theorem 5.12.
∎

Remark 5.17.

We have deduced the coincidence of
\underline{\mathcal{B}}
and
\underline{\mathcal{B}}^{\natural}
by
making use of the crystal structure of the basis
\underline{\mathbf{B}}^{*}
of
K(\mathscr{L})
.
However, if we assume the properties of canonical basis as in (5.15.4),
then the coincidence
\underline{\mathcal{B}}=\underline{\mathcal{B}}^{\natural}
is shown,
directly without appealing KLR algebras, by using a similar formula as
(5.15.3) for
\underline{\mathcal{B}}
.

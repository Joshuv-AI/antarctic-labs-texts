Formal conjugacy growth in graph products II
L. Ciobanu, S. Hermiller, V. Mercier

Abstract
In this paper we give an algorithm for computing the conjugacy growth series for a right-angled Artin group, based on a natural language of minimal length conjugacy representatives. In addition, we provide a further language of unique conjugacy geodesic representatives of the conjugacy classes for a graph product of groups. The conjugacy representatives and growth series here provide an alternate viewpoint, and are more amenable to computational experiments compared to those in our previous paper [ 6 ] . Examples of applications of this algorithm for right-angled Artin groups are provided, as well as computations of conjugacy geodesic growth growth series with respect to the standard generating sets. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 20F69, 20F65, 68Q45. Key words: Conjugacy growth, right-angled Artin groups, graph products.

Introduction

The
spherical standard growth function
of a group
G
with respect to a finite inverse-closed generating set
X
records the size of the sphere of size
n
in the Cayley graph of
G
with respect to
X
, for any
n\geq 0
, and the
spherical conjugacy growth function
counts the number of conjugacy classes intersecting the sphere of radius
n
but not the ball of radius
n-1
.
Furthermore, the
spherical standard growth series
and

spherical conjugacy growth series

are those generating functions whose coefficients are the spherical growth function and spherical conjugacy growth function values, respectively.
For a group that has a regular language of shortlex normal forms, such as word hyperbolic groups and all shortlex automatic groups
[
9
]
,
the spherical standard growth series is a rational function that can be computed using functions in the KBMAG package in GAP
[
16
,
13
]
.

However, even for nonabelian free groups with respect to the standard generating set, the spherical conjugacy growth series is not rational; this was shown by Rivin
[
20
,
21
]
in his work introducing the first conjugacy growth series computations, in which he showed the stronger result that the series for free groups are transcendental.
Conjugacy growth series have also been studied in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
8
,
10
,
19
]
, where virtually abelian groups, Baumslag-Solitar groups, acylindrically hyperbolic groups, free and wreath products, and more, were explored. Except for virtually abelian groups, all of the spherical conjugacy growth series studied so far have been shown to be transcendental, and it has been conjectured that virtually abelian groups are the only ones with rational conjugacy growth series
[
3
, Conjecture 7.2]
.

We continue work begun in
[
6
]
, analyzing spherical conjugacy growth series and related languages for graph products of groups.
In our previous paper
[
6
]
we computed the conjugacy growth series of a graph product starting with the splitting of a graph product as an amalgamated product, finding minimal length conjugacy representatives among particular normal forms for amalgamated products, and computing the growth series of appropriate admissible transversals.
However, that method does not explicitly produce a language of conjugacy representatives, and is somewhat technical.
In this paper we study graph products of groups
from an alternate viewpoint, to obtain a
language of unique
conjugacy geodesic representatives of conjugacy classes
in a graph product of groups that is built
from shortlex conjugacy languages for subgraph products.
This reduces the computation of the spherical
conjugacy growth series of the entire graph product to
computation of the spherical conjugacy growth series for
subgraph products over indecomposable subsets of vertices,
in Theorem
3.7
.

Sections
2
,
3.1
,
and
4.1
contain complete definitions of the terminology and notation used below and throughout this paper.
Given a finite simple graph
\Gamma=(V,E)
with a group
G_{v}
generated by
X_{v}

attached to each vertex
v\in V
, the associated
graph product

G_{V}
is the
group generated by the vertex groups with the added relations that
elements of groups attached to adjacent vertices commute. We will often use the
complement
of the graph

, which is the graph

\overline{\Gamma}=(V,\overline{E})
with the same vertex set as
\Gamma

and edge set that is the complement of

.

In Section
3
, we give a decomposition of the spherical conjugacy growth series of a graph product group

in terms of
subgraph products

induced by subsets

. The subgraph
\overline{\Gamma_{U}}
of
\overline{\Gamma}
induced by
U
consists of connected components induced by
subsets
U_{1},...,U_{n}
of
U
; we write this decomposition as

\delta_{\ll}(U)=(U_{1},...,U_{m})
(see Definition
3.4
for more details),
and note that
G_{U}\cong G_{U_{1}}\times\cdots\times G_{U_{n}}
.
Given a total order
<_{V}
on the generating set
X_{V}:=\cup_{v\in V}X_{v}

of

and the corresponding shortlex order
<_{sl}
on
the set
X_{V}^{*}
of words over

,
the
shortlex conjugacy language

\mathsf{ConjSL}=\mathsf{ConjSL}(G_{V},X_{V})

is the set consisting of the shortlex least word representing
an element in each conjugacy class.
For any
U\subseteq V
, let
\mathsf{ConjSL}^{U}
denote the set of
words with
support
equal to
U
;
that is, words

w\in\mathsf{ConjSL}
such that
w
contains only letters in

X_{U}:=\cup_{u\in U}X_{u}
but

is not contained in

X_{U^{\prime}}^{*}
for any

.
In our earlier paper
[
6

we showed
that
\mathsf{ConjSL}(G_{V},X_{V})^{U}=\mathsf{ConjSL}(G_{U},X_{U})^{U}

can be computed in the subgraph product

generated by
X_{U}
.
The growth series
F_{\mathsf{ConjSL}}
of
\mathsf{ConjSL}
is
equal to

{\widetilde{\sigma}}_{(G_{V},X_{V})}
, but the growth of this
language is not readily computed;
in the following we provide another formula for
{\widetilde{\sigma}}_{(G_{V},X_{V})}

in terms of the growth functions

Theorem
3.7
.

Let

be a graph product group over a graph

be
a union of inverse-closed generating sets for the

be a total order of

that is compatible with

<_{sl}

Then
the language

is a set of unique

{\widetilde{\sigma}}_{(G_{V},X_{V})}=1+\sum_{\emptyset\neq U\subseteq V,~\delta_{\ll}(U)=(U_{1},...,U_{m})}F_{\mathsf{ConjSL}^{U_{1}}}\cdots F_{\mathsf{ConjSL}^{U_{m}}}.

For the remainder of the paper, we focus on the special case of

right-angled Artin groups
, or
RAAGs
,
in order to obtain a more computable formula for the spherical growth series
and calculate examples.
RAAGs are the graph products of infinite cyclic groups, and
the family of RAAGs has been widely studied and applied in many contexts.
Building on Theorem
3.7

and work of Crisp, Godelle, and Wiest
[
7
]

we give another set of conjugacy geodesic
normal forms for the conjugacy classes of a RAAG
with respect to the
Artin generating set

,
consisting of a cyclic generator and its inverse for each

in Corollary

. The elements of this new set of conjugacy class representatives
are
cyclic shortlex
;
that is, they lie in the set

\mathsf{CycSL}=\mathsf{CycSL}(G_{V},X_{V})
of words

satisfying
the property that for every cyclic permutation
w^{\prime}
of
w
,
the word
w^{\prime}

is shortlex least among the words representing the same element
of
G_{V}
as
w^{\prime}
.

Unlike the languages
\mathsf{ConjSL}
and
\mathsf{ConjSL}^{U}
, the languages

\mathsf{CycSL}
and
\mathsf{CycSL}^{U}

(where
\mathsf{CycSL}^{U}
is the
set of words in
\mathsf{CycSL}
with support equal to
U
)
are readily computed for RAAGs.
The next main result of our paper gives a formula for
the spherical growth series of a RAAG in terms of
the strict growth series of the latter languages.

Theorem
4.9
.

Let
G_{V}
be a right-angled Artin group on a graph
\Gamma=(V,E)

and let
X_{V}
be the Artin generating set.
The spherical conjugacy growth series of
G_{V}

with respect to
X_{V}
satisfies
the formula

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\sigma}}_{(G_{V},X_{V})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{\emptyset\neq U\subseteq V,~\delta_{\ll}(U)=(U_{1},...,U_{m})}\mathsf{\rho}(F_{\mathsf{CycSL}^{U_{1}}})\cdots\mathsf{\rho}(F_{\mathsf{CycSL}^{U_{m}}})

(1)

where
for any formal complex power series
f
with
integer coefficients and constant term equal to 0,

\mathsf{\rho}(f)(z):=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\sum_{k\geq 1}\phi(k)f(t^{k})}{t}\,dt.

(2)

The proof of Theorem
4.9

combines the conjugacy class representatives in Corollary
4.5

with a method from analytic combinatorics for counting words up to cyclic permutation;
since this tool is not standard in group theory, we give a full account of the background for the operator
\mathsf{\rho}

in Section
2.2
.
In particular,
when
F_{L}
is the growth series of a
set
L
of nonempty words over
X
that is closed under cyclic permutation
and taking powers,
\mathsf{\rho}(F_{L})
is the growth function of
a subset of
L
containing exactly one representative of each
cyclic permutation class from
L
(see Corollary
2.5
).

In Part I of this series of papers
[
6
]
, we provided a
different formula for the spherical conjugacy growth series of
a RAAG, and more generally for a graph product
G_{V}
,
based on the spherical growth series

{\sigma}_{U}
and spherical conjugacy growth series
{\widetilde{\sigma}}_{U}
of
the subgraph products
G_{U}=\langle X_{U}\rangle

induced by subsets
U\subset V
.
In
[
6
, Theorem A]
the formula
is obtained
from splitting the graph product as an amalgamated product;
given any vertex
v\in V
,
the spherical growth
series of the graph product
G_{V}

is given by

{\widetilde{\sigma}}_{V}=\,{\widetilde{\sigma}}_{V\setminus\{v\}}+{\widetilde{\sigma}}_{\mathsf{Lk}(v)}({\widetilde{\sigma}}_{\{v\}}-1)+\sum_{S\subseteq\mathsf{Lk}(v)}{\widetilde{\sigma}}^{\mathcal{M}}_{S}~\mathsf{N}\left(\left(\frac{{\sigma}_{\mathsf{Lk}(S)\setminus\{v\}}}{{\sigma}_{\mathsf{Lk}(v)\cap\mathsf{Lk}(S)}}-1\right)({\sigma}_{\{v\}}-1)\right),

(3)

where

\mathsf{Lk}(v)
is the set of vertices adjacent to
v

in the defining graph
\Gamma=(V,E)
of the graph product,

{\widetilde{\sigma}}^{\mathcal{M}}_{S}:=\sum_{S^{\prime}\subseteq S}(-1)^{|S|-|S^{\prime}|}{\widetilde{\sigma}}_{S^{\prime}}
,
and for any formal complex power series
f(z)
,

\mathsf{N}(f)(z):=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{\phi(k)}{kl}\big(f(z^{k})\big)^{l}

(4)

in which
\phi
is the Euler totient function.
In contrast to Theorem
4.9
, the formula in
Equation (
3
) is recursive,
requiring iteration to compute the spherical conjugacy
growth series of subsequent subgraph products.
For the operator
\mathsf{N}
,
when
F_{L}
is the growth series of a
set
L
of words over
X
,

\mathsf{N}(F_{L})

is the growth function of

\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}(L^{n}/C_{n})
,
where
L^{n}
denotes a Cartesian product of
n
copies of
L

and the cyclic group of order
n
,
C_{n}
, acts by permutation.
In words, with the operator
\mathsf{N}

in Equation (
3
)
we take Cartesian products of a language and then
quotient by appropriate cyclic group actions,
whereas with the operator
\mathsf{\rho}

in Equation (
1
)
we take the quotient on the language itself.

As a first illustration of the potential for applications
of Theorem
4.9
,
our next corollary provides a formula for
computing the spherical growth series
of a free product of a RAAG with a free group.

Corollary
4.11
.

Let
G_{W}
be a right-angled Artin group on a simple graph
\Gamma_{W}=(W,E)
,
and let
G_{W}\ast F_{m}
be the free product of
G_{W}
with
a free group of rank

with free basis

.
Viewing
G_{W}\ast F_{m}
as a RAAG with defining graph
\Gamma_{V}:=(V,E)
where

,
let
X_{W}
and
X_{V}
be the Artin generating sets
of
G_{W}
and
G_{W}\ast F_{m}
, respectively.
Then

{\widetilde{\sigma}}_{(G_{W}\ast F_{m},X_{V})}={\widetilde{\sigma}}_{(G_{W},X_{W})}+\mathsf{\rho}\left(F_{\mathsf{CycSL}(G_{W}\ast F_{m},X_{V})}-F_{\mathsf{CycSL}(G_{W},X_{W})}\right).

In Section
5
we discuss methods for computing
the languages
\mathsf{CycSL}^{U}
and their strict growth series

for right-angled Artin groups
using the KBMAG
[

]
package in the computational
algebra software GAP
[

]
.
Because the computations are quite similar,
we also consider computations of the

We accompany this discussion with specific

For the remainder of Section

In Example

Notation and terminology

We use standard notation from formal language theory:
For any finite set

over

.
We write

For each word

X
.

All groups in this paper are finitely generated, and all generating sets finite and inverse-closed.
For a group

with inverse-closed generating set

,
let
\pi:X^{*}\rightarrow G
be the natural projection onto

, and let

=_{G}

For

of

Let

Let

||g||_{\sim}:=\min\{||h||\mid h\in[g]_{\sim}\}.

A word

\displaystyle\mathsf{Geo}=\mathsf{Geo}(G,X)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{w\in X^{*}\mid l(w)=||\pi(w)||\},

\displaystyle\mathsf{ConjGeo}=\mathsf{ConjGeo}(G,X)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{w\in X^{*}\mid l(w)=||\pi(w)||_{\sim}\}.

Fix a total order of

, and

be

(for which

For each

.
For each conjugacy class

, the

.
The

and

for

\displaystyle\mathsf{SL}=\mathsf{SL}(G,X)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{y_{g}\mid g\in G\},

\displaystyle\mathsf{ConjSL}=\mathsf{ConjSL}(G,X)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{z_{c}\mid c\in G/\sim\}.

A
cyclic permutation
of a word

is a word of the form

such that

u,v\in X^{*}
and
w=uv
.
A word

is

lies in
\mathsf{Geo}(G,X)
.
Similarly, given a total order on

over

.
The

for

\displaystyle\mathsf{CycGeo}=\mathsf{CycGeo}(G,X)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{\text{cyclically geodesic words for }G\text{ over }X\},

\displaystyle\mathsf{CycSL}=\mathsf{CycSL}(G,X)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{\text{cyclically shortlex words for }G\text{ over }X\}.

Any language

over

gives rise to a

2.2
Cyclic representatives of languages

In this section we provide a tool for computing the conjugacy growth series of a right-angled Artin group that will be applied in Section
4
.
This language theoretic analysis appears in several references (
[
11
,
12
]
) in analytic combinatorics, but since it is not standard in group theory, we provide here the computations for the formulas.

Let
X
be a finite set. If
L
is a language over
X
, then
define

\mathsf{CycPerm}(L):=\{vu:uv\in L\},

the set of all cyclic permutations of words in
L
.
As an example, we note that for any group
G
with
finite inverse-closed generating set
X
, a cyclic permutation
of a word
w
over
X
represents a conjugate of
w
in
G
, and so
the language
\mathsf{ConjGeo}(G,X)
of conjugacy geodesics is closed under
cyclic permutation; hence
\mathsf{CycPerm}(\mathsf{ConjGeo}(G,X))=\mathsf{ConjGeo}(G,X)
.
For any language
L\subseteq X^{*}
satisfying
\mathsf{CycPerm}(L)=L
,
we further define an equivalence relation on
L
by

uv\sim vu
for all
uv\in L
;
equivalence classes are called
cycles
,
or
cyclic permutation classes
, of
L
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a finite set with a total order and
let
L
be a language over
X
satisfying
\mathsf{CycPerm}(L)=L
.
For any word
w\in L
let
w_{p}
be the word that is lexicographically
least among all cyclic permutations of
w
.
The language of
cyclic representatives
of
L
is

\mathsf{CycRep}(L):=\{w_{p}\mid w\in L\}.

Since all of the words in a cycle have the same length,
the length function on words in
L
can be extended to the set of cycles of
L
.
Note that there is a length-preserving bijection between
\mathsf{CycRep}(L)
and the set of cycles of
L
.

The goal of this section is to give a formula for the growth
series of
\mathsf{CycRep}(L)
in terms of the growth series for
L
.
We first need further notation and lemma.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
L^{\wedge n}=\{u^{n}\mid u\in L\}

denote the set of all
n
th powers of words in
L
.
The language of
primitive words
in
L
is defined to be

\mathsf{Prim}(L):=\{w\in L\mid\nexists~k>1,v\in L\textrm{ such that }v^{k}=w\}.

(For example, if L is the set
\mathsf{Prim}(A^{*})
of all primitive words over
A
, then

\mathsf{CycRep}(L)
is the set of Lyndon words over
A
.)

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X
be a finite set, let

L
be a language over
X
, and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then the following hold.

1.

F_{L^{\wedge n}}(z)=F_{L}(z^{n})
.

2.

If
L
does not contain the empty word, then

F_{L}(z)=\sum_{k\geq 1}F_{\mathsf{Prim}(L)}(z^{k})
.

Proof.

For completeness we include details of the proof of part
2
.
Since
\lambda\notin L
,
then every word in
L
can be written uniquely as a
power of a primitive word; that is,

L=\bigsqcup_{k\geq 1}(\mathsf{Prim}(L))^{\wedge k},

and so
F_{L}(z)=\sum_{k\geq 1}F_{\mathsf{Prim}(L)^{\wedge k}}(z)=\sum_{k\geq 1}F_{\mathsf{Prim}(L)}(z^{k})
, using part
1
of
Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
L\subseteq X^{*}
be a language satisfying

L^{\wedge k}\subseteq L
for all
k\geq 1
,

\lambda\notin L
, and
\mathsf{CycPerm}(L)=L.

Then the generating function of
\mathsf{CycRep}(L)
is

F_{\mathsf{CycRep}(L)}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\sum_{k\geq 1}\phi(k)F_{L}(t^{k})}{t}\,dt,

where
\phi
is the Euler totient function.

Proof.

Notice that
for any word
w\in(\mathsf{Prim}(L))^{\wedge k}
, the equivalence class (cycle)
of
w
is also contained in
(\mathsf{Prim}(L))^{\wedge k}
, and so

\mathsf{CycRep}((\mathsf{Prim}(L))^{\wedge k})=\mathsf{CycRep}(\mathsf{Prim}(L))^{\wedge k}
.
Moreover,
\mathsf{CycRep}(L)=\bigsqcup_{k\geq 1}\mathsf{CycRep}((\mathsf{Prim}(L))^{\wedge k});

then Lemma
2.2
part
1
yields

F_{\mathsf{CycRep}(L)}(z)=\sum_{k\geq 1}F_{\mathsf{CycRep}(\mathsf{Prim}(L))}(z^{k}).

(5)

The number of cycles of length
n>0
with representatives in
\mathsf{Prim}(L)
is

\frac{1}{n}\theta_{\mathsf{Prim}(L)}(n)
, and
similarly, the number of distinct cycles of length
nk
with representatives
in
(\mathsf{Prim}(L))^{\wedge k}
is also

\frac{1}{n}\theta_{(\mathsf{Prim}(L))^{\wedge k}}(nk)=\frac{1}{n}\theta_{\mathsf{Prim}(L)}(n)
.
Thus for a fixed
k
a formal power series manipulation gives

\displaystyle F_{\mathsf{CycRep}(\mathsf{Prim}(L))}(z^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\theta_{\mathsf{CycRep}(\mathsf{Prim}(L))}(n)(z^{k})^{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{k}{kn}\theta_{\mathsf{Prim}(L)}(n)z^{kn}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{z}k\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\theta_{\mathsf{Prim}(L)}(n)t^{kn-1}\,dt=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{kF_{\mathsf{Prim}(L)}(t^{k})}{t}\,dt,

and using Equation (
5

F_{\mathsf{CycRep}(L)}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\sum_{k\geq 1}kF_{\mathsf{Prim}(L)}(t^{k})}{t}\,dt.

It remains to show that

\sum_{k\geq 1}kF_{\mathsf{Prim}(L)}(t^{k})=\sum_{k\geq 1}\phi(k)F_{L}(t^{k}).

We use the number theoretic identity
k=\sum_{d/k}\phi(d)
and write

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}kF_{\mathsf{Prim}(L)}(t^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{d/k}\phi(d)F_{\mathsf{Prim}(L)}(t^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{d/k,m=\frac{k}{d}}\phi(d)F_{\mathsf{Prim}(L)}(t^{md})=\sum_{d\geq 1}\phi(d)\sum_{m\geq 1}F_{\mathsf{Prim}(L)}(t^{md})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{d\geq 1}\phi(d)F_{L}(t^{d}),

using in the last equality the fact that

F_{L}(z)=\sum_{k\geq 1}F_{\mathsf{Prim}(L)}(z^{k})
from
Lemma
2.2
part
2
.
∎

Proposition
2.3

Definition 2.4
.

(6)

The following is an immediate corollary of Proposition

.

2.3
Regular languages

We refer to
[
17
]
for details on regular languages
and finite state automata in this section.

For subsets
A,B
of
X^{*}
, the language
AB
is the set of concatenations

wu
with
w\in A
and
u\in B
.
Similarly for each
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
the set
A^{n}

is defined by

A^{0}:=\{\lambda\}
,
A^{1}:=A
, and for each

n\geq 2
,
A^{n}:=A^{n-1}A
is the set of
concatenations of
n
words from
A
.
Let
A^{*}:=\cup_{n=0}^{\infty}A^{n}
and
A^{+}=\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}A^{n}
.

A language over
X
is
regular
if it can
be built out of finite subsets of
X
using the operations of union,
intersection, complementation,
concatenation, and
*
;
such an expression for a
language is called a
regular expression
.

Regular languages are exactly the languages
accepted by finite state automata.
A
finite state automaton
, or
FSA
,
is a 5-tuple
M=(X,Q,q_{0},P,\delta)

where
X
is a finite set called the
alphabet
,

Q
is a finite set known as the
set of states
,

q_{0}\in Q
is the
initial state
,

P\subseteq Q
is the
set of accept states
,
and
\delta:Q\times X\rightarrow Q
is known as
the
transition function
.
The transition function can be extended
to
\widehat{\delta}:Q\times X^{*}\rightarrow Q

recursively, by defining

\widehat{\delta}(q,wx):=\delta(\widehat{\delta}(q,w),x)

for all
q\in Q
,
w\in X^{*}
, and
x\in X
.
The
language accepted by the FSA

M

is the set of all words
w\in X^{*}

such that
\widehat{\delta}(q_{0},w)\in P
.

For any regular language
L
, the strict
growth series
F_{L}(z)
is a rational function.
This can be effectively computed, and in
particular the

GrowthFSA
command of the
KBMAG package
[
16
]
in
the GAP computational algebra software
[
13
]

implements the computation of this
rational function for an FSA input.

3
Graph products and a decomposition of

{\widetilde{\sigma}}

In this section we obtain a
language of unique
conjugacy geodesic representatives of conjugacy classes
in a graph product of groups that is built
from shortlex conjugacy languages for subgraph products.
This reduces the computation of the spherical
conjugacy growth series of the entire graph product to
computation of the spherical conjugacy growth series for
subgraph products over indecomposable subsets of vertices,
in Theorem
3.7
.
In Section
4
we use these results
in the case of graph products of infinite cyclic
groups (that is, RAAGs) to produce yet another language
of conjugacy geodesic representatives that is more
amenable to computation.

We begin in Section 3.1 with terminology, notation, and
known results on graph products of groups.

3.1
Background on graph products and languages

Let
\Gamma=(V,E)
be a finite simple graph;
that is, an undirected graph without loops or multiple edges
with vertex set
V
and edge set

E
consisting of pairs of elements of
V
.
Also throughout Section
3
,
let
\ll
be a fixed total order of
V
.

For each vertex
v
of
\Gamma
, let

G_{v}
be a nontrivial group with an inverse-closed generating set
X_{v}
.
The
graph product of the groups
G_{v}

with respect to
\Gamma
, denoted
G_{\Gamma}

or
G_{V}
, is the quotient of
the free product of the groups
G_{v}
by the normal
closure of the set of relators
[g_{v},g_{w}]
for all

g_{v}\in G_{v}
and
g_{w}\in G_{w}

such that
\{v,w\}\in E
. The group
G_{V}

is generated by the (inverse-closed) set
X_{V}=\cup_{v\in V}X_{v}
.

For any subset
U\subseteq V
, the graph
induced
by

U
is the graph
\Gamma_{U}=(U,E_{U})
with edges

E_{U}=\{\{a,b\}\in E\mid a,b\in U\}
.
The
subgraph product
of
G_{V}
associated to
U
is the subgroup

G_{U}:=\langle\{G_{v}\mid v\in U\}\rangle
of
G_{V}
.
By
[
14
, Proposition 3.31]
,
G_{U}
is isomorphic to the graph product of
the groups
G_{v}
(
v\in U
) on the induced subgraph
\Gamma_{U}
.
Note that
G_{\emptyset}
is the trivial group.

The
support
of a word
w\in X_{V}^{*}

is the set

\mathsf{Supp}(w)=\{v\in V\mid\text{ a letter in }X_{v}\text{ appears in }w\}.

We will often work with the complement graph rather
than with
\Gamma
itself.
The
complement
of the graph
\Gamma
is the graph

\overline{\Gamma}=(V,\overline{E})
with the same vertex set as
\Gamma

and edge set that is the complement of
E
; that is,

\overline{E}:=\{\{u,v\}\mid u,v\in V\text{ and }\{u,v\}\notin E\}.

Note that if
U
and
U^{\prime}
are subsets of
V
that
induce distinct connected components of
\overline{\Gamma}
,
then for any
g\in G_{U}
and
g^{\prime}\in G_{U^{\prime}}
, the elements

g
and
g^{\prime}
commute in the graph product
G_{V}
.
This yields the following.

Remark 3.1
.

If
V_{1},...,V_{m}\subset V
induce the connected
components of
\overline{\Gamma_{V}}
, then the graph product

G_{V}\cong G_{V_{1}}\times\cdots\times G_{V_{m}}

is the direct product of the subgraph products
G_{V_{i}}
.

A total order
<_{V}
of the generating set
X_{V}=\cup_{v\in V}X_{v}

of a graph product group
G_{V}
on a graph
\Gamma=(V,E)

is called
compatible with

a total order
\ll
of the vertex set
V

of
\Gamma
if
for each vertex
v\in V
there is a total order

<_{v}
of the set
X_{v}
such that for all
a,b\in X_{V}

we have
a<_{V}b
if and only if either

\mathsf{Supp}(a)=\{u\}
and
\mathsf{Supp}(b)=\{u^{\prime}\}
with
u\ll u^{\prime}
,
or
\mathsf{Supp}(a)=\mathsf{Supp}(b)=\{u\}
and
a<_{u}b
.

Proposition 3.2
.

[
6
, Cor. 3.3 and Prop.3.7]

Let
G_{V}
be a graph product group with generating set
X_{V}
, and
let
U
be any subset of
V
.
Let
<_{sl}
be a shortlex order on
X_{V}^{*}
induced by an order on
X_{V}

compatible with a total order
\ll
on
V
, and let the shortlex
order on
X_{U}^{*}
be the restriction of the order
<_{sl}
.
Then

\displaystyle\mathsf{SL}(G_{V},X_{V})\cap X_{U}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathsf{SL}(G_{U},X_{U}),

\displaystyle\mathsf{CycSL}(G_{V},X_{V})\cap X_{U}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathsf{CycSL}(G_{U},X_{U})

\displaystyle\mathsf{ConjSL}(G_{V},X_{V})\cap X_{U}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathsf{ConjSL}(G_{U},X_{U}),

\displaystyle\mathsf{Geo}(G_{V},X_{V})\cap X_{U}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathsf{Geo}(G_{U},X_{U}),

\displaystyle\mathsf{CycGeo}(G_{V},X_{V})\cap X_{U}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathsf{CycGeo}(G_{U},X_{U}),\text{ and}

\displaystyle\mathsf{ConjGeo}(G_{V},X_{V})\cap X_{U}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathsf{ConjGeo}(G_{U},X_{U}).

The
shuffle
operation on a word
w\in X_{V}^{*}
is an application
of a commuting relation from the graph product construction;
that is, if we can write
w=yabz
with
y,z\in X_{V}^{*}
,

a\in X_{v}^{+}
, and
b\in X_{v^{\prime}}^{+}
satisfying
\{v,v^{\prime}\}

is an edge of
\Gamma
, then there is a shuffle operation
from
w
to the word
ybaz
.
We write
w\stackrel{{\scriptstyle sh}}{{\longrightarrow}}w^{\prime}
if there is
a finite sequence of shuffle operations from
w
to
w^{\prime}
.

3.2
A decomposition of
{\widetilde{\sigma}}(G_{V},X_{V})
for graph products

Let
<_{V}
be a total order of the generating set
X_{V}=\cup_{v\in V}X_{v}

of a graph product group
G_{V}
on a graph
\Gamma=(V,E)

that is compatible with
a total order
\ll
of the vertex set
V

of
\Gamma
.

Given any language
L\subset X_{V}^{*}
and
subset
U\subseteq V
, let

L^{U}:=\{w\in L\mid\mathsf{Supp}(w)=U\}.

Remark 3.3
.

Note that for any language
L\subseteq X_{V}^{*}
and subset
U\subseteq V
,

L^{U}=L\cap(\cap_{u\in U}X_{V}^{*}X_{u}X_{V}^{*}).

(8)

Hence if
L
is a regular language, then so is the language

L^{U}
.

For any subset
U\subseteq V
,
Proposition
3.2
shows that

\mathsf{ConjSL}(G_{V},X_{V})^{U}=\mathsf{ConjSL}(G_{U},X_{U})^{U};

we also denote this set by
\mathsf{ConjSL}^{U}
.
Thus this set can be viewed
as the set of shortlex conjugacy normal forms for the pair
(G_{U},X_{U})

that have “full support”, in the sense that
they include at least one letter from
X_{v}
for every
v\in U
.
Then
\mathsf{ConjSL}(G_{V},X_{V})
is a disjoint union

\mathsf{ConjSL}(G_{V},X_{V})=\bigsqcup_{U\subseteq V}\mathsf{ConjSL}^{U}.

Consequently the spherical conjugacy growth series
satisfies

{\widetilde{\sigma}}_{(G_{V},X_{V})}=F_{\mathsf{ConjSL}(G_{V},X_{V})}=\sum_{U\subseteq V}F_{\mathsf{ConjSL}^{U}};

(9)

that is,
{\widetilde{\sigma}}_{(G_{V},X_{V})}
is the sum of the generating
functions for the languages
\mathsf{ConjSL}^{U}
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E)
and let
\ll
be a total
order of
V
.
The
\ll
-decomposition
of a nonempty subset
U\subseteq V
is the
ordered tuple

\delta_{\ll}(U)=(U_{1},U_{2},...,U_{m})

such that

(a)

the connected components of the complement graph

\overline{\Gamma_{U}}
are the subgraphs induced by
U_{1},...,U_{m}
, and

(b)

for each
1\leq i<m
, there is a vertex

u\in U_{i}
satisfying
u\ll v
for all
v\in\cup_{j=i+1}^{m}~U_{j}
.

A subset
U\subseteq V
is called
indecomposable
, or
non-split
,
if
U
is nonempty and
the induced complement subgraph
\overline{\Gamma_{U}}
has exactly
one connected component.

This terminology comes from Remark
3.1
;
if
\delta_{\ll}(U)=(U_{1},U_{2},...,U_{m})
, then

G_{U}\cong G_{U_{1}}\times\cdots\times G_{U_{m}}
decomposes,
or splits, as a direct product of subgraph products.
Note that in the case that
U=\emptyset
, the graph
\overline{\Gamma_{U}}

has no components;
hence
U
is not called indecomposable.

4
RAAGs:
Conjugacy representatives and a formula for
{\widetilde{\sigma}}

The goal of Section
4
is to build upon
the conjugacy geodesic representatives of conjugacy classes from
Section
3.2
in the case of right-angled
Artin groups,
in order to produce another language of conjugacy geodesic representatives
that naturally extends the language of cyclically reduced words in free groups
(but is different from
\mathsf{ConjSL}
), and to
give a formula for the spherical conjugacy growth series of a RAAG.
This language of conjugacy geodesic representatives
also satisfies the property that its growth series can be easily obtained using the formulas in Section
2.2
, provided that the growth series of a regular language built out of shortlex representatives is available;
applications to computing the conjugacy growth
series for some families of RAAGs are discussed in
Sections
4.2
and
5
.

We first gather some more terminology and known
results on languages associated to RAAGs in Section
4.1
.

4.1
Further background on RAAGs and languages

A
right-angled Artin group
,
or
RAAG
, with respect to a finite simple graph

\Gamma=(V,E)
is the graph product group
G_{V}
such that
each vertex group
G_{v}

is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
.
If each vertex generating set

X_{v}=\{x_{v},x_{v}^{-1}\}
consists of a generator of
G_{v}

and its inverse, we refer to the generating set

X_{V}=\cup_{v\in V}X_{v}
as the

Artin generating set
for the RAAG
G_{V}
.

For RAAGs, two of the languages discussed
in this paper coincide.

Proposition 4.1
.

[
5
, Corollary 2.5]

If
G_{V}
is a right-angled Artin group,
then a word
w
over the Artin generating
set
X_{V}
is a conjugacy geodesic
if and only if every cyclic conjugate
of
w
is geodesic; that is,
\mathsf{ConjGeo}(G_{V},X_{V})=\mathsf{CycGeo}(G_{V},X_{V})
.

Recall that a shuffle operation on a word
w\in X_{V}^{*}
is
an application of a commuting relation,
replacing a word
yabz
by the word
ybaz
whenever

y,z\in X_{V}^{*}
,

a\in X_{v}^{+}
,
b\in X_{v^{\prime}}^{+}
, and
\{v,v^{\prime}\}

is an edge of the graph
\Gamma
.
A
deletion
exchanges a word of
the form
yay^{\prime}a^{-1}y^{\prime\prime}
by the word
yy^{\prime}y^{\prime\prime}

whenever
y,y^{\prime},y^{\prime\prime}\in X_{V}^{*}
,
a\in X_{v}^{*}
for some
v\in V
,
and for each
v^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Supp}(y^{\prime})
, either

v^{\prime}=v
or
v
and
v^{\prime}
are joined by an edge in
\Gamma
.
We write
w\stackrel{{\scriptstyle sh}}{{\longrightarrow}}w^{\prime}
if there is
a finite sequence of shuffle operations from
w
to
w^{\prime}
,
and
w\stackrel{{\scriptstyle shde}}{{\longrightarrow}}w^{\prime}
if there is
a finite sequence of shuffle and deletion
operations from
w
to
w^{\prime}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

[
22
, p. 36]
,
[
4

4.2

We begin this section with Theorem

and Corollary

that establish the set of conjugacy geodesic

To accomplish this, we combine our setup from
Sections
2
and
3

with results of Crisp, Godelle, and Wiest in

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
G_{V}
be a right-angled Artin group with Artin generating
set
X_{V}
, let
<_{V}
be a total order of
X_{V}

that is compatible with a total order
\ll
of
V
,
and let
<_{sl}
be the corresponding shortlex order on
X_{V}^{*}
.
Let
U
be a subset of
V
.
If
U
is indecomposable, then there is a well-defined bijection

\Phi:\mathsf{CycRep}(\mathsf{CycSL}(G_{V},X_{V})^{U})\rightarrow\mathsf{ConjSL}(G_{V},X_{V})^{U}

that preserves conjugacy class and word length.

Proof.

Suppose that
U
is an indecomposable subset of
V
.
For each element
w
of
\mathsf{CycRep}(\mathsf{CycSL}^{U})
, define

\Phi(w)
to be the conjugacy shortlex normal form of
the conjugacy class containing
w
.

Let
w
be any element of
\mathsf{CycRep}(\mathsf{CycSL}^{U})\subseteq\mathsf{CycSL}^{U}
.
Since every shortlex normal form is geodesic,
then
w\in\mathsf{CycGeo}^{U}
as well.
Now
\Phi(w)\in\mathsf{ConjSL}(G_{V},X_{V})\subseteq\mathsf{ConjGeo}(G_{V},X_{V})
,
and so Proposition
4.1
says
that
\Phi(w)\in\mathsf{CycGeo}(G_{V},X_{V})
also. Now
Lemma 2.9 of
[
7
]
says that the two cyclically geodesic
words

and
\Phi(w)
, which represent conjugate elements of
G_{V}
,
are related by a sequence of cyclic permutations and commutation
relations (i.e., shuffles). Hence these two words have the
same support, and so
\Phi(w)\in\mathsf{ConjSL}(G_{V},X_{V})^{U}
, as required.

Applying Proposition
4.1
to the word
w
shows that

w
is a conjugacy geodesic, as is the word
\Phi(w)
,
and hence the function
\Phi
preserves word length.

Now suppose that
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathsf{CycRep}(\mathsf{CycSL}^{U})
and
\Phi(w)=\Phi(w^{\prime})
.
Then
w
and
w^{\prime}
are cyclically shortlex normal forms
that represent elements in the same conjugacy class,
and so
[
7
, Proposition 2.21]
shows that

w^{\prime}
is a cyclic permutation of
w
. But since both words
are lexicographically least among their common set of cyclic permutations,

and
\Phi
is injective.

Finally,
[
7
, Propositions 2.18 and 2.20]
show
that every word in
\mathsf{CycGeo}^{U}
, and hence every
word in
\mathsf{ConjSL}^{U}
,
represents an element of
G_{V}
conjugate to an element
represented by a cyclically shortlex word.
Therefore
\Phi
is also surjective.
∎

and

.

Since

to

or commute via a shuffle with

or

Now

and for each vertex

Since

Note that

\mathsf{CycSL}(G_{V},X_{V})=X^{*}_{V}\setminus\mathsf{CycPerm}(X^{*}_{V}\setminus\mathsf{SL}(G_{V},X_{V}))

the set

[

,
23
]

and a finite state automaton for the language

[

[

]

Commands are also available in the GAP KBMAG package
for manipulating FSAs, and in particular
producing FSAs that accept the complement of a language or

An automaton recognizing the regular language

can be computed from this, and then a further

For each

Example 5.2
.

The free abelian group on
n
generators

\mathbb{Z}^{n}=G_{V}=\langle a_{1},...,a_{n}\mid[a_{i},a_{j}]=1
for all
i\neq j\rangle

is the RAAG with defining graph

\Gamma_{V}
that is the complete graph on the
n
vertices in
V
.

For these abelian groups, each conjugacy class is
a singleton, and so growth and conjugacy growth coincide.
The spherical (conjugacy) growth series
of this group with respect to the usual generating set

X_{V}=\{a_{1},...,a_{n}\}^{\pm 1}
can be computed completely as

{\widetilde{\sigma}}_{(\mathbb{Z}^{n},X_{V})}={\sigma}_{(\mathbb{Z}^{n},X_{V})}=\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right)^{n};

that is,
{\widetilde{\sigma}}
is the product of the spherical conjugacy
growth series of the
n
vertex groups.

The geodesic and geodesic conjugacy growth series also
coincide for abelian groups.
In
[
18
, Proposition 1]
Loeffler, Meier, and
Worthington show that whenever

{\gamma}_{(H,Y)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}h_{k}z^{k}
and

{\gamma}_{(J,Z)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}j_{k}z^{k}
, then
the direct product of
H
and
J
has geodesic growth series
{\gamma}_{(H\times J,Y\cup Z)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}m_{k}z^{k}

where
m_{k}:=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}h_{i}j_{k-i}
for all
k
;
in
[
4
, Theorem 3.8]
the first two authors
show that the same formula holds
for the conjugacy geodesic growth series of a direct product in terms of
the conjugacy geodesic growth series of the direct factors.
However, in both cases this formula
does not directly show how to compute a rational expression for

{\widetilde{\gamma}}(H\times J,Y\cup Z)
from rational expressions for the conjugacy
growth series of the factors.
Using Equation (
17
)
and GAP again,
the conjugacy geodesic growth series of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
for

2\leq n\leq 5
has the form

{\widetilde{\gamma}}_{(\mathbb{Z}^{n},X_{V})}={\gamma}_{(\mathbb{Z}^{n},X_{V})}=\frac{1+(n(3-n)/2)z+\cdots+(n!)z^{n}}{(1-z)(1-2z)\cdots(1-nz)}

(18)

(where we do not imply a pattern for the other coefficients
in the numerator here),
which led us to conjecture that the (conjugacy) geodesic
growth series for
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
for all
n

is a rational function with denominator of the form

(1-z)(1-2z)\cdots(1-nz)
, and hence the geodesic
growth function for
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
satisfies

\Theta_{\mathsf{Geo}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},X_{V})}(k)=a_{n,1}(1^{k})+a_{n,2}(2^{k})+...+a_{n,n}(n^{k})

for all
k\geq 1
and for some constants
a_{n,j}
.
The rational functions computed in GAP for
2\leq n\leq 5

satisfy
a_{n,j}=(-1)^{n-j}(2^{j})\binom{n}{j}
.
Applying the formula above from
[
18
,
4
]

for computing the (conjugacy) geodesic growth
series of a direct product
to
\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{Z}
, along with induction on
n
,
shows that this conjecture holds and that
this formula holds for the constants
a_{n,j}
for all
n
,
yielding Theorem
5.3
.

Theorem 5.3
.

The geodesic and conjugacy geodesic growth series
for
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with respect to the standard generating set
X

satisfy

{\widetilde{\gamma}}_{(\mathbb{Z}^{n},X)}={\gamma}_{(\mathbb{Z}^{n},X)}=1+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}(2^{j})\binom{n}{j}\frac{jz}{1-jz}

for all
n\geq 0
.

The above formula can also be obtained directly by counting geodesic paths of a given length
m
starting at the origin in each of the
2^{n}
orthants of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, (there are
n^{m}
such geodesic paths in an orthant of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
), noting that no geodesic can cross between orthants, and controlling the overcounting and undercounting in the intersections of orthants by inclusion-exclusion.

We note that the GAP code described earlier in Section
5

and illustrated in

6.2
is not able to
compute the rational expression for
{\widetilde{\gamma}}

in Example
5.4
directly;
as noted in the manual
[
16
]
for the KBMAG package,
when coefficients in the rational function are greater than
roughly 16,000, the software fails to complete the computation
of the growth series of the finite state automaton.
As a consequence, we computed the conjugacy geodesic growth
series with a slightly altered method, applying the principle
of inclusion-exclusion, which can be used
more generally in the computation for
{\widetilde{\gamma}}
for any RAAG
G_{V}
over an
Artin generating set
X_{V}
as follows.
For each vertex
v\in V
, let
L_{v}^{\prime}
be the
language over
X_{V}
defined by

L_{v}^{\prime}=\{w_{1}x_{v}^{e}w_{2}x_{v}^{-e}w_{3}\mid e\in\{\pm 1\},w_{1},w_{3}\in X_{\mathsf{Lk}(v)}^{*},\text{ and }w_{2}\in X_{V}^{*}\}

(where as before,
\mathsf{Lk}(v)
is the set of all
vertices of
V
that are adjacent to
v
in the graph

\Gamma=(V,E)
defining
G_{V}
).
This language
L_{v}^{\prime}
is the set of all words
w
over
X_{V}

satisfying the property that for some cyclic permutation
w^{\prime}

of
w
, a finite sequence of shuffles can be applied to
w^{\prime}

to obtain a word with a subword of the form

x_{v}x_{v}^{-1}
or
x_{v}^{-1}x_{v}
.
Hence Propositions
4.1

and
4.2
show that words in
\mathsf{Geo}(G_{V},X_{V})

fail to be in
\mathsf{ConjGeo}(G_{V},X_{V})=\mathsf{CycGeo}(G_{V},X_{V})

if and only if they lie on one of
these languages
L_{v}^{\prime}
; that is,
the language
\mathsf{ConjGeo}(G_{V},X_{V})
is
obtained from
\mathsf{Geo}(G_{V},X_{V})
by

\mathsf{ConjGeo}(G_{V},X_{V})=\mathsf{Geo}(G_{V},X_{V})-\bigcup_{v\in V}\left(L_{v}^{\prime}\cap\mathsf{Geo}(G_{V},X_{V})\right).

Now the growth series for these languages satisfy

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\gamma}}_{(G_{V},X_{V})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\gamma}_{(G_{V},X_{V})}-\sum_{v\in V}F_{\mathsf{Geo}(G_{V},X_{V})\cap L_{v}^{\prime}}+\sum_{\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\subseteq V}F_{\mathsf{Geo}(G_{V},X_{V})\cap L_{v_{1}}^{\prime}\cap L_{v_{2}}^{\prime}}

(19)

\displaystyle+\cdots+(-1)^{i}\sum_{\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{i}\}\subseteq V}F_{\mathsf{Geo}(G_{V},X_{V})\cap L_{v_{1}}^{\prime}\cap\cdots\cap L_{v_{i}}^{\prime}}+\cdots

\displaystyle+(-1)^{|V|}F_{\mathsf{Geo}(G_{V},X_{V})\cap L_{v_{1}}^{\prime}\cap\cdots\cap L_{v_{|V|}}^{\prime}}

where the sum in the
i
-th term

(-1)^{i}\sum_{\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{i}\}\subseteq V}F_{\mathsf{Geo}(G_{V},X_{V})\cap L_{v_{1}}^{\prime}\cap\cdots\cap L_{v_{i}}^{\prime}}

is taken over the sets of
i
distinct vertices
v_{1},...,v_{i}
of
V
.
The languages
L_{v}^{\prime}
are regular, and each
L_{v}^{\prime}
is the accepted
language of an FSA with 6 states which can be input into KBMAG.
The FSA for the language
\mathsf{Geo}(G_{V},X_{V})
can be computed as described
earlier in Section
5
, and commands in KBMAG/GAP can
be used to create FSAs and compute their growth
for the intersections of languages appearing in Equation (
19
).

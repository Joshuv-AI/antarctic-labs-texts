Metal-insulator transition in n n -type bulk crystals and films of strongly compensated SrTiO 3
Yi Huang (黄奕), Y. Ayino, B. I. Shklovskii

Abstract
We start by analyzing experimental data of Spinelli et al Spinelli et al. 2010 for the conductivity of n n -type bulk crystals of SrTiO 3 (STO) with broad electron concentration n n range of 4 × 10 15 4\times 10^{15} - 4 × 10 20 4\times 10^{20} cm -3 , at low temperatures. We obtain good fit of the conductivity data, σ ⁡ ( n ) \sigma(n) , by the Drude formula for n ≥ n c ≃ 3 × 10 16 n\geq n_{c}\simeq 3\times 10^{16} cm -3 assuming that used for doping insulating STO bulk crystals are strongly compensated and the total concentration of background charged impurities is N = 10 19 N=10^{19} cm -3 . At n < n c n<n_{c} , the conductivity collapses with decreasing n n and the Drude theory fit fails. We argue that this is the metal-insulator transition (MIT) in spite of the very large Bohr radius of hydrogen-like donor state a B ≃ 700 a_{B}\simeq 700 nm with which the Mott criterion of MIT for a weakly compensated semiconductor, n ​ a B 3 ≃ 0.02 na_{B}^{3}\simeq 0.02 , predicts 10 5 10^{5} times smaller n c n_{c} . We try to explain this discrepancy in the framework of the theory of the percolation MIT in a strongly compensated semiconductor with the same N = 10 19 N=10^{19} cm -3 . In the second part of this paper, we develop the percolation MIT theory for films of strongly compensated semiconductors. We apply this theory to doped STO films with thickness d ≤ 130 d\leq 130 nm and calculate the critical MIT concentration n c ​ ( d ) n_{c}(d) . We find that, for doped STO films on insulating STO bulk crystals, n c ​ ( d ) n_{c}(d) grows with decreasing d d . Remarkably, STO films in a low dielectric constant environment have the same n c ​ ( d ) n_{c}(d) . This happens due to the Rytova-Keldysh modification of a charge impurity potential which allows a larger number of the film charged impurities to contribute to the random potential.

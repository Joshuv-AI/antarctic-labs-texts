Equivariant formality in complex-oriented theories
Shaoyun Bai, Daniel Pomerleano

Abstract
. Let G G be a product of unitary groups and let ( M , ω ) (M,\omega) be a compact symplectic manifold with Hamiltonian G G -action. We prove an equivariant formality result for any complex-oriented cohomology theory 𝔼 ∗ \mathbb{E}^{*} (in particular, integral cohomology). This generalizes the celebrated result of Atiyah–Bott–Kirwan for rational cohomology from the 1980s. The proof does not use classical ideas but instead relies on a recent cohomological splitting result of Abouzaid–McLean–Smith [ AMS21 ] for Hamiltonian fibrations over ℂ ​ ℙ 1 . \mathbb{CP}^{1}. Moreover, we establish analogues of the “localization” and “injectivity to fixed points” theorems for certain cohomology theories studied by Hopkins–Kuhn–Ravenel in [ HKR00 ] . As an application of these results, we establish a Goresky–Kottwitz–MacPherson theorem with Morava K K -theory coefficients for Hamiltonian T T -manifolds.



1.
Introduction

In
[
GKM98
]
, Goresky, Kottwitz, and MacPherson introduced the notion of an equivariantly formal space. Let
G
be a compact, connected Lie group,
T\subset G
a maximal torus and
R
a commutative ring. A
G
-space

is called equivariantly formal over
R
if the Leray spectral sequence

(1.1)

\displaystyle H^{p}(BG;H^{q}(M;R))=>H_{G}^{p+q}(M;R)

degenerates. The main examples of equivariantly formal-spaces (c.f.
[
GKM98
, §14.1]
) arise from the following celebrated result of Atiyah–Bott–Kirwan (
[
AB84
, §7]
,
[
Kir84
, §5]
):

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact symplectic manifold equipped with a Hamiltonian
G
-action. Then
M
is equivariantly formal over any
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
R
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
easily reduces to the case
G=T
via taking Weyl invariants. In the torus-equivariant case, the idea is to argue that the moment map for a generic one-parameter subgroup of
T
is equivariantly perfect. Theorem
1.1
naturally leads one to ask what happens with integral coefficients. The proof of this claim in these classical references relies on the so-called Atiyah–Bott lemma (
[
AB83
, Proposition 13.4]
,
[
Kir84
, Lemma 2.18]
), whose proof may fail when
R=\mathbb{Z}
(or even
R=\mathbb{F}_{p})
and the cohomology of the critical sets have torsion (see e.g.
[
AB83
, §13]
,
[
HL07
, Remark 2.4]
,
[
TW99
, §4]
). The main result of this note is the following somewhat surprising generalization of Theorem
1.1
to any complex-oriented cohomology theory
\mathbb{E}^{*}
, including integral cohomology as a special case:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact symplectic manifold with Hamiltonian
G
-action.

(1)

Suppose
G
is a product of unitary groups. For any complex oriented cohomology theory
\mathbb{E}^{*}
, the Leray spectral sequence

(1.2)

\displaystyle H^{p}(BG;\mathbb{E}^{q}(M))=>\mathbb{E}_{G}^{p+q}(M)

degenerates. Moreover, there is a splitting:

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{*}_{G}(M)\cong\mathbb{E}^{*}(M\times BG)

of
\mathbb{E}^{*}(BG)
modules.
1
1

1

Contrary to the usual convention in equivariant homotopy theory, we use the notation
\mathbb{E}_{G}^{*}(M)
to denote the Borel-equivariant cohomology.

(2)

For general

is an algebra over a field

for which the natural map

(1.4)

\displaystyle H^{*}(BG;\mathbf{k})\to H^{*}(BT;\mathbf{k})

is injective. Then the Leray spectral sequence (

) degenerates.

Specializing to the case of classical cohomology implies the following result, which already appears to be new:

.

Let

be a compact symplectic manifold with Hamiltonian

(1)

\displaystyle H_{G}^{*}(M;R)\cong H^{*}(M;R)\otimes_{R}H_{G}^{*}(pt;R).

(1.6)

\displaystyle H^{*}(BG;\mathbf{k})\to H^{*}(BT;\mathbf{k})

.

As far as the authors know, for

Theorem
1.3
may be unexpected as many of the other well-known consequences of the Atiyah–Bott lemma are known to fail with integral coefficients (or finite field coefficients). For example, it is easy to construct examples of Hamiltonian
T
-manifolds
M
for which the restriction to the fixed point locus
j^{*}:H_{T}^{*}(M,\mathbb{Z})\to H_{T}^{*}(M^{T},\mathbb{Z})
fails to be injective. Indeed, we do not know how to prove Theorem
1.2
(or even Theorem
1.3
) using this circle of ideas.

Instead, we deduce Theorem
1.2
from a more general result in symplectic topology. Recall that a locally trivial fiber bundle
M\to P\to B
with fiber a closed symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
is said to be Hamiltonian if its structure group can be reduced to the group of Hamiltonian symplectomorphisms
\operatorname{Ham}(M,\omega)
. We prove the following cohomological splitting result for Hamiltonian fibrations:

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
M\to P\to B
be Hamiltonian fibration over a base
B
which is a product of complex Grassmannians. Then there is a non-canonical isomorphism of
\mathbb{E}^{*}(B)
modules

(1.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{*}(P)\cong\mathbb{E}^{*}(M)\otimes_{\mathbb{E}^{*}(pt)}\mathbb{E}^{*}(B)

The case where
B=\mathbb{CP}^{1}
is an important recent result of Abouzaid–McLean–Smith
[
AMS21
]
(see also
[
BX22
]
for a more direct argument in the case of integral cohomology). Their argument is based on “hard” pseudo-holomorphic curve methods first introduced by
[
LMP99
]
. We are able to formally “bootstrap” this result to prove Proposition
1.8
by sharpening the “soft” topological approach to cohomological splitting of Hamiltonian fibrations by Lalonde–McDuff
[
LM03
]
.

Remark 1.9
.

In the introduction to their paper
[
AMS21
]
, the authors suggest that one could use the arguments of
[
LM03
]
to prove Proposition
1.8
for
B
a product of projective spaces and cohomology with coefficients in any
field
. The goal of this note is to carry out and considerably generalize this idea (by establishing cohomological splitting for Hamiltonian fibrations over more general
B
and with coefficients in any complex-oriented cohomology theory) as well as to make the connection to classical concepts in equivariant symplectic geometry.

To finish this note, we include two applications of our equivariant formality result. Our applications both concern a class of cohomology theories studied by Hopkins–Kuhn–Ravenel in
[
HKR00
]
(cf. Definition
3.1
). For the first application, we establish an Atiyah–Bott style localization theorem (Lemma
3.6
) for HKR theories which are futhermore integral and of finite Krull dimension in the sense of Definition
3.3
. Combining this localization theorem with our equivariant formality result, we deduce the following:

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact symplectic manifold with Hamiltonian
T
-action. Let
\mathbb{E}^{*}
be an HKR cohomology theory which is integral and of finite Krull dimension. Suppose that
\mathbb{E}^{*}(M)
is free as a module over the ground ring
\mathbb{E}_{*}
. Then the restriction map on equivariant cohomology

(1.9)

\displaystyle j^{*}:\mathbb{E}_{T}^{*}(M)\to\mathbb{E}_{T}^{*}(M^{T})

is injective. In particular, if
\mathbb{E}_{*}
is a graded field,
j^{*}
is injective for any compact symplectic manifold
M
with Hamiltonian
T
-action.

This is in contrast to the aforementioned failure of injectivity of
j^{*}
for ordinary cohomology with finite field coefficients. The ingredients in the proof can further be used to prove the Atiyah–Bott “integration formula” with
\mathbb{E}^{*}
-coefficients, which is completely analogous to the classical statement for the rational cohomology; see Theorem
3.7
.

The second application concerns one of the most interesting classes of HKR theories, the Morava
K
-theories
K_{p}(n)
. For
\mathbb{E}=K_{p}(n)
, we can prove the following Goresky–Kottwitz–MacPherson type theorem which calculates the (equivariant) Morava
K
-theories of GKM spaces.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact symplectic manifold with Hamiltonian torus action by
T=(S^{1})^{m}
. It is said to satisfy the
GKM conditions
if
T
acts with only finitely many fixed points
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
, and the union of
T
-orbits of corank at most
1
is a union of invariant two-spheres
\overline{E}_{1},\dots,\overline{E}_{l}
. Each
\overline{E}_{j}
is isomorphic to
S^{2}=\mathbb{C}^{\times}\cup\{x_{j_{0}}\}\cup\{x_{j_{\infty}}\}=E_{j}\cup\{x_{j_{0}}\}\cup\{x_{j_{\infty}}\}
for some
x_{j_{0}},x_{j_{\infty}}\in\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\}
, and the action of
T
on
E_{j}
factors through a group homomorphism
\Theta_{j}:T\to S^{1}
where
S^{1}
rotates the sphere
\overline{E}_{j}
with fixed points
x_{j_{0}}
and
x_{j_{\infty}}
.

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact symplectic manifold with Hamiltonian torus action satisfying the GKM conditions and let
\mathbb{E}^{*}=K_{p}(n)
. The restriction to fixed points

(1.10)

\mathbb{E}^{*}_{T}(M)\to\bigoplus_{x_{i},1\leq i\leq k}\mathbb{E}^{*}(pt)[\![u_{1},\dots,u_{m}]\!]

is injective. Furthermore, the image is given by

(1.11)

\big\{(f_{1},\dots,f_{k})\quad|\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq l,\quad f_{j_{0}}|_{\mathbb{E}^{*}_{\ker(\Theta_{j})}}=f_{j_{\infty}}|_{\mathbb{E}^{*}_{\ker(\Theta_{j})}}\big\},

where the restriction

denotes the homomorphism

\ker(\Theta_{j})\hookrightarrow T
of the kernel of

.

This statement reduces the calculation of the cohomology rings

and

to the combinatorics of the

2.
Complex-oriented splitting

2.1.
Complex-oriented theories

Suppose
\mathbb{E}^{*}
is a complex-oriented cohomology theory with coefficient ring
\mathbb{E}_{*}
. Recall that this means that there exists a class
u\in\mathbb{E}^{2}(BS^{1})
that
restricts to a generator of the reduced cohomology,
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}^{*}(\mathbb{CP}^{1})
(a free rank one
\mathbb{E}_{*}
–module). Such
a class
u
is called a complex orientation of
\mathbb{E}^{*}
. The universal example of such a cohomology theory is complex cobordism
MU^{*}
. For a multiplicative cohomology theory
\mathbb{E}^{*}
, complex orientations are in bijective correspondence with multiplicative natural transformations to
\mathbb{E}^{*}
from complex cobordism
MU^{*}
. A choice of orientation
u\in\mathbb{E}^{2}(BS^{1})
gives rise to isomorphisms

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{*}(BS^{1})=\mathbb{E}_{*}[\![u]\!],\quad\mathbb{E}^{*}(B(S^{1})^{m})=\mathbb{E}_{*}[\![u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{m}]\!].

It follows from this that the complex orientation of
\mathbb{E}^{*}
has an associated formal group law. Let
\mu:BS^{1}\times BS^{1}\to BS^{1}
denote the universal classifying map for tensor product of line bundles. The formal group law is by definition the pull-back of
u
under this classifying map:

(2.2)

\displaystyle u_{1}+_{L_{\mathbb{E}}}u_{2}=L_{\mathbb{E}}(u_{1},u_{2})=\mu^{*}(u)\in\mathbb{E}_{*}[\![u_{1},u_{2}]\!].

From the formal group law, one can extract the
\ell
-series, which is given by:

(2.3)

\displaystyle[\ell]\cdot_{\mathbb{E}}u=\underbrace{u+_{L_{\mathbb{E}}}u+_{L_{\mathbb{E}}}\cdots+_{L_{\mathbb{E}}}u}_{l\text{ times}}\in\mathbb{E}_{*}[\![u]\!].

We also record the following technical fact which will be used in the sequel:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathbb{E}^{*}
be a complex-oriented cohomology theory and let
B
be a finite-type CW complex (meaning
B
has finitely many cells in each degree) with only even cells. The Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence

(2.4)

\displaystyle H^{p}(B,\mathbb{E}^{q}(pt))=>\mathbb{E}^{p+q}(B)

degenerates.

Proof.

Because the cohomology groups
H^{p}(B,\mathbb{Z})
are free, the
E_{2}
page of the spectral sequence can be identified with

(2.5)

\displaystyle E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}(B)\otimes\mathbb{E}^{q}(pt).

The spectral sequence is linear over
\mathbb{E}^{q}(pt)
and it suffices to prove that

(2.6)

\displaystyle d^{r}(\alpha\otimes 1)=0\text{ for all }\alpha\in H^{*}(B).

If
\mathbb{E}^{*}=MU^{*}
is complex cobordism the spectral sequence collapses because the
E_{2}
page is concentrated in even degree. In general, the complex orientation of
\mathbb{E}^{*}
is induced by a natural transformation (of multiplicative cohomology theories)
MU^{*}=>\mathbb{E}^{*}
and the Atiyah-Hirzebruch sequence is natural in the cohomology theory. The result therefore follows from the case of complex cobordism. ∎

2.2.
Splittings

Next, we discuss cohomological splittings with
\mathbb{E}^{*}
coefficients, where
\mathbb{E}^{*}
is a complex-oriented cohomology theory.

Definition 2.2
.

Suppose that we have a fibration
M\xrightarrow{i}P\xrightarrow{\pi}B
. A fibration is
c-split
if there exists a map of
\mathbb{E}_{*}
modules
s:\mathbb{E}^{*}(M)\to\mathbb{E}^{*}(P)
so that
i^{*}\circ s=\operatorname{id}.
We will refer to
s
as a
c-splitting
.

Definition 2.3
.

A smooth manifold
B
is called
Hamiltonian c-split
for
\mathbb{E}^{*}
if every Hamiltonian fibration
M\xrightarrow{i}P\xrightarrow{\pi}B
is c-split.

We suppress the cohomology theory
\mathbb{E}
when it is clear from the context. The key result for us is the following result of Abouzaid–McLean–Smith:

Theorem 2.4

(
[
AMS21
]
)
.

The one-dimensional projective space,
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
, is Hamiltonian c-split.

Remark 2.5
.

When
\mathbb{E}^{*}
is ordinary cohomology with coefficients in a commutative ring
R
, the first author and Xu (
[
BX22
]
) have given a more direct proof of Theorem
2.4
which avoids the chromatic homotopy theory arguments from
[
AMS21
]
.

The following two Lemmas are refinements of arguments in
[
LM03
]
(cf. Corollary 4.2 and Lemma 4.3 of
loc. cit.
).

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\tau^{B}:\tilde{B}\to B
be a birational map of smooth projective varieties. If
\tilde{B}
is Hamiltonian c-split, then so is
B
.

Proof.

Suppose that we have a Hamiltonian fibration
M\xrightarrow{i}P\xrightarrow{\pi}B
over
B
and let
M\xrightarrow{\tilde{i}}\tilde{P}\xrightarrow{\tilde{\pi}}\tilde{B}
be the pull-back Hamiltonian fibration, which admits a map
\tau^{P}:\tilde{P}\to P.
We can factor
\tau^{B}
as an inclusion followed by a projection
\tilde{B}\to\mathbb{CP}^{r}\times B\to B
and similarly for
\tau^{P}
. This shows that the map
\tau^{P}
is compatible with complex-orientations. Set
U\subset B
to be the locus over which
\tau^{B}
is an isomorphism and let
P_{U}
denote the restriction of the bundle to this locus. Note that we can assume that our reference fiber
M_{b}
for both
\tilde{P}
and
P
lies over a point
b\in U
. We have a Cartesian diagram of complex-oriented manifolds/maps:

\textstyle{P_{U}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tilde{j}}
\textstyle{\tilde{P}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tau^{P}}
\textstyle{P_{U}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{j}
\textstyle{P.}

For such Cartesian squares, the Umkëhr maps on cohomology are compatible with pullback/restriction maps; see
[
Qui71
, 1.7]
for complex cobordism,
[
BE90
, E(iii)]
in general, and
[
CJ98
, Proposition 12.9]
for a statement at the level of spectra, which implies the statement for cohomology by taking homotopy groups. In the present case this means that:

(2.7)

\displaystyle j^{*}\circ\tau^{P}_{*}=\tilde{j^{*}}\text{ and hence }i^{*}\circ\tau^{P}_{*}=\tilde{i}^{*}.

Suppose we are given a splitting
\tilde{s}:\mathbb{E}^{*}(M)\to\mathbb{E}^{*}(\tilde{P})
such that
(\tilde{i})^{*}\circ\tilde{s}=\operatorname{id}
. Then it follows from (
2.7
) that

(2.8)

\displaystyle s=\tau^{P}_{*}\circ\tilde{s}:\mathbb{E}^{*}(M)\to\mathbb{E}^{*}(P)

satisfies
i^{*}\circ s=\operatorname{id}.

∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose that we have a Hamiltonian fibration
M\xrightarrow{i}P\xrightarrow{\pi}B
with
B
simply connected. Suppose that
M
and
B
are Hamiltonian c-split. Then
P
is also Hamiltonian c-split.

Proof.

Let
F\to E\to P
be a Hamiltonian bundle over
P
. After restricting to
M
we get a Hamiltonian bundle
F\to W\to M
which by assumption gives rise to a c-splitting:

(2.9)

\displaystyle s_{M}:\mathbb{E}^{*}(F)\to\mathbb{E}^{*}(W).

The composite bundle
W\to E\to B
is also Hamiltonian
[
LM03
, Lemma 3.9]
and thus gives rise to a c-splitting:

(2.10)

\displaystyle s_{B}:\mathbb{E}^{*}(W)\to\mathbb{E}^{*}(E).

The desired c-splitting for
F\to E\to P
is the map
s=s_{B}\circ s_{M}
. ∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
B
be a product of complex projective spaces. Then
B
is Hamiltonian c-split.

Proof.

In view of Lemma
2.7
, it suffices to consider the case where
B=\mathbb{CP}^{k}
is a single projective space. We proceed by induction on
k
. The base case where
k=1
is Theorem
2.4
. Consider the blow-up
\tilde{B}
of
\mathbb{CP}^{k}
at a point. Then
\tilde{B}
can be identified with the
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
-bundle:

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{CP}^{k-1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{CP}^{k-1}}(-1))\to\mathbb{CP}^{k-1}.

The inductive hypothesis together with Lemma
2.7
shows that
\tilde{B}
is Hamiltonian c-split. It then follows from Lemma
2.6
that
\mathbb{CP}^{k}
is c-splitting. ∎

More generally, we have the following result:

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
B
be a product of Grassmannians. Then
B
is Hamiltonian c-split.

Proof.

Again, it suffices to consider a single Grassmannian. Recall that the Schubert varieties of the complete flag variety
GL(n,{\mathbb{C}})/B
are indexed by elements
w
of the symmetric group
S_{n}
. If we consider the Schubert variety
Z_{w}
corresponding to the permutation
w(i)=i+k(\operatorname{mod}n)
, this maps birationally onto the complex Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)
via the projection
GL(n,{\mathbb{C}})/B\to\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)
(see discussion above Example 1.2.3 in
[
Bri05
]
). Now take the Bott–Samelson variety
Z_{\underline{w}}
associated to a reduced decomposition
\underline{w}
of
w
. By e.g.
[
Bri05
, Proposition 2.2.1 (iii), (iv)]
, this is an iterated fibration of Kähler manifolds

(2.12)

\displaystyle M_{k}\to M_{k-1}\to\cdots\to M_{1}=\mathbb{CP}^{1}

with fiber
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
, which maps birationally onto
Z_{w}
and hence onto
\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)
. The statement again follows from Lemma
2.6
and Lemma
2.7
.
∎

2.3.
Proof of Theorems

We now turn to giving our main arguments. As in previous sections,
\mathbb{E}^{*}
will denote a complex-oriented cohomology theory.
We will make use of the following technical variant of the Leray–Hirsch theorem:

Lemma 2.10
.

Suppose that we have a c-split fibration
M\xrightarrow{i}P\xrightarrow{\pi}B
, where
B
is a finite type CW-complex with only even dimensional cells. Then the following hold:

(1)

If
B
is finite, the natural map

(2.13)

\displaystyle\phi:\mathbb{E}^{*}(M)\otimes_{\mathbb{E}_{*}}\mathbb{E}^{*}(B)\to\mathbb{E}^{*}(P)

is an isomorphism.

(2)

For general
B
as above, the c-splitting induces an isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{*}(M\times B)\cong\mathbb{E}^{*}(P).

of
\mathbb{E}^{*}(B)
-modules.

Proof.

Part (1):
We follow the proof of the Leray–Hirsch theorem in
[
Hat02

. We assume that
B
has dimension

and proceed by induction on

B^{\prime}
be the subspace obtained by deleting a point

from each
m

and let

denote the restriction of the fibration to
B^{\prime}

is also a c-split fibration. We let

be small disc about

over which the fibration is trivial.

We note that the cohomologies
\mathbb{E}^{*}(B,B^{\prime})
,
\mathbb{E}^{*}(B)
,
\mathbb{E}^{*}(B^{\prime})
are all free over
\mathbb{E}_{*}
. For
\mathbb{E}^{*}(B)
,
\mathbb{E}^{*}(B^{\prime})
this follows from Lemma

, together with the fact that

retracts onto the

Lemma
2.1
implies that the maps
\mathbb{E}^{*}(B)\to\mathbb{E}^{*}(B^{\prime})
are surjections. It follows from the long exact sequence for the pair

that the connecting maps

(2.15)

\displaystyle\delta:\mathbb{E}^{*}(B^{\prime})\to\mathbb{E}^{*+1}(B,B^{\prime})\text{ are zero.}

This means that the long exact sequence gives rise to a short exact sequence:

(2.16)

\displaystyle 0\to\mathbb{E}^{*}(B,B^{\prime})\to\mathbb{E}^{*}(B)\to\mathbb{E}^{*}(B^{\prime})\to 0

Because all of these groups are finite free over
\mathbb{E}_{*}

3.1.

We next turn to discussing a particular class of complex-oriented cohomology theories. Throughout the discussion, we fix a prime number

Cohomology theories of this kind were considered by Hopkins–Kuhn–Ravenel

, §5]

(3.1)

\displaystyle E(n)_{*}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[v_{1},\cdots,v_{n-1},v_{n},v_{n}^{-1}],

(1)

\displaystyle\hat{K}(n)_{*}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[v_{n},v_{n}^{-1}].

(3)

Finally the Morava

is an isomorphism. This proves the invertibility of the Euler classes, so the statement follows accordingly.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

Following
[
AB84
, Section 3]
, we can prove Theorem
3.7
in a slightly different way. For the connected component
M^{T}_{i}
, we can decompose its normal bundle into a direct sum of irreducible
T
-eigenbundles

(3.16)

\nu_{i}=\nu_{i}^{(1)}\oplus\cdots\oplus\nu_{i}^{(k)}

such that for each
1\leq\alpha\leq k
, the corresponding character
\varphi_{\alpha}:T\to U(1)
is nontrivial. If we write
u_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{E}_{*}[\![u_{1},\dots,u_{m}]\!]
the image of the generator of
\mathbb{E}^{*}(BS^{1})
in
\mathbb{E}^{*}(BT)
induced by
\varphi_{\alpha}
, then

(3.17)

\mathit{eu}^{T}_{\mathbb{E}}(\nu_{i})=\prod_{1\leq\alpha\leq k}(\mathit{eu}_{\mathbb{E}}(\nu_{i}^{(\alpha)})+_{\mathbb{E}}u_{\alpha}),

where we abuse the notation using
+_{\mathbb{E}}
to denote the element in
\mathbb{E}_{T}^{*}(M^{T})
obtained by plugging in the given two elements in the formal group law associated with
\mathbb{E}^{*}
. The Euler class of line bundles, which coincides with the first Chern class in
\mathbb{E}^{*}
, is nilpotent. Indeed, for any finite-dimensional CW complex
X
, the set of isomorphism classes of complex bundles on
X
have a one-to-one correspondence with the set of homotopy classes of maps from
X
to
\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
. If a line bundle
L\to X
is classified by
f:X\to\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
, then
\mathit{eu}_{\mathbb{E}}(L)=f^{*}(u)
, where
u
is the generator of
\mathbb{E}^{*}(\mathbb{CP}^{\infty})
. By naturality, we know that
\mathit{eu}_{\mathbb{E}}(L)^{N}=f^{*}(u^{N})
for any
N\in\mathbb{Z}
. On the other hand,
u^{N}
is the Poincaré dual of a complex codimension
N
subspace in
\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
. So, for
N\gg 1
, we can choose a homotopy of
f
to show that
f^{*}(u^{N})=0
, which proves the nilpotency. Accordingly, we see that
\mathit{eu}^{T}_{\mathbb{E}}(\nu_{i})
is invertible after tensor with the fraction field
R
.

3.3.
GKM theorem

In this subsection, we specialize our discussion to the case where
\mathbb{E}^{*}
is a Morava
K
-theory
K(n)
. We show that the so-called Chang–Skjelbred lemma works verbatim for
K(n)
coefficients, and deduce the GKM calculation of equivariant cohomology as a corollary.

Let
M
be a compact smooth
T
-manifold. Denote by
\mathcal{P}
the set of closed subschemes
\mathcal{Z}_{K}:=\operatorname{supp}(\mathbb{E}^{*}_{T}(T/K))\subset\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{E}_{*}[\![u_{1},\dots,u_{m}]\!])
where
K
is a stabilizer of some point
x\in M\setminus M^{T}
(we give
\mathcal{Z}_{K}
the reduced-induced scheme structure). This is a partially ordered set, with partial order given by inclusion. Note that by Lemma
3.5
(2), the scheme
\mathcal{Z}_{K}
only depends on the connected component of the identity element
K^{0}
.

Let
\delta^{\prime}
denote the connecting homomorphism of the
\mathbb{E}^{*}_{T}
-cohomology of the pair
(M,M^{T})
. Given
\xi\in\mathbb{E}^{*}_{T}(M^{T})
, write
I(\xi)
the ideal of annihilators of of
\delta^{\prime}(\xi)
. On the other hand, for each
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{P}
, we write
M^{\mathfrak{m}}
the subset of
M
consisting of points
x\in M
whose stabilizer group
K_{x}
satisfies
\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathcal{Z}_{K_{x}}
. Denote the connecting homomorphism of
\mathbb{E}^{*}_{T}
-cohomology of the pair
(M,M^{\mathfrak{m}})
by
\delta^{\mathfrak{m}}
. The following technical statement is proved in the same way as
[
CS74
, Lemma 2.3]
and
[
GKM98
, Lemma 15.8]
.

Lemma 3.9
.

For
\xi\in\mathbb{E}^{*}_{T}(M^{T})
, if
\delta^{\prime}(\xi)\neq 0
, then the variety defined by
I(\xi)
satisfies

(3.18)

V(I(\xi))\subset\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{P}\\
\delta^{\mathfrak{m}}(\xi)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{m}.

in the scheme
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{E}_{*}[\![u_{1},\dots,u_{m}]\!])
. ∎

Denote by
M_{1}\subset M
the set of points consisting of the union of
0
and
1
-dimensional orbits of
T
. In particular,
M^{T}\subset M_{1}
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
\mathbb{E}^{*}
be a Morava
K
-theory. If the equivariant cohomology
\mathbb{E}^{*}_{T}(M)
is a free module over
\mathbb{E}^{*}(BT)
, then the sequence

(3.19)

0\longrightarrow\mathbb{E}^{*}_{T}(M)\xrightarrow{j^{*}}\mathbb{E}^{*}_{T}(M^{T})\xrightarrow{\delta}\mathbb{E}^{*+1}_{T}(M_{1},M^{T})

is exact, where
\mathbb{E}^{*+1}_{T}(M_{1},M^{T})
denotes the relative equivariant cohomology of the pair
(M_{1},M^{T})
and
\delta
is the connecting homomorphism.

Proof.

The fact that
\ker(j)^{*}
is trivial follows immediately from Theorem
1.10
because the coefficient ring is a graded field. Because the connecting homomorphism
\delta
factors as the composition

(3.20)

\mathbb{E}^{*}_{T}(M^{T})\xrightarrow{\delta^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}^{*+1}_{T}(M,M^{T})\rightarrow\mathbb{E}^{*+1}_{T}(M_{1},M^{T})

where recall that
\delta^{\prime}
is the connecting homomorphism for the pair
(M,M^{T})
, the long exact sequence implies that
\delta\circ j^{*}=0
. To finish the proof, suppose
\xi\in\mathbb{E}^{*}_{T}(M^{T})
satisfies
\delta(\xi)=0
. To show it lies in the image of
j^{*}
, we need to show that
\delta^{\prime}(\xi)=0
.

Suppose this is not the case. We claim that the ideal
I(\xi)
is a principal. We follow the proof of
[
CS74
, Lemma 2.2]
. Indeed, let
0\neq a\in I(\xi)
be an element which is not divisible by any other element and suppose
b\in I(\xi)
. Choosing a basis
\{x_{i}\}\subset\mathbb{E}^{*}_{T}(M)
, we can find
a_{i},b_{i}\in\mathbb{E}^{*}_{T}(pt)
such that
a\xi=j^{*}(\sum a_{i}x_{i})
and
b\xi=j^{*}(\sum b_{i}x_{i})
using the long exact sequence for the pair
(M,M^{T})
. Then we know that
j^{*}(\sum ba_{i}x_{i})=j^{*}(\sum ab_{i}x_{i})
, so
\sum ba_{i}x_{i}=\sum ab_{i}x_{i}
by the injectivity of
j^{*}
. This shows that for each
i
, we have
ba_{i}=ab_{i}
. As the
a_{i}
must have no common factor, we see that
b
is divisible by
a
, which proves the claim.

By Lemma
3.9
, as
I(\xi)
is principal, there must be one
\mathfrak{m}
which has codimension
1
in
T
. Because of the inclusion
M^{\mathfrak{m}}\subset M_{1}
, the connecting homomorphism
\delta^{K}
factors as

(3.21)

\mathbb{E}^{*}_{T}(M^{T})\xrightarrow{\delta}\mathbb{E}^{*+1}_{T}(M_{1},M^{T})\to\mathbb{E}^{*+1}_{T}(M^{\mathfrak{m}},M^{T}),

which tells us that
\delta(\xi)\neq 0
, leading to a contradiction.
∎

1.11
:

This follows from the proof of

, Theorem 7.2]
, which we reproduce for completeness. Because
\mathbb{E}^{*}_{T}(M)

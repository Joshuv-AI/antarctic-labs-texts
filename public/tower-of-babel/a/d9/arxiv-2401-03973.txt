Riesz completions of some spaces of regular operators
A.W. Wickstead

Abstract
. We describe the Riesz completion (in the sense of van Haandel) of some spaces of regular operators as explicitly identified subspaces of the regular operators into larger range spaces.

1.
Introduction

The space of regular operators between two Archimedean Riesz spaces is only a Riesz space under the operator order in a few special circumstances, the most well known being when the range space is Dedekind complete (see
[
Z
]
for any terms not defined in this paper.) In an effort better to understand partially ordered vector spaces which are not Riesz spaces, van Haandel
[
vH
]
introduced the notion of the
Riesz completion
of a partially ordered vector space. Basically this is the smallest Riesz space into which it may be nicely embedded (precise definitions will be given later.) A very readable account of this and much subsequent related work may be found in
[
KvG
]
. In the case of spaces of regular operators between two Archimedean Riesz spaces,
{\mathcal{L}}^{r}(E,F)
, it would be nice to be able to identify the Riesz completion with some other space of operators. One obvious starting point would be to replace the range space
F
by its Dedekind completion
\hat{F}
and try to find a realization of the Riesz completion of
{\mathcal{L}}^{r}(E,F)
as a certain Riesz subspace of
{\mathcal{L}}^{r}(E,\hat{F})
. Unfortunately, such a naive approach is doomed to failure as all lattice operations in
{\mathcal{L}}^{r}(E,\hat{F})
satisfy the Riesz-Kantorovich formula but there may be existing lattice operations in
{\mathcal{L}}^{r}(E,F)
which do not, see
[
E
]
. It is not difficult to see that this precludes the possibility of realizing the Riesz completion inside
{\mathcal{L}}^{r}(E,\hat{F})
in general. There remains some hope if it is, for some reason, known that any finite suprema that do exist in
{\mathcal{L}}^{r}(E,F)
do satisfy the Riesz-Kantorovich formula.

In this note we carry out such an identification in a particularly simple case, namely when the domain is
\ell^{\infty}_{0}
, sequences of reals which are eventually constant. The inspiration for considering this domain came from Example 5.4.15 of
[
KvG
]
where there is a description of a vector lattice cover of
{\mathcal{L}}^{r}(\ell^{\infty}_{0})
in terms of matrices representing the operators. Our approach avoids such a representation or any similar concepts and deals solely with operators, in what seems to us a more transparent and natural approach. The same example from
[
KvG
]
also prompted us to study the Riesz completion of the space of order continuous operators, in the presence of a mild extra hypothesis on
F
. We conclude the paper by pointing out that many of our results extend to operators from
c
into a uniformly complete Riesz space and posing some questions concerning dropping the assumption of uniform completeness.

The author would like to thank Michael Elliott, Onno van Gaans, Anke Kalauch and the referee for correcting several typographical errors and omissions in previous drafts of this paper.

Theorem 3.5
.

If

is an Archimedean Riesz space then the Riesz completion of

In this proof we will denote

with
S\boldsymbol{1}=y
and
Se_{n}=0
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. It is simple to see that
0\leq S\leq T
and that
S\neq 0
. In the second case, one of the

. Without loss of generality, let us assume that

. Using Proposition

there is
S\in{\mathcal{L}}^{r}(\ell^{\infty}_{0},F)
with

and

4.
Order continuous operators defined on
\ell^{\infty}_{0}

A net
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
in an Archimedean Riesz space
E
is
order convergent
to
x\in E
if there is a net
(a_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
in
E
such that
a_{\lambda}\downarrow 0
and for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
there is
\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma
such that, for all
\gamma\geq\gamma_{0}
,
|x_{\gamma}-x|\leq a_{\lambda}
. In this case we write
x_{\gamma}\xrightarrow{o}x
. Similarly, but rather more simply, a sequence
(x_{n})
in
E
is
order convergent
to
x\in E
if there is a sequence
(a_{n})
in
E
such that
a_{n}\downarrow 0
and
|x_{n}-x|\leq a_{n}
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Again, we write
x_{n}\xrightarrow{o}x
.

If
E
and
F
are Archimedean Riesz spaces then a linear operator
T:E\to F
is
order continuous
if whenever
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in A}
is a net in
E
such that
x_{\alpha}\xrightarrow{o}0
then
Tx_{\alpha}\xrightarrow{o}0\in F
. Similarly,
T:E\to F
is
\sigma
-order continuous
if this holds for sequences. For positive operators, there is a simpler characterization that
T
is order continuous if and only if
Tx_{\alpha}\downarrow 0
whenever
x_{\alpha}\downarrow 0
, with a similar result for
\sigma
-order continuity.

By Theorem 2.1 and Remark 2.2 of
[
AS
]
, every order continuous operator is order bounded, but
\sigma
-order continuous operators need not be order bounded. Accordingly, we write
{\mathcal{L}}^{n}(E,F)
(resp.
{\mathcal{L}}^{c}(E,F)
) for the set of order continuous (resp.
\sigma
-order continuous and order bounded) operators from
E
into
F
. The fundamental properties of order convergence in Riesz spaces make it clear that both
{\mathcal{L}}^{n}(E,F)
and
{\mathcal{L}}^{c}(E,F)
are linear subspaces of
{\mathcal{L}}^{b}(E,F)
. At present we are unable to say much more in general. This section is devoted to investigating order continuous operators defined on
\ell^{\infty}_{0}
, before attempting, with only partial success, to obtain a description of the Riesz completion. Note that in view of Theorem
2.1
order continuous operators defined on
\ell^{\infty}_{0}
, being order bounded, are automatically regular. This does not, however, mean that every order continuous operator is the difference of two positive order continuous operators.

We make a start on describing order continuous operators on
\ell^{\infty}_{0}
with a simple necessary condition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
F
be an Archimedean Riesz space and
T\in{\mathcal{L}}^{r}(\ell^{\infty}_{0},F)_{+}
then the following are equivalent:

(1)

T
is order continuous.

(2)

T
is
\sigma
-order continuous.

(3)

T\boldsymbol{1}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}Te_{k}\downarrow 0\in F
.

Proof.

Certainly (1) implies (2). Assuming (2), take
x_{n}=\boldsymbol{1}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}e_{k}
and observe that
x_{n}\downarrow 0
in
\ell^{\infty}_{0}
so
Tx_{n}\downarrow 0
in
F
, giving us (3) Now assume (3). Let
(x^{\alpha})_{\alpha\in A}
be a positive net in
\ell^{\infty}_{0}
decreasing to 0 and set
x^{\alpha}=(x^{\alpha}_{1},x^{\alpha}_{2},\dots)
. From Proposition
2.4
the fact that
x^{\alpha}\downarrow 0\in c
implies that for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have
x^{\alpha}_{n}\downarrow 0\in\mathbb{R}
and also that
(\lim_{n\to\infty}x^{\alpha}_{n})_{\alpha\in A}\downarrow
(but not necessarily to 0.) Pick any
\alpha_{0}\in A
and let
M=\|x^{\alpha_{0}}\|_{\infty}
. For any
\epsilon>0
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we may find
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n}\geq\alpha_{0}
such that
0\leq x^{\alpha_{k}}_{k}<\epsilon
. Now choose
\beta_{\epsilon,n}\geq\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\dots\alpha_{n}
, so that
0\leq x^{\beta_{\epsilon,n}}_{k}<\epsilon
for
1\leq k\leq n
and
0\leq x^{\beta_{\epsilon,n}}_{m}\leq x^{\alpha_{0}}_{m}\leq M
for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. It follows that

0\leq x^{\beta_{\epsilon,n}}\leq\epsilon\boldsymbol{1}+(0,0,\dots,0,M,M,\dots)=\epsilon\boldsymbol{1}+M(\boldsymbol{1}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}e_{k}).

Hence
0\leq T(x^{\beta_{\epsilon,n}})\leq\epsilon T\boldsymbol{1}+M(T\boldsymbol{1}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}Te_{k})
so that

0\leq\bigwedge_{\alpha\in A}T(x^{\alpha})\leq\bigwedge_{n\in{\mathbb{N}},\epsilon>0}\epsilon T\boldsymbol{1}+M(T\boldsymbol{1}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}Te_{k})=0.

I.e.
Tx^{\alpha}\xrightarrow{o}0
. The extension to an arbitrary net
(x^{\alpha})_{\alpha\in A}
for which
x_{\alpha}\xrightarrow{o}0
is routine.
∎

We deal next with the special case of operators that have a positive part, which is fairly easy to obtain. We need two simple preliminary results.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
F
be an Archimedean Riesz space, and
(x_{n}),(y_{n})
be increasing sequences in
F
with
x_{n}-y_{n}\xrightarrow{o}0
. The three sets
A=\{z\in F:z\geq x_{n}\ \forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
,
B=\{z\in F:z\geq y_{n}\ \forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
and
C=\{z\in F:z\geq x_{n}\vee y_{n}\ \forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
are all equal.

Proof.

Clearly
C\subseteq A,B
. We prove that
A\subseteq C
, with the proof that
B\subseteq C
being virtually identical. Suppose that
z\in A\setminus C
, then
x_{n}\leq z
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, but there is
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
x_{m}\vee y_{m}\not\leq z
, so that
y_{m}\not\leq z
, so that
0\leq(y_{m}-z)^{+}\neq 0
. If
n\geq m
then

(y_{m}-z)^{+}\leq(y_{n}-z)^{+}\leq(y_{n}-x_{n})^{+}\leq|y_{n}-x_{n}|,

which contradicts
x_{n}-y_{n}\xrightarrow{o}0
.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
F
be an Archimedean Riesz space, and
(x_{n}),(y_{n})
be increasing sequences in
F
with
x_{n}\vee y_{n}\uparrow\ell\in F
. If
x_{n}-y_{n}\xrightarrow{o}0
then
x_{n}\uparrow\ell
and
y_{n}\uparrow\ell
.

Proof.

With the notation of the preceding lemma,
C
has a least element,
\ell
, hence so have
A
and
B
, from which the claim is immediate.
∎

Theorem 4.4

Let
F
be an Archimedean Riesz space and let

(1)

is order continuous.

(2)

is
\sigma
-order continuous.

(3)

T^{+}
and

(4)

T\boldsymbol{1}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}Te_{k}\xrightarrow{o}0

Proof.

We already know that (1) implies (2), which as in the proof of Proposition
4.1
implies (4), and also that (4) implies (1) if we also assume that

is positive. It is also clear that (3) implies (2). The collection of operators
T
in
{\mathcal{L}}^{r}(\ell^{\infty}_{0},F)

. As

has a positive part, Theorem 4.4 of
[

]
tells us that

for

, and that

\displaystyle T^{+}\boldsymbol{1}

\displaystyle=\sup\{[\sum_{k=1}^{n}(Te_{k})^{+}]\vee[T\boldsymbol{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(Te_{k})^{-}]:n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{\sum_{k=1}^{n}(Te_{k})^{+}:n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}\vee\sup\{T\boldsymbol{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(Te_{k})^{-}:n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}

with this supremum existing in

. By (4),

[formula]

[T\boldsymbol{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(Te_{k})^{-}]-[\sum_{k=1}^{n}(Te_{k})^{+}]=T\boldsymbol{1}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}Te_{k}\xrightarrow{o}0,

so Corollary
4.3
tells us that

Hence

, and certainly

,

∎

Corollary 4.5

Let

be a Dedekind

-complete Riesz space then

(2)

.

However this band need not be a projection band see

. In Theorem 14 of

Example 4.14
.

{\mathcal{L}}^{n}(\ell^{\infty}_{0})
is a band but not a projection band in
{\mathcal{L}}^{r}(\ell^{\infty}_{0})
.

Proof.

As
\ell^{\infty}_{0}
is almost Dedekind
\sigma
-complete, Corollary
4.13
tells us that
{\mathcal{L}}^{n}(\ell^{\infty}_{0})
is a band in
{\mathcal{L}}^{r}(\ell^{\infty}_{0})
.
Using Propositions
2.3
and
2.4
, there are operators
T
and
T_{m}
in
{\mathcal{L}}^{r}(\ell^{\infty}_{0})_{+}
for
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, with (i)
T\boldsymbol{1}=\boldsymbol{1}
and
Te_{n}=e_{2n}
for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and (ii)
T_{m}\boldsymbol{1}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}e_{2k}
and
T_{m}e_{n}=e_{2n}
if
n\leq m
else 0. By Theorem
4.7
each
T_{m}\in{\mathcal{L}}^{n}(\ell^{\infty}_{0})
. It is routine to check that
0\leq T_{m}\leq T
for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Suppose that
P
were the band projection of
{\mathcal{L}}^{r}(\ell^{\infty}_{0})
onto
{\mathcal{L}}^{n}(\ell^{\infty}_{0})
.
Then for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
0\leq P(T)e_{n}\leq Te_{n}=e_{2n}
so that
P(T)e_{n}=\alpha_{2n}e_{2n}
for
\alpha_{2n}\in[0,1]
. As
\sum_{k=1}^{n}P(T)e_{n}\uparrow P(T)\boldsymbol{1}
, and odd entries in each
\sum_{k=1}^{n}P(T)e_{n}
are all zero, the eventual constant value of

is zero. Hence
(\alpha_{2n})
is eventually zero. On the other hand
\alpha_{2n}e_{2n}=P(T)e_{n}\geq P(T_{n})e_{n}=T_{n}e_{n}=e_{2n}
for all

, which is a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
E
be an Archimedean Riesz space with a strong order unit
u
and
\overline{E}
be its completion for the order unit norm,
\|\cdot\|_{u}
, induced by
u
. A net
(a_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is order convergent to 0 in
E
if and only if it is order convergent to 0 in
\overline{E}
.

Proof.

If
a_{\gamma}\xrightarrow{o}0
in
E
then certainly
a_{\gamma}\xrightarrow{o}0
in
\overline{E}
. Suppose that
a_{\gamma}\xrightarrow{o}0
in
\overline{E}
, so there is a net
(b_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
in
\overline{E}
decreasing to 0 and such that for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
there is
\gamma_{0}
such that
|a_{\gamma}|\leq b_{\lambda}
for all
\gamma\geq\gamma_{0}
. We need to replace the net
(b_{\lambda})
in
\overline{E}
by a net in
E
. For each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\lambda\in\Lambda
, let
b_{\lambda,n}\in E
with
b_{\lambda}\leq b_{\lambda,n}\leq b_{\lambda}+u/n
. Certainly the net
(b_{\lambda,n})_{\Gamma\times{\mathbb{N}}}
lies in
E
and has infimum 0, but may not be decreasing, so we take our net to be indexed by
n
-tuples of arbitrary length from
\Lambda\times{\mathbb{N}}
, with

c_{((\lambda_{1},n_{1}),(\lambda_{2},n_{2}),\dots,(\lambda_{m},n_{m}))}=\bigwedge_{k=1}^{m}b_{\lambda_{k},n_{k}},

which now does decrease to 0. Given an index
((\lambda_{1},n_{1}),\dots,(\lambda_{m},n_{m}))
there are
\gamma_{k}
such that
|a_{\gamma}|\leq b_{\lambda_{k}}
if
\gamma\geq\gamma_{k}
. Now take
\gamma_{0}\geq\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\dots,\gamma_{m}
. If
\gamma\geq\gamma_{0}
then for each
k=1,2,\dots,m
,
|a_{\gamma}|\leq b_{\lambda_{k}}\leq b_{\lambda_{k},n_{k}}
, so that

|a_{\gamma}|\leq\bigwedge_{k=1}^{m}b_{\lambda_{k},n_{k}}=c_{(\lambda_{1},n_{1}),\dots,(\lambda_{m},n_{m}))}.

∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
F
be an almost Dedekind
\sigma
-complete Riesz space and
T\in{\mathcal{L}}^{b}(c,F)
, then the following are equivalent:

(1)

T
is order continuous.

(2)

T
is
\sigma
-order continuous.

(3)

T\boldsymbol{1}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}Te_{k}\xrightarrow{o}0
.

Proof.

It is only (3) implies (1) that we need to prove. As
T
is order bounded
T(c)
is contained in a principal ideal
F_{u}
in
F
. Consider
T
as an operator from
c
into the
\|\cdot\|_{u}
completion of
F_{u}
. It is simple to see that this completion is almost Dedekind
\sigma
-complete if
F
is. If
T\boldsymbol{1}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}Te_{k}\xrightarrow{o}0
in
E
then certainly
T\boldsymbol{1}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}Te_{k}\xrightarrow{o}0
in
\overline{F_{u}}
. As
\overline{F_{u}}
is uniformly complete,
T\in{\mathcal{L}}^{n}(c,\overline{F_{u}})
. If
(x_{\gamma})
is a net in
c
with
x_{\gamma}\xrightarrow{o}0
then
Tx_{\gamma}\xrightarrow{o}0
in
\overline{F_{u}}
as
T\in{\mathcal{L}}^{n}(c,\overline{F_{u}})
. By Lemma
5.2

Tx_{\gamma}\xrightarrow{o}0
in
F_{u}
, and hence in
F
, so that
T\in{\mathcal{L}}^{n}(c,F)
.
∎

Unfortunately, even with this result we do not know that
{\mathcal{L}}^{n}(c,F)
is positively generated so it may not be pre-Riesz.

Question 5.4
.

Is
{\mathcal{L}}^{n}(c,F)
positively generated for all Archimedean Riesz spaces
F
? Recall that order boundedness is part of our definition of an operator being in
{\mathcal{L}}^{n}(c,F)
. If it makes a difference, an answer for the space of regular order continuous operators would be of interest.

One important case of
F
being uniformly complete is when it is a Banach lattice. In this case we have the regular norm on
{\mathcal{L}}^{r}(c,F)
and also the regular norm on our concrete realization of its Riesz completion. An obvious question to ask is now the two are related. First we need to put a norm on
F^{\sigma}
. This is easy, though by no means unique. Indeed, Lemma 3.2.1 of
[
KvG
]
tells us that defining
\|y\|=\inf\{\|x\|:x\in F,\ x\geq|y|\}
, the
maximal
extension, extends the Riesz norm on
F
to a Riesz norm on
F^{\sigma}
. There is, of course, no guarantee that
F^{\sigma}
will be complete for this norm.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
F
be a Banach lattice. On
{\mathcal{L}}^{r}(c,F)^{\rho}=\{T\in{\mathcal{L}}^{r}(c,F^{\sigma}):T(c_{0})\subset F\}
, the regular norm is a Riesz norm that extends the regular norm on
{\mathcal{L}}^{r}(c,F)
. Hence the regular norm on
{\mathcal{L}}^{r}(c,F)
is a pre-Riesz norm (
[
KvG
]
, Definition 3.2.24.)

Proof.

We need to show that if
T\in{\mathcal{L}}^{r}(c,F)
then its regular norm,
\|T\|_{1}
, is the same as the regular norm,
\|T\|_{2}
, when we consider it as an element of
{\mathcal{L}}^{r}(c,F)^{\rho}
. As an element of
{\mathcal{L}}^{r}(c,F)^{\rho}
it has a modulus and
\|T\|_{2}
is just the operator norm of
|T|
. As
c
has a strong order unit,
\boldsymbol{1}
, this operator norm is precisely
\big\||T|\boldsymbol{1}\big\|
. By definition of the extension of the norm on
F
to
F^{\sigma}
,
\big\||T|\boldsymbol{1}\big\|=\inf\{\|x\|:x\in F,x\geq|T|\boldsymbol{1}\}
. For each such
x
, there is
T_{x}\in{\mathcal{L}}^{r}(c,F)
with
T_{x}e_{n}=|T|e_{n}
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
T_{x}\boldsymbol{1}=x
. It is routine to check that
T_{x}\geq|T|\geq\pm T
, so that
\|T\|_{1}
is at most the operator norm of
T_{x}
, which is precisely
\|x\|
. Hence
\|T\|_{1}\leq\|T\|_{2}
. As the reverse inequality is clear, we have
\|T\|_{1}=\|T\|_{2}
.

The final statement follows from
Theorem 3.2.31 of
[
KvG
]
.
∎

If
F
is, in addition, almost Dedekind
\sigma
-complete then we can say more. We simply need the fact that
F^{\sigma}
is a Banach lattice in this case. We know no convenient reference for this explicit result. It seems to be a direct consequence of
[
VL
]
but the author does not have access to it. In view of this, and the difficulty that readers may have in accessing it, we give the short proof here.

Theorem 5.6
.

If
F
is an almost Dedekind
\sigma
-complete Banach lattice then
F^{\sigma}
under the maximal extension norm is norm complete.

Proof.

In order to prove that
F^{\sigma}
is norm complete, it suffices to show that every absolutely convergent series in
F^{\sigma}
is convergent. If
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|x_{n}\|<\infty
then also
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|x_{n}^{\pm}\|<\infty
, so it suffices to consider an absolutely convergent series of positive terms,
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}
. By definition of the maximal norm, we may find
y_{n}\in F
with
0\leq x_{n}\leq y_{n}
and
\|y_{n}\|\leq 2\|x_{n}\|
. Thus
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}y_{n}
is absolutely convergent, and hence convergent in
F
. Thus
\|\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}y_{n}\|\to 0
as
m\to\infty
. We know that
F^{\sigma}
is Dedekind
\sigma
-complete, so
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}\xrightarrow{o}x\in F^{\sigma}
. As the order convergent sum
\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}x_{n}
is smaller than the norm convergent sum
\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}y_{n}
, we have
\|\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}x_{n}\|\to 0
as
m\to\infty
. I.e.

\|\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}x_{n}-x\|\to 0
as
m\to\infty
.
∎

Corollary 5.7

If
F

In particular the last conclusion holds if

Miscellaneous observations

It is worth recording one special example. Recall the classical description of infinite matrices which act on
c

Theorem 6.1

(Kojima-Schur)
.

The infinite matrix
A=(a_{mn})
transforms
c
into itself via matrix multiplication,
x=(x_{n})\mapsto(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{mn}x_{n})_{m=1}^{\infty}
if and only if the following three conditions hold:

(KS1)

\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{mn}|:m\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
is bounded.

(KS2)

\lim_{m\to\infty}a_{mn}
exists for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

(KS3)

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{mn}
exists.

A proof may be found in
[
H
]
, §3.2, Theorem 1. By considering the image of standard basic vectors it is clear that the induced operator is positive if and only if each
a_{mn}\geq 0
, i.e.
A
is a positive matrix.
Perusal of the proof in
[
H
]
makes it clear that condition (KS1) states precisely that
A
maps
c
into
\ell^{\infty}
. It is also evident that (KS2) is equivalent to
A
mapping standard basic vectors into
c
, from which it is a small step to concluding that
A
maps
c_{0}
into
c
. Condition (KS3) is what is needed for
\boldsymbol{1}
to be mapped into
c
. It should be apparent that the operators induced by these matrices are precisely the operators in
{\mathcal{L}}^{\diamond}(c)
and hence, as
c
is almost Dedekind
\sigma
-complete, in
{\mathcal{L}}^{n}(c)
. Therefore, as
c^{\sigma}=\ell_{\infty}
, we have:

Theorem 6.2
.

The Riesz completion of
{\mathcal{L}}^{n}(c)
may be identified with the infinite matrices
A
satisfying (KS1) and (KS2) of Theorem
6.1
.

Finally, let us point out that there will be versions of most of our results for analogues of
\ell^{\infty}_{0}
and

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}
denote the open unit disk of the complex plane and
H(\mathbb{D})
denote the set of all analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
.

For
\alpha>0
, the
\alpha
-Bloch space (also called Bloch type space), denoted by
\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}
, consists of those functions
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
for which

\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}}=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}|f^{\prime}(z)|<\infty.

The classical Bloch space
\mathscr{B}
is just
\mathscr{B}_{1}
.
It is easy to check that
\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}
equipped with the norm
\|f\|=|f(0)|+\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}}
is a Banach space. We refer to
[
20
]
for more information of
\alpha
-Bloch space.

If
0<p<\infty
, the Bergman space
A^{p}
consists of those functions
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
satisfying

\|f\|_{A^{p}}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}dA(z)<\infty,

where
dA
denotes the normalized Lebesgue area measure on
\mathbb{D}
. See
[
6
]
for the theory of Bergman spaces.

Let us recall the definition of Carleson-type measures. If
s>0
and
\mu
is a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{D}
. Then
\mu
will be called an
s
-Carleson measure if there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\mu(S(I))\leq C|I|^{s}

for every set
S(I)
of the form

S(I)=\{z=re^{it}:~e^{it}\in I;~1-\frac{|I|}{2\pi}\leq r\leq 1\},

where
I
is an interval of
\partial\mathbb{D}
and
|I|
denotes the length of
I
. If
\mu
satisfies
\lim_{|I|\to 0}\frac{\mu(S(I))}{|I|^{s}}=0
, we say that
\mu
is a vanishing s-Carleson measure. It is well known
[
11
]
that for
0<p\leq q<\infty
,
\mu
is a
\frac{2q}{p}
-Carleson measure if and only if
there exists a positive constant
C
such that the inequality

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{q}d\mu(z)\right\}^{1/q}\leq C\|f\|_{A^{p}}

(1.1)

holds for all
f\in A^{p}.

Let
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{D}
. For
0\leq\alpha<\infty
and
0<s<\infty
, we say that
\mu
is an
\alpha
-logarithmic
s
-Carleson measure, if there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\frac{\mu(S(I))(\log\frac{2\pi}{|I|})^{\alpha}}{|I|^{s}}\leq C,\quad\mbox{for~all~interval}~I\subset\partial\mathbb{D}.

If
\mu(S(I))(\log\frac{2\pi}{|I|})^{\alpha}=o(|I|^{s})
, as
|I|\to 0
, we say that
\mu
is a vanishing
\alpha
-logarithmic
s
-Carleson measure (cf.
[
13
,
19
]
).

A positive Borel measure
\mu
on
[0,1)
can be seen as a Borel measure on
\mathbb{D}
by identifying it with the measure
\widetilde{\mu}
defined as

\widetilde{\mu}(A)=\mu(A\cap[0,1)),

\mbox{for~any~Borel~subset}~A~\mbox{of}~\mathbb{D}.

In this way, we say that a positive Borel measure
\mu
on [0,1) is an
s
-Carleson measure if and only if there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\mu([t,1))\leq C(1-t)^{s},\quad 0\leq t<1.

Also, we have similar statements for the other cases.

If
\mu
is a positive Borel measure on
[0,1)
, for
\alpha>0
, we define
\mathcal{H}_{\mu,\alpha}=(\mu_{n,k,\alpha})_{n,k\geq 0}
to be the Hankel matrix with entries
\mu_{n,k,\alpha}=\displaystyle\int_{[0,1)}\frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha)}{n!\Gamma(\alpha)}t^{n+k}d\mu(t)
. The matrix
\mathcal{H}_{\mu,\alpha}
can be viewed as an operator on
H(\mathbb{D})
by its action on the Taylor coefficients:

a_{n}\to\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k,\alpha}a_{k},\quad n=0,1,2,\cdots.

To be precise, if
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\in H(\mathbb{D})
, we define the Hankel operator
\mathcal{H}_{\mu,\alpha}
as

\mathcal{H}_{\mu,\alpha}(f)(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k,\alpha}a_{k}\big)z^{n},

(1.2)

whenever the right hand side makes sense and defines an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
. The operators
\mathcal{H}_{\mu,1}
have been extensively studied in
[
2
,
3
,
8
,
10
,
12
]
.
In this case, if we let
\mu
be the Lebesgue measure on
[0,1)
, we can find that
\mathcal{H}_{\mu,1}
is just the classical Hilbert matrix
\mathcal{H}=\big((n+k+1)^{-1}\big)
, which induces the classical Hilbert operator (see
[
1
,
4
,
7
]
for more details). For the case
\alpha=2
, we have studied the operator in
[
16
,
17
]
and we call
\mathcal{H}_{\mu,2}
the Derivative-Hilbert operator. In this paper, we also call
\mathcal{H}_{\mu,\alpha}

(\alpha>0)
the generalized Hilbert operator.

Galanopoulos and Pel
\acute{\text{a}}
ez proved in
[
8
]
that the operator
\mathcal{H}_{\mu,1}
is well defined in
H^{1}
when
\mu
is a Carleson measure. See
[
5
]
for more details on Hardy spaces. Also, they obtained the following integral representation

\mathcal{H}_{\mu,1}(f)(z)=\int_{[0,1)}\frac{f(t)}{1-tz}d\mu(t),\quad z\in\mathbb{D},~\mbox{for~all}~f\in H^{1}.

In
[
16
]
and
[
17
]
, we obtained the following integral representation

\mathcal{H}_{\mu,2}f(z)=\int_{[0,1)}\frac{f(t)}{(1-tz)^{2}}d\mu(t),~z\in\mathbb{D},

for all
f\in A^{p}
(
0<p<\infty
) and for all
f\in\mathscr{B}
respectively.

For
\alpha>0
, we define the generalized integral-Hilbert operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\alpha}(f)(z)=\int_{[0,1)}\frac{f(t)}{(1-tz)^{\alpha}}d\mu(t),

(1.3)

whenever the right hand side makes sense and defines an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
. Similar to the cases
\alpha=1
and
\alpha=2
,in this paper, we can also obtain the operators
\mathcal{H}_{\mu,\alpha}
and

are closely related for all

In this article we characterize those measures

for which

(

) is a bounded (resp., compact) operator from Bloch type space

(

) into

. We also give a necessary condition for general cases

As usual, throughout this paper,

denotes a positive constant which depends only on the displayed parameters but not necessarily the same from one occurrence to the next.

Proposition 2.2

Suppose

,

into

.

(ii)

\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\alpha}
is a compact operator from
\mathscr{B}_{\beta}
into

(iii)

The measure

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) is trivial.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
Assume that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\overline{I_{\mu,\alpha}(f_{n})(rz)}g(rz)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha-2}dA(z)=

\displaystyle\sup_{0<t<1}|f_{n}(t)|\int_{[0,1)}|g(r^{2}t)|d\mu(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sup_{0<t<1}|f_{n}(t)|\|g\|_{A^{1}},

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\overline{I_{\mu,\alpha}(f_{n})(rz)}g(rz)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha-2}dA(z)=0

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\lim_{r\rightarrow 1}\left|\int_{\mathbb{D}}\overline{I_{\mu,\alpha}(f_{n})(rz)}g\left(rz\right)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha-2}dA(z)\right|\right)=0,\quad\text{ for all }g\in A^{1}.

into

.

Suppose now that

is a compact operator from
\mathscr{B}
into
\mathscr{B}_{\alpha-1}
. Let

be any sequence with

. We set

A calculation shows that

,
\sup_{n\geq 1}\|f_{a_{n}}\|_{\mathscr{B}}<\infty
and

is bounded uniformly sequence in

\mathscr{B}
which converges to
0
uniformly on every compact subset of
\mathbb{D}
. Then
\{\mathcal{I}_{\mu_{2}}(f_{a_{n}})\}
converges to 0 in
\mathscr{B}_{\alpha-1}
. We obtain that for every

,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{[0,1)}\overline{f_{a_{n}}(t)}g_{a_{n}}(r^{2}t)d\mu(t)\\
=&\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\overline{I_{\mu,\alpha}(f_{a_{n}})(rz)}g_{a_{n}}(rz)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha-2}dA(z)=0,\quad 0<r<1.\end{split}

(2.7)

We also set

g_{a_{n}}(z)=\left(\frac{1-a_{n}^{2}}{(1-{a_{n}}z)^{2}}\right)^{2}\in A^{1}.

For

we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1)}\overline{f_{a_{n}}(t)}g_{a_{n}}(r^{2}t)d\mu(t)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{a_{n}}^{1}\left(\frac{1-a_{n}^{2}}{(1-{a_{n}}r^{2}t)^{2}}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{\log\frac{2}{1-a_{n}^{2}}}\left(\log\frac{2}{1-a_{n}t}\right)^{2}d\mu(t)

\displaystyle C\frac{\log\frac{2}{1-a_{n}^{2}}}{\left(1-a_{n}^{2}\right)^{2}}\mu([a_{n},1)).

Letting

and bearing in mind that
\{a_{n}\}\subset(0,1)

which is equivalent to saying that
\mu
is a vanishing 1-logarithmic 2-Carleson measure. This completes the proof.

is a bounded operator acting on Bloch type spaces for general cases. Then we characterize the measures

\mathcal{H}_{\mu,\alpha}
(

\mathscr{B}_{\alpha-1}

\mu
the operator
\mathcal{H}_{\mu,\alpha}
is well defined on the Bergman space

(

[

Proposition 3.1

Suppose
0<p<\infty
,
\alpha>0
and let
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
[0,1)
. Then the power series in
(\ref{eq2})
defines an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
for every
f\in A^{p}
in any of the three following cases.

(i)

\mu
is a
2/p
-Carleson measure if
0<p\leq 1
.

(ii)

\mu
is a
\left(\frac{2-(p-1)^{2}}{p}\right)
-Carleson measure if
1\leq p\leq 2
.

(iii)

\mu
is a
1/p
-Carleson measure if
2\leq p<\infty
.

Furthermore, in such cases we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mu,\alpha}(f)(z)=\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\alpha}(f)(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{D},~f\in A^{p}.

(3.1)

(i) Suppose that
\mu
is a
2/p
-Carleson measure. By Proposition 1 in
[
3
]
, we obtain that there exists a positive constant
C
which is independent of
n,~k
such that

|\mu_{n,k,\alpha}|\leq C\frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha)}{n!\Gamma(\alpha)}\frac{1}{(k+1)^{2/p}}.

This and Theorem 4 in
[
6
, p.85]
imply that, for every
n
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left|\mu_{n,k,\alpha}\right|\left|a_{k}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha)}{n!\Gamma(\alpha)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left|a_{k}\right|^{p}\left|a_{k}\right|^{1-p}}{(k+1)^{2/p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha)}{n!\Gamma(\alpha)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left|a_{k}\right|^{p}\left|k+1\right|^{\frac{(2-p)(1-p)}{p}}}{(k+1)^{2/p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha)}{n!\Gamma(\alpha)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+1)^{p-3}\left|a_{k}\right|^{p}.

This converges by Theorem 3 in
[
6
, p.83]
. So the power series in (
1.2
) is well defined analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
and

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k,\alpha}a_{k}=\int_{[0,1)}\frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha)}{n!\Gamma(\alpha)}t^{n}f(t)d\mu(t).

On the other hand, notice that for each
z\in\mathbb{D}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\int_{[0,1)}\frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha)}{n!\Gamma(\alpha)}t^{n}|f(t)|d\mu(t))|z|^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}|a_{k}|\right)|z|^{n}\leq C\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left|a_{k}\right|^{p}(k+1)^{p-3}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha)}{n!\Gamma(\alpha)}|z|^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{(1-|z|)^{\alpha}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left|a_{k}\right|^{p}(k+1)^{p-3}<\infty.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mu,\alpha}(f)(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{[0,1)}\frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha)}{n!\Gamma(\alpha)}t^{n}f(t)d\mu(t)\right)z^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1)}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha)}{n!\Gamma(\alpha)}(tz)^{n}f(t)d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1)}\frac{f(t)}{(1-tz)^{\alpha}}d\mu(t)=\mathcal{I}_{\mu,\alpha}(f)(z),

for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
f\in A^{p}
.
The proof of (ii) and (iii) is analogous to the proof of (i). We omit the details.

Lemma 3.1

Proof of Proposition

.
Since

-Carleson measure, by Proposition 1 in

Notice that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=2^{n}+1}^{2^{n+1}}\big|\frac{\mu_{n,k,\alpha}}{k^{1-\beta}}\big|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(n^{\alpha-1}\sum_{k=2^{n}+1}^{2^{n+1}}\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\leq C\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{n^{(\alpha-1)/2}}{2^{n/2}}<\infty.

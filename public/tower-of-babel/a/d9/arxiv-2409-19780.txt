Abstract
. Assuming the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis and the Generalized Ramanujan Conjecture, we determine the order of the 2 ​ ( k 1 , … , k r ) 2(k_{1},\dots,k_{r}) th moment of a product of distinct irreducible L L -functions on the critical line. As a consequence, we obtain conditional information about the independence of these L L -functions in the large deviations regime. We also obtain sharp moment bounds for Hurwitz zeta functions with rational parameter, and a certain family of Dedekind zeta functions.

1.1.
The statistical behaviour of
L

There are many reasons to be interested in the moments of products of

The study of the statistical behaviour of

1.3.
Proof strategy

The strategy to prove Theorem
1
is due to Heath–Brown
[
9
]
and dates back to the 1980’s. He used this method to prove sharp lower bounds for the
2k
th moments of the Riemann zeta function
5
5

5

He also used it to prove some sharp upper bounds.
. The idea is roughly as follows. For
\text{Re}(s)>1
, we have

L^{k_{1}}(s,\pi_{1})\cdots L^{k_{r}}(s,\pi_{r})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbf{h}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(n)}{n^{s}},

for some coefficients
\mathbf{h}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(n)
that can be explicitly written down. A trivial application of the triangle inequality tells us that

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{T}\left|\sum_{n\leq N}\frac{\mathbf{h}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(n)}{n^{1/2+it}}\right|^{2}\,\text{d}t

\displaystyle\ll\int_{1}^{T}|L(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\pi_{1})|^{2k_{1}}\cdots|L(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\pi_{r})|^{2k_{r}}\,\text{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{1}^{T}\left|L^{k_{1}}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\pi_{1})\cdots L^{k_{r}}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\pi_{r})-\sum_{n\leq N}\frac{\mathbf{h}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(n)}{n^{1/2+it}}\right|^{2}\,\textup{d}t.

On the right hand side of the inequality, the first term is the quantity we want to bound from below. On the left hand side, we have something that we can compute using the Montgomery–Vaughan mean value theorem. Thus we are left with estimating the last term on the right hand side of the inequality. Using certain convexity theorems we are able to reduce this computation to the same integral but evaluated in
\tfrac{5}{4}+it
instead of
\tfrac{1}{2}+it
. On
\text{Re}(s)=5/4
, the Dirichlet series definition for
L^{k_{1}}\cdots L^{k_{r}}
is valid, and so the computation of the integral quickly reduces to a mean-value computation that again can be computed by using Montgomery–Vaughan. The main point of this last maneuvre is that the integrand on
\text{Re}(s)=5/4
is given by the tail of an absolutely convergent Dirichlet series, and its mean value squared is clearly very small. Our execution of this method is more or less identical to that of Heath-Brown.

The strategy for proving Theorem
2
is more recent, and is an accumulation of methods of Soundararajan, Radziwiłł  and Harper, which we quickly survey. The first contribution due to Soundararajan appears in
[
22
]
. There he shows on RH that
\log|\zeta(1/2+it)|
can be bounded above by a sum over primes, with no contribution from a sum over non-trivial zeroes. Specifically, this lets us bound
|\zeta(1/2+it)|
by an Euler product of suitable length. Chandee
[
6
]
generalized Soundararajan’s result to more general
L
-functions. Her inequality will be our point of departure for proving Theorem
2
.

The next idea we will utilize is due to Radziwiłł  and first appeared in
[
23
]
. Roughly speaking, it allows us to compute moments of Euler products
\mathcal{P}
on
[T,2T]
, under the assumption that we can restrict to certain subsets of
[T,2T]
where
\mathcal{P}
is of
typical
size in a statistical sense (not deviating too much from its mean). Let us decompose
[T,2T]
as
\mathscr{G}\cup\mathscr{B}
, where
\mathscr{G}
is the set where
|\mathcal{P}|
is of typical size, and
\mathscr{B}
is the set where
|\mathcal{P}|
is large. To compute an upper bound for
I_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{r})
we first apply Chandee’s inequality to go from
\prod_{j=1}^{r}L(\frac{1}{2}+it,\pi_{j})^{2k_{j}}
to an Euler product
\mathcal{P}
. The integral over
\mathscr{G}
quickly reduces to a mean-value computation that is admissible for Montgomery–Vaughan. The remaining set
\mathscr{B}
will be so small in measure that the integral over
\mathscr{B}
will not contribute anything more than the main term coming from the integral over
\mathscr{G}
. The main point of this method is that when we integrate over
\mathscr{G}
, instead of the whole range
[T,2T]
, we can let the length of
\mathcal{P}
be significantly longer. We refer to
[
10
, p. 3–4]
for an illustrative example of this idea.

The idea described in the paragraph above will almost give sharp upper bounds. To get our desired sharp upper bounds we have to push the length of the Euler product to be slightly longer. The final idea we need appears in both
[
8
]
and
[
25
]
. It revolves around splitting up our Euler product into many smaller Euler products whose variance (i.e. deviation from the mean) gets progressively smaller. Applying the idea of Radziwiłł  locally to each of the Euler products, we can choose the original Euler product to be sufficiently long. Harper successfully applied this approach to exhibit conditional sharp upper bounds for the
2k
th moments of
\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)
, and several authors have used the recipe outlined above to establish sharp moments bounds since.

We find that many of the ideas mentioned above are applicable to our setting, without any significant modification. There are several ways to execute Harper’s method in our setting, and we will use one that is slightly different from Harper’s original approach, but aligns more with the approach of Heap
[
10
]
.

The reader may have observed that the result

I_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{r})\ll T(\log T)^{k_{1}^{2}+\dots+k_{r}^{2}+\varepsilon}

of Milinovich and Turnage-Butterbaugh does not assume GRC, but Hypothesis H instead, which is a weaker assumption. We believe that this could be done in this setting as well, at the cost of more unpleasant combinatorics in our proof.

It remains an interesting question how much of our work that could be recovered unconditionally, especially the lower moment bound, because the analogous bounds for
\zeta(s)

L
-functions. A natural starting point for such an investigation could be the moments

\int_{1}^{T}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2k_{1}}|L(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\chi)|^{2k_{2}}\,\textup{d}t,

with
0<k_{1},k_{2}\leq 2
.

Another interesting question to ask is to what extent Theorem
1
and
2
can be made uniform in
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}
. This would be desireable as uniformity in certain ranges of
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}
, would let us vary

in some window in Theorem
3
, instead of keeping them fixed.

For the proof of Theorem

We choose a branch of the logarithm so that

\log L(s,\pi_{i})=\sum_{p}\sum_{j=1}^{m_{i}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha_{\pi_{i}}(j,p)^{\ell}}{\ell p^{\ell s}}.

This, in turn defines a branch of

L^{k_{1}}(s,\pi_{1})\cdots L^{k_{r}}(s,\pi_{r})=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{p}\prod_{j=1}^{m_{i}}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{\pi_{i}}(j,p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-k_{i}}.

For

(1-z)^{-k}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(k+\ell)}{\Gamma(k)\ell!}z^{\ell}.

Observe that by (

\left|\frac{\alpha_{\pi_{i}}(j,p)}{p^{s}}\right|\leq p^{1/2-\frac{1}{m^{2}+1}-\text{Re}(s)}<1

and extend multiplicatively. Furthermore we define

Again we notice that

.
The formulas above yield the following expression

\displaystyle L^{k_{1}}(s,\pi_{1})\cdots L^{k_{r}}(s,\pi_{r})

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{p}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{h_{k_{i}}(p^{\ell})}{p^{\ell s}}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{h_{k_{i}}(n)}{n^{s}}

for

Lemma 6
.

Let
\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{r}
be distinct irreducible cuspidal automorphic representations of
\textup{GL}(m_{1}),\dots,\textup{GL}(m_{r})
over
\mathbb{Q}
respectively, with unitary central character. Let

|\mathbf{h}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(p^{\ell})|\ll p^{\ell\varepsilon},

for any

\sum_{p,\ell\geq 2}\frac{|\mathbf{h}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(p^{\ell})|^{2}}{p^{\ell}}<\infty.

Proof.

This follows from standard divisor coefficient facts (see e.g.
[

\displaystyle\mathbf{h}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(p^{\ell})

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1}+\dots+\ell_{r}=\ell,\\
\ell_{j}\geq 0\end{subarray}}|h_{k_{1}}(p^{\ell_{1}})|\cdots|h_{k_{r}}(p^{\ell_{r}})|

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1}+\dots+\ell_{r}=\ell,\\
\ell_{j}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\Big(\prod_{\eta=1}^{r}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{1}+\dots+r_{m_{\eta}}=\ell_{\eta},\\

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1}+\dots+\ell_{r}=\ell,\\
\ell_{j}\geq 0\end{subarray}}d_{k_{1}m_{1}}(p^{\ell_{1}})\cdots d_{k_{r}m_{r}}(p^{\ell_{r}})

Lemma 7
.

Let
\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{r}
be distinct irreducible cuspidal automorphic representations of
\textup{GL}(m_{1}),\dots,\textup{GL}(m_{r})
over
\mathbb{Q}
respectively, with unitary central character. Let
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}>0
. Then there exists a constant
C_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}>0
such that

(i)

\displaystyle\kern 5.0pt\sum_{n\leq N}\frac{|\mathbf{h}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(n)|^{2}}{n^{2\sigma}}\asymp\left(\sigma-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-(k_{1}^{2}+\dots+k_{r}^{2})}\kern 5.0pt\textup{uniformly for }\frac{1}{2}+\frac{C_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}{\log N}\leq\sigma\leq 1,

(ii)

\displaystyle\kern 5.0pt\sum_{n\leq N}\frac{|\mathbf{h}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(n)|^{2}}{n}\asymp(\log N)^{k_{1}^{2}+\dots+k_{r}^{2}}.

Proof.

The second point follows readily from the first, so we will only prove the first point. The statement is clear when
\sigma

by an absolute constant, because the sum is bounded in that case. Thus it suffices to consider the case when
\sigma
is close to
\frac{1}{2}
. Let us by

, where
p

E_{1}(x)=-\gamma-\log x-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-x)^{k}}{k!k}

for

\displaystyle\sum_{p}\frac{|\mathbf{h}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(p)|^{2}}{p^{2\sigma}}

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))(k_{1}^{2}+\dots+k_{r}^{2})\int_{3}^{\infty}\frac{\textup{d}t}{t^{2\sigma}\log t}

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))(k_{1}^{2}+\dots+k_{r}^{2})E_{1}((2\sigma-1)\log 3)

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))\log\left((\sigma-\tfrac{1}{2})^{-(k_{1}^{2}+\dots+k_{r}^{2})}\right).

Thus

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq Y}\frac{|\mathbf{h}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(p)|^{2}}{p^{2\sigma}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p}\frac{|\mathbf{h}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(p)|^{2}}{p^{2\sigma}}-\sum_{p>Y}\frac{|\mathbf{h}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(p)|^{2}}{p^{2\sigma}}

\displaystyle=\log\left((\sigma-\tfrac{1}{2})^{-(k_{1}^{2}+\dots+k_{r}^{2})}\right)+O\left((k_{1}^{2}+\dots+k_{r}^{2})E_{1}((2\sigma-1)\log Y)\right),

for any

. We want to ensure that the error term here is

, and for that we have to ensure that

\sigma\geq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{C}{(k_{1}^{2}+\dots+k_{r}^{2})\log Y}
. The analysis above does not impose any condition on this constant - only that it has to be positive and absolute. We will choose it towards the end of the proof. Thus we conclude under this condition that

(11)

\sum_{p\leq Y}\frac{|\mathbf{h}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(p)|^{2}}{p^{2\sigma}}=\log\left((\sigma-\tfrac{1}{2})^{-(k_{1}^{2}+\dots+k_{r}^{2})}\right)+O\left(1\right).

We first deal with the upper bound implicit in (i). Bounding by

\sum_{n\leq N}\frac{|\mathbf{h}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(n)|^{2}}{n^{2\sigma}}\leq\sum_{n\in S(N)}\frac{|\mathbf{h}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(n)|^{2}}{n^{2\sigma}}=\prod_{p\leq N}\left(1+\frac{|\mathbf{h}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(p)|^{2}}{p^{2\sigma}}+\frac{|\mathbf{h}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(p^{2})|^{2}}{p^{4\sigma}}+\dots\right).

Because

assuming

Let us now turn to the lower bound. To this end, let

Proposition 8

\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(\alpha+\varepsilon+it)|^{2}\,\textup{d}t

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(\gamma+it)|^{2}\,\textup{d}t\leq\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(\alpha+it)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{\beta-\gamma}{\beta-\alpha}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(\beta+it)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-\alpha}{\beta-\alpha}}.

Proof.

Let

[

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(\gamma+\varepsilon+it)|^{2}\,\textup{d}t\leq\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(\alpha+\varepsilon+it)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{(\beta-\varepsilon)-(\gamma+\varepsilon)}{(\beta-\varepsilon)-(\alpha+\varepsilon)}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(\beta-\varepsilon+it)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{(\gamma+\varepsilon)-(\alpha+\varepsilon)}{(\beta-\varepsilon)-(\alpha+\varepsilon)}}.

We now let

. Choosing
\varepsilon=(k_{1}m_{1}+\dots+k_{r}m_{r})^{-1}
, we conclude that

g_{N}(s)\ll T+t

for
|s-1|\geq\frac{1}{10}
(to stay away from a possible pole) and
\frac{1}{2}\leq\sigma\leq\frac{5}{4}
. Using these bounds we have

\int_{-\infty}^{\tau/2}\left|g_{N}(7/8+it)e^{\frac{1}{2}(7/8+i(t-\tau))^{2}}\right|^{2}\,\text{d}t\ll T^{2}e^{-C\tau^{2}}

for some positive
C>0
and similarly

\int_{3\tau/2}^{\infty}\left|g_{N}(7/8+it)e^{\frac{1}{2}(7/8+i(t-\tau))^{2}}\right|^{2}\,\text{d}t\ll T^{2}e^{-C\tau^{2}}.

Thus

(16)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|g_{N}(7/8+it)e^{\frac{1}{2}(7/8+i(t-\tau))^{2}}\right|^{2}\,\text{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\tau/2}^{3\tau/2}\left|g_{N}(7/8+it)e^{\frac{1}{2}(7/8+i(t-\tau))^{2}}\right|^{2}\,\text{d}t+O\left(T^{2}e^{-C\tau^{2}}\right).

Let us write
f(s)=g_{N}(s)e^{\frac{1}{2}(s-i\tau)^{2}}
for convenience from now on. Let furthermore

F(z)=f\left(z+\frac{7}{8}+i\tau\right)

and

z_{0}=\frac{3}{8}+\frac{1}{2}i\tau.

With this notation we have the following lemma originally due to Gabriel
[
7
]
in a more general setting.

Lemma 11
.

Let
F(z)
be the function displayed above. Let
R
be the rectangle whose vertices are
z_{0},\overline{z_{0}},-z_{0}
and
-\overline{z_{0}}
. Let
L
be the line connecting
-i\textup{Im}(z_{0})
and
i\textup{Im}(z_{0})
; let
P_{1}
consist of the three line segments connecting
-i\textup{Im}(z_{0}),\overline{z_{0}},z_{0}
and
i\textup{Im}(z_{0})
respectively; and let finally
P_{2}
be the mirror image of
P_{1}
about
L
. Then

\int_{L}|F(z)|^{2}\,|\textup{d}z|\leq\left(\int_{P_{1}}|F(z)|^{2}\,|\textup{d}z|\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{P_{2}}|F(z)|^{2}\,|\textup{d}z|\right)^{1/2}.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be small. Let
R_{\varepsilon}
be
R
but with
z_{0}
interchanged with
z_{0}-\varepsilon
. We call the new
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
for
P_{1,\varepsilon}
and
P_{2,\varepsilon}
respectively. Since we are assuming GRH,
F
is single-valued and an analytic function in
R_{\varepsilon}
. This also has to be the case for
\overline{F(-\overline{z})}
. Observe that on
L
,
\overline{F(z)}=\overline{F(-\overline{z})}
. Cauchy’s theorem then implies

\int_{L}|F(z)|^{2}\,|\text{d}z|=\left|\int_{P_{1,\varepsilon}}F(z)\overline{F(-\overline{z})}\,\text{d}z\right|\leq\int_{P_{1,\varepsilon}}|F(z)||F(-\overline{z})|\,|\text{d}z|.

By Cauchy–Schwarz and the definition of
P_{2}
we have

\int_{P_{1,\varepsilon}}|F(z)||F(-\overline{z})|\,|\text{d}z|\leq\left(\int_{P_{1,\varepsilon}}|F(z)|^{2}\,|\text{d}z|\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{P_{2,\varepsilon}}|F(z)|^{2}\,|\text{d}z|\right)^{1/2}.

Now we let
\varepsilon\to 0
, which by continuity of the integral as a function of
\text{Re}(z)
, implies the theorem.
∎

With the notation from the proposition we have

\int_{L}|F(z)|^{2}\,|\text{d}z|=\int_{\tau/2}^{3\tau/2}|f(\tfrac{7}{8}+it)|^{2}\,\text{d}t.

Furthermore

\int_{P_{1}}|F(z)|^{2}\,|\text{d}z|=\int_{\tau/2}^{3\tau/2}|f(\tfrac{5}{4}+it)|^{2}\,\text{d}t+\int_{\tfrac{7}{8}}^{\tfrac{5}{4}}\left(|f(\eta+\tfrac{1}{2}i\tau)|^{2}+|f(\eta+\tfrac{3}{2}i\tau)|^{2}\right)\,\text{d}\eta.

Using the same bound as earlier,
g_{N}(s)\ll T+t
, we bound the last integral by

\ll T^{2}e^{-C\tau^{2}}.

Thus

\int_{P_{1}}|F(z)|^{2}\,|\text{d}z|=\int_{\tau/2}^{3\tau/2}|f(\tfrac{5}{4}+it)|^{2}\,\text{d}t+O(T^{2}e^{-C\tau^{2}}).

By the exact same reasoning,

\int_{P_{2}}|F(z)|^{2}\,|\text{d}z|=\int_{\tau/2}^{3\tau/2}|f(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}\,\text{d}t+O(T^{2}e^{-c\tau^{2}}).

Thus our application of Proposition
11
reads

(17)

\displaystyle\int_{\tau/2}^{3\tau/2}|f(\tfrac{7}{8}+it)|^{2}\,\text{d}t

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\tau/2}^{3\tau/2}|f(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}\,\text{d}t\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\tau/2}^{3\tau/2}|f(\tfrac{5}{4}+it)|^{2}\,\text{d}t\right)^{1/2}+O(T^{2}e^{-C\tau^{2}}).

Putting (
16
) together with (
17
), we get

(18)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(\tfrac{7}{8}+it)|^{2}\,\text{d}t

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\tau/2}^{3\tau/2}|f(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}\,\text{d}t\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\tau/2}^{3\tau/2}|f(\tfrac{5}{4}+it)|^{2}\,\text{d}t\right)^{1/2}+O(T^{2}e^{-C\tau^{2}}).

Going back to (
15
), and putting (
18
) into this equation we have (as long as
1/2\leq\sigma\leq 7/8
) that

(19)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(\sigma+it)|^{2}\,\text{d}t

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}\,\text{d}t\right)^{\frac{7-8\sigma}{3}}\left(\left(\int_{\tau/2}^{3\tau/2}|f(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}\,\text{d}t\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\tau/2}^{3\tau/2}|f(\tfrac{5}{4}+it)|^{2}\,\text{d}t\right)^{1/2}\right)^{\frac{8\sigma-4}{3}}

\displaystyle+\left(T^{2}e^{-C\tau^{2}}\right)^{\frac{8\sigma-4}{3}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}\,\text{d}t\right)^{\frac{7-8\sigma}{3}}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}\,\text{d}t\right)^{\frac{5-4\sigma}{3}}\left(\int_{\tau/2}^{3\tau/2}|f(\tfrac{5}{4}+it)|^{2}\,\text{d}t\right)^{\frac{4\sigma-2}{3}}

\displaystyle+\left(T^{2}e^{-C\tau^{2}}\right)^{\frac{8\sigma-4}{3}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}\,\text{d}t\right)^{\frac{7-8\sigma}{3}}.

By Hölder’s inequality in the form
\int|f|^{\alpha}|g|^{\beta}\leq(\int|f|)^{\alpha}(\int|g|)^{\beta}
for
\alpha+\beta=1
, we have, since
\frac{5-4\sigma}{3}+\frac{4\sigma-2}{3}=1
, that

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}\,\text{d}t\right)^{\frac{5-4\sigma}{3}}\left(\int_{\tau/2}^{3\tau/2}|f(\tfrac{5}{4}+it)|^{2}\,\text{d}t\right)^{\frac{4\sigma-2}{3}}\,\text{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{T}^{2T}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}\,\text{d}t\text{d}\tau\right)^{\frac{5-4\sigma}{3}}\left(\int_{T}^{2T}\int_{\tau/2}^{3\tau/2}|f(\tfrac{5}{4}+it)|^{2}\,\text{d}t\text{d}\tau\right)^{\frac{4\sigma-2}{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{T}^{2T}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|g_{N}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}e^{-(t-\tau)^{2}}\,\text{d}t\text{d}\tau\right)^{\frac{5-4\sigma}{3}}\left(\int_{T}^{2T}\int_{\tau/2}^{3\tau/2}|g_{N}(\tfrac{5}{4}+it)|^{2}e^{-(t-\tau)^{2}}\,\text{d}t\text{d}\tau\right)^{\frac{4\sigma-2}{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K(1/2,T)^{\frac{5-4\sigma}{3}}\left(\int_{T}^{2T}\int_{\tau/2}^{3\tau/2}|g_{N}(\tfrac{5}{4}+it)|^{2}e^{-(t-\tau)^{2}}\,\text{d}t\text{d}\tau\right)^{\frac{4\sigma-2}{3}},

upon changing the order of integration in the end. Similary, as
\frac{7-8\sigma}{3}+\frac{8\sigma-4}{3}=1
, Hölder again gives

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}\left(T^{2}e^{-C\tau^{2}}\right)^{\frac{8\sigma-4}{3}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}\,\text{d}t\right)^{\frac{7-8\sigma}{3}}\,\text{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(T^{2}e^{-CT^{2}}\right)^{\frac{8\sigma-4}{3}}K(1/2,T)^{\frac{7-8\sigma}{3}}.

Inserting these two applications of Hölder’s inequality in (
19
) gives

(20)

\displaystyle K(\sigma,T)

\displaystyle\ll K(1/2,T)^{\frac{5-4\sigma}{3}}\left(\int_{T}^{2T}\int_{\tau/2}^{3\tau/2}|g_{N}(\tfrac{5}{4}+it)|^{2}e^{-(t-\tau)^{2}}\,\text{d}t\text{d}\tau\right)^{\frac{4\sigma-2}{3}}

\displaystyle+\left(T^{2}e^{-CT^{2}}\right)^{\frac{8\sigma-4}{3}}K(1/2,T)^{\frac{7-8\sigma}{3}}.

To estimate the last factor in the first term in the displayed equation above, we use Montgomery–Vaughan and the Dirichlet series definition of
L^{k_{1}}(s,\pi_{1})\cdots L^{k_{r}}(s,\pi_{r})
which is valid
\text{Re}(s)>1
:

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}\int_{\tau/2}^{3\tau/2}|g_{N}(\tfrac{5}{4}+it)|^{2}e^{-(t-\tau)^{2}}\,\text{d}t\text{d}\tau

\displaystyle\ll\int_{T/2}^{3T/2}|g_{N}(\tfrac{5}{4}+it)|^{2}\,\text{d}t

\displaystyle\ll T\sum_{n>N}\frac{|\mathbf{h}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(n)|^{2}}{n^{5/2}}\ll TN^{\varepsilon-3/2},

where we again have used in the end that
\mathbf{h}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}(n)\ll n^{\varepsilon}
. In light of this, (
20
) reads

\displaystyle K(\sigma,T)

\displaystyle\ll K(1/2,T)^{\frac{5-4\sigma}{3}}T^{\frac{4\sigma-2}{3}}N^{(\varepsilon-\frac{3}{2})\frac{4\sigma-2}{3}}+\left(T^{2}e^{-CT^{2}}\right)^{\frac{8\sigma-4}{3}}K(1/2,T)^{\frac{7-8\sigma}{3}}

\displaystyle\ll K(1/2,T)^{\frac{5-4\sigma}{3}}T^{\frac{4\sigma-2}{3}}N^{(\varepsilon-\frac{3}{2})\frac{4\sigma-2}{3}}+K(1/2,T)^{\frac{7-8\sigma}{3}}

Recall that we assumed
K(1/2,T)\geq T
for this case, so with the choices
\varepsilon=1/2
,
N=T^{1/2}
, the inequality reads as:

(21)

K(\sigma,T)\ll K(1/2,T)T^{\frac{1-2\sigma}{3}},

for
\tfrac{1}{2}\leq\sigma\leq\tfrac{3}{4}
.

With all the information we need about
K(\sigma,T)
proved, we now tidy things up and finish the proof. By (
13
), (
14
) and (
21
) we have

H(\sigma,T)\ll J(\sigma,T)+(H(1/2,T)+J(1/2,T))T^{\frac{1-2\sigma}{3}}.

Thus at least one of the following inequalities have to hold:

H(\sigma,T)\ll H(1/2,T)T^{\frac{1-2\sigma}{3}}\qquad\text{or}\qquad H(\sigma,T)\ll J(\sigma,T)+J(1/2,T)T^{\frac{1-2\sigma}{3}}.

Assume the first inequality holds. We take
\sigma=\tfrac{1}{2}+\frac{\eta}{\log T}
for some
\eta>C_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}
. By Lemma
7
, the displayed inequality above reads

e^{\eta}\leq D_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}\eta^{k_{1}^{2}+\dots+k_{r}^{2}}

for some fixed constant
D_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}>0
. Taking
\eta
sufficiently big, we contradict this inequality. Thus we conclude that for this
\eta
,

(22)

H(\widetilde{\sigma},T)\leq J(\widetilde{\sigma},T)+J(1/2,T)T^{\frac{1-2\sigma}{3}}

must hold when
\widetilde{\sigma}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\eta}{\log T}
. We now claim that

J(\widetilde{\sigma},T)\ll T^{\widetilde{\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}}J(1/2,T)^{\frac{3}{2}-\widetilde{\sigma}}

for some positive constant
C>0
. Assuming the truth of this, we get from (
22
) and Lemma
7
that

T(\log T)^{k_{1}^{2}\dots+k_{r}^{2}}\ll J(1/2,T),

which is just what we wanted to prove. Thus the only thing left to prove to finish the proof is our claim above. It will follow from

Lemma 12
.

Let
\tfrac{1}{2}\leq\sigma\leq\tfrac{3}{4}
. Then
J(\sigma,T)\ll T^{\sigma-1/2}J(1/2,T)^{3/2-\sigma}
.

Proof.

There are two cases to consider. If none of the
L
-functions
L(s,\pi_{1}),\dots,L(s,\pi_{r})
are the Riemann zeta function, then we have no pole at
s=1
for the function
f(s)=L^{k_{1}}(s,\pi_{1})\cdots L^{k_{r}}(s,\pi_{r})e^{(s-i\tau)^{2}/2}
. In the other case, where
L(s,\pi_{1})=\zeta(s)
say, we consider the function
f(s)=(s-1)^{k_{1}}L^{k_{1}}(s,\pi_{1})\cdots L^{k_{r}}(s,\pi_{r})e^{(s-i\tau)^{2}}
. In any case, we apply Proposition
8
to
f
with
\alpha=1/2
,
\beta=3/2
and
\gamma=\sigma
. The procedure for both the cases are very similar, the second being a tiny bit more computational. For the first case, the result is immediate after applying Proposition
8
and Jensen’s inequality. For the second case, we follow the same steps as Heath-Brown
[
9
, p. 71]
and also apply Jensen’s inequality in the end.
∎

4.
Upper bound for moments

In this section we work with the moments

\int_{T}^{2T}\prod_{j=1}^{r}|L(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\pi_{j})|^{2k_{j}}\,\textup{d}t.

Theorem
2
then follows from a dyadic decomposition. Our point of departure will be the following lemma due to Chandee
[
6
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Lemma 13
.

Assume that
L(s,\pi)
is the Riemann zeta function or that the completed
L
-function
\Phi(s,\pi)
of
L(s,\pi)
has no pole or zero at
s=0,1
. Assume that RH and the Generalized Ramanujan conjecture holds for
L(s,\pi)
. Assume furthermore that
L
has degree
m
. Then for any
e^{2}\leq x\leq T^{2}
and any
T\leq t\leq 2T
, for sufficiently large
T
, we have

\log\left|L\left(\frac{1}{2}+it,\pi\right)\right|\leq\textup{Re}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x,\\
n=p,p^{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{\Lambda_{\pi}(n)}{n^{1/2+1/\log x+it}\log n}\frac{\log(x/n)}{\log x}\right)+m\frac{\log T}{\log x}+O(1).

Proof.

We will use the formulation from
[
17
, Lemma 3.3]
. Choosing
\lambda=1
therein gives that

\log\left|L\left(1/2+it,\pi\right)\right|\leq\text{Re}\left(\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\Lambda_{\pi}(n)}{n^{1/2+1/\log x+it}\log n}\frac{\log(x/n)}{\log x}\right)+\frac{m\log T}{\log x}+O\left(\frac{1}{\log x}\right)

for
t\in[T,2T]
. By means of (
10
), we see that

\sum_{p^{j}\leq x,j\geq 3}\left|\frac{\Lambda_{\pi}(p^{j})}{(p^{j})^{1/2+1/\log x+it}\log p^{j}}\frac{\log(x/p^{j})}{\log x}\right|\ll\sum_{p^{j}\leq x,j\geq 3}\frac{1}{jmp^{j/2}}=O(1).

∎

We keep the same representations
\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{r}
as in the previous section. Recall that they were of degree
m_{1},\dots,m_{r}
respectively. Following Harper we shall split
[T,2T]
into many sets depending on how small (or large) the magnitude of certain pieces of the Dirichlet polynomial

\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{k_{1}\Lambda_{\pi_{1}}(p)+\dots+k_{r}\Lambda_{\pi_{r}}(p)}{p^{1/2+1/\log x+it}\log(p)}\frac{\log(x/p)}{\log x}

is when evaluated in
t
. It will be very convenient to have a shorthand notation for the coefficients appearing in the Dirichlet polynomial above. Therefore we define

\mathbf{\Lambda}_{x}(n)\coloneq\frac{k_{1}\Lambda_{\pi_{1}}(n)+\dots+k_{r}\Lambda_{\pi_{r}}(n)}{\log(n)}\frac{\log(x/n)}{n^{1/\log x}\log x}.

Observe that
\log(x/n)/(n^{1/\log x}\log x)\leq 1
for
n\leq x
. We are now ready to set up all the necessary notation for the proof.

\displaystyle\widehat{k}

\displaystyle\coloneq\max\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}k_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{r}k_{i}^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\theta_{j}

\displaystyle\coloneq\frac{e^{j-1}}{(\log\log T)^{2}}

\displaystyle T_{j}

\displaystyle\coloneq\left\{\begin{matrix}1&j=0\\
T^{\theta_{j}}&j\geq 1\end{matrix}\right.

\displaystyle K_{j}

\displaystyle\coloneq\widehat{k}^{1/2}\theta_{j}^{-3/4}.

Define
J
to be the largest integer such that

T_{J}\leq T^{e^{-1000\widehat{k}}}.

Observe in particular that this implies
J-1\leq 2\log_{3}T-1000\widehat{k}\asymp\log_{3}T
. Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{j,x}(s)

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{T_{j-1}<p\leq T_{j}}\frac{\mathbf{\Lambda}_{x}(p)}{p^{s}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{j,x}(s)

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega(n)\leq 10K_{j},\\
p\mid n\implies T_{j-1}<p\leq T_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathfrak{g}_{x}(n)}{n^{s}},

where
\mathfrak{g}_{x}(n)
is defined on prime powers by

\mathfrak{g}_{x}(p^{\eta})=\frac{1}{\eta!}\mathbf{\Lambda}_{x}(p)^{\eta},

and extended multiplicatively. A simple computation shows that the length of the full product
\prod_{j=1}^{\mathscr{J}}|\mathcal{N}_{j,x}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}
is
\leq T^{6/10}
.

If
\mathcal{P}_{j,x}(s)
is of “typical” size, we can estimate
\exp(\mathcal{P}_{j,x}(s))
rather sharply by
\mathcal{N}_{j,x}(s)
. We capture this behaviour by the following set:

\mathscr{G}\coloneq\left\{t\in[T,2T]:\left|\mathcal{P}_{j,T_{J}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|\leq K_{j}\kern 5.0pt\forall 1\leq j\leq J\right\}.

On some other sets we will have typical behaviour of the
\mathcal{P}_{j,x}
for all
j
up to some index
\ell
, then some irregular behaviour after that. We capture this behaviour by the following sets

\mathscr{B}_{j}\coloneq\left\{t\in[T,2T]:\begin{split}\left|\mathcal{P}_{r,T_{s}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|&\leq K_{r}\kern 5.0pt\forall 1\leq r<j\text{ and }r\leq s\leq J,\text{but}\\
\left|\mathcal{P}_{j,T_{s}}\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|&>K_{j}\text{ for some }j\leq s\leq J.\end{split}\right\}.

Observe that

[T,2T]=\mathscr{G}\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{J}\mathscr{B}_{j}.

It will also be nice to further decompose
\mathscr{B}_{j}
into sets

\mathscr{B}_{j,\ell}\coloneq\left\{t\in[T,2T]:\begin{split}|\mathcal{P}_{r,T_{j-1}}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|&\leq K_{r}\kern 5.0pt\forall 1\leq r<j,\\
\textup{but }|\mathcal{P}_{j,T_{\ell}}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|&>K_{j}\end{split}\right\}.

This concludes the setup of all the necessary notation.

We shall need the following basic lemma.

Lemma 14
.

Assume we are given a Dirichlet polynomial
D(s)=\sum_{p\leq Y}a(p)p^{-s}
. If
t\in[T,2T]
is such that
|D(s)|\leq V
, then

\exp\left(2\textup{Re}D(s)\right)=(1+O(e^{-9V}))\left|\sum_{j\leq 10V}\frac{(D(s))^{j}}{j!}\right|^{2}.

In particular if
|\mathcal{P}_{j,X}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|\leq K_{j}
for
1\leq\mathscr{J}\leq J
, then

\prod_{j=1}^{\mathscr{J}}\exp\left(2\textup{Re}\mathcal{P}_{j,X}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)\right)\ll\prod_{j=1}^{\mathscr{J}}|\mathcal{N}_{j,x}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}

Proof.

The first part follows from
[
10
, Eq. 37]
. Applying the first part of the lemma, to each of the
\exp\left(2\textup{Re}\mathcal{P}_{j,X}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)\right)
, then using the multinomial theorem, we obtain the desired result.
∎

Proposition 15
.

For any positive integers
s_{j}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}\prod_{i=1}^{r}|L(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\pi_{i})|^{2k_{i}}\,\textup{d}t

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}|\mathcal{M}_{T_{J}}(1+2it)|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{J}|\mathcal{N}_{j,T_{J}}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}\,\textup{d}t

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq J,\\
j\leq\ell\leq J\end{subarray}}\int_{T}^{2T}|\mathcal{M}_{T_{\ell}}(1+2it)|^{2}\exp\left(\frac{2\widehat{k}}{\theta_{j-1}}\right)\left(\frac{|\mathcal{P}_{j,T_{\ell}}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|}{K_{j}}\right)^{2s_{j}}\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}|\mathcal{N}_{i,T_{\ell}}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}\,\textup{d}t

\displaystyle+\,\,

\displaystyle O((\log\log\log T)^{2}T(\log T)^{-1})

where

\mathcal{M}_{x}(s)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega(n)\leq 10(\log\log T)^{2},\\
p\mid n\implies p\leq\log T\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathfrak{h}_{x}(n)}{n^{s}},

and
\mathfrak{h}_{x}(n)
is multiplicative, defined on prime powers by

\mathfrak{h}_{x}(p^{\eta})=\frac{1}{\eta!}\mathbf{\Lambda}_{x}(p^{2})^{\eta}.

Proof.

Starting with some
t\in[T,2T]
we have the following inequality

(23)

\prod_{i=1}^{r}|L(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\pi_{i})|^{2k_{i}}\leq\mathbf{1}_{t\in\mathscr{G}}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r}|L(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\pi_{i})|^{2k_{i}}+\sum_{1\leq j\leq J}\mathbf{1}_{t\in\mathscr{B}_{j}}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r}|L(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\pi_{i})|^{2k_{i}}.

We now aim to apply Lemma
13
to all of the terms above by themselves. We shall start by showing that we can take the sum over prime squares in this approximation to be very short. To this end, define

E_{x}\coloneq\left\{t\in[T,2T]:\left|\sum_{(\log T)^{5(k_{1}^{2}+\dots+k_{r}^{2}+1)}<p\leq x}\frac{\mathbf{\Lambda}_{x}(p^{2})}{p^{1+2it}}\right|>k_{1}m_{1}+\dots+k_{r}m_{r}\right\},

where we assume
x>(\log T)^{5(k_{1}^{2}+\dots+k_{r}^{2}+1)}
(which will be the case in our applications). Applying Chebyshev’s inequality and GRC, one sees that
\text{meas}(E_{x})\ll T(\log T)^{-5(k_{1}^{2}+\dots+k_{r}^{2}+1)}
. By Cauchy–Schwarz and bounds for the
4(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})
th moment given in
[
17
, Theorem 1.1.]
, we thus get that

\int_{E_{x}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}|L(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\pi_{1})|^{2k_{i}}\,\textup{d}t\ll T(\log T)^{-1}.

Applying Lemma
13
with

\mathcal{I}_{j}
, we leave the integral as it is. If this is not the case, it means that at least one of the sides in the box take the form
\mathcal{I}_{j}^{-}
or
\mathcal{I}_{j}^{+}
. Without loss of generality, say
j=1
(up to a possible permutation). Let us name the other sides of the box
S_{2},\dots,S_{r}
. Thus, if
k_{j}=C_{j}/\sqrt{2}
, Lemma
21
implies

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{2\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\log\log T}(k_{1}W_{1}+\dots+k_{r}W_{r})}\Phi_{T}(W_{1},\dots,W_{r})\,\text{d}W_{1}\cdots\text{d}W_{r}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\int_{I_{1}}\cdots\int_{I_{r}}e^{2\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\log\log T}(k_{1}W_{1}+\dots+k_{r}W_{r})}\Phi_{T}(W_{1},\dots,W_{r})\,\text{d}W_{1}\cdots\text{d}W_{r}

\displaystyle+O\left(\frac{T}{(\log\log T)^{r/2}}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\exp\left(\frac{V_{1}^{2}}{2}+\dots+\frac{V_{j}^{2}}{2}(1-\varepsilon^{2})+\dots+\frac{V_{r}^{2}}{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\ll\,

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{r}V_{1}\cdots V_{r}\Phi_{T}(V_{1}(1-\varepsilon),\dots,V_{r}(1-\varepsilon))\exp\left(2\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\log\log T}(1+\varepsilon)(k_{1}V_{1}+\dots+k_{r}V_{r})\right)

\displaystyle+O\left(\frac{T}{(\log\log T)^{r/2}}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\exp\left(\frac{V_{1}^{2}}{2}+\dots+\frac{V_{j}^{2}}{2}(1+\varepsilon^{2})+\dots+\frac{V_{r}^{2}}{2}\right)\right)

Tidying up and using Lemma
20
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\Phi_{T}(V_{1}(1-\varepsilon),\dots,V_{r}(1-\varepsilon))

\displaystyle+O\left(\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{r}\exp\left(-\frac{V_{1}^{2}}{2}(1+2\varepsilon)-\dots-\frac{V_{j}^{2}}{2}(1+2\varepsilon-\varepsilon^{2})-\dots-\frac{V_{r}^{2}}{2}(1+2\varepsilon)\right)}{(\log\log T)^{r/2}\varepsilon^{r}V_{1}\cdots V_{r}}\right)

\displaystyle\gg\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\frac{\exp\left(-(1+\varepsilon)\left(\frac{V_{1}^{2}}{2}+\dots+\frac{V_{r}^{2}}{2}\right)\right)}{(\log\log T)^{r/2}\varepsilon^{r}V_{1}\cdots V_{r}}

\displaystyle\kern 5.0pt\times\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{2\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\log\log T}(k_{1}W_{1}+\dots+k_{r}W_{r})}\Phi_{T}(W_{1},\dots,W_{r})\,\text{d}W_{1}\cdots\text{d}W_{r}

\displaystyle\gg\,

\displaystyle\frac{\exp\left(-(1+\varepsilon)\left(V_{1}^{2}+\dots+V_{r}^{2}\right)\right)}{(\log\log T)^{r}\varepsilon^{r}V_{1}\cdots V_{r}}(\log T)^{k_{1}^{2}+\dots+k_{r}^{2}}

\displaystyle\gg

\displaystyle\exp\left(-(1+o(1))\left(\frac{V_{1}^{2}}{2}+\dots+\frac{V_{r}^{2}}{2}\right)\right),

with
\varepsilon=\frac{D}{(\log\log T)^{2}}
for some constant
D
. Comparing the error term on the left hand side to main term, we can thus conclude that

\frac{1}{T}\Phi_{T}(V_{1}(1-\varepsilon),\dots,V_{r}(1-\varepsilon))\gg\exp\left(-(1+o(1))\left(\frac{V_{1}^{2}}{2}+\dots+\frac{V_{r}^{2}}{2}\right)\right).

Letting
V_{j}\mapsto\frac{V_{j}}{1-\varepsilon}
, the desired inequality follows.

∎

Proof of Corollary
5
.

Theorem
2
give us sharp upper bound for
2k
th moment for any Dirichlet
L
-function with primitive character, and hence also any imprimitive character, up to order. If
k>1/2
, we use Jensen’s inequality and (
5
) to see that

|\zeta(s,\alpha)|^{2k}\ll\sum_{\chi}|L(s,\chi)|^{2k},

which gives us the desired upper bound. If
k\leq 1/2
, we instead apply the inequality
(x+y)^{1/p}\leq x^{1/p}+y^{1/p}
, which is valid for any
x,y\geq 0
,
p\geq 1
.

For the lower bound, we apply Hölder’s inequality to the function
q^{-it}\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\alpha)\overline{\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2(k-1)}
in the following way:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\left|\int_{1}^{T}q^{-it}\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\alpha)\overline{\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2(k-1)}\,\textup{d}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{T}\int_{1}^{T}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\alpha)|^{2k}\,\textup{d}t\right)^{1/(2k)}\left(\frac{1}{T}\int_{1}^{T}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2k}\,\textup{d}t\right)^{(2k-1)/(2k)}

By the sharp moment bound for the Riemann zeta function
[
8
]
, we conclude from the above that

\frac{1}{T}\int_{1}^{T}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\alpha)|^{2k}\,\textup{d}t\geq\frac{1}{T}\left|\int_{1}^{T}q^{-it}\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\alpha)\overline{\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2(k-1)}\,\textup{d}t\right|^{2k}(\log T)^{k^{2}(1-2k)}.

If we can show that the integral on the right hand side is
\asymp T(\log T)^{k^{2}}
, then the desired conclusion follows. This is precisely what we expect as well, because
q^{-it}\overline{\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2(k-1)}
should “pick out” the term
L(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\chi_{0})\approx\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)
from the sum in (
5
), and by independence of we expect the other moments to be smaller. Indeed, opening up the integral we see

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{T}q^{-it}\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\alpha)\overline{\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2(k-1)}\,\textup{d}t

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\frac{q^{1/2}}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi}\overline{\chi(a)}\int_{1}^{T}L(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\chi)\overline{\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2(k-1)}\,\textup{d}t

\displaystyle\asymp\,

\displaystyle T(\log T)^{k^{2}}+O\left(T(\log T)^{k^{2}-k+\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Here we have used Theorem
1
and
2
to compute the moments. Because
k\geq\frac{1}{2}
, this finishes the proof.

∎

Quantum Energy Teleportation versus Information Teleportation
Jinzhao Wang, Shunyu Yao

Abstract
Quantum energy teleportation (QET) is the phenomenon in which locally inaccessible energy is activated as extractable work through collaborative local operations and classical communication (LOCC) with an entangled partner. It closely resembles the more well-known quantum information teleportation (QIT) where quantum information can be sent through an entangled pair with LOCC. It is tempting to ask how QET is related to QIT. Here we report a first study of this connection. Despite the apparent similarity, we show that these two phenomena are not only distinct but moreover are mutually competitive. We show a perturbative trade-off relation between their performance in a thermal entangled chaotic many-body system, in which both QET and QIT are simultaneously implemented through a traversable wormhole in an emergent spacetime. Motivated by this example, we study a generic setup of two entangled qudits and prove a universal non-perturbative trade-off bound. It shows that for any teleportation protocol, the overall performance of QET and QIT together is constrained by the entanglement resource. We discuss some explanations of our results.



Introduction.
—Quantum (information) teleportation (QIT) is a protocol in which the quantum entanglement shared between two distant agents can be turned into quantum communication with the help of local operations and classical communication (LOCC)

Could one teleport something other than information when combining the resource of entanglement with LOCC? Quantum energy teleportation (QET) is the next of kin

;
Hotta 2008b

Rodríguez-Briones et al. 2023

Here is how QET works. Suppose Bob would like to locally extract some energy/work from the system he has. Without accessing auxiliary systems like a thermal bath, Bob cannot extract energy if his system is in a
passive
state, such as the thermal state or the ground state. Mathematically, passivity means whatever unitary Bob applies to his system makes the energy higher, so by energy conservation, Bob is injecting rather than extracting energy. However, if Bob’s system is entangled with a part shared by his partner Alice, Bob could use Alice’s help by asking her to make some measurements on her system and communicating the results to Bob. Bob then applies conditional operations based on Alice’s results. It turns out that when acted in accordance, such a strategy could allow Bob to inject negative energy into the system and hence equivalently to energy extraction.

One immediate question that comes to our mind is how is QET related to QIT? Could we achieve both goals with one protocol? Naively, we thought QET and QIT should go hand in hand simply because they work analogously.

Discussions.
—An operational explanation of the trade-off bounds is as follows. The optimal protocol for QIT cannot work well for QET and vice versa. This is because for QIT, Alice wants a measurement that reveals as little information as possible so as to “wire up” the quantum correlation between Bob and the reference as neatly as possible; whereas for QET, Alice wants a measurement that reveals some information that pertains to the Hamiltonian, to instruct Bob to extract energy accordingly. These goals contradict each other so one has to compromise when limited entanglement resources are available. Therefore, the total entanglement shared among Alice and Bob determines the optimal overall performance of both QET and QIT.

We also propose a more physical explanation inspired by the perturbative trade-off observed in the traversable wormhole protocol (cf. Fig.
3
). We can rearrange (
4
) to
S(B)\leq\beta(E_{\max}-E-F)\
, where
E_{\max}=\mathrm{Tr}\,H\tau_{\beta}
is the maximal extractable energy from Bob’s system (assuming the ground state energy is zero), and
F=-\log Z<0
is Bob’s equilibrium free energy at
\beta
. The same manipulation also applies for (
8
). This bound on entropy can be understood as a Bekenstein bound, that the energy cost of sending
S(B)
amount of information is the energy deficit
(E_{\max}-E)
(up to the additive constant of
F
). Bob could have gained had they used the optimal QET protocol. It always consumes positive energy to transmit information, which acts against QET. The same principle is manifest in the bulk picture of the traversable wormhole protocol as we have shown above.

\Pi_{x}:=\Pi_{i}P_{i,\pm}^{A}
made by Alice, with outcomes

U_{x}:=\Pi_{i}e^{\mp ig\psi_{0}^{B}\psi_{i}^{B}}
implemented by Bob.

Appendix B
Traversable wormhole as QET

In this section, we present the details for computing the energy change in Bob’s system, using the Schwarzian description of JT gravity
Maldacena et al. 2016
,
Kitaev
,
Maldacena and Stanford 2016
. By the arguments presented in the previous section, the teleported energy extracted via LOCC can be calculated by comparing the
energy change
before and after the double trace deformation.

Due to the topological nature of JT gravity, the gravitational degree of freedom is described by a boundary reparametrization mode denoted by
\tau(u)
. We will compute the energy change by first computing in Euclidean (imaginary time) configuration and then analytically continue the result to Lorentzian (real) time. On the Euclidean disk, the saddle point solution is given by
\tau(u)=u
. We expand the Schwarzian variable to
\tau(u)=u+\epsilon(u)
, at leading order, the Schwarzian action becomes

S_{E}=\frac{C}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}du\,\epsilon^{\prime\prime}(u)^{2}-\epsilon^{\prime}(u)^{2}\ .

(10)

Here
C
is the coupling constant, and we set
\beta=2\pi
for notational clarity in this computation. To compute the energy change, we want to compute the classical solution with a double trace deformation
e^{ig\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\psi_{i}^{A}(u_{1})\psi_{i}^{B}(u_{2})}
. In Schwarzian formalism, it can be rewritten as
e^{igG(u_{1},u_{2})}
, where

G(u_{1},u_{2})=\left(\frac{\tau^{\prime}(u_{2})\tau^{\prime}(u_{1})}{(\tau(u_{2})-\tau(u_{1}))^{2}}\right)^{\Delta}=\Delta\left(\epsilon^{\prime}(u_{2})+\epsilon^{\prime}(u_{1})+(\epsilon(u_{1})-\epsilon(u_{2}))\cot(\frac{u_{2}-u_{1}}{2})\right)\frac{1}{\sin^{2\Delta}(\frac{u_{2}-u_{1}}{2})}\ .

(11)

For the energy teleportation protocol that we considered, we need to solve the saddle point of total action
-S_{E}+igG(0,\tau)
. We leave
\tau
general here and we will analytically continue it to
\pi+it
later. The equation of motion for the whole action is

C(\epsilon^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(u)+\epsilon^{\prime\prime}(u))+ig\Delta\frac{1}{\sin^{2\Delta}(\tau/2)}(\delta^{\prime}(0)+\delta^{\prime}(\tau)-(\delta(0)-\delta(\tau))\cot(\tau/2))=0\ .

(12)

We can solve the classical solution explicitly, but for our purpose, we care about how energy

H=C\left[\frac{1}{2}+(\epsilon^{\prime}(u)+\epsilon^{\prime\prime\prime}(u))\right]

(13)

changes before and after the operator insertion. The only contribution to the energy change before and after operator insertion at
\tau
comes from the delta function in (
12
), thus we conclude that the energy change is

-ig\Delta\frac{\cot(\tau/2)}{\sin^{2\Delta}(\tau/2)}\ .

(14)

Now we can analytically continue to Lorentzian time
\tau_{1}=\pi+it
to obtain the averaged teleported energy
E
as the negative of the energy change

E=g\Delta\frac{\tanh(t/2)}{\cosh^{2\Delta}(t/2)}\ .

(15)

Putting back the
\beta

E=g\Delta\left(\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\right)^{2\Delta+1}\frac{\tanh(\frac{\pi}{\beta}t)}{\cosh^{2\Delta}(\frac{\pi}{\beta}t)}\ .

(16)

In conclusion, Bob can extract energy if he applies the unitary on his share of the thermal field double state with a delay
t>0

Appendix C
Proof of Theorem 1

Proof.

We can rewrite the thermal entangled state as
\sqrt{\tau_{\beta}}_{B}|\mathbb{I}\rangle_{AB}
where the square root of the density matrix
\tau_{\beta}=\sum_{x}e^{-\beta H}\left|E_{x}\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle E_{x}\right|_{B}
acts on the (super-normalized) maximally entangled state
|\mathbb{I}\rangle_{AB}=\sum_{x}|x\rangle_{A}|x\rangle_{B}
. In this way, it is manifest that Bob’s density matrix is
\tau_{\beta}
.

Any rank-one projector
\Pi_{x}
on
AA^{\prime}
can be written as
\Pi_{x}=\left|\Pi_{x}\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\Pi_{x}\right|_{AA^{\prime}}
. By virtue of the Schmidt decomposition of
|\Pi_{x}\rangle_{AA^{\prime}}
, we can write any pure state as
|\Pi_{x}\rangle_{AA^{\prime}}=\sqrt{\sigma_{x\,A}}\otimes U_{x\,A^{\prime}}|\mathbb{I}\rangle_{AA^{\prime}}
for some positive operator
\sigma_{x}\geq 0
on
A
and some unitary
U_{x}
on
A^{\prime}
. Note that
\{\sigma_{x}\}
forms a POVM set on
A
, because

\mathbb{I}_{A}=\mathrm{Tr}_{A^{\prime}}\sum_{x}\Pi_{x}=\sum_{x}\mathrm{Tr}_{A^{\prime}}\left(\sqrt{\sigma_{x\,A}}\otimes U_{x\,A^{\prime}}\left|\mathbb{I}\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\mathbb{I}\right|_{AA^{\prime}}\sqrt{\sigma_{x\,A}}\otimes U^{\dagger}_{x\,A^{\prime}}\right)=\sum_{x}\sigma_{x\,A}\ .

The probability of Alice measuring
\Pi_{x}
is

p_{x}=\langle\tau_{\beta}|_{AB}\langle\Phi|_{A^{\prime}R}\sqrt{\sigma_{x\,A}}\otimes U_{x\,A^{\prime}}\left|\mathbb{I}\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\mathbb{I}\right|_{AA^{\prime}}\sqrt{\sigma_{x\,A}}\otimes U^{\dagger}_{x\,A^{\prime}}|\tau_{\beta}\rangle_{AB}|\Phi\rangle_{A^{\prime}R}=\mathrm{Tr}\,\sigma_{x}^{\top}\tau_{\beta}=\mathrm{Tr}\,\sigma_{x}\tau_{\beta}\ ,\

(17)

where we use the fact that
\tau_{\beta}^{\top}=\tau_{\beta}
and
\mathrm{Tr}\,\sigma_{x}^{\top}\tau_{\beta}^{\top}=\mathrm{Tr}\,\sigma_{x}\tau_{\beta}
in the last step.

The post-measurement state reads,

|\rho_{x}\rangle_{BR}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_{x}}}\langle\mathbb{I}|_{AA^{\prime}}\sqrt{\sigma_{x\,A}}\otimes U^{\dagger}_{x\,A^{\prime}}|\tau_{\beta}\rangle_{AB}|\Phi\rangle_{A^{\prime}R}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_{x}}}(\sqrt{\sigma_{x}}^{\top}\sqrt{\tau_{\beta}})_{B}\otimes\bar{U}_{x\,R}|\mathbb{I}\rangle_{BR}\ ,

(18)

and the post-measurement marginal on
B
reads,

\rho_{x}:=\mathrm{Tr}_{R}\,\rho_{BR|x}=\sqrt{\tau_{\beta}}\sigma_{x}^{\top}\sqrt{\tau_{\beta}}/p_{x}\ ,

(19)

and they average to

\sum_{x}p_{x}\rho_{x}=\sum_{x}\sqrt{\tau_{\beta}}\sigma_{x}^{\top}\sqrt{\tau_{\beta}}=\tau_{\beta}\ ,

(20)

as
\{\sigma_{x}\}
forms a POVM set on
A
and so does
\{\sigma_{x}^{\top}\}
on
B
.

Consider now Bob’s extracted energy upon learning Alice’s measurement outcome
x
,

E_{x}=\mathrm{Tr}\,H(\rho_{x}-U_{x}\rho_{x}U_{x}^{\dagger})=\frac{1}{\beta}\left(S(\rho_{x}||\tau_{\beta})-S(U_{x}\rho_{x}U_{x}^{\dagger}||\tau_{\beta})\right)\leq\frac{1}{\beta}S(\rho_{x}||\tau_{\beta})\ ,

(21)

where
S(\rho_{x}||\tau_{\beta}):=\beta\langle H\rangle_{\rho_{x}}+\log Z-S(\rho_{x})
, and we have arranged the energy extracted as the difference between two relative entropies so we can dump the second relative entropy term which is always positive. We therefore have

S(\rho_{x})+\beta E_{x}\leq-\langle\log\tau_{\beta}\rangle_{\rho_{x}}\ .

(22)

Taking the expectation over the measurement outcome,

\sum_{x}p_{x}\left[S(\rho_{x})+\beta E_{x}\right]\leq-\sum_{x}p_{x}\langle\log\tau_{\beta}\rangle_{\rho_{x}}=-\langle\log\tau_{\beta}\rangle_{\tau_{\beta}}=S(\tau_{\beta})\ ,

(23)

where we have used (
20
).
∎

Appendix D
Proof of Theorem 2

For the proof, we will be using an important quantity called the max-relative entropy
Renner 2008
,
Datta 2009
,

S_{\max}(\rho||\sigma):=\log\min\{\alpha:\rho\leq\alpha\,\sigma\}\ .

(24)

The definition essentially says that

\rho\leq 2^{S_{\max}(\rho||\sigma)}\,\sigma\

(25)

is the tightest possible operator inequality upper-bounding
\rho
with
\sigma
. Often it is more operational to use the smoothed max-relative entropy,

S_{\max}^{\varepsilon}(\rho||\sigma):=\min_{\rho^{\prime}\approx^{\varepsilon}\rho}S_{\max}(\rho^{\prime}||\sigma)\ ,

(26)

where
\rho^{\prime}
is close to
\rho
in the purified distance
P(\rho^{\prime},\rho):=\sqrt{1-F(\rho^{\prime},\rho)}
.

The intuition behind working with the max-relative entropy is that: By taking the logarithm of the operator inequality (
25
) and then taking the expectation value w.r.t.
\rho
, we already get something close to what we want. To relate the max-relative entropy to the standard Umegaki relative entropy, we need the following typicality property.

Lemma 1

(Fully quantum asymptotic equipartition
Tomamichel et al. 2009
,
Tomamichel 2015
)
.

Let
0<\varepsilon<1
, and
\rho,\sigma
be any quantum states such that
\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}S_{\max}^{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\otimes n}||\sigma^{\otimes n})=S(\rho||\sigma)\ .

(27)

Some other lemmas shall prove handy as well.

Lemma 2

(Continuity of energy)
.

Let
H
be a bounded Hamiltonian with ground state energy set at zero, and
\rho,\sigma
be two quantum states,

|\mathrm{Tr}\,H(\rho-\sigma)|\leq\frac{1}{2}||\rho-\sigma||_{1}||H||_{\infty}\leq P(\rho,\sigma)||H||_{\infty}\ .

(28)

This is a direct consequence of Hölder’s inequality and that the purified distance is larger than the trace distance.

Lemma 3

(Continuity of entropy
Fannes 1973
,
Audenaert 2007
)
.

Let
\rho,\sigma
be two
d
-dimensional quantum states such that their trace distance is close,
\frac{1}{2}||\rho-\sigma||_{1}\leq\varepsilon
, then their von Neumann entropy is also close,

|S(\rho)-S(\sigma)|\leq\varepsilon\log(d-1)+h_{2}(\varepsilon)\ .

(29)

where
h_{2}(\varepsilon):=-\varepsilon\log\varepsilon-(1-\varepsilon)\log(1-\varepsilon)
is the binary entropy function.

Now we are ready to prove Theorem 2.

Proof.

Just like in the last proof, the Schmidt decomposition guarantees that any bipartite pure state can be written as

|\rho_{AB}\rangle=U_{A}\otimes\sqrt{\rho_{B}}|\mathbb{I}\rangle_{AB}\ .

(30)

Let us consider an arbitrary QET protocol
(\Pi_{x},U_{x})
, where
\{\Pi_{x}\}
are rank-one projectors on
A^{n}A^{\prime n}
. Following the same argument as the last proof, we can show that upon obtaining outcome
x
, the post-measurement state on
B^{n}
reads

\rho_{B^{n}|x}:=\sqrt{\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}}\sigma_{x}\sqrt{\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}}/p_{x}\ ,\quad p_{x}:=\mathrm{Tr}_{AB}\,\Pi_{x}\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}\Pi_{x}=\mathrm{Tr}_{B}\,\sigma_{x}\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}\ ,\quad\sigma_{x}:=\mathrm{Tr}_{A}U^{\top}\Pi_{x}\bar{U}\ ,

(31)

where
\{\sigma_{x}\}
forms a POVM set on
B^{n}
.
The ensemble
\{p_{x},\rho_{B^{n}|x}\}
averages to
\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}
,

\sum_{x}p_{x}\rho_{B^{n}|x}=\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}\ .

(32)

Consider the smooth max-relative entropy between
\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}
and
\tau^{\otimes n}_{\beta}:=e^{-\beta\sum_{i=1}^{n}H^{(i)}}/Z
for any
\beta
, where
Z
is the partition function
Z=\mathrm{Tr}e^{-\beta\sum_{i=1}^{n}H^{(i)}}
throughout this proof. Let
\rho^{\prime}_{B^{n}}
be the minimizer of it,

S_{\max}^{\varepsilon}(\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}||\tau^{\otimes n}_{\beta})=S_{\max}(\rho^{\prime}_{B^{n}}||\tau^{\otimes n}_{\beta})\ .

(33)

By definition, we have
\frac{1}{2}||\rho^{\prime}_{B^{n}}-\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}||_{1}\leq P(\rho^{\prime}_{B^{n}},\rho_{B}^{\otimes n})\leq\varepsilon
, and

\rho^{\prime}_{B^{n}}\leq 2^{S^{\varepsilon}_{\max}(\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}||\tau^{\otimes n}_{\beta})}\tau^{\otimes n}_{\beta}\ .

(34)

Taking the logarithm gives,

\log\rho^{\prime}_{B^{n}}\leq S^{\varepsilon}_{\max}(\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}||\tau^{\otimes n}_{\beta})-\log Z-\beta\sum_{i=1}^{n}H^{(i)}\ .

(35)

Then taking the expectation value on the state
\rho^{\prime}_{B^{n}}
yields

\langle\log\rho^{\prime}_{B^{n}}\rangle_{\rho^{\prime}_{B^{n}}}=-S(\rho^{\prime}_{B^{n}})\leq S^{\varepsilon}_{\max}(\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}||\tau^{\otimes n}_{\beta})-\log Z-\beta\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n}H^{(i)}\rangle_{\rho^{\prime}_{B^{n}}}\ .

(36)

Since
\rho^{\prime}_{B^{n}}
and
\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}
are close in trace distance, we can bound the energy difference between them by Lemma
2

\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n}H^{(i)}\rangle_{\rho^{\prime}_{B^{n}}}\geq\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n}H^{(i)}\rangle_{\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}}-n\varepsilon||H||_{\infty}\ ,

(37)

where

is finite by assumption. It follows that

-S(\rho^{\prime}_{B^{n}})\leq S^{\varepsilon}_{\max}(\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}||\tau^{\otimes n}_{\beta})-\beta\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n}H^{(i)}\rangle_{\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}}-\log Z+n\varepsilon||H||_{\infty}\ .

(38)

Using (

-S(\rho^{\prime}_{B^{n}})\leq S^{\varepsilon}_{\max}(\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}||\tau^{\otimes n}_{\beta})-\beta\sum_{x}p_{x}\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n}H^{(i)}\rangle_{\rho_{B^{n}|x}}-\log Z+n\varepsilon||H||_{\infty}\ .

(39)

Using the definition of extracted energy
E
, we have

-S(\rho^{\prime}_{B^{n}})\leq S^{\varepsilon}_{\max}(\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}||\tau^{\otimes n}_{\beta})-\beta\sum_{x}p_{x}E(\rho_{B^{n}|x})-\beta\sum_{x}p_{x}\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n}H^{(i)}\rangle_{U_{x}\rho_{B^{n}|x}U_{x}^{\dagger}}-\log Z+n\varepsilon||H||_{\infty}\ ,

(40)

where
U_{x}
is the optimal energy extractor for
\rho_{B^{n}|x}

Consider the relative entropy
S(U_{x}\rho_{x}U_{x}^{\dagger}||\tau_{\beta})

S(U_{x}\rho_{B^{n}|x}U_{x}^{\dagger}||\tau_{\beta})=\beta\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n}H^{(i)}\rangle_{U_{x}\rho_{B^{n}|x}U_{x}^{\dagger}}+\log Z-S(\rho_{B^{n}|x})\geq 0\ .

(41)

Plugging the above inequality to (

-S(\rho^{\prime}_{B^{n}})\leq S^{\varepsilon}_{\max}(\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}||\tau^{\otimes n}_{\beta})-\beta\sum_{x}p_{x}E(\rho_{B^{n}|x})-\sum_{x}p_{x}S(\rho_{B^{n}|x})+n\varepsilon||H||_{\infty}\ .

(42)

Using Lemma
3

S(\rho_{B}^{\otimes n})(=nS(\rho_{B}))

\varepsilon

-nS(\rho_{B})-\varepsilon n\log d-h_{2}(\varepsilon)\leq S^{\varepsilon}_{\max}(\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}||\tau^{\otimes n}_{\beta})-\beta\sum_{x}p_{x}E(\rho_{B^{n}|x})-\sum_{x}p_{x}S(\rho_{B^{n}|x})+n\varepsilon||H||_{\infty}\ .

(43)

Now taking the per-copy average and sending

S(B)_{\rho}-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\beta}{n}\sum_{x}p_{x}E(\rho_{B^{n}|x})+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}S^{\varepsilon}_{\max}(\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}||\tau^{\otimes n}_{\beta})\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{x}p_{x}S(\rho_{B^{n}|x})-\varepsilon(\log d+||H||_{\infty})\ .

(44)

1

S(B)_{\rho}\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\beta}{n}\sum_{x}p_{x}E(\rho_{B^{n}|x})-S(\rho_{B}||\tau_{\beta})+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{x}p_{x}S(\rho_{B^{n}|x})-\varepsilon(\log d+||H||_{\infty})\ .

(45)

which according to our definition is,

S(B)_{\rho}\geq\beta\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{x}p_{x}E(\rho_{B^{n}|x})-\frac{1}{\beta}S(\rho_{B}||\tau_{\beta})\right)+S^{\mathrm{reg}}(B)-\varepsilon(\log d+||H||_{\infty})\ .

(46)

Appendix E
Teleportation schemes with general measurements.

The proofs we give above consider only the optimal rank-one projective measurements. When the measurement operators
\{\Pi_{x}\}
are not rank-one projectors, the post-measurement state
\rho_{RB|x}
is mixed.

Consider any measurement instruments described by a set of Kraus operators
\{\Pi_{x}\}
. Let the corresponding POVM element on
AA^{\prime}
be
\Sigma_{x}:=\Pi_{x}^{\dagger}\Pi_{x}
. Let
\sigma_{x}=\mathrm{Tr}_{A^{\prime}}\Sigma_{x}
, which form a POVM set on
A
. Upon obtaining the outcome
x
, the post-measurement state reads

\rho_{RA^{\prime}AB|x}=\Pi_{x}|\Phi\rangle_{RA^{\prime}}|\tau_{\beta}\rangle_{AB}/p_{x},\quad p_{x}=\langle\Phi|_{RA^{\prime}}\langle\tau_{\beta}|_{AB}\Sigma_{x}|\Phi\rangle_{RA^{\prime}}|\tau_{\beta}\rangle_{AB}=\mathrm{Tr}\,\tau_{\beta}\sigma_{x}\ ,

(47)

and the post-measurement marginal on B reads,

\rho_{x}:=\sqrt{\tau_{\beta}}\sigma_{x}^{\top}\sqrt{\tau_{\beta}}/p_{x}\ ,\quad p_{x}=\mathrm{Tr}\,\tau_{\beta}\sigma_{x}\ ,

(48)

which is identical to (
19
). By the same steps as in the proof for Theorem 1, it follows that

\sum_{x}p_{x}\left[S(B)_{\rho_{x}}+\beta E_{x}\right]\leq S(B)_{\tau_{\beta}}\ .

(49)

Hence, the claims we made in Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 still hold for general measurements.

The caveat is that the entropy
S(B)_{\rho_{x}}
doesn’t directly measure the entanglement for mixed states. Nonetheless, it upper-bounds the entanglement of formation of

\sum_{x}p_{x}\left[E_{f}(\rho_{x})+\beta E_{x}\right]\leq S(B)_{\tau_{\beta}}\ .

Appendix F
Variants of the trade-off relation (4)

There are some variants of our trade-off relation (4). Let
\rho_{B}:=\sum_{x}p_{x}\rho_{x}
be the average output state of the teleportation protocol. Then we have

E_{C}(\rho)+\beta E\leq E_{f}(\rho)+\beta E\leq S(B)_{\tau_{\beta}}\ ,

(51)

where
E_{f}(\rho):=\min_{\{p_{i},\rho_{i}\}}\sum_{i}p_{i}S(\rho_{i})
is the entanglement of formation
Hill and Wootters 1997
,
Wootters 1998
,
Horodecki et al. 2009
. The bound in the middle follows from (4) because
S_{f}(\rho)
is defined as the minimization over all ensemble decompositions
\{p_{i},\rho_{i}\}
of
\rho
, and
\{p_{x},\rho_{x}\}
is one such instance. Entanglement of formation measures how much entanglement (measured in the number of qubit Bell pairs) is needed on average to prepare an entangled state
\rho
. It asymptotes to the entanglement cost
Bennett et al. 1996
,
Hayden et al. 2001

E_{C}(\rho)=\lim_{n\to\infty}E_{f}(\rho^{\otimes n})/n
. Since
E_{f}
is subadditive, we can further lower-bound it by
E_{C}(\rho)
, which yields the bound on the left.

Automorphisms of the Quantum Cohomology of the Springer Resolution and Applications
Changzheng Li, Changjian Su, Rui Xiong

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce quantum Demazure–Lusztig operators acting by ring automorphisms on the equivariant quantum cohomology of the Springer resolution. Our main application is a presentation of the torus-equivariant quantum cohomology in terms of generators and relations. We provide explicit descriptions for the classical types. We also recover Kim’s earlier results for the complete flag varieties by taking the Toda limit.



1.
Introduction

The quantum cohomology ring
QH^{*}(X)
of a complex projective manifold
X
is a deformation of the classical cohomology ring
H^{*}(X)=H^{*}(X,\mathbb{C})
by incorporating the Gromov–Witten invariants.
When
X
is a non-compact algebraic variety equipped with a nice reductive group
\mathbb{G}
action, the equivariant quantum product can still be defined via localizations (see e.g.
[
Liu13
]
). An interesting example of such varieties is the symplectic resolution, which usually comes in pairs called symplectic duals
[
Kam22
]
.
The symplectic duality is a 3-dimensional mirror symmetry, generalizing well-known structures in geometric representation theory
[
BLPW16
]
.
Okounkov and his collaborators initiated the study of the symplectic duality via the enumerative geometry of the symplectic resolution, which is deeply related to various structures in geometry, representation theory, and mathematical physics
[
Oko18
]
.
There have been studies of
QH^{*}_{\mathbb{G}}(X)
for various examples of symplectic resolutions
[
OP10
,
BMO11
,
Su16
,
MS13
,
MO19
,
Dan22
,
MP15
,
KMP21
]
.

The Springer resolution, which is the cotangent bundle of the complete flag variety
\mathcal{B}
of a complex semisimple, simply-connected Lie group
G
, is the most classical example of the symplectic resolutions.
It is a resolution of the nilpotent cone in
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}(G)
and admits a natural action by
\mathbb{G}=G\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
, where

\mathbb{C}^{*}
dilates the cotangent fibers.
Let
T
be a maximal torus of
G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{t}
, and denote
\mathbb{T}:=T\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
.
The
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant quantum cohomology ring
QH^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})
can be defined as the deformation

QH^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})=\big(H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})\otimes\mathcal{O}(T^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}_{\reg}),\,*\,\big),

due to the equivariant quantum Chevalley formula proved by Braverman, Maulik, and Okounkov
[
BMO11
, Theorem 3.2]
. Here
T^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}_{\reg}
is the complement of the union of root hyperplanes in the complex dual torus
T^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}
(see Section
2
).

Our first main theorem reveals a surprising symmetry in the equivariant quantum cohomology ring of
T^{*}\mathcal{B}
.
To explain this symmetry, let us start with the classical Springer theory. Recall that the degenerate affine Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\hbar}
is a
\mathbb{C}[\hbar]
-algebra generated by
\Sym(\mathfrak{t}^{*})
and the group algebra
\mathbb{C}[W]
, subject to interacting relations between them.
As will be reviewed in Section
2.1
,

\mathcal{H}_{\hbar}
acts on the
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant cohomology
H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})
, through an isomorphism of convolution algebras
[
CG10
,
Lus88
]
.
The operators corresponding to
w\in W\subset\mathcal{H}_{\hbar}
are usually referred to as the Demazure–Lusztig operators
[
Gin98
]
.
On the other hand, there is a natural Weyl group action on the coefficient ring
\mathcal{O}(T^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}_{\reg})
induced by the Weyl group action on
T^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}_{\reg}
. The
quantum Demazure–Lusztig operators

T_{w}
over
QH^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})=H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})\otimes\mathcal{O}(T^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}_{\reg})
is the diagonal tensor product of these two actions, c.f. Definition
3.1
.
Our first main theorem is the following unexpected result, for which we refer to Example
3.3
for a quick illustration with
T^{*}\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Theorem A

(Theorem
3.2
)
.

Every quantum Demazure–Lusztig operator is a ring automorphism of
QH^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})
.

Observe that the usual Demazure–Lusztig operator interacts with the equivariant cup product via Leibniz-type formulas
[
Gin98
, §12]
, which is never a ring automorphism except for the trivial case.
As the quantum Demazure–Lusztig operators that we have defined create a pole at the origin of the quantum variables, they cannot be projected down to the equivariant cohomology.
The key ingredient in our proof is the stable basis introduced by Maulik and Okounkov
[
MO19
]
.
We achieve this aim by direct computations using an explicit formula about the multiplication of a divisor class with the stable basis due to the second named author
[
Su16
]
. The proof uses some crucial structures appearing in the cotangent bundle situation.

As an application of the above theorem, we obtain a ring presentation for
QH^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})
.
For any dominant weight
\lambda
, we define in (
4.3
) a square matrix
\Theta(\lambda)
with values in
QH^{2}_{\mathbb{T}}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})
, inspired from the quantum Chevalley formula.
It leads to an effective way of finding relations in the equivariant quantum cohomology, due to an observation in communicative algebra as in Lemma
4.9
.
Via a Deformation Principle in Propositon
4.11
,
we obtain our second main result.

Theorem B

(Theorem
4.1
)
.

The
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant quantum cohomology
QH_{\mathbb{T}}^{*}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})
is generated by divisors with the relations

\operatorname{tr}\big(\Theta(\lambda)^{k}\big)=\sum_{\mu\in W\!\lambda}\mu^{k}

for all dominant weights
\lambda
and all
k\geq 1
.

The above ring representation has an application in the study of the Calogero–Moser system, which is an integrable system arising from the one-dimensional many-body problems (see e.g.
[
Eti07
,
BH18
]
and references therein).
Combining Theorem
B
and
[
BMO11
, Theorem 3.2]
, we obtain an explicit description of the classical trigonometric Calogero–Moser map in Corollary
4.13
.

We are going to provide an explicit ring presentation of
QH_{\mathbb{T}}^{*}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})
in all classical types. Instead of using the relations in Theorem
B
directly, we compute the characteristic polynomial of
\Theta(\lambda)
.
For instance, in type
A_{n-1}
,
the flag variety
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{F}\ell_{n}
parameterizes complete flags in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
the Springer resolution
T^{*}\mathcal{F}\ell_{n}
parameterizes pairs
(A,\phi)
for
A
an
n\times n
nilpotent matrix and
\phi\in\mathcal{F}\ell_{n}
a complete flag of
A
-invariant subspaces.
Among the family of
\Theta(\lambda)
, We can find one matrix of the form

\left[\begin{matrix}\chi_{1}&\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{1}/q_{2}}&\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{1}/q_{3}}&\cdots&\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{1}/q_{n}}\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{2}/q_{1}}&\chi_{2}&\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{2}/q_{3}}&\cdots&\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{2}/q_{n}}\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{3}/q_{1}}&\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{3}/q_{2}}&\chi_{3}&\cdots&\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{3}/q_{n}}\\[4.30554pt]
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{n}/q_{1}}&\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{n}/q_{2}}&\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{n}/q_{3}}&\cdots&\chi_{n}\end{matrix}\right],

(1.1)

where

\chi_{i}=x_{i}+\hbar\sum_{a<i}\frac{q_{a}/q_{i}}{1-q_{a}/q_{i}}-\hbar\sum_{i<b}\frac{q_{i}/q_{b}}{1-q_{i}/q_{b}}
.
The matrix (
1.1
) happens to coincide with the one in
[
Eti07
, §2.8]

The presentation for the
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant quantum cohomology of
T^{*}\mathcal{B}
can be stated as follows,

Theorem C

(Theorem
5.1
)
.

The torus-equivariant quantum cohomology of
T^{*}\mathcal{F}\ell_{n}
is generated by divisors
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
with relations
\mathcal{E}_{k}(\chi)-e_{k}(t)
for
k=1,\ldots,n
. Here
\mathcal{E}_{k}(\chi)
are the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial of the matrix (
1.1
).

We will give a combinatorial description of
\mathcal{E}_{k}(\chi)
in Theorem
5.2
via matching over
k
-subsets of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
, which coincides with Feynman’s elementary computation
[
Pol19
]
.
The computations for other classical types are given in Theorem
5.4
(type
B
and type
C
) and Theorem
5.7
(type
D
).

There is another application of our explicit description of
QH^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})
. As explained in
[
BMO11
, §8]
, we can recover
QH^{*}_{T}(\mathcal{B})
by taking the Toda limit. In the context of quantum Schubert calculus and mirror symmetry, it is an important problem to provide a precise description of the ring presentation of the (
T
-equivariant) quantum cohomology of
\mathcal{B}
or, more generally, of a partial flag variety.
This problem has been solved for partial flag varieties of type
A

[
GK95
,
AS95
,
CF99
]
and for the complete flag variety
\mathcal{B}
of general Lie type by Kim
[
Kim99
]
. However, a precise description for partial flag varieties of non-
A
type in general is not yet available.
By taking the Toda limit, we are able to reobtain the presentation of
QH^{*}_{T}(\mathcal{B})
for classical Lie types and discuss its connection with Kim’s description.
For type
A_{n-1}
, the matrix (
1.1
) (after conjugation) converges, under the Toda limit, to the well-known tridiagonal matrix in quantum Schubert calculus:

\left[\begin{matrix}x_{1}&-1&0&\cdots&0\\[4.30554pt]
q_{1}/q_{2}&x_{2}&-1&\cdots&0\\[4.30554pt]
0&q_{2}/q_{3}&x_{3}&\cdots&0\\[4.30554pt]
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\[4.30554pt]
0&0&0&\cdots&x_{n}\end{matrix}\right].

(1.2)

This recovers the presentation obtained by Givental and Kim
[
GK95
]
.
It would be interesting to establish a direct (type-free) connection between both descriptions.
Our approach to finding the ring presentation of
QH^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})
may be generalized to the cotangent bundle of a partial flag variety. Hence, it is potentially useful in solving the aforementioned problem for the partial flag varieties.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we review the background of equivariant quantum cohomology of the Springer resolution
T^{*}\mathcal{B}
.
In Section
3
, we define quantum Demazure–Lusztig operators and show that the operators act on the equivariant quantum cohomology of
T^{*}\mathcal{B}
by ring automorphisms.
In Section
4
, we give the ring presentation of
QH^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})
, as well as an explicit description of the classical trigonometric Calogero–Moser system.
In Section
5
, we carry out computations of the ring presentation of
QH^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})
for all classical Lie types.
Finally, in Appendix
A
and Appendix
B
, we discuss the ring presentation of
QH^{*}_{T}(\mathcal{B})
obtained by taking the Toda limit and provide some details for the computations of
QH^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})
in type
A_{n-1}
.

Preliminaries

Notations

Let
G
be a complex, semi-simple, simply connected group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, and
T\subset G
be a maximal torus with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{t}

be the root system of

Fix a base

Let

G
is simply connected, we can canonically identify the (co)character lattice with the weight (resp. coroot) lattice:

X^{*}(T)=\operatorname{Hom}(T,\mathbb{C}^{*})\cong\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\{\omega_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r},\quad\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{*},T)\cong\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{Z}}R^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}.

Unless otherwise stated, by
\alpha>0
we will always mean an element
\alpha
in the set
R^{+}
of positive roots.
The associated reflection
s_{\alpha}:\mathfrak{t}^{*}\to\mathfrak{t}^{*}
is defined by
s_{\alpha}(\lambda)=\lambda-\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}\rangle\alpha
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathfrak{t}^{*}\times\mathfrak{t}\to\mathbb{C}
is the natural pairing. We will also denote by
s_{\alpha}
the induced reflection on
\mathfrak{t}
.
The Weyl group
W
is generated by simple reflections
s_{i}=s_{\alpha_{i}}
, for
i=1,\ldots,r
. It is equipped with the Bruhat order
\geq
, and admits a standard length function
\ell:W\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Let
X^{*}(T)_{+}
denote the set of dominant weights in
X^{*}(T)
.
For any
\pm\lambda\in X^{*}(T)_{+}
we denote by
W_{\lambda}
the subgroup of
W
that stabilizes
\lambda
, which is generated by simple reflections
s_{i}
with
\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}\rangle=0
. Each coset
uW_{\lambda}
in
W/W_{\lambda}
has a unique minimal length representative, denoted as
\bar{u}
.
Therefore
W/W_{\lambda}
can be identified with the subset
W^{\lambda}\subset W
of minimal length representatives.

The
degenerate affine Hecke algebra

\mathcal{H}_{\hbar}
is a graded
\mathbb{C}[\hbar]
-algebra generated by
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}}
and
W
such that

(1)

x_{c\lambda+\mu}=cx_{\lambda}+x_{\mu}
,
x_{\lambda}x_{\mu}=x_{\mu}x_{\lambda}
,
\forall\lambda,\mu\in\mathfrak{t}^{*},\forall c\in\mathbb{C}
;

(2)

the elements
w\in W
form the Weyl group inside
\mathcal{H}_{\hbar}
.

(3)

s_{i}x_{\lambda}-x_{s_{i}(\lambda)}s_{i}=\hbar\langle\alpha_{i}^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}},\lambda\rangle
,
\forall\lambda\in\mathfrak{t}^{*},\forall i=1,\ldots,r
;

The grading on
\mathcal{H}_{\hbar}
is defined by

\deg x_{\lambda}=2,\deg w=0,\deg\hbar=2
.

Let
T^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}:=\mathbb{C}^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}X^{*}(T)
be the complex dual torus, and
T^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}_{\reg}
be the complement of all the root hyperplanes
\{q^{\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}}=1\}_{\alpha\in R^{+}}
. Here
q^{\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}}
is viewed as a function over
T^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}
, and
q^{\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}}=\prod_{i}(q^{\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}_{i}})^{a_{i}}

\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}=\sum_{i}a_{i}\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}_{i}
. In the present paper, we will mainly take coefficients in

\mathcal{O}(T^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}_{\reg})=\mathbb{C}\big[\,q^{\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}},\tfrac{1}{1-q^{\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}}}\,\big|\,\alpha\in R\,\big].

(2.1)

For any weight
\lambda\in X^{*}(T)
, we will consider the linear function
{p}_{\lambda}
in
\mathcal{O}(\mathfrak{t})

and the differential operator
\partial_{\lambda}
on
\mathcal{O}(T^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}_{\reg})
,
defined respectively by

{p}_{\lambda}(\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}})=\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}\rangle,\qquad\partial_{\lambda}q^{\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}}=\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}\rangle q^{\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}}.

(2.2)

We will also consider actions by the groups

\mathbb{G}:=G\times\mathbb{C}^{*},\quad\mathbb{T}:=T\times\mathbb{C}^{*}.

2.1.
Equivariant cohomology of Springer resolution

The complete flag variety
\mathcal{B}
of
G
parameterizes Borel subalgebras

of
\mathfrak{g}

Let
\mathcal{N}
denote the cone of nilpotent elements in
\mathfrak{g}

with
\mbox{ad}_{x}\in\mbox{End}(\mathfrak{g})

The cotangent bundle

is isomorphic to the subvariety

\tilde{\mathcal{N}}=\{(A,\mathfrak{b})\mid A\in\mathcal{N},A\in\mathfrak{b}\}\subset\mathcal{N}\times\mathcal{B}

via the identification

. The natural projection

, or equivalently the composition

Let

denote the standard Borel subgroup of

, whose Lie algebra is spanned by

and those root spaces parameterized by positive roots. With the identification
\mathcal{B}=G/B
, the cotangent bundle

admits a natural

-action with

\mathcal{B}
by left multiplication and

\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action given by dilation. Precisely, for any
(g,z)\in\mathbb{G}
, the action is given by

(g,z)\cdot(xB,\xi)=(gxB,z^{-1}g^{*}\xi),

where
\xi
is a cotangent vector at

and

is pull back of it to cotangent vector at
gxB

.

Let

\displaystyle H^{*}_{\mathbb{G}}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})

(2.4)

\displaystyle\text{and \quad}H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})

2.2.
Calogero–Moser system

The Calogero–Moser system is a Hamiltonian system in the study of one-dimensional many-body problems. The system was originally written down in its rational version and was generalized to the trigonometric case and the elliptic case by Sutherland and Krichever, respectively (see e.g.
[
Eti07
,
BH18
]
and references therein).

For our purpose, we need the
trigonometric Calogero–Moser system
mostly and it is an integrable system over
\mathcal{O}(T^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}_{\reg}\times\mathfrak{t})[\hbar]
with Hamiltonian defined as follows. For any non-degenerate
W
-invariant quadratic form
C\in\Sym^{2}(\mathfrak{t}^{*})^{W}
, we define the Hamiltonian to be

C(p)-\hbar^{2}\sum_{\alpha>0}\frac{C(\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}},\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}})}{(q^{\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}/2}-q^{-\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}/2})^{2}}\in\mathcal{O}(T^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}_{\reg}\times\mathfrak{t})[\hbar],

(2.8)

where
{p}_{\lambda}
is defined (
2.2
) and naturally viewed as a function in
\mathcal{O}(T^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}_{\reg}\times\mathfrak{t})
.
The trigonometric Calogero–Moser system can be described by a
\mathbb{C}[\hbar]
-linear embedding

\eta_{\CM,\hbar}^{\cl}:\Sym(\mathfrak{t}^{*})^{W}[\hbar]\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}(T^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}_{\reg}\times\mathfrak{t})[\hbar],

(2.9)

whose image consists of Possion commuting functions characterized by

(1)

for any
f\in\Sym(\mathfrak{t}^{*})^{W}
,
f(p)

2.3.
Quantum cohomology

We will study the
A
-equivariant quantum cohomology
QH_{A}^{*}(X)
of a space
X\in\{\mathcal{B},T^{*}\mathcal{B}\}
. Here
A\leq\mathbb{G}
is a complex reductive subgroup. In particular for
X=T^{*}\mathcal{B}
, we require that
A
contains

\{e\}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
, so that the
A
-fixed locus

is always compact.

Note that
G
is simply connected. We have the identifications

\mbox{Span}_{\mathbb{Z}}R^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}=\Hom(\mathbb{C}^{*},T)\simeq H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z}).

Every positive coroot

For each effective curve class

k

\left<\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k}\right>_{0,k,\beta}^{X}=\int_{[\overline{M}_{0,k}(X,\beta)]^{\mathrm{vir}}}\ev^{*}(\gamma_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\gamma_{k}),

[

and
[

For

[

The

H_{A}^{*}(X)

The quantum product

3.
Automorhisms of
QH^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})

Let us define the main objects of this paper.

Definition 3.1

(Quantum Demazure–Lusztig operators)
.

For any
u\in W
, we define the
quantum Demazure–Lusztig operator

T_{u}
to be the
operator on
QH^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})=H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})\otimes\mathcal{O}(T^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}_{\reg})
given by

T_{u}=u\otimes u,

(3.1)

where the first
u
on the right-hand-side is an element of
\mathcal{H}_{\hbar}
, i.e. a usual Demazure–Lusztig operator,
and the second
u
denotes the induced action on
\mathcal{O}(T^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}_{\reg})
from that on
T^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}_{\reg}
, namely

u(q^{\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}}):=q^{u\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}}
.

This section is devoted to the following surprising theorem, which will play an important role in finding the presentation of
QH^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})
in Section
4
.

Theorem 3.2
.

For any
u\in W
and
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in QH_{\mathbb{T}}^{*}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})
,

T_{u}(\gamma_{1}*\gamma_{2})=T_{u}(\gamma_{1})*T_{u}(\gamma_{2}).

(3.2)

As a result,
T_{u}
is a ring automorphism with respect to the quantum product.

Example 3.3
.

Consider the case
G=SL(2,\mathbb{C})
.
Let
s:=s_{\alpha}\in W
be the only simple reflection,
\varpi
be the fundamental weight, and
q:=q^{\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}}
denote the unique primitive effective curve class. Note that
-D_{\varpi}=D_{-\varpi}\in H_{\mathbb{T}}^{*}(T^{*}\mathbb{P}^{1})
is the pullback of the equivariant first Chern class

By Proposition
2.2
(
1

D_{\varpi}*D_{-\varpi}=D_{\varpi}D_{-\varpi}+\hbar\frac{q}{1-q}(D_{\varpi}-D_{-\varpi}-\hbar).

We have

\displaystyle T_{s}(D_{\varpi})*T_{s}(D_{-\varpi})

\displaystyle=(D_{-\varpi}+\hbar)*(D_{\varpi}-\hbar)

\displaystyle=D_{\varpi}D_{-\varpi}+\hbar\frac{1}{1-q}(D_{\varpi}-D_{-\varpi}-\hbar),

\displaystyle T_{s}(D_{\varpi}*D_{-\varpi})

\displaystyle=D_{\varpi}D_{-\varpi}+\hbar\frac{q^{-1}}{1-q^{-1}}(D_{-\varpi}-D_{\varpi}+\hbar)

\displaystyle=D_{\varpi}D_{-\varpi}+\hbar\frac{1}{1-q}(-D_{-\varpi}+D_{\varpi}-\hbar).

Thus

T_{s}(D_{\varpi}*D_{-\varpi})=T_{s}(D_{\varpi})*T_{s}(D_{-\varpi}).

\eta_{\CM,\hbar}^{\cl}\bigg(\sum_{u\in W^{\lambda}}(u\lambda)^{k}\bigg)=\operatorname{tr}(Y(\lambda)^{k})\in\mathcal{O}(T^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}_{\reg}\times\mathfrak{t})[\hbar].

Remark 4.14
.

P. Etingof shared with us in private communication that our matrix
Y(\lambda)
can be viewed as the matrix of the Dunkl operator under the basis
W
of
\mathcal{O}(T^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}_{\reg}\times\mathfrak{t})[\hbar]\rtimes\mathbb{C}[W]
, provided that
\lambda
is strictly dominant (i.e.,
W_{\lambda}
is trivial). In the rational case (analogous to our case), this method yields the known explicit formula for the rational Calogero–Moser map in both classical and quantum cases, obtained based on Lax pairs
[
SS93
]
. In the trigonometric case (our case), Corollary
4.13
can be translated to the following trace-free formula:

\operatorname{tr}\left(\sum_{w\in W}\Dun_{w\lambda}^{k}-\sum_{w\in W}w\Dun_{\lambda}^{k}w^{-1}\right)=0.

(4.11)

Recall that
\Dun_{\lambda}
is the classical trigonometric Dunkl operator defined in (
2.10
).
It is worth noting that the trigonometric Dunkl operators and Weyl group elements generate the degenerate Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\hbar}
, and thus, unlike the rational case, the two sums in (
4.11
) are not equal.
To relate our result to the above formula, we need to note that

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}(Y(\lambda)^{k})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}(\Dun_{\lambda}^{k})=\frac{1}{|W|}\operatorname{tr}\left(\sum_{w\in W}w\Dun_{\lambda}^{k}w^{-1}\right).

On the other hand, by extending
[
EFMV11
, Lemma 2.2]
to the trigonometric case, we can conclude that
\sum_{w\in W}\Dun_{w\lambda}^{k}
is the scalar matrix of
\eta_{\CM,\hbar}^{\cl}\left(\sum_{w\in W}(w\lambda)^{k}\right)
. Hence,

\displaystyle\eta_{\CM,\hbar}^{\cl}\left(\sum_{w\in W}(w\lambda)^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|W|}\operatorname{tr}\left(\sum_{w\in W}\Dun_{w\lambda}^{k}\right).

Corollary
4.13
is thus equivalent to (
4.11
).
It is interesting to ask for a direct proof of (
4.11
).
Additionally, it should be noted that the trace-free formula (
4.11
) is not valid for the quantum case.

In practical computation, rather than substituting square matrices of size
|W|
into a symmetric polynomial, our matrix is small and involves only matrix powers. This reduces the computational complexity significantly.

Example 4.15

(Hamiltonian)
.

Let
\lambda\in X^{*}(T)_{+}
be strictly dominant such that
W_{\lambda}
is trivial.
By direct computations, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}\big(Y(\lambda)^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in W}{p}_{w\lambda}^{2}+\hbar^{2}\sum_{w\in W}\sum_{\alpha>0}\left<\lambda,\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}\right>^{2}\frac{1}{\big(1-q^{w\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}}\big)\big(1-q^{-w\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}}\big)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in W}{p}_{w\lambda}^{2}-\hbar^{2}\sum_{\alpha>0}\bigg(\sum_{w\in W}\left<w\lambda,\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}\right>^{2}\bigg)\frac{1}{\big(q^{\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}/2}-q^{-\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}/2}\big)^{2}}.

Let
C\in\Sym^{2}(\mathfrak{t}^{*})
be a non-degenerate quadratic form, which is unique up to a scalar.
Thus,

0\neq\sum_{w\in W}(w\lambda)^{2}=c_{\lambda}\cdot C\in\Sym^{2}(\mathfrak{t}^{*}),

for some constant
c_{\lambda}
.
Then we can rewrite the above by

c_{\lambda}\cdot\left(C({p})-\hbar^{2}\sum_{\alpha>0}\frac{C(\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}},\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}})}{\big(q^{\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}/2}-q^{-\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}/2}\big)^{2}}\right).

Corollary
4.13
shows

\eta_{\CM,\hbar}^{\cl}(C)=C({p})-\hbar^{2}\sum_{\alpha>0}\frac{C(\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}},\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}})}{\big(q^{\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}/2}-q^{-\alpha^{\mathchoice{\vee}{\vee}{\vee}{\mathsf{v}}}/2}\big)^{2}},

which is the Hamiltonian of the classical Calogero–Moser system (
2.8
).

Appendix A
Toda limit

The equivariant quantum cohomology of
\mathcal{B}
can be obtained from that of
T^{*}\mathcal{B}
, by taking a Toda limit as follows.

Definition A.1
.

The
Toda limit
of a class

\gamma\in QH^{*}_{\mathbb{G}}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})

is defined to be

\lim\nolimits^{\mathrm{td}}\gamma=\lim_{\hbar\to\infty}\gamma(\hbar,\hbar^{-2\rho}q)\in QH_{G}^{*}(\mathcal{B}),

where
\gamma(\hbar,\hbar^{-2\rho}q)
denotes the class obtained from

When

\gamma\in QH^{*}_{T}(\mathcal{B})
is also called the Toda limit.

Proposition A.2

(
[
BMO11
, Proposition 8.1]
)
.

For any classes
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in QH^{*}_{\mathbb{G}}(T^{*}\mathcal{B})
whose Toda limits both converge, we have

\big(\lim\nolimits^{\mathrm{td}}\gamma_{1}\big)*\big(\lim\nolimits^{\mathrm{td}}\gamma_{2}\big)=\lim\nolimits^{\mathrm{td}}(\gamma_{1}*\gamma_{2})

These also hold if we replace
\mathbb{G}
by

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi}J(\pi)V(\pi),

with the sum over all matchings
\pi
over
\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Proof.

By the expansion in polynomials in
\chi_{i}
’s, the determinant is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{A}{\chi}_{a_{1}}\cdots{\chi}_{a_{s}}\det\left[\begin{matrix}0&\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{b_{1}}/q_{b_{2}}}&\cdots&\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{b_{1}}/q_{b_{t}}}\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{b_{2}}/q_{b_{1}}}&0&\cdots&\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{b_{2}}/q_{b_{t}}}\\[4.30554pt]
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{b_{d}}/q_{b_{1}}}&\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{b_{d}}/q_{b_{2}}}&\cdots&0\end{matrix}\right]

where the sum goes over all subsets
A=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}\}
of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
with complement
\{b_{1}<\cdots<b_{t}\}
.
Note that by Lemma
B.2

\displaystyle\quad\det\left[\begin{matrix}0&\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{b_{1}}/q_{b_{2}}}&\cdots&\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{b_{1}}/q_{b_{t}}}\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{b_{2}}/q_{b_{1}}}&0&\cdots&\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{b_{2}}/q_{b_{t}}}\\[4.30554pt]
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{b_{t}}/q_{b_{1}}}&\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{b_{t}}/q_{b_{2}}}&\cdots&0\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle=\hbar^{t}q_{b_{1}}\cdots q_{b_{t}}\det\left[\begin{matrix}0&\frac{1}{q_{b_{2}}-q_{b_{1}}}&\cdots&\frac{1}{q_{b_{t}}-q_{b_{1}}}\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{1}{q_{b_{1}}-q_{b_{2}}}&0&\cdots&\frac{1}{q_{b_{t}}-q_{b_{2}}}\\[4.30554pt]
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{1}{q_{b_{1}}-q_{b_{t}}}&\frac{1}{q_{b_{2}}-q_{b_{t}}}&\cdots&0\end{matrix}\right]=\sum_{\pi}V(\pi),

where the sum is over perfect matchings
\pi
over
\{b_{1}<\ldots<b_{t}\}
. We can extend
\pi
to a (not necessarily perfect) matching over
\{1,\ldots,n\}
by setting
\pi(a_{i})=a_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,s
.
Thus the determinant equals

\sum J(\pi)V(\pi)

with sum over all matchings of
\pi
over
\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.2
.

Similarly to the proof of Lemma
B.3
, the coefficient of
y^{n-k}
in
\det\big(y1_{n}+M(\chi)\big)
is

\displaystyle\sum_{K}\det\left[\begin{matrix}\chi_{c_{1}}&\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{c_{1}}/q_{c_{2}}}&\cdots&\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{c_{1}}/q_{c_{k}}}\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{c_{2}}/q_{c_{1}}}&\chi_{c_{2}}&\cdots&\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{c_{2}}/q_{c_{k}}}\\[4.30554pt]

\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{c_{k}}/q_{c_{1}}}&\frac{\hbar}{1-q_{c_{k}}/q_{c_{2}}}&\cdots&\chi_{c_{k}}\end{matrix}\right]

where the sum goes over all subsets

By Lemma

, it is equal to

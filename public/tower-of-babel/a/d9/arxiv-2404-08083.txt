On the Class 𝒮 \mathcal{S} Origin of Spindle Solutions

Abstract
We analyse the backreacted geometry corresponding to a stack of M5-branes wrapped on a spindle, with a view towards precision tests of the dual 𝒩 = 1 \mathcal{N}=1 superconformal field theory. We carefully study the singular loci of the uplifted geometry and show that these correspond to 𝐂 3 / 𝐙 n \mathbf{C}^{3}/\mathbf{Z}_{n} conical singularities. Therefore, these solutions present one of the first explicit realisations of honest locally 𝒩 = 1 \mathcal{N}=1 preserving punctures in class 𝒮 \mathcal{S} . Additionally we study the symmetries and anomalies of the dual field theory through anomaly inflow and compute a variety of holographic observables including dimensions of BPS operators. This work paves the way for advancements in the study and identification of the precise dual field theories.

Placing a supersymmetric field theory on a curved background generically completely breaks supersymmetry. However, one can preserve supersymmetry by coupling the theory to a collection of (rigid supersymmetry) background fields

Given this novel mechanism for preserving supersymmetry a natural question is:
What is the dual field theory?

Doing so allows us to scrutinise the internal space and give a detailed description of the geometry. Unlike the analogous M2-, D3- solutions

The analogous D4/D8-system also admits singularities in the uplift.

We carefully demonstrate that each orbifold singularity of the spindle results in a pair of orbifold singularities in the uplifted geometries, locally taking the form
\mathop{\rm AdS}\nolimits_{5}\times\mathbf{C}^{3}/\mathbf{Z}_{n_{\pm}}
where the precise form of the orbifold action is determined by the orbibundle data of the seven-dimensional solution. Similar to the
\mathcal{N}=2
class
\mathcal{S}

Indeed, this can be seen from expanding the M-theory three-form potential, or equivalently the four-form flux, on the internal cycles of the manifold. Analysing the crepant resolutions of such orbifold singularities, following Ito-Reid
[
59
]
, we show that there are additional localised compact cycles dual to the exceptional divisors of the blow-up that give rise to vector fields for the additional global symmetries. We carefully analyse the resulting flavour symmetries for a variety of orbifold actions and describe the precise form of the possibly non-abelian flavour symmetry as well as the flavour anomalies

\mathcal{N}=1

In addition to finding the global symmetry, we carefully study the anomalies of the four-dimensional SCFT through anomaly inflow by integrating the twelve-form characteristic class

[

Finally, we compute the conformal dimensions of a variety of chiral primaries corresponding to M2-branes wrapping calibrated cycles as well as study the allowed probe M5 branes allowing us to probe a rich spectrum of information of the dual SCFT.

Outlook

In this work we have carefully analysed the local geometry describing the singular loci of the uplifted solutions corresponding to M5-branes wrapped on a spindle. We found that these singular loci describe locally
\mathcal{N}=1
punctures and explained how to extract a variety of observables of the dual SCFTs from such singularities.

The type of singularities we study is far from the most general class. In order to preserve
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetry such singularities are restricted to be locally
\mathbf{C}^{3}/\Gamma
, where
\Gamma
is a finite subgroup of
\mathrm{SU}(3)
. Such subgroups have been classified
[

,
62
,
63
,
64

and include more options then the abelian orbifold with
\Gamma=\mathbf{Z}_{n}
that arise in this work. There are more general abelian orbifolds with
\Gamma=\mathbf{Z}_{m}\times\mathbf{Z}_{n}
as well as a variety of non-abelian finite subgroups
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{SU}(3)
. It would be interesting to investigate whether we can find generalisations of the solutions described in this work along the lines of
[
5
,
37
,
38
,
40
]
. To make progress in this direction it may be beneficial to embed the solutions found here in the framework of
[
9
]
and look for a generalisation of the
\mathcal{N}=2
electrostatics picture allowing one to generalise the solutions to the relevant Monge-Ampére equation.

Further, having described such general punctures on the spindle a natural question is whether we can construct solutions describing M5-branes on higher genus Riemann surfaces and include these more general punctures. Similar to the
\mathcal{N}=2
case, finding global solutions with a variety of punctures is prohibitively hard. However, using the newly developed tools of equivariant localisation in supergravity
[
65
,
66
,
67
,
68
,
69
,
70
]
it should nonetheless be possible to extract a wealth of information from a careful local analysis of the relevant singularities.

Finally, in this note we studied in detail the internal geometry corresponding to the M5 spindle solutions. We shed new light on a variety of observables and clarified various aspects of the dual SCFTs. However, it would be very interesting to exactly determine the dual SCFT with said properties. The variety of punctures described holographically could have a dual description as
\mathcal{N}=1
quiver tails which could be added to SCFTs such as BBBW and its generalisations providing a new larger set of
\mathcal{N}=1
class
\mathcal{S}
SCFTs. We hope to come back to these question in the future.

3.2  Flux Quantisation

In order for the backgrounds described above to be consistent backgrounds of M-theory, we impose that the
G_{4}
flux is quantised along any four-cycle in
\mathcal{M}_{4}
.

The first non-trivial cycle we consider, which we denote by
\mathcal{C}_{4}
, has the topology of
S^{4}
and can be obtained as the
S^{4}
fibre over a generic point on the spindle. This four-cycle can be obtained by taking a slice of the prism perpendicular to the
w
-axis in Figure
2
and integrating along the both
{\rm d}\phi_{i}
circles. Using the expression for the four-form flux above, we have the following quantisation condition

\frac{1}{(2\pi\ell_{p})^{3}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{4}}G_{4}=N\in\mathbf{Z}

(3.13)

This flux can be interpreted as the number of M5-branes wrapping the spindle. As a consequence, we find the length scale
L
of the uplifted solution is quantised as

L=(\pi N)^{\frac{1}{3}}\ell_{p}\,.

(3.14)

Next, consider the four-cycles
\mathcal{D}_{4}^{\pm}
obtained by going to one of the orbifold points at the poles of the spindle. The fibres over these points take the form
S^{4}/\mathbf{Z}_{n_{\pm}}
. Similar to the above we can consider the flux quantisation through the fibres,

\frac{1}{(2\pi\ell_{p})^{3}}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{4}^{\pm}}G_{4}=\frac{N}{n_{\pm}}\,.

(3.15)

The cycles
\mathcal{D}_{4}^{\pm}
are not independent from the cycle
\mathcal{C}_{4}
but instead we have the homology relations

\left[\mathcal{C}_{4}\right]=n_{\pm}\left[\mathcal{D}_{4}^{\pm}\right]\,.

(3.16)

This agrees with the analysis of
[
67
]
using equivariant localisation.

4  Wrapped M2- and M5-branes

Probe M5- and M2-branes wrapping cycles of the internal manifold give rise to an important set of protected data in the dual field theory. Probe BPS M2-branes wrapping calibrated two-cycles correspond to chiral primaries in the dual SCFTs
[
81
]
, while the probe BPS M5-branes can be understood as parametrising a class of supersymmetric deformations of the four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=1
SCFT. In this section we study the possible embeddings of BPS probe branes within the spindle solution. We will find that there is a richer structure of probe branes that may be embedded in comparison with the BBBW solutions
[
23
]
as studied in
[
12
]
. In particular, on top of the BPS M5-branes which deform the theory by changing/adding a puncture, as in
[
12
]
, there are additional M5 branes wrapping the isometry circle of the spindle. These are located away from the conical defects and provide a new set of codimension-1 operators in the 6d
(2,0)
which deform the resulting four-dimensional theory. One can attribute the presence of these additional probe M5-branes to a larger moduli space for the probe M5-branes in the setup and therefore a richer structure in the dual SCFT.

The analysis of
[
12
]
was performed by embedding their setup into the GMSW framework, and exemplified with the BBBW
[
23
]
and
\mathcal{N}=1
Maldacena–Nunez solutions
[
27
]
. After embedding our solution into this general framework, which is detailed in Appendix
B
, the analysis can be repeated verbatim. However, due to the richer setup our results include a larger set of (new) probe branes.

4.1  Probe M5 branes

In order for a probe M5-brane to preserve the symmetries of our solutions it is forced to wrap all of AdS
5
together with a one-cycle in the internal space. Let
\{\sigma^{i}\}
with

be the coordinates in AdS
5
and let
\tau=\sigma^{5}
be the proper length parameter along the one-cycle within
M_{6}
wrapped by the probe M5-brane. The calibration condition for a probe M5-brane fully wrapping AdS
5
, written using the canonical

), is

\mathrm{e}^{-3\lambda}{\rm d}s_{4}\Big|_{{\rm d}\tau}=0\,,\qquad\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-3\lambda}}{\cos\zeta}{\rm d}y\Big|_{{\rm d}\tau}=0\,,\qquad\frac{\sin\zeta\cos\zeta}{3}({\rm d}\psi+P)\Big|_{{\rm d}\tau}=0\,,

where
{\rm d}s^{2}_{4}
is the (

. The component

of the induced metric is

g_{\tau\tau}=\mathrm{e}^{-6\lambda}\left[{\rm d}s_{4}\Big|_{{\rm d}\tau}\right]^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-6\lambda}}{\cos^{2}\zeta}\left[{\rm d}y|_{{\rm d}\tau}\right]^{2}+\frac{\cos^{2}\zeta}{9}\left[({\rm d}\psi+P)\Big|_{{\rm d}\tau}\right]^{2}\,,

(4.2)

which we require to be non-zero. The only option, giving a non-trivial induced metric

, and satisfying the calibration condition is to set
\sin\zeta=0
, which is equivalent to
y=0
. This immediately satisfies the second condition in (
4.1

y
as well as the third one. It remains to satisfy the first condition in (
4.1
). From the embedding of the spindle solution in the GMSW classification we have that the coordinate
y
is given by
y=w\cos\xi
and therefore we have that
y=0
if and only if
\cos\xi=0
. This then places us at the equator of the
S^{4}
which has the topology of a three-sphere.
The location of the probe M5-branes is then at a point on the diagonal face of figure
2
. For ease of reference let us denote this face by
\mathcal{F}_{2}
.

As the metric on
M_{6}
is toric, it admits a

action which is fibered over the prism in figure

\mathcal{F}_{2}
the M5-brane will then break a
\mathrm{U}(1)^{2}\subset\mathrm{U}(1)^{3}

\mathcal{F}_{2}
where an additional cycle shrinks and therefore this symmetry is not broken by the presence of the probe brane.

Once we have restricted to the equator of the four-sphere, it remains to satisfy the first of calibration condition, (
4.1

{\rm d}s^{2}_{4}
, which we derive in appendix
B
in terms of the canonical GMSW form. These vielbein are adapted to give the Kähler form on the base in the canonical way. The pertinent vielbein are the ones involving the angular coordinates on the four-dimensional base, which evaluated at
y=0

\begin{split}e^{2}\Big|_{y=0}&=\frac{\cos\theta\sin\theta}{\sqrt{\Theta(w,\theta)}}\left(h_{1}(w)\frac{{\rm d}\phi_{1}}{{\rm d}\tau}-h_{2}(w)\frac{{\rm d}\phi_{2}}{{\rm d}\tau}\right){\rm d}\tau\,,\\

(4.3)

To solve (

Generic point in
\mathcal{F}_{2}

At a generic point

\frac{{\rm d}\phi_{1}}{{\rm d}\tau}=\frac{h_{2}(w_{*})}{h_{1}(w_{*})}\frac{{\rm d}\phi_{2}}{{\rm d}\tau}\,,\qquad\frac{{\rm d}\phi_{1}}{{\rm d}\tau}=-\tan^{2}\theta_{*}\frac{{\rm d}\phi_{2}}{{\rm d}\tau}\,.

North and south pole of

Spindle fixed points

The remaining possibility is to put the probe M5-branes at the poles of the spindle

For rational spindle SCFTs, we may wrap an additional probe M5-brane along a non-trivial cycle within

To summarise, we have found that there are two distinct types of probe M5-branes. One class wraps the spindle circle and the other is located at the poles of the spindle and wraps a one-cycle inside the equator of
S^{4}

5  Symmetries and anomalies

After carefully scrutinising the internal geometry and identifying the precise form of the singularities in the eleven-dimensional geometry, we proceed to analyse the global symmetries and ’t Hooft anomalies of the dual SCFTs. The eleven-dimensional geometry admits a
\mathfrak{u}(1)^{3}
isometry algebra dual to the superconformal
R
symmetry and two additional
\mathfrak{u}(1)
flavour symmetries of the dual SCFT. In addition, the dual SCFT enjoys flavour symmetries associated to the
\mathbf{C}^{3}/\mathbf{Z}_{n_{\pm}}
orbifold singularities.

The analysis of the symmetries as well as their ’t Hooft anomalies can be performed systematically, following the anomaly inflow methods developed in
[
10
,
84
,
85
,
86
,
87
]
, building on the work of
[
60
,
88
]
. This analysis employs an auxiliary twelve-manifold
\mathcal{M}_{12}
, realised as an
\mathcal{M}_{6}
fibration over a closed six-manifold
\mathcal{N}_{6}
, i.e.
\mathcal{M}_{6}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{N}_{12}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{6}
. The space
\mathcal{N}_{6}
is interpreted as a fibration over the external spacetime on which the anomaly polynomial lives. In particular, we will obtain the anomaly polynomial of the dual four-dimensional SCFT by integrating the twelve-form
\mathcal{I}_{12}
over the internal space
\mathcal{M}_{6}
.

\mathcal{I}_{6}=\int_{\mathcal{M}_{6}}\mathcal{I}_{12}\,.

(5.1)

Moreover, through the usual descent procedure, the six-or twelve-form anomaly polynomial is related to a five- or eleven-form which can be identified with the topological couplings in the relevant five- or eleven-dimensional supergravity theory,

\mathcal{I}_{d+2}={\rm d}\mathcal{I}^{(0)}_{d+1}\,,\qquad\qquad S^{\rm(top)}_{d+1}=\int_{\mathcal{N}_{d}}\mathcal{I}^{(0)}_{d+1}\,.

(5.2)

The twelve-form
\mathcal{I}_{12}
was described in
[

88
]

\mathcal{I}_{12}=\frac{1}{6}E_{4}\wedge E_{4}\wedge E_{4}+E_{4}\wedge X_{8}\,,

(5.3)

X_{8}=\frac{1}{192}\left[p_{1}(T\mathcal{M}_{11})^{2}-4\,p_{2}(T\mathcal{M}_{11})\right]\,.

(5.4)

The four-form
E_{4}
appearing in (

\mathcal{N}_{12}
with integral periods on all four-cycles in
\mathcal{N}_{12}

flux that supports the

5.2  Flavour symmetries from punctures

In addition to the flavour symmetries discussed so far, arising from isometries of the internal manifold, the dual SCFT can have baryonic zero-form flavour symmetries arising from non-trivial two-cycles in the internal geometry. Integrating the M-theory three-form on such cycles gives rise to vector potentials in five dimensional gauged supergravity sitting in so-called Betti multiplets. More generally, wrapping the M-theory three-form on non-trivial
p
-cycles in the internal manifold can give rise to
(2-p)
-form symmetries in the dual SCFT. In our setup, such additional non-trivial cycles arise from the blow-up of the
\mathbf{C}^{3}/\mathbf{Z}_{n_{\pm}}
orbifold singularities.

In order to understand the resulting flavour symmetry we first will have a look at the resolutions of generic
\mathbf{C}^{3}/\Gamma
orbifolds, where
\Gamma
is a finite subgroup of
\mathrm{SU}(3)
, following the work of Ito and Reid
[
59
]
. In our context, the requirement that
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{SU}(3)
can be physically motivated by the fact that this class of orbifolds is Calabi-Yau and therefore gives rise to at least
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetry in the dual SCFT. Indeed, this requirement has already been derived in seven dimensions when demanding the existence of a globally well-defined Killing spinor.

Denote the conjugacy classes of
\Gamma
by
\gamma_{i}
. All the group elements in a given conjugacy class have the same eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
, which can be written as

\lambda_{i}=\left\{\exp\left(2\pi{\rm i}\,\frac{a_{i}}{r_{i}}\right)\,,\,\exp\left(2\pi{\rm i}\,\frac{b_{i}}{r_{i}}\right)\,,\,\exp\left(2\pi{\rm i}\,\frac{c_{i}}{r_{i}}\right)\right\}\,,

(5.11)

where
r_{i}
is the order of the elements in
\gamma_{i}
and
0\leq a_{i},b_{i},c_{i}\leq r_{i}
. Define the age of the conjugacy class as the integer
{\rm age}(\gamma_{i})=\frac{1}{r_{i}}(a_{i}+b_{i}+c_{i})
and denote the set of conjugacy classes of age
m
by
\Gamma_{m}
. Ito and Reid proved that for each orbifold
\mathbf{C}^{3}/\Gamma
there exists a crepant resolutions

\pi:\widetilde{X}\rightarrow\mathbf{C}^{3}/\mathbf{Z}_{k}\,,

(5.12)

such that
b_{2m}(\widetilde{X})=|\Gamma_{m}|
and
b_{3}(\widetilde{X})=0
.

Coming back to our setup, the exceptional divisors
S_{\alpha}\in H_{4}(\widetilde{X},\mathbf{R})
from the blow-up are dual to the compactly supported 2-forms
\widetilde{\omega}_{(2)\alpha}\in H^{2}_{c}(\widetilde{X},\mathbf{R})
which can be thought of as forms localised at the singular points in our original geometry. We can therefore consider the following additional terms in the expansion of
E_{4}
.

E^{\rm punc.}_{4}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{b_{4}(\widetilde{X})}\frac{\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}}{2\pi}\wedge\widetilde{\omega}_{(2)\alpha}\,.

(5.13)

where
\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}={\rm d}\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}
are external gauge fields for the resulting flavour symmetries. In the lower dimensional gauged supergravity these therefore give rise to massless gauge fields in the Cartan of the gauge group, where the rank of the gauge group is given by
r=b_{4}(\widetilde{X})
. In various cases, the gauge symmetry gets enhanced to a non-abelian symmetry at the SCFT point.

In particular, one can wrap M2-branes on rational curves inside a compact divisor
S_{\beta}
. These curves can be described by a geometric ruling of the divisor by rational curves
f_{\beta}
, i.e.
\mathbf{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
s. In the dual QFT (in the Coulomb phase) such wrapped M2-branes give rise to charged particles, i.e. W-bosons. The charge of the particle under the Cartan corresponding to a divisor
S_{\alpha}
is given by

S_{\alpha}\cdot f_{\beta}=-C_{\alpha\beta}\,,

(5.14)

where the left hand side denotes the intersection number in
\widetilde{X}
and can be computed from the triple intersection numbers
S_{\beta}^{2}\cdot S^{\alpha}
. The mass of the particle is determined by the symplectic volume of
f_{\alpha}
such that in the singular limit where the resolution cycles shrink these particles become the massless W-bosons enhancing the
\mathfrak{u}(1)^{r}
symmetry to a possibly non-abelian
\mathfrak{g}
symmetry. In particular, the matrix
C_{\alpha\beta}
reproduces the entries of Cartan matrix of the flavour symmetry of the dual SCFT. In addition to providing a holographic description of the flavour symmetry associated to the punctures the additional terms in the expansion of
E_{4}
give rise to Chern-Simons terms in the five-dimensional supergravity theory which describe the (mixed) ’t Hooft anomalies of the dual SCFT which are therefore controlled by the triple intersection numbers of compact divisors
k_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=S_{\alpha}\cdot S_{\beta}\cdot S_{\gamma}
. Furthermore, the crepant resolutions do not have odd dimensional cycles, so they do not contribute to the one-form symmetry of the dual SCFTs.

Hence, the 5d theory obtained after compactification is a supergravity theory. For this reason we are solely interested in the compact cycles resulting in gauge symmetries in the bulk. 5.3  Anomaly inflow

Having constructed the equivariantly closed form
E_{4}
and analysed the additional flavour symmetries from the punctures, it is now an algorithmic task to compute
\mathcal{I}_{12}
and integrate along
\mathcal{M}_{6}
. Writing
E_{4}
as,

E_{4}=G_{4}^{(g)}+\frac{\mathcal{F}^{a}}{2\pi}\wedge\omega_{(2)a}^{(g)}+\frac{\mathcal{F}^{a}}{2\pi}\wedge\frac{\mathcal{F}^{b}}{2\pi}\,\omega_{(0)ab}\,,

(5.16)

where the index
a,b\in\{I\,,\alpha\}
collectively run over the flavour symmetries from isometries as well as two-cycles in the internal manifold. Substituting this into the expression for
\mathcal{I}_{12}
we find that the
E_{4}^{3}
term becomes,

\frac{1}{6}E_{4}^{3}=\frac{\mathcal{F}^{a}}{2\pi}\wedge\frac{\mathcal{F}^{b}}{2\pi}\wedge\frac{\mathcal{F}^{c}}{2\pi}\wedge\left(\widetilde{G}_{4}\wedge\omega_{(2)a}\omega_{(0)bc}+\frac{1}{6}\omega_{(2)a}\wedge\omega_{(2)b}\wedge\omega_{(2)c}\right)+\cdots\,,

(5.17)

where the dots denote terms that do not have the appropriate number of legs along the internal space and hence will not contribute to the anomaly polynomial of the resulting four-dimensional theory. Similarly, the higher derivative contributions from
E_{4}\wedge X_{8}
give rise to the following contributions,

E_{4}\wedge X_{8}=-\frac{1}{96}\frac{\mathcal{F}^{a}}{2\pi}\wedge p_{1}(T\mathop{\rm AdS}\nolimits_{5})\wedge\omega_{(2)a}\wedge p_{1}(T\mathcal{M}_{6})+\cdots\,,

(5.18)

where we used the splitting principle to rewrite (
5.4
) in terms of the Pontryagin classes of the internal resp.
\mathop{\rm AdS}\nolimits
space. In order to obtain the anomaly polynomial of the resulting four-dimensional theory all that is left is to integrate these expressions over the internal space
\mathcal{M}_{6}
. The resulting expression is given by

\mathcal{I}_{6}=\frac{1}{6}k_{abc}\,c_{1}(\mathcal{F}^{a})\wedge c_{1}(\mathcal{F}^{b})\wedge c_{1}(\mathcal{F}^{c})-\frac{1}{24}k_{a}\,c_{1}(\mathcal{F}^{a})\wedge p_{1}(T\mathop{\rm AdS}\nolimits_{5})\,,

(5.19)

where
k_{abc}
and
k_{a}
are defined as

\displaystyle k_{abc}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}_{6}}\left(\omega_{(2)a}\wedge\omega_{(2)b}\wedge\omega_{(2)c}+6\widetilde{G}_{4}\wedge\omega_{(2)a}\omega_{(0)bc}\right)\,,

(5.20)

\displaystyle k_{a}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{6}}\omega_{(2)a}\wedge p_{1}(T\mathcal{M}_{6})\,,

(5.21)

and the terms proportional to
\widetilde{G}_{4}
vanish identically. Comparing this with the anomaly polynomial of a generic four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=1
SCFT,

\mathcal{I}_{6}=\frac{8}{27}\left(5a-3c\right)c_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{R})^{3}+\frac{2}{3}\left(c-a\right)c_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{R})p_{1}\left(T\mathcal{M}_{4}\right)\,,

(5.22)

the above expressions reduce to the well-known expressions for the conformal anomalies
a
and
c
,

a=\frac{3}{32}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{6}}\left(3\,k_{RRR}-k_{R}\right)\,,\qquad\quad c=\frac{1}{32}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{6}}\left(9\,k_{RRR}+5\,k_{R}\right)\,.

(5.23)

At leading order in the large
N
expansion we have
a\simeq c
and it follows from (
5.10
) above that our expression reduces precisely to

a\simeq\frac{N^{3}}{128\pi^{3}}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{6}}\mathrm{e}^{9\lambda}\,{\rm vol}_{\mathcal{M}_{6}}\,,

(5.24)

which agrees with the expression obtained in
[
81
]
. Note that with the choice of
E_{4}
introduced above one obtains the integrand up to total derivatives only. The end result after integration is obviously independent of such terms. We can make this more explicit by explicitly performing the equivariant integration of
E_{4}^{3}
over
\mathcal{M}_{6}
. Indeed, in line with the results in
[
65
,
67
]
we find that the leading order central charge is given by

a=\frac{N^{3}}{2^{5}3^{4}}\sum_{F_{0}}\frac{1}{d_{F_{0}}}\frac{y^{3}}{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{3}}\,.

(5.25)

This sum goes over the four fixed orbifold points where the weights are given in (
3.10
). Hence, the resulting expression for the conformal
a
-anomaly manifestly agrees with
[
32
,
65
,
67
]
. At subleading order in
N
,
a
and
c
typically differ. From the anomaly polynomial above we can read off that

a-c=\frac{N}{128\pi}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{6}}\omega_{(2)R}\wedge p_{1}(T\mathcal{M}_{6})\,,

(5.26)

which can be computed by direct integration or more elegantly using equivariant localisation. The flavour central charges
k_{abc}
resulting from the isometries of the internal manifold can be computed by direct (equivariant) integration after substituting the relevant forms from (
5.10
). The
k_{\phi_{i}\phi_{j}\phi_{k}}
are trivially vanishing. In addition, in line with
a
-maximisation we find that
\partial_{s}a(s)\simeq k_{RR\phi_{i}}=0
and
\partial^{2}_{s}a(s)\simeq k_{R\phi_{i}\phi_{j}}<0
. The computation of the flavour central charges for the flavours corresponding to the puncture can be computed as the triple intersection number

k_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=S_{\alpha}\cdot S_{\beta}\cdot S_{\gamma}\,,

(5.27)

which can be read off from the toric diagram as instructed above. Finally, we can consider the mixed flavour central charges between the R-symmetry or flavour symmetries from isometries and the flavour symmetries from the puncture. Since the puncture divisors are localised at the puncture location and provide a basis of harmonic two-forms one should expand the two form corresponding to the R-symmetry and mesonic flavour symmetries in terms of the localised cycles at the puncture location. After this one can compute these mixed flavour central charges in a similar manner as a linear combination of intersection numbers. However, we do not pursue this computation in the present work. Similarly, we leave the computation of the
k_{a}
open for future research endeavours. Note however that, as can be observed from
[
32
,
65
,
67
]
, in all anomalies and central charges related to the internal symmetries, the dependence on
m_{\pm}^{(i)}
can be absorbed in the fluxes
p_{i}
. On the other hand, the flavour anomalies for the additional global symmetries arising from the four orbifold singularities do contain dependence on the
m_{\pm}^{(i)}
. This is natural as we expect these anomalies to explicitly depend on the specific choice of punctures.

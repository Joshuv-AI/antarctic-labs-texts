Mutation graph of support τ \tau -tilting modules over a skew-gentle algebra Thanks: The work was supported by National Natural Science Foundation of China (Grants No. 11801297, 11671221).
Ping He, Yu Zhou, Bin Zhu

Abstract
. Let 𝒟 \mathcal{D} be a Hom-finite, Krull-Schmidt, 2-Calabi-Yau triangulated category with a rigid object R R . Let Λ = End 𝒟 ⁡ R \Lambda=\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{D}}R be the endomorphism algebra of R R . We introduce the notion of mutation of maximal rigid objects in the two-term subcategory R ∗ R ⁡ [ 1 ] R\ast R[1] via exchange triangles, which is shown to be compatible with mutation of support τ \tau -tilting Λ \Lambda -modules. In the case that 𝒟 \mathcal{D} is the cluster category arising from a punctured marked surface, it is shown that the graph of mutations of support τ \tau -tilting Λ \Lambda -modules is isomorphic to the graph of flips of certain collections of tagged arcs on the surface, which is moreover proved to be connected. As a direct consequence, the mutation graph of support τ \tau -tilting modules over a skew-gentle algebra is connected.



Introduction

Cluster algebras were introduced by Fomin and Zelevinsky
[
FZ
]
around 2000. The geometric aspect of cluster theory was explored and developed by Fomin, Shapiro and Thurston
[
FST
]
and Labardini-Fragoso
[
LF1
]
, where they construct a quiver with potential
[
DWZ
]
from any triangulation of a marked surface. On the other hand, cluster categories of acyclic quivers were introduced by Buan, Marsh, Reineke, Reiten and Todorov
[
BMRRT
]
in order to categorify cluster algebras, which were generalized later by Amiot
[
A1
]
to cluster categories of quivers with potential.

The indecomposable objects in the cluster category from a marked surface without punctures are classified via curves by Brüstle and Zhang
[
BZ
]
, where the Auslander-Reiten translation is realized by the rotation. The dimension of
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
between certain indecomposable objects is shown by Zhang, Zhou and Zhu
[
ZZZ
]
to equal the intersection number between the corresponding curves, and the middle terms between such extensions are explicitly described by Canakci and Schroll
[
CS
]
via smoothing. The Calabi-Yau reduction introduced by Iyama and Yoshino
[
IY
]
in this case is interpreted via the cutting of surface by Marsh and Palu
[
MP
]
. For the punctured case (with non-empty boundary), Brüstle and Qiu
[
BQ
]
realize the Auslander-Reiten translation via the tagged rotation, Qiu and Zhou
[
QZ
]
classify certain indecomposable objects via tagged curves and show the equality between the dimension of
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
and the intersection number, and Amiot and Plamondon
[
AP
]
give another approach via group actions and orbifolds.

The Jacobian algebra of the quiver with potential associated to a certain triangulation of a marked surface with non-empty boundary is a skew-gentle algebra with some properties (e.g. Gorenstein dimension at most one)
[
ABCP
,
GLS
,
QZ
]
. Skew-gentle algebras were introduced by Geiß and de la Peña
[
GP
]
, whose indecomposable modules are classified by Bondarenko
[
B
]
, Crawley-Boevey
[
CB
]
and Deng
[
D
]
, and whose morphism spaces are described by Geiß
[
G
]
. In a previous work
[
HZZ
]
, we give a geometric model of the module category of an arbitrary skew-gentle algebra, inspired by the geometric model
[
QZ
]
of cluster categories of punctured marked surfaces and the geometric model of the module categories of gentle algebras given by Baur and Simões
[
BCS
]
. There are also some work on geometric models of the derived categories of gentle/skew-gentle algebras, cf. e.g.
[
HKK
,
LP
,
OPS
,
O
,
APS
,
AB
,
A2
,
LSV
]
.

Adachi, Iyama and Reiten
[
AIR
]
introduced
\tau
-tilting theory to generalize the cluster structure to arbitrary finite dimensional algebras, via mutation of support
\tau
-tilting modules. The support
\tau
-tilting modules have been found to be deeply connected with other contents of representation theory, such as functorially finite torsion classes, 2-term silting objects, cluster tilting objects and immediate t-structures. In contrast to the classical tilting case, where an almost complete tilting module may have exactly one complement, any support
\tau
-tilting module can be always mutated at an arbitrary indecomposable direct summand to obtain a new support
\tau
-tilting module. The exchange graph
\operatorname{EG}(\mathrm{s}\tau\operatorname{-tilt}A)
of support
\tau
-tilting modules of a finite dimensional algebra
A
has (isoclasses of) basic support
\tau
-tilting modules over
A
as vertices and has mutations as edges. One important problem is to count the number of connected components of
\operatorname{EG}(\mathrm{s}\tau\operatorname{-tilt}A)
.

In our previous work
[
HZZ
]
, using a geometric model, we classify support
\tau
-tilting modules of skew-gentle algebras via certain dissections of marked surfaces. In the current paper, the main result is the connectedness of the exchange graph
\operatorname{EG}(\mathrm{s}\tau\operatorname{-tilt}A)
for
A
a skew-gentle algebra. For this, we need to generalize the geometric model from skew-gentle algebras to the endomorphism algebras of rigid objects in the cluster categories arising from punctured marked surfaces. This forces us to establish a framework on the theory of mutation in two-term subcategories of a 2-Calabi-Yau triangulated category with respect to rigid objects. We note that the connectedness of
\operatorname{EG}(\mathrm{s}\tau\operatorname{-tilt}A)
for the case that
A
is a gentle algebra is obtained in
[
FGLZ
]
, and it is shown in
[
As
]
that
\operatorname{EG}(\mathrm{s}\tau\operatorname{-tilt}A)
has one or two components in the case that
A
is a complete gentle (or more generally, special biserial) algebra.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
1
, we introduce and investigate mutation in two-term subcategories of a 2-Calabi-Yau triangulated category. In Section
2
, we recall basic notions and results on the cluster categories from punctured marked surfaces. In Section
3
, we give a geometric model for the endomorphism algebra of a rigid object in the cluster category arising from a punctured marked surface and show that this includes the class of skew-gentle algebras. Moreover, we classify support
\tau
-tilting modules via certain dissections. In Section
4
, we introduce the notion of flip of dissections and show that it is compatible with mutation of support
\tau
-tilting modules. As an application, the connectedness of the exchange graph of support
\tau
-tilting modules over a skew-gentle algebra is obtained.

Convention

Throughout this paper, we assume
\mathbf{k}
to be an algebraically closed field. Any additive category
\mathcal{D}
in this paper is assumed to be

(1)

\mathbf{k}
-linear and Hom-finite, i.e.,
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(X,Y)
is a finite-dimensional vector space over
\mathbf{k}
for any pair of objects
X,Y
, and

(2)

Krull-Schmidt, i.e., any object is isomorphic to a finite direct sum of objects whose endomorphism rings are local.

We use
X\in\mathcal{D}
to denote that
X
is an object in
\mathcal{D}
. For any
X\in\mathcal{D}
, denote by

(1)

|X|
the number of isomorphism classes of indecomposable direct summands of
X
,

(2)

\operatorname{add}X
the additive hull of
X
, i.e., the smallest subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
, which contains
X
and is closed under isomorphisms, finite direct sums and direct summands, and

(3)

{}^{\perp}X
and
X^{\perp}
the full subcategories of
\mathcal{D}
consisting of all objects
Y
such that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(Y,X)=0
and
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(X,Y)=0
, respectively.

We call
X\in\mathcal{D}

basic
if
|X|
is the number of indecomposable direct summands of
X
, i.e., any two distinct indecomposable direct summands of
X
are not isomorphic. For any object
X\in\mathcal{D}
and any direct summand
Y
of
X
, we denote by
X\setminus Y
the direct summand of
X
such that
X=Y\oplus(X\setminus Y)
.

We call a morphism
g\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(X,Y)

right minimal
if for any
h\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(X,X)
such that
g\circ h=g
, we have that
h
is an isomorphism. For any subcategory
\mathcal{T}
of
\mathcal{D}
, we call
f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(X,Y)
a
right
\mathcal{T}
-approximation
of
Y\in\mathcal{D}
if
X\in\mathcal{T}
and

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(-,X)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(-,f)}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(-,Y)\longrightarrow 0

is exact as functors on
\mathcal{T}
. A right
\mathcal{T}
-approximation is said to be
minimal
if it is right minimal. We call
\mathcal{T}
a
contravariantly finite subcategory
of
\mathcal{D}
if any
Y\in\mathcal{D}
admits a right
\mathcal{T}
-approximation. The
left minimal
maps,
(minimal) left
\mathcal{T}
-approximations
and
covariantly finite subcategories
are defined dually. A subcategory is said to be
functorially finite
if it is both covariantly and contravariantly finite.

When
\mathcal{D}
is a triangulated category, for any
X,Y\in\mathcal{D}
, denote by
X\ast_{\mathcal{D}}Y
the full subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
consisting of all
M\in\mathcal{D}
such that there is a triangle

X_{M}\to M\to Y_{M}\to X[1]

with
X_{M}\in\operatorname{add}X
and
Y_{M}\in\operatorname{add}Y
. When there is no confusion arising, we simply denote
X\ast Y=X\ast_{\mathcal{D}}Y
.

1.
Categorical Interpretation

Throughout this section, let
\mathcal{D}
be a triangulated category and we use
\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y)
to simply denote
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(X,Y)
. We assume that
\mathcal{D}
is 2-Calabi-Yau, i.e., there exists a bi-functorial isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y)\cong D\operatorname{Hom}(Y,X[2]),

for any
X,Y\in\mathcal{D}
, where
D=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{k}}(-,\mathbf{k})
.

Definition 1.1
.

An object
R\in\mathcal{D}
is called

(1)

rigid
provided that
\operatorname{Hom}(R,R[1])=0
,

(2)

maximal rigid
if it is maximal with respect to the rigid property, i.e.,
R
is rigid and for any object
N\in\mathcal{D}
with
N\oplus R
rigid, we have
N\in\operatorname{add}R
,

(3)

cluster tilting
if
R
is rigid and for any object
N\in\mathcal{D}
with
\operatorname{Hom}(R,N[1])=0
, we have
N\in\operatorname{add}R
.

Note that any cluster tilting object is maximal rigid, but the converse is not true in general (cf.
[
BIKR
,
KZ
]
). We also note that the triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
may not admit any cluster tilting object (cf.
[
BMV
]
). If
\mathcal{D}
admits a cluster tilting object, then any maximal rigid object is cluster tilting (see
[
ZZ
]
).

For a rigid object
R\in\mathcal{D}
, the full subcategory
R\ast R[1]
of
\mathcal{D}
is called the
two-term subcategory
with respect to
R
. In this section, we will extend the theory of mutation of cluster tilting objects, or more generally, mutation of maximal objects in
\mathcal{D}

[
IY
,
BMRRT
,
ZZ
]
to
R\ast R[1]
.

By
[
IY
, Proposition 2.1]
,
R\ast R[1]
is closed under taking direct summands. Any object
M\in R\ast R[1]
admits an
R
-presentation
, i.e., a triangle

(1.1)

R^{1}_{M}\to R^{0}_{M}\overset{\iota_{0}}{\longrightarrow}M\overset{\iota_{1}}{\longrightarrow}R^{1}_{M}[1]

with
R^{0}_{M},R^{1}_{M}\in\operatorname{add}R
. Since
R
is rigid, we have that
\iota_{0}
is a right
\operatorname{add}R
-approximation of
M
and
\iota_{1}
is a left
\operatorname{add}R[1]
-approximation of
M
. Moreover, in (
1.1
),
\iota_{0}
can be chosen to be right minimal, or equivalently,
\iota_{1}
can be chosen to be left minimal. In such case, we call (
1.1
) a
minimal

R
-presentation.

Proposition 1.2
.

If (
1.1
) is a minimal
R
-presentation, then
R^{1}_{M}
and
R^{0}_{M}
do not have an indecomposable direct summand in common.

Proof.

The proof of
[
DK
, Proposition 2.1]
also works here.
∎

1.1.
Rigid objects in two-term subcategories

Throughout the rest of this section, let
R
be a basic rigid object in
\mathcal{D}
. We introduce the notion of maximal rigid objects with respect to
R\ast R[1]
.

Definition 1.3
.

An object
U\in R\ast R[1]
is called
maximal rigid
with respect to
R\ast R[1]
provided that it is rigid and for any object
N\in R\ast R[1]
with
N\oplus U
rigid, we have
N\in\operatorname{add}U
.

Denote by
\operatorname{rigid-}(R\ast R[1])
the set of (isoclasses of) basic rigid objects in
R\ast R[1]
, and by
\operatorname{max}\operatorname{rigid-}(R\ast R[1])
the set of (isoclasses of) basic maximal rigid objects with respect to
R\ast R[1]
.

In the case that
R
is maximal rigid (resp. cluster tilting), by
[
ZZ
, Corollary 2.5]
, any rigid object in
\mathcal{D}
also belongs to
R\ast R[1]
. Therefore, the maximal rigid objects with respect to
R\ast R[1]
are exactly the maximal rigid (resp. cluster tilting) objects in
\mathcal{D}
.

Lemma 1.4
.

For any
U\in\operatorname{max}\operatorname{rigid-}(R\ast R[1])
, we have
R\in\operatorname{rigid-}(U[-1]\ast U)
. For any triangle

(1.2)

U^{0}_{R}[-1]\overset{\iota_{0}}{\longrightarrow}R\overset{\iota_{1}}{\longrightarrow}U^{1}_{R}\overset{\iota}{\longrightarrow}U^{0}_{R},

the following hold.

(1)

If
\iota_{0}
is a right
\operatorname{add}U[-1]
-approximation of
R
, then
U^{1}_{R}\in\operatorname{add}U
.

(2)

If
\iota_{1}
is a left
\operatorname{add}U
-approximation of
R
, then
U^{0}_{R}\in\operatorname{add}U
.

Proof.

Due to the existence of right
\operatorname{add}U[-1]
-approximations (resp. left
\operatorname{add}U
-approximations) of
R
, the assertion (1) (resp. (2)) implies
R\in\operatorname{rigid-}(U[-1]\ast U)
. So it suffices to show (1) and (2). We only prove (1), since (2) can be proved dually.

Since
\iota_{0}
is a right
\operatorname{add}U[-1]
-approximation, we have
U^{0}_{R}\in\operatorname{add}U
. Since
U\in R\ast R[1]
and
R\ast R[1]
is closed under taking direct summands, we have
U^{0}_{R}\in R\ast R[1]
. Hence
U^{1}_{R}\in R\ast U^{0}_{R}\subseteq R\ast R[1]
. Applying
\operatorname{Hom}(U[-1],-)
to the triangle (
1.2
), we get a long exact sequence in
\mathcal{D}

\begin{array}[]{rl}&\operatorname{Hom}(U[-1],U^{0}_{R}[-1])\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Hom}(U[-1],\iota_{0})}\operatorname{Hom}(U[-1],R)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Hom}(U[-1],\iota_{1})}\operatorname{Hom}(U[-1],U^{1}_{R})\\
\to&\operatorname{Hom}(U[-1],U^{0}_{R}).\end{array}

Since
U
is rigid, the last term
\operatorname{Hom}(U[-1],U^{0}_{R})=0
. Since
\iota_{0}
is a right
\operatorname{add}U[-1]
-approximation of
R
, the morphism
\operatorname{Hom}(U[-1],\iota_{0})
is surjective. So
\operatorname{Hom}(U[-1],U^{1}_{R})=0
, which implies
\operatorname{Hom}(U^{1}_{R},U[1])=0
by the 2-Calabi-Yau property. In particular,
\operatorname{Hom}(U^{1}_{R},U^{0}_{R}[1])=0
.

For any

, consider the following diagram.

\iota[1]\circ f\in\operatorname{Hom}(U^{1}_{R},U^{0}_{R}[1])=0

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
U=\oplus^{m}_{i=1}U_{i}
be a basic rigid object in
R\ast R[1]
with
U_{i},1\leq i\leq m
, indecomposable. Then the elements
\operatorname{ind}_{R}U_{i},1\leq i\leq m
, are linearly independent in

.

The proof of
[

also works here.

We have the following criterion of a rigid object in
R\ast R[1]
to be maximal rigid with respect to
R\ast R[1]
, by counting rank.

Proposition 1.7
.

For any
U\in\operatorname{rigid-}(R\ast R[1])
, we have
|U|\leq|R|
, where equality holds if and only if
U\in\operatorname{max}\operatorname{rigid-}(R\ast R[1])
.

Proof.

Let
U=\oplus^{m}_{i=1}U_{i}
be a basic rigid object in
R\ast R[1]
with
U_{i},1\leq i\leq m
, indecomposable. By Proposition
1.6
, we have

|U|=\operatorname{rank}\{\operatorname{ind}_{R}U_{j}|1\leq j\leq m\}\leq\operatorname{rank}\{\operatorname{ind}_{R}R_{i}|1\leq i\leq n\}=|R|,

where
\operatorname{rank}X
denotes the rank of a set
X\subseteq K_{0}^{\mbox{sp}}(R)
.

Let
U
be a maximal rigid object with respect to
R\ast R[1]
. On the one hand,
U
is rigid in
R\ast R[1]
, which implies
|U|\leq|R|
. On the other hand, by Lemma
1.4
, we have
R\in\operatorname{rigid-}(U[-1]\ast U)
, which implies
|R|\leq|U|
. Hence we have
|R|=|U|
. Conversely, let
U\in\operatorname{rigid-}(R\ast R[1])
with
|U|=|R|
. For any
N\in R\ast R[1]
such that
U\oplus N
is rigid, we have
|U\oplus N|\leq|R|
. Then
|U|=|U\oplus N|
, which implies
N\in\operatorname{add}U
. So
U
is maximal rigid with respect to
R\ast R[1]
.
∎

As a consequence of Lemma
1.4
and Proposition
1.7
, we have the following dual relation between two rigid objects.

Corollary 1.8
.

Let
U
and
R
be rigid objects in
\mathcal{D}
. Then
U\in\operatorname{max}\operatorname{rigid-}(R\ast R[1])
if and only if
R\in\operatorname{max}\operatorname{rigid-}(U[-1]\ast U)
.

Proof.

For any
U\in\operatorname{max}\operatorname{rigid-}(R\ast R[1])
, by Lemma
1.4
, we have
R\in\operatorname{rigid-}(U[-1]\ast U)
, and by Proposition
1.7
we have
|U|=|R|
. Then
|R|=|U[-1]|
. So by Proposition
1.7
again, we have
R\in\operatorname{max}\operatorname{rigid-}(U[-1]\ast U)
. The opposite implication can be obtained by switching
R
with
U[-1]
.
∎

The following lemma is useful.

Lemma 1.9
.

Let
U
and
R
be basic rigid objects in
\mathcal{D}
. If
R\in\operatorname{max}\operatorname{rigid-}(U[-1]\ast U)
, then for any indecomposable summand
Y
of

, we have
[\operatorname{ind}_{U[-1]}R:Y[-1]]\neq 0
.

Proof.

Let
N=U\setminus Y
. If
[\operatorname{ind}_{U[-1]}R:Y[-1]]=0
then, by the definition of index,

admits an

-presentation. So by Proposition

, we have
|R|\leq|N|<|U|
. Since
R\in\operatorname{max}\operatorname{rigid-}(U[-1]\ast U)
, again by Proposition
1.7
, we have
|R|=|U|
, a contradiction.
∎

1.2.
Mutation in two-term subcategories

By Proposition
1.7
, any rigid object in
R\ast R[1]
can be completed to a maximal rigid object with respect to
R\ast R[1]
.

Definition 1.10
.

A basic rigid object
N
in
R\ast R[1]
is called
almost maximal rigid
with respect to
R\ast R[1]
if
|N|=|R|-1
.

Let
N
be an almost maximal rigid object with respect to
R\ast R[1]
. An indecomposable object
Y
is called a
completion
of
N
if
N\oplus Y
is maximal rigid with respect to
R\ast R[1]
.

Lemma 1.11
.

Let
N
be an almost maximal rigid object with respect to
R\ast R[1]
, and
Y,Y^{\prime}

1.3.

We briefly recall the

(1)

(2)

(3)

-rigid pair and

-tilting module

For any basic support

-tilting module

-tilting pair. Hence, one can identify basic support

-tilting modules with basic

-tilting pairs. There is a partial order on the set of basic support

Theorem 1.19

(

Any basic almost complete

-tilting pair

or

. Then
(M,P)
is called the

of
(M^{\prime},P^{\prime})
at

of

at
(N,Q)

Definition 1.20

The exchange graph
\operatorname{EG}(\mathrm{s}\tau\operatorname{-tilt}\Lambda)

(

The functor

Corollary 2.18
.

Let
\mathbf{T}
be a tagged triangulation of
\mathbf{S}
. Then for any
\gamma,\delta\in\mathbf{T}

0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

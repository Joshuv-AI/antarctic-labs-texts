The well-posedness, ill-posedness and non-uniform dependence on initial data for the Fornberg-Whitham equation in Besov spaces
Yingying Guo

Abstract
In this paper, we first establish the local well-posedness (existence, uniqueness and continuous dependence) for the Fornberg-Whitham equation in both supercritical Besov spaces B p , r s , s > 1 + 1 p , 1 ≤ p , r ≤ + ∞ B^{s}_{p,r},\ s>1+\frac{1}{p},\ 1\leq p,r\leq+\infty and critical Besov spaces B p , 1 1 + 1 p , 1 ≤ p < + ∞ B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1},\ 1\leq p<+\infty , which improves the previous work [ 36 , 21 , 20 ] . Then, we prove the solution is not uniformly continuous dependence on the initial data in supercritical Besov spaces B p , r s , s > 1 + 1 p , 1 ≤ p ≤ + ∞ , 1 ≤ r < + ∞ B^{s}_{p,r},\ s>1+\frac{1}{p},\ 1\leq p\leq+\infty,\ 1\leq r<+\infty and critical Besov spaces B p , 1 1 + 1 p , 1 ≤ p < + ∞ B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1},\ 1\leq p<+\infty . At last, we show that the solution is ill-posed in B p , ∞ σ B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty} with σ > 3 + 1 p , 1 ≤ p ≤ + ∞ \sigma>3+\frac{1}{p},\ 1\leq p\leq+\infty .

1
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the Cauchy problem for the following Fornberg-Whitham (FW) equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{xxt}-u_{t}+\frac{9}{2}u_{x}u_{xx}+\frac{3}{2}uu_{xxx}-\frac{3}{2}uu_{x}+u_{x}=0,&t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R},\end{array}\right.

which was proposed by Whitham and Fornberg
[
13
]
as a model for breaking waves. Note that
(1-\partial_{xx})^{-1}f={\rm{p}}\ast f
for any
f\in L^{2}
, where
\ast
denote the convolution and
{\rm{p}}(x)=\frac{1}{2}e^{-|x|}
.
We can rewrite Eq. (
1
) in non-local form

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}+\frac{3}{2}uu_{x}=(1-\partial_{xx})^{-1}\partial_{x}u=\partial_{x}{\rm p}\ast u,&t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{array}\right.

In this form, the FW equation was compared with the famous Korteweg–de Vries (KdV) equation
[
23
]

\displaystyle u_{t}+6uu_{x}=-\partial_{xxx}u

and the classical Camassa-Holm (CH) equation
[
4
,
9
]

\displaystyle u_{t}+uu_{x}=-\partial_{x}(1-\partial_{xx})^{-1}\Big(u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u^{2}_{x}\Big).

The KdV equation admits solitons or solitary traveling wave solutions which maintain a constant shape and move at constant velocity. Indeed, the solitary wave solutions of the KdV equation in the non-periodic case are shown as

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\frac{c}{2}{\rm sech}^{2}\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{c}}{2}(x-ct)\bigg),

where the constant
c
is the wave speed. Unfortunately, the KdV equation did not have the property of wave breaking. Furthermore, it did not produce solitary waves of greatest height with a sharp peaked crest which are now called peakons.

In 1993, Camassa and Holm
[
4
]
found an integrable shallow water equation with peakon (peaked solitons) solutions which are solitons with discontinuous first derivative. The simplest one in the non-periodic case is of the form

\displaystyle u(t,x)=ce^{-|x-ct|},

where
c
is a positive constant. The local well-posedness and local ill-posedness of the Cauchy problem for the CH equation in Sobolev spaces and Besov spaces have been investigated in
[
8
,
11
,
12
,
14
,
34
,
28
,
25
,
29
]
. Moreover, the CH equation has global strong solutions, blow-up strong solutions, global weak solutions, global conservative weak solutions and dissipative weaak solutions, see
[
5
,
6
,
8
,
7
,
28
,
2
,
3
,
10
,
19
,
33
]
.
Further, the non-uniform continuity of the CH equation in Sobolev spaces and Besov spaces has been studied in many papers, see
[
18
,
16
,
17
,
26
,
24
]
.

It is interesting that the FW equation does not only admit solitary traveling wave solutions like the KdV equation, but also possess peakon solutions (or peaked traveling wave solutions) as the CH equation which are of the form

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\frac{8}{9}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|x-\frac{4}{3}t|},

which were first found in
[
13
]
. A classification of other traveling wave solutions of the FW equation was presented by Yin, Tian and Fan
[
35
]
. It’s worth noting that the KdV equation and CH equation are integrable, and they possess infinitely many conserved quantities, an infinite hierarchy of quasi-local symmetries, a Lax pair and a bi-Hamiltonian structure. However, the FW equation is not integrable and the only useful conservation law we know so far is
\|u\|_{L^{2}}
. Therefore, the analysis of the FW equation would be somewhat more difficult.

Recently, the local well-posedness for (
1
) in Sobolev spaces
H^{s},\ s>\frac{3}{2}
and Besov spaces
B^{s}_{2,r},\ s>\frac{3}{2},\ 1<r<+\infty
or
s\geq\frac{3}{2},\ r=1
were established in
[
21
,
20
]
. They also proved that the data-to-solution map is not uniformly continuous but Hölder continuous in some given topology. Furthermore, a blowup criterion for solutions was given. Later, Haziot
[
15
]
, Hörmann
[
22
]
, Wei
[
30
,
31
]
and Wu et al
[
32
]
sharpened this blowup criterion and presented the sufficient conditions about the initial data to guarantee wave-breaking in finite time for the FW equation on the line and on the circle.

However, the local well-posedness for (
1
) in Besov space
B_{p,r}^{s},\ s>1+\frac{1}{p},\ 1\leq p,\ r\leq+\infty
or
s=1+\frac{1}{p},\ 1\leq p<+\infty,\ r=1
has not been studied. The non-uniform dependence on initial data for (
1
) in
B_{p,r}^{s},\ s>1+\frac{1}{p},\ 1\leq p\leq+\infty,\ 1\leq r<+\infty
or
s=1+\frac{1}{p},\ 1\leq p<+\infty,\ r=1
and local ill-posedness for (
1
) in
B_{p,\infty}^{s},\ s>1+\frac{1}{p},\ 1\leq p\leq+\infty
have also not been investigated yet. In the paper, following the idea of
[

,
26
,
24
]
, we aim to study the local well-posedness, local ill-posedness and non-uniform dependence on initial data for (
1
) in Besov spaces.

our main results are stated as follows.

.

Let

(s,p,r)
satisfy the condition

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}.

(1.6)

Remark 1.2

Our result covers the well-posedness
results in
[
21

20
]
. In fact, when

From our well-posedness result, we are also able to demonstrate that the dependence on the initial data in Besov space

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R},\ 1\leq p,\ r\leq\infty
and let
(s,p,r)
satisfy the condition

s>1+\frac{1}{p},\ 1\leq p\leq+\infty,\ 1\leq r<+\infty\quad\text{or}\quad s=1+\frac{1}{p},\ 1\leq p<+\infty,\ r=1.

(1.7)

Then the solution map of problem (
1
) is not uniformly continuous from any bounded subset in
B^{s}_{p,r}
into
C([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
. More precisely, there exists two sequences of solutions
u^{n}
and
{\rm w}^{n}
with the initial data
u^{n}_{0}={\rm w}^{n}_{0}+{\rm v}_{0}^{n}
and
{\rm w}^{n}_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\|{\rm w}^{n}_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\lesssim 1\qquad and\qquad\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|{\rm v}^{n}_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}=0,

but

\displaystyle\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Big\|u^{n}-{\rm w}^{n}\Big\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\gtrsim t,\qquad\forall\ t\in[0,T_{0}],

with small time

.

Remark 1.4
.

For the non-uniform denpendence of the solutions to (
1
) in Besov spaces, the key argument is to construct the initial data.

Thanks to Theorem
1.1
, for
r=+\infty
, the solution map of (
1
) is weak continuous with respect to the initial data
u_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,\infty}
with
s>1+\frac{1}{p},\ 1\leq p\leq+\infty
. In fact, the data-to-solution map of (
1
) is not continuous, i.e. the Cauchy problem of the FW equation (
1
) is ill-posed in
B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}
with
\sigma>3+\frac{1}{p},\ 1\leq p\leq+\infty
, which is achieved in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\sigma>3+\frac{1}{p}
with
1\leq p\leq\infty
. There exists a initial data
u_{0}\in B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}
and a positive constant
\varepsilon_{0}
such that the data-to-solution map
u_{0}\mapsto u(t)
of (
1
) satisfies

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}\|u(t)-u_{0}\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}}\geq\varepsilon_{0}.

Remark 1.6
.

Theorem
1.5
demonstrates the ill-posedness of the FW equation in
B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}
. More precisely, there exists a
u_{0}\in B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}
such that the corresponding solution to the FW equation that starts from
u_{0}
does not converge back to
u_{0}
in the sense of
B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}
-norm as time goes to zero. Our key argument is to construct a initial data
u_{0}
.

with

V^{\prime}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\|\partial_{x}v(t)\|_{B^{\frac{1}{p}}_{p,\infty}\cap L^{\infty}},&\text{if}\ \theta<1+\frac{1}{p},\\
\|\partial_{x}v(t)\|_{B^{\theta}_{p,r}},&\text{if}\ \theta=1+\frac{1}{p},\ r>1,\\
\|\partial_{x}v(t)\|_{B^{\theta-1}_{p,r}},&\text{if}\ \theta>1+\frac{1}{p}\ (\text{or}\ \theta=1+\frac{1}{p},\ r=1).\end{array}\right.

If
\theta>0
, then there exists a constant
C=C(p,r,\theta)
such that the following statement holds

\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{B^{\theta}_{p,r}}\leq\|f_{0}\|_{B^{\theta}_{p,r}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|g(\tau)\|_{B^{\theta}_{p,r}}{{\rm d}}\tau+C\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\|f(\tau)\|_{B^{\theta}_{p,r}}\|\partial_{x}v(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}v(\tau)\|_{B^{\theta-1}_{p,r}}\|\partial_{x}f(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big){{\rm d}}\tau.

In particular, if
f=av+b,\ a,\ b\in\mathbb{R},
then for all
\theta>0,

V^{\prime}(t)=\|\partial_{x}v(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}.

3
The local well-posedness in both supercritical and critical Besov spaces

In this section, we present the local well-posedness for the Cauchy problem (
1
) in both supercritical Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,r}
with
s>1+\frac{1}{p},\ 1\leq p,\ r\leq+\infty
and critical Besov spaces
B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}
with
1\leq p<+\infty
, which is different from the previous work
[
21
,
20
,
36
]
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
:
.

we divide four steps to prove it.

Step 1. We will structure a family of approximate solution sequences by iterative scheme.

Assuming that
u^{0}=0
, we define by induction a sequence
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of smooth functions by solving the following linear transport equation:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}u^{n+1}+\frac{3}{2}u^{n}\partial_{x}u^{n+1}=(1-\partial_{xx})^{-1}\partial_{x}u^{n}=\partial_{x}{\rm p}\ast{u^{n}},\\
u^{n+1}|_{t=0}=u_{0}.\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

Assume that
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
belongs to
E^{s}_{p,r}(T)
for all
T>0
. We know from Lemma
2.5
(1) that
B^{s}_{p,r},\ B^{s-1}_{p,r}
are algebras and the embedding
B^{s}_{p,r}\hookrightarrow B^{s-1}_{p,r}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}
holds. Note that the operator
\partial_{x}{\rm p}\ast
is a
S^{-1}
-mutiplier. Then, we have

\displaystyle\|(1-\partial_{xx})^{-1}\partial_{x}u^{n}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}=\|\partial_{x}{\rm p}\ast{u^{n}}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\leq C\|u^{n}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}.

From Lemma
2.7
, we know (
3.1
) has a global solution
u^{n+1}\in E^{s}_{p,r}(T)
.
Thanks to Lemma
2.8
, we infer that

\displaystyle\|u^{n+1}(t)\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\leq e^{C\int_{0}^{t}\|u^{n}(t^{\prime})\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}{{\rm d}}t^{\prime}}\bigg(\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+C\int_{0}^{t}e^{-C\int_{0}^{\tau}\|u^{n}(\tau^{\prime})\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}{{\rm d}}\tau^{\prime}}\|u^{n}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}{{\rm d}}\tau\bigg).

(3.2)

Fix a
T>0
such that

T<\min\Big\{\frac{1}{2C\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}},\frac{\ln 2}{C}\Big\}
and suppose by induction that

\displaystyle\|u^{n}(t)\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\leq\frac{2\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}}{1-2C\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}t},\ \ \ \ \ \ \forall\ t\in[0,T].

(3.3)

Plugging (
3.3
) into (
3.2
) yields

\displaystyle\|u^{n+1}(t)\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\leq

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}e^{C\int_{0}^{t}\|u^{n}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}{{\rm d}}\tau}\times e^{Ct}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}}{1-2C\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}t},\qquad\quad\forall\ t\in[0,T].

Therefore,
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}\big(0,T;B^{s}_{p,r}\big)
. Consequently, the right side of Eq. (
3.1
) is bounded in
L^{\infty}\big(0,T;B^{s}_{p,r}\big)
, which follows that
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded in
E^{s}_{p,r}(T)
.

Step 2. We shall prove the sequence of approximate solutions
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence and the limit

1
).

We will show that
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
C\big([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r}\big)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}(u^{n+m+1}-u^{n+1})+\frac{3}{2}u^{m+n}\partial_{x}(u^{n+m+1}-u^{n+1})=\frac{3}{2}(u^{n}-u^{n+m})\partial_{x}u^{n+1}+\partial_{x}{\rm p}\ast(u^{n+m}-u^{n}),\\
(u^{n+m+1}-u^{n+1})|_{t=0}=0.\end{array}\right.

(3.4)

Thanks to Lemmas
2.7
–
2.8
and the uniform boundedness of
u^{n}
, we see for any

,

\displaystyle\|(u^{n+m+1}-u^{n+1})(t)\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{C\int_{0}^{t}\|u^{n+m}(t^{\prime})\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}{{\rm d}}t^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-C\int_{0}^{\tau}\|u^{n+m}(\tau^{\prime})\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}{{\rm d}}\tau^{\prime}}\|(u^{n+m}-u^{n})(\tau)\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}{{\rm d}}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{T}\int_{0}^{t}\|(u^{n+m}-u^{n})(\tau)\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}{{\rm d}}\tau.

(3.5)

Thereby, taking advantage of the induction, we known

\displaystyle\|u^{n+m+1}-u^{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\leq\frac{(TC_{T})^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\|u^{m}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\leq C_{T}2^{-n}

which implies that
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
C\big([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r}\big)
. Hence,
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to some limit function
u\in C\big([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r}\big)
.

We next need to verify that the limit

indeed belongs to

and satisfies (
1
). Since

is uniformly bounded in

\displaystyle u^{n}\rightarrow u\qquad\text{in $C\big([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r}\big)$}

and the interpolation inequality, we get

\displaystyle u^{n}\rightarrow u\qquad\text{in $C\big([0,T];B^{s^{\prime}}_{p,r}\big)$ for any $s^{\prime}<s.$}

It is a routine method to pass to the limit in Eq. (
3.1
) and to deduce that
u
is solution of (
1
).
Note that the right side of Eq. (
1
) belongs to
L^{\infty}\big(0,T;B^{s}_{p,r}\big)
. Thus, according to Lemma
2.7
, we see that
u
is in
C\big([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r}\big)
(resp.,
C_{w}\big([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r}\big)

r<\infty
(resp.,

\partial_{t}u

if

, or belongs to
L^{\infty}(0,T;B^{s-1}_{p,\infty})

belongs to
E^{s}_{p,r}(T)
.

Step 3. Uniqueness.

Assume
u^{1},\ u^{2}
are two solutions of (
1
) with initial data
u^{1}_{0},\ u^{2}_{0}
, then

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}u^{12}+\frac{3}{2}u^{1}\partial_{x}u^{12}=-\frac{3}{2}u^{12}\partial_{x}u^{2}+\partial_{x}{\rm p}\ast u^{12},\\

(3.6)

Similar to (
3.5

\displaystyle\|u^{12}(t)\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{C\int_{0}^{t}\|u^{1}(t^{\prime})\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}{{\rm d}}t^{\prime}}\Big(\|u^{1}_{0}-u^{2}_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-C\int_{0}^{\tau}\|u^{1}(\tau^{\prime})\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}{{\rm d}}\tau^{\prime}}\|u^{12}(\tau)\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}{{\rm d}}\tau\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u^{1}_{0}-u^{2}_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}.

So if

, we can immediately obtain the uniqueness.

Case 1.
For

(3.9)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}u^{n}\rightarrow\partial_{x}u^{\infty}\qquad\text{in}\quad C\big([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r}\big).

(3.11)

Combining (

Case 2.
For
r=\infty
, noting that

for any

. For fixed

\displaystyle\big\langle u^{n}(t)-u^{\infty}(t),\phi\big\rangle=

\displaystyle=

By means of Proposition

\displaystyle\big|\big\langle u^{n}(t)-u^{\infty}(t),S_{j}\phi\big\rangle\big|\leq C\|u^{n}-u^{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;B^{s-1}_{p,\infty})}\|S_{j}\phi\|_{B^{1-s}_{p^{\prime},1}},

Non-uniform continuous dependence in both supercritical and critical Besov spaces

Before that, we introduce smooth, radial cut-off functions in frequency space. Let

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}|\xi|\hat{\psi}(\xi){{\rm d}}\xi\leq C,

\displaystyle\psi(0)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\psi}(\xi){{\rm d}}\xi.

Proof.

\displaystyle\|u(t)-u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}\leq Ct\big(\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}+\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}+\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s+1}_{p,\infty}}\big),

\displaystyle\|u(t)-u_{0}\|_{B^{s+1}_{p,\infty}}\leq Ct\big(\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}+\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s+1}_{p,\infty}}+\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s+2}_{p,\infty}}\big).

Proof.

For
\gamma>0
, taking advantage of Lemma
2.8
and Theorem
1.1
, we find

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}B^{\gamma}_{p,\infty}}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{B^{\gamma}_{p,\infty}}.

(5.6)

By the Mean Value Theorem, we obtain from (
5.6
) that

\displaystyle\|u(t)-u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}\leq

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\|\partial_{\tau}u\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}{{\rm d}}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\|(1-\partial_{xx})^{-1}\partial_{x}u\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}{{\rm d}}\tau+\frac{3}{2}\int^{t}_{0}\|uu_{x}\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}{{\rm d}}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ct\big(\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}+\|u\|^{2}_{L_{t}^{\infty}B^{s}_{p,\infty}}+\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\|u_{x}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}B^{s}_{p,\infty}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ct\big(\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}+\|u\|^{2}_{L_{t}^{\infty}B^{s}_{p,\infty}}+\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}B^{s+1}_{p,\infty}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ct\big(\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}+\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}+\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s+1}_{p,\infty}}\big).

Following the same arguments, we get

\displaystyle\|u(t)-u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}\leq

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\|\partial_{\tau}u\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}{{\rm d}}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\|(1-\partial_{xx})^{-1}\partial_{x}u\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}{{\rm d}}\tau+\frac{3}{2}\int^{t}_{0}\|uu_{x}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}{{\rm d}}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ct\Big(\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}+\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}B^{s}_{p,\infty}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ct\Big(\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}+\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}\Big)

and

\displaystyle\|u(t)-u_{0}\|_{B^{s+1}_{p,\infty}}\leq

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\|\partial_{\tau}u\|_{B^{s+1}_{p,\infty}}{{\rm d}}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\|(1-\partial_{xx})^{-1}\partial_{x}u\|_{B^{s+1}_{p,\infty}}{{\rm d}}\tau+\frac{3}{2}\int^{t}_{0}\|uu_{x}\|_{B^{s+1}_{p,\infty}}{{\rm d}}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ct\big(\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}B^{s}_{p,\infty}}+\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}B^{s}_{p,\infty}}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}B^{s+1}_{p,\infty}}+\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}B^{s+2}_{p,\infty}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ct\big(\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}+\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s+1}_{p,\infty}}+\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s+2}_{p,\infty}}\big).

Thus, we finish the proof of Proposition
5.3
.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
s=\sigma-2
and
u_{0}\in B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}
. Assume that
u\in L^{\infty}_{T}B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}
be the solution to the Cauchy problem (
1
), we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}(t,u_{0})\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}\leq

\displaystyle Ct^{2}\Big(\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}+\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}+\|u_{0}\|^{3}_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}+\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s+1}_{p,\infty}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s+2}_{p,\infty}}\Big),

here and in what follows we denote

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}(t,u_{0}):=u(t)-u_{0}-t\mathbf{T}(u_{0}),

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}(u_{0}):=-\frac{3}{2}u_{0}\partial_{x}u_{0}+(1-\partial_{xx})^{-1}\partial_{x}u_{0}.

In particular, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}(t,u_{0})\|_{B^{\sigma-2}_{p,\infty}}\leq C\big(\|u_{0}\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}}\big)t^{2}.

Proof.

Taking advantage of the Mean Value Theorem and Eq. (
1
), and then using Lemma
2.5
, we see

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}(t,u_{0})\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}\leq

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\|\partial_{\tau}u-\mathbf{T}(u_{0})\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}{{\rm d}}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\|(1-\partial_{xx})^{-1}\partial_{x}(u-u_{0})\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}{{\rm d}}\tau+\frac{3}{2}\int^{t}_{0}\|u\partial_{x}u-u_{0}\partial_{x}u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}{{\rm d}}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int^{t}_{0}\|u(\tau)-u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}{{\rm d}}\tau+C\int^{t}_{0}\|u(\tau)-u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}{{\rm d}}\tau

\displaystyle+C\int^{t}_{0}\|u(\tau)-u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}\|u(\tau)\|_{B^{s+1}_{p,\infty}}{{\rm d}}\tau+C\int^{t}_{0}\|u(\tau)-u_{0}\|_{B^{s+1}_{p,\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}{{\rm d}}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ct^{2}\Big(\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}+\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}+\|u_{0}\|^{3}_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}+\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s+1}_{p,\infty}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s+2}_{p,\infty}}\Big),

where we have used Proposition
5.3
in the last step.

Thus, we complete the proof of Proposition
5.4
.
∎

Now we present the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

The proof of Theorem
1.5
:

Using Proposition
2.1
, Lemma
5.2
and Proposition
5.4
, we get

\displaystyle\|u(t)-u_{0}\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}}\geq

\displaystyle 2^{{ln\sigma}}\big\|\Delta_{ln}\big(u(t)-u_{0}\big)\big\|_{L^{p}}=2^{{ln\sigma}}\big\|\Delta_{ln}\big(t\mathbf{T}(u_{0})+\mathbf{w}(t,u_{0})\big)\big\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle ct2^{{ln}}-Ct-C2^{2{ln}}t^{2}.

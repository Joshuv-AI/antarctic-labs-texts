1.
Introduction

In this paper, a stochastic differential equation is formulated and solved, to describe laser propagation into absorbing matter where the absorption coefficient is a random Gaussian function; that is, the absorption coefficient is a randomly fluctuating spatial function, which fluctuates about a mean value and has Gaussian statistics. We first consider the fundamental description of laser propagation in matter where the absorption and scatter coefficients are constant and homogenous, in terms of the radiative transfer (RT) of photons which are scattered and absorbed by the matter. The fluence or flux evolving via radiative transfer equation (RTE). This is the fundamental equation of ’radiation hydrodynamics’ and has found very many applications in physics and engineering
[1-12].

(1)

In astrophysics to describe radiative transfer in stars; in nuclear engineering to describe neutron transport in reactors; in laser-matter physics, to describe the evolution of an initially collimated laser beam into a scattered distribution of light and energy within matter, and the propagation of scatted light in turbid solids, liquids and medias. Radiation hydrodynamics is also necessary to compute the propagation of laser energy into the fuel capsule during laser-induced fusion or inertial confinement experiments.
[1-11]
.

(2)

In biomedical optics, the RTE and its numerical and diffusion approximation have been extensively applied to the description of photon transport and scattering within biological tissues, whole blood and blood cell suspensions; in the development of laser scalpels and laser surgery, where the penetration of laser energy into the tissue determines how deeply the laser scalpel will cut; in photodynamic cancer therapy and cancer radiation dosimetry
[12-21]
.

The fundamental radiative transport equation (RTE) is related to the Boltzmann equation of a gas from kinetic theory, and describes the evolution of
a flux or intensity
\psi(\mathbf{x},t,\widehat{\bm{\omega}})
, from initial data and with suitable boundary conditions on
\partial\mathbb{I\!D}
. The RTE states that an initial ’beam’ or flux of photons loses energy through scattering away from the beam and through absorption/capture, and gains energy from photon sources or emissivity within the medium and scattering directed towards the beam. In effect, it is an energy balance law and given by the integro-differential equation. Details of radiative transfer and the RTE and its many applications are given in many classic and modern texts and there is a very extensive literature.
[1-12
, and references therein].

2.
Stochastic media with a random Gaussian absorption coefficient

Suppose now, the matter is considered as a random or stochastic medium with a randomly fluctuating absorption coefficient. This is more realistic for matter comprised of
stochastic mixtures since absorption properties of the matter will not be uniformly homogeneous throughout; liquid media, particle suspensions subjected to thermal noise; for medical laser applications, living tissue which is ’physiologically noisy’ due to random variations in temperature, blood flow, chemical composition, oxygenation etc.
Propagation of beams, waves and radio waves in turbulent medias with random refractive index have been extensively studied as well as wave and beam propagation in a
turbulent atmosphere
[22-25]
. A stochastic scatter coefficient can also be considered, but we will consider only absorption by matter. The random absorption coefficient can be interpreted as a stochastic function or noise, specifically a Gaussian random function or field. Gaussian random fields or functions (GRFs) are defined and presented in more detail in Appendix A. These GRF can be spatio-temporal or just purely spatial. Only purely spatial GRFs are considered.

Classical random fields or functions are well suited to describe structures and properties of systems, that vary randomly in space and/or time. They have found a myriad of useful applications in applied and computational mathematics and science: in the statistical theory of turbulence, medical science, geoscience, engineering, imaging, computer graphics, statistical mechanics and statistics, biology and cosmology
\mathbf{[26-45]}
. Gaussian random fields (GRFs) are of special significance as they can occur spontaneously via the central limit theorem, in systems with a large number of degrees of freedom. GRFs also arise in the study of complex systems like spin glasses, in optimization problems and protein folding
[43-45]
. Coupling random fields or noise to ODEs or PDEs is also a useful methodology for studying turbulence, random systems, chaos, pattern formation etc. Within pure and applied mathematics, stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs), arising from the coupling of random fields/noises to PDEs is an evolving research field
[46-49]
. SPDES can model the propagation of heat, diffusions and waves in random medias or turbulent medias. Many dynamical systems are also affected or influenced by noise, which can either destabilize or stabilize a dynamical system
[51-54]
. All physical systems invariably possess noise on some scale, either of thermal, mechanical or even quantum origin.

Returning to radiative transport and the RTE for neutrons applied to nuclear engineering, reactor noise refers to random fluctuations in the output signal from neutron detectors or sensors placed within the reactor or on its boundary. This has been an important area of investigation from the beginning since neutron flux estimates are required to determine safety and shut-down protocols. Thermal dependence of material properties and cross-sections, mechanical vibrations in the reactor and fuel rods, pressure fluctuations, fluctuations in coolant flow etc.,
[55-59]
can all affect the neutron flux within the reactor, absorption/capture cross-sections and bulk absorption or capture coefficient
\mathsf{A}
, by making them randomly fluctuating functions in space and/or time.

Random Gaussian fluctuations of the absorption coefficient about the mean value
\mathsf{A}

Lemma 2.3
.

The mean free path (MFP) to absorption is
\mathsf{M}=\mathsf{A}^{-1}
for a constant coefficient
\mathsf{A}
. Given the random function or field

\mathbf{A}({z})
then the stochastic MFP is

(2.21)

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}({z})=|\mathbf{A}({z})|^{-1}

and the expectation is

(2.22)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\{\mathsf{M}({z})\big\}=\mathbb{E}\big\{|\mathbf{A}({z})|^{-1}\big\}=\mathsf{A}(1+|\mathcal{S}|)=\mathsf{A}\mathcal{B}

where
\mathcal{S}|\ll 1
, so that the mfp is shifted or ’boosted’ by a small factor
\mathcal{B}=(1+\mathcal{S})
.

Proof.

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}(z)=|\mathscr{A}({z})|^{-1}=[\mathsf{A}+\alpha\mathsf{A}{\mathbf{G}}(z)]^{-1}=\mathsf{A}^{-1}[1+\alpha\mathbf{G}({z})]^{-1}

(2.23)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{A}^{-1}\bigg(\sum_{Q=0}^{\infty}\binom{-1}{Q}\alpha^{Q}|{\mathbf{G}}(z)|^{Q}\bigg)

using the binomial series. Their expectations or averages are then

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}(z)=\mathbb{E}\bigg\{\mathbf{M}(z)\bigg\}=\mathbb{E}\bigg\{\frac{1}{\mathbf{A}(z)}\bigg\}=\mathsf{A}^{-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg\{\big(1+\alpha{\mathbf{G}}(z))^{-1}\bigg\}

(2.24)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{A}^{-1}\left(1+\sum_{Q=1}^{\infty}\binom{-1}{Q}|\mathcal{R}(\alpha,\bm{\mathsf{C}},Q|^{Q}\right)=\mathsf{A}^{-1}(1+\mathcal{S})

where
\mathcal{S}=\sum_{Q=1}^{\infty}\binom{-1}{Q}|\mathcal{R}(\alpha,\bm{\mathsf{C}},Q|^{Q}
and

(2.25)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\alpha,\bm{\mathsf{C}},Q|=\alpha\left\{\frac{1}{2}(\bm{\mathsf{C}}^{Q/2}+(-1)^{Q}\bm{\mathsf{C}}^{Q/2})\right\}^{1/Q}=\alpha\mathbb{E}\{|\mathbf{G}(z)|^{Q}\}

The (binomial) series will converge if
\mathcal{R}(\beta,\bm{\mathsf{C}},Q)<1
. On averaging, the mfp is then shifted by a
small factor
\mathcal{B}=(1+\mathcal{S})
, and
\mathcal{S}
is small if
\alpha
and
\bm{\mathsf{C}}
are small.
∎

3.
Stochastic differential equation for beam propagation in stochastic absorbing medias

A stochastic differential equation for laser propagation and attenuation within matter with a ’noisy’ or random Gaussian absorption coefficient
\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x})
can then be formulated as follows.

Proposition 3.1
.

(
Stochastic differential equation for beam propagation
)

Let absorbing matter have a random Gaussian absorption coefficient
\mathbf{A}(z)=\mathsf{A}+\alpha\mathsf{A}\mathbf{G}(z)

as previously defined. Replacing
\mathsf{A}
with
\mathbf{A}(z)
gives

\displaystyle\frac{\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})}}{dz}=\mathbf{A}(z)\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})}

(3.1)

\displaystyle=-(\mathsf{A}+\alpha\mathsf{A}{\mathbf{G}}(z))\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})},~~~~~~z\in\mathbb{I\!D}=[0,\mathrm{L}]

In differential form

\displaystyle d\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})}=-\mathbf{A}(z))\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})}dz

(3.2)

\displaystyle=-(\mathsf{A}+\alpha{A}\mathbf{G}(z))\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})}dz=-\mathsf{A}\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})}dz-\alpha\mathsf{A}\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})}\mathbf{G}(z)dz,~~~~~~z\in\mathbb{I\!D}

Since the flux/intensity solution itself will now be stochastic, we write
\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})}
instead of

{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{d\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3}})}{\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})}}=-\mathbf{A}(z))dz=-(\mathsf{A}+\alpha\mathsf{A}{\mathbf{G}}(z))dz

(3.3)

\displaystyle=-\mathsf{A}dz-\alpha\mathsf{A}{\mathbf{G}}(z)dz,~~~~~~z\in\mathbb{I\!D}

The linear SDE (3.2) can be solved exactly.

Theorem 3.2
.

The exact solution of this SDE is given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})}=\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}~z)\exp\left(-\alpha\mathsf{A}\int_{0}^{z}\mathbf{G}(\bar{z})d\bar{z}\right)

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})}\exp\left(-\alpha\mathsf{A}\int_{0}^{z}\mathbf{G}(\bar{z})d\bar{z}\right)

with stochastic expectation

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})=\mathbb{E}\big\{\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})}\big\}=\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}~z)\mathbb{E}\left\{\exp\left(-\alpha\mathsf{A}\int_{0}^{z}\mathbf{G}(\hat{z})d\hat{z}\right)\right\}

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\psi(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})\mathbb{E}\left\{\exp\left(-\alpha\mathsf{A}\int_{0}^{z}\mathbf{G}(\hat{z})d\hat{z}\right)\right\}

Proof.

Integrating (3.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\psi_{o}}^{\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})}}\frac{d\widehat{\bm{\Psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})}}{\widehat{\bm{\Psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})}}=\int_{0}^{z}\mathbf{A}(z))dz=-\int_{0}^{z}(\mathsf{A}+\alpha\mathsf{A}\mathbf{G}(z))dz

(3.6)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{z}\mathsf{A}dz-\alpha\mathsf{A}\int_{0}^{z}\mathbf{G}(z)dz,~~~~~~z\in\mathbb{I\!D}

Then

(3.7)

\displaystyle\log\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})}-\log\psi_{o}\equiv\log\left(\frac{\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})}}{\psi_{o}}\right)=-\int_{0}^{z}\mathsf{A}d\bar{z}-\alpha\mathsf{A}\int_{0}^{z}\mathbf{G}(\bar{z})d\bar{z}

Equation (3.4) then follows from taking the exponential of both sides, and (3.5) from taking the expectation.
∎

Note that since the Gaussian function
\mathbf{G}(z)
is non-white with a regulated binary correlation, then its derivative exists and can be defined and so regular calculus can be used to find the solution.

The expectation (3.5) can be evaluated via a Van Kampen-type cumulant or cluster expansion
[60-63]
. This is a useful tool
in evaluating expectations of exponentials of stochastic integrals. Due to
\mathbf{G}(z)
being Gaussian, all high-order cumulants beyond 2nd order are equal to zero.

Theorem 3.3
.

(
Cumulant expansion of the stochastic exponential
)

The expectation of the solution has the following Van-Kampen-type cumulant expansion

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})=\bigg\{\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbb{e}}_{3})}\bigg\}=\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}~z)\mathbb{E}\left\{\exp\left(-\alpha\mathsf{A}\int_{0}^{z}{\mathbf{G}}(z)dz\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}~z)\exp\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{-(\alpha\mathsf{A})^{m}}{m!}{\int}_{\mathbb{D}}\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{m}[z]{\mathbb{K}}\left\{\prod_{q=0}^{m}\otimes{\mathbf{G}}(z)\right\}\right)

(3.8)

\displaystyle\equiv\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}~z)\exp\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{-(\alpha\mathsf{A})^{m}}{m!}{\int}_{0}^{z}...{\int}_{0}^{z_{m-1}}dz_{1}...dz_{m}\mathbb{K}\bigg\{{\mathbf{G}}(z_{1})\otimes...\otimes{\mathbf{G}}(z_{m})\bigg\}\right)

Proof.

Given a set of planes
(z_{1},...z_{m})\in\mathbb{I\!D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{(+)}
, the mth-order moments and cumulants for the GRFs are given by (2.7) and (2.8) so that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{\mathbf{N}}\mathbb{E}\bigg\{{\mathbf{G}}(z_{1})\otimes...\otimes{\mathbf{G}}(z_{m})\bigg\}=\overrightarrow{\mathbf{N}}\mathbb{E}\left\{\prod_{q=1}^{m}{\mathbf{G}}(z_{q})\right\}

(3.10)

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{\mathbf{N}}\mathbb{K}\bigg\{{\mathbf{G}}(z_{1})\otimes...\otimes{\mathbf{G}}(z_{m})\bigg\}=\overrightarrow{\mathbf{N}}\mathbb{K}\left\{\prod_{q=1}^{m}{\mathbf{G}}(z_{q})\right\}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{\mathbf{N}}\mathbb{K}\bigg\{{\mathbf{G}}(z_{1})\otimes...\otimes{\mathbf{G}}(z_{m})\bigg\}=\overrightarrow{\mathbf{N}}\mathbb{K}\left\{\prod_{q=1}^{m}{\mathbf{G}}(z_{q})\right\}_{m\geq 3}=0

where
\overrightarrow{\mathbf{N}}
is a ’normal ordering operator’. For example
\overrightarrow{\mathbf{N}}f(t_{2})f(t_{3})f(t_{1})=f(t_{1})f(t_{2})f(t_{3})
for
t_{1}<t_{2}<t_{3}
and so on, for any function
f(t)
.

The moment generating function (MGF)
{\mathscr{M}}[\mathbf{G}(z)]
and the cumulant-generating function [CGF]

{\mathscr{C}}[\mathbf{G}(z)]
are given by

(3.12)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{M}}[\mathbf{G}(z)]=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\beta^{m}}{m!}\int_{0}^{z}...\int_{0}^{z_{m-1}}dz_{1}...dz_{m}\overrightarrow{\mathbf{N}}\mathbb{E}\left\{\prod_{q=1}^{m}{\mathbf{G}}(z_{q})\right\}

(3.13)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{C}}[{\mathbf{G}}(z)]=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\beta^{m}}{m!}\int_{0}^{z}...\int_{0}^{z_{m-1}}dz_{1}...dz_{m}\overrightarrow{\mathbf{N}}\mathbb{K}\left\{\prod_{q=1}^{m}{\mathbf{G}}(z_{q})\right\}

where
\beta
is an arbitrary constant. It is important to note that the summation in (3.13) begins from
m=1
and not
m=0
. These can also be written as

(3.14)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{M}}[\mathbf{G}(z)]=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{{\beta}^{m}}{m!}\int\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{m}[z]\overrightarrow{\mathbf{N}}\mathbb{E}\left\{\prod_{q=1}^{m}{\mathbf{G}}(z_{q})\right\}

(3.15)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{C}}[\mathbf{G}(z)]=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{{\beta}^{m}}{m!}\int\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{m}[z]\overrightarrow{\mathbf{N}}\mathbb{K}\left\{\prod_{q=1}^{m}{\mathbf{G}}(z_{q})\right\}

where
\int\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{m}(z)=\int...\int dz_{1}...dz_{m}
is a ’path integral’. Choosing
{\beta}=-\alpha\mathsf{A}

(3.16)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{M}}[\mathbf{G}(z)]=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}(\alpha\mathsf{A})^{m}}{m!}\int\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{m}[z]\overrightarrow{\mathbf{N}}\mathbb{E}\left\{\prod_{q=1}^{m}{\mathbf{G}}(z_{q})\right\}

(3.17)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{C}}[\mathbf{G}(z)]=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}(\alpha\mathsf{A})^{m}}{m!}\int\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{m}[z]\overrightarrow{\mathbf{N}}\mathbb{K}\left\{\prod_{q=1}^{m}{\mathbf{G}}(z_{q})\right\}

The relation between the MGF and the CGF is
[60,62]

(3.18)

\displaystyle\log{\mathscr{M}}[\mathbf{G}(z)]={\mathscr{C}}[\mathbf{G}(z)]

so that

(3.19)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{M}}[\mathbf{G}(z)]=\exp\big({\mathscr{C}}[\mathbf{G}(z)]\big)

Hence

(3.20)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}(\alpha\mathsf{A})^{m}}{m!}\int\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{m}[z]\overrightarrow{\mathbf{N}}\mathbb{E}\left\{\prod_{q=1}^{m}{\mathbf{G}}(z_{q})\right\}

(3.21)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}(\alpha\mathsf{A})^{m}}{m!}\int\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{m}[z]\overrightarrow{\mathbf{N}}\mathbb{K}\left\{\prod_{q=1}^{m}\mathbf{G}(z_{q})\right\}\right)

However

\displaystyle{\mathscr{M}}[\mathbf{G}(z)]\equiv\mathbb{E}\left\{\exp\left(-\alpha\mathsf{A}\int_{0}^{z}{\mathbf{G}}(\bar{z})d\bar{z}\right)\right\}

(3.22)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}(\alpha\mathsf{A})^{m}}{m!}\int\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{m}[z]\overrightarrow{\mathbf{N}}\mathbb{E}\left\{\prod_{q=1}^{m}{\mathbf{G}}(z_{q})\right\}

giving a perturbation series in terms of cumulants to all orders.

\displaystyle{\mathscr{M}}[\mathbf{G}(z)]\equiv\mathbb{E}\left\{\exp\left(-\alpha\mathsf{A}\int_{0}^{z}\mathbf{G}(\bar{z})d\bar{z}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}(\alpha\mathsf{A})^{m}}{m!}\int\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{m}[z]\overrightarrow{\mathbf{N}}\mathbb{E}\left\{\prod_{q=1}^{m}{\mathbf{G}}(z_{q})\right\}

(3.23)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}(\alpha\mathsf{A})^{m}}{m!}\int\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{m}[z]\overrightarrow{\mathbf{N}}\mathbb{K}\left\{\prod_{q=1}^{m}{\mathbf{G}}(z_{q})\right\}\right)

The solution is then (3.8).
∎

With Gaussian random functions, the summation only needs to be taken to 2nd order (m=2) since all higher-order cumulants are zero. That is

(3.24)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=3}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}(\alpha\mathsf{A})^{m}}{m!}\int\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{m}[z]\overrightarrow{\mathbf{N}}\mathbb{K}\left\{\prod_{q=1}^{m}{\mathbf{G}}(z_{q})\right\}=0

Lemma 3.4
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})=\mathbb{E}\big\{\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})}\big\}=\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}~z)\mathbb{E}\left\{\exp\left(-\alpha\mathsf{A}\int_{0}^{z}{\mathbf{G}}(z)dz\right)\right\}

(3.25)

\displaystyle=\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}~z)\exp\left(\alpha^{2}\mathsf{A}^{2}\int_{0}^{z}\int_{0}^{z_{2}}dz_{1}dz_{2}\phi(z_{1},z_{2};\xi)\right)

Proof.

Expanding (3.8) to second order, all high-order terms vanish

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})=\big\{\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})}\bigg\}=\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}~z)\mathbb{E}\left\{\exp\left(-\alpha\mathsf{A}\int_{0}^{z}{\mathbf{G}}(z)dz\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}~z)\exp\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}(\alpha\mathsf{A})^{m}}{m!}\int\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{m}[z]\overrightarrow{\mathbf{N}}\mathbb{K}\left\{\prod_{q=1}^{m}\mathbf{G}(z_{q})\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}~z)\exp\left(\sum_{m=1}^{2}\frac{(-1)^{m}(\alpha\mathsf{A})^{m}}{m!}\int\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{m}[z]\overrightarrow{\mathbf{N}}\mathbb{K}\left\{\prod_{q=1}^{m}\mathbf{G}(z_{q})\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}~z)\exp\bigg(-\alpha\mathsf{A}\int_{0}^{z}dz_{1}\mathbb{K}\big\{{\mathbf{G}}(z)\big\}

\displaystyle+\alpha^{2}\mathsf{A}^{2}\int_{0}^{z}\int_{0}^{z_{1}}dz_{1}dz_{2}\mathbb{K}\big\{{\mathbf{G}}(z_{1})\otimes{\mathbf{G}}(z_{2})\big\}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}~z)\exp\bigg(-\alpha\mathsf{A}\int_{0}^{z}dz_{1}\mathbf{E}\big\{{\mathbf{G}}(z)\big\}

\displaystyle+\alpha^{2}\mathsf{A}^{2}\int_{0}^{z}\int_{0}^{z_{1}}dz_{1}dz_{2}\mathbb{E}\big\{{\mathbf{G}}(z_{1}){\otimes}{\mathbf{G}}(z_{2})\big\}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}~z)\exp\left(\alpha^{2}\mathsf{A}^{2}\int_{0}^{z}\int_{0}^{z_{1}}dz_{1}dz_{2}\mathbb{E}\big\{{\mathbf{Q}}(z_{1})\otimes{\mathbf{Q}}(z_{2})\big\}\right)

(3.26)

\displaystyle=\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}~z)\exp\left(\alpha^{2}\mathsf{A}^{2}\int_{0}^{z}\int_{0}^{z_{1}}dz_{1}dz_{2}\phi(z_{1},z_{2};\xi)\right)

since
\mathbb{K}\{\mathbf{G}(z_{1})\}\equiv\mathbb{E}\{\mathbf{G}(z_{1})\}=0
and
\mathbb{K}\{\mathbf{G}(z_{1})\otimes\mathbf{G}(z_{2})\}=\mathbb{E}\{\mathbf{G}(z_{1})\otimes\mathbf{G}(z_{2})\}=\phi(z_{1},z_{2};\xi)

∎

Corollary 3.5
.

(3.27)

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})}\longrightarrow\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}~z)

as
\alpha\rightarrow 0
or
\mathbf{G}(z)\rightarrow 0
and the original Beer’s law solution is recovered.

Lemma 3.6
.

The stochastic average
\mathbb{I}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})
is a solution of the ordinary differential equation

(3.28)

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathbb{I}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})}{dz}=\mathsf{A}\big(\alpha^{2}\mathsf{A}\theta(z)-1\big)\mathbb{I}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})

where
\theta(z)
is the integral

(3.29)

\displaystyle\theta(z_{1})=\int_{0}^{z_{1}}\phi(z_{1},z_{2};\xi)dz_{2}

and so Beer’s Law is recovered for
\alpha=0
.

Proof.

Taking the derivative of (3.25) gives

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathbb{I}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3}}{dz}=-\mathsf{A}\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}z)\exp\left(\alpha^{2}\mathsf{A}^{2}\int_{0}^{z}\theta(z_{1})dz_{1}\right)

(3.30)

\displaystyle+\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}z)\alpha^{2}\mathsf{A}^{2}\theta(z)\exp\left(\alpha^{2}\mathsf{A}^{2}\int_{0}^{z}\theta(z_{1})dz_{1}\right)

and so (3.28) follows
∎

3.1.
Estimate of
\mathbb{E}\big\{\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})}\big\}
for a Gaussian decay correlation

The expression is now explicitely computed for a Gaussian correlation function

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\{\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}})}\big\}\equiv\mathbb{I}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})=\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}z)\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}\mathsf{A}^{2}\bm{\mathsf{C}}\int_{0}^{z}\int_{0}^{z_{1}}\phi(z_{1},z_{2};\xi)dz_{1}dz_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}z)\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}\mathsf{A}^{2}\bm{\mathsf{C}}\int_{0}^{z}\int_{0}^{z_{1}}\exp(-|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}\xi^{-2}dz_{1}dz_{2}\right)

(3.31)

\displaystyle=\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}z)\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}\mathsf{A}^{2}\bm{\mathsf{C}}{\mathcal{Y}}(z)\right)=\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}z){\mathcal{B}}(\alpha,\mathsf{A},z)

This is then Beer’s law of exponential attenuation
\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}z)
multiplied by a small ’boost’ factor
\mathcal{B}(\alpha,\mathsf{A},z)
.
Note that because
\alpha
is small for small fluctuations about the mean with
\alpha\sim 0
or
\alpha\ll 1
, the exponential term
\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}\mathsf{A}^{2}\bm{\mathsf{C}}{\mathcal{Y}}(z)\right)

\mathcal{Y}(z)
.

Lemma 3.7
.

(3.32)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Y}}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\int_{0}^{z_{1}}\exp(-|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}\xi^{-2})dz_{1}dz_{2}

then

(3.33)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Y}}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\bigg|\int_{0}^{z_{1}}\exp(-|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}\xi^{-2})dz_{2}\bigg|dz_{1}=\int_{0}^{z}{\mathcal{W}}(z_{1})dz_{1}

The evaluated integral is then

(3.34)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Y}}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\mathcal{W}(z_{1})dz_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\xi\left[\exp(-z^{2}/\xi^{2})\left(\sqrt{\pi}zErf\left(\frac{z}{\xi}\right)\exp\left(\frac{z^{2}}{\xi^{2}}\right)+{\xi}\right)-{\xi}\right]

Proof.

The inner integral is an incomplete Gaussian integral which gives the standard error function Erf so that

(3.35)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(z_{1})=\int_{0}^{z_{1}}\exp(-|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}\xi^{-2})dz_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\pi}Erf\left(\frac{z_{1}}{\xi}\right)\xi

The error function has the properties
Erf(0)=0
and
Erf(\infty)=1
. Then

(3.36)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Y}}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}{\mathcal{W}}(z_{1})dz_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\pi}\int_{0}^{z}Erf\left(\frac{z_{1}}{\xi}\right)\xi dz_{1}

The full integral is then

(3.37)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\mathcal{W}(z_{1})dz_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\xi\left[\exp(-z^{2}/\xi^{2})\left(\sqrt{\pi}zErf\left(\frac{z}{\xi}\right)\exp\left(\frac{z^{2}}{\xi^{2}}\right)+\xi\right)-\xi\right]

and so

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}\mathsf{A}^{2}\bm{\mathsf{C}}{\mathcal{Y}}(z)\right)=\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}\mathsf{A}^{2}\bm{\mathsf{C}}\int_{0}^{z}\bigg|\int_{0}^{z_{1}}\exp(-|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}\xi^{-2})dz_{2}\bigg|dz_{1}\right)

(3.38)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{1}{4}\alpha^{2}\mathsf{A}^{2}\bm{\mathsf{C}}\xi\left[\exp(-z^{2}/\xi^{2})\left(\sqrt{\pi}zErf\left(\frac{z}{\xi}\right)\exp\left(\frac{z^{2}}{\xi^{2}}\right)+\xi\right)-\xi\right]\right)

∎

Note that
{\mathcal{Y}}(z)
is dimensionless as required. The following theorem has now been established and proved.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\mathbb{I\!D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a slab geometry along of depth
\mathrm{L}
with boundaries
z=0
and
z=\mathrm{L}
and
\mathrm{L}\gg 1

\psi_{o}

\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3}

\mathsf{A}

\mathsf{A}

\psi(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})=\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}z)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\{{\mathbf{G}}(z_{1}){\otimes}{\mathbf{G}}(z_{2})\big\}=\phi(z_{1},z_{2};\xi)=\bm{\mathsf{C}}\exp(-|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}\xi^{-2})

\mathbb{E}\{\mathbf{G}(z)\}=0
and correlation length
\xi
. The beam propagation and attenuation within the stochastic medium is then described by the stochastic differential equation

(3.41)

\displaystyle d\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})}=-\mathsf{A}\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\mathbf{e}_{3})}dz-\alpha\mathsf{A}\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\mathbf{e}_{3})}{\mathbf{G}}(z)dz

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})=\mathbb{E}\big\{\widehat{\bm{\psi}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}}_{3})}\big\}=\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}z)\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}\mathsf{A}^{2}\bm{\mathsf{C}}\int_{0}^{z}\bigg|\int_{0}^{z_{1}}{\phi}(z_{1},z_{2};\xi))dz_{2}\bigg|dz_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}z)\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}\mathsf{A}^{2}\bm{\mathsf{C}}\int_{0}^{z}\bigg|\int_{0}^{z_{1}}\exp(-|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}\xi^{-2})dz_{2}\bigg|dz_{1}\right)

(3.42)

\displaystyle=\psi_{o}\exp(-\mathsf{A}z)\exp\left(\frac{1}{4}\alpha^{2}\mathsf{A}^{2}\bm{\mathsf{C}}\xi\left[\exp(-z^{2}/\xi^{2})\left(\sqrt{\pi}zErf\left(\frac{z}{\xi}\right)\exp\left(\frac{z^{2}}{\xi^{2}}\right)+\xi\right)-\xi\right]\right)

\mathbb{I}(z,\widehat{\mathbf{e}_{3}})

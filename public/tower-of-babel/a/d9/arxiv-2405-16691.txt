Abstract
. A walk of length n n in a graph is consistent if there exists an automorphism of the graph that maps the initial n − 1 n-1 vertices to the final n − 1 n-1 vertices of the walk. In this paper we find some sufficient conditions for a consistent walk in an arc-transitive graph to have a trivial pointwise stabilizer. We show that in that case, the size of the smallest generating set of the group is bounded by the valence of the graph.

05E18, 05C25.

1.
Introduction

For a graph
\Gamma
and a group
G\leq\text{{\rm Aut}}(\Gamma)
, we say that a walk
(v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
of length
n\geq 1
is
G
-consistent
if there exists an automorphism
g\in G
(called a
shunt
for the walk) such that
v_{i}^{g}=v_{i+1}
for all
i\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
.

Consistent walks play a prominent role in algebraic graph theory and feature already in the seminal work of Tutte
[
16
,
17
]
on cubic arc-transitive graphs, where he proved that the order of a vertex stabilizer
G_{v}
in a
G
-arc-transitive
3
-valent graph is bounded from above by the constant
48
. In his proof, he considered the largest value of
s
such that
G
acts transitively on the set of all
s
-arcs of
\Gamma
(observe that then every
s
-arc is a
G
-consistent walk). Tutte’s proof consists of two parts:

(i)

show that the stabilizer of an
s
-arc is trivial,

(ii)

show that
s
is bounded by
5
.

Bounding the order of a vertex-stabilizer in highly symmetric graphs has become a classical theme in algebraic graph theory, see
[
13
,
12
,
14
,
15
,
18
,
19
,
20
]
.
In this paper, we will be interested in generalizations of part (i) of Tutte’s approach. In particular, we will be interested in circumstances in which we can guarantee the existence of a
G
-consistent walk with a trivial stabilizer. We will focus on the conditions imposed on the
local group

G_{v}^{\Gamma(v)}
, that is, the permutation group induced by the action of a vertex stabilizer on the neighborhood of that vertex. As a consequence of a technical result, Lemma
3.6
proved in Section
3
, the following two results are obtained: if
\Gamma
is a
G
-arc-transitive graph and either its valence is at most
4
, or the local group
G_{v}^{\Gamma(v)}
is a weakly
p
-subregular group, then there exists a
G
-consistent walk with a trivial stabilizer (see Definition
4.1
in Section
4
). In Section
6
we find examples of graphs in which no
G
-consistent walk has a trivial stabilizer.

Finally, in Section
7
, we use the techniques and results developed in this paper
to address a question raised by Marco Barbieri and Pablo Spiga in
[
3
]
. Indeed,
[
3
, Problem 4]
asks under which additional assumptions one can guarantee existence of a function
f(d)
such that every vertex-transitive group of automorphisms of a finite
d
-valent graph can be generated by
f(d)
generators. As a partial answer to this question,
we show that whenever a vertex-transitive group
G
of automorphisms of a finite
d
-valent graph
\Gamma
is such that there exists a
G
-consistent walk with a trivial stabilizer in
G
, the size of the smallest generating set of
G
is bounded from above by the valence of
\Gamma
(see Corollary
7.4
for a detailed statement). In particular, this implies that the assumption of having weakly
p
-subregular local group implies that
G
is
3
-generated.

Definition 2.2
.

Let

Let

Let

\displaystyle={\alpha}^{\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}({\alpha})}

\displaystyle=\beta^{G_{(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})}}.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\Gamma

be a vertex-transitive subgroup of

Take

Now observe that

3.
Some conditions for a
G
-consistent walk to have a trivial stabilizer

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph and let
G\leq\text{{\rm Aut}}(\Gamma)
.
Fix a
G
-consistent walk
\alpha=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{n})
of length
n\geq 2
.
For two walks
\beta=(b_{0},\ldots,b_{n-1}),\ \gamma=(c_{0},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in\hat{\alpha}^{G}
we write
\beta\sim_{\alpha}\gamma
with respect to
G
provided that there exists a sequence of walks
\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{m}\in\alpha^{G}
such that
\hat{\beta_{0}}=\beta,\ \hat{\beta_{m}}=\gamma
and that for every
i\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
the walk
\beta_{i+1}
is a
G
-successor of the walk
\beta_{i}
.

We will just say that
\beta\sim_{\alpha}\gamma
and omit
G
when
G
is clear from context.
The next result appears without proof in
[
18
]
, we will prove it here for the convenience of the reader. The rest of the results in this section are also inspired by
[

]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group that acts transitively on a set
X
. If
R
is a transitive nonempty binary relation on the set
X
that is preserved by
G
, it is an equivalence relation.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma
be a not neccessarily simple digraph with vertex set
V(\Gamma)=X
and arc set
A(\Gamma)=\{(x,y)\mid xRy\}
. Since the group
G
preserves the relation
R
, it acts on the digraph
\Gamma
as a group of automorphisms, this action is vertex-transitive.
Suppose that
R
is not a symmetric relation. Since
G
is transitive, this implies that for every vertex
u_{0}
there exists an arc
(u_{0},u_{1})\in A(\Gamma)
, such that
(u_{1},u_{0})\notin A(\Gamma)
. Since
X
is finite, there exists a cycle
(v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n},v_{0})
in
\Gamma
, such that
(v_{i},v_{i+1})\in A(\Gamma)
and
(v_{i+1},v_{i})\notin A(\Gamma)
for all
i\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
, where indices are computed modulo
n
. However, by transitivity of the relation
R
we see that
(v_{1},v_{0})\in A(\Gamma)
, which is a contradiction, implying that
R
is a symmetric relation. Since
R\neq\emptyset
, transitivity of
G
implies that for every
u\in X
there exists
v\in X
such that
uRv
. Then
vRu
, and by transitivity of
R
, also
uRu
. This shows that
R
is reflexive and thus an equivalence relation.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph, let
G\leq\text{{\rm Aut}}(\Gamma)
and let
\alpha=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{n})
be a
G
-consistent walk. Then the relation
\sim_{\alpha}
is an equivalence relation.

Proof.

Througout this proof we will use the symbol
\sim
to denote the relation
\sim_{\alpha}
defined on the set
\hat{\alpha}^{G}
.
The relation is nonempty, since
\hat{\alpha}\sim\hat{\alpha}
.

Let us now show that the relation
\sim
is transitive. Let
\beta,\gamma,\delta\in\hat{\alpha}^{G}
such that
\beta\sim\gamma
and
\gamma\sim\delta
. Then there exist sequences of walks
\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{m}
, and
\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{k}
, where
\beta_{i},\gamma_{i}\in\alpha^{G}
, such that
\hat{\beta_{0}}=\beta,\hat{\beta_{m}}=\gamma
,
\hat{\gamma_{0}}=\gamma,\hat{\gamma_{k}}=\delta
, and that every walk in each of these two sequences is a
G
-successor of the previous walk in the sequence.
If
\beta=\gamma
, then
\beta\sim\delta
. If
\beta\neq\gamma
, then
m\geq 1
. Note that since
\hat{\gamma_{0}}=\hat{\beta_{m}}
and since

, the walk
\gamma_{0}
is a
G
-successor of the walk
\beta_{m-1}

In the sequence of walks
\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{m-1},\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{k}
, every walk is a
G
-successor of the previous walk, thus
\beta\sim\delta
. This proves that the relation
\sim
is transitive.

Let us now prove that the relation
\sim
is preserved by

. Let
g\in G
and suppose that
\beta\sim\gamma
for two walks
\beta,\gamma\in\hat{\alpha}^{G}
. Let
\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{m}
be the required walk from the definition of

. Then
\beta_{0}^{g},\ldots,\beta_{m}^{g}
is a sequence of walks in
\alpha^{G}
that shows that
\beta^{g}\sim\gamma^{g}
. Hence

preserves the relation
\sim
.
Since

acts transitively on its orbit

,
Lemma

\sim
implies that

is an equivalence relation.
∎

The following property of consistent walks will play a crucial role in the forthcoming analysis. Intuitively a
G
-consistent walk
\alpha
has this property if every vertex of the graph can be reached from
\alpha
by a sequence of
G
-successors. Hence we shall call it

or (R) for short.

Property (R)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph and let
G
be an arc-transitive subgroup of
\text{{\rm Aut}}(\Gamma)
. We say that a
G
-consistent walk
\alpha
has Property (R) with respect to
G
, provided that for every
v\in V(\Gamma)
there exists a sequence of walks
\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{m}
such that
\beta_{0}=\alpha
, the last vertex of the walk
\beta_{m}
is
v
, and
\beta_{i+1}\in\text{{\rm Succ}}_{G}(\beta_{i})
for all
i\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
.

We will again omit
G
when it is clear from context, and just say that a walk has Property (R).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph, let
G\leq\text{{\rm Aut}}(\Gamma)
and let
\alpha
be a
G
-consistent walk. Then
\alpha
has Property (R) with respect to
G
if and only if the group
\langle\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\alpha)\rangle
generated by all the shunts of
\alpha
contained in
G
acts transitively on

.

Proof.

Let
\alpha=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{n})
. Suppose first that
\langle\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\alpha)\rangle
acts transitively on
V(\Gamma)
. Let
v\in V(\Gamma)
. If
v=v_{n}
, then there is nothing to prove. Henceforth assume
v\neq v_{n}
. By transitivity of
\langle\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\alpha)\rangle
there exists an automorphism
g=g_{1}\cdots g_{\ell}\in\langle\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\alpha)\rangle
, where
g_{i}\in\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\alpha)
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
, such that
v_{n}^{g}=v
. Let
\beta_{0}=\alpha
and for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
recursively define
h_{i}=g_{\ell-i+1}^{h_{1}\cdots h_{i-1}}
and
\beta_{i}=\alpha^{h_{1}\cdots h_{i}}
. We will prove that for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}

(
a
)

h_{1}\cdots h_{i}=g_{\ell-i+1}\cdots g_{\ell}
,

(
b
)

h_{i}\in\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\beta_{i-1})
.

In case when
i=1
the claims
(
a
)
and
(
b
)
follow directly from the fact that
h_{1}=g_{\ell}\in\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\alpha)=\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\beta_{0})
. Suppose that
i\geq 2
and that
(
a
)
and
(
b
)
hold for all smaller

. Then
h_{1}\cdots h_{i-1}h_{i}=h_{1}\cdots h_{i-1}g_{\ell-i+1}^{h_{1}\cdots h_{i-1}}=g_{\ell-i+1}h_{1}\ldots h_{i-1}=g_{\ell-i+1}g_{\ell-i+2}\cdots g_{\ell}
,
so Claim
(
a
)
is true. For any
G
-consistent walk
\gamma
and an automorphism

note that
\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\gamma^{f})=\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\gamma)^{f}
.
Since
g_{\ell-i+1}\in\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\alpha)
and
h_{1}\cdots h_{i-1}
maps
\alpha
to
\beta_{i-1}
, it follows that
h_{i}=g_{\ell-i+1}^{h_{1}\cdots h_{i-1}}\in\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\beta_{i-1})
, which proves Claim

b
)
.
We proved that for all

the walk

is a

and that

, thus the last vertex of the walk

is

. Therefore

has Property (R).

Suppose now that the walk
\alpha
has Property (R). Let

be a vertex in
\Gamma
. We will show that there exists

such that

. Let
\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{\ell}
be a sequence of walks such that
\beta_{0}=\alpha
, the last vertex of the walk

is
v
, and
\beta_{i}=\beta_{i-1}^{h_{i}}

for all

. Let

. Then
v_{n}^{h}=v
. For all

note that
\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\beta_{i})=\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\beta_{i-1})^{h_{i}}
, and

Suppose that the Condition

We showed that

Let

4.
Graphs with locally weakly

-subregular group actions

In this section we will consider

-arc-transitive graphs where the local group

on the set

, belongs to a class of

-subregular
permutation groups, first defined in

Definition 4.1
.

Let
p
be a prime number. A transitive permutation group

is weakly
p
-subregular on the set
\Omega
, if there exist
x,y\in\Omega
such that
|L_{x}|=p
and
x^{\overline{L}}\cup y^{\overline{L}}=\Omega
, where
\overline{L}=\langle L_{x},L_{y}\rangle
.

Some examples of weakly
p
-subregular permutation groups include the dihedral groups in their natural actions (for
p=2
), the wreath product
C_{p}\wr C_{2}
in its natural imprimitive action on
\{1,\ldots,p\}\times\{1,2\}
as well as
\text{SL}(2,p)
on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
, see
[
18
,
19
]
for further examples.

The following lemma was proved as Claim 1 in the proof of Theorem 2.2 in
[
19
]
. Note that the formulation here is equivalent to formulation in
[
19
]
, since

\langle\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}((x,v,y))\rangle=\langle G_{(x,v)},g\rangle
, where
g\in G
is any such automorphism that
(x,v)^{g}=(v,y)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a connected graph, let
G
be an arc-transitive subgroup of
\text{{\rm Aut}}(\Gamma)
, let

and let

. Suppose that there exist

, such that

, where

. Then
\langle\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}((x,v,y))\rangle
is transitive on

.

Theorem 4.3
.

let

be an arc-transitive subgroup of the automorphism group of a graph

-consistent walk

Proof.

Let

. Then by assumptions there exist vertices

such that

and

Let
\alpha
be a maximal

-consistent walk that begins with the sequence

.
We first need to show that such walks exist; we will show that

.
Let

and

, and let

Denote also

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a connected vertex-transitive graph that has no twin vertices and is not isomorphic to
\text{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},S)
with
\pm 1\in S
for any

. Then
\tilde{\Gamma}=\Gamma[\overline{K_{m}}]
contains no consistent walk with a trivial stabilizer.

Proof.

Every consistent walk induces a consistent cycle, whose stabilizer is a subgroup of the stabilizer of the consistent walk. Hence it is enough to show that
\tilde{\Gamma}
contains no consistent cycle with a trivial stabilizer.
If a consistent cycle in
\Gamma
contains all the vertices of
\Gamma
, then its shunt generates a cyclic vertex-regular subgroup of
\text{{\rm Aut}}(\Gamma)
, which contradicts the assumption that
\Gamma
is not isomorphic to
\text{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},S)
with
\pm 1\in S
for some

.
Hence there exists no consistent cycle in
\Gamma
that constains all the vertices of
\Gamma

Suppose that
\Gamma
is a

, there exist

\Gamma
. We denote their representatives by
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}
(note that one of them is the trivial cycle that only contains two distinct vertices). For every

we can build

\beta_{i,1},\beta_{i,2}\ldots\beta_{i,m}
in
\tilde{\Gamma}
. Let
\alpha_{i}=(v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{k-1},v_{k},v_{0})
, let

be a shunt for
\alpha_{i}
and let

. Then, define

\displaystyle\beta_{i,1}

\displaystyle=((v_{0},0),(v_{1},0),\ldots,(v_{k-1},0)(v_{k},0),(v_{0},0)),

\displaystyle\beta_{i,2}

\displaystyle=((v_{0},0),(v_{1},0),\ldots,(v_{k-1},0)(v_{k},0),(v_{0},1),(v_{1},1),\ldots,(v_{k-1},1)(v_{k},0)),

\displaystyle\beta_{i,m}

\displaystyle=((v_{0},0),(v_{1},0),\ldots,(v_{k-1},0)(v_{k},0),(v_{0},1),(v_{1},1),\ldots,(v_{k-1},1)(v_{k},2),\ldots

\displaystyle\ldots,(v_{0},m-1),(v_{1},m-1),\ldots,(v_{k-1},m-1)(v_{k},0)).

Note that all the sequences
\beta_{i,1},\beta_{i,2},\ldots,\beta_{i,m}
are cycles in
\tilde{\Gamma}
. They are consistent, since

(where

acts naturally on

) is a subgroup
of the automorphism group of

, and
(((0\ 1\ \ldots\ j),\text{id},\ldots,\text{id}),g_{i})\in S_{m}\wr\text{{\rm Aut}}(\Gamma)

We claim that the cycles

are representatives of different

-orbits of consistent cycles in
\tilde{\Gamma}
. Observe that for all

, the cycles

and

have different length, hence they are included in different
\text{{\rm Aut}}(\tilde{\Gamma})
-orbit of consistent cycles.

We are left with the case

.
Suppose there exist

and

such that

and

For every

.
Then every permutation

(v_{i},0)
and
(v_{i},1)

while fixing all other vertices of
\tilde{\Gamma}
is an automorphism of
\tilde{\Gamma}
that fixes
\beta_{i,j}
. Hence
\beta_{i,j}
does not have a trivial stabilizer.

Since
\tilde{\Gamma}
is a vertex-transitive
dm
-valent graph, there exists
dm

\text{{\rm Aut}}(\tilde{\Gamma})
-orbits of consistent cycles by Theorem
2.1
. There are
dm
many cycles
\beta_{i,j}
, it follows that must include one representative from each
\text{{\rm Aut}}(\tilde{\Gamma})
-orbit of consistent cycles. Therefore no consistent cycle in
\tilde{\Gamma}
, and hence no consistent walk in
\tilde{\Gamma}
, has a trivial stabilizer.
∎

7.
Consistent cycles and group generators

Let
\Gamma
be a connected
d
-valent graph and let
G
be a vertex-transitive subgroup of
\text{{\rm Aut}}(\Gamma)
.
Recall, that by a result of Conway
[
4
,
10
]
there exist
d

G
-orbits of
G
-consistent cycles in
\Gamma
. Moreover, as proved in
[
11
, Lemma 3.5]
, one can choose one representative from each of the orbits so that the representatives of any two orbits share as many initial vertices as is possible for any two cycles from those orbits. Let us make this more precise.

Definition 7.1

(
[
11
]
)
.

Let
\alpha=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{k})
and
\beta=(b_{0},\ldots,b_{\ell})
be two walks. Then their overlap
m(\alpha,\beta)=-1
if
a_{0}\neq b_{0}
, and, if
a_{0}=b_{0}
,

m(\alpha,\beta)=\text{max}\{t\mid a_{i}=b_{i}\hbox{ for }i=0,1,2,...,t\}.

Similarly, if
A=\alpha^{G}
and
B=\beta^{G}
are two distinct
G
-orbits of
G
-consistent walks, then

m(A,B)=\max\{m(\alpha,\beta):\alpha\in A,\beta\in B\}.

Lemma 7.2

(
[
11
, Lemma 3.5]
)
.

If
G\leq\text{{\rm Aut}}(\Gamma)
, then there exists a complete set of representatives
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{d}
of the
G
-orbits of
G
-consistent cycles such that
m(\beta_{i},\beta_{j})=m(\beta_{i}^{G},\beta_{j}^{G})
for every pair
i,j
,

.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{d}\}
be such a set of representatives of
G
-orbits of
G
-conistent cycles and let
g_{1},\ldots,g_{d}\in G
be one shunt for each of
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{d}
respectively.
Let
S=\langle g_{1},\ldots,g_{d}\rangle
. We will prove that if there exists a
G
-consistent cycle
\alpha
such that
G_{\alpha}=1
, then
S=G
. In particular
\mathbf{d}(G)\leq\text{val}(G)
, where
\mathbf{d}(G)
denotes the size of the smallest generating set of

.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a connected graph, let
G
be a vertex-transitive subgroup of
\text{{\rm Aut}}(\Gamma)
and let
\tau
be a
G
-consistent walk. Suppose that

is such that

and that

.

Proof.

Let
g\in\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\tau)
and
let
\alpha=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{n})
be the
G
-consistent cycle induced by
g
and
\tau
. We can assume that
\alpha=\beta_{1}
and

. For

.
Since
G
acts on the set of all
G
-consistent walks of length

, note that for every

(see Lemma
2.3
):

(4)

\displaystyle G_{\alpha_{i-1}}=\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\alpha_{i})g^{-1}.

We will inductively prove that for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
:

(1)

G_{\alpha_{i-1}}\leq H
,

(2)

\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\gamma)\subseteq H
for every
G
-consistent cycle
\gamma

m(\gamma,\alpha)\geq i
.

First let
i=n
. Then the walks

and

contain the same vertices, hence

. The only
G
-consistent cycle that has overlap at least

\gamma=\alpha
. Since
\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\alpha)=G_{\alpha_{n-1}}g

\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\gamma)\subseteq H
.

Suppose now that the claim is true for
i+1
, we will prove that it is then true for
i
.
Let
\gamma
be a
G
-consistent cycle such that
m(\gamma,\alpha)\geq i
. There exist

\beta\in\mathcal{C}
and

such that
\beta^{h}=\gamma
. Let

be a shunt for
\beta
.
Then

. Since
\beta
has the largest possible overlap with

of any

, hence

. Therefore

.
Let
\gamma_{i+1}=(c_{0},\ldots,c_{i},c_{i+1})
be the subwalk of
\gamma
that contains the first

\gamma
.
Then
\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\gamma)\subseteq\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\gamma_{i+1})
. Since the overlap of

for all

,
\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\gamma_{i+1})=G_{\alpha_{i}}s
, hence
\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\gamma)\subseteq H

2

To prove part
1
of the claim note that

\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\alpha_{i})=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{$\delta$; $\delta$ is a $G$-consistent cycle}\\
\text{and }\ m(\delta,\alpha)\geq i\end{subarray}}\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\delta),

since for every element of

-consistent cycle with a shunt

.
Hence by part

\text{{\rm Sh}}_{G}(\alpha_{i})\leq H

Since

We proved that
G_{v_{0}}\leq H

There exist

By a connectedness argument it follows that

∎

Corollary 7.4
.

Let

-valent graph

In particular, if

1.
Introduction

Let
R=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the standard graded polynomial ring over a field
K
with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
, and let
I\subset R
be a graded ideal. In
[
9
]
Cooper
et al.
introduced a new invariant associated to
I
which they called the
\operatorname{v}
-number of
I
, in honor of the mathematician Wolmer Vasconcelos. Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
be an associated prime. Since
I
is graded,
\mathfrak{p}
is a graded prime ideal and there exists an homogeneous polynomial
f\in R
such that
(I:f)=\mathfrak{p}
. The
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-number
of
I
is the least degree of such an element
f
. The
\operatorname{v}
-number
of
I
is defined as
\operatorname{v}(I)=\min_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)}\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
.

By Brodmann
[
7
]
, see also
[
14
]
, the set
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
stabilizes:
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k+1})=\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
. We denote the common sets
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
for
k\gg 0
by
\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
.

In
[
20
, Theorem 3.1]
, and independently by Conca in
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
, it was proved that the functions
k\mapsto\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})
, for
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
, and
k\mapsto\operatorname{v}(I^{k})
are linear for
k\gg 0
. By
[
20
, Theorem 4.1]
, we have
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\alpha(I)k+b
for
k\gg 0
, where
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\alpha(I)=\min\{d:(I/\mathfrak{m}I)_{d}\neq 0\}
is the
initial degree
of
I
. These results inspired many papers
[
5
,
6
,
8
,
15
,
19
,
20
,
22
,

31
,
32
,
37
,
39
]
, and a
Macaulay2
package
[
21
]
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph on the vertex set
V(G)=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
and edge set
E(G)
. The ideal
I(G)=(x_{i}x_{j}:\{x_{i},x_{j}\}\in E(G))
is called the
edge ideal
of
G
. A beautiful result of Biswas, Mandal, and Saha
[
6
, Theorem 5.1]
guarantees that
\operatorname{v}(I(G)^{k})=2k-1
for all
k\geq|E(G)|+1
if
G
is a connected graph. If
G
is an arbitrary graph with
c
connected components
G_{1},\dots,G_{c}
, then one could expect that
\operatorname{v}(I(G)^{k})=2k+(c-2)
for all
k\gg 0
. We show in Corollary
5.4
that this is indeed the case. Notice that
I(G)=I(G_{1})+\dots+I(G_{c})
and the generators of the ideals
I(G_{i})
,
i=1,\dots,c
, are monomials in pairwise disjoint sets of variables.

This fact suggest to study the following more general problem. Let
K
be a field, and let
I\subset R=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and
J\subset T=K[y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]
be proper homogeneous ideals. Set
S=R\otimes_{K}T=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]
. By abuse of notation we denote the extended ideals
IS
ans
JS
again by
I
and
J
.

Question 1.1
.

What can we say about the
\operatorname{v}
-functions
\operatorname{v}((IJ)^{k})
and
\operatorname{v}((I+J)^{k})
in terms of the
\operatorname{v}
-functions
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})
and
\operatorname{v}(J^{k})
?

To address Question
1.1
will be our main concern. The result in Corollary
5.4
, which was our original motivation for writing this paper, will be a consequence of the more general theory we are going to develop.

Now, we outline the main contents and findings of this paper. In Section
2
we address and completely solve Question
1.1
in the case of the product
(IJ)^{k}
. It is noted in Lemma
2.1
that
\operatorname{Ass}((IJ)^{k})=\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})\cup\operatorname{Ass}(J^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
. Hence, in Theorem
2.2
the local
\operatorname{v}
-functions of
(IJ)^{k}
are calculated and in Corollary
2.3
we prove that
\operatorname{v}((IJ)^{k})=\min\{\operatorname{v}(I^{k})+\alpha(J)k,\,\operatorname{v}(J^{k})+\alpha(I)k\}
for
k\gg 0
. These results and the general computation of the
\operatorname{v}
-number rest on the following result due to Conca
[
8
]
. Denote by
\operatorname{Max}(I)
the set of prime ideals of
\operatorname{Ass}(I)
maximal with respect to inclusion. For an homogeneous ideal
I\subset S
and
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
, set

X_{\mathfrak{p}}\ =\ \begin{cases}\displaystyle(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)\ :\ \mathfrak{p}\subsetneq\mathfrak{p}_{1}}\mathfrak{p}_{1})&\textit{if}\ \ \mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)\setminus\operatorname{Max}(I),\\
\hfill S&\hfill\textit{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(1)

Lemma 1.2
.

[
8
, Lemma 1.2]
Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal and
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
. Then,

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)\ =\ \alpha\biggl(\frac{(I:\mathfrak{p})}{I:(\mathfrak{p}+X_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\infty})}\biggr).

Here
I:(\mathfrak{p}+X_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\infty})=\bigcup_{k\geq 0}(I:(\mathfrak{p}+X_{\mathfrak{p}}^{k}))
.

Regarding the
\operatorname{v}
-function
\operatorname{v}((I+J)^{k})
, we do not have yet a complete solution to Question
1.1
. But we can provide a satisfactory answer when
I
and
J
are monomial ideals, as we describe next.

We take a moment to justify why we confine ourselves only to monomial ideals. From the computational point of view, it is reasonable to restrict our attention only to monomial ideals. Indeed, the associated primes of a monomial ideal are monomial prime ideals
[
28
, Corollary 1.3.9]
, that is, ideals generated by a subset of the variables. Thus, if
L\subset S
is a monomial ideal, there are only finitely many candidates that can be prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(L)
, precisely the
2^{\dim S}-1=2^{n+m}-1
prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}_{A}=(z:z\in A)
where
A\subseteq\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{m}\}
is non-empty. For each
\mathfrak{p}_{A}
, Theorem
3.2
is an efficient computational criterion, which was implemented in the
Macaulay2

[
25
]
package
VNumber

[
21
]
, to check if
\mathfrak{p}_{A}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(L)
. This result was firstly noted by Bayati, Herzog, and Rinaldo in
[
2
, Section 2]
and implemented in
[
3
]
, and takes advantage of the so-called
monomial localization
. Ordinary localization would destroy the multigraded structure of
L
. On the other hand, when
L
and
\mathfrak{p}
are arbitrary homogeneous ideals of
S
, one can not use monomial localization and Theorem
3.2
becomes inefficient from the computational point of view as pointed out in
[
22
, Section 4]
. Moreover, for a general homogeneous ideal
L
, a priori there is no obvious finite set of homogeneous prime ideals which contains
\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(L)
. In
[
30
]
Kim and Swanson even provided examples of prime ideals of
S
whose number of associated primes of powers are exponential in the number of variables of
S
.

Coming back to the computation of
\operatorname{v}((I+J)^{k})
, notice that for
k\gg 0

\operatorname{v}((I+J)^{k})\ =\ \min_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I+J)}\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}((I+J)^{k}).

Thus, to study this function one has to know the set
\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I+J)
in terms of the sets
\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
and
\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(J)
. This problem was already solved by Nguyen and Tran
[
35
, Theorem 4.5]
. For an ideal
L\subset S
, we set
\operatorname{Ass}^{*}(L)=\bigcup_{k\geq 1}\operatorname{Ass}(L^{k})
. By Brodmann
\operatorname{Ass}^{*}(L)
is a finite set
[
7
]
. Nguyen and Tran’s Theorem (here Theorem
3.1
) says that

\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I+J)\ =\ \bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{*}(I)\\
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(J)\end{subarray}}\{\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{q}\}\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)\\
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{*}(J)\end{subarray}}\{\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{q}\}.

The proof of Nguyen and Tran is based on the computation by Hà, Trung, and Trung of some filtrations of
S/(I+J)^{k}
and a well-known formula for
\operatorname{Ass}_{S}(M\otimes_{K}N)
where
M
is a
R
-module and
N
is a
T
-module
[
27
]
. In Section
3
we offer a new and relatively easy proof of this result when
I
and
J
are monomial ideals. The key ingredients of this new proof are Theorem
3.2
which was showed in
[
22
, Theorem 2.1]
and some easy basic facts from Hilbert function and Krull dimension theories. These facts are recalled in Remarks
3.3
,
3.4
and
3.5
. At the moment we do not know if our argument can be extended to all homogeneous ideals
I\subset R
and
J\subset T
.

In Section
4
we prove a formula for the local
\operatorname{v}
-numbers of
(I+J)^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
, when
I
and
J
are monomial ideals (Theorem
4.1
). For
k=1
we have
\operatorname{v}(I+J)=\operatorname{v}(I)+\operatorname{v}(J)
, see
[
38
, Proposition 3.9]
due to Saha and Sengupta. Due to some experimental evidence we could expect that the formula given in Theorem
4.1
is valid for any graded ideals
I\subset R
and
J\subset T
. Some results by Ambhore, Saha, and Sengupta
[
1
]
support this expectation.

In
[
20
, Question 5.1]
it is asked whether the inequality
\operatorname{v}(L^{k})<\operatorname{reg}(L^{k})
holds for all
k\gg 0
and all graded ideals
L\subset S
. In Corollary
2.4
we show that to solve this question we may assume that
L
can not be written as a product
L=IJ
, where
I
and
J
are homogeneous ideals whose generators are polynomials in pairwise disjoint sets of variables. In Corollary
4.4
we show that in the monomial case, we can also assume that
L
can not be written as the sum
I+J
of two proper monomial ideals
I
and
J
whose generators are monomials in pairwise disjoint sets of variables.

Finally, Section
5
contains several applications of Theorem
4.1
. It was proved in
[
19
, Proposition 2.2]
that for any monomial ideal
L\subset S
we have the natural lower bound
\operatorname{v}(L^{k})\geq\alpha(L)k-1
for all
k\geq 1
. Let
I_{1},\dots,I_{t}\subset S
be monomial ideals whose generators are monomials in pairwise disjoint sets of variables. Assuming that
\operatorname{v}(I_{j}^{k})=\alpha(I_{j})k-1
for all
j=1,\dots,t
and all
k\gg 0
, in Theorem
5.2
we provide for all
k\geq 1
a natural lower bound of
\operatorname{v}((I_{1}+\dots+I_{t})^{k})
. Under some additional assumptions this lower bound is achieved. This result is applied to edge ideals, monomial complete intersections and vertex splittable monomial ideals.

2.
The
\operatorname{v}
-function of products

Here and in the next section, we maintain the following notation.

Let
K
be a field, and let
I\subset R=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and
J\subset T=K[y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]
be proper homogeneous ideals. Let
S=R\otimes_{K}T=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]
. Notice that for any ideal
L\subset R
we have
\operatorname{Ass}(L)=\operatorname{Ass}(LS)
and for any ideal
H\subset T
we have
\operatorname{Ass}(H)=\operatorname{Ass}(HS)
. This fact justifies our next notational choice. With abuse of notation we denote the extended ideals
IS
and
JS
again by
I
and
J
.

The purpose of this section is to compute the (local)
\operatorname{v}
-function of
IJ
in terms of the (local)
\operatorname{v}
-functions of
I
and
J
. We begin with the next elementary observation.

Lemma 2.1
.

With the notation introduced, for all
k\geq 1
we have

\operatorname{Ass}((IJ)^{k})\ =\ \operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})\cup\operatorname{Ass}(J^{k}).

In particular,
\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(IJ)\ =\ \operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)\cup\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(J)
.

Proof.

Note that
(IJ)^{k}=I^{k}J^{k}=I^{k}\cap J^{k}
. The short exact sequence

0\rightarrow S/(I^{k}J^{k})\rightarrow S/I^{k}\oplus S/J^{k}\rightarrow S/(I^{k}+J^{k})\rightarrow 0

implies that
\operatorname{Ass}((IJ)^{k})\ \subseteq\ \operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})\cup\operatorname{Ass}(J^{k})
(see
[
16
, Lemma 3.6]
).

For the opposite inclusion, let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
. Then, there exists
f\in R
such that
(I^{k}:f)=\mathfrak{p}
. Let
g\in J^{k}\cap T
. Since
(IJ)^{k}=I^{k}J^{k}=I^{k}\cap J^{k}
, then

((IJ)^{k}:fg)\ =\ ((I^{k}\cap J^{k}):fg)\ =\ (I^{k}:fg)\cap(J^{k}:fg).

(2)

Notice that

(I^{k}:fg)\ =\ ((I^{k}:f):g)\ =\ (\mathfrak{p}:g)\ =\ \mathfrak{p},

(3)

because
g\in T
while
\mathfrak{p}
is an ideal whose generators are polynomials of
R
. Since
fg\in J^{k}
because
g\in J^{k}
, then
(J^{k}:fg)=S
. This fact and equations (
2
) and (
3
) imply that
((IJ)^{k}:fg)=\mathfrak{p}
. Hence
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}((IJ)^{k})
. The case
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}(J^{k})
is analogous.

∎

Theorem 2.2
.

With the notation introduced, we have

(a)

For all
k\geq 1
and all
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
, we have
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}((IJ)^{k})=\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})+\alpha(J)k
.

(b)

For all
k\geq 1
and all
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}(J^{k})
, we have
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}((IJ)^{k})=\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(J^{k})+\alpha(I)k
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
. Since
I
and
J
are ideals whose generators are in pairwise disjoint sets of variables,
\mathfrak{p}\subset R
and
\operatorname{Ass}((IJ)^{k})=\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})\cup\operatorname{Ass}(J^{k})
, we have that
X_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset R
, where
X_{\mathfrak{p}}
is defined in (
1
). By Lemma
1.2
and what we observed,

\displaystyle\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}((IJ)^{k})\

\displaystyle=\ \alpha\big(\frac{((IJ)^{k}:\mathfrak{p})}{(IJ)^{k}:(\mathfrak{p}+X_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\infty})}\big)\ =\ \alpha\big(\frac{J^{k}(I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})}{J^{k}(I^{k}:(\mathfrak{p}+X_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\infty}))}\big)

\displaystyle=\ \alpha\big(\frac{(I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})}{(I^{k}:(\mathfrak{p}+X_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\infty}))}\big)+\alpha(J^{k})

\displaystyle=\ \operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})+\alpha(J)k.

Statement (a) follows. The proof of statement (b) is analogous.

∎

Corollary 2.3
.

With the notation introduced, for all
k\gg 0
we have

\operatorname{v}((IJ)^{k})\ =\ \min\{\operatorname{v}(I^{k})+\alpha(J)k,\,\operatorname{v}(J^{k})+\alpha(I)k\}.

Corollary 2.4
.

With the notation introduced, suppose that
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})<\operatorname{reg}(I^{k})
and
\operatorname{v}(J^{k})<\operatorname{reg}(J^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
. Then
\operatorname{v}((IJ)^{k})<\operatorname{reg}((IJ)^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
.

Proof.

Notice that
\operatorname{reg}(I^{k})\geq\alpha(I)k
and
\operatorname{reg}(J^{k})\geq\alpha(J)k
for all
k\geq 1
. Moreover, by
[
29
, Corollary 3.2]
we have
\operatorname{reg}((IJ)^{k})=\operatorname{reg}(I^{k})+\operatorname{reg}(J^{k})
. Using the assumption, we obtain that
\operatorname{reg}((IJ)^{k})>\operatorname{v}(I^{k})+\alpha(J)k
and also
\operatorname{reg}((IJ)^{k})>\alpha(I)k+\operatorname{v}(J^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
. Applying Corollary
2.3
the assertion follows.

∎

3.
The set of stable primes of sums of monomial ideals

Let
K
be a field, and let
I\subset R=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and
J\subset T=K[y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]
be proper homogeneous ideals. Set
S=R\otimes_{K}T=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]
.

We set
\operatorname{Ass}^{*}(I)=\bigcup_{k\geq 1}\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
. By Brodmann,
\operatorname{Ass}^{*}(I)
is a finite set
[
7
]
. The following result was shown by Nguyen and Tran
[
35
, Theorem 4.5]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

With the notation introduced, we have

\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I+J)\ =\ \bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{*}(I)\\
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(J)\end{subarray}}\{\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{q}\}\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)\\
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{*}(J)\end{subarray}}\{\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{q}\}.

The proof of
[
35
, Theorem 4.5]
follows by lengthy computations involving some filtrations of
S/(I+J)^{k}
and a well-known formula for
\operatorname{Ass}_{S}(M\otimes_{K}N)
where
M
is a
R
-module and
N
is a
T
-module
[
26
, Theorem 2.5]
.

In this section, we provide a more elementary proof when
I
and
J
are monomial ideals. The new argument we use is based on the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
22
, Theorem 2.1]
Let
R=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring over a field
K
. Let
I\subset R
be a homogeneous ideal and
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
.

(b)

The Krull dimension
\dim H_{\mu(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})-1}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\,;\,\mathcal{R}(I)_{\mathfrak{p}})
is positive.

Here:

-

\mathcal{R}(I)=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}I^{k}
is the Rees algebra of
I
,

-

\mu(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})=\dim_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}^{2}R_{\mathfrak{p}})
is the minimal number of generators of
\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
,

-

and
H_{\mu(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})-1}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\,;\,\mathcal{R}(I)_{\mathfrak{p}})
is the
(\mu(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})-1)
th Koszul homology module of
\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
with respect to
\mathcal{R}(I)_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

It is noted in the proof of
[
22
, Theorem 2.1]
that

H_{\mu(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})-1}(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\,;\,\mathcal{R}(I)_{\mathfrak{p}})\ =\ \bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\frac{(I_{\mathfrak{p}}^{k}:\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})}{I_{\mathfrak{p}}^{k}}.

(4)

In order to prove Theorem
3.1
in the monomial case, we recall a few basic facts from (Krull) dimension theory and Hilbert functions. For the convenience of the reader we provide a proof for each of these facts.

Remark 3.3
.

Let
R=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}R_{k}
be a Noetherian graded
K
-algebra,
K
a field, and let
M=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}M_{k}
be a non-zero finitely generated graded
R
-module. Then, the Krull dimension
\dim M
is positive if and only if
M_{k}\neq 0
for infinitely many
k
.

Proof.

Recall that the Hilbert series of
M
is
\textup{Hilb}_{M}(t)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\dim_{K}(M_{k})t^{k}
. By
[
28
, Theorem 6.1.3]
there exists a Laurent polynomial
Q_{M}(t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t,t^{-1}]
such that
\textup{Hilb}_{M}(t)=Q_{M}(t)/(1-t)^{d}
, where
d=\dim M
. It follows that
d=0
if and only if
\textup{Hilb}_{M}(t)=Q_{M}(t)
. Since
Q_{M}(t)
has only finitely many summands, this is the case if and only if
\dim_{K}M_{k}=0
for all
k\gg 0
, if and only if
M_{k}=0
for all
k\gg 0
. Consequently,
\dim M>0
if and only if
M_{k}\neq 0
for infinitely many
k
.

∎

For a graded module
M=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}M_{k}
we denote by
M_{\geq h}=\bigoplus_{k\geq h}M_{k}
a truncation. We say that two graded modules
M=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}M_{k}
and
N=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}N_{k}
are equal up to truncation if there exist integers
h
and
\ell
such that
M_{\geq h}=N_{\geq\ell}
.

Remark 3.4
.

Let
R=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}R_{k}
be a Noetherian graded
K
-algebra,
K
a field, and let
M=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}M_{k}
and
N=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}N_{k}
be two finitely generated graded
R
-modules. If
M
and
N
are equal up to truncation then
\dim M=\dim N
.

Proof.

Let
h,\ell>0
such that
M_{\geq h}=N_{\geq\ell}
. Then
\dim M_{\geq h}=\dim N_{\geq\ell}
. Now, if
\dim{M_{\geq h}}=0
then
M_{k}=N_{k}=0
for all
k\gg 0
. This implies
\dim N=\dim M=0
. Suppose now that
\dim M_{\geq h}=d>0
. We claim that
\dim M_{\geq h}=\dim M
and that
\dim N_{\geq\ell}=\dim N
. By
[
28
, Theorem 6.1.3.(b)]
the Hilbert function
\textup{Hilb}(M_{\geq h},k)=\dim_{K}({M_{\geq h}})_{k}
agrees with a polynomial
P(x)\in{\mathbb{Q}}[x]
of degree
d-1
for all
k\gg 0
. Since
\textup{Hilb}(M,k)=\textup{Hilb}(M_{\geq h},k)
for all
k\geq h
, it follows that
\textup{Hilb}(M,k)
agrees with
P(x)
for all
k\gg 0
. Now
[
28
, Theorem 6.1.3.(b)]
implies
\dim M=d
, as well.

∎

Remark 3.5
.

Let
R=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}R_{k}
be a Noetherian graded
K
-algebra,
K
a field, and let

and

be two graded ideals of

. Then
\dim IJ>0
if and only if
\dim I>0
and
J
is non-zero or
\dim J>0
and
I
is non-zero.

Proof.

Notice that

is a graded ideal with

. Remark
3.3
implies that

if and only if for all

and

. From this the assertion follows immediately.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
, monomial case.

Let

[

. Thus, to characterize the set
\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I+J)
we only need to consider monomial prime ideals containing
I+J
. Let
\mathfrak{p}=(x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{r}},y_{j_{1}},\dots,y_{j_{t}})
be such an ideal. To compute the Koszul homology module (
4

L\subset S
, the
monomial localization
of
L
is the monomial ideal
L(\mathfrak{p})
of the polynomial ring
S(\mathfrak{p})=K[x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{r}},y_{j_{1}},\dots,y_{j_{t}}]
obtained by applying the substitutions
x_{i}\mapsto 1
and

for

and

\frac{((I+J)_{\mathfrak{p}}^{k}:\mathfrak{p}S_{\mathfrak{p}})}{(I+J)_{\mathfrak{p}}^{k}}\ =\ \frac{((I+J)^{k}:\mathfrak{p})}{(I+J)^{k}}S_{\mathfrak{p}}\ =\ \frac{((I(\mathfrak{p})+J(\mathfrak{p}))^{k}:\mathfrak{p})}{(I(\mathfrak{p})+J(\mathfrak{p}))^{k}}S_{\mathfrak{p}}.

Hence

H_{\mu(\mathfrak{p}S_{\mathfrak{p}})-1}(\mathfrak{p}S_{\mathfrak{p}}\,;\,\mathcal{R}(I+J)_{\mathfrak{p}})\ =\ H_{\mu(\mathfrak{p})-1}(\mathfrak{p}\,;\,\mathcal{R}(I(\mathfrak{p})+J(\mathfrak{p}))\,)S_{\mathfrak{p}}.

Since
S_{\mathfrak{p}}
is again a polynomial ring and
I(\mathfrak{p})
and
J(\mathfrak{p})
are monomial ideals of
S_{\mathfrak{p}}
, we may assume from the very beginning that
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
is the maximal ideal of
S
. We set
\mathfrak{m}_{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
and
\mathfrak{m}_{y}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})

Let

Let

,

(5)

To show the opposite inclusion, using the subsequent calculation (
6

C(I+J)
,
C(I)
and

are generated by residue classes of monomials. Hence

u+(I+J)^{k+1}\in C(I+J)_{k}

and

. Let
1\leq i\leq n

and
\alpha

We claim that

and

,

C(I+J)_{k}\ =\ \frac{((I+J)^{k+1}:\mathfrak{m})\cap(I+J)^{k}}{(I+J)^{k+1}}.

(6)

(For this computation see also
[

for all

. Similarly,

4.
The
\operatorname{v}
-function of sums

The purpose of this section is to prove the following very useful formula.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
I\subset R
,
J\subset T
be proper monomial ideals, and let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
and
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Spec}(T)
be monomial prime ideals such that
\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}((I+J)^{k})
. Then,

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{q}}((I+J)^{k})\ =\ \min_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\ell<k\\
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k-\ell})\\
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}(J^{\ell+1})\end{subarray}}(\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k-\ell})+\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(J^{\ell+1})).

Before we prove the theorem we recall few basic facts about monomial ideals.

Let
I\subset R=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a proper monomial ideal. As is customary, we denote by
\mathcal{G}(I)
the minimal monomial generating set of
I
. Let
v\in R
be a monomial. By
[
28
, Proposition 1.2.2]
, a set of generators of
(I:v)
is

\Big\{\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(u,v)}{v}\ :\ u\in\mathcal{G}(I)\Big\}.

(7)

From this fact it follows immediately that
(\sum_{j=1}^{t}I_{t}):v=\sum_{j=1}^{t}(I_{j}:v)
, for any proper monomial ideals
I_{1},\dots,I_{t}\subset R
.

For vectors
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
and
{\bf b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{m})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{m}
we set
{\bf x^{a}}=\prod_{i}x_{i}^{a_{i}}
and
{\bf y^{b}}=\prod_{i}y_{i}^{b_{i}}
. In particular,
{\bf x^{0}}={\bf y^{0}}=1
for
{\bf 0}=(0,0,\dots,0)
.

Let
I\subset R
and
J\subset T
be proper monomial ideals. We claim that

(IJ:{\bf x^{a}}{\bf y^{b}})\ =\ (I:{\bf x^{a}})(J:{\bf y^{b}}).

(8)

Indeed,
\mathcal{G}(IJ)=\{uv:u\in\mathcal{G}(I),v\in\mathcal{G}(J)\}
. Then, by equation (
7
) any generator of the colon is of the form
\operatorname{lcm}(uv,{\bf x^{a}y^{b}})/({\bf x^{a}y^{b}})
, for
u\in\mathcal{G}(I)
and
v\in\mathcal{G}(J)
. It is immediate to see that

\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(uv,{\bf x^{a}}{\bf y^{b}})}{{\bf x^{a}y^{b}}}\ =\ \frac{\operatorname{lcm}(u,{\bf x^{a}})\operatorname{lcm}(v,{\bf y^{b}})}{{\bf x^{a}y^{b}}}\ =\ \frac{\operatorname{lcm}(u,{\bf x^{a}})}{{\bf x^{a}}}\cdot\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(v,{\bf y^{b}})}{{\bf y^{b}}}.

From this fact equation (
8
) follows immediately.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
and
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Spec}(T)
be monomial prime ideals such that
\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}((I+J)^{k})
. Up to a suitable relabeling, we may assume that
\mathfrak{p}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{p})
for some
1\leq p\leq n
and
\mathfrak{q}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{q})
for some
1\leq q\leq m
.

Let
f={\bf x^{a}}{\bf y^{b}}\in S
be a monomial such that
(I+J)^{k}:f=\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{q}
and with
\deg(f)=\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{q}}((I+J)^{k})
. Note that

\displaystyle(I+J)^{k}:f

\displaystyle=\ (\sum_{h=0}^{k}I^{k-h}J^{h}):{\bf x^{a}}{\bf y^{b}}\ =\ \sum_{h=0}^{k}(I^{k-h}J^{h}:{\bf x^{a}}{\bf y^{b}})

(9)

\displaystyle=\ \phantom{(\!}\sum_{h=0}^{k}(I^{k-h}:{\bf x^{a}})(J^{h}:{\bf y^{b}}).

Therefore, for each
1\leq i\leq p
there exists
0\leq\ell_{i}\leq k
such that
x_{i}f\in I^{k-\ell_{i}}J^{\ell_{i}}
. Let
\ell=\max\{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{p}\}
. Then
x_{i}{\bf x^{a}}\in I^{k-\ell}
for all
1\leq i\leq p
and
{\bf y^{b}}\in J^{\ell}
. Thus
x_{i}f\in I^{k-\ell}J^{\ell}
for all
1\leq i\leq p
. Notice that
\ell<k
. Otherwise if
\ell=k
, since
{\bf y^{b}}\in J^{k}
then
f={\bf x^{a}y^{b}}\in J^{k}\subset(I+J)^{k}
and
(I+J)^{k}:f=S
against the assumption.

Since
{\bf y^{b}}\in J^{\ell}
, the
\ell
th summand
(I^{k-\ell}:{\bf x^{a}})(J^{\ell}:{\bf y^{b}})
in (
9
) is equal to
(I^{k-\ell}:{\bf x^{a}})
and by our discussion contains
\mathfrak{p}
. Notice that
(I^{k-\ell}:{\bf x^{a}})
is a monomial ideal of
R
. Therefore,
(I^{k-\ell}:{\bf x^{a}})
must be equal to
\mathfrak{p}
, for otherwise
(I+J)^{k}:f
would strictly contain
\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{q}
, against the assumption. Hence
(I^{k-\ell}:{\bf x^{a}})=\mathfrak{p}
and so

\deg({\bf x^{a}})\ \geq\ \operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k-\ell}).

(10)

Since
x_{1}{\bf x^{a}}\in I^{k-\ell}
, there exist
k-\ell
monomials
u_{1},u_{2}\dots,u_{k-\ell}\in I
such that
x_{1}{\bf x^{a}}=u_{1}\cdots u_{k-\ell}
. Consequently,
x_{1}
divides
u_{j}
for some
1\leq j\leq k-\ell
. Up to relabeling we may assume
j=k-\ell
. We deduce that
u_{1}\cdots u_{k-\ell-1}
divides
{\bf x^{a}}
. Hence
{\bf x^{a}}\in I^{k-(\ell+1)}
and the
(\ell+1)
th summand in (
9
) becomes

(I^{k-(\ell+1)}:{\bf x^{a}})(J^{\ell+1}:{\bf y^{b}})\ =\ (J^{\ell+1}:{\bf y^{b}}).

Since
{\bf x^{a}}\in I^{k-(\ell+1)}
, then
(I^{k-h}:{\bf x^{a}})=S
for all
\ell+1\leq h\leq k
. Similarly, since
{\bf y^{b}}\in J^{\ell}
, then
(J^{h}:{\bf y^{b}})=S
for all
1\leq h\leq\ell
. Thus, equation (
9
) simplifies to

(I+J)^{k}:f\ =\ \sum_{h=0}^{\ell}(I^{k-h}:{\bf x^{a}})+\sum_{h=\ell+1}^{k}(J^{h}:{\bf y^{b}}).

Noticing that
(I^{k}:{\bf x^{a}})\subseteq\cdots\subseteq(I^{k-\ell}:{\bf x^{a}})
and
(J^{\ell+1}:{\bf y^{b}})\supseteq\cdots\supseteq(J^{k}:{\bf y^{b}})
, the previous formula simplifies to

(I+J)^{k}:{\bf x^{a}y^{b}}\ =\ (I^{k-\ell}:{\bf x^{a}})+(J^{\ell+1}:{\bf y^{b}}).

Since
(I+J)^{k}:{\bf x^{a}y^{b}}=\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{q}
by assumption and
(I^{k-\ell}:{\bf x^{a}})=\mathfrak{p}
we conclude that
(J^{\ell+1}:{\bf y^{b}})=\mathfrak{q}
. Hence

\deg({\bf y^{b}})\ \geq\ \operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(J^{\ell+1}).

(11)

Putting together (
10
) and (
11
), we deduce that

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{q}}((I+J)^{k})\ =\ \deg(f)\ =\ \deg({\bf x^{a}})+\deg({\bf y^{b}})\ \geq\ \operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k-\ell})+\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(J^{\ell+1}),

where
0\leq\ell<k
. Hence

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{q}}((I+J)^{k})\ \geq\ \min_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\ell<k\\
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k-\ell})\\
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}(J^{\ell+1})\end{subarray}}(\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k-\ell})+\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(J^{\ell+1})).

To show the opposite inequality, let
0\leq\ell<k
such that
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k-\ell})
and
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}(J^{\ell+1})
. Let
{\bf x^{a}},{\bf y^{b}}\in S
be monomials such that
(I^{k-\ell}:{\bf x^{a}})=\mathfrak{p}
,
(J^{\ell+1}:{\bf y^{b}})=\mathfrak{q}
,
\deg({\bf x^{a}})=\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k-\ell})
and
\deg({\bf y^{b}})=\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(J^{\ell+1})
.

Set
f={\bf x^{a}}{\bf y^{b}}
. We claim that
(I+J)^{k}:f=\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{q}
. This implies the desired inequality and concludes the proof.

Arguing as in the previous part of the proof, we see that
{\bf x^{a}}\in I^{k-(\ell+1)}
and
{\bf y^{b}}\in J^{\ell}
. Hence
(I^{k-h}:{\bf x^{a}})=S
for all
\ell+1\leq h\leq k
, and
(J^{h}:{\bf y^{b}})=S
for all
1\leq h\leq\ell
. Then equation (
9
) which is valid in our setting, simplifies to

(I+J)^{k}:f\ =\ \sum_{h=0}^{\ell}(I^{k-h}:{\bf x^{a}})+\sum_{h=\ell+1}^{k}(J^{h}:{\bf y^{b}}).

Since
(I^{k}:{\bf x^{a}})\subseteq\cdots\subseteq(I^{k-\ell}:{\bf x^{a}})=\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{q}=(J^{\ell+1}:{\bf y^{b}})\supseteq\cdots\supseteq(J^{k}:{\bf y^{b}})
, the previous equation becomes
(I+J)^{k}:f=\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{q}
, as desired.

∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
I\subset R
,
J\subset T
be proper monomial ideals. Then,

\operatorname{v}((I+J)^{k})\ =\ \min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)\\
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Spec}(T)\\
\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}((I+J)^{k})\end{subarray}}(\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\ell<k\\
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k-\ell})\\
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}(J^{\ell+1})\end{subarray}}(\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k-\ell})+\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(J^{\ell+1}))).

Due to some experimental evidence, we could expect that the formula given in Theorem
4.1
holds true for any graded ideals
I\subset R
and
J\subset T
.

In
[
22
]
the following question was posed.

Question 4.3
.

[
22
, Question 5.1]
Let
I\subset S
be an homogeneous ideal. Is it true that the inequality
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})<\operatorname{reg}(I^{k})
holds for all
k\gg 0
?

Some partial results supporting this expectation are provided in
[
6
,
20
,
39
]
.

The
support
of a monomial
u\in S
is defined as the set
\operatorname{supp}(u)=\{x_{i}:x_{i}\ \textup{divides}\ u\}
. For a proper monomial ideal
I\subset S
, we define the
support
of
I
to be the set
\operatorname{supp}(I)=\bigcup_{u\in\mathcal{G}(I)}\operatorname{supp}(u)
.

We say that a monomial ideal
I\subset S
is
connected
if for any
u,v\in\mathcal{G}(I)
there exist
w_{0},w_{1},\dots,w_{r}\in\mathcal{G}(I)
such that
w_{0}=u
,
w_{r}=v
and
\operatorname{supp}(w_{i})\cap\operatorname{supp}(w_{i+1})\neq\emptyset
for all
0\leq i\leq r-1
. It is clear that any monomial ideal
I\subset S
can be written uniquely as
I=I_{1}+\dots+I_{t}
, where each
I_{j}
is a connected monomial ideal, and
\mathcal{G}(I)
is the disjoint union
\bigcup_{j=1}^{t}\mathcal{G}(I_{j})
. Notice in particular that
I_{1},\dots,I_{t}
have pairwise disjoint supports.

In the next corollary, we show that to prove Question
4.3
for all monomial ideals, it is enough to consider only connected monomial ideals.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
I\subset R
and
J\subset T
be proper monomial ideals. Suppose that
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})<\operatorname{reg}(I^{k})
and
\operatorname{v}(J^{k})<\operatorname{reg}(J^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
. Then
\operatorname{v}((I+J)^{k})<\operatorname{reg}((I+J)^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
.

Proof.

Applying
[
27
, Theorem 3.3]
for all
k\geq 1
we have

\operatorname{reg}\frac{(I+J)^{k-1}}{(I+J)^{k}}\ =\ \max_{i+j=k-1}\big\{\!\operatorname{reg}\frac{I^{i}}{I^{i+1}}+\operatorname{reg}\frac{J^{j}}{J^{j+1}}\big\}.

Setting
j=\ell
, we have
0\leq\ell<k
and
i+1=k-\ell
, and the formula becomes

\operatorname{reg}\frac{(I+J)^{k-1}}{(I+J)^{k}}\ =\ \max_{0\leq\ell<k}\big\{\!\operatorname{reg}\frac{I^{k-\ell-1}}{I^{k-\ell}}+\operatorname{reg}\frac{J^{\ell}}{J^{\ell+1}}\big\}.

By
[
27
, Lemma 5.1]
, we have
\operatorname{reg}L^{p-1}/L^{p}=\operatorname{reg}L^{p}-1
, for any homogeneous ideal
L\subset S
and all
p\gg 0
. Thus, for any
k\gg 0
we can find
0\leq\ell<k
such that
k-\ell
is also large enough and such that

(a)

\operatorname{reg}((I+J)^{k})\geq\operatorname{reg}(I^{k-\ell})+\operatorname{reg}(J^{\ell+1})-1
,

(b)

\operatorname{v}(I^{k-\ell})<\operatorname{reg}(I^{k-\ell})
, and

(c)

\operatorname{v}(J^{\ell+1})<\operatorname{reg}(J^{\ell+1})
.

There exist
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k-\ell})
and
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}(J^{\ell+1})
such that
\operatorname{v}(I^{k-\ell})=\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k-\ell})
and
\operatorname{v}(J^{\ell+1})=\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(J^{\ell+1})
. Applying Theorem
4.1
and (a), (b) and (c) we conclude that

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}((I+J)^{k})\

\displaystyle\geq\ \operatorname{reg}(I^{k-\ell})+\operatorname{reg}(J^{\ell+1})-1\ >\ \operatorname{v}(I^{k-\ell})+\operatorname{reg}(J^{\ell+1})-1

\displaystyle\geq\ \operatorname{v}(I^{k-\ell})+\operatorname{v}(J^{\ell+1})\ =\ \operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k-\ell})+\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(J^{\ell+1})

\displaystyle\geq\ \operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{q}}((I+J)^{k})\ \geq\ \operatorname{v}((I+J)^{k}),

as desired.

∎

5.
Consequences

In this section we record several consequences of Theorem
4.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal and let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. Let
f(k)=ak+b
be the (unique) linear function such that
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=f(k)
for all
k\gg 0
. The
\operatorname{v}
-stability index
of
I
, denoted by
\textup{v-stab}(I)
, is defined as the smallest
k_{0}
such that
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=f(k)
for all
k\geq k_{0}
. Likewise, let
g(k)=ck+d
be the (unique) linear function such that
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})=g(k)
for all
k\gg 0
. The
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-stability index
of
I
, denoted by
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{-stab}(I)
, is defined as the smallest
k_{0}
such that
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})=g(k)
for all
k\geq k_{0}
.

Example 5.1
.

Let
{f\in S}
be a homogeneous polynomial and consider the principal ideal
{I=(f)}
. If
{f=f_{1}^{\,a_{1}}\cdots f_{s}^{\,a_{s}}}
is the decomposition of
f
into irreducible polynomials, with
{\deg f_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\deg f_{s}}
, then
{\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)=\operatorname{Ass}(I)=\{(f_{1}),\ldots,(f_{s})\}}
, and we have that for any
{k>0}
, and any
{r\in\{1,\ldots,s\}}
,
{(I^{k}:(f^{k}/f_{r}))=(f_{r})}
. So the
\operatorname{v}
-number of
I^{k}
is attained by
{(I^{k}:(f^{k}/f_{s}))=(f_{s})}
, and we have

\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=k\deg f-\deg f_{s}=\alpha(I)k-\deg f_{s}.

In particular, the
\operatorname{v}
-stability index of
I
is
1
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
I_{1},\dots,I_{t}\subset S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be monomial ideals with pairwise disjoint supports. Suppose that
\operatorname{v}(I_{j}^{k})=\alpha(I_{j})k-1
for all
k\gg 0
and all
j=1,\dots,t
. Then, for all
k\geq 1
,

\operatorname{v}((\sum_{j=1}^{t}I_{j})^{k})\ \geq\ (\min_{1\leq j\leq t}\alpha(I_{j}))k+(\sum_{j=1}^{t}\alpha(I_{j})-\min_{1\leq j\leq t}\alpha(I_{j})-t).

Equality holds for all
k\gg 0
if
\alpha(I_{1})=\alpha(I_{2})=\cdots=\alpha(I_{t})
or for all
k\geq 1
if
\textup{v-stab}(I_{1})=\textup{v-stab}(I_{2})=\cdots=\textup{v-stab}(I_{t})=1
.

Proof.

Up to relabeling, we may assume that
\alpha(I_{1})\leq\alpha(I_{2})\leq\cdots\leq\alpha(I_{t})
. Proceeding by induction on
t\geq 1
we show that

\operatorname{v}((\sum_{j=1}^{t}I_{j})^{k})\ \geq\ \alpha(I_{1})k+(\sum_{j=2}^{t}\alpha(I_{j})-t)

(12)

for all
k\geq 1
, and equality holds for all
k\gg 0
if
\alpha(I_{1})=\alpha(I_{2})=\dots=\alpha(I_{t})
or for all
k\geq 1
if
\textup{v-stab}(I_{1})=\textup{v-stab}(I_{2})=\cdots=\textup{v-stab}(I_{t})=1
.

For
t=1
, the inequality holds by
[
19
, Proposition 2.2]
and the equality holds for all
k\gg 0
by assumption, and for all
k\geq 1
if
\textup{v-stab}(I_{1})=1
by definition of
\operatorname{v}
-stability.

Let
t>1
. We set
I=I_{1}+\dots+I_{t-1}
and
J=I_{t}
. By inductive hypothesis

\operatorname{v}(I^{k})\ =\ \operatorname{v}((\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}I_{j})^{k})\ \geq\ \alpha(I_{1})k+(\sum_{j=2}^{t-1}\alpha(I_{j})-(t-1))

(13)

for all
k\geq 1
.

Since
\operatorname{v}((I+J)^{k})=\min_{P\in\operatorname{Ass}((I+J)^{k})}\operatorname{v}_{P}((I+J)^{k})
, to prove the inequality (
12
), it is enough to show that
\operatorname{v}_{P}((I+J)^{k})
satisfies the corresponding inequality for all
P\in\operatorname{Ass}((I+J)^{k})
and all
k\geq 1
.

Let
P\in\operatorname{Ass}((I+J)^{k})
. Then
[
28
, Corollary 1.3.9]
implies that
P=\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{q}
where
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
and
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Spec}(T)
are monomial prime ideals. By Theorem
4.1
,

\operatorname{v}_{P}((I+J)^{k})\ =\ \min_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\ell<k\\
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k-\ell})\\
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}(J^{\ell+1})\end{subarray}}(\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k-\ell})+\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(J^{\ell+1})).

Now, let
0\leq\ell<k
such that
\operatorname{v}_{P}((I+J)^{k})=\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k-\ell})+\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(J^{\ell+1})
. Then, using (
13
) and since by
[
19
, Proposition 2.2]
we have
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(J^{\ell+1})\geq\alpha(J)(\ell+1)-1=\alpha(I_{t})(\ell+1)-1
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\operatorname{v}_{P}((I+J)^{k})\

\displaystyle=\ \operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k-\ell})+\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(J^{\ell+1})\ \geq\ \operatorname{v}(I^{k-\ell})+\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(J^{\ell+1})

\displaystyle\geq\ \alpha(I_{1})(k-\ell)+(\sum_{j=2}^{t-1}\alpha(I_{j})-(t-1))+\alpha(I_{t})(\ell+1)-1

\displaystyle\geq\ \alpha(I_{1})(k-\ell)+(\sum_{j=2}^{t-1}\alpha(I_{j})-(t-1))+\alpha(I_{1})\ell+\alpha(I_{t})-1

\displaystyle=\ \alpha(I_{1})k+(\sum_{j=2}^{t}\alpha(I_{j})-t).

Consequently, inequality (
12
) holds.

Suppose now that
\alpha(I_{1})=\alpha(I_{2})=\dots=\alpha(I_{t})=a
. To prove that equality holds in (
12
) for all
k\gg 0
, let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
,
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(J)
such that
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})=\operatorname{v}(I^{k})
and
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(J^{k})=\operatorname{v}(J^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
. Set
P=\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{q}
. Then
P\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I+J)
by Theorem
3.1
. For all
k\gg 0
we can find
0\leq\ell<k
big enough such that
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k-\ell})
,
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k-\ell})=\operatorname{v}(I^{k-\ell})=a(k-\ell)+((t-2)a-(t-1))
,
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}(J^{\ell+1})
and
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(J^{\ell+1})=\operatorname{v}(J^{\ell+1})=a(\ell+1)-1
. Again by Theorem
4.1
we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{v}((I+J)^{k})\

\displaystyle\leq\ \operatorname{v}_{P}((I+J)^{k})\ \leq\ \operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k-\ell})+\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(J^{\ell+1})

\displaystyle=\ a(k-\ell)+((t-2)a-(t-1))+a(\ell+1)-1

\displaystyle=\ ak+((t-1)a-t).

This last inequality together with inequality (
12
) implies the assertion.

Finally, suppose that
\textup{v-stab}(I_{1})=\textup{v-stab}(I_{2})=\cdots=\textup{v-stab}(I_{t})=1
. To prove that equality holds in (
12
) for all
k\geq 1
, let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
such that
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})=\operatorname{v}(I^{k})
and let
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}(J)
such that
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(J)=\operatorname{v}(J)
. Set
P=\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{q}
. Then
P\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I+J)
by Theorem
3.1
. By induction on
t
,
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})=\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\alpha(I_{1})k+(\sum_{j=2}^{t-1}\alpha(I_{j})-(t-1))
for all
k\geq 1
and since
\textup{v-stab}(I_{t})=1
,
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(J)=\operatorname{v}(J)=\alpha(J)-1=\alpha(I_{t})-1
. Again by Theorem
4.1
we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{v}((I+J)^{k})\

\displaystyle\leq\ \operatorname{v}_{P}((I+J)^{k})\ \leq\ \operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})+\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(J)

\displaystyle=\ \alpha(I_{1})(k-\ell)+(\sum_{j=2}^{t-1}\alpha(I_{j})-(t-1))+\alpha(I_{t})-1

\displaystyle=\ \alpha(I_{1})(k-\ell)+(\sum_{j=2}^{t}\alpha(I_{j})-t).

This last inequality together with inequality (
12
) implies the assertion.

∎

Notice that for any graded ideal
I\subset S
, we have
\operatorname{reg}(I^{k})\geq\alpha(I)k
for all
k\geq 1
. Combining this observation with Theorem
5.2
and Corollary
4.4
we obtain

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
I_{1},\dots,I_{t}\subset S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be monomial ideals with pairwise disjoint supports. Suppose that
\operatorname{v}(I_{j}^{k})=\alpha(I_{j})k-1
for all
k\gg 0
and all
j=1,\dots,t
. Then, for all
k\gg 0

\operatorname{reg}((\sum_{j=1}^{t}I_{j})^{k})\ >\ \operatorname{v}((\sum_{j=1}^{t}I_{j})^{k})\ \geq\ (\min_{1\leq j\leq t}\alpha(I_{j}))k+(\sum_{j=1}^{t}\alpha(I_{j})-\min_{1\leq j\leq t}\alpha(I_{j})-t).

Let
G
be a finite simple graph on the vertex set
V(G)=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
and edge set
E(G)
. The
edge ideal
of
G
is the monomial ideal
I(G)
of
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
generated by the monomials
x_{i}x_{j}
such that
\{x_{i},x_{j}\}\in E(G)
.

We denote by
c(G)
the number of connected components of
G
.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph. Then, for all
k\gg 0
,

\operatorname{v}(I(G)^{k})\ =\ 2k+(c(G)-2).

Proof.

Let
G_{1},\dots,G_{c(G)}
be the connected components of
G
. Set
I_{i}=I(G_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,c(G)
. Then
I(G)=I_{1}+\dots+I_{c(G)}
. It is proved in
[
6
, Theorem 5.1]
that if
H
is a connected graph, then
\operatorname{v}(I(H)^{k})=2k-1
for all
k\gg 0
. Hence, we have
\operatorname{v}(I_{i}^{k})=2k-1
for all
i=1,\dots,c(G)
and all
k\gg 0
. Since
I_{1},\dots,I_{c(G)}
have pairwise disjoint supports, Theorem
5.2
implies that

\displaystyle\operatorname{v}(I(G)^{k})\

\displaystyle=\ (\min_{1\leq j\leq c(G)}\alpha(I_{j}))k+(\sum_{j=1}^{c(G)}\alpha(I_{j})-\min_{1\leq j\leq c(G)}\alpha(I_{j})-t)

\displaystyle=\ 2k+(2c(G)-2-c(G))

\displaystyle=\ 2k+(c(G)-2),

as desired.

∎

Example 5.5
.

Let
G=C_{5}
be the 5-cycle on vertex set
\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}\}
. That is
I(G)=(x_{1}x_{2},x_{2}x_{3},x_{3}x_{4},x_{4}x_{5},x_{5}x_{1})
. By
[

, Proposition 5.11]

\operatorname{v}(I(G)^{k})=2k-1

. Using the package
VNumber

[

]

\textup{v-stab}(I(G))=2

The conclusion of Theorem
5.2
is no longer valid if the ideals

do not have the same initial degree, or if

Example 5.6
.

Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{5},y_{1},\dots,y_{5},z_{1},\dots,z_{5}]
, and let

\displaystyle I\

\displaystyle=\ (x_{1}x_{2},x_{2}x_{3},x_{3}x_{4},x_{4}x_{5},x_{5}x_{1}),

\displaystyle J\

\displaystyle=\ (y_{1}y_{2},\,y_{2}y_{3},\,y_{3}y_{4},\,y_{4}y_{5},\,y_{5}y_{1}),

\displaystyle L\

\displaystyle=\ (z_{1}z_{2},\,z_{2}z_{3},\,z_{3}z_{4},\,z_{4}z_{5},\,z_{5}z_{1}).

We set
I_{1}=I
and
I_{2}=JL
. Then
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
have disjoint supports. Notice that
I,J,L
are the edge ideals of the 5-cycles on vertex sets
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{5}\}
,
\{y_{1},\dots,y_{5}\}
,
\{z_{1},\dots,z_{5}\}
, respectively. By Example
5.5
, we have

\operatorname{v}(I^{k})\ =\ \operatorname{v}(J^{k})\ =\ \operatorname{v}(L^{k})\ =\ \begin{cases}\hfill 2&\textup{if}\ k=1,\\
2k-1&\textup{otherwise.}\end{cases}

By Corollary
2.3
, we obtain that

\operatorname{v}(I_{2}^{k})\ =\ \begin{cases}\hfill 4&\textup{if}\ k=1,\\
4k-1&\textup{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Thus
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
satisfy the equation
\operatorname{v}(I_{i}^{k})=\alpha(I_{i})k-1
for all
k\geq 2
. Notice that
\alpha(I_{1})=2\neq 4=\alpha(I_{2})
and
\textup{v-stab}(I_{1})=\textup{v-stab}(I_{2})=2>1
. By Theorem
5.2
we have that
\operatorname{v}((I_{1}+I_{2})^{k})\ \geq\ 2k+2
for all
k\gg 0
. We now show that

\operatorname{v}((I_{1}+I_{2})^{k})\ =\ 2k+3

for all
k\gg 0
.

For this aim, we apply Theorem
4.1
. Let
k\gg 0
and let
P\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I_{1}+I_{2})
. There exist
0\leq\ell<k
,
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I_{1}^{k-\ell})
and
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I_{2}^{\ell+1})
such that

. We have

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I_{1}^{k-\ell})+\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(I_{2}^{\ell+1})\ \geq\ \operatorname{v}(I_{1}^{k-\ell})+\operatorname{v}(I_{2}^{\ell+1})\ =\ \begin{cases}\hfill 2k+3&\textup{if}\ \ell=0,\\
2k+(2\ell+2)&\textup{if}\ 0<\ell<k.\end{cases}

By Theorem
5.2
we then deduce that
\operatorname{v}_{P}((I_{1}+I_{2})^{k})\geq 2k+3
for all

. Since

is arbitrary, then
\operatorname{v}((I_{1}+I_{2})^{k})\geq 2k+3
for all

. By choosing
\ell=0
,
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I_{1}^{k})
such that
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I_{1}^{k})=\operatorname{v}(I_{1}^{k})=2k-1
and
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I_{2})

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{q}}(I_{2})=\operatorname{v}(I_{2})=4

.

5.3
and
5.4
. However, we provide a different proof using a result of Beyarslan, Hà and Trung
[

, Theorem 4.5]
.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph. Then, for all
k\gg 0
,

\operatorname{v}(I(G)^{k})\ <\ \operatorname{reg}(I(G)^{k}).

Proof.

Let
M=\{e_{1},\dots,e_{r}\}
be a subset of
E(G)
. Recall that
M
is a called a
r
-matching of
G
if
e_{i}\cap e_{j}=\emptyset
for all
i\neq j
. Whereas,
M
is called an induced
r
-matching if
M
is a
r
-matching and the only edges of the induced subgraph of
G
on the vertex set
V(M)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}e_{i}
are the edges of
M
. We call
r
the size of
M
. We denote by
\textup{im}(G)
the largest size of an induced matching of
G
. It is clear that
\textup{im}(G)\geq c(G)
. Indeed, let
G_{1},\dots,G_{c(G)}
be the connected components of
G
, and pick an edge
e_{i}\in E(G_{i})
for each
i=1,\dots,c(G)
. Then
M=\{e_{1},\dots,e_{c(G)}\}
is an induced
c(G)
-matching of
G
.

It is shown in
[
4
, Theorem 4.5]
that
\operatorname{reg}(I(G)^{k})\geq 2k+\textup{im}(G)-1
for all
k\geq 1
. Since
\textup{im}(G)\geq c(G)
and by Corollary
5.4
we have
\operatorname{v}(I(G)^{k})=2k+(c(G)-2)
for all

, the assertion follows.

Recall that an ideal
I\subset S
is called a
complete intersection
if
I
is generated by a regular sequence. It is well-known that a monomial ideal
I\subset S
is a complete intersection if and only if
\operatorname{supp}(u)\cap\operatorname{supp}(v)=\emptyset
for all
u,v\in\mathcal{G}(I)
with
u\neq v
.

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
I\subset S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a monomial complete intersection.

(a)

We have

\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)\ =\ \{\!\!\sum_{u\in\mathcal{G}(I)}(x_{u})\ :\ x_{u}\in\operatorname{supp}(u)\}.

(b)

\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})=\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
for all
k\geq 1
.

(c)

For all
k\geq 1
,

\operatorname{v}(I^{k})\ =\ \alpha(I)k+(\!\!\sum_{u\in\mathcal{G}(I)}\deg(u)-\alpha(I)-\mu(I)).

Proof.

Let
u=x_{i_{1}}^{a_{i_{1}}}\cdots x_{i_{d}}^{a_{i_{d}}}\in S
be a monomial with
i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{d}
and
a_{i_{j}}\geq 1
for all
1\leq j\leq d
. Then
(x_{i_{1}}^{a_{i_{1}}})\cap\dots\cap(x_{i_{d}}^{a_{i_{d}}})
is the primary decomposition of
I=(u)
, and
\operatorname{Ass}(I)=\{(x_{i}):x_{i}\in\operatorname{supp}(u)\}
. Notice that
\operatorname{supp}(u)=\operatorname{supp}(u^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
. Hence
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})=\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
for all
k\geq 1
. Furthermore, for each
(x_{i_{j}})\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
, we have
(I^{k}:(u/x_{i_{j}}))=(x_{i_{j}})
and so
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})\leq\deg(u^{k}/x_{i_{j}})=\deg(u^{k})-1=\alpha(I)k-1
for all
k\geq 1
. By
[
19
, Proposition 2.2]
we also have
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})\geq\alpha(I)k-1
for all
k\geq 1
. Hence
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\alpha(I)k-1
for all
k\geq 1
. Thus (a), (b) and (c) hold if
\mu(I)=1
.

Suppose now that
\mu(I)>1
. Let
\mathcal{G}(I)=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\}
. Set
J=(u_{1},\dots,u_{m-1})
and
L=(u_{m})
. Then
I=J+L
and
J
and
L
have pairwise disjoint supports. By induction on
\mu(I)
, the ideals
J
and
L
satisfy statements (a), (b) and (c). By
[
35
, Theorem 4.1 (3)]
we have for all
k\geq 1

\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})\ =\ \bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\ell<k\\
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(J^{k-\ell})\\
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}(L^{\ell+1})\end{subarray}}\{\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{q}\}.

Since
\operatorname{Ass}(J^{k})=\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(J)
and
\operatorname{Ass}(L^{k})=\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(L)
for all
k\geq 1
, the previous equation implies statements (a) and (b). Statement (c) follows by Theorem
5.2
since
\operatorname{v}((u_{i})^{k})=\deg(u_{i})k-1
and
\textup{v-stab}((u_{i}))=1
for all
1\leq i\leq m
.

∎

We end the paper with another application of Theorem
5.2
.

Following
[
33
]
, (see also
[
13
,
34
]
) we say that a monomial ideal
I\subset S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is
vertex splittable
if it can be obtained by the following recursive procedure.

(i)

If
u
is a monomial and
I=(u)
,
I=0
or
I=S
, then
I
is vertex splittable.

(ii)

If there exists a variable
x_{i}
and vertex splittable ideals
I_{1}\subset S
and
I_{2}\subset K[x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{n}]
such that
I=x_{i}I_{1}+I_{2}
,
I_{2}\subseteq I_{1}
and
\mathcal{G}(I)
is the disjoint union of
\mathcal{G}(x_{i}I_{1})
and
\mathcal{G}(I_{2})
, then
I
is vertex splittable.

In the case (ii), the decomposition
I=x_{i}I_{1}+I_{2}
is called a
vertex splitting
of
I
and
x_{i}
is called a
splitting vertex
of
I
.

Examples 5.9
.

Many classes of monomial ideals are vertex splittable.

(a) Edge ideals with linear resolution are vertex splittable, see
[

, Theorem 3.7 and Corollary 3.8]
.

(b) Cover ideals of Cohen-Macaulay very well-covered graphs, which include the Hibi ideals, are vertex splittable, see the proofs of
[
12

[

, Proposition 2.3 and Lemma 3.3]
.

(c) Let
{\bf t}=(t_{1}\dots,t_{d-1})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{d-1}

. Following
[
17
,
10
]
, we say that

,

, is a
{\bf t}
-spread monomial if

and
i_{j+1}-i_{j}\geq t_{j}
for

. A monomial ideal

(d) For a monomial
u\in S
, we set
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)=\max\{j:x_{i}^{j}\ \textup{divides}\ u\}
. A monomial ideal

generated in a single degree is called polymatroidal if the exchange property holds: for all

and all

with

with
\deg_{x_{j}}(u)<\deg_{x_{j}}(v)
and
x_{j}(u/x_{i})\in\mathcal{G}(I)
. Let
I_{\langle j\rangle}
be the monomial ideal generated by the monomials of degree

belonging to

is componentwise polymatroidal if

[

The following is an important property of vertex splittable ideals.

Proposition 5.10
.

[
6
, Theorem 4.15]

Fixed point theorems in controlled rectangular metric spaces
Mohamed Rossafi 1∗ and Abdelkarim Kari 2

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce an extension of rectangular metric spaces called controlled rectangular metric spaces, by changing the rectangular inequality as follows: d ⁡ ( x , y ) ≤ α ⁡ ( x , u ) ​ d ​ ( x , u ) + α ⁡ ( u , v ) ​ d ​ ( u , v ) + α ⁡ ( v , y ) ​ d ​ ( v , y ) , d(x,y)\leq\alpha(x,u)d(x,u)+\alpha(u,v)d(u,v)+\alpha(v,y)d(v,y), for all distinct x , y , u , v ∈ X x,y,u,v\in X with the function α : X × X → [ 1 , ∞ [ \alpha:X\times X\rightarrow[1,\infty[ . We also establish some fixed point theorems for self-mappings defined on such spaces. Our main results extends and improves many results existing in the literature. Moreover, an illustrative example is presented to support the obtained results.

We define Cauchy and convergent sequences in controlled rectangular metric space as follows:

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\left(X,d\right)
be a controlled rectangular metric space.
\{x_{n}\}
be a sequence in X and
x\in X
. The sequence
\{x_{n}\}
converges to
x
if and only if
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}d\left(x_{n},x\right)=0.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(X,d)
be a controlled rectangular metric space and
\{x_{n}\}
be a sequence in
X
. We say that

(i)

\{x_{n}\}
is a Cauchy if and only if for every
\varepsilon

>0
there exists a positive integer
N=N(\varepsilon)
such that
d(x_{n},x_{m})<\varepsilon
, for all
n,m\geq N.

(ii)

(X,d)
is complete if and only if each Cauchy sequence in
X
is
convergent.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\left(X,d\right)
be a controlled rectangular metric space
and
\{x_{n}\}
a Cauchy sequence in X. If
\{x_{n}\}
converges to
x\in X
and converges to
y\in X,

we assume that

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\alpha(x_{n},x),\ \lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\alpha(x,x_{n})\ and\ \lim\limits_{n,m\rightarrow+\infty}\alpha(x_{n},x_{m})\ exist\ and\ are\ finite\ \forall n,m\in\mathbb{N},\ n\neq m.

then

Proof.

If
\{x_{n}\}
a Cauchy sequence in
X
has two limit point

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}d\left(x_{n},x\right)=0\ and\ \lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}d\left(x_{n},y\right)=0.

Since,
\{x_{n}\}
is Cauchy, then so from

d\left(x,y\right)\leq\alpha\left(x,x_{n}\right)d\left(x,x_{n}\right)+\alpha\left(x_{n},x_{n+1}\right)d\left(x_{n},x_{n+1}\right)+\alpha\left(x_{n+1},y\right)d\left(x_{n+1},y\right).

By letting

Therefore,

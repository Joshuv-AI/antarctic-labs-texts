II
Monopole charge and Chern number

In this section, we review the relationship between monopole charge and Chern number associated with topological Bloch states and the corresponding monopole pairing order.
In particular, we review how an obstruction to smoothly defining the gauge for Bloch states over a Fermi surface indicates a nonzero Chern number as well as a nonzero monopole charge associated with the corresponding pairing order.

II.1
Monopole charge and Chern number for Bloch states

The topological structure of a Fermi surface with a nonzero Chern number closely resembles that of a Dirac magnetic monopole.
Consider a Bloch state
|\psi_{\bf k}\rangle
on a spherical Fermi surface. For a Fermi surface with Chern number
C
, the Bloch state at the Fermi surface experiences a fictitious magnetic field
\mathbf{B}_{\bf k}=q\hat{\mathbf{k}}
from a momentum-space monopole charge
q\equiv\frac{1}{4\pi}\iint_{FS}\nabla\times\mathbf{A}_{\bf k}\equiv C/2
.
The corresponding vector potential, the Berry connection
\mathbf{A}_{\bf k}=i\langle\psi_{\bf k}|\mathbf{\nabla}_{\bf k}|\psi_{\bf k}\rangle
, is piecewise well defined over the northern and the southern hemispheres.

The Bloch states
\psi_{\bf k}
cannot be smoothly defined globally over the entire Fermi surface when
q\neq 0
but can be smoothly defined locally over the north and the south hemispheres under appropriate gauges related at the equator by

|\psi^{N}\rangle=e^{-2iq\phi_{\bf k}}|\psi^{S}\rangle.

(5)

Here
N
and
S
refer to the gauges for which the states are smooth over the patches
\theta_{\bf k}<\pi/2+\delta
covering the northern hemisphere and
\theta_{\bf k}>\pi/2-\delta
covering the southern hemisphere for a small angle
\delta>0
. Eq. (
5
) describing the transition function then holds on the overlap,
\pi/2+\delta\geq\theta_{\bf k}\geq\pi/2-\delta
.
The components of
|\psi\rangle
can be understood as sections of a complex line bundle and written in terms of monopole harmonics
Wu and Yang 1976
.

The Chern number can be calculated from the Berry connection
A^{j}=\langle\psi^{j}|i\nabla_{\bf k}|\psi^{j}\rangle
in each patch
j=N,S
. From the transition function,
A^{N}=A^{S}+\frac{2q}{\sin\theta_{\bf k}}\hat{\phi}
. The Chern number is then

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2\pi}\left[\iint_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}\nabla\times A^{N}+\iint_{\mathcal{S}^{S}}\nabla\times A^{S}\right]

(6)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\mathcal{C}_{E}}A^{N}-A^{S}=2q,

where
\mathcal{S}^{N}=\{(\theta_{\bf k},\phi_{\bf k})|\theta_{\bf k}\leq\pi/2,\phi_{\bf k}\in[0,2\pi)\}
and
\mathcal{S}^{S}=\{(\theta_{\bf k},\phi_{\bf k})|\theta_{\bf k}\geq\pi/2,\phi_{\bf k}\in[0,2\pi)\}
are the northern and southern hemispheres and
\mathcal{C}_{E}=\{(\theta_{\bf k},\phi_{\bf k})|\theta_{\bf k}=\pi/2,\phi_{\bf k}\in[0,2\pi)\}
is the equator oriented in the
+\hat{\phi}
direction so that the boundary of
\mathcal{S}^{N}
is
\mathcal{C}_{E}
and the boundary of
\mathcal{S}^{S}
is
-\mathcal{C}_{E}
. Thus, the Chern number can be read immediately from the transition function, and a nontrivial transition function implies a nonzero Chern number.
It is important to note that the above Chern number calculation is for an electron pocket, where the normal direction points out of the sphere. For a hole pocket, with normal direction pointing into the sphere, the Chern number for the same transition function is
-2q
.

II.2
Projected gap function and pair monopole charge

The projected gap function follows from the paired band eigenstates at the Fermi surface and the form of the pairing matrix. For a pairing matrix
\hat{\Delta}
, the BdG matrix for zero center-of-mass momentum pairing in a parity-symmetric system takes the form

\mathcal{H}_{BdG}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{H}({\bf k})&\hat{\Delta}\\
\hat{\Delta}^{\dagger}&-\mathcal{H}^{*}(-{\bf k})\end{pmatrix},

(7)

with the
n
-band system described in a spin-orbital or pseudospin basis by an
n\times n
matrix
\mathcal{H}
. The particle,
\mathcal{H}({\bf k})
, and hole,
-\mathcal{H}^{*}(-{\bf k})
, blocks can be diagonalized by unitary matrices
U_{\bf k}
and
U_{-{\bf k}}^{*}
, where the
j
th column of
U_{\bf k}
is the
j
th eigenstate of
\mathcal{H}({\bf k})
. Importantly, since eigenstates describing Fermi surfaces with nonzero Chern number require two gauges on two patches covering the Fermi surface to be smoothly defined, the unitary matrix will generally require two gauges as well, related by
U^{N}_{\bf k}=U^{S}_{\bf k}D^{SN}
on the patch overlap. The
j
th diagonal element of the diagonal matrix
D^{SN}
is the transition function
e^{-2iq_{j}\phi_{\bf k}}
for eigenstate
j
. Using
U^{N/S}_{BdG,{\bf k}}=\mathrm{diag}[U^{N/S}_{\bf k},U^{N/S*}_{-{\bf k}}]
to diagonalize the band and hole blocks,

U^{N/S\dagger}_{BdG,{\bf k}}\mathcal{H}_{BdG}U^{N/S}_{BdG,{\bf k}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{E}({\bf k})&U^{N/S\dagger}_{{\bf k}}\hat{\Delta}U^{N/S*}_{-{\bf k}}\\
\left(U^{N/S\dagger}_{{\bf k}}\hat{\Delta}U^{N/S*}_{-{\bf k}}\right)^{\dagger}&-\mathcal{E}(-{\bf k})\end{pmatrix},

(8)

where
\mathcal{E}({\bf k})
is an
n\times n
diagonal matrix with
j
th diagonal element the energy
E_{j}({\bf k})
of the
j
th eigenstate of
\mathcal{H}({\bf k})
. In this band-diagonal basis, the Fermi surface projection identifies the pairing between states at the Fermi surface as the projected gap function. For a Fermi surface described by eigenstate
j
,
E_{j}({\bf k})=0
, and the projected gap function to lowest order is the component
\left(U^{N/S\dagger}_{{\bf k}}\hat{\Delta}U^{N/S*}_{-{\bf k}}\right)_{jj}
. Noting that the
j
th column of
U^{N/S}_{{\bf k}}
is the eigenvector
|\psi^{N/S}_{j}({\bf k})\rangle
of
\mathcal{H}({\bf k})
and the
j
th column of
U^{N/S*}_{-{\bf k}}
is the eigenvector
|\psi^{N/S}_{h,j}({\bf k})\rangle
of the hole block
-\mathcal{H}^{*}(-{\bf k})
, the projected gap function is

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathrm{proj}}^{N/S}

\displaystyle=\langle\psi^{N/S}_{j}({\bf k})|\hat{\Delta}|\psi^{N/S}_{h,j}({\bf k})\rangle

(9)

\displaystyle=\langle\psi^{N/S}_{j}({\bf k})|\hat{\Delta}\Pi\mathcal{K}|\psi^{N/S}_{j}({\bf k})\rangle.

Here
\mathcal{K}
is the complex conjugation operator and
\Pi
is the appropriate representation of the parity operator in the spin-orbital or pseudospin basis. Thus, assuming parity symmetry, we write
|\psi^{N/S}_{h,j}({\bf k})\rangle=\mathcal{K}|\psi^{N/S}_{j}(-{\bf k})\rangle=\Pi\mathcal{K}|\psi^{N/S}_{j}({\bf k})\rangle
. The projected gap function then inherits a transition function from the single-particle eigenstates,

\Delta_{\mathrm{proj}}^{N}=e^{4iq_{j}}\Delta_{\mathrm{proj}}^{S}.

(10)

Thus, the projected gap function is described by monopole harmonics
Y_{qlm}

Wu and Yang 1976
with
q=2q_{j}=C_{j}
.

Similarly to how the Chern number can be identified from the transition function of a single-particle state, the pair monopole charge can be identified from the transition function of the projected gap function. The pair monopole charge
q_{p}
follows from integrating the pair Berry curvature over the Fermi surface and is related to the total vorticity
\nu_{tot}=2q_{p}

Murakami and Nagaosa 2003
;
Li and Haldane 2018
. As the total vorticity is defined in terms of the integral of the circulation field
\mathbf{v}_{\bf k}=\nabla_{\bf k}\varphi_{\bf k}-A_{p}({\bf k})
, the gauge invariance of the circulation field, and hence the vorticity, follows from the pair Berry connection
A_{p}({\bf k})
and pairing phase
\varphi_{\bf k}
transforming the same way. Explicitly, in terms of the projected gap function, the pairing phase is the complex argument
\varphi_{\bf k}=\mathrm{arg}[\Delta_{\mathrm{proj}}({\bf k})]
, and
\nabla_{\bf k}\varphi_{\bf k}^{N}=\nabla_{\bf k}\varphi_{\bf k}^{S}+\frac{4q_{j}}{k\sin\theta_{\bf k}}\hat{\phi}_{\bf k}
. The pair state transforms with a transition function given by the product of the single-particle transition functions, and thus
A_{p}^{N}({\bf k})=A_{p}^{S}({\bf k})+\frac{4q_{j}}{k\sin\theta_{\bf k}}\hat{\phi}_{\bf k}
. Integrating the Berry connection
\nabla\times A_{p}
over the Fermi surface, a calculation similar to Eq. (
6
) shows
q_{p}=2q_{j}
, and the pair monopole charge can thus be read from the transition function of
\Delta_{\mathrm{proj}}({\bf k})
.

For any matrix part, the momentum irrep must be chosen so that the condition required by Fermi statistics,
\Delta_{\alpha\beta}({\bf k})=-\Delta_{\beta\alpha}(-{\bf k})
, is satisfied. Further, the projected gap function vanishes for pairings with matrix part
M_{01}
,
M_{10}
,
M_{11}\pm M_{22}
,
M_{20}
,
M_{23}
, or
M_{31}
.
Note that the projected gap function to lowest order is calculated from
\Delta_{proj}({\bf k})=\langle\psi_{s_{1},s_{2}}|\hat{\Delta}({\bf k})|\psi^{h}_{-s_{1},s_{2}}\rangle=\langle\psi_{s_{1},s_{2}}|\hat{\Delta}({\bf k})(-\Pi)\mathcal{K}|\psi_{s_{1},s_{2}}\rangle
, with
\Pi=M_{03}
the parity operator and
\mathcal{K}
the complex conjugation operator. The projected gap function to lowest order is thus of the form
v^{T}Mv
and vanishes when the matrix
M=-\hat{\Delta}({\bf k})\Pi
is antisymmetric,
M=-M^{T}
, as is the case for
\hat{\Delta}({\bf k})\propto M_{01},M_{11}\pm M_{22},M_{20},M_{23}
and
M_{31}
. These gap functions thus vanish as a result of parity symmetry. The final vanishing gap function,
\hat{\Delta}({\bf k})\propto M_{10}
, is a special case for the
\beta_{s}=\beta_{p}
model that vanishes since
\psi_{s1,s2}^{(1)}\psi_{s1,s2}^{(3)}-\psi_{s1,s2}^{(2)}\psi_{s1,s2}^{(4)}=0
, where
\psi_{s1,s2}^{(j)}
is the
j
th component of
|\psi_{s1,s2}\rangle
in the spin-orbital basis. In the full model, Eq. (
4
) with
\beta_{s}\neq\beta_{p}
, we expect a pairing with matrix
M_{10}
to have qualitatively similar projected gap function structure to one with
M_{13}
, both of which are in the
A_{g}
representation.

Self-testing of any pure entangled state with minimal number of measurements and optimal randomness certification in one-sided device-independent scenario
Shubhayan Sarkar, Jakub J. Borkała, Chellasamy Jebarathinam, Owidiusz Makuta, Debashis Saha, Remigiusz Augusiak

Abstract
Certification of quantum systems and their properties has become a field of intensive studies. Here, taking advantage of the one-sided device-independent scenario (known also as quantum steering scenario), we propose a self-testing scheme for all bipartite entangled states using a single family of steering inequalities with the minimal number of two measurements per party. Building on this scheme we then show how to certify all rank-one extremal measurements, including non-projective d 2 d^{2} -outcome measurements, which in turn can be used for certification of the maximal amount of randomness, that is, 2 ​ log 2 ​ d 2\log_{2}d bits. Finally, in a particular case of d = 3 d=3 , we extend our self-testing results to the fully device-independent setting.



Introduction.—
Randomness plays an essential role in numerous fields such as simulations, cryptography and sampling
Pirandola et al. 2020
. Interestingly, measurements on quantum systems can be used to generate true randomness. One of the most secure scenarios for random number generation is based on quantum non-locality
Brunner et al. 2014
;
Cavalcanti and Skrzypczyk 2016
. Recently, quantum non-locality, which can be observed via the violation of Bell inequalities
Bell 1964
, has been identified as a resource for certifying randomness in a device-independent (DI) way, that is, independent of the detailed physical description of the experimental set-up
Acín and Masanes 2016
. However, most of the known results in the DI setting concern composite quantum systems of relatively low dimensions
Acín et al. 2016
;
Woodhead et al. 2020
;
Tavakoli et al. 2021
. In fact, there exist only a few protocols for randomness certification in systems of arbitrary dimension; see, e.g., Ref.
Sarkar et al. 2021a
where such a scheme for certification of
\log_{2}d
random bits based on the maximally entangled states of local dimension
d
and projective measurements was proposed.
It remains a highly non-trivial problem to find methods of optimal randomness certification using any bipartite entangled state and non-projective measurements.

From an experimental point of view, implementation of Bell nonlocality is challenging due to the requirement of low levels of noise and high detector efficiencies. On the other hand, the observation of asymmetric form of quantum nonlocality, known as quantum steering
Wiseman et al. 2007
, can be revealed through violation of steering inequalities
Cavalcanti et al. 2009
, and is more robust to noise and imperfections of the detectors
Passaro et al. 2015
. Motivated by this, quantum steering has been intensively studied as a resource for various protocols in a one-sided device-independent (
1
SDI) scenario, in which a part of the experimental set-up is assumed to be characterized and trusted
Branciard et al. 2012
;
Law et al. 2014
;
Šupic and Hoban 2016
. Recently, the possibilities for certification of quantum states and measurements in 1SDI scenario were investigated in Refs.
Šupic and Hoban 2016
;
Gheorghiu et al. 2017
;
Goswami et al. 2018
;
Shrotriya et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2016
;
Sarkar et al. 2021b
.
Moreover, the growing interest in practical quantum randomness certification provides additional motivation to look for scenarios that are easier to implement and have the highest possible efficiency, that is, those that employ minimal resources in terms of states and measurements and exhibit high noise resistance
Friis et al. 2019
;
Kaniewski et al. 2019
;
Guo et al. 2019
;
Designolle et al. 2021
. In fact, randomness generation within the 1SDI scenario has recently been studied
Passaro et al. 2015
;
Skrzypczyk and Cavalcanti 2018
; in particular Ref.
Skrzypczyk and Cavalcanti 2018
provides a scheme for the generation of maximal amount of
\log_{2}d
with the aid
of
d
-outcome projective measurements. However, it remains an open and highly nontrivial problem whether one can generate the maximal amount of randomness achievable in quantum systems of dimension
d
, i.e.,
2\log_{2}d
bits.

This work aims to solve the above problem and takes advantage of the one-sided device-independent setting to propose a scheme for optimal randomness certification in quantum systems of arbitrary local dimension. The first step we make on the way to achieve this result is to construct a class of steering inequalities that are maximally violated by any pure entangled two qudit state and just two measurements on each side, which is the minimum number needed to observe quantum steering. Importantly, we then show that maximal violation of these inequalities can be used for 1SDI certification of any two-qudit pure entangled state and mutually unbiased measurements on Bob’s side. Additionally, we show that any rank-one extremal measurement can be certified using our scheme. Based on the above results, we present a certification method of
2\log_{2}d
bits of randomness from maximally entangled state of any local dimension
d
and also from some partially entangled states of local dimension
3,4,5
and
6
.
In the particular case of
d=3
, we finally extend our self-testing results to the device-independent setting.

Preliminaries.—
We first provide some background information necessary for further
considerations.

The scenario.
We consider the
1
SDI scenario consisting of
two parties Alice and Bob, located in separated laboratories that share some quantum state
\rho_{AB}
.
Both parties have access to measuring devices which they use to perform measurements on their shares of
\rho_{AB}
. Contrary to the device-independent scenario, here we assume that one of the measuring devices is trusted (say, the one held by Alice) and performs known measurements. Yet, unlike in the standard steering scenario, we do not need to assume that her device performs full quantum tomography. On the other hand, Bob’s device remains untrusted and can be treated as a ’black box’. The measurement choices of Alice and Bob are denoted
x
and
y
, whereas the obtained outcomes
a
and
b
, respectively.

The correlations obtained in this scenario are captured by a set of probability distributions
\vec{p}=\{p(a,b|x,y)\}
, where
p(a,b|x,y)
is the probability of observing outcomes
a

and
b
by Alice and Bob after performing measurements
x
and
y
, respectively; it expresses as

p(a,b|x,y)=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{AB}(M_{a|x}\otimes N_{b|y})\right],

(1)

where
\rho_{AB}
is the state shared by Alice and Bob, and
M_{x}=\{M_{a|x}\}
and

N_{y}=\{N_{b|y}\}
are Alice’s and Bob’s measurements; recall that the measurement operators
M_{a|x}
and
N_{b|y}
are positive semi-definite and
sum up to the identity on the corresponding Hilbert space for each
x
and
y
.
In what follows, we assume that Alice performs projective measurements; then
M_{a|x}
are pairwise orthogonal projections. On the other hand, we make no assumptions about Bob’s measurements or the shared state; in fact, we consider a fully general situation of
\rho_{AB}
being mixed and Bob’s measurement being POVM’s.

In
1
SDI scenario, quantum steering is demonstrated if the probability distribution
\{p(a,b|x,y)\}
cannot be described by a local hidden state (LHS) model
Wiseman et al. 2007
, that is,

p(a,b|x,y)=\sum_{\lambda}p(\lambda)\mathrm{Tr}[M_{a|x}\,\rho^{\lambda}_{A}]p(b|y,\lambda),

(2)

where
\lambda
is a hidden variable distributed according to the probability distribution
p(\lambda)
and determines both the state
\rho^{\lambda}_{A}
sent to Alice and Bob’s response
p(b|y,\lambda)
. Any set of probability distribution
\{p(a,b|x,y)\}
which does not admit the above form can be detected through the violation of a linear steering inequality,

\mathcal{B}[p(a,b|x,y)]\leq\beta_{L}
, where
\mathcal{B}
is a linear combination of the probabilities
p(a,b|x,y)
,
where
\beta_{L}
is the classical or LHS bound
Cavalcanti et al. 2009
.

It is beneficial to express
the observed correlations in terms of the
generalized expectation values defined as

\langle A_{k|x}B_{l|y}\rangle=\sum^{d-1}_{a,b=0}\omega^{ak+bl}p(a,b|x,y),

(3)

where
\omega=\exp(2\pi\mathbbm{i}/d)
and
k,l=0,\ldots,d-1
. If
p(a,b|x,y)
are quantum,
these can be expressed as
\langle A_{k|x}B_{l|y}\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}[(A_{x}^{k}\otimes B_{l|y})\rho_{AB}]
, where
A_{x}^{k}
are
k
th powers of a unitary observable
A_{x}
(
A_{x}^{d}=\mathbbm{1}_{d}
), whereas
B_{l|y}
are Fourier transforms of the measurement operators
N_{b|y}
:
B_{l|y}=\sum_{b}\omega^{lb}N_{b|y}
. In fact, the operators
B_{l|y}
provide an alternative, but unique representation of the corresponding measurement
N_{y}
. We can thus refer to the set
B_{y}=\{B_{l|y}\}_{l=0}^{d-1}
as Bob’s measurement too. If
N_{y}
is projective, then
B_{l|y}
are
l
th powers of a unitary observable, which, slightly abusing the notation, we also denote
B_{y}

(B_{y}^{d}=\mathbbm{1})
, that is,
B_{l|y}=B_{y}^{l}
(see Appendix
A
).

Randomness certification.
In order to certify true randomness in Bob’s outcomes, we consider an adversarial scenario, where an eavesdropper Eve has access to Bob’s laboratory. We therefore assume the state
\rho_{AB}
to be a reduced state of a (in general entangled) state
\rho_{ABE}
shared by Alice, Bob and Eve, i.e.
\rho_{AB}=\mathrm{Tr}_{E}[\rho_{ABE}]
. We can safely assume it to be pure. Now, Eve’s aim is to correctly guess the outcome of one of Bob’s measurements, say
N_{y}
. To do so, on her share of
\rho_{ABE}
she performs a measurement
Z=\{E_{e}\}
whose outcome
e
is the best guess on Bob’s outcome. We quantify the amount of random bits obtained from Bob’s measurement with
H_{\min}=-\log_{2}G(y,\vec{p})
, where
G(y,\vec{p})
is the local guessing probability defined as

G(y,\vec{p})=\sup_{S\in S_{\vec{p}}}\sum_{b}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{ABE}\left(\mathbbm{1}\otimes N_{b|y}\otimes E_{b}\right)\right],

(4)

where
S_{\vec{p}}
is the set of all Eve’s strategies (consisting of the shared state
\rho_{ABE}
and the measurement
Z
) that reproduce the correlations
\vec{p}
observed by Alice and Bob.
As was demonstrated in
Passaro et al. 2015
, quantum realisations that violate steering inequalities can be used to certify genuine randomness on the untrusted side. One of our aims here is to show that the maximal amount of
2\log_{2}d
random bits can be certified from pure entangled bipartite states of Schmidt rank
d
in the 1SDI scenario.

Results.—
We first introduce a general class of steering inequalities that are maximally violated by any two-qudit pure entangled state and two measurements on both sides. Consider an entangled state from
\mathbbm{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbbm{C}^{d}

in its Schmidt decomposition

\displaystyle|\psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\rangle_{AB}=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\alpha_{i}|i\rangle_{A}|i\rangle_{B},

(5)

where
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is a vector composed of the Schmidt coefficients
\alpha_{i}\geq 0

satisfying
\alpha_{0}^{2}+\ldots+\alpha_{d-1}^{2}=1
.
Clearly, without any loss of generality,
we can fix the local bases to be the computational basis of
\mathbbm{C}^{d}
.
We can also assume that all
\alpha_{i}>0
, or, equivalently,
that the Schmidt rank of
|\psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\rangle
is maximal;
otherwise
|\psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\rangle
is not a two-qudit state.

Now, our steering inequality maximally violated by
(
5
) for a given
\boldsymbol{\alpha}

can be stated in the observable picture as

\mathcal{B}_{d}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\!\!=\!\!\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\left\langle A_{0}^{k}\otimes B_{k|0}\!+\!\gamma(\boldsymbol{\alpha})A_{1}^{k}\otimes B_{k|1}\!+\!\delta_{k}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})A_{0}^{k}\right\rangle\leq\beta_{L},

(6)

where
\gamma(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
and
\delta_{k}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
are
d
coefficients given by

\displaystyle\gamma(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\frac{d}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=0\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{d-1}\frac{\alpha_{i}}{\alpha_{j}}},\quad\delta_{k}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=-\frac{\gamma}{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=0\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{d-1}\frac{\alpha_{i}}{\alpha_{j}}\omega^{k(d-j)}.

(7)

Whereas Bob performs two arbitrary measurements
B_{0}
and
B_{1}
, Alice’s observables
are known and fixed to be simply
A_{0}=Z_{d}
and
A_{1}=X_{d}
, where

\displaystyle Z_{d}=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\omega^{i}|i\rangle\!\langle i|,\qquad X_{d}=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}|i+1\rangle\!\langle i|

(8)

are generalizations of Pauli matrices to
d
-dimensional Hilbert spaces. It should be mentioned
that an alternative construction of steering inequalities maximally violated by
two-qudit pure entangled states is given in Ref.
Skrzypczyk and Cavalcanti 2018
,
however, except for the particular case of the maximally entangled state, those inequalities require
d+1
measurements on the trusted side to observe the maximal quantum violation.
Our inequalities require only two measurements on the trusted side for any state (
5
).

While it is in general challenging to determine the classical bound
\beta_{L}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})

for any
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
, one can
derive (see Appendix
B
for details) the following upper bound on it:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{L}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{|\phi\rangle}\left\{d\max_{a}\{|\eta_{a}|^{2}\}+\gamma(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\left(\sum_{a=0}^{d-1}|\eta_{a}|\right)^{2}\right.

(9)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\left.-\gamma(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\sum_{i,a=0}^{d-1}\alpha_{i}\frac{|\eta_{a}|^{2}}{\alpha_{a}}\right\},

where the maximization is over
d
numbers
|\eta_{i}|
obeying

|\eta_{0}|^{2}+\ldots+|\eta_{d-1}|^{2}=1
, which come from the decomposition of some quantum state

|\phi\rangle=\sum_{i}\eta_{i}|i\rangle\in\mathbbm{C}^{d}
.

On the other hand, the maximal quantum value of
\mathcal{B}_{d}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})

can be analytically found for any choice of
\alpha_{i}>0
and reads
\beta_{Q}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=d
(see Appendix
C
for the proof).
Crucially, this value is achieved by the state (
5
)
and Bob’s measurements being simply
B_{0}=Z_{d}^{*}
and
B_{1}=X_{d}
.
For a remark, it is interesting to notice that for this choice of observables
the steering operator corresponding to (
6
) becomes simply a combination of two stabilizing operators that stabilize the state (
5
). Moreover,

\beta_{Q}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
is strictly larger than
\beta_{L}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
for any

\boldsymbol{\alpha}
such that each
\alpha_{i}>0
.

Theorem 1
.

For any set of positive and normalized coefficient
\alpha_{i}
,
the maximal quantum value of
\mathcal{B}_{d}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
is

\beta_{Q}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=d
and obeys
\beta_{Q}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})>\beta_{L}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
.

We thus have a general class of nontrivial steering inequalities maximally violated by any pure entangled state (
5
) and two measurements on Bob’s side, which is, in fact, the minimal number necessary to observe steerability. Moreover, as we show in the following theorem (see Appendix
D
for a proof), their maximal violation can be used for 1SDI self-testing of any entangled state (
5
).

Theorem 2
.

Assume that the steering inequality (
6
) with
A_{0}=Z_{d}
and
A_{1}=X_{d}
is maximally violated by
|\psi\rangle_{ABE}\in(\mathbbm{C}^{d})_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{E}
and Bob’s measurements
B_{i}

(i=1,2)
. Then, the following statements hold true for any
d
: (i)
\mathcal{H}_{B}=(\mathbbm{C}^{d})_{B^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B^{\prime\prime}}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{B^{\prime\prime}}
is some finite-dimensional Hilbert space, (ii) Bob’s measurements are projective and there is a local unitary transformation
U_{B}:\mathcal{H}_{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{B}
, such that

\displaystyle\forall i,\quad U_{B}\,B_{i}\,U_{B}^{\dagger}=A_{i}^{*}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{B^{\prime\prime}},

(10)

where
\mathbbm{1}_{B^{\prime\prime}}
is the identity on
\mathcal{H}_{B^{\prime\prime}}
and for some
|\xi_{B^{\prime\prime}E}\rangle
, the state
|\psi_{ABE}\rangle
is given by

\displaystyle(\mathbbm{1}_{AE}\otimes U_{B})|\psi_{ABE}\rangle=|\psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\rangle_{AB^{\prime}}\otimes|\xi_{B^{\prime\prime}E}\rangle.

(11)

It should be stressed that since we consider
the adversarial scenario, we do not assume the state shared by Alice and Bob to be pure. Instead, we consider an arbitrary mixed state represented by a purification
|\psi_{ABE}\rangle
. Moreover, we do not take advantage of the assumption that Bob’s measurements are projective; we infer this from maximal violation of our inequalities.

Certification of all rank-one extremal POVM’s.—
Let us now proceed to another result, which is a consequence of Theorem
2
. Based on it, we can
design a simple scheme for 1SDI certification of any extremal rank-one POVM.
Recall that a POVM
\{\mathcal{I}_{b}\}
, where
b
denotes its outcomes, is called extremal if it cannot be decomposed as a convex mixture of other POVMs
D'Ariano et al. 2005
; moreover, a rank-one POVM is one for which the measurement operators are rank-one:
\mathcal{I}_{b}=\lambda_{b}|\mu\rangle\!\langle\mu|
with
|\mu\rangle\in\mathbbm{C}^{d}
.

Let us go back to the 1SDI set-up and slightly modify it by adding a third measurement on the untrusted side, which is a POVM with
d^{2}-
outcomes, denoted by
\{R_{b}\}
. Moreover, we now let Alice gather statistics corresponding to
d^{2}
observables constructed from the operators
X_{d}^{i}Z_{d}^{j}
for
i,j=0,1,\ldots,d-1

foo
. Notice that they contain
X_{d}
and
Z_{d}
used in our inequalities (
6
).
Now, the maximal violation of (
6
) by correlations obtained when Alice and Bob measure, respectively,
X_{d}
and
Z_{d}
, and
B_{0}
and
B_{1}
are used to certify the state
|\psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\rangle
shared among them (see Theorem
2
). Then, the correlations observed for the remaining observables of Alice and Bob’s third measurement are exploited to certify the extremal POVM
\{R_{b}\}
.
Indeed, generalizing the results of
Woodhead et al. 2020
, in Appendix
D
we prove the following fact.

Theorem 3
.

If for any
i,j=0,\ldots,d-1
the identities

\displaystyle\langle X_{d}^{i}Z_{d}^{j}\otimes R_{b}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{E}\rangle_{|\psi_{ABE}\rangle}=\langle X_{d}^{i}Z_{d}^{j}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{b}\rangle_{|\psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\rangle}

(12)

hold true for the correlations obtained from the POVM
\{R_{b}\}_{b}
while measured on
the certified state
U_{B}|\psi_{ABE}\rangle=|\psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\rangle\otimes|\xi_{B^{\prime\prime}E}\rangle
for some unitary transformation
U_{B}:\mathcal{H}_{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{B}

and those obtained from an extremal reference POVM
\{\mathcal{I}_{b}\}
with the
ideal state
|\psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\rangle
, then
the elements of the POVM are given by
U_{B}R_{b}U_{B}^{\dagger}=\mathcal{I}_{b}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{B^{\prime\prime}}

Certification of maximal randomness.—
An important consequence of Theorem
3
is that one can certify
2\log_{2}d
bits of randomness on the untrusted side. This is the maximum amount of randomness that can be extracted from a
d
dimensional quantum system by performing a local measurement on it. Indeed, by virtue of Theorem
2
, maximal violation of our inequality for some
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
implies that
the joint state
|\psi_{ABE}\rangle
, after an application of a suitable local unitary

U_{B}
, takes the form (
11

3
implies that
under the same unitary Bob’s additional measurement
\{R_{b}\}
is
R_{b}=\mathcal{I}_{b}\otimes\mathbbm{1}
. Plugging all this into Eq. (
4

y=2

G(y=2,\vec{p})=\sup_{\sigma_{E},Z}\sum_{b}\mathrm{Tr}[\mathcal{I}_{b}\rho_{B^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})]\mathrm{Tr}[Z_{b}\sigma_{E}],

(13)

where the optimization over all Eve’s strategies reduces to one over the states
\sigma_{E}=\mathrm{Tr}_{B^{\prime\prime}}|\xi_{B^{\prime\prime}E}\rangle\!\langle\xi_{B^{\prime\prime}E}|
and Eve’s measurement
Z
, and
\rho_{B^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\mathrm{Tr}_{A}|{\psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})}\rangle\!\langle{\psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})}|_{AB^{\prime}}

for any extremal POVM
\{\mathcal{I}_{b}\}

\mathrm{Tr}[\mathcal{I}_{b}\rho_{B}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})]=1/d^{2},

(14)

the guessing probability (

Thus, the maximal violation of our inequality (
6

with conditions (

2\log_{2}d

of random bits from Bob’s POVM using any pure entangled state provided that there exists extremal POVM’s
\{\mathcal{I}_{b}\}
that satisfy (
14

d^{2}

and
\rho_{B^{\prime}}=\mathbbm{1}/d
(which corresponds to the maximally entangled state of two qudits) was found in Ref.
D'Ariano et al. 2005
, and for completeness is presented in Appendix

d=3,\ldots,6
we also provide constructions of extremal POVM’s obeying the condition (
14

Conclusions and outlook.—
In this work, we constructed a class of steering inequalities maximally violated by every entangled bipartite state.
Our inequalities are more experimentally friendly, since they require performing only two measurements, as compered to those constructed in Ref.
Skrzypczyk and Cavalcanti 2018
that are based on
d+1
measurements. We then provided a method of certification of quantum realisations that maximally violate our steering inequalities. Contrary to the previous approach
Shrotriya et al. 2021
, which is a direct adaptation of the ’projection’ method of Ref.
Coladangelo et al. 2017
to the steering scenario, our scheme uses only two genuinely
d
-outcome measurements per site. This might facilitate the implementation of our method in practical scenarios. Using the self-testing results, we also introduced a simple scheme for certification of any rank-one extremal POVM, which is used to show that the maximal amount of
2\log_{2}d
bits of randomness can be generated using maximally entangled state of local dimension
d
in the 1SDI scenario as well as some partially entangled states for
d=3,4,5,6
, extending at the same time the results of Ref.
Woodhead et al. 2020
.

Several follow-up questions arise from our work. First, it would be interesting to explore whether, by building on our construction, one can design Bell inequalities which are maximally violated by any entangled bipartite state and two measurements per site. Then, a related question is whether one can generalize our extended Bell scenario to other situations, such as, for instance, quantum states of higher local dimension or multipartite quantum states.

As for randomness generation, one possibility is to find extremal POVM’s of maximal number of outcomes that would result in optimal randomness generation using any pure bipartite, even arbitrarily little entangled states. Another one, certainly more challenging, is to attack the open and highly nontrivial problem of designing a DI strategy for certification of maximal randomness in quantum systems of arbitrary local dimension. A possible solution here would be to again combine our one-sided device-independent scheme with a device-independent method that allows for self-testing of measurements on Alice’s side. In the particular case of
d=3
, a scheme of this type that allows for certification of
2\log_{2}3
bits of local randomness from any entangled two-qutrit state of Schmidt rank three can be proposed
Borkała et al. 2022
(see also Ref.
Tavakoli et al. 2021
).

where we have also used Eq. (

(60)

B_{k|0}
is unitary for any

B_{k|0}
is projective, that is, the positive semi-definite measurement operators

N_{b|0}
are mutually orthogonal projectors. Moreover,
B_{k|0}
are powers of
B_{1|0}

and thus, in what follows, we will simply denote

B_{0}\equiv B_{1|0}

Let us now move to proving that the second Bob’s measurement is projective too.
To this end, we will use the second condition (

the following representation of the state

Observation 2.1
.

The matrix

\tilde{Z}=\overline{Z}_{A}+\gamma(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\mathbbm{1}=[1+\gamma(\boldsymbol{\alpha})]\mathbbm{1}-\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\delta_{k}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})Z_{d}^{k}

\gamma
and
\delta_{k}
defined in Eq. (

Proof.

First of all we notice that the above operator is Hermitian which is a consequence of
the facts that
A_{0}=Z_{d}
and
\delta_{k}^{*}=\delta_{d-k}
. Moreover, by using the explicit form of
\delta_{k}
it is straightforward to see that the eigenvalues of (
99
) are given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{l}=1+\gamma(\boldsymbol{\alpha})+\frac{\gamma(\boldsymbol{\alpha})}{d}\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=0\\

(100)

where

. After simple manipulations the above expression simplifies to

\lambda_{l}=\gamma(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\end{subarray}}^{d-1}\frac{\alpha_{i}}{\alpha_{l}}.

Let us note that

and

Appendix E
Certification of all rank-one extremal POVM

To certify any rank-one extremal POVM, we add to our
1
SDI set-up a third measurement on the untrusted Bob’s side, which is a POVM with
d^{2}-
outcomes, denoted by
\{R_{b}\}
. In this updated
1
SDI set-up, Alice needs to perform
d^{2}
number of measurements corresponding to
X_{d}^{i}Z_{d}^{j}
for
i,j=0,1,\ldots,d-1
and Bob needs to perform three measurements. The statistics obtained when Alice performs
X_{d},Z_{d}
with Bob performing his first two measurements are used to certify the state shared among them as shown in Theorem
2
and the rest of the statistics are used to certify the POVM.
Here we present the proof of Theorem 3 that along with the self-testing statement
in Theorem
2
gives a method of certifying all rank-one extremal POVMs within
the steering scenario. Recall that up to a local unitary operation
U_{B}
on Bob’s side
any state
|\psi_{ABE}\rangle
maximally violating
our steering inequality takes the form

(\mathbbm{1}_{AE}\otimes U_{B})|\psi_{ABE}\rangle=|\psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\rangle_{AB^{\prime}}\otimes|\xi_{B^{\prime\prime}E}\rangle,

(102)

where

|\psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\rangle_{AB^{\prime}}=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\alpha_{i}|ii\rangle

(103)

with
\alpha_{i}>0
for any
i
.

Theorem 3
.

Consider a POVM
\{R_{b}\}_{b}
defined on Bob’s Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{B}=\mathbbm{C}^{d}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B^{\prime\prime}}
. If for some extremal
POVM
\{\mathcal{I}_{b}\}_{b}
defined on
\mathbbm{C}^{d}
the following identities

\displaystyle\langle X^{i}Z^{j}\otimes R_{b}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{E}\rangle_{|\psi_{ABE}\rangle}=\langle X^{i}Z^{j}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{b}\rangle_{|\psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\rangle}

(104)

are fulfilled for any
i,j=0,\ldots,d-1
, where
|\psi_{ABE}\rangle
and
|\psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\rangle_{AB^{\prime}}
are defined in Eqs. (
102
)
and (
103
), respectively, then there exist a unitary transformation
U_{B}:\mathcal{H}_{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{B}
such that
the elements of the POVM are given by
U_{B}R_{b}U_{B}^{\dagger}=\mathcal{I}_{b}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{B^{\prime\prime}}
for all
b
.

Proof.

The proof presented here generalises the one of Ref.
Woodhead et al. 2020
to arbitrary dimension given that Alice is trusted. Eve’s strategy implies that any POVM
\{R_{b}\}_{b}
must reproduce the same statistics as the ideal POVM
\{\mathcal{I}_{b}\}_{b}
when Eve is absent

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{ABE}|X^{i}Z^{j}\otimes R_{b}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{E}|\psi_{ABE}\rangle=\langle\psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})|X^{i}Z^{j}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{b}|\psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\rangle\quad\forall\ b.

(105)

Let us first notice that any state
|\psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\rangle
can be represented in
terms of the maximally entangled state of two qudits
|\phi_{+}^{d}\rangle
as

|\psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\rangle=[\mathbbm{1}_{A}\otimes P(\boldsymbol{\alpha})]|\phi_{+}^{d}\rangle,

(106)

where

P(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\alpha_{i}|i\rangle\!\langle i|

(107)

and, as before, all
\alpha_{i}
are assumed to be positive. Based on this representation, let us then introduce an operator basis for operators acting on
\mathbbm{C}^{d}
given by

W_{i,j}:=P(\boldsymbol{\alpha})^{-1}\left(X^{i}Z^{j}\right)^{*}P(\boldsymbol{\alpha})^{-1}.

(108)

Notice that the elements of this basis are linearly independent because
P(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
is invertible and
X^{i}Z^{j}
are orthogonal in the Hilbert-Schmidt scalar product.

Now, exploiting the fact that
W_{i,j}
form a basis, we can express each measurement operator
\mathcal{I}_{b}
of the ideal POVM as

\mathcal{I}_{b}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{d-1}l^{b}_{i,j}W_{i,j},

(109)

where
l_{i,j}^{b}
are some in general complex coefficients. Analogously, taking
advantage of the fact that Bob’s Hilbert space decomposes as
\mathcal{H}_{B}=\mathbbm{C}^{d}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B^{\prime\prime}}
, we
can represent each
R_{b}
in the following way

\displaystyle U_{B}R_{b}U_{B}^{\dagger}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{d-1}W_{i,j}\otimes\widetilde{R}_{i,j}^{b},

(110)

where
\widetilde{R}_{i,j}^{b}
are some matrices acting on
\mathcal{H}_{B^{\prime\prime}}
and
U_{B}:\mathcal{H}_{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{B}
is a unitary transformation such that from Eq. (
102
),

\displaystyle(\mathbbm{1}_{AE}\otimes U_{B})|\psi_{ABE}\rangle=|\psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\rangle_{AB^{\prime}}\otimes|\xi_{B^{\prime\prime}E}\rangle.

(111)

Substitution of (
109
) into (
105
) yields

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{ABE}|X^{i}Z^{j}\otimes R_{b}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{E}|\psi_{ABE}\rangle=\sum_{m,n}l^{b}_{m,n}\langle\psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})|X^{i}Z^{j}\otimes P(\boldsymbol{\alpha})^{-1}\left(X^{m}Z^{n}\right)^{*}P(\boldsymbol{\alpha})^{-1}|\psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\rangle

(112)

for any
b
. Using then Eq. (
106
) and the fact that

(Q\otimes R)\,|\phi_{+}^{d}\rangle=(\mathbbm{1}\otimes RQ^{T})|\phi_{+}^{d}\rangle
holds true for any two matrices
Q
and
R
as well as the fact that
X^{i}Z^{j}
form an orthogonal basis in the Hilbert-Schmidt scalar product, one obtains

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{ABE}|X^{i}Z^{j}\otimes R_{b}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{E}|\psi_{ABE}\rangle=\sum_{m,n}l^{b}_{m,n}\langle\phi_{+}^{d}|X^{i}Z^{j}\otimes\left(X^{m}Z^{n}\right)^{*}|\phi_{+}^{d}\rangle=l^{b}_{i,j}.

(113)

Now, expanding the left hand side of (
113
) by using the above mentioned form of
R_{b}
(
110
), we have

\displaystyle\langle\xi_{B^{\prime\prime}E}|\widetilde{R}_{i,j}^{b}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{E}|\xi_{B^{\prime\prime}E}\rangle=l_{i,j}^{b}=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\widetilde{R}_{i,j}^{b}\sigma_{B^{\prime\prime}}\right),

(114)

where
\sigma_{B^{\prime\prime}}=\mathrm{Tr}_{E}(|\xi_{B^{\prime\prime}E}\rangle\!\langle\xi_{B^{\prime\prime}E}|)
. Using then the eigendecomposition of
\sigma_{B^{\prime\prime}}
, i.e.,
\sigma_{B^{\prime\prime}}=\sum_{k}p_{k}|k\rangle\!\langle k|
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\sum_{k}p_{k}\langle k|\widetilde{R}_{i,j}^{b}|k\rangle=l_{i,j}^{b}.

(115)

Next, we introduce a collection of POVMs
\{\mathcal{I}_{b}^{k}\}_{b}

numbered by
k
, whose measurement operators are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{b}^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{B^{\prime\prime}}\left[\left(\mathbbm{1}_{B^{\prime}}\otimes|k\rangle\!\langle k|_{B^{\prime\prime}}\right)R_{b}\right]

(116)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=0}^{d-1}\langle k|\widetilde{R}_{i,j}^{b}|k\rangle W_{i,j}.

Since
R_{b}\geq 0
it directly follows from the above equation that
\mathcal{I}_{b}^{k}\geq 0
for any
k
and
b
. Moreover,
\sum_{b}R_{b}=\mathbbm{1}_{B}
implies that
\sum_{b}\mathcal{I}_{b,k}=\mathbbm{1}_{B^{\prime}}
for any
k
. All this means that
\{\mathcal{I}_{b}^{k}\}_{b}
are indeed proper quantum measurements.

These additional POVMs, through Eq. (
115
), allows us to decompose
the ideal POVM
\{\mathcal{I}_{b}\}
in the following way

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{b}=\sum_{k}p_{k}\mathcal{I}_{b}^{k},

(117)

which, given that the ideal POVM is assumed to be extremal, implies that

\displaystyle\forall k\qquad\mathcal{I}_{b}^{k}=\mathcal{I}_{b},

(118)

which can equivalently understood as

\displaystyle\forall k\qquad\langle k|\widetilde{R}_{i,j}^{b}|k\rangle=l_{i,j}^{b}.

(119)

Now, we consider the following vectors from
\mathbbm{C}^{d}
:

\displaystyle|\varphi_{a,s,t}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(|s\rangle\pm\mathbbm{i}^{a}|t\rangle\right),

(120)

where
|s\rangle
and
|t\rangle
are vectors belonging to the eigenbasis
\{|k\rangle\}
of
\sigma_{B^{\prime\prime}}
such that
s\neq t
and
a=0,1
. Let us look at the quantity

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{B^{\prime\prime}}\left[\left(\mathbbm{1}_{B^{\prime}}\otimes|\varphi_{a,s,t}\rangle\!\langle\varphi_{a,s,t}|_{B^{\prime\prime}}\right)R_{b}\right]=\sum_{i,j}\mathrm{Tr}(|\varphi_{a,s,t}\rangle\!\langle\varphi_{a,s,t}|_{B^{\prime\prime}}\widetilde{R}_{i,j}^{b})W_{i,j},

(121)

which with the aid of the explicit form of the vector (
120
)
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{B^{\prime\prime}}\left[\left(\mathbbm{1}_{B^{\prime}}\otimes|\varphi_{a,s,t}\rangle\!\langle\varphi_{a,s,t}|_{B^{\prime\prime}}\right)R_{b}\right]=\mathcal{I}_{b}\pm\mathrm{Tr}_{B^{\prime\prime}}\left[(\mathbbm{1}_{B^{\prime}}\otimes L^{a}_{B^{\prime\prime}})R_{b}\right],

(122)

where
L_{B^{\prime\prime}}^{a}=(\mathbbm{i}^{a}/2)\left(|t\rangle\!\langle s|+(-1)^{a}|s\rangle\!\langle t|\right)
. Using the fact that
R_{b}
is positive, implying that the left-hand side of the above
relations is nonnegative, allows us to conclude that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{b}\geq\pm\mathrm{Tr}_{B^{\prime\prime}}\left[(\mathbbm{1}_{B^{\prime}}\otimes L^{a}_{B^{\prime\prime}})R_{b}\right].

(123)

From Ref.
D'Ariano et al. 2005
it follows that the measurement operators of any rank-one extremal POVM can be written as
\mathcal{I}_{b}=\lambda_{b}|\mu_{b}\rangle\!\langle\mu_{b}|
, where for each
b
,
|\mu_{b}\rangle
is a normalized vector from
\in\mathbbm{C}^{d}
. As it is explicitly demonstrated at the end of the proof, the fact that
\mathcal{I}_{b}
is rank one implies that the operator appearing on the right-hand side of Eq. (
123
) must be rank one too, and, moreover, it must be of the following form

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{B^{\prime\prime}}\left[(\mathbbm{1}_{B^{\prime}}\otimes L^{a}_{B^{\prime\prime}})R_{b}\right]=\lambda^{\prime}_{b}|\mu_{b}\rangle\!\langle\mu_{b}|,

(124)

where
\lambda_{b}\geq\pm\lambda^{\prime}_{b}
. Using then the facts that
\sum_{b}R_{b}=\mathbbm{1}
and that

\mathrm{Tr}L_{B^{\prime\prime}}^{a}=0
for any
a
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{b}\mathrm{Tr}_{B^{\prime\prime}}\left[(\mathbbm{1}_{B^{\prime}}\otimes L^{a}_{B^{\prime\prime}})R_{b}\right]=0=\sum_{b}\lambda_{b}^{\prime}|\mu_{b}\rangle\!\langle\mu_{b}|.

(125)

Since,
\mathcal{I}_{b}
are linearly independent the above condition implies that
\lambda^{\prime}_{b}=0
for all
b
. Thus, from (
124
) we can conclude that
\mathrm{Tr}_{B^{\prime\prime}}\left[(\mathbbm{1}_{B^{\prime}}\otimes L^{a}_{B^{\prime\prime}})R_{b}\right]=0
for any
b
and
a
, which
taking into account the explicit form of
L^{a}_{B^{\prime\prime}}
results in the following conditions

\displaystyle\left(X^{i}Z^{j}\right)^{*}\left(\langle s|\widetilde{R}_{i,j}^{b}|t\rangle+\langle t|\widetilde{R}_{i,j}^{b}|s\rangle\right)=0,

(126)

for
a=0
and for
a=1
, we have

\displaystyle\left(X^{i}Z^{j}\right)^{*}\left(\langle t|\widetilde{R}_{i,j}^{b}|s\rangle-\langle s|\widetilde{R}_{i,j}^{b}|t\rangle\right)=0,

(127)

where we used the fact that
X^{i}Z^{j}
are linearly independent for all
i,j
. The only possible solution of equations (
126
) and (
127
) is
\langle s|\widetilde{R}_{i,j}^{b}|t\rangle=0
for
s\neq t
. Thus,
from Eq. (
119
) we conclude that the POVM acting on Bob’s part is given by
R_{b}=\mathcal{I}_{b}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{B^{\prime\prime}}
for all
b^{\prime}s
.

To complete the proof let finally show that Eq. (
124
) holds true for any
b
. To this end, let us pick a particular
b
. Clearly, using the corresponding vector
|\mu_{b}\rangle
we can construct an orthonormal basis

|\phi_{i}\rangle
with
i=0,\ldots,d-1
such that
|\phi_{b}\rangle=|\mu_{b}\rangle
.
Sandwiching then inequalities (
123
) with
|\mu_{j}\rangle
and
|\mu_{i}\rangle
,
one obtains

\displaystyle\forall_{i,j}\qquad\langle\mu_{j}|\mathcal{I}_{b}|\mu_{i}\rangle\geq\pm\langle\mu_{j}|\mathrm{Tr}_{B^{\prime\prime}}\left[(\mathbbm{1}_{B^{\prime}}\otimes L^{a}_{B^{\prime\prime}})R_{b}\right]|\mu_{i}\rangle.

(128)

For
i\neq b
or
j\neq b
the above gives

\displaystyle 0\geq\pm\langle\mu_{j}|\mathrm{Tr}_{B^{\prime\prime}}\left[(\mathbbm{1}_{B^{\prime}}\otimes L^{a}_{B^{\prime\prime}})R_{b}\right]|\mu_{i}\rangle,

(129)

which directly implies that
\langle\mu_{j}|\left[(\mathbbm{1}_{B^{\prime}}\otimes L^{a}_{B^{\prime\prime}})R_{b}\right]|\mu_{i}\rangle=0
for any
i\neq b
or
j\neq b
and simultaneously proves Eq. (
124
). Moreover, for
i=j=b
, Eq. (
128
) gives

\lambda_{b}\geq\pm\langle\mu_{b}|\mathrm{Tr}_{B^{\prime\prime}}\left[(\mathbbm{1}_{B^{\prime}}\otimes L^{a}_{B^{\prime\prime}})R_{b}\right]|\mu_{b}\rangle,

(130)

which additionally imposes that
\lambda_{b}\geq\pm\lambda_{b}^{\prime}
, completing the proof.

Appendix F
Construction of
d^{2}
-outcome extremal POVM

Here we provide an example of extremal qudit POVM that produces locally uniformly random results from two-qudit maximally entangled state
|\psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\rangle_{AB}
.

It should be mentioned that such extremal POVMs were already
studied in Ref.
D'Ariano et al. 2005
. In particular, it was proven there that
the measurement operators defining
d^{2}
-outcome extremal POVMs must necessarily be rank-one, and, moreover, an exemplary construction thereof was given in Hilbert space of arbitrary dimension
d
. For completeness we begin our considerations by recalling this construction. To this aim, let us
define the following set of
d^{2}
unitary operators

U_{k,l}=X_{d}^{k}Z_{d}^{l},

(131)

where
k,l=0\dots d-1
. An example of extremal
d^{2}
-outcome POVM
\{\mathcal{I}_{k,l}\}
in dimension
\mathbbm{C}^{d}
is then given by

\mathcal{I}_{k,l}:=\frac{1}{d}U_{k,l}|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|U^{\dagger}_{k,l},

(132)

where
|\nu\rangle
is any pure state from the
d-
dimensional Hilbert space that satisfies the condition
\mathrm{Tr}[U^{\dagger}_{k,l}|\nu\rangle]\neq 0
for all
k,l
. Noticeably, such a POVM can be used to generate
2\log_{2}d
bits of randomness from the two-qudit maximally entangled state
|\phi_{+}^{d}\rangle
since both its reduced density matrices are simply the maximally mixed state
\rho_{A}=\rho_{B}=\mathbbm{1}_{d}/d
, and therefore

\mathrm{Tr}[\mathcal{I}_{k,l}\rho_{B}]=1/d^{2}
.

Now, we demonstrate the existence of extremal qudit POVMs with
d^{2}
outcomes for
d=3,4,5,6
, that result in
2\log_{2}d
bits of randomness from partially entangled pure states
|\psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\rangle_{AB}

for
\alpha_{i}\geq 1/d
for
i=0,1,\ldots,d-2
.
Let us consider the following rank-one positive semi-definite operators:

\mathcal{I}_{b}:=\lambda_{b}|\delta_{b}\rangle\!\langle\delta_{b}|,

(133)

with
b=0,\ldots,d^{2}-1
, we have

\displaystyle|\delta_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|i\rangle,\qquad(i=0,\ldots,d-2).

(134)

The rest of the vectors, that is, those for
b=d-1,d,\ldots,d^{2}-1
are given by

\displaystyle|\delta_{b}\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\mu_{i}\exp{\left(\frac{2\pi\mathbbm{i}\xi_{i}(b-d+1)}{d^{2}-d+1}\right)}|i\rangle,

(135)

where

\displaystyle\mu_{i}=\sqrt{\frac{1-\lambda_{i}}{(d^{2}-d+1)\lambda_{d}}}\qquad(i=0,1,\ldots,d-2),

(136)

and

\quad\mu_{d-1}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{(d^{2}-d+1)\lambda_{d}}}.

(137)

The
\lambda_{b}^{\prime}s
are given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}=\frac{1}{d^{2}\alpha_{i}^{2}}\qquad(i=0,1,\ldots,d-2),

(138)

and

\lambda_{d-1}=\lambda_{d}=\ldots=\lambda_{d^{2}-1}=\frac{1}{d^{2}-d+1}\left(d-\sum_{i=0}^{d-2}\lambda_{i}\right).

(139)

Finally, we provide the
\xi_{i}
coefficients. Let us note here that to ensure the linear independence of the POVM elements (
133
), the
\xi_{i}
coefficients should satisfy the following property
\forall\,i\neq j,\,\,|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|=n
, where
n=1,\dots,d(d-1)/2
. Below we list examples of possible solutions for the
\xi_{i}
coefficients in a given dimension.

For
d=3
:

\displaystyle\xi_{0}=0,\quad\xi_{1}=1\quad\text{and}\quad\xi_{2}=3.

(140)

For
d=4
:

\displaystyle\xi_{0}=0,\quad\xi_{1}=1,\quad\xi_{2}=3\quad\text{and}\quad\xi_{3}=9.

(141)

For
d=5
:

\displaystyle\xi_{0}=0,\quad\xi_{1}=1,\quad\xi_{2}=4,\quad\xi_{3}=14\quad\text{and}\quad\xi_{4}=16.

(142)

For
d=6
:

\displaystyle\xi_{0}=0,\quad\xi_{1}=1,\quad\xi_{2}=3,\quad\xi_{3}=8,\quad\xi_{4}=12\quad\text{and}\quad\xi_{4}=18.

(143)

Appendix G
Extended Bell scenario

This section shows how our approach to certification of all bipartite entangled states
from
\mathbbm{C}^{3}\otimes\mathbbm{C}^{3}
based on quantum steering can be made fully device-independent by combining it with the current self-testing strategy for the maximally entangled state of two qutrits and mutually unbiased bases provided in Ref.
Kaniewski et al. 2019
.
This strategy relies on violation of a certain Bell inequality that for the particular case
of
d=3
considered here can be stated as

\sum_{k=1}^{2}\sum_{x,y=0}^{2}\lambda_{k}\,\omega^{kxy}\left\langle A_{k|x}\otimes B_{k|y}\right\rangle\leq 6\sqrt{3}\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{9}\right),

(144)

where
\lambda_{0}=1
,
\lambda_{1}=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathbbm{i}\pi/18},\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{1}^{*}
. The above inequality is maximally violated by the maximally entangled state of two qutrits
|\phi_{+}^{3}\rangle
and observables
A_{x},B_{y}
for
x,y\in\{0,1,2\}
acting on, respectively,
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
whose eigenvectors form mutually unbiased bases. Actually, the following statement follows directly from Ref.
Kaniewski et al. 2019
.

Fact 1
.

Assume that
|\psi_{ABE}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{E}

together with the measurements
A_{x}=\{A_{k|x}\}
and
B_{y}=\{B_{k|y}\}
maximally violate the inequality (
144
). Then,

\mathcal{H}_{A}=\mathbbm{C}^{3}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{A^{\prime\prime}}
and

\mathcal{H}_{B}=\mathbbm{C}^{3}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B^{\prime\prime}}
for some Hilbert spaces on Alice’s and Bob’s sides,
\mathcal{H}_{A^{\prime\prime}}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B^{\prime\prime}}
, whose dimensions are unknown but finite. Moreover, Alice’s and Bob’s measurements are projective, meaning that

A_{k|x}=A^{k}_{x}
for some unitary
A_{x}
such that
A_{x}^{3}=\mathbbm{1}
(the same holds for Bob’s measurements). Finally, there exist unitary operations

U_{A}:\mathcal{H}_{A}\to\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
U_{B}:\mathcal{H}_{B}\to\mathcal{H}_{B}

such that in particular the state and two observables of Alice, say
A_{0}
and
A_{1}
,
obey

U_{A}\otimes U_{B}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{E}|\psi_{ABE}\rangle=|\phi_{+}^{3}\rangle_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\otimes|\varphi_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}E}\rangle,

(145)

and

\displaystyle U_{A}\,A_{0}\,U_{A}^{\dagger}=Z_{3}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime\prime}},\qquad U_{A}A_{1}U_{A}^{\dagger}=X_{3}\otimes Q_{A^{\prime\prime}}+X_{3}^{T}\otimes Q_{A^{\prime\prime}}^{\perp},

(146)

where
Q_{A^{\prime\prime}}
and
Q_{A^{\prime\prime}}^{\perp}
are orthogonal projections
such that
Q_{A^{\prime\prime}}+Q_{A^{\prime\prime}}^{\perp}=\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime\prime}}
.

Let us stress here that in Ref.
Kaniewski et al. 2019
an analogous characterization was also given to the third observable of Alice and all three observables of Bob. However, since they are not used in the considerations that follow, we do not present their explicit forms.

Let us now consider a device-independent scenario with Alice and
Bob, having access to untrusted measuring devices in their laboratories, and Charlie possessing a preparation device
\mathcal{P}
that prepares two different, in general mixed states
\rho_{AB}^{i}

(i=1,2)
that are distributed to Alice and Bob; we will denote purifications of these states by
|\psi_{ABE}^{i}\rangle
.
Alice’s and Bob’s measuring devices perform now, respectively, three and five measurements, which might in general be arbitrary POVM’s; slightly abusing the
notation we denote them
A_{x}

(x=0,1,2)
and
B_{y}

(y=0,\ldots,4)
.

Our scheme consists of two steps. In the first one, Alice and Bob consider the statistics corresponding to the preparation
|\psi_{ABE}^{1}\rangle
and the measurements
A_{x},B_{y}

(x,y=0,1,2)
.
If these correlations maximally violate the Bell inequality (
144
), Alice can deduce through Fact
1
that all her measurements are projective, meaning that
A_{x}

(x=0,1,2)
are quantum observables, and that they are of the form

\displaystyle A_{0}=U_{A}^{\dagger}(Z_{3}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime\prime}})\,U_{A},\qquad A_{1}=U_{A}^{\dagger}(X_{3}\otimes Q_{A^{\prime\prime}}+X_{3}^{T}\otimes Q_{A^{\prime\prime}}^{\perp})\,U_{A},

(147)

where the auxiliary Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{A^{\prime\prime}}
as well as the unitary
U_{A}

remain unknown; we only know that these two objects exist and that
\mathcal{H}_{A^{\prime\prime}}
is finite-dimensional. In this sense, maximal violation of (
144
) provides only a partial characterization of Alices observables, which, nevertheless, is sufficient for the second part of our scheme which exploits the steering inequalities introduced in the present work. In fact, one can check that for this type of observables on Alice’s side our steering inequalities (
22
) remain nontrivial, i.e.,
\beta_{L}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})<\beta_{Q}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
for any choice of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
such that
\alpha_{i}>0
. This is a consequence of the fact that they are constructed from
X_{3}
and
Z_{3}
operators.

Next, the correlations corresponding to the second preparation
|\psi_{ABE}^{2}\rangle
and the measurements
A_{0},A_{1}
(that have just been certified to be of the form (
147

B_{3},B_{4}
are considered along with the steering inequality (
22
) to verify that the second state prepared by the device
\mathcal{P}
is, up to certain equivalences,
|\psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\rangle
. Specifically, the maximal violation of (
22
) by

A_{0}
and
A_{1}
given in (
147
) and the other two measurements of Bob,

B_{3}
and
B_{4}
, implies that: (i)
\mathcal{H}_{B}=\mathbbm{C}^{3}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B^{\prime\prime}}
for some finite-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{B^{\prime\prime}}
; (ii) both
B_{3}
and
B_{4}
are quantum observables; (iii) there exists a unitary on Bob’s Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{B}

for some state

)
with Alice’s observables of the following form

\displaystyle A_{0}^{\prime}=Z_{3}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime\prime}},\qquad A_{1}^{\prime}=X_{3}\otimes Q_{A^{\prime\prime}}+X_{3}^{T}\otimes Q_{A^{\prime\prime}}^{\perp}.

(149)

U_{A}
,
one observes that the second Alice’s observable is a combination of

E_{i}E_{i}^{\dagger}\leq\mathbbm{1}
and

E_{i}^{\prime}E_{i}^{\prime\dagger}\leq\mathbbm{1}
.

To prove this statement we consider a general block form of
B_{1|i}
in terms of
the subspace
V_{1}
and its orthocomplement,

B_{1|i}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\widetilde{B}_{i}&C_{i}\\
D_{i}&E_{i}\end{array}\right),

(161)

where
\widetilde{B}_{i}
are unitary on
V_{i}
and
C_{i}
,
D_{i}
and
E_{i}
are some blocks.
Using the fact that
B_{1|i}B_{1|i}^{\dagger}\leq\mathbbm{1}
, we arrive at the following relation

B_{1|i}B_{1|i}^{\dagger}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbbm{1}+C_{i}C_{i}^{\dagger}&\widetilde{B}_{i}D_{i}^{\dagger}+C_{i}E_{i}^{\dagger}\\
D_{i}\widetilde{B}_{i}^{\dagger}+E_{i}C_{i}^{\dagger}&D_{i}D_{i}^{\dagger}+E_{i}E_{i}^{\dagger}\end{array}\right)\leq\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbbm{1}&0\\
0&\mathbbm{1}\end{array}\right),

(162)

which implies in particular that
\mathbbm{1}+C_{i}C_{i}^{\dagger}\leq\mathbbm{1}
. As a result
C=0
. Similarly, using the fact that
B_{1|i}^{\dagger}B_{1|i}\leq\mathbbm{1}
(cf. Appendix
A
) we obtain
D=0
, and finally
E_{i}E_{i}^{\dagger}\leq\mathbbm{1}
as well as
E_{i}^{\dagger}E_{i}\leq\mathbbm{1}
. In analogous manner one also derives (
160
).

Using then the block forms (
159
) and (
160
), one immediately realizes
that

[B_{i},R_{1}]=[B_{i},R_{2}]=0.

(163)

On the other hand, following the reasoning of
the proof of Theorem
2
, one realizes that
the projected observables
\widetilde{B}_{i}
and
\widetilde{B}_{i}^{\prime}

fulfil the following identities,

\widetilde{B}_{0}\widetilde{B}_{1}=\omega^{2}\widetilde{B}_{1}\widetilde{B}_{0}

(164)

and

\widetilde{B}^{\prime}_{0}\widetilde{B}^{\prime}_{1}=\omega\widetilde{B}^{\prime}_{1}\widetilde{B}^{\prime}_{0},

(165)

which by virtue of the commutation relations (
163
)
can be rewritten as

B_{0}B_{1}R_{1}=\omega^{2}B_{1}B_{0}R_{1}

(166)

and

B_{0}B_{1}R_{2}=\omega B_{1}B_{0}R_{2}.

(167)

Now, we conjugate the second equation and apply it to the first one. This leads us to

R_{2}B_{0}^{\dagger}B_{1}^{\dagger}B_{1}B_{0}R_{1}=\omega R_{2}B_{0}^{\dagger}B_{1}^{\dagger}B_{1}B_{0}R_{1},

(168)

which due to the fact that both
B_{i}
are unitary implies that
R_{2}R_{1}=\omega R_{2}R_{1}

meaning that
R_{2}R_{1}=0
. Thus, both subspaces
V_{i}
are orthogonal; in fact,
\mathcal{H}_{B}=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
. Consequently, since both unitary operations
V_{B}^{i}

act on orthogonal subspaces they can be used to construct a single unitary operation

U_{B}=V_{B}^{1}\oplus V_{B}^{2}
for which, taking into account Eqs. (
156
) and (
157
)
and Eq. (
150
), one has

(\mathbbm{1}_{A}\otimes U_{B})|\psi_{ABE}^{\prime}\rangle=(\alpha_{0}|00\rangle+\alpha_{1}|11\rangle+\alpha_{2}|22\rangle)\otimes|\eta\rangle_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}E},

(169)

where
|\eta\rangle_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}E}=\alpha|\xi_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}E}^{1}\rangle+\beta|\xi_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}E}^{2}\rangle
.
Taking into account the fact that
|\psi_{ABE}^{\prime}\rangle=(U_{A}\otimes\mathbbm{1})|\psi_{ABE}^{2}\rangle
, we eventually arrive at (
148
).

Abstract
. We study the problem of classifying the lines of the projective 3 3 -space P ​ G ​ ( 3 , q ) PG(3,q) over a finite field 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} into orbits of the group G = P ​ G ​ L 2 ​ ( q ) G=PGL_{2}(q) of linear symmetries of the twisted cubic C C . A generic line neither intersects C C nor lies in any of its osculating planes. While the non-generic lines have been classified into G G -orbits in literature, it has been an open problem to classify the generic lines into G G -orbits. For a general field F F of characteristic different from 3 3 , the twisted cubic determines a symplectic polarity on ℙ 3 \mathbb{P}^{3} . In the Klein representation of lines of ℙ 3 \mathbb{P}^{3} , the tangent lines of C C are represented by a degree 4 4 rational normal curve in a hyperplane ℋ \mathcal{H} of the second exterior power ℙ 5 \mathbb{P}^{5} of ℙ 3 \mathbb{P}^{3} . Atiyah [ 1 ] studied the lines of ℙ 3 \mathbb{P}^{3} with respect to C C , in terms of the geometries of these two curves. We show that ℋ \mathcal{H} can be identified in a P ​ G ​ L 2 PGL_{2} -equivariant way with the space of binary quartic forms over F F , and that pairs of polar dual lines of ℙ 3 \mathbb{P}^{3} correspond to binary quartic forms whose apolar invariant is a square. We first solve the open problem of classifying binary quartic forms over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} into G G -orbits, and then use it to solve the main problem.

Lemma 4.1
.

The homomorphism

and

, and

to the involution

Lemma 4.2
.

The function
\jmath(\lambda)
gives a bijection between the image
\jmath(F\setminus\{0,1\})
and the set of

. Each of these

The cubic resolvent

of

is defined as follows. We assume

Lemma 4.4

\det(g)^{-4}\textstyle\prod_{i=1}^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{2}(t_{i}s_{j}^{\prime}-s_{i}t_{j}^{\prime})

Let

and

for some

. Clearly, Res

. We have

In this section, we address the questions of when the
GL_{2}(F)
-modules
D_{m}V,\text{Sym}^{m}V
and
\text{Sym}^{m}(V^{*})
are i) irreducible, and ii) isomorphic.
We work over an arbitrary field
F
but for one assumption:

Assumption 7.1
.

m+2\leq|F|
in case
F
is finite.

Theorem 7.2
.

The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

The binomial coefficients
\binom{m}{0},\dots,\binom{m}{m}
are all nonzero in
F
.

(2)

Either char
(F)=0
or
\text{char}(F)=p
and
\ell\leq p
for every prime divisor
\ell
of
(m+1)
.

(3)

The intersection of all osculating hyperplanes of
C_{m}(F)
is empty.

(4)

D_{m}V
is an irreducible representation of
GL_{2}(F)
.

(5)

The spaces
D_{m}V,\text{Sym}^{m}V
and
\text{Sym}^{m}(V^{*})\otimes(\otimes^{m}\wedge^{2}V)
are isomorphic as
GL_{2}(F)
-modules.

We first show the equivalence of (1), (2) and (3). Let
A_{m}
be the matrix in (
4
) and let
T_{m}
be the matrix

(56)

T_{m}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\binom{m}{0}&&&\\
&\binom{m}{1}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&\binom{m}{m}\end{smallmatrix}\right),\qquad A_{m}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}&&&&\binom{m}{0}\\
&&&-\binom{m}{1}&\\
&&\iddots&&\\
&(-1)^{m-1}\binom{m}{m-1}&&&\\
(-1)^{m}\binom{m}{m}&&&&\end{smallmatrix}\right).

We recall from Definition
2.1
the bilinear form
\Omega_{m}
on
D_{m}V
whose matrix with respect to the basis
e_{1}^{[m+1-i]}e_{2}^{[i-1]},i=1\dots m+1
of
D_{m}V
is
A_{m}
.
The equivalence of the first two conditions is obvious if char
(F)=0
. In case char
(F)=p
, we use the identity

\text{lcm}\{\tbinom{m}{0},\dots,\tbinom{m}{m}\}=\frac{\text{lcm}\{1,\dots,m+1\}}{m+1},

to show that
\tbinom{m}{0},\dots,\tbinom{m}{m}
are all relatively prime to
p
if and only if

\ell\leq p
for every prime divisor
\ell
of
(m+1)
. The osculating hyperplane
\mathcal{O}_{m-1}(P)
to

at

(m+1)
-points on

.

We now show the equivalence of (5) and (1):
It follows from Proposition

-modules.

&\lambda\end{smallmatrix}\right)

Proposition 7.3

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{GL_{2}(F)}\left(D_{m}V,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}(V^{*})\otimes(\otimes^{m}\wedge^{2}V)\right)
is the one-dimensional space generated by

.

(2)

There is a

-equivariant linear isomorphism
\mathbb{P}(D_{m}V)\to\mathbb{P}(\text{Sym}^{m}(V^{*}))

are all nonzero in

is the unique such isomorphism.

(3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{GL_{2}(F)}\left(D_{m}V,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}V\right)
is the one-dimensional space generated by
T_{m}
.

(4)

There is a
PGL_{2}(F)
-equivariant linear isomorphism
\mathbb{P}(D_{m}V)\to\mathbb{P}(\text{Sym}^{m}V)
if and only if
\binom{m}{0},\dots,\binom{m}{m}
are all nonzero in

. In this case,
T_{m}
is the unique such isomorphism.

Proof.

Proof of (1): Let
A\in\text{Hom}_{GL_{2}(F)}\left(D_{m}V,\text{Sym}^{m}(V^{*})\otimes(\otimes^{m}\wedge^{2}V)\right)
, i.e.

(g^{[m]})^{t}Ag^{[m]}=\text{det}(g)^{m}A,\quad\text{ for all $g\in GL(V)$}.

Taking
g
to be represented by the matrix
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\\
&\lambda\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, we note that
g^{[m]}
is represented by the matrix

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&&&\\
&\lambda&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&\lambda^{m}\end{smallmatrix}\right).

Therefore,
(\lambda^{i+j-2}-\lambda^{m})A_{ij}=0
. Suppose
i+j\neq m+2
and
A_{ij}\neq 0
, then we must have
\lambda^{m+2-i-j}=1
for all
\lambda\in F^{\times}
.
If
F
is infinite, this is impossible. If
|F|=q
is finite, then by Assumption
7.1
we have
|m+2-i-j|\leq m\leq q-2
which implies that
(q-1)
does not divide
(m+2-i-j)
and hence the condition that
\lambda^{m+2-i-j}=1
for all
\lambda\in F^{\times}
is impossible. Therefore, we conclude that all non-zero entries of
A
are on the anti-diagonal. Let

A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}&&&a_{1}\\
&&a_{2}&\\
&\iddots&&\\
a_{m+1}&&&\end{smallmatrix}\right).

Taking
g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}&1\\
1&\end{smallmatrix}\right)
we get
a_{i}=a_{m+2-i}(-1)^{m}
.
Next, taking
g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, we note that
g^{[m]}
is represented by the lower triangular matrix whose
ij
-th entries are
\binom{i-1}{j-1}
and hence:

a_{i}\tbinom{m+1-i}{j-1}=\tbinom{m+1-j}{i-1}(-1)^{m+2-j-i}a_{m+2-j}=\tbinom{m+1-j}{i-1}(-1)^{i+j}a_{j}.

Putting
i=1
, we get

a_{j}=(-1)^{j-1}\tbinom{m}{j-1}a_{1},

i.e.
A=a_{1}A_{m}
as required.

Proof of (3): Let
T\in\text{Hom}_{GL_{2}(F)}\left(D_{m}V,\text{Sym}^{m}V\right)
, i.e.

Tg^{[m]}=g^{m}T,\quad\text{ for all $g\in GL(V)$}.

Taking
g
to be represented by the matrix
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\\
&\lambda\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, we note that both
g^{[m]}
and
g^{m}
are represented by the matrix

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&&&\\
&\lambda&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&\lambda^{m}\end{smallmatrix}\right).

Therefore,
(\lambda^{i-j}-1)T_{ij}=0
. Suppose
i\neq j
and
T_{ij}\neq 0
, then we must have
\lambda^{i-j}=1
for all
\lambda\in F^{\times}
.
If
F
is infinite, this is impossible. If
|F|=q
is finite, then by Assumption
7.1
, we have
|i-j|\leq m\leq q-2
and hence
(q-1)
does not divide
|i-j|
.
Therefore, we conclude that
T
is a diagonal matrix. Let
t_{i}
denote
T_{ii}
. Taking
g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}&1\\
1&\end{smallmatrix}\right)
we get
t_{i}=t_{m+2-i}
. Next, taking
g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, we note that
(g^{[m]})_{ij}=\binom{i-1}{j-1}
as above and
(g^{m})_{ij}=\binom{m+1-j}{m+1-i}
we get:

a_{i}\tbinom{i-1}{j-1}=\tbinom{m+1-j}{m+1-i}a_{j},

and hence
a_{i}=\binom{m}{i-1}a_{1}
, i.e.
T=a_{1}T_{m}
.

Proofs of (2) and (4): If
\binom{m}{0},\dots,\binom{m}{m}
are all nonzero in
F
, then the matrices
A_{m}
and
T_{m}
are invertible and represent
PGL_{2}(F)
-equivariant maps

\mathbb{P}(D_{m}V)\to\mathbb{P}(\text{Sym}^{m}(V^{*}))
and
\mathbb{P}(D_{m}V)\to\mathbb{P}(\text{Sym}^{m}V)
respectively.
We now treat the converse of assertions (2) and (4). If the matrix
A\in GL_{m+1}(F)
represents a
PGL_{2}(F)
-equivariant linear isomorphism
\mathbb{P}(D_{m}V)\to\mathbb{P}(\text{Sym}^{m}(V^{*}))
, then there is a function
\chi:GL_{2}(F)\to F^{\times}
such that then we have
(g^{[m]})^{t}Ag^{[m]}=\chi(g)A
for each
g\in GL_{2}(F)
.
Taking indices
ij
such that
A_{ij}\neq 0
, and comparing the left and right sides of

(g^{[m]})^{t}Ag^{[m]}=\chi_{g}A
, it follows that there is a homogeneous polynomial
\tilde{\chi}(a,b,c,d)
of degree
2m
in the polynomial ring
F[a,b,c,d]
such that
\chi(g)=\tilde{\chi}(g)
. It is also clear that
\chi(g)
is a homomorphism. We recall that any rational homomorphism
GL_{2}(F)\to F^{\times}
is of the form
\chi_{r}(g)=\det(g)^{s}
for
s\in\mathbb{Z}
. If
|F|=q
is finite then
s\in\mathbb{Z}/(q-1)\mathbb{Z}
i.e.
0\leq s\leq q-2
.
Similarly, assertion (4) is equivalent to the existence of
T\in GL_{m+1}(F)
for which
Tg^{[m]}=\chi^{\prime}(g)g^{m}T
for some rational homomorphism
\chi^{\prime}:GL_{2}(F)\to F^{\times}
. Let
\chi(g)=\chi_{r}(g)
and let
\chi^{\prime}(g)=\chi_{r^{\prime}}(g)
. By taking the determinant of
(g^{[m]})^{t}Ag^{[m]}=\det(g)^{r}A

and
Tg^{[m]}=\det(g)^{r^{\prime}}g^{m}T
and using the fact that det
(g^{[m]})=\det(g^{m})=\det(g)^{m(m+1)/2}
we get
\det(g)^{(m+1)(m-r)}=1
and
\det(g)^{r^{\prime}(m+1)}=1
. If
F
is infinite, this immediately forces
r=m
and
r^{\prime}=0
. At this stage, we have
(g^{[m]})^{t}Ag^{[m]}=\det(g)^{m}A
and
Tg^{[m]}=g^{m}T
, and we appeal to parts 1) and 3) to conclude that
A=A_{m}
and
T=T_{m}
(up to a scalar multiple) respectively. If
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}
is finite, we will again show that
r=m
and
r^{\prime}=0
(and hence
conclude
A=A_{m}
and
T=T_{m}
), but it requires a more careful analysis.

We take
0\leq r,r^{\prime}\leq q-2
. We first consider assertion 2). Taking
g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, we have
\chi(g)=(ad)^{r}
, and the
(ij)
-th entry of the equation
(g^{[m]})^{t}Ag^{[m]}=\det(g)^{r}A
reads
(a^{2m-(i+j-2)}d^{(i+j-2)}-(ad)^{r})T_{ij}=0
. In other words, if
T_{ij}\neq 0
we have:

a^{2m-(i+j-2)-r}d^{i+j-2-r}=1\quad\text{ for all $a,d\in F^{\times}$}.

This in turn gives:

i+j-2\equiv r\mod(q-1),\qquad 2(m-r)\equiv 0\mod(q-1).

If
q
is even, the above conditions are equivalent to
m=r
(and
i+j-2=m
) as desired. If
q
is odd, the condition
2(m-r)\equiv 0\mod(q-1)
together with the assumption that
m\leq q-2
gives
r\in\{m-\tfrac{q-1}{2},m,m+\tfrac{q-1}{2}\}
. We argue that the cases
r=m\pm\tfrac{q-1}{2}
are not possible: if
r=m-\tfrac{q-1}{2}
then we have
T_{ij}\neq 0
only if
i+j-2\equiv m-\tfrac{q-1}{2}
which is equivalent to
i+j-2\in\{m-\tfrac{q-1}{2},m+\tfrac{q-1}{2}\}
. Similarly, if
r=m+\tfrac{q-1}{2}
, we again get
i+j-2\in\{m-\tfrac{q-1}{2},m+\tfrac{q-1}{2}\}
. In this case the
(q-2-m)
columns
m+2-\tfrac{q-1}{2},\dots,\tfrac{q-1}{2}
of
A
have all entries
0
which contradicts the fact that
\det(A)\neq 0
. Therefore,
m=q-2
and the
(q-1)\times(q-1)
matrix
A
is of the form

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\begin{smallmatrix}&&\alpha_{\frac{q-1}{2}}\\
&\iddots&\\
\alpha_{1}&&\end{smallmatrix}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&\begin{smallmatrix}&&\beta_{\frac{q-1}{2}}\\
&\iddots&\\
\beta_{1}&&\end{smallmatrix}\end{array}\right).

Next we take
g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
for which
\det(g)
and hence
\chi(g)=1
. We note that
g^{[m]}
is represented by the lower triangular matrix whose
ij
-th entries are
\binom{i-1}{j-1}
and hence, the
(m+1,1)
-th entry of the equation
(g^{[m]})^{t}Ag^{[m]}=\chi(g)A
reads
\beta_{1}=0
, which contradicts
\det(A)\neq 0
.

For assertion 4), again taking
g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, we have
\chi(g)=(ad)^{r^{\prime}}
, and the
(ij)
-th entry of the equation
Tg^{[m]}=\det(g)^{r^{\prime}}g^{m}T
reads
(a^{m-j+1)}d^{j-1}-a^{m-i+1+r^{\prime}}d^{i-1+r^{\prime}})T_{ij}=0
. In other words, if
T_{ij}\neq 0
we have:

a^{i-j-r^{\prime}}d^{j-i-r^{\prime}}=1\quad\text{ for all $a,d\in F^{\times}$}.

If
q
is even, this is equivalent to
r^{\prime}\equiv 0\mod(q-1)
(and
j-i\equiv 0\mod(q-1)
) as required.
If
q
is odd, we get

j-i\equiv r^{\prime}\equiv 0\mod(q-1)/2.

We will show that the case
r^{\prime}\equiv(q-1)/2\mod(q-1)
is not possible: indeed, if
r^{\prime}=(q-1)/2
, then the nonzero entries of
T_{ij}
occur only if
|i-j|=(q-1)/2
. The
(q-2-m)
columns with indices
m+2-\tfrac{q-1}{2},\dots,\tfrac{q-1}{2}
have all entries
0
which contradicts
\det(T)\neq 0
. Therefore, we must have
m=q-2
and hence the

(q-1)\times(q-1)
matrix
T
is of the form

T=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}0&\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\alpha_{\frac{q-1}{2}}\end{smallmatrix}\\
\hline\cr\begin{smallmatrix}\beta_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\beta_{\frac{q-1}{2}}\end{smallmatrix}&0\end{array}\right).

Next taking
g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
for which
\det(g)
and hence
\chi^{\prime}(g)=1
. We note that

(g^{[m]})_{ij}=\tbinom{i-1}{j-1}
and
(g^{m})_{ij}=\tbinom{q-1-j}{q-1-i}
. The
(1,1)
-entry of the equation
Tg^{[m]}=\chi^{\prime}(g)g^{m}T
reads
\alpha_{1}=0
contradicting the fact that
\det(T)\neq 0
.
∎

Remark
7.4
.

We recall the standard fact that for a finite-dimensional vector space
W
over an arbitrary field
F
, unlike the case with Sym
{}^{m}(W)
, there is a
GL(W)
equivariant isomorphism between alternating power vector spaces
\wedge^{m}W
and
(\wedge^{m}W^{*})^{*}
: for
w_{1},\dots,w_{m}\in W
and
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{m}\in W^{*}
the determinant of the
m\times m
matrix whose
ij
-th entry is
\phi_{i}(w_{j})
gives a nondegenerate
GL(W)
-invariant bilinear map
\wedge^{m}W\times\wedge^{m}W^{*}\to F
, or equivalently a
GL(W)
-equivariant linear isomorphism between
\wedge^{m}W
and
(\wedge^{m}W^{*})^{*}
.

Continuity of the extremal decomposition of the free state for finite-spin models on Cayley trees
Loren Coquille, Christof Külske, Arnaud Le Ny

Abstract
We prove the continuity of the extremal decomposition measure of the free state of low temperature Potts models, and more generally of ferromagnetic finite-spin models, on a regular tree, including general clock models. The decomposition is supported on uncountably many inhomogeneous extremal states, that we call glassy states . The method of proof provides explicit concentration bounds on branch overlaps , which play the role of an order parameter for typical extremals. The result extends to the counterpart of the free state (called central state ) in a wide range of models which have no symmetry, allowing also the presence of sufficiently small field terms. Our work shows in particular that the decomposition of central states into uncountably many glassy states in finite-spin models on trees at low temperature is a generic phenomenon, and does not rely on symmetries of the Hamiltonian.

60K35, 82B20, 82B26

Gibbs measures, DLR formalism, spin models on trees, disordered systems, extremal decompositions, Markov fields, Markov chains.

1
Introduction

Models of statistical mechanics supported on trees and more generally non-amenable graphs,
are known to show rather interesting behavior with multiple critical values, which displays
complex phenomenology and challenges for a rigorous understanding.

The free state of the Ising model in zero external magnetic field on a regular tree, obtained as the infinite-volume Gibbs measure with free (open) boundary conditions, is such an example.
This homogenous (tree-automorphism invariant) Gibbs measure indeed displays a complex structure at low temperature, which took a long history to understand.

First recall
[
5
]
that the free state
\mu^{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}_{\beta}
of the Ising model on the lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
at inverse temperature
\beta

decomposes into the symmetric convex combination

\begin{split}\mu^{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}_{\beta}=(\mu^{+,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}_{\beta}+\mu^{-,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}_{\beta})/2,\quad\text{for all }\beta>0.\end{split}

(1)

where
\mu^{+,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}_{\beta}
(respectively
\mu^{-,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}_{\beta}
) is obtained as the infinite-volume Gibbs measure with with
+
(respectively
-
)-boundary conditions.
The extremal decomposition of this form becomes non-trivial
below the critical temperature of the model, i.e.
for all
\beta>\beta_{c}^{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}:=\inf\{\beta>0,\mu^{+,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}_{\beta}\neq\mu^{-,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}_{\beta}\}
. By abstract Gibbs theory
[
19
]
for any spin-model on countable graphs, a unique decomposition into extremal (pure, non
-
decomposable)
infinite-volume Gibbs measures always holds.

On the tree
\mathcal{T}^{d}
with
d+1>2
nearest neighbors however,

\begin{split}\mu^{\mathcal{T}^{d}}_{\beta}\neq(\mu^{+,\mathcal{T}^{d}}_{\beta}+\mu^{-,\mathcal{T}^{d}}_{\beta})/2\end{split}

(2)

in the whole non-uniqueness region
d(\tanh\beta)>1
corresponding to

\beta>\beta_{c}^{\mathcal{T}^{d}}

[
29
]
.
Moreover, the free state is non-extremal in the even lower temperature region
d(\tanh\beta)^{2}>1
, which
was shown in the sequence of works
[
20
,
2
,
4
,
22
,
21
]
.

Note that Ising models on a branching plane
\mathcal{T}^{d}\times{\mathbb{Z}}
were investigated in
[
28
]
and display three parameter regimes corresponding to: (i) uniqueness, (ii) non-uniqueness with tree-like structure (
2
) and (iii) non-uniqueness with lattice-like structure (
1
).

Understanding the extremal decomposition of the Ising free state on a tree is known
to be particularly complex as it involves uncountably many Gibbs measures on which the extremal
decomposition measure is supported,
see the much more recent work of
[
15
]
. These states have a broken translational symmetry,
and show characteristics of glassy behavior.

To the best of our knowledge there are no result concerning the extremal decomposition
measure of the Ising free state in an external field, nor of the one of the Potts
free state without a field or in a field, and how sensitive the decomposition measure depends on particularities
and symmetries of the model.
Our purpose in the present work is to investigate this complexity in the general framework of
ferromagnetic finite-spin models with nearest neighbor interactions.

In the paper
[
10
]
, we prove the existence of an uncountable family of extremal inhomogeneous states for a large class of models on regular trees, including finite-spin models. More precisely, we give an explicit description of a family of local ground state configurations
\Omega_{GS}
, which have a sparse enough set of broken bonds, and which give rise to extremal low temperature states, in the sense that for any
\omega\in\Omega_{GS}
, the weak limit of finite volume measures with boundary condition
\omega
exists and is an extremal state. Note that many other extremal states exist, see discussions in
[
10
,
24
,
17
]
and references therein.

The purpose of the current paper is to show that uncountably many states, which can be seen as perturbations of the above inhomogeneous states, enter the extremal decomposition of the free state. The result extends to the counterpart of the free state (called
central state
), in a wide range of asymmetric models
e.g.
where small inhomogenous field terms are added.
Our approach has been inspired by the work of Gandolfo, Maes, Ruiz and Shlosman
[
15
]
, who discuss these questions in the case of the Ising model in zero external field. Our approach
is nevertheless essentially different as we develop a new method based on
concentration
of branch overlaps
, see below.

In the Ising case, soon after the characterization of the homogeneous Gibbs measures as Markov chains by Spitzer
[
31
]
, Higuchi
[
20
]
proved the non-extremality of the ’third Markov chain’ (the free state) for
d(\tanh\beta)^{2}>1
. These works were complemented by Bleher
et al.

[
4
]
and Ioffe
[
22
]
who proved extremality of the free state for
d(\tanh\beta)^{2}<1
. In the latter, and already in
[
9
,
13
]
, the fluctuations leading to non extremality have been related to fluctuations of the spin-glass order paramater on trees used as a discriminating tail observable, the so-called
Edwards-Anderson parameter
, which is nothing but the variance of the root magnetization.
Note that non-extremality of the free state was also proved by Markov chains Kesten-Stigum techniques in
[
27
]
, who also conjectured independently the extremality at intermediate temperatures.

For general finite spin models, including
q
-clock models, we introduce a generalised version of the Edwards-Anderson parameter measuring the non-degeneracy of the law of the root-spin when the boundary condition at infinity is sampled according to the free/central state
\mu
:

q_{EA}=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{a\in\{1,\ldots,q\}}{\rm Var}_{\mu}\bigl({\pi({\sigma_{0}=a}|\cdot})\bigr)

were the
\pi
-kernel
\pi(\cdot|\omega)
is the Gibbs-measure with boundary-condition
\omega

at infinity, which exists and is extremal for
\mu
-almost every
\omega
(for precise definitions, see Section
2.2
below).
We prove
q_{EA}
to be strictly positive at large enough
\beta
(see Theorem
3.2
), implying that the free/central state is not extremal at low temperature.
From an information-theoretic view, this means the model is reconstruction-solvable
[
26
]

as the
\pi
-kernel is able to restore information which was sent from the root to infinity
by means of the measure
\mu
.

Our main results concern the extremal decomposition of the free/central state
\mu
.
The DLR equations imply

\displaystyle\mu(\cdot)=\int_{\Omega}\pi(\cdot|\omega)d\mu(\omega)=\int_{{{\rm ex}\mathcal{G}(\gamma)}}\nu(\cdot){\rm d}\alpha_{\mu}(\nu)

where
\alpha_{\mu}
is the
extremal decomposition measure
of the free state.
In Theorem

, we prove that if we sample independently at random two boundary conditions
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
under the free state
\mu
, then the states
\pi(\cdot|\omega)
and
\pi(\cdot|\omega^{\prime})
are almost surely singular with respect to each other:

\displaystyle\mu\otimes\mu(\{(\omega,\omega^{\prime}):\pi(\cdot|\omega)\perp\pi(\cdot|\omega^{\prime})\})=1.

which implies that the measure
\alpha_{\mu}
has no atom. The extremal decomposition of the free state
\mu
is thus supported on an uncountable family of extremal states.

To this purpose, we introduce the following tail measurable observable

\begin{split}&\underline{\phi}^{\omega}=\liminf_{n\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|}\sum_{v\in\Lambda_{n}}1_{\sigma_{v}=\omega_{v}}\cr\end{split}

which we call
branch overlap
, and which measures how much a configuration
\sigma
agrees with
\omega
on a (sparse enough) sequence of vertices lying on a branch of the tree.
We prove that
\underline{\phi}^{\omega}
has different expectations under
\pi(\cdot|\omega)
and
\pi(\cdot|\omega^{\prime})
, which is enough to imply almost sure singularity.
More precisely, we prove that
\underline{\phi}^{\omega}
has an expectation tending to 1 under
\pi(\cdot|\omega)
, and tending to
\sum_{a}\mu(\sigma_{0}=a)^{2}
(which equals
1/q
in the case of the
q

\pi(\cdot|\omega^{\prime})
, as
\beta\to\infty
, with explicit bounds. This can be viewed as a quantitative statement of some ”boundary condition resampling chaos”.

The paper is organized as follows:
In Section
2
we first recall the necessary backgrounds on Gibbs measures, extremal decompositions and Markov chains on Cayley trees. We then state our results in Section
3
and provide the proofs in Section
4
.

2.1
Models

.
We are interested in probability measures on the product space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})=(\Omega_{0}^{V},\mathcal{E}^{\otimes V})
.
For any subset

{\sigma_{\Lambda}(\omega)}=\omega_{\Lambda}=(\omega_{v})_{v\in\Lambda}

If

\Lambda\Subset V
. We write
\partial\Lambda=\{v\in V{\backslash\Lambda}:\exists w\in\Lambda,v\sim w\}

We also denote by

\sigma
-algebra generated by the variables
\sigma_{\Lambda}=(\sigma_{v})_{v\in\Lambda}
, or equivalently by the cylinders denoted
\{\sigma_{{\Lambda}}=\omega_{\Lambda}\}
.
Another important

-algebra will be the tail
\sigma
-algebra of asymptotic events
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}=\cap_{\Lambda\Subset V}\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda^{c}}
where
the intersection runs over finite subsets.

We introduce an interaction potential
\Phi
and consider equilibrium states to be Gibbs measures built with the DLR framework, see e.g.
[
19
]
: they are the probability measures
\mu
consistent with the Gibbsian specification
\gamma^{\Phi}
in the sense that a version of their conditional probabilities w.r.t. the outside of any finite set

\displaystyle\forall\Lambda\Subset V,\;\forall\omega_{\Lambda}\in\Omega_{\Lambda},\;\mu[\sigma_{\Lambda}=\omega_{\Lambda}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda^{c}}](\cdot)=\gamma_{\Lambda}^{\Phi}(\omega_{\Lambda}\mid\cdot),\;\mu-a.s.

(3)

where the elements of the Gibbs specification

are the probability kernels

to

defined for all

as

\displaystyle\gamma_{\Lambda}^{\Phi}(\omega_{\Lambda}\mid\omega_{\Lambda^{c}})=\frac{1}{Z_{\Lambda}^{\omega}}e^{-\beta H_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda}\omega_{\Lambda^{c}})}.

(4)

The partition function

is the normalization constant for a boundary condition
\omega_{\Lambda^{c}}
, at finite volume
\Lambda
, and the corresponding Hamiltonian
H_{\Lambda}^{\Phi}
with b.c.
\omega_{\Lambda^{c}}
is provided by

\displaystyle H_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda}\omega_{\Lambda^{c}})=\sum_{A\cap\Lambda\neq\emptyset}\Phi_{A}(\omega_{\Lambda}\omega_{\Lambda^{c}})

(5)

where
\omega_{\Lambda}\omega_{\Lambda^{c}}
denotes the concatenation of
\omega_{\Lambda}
and
\omega_{\Lambda^{c}}
.
We write

for the Hamiltonian with free boundary conditions:

H_{\Lambda}(\omega)=\sum_{A\subset\Lambda}\Phi_{A}(\omega_{\Lambda}).

(6)

and define the

to be the Gibbs measure obtained by taking the infinite-volume limit with free boundary conditions.
The limit exists in particular, when all finite volume measures are consistent, which is e.g. the case
for the Ising model or the Potts model in zero external
field. In general, in a non-zero field one could think to consider sub-sequences with free
boundary conditions,
but it is necessary to put suitable non-deterministic
boundary conditions generalizing the free ones,
for which we will need the theory of boundary laws, see
Section
4.1
and in particular Theorem
4.1
.

Throughout the paper we consider ferromagnetic nearest-neighbor (n.n.) potentials of the form

\displaystyle\Phi_{\{v,w\}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{q}u_{i,j}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{(\omega_{v},\omega_{w})=(i,j)\}}{\cdot}{\mathbf{1}_{v\sim w}}

\displaystyle\text{where }u_{i,i}

\displaystyle=0\text{ and }0<u:=\min_{i\neq j}u_{i,j}\leq\max_{i,j}u_{i,j}=:U

(7)

The strict positivity of the lower bound expresses that homogeneous spin
configurations are energetically favored by the pair interaction, while the energies
of excitations
w.r.t. different ground states may depend in general on the ground state.
The latter includes the
q
-state Potts model (the Ising model corresponding to

), for which

\Phi^{\text{Potts}}_{\{v,w\}}(\omega)=\mathbf{1}_{\omega_{v}\neq\omega_{w}}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{v\sim w}.

(8)

More generally than the Potts model, we consider

q
-state

as an important sub-class. In these models the pair potential has a discrete
rotational symmetry which means that there is a positive function
\bar{u}
for which

u_{i,j}=\bar{u}(|i-j|)

(9)

where
|i-j|
is the distance between
i
and

. As the finite-volume Gibbs measures
with free boundary conditions are consistent measures in clock-models, they immediately yield a well-defined free state in infinite volume.

Finally we extend our framework from the free state in clock models
to
central states
(see the definition in Section
4.1.3
), which
are constructed as (small) deformations of a free state.
Here we do not assume the strict
discrete clock-symmetry of the interaction.
Observe that the simplest example of such a central state which is not a free state, is obtained for the low-temperature Ising model in a small field.

As our proofs
do not rely on symmetry considerations, we are indeed able to extend them to Hamiltonians of the following type

\begin{split}H(\omega)=\sum_{v\sim w}\Phi_{\{v,w\}}(\omega)+\sum_{v\in V}\Psi(\omega_{v}).\end{split}

with a pair potential

\|\Psi\|_{\infty}\leq u(d-1)/8.

(11)

We moreover need a suitable assumption on the pair potential and the single-site potential imposed by the ’lazyness’ condition (see Definition

Choquet simplex of DLR measures

We denote by

To briefly formalize this, denote

Definition 2.1

(see

)

Any

1.

Tail-triviality:

A\in\mathcal{F}_{\infty}\Longrightarrow\mu(A)\in\{0,1\}.

2.

\lim_{\Lambda\uparrow V}\sup_{B\in\mathcal{F}_{\Lambda^{c}}}\big|\mu(A\cap B)-\mu(A)\mu(B)\big|=0.

By consistency, for any

is a regular version

which means in particular that for any sequence

\forall\mu\in\mathcal{G}(\gamma),\;\mu(\cdot)=\int_{\Omega}\gamma_{\Lambda_{n}}(\cdot|\omega)\;d\mu(\omega).

One eventually gets the starting point of the simplicial decomposition

\forall\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{+}(\Omega),\;\mu(\cdot)=\int_{\Omega}\mu[\cdot\,|\mathcal{F}_{\infty}](\omega)\;d\mu(\omega)

to be the tail-measurable kernel

\displaystyle\pi(\cdot|\omega)=\lim_{\Lambda\uparrow\mathcal{T}^{d}}\gamma_{\Lambda}(\cdot|\omega)

if the limits exists for all cylinder events (generating

),
and

Theorem 2.1

(see
[

]
)
.

The limit (

Equation (

\displaystyle\mu(\cdot)=\int\pi(\cdot|\omega)d\mu(\omega).

The

2.3
Tree-indexed Markov chains

A probability measure
\mu
on
({\mathbb{Z}}_{q})^{V}

is a homogeneous tree-indexed Markov chain if and only if it allows the following iterative construction:

1.

Sample
\sigma_{0}
at (arbitrary) root
0
according to single-site marginal of
\mu
.

2.

Sample
\sigma_{w}
via some transition matrix
P(\omega_{v},\omega_{w})

from inside to outside.

The abstract general definition of tree-indexed Markov chain which does not
assume any invariance under any tree-automorphism requires that

\mu(\sigma_{w}=\cdot|{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{past of }(v,w)})=\mu(\sigma_{w}=\cdot|{\mathcal{F}}_{v})

(20)

holds for all oriented edges
(v,w)
, and the past of an oriented edge is the set
of vertices which are closer to
v
than to
w
.

We note that in the trivial case
d=1
(that is excluded for the most part in the analysis
of this paper)
\mu
is a homogeneous Markov chain if it is shift-invariant and

reversible
. This is clear, as time-reversal corresponds to a reflection of
{\mathbb{Z}}
.
We note the important background theorem:

Theorem 2.3

(
[
19
]
Theorem 12.6)
.

If
\mu
is an extremal Gibbs measure then
\mu
is a tree-indexed
Markov chain.

The converse is not true, as the famous example of the free state
for the Ising model in zero external field shows (see Higuchi
[
20
]
, Remark p342).

In the whole article all we need to suppose is
that
\mu
is a homogeneous tree-indexed Markov chain for a Hamiltonian in the
class (
10
), with a certain lazyness property in the following sense.

Definition 2.2

(Lazyness parameter)
.

Let
\mu
be a homogeneous tree-indexed Markov chain.
Denote by
P
its transition matrix (which is then homogeneous, too) and define the corresponding maximal jump probability to be

\displaystyle p_{1}=p_{1}(\mu):=\max_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}}\sum_{j\neq i}P(i,j).

(21)

Equivalently, for all
v\sim 0
we have

\displaystyle p_{1}=\max_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}}\mu(\sigma_{v}\neq i|\sigma_{0}=i).

(22)

In the course of the proof of our main theorem in Section
3
, which asserts the continuity of the extremal
decomposition measure of a Markov chain
\mu
,
we ask for sufficient smallness
of the quantity
p_{1}(\mu)
.
This requirement means that all states are sufficiently lazy, i.e. with large probability
the chain stays in each of the states.

Below we will see that the examples of
central states

obtained by small perturbations of low temperature free states of
clock models keep the Markov chain property and the small
p_{1}
property.
The reason
is that
p_{1}
deforms smoothly under perturbations, and so
lazyness carries over from the unperturbed model.

3.1
Reconstruction bounds for central states

We have the following reconstruction statement, for
the central states in any model with Hamiltonian of the class (

Theorem 3.1

Consider any central state

in the class of models with Hamiltonian of the form (

\displaystyle\mu\Bigl(\{\omega:\pi(\sigma_{0}=a|\omega)\leq 1-\epsilon_{1}(\beta)\}\Bigl|\sigma_{0}=a\Bigr)\leq\epsilon_{2}(p_{1})

In particular, if

From a signal recontruction point of view (see

3.2
Positivity of the Edwards-Anderson parameter and non-extremality

The so-called Edwards-Anderson parameter is usually defined in spin glass models as a quantity measuring the degree of randomness of the (random) spin magnetisation at the origin (see e.g.
[
9
]
). In our context, the analogue should be a quantity measuring the degree of randomness of the probability vector

(\pi(\sigma_{0}=a|\omega))_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}}
, when
the boundary condition at infinity
\omega
is distributed according to

3.5
Structure of the proofs

To prove the concentration bound of Theorem
3.4

it is useful to adopt a quenched-disordered systems view (we refer to
[
7
]
for a general introduction to statistical mechanics of disordered systems). A configuration
\omega
drawn from the free/central state
\mu
corresponds to a boundary condition at infinity, but also plays the role of disorder.
The intuition behind is that the extremal measure
\pi(\cdot|\omega)
, which is in general inhomogeneous
and plays the role of a quenched disordered state,
mostly resembles
\omega
locally, and for
\mu
-typical
\omega
.

To make this precise, we will need contours, as introduced in
[
10
]
,
and Peierls bounds relative to the reference configuration
\omega
.
Typically
\omega
will contain a small density of broken bonds (along which
\omega
changes). However
the latter will not be uniformly sparse, as needed in
[
10
]
and
[
16
]
to ensure the excess energy estimate leading to Peierls bounds.
To treat the rare but arbitrarily large regions where the Peierls bound locally fails, we introduce a notion of ”bad site” (see Definition
4.5
), which is an essential tool.
Our good/bad site decomposition is somewhat reminiscent to that invented by Chayes-Chayes-Fröhlich in
[
8
]
, to treat lattice Ising models with i.i.d. random bonds, which are mostly but not strictly ferromagnetic.
However, we work on the tree, and in a regime where the ”disorder-measure”
\mu
is not tail-trivial, it is far from an i.i.d. disorder measure, making things more intricate.

Nevertheless, as we shall see, there is one-dimensional correlation decay, conditionally on the state of the root, along a branch of the tree.
This will be exploited to prove exponential decorrelation of bad sites in their distance, see Lemma
4.7
,
leading to concentration of thinned branch overlaps around their means, under the ”quenched measures”
\pi(\cdot|\omega)
, for
\mu
-almost every
\omega
, see Lemma
4.9
.

4.1
Markov chains on trees and boundary laws.

Being nearest neighbor (n.n.), our class of models (
10

including the extremal Gibbs measures but not equal to them,
which has a direct transcription in terms of Markov chains on trees. These are described

32

are (non-normalized) positive measures which are invariant under an interaction-dependent
non-linear map along the tree. Moreover they are closely related (but not equal) to
the invariant single-site probability measures for the associated
one-step Markov chain transition matrix, see below, and
see Georgii
[
19

for details.

In our case of a homogeneous n.n. interaction on the tree,
the specification (
4

\gamma_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda}\mid\omega_{\Lambda^{c}})=\frac{1}{Z^{\omega}_{\Lambda}}\prod_{{\{v,w\}\cap\Lambda\neq\emptyset}\atop{v\sim w}}Q(\omega_{v},\omega_{w}).

Q\mapsto p_{1}(\mu_{Q})
is a continuously differentiable function.
In particular, whenever the reference clock model
Q_{0}
has small

\displaystyle p_{1}(\mu_{Q_{0}})=\frac{\sum_{i\neq 0}Q_{0}(i)}{\|Q_{0}\|_{1}}\leq(q-1)e^{-\beta u},

(45)

this smallness carries over to
p_{1}(Q)
for
Q

in a sufficiently small neighborhood around
Q_{0}
. Specifically for the Potts model
as a reference clock model we have

p_{1}(Q_{0})=\frac{q-1}{\mathrm{e}^{\beta}+q-1}

(46)

which tends to 0 as
\beta
tends to infinity.

4.2
Contours and Excess Energy Lemma

Definition 4.3

(Contour with respect to a fixed configuration
\omega^{0}
)
.

Let
\omega^{0}\in\Omega_{0}^{V}
be a fixed reference
configuration.
A contour for the spin configuration
\omega\in\Omega_{0}^{V}
relative to
\omega^{0}

is a pair

\bar{\gamma}=(\gamma,\omega_{\gamma})

where the support

\gamma\ {\subset}\ \{v\in V:\omega_{v}\neq\omega_{v}^{0}\}
is
a connected component of the set of incorrect points for

\omega
(with respect to
\omega^{0}
), and
\omega_{\gamma}=(\omega_{v})_{v\in\gamma}
.

Given a fixed reference configuration
\omega^{0}
, we will work with a contour representation of the spin partition function
in a finite volume
\Lambda
with boundary condition equal to
\omega^{0}
, which reads

\begin{split}Z_{\Lambda}^{\omega^{0}}={\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}}\sum_{\bar{\gamma}_{1},\dots,\bar{\gamma}_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\rho[\omega^{0}](\bar{\gamma}_{i})\end{split}

(47)

where the sum is over pairwise compatible contours
\bar{\gamma}
with activities

\rho[\omega^{0}](\bar{\gamma})=\exp({-\beta(H_{\gamma\cup\partial\gamma}(\omega_{\gamma\cup\partial\gamma})-H_{\gamma\cup\partial\gamma}(\omega^{0}_{\gamma\cup\partial\gamma}))})

given in terms of the excess energy of
\omega
with respect to
\omega^{0}
.
We write
\bar{\gamma}_{1}\sim\bar{\gamma}_{2}
whenever
\bar{\gamma}_{1}
and

\bar{\gamma}_{2}
are compatible.
This representation together with the tree structure of our graph (where contours have no interior) imply the following Peierls bound:

Lemma 4.3

(Peierls bound)
.

Consider
\omega^{0}\in\Omega
and a volume
\Lambda\Subset V
. Then

\displaystyle\mu^{\omega^{0}}_{\Lambda}(\sigma_{v}\neq\omega^{0}_{v})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bar{\gamma}:\gamma\ni v}^{\Lambda}\rho[\omega^{0}](\bar{\gamma})\cdot\frac{{\sum_{n}}\sum^{\Lambda}_{\bar{\gamma}_{1},\dots,\bar{\gamma}_{n}\sim\bar{\gamma}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\rho[\omega^{0}](\bar{\gamma}_{i})}{{\sum_{n}}\sum^{\Lambda}_{\bar{\gamma}_{1},\dots,\bar{\gamma}_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\rho[\omega^{0}](\bar{\gamma}_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\bar{\gamma}:\gamma\ni v}^{\Lambda}\rho[\omega^{0}](\bar{\gamma}).

(48)

where the sum is over contours
\bar{\gamma}
in the finite volume
\Lambda
.

Definition 4.4

(Set of broken bonds)
.

We define the set of broken bonds of the configuration
\omega\in\Omega
by

D(\omega)=\{(v,w)\in E:\omega_{v}\neq\omega_{w}\}

and denote the set of edges attached to
\gamma\subset V
by

\begin{split}E(\gamma)&=\{\{x,y\}\in E,\{x,y\}\cap\gamma\neq\emptyset\}.\end{split}

(49)

Lemma 4.4

(Entropy bound, [
[
16
]
,Lemma 6])
.

We have the following upper bound on the number of connected subsets of vertices of
\mathcal{T}^{d}=(V,E)
:

\#\{\gamma\subset V:\gamma\text{ connected ,}\gamma\ni 0,|\gamma|=\ell\}\leq(d+1)^{2(\ell-1)}.

Lemma 4.5

(Excess Energy Lemma [
[
10
]
, Lemma 2])
.

Let
\mu
be a central state of any model with Hamiltonian in the class (
10
) fulfilling the bounds (
2.1
) and (
11
).
Let
\bar{\gamma}=(\omega^{\prime}_{\gamma},\gamma)

be a contour relative to the fixed configuration

\omega
.
Denote
\omega^{\prime}=(\omega^{\prime}_{\gamma}\omega_{\gamma^{c}})

the corresponding excited spin configuration.
Then the excess energy satisfies

\begin{split}H(\omega^{\prime})&-H(\omega)\geq\\
&(d-1)u|\gamma|-(U+u)|D(\omega)\cap E(\gamma)|+\sum_{v\in\gamma}(\Psi(\omega^{\prime}_{v})-\Psi(\omega_{v})).\end{split}

(50)

In particular, if there exist
\delta\geq 0
and
\epsilon>0
such that
|D(\omega)\cap E(\gamma)|\leq\delta|\gamma|
and
\|\Psi\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon/2
then

H(\omega^{\prime})-H(\omega)\geq|\gamma|\Big((d-1)u-(U+u)\delta-\epsilon\Big).

(51)

Observe that in (
51
), if
\epsilon=0
and
\delta<\frac{(d-1)u}{U+u}
then the energy cost of the contour
\bar{\gamma}
is positive and proportional to its volume
|\gamma|
. This fact was crucially used in our paper
[
10
]
to exhibit low temperature local ground states which give rise to a wide family of inhomogenous extremal states. We thus introduce the following definition of bad contours.

Definition 4.5

(Bad events)
.

Consider any model in the class (
10
). Let

\delta_{0}:=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{(d-1)u}{(u+U)}

(52)

where
u,U\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
are the bounds on energy costs defined in (
2.1
) and
d
is the branching number of the tree.

Denote by
B_{v}
the bad event
that there exists a contour around
v
with respect to
\omega
which
does not have good enough excess energy in the sense that

\begin{split}B_{v}&:=\bigcup_{\gamma:\gamma\ni v}B(\gamma)\text{ where }B(\gamma):=\{\omega:|D(\omega)\cap E(\gamma)|\geq\delta_{0}|\gamma|\}.\end{split}

(53)

Definition 4.6
.

Suppose that
\mu
is a homogeneous tree-indexed Markov chain for a Hamiltonian in the class (
10
) fulfilling the bounds (
2.1
) and (
11
). Let
p_{1}=p_{1}(\mu)
be its lazyness parameter as in Defintion
2.2
. Let
\delta_{0}
as in (
52
). Define
\lambda(p_{1})=\lambda(p_{1},u,U,d)
as

\begin{split}&e^{-\lambda(p_{1})}:=\inf_{t\geq 0}e^{-t\delta_{0}}(p_{1}e^{t}+1-p_{1})^{(d+1)}=\left(\frac{p_{1}}{\delta_{0}}\right)^{\delta_{0}}\Bigl(\frac{1-p_{1}}{d+1-\delta_{0}}\Bigr)^{1-\delta_{0}}.\end{split}

(54)

Note that
\lambda(p_{1})\uparrow\infty
or equivalently
e^{-\lambda(p_{1})}\downarrow 0\text{ as }p_{1}\downarrow 0
. In particular, if
\mu_{\beta}
is the free state of the
q
-clock model at inverse temperature
\beta
(see (
46
)),

\lambda(p_{1}(\mu_{\beta}))\uparrow\infty\text{ as }\beta\uparrow\infty.

4.3
Key lemma

Lemma 4.6

(Key Lemma)
.

Let
\mu
be the central state of any model with Hamiltonian in the class (
10
) fulfilling the bounds (
2.1
) and (
11
). Then for
\beta
large enough we have, for
\mu
-almost every
\omega

\begin{split}\pi(\sigma_{v}\neq\omega_{v}|\omega)\leq 1_{B_{v}}(\omega)+\epsilon_{1}(\beta)\end{split}

(55)

where the event
B_{v}
is defined in (
4.5
),
and

\epsilon_{1}(\beta)\downarrow 0
as
\beta\uparrow\infty
.
Moreover,

\mu(B(\gamma))\leq e^{-\lambda(p_{1})|\gamma|}

(56)

where
\lambda(p_{1})
is defined in (
54
) and for
p_{1}
small enough, for any
v\in V

m(p_{1}):=\mu(B_{v})\leq\epsilon_{2}(p_{1})

(57)

where
\epsilon_{2}(p_{1})\downarrow 0
as
p_{1}\downarrow 0
.

Proof.

We decompose

\displaystyle\pi(\sigma_{v}\neq\omega_{v}|\omega)=\pi(\sigma_{v}\neq\omega_{v}|\omega)(1_{B_{v}}(\omega)+1_{B^{c}_{v}}(\omega))

(58)

and we use the infinite-volume version of the Peierls bound of Lemma
4.3
(removing the restriction to finite volume always gives an upper bound) applied to the complement of a bad event at
v
to bound
the probability of a mismatch at
v
by the sum over contours attached at
v
.
This gives

\begin{split}&\pi(\sigma_{v}\neq\omega_{v}|\omega)1_{B^{c}_{v}}(\omega)\leq\sum_{\bar{\gamma},\gamma\ni v}\rho[\omega](\bar{\gamma})1_{B^{c}_{v}}(\omega)\end{split}

(59)

where
\rho[\omega](\bar{\gamma})
is the contour activity relative to
\omega
.
Note that
\omega
is not assumed to be a local ground state (in the sense of
[
10
]
), but we may
still use the bound on the excess energy of a local perturbation given in Lemma
4.5
.

This delivers, using the assumptions on the potential in the class (
10
) and the Definition
4.5
of
B_{v}
,

\begin{split}&\rho[\omega](\bar{\gamma})1_{B^{c}_{v}}(\omega)\cr&\leq\exp({-\beta(d-1)u|\gamma|+\beta(u+U)|D(\omega)\cap E(\gamma)|+{\|\Psi\|}_{\infty}|\gamma|})1_{B^{c}_{v}}(\omega)\cr&\leq\exp({-\beta|\gamma|((d-1)u-(u+U)\delta_{0}-{\|\Psi\|}_{\infty})})\cr&=\exp({-\beta|\gamma|{(d-1)u}/4})\end{split}

(60)

Using the entropy bound of Lemma
4.4

we deduce that there exists
C^{\prime},c^{\prime}>0
such that

\begin{split}&\pi(\sigma_{v}\neq\omega_{v}|\omega)1_{B^{c}_{v}}(\omega)\leq\sum_{\bar{\gamma},\gamma\ni v}e^{-\beta c|\gamma|}{\leq\sum_{\ell\geq 1}(d+1)^{2(\ell-1)}(q-1)^{\ell}e^{-\beta\ell(d-1)u/4}}\cr&\leq C^{\prime}e^{-c^{\prime}\beta}=:\epsilon_{1}(\beta)\end{split}

(61)

for
\beta>4\log((q-1)(d+1)^{2})/(u(d-1))
, which proves (
55
).

Next we use the exponential Markov inequality to bound
\mu(B_{v}(\gamma))

and sum over the contours afterwards to bound

\mu(B_{v})
, as will be described now.

\displaystyle\mu(B_{v}(\gamma))

\displaystyle=\mu\bigl(|D(\omega)\cap E(\gamma)|\geq\delta_{0}|\gamma|\bigr)

(62)

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{t\geq 0}e^{-t\delta_{0}|\gamma|}\mu(e^{t|D(\omega)\cap E(\gamma)|})

(63)

It suffices to fix any
x\in\gamma
, look at the conditional measure

\mu_{a}(\cdot):=\mu(\cdot|\sigma_{x}=a)
for any fixed
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}
, and find an upper bound which does not depend on
a
.

Using the Markov chain property of the measure
\mu_{a}
, successive applications of the homogeneous transition matrix

P
yield

\begin{split}&\mu_{a}(e^{t|D(\omega)\cap E(\gamma)|})\leq(p_{1}e^{t}+1-p_{1})^{(d+1)|\gamma|}\end{split}

(64)

which follows from

\max_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}}\sum_{b\neq a}P(a,b)e^{t}+P(a,a)\leq p_{1}e^{t}+1-p_{1}

and the bound
|E(\gamma)|\leq(d+1)|\gamma|.

Optimizing over
t
leads to the bound

\begin{split}&\mu(B_{v}(\gamma)){\leq\Bigl(\inf_{t\geq 0}e^{-t\delta_{0}}(p_{1}e^{t}+1-p_{1})^{(d+1)}\Bigr)^{|\gamma|}}=e^{-\lambda(p_{1})|\gamma|}\end{split}

(65)

which proves (
56
), since the definition (
54
) of
\exp(-\lambda(p_{1}))
is made to be the value of the above infimum.
Finally using the entropy bound of Lemma
4.4
on the number of contours of fixed length
which are attached to a given point, the estimate for

\mu(B_{v})
follows. Indeed, there exists
C>0
such that for any
c\in(0,1)
we have

\displaystyle m(p_{1}):=\mu(B_{v})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\gamma:\gamma\ni v}\mu(B_{v}(\gamma))\leq\sum_{\gamma:|\gamma|\geq 1}e^{-\lambda(p_{1})|\gamma|}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell\geq 1}(d+1)^{2(\ell-1)}(q-1)^{\ell}e^{-\lambda(p_{1})\ell}

(66)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-c\lambda(p_{1})}=:\epsilon_{2}(p_{1})

(67)

for
p_{1}
sufficiently small, which proves (
57
).
∎

4.4
Positivity of the Edwards-Anderson parameter

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

The proof is short and elementary, given the reconstruction bound of Theorem
3.1
.
Observe that

\begin{split}&q_{\rm EA}^{\mu}=\sum_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}}\Bigl(\mu\Bigl(\pi({\sigma_{0}=a}|\cdot)^{2}\Bigr)-\mu(\sigma_{0}=a)^{2}\Bigr)\cr&=\sum_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}}\Bigl(\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}}\mu(\sigma_{0}=b)\mu\Bigl(\pi({\sigma_{0}=a}|\cdot)^{2}|\sigma_{0}=b\Bigr)-\mu(\sigma_{0}=a)^{2}\Bigr)\cr&\geq\sum_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}}\Bigl(\mu(\sigma_{0}=a)\mu\Bigl(\pi({\sigma_{0}=a}|\cdot)^{2}|\sigma_{0}=a\Bigr)-\mu(\sigma_{0}=a)^{2}\Bigr)\cr\end{split}

(68)

which, by the quantitative reconstruction Theorem
3.1
gives the lower bound

\begin{split}q_{\rm EA}^{\mu}&\geq\sum_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}}\Bigl(\mu(\sigma_{0}=a)(1-\epsilon_{1}(\beta))^{2}(1-\epsilon_{2}(p_{1}))-\mu(\sigma_{0}=a)^{2}\Bigr)\\
&\geq(1-\epsilon_{1}(\beta))^{2}(1-\epsilon_{2}(p_{1}))-\max_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}}\mu(\sigma_{0}=a)\end{split}

(69)

which proves the theorem.
∎

4.5
Overlap control for typical extremals

4.5.1
Exponential decorrelation of bad sites

Suppose that
\mu
is a homogeneous
tree-indexed Markov chain.
We do not need to assume any invariances of the measure
\mu

under joint transformations of the local spin-spaces (like a discrete clock-rotation
or even permutation symmetry).
We will only assume that the jump probability
p_{1}
(see Definition
2.2
), which gives a bound on
the density of broken bonds in the configuration drawn from
\mu
,
is small enough. Using this assumption, we derive decorrelation between two bad events occuring at the sites
u
and
v
(see Definition
4.5
), with an upper bound
that is exponential small in the distance between
u
and
v
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Consider the central state
\mu
of any model with Hamiltonian in the class (
10
) fulfilling the bounds (
2.1
) and (
11
).
Suppose that the transition matrix
P
of the state
\mu
is irreducible
and aperiodic. Recall the Definition
4.5
of a bad event
B_{v}
at
v\in V
and the notation for its expectation
\mu(B_{v})=:m(p_{1})
(which does not depend on
v
).
Let

\mbox{$\mathrm{C}ov$}(u,v):=\mu\Bigl((1_{B_{v}}(\omega)-m(p_{1}))(1_{B_{u}}(\omega)-m(p_{1}))\Bigr).

(70)

Then for small enough
p_{1}
, there exist
C>0
such that for any
c_{1}\in(0,\frac{1}{3})
and
c_{2}\in(0,1)
, for any
u,v\in V
,

\begin{split}|\mbox{$\mathrm{C}ov$}(u,v)|&\leq Ce^{-c|v-u|}\end{split}

(71)

where
|v-u|
is the graph distance in the tree and

\begin{split}&e^{-c}=\max\{e^{-c_{1}\lambda(p_{1})},|\lambda_{2}(P)|^{c_{2}}\}\end{split}

(72)

where
\lambda(p_{1})
is defined in (
54
) and
\lambda_{2}(P)
is the second largest eigenvalue (in modulus)
of the transition matrix
P
of
\mu
.

The proof uses a combination of two facts.
First, long bad contours attached to
a given site are exponentially improbable, see Lemma
4.6
.
This explains the occurence of
\lambda(p_{1})
.
Second there is exponential relaxation of the one-dimensional
Markov chain obtained by restriction of
\mu
to a branch, conditionally on a root, which explains the appearance of the second largest eigenvalue
\lambda_{2}(P)
of the transition matrix. Of course
|\lambda_{2}(P)|<1
.

Proof.

We first define several objects and events which are depicted on Figure
1
.

Figure 1:
Objects appearing in the proof of Lemma
4.7
of exponential decorrelation of bad events at
u
and
v
. On the picture
d=2,|u-v|=11,r=3
. Half-trees
H_{u}(3)
and
H_{v}(3)
are delimited by the two vertical lines. One of the midpoints between
u
and
v
is denoted by
x
. The support of the bad event
B_{u}^{1}
, depicted with a dashed line, stays inside
H_{u}(3)
, while the support of the bad event
B_{v}^{2}
, depicted with a dotted line, exits
H_{v}(3)
. The vertex
y
is the closest to
u
on the path from
u
to
x
which does not touch the support of
B_{u}^{1}
.

Denote by the half-trees
H_{u}(r)
and
H_{v}(r)
the sets

\begin{split}&H_{u}:=H_{u}(r):=\{x\in V,|x-u|\leq|x-v|-r\}\cr&H_{v}:=H_{v}(r):=\{x\in V,|x-v|\leq|x-u|-r\}\cr\end{split}

(73)

We will choose
r=\lfloor|u-v|/3\rfloor
.
Clearly
H_{u}
and
H_{v}
are disjoint, with their distance growing proportionally
to
|u-v|
.
Define

\begin{split}&B^{1}_{u}:=\bigcup_{\gamma:H_{u}\supset\gamma\ni u}B_{u}(\gamma),\quad B^{2}_{u}:=B_{u}\backslash B^{1}_{u}\cr&B^{1}_{v}:=\bigcup_{\gamma:H_{v}\supset\gamma\ni u}B_{v}(\gamma),\quad B^{2}_{v}:=B_{v}\backslash B^{1}_{v}.\cr\end{split}

(74)

The sets
B^{1}_{u}
and
B^{1}_{v}
describe bad events at
u
(resp.
v
) which are produced by contours
included in
H_{u}
(resp.
H_{v}
).
The sets
B^{2}_{u}
and
B^{2}_{v}
describe bad events which are not produced by the previous contours and may
connect
u
and
v
.

Bad event caused by long contours are exponentially improbable
in the minimal length of such contours. Namely, by the Key Lemma
4.6
and the entropy bound given in Lemma
4.4
, there exist
C_{1}>0
such that for any
c_{1}\in(0,\frac{1}{3})
we have

\begin{split}\mu(B^{2}_{u})=\mu(B^{2}_{v})&\leq\sum_{\gamma:\gamma\ni u,\gamma\cap H^{c}_{u}\neq\emptyset}\mu(B_{u}(\bar{\gamma}))\leq\sum_{\gamma:\gamma\ni u,|\gamma|\geq|u-v|/3}e^{-\lambda(p_{1})|\gamma|}\\
&\leq\sum_{\ell\geq|u-v|/3}(d+1)^{2(\ell-1)}(q-1)^{\ell}e^{-\lambda(p_{1})\ell}\\
&\leq C_{1}\exp({-c_{1}\lambda(p_{1})|u-v|}).\end{split}

(75)

Spelling out the correlation function between the bad events in terms
of the decomposition (
74
), we therefore have

\begin{split}&|\mbox{$\mathrm{C}ov$}(u,v)|=|\mu(B^{1}_{u};B^{1}_{v})+\mu(B^{1}_{u};B^{2}_{v})+\mu(B^{2}_{u};B^{1}_{v})+\mu(B^{2}_{u};B^{2}_{v})|\cr&\leq|\mu(B^{1}_{u};B^{1}_{v})|+3\min\{\mu(B^{2}_{u}),\mu(B^{2}_{v})\}\\
&\leq|\mu(B^{1}_{u};B^{1}_{v})|+3C_{1}\exp({-c_{1}\lambda(p_{1})|u-v|}).\cr\end{split}

(76)

It remains to control the decay of
|\mu(B^{1}_{u};B^{1}_{v})|
.
We would have the exact decorrelation

\mu(B^{1}_{u};B^{1}_{v})=0
in the free state of a clock model, but
for an non-symmetric model there is no reason to assume that such
decorrelation holds.
Nevertheless, we may use the Markov chain property of
\mu
along the path from
u
to
v
and
the exponential convergence of the corresponding 1d-Markov chain, to arrive
at the decorrelation which is exponential in the distance
|u-v|
(with our irreducibility and aperiodicity assumptions).

Indeed, choose
x=x(u,v)
to be the (or one of the at most two) middle sites
between
u
and
v
(which means that the distances
|u-x|
and
|v-x|
differ at most by one).
From the tree-indexed Markov chain property of
\mu
, using conditional independence, we have

\begin{split}&\mu(B^{1}_{u}\cap B^{1}_{v})=\sum_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}}\mu(B^{1}_{u}|\sigma_{x}=a)\mu(B^{1}_{v}|\sigma_{x}=a)\mu(\sigma_{x}=a).\end{split}

(77)

Let
\Delta_{u,x}(a):=\mu(B^{1}_{u}|\sigma_{x}=a)-\mu(B^{1}_{u})
and similarly for
v
. We have

\displaystyle|\mu(B^{1}_{u};B^{1}_{v})|

\displaystyle=\big|\sum_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}}\Bigl(\mu(B^{1}_{u})\Delta_{v,x}(a)+\mu(B^{1}_{v})\Delta_{u,x}(a)+\Delta_{u,x}(a)\Delta_{v,x}(a)\Bigr)\mu(\sigma_{x}=a)\big|

\displaystyle\leq 2\max_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}}|\Delta_{u,x}(a)|+\max_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}}|\Delta_{v,x}(a)|.

(78)

By symmetry it suffices to bound
|\Delta_{u,x}(a)|
.
Choose
y
on the path
[x,u]
, between
x
and
u
, such that it is the closest to
u
with the property that all the contours appearing in the definition of
B^{1}_{u}
do not touch

(x,y]
.
To control the difference between
\mu(B^{1}_{u}|\sigma_{x}=a)
and
\mu(B^{1}_{u})

we use the exponential relaxation of the homogenous 1d Markov chain which arises as restriction of
\mu
, on the path from
x
to
y
.
There exists
C_{2}>0
such that for any
c_{2}\in(0,1)
,

\begin{split}&|\Delta_{u,x}(a)|=|\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}}\mu(B^{1}_{u}|\sigma_{y}=b)(\mu(\sigma_{y}=b|\sigma_{x}=a)-\mu(\sigma_{y}=b))|\cr&\leq\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}}|\mu(\sigma_{y}=b|\sigma_{x}=a)-\mu(\sigma_{y}=b))|\cr&=\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}}|P^{|x-y|}(a,b)-\mu(\sigma_{y}=b))|\cr&\leq C_{2}|\lambda_{2}(P)|^{c_{2}|x-y|}\end{split}

(79)

where the last line follows e.g. from
[
6
, Example 4.3.9:
Rates of Convergence via the Perron-Frobenius Theorem]
.
As by construction
|y-x|\sim\frac{1}{6}|u-v|
, as
|u-v|\uparrow\infty
, this implies the
desired exponential decorrelation of
|\mu(B^{1}_{u};B^{1}_{v})|
, and concludes the proof.
∎

4.5.2
Almost sure convergence of empirical means of bad sites

As a consequence of the exponential decorrelation of bad events we
harvest the following lemma.

Lemma 4.8
.

Consider the central state
\mu
of any model with Hamiltonian of the form (
10
) fulfilling the bounds (
2.1
) and (
11
).
For any sequence of subsets
\Lambda_{n}=\Lambda_{n}^{r}
of a branch of the tree such that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{|\Lambda_{n}|}^{-1}<\infty
and
for
\mu
-almost every
\omega
, the following limit holds

\begin{split}&\lim_{n\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|}\sum_{v\in\Lambda_{n}}1_{B_{v}}(\omega)=m(p_{1})\end{split}

(80)

where
m(p_{1})=\mu(B_{0})
.

While the limit in question, if it exists is necessarily tail-trivial, the statement is non-trivial
as the measure
\mu
is not extremal at low temperature.
So it must be proved manually.

Proof.

The almost sure limit holds, if we can
show that for any fixed
\delta>0
the number of indices

n
for which

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|}\Bigl|\sum_{v\in\Lambda_{n}}(1_{B_{v}}(\omega)-m(p_{1}))\Bigr|\geq\delta\end{split}

(81)

is finite, for
\mu
-almost every
\omega
.
By Borel-Cantelli it suffices to show that

\begin{split}&\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu\Bigl(\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|}\Bigl|\sum_{v\in\Lambda_{n}}(1_{B_{v}}(\omega)-m(p_{1}))\Bigr|>\delta\Bigr)<\infty.\end{split}

(82)

For this we use the quadratic Chebychev inequality to bound the probability
in the above sum by

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|^{2}\delta^{2}}\sum_{v,u\in\Lambda_{n}}\mu\Bigl((1_{B_{v}}(\omega)-m(p_{1}))(1_{B_{u}}(\omega)-m(p_{1}))\Bigr).\end{split}

(83)

Recalling the definition (
70
) of site covariances
\mbox{$\mathrm{C}ov$}(u,v)
and using Lemma
4.7
, we deduce that there exists
C>0
such that

\begin{split}&\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|^{2}}\sum_{v,u\in\Lambda_{n}}\mbox{$\mathrm{C}ov$}(u,v)\cr&\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Biggl(\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|}\mbox{$\mathrm{C}ov$}(0,0)+\frac{2}{|\Lambda_{n}|^{2}}\sum_{v\in\Lambda_{n}}\sum_{u\in\Lambda_{n},u>v}\mbox{$\mathrm{C}ov$}(u,v)\Biggr)\cr&\leq C\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|}<\infty\end{split}

(84)

since for any fixed vertex
v\in V
, the sum
\sum_{u>v}\mbox{$\mathrm{C}ov$}(u,v)\leq C^{\prime}<\infty
uniformly in
v
by Lemma
4.7
.
This proves the existence of the almost sure limit of empirical
sums of indicators of bad points, along the quadratic volume sequences defined in (
30
).
∎

4.5.3
Concentration of spin-overlaps under typical extremals

Lemma 4.9
.

Consider the central state
\mu
of any model with Hamiltonian in the class (
10
) fulfilling the bounds (
2.1
) and (
11
).

There exist a sequence of spacings
r=(r_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
,
in general depending on model parameters, such that
for any sequence of subsets
\Lambda_{n}=\Lambda^{r}_{n}
of a branch of the tree such that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{|\Lambda_{n}|}^{-1}<\infty
, the following holds.

For
\mu
-almost every
\omega
, for
\pi(\cdot|\omega)
-almost every realizations of
\sigma

we have

\begin{split}\limsup_{n\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|}\Bigl|\sum_{v\in\Lambda_{n}}(1_{\sigma_{v}\neq\omega_{v}}-\pi(\sigma_{v}\neq\omega_{v}|\omega))\Bigr|=0.\end{split}

(85)

Proof.

It suffices to prove that, for any
\delta>0
we have, for
\mu
-a.e.
\omega
,

\begin{split}\pi\left(\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|}\Bigl|\sum_{v\in\Lambda_{n}}(1_{\sigma_{v}\neq\omega_{v}}-\pi(\sigma_{v}\neq\omega_{v}|\omega))\Bigr|>\delta\text{ for infinitely many }n\,\middle|\,\omega\right)=0\end{split}

(86)

Using the Borel-Cantelli lemma it suffices to show that, for
\mu
-a.e.
\omega
,

\begin{split}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\pi\left(\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|}\Bigl|\sum_{v\in\Lambda_{n}}(1_{\sigma_{v}\neq\omega_{v}}-\pi(\sigma_{v}\neq\omega_{v}|\omega))\Bigr|>\delta\,\middle|\,\omega\right)<\infty.\end{split}

(87)

Now by the quadratic Chebychev inequality, it is enough to
show that

\begin{split}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|^{2}}\sum_{v,u\in\Lambda_{n}}|\pi(\sigma_{v}\neq\omega_{v};\sigma_{u}\neq\omega_{u}|\omega)|<\infty\end{split}

(88)

holds for
\mu
-a.e.
\omega
.
We would like to use the abstract fact that

\pi(\cdot|\omega)
is an extremal Gibbs measure, and hence decorrelates in the sense of (
12
). Indeed, by extremality of
\pi(\cdot|\omega)
at fixed
\omega
, for any fixed
u
and any sequence
v\uparrow\infty

(meaning that
v
leaves any finite ball) the following non-quantative result holds: For
\mu
-a.e.
\omega
,

\begin{split}\lim_{v\uparrow\infty}|\pi(\sigma_{v}\neq\omega_{v};\sigma_{u}\neq\omega_{u}|\omega)|=0.\end{split}

(89)

The problem is that the rate of convergence may depend on
\omega
, while
we want a speed which is uniform in all the extremals (in order for the limit (
85
) to hold along the same sparse sequence of volumes for
\mu
-a.e.
\omega
). An explicit analysis
is difficult, due to the lack of spatial homogeneity.
To bypass this difficulty, note that to ensure (
88
) it is sufficient to ensure that

\begin{split}\int\mu(d\omega)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|^{2}}\sum_{v,u\in\Lambda_{n}}|\pi(\sigma_{v}\neq\omega_{v};\sigma_{u}\neq\omega_{u}|\omega)|<\infty.\end{split}

(90)

Using monotone convergence for non-negative functions
to interchange the
\mu
-integral and the infinite sum,
we can deduce this from

\begin{split}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|^{2}}\sum_{v,u\in\Lambda_{n}}\underbrace{\int\mu(d\omega)|\pi(\sigma_{v}\neq\omega_{v};\sigma_{u}\neq\omega_{u}|\omega)|}_{=:{c(u,v)}}<\infty\end{split}

(91)

By dominated convergence we have, for any
u\in V
,

\begin{split}{\lim_{v\uparrow\infty}c(u,v)=0}\end{split}

(92)

We look at the off-diagonal terms in the double-sum (
91
) as in (
84
).
Now we can achieve the desired summability of

\begin{split}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{|\Lambda_{n}|}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}c(v_{j},v_{i})<\infty\end{split}

(93)

by iteratively choosing
v_{i}
given
v_{1},\dots,v_{i-1}
on the same branch so large
that

\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}c(v_{j},v_{i})<i^{-2}.

The above choice provides the following upper bound of (
93
) as

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}i^{-2}

which is finite. ∎

4.5.4
Proof of Theorem
3.4

Proof.

Applying Lemma
4.9
, the Key Lemma
4.6
, and Lemma
4.8
, we deduce that for sparse enough sets
\Lambda_{n}=\Lambda_{n}^{\rm r}
the following holds.
For
\mu
-almost every
\omega
,
\pi(\cdot|\omega)
-almost surely,

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|}\sum_{v\in\Lambda_{n}}1_{\sigma_{v}=\omega_{v}}

\displaystyle\geq 1-\limsup_{n\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|}\sum_{v\in\Lambda_{n}}\pi(\sigma_{v}\neq\omega_{v}|\omega)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\liminf_{n\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|}\sum_{v\in\Lambda_{n}}1_{B_{v}}(\omega)-\epsilon_{1}(\beta)

\displaystyle=1-\epsilon_{1}(\beta)-m(p_{1})

\displaystyle\geq 1-\epsilon_{1}(\beta)-\epsilon_{2}(p_{1})

with
\epsilon_{1}(\beta)=C^{\prime}e^{-c^{\prime}\beta}
, see (
61
), and
\epsilon_{2}(p_{1})=Ce^{-c\lambda(p_{1})}
, see (
4.3
) and Definition
2.2
. This proves the theorem.
∎

4.6
From overlap control to almost sure singularity of extremals

Proof of Corollary
3.2
.

On the one hand we have the following lower bound from Theorem
3.4
and monotone convergence. For sparse enough sets
\Lambda_{n}=\Lambda_{n}^{\rm r}
, for
\mu
almost every
\omega
,

\begin{split}&\pi(\underline{\phi}^{\omega}|\omega)=\liminf_{n\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|}\sum_{v\in\Lambda_{n}}\pi(\sigma_{v}=\omega_{v}|\omega)\geq 1-\epsilon_{1}(\beta)-\epsilon_{2}(p_{1}).\end{split}

(94)

On the other hand, we use

\begin{split}1_{\sigma_{v}=\omega_{v}}\leq 1_{\omega_{v}=\omega^{\prime}_{v}}+1_{\sigma_{v}\neq\omega^{\prime}_{v}}\end{split}

(95)

to write the upper bound

\begin{split}\pi(\underline{\phi}^{\omega}|\omega^{\prime})\leq\limsup_{n\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|}\sum_{v\in\Lambda_{n}}1_{\omega_{v}=\omega^{\prime}_{v}}+\pi\left(\limsup_{n\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|}\sum_{v\in\Lambda_{n}}1_{\sigma_{v}\neq\omega^{\prime}_{v}}\middle|\omega^{\prime}\right).\end{split}

(96)

The first term in the r.h.s. is equal to
{1}/{q}
for
\mu\otimes\mu
-a.e. pair
(\omega,\omega^{\prime})
, in the case of clock models. Indeed, the Borel-Cantelli argument, together with the Chebychev inequality as in the proof of Lemma
4.8

shows that the desired strong law of large numbers follows (since exponential decorrelation of the local events
(1_{\omega_{v}=a})_{v\in\Lambda_{n}^{r}}
follows from exponential decorrelation of the 1d Markov chains as in (
79
)).
In the general case of central
states this term equals
\sum_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}}\mu(\sigma_{0}=a)^{2}
; it is close to
1/q

for perturbed free states of clock models, see Section
4.1.4
.

The second term in the r.h.s. is
\pi(\cdot|\omega^{\prime})
-a.s. controlled
via Theorem
3.4
by

\begin{split}\limsup_{n\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{n}|}\sum_{v\in\Lambda_{n}}1_{\sigma_{v}\neq\omega^{\prime}_{v}}\leq\epsilon_{1}(\beta)+\epsilon_{2}(p_{1}).\end{split}

(97)

So the statement of the theorem follows once

1-\epsilon_{1}(\beta)-\epsilon_{2}(p_{1})>\sum_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}}\mu(\sigma_{0}=a)^{2}+\epsilon_{1}(\beta)+\epsilon_{2}(p_{1})

which concludes the proof.
∎

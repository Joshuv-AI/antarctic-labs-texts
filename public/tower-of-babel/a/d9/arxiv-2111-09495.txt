Quasilinear wave equations on Schwarzschild–de Sitter
Georgios Mavrogiannis

Abstract
. We give an elementary new argument for global existence and exponential decay of solutions of quasilinear wave equations on Schwarzschild–de Sitter black hole backgrounds, for appropriately small initial data. The core of the argument is entirely local, based on time translation invariant energy estimates in spacetime slabs of fixed time length. Global existence then follows simply by iterating this local result in consecutive spacetime slabs. We infer that an appropriate future energy flux decays exponentially with respect to the energy flux of the initial data.

Theorem 1

(rough version)
.

Let

\delta=\delta(\tau_{\textit{step}})>0,\qquad\epsilon=\epsilon(\tau_{\textit{step}},\delta)>0

\tau_{1}\geq 0

(1.10)

\tau_{2}=\tau_{1}+\tau_{\textit{step}}

\displaystyle E_{\mathcal{G},k}[\psi](\tau_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{L}E_{\mathcal{G},k}[\psi](\tau_{1}),

and

(1.13)

\displaystyle E_{\mathcal{G},k}[\psi](\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq CE_{\mathcal{G},k}[\psi](\tau_{1}),

\displaystyle E_{k+1}[\psi](\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq CE_{k+1}[\psi](\tau_{1}),

\displaystyle E_{k+2}[\psi](\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq CE_{k+2}[\psi](\tau_{1}),

for all
\tau^{\prime}\in[\tau_{1},\tau_{1}+\tau_{\textit{step}}]

Finally,

\displaystyle E_{k+1}[\psi](\tau_{2})\leq Ce^{-\frac{1}{3}\log(L)+\frac{2}{3}\log C}\left(E_{\mathcal{G},k}[\psi](\tau_{1})\right)^{1/3}\left(E_{k+2}[\psi](\tau_{1})\right)^{2/3}.

Theorem 2

(rough version)
.

Let
k\geq 7
. Then, there exist constants
c_{\text{d}},c_{\text{g}}>0
and there exists a
\delta>0
and an
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small, such that if the initial energy satisfies

(1.15)

E_{k+2}[\psi](0)\leq\epsilon,

on
\Sigma=\{\bar{t}=0\}
then the solution exists globally on
\mathcal{M}_{\delta}
and the energy
E_{\mathcal{G},k}[\psi]
decays exponentially

(1.16)

E_{\mathcal{G},k}[\psi](\tau)\lesssim e^{-c_{\text{d}}\tau}E_{\mathcal{G},k}[\psi](0).

We note that the classical Sobolev energies

E_{k-1}[\psi](\tau)\lesssim e^{-c_{\text{d}}\tau}E_{\mathcal{G},k}[\psi](0),\qquad E_{k+1}[\psi](\tau)\lesssim e^{-c_{\text{d}}\tau}E_{k+2}[\psi](0)

while for the top order energy we only have

(1.18)

E_{k+2}[\psi](\tau)\lesssim e^{c_{\text{g}}\tau}E_{k+2}[\psi](0).

Wonderful compactifications and rational curves with cyclic action
Emily Clader, Chiara Damiolini, Shiyue Li, Rohini Ramadas

Abstract
. We prove that the moduli space of rational curves with cyclic action, constructed in our previous work, is realizable as a wonderful compactification of the complement of a hyperplane arrangement in a product of projective spaces. By proving a general result on such wonderful compactifications, we conclude that this moduli space is Chow-equivalent to an explicit toric variety (whose fan can be understood as a tropical version of the moduli space), from which a computation of its Chow ring follows.



1.
Introduction

The moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
of rational curves with cyclic action was constructed in our previous work
[
CDH+21
]
as a generalization of Losev and Manin’s moduli space of rational curves with weighted marked points. In particular, the Losev–Manin space
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{n}
, introduced in
[
LM00
]
, is a toric variety whose associated polytope is the permutohedron
\Pi_{n}
, and the torus-invariant subvarieties of
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{n}
have a modular interpretation as “boundary strata,” so one obtains an inclusion- and dimension-preserving bijection between the boundary strata of
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{n}
and the faces of
\Pi_{n}
. This work was generalized by Batyrev and Blume, who in
[
BB11
]
constructed a toric moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{n}
of rational curves with involution whose boundary strata are encoded by the faces of the signed permutohedron. Generalizing the story further, the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
parameterizes certain rational curves with an automorphism of order
r
and weighted orbits. Although
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
is not toric when
r>2
, its boundary strata are nevertheless encoded by a polyhedral object: not a polytope, in this case, but a polytopal complex. In this way,
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
appears to occupy an intriguing middle ground between toric varieties and more general moduli spaces of rational curves.

The goal of the current work is to realize
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
as a wonderful compactification of the complement of a particular arrangement of hyperplanes in
(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{n}
, and in doing so, to give a combinatorial description of its Chow ring. Wonderful compactifications were introduced by De Concini and Procesi in
[
DCP95
]
as a way to compactify the complement of an arrangement of hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
so that much of the geometry of the compactification is encoded in the combinatorics of the original hyperplane arrangement. The geometry of these spaces has been used to resolve long-standing conjectures in combinatorics like the log-concavity of characteristic polynomials of matroids
[
AHK18
]
and the Dowling–Wilson top-heavy conjecture
[
BHM+20
]
. On the other hand, they have also provided a valuable new perspective in geometry; perhaps the most relevant example for the present work is the Deligne–Mumford–Knudsen compactification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
, which can be realized as a wonderful compactification of the braid arrangement complement in
\mathbb{P}^{n-3}
, from which one obtains an elegant presentation of its Chow ring.

One way in which to understand the Chow ring in this setting, as shown by Feichtner and Yuzvinsky in
[
FY04
]
, is as the Chow ring of the toric variety of a fan
\Sigma_{\mathcal{G}}
that can be combinatorially associated to a hyperplane arrangement in projective space together with a “building set”
\mathcal{G}
. In particular, the data of
\mathcal{G}
specifies a wonderful compactification
\overline{Y}_{\mathcal{G}}
of the arrangement complement, and Feichtner–Yuzvinsky prove that the Chow ring of
\overline{Y}_{\mathcal{G}}
is isomorphic to that of the toric variety
X_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{G}}}
.

The construction of wonderful compactifications was generalized by Li Li in
[
Li09b
]
to complements of arrangements of subvarieties in a smooth variety, but some of their combinatorial nature is lost in this generality. In particular, the geometry of a wonderful compactification
\overline{Y}_{\mathcal{G}}
is not determined merely by the intersection combinatorics of the subvarieties in the arrangement—which is what determines
\Sigma_{\mathcal{G}}
—but by the particular geometry of the subvarieties themselves. Thus, one should not expect the Chow ring of
\overline{Y}_{\mathcal{G}}
to be isomorphic to that of a toric variety in general.

The case
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
of interest for our work is a wonderful compactification of a hyperplane arrangement not in a projective space (as in De Concini–Procesi’s original work) but in a product of projective spaces. Specifically, it is a “product arrangement” in the sense that the hyperplanes are pulled back via projection to the individual projective space factors. We begin by proving that, for arrangements of this form, the Chow ring of the wonderful compactification is still combinatorial: one can associate a fan
\Sigma_{\mathcal{G}}
(defined in Definition
2.8
below) generalizing the fan of Feichtner–Yuzvinsky, and the resulting toric variety has isomorphic Chow ring to
\overline{Y}_{\mathcal{G}}
.

Theorem
2.10

(See Section
2.4
for precise statement)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a product arrangement in
\mathbb{P}^{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{k_{n}}
, let
\mathcal{G}
be a building set for its intersection lattice, and let
\Sigma_{\mathcal{G}}
be the associated nested set fan.
Then there is a Chow-equivalence

A^{*}(\overline{Y}_{\mathcal{G}})=A^{*}(X_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{G}}}).

Equipped with this result, we specifically consider the arrangement of hyperplanes

\widetilde{H}_{i}^{j}=\{(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{n}\;|\;p_{i}=\zeta^{j}\}

for each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and each
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,r-1\}
, where
\zeta
is a fixed
r
th root of unity. We prove in Theorem
3.7
that
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
is the wonderful compactification of this arrangement with its maximal building set. Denoting the nested set fan in this case by
\Sigma^{r}_{n}
, we obtain by Theorem
2.10
an explicit computation of the Chow ring
A^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n})
.

To describe this computation, we first recall from
[
CDH+21
]
that there is a special codimension-
1
subvariety
D_{\widetilde{I}}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
—specifically, a boundary divisor—associated to any “
\mathbb{Z}_{r}
-decorated subset of
[n]
,” which is a pair
\widetilde{I}=(I,\mathfrak{a})
in which
I\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and
\mathfrak{a}
is a function
I\rightarrow\{0,1,\ldots,r-1\}
. There is a partial ordering on decorated subsets given by

(I,\mathfrak{a})\leq(J,\mathfrak{b})\;\text{ if and only if}\;I\subseteq J\text{ and }\mathfrak{a}(i)=\mathfrak{b}(i)\text{ for all }i\in I.

With this notation, the presentation of
A^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n})
is as follows.

Theorem
4.13
.

The Chow ring of
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
is generated by the boundary divisors
D_{\widetilde{I}}
for each
\mathbb{Z}_{r}
-decorated subset
\widetilde{I}
of
\{1,\dots,n\}
, with relations given by

•

D_{\widetilde{I}}\cdot D_{\widetilde{J}}=0
unless either
\widetilde{I}\leq\widetilde{J}
or
\widetilde{J}\leq\widetilde{I}
;

•

for all
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and all
a,b\in\{0,1,\ldots,r-1\}
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{I}\text{ s.t. }\\
i\in I,\;\mathfrak{a}(i)=a\end{subarray}}D_{\widetilde{I}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{I}\text{ s.t. }\\
i\in I,\;\mathfrak{a}(i)=b\end{subarray}}D_{\widetilde{I}}.

We conclude the paper by giving two other interpretations of the fan
\Sigma^{r}_{n}
, which are interesting in their own right. First, analogously to the case of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
, we show in Proposition
5.3
that this fan can be identified with a moduli space
L^{r,\text{trop}}_{n}
of “tropical
(r,n)
-curves.” And second, analogously to the way in which the permutohedron
\Pi_{n}
is the normal polytope of the fan of Losev–Manin space
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{n}
, we show in Proposition
5.6
that the polytopal complex
\Delta^{r}_{n}
constructed in
[
CDH+21
]
is a normal complex of
\Sigma^{r}_{n}
, in the sense developed by Nathanson–Ross in
[
NR21
]
. This gives a more geometric interpretation of the correspondence between the boundary strata of
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
and the faces of
\Delta^{r}_{n}
that was proven combinatorially in our previous work.

Leveraging the above connection to tropical geometry, we hope in future work to use tropical intersection theory on
L^{r,\text{trop}}_{n}
to study intersection numbers on
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
(along the lines of
[
Kat12
,
KM09
,
HL21
]
). We may also study the reduced rational cohomology of the locus of tropical curves with total edge length
1
in
L^{r,\text{trop}}_{n}
to understand the mixed Hodge structure of
\mathcal{L}^{r}_{n}

[
Del71

Del74
]
and along the lines of
[
CGP21
,
KLSY20
]
. This is made possible by the observation that the boundary
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}\setminus\mathcal{L}^{r}_{n}

CDH+21

.

2.
Wonderful compactifications

Wonderful compactifications were introduced by De Concini and Procesi
[
DCP95
]
in the context of linear subvarieties of a projective space. Roughly speaking, given a collection of linear subvarieties in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, a wonderful compactification is a way of replacing
\mathbb{P}^{n}
by a different ambient variety in such a way that the complement of the linear subvarieties is preserved but the subvarieties themselves are replaced by a divisor with normal crossings. The construction of wonderful compactifications was later generalized by Li Li
[
Li09b
]
to more general collections of subvarieties in a smooth variety. In this section, we briefly review the necessary definitions for the current work, but we refer the reader to many more in-depth references—including
[
DCP95
,
Den14
,
Fei05
,
FY04
,
Li09b
]
—for details. Throughout, we consider all varieties over
\mathbb{C}
.

2.1.
Wonderful compactifications of arrangements of subvarieties

Let
Y
be a smooth variety. An
arrangement
of subvarieties of
Y
is a finite collection of smooth subvarieties and that pairwise intersect “cleanly” (see
[
Li09b
, Definition 2.1]
). If

\mathcal{A}=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}\}

is an arrangement, we denote by
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}
the intersection lattice of
\mathcal{A}
; this is the poset of all intersections of subsets of
\mathcal{A}
, ordered by reverse inclusion. In particular, the unique minimal element of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}
is
\hat{0}=Y
, which we view as the empty intersection. By the
complement
of
\mathcal{A}
, we mean

Y^{\circ}:=Y\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}X_{i}.

Some of the subvarieties in
\mathcal{A}
may intersect non-transversally, and the goal of a wonderful compactification of
Y^{\circ}
is to modify the ambient variety
Y
in such a way that the arrangement is replaced by a simple normal crossings divisor. It is not surprising that the way to do so is to perform an iterated blow-up. While one can obtain a wonderful compactification by blowing up at every element of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}
(in a carefully-prescribed order explained below), some subsets of
\mathcal{A}
may already intersect transversally, so one can often obtain a compactification with similar properties by blowing up only at a subset of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}
. The particular subsets that give rise to wonderful compactifications are known as
building sets
; for the precise definition, see
[
Li09b
, Definition 2.2]
. The most important example of a building set for the current work is the
maximal building set

\mathcal{G}:=\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{\widehat{0}\}
, which corresponds to blowing up every intersection of elements of
\mathcal{A}
.

In general, a choice of a building set
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{\widehat{0}\}
gives rise to a
wonderful compactification

\overline{Y}_{\mathcal{G}}
of
Y^{\circ}
in the following way. First, choose an ordering of the elements of
\mathcal{G}
that is compatible with inclusion; that is, let

\mathcal{G}=\{G_{1},\ldots,G_{N}\}

in which
i\leq j
if
G_{i}\subseteq G_{j}
. Then, perform the following sequence of blow-ups:

•

blow up
Y
along
G_{1}
,

•

blow up the result along the proper transform of
G_{2}
,

•

blow up the result along the proper transform of
G_{3}
,

and so on. Then, as shown in
[
Li09b
, Proposition 2.13]
, the wonderful compactificaiton
\overline{Y}_{\mathcal{G}}
is the end result after blowing up along the proper transform of
G_{N}
.

Since the blow-ups that form
\overline{Y}_{\mathcal{G}}
are only at intersections of the subvarieties
X_{i}
, there is an inclusion

Y^{\circ}\hookrightarrow\overline{Y}_{\mathcal{G}},

and we refer to the complement
\overline{Y}_{\mathcal{G}}\setminus Y^{\circ}
as the
boundary
of the wonderful compactification. Among the “wonderful” properties of
\overline{Y}_{\mathcal{G}}
is the extent to which the structure of this boundary is encoded in the combinatorics of
\mathcal{G}
. In particular, the boundary is a union of divisors
D_{G}
for each
G\in\mathcal{G}
, and the intersection
D_{T_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap D_{T_{r}}
is nonempty if and only if
\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{r}\}
forms a
\mathcal{G}
-nested set
. The definition of
\mathcal{G}
-nested set is purely combinatorial and can be stated in a number of equivalent ways (see, for example,
[
Li09b
, Definition 2.3]
or
[
Fei05
, Definition 3.2]
). In the case where
\mathcal{G}
is the maximal building set, a
\mathcal{G}
-nested set is precisely a chain in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{\widehat{0}\}
as a poset.

2.2.
Wonderful compactifications of hyperplane arrangements

In their original work introducing wonderful compactifications
[
DCP95
]
, De Concini and Procesi proved that if
\mathcal{A}
is an arrangement of hyperplanes in projective space, then the cohomology (which is isomorphic to the Chow ring, for example by
[
Kee92
]
) of a wonderful compactification can be read off combinatorially from the lattice
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}
and its building set. Feichtner and Yuzvinsky reinterpreted this calculation in
[
FY04
]
, constructing a fan
\Sigma_{\mathcal{G}}
associated to any lattice
\mathcal{L}
with building set
\mathcal{G}
and proving that, in the case where
\mathcal{L}
is the intersection lattice of a hyperplane arrangement in projective space, the Chow ring of the toric variety
X_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{G}}}
coincides with De Concini–Procesi’s calculation of the Chow ring of the wonderful compactification
\overline{Y}_{\mathcal{G}}
of the complement of
\mathcal{A}
. In this section, we review the parts of this story that are necessary for what follows.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{H^{0},\ldots,H^{r-1}\}
be a collection of hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{k}
. We assume in what follows that
\mathcal{A}
is
essential
, meaning that

\bigcap_{i=0}^{r-1}H^{i}=\emptyset.

In this case, there is an inclusion

i:\mathbb{P}^{k}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{r-1}

under which
H^{0},\ldots,H^{r-1}
map to the coordinate hyperplanes; namely, if
H^{i}=\mathcal{V}(f_{i})
for linear polynomials
f_{i}\in\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{k}]
, then

i(p)=[f_{0}(p):\cdots:f_{r-1}(p)].

It follows that
i
maps the complement

Y^{\circ}=\mathbb{P}^{k}\setminus\bigcup_{i=0}^{r-1}H^{i}

of
\mathcal{A}
into the complement of the coordinate hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{r-1}
, or in other words into the algebraic torus

\mathbb{T}^{r-1}=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{r-1}.

By identifying
Y^{\circ}
with its image under
i
, then, we can view
Y^{\circ}
as a
very affine variety
—that is, a closed subvariety of a torus.

For any building set
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{\hat{0}\}
, one defines the
nested set fan

\Sigma_{\mathcal{G}}
of
(\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}},\mathcal{G})
as follows. First, let

V_{\mathcal{A}}:=\mathbb{R}^{r}/\mathbb{R},

where the quotient is by the diagonal, and denote the images of the standard basis vectors by
e^{0},\ldots,e^{r-1}
. For each
G\in\mathcal{G}
, define

v_{G}:=\sum_{H^{j}\supseteq G}e^{j}\in V_{\mathcal{A}}.

Then
\Sigma_{\mathcal{G}}
is defined as the fan in
V_{\mathcal{A}}
whose cones are

\sigma_{S}:=\text{Cone}\{v_{G}\;|\;G\in S\}\subseteq V_{\mathcal{A}}

for each
\mathcal{G}
-nested set
S\subseteq\mathcal{G}
.

Note that the toric variety
X_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{G}}}
has
\mathbb{T}^{r-1}
as its torus, so in particular, we have

Y^{\circ}\subseteq\mathbb{T}^{r-1}\subseteq X_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{G}}}.

By reinterpreting
\Sigma_{\mathcal{G}}
in terms of a stellar subdivision procedure as in
[
FY04
, Section 6]
(which corresponds to regarding
X_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{G}}}
as an iterated blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{r-1}
), one sees that the wonderful compactification
\overline{Y}_{\mathcal{G}}
is equal to the closure of
Y^{\circ}
inside of
X_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{G}}}
. Moreover, by
[
FY04
, Corollary 2]
, the inclusion

\overline{Y}_{\mathcal{G}}\hookrightarrow X_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{G}}}

is a Chow equivalence. This allows one to give a presentation of
A^{*}(\overline{Y}_{\mathcal{G}})
that can be read off directly from the combinatorics of the lattice
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}
with its building set
\mathcal{G}
.

Remark 2.3
.

The moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
can be obtained as the wonderful compactification of the braid arrangement
\mathcal{A}_{n-2}
(the arrangement of hyperplanes
\{x_{i}=x_{j}\}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n-3}
for all
i\neq j
), with an appropriate choice of building set
[
DCP95
, Section 4.3]
. In this case, the above results lead to an elegant presentation of the Chow ring of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
, as described in
[
Fei05
, Section 4.2]
. Moreover, the nested set fan can be interpreted in this context as the Bergman fan of a particular matroid, or as the moduli space of tropical curves. These results were generalized in
[
CHMR16
]
to all genus-zero Hassett spaces with weight system of “heavy/light” type, leading to a presentation of the Chow ring of such spaces in
[
KKL21
]
.

2.4.
Wonderful compactifications of product arrangements

The case of interest in the current work is the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
, which, as we prove below, is a wonderful compactification of the complement of an arrangement of hyperplanes not in a single projective space but in a product of projective spaces. Although such wonderful compactifications have been constructed via iterated blow-up (through the much more general work of Li Li described above), there is not, to our knowledge, a construction in this setting as the closure inside of a toric variety analogous to
X_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{G}}}
. We prove such a presentation in this subsection, and as a result, we obtain an identification of the Chow ring of such wonderful compactifications with the Chow ring of a toric variety that can be read off combinatorially from the intersection lattice and its building set.

Here, and in what follows, for positive integers
n
and
r
we use the notation

[n]:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}

and

\mathbb{Z}_{r}:=\{0,1,2,\ldots,r-1\}.

We choose these sets to index the hyperplanes in a product arrangement for consistency with the application to
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
that follows.

For each
i\in[n]
, fix positive integers
r_{i}
and
k_{i}
and an essential hyperplane arrangement

(1)

\mathcal{A}_{i}=\{H^{0}_{i},\ldots,H^{r_{i}-1}_{i}\}.

inside
\mathbb{P}^{k_{i}}
. Let
Y_{i}^{\circ}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{k_{i}}
denote the complement of the arrangement
\mathcal{A}_{i}
. Then the product

Y^{\circ}:=Y_{1}^{\circ}\times\cdots\times Y_{n}^{\circ}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{k_{n}}

is also the complement of a hypersurface arrangement: namely, it is the complement of

\mathcal{A}:=\{\widetilde{H}^{j}_{i}\;|\;i\in[n],\;j\in\mathbb{Z}_{r_{i}}\},

in which

\widetilde{H}_{i}^{j}:=p_{i}^{-1}\left(H_{i}^{j}\right)

is the pullback of
H_{i}^{j}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{k_{i}}
under the projection
p_{i}:\mathbb{P}^{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{k_{n}}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{k_{i}}
to the
i
th factor. We refer to
\mathcal{A}
as the
product arrangement
induced by
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{n}
.

Remark 2.5
.

The variety
Y^{\circ}
is very affine, since the embeddings
Y_{i}^{\circ}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{T}^{r_{i}-1}
described in Section
2.2
combine to give

(2)

Y^{\circ}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{T}^{r_{1}-1}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{T}^{r_{n}-1}=\mathbb{T}^{r},

where
r:=r_{1}+\cdots+r_{n}-n
. Moreover,
Y^{\circ}
is linear in the sense of
[
Gro15
]
(that is, it is cut out by linear equations in coordinates on
\mathbb{T}^{r}
), because each factor
Y_{i}^{\circ}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{T}^{r_{i}-1}
is linear. This observation plays a key role in the proof of Theorem
2.10
below.

In fact, for Theorem
2.10
, it is enough to know that
Y^{\circ}
is quasilinear in the sense of
[
Sch21
]
. Schock introduced quasilinear varieties in
[
Sch21
]
as a generalization of linear varieties that retains the key property that, if
Y^{\circ}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{T}
is quasilinear and
\overline{Y}\hookrightarrow X_{\Sigma}
is a “tropical compactification” of
Y^{\circ}
, then
\overline{Y}
is Chow-equivalent to
X_{\Sigma}
. Given that
[
Sch21
, Theorem 6.4]
shows that products of quasilinear varieties are quasilinear, it is immediate from (
2
) that
Y^{\circ}
is quasilinear in our case.

Example 2.6
.

A simple but illustrative example, which is relevant for the application to
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
below, is to take
n=2
and set

\mathcal{A}_{1}=\mathcal{A}_{2}:=\{[1:1],\;[1:-1]\}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{1}.

Then the product arrangement
\mathcal{A}
consists of four hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
:

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle=\{\widetilde{H}_{1}^{0},\;\widetilde{H}_{1}^{1},\;\widetilde{H}_{2}^{0},\;\widetilde{H}_{2}^{1}\}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\{[1:1]\}\times\mathbb{P}^{1},\;\{[1:-1]\}\times\mathbb{P}^{1},\;\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\{[1:1]\},\;\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\{[1:-1]\}\Big\}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}.

In this case, the embeddings
i_{1}:Y_{1}^{\circ}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{T}^{1}
and
i_{2}:Y_{2}^{\circ}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{T}^{1}
are equal and are in fact isomorphisms; indeed, they both come from the embedding (in fact, change of coordinates)
i_{1}=i_{2}:\mathbb{P}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
given by

[x:y]\mapsto[x-y:x+y],

which sends the hyperplanes in
\mathcal{A}_{1}=\mathcal{A}_{2}
to the coordinate hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Thus, the product

i=i_{1}\times i_{2}:\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{T}^{1}

sends
Y^{\circ}
isomorphically to
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{T}^{1}=\mathbb{T}^{2}
.

The intersection lattice
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}
is the product of the lattices
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}
with the product order, and from this one finds two combinatorial consequences that are important in what follows.

Lemma 2.7
.

Fix building sets
\mathcal{G}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{G}_{n}
for the arrangements
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{n}
, respectively. For each
i
, view
\mathcal{G}_{i}
as a subset of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}
by identifying
X\in\mathcal{G}_{i}
with
p_{i}^{-1}(X)\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}
. Then we have the following:

(a)

The union
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{G}_{i}
is a building set for
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}
.

(b)

If
S_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{i}
for each
i
, then

S_{i}\text{ is }\mathcal{G}_{i}\text{-nested for each }i\;\Leftrightarrow\;\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}\text{ is }\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{G}_{i}\right)\text{-nested}.

Proof.

(a)
By the definition of building sets (see, for example,
[
FY04
, Definition 1]

(b)

\mathcal{A}
be a product arrangement induced by arrangements

\mathcal{G}

4.7.
Boundary strata and chains

In order to describe the boundary strata of
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
, we first explain how components of an
(r,n)
-curve are labeled. Let
(C;\sigma;x^{\pm},\{y^{k}\},\{z_{i}^{j}\})
be a stable
(r,n)
-curve, and suppose that
C
has “length”
\ell
in the sense that each of its
r
spokes (chains of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
’s emanating from the central component) consists of
\ell
components. Then, for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{r}
, we denote the components of the spoke containing
y^{k}
by

C^{k}_{1},C^{k}_{2},\ldots,C^{k}_{\ell},

where
y^{k}\in C^{k}_{1}
and the other components are labeled in order from outermost to innermost. We denote the central component by
C_{\ell+1}
.

Given this labeling, the idea of the association of a boundary stratum to a chain is that the outermost components
\{C^{k}_{1}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{r}}
contain the marked points indexed by
I_{1}
(in an order dictated by
\mathfrak{a}
), the next-outermost components
\{C^{k}_{2}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{r}}
contain the marked points indexed by
I_{2}\setminus I_{1}
, and so on, until
[n]\setminus I_{\ell}
, which indexes the marked points on the central component. More precisely, the association is as follows.

Definition 4.8
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}=(I_{1},\ldots,I_{\ell},\mathfrak{a})
be a chain. We say that
(C;\sigma\;x^{\pm},\{y^{k}\},\{z_{i}^{j}\})\in\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
is of type
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}
if
C
is an
r
-pinwheel curve of length
\ell
and, using the above notation, we have

(1)

for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
, the light marked points on
C^{0}_{j}
are precisely

\{z_{i}^{\mathfrak{a}(i)}\;|\;i\in I_{j}\setminus I_{j-1}\},

where
I_{0}:=\emptyset
;

(2)

the light marked points on the central component
C_{\ell+1}
are

\{z_{i}^{k}\;|\;i\in[n]\setminus I_{\ell},\;k\in\mathbb{Z}_{r}\}\cup\{x^{\pm}\}.

We define the boundary stratum
S_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
to be the closure of the locus of curves of type
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}
.

The
(3,4)
-curve of Figure
1
, for example, is a generic element of the boundary stratum
S_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
in which
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}=(I_{1},I_{2},\mathfrak{a})
for

I_{1}=\{3\},\;\;I_{2}=\{2,3,4\}

and
\mathfrak{a}:I_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{3}
given by

\mathfrak{a}(2)=1,\;\;\mathfrak{a}(3)=0,\;\;\mathfrak{a}(4)=2.

Remark 4.9
.

The first condition in Definition
4.8
implies that, for an
(r,n)
-curve of type
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}
, the light marked point
z_{i}^{0}
is on the same spoke of
C
as
y^{-\mathfrak{a}(i)}
. Given that the positions of all other light marked points are determined by the location of the points
z_{i}^{0}
, this helps to explain why
-\mathfrak{a}(i)
appears in the definitions of
H_{\widetilde{I}}
and
\sigma_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
above.

We proved in
[
CDH+21
, Proposition 5.4]
that the association
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}\mapsto S_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
is a bijection from chains to boundary strata in
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
, and that under this bijection, the codimension of
S_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
corresponds to the length of
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}
whereas an inclusion of boundary strata
S_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}\subseteq S_{\widetilde{\mathbf{J}}}
corresponds to the statement that
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}
“refines”
\widetilde{\mathbf{J}}
in the sense of
[
CDH+21
, Definition 4.2]
. In particular, the boundary divisors are associated to chains of length
1
, which are
\mathbb{Z}_{r}
-decorated subsets of
[n]
. We denote by

D_{\widetilde{I}}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}

the boundary divisor corresponding to the decorated set
\widetilde{I}=(I,\mathfrak{a})
.

Now, returning to the fan
\Sigma^{r}_{n}
of Definition
4.4
, for any chain
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}
, denote by
X_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}\subseteq X_{\Sigma^{r}_{n}}
the torus-invariant stratum associated to the cone
\sigma_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
of
\Sigma^{r}_{n}
. Then we have the following correspondence between the strata
X_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
and the strata
S_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
.

Proposition 4.10
.

Under the inclusion
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}\hookrightarrow X_{\Sigma^{r}_{n}}
given by Theorems
2.10
and
3.7
, the pullback of the torus-invariant stratum
X_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
is the boundary stratum
S_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
. In particular, the pullback of the torus-invariant divisor
X_{\widetilde{I}}
is the boundary divisor
D_{\widetilde{I}}
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the claim for divisors, since any torus-invariant stratum (respectively, boundary stratum) is the intersection of the torus-invariant divisors (respectively, boundary divisors) that contain it, and in both cases, the intersection of the stratum indexed by
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}
and the stratum indexed by
\widetilde{\mathbf{J}}
is the stratum indexed by the chain
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}\cap\widetilde{\mathbf{J}}
described in Remark
4.5
. Thus, we fix a decorated set
\widetilde{I}=(I,\mathfrak{a})
and consider the corresponding boundary divisor
D_{\widetilde{I}}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
.

From the last paragraph of the proof of Theorem
3.7
, one can view
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
as an iterated blow-up

\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}=Y_{n}\longrightarrow Y_{n-1}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow Y_{1}\longrightarrow Y_{0}=(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{n},

where
Y_{k+1}
is obtained from
Y_{k}
by blow-up along the proper transform of the locus

W_{k}:=\bigcup_{\widetilde{J}\;\big|\;|J|=n-k}H_{\widetilde{J}}\subseteq(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{n}.

If
E_{\widetilde{I}}\subseteq Y_{n-|I|+1}
denotes the exceptional divisor over
H_{\widetilde{I}}
, then from this perspective,
D_{\widetilde{I}}
is the proper transform in
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
of
E_{\widetilde{I}}
.

On the other hand, one can also view
X_{\Sigma^{r}_{n}}
as an iterated blow-up, by the stellar subdivision perspective of Lemma
2.11
. Namely, let
\Sigma_{r}
be the nested set fan for the arrangement (
13
), as described in Remark
4.6
. Then
\Sigma_{r}
is obtained from the fan for
\mathbb{P}^{r-1}
by removing all but the
1
-dimensional cones, so

X_{\Sigma_{r}}=\mathbb{P}^{r-1}\setminus\bigcup_{j\neq\ell}(\widehat{H}_{j}\cap\widehat{H}_{\ell}),

where
\widehat{H}_{j}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r-1}
denotes the
j
th coordinate hyperplane; in other words, a point of
\mathbb{P}^{r-1}
belongs to
X_{\Sigma^{r}_{n}}
if and only at most one of its coordinates is zero. Thus,

X_{(\Sigma_{r})^{\times n}}=\left(\mathbb{P}^{r-1}\setminus\bigcup_{j\neq\ell}(\widehat{H}_{j}\cap\widehat{H}_{\ell})\right)^{n},

and Lemma
2.11
says that
X_{\Sigma^{r}_{n}}
can be obtained from this variety by an iterated blow-up along the torus-invariant subvarieties
\widehat{H}_{\widetilde{J}}
associated to the cones
\text{Cone}(v_{H_{\widetilde{J}}})
for each nested set
\widetilde{J}=(J,\mathfrak{b})
. Specifically, we have

\widehat{H}_{\widetilde{J}}:=\bigcap_{i\in J}\widehat{H}_{i}^{-\mathfrak{b}(i)},

where
\widehat{H}_{i}^{j}
denotes the pullback of
\widehat{H}_{j}
along the projection of
X_{(\Sigma_{r})^{\times n}}
to the
i
th factor. Thus, we have a sequence of blow-ups

X_{\Sigma^{r}_{n}}=\widehat{Y}_{n}\longrightarrow\widehat{Y}_{n-1}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\widehat{Y}_{1}\longrightarrow\widehat{Y}_{0}=X_{(\Sigma_{r})^{\times n}},

where
\widehat{Y}_{k+1}
is obtained from
\widehat{Y}_{k}
by blow-up along the proper transform of the locus

\widehat{W}_{k}:=\bigcup_{\widetilde{J}\;\big|\;|J|=n-k}\widehat{H}_{\widetilde{J}}\subseteq X_{(\Sigma_{r})^{\times n}}.

This is exactly analogous to the situation for
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
described above, and also as in that situation, if
\widehat{E}_{\widetilde{I}}\subseteq\widehat{Y}_{n-|I|+1}
denotes the exceptional divisor over
\widehat{W}_{\widetilde{I}}
, then the torus-invariant stratum
X_{\widetilde{I}}
is the proper transform of
\widehat{E}_{\widetilde{I}}
in
X_{\Sigma^{r}_{n}}
.

Now, let

i:Y_{0}\hookrightarrow\widehat{Y}_{0}

be the linear inclusion of
(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{n}
into
(\mathbb{P}^{r-1})^{n}
sending the
r
th root of unity
\zeta^{j}
in each factor to the coordinate hyperplane
\widehat{H}_{j}
. Then

W_{\widetilde{I}}=i^{-1}\left(\widehat{W}_{\widetilde{I}}\right),

so the blow-up closure lemma shows that
Y_{n-|I|+1}\hookrightarrow\widehat{Y}_{n-|I|+1}
in such a way that
E_{\widetilde{I}}
is the restriction of
\widehat{E}_{\widetilde{I}}
. Taking proper transforms, then, we see that
D_{\widetilde{I}}
is the restriction of
X_{\widetilde{I}}
, as claimed.
∎

4.12.
Calculation of the Chow ring of
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}

Equipped with the results of the previous subsections, the calculation of
A^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n})
is essentially immediate.

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
n\geq 0
. The Chow ring of
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
is generated by the boundary divisors
D_{\widetilde{I}}
for each decorated subset
\widetilde{I}
of
[n]
, with relations given by

•

D_{\widetilde{I}}\cdot D_{\widetilde{J}}=0
unless either
\widetilde{I}\leq\widetilde{J}
or
\widetilde{J}\leq\widetilde{I}
;

•

for all
i\in[n]
and all
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{r}
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{I}\text{ s.t. }\\
i\in I,\;a(i)=a\end{subarray}}D_{\widetilde{I}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{I}\text{ s.t. }\\
i\in I,\;a(i)=b\end{subarray}}D_{\widetilde{I}}.

Proof.

Theorem
2.10
shows that
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}\hookrightarrow X_{\Sigma^{r}_{n}}
is a Chow equivalence, and standard toric geometry machinery (see, for example,
[
CLS11
]
) calculates the Chow ring of
X_{\Sigma^{r}_{n}}
. Namely, it is generated by the torus-invariant divisors, which correspond to the rays of
\Sigma^{r}_{n}
and are thus of the form
X_{\widetilde{I}}
for each decorated set
\widetilde{I}
. The relations between these generators are given by

(15)

X_{\widetilde{I_{1}}}\cdots X_{\widetilde{I_{k}}}=0\;\text{ if }\;\text{Cone}\{\sigma_{\widetilde{I_{1}}},\ldots,\sigma_{\widetilde{I_{k}}}\}\notin\Sigma^{r}_{n}

and

(16)

\sum_{\widetilde{I}}\langle v,u_{\widetilde{I}}\rangle X_{\widetilde{I}}=0\;\;\text{ for all }v\in(V_{\mathcal{A}})^{\vee},

where

u_{\widetilde{I}}=\sum_{i\in I}e_{i}^{-a(i)}

is the primitive integral generator of
\sigma_{\widetilde{I}}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the natural pairing between
V_{\mathcal{A}}
and
(V_{\mathcal{A}})^{\vee}
.

By the definition of
\Sigma^{r}_{n}
and the result of Proposition
4.10
, the relation (
15
) pulls back to

D_{\widetilde{I_{1}}}\cdots D_{\widetilde{I_{k}}}=0\;\text{ if }\;\{\widetilde{I}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{I}_{k}\}\text{ is not a chain},

which is equivalent to the first relation in the statement of the theorem. In the relation (
16
), we can let
v
range over the dual basis to the basis
\{e_{i}^{j}\}
for
V_{\mathcal{A}}
, where
i\in[n]
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,r-1\}
; note that in this basis, we have

e_{i}^{0}=-e_{i}^{1}-\cdots-e_{i}^{r-1}

by the definition of
V_{\mathcal{A}}
as a quotient. When
v
is dual to
e_{i}^{j}
, the pullback of (
16
) becomes

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{I}\text{ s.t. }\\
i\in I,\;a(i)=-j\end{subarray}}D_{\widetilde{I}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{I}\text{ s.t. }\\
i\in I,\;a(i)=0\end{subarray}}D_{\widetilde{I}}=0.

Varying over all
v
in the dual basis yields the second relation in the statement of the theorem, so the theorem is proved.
∎

3.3
that setting

4.16.
The Betti numbers of
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}

While the computation of
A^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n})
in the previous subsection relies critically on the Chow equivalence with
X_{\Sigma^{r}_{n}}
provided by Theorem
2.10
, one can compute
A^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n})
as an additive group without passing through that theorem. Indeed, in
[
Li09a
]
, Li Li gives a presentation of the Chow groups
A^{*}(\overline{Y}_{\mathcal{G}})
for any wonderful compactification
\overline{Y}_{\mathcal{G}}
. In the case of
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
, that presentation is the following.

First, for any chain
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}=(I_{1},\ldots,I_{\ell},\mathfrak{a})
, set

j(\widetilde{\mathbf{I}})=(j_{1}(\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}),\ldots,j_{\ell}(\widetilde{\mathbf{I}})):=(|I_{1}|,|I_{2}|-|I_{1}|,\ldots,|I_{\ell}|-|I_{\ell-1}|),

which we refer to as the
jump type
of
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}
. Then, define

M_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}:=\{\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}\;|\;1\leq\mu_{i}<j_{i}(\widetilde{\mathbf{I}})\;\text{ for all }i\}.

Note that
M_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
depends only on the jump type of
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}
, and it is nonempty if and only if each entry in
j(\widetilde{\mathbf{I}})
is at least two. In light of this, for any vector
\bf{j}=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{\ell})\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2})^{\ell}
, let

M_{\bf{j}}:=\{\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}\;|\;1\leq\mu_{i}<j_{i}\;\text{ for all }i\},

and let
N_{\bf j}
be the number of chains of jump type
\bf{j}
; explicitly,

N_{\bf j}:=\binom{n}{j_{1},\ldots,j_{\ell}}r^{|\bf{j}|},

where
|\bf{j}|:=j_{1}+\cdots+j_{\ell}
. Then the presentation of the Chow groups
A^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n})
is the following.

Theorem 4.17
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
, there is an isomorphism of additive groups

A^{k}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n})\cong A^{k}((\mathbb{P}^{1})^{n})\oplus\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
\bf{j}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2})^{\ell}\end{subarray}}\left(\bigoplus_{\mu\in M_{\bf{j}}}A^{k-|\mu|}\left((\mathbb{P}^{1})^{n-|\bf{j}|}\right)\right)^{\oplus N_{\bf{j}}}.

Proof.

This is a direct application of
[
Li09a
, Theorem 3.1]
. The sum over
\mathcal{G}
-nested sets
\mathcal{T}
in that theorem becomes a sum over chains
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}
, and (after correcting the typo that
\{\mu_{G}\}_{G\in\mathcal{G}}
should be
\{\mu_{G}\}_{G\in\mathcal{T}}
in
[
Li09a
, page 9]
) the set
M_{\mathcal{T}}
becomes the set
M_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
. The space
Y_{0}\mathcal{T}
in that theorem is the minimal subvariety (under inclusion) in the chain
\mathcal{T}
, which in our case is

H_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}:=\sum_{i\in I_{\ell}}H_{i}^{\mathfrak{a}(i)}\cong(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{n-|I_{\ell}|}.

Since
|I_{\ell}|=|\bf{j}|
for any chain
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}
of jump type
\bf{j}
, the above isomorphism follows.
∎

5.
Connection to tropical curves with cyclic action

We have now seen that the nested set fan
\Sigma^{r}_{n}
given by Definition
4.4
yields a toric variety whose Chow ring is isomorphic to
A^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n})
. This fan has another interpretation, however: its support can be identified with the moduli space of “tropical
(r,n)
-curves,” and under this identification, the subdivision of
|\Sigma^{r}_{n}|
into cones coincides with the stratification of the tropical moduli space by analogues of boundary strata. The goal of this section is to prove these assertions. As a consequence, we also find a new interpretation of the polytopal complex
\Delta^{r}_{n}
introduced in
[
CDH+21
]
.

5.1.
The fan
\Sigma^{r}_{n}
as the tropical moduli space

Recall that the dual graph of an element of
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
is a combinatorial graph with a vertex for each irreducible component of the underlying curve, an edge for each node, and a half-edge for each marked point (see
[
CDH+21
, Definition 2.9]
). If
\Gamma
is the dual graph of an element
(C;\sigma;x^{\pm},\{y^{\ell}\},\{z_{i}^{j}\})
of
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
, then
\sigma
induces a unique automorphism
\sigma_{\Gamma}
of
\Gamma
. Given this, tropical
(r,n)
-curves are defined as follows.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
n\geq 0
and
r\geq 2
. A
tropical
(r,n)
-curve
is a triple
(\Gamma,\sigma_{\Gamma},L)
, where
\Gamma
is the dual graph of an element
(C;\sigma;x^{\pm},\{y^{\ell}\},\{z_{i}^{j}\})
in
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
,
\sigma_{\Gamma}
is the unique automorphism on the graph
\Gamma
determined by
\sigma
, and

L:E(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}

is a “length” function on the edges of
\Gamma
such that

L(e)=L(\sigma_{\Gamma}(e))

for all
e\in E(\Gamma)
.

We denote by
L^{r,\text{trop}}_{n}
the set of all tropical
(r,n)
-curves. Our goal, now, is to identify
L^{r,\text{trop}}_{n}
with
|\Sigma^{r}_{n}|
. In particular, the cones of
\Sigma^{r}_{n}
will be identified with subsets of
L^{r,\text{trop}}_{n}
, and in order to do so, we recall from Remark
4.11
that the cones of
\Sigma^{r}_{n}
are in inclusion-reversing bijection with the boundary strata
S_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
of
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
. Thus, for any chain
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}
, we define
T_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}\subseteq L^{r,\text{trop}}_{n}
as the subset consisting of tropical curves
(\Gamma,L)
where the boundary stratum with dual graph
\Gamma
contains
S_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
. More explicitly, if
\Gamma_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
denotes the dual graph of a curve of type
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}
(as in Definition
4.8
), we have

T_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}:=\{(\Gamma,L)\in L^{r,\text{trop}}_{n}\;|\;\Gamma\text{ is obtained from }\Gamma_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}\text{ by contracting edges}\}.

Given this definition, we can state the correspondence between
L^{r,\text{trop}}_{n}
and
|\Sigma^{r}_{n}|
as follows.

Proposition 5.3
.

There is a natural bijection between
L^{r,\text{trop}}_{n}
and
|\Sigma^{r}_{n}|
, under which the subset
T_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
corresponds to the cone
\sigma_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
.

Proof.

Recall from Remark
4.6
that
\Sigma_{r}^{n}
is obtained by stellar subdivision from the fan
\Sigma_{r}^{\times n}
, where
\Sigma_{r}
is the
1
-dimensional fan in
\mathbb{R}^{r}/\mathbb{R}
with
r
rays, one spanned by the image of each of the standard basis vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{r}
. Thus, one has

(17)

|\Sigma^{r}_{n}|=|\Sigma_{r}|^{\times n}=\{x_{1}e_{1}^{a_{1}}+\cdots+x_{n}e_{n}^{a_{n}}\;|\;a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{r},\;x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}\;\text{ for all }i\}\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{r}/\mathbb{R})^{\oplus n}.

In order to identify
L^{r,\text{trop}}_{n}
with this set, we associate to each
(\Gamma,L)\in L^{r,\text{trop}}_{n}
a point in
(\mathbb{R}^{r}/\mathbb{R})^{\oplus n}
. Specifically, let
L_{i}
denote the total length of the edges of
\Gamma
in a path from the central vertex to the vertex containing
z^{0}_{i}
, and assuming
L_{i}\neq 0
, define
\ell_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{r}
by the condition that
z^{0}_{i}
is on the same spoke as
y^{\ell_{i}}
. Then we identify
(\Gamma,L)\in L^{r,\text{trop}}_{n}
with the point

\sum_{i\;|\;L_{i}\neq 0}L_{i}e_{i}^{\ell_{i}}\in(\mathbb{R}^{r}/\mathbb{R})^{\oplus n}.

Given that
L_{i}
varies over all nonnegative real numbers and
\ell_{i}
varies over all elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{r}
, the image of
L^{r,\text{trop}}_{n}
under this identification is precisely the set (
17
).

To understand the image of
T_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
under this identification, recall that in the dual graph
\Gamma_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
of a generic element of
S_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
, the marked points
z_{i}^{j}
with
i\in I_{1}
are on the outermost vertices, so in the image of a tropical curve
(\Gamma_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}},L)
, the
L_{i}
with
i\in I_{1}
are equal and largest among all
L_{i}
. Similarly, the marked points with
z_{i}^{j}
with
i\in I_{2}\setminus I_{1}
are on the next-to-outermost vertices, so the
L_{i}
with
i\in I_{2}\setminus I_{1}
are equal and next-largest. This continues until the marked points
z_{i}^{j}
with
i\in[n]\setminus I_{\ell}
, which are on the central vertex, so
L_{i}=0
for
i\in[n]\setminus I_{\ell}
. It follows that the set
T_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}\subseteq L^{r,\text{trop}}_{n}
corresponds under the above identification to the set of points

L_{1}e_{1}^{-a(1)}+\cdots+L_{n}e_{n}^{-a(n)}\in(\mathbb{R}^{r}/\mathbb{R})^{\oplus n}

for which
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}
satisfy the following conditions:

•

if
i,i^{\prime}\in I_{j}\setminus I_{j-1}
for some
j
, then
L_{i}=L_{i^{\prime}}
;

•

if
i_{1}\in I_{1},i_{2}\in I_{2},\ldots,i_{\ell}\in I_{\ell}
, then

L_{i_{1}}\geq L_{i_{2}}\geq\cdots\geq L_{i_{\ell}};

•

if
i\in[n]\setminus I_{\ell}
, then
L_{i}=0
.

To see that this set coincides with
\sigma_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
, recall from Definition
4.4
that

\displaystyle\sigma_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}

\displaystyle:=\text{Cone}\left\{\sum_{i\in I_{1}}e_{i}^{-a(i)},\ldots,\sum_{i\in I_{\ell}}e_{i}^{-a(i)}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left.c_{1}\sum_{i\in I_{1}}e_{i}^{-a(i)}+\cdots+c_{\ell}\sum_{i\in I_{\ell}}e_{i}^{-a(i)}\;\right|\;c_{1},\ldots,c_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}\right\}.

Collecting the terms in a different way and using that
I_{1}\subseteq I_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq I_{\ell}
, an arbitrary point in
\sigma_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
can be expressed as

\sum_{i\in I_{1}}(c_{1}+\cdots+c_{\ell})e_{i}^{-a(i)}+\sum_{i\in I_{2}\setminus I_{1}}(c_{2}+\cdots+c_{\ell})e_{i}^{-a(i)}+\cdots+\sum_{i\in I_{\ell}\setminus I_{\ell-1}}c_{\ell}\;e_{i}^{-a(i)}

for
c_{1},\ldots,c_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}
. Thus, the coefficient on
e_{i}^{-a(i)}
for any
i\in I_{1}
is the same, and these are the largest coefficients; the coefficients on
e_{i}^{-a(i)}
for any
i\in I_{2}\setminus I_{1}
are the same, and these are the next-largest coefficients; and so on. This is precisely the set of points satisfying the conditions mentioned above, so the identification of
\sigma_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
with
T_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
is complete.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Aside from the direct definition of
L^{r,\text{trop}}_{n}
given above, there is another sense in which one might “tropicalize” the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
. Namely, one can embed
\mathcal{L}^{r}_{n}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{T}^{r}
as a closed subvariety (as in Remark
2.5
), and as such there is an associated geometric tropicalization
\text{Trop}(\mathcal{L}^{r}_{n})
in the sense of
[
HKT09
]
. To see that these two notions of the tropical moduli space coincide, recall from the proof of Theorem
2.10
that
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}\subseteq X_{\Sigma^{r}_{n}}
is a tropical compactification, meaning in particular that

|\Sigma^{r}_{n}|=\text{Trop}(\mathcal{L}^{r}_{n}).

Combining this with Proposition
5.3
gives an identification

L^{r,\text{trop}}_{n}=\text{Trop}(\mathcal{L}^{r}_{n}).

5.5.
The polytopal complex
\Delta^{r}_{n}
as a normal complex of
\Sigma^{r}_{n}

The results of the previous subsection generalize the situation for Losev–Manin space
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{n}
, which—as explained in
[
CDH+21
]
—is “morally” the
r=1
case of the spaces
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
. In particular, Losev and Manin showed in
[
LM00
]
that
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{n}
is a toric variety whose associated fan can be identified with the tropical moduli space
L_{n}^{\text{trop}}
. Because this is a complete fan, though, one can also view the connection in terms of polytopes: namely, the normal polytope to
L_{n}^{\text{trop}}
is the polytope of
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{n}
as a toric variety, meaning that its faces are identified with the torus-invariant strata. In fact, this normal polytope is the
(n-1)
-dimensional permutohedron
\Pi_{n}
, and the torus-invariant strata are precisely the boundary strata, so one obtains an identification between the faces of
\Pi_{n}
and the boundary strata in
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{n}
.

In the case of
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
, the moduli space itself is not toric but sits inside of (and is Chow-equivalent to) the toric variety
X_{\Sigma^{r}_{n}}
whose fan we have now identified with
L^{r,\text{trop}}_{n}
. However,
\Sigma^{r}_{n}
is not a complete fan in
(\mathbb{R}^{r}/\mathbb{R})^{\oplus n}
for
r>2
, so the usual construction of the normal polytope does not apply; it produces a polytope, but one of larger dimension than
|\Sigma^{r}_{n}|
. There is a substitute for the normal polytope for non-complete fans, though, which is the “normal complex” introduced by Nathanson–Ross
[
NR21
]
. This is a polytopal complex that one can view as the result of truncating
\Sigma^{r}_{n}
by normal hyperplanes. To complete the analogy to Losev–Manin space, then, one would hope to identify the faces of this normal complex—for an appropriate interpretation of “faces” of a polytopal complex—with the boundary strata in
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
.

In our previous work
[
CDH+21
]
, we have already identified the boundary strata in
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{n}
with the “
\Delta
-faces” of another polytopal complex
\Delta^{r}_{n}
. This polytopal complex was constructed as a subset of

(\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}\cdot\mu_{r})^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n},

where
\mu_{r}
denotes the set of
r
th roots of unity. However, we can identify

(\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}\cdot\mu_{r})^{n}\leftrightarrow|\Sigma^{r}_{n}|

by identifying

\left(x_{1}\zeta^{a_{1}},\ldots,x_{n}\zeta^{a_{n}}\right)\leftrightarrow x_{1}e_{1}^{a_{1}}+\cdots+x_{n}e_{n}^{a_{n}},

and using this, we can view
\Delta^{r}_{n}
as a subset of
(\mathbb{R}^{r}/\mathbb{R})^{\oplus n}
. Explicitly,

(18)

\Delta^{r}_{n}:=\bigcup_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{r}}\left\{x_{1}e_{1}^{a_{1}}+\cdots+x_{n}e_{n}^{a_{n}}\;\left|\;x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}\;\text{ for all }i,\;\sum_{i\in I}x_{i}\leq\delta^{n}_{|I|}\;\text{ for all }I\subseteq[n]\right.\right\},

where

\delta^{n}_{k}:=n+(n-1)+(n-2)+\cdots+(n-k+1).

We claim that this complex
\Delta^{r}_{n}
is the normal complex of
\Sigma^{r}_{n}
. (In the case
r=n=2
, the fan
\Sigma^{2}_{2}
is the complete fan shown in Figure
5
, whose normal complex is in fact a normal polytope: the octagon, which is the signed permutohedron when
n=2
and equals
\Delta^{2}_{2}
. In the case
r=3
and
n=2
, we illustrate the claim in Figure
6
.)

e_{1}^{0}\times\Sigma_{3}
0\times e^{1}_{2}
0\times e^{2}_{2}
0\times e^{0}_{2}
e_{1}^{0}\times 0
e_{1}^{0}\times e^{2}_{2}

portion of
\Sigma^{3}_{2}

\Delta^{3}_{2}

.
The fan
\Sigma_{2}^{3}
is obtained as stellar subdivision of
\Sigma_{3}\times\Sigma_{3}
, so we can obtain a portion of it by stellar subdivision of
e_{1}^{0}\times\Sigma_{3}
, as shown in the middle figure. Taking the dual complex to this fan, we recover a portion of the complex
\Delta^{3}_{2}
illustrated in

.

More precisely, normal complexes of fans depend on three choices: an inner product on the ambient vector space, a vector
\vec{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma^{r}_{n}(1)}
, and a distinguished generator
u_{\rho}
of each ray
\rho\in\Sigma^{r}_{n}(1)
. The inner product in our case is the dot product on
(\mathbb{R}^{r}/\mathbb{R})^{\oplus n}
in the basis
\{e_{i}^{j}\}_{i\in[n],j\in[r-1]}
, which we denote by
\ast
. As for the vector
\vec{z}
, since the rays of
\Sigma^{r}_{n}
are the cones
\sigma_{\widetilde{I}}
for each decorated set
\tilde{I}
, we can define
\vec{z}
by setting

(19)

z_{\tilde{I}}:=\delta^{n}_{|I|}

for each decorated set
\widetilde{I}
. Finally, for the generator of the ray associated to
\widetilde{I}
, we choose

(20)

u_{\widetilde{I}}:=\sum_{i\in I}e_{i}^{-a(i)}.

Equipped with this notation, the final perspective we present on the fan
\Sigma^{r}_{n}
is the following.

Proposition 5.6
.

The polytopal complex
\Delta^{r}_{n}
is the normal complex of the fan
\Sigma^{r}_{n}
with respect to the inner product
\ast
, the vector
\vec{z}
defined by (
19
), and the ray generators defined by (
20
).

Proof.

To define the normal complex of
\Sigma^{r}_{n}
, one first truncates all faces by normal hyperplanes. Explicitly, for each face
\sigma_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
of
\Sigma^{r}_{n}
, let

P_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}:=\sigma_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}\cap\{v\in(\mathbb{R}^{r}/\mathbb{R})^{\oplus n}\;|\;v\ast u_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}\leq z_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}\;\text{ for all }\rho\in\sigma_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}(1)\}.

Then the normal complex, by definition, is the union of all faces of the polytopes
P_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
, over all cones
\sigma_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
of
\Sigma^{r}_{n}
. Because we include faces in this union, it suffices to consider only maximal cones, which are those associated to chains
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}=(I_{1},\ldots,I_{n};\mathfrak{a})
of length
n
. For such chains, we have

\sigma_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}=\{x_{1}e_{1}^{-\mathfrak{a}(1)}+\cdots+x_{n}e_{n}^{-\mathfrak{a}(n)}\;|\;x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}\;\text{ for all }i\},

and the rays
\rho\in\sigma_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}(1)
are the cones generated by
u_{(I_{j},\mathfrak{a}|_{I_{j}})}
for
j\in[n]
. Thus, the inequalities in the definition of
P_{\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}}
amount to the condition that

\sum_{i\in I_{j}}x_{i}\leq\delta^{n}_{|I_{j}|}

for all
j\in[n]
. As
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}
ranges over all maximal chains, the exponents
\mathfrak{a}(i)
range over all elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{r}
and the sets
I_{j}
range over all subsets of
[n]
, so the normal complex precisely coincides with the set
\Delta^{r}_{n}
of (
18
).
∎

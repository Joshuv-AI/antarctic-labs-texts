Symplectic geometry of Teichmüller spaces for surfaces with ideal boundary
Anton Alekseev, Eckhard Meinrenken

Abstract
. A hyperbolic 0-metric on a surface with boundary is a hyperbolic metric on its interior, exhibiting the boundary behavior of the standard metric on the Poincaré disk. Consider the infinite-dimensional Teichmüller spaces of hyperbolic 0-metrics on oriented surfaces with boundary, up to diffeomorphisms fixing the boundary and homotopic to the identity. We show that these spaces have natural symplectic structures, depending only on the choice of an invariant metric on 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 , ℝ ) \mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R}) . We prove that these Teichmüller spaces are Hamiltonian Virasoro spaces for the action of the universal cover of the group of diffeomorphisms of the boundary. We give an explicit formula for the Hill potential on the boundary defining the moment map. Furthermore, using Fenchel-Nielsen parameters we prove a Wolpert formula for the symplectic form, leading to global Darboux coordinates on the Teichmüller space.



1.
Introduction

A hyperbolic structure on a compact, oriented surface

\Sigma
without boundary may be described by an atlas with oriented charts taking values in the Poincaré disk
\mathbb{D}
, with constant transition functions given by orientation preserving isometries of
\mathbb{D}
. The same definition may be used for surfaces
\Sigma

with boundary
, using as the model space the
closed
Poincaré disk
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. Given a hyperbolic structure, the interior of the surface acquires a
hyperbolic metric, exhibiting the same boundary behaviour as the standard metric on the
Poincaré disk. Metrics of this type are known as
conformally compact hyperbolic metrics
or
hyperbolic 0-metrics
.
The boundary components are regarded as a boundary at infinity, called
ideal boundary
. One pictures
\Sigma
as a surface with funnel ends, also known as
trumpets
:

In this paper, we consider the Teichmüller space of hyperbolic structures,

\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)=\operatorname{Hyp}(\Sigma)/\phantom{\cdot}^{0}\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\Sigma),

where
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{0}\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\Sigma)
are the diffeomorphisms fixing the boundary and isotopic to the identity. If
\partial\Sigma\neq\emptyset
, the space
\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)
is infinite-dimensional. It has a
residual action of the universal cover of the group
\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\partial{\Sigma})
of orientation preserving diffeomorphisms of the boundary,
and of the mapping class group
\operatorname{MCG}(\Sigma)
.
The infinite-dimensional Teichmüller space, and corresponding Riemann moduli space
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)=\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)/\operatorname{MCG}(\Sigma)
, have been
studied in the literature from the perspective of complex geometry and quasi-conformal mappings. See for example
Bers
[
5
, Section 19]
, Thurston
[
34
, Remark 4.6.17]
, and the work of Takhtajan-Teo
[
32
,
33
]
; see Schippers-Staubach
[
28
]
for a comprehensive overview. However, it appears that the symplectic aspects of this
space have been neglected. We will show:

Theorem A.
The space
\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)
has a natural (weak) symplectic structure, invariant under the action of the mapping class group and of the
universal cover of
\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\partial{\Sigma})
.

The action of the universal cover of
\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\partial{\Sigma})
admits a moment map, turning
\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)
into a Hamiltonian Virasoro space. Recall that
the Virasoro Lie algebra
\mathfrak{vir}(\partial\Sigma)
is a central extension of the Lie algebra
\operatorname{Vect}(\partial{\Sigma})
of vector fields on the boundary.
Its smooth dual space comes with a map to
\mathbb{R}
, and the affine hyperplane

\mathfrak{vir}(\partial{\Sigma})^{*}_{1}

at level
1
is identified with the space
\operatorname{Hill}(\partial{\Sigma})
of Hill operators on the boundary.
Given a local coordinate
x
on the boundary,
Hill operators are of the form
L=\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+T
for a Hill potential
T(x)
.
The action of diffeomorphisms on Hill potentials
T(x)
is given by the expression

({\mathsf{F}}^{-1}.T)(x)={\mathsf{F}}^{\prime}(x)^{2}\,T({\mathsf{F}}(x))+{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{S}({\mathsf{F}})(x)

involving the
Schwarzian derivative

\mathcal{S}
.

Theorem B.

The action of the universal cover of
\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\partial{\Sigma})
on
\operatorname{Teich}(\partial{\Sigma})
is Hamiltonian, with a canonically defined equivariant moment map

\Phi\colon\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)\to\operatorname{Hill}(\partial{\Sigma})^{-}
.

Here
\operatorname{Hill}(\partial{\Sigma})^{-}=\mathfrak{vir}(\partial{\Sigma})^{*}_{-1}
is the affine subspace at level
-1
(consisting of
all
-L
where
L
is a Hill operator).
The quickest description of the moment map is based on the observation that a hyperbolic structure on
\Sigma
determines a projective structure on the boundary (by restricting the
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
-valued charts of
\Sigma
to the boundary). As is well-known (see e.g.
[
25
]
), projective structures on oriented 1-manifolds are equivalent to Hill operators. The moment map takes
[\mathsf{g}]
to
-L
where
L
is the Hill operator for the projective structure. For an explicit description, choose a local coordinate

x
on the boundary, and complete to a local coordinate system
x,y
where
y
is a boundary defining function.
Let
a(x)>0

be the positive function obtained from the most singular part of the
Riemannian volume form:
{\mathsf{d}}\operatorname{vol}_{\mathsf{g}}=y^{-2}a(x){\mathsf{d}}x\wedge{\mathsf{d}}y+O(y^{-1})
. Let
k(x,y)
be the geodesic curvature
of the curve
(x,y)\mapsto(x+t,y)
; one finds
k(x,y)=1+c(x)y^{2}+O(y^{3})
.

Theorem C.

The boundary Hill potential for the hyperbolic 0-metric
\mathsf{g}
is given by

T={\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{a^{\prime\prime}}{a}-\frac{3}{2}\Big(\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\Big)^{2}\right)+\frac{a^{2}}{2}c.

If
\Sigma
has negative Euler characteristic, we may use a pants decomposition
to obtain a Fenchel-Nielsen parametrization

\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)=(\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\mathbb{R})^{3g-3+r}\times(\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\widetilde{\operatorname{Diff}}_{\mathsf{o}}(S^{1}))^{r}.

Let
(\ell_{i},\tau_{i})
be the length and twist parameters for the first
3g-3+r
factors (corresponding to gluing circles), and
\ell_{j},{\mathsf{F}}_{j}
the parameters for the last
r
factors (corresponding to trumpet ends). We have the following version of Wolpert’s formula for the symplectic form (Theorems
8.3
and Theorem
8.2
):

Theorem D.

In Fenchel-Nielsen parameters, the symplectic form on
\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)
is given by

\omega={\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{3g-3+r}{\mathsf{d}}\ell_{i}\wedge{\mathsf{d}}\tau_{i}-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{r}{\mathsf{d}}\int_{S^{1}}\big(\ell_{j}^{2}{\mathsf{F}}^{\prime}_{j}\,{\mathsf{d}}{\mathsf{F}}_{j}+({\mathsf{F}}^{\prime}_{j})^{-1}{\mathsf{d}}{\mathsf{F}}^{\prime\prime}_{j}\big)

Here
{\mathsf{d}}
is the exterior differential on the diffeomorphism group, and
′
denotes a derivative in the
S^{1}
-direction.

The terms in the second sum are symplectic forms on the factors
\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\widetilde{\operatorname{Diff}}_{\mathsf{o}}(S^{1})
, which may be interpreted as moduli spaces for the trumpet (with one geodesic end and one ideal boundary). We shall show (Proposition
8.5
) how to introduce global Darboux coordinates for the trumpet moduli space, resulting in global Darboux coordinates for the space
\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)
.

One of the motivations for this work are recent developments in the physics literature on Jackiw-Teitelboim gravity, notably the articles by Saad-Shenker-Stanford
[
27
]
, Maldacena-Stanford-Yang
[
20
]
, Cotler-Jensen-Maloney
[
7
]
,
and Stanford-Witten
[
31
]
. The discussion in these articles involves hyperbolic surfaces with funnel ends (‘trumpets’),
using cut-off along ‘wiggly boundaries’ to create surfaces of finite volume, leading to a theory governed by a
Schwarzian action
. As shown by the physicists, this relates JT gravity to mathematical concepts such as the Mirzakhani recursion formulas for Weil-Petersson volumes, topological recursion and random matrix theory, Duistermaat-Heckman theory for Virasoro coadjoint orbits, and more.

A second motivation is our program to develop a theory of Hamiltonian Virasoro spaces, analogous to the theory of Hamiltonian loop group spaces
[
23
]
. An important example of such a space is the infinite dimensional moduli space

\mathcal{M}_{G}(\Sigma)=\frac{\{A\in\Omega^{1}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{g})|\ {\mathsf{d}}A+{\frac{1}{2}}[A,A]=0\}}{\{g\colon\Sigma\to G\colon g|_{\partial\Sigma}=e\}}

of flat
G
-connections, where
G
is a simply connected Lie group with an invariant metric on its Lie algebra. This space has a symplectic form given by reduction, and the residual
action of
\operatorname{Map}(\partial\Sigma,G)
is Hamiltonian, with affine moment map given by the pullback of the connection,
[A]\mapsto\iota_{\partial{\Sigma}}^{*}A
. It is natural to have a similar example for the Virasoro setting; in fact the two situations are related by Drinfeld-Sokolov reduction.
As shown in
[
2
]
, Hamiltonian loop group spaces with proper moment map are equivalent to finite-dimensional quasi-Hamiltonian
G
-spaces; the results of
[
3
]
pave the way for a similar correspondence for Hamiltonian Virasoro spaces.

Let us briefly summarize our construction of the symplectic form on
\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)
.
By a famous result of Goldman
[
13
]
and Hitchin
[
16
]
, the
Teichmüller space for a surface
\Sigma

without boundary
, of genus
\mathsf{g}\geq 2
, is a moduli space
\mathcal{A}_{\operatorname{flat}}(P)/\operatorname{Gau}(P)
of flat connections on a principal
G
-bundle
P\to\Sigma
of Euler number
2\mathsf{g}-2
. In particular, the Weil-Petersson symplectic form
is obtained by reduction of the Atiyah-Bott symplectic structure on the space of connections. There does not seem to be an immediate generalization of this result to the case
of non-empty boundary. Instead, motivated by the theory of geometric structures
[
14
]

we take as our starting point is a principal
G
-bundle
P\to\Sigma
together with a
G
-equivariant morphism

\sigma\colon P\to\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, called a
developing section
. For suitable choice of
(P,\sigma)
, we define

\widehat{\operatorname{Teich}}(\Sigma)=\mathcal{A}_{\operatorname{flat}}^{\operatorname{pos}}(P)/\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,\partial P,\sigma),

the quotient of the space of flat connections satisfying a certain positivity condition with respect to
\sigma
, by the identity component of automorphisms preserving
\sigma
and
trivial along the
boundary. (This space may be interpreted as elements of
\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)
together with
developing sections for the projective structure on the boundary.) We show that this space is a symplectic quotient for the Atiyah-Bott symplectic structure on
\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}(P)
.
It comes with a residual action of
\operatorname{Gau}(\partial P,\partial\sigma)
, the gauge transformations of
\partial P=P|_{\partial{\Sigma}}
preserving
\partial\sigma=\sigma|_{\partial P}
. We prove that the latter action
is Hamiltonian, with moment map image a single coadjoint orbit
\mathcal{O}
, and

\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)=\big(\widehat{\operatorname{Teich}}(\Sigma)\times\mathcal{O}^{-}\big)/\kern-3.01385pt/\operatorname{Gau}(\partial P,\partial\sigma)

(a symplectic reduction). This defines the symplectic structure on
\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)
(Theorem A). The moment map (Theorem B) is obtained by explicit calculation, beginning with the moment map for the action of the full group of automorphisms on the space
\mathcal{A}(P)
. The relevant background material on the Atiyah-Bott construction is provided in the appendix.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section 2, we recall basic definitions and properties of hyperbolic structures on surfaces with boundary,
and introduce infinite dimensional Teichmüller spaces
{\rm Teich}(\Sigma)
. In Section 3, using the moving coframe formalism of É. Cartan, we describe the relation between hyperbolic metrics and flat
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
connection 1-forms. We prove local normal forms for coframes; as a by-product this gives a new proof of the local normal form for hyperbolic 0-metrics near the ideal boundary. In Section 4, we use a more global approach, considering principal
{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
bundles
P\to\Sigma
equipped with a developing section
\sigma
. For suitable choice of
P
, we exhibit
\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)
as a space of
\sigma
-positive flat connections modulo a subgroup of bundle automorphisms preserving
\sigma
. Section 5 is devoted to a detailed study of this group of automorphisms. In Section 6, we apply the Atiyah-Bott construction to the space of positive connections, and explain how to obtain
{\rm Teich}(\Sigma)
by reduction.
In Section 7, we prove that the diffeomorphisms of the boundary act on
{\rm Teich}(\Sigma)
, that this action is Hamiltonian, and that it corresponds to the Virasoro central extension of the diffeomorphism group. In Section 8, we give an explicit description of the symplectic structure in Fenchel-Nielsen parameters, and give a construction of Darboux coordinates on
{\rm Teich}(\Sigma)
which combines the classical Wolpert formula
[
37
]
with the construction of Darboux coordinates on hyperbolic Virasoro coadjoint orbits
[
1
]
.

2.
Hyperbolic structures on surfaces with boundary

2.1.
Hyperbolic and projective structures

The model space for hyperbolic structures on surfaces
\Sigma
without boundary is the Poincaré disk

\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}|\ |z|<1\},

with the action of
G=\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
by Möbius transformations. The
G
-action on
\mathbb{D}
extends to the closed Poincaré disk

\overline{\mathbb{D}}
; this will be our model space for surfaces with boundary:

Definition 2.1
.

A
hyperbolic structure
on an oriented surface
\Sigma
with boundary
\partial\Sigma
is an oriented atlas with
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
-valued charts, with constant transition maps given by elements of
G
. The space of all hyperbolic structures on
\Sigma
will be denoted

\operatorname{Hyp}(\Sigma)
.

Remark 2.2
.

A hyperbolic structure on
\Sigma
pulls back to a hyperbolic structure on every covering space of
\Sigma
.
If
\Sigma
is compact and connected, then the universal covering space is of the form
\overline{\mathbb{D}}-\mathfrak{L}
where
\mathfrak{L}\subseteq\partial\mathbb{D}
is a set of
limit points
, and

(1)

\Sigma=(\overline{\mathbb{D}}-\mathfrak{L})/\Gamma

where
\Gamma\subseteq G
is a Fuchsian group of Schottky type. See
[
6
]
or
[
30
]
.

In a similar way, taking the boundary of the Poincaré disk as the model space for projective structures, we define:

Definition 2.3
.

A
projective structure
on an oriented 1-manifold
\mathsf{C}
(without boundary) is an oriented atlas with
\partial\mathbb{D}
-valued charts, with constant transition maps given by elements of
G
. The space of all projective structures on
\mathsf{C}
will be denoted

\operatorname{Proj}(\mathsf{C})
.

A hyperbolic structure on
\Sigma
determines a projective structure on the boundary – one simply restricts the
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
-valued charts. This gives a canonical map

(2)

\operatorname{Hyp}(\Sigma)\to\operatorname{Proj}(\partial{\Sigma}).

2.2.
Hyperbolic 0-metrics

The
G=\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
-action on the Poincaré disk preserves the Poincaré metric, written in polar coordinates
z=re^{i\varphi}
as

(3)

\frac{4}{(1-r^{2})^{2}}({\mathsf{d}}r^{2}+r^{2}{\mathsf{d}}\varphi^{2}).

Hence, a hyperbolic structure on
\Sigma
determines a Riemannian metric
\mathsf{g}
on the interior
\operatorname{int}(\Sigma)
, by pulling back (
3
) under the coordinate charts. At the boundary
\partial\Sigma
, this metric becomes singular in the same way as the metric on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
: letting
\varrho
be any boundary defining function, the product
\varrho^{2}\mathsf{g}
extends to an ordinary metric on
\Sigma
.
Riemannian metrics with this property may be seen as ordinary Euclidean metrics on the
0-tangent bundle
of Mazzeo-Melrose
[
21
,
22
]
, i.e., the Lie algebroid

\phantom{\cdot}{}^{0}T\Sigma\to\Sigma

whose sections are the vector fields on
\Sigma
that vanish along the boundary. A given

0-metric
is called hyperbolic if its restriction to the interior is
hyperbolic in the sense that it has Gauss curvature
K_{\mathsf{g}}=-1
. Theorem
3.9
below says that all hyperbolic 0-metrics
arise from hyperbolic structures as in Definition
2.1
.

2.3.
Teichmüller spaces

For groups of diffeomorphisms of a compact, oriented manifold, we use a subscript
+
to indicate
diffeomorphisms preserving orientation, and subscript
\mathsf{o}
to indicate diffeomorphisms isotopic to the identity.
We recall that the universal cover of
\operatorname{Diff}_{+}(S^{1})=\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(S^{1})
is identified with
\mathbb{Z}
-equivariant diffeomorphisms of
\mathbb{R}
.

Let
\Sigma
be compact, connected, and oriented. Denote by
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}\operatorname{Diff}(\Sigma)
the diffeomorphisms preserving the boundary, and by
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{0}\operatorname{Diff}(\Sigma)
the subgroup of diffeomorphisms fixing the boundary pointwise. We hence have subgroups

(4)

\phantom{\cdot}^{b}\operatorname{Diff}(\Sigma)\supseteq\!\phantom{\cdot}^{b}\operatorname{Diff}_{+}(\Sigma)\supseteq\!\phantom{\cdot}^{b}\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\Sigma)

and similarly for
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{0}\operatorname{Diff}(\Sigma)
.
(The superscripts
0,b
are omitted if
\partial{\Sigma}=\emptyset
.) The mapping class group is the quotient

\operatorname{MCG}(\Sigma)=\phantom{\cdot}^{0}\operatorname{Diff}_{+}(\Sigma)/\phantom{\cdot}^{0}\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\Sigma)
.

Definition 2.4
.

The (infinite-dimensional)
Teichmüller space
is the space of hyperbolic structures on
\Sigma
, up to diffeomorphisms
fixing the boundary and homotopic to the identity:

(5)

\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)=\operatorname{Hyp}(\Sigma)/\phantom{\cdot}^{0}\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\Sigma).

The (infinite-dimensional) (Riemann)
moduli space
is the quotient

(6)

\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)=\operatorname{Hyp}(\Sigma)/\phantom{\cdot}^{0}\operatorname{Diff}_{+}(\Sigma).

Equivalently,
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)
is the quotient of
\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)
under the action of the mapping class group.
Both (
5
) and (
6
) are endowed with residual actions of boundary diffeomorphisms. Since

\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\Sigma)/\phantom{\cdot}^{0}\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\Sigma)=\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\partial{\Sigma})
, there is an induced action

\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\partial{\Sigma})\circlearrowright\mathcal{M}(\Sigma).

Similarly, the quotient
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\Sigma)/\phantom{\cdot}^{0}\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\Sigma)
acts on
\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)
.
This group is a covering of
\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\partial{\Sigma})
(not always the universal cover; see examples below).
In any case, there is an induced action

\widetilde{\operatorname{Diff}}_{\mathsf{o}}(\partial{\Sigma})\circlearrowright\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)

of the universal cover of the identity component
\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\partial{\Sigma})
. (If
\partial{\Sigma}
has several components, we may also consider diffeomorphisms
interchanging boundary components.)
The map (
2
) descends to maps

(7)

\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)\to\operatorname{Proj}(\partial{\Sigma}),\ \ \mathcal{M}(\Sigma)\to\operatorname{Proj}(\partial{\Sigma}),

which are equivariant for these actions. As we shall explain in this paper, these will be identified as moment maps for Hamiltonian Virasoro spaces.

2.4.
Special cases

One has more concrete descriptions of the Teichmüller spaces, as follows. Let
g
be the genus of
\Sigma
and

r
the number of boundary components. The Euler characteristic is thus

\chi(\Sigma)=2-2g-r
.

2.4.1.
Poincaré disk

(
g=0,\ r=1
.)
The standard metric on the closed Poincaré disk
\overline{\mathbb{D}}

is the unique hyperbolic 0-metric on the disk, up to diffeomorphism. The stabilizer of the standard metric under the action of
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}\operatorname{Diff}_{+}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
is
G=\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
. It follows that

\operatorname{Hyp}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})=\phantom{\cdot}^{b}\operatorname{Diff}_{+}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})/G
, and hence

\operatorname{Teich}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})=\mathcal{M}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})=\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\partial\mathbb{D})/G

(since every diffeomorphism in
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{0}\operatorname{Diff}_{+}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
is isotopic to the identity).
This space is closely related to Bers’
universal Teichmüller space

[
4
,
32
]
; it has an interpretation as a coadjoint orbit of the Virasoro group.

2.4.2.
Hyperbolic cylinders

(
g=0,\ r=2
.)
Let
\mathbb{A}=S^{1}\times(-\infty,\infty)
be the infinite cylinder (open annulus),
with coordinates
(x,u)
where
x\in S^{1}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
. Denote by
\overline{\mathbb{A}}=S^{1}\times[-\infty,\infty]
its
compactification, with
boundary defining function
\varrho(x,u)=\cosh(u)^{-1}
. Here
\operatorname{MCG}(\overline{\mathbb{A}})=\mathbb{Z}
, generated by Dehn twists

(x,u)\mapsto(x+f(u),u)
for
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
f(u)=0
for
u<-R
and
f(u)=1
for
u>R
, for some
R>0
.

Given
\ell>0
, the formula

(8)

\mathsf{g}=\cosh^{2}(u)\ell^{2}{\mathsf{d}}x^{2}+{\mathsf{d}}u^{2}

defines a hyperbolic 0-metric, with the curve
u=0
as its unique closed simple geodesic.

Conversely, every hyperbolic 0-metric on
\overline{\mathbb{A}}
admits a unique
closed simple geodesic; letting
\ell
be its length, the metric is obtained from (
8
) by the action of an
element of
\operatorname{Diff}_{+}(\overline{\mathbb{A}})
preserving the two boundary components.
The stabilizer of (
8
) under this action is
S^{1}
, acting by rotations of the cylinder.
This gives

\mathcal{M}(\overline{\mathbb{A}})=\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times(\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(S^{1})\times\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(S^{1}))/S^{1}

with the anti-diagonal embedding of
S^{1}
. For the Teichmüller space, we note that compactly supported diffeomorphisms of

(-\infty,0]\times S^{1}
, modulo the subgroup fixing the boundary, is the universal cover

\widetilde{\operatorname{Diff}}_{\mathsf{o}}(S^{1})
. This gives

\operatorname{Teich}(\overline{\mathbb{A}})=\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times(\widetilde{\operatorname{Diff}}_{\mathsf{o}}(S^{1})\times\widetilde{\operatorname{Diff}}_{\mathsf{o}}(S^{1}))/\mathbb{R}

where
\mathbb{R}
is embedded
anti-diagonally. For
\overline{\mathbb{A}}
with the 0-metric (
8
), the subset given by
u\geq 0
is called a
trumpet
(also called
flare
or
funnel
).
It has one geodesic boundary component and one boundary at infinity.

2.4.3.
Surfaces of negative Euler characteristic (
g=0,\ r>2
or
g\geq 1,\ r\geq 1
)

After choice of a Fenchel-Nielsen parametrization, one finds

(9)

\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)\cong(\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\mathbb{R})^{3g-3+r}\times\prod_{j=1}^{r}(\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\widetilde{\operatorname{Diff}}_{\mathsf{o}}(S^{1})).

We will give details in Section
8
below. At this point, we just mention that the Fenchel-Nielsen parametrization involves a pants decomposition of the surface. The boundaries of the pants which are not among the boundaries of
\Sigma

form a system of

3g-3+r
circles separating the pants; given a hyperbolic 0-metric these are realized as geodesics, and
the factors
\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\mathbb{R}
in (
9
) are the corresponding length and twist parameters. The boundary components of pants
which are also boundaries of the surface correspond to trumpets as discussed in
2.4.2
; the

\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\widetilde{\operatorname{Diff}}_{\mathsf{o}}(S^{1})
-factors signify the length of the geodesic end of the trumpet and a twist by rotating the ideal boundary of the trumpet.

3.
Coframe formalism

For calculations in this section, we will prefer the half-plane model of hyperbolic geometry. Let

\mathbb{H}=\{z=x+iy|y>0\},

with the standard hyperbolic metric

(10)

\frac{1}{y^{2}}({\mathsf{d}}x^{2}+{\mathsf{d}}y^{2}).

The map
\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{D},\ z\mapsto(z-i)/(1-iz)
is an isometric isomorphism, equivariant with respect to

(11)

\operatorname{Ad}_{q}\colon\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})\to\operatorname{PSU}(1,1),\ \ \ q=\Big[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&-i\\
-i&1\end{array}\Big].

Here the bracket notation
[A]\in\operatorname{PGL}(2,\mathbb{C})
denotes the image of an element
A\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{C})
. The isomorphism extends to a bijection

\overline{\mathbb{H}}\cup\{\infty\}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}}
taking
0\in\partial\mathbb{H}
to
-i\in\partial\mathbb{D}
.
Throughout this section,
G
denotes the group
\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
, and
\mathfrak{g}
its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
.

3.1.
Cartan coframe formalism

A convenient tool for dealing with 0-metrics is the
moving coframe formalism
due to É. Cartan. Let
\Sigma
be an oriented surface with boundary. Let
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{0}\Omega^{k}(\Sigma)=\Gamma(\bigwedge^{k}\,(\phantom{\cdot}^{0}T\Sigma)^{*})
denote the de Rham complex
of the Lie algebroid
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{0}T\Sigma
. Elements of this space may be seen as ordinary
k
-forms on
\operatorname{int}(\Sigma)
such that
\varrho^{k}\alpha
extends to all of
\Sigma
, for any boundary defining function
\varrho
.

An
oriented coframe
over an open subset
U\subseteq\Sigma
is
pair of 0-covector fields

\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\phantom{\cdot}^{0}\Omega^{1}(U)

giving an oriented frame for
(\phantom{\cdot}^{0}T\Sigma)^{*}|_{U}
. Given a 0-metric
\mathsf{g}
on
\Sigma
, the coframe is
orthonormal
if

\mathsf{g}|_{U}=(\alpha_{1})^{2}+(\alpha_{2})^{2}.

In this case, the Riemannian volume form
{\mathsf{d}}\operatorname{vol}_{\mathsf{g}}\in\phantom{\cdot}^{0}\Omega^{2}(\Sigma)
is given by

{\mathsf{d}}\operatorname{vol}_{\mathsf{g}}|_{U}=\alpha_{1}\wedge\alpha_{2}.

Any two oriented orthonormal coframes for
\mathsf{g}
are related,
on the overlap of their domains, by a
coframe rotation

(12)

\alpha_{1}^{\prime}=\cos\varphi\alpha_{1}+\sin\varphi\alpha_{2},\ \ \alpha_{2}^{\prime}=-\sin\varphi\alpha_{1}+\cos\varphi\alpha_{2}

with
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(U\cap U^{\prime},\mathbb{R})
. The
spin connection
for an oriented coframe is the
0
-covector field

\kappa\in\phantom{\cdot}^{0}\Omega^{1}(U)

defined by the equations

{\mathsf{d}}\alpha_{1}=-\kappa\wedge\alpha_{2},\ \ {\mathsf{d}}\alpha_{2}=\kappa\wedge\alpha_{1}.

Under coframe rotation, the spin connection changes to
\kappa^{\prime}=\kappa-{\mathsf{d}}\varphi
.
This shows that there exists a globally defined function
K_{\mathsf{g}}\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
such that

K_{\mathsf{g}}\,{\mathsf{d}}\operatorname{vol}_{\mathsf{g}}\big|_{U}={\mathsf{d}}\kappa.

This function is the
Gauss curvature
of the metric. The three equations

(13)

{\mathsf{d}}\alpha_{1}=-\kappa\wedge\alpha_{2},\ \ {\mathsf{d}}\alpha_{2}=\kappa\wedge\alpha_{1},\ \ {\mathsf{d}}\kappa=K_{\mathsf{g}}\ \alpha_{1}\wedge\alpha_{2}

are
Cartan’s structure equations
.

Examples 3.1
.

We list some standard coframes for hyperbolic 0-metrics, and the
resulting spin connections.

(a)

Upper half plane
\bar{\mathbb{H}}
:

\alpha_{1}=\frac{{\mathsf{d}}x}{y},\ \alpha_{2}=\frac{{\mathsf{d}}y}{y},\ \kappa=-\frac{{\mathsf{d}}x}{y}.

(b)

Poincare disk
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
:

\alpha_{1}=\frac{2}{1-r^{2}}r{\mathsf{d}}\varphi,\ \ \alpha_{2}=\frac{2}{1-r^{2}}{\mathsf{d}}r,\ \ \kappa=\frac{1+r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}{\mathsf{d}}\varphi.

Replacing
r
with the boundary defining function

y=\frac{1-r}{1+r}
, this becomes

\alpha_{1}=\frac{1-y^{2}}{2}\frac{{\mathsf{d}}\varphi}{y},\ \ \alpha_{2}=\frac{{\mathsf{d}}y}{y},\ \ \kappa=-\frac{1+y^{2}}{2}\frac{{\mathsf{d}}\varphi}{y}

which more clearly exhibits the coframe as 0-covector fields.

(c)

Hyperbolic cylinder
\overline{\mathbb{A}}=S^{1}\times[\infty,\infty]

with parameter
\ell>0
: Using coordinates
x,u
,

\alpha_{1}=\cosh(u)\ell\ {\mathsf{d}}x,\ \ \alpha_{2}=-{\mathsf{d}}u,\ \ \kappa=-\sinh(u)\ell{\mathsf{d}}x.

Putting
y=e^{-u}
(which is a boundary defining function near the boundary
u=\infty
), this becomes

\alpha_{1}=\ell\frac{1+y^{2}}{2}\ \frac{{\mathsf{d}}x}{y},\ \ \alpha_{2}=\frac{{\mathsf{d}}y}{y},\ \ \kappa=-\ell\frac{1-y^{2}}{2}\ \frac{{\mathsf{d}}x}{y}.

(d)

Fefferman-Graham coframe: For any function
T(x)
, the formulas

\alpha_{1}=\big(1-y^{2}T(x)\big)\frac{{\mathsf{d}}x}{y},\ \ \alpha_{2}=\frac{{\mathsf{d}}y}{y},\ \ \kappa=-\big(1+y^{2}T(x)\big)\frac{{\mathsf{d}}x}{y}

define a coframe for a hyperbolic 0-metric on
\{(x,y)|\ y\geq 0,\ y^{2}T(x)<1\}
. This unifies the boundary behaviour of the previous examples.

Remark 3.2
.

From now on, the letters
x,y
will be reserved for local coordinates on
U\subseteq\Sigma
that are
oriented

(i.e.,
{\mathsf{d}}x\wedge{\mathsf{d}}y>0
) and
adapted to the boundary
, in the sense that

y
is a boundary defining function for
\partial\Sigma\cap U
. If
U
is contained in the interior, this just means
y>0
everywhere on

.

3.2.
The connection 1-form associated with an orthonormal coframe

Given a local orthonormal coframe
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\phantom{\cdot}^{0}\Omega^{1}(U)
for a
0
-metric
\mathsf{g}
, with associated spin connection
\kappa
, define a 0-connection 1-form

(14)

A={\frac{1}{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha_{2}&\alpha_{1}-\kappa\\
\alpha_{1}+\kappa&-\alpha_{2}\end{array}\right)\in\phantom{\cdot}^{0}\Omega^{1}(U,\mathfrak{g}).

The curvature
F_{A}={\mathsf{d}}A+{\frac{1}{2}}[A,A]
is given by

(15)

F_{A}=(K_{\mathsf{g}}+1)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-1\\
1&0\end{array}\right)\ {\mathsf{d}}\operatorname{vol}_{\mathsf{g}}.

In particular, the 0-metric
\mathsf{g}
is hyperbolic if and only if the connection 1-form

A
is flat. Let
K\subseteq G=\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
be the maximal compact subgroup given as the stabilizer of
i\in\mathbb{H}
. It is identified with
\operatorname{SO}(2)

by the map

(16)

\operatorname{SO}(2)\to K,\ \ R(\varphi)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos(\varphi)&-\sin(\varphi)\\
\sin(\varphi)&\cos(\varphi)\end{array}\right)\mapsto[R(\varphi/2)].

Under coframe rotations (
12
), the 0-connection 1-form
A
transforms by

(17)

A^{\prime}=[R\left(\varphi/2\right)]\bullet A

where

\bullet
signifies a gauge transformation

g\bullet A=\operatorname{Ad}_{g}(A)-({\mathsf{d}}g)g^{-1}

for
g\in C^{\infty}(U,G)
.
We hence see that the
K
-action on connection 1-forms translates into the
\operatorname{SO}(2)
-action on coframes. Observe that
A
does
not
transform as a connection on the tangent bundle.

Example 3.3
.

For the upper half plane
\bar{\mathbb{H}}
with its standard coframe (Example

),

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{2y}\ {\mathsf{d}}y&\frac{1}{y}{\mathsf{d}}x\\
0&-\frac{1}{2y}\ {\mathsf{d}}y\end{array}\right).

Note that
A=g^{-1}\bullet 0
where

g=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&x\\
0&1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}y^{\frac{1}{2}}&0\\

.

3.3.
Adapted coframes

For any manifold
M
with boundary
\partial M
, the restriction of the 0-cotangent bundle to the boundary has a distinguished trivial subbundle

\partial M\times\mathbb{R}\subseteq(\phantom{\cdot}^{0}TM)^{*}|_{\partial M},

spanned by the restriction of
\varrho^{-1}{\mathsf{d}}\varrho\in\phantom{\cdot}^{0}\Omega^{1}(M)
, for any boundary defining function
\varrho
. (Changing
\varrho
by a positive function changes this expression by an
ordinary
(exact) 1-form, but the restriction of a regular 1-form as a section of the 0-tangent bundle vanishes.)

Definition 3.5
.

An oriented coframe
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\phantom{\cdot}^{0}\Omega^{1}(U)
, defined on a neighborhood
U\subseteq\Sigma
of the boundary, is
adapted to the boundary
if
\alpha_{2}|_{\partial{\Sigma}}
is the canonical
section of
(\phantom{\cdot}^{0}T\Sigma)^{*}|_{\partial{\Sigma}}
.
That is,

\alpha_{2}=\frac{d\varrho}{\varrho}+O(\varrho^{0}).

Here we write
\alpha=O(\varrho^{k})
if
\varrho^{-k}\alpha
extends smoothly to the boundary (as an ordinary differential form).
Note that for an adapted coframe,
\varrho\alpha_{1}
pulls back to a volume form on
\partial\Sigma
.

The coframes in parts (
a
), (
b
), (
d
) of Examples
3.1
are adapted to the boundary.
(But (
b
) is not defined at the center of
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.)
The coframe in (
c
) for the double trumpet is adapted to the boundary at
u=\infty
but not at
u=-\infty
. One can turn it into an adapted coframe by applying a coframe rotation
R(\phi
with
\phi|_{u=-\infty}=\pi,\ \phi|_{u=0}=0
.
If
\Sigma
is compact and connected, of non-zero Euler characteristic,
it is impossible, by Poincaré’s theorem on zeroes of vector fields on surfaces, to find a
global
oriented coframe that is adapted to all the boundary components. On the other hand, we have:

Lemma 3.6
.

Every hyperbolic 0-metric
\mathsf{g}
admits an oriented orthonormal coframe
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
on some collar neighborhood of the boundary, which is adapted to the boundary. The spin connection of such a coframe satisfies

\kappa=-\alpha_{1}+O(\varrho^{1}).

Proof.

Choose an oriented orthonormal coframe
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\phantom{\cdot}^{0}\Omega(U)
on a collar neighborhood
U
of the boundary,
with the property that

\alpha_{2}|_{\partial\Sigma}
is a positive function times the canonical section of
(\phantom{\cdot}^{0}T\Sigma)^{*}|_{\partial{\Sigma}}
.
Using that
\mathsf{g}
is hyperbolic, we shall show that
\alpha_{2}|_{\partial\Sigma}
must then be
equal to
the canonical section.
It suffices to prove this in local coordinates
x,y
adapted to the boundary (cf. Remark
3.2
).
Consider the Laurent expansions
in powers of
y
,

\alpha_{1}=\frac{1}{y}\beta_{1}+\gamma_{1}+O(y^{1}),\ \ \alpha_{2}=\frac{h}{y}{\mathsf{d}}y+\gamma_{2}+O(y^{1}),\ \ \kappa=\frac{1}{y}\beta_{3}+\gamma_{3}+O(y^{1}),

where
\beta_{i},\gamma_{i}
, are of the form
f_{i}(x){\mathsf{d}}x+g_{i}(x)\ {\mathsf{d}}y
, and where
h
is a function of
x
, with

h(x)>0
.
Comparing coefficients of
y^{-2}
in the structure equations (
13
) (with
K_{\mathsf{g}}=-1
) gives conditions

(18)

\beta_{1}\wedge{\mathsf{d}}y=-h\beta_{3}\wedge{\mathsf{d}}y,\ \ 0=\beta_{3}\wedge\beta_{1},\ \ \beta_{3}\wedge{\mathsf{d}}y=-h\beta_{1}\wedge{\mathsf{d}}y.

From

{\mathsf{d}}\operatorname{vol}_{\mathsf{g}}=\alpha_{1}\wedge\alpha_{2}=\frac{1}{y^{2}}h\beta_{1}\wedge{\mathsf{d}}y+O(y^{-1})

we see that
\beta_{1},{\mathsf{d}}y
are pointwise linearly independent;
in particular
\beta_{1}
is non-vanishing. Hence, the second equation in (
18
) shows that

\beta_{3}
is a scalar multiple of
\beta_{1}
, and so the other two equations give
\beta_{1}=-h\beta_{3},\ \beta_{3}=-h\beta_{1}
.
Hence
h=1
and
\beta_{3}=-\beta_{1}
.
At this stage, the expressions for the coframe have simplified to

\alpha_{1}=\frac{1}{y}\beta+\gamma_{1}+O(y^{1}),\ \ \alpha_{2}=\frac{1}{y}{\mathsf{d}}y+\gamma_{2}+O(y^{1}),\ \ \kappa=-\frac{1}{y}\beta+\gamma_{3}+O(y^{1})

(where we write
\beta=\beta_{1}
). From the sum of the first and third structure equations,

{\mathsf{d}}(\alpha_{1}+\kappa)=-(\alpha_{1}+\kappa)\wedge\alpha_{2}
we obtain, by comparing coefficients of
y^{-1}
, that

(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{3})\wedge{\mathsf{d}}y=0
. On the other hand,
{\mathsf{d}}\alpha_{2}=\kappa\wedge\alpha_{1}
gives

(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{3})\wedge\beta=0
. Using again that
\beta,{\mathsf{d}}y
are pointwise linearly independent, we conclude
\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{3}=0
, hence
\alpha_{1}+\kappa=O(y^{1})
.
∎

Locally, one can achieve an even better normal form for the coframe.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\mathsf{g}
be a hyperbolic
0
-metric on
\Sigma
. For every
m\in\Sigma
there exists an adapted oriented orthonormal coframe
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
on some open neighborhood of
m

such that the associated spin connection is

\kappa=-\alpha_{1}.

Equivalently, the connection 1-form (
14
) is upper triangular.

Proof.

Begin by choosing any adapted oriented orthonormal coframe
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
for
\mathsf{g}
, and let
A
be the associated connection 1-form.

Consider the case that
m
is an interior point. Let
U\subseteq\Sigma
be a simply connected open neighborhood of
m
.
Since
A
is flat, it determines a parallel transport
g\in C^{\infty}(U,\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}))
, i.e.
A=g\bullet 0=-({\mathsf{d}}g)g^{-1}
with initial condition
g(m)=e
. The Iwasawa decomposition for
\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
gives a map
R(\psi)\colon U\to\operatorname{SO}(2)
such that

R(\psi)g
is upper triangular. It hence follows that
R(\psi)\bullet A=(R(\psi)g)\bullet 0
is upper triangular.

The case that
m
is a boundary point requires more care, since we need a coframe rotation that extends all the way to the boundary.
Pick adapted local coordinates
x,y
on a simply connected open neighborhood
U\subseteq\Sigma
of
m
, and write
\alpha_{1}=\frac{1}{y}\beta+O(y^{0})
as in the proof of the previous lemma. Then

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{2y}\ {\mathsf{d}}y+O(y^{0})&\frac{1}{y}\beta+O(y^{0})\\
O(y^{1})&-\frac{1}{2y}\ {\mathsf{d}}y+O(y^{0})\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}y^{-1/2}&0\\
0&y^{1/2}\end{array}\right)\bullet A^{\prime},

where
A^{\prime}\in\Omega^{1}(U,\mathfrak{g})
is a
regular
flat connection on
U
– all matrix entries extend smoothly to the boundary.
This determines a parallel transport
g^{\prime}\colon U\to\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
i.e.

A^{\prime}=g^{\prime}\bullet 0=-({\mathsf{d}}g^{\prime})g^{\prime-1}
with initial condition
g^{\prime}|_{m}=e
. Away from the boundary, we obtain
A=g\bullet 0
with

(19)

g=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}y^{-1/2}&0\\
0&y^{1/2}\end{array}\right)g^{\prime}\colon\ U-\partial\Sigma\to\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}).

By the Iwasawa decomposition for
\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})

there is a unique map
R(\psi)\colon U-\partial\Sigma\to\operatorname{SO}(2)
with the property that
R(\psi)g
is upper triangular with positive diagonal. With this choice,
R(\psi)\bullet A
is upper triangular on
U-\partial\Sigma
. To show that
\psi
extends smoothly to the boundary, write

(20)

g=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right),\ \ g^{\prime}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}\end{array}\right).

Then

\psi=-\arctan(c/a)=-\arctan(yc^{\prime}/a^{\prime}).

Since
a^{\prime}|_{m}=1
, this extends smoothly to all of
U
.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

The parallel transport (
19
), as a map into
G=\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
, is well-defined only away from the boundary.
It becomes well-defined up to the boundary if it is regarded as a map to the ‘wonderful’ compactification
\overline{G}
.

3.4.
Local normal form

Lemma
3.7
allows us to give a quick proof of the local normal form for hyperbolic 0-metrics. Earlier proofs proceed
through uniformization and the classification of ends
[
6
, Theorem 2.3]
, or through estimates for sectional curvatures

[
15
]
. (We thank D. Borthwick for these references.)

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\mathsf{g}
be a hyperbolic
0
-metric on
\Sigma
. Every
m\in\Sigma
admits an open neighborhood
U
and a
0
-isometry
U\to\overline{\mathbb{H}}
.

Proof.

Consider the case that
m
is a boundary point. (For interior points the argument is similar.) By Lemma
3.7
, we may choose
an adapted
oriented orthonormal coframe
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
with
\kappa=-\alpha_{1}
. The second structure equation (
13
) gives

{\mathsf{d}}\alpha_{2}=-\kappa\wedge\alpha_{1}=0
. Since the coframe is adapted, the difference

\alpha_{2}-\frac{{\mathsf{d}}\varrho}{\varrho}
extends smoothly to the boundary, and hence may be written as

{\mathsf{d}}f
near
m
. Hence, taking
y=e^{f}\varrho
as a coordinate near
m
, we obtain

\alpha_{2}=y^{-1}{\mathsf{d}}y
.
The first structure equation shows that
y\alpha_{1}
is closed:
{\mathsf{d}}(y\alpha_{1})={\mathsf{d}}y\wedge\alpha_{1}-y\kappa\wedge\alpha_{2}=0
. We may therefore choose the coordinate
x
near
m
so that

y\alpha_{1}={\mathsf{d}}x
, which gives
\alpha_{1}=y^{-1}\ {\mathsf{d}}x
.
This proves the existence of an isometric diffeomorphism from an open neighborhood
U
of
m\in\Sigma
onto an open subset of
\bar{\mathbb{H}}
.
∎

4.
Hyperbolic structures from flat connections

The symplectic structure on the infinite-dimensional Teichmüller space
\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)
will be obtained
by a reduction procedure, starting from the usual Atiyah-Bott symplectic structure on a space of connections. We encountered
flat connection 1-forms in the coframe formalism, see (
14
).
Note however that these 1-forms become singular at the boundary. While it is possible to work with these singular connections, we will pursue a different approach where (
14
) represents an
ordinary
connection 1-form
\theta
on a principal bundle. This is motivated by the theory of geometric structures (cf.
[
12
,
14
,
34
]
).

Throughout, we take
G=\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
, with the action on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
regarded as

\overline{\mathbb{D}}=\overline{\mathbb{H}}\cup\{\infty\}
, or equivalently via

\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})\cong\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
(cf. (
11
)).

4.1.
Flat bundles from hyperbolic structures

In Definition
2.1
, hyperbolic structures on surfaces
\Sigma
with boundary were described in terms of charts
\phi_{\alpha}\colon U_{\alpha}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}}
with transition functions
h_{\alpha\beta}\in G

\phi_{\alpha}(x)=h_{\alpha\beta}.\phi_{\beta}(x)
on
U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta}

G
-bundle

\pi\colon P\to\Sigma,

obtained from
\bigsqcup_{\alpha}(U_{\alpha}\times G)
by identifying
(x,g)\in U_{\beta}\times G
with

(x,h_{\alpha\beta}\,g)\in U_{\alpha}\times G
.
The charts themselves determine a
G
-equivariant morphism of manifolds with boundary
1
1

1

A
morphism of manifolds with boundary

F\colon M_{1}\to M_{2}
is a smooth map with the property that the pullback of a boundary defining function on
M_{2}
is a boundary defining function on
M_{1}
. Note that such a map determines a morphism of the 0-tangent bundles.

\sigma\colon P\to\overline{\mathbb{D}}

given in the local trivializations by

\sigma
as a

, since it may be regarded as a section of the associated bundle with fiber

.

The principal bundle

comes equipped with a flat connection

\operatorname{At}(P)=TP/G
be the Atiyah algebroid (see Appendix

), and

the horizontal lift defined by

Then the composition

is an

. In terms of the local trivialization

Remark 4.1
.

If the surface is the closed Poincaré disk itself, these constructions become tautological: The principal bundle is the trivial bundle

with the trivial connection
\operatorname{pr}_{2}^{*}\theta^{L}

More generally, if

Similarly, any projective structure on an oriented 1-manifold

(Definition

4.2.
Hyperbolic structures from flat bundles

We shall now reverse the procedure and take as our starting point the data

(23)

\textstyle{P\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

of a principal

\sigma

Over the interior

\sigma
takes values in
\mathbb{D}=G/K
(where

is the stabilizer of

On the other hand, the boundary restriction

The role of

Definition 4.2
.

A connection
\theta\in\mathcal{A}(P)
is called
\sigma

Denote by
\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}(P)
the space of positive connections, and by
\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}_{\operatorname{flat}}(P)
those which are furthermore flat.

There is a natural map from
\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}(P)
to the space of 0-metrics: The standard 0-metric on
T\overline{\mathbb{D}}
gives a
0-metric on
V_{\sigma}=\sigma^{*}T\overline{\mathbb{D}}/G
; a

.

Proposition 4.3
.

If
\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}(P)
is flat, then the 0-metric
\mathsf{g}
defined by
\theta
is hyperbolic.

Proof.

Choose local trivializations
P|_{U_{\alpha}}\cong U_{\alpha}\times G
taking
\theta
to the trivial connection, and write

\sigma(x,g)=g^{-1}.\phi_{\alpha}(x)
in terms of the trivialization. The positivity condition ensures that the maps
\phi_{\alpha}\colon U_{\alpha}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}}
are orientation preserving diffeomorphisms onto their image, and so define a hyperbolic structure. Clearly,
\mathsf{g}
is the 0-metric associated to
this hyperbolic structure.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

The construction may also be understood as follows: A flat connection
\theta
determines a horizontal foliation of
P
.
Positivity means exactly that
\sigma
restricts to orientation-preserving local diffeomorphisms from the horizontal leaves
to
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. Hence, the hyperbolic structure on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
pulls back to a
G
-invariant
hyperbolic structure on the horizontal foliation, which then descends to
\Sigma
.

Given an oriented 1-manifold
\mathsf{C}
, we may similarly consider the data

(26)

\textstyle{Q\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tau}
\textstyle{\partial{\mathbb{D}}}
\textstyle{\mathsf{C}}

of a principal
G
-bundle over
\mathsf{C}
with a developing section (a
G
-equivariant map to
\partial\mathbb{D}\cong\mathbb{R}\!\operatorname{P}(1)
). A connection
\vartheta
on
Q
is called positive if the
map (
22
) is an orientation preserving isomorphism. Such a connection defines a projective structure on
\mathsf{C}
; conversely, every projective structure on
\mathsf{C}
arises in this way.

Returning to the pair
(P,\sigma)
for surfaces with boundary, we have:

Proposition 4.5
.

A connection
\theta\in\mathcal{A}(P)
satisfies the
\sigma
-positivity condition
along the boundary
\partial{\Sigma}
if and only if the pullback connection
\partial\theta\in\mathcal{A}(\partial P)
is
\partial\sigma
-positive.

Proof.

The map
T\Sigma\to V_{\sigma}
given by
\theta
restricts to the map
T\partial{\Sigma}\to V_{\partial\sigma}
given by
\partial\theta
. The resulting map
on quotients,

\nu(\Sigma,\partial{\Sigma})\to V_{\sigma}|_{\partial\Sigma}/V_{\partial\sigma},

does not depend on the choice of
\theta
. In fact, it is simply
the map obtained by applying the normal bundle functor to the map of pairs
\sigma\colon(P,\partial P)\to(\mathbb{D},\partial\mathbb{D})
, using that

\nu(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)=\nu(P,\partial P)/G,\ \ \ \ V_{\sigma}|_{\partial\Sigma}/V_{\partial\sigma}=(\partial\sigma)^{*}\nu(\mathbb{D},\partial\mathbb{D})/G.

In particular, the map on quotients is always an orientation preserving isomorphism. We conclude that
T\Sigma|_{\partial{\Sigma}}\to V_{\sigma}|_{\partial{\Sigma}}
is an orientation preserving isomorphism if and only if
T\partial{\Sigma}\to V_{\partial\sigma}
is an orientation preserving isomorphism.
∎

4.3.
Relationship with coframe formalism

Given
(P,\sigma)
, consider the reduction of structure group (
24
) to
K\subseteq G
. A trivialization of
P_{K}

over
U\subseteq\operatorname{int}(\Sigma)
determines a trivialization
P|_{U}=U\times G
such that
\sigma(m,g)=g^{-1}.i
.
Let
A\in\Omega^{1}(U,\mathfrak{g})
be the connection 1-form of
\theta
in this trivialization. Define 1-forms
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
, and
\kappa
by writing

(27)

A={\frac{1}{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha_{2}&\alpha_{1}-\kappa\\
\alpha_{1}+\kappa&-\alpha_{2}\end{array}\right).

Proposition 4.6
.

The connection
\theta\in\mathcal{A}(P)
is positive over
U
if and only if
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
are an oriented coframe.
In this case,
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
is an orthonormal coframe for the metric
\mathsf{g}
defined by
\theta
; if the connection is flat then
\kappa
is the spin connection for this coframe.

Proof.

Since
T\mathbb{D}\cong T\mathbb{H}=T(G/K)=G\times_{K}\mathfrak{k}^{\perp}
, we have

V_{\sigma}|_{\operatorname{int}(\Sigma)}=(P_{K}\times\mathfrak{k}^{\perp})/K=\mathfrak{k}^{\perp}(P_{K}).

Hence,
V_{\sigma}|_{U}=U\times\mathfrak{k}^{\perp}
, and (
21
)
becomes a map

(28)

TU\to U\times\mathfrak{k}^{\perp}.

Viewed as an element of
\Omega^{1}(U,\mathfrak{k}^{\perp})
, this map is the symmetric part of the connection 1-form
A
. The condition that (
28
) is an orientation preserving isomorphism means exactly that
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
is an oriented orthonormal coframe. Finally, the metric on
V_{\sigma}|_{U}
corresponds to the standard metric on
\mathfrak{k}^{\perp}=T_{i}\mathbb{H}
, and the
metric on
TU
induced by (
28
) is exactly the one defined by the coframe
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
.
∎

The reduction of structure group to
K
does not extend to the boundary. To describe the limiting behaviour, we shall work with the following lemma.
Let
\varrho\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
be a boundary defining function.

Lemma 4.7

(Normal form at boundary)
.

Given
(P,\sigma)
, there exists
\epsilon>0
and a trivialization
P|_{U}=U\times G
over
U=\varrho^{-1}\big([0,\epsilon)\big)
such that, in terms of the trivialization,

\sigma(m,g)=g^{-1}.(i\varrho(m)).

As usual, we identify
\overline{\mathbb{D}}\cong\overline{\mathbb{H}}\cup\{\infty\}
; thus
i\varrho(m)
is regarded as an element of the closed upper half plane.

Proof.

The choice of a trivialization of
\sigma^{-1}(0)=(\partial P)_{B^{-}}
gives a trivialization
\partial P=\partial{\Sigma}\times G
such that

(\partial\sigma)(m,g)=g^{-1}.0
for
m\in\partial{\Sigma}
. Extend it to a trivialization of
P
over

U=\varrho^{-1}\big([0,\epsilon)\big)
for some
\epsilon>0
.
In terms of this trivialization,
\sigma
is of the form

\sigma(m,g)=g^{-1}.f(m),

where
f\colon U\to\overline{\mathbb{D}}\cong\overline{\mathbb{H}}\cup\{\infty\}

is a morphism of manifolds with boundary, with
f|_{\partial{\Sigma}}=0
. Taking
\epsilon
smaller if needed, we may assume
f
takes values in
\overline{\mathbb{H}}\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. In particular, the imaginary part
\operatorname{Im}(f)
is a boundary defining function, and so

\varrho=u^{2}\,\operatorname{Im}(f)
for some function
u\in C^{\infty}(U,\mathbb{R}_{>0})
. We have

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}u&0\\
0&u^{-1}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&-\operatorname{Re}(f)\\
0&1\end{array}\right].f=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}u&0\\
0&u^{-1}\end{array}\right].i\operatorname{Im}(f)=i\varrho

Hence, a gauge transformation by
\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}u&0\\
0&u^{-1}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&-\operatorname{Re}(f)\\
0&1\end{array}\right]
replaces
\sigma(m,g)=g^{-1}.f(m)
with
g^{-1}.i\varrho(m)
.
∎

Given a connection
\theta\in\mathcal{A}(P)
, let
A\in\Omega^{1}(U,\mathfrak{g})
be its connection 1-form in terms of the
trivialization from this lemma. Over
U-\partial\Sigma
, the gauge transformation by

(29)

h=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\rho^{1/2}&0\\
0&\rho^{-1/2}\end{array}\right]\colon U-\partial{\Sigma}\to G

is defined, and satisfies
h^{-1}.i\varrho(m)=i
. Hence,
(h^{-1}.\sigma)(m,g)=g^{-1}.i
, and

h^{-1}\bullet A
is of the form (
27
), defining
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\kappa
.
As we saw above, the positivity condition
on
\theta
means that over the interior,
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
are an oriented orthonormal coframe. The original connection 1-form is expressed in terms of these data as

A={\frac{1}{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha_{2}-\varrho^{-1}{\mathsf{d}}\varrho&\varrho(\alpha_{1}-\kappa)\\
\varrho^{-1}(\alpha_{1}+\kappa)&-\alpha_{2}+\varrho^{-1}{\mathsf{d}}\varrho\end{array}\right).

Since
A
is a
regular
connection 1-form, this shows that
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
extend to elements of
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{0}\Omega^{1}(U)
,
and define an adapted orthonormal coframe.

4.4.
Existence of positive connections

Recall the classification of principal bundles over compact, connected, oriented surfaces

\Sigma
with boundary. Let
H
be a connected Lie group, and
R\to\Sigma
a principal
H
-bundle with a given homotopy class of trivializations (framings) of
R|_{\partial{\Sigma}}
. Pick
x_{0}\in\operatorname{int}(\Sigma)
, and choose a trivialization of
R
over the punctured surface
\Sigma-\{x_{0}\}
such that the trivialization along the boundary is in the prescribed class.
Also choose a trivialization of
R
over an embedded disk
D\subseteq\operatorname{int}(\Sigma)
around
x_{0}
. The
homotopy class of the transition map
D-\{x_{0}\}\to H
defines an element

\mathsf{e}(R)\in\pi_{1}(H).

If
R_{1},R_{2}
are two principal
H
-bundles with homotopy classes of trivializations along the boundary, we have
\mathsf{e}(R_{1})=\mathsf{e}(R_{2})
if and only if there exists a bundle isomorphism
R_{1}\to R_{2}
which intertwines the homotopy classes of
trivializations over the boundary. In particular, taking
R=\operatorname{Fr}_{\operatorname{SO}(2)}(\Sigma)
to be the
oriented orthonormal frame bundle for a Riemannian metric, with its standard trivialization along the boundary (where the first element of a frame is tangent to the boundary, pointing in the positive direction), the element
\mathsf{e}(R)\in\pi_{1}(\operatorname{SO}(2))=\mathbb{Z}
is the Euler characteristic
\chi(\Sigma)
of the surface. Given a principal
G
-bundle
P\to\Sigma
with developing section
\sigma\colon P\to\overline{\mathbb{D}}=\overline{\mathbb{H}}\cup\{\infty\}
as above, there is a distinguished homotopy class of trivializations along the boundary – those trivializations for which
\sigma(m,g)=g^{-1}.0
for
m\in\partial{\Sigma}
.
Let

\mathsf{e}(P,\sigma)\in\pi_{1}(G)=\mathbb{Z}

be the resulting invariant.

Proposition 4.8
.

Given two pairs
(P,\sigma)
and
(P^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
, we have
\mathsf{e}(P,\sigma)=\mathsf{e}(P^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
if and only if there exists an isomorphism

P\to P^{\prime}
taking
\sigma
to
\sigma^{\prime}
and inducing the identity on the base.

Proof.

The necessity of the condition is obvious. To show that it is also sufficient, suppose
\mathsf{e}(P,\sigma)=\mathsf{e}(P^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
.
We may assume
P=P^{\prime}
, and that
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
define the same homotopy class of trivializations of
\partial P=P|_{\partial{\Sigma}}
.
Using the normal form near the boundary (Lemma
4.7
), we may assume that
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
coincide over an open neighborhood of the boundary. Over the interior,
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
may be regarded as sections of the associated bundle with fiber
\mathbb{D}
, which agree near the boundary. As is well-known, given any two distinct points
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{D}
there is a unique element
\phi(z,z^{\prime})\in G
taking
z
to
z^{\prime}
and preserving the geodesic through
z,z^{\prime}
. It extents smoothly to a map
\phi\colon\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}\to G
with
\phi(g.z,g.z^{\prime})=g\phi(z,z^{\prime})g^{-1}
.
Consequently,we obtain a gauge transformation
h\in\operatorname{Gau}(P)
taking
\sigma
to
\sigma^{\prime}
.
This gauge
transformation is trivial near the boundary since
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
agree there.
∎

The existence of
\sigma
-positive connections places a topological condition on
(P,\sigma)
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\Sigma
be a compact, connected, oriented surface with boundary, and
P\to\Sigma
a principal
G
-bundle with a developing section
\sigma\colon P\to\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. Then the space of positive connections is empty unless
\mathsf{e}(P,\sigma)=\chi(\Sigma)
.

Proof.

The oriented rank 2 bundle
V_{\sigma}|_{\partial\Sigma}
has a distinguished rank 1 subbundle
V_{\partial\sigma}
; hence there is
a unique homotopy class of trivializations of
V_{\sigma}
along the boundary, taking the subbundle to

\mathbb{R}\oplus 0\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Letting
\operatorname{Fr}_{\operatorname{SO}(2)}(V_{\sigma})
be the frame bundle for (any) fiber metric, the invariant
\mathsf{e}(\operatorname{Fr}_{\operatorname{SO}(2)}(V_{\sigma}))\in\pi_{1}(\operatorname{SO}(2))=\mathbb{Z}
is defined. We claim that

(30)

\mathsf{e}(\operatorname{Fr}_{\operatorname{SO}(2)}(V_{\sigma}))=\mathsf{e}(P,\sigma).

To see this, choose a covering of
\Sigma
, consisting of an open subset
U_{1}=\Sigma-\{x_{0}\}
where
x_{0}\in\operatorname{int}(\Sigma)
, and
an open neighborhood
U_{2}
of
x_{0}
, contained in the interior of
\Sigma
and diffeomorphic to an open disk. Choose a trivialization
of
P
over
U_{1}
, inducing the given class of trivializations along the boundary, and choose also a trivialization over
U_{2}
.
Let
f_{i}\colon U_{i}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}}
be the maps describing
\sigma
in these trivializations. We may arrange that
f_{1},f_{2}
are both constant
(equal to
i
) over
U_{1}\cap U_{2}
. Then the transition map
U_{2}-\{x_{0}\}=U_{1}\cap U_{2}\to G
takes values in
K
. The trivializations of

P|_{U_{i}}
also trivialize
V_{\sigma}|_{U_{i}}
, and the transition map for its frame bundle agrees with that for
P
under the isomorphism
K\cong\operatorname{SO}(2)
. This proves (
30
).

A positive connection determines an oriented vector bundle isomorphism
T\Sigma\to V_{\sigma}
, and hence

\chi(\Sigma)=\mathsf{e}(\operatorname{Fr}_{\operatorname{SO}(2)}(T\Sigma))=\mathsf{e}(\operatorname{Fr}_{\operatorname{SO}(2)}(V_{\sigma}))=\mathsf{e}(P,\sigma).\qed

5.
Automorphisms

In this section, we discuss the structure of the groups of automorphisms preserving a given developing section, for
G
-bundles over surfaces and over curves.
Recall that
B^{-}\subseteq G=\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
is the image of lower triangular matrices with positive diagonal entries. Thus
B^{-}=AN^{-}

where
A
is the image of positive diagonal matrices, and
N^{-}=[B^{-},B^{-}]
is the image of lower triangular matrices with
1
’s on the diagonal.

5.1.
The group
\operatorname{Aut}(Q,\tau)

Let
Q\to\mathsf{C}
be a principal
G
-bundle over a compact oriented 1-manifold, and
\tau\colon Q\to\partial\mathbb{D}=\partial\mathbb{H}\cup\{\infty\}
a developing section.

Proposition 5.1
.

The group
\operatorname{Gau}(Q,\tau)
of gauge transformations preserving
\tau

is the group of sections of a group bundle
G(Q,\tau)\to\mathsf{C}
, with typical fiber
B^{-}=AN^{-}
.
It fits into an exact sequence with the group
\operatorname{Aut}(Q,\tau)
of
automorphisms of preserving
\tau
,

1\to\operatorname{Gau}(Q,\tau)\to\operatorname{Aut}(Q,\tau)\to\operatorname{Diff}(\mathsf{C})\to 1.

Infinitesimally,
\mathfrak{gau}(Q,\tau)\subseteq\mathfrak{aut}(Q,\tau)
are the sections of a Lie algebroids
\mathfrak{g}(Q,\tau)\subseteq\operatorname{At}(Q,\tau)
,
described as the kernel of the bundle maps
\mathfrak{g}(Q)\subseteq\operatorname{At}(Q)\to V_{\tau}
.
We have an exact sequence of Lie algebroids

0\to\mathfrak{g}(Q,\tau)\to\operatorname{At}(Q,\tau)\to T\mathsf{C}\to 0.

Proof.

The developing section defines a reduction of structure group
Q_{B^{-}}=\tau^{-1}(0)\subseteq Q
, and the groups

\operatorname{Gau}(Q,\tau)\subseteq\operatorname{Aut}(Q,\tau)
are identified with the gauge transformations and automorphisms of

Q_{B^{-}}
. (Every
B^{-}
-equivariant diffeomorphism of
Q_{B^{-}}
extends uniquely to a
G
-equivariant diffeomorphism of
Q
; the latter preserves
\tau
.) In particular,
\operatorname{At}(Q,\tau)
is just the Atiyah algebroid of
Q_{B^{-}}
. The sections of

\operatorname{At}(Q,\tau)
are identified with the
B^{-}
-invariant vector fields on
Q_{B^{-}}
, or equivalently with the

G
-invariant vector fields on
Q
that are
\tau
-related to
0
. Equivalently, this is the kernel of
the bundle map to
V_{\tau}
.
∎

5.2.
The group
\operatorname{Aut}(P,\sigma)

We now give a similar discussion for principal bundles over oriented surfaces
\Sigma
with boundary.
Let
(P,\sigma)
as in (
23
). As we saw,
\sigma
gives a simultaneous description of two reductions of structure group: Over the interior, the structure group of
P
is reduced to
K
, over
\partial{\Sigma}
it is reduced to
B^{-}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

The groups of gauge transformations and automorphism of
P
preserving
\sigma
fit into an exact sequence

1\to\operatorname{Gau}(P,\sigma)\to\phantom{\cdot}^{b}\operatorname{Aut}(P,\sigma)\to\phantom{\cdot}^{b}\operatorname{Diff}(\Sigma)\to 1.

The group
\operatorname{Gau}(P,\sigma)
is the group of sections of a family of Lie groups

G(P,\sigma)
, with typical fibers
K
over interior points and
N^{-}
at boundary points; restriction to the boundary identifies

G(P,\sigma)|_{\partial{\Sigma}}=[G(\partial P,\partial\sigma),G(\partial P,\partial\sigma)].

(A
family of Lie groups
is a Lie groupoid for which source and target map coincide. It need not be locally trivial.)

Proof.

Over the interior of
\Sigma
, the developing section defines a reduction of the structure group to
K\subseteq G
,
and the groups

\operatorname{Gau}(P|_{\operatorname{int}(\Sigma)},\sigma)\subseteq\operatorname{Aut}(P|_{\operatorname{int}(\Sigma)},\sigma)
are identified with the gauge transformations and automorphisms of
P_{K}
. The main task in proving Proposition
5.2
is to understand the behavior near
the boundary. We shall use the local normal form, Lemma
4.7
.
Thus let

U=\partial\Sigma\times[0,\epsilon),\ \ P|_{U}=U\times G,

with
\sigma(m,g)=g^{-1}.(i\varrho(m))
.
Denote the points of
U
by
m=(x,y)
, so that
\varrho(m)=y
.
The automorphisms of
P|_{U}
may be written as pairs
(h,\Phi)
, where
\Phi\in\phantom{\cdot}^{b}\operatorname{Diff}(U)
and

h\in\operatorname{Gau}(P)=C^{\infty}(U,G)
. Such an automorphism preserves the developing section
\sigma(m,g)=g^{-1}.f(m)
if and only if

h(m).f(\Phi^{-1}(m))=f(m).

In our case,
f(x,y)=iy
, we have:

Lemma 5.3
.

The elements of
\operatorname{Gau}(P|_{U},\sigma)
with compact support in
U
are of the form

\exp\Big(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-\chi\varrho^{2}\\
\chi&0\end{array}\Big)

for compactly supported
\chi\in C^{\infty}(U)
. Every element of

\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}\!\operatorname{Aut}(P|_{U},\sigma)
whose base map is compactly supported in
U

is uniquely the product of such a gauge transformation and
an automorphism

\left(\Big[\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{-\lambda/2}&0\\
0&e^{\lambda/2}\end{array}\Big],\ \Phi\right)

where
\Phi\in\phantom{\cdot}^{b}\operatorname{Diff}(U)
has compact support in
U
, and
\lambda\in C^{\infty}(U)
is the compactly supported function defined by
\Phi_{*}\varrho=e^{\lambda}\,\varrho
.

Proof.

A gauge transformation
h\in C^{\infty}(U,G)
fixes
\sigma
if and only if
h(x,y)\in G_{iy}
for all
y
.
For
y>0
, the stabilizer of
iy\in\mathbb{H}
in
G
is

(31)

G_{iy}=\left\{\exp\Big(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-t\,y^{2}\\
t&0\end{array}\Big)\Big|\ t\in\mathbb{R}\right\}\cong K

This fits uniquely into a smooth family of subgroups of
\{(x,y)\}\times G
for all
y\geq 0
, by taking the fiber for
y=0
to be

N^{-}
. A smooth gauge transformation fixing
\sigma
must take values in this family of Lie groups.
Consider next a compactly supported diffeomorphism
\Phi\in\phantom{\cdot}^{b}\operatorname{Diff}_{+}(U)
. The push-forward
\Phi_{*}\varrho=\varrho\circ\Phi^{-1}
is again a boundary defining function, and so is of the form
e^{\lambda}\varrho
. The hyperbolic transformation
given by the diagonal matrix with entries
e^{-\lambda(m)/2},e^{\lambda(m)/2}
down the diagonal takes

f(\Phi^{-1}(m))=i\,e^{\lambda(m)}y
back to
iy
.
∎

The Lemma (and its proof) verify that
\operatorname{Gau}(P,\sigma)
are the sections of a family of Lie groups
G(P,\sigma)
. It also shows that
every diffeomorphism in
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}\operatorname{Diff}(\Sigma)
lifts to an automorphism in

\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}\operatorname{Aut}(P,\sigma)\to\phantom{\cdot}^{b}\operatorname{Diff}(\Sigma)
: For diffeomorphisms
supported in the interior of
\Sigma
this is done by lifting to an automorphisms of
P_{K}\subseteq P_{\operatorname{int}(\Sigma)}
; for
diffeomorphisms supported in a collar neighborhood of the boundary the Lemma gives an explicit lift.
∎

We see in particular that the restriction map
\operatorname{Gau}(P,\sigma)\to\operatorname{Gau}(\partial P,\partial\sigma)
is
not
surjective. On the other hand, we have:

Proposition 5.4
.

The restriction map

\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}\!\!\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,\sigma)\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(\partial P,\partial\sigma)

is surjective. In fact, every
element of
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(\partial P,\partial\sigma)
admits an extension to an element of
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}\!\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,\sigma)
which is supported on a collar neighborhood of the boundary.

Proof.

We work with the normal form (Lemma
4.7
) over a collar neighborhood
U
of the boundary. Given an element of
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(\partial P,\partial\sigma)
, with base map
\partial\Phi\in\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\partial\Sigma)
, choose an extension to a diffeomorphism
\Phi
with compact support on
U
. Lemma
5.3
shows how to lift

\Phi
to an element of
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}\!\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,\sigma)
, extending the given automorphism along the boundary.
∎

For the sake of completeness, we also give the infinitesimal descriptions. Recall that the
b
-tangent bundle
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}TM

of a manifold
M
with boundary is the vector bundle whose sections are vector fields tangent to the boundary
\partial M
.

Proposition 5.5
.

The kernel of bundle map
\operatorname{At}(P)\to V_{\sigma}
is a Lie subalgebroid
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}\operatorname{At}(P,\sigma)
of the Atiyah algebroid, with

\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}\mathfrak{aut}(P,\sigma)
as its space of sections. It fits into an exact sequence of Lie algebroids

(32)

0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}(P,\sigma)\longrightarrow\operatorname{At}(P,\sigma)\longrightarrow\phantom{\cdot}^{b}T\Sigma\longrightarrow 0.

Restriction to the boundary is a Lie algebroid isomorphism

(33)

\operatorname{At}(P,\sigma)|_{\partial\Sigma}\cong\operatorname{At}(\partial P,\partial\sigma)

inducing the inclusion
\mathfrak{g}(P,\sigma)|_{\partial{\Sigma}}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{g}(\partial P,\partial\sigma)
.

Proof.

Over the the interior, the kernel of
\operatorname{At}(P)\to V_{\sigma}
is the subalgebroid
\operatorname{At}(P_{K})
given by the reduction of structure group

P_{K}\subseteq P|_{\operatorname{int}(\Sigma)}
. Hence, it suffices to study the situation near the boundary. Using the normal form from Lemma
4.7
, we have
P|_{U}=U\times G
, with
\sigma(m,g)=g^{-1}.f(m)
for
f(x,y)=iy
.
The bundle map
\operatorname{At}(P|_{U})=TU\times\mathfrak{g}\to V_{\sigma}|_{U}=f^{*}T\overline{\mathbb{H}}
is given by

(v,\xi)\mapsto(T_{m}f)(v)-\xi^{\sharp}|_{f(m)},

where
\xi^{\sharp}
is the vector field on
\overline{\mathbb{H}}
generated by
X
. Since
\xi^{\sharp}
is tangent to
\partial\mathbb{H}
, we see that
for elements
(v,\xi)\in\operatorname{At}(P)|_{m},\ m\in\partial{\Sigma}
in the kernel of the map to
V_{\sigma}
, the vector
v
must itself be tangent to
\partial\Sigma
. The rest of the discussion is
as in the proof of Lemma
5.3
. In particular, infinitesimal gauge transformations are given by functions

\chi\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-\varrho^{2}\\
1&0\end{array}\right)\in C^{\infty}(U,\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{gau}(P|_{U}).

Every compactly supported vector field

tangent to

\left(\Big(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\lambda/2&0\\

∎

Remark 5.6
.

For the model case

,
the Lie algebroid

6.
Symplectic structure on
\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)

We shall now construct a symplectic structure
\omega
on the Teichmüller spaces of hyperbolic structures
on surfaces
\Sigma
with boundary.
Throughout,
\Sigma
will be compact, connected, and oriented, with a given pair
(P,\sigma)
as in (
23
),
satisfying
\mathsf{e}(P,\sigma)=\chi(\Sigma)
.
By Proposition
4.9
,
the space
\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}(P)
of
\sigma
-positive connections is non-empty.

6.1.
Hyperbolic metrics from positive connections

Our starting point is the following description of
\operatorname{Hyp}(\Sigma),\ \operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)
as quotients of spaces of flat connections.

Theorem 6.1
.

The space of hyperbolic structures on
\Sigma
is a quotient,

(34)

\operatorname{Hyp}(\Sigma)=\mathcal{A}_{\operatorname{flat}}^{\operatorname{pos}}(P)/\operatorname{Gau}(P,\sigma).

Taking a further quotient by the action of
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{0}\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\Sigma)
, we obtain

(35)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{\operatorname{flat}}^{\operatorname{pos}}(P)/\phantom{\cdot}^{0}\!\!\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,\sigma).

Proof.

By Proposition
4.3
, every
\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}_{\operatorname{flat}}(P)
determines a hyperbolic 0-metric
\mathsf{g}
on
\Sigma
; changing
\theta
by a gauge transformation in
\operatorname{Gau}(P,\sigma)
does not change
\mathsf{g}
.
Conversely, every hyperbolic 0-metric on
\Sigma
arises in this way, where
\theta
is unique up to the action of
\operatorname{Gau}(P,\sigma)
.
Indeed,
\mathsf{g}
determines a triple
(P^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime},\theta)
, with
\mathsf{e}(P^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})=\chi(\Sigma)
; hence
(P^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
is related to
(P,\sigma)
by a bundle isomorphism. It follows that
\mathsf{g}
is defined by a flat connection
\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}_{\operatorname{flat}}(P)
(the image of
\theta^{\prime}
under this isomorphism). This proves the description of
\operatorname{Hyp}(\Sigma)
; the remaining assertions are clear.
∎

Similarly, given
(Q,\tau)
as in (
26
), we saw that every projective structure on an oriented 1-manifold
\mathsf{C}
is obtained from a
\tau
-positive connection on
Q
.
The latter is unique up to the action of
\operatorname{Gau}(Q,\tau)
. Hence

\operatorname{Proj}(\mathsf{C})=\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}(Q)/\operatorname{Gau}(Q,\tau).

The quotient map intertwines the action of

\operatorname{Aut}_{+}(Q,\tau)
on connections with the action of
\operatorname{Diff}_{+}(\mathsf{C})=\operatorname{Aut}_{+}(Q,\tau)/\operatorname{Gau}(Q,\tau)
on projective structures.
Letting
Q=\partial P
and
\tau=\partial\sigma
, we see that the pullback map
\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}_{\operatorname{flat}}(P)\to\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}(\partial P)
descends to the natural map
\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)\to\operatorname{Proj}(\partial{\Sigma})
.

6.2.
Atiyah-Bott symplectic structure

Let
\cdot
denote the nondegenerate invariant symmetric bilinear form (‘metric’) on
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
given by

(36)

\xi\cdot\eta=\operatorname{tr}(\xi\eta).

It determines a bundle metric on
\mathfrak{g}(P)
, and an Atiyah-Bott symplectic structure on the
space
\mathcal{A}(P)
of connections by

\omega_{AB}(a,b)=\int_{\Sigma}a\stackrel{{\scriptstyle.}}{{\wedge}}b,

for

a,b\in T_{\theta}\mathcal{A}(P)=\Omega^{1}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{g}(P))
.
The symplectic structure is invariant under the action of
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}\!\operatorname{Aut}_{+}(P)
, and there is a natural affine moment map
for this action, involving both a bulk term and a boundary term. We refer to Appendix
B
for details.

For now, we consider the subgroup
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{0}\!\operatorname{Aut}_{+}(P)
of automorphisms
whose base map fixes the boundary. The moment map for this subgroup is given by

(37)

\mathcal{A}(P)\to\Omega^{2}(\Sigma,\operatorname{At}(P)^{*})\times\mathcal{A}(\partial P),\ \ \theta\mapsto\big(-F^{\theta}\cdot s^{\theta},\ \partial\theta\big).

Here
F^{\theta}\in\Omega^{2}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{g}(P))
is the curvature, and

(38)

s^{\theta}\colon\operatorname{At}(P)=TP/G\to\mathfrak{g}(P)

is the splitting (‘vertical projection’) defined by connection
\theta\in\mathcal{A}(P)
.

One obtains an affine moment map for the action of
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{0}\!\operatorname{Aut}_{+}(P,\sigma)\subseteq\phantom{\cdot}^{0}\!\operatorname{Aut}_{+}(P)
by projection, replacing
s^{\theta}
with
s^{\theta}|_{\operatorname{At}(P,\sigma)}
.
We would like to interpret (
35
) as a symplectic reduction for this moment map. As we will see, the boundary term causes some complications. Let us therefore begin with the case
\partial{\Sigma}=\emptyset
.

6.3.
Symplectic structure: the case
\partial{\Sigma}=\emptyset

If the boundary is empty, only the bulk term
-F^{\theta}\cdot s^{\theta}
of the moment map remains. The moment map for
\operatorname{Aut}_{+}(P,\sigma)
is thus given by

(39)

\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}(P)\to\mathfrak{aut}(P,\sigma)^{*}=\Omega^{2}(\Sigma,\operatorname{At}(P,\sigma)^{*}),\ \theta\mapsto-F^{\theta}\cdot s^{\theta}|_{\operatorname{At}(P,\sigma)}.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be a compact oriented surface without boundary, and
P\to\Sigma
a principal
G
-bundle with developing section satisfying
\mathsf{e}(P,\sigma)=\chi(\Sigma)
. Then

\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)=\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}(P)/\kern-3.01385pt/\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,\sigma),

a symplectic quotient, with a residual action of
\operatorname{MCG}(\Sigma)
preserving the symplectic structure.

Proof.

We claim that the zero level set of (
39
) is the space
\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}_{\operatorname{flat}}(P)
of flat connections.
The map
{s^{\theta}}\colon\operatorname{At}(P,\sigma)\to\mathfrak{g}(P)
restricts to the identity on the subbundles
\mathfrak{g}(P,\sigma)
, and gives a
commutative diagram

\textstyle{\operatorname{At}(P,\sigma)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{s^{\theta}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{g}(P)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{T\Sigma\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{V_{\sigma}}

where the vertical maps are the quotients maps for the subbundle
\mathfrak{g}(P,\sigma)
. For
\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}(P)
the lower horizontal map is an isomorphism, hence so is the upper map. Hence
F^{\theta}\cdot s^{\theta}(v)=0
for all

v\in\mathfrak{aut}(P,\sigma)=\Gamma(\operatorname{At}(P,\sigma))
if and only if
F^{\theta}=0
. Since
\operatorname{Aut}_{+}(P,\sigma)
preserves the
Atiyah-Bott form, the induced action of
\operatorname{Aut}_{+}(P,\sigma)/\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,\sigma)\cong\operatorname{MCG}(\Sigma)
is again symplectic.
∎

Since the condition
\mathsf{e}(P)=\chi(\Sigma)
determines
P

up to isomorphism, the symplectic 2-form
\omega
on
\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)
does not depend on its choice.
We will verify in Section
8
that this symplectic form is the standard Weil-Petersson form, given in Fenchel-Nielsen coordinates by Wolpert’s theorem.

It is clear from the construction that the residual action of the mapping class group
\operatorname{MCG}(\Sigma)
preserves the symplectic structure. Hence, we also have

\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)=\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}(P)/\kern-3.01385pt/\operatorname{Aut}_{+}(P,\sigma).

Remark 6.3
.

By a classical result, obtained independently by Goldman

[
13
]
and Hitchin
[
16
]
, the symplectic structure on Teichmüller space
\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)
may be obtained directly as a moduli space of flat connections, without having to invoke developing sections. That is,

(40)

\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)=\mathcal{A}(P)/\kern-3.01385pt/\operatorname{Gau}(P)

for any choice of
G
-bundle with
\mathsf{e}(P)=\chi(\Sigma)
.
The proof of (
40
) is more involved; we do not know of
an independent argument obtaining this result from
Proposition
6.2
.

6.4.
Symplectic structure: the case
\partial{\Sigma}\neq\emptyset

We now turn to the case of a possibly non-empty boundary. We shall denote
Q=\partial P,\ \tau=\partial\sigma
.
Equation (
35
) represents the Teichmüller space as a quotient
by the group
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{0}\!\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,\sigma)
, the identity component of automorphisms whose base map fixes
\partial{\Sigma}
.
However:

The pullback of
\omega_{AB}
to
\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}_{\operatorname{flat}}(P)
does
not
descend to the quotient.

The problem is that the restriction map
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{0}\!\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,\sigma)\to\operatorname{Gau}(Q)
is non-trivial (in fact, it is surjective). Hence, the boundary terms of the moment map are present.
Our strategy is to carry out the reduction in stages. Let

\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,Q,\sigma)\subseteq\phantom{\cdot}^{0}\!\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,\sigma)

be the kernel of the restriction map, and put

(41)

\widehat{\operatorname{Teich}}(\Sigma)=\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}_{\operatorname{flat}}(P)/\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,Q,\sigma)=\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)\times_{\operatorname{Proj}(\partial{\Sigma})}\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}(Q).

Elements of this space are represented by hyperbolic structures on
\Sigma
together with a lift of the corresponding projective structure on the boundary to a
\tau
-positive connection on
Q
.

Lemma 6.4
.

\widehat{\operatorname{Teich}}(\Sigma)
is a symplectic quotient

\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}(P)/\kern-3.01385pt/\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,Q,\sigma)
.

Proof.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{aut}(P,Q,\sigma)
is the space of sections of
\operatorname{At}(P,\sigma)
vanishing along the boundary
\partial{\Sigma}
.
By the same argument as in the proof of Proposition
6.2
, if

\theta\in\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}(P)
, we have
F^{\theta}\cdot s^{\theta}(v)=0
for all
v\in\mathfrak{aut}(P,Q,\sigma)
if and only if
F^{\theta}=0
.
Hence, the symplectic quotient by this subgroup is

\widehat{\operatorname{Teich}}(\Sigma)
.
∎

According to Proposition
5.4
, the map
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{0}\!\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,\sigma)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Gau}(Q,\tau)
is surjective; hence

\widehat{\operatorname{Teich}}(\Sigma)
has a residual action of the group
\operatorname{Gau}(Q,\tau)
, with quotient
{\operatorname{Teich}}(\Sigma)
.
Let

\mathcal{A}(Q,\tau)=\mathcal{A}(Q)/\operatorname{ann}(\mathfrak{gau}(Q,\tau)).

This is an affine
\operatorname{Gau}(Q,\tau)
-space, with linear action the coadjoint action on
\mathfrak{gau}(Q,\tau)^{*}
.

Lemma 6.5
.

The moment map for the action of
\operatorname{Gau}(Q,\tau)
on
\widehat{\operatorname{Teich}}(\Sigma)
is given by

\Psi\colon\widehat{\operatorname{Teich}}(\Sigma)\to\mathcal{A}(Q,\tau),\ \ [\theta]\mapsto\ \partial\theta\!\!\mod\operatorname{ann}(\mathfrak{gau}(Q,\tau)).

Proof.

This follows by reduction, since the boundary term of the moment map for the
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{0}\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P)
-action on
\mathcal{A}(P)
is
\theta\mapsto\partial\theta
.
∎

Note that
\Psi
takes values in the subspace
\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}(Q,\tau)=\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}(Q)/\operatorname{ann}(\mathfrak{gau}(Q,\tau))
.
It turns out that the image of this map is a single coadjoint orbit:

Lemma 6.6
.

The action of
\operatorname{Gau}(Q,\tau)
on
\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}(Q,\tau)
is free and transitive.

Proof.

This is a well-known fact from Drinfeld-Sokolov theory. We may assume that
Q=S^{1}\times G
, with
\tau(g,x)=g^{-1}\cdot 0
. The connections on
Q
are described by their connection 1-forms
A\in\Omega^{1}(S^{1},\mathfrak{g})
. Write

(42)

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{\frac{1}{2}}s&a\\
u&-{\frac{1}{2}}s\end{array}\right)\ {\mathsf{d}}x

with functions
a,s,u\in C^{\infty}(S^{1})
; the connection is
\tau
-positive if and only if
a>0
.
Taking the quotient by
\operatorname{ann}(\mathfrak{gau}(Q,\tau))\cong\Omega^{1}(S^{1},\mathfrak{n^{-}})
amounts to omitting the lower left corner; hence
a,s
serve as parameters on
\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}(Q,\tau)
. There is a unique gauge transformation by an element
h\in C^{\infty}(S^{1},B^{-})=\operatorname{Gau}(Q,\tau)
putting (
42
) into Drinfeld-Sokolov normal form,
that is, having
1
in the upper right corner and with vanishing diagonal entries. Explicitly,

h=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
-{\frac{1}{2}}s+{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}&1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a^{-\frac{1}{2}}&0\\
0&a^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{array}\right).

In other words,
h
is the unique element taking the class of
A
in
\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}(Q,\tau)
to the base point of
\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}(Q,\tau)
given by

.
∎

Since
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}(Q,\tau)
is a coadjoint orbit, it has a unique symplectic structure such that the

\operatorname{Gau}(Q,\tau)
-action is Hamiltonian, with moment map the inclusion. (See Appendix
A.1
.)

By the well-known ‘shifting trick’ from symplectic geometry, the quotient

\widehat{\operatorname{Teich}}(\Sigma)/\operatorname{Gau}(Q,\tau)
may be recast as a symplectic quotient. Let
\mathcal{O}^{-}
be the space
\mathcal{O}
with the opposite symplectic structure.

Theorem 6.7
.

The Teichmüller space is a symplectic quotient

\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)=(\widehat{\operatorname{Teich}}(\Sigma)\times\mathcal{O}^{-})/\kern-3.01385pt/\operatorname{Gau}(Q,\tau),

where
\widehat{\operatorname{Teich}}(\Sigma)=\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}(P)/\kern-3.01385pt/\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,Q,\sigma)
.
In particular,
\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)
acquires a symplectic structure. The action of

\operatorname{MCG}(\Sigma)
on
\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)
preserves the symplectic structure.

Proof.

Only the final claim remains to be proved. The action
of
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}\operatorname{Aut}_{+}(P,\sigma)
on the space
\mathcal{A}^{\operatorname{pos}}(P)
preserves the Atiyah-Bott symplectic structure, and restricts to an action on the space of flat connections. We hence obtain a symplectic action of
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}\operatorname{Aut}_{+}(P,\sigma)/\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,Q,\sigma)
on
\widehat{\operatorname{Teich}}(\Sigma)
.
This contains
\operatorname{MCG}(\Sigma)=\operatorname{Aut}_{+}(P,Q,\sigma)/\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,Q,\sigma)

and
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{0}\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,\sigma)/\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{o}}(P,Q,\sigma)=\operatorname{Gau}(Q,\tau)
as commuting subgroups.
The moment map for the
\operatorname{Gau}(Q,\tau)
-action is
\operatorname{MCG}(\Sigma)
-invariant; hence we obtain a
symplectic action of
\operatorname{MCG}(\Sigma)

on the quotient.
∎

The symplectic structure obtained in this way does not depend on the choice of
(P,\sigma)
subject to the condition

\mathsf{e}(P,\sigma)=\chi(\Sigma)
, since any two choices are related by a bundle isomorphism (Proposition

4.8
).
The intermediate space

\widehat{\operatorname{Teich}}(\Sigma)
depends on the choice, but only through the boundary restriction
(Q,\tau)
.
There is a canonical choice for the boundary restriction, and hence of the space
\widehat{\operatorname{Teich}}(\Sigma)
,
coming from the theory of Drinfeld-Sokolov reduction. We will discuss it in the next
section, since it will also lead to a simpler description of
\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)
.

Remark 6.8
.

It would be interesting to have a construction of the symplectic structure directly from the metric, as in the work of
Tromba
[
35
]
(see also Donaldson
[

]
, Diez-Ratiu
[

]
).

7.
\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)
as a Hamiltonian Virasoro space

We will now verify that the map
\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)\to\operatorname{Proj}(\partial{\Sigma})
, taking the equivalence class of a hyperbolic structure on a surface with boundary to the induced projective structure on the boundary, is an affine moment map.
The affine structure on

\operatorname{Proj}(\partial{\Sigma})
comes from its identification with an affine subspace of the dual of the Virasoro Lie algebra
\mathfrak{vir}(\partial{\Sigma})
, at a suitable non-zero level. We will give explicit formulas for the Hill operator on the boundary, in terms of data coming from the hyperbolic 0-metric.

7.1.

We shall need some background material. For more detailed information, see the standard references

25

as well as
our earlier paper
[
3

The Hill potential corresponding to the hyperbolic 0-metric

7.3.
Hill potential in terms of geodesic curvature

We will now give a second description of the Hill operator of a hyperbolic 0-metric
\mathsf{g}
, motivated by the discussion in Maldacena-Stanford-Yang
[
20
, Section 3]
. Observe that the function
a(x)

in (
52
) may be read off from the leading term of the volume form;

{\mathsf{d}}\operatorname{vol}_{\mathsf{g}}=\frac{1}{y^{2}}\big(a(x)+O(y^{1})\big){\mathsf{d}}x\wedge{\mathsf{d}}y.

For
y>0
, let
k(x,y)
be the geodesic curvature of the curve
t\mapsto(x+t,y)
.
Recall that for the standard hyperbolic metric on the upper half plane, the horizontal lines all have geodesic curvature equal to
1
. Hence

k(x,y)=1
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{H}
. It turns out that in general,
k(x,y)=1+O(y^{2})
:

Lemma 7.3
.

For every hyperbolic 0-metric, the limit

c(x)=\lim_{y\to 0}\frac{k(x,y)-1}{y^{2}}

exists and defines a smooth function of
x
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.9
, the hyperbolic 0-metric may be written

\mathsf{g}=\frac{1}{g^{2}}({\mathsf{d}}f^{2}+{\mathsf{d}}g^{2})

for functions
f,g
with
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x,0)>0
and
g(x,0)=0,\ \frac{\partial g}{\partial y}(x,0)>0
.
That is,
(f,g)
defines a local isometry to
\overline{\mathbb{H}}
.
The image of the curve
t\mapsto(x+t,y)
under this isometry is the curve
t\mapsto(f(x+t,y),g(x+t,y))
in
\mathbb{H}
; its geodesic curvature
k(x,y)
is computed as

k=\frac{f^{\prime}}{\big((f^{\prime})^{2}+(g^{\prime})^{2}\big)^{1/2}}+f\frac{f^{\prime}g^{\prime\prime}-f^{\prime\prime}g^{\prime}}{\big((f^{\prime})^{2}+(g^{\prime})^{2}\big)^{3/2}},

where the prime denotes
x
-derivatives. Substituting Taylor series

f(x,y)\sim\sum_{i}f_{i}(x)y^{i},\ g(x,y)\sim\sum_{i}g_{i}(x)y^{i}

one finds, by direct but somewhat lengthy calculation,

k(x,y)=1+c(x)y^{2}+y^{3}

with

(53)

c=\frac{g_{1}g_{1}^{\prime\prime}}{(f_{0}^{\prime})^{2}}-\frac{g_{1}g_{1}^{\prime}f_{0}^{\prime\prime}}{(f_{0}^{\prime})^{3}}-{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{(g_{1}^{\prime})^{2}}{(f_{0}^{\prime})^{2}}.

(The calculation requires writing
f,g
up to second order, but
f_{1},f_{2},g_{2}
do not enter the final expression.)
∎

Theorem 7.4
.

The Hill potential is given by

(54)

T={\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{a^{\prime\prime}}{a}-\frac{3}{2}\big(\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\big)^{2}\right)+\frac{a^{2}}{2}c

where
c
is obtained from the limit of the geodesic curvatures of the curves
t\mapsto(x+t,y)
as

c(x)=\lim_{y\to 0}(k(x,y)-1)/y^{2}
.

Proof.

Continuing the notation from the proof of Lemma
7.3
, we may take

\alpha_{1}=\frac{{\mathsf{d}}f}{g},\ \alpha_{2}=\frac{{\mathsf{d}}g}{g}

as an adapted orthonormal coframe. Using the Taylor expansion of
f,g
, we find

\alpha_{1}=\frac{1}{y}(\frac{f_{0}^{\prime}}{g_{1}}{\mathsf{d}}x+\ldots),\ \ \ \alpha_{2}=\frac{{\mathsf{d}}y}{y}+\frac{g_{1}^{\prime}}{g_{1}}{\mathsf{d}}x+\ldots,\ \ \alpha_{1}+\kappa=0

where dots indicate terms that pull back to zero on the boundary
y=0
. Hence, the functions
a,s,u
are given by

a=\frac{f_{0}^{\prime}}{g_{1}},\ \ s=\frac{g_{1}^{\prime}}{g_{1}},\ \ u=0.

Using the formula (
53
) for
k(x)
, this gives

c=\frac{1}{a^{2}}\Big(s^{\prime}-\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}s-\frac{1}{2}s^{2}\Big).

Now use (
52
).
∎

8.
The symplectic form in Fenchel-Nielsen coordinates

8.1.
Fenchel-Nielsen parameters

Let
\Sigma
be a compact, connected, oriented surface (possibly with boundary), of negative Euler characteristic

\chi(\Sigma)<0
.
The construction of Fenchel-Nielsen parameters on
\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)

for surfaces without boundary is well-explained in

[
11
]
; we describe a straightforward generalization to the case of a possibly non-empty boundary.

Recall first that every simple, closed curve
\mathsf{D}\subseteq\Sigma
, neither contractible nor homotopic to a boundary
component, determines a
twist flow

\mathbb{R}\times\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)\to\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)
:
Given
\mathsf{g}
, one obtains a new metric
\mathsf{g}_{\tau}
by cutting the surface along the geodesic homotopic to
\mathsf{D}
,
and gluing the two sides back together after rotating (twisting) one of the ends by an amount
\tau
.

Remark 8.1
.

A more detailed description: Given
[\mathsf{g}]\in\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)
, choose a representative
\mathsf{g}

having
\mathsf{D}
as a closed geodesic. A collar neighborhood
U
of
\mathsf{D}
is isometric to a neighborhood of the geodesic of a hyperbolic cylinder (see
2.4.2
), and so is isometric to
\mathsf{D}\times(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
with the hyperbolic metric (
8
). For any
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain a new hyperbolic metric
\mathsf{g}_{\tau}
by letting
\mathsf{g}_{\tau}|_{\Sigma-U}=\mathsf{g}|_{\Sigma-U}
and taking

\mathsf{g}_{\tau}|_{U}
to be the pullback of
\mathsf{g}|_{U}
under the diffeomorphism

(56)

(x,u)\mapsto\big(x+\frac{\tau}{\ell}f(u),u\big),

where
f(u)=0
for
u<-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon
and
f(u)=1
for
u>{\frac{1}{2}}\epsilon
.
The twist flow is given by
[\mathsf{g}]\mapsto[\mathsf{g}_{\tau}]
.

Since
\chi(\Sigma)<0
, we may choose a pairs-of-pants decomposition of
\Sigma
. There are
2g-2+r=-\chi(\Sigma)
distinct pants; their boundary curves consist of the boundary loops

\mathsf{C}_{j},\ j=1,\ldots,r
of
\Sigma
and
3g-3+r
simple closed curves
\mathsf{D}_{i}\subseteq\operatorname{int}(\Sigma)
.

Each of the
\mathsf{D}_{i}
defines a length parameter
\ell_{i}>0
(the length of the unique closed geodesic homotopic to
\mathsf{D}_{i}
), as well as a twist flow.
In addition, each boundary component
\mathsf{C}_{j}
determines a length parameter
\ell_{j}
(given by the length of the unique geodesic
of
[\mathsf{g}]
homotopic to the ideal boundary
\mathsf{C}_{j}
) as well as an action of
\widetilde{\operatorname{Diff}}_{\mathsf{o}}(\mathsf{C}_{j})
(coming from the action of diffeomorphisms in
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathsf{o}}(\Sigma)
that are supported in collar neighborhoods of the
\mathsf{C}_{j}
). These actions
on
\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)
all commute, with quotient
\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{3g-3}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{r}
given by the length parameters.
This action has a global slice, determined by the choice of a system of
model seams
. Choose an embedded 1-dimensional submanifold
E\subseteq\Sigma

with boundary
\partial E\subseteq\partial\Sigma
, in such a way for any two distinct boundary circles of a given pants
P
,
there is a unique component of
P\cap E
connecting those two boundary components. We also assume that
P\cap E
meets these
boundary components transversely. The three components of
P\cap E
are the model seams for the pair of pants
P
.
Finally, choose orientation preserving parametrizations
\mathsf{C}_{j}\cong S^{1}
, such that

\partial E\cap\mathsf{C}_{j}
maps to the antipodal points
\{-1,1\}\in S^{1}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
.

The desired slice consists of all
[\mathsf{g}]\in\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)
, where
\mathsf{g}
is a hyperbolic 0-metric such that
(i) all
\mathsf{D}_{i}
are geodesics, (ii) the connected components of
E-\partial E\subseteq\operatorname{int}(\Sigma)
are geodesics, (iii) the projective structure on the boundary components
\mathsf{C}_{j}
is constant (i.e.,
S^{1}
-equivariant). This gives
an identification

(57)

\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)\cong(\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\mathbb{R})^{3g-3+r}\times\prod_{j=1}^{r}(\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\widetilde{\operatorname{Diff}}_{\mathsf{o}}(S^{1}))

with the slice given as
(\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times 0)\times\prod_{j=1}^{r}(\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\operatorname{Id})^{r}
. Denote the corresponding
parameters by
\ell_{i},\tau_{i},\ell_{j},{\mathsf{F}}_{j}
; we choose the parametrization in such a way that the
i
-th twist flow is given by

\tau_{i}\mapsto\tau_{i}+\tau
(leaving all other parameters unchanged) and the
j
-th action of
{\mathsf{F}}\in\widetilde{\operatorname{Diff}}_{\mathsf{o}}(S^{1})

is given by
{\mathsf{F}}_{j}\mapsto{\mathsf{F}}_{j}\circ{\mathsf{F}}^{-1}
(leaving all other parameters unchanged).

8.2.
Related Teichmüller spaces

Given a hyperbolic 0-metric
\mathsf{g}
on
\Sigma
, each boundary component
\mathsf{C}_{j}
determines a unique simple, closed geodesic
\mathsf{C}_{j}^{\prime}\subseteq\Sigma
homotopic to
\mathsf{C}_{j}
; this is the geodesic end of the
j
-th boundary trumpet. Removing the trumpets creates a surface

\Sigma^{\prime}
with geodesic boundary
\sqcup_{j}\mathsf{C}_{j}^{\prime}
, called the
compact core
of
\Sigma
. Of course,
\Sigma^{\prime}
is diffeomorphic to
\Sigma

(as a surface with boundary).
The map

(58)

\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Teich}_{\operatorname{geod}}(\Sigma),\ \ \

taking the equivalence class of a hyperbolic 0-metric on
\Sigma
to the equivalence class of the (ordinary) hyperbolic metric on
\Sigma
with

, is the quotient maps for the action of
\prod_{i=1}^{r}\widetilde{\operatorname{Diff}}_{\mathsf{o}}(\mathsf{C}_{i})
. The corresponding Fenchel-Nielsen description just omits the
\widetilde{\operatorname{Diff}}_{\mathsf{o}}(S^{1})
-factors in (
57

of the boundary components, one obtains the space
\operatorname{Teich}_{\operatorname{geod}}(\Sigma,b_{1},\ldots,b_{r})
.
As another variation, having chosen parametrizations
\mathsf{C}_{j}\cong S^{1}
of the boundary components,
we may consider the subspace

\operatorname{Teich}_{\operatorname{bordered}}(\Sigma)\subseteq\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma)

for
which the projective structure on each
\mathsf{C}_{j}
is ‘constant’ (i.e., invariant under rigid rotations). On this subspace, we have a residual action of
\mathbb{R}^{r}
, where the
j
-th copy of
\mathbb{R}
rotates the
j
-th boundary component. This version of the Teichmüller space may be interpreted as a space of hyperbolic metrics with geodesic boundary, together with a ‘marking’ on each boundary component.
This space of ‘bordered’ hyperbolic metrics appears in Mirzakhani’s work, see
[
24
, Section 4]
.
The Fenchel-Nielsen description becomes

\operatorname{Teich}_{\operatorname{bordered}}(\Sigma)\cong(\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\mathbb{R})^{3g-3+2r}.

One can also consider mixtures of such spaces, e.g., taking some boundary components to be ideal boundaries (with
\varrho^{-2}
-boundary behaviour of the metric), other boundaries as marked
geodesic boundaries.

8.5.
Darboux coordinates on the trumpet space

Consider the symplectic structure on

60

with
{\mathsf{F}}

The expression

Proposition 8.4
.

\omega_{\widetilde{N}}=-{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathsf{d}}\ell\wedge{\mathsf{d}}u_{0}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{S^{1}}\ {\mathsf{d}}u\wedge{\mathsf{d}}u^{\prime}

(a scalar function of
(\ell,\widetilde{\mathsf{F}})\in\widetilde{N}

Proof.

We work out the terms appearing in

according to their homogeneity with respect to

The term of homogeneity

The terms of homogeneity

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{S^{1}}\ell^{2}{\mathsf{d}}\widetilde{{\mathsf{F}}}\wedge{\mathsf{d}}\widetilde{{\mathsf{F}}}^{\prime}+{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{S^{1}}(\widetilde{{\mathsf{F}}}^{\prime}-1){\mathsf{d}}\widetilde{{\mathsf{F}}}\wedge\ell{\mathsf{d}}\ell

Appendix A
Affine moment maps, central extensions

A.1.
Central extensions

Let
H
be a Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
. Given a central extension

0\to\mathbb{R}\to\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}\to\mathfrak{h}\to 0,

let
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
be the affine space of linear functionals taking
1\in\mathbb{R}
to
1
. The adjoint action of
H
on
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}

defines an affine
H
-action on this space, with linear part the coadjoint action on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
.
Conversely, suppose
\mathcal{E}
is an affine space with an affine
H
-action, with underlying linear
H
-space the
coadjoint representation. For
\mu\in\mathcal{E}
, the map
\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}^{*},\ \xi\mapsto\xi.\mu
,
defined by the infinitesimal action, is a
Lie algebra cocycle. Suppose this cocycle is skew-symmetric:

(64)

\langle\xi.\mu,\ \eta\rangle=-\langle\eta.\mu,\ \xi\rangle

for all
\xi,\eta
. (This condition does not depend on the choice of
\mu
.) Then one obtains a central extension:
Take
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}
to be the vector space of affine-linear functions on
\mathcal{E}
, with bracket

[\widehat{\xi},\widehat{\eta}](\mu)=\langle\xi.\mu,\ \eta\rangle.

Here
\xi,\eta
are the linear functionals underlying
\widehat{\xi},\widehat{\eta}\in\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}
. The affine space
\mathcal{E}
is a Poisson submanifold of
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
, with symplectic leaves the coadjoint orbits. The action groupoid
H\times\mathcal{E}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{E}
has a canonical
symplectic structure making it into a symplectic groupoid.
See
[
3
, Example A.12]
for further discussion.

A.2.
Affine moment maps

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an affine
H
-space as above, with underlying linear
H
-space the coadjoint representation.
Given an
H
-manifold
M
with a closed invariant 2-form
\omega
, we may consider
\mathcal{E}
-valued affine moment maps

\Phi\colon M\to\mathcal{E};

that is,
\Phi
is
H
-equivariant and satisfies the moment map condition
4
4

4

Generating vector fields are defined in terms of their action on functions as
(\xi_{M}f)(m)=\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}f\big(\exp(-t\xi).m\big)
. With this convention,
\xi\mapsto\xi_{M}
is a Lie algebra morphism. Note that if
M=\mathcal{E}
is an affine space, then

\xi_{M}(\mu)=-\xi.\mu
.

\omega(\xi_{M},\cdot)=-\langle{\mathsf{d}}\Phi,\xi\rangle.

Note that the differential
{\mathsf{d}}\Phi
is a 1-form with values in the linear space
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, hence its pairing with
\xi
is defined.
Examples of Hamiltonian
H
-spaces with
\mathcal{E}
-valued moment maps are the coadjoint orbits in
\mathcal{E}
, with the KKS symplectic structure.

A necessary condition for the existence of an affine moment map (for some
\mathcal{E}
) is that the 1-forms
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M,\mathfrak{h}^{*})
, given as

\langle\alpha,\xi\rangle=-\omega(\xi_{M},\cdot)
, are exact. In fact, this condition is sufficient as well:

Proposition A.1
.

Let
M
be a connected
H
-manifold, with an invariant closed 2-form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(M)
. Suppose that the 1-form

\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M,\mathfrak{h}^{*})

given as
\langle\alpha,\xi\rangle=\omega(\xi_{M},\cdot)
is exact. Let
\mathcal{E}
be the affine space of all primitives of
\alpha
, and let

\Phi\colon M\to\mathcal{E}

be the map taking any point of
M
to the unique primitive vanishing at that point.
Then
\mathcal{E}
is an affine
H
-space, with underlying linear action the coadjoint action, satisfying the skew-symmetry (
64
).
Furthermore,
\Phi

is an affine moment map.

Proof.

Since
M
is connected, a primitive of
\alpha
is unique up to a constant
function with values in
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. This shows that
\mathcal{E}
is an affine space over
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. The group
H
acts on
\mathcal{E}\subseteq C^{\infty}(M,\mathfrak{h}^{*})
by

(h.f)(m)=\operatorname{Ad}_{h}\big(f(h^{-1}.m)\big)
; hence the difference of two elements transforms under the coadjoint action.
For
f\in\mathcal{E}
we have

\langle\xi.f,\eta\rangle=\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{M}}f+\operatorname{ad}_{\xi}f,\eta\rangle=-\omega(\xi_{M},\eta_{M})-\langle f,[\xi,\eta]\rangle,

which is skew-symmetric in
\xi,\eta
. To verify that

\Phi
(as in the proposition) is a moment map, fix
m_{0}\in M
.
Then
\Phi(m_{0})(m)=\int_{m_{0}}^{m}\alpha=-\Phi(m)(m_{0})
. Hence, the map
m\mapsto\Phi(m)|_{m_{0}}
is a primitive for
-\alpha
. This shows

\langle{\mathsf{d}}\Phi,\xi\rangle=-\alpha(\xi)=-\omega(\xi_{M},\cdot)
.
∎

Note that
\mathcal{E}
, and hence the central extension of
\mathfrak{h}
, is determined by the pullback of
\omega
to any
G
-invariant submanifold of
M
. For example, if the action has a fixed point, or more generally if it admits an invariant isotropic submanifold, then the central extension is trivial, and the action admits an ordinary
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
-valued moment map.

The constructions above apply to infinite-dimensional settings, provided that one has a reasonable notion of smooth dual.
In particular, the affine action of
\operatorname{Diff}_{+}(\mathsf{C})
on the space
\operatorname{Hill}(\mathsf{C})
of Hill operators (Section
7.1
)
defines a central extension of
\operatorname{Vect}(\mathsf{C})
, the Virasoro algebra.

Appendix B
Gauge theory constructions

In this appendix, we review the Atiyah-Bott construction for principal
G
-bundles
P\to\Sigma
over oriented surfaces with boundary. Here

G
is any Lie group with an invariant inner product on its Lie algebra (we are mainly interested in the case
G=\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
).

B.1.
Atiyah algebroid, connections

Let
P\to M
be a principal
G
-bundle. The groups of gauge transformations and automorphisms are denoted
\operatorname{Gau}(P)\subseteq\operatorname{Aut}(P)
.
The
Atiyah algebroid

\operatorname{At}(P)=TP/G\to M
is the Lie algebroid whose sections are the

G
-invariant vector fields on
P
, that is, infinitesimal automorphisms. It fits into the exact sequence of Lie algebroids

(65)

0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}(P)\longrightarrow\operatorname{At}(P)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{a}}}{{\longrightarrow}}TM\longrightarrow 0,

with the adjoint bundle
\mathfrak{g}(P)=P\times_{G}\mathfrak{g}
. On the level of sections, this is the exact sequence

0\to\mathfrak{gau}(P)\to\mathfrak{aut}(P)\to\operatorname{Vect}(M)\to 0
.
A principal connection
\theta\in\Omega^{1}(P,\mathfrak{g})^{G}
is equivalent to a vector bundle splitting of the Atiyah sequence.
This may be described by either of the bundle maps

s^{\theta}\colon\operatorname{At}(P)\to\mathfrak{g}(P),\ \ \ j^{\theta}\colon TM\to\operatorname{At}(P)

called
vertical projection
and
horizontal lift
; thus
s^{\theta}|_{\mathfrak{g}(P)}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{g}(P)}
,

\mathsf{a}\circ j^{\theta}=\operatorname{id}_{TM},\ s^{\theta}\circ j^{\theta}=0
. The section
s^{\theta}(v)
corresponds to
\iota_{v}\theta
under the identification
\mathfrak{g}(Q)\cong\Omega^{0}(Q,\mathfrak{g})^{G}
.
We denote by

{\mathsf{d}}^{\theta}\colon\Omega^{p}(M,\mathfrak{g}(P))\to\Omega^{p+1}(M,\mathfrak{g}(P))

the covariant derivative; in terms of the identification of
forms
\beta\in\Omega^{\bullet}(M,\mathfrak{g}(P))
with
G
-basic forms

\widetilde{\beta}\in\Omega^{\bullet}(P,\mathfrak{g})
we have
\widetilde{{\mathsf{d}}^{\theta}\beta}={\mathsf{d}}\widetilde{\beta}+[\theta,\widetilde{\beta}]
. The curvature
F^{\theta}\in\Omega^{2}(M,\mathfrak{g}(P))
corresponds to the basic form
\widetilde{F}^{\theta}={\mathsf{d}}\theta+{\frac{1}{2}}[\theta,\theta]
.

The set
\mathcal{A}(P)
of principal connections is an affine space over

\Omega^{1}(M,\mathfrak{g}(P))
: given a smooth family of connections
\theta_{t}
with
\theta_{0}=\theta
, the corresponding tangent vector

\beta\in\Omega^{1}(M,\mathfrak{g}(P))
is determined by either one of the equations

\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\theta_{t}=\widetilde{\beta},\ \ \ \frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}s^{\theta_{t}}=\beta\circ\mathsf{a},\ \ \ \frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}j^{\theta_{t}}=-\beta.

Lemma B.1
.

The generating vector fields for the
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
-action on
\mathcal{A}(P)
are given by

(66)

v_{\mathcal{A}(P)}|_{\theta}=-{\mathsf{d}}^{\theta}(s^{\theta}(v))-\iota_{\mathsf{a}(v)}F^{\theta},\ \ v\in\mathfrak{aut}(P)=\Gamma(\operatorname{At}(P)).

In particular, for
v\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{g}(P))=\Omega^{0}(M,\mathfrak{g}(P))
, the generating vector field is
-{\mathsf{d}}^{\theta}v
.

Proof.

By our sign convention for generating vector fields,
v_{\mathcal{A}(P)}|_{\theta}=-v.\theta
. Regarding
v
as a
G
-invariant vector field
\widetilde{v}
on
P
, we have

\widetilde{v.\theta}=\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{v}}\theta={\mathsf{d}}\iota_{\widetilde{v}}\theta+\iota_{\widetilde{v}}{\mathsf{d}}\theta=({\mathsf{d}}+[\theta,\cdot])\iota_{\widetilde{v}}\theta+\iota_{\widetilde{v}}\widetilde{F}^{\theta}=\widetilde{{\mathsf{d}}^{\theta}(s^{\theta}(v))}+\widetilde{\iota_{\mathsf{a}(v)}F^{\theta}}.\qed

B.2.
Trivializations

Suppose
P\to M
admits a section
\iota\colon M\to P
, defining a trivialization

P\cong M\times G
such that
\iota(m)=(m,e)
with the principal action
a.(m,g)=(m,ga^{-1})
.
Connections
\theta\in\mathcal{A}(P)
are described in terms of their connection 1-forms
A=\iota^{*}\theta
by

\theta=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}(A)+\operatorname{pr}_{2}^{*}\theta^{L}

The trivial connection (given by
\theta=\operatorname{pr}_{2}^{*}\theta^{L}
) gives a splitting of the Atiyah algebroid

\operatorname{At}(P)\cong TM\oplus\mathfrak{g}(P)
. The trivialization of
P
determines a trivialization of all its associated bundles, and in particular
gives an isomorphism

(67)

\mathfrak{g}(P)\cong M\times\mathfrak{g}.

We have

s^{\theta}(X,\xi)=\xi+\iota_{X}A,\ \ j^{\theta}(X)=(X,-\iota_{X}A).

The covariant derivative is the operator on
\Omega^{\bullet}(M,\mathfrak{g}(P))\cong\Omega^{\bullet}(M,\mathfrak{g})
given by
{\mathsf{d}}_{A}={\mathsf{d}}+[A,\cdot]
; the curvature is
F_{A}=\iota^{*}\widetilde{F}^{\theta}={\mathsf{d}}A+{\frac{1}{2}}[A,A]
.

Remark B.2

(Signs, I)
.

We stress that the isomorphism

(68)

\mathfrak{gau}(P)=C^{\infty}(M,\mathfrak{g})

given by (
67
) differs by sign from the ‘standard’ identification as the Lie algebra of

\operatorname{Gau}(P)=C^{\infty}(M,G)
(with pointwise multiplication). In fact, (
68
) takes a function
\xi\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathfrak{g})
to the vertical vector field whose restriction to
\{m\}\times G
is
\xi(m)^{R}
. In particular, (
68
) induces
minus
the pointwise Lie bracket on
C^{\infty}(M,\mathfrak{g})
. More generally,

\mathfrak{aut}(P)=C^{\infty}(M,\mathfrak{g})\rtimes\operatorname{Vect}(M)
with the bracket

[(\xi,X),(\eta,Y)]=(-[\xi,\eta],\mathcal{L}_{X}\eta-\mathcal{L}_{Y}\xi).

Suppose

is a principal

carries an
invariant metric (denoted by a dot). Then
\mathfrak{g}(Q)
inherits a bundle metric. The affine space
\mathcal{A}(Q)

of connections has
\mathfrak{gau}(Q)^{*}=\Omega^{1}(\mathsf{C},\mathfrak{g}(Q))
as its underlying linear space,
where the pairing with
\mathfrak{gau}(Q)=\Omega^{0}(\mathsf{C},\mathfrak{g}(Q))
is given by the metric followed by integration. This identification takes
the linear part of the gauge action to the coadjoint action. Furthermore,
\langle\xi.\theta,\eta\rangle=-\langle{\mathsf{d}}^{\theta}\xi,\eta\rangle

satisfies the skew-symmetry condition
(
64
). By the discussion of Appendix
A.1
, this defines a
central extension of
\mathfrak{gau}(Q)
.

Remark B.3

(Signs, II)
.

For trivial bundles
Q=\mathsf{C}\times G
, one often uses the
‘standard’ identification
\mathfrak{gau}(Q)\cong C^{\infty}(\mathsf{C},\mathfrak{g})
to define the pairing. As explained in Remark
B.2
, this is opposite to the identification coming from
\mathfrak{g}(Q)\cong\mathsf{C}\times\mathfrak{g}
, and hence results in the opposite pairing.

We may also consider the larger group
\operatorname{Aut}(Q)
of all principal bundle automorphisms, with Lie algebra

\mathfrak{aut}(Q)=\Gamma(\operatorname{At}(Q))
; its smooth dual is
\mathfrak{aut}(Q)^{*}=\Omega^{1}(\mathsf{C},\operatorname{At}(Q)^{*})
.
Let

(69)

\mathcal{E}(Q)\subseteq\Gamma(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}\operatorname{At}(Q)^{*})

be the affine space of all fiberwise quadratic forms on
\operatorname{At}(Q)
whose restriction to
\mathfrak{g}(Q)
is given by
\xi\mapsto{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\cdot\xi
.
The underlying linear space consists of quadratic forms on
\operatorname{At}(Q)
whose restriction to
\mathfrak{g}(Q)
is zero; it is identified with
the space
\Omega^{1}(\mathsf{C},\operatorname{At}(Q)^{*})
, where an element
\gamma
of this space defines the quadratic form
w\mapsto\langle\gamma(\mathsf{a}(w)),w\rangle
.
The group
\operatorname{Aut}(Q)
acts on
\mathcal{E}(Q)
, and the underlying linear action is
coadjoint action. One may also check that it satisfies the skew-symmetry property (
64
); hence one obtains a
central extension of
\mathfrak{aut}(Q)
.

Remark B.4
.

There is a natural map

\mathcal{E}(Q)\to\mathcal{A}(Q),\ \ \phi\mapsto\theta

given by
s^{\theta}(v)\cdot\xi=-2\phi(v,\xi)
for
v\in\Gamma(\operatorname{At}(Q),\ \xi\in\mathfrak{g}(Q)

(where we think of
\phi
as a symmetric bilinear form).
This map is affine with respect to the quotient map
\Omega^{1}(\mathsf{C},\operatorname{At}(Q)^{*})\to\Omega^{1}(\mathsf{C},\mathfrak{g}(Q)^{*})
,
and determines a Lie algebra morphism
\widehat{\mathfrak{gau}}(Q)\to\widehat{\mathfrak{aut}}(Q)
lifting the natural inclusion.

Remark B.5
.

There is a natural

\operatorname{Aut}(Q)
-equivariant section
section

\mathcal{A}(Q)\to\mathcal{E}(Q),\ \ \theta\mapsto{\frac{1}{2}}s^{\theta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle.}}{{\vee}}s^{\theta}.

Here

denotes the product in the symmetric algebra. The map (
70
) is the moment map for the
\operatorname{Aut}(Q)
-action on

For a trivial bundle

B.4.
Atiyah-Bott

We now assume that
\Sigma
is a compact oriented surface, possibly with boundary, and that the Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}(G)
comes with an invariant metric. Let
P\to\Sigma
be a principal
G
-bundle.

Definition B.6
.

The
Atiyah-Bott form
on
\mathcal{A}(P)
is given by

\omega_{AB}(a,b)=\int_{\Sigma}a\cdot b,\ \ \ \ a,b\in\Omega^{1}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{g}(P)).

The Atiyah-Bott form
\omega_{AB}
is closed, by translation invariance, and nondegenerate in the weak sense that
\omega_{AB}(a,\cdot)=0\Leftrightarrow a=0
. The 2-form
is invariant under the action of the group
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}\!\!\operatorname{Aut}_{+}(P)
of principal bundle automorphisms whose base map lies in
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}\operatorname{Diff}_{+}(\Sigma)
. We are interested in a moment map for this action. The Lie algebra
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}\!\!\mathfrak{aut}(P)
consists of

G
-invariant vector fields on
P
that are tangent to
\partial P
; these are the sections of a Lie algebroid

\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}\!\!\operatorname{At}(P)
.
Let
\partial P=P|_{\partial\Sigma}
the boundary restriction of

P
. For a connection
\theta
, denote by
\partial\theta
its pullback to a connection on
P
.

Proposition B.7
.

The action of
\phantom{\cdot}{}^{b}\!\!\operatorname{Aut}_{+}(P)
on
\mathcal{A}(P)
has moment map

\mathcal{A}(P)\to\Omega^{2}(\Sigma,\operatorname{At}(P)^{*})\times\mathcal{E}(\partial P),\ \theta\mapsto(-F^{\theta}\cdot s^{\theta},{\frac{1}{2}}s^{\partial\theta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle.}}{{\vee}}s^{\partial\theta}).

For the action of
\operatorname{Gau}(P)
, the moment map takes values in
\Omega^{2}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{g}(P))\times\mathcal{A}(\partial P)
, and is given by

(-F^{\theta},\partial\theta)
.

Proof.

Let
v\in\phantom{\cdot}^{b}\mathfrak{aut}(P)=\Gamma(\phantom{\cdot}^{b}\!\!\operatorname{At}(P))
, with boundary restriction
\partial v
.
Given
b\in T_{\theta}\mathcal{A}(P)=\Omega^{1}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{g}(P))
, we have, using (
66
),

\displaystyle\omega_{AB}(v^{\sharp}|_{\theta},\,b)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}v^{\sharp}|_{\theta}\cdot b

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Sigma}\iota_{\mathsf{a}(v)}F^{\theta}\cdot b-\int_{\Sigma}{\mathsf{d}}^{\theta}(s^{\theta}(v))\cdot b

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\iota_{\mathsf{a}(v)}b\cdot F^{\theta}+\int_{\Sigma}s^{\theta}(v)\cdot{\mathsf{d}}^{\theta}b-\int_{\partial{\Sigma}}s^{\partial\theta}(\partial v)\cdot\partial b.

where
\partial b\in T_{\partial\theta}\mathcal{A}(\partial P)
is the pullback of
b
to the boundary.
Suppose
b\in T_{\theta}\mathcal{A}(P)
is realized as the velocity vector for a family of connections
\theta_{t}
with

\theta_{0}=\theta,\ \ \ \frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\theta_{t}=\widetilde{b}.

Then
\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}s^{\theta_{t}}=b\circ\mathsf{a},\ \frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}F^{\theta_{t}}={\mathsf{d}}^{\theta_{t}}b
,
and therefore

\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}F^{\theta_{t}}\cdot s^{\theta_{t}}=(b\circ\mathsf{a})\cdot F^{\theta}+s^{\theta}\cdot{\mathsf{d}}^{\theta}b.

Here
b\circ\mathsf{a},\ s^{\theta}
are regarded as sections of
\operatorname{At}(P)^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{g}(P)
, while
F^{\theta},\ {\mathsf{d}}^{\theta}b
are elements of

\Omega^{2}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{g}(P))
.
For the boundary term, we note

\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\left({\frac{1}{2}}s^{\partial\theta_{t}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle.}}{{\vee}}s^{\partial\theta_{t}}\right)=s^{\partial\theta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle.}}{{\vee}}(\partial b\circ\mathsf{a})=s^{\partial\theta}\cdot\partial b

where the last equality comes from the inclusion
\Omega^{1}(\partial{\Sigma},\operatorname{At}(\partial P)^{*})\hookrightarrow\Gamma(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\operatorname{At}(\partial P)^{*}))
.
This gives

\omega_{AB}(v^{\sharp}|_{\theta},\,b)=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\int_{\Sigma}F^{\theta_{t}}\cdot s^{\theta_{t}}(v)-\int_{\partial{\Sigma}}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}{\frac{1}{2}}s^{\partial\theta_{t}}\vee s^{\partial\theta_{t}}\Big)(\partial v,\cdot)\Big).\qed

Remark B.8

(Signs, III)
.

The sign in the moment map for
\operatorname{Gau}(P)
depends on the identifications
\mathfrak{gau}(P)\cong\Omega^{2}(P,\mathfrak{g}(P))
and

\mathfrak{gau}(\partial P)\cong\Omega^{1}(\partial P,\mathfrak{g}(\partial P))

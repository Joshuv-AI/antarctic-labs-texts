Affine vs. Stein in rigid geometry
Marco Maculan, Jérôme Poineau

Abstract
. We investigate the relationship between affine and Stein varieties in the context of rigid geometry. We show that the two concepts are much more closely related than in complex geometry, e.g. they are equivalent for surfaces. This rests on the density of algebraic functions in analytic functions. One key ingredient to prove such a density statement is an extension result for Cartier divisors.

Introduction

0.1.
Motivation

Let
K
be a complete non-trivially valued non-Archimedean field. A variety
X
over
K
, that is, a separated
K
-scheme of finite type, is Stein if the
K
-analytic space
X^{\textup{an}}
attached to
X
admits a closed
K
-analytic embedding in
\mathbb{A}^{n,\textup{an}}_{K}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
; see
4.1
. Needless to say, affine varieties are Stein. The aim of the present note is to investigate to which extent the converse statement holds true. The complex analytic version of the question is a classical problem, whose history (recalled below) consists mainly of negative results. For instance, given an integer
d\geqslant 2
, there is a
d
-dimensional smooth complex variety that is not affine but whose associated complex manifold is Stein. In stark contrast, and breaking the persisting analogy with complex geometry, we derive several general consequences of the Stein property for non-Archimedean varieties summarized as follows (see
theorems

8.1
,
8.2
and
8.3
):

Theorem A
.

A Stein variety
X
is quasi-affine and, for any closed subscheme
Y
of dimension
\leqslant 1
, the restriction map
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\to\Gamma(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})
is surjective. Furthermore, if the ring
\Gamma(X_{\red},\mathcal{O}_{X})
is Noetherian or if the
K
-algebra
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
is finitely generated, then
X
is affine.

Recall that a variety
X
is quasi-affine if it admits an open embedding into an affine variety. The
K
-algebra of regular functions on an algebraic group is finitely generated
[
DG70
, III.3.8]
. Therefore as a consequence we obtain a new proof of the following result previously proved in
[
Mac22
]
:

Corollary
.

An algebraic group is Stein if and only if it is affine.

Also, a result of Goodman-Landman (see
[
GL73
, proposition 3.13]
, or
proposition

7.12
) permits to derive the equivalence in the case of surfaces (see
corollary

8.4
):

Corollary
.

An algebraic surface is Stein if and only if it is affine.

0.2.
Complex background

As we are about to remind, the complex analogue of the previous statements is false—not to make too fine a pun, the behaviour in non-Archimedean geometry is far more rigid. Let
M_{\textup{dR}}(E)
be the moduli space of line bundles endowed with a connection on a complex elliptic curve
E
. The line bundles in question are necessarily of degree
0
, and forgetting the connection gives rise to a surjective map of complex varieties
\pi\colon M_{\textup{dR}}(E)\to\Pic^{0}(E)=E
. Tensor product of line bundles makes
M_{\textup{dR}}(E)
an algebraic group and
\pi
a morphism of algebraic groups. Its kernel is identified with the space of differential forms on
E
:

0\longrightarrow\mathbb{V}(\Gamma(E,\Omega^{1}_{E}))\longrightarrow M_{\textup{dR}}(E)\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow 0.

The algebraic group
M_{\textup{dR}}(E)
is
not
affine—the quotient of an affine group by a subgroup is affine, whereas the elliptic curve
E
is most certainly not. Actually, more is true: it is not even quasi-affine since every algebraic function on
M_{\textup{dR}}(E)
is constant (see
[
Bri09
, proposition 2.3]
). From the transcendental perspective, Riemann-Hilbert’s correspondence sets up a biholomorphism of
M_{\textup{dR}}(E)^{\textup{an}}
with the group of characters of the topological fundamental group of the elliptic curve

\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(\pi_{1}(E(\mathbb{C}),0),\mathbb{C}^{\times})\cong\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\mathbb{C}^{\times}.

In particular,
M_{\textup{dR}}(E)^{\textup{an}}
admits a closed holomorphic embedding in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
. Summing up, the complex algebraic group
M_{\textup{dR}}(E)
is not affine, but the complex Lie group
M_{\textup{dR}}(E)^{\textup{an}}
is Stein. Since it is of dimension
2
, this shows that the analogues of both corollaries to
A
do not hold over the complex numbers.

Main results

Over the non-Archimedean field

the technical crux leading to

Theorem B
.

Let

\Gamma(X,F)
is dense in
\Gamma(X^{\textup{an}},F^{\textup{an}})

See

. A series of comments:

(1)

Within the framework of Berkovich spaces, the topological space underlying the
K
-analytic space

is locally compact: when

is reduced, the topology on
\Gamma(X^{\textup{an}},\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\textup{an}})
is that of uniform convergence on compact subsets; when

is not reduced, as in complex analysis, the topology is finer.

(2)

An
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module

is

if the natural homomorphism

is injective. Therefore

applies in particular to vector bundles and coherent sheaves of ideals. However, we ignore whether it stays true for any coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module.

(3)

The techniques involved in the proof are fairly general and permit to carry on the proof when

is replaced by an affinoid

-algebra

(in the sense of Berkovich) and

by a separated
A
-scheme of finite type.

(4)

Let

be a variety and

a coherent

-module. If

is affine, then the density of
\Gamma(X,F)
in

is proper, the non-Archimedean GAGA theorem implies that the natural map

is an isomorphism. Therefore,

is most interesting when

belongs to neither of these two classes, and especially when

does not admit a proper morphism onto an affine variety.

(5)

has little to do with algebraic varieties: the only property we use is that they can be “compactified”. The general statement concerns the complement of an effective Cartier divisor in a proper

-analytic space (see
theorem

5.13
) or, even more generally, holomorphically convex (see

).

(6)

The argument for deducing

is a complex variety such that

is dense in

is Stein, then it satisfies the conclusions of

.

(7)

Consider the moduli space

of line bundles endowed with a connection on a complex elliptic curve

. Then, all of the algebraic functions on

are constant (see for instance

, proposition 2.3]
), while

is biholomorphic to

1.
Extending Cartier divisors: the algebraic case

1.1.
Statements

We start by recalling some basic notions concerning Cartier divisors in a scheme
X
. For a closed subscheme
Z\subset X
let
I_{Z}
denote the kernel of the restriction map
\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{Z}
. An
effective Cartier divisor

D\subset X
is a closed subscheme whose defining sheaf of ideals
I_{D}
is invertible. Let
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
denote the dual of
I_{D}
. The homomorphism
s_{D}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
dual to the inclusion
I_{D}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
is called the
canonical section
associated with
D
. For effective Cartier divisors
D,D^{\prime}\subset X
, the Cartier divisor
D+D^{\prime}
is the closed subscheme with sheaf of ideals
I_{D}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}I_{D^{\prime}}
whence the identity
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D+D^{\prime})=\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D^{\prime})
. If the closed immersion
D\to X
factors through
D^{\prime}
, then
I_{D^{\prime}}\subset I_{D}
and the sheaf of ideals
I_{D^{\prime}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
defines the Cartier divisor
D^{\prime}-D
. A closed subscheme
Z
of a scheme
X
is
finitely presented
if the closed immersion
i\colon Z\to X
is of finite presentation. Let
U\subset X
be an open subscheme. A morphism
X^{\prime}\to X
is called a
U
-admissible blow-up
if there exists a finitely presented closed subscheme
Z\subset X\smallsetminus U
such that
X^{\prime}
is isomorphic to the blow-up of
X
along
Z
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
D
be a closed subscheme of a Noetherian scheme

. Let

be an open subset such that

(1)

D\cap U
is scheme-theoretically dense in
D
;

(2)

D\cap U
is an effective Cartier divisor in
U
.

Then there is a
U
-admissible blow-up
\pi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
such that the strict transform of
D
in
X^{\prime}
is an effective Cartier divisor.

Over the ring of integers

Let

The strict transform

3.
Approximating essential singularities by poles: the formal case

Let
R
be the ring of integers of a field
K
complete with respect to a on-trivial non-Archimedean absolute value.

3.1.
Functions with polar singularities

The main result of this section concerns the approximation of formal functions having essential singularities at infinity by those having polar singularities. More precisely, let
D
be an effective Cartier divisor on a scheme
X
. Given an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
F
, for
d\in\mathbb{N}
set
F(dD):=F\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{O}_{X}(dD)
and let

F(\ast D):=\colim_{d\in\mathbb{N}}F(dD)

be the sheaf of sections of
F
with polar singularities along
D
, where the transition maps are induced by the canonical section
s_{D}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
. The
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
F
is
D
-torsion free
if
\id_{F}\otimes s_{D}\colon F\to F(D)
is injective. Let
U=X\smallsetminus D
and let
j\colon U\to X
be the open immersion. The canonical section induces an isomorphism
\mathcal{O}_{U}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)_{\rvert U}
hence an isomorphism
j^{\ast}F\cong j^{\ast}F(dD)
for each
d\in\mathbb{N}
. By taking the injective limit of the homomorphisms
F(dD)\to j_{\ast}j^{\ast}F(dD)\to j_{\ast}j^{\ast}F
, the latter map being the inverse of previous isomorphism, one obtains a homomorphism
F(\ast D)\to j_{\ast}j^{\ast}F
that will be referred to as the
restriction map
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
D
be an effective Cartier divisor in a quasi-compact scheme
X
and
F
a quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module. Then the restriction map induces an isomorphism

\colim_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\Gamma(X,F(dD))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Gamma(X\smallsetminus D,F).

Moreover if
X
is quasi-separated, then the map
F(\ast D)\to j_{\ast}j^{\ast}F
given by restriction is an isomorphism where
j\colon X\smallsetminus D\to X
is the open immersion.

Proof.

For an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
E
let
\Gamma(E,D):=\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\Gamma(X,E(dD))
. With this notation
\colim_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\Gamma(X,F(dD))
is the homogeneous localization of the
\Gamma(\mathcal{O}_{X},D)
-graded module
\Gamma(F,D)
with respect to the canonical section
s_{D}
, which is a homogeneous element of degree
1
. The statement then follows from
[
EGA III1
, proposition 1.4.5]
. For the latter statement notice that on a quasi-compact scheme, every open cover may be refined by a finite open affine cover. Moreover, if the scheme is quasi-separated, then the intersection of affine open subsets is a finite union of open affine subsets, hence quasi-compact. Thus the natural map

\colim_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\Gamma(V,F(dD))\to\Gamma(V,F(\ast D))
is an isomorphism for any quasi-compact open subset

by
[
Stacks
,
lemma 009F (4)
]
. Applying statement (1) to

, the composite map

\colim_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\Gamma(V,F(dD))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Gamma(V,F(\ast D))\longrightarrow\Gamma(V\cap(X\smallsetminus D),F)=\Gamma(V,j_{\ast}j^{\ast}F)

is an isomorphism. The result follows because quasi-compact open subsets form a basis for the topology of
X
.
∎

4.
A Remmert factorization theorem with boundary

Let
K
be a complete non-trivially valued non-Archimedean field. The analogue of
theorem

3.3
in the context of analytic spaces is naturally stated for holomorphically convex spaces. In absence of boundary these spaces have been characterized in
[
MP21
, theorem 6.1]
as being proper over boundaryless Stein spaces—this is what is called the Remmert factorization theorem in complex analysis. However affinoid spaces do have boundary and in order to include them we present here a version of Remmert factorization for spaces with boundary.

4.1.
Reminder on Stein spaces

We begin with a reminder on Stein spaces in the non-Archimedean framework, see also
[
MP21
]
. Recall that a
K
-analytic space

is said to

if, for

distinct, there is

such that
|f(x)|\neq|f(x^{\prime})|
. It is said
holomorphically convex
if, for each
C\subset X
compact, the holomorphically convex hull of
C
,

\hat{C}_{X}:=\{x\in X:|f(x)|\leqslant\|f\|_{C},f\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\}

is compact, where
\|f\|_{C}=\sup_{x\in C}|f(x)|
. An
affinoid Stein exhaustion
of
X
is a G-cover
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
X
made of affinoid domains such that, for
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
X_{i}
is a Weierstrass domain in

.
Here we say that

is
Stein
if it admits an affinoid Stein exhaustion. This is what is called being being W-exhausted by affinoid domains in
[
MP21
]
. A
K
-analytic space Stein
X
is holomorphically separable and holomorphically convex by
[
MP21
, theorem 1.1]
. Let

be a
K
-analytic space admitting an affinoid Stein exhaustion

. Then, for each coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module

(1)

for all

, the restriction map
\Gamma(X,F)\to\Gamma(X_{i},F)

(2)

for all

vanishes.

Given a

-affinoid space

-analytic space without boundary such that

and a closed embedding

-scheme of finite type

Let

.

-analytic spaces

5.

Let

5.1.
Statements

Given a coherent sheaf

Lemma 5.1

\colim_{i\in I}\Gamma(X,F_{i})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Gamma(X,\colim_{i\in I}F_{i}).

Proof.

Strictly speaking, one cannot apply directly
[

,

]
because the statement there is for a honest topological space, instead of a mere site. However, the proof goes through

replacing open subsets by compact analytic domains and open covers by

-covers.

7.2.
Stable codimension and affineness

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme. The codimension
\codim_{X}(x)
of a point
x\in X
is the dimension of the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
. Let
Z
be a closed subset of
X
. A generic point of
Z
is a generic point of one of its irreducible components. The codimension of
Z
in
X
is supremum of the codimension of the generic points:

\codim_{X}(Z):=\sup\{\codim_{X}(x):x\in Z\textup{ generic}\},

with the convention that it is
-\infty
when
Z
is empty.

Definition 7.14
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme and
Z
a closed subscheme of
X
.

(1)

The
stable codimension
of
Z
in
X
is
\codim^{\textup{s}}_{X}(Z):=\sup\codim_{X^{\prime}}(\pi^{-1}(Z))
the supremum ranging on the morphisms
\pi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
with
X^{\prime}
Noetherian.

(2)

The
finite type stable codimension
is
\codim^{\textup{sft}}_{X}(Z):=\sup\codim_{X^{\prime}}(\pi^{-1}(Z))
the supremum ranging on the finite type morphisms
\pi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
.

For affine Noetherian schemes in the literature the stable codimension (resp. the finite type stable codimension) is called the super height of the ideal defining the closed subset (with the reduced structure, say). Notwithstanding we lean for geometric-flavoured term codimension. Given a closed subset
Z
of a Noetherian scheme
X
and a finite open cover
X=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
we have

\codim^{\star}_{X}(Z)=\max_{i=1,\dots,n}\codim^{\star}_{X_{i}}(Z\cap X_{i}),\hskip 10.00002pt\star\in\{\textup{s},\textup{sft}\}.

Moreover the stable codimension
\codim^{\textup{s}}_{X}(Z)
of
Z
equals
\sup\dim A
the supremum ranging on Noetherian complete local rings
A
with a morphism
\pi\colon\Spec A\to X
such that
\pi^{-1}(Z)
is the unique closed point of
\Spec A
. Needless to say,

\codim_{X}(Z)\leqslant\codim^{\textup{sft}}_{X}(Z)\leqslant\codim^{\textup{s}}_{X}(Z).

The first inequality is in general strict, as the example of a non-irreducible scheme
X
shows with
Z
being one of the irreducible components. Instead, the key result is a theorem of Koh
[
Koh88
, theorem 1]
saying that the second is an equality for varieties over a field:

Theorem 7.15
.

If
X
is of finite type over a field, then
\codim^{\textup{sft}}_{X}(Z)=\codim^{\textup{s}}_{X}(Z)

Suppose

.

Let

be an

-scheme of finite type of dimension

. Then, the following are equivalent:

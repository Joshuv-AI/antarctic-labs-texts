1.
Introduction

1.1.
Introduction

Consider
\mathbb{R}^{N}
equipped with a root system
R
and a non-negative multiplicity function
k\geq 0
.
Let
dw(\mathbf{x})=\prod_{\alpha\in R}|\langle\alpha,\mathbf{x}\rangle|^{k(\alpha)}\;d\mathbf{x}

be the associated measure. For
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(dw)
and a measurable bounded set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we denote

(1.1)

f_{E}:=\frac{1}{w(E)}\int_{E}f(\mathbf{x})\,dw(\mathbf{x}).

Let
G
be the Coxeter group generated by the reflections
\sigma_{\alpha}
,

\alpha\in R
. For
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we set

\mathcal{O}(E)=\{\sigma(\mathbf{x}):\sigma\in G,\ \mathbf{x}\in E\}.

In Han, Lee, Li, and Wick
[
14
]
the authors investigated two types of
{\rm BMO}
and
{\rm VMO}
spaces in the Dunkl setting which are connected with two distances: the Euclidean distance
\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|
and the orbit distance
d(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\min_{\sigma\in G}\|\mathbf{x}-\sigma(\mathbf{y})\|
. The spaces
{\rm BMO}
and
{\rm BMO}_{d}
are defined as

\displaystyle{\rm BMO}=\{b\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(dw):\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}}<\infty\},\ \ \|b\|_{{\rm BMO}}:=\sup_{B}\frac{1}{w(B)}\int_{B}|b(\mathbf{x})-b_{B}|\,dw(\mathbf{x}),

\displaystyle{\rm BMO}_{d}=\{b\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(dw):\|b\|_{d}<\infty\},\ \ \|b\|_{d}:=\sup_{B}\frac{1}{w(\mathcal{O}(B))}\int_{\mathcal{O}(B)}|b(\mathbf{x})-b_{{\mathcal{O}(B)}}|\,dw(\mathbf{x}),

where the supremum is taken over all Euclidean balls
B=B(\mathbf{y},r)=\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{z}\|<r\}
. The space
{\rm BMO}_{d}
is a proper subspace of
{\rm BMO}
(see
[
16
]
) and

\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}}\leq C\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}_{d}}\quad\text{for }b\in{\rm BMO}_{d}.

In
[
14
]
commutators of
{\rm BMO}
and
{\rm BMO}_{d}
functions with the Dunkl-Riesz transforms
R_{j}
are studied. The Dunkl-Riesz transforms are Calderón-Zygmund type operators which are formally
defined by
R_{j}=T_{e_{j}}(-\Delta_{k})^{-1/2}
, where
T_{e_{j}}
are the Dunkl
operators (see (
2.6
)) and
\Delta_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}T_{e_{j}}^{2}
is the Dunkl Laplacian. They were studied by Thangavelu and Xu
[
24
]
(in dimension 1 and in the product case) and by Amri and Sifi
[
1
]
(in higher dimensions) who proved their bounds on
L^{p}(dw)
spaces. One of the main results of
[
14
]
asserts that if
b\in{\rm BMO}_{d}
, then the commutator
[b,R_{j}]f(\mathbf{x})=b(\mathbf{x})R_{j}f(\mathbf{x})-R_{j}(b(\cdot)f(\cdot))(\mathbf{x})
is a bounded operator on
L^{p}(dw)
for
1<p<\infty
and

(1.2)

\|[b,R_{j}]\|_{L^{p}(dw)\to L^{p}(dw)}\lesssim\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}_{d}}.

Conversely, if for
b\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(dw)
, the commutator
[b,R_{j}]
is bounded on
L^{p}(dw)
for some
1<p<\infty
, then
b\in{\rm BMO}
and

(1.3)

\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}}\lesssim\|[b,R_{j}]\|_{L^{p}(dw)\to L^{p}(dw)}.

The authors of
[
14
]
raised the question if the possible lower bound
\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}_{d}}\lesssim\|[b,R_{j}]\|_{L^{p}(dw)\to L^{p}(dw)}
holds true.

Our first goal in this note is to improve (
1.2
) by showing that it holds for
b\in{\rm BMO}
, that is, there is a constant
C_{p}>0

such that

(1.4)

\|[b,R_{j}]\|_{L^{p}(dw)\to L^{p}(dw)}\leq C_{p}\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}}

(see Theorem
3.1
).

Let us point out that (
1.4
) gives a negative answer to the question formulated above, because otherwise we would get
\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}_{d}}\lesssim\|[b,R_{j}]\|_{L^{p}(dw)\to L^{p}(dw)}\lesssim\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}}
, which is impossible (see
[
16
]
).

An essential part of
[
14
]
is devoted for studying compactness of the commutators of
{\rm VMO}
functions with the Dunkl-Riesz transforms. The
{\rm VMO}
and
{\rm VMO}_{d}
spaces are defined as the closures of the sets of compactly supported functions from the Lipschitz spaces
\Lambda
and
\Lambda_{d}
in the norms
\|\cdot\|_{{\rm BMO}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{{\rm BMO}_{d}}
respectively. Then
{\rm VMO}_{d}\subset{\rm VMO}
and
\|b\|_{{\rm VMO}}\lesssim\|b\|_{{\rm VMO}_{d}}
and, thanks to
[
5
, Theorem 4.1]
, the dual space to
{\rm VMO}
is the Hardy space
H^{1}
considered in
[
2
]
and
[
10
]
. Theorem 1.5 of
[
14
]
states that if
b\in{\rm VMO}_{d}
, then
the commutator
[b,R_{j}]
is a compact operator on
L^{p}(dw)
for all
1<p<\infty
. Our second aim is to extend this result for all
b\in{\rm VMO}
(see Theorem
3.2
).
Actually we will prove (
1.2
) and the compactness result for commutators
[b,\mathbf{T}]
of
{\rm BMO}
and
{\rm VMO}
functions with Dunkl singular integral operators
\mathbf{T}
of convolution type (under certain regularity for the associated kernels
K(\mathbf{x})
). The Dunkl–Riesz transforms are the basic examples of such operators.

Let us remark that for the Riesz transforms, the lower bounds (
1.3
) proved in
[
14
]
together with the upper bounds (
1.4
) (see Theorem
3.1
) generalize (to the Dunkl setting) the classical results of Coifman, Rochberg and Weiss
[
4
]
and Janson
[
15
]
obtained on the Euclidean spaces
(\mathbb{R}^{N},\,d\mathbf{x})
. As far as the compactness is concerned, the necessity result
[
14
, Theorem 1.5]
together with the sufficiency result (see Theorem
3.2
) extend to the Dunkl theory the classical theorems of Uchiyama
[
26
]
about the characterization of the
{\rm VMO}
functions by commutators with the Riesz transforms.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Dunkl theory

In this section we present basic facts concerning the theory of the Dunkl operators. For more details we refer the reader to
[
7
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
19
]
, and
[
20
]
.

We consider the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with the scalar product
\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}y_{j}
, where
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},...,x_{N})
,
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},...,y_{N})
, and the norm
\|\mathbf{x}\|^{2}=\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}\rangle
.

A
normalized root system
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a finite set
R\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
such that
R\cap\alpha\mathbb{R}=\{\pm\alpha\}
,
\sigma_{\alpha}(R)=R
, and
\|\alpha\|=\sqrt{2}
for all
\alpha\in R
, where
\sigma_{\alpha}
are defined by

(2.1)

\sigma_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{x}-2\frac{\langle\mathbf{x},\alpha\rangle}{\|\alpha\|^{2}}\alpha.

The finite group
G
generated by the reflections
\sigma_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in R
, is called the
Coxeter group
(
reflection group
) of the root system.

A
multiplicity function
is a
G
-invariant function
k:R\to\mathbb{C}
which will be fixed and
\geq 0
throughout this paper.

The associated measure
dw
is defined by
dw(\mathbf{x})=w(\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf{x}
, where

(2.2)

w(\mathbf{x})=\prod_{\alpha\in R}|\langle\mathbf{x},\alpha\rangle|^{k(\alpha)}.

Let
\mathbf{N}=N+\sum_{\alpha\in R}k(\alpha)
. Then,

(2.3)

w(B(t\mathbf{x},tr))=t^{\mathbf{N}}w(B(\mathbf{x},r))\ \ \text{\rm for all }\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\ t,r>0.

Observe that there is a constant
C>0
such that for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
r>0
we have

(2.4)

C^{-1}w(B(\mathbf{x},r))\leq r^{N}\prod_{\alpha\in R}(|\langle\mathbf{x},\alpha\rangle|+r)^{k(\alpha)}\leq Cw(B(\mathbf{x},r)),

so
dw(\mathbf{x})
is doubling. Moreover, there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that,
for every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and for all
r_{2}\geq r_{1}>0
,

(2.5)

C^{-1}\Big(\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}\Big)^{N}\leq\frac{{w}(B(\mathbf{x},r_{2}))}{{w}(B(\mathbf{x},r_{1}))}\leq C\Big(\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}.

For
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the
Dunkl operators

T_{\xi}
are the following
k
-deformations of the directional derivatives
\partial_{\xi}
by difference operators:

(2.6)

T_{\xi}f(\mathbf{x})=\partial_{\xi}f(\mathbf{x})+\sum_{\alpha\in R}\frac{k(\alpha)}{2}\langle\alpha,\xi\rangle\frac{f(\mathbf{x})-f(\sigma_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x}))}{\langle\alpha,\mathbf{x}\rangle}.

The Dunkl operators
T_{\xi}
, which were introduced in
[
7
]
, commute and are skew-symmetric with respect to the
G
-invariant measure
dw
.

The closures of connected components of

\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\;:\;\langle\mathbf{x},\alpha\rangle\neq 0\text{ for all }\alpha\in R\}

are called (closed)
Weyl chambers
. We remark that
\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|=d(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
if and only if
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
belong to the same closed Weyl chamber.

Let

(2.7)

Mf(\mathbf{x})=\sup_{B\ni\mathbf{x}}\frac{1}{w(B)}\int_{B}|f(\mathbf{y})|\,dw(\mathbf{y}),

denote the (uncentered) Hardy–Littlewood maximal function on
(\mathbb{R}^{N},\|\cdot\|,dw)
.

2.2.
Dunkl transform

For fixed
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the
Dunkl kernel

\mathbf{x}\longmapsto E(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
is a unique solution to the system

T_{\xi}f=\langle\xi,\mathbf{y}\rangle f,\quad f(0)=1.

The function
E(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
, which generalizes the exponential function
e^{\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle}
, has a unique extension to a holomorphic function
E(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w})
on
\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{C}^{N}
.
Let
f\in L^{1}(dw)
. We define the
Dunkl transform
\mathcal{F}f
of
f
by

(2.8)

\mathcal{F}f(\xi)=\mathbf{c}_{k}^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(\mathbf{x})E(\mathbf{x},-i\xi)\,{dw}(\mathbf{x}),\ \ \mathbf{c}_{k}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-\frac{{\|}\mathbf{x}{\|}^{2}}{2}}\,{dw}(\mathbf{x}){>0}

The Dunkl transform is a generalization of the Fourier transform on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. It was introduced in
[
8
]
for
k\geq 0
and further studied in
[
6
]
in the more general context. It was proved in
[
8
, Corollary 2.7]
(see also
[
6
, Theorem 4.26]
) that it extends uniquely to an isometry on
L^{2}(dw)
.
We have also the following inversion theorem (
[
6
, Theorem 4.20]
): for all
f\in L^{1}(dw)
such that
\mathcal{F}f\in L^{1}(dw)
we have
f(\mathbf{x})=(\mathcal{F})^{2}f(-\mathbf{x})\text{ for almost all }\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\textup{.}
The inverse
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
of
\mathcal{F}
has the form

(2.9)

\mathcal{F}^{-1}f(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{c}_{k}^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(\xi)E(i\xi,\mathbf{x})\,dw(\xi)=\mathcal{F}f(-\mathbf{x})\quad\text{\rm for }f\in L^{1}(dw)\textup{.}

2.3.
Dunkl translations

Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

(the Schwartz class of functions on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
) and
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We define the
Dunkl translation
\tau_{\mathbf{x}}f
of
f
to be

(2.10)

\tau_{\mathbf{x}}f(-\mathbf{y})=\mathbf{c}_{k}^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{E}(i\xi,\mathbf{x})\,{E}(-i\xi,\mathbf{y})\,\mathcal{F}f(\xi)\,{dw}(\xi)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(E(i\cdot,\mathbf{x})\mathcal{F}f)(-\mathbf{y}).

The Dunkl translation was introduced in
[
17
]
. The definition can be extended to functions which are not necessary in
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. For instance, thanks to the Plancherel’s theorem, one can define the Dunkl translation of
L^{2}(dw)
function
f
by

(2.11)

\tau_{\mathbf{x}}f(-\mathbf{y})=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(E(i\cdot,\mathbf{x})\mathcal{F}f(\cdot))(-\mathbf{y})

(see
[
17
]
of
[
23
, Definition 3.1]
). In particular, the operators
f\mapsto\tau_{\mathbf{x}}f
are contractions on
L^{2}(dw)
. Here and subsequently, for a reasonable function
g(\mathbf{x})
, we write
g(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}):=\tau_{\mathbf{x}}g(-\mathbf{y})
.

We will need the following result concerning the support of the Dunkl translation of a compactly supported function.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
9
, Theorem 1.7]
)
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(dw)
,
\text{\rm supp}\,f\subseteq B(0,r)
, and
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then

(2.12)

\text{\rm supp}\,\tau_{\mathbf{x}}f(-\,\cdot)\subseteq\mathcal{O}(B(\mathbf{x},r)).

2.4.
Dunkl convolution

Assume that
f,g\in L^{2}(dw)
. The
generalized convolution
(or
Dunkl convolution
)
f*g
is defined by the formula

(2.13)

f*g(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{c}_{k}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big((\mathcal{F}f)(\mathcal{F}g)\big)(\mathbf{x}),

equivalently, by

(2.14)

(f*g)(\mathbf{x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(\mathbf{y})\,\tau_{\mathbf{x}}g(-\mathbf{y})\,{dw}(\mathbf{y})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(\mathbf{y})\,\tau_{\mathbf{x}}f(-\mathbf{y})\,{dw}(\mathbf{y}).

Generalized convolution of
f,g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
was considered in
[
17
]
and
[
25
]
, the definition was extended to
f,g\in L^{2}(dw)
in
[
23
]
.

2.5.
Singular integral kernels

Let us consider a (non-radial) kernel
K(\mathbf{x})
which has properties similar to those
from the classical theory. Namely, let
s_{0}
be an even positive integer larger than
\mathbf{N}
, which will be fixed in the whole paper. Consider a function
K\in C^{s_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
such that

(A)

\sup_{0<a<b<\infty}\Big|\int_{a<\|\mathbf{x}\|<b}K(\mathbf{x})\,dw(\mathbf{x})\Big|<\infty,

(D)

\Big|\frac{\partial^{\beta}}{\partial\mathbf{x}^{\beta}}K(\mathbf{x})\Big|\leq C_{\beta}\|\mathbf{x}\|^{-\mathbf{N}-|\beta|}\quad\text{for all}\ |\beta|\leq s_{0},

(L)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\varepsilon<\|\mathbf{x}\|<1}K(\mathbf{x})\,dw(\mathbf{x})=L\text{ for some }L\in\mathbb{C}.

Set

\displaystyle K^{\{t\}}(\mathbf{x})=K(\mathbf{x})(1-\phi(t^{-1}\mathbf{x})),

where
\phi
is a fixed radial
C^{\infty}
-function supported by the unit ball
B(0,1)
such that
\phi(\mathbf{x})=1
for
\|\mathbf{x}\|<1/2
. The following theorem was proved in
[
11
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume that (
A
) and (
D
) are satisfied, then

(i)

(
Theorems 4.1 and 4.2 of
[
11
]
)
the operators
f\mapsto f*K^{\{t\}}
are bounded on
L^{p}(dw)
for
1<p<\infty
and they are of weak–type
(1,1)
with the bounds independent of
t>0
;

(ii)

(
Theorems 3.7 and 4.3 of
[
11
]
)
assuming additionally (
L
), the limit
\lim_{t\to 0}f*K^{\{t\}}(\mathbf{x})
exists and defines a bounded operator
\mathbf{T}
on
L^{p}(dw)
for
1<p<\infty
, which is of weak-type (1,1) as well.

3.
Statement of the results

3.1.
Commutators

In order to define the commutator operator, we come back to the definition of the limit operator
\mathbf{T}
. Let
0<\varepsilon<\min(1,s_{0}-\mathbf{N})
. For any
t>0
let us denote
K^{\{t/2,t\}}:=K^{\{t/2\}}-K^{\{t\}}
.
Then
K^{\{t/2,t\}}
is
C^{s_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-function supported by
B(0,t)\setminus B(0,t/4)
(cf.
[
11
, (3.1)]
). In order to simplify the notation, we write
K_{\ell}=K^{\{2^{\ell-1},2^{\ell}\}}
. It is proved in
[
12
, (4.24), (4.25)]
that for all
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

(3.1)

|K_{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})|\leq{{C}}\left(1+\frac{\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|}{2^{\ell}}\right)^{-\varepsilon}w(B(\mathbf{x},2^{\ell}))^{-1/2}w(B(\mathbf{y},2^{\ell}))^{-1/2},

(3.2)

\begin{split}&|K_{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})-K_{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}^{\prime})|\\
&\leq{{C}}\frac{\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\|^{\varepsilon}}{2^{\varepsilon\ell}}\left(1+\frac{\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|}{2^{\ell}}\right)^{-\varepsilon}w(B(\mathbf{x},2^{\ell}))^{-1/2}\left(w(B(\mathbf{y},2^{\ell}))^{-1/2}+w(B(\mathbf{y}^{\prime},2^{\ell}))^{-1/2}\right).\end{split}

Moreover,
K_{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=K_{\ell}(-\mathbf{y},-\mathbf{x})
and by
[
12
, proof of Theorem 4.6]
,

(3.3)

\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}|K_{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})|\leq Cw(B(\mathbf{x},d(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})))^{-1}\frac{d(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})^{\varepsilon}}{\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|^{\varepsilon}}

for all
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
d(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\neq 0
and

(3.4)

\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}|K_{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})-K_{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}^{\prime})|\leq C\frac{\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\|^{\varepsilon}}{\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|^{\varepsilon}}w(B(\mathbf{x},d(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})))^{-1}

for all
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\|<\frac{d(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})}{2}
. The number
\varepsilon>0
will be fixed in the whole paper.
Thanks to (
3.3
) the function

(3.5)

K(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}K_{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})

is well defined for
d(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})>0
and, by Theorem
2.2
, it is the associated kernel to the operator
\mathbf{T}
, that is,

(3.6)

\mathbf{T}f(\mathbf{x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})f(\mathbf{y})\,dw(\mathbf{y})

for
f\in L^{p}(dw)
and
\mathbf{x}\notin\text{supp}\,f
.

Let us emphasize that the estimate for
K(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
which are consequences of (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) turn out to be very useful in handling some harmonic analysis problems in the Dunkl setting (see
[
22
]
).

From now on we fix a kernel
K\in C^{s_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
satisfying (
A
), (
L
), and (
D
) for some
s_{0}>\mathbf{N}
. Let
b\in{\rm BMO}
. For any compactly supported
f\in L^{p}(dw)
for some
p>1
, we define

(3.7)

\mathcal{C}f(\mathbf{x})={[b,\mathbf{T}]}f(\mathbf{x})=\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(b(\mathbf{x})-b(\mathbf{y}))\sum_{\ell=-m}^{m}K_{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})f(\mathbf{y})\,dw(\mathbf{y})=\lim_{m\to\infty}\mathcal{C}_{m}f(\mathbf{x})

The existence of the limit in (
3.7
) in any
L^{p_{0}}(dw)
-norm, provided
1<p_{0}<p
, is proved in Lemma
4.2
. Then, Theorem
3.1
and its proof allows one to extend the definition for all
f\in L^{p}(dw)
.

3.2.
Statements of main theorems

Our main results are the following two theorems.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
p>1
. Assume that a kernel
K\in C^{s_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
satisfies (
A
), (
L
), (
D
) for a certain even integer
s_{0}>\mathbf{N}
, and
b\in{\rm BMO}
. Then there is a constant
C>0
independent of
b
such that for all
f\in L^{p}(dw)
we have

(3.8)

\|\mathcal{C}f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}\leq C\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}.

In order to formulate our second theorem, recall that
{\rm VMO}
is the closure in
{\rm BMO}
of compactly supported Lipschitz functions, i.e., functions
f
satisfying
\sup_{\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}}\frac{|f(\mathbf{x})-f(\mathbf{y})|}{\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|}<\infty
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume
b\in{\rm VMO}
. For
1<p<\infty
, the commutator
\mathcal{C}
is a compact operator on
L^{p}(dw)
.

To prove the theorems, we adapt the ideas of classical proofs (cf. e.g.
[
13
]
) to apply estimates for integral kernels of operators which are expressed in terms of the orbit distance
d(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
and the Euclidean metric, see (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) (cf. also
[
14
]
). These require in some places much careful analysis. For example, we use the Coxeter group for decomposing
L^{p}(dw)
-functions (see (
4.9
)) or we split integration over
\mathbb{R}^{N}
onto the Weyl chambers (see (
4.10
)).

4.
Proof of Theorem
3.1

Let us begin with the following lemma. Recall that
b_{B(\mathbf{x},r)}
is defined by (
1.1
).

Lemma 4.1
.

There is a constant
C>0
such that for all
b\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(dw)
,
r_{1}>r>0
,
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and
\sigma\in G
, we have:

(4.1)

|b_{B(\mathbf{x},r)}-b_{B(\mathbf{x},r_{1})}|\leq C\log(r_{1}/r)\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}},

(4.2)

|b_{B(\mathbf{x},r)}-b_{B(\mathbf{y},r)}|\leq C\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}}\quad\text{\rm provided }\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|\leq 2r,

(4.3)

|b_{B(\mathbf{x},r)}-b_{B(\sigma(\mathbf{x}),r)}|\leq C\log\left(\frac{\|\sigma(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{x}\|}{r}+4\right)\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}},

(John–Nirenberg inequality) for any
1\leq s<\infty
there is
C_{s}>0
such that for all positive integers
j
we have

(4.4)

\Big(\frac{1}{w(B(\mathbf{x},2^{j}r))}\int_{B(\mathbf{x},2^{j}r)}|b(\mathbf{y})-b_{B(\mathbf{x},r)}|^{s}\,dw(\mathbf{y})\Big)^{1/s}\leq C_{s}j\|b\|_{\rm BMO}.

Proof.

Inequalities (
4.1
), (
4.2
), and (
4.4
) are consequences of the well-known results in metric spaces with doubling measures (see e.g.
[
3
]
,
[
5
]
, or
[
16
, Section 4]
in the Dunkl setting). In order to prove (
4.3
), note that if
\|\mathbf{x}-\sigma(\mathbf{x})\|\leq 2r
, then (
4.3
) follows by (
4.2
). Assume that
\|\mathbf{x}-\sigma(\mathbf{x})\|>2r
and let
j
be the smallest positive integer such that
\|\mathbf{x}-\sigma(\mathbf{x})\|\leq 2^{j}r
. Then
B(\mathbf{x},2^{j+2}r)\cap B(\sigma(\mathbf{x}),2^{j+2}r)\neq\emptyset
.
So, applying (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we get

\begin{split}&|b_{B(\mathbf{x},r)}-b_{B(\sigma(\mathbf{x}),r)}|\\
&\leq|b_{B(\mathbf{x},r)}-b_{B(\mathbf{x},2^{j+2}r)}|+|b_{B(\mathbf{x},2^{j+2}r)}-b_{B(\sigma(\mathbf{x}),2^{j+2}r)}|+|b_{B(\sigma(\mathbf{x}),2^{j+2}r)}-b_{B(\sigma(\mathbf{x}),r)}|\\
&\leq Cj\|b\|_{\rm BMO}\leq C^{\prime}\log\left(\frac{\|\mathbf{x}-\sigma(\mathbf{x})\|}{r}+4\right)\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}}.\end{split}

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
p>p_{0}>1
. If
f\in L^{p}(dw)
is compactly supported, then
\mathcal{C}_{m}f\in L^{p_{0}}(dw)
for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\mathcal{C}_{m}f=\mathcal{C}f\text{ in }L^{p_{0}}(dw).

Proof.

Let
B=B(0,r)
be any ball such that
{\rm supp\;}f\subseteq B
. We write

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{m}f(\mathbf{x})=(b(\mathbf{x})-b_{B})\sum_{\ell=-m}^{m}K_{\ell}*f(\mathbf{x})+\sum_{\ell=-m}^{m}K_{\ell}*((b-b_{B})f))(\mathbf{x}).

By Hölder’s inequality with
s=p/p_{0}
and the John-Nirenberg inequality (
4.4
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B}|b(\mathbf{x})-b_{B}|^{p_{0}}|f(\mathbf{x})|^{p_{0}}\;dw(\mathbf{x})\right)^{1/p_{0}}\leq Cw(B)^{1/(p_{0}s^{\prime})}\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(dw)},

where
s^{\prime}>1
is such that
s+s^{\prime}=ss^{\prime}
, so
(b-b_{B})f\in L^{p_{0}}(dw)
. Hence, by Theorem
2.2
(
ii
),

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{\ell=-m}^{m}K_{\ell}*((b-b_{B})f))

exists in the
L^{p_{0}}(dw)
-norm and it is equal to
\mathbf{T}((b-b_{B})f)\in L^{p_{0}}(dw)
. Again, by Theorem
2.2
(
ii
), the limit

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{\ell=-m}^{m}K_{\ell}*f

exists in the
L^{p_{0}}(dw)
and
L^{p}(dw)
-norms and it is equal to
\mathbf{T}f
. We write

\displaystyle(b(\mathbf{x})-b_{B})\mathbf{T}f(\mathbf{x})=(b(\mathbf{x})-b_{B})\mathbf{T}f(\mathbf{x})\chi_{5B}(\mathbf{x})+(b(\mathbf{x})-b_{B})\mathbf{T}f(\mathbf{x})\chi_{(5B)^{c}}(\mathbf{x})=:g_{1}(\mathbf{x})+g_{2}(\mathbf{x}).

We will show that
g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{p_{0}}(dw)
. For
g_{1}
, by Hölder’s inequality with
s=p/p_{0}
, the John-Nirenberg inequality (
4.4
), and
L^{p}(dw)
-boundedness of
\mathbf{T}
(Theorem
2.2
(
ii
)), we get

\displaystyle\left(\int_{5B}|b(\mathbf{x})-b_{B}|^{p_{0}}|\mathbf{T}f(\mathbf{x})|^{p_{0}}\;dw(\mathbf{x})\right)^{1/p_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{5B}|b(\mathbf{x})-b_{B}|^{p_{0}s^{\prime}}\,dw(\mathbf{x})\right)^{1/(p_{0}s^{\prime})}\left(\int_{5B}|\mathbf{T}f(\mathbf{x})|^{p}\;dw(\mathbf{x})\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq Cw(B)^{1/(p_{0}s^{\prime})}\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}.

In order to prove that
g_{2}\in L^{p_{0}}(dw)
, we observe that, by (
2.3
) and (
2.4
),
w(B(\mathbf{x},d(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})))\sim w(B(0,\|\mathbf{x}\|))=c\|\mathbf{x}\|^{\mathbf{N}}
for
\mathbf{y}\in B
and
\mathbf{x}\in(5B)^{c}
. Using (
3.3
) we have

(4.5)

\begin{split}\chi_{(5B)^{c}}(\mathbf{x})|\mathbf{T}f(\mathbf{x})|&\leq C\chi_{(5B)^{c}}(\mathbf{x})\int_{B}\frac{|f(\mathbf{x})|}{w(B(\mathbf{x},d(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})))}\,dw(\mathbf{y})\\
&\leq C\int_{B}\frac{|f(\mathbf{x})|}{w(B(0,\|\mathbf{x}\|)))}\,dw(\mathbf{y})\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}\frac{w(B)^{1/p^{\prime}}}{w(B(0,\|\mathbf{x}\|))}.\\
\end{split}

Consequently,

(4.6)

\begin{split}\|g_{2}\|_{L^{p_{0}}(dw)}^{p_{0}}&\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}^{p_{0}}w(B)^{p_{0}/p^{\prime}}\int_{\|\mathbf{x}\|>5r}\frac{|b(\mathbf{x})-b_{B}|^{p_{0}}}{w(B(0,\|\mathbf{x}\|))^{p_{0}}}\,dw(\mathbf{x})\\
&\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}^{p_{0}}w(B)^{p_{0}/p^{\prime}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{\|\mathbf{x}\|\leq 2^{j}r}\frac{|b(\mathbf{x})-b_{B}|^{p_{0}}}{w(B(0,2^{j}r))^{p_{0}}}\,dw(\mathbf{x}).\end{split}

Now, applying the John-Nirenberg inequality (
4.4
) we get

(4.7)

\begin{split}\|g_{2}\|_{L^{p_{0}}(dw)}^{p_{0}}&\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}^{p_{0}}w(B)^{p_{0}/p^{\prime}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}}^{p_{0}}\frac{j^{p_{0}}}{(2^{j}r)^{(p_{0}-1)\mathbf{N}}}\\
&\leq C^{\prime}\|f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}^{p_{0}}w(B)^{p_{0}/p^{\prime}}\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}}^{p_{0}}r^{-(p_{0}-1)\mathbf{N}}.\\
\end{split}

∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

We shall prove the inequality (

) for compactly supported functions

which form a dense subspace in

. To this end, thanks to Lemma

and
[

, it suffices to verify that

\|(\mathcal{C}f)^{\#}\|_{L^{p}(dw)}\leq C\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(dw)},

where

denotes the sharp maximal function

Let
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and let

be any ball which contains

. We write all the elements of

in a sequence
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{|G|-1}

\begin{split}&U_{1}:=\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\;:\;\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}_{0}\|>5r,\ \|\mathbf{z}-\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\|\leq 5r\},\\
&U_{j+1}:=\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\;:\;\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}_{0}\|>5r,\ \|\mathbf{z}-\sigma_{j+1}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\|\leq 5r\}\setminus\left(\bigcup_{j_{1}=1}^{j}U_{j_{1}}\right)\text{ for }j\geq 1.\end{split}

For

we set

\begin{split}&g_{1}(\mathbf{y}):=\mathcal{C}f_{1}(\mathbf{y})=(b(\mathbf{y})-b_{B})\mathbf{T}f_{1}(\mathbf{y})+\mathbf{T}((b_{B}-b)f_{1})(\mathbf{y})=:g_{11}(\mathbf{y})+g_{12}(\mathbf{y}),\\

\end{split}

Fix

. Further, by the fact that
|(g_{11})_{B}|\leq\frac{1}{w(B)}\int_{B}|g_{11}(\mathbf{y})|\,dw(\mathbf{y})
, by the definition of

\begin{split}\frac{1}{w(B)}&\int_{B}|g_{11}(\mathbf{y})-(g_{11})_{B}|\;dw(\mathbf{y})\leq\frac{2}{w(B)}\int_{B}|g_{11}(\mathbf{y})|\,dw(\mathbf{y})\\

where

is such that
\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}=1
. Applying inequality (
4.4
) we conclude that

g_{11}^{\#}(\mathbf{x})\leq C\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}}(M(|\mathbf{T}f_{1}|^{s})(\mathbf{x}))^{1/s}.

The same analysis gives

\begin{split}&g_{21}^{\#}(\mathbf{x})\leq C\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}}(M(|\mathbf{T}f_{2}|^{s})(\mathbf{x}))^{1/s},\ \ \ g_{\sigma_{j}1}^{\#}(\mathbf{x})\leq C\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}}(M(|\mathbf{T}f_{\sigma_{j}}|^{s})(\mathbf{x}))^{1/s}.\\
\end{split}

To deal with

, we choose

such that

. Then, by Hölder’s inequality and

\begin{split}\frac{1}{w(B)}&\int_{B}|g_{12}(\mathbf{y})-(g_{12})_{B}|\;dw(\mathbf{y})\leq\frac{2}{w(B)}\int_{B}|g_{12}(\mathbf{y})|\,dw(\mathbf{y})\\
&\leq 2\frac{1}{w(B)}\int_{B}|\mathbf{T}((b(\cdot)-b_{B})\cdot f_{1})(\mathbf{y})|\,dw(\mathbf{y})\\
&\leq 2\Big(\frac{1}{w(B)}\int_{B}\mathbf{T}((b(\cdot)-b_{B})\cdot f_{1})(\mathbf{y})|^{q}\,dw(\mathbf{y})\Big)^{1/q}\\
&\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{w(5B)}\int_{5B}|(b(\mathbf{y})-b_{B})\cdot f_{1}(\mathbf{y})|^{q}\,dw(\mathbf{y})\Big)^{1/q}\\
&\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{w(5B)}\int_{5B}|b(\mathbf{y})-b_{B}|^{qv^{\prime}}dw(\mathbf{y})\Big)^{1/(qv^{\prime})}\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{w(5B)}\int_{5B}|f_{1}(\mathbf{y})|^{qv}\,dw(\mathbf{y})\Big)^{1/(qv)}.\end{split}

Hence, applying the John-Nirenberg inequality (
4.4
), we get

g_{12}^{\#}(\mathbf{x})\leq C\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}}(M(|f_{1}|^{s})(\mathbf{x}))^{1/s}.

We turn to analyse
g_{22}
. Observe that for
\mathbf{z}\notin\mathcal{O}(5B)
and
\mathbf{y}\in B
we have
\|\mathbf{x}_{0}-\mathbf{y}\|\leq d(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{z})
.
Let
\Gamma
be a fixed closed Weyl chamber such that
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\Gamma
, then by the estimates (
3.4
),

(4.10)

\begin{split}|g_{22}(\mathbf{y})-g_{22}(\mathbf{x}_{0})|&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}|K_{\ell}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{y})-K_{\ell}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x}_{0})||b_{B}-b(\mathbf{z})||f_{2}(\mathbf{z})|\,dw(\mathbf{z})\\
&=\sum_{\sigma\in G}\int_{\mathbf{z}\in\sigma(\Gamma)}\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}|K_{\ell}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{y})-K_{\ell}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x}_{0})||b_{B}-b(\mathbf{z})||f_{2}(\mathbf{z})|\,dw(\mathbf{z})\\
&\leq C\sum_{\sigma\in G}\int_{\mathbf{z}\in\sigma(\Gamma)}\frac{\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}_{0}\|^{\varepsilon}}{\|\mathbf{x}_{0}-\mathbf{z}\|^{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{w(B(\mathbf{x}_{0},d(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{z}))}|b_{B}-b(\mathbf{z})||f_{2}(\mathbf{z})|\,dw(\mathbf{z})\\

\end{split}

In dealing with

\begin{split}&\|\mathbf{x}_{0}-\mathbf{z}\|\geq\max(\|\mathbf{x}_{0}-\sigma(\mathbf{x}_{0})\|/2,r)\quad\text{for }\mathbf{z}\in\sigma(\Gamma),\\
&\|\sigma(\mathbf{x}_{0})-\sigma(\mathbf{x})\|\leq r<5r\leq\|\sigma(\mathbf{x}_{0})-\mathbf{z}\|=d(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{z})\leq\|\mathbf{x}_{0}-\mathbf{z}\|\quad\text{for }\mathbf{z}\in\sigma(\Gamma),\ \mathbf{z}\notin\mathcal{O}(5B).\end{split}

\begin{split}&J_{\sigma}(\mathbf{y})\leq C\int_{\mathbf{z}\in\sigma(\Gamma)}\frac{r^{\varepsilon}}{\|\mathbf{x}_{0}-\mathbf{z}\|^{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{w(B(\sigma(\mathbf{x}_{0}),\|\sigma(\mathbf{x}_{0})-\mathbf{z}\|))}|b_{B}-b_{\sigma(B)}||f_{2}(\mathbf{z})|\,dw(\mathbf{z})\\

Further, by (

),

(4.11)

\begin{split}J_{\sigma,1}(\mathbf{y})&\leq C\int_{\mathbf{z}\in\sigma(\Gamma)}\frac{r^{\varepsilon/2}}{\|\mathbf{x}_{0}-\mathbf{z}\|^{\varepsilon/2}}\frac{r^{\varepsilon/2}}{r^{\varepsilon/2}+\|\mathbf{x}_{0}-\sigma(\mathbf{x}_{0})\|^{\varepsilon/2}}\log\Big(\frac{\|\mathbf{x}_{0}-\sigma(\mathbf{x}_{0})\|}{r}+4\Big)\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}}\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\times\frac{|f_{2}(\mathbf{z})|}{w(B(\sigma(\mathbf{x}_{0}),\|\sigma(\mathbf{x}_{0})-\mathbf{z}\|))}\,dw(\mathbf{z})\\

\begin{split}\frac{1}{w(B)}&\int_{B}|g_{\sigma_{j}2}(\mathbf{y})-(g_{\sigma_{j}2})_{B}|\,dw(\mathbf{y})\leq\frac{2}{w(B)}\int_{B}|g_{\sigma_{j}2}(\mathbf{y})|\,dw(\mathbf{y})\\

so

Finally we end up with the estimate

(4.14)

\begin{split}(\mathcal{C}f)^{\#}(\mathbf{x})&\leq C\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}}\Big((M(|\mathbf{T}f_{1}|^{s})(\mathbf{x})))^{1/s}+M(|\mathbf{T}f_{2}|^{s}(\mathbf{x})))^{1/s}\\
&+\sum_{j=1}^{|G|-1}M(|\mathbf{T}f_{\sigma_{j}}|^{s}(\mathbf{x})))^{1/s}+\sum_{\sigma\in G}\big(Mf(\sigma(\mathbf{x}))+(M|f|^{s}(\sigma(\mathbf{x})))^{1/s}\big)\Big).\end{split}

Hence, using the
L^{p_{1}}(dw)
-boundedness of the Hardy–Littlewood maximal
M
function for all
1<p_{1}<\infty
and the fact that the measure
dw
is
G
-invariant, we conclude (
4.8
) from (
4.14
) and (
4.9
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.2

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Assume that
b
is a compactly supported Lipschitz function. Then

(5.1)

\lim_{m\to\infty}\|\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{C}_{m}\|_{L^{p}(dw)\longmapsto L^{p}(dw)}=0.

Proof.

Let
r_{b}>1
be such that
\text{supp}\,b\subset B(0,r_{b})
and let
L_{b}>0
be such that
|b(\mathbf{x})-b(\mathbf{y})|\leq L_{b}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|
for all
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. By (
3.7
), it is enough to prove that there is a constant
C>0
such that for all positive integers
m
such that
2^{m}\geq 2r_{b}
,
f\in L^{p}(dw)
, and
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we have

(5.2)

\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{-m-1}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(b(\mathbf{x})-b(\mathbf{y}))K_{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})f(\mathbf{y})dw(\mathbf{y})\right|\leq C{L_{b}}2^{-\varepsilon m}\sum_{\sigma\in G}Mf(\sigma(\mathbf{x})),

(5.3)

\sum_{\ell=m+1}^{\infty}\left|b(\mathbf{x})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K_{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})f(\mathbf{y})dw(\mathbf{y})\right|\leq C{\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}}2^{-mN/p}\chi_{B(0,r_{b})}(\mathbf{x})\|f\|_{L^{p}(dw)},

(5.4)

\sum_{\ell=m+1}^{\infty}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K_{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})b(\mathbf{y})f(\mathbf{y})dw(\mathbf{y})\right|\leq C{\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}}\left(\chi_{B(0,2^{m})}(\mathbf{x})2^{-m\mathbf{N}}+\chi_{B(0,2^{m})^{c}}(\mathbf{x})\|\mathbf{x}\|^{-\mathbf{N}}\right)\|f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}.

Let us note that by Theorem
2.1
,
\text{supp }K_{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\cdot)\subseteq\mathcal{O}(B(\mathbf{x},2^{\ell}))
. Hence, by (
3.1
) and the doubling property of the measure
dw
(see (
2.5
)) we have

(5.5)

|K_{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})|\leq C\left(1+\frac{\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|}{2^{\ell}}\right)^{-\varepsilon}w(B(\mathbf{x},2^{\ell}))^{-1}.

In order to prove (
5.2
), we use now the Lipschitz condition for
b
and get

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{-m-1}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(b(\mathbf{x})-b(\mathbf{y}))K_{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})f(\mathbf{y})dw(\mathbf{y})\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{-m-1}\frac{{L_{b}}}{w(B(\mathbf{x},2^{\ell}))}\int_{\mathcal{O}(B(\mathbf{x},2^{\ell}))}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|^{\varepsilon}\frac{2^{\varepsilon\ell}}{\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|^{\varepsilon}}|f(\mathbf{y})|\,dw(\mathbf{y})\leq C{L_{b}}2^{-\varepsilon m}\sum_{\sigma\in G}Mf(\sigma(\mathbf{x})).

In order to prove (
5.3
), let us note that by (
2.4
),
w(B(\mathbf{x},2^{\ell}))\geq c2^{\ell N}
for all
\ell\geq 0
. Hence, from (
5.5
), Hölder’s inequality, and the fact that
\text{supp }b\subseteq B(0,r_{b})
we conclude that

(5.6)

\begin{split}&\sum_{\ell=m+1}^{\infty}\left|b(\mathbf{x})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K_{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})f(\mathbf{y})\,dw(\mathbf{y})\right|\\
&\leq C\chi_{B(0,r_{b})}(\mathbf{x})\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{\ell=m+1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathcal{O}(B(\mathbf{x},2^{\ell}))}\frac{1}{w(B(\mathbf{x},2^{\ell}))^{p^{\prime}}}\,dw(\mathbf{y})\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}\\
&\leq C\chi_{B(0,r_{b})}(\mathbf{x})\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}2^{-mN/p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}.\end{split}

Finally, for (
5.4
), we consider two cases. If
\|\mathbf{x}\|\leq 2^{m}
, then similarly to (
5.6
) we get

(5.7)

\begin{split}&\sum_{\ell=m+1}^{\infty}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K_{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})b(\mathbf{y})f(\mathbf{y})dw(\mathbf{y})\right|\\
&\leq C\chi_{B(0,2^{m})}(\mathbf{x})\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{\ell=m+1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{B(0,r_{b})}\frac{1}{w(B(\mathbf{y},2^{\ell}))^{p^{\prime}}}\,dw(\mathbf{y})\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}\\
&\leq C\chi_{B(0,2^{m})}(\mathbf{x})\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}2^{-m\mathbf{N}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}.\end{split}

Now we assume that
\|\mathbf{x}\|>2^{m}
. Recall that
2^{m}\geq 2r_{b}
. From Theorem
2.1
and the fact that
\text{supp }K_{\ell}\subseteq B(0,2^{\ell})
, we conclude that

K_{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=0\quad\text{ for all }\mathbf{y}\in B(0,r_{b})\ \text{ and }\ell\geq m\ \text{ such that }2^{\ell+1}<\|\mathbf{x}\|.

Further, by (
5.5
) and the doubling property (
2.5
) of
dw
,

\displaystyle|K_{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})|\leq\frac{C}{w(B(0,2^{\ell}))}\quad\text{ for all }\mathbf{y}\in B(0,r_{b})\ \text{ and }\ell\geq m\ \text{ such that }2^{\ell+1}\geq\|\mathbf{x}\|.

Hence, by the fact that
b
is supported by
B(0,r_{b})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=m+1}^{\infty}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K_{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})b(\mathbf{y})f(\mathbf{y})dw(\mathbf{y})\right|\leq C\sum_{\ell=\lfloor\log(\|\mathbf{x}\|)\rfloor-2}^{\infty}\int_{B(0,r_{b})}\frac{\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}}{w(B(0,2^{\ell}))}|f(\mathbf{y})|\,dw(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}}{w(B(0,\|\mathbf{x}\|))}\int_{B(0,r_{b})}|f(\mathbf{y})|\,dw(\mathbf{y})\leq C{r_{b}^{\mathbf{N}/p^{\prime}}}\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\mathbf{x}\|^{-\mathbf{N}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(dw)}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Let us recall that the compactly supported Lipschitz functions form a dense subspace in
VMO
. Hence, by Theorem
3.1
, it suffices to show that
\mathcal{C}
is a compact operator on
L^{p}(dw)
for any compactly supported Lipschitz function
b
.
Further, thanks to Lemma
5.1
, it is enough to prove that for any compactly supported Lipschitz function
b
, the commutators
\mathcal{C}_{m}
are compact operators on
L^{p}(dw)
for large enough positive integers
m
. To this end, let
r_{b}>1
be such that
\text{supp}\,b\subset B(0,r_{b})
. First we note that if
f
is supported by
B(0,r_{b}+2^{m})^{c}
, then
b\cdot f\equiv 0
. Moreover, since
\text{supp }\sum_{\ell=-m}^{m}K_{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\cdot)\subseteq\mathcal{O}(B(\mathbf{x},2^{m}))
(cf. Theorem
2.1
), we have
\sum_{\ell=-m}^{m}K_{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\cdot)\cdot f(\cdot)\equiv 0
for all
\mathbf{x}\in B(0,r_{b})
. Consequently, for all
f\in L^{p}(dw)
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{m}f(\mathbf{x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(b(\mathbf{x})-b(\mathbf{y}))\sum_{\ell=-m}^{m}K_{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})f(\mathbf{y})\,dw(\mathbf{y})=\mathcal{C}_{m}(f\cdot\chi_{B(0,r_{b}+2^{m})})(\mathbf{x}).

Moreover, since
\text{supp }\sum_{\ell=-m}^{m}K_{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\cdot)\subseteq\mathcal{O}(B(\mathbf{x},2^{m}))
,

\displaystyle\text{supp }\mathcal{C}_{m}(f)=\text{supp }\mathcal{C}_{m}(f\cdot\chi_{B(0,r_{b}+2^{m})})\subseteq B(0,r_{b}+2^{m+1}).

Let
\Omega=B(0,r_{b}+2^{m+1})
. The proof now is reduced to showing that
\mathcal{C}_{m}
is a compact operator
L^{p}(\Omega,dw)\longmapsto L^{p}(\Omega,dw)
. The estimates (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) imply

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{m}f(\mathbf{x})|\leq C_{\Omega,m}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,dw)},

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{m}f(\mathbf{x})-\mathcal{C}_{m}f(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})|\leq C_{\Omega,m}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\|^{\varepsilon}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,dw)}

for all
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. By the Arzelá-Ascoli theorem the set

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{C}_{m}f:\ f\in L^{p}(\Omega,dw),\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,dw)}\leq 1\}

is relatively compact in
C(\Omega)
with the
\sup
-norm. Hence it is relatively compact in
L^{p}(\Omega,dw)
, since
w(\Omega)
is finite.
∎

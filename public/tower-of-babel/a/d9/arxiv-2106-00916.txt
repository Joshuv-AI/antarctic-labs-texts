Lê Quy Thuong

Abstract
. The paper studies categories of definable subassignments with some category equivalences to semi-algebraic and constructible subsets of arc spaces of algebraic varieties. These materials allow us to compare the motivic measure of Cluckers-Loeser and the one of Denef-Loeser in certain classes of definable subassignments.

2.
Grothendieck rings of varieties

Let
k
be a field of characteristic zero, and
S
an algebraic
k
-variety. As usual (cf.
[
7
,
8
]
), we denote by
\mathrm{Var}_{S}
the category of
S
-varieties and
K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{S})
its Grothendieck ring. By definition,
K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{S})
is the quotient of the free abelian group generated by the
S
-isomorphism classes
[X\to S]
in
\mathrm{Var}_{S}
modulo the following relation

[X\to S]=[Y\to S]+[X\setminus Y\to S]

for
Y
being Zariski closed in
X
. Together with fiber product over
S
,
K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{X})
is a commutative ring with unity
1=[\mathrm{Id}:S\to S]
. Put

\mathbb{L}=[\mathbb{A}_{k}^{1}\times_{k}S\to S]

and write
\mathscr{M}_{S}
for the localization of
K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{S})
inverting
\mathbb{L}
. Denote by
\mathscr{M}_{S,\mathrm{loc}}
the localization of
\mathscr{M}_{S}
inverting
\mathbb{L}^{n}-1
for all
n
in
\mathbb{N}^{*}
.

Let
C\mathrm{Var}_{S}
be the category whose objects are constructible morphisms from constructible sets over
k
to
S
with the set of morphisms between objects
X\to S
and
Y\to S
given by

\mathrm{Mor}_{C\mathrm{Var}_{S}}(X\to S,Y\to S):=K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{X\times_{S}Y}).

In other words, a morphism from
X\to S
to
Y\to S
in
C\mathrm{Var}_{S}
is a finite sum of elements of the form
[U\to X\times_{S}Y]
(in
K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{X\times_{S}Y})
), with
U
being an algebraic
k
-variety. The composition of two basic morphisms
[U\to X\times_{S}Y]
and
[V\to Y\times_{S}Z]
is the following morphism

[V\to Y\times_{S}Z]\circ[U\to X\times_{S}Y]:=[U\times_{Y}V\to X\times_{S}Z].

This definition makes sense since the morphism
U\times_{Y}V\to X\times_{S}Z
commutes with the structural morphisms to
S
, and it can be also extended by additivity. Clearly, the identity morphism of
X
in
C\mathrm{Var}_{k}
is the class in
K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{X\times_{k}X}
of the diagonal morphism
X\to X\times_{k}X
.

Denote by
\mathrm{Cons}_{S}
the subcategory of
C\mathrm{Var}_{S}
in which objects of
\mathrm{Cons}_{S}
are objects of
C\mathrm{Var}_{S}
and a morphism of
\mathrm{Cons}_{S}
from
X\to S
to
Y\to S
is an element

[(\mathrm{Id}_{X},f):X\to X\times_{S}Y]

in
K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{X\times_{S}Y})
. By definition, each morphism of
\mathrm{Cons}_{S}
from
X\to S
to
Y\to S
is determined uniquely, up to
S
-automorphism on
X
, by the constructible
S
-morphism of constructible sets
f:X\to Y
, or alternatively, by the graph of such an
f
. Using the above definition of Grothendieck ring for the category
\mathrm{Cons}_{S}
we get

K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{S})\cong K_{0}(\mathrm{Cons}_{S}).

Let
X
be an algebraic
k
-variety, and let
G
be an algebraic group which acts on
X
. The
G
-action is called
good
if every
G
-orbit is contained in an affine open subset of
X
. Now we fix a good action of
G
on the
k
-variety
S
. By definition, the
G
-equivariant Grothendieck group
K_{0}^{G}(\mathrm{Var}_{S})
of
G
-equivariant morphisms of
k
-varieties
X\to S
, where
X
is endowed with a good
G
-action, is the quotient of the free abelian group generated by the
G
-equivariant isomorphism classes
[X\to S,\sigma]
modulo the following relations

[X\to S,\sigma]=[Y\to S,\sigma|_{Y}]+[X\setminus Y\to S,\sigma|_{X\setminus Y}]

for
Y
being
\sigma
-stable Zariski closed in
X
, and

[X\times_{k}\mathbb{A}_{k}^{n}\to S,\sigma]=[X\times_{k}\mathbb{A}_{k}^{n}\to S,\sigma^{\prime}]

if
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
lift the same
G
-action on
X
to an affine action on
X\times\mathbb{A}_{k}^{n}
. As above, we have the commutative ring with unity structure on
K_{0}^{G}(\mathrm{Var}_{S})
by fiber product, where
G
-action on the fiber product is through the diagonal
G
-action, and we may define the localization
\mathscr{M}_{S}^{G}
of the ring
K_{0}^{G}(\mathrm{Var}_{S})
by inverting
\mathbb{L}
. In this article, we also consider the localization
\mathscr{M}_{S,\mathrm{loc}}^{G}
of
\mathscr{M}_{S}^{G}
with respect to the multiplicative family generated by the elements
1-\mathbb{L}^{-n}
with
n
in
\mathbb{N}^{*}
.

Since a constructible subset
X
of a
k
-variety is a finite disjoint union of locally closed subsets, we can endow
X
with good
G
-action via its locally closed subsets. A constructible morphism is
G
-equivariant if its graph admits a good
G
-action induced from the ones on its source and target. So we can define categories
C\mathrm{Var}_{S}^{G}
and
\mathrm{Cons}_{S}^{G}
as follows. As avove, fix a good
G
-action on the
k
-variety
S
. Objects of
C\mathrm{Var}_{S}^{G}
are
G
-equivariant constructible morphisms from constructible sets endowed with a good
G
-action to
S
(over
k
), the set of morphisms between objects
X\to S
and
Y\to S
is

\mathrm{Mor}_{C\mathrm{Var}_{S}^{G}}(X\to S,Y\to S):=K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{X\times_{S}Y}^{G}).

Objects of
\mathrm{Cons}_{S}^{G}
are objects of
C\mathrm{Var}_{S}^{G}
, and a morphism of
\mathrm{Cons}_{S}^{G}
from
X\to S
to
Y\to S
is

\left[(\mathrm{Id},f):X\to X\times_{S}Y\right],

where
f:X\to Y
is a
G
-equivariant constructible
S
-morphism of constructible sets. Similarly as previous, we can define the
G
-equivariant Grothendieck ring
K_{0}(\mathrm{Cons}_{S}^{G})
in the usual way, and obtain a canonical isomorphism of rings

K_{0}^{G}(\mathrm{Var}_{S})\cong K_{0}(\mathrm{Cons}_{S}^{G}).

Let
\hat{\mu}
be the group scheme of roots of unity, which is the projective limit of group schemes
\mu_{n}=\mathrm{Spec}k[t]/(t^{n}-1)
together transitions
\mu_{mn}\to\mu_{n}
induced by
\lambda\mapsto\lambda^{m}
. A good
\hat{\mu}
-action on an
S
-variety
X
is a good
\mu_{n}
-action on the
S
-variety
X
for some
n
in
\mathbb{N}^{*}
. We define

K_{0}^{\hat{\mu}}(\mathrm{Var}_{S})=\varinjlim K_{0}^{\mu_{n}}(\mathrm{Var}_{S}),\quad\mathscr{M}_{S}^{\hat{\mu}}=K_{0}^{\hat{\mu}}(\mathrm{Var}_{S})\left[\mathbb{L}^{-1}\right],

and

\mathscr{M}_{S,\mathrm{loc}}^{\hat{\mu}}=K_{0}^{\hat{\mu}}(\mathrm{Var}_{S})\left[\mathbb{L}^{-1},(\mathbb{L}^{n}-1)^{-1}\right]_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}.

Clearly, we have the identities

\mathscr{M}_{S}^{\hat{\mu}}=\varinjlim\mathscr{M}_{S}^{\mu_{n}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{M}_{S,\mathrm{loc}}^{\hat{\mu}}=\varinjlim\mathscr{M}_{S,\mathrm{loc}}^{\mu_{n}}.

By abuse of notation, we shall write
\mathrm{loc}
for any of the following localization morphisms
\mathscr{M}_{S}\to\mathscr{M}_{S,\mathrm{loc}}
,
\mathscr{M}_{S}^{\mu_{n}}\to\mathscr{M}_{S,\mathrm{loc}}^{\mu_{n}}
and
\mathscr{M}_{S}^{\hat{\mu}}\to\mathscr{M}_{S,\mathrm{loc}}^{\hat{\mu}}
.

When
S
is
\mathrm{Spec}k
, we shall write simply
\mathrm{Var}_{k}
,
\mathscr{M}_{k}
,
\mathscr{M}_{k}^{G}
,
\mathscr{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
and
\mathscr{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{G}
instead of
\mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{Spec}k}
,
\mathscr{M}_{\mathrm{Spec}k}
,
\mathscr{M}_{\mathrm{Spec}k}^{G}
,
\mathscr{M}_{\mathrm{Spec}k,\mathrm{loc}}
and
\mathscr{M}_{\mathrm{Spec}k,\mathrm{loc}}^{G}
, respectively.

3.
Arc spaces and rational series

3.1.
Arc spaces

Let
X
be an algebraic
k
-variety. For
e\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, let
\mathscr{L}_{e}(X)
be the space of
e
-jet schemes of
X
, which is actually a
k
-scheme representing the functor sending a
k
-algebra
A
to the set of morphisms of
k
-schemes

\mathrm{Spec}(A[t]/(t^{e+1}))\to X.

Thus, the set of
A
-rational points of
\mathscr{L}_{e}(X)
is naturally identified with the set of
A[t]/(t^{e+1})
-rational points of
X
.

For
d\geq e
in
\mathbb{N}^{*}
, the truncation modulo
t^{e+1}
induces an affine morphism of
k
-schemes

\mathscr{L}_{d}(X)\to\mathscr{L}_{e}(X)

denoted by
\pi_{e}^{d}
. If
X
is a smooth variety of dimension
d
, the morphism
\pi_{e}^{d}
is a locally trivial fibration with fiber
\mathbb{A}_{k}^{(d-e)\dim_{k}X}
.

The above jet schemes
\mathscr{L}_{e}(X)
and truncation morphisms
\pi_{e}^{d}
form in a natural way a projective system of
k
-schemes. As the truncation morphisms are affine, the projective limit of this system exists in the category of
k
-schemes and is called
arc space of
X
and denoted by
\mathscr{L}(X)
with truncation morphisms

\pi_{e}:\mathscr{L}(X)\rightarrow\mathscr{L}_{e}(X).

If
k\subseteq K
is a field extension of
k
, then the

K
-rational points of
\mathscr{L}(X)
correspond one-to-one to the
K[\![t]\!]
-rational points of
X
.

Recall from
[
8
, Section 2]
, for any algebraically closed field
K
containing
k
, that a subset of
K(\!(t)\!)^{m}\times\mathbb{Z}^{r}
is
semi-algebraic
if it is a finite boolean combination of sets of the forms

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left\{(x,\alpha)\in K(\!(t)\!)^{m}\times\mathbb{Z}^{r}\mid\mathrm{ord}_{t}f(x)\geq\mathrm{ord}_{t}g(x)+\ell(\alpha)\right\},

and

(3.2)

\displaystyle\left\{(x,\alpha)\in K(\!(t)\!)^{m}\times\mathbb{Z}^{r}\mid\mathrm{ord}_{t}f(x)\equiv\ell(\alpha)\mod n\right\},

and

(3.3)

\displaystyle\left\{(x,\alpha)\in K(\!(t)\!)^{m}\times\mathbb{Z}^{r}\mid\Phi(\overline{\mathrm{ac}}(f_{1}(x)),\dots,\overline{\mathrm{ac}}(f_{p}(x)))=0\right\},

where
f
,
g
,
f_{i}
and
\Phi
are
k
-polynomials,
\ell
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-polynomial of degree at most 1,
n
is in
\mathbb{N}
, and
\overline{\mathrm{ac}}(f_{i}(x))
is the angular component of
f_{i}(x)
. One calls a collection of formulas defining a semi-algebraic set a
semi-algebraic condition
. A subset
A
of
\mathscr{L}(X)
is called
semi-algebraic
if there exists a covering of
X
by affine Zariski open sets
U
such that
A\cap\mathscr{L}(U)
is of the form

(3.4)

\displaystyle A\cap\mathscr{L}(U)=\{x\in\mathscr{L}(U)\mid\theta(f_{1}(\tilde{x}),\dots,f_{p}(\tilde{x});\alpha)\},

where
f_{i}
are regular functions on
U
,
\theta
is a semi-algebraic condition,
\alpha
may be a given tuple of integers or nothing, and
\tilde{x}
is the element in
\mathscr{L}(U)(k(x))
corresponding to a point
x
in
\mathscr{L}(U)
of residue field
k(x)
.

By
[
17
]
, if
g:X\to Y
is a morphism of algebraic
k
-varieties and
A
is a semi-algebraic subset of
\mathscr{L}(X)
, then
g(A)
is a semi-algebraic subset of
Y
. Then the map
g:A\to g(A)
is called a
semi-algebraic morphism
of semi-algebraic sets. More generally, let
A
and
B
be semi-algebraic subsets of
\mathscr{L}(X)
and
\mathscr{L}(Y)
, the arc spaces of
k
-varieties
X
and
Y
, respectively, and let
h:A\to B
be a map. Then
h
is called a
semi-algebraic morphism
if its graph is a semi-algebraic subset of
\mathscr{L}(X\times_{k}Y)
. Denote by
\mathrm{SA}_{k}
be the category whose objects are pairs
(A,\mathscr{L}(X))
, where
A
is a semi-algebraic subset of the arc space
\mathscr{L}(X)
of an algebraic
k
-variety
X
, and a morphism of
\mathrm{SA}_{k}
between two objects
(A,\mathscr{L}(X))
and
(B,\mathscr{L}(Y))
is a semi-algebraic morphism of semi-algebraic sets
A\to B
. For a given
k
-variety
X
, we can consider the full subcategory
\mathrm{SA}_{k}(X)
of
\mathrm{SA}_{k}
consists of semi-algebraic subsets of
\mathscr{L}(X)
.

In the sense of
[
8
, Definition-Proposition 3.2]
, Denef-Loeser’s motivic volume is defined on
\mathrm{Ob}\mathrm{SA}_{k}(X)
the set of all the semi-algebraic subsets of
\mathscr{L}(X)
with the reasonable properties. By
[
4
, Remark 16.3.2]
, this motivic volume essentially takes values in
\mathscr{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
. In the present article, we denote Denef-Loeser’s motivic volume by
\mu^{\prime}
(the symbol
\mu
will be devoted to indicate Cluckers-Loeser’s motivic volume
[
4
]
).

3.2.
Rationality

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a commutative ring with unity containing
\mathbb{L}
and
\mathbb{L}^{-1}
, and let
\mathscr{M}[[T]]
be the set of formal power series in
T
with coefficients in
\mathscr{M}
, which is a ring and also a
\mathscr{M}
-module with respect to usual operations for series. Denote by
\mathscr{M}[[T]]_{\mathrm{sr}}
the submodule of
\mathscr{M}[[T]]
generated by 1 and by finite products of terms

\frac{\mathbb{L}^{p}T^{q}}{(1-\mathbb{L}^{p}T^{q})}

for
(p,q)
in
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{N}^{*}
. An element of
\mathscr{M}[[T]]_{\mathrm{sr}}
is called a
rational
series. By
[
7
]
, there exists a unique
\mathscr{M}
-linear morphism

\lim_{T\to\infty}:\mathscr{M}[[T]]_{\mathrm{sr}}\to\mathscr{M}

such that for any
(p,q)
in
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{N}^{*}
,

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{L}^{p}T^{q}}{(1-\mathbb{L}^{p}T^{q})}=-1.

Let us recall some examples on the rationality. Let
X
be a smooth algebraic
k
-variety of pure dimension
m
, and
f
a regular function on
X
with zero locus
X_{0}\not=\emptyset
. For
e
in
\mathbb{N}^{*}
, put

X[e]=\left\{\gamma\in\mathscr{L}_{e}(X)\mid f(\gamma)=t^{e}\mod t^{e+1}\right\},

which is naturally an
X_{0}
-variety and stable under the action
\lambda\cdot\gamma(t):=\gamma(\lambda t)
of
\mu_{e}
on
\mathscr{L}_{e}(X)
.
Write simply
\big[X[e]\big]
for the class
\big[X[e]\to X_{0}\big]
in
\mathscr{M}_{X_{0}}^{\mu_{e}}
. It is proved in
[
7
]
that the series

\displaystyle Z_{f}(T):=\sum_{e\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\big[X[e]\big]\mathbb{L}^{-em}T^{e},

is a rational series, i.e., in
\mathscr{M}_{X_{0}}^{\hat{\mu}}[[T]]_{\mathrm{sr}}
. More general, we can obtain the rationality of a series generalizing
Z_{f}(T)
without assuming that
X
is smooth, and with
f
concerning several semi-algebraic subsets in
\mathscr{L}(X)
. Let
\mu^{\prime}
be Denef-Loeser’s motivic volume defined in
[
8
]
. The below theorem is a result given in
[
13
, Proposition 4.6]
.

Theorem 3.1

(Lê-Nguyen
[
13
]
)
.

Let
X
be a
k
-variety and
f
a regular function on
X
. Let
A_{\alpha}
,
\alpha
in
\mathbb{N}^{r}
, be a family of semi-algebraic subsets of
\mathscr{L}(X)
such that there exists a covering of
X
by affine Zariski open sets
U
satisfying the condition that
A_{\alpha}\cap\mathscr{L}(U)
are finite boolean combinations of sets of the forms (
3.1
) and (
3.2
). Assume that, for every
\alpha
in
\mathbb{N}^{r}
,
A_{\alpha}
is stable in the sense of
[
8
]
and disjoint with
\mathscr{L}(X_{\text{Sing}})
. For

, we put

A_{e,\alpha}:=\left\{\gamma\in A_{\alpha}\mid f(\gamma)=t^{e}\mod t^{e+1}\right\}.

Let
\Delta
be a rational polyhedral convex cone in

and
\bar{\Delta}
its closure. Let

and

be integral linear forms on

with

and

4.
Categories of definable subassignments

In this section, we recall some concepts and results on motivic integration in the sense of Cluckers-Loeser
[
4
]
. We also provide an equivariant version for objects concerning definable
T_{\mathrm{acl}}
-subassignments, where
T_{\mathrm{acl}}
is the theory of all algebraically closed fields containing
k
.

4.1.
Definable subassignments

We consider the formalism of Cluckers and Loeser
[
4
]
with a concrete Denef-Pas language
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}
consisting of the ring language
\mathbf{L}_{\mathrm{Rings}}=\left\{+,-,\cdot,0,1\right\}
for valued fields, also the ring language
\mathbf{L}_{\mathrm{Rings}}
for residue fields, and the Presburger language
\mathbf{L}_{\mathrm{PR}}
for value groups, where

\mathbf{L}_{\mathrm{PR}}=\left\{+,-,0,1,\leq\right\}\cup\left\{\equiv_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\right\},

and
\equiv_{n}
is the equivalence relation modulo
n
.

Let
\mathrm{Field}_{k}
be the category of all fields
K
containing
k
whose morphisms are field morphisms. For any
K
in
\mathrm{Field}_{k}
, we consider the natural valuation map
\mathrm{ord}_{t}:K(\!(t)\!)^{\times}\to\mathbb{Z}
augmented by
\mathrm{ord}_{t}(0)=+\infty
, and the natural angular component map
\overline{\mathrm{ac}}:K(\!(t)\!)^{\times}\to K
augmented by
\overline{\mathrm{ac}}(0)=0
.

For a basic set

V:=\mathbb{A}_{k(\!(t)\!)}^{m}\times\mathbb{A}_{k}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{r},

with
m
,
n
,
r
in
\mathbb{N}
, we consider the functor
h_{V}
(also denoted by
h[m,n,r]
) from
\mathrm{Field}_{k}
to the category of sets defined by

h_{V}(K)=h[m,n,r](K):=K(\!(t)\!)^{m}\times K^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{r}.

If
\mathsf{X}
is a map sending each object
K
of
\mathrm{Field}_{k}
to a subset
\mathsf{X}(K)
of
K(\!(t)\!)^{m}\times K^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{r}
, then
\mathsf{X}
is called a
affine subassignment
(or, shortly speaking,
subassignment
) of
h_{V}=h[m,n,r]
. Note that
\mathsf{X}
is not necessarily a subfunctor of
h[m,n,r]
. In the same way, we can define morphisms of subassignments and their graph, as well as union, subtraction, Cartesian product and fiber product of two subassignments.

A subassignment
\mathsf{X}
of
h[m,n,r]
is called
definable
if there exists a formula
\varphi
in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}
with
k(\!(t)\!)
-coefficients and
m
free variables in the valued field sort,
k
-coefficients and
n
free variables in residue field sort, and
r
free variables in the value group sort, such that, for any
K
in
\mathrm{Field}_{k}
,

\mathsf{X}(K)=\left\{x\in K(\!(t)\!)^{m}\times K^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{r}\mid(K(\!(t)\!),K,\mathbb{Z})\models\varphi(x)\right\}.

In this setting, we also write
h_{\varphi}
for the definable subassignment
\mathsf{X}
. Denote by
\emptyset
the empty definable subassignment, with
\emptyset(K)=\emptyset
for any
K
in
\mathrm{Field}_{k}
. For
\mathsf{X}
and
\mathsf{X}^{\prime}
being definable subassignments of
h[m,n,r]
and
h[m^{\prime},n^{\prime},r^{\prime}]
, respectively, a
definable
morphism
\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{X}^{\prime}
is a morphism of subassignments
\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{X}^{\prime}
such that its graph is a definable subassignment of
h[m+m^{\prime},n+n^{\prime},r+r^{\prime}]
.

For a set

W:=\mathcal{X}\times X\times\mathbb{Z}^{r},

with
\mathcal{X}
an algebraic
k(\!(t)\!)
-variety, and
X
an algebraic
k
-variety, we define

h_{W}(K):=\mathcal{X}(K(\!(t)\!))\times X(K)\times\mathbb{Z}^{r},

for any
K
in
\mathrm{Field}_{k}
. In general, we can define definable subassignments of
h_{W}
, definable morphisms of definable subassignments, and usual operations on definable subassignments of functors of the form
h_{W}
using a glueing procedure (as in
[
4
, Section 2.3]
): taking finite covers (always exist) of
\mathcal{X}
and
X
by affine open
k(\!(t)\!)
-subvarieties and
k
-subvarieties, respectively, going back to the definition of affine definable subassignment, and glueing them.

We consider the category
\mathrm{Def}_{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}})
(or
\mathrm{Def}_{k}
for short) of affine definable subassignments, where its objects are pairs
(\mathsf{X},h[m,n,r])
with
\mathsf{X}
being a definable subassignment of
h[m,n,r]
, and a morphism

(\mathsf{X},h[m,n,r])\to(\mathsf{X}^{\prime},h[m^{\prime},n^{\prime},r^{\prime}])

in
\mathrm{Def}_{k}
is a definable morphism
\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{X}^{\prime}
. We also consider the category
\mathrm{GDef}_{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}})
(or
\mathrm{GDef}_{k}
for short) of global definable subassignments, where objects of
\mathrm{GDef}_{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}})
are pairs
(\mathsf{X},h_{W})
with
h_{W}
as above and
\mathsf{X}
being a definable subassignment of
h_{W}
, and a morphism

(\mathsf{X},h_{W})\to(\mathsf{X}^{\prime},h_{W^{\prime}})

in
\mathrm{GDef}_{k}
is a definable morphism
\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{X}^{\prime}
. For any affine definable subassignment
\mathsf{S}
, we denote by
\mathrm{Def}_{\mathsf{S}}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}})
(or
\mathrm{Def}_{\mathsf{S}}
for short) the category of morphisms
\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{S}
in
\mathrm{Def}_{k}
, and a morphism in
\mathrm{Def}_{\mathsf{S}}
between
\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{S}
and
\mathsf{X}^{\prime}\to\mathsf{S}
is a morphism
\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{X}^{\prime}
in
\mathrm{Def}_{k}
which is compatible with the morphisms to
\mathsf{S}
. For any definable subassignment
\mathsf{S}
, the category
\mathrm{GDef}_{\mathsf{S}}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}})
(or
\mathrm{GDef}_{\mathsf{S}}
for short) can be defined in the same way as
\mathrm{GDef}_{k}
with
h_{\mathrm{Spec}k}
replaced by
\mathsf{S}
.

When we consider the category
\mathrm{Field}_{k}(T_{\mathrm{acl}})
of all algebraically closed fields containing
k
in stead of
\mathrm{Field}_{k}
, and use the language
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k)
(see the definition in Section
4.3
) instead of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}
, we obtain corresponding notions of
T_{\mathrm{acl}}
-subassignment,
\mathrm{Def}_{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})
and
\mathrm{GDef}_{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})
.

4.2.
Points on definable subassignments

Let
\mathsf{X}
be an object in
\mathrm{GDef}_{k}
. A point

on
\mathsf{X}
is a tuple
x=(x_{0},K)
such that
K
is in
\mathrm{Field}_{k}
and
x_{0}
is in
\mathsf{X}(K)
. For such a point
x
on
\mathsf{X}
we usually write
k(x)
for
K
and call it the residue field of
x
. Let

f:\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{Y}

be a morphism in
\mathrm{Def}_{k}
, with

\mathsf{X}=(\mathsf{X}_{0},h[m,n,r])

and

\mathsf{Y}=(\mathsf{Y}_{0},h[m^{\prime},n^{\prime},r^{\prime}]),

whose graph is defined by a formula
\varphi(x,y)
, where
x
is in
h[m,n,r]
and
y
is in
h[m^{\prime},n^{\prime},r^{\prime}]
. One defines the fiber of

over a point

on

to be the definable subsassignment
\mathsf{X}_{y}
in
\mathrm{Def}_{k(y)}
given by the formula

. In the category
\mathrm{GDef}_{k}
, fibers of a morphism are defined in the same way by using affine covers.

4.3.
Categories
\mathrm{SDef}_{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})
,
\mathrm{SDef}_{k}(X,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})
,
\mathrm{RDef}_{h_{S}}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})

Denote by
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k)
the language extending
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}
by adding constants in
k
so that all polynomials in both valued field sort and residue field sort having coefficients in
k
. Let
X
be an algebraic
k
-variety, let

\mathcal{X}:=X\times_{k}\mathrm{Spec}k(\!(t)\!),

and
\mathsf{A}
a definable subassignment of
h_{\mathcal{X}}
defined by a formula in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k)
. Assume first
\mathcal{X}
is a closed subscheme in
\mathbb{A}_{k(\!(t)\!)}^{m}
, for some

in
\mathbb{N}
, such that the ideal defining
\mathcal{X}
generated by polynomials with coefficients in
k[[t]]
. The above mentioned definable subassignment
\mathsf{A}
(i.e., defined by a formula in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k)
) is called

\mathsf{A}
is contained in the following definable subassignment

\left\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in h[m,0,0]\mid\mathrm{ord}_{t}x_{i}\geq 0,1\leq i\leq m\right\}.

For
\mathcal{X}
not necessarily affine, we call

small
if there exists a cover of
\mathcal{X}
by open affine
k(\!(t)\!)
-subvarieties
\mathcal{U}_{i}
defined by the vanishing of polynomials with coefficients in

such that
\mathsf{A}\cap h_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}
are small for all

. Let
\mathrm{SDef}_{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})
be the subcategory of
\mathrm{GDef}_{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}},T_{\mathrm{acl}})
whose objects are pairs

(\mathsf{A},h_{X\times_{k}\mathrm{Spec}k(\!(t)\!)}),

where
X
is an algebraic
k
-variety and
\mathsf{A}
is a small definable

-subassignment of
h_{X\times_{k}\mathrm{Spec}k(\!(t)\!)}
, and a morphism in
\mathrm{SDef}_{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})
between objects

(\mathsf{A},h_{X\times_{k}\mathrm{Spec}k(\!(t)\!)})

and

(\mathsf{B},h_{Y\times_{k}\mathrm{Spec}k(\!(t)\!)})

is a
T_{\mathrm{acl}}
-morphism of
T_{\mathrm{acl}}
-subassignments

such that its graph is a small definable

-subassignment of

Fixing an algebraic

-variety

, we define a category denoted by

which is the full subcategory of

-subassignments of

Theorem 4.1
.

The categories
\mathrm{SDef}_{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})
and
\mathrm{SA}_{k}
are equivalent. If

is an algebraic

-variety, then the categories
\mathrm{SDef}_{k}(X,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})
and

are equivalent.

Proof.

At the moment, for short, we write
(\mathsf{A},X)
instead of
(\mathsf{A},h_{X\times_{k}\mathrm{Spec}k(\!(t)\!)})
for an object of
\mathrm{SDef}_{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})

First, let us construct a functor
\mathcal{F}
from

. Let

be an object of

. Then, there is a cover of

by Zariski open affine

-subvarieties

, with

-subvariety in some

(we can take

) and the embedding defined over

, such that for the standard coordinates

of

and any point

. Moreover,

is defined by a formula

and

are free variables in value group sort, namely,

By Denef-Pas’ quantifier elimination for algebraically closed fields (cf. Corollary 2.1.2 of

),

is equivalent to a finite disjunction of formulas of the form

(4.1)

\displaystyle\psi(\overline{\mathrm{ac}}g_{1}(\underline{x}),\dots,\overline{\mathrm{ac}}g_{q}(\underline{x}))\wedge\vartheta(\mathrm{ord}_{t}f_{1}(\underline{x}),\dots,\mathrm{ord}_{t}f_{p}(\underline{x}),\alpha),

) and (

Since all polynomials

and

have coefficients in

have coefficients in

-subvariety

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}=U\times_{k}\mathrm{Spec}k(\!(t)\!),

hence we have a cover

of

by Zariski open affine
k
-subvarieties. The above
x_{i}
induce regular functions

on

are standard coordinate components in

for every

, and that

is defined by the vanishing of

for

. Now we put

A_{U}:=\left\{x\in\mathscr{L}(U)\mid\varphi(x^{\prime}_{1}(\tilde{x}),\dots,x^{\prime}_{m}(\tilde{x}),\alpha)\right\},

where

is defined after (

), and glue

’s into a semi-algebraic subset

of
\mathscr{L}(X)

. Let us define

\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{A},X):=(A,X),

which is an object in
\mathrm{SA}_{k}

We shall construct a morphism
\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{f})
in
\mathrm{SA}_{k}
which is the image under

\mathsf{f}
in
\mathrm{SDef}_{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{g}\circ\mathsf{f})=\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{g})\circ\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{f}).

Consider a morphism

for short, in

Since any morphism

-subassignments. By definition, for the

\mathsf{pr}_{\mathsf{B}}:(\mathsf{A}\times\mathsf{B},X\times_{k}Y)\to(\mathsf{B},Y)

we have

\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{pr}_{\mathsf{B}})=\mathrm{pr}_{B}:A\times B\to B,

the
B
-projection of semi-algebraic subset
A\times B
of
\mathscr{L}(X\times_{k}Y)=\mathscr{L}(X)\times_{k}\mathscr{L}(Y)
onto
B
, which is a morphism in
\mathrm{SA}_{k}
. Also, for a morphism
\mathsf{i}_{\mathsf{AB}}:(\mathsf{A},X)\to(\mathsf{B},X)
in
\mathrm{SDef}_{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})
induced by an inclusion
\mathsf{A}\hookrightarrow\mathsf{B}
of small definable
T_{\mathrm{acl}}
-subassignments, we have
\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{i}_{\mathsf{AB}})(x)=x
for all
x\in A
. Now, let us consider

\mathsf{i}:(\mathsf{C},X\times_{k}Y)\to(\mathsf{A}\times\mathsf{B},X\times_{k}Y),

which is a morphism in the category
\mathrm{SDef}_{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})
induced by an inclusion
\mathsf{C}\hookrightarrow\mathsf{A}\times\mathsf{B}
. By definition, it is clear that

\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{pr}_{\mathsf{B}}\circ\mathsf{i})=\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{pr}_{\mathsf{B}})\circ\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{i}).

In the same way, for a morphism

\mathsf{j}:(\mathsf{B},Y)\to(\mathsf{E},Y)

in
\mathrm{SDef}_{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k))
induced by an inclusion
\mathsf{B}\hookrightarrow\mathsf{E}
, we have

\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{j}\circ\mathsf{pr}_{\mathsf{B}})=\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{j})\circ\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{pr}_{\mathsf{B}}).

We now construct a functor
\mathcal{G}
from
\mathrm{SA}_{k}
to
\mathrm{SDef}_{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})
which is naturally inverse to
\mathcal{F}
. Let
(A,X)
be an object of
\mathrm{SA}_{k}
, i.e.,
A
is a semi-algebraic subset of
\mathscr{L}(X)
. By definition, there exist a cover of
X
by Zariski open affine
k
-subvarieties
V
(viewed as a closed
k
-subvariety of
\mathbb{A}_{k}^{m}
), and for each
V
, regular functions
h_{i}
on
V
,
1\leq i\leq m
, a semi-algebraic condition
\varphi
(with
h_{i}
and
\varphi
depending on
V
) such that

A\cap\mathscr{L}(V)=\left\{x\in\mathscr{L}(V)\mid\varphi(h_{1}(\tilde{x}),\dots,h_{m}(\tilde{x}),\alpha)\right\}.

Clearly,

\mathcal{V}:=V\times_{k}\mathrm{Spec}k(\!(t)\!)

is embedded over
k[[t]]
into
\mathbb{A}_{k(\!(t)\!)}^{m}
, and they form a cover of
X\times_{k}\mathrm{Spec}k(\!(t)\!)
. Note that
\varphi
is a formula in the language
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k)
, and that each
h_{i}
induces a definable morphism of definable subassignments

x_{i}:h_{\mathcal{V}}\to h[1,0,0].

Put

\mathsf{A}_{\mathcal{V}}=\left\{x\in h_{\mathcal{V}}\mid\varphi(x_{1}(x),\dots,x_{m}(x),\alpha),\mathrm{ord}_{t}x_{i}(x)\geq 0,1\leq i\leq m\right\},

and glue all
\mathsf{A}_{\mathcal{V}}
along the cover
\{\mathcal{V}\}
of

to get a small definable
T_{\mathrm{acl}}
-subassignment
\mathsf{A}
of
h_{X\times_{k}\mathrm{Spec}k(\!(t)\!)}
. We can prove that the construction of
\mathsf{A}
is up to definable isomorphism independent of the choice of the cover

. So we can define

\mathcal{G}(A,X)=(\mathsf{A},X),

which is an object of
\mathrm{SDef}_{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})
. Similarly, we can define
\mathcal{G}(f)
to be a morphism of
\mathrm{SDef}_{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})
when

is a morphism of
\mathrm{SA}_{k}
, which satisfies

\mathcal{G}(f\circ g)=\mathcal{G}(f)\circ\mathcal{G}(g).

The existence of natural isomorphisms

\varepsilon:\mathcal{F}\circ\mathcal{G}\to\mathrm{Id}_{\mathrm{SA}_{k}}

and

\eta:\mathrm{Id}_{\mathrm{SDef}_{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})}\to\mathcal{G}\circ\mathcal{F}

follows from the fact that the construction of
\mathsf{A}
from

and vice versa is independent of the choice of covers by open affine subvarieties.
∎

Let
\mathsf{S}
be an object in
\mathrm{GDef}_{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})
. Let
\mathrm{RDef}_{\mathsf{S}}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})
denote the full subcategory of
\mathrm{GDef}_{\mathsf{S}}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})
such that each object
\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{S}
of
\mathrm{RDef}_{\mathsf{S}}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})
is the
\mathsf{S}
-projection of a definable subassignment
\mathsf{X}
of
\mathsf{S}\times h_{\mathbb{A}_{k}^{n}}
, for some
n
in
\mathbb{N}
. We first mention a special case when
\mathsf{S}=h_{S}
as follows. Let
S
be a closed
k
-subvariety of
\mathbb{A}_{k}^{d}
, for a given

in
\mathbb{N}
. Then the category
\mathrm{RDef}_{h_{S}}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})
defined previously is just the full subcategory of
\mathrm{Def}_{h_{S}}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})
, its objects

are the

-projection of definable subassignments

of

, with

being variable in

.

Theorem 4.2
.

For any
k
-variety
S
, the categories
\mathrm{RDef}_{h_{S}}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})
and
\mathrm{Cons}_{S}
are equivalent.

Proof.

Using the argument in
[
4
, Section 16.2]
, under some elimination theorems, we have that objects of
\mathrm{Def}_{k}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}})
are defined by formulas without quantifiers in the Denef-Pas language
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}
. Since Chevalley’s constructibility theorem in algebraic geometry (over
k
) is nothing else than the quantifier elimination theorem for the theory of algebraically closed fields containing
k
, a formula defining a definable subassignment of

defines a constructible subset of
S\times_{k}\mathbb{A}_{k}^{n}
, and via graph, a definable morphism of definable subassignments gives rise to a constructible morphism of constructible sets, and vice versa. For a detailed argument, we can use the strategy in the proof of Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Let
\mathsf{S}

4

, the Grothendieck semigroup

of the category
\mathrm{RDef}_{\mathsf{S}}

[\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{S}]
with
\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{S}

\mathrm{RDef}_{\mathsf{S}}
modulo the following relations:

[\emptyset\to\mathsf{S}]=0,

[\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{S}]=[\mathsf{Y}\to\mathsf{S}]

and
\mathsf{Y}\to\mathsf{S}

\mathrm{RDef}_{\mathsf{S}}
, and

for definable subassignments
\mathsf{X}
and
\mathsf{Y}
of
\mathsf{S}\times h_{\mathbb{A}_{k}^{n}}
, for some
n
in
\mathbb{N}
, and morphisms of
\mathsf{X}
and
\mathsf{Y}
to
\mathsf{S}
factorizing through
\mathsf{S}
-projection. Denote by
K_{0}(\mathrm{RDef}_{\mathsf{S}})
the group associated to the Grothendieck semigroup

. If we provide

and
K_{0}(\mathrm{RDef}_{\mathsf{S}})
with a product induced by the fiber product over
\mathsf{S}
of morphisms of subassignments to
\mathsf{S}
defined in Section 2.2 of
[
4
]
, then they are commutative semiring and ring with unity, respectively. Remark that the canonical morphism

is not necessarily injective.

Let
S
be a
k
-variety endowed with a given
G
-action.
The
G
-equivariant Grothendieck group
K_{0}^{G}(\mathrm{RDef}_{h_{S}})
is the quotient of the free abelian group generated by symbols

[\mathsf{X}\to h_{S},\sigma]

with
\mathsf{X}
being a definable subassignment of
h_{S\times_{k}\mathbb{A}_{k}^{n}}
, for some
n
in
\mathbb{N}
, endowed with a good
G
-action
\sigma
, and
\mathsf{X}\to h_{S}
being a morphism in
\mathrm{Def}_{k}
, modulo the following relations

[\mathsf{X}\to h_{S},\sigma]=[\mathsf{Y}\to h_{S},\sigma^{\prime}]

if there exists a
G
-equivariant definable morphism
\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{Y}
which commutes with the definable morphisms to
h_{S}
,

[\mathsf{X}\to h_{S},\sigma]=[\mathsf{Y}\to h_{S},\sigma|_{\mathsf{Y}}]+[\mathsf{X}\setminus\mathsf{Y}\to h_{S},\sigma|_{\mathsf{X}\setminus\mathsf{Y}}]

for
\mathsf{Y}
being
\sigma
-stable definable subassignment of
\mathsf{X}
, and

[\mathsf{X}\times h_{\mathbb{A}_{k}^{m}}\to h_{S},\sigma]=[\mathsf{X}\times h_{\mathbb{A}_{k}^{m}}\to h_{S},\sigma^{\prime}]

if
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
lift the same
G
-action on
\mathsf{X}
to an affine action on
\mathsf{X}\times h_{\mathbb{A}_{k}^{m}}
, for any
m\geq 0
. As above, with respect to fiber product of subassignments endowed with diagonal
G
-action, the group
K_{0}^{G}(\mathrm{RDef}_{h_{S}})
is a commutative rings with unity.

The Grothendieck rings
K_{0}(\mathrm{RDef}_{\mathsf{S}}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}}))
and
K_{0}(\mathrm{RDef}_{h_{S}}^{G}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{P}}(k),T_{\mathrm{acl}}))

Lemma 4.4
.

For any

-variety

, there are canonical isomorphisms

and

This statement is a direct corollary of Theorem

and Lemma

and

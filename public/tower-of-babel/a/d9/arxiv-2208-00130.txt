On weak laws of large numbers for maximal partial sums of pairwise independent random variables
Lê Vǎn Thành

Abstract
. This paper develops Rio’s method [C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris Sér. I Math., 1995] to prove the weak law of large numbers for maximal partial sums of pairwise independent random variables. The method allows us to avoid using the Kolmogorov maximal inequality. As an application, a weak law of large numbers for maximal partial sums of pairwise independent random variables under a uniform integrability condition is also established. The sharpness of the result is illustrated by an example.

1.
Introduction and results

A real-valued function
L(\cdot)
is said to be
slowly varying
(at infinity) if it is
a positive and measurable function on
[A,\infty)
for some
A\geq 0
, and for each
\lambda>0
,

\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{L(\lambda x)}{L(x)}=1.

In
[
6
]
, de Bruijn proved that if
L(\cdot)
is a slowly varying function, then
there exists a slowly varying function
\tilde{L}(\cdot)
, unique up to asymptotic equivalence, satisfying

\lim_{x\to\infty}L(x)\tilde{L}\left(xL(x)\right)=1\ \text{ and }\lim_{x\to\infty}\tilde{L}(x)L\left(x\tilde{L}(x)\right)=1.

The function
\tilde{L}(\cdot)
is called the de Bruijn conjugate of
L(\cdot)
(
[
2
, p. 29]
).
Bojanić and Seneta
[
3
]
showed that for most of “nice” slowly varying functions,
we can choose
\tilde{L}(x)=1/L(x)
.
Especially, if
L(x)=\log^{\gamma}(x)
or
L(x)=\log^{\gamma}(\log(x))
for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, then
\tilde{L}(x)=1/L(x)
.
Here and thereafter, for a real number
x
,
\log(x)

denotes the natural logarithm (base
\mathrm{e}
) of
\max\{x,\mathrm{e}\}
.

Let
L(\cdot)
be a slowly varying function and let
r>0
. By using a suitable asymptotic equivalence version (see Lemma 2.2 and Lemma 2.3 (i) in Anh et al.
[
1
]
),
we can assume that
L(\cdot)
is positive and
differentiable on
[a,\infty)
, and
x^{r}L(x)
is strictly increasing on
[a,\infty)
for some large
a
. Next, let

L_{1}(\cdot)
be a slowly varying function with

L_{1}(0)=0
with a linear growth to
L(a)

over
[0,a)
, and
L_{1}(x)\equiv L(x)
on
[a,\infty)
.
Then (i)
L_{1}(x)
is continuous on
[0,\infty)
and differentiable on
[a,\infty)
, and
(ii)
x^{r}L_{1}(x)
is strictly increasing on
[0,\infty)
.
In this paper, we will assume, without loss of generality, that properties (i) and (ii) are fulfilled for all slowly varying functions.

The starting point of the current research is the following weak law of large numbers (WLLN) which was proved by Gut
[
8
]
. Hereafter,
\mathbf{1}(A)
denotes the indicator function of
a set
A
.

Theorem 1

(Gut
[
8
]
)
.

Let
0<p\leq 1
and let

\{X,X_{n},\,n\geq 1\}
be a sequence of independent identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables. Let
L(\cdot)
be a slowly varying function
and let
b_{x}=x^{1/p}L(x),\ x\geq 0
.
Then

(1.1)

\begin{split}\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}-n\mathbb{E}\left(X\mathbf{1}(|X|\leq b_{n})\right)}{b_{n}}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}0\ \text{ as }n\to\infty\end{split}

if and only if

(1.2)

\lim_{n\to\infty}n\mathbb{P}(|X|>b_{n})=0.

The above WLLN
has been extended in
several directions, see
[
12
,
13
]
for
WLLNs with random indices for arrays of independent random variables
taking values in
Banach spaces, and see
[
4
,
5
,
9
,
10
]
and the references therein
for WLLNs for dependent random variables and dependent random vectors. Boukhari
[
4
, Theorem 1.2]
showed that for
0<p<1
, condition (
1.2
)
implies

(1.3)

\begin{split}\dfrac{\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}X_{i}\right|}{b_{n}}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}0\ \text{ as }n\to\infty,\end{split}

irrespective of the joint distribution of the
X_{n}
’s.
Boukhari
[
4
]
presented an example showing that his result
does not hold when
p=1
.
The proof of
the sufficient part of Theorem
1
in
[
8
]
works well with pairwise independent random variables
since we do not involve the maximal partial sums.
Krulov
[
10
]
and Chandra
[
5
]

established WLLNs for maximal partial sums for the case where the summands are negatively associated and asymptotically almost negatively associated, respectively.
The authors in
[
5
,
10
]
considered general normalizing constants, which
showed that the sufficient part of Theorem
1
also holds for
1\leq p<2
.
However, the method used in
[
5
,
10
]
requires
a Kolmogorov-type maximal inequality (see Lemma 1.2 in
[
5
]
) which does not hold for pairwise independent random variables.

The aim of this paper is to establish WLLNs for maximal partial sums of pairwise independent random variables thereby
extending the sufficient part of
Theorem
1
for the case
p=1
to WLLN for maximal partial sums from sequences of pairwise independent random variables.
We use a technique developed by Rio
[
11
]
to avoid using the Kolmogorov
maximal inequality.
In addition, we also establish
a WLLN for maximal partial sums
of pairwise independent random variables
under a uniform integrability condition,
and present an example to show that this result does not hold in general if the
uniform integrability assumption is weakened to the uniform boundedness of the moments.

Let
\Lambda
be a nonempty index set.
A family of random variables
\{X_{\lambda},\lambda\in\Lambda\}
is said to be
stochastically
dominated
by a random variable
X
if

(1.4)

\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathbb{P}(|X_{\lambda}|>t)\leq\mathbb{P}(|X|>t)\mbox{ for all }t\geq 0.

Some authors use an apparently weaker definition of
\{X_{\lambda},\lambda\in\Lambda\}
being
stochastically dominated by a random variable
Y
, namely that

(1.5)

\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathbb{P}(|X_{\lambda}|>t)\leq C_{1}\mathbb{P}(C_{2}|Y|>t)\mbox{ for all }t\geq 0

for some constants
C_{1},C_{2}\in(0,\infty)
.
It is shown recently by Rosalsky and Thành
[
14
]
that
(
1.4
) and (
1.5
) are indeed equivalent. We note that if (
1.4
) is satisfied, then for all
t>0
and
r>0

\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathbb{E}(|X_{\lambda}|^{r}\mathbf{1}(|X_{\lambda}|\leq t))\leq\mathbb{E}(|X|^{r}\mathbf{1}(|X|\leq t))+t^{r}\mathbb{P}\{|X|>t\},

and

\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathbb{E}(|X_{\lambda}|^{r}\mathbf{1}(|X_{\lambda}|>t))\leq\mathbb{E}(|X|^{r}\mathbf{1}(|X|>t)).

The following theorem is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 2
.

Let
1\leq p<2
and let

\{X_{n},\,n\geq 1\}
be a sequence of pairwise independent random variables which is stochastically dominated by a random variable
X
.
Let
L(\cdot)
be a slowly varying function and let
b_{n}=n^{1/p}L(n)
,
n\geq 1
.
If

(1.6)

\lim_{n\to\infty}n\mathbb{P}(|X|>b_{n})=0,

then

(1.7)

\begin{split}\dfrac{\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(X_{i}-\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}\mathbf{1}(|X_{i}|\leq b_{n})\right)\right)\right|}{b_{n}}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}0\ \text{ as }n\to\infty.\end{split}

We postpone the proof of Theorem
2
to Section
2
.
From Theorem 3.2 of Boukhari
[
4
]
, we have that if
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
is a sequence of
pairwise independent random variables, and
\{b_{n},n\geq 1\}
is a sequence of
positive constants, then

(1.8)

\frac{\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|X_{i}|}{b_{n}}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}0\text{ if and only if }\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(|X_{i}|>b_{n}\varepsilon)\to 0\text{ for all }\varepsilon>0.

By using Theorem
2
and (
1.8
), we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3
.

Let
\{X,X_{n},\,n\geq 1\}
be a sequence of pairwise i.i.d. random variables.
Let
p
,
L(\cdot)
,
b_{n}
be as in Theorem
2
. Then (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) are equivalent.

Proof.

If (
1.6
) holds, then (
1.7
) follows immediately from Theorem
2
. Now, assume that (
1.7
) holds.
By the symmetrization procedure, it suffices to check the case where the random variables
X_{n}
,
n\geq 1
are symmetric. In this case,
(
1.7
) becomes

(1.9)

\dfrac{\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\left|S_{j}\right|}{b_{n}}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}0\ \text{ as }n\to\infty,

where
S_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}X_{i},j\geq 1
. Putting
S_{0}=0
, and applying (
1.9
) and inequality

\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|X_{j}|\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|S_{j}|+\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|S_{j-1}|,

we obtain

(1.10)

\dfrac{\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\left|X_{j}\right|}{b_{n}}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}0\ \text{ as }n\to\infty.

By combining (
1.8
) and (
1.10
), and using the identical distribution assumption, we obtain (
1.6
).
∎

Theorem
2
also enables us to establish
a WLLN for maximal partial sums
of pairwise independent random variables
under a uniform integrability condition.
After this paper was submitted, Thành
[
16
, Corollary 4.10]
established a similar
WLLN for triangular arrays of random variables satisfying a Kolmogorov-type maximal inequality. Theorem
4

and Corollary 4.10 of Thành
[
16
]
do not imply each other.

Hereafter,
we denote the de Bruijn
conjugate of a slowly varying function
L(\cdot)

by
\tilde{L}(\cdot)
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
1\leq p<2
and let
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
be a sequence of pairwise independent random variables.
Let
L(\cdot)
be a slowly varying function.
If
\{|X_{n}|^{p}L(|X_{n}|^{p}),n\geq 1\}
is uniformly integrable,
then

(1.11)

\begin{split}\dfrac{\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(X_{i}-\mathbb{E}(X_{i})\right)\right|}{b_{n}}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}0\ \text{ as }n\to\infty,\end{split}

where
b_{n}=n^{1/p}\tilde{L}^{1/p}(n)
,
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
f(x)=x^{p}L(x^{p}),\ g(x)=x^{1/p}\tilde{L}^{1/p}(x),\ x\geq 0
.
Recalling that we have assumed, without loss of generality, that
f
and
g
are strictly increasing on
[0,\infty)
.
From Lemma 2.5 in Anh et al.
[
1
]
, we have

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{f(g(x))}{x}=1,

and therefore

(1.12)

f(g(n))>n/2\text{ for all large }n.

By the de La Vallée Poussin criterion for uniform integrability,
there exists a nondecreasing function
h
defined on
[0,\infty)
with
h(0)=0
such that

(1.13)

\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{h(x)}{x}=\infty,

and

(1.14)

\sup_{i\geq 1}\mathbb{E}(h(f(|X_{i}|)))=\sup_{i\geq 1}\mathbb{E}(h(|X_{i}|^{p}L(|X_{i}|^{p})))<\infty.

By using Theorem 2.5 (i) of Rosalsky and Thành
[
14
]
, (
1.14
) implies that
the sequence
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
is stochastically dominated by a nonnegative random variable
X
with
distribution function

F(x)=1-\sup_{i\geq 1}\mathbb{P}(|X_{i}|>x),\ x\in\mathbb{R}.

We thus have by (
1.12
), (
1.13
), (
1.14
) and the Markov inequality that

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}n\mathbb{P}\left(X>b_{n}\right)&=\lim_{n\to\infty}n\sup_{i\geq 1}\mathbb{P}(|X_{i}|>g(n))\\
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}n\sup_{i\geq 1}\mathbb{P}(f(|X_{i}|)>f(g(n)))\\
&\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}n\sup_{i\geq 1}\mathbb{P}(f(|X_{i}|)\geq n/2)\\
&\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}n\sup_{i\geq 1}\mathbb{P}(h(f(|X_{i}|))\geq h(n/2))\\
&\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}n\sup_{i\geq 1}\dfrac{\mathbb{E}(h(f(|X_{i}|)))}{h(n/2))}\\
&=2\sup_{i\geq 1}\mathbb{E}(h(f(|X_{i}|)))\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{n/2}{h(n/2)}=0.\end{split}

Applying Theorem
2
, we obtain

(1.15)

\begin{split}\dfrac{\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(X_{i}-\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}\mathbf{1}(|X_{i}|\leq b_{n})\right)\right)\right|}{b_{n}}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}0\ \text{ as }n\to\infty.\end{split}

By the same argument as in the proof of Corollary 4.10 of Thành
[
16
]
(see Equation (4.45) in
[
16
]
), we have

(1.16)

\begin{split}\dfrac{\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i}\mathbf{1}(|X_{i}|>b_{n})\right)\right|}{b_{n}}\to 0\ \text{ as }n\to\infty.\end{split}

Combining (
1.15
) and (
1.16
) yields (
1.11
).
∎

The following example shows that in Theorem
4
,
the assumption that
\{|X_{n}|^{p}L(|X_{n}|^{p}),n\geq 1\}
is uniformly integrable cannot be weakened to

(1.17)

\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}(|X_{n}|^{p}L(|X_{n}|^{p}))<\infty.

Example 5
.

Let
1\leq p<2
, and let

\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
be a sequence of
independent symmetric random variables with

\mathbb{P}(X_{n}=0)=1-\frac{1}{n},\ \mathbb{P}(X_{n}=n^{1/p})=\mathbb{P}(X_{n}=-n^{1/p})=\frac{1}{2n},\ n\geq 1.

Consider the case where the slowly varying function
L(x)\equiv 1
. Then it is clear that

\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}(|X_{n}|^{p}L(|X_{n}|^{p}))=\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}(|X_{n}|^{p})=1<\infty

and

\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}(|X_{n}|^{p}L(|X_{n}|^{p})\mathbf{1}(|X_{n}|^{p}L(|X_{n}|^{p})>a))=\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}(|X_{n}|^{p}\mathbf{1}(|X_{n}|>a^{1/p}))=1

for all
a>0
. Therefore (
1.17
) is satisfied but
\{|X_{n}|^{p}L(|X_{n}|^{p}),n\geq 1\}
is not uniformly integrable.
For a real number
x
, let
\lfloor x\rfloor
denote the greatest integer that is smaller than or equal to
x
. Then for
0<\varepsilon<1/4
and
for
n\geq 2
, we have

(1.18)

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(|X_{i}|>\varepsilon n^{1/p})&\geq\sum_{i=\lfloor n/2\rfloor}^{n}\mathbb{P}(|X_{i}|>\varepsilon n^{1/p})\\
&\geq\sum_{i=\lfloor n/2\rfloor}^{n}\frac{1}{n}\geq\frac{1}{2}.\end{split}

Combining (
1.8
) and (
1.18
) yields

\frac{\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|X_{i}|}{n^{1/p}}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\nrightarrow}0.

This implies that (
1.11
) (with
b_{n}\equiv n^{1/p}
) fails.

2.
Proof of Theorem
2

The following lemma plays an important role in the proof of Theorem
2
. It gives a general approach to the WLLN.
In this lemma, we do not require any dependence structure. Throughout this section, we use the symbol
C
to denote a universal positive constant
which is not necessarily the same in
each appearance.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\{b_{n},n\geq 1\}
be a nondecreasing sequence of positive numbers satisfying

(2.1)

\sup_{m\geq 1}\frac{b_{2^{m}}}{b_{2^{m-1}}}<\infty.

Let
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
be a sequence of
random variables which is stochastically dominated by a random variable
X
and let
X_{i,n}=X_{i}\mathbf{1}(|X_{i}|\leq b_{n}),\ n\geq 1,i\geq 1
. Assume that

(2.2)

\lim_{n\to\infty}n\mathbb{P}(|X|>b_{n})=0.

Then

(2.3)

\dfrac{\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(X_{i}-\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i,n}\right)\right)\right|}{b_{n}}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}0\ \text{ as }n\to\infty

if and only if

(2.4)

\dfrac{\max_{1\leq j<2^{n}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(X_{i,2^{n}}-\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i,2^{n}}\right)\right)\right|}{b_{2^{n}}}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}0\ \text{ as }n\to\infty.

Proof.

We firstly prove under (
2.2
) that

(2.5)

\begin{split}\dfrac{\max_{1\leq j<2^{n}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(X_{i}-X_{i,2^{n}}\right)\right|}{b_{2^{n}}}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}0\ \text{ as }n\to\infty.\end{split}

To see this, let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. Then

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left(\dfrac{\max_{1\leq j<2^{n}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(X_{i}-X_{i,2^{n}}\right)\right|}{b_{2^{n}}}>\varepsilon\right)&\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{2^{n}-1}\left(X_{i}\not=X_{i,2^{n}}\right)\right)\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n}-1}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{i}\not=X_{i,2^{n}}\right)\\
&\leq 2^{n}\mathbb{P}\left(|X|>b_{2^{n}}\right)\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty\text{ (by \eqref{lem.eq03})}\end{split}

thereby proving (
2.5
) since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary.

Next, assume that (
2.3
) holds. Then

(2.6)

\dfrac{\max_{1\leq j<2^{n}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(X_{i}-\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i,2^{n}}\right)\right)\right|}{b_{2^{n}}}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}0\ \text{ as }n\to\infty.

Combining (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), we obtain (
2.4
).

Finally, assume that (
2.4
) holds.
It follows from (
2.4
) and
(
2.5
) that

(2.7)

\begin{split}\dfrac{\max_{1\leq j<2^{n}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(X_{i}-\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i,2^{n}}\right)\right)\right|}{b_{2^{n}}}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}0\ \text{ as }n\to\infty.\end{split}

Now, for
m\geq 1
, set

K_{m}=\max_{2^{m-1}\leq n<2^{m}}\frac{\max_{1\leq j<2^{m}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\mathbb{E}(X_{i,2^{m}}-X_{i,n})\right|}{b_{2^{m-1}}}.

Then by using (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and the stochastic domination assumption, we have

(2.8)

\begin{split}K_{m}&\leq\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{2^{m}}\mathbb{E}\left(|X_{i}|\mathbf{1}(b_{2^{m-1}}<|X_{i}|\leq b_{2^{m}})\right)}{b_{2^{m-1}}}\\
&\leq\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{2^{m}}b_{2^{m}}\mathbb{P}(|X_{i}|>b_{2^{m-1}})}{b_{2^{m-1}}}\\
&\leq C2^{m}\mathbb{P}(|X|>b_{2^{m-1}})\to 0\text{ as }m\to\infty.\end{split}

For
n\geq 1
, let
m\geq 1
be such that
2^{m-1}\leq n<2^{m}
. Then by (
2.1
), (
2.7
) and (
2.8
)), we have

\begin{split}\frac{\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}(X_{i}-\mathbb{E}(X_{i,n}))\right|}{b_{n}}&\leq\dfrac{\max_{1\leq j<2^{m}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}(X_{i}-\mathbb{E}(X_{i,2^{m}}))\right|}{b_{2^{m-1}}}\\
&\qquad+\dfrac{\max_{1\leq j<2^{m}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\mathbb{E}(X_{i,2^{m}}-X_{i,n})\right|}{b_{2^{m-1}}}\\
&\leq\dfrac{C\max_{1\leq j<2^{m}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}(X_{i}-\mathbb{E}(X_{i,2^{m}}))\right|}{b_{2^{m}}}+K_{m}\\
&\to 0\text{ as }m\to\infty\end{split}

thereby establishing (
2.3
).

∎

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Let

X_{i,n}=X_{i}\mathbf{1}(|X_{i}|\leq b_{n}),\ n\geq 1,i\geq 1.

It is clear that the sequence
\{b_{n},n\geq 1\}
satisfies (
2.1
). By Lemma
6
, it suffices to show that

(2.9)

\begin{split}\dfrac{\max_{1\leq j<2^{n}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(X_{i,2^{n}}-\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i,2^{n}}\right)\right)\right|}{b_{2^{n}}}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}0\ \text{ as }n\to\infty.\end{split}

For
m\geq 0,
set
S_{0,m}=0
and

S_{j,m}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}(X_{i,2^{m}}-\mathbb{E}X_{i,2^{m}}),\ j\geq 1.

We will use techniques developed by Rio
[
11
]
(see also
[
15
]
for the case of regular varying normalizing sequences) as follows.
For
n\geq 1
,
1\leq j<2^{n}
and for
0\leq m\leq n
, let
k=\lfloor j/2^{m}\rfloor
be the greatest integer which is
less than or equal to
j/2^{m}
. Then
0\leq k<2^{n-m}
and
k2^{m}\leq j<(k+1)2^{m}
. Let
j_{m}=k2^{m}
, and

Y_{i,m}=\left|X_{i,2^{m}}-X_{i,2^{m-1}}\right|-\mathbb{E}\left(\left|X_{i,2^{m}}-X_{i,2^{m-1}}\right|\right).

Then we can show that (see
[
15
, Page 1236]
)

(2.10)

\begin{split}\max_{1\leq j<2^{n}}\left|S_{j,n}\right|&\leq\sum_{m=1}^{n}\max_{0\leq k<2^{n-m}}\left|\sum_{i=k2^{m}+1}^{k2^{m}+2^{m-1}}\left(X_{i,2^{m-1}}-\mathbb{E}(X_{i,2^{m-1}})\right)\right|+\sum_{m=1}^{n}\max_{0\leq k<2^{n-m}}\left|\sum_{i=k2^{m}+1}^{(k+1)2^{m}}Y_{i,m}\right|\\
&\quad+\sum_{m=1}^{n}2^{m+1}b_{2^{m}}\mathbb{P}\left(|X|>b_{2^{m-1}}\right).\end{split}

By (
1.6
), we have

(2.11)

2^{m+1}\mathbb{P}\left(|X|>b_{2^{m-1}}\right)\to 0\text{ as }m\to\infty.

It follows from Karamata’s theorem (see, e.g.,
[
2
, p. 30]
) that

(2.12)

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha^{k}L\left(\beta^{k}\right)\leq C\alpha^{n}L(\beta^{n})\text{ for all }\alpha>1,\beta\geq 1.

By using (
2.11
), (
2.12
) and Toeplitz’s lemma, we have

(2.13)

\begin{split}&\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\sum_{m=1}^{n}b_{2^{m}}2^{m+1}\mathbb{P}\left(|X|>b_{2^{m-1}}\right)}{b_{2^{n}}}\\
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\sum_{m=1}^{n}2^{m/p}L(2^{m})2^{m+1}\mathbb{P}\left(|X|>b_{2^{m-1}}\right)}{2^{n/p}L(2^{n})}=0.\end{split}

Let
\varepsilon_{1}>0
be arbitrary, and let

a
and
b
be positive constants satisfying

1/2<a<1/p,\ a+b=1/p.

For
n\geq 1
,
0\leq m\leq n
, let

(2.14)

\lambda_{m,n}=\varepsilon_{1}2^{bm}2^{an}L(2^{n}).

An elementary calculation shows (see
[
15
, p. 1236]
)

(2.15)

\begin{split}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\lambda_{m,n}\leq\dfrac{2^{b}\varepsilon_{1}b_{2^{n}}}{2^{b}-1}:=C_{1}(b)\varepsilon_{1}b_{2^{n}}.\end{split}

By (
2.10
), (
2.13
), the proof of (
2.9
) is completed if we show that

(2.16)

I_{n}:=\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{n}\max_{0\leq k<2^{n-m}}\left|\sum_{i=k2^{m}+1}^{(k+1)2^{m}}Y_{i,m}\right|\geq C_{1}(b)\varepsilon_{1}b_{2^{n}}\right)\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty,

and

(2.17)

J_{n}:=\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{n}\max_{0\leq k<2^{n-m}}\left|\sum_{i=k2^{m}+1}^{k2^{m}+2^{m-1}}\left(X_{i,2^{m-1}}-\mathbb{E}(X_{i,2^{m-1}})\right)\right|\geq C_{1}(b)\varepsilon_{1}b_{2^{n}}\right)\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty.

We note that for each
m\geq 1
,
Y_{i,m},i\geq 1
are mean
0
and pairwise independent random variables.
Therefore

(2.18)

\begin{split}I_{n}&\leq\sum_{m=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{0\leq k<2^{n-m}}\left|\sum_{i=k2^{m}+1}^{(k+1)2^{m}}Y_{i,m}\right|\geq\lambda_{m,n}\right)\text{ (by \eqref{30})}\\
&\leq\sum_{m=1}^{n}\lambda_{m,n}^{-2}\mathbb{E}\left(\max_{0\leq k<2^{n-m}}\left|\sum_{i=k2^{m}+1}^{(k+1)2^{m}}Y_{i,m}\right|\right)^{2}\text{ (by Markov's inequality)}\\
&\leq\sum_{m=1}^{n}\lambda_{m,n}^{-2}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n-m}-1}\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{i=k2^{m}+1}^{(k+1)2^{m}}Y_{i,m}\right)^{2}\\
&=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\lambda_{m,n}^{-2}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n-m}-1}\sum_{i=k2^{m}+1}^{(k+1)2^{m}}\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{i,m}\right)^{2}\\
&\leq\sum_{m=1}^{n}\lambda_{m,n}^{-2}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n-m}-1}\sum_{i=k2^{m}+1}^{(k+1)2^{m}}\mathbb{E}\left(X_{i,2^{m}}-X_{i,2^{m-1}}\right)^{2}\\
&\leq\sum_{m=1}^{n}2^{n}\lambda_{m,n}^{-2}b_{2^{m}}^{2}\mathbb{P}(|X|>b_{2^{m-1}})\text{ (by the stochastic domination assumption)}\\
&=\varepsilon_{1}^{-2}\frac{1}{2^{n(2a-1)}L^{2}(2^{n})}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{n}2^{m(2a-1)}L^{2}(2^{m})2^{m}\mathbb{P}(|X|>b_{2^{m-1}})\right)\text{ (by \eqref{eq.main24})}\\
&\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty\text{ (by noting $2a-1>0$ and using \eqref{29}, \eqref{rem08}, and Toeplitz's lemma)}.\end{split}

Similarly,

(2.19)

\begin{split}J_{n}&\leq\sum_{m=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{0\leq k<2^{n-m}}\left|\sum_{i=k2^{m}+1}^{k2^{m}+2^{m-1}}\left(X_{i,2^{m-1}}-\mathbb{E}(X_{i,2^{m-1}})\right)\right|\geq\lambda_{m,n}\right)\\
&\leq\sum_{m=1}^{n}2^{n}\lambda_{m,n}^{-2}\left(\mathbb{E}X^{2}\mathbf{1}(|X|\leq b_{2^{m-1}})+b_{2^{m-1}}^{2}\mathbb{P}(|X|>b_{2^{m-1}})\right)\\
&=\sum_{m=1}^{n}2^{n}\lambda_{m,n}^{-2}\mathbb{E}X^{2}\mathbf{1}(|X|\leq b_{2^{m-1}})+o(1),\end{split}

where we have applied (
2.18
) in the final step. By using integration by parts, and proceeding in a similar manner as the last two lines of (
2.18
), we have

(2.20)

\begin{split}&\sum_{m=1}^{n}2^{n}\lambda_{m,n}^{-2}\mathbb{E}X^{2}\mathbf{1}(|X|\leq b_{2^{m-1}})\leq\sum_{m=1}^{n}2^{n}\lambda_{m,n}^{-2}\int_{0}^{b_{2^{m-1}}}2x\mathbb{P}(|X|>x)\operatorname{d}x\\
&\leq\varepsilon_{1}^{-2}2^{n(1-2a)}L^{-2}(2^{n})\sum_{m=1}^{n}\left(2^{-2mb}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{b_{2^{k-1}}}^{b_{2^{k}}}2x\mathbb{P}(|X|>x)\operatorname{d}x+2^{-2mb}\int_{0}^{b_{1}}2x\operatorname{d}x\right)\\
&\leq\varepsilon_{1}^{-2}2^{n(1-2a)}L^{-2}(2^{n})\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{m=k}^{n}2^{-2bm}\right)b_{2^{k}}^{2}\mathbb{P}\left(|X|>b_{2^{k-1}}\right)+\sum_{m=1}^{n}2^{-2mb}b_{1}^{2}\right)\\
&\leq\dfrac{C}{2^{n(2a-1)}L^{2}(2^{n})}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}2^{k(2a-1)}L^{2}(2^{k})2^{k}\mathbb{P}\left(|X|>b_{2^{k-1}}\right)+1\right)\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty.\end{split}

Combining (
2.18
)–(
2.20
), we obtain (
2.16
) and (
2.17
) thereby completing the proof of (
2.9
).

∎

3.
Concluding remarks

This paper establishes WLLNs for maximal partial sums of pairwise independent random variables
without using the Kolmogorov maximal inequality.
The method can be easily adapted to
dependent random variables. We have the following result:

Theorem 7
.

Let
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}

be a sequence of random variables and let
p
,
L(\cdot)
and
b_{n}
be as in Theorem
2
. Assume that there exists a constant
C
such that
for all nondecreasing functions
f_{i}
,
i\geq 1
we have

(3.1)

\operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_{i=k+1}^{k+\ell}f_{i}(X_{i})\right)\leq C\sum_{i=k+1}^{k+\ell}\operatorname{Var}(f_{i}(X_{i})),\ k\geq 0,\ell\geq 1,

provided the variances exist.
If
\{X_{n},\,n\geq 1\}
is stochastically dominated by a random variable
X
such that
(
1.6
) is satisfied, then we obtain WLLN (
1.7
).

Theorem
7
can be proved by assuming that
X_{n}\geq 0
,
n\geq 1
since we can use identity
X_{n}=X_{n}^{+}-X_{n}^{-}

in the general case. We then use truncation
X_{i,n}=X_{i}\mathbf{1}(X_{i}\leq b_{n})+b_{n}\mathbf{1}(X_{i}>b_{n}),\ n\geq 1,i\geq 1

(to ensure that the truncated sequence

\{X_{n,i},i\geq 1\}
satisfies
(
3.1
)), and modify
the arguments given in
the proofs of Lemma
6
and Theorem
2

accordingly. The details are straightforward and, hence, are omitted.

Many dependence structures satisfy (
3.1
), including
m
-pairwise negative dependence, extended negative dependence,
\varphi
-mixing, etc (see, e.g.,
[
7
,
11
]
).

It is obvious that (
1.7
) implies (
1.1
). For the i.i.d case and
1\leq p<2
, Kruglov
[
10
, Theorem 2]
proved that
(
1.7
) and (
1.1
) are equivalent. It is an open problem as to whether or not this also holds for the pairwise i.i.d. case.

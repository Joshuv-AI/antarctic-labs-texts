M. Farber and J. Oprea

Abstract
. Parametrized motion planning algorithms [ 3 ] have a high degree of universality and flexibility; they generate the motion of a robotic system under a variety of external conditions. The latter are viewed as parameters and constitute part of the input of the algorithm. The concept of sequential parametrized topological complexity 𝖳𝖢 r [ p : E → B ] {\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B] is a measure of the complexity of such algorithms. It was studied in [ 3 , 4 ] for r = 2 r=2 and in [ 10 ] for r ≥ 2 r\geq 2 . In this paper we analyse the dependence of the complexity 𝖳𝖢 r [ p : E → B ] {\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B] on an initial bundle with structure group G G and on its fibre X X viewed as a G G -space. Our main results estimate 𝖳𝖢 r [ p : E → B ] {\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B] in terms of certain invariants of the bundle and the action on the fibre. Moreover, we also obtain estimates depending on the base and the fibre. Finally, we develop a calculus of sectional categories featuring a new invariant 𝗌𝖾𝖼𝖺𝗍 f [ p : E → B ] {\sf secat}_{f}[p:E\to B] which plays an important role in the study of sectional category of towers of fibrations.

1.
Introduction

Motion planning algorithms of robotics control autonomous robots in engineering, see
[
17
]
.
A motion planning algorithm takes as input the initial and the desired states of the system and produces as output a motion of the system starting at the initial states and ending at the desired states.
A robot is “told” where it needs to go and the execution of this task, including selection of a specific route of motion, is made by the robot itself, i.e. by the robot’s motion planning algorithm.
Typically it is understood that the external conditions (such as the positions of the obstacles and the geometry of the enclosing domain) are known and are constant during the motion.

In papers
[
3
,
4
]
, motion planning algorithms of a new type were analyzed.
These are
parametrized motion planning algorithms
, which, besides the initial and desired states, take as input the parameters characterising the external conditions. The output of a parametrized motion planning algorithm is a continuous motion of the system from the initial to the desired state, respecting the given external conditions. The papers
[
3
,
4
]
laid out the new formalism and analysed in full detail the problem of moving an arbitrary number
n
of robots in the domain with
m
a priori unknown obstacles.

The recent paper
[
10
]
developed a generalisation where the robot must perform
a sequence
of tasks. The topological complexity of such an algorithm is called
sequential parametrized topological complexity

{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]
, where
r=2,3,\dots
, and the case
r=2
corresponds to the situations analysed in
[
3
,
4
]
.
Formally,
{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]
is an integer associated with a fibration
p:E\to B
where the points of the base
b\in B
parametrize the external conditions (for example, positions of the obstacles) and for each
b\in B
the fibre
X_{b}=p^{-1}(b)\subset E
is the space of all admissible configurations of the system under the external conditions
b
. To make the present work independent, we include the definition of the concept
{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]
and its major properties in §
2
.

In this paper we further analyse the invariant
{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]
trying to understand its dependence on classical invariants of the initial bundle
p:E\to B
: in particular, on its base
B
and on its fibre
X
. As with all such invariants, exact calculation is generally hard and the development of lower and upper bounds is an essential part of the subject. This is the focus of our work.

We first show that, if the bundle
p:E\to B
has structure group
G
with fibre
X
a
G
-space, then the equivariant sequential topological complexity of
X
(developed in
[
5
,
1
]
) serves as an upper bound for
{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]
(see Theorem
9
). The case when
G
acts freely on
X
is especially interesting and leads to several somewhat surprising estimates. But
using equivariant topological complexity as an upper bound is fraught with danger since it can be infinite in what appear to be innocuous situations.

As an alternative we develop the notion of
weak sequential equivariant complexity
, denoted by
{\sf TC}^{w}_{r,G}(X)
, and its variant

{\sf TC}^{w}_{r,G}(X;P)
which we are tempted (but loathe) to call
weak sequential equivariant complexity with coefficients

P
(see §
7
).
We will give several examples showing that these invariants are finite even when equivariant topological complexity is infinite, so they offer the opportunity
for effective estimation in many situations.
Indeed, our main result Theorem
8.1
gives lower and upper bounds for
{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]
in terms of these invariants. To state it one needs to recall the
invariant

G\mbox{-}{\sf cat}[p:E\to B]

introduced by I. M. James (
[
16
]
, p. 342). It is defined as the smallest integer
k\geq 0
such that the base
B
admits an open cover
B=U_{0}\cup U_{1}\cup\dots\cup U_{k}
with the property that over each set
U_{i}
the bundle
E
is trivial
as a
G
-bundle
. Clearly,
G\mbox{-}{\sf cat}[p:E\to B]
equals the sectional category
{\sf secat}[\tau:P\to B]
of the associated principal bundle
that constructs
p\colon E\to B
.
In general,

(1)

\displaystyle G\mbox{-}{\sf cat}[p:E\to B]\,\leq\,{\sf cat}(B)\,\leq\dim B

and
if the group
G
is
2
-connected (as is the case for simply connected compact Lie groups for instance)
then we can say

(2)

\displaystyle G\mbox{-}{\sf cat}[p:E\to B]\,\leq\,\left\lceil\frac{\dim B-3}{4}\right\rceil

as follows by applying Theorem 5 from
[
24
]
to
{\sf secat}[\tau:P\to B]
.
If the structure group
G
is discrete then instead of (
1
) one has a stronger inequality

(3)

\displaystyle G\mbox{-}{\sf cat}[p:E\to B]\,\leq\,{\sf cat}_{1}(B),

where
{\sf cat}_{1}(B)
is the sectional category of the universal cover
\tilde{B}\to B
.
Our main result (Theorem
8.1
) then is the following.

Theorem
.

For a locally trivial bundle
p:E=X\times_{G}P\to B=P/G
one has the inequalities

(4)

\displaystyle{\sf TC}_{r,G}^{w}(X;P)\,\leq\,{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]\,\leq\,G\mbox{-}{\sf cat}[p:E\to B]\,+\,{\sf TC}_{r,G}^{w}(X).

Note that the first summand in the right hand side (RHS) of (
4
) is independent of
r
and is bounded above by the Lusternik - Schnirelmann category of the base
B
; the second term is the weak equivariant sequential topological complexity of the fibre
X
. In our view, this estimate gets at the heart of the matter. After all, what
is
a bundle? It is just a principal bundle together with
an action of the structure group on the fibre and our upper bound is expressed exactly in numerical quantities derived from these objects.
In Example
8.4
we shall see that the RHS can be
an equality, so at least in some cases the upper bound can be sharp. Such
a posteriori
knowledge then warrants a deeper study of the invariants
{\sf TC}_{r,G}^{w}(X)
and

{\sf TC}_{r,G}^{w}(X;P)
and we hope the present work elicits this.

Beyond defining and applying the new invariants
{\sf TC}_{r,G}^{w}(X)
and
{\sf TC}_{r,G}^{w}(X;P)
, in sections §§
4
,
5
,
6

we develop a calculus of sectional categories, including a new notion denoted by
{\sf secat}_{f}[p:E\to B]
where
f:B\to C
is a continuous map.
It is this notion that allows us to estimate the sectional category of towers of fibrations which serves as the crucial technical tool in the proof of our main results.
We believe that
{\sf secat}_{f}[p:E\to B]
holds independent interest and should find application in many situations orbiting the twin galaxies of
Lusternik-Schnirelmann category and topological complexity.

2.
The concept of sequential parametrized topological complexity

In this section we recall the notion of sequential parametrized topological complexity introduced in
[
10
]
. It is a generalisation of the concept of topological complexity
[
9
]
and its parametrized version
[
3
]
.

Let
p:E\to B
be a Hurewicz fibration with fibre
X
. Fix an integer
r\geq 2
and denote

E^{r}_{B}=\{(e_{1},\cdots,e_{r})\in E^{r};\,p(e_{1})=\cdots=p(e_{r})\}.

The symbol
I=[0,1]
denotes the unit interval. Let
E^{I}_{B}\subset E^{I}
be
the space of all paths
\gamma:I\to E
such that the path
p\circ\gamma:I\to B
is constant. Fix
r
points

0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\dots<t_{r}\leq 1

and
consider the evaluation map

(5)

\displaystyle\Pi_{r}:E^{I}_{B}\to E^{r}_{B},\quad\Pi_{r}(\gamma)=(\gamma(t_{1}),\gamma(t_{2}),\dots,\gamma(t_{r})).

\Pi_{r}
is a Hurewicz fibration, see
[
4
, Appendix]
. The fibre of
\Pi_{r}
is
(\Omega X)^{r-1}
, the Cartesian
(r-1)
-th power of the based loop space
\Omega X
.
A section
s:E^{r}_{B}\to E^{I}_{B}
of the fibration
\Pi_{r}
can be interpreted as
a parametrized motion planning algorithm
, i.e.
a function which assigns to every sequence of points
(e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{r})\in E^{r}_{B}
a continuous path
\gamma:I\to E
(representing motion of the system) satisfying
\gamma(t_{i})=e_{i}
for every
i=1,2,\dots,r
and such that the path

p\circ\gamma:I\to B
is constant. The latter condition means that the system moves under
constant external conditions

(such as positions of the obstacles etc).

Typically, the fibration
\Pi_{r}
does not admit continuous sections, see Corollary 1 and Lemma 1 in
[
12
]
which deal with the case
r=2
; when
r>2
the arguments are similar. Therefore the
motion planning algorithms are necessarily discontinuous in most situations.

The following definition
[
10
]
gives a measure of complexity of sequential parametrized motion planning algorithms.

Definition 2.1
.

The
r
-th sequential parametrized topological complexity
of the fibration
p:E\to B
, denoted
{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]
, is defined as the sectional category of the fibration
\Pi_{r}
, i.e.

(6)

\displaystyle{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]:={\sf secat}(\Pi_{r}).

In more detail,
{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]
is the minimal integer
k
such that there is an open cover

\{U_{0},U_{1},\dots,U_{k}\}
of
E^{r}_{B}
with the property that each open set
U_{i}

admits a continuous section
s_{i}:U_{i}\to E^{I}_{B}
of
\Pi_{r}
.

Under some mild assumptions instead of open covers one can consider totally general partitions:

Proposition 2.2

(see
[
10
]
, Proposition 3.6)
.

Let
E
and
B
be metrizable separable ANRs and let
p:E\to B
be a locally trivial fibration. Then the sequential parametrized topological complexity
{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]
equals the smallest integer
n
such that
E_{B}^{r}
admits a partition

E_{B}^{r}=F_{0}\sqcup F_{1}\sqcup...\sqcup F_{n},\quad F_{i}\cap F_{j}=\emptyset\text{ \ for }i\neq j,

with the property that on each set
F_{i}
there exists a continuous section
s_{i}:F_{i}\to E_{B}^{I}
of
\Pi_{r}
.

If two fibrations
p:E\to B
and
p^{\prime}:E^{\prime}\to B
are fibrewise homotopy equivalent then

{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]={\sf TC}_{r}[p^{\prime}:E^{\prime}\to B],

see
[
10
]
, Corollary 4.2.

The following upper bound is a reformulation of Proposition 6.1 from
[
10
]
:

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
p:E\to B
be a locally trivial fibration with fiber
X
, where
E,B,X
are CW-complexes. Assume that the fiber
X
is
k
-connected, where
k\geq 0
. Then

(7)

\displaystyle{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]\leq\left\lceil\frac{r\dim X+\dim B-k}{1+k}\right\rceil.

We refer the reader to
[
10
]
for proofs and further detail.

3.
Relation with the equivariant sequential topological complexity

In this section we show that
{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]
admits as an upper bound the sequential equivariant topological complexity
[
5
,
1
]
of the fibre
X
. This leads to simple estimates in terms of the dimension of the fibre in the case when the structure group
G
of the fibration acts freely on
X
, see Lemma
3.5
and Corollary
3.6
.

3.1.
Equivariant topological complexity

We shall recall a sequential analogue of the notion of equivariant topological complexity introduced by M. Bayeh and S. Sarkar
[
1
]
; it generalizes the concept of equivariant topological complexity originally introduced and studied by H. Colman and M. Grant
[
5
]
.

3.2.

Let
G
be a topological group acting on a topological space
X
from the left. The papers
[
5
,
1
]

require
G
to be compact but we do not impose this assumption at this stage.

The symbol
X^{I}
denotes the space of all continuous paths
\gamma:I\to X
where
I=[0,1]
equipped with the compact-open topology. The group
G
acts naturally on
X^{I}
, where
(g\gamma)(t)=g\gamma(t)
, for
t\in I
.

For an integer
r\geq 2
and consider the Cartesian power
X^{r}=X\times X\times\dots\times X
(
r

G
on
X^{r}
.

Fix
r
points
0=t_{1}<t_{2}<\dots<t_{r}=1
in the unit interval
I=[0,1]
and consider the evaluation map

(8)

\displaystyle\rho_{r}:X^{I}\to X^{r},

where
\rho_{r}(\gamma)=(\gamma(t_{1}),\dots,\gamma(t_{r}))
.
Clearly,
\rho_{r}
a
G
-equivariant map.

Definition 3.1
.

For a path-connected
G
-space
X
, we denote by
{\sf TC}_{r,G}(X)
the smallest integer
k\geq 0
such that the Cartesian power
X^{r}=X\times X\times\dots\times X
(

X^{r}=U_{0}\cup U_{1}\cup\dots\cup U_{k}
with the following properties: each set
U_{i}
is
G
-invariant and admits a continuous

-equivariant section
s_{i}:U_{i}\to X^{I}
of the fibration
\rho_{r}
. If no such cover exists we set

{\sf TC}_{r,G}(X)=\infty.

The invariant
{\sf TC}_{2,G}(X)
coincides with the equivariant topological complexity
{\sf TC}_{G}(X)
of Colman and Grant
[
5
]
.

It is obvious from Definition
3.1
that

{\sf TC}_{r}(X)\leq{\sf TC}_{r,G}(X),

where
{\sf TC}_{r}(X)
is the sequential topological complexity of

introduced by Rudyak

[

]
.

An alternative definition of
{\sf TC}_{r,G}(X)
is obtained as follows (compare
[
10
]
, Lemma 3.5).
Let
K
be a path-connected locally compact metrizable space and let
k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}\in K
be a set of
r
pairwise distinct points.
Consider the set
X^{K}
of continuous maps
\alpha:K\to X
equipped with compact-open topology. The evaluation map

\rho^{K}_{r}:X^{K}\to X^{r},

where
\Pi^{K}_{r}(\alpha)=(\alpha(k_{1}),\dots,\alpha(k_{r}))\in X^{r}
, is continuous and
G
-equivariant,
where we view
X^{r}
with the diagonal action of
G
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any path-connected locally compact metrizable space
K
,
the number
{\sf TC}_{r,G}(X)
equals the smallest integer
k\geq 0
such that the Cartesian power
X^{r}

admits an open cover

X^{r}=U_{0}\cup U_{1}\cup\dots\cup U_{k}
with the following properties: each set
U_{i}
is
G
-invariant and admits a continuous
G
-equivariant section
s_{i}:U_{i}\to X^{K}
of
\rho^{K}_{r}
.

Proof.

Consider the commutative diagram

\textstyle{X^{I}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\rho_{r}}
\textstyle{X^{K}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F}
\scriptstyle{\rho^{K}_{r}}
\textstyle{X^{r}}

where the maps
F:X^{K}\to X^{I}
and
F^{\prime}:X^{I}\to X^{K}
are defined as follows. Fix a path
\gamma:I\to K
satisfying
\gamma(t_{i})=k_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,r
. Then
F(\alpha)=\alpha\circ\gamma:I\to X
, where
\alpha\in X^{K}
.

To define the map
F^{\prime}:X^{I}\to X^{K}
we first construct a continuous function
f:K\to I

for all

. Applying Tietze extension theorem we find continuous functions

. Then the function

, has the required properties. The map

is defined by

.

Clearly the maps

and

are

-invariant subset

any
G

G
-equivariant section

of
\rho^{K}_{r}
. And vice versa, any

G
-equivariant section
s^{\prime}:U\to X^{K}

.

This completes the proof.
∎

Yet another equivalent characterization of
{\sf TC}_{r,G}(X)
is given by the following (see
[
1
]
):

Lemma 3.3
.

For a
G
-space
X
and
r\geq 2
the integer
{\sf TC}_{r,G}(X)
equals the smallest
k\geq 0
such that
X^{r}
admits an open cover
X^{r}=U_{0}\cup U_{1}\cup\dots\cup U_{k}
by
G
-invariant open sets

with the property that each inclusion
U_{j}\to X^{r}
is

.

Now we can state our result relating the sequential parametrised topological complexity of a fibration with the equaivariant sequential topological complexity of the fibre:

Theorem 3.4
.

Consider a locally trivial bundle
p:E\to B
with path-connected fibre
X
and structure group
G
. Let

\tau:P\to B
be a
G
-principal bundle such that
p:E\to B
coincides with the associated bundle
p:E=X\times_{G}P=(X\times P)/G\to P/G=B
. Then the sequential parametrized topological complexity
{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]
is bounded above by
{\sf TC}_{r,G}(X)
, i.e.

(9)

\displaystyle{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]\,\leq\,{\sf TC}_{r,G}(X).

Note that the RHS of inequality (
9
) depends only on the fibre
X
viewed as a
G
-space where

G
is the structure group of the bundle.

Proof.

First we note that there exists the commutative diagram

\textstyle{X^{I}\times_{G}P\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{}\hskip 14.22636pt\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\rho_{r}\times_{G}1}
\textstyle{E^{I}_{B}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Pi_{r}}
\textstyle{X^{r}\times_{G}P\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\hskip 11.38092pt\beta}
\textstyle{E^{r}_{B}}

where
\alpha
and

are homeomorphisms. Therefore,

{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]={\sf secat}[\Pi_{r}:E^{I}_{B}\to E^{r}_{B}]={\sf secat}[\rho_{r}\times_{G}1:X^{I}\times_{G}P\to X^{r}\times_{G}P].

For

let

X^{r}=U_{0}\cup U_{1}\cup\dots\cup U_{k}
be an open cover as in Definition
3.1

Consider the sets

W_{i}=(U_{i}\times P)/G\subset(X^{r}\times P)/G.

They are open and cover

s_{i}:U_{i}\to X^{I}
of the fibration

obviously defines the section

{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]={\sf secat}[\rho_{r}\times_{G}1:X^{I}\times_{G}P\to X^{r}\times_{G}P]\leq k.

[

Lemma 3.5
.

Consider a locally trivial bundle

Assume that the group

{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]\,\leq\,{\sf TC}_{r,G}(X)\leq{\sf cat}(X^{r}/G)\leq\dim(X^{r}/G).

In view of Theorem

{\sf TC}_{r,G}(X)\leq{\sf cat}(X^{r}/G)

.
Applying Lemma

.

Corollary 3.6
.

Consider a locally trivial bundle
p:E\to B
with fibre
X
(which is a path-connected separable metric space) and a compact Lie group
G
acting freely on
X
, as the structure group. Then for any
r\geq 2
one has

(10)

\displaystyle{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]\,\leq\,{\sf cat}(X^{r}/G)\,\leq\,r\dim X-\dim G.

Proof.

First we note that due to the theorem of Palais (
[
2
]
, Theorem II.7.3) the assumptions of Lemma
3.5
are satisfied. We are only left to note that
\dim X^{r}/G=\dim X^{r}-\dim G\leq r\dim X-\dim G
, see
[
22
]
, Corollary 1.7.32.
∎

One can use Lemma
3.5
to
give an alternative proof of Proposition 3.3
from
[
10
]
(see also
[
3
]
, Proposition 4.3) with some minor additional assumptions:

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
G\to P\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\to}}B
be a principal bundle, where
G
is a path-connected topological group which has the topology of a separable metric space. Then

{\sf TC}_{r}[\tau\colon P\to B]={\sf cat}(G^{r-1})\quad\mbox{for any}\quad r\geq 2.

Proof.

By §3 of
[
10
]
we know that

{\sf TC}_{r}[\tau\colon P\to B]\geq{\sf TC}_{r}(G)={\sf cat}(G^{r-1})
.
We view the fibre
G
as acting on itself by left translations and acting diagonally on
G^{r}
.
The quotient map
q_{r}:G^{r}\to G^{r}/G
admits a section

s:G^{r}/G\to G^{r}
, given by
s(g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{r})=(e,g_{1}^{-1}g_{2},g_{1}^{-1}g_{3},\dots g_{1}^{-1}g_{r})
. Therefore,
we explicitly obtain a
G
-homeomorphism
G^{r}\cong G^{r}/G\times G
so that

q_{r}
is a trivial bundle

and, as such, has the

-HLP.
Lemma
3.5
then applies and gives the upper bound

{\sf TC}_{r}[\tau\colon P\to B]\leq{\sf cat}(G^{r}/G)={\sf cat}(G^{r-1})
. Comparing, we see that both
bounds are in fact equalities.
∎

4.
Calculus of sectional categories

In this section we introduce a new invariant
{\sf secat}_{f}[p:E\to B]
which generalises the concept of sectional category of a fibration.
This invariant plays a role in estimating sectional category of towers of fibrations, see Theorem
5.1
.

4.1.

Let
p:E\to B
be a fibration and let
f:B\to C
be a continuous map.

Definition 4.1
.

We define the invariant

{\sf secat}_{f}[p:E\to B]

to be the smallest integer
k\geq 0
such that
C
admits a family of open subsets
U_{0},U_{1},\dots,U_{k}
with the following properties:

(a)
U_{0}\cup U_{1}\cup\dots\cup U_{k}\supset f(B)
or, equivalently,
B=\cup_{i=1}^{k}f^{-1}(U_{i})
;

(b) the fibration
p:E\to B
admits a continuous section over each open set
f^{-1}(U_{i})
, where
i=0,1,\dots,k
.

We set

{\sf secat}_{f}[p:E\to B]=\infty
if no such family exists.

Open sets of the form
f^{-1}(U)\subset B
where
U\subset C
, can be called
f
-saturated
. Definition
4.1
can be rephrased as
dealing with covers of the base
B
by
f
-saturated open sets admitting continuous section of the fibration
p:E\to B
.

4.2.
Finiteness

The following Lemma summarises information about finiteness of the invariant
{\sf secat}_{f}[p:E\to B]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
p:E\to B
be a fibration and let
f:B\to C
be a continuous map.

(A)

If for some
x\in f(B)\subset C
one has
{\sf secat}[p:p^{-1}f^{-1}(x)\to f^{-1}(x)]>0
then
{\sf secat}_{f}[p:E\to B]=\infty
.

(B)

If
B
is compact and every point
x\in f(B)\subset C
has an open neighbourhood
U\subset C
such that

{\sf secat}[p:p^{-1}f^{-1}(U)\to f^{-1}(U)]=0
then
{\sf secat}_{f}[p:E\to B]<\infty
.

Proof.

Under the assumption (A) there is no open set
U\subset C
containing
x
with
f^{-1}(U)
having a continuous section. The statement (B) is obvious.
∎

In our applications we shall typically have the map
f:B\to C
surjective, and more specifically, it will often be the quotient map with respect to a group action. However, it is convenient to make no additional assumptions at this stage.

4.3.
Dependence on
f

In the special case when the map
f:B\to C=B
is the identity map, the number
{\sf secat}_{f}[p:E\to B]
turns into the usual sectional category
{\sf secat}[p:E\to B]
. In general, obviously,

(11)

\displaystyle{\sf secat}[p:E\to B]\leq{\sf secat}_{f}[p:E\to B]

and

(12)

\displaystyle{\sf secat}[p:E\to B]={\sf secat}_{f}[p:E\to B]

assuming that
{\sf secat}[p:E\to B]=0
.

Moreover, for
B\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}C\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\to}}C^{\prime}
one clearly has

(13)

\displaystyle{\sf secat}_{f}[p:E\to B]\leq{\sf secat}_{gf}[p:E\to B].

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
p:E\to B
be a fibration and let
f:B\to C
and
f^{\prime}:B\to C^{\prime}
be two continuous maps. (a) If there is a continuous map

h:C\to C^{\prime}
such that
f^{\prime}=h\circ f
then

{\sf secat}_{f}[p:E\to B]\leq{\sf secat}_{f^{\prime}}[p:E\to B].

Moreover, (b) If
h:C\to C^{\prime}
as above is such that its restriction induces a homeomorphism
f(B)\to f^{\prime}(B)
then

{\sf secat}_{f}[p:E\to B]={\sf secat}_{f^{\prime}}[p:E\to B].

Proof.

The statement (a) follows from inequality (
13
). To prove (b) assume that
U\subset C
is an open subset with the property that
f^{-1}(U)
admits a section of
p
. Then

h(U\cap f(B))=U^{\prime}\subset f^{\prime}(B)

is an open subset of
f^{\prime}(B)
and hence there exists an open subset
V\subset C^{\prime}
with
V\cap f^{\prime}(B)=U^{\prime}
. Then,
f^{\prime-1}(V)=f^{-1}(U)
admits a section of
p
.
Thus any family of open sets

U_{0}\cup U_{1}\cup\dots\cup U_{k}\supset f(B)
such that
f^{-1}(U_{i})
admits a section of
p
determines a family of open subsets of the same cardinality,
V_{0}\cup V_{1}\cup\dots\cup V_{k}\supset f^{\prime}(B)
with the preimages
f^{\prime-1}(V_{j})
admitting sections of
p
. This shows the inverse inequality

{\sf secat}_{f}[p:E\to B]\geq{\sf secat}_{f^{\prime}}[p:E\to B]
.
∎

4.5.
Homotopical dimension

For a topological space

having the homotopy type of a finite-dimensional CW-complex we shall denote by

The following Lemma will be used later in our paper.

.

Consider a locally trivial bundle

Let

4.6.

The following statement gives a useful upper bound for the invariant

Remark 4.11
.

In
[
11
]
an upper bound for topological complexity was derived that made use of an
invariant which was called

\widetilde{{\sf TC}}(X)=\widetilde{{\sf secat}}(E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\to}}\bar{X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle q}}{{\to}}X)

there, but which we recognize in fact to be
{\sf secat}_{q}[p:E\to\bar{X}]
here. In
[
11
]
it was further
shown that
\widetilde{{\sf TC}}(X)
could be identified with the the notion of strongly invariant
topological complexity

introduced by A. Dranishnikov
[
8
]
earlier.
In
[
21
]
, A. K. Paul and D. Sen extended both the invariant

and the strongly invariant topological complexity to the realm of sequential topological complexity
and proved the analogous identification.
This identification, in some sense, was the genesis of our calculus of sectional categories and
together with Theorem

begs the question of exactly how parametrized topological
complexity and various forms of equivariant topological complexity are intertwined, especially
in the case of locally trivial fibre bundles.

5.
Sectional category of towers of fibrations

5.1.

Consider a tower of fibrations

E_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p_{r}}}{{\to}}E_{r-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p_{r-1}}}{{\to}}E_{r-2}\to\dots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p_{1}}}{{\to}}E_{0}

and the total fibration

p=p_{1}p_{2}\dots p_{r}:E_{r}\to E_{0}.

We shall assume that all spaces
E_{i}
are normal.

Theorem 5.1
.

The sectional category
{\sf secat}[p:E_{r}\to E_{0}]
of the total fibration admits the following lower and upper bounds:

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt{\sf secat}[p_{1}:E_{1}\to E_{0}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\sf secat}[p:E_{r}\to E_{0}]\,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,{\sf secat}[p_{1}:E_{1}\to E_{0}]+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}{\sf secat}_{(p_{1}p_{2}\dots p_{i})}[p_{i+1}:E_{i+1}\to E_{i}].

Here
p_{1}p_{2}\dots p_{i}:E_{i}\to E_{0}
denotes the composition.

Lemma
5.2
below will be used in the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
C
be a normal space. Consider properties
A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{r}
of open subsets of
C
, such that each property
A_{i}
is inherited by open subsets and disjoint unions. Assume that for each
i=1,2,\dots,r

C
admits an open cover consisting of

n_{i}+1
open sets satisfying the property
A_{i}
. Then
C
admits an open cover consisting of
N+1
open sets, where
N=\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}
, satisfying all the properties
A_{1},\dots,A_{r}
.

Proof.

For
r=2
this statement was proven in
[
19
]
as Lemma 4.3. The case

follows from this by induction.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Since the left inequality in (
5.1
) is obvious we shall concentrate on the right one and use
Lemma
5.2
to prove it. Consider the following properties
A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{r}
of open subsets of
E_{0}
. We shall say that an open subset
U\subset E_{0}
satisfies
A_{1}
if
U
has a continuous section of the fibration
p_{1}
.
For
2\leq i\leq r
, we shall say that an open subset
U\subset E_{0}
satisfies the property
A_{i}
if the open set

p_{i-1}^{-1}\dots p_{2}^{-1}p_{1}^{-1}(U)\subset E_{i-1}
admits a continuous section of
p_{i}
. By definition, for any
i=1,2,\dots r
, the set

admits an open cover of cardinality
{\sf secat}_{(p_{1}p_{2}\dots p_{i-1})}[p_{i}:E_{i}\to E_{i-1}]+1

with each set satisfying

. Applying Lemma

, we obtain that

admits an open cover

of cardinality

such that each set

satisfies all the properties

.
This means that there exists a continuous section
s_{0}:U_{j}\to E_{1}
of

and

of the fibration

This gives the inequality (

∎

Lemma

distills the main results of

, but for the

An open cover

of a space

if every subcollection

\{W_{j_{0}},W_{j_{1}},\ldots,W_{j_{m}}\}

from

also covers

.
The following simple observation (see
[

]
for instance)
is the basis for many arguments in this approach.

Lemma 6.1
.

A cover
{\mathcal{W}}=\{W_{0},W_{1},\ldots,W_{k+m}\}
is an
(m+1)
-cover of
C

if and only if each
x\in C
is contained in at least
k+1
sets of

{\mathcal{W}}
.

An open cover can be lengthened to a
(k+1)
-cover,
while retaining certain essential properties of the sets in the cover.

Theorem 6.2

(
[
20
,
6
]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{U}}=\{U_{0},\ldots,U_{k}\}
be an open cover of a normal space
C
.
Then, for any
m=k,k+1,\ldots,\infty
, there is an open
(k+1)
-cover
of
C
,
\{U_{0},\ldots,U_{m}\}
, extending
{\mathcal{U}}
such that for
n>k
,

U_{n}
is a disjoint union of open sets that are subsets of the

U_{j}
,
0\leq j\leq k
.

We use these facts to obtain inequalities for product fibrations.

Lemma 6.3

(Product Inequality, I)
.

Let
p:E\to B
and
p^{\prime}:E^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}
be fibrations and let
f:B\to C
and
f^{\prime}:B^{\prime}\to C^{\prime}

be continuous maps. Assume that the spaces
f(B)
and
f^{\prime}(B^{\prime})
with topology induced from
C
and
C^{\prime}
respectively
are normal. Then the sectional category of the product fibration

{\sf secat}_{f\times f^{\prime}}[p\times p^{\prime}:E\times E^{\prime}\to B\times B^{\prime}]

is bounded above by the sum

{\sf secat}_{f}[p:E\to B]+{\sf secat}_{f^{\prime}}[p^{\prime}:E^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}]

and it is bounded below by

\max\{{\sf secat}_{f}[p:E\to B],\,{\sf secat}_{f^{\prime}}[p^{\prime}:E^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}]\}.

Proof.

First we deal with the lower bounds. Fix a point
b^{\prime}_{0}\in B^{\prime}
and embed
B
into
B\times B^{\prime}
via

b\mapsto(b,b^{\prime}_{0})
; also, embed
C
into
C\times C^{\prime}
via
x\mapsto(x,x_{0}^{\prime})
where
x^{\prime}_{0}=f^{\prime}(b^{\prime}_{0})
.
For an open subset
U\subset C\times C^{\prime}
, a section of
p\times p^{\prime}
over
(f\times f^{\prime})^{-1}(U)\subset B\times B^{\prime}

determines obviously a section of
p
over
f^{-1}(U\cap(C\times x^{\prime}_{0}))
. This implies the inequality

{\sf secat}_{f\times f^{\prime}}[p\times p^{\prime}:E\times E^{\prime}\to B\times B^{\prime}]\geq{\sf secat}_{f}[p:E\to B].
Similarly, one obtains

{\sf secat}_{f\times f^{\prime}}[p\times p^{\prime}:E\times E^{\prime}\to B\times B^{\prime}]\geq{\sf secat}_{f^{\prime}}[p^{\prime}:E^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}].

Now we prove the upper bound.
Let
{\sf secat}_{f}[p:E\to B]=k
be realized by open sets
U_{0},\ldots,U_{k}\subset C
covering
f(B)\subset C
,
with continuous sections

of

, and let
{\sf secat}_{f^{\prime}}[p^{\prime}:E^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}]=m

be realized by open sets

covering
f^{\prime}(B^{\prime})
,
with sections

s^{\prime}_{j}\colon f^{\prime-1}(V_{j})\to E^{\prime}
of
p^{\prime}
.
By Theorem
6.2
we can extend the family
U_{0},\ldots,U_{k}
to a family of open subsets

U_{0},\ldots,U_{k+m}
of
C
such that any
k+1
members of this family cover
f(B)
. Similarly,
we can find a family
V_{0},\ldots,V_{k+m}
of open subsets of
C^{\prime}
extending the initial family

V_{0},\dots,V_{m}

such that
any
m+1
members of this extended family cover
f^{\prime}(B^{\prime})
.
Theorem
6.2
guarantees that every set of the form
f^{-1}(U_{j})
or
f^{\prime-1}(V_{j})
admits a continuous section of
p
or
p^{\prime}
respectively, where
j=0,1,\dots,k+m
.

Denotting
W_{j}=U_{j}\times V_{j}
, where

j=0,\ldots,k+m
, we see that each set

(f\times f^{\prime})^{-1}(W_{j})=f^{-1}(U_{j})\times f^{\prime-1}(V_{j})
admits a continuous section of
p\times p^{\prime}
.
We show below
that the sets
W_{j}
cover
f(B)\times f^{\prime}(B^{\prime})
which implies that
{\sf secat}_{f\times f^{\prime}}[p\times p^{\prime}:E\times E^{\prime}\to B\times B^{\prime}]\leq k+m
.

Suppose that a point
(x,y)\in f(B)\times f^{\prime}(B^{\prime})
is not in any of the sets
W_{j}
, where
j=0,\ldots,k+m
.
Since any
k+1

cover

, we know that

belongs to at least
m+1
of the

, by Lemma

.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that

, in view of our assumption.
Therefore,
y
can only lie in the sets

V_{m+1},\ldots,V_{k+m}
which is a contradiction since
y
belongs to at least
k+1
of the
sets
V_{j}
, by Lemma
6.1
.
∎

r
for
n
even and
r-1
for
n
odd), we obtain that for any even
n

(27)

\displaystyle{\sf TC}_{r,G}^{w}(S^{n})=r.

For
n
odd our inequalities imply that
{\sf TC}_{r,G}^{w}(S^{n})
equals either
r-1
or
r
.

Example 7.9
.

Let
S^{1}
act on
S^{2}
by
rotations about the
z
-axis. The fixed point set of the action
is the disconnected set
\{N,S\}
, where
N
and
S
are the North and South Poles
respectively, so the equivariant topological complexity is infinite,
{\sf TC}_{r,S^{1}}(S^{2})=\infty
for all
r\geq 2
.

Let us now examine the weak equivariant topological complexity
{\sf TC}_{2,S^{1}}^{w}(S^{2})
. Fix an orbit
O\subset S^{2}
given by the equator and
fix an orientation of
O
.
Consider the open cover
S^{2}\times S^{2}=U_{0}\cup U_{1}\cup U_{2}

where

\displaystyle U_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{(x,y)\,|\,x\not=-y\},

\displaystyle U_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{(x,y)\,|\,x\not=y\}-\{(N,S),(S,N)\},

\displaystyle U_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{(x,y)\,|\,x\not\in O\,\mbox{and}\,y\not\in O\}.

Clearly, the sets
U_{0},U_{1},U_{2}
are
S^{1}
-invariant. We may define the motion planning rules over each of the sets
U_{i}
as follows.
For
(x,y)\in U_{0}
, go from
x
to
y
along the shortest geodesic arc. For
(x,y)\in U_{1}
the point
x
moves along the shortest geodesic arc first to the closest point of
O
, then along
O
in the positive direction to the point closest to
y
, and finally to
y
. For
(x,y)\in U_{2}
the point
x
moves along the shortest geodesic arc to the closest Pole (
N
or
S
), then to the closest Pole to
y
along a fixed path and then to
y
; the first and the third portions are along the shortest geodesic arc on the sphere
S^{2}
. Hence
{\sf TC}_{2,S^{1}}^{w}(S^{2})\leq 2
.
Since
2={\sf TC}(S^{2})\leq{\sf TC}_{2,S^{1}}^{w}(S^{2})
, we see that
{\sf TC}_{2,S^{1}}^{w}(S^{2})=2
.

8.
Bounds for the sequential parametrized topological complexity

Finally we are in position to state and prove the main result of this paper:

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
p:E\to B
be a locally trivial fibre bundle with structure group
G
, the fibre
X
and the associated principal bundle
\tau:P\to B
. Then the sequential parametrized topological
complexity
{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]

admits the following upper and lower bounds

(28)

\displaystyle{\sf TC}_{r,G}^{w}(X;P)\,\leq\,{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]\,\leq\,G\mbox{-}{\sf cat}[p:E\to B]\,+\,{\sf TC}_{r,G}^{w}(X;P).

Proof.

Since
E=X\times_{G}P
we have

E^{r}_{B}=X^{r}\times_{G}P\quad\mbox{ and}\quad E^{I}_{B}=X^{I}\times_{G}P\quad\mbox{for any}\quad r\geq 2.

The map
\Pi_{r}:E^{I}_{B}\to E^{r}_{B}
becomes

\rho_{r}\times 1:X^{I}\times_{G}P\to X^{r}\times_{G}P\quad\mbox{where}\quad\rho_{r}(\gamma)=(\gamma(t_{0}),\dots,\gamma(t_{r})).

Consider the commutative diagram

\textstyle{X^{I}\times P\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{Q^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\rho_{r}\times 1}
\textstyle{X^{I}\times_{G}P\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho_{r}\times_{G}1}
\textstyle{X^{r}\times P\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{Q}
\textstyle{X^{r}\times_{G}P}

where
Q:X^{r}\times P\to X^{r}\times_{G}P
and
Q^{\prime}:X^{I}\times P\to X^{I}\times_{G}P

are the natural projections.
Using Lemma
4.5
and Theorem
5.1
we have

\displaystyle{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\sf secat}[\rho_{r}\times_{G}1:X^{I}\times_{G}P\to X^{r}\times_{G}P]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\sf secat}[(\rho_{r}\times_{G}1)\circ Q^{\prime}:X^{I}\times P\to X^{r}\times_{G}P]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\sf secat}[(Q\circ(\rho_{r}\times 1):X^{I}\times P\to X^{r}\times_{G}P]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\sf secat}[Q:X^{r}\times P\to X^{r}\times_{G}P]+{\sf secat}_{Q}[\rho_{r}\times 1:X^{I}\times P\to X^{r}\times P]

Next we observe that

{\sf secat}[X^{r}\times P\to X^{r}\times_{G}P]\leq{\sf secat}[\tau:P\to B]=G\mbox{-}{\sf cat}[p:E\to B]

and

{\sf secat}_{Q}[X^{I}\times P\to X^{r}\times P]={\sf TC}^{w}_{r,G}(X;P).

Thus, we obtain the right inequality in (
28
).

For the left inequality (
28
) we consider again diagram (
8
) and observe that the fibration

\pi_{r}\times 1:X^{I}\times P\to X^{r}\times P
is induced from
\pi_{r}\times_{G}1:X^{I}\times_{G}P\to X^{r}\times_{G}P
via
Q
. Therefore, using Lemma
4.4
we obtain

\displaystyle{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\sf secat}[\rho_{r}\times 1:X^{I}\times_{G}P\to X^{r}\times_{G}P]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle{\sf secat}_{Q}[\rho_{r}\times 1:X^{I}\times P\to X^{r}\times P]={\sf TC}^{w}_{r,G}(X;P).

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 8.2
.

Due to the right inequality (
25
), the upper bound in (

) gives

(34)

\displaystyle{\sf TC}_{r}[p:E\to B]\,\leq\,G\mbox{-}{\sf cat}[p:E\to B]\,+\,{\sf TC}_{r,G}^{w}(X).

The RHS of this inequality has two terms, one depending only on the initial bundle
p:E\to B
and the other depending only on the fibre,

viewed as a
G

Theorem
8.1
implies that for the trivial bundle
p:E\to B
with fibre

, see

[

]
.
Indeed, in this case
G
-
{\sf cat}[p:E\to B]=0
and
{\sf TC}_{r,G}^{w}(X,P)={\sf TC}_{r}(X)
and hence the statement follows from (

).

Example 8.3
.

The Klein bottle
K
is the total space of the bundle

p:K=S^{1}\times_{{\rm\mathbb{Z}}/2}S^{1}\to S^{1}

with the associated principal bundle the
2
-fold covering

\tau:S^{1}\to S^{1}
and the action of

on the fibre
S^{1}
being given by
reflection in the last coordinate. The inequality (

and the result of Example

give

(35)

\displaystyle{\sf TC}[p:K\to S^{1}]={\sf TC}_{2}[p:K\to S^{1}]\leq 1+1=2.

Mark Grant observed that (

) is in fact an equality. The inequality
{\sf TC}[p:K\to S^{1}]\geq 2
can be obtained by applying Theorem 2 from
[
12
]
. The bundle

is the unit sphere bundle of a rank 2 vector bundle
\xi
over the circle

. One has
\rm w_{2}(\xi)=0
(for dimensional reasons) and
\rm w_{1}(\xi)\not=0
(since

\mathfrak{h}(\rm w_{1}(\xi)|\rm w_{2}(\xi))=1
equals one. Theorem 2 from
[
12
]
now applies and gives an equality

{\sf TC}[p:K\to S^{1}]=2
.

Example 8.4
.

Consider the principal
G
-bundle
\tau:P\to B
where
G=S^{1}
,
P=S^{2n+1}
and

(the Hopf bundle).
Here the sphere

In

the authors studied parametrized topological complexity of sphere bundles. The sphere bundle

which was discussed in the previous paragraph is the unit sphere bundle associated with the rank 3 vector bundle

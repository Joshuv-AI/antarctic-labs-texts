Alexander Duals of Symmetric Simplicial Complexes and Stanley-Reisner Ideals
Ayah Almousa, Kaitlin Bruegge, Martina Juhnke-Kubitzke, Uwe Nagel, Alexandra Pevzner

Abstract
. Given an ascending chain ( I n ) n ∈ N (I_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}} of Sym \operatorname{Sym} -invariant squarefree monomial ideals, we study the corresponding chain of Alexander duals ( I n ∨ ) n ∈ N (I_{n}^{\vee})_{n\in\mathbb{N}} . Using a novel combinatorial tool, which we call avoidance up to symmetry , we provide an explicit description of the minimal generating set up to symmetry in terms of the original generators. Combining this result with methods from discrete geometry, this enables us to show that the number of orbit generators of I n ∨ I_{n}^{\vee} is given by a polynomial in n n for sufficiently large n n . The same is true for the number of orbit generators of minimal degree, this degree being a linear function in n n eventually. The former result implies that the number of Sym \operatorname{Sym} -orbits of primary components of I n I_{n} grows polynomially in n n for large n n . As another application, we show that, for each i ≥ 0 i\geq 0 , the number of i i -dimensional faces of the associated Stanley-Reisner complexes of I n I_{n} is also given by a polynomial in n n for large n n .



1. Introduction

We consider families of simplicial complexes
\Delta_{n}

\operatorname{Sym}(n)
on
n

of

-dimensional simplex for some fixed integer

are precisely the

-orbit of
[n-d+1]

However, if

\sigma\cdot j_{i}=(\sigma(j))_{i}

-invariant chain

stabilizes

eventually as a consequence of, e.g.,
[

There is a rich literature relating properties of a simplicial complex

(see, e.g.,

5

-invariant chain

of symmetric simplicial complexes. By

For any

-invariant chain

By the Stanley-Reisner correspondence, simplicial complexes can be equivalently described by squarefree monomial ideals. Thus, we also consider

Our methods are constructive. We explicitly describe the

2. Order Ideals and Avoidance up to Symmetry

The goal of this section is to establish a result we dub
avoidance up to symmetry
.
We begin by recalling definitions and establishing notation for (upper) order ideals in a Boolean poset. As we shall see, order ideals capture information about the columns of exponent matrices of squarefree monomial ideals, and the tools we develop in this section will be central to understanding which orbit classes of squarefree monomials do or do not appear in the Alexander duals of Sym-invariant chains of squarefree monomial ideals.

2.1 Order Ideals

Definition 2.1
.

Fix a finite set
M
and denote by
2^{M}
its Boolean lattice ordered by inclusion.
For any subposet
K
of
2^{M}
and
J\subset K
, say
J
is an
(upper) order ideal
if
J
is closed under taking supersets, i.e., whenever
T\in J
and
S\supset T
, one has
S\in J
.

Remark 2.2
.

Unless stated otherwise, an “order ideal” is always assumed to be an upper order ideal.

For subsets
S
and
T
of any set, we denote its difference set by
S-T
. For an element
t\in T
, we often write for simplicity
S-t
instead of
S-\{t\}
. If
S
and
T
are disjoint we use
S\sqcup T
to indicate a decomposition of
S\cup T
.

Notation 2.3
.

For any
T\subset M
, let
T^{C}\coloneqq M-T
denote the complement of
T
in
M
. Given a subposet
K
of
2^{M}
and any subset
J\subset K
, we denote by
\overline{J}
the set

\overline{J}\coloneqq\{T^{C}~\mid~T\in J\}.

We emphasize that
\overline{J}
consists of the complements of all elements in
J
, it is
not
the complement of
J
in
2^{M}
, which we will typically denote by
2^{M}-J
.

Since the map
\phi:2^{M}\rightarrow 2^{M}
such that
\phi(T)=T^{C}
for any
T\in 2^{M}
is a bijection, we have the following useful properties of order ideals.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
I,J\subseteq 2^{M}
with
I\subseteq J
. Then the following hold:

(1)

\overline{I}\subset\overline{J}
;

(2)

\overline{J-I}=\overline{J}-\overline{I}
.

These simple properties imply the following lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
K
be a subposet of
2^{M}
. For a subset
J\subseteq K
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

J
is an order ideal of
K
.

(2)

\overline{K}-\overline{J}
is an order ideal of
\overline{K}
.

Proof.

Assume
(a)
is true.
Fix
S\in\overline{K}-\overline{J}
and let
T\supset S
. By Lemma
2.4
,
\overline{K}-\overline{J}=\overline{K-J}
and since
S\in\overline{K}-\overline{J}
, it follows that
S^{C}\notin J
. Since
T^{C}\subset S^{C}
and
J
is an order ideal, we get
T^{C}\notin J
, and so
T\in\overline{K}-\overline{J}
, proving
(b)
.

The argument that
(b)
implies
(a)
is similar.
∎

In the following, we use
\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{N}_{0}
to denote the set of positive and non-negative integers, respectively.

2.2 Avoidance up to symmetry

In this subsection, we prove a combinatorial criterion which detects whether the images of an element
i
in a set
N
under two different maps
f
and
g
into a subposet of the Boolean lattice share elements “up to symmetry”; that is, whether
f(i)\cap g(\sigma(i))=\varnothing
for some
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(N)
. The next lemma will be crucial to prove this criterion.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
K
be a subposet of
2^{M}
. Consider two maps

K\to\mathbb{N}_{0},\quad T\mapsto k_{T},

\overline{K}\to\mathbb{N}_{0},\quad S\mapsto\ell_{S}

satisfying
\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in K}k_{T}=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in K}\ell_{T^{C}}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

For every order ideal
J
of
K
, one has
\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}k_{T}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}\ell_{T^{C}}
.

(2)

For every order ideal
I
of
\overline{K}
, one has
\sumop\displaylimits_{S\in I}\ell_{S}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{S\in I}k_{S^{C}}
.

Proof.

Set
n\coloneqq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in K}k_{T}=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in K}\ell_{T^{C}}
. Then for any order ideal
J
of
K
, one has

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}k_{T}

\displaystyle=n-\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in K-J}k_{T},

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}\ell_{T^{C}}

\displaystyle=n-\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in K-J}\ell_{T^{C}}.

Hence, the inequality
\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}k_{T}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}\ell_{T^{C}}
holds if and only if
\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in K-J}\ell_{T^{C}}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in K-J}k_{T}
holds. Since
T\in K-J
if and only if
T^{C}\in\overline{K-J}
, the latter inequality is equivalent to

\sumop\displaylimits_{S\in\overline{K-J}}\ell_{S}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{S\in\overline{K-J}}k_{S^{C}}.

By Lemma
2.5
,
J
is an order ideal of
K
if and only if
\overline{K-J}
is an order ideal of
\overline{K}
, and so the equivalence of (a) and (b) is proven.
∎

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
K
be a nonempty subposet of
2^{M}
and let
N
be a finite set. Consider maps

f:N\to K

g:N\to\overline{K}.

Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

For every order ideal
J
of
K
,

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}\left|f^{-1}(T)\right|\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}\left|g^{-1}(T^{C})\right|.

(2)

For every order ideal
I
of
\overline{K}
,

\sumop\displaylimits_{S\in I}\left|g^{-1}(S)\right|\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{S\in I}\left|f^{-1}(S^{C})\right|.

(3)

There exists a permutation
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(N)
such that
f(i)\cap g(\sigma(i))=\varnothing
for every
i\in N
.

Proof.

For every
T\in K
, set
k_{T}:=|f^{-1}(T)|
and for every
S\in\overline{K}
, set
\ell_{S}:=|g^{-1}(S)|
. Then the assumption reads
\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in K}k_{T}=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in K}\ell_{T^{C}}
. Lemma
2.6
shows that (a) and (b) are equivalent.

Next, we argue that
(c)
implies
(a)
. Fix a permutation
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(N)
such that
f(i)\cap g(\sigma(i))=\varnothing
for all
i\in N
. Let
J
be an order ideal of
K
. Given
i\in N
, it follows from our assumption that
f(i)\subset g(\sigma(i))^{C}
. Hence, if
f(i)\in J
then
g(\sigma(i))^{C}\in J
. Setting

D:=\{i\in N:f(i)\in J\}

C:=\{j\in N:g(j)\in\overline{J}\},

we have an injective map

D\hookrightarrow C,\quad i\mapsto\sigma(i).

Hence
|D|\leq|C|
. But
|D|=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}k_{T}
and
|C|=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}\ell_{T^{C}}
, and so we have proven that Condition
(a)
is satisfied.

Now we prove that
(a)
implies
(c)
by induction on
|N|
. Note that replacing
g
by
g\circ\tau
for any
\tau\in\operatorname{Sym}(N)
changes neither the assumption nor the assertion.

First assume that
|N|=1
and
N=\{i\}
. Let
T_{0}:=f(i)\in K
, and so
k_{T_{0}}=1
and all other
k_{T}=0
. Let
J
be the order ideal of
K
that is generated by
T_{0}
. By our assumption, there exists some
S_{0}\in J
such that
\ell_{S_{0}^{C}}\geq 1
. Since
S_{0}\in J
, we have
T_{0}\subseteq S_{0}
and hence
T_{0}\cap S_{0}^{C}=\varnothing
. Moreover,
S_{0}^{C}=g(i)
. Taking
\sigma
to be the identity permutation, we have

f(i)\cap g(\sigma(i))=T_{0}\cap S_{0}^{C}=\varnothing,

as desired.

Now assume that
|N|\geq 2
. If
f(N)=\{\varnothing\}
, then we are done. So assume there is a nonempty
T_{0}\in K
such that
k_{T_{0}}>0
. Then by the same argument as above, there is some
S_{0}\in K
such that
T_{0}\subseteq S_{0}
and
\ell_{S_{0}^{C}}>0
. Fix an element
i\in f^{-1}(T_{0})
. After possibly applying a permutation to
N
, the domain of
g
, one can assume that
f(i)=T_{0}
and
g(i)=S_{0}^{C}
. Set
\widetilde{N}:=N-\{i\}
, and let
\tilde{f},\tilde{g}
be the restrictions of
f,g
to
\widetilde{N}
, respectively. Let
\tilde{k}_{T}:=|\tilde{f}^{-1}(T)|
and
\tilde{\ell}_{S}:=|\tilde{g}^{-1}(S)|
for each
T\in K
and each
S\in\overline{K}
. We now consider two cases.

Case 1:
For every order ideal
J
of
K
, one has

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}\tilde{k}_{T}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}\tilde{\ell}_{T^{C}}.

Then, by induction, there is a permutation
\tilde{\sigma}\in\operatorname{Sym}(\widetilde{N})
such that
\tilde{f}(j)\cap\tilde{g}(\sigma(j))=\varnothing
for all
j\in\widetilde{N}
. Let
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(N)
be the permutation defined by

\sigma(j)=\begin{cases}\tilde{\sigma}(j)&\text{if }j\in\widetilde{N}\\
i&\text{if }j=i\end{cases}.

Since
\tilde{\sigma}
is a bijection from
\widetilde{N}
to
\widetilde{N}
, the map
\sigma
is a well-defined permutation of
N
. Furthermore, one has that
f(j)\cap g(\sigma(j))=T_{0}\cap S_{0}^{C}=\varnothing
for all
j\in N
, which proves Condition
(c)
.

Case 2:
There exists an order ideal
J_{0}
of
K
such that

(1)

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J_{0}}\tilde{k}_{T}>\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J_{0}}\tilde{\ell}_{T^{C}}.

Note that
\tilde{k}_{T_{0}}=k_{T_{0}}-1
and
\tilde{k}_{T}=k_{T}
for all
T\neq T_{0}
. Similarly,
\tilde{\ell}_{S_{0}^{C}}=\ell_{S_{0}^{C}}-1
and
\tilde{\ell}_{S}=\ell_{S}
for all
S\neq S_{0}^{C}
. Since
\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J_{0}}k_{T}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J_{0}}\ell_{T^{C}}
by Condition

, in order for (
1
) to occur, we must have had

(2)

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J_{0}}k_{T}=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J_{0}}\ell_{T^{C}}

and
T_{0}\in K-J_{0}
,
S_{0}\in J_{0}
.
Define subsets
N^{\prime},N^{\prime\prime}
of
N
as follows:

\displaystyle N^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=\{j\in N\;\mid\;f(j)\in J_{0}\},

\displaystyle N^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle:=\{j\in N\;\mid\;f(j)\in K-J_{0}\}.

Then we get the set partition

. Note that

, since
f(i)=T_{0}
and

. Hence

is nonempty. Moreover, combining Equation (

|N^{\prime\prime}|=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in K-J_{0}}k_{T}=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in K-J_{0}}\ell_{T^{C}}<|N|.

The strict inequality holds since

and

and

. Furthermore, the map

.

Now use the map

of

\displaystyle M^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=\{j\in N\;\mid\;g(j)\in\overline{J_{0}}\},

\displaystyle M^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle:=\{j\in N\;\mid\;g(j)\in\overline{K}-\overline{J_{0}}\}.

Thus,

is a partition of

. Combined with Equation (

, and so

. Hence, there is a bijection

satisfying

and
\sigma(M^{\prime\prime})=N^{\prime\prime}
. It follows that after applying

to the domain of
g
, we obtain a map, call it again
g
, with
g(N^{\prime})\subseteq\overline{J_{0}}
and
g(N^{\prime\prime})\subseteq\overline{K}-\overline{J_{0}}
. Thus,
g
induces maps
g^{\prime}\colon N^{\prime}\to\overline{J_{0}}
and
g^{\prime\prime}\colon N^{\prime\prime}\to\overline{K}-\overline{J_{0}}
. Our goal is to apply the induction hypothesis on the pairs of maps
f^{\prime},g^{\prime}
and
f^{\prime\prime},g^{\prime\prime}
.

First consider the maps
f^{\prime}:N^{\prime}\to J_{0}
and
g^{\prime}:N^{\prime}\to\overline{J_{0}}
. Since
f^{-1}(T)=(f^{\prime})^{-1}(T)
whenever
T\in J_{0}
and
g^{-1}(T^{C})=(g^{\prime})^{-1}(T^{C})
whenever
T\in J_{0}
, Equation (
2
) implies

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J_{0}}\left|(f^{\prime})^{-1}(T)\right|=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J_{0}}\left|(g^{\prime})^{-1}(T^{C})\right|.

Moreover, since any order ideal
J
of
J_{0}
is also an order ideal of
K
, we obtain

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}\left|(f^{\prime})^{-1}(T)\right|=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}|f^{-1}(T)|\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}|g^{-1}(T^{C})|=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}\left|(g^{\prime})^{-1}(T^{C})\right|.

Hence, the induction hypothesis applies to
f^{\prime},g^{\prime}
, and so there exists a permutation
\sigma^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Sym}(N^{\prime})
such that for each
j\in N^{\prime}
, we have
f^{\prime}(j)\cap g^{\prime}(\sigma^{\prime}(j))=\varnothing
.

Now consider
f^{\prime\prime}\colon N^{\prime\prime}\to K-J_{0}
and
g^{\prime\prime}\colon N^{\prime\prime}\to\overline{K}-\overline{J_{0}}
. By Lemmas
2.4
and
2.5
, we know that
\overline{K}-\overline{J_{0}}=\overline{K-J_{0}}
is an order ideal of
\overline{K}
. It follows from our assumptions on
f
and
g
and from Equation (
2
), that

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in K-J_{0}}\left|(f^{\prime\prime})^{-1}(T)\right|=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in K-J_{0}}\left|(g^{\prime\prime})^{-1}(T^{C})\right|.

Given an order ideal
I
of
\overline{K}-\overline{J_{0}}
, it is also an order ideal of
\overline{K}
. Hence by equivalence of (a) and (b) we have

\sumop\displaylimits_{S\in I}\left|(g^{\prime\prime})^{-1}(S)\right|=\sumop\displaylimits_{S\in I}|g^{-1}(S)|\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{S\in I}|f^{-1}(S^{C})|=\sumop\displaylimits_{S\in I}\left|(f^{\prime\prime})^{-1}(S^{C})\right|.

Thus, the induction hypothesis applies to
f^{\prime\prime},g^{\prime\prime}
, and so there exists a permutation
\sigma^{\prime\prime}\in\operatorname{Sym}(N^{\prime\prime})
such that
f^{\prime\prime}(j)\cap g^{\prime\prime}(\sigma^{\prime\prime}(j))=\varnothing
for every
j\in N^{\prime\prime}
.

Finally, define a permutation
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(N)
by

\sigma(j)=\begin{cases}\sigma^{\prime}(j)&\text{if }j\in N^{\prime}\\
\sigma^{\prime\prime}(j)&\text{if }j\in N^{\prime\prime}\end{cases}.

Then
\sigma
is the desired permutation which satisfies Condition (c) of the theorem.
∎

3.
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant Chains and Alexander Duality

We now recall some concepts and results that will be used throughout this paper. For further information on combinatorial commutative algebra we refer to
[
9
]
.

3.1 Invariant Chains of Ideals

Notation 3.1
.

We denote by
\mathbb{N}
the set of positive integers. Fix
c\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\mathbf{k}
be any field. Set
R=\mathbf{k}[x_{i,j}\mid i\in[c],j\geq 1]
to be a polynomial ring in infinitely many variables. One may view
R
as the colimit of the ascending chain of polynomial rings

R_{1}\subseteq R_{2}\subseteq\dots\subseteq R_{n}\subseteq\dots

where
R_{n}=\mathbf{k}[x_{i,j}\mid i\in[c],j\in[n]]
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Set
\operatorname{Sym}(\infty)=\bigcupop\displaylimits_{n\geq 1}\operatorname{Sym}(n)
to be the infinite symmetric group, where
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
is the symmetric group on
\{1,\dots,n\}
. Embedding
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
as the stabilizer of
n+1
in
\operatorname{Sym}(n+1)
, one may view
\operatorname{Sym}(\infty)
as the colimit of the ascending chain of finite groups

\operatorname{Sym}(1)\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}(2)\subseteq\dots\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}(n)\subseteq\dots.

We always consider the action of
\operatorname{Sym}(\infty)
on
R
that is induced by
\sigma\cdot x_{i,j}=x_{i,\sigma(j)}
for
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(\infty)
, and this action restricts to an action of
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
on the polynomial ring
R_{n}
.

Definition 3.2
.

Adopt Notation
3.1
. An ideal
I\subseteq R
is said to be
\operatorname{Sym}(\infty)
-invariant
(or
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant
for short) if
\sigma(f)\in I
for any
f\in I
and any
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(\infty)
. A
\operatorname{Sym}(\infty)
-invariant chain
is a sequence
(I_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of ideals
I_{n}\subseteq R_{n}
satisfying

\operatorname{Sym}(n)(I_{m})=\left\{\sigma(f)\mid f\in I_{m},\ \sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)\right\}\subseteq I_{n}\text{ whenever }m\leq n.

The colimit
I=\lim I_{n}
of any such chain is isomorphic to the union of the extension ideals
I_{n}
in
R
. Thus,
I
is a
\operatorname{Sym}(\infty)
-invariant ideal of
R
.
By results of Cohen, Aschenbrenner-Hillar, and Hillar-Sullivant,
[
3
,
1
,
8
]
the ring
R
is
\operatorname{Sym}(\infty)
-Noetherian
, meaning that any
\operatorname{Sym}(\infty)
-invariant ideal is generated by finitely many
\operatorname{Sym}(\infty)
-orbits. This gives one the ability to study
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant ideals via orbit classes of generators.

3.2 Alexander duality and Stanley-Reisner complexes

We will restrict our attention to the case of
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant squarefree monomial ideals, also known as Stanley-Reisner ideals. This is a particularly interesting class of ideals because homological information about these chains of ideals also yields topological information about the associated chains of simplicial complexes with
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant facets. We begin by recalling some essential tools in the theory of squarefree monomial ideals, such as Alexander duality and Stanley-Reisner theory.

Notation 3.3
.

Let
S=\mathbf{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring over a field
\mathbf{k}
in
n
variables. The monomial
\mathbf{X}=x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}
denotes the product of all the variables.
Given a simplicial complex
\Delta
on
n
vertices, we will frequently refer to faces of simplicial complexes as monomials
\mathbf{m}\in S
rather than specifying the subset of
[n]
corresponding to the face.
If
\mathbf{m}
is a monomial in
S
, write
P_{\mathbf{m}}
for the prime ideal generated by the variables dividing
\mathbf{m}
, that is,
P_{\mathbf{m}}\coloneqq\langle x_{i}\mid x_{i}\text{ divides }\mathbf{m}\rangle
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
I
be a squarefree monomial ideal in a polynomial ring
S
. Then the
Stanley-Reisner complex
of
I
, denoted
\Delta(I)
, is the simplicial complex consisting of squarefree monomials
not
in
I
,

\Delta_{I}=\{\mathbf{m}\text{ a squarefree monomial in }S\mid\mathbf{m}\notin I\}.

For a simplicial complex
\Delta
, the
Stanley-Reisner ideal
of
\Delta
is the squarefree monomial ideal generated by the non-faces of
\Delta
,

I_{\Delta}=\langle\mathbf{m}\text{ a monomial in }S\mid\mathbf{m}\notin\Delta\rangle.

It is well-known that the above constructions give bijections between the set of squarefree monomial ideals of
S
and the set of simplicial complexes on the vertex set
[n]
.

Definition 3.5
.

If
\Delta
is a simplicial complex on
[n]
, the
Alexander dual
of
\Delta
, denoted by
\Delta^{\vee}
, is the simplicial complex with faces
\{\frac{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{m}}\mid\mathbf{m}\notin\Delta\}
. In particular, the facets of
\Delta^{\vee}
are exactly the complements of the minimal non-faces of
\Delta
.

If
I
is a squarefree monomial ideal, then the
Alexander dual
of
I
is

I^{\vee}\coloneqq\langle\mathbf{m}\mid P_{\mathbf{m}}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)\rangle,

where
\operatorname{Ass}(I)
denotes the set of associated primes of
I
. In this way, associated primes of
I
correspond to the generators of
I^{\vee}
.

The two concepts of Alexander duals are compatible because
I_{\Delta}^{\vee}
is the Stanley-Reisner ideal of
\Delta^{\vee}
.

The following fact follows from tracing through the definitions of Alexander duals. It is a useful criterion for translating between generators of the Alexander dual of an ideal and the facets of its associated Stanley-Reisner complex.

Fact 3.6
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex. Then the facets of
\Delta^{\vee}
are the monomials
\frac{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{m}}
where
\mathbf{m}
ranges over the monomial minimal generators of
I_{\Delta}
. Analogously, if
I
is a squarefree monomial ideal, then the generators of
I^{\vee}
are the monomials
\frac{\mathbf{X}}{F}
, where
F
ranges over the facets of
\Delta(I)
.

Note that any monomial ideal
I
has a unique minimal generating set
G(I)
consisting only of monomials. For brevity, we often refer to an element of
G(I)
simply as a minimal generator of
I
.

Using the minimal generating set of a squarefree monomial ideal, one can characterize monomials in its Alexander dual more directly. This is well-known and follows, for example, from
[
9
, Corollary 5.25]
.

Fact 3.7
.

Consider an ideal
I\subset S
that is minimally generated by squarefree monomials
\mathbf{m}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{m}_{t}
. Then a squarefree monomial
\mathbf{m}\in S
is not in
I^{\vee}
if and only if there exists
1\leq i\leq t
such that the monomial
\mathbf{m}\cdot\mathbf{m}_{i}
is squarefree.

3.3 Alexander Duals of Sym-Invariant Chains

We consider the following question.

Question 3.8
.

Given a
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant chain
(I_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of ideals with
I_{n}\subseteq R_{n}
generated by
\operatorname{Sym}
-orbits of squarefree monomials, what are the generators of its Alexander dual
I_{n}^{\vee}
(or, by taking complements, the facets of their associated Stanley-Reisner complexes
\Delta(I_{n})
)?

We will observe that the Alexander dual of
I_{n}
is also a
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant ideal, implying that we can study this question by aiming to understand monomial orbit generators of
I_{n}^{\vee}
. This allows us to refine the above question.

We call a simplicial complex
\Delta
on vertex set
[n]

\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant
if
F\in\Delta
implies
\sigma(F)\in\Delta
for all
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)
. In particular,
F
and
\sigma(F)
have the same dimension in this case. The next proposition relates
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant complexes and ideals.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on vertex set
[n]^{c}
. Then
\Delta
is
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
-invariant if and only if
I_{\Delta}
is
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
-invariant.

Proof.

First suppose that
I_{\Delta}
is
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
-invariant. Let
F\in\Delta
and assume by contradiction that
\sigma(F)\notin\Delta
, i.e.,
\sigma(F)\in I_{\Delta}
. Since
I_{\Delta}
is
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant, it follows that
F\in I_{\Delta}
and hence
F\notin\Delta
which yields a contradiction. The other direction follows by a similar argument.
∎

The next proposition shows that
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariance is preserved under taking Alexander duals.

Proposition 3.10
.

A simplicial complex
\Delta
is
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
-invariant if and only if
\Delta^{\vee}
is
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
-invariant.

Proof.

Let
\Delta
be
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
-invariant and let
V
be the vertex set of
\Delta
. Assume by contradiction that there exist
F\in\Delta^{\vee}
and
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)
with
\sigma(F)\notin\Delta^{\vee}
. Interpreting faces of
\Delta
as monomials, it follows that
\frac{\textbf{X}}{\sigma(F)}\in\Delta
. Since
\Delta
is
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
-invariant, we have that
\frac{\textbf{X}}{F}\in\Delta
, i.e.,
F\notin\Delta^{\vee}
, which is a contradiction. The other direction follows from the first one since
(\Delta^{\vee})^{\vee}=\Delta
.
∎

Combining
3.9
and
3.10
gives the following result.

Proposition 3.11
.

A squarefree monomial ideal
I
of
R_{n}
is
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
-invariant if and only if its Alexander dual
I^{\vee}
is
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
-invariant.

The following useful fact is likely known to experts.

Proposition 3.12
.

If
I,J
are squarefree monomial ideals of
S
with
I\subseteq J
, then
J^{\vee}\subseteq I^{\vee}
.

Proof.

For the convenience of the reader, we provide an argument.

Denote by
\Delta(I)
and
\Delta(J)
the simplicial complexes associated to
I
and
J
. Let
\mathbf{m}_{J}
be a minimal generator of
J^{\vee}
. Thus,
\mathbf{m}_{J}
corresponds to the complement of a facet
F_{J}
of
\Delta_{J}
. Since
I\subseteq J
, we have that
\Delta_{J}
is a subcomplex of
\Delta_{I}
. Hence
F_{J}
is contained in some facet
F_{I}
of
\Delta_{I}
, and so the complement
\mathbf{m}_{I}\in I^{\vee}
of
F_{I}
divides
\mathbf{m}_{J}
, which shows
\mathbf{m}_{J}\in I^{\vee}
.
∎

The next corollary is immediate from
3.12
.

Corollary 3.13
.

If
(I_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant chain of squarefree monomial ideals, then, viewing
I_{n}^{\vee}
as an ideal of
R_{n+1}
, we have
I_{n+1}^{\vee}\subseteq I_{n}^{\vee}\cdot R_{n+1}
.

The following result describes the behavior of the Stanley-Reisner complexes of a
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant chain of squarefree monomial ideals.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
(I_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant chain of squarefree monomial ideals. Then

\Delta(I_{n})
is a subcomplex of
\Delta(I_{n+1})
for
n\gg 0
.

Proof.

Let
n\gg 0
such that the chain has stabilized, i.e.,
I_{n+1}=\operatorname{Sym}(n+1)(I_{n})
.
Let
F
be a face of
\Delta(I_{n})
, i.e.,
F\notin I_{n}
, where we identify faces with monomials. If, by contradiction,
F\notin\Delta(I_{n+1})
, then
F\in I_{n+1}=\operatorname{Sym}(n+1)(I_{n})
. As
F\in\Delta(I_{n})
, it follows that
F\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)(I_{n})
, which yields a contradiction.
∎

These results lead us to the following questions, which the remainder of this paper will be dedicated to answering. Recall that any
\operatorname{Sym}(\infty)
-invariant monomial ideal of
R
is generated by the
\operatorname{Sym}(\infty)
-orbits of finitely many monomials.

Question 3.15
.

Consider a
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant chain
(I_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of squarefree monomial ideals. What are the monomials whose
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
-orbits minimally generate
I_{n}^{\vee}
for large
n
in terms of the finitely many monomials whose
\operatorname{Sym}(\infty)
-orbits generate the colimit
\lim I_{n}
? How does the number of orbit generators of
I_{n}^{\vee}
grow as
n\rightarrow\infty
?

3.4 Invariant Chains of Simplicial Complexes

By Stanley-Reisner theory,
3.15
can also be stated in terms of simplicial complexes. We make this explicit now.

Definition 3.16
.

Consider a sequence
(\Delta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
simplicial complexes
\Delta_{n}
, where
\Delta_{n}
has vertex set
V_{n}=[n]^{c}\coloneqq\{j_{i}\;\mid\;i\in[c],j\in[n]\}
.
Define an action of
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
on
V_{n}
by
\sigma\cdot j_{i}=(\sigma(j))_{i}
. The sequence
(\Delta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is called a
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant chain
if
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
maps any face of
\Delta_{m}
onto a face of
\Delta_{n}
whenever
m\leq n
and the
c
-fold cone over
\Delta_{n}
with apices
V_{n+1}-V_{n}
is contained in
\Delta_{n+1}
.

As a consequence of the previous definition, every simplicial complex in a
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant chain is
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant as defined in the previous subsection. As mentioned in the introduction, any
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant chain of simplicial complexes stabilizes.

Proposition 3.17
.

If
(\Delta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant chain of simplicial complexes, then there is some integer
n_{0}
such that
\operatorname{Sym}(n+1)
-orbit of the cone over
\Delta_{n}
with apices
V_{n+1}-V_{n}
equals
\Delta_{n+1}
whenever
n\geq n_{0}

Proof.

Identify any vertex
j_{i}\in V_{n}
and the variable
x_{i,j}\in R_{n}
. Since each simplicial complex
\Delta_{n}
is
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
-invariant,
3.9
combined with
3.10
implies that the Alexander duals
I_{\Delta_{n}^{\vee}}=(I_{\Delta_{n}})^{\vee}
are
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
-invariant. The assumption that the cone over

\Delta_{n}
with apices
V_{n+1}-V_{n}
is contained in
\Delta_{n+1}
implies that the extension ideal
I_{\Delta_{n}}\cdot R_{n+1}
contains
I_{\Delta_{n+1}}
and by
3.13
we get that
I_{\Delta_{n}^{\vee}}R_{n+1}\subseteq I_{\Delta_{n}^{\vee}}
. Hence, the sequence
(I_{\Delta_{n}^{\vee}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant.
It follows from results in
[
3
,
8
]
or
[
11
]
that the sequence stabilizes, that is, there is some integer
n_{0}
such that
I_{\Delta_{n}^{\vee}}\cdot R_{n+1}=I_{\Delta_{n+1}^{\vee}}
for
n\geq n_{0}
. The required stabilization for
(\Delta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
follows.
∎

This result shows that
3.15
is equivalent to the problem of describing the facets of
\Delta_{n}^{\vee}
and the number of
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
-orbits of facets of
\Delta_{n}^{\vee}
for
n\gg 0
if
(\Delta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant chain of simplicial complexes.

4. Alexander Duals of
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant Chains and Order Ideals

We introduce some notation specific to the case of squarefree monomial orbit generators that we will use throughout the rest of the paper. With this notation in hand, we can translate the question of determining whether or not a certain monomial belongs to the Alexander dual of a
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant ideal into a combinatorial question closely related to the results from Section
2
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathbf{m}
be a squarefree monomial in
R_{n}=\mathbf{k}[x_{i,j}~\mid~i\in[c],j\in[n]]
for a fixed
c,n
. Then the
exponent matrix
associated to
\mathbf{m}
is the
c\times n
matrix
[b_{i,j}]\in\{0,1\}^{c\times n}
, where
b_{i,j}=1
if
x_{i,j}
divides
\mathbf{m}
and
b_{i,j}=0
otherwise. If
B
is the exponent matrix of
\mathbf{m}
, we write
\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{x}^{B}
.

Example 4.2
.

For
c=3
, the monomial
x_{1,1}x_{1,4}x_{1,5}x_{2,1}x_{2,2}x_{2,5}x_{3,1}x_{3,2}x_{3,5}\in R_{5}
has exponent matrix

B=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0&1&1\\
1&1&0&0&1\\
1&1&0&0&1\end{bmatrix}.

Given an orbit generator
\mathbf{x}^{B}
of a
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant squarefree monomial ideal
I_{n}
in
R_{n}
and a permutation
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)
, denote by
\sigma(B)
the matrix obtained from
B
by applying the permutation
\sigma
to its set of column vectors. Since any monomial

\sigma(\mathbf{x}^{B})=\mathbf{x}^{\sigma(B)}
also could have been chosen as an orbit generator of
I_{n}
, we would like to have a
“standard” representative for monomial orbit generators to avoid ambiguity.

Notation 4.3
.

For a subset
S\subseteq[c]
and an integer
k_{S}\geq 0
, let
\displaystyle A_{S}(k_{S})\in\{0,1\}^{c\times k_{S}}
denote the exponent matrix of the monomial
\displaystyle\prodop\displaylimits_{i\in S}\prodop\displaylimits_{j\in[k_{S}]}x_{i,j}
, i.e., the
(i,j)
entry of
A_{S}(k_{S})
with
k_{S}>0
is
1
if and only if
i\in S
and it is
0
, otherwise.

Order the subsets of
[c]
with a lexicographic ordering induced by the ordering
1>2>\cdots>c
on
[c]
.
That is, for subsets
S\neq T
of
[c]
, we let
S>T
if

•

|S|>|T|
or,

•

\lvert S\rvert=\lvert T\rvert
and
s_{i}>t_{i}
, where
S=\{s_{1}<s_{2}<\cdots<s_{k}\}
,
T=\{t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{k}\}
and
i=\min(\ell~\mid~s_{\ell}\neq t_{\ell})
.

Definition 4.4
.

For any monomial
\mathbf{x}^{B}\in R_{n}
, we call the unique matrix

A=\left[A_{S_{1}}(k_{S_{1}})\;\bigg|\;A_{S_{2}}(k_{S_{2}})\;\bigg|\;\cdots\;\bigg|\;A_{S_{2^{c}-1}}(k_{S_{2^{c}-1}})\right]

with
\displaystyle[c]=S_{1}>S_{2}>\cdots>S_{2^{c}-1}=\{c\}
and suitable integers
k_{S_{i}}\geq 0
such that
\mathbf{x}^{A}\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)\cdot\mathbf{x}^{B}
the
standard matrix
of
\mathbf{x}^{B}
.
Using the ordering on the subsets from Notation
4.3
, we often simply write

A=\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{\emptyset\neq T\subseteq[c]}A_{T}(k_{T})\right]

to denote the standard matrix.

Remark 4.5
.

If
A
is a standard matrix with
k<n
columns, then it is understood that the monomial
\mathbf{x}^{A}\in R_{n}
refers to the monomial with exponent matrix
A
padded with
n-k
columns of zeroes at the end.

Example 4.6
.

For
c=3
, the monomial
\mathbf{x}^{B}
in Example
4.2
has standard matrix

A=\left[A_{[3]}(2)\;\bigg|\;A_{\{2,3\}}(1)\;\bigg|\;A_{\{1\}}(1)\right]=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&0&1\\
1&1&1&0\\
1&1&1&0\end{bmatrix}

where
k_{S}=0
for all other subsets.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
A\in\{0,1\}^{c\times n}
be a
0-1
-matrix with columns
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
.
Define the
support
of a column
A_{i}
, denoted
\operatorname{Supp}(A_{i})
, to be the set of row indices for which the column
A_{i}
has nonzero entries. We also say that
A_{i}
has
support
in
S\subseteq[c]
or
is supported
in
S
if
S=\operatorname{Supp}(A_{i})
.

The
support
of the matrix
A
is the set

\operatorname{Supp}(A)=\{T\subseteq[c]\;\mid\;T=\operatorname{Supp}(C)\text{ for some column $C$ of $A$}\}.

We note that matrices
A
and
\sigma(A)
have the same support set for any
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)
.
With this notation established, we now apply Theorem
2.7
to study Question
3.15
in the case where ideal
I_{n}\subset R_{n}
is generated by a single squarefree monomial orbit
\operatorname{Sym}(n)\cdot\mathbf{x}^{A}
for
n\gg 0
. We characterize when a monomial
\mathbf{x}^{B}\in R_{n}
is in the Alexander dual of
I_{n}
for a fixed
n
. In this case, the image of the map
f
in the hypotheses of Theorem
2.7
will correspond to the support set of the standard matrix of
\mathbf{x}^{A}
, while the image of
g
will be given by the support set of the standard matrix of
\mathbf{x}^{B}
.

Definition 4.8
.

Given a matrix
A\in\{0,1\}^{c\times m}
with

A=\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in 2^{[c]}}A_{T}(k_{T})\right],\quad k_{T}\geq 0,

the
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant chain
generated by
\mathbf{x}^{A}
is the chain of ideals
(I_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
I_{n}=\langle\operatorname{Sym}(n)\cdot\mathbf{x}^{A}\rangle\subset R_{n}
if
n\geq m
and
I_{n}=0
otherwise.

Proposition 4.9

(Alexander Dual Membership Criterion)
.

Let
(I_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant chain of ideals generated by
\mathbf{x}^{A}
with
A=\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in 2^{[c]}}A_{T}(k_{T})\right]\in\{0,1\}^{c\times m}
.
Fix an integer
n\geq m
and consider a matrix
B=\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in 2^{[c]}}A_{T}(\ell_{T})\right]\in\{0,1\}^{c\times n}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

The monomial
\mathbf{x}^{B}
does not lie in
I_{n}^{\vee}
.

(2)

For every order ideal
J
of
2^{[c]}
, one has
\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}k_{T}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}\ell_{T^{C}}
.

(3)

For every order ideal
J
of
2^{[c]}
, one has
\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}\ell_{T}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}k_{T^{C}}
.

Proof.

Define maps
f,g\colon[n]\to 2^{[c]}
by

f(i)=\operatorname{Supp}(A_{i}),\quad\text{and}\quad g(i)=\operatorname{Supp}(B_{i}).

3.7
implies that
\mathbf{x}^{B}\notin I_{n}^{\vee}
if and only if there exists a permutation
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)
such that
f(i)\cap g(\sigma(i))=\varnothing
for every
i\in[n]
. By Theorem
2.7
, this is equivalent to conditions
(b)
and
(c)
, where
k_{T}=|f^{-1}(T)|
and
\ell_{T}=|g^{-1}(T)|
for each
T\subseteq[c]
.
∎

Definition 4.10
.

Consider two matrices
B,B^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{c\times n}
. We say that
\mathbf{x}^{B^{\prime}}

divides
\mathbf{x}^{B}
up to symmetry
if there exists
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)
such that
\mathbf{x}^{B^{\prime}}
divides
\mathbf{x}^{\sigma\cdot B}
.

We will frequently say that
\mathbf{x}^{B^{\prime}}
divides
\mathbf{x}^{B}
but mean that this is up to symmetry.

The following criterion for determining divisibility up to symmetry is also a consequence of Theorem
2.7
. It will be critical for constructing a minimal generating set for
I_{n}^{\vee}
in both the one-orbit and the general case.

Lemma 4.11

(Divisibility Lemma)
.

Consider two matrices

B=\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in 2^{[c]}}A_{T}(\ell_{T})\right]\quad\text{and}\quad B^{\prime}=\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in 2^{[c]}}A_{T}(j_{T})\right]

in
\{0,1\}^{c\times n}
.
Then
\mathbf{x}^{B^{\prime}}
divides
\mathbf{x}^{B}
up to symmetry if and only if for every order ideal
J
of
2^{[c]}
that is generated by a non-empty subset of
\operatorname{Supp}(B^{\prime})
, one has

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}j_{T}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}\ell_{T}.

Proof.

By definition,
\mathbf{x}^{B^{\prime}}
divides
\mathbf{x}^{B}
up to symmetry if and only if, up to a permutation of columns, one has
\operatorname{Supp}(B^{\prime}_{i})\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}(B_{i})
for each column
i
. Define
\overline{B}
to be the matrix whose column supports are the complements of those of
B
; that is,
\operatorname{Supp}(\overline{B}_{i})=\operatorname{Supp}(B_{i})^{C}
for each
i\in[n]
. Then
\mathbf{x}^{B^{\prime}}
divides
\mathbf{x}^{B}
up to symmetry if and only if
\operatorname{Supp}(\overline{B}_{i})\cap\operatorname{Supp}(B_{i}^{\prime})=\varnothing
for all
i
.
By
Theorem

2.7
, this equality is true for some permutation of the columns of
\overline{B}
exactly if

(3)

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}j_{T}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}\ell_{{(T^{C})}^{C}}=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}\ell_{T}

for every order ideal
J
of
2^{[c]}
.

It remains to show that is suffices to check Inequality (
3
) for order ideals generated by non-empty subsets of
\operatorname{Supp}(B^{\prime})
. To this end consider any order ideal
I
of
2^{[c]}
and assume that Inequality (
3
) is true for
\langle E\rangle
, where
E=I\cap\operatorname{Supp}(B^{\prime})
. Then,

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in I}j_{T}=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle E\rangle}j_{T}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle E\rangle}\ell_{T}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in I}\ell_{T}

which shows Inequality (
3
) for
I
. This completes the argument.
∎

Remark 4.12
.

For
J=2^{[c]}
, the condition
\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}j_{T}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}\ell_{T}
is automatically true with equality as both sides equal
n
, the number of columns of
B^{\prime}
and of
B
. Thus, in
Lemma

4.11
, it is enough to check the stated condition for order ideals
J\neq 2^{[c]}
.

We close this section with another result that we will use repeatedly to simplify our arguments and to reduce the number of possible monomials under consideration for the minimal orbit generators of
I_{n}^{\vee}
.

Lemma 4.13

(Exchange Lemma)
.

Adopt the notation of
4.9
. Let
J
be an order ideal of
2^{[c]}
.
Assume that
\mathbf{x}^{B}
is a minimal generator of
I_{n}^{\vee}
satisfying

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}\ell_{T}>\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}k_{T^{C}}.

Then the following hold:

(a)

If
\ell_{S}>0
for some
S\in J
, then
S
is a minimal element of
J
.

(b)

\ell_{U}=0
for all
\emptyset\neq U\in 2^{[c]}-J
and
\ell_{\emptyset}=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in 2^{[c]}-\overline{J}}k_{T}-1=n-1-\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}k_{T^{C}}
.

Proof.

For (a), assume by contradiction that there are elements
S^{\prime}\subsetneqq S
in
J
with
\ell_{S}>0
.
Consider the matrix

B^{\prime}=\left[A_{S^{\prime}}(\ell_{S^{\prime}}+1)\right]\ast\left[A_{S}(\ell_{S}-1)\right]\ast\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{U\in 2^{[c]}-\{S,S^{\prime}\}}A_{U}(\ell_{U})\right].

It differs from
B
in only two columns. Since
S^{\prime}\subsetneqq S
, we know that
\mathbf{x}^{B^{\prime}}
is a proper divisor of
\mathbf{x}^{B}
. Thus, it suffices to show that
\mathbf{x}^{B^{\prime}}\in I_{n}^{\vee}
. This follows by the Alexander Dual Membership Criterion from the fact that the sums of the number of columns of
B
and
B^{\prime}
that are supported in
S
or
S^{\prime}
are the same.

For (b), we let
a\coloneqq n-\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}\ell_{T}
. Setting
\tilde{B}=[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in J}A_{T}(\ell_{T})\ast A_{\emptyset}(a)]
, the Alexander Dual Membership Criterion directly implies that
\mathbf{x}^{\tilde{B}}\in I_{n}^{\vee}
. Since
\mathbf{x}^{\tilde{B}}
divides
\mathbf{x}^{B}
and the latter is a minimal generator, we must have
\mathbf{x}^{B}=\mathbf{x}^{\tilde{B}}
and hence
\ell_{U}=0
for all
\emptyset\neq U\in 2^{[c]}-J
. Moreover, we claim that

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}\ell_{T}=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}k_{T^{C}}+1.

Otherwise, assume that
\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}\ell_{T}=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}k_{T^{C}}+c
for some
c\in\mathbb{N}
with
c>1
. Denote by
\overline{B}
a matrix obtained from
B
by deleting
c-1
columns with support in some element of
J
and adding
c-1
columns of all zeroes. The Alexander Dual Membership Criterion implies that
\mathbf{x}^{\overline{B}}\in I_{n}^{\vee}
. As
\mathbf{x}^{\overline{B}}
properly divides
\mathbf{x}^{B}
by construction, we arrive at a contradiction and the claim is shown.
It follows that

\ell_{\emptyset}=n-\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}\ell_{T}=n-\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}k_{T^{C}}-1=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in 2^{[c]}-\overline{J}}k_{T}-1,

which finishes the proof.
∎

5. The Case of One Orbit

Aided by the results and setting established in Section
4
, we produce the generating sets for the Alexander duals of
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant chains of Stanley-Reisner ideals in the case where they have a single generator up to symmetry. We treat this case separately since there the ideas are more transparent and will be helpful for proving the general case.

Notation 5.1
.

Throughout this section, fix a nonzero matrix
A\in\{0,1\}^{c\times m}
given by

A=\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in 2^{[c]}}A_{T}(k_{T})\right],\quad k_{T}\geq 0,

\operatorname{Sym}

(see

Below we outline the basic construction for obtaining a generating set for

Construction 5.2
.

Let
n\geq m
. By adding

n=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in 2^{[c]}}k_{T}=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in 2^{[c]}}\ell_{T^{C}}
. Hence
4.9
yields that
the monomial
\mathbf{x}^{B}

I_{n}^{\vee}
if and only if there exists a proper order ideal
J\subsetneqq 2^{[c]}
such that

(4)

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}\ell_{T}>\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J}k_{T^{C}}.

If, in addition,
\mathbf{x}^{B}
is a minimal generator of

, then by the Exchange Lemma we have
\operatorname{Supp}(B)\subseteq\mathcal{C}\cup\{\emptyset\}
, where
\mathcal{C}
is the antichain generating

\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(n):=\left\{\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in\mathcal{C}}A_{T}(j_{T})\right]\;\mid\;j_{T}\geq 0,\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{C}}j_{T}=n+1-k_{\mathcal{C}}\right\},

where

(5)

k_{\mathcal{C}}:=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in 2^{[c]}-\overline{\langle\mathcal{C}\rangle}}k_{T}.

In the proof of

, we showed that (

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in 2^{[c]}-\overline{J}}k_{T}>\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in 2^{[c]}-\overline{J}}\ell_{T^{C}}\geq 0.

It follows that

.

Since non-empty antichains are in one-to-one correspondence with proper order ideals, the above construction yields the following generating set, which is not necessarily minimal.

Proposition 5.3
.

For any

, the Alexander dual

is generated by the set

\bigcupop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{ $\mathcal{C}$ antichain}\\
\varnothing\neq\mathcal{C}\subsetneqq 2^{[c]},\ k_{\mathcal{C}}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\left\{\mathbf{x}^{B}\;\mid\;B\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(n)\right\}.

Observe that the condition

implies that the order ideal generated by
\mathcal{C}
is not equal to

.

We discuss

.

Example

.

For any antichain
\mathcal{C}
of

, one has:

(a)

No two distinct elements of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(n)

(b)

If

As further preparation, we establish the following useful criterion.

Lemma 5.6
.

For nonempty antichains
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
of

satisfying
\{\varnothing\}\neq\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subset\left\langle\mathcal{C}\right\rangle_{\text{lower}}
and some integer

B=\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in\mathcal{C}}A_{T}(j_{T})\right]
with
j_{T}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and a set

\Lambda=\big\{(i_{S})_{S\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}\;\mid\;\sumop\displaylimits_{S\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}i_{S}=\lambda,i_{S}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\big\}.

Then there is some
(\ell_{S})_{S\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}\in\Lambda
such that
\mathbf{x}^{B^{\prime}}
with
B^{\prime}=[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{S\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}A_{S}(\ell_{S})]

\mathbf{x}^{B}

\lambda\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{C}\cap\langle\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\rangle}j_{T}.

Proof.

If
\mathbf{x}^{B^{\prime}}

\mathbf{x}^{B}
then, applying the Divisibility Lemma
4.11

J=\langle\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\rangle

\lambda=\sumop\displaylimits_{S\in\langle\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\rangle}\ell_{S}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\rangle}j_{T}=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\rangle\cap\mathcal{C}}j_{T},

We show the converse by using induction on the cardinality of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. If
|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}|=1

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\{S\}

\Lambda=\{\lambda\}
. Since
\lambda\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle S\rangle\cap\mathcal{C}}

\mathbf{x}^{B^{\prime}}

\mathbf{x}^{B}
.

Assume

Since

, the induction hypothesis applied to

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}-S}\ell_{T}=\lambda-\ell_{S}

with

\mathbf{x}^{B}
. By the Divisibility Lemma

, this means that for every subset
\mathcal{D}\neq\varnothing

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{D}}\ell_{T}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{C}\cap\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle}j_{T}.

\mathbf{x}^{B^{\prime}}

\mathbf{x}^{B}
up to symmetry.
We need to show that for every subset
\mathcal{D}\neq\varnothing
of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, one has
\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{D}}\ell_{T}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{C}\cap\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle}j_{T}.
Indeed, if

, this is true by Inequality (

). If

, and so

\displaystyle\ell_{S}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in(\mathcal{C}\cap\langle S\rangle)-(\mathcal{C}\cap\langle\mathcal{C}^{\prime}-S\rangle)}j_{T}

\displaystyle\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in(\mathcal{C}\cap\langle S\rangle)-(\mathcal{C}\cap\langle\mathcal{D}-S\rangle)}j_{T}.

Using Inequality (

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{D}-S}\ell_{T}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{C}\cap\langle\mathcal{D}-S\rangle}j_{T}.

Adding the last two inequalities, we obtain

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{D}}\ell_{T}

\displaystyle\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{C}\cap\langle\mathcal{D}-S\rangle}j_{T}+\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in(\mathcal{C}\cap\langle S\rangle)-(\mathcal{C}\cap\langle\mathcal{D}-S\rangle)}j_{T}

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{C}\cap\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle}j_{T}

∎

We now apply this result to our particular generating set from Proposition

to find a minimal generating set. For stating the result, we make the following definition.
Recall that

(see

Definition 5.7
.

For any antichain
\mathcal{C}\subset 2^{[c]}
with

, set

(10)

\displaystyle M\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(n):=

\displaystyle\left\{\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in\mathcal{C}}A_{T}(j_{T})\right]\;\mid\;j_{T}\geq 0,\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{C}}j_{T}=n+1-k_{\mathcal{C}}\ \text{ and }\right.

Theorem 5.8
.

If
n\geq\max\{k_{\mathcal{C}}-1:\mathcal{C}\text{ an antichain in }2^{[c]}\text{ with }k_{\mathcal{C}}\geq 1\}

I_{n}=\left\langle\operatorname{Sym}(n)\cdot\mathbf{x}^{A}\right\rangle
is minimally generated by the
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
-orbits of the monomials in the set

\mathcal{G}(n)\coloneqq\bigcupop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{C}\text{ antichain}\\
\varnothing\neq\mathcal{C}\subset 2^{[c]},k_{\mathcal{C}}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\left\{\mathbf{x}^{B}~\mid~B\in M\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(n)\right\}.

Proof.

We proceed in several steps.

(I) Observe that for any element
B=\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in\mathcal{C}}A_{T}(j_{T})\right]\in M\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(n)
and any antichain
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, one has

n+1-k_{\mathcal{C}}=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{C}}j_{T}=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{C}\cap\left\langle\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\right\rangle}j_{T}+\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{C}-\left\langle\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\right\rangle}j_{T}.

Thus, the condition

n+1-k_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{C}\cap\langle\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\rangle}j_{T}

is equivalent to

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{C}-\left\langle\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\right\rangle}j_{T}\leq k_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}-k_{\mathcal{C}}.

(II) We now show that
\mathcal{G}(n)
generates the ideal
I_{n}^{\vee}
. Using the generating set given in
5.3
, it suffices to prove that any monomial
\mathbf{x}^{B}
with
B\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(n)-M\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(n)
is divisible by a monomial in
\mathcal{G}(n)
. By Step (I), the fact that
B
is not in
M\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(n)
implies the existence of an antichain
\mathcal{C}_{1}\subseteq\left\langle\mathcal{C}\right\rangle_{\text{lower}}-\{\varnothing,\mathcal{C}\}
satisfying

n+1-k_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{C}\cap\langle\mathcal{C}_{1}\rangle}j_{T}.

Hence
Lemma

5.6
shows that there is a matrix
B_{1}=[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{S\in\mathcal{C}_{1}}A_{S}(\ell_{S})]\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}(n)
such that
\mathbf{x}^{B_{1}}
divides
\mathbf{x}^{B}
. Note that
\mathcal{C}_{1}\neq\mathcal{C}
implies
\left\langle\mathcal{C}_{1}\right\rangle_{\text{lower}}\subsetneqq\left\langle\mathcal{C}\right\rangle_{\text{lower}}
.

If
B_{1}
is not in
M\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}(n)
, repeating the above argument gives the existence of a matrix
B_{2}=[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{S\in\mathcal{C}_{2}}A_{S}(\ell_{S})]\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}(n)
for some antichain
\mathcal{C}_{2}\subseteq\left\langle\mathcal{C}_{1}\right\rangle_{\text{lower}}-\{\varnothing,\mathcal{C}_{1}\}
such that
\mathbf{x}^{B_{2}}
divides
\mathbf{x}^{B_{1}}
. Moreover, we have strict inclusions

\left\langle\mathcal{C}_{2}\right\rangle_{\text{lower}}\subsetneqq\left\langle\mathcal{C}_{1}\right\rangle_{\text{lower}}\subsetneqq\left\langle\mathcal{C}\right\rangle_{\text{lower}}.

Since any such decreasing chain is finite, repeating the above argument sufficiently often we eventually get an antichain
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}\neq\varnothing
and some
\tilde{B}\in M\mathcal{G}_{\tilde{\mathcal{C}}}(n)
such that
\mathbf{x}^{\tilde{B}}
divides
\mathbf{x}^{B}
. Since

\mathbf{x}^{\tilde{B}}
is in
\mathcal{G}(n)
, this proves that
\mathcal{G}(n)
is a generating set of
I_{n}^{\vee}
.

(III) Finally, we show that
\mathcal{G}(n)
minimally generates
I_{n}^{\vee}
, that is,
\mathcal{G}(n)
does not contain any redundant monomials.

Suppose there are monomial
\mathbf{x}^{B}\neq\mathbf{x}^{B^{\prime}}
in
\mathcal{G}(n)
with
B=[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in\mathcal{C}}A_{S}(j_{T})]\in M\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(n)
and
B^{\prime}=[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in\tilde{\mathcal{C}}}A_{S}(i_{T})]\in M\mathcal{G}_{\tilde{\mathcal{C}}}(n)
for some nonempty antichains
\mathcal{C},\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
with
k_{\mathcal{C}}\geq 1
and
k_{\tilde{\mathcal{C}}}\geq 1
such that
\mathbf{x}^{B^{\prime}}
divides
\mathbf{x}^{B}
.
Hence,
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}:=\operatorname{Supp}(B^{\prime})
is a nonempty antichain, and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
implies
k_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}\geq k_{\tilde{\mathcal{C}}}\geq 1
.
Since
\mathbf{x}^{B^{\prime}}
divides
\mathbf{x}^{B}
we obtain from the Divisibility Lemma
4.11
applied with

J=\langle\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\rangle
that
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subset\left\langle\mathcal{C}\right\rangle_{\text{lower}}
and

n+1-k_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}\leq n+1-k_{\tilde{\mathcal{C}}}=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\tilde{\mathcal{C}}}i_{T}=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}i_{T}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\rangle\cap\mathcal{C}}j_{T}.

It follows that
B
is not in
M\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(n)
. This is a contradiction and concludes the argument.
∎

We now simplify the description of
M\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(n)
by showing that it suffices to test the condition in
Definition

5.7
for fewer antichains
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.

Proposition 5.9
.

If
\mathcal{C}\subset 2^{[c]}
is an antichain with
k_{\mathcal{C}}\geq 1
then one has

(11)

\displaystyle M\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(n)=

\displaystyle\left\{\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in\mathcal{C}}A_{T}(j_{T})\right]\;\mid\;j_{T}\geq 1,\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{C}}j_{T}=n+1-k_{\mathcal{C}}\ \text{ and }\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\left.\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{D}}j_{T}>k_{\mathcal{D}}-k_{\mathcal{C}}\text{ for any subset }\varnothing\neq\mathcal{D}\subsetneqq\mathcal{C}\right\}.

Proof.

Fix a subset
\mathcal{D}\neq\varnothing
with
\mathcal{D}\neq\mathcal{C}
and consider the set
\mathcal{U}
of antichains
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subseteq\left\langle\mathcal{C}\right\rangle_{\text{lower}}-\{\varnothing,\mathcal{C}\}
with
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{C}\cap\langle\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\rangle
. Note that
\mathcal{D}
is in
\mathcal{U}
. Furthermore, for any
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}
, one has
\mathcal{C}-\left\langle\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\right\rangle=\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\left\langle\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\right\rangle
. The latter containment gives
\left\langle\mathcal{D}\right\rangle\subseteq\left\langle\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\right\rangle
, which implies

k_{\mathcal{D}}\geq k_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
. Hence, for
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}
, the condition
\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{C}-\left\langle\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\right\rangle}j_{T}>k_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}-k_{\mathcal{C}}
is strongest if
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\mathcal{D}
.

It remains to show that
\operatorname{Supp}(\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in\mathcal{C}}A_{T}(j_{T})\right])=\mathcal{C}
for all
\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in\mathcal{C}}A_{T}(j_{T})\right]\in M\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(n)
. If
j_{T}=0
for some
T\in\mathcal{C}
, then, setting
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{C}-\{T\}
, we have

\sumop\displaylimits_{S\in\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{D}}j_{S}=0
on the one side and
k_{\mathcal{D}}-k_{\mathcal{C}}\geq 0
on the other side, which is a contradiction to the second condition.
∎

6. The General Case

Given any
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant chain
(I_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of squarefree monomial ideals, we produce a generating set of the Alexander dual
I_{n}^{\vee}
for any
n\gg 0
. We proceed in several steps. Using the results of the previous section, we first obtain a rather redundant generating set. In a second step, we trim down this set by identifying additional properties of any minimal generator of
I_{n}^{\vee}
. Since the resulting generating set is still not necessarily minimal, we need to adjust it to achieve minimality.

Notation 6.1
.

Throughout this section, fix positive integers
c
and
m
as well as
0-1
-matrices
A_{1},\ldots,A_{s}
of the form

A_{i}=\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{\varnothing\neq T\in 2^{[c]}}A_{T}(k_{T}^{(i)})\right]

with
c
rows and at most
m
columns, where
k_{T}^{(i)}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Furthermore, let
(I_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant chain of ideals generated by
\mathbf{x}^{A_{1}},\dots,\mathbf{x}^{A_{s}}
, that is,
I_{n}=0
if
n<m
and

I_{n}=\langle\operatorname{Sym}(n)\cdot(\mathbf{x}^{A_{1}},\dots,\mathbf{x}^{A_{s}})\rangle\subset R_{n}=\mathbf{k}[x_{i,j}\mid i\in[c],j\in[n]]
if
n\geq m
.
When thinking of
A_{i}
as an exponent matrix of a monomial in
R_{n}
, we abuse notation and follow our convention by considering
A_{i}
as a
c\times n
matrix padded with zero columns, that is, we use

A_{i}=\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in 2^{[c]}}A_{T}(k_{T}^{(i)})\right]\in\{0,1\}^{c\times n}

with
k_{\varnothing}^{(i)}=n-\sumop\displaylimits_{\varnothing\neq T\in 2^{[c]}}k_{T}^{(i)}
.

We now outline a construction that produces a generating set of
I_{n}^{\vee}
.

Construction 6.2
.

Let
n\geq m
and consider a matrix

B=\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in 2^{[c]}}A_{T}(\ell_{T})\right]\in\{0,1\}^{c\times n}

with
\ell_{T}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Using the fact that
I_{n}^{\vee}=\bigcapop\displaylimits_{i\in[s]}\left\langle\operatorname{Sym}(n)\cdot\mathbf{x}^{A_{i}}\right\rangle^{\vee}
and the Alexander Dual Membership Criterion, it follows that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{x}^{B}}
is in
I_{n}^{\vee}
if and only if for every
i\in[s]
, there is an order ideal
\varnothing\neq J_{i}\subsetneqq 2^{[c]}
with

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J_{i}}k_{T}^{(i)}>\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J_{i}}\ell_{T^{C}}.

Hence, to obtain a generating set of
I_{n}^{\vee}
, fix any
s
-tuple
\mathbf{J}=(J_{1},\dots,J_{s})
of proper order ideals of
2^{[c]}
, and observe that it determines a set of monomials
\mathbf{x}^{B}\in I_{n}^{\vee}
satisfying the system of inequalities

(12)

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J_{i}}k_{T}^{(i)}>\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in J_{i}}\ell_{T^{C}}=\sumop\displaylimits_{S\in\overline{J}_{i}}\ell_{S},\quad i\in[s].

Define
\mathcal{R}\coloneqq\overline{J}_{1}\cup\dots\cup\overline{J}_{s}
.
Now, fix a solution
L=(\ell_{S}\;\mid\;S\in\mathcal{R})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{|\mathcal{R}|}
of the system of inequalities (
12
). In order to describe a matrix
B
with
\mathbf{x}^{B}\in I_{n}^{\vee}
, it remains to determine the columns of
B
whose support is not in
\mathcal{R}
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{J}}
be the antichain generating
2^{[c]}-\mathcal{R}
which is an order ideal by
Lemma

2.5
. By a similar argument as in the proof of part (a) of the Exchange
Lemma

4.13
, it follows that for the description of a minimal generating set of
I_{n}^{\vee}
it suffices to consider matrices
B
whose columns (with support not in
\mathcal{R}
) are supported on minimal elements of
2^{[c]}-\mathcal{R}
, i.e.,
\ell_{T}=0
if
T\notin\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{J}}
.

The following result is now immediate.

Proposition 6.3
.

Adopt the notation and hypotheses of Construction
6.2
.
For
n\geq m
, the ideal
I_{n}^{\vee}
is generated by the
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
-orbits of monomials
\mathbf{x}^{B}
with
B
in the set

(13)

\bigcupop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{J}=(J_{1},\dots,J_{s})\\
\varnothing\neq J_{i}\subsetneqq 2^{[c]}\end{subarray}}\left\{\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{S\in\mathcal{R}}A_{S}(\ell_{S})\right]\ast\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{S\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{J}}}A_{S}(\ell_{S})\right]\;\;\mid\;\;\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{S}\geq 0,\ L=(\ell_{S}\;\mid\;S\in\mathcal{R})\text{ a solution of }(\ref{eq: generalIneqs}),\\
\sumop\displaylimits_{S\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{J}}}\ell_{S}=n-\sumop\displaylimits_{S\in\mathcal{R}}\ell_{S}\end{subarray}\right\}.

The above generating set is typically not minimal. The following example gives a hint on how to trim down the generating set.

Example 6.4
.

Fix matrices

A_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&1\\

1&1&1\\

and consider the ideal

. For order ideals
J_{1}=\langle 12,13\rangle
and
J_{2}=\langle 12,23\rangle
form the tuple
\mathbf{J}=(J_{1},J_{2})
. Here, we omit commas and parentheses around and between elements of a set. In this case,

and

is generated by the antichain

.
The system of equations corresponding to Equation (

\displaystyle k_{12}^{(1)}+k_{13}^{(1)}+k_{123}^{(1)}=3

\displaystyle>\ell_{3}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{\varnothing}

\displaystyle k_{12}^{(2)}+k_{23}^{(2)}+k_{123}^{(2)}=3

\displaystyle>\ell_{3}+\ell_{1}+\ell_{\varnothing}.

In order to avoid exponent matrices from (

equal to

. This forces

and

\sumop\displaylimits_{S\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{J}}}\ell_{S}=\ell_{12}+\ell_{13}+\ell_{23}=n-\sumop\displaylimits_{S\in\mathcal{R}}\ell_{S}=n-2.

For instance, in the case where

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}0&0&1&1\\

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}0&0&1&0\\

We need some notation in order to identify non-minimal generators of

.

Notation 6.5
.

For any
s
-tuple
\mathbf{J}=(J_{1},\dots,J_{s})
of proper order ideals of

and any

\bigcapop\displaylimits_{k\in\Lambda}\overline{J}_{k}-\bigcupop\displaylimits_{k\in[s]-\Lambda}\overline{J}_{k}

Remark 6.6
.

Note that

and

is the antichain generating the order ideal

.

The following result is a first pass at a description of the minimal generators of

.

Lemma 6.7

(Restriction Lemma)
.

If
\mathbf{x}^{B}
is a minimal generator of
I_{n}^{\vee}
, then
B
is of the form

B=\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{S\in F}A_{S}(\ell_{S})\right]\ast\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{S\in\mathcal{C}}A_{S}(\ell_{S})\right]\in\{0,1\}^{c\times n},

where:

(1)

\mathcal{C}=D_{\mathbf{J},\varnothing}
for some
s
-tuple of proper order ideals
\mathbf{J}
;

(2)

\displaystyle{F\subseteq\sqcup_{\varnothing\neq\Lambda\subseteq[s]}D_{\mathbf{J},\Lambda}}
;

(3)

F\cap\langle\mathcal{C}\rangle=\varnothing
;

(4)

F\subset\operatorname{Supp}B
, i.e.,
\ell_{S}>0
if
S\in F
; and

(5)

setting
\ell_{S}=0
for any
S\in\mathcal{R}-F
, the
|\mathcal{R}|
-tuple
(\ell_{S}:S\in\mathcal{R})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{|\mathcal{R}|}
is a solution of the system of inequalities (
12
).

Proof.

Proposition
6.3
implies Properties
(i)
and
(v)
as well as
\operatorname{Supp}B\subseteq\mathcal{R}\cup\mathcal{C}
.
Next we establish Property
(ii)
. Note that

\mathcal{R}=\overline{J}_{1}\cup\dots\cup\overline{J}_{s}=\bigsqcupop\displaylimits_{\varnothing\neq\Lambda\subseteq[s]}\left(\bigcapop\displaylimits_{k\in\Lambda}\overline{J}_{k}-\bigcupop\displaylimits_{k\in[s]-\Lambda}\overline{J}_{k}\right).

By a similar argument as in the proof of part (a) of the Exchange Lemma
4.13
, we see that
\operatorname{Supp}B\subseteq F\sqcup\mathcal{C}
for some

. As making
F

Property (iii) follows from the following chain of containments

∎

Notation 6.8
.

Recall the definition of

from Notation
6.5
. Denote by

the set of pairs

•

, and

Definition 6.9
.

For any integer

and any pair

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{C}}(n)

\displaystyle=\left\{\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{S\in F}A_{S}(\ell_{S})\right]\ast\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{S\in\mathcal{C}}A_{S}(\ell_{S})\right]\;\mid\;\sumop\displaylimits_{S\in\mathcal{C}}\ell_{S}=n-\sumop\displaylimits_{S\in F}\ell_{S}\text{ and }\ell_{S}\geq 0\text{ if }S\in\mathcal{C}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{M_{\mathcal{F}}\ast\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{S\in\mathcal{C}}A_{S}(\ell_{S})\right]\;\mid\;\sumop\displaylimits_{S\in\mathcal{C}}\ell_{S}=n-\sumop\displaylimits_{S\in F}\ell_{S}\text{ and }\ell_{S}\geq 0\text{ if }S\in\mathcal{C}\right\},

Some observations are in order.

.

(1)

By definition, the support of the matrix

is
F
. Sometimes we will refer to

.

(2)

.

Corollary 6.11
.

For
n\geq m
, the ideal
I_{n}^{\vee}
is generated by the
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
-orbits of monomials
\mathbf{x}^{B}
with

in the set

\bigcupop\displaylimits_{(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{C})\in\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{C}}(n)
.

Proof.

The given set is obtained from the generating set in

7. Cone Decompositions

We consider a particular class of polyhedra. Their characteristic feature is that the bounding hyperplanes are defined by conditions on sums of variables. Our main result is that any such polyhedron can be decomposed as union of pointed rational cones. Thus, Ehrhart theory can be applied to study the number of integer points of the polyhedron.

Notation 7.1
.

Given any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, fix
\mathbf{a}=(a_{T}~\mid~T\subseteq[k])\in\mathbb{Z}^{2^{[k]}-\{\varnothing\}}
and
\mathbf{b}=(b_{T}~\mid~:T\in U)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2^{|U|}}
for some fixed
U\subseteq 2^{[k]}
. Define a polyhedron
P_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}
as the set

(15)

P_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}:=\left\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}~\mid~\sumop\displaylimits_{i\in T}x_{i}\geq a_{T}\text{ for all }\varnothing\neq T\subseteq[k],\sumop\displaylimits_{i\in T}x_{i}\leq b_{T}\text{ for all }T\in U\right\}.

If
U=\emptyset
, we write

for short.

The goal of this section is to determine the cardinality of the set of integer points of

Theorem 7.2
.

Adopt Notation

Proof of

.

We assume that

First consider the case that

We prove the statement by induction on

has the form

by their

P_{\mathbf{a}}^{(i)}\coloneqq\left\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}~\mid~x_{k}=i,\sumop\displaylimits_{j\in T}x_{j}\geq\max(a_{T},a_{T\cup\{i\}}-i)\text{ for all }\varnothing\neq T\subseteq[k-1]\right\}.

Note that

is simply the intersection of

with the plane

, since the inequalities from

Now assume that

for all

We then have

Let

and without loss of generality assume

for some

. If

. In particular, it is finite and there is nothing to show. Assume

.

If

Proof.

. For each orthant

, the integer points in the intersection of

with the hyperplane

The goal of this section is to study the number of minimal generators of Alexander duals to any

We continue to use the notation from Section

For counting the number of elements of

Lemma 8.4
.

Consider
(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{C})\neq(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
in
\mathcal{Q}
. Set

M_{\mathcal{F}}=\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in F}A_{T}(j_{T})\right]\quad\text{and}\quad M_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}=\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in F^{\prime}}A_{T}(\ell_{T})\right].

Then one has that
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{C}}(n)\cap\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(n)\neq\varnothing
for all
n>\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in F}j_{T}+\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in F^{\prime}}\ell_{T}
if and only if the following three conditions are satisfied:

(1)

F\subseteq F^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
and
F^{\prime}\subseteq F\cup\mathcal{C}
;

(2)

If
T\in F\cap F^{\prime}
, then
j_{T}=\ell_{T}
; and

(3)

\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
.

In this case, we have
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{C}}(n)\cap\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(n)=\mathcal{G}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}},\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
,
where
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}:=(i_{T}~\mid~T\in F\cup F^{\prime})
with
i_{T}=j_{T}
if
T\in F
and
i_{T}=\ell_{T}
if
T\in F^{\prime}
.

We note that
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
is well-defined due to
(ii)
.

Proof.

For ease of notation, set
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}=\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.

First we show necessity of Conditions
(i)
–
(iii)
. Consider any matrix
B=\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in 2^{[c]}}A_{T}(i_{T})\right]\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{C}}(n)\cap\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(n)
. Then
F\cup F^{\prime}\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}B\subseteq(F\sqcup\mathcal{C})\cap(F^{\prime}\sqcup\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
, giving necessity of Condition
(i)
. We also have the following facts:

(1)

If
T\in F\cap F^{\prime}
, then
i_{T}=\ell_{T}=j_{T}
. This is exactly Condition
(ii)
.

(2)

If
T\in F\cap\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, then
i_{T}=j_{T}
,

(3)

If
T\in F^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{C}
, then
i_{T}=\ell_{T}
,

(4)

If
T\in\mathcal{C}-(F^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
or if
T\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}-(\mathcal{C}\cup F)
, then
i_{T}=0
, and so,

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}i_{T}=n-\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in F}j_{T}-\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in F^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{C}}\ell_{T}.

Since we assume
n>\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in F}j_{T}+\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in F^{\prime}}\ell_{T}\geq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in F}j_{T}+\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in F^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{C}}\ell_{T}
, Fact (d) forces
\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
. Moreover, it follows that
B
is in
\mathcal{G}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}},\tilde{\mathcal{C}}}(n)
.

To show sufficiency of
(i)
–
(iii)
, observe that Condition
(i)
implies

F\sqcup(F^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{C})=(F\cap F^{\prime})\sqcup(F\cap\mathcal{C}^{\prime})\sqcup(F^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{C})=F^{\prime}\sqcup(F\cap\mathcal{C}^{\prime}).

Together with Condition
(ii)
, this gives

M_{\mathcal{F}}\ast\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in F^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{C}}A_{T}(\ell_{T})\right]=M_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}\ast\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in F\cap\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}A_{T}(j_{T})\right].

It follows that
\mathcal{G}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}},\tilde{\mathcal{C}}}(n)
is contained in
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{C}}(n)\cap\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(n)
. Moreover,
\mathcal{G}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}},\tilde{\mathcal{C}}}(n)
is not empty if
n>\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in F}j_{T}+\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in F^{\prime}}\ell_{T}
.

Corollary 8.5
.

Consider any subset
U
of
\mathcal{P}
. If

, then the set

In the latter case,

is a polynomial in

of degree
-1+\#\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
, where

is the number of columns of the matrix
M_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}
.

Proof.

We use induction on
\#U
to prove the assertion and to prove that

has at most

columns.
If
U
is empty, there is nothing to show. Assume
U=\{(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{C})\}
. By the definition of
\mathcal{P}
, the pair
(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{C})
is determined by an

s
-tuple
\mathbf{J}=(J_{1},\dots,J_{s})
of proper order ideals of

. Since every matrix
A_{i}
has at most
m
nonzero columns, the System of Inequalities (

) shows that any solution

satisfies

has at most
s(m-1)
columns. This completes the argument if

Assume

In order to describe an arbitrary intersection as specified in

for the one-orbit case. It may also be used to identify redundancies in the generating set in

.

Lemma 8.6

(Membership Lemma)
.

Let
(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{C})\in\mathcal{Q}
be any pair, where
M_{\mathcal{F}}=\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in F}A_{T}(\ell_{T})\right]
(and so
F=\operatorname{Supp}(M_{\mathcal{F}})
).
For any integer
\lambda\geq 0
, set
n=\lambda+\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in F}\ell_{T}
and consider any
0-1
-matrix
B=\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in 2^{[c]}}A_{T}(j_{T})\right]
in
\mathbb{Z}^{c\times n}
.
Then
\mathbf{x}^{B}
is in the ideal
I_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{C}}(n)
if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied:

(1)

For every nonempty subset
E\subseteq F
with
\mathcal{C}\nsubseteq\langle E\rangle
, one has

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle E\rangle}j_{T}\geq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in F\cap\langle E\rangle}\ell_{T}.

(2)

For every subset
E\subseteq F\cup\mathcal{C}
with
\mathcal{C}\subseteq E
, one has

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle E\rangle}j_{T}\geq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle E\rangle}\ell_{T}=\lambda+\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in F\cap\langle E\rangle}\ell_{T}.

Proof.

Applying the Divisibility Lemma
4.11
to the case where
J=\langle E\rangle
gives
\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle E\rangle}j_{T}\geq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle E\rangle}\ell_{T}
, which shows
necessity of the conditions in
(i)
and
(ii)
.
Notice that the obtained inequality is stronger than the condition in
(i)
for the case where
E\subseteq F
.

To prove that Conditions
(i)
and
(ii)
are sufficient, we proceed by induction on
\lvert\mathcal{C}\rvert
. Assume that
\#\mathcal{C}=1
. By Lemma
4.11
, we have to show that
\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle E\rangle}j_{T}\geq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle E\rangle}\ell_{T}

for any nonempty subset
E
of
F\cup\mathcal{C}
.
We consider several cases. If
\mathcal{C}\subseteq E
, then the desired inequality is true by Condition (ii). Suppose
\mathcal{C}\nsubseteq E
.
Since
\#\mathcal{C}=1
and
E\subset F\cup\mathcal{C}
this implies
E\subseteq F
. If
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\langle E\rangle
, the divisibility condition of Lemma
4.11
for
J=\langle E\rangle
is the same as condition
(ii)
for
E\cup\mathcal{C}
.
If
\mathcal{C}\nsubseteq\langle E\rangle
, then the assumption
\#\mathcal{C}=1
implies that
\mathcal{C}\cap\langle E\rangle=\varnothing
, and so the divisibility condition for
J=\langle E\rangle
is true by Condition
(i)
.

Now suppose that
\#\mathcal{C}\geq 2
. Choose any
S\in\mathcal{C}
and set

\ell_{S}=\min\big\{\lambda,\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle S\rangle-\langle F\cup\mathcal{C}-S\rangle}j_{T}\big\}.

By the induction hypothesis, there is a fixed tuple
(\ell_{T})_{T\in\mathcal{C}-S}
such that
\mathbf{x}^{B}
is divisible by
\mathbf{x}^{\tilde{B}}
with

\tilde{B}=M_{F}\ast\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in\mathcal{C}-S}A_{T}(\ell_{T})\right],

where
\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{C}-S}\ell_{T}=\lambda-\ell_{S}
.
By Divisibility Lemma
4.11
, this means that for every subset
\varnothing\neq E\subseteq F\cup\mathcal{C}-S
, one has

(19)

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle E\rangle-S}\ell_{T}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle E\rangle}j_{T}.

We claim that
\mathbf{x}^{B}
is divisible by
\mathbf{x}^{B^{\prime}}
up to symmetry with

B^{\prime}=M_{\mathcal{F}}\ast\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in\mathcal{C}}A_{T}(\ell_{T})\right]=\tilde{B}\ast A_{S}(\ell_{S}),

that is, for every
\varnothing\neq E\subseteq F\cup\mathcal{C}
, one has

(20)

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle E\rangle}\ell_{T}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle E\rangle}j_{T}.

To this end we consider several cases.
If
S\in\langle F\cup\mathcal{C}-S\rangle
, then
\ell_{S}=0
by definition, and so
B^{\prime}=\tilde{B}
. Therefore, assume that
S\notin\langle F\cup\mathcal{C}-S\rangle
. For every subset
E\subset F\cup\mathcal{C}
with
S\notin E
, this implies that
S\notin\langle E\rangle
, so Inequality (
20
) follows from Inequality (
19
).

Consider now any subset
E\subseteq F\cup\mathcal{C}
with
S\in E
. By definition of
\ell_{S}
, one has

(21)

\ell_{S}\quad\leq\quad\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle S\rangle-\langle F\cup\mathcal{C}-S\rangle}j_{T}\quad\leq\quad\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle S\rangle-\langle E-S\rangle}j_{T}.

Moreover, Inequality (
19
) applied to
E-S
gives

(22)

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle E-S\rangle-S}\ell_{T}\;\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle E-S\rangle}j_{T}.

Notice the decomposition

(23)

\langle E\rangle=\langle E-S\rangle\sqcup(\langle S\rangle-\langle E-S\rangle).

Thus, adding Inequalities (
21
) and (
22
) yields

(24)

\ell_{S}+\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle E-S\rangle-S}\ell_{T}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle E\rangle}j_{T}.

Since we chose
S
in
\mathcal{C}
, we get
F\cap\langle S\rangle\subseteq F\cap\langle\mathcal{C}\rangle=\varnothing
by assumption, and so Equation (
23
) implies

(25)

F\cap\langle E\rangle=(F\cap\langle E-S\rangle)\sqcup(F\cap(\langle S\rangle-\langle E-S\rangle))=F\cap\langle E-S\rangle.

Using Equation (
23
) again, we obtain

\mathcal{C}\cap\langle E\rangle=(\mathcal{C}\cap\langle E-S\rangle)\sqcup\big(\mathcal{C}\cap(\langle S\rangle-\langle E-S)\rangle\big).

We also have that
\mathcal{C}\cap\big(\langle S\rangle-\langle E-S\rangle\big)\subseteq\mathcal{C}\cap\langle S\rangle=\{S\}
because
\mathcal{C}
is an antichain that contains
S
. It follows that

(\mathcal{C}\cap\langle E\rangle)-S\subseteq\mathcal{C}\cap\langle E-S\rangle,

which implies

(\mathcal{C}\cap\langle E\rangle)-S=(\mathcal{C}\cap\langle E-S\rangle)-S.

Combining this with Equation (
25
) and the fact that
F\cap\mathcal{C}=\varnothing
by assumption, and so
S\notin F
, we conclude

\displaystyle\big((F\cup\mathcal{C})\cap\langle E-S\rangle\big)-S

\displaystyle=(F\cap\langle E-S\rangle)\sqcup\big((\mathcal{C}\cap\langle E-S\rangle)-S.\big)

\displaystyle=(F\cap\langle E\rangle)\sqcup\big((\mathcal{C}\cap\langle E\rangle)-S\big)

\displaystyle=(F\cup\mathcal{C})\cap\langle E\rangle)-S.

This shows that

\ell_{S}+\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle E-S\rangle-S}\ell_{T}=\ell_{S}+\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle E\rangle-S}\ell_{T}=\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle E\rangle}\ell_{T}.

Hence, Inequality (
24
) gives

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle E\rangle}\ell_{T}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\langle E\rangle}j_{T},

which completes the argument.
∎

We now apply Membership
Lemma

8.6
to the case where the matrix
B
belongs to some set
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(n)
with
(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathcal{C}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{Q}
.

Corollary 8.7
.

Consider any pairs
(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{C}),(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathcal{C}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{Q}
with
M_{\mathcal{F}}=\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in F}A_{T}(j_{T})\right]
and
M_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}=\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in F^{\prime}}A_{T}(\ell_{T})\right]
.
Then a monomial
\mathbf{x}^{B}
with
B=M_{\mathcal{F}}\ast\left[\mathop{\scalebox{2}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{T\in\mathcal{C}}A_{T}(j_{T})\right]\in G_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{C}}(n)
belongs to the ideal
I_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(n)
if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied:

(1)

For every nonempty subset
E\subseteq F^{\prime}
with
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\nsubseteq\langle E\rangle
, one has

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{C}\cap\langle E\rangle}j_{T}\geq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in F^{\prime}\cap\langle E\rangle}\ell_{T}-\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in F\cap\langle E\rangle}j_{T}.

(2)

For any subset
E\subseteq F^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
with
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subseteq E

There are two cases to consider:

•

If
\varnothing\neq E\subseteq F^{\prime}
satisfies
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\nsubseteq\langle E\rangle
, then Condition (i) from Membership Lemma

gives

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in F\cap\langle E\rangle}j_{T}+\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in\mathcal{C}\cap\langle E\rangle}j_{T}\geq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in F^{\prime}\cap\langle E\rangle}\ell_{T}

which is equivalent to Condition (i) in the claim.

•

If
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subseteq E\subseteq F^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, then Condition (ii) in Membership Lemma
8.6
yields

(26)

\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in(F\cup\mathcal{C})\cap\langle E\rangle}j_{T}\geq\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in F^{\prime}\cap\langle E\rangle}\ell_{T}+n-\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in F^{\prime}}\ell_{T}=n-\sumop\displaylimits_{T\in F^{\prime}-\langle E\rangle}\ell_{T}

where the lefthand side is equal to

Therefore, Inequality (
26
) is equivalent to Condition (ii) in the claim.

Theorem 8.9
.

Consider nonzero ideals
I_{n}=\langle\operatorname{Sym}(n)(\mathbf{x}^{A_{1}},\ldots,\mathbf{x}^{A_{s}})\rangle
, where
A_{i}\in\{0,1\}^{c\times m}
for
1\leq i\leq s
and
n\geq m
.
The number of
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
-orbits needed to minimally generate
I_{n}^{\vee}
is a polynomial in
n
whose degree is at most

\binom{c}{\lfloor\frac{c}{2}\rfloor}-1
if
n
is sufficiently large.

Moreover, there is a linear function
d\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that, for
n\gg 0
, the least degree of a minimal generator of
I_{n}^{\vee}
equals
d(n)
. The number of
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
-orbits needed to produce the minimal generators of
I_{n}^{\vee}
of degree
d(n)
is given by a polynomial in
n
.

Proof.

In order to establish the first statement, using
Theorem

6.12
, we have to determine the cardinality of the set
\mathcal{G}(I_{n}^{\vee})
of matrices.
8.1
shows that
\#\mathcal{G}(I_{n}^{\vee})
is given by a polynomial in
n
for
n\gg 0
if this is true for the cardinality of any intersection
\bigcapop\displaylimits_{(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{C})\in U}\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{C}}(n)\cap\bigcapop\displaylimits_{(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathcal{C}^{\prime})\in V}I_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(n)
with subsets
U,V
of
\mathcal{P}
(see
Remark

8.2
). Fix
U
and
V
and assume that the corresponding intersection, denoted
\mathcal{I}
, is not empty. In this case,
8.8
shows that
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{G}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}},\tilde{\mathcal{C}}}(n)\cap\bigcapop\displaylimits_{(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathcal{C}^{\prime})\in V}I_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(n)
for some

(\tilde{\mathcal{F}},\tilde{\mathcal{C}})\in\mathcal{Q}

9. An application to face numbers

The goal of this section is to provide an application of one of our main results,
Theorem

8.9
, to chains of simplicial complexes. More precisely, given a
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant chain
(I_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of squarefree monomial ideals
I_{n}\subseteq R_{n}=\mathbf{k}[x_{i,j}~\mid~i\in[c],j\in[n]]
, we are interested in the face numbers of the associated Stanley-Reisner complexes
(\Delta(I_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Since by
3.9

\Delta(I_{n})
is
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
-invariant, for every
0\leq i\leq\dim\Delta(n)-1
there exist finitely many
i
-dimensional faces
F_{1},\ldots,F_{t}
of
\Delta(I_{n})
such that
\operatorname{Sym}(n)\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{t}\}
equals the set of all
i
-faces of
\Delta(I_{n})
, i.e., the set of
i
-faces of
\Delta(I_{n})
consists of only finitely many
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
-orbits. In the following, we let
f_{i}(n)^{\operatorname{Sym}}
denote this number. Our goal is to prove that
f_{i}(n)^{\operatorname{Sym}(n)}
is a polynomial in
n
for
n
sufficiently large.

Since the chain
(I_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
stabilizes, as in the previous sections, we can assume that
I_{n}=(0)
for
n\leq m
and
I_{n}=\operatorname{Sym}(n)\cdot\langle\mathbf{x}^{A_{1}},\ldots,\mathbf{x}^{A_{s}}\rangle
for
n\geq m
, where
A_{1},\ldots,A_{s}
are
0-1
-matrices of size at most
c\times m
.

The following lemma will be crucial.

Lemma 9.1
.

Fix
j\geq 0
. Then the number of
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
-orbits of facets of dimension
j
in
\Delta(I_{n})
is eventually constant.

Proof.

Let
n\geq\max(m,j+1)
and let
F
be a facet of
\Delta(I_{n+1})
with
\dim F=j
. Then, by
3.6

\frac{\textbf{X}}{F}\in I_{n+1}^{\vee}
. Let
A
be the exponent matrix of
\frac{\textbf{X}}{F}
. It has
c(n+1)-(j+1)
entries equal to
0
. Since
n\geq j+1
, there has to exist a column of
A
with support
[c]
. We can further assume that this is the last column of
A
. Let
B
be the matrix obtained from
A
by removing the last column. Then
3.7
implies that
\mathbf{x}^{B}\in I_{n}^{\vee}
, i.e.,
F\in\Delta(I_{n})
. It remains to show that
F
is a facet of
\Delta(I_{n})
. Otherwise, there exists some
G\in\Delta(I_{n})
with
F\subsetneq G
. By
Lemma

3.14
it follows that
G\in\Delta(I_{n+1})
, which yields a contradiction.
∎

We now formulate our main result of this section.

Theorem 9.2
.

Given a
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant chain of squarefree monomial ideals
(I_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and fixing an integer
j\geq 0
, the number

of
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
-orbits that form the
j
-faces of
\Delta(I_{n})
is a polynomial in
n
when
n
is sufficiently large.

Proof.

We first assume that
\Delta(I_{n})
is pure for
n\gg 0
.
We denote by
\Delta^{(j)}(I_{n})
the
j
-skeleton of
\Delta(I_{n})
, i.e., the simplicial complex consisting of all faces of
\Delta(I_{n})
of dimension at most
j
. Algebraically, this means that
I_{\Delta^{(j)}(I_{n})}=I_{\Delta(I_{n})}+J_{j,n}
, where
J_{j,n}\subset R_{n}
is the ideal generated by all squarefree monomials of degree
j+2
. It is easy to see that
(J_{j,n})_{n\geq j+2}
is a
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant chain, hence so is
(I_{n}+J_{j,n})_{n\geq\max(m,j+2)}
. The claim follows by combining
3.6
and
Theorem

8.9
.

Now assume that
\Delta(I_{n})
is not pure eventually. As above, the simplicial complex
\Delta(I_{n}+J_{j,n})
consists of all faces of
\Delta(I_{n})
of dimension at most
j
. However, since
\Delta(I_{n})
need not be pure, there may be facets in
\Delta(I_{n})
and hence of
\Delta(I_{n}+J_{j,n})
of dimension strictly less than
j
. By Lemma
9.1
, we can choose
N
large enough so that for all
i<j
, the number of facets of
\Delta(I_{n})
of dimension
i
is constant whenever
n\geq N
.

Let
i=\max\left\{\dim(F)~\mid~F\text{ a facet of }\Delta(I_{n})\text{ of dimension}<j\right\}
.
Let

K_{n}=I_{n}+J_{j,n}+\langle\mathbf{x}^{F}~\mid~F\in\Delta(I_{n})\text{ facet},\;\dim F=i\rangle.

It is easy to see that
(K_{n})_{n\geq N}
is a
\operatorname{Sym}
-invariant chain of ideals.

We may repeat the process of replacing

with
K_{n}
, resulting in a removal of facets of dimension
i^{\prime}
for some fixed
i^{\prime}<i
. This process will terminate and
\Delta(K_{n})
will have no facets of dimension
<j
and in particular will be pure. Now the claim follows from the pure case.
∎

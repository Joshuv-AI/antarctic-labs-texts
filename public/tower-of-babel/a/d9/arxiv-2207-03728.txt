Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the Laguerre bounded variation space and the Laguerre perimeter, thereby investigating their properties. Moreover, we prove the isoperimetric inequality and the Sobolev inequality in the Laguerre setting. As applications, we derive the mean curvature for the Laguerre perimeter.

The spaces BV of functions of bounded variation in Euclidean spaces
have been a classical setting now where several problems, mainly
(but not exclusively) of variational nature, find their nature
framework. For instance, when working with minimization problems,
reflexivity or the weak compactness property of the function space

W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
p>1
, the space BV usually plays an
important role. For the case of the space
W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, one
possible way to deal with this lack of reflexivity is to consider
the space
BV(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. However, the importance of generalizing the
classical notion of variation has been pointed out in several
occasions by E. De. Giorgi in
[
4
]
. Recently, Huang, Li and
Liu in
[
11
]
investigate the capacity and perimeters derived
from
\alpha
-Hermite bounded variation. In a general framework of
strictly local Dirichlet spaces with doubling measure, Alonso-Ruiz,
Baudoin and Chen et al. in
[
1
]
introduce the
class of bounded variation functions and proved the Sobolev
inequality under the Bakry-
\acute{\text{E}}
mery curvature type
condition. For further information on this topic, we refer the
reader to
[
12

6

13
]
and the references therein.

One of the aims of this paper is intended to discuss several basic
questions of geometric measure theory related to the Laguerre
operator in Laguerre BV spaces. At first, we will present a very
short introduction to the Laguerre operator.

Given a multiindex
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{d})
,
\alpha\in(-1,\infty)^{d}
, the Laguerre differential operator is defined by:

\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}=-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Big[x_{i}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{x_{i}}^{2}}+(\alpha_{i}+1-x_{i})\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{i}}}\Big].

Consider the probabilistic gamma measure
\mu_{\alpha}
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}=(0,\infty)^{d}
given by

d\mu_{\alpha}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\frac{{x_{i}}^{\alpha_{i}}e^{-x_{i}}}{\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+1)}dx:=\omega(x)dx.

It is well-known that
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
is positive and symmetric in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},d\mu_{\alpha})
. Moreover,
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
has a closure which is selfadjoint in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},d\mu_{\alpha})
and which also will be denoted by
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
.
We define the
i
-th partial derivative associated with
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
by

\delta_{i}=\sqrt{x_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{i}}},

see
[
9
]
or
[
10
]
. One of the motivations of such
definition is that

\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\delta_{i}^{*}\delta_{i},

where

\delta_{i}^{*}=-\sqrt{x_{i}}\Big(\partial{x_{i}}+\frac{\alpha_{i}+\frac{1}{2}-x_{i}}{x_{i}}\Big)

is the formal disjoint of
\delta_{i}
in

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha})
. Throughout this
paper, we always assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
be an open set. For

u\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})
and
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\ldots,\varphi_{d})\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we introduce the
following
{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}
-gradient operator and
{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}
-divergence operator associated to

\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}u&:=(\delta_{1}u,\ldots,\delta_{d}u),\\
\mathrm{div}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\varphi&:=\delta^{*}_{1}{\varphi_{1}}+\delta^{*}_{2}{\varphi_{2}}+\cdots+\delta^{*}_{d}{\varphi_{d}},\end{aligned}\right.

which also gives

\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}u=\mathrm{div_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}}(\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}u)=-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Big[x_{i}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{x_{i}}^{2}}+(\alpha_{i}+1-x_{i})\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{i}}}\Big].

-BV in short) on

2.
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
-BV functions

2.1.
Fundamentals of
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
-BV Space

In this section, we introduce the
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
-BV space, i.e. the class of
all functions with the Laguerre bounded variation and investigate
its properties. The Laguerre variation (
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
-variation in short) of

f\in{L^{1}}(\Omega,d\mu_{\alpha})
is defined by

|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f|(\Omega)=\mathop{\sup}\limits_{\varphi\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega)}\left\{\int_{\Omega}f(x)\mathrm{{div}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}}\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right\},

where
{\mathcal{F}}(\Omega)
denotes the class of all
functions

\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\ldots,\varphi_{d})\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})

satisfying

\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}=\mathop{\sup}\limits_{x\in\Omega}\Big\{(|\varphi_{1}(x)|^{2}+\ldots+|\varphi_{d}(x)|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\}\leq 1.

An function
f\in{L^{1}}(\Omega,d\mu_{\alpha})
is said to have the
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
-bounded variation on
\Omega
if

|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f|(\Omega)<\infty,

and the collection of all such functions is
denoted by
{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\Omega)
, which is a Banach spaces with the norm

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\Omega)}=\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega,d\mu_{\alpha})}+|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f|(\Omega).

Definition
.

Suppose
\Omega
is an open set in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
.
Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. The Sobolev space
W_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}^{k,p}(\Omega)
associated with
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
is defined as the set of all functions
f\in{L^{p}}(\Omega,d\mu_{\alpha})
such that

\delta_{j_{1}}\ldots\delta_{j_{m}}f\in{L^{p}}(\Omega,d\mu_{\alpha}),\ 1\leq{j_{1}},\ldots,{j_{m}}\leq d,\ 1\leq m\leq k.

The norm of
f\in W_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}^{k,p}(\Omega)
is given by

\|f\|_{W_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}^{k,p}}:=\sum\limits_{1\leq{j_{1}}\ldots{j_{m}}\leq d,\ 1\leq m\leq k}{\|{\delta_{j_{1}}}\ldots\delta_{j_{m}}f\|}_{L^{p}(\Omega,d\mu_{\alpha})}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,d\mu_{\alpha})}.

In what follows, we will collect some properties of the space

\mathcal{BV}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(i)
Suppose
f\in W_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}^{1,1}(\Omega)
, then

|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f|(\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f(x)|d\mu_{\alpha}(x),

which implies
W_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}^{1,1}(\Omega)\subseteq\mathcal{BV}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\Omega).

(ii)
(Lower semicontinuity). Suppose
f_{k}\in{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\Omega),\ k\in\mathbb{N}
and

f_{k}\to f
in
L_{loc}^{1}(\Omega,d\mu_{\alpha})
, then

|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f|(\Omega)\leq\mathop{\lim\inf}\limits_{k\to\infty}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f_{k}|(\Omega).

Proof.

(i) For every
\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
with

\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}f(x)\mathrm{div}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f(x)\cdot\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right|\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f(x)|d\mu_{\alpha}(x).

By taking the supremum over
\varphi
, it is obvious that

|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f|(\Omega)\leq\int_{\Omega}{|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f(x)|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)}.

Define
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
as follows:

(4)

\varphi(x):=\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f(x)}{|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f(x)|},\ &\text{if $x\in\Omega$ and $\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f(x)\neq 0$,}\\
0,\ &\text{otherwise.}\end{aligned}\right.

It is easy to see that
\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1
.
Moreover, we can obtain the approximating smooth fields
{\varphi_{n}}:=(\varphi_{n,1},\ldots,\varphi_{n,d})
such that

\varphi_{n}\to\varphi
pointwise as
n\to\infty
, with

\|\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Combining the definition of
|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f|(\Omega)
with
integration by parts derives that for every
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f|(\Omega)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}f(x)\mathrm{div}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}{\varphi_{n}}(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f(x)(\delta_{1}^{*}\varphi_{n,1}(x)+\cdots+\delta_{d}^{*}\varphi_{n,d}(x))d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f(x)\cdot{\varphi_{n}}(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x).

Using the dominated convergence theorem and the definition of

\varphi
in (
4
), we have

|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f|(\Omega)\geq\int_{\Omega}{|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f(x)|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)}

by letting
n\to\infty
.

(ii) Fix
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1
. We use the definition of

|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f_{k}|(\Omega)
to obtain

|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}{f_{k}}|(\Omega)\geq\int_{\Omega}{f_{k}}(x)\mathrm{div}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x).

The convergence of
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in

L_{loc}^{1}(\Omega,d\mu_{\alpha})
to
f
implies that

\mathop{\lim\inf}\limits_{k\to\infty}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}{f_{k}}|(\Omega)\geq\int_{\Omega}f(x)\mathrm{div}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x).

Therefore, (ii) can be proved by the definition of
|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f|(\Omega)
and the arbitrariness of such
functions
\varphi
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

The space
\big({\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\Omega),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\Omega)}\big)
is a Banach space.

Proof.

It is easy to check that
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{BV_{k}(\Omega)}
is a norm and we omit the details. In what follows,
we prove the completeness of
{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\Omega)
. Let
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\Omega)
be a Cauchy sequence, namely, for
every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\forall n,m\geq n_{0}
, we have

\left|\nabla_{{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}}\left(f_{m}-f_{n}\right)\right|\left(\Omega\right)<\varepsilon.

Especially,
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in the Banach space

\big(L^{1}(\Omega,d\mu_{\alpha}),\|\cdot\|_{L^{1}(\Omega,d\mu_{\alpha})}\big),

which
implies that there exists
f\in L^{1}\left(\Omega,d\mu_{\alpha}\right)
with

\left\|f_{n}-f\right\|_{L^{1}\left(\Omega,d\mu_{\alpha}\right)}\to 0
as

n\to\infty
. Hence, via Lemma
2.1
(ii), we have

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(f-f_{m})\right|(\Omega)\leq\liminf_{n}\left|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(f_{m}-f_{n})\right|(\Omega)\leq\varepsilon,\ (\forall m\geq n_{0})

which implies that
\left|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(f_{m}-f)\right|\left(\Omega\right)\to 0
as
m\to\infty
. This completes the proof.
∎

The following lemma gives the structure theorem for
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
-BV functions and it can be proved by the Hahn-Banach theorem and the Riesz representation theorem.

Lemma 2.3
.

(Structure Theorem for
\mathcal{BV}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}
functions). Let
f\in{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\Omega)
. Then there exists a Radon measure
\mu_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}
on
\Omega
such that

\int_{\Omega}f(x)\mathrm{div}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\cdot d\mu_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(x)

for every
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})

and

|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f|(\Omega)=|\mu_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}|(\Omega),

where
|\mu_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}|
is the total variation of the measure
\mu_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that

\left|\int_{\Omega}f(x)\mathrm{div}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right|\leq|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f|(\Omega)\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\ \forall\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Denote by the functional
\Phi
with

\Phi:C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R},

where

\left\langle\Phi,\varphi\right\rangle:=\int_{\Omega}f(x)\mathrm{div}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x).

Then the Hahn-Banach theorem derives that there exists a linear and
continuous extension
L
of
\Phi
to the normed space
\big(C_{c}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\big)
such that

\left\|L\right\|=\left\|\Phi\right\|=|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f|\left(\Omega\right).

By the Riesz representation Theorem (cf.
[
2
, Corollary
1.55]
), there exists a unique
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued finite
Radon measure
\mu_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}
with

L(\varphi)=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\cdot d\mu_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(x),\ \forall\varphi\in C_{c}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})

and so that
|\mu_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}|(\Omega)=\|L\|.
Thus we have

|\mu_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}|(\Omega)=|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f|(\Omega)
, which completes
the proof.
∎

In the following theorem, we can obtain the approximation
result for the
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
-variation.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\Omega_{1}
be an open set defined in (
3
).
Assume that
u\in{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\Omega_{1})
, then there exists a sequence

\{u_{h}\}_{h\in\mathbb{N}}\in{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\Omega_{1})\cap C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})
such that

\mathop{\lim}\limits_{h\to\infty}\|u_{h}-u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{1},d\mu_{\alpha})}=0

and

\mathop{\lim}\limits_{h\to\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}u_{h}(x)|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)=|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}u|(\Omega_{1}).

Proof.

We adapt the method of the proof in
[
7
, Section 5.2.2, Theorem
2]
, but different from its proof,
we need to use the mean value
theorem of multivariate functions and the intrinsic nature of the

\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
-variation. Via the lower semicontinuity of
{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}
-BV functions, we only need to show that for

\varepsilon>0
, there exists a function
u_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})
such that

\int_{\Omega_{1}}|{u_{\varepsilon}}(x)-u(x)|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)<\varepsilon

and

|{\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}}u_{\varepsilon}|(\Omega_{1})\leq|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}u|(\Omega_{1})+\varepsilon.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Given a positive integer
m
, define a sequence of open sets,

{\Omega_{1,j}}:=\Big\{x\in\Omega_{1}:\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial\Omega_{1})>\frac{1}{m+j}\Big\}\cap B(0,m+j),\ j\in\mathbb{N},

where
\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial\Omega_{1})=\inf\{|x-y|:y\in\partial\Omega_{1}\}
. Note that
{\Omega_{1,j}}\subset{\Omega_{1,j+1}}\subset\Omega_{1},\ j\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathop{\cup}\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}{\Omega_{1,j}}=\Omega_{1}
. Since
|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}u|(\cdot)
is a measure, then choose a

m\in\mathbb{N}
so large such that

(5)

|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}u|(\Omega_{1}\backslash\Omega_{1,0})<\varepsilon.

Set
{U_{0}}:=\Omega_{1,0}
and
U_{j}:=\Omega_{1,j+1}\backslash{\overline{\Omega}}_{1,j-1}
for
j\geq 1
. By standard results from
[
7
, Section 5.2.2, Theorem 2]
, we conclude that there is a partition of unity associated to the covering
\{U_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
. Namely, there exist functions
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U_{j})
such that
0\leq f_{j}\leq 1,\ j\geq 0
and
\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}f_{j}=1
on

\Omega_{1}
.
Thus we have the fact that

(6)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f_{j}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sqrt{x_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{1}}}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}f_{j}\Big),\sqrt{x_{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{2}}}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}f_{j}\Big),\cdots,\sqrt{x_{d}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{d}}}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}f_{j}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=0

on
\Omega_{1}
. Given
\varepsilon>0
and
u\in{L^{1}}(\Omega_{1},\mathbb{R})
, extended to zero out of
\Omega_{1}
, we define the
following regularization

{u_{\varepsilon}}(x):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{B(x,\varepsilon)}\eta\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}\Big)u(y)d\mu_{\alpha}(y),

where
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})
is a
nonnegative radial function satisfying

\frac{1}{\epsilon_{j}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\eta\big(\frac{x-y}{\epsilon_{j}}\big)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)=1,\ \forall\ j\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\text{supp}\ \eta\subset B(0,1)\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
. Then for
each
j
, there exists
0<\varepsilon_{j}<\varepsilon
so small such that

(7)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \hskip-14.22636pt{\rm{supp}}({({{f_{j}}u})}_{\varepsilon_{j}})\subseteq{U_{j}},\\
\int_{\Omega_{1}}|({f_{j}}u)_{\varepsilon_{j}}(x)-{f_{j}}u(x)|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)<\varepsilon 2^{-(j+1)},\\
\;\int_{\Omega_{1}}|(u\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}{f_{j}})_{\varepsilon_{j}}(x)-u\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}{f_{j}}(x)|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)<\varepsilon 2^{-(j+1)}.\end{aligned}\right.

Construct

{v_{\varepsilon}}(x):=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}{(u{f_{j}})}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(x).

In some neighborhood of each point
x\in\Omega_{1}
, there are only finitely many nonzero terms in this sum, hence
v_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})
and
u=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}{u{f_{j}}}
. Therefore, by a simple computation, we obtain

\|v_{\varepsilon}-u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{1},d{\mu}_{\alpha})}\leq\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}|{({f_{j}}u)}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(x)-{f_{j}}(x)u(x)|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)<\varepsilon.

Consequently,

v_{\varepsilon}\to u\ \ \mathrm{in}\ \ {L^{1}}(\Omega_{1},d\mu_{\alpha})\ \ \mathrm{as}\ \ \varepsilon\to 0.

Now, assume

and

|\varphi|\leq 1
. We decompose the integral as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{1}}v_{\varepsilon}(x)\mathrm{div}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}}\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{1}}\Big(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}{(u{f_{j}})}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(x)\Big)\mathrm{div}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}}\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}{(u{f_{j}})}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(x)\Big({\delta^{*}_{1}}{\varphi_{1}}(x)+{\delta^{*}_{2}}{\varphi_{2}}(x)+\cdots+{\delta^{*}_{d}}{\varphi_{d}}(x)\Big)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

where

\left\{\begin{aligned} I&:=-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}{(u{f_{j}})}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(x)\Big(\sqrt{x_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{1}}}{\varphi_{1}}(x)+\cdots+\sqrt{x_{d}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{d}}{\varphi_{d}}(x)\Big)d\mu_{\alpha}(x),\\

For the sake of research, let

(8)

\mathrm{\widetilde{div}}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\varphi=\delta_{1}{\varphi_{1}}+\delta_{2}{\varphi_{2}}+\cdots+\delta_{d}{\varphi_{d}}.

As for

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}{(u{f_{j}})}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(x)\mathrm{\widetilde{div}}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}}\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}(u{f_{j}})(y)\mathrm{\widetilde{div}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}}}(\eta_{\varepsilon_{j}}*\varphi(y))d\mu_{\alpha}(y)

\displaystyle=-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}u(y)\mathrm{\widetilde{div}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}}({f_{j}}(\eta_{\varepsilon_{j}}*\varphi))(y)d\mu_{\alpha}(y)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}u(y)\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}{f_{j}}\cdot(\eta_{\varepsilon_{j}}*\varphi)(y)d\mu_{\alpha}(y)

\displaystyle=-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}u(y)\mathrm{\widetilde{div}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}}({f_{j}}(\eta_{\varepsilon_{j}}*\varphi))(y)d\mu_{\alpha}(y)

\displaystyle\quad\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\varphi(y)\Big(\eta_{\varepsilon_{j}}*(u\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}{f_{j}})(y)-u\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}{f_{j}}(y)\Big)d\mu_{\alpha}(y)

where in the last equality we have used the fact (
6

, and each point in

(

On the other hand, we change the order of integration to get

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle=-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}{(u{f_{j}})}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(x)\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\frac{1}{2}-x_{1}}{\sqrt{x_{1}}}\varphi_{1}(x)+\cdots+\frac{\alpha_{d}+\frac{1}{2}-x_{d}}{\sqrt{x_{d}}}\varphi_{d}(x)\right)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}^{d}}\eta\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon_{j}}\Big)u(y){f_{j}}(y)\Big(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}\frac{\alpha_{k}+\frac{1}{2}-y_{k}}{\sqrt{y_{k}}}{\varphi_{k}}(x)\Big)d\mu_{\alpha}(y)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}u(y){f_{j}}(y)\Big(\Big(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}\frac{\alpha_{k}+\frac{1}{2}-y_{k}}{\sqrt{y_{k}}}{\varphi_{k}}\Big)\ast\eta_{\varepsilon_{j}}(y)\Big)d\mu_{\alpha}(y)

\displaystyle\quad-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}^{d}}\eta\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon_{j}}\Big)u(y){f_{j}}(y)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}\Big(\frac{\alpha_{k}+\frac{1}{2}-x_{k}}{\sqrt{x_{k}}}-\frac{\alpha_{k}+\frac{1}{2}-y_{k}}{\sqrt{y_{k}}}\Big){\varphi_{k}}(x)\right)d\mu_{\alpha}(y)d\mu_{\alpha}(x).

Thus, the above estimate of the term

shows that

where

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle:=\Bigg|-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}u(y)\mathrm{\widetilde{div}}(f_{j}(\eta_{\varepsilon_{j}}*\varphi))(y)d\mu_{\alpha}(y)

\displaystyle\quad-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}u(y){f_{j}}(y)\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}\frac{\alpha_{k}+\frac{1}{2}-y_{k}}{\sqrt{y_{k}}}({\varphi_{k}}*\eta_{\varepsilon_{j}}(y))\right)d\mu_{\alpha}(y)\Bigg|

and

\displaystyle J_{2}:

\displaystyle=\Bigg|-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}^{d}}\eta\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon_{j}}\Big)u(y){f_{j}}(y)

\displaystyle\times\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}\Big(\frac{\alpha_{k}+\frac{1}{2}-x_{k}}{\sqrt{x_{k}}}-\frac{\alpha_{k}+\frac{1}{2}-y_{k}}{\sqrt{y_{k}}}\Big){\varphi_{k}}(x)\right)d\mu_{\alpha}(y)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\Bigg|.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\Bigg|-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}u(y)\mathrm{\widetilde{div}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}}(f_{j}(\eta_{\varepsilon_{j}}*\varphi))(y)d\mu_{\alpha}(y)

\displaystyle\quad-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}u(y){f_{j}}(y)\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}\frac{\alpha_{k}+\frac{1}{2}-y_{k}}{\sqrt{y_{k}}}\varphi_{k}*\eta_{\varepsilon_{j}}(y)\right)d\mu_{\alpha}(y)\Bigg|

\displaystyle\quad-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}u(y){f_{j}}(y)\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}\frac{\alpha_{k}+\frac{1}{2}-y_{k}}{\sqrt{y_{k}}}{\varphi_{k}}*\eta_{\varepsilon_{j}}(y)\right)d\mu_{\alpha}(y)\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq|\nabla_{{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}}u|(\Omega_{1})+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}|\nabla_{{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}}u|(U_{j})

\displaystyle\leq|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}u|(\Omega_{1})+|\nabla_{{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}}u|(\Omega_{1}\backslash\Omega_{1,0})

\displaystyle\leq|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}u|(\Omega_{1})+3\varepsilon,

where we have used the fact (
5
) in the last
inequality. Note that
\psi(x_{k})=\frac{\alpha_{k}+\frac{1}{2}-x_{k}}{\sqrt{x_{k}}}
,
\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
and
\text{supp}\ \eta\subseteq B(0,1)\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
. When

such that

\displaystyle|\psi(x_{k})-\psi(y_{k})|

\displaystyle=\Big|\frac{\alpha_{k}+\frac{1}{2}}{2}(y_{k}+\theta(x_{k}-y_{k}))^{-\frac{3}{2}}+\frac{1}{2}(y_{k}+\theta(x_{k}-y_{k}))^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big||x_{k}-y_{k}|

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{|\alpha_{k}+\frac{1}{2}|}{2}|y_{k}+\theta(x_{k}-y_{k})|^{-\frac{3}{2}}+\frac{1}{2}|y_{k}+\theta(x_{k}-y_{k})|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big)|x_{k}-y_{k}|.

Consequently, we obtain

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=\Bigg|\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}^{d}}\eta\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon_{j}}\Big)u(y){f_{j}}(y)\Bigg(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}\Big(\frac{\alpha_{k}+\frac{1}{2}-x_{k}}{\sqrt{x_{k}}}-\frac{\alpha_{k}+\frac{1}{2}-y_{k}}{\sqrt{y_{k}}}\Big){\varphi_{k}}(x)\Bigg)

\displaystyle\quad\times d\mu_{\alpha}(y)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{j}}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\Big|\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}^{d}}\eta\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon_{j}}\Big)u(y){f_{j}}(y)\Big|\sum_{k=1}^{d}\Big|\alpha_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\Big||y_{k}+\theta(x-y_{k})|^{-\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times|\varphi_{k}(x)|d\mu_{\alpha}(y)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\varepsilon_{j}}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\Big|\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}^{d}}\eta\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon_{j}}\Big)u(y){f_{j}}(y)\Big|\sum_{k=1}^{d}|y_{k}+\theta(x_{k}-y_{k})|^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times|\varphi_{k}(x)|d\mu_{\alpha}(y)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon_{j}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\Big|\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}^{d}}\eta\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon_{j}}\Big)u(y){f_{j}}(y)\Big|\sum_{k=1}^{d}\Big|\alpha_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\Big||y_{k}|^{-\frac{3}{2}}d\mu_{\alpha}(y)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+C\varepsilon_{j}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\Big|\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}^{d}}\eta\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon_{j}}\Big)u(y){f_{j}}(y)\Big|\sum_{k=1}^{d}|y_{k}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}d\mu_{\alpha}(y)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon_{j}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\Big|\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}^{d}}\eta\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon_{j}}\Big)\Big|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\sum_{k=1}^{d}\Big|\alpha_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\Big||u(y)||{f_{j}}(y)|{|y_{k}|}^{-\frac{3}{2}}d\mu_{\alpha}(y)

\displaystyle\quad+C\varepsilon_{j}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\Big|\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{j}^{d}}\eta\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon_{j}}\Big)\Big|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\sum_{k=1}^{d}|u(y)||f_{j}(y)||y_{k}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}d\mu_{\alpha}(y)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon_{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\Big|\frac{1}{\epsilon_{j}^{d}}\eta\Big(\frac{x-y}{\epsilon_{j}}\Big)\Big|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\Big|\alpha_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\Big||u(y)|\Big|\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{f_{j}}(y)\Big||y_{k}|^{-\frac{3}{2}}d\mu_{\alpha}(y)

\displaystyle\quad+C\varepsilon_{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\Big|\frac{1}{\epsilon_{j}^{d}}\eta\Big(\frac{x-y}{\epsilon_{j}}\Big)\Big|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega_{1}}|u(y)|\Big|\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{f_{j}}(y)\Big||y_{k}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}d\mu_{\alpha}(y)

\displaystyle=C\varepsilon_{j}\int_{\Omega_{1}}|u(y)|\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}\Big|\alpha_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\Big||y_{k}|^{-\frac{3}{2}}d\mu_{\alpha}(y)+C\varepsilon_{j}\int_{\Omega_{1}}|u(y)|\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}|y_{k}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}d\mu_{\alpha}(y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon,

where we have used the facts that

(9)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \hskip-14.22636pt{\int_{\Omega_{1}}|u(y)|\sum_{k=1}^{d}|y_{k}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}d\mu_{\alpha}(y)<\infty,}\\
{\int_{\Omega_{1}}|u(y)|\sum_{k=1}^{d}\Big|\alpha_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\Big||y_{k}|^{-\frac{3}{2}}d\mu_{\alpha}(y)<\infty,}\end{aligned}\right.

and in the third inequality we have used the fact that

\displaystyle|y_{k}+\theta(x_{k}-y_{k})|\geq|y_{k}|-\theta|x_{k}-y_{k}|=\Big(1-\frac{\theta}{2}\Big)|y_{k}|.

By taking the supremum over
\varphi
and
the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon>0
, the theorem can be proved.
∎

Theorem 2.6

Let
\Omega_{1}

|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\max\{u,v\}|(\Omega_{1})+|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\min\{u,v\}|(\Omega_{1})\leq|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}u|(\Omega_{1})+|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}v|(\Omega_{1}).

By Theorem

, we take two functions

u_{h},v_{h}\in{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\Omega_{1})\cap C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{1}),\ \ h=1,2,...,

\begin{cases}u_{h}\rightarrow u,v_{h}\rightarrow v\ \ \hbox{in}\ \ L^{1}(\Omega_{1},d\mu_{\alpha}),\\

\max\{u_{h},v_{h}\}\rightarrow\max\{u,v\}\ \ \&\ \ \min\{u_{h},v_{h}\}\rightarrow\min\{u,v\}\ \ \hbox{in}\ \ L^{1}(\Omega_{1},d\mu_{\alpha}).

Via Lemma
2.1

\displaystyle|

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\max\{u,v\}|(\Omega_{1})+|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\min\{u,v\}|(\Omega_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{\lim\inf}\limits_{h\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\max\{u_{h},v_{h}\}|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)+\mathop{\lim\inf}\limits_{h\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\min\{u_{h},v_{h}\}|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{\lim\inf}\limits_{h\rightarrow\infty}\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{1}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\max\{u_{h},v_{h}\}|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)+\int_{\Omega_{1}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\min\{u_{h},v_{h}\}|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{\lim\inf}\limits_{h\rightarrow\infty}\bigg(\int_{\{x\in\Omega_{1}:u_{h}\leq v_{h}\}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}v_{h}|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)+\int_{\{x\in\Omega_{1}:u_{h}>v_{h}\}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}u_{h}|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\{x\in\Omega_{1}:u_{h}\leq v_{h}\}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}u_{h}|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)+\int_{\{x\in\Omega_{1}:u_{h}>v_{h}\}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}v_{h}|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{h\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}u_{h}(x)|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)+\lim_{h\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}v_{h}(x)|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}u|(\Omega_{1})+|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}v|(\Omega_{1}).

2.2.
Basic properties of Laguerre perimeter

In this subsection, we introduce a kind of new perimeter: the
Laguerre perimeter (
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
-perimeter in short). Moreover,
we establish the related results for it.

The
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
-perimeter of
E\subset\Omega
can be defined
as follows:

(10)

P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega)=|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}1_{E}|(\Omega)=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega)}\Big\{\int_{E}\mathrm{div}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\Big\},

where
\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
is defined in Section
2.1
. In particular,
we shall also write

P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})=P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E)

.

The following conclusion is a direct corollary of Lemma
2.1
to replace
f
with
1_{E}
.

Corollary 2.7
.

(Lower semicontinuity of
P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}
).
Suppose
1_{E_{k}}\to 1_{E}
in
L_{loc}^{1}(\Omega,d\mu_{\alpha})
, where
E
and

E_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N},
are subsets of
\Omega
, then

P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega)\leq\mathop{\lim\inf}\limits_{k\to\infty}P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{k},\Omega).

Moreover, by Theorem
2.6
, via choosing
u=1_{E}
and

v=1_{F}
for any compact subsets
E,F
in
\Omega_{1}
, we immediately
obtain the following corollary. According to Xiao and Zhang’s result
in
[
16
, Section 1.1 (iii)]
, we also give the equality
condition of (
11
).

Corollary 2.8
.

For any compact subsets
E,F
in
\Omega_{1}
, we have

(11)

P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E\cap F,\Omega_{1})+P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E\cup F,\Omega_{1})\leq P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega_{1})+P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F,\Omega_{1}),

where
\Omega_{1}
is an open set defined in
(
3
).
Especially, if
P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E\setminus(E\cap F),\Omega_{1})\cdot P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F\setminus(F\cap E),\Omega_{1})=0
, the equality of
(
11
) holds true.

Proof.

Since (
11
) is valid, we only need to prove its converse
inequality holds true under the above condition. Obviously, the
condition
P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E\setminus(E\cap F),\Omega_{1})\cdot P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F\setminus(E\cap F),\Omega_{1})=0
implies that
P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E\setminus(E\cap F),\Omega_{1})=0
or
P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F\setminus(E\cap F),\Omega_{1})=0
. Suppose
P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E\setminus(E\cap F),\Omega_{1})=0
. Via (
11
), we have

(12)

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega_{1})

\displaystyle=P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}((E\setminus(E\cap F))\cup(E\cap F),\Omega_{1})

\displaystyle\leq P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E\setminus(E\cap F),\Omega_{1})+P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E\cap F,\Omega_{1})

\displaystyle=P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E\cap F,\Omega_{1}).

Using (
10
) and
E\cup F=F\cup(E\setminus(E\cap F))
, we
obtain

(13)

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F,\Omega_{1})

\displaystyle=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega_{1})}\Big\{\int_{F}\mathrm{div}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\Big\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega_{1})}\Big\{\int_{E\cup F}\mathrm{div}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)-\int_{E\setminus(E\cap F)}\mathrm{div}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega_{1})}\Big\{\int_{E\cup F}\mathrm{div}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\Big\}

(14)

\displaystyle\quad+\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega_{1})}\Big\{\int_{E\setminus(E\cap F)}\mathrm{div}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\Big\}

\displaystyle=P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E\cup F,\Omega_{1})+P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E\setminus(E\cap F),\Omega_{1})

\displaystyle=P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E\cup F,\Omega_{1}).

Combining (
12
) with (
13
) deduces that

P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega_{1})+P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F,\Omega_{1})\leq P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E\cup F,\Omega_{1})+P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E\cap F,\Omega_{1}),

which derives the desired result. Another case can be
similarly proved, we omit the details.
∎

Next we show that the Gauss-Green formula is valid on sets of finite

\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
-perimeter.

Theorem 2.9
.

(Gauss-Green formula). Let
E\subseteq\Omega
be subset with finite
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
-perimeter. Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{E}\mathrm{\widetilde{div}}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\varphi\left(x\right)d\mu_{\alpha}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial{E^{c}}}(\sqrt{x_{1}}\varphi_{1}(x),\cdots,\sqrt{x_{d}}\varphi_{d}(x))\cdot\vec{n}\omega(x)d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)

\displaystyle-\int_{E}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\alpha_{i}+\frac{1}{2}-x_{i}}{\sqrt{x_{i}}}\varphi_{i}(x)\omega(x)dx,

where the unit vector
\vec{n}(x)
is the outward normal to
E
and

\mathrm{\widetilde{div}}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\cdot)
is defined in
(
8
).

Proof.

By calculating, we have

\displaystyle\int_{E}\mathrm{\widetilde{div}}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\varphi\left(x\right)d\mu_{\alpha}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{E}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sqrt{x_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{i}}}\varphi_{i}(x)\right)\omega(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{E}\mathrm{div}(\sqrt{x_{1}}\varphi_{1}(x)\omega(x),\cdots,\sqrt{x_{d}}\varphi_{d}(x)\omega(x))dx-\int_{E}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sqrt{x_{i}}\varphi_{i}(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{i}}}\omega(x)dx

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\int_{E}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{\sqrt{x_{i}}}\varphi_{i}(x)\omega(x)dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\partial{E^{c}}}(\sqrt{x_{1}}\varphi_{1}(x),\cdots,\sqrt{x_{d}}\varphi_{d}(x))\cdot\vec{n}\omega(x)d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{E}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sqrt{x_{i}}\varphi_{i}(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{i}}}\omega(x)dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{E}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{\sqrt{x_{i}}}\varphi_{i}(x)\omega(x)dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\partial{E^{c}}}(\sqrt{x_{1}}\varphi_{1}(x),\cdots,\sqrt{x_{d}}\varphi_{d}(x))\cdot\vec{n}\omega(x)d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{E}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\alpha_{i}+\frac{1}{2}-x_{i}}{\sqrt{x_{i}}}\varphi_{i}(x)\omega(x)dx,

where we have used the classical Gauss-Green formula and the following facts for the derivatives of
\omega(x)
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{i}}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{{x_{j}}^{\alpha_{j}}e^{-x_{j}}}{\Gamma(\alpha_{j}+1)}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{d}\frac{{x_{j}}^{\alpha_{j}}e^{-x_{j}}}{\Gamma(\alpha_{j}+1)}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+1)}(-e^{-x_{i}}{x_{i}}^{\alpha_{i}}+\alpha_{i}e^{-x_{i}}{x_{i}}^{\alpha_{i}-1})

\displaystyle=\Big(-1+\frac{\alpha_{i}}{x_{i}}\Big)\omega(x)

for
1\leq i\leq d
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

For any set
E
in
\Omega
with finite
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
-perimeter, then

{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega)={P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\Omega\backslash E,\Omega).

Proof.

For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})
, since
{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega)<\infty
,
then

\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})}\int_{E}{\mathrm{div}}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)<\infty.

Via the extended Gauss-Green formula (Theorem
2.9
) and
noting the compact support of
\varphi
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{E}\mathrm{div}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=-\int_{E}\widetilde{\mathrm{div}}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\varphi_{1}(x),\ldots,\varphi_{d}(x))d\mu_{\alpha}(x)-\int_{E}\sum^{d}_{k=1}\frac{\alpha_{i}+\frac{1}{2}-x_{i}}{\sqrt{x_{i}}}\varphi_{i}(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial{E^{c}}}(\sqrt{x_{1}}\varphi_{1}(x),\cdots,\sqrt{x_{d}}\varphi_{d}(x))\cdot\vec{n}\omega(x)d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{E}\sum^{d}_{k=1}\frac{\alpha_{i}+\frac{1}{2}-x_{i}}{\sqrt{x_{i}}}\varphi_{i}(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)-\int_{E}\sum^{d}_{k=1}\frac{\alpha_{i}+\frac{1}{2}-x_{i}}{\sqrt{x_{i}}}\varphi_{i}(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial{E}}(\sqrt{x_{1}}\varphi_{1}(x),\cdots,\sqrt{x_{d}}\varphi_{d}(x))\cdot\vec{n}\omega(x)d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)

\displaystyle=-\int_{E^{c}}\widetilde{\mathrm{div}}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)-\int_{E^{c}}\sum^{d}_{k=1}\frac{\alpha_{i}+\frac{1}{2}-x_{i}}{\sqrt{x_{i}}}\varphi_{i}(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{E^{c}}\mathrm{div}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x),

where
\vec{n}(x)
is the unit exterior normal to
E
at
x
. Due to the arbitrariness
of
\varphi
, taking the supremum reaches

{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega)={P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\Omega\backslash E,\Omega).

∎

2.3.
Coarea formula of
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
-BV functions and the Sobolev inequality

Below we prove the coarea formula and the Sobolev inequality for

\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
-perimeter.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
\Omega_{1}
be an open set defined in (
3
). If
f\in{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\Omega_{1})
, then

(15)

|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f|(\Omega_{1})\approx\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{t},\Omega_{1})dt,

where
E_{t}=\{x\in\Omega_{1}:f(x)>t\}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Firstly, assume

\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\ldots,\varphi_{d})\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega_{1},\mathbb{R}^{d}).

We can
easily prove that for
i=1,2,\ldots,d
,

\int_{\Omega_{1}}f(x)\sqrt{x_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{i}}}\varphi_{i}(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\Big(\int_{E_{t}}\sqrt{x_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{i}}}\varphi_{i}(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\Big)dt,

and

\int_{\Omega_{1}}f(x)\frac{\alpha_{i}+\frac{1}{2}-x_{i}}{\sqrt{x_{i}}}\varphi_{i}(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\Big(\int_{E_{t}}\frac{\alpha_{i}+\frac{1}{2}-x_{i}}{\sqrt{x_{i}}}\varphi_{i}(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\Big)dt,

where the latter can be seen in the proof of
[
7
, Section 5.5, Theorem 1]
. It follows that

\int_{\Omega_{1}}f(x)\mathrm{div}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)=\int^{+\infty}_{-\infty}\Big(\int_{E_{t}}\mathrm{div}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\Big)dt.

Therefore, we conclude that for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega_{1})
,

\int_{\Omega_{1}}f(x)\mathrm{div}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\leq\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{t},\Omega_{1})dt.

Furthermore,

|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f|(\Omega_{1})\leq\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{t},\Omega_{1})dt}.

Secondly, without loss of generality, we only need to verify that

|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f|(\Omega_{1})\geq\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{t},\Omega_{1})dt}

holds for
f\in\mathcal{BV}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\Omega_{1})\bigcap C^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})
. This proof can refer to the idea of

[
14
, Proposition 4.2]
. Let

m(t)=\int_{\{x\in\Omega_{1}:\ f(x)\leq t\}}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sqrt{x_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{i}}}f(x)\Big|d\mu_{\alpha}(x).

It is obvious that

\int^{+\infty}_{-\infty}m^{\prime}(t)dt=\int_{\Omega_{1}}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sqrt{x_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{i}}}f(x)\Big|d\mu_{\alpha}(x).

Define the following function
g_{h}
as

g_{h}(s):=\begin{cases}0,&\mathrm{if}\ s\leq t,\\
h(s-t),&\mathrm{if}\ t\leq s\leq t+1/h,\\
1,&\mathrm{if}\ s\geq t+1/h,\end{cases}

where
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Set the sequence
{v_{h}}(x):=g_{h}(f(x))
.
At this time,
{v_{h}}\to{1_{{E_{t}}}}
in
L^{1}(\Omega_{1},d\mu_{\alpha})
. In fact,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{1}}|v_{h}(x)-1_{E_{t}}|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\{x\in\Omega_{1}:t<f(x)\leq t+1/h\}}|g_{h}(f(x))-1|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{x\in\Omega_{1}:t<f(x)\leq t+1/h\}}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\to 0.

Since
\{x\in\Omega_{1}:t<f(x)\leq t+1/h\}\rightarrow\emptyset
as

h\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{1}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}{v_{h}}(x)|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\{x\in\Omega_{1}:t<f(x)\leq t+{1/h}\}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(h(f(x)-t))|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\{x\in\Omega_{1}:f(x)\geq t+1/h\}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}1|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=h\int_{\{x\in\Omega_{1}:t<f(x)\leq t+{1/h}\}}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sqrt{x_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{i}}}f(x)\Big|d\mu_{\alpha}(x).

Taking the limit with
h\rightarrow\infty
and using Theorem
2.4
, we obtain

(16)

\begin{split}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}1_{E_{t}}|(\Omega_{1})&\leq\mathop{\lim\sup}\limits_{h\to\infty}\int_{\Omega_{1}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}v_{h}(x)|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\\
&=h\mathop{\lim\sup}\limits_{h\to\infty}\int_{\{x\in\Omega_{1}:t<f(x)\leq t+{1/h}\}}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sqrt{x_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{i}}}f(x)\Big|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\\
&=m^{\prime}(t).\end{split}

Integrating (
16
) reaches

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{t},\Omega_{1})dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}m^{\prime}(t)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{1}}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sqrt{x_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{i}}}f(x)\Big|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Omega_{1}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f(x)|d\mu_{\alpha}(x).

Finally, by approximation and using the lower semicontinuity of the

\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
-perimeter, we conclude that (
15
) holds
true for all
f\in\mathcal{BV}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\Omega_{1})
.
∎

Finally, we can develop the Sobolev inequality and the isoperimetric
inequality for
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
-BV functions. Since the domain
\Omega_{1}
is
a reasonable substitute of
\Omega
, we can obtain the isoperimetric
inequality and the Sobolev inequality for

f\in\mathcal{BV}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\Omega_{1})
, where
\Omega_{1}
is given in
(
3
).

Theorem 2.12
.

(i)
(Sobolev inequality). Let
\Omega_{1}
be an open set defined in (
3
).
Then for all
f\in{\mathcal{BV}}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\Omega_{1})
, we have

(17)

\|f\|_{L^{\frac{d}{d-1}}(\Omega_{1},d\mu_{\alpha})}\lesssim|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f|(\Omega_{1}).

(ii)
(Isoperimetric inequality). Let
E
be a bounded set of finite
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
-perimeter in
\Omega_{1}
. Then

(18)

{\mu_{\alpha}(E)}^{\frac{d-1}{d}}\lesssim P_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega_{1}).

(iii)
The above two statements are equivalent.

Proof.

(i) Choose

f_{k}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega_{1})\cap\mathcal{BV}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\Omega_{1}),\ k=1,2,\ldots,

such that

\begin{cases}f_{k}\rightarrow f\ \hbox{in}\ L^{1}(\Omega_{1},d\mu_{\alpha}),\\
\int_{\Omega_{1}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f_{k}(x)|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\rightarrow\parallel\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f\parallel(\Omega_{1}).\end{cases}

Since
\Omega_{1}=\Omega\setminus\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}:\exists i\in 1,\cdots,d\ \mathrm{such}\ \mathrm{that}\ \sqrt{x_{i}}<1\}
,
then for any
i=1,\cdots,d
, we obtain
\sqrt{x_{i}}\geq 1
. It is
easy to see that

(19)

|\nabla f(x)|\leq|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f(x)|=\Bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Big(\sqrt{x_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{i}}}f(x)\Big)^{2}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then by Fatou’s lemma and the weighted Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev
inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\frac{d}{d-1}}(\Omega_{1},d\mu_{\alpha})}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|f_{k}\|_{L^{\frac{d}{d-1}}(\Omega_{1},d\mu_{\alpha})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{1},d\mu_{\alpha})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{1},d\mu_{\alpha})}=|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f|(\Omega_{1}),

where we have used the relation between the gradient
\nabla
and the Laguerre gradient
\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}
in (
19
).

(ii) We can show that (
18
) is valid via letting
f=1_{E}
in (
17
).

(iii) Apparently, (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) has been proved.
In what follows, we prove (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i). Assume that
0\leq f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega_{1})
. By the coarea formula in Theorem
2.11
and (ii), we have

\int_{\Omega_{1}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f(x)|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}1_{E_{t}}|(\Omega_{1})\,dt\gtrsim\int_{0}^{+\infty}|E_{t}|^{\frac{d-1}{d}}dt,

where
E_{t}=\big\{x\in\Omega_{1}:\ f(x)>t\big\}
. Let

f_{t}=\min\{t,f\}\ \ \&\ \ \chi(t)=\left(\int_{\Omega_{1}}f_{t}^{\frac{d}{d-1}}(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right)^{\frac{d-1}{d}},\ \forall\ t\in\mathbb{R}.

It is easy to see that

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\chi(t)=\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{1}}|f(x)|^{\frac{d}{d-1}}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\bigg)^{\frac{d-1}{d}}.

In addition, we can check that
\chi(t)
is nondecreasing on

(0,\infty)
and for
h>0
,

0\leq\chi(t+h)-\chi(t)\leq\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{1}}|f_{t+h}(x)-f_{t}(x)|^{\frac{d}{d-1}}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\bigg)^{\frac{d-1}{d}}\leq h|E_{t}|^{\frac{d-1}{d}}.

Then
\chi(t)
is locally a Lipschitz function and

\chi^{\prime}(t)\leq|E_{t}|^{\frac{d-1}{d}}
for a.e.
t\in(0,\infty)
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{1}}|f(x)|^{\frac{d}{d-1}}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\bigg)^{\frac{d-1}{d}}=\int^{\infty}_{0}\chi^{\prime}(t)dt

\displaystyle\leq\int^{\infty}_{0}|E_{t}|^{\frac{d-1}{d}}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Omega_{1}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f(x)|d\mu_{\alpha}(x).

For all
f\in{\mathcal{BV}}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\Omega_{1})
, we conclude that (
17
) is valid by Theorem
2.4
.
∎

As a direct result of the proof of (i) in Theorem
2.12
, we
can get the following corollary.

Corollary 2.13
.

Let
1<p<d
and let
\Omega_{1}
be an open set defined in (
3
). For any
f\in W_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}^{1,1}(\Omega_{1})
one has

(20)

\|f\|_{L^{\frac{dp}{d-p}}(\Omega_{1},d\mu_{\alpha})}\lesssim\|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{1},d\mu_{\alpha})}.

Proof.

For some
\gamma>1
to be fixed later, via the Lemma
2.1
(i) and Hölder inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\Bigg(\int_{\Omega_{1}}

\displaystyle|f(x)|^{\frac{\gamma d}{d-1}}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\Bigg)^{\frac{d-1}{d}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Omega_{1}}|f(x)|^{\gamma-1}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f(x)|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\Omega_{1}}|f(x)|^{\frac{p(\gamma-1)}{p-1}}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{1}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}f(x)|^{p}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Choosing

\gamma=\frac{p(d-1)}{d-p}

and noting

\gamma-1=\frac{d(p-1)}{d-p},

then we conclude that (
20
) holds true.

∎

3.
Laguerre mean curvature

In this section we focus on the question whether every set of
finite
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
-perimeter in
\Omega_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
has
mean curvature in
L^{1}(\Omega_{1},d\mu_{\alpha})
. For the classical
case, please refer to
[
3
]
for details. During the proof
of Theorem
3.1
, we need to use the important result for
the Laguerre perimeter in Corollary
2.8
. Therefore, we
assume that the dimension
d\geq 3
via Remark
2.5
.

For a given
u\in L^{1}(\Omega_{1},d\mu_{\alpha})
, the Massari type
functional corresponding to the
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
-perimeter is defined as

\mathscr{F}_{u,\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E):={P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega_{1})+\int_{E}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x),

where
E
is an arbitrary set of finite
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
-perimeter in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For every set
E
of finite
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
-perimeter in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
, there exists a function
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},d\mu_{\alpha})
such that

\mathscr{F}_{u,\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E)\leq\mathscr{F}_{u,\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F)

holds for every set
F
of finite
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
-perimeter in
\Omega_{1}
.

Proof.

At first, for a given set
E
, we need to find a function
u\in L^{1}(\Omega_{1},d\mu_{\alpha})
such that

(21)

\mathscr{F}_{u,\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E)\leq\mathscr{F}_{u,\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F)

holds for every
F
with either
F\subset E
or
E\subset F
, then
Theorem
3.1
is proved, i.e. (
21
) holds for every

F\subset\Omega_{1}
. In fact, by adding the inequality
(
21
) corresponding to the test sets
E\cap F
and
E\cup F
,

\begin{cases}{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega_{1})+\int_{E}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E\cap F,\Omega_{1})+\int_{E\cap F}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x),\\
{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega_{1})+\int_{E}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E\cup F,\Omega_{1})+\int_{E\cup F}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x).\end{cases}

Then noting that

(22)

{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E\cap F,\Omega_{1})+{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E\cup F,\Omega_{1})\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega_{1})+{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F,\Omega_{1}),

we can get

\displaystyle 2{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega_{1})+2\int_{E}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E\cap F,\Omega_{1})+{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E\cup F,\Omega_{1})+\int_{E\cap F}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)+\int_{E\cup F}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega_{1})+{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F,\Omega_{1})+\int_{E}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)+\int_{F}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x),

that is, (
21
) holds for arbitrary
F
. Also, if
(
21
) holds for
F\subset E
, then for the set
F
such
that
E\subset F
, i.e.
\Omega_{1}\backslash F\subset\Omega_{1}\backslash E
,

\displaystyle{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega_{1})+\int_{E}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle={P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\Omega_{1}\backslash E,\Omega_{1})+\int_{\Omega_{1}\backslash E}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)-\int_{\Omega_{1}\backslash E}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)+\int_{E}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(\Omega_{1}\backslash F,\Omega_{1})+\int_{\Omega_{1}\backslash F}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)-\int_{\Omega_{1}\backslash E}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)+\int_{E}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle={P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F,\Omega_{1})+\int_{\Omega_{1}\backslash F}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)-\int_{\Omega_{1}\backslash E}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)+\int_{E}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{F}_{u,\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F)-\int_{F}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)+\int_{\Omega_{1}\backslash F}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)-\int_{\Omega_{1}\backslash E}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{E}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{F}_{u,\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F)+\int_{F\backslash E}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)-\int_{(\Omega_{1}\backslash E)/(\Omega_{1}\backslash F)}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{F}_{u,\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F),

where we have used the fact that
u
vanishes outside the set
E
and Lemma
2.10
.
Hence, we only need to prove that
u
defined on
E
is integrable and (
21
) holds for any
F\subset E
.

Step I.
Denote by
h(\cdot)
a measurable function
satisfying
h>0
on
E
and
\int_{E}h(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)<\infty
, and denote
by
\Lambda
the (positive and totally finite) measure:

\Lambda(F)=\int_{F}h(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x),\ F\subset E.

It is obvious that
\Lambda(F)=0
if and only if
\mu_{\alpha}(F)=0
. For
\lambda>0
and
F\subset E
, consider the functional

\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}(F):={P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F,\Omega_{1})+\lambda\Lambda(E\setminus F).

It is well known that every minimizing sequence is compact in

L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega_{1},d\mu_{\alpha})
and the functional is
lower semi-continuous with respect to the same convergence. Hence, we conclude that, for every
\lambda>0
, there is
a solution
E_{\lambda}
to the problem:

\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}(F)\rightarrow\text{min},\ F\subset E.

Choose a sequence
\{\lambda_{i}\}
of positive numbers which is
strictly increasing to
\infty
and denote the corresponding
solutions by
E_{i}\equiv E_{\lambda_{i}}
, so that
\forall i\geq 1
,

(23)

\mathscr{F}_{\lambda_{i}}(E_{i})\leq\mathscr{F}_{\lambda_{i}}(F),\ \forall\ F\subset E.

Given
i<j
. Let
F=E_{i}\cap E_{j}
. It follows from (
23
) that

\mathscr{F}_{\lambda_{i}}(E_{i})\leq\mathscr{F}_{\lambda_{i}}(E_{i}\cap E_{j}),

that is,

{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i},\Omega_{1})+\lambda_{i}\Lambda(E\setminus E_{i})\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i}\cap E_{j},\Omega_{1})+\lambda_{i}\Lambda(E\setminus(E_{i}\cap E_{j})),

which
implies

{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i},\Omega_{1})+\lambda_{i}\int_{E\setminus E_{i}}h(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i}\cap E_{j},\Omega_{1})+\lambda_{i}\int_{E\setminus(E_{i}\cap E_{j})}h(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x).

A direct computation gives

{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i},\Omega_{1})\leq\lambda_{i}\int_{E_{i}\setminus E_{j}}h(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)+{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i}\cap E_{j},\Omega_{1}).

On the other hand, taking
F=E_{i}\cup E_{j}\subset E
in (
23
), we can get

\mathscr{F}_{\lambda_{j}}(E_{j})\leq\mathscr{F}_{\lambda_{j}}(E_{i}\cup E_{j})
.
Hence,

{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{j},\Omega_{1})+\lambda_{j}\int_{E\setminus E_{j}}h(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i}\cup E_{j},\Omega_{1})+\lambda_{j}\int_{E\setminus(E_{i}\cup E_{j})}h(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x),

equivalently,

{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{j},\Omega_{1})+\lambda_{j}\int_{E_{i}\setminus E_{j}}h(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i}\cup E_{j},\Omega_{1})

which implies that

\displaystyle{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i},\Omega_{1})

\displaystyle+{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{j},\Omega_{1})+\lambda_{j}\int_{E_{i}\setminus E_{j}}h(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i}\cup E_{j},\Omega_{1})+\lambda_{i}\int_{E_{i}\setminus E_{j}}h(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)+{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i}\cap E_{j},\Omega_{1}).

Recall that
h>0
. The above estimate, together with (
22
) and the fact that
\lambda_{i}<\lambda_{j}
, indicates that

(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i})\Lambda(E_{i}\setminus E_{j})=(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i})\int_{E_{i}\setminus E_{j}}h(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)=0,

that is,
E_{i}\subset E_{j}
and the sequence of minimizers
\{E_{i}\}
is increasing. On the other hand, letting
F=E
, we get

{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i},\Omega_{1})+\lambda_{i}\Lambda(E\setminus E_{i})\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega_{1})+\lambda_{i}\Lambda(E\setminus E)={P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega_{1})\ \ \forall\ i\geq 1,

which
deduces that
E_{i}
converges monotonically and in

L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},d\mu_{\alpha})
to
E
. Via Lemma
2.1
(ii), we get

\begin{cases}{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega_{1})\leq\mathop{\lim\inf}\limits_{i\rightarrow\infty}{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i},\Omega_{1})\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega_{1}),\\
{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega_{1})\leq\mathop{\lim\inf}\limits_{i\rightarrow\infty}{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i},\Omega_{1})\leq\mathop{\lim\sup}\limits_{i\rightarrow\infty}{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i},\Omega_{1})\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E),\end{cases}

which means

(24)

{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega_{1})=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i},\Omega_{1}).

Step II.
Let
\lambda_{0}=0
and
E_{0}=\emptyset
, and define

u(x)=\begin{cases}-\lambda_{i}h(x),\ x\in E_{i}\backslash E_{i-1},\ i\geq 1,\\
0,\ \text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Clearly,
u
is negative almost everywhere on
E
, and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}|u(x)|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\cup^{\infty}_{i=0}E_{i+1}\backslash E_{i}}|u(x)|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{i=0}\int_{E_{i+1}\backslash E_{i}}\lambda_{i+1}h(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{i=0}\lambda_{i+1}\Lambda(E_{i+1}\backslash E_{i}).

In (
23
), taking
F=E_{i+1}
, we have

{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i},\Omega_{1})+\lambda_{i}\Lambda(E\setminus E_{i})\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i+1},\Omega_{1})+\lambda_{i}\Lambda(E\setminus E_{i+1}),

that is, for every
i\geq 0
,

\lambda_{i}\Lambda(E_{i+1}\backslash E_{i})\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i+1},\Omega_{1})-{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i},\Omega_{1}).

Then for sufficiently large
N
,

\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=0}\lambda_{i}\Lambda(E_{i+1}\backslash E_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{N}_{i=0}\Big[{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i+1},\Omega_{1})-{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i},\Omega_{1})\Big]

\displaystyle={P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{N},\Omega_{1})-{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{0},\Omega_{1})

\displaystyle={P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{N},\Omega_{1}).

Letting
N\rightarrow\infty
, (
24
) indicates that

\sum^{\infty}_{i=0}\lambda_{i}\Lambda(E_{i+1}\backslash E_{i})\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega_{1}).

We make the additional assumption that
0<\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{i}\leq c
,
i\geq 0
, where
c
is a constant independent of
i
.
Then for any
N>0
,

\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=0}(\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{i})\Lambda(E_{i+1}\backslash E_{i})

\displaystyle\leq c\sum^{N}_{i=0}\Lambda(E_{i+1}\backslash E_{i})

\displaystyle=c\sum^{N}_{i=0}\int_{E_{i+1}\backslash E_{i}}h(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=c\int_{\cup^{N}_{i=0}(E_{i+1}\backslash E_{i})}h(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x),

which gives

\sum^{\infty}_{i=0}(\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{i})\Lambda(E_{i+1}\backslash E_{i})\leq c\Lambda(E).

Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}|u(x)|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{i=0}\lambda_{i+1}\Lambda(E_{i+1}\backslash E_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{i=0}(\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{i})\Lambda(E_{i+1}\backslash E_{i})+\sum^{\infty}_{i=0}\lambda_{i}\Lambda(E_{i+1}\backslash E_{i})

\displaystyle\leq c\Lambda(E)+{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega_{1})<\infty.

In conclusion,
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},d\mu_{\alpha})
.

Step III.
We claim that for every
i\geq 1
the inequality

(25)

{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i},\Omega_{1})\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F,\Omega_{1})+\sum^{i}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}\Lambda((E_{j}\backslash E_{j-1})\backslash F)

holds for any
F\subset E
.

For
i=1
,
E_{i-1}=E_{0}=\emptyset
. Then (
25
) becomes

{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{1},\Omega_{1})\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F,\Omega_{1})+\lambda_{1}\Lambda(E_{1}\backslash F),

which coincides with (
23
) for
i=1
.

Now we assume that (
25
) holds for a fixed
i\geq 1
and
every
F\subset E
. Take
F\cap E_{i}
as a test set. Note that

\{E_{j}\}
is increasing. It is easy to see that

(E_{j}\backslash E_{j-1})\backslash(F\cap E_{i})=(E_{j}\backslash E_{j-1})\backslash F.

Then

\displaystyle{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i},\Omega_{1})

\displaystyle\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F\cap E_{i},\Omega_{1})+\sum^{i}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}\Lambda((E_{j}\backslash E_{j-1})\backslash(F\cap E_{i}))

\displaystyle={P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F\cap E_{i},\Omega_{1})+\sum^{i}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}\Lambda((E_{j}\backslash E_{j-1})\backslash F).

On the other hand,
E_{i+1}
is a minimizer of
\mathscr{F}_{\lambda_{i+1}}
. Hence,

\mathscr{F}_{\lambda_{i+1}}(E_{i+1})\leq\mathscr{F}_{\lambda_{i+1}}(F\cup E_{i}),

and noticing that

E\backslash E_{i}=(E\backslash E_{i+1})\cup(E_{i+1}\backslash E_{i}),

we can get

E\backslash(F\cup E_{i})=((E\backslash E_{i+1})\backslash F)\cup((E_{i+1}\backslash E_{i})\backslash F).

This gives

\displaystyle{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i+1},\Omega_{1})+\lambda_{i+1}\Lambda(E\backslash E_{i+1})

\displaystyle\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F\cup E_{i},\Omega_{1})+\lambda_{i+1}\Lambda(E\backslash(F\cup E_{i}))

\displaystyle\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F\cup E_{i},\Omega_{1})+\lambda_{i+1}\Lambda((E\backslash E_{i+1})\backslash F)

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda_{i+1}\Lambda((E_{i+1}\backslash E_{i})\backslash F).

Therefore, we obtain that

\displaystyle{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i},\Omega_{1})+{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i+1},\Omega_{1})+\lambda_{i+1}\Lambda(E\backslash E_{i+1})

\displaystyle\quad\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F\cap E_{i},\Omega_{1})+\sum^{i}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}\Lambda((E_{j}\backslash E_{j-1})\backslash F)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F\cup E_{i},\Omega_{1})+\lambda_{i+1}\Lambda((E\backslash E_{i+1})\backslash F)+\lambda_{i+1}\Lambda((E_{i+1}\backslash E_{i})\backslash F)

\displaystyle\quad\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i},\Omega_{1})+{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F,\Omega_{1})+\sum^{i+1}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}\Lambda((E_{j}\backslash E_{j-1})\backslash F)+\lambda_{i+1}\Lambda((E\backslash E_{i+1})\backslash F)

\displaystyle\quad\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i},\Omega_{1})+{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F,\Omega_{1})+\sum^{i+1}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}\Lambda((E_{j}\backslash E_{j-1})\backslash F)+\lambda_{i+1}\Lambda(E\backslash E_{i+1}),

that is, (
25
) holds for
i+1
. Finally,

\displaystyle{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E,\Omega_{1})

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(E_{i},\Omega_{1})

\displaystyle\leq{P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F,\Omega_{1})+\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\sum^{i}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}\Lambda((E_{j}\backslash E_{j-1})\backslash F)

\displaystyle={P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F,\Omega_{1})-\int_{\cup^{\infty}_{j=0}(E_{j}\backslash E_{j-1})\backslash F}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle={P}_{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}(F,\Omega_{1})-\int_{E\backslash F}u(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x),

which gives (
23
).
∎

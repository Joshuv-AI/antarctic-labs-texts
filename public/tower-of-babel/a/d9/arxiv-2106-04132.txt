Galois correspondence for augmented monads
Johan Felipe García Vargas

Abstract
. We establish a Galois connection between sub-monads of an augmented monad and sub-functors of the forgetful functor from its Eilenberg-Moore category. This connection is given in terms of invariants and stabilizers defined through universal properties. An explicit procedure for the computation of invariants is given assuming the existence of suitable right adjoints. Additionally, in the context of monoidal closed categories, a characterization of stabilizers is made in terms of Tannakian reconstruction.

Introduction

The structural approach to Galois theory, as develop by Emil Artin and taught in
most algebra textbooks, place as fundamental theorem the correspondence, given
by invariants and stabilizers, between subgroups of symmetries and intermediate
structures.

In
[
4
, Exposé V]
, Grothendieck reframed Galois theory as an equivalence
between a category of interest and the category of actions of a group.
Since the actions of a group
G
form the
Eilenberg-Moore category for the monad
G\times\mathbf{\qm}
,
Grothendieck’s formulation is a monadicity result.
This article proposes a path for recovering
the Galois correspondence from a monadicity result.

Our main hypothesis is that the monad possess
an augmentation. Where, an
augmentation

is a monad homomorphism to the identity of the base category.
This hypothesis is strong, because in the case of Hopf monads
it implies that the monad is
\otimes
-representable,
as shown in
[
2
, Section 5]
.

Morally, having an augmentation distinguish groups from groupoids.
More precisely, the monad induced by a groupoid has an augmentation
only when every morphism in the groupoid is an endomorphism.
However, this makes sense, because there is no clear meaning for
invariants of a groupoid action when it moves elements from a set
to another.

Before presenting the details, let me exhibit the intuition behind them.
Imagine the monad
T
as an algebraic gadget useful for “capturing the
symmetries” of some kind of structure defined over a category
\mathzc{C}
.
With this in mind, we regard the forgetful functor
U

as the “universal
T
-structure”.
A Galois correspondence will emerge from
comparing the action
of a part of the
symmetries
,
parametrized by a monad homomorphism
{h}:{S}\Rightarrow{T}
,
over a part of the of the
structure
,
parametrized by a natural transformation
{\alpha}:{V}\Rightarrow{U}
,
against a trivial action induced by an augmentation of the monad.

The next example illustrates the notions that we aim to generalize and
might be considered a trailer for the paper:

2.1.
Monadic lifting to Eilenberg-Moore categories

It is well known, see
[
5

every adjunction yields a monad and that the Eilenberg-Moore construction allow
to decompose a monad into an adjunction in a “universal” way. Where by
universal, we mean that every other adjunction, yielding the same monad, is
included in it. It is less well known, that the lift of functors to
Eilenberg-Moore categories can be understood in terms of natural
transformations of the base categories. We will make a quick review of this and
refer the reader to
[
1

for
the details.

Definition 2.2

Let

(1)

A

, where

\mathzc{C}
and

\displaystyle r_{M}\mu_{X}

\displaystyle r_{M}\eta_{X}

(2)

A

(3)

The

(4)

2.2.
Monoidal closed categories and Hopf monads

A
monoidal category
is a category
\mathzc{C}
endowed with
a functor
{\otimes}:{\mathzc{C}\times\mathzc{C}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{C}}
called
monoidal product
,
an object
\mathbbm{1}
in
\mathzc{C}
called
unit object
,
and natural isomorphisms called associativity and unit constrains
which are subject to well known axioms of coherence.
In virtue of the strictness theorems,
— see
[
1
, Lemma 3.7 and 3.8]
,
[
5
, Section VII.2]
or
[
3
, Section 2.8]
—
it is unnecessary to explicitly keep track of
the associative or unit constraints in any monoidal category,
as long as you are only interested in properties up to
monoidal equivalence of categories.

One key feature of monoidal categories is that an object
X
,
can be regarded as an endofunctor
X\otimes\mathbf{\qm}
;
this kind of functors are called
\otimes
-
representable
.
The same idea applies to morphisms and natural transformations.
For instance,
\otimes
-representable monads correspond to monoids.
Furthermore, the Eilenberg-Moore category of such a monad is
the category of actions of the monoid.

Bimonads are monads which lift the monoidal product to the
Eilenberg-Moore category.
More precisely, a monad
T
over
(\operatorname{\mathzc{C}},\otimes.\mathbbm{1})
is a
bimonad

if and only if
C^{T}
is also monoidal and the forgetful functor

{U^{T}}:{\mathzc{C}^{T}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{C}}
is strong monoidal.
By means of Theorem
2.4
,
Bimonads are characterized as opmonoidal monads, see
[
1
, Theorem 3.19]
.

A monoidal category is
left closed
if every
\otimes
-representable
functor
\mathbf{\qm}\otimes X
has a right adjoint, denoted
{\left[X,\mathbf{\qm}\right]}^{\ell}
.
In this case, we call
{\left[X,Z\right]}^{\ell}
the
left internal Hom

from
X
to
Z
.
Since
the adjunction

(\eta^{X},\mathbf{\qm}\otimes X\dashv{\left[X,\mathbf{\qm}\right]}^{\ell},\varepsilon^{X})
, gives a
biyection between
\operatorname{Hom}\left(Y\otimes X,Z\right)
and
\operatorname{Hom}\left(Y,{\left[X,Z\right]}^{\ell}\right)
.
Borrowing the terminology from computer science, we define the

currying
of any
{Y\otimes X}\xrightarrow{f}{Z}
as

\ulcorner f\urcorner={\left[X,f\right]}^{\ell}\eta^{X}_{Y}
, and the
uncurrying
of

{Y}\xrightarrow{g}{{\left[X,Z\right]}^{\ell}}
as

\llcorner g\lrcorner=\varepsilon^{X}_{Z}(g\otimes X)
.
It is worth noticing that by the theorem on adjunctions with a
parameter — see
[
5
, Theorem 3 in IV.7.]
—

{\left[\mathbf{\qm}_{1},\mathbf{\qm}_{2}\right]}^{\ell}
is functor from
\mathzc{C}^{op}\otimes\mathzc{C}
to

\mathzc{C}
, when
\mathzc{C}
is left monoidal closed.

When the functor
{\left[X,\mathbf{\qm}\right]}^{\ell}
is
\otimes
-representable,
i.e.
{\left[X,\mathbf{\qm}\right]}^{\ell}=\mathbf{\qm}\otimes{X}^{\ell}
we call
{X}^{\ell}
the
left dual
of
X
.
A monoidal category is
left rigid
(also called compact or autonomous)
if every object has a left dual.

Since a bimonad
T
lifts the monoidal product,
for every
T
-action
M=(X,r_{M})
the functor

{\mathbf{\qm}\otimes M}:{\mathzc{C}^{T}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{C}^{T}}
is a lift
of
{\mathbf{\qm}\otimes X}:{\mathzc{C}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{C}}
.
The bimonad
T
is a
left Hopf monad
if whenever
\mathbf{\qm}\otimes X
has a right adjoint,
the adjoint lifts to an adjoint of
\mathbf{\qm}\otimes M
.
Consequently, if
\mathzc{C}
is left monoidal closed (resp. rigid) and

T
is a left Hopf monad, then
\mathzc{C}^{T}
is also left closed (resp. rigid)
and the forgetful functor preserves internal Homs.
Left Hopf monads are characterized by the invertibility of the left fusion operator,
see
[
2
, Theorem 3.6]
or

[
1
, Theorem 3.27]
.

Another key feature of monoidal categories is that,
in addition to the usual duality between
a category and its opposite, there is a left-right duality obtained by
exchanging the order in the monoidal product.
Implying that there are right variants for each of the above defined concepts.
When the laterality is omitted it is tacitly understood that both variants holds.
For instance, a rigid category is both left and right rigid.

A gadget for comparing left and right
\otimes
-representable functors
is called a braiding. More precisely,
a
lax half-braiding
on a monoidal category
\mathzc{C}
is a pair
(A,\sigma)
where
A
is
an object and
{\sigma}:{A\otimes\mathbf{\qm}}\Rightarrow{\mathbf{\qm}\otimes A}
is a

\otimes
-
multiplicative
natural transformation.
Where
\otimes
-multiplicative means that
\sigma_{\mathbbm{1}}=\mathrm{id}_{A}
and

\sigma_{X\otimes Y}=(X\otimes\sigma_{Y})(\sigma_{X}\otimes Y),

for arbitrary
X
and
Y
in
\mathzc{C}
.
The adjective lax is dropped when
\sigma
is an isomorphism.
A
braiding
is a
\otimes
-multiplicative choice of a half braiding for every
object in
\mathzc{C}
.

A
lax central bimonoid
is simultaneously a lax central comonoid and monoid such that the multiplication and unit are comonoid homomorphisms.
Explicitly, for a lax central bimonoid
A=(A,\sigma,\delta,\mu,\varepsilon,\eta)
:

(A,\sigma)
is a lax half-braiding,

(A,\delta,\varepsilon)
is a comonoid,

(A,\mu,\eta)
is a monoid,
and they satisfy
(\mu\otimes\mu)(A\otimes\sigma_{A}\otimes A)(\delta\otimes\delta)=\delta\mu
,

\varepsilon\otimes\varepsilon=\varepsilon\mu
,
\eta\otimes\eta=\delta\eta
and
\varepsilon\eta=\mathbbm{1}
.
Every lax central bimonoid defines a bimonad given by the functor
A\otimes\mathbf{\qm}
,
see
[
2
, Lemma 5.6]
.

A lax central bimonoid
H=(H,\sigma,\delta,\mu,\varepsilon,\eta)
is
called
lax Hopf monoid
if the fusion morphism

(H\otimes\mu)(\delta\otimes H)
is an isomorphism. By the the
relation between convolution and fusion morfism,
a lax Hopf monoid has an
antipode

{H}\xrightarrow{s}{H}
such that

\mu(s\otimes H)\delta=\eta\varepsilon=\mu(H\otimes s)\delta.

The adjective lax is dropped when both
\sigma
and
s
are isomorphisms.

Since this paper is about augmented monads is very significant for us
that in the Hopf case every augmented monad is representable.

Theorem 2.7
.

[
2
, Corollary 5.18]

There is an equivalence between the categories of
augmented left Hopf monads and lax central Hopf monoids,
which restricts to an equivalence between augmented Hopf monads and
central Hopf monoids.

2.3.
Galois connections induced by functorial relations

This is slight generalization of the Galois connection induced by a relation
relationship on sets. It is completely elementary and should be
folklore, but the author didn’t find any appropriate reference and decided to
include brief but self contained exposition of this.

Definition 2.8
.

We call
functorial relation
between the categories
\mathzc{X}
and

\mathzc{Y}
to a functor
{R}:{\mathzc{X}\times\mathzc{Y}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{2}}
, where

\mathzc{2}=\{\mathrm{true}\to\mathrm{false}\}
is thought as the truth
values of the relation.
In other words, for objects
X
and
Y
in
\mathzc{X}
and
\mathzc{Y}
, respectively,
we say that
X

is related to

Y
when
R(X,Y)=\mathrm{true}
.

Notice that asserting that
R
is a functor simply means that for any pair
of morphisms,
{X^{\prime}}\xrightarrow{f}{X}
and
{Y^{\prime}}\xrightarrow{g}{}Y
, if
X
is related
to
Y
then
X^{\prime}
is related
Y^{\prime}
.

Definition 2.9
.

The
R
-
representant
for an object
X
in
\mathzc{X}
is an object, denoted
R^{\ast}X
, such that for every
Y
in
\mathzc{Y}
,
X
is related to
Y
if and only if there is an unique morphism
Y\to R^{*}X
.
The
R
-representant for a
Y
in
\mathzc{Y}
, denoted

is analogously defined.

The next definition generalizes the case of a Galois connection between
partially ordered sets, to adjunctions with a pre-order as its core.
Remember that a pre-order is a category with at most one morphism among any pair
of objects.

Definition 2.10
.

A
Galois connection
, between the categories
\mathzc{X}
and
\mathzc{Y}
, is a contravariant adjunction
{G}:{\mathzc{Y}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{X}^{op}}\dashv{F}:{\mathzc{X}^{op}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{Y}}
such that

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathzc{Y}}\left(Y,FX\right)\approx\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathzc{X}}\left(X,GY\right)
has at most one element, for arbitrary
X
and
Y
.

Remark 2.11
.

Let
{G}:{\mathzc{Y}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{X}^{op}}\dashv{F}:{\mathzc{X}^{op}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{Y}}
be a Galois connection.

(1)

For any
X
in
\mathzc{X}
, there is a unique morphism

X\to GFX
. We say that
X
is
Galois closed
if this is
an isomorphism. The Galois closed objects in
\mathzc{X}
form a
pre-order.

(2)

Since evaluating
G
at
{Y}\xrightarrow{}{FGY}
gives a morphism

{GFGY}\xrightarrow{}{GY}
, for any
Y
in
\mathzc{Y}
,
GY
is Galois
closed in
\mathzc{X}
. Analogously, for any
X
in
\mathzc{X}
,
FX

is closed in
\mathzc{Y}
.

(3)

In conclusion,
F
and
G
induce an order-reversing equivalence
between Galois closed objects. This is known as the induced

Galois correspondance
.

Now we are able to define the
Galois connection induced by

R
.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
R
be a functorial relationship between
X
and
Y
.

(1)

If every object in
\mathzc{X}
has a representant, then mapping

X\mapsto R^{\ast}X
defines a functor

{R^{\ast}}:{\mathzc{X}^{op}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{Y}}
.

Analogously, when every object in
\mathzc{Y}
has a representant there is
{R_{\ast}}:{\mathzc{Y}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{X}^{op}}
.

(2)

If both
R^{\ast}
and
R_{\ast}
are defined, then they form a Galois connection.

Proof.

Given a morphism
{X^{\prime}}\xrightarrow{f}{X}
in
\mathzc{X}
, since
R
is functorial and
X
is related to
R^{\ast}X
, then
X^{\prime}
is related to
R^{\ast}X
; hence there is an unique

R^{\ast}X\to R^{\ast}X^{\prime}
.

The adjunction plainly recalls that the existence of maps
Y\to R^{\ast}X
and
X\to R_{\ast}Y
are both equivalent to
X
is related to
Y
.
∎

3.
Invariants and stabilizers for augmented monads

This section contains the main result of this paper. It states
that in an augmented monad it is possible to define of invariants
and stabilizers, in a way that naturally establish a Galois correspondence
when they exist.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
T=(T,\mu,\eta)
be a monad over
\mathzc{C}
.
An
augmentation
of
T
is a monad homomorphism

{e}:{T}\Rightarrow{\mathrm{Id}_{\mathzc{C}}}
. This is a natural transformation which satisfies

e\cdot\eta=\mathrm{Id}_{\mathzc{C}}
and
e\cdot\mu=e\star e
.
A monad endowed with an augmentation is called augmented monad.

The intuition for an augmentation, is that the morphism
{TX}\xrightarrow{e_{X}}{X}
is the trivial action for any
X
in
\mathzc{C}
; the action which forgets
T
and leaves
X
fixed.
During this whole section we will work in an augmented monad
T=(T,\mu,\eta,e)
over
a category
\mathzc{C}
and denote by
(\eta,F\dashv U,\varepsilon)
the
Eilenberg-Moore decomposition of
T
; where
{U}:{\mathzc{C}^{T}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{C}}
is
the forgetful functor from the category of
T
-actions, and its left adjoint

F
sends every object in
C
to the free action on it. See definition

2.2
for the details.

3.1.
The fix relation

Let
\operatorname{\mathzc{Monad}}_{\mathzc{C}}
be the category of monads over
\mathzc{C}
.
For brevity, we denote by
S_{h}
a monad homomorphism
{h}:{S}\Rightarrow{T}

considered as object of
\operatorname{\mathzc{Monad}}_{\mathzc{C}}/T
and by
V_{\alpha}
a
natural transformation
{\alpha}:{V}\Rightarrow{U}
from a functor

{V}:{\mathzc{C}^{T}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{C}}
considered as object of

\operatorname{\mathzc{Fun}}(\mathzc{C}^{T},\mathzc{C})/U
.

Since the augmentation provides us with a notion of trivial action, we can
compare any action with the trivial one over the same object. Moreover, we can
restrict this comparison to a part of the monad or to a part of the object. The
next definition does both restrictions simultaneously and then performs the
comparison.

Definition 3.2
.

We say that
S_{h}

fixes

V_{\alpha}
when

(U\varepsilon)\cdot(h\star\alpha)=(eU)\cdot(h\star\alpha).

Let’s expand the above definition for a given
T
-action

M=(X,{TX}\xrightarrow{r_{M}}{X})
:

Since
X=UM
and the action morphism
r_{M}
equals

U\varepsilon_{M}
, then
the left hand side is obtained
by composing
r_{M}
with

(h\star\alpha)_{M}=(T\alpha_{M})h_{VM}=h_{X}(S\alpha_{M})
.

On the other hand,
(eU)_{M}=e_{X}
is the trivial action;
therefore, when
S_{h}
fixes
V_{\alpha}
the restriction of
the trivial action equals the restricted action, this is

r_{M}h_{X}(S\alpha_{M})=e_{X}h_{X}(S\alpha_{M}).

Remark 3.3
.

By Corollary
2.5
, every monad homomorphism

{h}:{S}\Rightarrow{T}
corresponds to a functor
{H}:{\mathzc{C}^{T}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{C}^{S}}

such that
U^{S}H=U
, where
{U^{S}}:{\mathzc{C}^{S}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{C}}
is the forgetful
functor from the category of
S
-actions.
In particular, the augmentation
{e}:{T}\Rightarrow{\mathrm{Id}_{\mathzc{C}}}

corresponds a functor
{E}:{\mathzc{C}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{C}^{T}}
which is
a section of the forgetful functor, i.e.
UE=\mathrm{Id}_{\mathzc{C}}
.

Recalling the definitions, any
X
in
\mathzc{C}
gets mapped to

EX=(X,{TX}\xrightarrow{e_{X}}{X})
, and any
M=(X,{TX}\xrightarrow{r_{M}}{X})
in
\mathzc{C}^{T}

gets mapped to
HM=(X,{SX}\xrightarrow{r_{M}h_{X}}{X})
.

The next lemma allow us to reinterpret the definition of acting trivially in terms of the functors defined in the above remark.

Lemma 3.4
.

The homomorphism
{h}:{S}\Rightarrow{T}
fixes
{\alpha}:{V}\Rightarrow{U}
if and only if there is a natural transformation

{\hat{\alpha}}:{HEV}\Rightarrow{H}
such that
U^{S}\hat{\alpha}=\alpha
.

Proof.

First notice that
H
and
HEV
are a lifts of
U
and
V
, respectively,
along the monads
\mathrm{Id}_{\mathzc{C}^{T}}
and
S
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathzc{C}^{T}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathzc{C}^{S}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathzc{C}^{T}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathzc{C}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U^{S}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Id}_{\mathzc{C}^{T}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces HEV}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bar{\alpha}}

The lifting data for
H
over
U
is
(U\varepsilon)\cdot(hU)

and for
HEV
over
V
is
(e\cdot h)V
.

As an application of Proposition
2.6
, there is a lift
\bar{\alpha}
if and only if

\alpha\cdot((e\cdot h)V)=(eU)\cdot(h\star\alpha)

equals

(U\varepsilon)\cdot(hU)\cdot(S\alpha)=(U\varepsilon)\cdot(h\star\alpha)

which is the definition of

.
∎

Now we will check that the fix relationship is preserved
whenever we restrict further.
In other words that fixing defines a functorial relation
between
\operatorname{\mathzc{Monad}}_{\mathzc{C}}/T
and
\operatorname{\mathzc{Fun}}(\mathzc{C}^{T},\mathzc{C})/U
.

Afterwards, we will apply Definition
2.9
and Proposition
2.12
,
to this relation, in order to define a Galois connection.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
{g}:{S^{\prime}}\Rightarrow{S}
be any monad homomorphism and

{\beta}:{V^{\prime}}\Rightarrow{V}
be any natural transformation.
If
{h}:{S}\Rightarrow{T}
fixes
{\alpha}:{V}\Rightarrow{U}
, then
{h\cdot g}:{S^{\prime}}\Rightarrow{T}
fixes
{\alpha\cdot\beta}:{V^{\prime}}\Rightarrow{U}
.

Proof.

This is obvious from the facts that
(h\cdot g)\star(\alpha\cdot\beta)=(h\star\alpha)\cdot(g\star\beta)
and the definition of fixing

(U\varepsilon)\cdot(h\star\alpha)=(eU)\cdot(h\star\alpha)
.
∎

Definition 3.6
.

Let
{h}:{S}\Rightarrow{T}
be a monad homomorphism and
{\alpha}:{V}\Rightarrow{U}
a natural transformation,
where
{U}:{\mathzc{C}^{T}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{C}}
is the forgetful functor.

(1)

We say that
h
is
the stabilizer of

\alpha
(or
S

is the stabilizer of
V
) if: it fixes
\alpha
and for every
monad homomorphism
{h^{\prime}}:{S^{\prime}}\Rightarrow{T}
that also fixes
\alpha
there
is an unique
{g}:{S^{\prime}}\Rightarrow{S}
such that
h^{\prime}=h\cdot g
.

(2)

We say that
\alpha
is the
h
-
invariant inclusion
(or

V
is the functor of
S
-invariants) if: it is fixed by
h
and
for every functor
{\alpha^{\prime}}:{V^{\prime}}\rightarrow{U}
also fixed by
h
there is
an unique
{\beta}:{V^{\prime}}\rightarrow{V}
such that

\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha\cdot\beta
.

The next corollary is an straightforward application of Proposition
2.12
, to the fixing relationship.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
\mathzc{M}
and
\mathzc{N}
be subcategories of
\operatorname{\mathzc{Monad}}(\mathzc{C})/T

and
\operatorname{\mathzc{Fun}}(\mathzc{C}^{T},\mathzc{C})/U
, respectively.

(1)

If every
h
in
\mathzc{M}
has an
h
-invariant inclusion in

\mathzc{N}
, then the mapping
h\mapsto\operatorname{Inv}h
defines a
functor
{\operatorname{Inv}}:{\mathzc{M}^{op}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{N}}
. Analogously, If every

\alpha
in
\mathzc{N}
has an stabilizer in
\mathzc{M}
, then the
mapping
\alpha\mapsto\operatorname{Stab}\alpha
defines a functor

{\operatorname{Stab}}:{\mathzc{N}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{M}^{op}}
.

(2)

If both
\operatorname{Stab}
and
\operatorname{Inv}
are defined, then they form a Galois connection.

4.
Computing invariants via right adjoints

Now that we have an abstract definition of invariants and stabilizers, we face
the concrete — or slightly less abstract — questions of determining when
they exist and how can we compute it. This segment offers an answer for the case
of invariants, by linking them to right adjoints.
Remember that
(T,\mu,\eta,e)
is an augmented monad over
\mathzc{C}
.

First we observe that when the domain monad has a right adjoint we compute
invariants as an equalizer.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
{h}:{S}\Rightarrow{T}
be a monad homomorphism and assume that the monad

{S}:{\mathzc{C}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{C}}
has a right adjoint,

(\theta,S\dashv R,\upsilon)
.

For any
{\alpha}:{V}\Rightarrow{U}
,
S_{h}
fixes
V_{\alpha}
if and only if for
each
M=(X,r_{M})
in
\mathzc{C}^{T}
,

(R(eh)_{X})(\theta_{X})\alpha_{M}=(R(r_{M}h_{X}))(\theta_{X})\alpha_{M}
.

In consequence, when the pair
(R(eh)_{X})(\theta_{X})
and

(R(r_{M}h_{X}))(\theta_{X})
has an equalizer for every
M
, this
equalizer defines the functor of
S_{h}
-invariants.

Proof.

Just recall that
S_{h}
fixes
V_{\alpha}
when

r_{M}h_{X}(S\alpha_{M})=e_{X}h_{X}(S\alpha_{M})
for every
M
, and
bend
S
to
R
in both sides of the equation.
In other words,

(R((U\varepsilon)\cdot(hU)))\cdot(\theta U)\cdot\alpha=(R(e\cdot h)U)\cdot(\theta U)\cdot\alpha.

Since equalizers are funtorial, the last sentence is evident.
∎

The next example illustrate the above lemma in the case of a
\otimes
-representable monad.

Example 4.2
.

Let
T=A\otimes\mathbf{\qm}
for an augmented monoid

in a monoidal category
(\mathzc{C},\otimes,\mathbbm{1})
.
Assume that
A
is right closed in
\mathzc{C}

and that
\mathzc{C}
has equalizers.
The functor of
A
-invariants
\operatorname{Inv}A:=\operatorname{Inv}T

sends each action
M=(X,{A\otimes X}\xrightarrow{r_{M}}{X})

to the equalizer of

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\left[A,X\right]}^{\mathit{r}}.}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ulcorner r_{M}\urcorner}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ulcorner\epsilon\otimes X\urcorner}

Moreover, given a monoid homomorphism
{B}\xrightarrow{h}{A}
,
with
B
also right closed in
C
.

The functor of
B
-invariants
\operatorname{Inv}B:=\operatorname{Inv}(B\otimes\mathbf{\qm})_{h\otimes\mathbf{\qm}}

sends each action to the equalizer of

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\left[A,X\right]}^{\mathit{r}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\left[B,X\right]}^{\mathit{r}}}.}

4.1.
Invariants are right adjoints

Right adjoints not only allow us to compute invariants.
In fact, the
T
-invariants,
i.e the invariants for
{\mathrm{id}_{T}}:{T}\Rightarrow{T}
,
are right adjoints of the trivial action functor
E
.
Where
{E}:{\mathzc{C}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{C}}^{T}
is the functor corresponding to the
augmentation
{e}:{T}\Rightarrow{\mathrm{Id}_{\mathzc{C}}}
, remember that it endows
every object in
\mathzc{C}
with the trivial action given by the augmentation.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
E
has a right adjoint functor
{\Gamma}:{\mathzc{C}^{T}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{C}}
,
then
\Gamma
is the functor of
T
-invariants.

Proof.

Take the adjunction
(\zeta,E\dashv\Gamma,\bar{\gamma})
and define
{\gamma}:{\Gamma}\Rightarrow{U}
as
\gamma=U\bar{\gamma}
.
Hence by Lemma
3.4
,
T
fixes
\Gamma_{\gamma}
.
Moreover by the same lemma, if
T
fixes
{\alpha}:{V}\Rightarrow{U}

there is
{\bar{\alpha}}:{EV}\Rightarrow{\mathrm{Id}_{\mathzc{C}^{T}}}
such that
\alpha=U\bar{\alpha}
.
We need a
{\beta}:{V}\Rightarrow{\Gamma}
such that
\alpha=\gamma\cdot\beta=(U\bar{\gamma})\cdot(UE\beta)
since
U
is faithful
\bar{\alpha}=\bar{\gamma}\cdot(E\beta)
,
after bending the
E

we obtain that
(\Gamma\bar{\alpha})\cdot(\zeta V)=\beta
.

Since
E=(\bar{\gamma}E)\cdot(E\zeta)
then

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\bar{\alpha}\cdot(\bar{\gamma}EV)\cdot(E\zeta V)

\displaystyle=\bar{\gamma}\cdot(E\Gamma\bar{\alpha})\cdot(E\zeta V).

Therefore
\alpha=U\bar{\alpha}=\gamma\cdot(\Gamma\bar{\alpha})\cdot(\zeta V)
.
∎

Right adjoints also allow to change the category where one is calculating.
Let
{h}:{S}\Rightarrow{T}
be a monad homomorphism, with correspondent functor

{H}:{\mathzc{C}^{T}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{C}^{S}}
as in Remark
3.3
; in particular
U=U^{S}H
.

Notice that
(S,\mu^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},eh)
is also an augmented monad and
instead of computing
h
-invariants in
\mathzc{C}^{T}
, we can try to compute

S
-invariants in
\mathzc{C}^{S}
and pull them back.
The next two lemmas show that this strategy is successful when
H
has a right adjoint.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(\zeta,H\dashv K,\omega)
be an adjunction
with
{H}:{\mathzc{C}^{T}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{C}^{S}}
,
coming from a monad homomorphism
{h}:{S}\Rightarrow{T}
.
For any
{\alpha}:{V}\Rightarrow{U}
,
S_{h}
fixes
V_{\alpha}
is equivalent to

S
fixes
{\hat{\alpha}}:{VK}\Rightarrow{U^{S}}
,
where
\hat{\alpha}=(U^{S}\omega)\cdot(\alpha K)
.

Proof.

We will use Lemma
3.4
.
If
S_{h}
fixes
V_{\alpha}
, there is
{\bar{\alpha}}:{HEV}\Rightarrow{H}
such that
\alpha=U^{S}\bar{\alpha}
.
Therefore

\hat{\alpha}=(U^{S}\omega)\cdot(\alpha K)=U^{S}(\omega\cdot(\bar{\alpha}K)).

Hence, by the same lemma
S
fixes
(VK)_{\hat{\alpha}}
.

For the reciprocal, notice that
\alpha=(\hat{\alpha}H)\cdot(V\zeta)
by bending
K
to
H
.
Now the proof is completely analogous:

If
S
fixes
\hat{\alpha}
, there is
{\tilde{\alpha}}:{HEVK}\Rightarrow{\mathrm{Id}_{\mathzc{C}^{S}}}

such that
\hat{\alpha}=U^{S}\tilde{\alpha}
.
Therefore

\alpha=(\hat{\alpha}H)\cdot(V\zeta)=U^{S}((\tilde{\alpha}H)\cdot(HEV\zeta)),

since
U^{S}HE=UE=\mathrm{Id}_{\mathzc{C}}
.
The same lemma tells us that
S_{h}
fixes
V_{\alpha}
.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
{h}:{S}\Rightarrow{T}
be a monad homomorphism and suppose that

{H}:{\mathzc{C}^{T}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{C}^{S}}
has a right adjoint. If

{\gamma^{S}}:{\Gamma^{S}}\Rightarrow{U^{S}}
is the
S
-invariant inclusion, then

{\gamma^{S}H}:{\Gamma^{S}H}\Rightarrow{U}
is the
h
-invariant inclusion.

Proof.

With the notations from the previous lemma,

S_{h}
fixes
V_{\alpha}
if and only
if
S
fixes
\hat{\alpha}
.
Since
\gamma^{S}
is the
S
-invariant inclusion, there is an unique

{\hat{\beta}}:{\Gamma^{S}}\Rightarrow{VK}
such that

\hat{\alpha}=\gamma^{S}\cdot\hat{\beta}
.
Remembering that
\alpha=(\hat{\alpha}H)\cdot(V\omega)
we obtain

\alpha=((\gamma^{S}\cdot\hat{\beta})H)\cdot(V\omega)=(\gamma^{S}H)\cdot\beta

where
\beta=(\hat{\beta}H)\cdot(V\omega)
.
∎

We end this segment with a summary of the computation of invariants
by means of right adjoints.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
(T,\mu,\eta,e)
be an augmented monad,
and
{h}:{S}\Rightarrow{T}
a monad homomorphism.
Take
{E}:{\mathzc{C}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{C}}^{T}
and
{H}:{\mathzc{C}^{S}}\rightarrow{\mathzc{C}^{T}}
the functors corresponding to

and

, respectively;
and assume that they are part of adjunctions

and

.

Then
\operatorname{Inv}T=\Gamma_{\gamma}
with

,

\operatorname{Inv}S=\Gamma K_{\hat{\gamma}}
with

\hat{\gamma}=(U^{S}\omega)\cdot(U\bar{\gamma}K),

and
\operatorname{Inv}S_{h}=\Gamma KH_{\hat{\gamma}H}

with
{\hat{\gamma}H}=(U^{S}\omega H)\cdot(U\bar{\gamma}KH)
.

Proof.

This is Lemma

applied twice to

and

, and Corollary

.
Notice that when

has also a left adjoint

then

.
∎

5.
Computing stabilizers in monoidal closed categories

Currently we don’t know of any procedure for explicitly
describing the stabilizers for augmented monads in
categories without additional structure.
We imagine a description in terms of Kan extensions,
but we have not succeeded in formalizing it.
However, in the case of a
\otimes
-representable monad over a
monoidal closed category,
we can use
ends
as a tool for the computation of stabilizers.
This is a procedure inpired by Tannakian reconstruction.
encompass augmented Hopf monads in monoidal closed categories.

5.1.
The end enrichement of functor categories

For this section, we assume familiarity with the basic properties of ends, as presented for instance in
[
5
, Sections IX. 5-8]
.
For fixing notation, we briefly recall the definition.

Definition 5.1
.

A

for a functor

is a pair
(W,\xi_{X})_{X\in\mathzc{D}}

consisting of an object

in
\mathzc{C}
and

{W}\xrightarrow{\xi_{X}}{S(X,X)}
indexed by the objects in
\mathzc{C}

such that for any morphism
{X}\xrightarrow{f}{Y}
in
\mathzc{D}
the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{S(X,X)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{S(Y,Y)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{S(X,Y)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\xi_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S(X,f)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\xi_{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S(f,Y)}

The

of

\int_{\mathzc{D}}S=\int_{X\in\mathzc{D}}S(X,X)=(L,\lambda_{X})_{X\in\mathzc{D}}

\mathzc{C}

The next definition allow us to view the natural transformations
as an object in the co-domain category.

Definition 5.2
.

Let

and

\mathzc{D}
to

(\mathzc{C},\otimes,\mathbbm{1})

,

\mathzc{D}

.

, and denote it by

{\left[V,W\right]}^{\ell}:=\int_{\mathzc{D}}{\left[V\mathbf{\qm}_{1},W\mathbf{\qm}_{2}\right]}^{\ell}=\int_{D\in\mathzc{D}}{\left[VD,WD\right]}^{\ell}=({\left[V,W\right]}^{\ell},{{\left[V,W\right]}^{\ell}}\xrightarrow{\lambda_{D}}{{\left[VD,WD\right]}^{\ell}})_{D\in\mathzc{D}}.

If the internal Nat

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\left[V,W\right]}^{\ell}}}

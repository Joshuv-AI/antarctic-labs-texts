Cells in modified ı \imath quantum groups of type AIII and related Schur algebras
Weideng Cui

Abstract
. We provide a combinatorial characterization of two-sided cells in modified ı \imath quantum groups of type AIII. Our approach is to lift a corresponding description of two-sided cells in ȷ \jmath -Schur algebras associated to Iwahori–Hecke algebras of type B B . We further give a combinatorial description of two-sided cells in other two kinds of Schur-type algebras, namely ı \imath -Schur algebras and ı ~ \tilde{\imath} -Schur algebras.

Primary 17B10

1.
Introduction

1.1.

In the study of primitive ideals in the enveloping algebra of a complex semisimple Lie algebra, Joseph
[
Jo77
]
has defined the concept of left, right and two-sided cells in the Weyl group
W
. Joseph showed that each left cell can naturally carry a representation of
W
, and the definition of left cells and the corresponding Weyl group representations involved some unknown quantities: the multiplicities in the Jordan–Hölder series of the Verma modules with highest weight. Kazhdan and Lusztig
[
KL79
]
have proposed a conjectural algorithm for these multiplicities, which was soon proved by Beilinson–Bernstein
[
BB81
]
and Brylinski–Kashiwara
[
BK81
]
, respectively; moreover, they defined left, right and two-sided cells for an arbitrary Coxeter group through the so-called Kazhdan–Lusztig basis. Kazhdan and Lusztig’s definition of cells has the advantage that it gives rise not only to representations of the Coxeter group, but also of the corresponding Iwahori–Hecke algebra. Later on, Barbasch and Vogan
[
BV82
,
BV83
]
determined explicitly the equivalence relation
E\sim_{LR}E^{\prime}
on irreducible representations of
W
defined by the requirement that
E,E^{\prime}
appear in the same two-sided cell.

In
[
Lu10
, §29.4.2]
, Lusztig gave the definition of left, right and two-sided cells for an associative algebra with a given basis; in
[
Lu10
, Part IV]
and also
[
Lu95
]
, Lusztig has studied the structure of cells in the modified quantum group with respect to the canonical basis. In
[
Du96
]
, Du has given a combinatorial description of two-sided cells in the
q
-Schur algebra of type
A
with respect to its Kazhdan–Lusztig type basis, in terms of which he also gave a classification of two-sided cells in the modified quantum group of type
A
. In
[
McG03
]
, McGerty has obtained an explicit description of two-sided cells in the modified quantum group of affine type
A
via first investigating the structure of cells in affine
q
-Schur algebras of type
A
.

1.2.

Given an involution
\theta
on a complex simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, we can obtain a symmetric pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{\theta})
, or a pair of enveloping algebras
(\mathbf{U}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathbf{U}(\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}))
, where
\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}
denotes the fixed point subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
under
\theta
. The classification of symmetric pairs of finite type is closely related to that of real simple Lie algebras, which can be described in terms of the Satake diagrams. In the 1990’s, Noumi, Sugitani and Dijkhuizen
[
NS95
,
N96
,
NDS97
]
, based on solutions to the reflection equations, constructed quantum group analogs of
\mathbf{U}(\mathfrak{g}^{\theta})
as coideal subalgebras of
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
for all
\mathfrak{g}
of classical type, where
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
is the quantized enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. Independently, Letzter
[
Le99
,
Le02
]
systematically developed the theory of quantum symmetric pairs
(\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathbf{U}^{\imath})
of finite type as a quantization of
(\mathbf{U}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathbf{U}(\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}))
. The algebra
\mathbf{U}^{\imath}
will be referred to as an
\imath
quantum group. Kolb
[
Ko14
]
has further studied and generalized Letzter’s theory to the Kac–Moody type.

In recent years, Bao and Wang
[
BW18
]
have initiated a new theory of the canonical basis (called
\imath
-canonical basis) arising from quantum symmetric pairs of type AIII without black nodes; as an application, they established for the first time a Kazhdan–Lusztig theory for the BGG category
\mathcal{O}
of the ortho-symplectic Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(2m+1|2n)
(see also
[
B17
]
for the case of
\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(2m|2n)
). Simultaneously and independently from
[
BW18
]
, Ehrig and Stroppel
[
ES18
]
have discovered the connections between coideal subalgebras of type AIII and the parabolic category
\mathcal{O}
of type
D
; in particular, they gave a categorification of the coideal subalgebras and (quantum) skew Howe duality. Subsequently, Bao and Wang have developed a general theory of
\imath
-canonical bases for modified
\imath
quantum groups arising from quantum symmetric pairs of arbitrary finite type in
[
BW18b
]
and of Kac–Moody type in
[
BW21
]
, respectively.

In
[
BKLW18
]
the authors defined a new Schur-type algebra
S^{\jmath}(n,d)
, which is attached to the Iwahori–Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}
of type
B_{d}
with equal parameters and called the
\jmath
-Schur algebra, together with a canonical basis. Via a stabilization procedure as
d
varies, these algebras give rise to a limit algebra which was shown to be isomorphic to the idempotented
\imath
quantum group
\dot{\mathbf{U}}^{\jmath}(\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
. From the process, they established a natural surjective algebra homomorphism from
\dot{\mathbf{U}}^{\jmath}(\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
to
S^{\jmath}(n,d)
. Based on these, Li and Wang
[
LiW18
]
further defined and studied the modified
\imath
quantum group
\dot{\mathbf{U}}^{\jmath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
as well as its
\jmath
-canonical basis (
[
LiW18
, Theorem 5.5]
), which has many remarkable properties. For example, the
\jmath
-canonical basis has positive structure constants, and it is almost orthonormal and admits positivity with respect to a bilinear form on
\dot{\mathbf{U}}^{\jmath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
. Besides, there also exists a natural surjective homomorphism from
\dot{\mathbf{U}}^{\jmath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
to
S^{\jmath}(n,d)
, which sends canonical basis elements to canonical basis elements or zero as proven in
[
BSWW18
]
. Later on, Lai and Luo
[
LL21
]
have studied the
\jmath
-Schur algebras associated to Iwahori–Hecke algebras of type
B/C
with unequal parameters. Moreover, the authors
[
FLLLW20
,
FLLLW22
]
have given two constructions of the modified
\imath
quantum groups associated to some coideal subalgebras of quantum groups of affine type
A
via a geometric and algebraic approach, respectively.

In recent years, the
\imath
quantum groups have been studied through various approaches (see
[
W21
]
for a survey).

1.3.

In this paper, we would like to give a characterization of two-sided cells in the modified
\imath
quantum group
\dot{\mathbf{U}}^{\jmath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
with respect to the
\jmath
-canonical basis defined by Li and Wang. In order to do this, we first give a combinatorial description of two-sided cells in
S^{\jmath}(n,d)
with respect to its canonical basis, and then lift this description to
\dot{\mathbf{U}}^{\jmath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
via the algebra homomorphism mentioned above.

This paper has provided the first step towards understanding the structure of cells in modified
\imath
quantum groups. Since the
\imath
-canonical basis exists for modified
\imath
quantum groups of general type by Bao and Wang’s work, we are planning to investigate the corresponding structure of cells in a future publication.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall the definition of cells for an arbitrary associative algebra with a given basis following Lusztig.

In Section 3, we first present a classification of left, right and two-sided cells in
S^{\jmath}(n,d)
in terms of the ones in
\mathcal{H}
. Then we provide a combinatorial description of two-sided cells in
S^{\jmath}(n,d)
, using that of two-sided cells in
\mathcal{H}
due to Barbasch and Vogan and also a result of Du. Finally, we state a conjecture on the number of left cells in a two-sided cell of
S^{\jmath}(n,d)
.

In Section 4, we lift the combinatorial description of two-sided cells in
S^{\jmath}(n,d)
to give a characterization of two-sided cells in the modified
\imath
quantum group
\dot{\mathbf{U}}^{\jmath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
.

In Section 5, we focus on another Schur-type algebra associated to
\mathcal{H}
, namely the
\imath
-Schur algebra
S^{\imath}(n,d)
. Parallel to Section 3 we formulate a combinatorial description of two-sided cells in
S^{\imath}(n,d)
.

In Section 6, we consider the
\tilde{\imath}
-Schur algebra
\tilde{S}^{\imath}(n,d)
attached to
\mathcal{H}_{C_{d}}^{1}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{C_{d}}^{1}
is the specialization at
p=1
of the Iwahori–Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{C_{d}}^{p}
of type
C_{d}
with unequal parameters
p
and
q
. We give an approach to determining whether or not two canonical basis elements of
\tilde{S}^{\imath}(n,d)
lie in the same two-sided cell.

2.
Definition of cells

Let
q
be an indeterminate and set
\mathcal{A}={\mathbb{Z}}[q,q^{-1}]
. Assume that
R
is an arbitrary ring. Let
\mathfrak{A}
be an associative algebra over
R
and
\mathbb{B}
a basis of
\mathfrak{A}

as an
R
-module. We do not assume that
\mathfrak{A}
has
1
. The structure constants
h_{a,b}^{c}\in R
of
\mathfrak{A}
(where
a,b,c\in\mathbb{B}
) are defined by
ab=\sum\limits_{c\in\mathbb{B}}h_{a,b}^{c}c
. Then
\mathbb{B}
is divided into
cells
, by Lusztig
[
Lu10
, §29.4.2]
, via the equivalence relations on
\mathbb{B}
defined as follows.

For
b,b^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}
, we write
b^{\prime}\leftarrow_{L}b
(resp.
b^{\prime}\leftarrow_{R}b
) if there exists an element
c\in\mathbb{B}
such that the coefficient of
b^{\prime}
is nonzero when expanding
cb
(resp.
bc
); we say that
b^{\prime}\preceq_{L}b
(resp.
b^{\prime}\preceq_{R}b
) if there exists a sequence
b_{1}=b,b_{2},\ldots,b_{n}=b^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{B}
such that
b_{i+1}\leftarrow_{L}b_{i}
(resp.
b_{i+1}\leftarrow_{R}b_{i}
) for any
i=1,\ldots,n-1
. We say that
b^{\prime}\preceq_{LR}b
if there is a sequence
b_{1}=b,b_{2},\ldots,b_{n}=b^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{B}
such that for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
we have either
b_{i+1}\leftarrow_{L}b_{i}
or
b_{i+1}\leftarrow_{R}b_{i}
. Clearly
\preceq_{L}
,
\preceq_{R}
,
\preceq_{LR}
are preorders on
\mathbb{B}
. For
\star\in\{L,R,LR\}
and
b,b^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}
, we say that
b\sim_{\star}b^{\prime}
if
b\preceq_{\star}b^{\prime}
and
b^{\prime}\preceq_{\star}b
. Then
\sim_{L}
,
\sim_{R}
,
\sim_{LR}
give rise to equivalence relations on
\mathbb{B}
; the corresponding equivalence classes are called
left, right
and
two-sided cells
of
\mathfrak{A},
respectively.

The following lemma provides another characterization of these preorders.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
R=\mathcal{A}
and all
h_{a,b}^{c}\in\mathbb{N}[q,q^{-1}]
. Then
b^{\prime}\preceq_{L}b^{\prime\prime}
(resp.
b^{\prime}\preceq_{R}b^{\prime\prime}
;
b^{\prime}\preceq_{LR}b^{\prime\prime}
) if and only if there exists
\beta
(resp.
\beta^{\prime}
;
\beta,\beta^{\prime}
) in
\mathbb{B}
such that
e_{b^{\prime}}\neq 0
(resp.
f_{b^{\prime}}\neq 0
;
g_{b^{\prime}}\neq 0
), where
e_{b^{\prime}}
(resp.
f_{b^{\prime}}
;
g_{b^{\prime}}
) is defined by

\displaystyle\beta b^{\prime\prime}=\sum_{c\in\mathbb{B}}e_{c}c,\quad e_{c}\in\mathcal{A},

\displaystyle(resp.~b^{\prime\prime}\beta^{\prime}=\sum_{c\in\mathbb{B}}f_{c}c,\quad f_{c}\in\mathcal{A};\quad\beta b^{\prime\prime}\beta^{\prime}=\sum_{c\in\mathbb{B}}g_{c}c,\quad g_{c}\in\mathcal{A}).

Proof.

The sufficiency is obvious. Assume that
b^{\prime}\preceq_{L}b^{\prime\prime}
, by definition there exists a sequence
b_{1}=b^{\prime\prime},b_{2},\ldots,b_{n}=b^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{B}
and a sequence
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n-1}
in
\mathbb{B}
such that
h_{\beta_{i},b_{i}}^{b_{i+1}}\neq 0
for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
. Since all
h_{a,b}^{c}\in\mathbb{N}[q,q^{-1}]
, we see that when expanding
\beta_{n-1}\cdots\beta_{1}b^{\prime\prime}
, the coefficient of
b^{\prime}
is
h_{\beta_{1},b^{\prime\prime}}^{b_{2}}\cdots h_{\beta_{n-1},b_{n-1}}^{b^{\prime}}+f(q)
for some
f(q)\in\mathbb{N}[q,q^{-1}]
, which must be nonzero. Therefore, there exists some
\beta\in\mathbb{B}
such that the coefficient
e_{b^{\prime}}
of
b^{\prime}
is nonzero when expanding
\beta b^{\prime\prime}
.
∎

3.
Description of two-sided cells in
S^{\jmath}(n,d)

3.1.
Preliminaries

Fix
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
. Let
W_{B_{d}}
be the Weyl group of type
B_{d}
with generators
S=\{s_{0},s_{1},\ldots,s_{d-1}\}
and the identity element
e
. Let
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{B_{d}}
be the associated
Iwahori–Hecke algebra
over
\mathcal{A}
with a basis
\{T_{w}\>|\>w\in W_{B_{d}}\}
satisfying the following relations:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{w}T_{w^{\prime}}&=T_{ww^{\prime}}\quad\mathrm{if}~w,w^{\prime}\in W_{B_{d}}~\mathrm{with}~\ell(ww^{\prime})=\ell(w)+\ell(w^{\prime}),\\
T_{s_{i}}^{2}&=1+(q-q^{-1})T_{s_{i}}\quad\mathrm{for}~0\leq i\leq d-1,\end{split}

where
\ell
is the
length function
on
W_{B_{d}}
(cf.
[
Lu03
, §1.1]
).

By
[
Lu03
, Lemma 4.2]
there exists a unique
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra automorphism
\bar{\cdot}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
given by

\overline{q}=q^{-1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\overline{T_{w}}=T_{w^{-1}}^{-1}~(\forall~w\in W_{B_{d}}).

Fix
n=2r+1\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 3}
. Let
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
. Following
[
LL21
, (2.1.8)]
we set

\Lambda^{\jmath}(n,d):=\{\lambda=(\lambda_{i})_{-r\leq i\leq r}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\>|\>\lambda_{-i}=\lambda_{i}\mathrm{~for~}1\leq i\leq r,~\sum\limits_{i=-r}^{r}\lambda_{i}=2d+1\}.

For each
\lambda\in\Lambda^{\jmath}(n,d)
, let
W_{\lambda}
be the
parabolic subgroup
of
W_{B_{d}}
generated by the following simple reflections

S\setminus\{s_{\lfloor\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}\rfloor},s_{\lfloor\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}\rfloor+\lambda_{1}},\ldots,s_{\lfloor\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}\rfloor+\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{r-1}}\},

and set
x_{\lambda}:=\sum_{w\in W_{\lambda}}q^{\ell(w)}T_{w}
.

Following
[
BKLW18
, §2.3]
and also
[
LL21
, (3.1.6)]
we define the
\jmath
-Schur algebra
of type
B_{d}
over
\mathcal{A}
by

S^{\jmath}(n,d)={\mathrm{End}}_{\mathcal{H}}(\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\jmath}(n,d)}x_{\lambda}\mathcal{H}).

For
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda^{\jmath}(n,d)
, let
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda\mu}
(resp.
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda\mu}^{+}
) be the set of minimal (resp. maximal) length double coset representatives in
W_{\lambda}\backslash W_{B_{d}}/W_{\mu}
. Set

\Xi:=\{(\lambda,g,\mu)~|~\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda^{\jmath}(n,d),g\in\mathcal{D}_{\lambda\mu}\}

and

\widetilde{\Xi}:=\{(\lambda,g,\mu)~|~\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda^{\jmath}(n,d),g\in\mathcal{D}_{\lambda\mu}^{+}\}.

It is well-known that
\Xi
is in bijection with
\widetilde{\Xi}
(cf.
[
Lu03
, Proposition 9.15]
) and we shall identify them.

Let
D=2d+1
. Following
[
LL21
, (2.2.1-2)]
we set

\Theta_{n,d}:=\{(a_{ij})_{-r\leq i,j\leq r}\in\mathrm{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{N})\>|\>\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}=D\}

and

\Pi_{n,d}:=\{(a_{ij})\in\Theta_{n,d}\>|\>a_{ij}=a_{-i,-j}\mathrm{~for~all~}i,j\}.

Following
[
LL21
, (2.1.9)]
, for each
\lambda=(\lambda_{i})_{-r\leq i\leq r}\in\Lambda^{\jmath}(n,d)
and an integer
-r\leq i\leq r
we define an integer interval
R_{i}^{\lambda}\subset\mathbb{Z}
by

\displaystyle R_{i}^{\lambda}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Big[\lfloor\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}\rfloor+\sum\limits_{1\leq j<i}\lambda_{j}+1,\lfloor\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}\rfloor+\sum\limits_{1\leq j\leq i}\lambda_{j}\Big]&\hskip 9.95863pt\text{if $0<i\leq r$},\\[3.00003pt]
\big[-\lfloor\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}\rfloor,\lfloor\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}\rfloor\big]&\hskip 9.95863pt\text{if $i=0$},\\[3.00003pt]
-R_{-i}^{\lambda}&\hskip 9.95863pt\text{if $-r\leq i<0$}.\end{array}\right.

Note that by
[
LL21
, (2.1.10)]
the parabolic subgroup
W_{\lambda}
can be characterized by

\displaystyle W_{\lambda}=\bigcap_{0\leq i\leq r}\mathrm{Stab}(R_{i}^{\lambda})\quad\text{ for each $\lambda\in\Lambda^{\jmath}(n,d)$},

where
\mathrm{Stab}(R_{i}^{\lambda})
denotes the stabilizer of
R_{i}^{\lambda}
in
W_{B_{d}}
.

By
[
LL21
, Lemma 2.2.1]
(see also
[
JK81
, §1.3.8-10]
for the case of the symmetric group), we have the following results.

Lemma 3.1
.

(1)

For any
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda^{\jmath}(n,d)
and
x,y\in W_{B_{d}}
, we have
x\in W_{\lambda}yW_{\mu}
if and only if
\sharp\big(R_{i}^{\lambda}\cap y(R_{j}^{\mu})\big)=\sharp\big(R_{i}^{\lambda}\cap x(R_{j}^{\mu})\big)
for all integers
-r\leq i,j\leq r
.

(2)

Moreover, assuming that
\lambda=(\lambda_{i})_{-r\leq i\leq r}
and
\mu=(\mu_{i})_{-r\leq i\leq r}
, the map

\displaystyle f:W_{\lambda}yW_{\mu}\mapsto(a_{ij})_{-r\leq i,j\leq r},\quad\text{where $a_{ij}=\sharp\big(R_{i}^{\lambda}\cap y(R_{j}^{\mu})\big)$}

establishes a bijection between the set of double cosets of
W_{\lambda}
and
W_{\mu}
in
W_{B_{d}}
and the set of matrices
(a_{ij})
in
\Pi_{n,d}
which satisfy

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}=\sum\limits_{-r\leq j\leq r}a_{kj}\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_{k}=\sum\limits_{-r\leq i\leq r}a_{ik}\quad\text{ for $-r\leq k\leq r$}.

By Lemma
3.1
, there is a natural bijection between
\Xi
or
\widetilde{\Xi}
and
\Pi_{n,d}
, and we shall identify them. In the following, if
A\in\Pi_{n,d}
, we shall denote by
(ro(A),w_{A},co(A))
and
(ro(A),w_{A}^{+},co(A))
the corresponding element in
\Xi
and
\widetilde{\Xi}
, respectively.

For
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda^{\jmath}(n,d)
and
w\in\mathcal{D}_{\lambda\mu}
, we define
\phi_{\lambda,\mu}^{w}\in S^{\jmath}(n,d)
by

\phi_{\lambda,\mu}^{w}(x_{\nu}h)=\delta_{\mu,\nu}\sum_{z\in W_{\lambda}wW_{\mu}}q^{\ell(z)}T_{z}h\quad\mathrm{for~}h\in\mathcal{H}.

Then the set
\{\phi_{\lambda,\mu}^{w}\>|\>\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda^{\jmath}(n,d)\mathrm{~and~}w\in\mathcal{D}_{\lambda\mu}\}
forms an
\mathcal{A}
-basis of
S^{\jmath}(n,d)
(see
[
LL21
, Lemma 3.2]
).

Following
[
LL21
, (2.2.6)]
we define two subsets
I_{\mathfrak{a}}
and
I
in
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
by

(3.2)

\displaystyle I_{\mathfrak{a}}=(\{0\}\times[1,r])\sqcup([1,r]\times[-r,r])\quad\text{ and }\quad I=I_{\mathfrak{a}}\sqcup\{(0,0)\}.

For each
(i,j)\in I
, we set

\displaystyle a_{ij}^{\natural}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{2}(a_{ij}-1)&\hskip 9.95863pt\text{if $(i,j)=(0,0)$},\\[3.00003pt]
a_{ij}&\hskip 9.95863pt\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Following
[
LL21
, (4.2.1)]
or
[
BKLW18
, (3.16)]
, for each
A\in\Pi_{n,d}
, we define
d_{A}
by

d_{A}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in I}\Bigg(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq i\\
y>j\end{subarray}}a_{ij}^{\natural}a_{xy}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\geq i\\
y<j\end{subarray}}a_{ij}^{\natural}a_{xy}\Bigg).

For each
A\in\Pi_{n,d}
, if
A=(\lambda,w_{A},\mu)
, following
[
LL21
, (4.2.3)]
we set

(3.5)

\displaystyle[A]=q^{-d_{A}}\phi_{\lambda,\mu}^{w_{A}}.

Then the set
\{[A]\>|\>A\in\Pi_{n,d}\}
also forms an
\mathcal{A}
-basis of
S^{\jmath}(n,d)
, which is called the
standard basis
.

We define a bar involution
\bar{\cdot}
on
S^{\jmath}(n,d)
as follows: for each
f\in{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{H}}(x_{\nu}\mathcal{H},x_{\lambda}\mathcal{H})\subset S^{\jmath}(n,d)
, we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\bar{f}(x_{\nu^{\prime}}h)=\delta_{\nu^{\prime},\nu}\overline{f(\overline{x_{\nu}})}h\quad\mathrm{for~}h\in\mathcal{H}.

On
\Pi_{n,d}
we define a partial order denoted by
<
as follows:

(3.7)

\displaystyle A^{\prime}<A\mbox{ if and only if }ro(A^{\prime})=ro(A),co(A^{\prime})=co(A),w_{A^{\prime}}^{+}<w_{A}^{+},

where the
<
on
W_{B_{d}}
is the usual Bruhat ordering (cf.
[
Lu03
, §2.1]
). Recall that in
[
BKLW18
, §3.6]
, a
canonical basis
for
S^{\jmath}(n,d)
has been constructed, which is denoted by
\{\{A\}\>|\>A\in\Pi_{n,d}\}
. For each
A\in\Pi_{n,d}
,
\{A\}
is characterized by

\overline{\{A\}}=\{A\}

and

\{A\}=\sum\limits_{A^{\prime}\leq A}P_{A^{\prime},A}[A^{\prime}],

where
P_{A,A}=1
and
P_{A^{\prime},A}\in q^{-1}\mathbb{N}[q^{-1}]
for

.

Recall that in
[
KL79
]
, the left, right and two-sided cells with respect to the Kazhdan–Lusztig basis

of

have been defined. In

. Because of the intersection cohomology construction of

, the structure constants

associated to them also lie in

(see the proof of

). Using these and Lemma

, we can obtain a classification of left, right and two-sided cells for

with respect to the canonical basis
\{\{A\}\>|\>A\in\Pi_{n,d}\}
. (In the following, we shall write
\mathcal{C}_{y}\preceq_{\star}\mathcal{C}_{w}
as

and

as

for

Proposition 3.2
.

For

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of

3.2.
Description of two-sided cells in
\mathcal{H}

In this subsection, we recall the description of two-sided cells in
\mathcal{H}
with respect to the Kazhdan–Lusztig basis following
[
BV82
]
.

According to
[
Lu77
, §3]
and also
[
Lu79
, §5]
, we define a
symbol
in type
B_{d}
to be an array of nonnegative integers

\Lambda={\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\cdots<\lambda_{m+1}\choose\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}<\cdots<\mu_{m}}

such that
\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}\lambda_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mu_{j}=d+m^{2}
, where
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. We introduce an equivalence relation
\sim
on the set of symbols as the transitive closure of the ‘shift relations’

{\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\cdots<\lambda_{m+1}\choose\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}<\cdots<\mu_{m}}=\Lambda\sim\Lambda^{\prime}={0<\lambda_{1}+1<\lambda_{2}+1<\cdots<\lambda_{m+1}+1\choose 0<\mu_{1}+1<\mu_{2}+1<\cdots<\mu_{m}+1}.

We shall denote by
[\Lambda]
the equivalence class of a symbol
\Lambda
and
\Phi_{d}
the set of equivalence classes of symbols relative to
\sim
. Let
\Psi_{d}
be the set of ordered pairs
(\alpha,\beta)
of partitions
\alpha=(\alpha_{m+1},\ldots,\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1})
,
\beta=(\beta_{m},\ldots,\beta_{2},\beta_{1})
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}\alpha_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\beta_{j}=d,\alpha_{1}\geq 0,\beta_{1}\geq 0
. Then
\Psi_{d}
is in one-to-one correspondence with
\Phi_{d}
by associating to
(\alpha,\beta)
the equivalence class
[\Lambda]
of a symbol
\Lambda
defined by
\lambda_{i}=\alpha_{i}+i-1

(1\leq i\leq m+1)
,
\mu_{j}=\beta_{j}+j-1

(1\leq j\leq m)
. Both sets are in one-to-one correspondence with the set of complex irreducible representations of
W_{B_{d}}

(
up to isomorphism
)
(cf.
[
Lu79
, §5]
).

Given a symbol

\displaystyle\Lambda={\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\cdots<\lambda_{m+1}\choose\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}<\cdots<\mu_{m}}

for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we take the set
\{2\lambda_{i}+1,2\mu_{j}\>|\>1\leq i\leq m+1,1\leq j\leq m\}
and order it in a decreasing sequence, say
(\nu_{2m+1},\ldots,\nu_{1})
. Since
\sum_{i=1}^{2m+1}\nu_{i}=2d+2m^{2}+m+1
and
\nu_{2m+1}>\nu_{2m}>\cdots>\nu_{1}
, we see that
(\nu_{2m+1}-(2m+1)+1,\nu_{2m}-2m+1,\ldots,\nu_{1}-1+1)
is a partition of
2d+1
, which we shall denote by
par(\Lambda)
. By definition, it is easy to see that if
\Lambda\sim\Lambda^{\prime}
, then
par(\Lambda)
differs from
par(\Lambda^{\prime})
by possibly some 0’s, which can be regarded as the same partition and we denote it by
par[\Lambda]
. Let
\mathfrak{P}
be the set of partitions
par[\Lambda]
, where
[\Lambda]
runs over
\Phi_{d}
.

We now recall the Robinson–Schensted algorithm following
[
BV82
, p.171]
. Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{l(\lambda)})
be a partition of
2d+1
, where
l(\lambda)
is the number of nonzero components of
\lambda
. The Young diagram of
\lambda
is a collection of boxes, arranged in left justified rows with
\lambda_{1}
boxes in row 1,
\lambda_{2}
boxes in row 2, and so on. A
\lambda
-tableau
T
is obtained by filling in the boxes in the Young diagram of
\lambda
by the integers
1,2,\ldots,2d+1
, each occurring once; we will call
\lambda
the
shape
of
T
. A
\lambda
-tableau
T
is called
standard
if the entries decrease along each row and down each column.

For a standard tableau
T
and a positive integer
k
, we construct a new tableau
T\leftarrow k
by the following recursive algorithm:

(1)

If
k_{1}=k
is less than or equal to any integer in the first row of
T
, then
T\leftarrow k
is the tableau obtained by adding
k_{1}
at the end of the first row of
T
.

(2)

Otherwise, find the largest entry
k_{2}
in the first row satisfying
k_{2}<k_{1}
, and replace
k_{2}
by
k_{1}
.

(3)

Repeat the procedure for the second row of
T
with
k_{2}
, and so on.

The algorithm ends whenever an integer
k_{a}
is added at the end of the
a
-th row.

Given an element of
\mathfrak{S}_{2d+1}
:

w=\left(\hskip-4.55244pt\begin{array}[]{ccccccccccccccccccccccccc}1&2&\cdots&2d+1\\
w_{1}&w_{2}&\cdots&w_{2d+1}\\
\end{array}\hskip-4.55244pt\right),

we define a standard tableau
T(w)
recursively as follows:

P_{0}=\emptyset,\quad P_{t}=P_{t-1}\leftarrow w_{t}\mbox{ for }1\leq t\leq 2d+1,\quad T(w):=P_{2d+1}.

Then the map
w\mapsto(T(w),T(w^{-1}))
gives a bijection between
\mathfrak{S}_{2d+1}
and the set of all pairs of standard tableaux of the same shape, which is called the
Robinson–Schensted correspondence
.

We shall identify
W_{B_{d}}
with the group of permutations
w
on the set
\{-d,-(d-1),\ldots,-1,0,1,

\ldots,d-1,d\}
such that
w(-i)=-w(i)
for any
i
. Under the identification, we have

\displaystyle s_{0}=(-1,1),\quad s_{i}=(-i-1,-i)(i,i+1)\text{ for $1\leq i\leq d-1$.}

In particular,
w(0)=0
for any
w\in W_{B_{d}}
. For each
w\in W_{B_{d}}
, it can be considered as an element of
\mathfrak{S}_{2d+1}
under the identification
-d\leftrightarrow 1,-(d-1)\leftrightarrow 2,\ldots,0\leftrightarrow d+1,\ldots,d-1\leftrightarrow 2d,d\leftrightarrow 2d+1
, and therefore we can associate to it a pair of standard tableaux
(T(w),T(w^{-1}))
by applying the Robinson–Schensted correspondence for
\mathfrak{S}_{2d+1}
as above (cf.
[
BV82
, p.173]
); let us denote by
PT(w)
the shape of
T(w)
, which is a partition of
2d+1
. Then we have the following proposition due to Barbasch and Vogan.

Proposition 3.3
.

(
see
[
BV82
, Proposition 17]
)

For any
w\in W_{B_{d}}
,
PT(w)
belongs to the set
\mathfrak{P}.

We now define an equivalence relation
\approx
on
\Phi_{d}
. Given two elements of
\Phi_{d}
:

[\Lambda]=\bigg[{\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\cdots<\lambda_{m+1}\choose\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}<\cdots<\mu_{m}}\bigg]\quad\mbox{and}\quad[\Lambda^{\prime}]=\bigg[{\lambda_{1}^{\prime}<\lambda_{2}^{\prime}<\cdots<\lambda_{m+1}^{\prime}\choose\mu_{1}^{\prime}<\mu_{2}^{\prime}<\cdots<\mu_{m}^{\prime}}\bigg],

we say that
[\Lambda]\approx[\Lambda^{\prime}]
if and only if

\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{m+1},\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{m}\}\mbox{ is a permutation of }\{\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\lambda_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,\lambda_{m+1}^{\prime},\mu_{1}^{\prime},\mu_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,\mu_{m}^{\prime}\}.

It is easy to see that if
par[\Lambda]=par[\Lambda^{\prime}]
, then
[\Lambda]=[\Lambda^{\prime}]
(see
[
Mc96
, p.80]
). Thus, the equivalence relation
\approx
on
\Phi_{d}
induces an equivalence relation, which we shall denote by the same notation
\approx
, on the set
\mathfrak{P}
; we say that
par[\Lambda]\approx par[\Lambda^{\prime}]
if and only if
[\Lambda]\approx[\Lambda^{\prime}]
. Then we have the following proposition, which gives an explicit description of two-sided cells in
\mathcal{H}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

(
see
[
BV82
, Theorem 18]
)

For any two elements
w,w^{\prime}\in W_{B_{d}}
, we have
w\sim_{LR}w^{\prime}
if and only if
PT(w)\approx PT(w^{\prime})
.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{W_{B}}
be the subset of
\Phi_{d}
consisting of equivalence classes of symbols such that
\lambda_{i}\leq\mu_{i}\leq\lambda_{i+1}
for any
i
. We shall call each element of
\mathcal{G}_{W_{B}}

special
, which exactly corresponds to a so-called special representation of
W_{B_{d}}
(see
[
Lu79
]
and also
[
Lu82
]
). Let
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{W_{B}}
be the set of equivalence classes of
\Phi_{d}
relative to
\approx
. It is obvious that each equivalence class in
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{W_{B}}
contains exactly one element of
\mathcal{G}_{W_{B}}
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{d}
be the set of partitions of
2d+1
such that every
even
part occurs an even number of times. It is well-known that there exists a one-to-one correspondence between
\mathcal{P}_{d}
and the set of nilpotent orbits in type
B_{d}
by the Jordan block decomposition (cf.
[
CoMc93
, Theorem 5.1.2]
). We call an element
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
a
special partition
if its conjugate
\lambda^{t}
also belongs to
\mathcal{P}_{d}
(cf.
[
CoMc93
, §6.3]
). We denote by
\mathcal{SP}_{d}
the set of special partitions of
2d+1
. Then it is easy to check that the map,
\pi:[\Lambda]\mapsto par[\Lambda]
, gives a one-to-one correspondence between
\mathcal{G}_{W_{B}}
and
\mathcal{SP}_{d}
. (We refer to
[
Mc96
, p.80]
for a construction of the inverse of
\pi
, which is denoted by
\pi^{\prime}_{n}
.) Thus, by Proposition
3.4
, we see that there is a one-to-one correspondence between the set of two-sided cells in
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{SP}_{d}
.

3.3.
Description of two-sided cells in
S^{\jmath}(n,d)

In this subsection, we shall give a combinatorial description of two-sided cells in
S^{\jmath}(n,d)
.

We first recall a result of Du, which associates partitions to the matrices in
\Pi_{n,d}
and generalizes Greene’s method
[
Gr79
]
associating partitions to finite partially ordered sets. Let
P

in

which is totally ordered by the induced order of

. A
k

and

Let

be the map sending a matrix

-chain family of

an
F
-sum map and
\mathfrak{s}_{F}(A)
the
F
-sum of
A
. Let
\mathfrak{s}_{k}(A)
be the
maximum
value of
F
-sums of
A
for all
k
-chain families

. We have the following result due to Du.

Theorem 3.5
.

(
see
[

For each

. Then the permutation

may be read off from

by reading from left to right inside the sequences and from bottom to top up the columns, followed by left to right along the successive columns.

and the following relations:

\displaystyle K_{i}K^{\pm 1}_{j}=K^{\pm 1}_{j}K_{i},\quad K_{i}K_{i}^{-1}=1=K_{i}^{-1}K_{i},\quad K_{i}E_{j}K_{i}^{-1}=q^{\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\alpha_{j}\rangle}E_{j}\ ~\quad\hbox{for~all}~i,j\in\mathbb{I},

\displaystyle E_{i}F_{j}-F_{j}E_{i}=\delta_{i,j}\frac{K_{i}-K_{i}^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}},\quad K_{i}F_{j}K_{i}^{-1}=q^{-\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\alpha_{j}\rangle}F_{j}\ ~\quad\hbox{for~all}~i,j\in\mathbb{I},

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{s+t=1-\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\alpha_{j}\rangle}(-1)^{t}E_{i}^{(s)}E_{j}E_{i}^{(t)}=\sum\limits_{s+t=1-\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\alpha_{j}\rangle}(-1)^{t}F_{i}^{(s)}F_{j}F_{i}^{(t)}=0\ ~\quad\hbox{for~all }i\neq j.

We set

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}^{\jmath}=\{\diamond,\diamond+1,\ldots,\diamond+r-1\}.

By
[
BW18
, §6.1]
, the
\imath
quantum group
\mathbf{U}^{\jmath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
is an associative algebra over
\mathbb{Q}(q)
with generators

\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathcal{F}_{i},\mathcal{K}_{i}^{\pm 1}

(i\in\mathbb{I}^{\jmath})
and the following relations:

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{i}\mathcal{K}^{\pm 1}_{j}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{K}^{\pm 1}_{j}\mathcal{K}_{i},\quad\mathcal{K}_{i}\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1}=1=\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1}\mathcal{K}_{i},

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{i}\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1}

\displaystyle=q^{(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{-i},\alpha_{j})}\mathcal{E}_{j},\quad\mathcal{K}_{i}\mathcal{F}_{j}\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1}=q^{-(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{-i},\alpha_{j})}\mathcal{F}_{j},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{i}\mathcal{F}_{j}-\mathcal{F}_{j}\mathcal{E}_{i}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ for all $i\neq j$},\quad\mathcal{E}_{i}\mathcal{F}_{i}-\mathcal{F}_{i}\mathcal{E}_{i}=\frac{\mathcal{K}_{i}-\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}}\quad\text{ for all }i\neq\diamond,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{i}\mathcal{E}_{j}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{E}_{i},\quad\mathcal{F}_{i}\mathcal{F}_{j}=\mathcal{F}_{j}\mathcal{F}_{i}\quad\text{ for }|i-j|>1,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{i}^{2}\mathcal{E}_{j}+\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{E}_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=(q+q^{-1})\mathcal{E}_{i}\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{E}_{i},\quad\mathcal{F}_{i}^{2}\mathcal{F}_{j}+\mathcal{F}_{j}\mathcal{F}_{i}^{2}=(q+q^{-1})\mathcal{F}_{i}\mathcal{F}_{j}\mathcal{F}_{i}\quad\text{ for }|i-j|=1,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\diamond}^{2}\mathcal{E}_{\diamond}+\mathcal{E}_{\diamond}\mathcal{F}_{\diamond}^{2}

\displaystyle=(q+q^{-1})(\mathcal{F}_{\diamond}\mathcal{E}_{\diamond}\mathcal{F}_{\diamond}-q\mathcal{F}_{\diamond}\mathcal{K}_{\diamond}^{-1}-q^{-1}\mathcal{F}_{\diamond}\mathcal{K}_{\diamond}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\diamond}^{2}\mathcal{F}_{\diamond}+\mathcal{F}_{\diamond}\mathcal{E}_{\diamond}^{2}

\displaystyle=(q+q^{-1})(\mathcal{E}_{\diamond}\mathcal{F}_{\diamond}\mathcal{E}_{\diamond}-q^{-1}\mathcal{K}_{\diamond}\mathcal{E}_{\diamond}-q\mathcal{K}_{\diamond}^{-1}\mathcal{E}_{\diamond}).

By

, Proposition 6.2]
, There is an injective

-algebra homomorphism

, which is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{i}\mapsto E_{i}+K_{i}^{-1}F_{-i},\quad\mathcal{F}_{i}\mapsto F_{i}K_{-i}^{-1}+E_{-i},\quad\mathcal{K}_{i}\mapsto K_{i}K_{-i}^{-1}

is a (right)

). Then

is an example of quantum symmetric pairs (cf.
[

]
,
[

Let
\theta
be the involution of the lattice

defined by

\displaystyle(\mathcal{E}_{i}\mathcal{F}_{i}-\mathcal{F}_{i}\mathcal{E}_{i})1_{\lambda}

4.2.
Description of two-sided cells in
{}_{\mathcal{A}}\!\dot{\mathbf{U}}^{\jmath}

In
[
LiW18
, Theorem 5.5]

\mathcal{A}
-basis

of
{}_{\mathcal{A}}\!\dot{\mathbf{U}}^{\jmath}
, which is called the
\jmath
-canonical basis
, has been constructed via studying the asymptotical behavior of the canonical bases of
\jmath
-Schur algebras

with varying

under the transfer map. Furthermore, in
[

, Theorem 5.6]
it was proved that the structure constants with respect to the
\jmath
-canonical basis
\dot{\mathbf{B}}^{\jmath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
lie in
\mathbb{N}[q,q^{-1}]
.

In
[
LiW18
, (4.12)]
, a surjective algebra homomorphism
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}:{}_{\mathcal{A}}\!\dot{\mathbf{U}}^{\jmath}\rightarrow S^{\jmath}(n,d)
has been constructed for each
d
. The following theorem was proved in
[
BSWW18
, Corollary 6.6]
, improving
[
LiW18
, Proposition 5.11]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

(
see
[
BSWW18
, Corollary 6.6]
)

For each
b\in\dot{\mathbf{B}}^{\jmath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
, the image
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(b)\in\{0\}\cup\mathbf{B}_{d}^{\jmath}
, where
\mathbf{B}_{d}^{\jmath}
denotes the set of canonical basis elements for
S^{\jmath}(n,d)
. Moreover, the kernel of
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}
is spanned by the elements
b\in\dot{\mathbf{B}}^{\jmath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
such that
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(b)=0
.

In
[
LiW18
, (4.8)]
, the transfer map
\phi_{d+n,d}^{\jmath}:S^{\jmath}(n,d+n)\rightarrow S^{\jmath}(n,d)
has been defined. Moreover, in
[
LiW18
, Proposition 4.4]
it was proved that the maps
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}
and
\phi_{d+n,d}^{\jmath}
are compatible, that is,
\phi_{d+n,d}^{\jmath}\circ\phi_{d+n}^{\jmath}=\phi_{d}^{\jmath}
.

Set
\hat{S}^{\jmath}:=\lim\limits_{\longleftarrow}S^{\jmath}(n,d)
, where the limit is taken over the projective system given by the transfer maps
(\phi_{d+n,d}^{\jmath})_{d\in\mathbb{N}}
mentioned above. Since the maps
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}
are compatible with this system, there is a unique map
\phi:{}_{\mathcal{A}}\!\dot{\mathbf{U}}^{j}\rightarrow\hat{S}^{\jmath}
, which factors each of the maps
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}
through the canonical map
\hat{S}^{\jmath}\rightarrow S^{\jmath}(n,d)
.

Recall that a bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{d}
on

was defined in
[
BKLW18
, §3.7]
(and denoted by
(\cdot,\cdot)_{D}
therein with

). Using this, in
[
LiW18
, (5.1)]
, a bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\jmath}
on

has been constructed as follows:

(4.2)

\displaystyle\langle x,y\rangle_{\jmath}:=\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\big\langle\phi_{k+pn}^{\jmath}(x),\phi_{k+pn}^{\jmath}(y)\big\rangle_{k+pn}\quad\text{ for }x,y\in{}_{\mathcal{A}}\!\dot{\mathbf{U}}^{\jmath}.

Moreover, it was shown in
[
LiW18
, Proposition 5.7]
that the bilinear form

is non-degenerate. Using this and an argument similar to
[

, Proposition 7.1]
, we can prove the following result. (We also refer to
[
FLLLW20
, Proposition 6.5.1]
for a similar conclusion in the case of affine

type.)

Proposition 4.2
.

The homomorphism
\phi
is injective.

Proof.

Suppose that
u
is in the kernel of
\phi
. Then for each
d
we have
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(u)=0
, and hence by (
4.2
)
u
is in the radical of the bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\jmath}
. Since it is non-degenerate, we must have

.
∎

The following theorem gives a characterization of two-sided cells in
{}_{\mathcal{A}}\!\dot{\mathbf{U}}^{\jmath}
in terms of the ones in
\jmath
-Schur algebras
S^{\jmath}(n,d)
.

Theorem 4.3
.

For any two elements
b,b^{\prime}\in\dot{\mathbf{B}}^{\jmath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
, we have
b\sim_{LR}b^{\prime}
if and only if there exists some
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(b)\neq 0
,
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(b^{\prime})\neq 0
, and moreover,
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(b)\sim_{LR}\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(b^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Assume that
b\sim_{LR}b^{\prime}
. By Proposition
4.2
, we see that there exists some
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(b^{\prime})\neq 0
. By Lemma
2.1
, there exist
h,h^{\prime}\in\dot{\mathbf{B}}^{\jmath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
such that
b^{\prime}
appears with nonzero coefficient in the expansion of
hbh^{\prime}
as a linear combination of
\jmath
-canonical basis elements. By Theorem
4.1
, we see that the canonical basis element
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(b^{\prime})
appears with nonzero coefficient when expanding
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(h)\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(b)\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(h^{\prime})
as a linear combination of elements in
\mathbf{B}_{d}^{\jmath}
. Hence,
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(b)\neq 0
and
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(b^{\prime})\preceq_{LR}\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(b)
. Similarly, we can show that
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(b)\preceq_{LR}\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(b^{\prime})
. Thus, we have
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(b)\sim_{LR}\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(b^{\prime})
.

Conversely, assume that there exists some
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(b)\neq 0
,
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(b^{\prime})\neq 0
and
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(b)\sim_{LR}\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(b^{\prime})
. By Lemma
2.1
and the surjectivity of
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}
, we see that there exist
h,h^{\prime}\in{}_{\mathcal{A}}\!\dot{\mathbf{U}}^{\jmath}
such that
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(b^{\prime})
appears with nonzero coefficient in the expansion of
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(h)\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(b)\phi_{d}^{\jmath}(h^{\prime})
as a linear combination of elements in
\mathbf{B}_{d}^{\jmath}
. By Theorem
4.1
, we see that
b^{\prime}
appears with nonzero coefficient in the expansion of
hbh^{\prime}
as a linear combination of
\jmath
-canonical basis elements. Hence,
b^{\prime}\preceq_{LR}b
. Similarly, we can show that
b\preceq_{LR}b^{\prime}
. Thus, we have
b\sim_{LR}b^{\prime}
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

In
[
FLLLW20
,
FLLLW22
]
, the authors have constructed several types of affine
q
-Schur algebras as well as their canonical bases, and shown that there exist maps from the modified
\imath
quantum groups of affine
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}
type to them. If we can prove that some of these maps satisfy the properties similar to the ones that
\phi_{d}^{\jmath}
satisfies in Theorem
4.1
, and that an argument similar to Proposition
4.2
holds (cf.
[
FLLLW20
, Proposition 6.5.1]
), then we can give an assertion similar to Theorem
4.3
.

In
[
CLW20
, §5.3]
, we have taken the first step towards solving this problem, that is, we have provided a classification of two-sided cells in the affine
q
-Schur algebras of arbitrary type in terms of the ones in the corresponding affine Hecke algebras.

5.
Description of two-sided cells in
S^{\imath}(n,d)

In this section, we focus on the
\imath
-Schur algebra
S^{\imath}(n,d)
associated to
\mathcal{H}
and formulate a combinatorial description of two-sided cells in it. Moreover, we state a conjecture on the number of left cells in a two-sided cell of it.

5.1.
Preliminaries

Fix
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
. Let
W_{C_{d}}
be the Weyl group of type
C_{d}
with generators
S=\{s_{0},s_{1},\ldots,s_{d-1}\}
and with the same relations as in
W_{B_{d}}
. There is a canonical isomorphism between

and
W_{B_{d}}
, and we shall denote the Iwahori–Hecke algebra associated to

also by
\mathcal{H}
. Then
\mathcal{H}
is an algebra over
\mathcal{A}
with a basis
\{T_{w}\>|\>w\in W_{C_{d}}\}
satisfying the same relations as (
6.1
).

Fix
n=2r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
. Following
[
LL21
, §6.1]
we set

\Lambda^{\imath}(n,d)=\{\lambda=(\lambda_{i})_{i\in[-r,r]\setminus\{0\}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\>|\>\lambda_{-i}=\lambda_{i}\mathrm{~for~}1\leq i\leq r,~\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}=2d\}.

For each
\lambda\in\Lambda^{\imath}(n,d)
, we denote by
W_{\lambda}
the parabolic subgroup of
W_{C_{d}}
generated by the following simple reflections

S\setminus\{s_{0},s_{\lambda_{1}},\ldots,s_{\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{r-1}}\},

and set
x_{\lambda}:=\sum_{w\in W_{\lambda}}q^{\ell^{\prime}(w)}T_{w}
, where
\ell^{\prime}
is the length function on
W_{C_{d}}
(cf.
[
Lu03
, §1.1]
).

Following
[
BKLW18
, §5.1]
and also
[
LL21
, (6.1.2)]
we define the
\imath
-Schur algebra
of type
C_{d}
over
\mathcal{A}
by

S^{\imath}(n,d)={\mathrm{End}}_{\mathcal{H}}(\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\imath}(n,d)}x_{\lambda}\mathcal{H}).

For
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda^{\imath}(n,d)
, we denote by
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda\mu}
(resp.
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda\mu}^{+}
) the set of minimal (resp. maximal) length double coset representatives in
W_{\lambda}\backslash W_{C_{d}}/W_{\mu}
. Set

\Xi^{\imath}:=\{(\lambda,g,\mu)~|~\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda^{\imath}(n,d),g\in\mathcal{D}_{\lambda\mu}\}

and

\widetilde{\Xi}^{\imath}:=\{(\lambda,g,\mu)~|~\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda^{\imath}(n,d),g\in\mathcal{D}_{\lambda\mu}^{+}\}.

Then there exists a bijection between
\Xi^{\imath}
and
\widetilde{\Xi}^{\imath}
(cf.
[
Lu03
, Proposition 9.15]
) and we shall identify them.

Let
D=2d
. Following
[
LL21
, (6.1.1)]
we set

\Theta_{n,d}^{\imath}:=\{(a_{ij})_{i,j\in[-r,r]\setminus\{0\}}\in\mathrm{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{N})\>|\>\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}=D\}

and

\Pi_{n,d}^{\imath}:=\{(a_{ij})\in\Theta_{n,d}^{\imath}\>|\>a_{ij}=a_{-i,-j}\mathrm{~for~all~}i,j\}.

By
[
LL21
, Lemma 6.1.1]
, there is a natural bijection between
\Xi^{\imath}
or
\widetilde{\Xi}^{\imath}
and
\Pi_{n,d}^{\imath}
, and we shall identify them. If
A\in\Pi_{n,d}^{\imath}
, we denote by
(ro(A),w_{A},co(A))
and
(ro(A),w_{A}^{+},co(A))
the corresponding element in
\Xi^{\imath}
and
\widetilde{\Xi}^{\imath}
, respectively.

For
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda^{\imath}(n,d)
and

Then the set

forms an
\mathcal{A}
-basis of
S^{\imath}(n,d)
(see
[
LL21
, §6.1]
).

Using the above basis, similar to (
3.5
), we can define a standard basis of
S^{\imath}(n,d)
, denoted by
\{[A]\>|\>A\in\Pi_{n,d}^{\imath}\}
(see loc. cit.). Moreover, we can define a bar involution
\bar{\cdot}
on
S^{\imath}(n,d)
and a partial order

on
\Pi_{n,d}^{\imath}
, respectively (cf. (

)-(

, a

for

has been constructed, which is denoted by

.

The following proposition provides a classification of left, right and two-sided cells for

with respect to the canonical basis
\{\{A\}\>|\>A\in\Pi_{n,d}^{\imath}\}
, which can be proved in a manner similar to Proposition

.

Proposition 5.1
.

For
A,B\in\Pi_{n,d}^{i}
, we have the following results.

(1)

\{A\}\preceq_{L}\{B\}
if and only if
co(A)=co(B)
and
w_{A}^{+}\preceq_{L}w_{B}^{+}
. Similarly,
\{A\}\preceq_{R}\{B\}
if and only if
ro(A)=ro(B)
and
w_{A}^{+}\preceq_{R}w_{B}^{+}
.

(2)

\{A\}\sim_{L}\{B\}
if and only if
co(A)=co(B)
and
w_{A}^{+}\sim_{L}w_{B}^{+}
. Similarly,
\{A\}\sim_{R}\{B\}
if and only if
ro(A)=ro(B)
and
w_{A}^{+}\sim_{R}w_{B}^{+}
.

(3)

\{A\}\sim_{LR}\{B\}
if and only if
w_{A}^{+}\sim_{LR}w_{B}^{+}
.

5.2.
Another description of two-sided cells in
\mathcal{H}

In this subsection, we shall recall another description of two-sided cells in
\mathcal{H}
with respect to the Kazhdan–Lusztig basis following
[

]
.

We define the symbols in type
C_{d}
and the set
\Phi_{d}
as in §

. Given a symbol

\displaystyle\Lambda={\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\cdots<\lambda_{m+1}\choose\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}<\cdots<\mu_{m}}

for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we take the set
\{2\lambda_{i},2\mu_{j}+1\>|\>1\leq i\leq m+1,1\leq j\leq m\}
and order it in a decreasing sequence, say
(\nu_{2m+1},\ldots,\nu_{1})
. Since
\sum_{i=1}^{2m+1}\nu_{i}=2d+2m^{2}+m
and
\nu_{2m+1}>\nu_{2m}>\cdots>\nu_{1}
, we see that
(\nu_{2m+1}-(2m+1)+1,\nu_{2m}-2m+1,\ldots,\nu_{1}-1+1)
is a partition of
2d
, which we shall denote by
par_{\imath}(\Lambda)
. By definition, we see that if
\Lambda\sim\Lambda^{\prime}
, then
par_{\imath}(\Lambda)
differs from
par_{\imath}(\Lambda^{\prime})
by possibly some 0’s, which we regard as the same partition and denote it by
par_{\imath}[\Lambda]
. Let
\mathfrak{P}_{\imath}
be the set of partitions
par_{\imath}[\Lambda]
, where
[\Lambda]
runs over
\Phi_{d}
.

Similar to
\mathfrak{S}_{2d+1}
, we can define the Robinson–Schensted algorithm for
\mathfrak{S}_{2d}
. We shall identify
W_{C_{d}}
with the group of permutations
w
on the set
\{-d,-(d-1),\ldots,-1,1,

\ldots,d-1,d\}
such that
w(-i)=-w(i)
for any
i
. Under the identification, we have

\displaystyle s_{0}=(-1,1),\quad s_{i}=(-i-1,-i)(i,i+1)\text{ for $1\leq i\leq d-1$.}

For
w\in W_{C_{d}}
, we regard it as an element of
\mathfrak{S}_{2d}
under the identification
-d\leftrightarrow 1,\ldots,-1\leftrightarrow d,1\leftrightarrow d+1,\ldots,d\leftrightarrow 2d
; by applying the Robinson–Schensted correspondence for
\mathfrak{S}_{2d}
, we obtain a pair of standard tableaux
(T_{\imath}(w),T_{\imath}(w^{-1}))
attached to it. We denote by
PT_{\imath}(w)
the shape of
T_{\imath}(w)
, which is a partition of
2d
. Then we have the following proposition due to Barbasch and Vogan.

Proposition 5.2
.

(
see
[
BV82
, Proposition 17]
)

For any
w\in W_{C_{d}}
,
PT_{\imath}(w)
belongs to the set
\mathfrak{P}_{\imath}
.

Recall that in §
3.2
we have defined an equivalence relation
\approx
on
\Phi_{d}
. By an argument on
[
Mc96
, p.80]
, we see if
par_{\imath}[\Lambda]=par_{\imath}[\Lambda^{\prime}]
, then
[\Lambda]=[\Lambda^{\prime}]
. Therefore, the equivalence relation
\approx
on
\Phi_{d}
induces an equivalence relation on
\mathfrak{P}_{\imath}
, which we denote also by
\approx
; we say that
par_{\imath}[\Lambda]\approx par_{\imath}[\Lambda^{\prime}]
if and only if
[\Lambda]\approx[\Lambda^{\prime}]
. Then we have another explicit description of two-sided cells in
\mathcal{H}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

(
see
[
BV82
, Theorem 18]
)

For any two elements
w,w^{\prime}\in W_{C_{d}}
, we have
w\sim_{LR}w^{\prime}
if and only if
PT_{\imath}(w)\approx PT_{\imath}(w^{\prime})
.

We denote by
\mathcal{P}_{d}^{\imath}
the set of partitions of
2d
in which every
odd
part appears with even multiplicity. Then there exists a bijection between
\mathcal{P}_{d}^{\imath}
and the set of nilpotent orbits in type
C_{d}
(cf.
[
CoMc93
, Theorem 5.1.3]
). For
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{d}^{\imath}
, we call it
special
if we also have
\lambda^{t}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}^{\imath}
(cf.
[
CoMc93
, §6.3]
). Denote by
\mathcal{SP}_{d}^{\imath}
the set of special partitions of
2d
. Then the map,
\pi_{\imath}:[\Lambda]\mapsto par_{\imath}[\Lambda]
, is a bijection between
\mathcal{G}_{W_{B}}
and
\mathcal{SP}_{d}^{\imath}
(cf.
[
Mc96
, p.80]
). Thus, by Proposition
5.3
, we see that there is a bijection between the set of two-sided cells in
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{SP}_{d}^{\imath}
.

5.3.
Description of two-sided cells in
S^{\imath}(n,d)

In this subsection, we shall give a combinatorial description of two-sided cells in
S^{\imath}(n,d)
.

Set
([-r,r]\setminus\{0\})^{2}=([-r,r]\setminus\{0\})\times([-r,r]\setminus\{0\})\subset\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
. It is easy to see that
([-r,r]\setminus\{0\})^{2}
is a finite partially ordered set with the induced order by setting
(i,j)\leq(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
if
i\geq i^{\prime}
and
j\leq j^{\prime}
. If
A=(a_{ij})_{i,j\in[-r,r]\setminus\{0\}}\in\Pi_{n,d}^{\imath}
and
F
is a
k
-chain family of
([-r,r]\setminus\{0\})^{2}
, we define
\mathfrak{s}_{F}(A)
to be the sum of the entries
a_{ij}
with
(i,j)\in F
, and call it the
F
-sum of
A
. Let
\mathfrak{s}_{k}(A)
be the maximum value of
F
-sums of
A
for all
k
-chain families
F
. We have the following result similar to Theorem
3.5
.

Theorem 5.4
.

(
see
[
Du96
, Theorem 1.2]
)

For each
A\in\Pi_{n,d}^{\imath}
, we define
\sigma_{\imath}(A)=\mathfrak{s}_{i}(A)-\mathfrak{s}_{i-1}(A)
(with the convention that
\mathfrak{s}_{0}(A)=0
) for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Then
\sigma_{\imath}(A)=(\sigma_{1}(A),\sigma_{2}(A),\ldots,\sigma_{n}(A))
is a partition of
2d
.

Recall that in §
3.3
we have associated a permutation
y_{A}
to each
A\in\Pi_{n,d}
. In a similar way, for each
A\in\Pi_{n,d}^{\imath}
, we can associate a permutation
\tilde{y}_{A}
to it by replacing
\{-d,-(d-1),\ldots,-1,0,1,\ldots,d-1,d\}
with
\{-d,-(d-1),\ldots,-1,1,\ldots,d-1,d\}
.

By
[
LL21
, Lemma 6.1.1]
, we have an analog of Lemma
3.1
for
W_{C_{d}}
and
\Pi_{n,d}^{\imath}
. By arguments similar to the proofs of Lemmas
3.6
,
3.8
and Theorem
3.10
, we can prove the following three results (using Propositions
5.1
and
5.3
).

Lemma 5.5
.

Set
I^{\imath}=[1,r]\times([-r,r]\setminus\{0\})\subset\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
. For each
A=(ro(A),w_{A}^{+},co(A))\in\Pi_{n,d}^{\imath}
, we have
\tilde{y}_{A}=w_{A}^{+}
, and moreover,

(5.1)

\displaystyle\ell^{\prime}(w_{A}^{+})=d^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in I^{\imath}}\Bigg(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<i\\
y<j\end{subarray}}a_{ij}a_{xy}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x>i\\
y>j\end{subarray}}a_{ij}a_{xy}\Bigg)-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i>0\\
j>0\end{subarray}}a_{ij}.

Lemma 5.6
.

For each
A=(ro(A),w_{A}^{+},co(A))\in\Pi_{n,d}^{\imath}
, we have
\sigma_{\imath}(A)=PT_{\imath}(w_{A}^{+})
.

Theorem 5.7
.

For any two elements
A,A^{\prime}\in\Pi_{n,d}^{\imath}
, we have
\{A\}\sim_{LR}\{A^{\prime}\}
if and only if
\sigma_{\imath}(A)\approx\sigma_{\imath}(A^{\prime})
.

Remark 5.8
.

Similar to the arguments in Remark
3.11
, using Theorem
5.7
we can further show that there is a one-to-one correspondence between the set of two-sided cells in
S^{\imath}(n,d)
and special partitions of
2d
with at most
n
parts.

Example 5.9
.

and

[

\displaystyle A_{1}=\left(\hskip-2.84526pt\begin{array}[]{cc}2&0\\

We have

and special partitions of

We have

, we have
w_{A_{1}}=e
,

,

. Moreover, each

, we have

,

,

6.
An approach to determining two-sided cells in
\tilde{S}^{\imath}(n,d)

In this section, inspired by
[
B17
]
we consider the
\tilde{\imath}
-Schur algebra
\tilde{S}^{\imath}(n,d)
attached to
\mathcal{H}_{C_{d}}^{1}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{C_{d}}^{1}
is the specialization at
p=1
of the Iwahori–Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{C_{d}}^{p}
of type
C_{d}
with unequal parameters
p
and
q
. We shall give an approach to determining whether or not two canonical basis elements of
\tilde{S}^{\imath}(n,d)
lie in the same two-sided cell.

6.1.
Preliminaries

Fix
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 2}
. Recall that
\mathcal{A}={\mathbb{Z}}[q,q^{-1}]
. Let
p
be another indeterminate and set
\mathcal{B}={\mathbb{Z}}[p,p^{-1},q,q^{-1}]
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{C_{d}}^{p}
be the Iwahori–Hecke algebra of type
C_{d}
over
\mathcal{B}
(cf.
[
B17
, §3.1]
). It is generated by
T_{0},T_{1},\ldots,T_{d-1}
with the following relations:

\displaystyle T_{i}T_{i+1}T_{i}=T_{i+1}T_{i}T_{i+1}\quad\mathrm{for}~1\leq i\leq d-2,

\displaystyle T_{0}T_{1}T_{0}T_{1}=T_{1}T_{0}T_{1}T_{0},\qquad T_{i}T_{j}=T_{j}T_{i}\quad\mathrm{if}~|i-j|\geq 2,

\displaystyle(T_{0}-p)(T_{0}+p^{-1})=0,\qquad(T_{i}-q)(T_{i}+q^{-1})=0\quad\mathrm{for}~1\leq i\leq d-1.

Let
w\in W_{C_{d}}
. If
s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\cdots s_{i_{r}}
is a
reduced expression
of it, we set
T_{w}:=T_{i_{1}}T_{i_{2}}\cdots T_{i_{r}}
. It is well-known that
T_{w}
is independent of the choice of the reduced expression of
w
(cf.
[
Lu03
, §3.2]
). Let
\mathcal{H}_{C_{d}}^{1}
denote the Iwahori–Hecke algebra of type
C_{d}
over
\mathcal{A}
with the parameter
p=1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{D_{d}}
be the Iwahori–Hecke algebra of type
D_{d}
over
\mathcal{A}
(cf.
[
ES18
, §10.2]
and also
[
B17
, §3.2]
). It is generated by
T_{0},T_{1},\ldots,T_{d-1}
with the following relations:

\displaystyle T_{i}T_{i+1}T_{i}=T_{i+1}T_{i}T_{i+1}\quad\mathrm{for}~1\leq i\leq d-2,\qquad T_{0}T_{2}T_{0}=T_{2}T_{0}T_{2},

\displaystyle T_{i}T_{j}=T_{j}T_{i}\quad\mathrm{if}~1\leq i,j\leq d-1~\mathrm{with}~|i-j|\geq 2,\qquad T_{0}T_{k}=T_{k}T_{0}\quad\mathrm{for}~k\neq 2,

\displaystyle(T_{i}-q)(T_{i}+q^{-1})=0\quad\mathrm{for}~0\leq i\leq d-1.

Fix
n=2r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
. Recall that in §
5.1
, we have defined the set
\Lambda^{\imath}(n,d)
, the length function
\ell^{\prime}
on
W_{C_{d}}
, and the parabolic subgroup
W_{\lambda}
of
W_{C_{d}}
associated to
\lambda\in\Lambda^{\imath}(n,d)
. Note that each
W_{\lambda}
is generated by some simple reflections
s_{i}
’s (
1\leq i\leq d-1
).

We define a function
\ell^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{a}}
on
W_{C_{d}}
by letting
\ell^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{a}}(w)
be the total number of
s_{i}
’s (
1\leq i\leq d-1
) in a reduced expression of
w
; in particular, we have
\ell^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{a}}(s_{0})=0
and
\ell^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{a}}(s_{i})=1
for
1\leq i\leq d-1
. Then
\ell^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{a}}
is a
weight function
(cf.
[
Lu03
, §3.1]
and
[
GJ11
, Example 2.4.4(a)]
). Thus,
\mathcal{H}_{C_{d}}^{1}
can be regarded as the Iwahori–Hecke algebra over
\mathcal{A}
associated to the weight function
\ell^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{a}}
, and we can apply the results in
[
Lu03
]
and
[
GJ11
, §2.4]
.

Following
[
B17
, §3.1]
we define the
\tilde{\imath}
-Schur algebra

\tilde{S}^{\imath}(n,d)
associated to
\mathcal{H}_{C_{d}}^{1}
over
\mathcal{A}
by

\displaystyle\tilde{S}^{\imath}(n,d)={\mathrm{End}}_{\mathcal{H}_{C_{d}}^{1}}(\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\imath}(n,d)}x_{\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{C_{d}}^{1}),\quad\text{ where }x_{\lambda}=\sum\limits_{w\in W_{\lambda}}q^{\ell^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{a}}(w)}T_{w}.

Remark 6.1
.

In
[
BWW18
, §2.2]
and also
[
LL21
, §6.1]
, the authors have given a more general construction of
\tilde{S}^{\imath}(n,d)
, which is associated to
\mathcal{H}_{C_{d}}^{p}
.

For
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda^{\imath}(n,d)
and
g\in\mathcal{D}_{\lambda\mu}
, we define
\tilde{\phi}_{\lambda,\mu}^{g}\in\tilde{S}^{\imath}(n,d)
by

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}_{\lambda,\mu}^{g}(x_{\nu}h)=\delta_{\mu,\nu}T_{\lambda\mu}^{g}h\quad\mathrm{for~}h\in\mathcal{H}_{C_{d}}^{1},\text{ where }T_{\lambda\mu}^{g}=\sum_{w\in W_{\lambda}gW_{\mu}}q^{\ell^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{a}}(w)}T_{w}.

Then the set
\{\tilde{\phi}_{\lambda,\mu}^{g}\>|\>\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda^{\imath}(n,d)\mathrm{~and~}g\in\mathcal{D}_{\lambda\mu}\}
forms an
\mathcal{A}
-basis of
\tilde{S}^{\imath}(n,d)
(see
[
LL21
, §6.1]
).

By
[
Lu03
, Theorem 5.2]
, the Kazhdan–Lusztig basis
\{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{w}\>|\>w\in W_{C_{d}}\}
of
\mathcal{H}_{C_{d}}^{1}
can be defined. For
\lambda\in\Lambda^{\imath}(n,d)
, let
w^{\lambda}_{\circ}
be the longest element in
W_{\lambda}
. By
[
Xi94
, Proposition 1.17(ii)]
, we have
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{w^{\lambda}_{\circ}}=q^{-\ell^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{a}}(w^{\lambda}_{\circ})}x_{\lambda}
. Let
<
denote the Bruhat ordering on
W_{C_{d}}
. Since
\ell^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{a}}(s_{i})=1
for
1\leq i\leq d-1
, by
[
Lu03
, Theorem 6.6(b) and Corollary 6.7(b)]
we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 6.2
.

Given
1\leq i\leq d-1
and
w\in W_{C_{d}}
, we have
T_{i}\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{w}=q\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{w}
whenever
s_{i}w<w
;
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{w}T_{i}=q\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{w}
whenever
ws_{i}<w
.

For
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda^{\imath}(n,d)
, let
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda\mu}
be the
\mathcal{A}
-submodule of
\mathcal{H}_{C_{d}}^{1}
with a basis
\{T_{\lambda\mu}^{g}\}_{g\in\mathcal{D}_{\lambda\mu}}
. By Lemma
6.2
, we have
T_{w}x_{\lambda}=q^{\ell^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{a}}(w)}x_{\lambda}
for any
w\in W_{\lambda}
(cf. also
[
LL21
, Lemma 3.1.1]
). By this and
[
LL21
, (4.1.4)]
(see
[
Cur85
, (1.9)]
or
[
DDPW08
, Lemma 7.33, Proposition 7.34]
for the equal parameter case), we have the following results.

Lemma 6.3
.

6.4
) with
\lambda_{i}=\mu_{i}
for all
i

sym(s_{0}w)
is defined in a manner similar to
sym(w)

In fact, if the conjecture is proved, similar to Theorem

We illustrate the above conjecture with some examples.

Example 6.11
.

Assume that
d=2
.

(1) We have
PT_{\imath}(s_{0})=(2,1,1)=par_{\imath}[{1<2\choose 0}]

PT_{\imath}(e)=(1,1,1,1)=par_{\imath}[{0<1<2\choose 1<2}]

sym(s_{0})=[{1<2\choose 0<1}]=[{0<1\choose 1<2}]=[{0<1<2\choose 0<2<3}]=sym(e)

(2) We have
PT_{\imath}(s_{0}s_{1})=(2,2)=par_{\imath}[{0<2\choose 1}]
and
PT_{\imath}(s_{1})=(2,2)=par_{\imath}[{0<2\choose 1}]
. Then we have
sym(s_{0}s_{1})=[{0<2\choose 0<2}]=sym(s_{1})
.

(3) We have
PT_{\imath}(s_{0}s_{1}s_{0})=(2,2)=par_{\imath}[{0<2\choose 1}]
and
PT_{\imath}(s_{1}s_{0})=(2,2)=par_{\imath}[{0<2\choose 1}]
. Then we have
sym(s_{0}s_{1}s_{0})=[{0<2\choose 0<2}]=sym(s_{1}s_{0})
.

(4) We have
PT_{\imath}(s_{0}s_{1}s_{0}s_{1})=(4)=par_{\imath}[{0<3\choose 0}]
and
PT_{\imath}(s_{1}s_{0}s_{1})=(3,1)=par_{\imath}[{0<1\choose 2}]
. Then we have
sym(s_{0}s_{1}s_{0}s_{1})=[{0<3\choose 0<1}]=[{0<1\choose 0<3}]=sym(s_{1}s_{0}s_{1})

Remark 6.12
.

Since the
\tilde{\imath}
-Schur algebra
\tilde{S}^{\imath}(n,d)
is a specialization of a more general construction in
[
LL21
, §6.1]
(cf. Remark
6.1
), we can apply their results to our situation.

In
[
LL21
, §6.3]
, a stabilization algebra
\dot{\mathbb{K}}_{n}^{\imath}
has been constructed from the family of
\tilde{\imath}
-Schur algebras
\tilde{S}^{\imath}(n,d)
as
d
varies. From its construction, it should be possible to define a surjective algebra homomorphism from
\dot{\mathbb{K}}_{n}^{\imath}
to
\tilde{S}^{\imath}(n,d)
for each
d
(cf.
[
BKLW18
, Proposition A.17]
). Assume that
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
and
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
are the
\imath
quantum groups associated to
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n}
, which are the coideal subalgebras of
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
and
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
, respectively; let
\dot{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
and
\dot{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
are the modified
\imath
quantum groups attached to
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
and
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
; we refer the reader to
[
LiW18
, §6.4]
and also
[
LL21
, §7.3]
for the definition of these algebras.

In
[
LL21
, Theorem 7.3.2]
, an algebra isomorphism from
\dot{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
to
\dot{\mathbb{K}}_{n}^{\imath}
has been established, whose composition with the possible homomorphism from
\dot{\mathbb{K}}_{n}^{\imath}
to
\tilde{S}^{\imath}(n,d)
will yield a surjective algebra homomorphism from
\dot{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
to
\tilde{S}^{\imath}(n,d)
. Then, similar to the construction of
\phi_{d}^{\imath}
in
[
LiW18
, (6.11)]
, we can construct an algebra homomorphism
\tilde{\phi}_{d}^{\imath}:\dot{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n})\rightarrow\tilde{S}^{\imath}(n,d)
.

We conjecture that
\tilde{\phi}_{d}^{\imath}
sends each canonical basis element of
\dot{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
to a canonical basis element of
\tilde{S}^{\imath}(n,d)
or zero, and moreover, its kernel is spanned by the canonical basis elements whose images are zero under
\tilde{\phi}_{d}^{\imath}
. If the conjecture holds, we can lift the combinatorial description of two-sided cells in
\tilde{S}^{\imath}(n,d)
to give a characterization of two-sided cells in
\dot{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{\imath}(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n})
, which is similar to the claim of Theorem
4.3
in Section 4.

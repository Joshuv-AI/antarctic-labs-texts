II.1
Diquark-antidiquark currents and their mixing

There are two fully-strange diquark-antidiquark interpolating currents with the quantum number
J^{PC}=1^{--}
:

\displaystyle\eta_{1\mu}=

(18)

\displaystyle~~~(s_{a}^{T}\mathbb{C}\gamma_{5}s_{b})(\bar{s}_{a}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}\mathbb{C}\bar{s}_{b}^{T})-(s_{a}^{T}\mathbb{C}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}s_{b})(\bar{s}_{a}\gamma_{5}\mathbb{C}\bar{s}_{b}^{T})\,,

\displaystyle\eta_{2\mu}=

(19)

\displaystyle~~~(s_{a}^{T}\mathbb{C}\gamma^{\nu}s_{b})(\bar{s}_{a}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\mathbb{C}\bar{s}_{b}^{T})-(s_{a}^{T}\mathbb{C}\sigma_{\mu\nu}s_{b})(\bar{s}_{a}\gamma^{\nu}\mathbb{C}\bar{s}_{b}^{T})\,.

These two currents are independent of each other.

In Ref.
Chen:2008ej
we separately use
\eta_{1\mu}
and
\eta_{2\mu}
to perform QCD sum rule analyses, where we calculate only the diagonal correlation functions:

\langle 0|\eta_{1\mu}{\eta_{1\nu}^{\dagger}}|0\rangle~~~{\rm and}~~~\langle 0|\eta_{2\mu}{\eta_{2\nu}^{\dagger}}|0\rangle\,.

(20)

However, in Ref.
Chen:2018kuu
we find that the off-diagonal correlation function

\langle 0|\eta_{1\mu}{\eta_{2\nu}^{\dagger}}|0\rangle\neq 0\,,

(21)

is also non-zero, indicating that
\eta_{1\mu}
and
\eta_{2\mu}
are correlated with each other, so they can couple to the same physical state. To deal with this, in Ref.
Chen:2018kuu
we further construct two mixing currents:

\displaystyle J_{1\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos\theta~\eta_{1\mu}+\sin\theta~i~\eta_{2\mu}\,,

(22)

\displaystyle J_{2\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin\theta~\eta_{1\mu}+\cos\theta~i~\eta_{2\mu}\,.

(23)

When setting the mixing angle to be
\theta=-5.0^{\rm o}
, these two currents satisfy

\langle 0|J_{1\mu}{J_{2\nu}^{\dagger}}|0\rangle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\ll\langle 0|J_{1\mu}J_{1\nu}^{\dagger}|0\rangle\\
\ll\langle 0|J_{2\mu}J_{2\nu}^{\dagger}|0\rangle\end{array}\right.\,,

(24)

with the threshold value around
s_{0}\approx 6.0
GeV
2
and the Borel mass around
M_{B}^{2}\approx 2.5
GeV
2
. This condition indicates that the two currents
J_{1\mu}
and
J_{2\mu}
are non-correlated,
i.e.
, they can not mainly couple to the same state
Y
, otherwise,

\displaystyle\langle 0|J_{1\mu}J_{2\nu}^{\dagger}|0\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\delta(s-M^{2}_{n})\langle 0|J_{1\mu}|n\rangle\langle n|J_{2\nu}^{\dagger}|0\rangle+\cdots

(25)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\delta(s-M^{2}_{Y})\langle 0|J_{1\mu}|Y\rangle\langle Y|J_{2\nu}^{\dagger}|0\rangle+\cdots

\displaystyle\neq

\displaystyle 0\,.

Accordingly, we assume that
J_{1\mu}
and
J_{2\mu}
mainly couple to two different states
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
through

\displaystyle\langle 0|J_{1\mu}|Y_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{Y_{1}}~\epsilon_{\mu}\,,

(26)

\displaystyle\langle 0|J_{2\mu}|Y_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{Y_{2}}~\epsilon_{\mu}\,,

(27)

where
f_{Y_{1}}
and
f_{Y_{1}}
are the decay constants, and
\epsilon_{\mu}
is the polarization vector.

In Ref.
Chen:2018kuu
we use
J_{1\mu}
and
J_{2\mu}
to perform QCD sum rule analyses. When setting the working regions to be 5.0 GeV
{}^{2}<s_{0}<7.0
GeV
2
and 2.0 GeV
{}^{2}<M_{B}^{2}<4.0
GeV
2
, we calculate the masses of
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
to be

\displaystyle M_{Y_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2.41\pm 0.25{\rm~GeV}\,,

(28)

\displaystyle M_{Y_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2.34\pm 0.17{\rm~GeV}\,,

with the mass splitting

\Delta M=71^{+172}_{-~48}{\rm~MeV}\,.

(29)

The mass extracted from
J_{2\mu}
is consistent with the experimental mass of the
\phi(2170)

. The QCD sum rule result extracted from the non-correlated current

II.2
Meson-meson currents and Fierz rearrangement

Besides the diquark-antidiquark currents
\eta_{1\mu}
and
\eta_{2\mu}
, we can also construct the fully-strange meson-meson currents. There are four fully-strange meson-meson interpolating currents with the quantum number
J^{PC}=1^{--}
:

\displaystyle\xi_{1\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\bar{s}_{a}s_{a})(\bar{s}_{b}\gamma_{\mu}s_{b})\,,

(30)

\displaystyle\xi_{2\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\bar{s}_{a}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma_{5}s_{a})(\bar{s}_{b}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\gamma_{5}s_{b})\,,

(31)

\displaystyle\xi_{3\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\lambda_{ab}}{\lambda_{cd}}(\bar{s}_{a}s_{b})(\bar{s}_{c}\gamma_{\mu}s_{d})\,,

(32)

\displaystyle\xi_{4\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\lambda_{ab}}{\lambda_{cd}}(\bar{s}_{a}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma_{5}s_{b})(\bar{s}_{c}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\gamma_{5}s_{d})\,.

(33)

We can derive through the Fierz rearrangement that only two of them are independent,
e.g.
,

\displaystyle\xi_{3\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{5}{3}\xi_{1\mu}-i\xi_{2\mu}\,,\,\,

(34)

\displaystyle\xi_{4\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3i\xi_{1\mu}+\frac{1}{3}\xi_{2\mu}\,.

Moreover, we can also derive through the Fierz rearrangement the relations between the diquark-antidiquark currents
\eta_{i}
and the meson-meson currents
\xi_{i}
:

\displaystyle\eta_{1\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\xi_{1\mu}+i\xi_{2\mu}\,,

(35)

\displaystyle\eta_{2\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3i\xi_{1\mu}-\xi_{2\mu}\,.

(36)

Therefore, these two constructions are equivalent with each other, but note that this equivalence is just between the local diquark-antidiquark and meson-meson currents, while the tightly-bound diquark-antidiquark tetraquark states and the weakly-bound meson-meson molecular states are significantly different. To well describe them, we need the non-local currents, but we are still not able to use them to perform QCD sum rule analyses yet.

We can use Eqs. (
35
) and (
36
) to transform the mixing currents
J_{1\mu}
and
J_{2\mu}
to be

\displaystyle J_{1\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-0.74\xi_{1\mu}+1.08i\xi_{2\mu}\,,

(37)

\displaystyle J_{2\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2.90\xi_{1\mu}-1.08i\xi_{2\mu}\,.

(38)

These two Fierz identities will be used in Sec.
III
to study the strong decays of the two states
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
.

III
Relative branching ratios

In this section we study the strong decays of the fully-strange tetraquark states with the quantum number
J^{PC}=1^{--}
. As depicted in Fig.
2
, when one quark meets one antiquark and the other quark meets the other antiquark at the same time, a fully-strange tetraquark state can fall apart to two strangeonium mesons. This process can be described by the Fierz identities given in Eqs. (
37
) and (
38
).

Figure 2:
The fall-apart decay process of a fully-strange tetraquark state to two strangeonium mesons.

Let us start with Eq. (
37
) and perform analyses qualitatively. The strangeonium operators
J^{S}=\bar{s}_{a}s_{a}
and
J^{V}_{\mu}=\bar{s}_{b}\gamma_{\mu}s_{b}
couple to the
f_{0}(980)
and
\phi(1020)
, respectively. Hence, the meson-meson current
\xi_{1\mu}
well couples to the
\phi f_{0}(980)
channel, and the mixing current
J_{1\mu}
also couples to this channel. Accordingly, the state
Y_{1}
can decay to this channel. Similarly, we can derive six other possible channels to be
\phi\eta
,
\phi\eta^{\prime}
,
\phi f_{1}(1420)
,
h_{1}(1415)\eta
,
h_{1}(1415)\eta^{\prime}
, and
h_{1}(1415)f_{1}(1420)
. Among them, the
\phi\eta
,
\phi\eta^{\prime}
,
h_{1}(1415)\eta
, and
h_{1}(1415)\eta^{\prime}
channels are kinematically allowed.

In principle, we need the coupling of
J_{1\mu}
to
Y_{1}
as an input to quantitatively calculate the partial decay widths of these channels. This parameter has been defined in Eq. (
26
) as
f_{Y_{1}}
. However, it is not necessary if we just want to calculate the relative branching ratios. We still take Eq. (
37
) as an example, from which we can extract the couplings of the mixing current
J_{1\mu}
to the
\phi f_{0}(980)
and
\phi\eta
channels:

\displaystyle\langle 0|J_{1\mu}|\phi(p_{1},\epsilon_{1})~f_{0}(p_{2})\rangle

(50)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-0.74\times\epsilon_{1}^{\mu}~m_{f_{0}}f_{f_{0}}~m_{\phi}f_{\phi}\,,

\displaystyle\langle 0|J_{1\mu}|\phi(p_{1},\epsilon_{1})~\eta(p_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.54\times f_{\eta}f^{T}_{\phi}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}p_{2}^{\nu}(p_{1}^{\alpha}\epsilon_{1}^{\beta}-p_{1}^{\beta}\epsilon_{1}^{\alpha})\,.

Then we can extract the couplings of the state
Y_{1}
to the
\phi f_{0}(980)
and
\phi\eta
channels:

\displaystyle\langle Y_{1}(p,\epsilon)|\phi(p_{1},\epsilon_{1})~f_{0}(p_{2})\rangle

(52)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-0.74c\times\epsilon\cdot\epsilon_{1}~m_{f_{0}}f_{f_{0}}~m_{\phi}f_{\phi}\,,

\displaystyle\langle Y_{1}(p,\epsilon)|\phi(p_{1},\epsilon_{1})~\eta(p_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.54c\times f_{\eta}f^{T}_{\phi}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\epsilon^{\mu}p_{2}^{\nu}(p_{1}^{\alpha}\epsilon_{1}^{\beta}-p_{1}^{\beta}\epsilon_{1}^{\alpha})\,.

The overall factor
c
is related to the decay constant
f_{Y_{1}}
. After calculating the partial decay widths
\Gamma_{Y_{1}\to\phi f_{0}(980)}
and
\Gamma_{Y_{1}\to\phi\eta}
, we can eliminate this factor and obtain

{\mathcal{B}(Y_{1}\to\phi f_{0}(980))\over\mathcal{B}(Y_{1}\to\phi\eta)}=1.14\,.

(53)

Similarly, we can investigate the
\phi\eta^{\prime}
,
h_{1}(1415)\eta
, and
h_{1}(1415)\eta^{\prime}
channels to obtain:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\,Y_{1}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle~\phi\eta~:~\phi\eta^{\prime}~:~\phi f_{0}~:h_{1}(1415)\eta:h_{1}(1415)\eta^{\prime}\,)

(54)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1.00:\,0.71\,:\,1.14\,:~~~~0.74~~~~:~0.32\,.

The above calculations are done within the naive factorization scheme, so our uncertainty is significantly larger than the well-developed QCD factorization scheme

;
Beneke:2000ry
;
Beneke:2001ev

IV
Summary and discussions

In this paper we systematically study the strong decays of the
\phi(2170)
and
X(2436)
as two fully-strange tetraquark states with the quantum number
J^{PC}=1^{--}
. Their corresponding fully-strange tetraquark currents have been systematically constructed in our previous studies
Chen:2008ej
;
Chen:2018kuu
, where we consider both the diquark-antidiquark and meson-meson constructions. We have also derived their relations there through the Fierz rearrangement, which are used in the present study to study their strong decay properties.

There are two independent diquark-antidiquark currents, defined in Eqs. (
II.1
) and (
II.1
) as
\eta_{1\mu}
and
\eta_{2\mu}
. In Ref.
Chen:2008ej
we calculate their diagonal correlation functions, and in Ref.
Chen:2018kuu
we further calculate their off-diagonal correlation function. Based on the obtained results, we construct two mixing currents, defined in Eqs. (
22
) and (
23
) as
J_{1\mu}
and
J_{2\mu}
with the mixing angle
\theta=-5.0^{\rm o}
. These two mixing currents are non-correlated with each other, so they separately couple to two different states
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
, whose masses are calculated in Ref.
Chen:2018kuu
through the QCD sum rule method to be

\displaystyle M_{Y_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2.41\pm 0.25{\rm~GeV}\,,

\displaystyle M_{Y_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2.34\pm 0.17{\rm~GeV}\,.

These two values are consistent with the experimental masses of the
X(2436)
and
\phi(2170)
, indicating their possible explanations as the fully-strange tetraquark states
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
, respectively. Accordingly, we can use the mixing currents
J_{1\mu}
and
J_{2\mu}
to further study their decay properties.

We use the Fierz rearrangement to transform the mixing currents
J_{1\mu}
and
J_{2\mu}
to be the combinations of the meson-meson currents
\xi_{1\mu}
and
\xi_{2\mu}
, as defined in Eqs. (
30
) and (
31
). The obtained Fierz identities are given in Eqs. (
37
) and (
38
). Based on these results, we study the decay mechanism depicted in Fig.
2
, where a fully-strange tetraquark state fall-apart decays to two strangeonium mesons. We consider altogether seven possible channels:
\phi\eta
,
\phi\eta^{\prime}
,
\phi f_{0}(980)
,
\phi f_{1}(1420)
,
h_{1}(1415)\eta
,
h_{1}(1415)\eta^{\prime}
, and
h_{1}(1415)f_{1}(1420)
. Some of these channels are kinematically possible, whose relative branching ratios are calculated to be:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\,X(2436)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\phi\eta:\phi\eta^{\prime}:\phi f_{0}:h_{1}(1415)\eta:h_{1}(1415)\eta^{\prime}\,)

(58)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1\,:\,0.71\,:\,1.14\,:~~~0.74~~~:0.32\,.

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\,\phi(2170)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\phi\eta:\phi\eta^{\prime}:\phi f_{0}:h_{1}(1415)\eta\,)

(59)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1\,:\,0.63\,:19.52:~~~0.69\,.

The
X(2436)
and
\phi(2170)
satisfy that

\displaystyle R_{\eta/\eta^{\prime}}^{X(2436)}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}(X(2436)\to\phi\eta)\over\mathcal{B}(X(2436)\to\phi\eta^{\prime})}=1.40\,,

(60)

\displaystyle R_{\eta/\eta^{\prime}}^{\phi(2170)}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}(\phi(2170)\to\phi\eta)\over\mathcal{B}(\phi(2170)\to\phi\eta^{\prime})}=1.59\,,

(61)

and moreover, these two ratios do not depend on the mixing angle
\theta
. Compared to the BESIII measurement listed in Eq. (
I
), our theoretical results within the fully-strange tetraquark picture are consistent with their second solution
R_{\eta/\eta^{\prime}}^{\rm{exp}}=1.42_{-0.60}^{+1.03}
.

The
\phi(2170)
has been observed in the
\phi f_{0}(980)
,
\phi\eta
, and
\phi\eta^{\prime}
channels. Our results suggest that it can also be searched for in the
h_{1}(1415)\eta
channel. There are some evidences of the
X(2436)
in the
\phi f_{0}(980)
channel. Our results suggest that it can also be searched for in the
\phi\eta
,
\phi\eta^{\prime}
,
h_{1}(1415)\eta
, and
h_{1}(1415)\eta^{\prime}
channels. We propose to examine these decay channels in the future Belle-II, BESIII, COMPASS, GlueX, J-PARC, and PANDA experiments. Especially, the ratios
R_{\eta/\eta^{\prime}}^{\phi(2170)}\approx R_{\eta/\eta^{\prime}}^{X(2436)}\approx 1.5
can be useful in clarifying the nature of the
\phi(2170)
and
X(2436)
as the fully-strange tetraquark states with the quantum number
J^{PC}=1^{--}
.

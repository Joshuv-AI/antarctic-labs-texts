2-complex symmetric composition operators on H 2 H^{2} Thanks: ∗ \ast Corresponding author.
Lian Hu, Songxiao Li ∗ and Rong Yang

Abstract
. In this paper, we study 2-complex symmetric composition operators with the conjugation J J on the Hardy space H 2 H^{2} . More precisely, we obtain the necessary and sufficient condition for the composition operator C ϕ C_{\phi} to be 2-complex symmetric when the symbols ϕ \phi is an automorphism of 𝔻 {\mathbb{D}} . We also characterize the 2-complex symmetric composition operator C ϕ C_{\phi} on the Hardy space H 2 H^{2} when ϕ \phi is a linear fractional self-map of 𝔻 {\mathbb{D}} . Keywords : Composition operator; m m -complex symmetric; normal

1.
Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{D}}
and
\partial{\mathbb{D}}
be the open unit disk
\mathbb{C}
and the unit circle in the complex plane, respectively. Let
H({\mathbb{D}})
be the set of all analytic functions on
{\mathbb{D}}
. The Hardy space
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
is the space of all
f\in H({\mathbb{D}})
such that

\|f\|^{2}=\sup_{0<r<1}{1\over 2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{2}d\theta<\infty.

The space
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space, that is, for each
w\in{\mathbb{D}}
and
f\in H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
, there is a unique function
K_{w}\in H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
such that

\langle f,K_{w}\rangle=f(w),

where
K_{w}={1\over 1-\bar{w}z}
is said to be the reproducing kernel at
w
. For
g\in L^{\infty}(\partial{\mathbb{D}})
, the Toeplitz operator
T_{g}
is defined as
T_{g}f=P(fg)
for
f\in H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
, where
P
is the orthogonal projection of
L^{2}
onto
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
. Recall that

T_{g}^{\ast}K_{w}=\overline{f(w)}K_{w}

for each
w\in{\mathbb{D}}
and
f\in H^{\infty}
.

Let
\phi
be an analytic self-map of
{\mathbb{D}}
. Recall that the composition operator
C_{\phi}
is defined by

C_{\phi}f(z)=f(\phi(z)),~~~~~~~f\in H({\mathbb{D}}),~~~z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

It is easy to see that
C_{\phi}^{\ast}K_{w}=K_{\phi(w)}
foe each
w\in{\mathbb{D}}
.

Let
H
and
\mathcal{B}(H)
stand for a separable complex Hilbert space and the set of all continuous linear operators on
H
, respectively. An operator
C:H\to H
is said to be a conjugation on
H
if it is

(a)

anti-linear or conjugate-linear:
C(\alpha+\beta y)=\bar{\alpha}C(x)+\bar{\beta}C(y)
, for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
and
x,y\in H
,

(b)

isometric:
\|Cx\|=\|x\|
, for all
x\in H
,

(c)

involutive:
C^{2}=I_{d}
, where
I_{d}
is an identity operator.

It is easy to check that
(Jf)(z)=\overline{f(\bar{z})}
is a conjugation on the space
H^{2}
.

An operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(H)
is said to be complex symmetric if
T=CT^{\ast}C
, where
C
is a conjugation on
H
. We also say that
T
is complex symmetric with
C
or
C
-symmetric operator. The class of complex symmetric operators includes all normal operators, binormal operators, Hankel operators, compressed Toeplitz operators and the Volterra integration operator. The general study of complex symmetric operators was initiated by Garcia, Putinar and Wogen in
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
. See
[
13
,
17
,
19
,
12
,
6
,
18
,
20
,
21
,
7
]

for more results on complex symmetric operators.

Let
m
be a positive integer and
T\in\mathcal{B}(H)
. In
[
1
]
, the authors defined
m
-complex symmetric operators as follows:
T
is said to be an
m
-complex symmetric operator if there exists some conjugation
C
such that

\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-j}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m\\
j\end{array}\right)T^{*j}CT^{m-j}C=0.

In this case,
T
is said to be
m
-complex symmetric with
C
. When
m=2
, we get

CT^{2}C-2T^{\ast}CTC+{T^{\ast}}^{2}=0,

which is equivalent to

CT^{2}-2T^{\ast}CT+{T^{\ast}}^{2}C=0.

It is clear that
1
-complex symmetric operator is just the complex symmetric operator. By a simple calculation, we see that all complex symmetric operators are 2-complex symmetric operators. In
[
5
]
, the authors studied
m
-complex symmetric weighted shifts on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. We refer the reader to
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
14
]
for more details about
m
-complex symmetric operators.

In this paper, we study 2-complex symmetric composition operators with
J
on the Hardy space
H^{2}
. When the symbols
\phi
is an automorphism of
{\mathbb{D}}
, we see that the composition operator
C_{\phi}
is 2-complex symmetric with
J
if and only if
C_{\phi}
is normal. Furthermore, we also characterize the 2-complex symmetric composition operators on
H^{2}
when the induced map is a linear fractional self-map of
{\mathbb{D}}
.

2.
Main results

We begin this section with Cowen’s formula for the adjoint of a linear fractional self-map. For a linear fractional self-map
\phi(z)={az+b\over cz+d}
of
{\mathbb{D}}
, where
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{C}
and
ad-bc\neq 0
, Cowen in
[
3
]
obtained the following important formula

C_{\phi}^{\ast}=T_{g}C_{\sigma}T_{h}^{\ast},

where

\sigma(z)={\bar{a}z-\bar{c}\over-\bar{b}z+\bar{d}},~~g(z)={1\over-\bar{b}z+\bar{d}},~~h(z)=cz+d.

Lemma 1
.

[
4
]

Let
\phi
be an analytic self-map of
{\mathbb{D}}
. Then
C_{\phi}
is normal if and only if
\phi(z)=az
with
|a|\leq 1
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\phi(z)={az+b\over cz+d}
be a linear fractional self-map of
{\mathbb{D}}
. If
w\in{\mathbb{D}}
satisfies
\bar{a}w\neq\bar{c}
, then the following statements hold:

(a)

JC_{\phi}^{2}K_{w}(z)=K_{\bar{w}}(\overline{\phi_{2}(\bar{z})})
,

(b)

C_{\phi}^{\ast}JC_{\phi}K_{w}(z)=-\bar{c}{g(w)\over\sigma(w)}K_{\phi(0)}(z)+\overline{h({1\over\overline{\sigma(w)}})}g(w)K_{\phi(\overline{\sigma(w)})}(z),

(c)

{C_{\phi}^{\ast}}^{2}JK_{w}(z)=K_{\phi_{2}(\bar{w})}(z),

(d)

JC_{\phi}JC_{\phi}^{\ast}JK_{w}(z)=K_{\phi(\bar{w})}(\overline{\phi(\bar{z})})

where
\sigma(z)={\bar{a}z-\bar{c}\over-\bar{b}z+\bar{d}}
,
g(z)={1\over-\bar{b}z+\bar{d}}
, and
h(z)=cz+d
.

Proof.

Since
Jf(z)=\overline{f(\bar{z})}
and
f(w)=\langle f,K_{w}\rangle
for any
w\in{\mathbb{D}}
and
f\in H^{2}
, then
(a)
and
(c)
hold obviously. Now we only verify
(b)
and
(d)
. For any
w\in{\mathbb{D}}
with
\bar{a}w\neq\bar{c}
, we obtain

\displaystyle C_{\phi}^{\ast}JC_{\phi}K_{w}(z)=

\displaystyle C_{\phi}^{\ast}K_{\bar{w}}(\overline{\phi(\bar{z})})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\phi}^{\ast}{\bar{c}z+\bar{d}\over(\bar{c}-\bar{a}w)z+(\bar{d}-\bar{b}w)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\phi}^{\ast}\left({\bar{c}\over\bar{c}-\bar{a}w}+\left(\bar{d}-{\bar{c}(\bar{d}-\bar{b}w)\over\bar{c}-\bar{a}w}\right){1\over\bar{d}-\bar{b}w}{1\over 1-{\bar{a}w-\bar{c}\over\bar{d}-\bar{b}w}z}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bar{c}\over\bar{c}-\bar{a}w}C_{\phi}^{\ast}K_{0}(z)+\left(\bar{d}-{\bar{c}(\bar{d}-\bar{b}w)\over\bar{c}-\bar{a}w}\right){1\over\bar{d}-\bar{b}w}C_{\phi}^{\ast}K_{\overline{\sigma(w)}}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\bar{c}{g(w)\over\sigma(w)}K_{\phi(0)}(z)+\overline{h({1\over\overline{\sigma(w)}})}g(w)K_{\phi(\overline{\sigma(w)})}(z).

Clearly,

\displaystyle JC_{\phi}JC_{\phi}^{\ast}JK_{w}(z)

\displaystyle=JC_{\phi}JC_{\phi}^{\ast}K_{\bar{w}}(z)=JC_{\phi}JK_{\phi(\bar{w})}(z)

\displaystyle=JC_{\phi}K_{\overline{\phi(\bar{w})}}(z)=JK_{\overline{\phi(\bar{w})}}(\phi(z))

\displaystyle=K_{\phi(\bar{w})}(\overline{\phi(\bar{z})}).

The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
\phi
be an analytic self-map of
{\mathbb{D}}
. If
C_{\phi}
is normal, then
C_{\phi}^{\ast}
and
C_{\phi}
is 2-complex symmetric with
J
.

Proof.

Assume that
C_{\phi}
is normal. From Corollary 3.10 in
[
16
]
we see that
C_{\phi}
is complex symmetric with
J
. Hence,
C_{\phi}
is 2-complex symmetric with
J
. Write
\phi(z)=az
with
|a|\leq 1
from Lemma
1
. We notice that
T^{*}
is
2
-complex symmetric with
J
if and only if

{T^{\ast}}^{2}J-2JTJT^{\ast}J+JT^{2}=0.

In view of Lemma
2
, set
\sigma(z)=\bar{a}z
,
g(z)=h(z)=1
, we have

{C_{\phi}^{\ast}}^{2}JK_{w}(z)=K_{\phi_{2}(\bar{w})}(z)={1\over 1-\bar{a}^{2}wz},

\displaystyle JC_{\phi}JC_{\phi}^{\ast}JK_{w}(z)

\displaystyle=K_{\phi(\bar{w})}(\overline{\phi(\bar{z})})={1\over 1-\bar{a}^{2}wz}

and

JC_{\phi}^{2}K_{w}(z)=K_{\bar{w}}(\overline{\phi_{2}(\bar{z})})={1\over 1-w\overline{\phi_{2}(\bar{z})}}={1\over 1-\bar{a}^{2}wz}.

Thus, it is obvious that
{C^{\ast}_{\phi}}^{2}J-2JC_{\phi}JC^{\ast}_{\phi}J+JC^{2}_{\phi}=0
, which means that
C_{\phi}^{\ast}
is 2-complex symmetric with
J
.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
\phi
be an analytic self-map of
{\mathbb{D}}
. If
C_{\phi}
is 2-complex symmetric with
J
, then

2\phi_{2}(0)-4\phi(0)+\phi(0)\phi_{2}(0)=0.

Proof.

Since
f(w)=\langle f,K_{w}\rangle
for any
w\in{\mathbb{D}}
and
f\in H^{2}
, we have

\langle JC_{\phi}^{2}K_{0},K_{1\over 2}\rangle=\langle JK_{0},K_{1\over 2}\rangle=1,

\langle C_{\phi}^{\ast}JC_{\phi}K_{0},K_{1\over 2}\rangle=\langle C_{\phi}^{\ast}K_{0},K_{1\over 2}\rangle=\langle K_{\phi(0)},K_{1\over 2}\rangle={2\over 2-\overline{\phi(0)}}

and

\langle{C_{\phi}^{\ast}}^{2}JK_{0},K_{1\over 2}\rangle=\langle{C_{\phi}^{\ast}}^{2}K_{0},K_{1\over 2}\rangle=\langle K_{\phi_{2}(0)},K_{1\over 2}\rangle={2\over 2-\overline{\phi_{2}(0)}}.

By the assumption that
C_{\phi}
is 2-complex symmetric with
J
, we get that

1-{4\over 2-\overline{\phi(0)}}+{2\over 2-\overline{\phi_{2}(0)}}=0.

By a simple calculation, we get the desired result.
∎

Theorem 1
.

Let
\phi
be an automorphism of
{\mathbb{D}}
. Then
C_{\phi}
is 2-complex symmetric with
J
if and only if
C_{\phi}
is normal.

Proof.

Assume that
C_{\phi}
is normal. From Lemma 3 we see that
C_{\phi}
is 2-complex symmetric with
J
.

Now suppose that
\phi(z)=\lambda{a-z\over 1-\bar{a}z}

,

and

is 2-complex symmetric with

.

\displaystyle JC_{\phi}^{2}K_{w}(z)=K_{\bar{w}}(\overline{\phi_{2}(\bar{z})})={a(\bar{\lambda}z-\overline{\lambda a})-(az-1)\over a(\bar{\lambda}z-\overline{\lambda a})-(az-1)-w[\bar{\lambda}(\bar{\lambda}z-\overline{\lambda a})-\overline{\lambda a}(az-1)]},

\displaystyle C_{\phi}^{\ast}JC_{\phi}K_{w}(z)=

\displaystyle a{g(w)\over\sigma(w)}K_{\phi(0)}(z)+\overline{h({1\over\overline{\sigma(w)}})}g(w)K_{\phi(\overline{\sigma(w)})}(z)

(2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\displaystyle a\over(-\bar{\lambda}w+a)(1-\overline{\lambda a}z)}+{\bar{\lambda}w(|a|^{2}-1)\over(-\bar{\lambda}w+a)(-\overline{\lambda a}w+1)}

\displaystyle\cdot{-\overline{\lambda a}w+1+a\bar{\lambda}w-{a}^{2}\over-\overline{\lambda a}w+1+a\bar{\lambda}w-{a}^{2}-(\bar{\lambda}^{2}{w}-\bar{\lambda}^{2}\bar{a}^{2}{w}-\bar{\lambda}a+\overline{\lambda a})z}

\displaystyle{C_{\phi}^{\ast}}^{2}JK_{w}(z)=K_{\phi_{2}(\bar{w})}(z)={a(\bar{\lambda}w-\overline{\lambda a})-(aw-1)\over a(\bar{\lambda}w-\overline{\lambda a})-(aw-1)-[\bar{\lambda}(\bar{\lambda}w-\overline{\lambda a})-\overline{\lambda a}(aw-1)]z}

(3)

for any

.
Taking

in (

), (
2
) and (

), we have

JC_{\phi}^{2}K_{0}(z)=1,\,\,\,\,\,\,C_{\phi}^{\ast}JC_{\phi}K_{0}(z)={1\over 1-\overline{\lambda a}z},

and

{C_{\phi}^{\ast}}^{2}JK_{0}(z)={-\bar{\lambda}|a|^{2}+1\over-\bar{\lambda}|a|^{2}+1+(\bar{\lambda}^{2}\bar{a}-\overline{\lambda a})z}

for any
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
.
Since
C_{\phi}
is 2-complex symmetric with
J
, we get

\displaystyle{1+\overline{\lambda a}z\over 1-\overline{\lambda a}z}={1-\bar{\lambda}|a|^{2}\over-\bar{\lambda}|a|^{2}+1+(\bar{\lambda}^{2}\bar{a}-\overline{\lambda a})z}.

(4)

Calculating and noting that the coefficients of

must be
0
, we obtain that
\lambda^{2}a^{2}(\lambda-1)=0

and

. Hence, (

which implies that
\bar{a}z=0
for all

. Hence

\phi(z)=-\lambda z
with

. Lemma

gives that

is normal. The proof is complete.
∎

Observational Constraints on Asymptotic Safety Inflation in Gravity’s Rainbow
Phongpichit Channuie

Abstract
Using suitable Renormalization Group (RG) based re-summation of quantum corrections to R 2 R^{2} term, a re-summed version of the effective Lagrangian can be obtained [ 1 ] . In the context of gravity as an Asymptotically Safe (AS) theory, authors of Refs. [ 2 , 3 ] proposed a refined Starobinsky model, L AS = M p 2 ​ R / 2 + ( α / 2 ) ​ R 2 / [ 1 + β ​ ln ⁡ ( R / μ 2 ) ] L_{\rm AS}=M^{2}_{p}R/2+(\alpha/2)R^{2}/[1+\beta\ln(R/\mu^{2})] , where R R is the Ricci scalar, α \alpha and β \beta are constants and μ \mu is an energy scale. In the present work, we embed this underlying effective Lagrangian within the framework of gravity’s rainbow. By implementing the COBE normalization and the Planck constraint on the scalar spectrum, we demonstrate that the power spectrum of curvature perturbation relies on α \alpha and β \beta , as well as on a rainbow parameter. Similarly, the scalar spectral index n s n_{s} is influenced by β \beta and the rainbow parameter, yet remains unaffected by α \alpha . Additionally, the tensor-to-scalar ratio r r solely depends on the rainbow parameter. Remarkably, when requiring n s n_{s} to be consistent with the Planck collaboration at 1 ​ σ 1\sigma confidence level, the upper limit on the tensor-to-scalar ratio r < 0.036 r<0.036 can be naturally satisfied. This value potentially holds promise for potential measurement by Stage IV CMB ground experiments and is certainly within reach of future dedicated space missions.

\displaystyle A=-\frac{1}{H+\dot{F}/2F}\left[\frac{\nabla^{2}\mathcal{R}}{a^{2}\tilde{f}^{2}}+\frac{3H\dot{F}\dot{\mathcal{R}}}{2F(H+\dot{F}/2F)}\right]\ .

(27)

Using the background equation (
7
), we obtain from Eq.(
25
):

\displaystyle\dot{A}+\left(2H+\frac{\dot{F}}{2F}\right)A+\frac{\dot{\tilde{f}}A}{\tilde{f}}+\frac{3\dot{F}\dot{\Phi}}{2F}+\left[\frac{3\ddot{F}+6H\dot{F}}{2F}+\frac{\nabla^{2}}{a^{2}\tilde{f}^{2}}\right]\Phi+\frac{3\dot{F}}{2F}\frac{\Phi\dot{\tilde{f}}}{\tilde{f}}=0.

(28)

Substituting Eq.(
26
) and (
27
) into Eq.(
28
), we find that in Fourier space, the curvature perturbation satisfies the following equation:

\displaystyle\ddot{\mathcal{R}}+\frac{1}{a^{3}Q_{s}}\frac{d}{dt}(a^{3}Q_{s})\dot{\mathcal{R}}+\frac{\dot{\tilde{f}}}{\tilde{f}}\dot{\mathcal{R}}+\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}\tilde{f}^{2}}\mathcal{R}=0\ ,

(29)

where
k
is a comoving wave number and
Q_{s}
is defined by

\displaystyle Q_{s}\equiv\frac{3\dot{F}^{2}}{2\kappa^{2}F(H+\dot{F}/2F)^{2}}\ .

(30)

Introducing new variables
z_{s}=a\sqrt{Q_{s}}
and
u=z_{s}\mathcal{R}
, Eq.(
29
) can be simplified to:

\displaystyle u^{\prime\prime}+\left(k^{2}-\frac{z_{s}^{\prime\prime}}{z_{s}}\right)u=0\ ,

(31)

where a prime denotes a derivative with respect to the new time coordinate
\eta=\int(a\tilde{f})^{-1}dt
. To determine the spectrum of curvature perturbations, we define slow-roll parameters as:

\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}\equiv-\frac{\dot{H}}{H^{2}},\ \ \epsilon_{2}\equiv\frac{\dot{F}}{2HF},\ \ \epsilon_{3}\equiv\frac{\dot{E}}{2HE}\ ,

(32)

where
E\equiv 3\dot{F}^{2}/2\kappa^{2}
. Consequently,
Q_{s}
can be rewritten as:

\displaystyle Q_{s}=\frac{E}{FH^{2}(1+\epsilon_{2})^{2}}\ .

(33)

Here, the parameters
\epsilon_{i}
are assumed to be nearly constant during inflation, and
\tilde{f}\simeq(H/M)^{\lambda}
. These assumptions allow us to calculate

\eta
as
\eta=-1/[(1-(1+\lambda)\epsilon_{1})\tilde{f}aH]
. If
\dot{\epsilon_{i}}\simeq 0
, a term
z_{s}^{\prime\prime}/z_{s}
satisfies:

\displaystyle\frac{z_{s}^{\prime\prime}}{z_{s}}=\frac{\nu^{2}_{\mathcal{R}}-1/4}{\eta^{2}}\ ,

(34)

with:

\displaystyle\nu_{\mathcal{R}}^{2}=\frac{1}{4}+\frac{(1+\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}+\epsilon_{3})(2-\lambda\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}+\epsilon_{3})}{(1-(\lambda+1)\epsilon_{1})^{2}}\ .

(35)

Therefore we find the solution of Eq.(
31
) written in terms of a linear combination of Hankel functions

\displaystyle u=\frac{\sqrt{\pi|\eta|}}{2}\textmd{e}^{i(1+2\nu_{\mathcal{R}})\pi/4}\left[c_{1}\textmd{H}_{\nu_{\mathcal{R}}}^{(1)}(k|\eta|)+c_{2}\textmd{H}_{\nu_{\mathcal{R}}}^{(2)}(k|\eta|)\right]\ ,

(36)

where
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
are integration constants and
\textmd{H}_{\nu_{\mathcal{R}}}^{(1)}(k|\eta|)
,
\textmd{H}_{\nu_{\mathcal{R}}}^{(2)}(k|\eta|)
are the Hankel functions of the first kind and the second kind respectively.
In the asymptotic past
k\eta\rightarrow-\infty
, we find from Eq.(
36
)
u\rightarrow\textmd{e}^{-ik\eta}/\sqrt{2k}
. This implies
c_{1}=1
and
c_{2}=0
giving the following solutions

\displaystyle u=\frac{\sqrt{\pi|\eta|}}{2}\textmd{e}^{i(1+2\nu_{\mathcal{R}})\pi/4}\textmd{H}_{\nu_{\mathcal{R}}}^{(1)}(k|\eta|)\ .

(37)

By defining the power spectrum of curvature perturbations

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}\equiv\frac{4\pi k^{3}}{(2\pi)^{3}}|\mathcal{R}|^{2}\ ,

(38)

and using Eq.(
37
) and
u=z_{s}\mathcal{R}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}=\frac{1}{Q_{s}}\left[(1-(1+\lambda)\epsilon_{1})\frac{\Gamma(\nu_{\mathcal{R}})H}{2\pi\Gamma(3/2)}\left(\frac{H}{M}\right)^{\lambda}\right]^{2}\left(\frac{k|\eta|}{2}\right)^{3-2\nu_{\mathcal{R}}}\ ,

(39)

where we have used
\textmd{H}_{\nu_{\mathcal{R}}}^{(1)}(k|\eta|)\rightarrow-(i/\pi)\Gamma(\nu_{\mathcal{R}})(k|\eta|/2)^{-\nu_{\mathcal{R}}}
for
k|\eta|\rightarrow 0
. Since
\mathcal{R}
is frozen after the Hubble radius crossing,
P_{\mathcal{R}}
should be evaluated at
k=aH
. Now we define the spectral index
n_{\mathcal{R}}
as

\displaystyle n_{s}-1=\left.\frac{d\textmd{ln}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}}{d\textmd{ln}k}\right|_{k=aH}=3-2\nu_{\mathcal{R}}\ .

(40)

The spectral index can be written in terms of the slow-roll parameters as

\displaystyle n_{s}-1\simeq-2(\lambda+2)\epsilon_{1}+2\epsilon_{2}-2\epsilon_{3}\ ,

(41)

where during the inflationary epoch, we have assumed that
|\epsilon_{i}|\ll 1
. Notice that the spectrum is nearly scale-invariant when
|\epsilon_{i}|
are much smaller than unity, i.e.
n_{\mathcal{R}}\simeq 1
. Subsequently, the power spectrum of curvature perturbation takes the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}\approx\frac{1}{Q_{s}}\left(\frac{H}{2\pi}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{H}{M}\right)^{2\lambda}\ .

(42)

Note that we obtain the standard result when setting
\lambda=0

[
53
]
. We next consider the tensor perturbation. In general
h_{ij}
can be generally written as

\displaystyle h_{ij}=h_{+}e^{+}_{ij}+h_{\times}e^{\times}_{ij}\ ,

(43)

where
e^{+}_{ij}
and
e^{\times}_{ij}
are the polarization tensors corresponding to the two polarization states of
h_{ij}
. Let
\vec{k}
be in the direction along the z-axis, then the non-vanishing components of polarization tensors are
e^{+}_{xx}=-e^{+}_{yy}=1
and
e^{\times}_{xy}=e^{\times}_{yx}=1
. Without taking into account the scalar and vector perturbation, the perturbed FLRW metric can be written as

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-\frac{dt^{2}}{\tilde{f}(\varepsilon)^{2}}+a^{2}(t)h_{\times}dxdy+a^{2}(t)\left[(1+h_{+})dx^{2}+(1-h_{+})dy^{2}+dz^{2}\right].

(44)

Using Eq.(
5
), we can show that the Fourier components
h_{\chi}
satisfy the following equation

\displaystyle\ddot{h}_{\chi}+\frac{(a^{3}F)^{\cdot}}{a^{3}F}\dot{h}_{\chi}+\frac{\dot{\tilde{f}}}{\tilde{f}}\dot{h}_{\chi}+\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}\tilde{f}^{2}}h_{\chi}=0\ ,

(45)

where
\chi
denotes polarizations
+
and
\times
. Following a similar procedure to the case of curvature perturbation, let us introduce the new variables
z_{t}=a\sqrt{F}
and
u_{\chi}=z_{t}h_{\chi}/\sqrt{2\kappa^{2}}
. Therefore Eq. (
45
) can be written as

\displaystyle u^{\prime\prime}_{\chi}+\left(k^{2}-\frac{z_{t}^{\prime\prime}}{z_{t}}\right)u_{\chi}=0\ .

(46)

Notice that for a massless scalar field
u_{\chi}
has dimension of mass. By choosing
\dot{\epsilon}_{i}=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{z_{t}^{\prime\prime}}{z_{t}}=\frac{\nu^{2}_{t}-1/4}{\eta^{2}}\ ,

(47)

where

\displaystyle\nu^{2}_{t}=\frac{1}{4}+\frac{(1+\epsilon_{2})(2-(1+\lambda)\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2})}{(1-(1+\lambda)\epsilon_{1})^{2}}\ .

(48)

Similarly the solution to Eq.(
46
) can be also expressed in terms of a linear combination of Hankel functions. Taking into account polarization
states, the power spectrum of tensor perturbations
P_{T}
after the Hubble radius crossing reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{T}

\displaystyle=4\times\frac{2\kappa^{2}}{a^{2}F}\frac{4\pi k^{3}}{(2\pi)^{3}}|u_{\chi}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{16}{\pi}\left(\frac{H}{M_{P}}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{F}\left[(1-(1+\lambda)\epsilon_{1})\frac{\Gamma(\nu_{t})}{\Gamma(3/2)}\left(\frac{H}{M}\right)^{\lambda}\right]^{2}\left(\frac{k|\eta|}{2}\right)^{3-2\nu_{t}},

(49)

where we have used
\tilde{f}\simeq(H/M)^{\lambda}
. Therefore
\nu_{t}
can be estimated by assuming that the slow-roll parameters are very small during inflation as

\displaystyle\nu_{t}\simeq\frac{3}{2}+(1+\lambda)\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}\ .

(50)

In addition, the spectral index of tensor perturbations is determined via

\displaystyle n_{T}=\left.\frac{d\textmd{ln}\mathcal{P}_{T}}{d\textmd{ln}k}\right|_{k=aH}=3-2\nu_{t}\simeq-2(1+\lambda)\epsilon_{1}-2\epsilon_{2}\ .

(51)

The power spectrum
\mathcal{P}_{T}
can also be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{T}\simeq\frac{16}{\pi}\left(\frac{H}{M_{P}}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{F}\left(\frac{H}{M}\right)^{2\lambda}\ .

(52)

Also, the tensor-to-scalar ratio
r
can be determined by invoking the following definition:

\displaystyle r\equiv\frac{\mathcal{P}_{T}}{\mathcal{P}_{\cal R}}\simeq 48\epsilon_{2}^{2}\,.

(53)

IV
Concluding Remarks

In this work, we have considered an effective gravity model (
3
) motivated by asymptotic safety. This model has been proposed by Refs.
[
2
,
3
]
as a refined Starobinsky model in the context of gravity as an asymptotically safe theory. Our model can be viewed as an extension of Starobinsky’s
R^{2}
inflation. In the present work, we have considered the underlying effective Lagrangian within the framework of gravity’s rainbow. The COBE normalization and the Planck constraint on the scalar spectrum have been used to constrain the predictions.

We have demonstrated that the power spectrum of curvature perturbation relies on
\alpha
and
\beta
, as well as on a rainbow parameter. Similarly, the scalar spectral index
n_{s}
is influenced by
\beta
and the rainbow parameter, yet remains unaffected by
\alpha
. Additionally, the tensor-to-scalar ratio
r
solely depends on the rainbow parameter. Remarkably, when requiring
n_{s}
to be consistent with the Planck collaboration at
1\sigma
confidence level, the upper limit on the tensor-to-scalar ratio
r<0.036
can be naturally satisfied. This value potentially holds promise for potential measurement by Stage IV CMB ground experiments and is certainly within reach of future dedicated space missions such as LiteBIRD and COrE.

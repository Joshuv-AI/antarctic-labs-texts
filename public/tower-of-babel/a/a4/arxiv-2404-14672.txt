Locally dualisable modular representations and local regularity
Dave Benson, Srikanth B. Iyengar, Henning Krause and Julia Pevtsova

Abstract
. This work concerns the stable module category of a finite group over a field of characteristic dividing the group order. The minimal localising tensor ideals correspond to the non-maximal homogeneous prime ideals in the cohomology ring of the group. Given such a prime ideal, a number of characterisations of the dualisable objects in the corresponding tensor ideal are given. One characterisation of interest is that they are exactly the modules whose restriction along a corresponding π \pi -point are finite dimensional plus projective. A key insight is the identification of a special property of the stable module category that controls the cohomological behaviour of local dualisable objects. This property, introduced in this work for general triangulated categories and called local regularity, is related to strong generation. A major part of the paper is devoted to developing this notion and investigating its ramifications for various special classes of objects in tensor triangulated categories.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group and
k
a field of characteristic
p
.
In modular representation theory, traditionally one
restricted attention to finitely generated
kG
-modules.
These have a number of desirable properties that make them
easier to work with, such as the Krull–Remak–Schmidt Theorem:
every finitely generated module is a direct sum of indecomposables;
the indecomposables have local endomorphism rings, and the
isomorphism types and multiplicities of the direct summands are
uniquely determined. This implies cancellation: if
M\oplus X\cong N\oplus X
then
M\cong N
. Krull–Remak–Schmidt drives the theory of
vertices and sources, as well as various other well known developments in
the subject.

In the nineties and subsequent decades, it was found that one has to contend with some
infinitely generated modules, even if one is only interested in properties of the finitely generated ones. For example, in
[
5
]
, certain
infinitely generated idempotent modules are used in order to
classify the thick tensor ideals of the stable category of finitely
generated modules in terms of the prime ideal spectrum of the
cohomology ring
H^{*}(G,k)
.

An analogy from homotopy theory is as follows. Suppose that all you are really interested in is finite CW complexes, or even just closed manifolds. You might start to develop cohomology theories such as ordinary cohomology,
K
-theory, or cobordism theory. You then find that these are representable, but only by infinite CW complexes, so you are forced to study these. Cohomology operations then correspond to maps between these representing objects, and this is what makes calculation possible.

In modular representation theory, the idempotent modules above are representing objects in a similar way, and eventually these led to a classification
[
8
]

of the localising tensor ideals of the stable category
\operatorname{StMod}kG
of all
kG
-modules. There are local cohomology functors
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}
corresponding to the homogeneous prime ideals
of
H^{*}(G,k)
, that pick out the minimal localising tensor ideals
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{StMod}kG
, and all localising tensor ideals are determined by the minimal ones that they contain. This sets up a bijection with the subsets of the homogeneous primes in
H^{*}(G,k)
that are different from the maximal ideal of positive degree elements.

The problem with infinitely generated modules is that some rather
unsettling things happen. For example, as soon as the Sylow

p
-subgroups of
G
are non-cyclic, there are
kG
-modules
M

with
M\cong M\oplus M\oplus M
but
M\not\cong M\oplus M
.
So cancellation is violated, and furthermore,

M
and
M\oplus M
are examples of non-isomorphic modules,
each of which is isomorphic to a direct summand of the other.
This and further pathologies are described in
Brenner and Ringel
[
18
]
.
The idempotent modules appearing above do not participate in
such pathologies, and so it is natural to ask for more comfortable
contexts containing these, and sitting between the finitely generated
modules and all modules.

Our goal in this paper is to describe a thick subcategory of
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{StMod}kG
that is large enough to contain the modules used in various constructions, but small enough that pathological behaviour is avoided. The simplest description is that this consists of objects
M

of finite length
in the tensor triangulated category
\mathcal{T}=\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{StMod}kG
. This condition means that for each compact object
C
in
\mathcal{T}
the graded morphisms
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,M)
form a module of finite length over the graded endomorphism ring of the tensor unit. In particular,
M
is endofinite in the sense of
[
32
]
. This subcategory has many descriptions, and here we give some of these.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Proj}H^{*}(G,k)
and a
\pi
-point
\alpha\colon K[t]/(t^{p})\to KG
representing
\mathfrak{p}
. For any object
M
in the tensor triangulated category
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{StMod}kG
, the conditions below are equivalent.

(1)

M
has finite length in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{StMod}kG
;

(2)

M
is dualisable in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{StMod}kG
;

(3)

M
is in the thick subcategory generated by all
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}C
for
C\in\operatorname{stmod}kG
;

(4)

\alpha^{*}(K\otimes_{k}M)
is stably finite dimensional.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between non-maximal homogeneous prime ideals in
H^{*}(G,k)
and equivalence classes of
\pi
-points
\alpha
; see
[
24
]
. Condition (
4
) above means that the restriction of the
KG
-module
K\otimes_{k}M
to
K[t]/(t^{p})
is a direct sum of a finitely generated module and a projective module.

The compact objects in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{StMod}kG
have finite length, but the converse only holds when the prime
\mathfrak{p}
is minimal.
In particular, the tensor identity
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}k
is dualisable, but compact only when
\mathfrak{p}
is minimal. So given the result above we may think of the category of finite length objects as the tensor triangulated completion of the compact objects.

The equivalence (
1
)
\Leftrightarrow
(
4
) is verified in Section
9
. It applies equally to general finite group schemes and not only to finite groups. Condition (
1
) is equivalent to a noetherian property of the
\mathfrak{p}
-completion of
M
. This translation requires a version of Matlis duality for tensor triangulated categories, developed in Section
5
. Carlson
[
21
]
proves that this is equivalent to condition (
4
) when
\mathfrak{p}
is a closed point and
G
is elementary abelian.

The implications (
3
)
\Rightarrow
(
2
)
\Rightarrow
(
1
) hold in a broader context; see Sections
5
and
7
. They have been established in stable homotopy theory by Hovey and Strickland
[
29
]
. As explained in their work, the implication (
1
)
\Rightarrow
(
3
) does not hold there; see also
[
14
]
. The key property of
\operatorname{StMod}kG
that is exploited in our proof of this implication is expressed in the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

For each
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Proj}H^{*}(G,k)
, the subcategory

\operatorname{thick}\{\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}C\mid C\in\operatorname{stmod}kG\}

of
\operatorname{StMod}kG
has a strong generator.

The notion of a strong generator of a category is due to Bondal and van den Bergh
[
17
]
. For the derived category of a commutative noetherian ring
A
and a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq A
, the analogue of the above category is
\operatorname{thick}\{\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}P\mid P\text{ perfect}\}
, and this has a strong generator precisely when the local ring
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is regular. One can thus view the theorem above as stating that
\operatorname{StMod}kG
is
locally regular
. Establishing this property of
\operatorname{StMod}kG
in its various incarnations and exploring its ramifications forms a major part of this work. The regularity result itself appears in Theorem
8.3
; see also the discussion following that result.
The proof is quite involved because there are, in general, many more objects in the thick closure of
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(\operatorname{stmod}kG)
than in its idempotent completion. Examples are given in
[
22
]
.

We give many other equivalent descriptions of dualisable objects of
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{StMod}kG
in Section
8
based on the ground work in Sections
2
-
7
, leading to many pleasing properties of these modules. One of them is that the Krull–Remak–Schmidt Theorem holds, so there are no pathologies of the type mentioned earlier. Another is that dualisability is preserved by the appropriate versions of induction and restriction. In particular, this lays the foundation for the theory of vertices and sources, and makes the Green correspondence work the way one would desire. There is a version for
\operatorname{StMod}kG
, but it is in terms of equivalences of quotient categories rather than inducing and restricting indecomposable objects. The Burry–Carlson–Puig Theorem also works, as does Green’s Indecomposability Theorem; this fails in
\operatorname{StMod}kG

[
16
]
.

Here is a brief outline of this paper: Sections
2
-
3
provide a summary of basic facts about compactly generated triangulated categories with additional structure given by a linear action of a ring or a tensor product. Finite length objects are discussed in Section
4
. Brown–Comenetz duality, which may be viewed as the tensor triangular analogue of Matlis duality from commutative algebra, is the subject of Section
5
. Sections
6
-
7
are devoted to a notion of local regularity and various descriptions of local dualisable objects. The final Sections
8
-
9
contain our results on the modular representations of finite groups. As is clear from this description, a major part of this manuscript develops general machinery that may be of interest beyond modular representations.

2.
Rappels: cohomology and localisation

In this section we recall certain basic notions and constructions in
triangulated categories. Our main interest is in tensor triangulated
categories, but many of the fundamental constructions do not require
this additional structure, and are more transparent when treated in
greater generality. Primary references for the material presented here
are
[
6
,
9
]
.

Throughout we fix a graded commutative noetherian ring
R
. We consider only homogeneous elements and ideals in

and

we write

Fix a compactly generated triangulated category

; its full subcategory consisting of compact objects is denoted

Central actions

For objects

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(X,Y)\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,\Sigma^{i}Y)\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(X)\coloneqq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(X,X)\,.

Composition makes

\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
, there exists an integer
n
such that

\mathfrak{p}^{n}\cdot\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,-)=0=\mathfrak{p}^{n}\cdot\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,C)\,.

Proof.

The description of the compact objects in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
follows from
[
6
, Theorem 6.4]
; see also
[
7
, Proposition 2.7]
. Cosupport theory shows that
\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}({X}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{p}})={X}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{p}}
for any
object
X
in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
; see
[
9
, §4]
and in particular
[
9
, Lemma 4.12]
. This yields the
equality
{(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T})}^{\mathrm{c}}={(\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T})}^{\mathrm{c}}
, since
\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}
induces an equivalence
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
.
From
[
6
, Lemma 5.11]
it follows that
\mathfrak{p}^{n}\cdot\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C)=0
for
n\gg 0
.
This yields the second assertion for compacts in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
and
\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
.
∎

We also record the following observation, for later use. Given a graded
R
-module
M
, the object
{M}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{a}}
is the Koszul construction on
M
with respect to the ideal
\mathfrak{a}
, where
M
is viewed as an object in the derived category of graded
R
-modules. In particular, each
H^{i}({M}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{a}})
is itself a graded
R
-module.

Lemma 2.8
.

For objects
X,Y
in
\mathcal{T}
and an ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq R
one has

\displaystyle\operatorname{length}_{R}\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,{Y}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{a}})<\infty

\displaystyle\iff\operatorname{length}_{R}H^{*}({\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,Y)}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{a}})<\infty

\displaystyle\iff\operatorname{length}_{R}\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}({X}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{a}},Y)<\infty\,.

Proof.

We verify the first isomorphism; a similar argument gives the second one. It suffices to consider the case when
\mathfrak{a}=(r)
. Set
M=\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,Y)
and let
L
be the submodule of elements annihilated by
r
. From the construction of the Koszul object
{Y}/\!\!/{r}
one gets an exact sequence of
R
-modules

0\longrightarrow M/rM\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,{Y}/\!\!/{r})\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow 0\,.

Thus
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,{Y}/\!\!/{r})
has finite length if and only if both
M/rM
and
L
have finite length.
For the Koszul object
{M}/\!\!/{r}
we have

H^{i}({M}/\!\!/{r})=\begin{cases}L&i=-1\,,\\
M/rM&i=0\,,\\
0&i\neq-1,0\,.\end{cases}

The stated equivalences are now obvious.
∎

3.
Tensor structure and dualisable objects

In this section we recall some basic facts about compactly generated tensor triangulated categories. We pay particular attention to the notion of dualisability; a more detailed discussion can be found in
[
14
]
.

Fix a compactly generated tensor triangulated category
({\mathcal{T}},\otimes,\mathds{1})
, with tensor product
\otimes
and unit
\mathds{1}
. Throughout we assume the tensor product is symmetric. For now
\mathds{1}
need not be compact. Brown representability yields
functions objects

\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(X,Y)
satisfying an adjunction isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(X\otimes Y,Z)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(Y,Z))\quad\text{for all $X,Y,Z$ in ${\mathcal{T}}$.}

The
Spanier–Whitehead dual
of an object
X
is

D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}X\coloneqq\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(X,\mathds{1})\,.

The assignment
X\mapsto D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}X
is a contravariant functor
{\mathcal{T}}\to{\mathcal{T}}
.

Dualisable objects

An object
X
in
{\mathcal{T}}
is said to be
dualisable
if for all

Y
in
{\mathcal{T}}
the natural map

D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}X\otimes Y\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(X,Y)\,,

is an isomorphism.

The adjoint of the composite
X\otimes D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}X\xrightarrow{\gamma}D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}X\otimes X\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}\mathds{1}
of the braiding
\gamma
with the counit
\varepsilon
gives the natural double duality map

\rho\colon X\longrightarrow D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}(X)\,.

The result below is
[
14
, Proposition 2.2]
and collects some useful observations concerning these notions.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X
be an object in
{\mathcal{T}}
. The following statements hold.

(1)

The object
X
is dualisable if and only if there is a map
\eta\colon\mathds{1}\to X\otimes D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}X
making the following diagram commute

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathds{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\otimes D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(X,X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}X\otimes X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}

where the left hand map is the adjoint to the isomorphism
\mathds{1}\otimes X\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}X
.

(2)

If
X
is dualisable so is
D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}X
and
\rho\colon X\to D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}X
is an isomorphism.

(3)

If
X
is dualisable and
C\in{\mathcal{T}}
is compact, then
C\otimes X
is compact. ∎

Central actions

From this point on
({\mathcal{T}},\otimes,\mathds{1})
is a rigidly compactly generated tensor triangulated category, where
rigidity
means that compact and dualisable objects in
\mathcal{T}
coincide. Hence
\mathds{1}
is compact,
{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
is a tensor ideal thick subcategory, and Spanier–Whitehead duality induces an auto-equivalence on it.

Let
R
be a noetherian graded commutative ring that acts
canonically
on
\mathcal{T}
, that is to say, via a map of graded rings
R\to\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(\mathds{1})
.

We begin by recording some special features of the localisation and
completion functors, recalled in
Section
2
, in this context. For
any specialisation closed subset
V\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R
and object

X\in{\mathcal{T}}
there are natural isomorphisms

\varGamma_{V}X\cong\varGamma_{V}\mathds{1}\otimes X\qquad\text{and}\qquad L_{V}X\cong L_{V}\mathds{1}\otimes X\,.

By adjunction, these yield for any
Y\in\mathcal{T}
natural isomorphisms

\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(\varGamma_{V}X,Y)\cong\varLambda^{V}\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(X,Y)\cong\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(X,\varLambda^{V}Y)\,.

See
[
6
, Proposition 8.1]
and

[
9
, Proposition 8.3]
.
In particular, for any
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
and objects
X,Y\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
we have

(3.2)

\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}X,Y)\cong\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(X,Y)\cong\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(X,\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}Y)\,.

Tensor structures of subcategories

We describe the tensor structures of the subcategories
L_{V}\mathcal{T}
and

\varGamma_{V}\mathcal{T}
that are given by a specialisation closed subset

V\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R
. In particular we study their dualisable objects.

The following lemma is about the shadow of Spanier–Whitehead duality in
L_{V}{\mathcal{T}}
,
the subcategory of
{\mathcal{T}}
consisting of
V
-local objects.

Lemma 3.3
.

For each specialisation closed subset
V\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R
one has a commutative diagram of exact functors:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{V}{\mathcal{T}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{V}{\mathcal{T}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(-,\mathds{1})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{V}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(-,L_{V}\mathds{1})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{V}}

Proof.

Fix a compact object
C
in
{\mathcal{T}}
. The following series of isomorphisms verifies the commutativity of the diagram:

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(L_{V}\mathds{1}\otimes C,L_{V}\mathds{1})

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(L_{V}\mathds{1},\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(C,L_{V}\mathds{1}))

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(L_{V}\mathds{1},D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}{C}\otimes L_{V}\mathds{1})

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(L_{V}\mathds{1},L_{V}D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}C)

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(\mathds{1},L_{V}D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}C)

\displaystyle\cong L_{V}D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}C\,.

The second one holds because compact objects are dualisable, by rigidity of
\mathcal{T}
.
∎

The result below builds on Lemma
2.3
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
V\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R
be a specialisation closed subset. The category
L_{V}{\mathcal{T}}
is compactly generated and inherits a tensor product from
\mathcal{T}
. The unit is
L_{V}\mathds{1}
and the function object is the same as for
{\mathcal{T}}
. The compact objects and dualisable objects in
L_{V}{\mathcal{T}}
coincide and are the direct summands of objects in
L_{V}({\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}})
.

Proof.

The claim about compact generation and the description of compact objects is from Lemma
2.3
. For
X,Y
in
\mathcal{T}
the maps
X\to L_{V}X
and
Y\to L_{V}Y
induce isomorphisms

L_{V}(X\otimes Y)\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }L_{V}X\otimes Y\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }L_{V}X\otimes L_{V}Y\,.

It follows that when
X,Y
are
V
-local, so is
X\otimes Y
, and
hence that the tensor product on
\mathcal{T}
restricts to a tensor
product on
L_{V}\mathcal{T}
. Setting
Y=\mathds{1}
one gets also that

L_{V}\mathds{1}
is the unit in
L_{V}\mathcal{T}
. The isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(X,Y))\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(-\otimes X,Y)

shows that
\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(X,Y)
is
V
-local when
Y
is
V
-local. Thus the
function object in
L_{V}{\mathcal{T}}
is the same as the one in

\mathcal{T}
.

Since
L_{V}\mathds{1}
is compact in
L_{V}\mathcal{T}
, dualisable objects are
compact, by Proposition
3.1
. As to the converse, it
suffices to verify that objects of the form
L_{V}C
, with
C
in

{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}
, are dualisable in
L_{V}{\mathcal{T}}
. For any
Y
in

{L_{V}\mathcal{T}}
one has isomorphisms

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(L_{V}C,L_{V}\mathds{1})\otimes Y

\displaystyle\cong L_{V}\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(C,\mathds{1})\otimes Y

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(C,\mathds{1})\otimes Y

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(C,Y)

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(L_{V}C,Y)

where the first isomorphism is by the commutativity of the square in Lemma
3.3
. Thus
L_{V}C
is dualisable.
∎

Next we discuss the analogues of the preceding results for

\varGamma_{V}{\mathcal{T}}
, the subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
consisting of

V
-torsion objects.

Lemma 3.5
.

For each specialisation closed subset
V\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R
one has commutative diagrams of exact functors:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varGamma_{V}{\mathcal{T}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varLambda^{V}{\mathcal{T}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(-,\mathds{1})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varGamma_{V}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(-,\varGamma_{V}\mathds{1})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varLambda^{V}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varLambda^{V}{\mathcal{T}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varGamma_{V}{\mathcal{T}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(-,\mathds{1})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varLambda^{V}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varGamma_{V}\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(-,\varLambda^{V}\mathds{1})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varGamma_{V}}

Proof.

Fix a compact object
C
in
\mathcal{T}
. The commutativity of the square on the left is verified below:

\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(\varGamma_{V}C,\varGamma_{V}\mathds{1})\cong\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(C,\varLambda^{V}\mathds{1})\cong\varLambda^{V}\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(C,\mathds{1})\,.

Next we verify the commutativity of the square on the right:

\displaystyle\varGamma_{V}\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(\varLambda^{V}C,\varLambda^{V}\mathds{1})

\displaystyle\cong\varGamma_{V}\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(C,\varLambda^{V}\mathds{1})

\displaystyle\cong\varGamma_{V}\varLambda^{V}\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(C,\mathds{1})

It follows from the result below that, typically,
\varGamma_{V}{\mathcal{T}}

has more dualisable objects than compact ones.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let

be a specialisation closed subset. The category
\varGamma_{V}{\mathcal{T}}
is
compactly generated and inherits a tensor product from
\mathcal{T}
. The unit is
\varGamma_{V}\mathds{1}
and
the function object is
\varGamma_{V}\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(-,-)
. The compact objects in
\varGamma_{V}\mathcal{T}

{(\varGamma_{V}\mathcal{T})}^{\mathrm{c}}={\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}\cap\varGamma_{V}\mathcal{T}\,,

and these are dualisable. All objects in
\varGamma_{V}{({\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}})}
are dualisable.

Proof.

Compact generation and description of compact objects is from Lemma
2.3
. From the description of
\varGamma_{V}
as a tensor functor it is clear that the tensor product on

\mathcal{T}
restricts to a tensor product on
\varGamma_{V}{\mathcal{T}}
. The
functor
\varGamma_{V}
provides a right adjoint for the inclusion

\varGamma_{V}{\mathcal{T}}\to\mathcal{T}
, and therefore it preserves function objects.

It remains to verify that
\varGamma_{V}C
is dualisable in
\varGamma_{V}{\mathcal{T}}
for any compact object
C
in
\mathcal{T}
. For any

in
\varGamma_{V}{\mathcal{T}}
one gets

\displaystyle\varGamma_{V}\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(\varGamma_{V}C,\varGamma_{V}\mathds{1})\otimes Y

\displaystyle\cong\varGamma_{V}\varLambda^{V}D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}{(C)}\otimes Y

\displaystyle\cong\varGamma_{V}D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}{(C)}\otimes Y

\displaystyle\cong\varGamma_{V}(D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}{(C)}\otimes Y)

\displaystyle\cong\varGamma_{V}\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(C,Y)

\displaystyle\cong\varGamma_{V}\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(\varGamma_{V}C,Y)

where the first isomorphism follows from the commutativity of the second square in Lemma

.
Thus
\varGamma_{V}C
is dualisable.
∎

Next we focus on

in
\operatorname{Spec}R
. The Spanier–Whitehead dual in the category

\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(-,\mathds{1}_{\mathfrak{p}})=\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(-,\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathds{1})\,.

Since

, from Propositions

and

we get the result below.

Corollary 3.7
.

For each

in
\operatorname{Spec}R
the category

is

. The

.

.

For each
\mathfrak{p}

4.
Finite length objects

Let
R
be a graded commutative noetherian ring and
\mathcal{T}
a compactly generated
R
-linear triangulated category; it need not be tensor triangulated. In what follows we consider various types of finiteness conditions for objects in
\mathcal{T}
and its various subcategories.
Here are the relevant concepts.

Definition 4.1
.

An object
X
in
\mathcal{T}
is said to be

(1)

noetherian
over
R
if the
R
-module
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)
is noetherian for all
C\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}
,

(2)

artinian
over
R
if the
R
-module
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)
is artinian for all
C\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}
,

(3)

of finite length
over
R
if
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)
is of finite length over
R
for all
C\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}
.

For tensor triangulated categories there is a more intrinsic notion of a finite length object.

Remark 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{T}=(\mathcal{T},\otimes,\mathds{1})
be tensor triangulated and suppose it admits an
R
-action via a map of graded rings
R\to S=\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(\mathds{1})
. Then we say that an object
X
in
\mathcal{T}
is
of finite length
if
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)
is of finite length over
S
for all
C\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}
. This condition coincides with the one over
R
when, for example,
S
is finite over
R
or when
R
is local and
S
is its completion at the maximal ideal.

Remark 4.3
.

The above finiteness conditions on graded morphisms imply the analogous conditions for the degree zero morphisms, as a module over
R^{0}
, because for any objects
X,Y
in
\mathcal{T}
and
M=\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,Y)
, the poset of submodules of the
R^{0}
-module
M^{0}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,Y)
embeds into the poset of submodules of the graded
R
-module
M
. The map sends
U\subseteq M^{0}
to
RU
, and its inverse sends
V\subseteq M
to
V^{0}
.

We proceed with a characterisation of the finite length objects in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

For
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
and
X\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
the conditions below are equivalent.

(1)

X
is in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
and of finite length over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
;

(2)

X
is artinian over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
;

(3)

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)
is artinian over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
for each compact object
C
in
{\mathcal{T}}
.

Proof.

By Lemmas
2.3
and
2.5
we can pass to
\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
and suppose
R
is local with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
. In particular, (2) and (3) are equivalent.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2): Let
C
be a compact object in
\mathcal{T}
and fix
a sequence of elements
\boldsymbol{r}\coloneqq r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}
which
generates the ideal
\mathfrak{p}
. Then
{C}/\!\!/{\boldsymbol{r}}
is
\mathfrak{p}
-torsion and
hence in
{(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T})}^{\mathrm{c}}
. The hypothesis yields that the

R
-module
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}({C}/\!\!/{\boldsymbol{r}},X)
has finite length, and
in particular it is artinian. Since
{C}/\!\!/{\boldsymbol{r}}
is obtained from

C
by an iterated Koszul construction, it suffices to prove that
for any

and compact object

in
\mathcal{T}
, if the

R
-module
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}({C}/\!\!/{r},X)
is artinian, then so is

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(C,X)
.

By definition of
{C}/\!\!/{r}
one has an exact triangle
C\xrightarrow{r}\Sigma^{|r|}C\to{C}/\!\!/{r}\to
and that induces an exact sequence of
R
-modules

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}({C}/\!\!/{r},X)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*-|r|}(C,X)\xrightarrow{\ r\ }\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(C,X)\,.

The module
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(C,X)
is
\mathfrak{p}
-torsion, since

is in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
. As
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}({C}/\!\!/{r},X)
is artinian, Lemma
4.5
below yields that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(C,X)
is also artinian.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1): Artinian

-modules are
\mathfrak{p}
-torsion, so the hypothesis implies

is in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
. Any compact object

in

is also compact in

, so the hypothesis implies that the

-module
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)
is artinian; it is also annihilated by a power of
\mathfrak{p}
, by Lemma
2.7
. Thus it has finite length.
∎

The following observation was used in the argument above.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
R
be a local ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
and
M
an

\mathfrak{m}
-torsion
R
-module. If for an
r\in\mathfrak{m}
the
R
-module

\operatorname{Ker}(M\xrightarrow{r}M)
is artinian, then so is
M
.

Proof.

Since
M
is
\mathfrak{m}
-torsion its injective hull is a direct sum of copies of twists of
I(\mathfrak{m})
, the number of copies being equal to the length of
\operatorname{Soc}M
, the socle of
M
. The module
I(\mathfrak{m})
is
artinian by Matlis duality, so it suffices to verify that
\operatorname{Soc}M
is finitely generated.

Set
L\coloneqq\operatorname{Ker}(M\xrightarrow{r}M)
and consider the exact sequence of
R
-modules

0\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow M\xrightarrow{\ r\ }M\,.

Applying
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m},-)
to it yields the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Soc}L\longrightarrow\operatorname{Soc}M\xrightarrow{\ r\ }\operatorname{Soc}M

and the right hand map is zero. Thus
\operatorname{Soc}M
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{Soc}L
and therefore finitely
generated, because
L
is artinian.
∎

Minimal primes

Let
\operatorname{supp}_{R}\mathcal{T}
denote the
support
of
\mathcal{T}
, the set of points
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R
such that
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}X\neq 0
for some
X\in\mathcal{T}
.
The result below augments Proposition
4.4
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Suppose
\mathcal{T}
is noetherian and fix
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{supp}_{R}\mathcal{T}
. Then
\mathfrak{p}
is minimal in
\operatorname{supp}_{R}{\mathcal{T}}
if and only if in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
artinian and finite length objects over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
coincide.

Proof.

First observe that
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{supp}_{R}\mathcal{T}
is minimal if and only if
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. From this it follows with Proposition
4.4
that artinian and finite length coincide for the
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(C,X)
when
\mathfrak{p}
is minimal.

As to the converse, we use the object
T_{\mathfrak{p}}C
in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
which is defined below via (
5.2
). In particular, we have an isomorphism of
R
-modules

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{R}(\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C),I(\mathfrak{p}))\cong\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,T_{\mathfrak{p}}C)\,.

Since
\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C)
is noetherian,
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,T_{\mathfrak{p}}C)
is artinian. Moreover one of them has finite length if and only if the other does, by Matlis duality. This means that any compact object
C
in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
belongs to
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
. Thus
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and therefore
\mathfrak{p}
is minimal by our first observation.
∎

Pure-injectivity

Artinian objects enjoy further finiteness properties. To explain this we recall the notion of purity for compactly generated triangulated categories; see
[
30
, §1]
. A triangle
X\to Y\to Z\to
is
pure-exact
if for each compact object
C
the induced sequence

0\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,Y)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,Z)\to 0

is exact. In that case
X\to Y
is a
pure monomorphism
. One calls an object
X

pure-injective
if each pure monomorphism
X\to Y
splits, and
X
is
\Sigma
-pure-injective
if every coproduct of copies of
X
is pure-injective.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a compactly generated
R
-linear triangulated category and
X\in\mathcal{T}
artinian over
R
. Then
X
is
\Sigma
-pure-injective and decomposes into a coproduct of indecomposable objects with local endomorphism rings.

Proof.

Let
C\in\mathcal{T}
be compact. A subgroup of
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)
is said to be
of finite definition
if it is the image of a map
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(C^{\prime},X)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)
that is induced by a morphism
C\to C^{\prime}
between compact objects.
In
[
32
, Theorem 4.10]
it is proved that when the descending chain condition on subgroups of finite definition holds for all compact
C
in
\mathcal{T}
, the object
X
is
\Sigma
-pure-injective and admits a decomposition into indecomposable objects with local endomorphism rings. From this the assertion about artinian objects follows, using Remark
4.3
.
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{T}
admits a compact generator. Then the category of objects in
\mathcal{T}
that are artinian over
R
is a Krull–Schmidt category.

Proof.

Fix a compact generator
C
. We have already seen that any artinian object has a decomposition
X=\coprod_{i\in I}X_{i}
into indecomposables. We need to show that
I
is finite, but this is clear from the isomorphism
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)\cong\coprod_{i\in I}\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X_{i})
since this module is artinian.
∎

5.
Brown–Comenetz duality

In this section we explain how classical Matlis duality from commutative algebra, between artinian modules and noetherian modules over complete local rings lifts to the world of tensor triangulated categories, leading to Brown–Comenetz duality.
The motivation for this is our interest in the category of finite length objects in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
. Three other categories arise naturally in their study: artinian objects in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
, noetherian objects in
\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
, and a certain category of Matlis lifts of compacts objects, introduced below. These categories are related via Brown–Comenetz duality; see for instance Corollary
5.9
.

To begin with we record basic results on Matlis duality. Since this is a local construction, throughout this section we fix a graded commutative noetherian ring
R
and
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R
. We write
I(\mathfrak{p})
for the injective hull of
R/\mathfrak{p}
and, for any
R
-module
M
, write
M{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}
for the completion of
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
with respect to
\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
. We remind the reader that these constructions take place in the world of graded rings and modules.

Matlis duality

On the category of
R
-modules
Matlis duality at
\mathfrak{p}
is the functor

M\longmapsto D_{\mathfrak{p}}M\coloneqq\operatorname{Hom}_{R}^{*}(M,I(\mathfrak{p}))\,.

There is a canonical biduality map
M\to D_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}M
; it is a monomorphism and we say
M
is
Matlis reflexive
when it is an isomorphism. For
M=R
the map induces an isomorphism
R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{End}_{R}^{*}(I(\mathfrak{p}))
; this is part of Matlis duality which is given below. Therefore the Matlis dual
D_{\mathfrak{p}}M
admits a canonical
R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-action.

For any pair of
R
-modules
M,N
there are natural isomorphisms of
R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-modules

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{R}(M,D_{\mathfrak{p}}N)\cong\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N,I(\mathfrak{p}))\cong\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{R}(N,D_{\mathfrak{p}}M)\,.

Let
\operatorname{art}R
and
\operatorname{noeth}R
denote the categories of
R
-modules that are artinian and noetherian, respectively.
The result below is due to Matlis
[
34
, §4]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
R
be a graded commutative noetherian ring and
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
.
Then the following functors are mutually quasi-inverse contravariant equivalences:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{art}R_{\mathfrak{p}}=\operatorname{art}R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{noeth}R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}}(-,I(\mathfrak{p}))=\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{R}(-,I(\mathfrak{p}))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}}(-,I(\mathfrak{p}))}

Moreover, for a finitely generated
R
-module
M
the canonical map
M\to D_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}M
induces an isomorphism
M{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}D_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}M
.

These constructions and results carry over to the world of triangulated categories.

Matlis lifts

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a compactly generated
R
-linear triangulated category; it need not be tensor triangulated. For each object
C
in
{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}
Brown representability yields an object
T_{\mathfrak{p}}C
in
{\mathcal{T}}
such that

(5.2)

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,-),I(\mathfrak{p}))\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,T_{\mathfrak{p}}C)\,.

One can think of
T_{\mathfrak{p}}C
as a
Matlis lift
of
I(\mathfrak{p})
to
\mathcal{T}
, with respect to
C
. The assignment
C\mapsto T_{\mathfrak{p}}C
yields a functor
{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}\to{\mathcal{T}}
, and it follows from Lemma
2.5
that
T_{\mathfrak{p}}
admits a canonical factorisation

{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\to\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}\rightarrowtail\mathcal{T}\,.

Matlis duality implies that when
\mathcal{T}
is noetherian,
T_{\mathfrak{p}}C
is an artinian object in the
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-linear triangulated category
\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and belongs therefore to
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
; see also Lemma
6.2
. For tensor triangulated categories, one can lift Matlis duality to the level of objects, and not just on their cohomology. This is explained below.

Local Brown–Comenetz duality

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a rigidly compactly generated tensor triangulated category. We fix a graded commutative noetherian ring
R
and a canonical
R
-action on
\mathcal{T}
.

For an object
X\in\mathcal{T}
Brown representability yields an object
D_{\mathfrak{p}}{X}
in
{\mathcal{T}}
such that

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathds{1},X\otimes-),I(\mathfrak{p}))\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,D_{\mathfrak{p}}X).

This yields a contravariant functor
D_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon{\mathcal{T}}\to{\mathcal{T}}
called
local Brown–Comenetz duality
. The same notation
D_{\mathfrak{p}}
is used for modules and for objects in a triangulated category, but there is no danger of confusion.

In the following we collect some basic properties which will be used throughout, sometimes without any further reference.

Lemma 5.3
.

For
C\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}
and
X\in\mathcal{T}
there are natural isomorphism of
R
-modules

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(C,D_{\mathfrak{p}}X)\cong D_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}C,X)\,.

Proof.

The isomorphism is immediate from the defining isomorphism of
D_{\mathfrak{p}}X
, using the dualisablity of
C
.
∎

For any pair of objects
X,Y
in
\mathcal{T}
there is a natural isomorphism of
R
-modules

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,D_{\mathfrak{p}}Y)\cong\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(Y,D_{\mathfrak{p}}X)

which means that
D_{\mathfrak{p}}
yields an adjoint pair of functors
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{op}}\rightleftarrows\mathcal{T}
. Also, it is easy to verify that there is a natural isomorphism

D_{\mathfrak{p}}X\cong\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(X,D_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathds{1})\,.

Therefore
D_{\mathfrak{p}}
restricts to a functor
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}\to\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
, thanks to (
3.2
). For any compact (equivalently, dualisable) object
C
, one has that

D_{\mathfrak{p}}C\cong D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}C\otimes D_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathds{1}\,.

The following observation connects Matlis lifts with Brown–Comenetz duality.

Lemma 5.4
.

For any compact object
C
in
\mathcal{T}
there are natural isomorphisms

T_{\mathfrak{p}}C\cong C\otimes T_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathds{1}\cong D_{\mathfrak{p}}(D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}C)\,.

Proof.

Since
T_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathds{1}\cong D_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathds{1}
, from definitions, one gets the isomorphism on the right. The one on the left holds because for each
X\in\mathcal{T}
there are natural isomorphisms:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,T_{\mathfrak{p}}C)

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X),I(\mathfrak{p}))

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathds{1},D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}C\otimes X),I(\mathfrak{p}))

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}C\otimes X,T_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathds{1})

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,C\otimes T_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathds{1})\,.

The second and last isomorphisms hold because
C
is dualisable.
∎

Completed linear actions

We wish to establish a Matlis duality theorem for
\mathcal{T}
and need to assume that
\mathcal{T}
is noetherian over
R
. Recall that this means compact objects are noetherian: the
R
-module
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,D)
is finitely generated for all
C,D\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}
. The first step is to show that the
\mathfrak{p}
-torsion category and the
\mathfrak{p}
-complete category admit
R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-linear actions that extend the
R
-linear actions.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be noetherian.
For a compact object
X\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
the canonical morphism
X\to D_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}X
induces an isomorphism
\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}X\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}D_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}X
.

Proof.

We may assume that
R
is local with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
. Let
C\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}
. Since
\mathcal{T}
is noetherian over
R
, the
R
-module
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}C,X)
is finitely generated. Hence, the
R
-module
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,D_{\mathfrak{p}}X)\cong D_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}C,X)
is artinian. In particular
D_{\mathfrak{p}}X
is
\mathfrak{p}
-torsion, and hence
D_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}X\cong\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(D_{\mathfrak{p}}X,D_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathds{1})
is
\mathfrak{p}
-complete, by (
3.2
).

It follows that the canonical morphism
X\to D_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}X
factors through
X\to\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}X
.
For
\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}X\to D_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}X
to be an isomorphism, it suffices to show that the induced map

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}({C}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{p}},\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}X)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}({C}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{p}},D_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}X)

is a bijection; see Lemma
2.7
. For the same reason the induced map

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}({C}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{p}},X)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}({C}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{p}},\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}X)

is a bijection. But
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}({C}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{p}},X)
is of finite length over
R
, so it is Matlis reflexive.
This yields the first isomorphism below

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}({C}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{p}},X)\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}D_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}({C}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{p}},X)\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}({C}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{p}},D_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}X)

and the second is from Lemma
5.3
.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

The tensor triangulated categories
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
and
\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
have canonical
R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-linear actions, extending the
R
-action inherited from
\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

For each
X\in\mathcal{T}
there is a sequence of homomorphisms

R\to R_{\mathfrak{p}}\to R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{End}_{R}^{*}(I(\mathfrak{p}))\to\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(D_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathds{1})\to\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(D_{\mathfrak{p}}X)\,,

where the isomorphism is from Matlis duality. This yields a factorisation of the canonical homomorphism
R\to\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(D_{\mathfrak{p}}X)
.
We have
\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathds{1}_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong D_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}\mathds{1}_{\mathfrak{p}}
by Lemma
5.5
, and this gives the factorisation of
R\to\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathds{1}_{\mathfrak{p}})

by taking
X=D_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathds{1}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. This establishes the canonical
R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-linear action for
\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
, since
\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathds{1}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the tensor identity. The equivalence
\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
from (
2.6
) preserves the tensor identity, since
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathds{1}_{\mathfrak{p}})\cong\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathds{1}
, and this yields the assertion for
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
.
∎

Duality over the completion

In the following we restrict to
R
-modules and objects in
\mathcal{T}
that admit an
R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-action. Then we consider
the following modification of the Matlis duality:

M\longmapsto D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}M\coloneqq\operatorname{Hom}_{R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{*}(M,I(\mathfrak{p}))

Note that there is a natural morphism
D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}M\to D_{\mathfrak{p}}M
. This is an isomorphism when
M
is
\mathfrak{p}
-torsion, because the class of
R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-modules
M
such that this is an isomorphism includes the finite length modules and is closed under colimits, so it includes all
\mathfrak{p}
-torsion modules. There is also a canonical monomorphism
M\to(D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}})^{2}M
, which is an isomorphism when
M
is artinian or noetherian.

Analogously, for any object
X\in\mathcal{T}
such that the canonical
R
-action on
\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X)
factors through
R\to R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, Brown representability yields an object
D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}X
in
{\mathcal{T}}
with a natural isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathds{1},X\otimes-),I(\mathfrak{p}))\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(-,D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}X).

The asssignment
X\mapsto D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}X
yields a pair of contravariant functors
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}
and
\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}
, equipped with natural transformations
D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}\to D_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Lemma 5.7
.

For
X\in\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
the morphism
D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}X\to D_{\mathfrak{p}}X
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

It suffices to show that the induced map
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(C,D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}X)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(C,D_{\mathfrak{p}}X)
is an isomorphism for all compact
C\in\mathcal{T}
. But this follows from Lemma
5.3
and its analogue for
D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, since
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(C,X)
is
\mathfrak{p}
-torsion.
∎

We formulate an analogue of Matlis duality for modules. To this end, consider the following subcategories of
\mathcal{T}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{X\in\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}\mid\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)\in\operatorname{art}R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{ for all }C\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{X\in\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}\mid\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)\in\operatorname{noeth}R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{ for all }C\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}\}

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a rigidly compactly generated tensor triangulated category with a canonical
R
-action such that
\mathcal{T}
is noetherian over
R
. Fix
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R
. Then there are mutually quasi-inverse contravariant equivalences:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}=D_{\mathfrak{p}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}}

Moreover

{(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T})}^{\mathrm{c}}={(\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T})}^{\mathrm{c}}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}\,,

and there are equalities

(D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}})^{-1}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{p}})\cap\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad(D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}})^{-1}(\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{p}})\cap\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}\,.

Proof.

We apply the analogue of Lemma
5.3
for
D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and use the Matlis duality from Proposition
5.1
. In particular, for objects with an
R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-action, the assignment
X\mapsto D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}X
sends artinian to noetherian objects, and vice versa.
Any artinian
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module is
\mathfrak{p}
-torsion, so
D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}X\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}
for
X\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
On the other hand, for
X\in\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
there are isomorphisms

D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}X\cong D_{\mathfrak{p}}X\cong\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(X,D_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathds{1})\,,

and this lies in
\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
. Thus
D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}X\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}
for
X\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
For a compact
C\in\mathcal{T}
there is a natural isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(C,(D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}})^{2}X)\cong(D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}})^{2}\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)\,.

Thus the canonical morphism
X\to(D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}})^{2}X
is an isomorphism when
X
is in
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}
or in
\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. This gives the desired pair of equivalences.

For the statement about the categories of compacts, we recall from Lemma
2.7
that
{(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T})}^{\mathrm{c}}={(\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T})}^{\mathrm{c}}
, and
{(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T})}^{\mathrm{c}}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}
holds since
\mathcal{T}
is noetherian. For the final pair of equalities, let
X\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
such that
D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}X
is in
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Then
(D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}})^{2}X
is in
\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. The map
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)\to\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,(D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}})^{2}X)
is a monomorphism for all compact
C
, again by Lemma
5.3
. Thus
X
is in
\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. The other inclusion
(D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}})^{-1}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{p}})\cap\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}\supseteq\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is clear. The proof of the equality for

is analogous.
∎

.

For

Noetherian objects

The result below, which is an analogue of Proposition
4.4
, characterises objects in the local category
\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
with noetherian cohomology.

Proposition 5.10
.

Fix
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
. An object
X\in\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
is in
\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}
if and only if
X
has finite length over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Proof.

The module
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)
is
\mathfrak{p}
-torsion by Lemma
2.7
, and therefore the forward implication is clear.

Suppose the
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)
has finite length for each compact object
C
in
\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{T}}
. Then Lemma
5.3
implies that
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,D_{\mathfrak{p}}X)
has finite length over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
for each compact object
C
in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
. Thus Proposition
4.4
yields that the object
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}D_{\mathfrak{p}}X
is artinian over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Then its Brown–Comenetz dual is in
\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, by Theorem
5.8
.

The natural map
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}D_{\mathfrak{p}}X\to D_{\mathfrak{p}}X
induces the map on the right below:

X\longrightarrow D_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}X\longrightarrow D_{\mathfrak{p}}{\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}D_{\mathfrak{p}}X}\,.

The one of the left is the biduality map. So it suffices to verify that the composite
X\to D_{\mathfrak{p}}{\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}D_{\mathfrak{p}}X}
is an isomorphism.
Since source and target are in
\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
, it suffices to verify that the induced map

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(C,X)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(C,D_{\mathfrak{p}}{\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}D_{\mathfrak{p}}X})

is an isomorphism for each compact object
C
in
\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
. It remains to observe that one has isomorphisms

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(C,D_{\mathfrak{p}}{\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}D_{\mathfrak{p}}X})

\displaystyle\cong D_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}C,\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}D_{\mathfrak{p}}X)

\displaystyle\cong D_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}C,D_{\mathfrak{p}}X)

\displaystyle\cong D_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(C,X)

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(C,X)\,.

The first and the third isomorphism follow from Lemma
5.3
. The second isomorphism uses that
D^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{SW}}}C
is in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
, and the last one holds because the
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(C,X)
has finite length.
∎

6.
Strong generation

We introduce a notion of local regularity for compactly generated triangulated categories which builds on the notion of strong generation. A key consequence of this property is a strong finiteness condition for the class of artinian objects, analogous to finite global dimension in the setting of abelian categories. We begin by recalling the notion of a strong generator from
[
17
,
41
]
.

Strong generation

Fix a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
with suspension
\Sigma\colon\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathcal{T}
. For an object
X
in
\mathcal{T}
and
n\geq 0
set

\operatorname{thick}^{n}(X)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\{0\}&n=0,\\
\operatorname{add}\{\Sigma^{i}X\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\}&n=1,\\
\operatorname{add}\{\operatorname{cone}\phi\mid\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(\operatorname{thick}^{1}(X),\operatorname{thick}^{n-1}(X))&n>1,\end{cases}

where
\operatorname{add}\mathcal{X}
denotes the smallest full subcategory containing
\mathcal{X}
that is closed under finite direct sums and direct summands. An object
X
is a
strong generator
for
\mathcal{T}
if
\mathcal{T}=\operatorname{thick}^{n}(X)
for some integer
n\geq 0
. The category
\mathcal{T}
is
strongly generated
if it admits a strong generator; in this case any object
X
satisfying
\operatorname{thick}(X)=\mathcal{T}
is a strong generator.

Example 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category with an injective cogenerator
I
such that every object embeds into a finite direct sum of copies of
I
. Viewing
I
as a complex concentrated in degree zero, the equality
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A})=\operatorname{thick}(I)
holds if and only if each object in
\mathcal{A}
has finite injective dimension. Moreover,
\operatorname{thick}(I)
is strongly generated if and only if
\mathcal{A}
has finite global dimension. This is a consequence of the ghost lemma, cf.
[
31
, Lemma 2.4]
.

There is also a dual statement when
\mathcal{A}
has a projective generator. It implies, for example, that the category of perfect complexes over a local noetherian ring admits a strong generator if and only if the ring is regular; see
[
41
, Proposition 7.25]
.

Artinian objects

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a compactly generated
R
-linear triangulated category. The subcategory of objects that are artinian over
R
is denoted
\operatorname{art}_{R}\mathcal{T}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

When the
R
-linear category
\mathcal{T}
is noetherian one has inclusions

\displaystyle\operatorname{thick}\{\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}C\mid C\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}},\,\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Spec}R\text{ maximal}\}\subseteq\operatorname{art}_{R}\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle\operatorname{thick}\{T_{\mathfrak{m}}C\mid C\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}},\,\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Spec}R\text{ maximal}\}\subseteq\operatorname{art}_{R}\mathcal{T}\,.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{art}_{R}\mathcal{T}
is a thick subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
, it suffices to verify that it contains
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}C
for
\mathfrak{m}
maximal and
C
compact.
Using Proposition
4.4
it suffices to show that the
R_{\mathfrak{m}}
-module
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}}(X,\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}C)
has finite length for all
X\in{(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}\mathcal{T})}^{\mathrm{c}}
. We have
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}}(X,\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}C)\cong\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}}(X,C_{\mathfrak{m}})
for such
X
. This module is noetherian and
\mathfrak{m}
-torsion, so of finite length.
For the other inclusion, one uses the isomorphism defining
T_{\mathfrak{m}}C
. Matlis duality yields that
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}^{*}(-,I(\mathfrak{m}))
takes finitely generated modules to artinian modules, since
I(\mathfrak{m})
is artinian.
∎

Definition 6.3
.

We say that
\mathcal{T}
is
locally regular over
R
if it is noetherian and has a compact generator
G
such that
\operatorname{thick}(T_{\mathfrak{p}}G)
is strongly generated for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
.

Local regularity has useful consequences for the class of artinian objects. In particular,
\operatorname{thick}(T_{\mathfrak{p}}G)
does not depend on the choice of the compact generator
G
.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
R
be a graded local commutative noetherian ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
, and
\mathcal{T}
a compactly generated
R
-linear triangulated category. Assume
\mathcal{T}
is noetherian and has a compact generator
G
. If
\operatorname{thick}(T_{\mathfrak{m}}G)
is strongly generated, then
\operatorname{thick}(T_{\mathfrak{m}}G)=\operatorname{art}_{R}\mathcal{T}
. If in addition
\operatorname{thick}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}G)
is strongly generated, then

\operatorname{thick}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}G)=\operatorname{art}_{R}\mathcal{T}=\operatorname{thick}(T_{\mathfrak{m}}G)\,.

Proof.

We write
R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{m}}
for the completion of
R
at
\mathfrak{m}
and note that
\operatorname{End}^{*}_{R}(I(\mathfrak{m}))\cong R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{m}}
. Set
\mathcal{I}=\operatorname{thick}(T_{\mathfrak{m}}G)
and fix
X\in\operatorname{art}_{R}\mathcal{T}
.
Then
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,Y)
is naturally a finitely generated graded
R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{m}}
-module for all
Y\in\mathcal{I}
. This yields a cohomological functor

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,-)|_{\mathcal{I}}\colon\mathcal{I}\longrightarrow\operatorname{noeth}R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{m}}\,.

Since
\mathcal{I}
is strongly generated, a variation of Brown representability due to Rouquier
[
41
]
and extended by Letz to the graded setting in
[
33
, Theorem 2.7]
, implies that this functor is graded representable. Let
X^{\prime}
denote the representing object. By Yoneda’s lemma, an isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{I}}(X^{\prime},-)\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,-)|_{\mathcal{I}}

induces a morphism
X\to X^{\prime}
, which is an isomorphism since
T_{\mathfrak{m}}G
is a cogenerator for
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}\mathcal{T}
.
Thus
X
belongs to
\mathcal{I}
.

The proof for
\mathcal{J}=\operatorname{thick}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}G)
is analogous, using cohomological functors
\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\operatorname{noeth}R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{m}}
. Note that
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,Y)
is naturally a finitely generated graded
R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{m}}
-module for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{J}
since
\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{I}
.
∎

Hom-symmetry

The following symmetry statement is needed later. It assumes a weak Gorenstein property that holds when
\mathcal{T}
is a locally regular tensor triangulated category; see Lemma
7.2
and Proposition
6.4

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
and assume

\operatorname{thick}\{\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}C\mid C\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}\}=\operatorname{thick}\{T_{\mathfrak{p}}C\mid C\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}\}\,.

Then the following holds for each
X\in\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
.

(1)

The
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)
has finite length for each
C\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}
if and only if
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,C)
has finite length for each
C\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}
.

(2)

The
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)
has finite length for each
C\in{(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T})}^{\mathrm{c}}
if and only if
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,C)
has finite length for each
C\in{(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T})}^{\mathrm{c}}
.

Proof.

(1) The
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)
has finite length if and only if

D_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)\cong\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,T_{\mathfrak{p}}C)

has finite length. The assumption on
\mathcal{T}
implies that it is equivalent to the finiteness of the length of

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}C)\cong\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,C)\,.

(2) One has
{(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T})}^{\mathrm{c}}=\operatorname{thick}\{{C_{\mathfrak{p}}}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{p}}\mid C\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}\}
, by Lemma
2.7
. It then follows from Lemma
2.8
that the
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)
has finite length for each
C\in{(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T})}^{\mathrm{c}}
if and only if

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,{X}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{p}})
has finite length for each
C\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}
.
The same argument applies to
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,C)
. So we have reduced to the statement of part (1).
∎

Descent

Let
f\colon\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{U}
be an exact functor between
R
-linear triangulated categories. We say that
f
is
R
-linear
if for each object
X
in
\mathcal{T}
the canonical map

\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X)\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{U}}(fX)

is
R
-linear. An equivalent condition is that these are maps of
R
-algebras.

In the following we study adjoint pairs
(f,g)
of functors between compactly generated triangulated categories. We make frequent use of the fact that
f
preserves compacts if and only if
g
preserves coproducts.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
f\colon\mathcal{T}\rightleftarrows\mathcal{U}\penalty\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\mskip 2.0mug
be an adjoint pair of
R
-linear exact functors
between compactly generated
R
-linear triangulated categories such that both functors preserve products and coproducts. Suppose there exists an integer
d
such that for each
X\in\mathcal{T}
one has
X\in\operatorname{thick}^{d}(gfX)
. If
\mathcal{U}
is locally regular over
R
, then so is
\mathcal{T}
.

A sufficient condition for
(f,g)
to preserve products and coproducts is the existence of an equivalence
u\colon\mathcal{U}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathcal{U}
such that
(g,uf)
is an adjoint pair, because then the pair
(gu,f)
is adjoint.

Proof.

First observe that
f
admits a left adjoint
e\colon\mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{T}
that preserves compacts. This follows from Brown representability since
f
preserves products and coproducts. Also,
f
preserves compacts since
g
preserves coproducts.

Assume
\mathcal{U}
is noetherian over
R
. By adjunction, the
R
-module
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,gfD)
is noetherian for each pair of compact objects
C,D
in
\mathcal{T}
. Since
D
is in
\operatorname{thick}(gfD)
we conclude that that
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,D)
is noetherian. Thus
\mathcal{T}
is noetherian over
R
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{U}
is locally regular with compact generator
G
. Fix
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
. There exists an integer
n
such that

\operatorname{thick}^{n}(T_{\mathfrak{p}}G)=\operatorname{thick}(T_{\mathfrak{p}}G)=\operatorname{art}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{p}}\,,

where the first equality is from the definition of local regularity and the second one is by Propostion
6.4
. We claim

(1)

eG
is a compact generator for
\mathcal{T}
,

(2)

g(T_{\mathfrak{p}}G)\cong T_{\mathfrak{p}}(eG)
, and

(3)

\operatorname{thick}^{d+n}(T_{\mathfrak{p}}(eG))=\operatorname{thick}(T_{\mathfrak{p}}(eG))
,

which would prove that
\mathcal{T}
is locally regular over
R
, as desired.

Indeed, (1) holds because
e
preserves compactness and for each
X\in\mathcal{T}
one has
an isomorphism of
R
-modules

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{U}}(G,fX)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(eG,X)\,.

This isomorphism also yields isomorphisms

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,g(T_{\mathfrak{p}}G))

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{U}}(fX,T_{\mathfrak{p}}G)

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{U}}(G,fX),I(\mathfrak{p}))

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(eG,X),I(\mathfrak{p}))\,.

Therefore
g(T_{\mathfrak{p}}G)\cong T_{\mathfrak{p}}(eG)
holds, which justifies (2).

(3) Given Lemma
6.2
it suffices to prove that each artinian object
X
in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is in
\operatorname{thick}^{d+n}(T_{\mathfrak{p}}(eG))
. It is easy to verify that
fX
is in

\operatorname{art}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and hence in
\operatorname{thick}^{n}(T_{\mathfrak{p}}G)
. Thus we get that

X\in\operatorname{thick}^{d}(gfX)\subseteq\operatorname{thick}^{d+n}(g(T_{\mathfrak{p}}G))=\operatorname{thick}^{d+n}(T_{\mathfrak{p}}(eG))\,,

where the equality is by (2). This justifies (3).
∎

Change of rings

Let
\varphi\colon R\to S
be a finite morphism of graded commutative noetherian rings. The following lemma shows that local regularity is preserved when the linear action on a triangulated category is changed via
\varphi
.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a compactly generated
S
-linear triangulated category. Then one has
\operatorname{art}_{R}\mathcal{T}=\operatorname{art}_{S}\mathcal{T}
, and for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
and compact object
C
in
\mathcal{T}
there is an isomorphism

T_{\mathfrak{p}}C\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Spec}S\\
\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{q})=\mathfrak{p}\end{subarray}}T_{\mathfrak{q}}C\,.

Moreover
\mathcal{T}
is locally regular over
R
if and only if the same holds over
S
.

Proof.

The first assertion is clear, because an
S
-module is artinian if and only if it is artinian when viewed as an
R
-module via restriction of scalars along
\varphi
.
One has an isomorphism of
S
-modules

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(S,I(\mathfrak{p}))\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Spec}S\\
\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{q})=\mathfrak{p}\end{subarray}}I(\mathfrak{q})\,.

See, for instance,
[
40
, Theorem 1.1]
. Combining it with the adjunction isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,-),I(\mathfrak{p}))\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{S}(\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,-),\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(S,I(\mathfrak{p})))

and the defining isomorphism of
T_{\mathfrak{p}}C
, yields the isomorphism concerning
T_{\mathfrak{p}}C
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
. We localise at
\mathfrak{p}
and may assume
R
is local with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
. Consider the induced map
\operatorname{Spec}S\to\operatorname{Spec}R
. Since
R\to S
is finite, the fibre over
\mathfrak{p}
consists of a finite collection of maximal ideals, say
\mathfrak{q}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{q}_{r}
. It follows that any object
X
in
\mathcal{T}
admits a functorial decomposition

\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}X=\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}_{1}}X\oplus\cdots\oplus\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}_{r}}X\,.

In particular, any exact triangle supported at
\mathfrak{p}
decomposes into a direct sum of exact triangles supported at the
\mathfrak{q}_{i}
.
When applied to
T_{\mathfrak{p}}C
for a compact object
C
, one obtains the above decomposition
T_{\mathfrak{p}}C\cong T_{\mathfrak{q}_{1}}C\oplus\cdots\oplus T_{\mathfrak{q}_{r}}C
. Here, one uses that
T_{\mathfrak{p}}C
is in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
, so
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}T_{\mathfrak{p}}=T_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Now suppose
\mathcal{T}
is locally regular over
S
, with compact generator
G
. This yields integers
n_{i}
such that

\operatorname{thick}^{n_{i}}(T_{\mathfrak{q}_{i}}G)=\operatorname{thick}(T_{\mathfrak{q}_{i}}G)\qquad(i=1,\ldots,r)\,.

Using the above decomposition
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}=\bigoplus_{i}\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}_{i}}
, each object in
\operatorname{thick}(T_{\mathfrak{p}}G)
decomposes into objects from the categories
\operatorname{thick}(T_{\mathfrak{q}_{i}}G)
.
Hence
\operatorname{thick}^{n}(T_{\mathfrak{p}}G)=\operatorname{thick}(T_{\mathfrak{p}}G)
for
n=\max(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})
, and
\mathcal{T}
is regular over
R
.

For the converse suppose that
\mathcal{T}
is locally regular over
R
, with compact generator
G
, and fix
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Spec}S
. Set
\mathfrak{p}=\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{q})
. As above, we can localise at
\mathfrak{p}
and assume
R
is local with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
, and that
\mathfrak{q}
lies over
\mathfrak{p}
. It is easy to verify that

\operatorname{art}_{S_{\mathfrak{q}}}\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{q}}\subseteq\operatorname{art}_{S}\mathcal{T}=\operatorname{art}_{R}\mathcal{T}\,.

It follows from Proposition
6.4
that each artinian object
X
in the
S_{\mathfrak{q}}
-linear category
\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{q}}
belongs to
\operatorname{thick}^{n}(T_{\mathfrak{p}}G)
for some integer
n
. The decomposition of
T_{\mathfrak{p}}G
noted above implies that
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}}T_{\mathfrak{p}}G=T_{\mathfrak{q}}G
, so applying the functor
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}}
yields that
X
belongs to
\operatorname{thick}^{n}(T_{\mathfrak{q}}G)
. Thus
\mathcal{T}
is locally regular over
S
.
∎

Regular endomorphism rings

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a compactly generated triangulated category and
G
a compact generator. Set
A=\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(G)
and consider the cohomological functor

H\colon\mathcal{T}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Mod}A,\quad\text{where}\quad X\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(G,X).

For an injective
A
-module
E
let
T(E)
denote the object in
\mathcal{T}
such that

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(H(X),E)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,T(E)).

The existence and uniqueness of
T(E)
follows from Brown representability.

The result below uses Adams resolutions and is well known; see for instance
[
36
]
.

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{T}
be an object such that the
A
-module
H(X)
admits an injective resolution
\eta\colon H(X)\to I
where
I^{s}=0
for
s>n
. Then
X
belongs to the triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
generated by
T(I^{0}),\ldots,T(I^{n})
.

Proof.

The argument is an induction on
n
. The morphism
\eta\colon H(X)\to I^{0}
corresponds to a morphism
\widetilde{\eta}\colon X\to T(I^{0})
.
If
n=0
, then
\eta
is an isomorphism. It follows that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(G,\widetilde{\eta})
is an isomorphism, and hence so is
\widetilde{\eta}
, because
G
generates
\mathcal{T}
.

Assume
n\geq 1
and let
X^{\prime}
denote the cone of the morphism
\widetilde{\eta}
. This yields an exact sequence
0\to H(X)\to I^{0}\to H(X^{\prime})\to 0
. The induction hypothesis implies that
X^{\prime}
belongs to the triangulated subcategory generated by
T(I^{1}),\ldots,T(I^{n})
. Thus
X
belongs to the triangulated subcategory generated by
T(I^{0}),\ldots,T(I^{n})
.
∎

Theorem 6.9
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a compactly generated
R
-linear category that is noetherian. If there exists a compact generator
G
for
\mathcal{T}
such that
\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(G)
has finite global dimension, then
\mathcal{T}
is locally regular over
R
.

Proof.

Set
A=\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(G)
and fix
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
. Then
G_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a compact generator for
\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and one has an isomorphism of rings

\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}}(G_{\mathfrak{p}})\cong\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(G)_{\mathfrak{p}}=A\otimes_{R}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\,.

Since
A\otimes_{R}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is also of finite global dimension, we can replace
R
and
\mathcal{T}
by their localisations at
\mathfrak{p}
and assume
R
is local, with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
. The
A
-module
J=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(A,I(\mathfrak{p}))
is injective, and it is straightforward to verify that
T_{\mathfrak{p}}G=T(J)
. The desired result is thus that
\operatorname{thick}(T(J))
has a strong generator. Since
\mathcal{T}
is noetherian, arguing as in the proof of Lemma
6.2
one deduces that
T(J)
, and hence any object in
\operatorname{thick}(T(J))
, is artinian.

Let
X
be any artinian object in
\mathcal{T}
and set
M=\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(G,X)
. Then
M
is artinian as an
R
-module, so it embeds into a finite direct sum of copies of
I(\mathfrak{p})
. Thus as an
A
-module
M
embeds into a finite direct sum of copies of
J
. The cokernel of such an embedding is again artinian over
R
, and we obtain therefore an injective resolution of
M
over
A
involving in each degree finite direct sums of copies of
J
and their direct summands. Thus, if the global dimension of
A
is bounded by
n
, then
X
belongs to
\operatorname{thick}^{n+1}(T(J))
, as desired; see Lemma
6.8
and its proof.
∎

7.
Local regularity

In this section we consider a rigidly compactly generated tensor triangulated category
\mathcal{T}=(\mathcal{T},\otimes,\mathds{1})
and introduce in this setting a notion of local regularity. Then we are able to describe for each point its dualisable objects.

Definition 7.1
.

Set
R=\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathds{1})
. We say
\mathcal{T}
is
noetherian
to mean that
\mathcal{T}
is noetherian with respect to the canonical
R
-action, so the graded
R
-module
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,D)
is noetherian for all compact objects
C,D
in
\mathcal{T}
. In particular, the graded ring
R
, which is always commutative, is noetherian.

We say that
\mathcal{T}
is
locally regular
if it is noetherian and admits a compact generator
G
such that
\operatorname{thick}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}G)
is strongly generated for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
.

For any pair
G,G^{\prime}
of compact generators, one has
\operatorname{thick}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}G)=\operatorname{thick}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}G^{\prime})
for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
. Thus the definition of local regularity does not depend on the choice of a generator.

The result below reconciles this notion of local regularity with the one introduced in Section
6
for triangulated categories with linear actions. For tensor triangulated categories, the definition above is more intrinsic. In fact, the linear action of
\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathds{1})
plays a major role in our arguments. If this ring is too small, then the definition ought to be formulated in terms of prime ideals of the tensor triangulated category
{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}
. An example to keep in mind is the derived category of a non-affine scheme. Another such example from representation theory is the derived category of a Schur algebra, with subcategory of compact objects equivalent to the homotopy category of Young permutation modules.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a rigidly compactly generated tensor triangulated category. Assume
\mathcal{T}
is noetherian and has a compact generator
G
. Then the local regularity of
\mathcal{T}
is equivalent to strong generation of any one, equivalently, all of the triangulated categories:

\operatorname{thick}(T_{\mathfrak{p}}G)
,
\operatorname{thick}(D_{\mathfrak{p}}G)
and
\operatorname{thick}(\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}G_{\mathfrak{p}})
, for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
.

Proof.

This follows from Corollary
5.9
.
∎

Examples

The definition of local regularity is motivated by the following example. For a noetherian ring
A
we write
\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
for its full derived category; it is a rigidly compactly generated tensor triangulated category.

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
A
a commutative noetherian ring. Then the ring
A
is regular
if and only if the tensor triangulated category
\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
is locally regular.

Proof.

Local regularity is a local condition. For each
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}A
we have

\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)_{\mathfrak{p}}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A_{\mathfrak{p}})\,.

Thus we may assume
A
is local with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
; set
k=A/\mathfrak{p}
.

The triangulated category
\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
has a compact generator, namely,
A
, and is noetherian as an
A
-linear category.
It is convenient to check local regularity via the strong generation of
\operatorname{thick}(T_{\mathfrak{p}}A)
; see Lemma
7.2
.
The
A
-module
I(\mathfrak{p})
identifies with
T_{\mathfrak{p}}A
, and is an injective cogenerator of
\operatorname{art}A
, the category of artinian
A
-modules. Moreover, an
A
-complex
X
is artinian precisely when the
A
-module
H^{*}(X)
is artinian, so
\operatorname{art}_{A}\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
identifies with
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{art}A)
. Thus, given Example
6.1
, it remains to verify that
A
is regular if and only if
\operatorname{art}A
has finite global dimension. This follows from the result due to Auslander, Buchsbaum, and Serre that
A
is regular if and only if
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{n}(k,k)=0
for
n\gg 0
; see Proposition 1.3.1 and Corollary 2.2.7 in
[
19
]
.
∎

Here is another example of a locally regular category.

Example 7.4
.

Let
k
be a field and
A=\Lambda^{*}(V)
the exterior algebra on a finite dimensional
k
-vector space
V
which we view as a dg-algebra generated in degree
-1
with trivial differential. Let
\mathcal{T}=\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}A)
denote the homotopy category of injective
A
-modules. This category is compactly generated and the Hopf algebra structure gives rise to a tensor product. The category
\mathcal{T}
is noetherian and generated by the injective resolution of the trivial
A
-module
k
. The graded endomorphism ring identifies with a symmetric algebra, so
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(\mathbf{i}k)\cong S^{*}(V^{\vee}[2])
. Hence
\mathcal{T}
is locally regular by Theorem
6.9
.

Descent

The result below is a version of Proposition
6.6
for tensor triangulated categories. In the statement, the condition that
f
is strong symmetric monoidal means that
f(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{T}})\cong\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{U}}
and there is a natural isomorphism

f(X\otimes Y)\cong fX\otimes fY\quad\text{for $X,Y$ in $\mathcal{T}$.}

The condition on an adjoint pair
(f,g)
that both functors preserve products and coproducts arises naturally and has been studied in the context of tensor triangulated categories and Grothendieck–Neeman duality; see
[
3
, Theorem 1.7]
.

Proposition 7.5
.

Let
f\colon\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{U}
be an exact functor between rigidly compactly generated tensor triangulated categories that is strong symmetric monoidal and preserves coproducts. Suppose also that following conditions hold:

(1)

\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{T}}\in\operatorname{thick}(g(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{U}}))
;

(2)

the canonical map
\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathds{1})\to\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{U}}(\mathds{1})
is finite.

If
\mathcal{U}
is locally regular, then so is
\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

Set
R=\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathds{1})
and
S=\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{U}}(\mathds{1})
, and view
\mathcal{U}
as an
R
-linear category via the natural map
R\to S
. We claim that, under the hypothesis of the statement, when the ring
S
is noetherian so is
R
.

Indeed, assume the ring
S
is noetherian. Since
S
is finite as an
R
-module, for
I=\operatorname{Ker}(R\to S)
, the ring
R/I
is noetherian, by the theorem of Eakin and Nagata
[
35
, Theorem 3.7]
. Thus
S
is noetherian as an
R
-module. Then, since the adjunction isomorphism
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{T}},g(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{U}}))\cong\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{U}}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{U}},\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{U}})=S
is
R
-linear, the
R
-module
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{T}},g(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{U}}))
is noetherian. Since
\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{T}}
is in the thick subcategory generated by
g(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{U}})
, the
R
-module
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{T}},\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{T}})=R
is noetherian, as claimed.

Suppose
\mathcal{U}
is locally regular over
S
; in particular
S
, and hence also
R
, is noetherian. The map
R\to S
is finite, so Lemma
6.7
yields that
\mathcal{U}
is also locally regular over
R
. We claim Proposition
6.6
applies, so
\mathcal{T}
is locally regular over
R
.

Indeed, from
[
3
, Theorem 1.3]
we get that
f
and
g
both preserve products and coproducts. For
X
in
\mathcal{T}
the projection formula from
[
3
, Theorem 1.3]
yields

X\otimes gf(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{T}})\cong g(fX\otimes f\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{T}})\cong g(f(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{T}}))\cong gfX\,.

By hypothesis there exists an integer
d
such that
\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{T}}
is in
\operatorname{thick}^{d}(gf\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{T}})
. Applying
X\otimes-
and using the isomorphism above, we deduce that
X
is in
\operatorname{thick}^{d}(gfX)
.
∎

The following example illustrates the result above. In particular it shows that
\mathcal{T}
locally regular need not imply
\mathcal{U}
locally regular.

Example 7.6
.

Let
\varphi\colon A\to B
be a finite map of commutative noetherian rings such that
B
is faithful and of finite projective dimension as an
A
-module.
Then the adjoint pair

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}(B)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi^{*}=B\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{A}-}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{*}=\mathrm{res}}

satisfies the hypothesis of Proposition
7.5
. Indeed, it is clear that
\varphi^{*}
is strong symmetric monoidal, preserving coproducts. Since
\varphi_{*}B
is a perfect complex and has full support, the theorem of Hopkins
[
27
]
and Neeman
[
37
]
yields that
A
is in
\operatorname{thick}(\varphi_{*}B)
, so condition (1) holds. Condition (2) holds because the endomorphisms of the units in
\mathbf{D}(A)
and
\mathbf{D}(B)
are
A
and
B
, respectively. Proposition
7.5
subsumes the well known result that when
B
is regular so is
A
. The converse need not hold; consider the case
A
is a field.

Dualisable torsion objects

Consider for
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R
the category of
\mathfrak{p}
-local
\mathfrak{p}
-torsion objects and describe its dualisable objects.

Proposition 7.7
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a rigidly compactly generated tensor triangulated category that is locally regular. For
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R
and any object
X
in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{T}}
the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

X
is dualisable in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
;

(2)

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)
is artinian over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
for each compact object
C
in
\mathcal{T}
;

(3)

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)
has finite length over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
for each compact object
C
in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
;

(4)

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,C)
has finite length over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
for each compact object
C
in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
;

(5)

X
is in
\operatorname{thick}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}})
;

(6)

X\to(D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}})^{2}X
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

The proof combines various results from previous sections; only the implication (2)
\Rightarrow
(5) uses the local regularity condition.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2): This follows from Proposition
5.11
.

(2)
\Leftrightarrow
(3): This follows from Proposition
4.4
.

(3)
\Leftrightarrow
(4): This follows from Lemma
6.5
.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(5): This follows from Proposition
6.4
.

(5)
\Rightarrow
(1): This follows from Corollary
3.7
.

(2)
\Leftrightarrow
(6): For any
C\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}
the
R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)
is
\mathfrak{p}
-torsion, so the assertion follows from the analogue of Lemma
5.3
for
D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and Lemma
7.8
below.
∎

Lemma 7.8
.

Let
M
be an
R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module that is
\mathfrak{p}
-torsion. Then the canonical map
\delta_{M}\colon M\to(D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}})^{2}M
is an isomorphism if and only if
M
is artinian.

Proof.

From Matlis duality it follows that the map
\delta_{M}
is an isomorphism if
M
is artinian. Suppose
\delta_{M}
is an isomorphism. An application of the Snake Lemma and the fact that
\delta_{M}
is always a monomorphism shows that Matlis reflexive modules are closed under submodules and quotients.
In particular, the socle of
M
has finite length. This is an essential submodule since
M
is
\mathfrak{p}
-torsion, and therefore
M
is artinian.
∎

Compactness

Given
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
, we consider in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
the difference between compact and dualisable objects; in cohomological terms it is the difference between having finite length and being artinian.

Corollary 7.9
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be as in Proposition
7.7
. For
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R
and any object
X
in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{T}}
the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

X
is compact in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{T}}
;

(2)

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)
has finite length over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
for each compact object
C
in
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
;

(3)

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,C)
has finite length over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
for each compact object
C
in
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

In particular, compact objects and dualisable objects in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
coincide if and only if
\mathfrak{p}
is minimal in
\operatorname{supp}_{R}\mathcal{T}

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2): The
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)
is noetherian, because
X
is also compact in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and
\mathfrak{p}
-torsion, so it has finite length.

(2)
\Leftrightarrow
(3): This follows from Lemma
6.5
.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(1): Passing to
\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
we can assume
R
is local. The hypothesis implies
X
is dualisable in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
, by
Proposition
7.7
. Thus
{X}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{p}}\cong X\otimes{\mathds{1}}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{p}}
is compact in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
, by Proposition
3.1
, and hence also in
\mathcal{T}
. We claim
\operatorname{thick}(X)=\operatorname{thick}({X}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{p}})
, which implies
X
is compact as desired.

For the claim it suffices to show that
\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X)
is
\mathfrak{p}
-torsion, by
[
10
, Lemma 3.9]
.
First observe that for any object
C
there is an isomorphism of
R
-modules

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}C)\cong\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,C)

since
X
is
\mathfrak{p}
-torsion. Thus the hypothesis implies that the
R
-module
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}C)
has finite length for each compact object
C
in
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Since
X
is dualisable in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
, it is finitely built from objects of the form
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}C
; this is again from Proposition
7.7
. Thus
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,X)
has finite length, and in particular it is
\mathfrak{p}
-torsion.

The last part of the statement follows from Lemma
4.6
, using the cohomological descriptions of compact and dualisable objects given above.
∎

Dualisable complete objects

There is an equivalence
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
of compactly generated tensor triangulated categories, for
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R
. Clearly, this equivalence preserves and reflects dualisability, and restricts to a triangle equivalence

\operatorname{thick}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}})\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{thick}(\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathfrak{p}})\,.

This leads to an analogue of Proposition
7.7
for
\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
.

Proposition 7.10
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a rigidly compactly generated tensor triangulated category that is locally regular. For
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R
and any object
X
in
\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{T}}
the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

X
is dualisable;

(2)

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)
is noetherian over
R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}
for each compact object
C
in
\mathcal{T}
;

(3)

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(C,X)
has finite length over
R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}
for each compact object
C
in
\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
;

(4)

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,C)
has finite length over
R{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}
for each compact object
C
in
\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
;

(5)

X
is in
\operatorname{thick}(\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathfrak{p}})
;

(6)

X\to(D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}})^{2}X
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Given the equivalence
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
, the result follows from Proposition
7.7
. The only condition that does not translate directly is (2), but this is equivalent to (3) by Proposition
5.10
.
∎

Corollary 7.11
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a rigidly compactly generated tensor triangulated category that is locally regular. For
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R
the functor
D{}^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{p}}
restricts to a pair of mutually quasi-inverse contravariant equivalences
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}\rightleftarrows\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, identifying the dualisable objects in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
with the dualisable objects in
\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
. Hence the subcategories of compact objects in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
and
\varLambda^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
agree and equal
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Proof.

The pair of equivalences
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}\rightleftarrows\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}
has been established in Theorem
5.8
. The statement about dualisable objects then follows from Propositions
7.7
and
7.10
. The statement about compact objects follows from Corollary
7.9
.
∎

Strong generation

Here is a consequence of Proposition
7.7
and Lemma

8.
Finite groups

The main result in this section is that for any finite group
G
, the tensor triangulated category
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG)
is locally regular. Applications include the various characterisations of local dualisable objects stated in the introduction. With an eye towards future applications, we establish an intermediate step in the more general setting of finite group schemes. We begin with recalling some fundamental results about cohomology and representations of finite group schemes.

Finite dimensional Hopf algebras

Let
k
be a field of positive characteristic and
A
a finite dimension co-commutative Hopf algebra over
k
.
Equivalently,

is a group algebra of some finite group scheme

over

, that is to say, the
k
-linear dual to the coordinate algebra

of

. The homotopy category
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}A)
of injective

-modules is a compactly generated triangulated category, and the quotient functor

induces an equivalence

{\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}A)}^{\mathrm{c}}\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.80835pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }{\mathbf{D}}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}A)\,;

its quasi-inverse sends an
A
-complex

to its injective resolution,
\mathbf{i}M
; see
[

]
.

Endowed with the product given by

with diagonal

-action,
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}A)
is a tensor triangulated category, with unit
\mathbf{i}_{A}k
, where

is viewed as an
A
-module via the augmentation

. Moreover
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}A)
is rigidly compactly generated. For each compact object

in

, the

is noetherian, by

. In particular, the tensor triangulated category

is noetherian.

In the next theorem we show for any finite commutative unipotent group scheme, its homotopy category of injectives is locally regular.

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
k
be a field of positive characteristic
p
and
A
a finite dimensional co-commutative Hopf algebra over

. Assume that there exist integers

for

Then the tensor triangulated category
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}A)
is locally regular.

Proof.

Set
\mathcal{T}=\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}A)
and
R=\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathbf{i}_{A}k)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{A}(k,k)
. Since

{\mathbf{D}}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}A)
as a thick subcategory, so

is a compact generator

, by the equivalence (

has a strong generator for each

in
\operatorname{Spec}R
.

As is well-understood, the object

is independent of the coproduct on

; see
[

, Propostion 4.9]
. Thus the same is true of the local regularity condition. In what follows we assume that the coproduct on

is the one for which each

is primitive:
z_{i}\mapsto z_{i}\otimes 1+1\otimes z_{i}
. Let

be the Koszul complex of

on elements

, where

is viewed as a dg (=differential graded) Hopf

-algebra, as in
[

, §7]
; this is where the specific choice of the coproduct on

is needed. Let
\mathcal{U}
be the homotopy category of graded-injective dg

-modules; see
[
8
, §4]
. Let
\mathbf{i}_{B}k
be the semi-injective resolution of

viewed as a dg
B
-module via the augmentation

[

, Lemma 7.1]
. The triangulated category

is compactly generated by

and it is

[

, §4 and §6]
for details. The global dimension of

is

and so finite. Then, keeping in mind Lemma

to conclude that

is locally regular over

Consider the structure map

; this is a map of dg Hopf algebras, where

is viewed as a dg algebra with zero differential. As an

-module,

is finite free so one gets an exact functor

with right adjoint

induced by restriction along

is an injective resolution of

as an
A
-module, and hence a compact generator of
\mathcal{T}
. Note that

and let

be the natural map induced by

, and view
\mathcal{T}
as an
S
-linear category. Since

is a map of Hopf algebras, the functors

and

are

-linear, and satisfy the hypotheses of Proposition

.

Indeed, one has an isomorphism of dg

. Thus

is right adjoint to

is a commutative artinian local ring and

. Thus for

Since we already know that

∎

Finite groups

Let

be a finite group and

a field whose characteristic is positive and divides the order of

be the group algebra; this is a finite dimensional co-commutative Hopf algebra over

has a natural structure as a tensor triangulated category, and this is rigidly compactly generated.

Theorem 8.3
.

The tensor triangulated category
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG)
is locally regular.

Proof.

We can assume the characteristic of

is an elementary abelian

-group of rank

applies, yielding that

Let

be the (finite) collection of maximal elementary abelian

denote the restriction functor and induction functor,

, respectively. Observe that

is an adjoint triple. Consider functors

\displaystyle f^{*}\colon

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG)\longrightarrow\prod_{E\in U}\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kE)

\displaystyle{\text{where }X\mapsto(f^{*}_{E}X)}\,,

\displaystyle f_{*}\colon

\displaystyle\prod_{E\in U}\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kE)\longrightarrow\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG)

\displaystyle{\text{where }(Y_{E})\mapsto\bigoplus_{E\in U}f^{E}_{*}(Y_{E})}\,.

Indeed, it is clear that each

Putting together the result of the previous sections we are able to describe the dualisable objects for each point. Corollary 8.6

The dualisable modules in

Proof.

This follows from Proposition

a

-module is pure-injective if and only if it is pure-injective as an object of the triangulated category
\operatorname{StMod}kG
.
∎

in the equivalence class corresponding to
\mathfrak{p}
and
for any
kG
-module
M
, one has

\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{supp}(M)\Longleftrightarrow\alpha^{*}(M_{K})\neq 0\text{ in }\operatorname{StMod}K[t]/(t^{p})

where
\operatorname{supp}(M)
equals the set of points
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Proj}H^{*}(G,k)
such that
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}M\neq 0
.

Finite length

Our goal is a characterisation of finite length objects in the local category
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{StMod}kG
via
\pi
-points.
We begin with a simple observation.

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
K
be a field of positive characteristic
p
and
A\coloneqq K[t]/(t^{p})
. Let
\mathfrak{m}
be the unique point in
\operatorname{Proj}\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{A}(K,K)
. An
A
-module
N
is in
\operatorname{stmod}A
if and only if
the
\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{A}(K,K)_{\mathfrak{m}}
-module
\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{A}(K,N)_{\mathfrak{m}}
has finite length. ∎

We require also the following observation. Recall the convention about Koszul objects for graded modules, discussed above Lemma
2.8
.

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
R\to S
be a flat map of graded-commutative noetherian rings. Fix
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
and a point
\mathfrak{q}
in
\operatorname{Spec}S
lying over
\mathfrak{p}
. Let
X
be an
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module such that
\mathfrak{p}^{s}X=0
for some integer
s
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\operatorname{length}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}X<\infty
;

(2)

\operatorname{length}_{S_{\mathfrak{q}}}(k(\mathfrak{q})\otimes_{R}X)<\infty
;

(3)

\operatorname{length}_{S_{\mathfrak{q}}}H^{*}({(S\otimes_{R}X)_{\mathfrak{q}}}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{q}})<\infty
.

Proof.

We can assume
R
and
S
are local, with maximal ideals
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{q}
, respectively.
Let
k
and
l
be the corresponding residue fields.

(1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2): Since
\mathfrak{p}^{s}X=0
, one has that

\displaystyle\operatorname{length}_{R}X<\infty

\displaystyle\iff\operatorname{length}_{k}(k\otimes_{R}X)<\infty

\displaystyle\iff\operatorname{length}_{l}(l\otimes_{k}(k\otimes_{R}X))<\infty

\displaystyle\iff\operatorname{length}_{l}(l\otimes_{R}X))<\infty\,.

This is the desired equivalence.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(3): When
\operatorname{length}_{R}X
is finite, the
S
-module
S\otimes_{R}X
is finitely generated, and hence so is the
S
-module
H^{*}({(S\otimes_{R}X)}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{q}})
. Since the latter is also annihilated by
\mathfrak{q}
, it follows that it has finite length.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(2):
One has isomorphisms of
S
-modules

H^{0}({(S\otimes_{R}X)}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{q}})\cong\frac{(S\otimes_{R}X)}{\mathfrak{q}(S\otimes_{R}X)}\cong l\otimes_{S}(S\otimes_{R}X)\cong l\otimes_{R}X\,.

This justifies the claim.
∎

We are now ready to provide the proof of the main result of this section.

Proof of Theorem
9.1
.

Let
\mathcal{T}=\operatorname{StMod}kG
and
\mathcal{T}_{K}=\operatorname{StMod}KG
. Consider the
k
-algebra
R=H^{*}(G,k)
and let

R\longrightarrow S=H^{*}(G_{K},K)\cong K\otimes_{k}H^{*}(G,k)

be the map of rings induced by
k\to K
. This is a flat map.

(1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2):
Let
C
be the direct sum of a representative set of simple
kG
-modules. We provide a chain of equivalent conditions to get from (1) to (2).

Since the object
C
is a compact generator of
\mathcal{T}
, the description of the compacts in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}
from Lemma
2.7
, yields that that condition (1) is equivalent to:

\operatorname{length}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}({C}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{p}},M)<\infty\,.

Lemma
9.3
applies to
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}({C}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{p}},M)
since it is annihilated by
\mathfrak{p}^{s}
for some
s
, again by Lemma
2.7
. Thus the above condition is equivalent to

\operatorname{length}_{S_{\mathfrak{m}}}H^{*}({(S\otimes_{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}({C}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{p}},M))_{\mathfrak{m}}}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{m}})<\infty\,.

The isomorphism of
S
-modules

S\otimes_{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}({C}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{p}},M)\cong K\otimes_{k}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}({C}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{p}},M)\cong\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{K}}(({C}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{p}})_{K},M_{K})

implies that the last condition is further equivalent to

\operatorname{length}_{S_{\mathfrak{m}}}H^{*}({\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{K}}(({C}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{p}})_{K},M_{K})}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{m}})_{\mathfrak{m}}<\infty\,.

Now by Lemma
2.8
, this is equivalent to

\operatorname{length}_{S_{\mathfrak{m}}}\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{K}}(({C}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{p}})_{K}\otimes_{K}{K}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{m}},(M_{K}))_{\mathfrak{m}}<\infty\,.

In
\mathcal{T}_{K}
one has an isomorphism

({C}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{p}})_{K}\otimes_{K}{K}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{m}}\cong{C_{K}}/\!\!/{(\mathfrak{p}S+\mathfrak{m})}\,.

Since
\mathfrak{p}S\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
the
KG
-modules
{C_{K}}/\!\!/{(\mathfrak{p}S+\mathfrak{m})}
and
{C_{K}}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{m}}
generate the same thick subcategory of
\mathcal{T}_{K}
, and hence the last condition is equivalent to

\operatorname{length}_{S_{\mathfrak{m}}}\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{K}}({C_{K}}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{m}},M_{K})_{\mathfrak{m}}<\infty\,.

Finally, since
C_{K}
generates
\mathcal{T}_{K}
and one has an isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{K}}({C_{K}}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{m}},M_{K})_{\mathfrak{m}}\cong\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{K}}({C_{K}}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{m}},\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M_{K}))

another application of Lemma
2.7
yields that the previous condition is equivalent to
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M_{K})
having finite length in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}\mathcal{T}_{K}
.

(2)
\Leftrightarrow
(3):
From Lemma
9.4
below it follows that the object
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M_{K})
is of finite length in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}\operatorname{StMod}KG
if and only if

\operatorname{length}_{S_{\mathfrak{m}}}\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{K}}(\alpha_{*}K,M_{K})_{\mathfrak{m}}<\infty\,.

It remains to note the adjunction isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{K}}(\alpha_{*}K,M_{K})\cong\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{U}_{K}}(K,\alpha^{*}(M_{K}))

for
\mathcal{U}_{K}=\operatorname{StMod}K[t]/(t^{p})
and Lemma
9.2
.
∎

The result below, implicit in
[
12
, Section 9]
, was used in the argument above.

Lemma 9.4
.

In the notation of Theorem
9.1
, the thick subcategory generated by
\alpha_{*}K
in
\operatorname{StMod}KG
is
{(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}\operatorname{StMod}KG)}^{\mathrm{c}}
.

Proof.

Set
\mathcal{T}_{K}=\operatorname{StMod}KG
and
\mathcal{U}_{K}=\operatorname{StMod}K[t]/t^{p}
. The
KG
-module
\alpha_{*}K
is finite dimensional and hence compact in
\mathcal{T}_{K}
. It is easy to verify that
\operatorname{supp}(\alpha_{*}K)=\{\mathfrak{m}\}
; see, for example, the first part of the proof of
[
12
, Lemma 9.1]
. Thus
\alpha_{*}K
is in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}\mathcal{T}_{K}
. For any
N\neq 0
in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}\mathcal{T}
one has
\alpha^{*}N\neq 0
in
\operatorname{StMod}K[t]/(t^{p})
, and hence

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}_{K}}(\alpha_{*}K,N)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{U}_{K}}(K,\alpha^{*}N)\neq 0\,.

Given these facts, a standard argument yields that the localising subcategory generated by
\alpha_{*}K
equals
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}\mathcal{T}_{K}
. Since
\alpha_{*}K
is compact, the desired result follows.
∎

Some new results about q q -trinomial coefficients
Yifan Chen, Chang Xu, Xiaoxia Wang ∗

Abstract
. In this paper, we present several new congruences on the q q -trinomial coefficients introduced by Andrews and Baxter. A new congruence on sums of central q q -binomial coefficients is also established.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 33D15; Secondary 11A07, 11B65

1.
Introduction

In 2019, Straub
[
18
]
gave the following
q
-supercongruence:

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an}{bn}\equiv\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{a}{b}_{q^{n^{2}}}-(a-b)b\dbinom{a}{b}\frac{n^{2}-1}{24}(q^{n}-1)^{2}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)^{3}},

(1.1)

which is a
q
-analogue of the Wolstenholme–Ljunggren congruence: for any prime
p\geq 5
,

\dbinom{ap}{bp}\equiv\dbinom{a}{b}\pmod{p^{3}}.

Here and throughout the paper,
\Phi_{n}(q)
stands for the
n
-th
cyclotomic polynomial
in
q
:

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}(q)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant k\leqslant n\\
\gcd(n,k)=1\end{subarray}}(q-\zeta^{k}),

where
\zeta
is an
n
-th primitive root of unity. The
q
-binomial coefficient is defined as

\displaystyle\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k}=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k}_{q}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{(q;q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{k}(q;q)_{n-k}},&\text{if $0\leq k\leq n$; }\\[11.38109pt]
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where the
q
-shifted factorial
is defined as

(a;q)_{0}=1
and
(a;q)_{n}=(1-a)(1-aq)\cdots(1-aq^{n-1})
with
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and integer
j
with
-n\leq j\leq n
, the trinomial coefficient is the coefficient of
x^{j}
in the expansion of
(1+x+x^{-1})^{n}
. Namely,

\bigg(\!\!\dbinom{n}{j}\!\!\bigg)=[x^{j}](1+x+x^{-1})^{n},

and it has a simple expression (see
[
17
]
)

\bigg(\!\!\dbinom{n}{j}\!\!\bigg)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\dbinom{n}{k}\dbinom{n-k}{k+j}.

Six different
q
-analogues of the trinomial coefficients, which play significant roles in hard hexagon model, were introduced by Andrews and Baxter
[
1
]
. One of these
q
-analogues is

\bigg(\!\!\dbinom{n}{j}\!\!\bigg)_{q}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}q^{k(k+j)}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n-k}{k+j}.

(1.2)

During the past few years, some experts have paid attention to
q
-analogues of supercongruences.
We refer the reader to
[
14
,
15
,
9
,
5
,
6
]
for some of their work.
Moreover, some congruences for
q
-binomial coefficients and
q
-trinomial coefficients can be found in
[
7
,
16
,
21
,
3
,
13
,
2
,
8
]
.

Recently, Liu
[
12
]
established the following beautiful
q
-supercongruences:
for any positive integer
n
, modulo
\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}
,

\displaystyle\bigg(\!\!\dbinom{n}{0}\!\!\bigg)_{q}

\displaystyle\equiv\begin{cases}(-1)^{m}(1+q^{m})q^{m(3m-1)/2},&\text{if \ $n=3m$};\\[11.38109pt]
(-1)^{m}q^{m(3m+1)/2},&\text{if \ $n=3m+1$};\\[11.38109pt]
(-1)^{m}q^{m(3m-1)/2},&\text{if \ $n=3m-1$},\end{cases}

(1.3)

\displaystyle\bigg(\!\!\dbinom{2n}{n}\!\!\bigg)_{q}

\displaystyle\equiv\begin{cases}2(-1)^{m}(1+q^{m})q^{m(3m-1)/2}-3m(1-q^{3m}),&\text{if \ $n=3m$};\\[11.38109pt]
2(-1)^{m}q^{m(3m+1)/2}-(3m+1)(1-q^{3m+1}),&\text{if \ $n=3m+1$};\\[11.38109pt]
2(-1)^{m}q^{m(3m-1)/2}-(3m-1)(1-q^{3m-1}),&\text{if \ $n=3m-1$}.\end{cases}

(1.4)

Motivated by the work just mentioned, we shall establish the following
q
-supercongruence similar to (
1.3
) and (
1.4
).

Theorem 1.1
.

For any positive integer
n
, modulo
{\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}}
,

\displaystyle\bigg(\!\!\dbinom{2n}{0}\!\!\bigg)_{q}\equiv\begin{cases}2(-1)^{m}(1+q^{m})q^{m(3m-1)/2}-9m(1-q^{3m})+1,&\text{if \ $n=3m$};\\[11.38109pt]
2(-1)^{m}q^{m(3m+1)/2}-3(3m+1)(1-q^{3m+1})+1,&\text{if \ $n=3m+1$};\\[11.38109pt]
2(-1)^{m}q^{m(3m-1)/2}-3(3m-1)(1-q^{3m-1})+1,&\text{if \ $n=3m-1$}.\end{cases}

(1.5)

More generally, we shall give the following two new
q
-supercongruences.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any positive integers
a
and
n
, modulo
{\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}}
,

\displaystyle\bigg(\!\!\dbinom{an}{an-n}\!\!\bigg)_{q}\equiv\begin{cases}(-1)^{m}a(1+q^{m})q^{m(3m-1)/2}-3m(1-q^{3m}){\binom{a}{2}},&\text{if \ $n=3m$};\\[14.22636pt]
(-1)^{m}aq^{m(3m+1)/2}-(3m+1)(1-q^{3m+1})\binom{a}{2},&\text{if \ $n=3m+1$};\\[14.22636pt]
(-1)^{m}aq^{m(3m-1)/2}-(3m-1)(1-q^{3m-1})\binom{a}{2},&\text{if \ $n=3m-1$}.\end{cases}

(1.6)

Theorem 1.3
.

For any positive integers
a
and
n
with
a\geq 2
, modulo
{\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}}
,

\displaystyle\bigg(\!\!\dbinom{an}{an-2n}\!\!\bigg)_{q}

\displaystyle\equiv\begin{cases}(-1)^{m}2\binom{a}{2}(1+q^{m})q^{m(3m-1)/2}-9m(1-q^{3m})\binom{a+1}{3}+\frac{3a-a^{2}}{2},&\text{if \ $n=3m$};\\[11.38109pt]
(-1)^{m}2\binom{a}{2}q^{m(3m+1)/2}-3(3m+1)(1-q^{3m+1})\binom{a+1}{3}+\frac{3a-a^{2}}{2},&\text{if \ $n=3m+1$};\\[11.38109pt]
(-1)^{m}2\binom{a}{2}q^{m(3m-1)/2}-3(3m-1)(1-q^{3m-1})\binom{a+1}{3}+\frac{3a-a^{2}}{2},&\text{if \ $n=3m-1$}.\end{cases}

(1.7)

It is easily seen that Liu’s two supercongruences (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) are the special cases of Theorem
1.2
with
a=1
and
a=2
, respectively,
and Theorem
1.1
can be obtained by taking
a=2
in Theorem
1.3
.

As serendipitous discoveries, we obtain the following
q
-congruences which are the contiguous forms of (
1.6
) and (
1.3
) modulo
\Phi_{n}(q)
.

Theorem 1.4
.

For any integer
a\geq 1
, integer
j
and positive odd integer
n
with
0\leq j\leq n-1
, there holds

\displaystyle\bigg(\!\!\dbinom{an-1}{an-n+j}\!\!\bigg)_{q}

\displaystyle\equiv\bigg(\frac{n-j}{3}\bigg)q^{(n-j-1)(n-j-2)/6-(j^{2}+j)/2}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)}.

(1.8)

Here and in what follows,
\big(\frac{.}{p}\big)
denotes the Legendre symbol.

Theorem 1.5
.

For any integer
a\geq 2
, integer
j
and positive odd integer
n
with
0\leq j\leq n
, there holds, modulo
\Phi_{n}(q)
,

\displaystyle\bigg(\!\!\dbinom{an-1}{an-2n+j}\!\!\bigg)_{q}

\displaystyle\equiv(a-1)\bigg(\frac{n-j}{3}\bigg)q^{\frac{(n-j-1)(n-j-2)}{6}-\frac{j^{2}+j}{2}}+\bigg(\frac{j}{3}\bigg)q^{\frac{1-j^{2}}{3}}.

(1.9)

It should be mentioned that if we take
j=0
in Theorem
1.4
and
j=n
in Theorem
1.5
, then the right-hand sides of (
1.8
) and (
1.9
)
are congruent to each other modulo
\Phi_{n}(q)
.

Theorem 1.6
.

For any integer
a\geq 2
, positive integer
b
satisfying
a\leq b+2
and positive odd integer
n
, there holds

\displaystyle\bigg(\!\!\dbinom{an-1}{bn-1}\!\!\bigg)_{q}\equiv\dbinom{a}{b}+\dbinom{a-1}{b}\bigg(\frac{n+1}{3}\bigg)q^{n(n-1)/6}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)}.

(1.10)

By taking
j=n-1
in Theorem
1.5
and
b=a-1
in Theorem
1.6
, we can easily find that they have the same left-hand sides.
As a result, the right-hand sides of (
1.9
) and (
1.10
) are congruent to each other modulo
\Phi_{n}(q)
. In other words, for any integer
a\geq 2
and positive odd integer
n
, there holds

a+\bigg(\frac{n+1}{3}\bigg)q^{n(n-1)/6}\equiv(a-1)q^{-n(n-1)/2}+\bigg(\frac{n-1}{3}\bigg)q^{-n(n-2)/3}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)}.

1.2
and
1.3
.
The proofs of Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
will be presented in Sections
3
and
4
, respectively. In the last section, a sketch of the proof of Theorem
1.6
will be provided.

2.
Proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3

In order to prove Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
, we need the following two lemmas which have been proved by Liu.

Lemma 2.1

(Liu
[
11
]
)
.

For any non-negative integer
n
, there holds

\displaystyle(1-q^{n})\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\frac{(-1)^{k}q^{k(k-1)/2}}{1-q^{n-k}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n-k}{k}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{m}(1+q^{m})q^{m(3m-1)/2},&\text{if \ $n=3m$};\\[5.69054pt]
(-1)^{m}q^{m(3m+1)/2},&\text{if \ $n=3m+1$};\\[5.69054pt]
(-1)^{m}q^{m(3m-1)/2},&\text{if \ $n=3m-1$},\end{cases}

(2.1)

where
\lfloor x\rfloor
is the integral part of real
x
.

Lemma 2.2

(Liu
[
12
]
)
.

For any positive integer
n
, there holds

\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\frac{q^{-k(k-1)}}{[2k]_{q}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{2k}{k}\equiv\frac{(1-q)(1-R_{n}(q))}{1-q^{n}}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)},

where
R_{n}(q)
denotes the right-hand side of (
2.1
).

It is not difficult to see that
R_{n}(q)\equiv 1\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Firstly, we express the left-hand side of (
1.6
) by Andrews and Baxter’s expression (
1.2
). Note that
{an-k\brack k+an-n}
for any
k>\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
. Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\bigg(\!\!\binom{an}{an-n}\!\!\bigg)_{q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{an}q^{k(k+an-n)}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an}{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an-k}{k+an-n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}q^{k(k+an-n)}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an}{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an-k}{k+an-n}

\displaystyle=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an}{an-n}+\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}q^{k(k+an-n)}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an}{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an-k}{k+an-n}.

(2.2)

For
1\leq k\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
, we have

\displaystyle\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an}{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-q^{an})(1-q^{an-1})\cdots(1-q^{an+1-k})}{(1-q)(1-q^{2})\cdots(1-q^{k})}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{a(1-q^{n})(1-q^{-1})\cdots(1-q^{1-k})}{(1-q)(1-q^{2})\cdots(1-q^{k})}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{a(1-q^{n})(-1)^{k-1}q^{-k(k-1)/2}}{1-q^{k}}\quad\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}},

(2.3)

where the second relation is due to the fact that
1-q^{an}\equiv a(1-q^{n})\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}}
.

Because the reduced form of
{an\brack k}
contains the factor
\Phi_{n}(q)
, it is enough to consider the following
q
-binomial coefficient modulo
\Phi_{n}(q)
.
For
1\leq k\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
, we have

\displaystyle\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an-k}{k+an-n}

\displaystyle=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an}{an-n}\frac{(1-q^{n})(1-q^{n-1})\cdots(1-q^{n+1-2k})}{(1-q^{an})\cdots(1-q^{an-k+1})(1-q^{(a-1)n+1})\cdots(1-q^{(a-1)n+k})}

\displaystyle\equiv\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an}{n}\frac{1-q^{n}}{1-q^{an}}\frac{(1-q^{-1})\cdots(1-q^{1-2k})}{(1-q^{-1})\cdots(1-q^{-k+1})(1-q)\cdots(1-q^{k})}

\displaystyle\equiv\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an}{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{a}q^{-(3k-1)k/2}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{2k-1}{k}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)}.

(2.4)

Substituting the results (
2
) and (
2
) into the right-hand side of (
2
), we arrive at

\bigg(\!\!\binom{an}{an-n}\!\!\bigg)_{q}\equiv\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an}{n}-(1-q^{n})\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\frac{q^{-k(k-1)}}{1-q^{k}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{2k-1}{k}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}}.

(2.5)

Then by Lemma
2.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\frac{q^{-k(k-1)}}{1-q^{k}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{2k-1}{k}=\frac{1}{1-q}\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\frac{q^{-k(k-1)}}{[2k]_{q}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{2k}{k}\equiv\frac{1-R_{n}(q)}{1-q^{n}}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)}.

(2.6)

On the other hand, by the weaker version of (
1.1
), we have

\begin{split}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an}{n}&\equiv\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{a}{1}_{q^{n^{2}}}\equiv 1+q^{n^{2}}+q^{2n^{2}}+\cdots+q^{(a-1)n^{2}}\\
&\equiv a-((1-q^{n^{2}})+(1-q^{2n^{2}})+\cdots+(1-q^{(a-1)n^{2}}))\\
&\equiv a-n(1-q^{n})\frac{a(a-1)}{2}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}},\end{split}

(2.7)

where we have utilized the fact
q^{n}\equiv 1\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)}
and the identity

1-q^{kn^{2}}=(1-q^{n})(1+q^{n}+q^{2n}+\cdots+q^{(kn-1)n}).

Finally, substituting (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) into the right-hand side of (
2.5
), we are led to

\bigg(\!\!\binom{an}{an-n}\!\!\bigg)_{q}\equiv aR_{n}(q)-n(1-q^{n})\dbinom{a}{2}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}}

as desired.
∎

.

.

, we express

\displaystyle\equiv\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an}{2n}\>\frac{2}{a}\>(-1)^{k}q^{-(3k-1)k/2}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{2k-1}{k}\quad\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)}.

) and (

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

For the sake of proving Theorem
1.4
, we need the following congruence on the sum of central
q
-binomial coefficients.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any positive odd integer
n
and integer
j
with
0\leq j\leq n-1
, there holds

\sum_{k=0}^{n-j-1}q^{-k(k+1+j)}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{2k+j}{k}\equiv(-1)^{j}\bigg(\frac{n-j}{3}\bigg)q^{(n-j-1)(n-j-2)/6}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)}.

Obviously, the special case of Theorem
3.1
with
j=0
is

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}q^{-k(k+1)}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{2k}{k}\equiv\bigg(\frac{n}{3}\bigg)q^{(n-1)(n-2)/6}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)},

(3.1)

which is a
q
-analogue of a congruence by Sun and Tauraso
[
19
]
(the modulo
p
version): for any prime
p\geq 5
,

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\dbinom{2k}{k}\equiv\bigg(\frac{p}{3}\bigg)\pmod{p^{2}}.

In order to prove Theorem
3.1
, we recall the following result as the lemma, which firstly appeared in Ekhad and Zeilberger
[
4
]
and Krattenthaler’s note
[
10
]
and latter been proved by Warnaar
[
20
]
as a special case of a cubic summation formula.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any integer
n\geq 0
, there holds

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}q^{k(k-1)/2}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n-k}{k}=(-1)^{n}\bigg(\frac{n+1}{3}\bigg)q^{n(n-1)/6}.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
\omega=e^{2\pi mi/n}
with
\gcd(m,n)=1
. Then we have that

\omega^{n}=1
and
\omega^{k}\neq 1
for
1\leq k\leq n-1
.
Observing that

\displaystyle\omega^{-k(k+j+1)}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{2k+j}{k}_{\omega}=\omega^{-k(k+j+1)}\prod_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1-\omega^{2k+j+1-l}}{1-\omega^{l}}=(-1)^{k}\omega^{k(k-1)/2}\begin{bmatrix}n-k-j-1\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{\omega},

we immediately get

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-j-1}q^{-k(k+j+1)}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{2k+j}{k}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{n-j-1}(-1)^{k}q^{k(k-1)/2}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n-k-j-1}{k}

\displaystyle\equiv(-1)^{j}\bigg(\frac{n-j}{3}\bigg)q^{(n-j-2)(n-j-1)/6}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)},

where we have utilized Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Now we begin to prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

For
1\leq k\leq n-1
, we have

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an-1}{k}=\frac{(1-q^{an-1})\cdots(1-q^{an-k})}{(1-q)\cdots(1-q^{k})}\equiv(-1)^{k}q^{-k(k+1)/2}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)}.

(3.2)

We can also get the following congruence with
1\leq k\leq\lfloor(n-1-j)/2\rfloor
,

\displaystyle\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an-k-1}{an-n+k+j}

\displaystyle=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an}{n}\frac{(1-q^{n})\cdots(1-q^{n-2k-j})}{(1-q^{an})\cdots(1-q^{an-k})(1-q^{(a-1)n+1})\cdots(1-q^{(a-1)n+k+j})}

\displaystyle\equiv(-1)^{k+j}q^{-(3k+j+1)(k+j)/2}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{2k+j}{k}\quad\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)}.

(3.3)

By combining the congruence (
3.2
) with (
3
), we arrive at the following result

\displaystyle\bigg(\!\!\dbinom{an-1}{an-n+j}\!\!\bigg)_{q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n-j-1}{2}\rfloor}q^{k(k+an-n+j)}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an-1}{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an-k-1}{k+an-n+j}

\displaystyle\equiv\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an-1}{an-n+j}-(-1)^{j}q^{-(j^{2}+j)/2}\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n-j-1}{2}\rfloor}q^{-k^{2}-kj-k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{2k+j}{k}

\displaystyle\equiv\bigg(\frac{n-j}{3}\bigg)q^{(n-j-1)(n-j-2)/6-(j^{2}+j)/2}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)},

where we have applied Theorem
3.1
and
{2k+j\brack k}\equiv 0\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)}
for
\lfloor\frac{n-j-1}{2}\rfloor+1\leq k\leq n-j-1
in the last step.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.5

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Because of the different range of
k
, the proof of Theorem
1.5
is a little different from that of Theorem
1.4
. We first split the summation of the following
q
-trinomial coefficient into three parts as

\displaystyle\bigg(\!\!\dbinom{an-1}{an-2n+j}\!\!\bigg)_{q}=

\displaystyle\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an-1}{an-2n+j}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-j-1}q^{k(k+an-2n+j)}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an-1}{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an-k-1}{k+an-2n+j}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=n-j}^{\lfloor\frac{2n-j-1}{2}\rfloor}q^{k(k+an-2n+j)}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an-1}{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an-k-1}{k+an-2n+j}.

(4.1)

The first and the second parts of the right-hand side of (
4
) can be handled just as what we have done in the proof of Theorem
1.4
.
Thus, we can get the following result with no difficulty,

\displaystyle\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an-1}{an-2n+j}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-j-1}q^{k(k+an-2n+j)}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an-1}{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an-k-1}{k+an-2n+j}

\displaystyle\quad\equiv(a-1)\bigg(\frac{n-j}{3}\bigg)q^{(n-j-1)(n-j-2)/6-(j^{2}+j)/2}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)}.

(4.2)

Now we begin to calculate the third part of the right-hand side of (
4
). Since the third part will disappear if
j=0
,
we only need to consider the case where
j
is in the range
1\leq j\leq n
. Noticing that
{an-k-1\brack k+an-2n+j}
can be simplified through the method used in (
3
), and recalling the result (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=n-j}^{\lfloor\frac{2n-j-1}{2}\rfloor}q^{k(k+an-2n+j)}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an-1}{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an-k-1}{k+an-2n+j}

\displaystyle\ \equiv(-1)^{j}q^{-(j^{2}+j)/2}\sum_{k=n-j}^{\lfloor\frac{2n-j-1}{2}\rfloor}q^{-k^{2}-kj-k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{2k+j}{k}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)}.

(4.3)

Performing
k\to n-j+k
in the summation of the right-hand side of (
4
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=n-j}^{\lfloor\frac{2n-j-1}{2}\rfloor}q^{-k^{2}-kj-k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{2k+j}{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{j-1}{2}\rfloor}q^{-(k+n-j)^{2}-(k+n-j)j-(k+n-j)}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{2(k+n-j)+j}{k+n-j}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{j-1}{2}\rfloor}q^{-k^{2}+kj-k+j}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n+2k-j}{k}

\displaystyle\equiv(-1)^{n-j}\bigg(\frac{j}{3}\bigg)q^{(j-1)(j-2)/6}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)},

(4.4)

where we have utilized Theorem
3.1
with
j\to n-j
in the last step.

Finally, by combining the formulas from (
4
) to (
4
) together, we obtain

\displaystyle\bigg(\!\!\dbinom{an-1}{an-2n+j}\!\!\bigg)_{q}

\displaystyle\equiv(a-1)\bigg(\frac{n-j}{3}\bigg)q^{\frac{(n-j-1)(n-j-2)}{6}-\frac{j^{2}+j}{2}}+\bigg(\frac{j}{3}\bigg)q^{\frac{1-j^{2}}{3}}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)}.

(4.5)

The right-hand side of (
4
) equals
0
if
j=0
, and so (
4.5
) also holds when
j=0
.
Thus we finish the proof of Theorem
1.5
.
∎

5.
The Proof of Theorem

Sketch of Proof.

For
1\leq k\leq\lfloor(a-b)n/2\rfloor
, we have

\displaystyle\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an-k-1}{k+bn-1}

\displaystyle=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{an}{bn}\frac{(1-q^{(a-b)n})\cdots(1-q^{(a-b)n-2k+1})}{(1-q^{bn+1})\cdots(1-q^{bn+k-1})(1-q^{an-k})\cdots(1-q^{an})}

\displaystyle\equiv(-1)^{k-1}\dbinom{a-1}{b}q^{-3k(k-1)/2}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{2k-1}{k}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)}.

Similarly to the proof of Theorem
1.4
, we get

\displaystyle\bigg(\!\!\dbinom{an-1}{bn-1}\!\!\bigg)_{q}

where we have applied Lemma

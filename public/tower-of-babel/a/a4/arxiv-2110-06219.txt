Extracting work from correlated many-body quantum systems
Raffaele Salvia, Vittorio Giovannetti

Abstract
The presence of correlations in the input state of a non-interacting many-body quantum system can lead to an increase in the amount of work we can extract from it under global unitary processes (ergotropy). The present work explore such effect on translationally invariant systems relaying on the Matrix Product Operator formalism to define a measure of how much they are correlated. We observe that in the thermodynamic limit of large number of sites, complete work extraction can be attained for relatively small correlation strength (a reduction of a 2 factor in dB unit). Most importantly such an effect appears not to be associated with the presence of quantum correlations (e.g. entanglement) in the input state (classical correlation suffices), and to be attainable by only using incoherent ergotropy . As a byproduct of our analysis we also present a rigorous formulation of the heuristic typicality argument first formulated in [Alicki and Fannes, 2013], which gives the maximum work extractable for a set of many identical quantum systems in the asymptotic limit.



I
Introduction

For purely classical models, the maximum work
\Delta W^{(\max)}_{\rm{clas}}
that can be extracted from a closed system by means of
reversible, iso-entropic cycles
is equal to the difference between the input energy
E_{in}
and the energy
E_{th}

of the thermal equilibrium state that has the same entropy of the initial configuration, i.e.
\Delta W^{(\max)}_{\rm{clas}}=E_{in}-E_{th}

Thomson 1852
;
Fermi et al. 1955
. The corresponding quantity for a quantum system with Hamiltonian
H
, initialised in a state
\rho
, is called
ergotropy

\mathcal{E}(\rho;H)

Allahverdyan et al. 2004
and is
formally defined as the maximal amount of energy one can recover from the system by means of reversible coherent (i.e. unitary) processes. In general
\mathcal{E}(\rho;H)
is strictly smaller than
the threshold one would get from a naive translation of the classical optimal term
\Delta W^{(\max)}_{\rm{clas}}
, i.e. the difference

E(\rho;H)-\mathfrak{E}_{S(\rho)}
between the mean energy
E(\rho;H):=\mbox{Tr}[H\rho]
of the input state
\rho
, and the mean energy
\mathfrak{E}_{S(\rho)}:=\mbox{Tr}[H\omega_{\beta}]
of
the thermal Gibbs state
\omega_{\beta}:=e^{-\beta H}/Z_{\beta}
that has the same von Neumann entropy
S(\rho)
.
Nonetheless, thanks to the fact that
\mathcal{E}(\rho;H)
is an extensive super-additive functional, the gap between the work extractable at the quantum level and
E(\rho;H)-\mathfrak{E}_{S(\rho)}
can be progressively reduced by allowing
joint operations on an increasing collection of identical copies of the system.
In particular, indicating
with

H^{(N)}
the global Hamiltonian of the
N
copies written in terms of a sum of independent, uniform,
local terms (see Eq. (
2
) below), it turns out that the ergotropy per-site
{\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\otimes N};H^{(N)})}/{N}
of the model
is a non-decreasing function of
N
whose asymptotic value (a quantity called from time-to-time
total
ergotropy

Niedenzu et al. 2019
)
verifies the identity
Alicki and Fannes 2013

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\text{tot}}(\rho;H)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\otimes N};H^{(N)})}{N}

(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E(\rho;H)-\mathfrak{E}_{S(\rho)}\;.

The limit (
1
) corresponds to the saturation of the classical threshold for
reversible, iso-entropic work extraction processes involving a quantum system. In all non trivial cases it
is explicitly attained for an infinite number of copies, the only exceptions being associated to the cases where

\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\otimes N};H^{(N)})=0
for all
N
.
A direct consequence of this observation is that when operating on
non correlated input quantum system
\rho^{\otimes N}
we can extract less work than in the classical case.
Such limitation however does not necessary hold if we allowed quantum correlations in the input state of the system: indeed if we permit
operations that act on the entire many-body system,
the classical threshold (
1
) can be overcame allowing for the extraction of additional work
Huber et al. 2015
;
Francica et al. 2017
(Notice that this is the opposite of what happens if instead we are forced to act only on
individual parts of the quantum state: in this scenario in fact
correlations typically are detrimental for work extraction see e.g. Refs.
Alicki and Fannes 2013
;
Oppenheim et al. 2002
;
Vitagliano et al. 2018
;
Goold et al. 2016
;
Bera et al. 2017
;
Manzano et al. 2018
;
Andolina et al. 2019
).
Aim of the present article is to investigate this issue by studying the ergotropy
\mathcal{E}(\rho^{(N)};H^{(N)})
of correlated multipartite quantum states
\rho^{(N)}
of
translationally invariant many-body quantum systems composed by
N
sites.
To express how much the state
\rho^{(N)}
is correlated, we will use as a measure the minimum bond link rank
M
necessary to represent
\rho^{(N)}
as a Matrix Product Operator
Verstraete et al. 2004
: indicating with

r
the rank (number of strictly positive eigenvalues) of the single-site density matrix
\rho
of the model,
the quantity
M

can range from
1
(corresponding to factorized state
\rho^{(N)}=\rho^{\otimes N}
), to
r^{2}

(corresponding to the case of where
\rho^{(N)}
is a pure GHZ entangled state). Via constructive examples, we shall hence produce lower bounds for the maximum
ergotropy per site

\mathcal{E}(\rho^{(N)};H^{(N)})/N
attainable in the system for fixed values of the correlation parameter
M
,
comparing them to the classical threshold limit (
1
) reachable when the correlations are removed from the model.

Our main finding is to show that, while forcing
\rho^{(N)}
to be pure by taking
M=r^{2}

allows one to trivially boost
\mathcal{E}(\rho^{(N)};H^{(N)})/N
to its natural upper bound (i.e. the single-site mean energy
E(\rho;H)
),
in the limit of large
N
one can asymptotically reach the same result for much smaller values of
M
. In particular we prove that setting
M=r
is enough, corresponding to a reduction of a factor 2 in dB unit. Most importantly it turns out that while for
M=r^{2}
the saturation to

relays explicitly on the presence of the quantum correlations (entanglement) that forces
\rho^{(N)}
to be pure, for
M=r
the same can be attained by only exploiting classical correlations. Our analysis shows also that this effect
can be attained through operations which do no affect the coherence of the input state

: using the notation introduced in

Francica et al. 2020
;
Çakmak 2020
;
Touil et al. 2021
, this means that
the asymptotic saturation of
\mathcal{E}(\rho^{(N)};H^{(N)})/N
to
E(\rho;H)
we report here for

can be obtained by just using
incoherent ergotropy
.

We conclude mentioning that as a byproduct of our study we provide
a rigorous derivation of the heuristic typicality argument first formulated in
Alicki and Fannes 2013
, which gives the maximum work extractable for a set of identical quantum systems in the asymptotic limit of a large number of copies
N
.

II
The problem

In our analysis we shall focus on a many-body quantum system consisting on an ordered collection
Q_{1}
,
Q_{2}
,
\cdots
,
Q_{N}
of
N

d
-dimensional sites – see Fig.
1
. Indicating with
{\cal H}
and
{\cal H}^{\otimes N}
the
single-site and many-body Hilbert spaces, we represent with

\mathfrak{L}
and
\mathfrak{L}^{(N)}
the associated spaces of the
linear operators, and with

\mathfrak{S}
and
\mathfrak{S}^{(N)}

the sets of the corresponding density matrices.
Assuming no interactions among the various sub-systems,
we write the
joint Hamiltonian of the model as a sum of homogenous local terms

\displaystyle H^{(N)}:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}H_{\ell}\;,

(2)

with
H_{\ell}
representing the same single-site operator
H\in\mathfrak{L}
acting on the
\ell
-th subsystem
Q_{\ell}
. Without loss of generality in what follows we shall take
H
(hence also
H^{(N)}
) to be positive semidefinite and to have zero ground state eigenvalue; in particular we shall use the symbols
\epsilon_{j}
to represent its eigenvalues that we order via the inequalities,

\displaystyle\epsilon_{d}\geq\epsilon_{d-1}\geq\cdots\geq\epsilon_{2}\geq\epsilon_{1}=0\;,

(3)

and the symbols
|\epsilon_{i}\rangle
to represent their associated eigenvectors, i.e

\displaystyle H|\epsilon_{j}\rangle=\epsilon_{j}|\epsilon_{j}\rangle.

(4)

Given hence
\rho^{(N)}\in\mathfrak{S}^{(N)}
a generic (possible correlated) quantum state of the compound we
write its ergotropy as
Allahverdyan et al. 2004

{{\cal E}(\rho^{(N)};H^{(N)})}:=E(\rho^{(N)};H^{(N)})-\min_{U}E(U\rho^{(N)}U^{\dagger};H^{(N)})\;,

(5)

where the minimization is performed over all transformation
U\in{\bf U}({\cal H}^{\otimes N})
of the unitary set on
{\cal H}^{\otimes N}
, and where

E(\rho^{(N)};H^{(N)}):=\Tr[\rho^{(N)}H^{(N)}]\;,

(6)

represents the energy expectation values on
\rho^{(N)}
which by construction is non-negative.

,

,
Q_{N}

\rho^{(N)}

The system Hamiltonian consists into a sum of homogeneous local terms
with no interactions.
Fixing the single-site density matrix
\rho
of the model
(see Eq. (

),
the degree of intra-site correlations of
\rho^{(N)}
are measured
in terms of the parameter

which defines the
state BLR value (
12
): for
m=1
,
\rho^{(N)}
corresponds to the factorized state
\rho^{\otimes N}
, while for
m
equal to the square of the rank
r

of
\rho
, we obtain the maximally entangled state
|\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho}\rangle
of Eq. (
14
).

In this paper we will be concerned on

\rho^{(N)}
belonging to the special subset
\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N)}
of

\mathfrak{S}^{(N)}
formed by density matrices
that are invariant under ciclic translations of the indexes sites, i.e.

\displaystyle\rho^{(N)}=T\rho^{(N)}T^{\dagger}\;,

(7)

where
T
is the transformation on
{\cal H}^{\otimes N}
which for all
\ell=1,\cdots,N
maps
Q_{\ell}
into
Q_{\ell\oplus 1}
(here

represents the sum modulus

).
Since by construction these states
are locally uniform, we can associate to each one of them the same
single-site reduced density matrix

\Tr_{Q_{1}\dots Q_{\ell-1}Q_{\ell+1}\dots Q_{N}}[\rho^{(N)}]=\rho\;,\quad\forall\ell\in[1,N]\;,

(8)

that allows us to express the global mean energy of the system as

E\left(\rho^{(N)};H^{(N)}\right)=NE(\rho;H)\;.

(9)

Factorized density matrices
\rho^{(N)}=\rho^{\otimes N}
are special instances of

\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N)}
which, due to the absence of
correlations among the various subsystems,
are fully characterized by their single-site counterpart (
8
).
A proper representation of
\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N)}
can be obtained
in terms of the
translationally invariant matrix product operators
(TI-MPO) formalism
Verstraete et al. 2004
.
The key observation here is that given

\{\ket{i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{N}}=|i_{1}\rangle\otimes|i_{2}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|i_{N}\rangle\}
an orthonormal basis of
{\cal H}^{\otimes N}
constructed from the elements of reference, single-site basis
\{\ket{i};i=1,\cdots,d\}
,
for each
\rho^{(N)}\in\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N)}

it is possible to associate a four rank tensor
\mathbb{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times d\times M\times M}

via the identity

\displaystyle\rho^{(N)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1},j_{1},\dots,i_{N},j_{N}}\ket{i_{1}\dots i_{N}}\bra{j_{1}\dots j_{N}}

(10)

\displaystyle\;\times\Tr[{A}^{i_{1},j_{1}}\dots{A}^{i_{N},j_{N}}]\;,

with
\{A^{i,j}|{i,j\in 1,\cdots d}\}
being

d^{2}
matrices of dimension
M\times M
that we use to represent
\mathbb{A}
– see Sec.
III.1
for an explicit derivation of this fact.
In this construction
the single-site counterpart (
8
) of
\rho^{(N)}
rewrites

\rho=\sum_{i,j}\ket{i}\bra{j}\;\Tr[{A}^{i,j}{\bar{A}}^{N-1}]\;,\qquad\bar{A}:=\sum_{i}{A}^{i,i},

(11)

while the value of the parameter
M
can be used to gauge the strength of the correlations among the various
sites.
For instance, as we shall see, setting
M=d^{2}
one can construct multi-site GHZ entangled pure states (see Sec.
III.3
), while to get factorized states
\rho^{\otimes N}

it is sufficient to have
M=1
. Notice however the correspondence between
\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N)}
and the set of tensors

is not
one to one. In particular determining whether the right-hand-side term of Eq. (
10
)
would produce a proper density matrix for a given

is a NP-hard problem, and it becomes undecidable

.
Most importantly for our purposes, multiple inequivalent choices of

, characterized by different values of

, can be assigned to each
\rho^{(N)}\in\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N)}

To remove this ambiguity

\mathfrak{br}[\rho^{(N)}]:=\min\left\{M\geq 1\;\middle|\;\exists\;\mathbb{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times d\times M\times M}\right.\\
\left.\mbox{s.t. Eq.~(\ref{MPO_rhoN}) holds true}\right\}\;.

(12)

This quantity is a proper functional of
\rho^{(N)}
: in particular,

Unfortunately determining the exact value of
\mathfrak{br}[\rho^{(N)}]
is a rather challenging task.
For our analysis however it will be sufficient to identify educated bounds for a special sub-set of states.

Equipped with the above definitions we can now classify the elements of
\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N)}

in terms of their local properties and of their BLR.
Specifically, given
\rho\in\mathfrak{S}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N)}(\rho):=\Big\{\rho^{(N)}\in\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N)}\;\big|\;

\displaystyle\qquad\Tr_{Q_{1}\dots Q_{\ell-1}Q_{\ell+1}\dots Q_{N}}[\rho^{(N)}]=\rho\;,\;\forall\ell\in[1,N]\Big\}\;,

\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N)}(\rho)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N,m)}(\rho):=\Big\{\rho^{(N)}\in\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N)}(\rho)\;\big|\;\mathfrak{br}[\rho^{(N)}]\leq m\Big\}\;.

Notice that for

,
\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N,1)}(\rho)
contains only the factorized

\rho^{\otimes N}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}^{(N,1)}_{T}(\rho)=\Big\{\rho^{\otimes N}\Big\}\;.

(13)

On the contrary it can be shown that given
\mbox{r}[\rho]
the rank of the local density matrix

, the set
\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N,\mbox{r}^{2}[\rho])}(\rho)
includes
the maximally entangled pure state

\displaystyle|\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho}\rangle:=\sum_{i=1}^{\mbox{r}[\rho]}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}\;|\lambda_{i}\dots\lambda_{i}\rangle\;,

(14)

with
\lambda_{i}>0
being the non-zero eigenvalues of
\rho

being the associated eigenvectors – an explicit proof of this fact is presented in Sec.
III.3
.
More generally for fixed
\rho
, the
\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N,m)}(\rho)
’s form a family of sets
of increasing size which, in the non trivial case
\mbox{r}[\rho]\geq 2
,
partition the collection of density matrices

fulfilling the local

8
) into groups of increasing multi-site correlation strength, i.e.

\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N,m-1)}(\rho)\subseteq\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N,m)}(\rho)\;,\quad\forall m\geq 2\;,

(15)

(for

, i.e. when the local state is a pure, Eq. (

) makes no sense

Our goal is to characterize
the maximum ergotropy value that can be achieved

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{(N,m)}_{\max}(\rho;H):=\max_{\rho^{(N)}\in\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N,m)}(\rho)}\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho^{(N)};H^{(N)})}{N}\;,

that represents the maximum value of the ergotropy per-site that one can extract from the system when it is in a joint state

\rho^{(N)}
of
\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N,m)}(\rho)

From (

) inherits the monotonic behaviour

\mathcal{E}^{(N,m-1)}_{\max}(\rho;H)\leq\mathcal{E}^{(N,m)}_{\max}(\rho;H)\;,\quad\forall m\geq 2\;.

(17)

Notice also that from (

) we trivially get

\mathcal{E}^{(N,1)}_{\max}(\rho;H)=\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\otimes N};H^{(N)})}{N}\;,

(18)

that represents a lower bond for all the other

\mathcal{E}^{(\infty,1)}_{\max}(\rho;H):=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{E}^{(N,1)}_{\max}(\rho;H)=\mathcal{E}_{\text{tot}}(\rho;H)\;.

An upper bound for

can instead be easily obtained from (

) and (

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{(N,m)}_{\max}(\rho;H):=E(\rho;H)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\min_{\rho^{(N)}\in\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N,m)}(\rho)}\max_{U}\frac{E(U\rho^{(N)}U^{\dagger};H^{(N)})}{N}\;.

Remembering then that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{(N,m)}_{\max}(\rho;H)\leq E(\rho;H)\;,\quad\forall m\;.

Observe next that, for fixed

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{(N,m)}_{\max}(\rho;H)

\displaystyle\quad\geq E(\rho;H)-\mathfrak{E}_{s_{\bf C}}-(2\sqrt{2.01C\eta}+\epsilon_{d}e^{-\frac{2N\eta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}})\;,

where
\epsilon_{d}
,
C
, and
\alpha
are as in
Proposition
1
while now

\displaystyle s_{\bf C}:=\ln\left\lceil{r/{m}}\;\right\rceil+N^{-1}\ln{m}\;.

(28)

The derivation of this result is reported in Sec.
IV.1.2
: interestingly enough this is done by focusing on a special class of TI-MPO states
(i.e. the set

\rho^{(N)}_{{\bf C}}
of Sec.
III.4.3
) which represent classically correlated (not entangled) density matrices of the system which can be chosen
to be diagonal in the energy eigenbasis.
Similarly to what done for
Proposition
1
the analysis simplifies when
taking the proper
N\rightarrow\infty
limit.
In this case from Eq. (
27
) we get the inequality

\mathcal{E}^{(\infty,m)}_{\max}(\rho;H)\geq\mathcal{E}^{(\infty,m)}_{\bf{C}}(\rho;H)\;,\qquad\forall m\leq\mbox{r}[\rho]\;,

(29)

which for

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{(\infty,m)}_{\bf{C}}(\rho;H)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle E(\rho;H)-\mathfrak{E}_{\ln\left\lceil{\mbox{r}[\rho]/{m}}\right\rceil}\;,

(30)

represents a clear improvement with respect to the constraint imposed by Eq. (
26
) – see Fig.
2
.
Notice in particular that since
\mathcal{E}^{(\infty,\mbox{r}[\rho])}_{\bf{C}}(\rho;H)=E(\rho;H)
,
Eq. (
29
) predicts a saturation of the upper bound (
21
) already for

m=\mbox{r}[\rho]
, i.e. for values of
m
which are much smaller than those suggested by (
22
). Even more interesting is the fact that such asymptotic saturation is attained with states (the density matrices
\rho^{(N)}_{{\bf C}}
)
which, as already mentioned, contain no quantum correlations and which can be
chosen to the diagonal with respect to the energy eigenbasis. The first property
of the
\rho^{(N)}_{{\bf C}}
’s implies that, at least in the asymptotic regime of infinitely many sites, classical correlations are sufficient to enable full work extraction from the system. The second property instead tell us that this can be done via incoherent ergotropy

Francica et al. 2020
;
Çakmak 2020
;
Touil et al. 2021
.

To clarify the asymptotic attainability of the upper bound (
21
) for
large
m
we present a final statement that applies for
BLR values that larger than or equal to
\mbox{r}[\rho]
:

Proposition 3

(High BLR regime)
.

Given
N
integer and
a single-site density matrix
\rho
of rank
r
, for
m\in\{r,\cdots,r^{2}\}
the following inequality holds

\mathcal{E}^{(N,m)}_{\max}(\rho;H)\geq E(\rho;H)-\frac{\Delta}{N}\;\;,

(31)

with
\Delta

being a positive constant term which depends upon the spectra of
\rho
and

H
.

The proof of this result which at variance with
Propositions
1
and
2

does not require to have
N
large,
is given in Sec.
IV.2
by focusing on the special class of (non pure) quantum states
\rho_{\bf A}^{(N)}
that correspond to proper mixtures of multi-sites GHZ states (see
Sec.
III.4.1
). In that section the quantity
\Delta
appearing in Eq. (
31
) is also identified with the gap between the single-site mean energy, and the
single-site ergotropy, i.e.

\displaystyle\Delta:={E}(\rho,H)-{\cal E}(\rho,H)\;.

(32)

A direct consequence of Propositions
3
is the universal identity

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{(\infty,m)}_{\max}(\rho;H)=E(\rho;H)\;,\quad m\geq\mbox{r}[\rho]\;,

(33)

which clearly
superseds (
25
) in the high BRL regime, confirming the saturation of the bound (
21
) observed in Eq. (
29
) for
m=\mbox{r}[\rho]
.

As evident e.g. from Fig.
2
,
Propositions
2
and
3
provide our best estimations for
\mathcal{E}^{(N,m)}_{\max}(\rho;H)
. For large enough
N
and
m\geq r
they are optimal since lead to the
exact evaluation of
\mathcal{E}^{(\infty,m)}_{\max}(\rho;H)
.
For small
m
on the contrary
the constraint posed by Eq. (
27
) is certainly suboptimal:
to see this observe for instance that for
m=1
we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{(\infty,1)}_{\bf{C}}(\rho;H)=E(\rho;H)-\mathfrak{E}_{\ln\left\lceil{\mbox{r}[\rho]}\right\rceil}\;,

(34)

which is clearly not larger than the exact value (
19
) due the fact that

\mathfrak{E}_{\ln\left\lceil{\mbox{r}[\rho]}\right\rceil}
is always larger than or equal to the
thermal energy
\mathfrak{E}_{S(\rho)}
associated with the Gibbs state that
is iso-entropic with
\rho
(same considerations apply of course for
\mathcal{E}^{(\infty,1)}_{\bf{B}}(\rho;H)
).
An improvement for small
m
can however be obtained by invoking an heuristic (not rigorous) argument that we shall discuss in
Remark 3
of Sec.
IV.1.2
.
This suggests that for
m\ll r

it should be possible to identify a special sub-class of states
\rho^{(N)}_{{\bf C}}
we employed in the derivation of
Proposition
2
which allow us to replace
s_{\bf C}
in Eq. (
27
) with the improved value

\displaystyle\left.s_{\bf C}\right|_{\rm(heu)}:=S(\rho)-\frac{N-1}{N}\ln m\;,

(35)

and (
34
) with

\left.\mathcal{E}^{(\infty,1)}_{\bf{C}}(\rho;H)\right|_{\rm(heu)}=E(\rho;H)-\mathfrak{E}_{S(\rho)}=\mathcal{E}_{\text{tot}}(\rho;H)\;.

(36)

II.1.2
Lower bound for
\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\otimes N};H^{(N)})}{N}

As mentioned in the introduction a secondary result our analysis is
to set on rigorous ground of the typicality argument first formulated by Alicki and Fannes
Alicki and Fannes 2013
for the maximum work extractable for a set of many identical quantum systems. Specifically we show that

.

Given
\eta\in]0,1[
and a single-site density matrix
\rho
, for
N
sufficiently large the following inequality holds true

\frac{{\cal E}(\rho^{\otimes N};H^{(N)})}{N}\geq\mathcal{E}_{\text{tot}}(\rho;H)-(2\sqrt{2.01C\eta}+\epsilon_{d}e^{-\frac{2N\eta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}})\;,

(37)

with
\epsilon_{d}
,
C
, and
\alpha
as in
Proposition
1
.

The proof of this result can be seen as a special instance of

Proposition
1
and it
is given in Sec.
IV.3
.

III
TI-MPO representation of translationally invariant states

This section is dedicated to clarify some technical aspects of the TI-MPO representation and to present explicit examples of elements of
\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N,m)}(\rho)
to be used
to construct our lower bounds for
\mathcal{E}^{(N,m)}_{\max}(\rho;H)
.
First of all in Sec.
III.1
we formally show that any translational invariant state of the
N
sites,
admits a TI-MPO representation (
10
) for a proper choice of the tensor
\mathbb{A}
;
then in Sec.
III.2
we show that the definition of the BLR
\mathfrak{br}[\rho^{(N)}]
defined in Eq. (
12
) is unaffected by the choice of the local basis
\{\ket{i};i=1,\cdots,d\}
entering in the
TI-MPO representation (
10
);
in Sec.
III.3
we prove instead that the
GHZ-like states of the form (
14
) belong to the set
\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N,\mbox{r}^{2}[\rho])}(\rho)
; finally in Sec.
III.4
we provide an explicit TI-MPO representation for three different families of correlated states in
\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N)}(\rho)
.

III.1
Existence of TI-MPO representation

In order to show that the elements
\rho^{(N)}
of
\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N)}
can be expressed in the
TI-MPO representation of Eq. (
10
), let start from a not necessarily TI - MPO representation of such an element,
i.e.

\displaystyle\rho^{(N)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1},j_{1},\dots,i_{N},j_{N}}\ket{i_{1}\dots i_{N}}\bra{j_{1}\dots j_{N}}

(38)

\displaystyle\;\times\Tr[{A}_{1}^{i_{1},j_{1}}\dots{A}_{N}^{i_{N},j_{N}}]\;,

where for
k=1,\cdots,N
,
\{A_{k}^{i,j}|{i,j\in 1,\cdots d}\}
are a set of
d^{2}
,
\bar{M}\times\bar{M}
matrices associated
to the
k
-th site, which always exists for
\bar{M}
sufficiently large
Parker et al. 2020
.
Now exploiting the translational invariance property (
7
) and the ciclicity of the trace, it follows that
for all
\ell=0,\cdots,N-1
we can also write

\displaystyle\rho^{(N)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1},j_{1},\dots,i_{N},j_{N}}\ket{i_{1}\dots i_{N}}\bra{j_{1}\dots j_{N}}

(39)

\displaystyle\;\times\Tr[{A}_{1\oplus\ell}^{i_{1},j_{1}}\dots{A}_{N\oplus\ell}^{i_{N},j_{N}}]\;,

with
\oplus
representing the sum modulus
N
. Hence we get

\displaystyle\rho^{(N)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1},j_{1},\dots,i_{N},j_{N}}\ket{i_{1}\dots i_{N}}\bra{j_{1}\dots j_{N}}

(40)

\displaystyle\;\times\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-1}\Tr[{A}_{1\oplus\ell}^{i_{1},j_{1}}\dots{A}_{N\oplus\ell}^{i_{N},j_{N}}]\;,

which is of the form Eq. (
10
) by identifying
M=\bar{M}N
and taking the matrices
{A}^{i,j}
as

\displaystyle A^{i,j}=\frac{1}{{N}^{1/N}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}{A}_{\ell}^{i,j}\otimes|v_{\ell}\rangle\langle v_{\ell\oplus 1}|\;,

(41)

with
\{|v_{\ell}\rangle;\ell=1,\cdots,N-1\}
being orthonormal vectors of an auxiliary
N
-dimensional vector space (hereafter, in the writing of the MPO matrices we adopt the Dirac notation).

III.2
Invariance of the BLR with respect to the choice of the local basis

Here we show that the choice of the local basis
\{|i\rangle\}
entering the TI-MPO representation (
10
)
does not affect the definition
BLR (
12
) of a state
\rho^{(N)}\in\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N)}
.
To see this consider
\{\ket{\phi_{i}};i=1,\cdots,d\}
a new basis of
{\cal H}
connected to

\{\ket{i};i=1,\cdots,d\}
via a unitary transformation
U
, i.e.

\displaystyle\ket{\phi_{i}}=U\ket{i}\;,\forall i=1,\cdots,d\;.

(42)

Replacing this into (
10
) we get

\displaystyle\rho^{(N)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1},j_{1},\dots,i_{N},j_{N}}U^{{\dagger}\otimes N}\ket{\phi_{i_{1}}\dots\phi_{i_{N}}}\bra{\phi_{j_{1}}\dots\phi_{j_{N}}}U^{\otimes N}

(43)

\displaystyle\;\times\Tr[{A}^{i_{1},j_{1}}\dots{A}^{i_{N},j_{N}}]\;,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i^{\prime}_{1},j^{\prime}_{1},\dots,i^{\prime}_{N},j^{\prime}_{N}}\ket{\phi_{i^{\prime}_{1}}\dots\phi_{i^{\prime}_{N}}}\bra{\phi_{j^{\prime}_{1}}\dots\phi_{j^{\prime}_{N}}}

\displaystyle\;\times\Tr[{B}^{i^{\prime}_{1},j^{\prime}_{1}}\dots{B}^{i^{\prime}_{N},j^{\prime}_{N}}]\;,

where for
i^{\prime},j^{\prime}=1,\cdots,d

\displaystyle{B}^{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\langle\phi_{i^{\prime}}|U^{\dagger}|\phi_{i}\rangle{A}^{i,j}\langle\phi_{j}|U^{\dagger}|\phi_{j^{\prime}}\rangle

(44)

forming a new set of
d^{2}
,
M\times M
matrices. Equation (
44
) creates a one-to-one correspondence between
the TI-MPO representations of
\rho^{(N)}
constructed with the basis
\{\ket{\phi_{i}};i=1,\cdots,d\}
with bond link
M
, and the
TI-MPO representations associated
with the basis
\{\ket{i};i=1,\cdots,d\}
with the same bond link: accordingly
the BLR (
12
) computed with respect to those two basis will produce the same value.
Notice in particular that this allows us to identify
\ket{i}
appearing in (
10
)
with the eigenvectors
|\lambda_{i}\rangle
of the local density matrix
\rho
of Eq. (
8
), a choice that, via Eq. (
11
), will allow us to identify the
associated eigenvalues as

\displaystyle\Tr[{A}^{i,j}{\bar{A}}^{N-1}]=\lambda_{i}\;\delta_{i,j}\;.

(45)

III.3
TI-MPO representation for GHZ-like states

Consider
\rho
a single-site density matrix of rank
\mbox{r}[\rho]

\displaystyle\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{\mbox{r}[\rho]}\lambda_{i}|\lambda_{i}\rangle\langle\lambda_{i}|\;,

(46)

with
\{|\lambda_{i}\rangle,i=1,\cdots,d\}
its eigenvectors, and
\{\lambda_{i};i=1,\cdots,\mbox{r}[\rho]\}

the corresponding non-zero eigenvalues (for
i\geq\mbox{r}[\rho]+1
of course
\rho|\lambda_{i}\rangle=0
).
Consider also the associated
GHZ-like state (
14
)
which is explicitly translationally invariant. Here we prove that
|\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho}\rangle

belongs to the set
\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N,\mbox{r}^{2}[\rho])}(\rho)
, i.e.

\displaystyle|\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho}\rangle\langle\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho}|\in\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N,\mbox{r}^{2}[\rho])}(\rho)\;,

(47)

or equivalently that its
BLR (
12
) is smaller or equal than
\mbox{r}^{2}[\rho]
, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathfrak{br}\left[|\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho}\rangle\langle\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho}|\right]\leq\mbox{r}^{2}[\rho]\;.

(48)

To show this fact it is sufficient to exhibit a TI-MPO representation (
10
) of

|\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho}\rangle\langle\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho}|
with a
tensor
\mathbb{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times d\times M\times M}
characterized by
M=\mbox{r}^{2}[\rho]
.
Exploiting the observation of Sec.
III.2
we shall focus on representations
which uses the eigenvectors
\{|\lambda_{i}\rangle;i=1,\cdots,d\}
of
\rho
as local basis
\{|i\rangle;i=1,\cdots,d\}
.
Then we notice that the solution is provided by the identity

\displaystyle|\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho}\rangle\langle\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{\mbox{r}[\rho]}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}\;\ket{\lambda_{i}\dots\lambda_{i}}\bra{\lambda_{j}\dots\lambda_{j}}

(49)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1},j_{1},\dots,i_{N},j_{N}}\ket{\lambda_{i_{1}}\dots\lambda_{i_{N}}}\bra{\lambda_{j_{1}}\dots\lambda_{j_{N}}}

\displaystyle\;\times\Tr[{A}^{i_{1},j_{1}}\dots{A}^{i_{N},j_{N}}]\;,

where defining
\{|v_{i}\rangle;i=1,\cdots,\mbox{r}[\rho]\}
an orthonormal basis of an auxiliary vector space
{\cal H}_{A}
of dimension
\mbox{r}[\rho]
, we took
{A}^{i,j}
to be the
\mbox{r}^{2}[\rho]\times\mbox{r}^{2}[\rho]
matrices

\displaystyle{A}^{i,j}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left({\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2N}}\;|v_{i}\rangle\langle v_{i}|\otimes|v_{j}\rangle\langle v_{j}|\;,&\forall i,j\leq\mbox{r}[\rho]\;,\\
\\
0\;,&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

We remark that the argument we presented here doesn’t ensure that

\mathfrak{br}[|\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho}\rangle\langle\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho}|]
coincides with
\mbox{r}^{2}[\rho]
, but
(as implied by Eq. (
48
)) only that
the former is an upper bound for the latter. In other words, formally speaking, we cannot exclude that

|\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho}\rangle\langle\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho}|
belongs to
\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N,m)}(\rho)
for same
m<\mbox{r}^{2}[\rho]
(we conjecture
however that this is not the case).

III.4
TI-MPO representation of three families of correlated states of

\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N)}(\rho)

In this section we present an explicit TI-MPO representation for three different families of states in
\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N)}(\rho)
which, as pictorially depicted in Fig.
3
, allows us span various BLR values.
All these examples are constructed starting from the set of the non-null
eigenvalues of the single-site density matrix
\rho
of the model, i.e.

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{K}}[\rho]:=\{\lambda_{i};i=1,\cdots,\mbox{r}[\rho]\}\;,

(54)

with
\lambda_{i}
and
\mbox{r}[\rho]
defined as in Eq. (
46
), and dividing it into smaller, non-empty subsets. Specifically, given

L
integer greater than or equal to
1
and no larger than
\mbox{r}[\rho]
, we consider a collection

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{P}}:=\{{\mathfrak{K}}_{1},{\mathfrak{K}}_{2},\cdots,{\mathfrak{K}}_{L}\}\;,

(55)

of
L
non-overlapping, non-null subsets of
{\mathfrak{K}}[\rho]
, which provides a partition of such set, i.e.

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{K}}_{\ell}\cap{\mathfrak{K}}_{\ell^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\O\;,\qquad\forall\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}\;,

(56)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{M}{\mathfrak{K}}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{K}}[\rho]\;.

(57)

In what follow we shall use the symbol
\#{\mathfrak{K}}_{\ell}
to represent the cardinality of

{\mathfrak{K}}_{\ell}
and indicate with
\#_{\max}
and
|{\mathfrak{P}}|^{(p)}

their maximum value and the sum of their
p
-powers, i.e. the quantities

\displaystyle\#_{\max}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\max_{\ell=1,\cdots,L}\left(\#{\mathfrak{K}}_{\ell}\right)\;,

(58)

\displaystyle|{\mathfrak{P}}|^{(p)}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}(\#{\mathfrak{K}}_{\ell})^{p}\;,

(59)

which are related by the inequality

\displaystyle L\leq|{\mathfrak{P}}|^{(p)}\leq(\#_{\max})^{p}L\;.

(60)

By construction the
\#{\mathfrak{K}}_{\ell}
’s are all greater than or equal to
1
and
sum up to the rank of
\rho
, i.e.

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\#{\mathfrak{K}}_{\ell}=\mbox{r}[\rho]\;,

(61)

which implies

\displaystyle\frac{\mbox{r}[\rho]}{L}\leq

\displaystyle\#_{\max}

\displaystyle\leq\mbox{r}[\rho]-L+1\;,

(62)

\displaystyle\mbox{r}[\rho]\leq

\displaystyle|{\mathfrak{P}}|^{(p)}

\displaystyle\leq\mbox{r}^{p}[\rho]\;.

(63)

Notice that if
L
is an exact divisor of
\mbox{r}[\rho]

one can force all the subsets
\mathfrak{K}_{\ell}
to have the same cardinality, i.e.

\displaystyle\#{\mathfrak{K}}_{\ell}={\mbox{r}[\rho]}/{L}\;,\qquad\forall\ell\in 1,\cdots,L\;,

corresponding to an exact saturation of the lower bound in (
62

when this happens one gets

{\mathfrak{P}}

uniform

{\mathfrak{K}}[\rho]

In case
L
is not an exact divisor of
\mbox{r}[\rho]
we say instead that
{\mathfrak{P}}
is an
almost uniform
partition of
{\mathfrak{K}}[\rho]
if

\displaystyle\#_{\max}=\left\lceil{\mbox{r}[\rho]/L}\right\rceil\;,

(65)

leading to
|{\mathfrak{P}}|^{(p)}\leq(\left\lceil{\mbox{r}[\rho]/L}\right\rceil)^{p}L
.

Next we
introduce a labelling for the elements of each individual
subsets of
{\mathfrak{P}}
; specifically, given
\ell=1,\cdots,L
, we indicate with

\lambda_{k,\ell}
the
k
-th element of

{\mathfrak{K}}_{\ell}
, so that

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{K}}_{\ell}=\{\lambda_{k,\ell};k=1,\cdots,\#{\mathfrak{K}}_{\ell}\}\;.

(66)

Notice that, thanks to (
56
) and (
57

for each

there is a unique choice of the indexes

and

that identifies the
i
-th eigenvalue
of
\rho

k
element of the subset
{\mathfrak{K}}_{\ell}
: in the following we shall exploit this one-to-one correspondence indicating with
the symbols

and

those special values via the mapping

\displaystyle i\longmapsto k^{[i]},\ell^{[i]}\quad\mbox{s.t.}\quad\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{k^{[i]},\ell^{[i]}}\;.

(67)

Finally for all
\ell=1,\cdots,L

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}_{\ell}:=\sum_{k=1}^{\#{\mathfrak{K}}_{\ell}}\lambda_{k,\ell}\;,

(68)

to gauge the statistical

\mathfrak{K}_{\ell}
: these terms are clearly positive and

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\mathscr{S}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1\;.

(69)

\displaystyle\langle\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho_{\ell}}|\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}}\rangle=\delta_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\;,

(73)

and provide a decomposition of the state (

) via the identity

\displaystyle|\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho}\rangle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\sqrt{\mathscr{S}_{\ell}}\;|\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho_{\ell}}\rangle\;.

(74)

Furthermore by direct inspection one can easily verify that the convex convolution of rank
L
obtained by mixing the
vectors
|\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho_{\ell}}\rangle
with statistical weight
\mathscr{S}_{\ell}
, i.e. the density matrix

\displaystyle\rho^{(N)}_{\bf A}:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\mathscr{S}_{\ell}\;|\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho_{\ell}}\rangle\langle\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho_{\ell}}|\;,

(75)

is an element of
\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N)}(\rho)
, i.e. it admits
\rho
of Eq. (
46
) as single-site reduced density matrix (ultimately this is a consequence of the identity
\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\mathscr{S}_{\ell}\rho_{\ell}=\rho
), and has a rank equal to
L
as reported in Table
1
.
Notice in particular that as

varies from the extremal cases

and
L=\mbox{r}[\rho]

the states (

) interpolate from the GHZ-like configuration to its classically correlated counterpart, i.e.

\displaystyle\rho^{(N)}_{{\bf A}}\Big|_{L=1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho}\rangle\langle\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho}|\;,

(76)

\displaystyle\rho^{(N)}_{{\bf A}}\Big|_{L=\mbox{r}[\rho]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{(N)}_{\rm cc}:=\sum_{i=1}^{\mbox{r}[\rho]}\lambda_{i}\ket{\lambda_{i}\dots\lambda_{i}}\bra{\lambda_{i}\dots\lambda_{i}},

(77)

(see Fig.

We now show that the BLR of
\rho^{(N)}_{\bf A}
fulfils the inequality

\displaystyle\mathfrak{br}[\rho^{(N)}_{\bf A}]\leq|{\mathfrak{P}}|^{(2)}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}(\#{\mathfrak{K}}_{\ell})^{2}\;,

(78)

or equivalently that

\displaystyle\rho^{(N)}_{\bf A}\in\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N,|{\mathfrak{P}}|^{(2)})}(\rho)\;.

(79)

For the trivial choice

where
{\mathfrak{P}}
contains only
{\mathfrak{K}}[\rho]
as unique element,

corresponds to the GHZ-like state (

) which purifies
\rho
,
|{\mathfrak{P}}|^{(2)}=\mbox{r}^{2}[\rho]
, and Eq. (
79
) reduces to the property (
47
) which we proved in the previous section.
To show that (

) holds also for

we adopt a similar scheme and present an explicit TI-MPO decomposition

\displaystyle\rho^{(N)}_{\bf A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1},j_{1},\dots,i_{N},j_{N}}\ket{\lambda_{i_{1}}\dots\lambda_{i_{N}}}\bra{\lambda_{j_{1}}\dots\lambda_{j_{N}}}

(80)

\displaystyle\;\times\Tr[{A}^{i_{1},j_{1}}_{{\bf A}}\dots{A}^{i_{N},j_{N}}_{{\bf A}}]\;,

that employs the eigenvectors
\{|\lambda_{i}\rangle;i=1,\cdots,d\}
of
\rho
as local basis
and which explicitly uses matrices
|{\mathfrak{P}}|^{(2)}\times|{\mathfrak{P}}|^{(2)}
matrices
{A}^{i,j}_{{\bf A}}
.
For this purpose observe first that given
\ell=1,\cdots,L
, the state
|\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho_{\ell}}\rangle
admits
a TI-MPO representation

\displaystyle|\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho_{\ell}}\rangle\langle\mbox{GHZ}^{(N)}_{\rho_{\ell}}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1},j_{1},\dots,i_{N},j_{N}}\ket{\lambda_{i_{1}}\dots\lambda_{i_{N}}}\bra{\lambda_{j_{1}}\dots\lambda_{j_{N}}}

(81)

\displaystyle\;\times\Tr[{A}_{\ell}^{i_{1},j_{1}}\dots{A}_{\ell}^{i_{N},j_{N}}]\;,

with

{A}_{\ell}^{i,j}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left({\frac{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}{\mathscr{S}^{2}_{\ell}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2N}}\!\!|v_{k^{[i]}}^{(\ell)}\rangle\langle v_{k^{[i]}}^{(\ell)}|\otimes|v_{k^{[j]}}^{(\ell)}\rangle\langle v_{k^{[j]}}^{(\ell)}|,&\forall\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}\in{\mathfrak{K}}_{\ell},\\
\\
\\
\\
0\;,&\mbox{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

(82)

where
k^{[i]}
and
k^{[j]}
are defined by the mapping (
67
), and where

form an orthonormal basis of an auxiliary vector

{\cal H}_{\ell}
of dimension
\mbox{r}[\rho_{\ell}]
(notice that the

’s operate on two copies of

{\cal H}_{\ell}
, i.e. on

{\cal H}_{\ell}^{\otimes 2}
, and are hence

).
Equation (
80

{A}^{i,j}_{{\bf A}}

\ell

of the matrices (

admits

as an orthonormal set and
has dimension

\displaystyle\mbox{dim}[{\cal H}_{{\bf A}}]=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\mbox{dim}^{2}[{\cal H}_{\ell}]=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}(\#{\mathfrak{K}}_{\ell})^{2}=|{\mathfrak{P}}|^{(2)}\;.

(83)

On such space we then introduce the matrices

\displaystyle{A}^{i,j}_{{\bf A}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\ell=1}^{L}{\mathscr{S}_{\ell}}^{\tfrac{1}{N}}\;{A}_{\ell}^{i,j}\;,

(84)

which can be equivalently expressed as

\displaystyle{A}^{i,j}_{{\bf A}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sqrt{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}})^{1/N}\;\delta_{\ell^{[i]},\ell^{[j]}}

(85)

\displaystyle\times|v_{k^{[i]}}^{(\ell^{[i]})}\rangle\langle v_{k^{[i]}}^{(\ell^{[i]})}|\otimes|v_{k^{[j]}}^{(\ell^{[j]})}\rangle\langle v_{k^{[j]}}^{(\ell^{[j]})}|\;,

with
\ell^{[i]}
and
\ell^{[j]}

The identity (

\Tr[{A}^{i_{1},j_{1}}_{{\bf A}}\dots{A}^{i_{N},j_{N}}_{{\bf A}}]=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\mathscr{S}_{\ell}\Tr[{A}^{i_{1},j_{1}}_{\ell}\dots{A}^{i_{N},j_{N}}_{\ell}]\;,

(86)

and from (
81
) and (

).

The second family of elements of

We dub these states

.

The starting point of the analysis is again the partition (
55
)
of the non-null eigenvalues of
\rho

which satisfies the properties (
56
) and (
57
).
For the sake of simplicity we report here the explicit derivation for the special case
in which

is an exact divisor of
\mbox{r}[\rho]
and

{\mathfrak{P}}
is an uniform partition of
{\mathfrak{K}}[\rho]
, so that Eq. (
64
) holds true:
the extension of this construction to the general case is given in Appendix

.
Introduce next
an auxiliary Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{\bf B}
of dimension
L
, with an orthonormal basis
\left\{\ket{v_{\ell}};\ell=1,\cdots,L\right\}
and
replace the matrices

A^{ij}_{\bf{A}}
of Eq. (

L^{2}\times L^{2}

\displaystyle A^{ij}_{\bf{B}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}\left(\mathscr{S}_{{\ell^{[i]}}}\mathscr{S}_{{\ell^{[j]}}}\right)^{\frac{1-N}{2N}}\;\delta_{k^{[i]},k^{[j]}}

(87)

\displaystyle\times\ket{v_{\ell^{[i]}}}\bra{v_{\ell^{[i]}}}\otimes\ket{v_{\ell^{[j]}}}\bra{v_{\ell^{[j]}}}\;,

where
k^{[i]}
,
\ell^{[i]}
and
\mathscr{S}_{\ell}
defined as in Eqs. (
67
) and (
68
), respectively.
We now define the block-wise purified density operator
\rho^{(N)}_{{\bf B}}
via the TI-MPO representation

\displaystyle\rho^{(N)}_{{\bf B}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1},j_{1},\dots,i_{N},j_{N}}\ket{\lambda_{i_{1}}\dots\lambda_{i_{N}}}\bra{\lambda_{j_{1}}\dots\lambda_{j_{N}}}

(88)

\displaystyle\;\times\Tr[{A}^{i_{1},j_{1}}_{{\bf B}}\dots{A}^{i_{N},j_{N}}_{{\bf B}}]\;.

By construction we get that
\rho^{(N)}_{{\bf B}}

has non-zero entries

\displaystyle\bra{\lambda_{j_{1}}\lambda_{j_{2}}\dots\lambda_{j_{N}}}\rho^{(N)}_{{\bf B}}\ket{\lambda_{i_{1}}\lambda_{i_{2}}\dots\lambda_{i_{N}}}

(89)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{i_{1}}\lambda_{i_{2}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{N}}}{\mathscr{S}_{\ell^{[i_{1}]}}^{N-1}}}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{j_{1}}\lambda_{j_{2}}\cdots\lambda_{j_{N}}}{\mathscr{S}_{\ell^{[j_{1}]}}^{N-1}}}\;,

if the following conditions are true:

•

\ell^{[i_{1}]}=\ell^{[i_{2}]}=\cdots=\ell^{[i_{N}]}

•

\ell^{[j_{1}]}=\ell^{[j_{2}]}=\cdots=\ell^{[j_{N}]}
,

•

while

\bra{\lambda_{j_{1}}\lambda_{j_{2}}\dots\lambda_{j_{N}}}\rho^{(N)}_{{\bf B}}\ket{\lambda_{i_{1}}\lambda_{i_{2}}\dots\lambda_{i_{N}}}=0\;,

(90)

otherwise.
Making use of Eq. (
45
) and the fact that in the present case we have

\bar{A}_{\bf B}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\mathscr{S}_{{\ell}}^{\frac{1}{N}}\ket{v_{\ell}}\bra{v_{\ell}}\otimes\ket{v_{\ell}}\bra{v_{\ell}}
, it is not difficult to verify that

satisfies the partial trace condition (
8
): hence
we can claim that
\rho^{(N)}_{{\bf B}}
is an element of
\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N,L^{2})}(\rho)
, i.e.

\displaystyle\rho^{(N)}_{{\bf B}}\in\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N,L^{2})}(\rho)\;.

(91)

A more explicit form for the density matrix
\rho^{(N)}_{{\bf B}}
can be obtained
exploiting correspondence (
67
) and the fact that, thanks to the choice
of working with uniform partitions, the index

of
{\lambda}_{k_{a},\ell}
run from
1
to
\mbox{r}[\rho]/L
irrespectively from the value of
\ell
.
This leads to

\rho^{(N)}_{{\bf B}}=\sum_{\vec{k}}\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}=1}^{L}\sqrt{\tfrac{{\lambda}^{(N)}_{\vec{k},\ell}}{\mathscr{S}_{\ell}^{N-1}}}\sqrt{\tfrac{{\lambda}^{(N)}_{\vec{k},\ell^{\prime}}}{\mathscr{S}_{\ell^{\prime}}^{N-1}}}\ket{{\lambda}^{(N)}_{\vec{k},\ell}}\bra{{\lambda}^{(N)}_{\vec{k},\ell^{\prime}}}\;,

(92)

where the first sum runs over the

N
-uple
\vec{k}:=\left(k_{1},k_{2},\dots k_{N}\right)
, and where we used the short-hand notation

\displaystyle{\lambda}^{(N)}_{\vec{k},\ell}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\prod_{a=1}^{N}{\lambda}_{k_{a},\ell}\;,

(93)

\displaystyle\ket{\lambda^{(N)}_{\vec{k},\ell}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\ket{\lambda_{k_{1},\ell}\;\lambda_{k_{2},\ell}\;\dots\;\lambda_{k_{N},\ell}}\;.

(94)

IV.1
Proof of Propositions
1
and
2

Recalling the definition
\mathfrak{E}_{s}
of the thermal energy associated with the single-site entropy
s
given in Eq. (
B
) and
of the corresponding effective heat capacity
\mathfrak{C}_{s}
,
we start by proving the following statement:

Proposition 4
.

For any single-site entropy value
s_{0}>0
there exist
\eta_{\star}\in\;]0,1[
and a function

N_{\star}(\eta)
mapping
\eta\in\;]0,\eta_{\star}[
into

\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
such that given
\eta\in\;]0,\eta_{\star}[
and
N\geq N_{\star}(\eta)
, any state
\rho^{(N)}\in\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N)}
admitting a eigenvalue subset

{\mathfrak{T}}^{(N)}

that has cardinality and associated total
population satisfying the constraints

\displaystyle\#{\mathfrak{T}}^{(N)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{N\left(s_{0}+\eta\right)}\;,

(111)

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda_{i}^{(N)}\in{\mathfrak{T}}^{(N)}}\lambda_{i}^{(N)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1-e^{-\frac{2N\eta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}}\;,

(112)

for some given
\alpha>0
,
fulfils the following inequality

\displaystyle\frac{{\cal E}(\rho^{(N)};H^{(N)})}{N}

(113)

\displaystyle\quad\geq E(\rho;H)-\mathfrak{E}_{s_{0}}-(2\sqrt{2.01\mathfrak{C}_{s_{0}}\eta}+\epsilon_{d}e^{-\frac{2N\eta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}})\;,

where
\epsilon_{d}
is the largest eigenvalue of the single-site Hamiltonian
H
.

Proof:–

Given
s_{0}>0
and
\alpha>0
, assume that

\rho^{(N)}
is an element of
\mathfrak{S}_{T}^{(N)}
which
admits an subset

{\mathfrak{T}}^{(N)}
of eigenvalues fulfilling the conditions (
111
) and (
112
) for some
\eta>0
whose value will be determined in the following. Since the ergotropy, as defined in Eq. (
157
), is a maximum over the set of unitary transformation, to prove statement of the proposition
it would be sufficient to show an unitary transformation
U_{\star}\in{\bf U}({\cal H}^{\otimes N})
such that the associated mean energy difference

\displaystyle W_{U_{\star}}(\rho^{(N)};H^{(N)}):=E(\rho^{(N)};H^{(N)})-E(U_{\star}\rho^{(N)}U^{\dagger}_{\star};H^{(N)})

is greater than the right-hand side of (
113
). As suitable candidates for

U_{\star}
we consider a unitary transformation which exchanges the eigenvectors associated with the elements of the set
{\mathfrak{T}}^{(N)}
with a subset of the eigenvectors corresponding to the elements of the sets

{\mathfrak{A}_{\xi}^{(N)}}
of
Lemma
2
associated to the same value of
s_{0}

and to a proper choice of
\xi
.
This construction might be regarded as a more general case of the one proposed in
Perarnau-Llobet and Uzdin 2019
, where it is applied for reducing the fluctuations in the extracted work - while we are interested in the mean values
W_{U_{*}}\left(\rho^{\otimes N};H^{(N)}\right)
.
The transformation we are targeting clearly can be identified if in
{\mathfrak{T}}^{(N)}
there are more elements than in
{\mathfrak{A}^{(N)}}
, i.e.

\#{\mathfrak{A}_{\xi}^{(N)}}\geq\#{\mathfrak{T}}^{(N)}\;.

(115)

The hypothesis (
112
) gives us the upper bound for the cardinality of
{\mathfrak{T}}^{(N)}
, while

Lemma
2
provides the lower bound (
201
) for the cardinality of
{\mathfrak{A}_{\xi}^{(N)}}
providing that the selected
\xi
and
N

fulfils the constraints

\displaystyle\xi\leq\xi_{*}\;,\qquad N\geq N_{*}(\xi)\;,

(116)

with
\xi_{*}
and
N_{*}(\xi)
dependent upon
s_{0}
and the structure of single-site Hamiltonian – see

Remark 4
below
Lemma
2
in Appendix
B.1
.
Combining these facts, we can see that the requirement (

V
Conclusions

We derived some analytic lower bound for the work that, in the best case, can be extracted with unitary transformations from a translationally invariant correlated many-body system, using as a measure of correlation the minimum bond link rank (BLR) necessary to represent the state as a matrix product operator.
When the number
N
of copies of the system is finite, non-classical correlations are required to extract as work the full energy of the system. However, in the macroscopic limit
N\to\infty
, we found that this quantum feature disappears, and that it is possible to create many-body states with classically correlated state which have a relative low BRL (equal at most to to the rank
r
of the local state, out of a maximum of
r^{2}
).
Our bounds do not depend on the entropy
S(\rho)
on the state, so they are worse for states of low entropy. However, heuristic consideration suggest that, at least for small correlations strengths, the bounds can be improved with an explicit dependence on
S(\rho)
.

We conjecture that, for
N\geq 3
, the BLR of the family of states employed in our analysis is equal to the upper bounds that we found by explicit construction. If true, this could allow to derive also upper bound for the ergotropy of a translationally invariant state with a given correlation strength.
Another possible improvement of our work could be repeating the analysis with a measure of correlation more sophisticated the TI-MPO bond link rank, like some form of correlation entropy
Schindler et al. 2020
;
Rolandi and Wilming 2020
.

\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}\leq N(\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{V}]+\varepsilon)\right)\geq 1-e^{-\frac{2N\varepsilon^{2}}{(b-a)^{2}}}\;.

(168)

In this paper we will apply the following useful consequence of the Chernoff inequality:

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}=\{x_{i}\}_{i}
be a finite collection of positive real numbers
x_{i}\in]0,1]
, such that
\sum_{i}x_{i}=1
, and
-\sum_{i}x_{i}\ln x_{i}=s_{0}
.
Let
\mathfrak{X}^{\otimes N}
denote the set of
N
-ples
\vec{x}:=(x_{i_{1}},\dots x_{i_{N}})
. Then, for any
\eta>0
, the subset
{\mathfrak{X}}^{(N)}\subseteq\mathfrak{X}^{\otimes N}
defined by

{\mathfrak{X}}^{(N)}:=\left\{\vec{x}=(x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{N}})\middle|\prod_{k=1}^{N}x_{i_{k}}\geq e^{-N(s_{0}+\eta)}\right\}\;,

(169)

has cardinality

\displaystyle\#{\mathfrak{X}}^{(N)}\leq e^{N(s_{0}+\eta)}\;,

(170)

and satisfies the property

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{x}\in{\mathfrak{X}^{(N)}}}\prod_{k=1}^{N}x_{i_{k}}\geq 1-e^{-2N\eta^{2}/\alpha^{2}}\;,

(171)

where

\alpha:=\max_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\ln x-\min_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\ln x=\ln\left(x_{max}/x_{min}\right)\;.

(172)

Proof:

The bound (
170
) on the cardinality follows trivially from the definition (
169
) and the fact that

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{x}\in{\mathfrak{X}^{(N)}}}\prod_{k=1}^{N}x_{i_{k}}\leq\sum_{\vec{x}\in\mathfrak{X}^{\otimes N}}\prod_{k=1}^{N}x_{i_{k}}=1\;.

(173)

To prove (
171
)
it is sufficient to notice that the quantity

-\ln\prod_{k=1}^{N}x_{i_{k}}=-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\ln x_{i_{k}}

can be regarded as the sum of
N
extractions of the random variable
X:=-\ln x
, that is, of the variable that with probability
x_{i}
takes the value
X_{i}=-\ln x_{i}
.
Then the thesis (
171
) follows straightforwardly from (
168
).

\square

Appendix B
Gibbs states

The Gibbs states of a single-site of our model are the density matrices

\omega_{\beta}:=\frac{e^{-\beta H}}{Z_{\beta}}\,\;,\quad Z_{\beta}:=\Tr[e^{-\beta H}]\;,

(174)

where the parameter
\beta\geq 0
can be called the
inverse temperature
of the system, in analogy with the classical case.
They are diagonal in the eigenbasis
\{\ket{\epsilon_{i}};i\in[1,d]\}

of
H
,

\omega_{\beta}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\hat{\lambda}_{i}(\beta)\ket{\epsilon_{i}}\bra{\epsilon_{i}}\;,

(175)

with population given by

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}_{i}(\beta):=Z^{-1}_{\beta}e^{-\beta\epsilon_{i}}\;.

(176)

The quantity
Z_{\beta}
is usually called the
partition function
of the system and
allow us to establish a natural correspondences between the mean energy of

\omega_{\beta}
(a quantity that we shall refer to as the equilibrium energy of the model), its entropy, and the inverse temperature
\beta
.
Specifically we have

\displaystyle E_{\beta}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle E(\omega_{\beta};H)=\Tr[\omega_{\beta}H]=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\ln Z_{\beta}\;,

(177)

\displaystyle S_{\beta}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle S(\omega_{\beta})=-\Tr[\omega_{\beta}\ln\omega_{\beta}]=\beta E_{\beta}+\ln Z_{\beta}

(178)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\beta\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\ln Z_{\beta}+\ln Z_{\beta}\;,

which lead to the identity

\displaystyle\frac{\partial E_{\beta}}{\partial S_{\beta}}=1/\beta\;.

(179)

The first derivative with respect to
\beta
of the equilibrium energy (
177
) define the heat capacity functional of the model, specifically

\displaystyle C_{\beta}:=-\frac{\partial E_{\beta}}{\partial\beta}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial^{2}\beta}\ln Z_{\beta}\;,

(180)

which enters in the following Taylor expansions formulas

\displaystyle E_{\beta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{\beta}-(\beta^{\prime}-\beta)C_{\beta}+\mathcal{O}\left((\beta^{\prime}-\beta)^{2}\right)\;,

(181)

\displaystyle\ln Z_{\beta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln Z_{\beta}-(\beta^{\prime}-\beta)E_{\beta}+\frac{1}{2}(\beta^{\prime}-\beta)^{2}C_{\beta}

(182)

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathcal{O}\left((\beta^{\prime}-\beta)^{3}\right)\;,

(the minus sign in Eq. (
180
) accounts for the fact that that
\beta
is an inverse temperature).
The functional
\ln Z_{\beta}
can be shown to be decreasing and convex implying the inequality

\displaystyle\ln Z_{\beta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\ln Z_{\beta}+(\beta^{\prime}-\beta)\frac{\partial\ln Z_{\beta}}{\partial\beta}

(183)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln Z_{\beta}-(\beta^{\prime}-\beta)E_{\beta}\;,

valid for
\beta^{\prime}<\beta
.
This property also ensures also
the positivity of
C_{\beta}
which in turns implies
that both
E_{\beta}
and
S_{\beta}
are monotonically decreasing functions of
\beta

in agreement with Eq. (
179
).
Exploiting these one-to-one correspondences with
\beta
, we can naturally associate to each entropy value
s
a thermal energy value
\mathfrak{E}_{s}
,
a heat capacity
\mathfrak{C}_{s}
, and an inverse
temperature
\beta_{s}
via the identities

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mathfrak{E}_{s}:=E_{\beta}\;,\\
\\
\mathfrak{C}_{s}:=C_{\beta}\;,\\
\\
\beta_{s}:=\beta\;,\end{array}\right.\iff s=S(\omega_{\beta})\;.

More generally, given
\rho\in\mathfrak{S}
a generic single-site
state, we define its associated thermal energy
\mathfrak{E}(\rho)
, an effective heat capacity
\mathfrak{C}(\rho)
, and inverse effective temperature
\beta(\rho)
via the identities

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mathfrak{E}(\rho):=E_{\beta}\;,\\
\\
\mathfrak{C}(\rho):=C_{\beta}\;,\\
\\
\beta(\rho):=\beta\;,\end{array}\right.\iff S(\rho)=S(\omega_{\beta})\;.

Thanks to these definitions
we can rewrite the corresponding total ergotropy (
1
) as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\text{tot}}(\rho;H)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E(\rho;H)-\mathfrak{E}(\rho)\;.

(196)

We conclude by noticing that the above construction can be trivially generalized to the non-interacting

N
sites model.
Specifically from (
2
) it follows that
the associated Gibbs configurations are tensor product of single sites Gibbs states, i.e.

\omega^{\otimes N}_{\beta}
.
Introducing
\ket{\epsilon_{\vec{i}}}:=\ket{\epsilon_{i_{1}}}\otimes\dots\otimes\ket{\epsilon_{i_{N}}}
the eigenvectors of
H^{(N)}
we then observe that the following identity hold

\displaystyle H^{(N)}\ket{\epsilon_{\vec{i}}}=\epsilon_{\vec{i}}\ket{\epsilon_{\vec{i}}}\;,\qquad\omega^{\otimes N}_{\beta}\ket{\epsilon_{\vec{i}}}=\hat{\lambda}_{\vec{i}}\ket{\epsilon_{\vec{i}}}\;,

(197)

where

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\vec{i}}:=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\epsilon_{i_{k}}\;,\qquad\hat{\lambda}_{\vec{i}}(\beta):=\prod_{k=1}^{N}\hat{\lambda}_{i_{k}}(\beta)\;,

(198)

which from Eq. (
175
) imply

\ln\hat{\lambda}_{\vec{i}}(\beta)=N\ln Z_{\beta}+\beta\epsilon_{\vec{i}}\;.

(199)

B.1
A useful Lemma

In this section we provide an estimation of the number of
eiegenvalues of
H^{(N)}
with energy just above a given threshold linked via Eq. (
B
)
to single-site entropy values. Specifically, given

s_{0}>0
,
N
integer, and
\xi>0

define

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{A}_{\xi}^{(N)}}:=\left\{\epsilon_{\vec{i}}\middle|N\mathfrak{E}_{s_{0}}\leq\epsilon_{\vec{i}}\leq N(\mathfrak{E}_{s_{0}}+2\xi)\right\}\;,

(200)

the subset of the eigenvalues of the Hamiltonian
H^{(N)}
whose energy share per site is
2\xi
-close to

\mathfrak{E}_{s_{0}}
. Then the following property holds:

Lemma 2
.

Given
\zeta<1
a constant
strictly smaller than 1, for all
s_{0}>0
there exists
\xi_{*}\in\;]0,1[
such that for all
\xi\in\;]0,\xi_{*}[
, we can identify
N_{*}(\xi)
integer such that
for all
N\geq N_{*}(\xi)

the cardinality of
{\mathfrak{A}_{\xi}^{(N)}}
is bounded by the inequality

\displaystyle\#{\mathfrak{A}_{\xi}^{(N)}}\geq e^{N\left(s_{0}+\frac{\zeta\xi^{2}}{{2}\mathfrak{C}_{s_{0}}}\right)}\;.

(201)

Proof:–
We remind that according to the notation introduced in Eq. (
B
)

\mathfrak{E}_{s_{0}}
is the mean energy of
E_{\beta_{s_{0}}}
of a single-site Gibbs state
\omega_{\beta_{s_{0}}}

with entropy
s_{0}
,
{\beta_{s_{0}}}
being the corresponding inverse temperature defined as in
Eq. (
B
).
For
s_{0}
assigned and
\xi>0
sufficiently small consider the inverse temperature
\beta^{\prime}
defined by the identity

\displaystyle E_{\beta^{\prime}}=E_{\beta_{s_{0}}}+\xi=\mathfrak{E}_{s_{0}}+\xi\;,

(202)

which, by construction is slightly smaller than
\beta_{s_{0}}
.
By virtue of Eq. (
200
) and
(
199
) we have

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\vec{i}}\in{\mathfrak{A}_{\xi}^{(N)}}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\left\lvert\epsilon_{\vec{i}}-NE_{\beta^{\prime}}\right\rvert\leq N\xi

(203)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\left\lvert-\ln\hat{\lambda}_{\vec{i}}(\beta^{\prime})-NS_{\beta^{\prime}}\right\rvert\leq N\beta^{\prime}\xi\;,

which in particular implies

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\vec{i}}\in{\mathfrak{A}_{\xi}^{(N)}}\Longrightarrow\hat{\lambda}_{\vec{i}}(\beta^{\prime})\geq e^{-N(S_{\beta^{\prime}}+\beta^{\prime}\xi)}\;.

(204)

Remember then that the expected values of
-\ln\hat{\lambda}_{\vec{i}}(\beta^{\prime})
for the state
\omega^{\otimes N}_{\beta^{\prime}}
are:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[-\ln\hat{\lambda}_{\vec{i}}(\beta^{\prime})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Tr[\omega^{\otimes N}_{\beta^{\prime}}\ln\omega^{\otimes N}_{\beta^{\prime}}]=NS_{\beta^{\prime}}\;.

(205)

Identifying hence the variables
x_{i}
of
Lemma
1
of
Appendix
A
with the population
\hat{\lambda}_{i}(\beta^{\prime})
of the Gibbs state

\omega_{\beta^{\prime}}
, and
s_{0}
with the associated entropy
S_{\beta^{\prime}}
, from
Eq. (
171
) we can
claim that
in the state
\omega_{\beta^{\prime}}^{\otimes N}
, the set
{\mathfrak{A}_{\xi}^{(N)}}
hosts a total population of at least

\sum_{\vec{i}|\epsilon_{\vec{i}}\in{\mathfrak{A}_{\xi}^{(N)}}}\hat{\lambda}_{\vec{i}}(\beta^{\prime})\geq 1-e^{-\frac{2N\xi^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}_{d}}}\;,

(206)

where according to Eq. (

is the maximum eigenvalue of

.
Equation (
203
) also implies
that

\epsilon_{\vec{i}}\in{\mathfrak{A}_{\xi}^{(N)}}\Longrightarrow\hat{\lambda}_{\vec{i}}(\beta^{\prime})\leq e^{-N(S_{\beta^{\prime}}-\beta^{\prime}\xi)}\;.

(207)

From (
206
) and (
207

{\mathfrak{A}_{\xi}^{(N)}}
is at least

From the second order expansion (

\displaystyle e^{N(S_{\beta^{\prime}}-\beta^{\prime}\xi)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{N\left(s_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{C}_{s_{0}}(\beta_{0}-\beta^{\prime})^{2}+\mathcal{O}\left((\beta_{0}-\beta^{\prime})^{3}\right)\right)}

for

Replacing (

\displaystyle\#{\mathfrak{A}_{\xi}^{(N)}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\exp[N\left(s_{0}+\tfrac{\xi^{2}}{2\mathfrak{C}_{s_{0}}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\xi^{3}\right)\right)

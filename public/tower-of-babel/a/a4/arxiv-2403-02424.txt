Abstract
. We study the standard family of supercurves of genus 1 1 with an underlying odd spin structures. We give a simple algebraic description of this family and of the compactified family of stable supercurves with one Neveu-Schwarz puncture. We also describe the Gauss-Manin connection on the 1 1 st de Rham cohomology of this family, and compute the superperiods of global differentials.



1.
Introduction

The moduli spaces of supercurves (also known as SUSY curves) is an important geometric ingredient
for the superstring perturbation theory (see e.g.,
[
18
]
).
Recall that a supercurve over a superscheme
S
is a smooth
proper morphism
\pi:X\to S
of relative dimension
1|1
, together with a relative distribution
{\mathcal{D}}\subset{\mathcal{T}}_{X/S}
of rank
0|1
, which is everywhere
non-integrable, i.e., the supercommutator induces an isomorphism
{\mathcal{D}}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}\rTo{\sim}{\mathcal{T}}_{X/S}/{\mathcal{D}}
. We refer to
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
8
]

for background on supercurves.
The moduli space of supercurves, possibly with some Neveu-Schwarz punctures (i.e., sections
S\to X
),
has two connected components corresponding to whether the underlying spin structure is
even
or
odd
.

We refer to
[
17
]
and
[
9
]
for an overview of the genus
1
case.
In this paper we consider the case of supercurves of genus
1
with an underlying odd spin structure (this means that the underlying spin bundle on an elliptic curve
is trivial). For every
\tau
in the upper half-plane, there is a corresponding supercurve
X_{\tau}\to\C^{0|1}
of genus
1
with the underlying elliptic curve
\C/(\Z+\Z\tau)

equipped with the trivial spin structure. In fact, there is a standard family
X\to S
inducing all these supercurves, where
S
is the product of the upper half-plane with
\C^{0|1}
,
obtained as the quotient of the relative
\C^{1|1}
(see Sec.
2.2
). This family is equipped with a natural Neveu-Schwarz (NS) puncture.
Complementing known results on such supercurves (see
[
10
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
16
]
), we determine
the algebra
{\mathcal{O}}(X_{\tau}-p)
, where
p\subset X_{\tau}
is a natural puncture. We show that this leads to a simple algebraic description of
X_{\tau}
as a blow-up
of a cubic in the superprojective plane
{\mathbb{P}}^{2|1}
deforming the classical Weierstrass model (see Theorem
2.4
).
Furthermore, we use this model to extend this family to a family
\overline{X}\to\overline{S}
of stable supercurves with one NS puncture.
We compute the Kodaira-Spencer map for the extended family
proving that the corresponding map
\overline{S}\to\overline{{\mathcal{S}}}^{odd}_{1,1}
is an isomorphism on tangent spaces (see Theorem
2.6
).

The second topic of this paper is a calculation of the Gauss-Manin connection.
It is a classical fact that the cohomology group
H^{1}_{dR}(C)
of a smooth projective curve over
\C
can be computed as
the space of meromorphic differentials of the second kind modulo the exact ones (see
[
1
]
) and the Gauss-Manin connection can be calculated
in these terms (see
[
11
]
). In particular, for a point
p\in C
,
this cohomology group can be described as the quotient
\omega_{C}(C-p)/d({\mathcal{O}}(C-p))
.

We present a similar isomorphism for smooth proper supercurves
and describe explicitly the Gauss-Manin connection in these terms.
More precisely, given a section
p\subset X
of
\pi:X\to S
, we construct an isomorphism (assuming
S
is affine) of the relative de Rham cohomology
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{X/S}
with

\omega_{X/S}(X-p)/\delta({\mathcal{O}}(X-p))
,
where
\omega_{X/S}
is the relative Berezinian line bundle
and

\delta:{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to\omega_{X/S}

is the canonical derivation associated with
{\mathcal{D}}\subset{\mathcal{T}}_{X/S}
(see Proposition
3.2
).
We then give a recipe for computing the Gauss-Manin connection on
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{X/S}
in these terms (see Proposition
3.3
).
Using this recipe, we compute explicitly the Gauss-Manin connection on the de Rham cohomology space
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{X/S}

for the standard family
\pi:X\to S
of genus
1
supercurves with an odd underlying spin-structure (see Theorem
3.6
). We also compute the superperiods of the global differentials for this family, i.e., the map
\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}\to{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{X/S}
(see Corollary
3.8
).

Conventions
.
We mostly work in the complex analytic category. The general results about cohomology and Gauss-Manin connection in Section
3

also hold algebraically over a field of characteristic
0
.

There are two different sign conventions for the tensor category of graded superspaces (see
[
3
, Sec. 1.2.8]
). We use the Deligne’s convention that
the sign appearing when swapping two symbols (that have both the degree and the parity)
is the product of the signs for the degree and for the parity. Further, we consider the de Rham differential as being even and of degree
1
.
Thus, for a pair of functions
a
,
b
(even or odd), we have
d(ab)=da\cdot b+a\cdot db
.
The structure even derivation
\delta:{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to\omega_{X/S}
of a supercurve
in superconformal coordinates
(z,\theta)
is given by

\delta(f)=[dz|d\theta]\cdot D(f),

where
D=\partial_{\theta}+\theta\partial_{z}
(this differs from the convention in
[
2
]
). Thus, we have
\delta(z)=\delta(\theta)\cdot\theta
.
The corresponding
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-linear morphism
\varphi:\Omega^{1}_{X/S}\to\omega_{X/S}
is given by

\delta(df\cdot g)=\delta(f)g=[dz|d\theta]\cdot D(f)g.

In particular,
\ker(\varphi)\subset\Omega^{1}_{X/S}
is spanned by
dz-d\theta\cdot\theta=dz+\theta d\theta
.

2.1.
Weierstrass functions

Here we collect some formulas related to Weierstrass functions that we will use.
We consider the lattice

\tau\eta_{1}(\tau)-\eta_{2}(\tau)=2\pi i

\eta_{1}(\tau)=-(2\pi i)^{2}\cdot\frac{E_{2}(q)}{12},

for

(see
[
7
, Sec. 1.2]

and
\eta_{2}(\tau)

\zeta_{1}(z,\tau):=(-2\pi i)^{-1}(\zeta(z,\tau)-\eta_{1}(\tau)z)

Then
\zeta^{\prime}_{1}(z,\tau)=(2\pi i)^{-1}(\wp(z,\tau)+\eta_{1}(\tau))
, and from the Legendre’s relation we get

\zeta_{1}(z+1,\tau)=\zeta_{1}(z,\tau),\ \ \zeta_{1}(z+\tau,\tau)=\zeta_{1}(z,\tau)+1.

(2.1)

Then the periods of the meromorphic differentials of the second kind,

\int_{\alpha}dz=1,\ \ \int_{\beta}dz=\tau,\ \ \int_{\alpha}\zeta^{\prime}_{1}(z,\tau)dz=0,\ \ \int_{\beta}\zeta^{\prime}_{1}(z,\tau)dz=1.

2.2.
Standard family of cupercurves genus
1
with an underlying odd spin structure

Consider the family of supercurves
X\to S
over the domain
S\subset\C^{1|1}
with coordinates
(\tau,\varphi)
, defined by
\operatorname{Im}(\tau)>0
,
defined as the quotient of
\C^{1|1}\times S
by the
\Z^{2}
-action

(z,\theta)\mapsto(z+1,\theta),\ \ (z,\theta)\mapsto(z+\tau+\theta\varphi,\theta+\varphi).

(2.3)

This action preserves the vector field
D=\partial_{\theta}+\theta\partial_{z}
, and we equip
X
with the corresponding superconformal structure.
For each
\tau
, we denote by
X_{\tau}
the corresponding supercurve over
\C^{0|1}
(with the coordinate
\varphi
).

Note that we have an NS-puncture
p:S\to X
coming from the natural NS-puncture of
\C^{1|1}
at the origin given by the ideal
(z,\theta)
.

The family of supercurves
X\to S
can be viewed as a homogeneous variety for a noncommutative algebraic supergroup over
S
(see
[
16
, Sec. 3]
).
Namely, let us consider the supergroup scheme
G
, smooth of dimension
1|1
over
\Z
, defined as follows.
For a superring
A=A_{0}\oplus A_{1}
, the group of points
G(A)
is the group
A_{0}\oplus A_{1}
with the group law

(a_{0},a_{1})\cdot(a^{\prime}_{0},a^{\prime}_{1})=(a_{0}+a^{\prime}_{0}+a_{1}a^{\prime}_{1},a_{1}+a^{\prime}_{1}).

Note that
G\simeq{\mathbb{A}}^{1|1}
, and as a supergroup it is a central extension of the odd additive group by the even additive group.
Let
G_{S}
denote the corresponding constant group over
S
.
Then
X
can be identified with the quotient
G_{S}/(\Z^{2}_{S})
, where the subgroup
\Z^{2}_{S}\subset G_{S}
is generated by the sections

(1,0),(\tau,\varphi)
in
G_{S}(S)
. The superconformal structure on

is induced by the standard superconformal structure on
{\mathbb{A}}^{1|1}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Let us give a description of an equivalent family of supercurves in terms of an arbitrary lattice, so it will be manifestly
\operatorname{SL}(2,\Z)
-equivariant.
Starting with a lattice
\Lambda\subset\C
, we consider the
corresponding Weierstrass zeta function
\zeta(z,\Lambda)
satisfying

\zeta(z+\lambda,\Lambda)-\zeta(z,\Lambda)=\eta(\lambda,\Lambda)

for
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Then we can replace the

-action (

) by the following action of

(z,\theta)\mapsto(z+\lambda+\eta(\lambda,\Lambda)\cdot\theta\varphi,\theta+\eta(\lambda,\Lambda)\cdot\varphi),

for

with the superconformal automorphisms

A_{c^{1/2}}:(z,\theta)\mapsto(cz,c^{1/2}\theta)

\Lambda_{-\tau^{-1}}=\tau^{-1}\Lambda_{\tau}

explains the modular transformation

[

\operatorname{SL}(2,\Z)

\widehat{\operatorname{SL}}(2,\Z)\to\operatorname{SL}(2,\Z)

[

\Z/2\subset\widehat{\operatorname{SL}}(2,\Z)

The blow up

2.5.
Kodaira-Spencer map and compactification

Let us set
S^{\prime}=S/\Z
, where we take the quotient by the
\Z
-action
\tau\mapsto\tau+1
. Then
we have
S^{\prime}=\mathbb{D}\times\C^{0|1}
, where
\mathbb{D}
is the punctured unit disk
0<|q|<1
with the coordinate

q=\exp(2\pi i\tau)

Clearly, our family of supercurves with an NS-puncture
(X\to S,p)
descends to a family
(X^{\prime}\to S^{\prime},p^{\prime})
over
S^{\prime}
.
We want to extend it to a family of stable supercurves over
\overline{S}=\overline{\mathbb{D}}\times\C^{0|1}

\overline{\mathbb{D}}
is the unit disk
|q|<1
.
For this we note that the coefficients of the cubic

depend on

and

Theorem 2.6
.

Let us define the family
\overline{X}\to\overline{S}
with a section
\overline{p}\subset\overline{X}

as the blow up
\widetilde{C}_{q,\varphi}
of the cubic
C_{q,\varphi}

and the corresponding morphism to the moduli space,

\overline{S}\to\overline{{\mathcal{S}}}^{odd}_{1,1}

induces an isomorphism on tangent spaces.

Proof.

Step 1.

First, let us consider the affine cubic
C^{a}_{q,\varphi}:=C_{q,\varphi}-\overline{p}\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{2|1}_{\overline{S}}

C^{a}_{q,\varphi}
is smooth over
\overline{S}
everywhere except at one point in

q=0
. We claim that the odd derivation
\widetilde{D}

2.7
), (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) gives
a superconformal structure on the smooth locus of

. Indeed, we have to check that the vector fields
\widetilde{D}
and
\widetilde{D}^{2}
are linearly
independent at all smooth points. Note that we have the following relation in
\Omega^{1}_{C^{a}_{q,\varphi}/\overline{S}}
modulo

2ydy-12x^{2}dx+\frac{E_{4}}{12}dx\equiv 0\mod(\varphi).

First, we consider the locus
y\neq 0
. The above relation shows that
dx
and
d\psi
form a basis of
\Omega^{1}_{C^{a}_{q,\varphi}/\overline{S}}
on this locus. Hence, our claim on this locus follows from
the invertibility of

\left(\begin{matrix}\widetilde{D}(\psi)&\widetilde{D}(x)\\
\widetilde{D}^{2}(\psi)&\widetilde{D}^{2}(x)\end{matrix}\right)\equiv\left(\begin{matrix}1&y\psi\\
0&y\end{matrix}\right).

Next, we consider the locus
y=0

12x^{2}-\frac{E_{4}}{12}\neq 0

and

\Omega^{1}_{C^{a}_{q,\varphi}/\overline{S}}

\left(\begin{matrix}\widetilde{D}(\psi)&\widetilde{D}(y)\\
\widetilde{D}^{2}(\psi)&\widetilde{D}^{2}(y)\end{matrix}\right)\equiv\left(\begin{matrix}1&\widetilde{D}(y)\\
0&6x^{2}-\frac{E_{4}}{24}\end{matrix}\right).

Step 2.

Let
(z,\theta)
denote the formal relative superconformal coordinates on
C_{q,\varphi}
at infinity, induced by the coordinates on
\C^{1|1}
from the quotient construction.
Note that the functions
(x/y,\psi+2\widetilde{\varphi}x^{2}/y)

\widetilde{C}_{q,\varphi}
along

x/y=-2\pi iz+\ldots,\ \ \psi+2\widetilde{\varphi}x^{2}/y=(2\pi i)^{1/2}\theta+(2\pi i)\widetilde{\varphi}\frac{E_{2}}{12}z+\ldots

where the coefficients of the higher order terms are polynomials in

E_{4}
and
E_{6}

,
E_{4}
and

these formulas constitute an invertible change of variables everywhere along

, hence
(z,\theta)
extend to formal relative coordinates at
\overline{p}\subset\widetilde{C}_{q,\varphi}
.
Thus, we can view
\widetilde{C}_{q,\varphi}
as glued from
C_{q,\varphi}
and a relative formal superdisk at infinity with coordinates
(z,\theta)
.
Note that the superconformal structure on the smooth part of
C_{q,\varphi}
agrees with the superconformal structure given by
D=\partial_{\theta}+\theta\partial_{z}
on the formal
superdisk, hence, they glue into a superconformal structure on the smooth part of
\widetilde{C}_{q,\varphi}
.

Step 3.

We claim that

\overline{s}:=\delta(\psi)(1-x\widetilde{\varphi}\psi)

is a trivialization of the relative Berezinian of the affine cubic over
S
, which extends to a trivialization of the relative dualizing
sheaf
\omega_{\overline{X}/\overline{S}}
. Indeed, first we observe that
\omega_{\overline{X}/\overline{S}}
is a line bundle. Indeed, it is clear on the smooth part, while on the affine part
C^{a}_{q,\varphi}

it follows from the fact that it is a divisor in
{\mathbb{A}}^{2|1}
.

Next, on the affine part over
S
,
\overline{s}
differs by an invertible function from
\delta(\theta)
, so it is a generator. Also, near the infinite point we have

\overline{s}=(2\pi i)^{1/2}\cdot\delta(\theta)(1+(2\pi i)\frac{E_{2}}{12}\theta\varphi),

which is a generator everywhere near
\overline{p}
(since
E_{2}
is regular at
q=0
).
The above formula also shows that
\overline{s}
is a generator near infinity on the fiber over
q=0
. Hence,
\overline{s}
can have zeros only in codimension
\geq 2
.
Note that the family
\overline{X}\to\overline{S}
is the standard family of stable curves over
\{q\in\C\ |\ 0<|q|<1\}
, in particular,
\overline{X}_{\operatorname{bos}}
is smooth.
Hence,
\overline{s}|_{\overline{X}_{\operatorname{bos}}}
is non-vanishing everywhere, and so is
\overline{s}
.

Step 4.

We already equipped the smooth part of
\widetilde{C}_{q,\varphi}
with a superconformal structure. Next, we need to check that the corresponding derivation

\delta:{\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{X}}\to\omega_{\overline{X}/\overline{S}}
is regular near the singular point of the fiber over
q=0
and induces an isomorphism of the odd components.
For any function
f
on the smooth part of
C^{a}_{q,\varphi}
, we have

\delta(f)=\delta(\theta)\cdot D(f)=(2\pi i)^{-1/2}\overline{s}\cdot(1-(2\pi i)\frac{E_{2}}{12}\theta\varphi)D(f)=\overline{s}\cdot\widetilde{D}(f).

Hence, the regularity follows from the formulas (
2.7
), (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) for
\widetilde{D}
.
Also,
\delta(\psi)
generates
\omega_{C^{a}_{q,\varphi}}
near the singular point, so
\varphi
induces an isomorphism of the odd components.

Since the corresponding bosonic family is a family of stable curves of genus
1
with
1
marked point, we obtain that
(\overline{X}\to\overline{S},\overline{p})

is a family of stable supercurves of genus
1
with one NS puncture.

Step 5.
Computation of the Kodaira-Spencer map on
S
.

Recall that for a family
\pi:X\to S
of smooth supercurves with an NS puncture
p\subset X
, the Kodaira-Spencer map (KS map)
is associated with the exact sequence

0\to{\mathcal{A}}_{(X,p)/S}\to{\mathcal{A}}_{X,p}\to\pi^{-1}{\mathcal{T}}_{S}\to 0,

where
{\mathcal{A}}_{X,p}
is the sheaf of vector fields on
X
preserving the structure distribution
{\mathcal{D}}_{X/S}

and the puncture
p
,
{\mathcal{A}}_{(X,p)}
is the similar subsheaf of the relative tangent
bundle. Looking at the connecting homomorphism on the cohomology, we get for each
s\in S
, a map

KS:T_{s}S\to H^{1}(X_{s},{\mathcal{A}}_{X_{s},p}),

Note that the fact that the KS map is an isomorphism on the even components follows from the similar fact about the bosonization of our family.
It is still instructive to compute the KS map fully.

To compute the KS map we will use the covering of
X
by
U_{1}=X-p
and
U_{2}
, the formal neighborhood of
p
. For a given vector field
v
on the base,

KS(v_{s})
is given as the Cech cohomology class of the difference
\widetilde{v}_{1}-\widetilde{v}_{2}
, where
\widetilde{v}_{i}
are the lifts of
v
to
{\mathcal{A}}_{X,p}(U_{i})
, for
i=1,2
.
More precisely, we additionally use an isomorphism of sheaves

{\mathcal{A}}_{(X,p)/S}\simeq{\mathcal{T}}_{X}/{\mathcal{D}}_{X/S}(-D)\simeq\omega_{X/S}^{-2}(-D)

(see
[
6
, Prop. 3.9]
).
Furthermore, for
s\in S
, we have natural isomorphisms

{\mathcal{A}}_{X_{s}}^{+}\simeq\omega_{C_{s}}^{-1}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{C_{s}},\ \ (\omega_{X_{s}}^{-2})^{-}\simeq L\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{C_{s}},

where
(C_{s},L)
is the underlying elliptic curve with the trivial spin structure
L\simeq{\mathcal{O}}
.
One can check that the corresponding trivializing sections of
{\mathcal{T}}_{X}/{\mathcal{D}}_{X/S}
are
D^{2}
and
\theta D^{2}
.
These isomorphisms induce isomorphisms

H^{1}({\mathcal{A}}_{X_{s},p})^{+}\simeq H^{1}(C_{s},{\mathcal{O}}_{C_{s}}(-p)),\ \ H^{1}({\mathcal{A}}_{X_{s},p})^{-}\simeq H^{1}(C_{s},{\mathcal{O}}_{C_{s}}(-p)).

Thus, to compute
KS(\partial_{\tau})
, we need to find a lifting of
\partial_{\tau}
to a vector field on
U_{1}=X-p
preserving the structure distribution
(over
U_{2}
the obvious lift
\partial_{\tau}
is in
{\mathcal{A}}_{X,p}(U_{2})
). One can check that such a lift is provided by

D_{\tau}:=\partial_{\tau}+(\zeta_{1}+\theta\varphi\dot{\zeta}_{1})D^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Psi_{2}\cdot D=\partial_{\tau}+(\zeta_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\theta\varphi\dot{\zeta}_{1})\partial_{z}+\frac{1}{2}(\theta\zeta^{\prime}_{1}+\varphi\dot{\zeta}_{1})\partial_{\theta}.

(2.13)

Thus, the corresponding cocycle with values in superconformal vector fields is represented by
(\zeta_{1}+\theta\varphi\dot{\zeta}_{1})D^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Psi_{2}\cdot D
on
U_{12}
.
Hence, we get that
KS(\partial_{\tau})
is the class of
\zeta_{1}(z,\tau)=(2\pi i)^{-1}\frac{1}{z}+\ldots
in
H^{1}(C_{s},{\mathcal{O}}(-p))
,
which is a generator of the latter group (note that when considering
X_{s}
we set
\varphi=0
).

Similarly, as a lift of
\partial_{\varphi}
to
U_{1}
we can take

D_{\varphi}:=\partial_{\varphi}+[\varphi(\zeta_{1}^{2}-2\theta\zeta_{1})]D^{2}+[\zeta_{1}-\theta\varphi\zeta_{1}\zeta^{\prime}_{1})]D=\partial_{\varphi}+(\varphi\zeta_{1}^{2}-\theta\zeta_{1})\partial_{z}+(\zeta_{1}-\theta\varphi\zeta_{1}\zeta^{\prime}_{1})\partial_{\theta},

(2.14)

while on
U_{2}
we take
\partial_{\varphi}
. Hence
KS(\partial_{\varphi})
is the class of
-2\zeta_{1}(z,\tau)
in
H^{1}(C_{s},{\mathcal{O}}(-p))
.
This proves that the KS map is an isomorphism for any
s\in S
.

Step 6.
Computation of the Kodaira-Spencer map on
\overline{S}
.

As shown in
[
6

T_{\overline{S},\overline{S}_{0}}|_{s_{0}}\to H^{1}(X_{s_{0}},{\mathcal{A}}_{X_{s_{0}},p}),

where
\overline{S}_{0}\subset\overline{S}

.
For the base of our family, near

and

The fiber of our family over

(and

with the odd coordinate

The formulas (

2.8
) and (
2.9
) show that the vector field

and it is easy to check that

(resp.,

(resp.,

To compute the KS map, first, we need to check that the vector fields

and

D_{\tau}D-DD_{\tau}=-\frac{1}{2}D(\Psi_{2})D,

\dot{\zeta}_{1}+\zeta_{1}\zeta^{\prime}_{1}=(2\pi i)^{-2}\frac{1}{2}\wp^{\prime},\ \ \dot{\wp}+\zeta_{1}\wp^{\prime}=(2\pi i)^{-1}(2\wp^{2}+(2\pi i)^{2}\frac{E_{2}}{6}-\frac{g_{2}}{3}),\ \ \dot{\wp}^{\prime}+\zeta_{1}\wp^{\prime\prime}=(2\pi i)^{-1}3(\wp+(2\pi i)^{2}\frac{E_{2}}{12})\wp^{\prime},

3.
Cohomology and Gauss-Manin connection

3.1.
Cohomology and differentials

Let
\pi:X\to S
be a smooth proper supercurve over a smooth base
S
.
We work either in the category of schemes over a field
k
of characteristic zero and then assume that
S
is affine, or
in the holomorphic category and assume that
S
is Stein.

Following
[
2
]
, we use the identification of the structure distribution
{\mathcal{D}}\subset{\mathcal{T}}_{X/S}
with
\omega_{X/S}^{-1}
, and consider the dual
map
\delta:\Omega^{1}_{X/S}\to\omega_{X/S}
, which induces a chain map of complexes

\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}\to[{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\rTo{\delta}\omega_{X/S}],

(3.1)

where
\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}
is the relative de Rham complex.
It is well known that this map is a quasi-isomorphism in the analytic category (since both complexes are resolutions of the relative constant sheaf
\C_{X/S}
.
The following result gives a stronger algebraic verion of this statement.

Proposition 3.1
.

The chain map (
3.1
) fits into a short exact sequence

0\to K^{\bullet}_{X/S}\to\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}\to[{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\rTo{\delta}\omega_{X/S}]\to 0,

where the complex
K^{\bullet}_{X/S}
is canonically contractible, i.e., there is a canonical homotopy between
0
and identity maps

Proof.

It is known (see
[
2
]
) that we have an exact sequence of vector bundles on
X
,

0\to\omega^{2}_{X/S}\to\Omega^{1}_{X/S}\rTo{\delta}\omega_{X/S}\to 0,

(3.2)

where
\omega^{2}_{X/S}
has rank
1|0
and
\omega_{X/S}
has rank
0|1
.
Thus,
K^{\bullet}_{X/S}
is the following subcomplex of
\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}
:

0\to\omega^{2}_{X/S}\to\Omega^{2}_{X/S}\to\Omega^{3}_{X/S}\to\ldots.

Now we observe that by passing to the exterior powers,
the exact sequence (
3.2
) induces exact sequences

0\to\omega^{i+1}_{X/S}\to\Omega^{i}_{X/S}\rTo{\wedge^{i}(\delta)}\omega^{i}_{X/S}\to 0

for
i\geq 1
.
The obtained maps
\Omega^{2}_{X/S}\to\omega^{2}_{X/S}
, and

\Omega^{i}_{X/S}\to\omega^{i}_{X/S}\to\Omega^{i-1}_{X/S}

for
i>2
, give the required homotopy.
∎

Thus, the de Rham cohomology of
X/S
can be calculated as

{\mathcal{H}}^{i}_{X/S}:=R^{i}\pi_{*}[{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to\omega_{X/S}].

These are locally free
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
-modules of finite rank (by the general theory of
D
-modules on supervarieties developed in
[
13
]
, this follows from the existence of a flat connection on
{\mathcal{H}}^{i}_{X/S}
, see Sec.
3.2
below).

Let
i:S_{0}\hookrightarrow S
denote the bosonization of
S
, and let
\pi_{0}:C\to S_{0}
denote the family of usual curves
underlying
\pi:X\to S
. Then the pull back induces a natural isomorphism

i^{*}R\pi_{*}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S})\rTo{\sim}R\pi_{0*}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{C/S_{0}})

(see
[
15
]
). Furthermore, since the cohomology sheaves
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{X/S}
are locally free, in fact, we have

i^{*}{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{X/S}\simeq{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{C/S_{0}}.

(3.3)

Assume now that we have a section
p\subset X
of the projection
X\to S
.
Consider the flat covering
X=U_{1}\cup U_{2}
, where
U_{1}=X-p
, and
U_{2}
is a formal neighborhood of
p
.
Let
{\mathcal{C}}[{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to\omega_{X/S}]
be the corresponding relative Cech complex.

Proposition 3.2
.

The natural projection
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}[{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to\omega_{X/S}]\to\omega_{X/S}(U_{1})
induces an isomorphism

{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{X/S}=R^{1}\pi_{*}[{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to\omega_{X}]\rTo{\sim}\operatorname{coker}(\delta:{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(X-p)\to\omega_{X/S}(X-p)).

Proof.

The proof is similar to the classical case (cf.
[
14
, Lemma 5.1]
for a proof of a similar statement for the separating node degeneration of curves).
Namely, taking the quotient of the Cech complex of
[{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to\omega_{X/S}]
by an exact subcomplex, we get the quasi-isomorphic complex

{\mathcal{O}}(U_{1})\to\omega_{X/S}(U_{1})\to\omega_{X/S}(U_{12})/(\delta({\mathcal{O}}(U_{12})+\omega_{X/S}(U_{2})).

It remains to check that the image of
the restriction map
\omega_{X/S}(U_{1})\to\omega_{X/S}(U_{12})
is contained in

\delta({\mathcal{O}}(U_{12}))+\omega_{X/S}(U_{2})
. This can be checked as follows.
It is easy to check using local formal relative coordinates that

\omega_{X/S}(U_{12})/(\delta({\mathcal{O}}(U_{12}))+\omega_{X/S}(U_{2}))\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{S}

(the isomorphism is given by the residue, see
[
5
, Sec. 2.8]
).
On the other hand,
we have an isomorphism

\omega_{X/S}(U_{12})/(\delta({\mathcal{O}}(U_{12}))+\omega_{X/S}(U_{1})+\omega_{X/S}(U_{2}))\simeq{\mathcal{H}}^{2}_{X/S},

which is a locally free
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
-module of rank
1
.
Since the natural projection

{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\simeq\omega_{X/S}(U_{12})/(\delta({\mathcal{O}}(U_{12}))+\omega_{X/S}(U_{2}))\rTo{pr}\omega_{X/S}(U_{12})/(\delta({\mathcal{O}}(U_{12}))+\omega_{X/S}(U_{1})+\omega_{X/S}(U_{2}))\simeq{\mathcal{H}}^{2}_{X/S},

is surjective, it is an isomorphism. This implies that the map
pr
is an isomorphism, which implies the assertion.
∎

3.2.
Gauss-Manin connection: generalities

Recall that the Gauss-Manin connection
\nabla^{GM}
on the de Rham cohomology
R^{i}\pi_{*}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S})
arises as a connecting homomorphism associated with
the exact sequence of complexes

0\to\Omega^{\bullet-1}_{X/S}\otimes\pi^{*}\Omega^{1}_{S}\to\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}/(\Omega^{\bullet-2}_{X}\cdot\pi^{*}\Omega^{2}_{S})\to\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}\to 0

(see
[
15
]
).

It is easy to see that isomorphisms (
3.3
) are
compatible with the Gauss-Manin connections.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
p\subset X
be a section of
X\to S
.
Under the identification
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{X/S}\simeq\operatorname{coker}(\delta:{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(X-p)\to\omega_{X/S}(X-p))
(see Proposition
3.2
), the Gauss-Manin connection

\nabla^{GM}:{\mathcal{H}}^{1}\to{\mathcal{H}}^{1}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{S}

can be computed as follows. Start with a representative
\Phi\in\omega_{X/S}(U_{1})
. Then there exists a unique

\omega\in\Omega^{1}_{X/S}(U_{1})
such that
\delta(\omega)=\Phi
and
d_{X/S}\omega=0
.
Lift
\omega
to some
\widetilde{\omega}\in\Omega^{1}_{X}(U_{1})
and consider its total differential
d\widetilde{\omega}\in\Omega^{2}_{X}(U_{1})
.
Then
d\widetilde{\omega}\mod\Omega^{2}_{S}
is in the image of
\Omega^{1}_{X/S}(U_{1})\otimes\Omega^{1}_{S}
and we have

\nabla^{GM}([\Phi])=[(\delta\otimes\operatorname{id})(d\widetilde{\omega}\mod\Omega^{2}_{S})],

where on the right we consider the map
\delta\otimes\operatorname{id}:\Omega^{1}_{X/S}(U_{1})\otimes\Omega^{1}_{S}\to\omega_{X/S}(U_{1})\otimes\Omega^{1}_{S}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
, the map
\delta:\Omega^{1}_{X/S}(U_{1})\to\omega_{X/S}(U_{1})
is surjective and its kernel maps by
d_{X/S}
isomorphically to

\ker(d:\Omega^{2}_{X/S}(U_{1})\to\Omega^{3}_{X/S}(U_{1}))
. This implies that every
\Phi\in\omega_{X/S}(U_{1})
lifts uniquely to

\omega\in\Omega^{1}_{X/S}(U_{1})
such that
d_{X/S}(\omega)=0
.

By Proposition
3.2
, the class of
\Phi
in
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{X/S}
corresponds to a Cech cocycle

(f,\Phi,\alpha)\in{\mathcal{O}}(U_{12})\oplus\omega_{X/S}(U_{1})\oplus\omega_{X/S}(U_{2})={\mathcal{C}}^{1}[{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to\omega_{X/S}],

where

\delta(f)=-\Phi|_{U_{12}}+\alpha|_{U_{12}}
.
By Proposition
3.1
, it can be lifted to a Cech cocyle

(f,\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\in{\mathcal{O}}(U_{12})\oplus\Omega^{1}_{X/S}(U_{1})\oplus\Omega^{1}_{X/S}(U_{2})={\mathcal{C}}^{1}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}),

where

d_{X/S}f=-\omega_{1}|_{U_{12}}+\omega_{2}|_{U_{12}},\ d_{X/S}\omega_{1}=d_{X/S}\omega_{2}=0,\ \delta(\omega_{1})=\Phi.

Hence,
\omega_{1}=\omega
is the unique lift of
\Phi
to a
d_{X/S}
-closed
1
-form.

Next, by the definition of the connecting homomorphism, we have to lift
(f,\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
to Cech cochain

(f,\widetilde{\omega}_{1},\widetilde{\omega}_{2})\in{\mathcal{O}}(U_{12})\oplus\Omega^{1}_{X}(U_{1})\oplus\Omega^{1}_{X}(U_{2})={\mathcal{C}}^{1}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}),

and consider its differential

(-df+\widetilde{\omega_{2}}-\widetilde{\omega}_{1},d\widetilde{\omega}_{1},d\widetilde{\omega}_{2})\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X})
.
It will then belong to the subcomplex
\Omega^{\bullet-1}_{X/S}\otimes\pi^{*}\Omega^{1}_{S}
. Applying the projection (
3.1
) and using Proposition
3.2
,
we arrive at the claimed description of
\nabla^{GM}([\Phi])
.
∎

Remark 3.4

The formula of Proposition

should be compared with the following classical recipe

Radiative gravastar with Gibbons-Hawking temperature Preprint: OCU-PHYS-560 Preprint: AP-GR-180 Preprint: NITEP 133 Preprint: RUP-22-7
Ken-ichi Nakao 1,2, Kazumasa Okabayashi 1, Tomohiro Harada 3

Abstract
We study the quantum particle creation in a toy model of spherically symmetric gravitational collapse whose final product is not a black hole but a gravastar. Precedent studies revealed that even in the case of the gravitational collapse to form a horizonless ultra-compact object, thermal radiation named transient Hawking radiation is generated at the late stage of the gravitational collapse, and a sudden stop of collapsing motion to form a horizonless ultra-compact object causes one or two bursts of quantum particle creation. The very different behavior of the model studied in this paper from the precedent ones is quantum radiation with a thermal spectrum from the gravastar between two bursts. The temperature of the radiation is not the same as the Hawking one determined by the gravitational mass of the system but the Gibbons-Hawking one of the de Sitter core inside the gravastar.



I
Introduction

The gravitational collapse to form black holes is one of the most important and fascinating subjects in the physics of strong gravity
and has gathered special interests.
Recent observational developments have led to a more activity research field of the black hole physics;
the observations through gravitational
waves
LIGO:2016
;
LVK:2021
and the imaging of the so-called black hole shadow
EHT:2019
gave us very impressive evidence
of the existence of black hole candidates in our universe.

The black hole is defined as a complement of the causal past of the future conformal infinity, and
its boundary is called the event horizon
Penrose:1969
;
Hawking:1970
.
This definition captures the essence of the black hole and the event horizon.
On the other hand, any observational confirmation of the black hole defined in such a manner is impossible
Cardoso-P
;
Nakao-YH
.
Exactly speaking, the gravitational waves observed by LIGO and Virgo were generated by not black hole binaries but
collapsing objects; what was taken through EHT is not a direct image of a black hole but an image of
collapsing matter (see, for example, Appendix A of Ref.
OAN
).
This is true also for semi-classical processes which are quantum phenomena in classical spacetime.
The Hawking radiation is a celebrated example which is regarded as a characteristic of the black hole
Hawking:1974
;
Hawking:1975
. However,
Paranjape and Padmanabhan studied a free quantum field in the gravitational collapse to form a static horizonless
spherical ultra-compact object with a hollow inside and showed that even if no black hole eventually forms,
radiation power whose value is the same as that of the Hawking radiation
is generated at the late stage of the gravitational collapse
PP:2009
.
The study by Barceló, Liberati, Sonego and Visser
BLSV:2011
revealed that the
quantum radiation studied by Paranjape and Padmanabhan has the thermal spectrum of the Hawking temperature.
Even quantum radiation with thermal spectrum is not an evidence
of a black hole but merely implies it is a black hole candidate.

What we can observationally confirm is that
the black hole candidate is a black hole mimicker. The black hole mimicker is a horizonless ultra-compact object
observationally very similar to a black hole. Various black hole mimickers have been proposed (see eg. Ref.
Cardoso-P
).
The situation studied by Paranjape and Padmanabhan is regarded as a formation process of a black hole mimicker.
One of the present authors, TH, in collaboration with Cardoso and Miyata, studied the quantum particle creation
in the similar situation to that studied by Paranjape and Padmanabhan
Harada-CM
.
They showed that the two bursts of the particle creation occur due to the stop
of the gravitational collapse in addition to the thermal radiation revealed
by Paranjape and Padmanabhan.
Kokubu and TH studied the quantum field which interacts with the collapsing object
Kokubu-H

in the spacetime similar to that studied in Ref.
Harada-CM
.
They also studied the case in which the interaction between the quantum field and the collapsing object is represented
as the reflection boundary condition for the mode function at the surface of the collapsing object.
Their study revealed that, even in this case,
the transient Hawking radiation is generated, and furthermore only one burst of the particle creation due to the stop of the
collapse occurs. In Refs.
PP:2009
;
Harada-CM
;
Kokubu-H
, the gravitational collapse of
the spherically symmetric object with hollow inside, or in other words, an infinitesimally thin spherical
shell were studied. By contrast, the present authors studied the free quantum field in the gravitational
collapse of the spherically symmetric object composed of homogeneously distributed
matter enclosed by an infinitesimally thin crust, which will be called OHN model
Okabayashi-HN
.
The result obtained in Ref.
Okabayashi-HN
is basically the same as the results in Refs.
Harada-CM
:
the homogeneous distribution of matter does not affect the time variation of the radiation power of the
quantum particle creation even though the homogeneous component dominates the total mass of the system.

The final product of OHN model is the spherically symmetric ultra-compact horizonless object composed of homogeneously
distributed matter with infinitesimally thin crust. The equation of state of the homogeneous matter is
p=-\rho/3
, where
p
and
\rho

are the pressure and the energy density, respectively. By contrast, in this paper, we consider an OHM type model, but the final product
is a gravastar. The gravastar is one of black hole mimickers which was proposed by Mazur and Mottola in order to solve problems related to
the black hole (e.g., the information loss problem)
MM2004
. Its inside is occupied by the dark energy of
p=-\rho<0
, or equivalently,
the cosmological constant and hence the geometry of its inside is equivalent to that of the de Sitter spacetime.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sec. II. we briefly review the quantum particle creation of
the massless scalar field in the spherically symmetric spacetime and give a formula to calculate
the radiation power in the spacetime with a homogeneous star. In Sec. III, we explain the model of
the gravastar formation through the gravitational collapse of a spherically symmetric dust star.
In Sec. IV, we show an example of quantum particle creation in the gravastar formation process,
and briefly see what happens in the present model. In Sec. V, we estimate the
radiation power in the radiative gravastar phase and its duration. Sec. VI is devoted to summary.
Five Appendices are given at the end of the paper; Appendices A and B are for the readers unfamiliar to this topics, whereas Appendices C, D and E
show the detail of the calculations.

We adopt the natural unit
c=\hbar=1
. Newton’s gravitational constant
and the Boltzmann constant are denoted by
G
and
k_{\rm B}
, respectively. The sign convention of the metric
follows the text book written by Wald
Wald

II
Quantum particle creation

We study the quantum dynamics of the massless scalar field
\phi
in the spherically symmetric spacetime
by the so called semi-classical treatment in which the effect of the quantum field on the classical
spacetime geometry is ignored.

II.1
Radiation power

We consider the quantum particle creation in the spherically symmetric asymptotically flat spacetime.
By adopting the double null coordinates, the infinitesimal world interval can be written in the following form:

ds^{2}=-h^{2}(u,v)dudv+r^{2}(u,v)d\Omega^{2},

(1)

where
u
and
v
are the retarded and the advanced time coordinates, respectively, whereas
d\Omega^{2}

is the round metric. In the case of the Minkowski spacetime,
h=1
and the areal radius
r

agrees with
(v-u)/2
, and hence
u=v
at the symmetry center
r=0
.
By contrast, in general dynamical cases, the symmetry center
r=0
is not
v=u
but

v=F(u).

(2)

In order to study the quantum effect in this spacetime, we consider the massless free scalar field which is the simplest model but is
sufficient for the present purpose. The Lagrangian density is given as

{\cal L}=-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}(\partial_{\mu}\phi)\partial_{\nu}\phi,

(3)

where
g_{\mu\nu}
,
g^{\mu\nu}
and
g
are the metric tensor, its inverse and its determinant, respectively.
The average of radiation power of the massless scalar field due to the quantum effect caused by the spacetime curvature is estimated
through the expectation value of the stress-energy-momentum tensor
T_{\mu\nu}
as

\displaystyle P(u)=\oint_{r\rightarrow\infty}\langle 0|(\hat{T}_{uu}-\hat{T}_{vv})|0\rangle r^{2}d\Omega=\frac{1}{48\pi}\kappa^{2}(u),

(4)

where, being a prime to be a derivative with respect to
u
,

\displaystyle\kappa(u):=-\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(u)}{F^{\prime}(u)},

(5)

and we have ignored the total derivative term in
P
, since it does not contribute the total radiated energy.
This expression is derived by invoking the S-wave approximation whose sketch is given in Appendix A.
It is known that if the adiabatic condition
|\kappa^{\prime}|/\kappa^{2}\ll 1
is satisfied,
the spectrum of the radiation is thermal with the temperature
BLSV:2011

k_{\rm B}T=\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}.

(6)

II.2
How to calculate radiation power in the case of a homogeneous star

The outside of the star is assumed to be vacuum. By Birkhoff’s theorem, the outside domain is described
by the Schwarzschild geometry whose metric is given as

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=-f(r)dt^{2}+\frac{dr}{f(r)}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}=-f(r)\left(dt^{2}-dr_{*}^{2}\right)+r^{2}d\Omega^{2},

(7)

where

f(r)=1-\frac{2GM}{r},

(8)

and

r_{*}=\int\frac{dr}{f(r)}=r+2GM\ln\left|r-2GM\right|.

(9)

The null coordinates are defined as

u=t-r_{*}~~~~~{\rm and}~~~~~v=t+r_{*}.

(10)

As mentioned, the inside of the spherical star is assumed to be homogeneous and so described by
the Robertson-Walker geometry whose metric is given as

\displaystyle ds^{2}=a^{2}(\eta)\left[-d\eta^{2}+d\chi^{2}+\varSigma^{2}(\chi)d\Omega^{2}\right],

(11)

where

\varSigma(\chi)=\begin{cases}\sin\chi&\text{for positive curvature space,}\\
\chi&\text{for flat space,}\\
\sinh\chi&\text{for negative curvature space.}\end{cases}

(12)

The null coordinates are defined as

U:=\eta-\chi~~~~{\rm and}~~~~V:=\eta+\chi.

(13)

We denote the proper time along the world line of the surface of the star by
\tau
.
Then by using the coordinates outside the star, the world line of the surface of the star, which is a curve with constant round coordinates,
is represented in the form

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=u_{\rm s}(\tau)=t_{\rm s}(\tau)-r_{\rm s}(\tau)-2M\ln\left|r_{\rm s}(\tau)-2GM\right|,

(14)

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=v_{\rm s}(\tau)=t_{\rm s}(\tau)+r_{\rm s}(\tau)+2M\ln\left|r_{\rm s}(\tau)-2GM\right|,

(15)

whereas, by using the coordinates inside the star, the world line of the surface of the star is represented as

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=U_{\rm s}(\tau)=\eta_{\rm s}(\tau)-\chi_{\rm s}(\tau),

(16)

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=V_{\rm s}(\tau)=\eta_{\rm s}(\tau)+\chi_{\rm s}(\tau).

(17)

Since the coordinate system we adopt here is different from that of Eq. (
1
), we show how to derive
F^{\prime\prime}/F^{\prime}
in detail.
The ingoing radial null with
v=v_{\rm in}=
constant and
V=V_{\rm in}=
constant hits the surface of the star at
\tau=\tau_{\rm in}
.
After the radial null arrives at the origin
\chi=0
,
it becomes the outgoing null with
U=U_{\rm out}=V_{\rm in}
and
u=u_{\rm out}
and again hits the surface of the star at
\tau=\tau_{\rm out}
. Then, we denote

\dot{U}_{\rm out}=\dot{U_{\rm s}}(\tau_{\rm out}),~~\dot{V}_{\rm in}=\dot{V_{\rm s}}(\tau_{\rm in}),~~\dot{u}_{\rm out}=\dot{u_{\rm s}}(\tau_{\rm out})~~{\rm and}~~\dot{v}_{\rm in}=\dot{v_{\rm s}}(\tau_{\rm in}),

(18)

where a dot represents a derivative with respect to the proper time
\tau
. Since we have

1=\frac{dV_{\rm in}}{dU_{\rm out}}=\frac{\dot{V}_{\rm in}d\tau_{\rm in}}{\dot{U}_{\rm out}d\tau_{\rm out}},

(19)

we obtain

\frac{\dot{V}_{\rm in}}{\dot{U}_{\rm out}}=\frac{d\tau_{\rm out}}{d\tau_{\rm in}}.

(20)

Then, we have

\frac{dv_{\rm in}}{du_{\rm out}}=\frac{\dot{v}_{\rm in}}{\dot{u}_{\rm out}}\frac{d\tau_{\rm in}}{d\tau_{\rm out}}=\frac{\dot{v}_{\rm in}}{\dot{u}_{\rm out}}\frac{\dot{U}_{\rm out}}{\dot{V}_{\rm in}}.

(21)

We define
A_{\rm out}
and
B_{\rm in}
as

A_{\rm out}:=\frac{\dot{U}_{\rm out}}{\dot{u}_{\rm out}}~~~~{\rm and}~~~~B_{\rm in}:=\frac{\dot{V}_{\rm in}}{\dot{v}_{\rm in}}.

(22)

Then, we have

F^{\prime}(u_{\rm out})=\frac{dv_{\rm in}}{du_{\rm out}}=\frac{A_{\rm out}}{B_{\rm in}}.

(23)

By differentiating this expression with respect to
u
, we have

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}(u_{\rm out})

\displaystyle=\frac{dF^{\prime}(u_{\rm out})}{du_{\rm out}}=\frac{A_{\rm out}}{B_{\rm in}\dot{u}_{\rm out}}\left(\frac{d\ln A_{\rm out}}{d\tau_{\rm out}}-\frac{\dot{U}_{\rm out}}{\dot{V}_{\rm in}}\frac{d\ln B_{\rm in}}{d\tau_{\rm in}}\right).

(24)

Hence, by defining
C_{\rm out}
and
D_{\rm in}
as

\displaystyle C_{\rm out}

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{\dot{u}_{\rm out}}\frac{d\ln A_{\rm out}}{d\tau_{\rm out}},

(25)

\displaystyle D_{\rm in}

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{\dot{v}_{\rm in}}\frac{d\ln B_{\rm in}}{d\tau_{\rm in}},

(26)

we obtain

\displaystyle\kappa(u_{\rm out})

\displaystyle:=-(\ln F^{\prime})^{\prime}(u_{\rm out})=C_{\rm out}-\frac{A_{\rm out}}{B_{\rm in}}D_{\rm in}.

(27)

The world line of the surface of the star is represented as

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle=\eta_{\rm s}(\tau),

(28)

\displaystyle\chi

\displaystyle=\chi_{\rm s}(\tau)

(29)

in the inside coordinates, and

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=t_{\rm s}(\tau),

(30)

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=r_{\rm s}(\tau),

(31)

in the outside coordinates. Denoting the scale factor at the surface
a(\eta_{\rm s})
by
a_{\rm s}
,
we have

\displaystyle\frac{d\eta_{\rm s}}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\dot{\chi}_{\rm s}^{2}+\frac{1}{a_{\rm s}^{2}}},

(32)

\displaystyle\frac{dt_{\rm s}}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\dot{r}_{\rm s}^{2}+f(r_{\rm s})}}{f(r_{\rm s})},

(33)

and hence we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{U}_{\rm s}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\dot{\chi}_{\rm s}^{2}+\frac{1}{a_{\rm s}^{2}}}-\dot{\chi}_{\rm s},

(34)

\displaystyle\dot{V}_{\rm s}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\dot{\chi}_{\rm s}^{2}+\frac{1}{a_{\rm s}^{2}}}+\dot{\chi}_{\rm s},

(35)

\displaystyle\dot{u}_{\rm s}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\dot{r}_{\rm s}^{2}+f(r_{\rm s})}-\dot{r}_{\rm s}}{f(r_{\rm s})},

(36)

\displaystyle\dot{v}_{\rm s}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\dot{r}_{\rm s}^{2}+f(r_{\rm s})}+\dot{r}_{\rm s}}{f(r_{\rm s})}.

(37)

We introduce the following quantities:

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle:=\frac{\dot{U}_{\rm s}}{\dot{u}_{\rm s}}=\left(\sqrt{\dot{r}_{\rm s}^{2}+f(r_{\rm s})}+\dot{r}_{\rm s}\right)\left(\sqrt{\dot{\chi}_{\rm s}^{2}+\frac{1}{a_{\rm s}^{2}}}-\dot{\chi}_{\rm s}\right),

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle:=\frac{\dot{V}_{\rm s}}{\dot{v}_{\rm s}}=\left(\sqrt{\dot{r}_{\rm s}^{2}+f(r_{\rm s})}-\dot{r}_{\rm s}\right)\left(\sqrt{\dot{\chi}_{\rm s}^{2}+\frac{1}{a_{\rm s}^{2}}}+\dot{\chi}_{\rm s}\right),

(39)

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{\dot{u}_{\rm s}}\frac{d\ln A}{d\tau}=-\frac{1}{\dot{U}_{\rm s}}\frac{dA}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{\ddot{r}_{\rm s}}{\sqrt{\dot{r}_{\rm s}^{2}+f(r_{\rm s})}}-\frac{\ddot{\chi}_{\rm s}}{\sqrt{\dot{\chi}_{\rm s}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{a_{\rm s}^{2}}}}\right)\left(\sqrt{\dot{r}_{\rm s}^{2}+f(r_{\rm s})}+\dot{r}_{\rm s}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{GM\dot{r}_{\rm s}}{r_{\rm s}^{2}\sqrt{\dot{r}_{\rm s}^{2}+f(r_{\rm s})}}+\frac{1}{a_{\rm s}^{3}}\frac{da_{\rm s}}{d\eta_{\rm s}}\frac{\sqrt{\dot{r}_{\rm s}^{2}+f(r_{\rm s})}+\dot{r}_{\rm s}}{\sqrt{\dot{\chi}_{\rm s}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{a_{\rm s}^{2}}}-\dot{\chi}_{\rm s}},

(40)

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{\dot{v}_{\rm s}}\frac{d\ln B}{d\tau}=-\frac{1}{\dot{V}_{\rm s}}\frac{dB}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\ddot{r}_{\rm s}}{\sqrt{\dot{r}_{\rm s}^{2}+f(r_{\rm s})}}-\frac{\ddot{\chi}_{\rm s}}{\sqrt{\dot{\chi}_{\rm s}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{a_{\rm s}(\eta_{\rm s})}}}\right)\left(\sqrt{\dot{r}_{\rm s}^{2}+f(r_{\rm s})}-\dot{r}_{\rm s}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{GM\dot{r}_{\rm s}}{r_{\rm s}^{2}\sqrt{\dot{r}_{\rm s}^{2}+f(r_{\rm s})}}+\frac{1}{a_{\rm s}^{3}}\frac{da_{\rm s}}{d\eta_{\rm s}}\frac{\sqrt{\dot{r}_{\rm s}^{2}+f(r_{\rm s})}-\dot{r}_{\rm s}}{\sqrt{\dot{\chi}_{\rm s}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{a_{\rm s}^{2}}}+\dot{\chi}_{\rm s}}.

(41)

Then, we have

A_{\rm out}=A(\tau_{\rm out}),~~B_{\rm in}=B(\tau_{\rm in}),~~C_{\rm out}=C(\tau_{\rm out}),~~D_{\rm in}=D(\tau_{\rm in}).

(42)

III
Gravastar formation through dust collapse

As already mentioned, we consider the gravastar formation through the gravitational collapse of a spherically symmetric star.
The time evolution of the star is divided into three phases. In the first phase, the star is composed of homogeneously distributed
dust and begins collapsing from the momentarily static configuration. We call this period
the collapsing phase
.
In the second phase, the speed of the gravitational collapse slows down. This period is called
the slowing-down phase
.
The ingredient of the star is not the dust in this phase.
Finally, the star stops collapsing and becomes a static gravastar. We call this period
the gravastar phase
.
We explain the details of the model below.

III.1
Collapsing phase

The gravitational collapse of a spherically symmetric
star composed of homogeneous dust is described by the Oppenheimer-Snyder solution.
The metric inside the star is given by Eq. (
11
) with
\varSigma(\chi)=\sin\chi
.
The surface of the dust sphere is
\chi_{\rm s}=\chi_{\rm i}=
a positive constant, and we have

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\frac{GM}{\sin^{3}\chi_{\rm i}}\left(1+\cos\eta\right),

(43)

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{GM}{\sin^{3}\chi_{\rm i}}(\eta+\sin\eta),

(44)

where
M
is a positive constant representing the gravitational mass of the star,
and the domain of
\eta
is restricted to
\eta\geq 0
.
In this model, a black hole forms when
r_{\rm s}=a_{\rm s}\sin\chi_{\rm i}=2GM
is satisfied, or equivalently,

\eta=\eta_{\rm bh}:=\pi-2\chi_{\rm i}

(45)

is satisfied. Note that
\chi_{\rm i}
should be less than
\pi/2
so that
\eta_{\rm bh}
is positive.

III.2
Parameters to determine the duration and characteristic radius of each phase

As mentioned, we assume that the gravitational collapse stops before the formation of a black hole at
\eta=\eta_{\rm bh}
,
and the star eventually becomes a gravastar.
We represent the conformal time
\eta_{\rm G}
at which the star becomes the static gravastar by introducing a small positive parameter
\epsilon
as

\eta_{\rm G}=\eta_{\rm bh}-\epsilon^{2}.

(46)

By introducing a positive parameter
\beta
, the conformal time
\eta_{\rm S}
at which the collapsing motion begins slowing down denotes

\eta_{\rm S}=\eta_{\rm G}-\epsilon^{2\beta}=\eta_{\rm bh}-\epsilon^{2}-\epsilon^{2\beta}

(47)

so that
\eta_{\rm S}<\eta_{\rm G}
. Thus, the collapsing phase, the slowing-down phase and the gravastar phase are
0\leq\eta<\eta_{\rm S}
,

\eta_{\rm S}\leq\eta<\eta_{\rm G}
, and
\eta_{\rm G}\leq\eta
, respectively.

The areal radius of the star at the beginning of the slowing-down phase,
r_{\rm s}=R_{\rm S}
, is given as

R_{\rm S}=\frac{GM}{\sin^{2}\chi_{\rm i}}(1+\cos\eta_{\rm S})

(48)

Note that the areal radius of the gravastar,
R_{\rm G}
, is also a free parameter of the model.
By introducing a parameter
\alpha
restricted to
0<\alpha<1
, we represent it as

\displaystyle R_{\rm G}

\displaystyle=\frac{GM}{\sin^{2}\chi_{\rm i}}\left\{1+\cos\left[\eta_{\rm S}+(1-\alpha)\epsilon^{2\beta}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{GM}{\sin^{2}\chi_{\rm i}}\left[1+\cos(\eta_{\rm G}-\alpha\epsilon^{2\beta})\right]

(49)

so that
R_{\rm S}>R_{\rm G}
. Note that both
R_{\rm S}
and
R_{\rm G}
are larger than the gravitational radius
2GM
and approach
2GM

in the limit of
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
.

III.3
Gravastar phase

Before mentioning the detail of the slowing-down phase, we describe the gravastar as a final product in the present model.
The gravastar is a horizonless ultra-compact object composed of the dark energy with the equation of state
p=-\rho<0
, or equivalently,
the positive cosmological constant, which is
enclosed by an infinitesimally thin crust
MM2004
;
VW2004
. In the present model,
the equation of state of the crust is assumed to be determined through Darmois-Israel junction condition (see Appendix B)
Israel:1966
.
Due to the assumption of the spherical symmetry, the inside of the gravastar is described by the de Sitter geometry.
The scale factor of the de Sitter spacetime in the coordinate system of Eq. (
11
) with
\varSigma=\sin\chi

is given as

a(\eta)=\frac{1}{H\sin\left(\eta-\eta_{\rm c}\right)},

(50)

where
\eta_{\rm c}<\eta<\pi+\eta_{\rm c}
,
\eta_{\rm c}
is a constant determined later, and
H
is the so called Hubble parameter of the de Sitter spacetime
which is related to the positive cosmological constant
\Lambda
by

H=\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda}{3}}.

(51)

The final state of the gravastar is static, and its areal radius is equal to
R_{\rm G}
expressed as Eq. (
49
).
Due to the relation
r_{\rm s}=a_{\rm s}\sin\chi_{\rm s}
, the relation

R_{\rm G}=a_{\rm s}\sin\chi_{\rm s}
should be satisfied for the static gravastar. Hence, we have

\chi_{\rm s}=\arcsin\left[HR_{\rm G}\sin(\eta_{\rm s}-\eta_{\rm c})\right]

(52)

in the final state. Note that
\chi_{\rm s}
is not constant. It should also be noted that an inequality

R_{\rm G}<H^{-1}

(53)

should hold so that the timelike Killing vector in the de Sitter spacetime is tangent to the surface of the gravastar.

In the original scenario of the gravastar,
H^{-1}
is almost equal to
2GM
MM2004
. Thus, for simplicity, we assume here

H^{2}R_{\rm G}^{2}=\frac{2GM}{R_{\rm G}}.

(54)

By using formulae given in Appendix B, we find
that this condition leads to the equation of state of the crust whose surface energy density

S_{(0)(0)}
vanishes and tangential stresses
S_{(2)(2)}
and
S_{(3)(3)}
is given as

S_{(2)(2)}=S_{(3)(3)}=\frac{3M}{8\pi R_{\rm G}^{2}\sqrt{f(R_{\rm G})}}.

(55)

The crust of the gravastar does not satisfy reasonable energy conditions, e.g., the dominant energy condition(see Ref.
Wald
).
However, the possibility of such a gravastar model may not be excluded, since the gravastar model is a proposal by assuming
some unknown framework beyond Einstein’s theory, in which the
effective stress-energy-momentum tensor does not necessarily satisfy the reasonable energy conditions.

Here, we determine
\eta_{\rm c}
. From Eqs. (
45
) and (
46
), the formation time of the static gravastar is equal to
\eta_{\rm G}=\pi-2\chi_{\rm i}-\epsilon^{2}
.
Equation (
50
) implies that
a_{\rm s}
diverges in the limit of
\eta-\eta_{\rm c}\rightarrow\pi
, and hence
the static gravastar appears in
\eta_{\rm G}<\eta<\pi+\eta_{\rm c}
, or equivalently,
\pi-(2\chi_{\rm i}+\epsilon^{2})<\eta<\pi+\eta_{\rm c}
.
As a result, we find that the inequality

\eta_{\rm c}>-(2\chi_{\rm i}+\epsilon^{2})

(56)

should hold.
The variation of the areal radius of the star in the slowing-down phase,
R_{\rm G}-R_{\rm S}
, is approximately given as

R_{\rm G}-R_{\rm S}\simeq-\frac{GM\sin(2\chi_{\rm i}+\epsilon^{2})}{\sin^{2}\chi_{\rm s}}(1-\alpha)\epsilon^{2\beta}.

(57)

We impose that the variation of
\chi_{\rm s}
in the slowing-down phase is the order of max
\left[\epsilon^{2},\epsilon^{2\beta}\right]
:

\sin\chi_{\rm s}|_{\eta_{\rm s}=\eta_{\rm G}}-\sin\chi_{\rm i}=HR_{\rm G}\sin(\eta_{\rm G}-\eta_{\rm c})-\sin\chi_{\rm i}={\cal O}\left(\rm{max}\left[\epsilon^{2},\epsilon^{2\beta}\right]\right)<0,

(58)

where, since
\chi_{\rm s}
is a decreasing function of
\eta_{\rm s}
in the gravastar phase, we have also imposed the last inequality, i.e.,
the variation of
\chi_{\rm s}
is negative. Thus, for example, we put
\eta_{\rm c}
as

\eta_{\rm c}=-\left(\chi_{\rm i}+\frac{3}{2}\epsilon^{2}\right),

(59)

so that Eq. (
58
) holds (see Eq. (
188
) in the case of
0<\beta<1
), where we assume
\chi_{\rm i}>\epsilon^{2}
.

III.4
Slowing-down phase before gravastar formation

We determine the behavior of the star for the slowing-down phase,
\eta_{\rm S}<\eta<\eta_{\rm G}
.
We assume both the radial coordinates
\chi_{\rm s}
and
r_{\rm s}
of the surface of the star are
C^{3}
functions of
\eta_{\rm s}
.
Then, we represent
\chi_{\rm s}
as

V.1
Calculation of

The first radial null geodesic of Class III comes out from the star at

\chi=0

and

\eta-\eta_{\rm G}+\chi_{\rm s}|_{\eta_{\rm s}=\eta_{\rm G}}=-\chi
before this moment. The conformal time
at which the first radial null geodesic enters the star is denoted by
\eta_{\rm b}

Since the star is in the collapsing phase with
\chi_{\rm s}=\chi_{\rm i}
at this moment, we have

\eta_{\rm b}=\eta_{\rm G}-\chi_{\rm s}|_{\eta_{\rm s}=\eta_{\rm G}}-\chi_{\rm i}\simeq\pi-4\chi_{\rm i}.

(101)

By contrast, the last radial null geodesic in Class III enters the star at
\eta_{\rm s}=\eta_{\rm S}

The ingoing radial null arriving at the surface of the star at

\eta-\eta_{\rm S}=-\chi+\chi_{\rm i}

\chi_{\rm s}|_{\eta_{\rm s}=\eta_{\rm S}}=\chi_{\rm i}

\eta-\eta_{\rm rf}=\chi

Since
\eta_{\rm e}-\eta_{\rm rf}=\chi_{\rm s}|_{\eta_{\rm s}=\eta_{\rm e}}

52

Rewriting the left hand side of this equation as

\sin^{2}(\eta_{\rm e}-\eta_{\rm c})=\frac{\sin^{2}(\eta_{\rm c}-\eta_{\rm rf})}{1-2HR_{\rm G}\cos(\eta_{\rm c}-\eta_{\rm rf})+H^{2}R_{\rm G}^{2}}.

\sin(\eta_{\rm c}-\eta_{\rm rf})<0

\sin(\eta_{\rm e}-\eta_{\rm c})=-\frac{\sin(\eta_{\rm c}-\eta_{\rm rf})}{\sqrt{1-2HR_{\rm G}\cos(\eta_{\rm c}-\eta_{\rm rf})+H^{2}R_{\rm G}^{2}}}.

\displaystyle A_{\rm out}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a_{\rm s}}\left(\sqrt{1-\frac{R_{\rm G}^{2}}{a_{\rm s}^{2}}}+HR_{\rm G}\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{H^{2}a_{\rm s}^{2}}}\right)=\frac{\sin\chi_{\rm s}}{R_{\rm G}}\left(\cos\chi_{\rm s}+\sqrt{\cos^{2}\chi_{\rm s}-f(R_{\rm G})}\right),

(119)

\displaystyle C_{\rm out}

\displaystyle=H\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{H^{2}a_{\rm s}^{2}}}\left(\sqrt{1-\frac{R_{\rm G}^{2}}{a_{\rm s}^{2}}}-HR_{\rm G}\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{H^{2}a_{\rm s}^{2}}}\right)+H^{2}R_{\rm G}\left(1-\frac{2}{H^{2}a_{\rm s}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{R_{\rm G}}\sqrt{\cos^{2}\chi_{\rm s}-f(R_{\rm G})}\left(\cos\chi_{\rm s}-\sqrt{\cos^{2}\chi_{\rm s}-f(R_{\rm G})}\right)+\frac{\cos 2\chi_{\rm s}-f(R_{\rm G})}{R_{\rm G}}.

(120)

where we have used
a_{\rm s}=R_{\rm G}/\sin\chi_{\rm s}
. Since
0<f(R_{\rm G})=1-H^{2}R_{\rm G}^{2}\ll 1
is assumed, we obtain

\displaystyle A_{\rm out}

\displaystyle\simeq H\sin 2\chi_{\rm s},

(121)

\displaystyle C_{\rm out}

\displaystyle\simeq H\cos 2\chi_{\rm s}.

(122)

Since the first radial null geodesic comes out from the star with
\chi_{\rm s}=\chi_{\rm s}|_{\eta_{\rm s}=\eta_{\rm G}}\simeq\chi_{\rm i}
, we have

\displaystyle A_{\rm out}|_{\rm 1st}

\displaystyle\simeq H\sin 2\chi_{\rm i},

(123)

\displaystyle C_{\rm out}|_{\rm 1st}

\displaystyle\simeq H\cos 2\chi_{\rm i},

(124)

and

\kappa|_{\rm 1st}\simeq H\left(\frac{8\cos^{2}(2\chi_{\rm i})+4\cos(2\chi_{\rm i})+1}{4\cos(2\chi_{\rm i})+2}\right).

(125)

For
0<\chi_{\rm i}\ll 1
,
we have
\kappa|_{\rm 1st}\simeq 13H/6
and hence

P|_{\rm 1st}\simeq\left(\frac{13}{6}\right)^{2}P_{\rm GH},

(126)

where
P_{\rm GH}
is the radiation power of the Gibbons-Hawking radiation in the de Sitter spacetime with the Hubble constant
H
;

P_{\rm GH}=\frac{H^{2}}{48\pi}.

(127)

By using Eqs. (
52
) and (
111
), we have

\chi_{\rm s}|_{\eta_{\rm s}=\eta_{\rm e}}\simeq\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{2\beta}.

(128)

From this equation and Eqs. (

), (

) and (
122
), we obtain

\displaystyle A_{\rm out}|_{\rm last}

\displaystyle\simeq H\epsilon^{2\beta},

(129)

\displaystyle C_{\rm out}

\displaystyle\simeq H

(130)

and hence

\kappa|_{\rm last}\simeq H

(131)

and

P|_{\rm last}=\frac{1}{48\pi}\kappa^{2}\simeq P_{\rm GH}.

(132)

In the late stage of the radiative gravastar phase,
|\chi_{\rm s}|
becomes much less than unity. For

|A_{\rm out}|\ll H
and

hold

. Thus, in this stage, the adiabatic condition

holds, which implies
the thermal spectrum with the Gibbons-Hawking temperature

(133)

V.2
Duration of radiative gravastar phase

We estimate the duration of the radiative gravastar phase with respect to the time of the asymptotic observer.
From Eqs. (
111
) and (
187
), we have

\displaystyle HR_{\rm G}\sin^{2}(\eta_{\rm e}-\eta_{\rm c})-1-\cos(\eta_{\rm e}-\eta_{\rm c})\sqrt{1-H^{2}R_{\rm G}^{2}\sin^{2}(\eta_{\rm e}-\eta_{\rm c})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-HR_{\rm G})^{2}\sin^{2}(\eta_{\rm e}-\eta_{\rm c})}{HR_{\rm G}\sin^{2}(\eta_{\rm e}-\eta_{\rm c})-1+\cos(\eta_{\rm e}-\eta_{\rm c})\sqrt{1-H^{2}R_{\rm G}^{2}\sin^{2}(\eta_{\rm e}-\eta_{\rm c})}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{16}\alpha^{2}\epsilon^{8\beta}\cot^{2}\chi_{\rm i},

(134)

and

HR_{\rm G}\sin^{2}(\eta_{\rm e}-\eta_{\rm c})+1-\cos(\eta_{\rm e}-\eta_{\rm c})\sqrt{1-H^{2}R_{\rm G}^{2}\sin^{2}(\eta_{\rm e}-\eta_{\rm c})}\simeq 2.

Thus, we have, from Eq. (
93
), the time coordinate
t=t_{\rm s}
outside the star at
\eta=\eta_{\rm e}
as

(136)

This is the time just before the 2nd burst occurs. By contrast, since

\displaystyle\sin(\eta_{\rm G}-\eta_{\rm c})

\displaystyle=\sin\left(\pi-\chi_{\rm i}+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{2}\right)=\sin\chi_{\rm i}+{\cal O}(\epsilon^{2}),

(137)

\displaystyle\cos(\eta_{\rm G}-\eta_{\rm c})

\displaystyle=-\cos\chi_{\rm i}+{\cal O}(\epsilon^{2})

hold, we have

\displaystyle HR_{\rm G}\sin^{2}(\eta_{\rm G}-\eta_{\rm c})-1-\cos(\eta_{\rm G}-\eta_{\rm c})\sqrt{1-H^{2}R_{\rm G}^{2}\sin^{2}(\eta_{\rm G}-\eta_{\rm c})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-HR_{\rm G})^{2}\sin^{2}(\eta_{\rm G}-\eta_{\rm c})}{HR_{\rm G}\sin^{2}(\eta_{\rm G}-\eta_{\rm c})-1+\cos(\eta_{\rm G}-\eta_{\rm c})\sqrt{1-H^{2}R_{\rm G}^{2}\sin^{2}(\eta_{\rm G}-\eta_{\rm c})}}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{2}\epsilon^{4\beta}\cos^{2}\chi_{\rm i}}{4\left[HR_{\rm G}\sin^{2}\chi_{\rm i}-1-\cos\chi_{\rm i}\sqrt{1-H^{2}R_{\rm G}^{2}\sin^{2}\chi_{\rm i}}\right]},

(139)

Thus, we get, from Eq. (
93
), the time coordinate
t=t_{\rm s}
outside the star at
\eta=\eta_{\rm G}
as

\displaystyle t_{\rm s}|_{\eta_{\rm s}=\eta_{\rm G}}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{2H}\ln\left|\frac{4\left[(1+H^{2}R_{\rm G}^{2})\sin^{2}\chi_{\rm i}-2-2\cos\chi_{\rm i}\sqrt{1-H^{2}R_{\rm G}^{2}\sin^{2}\chi_{\rm i}}\right]}{\alpha^{2}\epsilon^{4\beta}\cos^{2}\chi_{\rm i}}\right|+t_{\rm c}.

(140)

At this moment, the 1st burst ceases. The duration
\Delta t
between 1st and 2nd bursts,
i.e., of the radiative gravastar phase is then given as

\Delta t=t_{\rm s}|_{\eta=\eta_{\rm e}}-t_{\rm s}|_{\eta=\eta_{\rm G}}=\frac{1}{2H}\ln\epsilon^{-4\beta}+{\rm constant}\simeq\frac{1}{H}\ln\epsilon^{-2\beta}.

(141)

By definition, the value of
\epsilon
determines when the slowing-down phase begins and ceases.
Smaller the value of
\epsilon
is, later the 1st burst occurs and the radiative gravastar phase starts.
Furthermore, as shown in the above, the value of
\epsilon
also determines the duration of the radiative gravastar phase.

From Eq. (
91
), the duration
\delta t
with respect to the outside time coordinate
t

from the beginning of the gravitational collapse,
\eta_{\rm s}=0
, to a moment before the slowing-down phase,

0<\eta_{\rm s}=\eta_{\rm bh}-2\varepsilon<\eta_{\rm S}
, is given as

\displaystyle\delta t

\displaystyle=\frac{GM\cos\chi_{\rm i}}{\sin^{3}\chi_{\rm i}}\biggl[\left(1+2\sin^{2}\chi_{\rm i}\right)(\pi-2\chi_{\rm i}-2\varepsilon)+\sin(\pi-2\chi_{\rm i}-2\varepsilon)

\displaystyle+\frac{2\sin^{3}\chi_{\rm i}}{\cos\chi_{\rm i}}\ln\left|\frac{\cot\chi_{\rm i}+\cot(\chi_{\rm i}+\varepsilon)}{\cot\chi_{\rm i}-\cot(\chi_{\rm i}+\varepsilon)}\right|\biggr].

(142)

Here we assume

{\max}\left[\epsilon^{2\beta},~\exp\left(-\frac{\pi}{2}\chi_{\rm i}^{-3}\right)\right]\ll\varepsilon\ll\chi_{\rm i}\ll 1.

(143)

Then, we have

\delta t\simeq\pi GM\chi_{\rm i}^{-3}\simeq\sqrt{\frac{3\pi}{32G\rho|_{\eta_{\rm s}=0}}},

(144)

where
\rho
is the energy density of the dust. This result implies that
the dust star shrinks to
r_{\rm s}=2GM(1+2\chi_{\rm i}^{-1}\varepsilon)\simeq 2GM
for the free-fall time.

After the star collapsed to
r_{\rm s}\simeq 2GM
, the areal radius of the surface of the star is determined by

\frac{dr_{\rm s}}{dt_{\rm s}}=-\frac{f(r_{\rm s})}{\cos\chi_{\rm i}}\sqrt{\cos^{2}\chi_{\rm i}-f(r_{\rm s})}\simeq-f(r_{\rm s})

(145)

and thus, for
t_{\rm s}>\delta t
, we have

r_{\rm s}-2GM\simeq 4GM\chi_{\rm i}^{-1}\varepsilon\exp\left(-\frac{t_{\rm s}-\delta t}{2GM}\right).

(146)

Denoting the time
t_{\rm s}
at the beginning of the slowing-down phase by
t_{\rm S}
, we have

R_{\rm S}\simeq 2GM\left[1+2\chi^{-1}\varepsilon\exp\left(-\frac{t_{\rm S}-\delta t}{2GM}\right)\right].

(147)

By comparing this equation with Eq. (
185
), we have

\epsilon^{2\beta}\simeq 2\varepsilon\exp\left(-\frac{t_{\rm S}-\delta t}{2GM}\right).

(148)

Substituting this result into Eq. (
141
), we obtain

\Delta t\simeq H^{-1}\left(\frac{t_{\rm S}-\delta t}{2GM}+\ln\varepsilon^{-1}\right).

(149)

Here note that Eqs. (
143
) and (
144
) imply
\delta t\gg GM\ln\varepsilon^{-1}
.
Thus, since
H^{-1}\simeq 2GM
holds in the present model,
if the period from the start of the gravitational collapse to the beginning time of the slowing-down phase is much longer
than the free-fall time of the system, the duration of the radiative gravastar phase is nearly equal to
t_{\rm S}
.

VI
Summary

We studied the quantum particle creation in the gravastar formation process through the gravitational collapse
of a spherically symmetric star. The star is assumed to be initially composed of homogeneously distributed dust
and collapses in the freely falling manner. Just before the formation of the event horizon, the gravitational collapse stops
due to the change of the equation of state of the star, and then the star eventually becomes the gravastar, in the present model.

At the late stage of the gravitational collapse of the dust, the thermal radiation is generated by the quantum effect as
pointed out by Hawking
Hawking:1974
;
Hawking:1975
. The sudden stop of the gravitational collapse causes two bursts of the particle creation.
The occurrence of two bursts was revealed by precedent researches in
which a star with hollow inside and that occupied homogeneous matter
enclosed by an infinitesimally thin crust were studied
Harada-CM
;
Okabayashi-HN
.
The characteristic behavior of the present gravastar formation model is
that non-vanishing radiation is released in the period between 1st and 2nd bursts.
Since this radiation is attributed to the gravastar, we have called this period the radiative gravastar phase.
Its spectrum approaches the thermal one with the Gibbons-Hawking temperature
of the de Sitter spacetime inside the gravastar.
Then, after the 2nd burst, no radiation is generated.
The duration of the radiative gravastar phase is almost equal to
t_{\rm S}
, i.e., the period from the start of the gravitational collapse
to the beginning of the slowing-down phase, if
t_{\rm S}
is much longer than the free-fall time of the system.
We should note that there is no black hole horizon and no cosmological horizon, but the transient thermal radiation appears.

Appendix A
Derivation of radiation power due to quantum effects

In this section, we review the formulation given by Ford and Parker to derive the radiation power
of quantum particle creation in general spherically symmetric gravitational collapse
Ford-Parker
.

The field operator
\hat{\phi}
is represented as

\hat{\phi}=\sum_{l,m}\int d\omega\left[a_{\omega lm}{\cal G}_{\omega lm}(t,r_{*},\Omega)+a^{\dagger}_{\omega lm}{\cal G}^{*}_{\omega lm}(t,r_{*},\Omega)\right],

(150)

where the mode function
{\cal G}_{\omega lm}
satisfies the equation of motion for the scalar field

\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}{\cal G}_{\omega lm}\right)=0,

(151)

and the orthonormal conditions with respect to the Klein-Gordon norm

\displaystyle({\cal G}_{\omega lm},{\cal G}_{\omega^{\prime},l^{\prime},m^{\prime}})

\displaystyle:=-i\int\left[{\cal G}_{\omega lm}\left(\partial_{\mu}{\cal G}^{*}_{\omega^{\prime},l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\right)+{\cal G}^{*}_{\omega^{\prime},l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\left(\partial_{\mu}{\cal G}_{\omega lm}\right)\right]\sqrt{-g}~n^{\mu}d\Sigma

\displaystyle=\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime})\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\delta_{mm^{\prime}},

(152)

where the integral is taken over a Cauchy surface and
n^{\mu}
is the unit normal to this Cauchy surface.
From the canonical commutation relation, we have

[a_{\omega lm},a_{\omega^{\prime},l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}]=0,~~[a^{\dagger}_{\omega lm},a^{\dagger}_{\omega^{\prime},l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}]=0,~~[a_{\omega lm},a^{\dagger}_{\omega^{\prime},l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}]=\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime})\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}

(153)

The vacuum state
|0\rangle
is defined as the state which satisfies

a_{\omega lm}|0\rangle=0.

(154)

We adopt the mode functions which agree with those of the Minkowski spacetime in the past null infinity
u\rightarrow-\infty
.
Hence, the mode functions in the asymptotic region
R\rightarrow\infty
take the following form:

{\cal G}_{\omega lm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi\omega}r}\left(e^{-i\omega F_{lm}(u)}+e^{-i\omega v}\right)Y_{lm}(\Omega),

(155)

where
Y_{lm}
is the spherical harmonics.
Here note that
F_{00}(u)
is equal to
F(u)
in Eq. (
2
). Then, the state
|0\rangle
defined
as Eq. (
154
) is regarded as the Minkowski vacuum in the past null infinity.

The stress-energy-momentum tensor operator of the scalar field is given as

\hat{T}_{\mu\nu}=(\partial_{\mu}\hat{\phi})\partial_{\nu}\hat{\phi}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}g^{\alpha\beta}(\partial_{\alpha}\hat{\phi})\partial_{\beta}\hat{\phi}.

(156)

The average energy flux is the expectation value of the following components of the stress-energy-momentum tensor:

\displaystyle\langle 0|(\hat{T}_{uu}-\hat{T}_{vv})|0\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle 0|\left[(\partial_{u}\hat{\phi})^{2}-(\partial_{v}\hat{\phi})^{2}\right]|0\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{lm}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\left[\left(\partial_{u}{\cal G}_{\omega lm}\right)\partial_{u}{\cal G}^{*}_{\omega lm}-\left(\partial_{v}{\cal G}_{\omega lm}\right)\partial_{v}{\cal G}^{*}_{\omega lm}\right].

(157)

In order to obtain the average energy flux detected in the asymptotic region, we substitute Eq. (
155
) into this equation,
and replacing
u
and
v
by
u+\epsilon
and
v+\epsilon
in
{\cal G}^{*}_{\omega lm}
,
we evaluate the integral with respect to
\omega
as

\displaystyle\langle 0|(\hat{T}_{uu}-\hat{T}_{vv})|0\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{4\pi r^{2}}\sum_{lm}|Y_{lm}|^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\omega\left[F_{lm}^{\prime}(u)F_{lm}^{\prime}(u+\epsilon)e^{i\omega\left[F_{lm}(u+\epsilon)-F_{lm}(u)\right]}-e^{i\omega\epsilon}\right]

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{48\pi r^{2}}\left[\left(\frac{F^{\prime\prime}}{F^{\prime}}\right)^{2}-2\left(\frac{F^{\prime\prime}}{F^{\prime}}\right)^{\prime}\right]|Y_{00}|^{2},

(158)

where a prime represents a derivative with respect to
u
, and
we have ignored the contributions from non-vanishing
l
modes
since those will not suffer the dynamical effect in the neighborhood of the center
r=0
due to the centrifugal potential
so much, and hence
|F^{\prime\prime}_{lm}|\ll|F_{00}^{\prime\prime}|=|F^{\prime\prime}|
holds for
l>0
. Hereafter, we ignored the total derivative term
(F^{\prime\prime}/F^{\prime})^{\prime}
in the last equality,
which does not contribute the total emitted energy.
The radiation power
P
detected in the asymptotic region is obtained as

\displaystyle P=\oint_{r\rightarrow\infty}\langle 0|(\hat{T}_{uu}-\hat{T}_{vv})|0\rangle r^{2}d\Omega=\frac{1}{48\pi}\left(\frac{F^{\prime\prime}}{F^{\prime}}\right)^{2}.

(159)

Equation of state

in the form
p=w\rho
, where
p
and
\rho
are the pressure and the energy density, respectively. The Einstein equations imply

\frac{1}{a}\frac{d^{2}a}{d\tau^{2}}=-\frac{4\pi G}{3}(\rho+3p)~~~\Longrightarrow~~~w=\frac{p}{\rho}=-\frac{1}{4\pi G\rho}\frac{1}{a}\frac{d^{2}a}{d\tau^{2}}-\frac{1}{3}

(189)

and

\left(\frac{1}{a}\frac{da}{d\tau}\right)^{2}=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho-\frac{1}{a^{2}}~~~\Longrightarrow~~~4\pi G\rho=\frac{3}{2a^{2}}\left[\left(\frac{da}{d\tau}\right)^{2}+1\right].

(190)

Combining these equations, we have

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=-\frac{2a}{3}\left[\left(\frac{da}{d\tau}\right)^{2}+1\right]^{-1}\frac{d^{2}a}{d\tau^{2}}-\frac{1}{3}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{3a}\left[\left(\frac{1}{a}\frac{da}{d\eta}\right)^{2}+1\right]^{-1}\left[\frac{d^{2}a}{d\eta^{2}}-\frac{1}{a}\left(\frac{da}{d\eta}\right)^{2}\right]-\frac{1}{3}.

(191)

In order to see
w
in the slowing-down phase, we may invoke Eq. (
88
) and

\frac{d^{2}a_{\rm s}}{d\eta_{\rm s}^{2}}=a_{\rm s}\left[\frac{1}{r_{\rm s}}\frac{d^{2}r_{\rm s}}{d\eta_{\rm s}^{2}}-\cot\chi_{\rm s}\frac{d^{2}\chi_{\rm s}}{d\eta_{\rm s}^{2}}-\frac{2}{r_{\rm s}}\cot\chi_{\rm s}\frac{dr_{\rm s}}{d\eta_{\rm s}}\frac{d\chi_{\rm s}}{d\eta_{\rm s}}+\frac{1+\cos^{2}\chi_{\rm s}}{\sin^{2}\chi_{\rm s}}\left(\frac{d\chi_{\rm s}}{d\eta_{\rm s}}\right)^{2}\right].

(192)

By Eqs. (
97
)–(
100
), we find

\frac{da_{\rm s}}{d\eta_{\rm s}}=O(\epsilon^{0})~~~~~{\rm and}~~~~\frac{d^{2}a_{\rm s}}{d\eta_{\rm s}^{2}}=O(\epsilon^{-2\beta}),

(193)

and hence

w=O(\epsilon^{-2\beta}).

(194)

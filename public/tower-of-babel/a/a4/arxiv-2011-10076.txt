Optimal Algorithms for Convex Nested Stochastic Composite Optimization Thanks: This research was partially supported by the ARO grant W911NF-18-1-0223 and the NSF grant 1953199.
Zhe Zhang, Guanghui Lan

Abstract
Recently, convex nested stochastic composite optimization (NSCO) has received considerable attention for its applications in reinforcement learning and risk-averse optimization. The current NSCO algorithms have worse stochastic oracle complexities, by orders of magnitude, than those for simpler stochastic composite optimization problems (e.g., sum of smooth and nonsmooth functions) without the nested structure. Moreover, they require all outer-layer functions to be smooth, which is not satisfied by some important applications. These discrepancies prompt us to ask: “does the nested composition make stochastic optimization more difficult in terms of the order of oracle complexity?” In this paper, we answer the question by developing order-tight algorithms for the convex NSCO problem constructed from an arbitrary composition of smooth, structured non-smooth and general non-smooth layer functions. When all outer-layer functions are smooth, we propose a stochastic sequential dual (SSD) method to achieve an oracle complexity of 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / ϵ 2 ) \mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{2}) ( 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / ϵ ) \mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon) ) when the problem is non-strongly (strongly) convex. When there exists some structured non-smooth or general non-smooth outer-layer function, we propose a nonsmooth stochastic sequential dual (nSSD) method to achieve an oracle complexity of 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / ϵ 2 ) \mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{2}) . We provide a lower complexity bound to show the latter 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / ϵ 2 ) \mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{2}) complexity to be unimprovable even under a strongly convex setting. All these complexity results seem to be new in the literature and they indicate that the convex NSCO problem has the same order of oracle complexity as those without the nested composition in all but the strongly convex and outer-non-smooth problem.

1.1
Motivation

Composite optimization has attracted
considerable interest for its applications in compressed sensing, image processing and machine learning.
Many algorithmic studies have been focused on composite optimization
of the form
\min_{x\in X}f(x)+g(x)
, where

is a nonsmooth function with certain special structures. Optimal first-order methods have been developed in

under different assumptions about

an accelerated stochastic approximation method that can achieve the
optimal iteration/sampling complexity when one only has access to stochastic (sub)gradients of the objective function (see, e.g.,
ghadimi2012optimal
;
GhaLan13-1
;
chen2014optimal
for extensions).

The study of composite optimization has later expanded to more complex nested composition problems. Specifically, Lewis and Wright
lewis2016proximal
developed a globally convergent algorithm for solving
\min_{x\in X}f(g(x))
, where the outer layer function
f
can be non-smooth, non-convex and extended-valued. Lan
Lan15Bundle
also studied the complexity of these problems when
f
is relatively simple.
Wang et al.
wang2017accelerating
are the first to study
nested stochastic composite optimization (NSCO) problems when

f
and
g
are given as expectation functions. Since NSCO finds wide applications in reinforcement learning
wang2017accelerating
, meta-learning
chen2020solving
, and risk-averse optimization
ruszczynski2020stochastic
, it becomes an important topic in stochastic optimization.

A key challenge in NSCO is the lack of unbiased gradient estimators for the nested function. This issue can be illustrated with a simple two-layer problem,
\min_{x\in X}\{f(x):=f_{1}(f_{2}(x))\}.

By the chain rule, the (sub)gradient of
f
is given by

f^{\prime}(x)=f_{1}^{\prime}(f_{2}(x))f^{\prime}_{2}(x)
①
①

①

We use the Jacobian matrix
f_{{i}}^{\prime}(y)\in R^{n\times m}
to represent the (sub)-gradient of
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It helps to simplify the notation when deriving the gradient of a nested composite function.
.
Now assume
f_{2}
is accessible only through a stochastic first-order oracle which returns unbiased estimators
(f_{2}(x,\xi),f^{\prime}_{2}(x,\xi)),
then the point at which to evaluate
f_{{1}}^{\prime}

f_{2}(x)

, the nested estimator is biased except for an affine

, i.e.

\mathbb{E}[f_{{1}}^{\prime}(f_{{2}}(x,\xi))]\neq\nabla f_{{1}}^{\prime}(f_{{2}}(x)).

(1.1)

So it is not possible to obtain an unbiased estimator for
f_{1}^{\prime}(f_{2}(x))
nor
f_{1}^{\prime}(f_{2}(x))f_{{2}}^{\prime}(x)
.

The current approach to resolve the aforementioned challenge in convex NSCO is gradient approximation, i.e., control the bias of the left-hand side of (
1.1
) by evaluating
f_{{1}}^{\prime}
at some ”close enough” estimate to
f_{{2}}(x)
.
For example, if
f_{{1}}
is assumed to be

N=\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{2})
samples,
\bar{y}(\xi):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}f_{{2}}(x,\xi_{i})/N

\mathbb{E}[\lVert\bar{y}(\xi)-f_{{2}}(x)\rVert]\leq\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)
such that

\mathbb{E}[\lVert f_{{1}}^{\prime}(\bar{y}(\xi))-f_{{1}}^{\prime}(f_{{2}}(x))\rVert]\leq\mathcal{O}(L_{{1}}\epsilon).

So the stochastic gradient descent method with
f_{1}^{\prime}(\bar{y}(\xi))f_{{2}}^{\prime}(x)
as the gradient-proxy can find an
\epsilon
-optimal solution, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}[f(x^{N})-f(x^{*})]\leq\epsilon
, within
\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{4})
calls to the stochastic oracle. Indeed, this simple method achieves the same oracle complexity as the SCGD algorithm in
mengdi2017stochastic
. However, the
\bar{y}(\xi)
-estimation is more subtle in the SCGD algorithm since it is computed as the faster part of a two-time-scale scheme.

Comparing this result to that for solving the simple one-layer stochastic optimization problem without the nested structure, we notice two discrepancies. First, the gradient-approximation type methods require the outer-layer function to have a Lipschitz-continuous gradient so that the desired gradient can be estimated by estimating its argument. Second, the oracle complexity are worse than the
\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{2})
complexity for the one-layer problem by orders of magnitude. These observations motivate us to ask the following research question:

Does the nested composition make stochastic composite optimization more difficult

in terms of the order of oracle complexity?

On the one hand, the answer appears to be negative for some non-convex NSCO problems. In the one-layer problem, we know it takes
\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{2})

ghadimi2013stochastic
queries to the stochastic oracle to find an
\epsilon
-stationary solution

, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}[\lVert\nabla f(\bar{x})\rVert^{2}]\leq\epsilon
. Recently, by using a specially-designed potential function, Ghadimi et al.
ghadimi2020single
developed an
\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{2})
algorithm for the two-layer problem and extended it in
balasubramanian2022stochastic
to solve the multi-layer problem with the same oracle complexity of
\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{2})
. Additionally, under some stronger smoothness assumptions, a variance reduction algorithm proposed in
zhang2019multi
can improve the oracle complexity further to
\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{1.5})

On the other hand, the answers is still unclear for convex NSCO problems. To find an
\epsilon
-optimal solution

for the one-layer problem, i.e.,

mengdi2017stochastic
;
Yang2019Multilevel
;
wang2017accelerating

In this paper, we answer the question by developing efficient methods to achieve order-optimal oracle complexities under some mild assumptions.
We study the two-layer nested problem with a (possibly strongly) convex regularization term
u(x)
given by

\min_{x\in X}\{f(x):=f_{1}\circ f_{2}(x)+u(x)\},

(1.2)

where
X
is a compact and convex set with a finite radius, i.e.,
\max_{x,\bar{x}\in X}\lVert x-\bar{x}\rVert\leq\mathcal{D}_{X}<\infty.

We impose the following
compositional convexity
assumption throughout the paper.

Assumption 1

A nested function
f_{1}\circ f_{2}(x)
in (
1.2
) is said to satisfy the compositional convexity assumption if

•

every layer function
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{i-1}}
is proper closed and convex.

•

f_{{1}}
is component-wise non-decreasing if
f_{2}
is not affine.

The convexity assumption implies a bi-conjugate reformulation important for our development:

f_{i}(y_{i})=\max_{\pi_{i}\in\Pi_{i}}\pi_{i}y_{i}-f^{*}_{i}(\pi_{i}),

(1.3)

where
f^{*}_{i}
is the Fenchel conjugate of
f_{i}
and
\Pi_{i}=\text{dom}(f^{*}_{i}):=\{\pi_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i-1}\times n_{i}}:f^{*}_{i,j}(\pi_{i,j})<\infty\ \forall j\in[n_{i-1}]\}
.
The above monotonicity and the layerwise convexity assumption together form the classic sufficient condition for a nested function to be convex (e.g. see
nesterov2003introductory
). Compared to
wang2017accelerating
;
mengdi2017stochastic
;
Yang2019Multilevel
, Assumption
1
is stronger than their assumption of the nested function
f
being convex. However, we do not require the outer-layer function
f_{{1}}
to be smooth.

Rather than the gradient approximation approach in
mengdi2017stochastic
;
wang2017accelerating
;
Yang2019Multilevel
, we take a reformulation approach.
We obtain a saddle-point problem by replacing every layer function with the maximum over some linearizations indexed by (sub)gradients (e.g. see (
1.3
)), i.e.,
f_{{1}}^{\prime}
and
f_{{2}}^{\prime}
play the roles of dual variables
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
. This relaxes
the tight coupling between
x
and the dual variables.
Instead of requiring
\pi_{1}^{t}\pi_{2}^{t}
to be the true gradient, we only need the iterative algorithm to generate
(x^{t};\pi_{1}^{t},\pi_{2}^{t})
to converge to some stationary point.
So rather than
\pi_{1}^{t}=f_{{1}}^{\prime}(f_{{2}}(x^{t}))
, we can select easier
\pi_{1}^{t}
’s with readily available unbiased estimators such that the update of
x^{t}
can be performed with unbiased arguments. The availability of unbiased arguments is the key to our order-optimal methods. Specifically, our development can be summarized into four steps.

First, we consider a simple two-layer problem with
f_{{1}}
and
f_{{2}}
being smooth. Linearizing both layer functions using the bi-conjugate in (
1.3
), we arrive at a “
\min_{x}\max_{\pi_{1}}\max_{\pi_{2}}
” saddle-point problem similar to that in
zhang2019efficient
. We extend the deterministic sequential dual (SD) method
zhang2019efficient
to a stochastic sequential dual (SSD) method, each iteration of which consists of computing prox-mappings for
\pi_{2}
and
\pi_{1}
and then computing the prox-mapping for
x
.
Choosing the prox-functions in a similar fashion as
LanZhou18RPDG
, the prox-mappings for
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
simplify to gradient evaluations so that the SSD method can be implemented in a primal form.
The SSD method achieves the oracle complexities of

\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{2})
under the non-strongly convex setting and
\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon)
under the strongly convex setting. As illustrated in Table
2
, they improve over the best oracle complexities in the literature by orders of magnitude and
have the same orders as those for solving the simpler one-layer problem.
Moreover, in the deterministic setting, the gradient oracle complexities of the SSD method matches that of Nesterov accelerated gradient method
nesterov2003introductory
.
We also consider a closely related problem where the layer function
f_{{1}}
is structured non-smooth
Nestrov2004Smooth
.
By selecting
\lVert\cdot\rVert^{2}
as the prox-function when performing the
\pi_{1}
-prox mapping, the SSD method achieves an oracle complexity of
\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{2})
. This complexity bound is clearly optimal for the non-strongly convex case, since it has the same order as the one for the simpler one-layer problem. Somewhat surprisingly, we demonstrate this bound is also tight for the strongly convex case, by presenting a new and matching lower complexity bound for this case.

Second, we consider a more complicated two-layer problem where both
f_{{1}}
and
f_{{2}}
are general non-smooth.
Such a problem is particularly challenging due to the non-smooth outer-layer function
f_{{1}}
. From the (sub)gradient approximation perspective, estimating
f_{{1}}^{\prime}(f_{{2}}(x))
is not possible because
f_{{1}}^{\prime}
may not be continuous with respect to its argument. From the SSD perspective, the efficient computation of the
\pi_{1}
-prox mapping with any strongly convex prox-function is difficult.
This motivates us to introduce a tri-conjugate reformulation to
f_{{1}}
,
f_{{1}}(y_{1})=\min_{v_{1}}\max_{\pi_{1}}\langle{\pi_{1},y_{1}-v_{1}}\rangle+f_{{1}}(v_{1}),
where
v_{1}
is an auxiliary primal variable. In the tri-conjugate reformulation, the prox-mappings for both
v_{1}
and
\pi_{1}
can be computed efficiently with
\lVert\cdot\rVert^{2}
as the prox-function.
So we propose a non-smooth stochastic sequential dual (nSSD) method, whose iteration consists of computing prox-mappings for
\pi_{2}
and
\pi_{1}
and then computing prox-mappings for
v_{1}
and
x
. The nSSD method achieves an oracle complexity of
\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{2})
. To the best of our knowledge, it is the first method to achieve any finite time convergence guarantee for the NSCO problem with a non-smooth outer-layer function. This complexity bound is optimal for the non-strongly convex case since it is in the same order of magnitude as the one for the simpler one-layer problem. Moreover, we present a lower complexity bound to show that it is not improvable for the strongly convex case as well.

Third, we extend the two-layer SSD and nSSD methods to the multi-layer setting where the
k
-layer problem is constructed from an arbitrary nested composition of smooth, structured non-smooth and general non-smooth layer functions.
In order to construct unbiased arguments for the prox-mappings, we propose a novel repeated sampling scheme to ensure conditional independence among estimators for the sequentially updated dual variables.
As shown in Table
2
, when all the outer-layer functions are smooth, the proposed multi-layer SSD method achieves the order-optimal oracle complexities of
\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{2})
in the non-strongly convex case and of
\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon)
in the strongly convex case, improving over the exponential dependence of the order of oracle complexity on
k
in
Yang2019Multilevel
. When there is either a structured non-smooth or a general non-smooth outer-layer function, the multi-layer SSD/nSSD method achieves an order-optimal oracle complexity of
\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{2})
. Such a complexity result appears to be the first finite-time convergence guarantee for the multi-layer NSCO problem with non-smooth outer-layer functions. Moreover,
the oracle complexity of
\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{2})
can be attained with parameter-free stepsizes, which could be helpful in practice.

Fourth, we illustrate the SSD and nSSD methods by applying them to two interesting examples; minimizing the mean-upper-semideviation risk of order
1
for a two-stage stochastic program and minimizing the maximum loss associated with a system of stochastic composite functions. A direct application of our methods leads to order-optimal oracle complexities. We show that the constant dependence of our methods can be further improved if some intra-layer problem structures are exploited.

Proof

First, for Part b), let

\displaystyle f_{2}(x)=\mathcal{L}_{2}(x;\hat{\pi}_{2}),\text{ and }f_{1}(f_{2}(x))=\mathcal{L}_{1}(x;\hat{\pi}_{1},\hat{\pi}_{2}).

Thus
f(x)=\mathcal{L}_{1}(x;\hat{\pi}_{1},\hat{\pi}_{2})+u(x)
.
Regarding Part a), for any

, the following decomposition is valid for any

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}(x;\hat{\pi}_{1},\hat{\pi}_{2})-{\mathcal{L}}(x;\pi_{1},\pi_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}(x;\hat{\pi}_{1},\hat{\pi}_{2})-\mathcal{L}(x;\pi_{1},\hat{\pi}_{2})+\mathcal{L}(x;\pi_{1},\hat{\pi}_{2})-\mathcal{L}(x;\pi_{1},\pi_{2})

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{1}(x;\hat{\pi}_{1},\hat{\pi}_{2})-\mathcal{L}_{1}(x;\pi_{1},\hat{\pi}_{2})}_{A}+\underbrace{\pi_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{2}(x;\hat{\pi}_{2})-\mathcal{L}_{2}(x;\pi_{2}))}_{B},

where

\hat{\pi}_{1}\in\argmax_{\pi_{1}\in\Pi_{1}}\{\pi_{1}f_{{2}}(x)-f^{*}_{1}(\pi_{1})\equiv\mathcal{L}_{1}(\pi_{1},\hat{\pi}_{2};x)\}

is affine,

is a singleton set such that

. Otherwise a non-negative

(Assumption

The relation

Part d) is a direct consequence of Parts a) and b).

2.3
Lower Complexity Bound

We present a lower complexity result for the strongly convex problem with a structured non-smooth outer-layer function.
Specifically, we develop a lower bound on the number of queries to
\mathcal{SO}_{2}
required to obtain an (expected)
\epsilon
-optimal solution for a class of first-order methods.
For simplicity, we assume
X
to be a ball centered on
0
,
B(0,r)
, and
u(x):=\alpha\lVert x\rVert^{2}/2
.

We take an approach similar to Nesterov

by proposing an abstract computation scheme which updates on affine subspaces reachable by
x
,
y_{1}
and
\pi_{1}
.
The abstract scheme consists of the following steps. In the beginning, provided with
x^{0}\in X
,
{y}^{0}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
and
\pi_{1}^{0}\in\Pi_{1}
, the affine sub-spaces are initialized to
\mathcal{X}^{0}:=\text{span}(x^{0})
,
\mathcal{Y}^{0}_{1}:=\{{y}^{0}_{1}\}
, and
{\Pi}^{0}_{1}=\text{span}(\pi_{1}^{0})
. In the
t
th
iteration, the updates are given by (the “+” in the definitions of
\mathcal{X}^{t}
,
\mathcal{Y}^{t}_{1}
and
\Pi_{1}^{t}
represents the Minkowski sum. )

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\text{Query}\ \mathcal{SO}_{2}\text{ to obtain }(f_{{2}}(y_{2}^{t},\xi_{2}^{t}),f_{{2}}^{\prime}(y_{2}^{t},\xi_{2}^{t}))\text{ at some }y_{2}^{t}\in\mathcal{X}^{t-1}.\\
&\mathcal{Y}^{t}_{1}:=\mathcal{Y}^{t-1}_{1}+\text{span}\{f_{{2}}(y_{2}^{t},\xi_{2}^{t})-y_{1}^{0}\}+\{f_{{2}}^{\prime}(y_{2}^{j},\xi_{2}^{j})x:j\leq t,x\in\mathcal{X}^{t-1}\}.\\
&{\Pi}^{t}_{1}:={\Pi}^{t-1}_{1}+\text{span}(\pi_{1}^{t})\text{ where }\pi_{1}^{t}={\textstyle\argmax}_{\pi_{1}\in\Pi_{1}}\langle{\pi_{1},y_{1}^{t}}\rangle-f^{*}_{1}(\pi_{1})-{\tau}_{1,t}\lVert\pi_{1}-\pi_{1}^{t-1}\rVert^{2}/2,\\
&\quad\quad\text{ for some }y_{1}^{t}\in\mathcal{Y}^{t}_{1},{\tau}_{1,t}\geq 0,\pi_{1}^{t-1}\in{\Pi}^{t-1}_{1}.\\
&\mathcal{X}^{t}:=\mathcal{X}^{t-1}+\{f_{{2}}^{\prime}(y_{2}^{j},\xi_{2}^{j})^{\top}\pi_{1}^{\top}:j\leq t,\pi_{1}\in\Pi_{1}^{t}\}.\end{split}

(2.28)

After
N
iterations, the scheme can output any
x^{N}\in\mathcal{X}^{N}
.

The abstract scheme is quite general. Since
X
is a zero-centered ball,
\mathcal{X}^{t}
includes all possible prox-mappings from every prox-center in
\mathcal{X}^{t-1}
, with every non-negative stepsize
{\eta_{t}}
and with every possible argument in

. In particular, since

\mathcal{Y}^{t}_{1}
contains every possible convex combination of evaluated function values
\{f_{{2}}(y_{2}^{j},\xi_{2}^{j})\}
and the prox-mapping for
\pi_{1}^{t}
simplifies to gradient evaluation if
{\tau}_{1,t}=0
, the argument set
\{\pi_{1}f_{{2}}^{\prime}(y_{2}^{j},\xi_{2}^{j})\}_{j\leq t,\pi_{1}\in\Pi_{1}^{t}}
covers the pseudo-gradient in gradient approximation type algorithm, i.e., the SCGD algorithm in
mengdi2017stochastic
is a special case. Moreover, since
\mathcal{Y}^{t}_{1}
also contains all possible momentum-extrapolation term in
\{f_{{2}}^{\prime}(y_{2}^{j},\xi_{2}^{j})x:j\leq t,x\in\mathcal{X}^{t-1}\}
, our SSD method (
2.16
) is also a special case. It is worth noting that one iteration of the SSD method corresponds to three iterations of the abstract scheme since it requires three independent queries to
\mathcal{SO}_{2}
at
\underline{y}^{t}_{2}
. The next theorem states the lower complexity result and its proof is deferred to the Appendix.

Theorem 2.4

Given problem parameters
\epsilon>0
,
{M}_{1}>0
,
\bar{\alpha}\leq M_{1}^{2}/(4\epsilon)
, and
\sigma_{f_{{2}}}\geq 4\epsilon/M_{1}
, there exists a nested two-layer problem (
1.2
) consisted of a structured non-smooth
f_{{1}}
and a stochastic linear
f_{{2}}
such that
f_{{1}}
is
{M}_{1}
-Lipschitz continuous (c.f. (
2.25
)), the variance of
f_{{2}}(x,\xi)
is bounded by
\sigma_{f_{{2}}}
(c.f. (
2.19
)), and

2.28
) is initialized to

,
\pi_{1}^{0}=0

{y}^{0}_{1}
and its output solution

The preceding lower bound shows the

In this section, we extend the SSD and the nSSD methods proposed in the last two sections to the multi-layer NSCO problem:

\min_{x\in X}\{f(x):=f_{1}\circ f_{2}\circ\ldots\circ f_{k}(x)+u(x)\}.

A nested function

6
Conclusion

To sum up, this paper studies the order of stochastic oracle complexity for the convex NSCO problem by proposing order-optimal algorithms under a mild compositional convexity assumption. Our complexity results reveal that the convex NSCO problem has the same order of oracle complexity as those without the nested composition in all but the strongly convex and outer-non-smooth problem. The proposed SSD/nSSD method is general since it can handle an arbitrary multi-layer composition of smooth, structured non-smooth and general non-smooth layer functions. Moreover, we introduced two motivating applications to show the method is flexible enough to allow modifications to exploit their special problem structures.

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}\langle{\hat{\pi},\delta}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\lVert\bar{\pi}-\hat{\pi}\rVert\lVert\delta\rVert]\leq\mathbb{E}[\lVert\delta\rVert^{2}/\mu]\leq\sigma^{2}/\mu.

On the rank of Leopoldt’s and Gross’s regulator maps
Alexandre Maksoud

Abstract
. We generalize Waldschmidt’s bound for Leopoldt’s defect and prove a similar bound for Gross’s defect for an arbitrary extension of number fields. As an application, we prove new cases of Gross’s finiteness conjecture (also known as the Gross-Kuz’min conjecture) beyond the classical abelian case, and we show that Gross’s p p -adic regulator has at least half of the conjectured rank. We also describe and compute non-cyclotomic analogues of Gross’s defect.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
11R27 (primary), 11R23 (secondary).

1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime number. Given a number field
K
, we denote by
S_{p}(K)
and
S_{\infty}(K)
the sets of
p
-adic places and archimedean places of
K
respectively. Fix an algebraic closure
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}
of
{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
and let
A^{\wedge}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}}\varprojlim_{n}A/p^{n}A
for any abelian group
A
.
The
Leopoldt regulator map
is the
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}
-linear map

(1)

\iota_{K}\colon\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,\wedge}\longrightarrow\prod_{\mathfrak{P}|p}\mathcal{O}_{K_{\mathfrak{P}}}^{\times,\wedge}

induced by the diagonal embedding of the unit group of
K
into all its
p
-adic completions.

Conjecture 1.1

(Leopoldt’s conjecture for
(K,p)
,
[
Leo62
]
)
.

The Leopoldt regulator map
\iota_{K}
is injective.

Ax’s method, combined with Brumer’s
p
-adic analogue of Baker’s theorem, implies Leopoldt’s conjecture for abelian extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
or of an imaginary quadratic field
[
Ax65
,
Bru67
]
. The same method also proves Leopoldt’s conjecture when
K
is an imaginary
A_{4}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}

[
EKW84
]
.

Another classical result concerning Leopoldt’s conjecture is obtained by Waldschmidt as an application of his study of transcendence properties of the exponential function in several variables. Let
\delta^{\textbf{L}}_{K}
be the dimension of
\ker\iota_{K}
. Leopoldt’s conjecture then predicts that
\delta^{\textbf{L}}_{K}=0
and
\delta^{\textbf{L}}_{K}
is called Leopoldt’s defect (we will omit its dependence on
p
).

Theorem 1.2

(Waldschmidt,
[
Wal81
]
)
.

For every number field
K
, we have

\delta^{\textbf{L}}_{K}\leq(|S_{\infty}(K)|-1)/2.

This bound is the best upper bound for Leopoldt’s defect so far.
The main goal in this work is to derive from Waldschmidt’s and Roy’s contributions in
p
-adic transcendence theory similar results concerning the Gross regulator map and to prove new cases of the Gross-Kuz’min conjecture, whose statement is now recalled.

Consider the
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
-hyperplane
\mathcal{H}
of
\prod_{\mathfrak{P}|p}{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
given by the equation
\sum_{\mathfrak{P}|p}s_{\mathfrak{P}}=0
, and the map

(2)

\mathscr{L}_{K}\colon\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}\mathcal{O}_{K}[\tfrac{1}{p}]^{\times,\wedge}&\longrightarrow&\mathcal{H}^{\wedge}\\
x&\mapsto&(-\log_{p}(\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{P}}(x)))_{\mathfrak{P}},\end{array}\right.

where
\mathcal{O}_{K}[\tfrac{1}{p}]^{\times}
is the group of
p
-units of
K
,
\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{P}}
is the local norm map for the extension
K_{\mathfrak{P}}/{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, and
\log_{p}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}\to{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is the usual Iwasawa
p
-adic logarithm. By the usual
product formula,
\mathscr{L}_{K}
is well-defined. We shall refer to
\mathscr{L}_{K}
as the (cyclotomic) Gross regulator map.

Conjecture 1.3

(Gross-Kuz’min’s conjecture for
(K,p)
,
[
Gro81
,
Kuz72
]
)
.

The Gross regulator map
\mathscr{L}_{K}
is surjective.

Like Leopoldt’s conjecture, the Gross-Kuz’min conjecture plays a central role in the formulation of
p
-adic analogues of Dirichlet’s class number formula. Leopoldt’s regulator appears in Colmez’s formula on the residue at
s=1
of the
p
-adic Dedekind zeta function of a totally real number field
[
Col88
]
, whereas Gross’s regulator plays the role of an
\mathcal{L}
-invariant in the celebrated Gross-Stark conjecture over a CM number field
[
Gro81
]
. When
K
is neither totally real nor CM, a conjectural interpretation of these regulators in terms of
p
-adic Artin
L
-functions is still available (see
[
Mak21
]
). Besides their potential applications to the equivariant Tamagawa number conjecture as in
[
BKS17
]
, the Gross-Kuz’min conjecture and its non-cyclotomic analogue discussed below also yield information on the fine structure of class groups attached to
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
-extensions of
K
(see e.g.
[
FG81
,
Kol91
,
FMD05
,
Jau17
]
).

The Gross-Kuz’min conjecture is true when
K/\mathbb{Q}
is abelian as shown by Greenberg
[
Gre73
]
. More recently, Kleine
[
Kle19
]
proved the conjecture for any
K
which has at most two
p
-adic primes. (We note that Kleine’s approach does not use
p
-adic transcendence theory).

Our first result gives an upper bound for the Gross defect
\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{K}=\dim\coker\mathscr{L}_{K}
as well as a slight generalization of Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
K/k
be an extension of number fields. The following inequalities hold:

\delta^{\textbf{L}}_{K}\leq\delta^{\textbf{L}}_{k}+\left(|S_{\infty}(K)|-|S_{\infty}(k)|\right)/2,\quad\quad\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{K}\leq\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{k}+\left(|S_{p}(K)|-|S_{p}(k)|\right)/2.

Moreover, if
K
has at least one real place and
|S_{p}(K)|\neq|S_{p}(k)|
, then the second bound is strict.

It follows from the last statement
k=\mathbb{Q}
that the Gross-Kuz’min conjecture holds for all cubic number fields.

A key observation that we use in the computation of
\delta^{\textbf{L}}_{K}
and
\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{K}
is that they are compatible with Artin formalism. For any number field
k\subset\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
of absolute Galois group
G_{k}=\Gal(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu/k)
, let
\textrm{Art}_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}}(G_{k})
be the set of finite dimensional
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}
-valued representations of
G_{k}
of finite image. We will define defects
\delta^{\textbf{L}}_{k}(\rho)
and
\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{k}(\rho)
associated with
\rho\in\textrm{Art}_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}}(G_{k})
which satisfy the usual Artin formalism. In particular, when
\rho=\mathrm{Ind}^{k}_{K}\mathds{1}_{K}
is the induction from
G_{K}
to
G_{k}
of the trivial representation, they coincide with
\delta^{\textbf{L}}_{K}
and
\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{K}
respectively. We will also define quantities
d(\rho)
,
d^{+}(\rho)
and
f(\rho)
which compute
[K:\mathbb{Q}]
,
|S_{\infty}(K)|
and
|S_{p}(K)|
, respectively, when
\rho=\mathrm{Ind}^{k}_{K}\mathds{1}_{K}
(see (
6
)). Our main theorem is the following.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\rho\in\textrm{Art}_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
be an irreducible representation and let
d=d(\rho)
,
d^{+}=d^{+}(\rho)
and
f=f(\rho)
. If
d^{+}=f=0
, then we have
\delta^{\textbf{L}}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\rho)=\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\rho)=0
. Otherwise, we have the following inequalities.

\delta^{\textbf{L}}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\rho)\leq\dfrac{(d^{+})^{2}}{d+d^{+}},\qquad\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\rho)\leq\dfrac{f^{2}}{d^{+}+2f}.

By Artin formalism, this yields the upper bound (e.g. for Leopoldt’s defect)
\delta^{\textbf{L}}_{k}(\rho)\leq d^{+}(\rho)/2
for an arbitrary representation
\rho\in\textrm{Art}_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}}(G_{k})
.
We immediately recover Theorem
1.4
by choosing
\rho
such that
\mathrm{Ind}^{k}_{K}\mathds{1}_{K}=\rho\oplus\mathds{1}_{k}
.

The first bound in Theorem
1.5
is Laurent’s main theorem in
[
Lau89
]
, but we will provide a much shorter proof of this result via a lemma on local Galois representations (Lemma
3.2.7
). The second bound, however, does not seem to follow from the classical methods employed by Laurent
[
Lau89
]
and Roy
[
Roy92
]
to study the
p
-adic closure of
S
-units of
K
, for a given finite set of places
S
.

Theorem
1.5
together with Artin’s formalism easily implies Leopoldt’s conjecture for abelian extensions of an imaginary quadratic field. In the same vein, we indicate the two main applications of Theorem
1.5
.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
k
be a totally real field and let
V
be a totally odd Artin representation of
G_{k}
. Then Gross’s
p
-adic regulator matrix
R_{p}(V)
defined in
[
Gro81
, (2.10)]
has rank at least half of its size.

This corollary strengthens Gross’s classical result stating that the matrix
R_{p}(V)
has positive rank
[
Gro81
, Prop. 2.13]
.

Corollary 1.7
.

The Gross-Kuz’min conjecture holds for abelian extensions of imaginary quadratic fields. It also holds for abelian extensions of real quadratic fields having at least one real place.

Theorem
4.3.2
provides a more extensive list of number fields for which the Gross-Kuz’min conjecture holds unconditionally. The first part of Corollary
1.7
is also proven in
[
Mak21
]
when
p\neq 2
using the language of Selmer groups.

We highlight in the last part of this article some interesting connections between non-cyclotomic analogues of the Gross-Kuz’min conjecture and algebraic independence of
p
-adic logarithms of units of number fields.

Given an arbitrary
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
-extension
K_{\infty}
of
K
, we will define a map
\mathscr{L}_{K_{\infty}/K}
specializing to
\mathscr{L}_{K}
if
K_{\infty}
is the cyclotomic extension of
K
. As noted in
[
Kis83
,
JS95
]
, there do exist examples of
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
-extensions
K_{\infty}/K
for which
\delta_{K_{\infty}/K}^{\textbf{G}}>0
, but a conjectural description of all such
K_{\infty}/K
is still missing.

In the next theorem, we fix an embedding
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\subset\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}
and we let
\Lambda
be the
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
-linear subspace of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}
generated by
1
and by
p
-adic logarithms of non-zero algebraic numbers.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
k
be an imaginary quadratic field, and
K
an abelian extension of
k
in which
p
splits completely.
Then there exist at most finitely many distinct
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
-extensions
k_{\infty}
of
k
for which
\delta_{Kk_{\infty}/K}^{\textbf{G}}>0
. Moreover, no such
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
-extensions exist if the polynomial

XYZ^{2}-(AX-BY)(CX-DY)\in\mathbb{Z}[A,B,C,D,X,Y,Z]

does not vanish on any
7
-tuple
(a,b,c,d,x,y,z)\in\Lambda^{7}
which form a
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
-linearly independent set.

This last condition should be true according to the weak
p
-adic Schanuel conjecture. In Proposition
3.1.1
we illustrate Theorem
1.8
with a classical application to the semi-simplicity of Iwasawa modules attached to
Kk_{\infty}/K
.

Theorem
1.8
can be generalized to arbitrary base fields
k
having at most
r
linearly disjoint
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
-extensions with
r\leq 2
(Theorem
5.1.1
). The main idea is that, under our assumption on
p
, one can parameterize
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
-extensions of
k
by points on a
(r-1)
-dimensional linear subspace
L
of
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}({\mathbb{Q}_{p}})
, where
n=[k:\mathbb{Q}]
. The condition
\delta_{Kk_{\infty}/K}^{\textbf{G}}>0
then cuts out a closed subvariety
\mathscr{C}
of
L
given by polynomial equations with coefficients in
\Lambda_{0}:=\log_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{K}[\tfrac{1}{p}]^{\times})\subset\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}
. We then exploit the fact that any linear (resp. algebraic) independence between elements of
\Lambda_{0}
implies strong conditions on the
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
-points (resp. the
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}
-points) of
\mathscr{C}
.

Theorem
1.8
was inspired by Betina-Dimitrov’s work
[
BD21
]
where the authors show the non-vanishing of a certain
\mathcal{L}
-invariant for Katz’s
p
-adic
L
-function restricted to the anticyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
-extension. In fact, their result generalizes to any
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
-extension with non-transcendental slope. We expect that our techniques can give further results on the non-vanishing of
\mathcal{L}
-invariants in more general contexts.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
we recall all the classical results in
p
-adic transcendence theory which we make use of. In Section
3
we describe Leopoldt’s and Gross’s defects via class field theory and we show that they are compatible with Artin formalism. Our main results and corollaries are proven in Section
4
, except for Theorem
1.8
whose proof is postponed to Section
5
.

2.
p
-adic transcendence theory

Throughout this section we fix an embedding
\iota_{p}\colon\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\hookrightarrow\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}
, allowing us to view algebraic numbers as
p
-adic numbers. The following very strong conjecture describes the algebraic dependence between logarithms of algebraic numbers (see
[
CM09
, Conjecture 3.10]
).

Conjecture

(Weak
p
-adic Schanuel conjecture)
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
be nonzero algebraic numbers. If
\log_{p}(\alpha_{1}),\ldots,\log_{p}(\alpha_{n})
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
, then they are algebraically independent over
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

We recall some classical results of Brumer, Waldschmidt and Roy and deduce some consequences that turn out to be useful in the study of the Gross-Kuz’min conjecture.

2.1.
The Baker-Brumer theorem

Brumer
[
Bru67
]
extended Baker’s method to the
p
-adic setting and proved the following theorem on linear independence of logarithms.

Theorem 2.1.1

(Baker-Brumer theorem)
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
be nonzero algebraic numbers. If
\log_{p}(\alpha_{1}),\ldots,\log_{p}(\alpha_{n})
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
, then they are linearly independent over
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
.

Recall that
\log_{p}
is normalized so that we have
\log_{p}(p)=0
.

Proposition 2.1.2
.

Let
H\subset\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
be a number field. The
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
-linear extension
\log_{p}\colon\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}H^{\times}\rightarrow\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}
,
c\otimes x\mapsto c\log_{p}(\iota_{p}(x))
of the
p
-adic logarithm has kernel the line
p^{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}
spanned by
1\otimes p
.

Proof.

Let
H_{p}
be the completion of
\iota_{p}(H)
inside
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}
, let
\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{H_{p}}
be its unit group and consider the abelian group
\mathcal{T}=\{x\in H^{\times}\ \colon\ \iota_{p}(x)\in\mathcal{O}_{H_{p}}^{\times}\}
. Then we clearly have
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\otimes H^{\times}=\left(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\otimes\mathcal{T}\right)\bigoplus p^{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}
. Moreover, the
p
-adic logarithm map is injective on
\mathcal{O}_{H_{p}}^{\times}
modulo the torsion, so multiplicatively independent numbers
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathcal{T}
have
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
-linearly independent
p
-adic logarithms by the Baker-Brumer theorem. This shows that the restriction of
\log_{p}
to
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\otimes\mathcal{T}
is injective, hence
\ker(\log_{p})=p^{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}
.
∎

2.2.
Waldschmidt’s and Roy’s theorem

Recall that
\Lambda
is the
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
-linear subspace of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}
generated by
1
and by
p
-adic logarithms of non-zero algebraic numbers.

Extensions of Baker’s method due to Waldschmidt and Roy give a lower bound for the rank of matrices with coefficients in
\Lambda
. To each matrix
M
with coefficients in
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}
, of size
m\times\ell
, they assign a number
\theta(M)
defined as the minimum of all ratios
\frac{\ell^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}
where
(m^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
runs among the pairs of integers satisfying
0<m^{\prime}\leq m
and
0\leq\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell
, for which there exist matrices
P\in\GL_{m}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
and
Q\in\GL_{\ell}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
such that the product
PMQ
can be written as

\begin{pmatrix}M^{\prime}&0\\
N&M^{\prime\prime}\end{pmatrix}

with
M^{\prime}
of size
m^{\prime}\times\ell^{\prime}
. Note that
\theta(M)\leq\tfrac{\ell}{m}
with equality if all the entries of
M
are
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
-linearly independent. The following theorem is Roy’s sharpening of Waldschmidt theorem (
[
Wal81
, Théorème 2.1.p]
,
[
Roy92
, Corollary 1]
).

Theorem 2.2.1
.

Let
M
be a matrix with coefficients in
\Lambda
, of size
m\times\ell
with
m,\ell>0
, and let
n
be its rank. We have

n\geq\frac{\theta(M)}{1+\theta(M)}\cdot m.

Roy also deduced a useful corollary for
3\times 2
matrices from Theorem
2.2.1
in
[
Roy92
, Corollary 2]
.

Corollary 2.2.2

(Strong six exponentials theorem)
.

Let
M
be a
(3\times 2)
-matrix with coefficients in
\Lambda
. If the rows of
M
are
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
-linearly independent, and if the columns of
M
are also
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
-linearly independent, then
M
has rank 2.

3.
Regulator maps and class groups

3.1.
Galois cohomology

For all fields
L\subset\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
and all finite sets
S
of places of
L
containing
S_{p}(L)
, we let
X(L)
(resp.
X^{\prime}_{S}(L)
) be the Galois group of the maximal abelian pro-
p
extension of
L
which is unramified everywhere (resp. unramified everywhere and totally split at all
v\in S
). If
S=S_{p}(L)
, we simply put
X^{\prime}(L)=X^{\prime}_{S}(L)
. Given a
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
-extension
K_{\infty}=\bigcup_{n}K_{n}
of
K
with Galois group
\Gamma
, we have
X(K_{\infty})=\varprojlim_{n}X(K_{n})
and
X_{S}^{\prime}(K_{\infty})=\varprojlim_{n}X_{S}^{\prime}(K_{n})
, the transition maps being the restriction maps. Therefore,
X(K_{\infty})
and
X^{\prime}_{S}(K_{\infty})
are modules over the Iwasawa algebra
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}[[\Gamma]]
. They are finitely generated torsion as shown by Iwasawa
[
Iwa73
]
. We let

\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{K_{\infty}/K}:=\rk_{{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}}X^{\prime}(K_{\infty})_{\Gamma},

where
(-)_{\Gamma}
means
\Gamma
-coinvariants. If
K_{\infty}
is the cyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
-extension
K_{\textrm{cyc}}
of
K
we simply write
\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{K}
for
\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{K_{\infty}/K}
. We will later see that this definition is compatible with that of the introduction. One motivation in classical Iwasawa theory to compute
\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{K_{\infty}/K}
originates in the following simple result by Jaulent and Sands
[
JS95
, Proposition 6]
.

Proposition 3.1.1
.

Let
\gamma
be a topological generator of
\Gamma
. If no
p
-adic prime of
K
splits completely in
K_{\infty}
and if
\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{K_{\infty}/K}=0
, then
\gamma-1
acts semi-simply on
X(K_{\infty})
. That is,
(\gamma-1)^{2}
does not divide the elements
P_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}[[\Gamma]]
appearing in any elementary module
\bigoplus_{i}{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}[[\Gamma]]/(P_{i})
pseudo-isomorphic to
X(K_{\infty})
.

Let
S_{0}\supseteq S_{p}(K)\bigcup S_{\infty}(K)
be a finite set of places of
K
. For any extension
L
of
K
and any discrete (resp. compact)
G_{L}
-module
M
which is unramified outside the places of
L
above
S_{0}
, we consider for all
i\geq 0
the
S_{0}
-ramified
i
-th cohomology group (resp. continuous cohomology group)

Lemma 3.1.2
.

Let

Remark 3.1.4
.

Since

Given a prime

Proposition 3.1.5
.

We have
\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{K_{\infty}/K}=\dim\coker(\mathscr{L}_{K_{\infty}/K})
.

Proof.

By Kummer theory and local class field theory, Tate’s local pairing
\mathrm{H}^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{P}},\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p})\times\mathrm{H}^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{P}},\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}(1))\rightarrow\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}
can be identified with the evaluation map
\Hom(K_{\mathfrak{P}}^{\times},\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p})\times K_{\mathfrak{P}}^{\times,\wedge}\rightarrow\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}
. Therefore, the orthogonal complement of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{P}},\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p})\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{P}},\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p})
is simply the kernel of
\rec_{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{P}}}\colon K_{\mathfrak{P}}^{\times,\wedge}\twoheadrightarrow\Gamma^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{P}}
. Using the fact that
\Sha^{1}(K,{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})=0
, Poitou-Tate’s duality then yields an isomorphism

\ker\left(\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\{p\}}(K,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p})\to\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{P}\in S_{p}(K)}\frac{\mathrm{H}^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{P}},\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p})}{\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{P}},\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p})}\right)\simeq\coker\left(\oplus_{\mathfrak{P}}\rec_{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{P}}}:\ \mathcal{O}_{K}[\tfrac{1}{p}]^{\times,\wedge}\to\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{P}\in S_{p}(K)}\Gamma^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{P}}\right).

In particular,
\dim\ker(\mathrm{Loc}_{K_{\infty}/K})+1=\dim\coker(\mathscr{L}_{K_{\infty}/K})+1

3.1.3
yields the desired equality.
∎

Lemma 3.2.1
.

Let
L/E
be a finite extension of number fields and let

.

(1)

The rule
\rho\mapsto a(\rho)
is compatible with Artin formalism.

(2)

We have

,
d^{+}(\mathds{1}_{L})=|S_{\infty}(L)|
and

. For any representation

(3)

Let
\rho\in\textrm{Art}_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}}(G_{L})
and let

be the field extension cut out by

. Then

if and only if

is totally real. If

, then

is totally real and

is a CM field. If

. If
L

(4)

Let

.

(5)

Assume now that

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}}\Hom(W_{\mathfrak{p}}^{0},k_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times,\wedge})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}}\Hom(W_{\mathfrak{p}}^{0},\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\wedge}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\oplus\mathrm{loc}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\oplus\mathrm{res}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\oplus\rec_{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}}}

Here,
\mathfrak{p}
runs over
S_{p}(k)
in each sum, and we implicitly used the fact that
\left(K_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}^{\times,\wedge}\right)^{G_{\mathfrak{p}}}=k_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times,\wedge}
.

Proof.

Let
j\colon\mathcal{H}_{K_{\infty}/K}\hookrightarrow\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{P}|p}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{P}}
be the inclusion map and let
j(\rho)
be its
\rho
-isotypic component.

Given a prime
\mathfrak{p}\in S_{p}(k)
and a fixed prime
\mathfrak{P}_{0}|\mathfrak{p}
of
K
as before, we have
\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{P}|\mathfrak{p}}K_{\mathfrak{P}}^{\times}=\mathrm{Ind}_{G_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{G}K_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}^{\times}
and
\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{P}|\mathfrak{p}}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{P}}=\mathrm{Ind}_{G_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{G}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}
as
G
-modules and Frobenius reciprocity shows that
\Hom_{G}(W,\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{P}|\mathfrak{p}}K_{\mathfrak{P}}^{\times,\wedge})\simeq\Hom_{G_{\mathfrak{p}}}(W,K_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}^{\times,\wedge})
and
\Hom_{G}(W,\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{P}|\mathfrak{p}}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{P}}^{\times,\wedge})\simeq\Hom_{G_{\mathfrak{p}}}(W,\Gamma_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}^{\times,\wedge})
, the isomorphisms being the natural projection maps. Therefore,
j(\rho)\circ\mathscr{L}_{k_{\infty}/k}(\rho)
can be identified with the composite map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom_{G}(W,\mathcal{O}_{K}[\tfrac{1}{p}]^{\times,\wedge})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}|p}\Hom_{G_{\mathfrak{p}}}(W,K_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}^{\times,\wedge})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}|p}\Hom_{G_{\mathfrak{p}}}(W,\Gamma_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}^{\wedge})=\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}|p}\Hom(W_{\mathfrak{p}}^{0},\Gamma_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}^{\wedge}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\oplus\mathrm{loc}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\oplus\rec_{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}}}

where the last identification is induced by
\oplus\mathrm{res}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Note that
\mathrm{res}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
\rec_{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}}
commute. Furthermore, letting
[n_{\mathfrak{p}}]:\Gamma_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}^{\wedge}\rightarrow\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\wedge}
be the multiplication by
n_{\mathfrak{p}}=[K_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}:k_{\mathfrak{p}}]
map, the functoriality of Artin’s reciprocity law shows that
\rec_{\mathfrak{p}}
coincides with
[n_{\mathfrak{p}}]\circ\rec_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}
on
k_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times,\wedge}
. Hence, if
\mathds{1}\not\subseteq\rho
, then the map
j(\rho)\circ\mathscr{L}_{k_{\infty}/k}(\rho)
coincides with the map of (
7
) under the identification
[n_{\mathfrak{p}}]:\Gamma_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}^{\wedge}\simeq\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\wedge}
. Since
j(\rho)
is an isomorphism in this case, we obtain the desired description of
\mathscr{L}_{k_{\infty}/k}(\rho)
. In the case where
\rho=\mathds{1}
, the map
\mathscr{L}_{K_{\infty}/K}(\mathds{1})
is nothing but the restriction
(\mathscr{L}_{K_{\infty}/K})^{G}\colon\mathcal{O}_{k}[\tfrac{1}{p}]^{\times,\wedge}\to(\mathcal{H}_{K_{\infty}/K}^{\wedge})^{G}
of
\mathscr{L}_{K_{\infty}/K}
to the
G
-invariants. Under the identifications

(\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{P}|p}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{P}}^{\wedge})^{G}=(\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}|p}\mathrm{Ind}_{G_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{G}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}^{\wedge})^{G}=\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}|p}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}^{\wedge}\simeq\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}|p}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\wedge}

induced by by Frobenius reciprocity and by
\oplus_{\mathfrak{p}}[n_{\mathfrak{p}}]
, it is clear that
(\mathcal{H}_{K_{\infty}/K}^{\wedge})^{G}
is mapped onto
\mathcal{H}_{k_{\infty}/k}^{\wedge}
, and that
\mathscr{L}_{K_{\textrm{cyc}}/K}(\mathds{1})
can be naturally identified with
\mathscr{L}_{k_{\infty}/k}
.

∎

Remark 3.2.3
.

The map
\mathscr{L}_{k_{\infty}/k}(\rho)
admits a more intrinsic description in terms of Bloch-Kato Selmer groups. Namely, let
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{f},p}(k,\widecheck{W})\subset\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,\widecheck{W})
be the Selmer group of
\widecheck{W}
defined by the Bloch-Kato condition at all places not dividing
p
(see
[
BK90
, § 3]
), and let
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\Gamma}(k_{\mathfrak{p}},\widecheck{W}^{0}_{\mathfrak{p}})
be the orthogonal complement of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}},W^{0}_{\mathfrak{p}})\subset\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{\mathfrak{p}},W)
under Tate’s local pairing. Then Kummer theory and the Inflation-Restriction exact sequence provide natural isomorphisms
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{f},p}(k,\widecheck{W})\simeq\Hom_{G_{k}}(W,\mathcal{O}_{K}[\tfrac{1}{p}]^{\times,\wedge})
and
\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{\mathfrak{p}},\widecheck{W})/\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\Gamma}(k_{\mathfrak{p}},\widecheck{W}^{0}_{\mathfrak{p}})\simeq\Hom(W_{\mathfrak{p}}^{0},\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}})
. An easy adaptation of the proof of Proposition
3.1.5
then shows that
\mathscr{L}_{k_{\infty}/k}(\rho)
coincides with the localization map
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f,p}(k,\widecheck{W})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}|p}\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{\mathfrak{p}},\widecheck{W})/\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\Gamma}(k_{\mathfrak{p}},\widecheck{W}^{0}_{\mathfrak{p}})
under these identifications.

Corollary 3.2.4
.

(1)

If
\rho=\mathds{1}
then we have
\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{k_{\infty}/k}(\mathds{1})=\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{k_{\infty}/k}
.

(2)

Assume

,
k_{\infty}=\mathbb{Q}_{\textrm{cyc}}
and
\mathds{1}\not\subset\rho
. Fix an embedding

and let

the map being the restriction-to-

map followed by the post-composition by

.

The first claim directly follows from Propositions
3.1.5
and

. The second claim follows from from Proposition

and from the fact that, if

, then the composite map

.
∎

Corollary 3.2.5
.

The assignment
\rho\mapsto\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{k_{\infty}/k}(\rho)
is compatible with Artin formalism. More precisely,

(a)

\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{k_{\infty}/k}(\rho)
does not depend on the choice of the splitting field
K
of
\rho\in\textrm{Art}_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}}(G_{k})
.

(b)

\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{k_{\infty}/k}(\rho_{1}\oplus\rho_{2})=\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{k_{\infty}/k}(\rho_{1})+\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{k_{\infty}/k}(\rho_{2})
for any
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in\textrm{Art}_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}}(G_{k})
.

(c)

If
k_{\infty}=kk^{\prime}_{\infty}
for some subfield
k^{\prime}\subset k
and
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
-extension
k^{\prime}_{\infty}/k^{\prime}
and if
\rho^{\prime}=\mathrm{Ind}_{k}^{k^{\prime}}\rho
is induced from
\rho\in\textrm{Art}_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}}(G_{k})
, then
\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{k^{\prime}_{\infty}/k^{\prime}}(\rho^{\prime})=\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{k_{\infty}/k}(\rho)
.

Proof.

Part (b) is obvious from the definition of
\mathscr{L}_{k_{\infty}/k}(\rho)
. Part (a) is true if
\rho
is trivial by Corollary
3.2.4
(1). Let
K^{\prime}/K/k
be Galois extensions, take
\mathds{1}\not\subseteq{\rho}\in\textrm{Art}_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}}(\Gal(K/k))
and denote by
\tilde{\rho}
its inflation to
\Gal(K^{\prime}/k)
. Then the maps of (
7
) attached to
\rho
and
\tilde{\rho}
coincide on
\Hom_{\Gal(K/k)}(W,\mathcal{O}_{K}[\tfrac{1}{p}]^{\times,\wedge})=\Hom_{\Gal(K^{\prime}/k)}(W,\mathcal{O}_{K^{\prime}}[\tfrac{1}{p}]^{\times,\wedge})
, so
\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{k_{\infty}/k}(\rho)=\delta^{\textbf{G}}_{k_{\infty}/k}(\tilde{\rho})
. Hence, (a) holds for every
\rho\in\textrm{Art}_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}}(G_{k})
.

As for (c), take any splitting field
K
of
\rho
which is Galois over
k^{\prime}
and put
G=\Gal(K/k)
,
G^{\prime}=\Gal(K/k^{\prime})
. Then Frobenius reciprocity identifies the
\rho^{\prime}
-isotypic component of
\mathscr{L}_{K_{\infty}/K}
(seen as a
G^{\prime}
-equivariant map) with the
\rho
-isotypic component of
\mathscr{L}_{K_{\infty}/K}
(seen as a
G
-equivariant map). Hence, the last property follows from Proposition

.
∎

We next define an analogous invariant for Leopoldt’s conjecture. For any Galois extension
K/k

Remark 3.2.6
.

In terms of Bloch-Kato Selmer groups for

).

The following lemma on local Galois representations with finite image will help us describe

in another way if

.

Lemma 3.2.7
.

Let
(W,\rho)\in\textrm{Art}_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}}(G_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}})
be a local representation factoring through the Galois group of a finite extension
E\subset\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}
of
{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
. Then the internal multiplication map
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}E\rightarrow\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}
induces an isomorphism

m\ \colon\ \Hom_{G_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}}(W,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}E)\simeq\Hom_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}}(W,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}).

Here, we let
G_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
act on
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}E
via
g(a\otimes x)=a\otimes g(x)
.

Proof.

Since
E\simeq{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}[\Gal(E/{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})]
as a Galois module, it is enough to show that
m
is injective. Choose any finite Galois extension
L/{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
which contains
E
and over which
\rho
is realizable,
i.e.
, there exist a
L[G_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}]
-module
W_{L}
and an isomorphism
W_{L}\otimes_{L}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}\simeq W
. Then we have to show that the map
\Hom_{L[G_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}]}(W_{L},L\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}E)\to\Hom_{L}(W_{L},L)
is injective. By considering
{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
-linear homomorphisms instead of
L
-linear ones in the previous map, it suffices to prove that the map
(V\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p})^{G_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}\to V\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}
given by
(v\otimes a)\otimes b\mapsto v\otimes(ab)
is injective, where
V=\Hom_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}(W_{L},{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})
and
G_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
acts on both factors of
V\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}
. But
V
is finite dimensional over
{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, so this follows from the
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}
-admissibility in the sense of Fontaine of Galois representations of finite image (see e.g.
[
FO08
, Proposition 2.7]
).
∎

Proposition 3.2.8
.

Let
(W,\rho)\in\textrm{Art}_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
, let
K\subset\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
be a splitting field of
\rho
and let
\mathfrak{P}_{0}
be the
p
-adic place of
K
defined by a fixed embedding
\iota_{p}\colon\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\hookrightarrow\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}
. We have

\delta^{\textbf{L}}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\rho)=\dim\ker\left[\mathcal{L}\ \colon\ \Hom_{G_{\mathbb{Q}}}(W,\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,\wedge})\rightarrow\Hom(W,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p})\right],

the map
\mathcal{L}
being the post-composition by
\log_{p}\colon\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,\wedge}\to\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}K_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}
followed by the internal multiplication
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}K_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}\to\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}
.

Proof.

First note that the
p
-adic logarithm
\log_{p}\colon\mathcal{O}_{E}^{\times}\rightarrow E
over a finite extension
E
of
{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
induces an isomorphism
\mathcal{O}_{E}^{\times,\wedge}\simeq\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}E
. Applying Lemma
3.2.7
to

we obtain isomorphisms

\Hom_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}(W,\mathcal{O}_{K_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}}}^{\times,\wedge})\simeq\Hom_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}(W,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}K_{\mathfrak{P}_{0}})\simeq\Hom(W,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p})
. The map

is simply the map
\iota_{\mathbb{Q}}(\rho)
composed with these isomorphisms, so the claim follows.
∎

4.
Bounds on Leopoldt’s and Gross’s defects

Throughout this section we fix an embedding
\iota_{p}\colon\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\hookrightarrow\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}
.

4.1.
Bounds on Leopoldt’s defect

Theorem 4.1.1
.

Let
\rho\in\textrm{Art}_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
be irreducible and let
d=d(\rho)
,
d^{+}=d^{+}(\rho)
. We have
\delta^{\textbf{L}}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\rho)\leq\frac{(d^{+})^{2}}{d+d^{+}}
.

Proof.

Since
\delta_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\textbf{L}}(\mathds{1})=\delta_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\textbf{L}}=0
, we may assume that
\rho\neq\mathds{1}
. Let
K
be a splitting field of
\rho
and let
G=\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q})
. Recall from Lemma
3.2.1
that
\dim\Hom_{G}(W,\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,\wedge})=d^{+}
. By Proposition
3.2.8
it is enough to show that the rank of the map
\Hom_{G}(W,\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,\wedge})\rightarrow\Hom(W,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p})
induced by
a\otimes x\mapsto a\log_{p}(\iota_{p}(x))
on
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,\wedge}
is at least
\frac{d\cdot d^{+}}{d+d^{+}}
.

Consider a
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
-structure on
W
, that is, a
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
-linear representation
W_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}
of
G
such that
W_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}\otimes_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}\simeq W
. Let also
w_{1},\ldots,w_{d}
be a
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
-basis of
W_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}
. We fix an isomorphism

e(\rho)\cdot\left(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\otimes\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\right)\simeq W_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}^{\oplus d^{+}},

where
e(\rho)
is the idempotent attached to
\rho
defined in (
5
). Using this isomorphism, we may define
G
-equivariant morphisms
\Psi_{1},\ldots,\Psi_{d^{+}}\colon W_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}\longrightarrow\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\otimes\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
which form a basis of
\Hom_{G}(W,\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,\wedge})
. Moreover, the elements
\Psi_{j}(w_{i})\in\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\otimes\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
for
1\leq i\leq d
,
1\leq j\leq d^{+}
are
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
-linearly independent by construction, as well as their
p
-adic logarithms by Proposition
2.1.2
. Hence, the matrix
M=(\log_{p}(\iota_{p}(\Psi_{j}(w_{i}))))_{i,j}
of size
d\times d^{+}
satisfies
\theta(M)=\frac{d^{+}}{d}
and Theorem
2.2.1
implies
\rk M\geq\frac{d\cdot d^{+}}{d+d^{+}}
as claimed.
∎

Corollary 4.1.2
.

(1)

Let
\rho\in\textrm{Art}_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}}(G_{k})
and let
d^{+}=d^{+}(\rho)
. We have
\delta_{k}^{\textbf{L}}(\rho)\leq d^{+}/2
.

(2)

For every finite extensions
K/k
of number fields, we have
\delta^{\textbf{L}}_{K}\leq\delta^{\textbf{L}}_{k}+\left(\rk\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}-\rk\mathcal{O}_{k}^{\times}\right)/2
.

Proof.

Since
d^{+}\leq d
, the first inequality obviously follows from Theorem
4.1.1
for irreducible
\rho\in\textrm{Art}_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
, hence it follows for general
\rho

4.2.

Theorem 4.2.1
.

Let
\rho\in\textrm{Art}_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})

Proof.

and

be a splitting field of

Corollary 4.2.2
.

(1)

Let

, let

<&f/2&\mbox{if $f\neq 0$ and $K$ contains at least one real place}\\
\leq&f/3&\mbox{if $K$ is totally real}\end{array}\right.

(2)

<&\delta_{k}^{\textbf{G}}+\left(|S_{p}(K)|-|S_{p}(k)|\right))/2&\mbox{if $|S_{p}(K)|\neq|S_{p}(k)|$ and $K$ contains at least one real place}\\
\leq&\delta_{k}^{\textbf{G}}+\left(|S_{p}(K)|-|S_{p}(k)|\right))/3&\mbox{if $K$ is totally real}\end{array}\right.

. We now explain how to prove (1). Again by Artin formalism, it suffices to prove (1) with

∎

In what follows we repeatedly use the following elementary fact. For all compact topological groups
\mathcal{G}

which arises as a quotient of

defines a continuous homomorphism

, which is unique up to scaling.

Fix

. We shall refer to the column matrices

and

as the coordinates of

.

Proposition 5.2.2
.

(1)

The map sending a

and

(2)

Let

The first point follows from Lemma

Let us prove the second claim. Fix

5.1.2
and by (
9

,

Example 5.2.3

If

5.3.
Proof of Theorem
5.1.1

We keep the notations of the preceding sections and, in particular, (

Proposition 5.3.1

Assume that

(1)

Let

(2)

Let
\mathscr{C}_{\varphi}(k)=\{k_{\infty}\in\mathscr{Z}(k)\ \colon\ \delta_{k_{\infty}/k}^{\textbf{G}}(\varphi)\neq 0\}

\mathscr{C}_{\varphi}(k)\neq\mathscr{Z}(k)

Proof.

Let us prove (1). Notice first that all

5.2.2

Assume first that

and

As

We no longer assume that

The proof of point (2) very similar to the previous one. Indeed, by Proposition

We end the proof of Theorem

with the case where

Proposition 5.3.2

Assume that

(1)

If
\varphi^{\tau}=\varphi

(2)

XYZ^{2}-(AX-BY)(CX-DY)

does not vanish on any

Let
k_{\infty}\in\mathscr{Z}(k)
of coordinates

. Take

to be the Galois closure over
\mathbb{Q}
of the field cut out by
\varphi
. Note that

and that
p
totally splits in

. By Proposition
5.2.2
,

belongs to

if and only if the matrix

N_{\varphi}(S)=\begin{pmatrix}s_{1}(L_{1,1,\varphi}-L_{1,1})-s_{2}L_{1,2}&s_{1}L_{1,2,\varphi}\\

,

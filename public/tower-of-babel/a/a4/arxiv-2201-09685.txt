Abstract
Intelligent reflecting surfaces (IRSs) have emerged as a promising economical solution to implement cell-free networks. However, the performance gains achieved by IRSs critically depend on smartly tuned passive beamforming based on the assumption that the accurate channel state information (CSI) knowledge is available, which is practically impossible. Thus, in this paper, we investigate the impact of the CSI uncertainty on IRS-assisted cell-free networks. We adopt a stochastic programming method to cope with the CSI uncertainty by maximizing the expectation of the sum-rate, which guarantees robust performance over the average. Accordingly, an average sum-rate maximization problem is formulated, which is non-convex and arduous to obtain its optimal solution due to the coupled variables and the expectation operation with respect to CSI uncertainties. As a compromising approach, we develop an efficient robust joint design algorithm with low-complexity. Particularly, the original problem is equivalently transformed into a tractable form, and then, the locally optimal solution can be obtained by employing the block coordinate descent method. We further prove that the CSI uncertainty impacts the design of the active transmitting beamforming of APs, but surprisingly does not directly impact the design of the passive reflecting beamforming of IRSs. It is worth noting that the investigated scenario is flexible and general, and thus the proposed algorithm can act as a general framework to solve various sum-rate maximization problems. Simulation results demonstrate that IRSs can achieve considerable data rate improvement for conventional cell-free networks, and confirm the resilience of the proposed algorithm against the CSI uncertainty.

Most importantly, by carefully tuning its passive reflecting beamforming, an IRS can achieve a considerable array performance gain.
With these advantages, IRSs have drawn so many interests recently with vast application prospects

To be specific, the key idea in these works was taking advantage of IRSs to establish a reliable communication link between APs with UEs, so as to economically implement a virtual cell-free network.
Accordingly, the efficient algorithms were provided to joint design the active transmitting beamforming and the passive reflecting beamforming of the AP and the IRS, respectively.

Motivated by the discussions as mentioned above, in this paper, we investigate the robust joint design in the IRS-assisted cell-free MIMO communication network, where the statistical CSI error model of all channels are assumed.

Accordingly, our goal is to maximize the average sum-rate by joint designing the active transmitting beamforming and the passive reflecting beamforming of the APs and the IRSs, respectively, while satisfying the power constraint at per AP and the constant modulus constraint at per PS of IRSs.

The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows

•

We study the robust joint design of IRS-assisted cell-free MIMO networks in the presence of imperfect CSI knowledge. It is worth pointing out that the considered scenario is general, i.e., multiple APs and UEs with multiple transmitting and receiving antennas, multiple IRSs with multiple PSs, and all the channels are imperfect, and hence, is flexible to change to a specific case. Therefore, the proposed algorithm can act as a general framework to solve various sum-rate maximization problems of the IRS-assisted systems;

•

The formulated problem is challenging to solve due to the non-convex objective function, the intricately coupled variables, the constant modulus constraints, and the expectation operator about the uncertainty terms. To this end, we present an efficient robust joint design algorithm to handle it. Particularly, as a compromising approach, we first transform the original non-convex problem to an equivalent but tractable form by extending the fractional programming method, e.g., quadratic transform (QT) and Lagrangian dual transform (LDT) into matrix-forms. Then, the transformed problem is decomposed into two subproblems, i.e., the active transmit beamforming matrices optimization and the passive reflecting beamforming matrices optimization. Next, the block coordinate descent (BCD) -based method is adopted to solve the two subproblems, where both the locally optimal solutions of active and passive beamforming matrices in nearly closed-forms can be alternately optimized;

•

Moreover, we further prove that the expectation value with respect to the CSI uncertainty surprisingly does not depend on phase-shifting of the passive reflecting beamforming of IRSs, but depends on the reflecting efficiency of IRSs, the total number of PSs at IRSs, and the active transmitting beamforming of APs. Consequently, the CSI uncertainty impacts the optimization of the active transmitting beamforming of APs, but surprisingly does not directly impact the
optimization of the passive reflecting beamforming of IRSs;

•

Simulation results demonstrate that IRSs can significantly improve the rate compared with conventional cell-free networks, and confirm that the proposed robust joint design algorithm has the resilience against the imperfect CSI knowledge.

\displaystyle{{\mathcal{R}}\left({\mathbf{\tilde{W}},\bm{\tilde{\Theta}}}\right)}

\displaystyle{\operatorname{s.t.}\quad}

\displaystyle{\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{{{\left\|{{\mathbf{W}_{l,k}}}\right\|}_{\operatorname{F}}^{2}}}\leq{P_{l}^{\max}},\forall\,l\in\mathcal{L}},

(10b)

\displaystyle\left|\bm{\Theta}_{n,n}\right|={\alpha},\forall n\in\mathcal{R}\mathcal{N},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\sum_{l=1}^{\mathcal{L}}{\sum_{i=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\delta_{\mathbf{D}_{l,k}}^{2}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{Y}_{k}{\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\right)\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{W}_{l,i}\mathbf{W}_{l,i}^{\operatorname{H}}\right)}}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\sum_{l=1}^{\mathcal{L}}{\sum_{i=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\alpha^{2}\delta_{\mathbf{S}_{l}}^{2}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}{\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\right)\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{W}_{l,i}\mathbf{W}_{l,i}^{\operatorname{H}}\right)}}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\sum_{l=1}^{\mathcal{L}}{\sum_{i=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\mathcal{R}\mathcal{N}\alpha^{2}\delta_{\mathbf{S}_{l}}^{2}\delta_{\mathbf{G}_{k}}^{2}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{Y}_{k}{\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\right)\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{W}_{l,i}\mathbf{W}_{l,i}^{\operatorname{H}}\right)}}}.

(13)

Proof.

The complete proof is given in Appendix

.
∎

The proposition verify that the expectation term in (
1
) surprisingly does not depend on phase-shifting of the passive reflecting beamforming matrices of IRSs, i.e.,
\bm{\widetilde{\Theta}}
, while depends on the reflecting efficiency of IRSs, i.e.,
\alpha
, the total number of PSs at IRSs, i.e.,
\mathcal{R}\times\mathcal{N}
, the introduced auxiliary matrices, i.e.,
\mathbf{\widetilde{U}}
and
\mathbf{\widetilde{Y}}
, and the active transmitting beamforming matrices of APs, i.e.,
\mathbf{\widetilde{W}}
. Consequently, the CSI uncertainty impacts the optimizations of the introduced auxiliary matrices and the active transmitting beamforming matrices of APs, but surprisingly does not directly impact the optimization of the passive reflecting beamforming of IRSs.

Note that the objective function in problem
\mathcal{P}_{2}
is convex with respect to any one of the four sets of variables
\mathbf{\tilde{U}}
,
\mathbf{\tilde{Y}}
,
\mathbf{\tilde{W}}
, and
\bm{\tilde{\Theta}}
when the other three being fixed. Thus, inspired by this fact, in the rest of this section, we decompose problem
\mathcal{P}_{2}
into several subproblems and solve them by employing the BCD algorithm
[
41
]
to obtain locally optimal solutions iteratively until converge.
The key steps for designing the IRS-assisted cell-free network are drawing as a flow chart in Fig.
2
. The solutions after the
t
-th iteration are denoted by
\left(\cdot\right)^{\left(\operatorname{t+1}\right)}
.

III-B

Optimization of Auxiliary Matrices

First, with fixed variables of
\mathbf{\tilde{W}}^{\left(\operatorname{t}\right)}
and
\bm{\tilde{\Theta}}^{\left(\operatorname{t}\right)}
, the optimal auxiliary variables
\mathbf{\tilde{Y}}^{\left(\operatorname{t+1}\right)}
and
\mathbf{\tilde{U}}^{\left(\operatorname{t+1}\right)}
can be determined. Particularly, note that the auxiliary variables
\mathbf{\tilde{Y}}
and
\mathbf{\tilde{U}}
only appear in the objective function and do not exist in any constraint sets, which implies that the calculation subproblems of the two auxiliary matrices constitute a pair of unconstrained optimization problems. As a result, the solutions can be obtained by setting the partial derivatives of
f_{1}\left(\mathbf{\tilde{W}},\bm{\tilde{\Theta}},\mathbf{\tilde{U}},\mathbf{\tilde{Y}}\right)
with respect to
\mathbf{\tilde{Y}}
and
\mathbf{\tilde{U}}
to be zeros, respectively.

After the matrix manipulations, the closed-form solution of
\mathbf{\tilde{Y}}
can be expressed by

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\left(\operatorname{t+1}\right)}=\mathbf{{\widetilde{V}}}_{k}^{\operatorname{-1}}\sum_{l=1}^{\mathcal{L}}{\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{l,k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{W}_{l,k}},\forall k.

(14)

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{{\widetilde{V}}}_{k}=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{\mathcal{L}}\sum_{i=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{l,k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{W}_{l,i}\mathbf{W}_{l,i}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{l,k}}

(15)

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=1}^{\mathcal{L}}\sum_{i=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\left(\delta_{\mathbf{D}_{l,k}}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{M}_{U}}+\alpha^{2}\delta_{\mathbf{S}_{l}}^{2}\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}+\alpha^{2}\mathcal{R}\mathcal{N}\delta_{\mathbf{G}_{k}}^{2}\delta_{\mathbf{S}_{l}}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{M}_{U}}\right)\operatorname{Tr}\left({\mathbf{W}_{l,i}\mathbf{W}_{l,i}^{\operatorname{H}}}\right)}

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=1}^{\mathcal{L}}\sum_{i=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\alpha^{2}\delta_{\mathbf{G}_{k}}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{M}_{U}}\operatorname{Tr}\left({\mathbf{\hat{S}}_{l}\mathbf{W}_{l,i}\mathbf{W}_{l,i}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{S}}_{l}^{\operatorname{H}}}\right)}+\sigma_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{M}_{U}}.

The optimal solution of the auxiliary matrices
\mathbf{\tilde{Y}}
determined by (
14
) is known as minimum
mean-square error (MMSE) decoding filter
[
42
]
.

Then, by substituting the obtained optimal solution of
\mathbf{\tilde{Y}}^{\left(\operatorname{t+1}\right)}
into
f_{1}\left(\mathbf{\tilde{W}},\bm{\tilde{\Theta}},\mathbf{\tilde{U}},\mathbf{\tilde{Y}}\right)
, the closed-form solution of
\mathbf{\tilde{U}}^{\left(\operatorname{t+1}\right)}
can be determined as follows

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\left(\operatorname{t+1}\right)}=\sum_{l=1}^{\mathcal{L}}{\mathbf{W}_{l,k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{l,k}\mathbf{\tilde{V}}_{k}^{\operatorname{-1}}\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{l,k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{W}_{l,k}},\forall k.

(16)

where
\mathbf{\tilde{V}}_{k}=\mathbf{\widetilde{V}}_{k}-\sum_{l=1}^{\mathcal{L}}{{\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{l,k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{W}_{l,k}\mathbf{W}_{l,k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{l,k}}}
.

III-C

Optimization of Active Transmitting Beamforming

In this subsection, we study the optimization of the active transmitting beamforming
\mathbf{\tilde{W}}
, while fixing
\mathbf{\tilde{Y}}^{\operatorname{\left(t+1\right)}}
,
\mathbf{\tilde{U}}^{\operatorname{\left(t+1\right)}}
, and
\bm{\tilde{\Theta}}^{\operatorname{\left(t\right)}}
. By omitting the irrelevant constant terms with respect to
\mathbf{\tilde{W}}
, i.e.,
\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\log\left|\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}\right|}
,
\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{U}_{k}\right)}
, and
\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\sigma_{k}^{2}\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{Y}_{k}\right)}
, which have no impact on updating of
\mathbf{\tilde{W}}
, the subproblem of optimizing the active transmitting beamforming can be simplified by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{3}:\;\mathbf{\tilde{W}}^{\left(\operatorname{t+1}\right)}\triangleq\arg\mathop{\min}_{\mathbf{\tilde{W}}}\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{\mathcal{L}}{\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{W}_{l,k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{A}_{l}\mathbf{W}_{l,k}\right)}}

\displaystyle-\sum_{l=1}^{\mathcal{L}}{\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}{\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{l,k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{W}_{l,k}}\right)}}

\displaystyle-\sum_{l=1}^{\mathcal{L}}{\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{l,k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{l,k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}\right)}},

(17a)

\displaystyle\quad\operatorname{s.t.}\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{W}_{l,k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{W}_{l,k}\right)}\leq\operatorname{P}_{l}^{\max},\forall l\in\mathcal{L},

(17b)

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{l}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{l,k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}\mathbf{\tilde{U}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{l,k}^{\operatorname{H}}}+\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}\left(\delta_{\mathbf{D}_{l,k}}^{2}+\alpha^{2}\mathcal{R}\mathcal{N}\delta_{\mathbf{G}_{k}}^{2}\delta_{\mathbf{S}_{l}}^{2}\right){\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{Y}_{k}{\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\right)}\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{M}_{B}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\alpha^{2}\delta_{\mathbf{G}_{k}}^{2}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{Y}_{k}{\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\right)}\mathbf{\hat{S}}_{l}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{S}}_{l}+\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\alpha^{2}\delta_{\mathbf{S}}^{2}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}{\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\right)}\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{M}_{B}}.

(18)

It can be verified that the objective function in (
17a
) and the constraint in (
17b
) are both convex with respect to in
\mathbf{\tilde{W}}
, and thus, problem
\mathcal{P}_{3}
constitutes a convex optimization problem, which can be efficiently solved by employing the generic convex solvers, e.g., CVX
[
43
]
. Instead of relying on the generic solver with high computational complexity, we provide a locally optimal solution in nearly closed-form of
\mathbf{\tilde{W}}
by employing Lagrangian dual multiplier method
[
44
]
.

Since problem
\mathcal{P}_{3}
satisfies the Slater’s condition and the dual gap is guaranteed to be zero, thus, the optimal solution can be obtained by solving its dual problem instead of its original one
[
45
]
. The Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions of problem
\mathcal{P}_{3}
with respect to
\mathbf{\tilde{W}}
are represented as

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{W}_{l,k}}{\mathcal{L}\left(\mathbf{\tilde{W}},\tilde{\lambda}\right)}=2\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}\left(\mathbf{\tilde{W}},\tilde{\lambda}\right)}{\partial{\mathbf{W}_{l,k}^{\ast}}}

\displaystyle=\bm{0},\forall l,k,

(19a)

\displaystyle\lambda_{l}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{W}_{l,k}^{\operatorname{H}}\left(\lambda_{l}\right)\mathbf{W}_{l,k}\left(\lambda_{l}\right)\right)}-\operatorname{P}_{l}^{\max}\right)

\displaystyle=0,\forall l,

(19b)

where
\mathcal{L}\left(\mathbf{\tilde{W}},\tilde{\lambda}\right)
is the Lagrangian dual function of problem
\mathcal{P}_{3}
and can be expressed by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(\mathbf{\tilde{W}},\tilde{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{\mathcal{L}}{\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{W}_{l,k}^{\operatorname{H}}\left(\mathbf{A}_{l}+\lambda_{l}\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{M}_{B}}\right)\mathbf{W}_{l,k}\right)}}-\sum_{l=1}^{\mathcal{L}}{\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}{\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{l,k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{W}_{l,k}}\right)}}

\displaystyle-\sum_{l=1}^{\mathcal{L}}{\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{l,k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{l,k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}\right)}}-\sum_{l=1}^{\mathcal{L}}{\lambda_{l}\operatorname{P}_{l}^{\max}},

(20)

and the Lagrangian multiplier
\lambda_{l}\geq 0
is associated with the constraint in (
17b
) for the maximal transmitting power of the
l
-th AP and
\tilde{\lambda}=\left\{\lambda_{l},\forall l\right\}
is the collecting of all the dual variables.

From the first KKT condition in (
19a
), the optimal solution of
\mathbf{W}_{l,k},\forall\left\{l,k\right\}
is determined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{l,k}\left(\lambda_{l}\right)=\left(\mathbf{A}_{l}+\lambda_{l}\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{M}_{B}}\right)^{\operatorname{-1}}\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{l,k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}\mathbf{U}_{k},\forall l,k.

(21)

The dual variable
\lambda_{l}
should be determined for ensuring the second KKT condition in (
19b
) are satisfied. Let
g_{l}\left(\lambda_{l}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{W}_{l,k}^{\operatorname{H}}\left(\lambda_{l}\right)\mathbf{W}_{l,k}\left(\lambda_{l}\right)\right)}-\operatorname{P}_{l}^{\max}
, if the inverse of
\mathbf{A}_{l}
exists and
g_{l}\left(0\right)\leq 0
holds, then the optimal active transmitting beamforming is given as

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{l,k}^{\operatorname{\left(t+1\right)}}=\mathbf{W}_{l,k}\left(0\right),\forall l,k.

(22)

Otherwise, we have to find
\lambda_{l}
for ensuring
g_{l}\left(\lambda_{l}\right)=0
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{W}_{l,k}^{\operatorname{H}}\left(\lambda_{l}\right)\mathbf{W}_{l,k}\left(\lambda_{l}\right)\right)}=\operatorname{P}_{l}^{\max}.

(23)

Note that
g_{l}\left(\lambda_{l}\right)
is a monotonically decreasing function of
\lambda_{l}
, we can find
\lambda_{l}
efficiently by employing the bisection search method
[
45
]
.
The detailed information for optimizing the active transmitting beamforming matrices are summarized in Algorithm
1
.

III-D

Optimization of Passive Reflecting Beamforming

In this subsection, we consider to optimize
\bm{\tilde{\Theta}}
, while fixing the auxiliary matrices
\mathbf{\tilde{U}}^{\left(\operatorname{t+1}\right)}
and
\mathbf{\tilde{Y}}^{\left(\operatorname{t+1}\right)}
, and the active transmitting beamforming
\mathbf{\tilde{W}}^{\left(\operatorname{t+1}\right)}
.
First, for the convenience of representation, we define the following matrix merging operations:

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{i}

\displaystyle=\left[\mathbf{W}_{1,i}^{\operatorname{T}},\mathbf{W}_{2,i}^{\operatorname{T}},\cdots,\mathbf{W}_{\mathcal{L},i}^{\operatorname{T}}\right]^{\operatorname{T}}\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{L}\mathcal{M}_{B}\times\mathcal{M}_{U}},

(24a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{\hat{D}}_{k}

\displaystyle=\left[\mathbf{\hat{D}}_{1,k}^{\operatorname{T}},\mathbf{\hat{D}}_{2,k}^{\operatorname{T}},\cdots,\mathbf{\hat{D}}_{\mathcal{L},k}^{\operatorname{T}}\right]^{\operatorname{T}}\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{L}\mathcal{M}_{B}\times\mathcal{M}_{U}},

(24b)

\displaystyle\mathbf{\hat{S}}

\displaystyle=\left[\mathbf{\hat{S}}_{1},\mathbf{\hat{S}}_{2},\cdots,\mathbf{\hat{S}}_{\mathcal{L}}\right]\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{\widetilde{N}}\times\mathcal{L}\mathcal{M}_{B}},

(24c)

where
\mathcal{\widetilde{N}}\triangleq\mathcal{R}\mathcal{N}
. Then, by defining

\mathbf{W}=\sum_{i=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\mathbf{W}_{i}\mathbf{W}_{i}^{\operatorname{H}}}
,
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{\mathcal{L}}{\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{l,k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{W}_{l,k}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{\hat{D}}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{W}_{k}+\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}{\bm{\Theta}}\mathbf{\hat{S}}\mathbf{W}_{k},

(25a)

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{\mathcal{L}}\sum_{i=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{l,k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{W}_{l,i}\mathbf{W}_{l,i}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{l,k}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{\hat{D}}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{\hat{D}}_{k}+\mathbf{\hat{D}}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{\hat{S}}^{\operatorname{H}}{\bm{\Theta}}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}

\displaystyle+\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}{\bm{\Theta}}\mathbf{\hat{S}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{\hat{D}}_{k}+\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}{\bm{\Theta}}\mathbf{\hat{S}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{\hat{S}}^{\operatorname{H}}{\bm{\Theta}}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}.

(25b)

By substituting (
25
) into
f_{1}\left(\mathbf{\tilde{W}},\bm{\tilde{\Theta}},\mathbf{\tilde{U}},\mathbf{\tilde{Y}}\right)
and omitting irrelevant constants with respect to
\bm{\tilde{\Theta}}
, i.e.,
\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{D}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{D}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}\right)}
,
\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{D}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{W}_{k}\right)}
,
\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{D}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}\right)}
, and
\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\sigma_{k}^{2}\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{Y}_{k}\right)}
, which have no impact on optimizing the passive reflecting beamforming at IRSs, we have the following simplified problem with respect to
\bm{\tilde{\Theta}}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{4}:\;\bm{\tilde{\Theta}}^{\left(\operatorname{t+1}\right)}\triangleq\arg\mathop{\min}_{\bm{\tilde{\Theta}}}\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}{\bm{\Theta}}\mathbf{\hat{S}}\mathbf{W}_{k}\right)}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{S}}^{\operatorname{H}}{\bm{\Theta}}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}\right)}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}{\bm{\Theta}}\mathbf{\hat{S}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{\hat{S}}^{\operatorname{H}}{\bm{\Theta}}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}\right)}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}{\bm{\Theta}}\mathbf{\hat{S}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{\hat{D}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}\right)}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{D}}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{\hat{S}}^{\operatorname{H}}{\bm{\Theta}}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}\right)},

(26a)

\displaystyle\operatorname{s.t.}\quad

\displaystyle\left|\bm{\Theta}_{n,n}\right|=\alpha,\forall n\in\mathcal{\widetilde{N}}.

(26b)

The above problem is still arduous to tackle. As follows, we transform problem
\mathcal{P}_{4}
into an equivalent but more tractably form by employing some further algebraic manipulations.

First, by defining
\mathbf{Z}_{k}=\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}
,
\mathbf{Z}=\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\mathbf{Z}_{k}}
, and
\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{\hat{S}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{\hat{S}}^{\operatorname{H}}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\bm{\Theta}\mathbf{\hat{S}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{\hat{S}}^{\operatorname{H}}\bm{\Theta}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}\right)}=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\bm{\Theta}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{Z}\bm{\Theta}\mathbf{Q}\right).

(27)

Then, by defining
\mathbf{C}_{k}=\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{D}}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{\hat{S}}^{\operatorname{H}}
and
\mathbf{C}=\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\mathbf{C}_{k}}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{D}}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{\hat{S}}^{\operatorname{H}}\bm{\Theta}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}\right)}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\bm{\Theta}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{C}\right),

(28a)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\bm{\Theta}\mathbf{\hat{S}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{\hat{D}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}\right)}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{C}^{\operatorname{H}}\bm{\Theta}\right).

(28b)

Next, by defining
\mathbf{E}_{k}=\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{S}}^{\operatorname{H}}
and
\mathbf{E}=\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\mathbf{E}_{k}}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{S}}^{\operatorname{H}}\bm{\Theta}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}\right)}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\Theta^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{E}\right),

(29a)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{K}}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{\bar{U}}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}^{\operatorname{H}}\bm{\Theta}\mathbf{\hat{S}}\mathbf{W}_{k}\right)}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{E}^{\operatorname{H}}\bm{\Theta}\right).

(29b)

Equivalently, we have a sequence of equalities as follows

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left(\bm{\Theta}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{Z}\bm{\Theta}\mathbf{Q}\right)=\bm{\theta}^{\operatorname{H}}\bm{\mathcal{Z}}\bm{\theta},\;\operatorname{Tr}\left(\bm{\Theta}^{\operatorname{H}}\bm{\Omega}\right)=\bm{\theta}^{\operatorname{H}}\bm{\omega},

(30)

where
\bm{\mathcal{Z}}\triangleq\mathbf{Z}\odot\mathbf{Q}^{\operatorname{T}}\in\mathbb{C}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\times\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}
,
\bm{\Omega}=\mathbf{E}-\mathbf{C}\in\mathbb{C}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\times\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}
,

\displaystyle\bm{\theta}=\operatorname{Vecd}\left(\bm{\Theta}\right)\in\mathbb{C}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\times 1},\;\bm{\omega}=\operatorname{Vecd}\left(\bm{\Omega}\right)\in\mathbb{C}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\times 1},

(31)

where
\operatorname{Vecd}\left(\mathbf{X}\right)
forms a vector out of the diagonal of its matrix argument. The sequence of equalities given above in (
30
) follow from the properties in
[
46
, Theorem 1.11]
.

Then, based on the aforementioned equivalent transformations, problem
\mathcal{P}_{4}
can be reformulated by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{5}:\;\bm{\tilde{}}\theta^{\operatorname{\left(t+1\right)}}\triangleq\mathop{\max}_{\bm{\tilde{}}\theta}

\displaystyle\quad-\bm{\theta}^{\operatorname{H}}\bm{\mathcal{Z}}\bm{\theta}+\bm{\theta}^{\operatorname{H}}\bm{\omega}+\bm{\omega}^{\operatorname{H}}\bm{\theta}

(32a)

\displaystyle\operatorname{s.t.}

\displaystyle\quad\left|\bm{\theta}_{n}\right|={\alpha},n=1,2,\cdots,\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}.

(32b)

Due to the non-convexity of the constant modulus constraints in (
32b
), the above optimization problem is still non-convex, and belongs to the class of NP-hard problems. The widely employed methods to solve problem
\mathcal{P}_{5}
with high-quality suboptimal solution are employing semidefinte relaxation
[
14
]
or quadratic relaxation
[
8
]
, however, both the relaxation-based techniques suffer from high computational complexities, i.e., the order are larger than
\mathcal{O}\left(\mathcal{R}^{6}\mathcal{N}^{6}\right)

[
8
]
. In practice, since
\mathcal{R}\times\mathcal{N}
is large, it is computational impractical in real world. Hence, in the following, we provide a low complexity alternate sequential optimization (ASO) algorithm
[
47
]
to solve this non-convex problem.

Note that the objective function in (
32a
) and the constant modulus constraints in (
32b
) in problem
\mathcal{P}_{5}
are separable with respect to
\bm{\theta}_{i},\forall i\in\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}

[

12
]

\mathcal{P}_{5}
into
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}

\displaystyle\bm{\theta}^{\operatorname{H}}\bm{\omega}=\sum_{n=1}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}{\bm{\theta}_{n}^{\ast}\bm{\omega}_{n}}=\bm{\theta}_{i}^{\ast}\bm{\omega}_{i}+\sum_{n=1,n\neq i}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}{\bm{\theta}_{n}^{\ast}\bm{\omega}_{n}}.

(33)

Meanwhile,
\bm{\theta}^{\operatorname{H}}\bm{\mathcal{Z}}\bm{\theta}
can be expanded as

\displaystyle\bm{\theta}^{\operatorname{H}}\bm{\mathcal{Z}}\bm{\theta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1,n\neq i}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}{\bm{\theta}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{z}_{n}\bm{\theta}_{n}}+{\bm{\theta}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{z}_{i}\bm{\theta}_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1,n\neq i}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}{\bm{\theta}_{i}^{\ast}z_{i,n}\bm{\theta}_{n}}+{\bm{\theta}^{\operatorname{H}}\mathbf{z}_{i}\bm{\theta}_{i}}+\sum_{m=1,m\neq n}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}{\sum_{p=1,p\neq i}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}{\bm{\theta}_{m}^{\ast}z_{m,p}\bm{\theta}_{p}}}

\displaystyle=\bm{\theta}_{i}^{\ast}z_{i,i}\bm{\theta}_{i}+\sum_{n=1,n\neq i}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}{\left(\bm{\theta}_{i}^{\ast}z_{i,n}\bm{\theta}_{n}+\bm{\theta}_{i}z_{n,i}\bm{\theta}_{n}^{\ast}\right)}+\sum_{m=1,m\neq n}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}{\sum_{p=1,p\neq i}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}{\bm{\theta}_{m}^{\ast}z_{m,p}\bm{\theta}_{p}}},

(34)

where
\bm{\mathcal{Z}}=\left[\mathbf{z}_{1},\mathbf{z}_{2},\cdots,\mathbf{z}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}\right]
with
\mathbf{z}_{n}=\left[z_{1,n},z_{2,n},\cdots,z_{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}},n}\right]^{\operatorname{T}}\in\mathbb{C}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\times 1}
. By using the property
z_{i,n}=z_{n,i}^{\ast}
and basing on the fact that
\bm{\mathcal{Z}}
is a positive semi-definite matrix, we have

\displaystyle-\bm{\theta}^{\operatorname{H}}\bm{\mathcal{Z}}\bm{\theta}+\bm{\theta}^{\operatorname{H}}\bm{\omega}+\bm{\omega}^{\operatorname{H}}\bm{\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{Re}\left\{\bm{\theta}_{i}^{\ast}\bm{\omega}_{i}+\sum_{n=1,n\neq i}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}{\bm{\theta}_{n}^{\ast}\bm{\omega}_{n}}-\sum_{n=1,n\neq i}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}{\bm{\theta}_{i}^{\ast}z_{i,n}\bm{\theta}_{n}}\right\}-\theta_{i}^{\ast}z_{i,i}\theta_{i}-\sum_{m=1,m\neq n}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}{\sum_{p=1,p\neq i}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}{\bm{\theta}_{m}^{\ast}z_{m,p}\bm{\theta}_{p}}}

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{Re}\left\{\bm{\theta}_{i}^{\ast}\bm{\mu}_{i}\right\}+\bm{\xi}

(35)

where

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{i}

\displaystyle=\bm{\omega}_{i}-\sum_{n=1,n\neq i}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}{z_{i,n}\bm{\theta}_{n}},

(36a)

\displaystyle\bm{\xi}

\displaystyle=2\operatorname{Re}\left\{\sum_{n=1,n\neq i}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}{\bm{\theta}_{n}^{\ast}\bm{\omega}_{n}}\right\}-\sum_{m=1,m\neq n}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}{\sum_{p=1,p\neq i}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}{\bm{\theta}_{m}^{\ast}z_{m,p}\theta_{p}}}-\bm{\theta}_{i}^{\ast}z_{i,i}\bm{\theta}_{i},

(36b)

where
\bm{\xi}

\bm{\theta}_{i}
(e.g.,
\bm{\theta}_{i}^{\ast}z_{i,i}\bm{\theta}_{i}=z_{i,i}\left|\bm{\theta}_{i}\right|^{2}={\alpha^{2}}{z_{i,i}}

Thus, we can only investigate
\operatorname{Re}\left\{\bm{\theta}_{i}^{\ast}\bm{\mu}_{i}\right\}
for sequentially optimizing each PS while fixing the remaining
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}-1
PSs. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{6}:\;\mathop{\max}_{\bm{\theta}_{i}}\quad

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\left\{\bm{\theta}_{i}^{\ast}\bm{\mu}_{i}\right\}

(37a)

\displaystyle\operatorname{s.t.}\quad

\displaystyle\left|\bm{\theta}_{i}\right|={\alpha},

(37b)

An equivalent expression for the above problem is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{7}:\;\mathop{\max}_{\phi_{i}}\quad

\displaystyle\cos\left(-\phi_{i}+\eta_{i}\right)

(38a)

\displaystyle\operatorname{s.t.}\quad

\displaystyle\phi_{i}\in\left[0,2\pi\right],

(38b)

where
\eta_{i}
and
-\phi_{i}
are the phase-shifting of
\bm{\mu}_{i}
and
\bm{\theta}_{i}^{\ast}
, respectively. Thus, problem
\mathcal{P}_{7}
has a closed-form optimal, which is given by

\displaystyle\phi_{i}=\eta_{i},\forall i\in\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}.

(39)

Accordingly, we have

\displaystyle\bm{\theta}_{i}={\alpha}e^{j\eta_{i}},\forall i\in\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}.

(40)

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we investigated the robust design for IRS-assisted cell-free networks. Particularly, we adopted a stochastic programming method to cope with the CSI uncertainty by maximizing the expectation of the sum-rate, which guaranteed robust performance over the average. Accordingly, we formulated an average sum-rate maximization problem and proposed the efficient BCD-based RJD algorithm to solve it and finally obtain the locally optimal solutions in nearly closed-forms.
Moreover, we further proved that the CSI uncertainty impacted the optimization of the active transmitting beamforming of APs, but surprisingly does not directly impact the optimization of the passive reflecting beamforming of IRSs.
Simulation results confirmed that deploying IRSs can considerably enhance the data rate compared with conventional cell-free networks. In addition, it was also shown that the proposed BCD-based RJD algorithm had a good convergence behavior and was robust against the CSI uncertainty.

Abstract
. In this paper we obtain new quantitative estimates that improve the classical inequalities: Poincaré–Ponce, Gaussian Sobolev, and John–Nirenberg. Our method is based on the K K -functionals and allows one to derive self-improving type inequalities. We show the optimality of the method by obtaining new Bourgain–Brezis–Mironescu and Maz’ya–Shaposhnikova limiting formulas. In particular, we derive these formulas for fractional powers of infinitesimal generators of operator semigroups on Banach spaces.

1.2.
Sharpened Poincaré–Ponce inequalities

As an application of Theorem

Compared to the classical results, our accomplishments are as follows:

(i)

We establish a strengthened version of (

1.15

\{I_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}

(1.17)

\int_{Q}|f-f_{Q}|^{p}\leq CI_{\varepsilon}(f)

for some constant

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}I_{\varepsilon}(f)=C^{\prime}\,\|\nabla f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(Q)}.

Our results show that the right-hand side of (

(1.18)

\int_{Q}|f-f_{Q}|^{p}\leq C(I_{\varepsilon}(f)-J_{\varepsilon}(f))

with

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}(I_{\varepsilon}(f)-J_{\varepsilon}(f))=C^{\prime}\,\|\nabla f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(Q)}.

The new family of inequalities (

(ii)

The case of higher-order derivatives is covered.

(iii)

We can deal with functions in arbitrary r.i. spaces (rearrangement invariant Banach function spaces) on

(iv)

(v)

As usual,

\Upsilon_{\varepsilon}(t):=(1-\log t)^{p}\int_{0}^{(1-\log t)^{-1}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(u)\,du+\int_{(1-\log t)^{-1}}^{1}\frac{\rho_{\varepsilon}(u)}{u^{p}}\,du,\qquad t\in(0,1).

9

9

.

In order to show that Theorem

The next natural step is to obtain the Maz’ya–Shaposhnikova counterparts for (

1.54

rather than

(cf. Examples

). Then (cf. Theorem

)

(1.57)

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left\|\Delta^{k}_{h}f\right\|_{X}^{p}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(|h|)\,dh\approx\left\|f\right\|_{X}^{p},

where
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
is given by

\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t)=\int_{t^{k}}^{\infty}u^{-\frac{N}{k}}\psi_{\varepsilon}(u)\,du.

In fact, the exact constant in (

) is achieved and can be easily computed for special cases of

(e.g. Lorentz spaces, see Example

).
To the best of our knowledge, this result is new even for the Lebesgue spaces

]

A far-reaching extension of (

2.
Self-improving via
K
-functionals

We are interested in the following basic question: Can one obtain an abstract approach to proving normed inequalities of type

(2.1)

\|f\|_{B}\leq C\,\|f\|_{A_{1}},

which are self-improving (in some sense)?
A possible answer relies on the classical interpolation theory. Recall that the interpolation space
(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,p},

\theta\in(0,1),

p\in[1,\infty)
, is equipped with the following norm

(2.2)

\|f\|_{(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,p}}:=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}[t^{-\theta}K(t,f;A_{0},A_{1})]^{p}\,\frac{dt}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Applying interpolation to (
2.1
), one has

(2.3)

\|f\|_{(B,B)_{\theta,p}}\leq C^{\theta}\,\|f\|_{(B,A_{1})_{\theta,p}}\qquad\text{for all}\qquad\theta\in(0,1).

Since it is known that (see e.g.
[
BL76
, p. 46]
)

(2.4)

\|f\|_{(B,B)_{\theta,p}}=\frac{1}{c_{\theta,p}}\,\|f\|_{B}

with
c_{\theta,p}=((1-\theta)\theta p)^{1/p}
, we arrive at the following estimate

(2.5)

\|f\|_{B}\leq C^{\theta}c_{\theta,p}\,\|f\|_{(B,A_{1})_{\theta,p}}\qquad\text{for all}\qquad\theta\in(0,1).

In particular, taking limits as
\theta\to 1^{-}
in (
2.5
) and invoking Milman’s extrapolation formula
10
10

10

Under mild assumptions on the pair
(A_{0},A_{1})
; see Remark
2.13
.

[
M05
, Theorem 1]
(see also (
1.8
))

(2.6)

\lim_{\theta\to 1^{-}}\,c_{\theta,p}\,\|f\|_{(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,p}}=\|f\|_{A_{1}},

we recover the original inequality (
2.1
) from (
2.5
). Specializing (
2.5
) with
(B,A_{1})=(L^{p},\dot{W}^{1,p})
(and using Lemma

), we obtain the classical BBM estimate (
1.16
). Hence (
2.5
) can be seen as an extension of (
1.16
) to an interpolation setting.

The above methodology does not work for the Poincaré–Ponce inequalities (
1.15
), because
the weights in the right-hand side prevent the use of interpolation technique. However, we are able to overcome this obstruction by putting (
2.5
) in a more general perspective of

-functionals. Specifically, note that (
2.4
) follows from the trivial formula

(2.7)

K(t,f;B,B)=\min\{1,t\}\,\|f\|_{B},\qquad\forall\,t>0,

and (cf. (
2.2
))

\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}[t^{-\theta}\min\{1,t\}]^{p}\,\frac{dt}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\frac{1}{c_{\theta,p}}.

Hence, (

Theorem

. Now, we give its proof.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The linearity of
T
and (
1.2
) imply

K(t,Tf;B,B)\leq\|T\|_{0}\,K\bigg(\frac{\|T\|_{1}}{\|T\|_{0}}\,t,f;A_{0},A_{1}\bigg)

\min\{1,t\}\,\|Tf\|_{B}\leq\|T\|_{0}\,K\bigg(\frac{\|T\|_{1}}{\|T\|_{0}}\,t,f;A_{0},A_{1}\bigg).

As a byproduct, we obtain (after a change of variables)

\bigg(\int_{0}^{\frac{\|T\|_{1}}{\|T\|_{0}}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt\bigg)\,\|Tf\|^{p}_{B}\leq\|T\|_{1}^{p}\,\int_{0}^{\frac{\|T\|_{1}}{\|T\|_{0}}}\bigg[\frac{K(t,f;A_{0},A_{1})}{t}\bigg]^{p}\rho_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt.

By (
1.3

\text{supp }\rho_{\varepsilon}\subset\big(0,\frac{\|T\|_{1}}{\|T\|_{0}}\big)

\|Tf\|_{B}^{p}\leq\|T\|_{1}^{p}\,\int_{0}^{\frac{\|T\|_{1}}{\|T\|_{0}}}\bigg(\frac{K(t,f;A_{0},A_{1})}{t}\bigg)^{p}\rho_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt.

∎

This is a consequence of the more general statement given below.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let

\{\rho_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)}

(i)

Remark 2.13

Let

3.

3.1.
Basic definitions

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
X
be a r.i. space on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, cf.
[
BS88
]
. The
Sobolev-type space

\dot{W}^{k}X
is defined to
be the set of all the distributions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
\partial^{\alpha}f\in X
,
for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{N}
with
|\alpha|=k
.
Moreover, for any
f\in\dot{W}^{k}X
,
let

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{k}X}:=\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\left\|\partial^{\alpha}f\right\|_{X}.

Note that

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{k}X}\approx\left\|\nabla^{k}f\right\|_{X},

where
\nabla^{k}f
denotes the vector of all
k
-th order weak derivatives of
f
and
\|\nabla^{k}f\|_{X}
is a simplified notation to mean
\|\,|\nabla^{k}f|\,\|_{X}
.
The
(inhomogeneous) Sobolev-type
space
W^{k}X

is defined to be the set of all the locally integrable
functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
\partial^{\alpha}f\in X
, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{N}

with
|\alpha|\leq k
. Moreover,
for any
f\in W^{k}X
, let

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W^{k}X}:=\|f\|_{X}+\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{k}X}.

Let
U
be a measurable bounded set in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. By
X(U)
we mean the local version of
X
equipped with the norm

(3.1)

\|f\|_{X(U)}:=\|f\mathbf{1}_{U}\|_{X}.

The
k
-th order moduli of smoothness
of
f\in X(Q)
is defined by

(3.2)

\omega_{k}(f,t)_{X(Q)}:=\sup_{|h|<t}\|\Delta_{h}^{k}f\|_{X(Q(k,h))},\qquad t>0,

where the difference
\Delta^{k}_{h}
is given by

(3.3)

\Delta^{k}_{h}f(x):=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j+k}\binom{k}{j}f(x+jh)\qquad\text{for}\qquad x\in Q(k,h).

When

4.1.
Proof of Theorem

Let

-Sobolev space associated with

, i.e.,

\|f\|_{W_{\mathcal{L}}L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N})}:=\|\mathcal{L}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N})}.

Accordingly, inequality (
1.38
) can be viewed in terms of boundedness properties of the linear operator
Tf=f-m(f)
, specifically,

(4.1)

T:W_{\mathcal{L}}L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N})\rightarrow L^{p}(\log L)^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N}).

On the other hand, clearly, there holds

(4.2)

T:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N}).

Note that the norm of
T
relative to both (
4.1
) and (

) is independent of
N
.
It follows from (
4.1
) and (

) that, with a constant independent of

,

K(t,Tf;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N}),L^{p}(\log L)^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N}))\lesssim K(t,f;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N}),W_{\mathcal{L}}L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N}))

and thus a simple change of variables leads to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\frac{K((1-\log t)^{-1},Tf;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N}),L^{p}(\log L)^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N}))}{(1-\log t)^{-1}}\bigg)^{p}\rho_{\varepsilon}((1-\log t)^{-1})\,\frac{dt}{t(1-\log t)^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip-312.9803pt=\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\frac{K(t,Tf;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N}),L^{p}(\log L)^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N}))}{t}\bigg)^{p}\,\rho_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt

(4.3)

\displaystyle\hskip-312.9803pt\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\frac{K(t,f;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N}),W_{\mathcal{L}}L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N}))}{t}\bigg)^{p}\,\rho_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt.

Recall the well-known fact that
W_{\mathcal{L}}L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma)
is the domain space related to the infinitesimal generator of the analytic semigroup
\{G_{t}\}_{t>0}
on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N})
. In particular, the Ditzian–Ivanov formula for
K
-functionals (see
[
DI93
, Theorem 5.1]
) can be applied to get

(4.4)

More precisely, an inspection of the proof of the estimate
\lesssim
in (

) given in
[
DI93
]
shows that the corresponding constant only depends on the following quantities

C_{1}=\sup_{t>0}\|G_{t}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N})}\qquad\text{and}\qquad C_{2}=\sup_{t>0}t\,\|\mathcal{L}G_{t}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N})}.

Note that both

and

are independent of

. More precisely, it is well known that
C_{1}\leq 1
and, on the other hand,
C_{2}\leq C(p)C(\frac{p}{p-1})
, where
C(p)=\big((2\pi)^{-1/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|s|^{p}e^{-s^{2}/2}\,ds\big)^{1/p}
; we refer to
[
K19
, p. 687, Proposition 4.1]
for further details.

It follows from (

) and Fubini’s theorem that

(4.5)

\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\frac{K(t,f;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N}),W_{\mathcal{L}}L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N}))}{t}\bigg)^{p}\,\rho_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|f(x)-G_{t}f(x)|^{p}}{t^{p}}\,\rho_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt\,d\gamma_{N}(x).

Next we turn our attention to the
K
-functional related to
(L^{p},L^{p}(\log L)^{p})
on
(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N})
. At this stage, additional obstacles arise since

can not be described as a classical interpolation space of

. However, we are able to overcome such obstacles by applying the modern theory of

, cf.
[

and the references therein. Indeed, we have that

The introduction of meaningful limiting interpolation spaces requires further modifications than just letting

in the classical norm

(cf. (

\displaystyle K((1-\log t)^{-1},f;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N}),L^{p}(\log L)^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma_{N}))

We will use the following notation. Given two normed spaces

By

5.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.47

Assume first that
|Q|=1
. Since

L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(Q)\qquad\text{and}\qquad L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(Q)

with related norms
1
, we can apply Theorem
1.1
to obtain

(5.1)

\|f\|_{L^{p}(Q)}\leq\left(\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\frac{K(t,f;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}{t}\bigg)^{p}\rho_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Recall that the
K
-functional related to
(L^{p},L^{\infty})
can be characterized as (see e.g.
[
BL76
, Theorem 5.2.1]
)

K(t,f;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\approx\bigg(\int_{0}^{t^{p}}(f^{*}(u))^{p}\,du\bigg)^{1/p},

where the equivalence constants are independent of
p
and
N
. As a consequence, we can estimate the right-hand side of (
5.1
) as follows

(5.2)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\frac{K(t,f;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}{t}\bigg)^{p}\rho_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\quad\approx\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{t^{p}}\int_{0}^{t^{p}}(f^{*}(u))^{p}\,du\,\rho_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dt\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}(f^{*}(t))^{p}\,\eta_{\varepsilon,p}(t)\,dt\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and inserting this into (

), we obtain

\|f\|_{L^{p}(Q)}\leq C_{N}\,\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}(f^{*}(t))^{p}\,\eta_{\varepsilon,p}(t)\,dt\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

In particular, the previous inequality applied to

gives

(5.3)

\|f^{\#}\|_{L^{p}(Q)}\leq C_{N}\,\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}(f^{\#*}(t))^{p}\,\eta_{\varepsilon,p}(t)\,dt\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

On the other hand, the classical Fefferman–Stein inequality asserts

\|f-f_{Q}\|_{L^{p}(Q)}\leq C_{N}p\,\|f^{\#}_{Q}\|_{L^{p}(Q)}.

As a combination of (

) and (

\|f-f_{Q}\|_{L^{p}(Q)}\leq C_{N}p\,\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}(f^{\#*}(t))^{p}\,\eta_{\varepsilon,p}(t)\,dt\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.47
if
|Q|=1
.
The general case of
Q
as stated in (

∎

5.2.
Proof of Theorem

By monotonicity properties of rearrangements and (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|Q|}\,\int_{0}^{|Q|}(f^{\#*}(t))^{p}\,\eta_{\varepsilon,p}\Big(\frac{t}{|Q|}\Big)\,dt

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{\text{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}\,\int_{0}^{1}\eta_{\varepsilon,p}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{\text{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}\,\int_{0}^{1}\int_{t^{\frac{1}{p}}}^{1}\frac{\rho_{\varepsilon}(u)}{u^{p}}\,du\,dt

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{\text{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}\,\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\varepsilon}(u)\,du

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{\text{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p},

where we have used (
1.3

Next we focus on (

6.4.
Limiting formulas for fractional powers of generators of semigroups

In this section, we deal with a Banach space

Following

(6.57)

(-\mathcal{A})^{\alpha}f:=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{[I-T_{t}]^{\alpha}f}{t^{\alpha}}

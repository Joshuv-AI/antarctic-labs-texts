On the functoriality of universal abelian factorizations
Luca Terenzi

Abstract
. In this note, we discuss several aspects of the functoriality of universal abelian factorizations associated to representations of quivers into abelian categories. After recalling the general construction of universal abelian factorizations, we review the canonical lifting procedures for exact functors and natural transformations thereof (already studied by F. Ivorra in a slightly different axiomatic framework) and we describe how these interact with general categorical constructions; for sake of simplicity, we mostly focus on the case where the quivers and representations considered are defined by actual (additive) categories and (additive) functors. We then extend these known results in two directions which have not been explored explicitly in the existing literature: on the one side, to the setting of multi-linear functors; on the other side, to the setting of abelian fibered categories. Combining these two extensions, we are able to discuss the case of monoidal structures on abelian fibered categories; the latter case has been successfully applied in the author’s construction of the tensor structure on perverse Nori motives.

History and previous work

Until the last decade, the range of applications of Nori’s categorical construction remained limited to representations of quivers into
\mathsf{mod}_{R}^{f.g.}
, possibly with further restrictions on the ring
R
. While this setting was certainly sufficient for Nori’s original applications to mixed motives, it appeared to be too rigid for possible generalization of Nori’s theory of motives to a theory of motivic sheaves. The basic idea would be to mimic Nori’s construction starting from a representation of some quiver into the abelian categories of constructible or perverse sheaves over algebraic varieties, which are not of the form
\mathsf{mod}_{R}^{f.g.}
despite being Noetherian (and even Artinian, in the case of perverse sheaves).

In the setting of ordinary constructible sheaves, Arapura introduced in
[
Ara13
]
a clever yet notationally intricate variant of Nori’s construction: roughly, one filters the category of constructible sheaves over a variety
S
by finer and finer stratifications of
S

Of course, Arapura’s approach cannot be applied to the setting of perverse sheaves over general varieties. In this setting, Ivorra observed in
[
Ivo17
]
that Nori’s construction can be extended to representation of quivers into arbitrary finite and hom-finite abelian categories: this works perfectly for representations into categories of perverse sheaves (whereas it would not work for representations into ordinary constructible sheaves over general varieties, since these do not form Artinian categories). Furthermore, it makes it very easy to understand the compatibility of the construction with respect to morphisms of quiver representations: given two quiver representations into finite and hom-finite abelian categories

It is natural to wonder whether Nori’s construction of universal abelian factorizations can be further generalized to representations of quivers into arbitrary abelian categories. This question was given an affirmative answer thanks to O. Caramello’s investigations around the categorical logic of Nori motives, which culminated in the paper
[
BVCL18
]
; in the subsequent paper
[
BVP18
]
, the same result was translated into the familiar language of categories. The construction of the universal abelian factorization of a general quiver representation
\beta:\mathscr{D}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}
can be described in a uniform and streamlined way as follows:

(Step 1)

Make the quiver
\mathscr{D}
into a category
\mathscr{D}^{cat}
, so that the representation
\beta
becomes a functor

\beta^{cat}:\mathscr{D}^{cat}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}.

(Step 2)

Make the category
\mathscr{D}^{cat}
into an additive category
\mathscr{D}^{\oplus}
, so that the functor
\beta^{cat}
extends to an additive functor

\beta^{\oplus}:\mathscr{D}^{\oplus}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}.

(Step 3)

Form the abelian hull
\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D}^{\oplus})
, so that the additive functor
\beta^{\oplus}
induces an exact functor

\beta^{+}:\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D}^{\oplus})\rightarrow\mathscr{A}.

(Step 4)

Define the Serre quotient

\mathbf{A}(\beta):=\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D}^{\oplus})/\ker(\beta^{+}).

Here, the first non-trivial passage is that from the additive category
\mathscr{D}^{+}
to its

\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D}^{+})
: the latter is an abelian category on which
\mathscr{D}^{+}
embeds fully faithfully, with the universal property that every additive functor from
\mathscr{D}^{+}
to some abelian category extends uniquely to an exact functor from the entire
\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D}^{+})
. The existence of the category
\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D}^{+})
was proved by P. Freyd in the classical paper
[

]
. Defining
\mathbf{A}(\beta)
as a Serre quotient as in the last passage amounts to rendering the exact functor
\beta^{+}:\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D}^{+})\rightarrow\mathscr{A}
a faithful exact functor in a universal way; with this explicit description, checking that the category
\mathbf{A}(\beta)
has the same universal property as Nori’s abelian category essentially reduces to writing down the various universal properties used in the passages above in sequence.
In the case of quiver representations arising from homological functors, a more streamlined description of the same construction was given in
[

]
, based on the study of Freyd’s abelian hulls of triangulated categories from
[

]
. Around the same time, the case of representations into abelian tensor categories was explored in detail in
[

. However, the compatibility of the latter construction of

with respect to functors and natural transformations has not been explored yet in full generality.

Main results

In the present note, we rephrase and extend the functoriality results of
[
Ivo17
]
and
[
BVHP20
]
using the most general construction of universal abelian factorization given in
[
BVP18
]
. Some of the fundamental results collected here are just obvious adaptations of those presented in
[
Ivo17
]
, using slightly more streamlined notation.

For sake of simplicity, we refrain from considering the general framework of representation of arbitrary quivers into abelian categories: all quivers and maps of quivers that we consider in this paper are defined by actual (additive) categories and (additive) functors. This choice is motivated by our application to perverse Nori motives and has two advantages: on the one hand, it allows us to avoid phrasing the main notions in the language of quivers and discussing their (often straightforward) extension to the additive level in detail; on the other hand, it allows us to study categorical notions (such as adjunctions, equivalences, and
2
-colimits) that are needed for our applications but lack a good analogue in the world of quivers.

The crucial functoriality results concern lifting of exact functors (
2.5
) and natural transformations thereof (
3.4
); combining these two fundamental lifting principles, we can easily study the compatibility of universal abelian factorizations with several common categorical notions, including adjunctions (
3.8
) and equivalences (
3.9
).

Our main original contribution concern the extension of these functoriality results to the case of multi-linear functors (
4.3
and
4.6
) and to the setting of abelian fibered categories (
5.8
and
5.10
); this leads to a similar extension to the setting of monoidal structures on abelian fibered categories (
6.4
).

Along the way, we also collect useful observations about the interaction between universal abelian factorizations and several common categorical constructions, such as direct products (
2.12
) and filtered
2
-colimits (
5.12
).

\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D})^{op}
satisfy the same universal property described in
1.2
(2)(i); of course, the resulting equivalence
\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D}^{op})=\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D})^{op}
coincides with the previous one.
∎

Remark 1.4
.

The abelian category
\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D})
defined as in (
1
) is usually called the
abelian hull
of the additive category
\mathscr{D}
. Note that this terminology is slightly misleading, in that the abelian hull
\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{A})
of an additive category
\mathscr{A}
is usually much bigger than
\mathscr{A}
itself! Indeed, in view of
1.2
(2)(i), the inclusion
\mathscr{A}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{A})
is an equivalence if and only if every additive functor from
\mathscr{A}
to an abelian category is automatically exact; by considering the functors
\textup{Hom}_{\mathscr{A}}(A,-):\mathscr{A}\rightarrow\mathbf{Ab}
for
A\in\mathscr{A}
, we deduce that this is the case if and only if all objects of
\mathscr{A}
are projective.

In the next two sections we will explain how to construct functors and natural transformations on the level of universal abelian factorizations. These constructions are based on the following easy consequence of
1.2
:

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
\mathscr{D}_{1}
and
\mathscr{D}_{2}
be two additive categories. Then:

(1)

Every additive functor
\phi:\mathscr{D}_{1}\rightarrow\mathscr{D}_{2}
extends uniquely to an exact functor

\phi^{+}:\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D}_{1})\rightarrow\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D}_{2}).

(2)

Given two additive functors
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}:\mathscr{D}_{1}\rightarrow\mathscr{D}_{2}
, every natural transformation
\kappa:\phi_{1}\rightarrow\phi_{2}
extends uniquely to a natural transformation of functors
\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D}_{1})\rightarrow\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D}_{2})

\kappa^{+}:\phi_{1}^{+}\rightarrow\phi_{2}^{+}.

Proof.

(1)

This follows by applying
1.2
(2)(i) to the composite functor
\mathscr{D}_{1}\xrightarrow{\phi}\mathscr{D}_{2}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D}_{2})
.

(2)

This follows by applying
1.2
(2)(ii) to the natural transformation of additive functors
\mathscr{D}_{1}\rightarrow\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D}_{2})
induced by
\kappa
.

∎

Remark 1.6
.

For every ring
R
there exists an
R
-linear variant of Freyd’s construction: given an
R
-linear additive category
\mathscr{D}
, it provides an
R
-linear abelian category
\mathbf{A}_{R}(\mathscr{D})
such that, for every
R
-linear abelian category
\mathscr{A}
, every
R
-linear additive functor
\mathscr{D}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}
extends uniquely to an
R
-linear exact functor
\mathbf{A}_{R}(\mathscr{D})\rightarrow\mathscr{A}
; see
[
BVP18
, § 1.4]
for more details.

For our applications we are mainly interested in the case where
R=\mathbb{Q}
, which is particularly easy to treat. Indeed, for an additive category, being
\mathbb{Q}
-linear is a property and not an additional structure; moreover, for every
\mathbb{Q}
-linear additive category
\mathscr{C}
, the categories
\mathbf{R}(\mathscr{C})
and
\mathbf{L}(\mathscr{C})
are easily seen to be
\mathbb{Q}
-linear as well. As a consequence, for every
\mathbb{Q}
-linear additive category
\mathscr{D}
, the abelian category
\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D})
is
\mathbb{Q}
-linear; since it obviously satisfies the universal property of
\mathbf{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathscr{D})
, it is canonically isomorphic to it.

1.2.
Universal abelian factorizations

It will prove convenient to treat representations of additive quivers into abelian categories as being the objects of a certain "
2
-category of representations". For the purposes of the present section, it suffices to consider objects of this
2
-category; the notions of
1
-morphism and
2
-morphism are deferred until

2
and
Section

, respectively.

Definition 1.7
.

(1)

We call
(additive) representation
a triple
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{E};R)
consisting of two (additive) categories
\mathscr{C}
,
\mathscr{E}
and one (additive) functor
R:\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathscr{E}
.

(2)

We say that an additive representation
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{E};R)
is

\mathscr{E}
is abelian.

We are ready to define universal abelian factorizations of abelian representations following

, § 1.4]
:

Definition 1.8
.

Let
(\mathscr{D},\mathscr{A};\beta)
be an abelian representation. We let
\mathbf{A}(\beta)
denote the abelian category obtained as the Serre quotient of the abelian hull
\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D})
by the Serre subcategory
\ker\left\{\beta^{+}:\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D})\rightarrow\mathscr{A}\right\}
, and we call it the

of
\beta
.

Notation 1.9
.

Given an abelian representation
(\mathscr{D},\mathscr{A};\beta)
, we will often use the following notation:

•

We let
\pi
denote the canonical quotient functor
\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D})\rightarrow\mathbf{A}(\beta)
, and we also write
\pi
for the composite additive functor
\mathscr{D}\rightarrow\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D})\rightarrow\mathbf{A}(\beta)
.

•

We let
\iota
denote the induced faithful exact functor
\mathbf{A}(\beta)\hookrightarrow\mathscr{A}
.

Whenever several abelian representations are involved, we adapt the above notation in an obvious manner.

The following basic consequence of
1.2
(2)(i) explains why universal abelian factorizations deserve such a name:

.

Let
(\mathscr{D},\mathscr{A};\beta)
be an abelian representation, with universal abelian factorization

\mathscr{D}\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathbf{A}(\beta)\xrightarrow{\iota}\mathscr{A}.

Moreover, let
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}
be another abelian category endowed with a faithful exact functor
\Phi:\mathscr{A}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}
, and suppose that there exists a representation
\phi:\mathscr{D}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}^{\prime}
making the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{A}^{\prime}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}

Remark 1.12
.

The result of
1.10
seems to suggest a possible approach to recover
\mathbf{A}(\beta)
directly as a subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
. Namely, consider the collection
\mathfrak{A}
of all those additive subcategories
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}
of
\mathscr{A}
which satisfy the following properties:

(i)

\mathscr{A}^{\prime}
contains
\beta(\mathscr{D})
;

(ii)

\mathscr{A}^{\prime}
is abelian;

(iii)

the inclusion functor
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}\subset\mathscr{A}
is exact.

The collection
\mathfrak{A}
is non-empty, since it contains
\mathscr{A}
itself. If one could order
\mathfrak{A}
by inclusion, then
1.10
would roughly state that the subcategory
\iota(\mathbf{A}(\beta))
of
\mathscr{A}
is the minimal object of
\mathfrak{A}
; one would thus be tempted to define
\mathbf{A}(\beta)
to be the intersection of all members of
\mathfrak{A}
. One issue with this approach is that, since the subcategories
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}
of
\mathscr{A}
above are not assumed to be full, it becomes somewhat problematic to check the stability of properties (ii) and (iii) above under intersection: for example, the kernel of the same morphism could be represented by distinct objects of
\mathscr{A}
in two different members of
\mathfrak{A}
. This issue cannot be removed by imposing that each member of
\mathfrak{A}
be stable under isomorphisms of its objects in
\mathscr{A}
, since this would force one to automatically include all automorphisms of each object in the image of
\beta
.

Before moving further, as a quick sanity check, let us note that the construction of universal abelian factorizations is idempotent in a precise sense:

Lemma 1.13
.

Let
(\mathscr{D},\mathscr{A};\beta)
be an abelian representation, with associated factorization

\beta:\mathscr{D}\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathbf{A}(\beta)\xrightarrow{\iota}\mathscr{A}.

Regard the triple
(\mathscr{D},\mathbf{A}(\beta);\pi)
as another abelian representation, and let

\pi:\mathscr{D}\xrightarrow{\rho}\mathbf{A}(\pi)\xrightarrow{\nu}\mathbf{A}(\beta)

denote the corresponding factorization. Then the canonical faithful exact functor
\nu:\mathbf{A}(\pi)\rightarrow\mathbf{A}(\beta)
is in fact an equivalence.

Proof.

It suffices to show that the two quotient functors

\pi:\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D})\rightarrow\mathbf{A}(\beta),\qquad\rho:\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D})\rightarrow\mathbf{A}(\pi)

have the same kernel. But, for every object
A\in\mathbf{A}(\mathscr{D})
, we have the implications

\pi(A)=0\iff\nu(\rho(A))=0\iff\rho(A)=0,

where the second passage follows from the conservativity of
\nu
. This proves the claim.
∎

2.
Lifting exact functors

In this section we explain how to define exact functors between universal abelian factorizations. This is essentially an adaptation of the results of
[
Ivo17
]
to the setting of
[
BVP18
]
; similar results are discussed in
[
IM19
, § 1]
. We firstly prove the analogue of
[
Ivo17
, Prop. 6.3]
in a quite rigid form and then explain how to make it more flexible; the latter form turns out to be a useful recognition principle in concrete applications.

2.1.
Main result

We start by proving the general lifting result for exact functors. In order to formulate it in a compact form, it is convenient to introduce the following notion of
1
-morphism of (additive) representations:

Definition 2.1
.

For
i=1,2
, let
(\mathscr{C}_{i},\mathscr{E}_{i};R_{i})
be a representation in the sense of

.

(1)

We call

consisting of two (additive) functors

and a natural isomorphism of functors
\mathscr{C}_{1}\rightarrow\mathscr{E}_{2}

We write it as

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{C}_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{E}_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{E}_{2}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}

where, for notational simplicity, we do not write the natural isomorphism

(2)

In case the representations

Remark 2.2
.

Let us stress that, in particular, given two (additive) categories

and
\mathscr{E}
and two (additive) functors

correspond to natural isomorphisms
\kappa:R_{1}\xrightarrow{\sim}R_{2}
rather than just to natural transformations.

Remark 2.3
.

There exists a natural notion of composition for
1
-morphisms of representations: given two

-morphisms of representations

, we define the

of representations as the triple

(F_{2}\circ F_{1},G_{2}\circ G_{1};\kappa_{2}\star\kappa_{1}):(\mathscr{C}_{1},\mathscr{E}_{1};R_{1})\rightarrow(\mathscr{C}_{2},\mathscr{E}_{1};R_{2}),

where
\kappa_{2}\star\kappa_{1}
denotes the natural isomorphism of functors
\mathscr{C}_{1}\rightarrow\mathscr{E}_{3}
defined by the formula

We write it as

, and we often depict it as a diagram of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{C}_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{C}_{3}}

.

Clearly, the composite of two exact

Let
(\mathscr{D},\mathscr{A};\beta)
be an abelian representation, and let

Then the family of exact functors

\left\{\iota_{i}\circ\overline{\phi}_{i}:\mathbf{A}(\beta)\rightarrow\mathscr{A}_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}

is conservative. Since the functors

3.
Lifting natural transformations

In this section we explain how to define natural transformations between exact functors on universal abelian factorizations. Again, the main result below is essentially a reformulation of
[

, Prop. 6.4]
to the setting of

]
and a similar result appears in
[

, § 1]
. After discussing the general result, we specialize this discussion to the cases of adjunctions and equivalences, which is inspired by
[

, § 2.7]
.

3.1.
Main result

Following the approach of the previous sections, we formalize our main definitions using a natural notion of
2
-morphism of (additive) representations:

Definition 3.1
.

For
i=1,2
let
(\mathscr{C}_{i},\mathscr{E}_{i};R_{i})

\alpha=(F,G;\kappa)

2

consisting of

•

•

Remark 3.2
.

Note that giving two (additive) functors
F,F^{\prime}:\mathscr{C}_{1}\rightarrow\mathscr{E}_{1}
together with a natural transformation
\lambda:F\rightarrow F^{\prime}
is the same a giving a single (additive) functor

(F\xrightarrow{\lambda}F^{\prime}):\mathscr{C}_{1}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}_{2}^{\mathscr{I}},\quad C_{1}\rightsquigarrow(F(C_{1}),F^{\prime}(C_{1});\lambda(C_{1})),

where
\mathscr{I}
denotes the category with two objects and exactly one non-identity arrow between them. Similarly, giving two (additive) functors
G,G^{\prime}:\mathscr{E}_{1}\rightarrow\mathscr{E}_{2}
together with a natural transformation
\Lambda:G\rightarrow G^{\prime}
is the same as giving a single (additive) functor

(G\xrightarrow{\Lambda}G^{\prime}):\mathscr{E}_{1}\rightarrow\mathscr{E}_{2}^{\mathscr{I}},\quad E_{1}\rightsquigarrow(G(E_{1}),G^{\prime}(E_{1});\Lambda(E_{1})).

Therefore we obtain two additive representations

(G\circ R_{1}\xrightarrow{\Lambda}G^{\prime}\circ R_{1}):\mathscr{C}_{1}\xrightarrow{R_{1}}\mathscr{E}_{1}\xrightarrow{(G\xrightarrow{\Lambda}G^{\prime})}\mathscr{E}_{2}^{\mathscr{I}},\qquad(R_{2}\circ F\xrightarrow{\lambda}R_{2}\circ F^{\prime}):\mathscr{C}_{1}\xrightarrow{(F\xrightarrow{\lambda}F^{\prime})}\mathscr{C}_{2}^{\mathscr{I}}\xrightarrow{R_{2}}\mathscr{E}_{2}^{\mathscr{I}}.

3.2.
The cases of adjunctions and equivalences

The results collected so far can be applied to lifting adjunctions between exact functors; this method has been successfully used in the proof of

In order to state our result correctly, we need to use the notion of adjunction in its precise form. Thus we say that an

F_{1}:\mathscr{C}_{1}\rightleftarrows\mathscr{C}_{2}:F_{2}

and the

.

4.
Extension to the multi-linear setting

In this section, we explain how the universal property of universal abelian factorizations can be used to define multi-exact functors and natural transformations of such. The results below generalize those of
Section

2
and
Section

3
. In order to prove them, we need to combine the main results of the previous sections, mostly via
3.11
.

The problem of lifting multi-exact functors to the level of universal abelian representations in a systematic way was addressed in
[
BVHP20
]
. The starting point is
[
BVHP20
, Prop. 1.4]
, which gives a canonical method to extend multi-linear functors from additive categories to their abelian hulls and shows that the resulting extension is right-exact in each variable. As pointed out in
[
BVHP20
, Ex. 1.12]
, the extension is not left-exact in each variable in general; however, as explained in
[
BVHP20
, Prop. 1.13]
, one can still use this method to construct multi-linear functors between universal abelian factorizations associated to certain representations, at least under suitable hypotheses.

Here we describe a slightly different construction that is tailored to our applications. The main difference with
[
BVHP20
]
is that we bypass the level of abelian hulls and get directly a multi-exact functor on universal abelian factorizations. Note that our construction works only if the given multi-linear functor on the underlying abelian categories is multi-exact, while the result of
[
BVHP20
, Prop. 1.13]
works under weaker assumptions (see
[
BVHP20
, Defn. 1.9]
) and can be applied, for instance, to Nori motives with integral coefficients (see
[
BVHP20
, Thm. 2.20, Cor. 4.4]
). In all those cases where both methods apply, the multi-exact functors obtained via the two distinct approaches necessarily coincide, since both of them satisfy the same uniqueness property.

4.1.
Lifting multi-exact functors

Given three categories
\mathscr{C},\mathscr{C}_{2}
and
\mathscr{C}
, a functor
F:\mathscr{C}_{1}\times\mathscr{C}_{2}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}
can be regarded in two equivalent ways:

–

as a
\mathscr{C}_{1}
-family of functors
\left\{F(C_{1},-):\mathscr{C}_{2}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}\right\}_{C_{1}\in\mathscr{C}_{1}}
;

–

as a
\mathscr{C}_{2}
-family of functors
\left\{F(-,C_{2}):\mathscr{C}_{1}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}\right\}_{C_{2}\in\mathscr{C}_{2}}
.

This observation generalizes to functors from arbitrary finite products of categories. In the course of this section we systematically adopt this point of view, and we often switch the roles of the two variables.

Definition 4.1
.

(1)

Let
\mathscr{C}_{1},\dots,\mathscr{C}_{n}
and
\mathscr{C}
be additive categories. A functor

F:\mathscr{C}_{1}\times\dots\times\mathscr{C}_{n}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}

is

On elliptic solutions of the associative Yang–Baxter equation
Igor Burban, Andrea Peruzzi

Abstract
. We give a direct proof of the fact that elliptic solutions of the associative Yang–Baxter equation arise from an appropriate spherical order on an elliptic curve.



1.
Introduction

Let
\lieA=\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be the algebra of square matrices of size
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

\bigl(\mathbb{C}^{3},0)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}}\lieA\otimes\lieA
be the germ of a meromorphic function. The following version of the associative Yang–Baxter equation (AYBE) with spectral parameters was introduced by Polishchuk in
[
9
]
:

(1)

r(u;x_{1},x_{2})^{12}r(u+v;x_{2},x_{3})^{23}=r(u+v;x_{1},x_{3})^{13}r(-v;x_{1},x_{2})^{12}+\\
r(v;x_{2},x_{3})^{23}r(u;x_{1},x_{3})^{13}.

The upper indices in this equation indicate the corresponding embeddings of

\lieA\otimes\lieA
into
\lieA\otimes\lieA\otimes\lieA
. For example,
the germ
r^{13}
is defined as

r^{13}:\mathbb{C}^{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}}\lieA\otimes\lieA\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\imath_{13}}}{{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}}\lieA\otimes\lieA\otimes\lieA,

where
\imath_{13}(x\otimes y)=x\otimes 1\otimes y
. Two other
germs
r^{12}
and
r^{23}
are defined in a similar way.
We are interested in those solutions of AYBE, which are non-degenerate, skew-symmetric (meaning that

r(v;x_{1},x_{2})=-r^{21}(-v;x_{2},x_{1})
) and which admit a Laurent expansion of the form

(2)

r(v;x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{\mathbbm{1}\otimes\mathbbm{1}}{v}+r_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})+vr_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})+v^{2}r_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})+\dots

All elliptic and trigonometric solutions of AYBE satisfying (
2
) were classified in
[
9
,
10
]
. Recall the description of elliptic solutions of AYBE.

Let
\varepsilon=\exp\bigl(\frac{2\pi id}{n}\bigr)
, where
0<d<n
is such that
\gcd(d,n)=1
. We put

(3)

X=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&0&\dots&0\\
0&\varepsilon&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&\varepsilon^{n-1}\end{array}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad Y=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&1&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&1\\
1&0&\dots&0\end{array}\right).

For any
(k,l)\in I:=\bigl\{1,\dots,n\bigr\}\times\bigl\{1,\dots,n\bigr\}
denote

Z_{(k,l)}=Y^{k}X^{-l}
and
Z_{(k,l)}^{\vee}=\frac{1}{n}X^{l}Y^{-k}.

Then the following expression

(4)

r_{((n,d),\tau)}(v;x_{1},x_{2})=\sum\limits_{(k,l)\in I}\exp\Bigl(\frac{2\pi id}{n}kx\Bigr)\sigma\Bigl(v+\frac{d}{n}\bigl(k\tau+l\bigr),x\Bigr)Z_{(k,l)}^{\vee}\otimes Z_{(k,l)}

is a solution of AYBE satisfying (
2
), where
x=x_{2}-x_{1}
and

(5)

\sigma(a,z)=2\pi i\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\exp(-2\pi inz)}{1-\exp\bigl(-2\pi i(a-2\pi in\tau)\bigr)}

is the Kronecker elliptic function
[
12
]
for
\tau\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\mathsf{Im}(\tau)>0
. See also
[
8
, Section III]
for a direct proof of this fact.

In his recent work
[
11
]
Polishchuk showed that non-degenerate skew-symmetric solutions of AYBE satisfying (
2
) can be obtained from appropriate triple Massey products in the perfect derived category of coherent sheaves
\Perf(\mathbb{E})
on a non-commutative projective curve
\mathbb{E}=(E,\mathcal{A})
, where
E
is an irreducible projective curve over
\mathbb{C}
of arithmetic genus one and
\mathcal{A}
is a symmetric spherical order on
E
.
A simplest example of such an order is given by
\mathcal{A}=\mbox{\emph{End}}_{E}(\mathcal{F})
, where
\mathcal{F}
is a simple vector bundle on
E
. Let
E=E_{\tau}:=\mathbb{C}/\langle 1,\tau\rangle
be the elliptic curve determined by
\tau\in\mathbb{C}
and
\mathcal{F}
be a simple vector bundle of rank
n
and degree
d
on
E
. It follows from results of Atiyah
[
1
]
that such
\mathcal{F}
exists and the sheaf of algebras
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{(n,d)}:=\mbox{\emph{End}}_{E}(\mathcal{F})
does not depend on the choice of
\mathcal{F}
. We show that the solution of AYBE arising from the non-commutative projective curve
\bigl(E_{\tau},\mathcal{A}_{(n,d)}\bigr)
is given by the formula (
4
). In
[
9
, Section 2]
, the corresponding computations were performed using the homological mirror symmetry and explicit formulae for triple Massey products in the Fukaya category of a torus. The expression for the resulted solution of AYBE (see
[
9
, formula (2.3)]
) was different from (
4
). Our computations are straightforward and based by techniques developed in the articles
[
6
,
5
]
.

2.
Symmetric spherical orders on curves of genus one and AYBE

In this section we make a brief review of Polishchuk’s construction
[
11
]
.
Let
E
be an irreducible projective curve over
\mathbb{C}
of arithmetic genus one,
\breve{E}
its smooth part,
\mathcal{O}
its structure sheaf,
\mathcal{K}
the sheaf of rational functions on
E
and
\Omega
the sheaf of regular differential one-forms on
E
.
There exists a regular differential one-form
\omega\in\Gamma(E,\Omega)
such that

\Gamma(E,\Omega)=\mathbb{C}\omega
. Such
\omega
also defines an isomorphism
\mathcal{O}\cong\Omega
. If
E
is singular then it is rational. In this case, let

\mathbb{P}^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu}}{{\rightarrow}}E
be the normalization morphism and
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}=\nu_{\ast}\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\bigr)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a sheaf of orders on
E
. By definition,
\mathcal{A}
is a torsion free coherent sheaf of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras on
E
such that
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{K}\cong\mbox{\emph{Mat}}_{n}(\mathcal{K})
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For any order
\mathcal{A}
we have the canonical trace morphism
\mathcal{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t}}{{\rightarrow}}\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}
, which coincides with the restriction of the trace morphism
\mathcal{A}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{K}\cong\mbox{\emph{Mat}}_{n}(\mathcal{K})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{K}
(if
E
is smooth then
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}=\mathcal{O}
). Following
[
11
]
, the order
\mathcal{A}
is called
symmetric spherical
if the following conditions are fulfilled:

•

The image of the trace morphism
t
is
\mathcal{O}
and the induced morphism of coherent sheaves
\mathcal{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t^{\sharp}}}{{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}}\mathcal{A}^{\vee}:=\mbox{\emph{Hom}}_{E}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{O})
is an isomorphism.

•

We have:
\Gamma(E,\mathcal{A})\cong\mathbb{C}
.

Consider the non-commutative projective curve
\mathbb{E}=(E,\mathcal{A})
. Let
\Coh(\mathbb{E})
be the category of coherent sheaves on
\mathbb{E}
(these are sheaves of
\mathcal{A}
-modules which are coherent as
\mathcal{O}
-modules) and
\Perf(\mathbb{E})
be the corresponding perfect derived category. Recall that
\Omega_{\mathbb{E}}:=\mbox{\emph{Hom}}_{E}(\mathcal{A},\Omega)
is a dualising bimodule of
\mathbb{E}
. If
\mathcal{A}
is symmetric then

\Omega_{\mathbb{E}}\cong\mathcal{A}
as
\mathcal{A}
-bimodules and
\Perf(\mathbb{E})
is a triangulated 1-Calabi–Yau category. The last assertion means that for any pair of objects
\mathcal{G}^{\scriptstyle{\bullet}},\mathcal{H}^{\scriptstyle{\bullet}}
in
\Perf(\mathbb{E})
there is an isomorphism of vector spaces

(6)

\Hom_{\mathbb{E}}\bigl(\mathcal{G}^{\scriptstyle{\bullet}},\mathcal{H}^{\scriptstyle{\bullet}}\bigr)\cong\Hom_{\mathbb{E}}\bigl(\mathcal{H}^{\scriptstyle{\bullet}},\mathcal{G}^{\scriptstyle{\bullet}}[1]\bigr)^{\ast}

which is functorial in both arguments.

Let
P=\underline{\Pic}^{0}(E)
be the Jacobian of
E
and
\mathcal{L}\in\Pic(P\times E)
be a universal line bundle. For any
v\in P
, let
\mathcal{L}^{v}:=\mathcal{L}\big|_{\{v\}\times E}\in\Pic^{0}(E)
and
\mathcal{A}^{v}:=\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{L}^{v}\in\Coh(\mathbb{E})
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The coherent sheaf
\mathcal{A}
is semi-stable of slope zero. Moreover,

(7)

\Gamma(E,\mathcal{A}^{v})=0=H^{1}(E,\mathcal{A}^{v})

for all but finitely many points
v\in P
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the kernel of the trace morphism
\mathcal{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{O}
. It follows from the long exact cohomology sequence of

0\rightarrow\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{O}\rightarrow 0

that
H^{0}(E,\mathcal{B})=0=H^{1}(E,\mathcal{B})
. Hence,
\mathcal{B}
is a semi-stable coherent sheaf on

E
of slope zero and
\mathcal{A}\cong\mathcal{B}\oplus\mathcal{O}
. It follows that
\mathcal{A}
is semi-stable, too. The latter fact also implies the vanishing
\Gamma(E,\mathcal{A}^{v})=0=H^{1}(E,\mathcal{A}^{v})
for all but finitely many
v\in P
.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

There exists a proper closed subset
D\subset P\times P
such that

(8)

\Hom_{\mathbb{E}}\bigl(\mathcal{A}^{v_{1}},\mathcal{A}^{v_{2}}\bigr)=0=\Ext^{1}_{\mathbb{E}}\bigl(\mathcal{A}^{v_{1}},\mathcal{A}^{v_{2}}\bigr)

for all
v_{1},v_{2}\in(P\times P)\setminus D
.

Proof.

This statement follows from the isomorphisms

(9)

\Ext^{i}_{\mathbb{E}}\bigl(\mathcal{A}^{v_{1}},\mathcal{A}^{v_{2}}\bigr)\cong H^{i}\bigl(E,\mbox{\emph{End}}_{\mathbb{E}}(\mathcal{A}^{v_{1}},\mathcal{A}^{v_{2}})\bigr)\cong H^{i}\bigl(E,\mathcal{A}^{v_{2}-v_{1}}\bigr),\quad i=0,1

and the vanishing (
7
).
∎

Recall that for any
x,y\in\breve{E}

we have the following standard short exact sequences

(10)

0\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\Omega\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\Omega(x)\xrightarrow{\underline{\res}_{x}}\mathbb{C}_{x}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow 0\quad\mbox{\rm and}\quad 0\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(-y)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}\xrightarrow{\underline{\ev}_{y}}\mathbb{C}_{y}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow 0,

where
\underline{\res}_{x}
and
\underline{\ev}_{y}
are the residue and evaluation morphisms, respectively. Using the isomorphism

\mathcal{O}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega}}{{\rightarrow}}\Omega
, we can rewrite the first short exact sequence as

(11)

0\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(x)\xrightarrow{\underline{\res}^{\omega}_{x}}\mathbb{C}_{x}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow 0.

For any
\mathcal{H}\in\Coh(\mathbb{E})
denote
\mathcal{H}\big|_{x}:=\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathbb{C}_{x}\in\Coh(\mathbb{E})
. Tensoring (
11
) by
\mathcal{A}^{v}
(where

v\in P
is an arbitrary point), we get the following short exact sequence in
\Coh(\mathbb{E})
:

0\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{v}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{v}(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{v}\big|_{x}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow 0.

Next, for any
(u,v)\in P\times P
we have the induced long exact sequence of vector spaces

0\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\Hom_{\mathbb{E}}(\mathcal{A}^{u},\mathcal{A}^{v}\bigr)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\Hom_{\mathbb{E}}(\mathcal{A}^{u},\mathcal{A}^{v}(x)\bigr)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\Hom_{\mathbb{E}}(\mathcal{A}^{u},\mathcal{A}^{v}\big|_{x}\bigr)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\Ext^{1}_{\mathbb{E}}(\mathcal{A}^{u},\mathcal{A}^{v}\bigr).

It follows from (
7
) that the linear map

(12)

\Hom_{\mathbb{E}}(\mathcal{A}^{u},\mathcal{A}^{v}(x)\bigr)\xrightarrow{\underline{\res}^{\mathcal{A}}(u,v;x)}\Hom_{\mathbb{E}}(\mathcal{A}^{v}\big|_{x},\mathcal{A}^{v}\big|_{x}\bigr)

is an isomorphism if
(u,v)\in(P\times P)\setminus D
.

Similarly, for any
v\in P
and
x\neq y\in\breve{E}
we have the following short exact sequence

0\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{v}(x-y)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{v}(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{v}(x)\big|_{y}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow 0

in
\Coh(\mathbb{E})
, which is obtained by tensoring the evaluation sequence (
10
) by
\mathcal{A}^{v}(x)
. After applying to it the functor

\Hom_{\mathbb{E}}(\mathcal{A}^{u},\,-\,)
and using a canonical isomorphism
\mathcal{A}^{v}\big|_{y}\cong\mathcal{A}^{v}(x)\big|_{y}
, we obtain a linear map

(13)

\Hom_{\mathbb{E}}(\mathcal{A}^{u},\mathcal{A}^{v}(x)\bigr)\xrightarrow{\underline{\ev}^{\mathcal{A}}(u,v;x,y)}\Hom_{\mathbb{E}}(\mathcal{A}^{u}\big|_{y},\mathcal{A}^{v}\big|_{y}\bigr).

\Hom_{\mathbb{E}}\bigl(\mathcal{A}^{u}\big|_{x},\mathcal{A}^{v}\big|_{x}\bigr)\xrightarrow{\alpha(u,v;x,y)}\Hom_{\mathbb{E}}\bigl(\mathcal{A}^{u}\big|_{y},\mathcal{A}^{v}\big|_{y}\bigr)
be (the unique) linear map making the following diagram of vector spaces

commutative. Let
\gamma(u,v;x,y)\in\Hom_{\mathbb{E}}\bigl(\mathcal{A}^{v}\big|_{x},\mathcal{A}^{u}\big|_{x}\bigr)\otimes\Hom_{\mathbb{E}}\bigl(\mathcal{A}^{u}\big|_{y},\mathcal{A}^{v}\big|_{y}\bigr)

Let

P\times P\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eta}}{{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}}P
be the group operation on
P
and
o\in P
be the corresponding neutral element (i.e.
\mathcal{O}\cong\mathcal{L}^{o}
). Consider the canonical projections

P\times P\times E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{i}}}{{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}}P\times E,\,(x_{1},x_{2};x)\mapstochar\rightarrow(x_{i},x)

and
P\times P\times E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{\circ}}}{{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}}P\times E,\,(x_{1},x_{2};x)\mapstochar\rightarrow(x_{1},x_{2})

(\eta\times\mathbbm{1})^{\ast}\mathcal{L}\cong\pi_{1}^{\ast}\mathcal{L}\otimes\pi_{2}^{\ast}\mathcal{L}\otimes\pi_{\circ}^{\ast}\mathcal{S}.

In particular,
\mathcal{L}^{v_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{v_{2}}\cong\mathcal{L}^{v_{1}+v_{2}}
, where

.

For any type of

(elliptic, nodal or cuspidal) there exists a complex analytic covering map

(\mathbb{C},+)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\chi}}{{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}}(P,\eta)
, which is also a group homomorphism. In this way we get a local coordinate on
P
in a neighbourhood of

. Next, we put:

\overline{\mathcal{L}}:=(\chi\times\mathbbm{1})^{\ast}\mathcal{L}
. Since any line bundle on
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}
is trivial, we get from (

) an induced isomorphism

(\bar{\eta}\times\mathbbm{1})^{\ast}\overline{\mathcal{L}}\cong\bar{\pi}_{1}^{\ast}\overline{\mathcal{L}}\otimes\bar{\pi}_{2}^{\ast}\overline{\mathcal{L}},

where
\bar{\eta}
(respectively,

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
r_{((n,d),\tau)}(v;x,y)
be the solution of AYBE corresponding to the datum
\bigl(E_{\tau},\mathcal{A}_{(n,d)}\bigr)
with respect to the trivializations
\xi
(respectively,
\zeta
) of

\mathcal{A}
(respectively,
\mathcal{L}
) introduced above. Then it
is given by the expression (
4
).

Proof.

We first compute an explicit basis of the vector space

\Sol\bigl((n,d),v,x\bigr)
.
Let

F(w)=\sum\limits_{(k,l)\in I}f_{(k,l)}(w)Z_{(k,l)}.

The condition
F\in\Sol\bigl((n,d),v,x\bigr)
yields the following constraints on
the coefficients
f_{(k,l)}
:

(39)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}f_{(k,l)}(w+1)&=&\varepsilon^{k}f_{(k,l)}(w)\\
f_{(k,l)}(w+\tau)&=&\varepsilon^{l}\varphi_{x-v}(w)f_{(k,l)}(w).\end{array}\right.

It follows from Lemma
4.2
that the vector space of holomorphic solutions of the system (
39
)
is one-dimensional and generated by the function

f_{(k,l)}(w)=\exp\Bigl(-\frac{2\pi id}{n}kw\Bigr)\theta\Bigl(w+\frac{1+\tau}{2}+v-x-\frac{d}{n}(k\tau-l)\Bigr).

From Proposition
4.3
and formula (
33
) it follows that
r_{((n,d),\tau)}(v;x,y)
is given by the following expression:

r_{((n,d),\tau)}(v;x,y)=\sum\limits_{(k,l)\in I}r_{(k,l)}(v;z)\,Z_{(k,l)}^{\vee}\otimes Z_{(k,l)},

where
z=y-x
and

r_{(k,l)}(v;z)=\exp\Bigl(-\frac{2\pi id}{n}kz\Bigr)\frac{\displaystyle\theta^{\prime}\Bigl(\frac{1+\tau}{2}\Bigr)\theta\Bigl(z+v+\frac{1+\tau}{2}-\frac{d}{n}(k\tau-l)\Bigr)}{\displaystyle\theta\Bigl(v+\frac{1+\tau}{2}-\frac{d}{n}(k\tau-l)\Bigr)\theta\Bigl(z+\frac{1+\tau}{2}\Bigr)}.

Relation (
35
) implies that

\frac{\displaystyle\theta^{\prime}\Bigl(\frac{1+\tau}{2}\Bigr)\theta\Bigl(z+v+\frac{1+\tau}{2}-\frac{d}{n}(k\tau-l)\Bigr)}{\displaystyle\theta\Bigl(v+\frac{1+\tau}{2}-\frac{d}{n}(k\tau-l)\Bigr)\theta\Bigl(z+\frac{1+\tau}{2}\Bigr)}=\frac{\displaystyle\theta^{\prime}\Bigl(\frac{1+\tau}{2}\Bigr)\bar{\theta}\Bigl(z+v-\frac{d}{n}(k\tau-l)\Bigr)}{\displaystyle i\exp\Bigl(-\pi i\frac{\tau}{4}\Bigr)\bar{\theta}\Bigl(v-\frac{d}{n}(k\tau-l)\Bigr)\bar{\theta}(z)}

Moreover, it follows from (
35
) that
\theta^{\prime}\Bigl(\dfrac{1+\tau}{2}\Bigr)=i\exp\Bigl(-\pi i\dfrac{\tau}{4}\Bigr)\bar{\theta}^{\prime}(0)
. Hence, we get:

r_{(k,l)}(v;z)=\exp\Bigl(-\frac{2\pi id}{n}kz\Bigr)\frac{\displaystyle\bar{\theta}^{\prime}(0)\bar{\theta}\Bigl(z+v-\frac{d}{n}(k\tau-l)\Bigr)}{\displaystyle\bar{\theta}\Bigl(v-\frac{d}{n}(k\tau-l)\Bigr)\bar{\theta}(z)}\\
=\exp\Bigl(-\frac{2\pi id}{n}kz\Bigr)\sigma\Bigl(v-\frac{d}{n}(k\tau-l),z\Bigr).

Here we use the fact that

5

\sigma(u,z)=\dfrac{\displaystyle\bar{\theta}^{\prime}(0)\bar{\theta}_{1}(u+z)}{\displaystyle\bar{\theta}(u)\bar{\theta}(z)}

[
13

\left\{1,\dots,n\right\}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\left\{0,\dots,n-1\right\},k\mapstochar\rightarrow(n-k)

we end up with the expression (

A discrete formulation of the Kane-Mele ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} invariant Preprint: YITP-23-61
Ken Shiozaki

Abstract
We present a discrete formulation of the Kane-Mele ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} invariant that is manifestly gauge-independent and quantized.



In this short note, we present a formulation of the Kane-Mele
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
invariant
Kane and Mele 2005
that does not require any gauge fixing conditions and is quantized for any discrete approximation of the Brillouin zone (BZ) torus.
A known gauge-fixing-free method involves tracking the flow of the center positions of the hybrid Wannier states or extracting one eigenvalue from each Kramers pair among the eigenvalues of the Wilson loops
Soluyanov and Vanderbilt 2011
;
Yu et al. 2011
.
The construction below is based on the time-reversal polarization
Fu and Kane 2006
and eliminates the time-reversal gauge condition in
Fukui and Hatsugai 2007
.

We denote the Bloch momentum in the BZ torus by
{\bm{k}}=(k_{x},k_{y})\in[-\pi,\pi]\times[-\pi,\pi]
.
Consider a
2N\times 2N
Hamiltonian
H({\bm{k}})
that satisfies time-reversal symmetry (TRS).
The time-reversal operator is defined as
T=U_{T}K
, where
U_{T}
is a
2N\times 2N
unitary matrix that satisfies
(U_{T})^{\rm tr}=-U_{T}
, and
K
represents complex conjugation.
TRS for
H({\bm{k}})
is expressed as
TH({\bm{k}})T^{-1}=H(-{\bm{k}})
.
Due to TRS, the independent region of BZ is the cylinder
{\cal C}=\{(k_{x},k_{y})|k_{x}\in[-\pi,\pi],k_{y}\in[0,\pi]\}
.
We introduce a lattice approximation
|{\cal C}|
of the cylinder
{\cal C}
, with
\delta k_{x}
and
\delta k_{y}
as the lattice spacings in the
k_{x}
and
k_{y}
directions, respectively, ensuring that
|{\cal C}|
includes the high-symmetry points
{\bm{k}}=(0,0),(\pi,0),(0,\pi),(\pi,\pi)
.
Denote the energy eigenvalues of the Hamiltonian
H({\bm{k}})
at Bloch momentum
{\bm{k}}
as
E_{1}({\bm{k}})\leq\cdots\leq E_{2N}({\bm{k}})
in ascending order.
We focus on a set of
2n
isolated bands
2p+1,\dots,2p+2n
, such that for any
{\bm{k}}\in[-\pi,\pi]\times[-\pi,\pi]
, the inequality
E_{2p}({\bm{k}})+\Delta<E_{2p+1}({\bm{k}})\leq\cdots\leq E_{2p+2n}({\bm{k}})<E_{2p+2n+1}({\bm{k}})-\Delta
holds with
\Delta>0
a finite energy gap.
At the lattice points
{\bm{k}}
, compute the orthonormal set of
2n
eigenstates
{\cal U}({\bm{k}})=\big(\ket{u_{2p+1}({\bm{k}})},\dots,\ket{u_{2p+2n}({\bm{k}}}\big)
of
H({\bm{k}})
, with
H({\bm{k}})\ket{u_{j}({\bm{k}})}=E_{j}({\bm{k}})\ket{u_{j}({\bm{k}})}
for
j=2p+1,\dots,2p+2n
.
In the following, we define the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
invariant
\nu\in\{0,1\}
that is invariant under the
U(2n)
gauge transformations

\displaystyle{\cal U}({\bm{k}})\mapsto{\cal U}({\bm{k}})V({\bm{k}}),\quad V({\bm{k}})\in U(2n).

(1)

We introduce the unitary matrix

\displaystyle w({\bm{k}})={\cal U}(-{\bm{k}})U_{T}{\cal U}({\bm{k}})^{*}\in U(2n).

(2)

Note that
w({\bm{k}})^{T}=-w(-{\bm{k}})
, and thus at the high-symmetric points
\bm{\Gamma}\equiv-\bm{\Gamma}
, the Pfaffian
{\rm Pf\,}[w(\bm{\Gamma})]\in U(1)
is defined.
Furthermore, at the high-symmetry points
\bm{\Gamma}
, the gauge transformation (
1
) changes
{\rm Pf\,}[w(\bm{\Gamma})]
as
{\rm Pf\,}[w(\bm{\Gamma})]\mapsto{\rm Pf\,}[w(\bm{\Gamma})]\det[V(\bm{\Gamma})]^{*}
.

At the boundary discrete circles of the cylinder
|{\cal C}|
, namely the lattice points on the
k_{y}=0
and
k_{y}=\pi
lines, the time-reversal polarization is defined
Fu and Kane 2006
:

\displaystyle P_{T,x}(\Gamma_{y})=\frac{1}{2\pi}{\rm arg}\left[\prod_{k_{x}=0}^{\pi-\delta k_{x}}\det[{\cal U}(k_{x}+\delta k_{x},\Gamma_{y})^{\dagger}{\cal U}(k_{x},\Gamma_{y})]\frac{{\rm Pf\,}[w(0,\Gamma_{y})]}{{\rm Pf\,}[w(\pi,\Gamma_{y})]}\right]\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z},\quad\Gamma_{y}=0,\pi.

(3)

Here,
{\rm arg}(z)
is the argument of the complex number
z
.
Note that the time-reversal polarization
P_{T,x}(\Gamma_{y})
does not depend on the gauge choice of
{\cal U}({\bm{k}})
and is well-defined modulo 1.
Using
{\cal U}(-{\bm{k}})=U_{T}{\cal U}({\bm{k}})^{*}w({\bm{k}})^{\dagger}
, we find that the usual polarization

\displaystyle P_{x}(\Gamma_{y})=\frac{1}{2\pi}{\rm arg}\left[\prod_{k_{x}=-\pi}^{\pi-\delta k_{x}}\det[{\cal U}(k_{x}+\delta k_{x},\Gamma_{y})^{\dagger}{\cal U}(k_{x},\Gamma_{y})]\right]\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z},\quad\Gamma_{y}=0,\pi,

(4)

has the following relation to the time-reversal polarization
P_{T,x}(\Gamma_{y})
:

\displaystyle P_{x}(\Gamma_{y})=2P_{T,x}(\Gamma_{y}){\rm\ mod\ }1.

(5)

Define the Berry flux through the plaquette
\Box_{\bm{k}}
formed by the four lattice points
(k_{x},k_{y}),(k_{x}+\delta k_{x},k_{y}),(k_{x}+\delta k_{x},k_{y}+\delta k_{y}),(k_{x},k_{y}+\delta k_{y})
as

\displaystyle F(\Box_{\bm{k}})

\displaystyle={\rm arg}\Big[\det[{\cal U}(k_{x},k_{y})^{\dagger}{\cal U}(k_{x},k_{y}+\delta k_{y})]\det[{\cal U}(k_{x},k_{y}+\delta k_{y})^{\dagger}{\cal U}(k_{x}+\delta k_{x},k_{y}+\delta k_{y})]

\displaystyle\det[{\cal U}(k_{x}+\delta k_{x},k_{y}+\delta k_{y})^{\dagger}{\cal U}(k_{x}+\delta k_{x},k_{y})]\det[{\cal U}(k_{x}+\delta k_{x},k_{y})^{\dagger}{\cal U}(k_{x},k_{y})]\Big]\in\mathbb{R}.

(6)

The Berry flux
F(\Box_{\bm{k}})
is gauge-invariant.
Moreover, even though
F(\Box_{\bm{k}})
is originally an
\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
-valued quantity, if the plaquette
\Box_{\bm{k}}
is sufficiently small compared to the variation of the Hamiltonian
H({\bm{k}})
,
F(\Box_{\bm{k}})
is close to
0
as an
\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
value and can be regarded as a real number.
The sum of the Berry fluxes over the plaquettes
\Box_{\bm{k}}
in the cylinder
|{\cal C}|
exactly matches the difference in polarization
P(\Gamma_{y})
at the boundaries of the cylinder:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{\Box_{\bm{k}}\in|{\cal C}|}F(\Box_{\bm{k}})\equiv P_{x}(0)-P_{x}(\pi)\quad{\rm\ mod\ }1.

(7)

From (
5
), the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
invariant
\nu\in\{0,1\}
is given by

\displaystyle\nu=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{\Box_{\bm{k}}\in|{\cal C}|}F(\Box_{\bm{k}})-2P_{T,x}(0)+2P_{T,x}(\pi)\in\{0,1\}\quad{\rm\ mod\ }2

(8)

The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
invariant
\nu
does not depend on the gauge of
{\cal U}({\bm{k}})
, and any time-reversal symmetric gauge condition is not necessary.
Moreover, by construction, the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
invariant
\nu
is quantized for any discrete approximation of the BZ torus.

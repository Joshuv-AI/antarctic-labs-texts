AdS Virasoro-Shapiro from single-valued periods
Luis F. Alday, Tobias Hansen and Joao A. Silva

Abstract
We determine the full 1 / λ 1/\sqrt{\lambda} correction to the flat-space Wilson coefficients which enter the AdS Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude in 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 SYM theory at strong coupling. The assumption that the Wilson coefficients are in the ring of single-valued multiple zeta values, as expected for closed string amplitudes, is surprisingly powerful and leads to a unique solution to the dispersive sum rules relating Wilson coefficients and OPE data obtained in Alday:2022uxp . The corresponding OPE data fully agrees with and extends the results from integrability. The Wilson coefficients to order 1 / λ 1/\sqrt{\lambda} can be summed into an expression whose structure of poles and residues generalises that of the Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude in flat space.

with coefficients

\displaystyle Q^{m}_{m+2}(n^{\bm{s}}_{1},n^{\bm{s}}_{2},1)

\displaystyle=1\,,\quad

\displaystyle Q^{m}_{m+2}(n^{\bm{s}}_{1},n^{\bm{s}}_{2},0)

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{\bm{s}}_{2}(n^{\bm{s}}_{2}+1)}{2}\,,

\displaystyle Q^{m}_{m+1}(n^{\bm{s}}_{1},n^{\bm{s}}_{2},1)

\displaystyle=2\,,\quad

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{\bm{s}}_{1}+n^{\bm{s}}_{2}(8n^{\bm{s}}_{2}+6)}{8}\,,

\displaystyle Q^{m}_{m}(n^{\bm{s}}_{1},n^{\bm{s}}_{2},1)

\displaystyle=0\,,\quad

\displaystyle Q^{m}_{m}(n^{\bm{s}}_{1},n^{\bm{s}}_{2},0)

\displaystyle=2-\frac{n^{\bm{s}}_{1}(n^{\bm{s}}_{1}+4n^{\bm{s}}_{2}-3)}{8}-\frac{n^{\bm{s}}_{2}(n^{\bm{s}}_{2}-1)}{2}\,,

and

p^{m}_{m+2}=\frac{1}{2}\,,\qquad p^{m}_{m+1}=1\,,\qquad p^{m}_{m}=0\,.

1/\lambda
expansion for these poles.

We could now apply the inverse of the flat space transform (
70
)

M(s_{1},s_{2})=\text{FS}^{-1}(A(S,T))=\frac{1}{2\lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}c}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}d\beta e^{-\beta}\beta^{5}A\left(\frac{\beta s_{1}}{2\sqrt{\lambda}},\frac{\beta s_{2}}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)\,,

(91)

to obtain the summed Mellin amplitude. As discussed in
Penedones:2010ue
, the poles of
A(S,T)
will lead to exponential integrals and hence branch cuts. These originate from many poles of the non-perturbative Mellin amplitude which have vanishing separation at large
\lambda
, but become separated when applying the flat space transform.

6
Conclusions

In this paper we determined the full
1/\sqrt{\lambda}
contribution to the Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude on AdS
{}_{5}\times
S
5
,
by solving the dispersive sum rules derived in
Alday:2022uxp
,
using the crucial assumption that the Wilson coefficients are single-valued periods. The resulting correction possesses an analytic structure which naturally generalises that of the Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude in flat space.

The natural next step is to determine the next layer of Wilson coefficients
\alpha^{(2)}_{a,b}
with similar arguments. A preliminary study shows that single-valuedness is also powerful enough to determine the coefficients
\alpha^{(2)}_{a,b}
uniquely, once quantities like
\langle f_{0}(\delta,\ell)\tau_{2}(\delta,\ell)^{2}\rangle
are provided. This would require solving a mixing problem to order
1/\sqrt{\lambda}
, considering more general correlators. Single-valuedness is not powerful enough if we treat these quantities as unknown, not surprisingly.

Certain universal parts of the answer, which do not depend on corrections to the CFT data, can be studied to all orders in
1/\lambda
, see appendix
D
. It would be interesting to explore this further.

Over the last few years there has been great progress in understanding how supersymmetry, via localisation results, gives integrated constraints for the correlator under consideration
Binder:2019jwn
;
Chester:2020dja
;
Chester:2019jas
;
Chester:2020vyz
;
Dorigoni:2021bvj
;
Dorigoni:2021guq
;
Collier:2022emf
. In the present context, this will lead to two linear constraints for the Wilson coefficients at each order in
1/\lambda
. These linear constrains are written in terms of single zeta values of odd arguments, and it would be interesting to understand how this arises from our procedure.

Combining these two linear constraints and the flat space limit with our new solution for
\alpha^{(1)}_{0,1}
, we can for the first time fully determine the
D^{8}R^{4}
term at planar order, which appears at
O(1/\lambda^{\frac{7}{2}})
and depends on four Wilson coefficients

\displaystyle M(s_{1},s_{2})=\frac{8}{(s_{1}-\frac{2}{3})(s_{2}-\frac{2}{3})(s_{3}-\frac{2}{3})}+\frac{120\zeta(3)}{\lambda^{3/2}}+\frac{210\left(3\sigma_{2}+7\right)\zeta(5)}{\lambda^{5/2}}+

(92)

\displaystyle\frac{140\left(108\sigma_{3}-99\sigma_{2}-320\right)\zeta(3)^{2}}{3\lambda^{3}}+\frac{35\left(2592\sigma_{2}^{2}-77328\sigma_{3}+73638\sigma_{2}+178909\right)\zeta(7)}{16\lambda^{7/2}}+O(\lambda^{-4})\,.

Among the CFT data provided by our solution we reproduce the anomalous dimensions of short Konishi-like operators, in full agreement with the results from integrability, together with their structure constants. It would be interesting to reproduce these structure constants from integrability methods, along the lines of
Basso:2022nny
. A related direction is the interplay between integrability and the conformal bootstrap, explored first in
Cavaglia:2021bnz
;
Cavaglia:2022qpg
and in our context in
Caron-Huot:2022sdy
. Now that our analytic methods allow to explore
1/\lambda
corrections, it would be interesting to feed this into the program of
Caron-Huot:2022sdy
.

Finally, we hope that our results can fuel progress towards determining the worldsheet theory for strings on AdS
{}_{5}\times
S
5
.
Recent progress on determining the vertex operators has been made in
Fleury:2021ieo
.
It would be very interesting to see explicitly how the expression for the AdS Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude, in a
1/\lambda
expansion, arises from the worldsheet theory.

Appendix A
Crossing-symmetric dispersion relations

Based on earlier work in
Auberson:1972prg
;
Sinha:2020win
, a crossing symmetric dispersion relation for Mellin amplitudes was derived in
Gopakumar:2021dvg
. The idea is to use the variables

a=\frac{y}{x}\,,\qquad\hat{s}_{k}(z,a)=a-\frac{a(z-z_{k})^{3}}{z^{3}-1}\,,\qquad z_{k}=e^{\frac{2}{3}\pi i(k-1)}\,,\quad k=1,2,3\,,

(93)

and in this appendix we used the rescaled variables

\hat{s}_{k}=\frac{s_{k}}{2}\,,\qquad x=\frac{\sigma_{2}}{8}\,,\qquad y=-\frac{\sigma_{3}}{8}\,.

(94)

One can derive a fixed-
a
dispersion relation by deforming an integration contour in
z
.
The derivation of the dispersion relation is described in detail in
Auberson:1972prg
and in the supplementary material of
Sinha:2020win
.
One starts with the expression

M(z,a)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{z^{3}}{1-z^{3}}\oint_{z}dz^{\prime}\frac{z^{\prime 3}-1}{z^{\prime 3}(z^{\prime}-z)}M(z^{\prime},a)+\text{const.}\,,

(95)

and deforms the integration contour, picking up the poles corresponding to OPE singularities, which lie on the unit circle (at least if
a
lies in a certain range).
A constant contribution is not determined by this relation because of poles at
z=0
and
z=\infty
. The Regge limit
|s_{k}|\to\infty
is mapped to the three roots of unity
z=z_{k}
, and one assumes the following bound in the Regge limit

M(s_{1},s_{2})=o(s_{1}^{2})\ \text{for}\ |s_{1}|\to\infty\,,\quad s_{2}\ \text{fixed}\,,

(96)

which amounts to

M(z,a)=o\left(\frac{1}{(z-z_{k})^{2}}\right)\ \text{for}\ |z|\to z_{k}\,,\quad a\ \text{fixed}\,.

(97)

The factor
(z^{\prime 3}-1)/z^{\prime 3}
in (
95
) is a subtraction that ensures that these singularities do not contribute.
In this way one finds the following crossing symmetric expression for the Mellin amplitude in terms of OPE data and Mack polynomials

M(s_{1},s_{2})=\sum\limits_{\tau,\ell,m}\frac{c_{\tau,\ell}^{(m)}}{\tau_{m}}P_{\tau,\ell}(\tau_{m},\hat{s}_{2}^{\prime}(\tau_{m},a))H(\tau_{m},\hat{s}_{1},\hat{s}_{2},\hat{s}_{3})+\text{ const.}\,,

(98)

where
\tau_{m}=\tau/2+m-2/3
,

\displaystyle c^{(m)}_{\tau,\ell}={}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{3\ell-7}\tau^{6}\Gamma\left(\ell+\frac{\tau}{2}+\frac{3}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\ell+\frac{\tau}{2}+\frac{5}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(m+\frac{\tau}{2}-1\right)^{2}}{(\ell+1)\Gamma(m+1)\Gamma\left(\ell+\frac{\tau}{2}+2\right)^{2}\Gamma(\ell+m+\tau+3)}f(\tau,\ell)\,,

(99)

is related to the OPE coefficient
f(\tau,\ell)
from (
7
)
and

\displaystyle H(\tau_{m},\hat{s}_{1},\hat{s}_{2},\hat{s}_{3})

\displaystyle=\frac{\hat{s}_{1}}{\tau_{m}-\hat{s}_{1}}+\frac{\hat{s}_{2}}{\tau_{m}-\hat{s}_{2}}+\frac{\hat{s}_{3}}{\tau_{m}-\hat{s}_{3}}\,,

(100)

\displaystyle\hat{s}_{2}^{\prime}\left(\tau_{m},a\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\tau_{m}}{2}\left(1-\left(\frac{\tau_{m}+3a}{\tau_{m}-a}\right)^{1/2}\right)\,,

\displaystyle P_{\tau,\ell}(\tau_{m},\hat{s}_{3})

\displaystyle=4^{-\ell}Q^{\tau+4,d}_{\ell,m}\left(2(\hat{s}_{3}+\tfrac{2}{3})-4\right)\,,

where the Mack polynomial
Q_{\ell,m}^{\tau,d}(t)
is defined as in
Alday:2022uxp
.

A.1
Wilson coefficients

An expression for the Wilson coefficients in the expansion of the Mellin amplitude

M(s_{1},s_{2})=\sum\limits_{a,b=0}^{\infty}=\mathcal{M}_{a,b}x^{a}y^{b}\,,

(101)

in terms of OPE data
was derived in
Gopakumar:2021dvg
by expanding
(
98
) in
x
and
y

\mathcal{M}_{a-b,b}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau,\ell,m\end{subarray}}^{\infty}c_{\tau,\ell}^{(m)}\mathcal{B}_{a,b}^{(\tau,\ell,m)}\,,a\geq 1\,.

(102)

Here
\mathcal{B}_{a,b}^{(\tau,\ell,m)}
is given by

\mathcal{B}_{a,b}^{(\tau,\ell,m)}=\sum_{q=0}^{b}\mathfrak{U}^{\left(\tau_{m}\right)}_{a,b,q}(-1)^{b+q}P_{\tau,\ell;q}\left(\tau_{m},0\right)\,,

(103)

where
P_{\tau,\ell;q}\left(\tau_{m},0\right)=\partial_{\hat{s}_{2}}^{q}P_{\tau,\ell}\left(\tau_{m},\hat{s}_{2}\right)|_{\hat{s}_{2}=0}
and

\begin{split}&\mathfrak{U}^{\left(\tau_{m}\right)}_{a,b,q}=\frac{(a-q-1)!(b+2a-3q)}{q!(a-b)!(b-q)!\left(\tau_{m}\right)^{b+2a-q+1}}\,_{4}F_{3}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{q}{2}+\frac{1}{2},\frac{q}{2},q-b,q+1-\frac{2a+b}{3}\\
q+1,q+1-a,q-\frac{2a+b}{3}\end{array};4\right]\,.\end{split}

(104)

By equating
M(s_{1},s_{2})
to our
reduced Mellin amplitude (
1
) (minus the supergravity amplitude), the expansion

\mathcal{M}_{a,b}=\sum\limits_{c=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{M}_{a,b}^{(c)}}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}(3+2a+3b+c/2)}}\,,

(105)

is related to our conventions by

\alpha^{(k)}_{a,b}=\frac{(-1)^{b}\mathcal{M}_{a,b}^{(2k)}}{\Gamma(6+2a+3b)}\,.

(106)

Now we can insert the OPE data expansions (
5
) and (
7
),
expand in large
\lambda
and sum over
m
(as discussed in
Alday:2022uxp
) to obtain the expressions for
\alpha^{(0)}_{a,b}
and
\alpha^{(1)}_{a,b}
in (
10

Alday:2022uxp
.

A.2
Recursion relations

For the reduced Mellin amplitude at hand, we can derive stronger crossing-symmetric dispersion relations and indeed recursion relations for the Wilson coefficients, because the reduced Mellin amplitude satisfies the stronger Regge bound (we assume that for string theory the chaos bound is not saturated)

M(s_{1},s_{2})=o(s_{1}^{-2})\ \text{for}\ |s_{1}|\to\infty\,,\quad s_{2}\ \text{fixed}\,,

(107)

or in terms of
z
and
a

M(z,a)=o\left((z-z_{k})^{2}\right)\ \text{for}\ |z|\to z_{k}\,,\quad a\ \text{fixed}\,.

(108)

This means we do not need the subtraction used above, and can even make use of the fact that the Mellin amplitude vanishes at
z=z_{k}
.
The trick to get a nice relation is to use the combination

\frac{z^{3}}{(z^{3}-1)^{2}}=-\frac{x}{27a^{2}}\,.

(109)

We first consider the expression

M(z,a)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{(z^{3}-1)^{2}}{z^{3}}\frac{z^{3}}{1-z^{3}}\oint_{z}dz^{\prime}\frac{z^{\prime 3}}{(z^{\prime 3}-1)^{2}}\frac{z^{\prime 3}-1}{z^{\prime 3}(z^{\prime}-z)}M(z^{\prime},a)\,,

(110)

which results in the following dispersion relation, that now also fixes the constant part of the Mellin amplitude (that means (
102
) is also valid for
a=0
)

M(s_{1},s_{2})=\sum\limits_{\tau,\ell,m}\frac{c_{\tau,\ell}^{(m)}}{\tau_{m}}P_{\tau,\ell}(\tau_{m},\hat{s}_{2}^{\prime}(\tau_{m},a))\frac{\tau_{m}^{3}}{x(\tau_{m}-a)}H(\tau_{m},\hat{s}_{1},\hat{s}_{2},\hat{s}_{3})\,.

(111)

The Regge bound is even strong enough to allow another ’addition’ so we can start with

M(z,a)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{(z^{3}-1)^{4}}{z^{6}}\frac{z^{3}}{1-z^{3}}\oint_{z}dz^{\prime}\frac{z^{\prime 6}}{(z^{\prime 3}-1)^{4}}\frac{z^{\prime 3}-1}{z^{\prime 3}(z^{\prime}-z)}M(z^{\prime},a)\,,

(112)

and find the relation

M(s_{1},s_{2})=\sum\limits_{\tau,\ell,m}\frac{c_{\tau,\ell}^{(m)}}{\tau_{m}}P_{\tau,\ell}(\tau_{m},\hat{s}_{2}^{\prime}(\tau_{m},a))\left(\frac{\tau_{m}^{3}}{x(\tau_{m}-a)}\right)^{2}H(\tau_{m},\hat{s}_{1},\hat{s}_{2},\hat{s}_{3})\,.

(113)

We can use the fact that we have two dispersion relations to obtain recursion relations. To this end we can combine the expansion in
x
and
y
with the one in OPE data and write

\mathcal{M}_{a,b}=\sum\limits_{\tau,\ell,m}\mathcal{M}_{a,b}(\tau,\ell,m)\,.

(114)

Equating (
111
) and (
113
) we find

M(s_{1},s_{2})=\sum_{a,b}\sum\limits_{\tau,\ell,m}\mathcal{M}_{a,b}(\tau,\ell,m)x^{a}y^{b}=\sum_{a,b}\sum\limits_{\tau,\ell,m}\frac{\tau_{m}x-y}{\tau_{m}^{3}}\mathcal{M}_{a,b}(\tau,\ell,m)x^{a}y^{b}\,,

(115)

which implies the recursion relation

\sum\limits_{\tau,\ell,m}\mathcal{M}_{a,b}(\tau,\ell,m)=\sum\limits_{\tau,\ell,m}\frac{1}{\tau_{m}^{2}}\mathcal{M}_{a-1,b}(\tau,\ell,m)-\frac{1}{\tau_{m}^{3}}\mathcal{M}_{a,b-1}(\tau,\ell,m)\,.

(116)

The next step is to expand in
1/\lambda
and do the sums over
m
. To leading order this implies the recursion relation

\alpha^{(0)}_{a,b}(\delta)=\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\alpha^{(0)}_{a-1,b}(\delta)+\frac{1}{\delta^{3}}\alpha^{(0)}_{a,b-1}(\delta)\,,

(117)

where
\alpha^{(0)}_{a,b}(\delta)
is the summand of
\alpha^{(0)}_{a,b}

\alpha^{(0)}_{a,b}=\sum\limits_{\delta=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{(0)}_{a,b}(\delta)\,.

(118)

One checks that (
10
) is a solution of this recursion for the boundary condition

\alpha^{(0)}_{-1,b}(\delta)=0
. As the boundary data
\alpha^{(0)}_{-b,b}(\delta)
is non-trivial (
\alpha^{(0)}_{-b,b}
vanishes only after summing over
\delta
), the recursion can be seen as a neat way to encode the
a
dependence of (
10
).

Appendix B
Alternative representation for spin sums

The formulas (
82
) imply that there is an alternative representation for
T^{(2)}_{m}(\delta)
and
F^{(2)}_{m}(\delta)
. Adapting a computation from
Green:2008uj

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}T^{(2)}_{m}(\delta)\left(\frac{z}{\delta}\right)^{m}={}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\delta-1}g_{n}(\delta)\binom{z+\delta-\frac{n}{2}-1}{\delta-n-1}^{2}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\delta-1}g_{n}(\delta)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{\delta-n-1}\left(1+\frac{z+\frac{n}{2}}{k}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\delta-1}g_{n}(\delta)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{\delta-n-1}\left(1+4\sum\limits_{s\in\{1,2\}}\left(\frac{z+\frac{n}{2}}{2k}\right)^{s}\right)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\delta-1}g_{n}(\delta)\sum\limits_{d=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta-n>k_{1}>\ldots>k_{d}>0\\
s_{1},\ldots,s_{d}\in\{1,2\}\end{subarray}}\frac{2^{2d-s_{1}-\ldots-s_{d}}(z+\frac{n}{2})^{s_{1}+\ldots+s_{d}}}{k_{1}^{s_{1}}\ldots k_{d}^{s_{d}}}

(119)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\delta-1}g_{n}(\delta)\sum\limits_{d=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{d}\in\{1,2\}}2^{2d-s_{1}-\ldots-s_{d}}\left(z+\frac{n}{2}\right)^{s_{1}+\ldots+s_{d}}Z_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{d}}(\delta-n-1)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\delta-1}g_{n}(\delta)\sum\limits_{w=0}^{\infty}\left(z+\frac{n}{2}\right)^{w}\frac{F^{(0)}_{w}(\delta-n)}{(\delta-n)^{w}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\delta-1}g_{n}(\delta)\sum\limits_{w=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{w}\binom{w}{m}\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{w-m}z^{m}\frac{F^{(0)}_{w}(\delta-n)}{(\delta-n)^{w}}\,,

we can read off

T^{(2)}_{m}(\delta)=\delta^{m}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\delta-1}g_{n}(\delta)\sum\limits_{w=m}^{2(\delta-n-1)}\binom{w}{m}\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{w-m}\frac{F^{(0)}_{w}(\delta-n)}{(\delta-n)^{w}}\,,

(120)

and similar for
F^{(2)}_{m}(\delta)
. We used in the final formula that
F^{(0)}_{w}(\delta-n)
vanishes for
w>2(\delta-n-1)
.

Appendix C
More Bootstrap Constraints

We have explored two types of constraints that the bootstrap imposes on heavy operators dual to short strings. One constraint is the fact that the coefficients in the low energy expansion of the Mellin amplitude are related to dimensions and OPE coefficients of heavy operators. Schematically,

\displaystyle\xi_{a,b}=\sum_{\delta=1}^{\infty}\frac{p_{\xi,b}(a,\delta)}{\delta^{r+2a}},

(121)

where
\xi_{a,b}
is some generic coefficient in the low energy expansion of the Mellin amplitude and the function
p_{\xi,b}(a,\delta)
depends on the OPE data through a sum over spins. It is expected that
\xi_{a,b}
is a single valued period, and so this constrains the CFT data.
Another constraint that the bootstrap imposes on the heavy operators is the equation

\displaystyle\xi_{a,b}=0,~~~a=-b,~...~,-1.

(122)

In this section, we explore a third type of constraint. The essential idea is that, at strong coupling, the Mellin amplitude should be similar to a tree level string amplitude. Tree level string amplitudes obey stringent bootstrap constraints, see
Caron-Huot:2016icg
;
Sever:2017ylk
;
Figueroa:2022onw
;
Geiser:2022icl
;
Coon:1969yw
.
We take advantage of this in the following way. We write down a two sided dispersion relation, using the
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
channels. This dispersion relation stops converging for values of
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}(s_{3})
sufficiently high, i.e. when we reach the first pole in
s_{3}
. Furthermore, the two sided dispersion relation must diverge in a precise way, such that it reproduces the residue in
s_{3}
. This gives nontrivial constraints.

C.1
Flat Space

This idea is easier to express in flat space. The Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude is given by

\displaystyle A^{(0)}(S,T)=-\frac{\Gamma\left(-S\right)\Gamma\left(-T\right)\Gamma\left(S+T\right)}{\Gamma\left(1+S\right)\Gamma\left(1+T\right)\Gamma\left(1-S-T\right)}\,.

(123)

We ask the following question. Without knowing the form of the amplitude (
123
), and assuming only the spectrum of particles exchanged (mass and spin) and Regge boundedness, i.e.

\displaystyle\lim_{S\rightarrow i\infty}|A^{(0)}(S,T)|\leq\frac{1}{|S|^{2}},~~~\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}(T)<0,

(124)

what can we say about particle couplings?

We write a two sided dispersion relation

\displaystyle A^{(0)}(S,T)={}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{U^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{S}+\frac{1}{T}\right)-\sum_{\delta=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=0,2}^{2\delta-2}\frac{f_{0}(\delta,\ell)C_{\ell}^{(1)}(1+\frac{2U}{\delta})}{(1+\ell)\delta^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{S-\delta}+\frac{1}{T-\delta}\right)\,,

(125)

where
U=-S-T
. By parametrising in this manner, the particle couplings
f_{0}(\delta,\ell)
turn out to be numerically equal to the OPE coefficients that enter in (
7
). This is due to the flat space transform (
70
). Also, we simply wrote down the part of the amplitude

\frac{1}{STU}=-\frac{1}{U^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{S}+\frac{1}{T}\right)\,,

(126)

because it won’t play any role.
The dispersion relation converges for
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}(U)<1
. For
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}(U)=1
, it must diverge, in such a way as to give rise to the correct pole and residue in
U
. One very natural way this can happen is if

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{\ell=0,2}^{2\delta-2}\frac{f_{0}(\delta,\ell)C_{\ell}^{(1)}(1+\frac{2U}{\delta})}{2(1+\ell)\delta^{3}}=f_{0}(\delta=1,\ell=0)\times\kappa\times\delta^{\kappa(U-1)-1}\,,

(127)

where
\kappa
is some number that is not fixed by this reasoning. The reason for this asymptotic is that

\displaystyle f_{0}(\delta=1,\ell=0)\times\kappa\times\int_{\delta_{0}}^{\infty}d\delta\delta^{\kappa(U-1)-1}\underbrace{=}_{U\rightarrow 1}-f_{0}(\delta=1,\ell=0)\frac{1}{U-1}.

(128)

This leads to the equation

\displaystyle\boxed{\lim_{\delta\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{\ell=0,2}^{2\delta-2}\frac{f_{0}(\delta,\ell)C_{\ell}^{(1)}(1+\frac{2}{\delta})}{(1+\ell)\delta^{2}}=\kappa\,,}

(129)

where
\kappa
is some positive number, whose value is not fixed by this reasoning. When we plug the numerical values of
f_{0}(\delta,\ell)
, it turns out that (
129
) is precisely obeyed for every
\delta\in\mathbb{N}
, for
\kappa=1
.

C.2
Flat Space from AdS

In this section we show how to derive (
129
) from: Mellin amplitudes in AdS, CFT Regge boundedness and formulas (
5
) and (
7
) for the spectrum of exchanged operators. Our discussion is similar to the one in
Penedones:2010ue
(though not exactly the same). The point of going through this is to later use the same line of reasoning to derive a generalisation of (

f_{2}(\delta,\ell)

The starting point is the dispersion relation

\displaystyle M(s_{1},s_{2})=\sum_{\text{stringy}}C_{\tau,\ell}^{2}\omega_{\tau,\ell}(s_{1},s_{2}),

(130)

where

\displaystyle\omega_{\tau,\ell}(s_{1},s_{2})

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{Q}_{\ell,m}^{\tau+4,d=4}\left(s_{3}-\frac{8}{3}\right)\left(\frac{1}{s_{1}+\frac{4}{3}-\tau-2m}+\frac{1}{s_{2}+\frac{4}{3}-\tau-2m}\right),

(131)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\ell,m}^{\tau,d}(t)

\displaystyle\equiv-\frac{2^{3\ell+2\tau-2}\Gamma\left(\ell+\frac{\tau}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\ell+\frac{\tau}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(-\frac{d}{2}+\ell+\tau+1\right)Q_{\ell,m}^{\tau,d}(t)}{\pi\Gamma(m+1)\Gamma\left(\ell+\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{2}\Gamma(\ell+\tau-1)\Gamma\left(-m-\frac{\tau}{2}+4\right)^{2}\Gamma\left(-\frac{d}{2}+\ell+m+\tau+1\right)}.

Q_{\ell,m}^{\tau,d}(t)
is a Mack polynomial, see
Alday:2022uxp
for our conventions.
This dispersion relation is valid at finite
\lambda
and also when the Mellin variables are finite. We now take the limit
s_{1}\rightarrow s_{1}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}},s_{2}\rightarrow s_{2}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
. As explained in
Penedones:2010ue
, this is what controls the flat space limit of AdS. In this regime, the sums in
m
are dominated by terms of order
\tau^{2}\sim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}
. We can thus do the substitution
m=4\delta\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}x
and
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\rightarrow 4\delta\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dx
and we get

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}M(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}s_{1},\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}s_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}}M^{(0)}(s_{1},s_{2})+O\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\right),

(132)

\displaystyle M^{(0)}(s_{1},s_{2})

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{\delta=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=0,2}^{\infty}f_{0}(\delta,\ell)\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{4x}}\left(-\frac{1}{\frac{s_{1}}{4\delta}-2x}-\frac{1}{\frac{s_{2}}{4\delta}-2x}\right)C_{\ell}^{(1)}\left(\frac{s_{3}}{4\delta x}+1\right)}{4096(\ell+1)\delta^{3}x^{6}}\,.

Note that Mack polynomials turn into Gegenbauer polynomials in this limit
Costa:2012cb
.

At strong coupling, the Mellin amplitude develops cuts, as can be seen by explicitly evaluating the above integrals for specific values of the spins. This is intuitive, because the location of the poles is at
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}=\tau+2m+\frac{4}{3}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, so if
\tau\sim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}
is parametrically large, there is no way of distinguishing between consecutive poles and the sequence of poles basically condenses into a line.
Because of this, it is useful to introduce the transform

\displaystyle A^{(0)}(S,T)=2\int_{\kappa-i\infty}^{\kappa+i\infty}\frac{d\alpha}{2\pi i}\alpha^{-6}e^{\alpha}M^{(0)}\left(\frac{2S}{\alpha},\frac{2T}{\alpha}\right).

(133)

Appendix D
Leading poles

The poles of highest order in
A^{(k)}(S,T)
are universal in the sense that they do not depend on corrections to the OPE data.
For
A^{(1)}(S,T)
these are the fourth order poles which originate from the terms in (
29
) that are polynomials of degree three in
a
and
b
.

To understand the structure of these terms better, we can compute the analogous terms at higher orders in
1/\sqrt{\lambda}
by expanding (
102
).
We compute the terms that produce the leading poles for
k\leq 5

and find that they have the following form, which we expect to hold for all
k

\alpha_{a,b}^{(k)}=\sum\limits_{\delta=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{b}\frac{1}{\delta^{3+2a+3b+k}}\frac{(-1)^{k}(2a+3b)^{3k}}{6^{k}\Gamma(k+1)}c^{(0)}_{a,b,m}F_{m}^{(0)}(\delta)+\text{ subleading poles in }A^{(k)}(S,T)\,.

(147)

This can be summed over
k
to give an exponential
\text{exp}\left(-\frac{(2a+3b)^{3}}{6\delta\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)
.
We can apply the flat space transform and sum over
a
and
b
to get

\displaystyle 2\sum\limits_{k,a,b=0}^{\infty}\frac{\hat{\sigma}_{2}^{a}\hat{\sigma}_{3}^{b}\alpha_{a,b}^{(k)}}{\lambda^{\frac{k}{2}}}={}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\delta=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\delta^{3}}\text{exp}\left(-\frac{(2x\partial_{x}+3y\partial_{y})^{3}}{6\delta\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)\frac{y+2}{1-x-y}\binom{z+\delta-1}{\delta-1}^{2}

(148)

\displaystyle+\text{ subleading poles}\,.

The leading poles arise when the derivatives act on the denominator

(2x\partial_{x}+3y\partial_{y})^{3k}\frac{y+2}{1-x-y}=\frac{(3k)!S^{3k+1}}{(\delta-S)^{3k+1}}+O\left(\frac{1}{(S-\delta)^{3k}}\right)\,,\qquad\delta=1,2,\ldots\,.

(149)

This gives

\displaystyle 2\sum\limits_{k,a,b=0}^{\infty}\frac{\hat{\sigma}_{2}^{a}\hat{\sigma}_{3}^{b}\alpha_{a,b}^{(k)}}{\lambda^{\frac{k}{2}}}={}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{k}{2}}}\left(\frac{R^{(k)}_{3k+1}(T,\delta)}{(S-\delta)^{3k+1}}+O\left(\frac{1}{(S-\delta)^{3k}}\right)\right)\,,

(150)

with

R^{(k)}_{3k+1}(T,\delta)=-\frac{(3k)!\delta^{2k-2}}{6^{k}k!}\frac{\Gamma(T+\delta)^{2}}{\Gamma(\delta)^{2}\Gamma(T+1)^{2}}\,.

(151)

The leading order poles can be Borel resummed. We obtain

\sum_{k=0}-\frac{(3k)!\delta^{2k-2}}{6^{k}k!}\frac{1}{\lambda^{k/2}(S-\delta)^{3k+1}}=-\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\frac{e^{\frac{\delta^{2}t^{3}}{6\sqrt{\lambda}(S-\delta)^{3}}-t}}{\delta^{2}(S-\delta)}\,.

(152)

The integral can be written in terms of Airy and hypergeometric functions, if desired.

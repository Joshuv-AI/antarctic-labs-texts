Noncommutative Riemann hypothesis
Gonçalo Tabuada

Abstract
. In this note, making use of noncommutative l l -adic cohomology, we extend the generalized Riemann hypothesis from the realm of algebraic geometry to the broad setting of geometric noncommutative schemes in the sense of Orlov. As a first application, we prove that the generalized Riemann hypothesis is invariant under derived equivalences and homological projective duality. As a second application, we prove the noncommutative generalized Riemann hypothesis in some new cases.



1.
Introduction and statement of results

Let
k
be a global field and
\Sigma_{k}
its (infinite) set of non-archimedean places.

Let
X
be a smooth proper
k
-scheme and
0\leq w\leq 2\mathrm{dim}(X)
an integer. Following Serre’s foundational work
[
19
,
20
]
(consult also Manin
[
14
]
), consider the
L
-function
L_{w}(X;s):=\prod_{\nu\in\Sigma_{k}}L_{w,\nu}(X;s)
of weight
w
. As proved in
loc. cit.
, this infinite product converges absolutely in the half-plane
\mathrm{Re}(s)>\frac{w}{2}+1
and is non-zero in this region. Moreover, the following two conditions are expected to hold:

(\mathrm{C}1)
The
L
-function
L_{w}(X;s)
admits a (unique) meromorphic continuation to the entire complex plane.

(\mathrm{C}2)
When
\mathrm{char}(k)=0
, the only possible pole of
L_{w}(X;s)
is located at
s=\frac{w}{2}+1
with
w
even.

When
\mathrm{char}(k)=0
, the conditions
(\mathrm{C}1)\text{-}(\mathrm{C}2)
have been proved in many cases: certain
0
-dimensional schemes, certain elliptic curves, certain modular curves, certain abelian varieties, certain varieties of Fermat type, certain Shimura varieties, etc. When
\mathrm{char}(k)>0
, condition
(\mathrm{C}1)
follows from Grothendieck’s work
[
5
]
.

The following conjecture, which implicitly assumes condition
(\mathrm{C}1)
, goes back to the work
[
18
]
of Riemann.

Generalized Riemann hypothesis

\mathrm{R}_{w}(X)
:
All the zeros of the
L
-function
L_{w}(X;s)
that are contained in the critical strip
\frac{w}{2}<\mathrm{Re}(s)<\frac{w}{2}+1
lie in the vertical line
\mathrm{Re}(s)=\frac{w+1}{2}
.

The generalized Riemann hypothesis play a central role in mathematics. For example, when
\mathrm{char}(k)=0
and
X=\mathrm{Spec}(k)
, the conjecture
\mathrm{R}_{0}(X)
reduces to the classical
extended Riemann hypothesis

\mathrm{ERH}_{k}
, i.e., all the zeros of the Dedekind zeta function
\zeta_{k}(s):=\sum_{I\triangleleft{\mathcal{O}}_{k}}\frac{1}{N(I)^{s}}
that are contained in the critical strip
0<\mathrm{Re}(s)<1
lie in the vertical line
\mathrm{Re}(s)=\frac{1}{2}
; note that in the particular case where
k=\mathbb{Q}
,
\mathrm{ERH}_{k}
is the famous Riemann hypothesis. The status of the generalized Riemann conjecture depends drastically on the characteristic of
k
. On the one hand, when
\mathrm{char}(k)=0
, no cases have been proved. On the other hand, when
\mathrm{char}(k)>0
, the generalized Riemann hypothesis follows from Deligne’s work
[
3
,
4
]
.

Now, let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a geometric noncommutative
k
-scheme in the sense of Orlov; consult §
4
below. A standard example is the canonical dg enhancement
\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X)
of the derived category of perfect complexes
\mathrm{perf}(X)
of a smooth proper
k
-scheme
X
(consult Keller’s survey
[
7
, §4.6]
); consult §
3
below for further examples. In §
6
below, making use of noncommutative
l
-adic cohomology, we construct the noncommutative counterparts
L_{\mathrm{even}}({\mathcal{A}};s):=\prod_{\nu\in\Sigma_{k}}L_{\mathrm{even},\nu}({\mathcal{A}};s)
and
L_{\mathrm{odd}}({\mathcal{A}};s):=\prod_{\nu\in\Sigma_{k}}L_{\mathrm{odd},\nu}({\mathcal{A}};s)
of the classical
L
-functions. Moreover, we prove the following noncommutative counterpart of Serre’s convergence result:

Theorem 1.1
.

The infinite product
L_{\mathrm{even}}({\mathcal{A}};s)
, resp.
L_{\mathrm{odd}}({\mathcal{A}};s)
, converges absolutely in the half-plane
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
, resp.
\mathrm{Re}(s)>\frac{3}{2}
, and is non-zero in this region.

Similarly to the above condition
\mathrm{(C1)}
, it is expected that the noncommutative
L
-functions
L_{\mathrm{even}}({\mathcal{A}};s)
and
L_{\mathrm{odd}}({\mathcal{A}};s)
admit a (unique) meromorphic continuation to the entire complex plane. Under this assumption, the generalized Riemann hypothesis admits the following noncommutative counterpart:

Noncommutative generalized Riemann hypothesis

\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{even}}({\mathcal{A}})
and
\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{odd}}({\mathcal{A}})
:
All the zeros of the noncommutative
L
-function
L_{\mathrm{even}}({\mathcal{A}};s)
, resp.
L_{\mathrm{odd}}({\mathcal{A}};s)
, that are contained in the critical strip
0<\mathrm{Re}(s)<1
, resp.
\frac{1}{2}<\mathrm{Re}(s)<\frac{3}{2}
, lie in the vertical line
\mathrm{Re}(s)=\frac{1}{2}
, resp.
\mathrm{Re}(s)=1
.

The noncommutative (generalized) Riemann hypothesis was originally envisioned by Kontsevich in his seminal talks
[
8
,
9
]
. The next result relates this conjecture with the generalized Riemann hypothesis:

Theorem 1.2
.

Given a smooth proper
k
-scheme
X
, we have the following implications:

(1.3)

\displaystyle\{\mathrm{R}_{w}(X)\}_{w\,\mathrm{even}}\Rightarrow\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{even}}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X))

\displaystyle\{\mathrm{R}_{w}(X)\}_{w\,\mathrm{odd}}\Rightarrow\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{odd}}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X))\,.

When
\mathrm{char}(k)>0
, the converse implications of (
1.3
) hold. Moreover, when
\mathrm{char}(k)=0
and the
L
-functions
\{L_{w}(X;s)\}_{0\leq w\leq 2\mathrm{dim}(X)}
satisfy condition
(\mathrm{C}2)
, the converse implications of (
1.3
) also hold.

Intuitively speaking, Theorem
1.2
shows that the generalized Riemann hypothesis belongs not only to the realm of algebraic geometry but also to the broad setting of geometric noncommutative schemes.

2.
Applications to commutative geometry

Let
k
be a global field; since the generalized Riemann hypothesis holds when
\mathrm{char}(k)>0
, we restrict ourselves to the case where
\mathrm{char}(k)=0
.
In this section, making use of Theorem
1.2
, we prove that the generalized Riemann hypothesis is invariant under derived equivalences and homological projective duality.

Derived invariance

Let
X
and
Y
be two smooth proper
k
-schemes. In what follows, we assume that the associated
L
-functions
\{L_{w}(X;s)\}_{0\leq w\leq 2\mathrm{dim}(X)}
and
\{L_{w}(Y;s)\}_{0\leq w\leq 2\mathrm{dim}(Y)}
satisfy condition
\mathrm{(C2)}
.

Corollary 2.1

(Derived invariance)
.

If the derived categories of perfect complexes
\mathrm{perf}(X)
and
\mathrm{perf}(Y)
are (Fourier-Mukai) equivalent, then we have the following equivalences:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\{\mathrm{R}_{w}(X)\}_{w\,\mathrm{even}}\Leftrightarrow\{\mathrm{R}_{w}(Y)\}_{w\,\mathrm{even}}

\displaystyle\{\mathrm{R}_{w}(X)\}_{w\,\mathrm{odd}}\Leftrightarrow\{\mathrm{R}_{w}(Y)\}_{w\,\mathrm{odd}}\,.

Proof.

If the triangulated categories
\mathrm{perf}(X)
and
\mathrm{perf}(Y)
are (Fourier-Mukai) equivalent, then the dg categories
\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X)
and
\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(Y)
are Morita equivalent. Consequently, we obtain the following equivalences:

\displaystyle\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{even}}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X))\Leftrightarrow\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{even}}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(Y))

\displaystyle\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{odd}}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X))\Leftrightarrow\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{odd}}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(Y))\,.

By combining them with Theorem
1.2
, we hence obtain the above equivalences (
2.2
).
∎

In the literature there are numerous examples of smooth proper
k
-schemes
X
and
Y
for which the above Corollary
2.1
applies; consult, for example, the book
[
6
]
and the references therein.

Homological Projective Duality

Homological Projective Duality (=HPD) was introduced by Kuznetsov in
[
13
]
as a tool to study the derived categories of perfect complexes of linear sections. Let
X
be a smooth proper
k
-scheme equipped with a line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}_{X}(1)
; we write
X\to\mathbb{P}(V)
for the associated map, where
V:=H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{L}}_{X}(1))^{\vee}
. Assume that we have a Lefschetz decomposition
\mathrm{perf}(X)=\langle\mathbb{A}_{0},\mathbb{A}_{1}(1),\ldots,\mathbb{A}_{i-1}(i-1)\rangle
with respect to
{\mathcal{L}}_{X}(1)
in the sense of
[
13
, Def. 4.1]
. Following
[
13
, Def. 6.1]
, let us write
Y
for the HP-dual of
X
,
{\mathcal{L}}_{Y}(1)
for the HP-dual line bundle, and
Y\to\mathbb{P}(V^{\vee})
for the associated map. Given a generic linear subspace
L\subset V^{\vee}
, consider the smooth linear sections
X_{L}:=X\times_{\mathbb{P}(V)}\mathbb{P}(L^{\perp})
and
Y_{L}:=Y\times_{\mathbb{P}(V^{\vee})}\mathbb{P}(L)
. In what follows, we assume that the associated
L
-functions
\{L_{w}(X_{L};s)\}_{0\leq w\leq 2\mathrm{dim}(X_{L})}
and
\{L_{w}(Y_{L};s)\}_{0\leq w\leq 2\mathrm{dim}(Y_{L})}
satisfy the above condition
\mathrm{(C2)}
.

Theorem 2.3

(HPD-invariance)
.

Assume that the triangulated category
\mathbb{A}_{0}
admits a full exceptional collection. Under this assumption, the following holds:

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{ERH}_{k}\Rightarrow\big(\{\mathrm{R}_{w}(X_{L})\}_{w\,\mathrm{even}}\Leftrightarrow\{\mathrm{R}_{w}(Y_{L})\}_{w\,\mathrm{even}}\big)

\displaystyle\{\mathrm{R}_{w}(X_{L})\}_{w\,\mathrm{odd}}\Leftrightarrow\{\mathrm{R}_{w}(Y_{L})\}_{w\,\mathrm{odd}}\,.

Remark 2.5
.

The assumption of Theorem
2.3
is quite mild since it holds in all the examples in the literature.

Roughly speaking, Theorem
2.3
“cuts in half” the difficulty of proving the generalized Riemann hypothesis, i.e., if the generalized Riemann hypothesis holds for a linear section, then it also holds for the HP-dual linear section. In the literature there are numerous examples of homological projective dualities for which the above Theorem
2.3
applies (e.g., Veronese-Clifford duality, Grassmannian-Pfaffian duality, Spinor duality, Determinantal duality, etc); consult, for example, the surveys
[
12
,
24
]
and the references therein.

3.
Applications to noncommutative geometry

Let
k
be a global field. In this section, making use of Theorem
1.2
, we prove the noncommutative generalized Riemann hypothesis in some new cases.

Noncommutative gluings of schemes

Let
X
and
Y
be two smooth proper
k
-schemes and
\mathrm{B}
a perfect dg
\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X)\text{-}\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(Y)
bimodule. Following Orlov
[
17
, Def. 3.5]
, we can consider the gluing
X\odot_{\mathrm{B}}Y
of the dg categories
\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X)
and
\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(Y)
via the dg bimodule
\mathrm{B}
(Orlov used a different notation). As proved by Orlov in
[
17
, Thm. 4.11]
,
X\odot Y
is a geometric noncommutative
k
-scheme.

Theorem 3.1
.

We have the following implications:

\displaystyle\{\mathrm{R}_{w}(X)\}_{w\,\mathrm{even}}+\{\mathrm{R}_{w}(Y)\}_{w\,\mathrm{even}}\Rightarrow\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{even}}(X\odot_{\mathrm{B}}Y)

\displaystyle\{\mathrm{R}_{w}(X)\}_{w\,\mathrm{odd}}+\{\mathrm{R}_{w}(Y)\}_{w\,\mathrm{odd}}\Rightarrow\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{odd}}(X\odot_{\mathrm{B}}Y)\,.

In particular, the conjectures
\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{even}}(X\odot_{\mathrm{B}}Y)
and
\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{odd}}(X\odot_{\mathrm{B}}Y)
hold when
\mathrm{char}(k)>0
.

Calabi-Yau dg categories associated to hypersurfaces

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
be a smooth hypersurface of degree
\mathrm{deg}(X)\leq n+1
. As proved by Kuznetsov in
[
11
, Cor. 4.1]
, we have a semi-orthogonal decomposition
\mathrm{perf}(X)=\langle{\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{O}}_{X},\ldots,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(n-\mathrm{deg}(X))\rangle
. Moreover, the full dg subcategory
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathrm{dg}}
of
\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X)
, consisting of the objects of
{\mathcal{T}}
, is a Calabi-Yau dg category of fractional CY-dimension
\frac{(n+1)(\mathrm{deg}(X)-2)}{\mathrm{deg}(X)}
. Note that
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathrm{dg}}
is a geometric noncommutative
k
-scheme. Note also that
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathrm{dg}}
is
not
Morita equivalent to a dg category of the form
\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(Y)
, with
Y
a smooth proper
k
-scheme, whenever its CY-dimension is
not
an integer.

Theorem 3.2
.

We have the following implications:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\{\mathrm{R}_{w}(X)\}_{w\,\mathrm{even}}\Rightarrow\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{even}}({\mathcal{T}}_{\mathrm{dg}})

\displaystyle\{\mathrm{R}_{w}(X)\}_{w\,\mathrm{odd}}\Rightarrow\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{odd}}({\mathcal{T}}_{\mathrm{dg}})\,.

In particular, the conjectures
\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{even}}({\mathcal{T}}_{\mathrm{dg}})
and
\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{odd}}({\mathcal{T}}_{\mathrm{dg}})
hold when
\mathrm{char}(k)>0
.

Finite-dimensional algebras of finite global dimension

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional
k
-algebra of finite global dimension. Examples include path algebras of finite quivers without oriented cycles and their admissible quotients. As proved by Orlov in
[
17
, Cor. 5.4]
,
A
is a geometric noncommutative
k
-scheme.

Example 3.4

(Dynkin quivers)
.

Let
\Delta
be a Dynkin quiver of type
A_{n}
,
D_{n}
,
E_{6}
,
E_{7}
or
E_{8}
. Recall that its Coxeter number

is equal to
n+1
,
2(n-1)
,
12
,
18
or
30
. It is well-known that the quiver
k
-algebra

has fractional CY-dimension
\frac{h-2}{h}
. Consequently, in all these cases the geometric noncommutative
k
-scheme
A
is
not
Morita equivalent to a dg category of the form
\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(Y)
, with

a smooth proper
k
-scheme.

Consider the largest semi-simple quotient
A/J
of
A
, where
J
stands for the Jacobson radical. Thanks to Artin-Wedderburn’s theorem,
A/J
is Morita equivalent to the product
D_{1}\times\cdots\times D_{n}
, where
V_{1},\ldots,V_{n}
stand for the simple (right)
A/J
-modules and
D_{1}:=\mathrm{End}_{A/J}(V_{1}),\ldots,D_{n}:=\mathrm{End}_{A/J}(V_{n})
for the associated division

k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}
the centers of the division

-algebras
D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Assume that the quotient
k
-algebra
A/J
is separable (this holds, for example, when

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{R}_{0}(\mathrm{Spec}(k_{i}))\Rightarrow\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{even}}(A)
. In particular, the conjecture
\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{even}}(A)
holds when
\mathrm{char}(k)>0
.

Remark 3.6

(Artin
L
-functions)
.

Since
A/J
is separable, the finite field extension

, with
1\leq i\leq n
, is also separable. Therefore, under the classical Galois-Grothendieck correspondence, the

\mathrm{Spec}(k_{i})
corresponds to the finite set
\mathrm{Spec}(k_{i})(\overline{k})
equipped with the continuous action of the absolute Galois group

L_{0}(\mathrm{Spec}(k_{i});s)
(used in conjecture
\mathrm{R}_{0}(\mathrm{Spec}(k_{i}))

-function

associated to the
\mathbb{C}
-linear representation
\rho_{i}\colon\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)\to\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathrm{Spec}(k_{i})(\overline{k})})

Lemma 4.1

Let

k

\mathfrak{A}

{\mathcal{A}}

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X)\otimes_{k}k^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X\times_{k}k^{\prime})

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}\mapsto{\mathcal{F}}\times_{k}k^{\prime}\,.

\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}

4.2.

Recall from

(i)

It sends Morita equivalences to isomorphisms.

(ii)

Let

.

Let

Noncommutative

(5.3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{even},l}({\mathcal{A}}):=\pi_{0}(K^{\mathrm{et}}({\mathcal{A}}\otimes_{k}\overline{k})_{\widehat{l}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}[1/l]

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{odd},l}({\mathcal{A}}):=\pi_{1}(K^{\mathrm{et}}({\mathcal{A}}\otimes_{k}\overline{k})_{\widehat{l}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}[1/l]\,.

Note that, by construction, the noncommutative
l
-adic cohomology groups (
5.3
) are
\mathbb{Q}_{l}
-vector spaces. Moreover, they are equipped with a continuous action of the absolute Galois group
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
. Consequently, we obtain the following well-defined functors with values in the category of
\mathbb{Q}_{l}
-linear
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
-modules:

(5.4)

\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{even},l}(-),\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{odd},l}(-)\colon\mathrm{dgcat}(k)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}\,.

Proposition 5.5
.

The functors (
5.4
) are additive invariants.

Proof.

The
l
-adic étale
K
-theory functor (
5.1
) is an additive invariant; consult
[
22
, §2.5]
. Therefore, the proof follows from the above general Lemma
4.3
.
∎

Proposition 5.6
.

Given a smooth proper
k
-scheme
X
, we have isomorphisms of
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
-modules

\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{even},l}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X))\simeq\bigoplus_{w\,\mathrm{even}}H^{w}_{\mathrm{et}}(X\times_{k}\overline{k};\mathbb{Q}_{l}(\frac{w}{2}))\quad\,\,\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{odd},l}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X))\simeq\bigoplus_{w\,\mathrm{odd}}H^{w}_{\mathrm{et}}(X\times_{k}\overline{k};\mathbb{Q}_{l}(\frac{w-1}{2}))\,,

where
H_{\mathrm{et}}(-)
stands for étale cohomology.

Proof.

Since the
k
-scheme
X
is smooth and proper, the associated
\overline{k}
-scheme
X\times_{k}\overline{k}
is, in particular, regular and separated. These conditions imply that Thomason’s étale descent spectral sequence
[
25
, Thm. 4.1]
is well-defined and degenerates rationally; consult Soulé
[
26
, §3.3.2]
. Consequently, we obtain an isomorphism of
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
-modules between
\pi_{0}(K^{\mathrm{et}}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X\times_{k}\overline{k}))_{\widehat{l}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}[1/l]
and the direct sum
\bigoplus_{w\,\mathrm{even}}H^{w}_{\mathrm{et}}(X\times_{k}\overline{k};\mathbb{Q}_{l}(\frac{w}{2}))
and between
\pi_{1}(K^{\mathrm{et}}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X\times_{k}\overline{k}))_{\widehat{l}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}[1/l]
and the direct sum
\bigoplus_{w\,\mathrm{odd}}H^{w}_{\mathrm{et}}(X\times_{k}\overline{k};\mathbb{Q}_{l}(\frac{w-1}{2})
. The proof follows now from the Morita equivalence
\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X)\otimes_{k}\overline{k}\to\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X\times_{k}\overline{k}),{\mathcal{F}}\mapsto{\mathcal{F}}\times_{k}\overline{k}
.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

Given a geometric noncommutative
k
-scheme
{\mathcal{A}}
, the associated
\mathbb{Q}_{l}
-linear
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
-modules (
5.3
) are finite-dimensional.

Proof.

By definition, there exists a smooth proper
k
-scheme
X
and an admissible triangulated subcategory
\mathfrak{A}
of
\mathrm{perf}(X)
such that
{\mathcal{A}}
and
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathrm{dg}}
are Morita equivalent. Let us denote by
{}^{\perp}\mathfrak{A}
the left orthogonal complement of
\mathfrak{A}
in
\mathrm{perf}(X)
and by
{}^{\perp}\mathfrak{A}_{\mathrm{dg}}
the associated full dg subcategory of
\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X)
. By construction, we have a semi-orthogonal decomposition
H^{0}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X))=\langle H^{0}(\mathfrak{A}_{\mathrm{dg}}),H^{0}({}^{\perp}\mathfrak{A}_{\mathrm{dg}})\rangle
, i.e.,
\mathrm{perf}(X)=\langle\mathfrak{A},{}^{\perp}\mathfrak{A}\rangle
. Therefore, making use of Proposition
5.5
, we obtain the following computations:

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{even},l}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X))\simeq\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{even},l}({\mathcal{A}})\oplus\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{even},l}({}^{\perp}\mathfrak{A}_{\mathrm{dg}})

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{odd},l}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X))\simeq\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{odd},l}({\mathcal{A}})\oplus\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{odd},l}({}^{\perp}\mathfrak{A}_{\mathrm{dg}})\,.

The proof follows now from Proposition
5.6
and from the fact that the étale cohomology
\mathbb{Q}_{l}
-vector spaces
\{H^{w}_{\mathrm{et}}(X\times_{k}\overline{k};\mathbb{Q}_{l}(\frac{w}{2}))\}_{w\,\mathrm{even}}
and
\{H^{w}_{\mathrm{et}}(X\times_{k}\overline{k};\mathbb{Q}_{l}(\frac{w-1}{2}))\}_{w\,\mathrm{odd}}

∎

6.
Noncommutative
L
-functions

Let
k
be a global field. We start by fixing some important notations:

Notation 6.1
.

Given a non-archimedean place
\nu\in\Sigma_{k}
, let us write
k_{\nu}
for the completion of
k
at
\nu
,
{\mathcal{O}}_{\nu}
for the valuation ring of
k_{\nu}
,
\kappa_{\nu}
for the residue field of
{\mathcal{O}}_{\nu}
,
p_{\nu}
for the characteristic of
\kappa_{\nu}
,
N_{\nu}
for the cardinality of the finite field
\kappa_{\nu}
,
I_{\nu}
for the
inertia subgroup
, i.e., the kernel of the canonical surjective map
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{k_{\nu}}/k_{\nu})\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\kappa_{\nu}}/\kappa_{\nu})
, and
\pi_{\nu}\in\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\kappa_{\nu}}/\kappa_{\nu})\simeq\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
for the
geometric Frobenius
, i.e., the inverse of the arithmetic Frobenius
\lambda\mapsto\lambda^{N_{\nu}}
.

Notation 6.2
.

Let us choose a prime number
l_{\nu}\neq p_{\nu}
and a field embedding
\iota_{\nu}\colon\mathbb{Q}_{l_{\nu}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
for every
\nu\in\Sigma_{k}
.

The above Lemmas
4.1
and
5.7
enable the following definition:

Definition 6.3

(Noncommutative
L
-functions)
.

Given a geometric noncommutative
k
-scheme
{\mathcal{A}}
, its noncommutative
L
-functions are defined as follows (we are implicitly using the field embedding
\iota_{\nu}
):

L_{\mathrm{even}}({\mathcal{A}};s):=\prod_{\nu\in\Sigma_{k}}L_{\mathrm{even},\nu}({\mathcal{A}};s)\quad\quad L_{\mathrm{even},\nu}({\mathcal{A}};s):=\frac{1}{\mathrm{det}\big(\mathrm{id}-N_{\nu}^{-s}(\pi_{\nu}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{l_{\nu}}}\mathbb{C})\,|\,\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{even},l_{\nu}}({\mathcal{A}}\otimes_{k}k_{\nu})^{I_{\nu}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{l_{\nu}}}\mathbb{C}\big)}

L_{\mathrm{odd}}({\mathcal{A}};s):=\prod_{\nu\in\Sigma_{k}}L_{\mathrm{odd},\nu}({\mathcal{A}};s)\quad\quad L_{\mathrm{odd},\nu}({\mathcal{A}};s):=\frac{1}{\mathrm{det}\big(\mathrm{id}-N_{\nu}^{-s}(\pi_{\nu}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{l_{\nu}}}\mathbb{C})\,|\,\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{odd},l_{\nu}}({\mathcal{A}}\otimes_{k}k_{\nu})^{I_{\nu}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{l_{\nu}}}\mathbb{C}\big)}\,.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
be geometric noncommutative
k
-schemes inducing a semi-orthogonal decomposition
H^{0}({\mathcal{A}})=\langle H^{0}({\mathcal{B}}),H^{0}({\mathcal{C}})\rangle
. Under these notations, we have the following equalities:

(6.5)

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{even}}({\mathcal{A}};s)=L_{\mathrm{even}}({\mathcal{B}};s)\cdot L_{\mathrm{even}}({\mathcal{C}};s)

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{odd}}({\mathcal{A}};s)=L_{\mathrm{odd}}({\mathcal{B}};s)\cdot L_{\mathrm{odd}}({\mathcal{C}};s)\,.

Proof.

Let
\nu\in\Sigma_{k}
be a non-archimedean place. Thanks to Proposition
5.5
and Lemma
4.3
, we have an isomorphism of
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{k_{\nu}}/k_{\nu})
-modules between
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{even},l_{\nu}}({\mathcal{A}}\otimes_{k}k_{\nu})
and
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{even},l_{\nu}}({\mathcal{B}}\otimes_{k}k_{\nu})\oplus\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{even},l_{\nu}}({\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{k}k_{\nu})
. This implies that
L_{\mathrm{even},\nu}({\mathcal{A}};s)=L_{\mathrm{even},\nu}({\mathcal{B}};s)\cdot L_{\mathrm{even},\nu}({\mathcal{C}};s)
. Consequently, the left-hand side of (
6.5
) follows from Definition
6.3
. The proof of the odd case is similar: simply replace the word “even” by the word “odd”.
∎

Proposition 6.6
.

Given a smooth proper
k
-scheme
X
, we have the following equalities:

(6.7)

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{even}}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X);s)=\prod_{w\,\mathrm{even}}L_{w}(X;s+\frac{w}{2})

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{odd}}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X);s)=\prod_{w\,\mathrm{odd}}L_{w}(X;s+\frac{w-1}{2})\,.

Roughly speaking, Proposition
6.6
shows that the noncommutative even/odd
L
-function of
\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X)
may be understood as the “weight normalization” of the product of the
L
-functions of
X
of even/odd weight.

Proof.

Recall first that the
L
-function of
X
of weight
w
is defined as follows (consult Notations
6.1
-
6.2
):

L_{w}(X;s):=\prod_{\nu\in\Sigma_{k}}L_{w,\nu}(X;s)\quad\quad L_{w,\nu}(X;s):=\frac{1}{\mathrm{det}(\id-N_{\nu}^{-s}(\pi_{\nu}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{l_{\nu}}}\mathbb{C})\,|\,H^{w}_{\mathrm{et}}((X\times_{k}k_{\nu})\times_{k_{\nu}}\overline{k_{\nu}})^{I_{\nu}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{l_{\nu}}}\mathbb{C})}\,.

Note that we have the following isomorphisms of
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\kappa_{\nu}}/\kappa_{\nu})
-modules

(6.8)

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{even},l_{\nu}}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X)\otimes_{k}k_{\nu})^{I_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{even},l_{\nu}}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X\times_{k}k_{\nu}))^{I_{\nu}}

(6.9)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\big(\bigoplus_{w\,\mathrm{even}}H^{w}_{\mathrm{et}}((X\times_{k}k_{\nu})\times_{k_{\nu}}\overline{k_{\nu}};\mathbb{Q}_{l_{\nu}}(\frac{w}{2}))\big)^{I_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{w\,\mathrm{even}}H^{w}_{\mathrm{et}}((X\times_{k}k_{\nu})\times_{k_{\nu}}\overline{k_{\nu}};\mathbb{Q}_{l_{\nu}}(\frac{w}{2}))^{I_{\nu}}

(6.10)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{w\,\mathrm{even}}\big(H^{w}_{\mathrm{et}}((X\times_{k}k_{\nu})\times_{k_{\nu}}\overline{k_{\nu}};\mathbb{Q}_{l_{\nu}})^{I_{\nu}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{l_{\nu}}}\mathbb{Q}_{l_{\nu}}(\frac{w}{2})\big)\,,

where (
6.8
) follows from the Morita equivalence
\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X)\otimes_{k}k_{\nu}\to\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X\times_{k}k_{\nu}),{\mathcal{F}}\mapsto{\mathcal{F}}\times_{k}k_{\nu}
, (
6.9
) from Proposition
5.6
, and (
6.10
) from the fact that the
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{k_{\nu}}/k_{\nu})
-module
\mathbb{Q}_{l_{\nu}}(\frac{w}{2})
is unramified (i.e.,
I_{\nu}
acts trivially). Moreover, since the action of the geometric Frobenius on
\mathbb{Q}_{l_{\nu}}(\frac{w}{2})
is given by multiplication by
N_{\nu}^{-\frac{w}{2}}
, the
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\kappa_{\nu}}/\kappa_{\nu})
-module (
6.10
) may be identified with
\bigoplus_{w\,\mathrm{even}}H^{w}_{\mathrm{et}}((X\times_{k}k_{\nu})\times_{k_{\nu}}\overline{k_{\nu}};\mathbb{Q}_{l_{\nu}})^{I_{\nu}}
where the geometric Frobenius acts diagonally as
\bigoplus_{w\,\mathrm{even}}\pi_{\nu}\cdot N_{\nu}^{-\frac{w}{2}}
. This implies the following equalities:

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{even},\nu}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X);s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{w\,\mathrm{even}}\frac{1}{\mathrm{det}(\id-N_{\nu}^{-(s+\frac{w}{2})}(\pi_{\nu}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{l_{\nu}}}\mathbb{C})\,|\,H^{w}_{\mathrm{et}}((X\times_{k}k_{\nu})\times_{k_{\nu}}\overline{k_{\nu}};\mathbb{Q}_{l_{\nu}})^{I_{\nu}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{l_{\nu}}}\mathbb{C})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{w\,\mathrm{even}}L_{w,\nu}(X;s+\frac{w}{2})\,.

Consequently, the left-hand side of (
6.7
) follows now from the following equalities:

L_{\mathrm{even}}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X);s):=\prod_{\nu\in\Sigma_{k}}L_{\mathrm{even},\nu}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X);s)=\prod_{\nu\in\Sigma_{k}}\prod_{w\,\mathrm{even}}L_{w,\nu}(X;s+\frac{w}{2})=\prod_{w\,\mathrm{even}}L_{w}(X;s+\frac{w}{2})\,.

The proof of the odd case is similar: simply replace the word “even” by the word “odd” and
\frac{w}{2}
by
\frac{w-1}{2}
.
∎

Notation 7.6

Let

Lemma 7.7

Let

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{even},l_{\nu}}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X)\otimes_{k}k_{\nu})^{I_{\nu}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{l_{\nu}}}\mathbb{C}

The following general result, whose proof is a simple linear algebra exercise, is well-known:

Lemma 7.12
.

Given an endomorphism

of a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-linear vector space, we have the equality of formal power series
\mathrm{log}(\frac{1}{\mathrm{det}(\id-tf|V)})=\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathrm{trace}(f^{\circ n})\frac{t^{n}}{n}

\mathrm{log}(t):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}(t-1)^{n}

Given a non-archimeadean place

\displaystyle\phi_{(+,\nu)}(t):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\#_{(+,\nu,n)}\frac{t^{n}}{n}

\displaystyle\phi_{(-,\nu)}(t):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\#_{(-,\nu,n)}\frac{t^{n}}{n}

Definition 7.13

Consider the following multiplicative Dirichlet series

\displaystyle\varphi_{-}(s):=\sum_{I}\frac{b_{(-,I)}}{N(I)^{s}}\,,

The following general result, concerning the absolute convergence of Dirichlet series, is well-known:

Lemma 7.14

We have the following equivalences

(7.15)

K_{0}\mathrm{rep}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
, and the composition law is induced by the (derived) tensor product of dg bimodules.

By assumption, we have a full exceptional collection
\mathbb{A}_{0}=\langle{\mathcal{E}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{E}}_{n}\rangle
. As explained in
[
21
, §2.4.2]
, this implies that
U(\mathbb{A}_{0,\mathrm{dg}})\simeq U(k)^{\oplus n}
in the additive category
\mathrm{Hmo}_{0}(k)
. Moreover, since
\mathfrak{a}_{r}
is an admissible triangulated subcategory of
\mathbb{A}_{0}
,
U(\mathfrak{a}_{r,\mathrm{dg}})
becomes a direct summand of
U(\mathbb{A}_{0,\mathrm{dg}})
. Thanks to the computation

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hmo}_{0}(k)}(U(\mathbb{A}_{0}),U(\mathbb{A}_{0}))

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hmo}_{0}(k)}(U(k)^{\oplus n},U(k)^{\oplus n})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hmo}_{0}(k)}(U(k),U(k))^{\oplus(n\times n)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{0}\mathrm{rep}(k,k)^{\oplus(n\times n)}\simeq K_{0}{\mathcal{D}}_{c}(k)^{\oplus(n\times n)}\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus(n\times n)}\,,

we observe that the endomorphism ring of
U(\mathbb{A}_{0,\mathrm{dg}})
is isomorphic to the ring of
(n\times n)
-matrices with
\mathbb{Z}
-coefficients. This implies that
U(\mathfrak{a}_{r,\mathrm{dg}})
is then necessarily isomorphic to
U(k)^{\oplus n_{r}}
for a certain integer
n_{r}\leq n
. Moreover, this integer
n_{r}
is equal to the rank of the (free) Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\mathfrak{a}_{r})
because

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hmo}_{0}(k)}(U(k),U(\mathfrak{a}_{r,\mathrm{dg}})):=K_{0}\mathrm{rep}(k,\mathfrak{a}_{r,\mathrm{dg}})\simeq K_{0}(\mathfrak{a}_{r})

\displaystyle\mathrm{and}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hmo}_{0}(k)}(U(k),U(k)^{\oplus n_{r}})\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus n_{r}}\,.

Now, recall from Proposition
5.5
and Lemma
4.3
that, given any non-archimedean place
\nu\in\Sigma_{k}
, the associated functors
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{even},l_{\nu}}(-\otimes_{k}k_{\nu})
and
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{odd},l_{\nu}}(-\otimes_{k}k_{\nu})
are additive invariants. Consequently, since these additive invariants factor through the additive category
\mathrm{Hmo}_{0}(k)
(consult the factorization (
4.2
)), we obtain an isomorphism of
\mathbb{Q}_{l_{\nu}}
-linear
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{k_{\nu}}/k_{\nu})
-modules between
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{even},l_{\nu}}(\mathfrak{a}_{r,\mathrm{dg}}\otimes_{k}k_{\nu})
and the direct sum
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{even},l_{\nu}}(k\otimes_{k}k_{\nu})^{\oplus n_{r}}
and between
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{odd},l_{\nu}}(\mathfrak{a}_{r,\mathrm{dg}}\otimes_{k}k_{\nu})
and the direct sum
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{odd},l_{\nu}}(k\otimes_{k}k_{\nu})^{\oplus n_{r}}
. Therefore, making use of the canonical Morita equivalence
k\to\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathrm{Spec}(k))
, of Proposition
5.6
, and of Definition
6.3
, we obtain the above computations (
9.9
).

Proof of Theorem

By construction of the noncommutative gluing

Proof of Theorem

Thanks to the decomposition
\mathrm{perf}(X)=\langle{\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{O}}_{X},\ldots,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(n-\mathrm{deg}(X))\rangle
, an iterated application of Proposition
6.4
yields the following equalities:

(10.1)

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{even}}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X);s)=L_{\mathrm{even}}({\mathcal{T}}_{\mathrm{dg}};s)\cdot\underbrace{\zeta_{k}(s)\cdots\zeta_{k}(s)}_{n-\mathrm{deg}(X)+1}

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{odd}}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X);s)=L_{\mathrm{odd}}({\mathcal{T}}_{\mathrm{dg}};s)\,.

On the one hand, since the Dedekind zeta function
\zeta_{k}(s)
satisfies conditions
\mathrm{(C1)}\text{-}\mathrm{(C2)}
, the left-hand side of (

) implies that
\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{even}}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X))\Rightarrow\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{even}}({\mathcal{T}}_{\mathrm{dg}})

) implies that
\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{odd}}(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X))\Leftrightarrow\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{odd}}({\mathcal{T}}_{\mathrm{dg}})

, given any non-archimedean place

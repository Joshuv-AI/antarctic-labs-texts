Well-posedness and large deviations of fractional McKean-Vlasov stochastic reaction-diffusion equations on unbounded domains
Zhang Chen ∗, Bixiang Wang †

Abstract




Abstract.
This paper is mainly concerned with the large
deviation principle of the fractional McKean-Vlasov stochastic
reaction-diffusion equation defined on
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
polynomial drift of any degree. We first prove the well-posedness of
the underlying equation under a dissipative condition, and then show
the strong convergence of solutions of the corresponding controlled
equation with respect to the weak topology of controls, by employing
the idea of uniform tail-ends estimates of solutions in order to
circumvent the non-compactness of Sobolev embeddings on unbounded
domains. We finally establish the large deviation principle of the
fractional McKean-Vlasov equation by the weak convergence method
without assuming the time Hölder continuity of the
non-autonomous diffusion coefficients.

Keywords.

Large deviation principle, McKean-Vlasov equation,
fractional Laplacian, unbounded domain, compactness,
tail-ends estimates.

60F10, 60H15, 37L55, 35R60.

Introduction

This paper
deals with
the well-posedness and
the large deviation
principle (LDP) of
the fractional McKean-Vlasov stochastic reaction-diffusion equation
driven by nonlinear noise defined on
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\displaystyle\displaystyle du^{\varepsilon}(t)+(-\triangle)^{\alpha}u^{\varepsilon}(t)dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle+f(t,x,u^{\varepsilon}(t),\mathcal{L}_{u^{\varepsilon}(t)})dt=g(t,x,u^{\varepsilon}(t),\mathcal{L}_{u^{\varepsilon}(t)})dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\sigma_{1,k}(t,x)+\kappa(x)\sigma_{2,k}(t,u^{\varepsilon}(t),\mathcal{L}_{u^{\varepsilon}(t)})\right)dW_{k}(t),

(1.1)

with initial data

\displaystyle\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}(0)=u_{0},

(1.2)

where

\displaystyle(-\triangle)^{\alpha}
with
\displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1)
is the fractional Laplace operator,

\displaystyle\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{u^{\varepsilon}(t)}

is the distribution law of
\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}(t)
,

\displaystyle f
is a nonlinear function with arbitrary growth rate,

\displaystyle g
is a Lipschitz function,

\displaystyle\sigma_{1,k}:\mathbb{R}\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

is given,

\displaystyle\kappa\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\bigcap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
and

\displaystyle\sigma_{2,k}
is a nonlinear diffusion term,
and

\displaystyle\{W_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of independent
standard real-valued Wiener processes
on a complete filtered probability space
\displaystyle\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0},\mathbb{P}\right)
.

In this paper, we investigate the

The LDP is concerned with the exponential rate of convergence of solutions of stochastic equations with small noise,
which has applications in many fields such as statistics, information theory and statistical physics

.
The classical method for proving the LDP is based on the time discretization and exponential tightness,
see, e.g.,

To avoid the complicated time discretization,

,

based on variational representations of positive functionals of Brownian motion,
which is effective for proving the LDP of a large class of stochastic equations,
see, e.g.

[

,

,

,

,

,

,

[

for equations driven by jump processes.
In particular, in the case of unbounded domains,
the LDP of the fractional reaction-diffusion equation
and the tamed 3D Navier-Stokes equations
was examined in
[
59
]
and
[
60
]
, respectively.

Currently, there are very few results on the LDP of the McKean-Vlasov stochastic equations,
see
[
58
,
61
,
62
,
63
,
64
]
.
It is worth mentioning that in
[

the authors established the LDP for a class of McKean-Vlasov stochastic equations
with locally monotone nonlinearity by the weak convergence method.
The results of both papers apply to many stochastic equations defined on bounded as well as unbounded domains,
including the reaction-diffusion equation and the Navier-Stokes equations.

However, the arguments of
[
63
,
64
]

have restrictions on the growth order of the nonlinear terms
and require the non-autonomous diffusion terms to be regular in time.
Specifically, for the reaction-diffusion equation (
1.1
),
the assumptions in
[
63
,
64
]

impose an upper bound on the growth order of
\displaystyle f
in its third argument and require
\displaystyle\sigma_{1,k}

and
\displaystyle\sigma_{2,k}
to be Hölder continuous in time.
Consequently, the results of
[
63
,
64
]

do not apply to (
1.1
)
when
\displaystyle f
has a polynomial growth of
arbitrary order in its third argument
or when
\displaystyle\sigma_{1,k}

or
\displaystyle\sigma_{2,k}
is not Hölder continuous in time.
In the present paper,
we deal with this case and prove the LDP of (
1.1
)
when
\displaystyle f
has an arbitrary growth order,

\displaystyle\sigma_{1,k}
and
\displaystyle\sigma_{2,k}
are continuous in time,
but not Hölder continuous.

To achieve our goal,
we need some kind of compactness related to the tightness of probability distributions of a family of solutions.
In the case of bounded domains,
such compactness can be obtained from
the regularity of solutions and the compactness of Sobolev embeddings.
However,
the McKean-Vlasov stochastic equation (
1.1
)
is defined on the unbounded domain
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}

and the Sobolev embeddings on
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
are no longer compact,
which causes an essential difficulty for proving the LDP of (
1.1
) with a polynomial drift
\displaystyle f
of arbitrary degree.
To solve the problem,
we will employ the idea of uniform tail-ends estimates of solutions to establish the strong convergence of a family of solutions
even though
\displaystyle f
has an arbitrary growth order and the diffusion terms are not Hölder continuous in time.
This approach was used in
[
59
]
for the standard fractional reaction-diffusion equation on
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}

where the drift and diffusion terms do not depend
on the probability distributions of solutions.

More precisely,
to prove the LDP of (
1.1
) with polynomial drift of any degree,
we first establish the existence and uniqueness of adapted solutions to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
)
by the decoupled method
(i.e., the method of freezing distributions), see Theorem
2.1
.
We then consider the controlled equation associated with (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) for every control
\displaystyle v\in L^{2}(0,T;l^{2})
,
and prove the well-posedness by the approximation arguments as in
[
63
,
64
]

combined with more complicated analysis, see Theorem
3.2
.
To overcome the non-compactness of Sobolev embeddings on
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
remove the assumptions of time Hölder continuity on diffusion coefficients in
[
63
,
64
]
,
we derive the uniform tail-ends estimates of solutions in Lemma
3.1

and further prove the LDP of solutions in the Polish space

\displaystyle C([0,T],L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))\bigcap L^{2}(0,T;H^{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))

by the weak convergence method, see Theorem
4.1
.
Under a stronger dissipativeness condition on the drift term
\displaystyle f
,
we finally establish the LDP of solutions to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) in the space

\displaystyle C([0,T],L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))\cap L^{p}(0,T;L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))

in Theorem
4.4
.

We first prove the well-posedness of (

)-(
1.2
)

We then establish the LDP of (

\displaystyle\displaystyle+2\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq r\leq t}\left|\int_{0}^{r}\left(u^{\varepsilon}(s),\ \sigma(s,u^{\varepsilon}(s),\mathcal{L}_{u^{\varepsilon}(s)})dW(s)\right)\right|\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t}\|u^{\varepsilon}(s)\|^{2}\right]+(1+2c^{2})\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\|\sigma(s,u^{\varepsilon}(s),\mathcal{L}_{u^{\varepsilon}(s)})\|_{L_{2}(l^{2},H)}^{2}ds\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t}\|u^{\varepsilon}(s)\|^{2}\right]+(1+2c^{2})M_{\sigma,T}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\left(1+\|u^{\varepsilon}(s)\|^{2}+\mathcal{L}_{u^{\varepsilon}(s)}(\|\cdot\|^{2})\right)ds\right],

where

is a positive constant from the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality.

Then by (

)-(

for all

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leqslant r\leqslant t}\left(\|u^{\varepsilon}(r)\|^{2}+2\int_{0}^{r}\|(-\triangle)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u^{\varepsilon}(s)\|^{2}ds+2\lambda_{1}\int_{0}^{r}\|u^{\varepsilon}(s)\|^{p}_{L^{p}}ds\right)\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\left(\|u_{0}\|^{2}\right)+2\int_{0}^{t}(2\|\psi_{1}(s)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\psi_{g}(s)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})\left(1+\mathcal{L}_{u^{\varepsilon}(s)}(\|\cdot\|^{2})\right)ds

\displaystyle\displaystyle+4\left(\|\psi_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}+2\|\psi_{g}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\right)\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left(\|u^{\varepsilon}(s)\|^{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\displaystyle+2(1+2c^{2})M_{\sigma,T}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\left(1+\|u^{\varepsilon}(s)\|^{2}+\mathcal{L}_{u^{\varepsilon}(s)}(\|\cdot\|^{2})\right)ds\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\left(\|u_{0}\|^{2}\right)+2\int_{0}^{t}(2\|\psi_{1}(s)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\psi_{g}(s)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})\left(1+\mathbb{E}\left(\|u^{\varepsilon}(s)\|^{2}\right)\right)ds

\displaystyle\displaystyle+4\left(\|\psi_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}+2\|\psi_{g}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\right)\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left(\|u^{\varepsilon}(s)\|^{2}\right)ds

From (

for all
\displaystyle t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leqslant r\leqslant t}\left(\|u^{\varepsilon}(r)\|^{2}+2\int_{0}^{r}\|(-\triangle)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u^{\varepsilon}(s)\|^{2}ds+2\lambda_{1}\int_{0}^{r}\|u^{\varepsilon}(s)\|^{p}_{L^{p}}ds\right)\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant\left(2\mathbb{E}\left[\|u_{0}\|^{2}\right]+c_{1}\right)e^{\int_{0}^{t}\left(c_{2}+4\|\psi_{1}(s)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+2\|\psi_{g}(s)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right)ds},

\displaystyle\displaystyle dy_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}(t)+(-\triangle)^{\alpha}y_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}(t)dt+f(t,\cdot,y_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}(t),\mu(t))dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle g(t,\cdot,y_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}(t),\mu(t))dt+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\sigma(t,y_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}(t),\mu(t))dW(t),

(4.3)

with initial data

\displaystyle\displaystyle y_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}(0)=u_{0}.

(4.4)

Since

\displaystyle\mu\in C([0,T],\mathcal{P}_{2}(H))

is fixed, problem
(
4.3
)-(
4.4
)
is similar to the classical
stochastic equation independent of
distributions as discussed in

[
11
]
, which means that
under conditions

\displaystyle({\bf\Sigma 1})
-
\displaystyle({\bf\Sigma 3})
,
for every

\displaystyle\varepsilon\in(0,1)

and
\displaystyle u_{0}\in H
, problem
problem
(
4.3
)-(
4.4
)
has a unique solution

y_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\Omega,C([0,T],H))\bigcap L^{2}(\Omega,L^{2}(0,T;V))\bigcap L^{p}(\Omega,L^{p}(0,T;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))).

Consequently,
there exists a Borel-measurable map
(see, e.g.,

[
58
, Theorem 3.6]
):

\mathcal{G}_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}:\ C([0,T],U)\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega,C([0,T],H))\bigcap L^{2}(\Omega,L^{2}(0,T;V))\bigcap L^{p}(\Omega,L^{p}(0,T;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})))

such that

\displaystyle y^{\varepsilon}_{\mu}=\mathcal{G}_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}(W)
.

Let

\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}

be the solution of
(
2.13
)-(
2.14
)
and
\displaystyle\mu^{\varepsilon}(t)=\mathcal{L}_{u^{\varepsilon}(t)}

for
\displaystyle t\in[0,T]
.
Then by Theorem
2.1
,
we find that

\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}=y^{\varepsilon}_{\mu^{\varepsilon}}

and hence

\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{G}^{\varepsilon}_{\mu^{\varepsilon}}(W)
.
Denote by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{G}^{\varepsilon}_{\mu^{\varepsilon}}
.
Then

\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{G}^{\varepsilon}(W)
, and for every control
\displaystyle v\in L^{2}(0,T;l^{2})
,

\displaystyle u_{v}^{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{G}^{\varepsilon}\left(W+\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(t)dt\right)

is the unique solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} &du_{v}^{\varepsilon}(t)+(-\triangle)^{\alpha}u_{v}^{\varepsilon}(t)dt+f(t,\cdot,u_{v}^{\varepsilon}(t),\mathcal{L}_{u^{\varepsilon}(t)})dt\\
&=g(t,\cdot,u_{v}^{\varepsilon}(t),\mathcal{L}_{u^{\varepsilon}(t)})dt+\sigma(t,u_{v}^{\varepsilon}(t),\mathcal{L}_{u^{\varepsilon}(t)})v(t)dt+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\sigma(t,u_{v}^{\varepsilon}(t),\mathcal{L}_{u^{\varepsilon}(t)})dW(t),\\
&u_{v}^{\varepsilon}(0)=u_{0}\in H,\end{aligned}\right.

which concludes the
proof.
∎

Now, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{0}:C([0,T],U)\rightarrow C([0,T],H)\bigcap L^{2}(0,T;V)\bigcap L^{p}(\Omega,L^{p}(0,T;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})))

by, for every
\displaystyle\zeta\in C([0,T],U)
,

\mathcal{G}^{0}(\zeta)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&u_{v},\ \ \ \text{if}\ \zeta=\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(t)dt\ \text{for\ some}\ v\in L^{2}(0,T;l^{2}),\\
&0,\ \ \ \text{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

(4.5)

where
\displaystyle u_{v}
is the solution to (
3.3
)-(
3.4
).

In the next subsection, we will
show
\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{0}

satisfies
\displaystyle({\bf H1})
in Proposition

4.1
.

4.3
Convergence of solutions of controlled equations

We now prove that
\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{0}

satisfies
the condition
\displaystyle({\bf H1})
; more precisely, we will show the
convergence of solutions
of the controlled equation
(
3.3
)
in the space

\displaystyle C([0,T],H)\bigcap L^{2}(0,T;V)

with respect to controls in the weak
topology of
\displaystyle L^{2}(0,T;l^{2})
.
The argument is similar to
the case of stochastic
partial differential equations
which are independent of distributions.
For the reader’s convenience, we sketch the proof with
necessary
modifications.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose that
\displaystyle({\bf\Sigma 1})
-
\displaystyle({\bf\Sigma 3})
hold,

\displaystyle u_{0}\in H
, and

\displaystyle v,v_{i}\in L^{2}(0,T;l^{2})
for
\displaystyle i\in\mathbb{N}
.
If
\displaystyle v_{i}\rightarrow v
weakly in
\displaystyle L^{2}(0,T;l^{2})
,
then

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{0}\big(\int_{0}^{\cdot}v_{i}(s)ds\big)

converges to

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{0}\big(\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(s)ds\big)

strongly
in
\displaystyle C([0,T],H)\bigcap L^{2}(0,T;V)
.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle u_{v}
and
\displaystyle u_{v_{i}}

be the solutions of (
3.3
)-(
3.4
) corresponding to
controls
\displaystyle v
and
\displaystyle v_{i}
, respectively.
By (
4.5
), we have

\displaystyle u_{v}=\mathcal{G}^{0}\big(\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(s)ds\big)

and

\displaystyle u_{v_{i}}=\mathcal{G}^{0}\big(\int_{0}^{\cdot}v_{i}(s)ds\big)
.
Therefore, we only need to prove
\displaystyle u_{v_{i}}
converges to
\displaystyle u_{v}
strongly in
\displaystyle C([0,T],H)\bigcap L^{2}(0,T;V)
.

Step 1:
equicontinuity of
\displaystyle\{u_{v_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}

in
\displaystyle\left(V\bigcap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)^{\ast}
.
Since
\displaystyle v_{i}\rightarrow v
weakly in
\displaystyle L^{2}(0,T;l^{2})
,
we know that
\displaystyle\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is bounded in
\displaystyle L^{2}(0,T;l^{2})
.
By Theorem
3.2
, there exists
\displaystyle C_{1}=C_{1}(T)>0
such
that for all
\displaystyle i\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|u_{v_{i}}\|_{C([0,T],H)}+\|u_{v_{i}}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;V)}+\|u_{v_{i}}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}+\|v_{i}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;l^{2})}\leqslant C_{1}.

(4.6)

By (
2.4
),
(
4.6
) and Theorem
3.1
,
one
find that there exists
\displaystyle C_{2}=C_{2}(T)>0
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|f(\cdot,\cdot,u_{v_{i}},\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(\cdot)})\|_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}^{q}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|f(t,x,u_{v_{i}}(t),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(t)})\right|^{q}dxdt

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[\lambda_{3}|u_{v_{i}}(t)|^{p-1}+\psi_{3}(t,x)\left(1+\|u^{0}(t)\|\right)\right]^{q}dxdt

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{\lambda_{3}|u_{v_{i}}(t)|^{p-1}+\psi_{3}(t,x)\left[1+M_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\|u_{0}\|)\right]\right\}^{q}dxdt

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2^{q-1}\lambda_{3}^{q}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{v_{i}}(t)|^{q(p-1)}dxdt+2^{q-1}\left[1+M_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\|u_{0}\|)\right]^{q}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\psi_{3}(t,x)|^{q}dxdt

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2^{q-1}\lambda_{3}^{q}\|u_{v_{i}}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}^{p}+2^{q-1}\left[1+M_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\|u_{0}\|)\right]^{q}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\psi_{3}(t,x)|^{q}dxdt\leq C_{2},

(4.7)

where
\displaystyle C_{2}=C_{2}(T)>0
.
Similar to (
2.15
),
by (
3.3
)-(
3.4
)
we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle u_{v_{i}}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}(-\triangle)^{\alpha}u_{v_{i}}(s)ds+\int_{0}^{t}f(s,\cdot,u_{v_{i}}(s),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(s)})ds

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}g(s,\cdot,u_{v_{i}}(s),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(s)})ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,u_{v_{i}}(s),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(s)})v_{i}(s)ds

(4.8)

in
\displaystyle\left(V\bigcap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)^{\ast}
.
Then for all
\displaystyle 0\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|u_{v_{i}}(t)-u_{v_{i}}(s)\|_{\left(V\bigcap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\|(-\triangle)^{\alpha}u_{\theta_{i}}(r)\|_{V^{\ast}}dr+\int_{s}^{t}\|f(r,\cdot,u_{v_{i}}(r),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(r)})\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}dr

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{t}\|g(r,\cdot,u_{v_{i}}(r),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(r)})\|dr+\int_{s}^{t}\|\sigma(r,u_{v_{i}}(r),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(r)})v_{i}(r)\|dr.

(4.9)

For the first term on the right-hand side of (
4.9
),
by
(
4.6
)
and the Hölder inequality,
we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\|(-\triangle)^{\alpha}u_{v_{i}}(r)\|_{V^{\ast}}dr\leqslant\int_{s}^{t}\|u_{v_{i}}(r)\|_{V}dr\leqslant C_{1}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.10)

For the second term on the right-hand side of (
4.9
),
by the Hölder inequality and (
4.7
), we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\|f(r,\cdot,u_{v_{i}}(r),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(r)})\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}dr\leqslant(t-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f(\cdot,\cdot,u_{v_{i}},\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(\cdot)})\|_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leqslant C_{2}^{\frac{1}{q}}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(4.11)

For the third term on the right-hand side of (
4.9
),
by (
2.7
) and Theorem
3.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\|g(r,\cdot,u_{v_{i}}(r),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(r)})\|dr

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g(\cdot,\cdot,u_{v_{i}},\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(\cdot)})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\{\int_{0}^{T}\left[4\|\psi_{g}(r)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\|u_{v_{i}}(r)\|^{2}+4M_{0}(1+\|u_{0}\|^{2})\|\psi_{g}(r)\|^{2}+2\|\psi_{g}(r)\|^{2}\right]dr\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\{2\sqrt{T}C_{1}\|\psi_{g}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}+(2\sqrt{M_{0}(1+\|u_{0}\|^{2})}+\sqrt{2})\|\psi_{g}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;l^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\right\}.

(4.12)

For the fourth term on the right-hand side of (
4.9
),
by (
2.11
), (
4.6
) and Theorem
3.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\|\sigma(r,u_{v_{i}}(r),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(r)})v_{i}(r)\|dr

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\|\sigma(r,u_{v_{i}}(r),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(r)})\|_{L_{2}(l^{2},H)}\|v_{i}(r)\|_{l^{2}}dr

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\int_{s}^{t}\|\sigma(r,u_{v_{i}}(r),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(r)})\|_{L_{2}(l^{2},H)}^{2}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v_{i}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;l^{2})}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left[\int_{s}^{t}M_{\sigma,T}\left(1+\|u_{v_{i}}(r)\|^{2}+\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(r)}(\|\cdot\|^{2})\right)dr\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v_{i}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;l^{2})}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\{M_{\sigma,T}\left[1+C_{1}^{2}+M_{0}\left(1+\|u_{0}\|^{2}\right)\right]\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}C_{1}|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.13)

Then from (
4.9
)-(
4.13
),
it follows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|u_{v_{i}}(t)-u_{v_{i}}(s)\|_{\left(V\bigcap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)^{\ast}}\leqslant C_{3}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{p}},

(4.14)

where
\displaystyle C_{3}=C_{3}(T)>0
.

From (
4.6
) and (
4.7
),
we know that there exist

z\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H)\bigcap L^{2}(0,T;V)\bigcap L^{p}(0,T;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})),\ \varphi\in L^{q}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})),

and a subsequence of
\displaystyle\{u_{v_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
(not relabeled)
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle u_{v_{i}}\rightarrow z\ \text{weak-star in}\ L^{\infty}(0,T;H),

(4.15)

\displaystyle\displaystyle u_{v_{i}}\rightarrow z\ \text{weakly in}\ L^{2}(0,T;V),

(4.16)

\displaystyle\displaystyle u_{v_{i}}\rightarrow z\ \text{weakly in}\ L^{p}(0,T;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})),

(4.17)

\displaystyle\displaystyle f(\cdot,\cdot,u_{v_{i}},\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(\cdot)})\rightarrow\varphi\ \text{weakly in}\ L^{q}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})).

(4.18)

Next, we prove the strong convergence
of
\displaystyle\{u_{v_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
in

\displaystyle L^{2}(0,T;H)
.

Step 2:
proving

\displaystyle\displaystyle u_{v_{i}}\rightarrow z\ \ \ \text{strongly in}\ \ L^{2}(0,T;H).

(4.19)

We firstly prove

\displaystyle u_{v_{i}}\rightarrow z\ \text{strongly in}\ C\left([0,T],\left(V\bigcap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)^{\ast}\right).

By Step 1, we know

\displaystyle\{u_{v_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}

is equicontinuous in

\displaystyle\left(V\bigcap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)^{\ast}

on
\displaystyle[0,T]
.
So by the Arzelà-Ascoli Theorem,
we only need to show that
for each
\displaystyle t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\{u_{v_{i}}(t)\}_{i=1}^{\infty}

is precompact in

\displaystyle\left(V\bigcap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)^{\ast}
.

Let
\displaystyle\zeta_{m}
be the cut-off function
as introduced in the proof of Lemma

3.1
.
Given
\displaystyle t\in[0,T]
, let

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}_{v_{i}}^{m}(t,x)=\zeta_{m}(x)u_{v_{i}}(t,x)

and

\displaystyle\widehat{u}_{v_{i}}^{m}(t,x)=(1-\zeta_{m}(x))u_{v_{i}}(t,x)

for
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then

\displaystyle u_{v_{i}}(t,x)=\widetilde{u}_{v_{i}}^{m}(t,x)+\widehat{u}_{v_{i}}^{m}(t,x).

for every
\displaystyle m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Moreover, by (
4.6
), we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\widehat{u}_{v_{i}}^{m}(t)\|\leqslant\|u_{v_{i}}\|_{C([0,T],H)}\leqslant C_{1},\ \ \ \forall\ i,m\in\mathbb{N},\ t\in[0,T].

(4.20)

Notice that

\displaystyle\widehat{u}_{v_{i}}^{m}(t,x)=0
for
\displaystyle|x|\geqslant m
and
\displaystyle t\in[0,T]
.
By (
4.20
) and the
compactness of the embedding

\displaystyle H^{\alpha}(|x|<m)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(|x|<m)
,
we infer that

\displaystyle\{\widehat{u}_{v_{i}}^{m}(t)\}_{i=1}^{\infty}

is precompact in

\displaystyle\left(V\bigcap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)^{\ast}
.

On the other hand, by Lemma
3.1
and (
4.6
),
we find that for every
\displaystyle\delta>0
,
there exists

\displaystyle m_{0}=m_{0}(u_{0},T,\delta)>0

such that for all
\displaystyle m\geqslant m_{0}
,
\displaystyle i\in\mathbb{N}
and
\displaystyle t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\widetilde{u}_{v_{i}}^{m}(t)\|_{\left(V\bigcap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)^{\ast}}^{2}\leqslant\|\widetilde{u}_{v_{i}}^{m}(t)\|_{H}^{2}\leqslant\int_{|x|\geqslant{\frac{1}{2}}m}|u_{v_{i}}(t,x)|^{2}dx<\frac{1}{4}\delta^{2}.

Then we infer that

\displaystyle\{u_{v_{i}}(t)\}_{i=1}^{\infty}=\{\widetilde{u}_{v_{i}}^{m}(t)+\widehat{u}_{v_{i}}^{m}(t)\}_{i=1}^{\infty}

has a finite open cover with radius
\displaystyle\delta
in

\displaystyle\left(V\bigcap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)^{\ast}
,
and hence

\displaystyle\{u_{v_{i}}(t)\}_{i=1}^{\infty}

is precompact in

\displaystyle\left(V\bigcap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)^{\ast}

for
\displaystyle t\in[0,T]
,
which along with (
4.15
)
implies that

\displaystyle\displaystyle u_{v_{i}}\rightarrow z\ \ \text{strongly in}\ C\left([0,T],\left(V\bigcap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)^{\ast}\right).

(4.21)

Since

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|u_{v_{i}}(t)-z(t)\|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle u_{v_{i}}(t)-z(t),u_{v_{i}}(t)-z(t)\right\rangle_{\left(V\bigcap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\left(V\bigcap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)^{\ast}\right)}dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|u_{v_{i}}(t)-z(t)\|_{V\bigcap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|u_{v_{i}}(t)-z(t)\|_{\left(V\bigcap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)^{\ast}}^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

it follows from (
4.6
) and (
4.21
) that

\displaystyle\displaystyle u_{v_{i}}\rightarrow z\ \ \text{strongly in}\ \ L^{2}(0,T;H).

Step 3:
proving
\displaystyle u_{v}=z
.

By (
3.3
), we obtain that for any

\displaystyle t\in[0,T]
and

\displaystyle\xi\in V\bigcap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(u_{v_{i}}(t),\xi\right)+\int_{0}^{t}\left((-\triangle)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u_{v_{i}}(s),(-\triangle)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\xi\right)ds

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(s,x,u_{v_{i}}(s),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(s)})\xi(x)dxds

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(u_{0},\xi\right)+\int_{0}^{t}\left(g(s,u_{v_{i}}(s),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(s)}),\xi\right)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sigma(s,u_{v_{i}}(s),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(s)})v_{i}(s),\xi\right)ds.

(4.22)

It follows from (
4.19
) in Step 2 that (up to a subsequence)

u_{v_{i}}\rightarrow z\ \ \ \text{a.e. on }\ (0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Then by the continuity of
\displaystyle f
, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle f(t,x,u_{v_{i}}(t),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(t)})\rightarrow f(t,x,z,\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(t)})\ \ \ \text{a.e. on }\ (0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(4.23)

By (
4.18
), (
4.23
) and Mazur’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\varphi=f(t,x,z,\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(t)})\ \ \ \text{a.e. on }\ (0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

(4.24)

and hence

\displaystyle\displaystyle f(\cdot,\cdot,u_{v_{i}},\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(\cdot)})\rightarrow f(\cdot,\cdot,z,\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(\cdot)})\ \ \text{weakly in}\ L^{q}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})).

(4.25)

Taking the limit
of the third term on the left-hand side of (
4.21
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(s,x,u_{v_{i}}(s),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(s)})\xi(x)dxds=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(s,x,z(s),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(s)})\xi(x)dxds

(4.26)

For the second term on the right-hand side of (
4.22
),
by (
2.6
) and (
4.19
), we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\left(g(s,u_{v_{i}}(s),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(s)})-g(s,z(s),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(s)}),\xi\right)ds\right|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|g(s,u_{v_{i}}(s),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(s)})-g(s,z(s),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(s)})\|\|\xi\|ds

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\psi_{g}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\|\xi\|t^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{v_{i}}(s)-z(s)\|ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\rightarrow\ 0,\ \ \ \text{as}\ i\to\infty.

(4.27)

For the third term on the right-hand side of (
4.22
), we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sigma(s,u_{v_{i}}(s),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(s)})v_{i}(s),\xi\right)ds

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left(\left(\sigma(s,u_{v_{i}}(s),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(s)})-\sigma(s,z(s),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(s)})\right)v_{i}(s),\xi\right)ds

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sigma(s,z(s),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(s)})v_{i}(s),\xi\right)ds.

(4.28)

For the first term on the right-hand side of (
4.28
),
by (
2.12
), (
4.6
) and (
4.19
), we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\int_{0}^{t}\left(\left(\sigma(s,u_{v_{i}}(s),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(s)})-\sigma(s,z(s),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(s)})\right)v_{i}(s),\xi\right)ds\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|\sigma(s,u_{v_{i}}(s),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(s)})-\sigma(s,z(s),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(s)})\|_{L_{2}(l^{2},H)}\|v_{i}(s)\|_{l^{2}}\|\xi\|ds

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle L_{\sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi\|\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{v_{i}}(s)-z(s)\|\|v_{i}(s)\|_{l^{2}}ds

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle L_{\sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi\|\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|u_{v_{i}}(s)-z(s)\|^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|v_{i}(s)\|_{l^{2}}^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\displaystyle\ 0,\ \ \ \text{as}\ i\to+\infty.

(4.29)

For the second term on the right-hand side of (
4.28
),
we define an operator
\displaystyle\mathcal{G}:L^{2}(0,T;l^{2})\rightarrow H
by

\mathcal{G}(\tilde{v})=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,z(s),\mathcal{L}_{u^{0}(s)})\tilde{v}(s)ds,\ \ \ \forall\ \tilde{v}\in L^{2}(0,T;l^{2}).

By (

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\Big(\|u_{v^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon}(r\wedge\tau_{R}^{\varepsilon})-u_{v^{\varepsilon}}(r\wedge\tau_{R}^{\varepsilon})\|^{2}+2\int_{0}^{r\wedge\tau_{R}^{\varepsilon}}\|(-\triangle)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left(u_{v^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon}(s)-u_{v^{\varepsilon}}(s)\right)\|^{2}ds\Big)

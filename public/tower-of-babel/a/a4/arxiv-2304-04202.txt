Continuous eigenfunctions of the transfer operator for Dyson models
Anders Johansson, Anders Öberg, and Mark Pollicott

Abstract
. In this article we address a well known problem at the intersection of ergodic theory and statistical mechanics. We prove that there exists a continuous eigenfunction for the transfer operator corresponding to pair potentials that satisfy a square summability condition on the variations, when the inverse temperature is subcritical. As a corollary we obtain a continuous eigenfunction for the classical Dyson model, with interactions J ⁡ ( k ) = β ​ k − α \mathrm{J}(k)=\beta\,k^{-\alpha} , k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 , in the whole subcritical regime β < β c \beta<\beta_{c} for which the parameter α \alpha is greater than 3 / 2 3/2 .

1.
Introduction

Ruelle
[
25
]
and Sinai
[
26
]
pioneered the study of long-range
models within statistical mechanics in terms of transfer operators and their
eigenfunctions and eigenmeasures, an important theory that later was further developed by
Walters
[
27
]
.

It is well known
[
27
]
that there exists a continuous and strictly
positive eigenfunction of a transfer operator defined on a symbolic shift space
with a finite number of symbols if the one-point potential
\phi
has summable
variations. Here we prove, for the particular class of potentials we study, the existence of a continuous
eigenfunction under the weaker condition of square summable variations of the potential
\phi
and a subcritical scaling parameter
\beta
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\{-1,+1\}
and
V=\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,\dots\}
or
V=\mathbb{Z}
. Elements
x\in X
of
X=\mathcal{S}^{\mathbb{N}}

are
one-sided

spin sequences
.
We will also consider
two-sided
spin sequences
x\in\bar{X}=\mathcal{S}^{\mathbb{Z}}

depending on the context.
Let
T:X\to X
be the left shift
(x_{i})\mapsto(x_{i+1})
.
Consider the positive operator
\mathscr{L}=\mathscr{L}_{\phi}
on the space of continuous function

C(X)
, given by

\mathscr{L}f(x)=\sum_{y\in T^{-1}x}e^{\phi(y)}\,f(y),

In this paper, we specify the

one-point potential

\phi(x)=\phi(x;\mathrm{J})\in C(X)
by a sequence
\mathrm{J}(k)\geq 0
,

\mathrm{J}(0)=0
, so that
\phi
takes the form

(1)

\phi(x)=x_{0}\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{J}(k)x_{k}.

This type of potential appears naturally in the context of statistical mechanics;
in the important special class of
Dyson potentials

we have
\mathrm{J}(k)=\beta\cdot k^{-\alpha}
for the interaction strength power
\alpha>1

and the inverse temperature
\beta>0
.
We assume the sequence
\mathrm{J}(k)
is summable, so that in particular, for
n\geq 0
,

r_{n}:=\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\mathrm{J}(k)\to 0\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$}.

Note that the
n
th variation
\operatorname{var}_{n}\phi
, as defined in section
2.1
, equals
2r_{n}
.
Let
\mathscr{M}(X)
denote the set of probability measures on
X
and let

\mathscr{M}_{\phi}=\{\nu\in\mathscr{M}(X):\forall\,f\in C(X)\,\int\mathscr{L}_{\phi}f\,d\nu=\lambda\int f\,d\nu,\lambda>0\}

be the set of normalised
eigenmeasures
for the unique positive eigenvalue

\lambda=\lambda_{\phi}
that equals the spectral radius of
\mathscr{L}
; we also write
\mathscr{L}^{*}\nu=\lambda\nu
.
From the Schauder-Tychonoff theorem applied to the mapping

\nu\mapsto\mathscr{L}^{*}\nu/\nu(X)
on
\mathscr{M}(X)
, we deduce that
\mathscr{M}_{\phi}\not=\emptyset
.

We prove the following general result.

Theorem 1
.

For
x\in X
, define
r(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}r_{n}x_{n}
. If
\nu\in\mathscr{M}_{\phi}
and

(2)

\int e^{r(x)}\,d\nu(x)<\infty

then there is a strictly positive
continuous
eigenfunction

h(x)\in C(X)
of
\mathscr{L}=\mathscr{L}_{\phi}
such that
\mathscr{L}h=\lambda h
.

A measure
\mu\in\mathscr{M}(X)
is a Doeblin measure (
[
7
]
, a.k.a as a

g
-measure
[
22
]
) if it is a
translation invariant
eigenmeasure of

\mathscr{L}_{\log g}^{*}
for some continuous function
g>0
with
\mathscr{L}_{\log g}1=1
. The
theory of Doeblin measures (
[
9
,
22
,
19
,
6
,
20
]
) is
close to the topic of this paper, since we can construct a Doeblin function
g
,
from a one-point potential
\phi
and a continuous eigenfunction
h(x)
of the
transfer operator
\mathscr{L}_{\phi}
by

(3)

g(x)=\frac{e^{\phi(x)}\,h(x)}{\lambda\,h(Tx)}.

From (
3
), we see that the measure
\mu(x)=h(x)\cdot\nu
is a translation
invariant eigenmeasure to the transfer operator
\mathscr{L}_{\log g}
, i.e., a Doeblin
measure. We refer to
\mu
as the
equilibrium
measure of
\phi
. Thus the
existence of a continuous eigenfunction of the transfer operator implies the

g
-measure property
of
\mu
. i.e., that
\mu
can be represented as a Doeblin measure
(a
g
-measure) for some continuous function
g>0
, i.e., so that it is a fixed point of
\mathscr{L}_{\log g}^{*}
. In contrast to our result, we
observe the result by Bissacot
et al.

[
8
]
, where they show
that the
g
-measure property does not hold in the context of the Dyson model, when
1<\alpha<2
is small enough and for
high values of
\beta
. In view of (
3
) this means that there is no
continuous eigenfunction. Note, however, that the
g
-measure property may hold even if a
continuous eigenfunction does not exist.

We may also construct
\nu
and
\mu
as long-range
Ising models
. For
V=\mathbb{Z}

and
V=\mathbb{N}
define the
potential
(see section
2.1.1
below)
\Phi_{V}

\Phi_{V}(x)=\sum_{ij}J_{V}(ij)x_{i}x_{j},\quad x\in\mathcal{S}^{V}

with
J_{V}(ij)=\mathrm{J}(|i-j|)
and where we sum over the set
ij\in V^{(2)}
of unordered
pairs of elements
i,j\in V
. We have

\Phi_{V}(x)=\sum_{k\in V}\phi(T^{k}x)

as an equality where we consider both the left and right hand side as potentials.

The set
\mathscr{M}_{\phi}
of eigenmeasures is equal to the
set of Gibbs measures
\mathscr{G}(\Phi_{\mathbb{N}})
(one-sided Ising models) consistent with the
potential
\Phi_{\mathbb{N}}(x)
. A two-sided Ising model
\mu(\bar{x})
,

\bar{x}\in{\{-1,+1\}}^{\mathbb{Z}}
, is a translation invariant Gibbs measure in

\mu\in\mathscr{G}(\Phi_{\mathbb{Z}})
. By the Fortuin-Kasteleyn (FK) correspondence, we can couple

\nu(x)
with a random cluster distribution
\nu(\mathit{G})
on graphs
\mathit{G}
with vertex
set
\mathbb{N}
and, similarly, couple
\mu(\bar{x})
to a random cluster distribution

\mu(\mathit{G})
on graphs with vertex set
\mathbb{Z}
. We write
\mu(x)
for the equilibrium measure, which we can
capture as the marginal distribution of
x=\bar{x}|_{\mathbb{N}}

under
\mu
. For fixed interactions
J_{V}(ij)
, it is well-known that there
is a critical
\beta_{c}(J_{\mathbb{N}})\in[0,\infty]
such that
|\mathscr{M}_{\beta\phi}|=|\mathscr{G}(\Phi_{\mathbb{N}})|=1
if

0\leq\beta<\beta_{c}(J_{\mathbb{N}})
(i.e.
uniqueness
) and non-uniqueness if
\beta>\beta_{c}(J_{\mathbb{N}})
.
We also have a critical
\beta_{c}(J_{\mathbb{Z}})
for uniqueness of
\mathscr{G}(\beta\Phi_{\mathbb{Z}})
.
These critical
\beta_{\mathbb{N}}
and
\beta_{\mathbb{Z}}
are also critical values for the existence of an infinite
cluster (i.e., percolation) in the corresponding random cluster models.

To derive the following theorem we use a result of
Hutchcroft
[
18
, Theorem 1.5]
about exponentially small tail
probabilities in the cluster size distribution of
\mu(\mathit{G})
in the subcritical
regime. It is a generalisation of a result by Duminil-Copin
et
al.

[
10
]
to the long-range setting that we are considering. (See
also the paper by Aoun
[
3
]
.)

Theorem 2
.

If
\phi
has square summable variations, i.e. if

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{(\operatorname{var}_{n}\phi)}^{2}<\infty
then there exists a unique continuous
eigenfunction
h
of
\mathscr{L}_{\beta\phi}
for all
\beta<\beta_{c}(J_{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Remark 1
.

We conjecture that
\beta_{c}(J_{\mathbb{Z}})=\beta_{c}(J_{\mathbb{N}})
.

\triangle

Remark 2
.

The condition of
square summable variations
was studied in the context of uniqueness and non-uniqueness of Doeblin measures (
g
-measures)
\mu

in
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
6
]
and
[
16
]
, i.e., when
\phi=\log g
and
\sum_{y\in T^{-1}x}g(y)=1
for all

and
\mathscr{L}_{\log g}^{*}\mu=\mu
. In
[
19
]
and
[
20
]

uniqueness of
\mu
was proved when
\sum_{n}(\operatorname{var}_{n}\log g)^{2}<\infty
, and Berger
et al
.
[
6
]
proved that this is sharp in the sense that for all
\varepsilon>0
we can have
\sum_{n}(\operatorname{var}_{n}\log g)^{2+\varepsilon}<\infty

and multiple solutions
\mu
of
\mathscr{L}_{\log g}^{*}\mu=\mu
. In Gallesco
et al
.
[
16
]
they study the ramifications of square summability even further.

\triangle

Remark 3
.

In
[
21
, Theorem 1]
we proved that
\beta_{c}(J_{\mathbb{Z}})\leq 8\beta_{c}(J_{\mathbb{N}})
,
which, in the light of the counterexample to uniqueness by Dyson
[
11
]
, shows that for general potentials
\phi
we would have examples such that for
\varepsilon>0
,
\sum_{n}(\operatorname{var}_{n}\phi)^{1+\varepsilon}<\infty
and with multiple eigenmeasures
\nu
, i.e., multiple solutions

\nu
of
\mathscr{L}^{*}\nu=\lambda\nu
. Hence it is clear that the condition of summable variations of general potentials is in the above sense sharp for uniqueness of an eigenmeasure
\nu
of
\mathscr{L}^{*}
, and similarly the condition of square summable variations is sharp for Doeblin measures (
g
-measures). In view of the conjugation (
3
),
where we under sufficiently strong conditions have a unique Doeblin measure
\mu
for the potential
\log g
, and a unique eigenmeaure
\nu
of
\mathscr{L}^{*}
for the potential
\phi
, it seems reasonable to think that for general continuous potentials (not only the potentials we consider here), the condition of square summable variations could be a very important condition for the existence of a continuous eigenfunction of the transfer operator, but we do not specify any conjecture in this direction.

\triangle

Remark 4
.

Walters considered
Bowen’s condition
and managed to obtain some regularity for an eigenfunction of the transfer operator in
[
28
, Theorem 5.1]
. In the case of the Dyson potentials it reduces to summable variations, and it is not clear whether a continuous eigenfunction follows in general from Bowen’s condition. Another interesting condition is the one provided by Berbee in
[
4
]
. Berbee proves that there exists a unique Gibbs measure for both one-sided and two-sided long-range models whenever

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-r_{1}-\cdots-r_{n}}=\infty,

where
r_{n}=\operatorname{var}_{n}\phi
, for some potential
\phi
, suggesting the possible existence of a continuous eigenfunction, but this is unknown. In the case of the Dyson model, Berbee’s condition reduces to the assumption
\alpha\geq 2
(and high temperatures in the case of
\alpha=2
). Note that
\alpha>2
is the region of summable variations in which
the Dyson model does not have a phase transition at any temperature.

\triangle

The existence of a continuous eigenfunction is also related to the rigidity of coboundaries; see especially the work of Quas
[
24

.

states

. We write

for the complement

. A
configuration
is an element

of the
product space

. If

or
V=\mathbb{N}
, we let
T
denote the left
shift on the symbolic space
X
, i.e.
{(Tx)}_{i}=x_{i+1}
with destruction

if

. We give the space

the usual product topology and the

\mathscr{F}
. For

and

, we write

for the restriction

of

to

and
\mathscr{F}_{G}
for the sigma-algebra
generated by
x_{G}
and
\mathrm{m}\mathscr{F}_{G}
for the
set of
\mathscr{F}_{G}
-measurable functions. We denote by
[x]_{G}
the cylinder set

[x]_{G}=\{y\mid y_{G}=x_{G}\}
.

For a function

the
variation
at
\Lambda\subset V
and

is

\operatorname{var}_{\Lambda}f(x)=\sup_{y,z\in[x]_{\Lambda}}|f(y)-f(z)|

and

\operatorname{var}_{\Lambda}f=\sup_{x}\operatorname{var}_{\Lambda}f(x)
. A function
f
is
local
if it is

\mathscr{F}_{\Lambda}
-measurable (
\operatorname{var}_{\Lambda}f=0
) at some
\Lambda\Subset V
. It is
continuous
at

x
if
\lim_{\Lambda_{n}\uparrow V}\operatorname{var}_{\Lambda_{n}}f(x)=0
, where
\Lambda_{n}\uparrow V
denotes an
increasing sequence of finite sets whose union is
V
. We denote by
C(X)
the
space of continuous functions. In case we consider the integer interval
\Lambda={\left[0,n\right)}
,
we replace subscript by
{\left[0,n\right)}
with the subscript
n
, thus

\operatorname{var}_{n}f=\operatorname{var}_{\left[0,n\right)}f,\quad\text{and}\quad[x]_{n}=[x]_{{\left[0,n\right)}},\ldots\ \text{etc}.

Let
\mathscr{M}(X)
denote the space of probability distributions on
X
. Elements

\alpha\in\mathscr{M}(X)
are often written as
\alpha(x)
in order to make it clear that
\alpha
is
the distribution
\mathsf{P}(x)
of the random configuration
x\in X
. If

then

refers to
f_{*}\alpha=\alpha\circ f^{-1}
. Sometimes, we introduce an underlying
probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{B},\mathsf{P})
with expectation operator
\mathsf{E}
, where it is clear
that
\alpha
is the distribution of
x
. In that case, we write
\mathsf{E}(f(x))=\int f\,d\alpha

and
\mathsf{P}(x\in A)=\int\mathsf{1}_{A}(x)\,d\alpha(x)
. We write
\mu(x)\prec\nu(x^{\prime})
to mean

stochastic domination
between elements in
\mathscr{M}(X)
, meaning that we can
couple
\mu(x)
and
\nu(x^{\prime})
so that
\mathsf{P}(x\leq x^{\prime})=1
with respect to the partial
order
\leq
on
X
induced by the order on
\mathscr{A}\subset\mathbb{Z}
.

A
Bernoulli measure

\eta(x;p)\in\mathscr{M}(X)
has as parameter an assignment

p={(p_{i})}_{i\in V}\in\mathscr{M}{(\mathscr{A})}^{V}
and

is the product measure

\bigotimes_{i\in V}p_{i}(x_{i})
. We use

2.1.1.
Potentials

The Hamming distance on
X=\mathscr{A}^{V}
is the cardinality of

\Delta(x,y):=\{i:x(i)\not=y(i)\}

and the Hamming graph (digraph) is the set of pairs

\{(x,x^{\prime}):|\Delta(x,x^{\prime})|=1\}
. A
potential
is a real valued function

\Delta\Phi(x,x^{\prime})
on the Hamming graph such that

\Delta\Phi(x_{1},x_{2})+\Delta\Phi(x_{2},x_{3})+\cdots+\Delta\Phi(x_{k},x_{1})=0

for
every cycle
\{(x_{1},x_{2}),\dots,(x_{k},x_{1})\}
. It is clear that potentials on

X
make up a real linear space. Formally, we write potentials as functions

\Phi(x)
on
X
, since any real-valued function
\phi(x)
defines the
potential
\Delta\phi(x,y)=\phi(x)-\phi(y)
and we can always represent a
potential
locally
at
\Lambda\Subset V
with a real valued function

\Phi_{\Lambda}
so that
\Delta\Phi(x,y)=\Phi_{\Lambda}(x)-\Phi_{\Lambda}(y)
for
all pairs
(x,y)
where
\Delta(x,y)\subset\Lambda
. We usually obtain a
potential
\Phi
as a
potential limit

\lim\Phi_{\Lambda_{n}}
from a
system of local functions
\Phi_{\Lambda}\in\mathrm{m}\mathscr{F}_{\Lambda}
, where the
limits of differences

\Delta\Phi(x,y):=\lim_{\Lambda_{n}\uparrow V}\Phi_{\Lambda_{n}}(x)-\Phi_{\Lambda_{n}}(y)

are finite and well defined for all pairs
(x,y)
with
\Delta(x,y)\Subset V
. A
potential
\Phi
is
continuous
at
x
if the difference

\Delta\Phi(x_{\vphantom{\bar{\Lambda}}{\Lambda}}x_{\bar{\Lambda}},y_{\vphantom{\bar{\Lambda}}{\Lambda}}x_{\bar{\Lambda}})

is a continuous function of
x_{\bar{\Lambda}}
for fixed
x_{\Lambda}
and
y_{\Lambda}
in
\mathscr{A}^{\Lambda}
.

We say that a probability measure
\alpha(x)\in\mathscr{M}(X)
is
fully
specified
if the system
\{\log\alpha([x]_{\Lambda_{n}})\}
defines a
potential
\operatorname{Log}\alpha
so that, for all pairs
x,y
with
|\Delta(x,y)|=1
, the limit

\lim_{\Lambda_{n}\uparrow V}\log\alpha([x]_{\Lambda_{n}})-\log\alpha([y]_{\Lambda_{n}})

exists as a real number. For instance, a Bernoulli measure
\eta(p)=\eta(x;p)

is fully specified with
\operatorname{Log}\eta(x)
given by the potential limit

\sum_{i\in V}\log p_{i}(x_{i})
. We say that
\alpha
is
consistent
with
potential
\Phi
if
\Phi=\operatorname{Log}\alpha
and we write
\mathscr{G}(\Phi)
to denote the set
of measures consistent with
\Phi
.

It is well known
that the set
\mathscr{G}(\Phi)
of probability measures consistent with potential
\Phi
is
non-empty whenever
\Phi
is continuous and the elements of
\mathscr{G}(\Phi)
are then said
to be
Gibbsian
. We have
uniqueness
of measures consistent with
\Phi

if
\mathscr{G}(\Phi)
contains only one element.

Given a fully specified measure
\alpha\in\mathscr{M}(X)
and a potential
\Phi
, we write

e^{\Phi}\ltimes\alpha
for the fully specified measures in
\mathscr{G}(\Phi+\operatorname{Log}\alpha)
.
If we have a unique element in

e^{\Phi}\ltimes\alpha
we write
\mu=e^{\Phi}\ltimes\alpha
.
If we can represent the
potential
\Phi
as a function such
that
e^{\Phi}\in L^{1}(\alpha)
then
e^{\Phi}\ltimes\alpha
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\alpha
and

(4)

e^{\Phi}\ltimes\alpha=\frac{e^{\Phi}\cdot\alpha}{\int e^{\Phi}\,d\alpha}.

Note that

(5)

e^{\Psi}\ltimes(e^{\Phi}\ltimes\alpha)=e^{\Psi+\Phi}\ltimes\alpha.

Furthermore, given a product of fully specified measures

\alpha(x)\otimes\beta(y)
and potentials
\Phi(x)
and
\Psi(y)
on
X
and

Y
, respectively, we obtain that

(6)

e^{\Phi+\Psi}\ltimes(\alpha\otimes\beta)=(e^{\Phi}\ltimes\alpha)\otimes(e^{\Psi}\ltimes\beta).

2.1.2.
Graphs

. A

A

. Denote by
\Gamma(G)
the space of spanning subgraphs of

\Gamma(V)=\Gamma(K(V))

. We can represent an element

\mathit{G}\in\Gamma(G)

\mathit{G}=(\mathit{G}_{e})\in{\{0,1\}}^{E(G)}

\mathit{G}\subset V^{(2)}

2.2.
The eigenfunction as a Radon-Nikodym derivative

A continuous eigenfunction means that there is a continuous Radon-Nikodym
derivative between the two-sided equilibrium measure (a translation invariant
Gibbs measure) and the one-sided Gibbs measure, that is, the marginal of the two-sided measure
obtained by taking the restriction to
\mathbb{N}
. Consider the transfer operator

\mathscr{L}=\mathscr{L}_{\phi}
from Theorem
1
. Let
\nu\in\mathscr{M}_{\phi}
and let
\mu\in\mathscr{M}(X)
be any

translation invariant
measure such that
\mu|_{\mathscr{F}_{n}}\ll\nu|_{\mathscr{F}_{n}}
, for all

n\geq 0
. For
x\in X
define the likelihood ratios
h_{n}(x)
,
n\geq 0
, by

(7)

h_{n}(x)=\frac{\mu\left([x]_{n}\right)}{\nu\left([x]_{n}\right)},

where
[x]_{n}=[x]_{{\left[0,n\right)}}
. The limit of
h_{n}
in (
7
) is well-defined

\nu
-almost everywhere by the martingale convergence theorem. If it exists in

L^{1}(\nu)
then
\mu\ll\nu
and the limit
h
is equal to the Radon-Nikodym derivative

h=d\mu/d\nu
. As shown in the lemma below, we can then deduce the existence of an
eigenfunction
h
in
L^{1}(\nu)
.

The following lemma states that if the sequence
h_{n}
converges uniformly, then
the continuous limit function indeed is a strictly positive eigenfunction. The
continuity is of course an elementary consequence of uniform convergence by
Cauchy’s theorem, and boundedness follows from the continuity of
h
on the
compact set
X
. Uniform convergence also implies convergence in
L^{1}(\nu)
and we
note that
\int h\;d\nu=1
.

Lemma 4

(Radon-Nikodym interpretation)
.

If
h_{n}(x)\to h(x)
uniformly as
n\to\infty
then
h
is a continuous eigenfunction of

\mathscr{L}
such that
\inf h(x)>0
.

Proof of Lemma
4
.

That the Radon-Nikodym derivative
h=d\mu/d\nu
, if it exists, is necessarily an
eigenfunction of the transfer operator
\mathscr{L}
follows from

\displaystyle\int g\cdot h\,d\nu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int(g\circ T)\cdot h\,d\nu\qquad(\mu=\mu\circ T^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathscr{L}(g\circ T\cdot h)\,d\nu\qquad(\text{$\nu$ eigenmeasure})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int g\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathscr{L}h\right)\,d\nu,

where the last equality follows from the definition of
\mathscr{L}
. This holds for
all
g\in C(X)
if and only if
\mathscr{L}h=\lambda h
,
\lambda>0
, as elements of
L^{1}(\nu)
.

We deduce that
\inf h>0
by the following argument. For an
x
such that

h(x)=0
we have
\mathscr{L}h(x)=\sum_{a\in A}e^{\phi(ax)}h(ax)=0
and hence
h(ax)=0

for all
a\in\mathscr{A}
. Thus the set of zeros of
h
is either empty or a dense subset
of
X
, implying that
\inf h>0
by continuity and compactness.
∎

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K_{0}}\,\int 2^{\omega_{n}(\mathit{G}_{+})-\omega_{n}(\mathit{G})}\cdot 2^{R_{0}(\mathit{G})}A_{n}(x,\mathit{G})\,d\alpha_{n}(\mathit{G}_{+})\,d\xi(W)\qquad\text{by~\eqref{eq:Bnfac}}.

Note that

(26)

\omega_{n}(\mathit{G}_{+})-\omega_{n}(\mathit{G})=R_{n}(\mathit{G})-R_{0}(\mathit{G})

since both sides equal the rank of the subgraph of
\tilde{\mathit{H}}^{0}
consisting of edges

ij\in H
with one endpoint in
\tilde{C}_{n}
. From (
26
), we deduce that

(27)

h_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{K_{0}}\cdot\int A_{n}(x,\mathit{G})\cdot 2^{R_{n}(\mathit{G})}\cdot\,d\alpha_{n}(\mathit{G}_{+})\,d\xi(W).

Let
N=N(W)
be the minimum
n
such that
{F_{R}}\subset{\left[0,n\right)}
and note that
Lemma
6
implies that
\xi(N\geq n)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. On account
of (
18
) and (
25
), it follows that the integrand

A_{n}(x,W)\cdot 2^{R_{n}(\mathit{G})}
in (
27
) equals
A(x,W)\cdot 2^{R(W)}

on the event
N<n
, since
B_{n}(x,\mathit{G}_{+})=1
,
\alpha_{n}
-almost surely. Let

h(x):=\frac{1}{K_{0}}\int A(x,W)2^{R(W)}\,d\alpha_{n}(\mathit{G}_{+})\,d\xi(W)=\frac{1}{K_{0}}\,\int A(x,W)2^{R(W)}\,d\xi(W).

Thus

\displaystyle|h_{n}(x)-h(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{K_{0}}\,\int\left|A_{n}(x,W)\cdot 2^{R_{n}(\mathit{G})}-A(x,W)\cdot 2^{R(\mathit{G})}\right|\,d\alpha_{n}(\mathit{G}_{+})\,d\xi(W)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K_{0}}\,\int_{N>n}\left|A_{n}(x,W)\cdot 2^{R_{n}(\mathit{G})}-A(x,W)\cdot 2^{R(\mathit{G})}\right|\,d\alpha_{n}(\mathit{G}_{+})\,d\xi(W).

Since

we conclude that

\displaystyle|h_{n}(x)-h(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{K_{0}}\int_{N>n}2^{R(W)}\,d\xi(W)\leq\frac{1}{K_{0}}\cdot\xi(N>n)\cdot\int 2^{R}\,d\xi,

where, on account of Lemma
6
, the right hand side tends to zero
with a rate independent of

. ∎

2.5.
Proof that Theorem
1
implies Theorem
2

Assume
\mathit{G}=\mathit{G}_{+}\in\Gamma(\mathbb{N})
with distribution
\nu(\mathit{G})=\mathsf{RC}(\mathit{G};p=1-e^{-\beta J_{\mathbb{N}}})
.
Recall that
J(ij)=\mathrm{J}(|i-j|)
satisfies the square summability condition

\sum r_{n}^{2}<\infty
with
r_{n}=\sum_{k=n+1}\mathrm{J}(k)
. Order the elements of
\mathscr{C}=\mathscr{C}(\mathit{G})
as

\mathscr{C}=\{C_{0},C_{1},\dots\}
so that
0=\iota_{0}<\iota_{1}<\dots
where

\iota_{n}=\min(i\in C_{n})=\inf\{i:i\not\in C_{1}\cup\dots\cup C_{n-1}\}.

We first show that if the cluster size distribution of
C_{0}
has

exponentially decreasing tails
, i.e. if, for some
K>0
and some
c>0
,
we have

(28)

\mathsf{P}(|C_{0}|>n)\leq Ke^{-cn},

then this implies the condition (
2
) or equivalently,
by (
10
), that

(29)

\mathsf{E}\left(\prod_{C\in\mathscr{C}(\mathit{G})}\cosh(r(C))\right)<\infty.

Thus (
28
) implies the conditions of Theorem
1
hold
and thus the existence of a continuous eigenfunction.

If we condition the random cluster model on the clusters
\{C_{0},\dots,C_{k-1}\}

that partition
\left[0,\iota_{k}\right)
then the distribution of the remaining graph

\mathit{G}\left[\overline{\left(\cup_{j=1}^{k-1}C_{j}\right)}\right]
is the random cluster
model with edge probabilities
p^{\prime}(ij)=p(ij)\mathsf{1}_{i,j\not\in\cup\mathscr{C}_{n}}
. (See
e.g.
[
5
]
.) It follows that the conditional distribution of
C_{k}
,
given
C_{1},\dots,C_{k-1}
, is stochastically dominated by the distribution of
C_{0}
shifted
\iota_{k}
steps to the right. In particular, it follows that the
conditional distribution of
|C_{k}|
has exponentially decreasing tails. Thus,
for some
K>0
and
c>0
as in (
28
)

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}\left(|C_{k}|^{n}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathsf{P}(|C_{0}|^{n}\geq x)\,dx

(30)

\displaystyle\leq K\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-cx^{1/n}}\,dx=K\cdot\frac{n!}{c^{n}}.

Since
r_{n}
is a decreasing sequence, we have
{r(C_{k})}\leq r_{\iota_{k}}\cdot|C_{k}|
and the
Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies that
{r(C_{k})}^{2}\leq R\cdot|C_{k}|
where

R=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}r_{i}^{2}
. Thus,

\displaystyle{\cosh(r(C_{k}))}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{{r(C_{k})}^{2n}}{(2n)!}\leq 1+{r^{2}_{\iota_{k}}\cdot|C_{k}|^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{{r(C_{k})}^{2n-2}}{(2n)!}

\displaystyle\leq 1+{r^{2}_{\iota_{n}}}\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{R^{n-1}|C_{k}|^{n+1}}{(2n)!}.

Taking the conditional expectation, using (
30
), gives

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}\left(\cosh(r(C_{k}))|C_{1},\dots,C_{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 1+{r^{2}_{\iota_{k}}}\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{KR^{n-1}\cdot(n+1)!\cdot c^{-{(n+1)}}}{(2n)!}

\displaystyle\leq 1+{r^{2}_{\iota_{k}}}M

where a term-wise comparison gives
M<\infty
. (E.g. using that

2^{n}\cdot{(n!)}^{2}\leq{(2n)!}
.)

We obtain

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}\left(\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\cosh(r(C_{n}))\right)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{E}\left(\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathsf{E}\left(\cosh(r(C_{n}))\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{n-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq e^{M\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r^{2}_{\iota_{n}}}<e^{MR}<\infty,

and thus we have shown that
\eqref{eq:geometric}\implies\eqref{eq:cosh}
.

Finally, we need to show that the condition
\beta<\beta_{c}(J_{\mathbb{Z}})
of Theorem
2

implies (
28
).

Since the weighting
J_{\mathbb{Z}}
is vertex-transitive, the result in Hutchcroft
[
18
]
says that if
\beta<\beta_{c}(J_{\mathbb{Z}})
, then for
the two-sided model
\mu(\mathit{G})
, the distribution
\mu(|C_{\mathit{G}}(\mathrm{o})|)
of the size of the cluster containing any
\mathrm{o}\in\mathbb{Z}
has exponentially decreasing tails. Since

\nu(\mathit{G}_{+})\prec\mu(\mathit{G}[\mathbb{N}])
and
C_{0}=C_{\mathit{G}_{+}}(0)\subset C_{\mathit{G}}(0)\cap\mathbb{N}
, the
condition (
28
) readily follows for the one-sided model
\nu
. ∎

Calabi-Yau property of Quantum Groups of GL ⁡ ( 2 ) \operatorname{GL}(2) Representation Type
Xiaolan YU, Xingting WANG

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove that the quantum groups 𝒢 ⁡ ( A , B ) \mathcal{G}(A,B) introduced by Mrozinski in [ 9 ] and their Hopf-Galois objects are twisted Calabi-Yau algebras, and give their Nakayama automorphisms explicitly.

Introduction

In order to classify the cosemisimple Hopf algebras whose corepresentation semi-ring is isomorphic to that of
\operatorname{GL}(2)
, C. Mrozinski introduced a new family of Hopf algebras
[
9
]
as follows.

Throughout, let
\mathbbm{k}
be an algebraically closed field. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geqslant 2
and let
A,B\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbbm{k})
. The algebra
\mathcal{G}(A,B)
is presented by generators
(u_{ij})_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n}
,
\mathbbm{D},\mathbbm{D}^{-1}
subject to relations

(1)

u^{t}Au=A\mathbbm{D},\;\;\;uBu^{t}=B\mathbbm{D},\;\;\;\mathbbm{D}\mathbbm{D}^{-1}=1=\mathbbm{D}^{-1}\mathbbm{D},

where
u
is the matrix
(u_{ij})_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n}
and
u^{t}
denotes its transpose. This algebra has a natural Hopf algebra structure described in
[
9
, Proposition 2.1]
. Let

A_{q}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-q&0\\
\end{pmatrix},\ \text{for some}\ q\in\mathbbm{k}^{\times}.

The Hopf algebra
\mathcal{G}(A_{q},A_{q}^{-1})
is just
\mathcal{O}(\operatorname{GL}_{q}(2))
, namely, the function algebra on the quantum group
\operatorname{GL}_{q}(2)
. For a matrix
A\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbbm{k})
, in
[
5
]
, Dubois-Violette and Launer defined the Hopf algebra
\mathcal{B}(A)
as the quantum automorphism group of the non-degenerate bilinear form associated to
A
. There is a surjective Hopf algebra morphism from
\mathcal{G}(A,A^{-1})
to the Hopf algebra
\mathcal{B}(A)
. In view of the definition, the Hopf algebras
\mathcal{G}(A,B)
might be viewed as a generalization of
the Hopf algebras
\mathcal{B}(A)
.

In
[
1
, Theorem 6.1 and Corollary 6.3]
, Bichon proved that the Hopf algebras
\mathcal{B}(A)
are smooth of dimension 3 and satisfy the Poincaré duality. It is equivalent to say that they are twisted Calabi-Yau (CY for short) algebras of dimension 3 (Definition
1.4
). CY algebras were introduced by Ginzburg in 2006
[
8
]
to transport the geometry of CY manifolds to noncommutative algebraic geometry. Nowadays, CY algebras and their categorifications are deeply entangled with noncommutative geometry, cluster algebras, topological field theories and representation theory of noncommutative algebras. Twisted (or “skew”) CY algebras are a generalization of CY algebras. Associated to a twisted CY algebra, there exists a so-called Nakayama automorphism. This algebra automorphism is unique up to an inner one. A CY algebra is a twisted CY algebras whose Nakayama automorphism is inner. Actually, the twisted CY property has been studied for many years, even before the definition of CY algebras, under the notion of Artin-Schelter (AS for short) regularity. AS-regular algebras play a central role in the study of noncommutative projective geometry. A connected graded algebra is graded twisted CY if and only if it is AS-regular (cf.
[
10
, Lemma 1.2]
). Therefore, twisted CY algebras can also be viewed as a generalization of AS-regular algebras. In particular in the case of Hopf algebras, it is proved in
[
4
]
(cf.
[
10
, Lemma 1.3]
) that Noetherian AS-regular Hopf algebras are twisted CY algebras (the twisted CY property is called “rigid Gorenstein” in
[
4
]
).

The main aim of this paper is to prove that the algebras
\mathcal{G}(A,B)
are twisted CY algebras. We construct a free Yetter-Drinfeld resolution (Definition
1.7
) of the trivial Yetter-Drinfeld module
\mathbbm{k}
over
\mathcal{G}(A,B)
(Theorem
2.3
). As a consequence, we obtain the following main theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
A,B\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbbm{k})\;(n\geqslant 2)
such that
B^{t}A^{t}BA=\lambda I_{n}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbbm{k}^{\times}
. The algebra
\mathcal{G}(A,B)
is a twisted CY algebra with Nakayama automorphism
\mu
defined by
\mu(u)=(A^{t})^{-1}AuB^{t}B^{-1}
and
\mu(\mathbbm{D}^{\pm 1})=\mathbbm{D}^{\pm 1}
.

In the study of homological property, a general classical problem is to find homological invariants under various kind of “deformations”. Motivated by the discussion of the twisted CY property of the Hopf algebras
\mathcal{B}(A)
, in
[
14
]
, the authors asked the following question:

Question 2
.

Let
H
and
L
be two Hopf algebras such that their comodule categories are monoidally equivalent. Suppose
H
is twisted CY. Is
L
always twisted CY?

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N},n,m\geqslant 2
and let
A,B\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbbm{k}),C,D\in\operatorname{GL}_{m}(\mathbbm{k})
such that
B^{t}A^{t}BA=\lambda I_{n}
,
D^{t}C^{t}DC=\lambda I_{m}
and
\operatorname{tr}(AB^{t})=\operatorname{tr}(CD^{t})=\mu
for some
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbbm{k}^{\times}
. Then the comodule categories over
\mathcal{G}(A,B)
and
\mathcal{G}(C,D)
are monoidally equivalent
[
9
]
. Theorem
1
shows that the algebras
\mathcal{G}(A,B)
provide an interesting example to study the above question.

As a corollary of Theorem
1
, we prove that Hopf-Galois objects of the algebras
\mathcal{G}(A,B)
are also twisted CY algebras (Theorem
3.2
).

Another application of the free Yetter-Drinfeld resolution we construct concerns Gerstenhaber-Schack cohomology (
[
6
,
7
]
), a cohomology theory specific to Hopf algebras. It has been useful in proving some fundamental results in Hopf algebra theory. Bialgebra cohomology is a special instance of Gerstenhaber-Schack cohomology. When the algebra
\mathcal{G}(A,B)
is cosemisimple, we compute its bialgebra cohomology and we prove that the Gerstenhaber-Schack cohomological dimension of
\mathcal{G}(A,B)
is 4.

1.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, we work over an algebraically closed fixed field
\mathbbm{k}
. Unless stated otherwise all algebras and vector spaces are over
\mathbbm{k}
. The unadorned tensor
\otimes
means

\operatorname{Hom}
means

For an

For a Hopf algebra, we use Sweedler’s (sumless) notation for its comultiplication and its coactions. The category of right
H

. Let
H
be a Hopf algebra, and
\xi:H\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}

It is easy to check that both left and right winding automorphisms are algebra automorphisms of

[
4
, §2.5]
).

(ii)

For a detailed introduction about cogroupoids, we refer to

In

[

introduced the notion of a cogroupoid to provide a categorial context for Hopf-(bi)Galois objects.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
2

Let

be a connected cogroupoid. Then for any

, we have equivalences of monoidal categories that are inverse of each other

\begin{array}[]{rclcrcl}\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{C}(X,X)}&\cong^{\otimes}&\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{C}(Y,Y)}&&\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{C}(Y,Y)}&\cong^{\otimes}&\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{C}(X,X)}\\

and

H=\mathcal{C}(X,X)
and
L=\mathcal{C}(Y,Y)
.

1.2.
Calabi-Yau algebras

In this subsection, we recall the definition of (twisted) Calabi-Yau algebras.

Definition 1.4
.

An algebra
A
is called a
twisted Calabi-Yau algebra of dimension

d
if

(i)

A
is
homologically smooth
, that is, as an
A^{e}
-module,
A
has
a finitely generated projective resolution of finite length;

(ii)

There is an automorphism
\mu
of
A
such that

(2)

\operatorname{Ext}_{A^{e}}^{i}(A,A^{e})\cong\begin{cases}0,&i\neq d;\\
A^{\mu},&i=d,\end{cases}

as
A^{e}
-modules.

If such an automorphism
\mu
exists, it is unique up to an inner automorphism and is called a
Nakayama automorphism
of
A
. When a Nakayama automorphism of a twisted Calabi-Yau algebra is an inner automorphism, it is called a
Calabi-Yau algebra
. In what follows, Calabi-Yau is abbreviated to CY for short.

As mentioned in the introduction, twisted CY algebras are closely related to Artin-Schelter (AS) regular algebras.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra, and let
\mathbbm{k}
also denote the trivial
H
-module.

(i)

The Hopf algebra
H
is said to be
left
AS-Gorenstein
, if

(a)

\operatorname{injdim}{{}_{H}H}=d<\infty
,

(b)

\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Ext}_{H}^{i}({{}_{H}\mathbbm{k}},{{}_{H}H})=\begin{cases}0,&i\neq d;\\
1&i=d.\end{cases}

A
right AS-Gorenstein
Hopf algebra can be defined similarly.

(ii)

If
H
is both left and right AS-Gorenstein, then
H
is called
AS-Gorenstein
.

(iii)

If, in addition, the global dimension of
H
is finite, then
H
is called
AS-regular
.

Compare with
[
4
, Definition 1.2]
, we do not require the Hopf algebra
H
to be Noetherian. For AS-regularity, the right global dimension always equals the left global dimension. When
H
is AS-Gorenstein and homologically smooth, the right injective dimension always equals the left injective dimension, which are both given by the integer
d
such that
\operatorname{Ext}_{H^{e}}^{d}(H,H^{e})\neq 0
.
we refer to
[
14
, Remark 2.1.5]
for an explanation.

Let
H
be a left AS-Gorenstein Hopf algebra of injective dimension
d
. The one dimensional right
H
-module
\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{H}({}_{H}\mathbbm{k},{{}_{H}H})
is usually denoted by
\int^{l}_{H}
, and is called the
left homological integral
of
H
. Let
x
be a nonzero element in
\int^{l}_{H}
, the right
H
-action defines an algebra homomorphism
\xi:H\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}
by
x\cdot h=\xi(h)x
, for any
h\in H
. That is,
\int^{l}_{H}\cong\mathbbm{k}_{\xi}
as right
H
-modules.

If
H
is a right AS-Gorenstein Hopf algebra of injective dimension
d
, one may define the
right homological integral

\int^{r}_{H}=\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{H}(\mathbbm{k}_{H},{H_{H}})
similarly.
There is an algebra homomorphism
\eta:H\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}
, such that
\int^{r}_{H}\cong{{}_{\eta}\mathbbm{k}}
as left
H
-modules.

The following lemma summarizes the relationship between twisted CY Hopf algebras and AS-Gorenstein Hopf algebras (not necessarily Noetherian). They are probably well-known. We list them here for the sake of readers’ convenience.

Lemma 1.6
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra with bijective antipode. Then the followings are equivalent,

(i)

H
is twisted CY;

(ii)

H
is left AS-Gorenstein and the left trivial module
\mathbbm{k}
admits a bounded projective resolution with each term finitely generated;

(iii)

H
is right AS-Gorenstein and the right trivial module
\mathbbm{k}
admits a bounded projective resolution with each term finitely generated.

Now suppose
H
is twisted CY.

(iv)

Let
\mu
be a Nakayama automorphism of
H
. We have
\int^{l}_{H}\cong\mathbbm{k}_{\xi}
as right
H
-modules and
\int^{r}_{H}\cong{}_{\eta}\mathbbm{k}
as left
H
-modules, where
\xi
and
\eta
are algebra homomorphism defined by
\xi=\varepsilon\mu
and
\eta=\varepsilon\mu^{-1}
, respectively.

(v)

If we know that
\int^{l}_{H}\cong\mathbbm{k}_{\xi}
as right
H
-modules, where
\xi:H\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}
is an algebra homomorphism, a Nakayama automorphism of
H
is given by
\mu=S^{2}[\xi]^{l}
. Alternatively, the algebra automorphism
S^{-2}[\eta S]^{r}
is also a Nakayama automorphism
H
, where
\eta:H\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}
is the algebra homomorphism such that
\int^{r}_{H}\cong{{}_{\eta}\mathbbm{k}}
as left
H
-modules.

Proof.

The equivalence of (i), (ii) and (iii) is
[
14
, Corollary 2.1.7]
. Therefore, if
H
is a twisted CY algebra, then
H
is both left and right AS-Gorenstein, that is,
H
is AS-Gorenstein.

(iv) follows from Lemma 2.15 and Remark 2.16 in
[
17
]
.

(v) Let
\xi
be the algebra homomorphism such that
\int^{l}_{H}\cong\mathbbm{k}_{\xi}
as right
H
-modules. From the proof Proposition 2.1.6 in
[
14
]
, we can see that

\operatorname{Ext}_{H^{e}}^{i}(H,H^{e})\cong\begin{cases}0,&i\neq d;\\
H^{\mu},&i=d,\end{cases}

where the automorphism
\mu
is defined by
\mu(h)=\xi(h_{(1)})S^{2}(h_{(2)})
. That is,
S^{2}[\xi]^{l}
is a Nakayama automorphism of
H
. Similarly, let
\eta:H\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}
be the algebra homomorphism such that
\int^{r}_{H}\cong{{}_{\eta}\mathbbm{k}}
as left
H
-modules, we have that

\operatorname{Ext}_{H^{e}}^{i}(H,H^{e})\cong\begin{cases}0,&i\neq d;\\
{}^{{}^{{S^{2}[\eta]^{r}}}}H,&i=d.\end{cases}

Therefore,
({S^{2}[\eta]^{r}})^{-1}={S^{-2}[\eta S]^{r}}
is also a Nakayama automorphism of
H
. ∎

1.3.
Yetter-Drinfeld modules

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra. A

(right-right) Yetter-Drinfeld module

V
over
H
is simultaneously a
right
H
-module and a right
H
-comodule satisfying the compatibility
condition

\delta(v\cdot h)=v_{(0)}\cdot h_{(2)}\otimes S(h_{(1)})v_{(1)}h_{(3)},

for any
v\in V
,
h\in H
. The category of Yetter-Drinfeld modules is usually denoted by
\mathcal{YD}^{H}_{H}
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra, and
V
a right
H
-comodule. The Yetter-Drinfeld module
V\boxtimes H
is defined by Bichon in
[
1
, Proposition 3.1]
as follows. The right
H
-module structure is defined
by multiplication on the right side, and the right comodule structure is defined by the linear map

\begin{array}[]{rcl}V\otimes H&\rightarrow&V\otimes H\otimes H\\
v\otimes h&\mapsto&v_{(0)}\otimes h_{(2)}\otimes S(h_{(1)})v_{(1)}h_{(3)}.\end{array}

A Yetter-Drinfeld module over
H
is said to be
free
if it is isomorphic to
V\boxtimes H
for some right
H
-comodule
V
.

A free Yetter-Drinfeld module is obviously free as a right
H
-module. We call a free Yetter-Drinfeld module
V\boxtimes H

finitely generated
if
V
is finite dimensional.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra and let
M\in\mathcal{YD}^{H}_{H}
. A
free Yetter-Drinfeld module resolution
of
M
is a complex of free Yetter-Drinfeld modules

\textbf{P}_{\bullet}:\cdots\rightarrow P_{i+1}\rightarrow P_{i}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow P_{1}\rightarrow P_{0}\rightarrow 0

together with a Yetter-Drinfeld module morphism
\varepsilon:P_{0}\rightarrow M
such that the complex

\cdots\rightarrow P_{i+1}\rightarrow P_{i}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow P_{1}\rightarrow P_{0}\xrightarrow{\epsilon}M\rightarrow 0

is an exact sequence.

The monoidal equivalence in Theorem
1.3
can be extended to categories of Yetter-Drinfeld modules.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a cogroupoid,
X,Y\in\operatorname{ob}(\mathcal{C})
and
V
a right
\mathcal{C}(X,X)
-module. Following from
[
2
, Proposition 6.2]
, we know that

V\otimes\mathcal{C}(X,Y)
has a right
\mathcal{C}(Y,Y)
-module structure defined by

(v\otimes a^{X,Y})\leftarrow b^{Y,Y}=v\cdot b_{(2)}^{X,X}\otimes S_{Y,X}(b_{(1)}^{Y,X})a^{X,Y}b_{(3)}^{X,Y}.

Together with the right
\mathcal{C}(Y,Y)
-comodule structure defined by
1\otimes\Delta_{X,Y}^{Y}
,
V\otimes\mathcal{C}(X,Y)
is a Yetter-Drinfeld module over
\mathcal{C}(Y,Y)
.
If moreover
V
is a Yetter-Drinfeld module, then
V\square_{\mathcal{C}(X,X)}\mathcal{C}(X,Y)
is a Yetter-Drinfeld submodule of
V\otimes\mathcal{C}(X,Y)
.

Theorem 1.8
.

[
1
, Theorem 4.4]

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a connected cogroupoid. Then for any
X,Y\in\operatorname{ob}(\mathcal{C})
, the functor

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{YD}^{\mathcal{C}(X,X)}_{\mathcal{C}(X,X)}&\longrightarrow&\mathcal{YD}^{\mathcal{C}(Y,Y)}_{\mathcal{C}(Y,Y)}\\
V&\longmapsto&V\square_{\mathcal{C}(X,X)}\mathcal{C}(X,Y)\end{array}

is an equivalence of monoidal categories. Moreover, we have natural isomorphisms

\begin{array}[]{rcl}(V\square_{\mathcal{C}(X,X)}\mathcal{C}(X,Y))\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(Y,Y)&\longrightarrow&(V\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(X,X))\square_{\mathcal{C}(X,X)}\mathcal{C}(X,Y)\\
v\otimes a^{XY}\otimes b^{YY}&\longmapsto&v\otimes b_{(2)}^{XX}\otimes S_{Y,X}(b_{(1)}^{YX})a^{XY}b_{(3)}^{XY}.\end{array}

for any
v\in V,a^{XY}\in\mathcal{C}(X,Y),b^{YY}\in\mathcal{C}(Y,Y)
.

2.
A resolution of the trivial Yetter-Drinfeld module

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
, and

4.
Gerstenhaber-Schack cohomology

Gerstenhaber-Schack cohomology of a Hopf algebra with coefficients in Hopf bimodules was introduced in
[
6
,
7
]
by using an explicit bicomplex. Since the category of Hopf bimodules and the category of Yetter-Drinfeld modules are equivalent, we can work with Yetter-Drinfeld modules. Let
H
be a Hopf and
V
a Yetter-Drinfeld module over
A
. The Gerstenhaber-Schack cohomology of
A
with coefficients in
V
is usually denoted by
\operatorname{H}^{*}_{GS}(H,V)
. A special instance of Gerstenhaber-Schack cohomology is bialgbra cohomology, given by
\operatorname{H}_{b}^{*}(H)=\operatorname{H}^{*}_{GS}(H,\mathbbm{k})
.

Taillefer proved that Gerstenhaber-Schack cohomology is in fact an
\operatorname{Ext}
-functor (
[
12
,
13
]
)

(13)

\operatorname{H}^{*}_{GS}(H,V)\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{YD}^{H}_{H}}^{*}(\mathbbm{k},V).

This description can be viewed as a defition of Gerstenhaber-Schack cohomology.

The Gerstenhaber-Schack cohomological dimension of a Hopf algebra

is defined to be

\operatorname{cd}_{GS}={\rm sup}\{n:\operatorname{H}^{*}_{GS}(H,V)\neq 0\text{ for some }V\in\mathcal{YD}^{H}_{H}\}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}

.

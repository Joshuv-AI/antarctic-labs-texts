Endpoint estimates for harmonic analysis operators associated with Laguerre polynomial expansions
Jorge J. Betancor 1, Estefanía Dalmasso 2,∗, Pablo Quijano 2,†, Roberto Scotto 3

Abstract
. 1.
Introduction and main results

We will consider harmonic analysis operators associated with Laguerre polynomial expansions and establish some endpoint estimates for them. More precisely, we shall prove their boundedness from
H^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
to
L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
and from
L^{\infty}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
to
\textup{BMO}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
, where
d\gamma_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{2}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}x^{2\alpha+1}e^{-x^{2}}dx
on
(0,\infty)
, for
\alpha>-\frac{1}{2}
.

We first recall the definitions and the main properties of Hardy and BMO spaces in our setting. We introduce the function
m(x)=\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{x}\right\}
,
x\in(0,\infty)
. Given
a>0
, an interval
(x-r,x+r)
, with
0<r\leq x
, is said to be in
\mathcal{B}_{a}
when
0<r\leq am(x)
. We denote
I(x,r):=(x-r,x+r)\cap(0,\infty)
, for every
x,r\in(0,\infty)
. Clearly, the measure
\gamma_{\alpha}
is not doubling but it has the doubling property on the intervals of each family
\mathcal{B}_{a}
, that is, there exists
C_{a}>0
for which

\gamma_{\alpha}(I(x,2r))\leq C_{a}\gamma_{\alpha}(I(x,r)),

provided that
I(x,r)\in\mathcal{B}_{a}
.

Let
1<q\leq\infty
. We say that a measurable function
b
defined on
(0,\infty)
is an
(a,q,\alpha)
-atom when
b(x)=1
for every
x\in(0,\infty)
, or there exists
0<r_{0}\leq x_{0}
such that
I(x_{0},r_{0})\in\mathcal{B}_{a}
and the following properties are satisfied:

(i)

\supp(b)\subset I(x_{0},r_{0})
;

(ii)

\|b\|_{L^{q}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})}\leq\gamma_{\alpha}(I(x_{0},r_{0}))^{\frac{1}{q}-1}
, being
\frac{1}{q}=0
when
q=\infty
;

(iii)

\int_{0}^{\infty}b(y)d\gamma_{\alpha}(y)=0
.

A function
f\in L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
is said to be in
H^{1,q}_{a}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
when
f=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}b_{j}
where, for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
b_{j}
is an
(a,q,\alpha)
-atom and
\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
being
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|<\infty.
If
f\in H^{1,q}_{a}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
we define

\|f\|_{H^{1,q}_{a}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})}=\inf\,\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|.

Here the infimum is taken over all the sequences
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of complex numbers such that
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|<\infty
and
f=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}b_{j}
, where
b_{j}
is an
(a,q,\alpha)
-atom, for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
. The space
H^{1,q}_{a}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
is endowed with the topology associated with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H^{1,q}_{a}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})}
. Thus,
H^{1,q}_{a}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
is a Banach space.

These Hardy spaces
H^{1,q}_{a}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
were introduced and studied in
[
4
]
, where the authors gave a characterization of
H^{1,q}_{a}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
by using a local maximal function. The space
H^{1,q}_{a}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
actually does not depend on
a
and
q
(
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
). To simplify, we write
H^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
instead of
H^{1,q}_{a}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
,
a>0
and
1<q\leq\infty
.

We shall now define our
BMO
space. Let
a>0
. We say that a function
{f\in L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})}
is in
\textup{BMO}_{a}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
when

\|f\|_{*,a,\alpha}:=\sup_{I\in\mathcal{B}_{a}}\frac{1}{\gamma_{\alpha}(I)}\int_{I}|f(y)-f_{I}|d\gamma_{\alpha}(y)<\infty,

where
f_{I}:=\frac{1}{\gamma_{\alpha}(I)}\int_{I}f(y)d\gamma_{\alpha}(y)
, for each
I\in\mathcal{B}_{a}
. We also define, for every
{f\in\textup{BMO}_{a}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})}
,

\|f\|_{\textup{BMO}_{a}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})}=\|f\|_{L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})}+\|f\|_{*,a,\alpha}.

The space
\left(\textup{BMO}_{a}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha}),\|\cdot\|_{\textup{BMO}_{a}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})}\right)
is a Banach space when the functions differing by a constant are identified.

As it happens with the Hardy space, the
\textup{BMO}_{a}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
space does not depend on the parameter
a
. Hence, we may write
\textup{BMO}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
instead of
\textup{BMO}_{a}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
, and
\|\cdot\|_{*,\alpha}
instead of
\|\cdot\|_{*,a,\alpha}
.

We now state the main properties of the space
\textup{BMO}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
that can be proved as the corresponding properties for the
BMO
space associated with the Gaussian measure (see
[
18
]
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha>-\frac{1}{2}
.

(a)

The dual space of
H^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
can be identified with
\textup{BMO}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
.

(b)

(John-Nirenberg type inequality) There exist
c,C>0
such that, for every
f\in\textup{BMO}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
and every
I\in\mathcal{B}_{1}
,

\gamma_{\alpha}\left(\{x\in I:\,|f(x)-f_{I}|>\lambda\}\right)\leq C\exp\left(-\frac{c\lambda}{\|f\|_{*,\alpha}}\right)\gamma_{\alpha}(I).

The key result for the aforementioned endpoint estimates for the harmonic analysis operators associated with Laguerre expansions will be a boundedness criterion, in the spirit of a similar result given in
[
18
]
on the Gaussian setting.

We shall be dealing with the following two classes of operators
T
, namely:

(I)

T
is a linear operator defined on
L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
into the space of measurable functions on
(0,\infty)
such that, for a certain measurable function
K:((0,\infty)\times(0,\infty))\setminus D\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, where
D=\{(x,x):x\in(0,\infty)\}
,

T(f)(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}K(x,y)f(y)d\gamma_{\alpha}(y),\quad\,x\in(0,\infty)\setminus\supp(f),

with
f\in L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
.

(II)

For every
t>0
,
T_{t}
is a linear operator defined on
L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
into the space of measurable functions such that, for a certain measurable function
K_{t}:(0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
,

T_{t}(f)(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}K_{t}(x,y)f(y)d\gamma_{\alpha}(y),\quad\,x\in(0,\infty),

where
f\in L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
. Suppose now that
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
is a Banach space of measurable complex functions defined on
(0,\infty)
, that the function

\displaystyle((0,\infty)\times(0,\infty))\setminus D

\displaystyle\rightarrow X,

\displaystyle(x,y)

\displaystyle\mapsto K_{(\cdot)}(x,y),

is
X
-strongly measurable and that, for every
f\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
, the integral
\int_{0}^{\infty}\|K_{(\cdot)}(x,y)\|_{X}f(y)d\gamma_{\alpha}(y)<\infty
, for every
x\in(0,\infty)\setminus\supp(f)
. The operator
T
is defined by

T(f)=\|T_{t}(f)\|_{X},\quad\,f\in L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha}).

Note that (II)-type operators are special cases of the so-called almost linear operators.

The boundedness criterion can be now stated.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\alpha>-\frac{1}{2}
. Suppose
T
is an operator as above, that is,
T
is of (I)-type or (II)-type.

(a)

Assume that
T
is bounded on
L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
and

\sup_{I\in\mathcal{B}_{1}}\sup_{x,z\in I}\int_{(2I)^{c}}\|K_{t}(x,y)-K_{t}(z,y)\|_{X}d\gamma_{\alpha}(y)<\infty,

(1.1)

where
X=\mathbb{C}
when the operator
T
is of (I)-type. Then,
T
is bounded from
L^{\infty}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
into
\textup{BMO}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
.

(b)

Assume that
T
is bounded on
L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
and it can be extended from
L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
to
L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
as a bounded operator from
L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
into
L^{1,\infty}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
. Also, suppose that

\sup_{I\in\mathcal{B}_{1}}\sup_{y,z\in I}\int_{(2I)^{c}}\|K_{t}(x,y)-K_{t}(x,z)\|_{X}d\gamma_{\alpha}(x)<\infty,

(1.2)

where, as above,
X=\mathbb{C}
when
T
is of (I)-type. Then,
T
can be extended from
H^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})\cap L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
to
H^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
as a
bounded operator from
H^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
into
L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
.

Remark 1.3
.

If the kernel
K_{t}
has certain regularity, conditions (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) can be deduced from

\sup_{I\in\mathcal{B}_{1}}r_{I}\sup_{x\in I}\int_{(2I)^{c}}\|\partial_{x}K_{t}(x,y)\|_{X}d\gamma_{\alpha}(y)<\infty,

(1.3)

and

\sup_{I\in\mathcal{B}_{1}}r_{I}\sup_{y\in I}\int_{(2I)^{c}}\|\partial_{y}K_{t}(x,y)\|_{X}d\gamma_{\alpha}(x)<\infty,

(1.4)

respectively, being
r_{I}
the radius of the interval
I
.

We now present the setting and the operators we shall be dealing with. Let
\alpha>-\frac{1}{2}
. For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the Laguerre polynomial
L_{k}^{\alpha}
of order
\alpha
and degree
k
is defined as in (
[
17
]
)

L_{k}^{\alpha}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha+k+1)k!}}e^{x}x^{-\alpha}\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}\left(e^{-x}x^{\alpha+1}\right),\quad x\in(0,\infty).

We consider the Laguerre operator
\widetilde{\Delta}_{\alpha}
given by

\widetilde{\Delta}_{\alpha}(f)(x)=-\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+\left(\frac{2\alpha+1}{x}-2x\right)\frac{d}{dx}\right)f(x),\quad\,f\in C^{2}(0,\infty).

By defining, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\alpha}(x)=L_{k}^{\alpha}(x^{2})
,
x\in(0,\infty)
, the sequence

\{\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\alpha}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is an orthonormal basis in
L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
. Furthermore,
\widetilde{\Delta}_{\alpha}\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\alpha}=k\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\alpha}
, for any

k\in\mathbb{N}
.

For every
f\in L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
, we set

c_{k}^{\alpha}(f)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\alpha}(x)d\gamma_{\alpha}(x),\quad\,k\in\mathbb{N}.

We consider the operator
\Delta_{\alpha}
given by

\Delta_{\alpha}(f)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}kc_{k}^{\alpha}(f)\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\alpha},\quad\,f\in D(\Delta_{\alpha}),

where
D(\Delta_{\alpha})=\{f\in L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha}):\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|kc_{k}^{\alpha}(f)|^{2}<\infty\}
. We have that
\Delta_{\alpha}(f)=\widetilde{\Delta}_{\alpha}(f)
for every
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,\infty)
, the space of smooth functions with compact support in
(0,\infty)
. The operator
\Delta_{\alpha}
is a self-adjoint and positive operator. Moreover, the operator
-\Delta_{\alpha}
generates a semigroup of operators
\{W_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t>0}
in
L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
where, for every
t>0
,

W_{t}^{\alpha}(f)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{-kt}c_{k}^{\alpha}(f)\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\alpha},\quad f\in L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha}).

By using the Hille-Hardy formula (
[
17
, (4.17.6)]
)
we can write, for every
x,y,t>0

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{-kt}\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\alpha}(x)\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\alpha}(y)=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{1-e^{-t}}\left(e^{-t/2}xy\right)^{-\alpha}I_{\alpha}\left(\frac{2e^{-t/2}xy}{1-e^{-t}}\right)e^{-\frac{e^{-t}(x^{2}+y^{2})}{1-e^{-t}}},

(1.5)

where
I_{\alpha}
denotes the modified Bessel function of the first kind and order
\alpha
.

We get, for every
f\in L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
and
t>0
,

W_{t}^{\alpha}(f)(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}W_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)f(y)d\gamma_{\alpha}(y),\quad\,x\in(0,\infty),

(1.6)

being
W_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)
the right-hand side of (
1.5
), for
x,y,t\in(0,\infty)
.

The integral in (
1.6
) is absolutely convergent for every
f\in L^{p}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
, with
1\leq p<\infty
. Furthermore, by defining, for every
t>0
,
W_{t}^{\alpha}
on
L^{p}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
,

1\leq p<\infty
, by (
1.6
)
\{W_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t>0}
turns out to be a symmetric diffusion semigroup in the sense of Stein (
[
30
]
).

We define the subordinated Poisson semigroup
\{P_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t>0}
associated with the Laguerre operator
\Delta_{\alpha}
by

P_{t}^{\alpha}(f)=\frac{t}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t^{2}/(4u)}W_{u}^{\alpha}(f)\frac{du}{u^{3/2}},\quad\,t>0.

(1.7)

Thus,
\{P_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t>0}
is a symmetric diffusion semigroup in the sense of Stein (
[
30
]
) as well.

Given
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the maximal operator
P_{*,k}^{\alpha}
is given by

P_{*,k}^{\alpha}(f)(x)=\sup_{t>0}\left|t^{k}\partial^{k}_{t}P_{t}^{\alpha}(f)(x)\right|,\quad x\in(0,\infty).

According to
[
30
, p. 73]
we have that
P_{*,0}^{\alpha}
is bounded on
L^{p}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
, for every
1<p<\infty
. The maximal operator
W_{*}^{\alpha}
associated with the heat semigroup
\{W_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t>0}
was studied by Muckenhoupt (
[
22
]
) (see also
[
7
]
). From Muckenhoupt’s results it follows that
P_{*,0}^{\alpha}
is bounded from
L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
into
L^{1,\infty}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
. The Laguerre semigroup
\{W_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t>0}
can be extended to complex values of the parameter
t
. The corresponding maximal operator was studied in
[
29
]
. From
[
15
, Remark 4.3]
the operator
P_{*,k}^{\alpha}
is bounded on
L^{p}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
, for every
1<p<\infty
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. By using
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
we deduce that
P_{*,k}^{\alpha}
is also bounded from
L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
into
L^{1,\infty}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

The Littlewood-Paley functions associated with the semigroup
\{P_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t>0}
can be defined as follows. For every
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
with
n+k\geq 1
, we consider the square function
g_{n,k}^{\alpha}
given by

g_{n,k}^{\alpha}(f)(x)=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|t^{n+k}\partial_{x}^{n}\partial_{t}^{k}P_{t}^{\alpha}(f)(x)\right|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/2},\quad\,x\in(0,\infty).

According to
[
30
, Chapter 4, Section 6, Corollary 1]
, for every
1<p<\infty
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\geq 1
,
g_{0,k}^{\alpha}
is bounded on
L^{p}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
. Moreover, in
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
it was proved that
g_{0,k}^{\alpha}
is bounded from
L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
into
L^{1,\infty}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\geq 1
. The authors proved in
[
3
, Theorem 1.1, (c)]
that
g_{n,k}^{\alpha}
is bounded on
L^{p}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
, for every
1<p<\infty
and
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
with
n+k\geq 1
. It is worth mentioning that Nowak (
[
23
, Theorems 6 and 7]
) established
L^{p}
-boundedness properties for Littlewood-Paley functions involving spatial derivatives (
n>0
) in other Laguerre contexts.

We now introduce the multipliers of Laplace transform type. A measurable function
M
is said to be of Laplace transform type when

M(x)=x\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-xy}\phi(y)dy,\quad\,x\in(0,\infty),

where
\phi\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty)
. Note that
M\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty)
provided that
M
is of Laplace transform type. We define the multiplier
T_{M}^{\alpha}
by

T_{M}^{\alpha}(f)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}M\left(\sqrt{k}\right)c_{k}^{\alpha}(f)\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\alpha},\quad\,f\in L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha}).

Since
M\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty)
,
T_{M}^{\alpha}
is bounded on
L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
. By
[
30
, Corollary 3, p. 121]

T_{M}^{\alpha}
can be extended from
L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})\cap L^{p}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
to
L^{p}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
as a bounded operator on
L^{p}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
, for every
1<p<\infty
. When
\beta>0
and
\phi(y)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-2\beta i)}y^{-2\beta i}
, for
y\in(0,\infty)
, then
T_{M}^{\alpha}=\Delta_{\alpha}^{\beta i}
.

Let
\omega>0
. We define the negative power
\Delta_{\alpha}^{-\omega}
of
\Delta_{\alpha}
by

\Delta_{\alpha}^{-\omega}(f)(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{-\omega}c_{k}^{\alpha}(f)\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\alpha},\quad\,f\in L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha}).

Thus,
\Delta_{\alpha}^{-\omega}
is bounded on
L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
. Note that the function
M(x)=x^{-2\omega}
, for
x\in(0,\infty)
, is not of Laplace transform type, so
\Delta_{\alpha}^{-\omega}
does not fall in the scope of multipliers of Laplace transform type. The operator
\Delta^{-\omega}_{\alpha}
can be extended from
L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})\cap L^{p}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
to
L^{p}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
as a bounded operator on
L^{p}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
, for every
1<p<\infty
(see
[
20
, Lemma 2.2]
for the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck operator case). However,
\Delta_{\alpha}^{-\omega}
is not bounded from
L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
into
L^{1,\infty}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
(see
[
10
, Proposition 6.2]
for the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck operator case).

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq 1
. We define the
n
-th order Riesz transform
R_{\alpha}^{n}
associated with
\Delta_{\alpha}
as follows. For every
f\in L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
,

R_{\alpha}^{n}(f)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{n/2}}c_{k}^{\alpha}(f)\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\alpha}.

By
[
24
]
,
R_{\alpha}^{n}
is bounded on
L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
. For every
f\in L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
we have that

R_{\alpha}^{n}(f)(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}K_{\alpha}^{n}(x,y)f(y)d\gamma_{\alpha}(y),\quad\text{a.e. }x\in(0,\infty)\setminus\supp(f),

where

K_{\alpha}^{n}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(n/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1+n/2}W_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)dt,\quad x,y\in(0,\infty),x\neq y.

By
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
,
[
26
, Theorem 1.1]
, and
[
23
, Theorem 13]
we deduce that each
R_{\alpha}^{n}
can be extended
from
L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})\cap L^{p}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
to
L^{p}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
as a
bounded operator on
L^{p}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
, for every
1<p<\infty
.
Furthermore,
R_{\alpha}^{1}
can be extended from
L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})\cap L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
to
L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})

as a bounded operator from
L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
into
L^{1,\infty}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
(see
[
9
]
).

Finally, we present variation operators. Let
\rho>2
. If
F
is a complex valued and measurable function defined on
(0,\infty)
, we define the
\rho
-variation
V_{\rho}(\{F(t)\}_{t>0})
as follows

V_{\rho}(\{F(t)\}_{t>0})=\sup_{0<t_{N}<t_{N-1}<...<t_{1},\;N\in\mathbb{N},\;N\geq 1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}|F(t_{j})-F(t_{j+1})|^{\rho}\right)^{1/\rho}.

According to
[
16
, Corollary 6.1]
(see also
[
14
, Theorem 3.3]
), the
\rho
-variation operator
V_{\rho}(\{t^{k}\partial_{t}^{k}P_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t>0})
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, is bounded on
L^{p}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
,
{1<p<\infty}
. Later, in
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
it was proved that
V_{\rho}(\{t^{k}\partial_{t}^{k}P_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t>0})
is bounded from
L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
into

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In order to prove
(a)
we can proceed as in the proof of
[
18
, Theorem 6.1, (i)]
. We sketch it here. Suppose that
T
is a (II)-type operator associated with the Banach space
X
. When
T
is an (I)-type operator the proof is similar.

Let
f\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
and
I\in\mathcal{B}_{1}
. We define
f_{1}=f\chi_{2I}
and
f_{2}=f-f_{1}
. We have that
T_{t}(f)=T_{t}(f_{1})+T_{t}(f_{2})
,
t>0
. Thus, we can write

\displaystyle\int_{I}

\displaystyle\left|T(f)(x)-\|(T_{t}(f))_{I}\|_{X}\right|d\gamma_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I}\|T_{t}(f)(x)-(T_{t}(f))_{I}\|_{X}d\gamma_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I}\|T_{t}(f_{1})(x)-(T_{t}(f_{1}))_{I}\|_{X}d\gamma_{\alpha}(x)+\int_{I}\|T_{t}(f_{2})(x)-(T_{t}(f_{2}))_{I}\|_{X}d\gamma_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{I}\|T_{t}(f_{1})(x)\|_{X}d\gamma_{\alpha}(x)+\int_{I}\|T_{t}(f_{2})(x)-(T_{t}(f_{2}))_{I}\|_{X}d\gamma_{\alpha}(x).

Since
T
is bounded on
L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
and
\gamma_{\alpha}
is doubling on
\mathcal{B}_{1}
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma_{\alpha}(I)}\int_{I}\|T_{t}(f_{1})(x)\|_{X}d\gamma_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{\alpha}(I)}\int_{I}|T(f_{1})(x)|^{2}d\gamma_{\alpha}(x)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{\alpha}(I)}\int_{2I}|f(x)|^{2}d\gamma_{\alpha}(x)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})}.

According to (
1.1
) and the properties of the Bochner integration we obtain for the other term

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma_{\alpha}(I)}

\displaystyle\int_{I}\|T_{t}(f_{2})(x)-(T_{t}(f_{2}))_{I}\|_{X}d\gamma_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\gamma_{\alpha}(I))^{2}}\int_{I}\left\|\int_{I}(T_{t}(f_{2})(x)-T_{t}(f_{2})(z))d\gamma_{\alpha}(z)\right\|_{X}d\gamma_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(\gamma_{\alpha}(I))^{2}}\int_{I}\int_{I}\left\|\int_{(2I)^{c}}(K_{t}(x,y)-K_{t}(z,y))f(y)d\gamma_{\alpha}(y)\right\|_{X}d\gamma_{\alpha}(z)d\gamma_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(\gamma_{\alpha}(I))^{2}}\int_{I}\int_{I}\int_{(2I)^{c}}\|K_{t}(x,y)-K_{t}(z,y)\|_{X}|f(y)|d\gamma_{\alpha}(y)d\gamma_{\alpha}(z)d\gamma_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})}\sup_{x,z\in I}\int_{(2I)^{c}}\|K_{t}(x,y)-K_{t}(z,y)\|_{X}d\gamma_{\alpha}(y)

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})}.

We conclude that
T(f)\in\textup{BMO}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
and

\|T(f)\|_{\textup{BMO}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})}.

Now, we are going to prove
(b)
. In the proof of
[
18
, Theorem 6.1, (ii)]
Mauceri and Meda used the duality between Hardy and BMO spaces. Here we cannot use duality arguments when we consider (II)-type operators. Assume that
T
is a (II)-type operator associated with a Banach space
X
. For (I)-type operators the same proof works.

Suppose that
b
is a
(1,2,\alpha)
–atom. If
b(x)=1
for every
x\in(0,\infty)
, we have that

\|T(b)\|_{L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})}\leq\|T(b)\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})}\leq C\|b\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})}\leq C.

Assume now that
0<r_{I}\leq x_{I}
are such that
I=(x_{I}-r_{I},x_{I}+r_{I})\in\mathcal{B}_{1}
and the following properties hold

(i)

\supp(b)\subset I
;

(ii)

\|b\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})}\leq\gamma_{\alpha}(I)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
;

(iii)

\int_{0}^{\infty}b(x)d\gamma_{\alpha}(x)=0
.

We can write

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|T(b)(x)|d\gamma_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{(2I)\cap(0,\infty)}|T(b)(x)|d\gamma_{\alpha}(x)+\int_{(0,\infty)\setminus(2I)}|T(b)(x)|d\gamma_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=A_{1}+A_{2}.

Since
T
is bounded on
L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
, by using
(ii)
we get

A_{1}\leq\|T(b)\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})}(\gamma_{\alpha}(2I))^{1/2}\leq C\|b\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})}(\gamma_{\alpha}(I))^{1/2}\leq C.

According to
(i)
and
(iii)
, the properties of (II)-type operators lead to

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{(2I)^{c}}\left\|\int_{I}b(y)(K_{t}(x,y)-K_{t}(x,x_{I}))d\gamma_{\alpha}(y)\right\|_{X}d\gamma_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I}|b(y)|\int_{(2I)^{c}}\|K_{t}(x,y)-K_{t}(x,x_{I})\|_{X}d\gamma_{\alpha}(x)d\gamma_{\alpha}(y)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{I}|b(y)|d\gamma_{\alpha}(y)\leq C\|b\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty,\gamma_{\alpha}))}(\gamma_{\alpha}(I))^{1/2}\leq C.

We conclude that

\|T(b)\|_{L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})}\leq C,

where
C>0
does not depend on
b
.

By proceeding as in the end of the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 1.2]
we can finish this one. Indeed, suppose that
f=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}b_{j}
, where, for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
b_{j}
is a
(1,2,\alpha)
–atom and
\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
being
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|<\infty
. The series defining
f
converges in
L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
. Since
T
is bounded from
L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
into
L^{1,\infty}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
we have that

T(f)=\lim_{k\to\infty}T\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k}\lambda_{j}b_{j}\right),\text{ in }L^{1,\infty}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha}).

Then, there exists an increasing function
\psi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that

T(f)(x)=\lim_{k\to\infty}T\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\psi(k)}\lambda_{j}b_{j}\right)(x),\quad\text{a.e. }x\in(0,\infty),

and thus, for almost every
x\in(0,\infty),

|T(f)(x)|=\lim_{k\to\infty}T\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\psi(k)}\lambda_{j}b_{j}\right)(x)\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\psi(k)}|\lambda_{j}|T(b_{j})(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|T(b_{j})(x).

It follows that

\|T(f)\|_{L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|\|T(b_{j})\|_{L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})}\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|.

We conclude that
\|T(f)\|_{L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})}
and the proof is now finished.

3.
Some technical lemmas

In this section we list and prove auxiliary results which are the key ingredients for the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We begin by establishing some estimates involving the integral kernel of the heat semigroup
\{W_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t>0}
. The integral representation of the modified Bessel function of the first kind
I_{\nu}
,
\nu>-\frac{1}{2}
(see
[
17
, (5.10.22)]
), leads to

W_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)=\frac{1}{(1-e^{-t})^{\alpha+1}}\int_{-1}^{1}\exp\left(-\frac{q\left(e^{-t/2}x,y,s\right)}{1-e^{-t}}+y^{2}\right)\Pi_{\alpha}(s)ds,

for
x,y,t\in(0,\infty)
, where
\Pi_{\alpha}(s):=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha+1/2)\sqrt{\pi}}(1-s^{2})^{\alpha-1/2}
for
s\in(-1,1)
and
q(x,y,s):=x^{2}+y^{2}-2xys
for
x,y\in(0,\infty)
and
s\in(-1,1)
.

For every
n\in\mathbb N
, we denote by
H_{n}
the Hermite polynomial of degree
n
given by

H_{n}(x)=(-1)^{n}e^{x^{2}}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\left(e^{-x^{2}}\right),\quad x\in\mathbb R.

(3.1)

Notice that for any
n\in\mathbb N
,
x,y,t\in(0,\infty)
and
s\in(-1,1)
we have that

\displaystyle\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\exp\left(-\frac{q\left(e^{-t/2}x,y,s\right)}{1-e^{-t}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\exp\left(-\left(\frac{e^{-t/2}x-ys}{\sqrt{1-e^{-t}}}\right)^{2}\right)e^{-\frac{1-s^{2}}{1-e^{-t}}y^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{e^{-t/2}}{\sqrt{1-e^{-t}}}\right)^{n}\left.\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}e^{-z^{2}}\right|_{z=\frac{e^{-t/2}x-ys}{\sqrt{1-e^{-t}}}}e^{-\frac{1-s^{2}}{1-e^{-t}}y^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{e^{-t/2}}{\sqrt{1-e^{-t}}}\right)^{n}H_{n}\left(\frac{e^{-t/2}x-ys}{\sqrt{1-e^{-t}}}\right)e^{-\frac{q\left(e^{-t/2}x,y,s\right)}{1-e^{-t}}}.

Then, given
x,y,t\in(0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{n}W_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-e^{-t})^{\alpha+1}}\left(-\frac{e^{-t/2}}{\sqrt{1-e^{-t}}}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{-1}^{1}H_{n}\left(\frac{e^{-t/2}x-ys}{\sqrt{1-e^{-t}}}\right)e^{-\frac{q\left(e^{-t/2}x,y,s\right)}{1-e^{-t}}+y^{2}}\Pi_{\alpha}(s)ds,

and we can also see that

\displaystyle\partial_{y}\partial_{x}W_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{2e^{-t/2}}{(1-e^{-t})^{\alpha+3/2}}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{-\frac{q\left(e^{-t/2}x,y,s\right)}{1-e^{-t}}+y^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\frac{s}{\sqrt{1-e^{-t}}}+\frac{2e^{-t/2}\left(e^{-t/2}y-xs\right)\left(e^{-t/2}x-ys\right)}{(1-e^{-t})^{3/2}}\right)\Pi_{\alpha}(s)ds.

From all of the above and considering, as usual, the change of variables
r=e^{-t/2}
,
t\in(0,\infty)
, we deduce the following result.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\alpha>-\frac{1}{2}
and
n\in\mathbb N
. There exists
C>0
such that for any
x,y\in(0,\infty)
and
r\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{x}^{n}W_{-2\log r}^{\alpha}(x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{n}\int_{-1}^{1}\left(1+\left|\frac{rx-ys}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\right|^{n}\right)\frac{e^{-\frac{q(rx,y,s)}{1-r^{2}}+y^{2}}}{(1-r^{2})^{\alpha+1+\frac{n}{2}}}\Pi_{\alpha}(s)ds,

and

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{y}\partial_{x}W_{-2\log r}^{\alpha}(x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq Cr\int_{-1}^{1}\left(\frac{|s|}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}+\frac{2r|ry-xs||rx-ys|}{(1-r^{2})^{3/2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{e^{-\frac{q(rx,y,s)}{1-r^{2}}+y^{2}}}{(1-r^{2})^{\alpha+\frac{3}{2}}}\Pi_{\alpha}(s)ds.

when

,

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

. Then

\left|\partial_{t}^{\sigma}\left(te^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4u}}\right)\right|\leq Ce^{-\frac{t^{2}}{8u}}u^{\frac{1-\sigma}{2}},\quad t,u\in(0,\infty).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\ell=0,1
. Then, there exists

such that for every

\left|t^{n+k}\partial_{y}^{\ell}\partial_{x}^{n+1-\ell}\partial_{t}^{k}P^{\alpha}_{t}(x,y)\right|\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\left|\partial_{y}^{\ell}\partial_{x}^{n+1-\ell}W_{-2\log r}^{\alpha}(x,y)\right|\frac{t^{n+k}e^{t^{2}/(16\log r)}}{(-\log r)^{\frac{k}{2}+1}}\frac{dr}{r}.

\displaystyle\left|t^{n+k}\partial_{y}^{\ell}\partial_{x}^{n+1-\ell}\partial_{t}^{k}P^{\alpha}_{t}(x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\left|\partial_{y}^{\ell}\partial_{x}^{n+1-\ell}W_{-2\log r}^{\alpha}(x,y)\right|\frac{t^{n+k}e^{t^{2}/(16\log r)}}{(-\log r)^{\frac{k}{2}+1}}\frac{dr}{r}.\qed

The following lemma establishes some properties for the function

, which are all straightforward from its definition.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
x,y\in(0,\infty)
,
r\in(0,1)
and

. Then, the following estimates hold.

\frac{q(rx,y,s)}{1-r^{2}}-y^{2}=\frac{q(x,ry,s)}{1-r^{2}}-x^{2}
;

(E1)

and so

(E2)

(E3)

;

(E5)

q(x,ry,s)\geq x^{2}r^{2}(1-s)

(E6)

q(x,ry,s)\geq y^{2}r^{2}(1-s)

(E7)

q(x,ry,s)\geq(x-rys)^{2}+y^{2}r^{2}(1-s^{2})

(E8)

Lemma 3.6
.

Let

. There exists

such that, for any interval

\sup_{y\in I}r_{I}\int_{(2I)^{c}}K(x,y)d\gamma_{\alpha}(x)\leq C

Proof.

Assume that
I\in\mathcal{B}_{1}
and

. For every

and

, by

we get

\displaystyle\sqrt{q(x,ry,s)}

\displaystyle\geq|x-ry|\geq|x-y|-(1-r)y\geq|x-y|-r_{I}\frac{y}{2(1+y)}

\displaystyle\geq|x-y|-\frac{r_{I}}{2}\geq\frac{|x-y|}{2},

(3.2)

for any

.

It yields

\begin{split}\int_{-1}^{1}&\int_{1-\frac{r_{I}}{2(1+y)}}^{1}e^{-\frac{q(x,ry,s)}{2(1-r^{2})}}\frac{dr}{(1-r^{2})^{\alpha+5/2}}\Pi_{\alpha}(s)ds\\
&\leq C\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{1-\frac{r_{I}}{2(1+y)}}^{1}e^{-\frac{q(x,ry,s)}{4(1-r^{2})}}\frac{e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{16(1-r^{2})}}}{(1-r^{2})^{\alpha+5/2}}dr\Pi_{\alpha}(s)ds\\
&\leq C\int_{1-\frac{r_{I}}{2(1+y)}}^{1}\frac{e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{16(1-r^{2})}}}{(1-r^{2})^{3/2}}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\Pi_{\alpha}(s)}{q(x,ry,s)^{\alpha+1}}dsdr,\end{split}

for
x\notin 2I
. According to
[
25
, Lemma 2.1]
and (
3
) we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{1-\frac{r_{I}}{2(1+y)}}^{1}&\frac{e^{-\frac{q(x,ry,s)}{2(1-r^{2})}}}{(1-r^{2})^{\alpha+5/2}}dr\Pi_{\alpha}(s)ds\\
&\leq C\int_{1-\frac{r_{I}}{2(1+y)}}^{1}\frac{e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{16(1-r^{2})}}}{(1-r^{2})^{3/2}}\frac{dr}{\mathfrak{m}_{\alpha}(I(x,|x-ry|))}\\
&\leq C\int_{1-\frac{r_{I}}{2(1+y)}}^{1}\frac{e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{16(1-r^{2})}}}{(1-r^{2})^{3/2}}\frac{dr}{\mathfrak{m}_{\alpha}(I(x,|x-y|/2))},\end{split}

for
x\notin 2I
. Here,
\mathfrak{m}_{\alpha}
denotes the measure defined by

in
(0,\infty)
.

Also, we can write

\int_{1-\frac{r_{I}}{2(1+y)}}^{1}\frac{e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{16(1-r^{2})}}}{(1-r^{2})^{3/2}}dr\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{u}}}{u^{3/2}}du\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|},

for

. Then,

\begin{split}\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{1-\frac{r_{I}}{2(1+y)}}^{1}&\frac{e^{-\frac{q(x,ry,s)}{2(1-r^{2})}}}{(1-r^{2})^{\alpha+5/2}}dr\Pi_{\alpha}(s)ds\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|\mathfrak{m}_{\alpha}(I(x,|x-y|/2))}\end{split}

for any
x\notin 2I
.

By using
[
25
, Lemma 2.2]
and
(E0)
,

\begin{split}\int_{(2I)^{c}}\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{1-\frac{r_{I}}{2(1+y)}}^{1}&\frac{e^{-\frac{q(x,ry,s)}{2(1-r^{2})}+x^{2}}}{(1-r^{2})^{\alpha+5/2}}dr\Pi_{\alpha}(s)dsd\gamma_{\alpha}(x)\\
&\leq C\int_{(2I)^{c}}\frac{x^{2\alpha+1}}{|x-y|^{2}\left(x+\frac{|x-y|}{2}\right)^{2\alpha+1}}dx\\
&\leq C\int_{(2I)^{c}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2}}dx\\
&\leq C\int_{r_{I}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{z^{2}}dz\\
&\leq\frac{C}{r_{I}}.\end{split}

Here
C>0
does not depend on
I
or
y\in I
.

To deal with the remaining term we consider, for every

, the Hankel translation defined by (see

)

{}_{\alpha}\tau_{x}(f)(y)=\int_{-1}^{1}f\left(\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}-2xys}\right)\Pi_{\alpha}(s)ds,\;\;x,\,y\in(0,\infty),

which is contractive in

for every

and

(

).

We deduce that, for every
y\in I

\begin{split}&\int_{(2I)^{c}}\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{0}^{1-\frac{r_{I}}{2(1+y)}}\frac{e^{-\frac{q(x,ry,s)}{2(1-r^{2})}+x^{2}}}{(1-r^{2})^{\alpha+5/2}}dr\Pi_{\alpha}(s)dsd\gamma_{\alpha}(x)\\

, we get

\displaystyle\left\|t^{n+k}\partial_{y}^{\ell}\partial_{x}^{n+1-\ell}\partial_{t}^{k}P^{\alpha}_{t}(x,y)\right\|_{L^{2}\left((0,\infty),\frac{dt}{t}\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\left|\partial_{y}^{\ell}\partial_{x}^{n+1-\ell}W_{-2\log r}^{\alpha}(x,y)\right|}{r(-\log r)^{\frac{k}{2}+1}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{2(n+k)}e^{t^{2}/(16\log r)}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/2}dr

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\left|\partial_{y}^{\ell}\partial_{x}^{n+1-\ell}W_{-2\log r}^{\alpha}(x,y)\right|}{r(-\log r)^{\frac{k}{2}+1}}(-\log r)^{\frac{n+k}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{n+k}e^{-v}\frac{dv}{v}\right)^{1/2}dr

\displaystyle\qquad=C\int_{0}^{1}\left|\partial_{y}^{\ell}\partial_{x}^{n+1-\ell}W_{-2\log r}^{\alpha}(x,y)\right|(-\log r)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{dr}{r}

whenever
n+k>0
. Thus, (
4.2
) holds.

In order to prove that
g_{n,k}^{\alpha}
is bounded from
L^{\infty}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
to
\textup{BMO}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
, we will see that

\sup_{I\in\mathcal{B}_{1}}r_{I}\sup_{x\in I}\int_{(2I)^{c}}\|\partial_{x}t^{n+k}\partial_{x}^{n}\partial_{t}^{k}P^{\alpha}_{t}(x,y)\|_{L^{2}\left((0,\infty),\frac{dt}{t}\right)}d\gamma_{\alpha}(y)<\infty.

(4.4)

From (
4.2
) with
\ell=0
we know that

\|\partial_{x}t^{n+k}\partial_{x}^{n}\partial_{t}^{k}P^{\alpha}_{t}(x,y)\|_{L^{2}\left((0,\infty),\frac{dt}{t}\right)}\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\left|\partial_{x}^{n+1}W_{-2\log r}^{\alpha}(x,y)\right|(-\log r)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{dr}{r}.

According to Lemma
3.1
and the boundedness of
\xi_{n}
given in Lemma
3.5
, we get

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}t^{n+k}\partial_{x}^{n}\partial_{t}^{k}P^{\alpha}_{t}(x,y)\|_{L^{2}\left((0,\infty),\frac{dt}{t}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r^{n}(-\log r)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}{(1-r^{2})^{\alpha+\frac{n}{2}+\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{-1}^{1}\left(1+\left|\frac{rx-ys}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\right|^{n+1}\right)e^{-\frac{q(rx,y,s)}{1-r^{2}}+y^{2}}\Pi_{\alpha}(s)dsdr

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(1-r^{2})^{\alpha+5/2}}\int_{-1}^{1}\left(1+\left|\frac{rx-ys}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\right|^{n+1}\right)e^{-\frac{q(rx,y,s)}{1-r^{2}}+y^{2}}\Pi_{\alpha}(s)dsdr.

By combining Lemmas
3.6
and
3.10
, (
4.4
) holds.

On the other hand, the endpoint estimate from
H^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
to
L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
for
g_{0,k}^{\alpha}
, follows by taking into account the symmetry of the Poisson kernel and (
4.4
).

4.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.4
for Riesz transforms

According to
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
,
[
26
, Theorem 1.1]
, and
[
23
, Theorem 13]
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N},n\geq 1
,
R_{\alpha}^{n}
can be extended
from
L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})\cap L^{p}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
to
L^{p}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
as a
bounded operator on
L^{p}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
, for every
1<p<\infty
.
Furthermore,
R_{\alpha}^{1}
can be extended from
L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})\cap L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
to
L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})

as a bounded operator from
L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
into
L^{1,\infty}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
(see
[
9
]
).

Let
n\in\mathbb N
,
n\geq 1
. The kernel of the Riesz transform of order
n
with respect to the measure
\gamma_{\alpha}
is given by

K_{\alpha}^{n}(x,y)=\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}r^{n-1}\left(\frac{-\log r}{1-r^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}H_{n}\left(\frac{rx-ys}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\right)\frac{e^{-\frac{q(rx,y,s)}{1-r^{2}}+y^{2}}}{(1-r^{2})^{\alpha+2}}dr\Pi_{\alpha}(s)ds,

for
x,y\in(0,\infty).
Recall that
H_{n}
denotes the Hermite polynomial of order
n
given in (
3.1
).

Then, from
[
17
, p. 62]
, we have that

\displaystyle\partial_{x}K_{\alpha}^{n}(x,y)

\displaystyle=-\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}r^{n}\left(\frac{-\log r}{1-r^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}H_{n+1}\left(\frac{rx-ys}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{e^{-\frac{q(rx,y,s)}{1-r^{2}}+y^{2}}}{(1-r^{2})^{\alpha+5/2}}dr\Pi_{\alpha}(s)ds,

and, therefore, by using the boundedness of
\xi_{n}
given in Lemma
3.5
we get

\left|\partial_{x}K_{\alpha}^{n}(x,y)\right|\leq C\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{-\frac{q(rx,y,s)}{2(1-r^{2})}+y^{2}}}{(1-r^{2})^{\alpha+5/2}}dr\Pi_{\alpha}(s)ds.

According to Lemma
3.6
and
(E0)
,

\sup_{I\in\mathcal{B}_{1}}r_{I}\sup_{x\in I}\int_{(2I)^{c}}\left|\partial_{x}K_{\alpha}^{n}(x,y)\right|d\gamma_{\alpha}(y)<\infty

so
R_{\alpha}^{n}
is bounded from
L^{\infty}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
to
\textup{BMO}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
by virtue of Theorem
1.2
, for any
n\in\mathbb N
with
n\geq 1
.

On the other hand, for
n=1
, by
(E0)
we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{y}K_{\alpha}^{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=e^{x^{2}}\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(1-r^{2})^{\alpha+2}}\left(\frac{-\log r}{1-r^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\partial_{y}\left[2\left(\frac{rx-ys}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\right)e^{-\frac{q(x,ry,s)}{1-r^{2}}}\right]dr\Pi_{\alpha}(s)ds

\displaystyle=-2e^{x^{2}}\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\varphi(r)^{1/2}}{(1-r^{2})^{\alpha+2}}e^{-\frac{q(x,ry,s)}{1-r^{2}}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\frac{s}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}+\frac{2r(rx-ys)(ry-xs)}{(1-r^{2})^{3/2}}\right)dr\Pi_{\alpha}(s)ds.

Hence, by Lemma
3.5
we can estimate

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{y}K_{\alpha}^{1}(x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{-\frac{q(x,ry,s)}{1-r^{2}}+x^{2}}}{(1-r^{2})^{\alpha+5/2}}dr\Pi_{\alpha}(s)ds

\displaystyle\quad+C\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r|rx-ys||ry-xs|}{(1-r^{2})^{\alpha+7/2}}e^{-\frac{q(x,ry,s)}{1-r^{2}}+x^{2}}dr\Pi_{\alpha}(s)ds.

By combining Lemmas
3.6
and
3.9
, we get

\sup_{I\in\mathcal{B}_{1}}r_{I}\sup_{y\in I}\int_{(2I)^{c}}\left|\partial_{y}K_{\alpha}^{n}(x,y)\right|d\gamma_{\alpha}(x)<\infty

so
(b)
holds for
R_{\alpha}^{1}
.

4.4.
Proof of Theorem
1.4
for multipliers of Laplace transform type

Suppose that
M
is of Laplace transform type given by

M(x)=x\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-xy}\phi(y)dy,\quad x\in(0,\infty),

where
\phi\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty)
. We consider the function

K_{\phi}^{\alpha}(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t)\left(-{\partial_{t}}\right)P^{\alpha}_{t}(x,y)dt,\quad x,y\in(0,\infty).

Note here that
K^{\alpha}_{\phi}(x,y)=K^{\alpha}_{\phi}(y,x)
for
x,y\in(0,\infty)
.

We have that, for
x,y\in(0,\infty)
,
x\neq y
,

\partial_{x}K^{\alpha}_{\phi}(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\phi(t)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{t}\left(te^{\frac{t^{2}}{8\log r}}\right)\partial_{x}W^{\alpha}_{-2\log r}(x,y)\frac{dr}{r(-\log r)^{3/2}}dt.

By using Lemma
3.3
with
\ell=n=0
and
k=1
, and computing the inner integral, we get

\begin{split}|\partial_{x}K^{\alpha}_{\phi}(x,y)|&\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\frac{t^{2}}{16\log r}}dt\frac{1}{r(-\log r)^{3/2}}|\partial_{x}W^{\alpha}_{-2\log r}(x,y)|dr\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{x}W^{\alpha}_{-2\log r}(x,y)|\frac{dr}{r(-\log r)},\end{split}

for every
x,y\in(0,\infty)
,
x\neq y.

Continuing as in Section
4.1
we can find
C>0
such that for every
I\in\mathcal{B}_{1}
,

\sup_{x\in I}r_{I}\int_{(2I)^{c}}|\partial_{x}K^{\alpha}_{\phi}(x,y)|d\gamma_{\alpha}(y)\leq C.

Since
T_{M}
is bounded from
L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
into itself, we can obtain the conclusion of Theorem
1.4
, (a), for the multiplier
T_{M}
.

The operator
T_{M}
is self-adjoint in
L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
. Then, a duality argument (see
[
19
, Lemma 7.1]
) allows us to conclude for
T_{M}
the property in Theorem
1.4

(b)
.

4.5.
Proof of Theorem
1.4
for variation operators

The
\rho
-variation operator
V_{\rho}(\{t^{k}\partial_{t}^{k}P_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t>0})
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, is bounded on
L^{p}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
,
{1<p<\infty}
(
[
16
, Corollary 6.1]
and
[
14
, Theorem 3.3]
) and from
L^{1}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
into
L^{1,\infty}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
(
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
).

Assume
g\in C^{1}(0,\infty)
. If
0<t_{N}<t_{N-1}<\dots<t_{1}
we have that

\begin{split}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}|g(t_{j})-g(t_{j+1})|^{\rho}\right)^{1/\rho}&\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}|g(t_{j})-g(t_{j+1})|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left|\int_{t_{j+1}}^{t_{j}}g^{\prime}(t)dt\right|\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|g^{\prime}(t)|dt.\end{split}

Then,

V_{\rho}\left(\{g(t)\}_{t>0}\right)\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|g^{\prime}(t)|dt.

Hence, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
we get

\begin{split}V_{\rho}\left(\{\partial_{x}t^{k}\partial_{t}^{k}P^{\alpha}_{t}(x,y)\}_{t>0}\right)&\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|\partial_{t}(\partial_{x}t^{k}\partial_{t}^{k}P^{\alpha}_{t}(x,y))|dt\\
&\leq k\int_{0}^{\infty}|t^{k-1}\partial_{x}\partial_{t}^{k}P^{\alpha}_{t}(x,y)|dt\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{\infty}|t^{k}\partial_{x}\partial_{t}^{k+1}P^{\alpha}_{t}(x,y)|dt.\end{split}

According to Lemma
3.3
and proceeding as in the previous section, we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}|t^{k}\partial_{x}\partial_{t}^{k+1}P^{\alpha}_{t}(x,y)|dt&\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{\frac{t^{2}}{16\log r}}}{r(-\log r)^{(k+3)/2}}|\partial_{x}W^{\alpha}_{-2\log r}(x,y)|drdt\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{x}W^{\alpha}_{-2\log r}(x,y)|\frac{dr}{r(-\log r)},\end{split}

for every
x,y\in(0,\infty)
,
x\neq y.

The other term can be controlled by proceeding in a similar way.

Therefore, as in the last section, we obtain the conclusion of Theorem
1.4
for the
\rho
-variation operator
V_{\rho}\left(\{\partial_{x}t^{k}\partial_{t}^{k}P^{\alpha}_{t}(x,y)\}_{t>0}\right)
.

4.6.
Proof of Theorem
1.4
for fractional integrals

Let
\omega>0
. Recall that the integral kernel of the operator
\Delta_{\alpha}^{-\omega}
is given by

\displaystyle H^{\omega}_{\alpha}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\omega)}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{-1}^{1}e^{-\frac{q(rx,y,s)}{1-r^{2}}+y^{2}}\Pi_{\alpha}(s)ds\frac{1}{(1-r^{2})^{\alpha+1}}-1\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{dr}{r(-\log r)^{1-\omega}},

for
x,y\in(0,\infty)
,
x\neq y
. Notice that
H^{\omega}_{\alpha}(x,y)=H^{\omega}_{\alpha}(y,x)
.

We can write, for
x,y\in(0,\infty)
,
x\neq y
,

\partial_{x}H^{\omega}_{\alpha}(x,y)=\frac{2}{\Gamma(\omega)}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{-\frac{q(rx,y,s)}{1-r^{2}}+y^{2}}\frac{rx-ys}{(1-r^{2})^{\alpha+2}}\frac{\Pi_{\alpha}(s)dsdr}{(-\log r)^{1-\omega}}

Then, according to
(E8)
we get

|\partial_{x}H^{\omega}_{\alpha}(x,y)|\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{-\frac{q(rx,y,s)}{1-r^{2}}+y^{2}}\Pi_{\alpha}(s)ds\frac{(-\log r)^{\omega-1}}{(1-r^{2})^{\alpha+3/2}}dr

By using Lemma
3.7
and
(E0)
we can find
C>0
such that

\sup_{x\in I}r_{I}\int_{(2I)^{c}}|\partial_{x}H^{\omega}_{\alpha}(x,y)|d\gamma_{\alpha}(y)\leq C

for every
I\in\mathcal{B}_{1}
. Since
\Delta^{-\omega}
is bounded in
L^{2}((0,\infty),\gamma_{\alpha})
, the property that we have just proved, together with the symmetry of the kernel, allow us to obtain the desired conclusion using Theorem
1.2
and duality.

Electromagnetic radiation and electromagnetic self-force of a point charge in the vicinity of Schwarzschild black hole
S. O. Komarov, A. K. Gorbatsievich, A. S. Garkun, G. V. Vereshchagin

Abstract
Аннотация Point charge, radially moving in the vicinity of a black hole is considered. Electromagnetic field in wave zone and in the small neighbourhood of the charge is calculated. Numerical results of the calculation of the spectrum of electromagnetic radiation of the point charge are presented. Covariant approach for the calculation of electromagnetic self-force is used for the case of the slowly moving charge. Numerical results for the self-force in the case of slow motion of the particle are obtained and compared to the results in literature.

1
Introduction

The problem of point charge motion in external gravitational field of a black hole is very important for the understanding of the mechanisms of plasma accretion e. g. within the recently proposed unified model of gamma-ray bursts and quasars
Ruffini2019IE
;
Ruffini2019
;
Ruffini2020
. There are many open questions about the electromagnetic radiation and the motion of such charges
Shatski-NovikovEN
;
Tursunov2018
;
Poisson2011
. The main difficulties emerge in the description of electromagnetic radiation reaction and in the description of the influence of external gravitational field on electromagnetic field distribution and on the motion of the charge.

In this paper we consider electromagnetic field of point charge moving radially in the vicinity of a Schwarzschild black hole. By using the approach that is based on the papers
Ruffini1971
;
Ruffini1972
we study analytically and numerically electromagnetic spectrum of radiation of the charge in wave zone. Also we consider electromagnetic field and electromagnetic self-force for the charge. Using covariant formalism for the description of electromagnetic field from the paper
Poisson2011
we obtain analytical formula for the self-force that can be used in vicinity of the event horizon of black hole. We obtain numerical results and compare it with those in the literature. The results are discussed in "Conclusions"section.

Latin indices have values from 1 to 4, Greek indices from 1 to 3. The signature of the metric is +2. We use the system of units where the speed of light in vacuum is
c=1
.

2
Formulation of the problem

Consider a point charge near spherically symmetric black hole. Metric of the spherically symmetric (Schwarzschild) black hole has the following form
Stephani
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}=\frac{\mathrm{d}r^{2}}{1-2M/r}+r^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}+

\displaystyle r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\mathrm{d}\varphi^{2}-\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)\mathrm{d}t^{2}\,.

(2.1)

Here
x^{i}=\{r,\,\theta,\,\varphi,\,t\}
are Schwarzschild coordinates.
M=Gm_{BH}
, where
G
is the gravitational constant and
m_{BH}
is the mass of the black hole.

Electromagnetic field
F_{ij}
of the charged particle can be found from the Maxwell equations in curved space-time:

\displaystyle F^{ls}{}_{;s}=4\pi j^{l}\,;

(2.2)

\displaystyle F_{ij;k}+F_{ki;j}+F_{jk;i}=0\,.

(2.3)

where
j^{l}
is four-current vector:

\displaystyle j^{l}(x^{k})=eu^{l}(x^{\alpha^{\prime}}(x^{4}),x^{4})\times

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta(x^{1}-x^{1^{\prime}}(x^{4^{\prime}}))\delta(x^{2}-x^{2^{\prime}}(x^{4^{\prime}}))\delta(x^{3}-x^{3^{\prime}}(x^{4^{\prime}}))}{\sqrt{-g}u^{4}(x^{\alpha^{\prime}}(x^{4}),x^{4})}\,.

It follows from equation (
2.3
) that electromagnetic field tensor
F_{ij}
can be expressed in the form

F_{ls}=A_{l;s}-A_{s;l}\,,

(2.5)

where
A_{k}
4-potential of electromagnetic field.

3
Radial motion of the particle

Electromagnetic energy radiated by a test charge, falling radially into the black hole, can be found following the papers
Ruffini1971
;
Ruffini1972
. For this purpose we are looking for the solution of (
2.2
) in the form of multipole expansion
Ruffini1971
:

\displaystyle A_{1}=\sum\limits_{l=0,\,m=0}^{\infty}h_{lm}(r,t)Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)\,;

\displaystyle A_{2}=\sum\limits_{l=0,\,m=0}^{\infty}k_{lm}(r,t)\frac{\partial Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)}{\partial\theta}\,;

\displaystyle A_{3}=\sum\limits_{l=0,\,m=0}^{\infty}k_{lm}(r,t)\frac{\partial Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)}{\partial\phi}\,;

\displaystyle A_{4}=\sum\limits_{l=0,\,m=0}^{\infty}f_{lm}(r,t)Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)\,.

(3.1)

Here,
Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)
are spherical functions.

Then, for the multipole components of
A_{k}
obtain:

\displaystyle(g^{11}b_{lm}{}_{,r})_{,r}+g^{44}b_{lm,44}-\frac{l(l+1)}{r^{2}}b_{lm}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l(l+1)}\left((r^{2}\psi_{lm})_{,r}-(r^{2}\eta_{lm})_{,4}\right)\,;

\displaystyle b_{lm}=\frac{r^{2}}{l(l+1)}(h_{lm}{}_{,r}-f_{lm}{}_{,r})\,;

\displaystyle k_{lm}{}_{,4}-f_{lm}=g^{11}b_{lm}{}_{,4}-\frac{r^{2}}{l(l+1)}\psi_{lm}\,;

\displaystyle h_{lm}-k_{lm}{}_{,r}=g^{44}b_{lm}{}_{,4}+\frac{r^{2}}{l(l+1)}\eta_{lm}\,.

(3.2)

Here functions
\psi
and
\eta
are multipole coefficients for the components of electric current
j_{k}
:

\displaystyle 4\pi j_{1}=\sum\limits_{l,m=0}^{\infty}\eta_{lm}(r,t)Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)\,;

\displaystyle 4\pi j_{4}=\sum\limits_{l,m=0}^{\infty}\psi_{lm}(r,t)Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)\,.

Introduce the Fourier components of the mentioned functions by using the following relations:

\displaystyle\psi_{lm}=\int\limits^{+\infty}_{-\infty}\tilde{\psi}_{lm}e^{-i\omega t}\mathrm{d}\omega\,;

\displaystyle\eta_{lm}=\int\limits^{+\infty}_{-\infty}\tilde{\eta}_{lm}e^{-i\omega t}\mathrm{d}\omega\,;

\displaystyle b_{lm}=\int\limits^{+\infty}_{-\infty}\tilde{b}_{lm}e^{-i\omega t}\mathrm{d}\omega\,;

Then, equation for
b_{lm}
has the following form:

\displaystyle\left[\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)\tilde{b}_{lm,r}\right]_{,r}+\left(\frac{\omega^{2}}{1-2M/r}-\frac{l(l+1)}{r^{2}}\right)\tilde{b}_{lm}=

\displaystyle a(r)\,.

(3.3)

Here we introduce function
a(r)
:

\displaystyle a(r)=\dfrac{q}{2\pi}\dfrac{2l+1}{l(l+1)}e^{it(r)}\times

\displaystyle\left[\dfrac{ME/r^{2}}{(E^{2}-1+2M/r)^{3/2}}-\dfrac{i\omega}{E^{2}-1+2M/r}\right]\,.

Solution of equation (
3.3
) must satisfy boundary conditions for radiation. They are require the following asymptotics at the event horizon and at infinity
MembraneParadigmEN
:

\displaystyle b_{lm}(r,\,\omega)\rightarrow e^{i\omega r}\,,\text{ for }r\rightarrow+\infty\,,

\displaystyle b_{lm}(r,\,\omega)\rightarrow e^{-i\omega r*}\,,\text{ for }r\rightarrow 2M\,.

(3.4)

Here
r*=r+2M\ln{(r-2M)}
is the turtle coordinate. Using Green’s function technique, we obtain solution of (
3.3
) in the form:

\displaystyle b_{lm}(r,\,\omega)=\dfrac{1}{W(r)(1-2M/r)}\times

\displaystyle\left[y_{1}(r)\int\limits^{+\infty}_{r}y_{2}(r^{\prime})a(r^{\prime})\mathrm{d}r^{\prime}+\right.

\displaystyle\left.y_{2}(r)\int\limits^{r}_{2M}y_{1}(r^{\prime})a(r^{\prime})\mathrm{d}r^{\prime}\right]\,.

(3.5)

Here
y_{1}(r)
and
y_{2}(r)
— linear independent solutions of the homogeneous equation with respect to (
3.3
),
W(r)
is Wronsky determinant:

W(r)=y_{1}(r)y^{\prime}_{2}(r)-y^{\prime}_{1}(r)y_{2}(r)\,.

In order to satisfy boundary conditions (
3.4
) it is necessary to choose solutions
y_{1}
as ingoing wave at the event horizon, and
y_{2}
as outgoing wave at the spatial infinity. First solution can be written by using confluent Heun functions for variable
\xi=\frac{r}{2M}
(see, e. g.
FizievHeunF
;
MaierHeunF
):

y_{1}=\xi^{2}(\xi-1)^{-2i\omega}e^{-2i\omega\xi}H(\xi)\,.

(3.6)

In Wolfram Mathematica it has the following representation:

H(\xi)=HeunC[q,\,\alpha,\,\gamma,\,\delta,\,\varepsilon,\xi]\,,

where

\displaystyle q=l(l+1)-2\,;

\displaystyle\alpha=-8i\omega\,;

\displaystyle\gamma=3\,;

\displaystyle\varepsilon=-4i\omega\,;

\displaystyle\delta=1-4i\omega\,.

Then, energy radiated by the point charge can be calculated from Fourier coefficients
b(\omega,r)
:

\frac{\mathrm{d}E}{\mathrm{d}\omega}=\frac{i}{4}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{\infty}l(l+1)\tilde{b}_{,r}(\omega,r)\tilde{b}^{*}(\omega,r)\,.

(3.7)

General solution for electromagnetic field and self-force

General solution for electromagnetic potential
A^{k}
can be found using the formalism of Synge’s world function (see, e. g.
Poisson2011
):

A^{l}=\left.q\frac{u^{l^{\prime}}g^{l}{}_{l^{\prime}}\sqrt{\Delta}}{\sigma_{k^{\prime}}u^{k^{\prime}}}\right|_{\sigma=0}+e\int\limits^{\tau^{\prime}}_{-\infty}V^{l}{}_{l^{\prime\prime}}u^{l^{\prime\prime}}\mathrm{d}\tau^{\prime\prime}\,.

(4.1)

Here
q
is the charge of the particle,
\sigma
is the Synge’s world function:

\displaystyle\sigma(x^{k},\,x^{k^{\prime}})=\frac{1}{2}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{0})\int\limits_{\lambda_{0}}^{\lambda_{1}}g_{ij}t^{i}t^{j}\mathrm{d}\lambda=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{0})^{2}||t^{j}||^{2}\,,

\lambda\in[\lambda_{0},\,\lambda_{1}]
is affine parameter on isotropic geodesic between
x^{k}
and
x^{k^{\prime}}
(see fig.
2
).

x^{i^{\prime}}
and the point of observation
x^{i}
.

A^{l}=\left.e\frac{u^{l^{\prime}}g^{l}{}_{l^{\prime}}\sqrt{\Delta}}{\sigma_{k^{\prime}}u^{k^{\prime}}}\right|_{\sigma=0}+e\int\limits^{\tau^{\prime}}_{-\infty}V^{l}{}_{l^{\prime\prime}}u^{l^{\prime\prime}}\mathrm{d}\tau^{\prime\prime}\,.

(4.2)

Here
V_{ii^{\prime}}
is a bitensor satisfying d’Alamber’s equation in curved space-time:

g^{ij}\frac{D^{2}}{Dx^{i}Dx^{j}}V^{l}{}_{l^{\prime\prime}}-R^{l}{}_{s}V^{s}{}_{l^{\prime\prime}}=0\,,

(4.3)

and initial condition

V_{l^{\prime}l^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{12}Rg_{l^{\prime}l^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}R_{l^{\prime}l^{\prime}}\,;

(4.4)

We use the "Method of images":

\left.q\frac{u^{l^{\prime}}g^{l}{}_{l^{\prime}}\sqrt{\Delta}}{\sigma_{k^{\prime}}u^{k^{\prime}}}\right|_{\sigma=0,r=2M}=\frac{q}{2r^{\prime}}\,.

(4.5)

This formalism gives us possibilities to calculate electromagnetic self-force
F^{r}
for the charged particle, fixed far from Schwarzschild black hole:

F^{r}\approx q^{2}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{0}V_{ji^{\prime};r}u^{i^{\prime}}g^{j}{}_{k^{\prime}}u^{k^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}\tau^{\prime}\approx\frac{q^{2}M}{r^{\prime 3}}\,.

(4.6)

This result coincides with those obtained in literature, where different approaches have been used (see e. g.
McGruder1978
;
Vilenkin
).

It follows from the approach for the calculation of self-force that the obtained formula depends only on the electric field distribution in the space and due to this must be valid not only for fixed point charge but for the moving charge as well. 5
Conclusions

In the present work we show that the solution for the spectrum of the electromagnetic radiation of the charge falling into Schwarzschild black hole can be presented in compact form by using the confluent Heun functions. The numerical results of calculation show that most of the energy radiated is determined by the first (
l=1
) multipole moment in expansion. This is in agreement with the results of previous papers, where only numerical solution of homogeneous equation for
b_{lm}
in limited region of space was calculated (see, e. g.
Ruffini1971
;
Ruffini1972
).

We found analytical and numerical solution for electromagnetic self-force of a charge moving in the vicinity of Schwarzschild black hole using covariant approach that is based on the results of the paper
Poisson2011
. Unlike post-Newtonian consideration in other papers
McGruder1978
;
Vilenkin
, the presented approach gives us possibilities to obtain analytical expression for the electromagnetic self-force that is valid in the small vicinity of the event horizon of Schwarzschild black hole. For the point charge far from black hole numerical and analytical results are in agreement with post-Newtonian consideration (see, e. g.
McGruder1978
;
Vilenkin
).

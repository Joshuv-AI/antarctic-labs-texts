Abstract
In this paper, we consider the problem of maintaining a ( 1 − ε ) (1-\varepsilon) -approximate maximum weight matching in a dynamic graph G G , while the adversary makes changes to the edges of the graph. In the fully dynamic setting, where both edge insertions and deletions are allowed, Gupta and Peng [ GP13 ] gave an algorithm for this problem with an update time of O ~ ε ​ ( m ) \tilde{O}_{\varepsilon}(\sqrt{m}) . We study a natural relaxation of this problem, namely the decremental model, where the adversary is only allowed to delete edges. For the cardinality version of this problem in general (possibly, non-bipartite) graphs, [ ABD22 ] gave a decremental algorithm with update time O ε ​ ( poly ​ ( log ⁡ n ) ) O_{\varepsilon}(\mbox{\rm poly}(\log n)) . However, beating O ~ ε ​ ( m ) \tilde{O}_{\varepsilon}(\sqrt{m}) update time remained an open problem for the weighted version in general graphs . In this paper, we bridge the gap between unweighted and weighted general graphs for the decremental setting. We give a O ε ​ ( poly ​ ( log ⁡ n ) ) O_{\varepsilon}(\mbox{\rm poly}(\log n)) update time algorithm that maintains a ( 1 − ε ) (1-\varepsilon) approximate maximum weight matching under adversarial deletions.Like the decremental algorithm of [ ABD22 ] , our algorithm is randomized, but works against an adaptive adversary. It also matches the time bound for the cardinality version upto dependencies on ε \varepsilon and a log ⁡ R \log R factor, where R R is the ratio between the maximum and minimum edge weight in G G .

Introduction

In dynamic algorithms, the main goal is to maintain a key property of the graph while an adversary makes changes to the edges of the graph. An algorithm is called incremental if it only handles edge insertions, and decremental if it handles only edge deletions and fully dynamic if it handles both edge insertions and deletions.

The problem of maintaining a
(1-\varepsilon)
-approximation to the maximum matching in a dynamic graph is a well-studied one. In the fully dynamic setting, the best known update time is
O(m^{0.5-\Omega_{\varepsilon}(1)})
(see
[
BKS23a
]
), and the conditional lower bounds proved in the
[
HKNS15
,
KPP16
]
suggest that this is a hard barrier to break through. Consequently, several relaxations of this problem have been studied. For example, one line of research has shown that we can get considerably faster update times if we settle for large approximation factors. Another research direction has been to consider more relaxed incremental or decremental models. In the incremental setting, there have been a series of upper and lower bound results (see
[
Dah16
]
and
[
Gup14
]
), culminating in the results of
[
GLS+19
]
(who give a
O(m\cdot(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\varepsilon}})^{O(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\varepsilon}})})
total update time algorithm for
(1-\varepsilon)
-approximate matching in
general graphs
) and of
[
BK23
]
(who give a
O(m\cdot\mbox{\rm poly}(1/\varepsilon))
total update time algorithm for
(1-\varepsilon)
-approximate matching in
bipartite graphs
). In contrast, for decremental matching
[
BGS20
,
JJST22
]
gave an
O(m\cdot\mbox{\rm poly}(\log n,\nicefrac{{1}}{{\varepsilon}}))
total update time algorithm for
(1-\varepsilon)
-approximate matching in bipartite graphs, and
[
ABD22
]
extended this algorithm to the case of general graphs by giving an algorithm for
(1-\varepsilon)
-approximate decremental matching with total time
O_{\varepsilon}(m\cdot\mbox{\rm poly}(\log n))
, thus completing the picture for approximate cardinality matching in the partially dynamic setting.

For the weighted case (in both fully and partially dynamic settings) the picture is less clear.
[
BDL21
]
showed that for the bipartite case, it is possible to reduce weighted matching to unweighted matching with an overhead of
(1-\varepsilon)
in the approximation factor, and at a loss of a factor of
\log W
and
(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\varepsilon}})^{O(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\varepsilon}})}
in the update time. Recently,
[
BKS23b
]
showed that this exponential-in-
1/\varepsilon
dependence was avoidable for bipartite graphs in the partially dynamic setting.

In contrast, for non-bipartite graphs, we don’t have such a general reduction. Thus, while there has been significant recent progress on unweighted matching, the state-of-art for weighted matching lags far behind. Our main contribution in this paper, is to close this gap between weighted and unweighted matching for general graphs in the decremental dynamic setting:

Theorem 1

Let
G
be a weighted graph with weight function

on the edges of the graph and let

with

2^{{\left(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\varepsilon}}\right)}^{O\left(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\varepsilon}}\right)}}

In comparison, the decremental algorithm for unweighted

1.1.1
Structural Results for Decremental Matching

The congestion balancing framework of
[
BGS20
]
maintains a fractional matching that is robust to deletions. In order to ensure that the matching is robust and “spread out”, they impose a capacity function
\kappa
on the edges of the graph. They repeatedly invoke a subroutine
\text{M-or-E*}()
that does one of the following (here
\mu(G)
refers to the size of maximum cardinality matching):

1.

Either output a fractional matching
\vec{x}
, such that
\sum_{e\in E}x(e)\geqslant(1-\varepsilon)\cdot\mu(G)
or,

2.

Output a set
E^{*}
of bottleneck edges along which we can increase capacities:

(i)

\kappa(E^{*})
is
O(\mu\log n)

(ii)

Every large matching in
G
has
\varepsilon\cdot\mu(G)
edges in
E^{*}
.

The authors of
[
BGS20
]
use this subroutine to get an algorithm for decremental matching as follows: either
M-or-E*
() outputs a fractional matching
\vec{f}
such that
\sum_{e\in E}\geqslant(1-\varepsilon)\cdot\mu(G)
, in which case we can use a black-box of
[
Waj20
]
to round this
\vec{f}
, or if there is no
\vec{f}
satisfying this property, then it outputs a set of edges
E^{*}
along which we can increase capacity. Note that,
2
2(i)
ensures that the total capacity increase is small, and
2
2(ii)
ensures that we only increase capacity along edges necessary to form a large matching. Thus, they are able to show that the capacities remain small on average.

Predictably in general graphs, implementing such a subroutine is challenging because of the blossom constraints which impose the following roadblocks:

1.

An arbitrary fractional matching in a general graph can suffer from an integrality gap, which is not the case for bipartite graphs. Consequently, we need to be able to check if a given general graph has a large fractional matching obeying capacities and the blossom constraints.

2.

Once we know there is a large fractional matching obeying the above requirements, we need to efficiently find such a fractional matching. In bipartite graphs, this can be done use approximate max flows. However, for general graphs this correspondence between fractional matchings and flows is lost.

3.

Finally, in the case of bipartite graphs, if the fractional matching is small, the bottleneck edges
E^{*}
along which we have to increase capacity correspond to a min-cut in the graph, however for the case of general graphs, this characterization is not clear.

The main contribution of
[
ABD22
]
is to address these challenges through three structural lemmas, we state these informally, and then state our weighted versions of these lemmas.

Lemma 2
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
\mu(G)
denotes the size of the optimal matching in
G
. Suppose
\kappa
is the capacity function on the edges of the graph, and let
\mu(G,\kappa)
denote the optimal fractional matching of
G
, obeying capacity function
\kappa
, and all odd set constraints. Sample every edge
e
with
p(e)\propto\kappa(e)
to create uncapacitated graph
G_{s}
. Then,
\mu(G_{s})\geqslant\mu(G,\kappa)-\varepsilon\cdot n
.

The above lemma gives us a way to determine whether a given capacitated graph has a large fractional matching obeying blossom constraints by reducing this problem to the problem of finding an integral matching in a general graph. For the latter problem, there are efficient algorithms known. This takes care of Issue

Let

4.1
Phase 1 of
WeightedM-or-E*
()

Recall that we used
mwm
(
G,\kappa
) to denote the weight of the maximum weight fractional matching of
G
obeying
\kappa
as well as the odd set constraints. As in the congestion balancing step, we want to estimate
\textsf{mwm}(G,\kappa)
. However, existing techniques only work for bipartite or general unweighted graphs. In this section, we extend this to the case of general weighted graphs. In particular, we extend the structural lemma to show that if in a weighted graph
G
, we sample every edge with probability
p(e)=\min\left\{1,\kappa(e)\cdot\rho_{\varepsilon}\right\}
to create a graph
G_{s}
, then
\textsf{mwm}(G_{s})\geqslant\textsf{mwm}(G,\kappa)-\varepsilon\cdot\textsf{mwm}(G)
. Thus, now we can estimate
\textsf{mwm}(G_{s})
by using existing results.

Lemma 38
.

Let
G
be an integer weighted multigraph with weights in
\left\{1,2,\cdots,W\right\}
, and with
\textsf{mwm}(G)\geqslant\max\left\{\nicefrac{{\varepsilon\cdot n}}{{16\cdot W}},\nicefrac{{\log n}}{{\varepsilon^{4}}}\right\}
, where
\varepsilon\in(0,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})
. Let
\kappa
be a capacity function on the edges of the graph, and let
G_{s}
be obtained by sampling every edge
e
independently with probability
p(e)=\min\left\{\kappa(e)\cdot\rho_{\varepsilon},1\right\}
. Let
\textsf{mwm}(G,\kappa)
be the weight of the maximum weight fractional matching of
G
that obeys the odd set constraints and the capacity constraints. Suppose
\textsf{mwm}(G,\kappa)\geqslant(1-\varepsilon)\cdot\textsf{mwm}(G)
, then, with high probability,
\textsf{mwm}(G_{s})\geqslant\textsf{mwm}(G,\kappa)-\varepsilon\cdot\textsf{mwm}(G)
.

Proof of
38
.

Let
\vec{x}
denote the fractional matching that realizes
mwm
(
G,\kappa
). In order to prove the statement, we will construct a vector
\vec{z}
in the support of
G_{s}
. This vector
\vec{z}
will have the following properties: it will satisfy the fractional matching constraints and small blossom constraints with high probability. Additionally, it will also have the property that
\sum_{e\in E)}w(e)\cdot z(e)\geqslant(1-\varepsilon)\cdot\sum_{e\in E}w(e)\cdot x(e)\geqslant\textsf{mwm}(G,\kappa)-\varepsilon\cdot\textsf{mwm}(G)
with high probability as well.

Since with high probability
G_{s}
, contains a fractional matching
\vec{z}
satisfying the above properties, there is an integral matching
M
in support of
G_{s}
with
w(M)\geqslant(1-\varepsilon)\cdot\sum_{e\in E}w(e)\cdot z(e)\geqslant\textsf{mwm}(G,\kappa)-\varepsilon\cdot\textsf{mwm}(G)
(see
71
).

Let
X_{e}
denote a random variable that takes value
1
if
e\in H_{s}
and value
0
otherwise. We define
\vec{z}
as follows:

\displaystyle z(e)=X_{e}\cdot\frac{x(e)}{\min\left\{1,\kappa(e)\cdot\rho_{\varepsilon}\right\}}\cdot(1-\varepsilon)

Note that
z(e)
are independent random variables. The following is true.

\displaystyle\Exp\left[\sum_{e\in E}z(e)\cdot w(e)\right]=(1-\varepsilon)\cdot\sum_{e\in E}w(e)\cdot x(e)

Since this is a sum of independent random variables, using Chernoff bound (see
36
), we have,

\displaystyle\Prob\left(\sum_{e\in E}z(e)\cdot w(e)\leqslant\sum_{e\in E}w(e)\cdot x(e)-2\cdot\varepsilon\cdot\textsf{mwm}(G)\right)=\exp\left(\nicefrac{{-\varepsilon^{2}\textsf{mwm}(G)}}{{2}}\right)

By assumption,
\textsf{mwm}(G)\geqslant\nicefrac{{100\cdot\log n}}{{\varepsilon^{4}}}
, we have that the above claim holds with probability at least
1-O(\nicefrac{{1}}{{n^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{\varepsilon}}}}})
. We will now show that it obeys fractional matching constraints with high probability as well. Consider an edge
e
with
\kappa(e)>\nicefrac{{1}}{{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}
, we know that
e\in G_{s}
. Consequently, we have
\textnormal{Var}\left[z(e)\right]=0
for such an edge, since
z(e)=x(e)
always. For any edge with
\kappa(e)<\nicefrac{{1}}{{\rho_{e}}}
, we have,

\displaystyle\textnormal{Var}\left[z(e)\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\cdot\left(\frac{x(e)}{\kappa(e)\cdot\rho_{\varepsilon}}\right)^{2}\cdot p(e)=(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\frac{x(e)^{2}}{\kappa(e)\cdot\rho_{\varepsilon}}\leqslant(1-2\varepsilon)\cdot\frac{x(e)}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}

Additionally, since
z(e)
are independent random variables, we have
\textnormal{Var}\left[\sum_{v\ni e}z(e)\right]=\sum_{v\ni e}\textnormal{Var}\left[z(e)\right]\leqslant(1-2\varepsilon)\cdot\rho_{\varepsilon}^{-1}
. Additionally, we know that
\Exp\left[\sum_{e\ni v}z(e)\right]\leqslant(1-\varepsilon)
. Thus, we want to compute the probability of the event that
\sum_{e\ni v}z(e)\geqslant 1
. Note that, in order to do this, it is sufficient to consider the edges
e
for which
\kappa(e)<\rho_{\varepsilon}^{-1}
, since for edges other than this,
z(e)=x(e)
. Thus, we have, by
37
, with
a=\varepsilon
,
M=\rho_{\varepsilon}^{-1}
,

\displaystyle\Prob\left(\sum_{e\ni v}z(e)>1\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\left(\frac{-\varepsilon^{2}}{\frac{1}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\right)=O\left(\frac{1}{n^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{\varepsilon}}}}\right)

∎

Taking a union bound over all vertices in the graph, we have our claim. Next, we want to compute the probability that
\vec{z}
also satisfies small odd set constraints. To see this, consider any odd set
B
such that
|B|\leqslant\nicefrac{{1}}{{\varepsilon}}
. We know that by definition of
\vec{x}
, we have,

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in G[B]}x(e)\leqslant\frac{|B|-1}{2}\text{ and,}

\displaystyle\Exp\left[\sum_{e\in G[B]}z(e)\right]\leqslant(1-\varepsilon)\cdot\frac{|B|-1}{2}

Similarly, we can bound variance as well,

\displaystyle\textnormal{Var}\left[\sum_{e\in G[B]}z(e)\right]\leqslant(1-\varepsilon)\cdot\frac{|B|-1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}

Consider the following subclaim.

Observation 39
.

Let
B
be any odd set such that
3\leqslant|B|\leqslant\nicefrac{{1}}{{\varepsilon}}
. Then,

\displaystyle\frac{|B|-1}{2}=(1-\varepsilon)\cdot\frac{|B|-1}{2}+\varepsilon\cdot\frac{|B|-1}{2}\geqslant(1-\varepsilon)\cdot\frac{|B|-1}{2}+\varepsilon

Let
\mathcal{E}_{B}
be the event that
\sum_{e\in G[B]}z(e)\geqslant\frac{|B|-1}{2}
,

\displaystyle\Prob\left(\mathcal{E}_{B}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\Prob\left(\sum_{e\in G[B]}z(e)\geqslant(1-\varepsilon)\cdot\frac{|B|-1}{2}+\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\frac{|B|-1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\right)

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{1}{n^{{\nicefrac{{1}}{{\varepsilon}}}}}\right)

The second equality follows from the fact that we are considering small blossoms, that is,
|B|\leqslant\nicefrac{{1}}{{\varepsilon}}
. Taking a union bound over all small blossoms, we have our claim.

4.2
Phase 2 of
WeightedM-or-E*
()

The algorithm
WeightedM-or-E*
() proceeds to Phase 2 only if in Phase 1, if
\textsf{mwm}(G,\kappa)\geqslant(1-\varepsilon)\cdot\textsf{mwm}(G)
(
38
). In Phase 2, we now want to construct a good fractional matching.

Definition 40
.

Let
G
be a multigraph, we define
E_{L}=\left\{e\in E\mid e\in D_{i}(u,v),\kappa(D_{i}(u,v))\leqslant\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha_{\varepsilon}^{2}}}\right\}
. Similarly, we define
\kappa^{+}(e)=\kappa(e)\cdot\alpha_{\varepsilon}
. Intuitively, these correspond to the low capacity edges.

Lemma 41
.

Let
G
be a weighted multigraph with maximum edge weight
W
. Suppose
\textsf{mwm}(G)\geqslant\max\left\{\nicefrac{{\varepsilon\cdot n}}{{16\cdot W}},\nicefrac{{\log n}}{{\varepsilon^{4}}}\right\}
Then, with high probability, for all
X\subseteq V
, we have

\displaystyle\textsf{mwm}(G_{s}[X])\leqslant\textsf{mwm}(G[X]\cap E_{L},\kappa^{+})+\varepsilon\cdot\textsf{mwm}(G)

Proof.

Consider a fixed
X
, and from now on, we use
H\coloneqq G[X]\cap E_{L}
and
H_{s}\coloneqq G_{s}[X]\cap E_{L}
. In order to prove this, we will make use of a primal-dual argument. Note that the problem of finding a
\textsf{mwm}(H,\kappa^{+})
can be formulated as a linear program as follows.

\begin{array}[]{ll@{}ll}\text{maximize}&\displaystyle\sum\limits_{e\in E}w(e)\cdot x(e)&\\
\text{subject to}&\\
\\
\sum_{e\ni v}x(e)&\leqslant 1&\forall v\in X\\
\sum_{e\in G[B]}x(e)&\leqslant\frac{|B|-1}{2}&\forall B\in X_{\text{odd}}\\
x(e)&\leqslant\kappa^{+}(e)&\forall e\in E\end{array}

(1)

The corresponding dual program is as follows:

\begin{array}[]{ll@{}ll}\text{minimize}&\displaystyle\sum\limits_{u\in V}y_{u}+\sum_{B\subseteq X_{\text{odd}}}r(B)\cdot\left(\frac{|B|-1}{2}\right)+\sum_{e\in E(H)}z(e)\cdot\kappa^{+}(e)&\\
\text{subject to}&\\
&\displaystyle y_{u}+y_{v}+z(e)+\sum\limits_{B:(u,v)\in G[B]}r(B)\geqslant w(e)&\forall e\text{ between }u,v,\forall u,v\in X\\
\end{array}

(2)

Let
f(y,z,r)
denote the optimal of the dual program, then by strong duality, we know that
\textsf{mwm}(H,\kappa^{+})=f(y,z,r)
. Similarly, for any uncapacitated graph we have the same primal and dual programs, except we don’t have the third constraint in the primal program, and in the dual program we omit the
z
variables.

\begin{array}[]{ll@{}ll}\text{maximize}&\displaystyle\sum\limits_{e\in E}w(e)\cdot x^{\prime}(e)&\\
\text{subject to}&\\
\\
\sum_{e\ni v}x^{\prime}(e)&\leqslant 1&\forall v\in X\\
\sum_{e\in G[B]}x^{\prime}(e)&\leqslant\frac{|B|-1}{2}&\forall B\in X_{\text{odd}}\end{array}

(3)

The corresponding dual program is as follows:

\begin{array}[]{ll@{}ll}\text{minimize}&\displaystyle\sum\limits_{u\in V}y^{\prime}_{u}+\sum_{B\subseteq X_{\text{odd}}}r^{\prime}(B)\cdot\left(\frac{|B|-1}{2}\right)&\\
\text{subject to}&&\\
\displaystyle y^{\prime}_{u}+y^{\prime}_{v}+&\sum\limits_{B:(u,v)\in G[B]}r^{\prime}(B)\geqslant w(e)&\forall e\text{ between }u,v,\forall u,v\in X\\
\end{array}

Let
g(y^{\prime},r^{\prime})
denote the optimal for dual program corresponding to
H_{s}
. Note that this is a random variable, since
H_{s}
is a random graph. We will show that with high probability,

\displaystyle g(y^{\prime},r^{\prime})\leqslant f(y,r,z)+\varepsilon\cdot\textsf{mwm}(G)

By weak duality, we have,
\textsf{mwm}(H_{s})\leqslant g(y^{\prime},r^{\prime})
, and
f(y,r,z)=\textsf{mwm}(H,\kappa^{+})
. This will show our claim for a fixed
H
, and then we take a union bound over all
H
.

We will use
\left\{\left\{y_{u}\right\}_{u\in X},\left\{r(B)\right\}_{B\in X_{\text{odd}}}\right\}
to get a solution for the dual program for
H_{s}
. We will refer to this set of dual variables as an attempted cover. Let
E^{\prime}=\left\{e\mid z(e)>0\right\}
. Observe that the edges left uncovered by attempted cover of
H_{s}
are precisely a subset of
E^{\prime}
. We now modify the cover as follows.

\displaystyle\Delta y_{u}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\ni v,e\in H_{s}}z(e)

\displaystyle y_{u}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=y_{u}+\Delta y_{u}

Note that
\left\{\left\{y_{u}^{\prime}\right\}_{u\in X},\left\{r(B)\right\}_{B\in X_{\text{odd}}}\right\}
is a valid cover of
H_{s}
. To see this, consider edge
e\in H_{s}
and let
u,v
be the endpoints of
H_{s}
. Suppose
e\notin E^{\prime}
, then,

\displaystyle w(e)

\displaystyle\leqslant y_{u}+y_{v}+z(e)+\sum_{e\in G[B]}r(B)

\displaystyle\leqslant y_{u}+y_{v}+\sum_{e\in G[B]}r(B)

\displaystyle\leqslant y^{\prime}_{u}+y^{\prime}_{v}+\sum_{e\in G[B]}r(B)

Similarly, for an edge
e\in E^{\prime}
, we have,

\displaystyle w(e)

\displaystyle\leqslant y_{u}+y_{v}+z(e)+\sum_{e\in G[B]}r(B)

\displaystyle\leqslant y_{u}+y_{v}+\Delta y_{u}+\Delta y_{v}+\sum_{e\in G[B]}r(B)

\displaystyle\leqslant y^{\prime}_{u}+y^{\prime}_{v}+\sum_{e\in G[B]}r(B)

Moreover,
g(y^{\prime},r^{\prime})=f(y,r,z)+\sum_{u\in X}\Delta y_{u}
. Thus, it is sufficient to bound
\sum_{u\in X}\Delta y_{u}
.

\displaystyle\Exp\left[\sum_{u\in X}\Delta y_{u}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E}z(e)\cdot\Prob\left(e\in H_{s}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{e\in E}z(e)\cdot\kappa(e)\cdot\rho_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{e\in E}\frac{z(e)\cdot\kappa^{+}(e)}{2^{\nicefrac{{W}}{{\varepsilon^{2}}}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\textsf{mwm}(H,\kappa^{+})\cdot 2^{\nicefrac{{-W}}{{\varepsilon^{2}}}}

Using Chernoff and choosing
\delta=\frac{\varepsilon\cdot\textsf{mwm}(G)\cdot 2^{\nicefrac{{W}}{{\varepsilon^{2}}}}}{\textsf{mwm}(H,\kappa^{+})}
, we have,

\displaystyle\Prob\left(\sum_{u\in X}\Delta y_{u}\geqslant 2\cdot\varepsilon\cdot\textsf{mwm}(G)\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\Prob\left(\sum_{u\in X}\Delta y_{u}\geqslant\textsf{mwm}(H,\kappa^{+})\cdot 2^{\nicefrac{{-W}}{{\varepsilon^{2}}}}+\varepsilon\cdot\textsf{mwm}(G)\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\left(\varepsilon\cdot\textsf{mwm}(G)-\varepsilon\cdot\textsf{mwm}(G)\cdot\log\left(1+\varepsilon\cdot 2^{\nicefrac{{W}}{{\varepsilon^{2}}}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\left(-\varepsilon\cdot\textsf{mwm}(G)\cdot\frac{W}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)

By assumption,
n\leqslant\frac{\textsf{mwm}(G)\cdot W}{\varepsilon}
, thus taking a union bound over all subsets, we have our theorem.
∎

Lemma 45

\begin{array}[]{ll@{}ll}\text{maximize}&\displaystyle\sum\limits_{e\in E}w(e)\cdot x(e)\\

\begin{array}[]{ll@{}ll}\text{minimize}&\displaystyle\sum\limits_{v\in L}y(v)+\sum\limits_{u\in R}y(u)+\sum_{e\in E}z(e)\cdot\kappa(e)&\\

. Additionally, before stating our algorithm, we give some definitions.

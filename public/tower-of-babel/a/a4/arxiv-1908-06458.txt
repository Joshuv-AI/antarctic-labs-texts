. 1.
Introduction

Let
\lambda
be
a frequency, i.e. a strictly increasing, unbounded sequence of non-negative real numbers.
Moreover, let
G
be a compact abelian group, and
\beta:\mathbb{R}\to G
a continuous homomorphism of groups with dense range such that for each character
e^{-i\lambda_{n}\cdot}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{T}
there is a (then unique) character

h_{\lambda_{n}}:G\to\mathbb{T}
with
e^{-i\lambda_{n}\boldsymbol{\cdot}}=h_{\lambda_{n}}\circ\beta
.

For

1\leq p\leq\infty
denote by
H^{\lambda}_{p}(G)
the Hardy space of all
f\in L_{p}(G)
which have a Fourier
transform
\hat{f}:\hat{G}\to\mathbb{T}
supported by all characters
h_{\lambda_{n}}
. It is known that for
1<p<\infty
every

f\in H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)
has an almost everywhere convergent Fourier series representation

f(\omega)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(h_{\lambda_{n}})h_{\lambda_{n}}(\omega)
.

Inspired by the work
[
10
]
of Hardy and Riesz on general Dirichlet series from 1915, we in this article study almost everywhere Riesz-summability of the Fourier series of functions
f\in H^{\lambda}_{1}(G)
.
The main tool is given by an appropriate weak-type
(1,\infty)
Hardy-Littlewood
maximal operator.

As a particular case we look at the frequency
\lambda=(\log n)
,
the infinite dimensional torus
G=\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
, and the Kronecker flow
\beta:\mathbb{R}\to G,t\mapsto(p_{k}^{it})
,
where
p_{k}
denotes the
k
th prime. Our results prove that each

f\in H_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
almost everywhere is the pointwise limit of its logarithmic Riesz means, whereas for arithmetic Riesz means (Cesàro means) this in general fails.

Most of our results have equivalent formulations in terms of
general Dirichlet series
\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
.
More precisely, vertical limits of Dirichlet series
\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
which belong to the Hardy space
\mathcal{H}_{1}(\lambda)
, are summable by their first Riesz means of any order
k>0
on the
imaginary axis
[\text{Re}=0]
(and consequently on the right half-plane).

Another application shows, that the Hardy space
H_{\infty}^{\lambda}(G)
may be identified with the Banach space of all bounded and holomorphic function on
[Re>0]
which for every
\varepsilon>0
are uniformly almost periodic on
[Re>\varepsilon]
, preserving the Fourier and Bohr coefficients.

In the following subsections of this introduction we substantiate all this and provide our reader with the needed preliminaries. In Section
2
we summarize all our results, and in Section
3

we prove them.

1.1.
Hardy spaces on Dirichlet groups

Let us briefly recall the general framework of Hardy spaces
H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)
on so-called

\lambda
-Dirichlet groups
(G,\beta)
from
[
4
]
.

A pair
(G,\beta)
of a compact abelian group
G
and a homomorphism
\beta\colon(\mathbb{R},+)\to G
is said to be a Dirichlet group, whenever
\beta
is continuous and has dense range. In this case, the dual map of
\beta
, that is the mapping

\widehat{\beta}\colon\widehat{G}\hookrightarrow\widehat{\mathbb{R}},\penalty\ \penalty\ \gamma\to\gamma\circ\beta
,
is injective, where
\widehat{G}
denotes the dual group of
G
. So, using the identification
\widehat{(\mathbb{R},+)}=\mathbb{R}
(which we do from now on), the dual group of
G
via
\widehat{\beta}
can be considered as a subset of
\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, if
x\in\mathbb{R}
lies in the image of
\widehat{\beta}
, we write
h_{x}:=(\widehat{\beta})^{-1}(x)
and obtain

\widehat{G}=\left\{h_{x}\mid x\in\operatorname{Im}\widehat{\beta}\right\}.

In other words, for
x\in\widehat{\beta}(\widehat{G})
the characters
t\mapsto e^{-ixt}
on
(\mathbb{R},+)
are precisely those, which allow a unique ’extension’
h_{x}\in\widehat{G}
such that
h_{x}\circ\beta=e^{-ix\cdot}
. Note that we do not force
\beta
to be injective.

Given a frequency
\lambda=(\lambda_{n})
, i.e a strictly increasing non-negative real sequence tending to
\infty
, we call the Dirichlet group
(G,\beta)
a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group whenever
\lambda\subset\widehat{\beta}(\widehat{G})
. Given such a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group
(G,\beta)
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we define Hardy space

H_{p}^{\lambda}(G):=\left\{f\in L_{p}(G)\mid\forall\penalty\ \gamma\in\widehat{G}:\penalty\ \widehat{f}(\gamma)\neq 0\Rightarrow\gamma=h_{\lambda_{n}}\penalty\ \text{for some n}\right\},

which being a closed subspace of
L_{p}(G)
, is a Banach space. Of course,
L_{p}(G)
is here formed with respect to the normalized Haar measure
m
on
G
.
Given two
\lambda
-Dirichlet groups
(G_{1},\beta_{1})
and
(G_{2},\beta_{2})
, a crucial fact is that the spaces
H_{p}^{\lambda}(G_{1})
and
H_{p}^{\lambda}(G_{2})
are isometrically isomorphic
(see
[
4
, Corollary 3.21]
). More precisely, there is an onto isometry

(1)

T\colon H_{p}^{\lambda}(G_{1})\to H_{p}^{\lambda}(G_{2}),\penalty\ \penalty\ f\mapsto g,

which preserves the Fourier coefficients, that is for all
x
we have

\widehat{f}\Big((\widehat{\beta_{1}})^{-1}(x)\Big)=\widehat{g}\Big((\widehat{\beta_{2}})^{-1}(x)\Big).

Let us collect a few crucial examples. The Bohr compactification
\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=\widehat{(\mathbb{R},d)}

of
\mathbb{R}
(
d
the discrete topology) together with the embedding

\beta_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}\colon\mathbb{R}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}},\,\,x\mapsto\left[t\mapsto e^{-ixt}\right]\,,

forms a Dirichlet group,
which obviously for any arbitrary frequency
\lambda
serves as a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group.

There are two basic examples which later in many more general situations will help us to keep orientation.
Consider the frequency
\lambda=(n)=(0,1,2,\ldots)
. Then
G:=\mathbb{T}
together with
\beta_{\mathbb{T}}(t):=e^{-it}
is a
(n)
-Dirichlet group, and
\mathcal{H}_{p}((n))
equals the classical Hardy space
H_{p}(\mathbb{T})
.
The second crucial example is
\lambda=(\log n)
. In this case, denoting by
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{n})
the sequence of prime numbers, the infinite dimensional torus

\mathbb{T}^{\infty}:=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{T}

(with its natural group structure)
together with the so-called
Kronecker flow

(2)

\beta_{\mathbb{T}^{\infty}}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{T}^{\infty},\penalty\ \penalty\ t\mapsto\mathfrak{p}^{-it}=(2^{-it},3^{-it},5^{-it},\ldots),

gives a
(\log n)
-Dirichlet group. Then

f\in H_{p}^{(\log n)}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
if and only if
f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
and
\hat{f}(\alpha)=0
for any finite sequence

\alpha=(\alpha_{k})
of integers with
\alpha_{k}<0
for some
k
. In other terms,

H_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})=H_{p}^{(\log n)}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})

holds isometrically, and
h_{\log n}=z^{\alpha}
whenever
n=\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha}
.

There is a useful reformulation of the Dirichlet group
(\mathbb{T}^{\infty},\beta_{\mathbb{T}^{\infty}})
. Denote by
\Xi
the set of
all characters
\chi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{T}
, i.e.
\chi
is completely multiplicative in the sense that

\chi(nm)=\chi(n)\chi(m)
for all
n,m
. So every character is uniquely determined by its values on the primes.
If we on
\Xi
consider
pointwise multiplication, then

\displaystyle\iota\colon\Xi\to\mathbb{T}^{\infty},\penalty\ \penalty\ \chi\mapsto\chi(\mathfrak{p})=(\chi(p_{n}))_{n},

is a group isomorphism which turns
\Xi
into a compact abelian group. The Haar measure
d\chi
on
\Xi
is the push forward of the normalized Lebesgue measure
dz
on
\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
through
\iota^{-1}
. Hence also
\Xi
together with

\beta_{\Xi}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\Xi,\penalty\ \penalty\ t\mapsto[p_{k}\to\chi(p_{k})],

forms a
(\log n)
-Dirichlet group.

Recall from
[
4
, Lemma 3.11]
that, given a Dirichlet group
(G,\beta)
and
f\in L_{1}(G)
, for almost all
\omega\in G
there are locally Lebesgue integrable functions
f_{\omega}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
f_{\omega}(t)=f(\omega\beta(t))
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}
. As we will see later, this way to ’restrict’ functions on the group
G
to
\mathbb{R}
, establishes a sort of bridge
between Fourier analysis on a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group
G
and Fourier analysis on
\mathbb{R}
.

In this context, the classical Poisson kernel
P_{u}(t)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{u}{u^{2}+t^{2}}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, where
u>0
, plays a crucial role. Since
P_{u}\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
has norm
1
, its push forward under
\beta

leads to a regular Borel probability measure
p_{u}
on
G
. We call
p_{u}
the Poisson measure on
G
,
and note that
\widehat{p_{u}}(h_{x})=e^{-u|x|}
for all
x\in\widehat{\beta}(\widehat{G})
.

1.2.
The reflexive case – functions

Given a compact and abelian group
G
with Haar measure
m
and a class
\mathcal{F}
of functions in
L_{p}(G),1\leq p\leq\infty
, one of the fundamental questions
of Fourier analysis certainly is to ask for necessary and sufficient conditions on
\mathcal{F}
under
which the Fourier series
\sum_{\gamma\in\widehat{G}}\widehat{f}(\gamma)\gamma
of each
f\in\mathcal{F}
approximates
f

in a reasonable way – e.g. almost everywhere pointwise or in the
L_{p}
-norm,
and with respect to a reasonable summation method like ordinary or Cesáro summation.

In the following we will carefully distinguish the reflexive case
1<p<\infty
from the
non-reflexive cases
p=1
and
\infty
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(G,\beta)
be a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group and
1<p<\infty
. There is a constant
\text{CH}_{p}>0
such that
for every
f\in H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)
we have

\Big(\int_{G}\sup_{x}\Big|\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}\widehat{f}(h_{\lambda_{n}})h_{\lambda_{n}}(\omega)\Big|^{p}d\omega\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\text{CH}_{p}\,\|f\|_{p}.

In particular,
\sum\widehat{f}(h_{\lambda_{n}})h_{\lambda_{n}}
approximates
f
almost everywhere pointwise
and in the
H_{p}
-norm.

For
\lambda=(n)
and the Dirichlet group
(\mathbb{T},\beta_{\mathbb{T}})
this is the celebrated Carleson-Hunt theorem,
and the constant
\text{CH}_{p}
mentioned above in fact equals the one from the maximal inequality in
the CH-theorem for one variable. Based on this one variable case and a method from
[
8
]
, Hedenmalm-Saksman in

[
12
, Theorem 1.5]
extend the CH-theorem to functions
f\in H_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
, which in the preceding theorem is reflected by the
(\log n)
-Dirichlet group

(\mathbb{T}^{\infty},\beta_{\mathbb{T}^{\infty}})
. The general case given above has to be credited to Duy from
[
6
]
; for our reformulation within the setting of arbitrary
\lambda
-Dirichlet groups we refer to
[
5
]
.

The CH-theorem in one variable fails for
p=1
, so clearly the preceding extension fails in this case.
On the other hand, it is well-known that every function
f\in H_{1}(\mathbb{T})

almost everywhere equals the pointwise limit of its Cesàro means (see e.g.
[
9
, Theorem 3.4.4, p.207]
), i.e.

(3)

f(z)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sum_{n\leq k}\widehat{f}(n)z^{n}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\widehat{f}(n)\Big(1-\frac{n}{N}\Big)z^{n}

for almost all
z\in\mathbb{T}
. Moreover, this is also true if the limits are taken with respect to the
H_{1}
-norm.
So it
seems natural to consider for a given
f\in H_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
the Cesàro means of the partial
sums

\sum_{\alpha:1\leq\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha}\leq k}\widehat{f}(\alpha)z^{\alpha},\,\,k\in\mathbb{N}\,,

and to ask whether almost everywhere pointwise and/or in the
H_{1}
-norm

(4)

f(z)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sum_{\mathfrak{p^{\alpha}}\leq k}\widehat{f}(\alpha)z^{\alpha}\,\,?

We will later see that this is in general false – but true, if we change Cesàro summation by more adapted summation methods
invented in
[
10
]
by Hardy and M. Riesz within the setting of general Dirichlet series.

1.3.
Riesz means – functions

The following definitions are inspired by
[
10
]
. Let
\lambda
be a frequency,
k\geq 0
, and

\sum c_{n}
a series in a Banach space
X
. Then we call the series
\sum c_{n}

(\lambda,k)
-Riesz summable if
the limit

\lim_{x\to\infty}\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda_{n}}{x}\Big)^{k}c_{n}

exists, and we call the finite sums

R_{x}^{\lambda,k}\Big(\sum c_{n}\Big):=\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda_{n}}{x}\Big)^{k}c_{n}

first
(\lambda,k)
-Riesz means of
\sum c_{n}
of length
x>0
.

Hardy and Riesz in
[
10
]

isolated the following fundamental properties of Riesz summability; the results are (in the order of the proposition) taken from
[
10
, Theorem 16, p. 29, Theorem 17, p. 30, and Theorem 21, p. 36]
.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
\lambda
be a frequency,
k\geq 0
, and
\sum c_{n}
a series in a Banach space
X
.

(1)

First theorem of consistency: If
\sum c_{n}
is
(\lambda,k)
-Riesz summable, then it is

(\lambda,\ell)
-Riesz summable for any
k\leq\ell
, and the associated limits coincide.
In particular, if
\sum c_{n}
is summable (i.e. the series converges), then for all
k>0

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda_{n}}{x}\Big)^{k}c_{n}.

(2)

Second theorem of consistency:
If
\sum c_{n}
is
(e^{\lambda},k)
-Riesz summable, then

\sum c_{n}
is
(\lambda,k)
-Riesz summable, and the associated limits coincide.

(3)

If
\sum c_{n}
is
(\lambda,k)
-summable summable with limit
C
, then

\lim_{N\to\infty}\Big(\frac{\lambda_{N+1}-\lambda_{N}}{\lambda_{N+1}}\Big)^{k}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}-C\Big)=0\,.

Note that,
if
\sum c_{n}
in view of Proposition
1.2
, (2) is not only

(\lambda,k)
-Riesz summable but even
(e^{\lambda},k)
-Riesz summable, then

(5)

\lim_{x\to\infty}R_{x}^{e^{\lambda},k}\big(\sum c_{n}\big)=\lim_{x\to\infty}R_{e^{x}}^{e^{\lambda},k}\big(\sum c_{n}\big)=\lim_{x\to\infty}\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}\Big(1-\frac{e^{\lambda_{n}}}{e^{x}}\Big)^{k}c_{n}\,;

we refer to the finite sums

S_{x}^{\lambda,k}\Big(\sum c_{n}\Big):=\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}\Big(1-\frac{e^{\lambda_{n}}}{e^{x}}\Big)^{k}c_{n}

as the second
(\lambda,k)
-Riesz means of
\sum c_{n}
of length
x>0
.

Take now some
\lambda
-Dirichlet group
(G,\beta)
and
f\in H_{1}^{\lambda}(G)
. Then we call
the Fourier series of
f

(\lambda,k)
-Riesz summable in
\omega\in G
if it is

(\lambda,k)
-Riesz summable in
\omega\in G
, in other terms the limit

\lim_{x\to\infty}\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}\hat{f}(h_{\lambda_{n}})\Big(1-\frac{\lambda_{n}}{x}\Big)^{k}h_{\lambda_{n}}(\omega)

exists. It is then needless to say what is meant by the phrase ’the Fourier series of
f
is
(\lambda,k)
-Riesz summable in the
H_{p}
-norm’. Moreover, the polynomial

R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f):=\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}\hat{f}(h_{\lambda_{n}})\Big(1-\frac{\lambda_{n}}{x}\Big)^{k}h_{\lambda_{n}}

is the so-called first
(\lambda,k)
-Riesz mean of
f
of length
x>0
, and

S_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f):=\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}\hat{f}(h_{\lambda_{n}})\Big(1-\frac{e^{\lambda_{n}}}{e^{x}}\Big)^{k}\,,

the second
(\lambda,k)
-Riesz mean of
f
of length
x>0
. Observe that if
the Fourier series of
f
is
(e^{\lambda},k)
-Riesz summable in
\omega\in G
, then as in (
5
)

(6)

\lim_{x\to\infty}R_{x}^{e^{\lambda},k}(f)(\omega)=\lim_{x\to\infty}R_{e^{x}}^{e^{\lambda},k}(f)(\omega)=\lim_{x\to\infty}S_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f)(\omega).

Let us again for a moment concentrate on the two in a sense extrem frequencies
\lambda=(n)
and
\lambda=(\log n)
.

As mentioned
\lambda=(n)
together with
(\mathbb{T},\beta_{\mathbb{T}})

forms a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group. Then the

(\lambda,1)
-Riesz mean of

f\in H_{1}(\mathbb{T})=\mathcal{H}_{1}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{T})
of
length
x
equals

(7)

R_{x}^{\lambda,1}(f)=\sum_{n<x}\widehat{f}(n)\Big(1-\frac{n}{x}\Big)z^{n},

which for
x=N\in\mathbb{N}
is nothing else than the Cesàro mean of the
N
th partial sum of the Fourier series of
f
considered in (
3
).

In this sense Riesz means generalize the Cesàro means for functions on
\mathbb{T}
to the much wider setting of functions on Dirichlet groups.

Let us also consider
\lambda=(\log n)
and the
\lambda
-Dirichlet group

(\mathbb{T}^{\infty},\beta_{\mathbb{T}^{\infty}})
.
For
f\in H_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})=H_{1}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
we refer to the first
(\lambda,k)
-Riesz means of
f
, that are

(8)

R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f)=\sum_{\log\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha}<x}\widehat{f}(\alpha)\Big(1-\frac{\log\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha}}{x}\Big)^{k}z^{\alpha},\penalty\ \penalty\ x>0,

as the logarithmic means of
f
.
Observe also, that in this case

(9)

\displaystyle R_{x}^{e^{\lambda},k}(f)(z)=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha}<x}\widehat{f}(\alpha)\Big(1-\frac{\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha}}{x}\Big)^{k}z^{\alpha}\,,

and hence for
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
k=1

(10)

\displaystyle R_{N}^{e^{\lambda},1}(f)(z)=\sum_{\mathfrak{p^{\alpha}}<N}\hat{f}(\alpha)\big(1-\frac{\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha}}{N}\big)z^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sum_{\mathfrak{p^{\alpha}}\leq k}\hat{f}(\alpha)z^{\alpha}.

Remark 1.3
.

Let
f\in H^{\lambda}_{1}(G)
,
\omega\in G
, and
k\geq 0
.
Then for
\lambda=(n)
the following are equivalent:

(1)

The Fourier series of
f
converges at
\omega
.

(2)

The Fourier series of
f
is
(e^{\lambda},k)
-Riesz summable at
\omega
.

In this case the limits coincide,
and a similar result holds true, whenever we replace convergence in
\omega
by convergence with respect to the

H_{p}
-norm,
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

Proof.

Part (3) of Proposition
1.2
proves the implication (2)
\Rightarrow
(1), and the reversed direction follows from Proposition
1.2
(1).
∎

So for the frequency
\lambda=(n)
, Riesz summability by second means seems not to be particularly interesting.

After all this, let us finally indicate the main challenge of this article:
For which frequencies
\lambda
and which
\lambda
-Dirichlet groups
(G,\beta)
do we for all
f\in H_{1}^{\lambda}(G)
have

(11)

\displaystyle f=\lim_{x\to\infty}R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f)

almost everywhere on
G
and/or in the
H_{1}
-norm?

1.4.
Hardy spaces of Dirichlet series

Given a frequency
\lambda=(\lambda_{n})
,
a
\lambda
-Dirichlet series is a (formal) sum of the form
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
, where
s
is a complex variable and the sequence
(a_{n})
form the so-called Dirichlet coefficients of
D
.
Finite sums of the form
D=\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
we call Dirichlet polynomials.
By
\mathcal{D}(\lambda)
we denote the space of all
\lambda
-Dirichlet series. It is well-known that
if
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
converges in
s_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
, then it also converges for all
s\in\mathbb{C}

with
Res>Res_{0}
, and its limit function
f(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}

defines a holomorphic function on
[Re>\sigma_{c}(D)]
, where

\sigma_{c}(D)=\inf\left\{\sigma\in\mathbb{R}\mid D\text{ converges on }[Re>\sigma]\right\}

determines the so-called abscissa of convergence.

In
[
4
]
we introduce an
\mathcal{H}_{p}
-theory of general Dirichlet series extending Bayart’s
\mathcal{H}_{p}
-theory of ordinary Dirichlet series
\sum a_{n}n^{-s}
(where
\lambda=(\log n)
) from
[
1
]
(see also e.g.
[
3
]
,
[
14
]
, and
[
20
]
for more information on the ’ordinary’ case). Let us briefly recall the general framework from
[
4
]
, which in particular shows, that there are several ways to produce Dirichlet series.

Fixing a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group
(G,\beta)
, we define
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
, where
1\leq p\leq\infty
, to be the space of all (formal)
\lambda
-Dirichlet series
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
for which there is
f\in H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)
such that that
a_{n}=\widehat{f}(h_{\lambda_{n}})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Endowed with the norm
\|D\|_{p}:=\|f\|_{p}
, we obtain a Banach space.

Note that by (
1
), the definition of
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
is independent of the chosen
\lambda
-Dirichlet group, and we by definition obtain the onto isometry

(12)

\mathcal{B}\colon H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)\to\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda),\penalty\ \penalty\ f\mapsto\sum\widehat{f}(h_{\lambda_{n}})e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\,;

for historical reasons we call this mapping Bohr transform.
From
[
4
, Theorem 3.26]
recall the following internal description of
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
: Since
D=\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
, considered as a function on the imaginary line, defines an almost periodic function, the limit

(13)

\|D\|_{p}:=\lim_{T\to\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}\Big|\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}it}\Big|^{p}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

exists and defines a norm on the space
Pol(\lambda)
of all
\lambda
-Dirichlet polynomials. Then
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
is the completion of
\big(Pol(\lambda),\|\cdot\|_{p}\big)
.

1.5.
Transference

We want to understand, how (pointwise) summability properties of the Fourier series
\sum\widehat{f}(h_{\lambda_{n}})h_{\lambda_{n}}

of functions
f\in H_{1}^{\lambda}(G)
transfer to summability properties of their associated Dirichlet series
D:=\mathcal{B}(f)
, and vice versa. Slightly more precise, but still vague,
we try to figure out how summation of these Fourier series by first or second Riesz means influences the
convergence properties
of so-called vertical limits of
D
.

Given a
\lambda
-Dirichlet series
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
, we call
the Dirichlet series

D_{z}:=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}

the translation of
D
about
z
, and we distinguish between horizontal translations
D_{u},u\in\mathbb{R}
, and vertical translations

D_{i\tau},\tau\in\mathbb{R}
.

If
(G,\beta)
is a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group and
D\in\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
is associated to

f\in H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)
, then for each
u>0

the horizontal translation
D_{u}
corresponds to the convolution of
f
with the Poisson measure
p_{u}
, i.e.

\mathcal{B}(f*p_{u})=D_{u}
(compare coefficients). In particular, we have that
D_{u}\in\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
.

Moreover, each Dirichlet series of the form

D^{\omega}=\sum a_{n}h_{\lambda_{n}}(\omega)e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\,,\,\,\omega\in G

is said to be a vertical limit of
D
. Examples are vertical translations

D_{i\tau}
with
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
,
and the terminology is explained by the fact that each vertical limit may be approximated by vertical translates. More precisely, given
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
which converges absolutely on the right half-plane, for every
\omega\in G
there is a sequence
(\tau_{k})_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}
such that
D_{i\tau_{k}}
converges to
D^{\omega}
uniformly on
[Re>\varepsilon]
for all
\varepsilon>0
.
Assume conversely that for
(\tau_{k})_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}
the vertical translations
D_{i\tau_{k}}
converge
uniformly on
[Re>\varepsilon]
for every
\varepsilon>0

to a holomorphic function
f
on
[Re>0]
. Then there is
\omega\in G
such that

f(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\omega(h_{\lambda_{n}})e^{-\lambda_{n}s}

for all
s\in[Re>0]
. For all this see
[
4
, Proposition 4.6]
.

The following lemma (to be proved in Section
3.4
) is our ’bridge’ comparing
almost everywhere Riesz-summability of the Fourier series of a function
f\in H_{1}^{\lambda}(G)

with the convergence of almost all vertical limits
D^{\omega}
of its associated Dirichlet series

D=\mathfrak{B}(f)
almost everywhere on the imaginary axis.

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
(G,\beta)
be a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group, and
f_{n},f\in H_{1}^{\lambda}(G)
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(\omega)=f(\omega)

for almost all
\omega\in G

(2)

\lim_{n\to\infty}(f_{n})_{\omega}(t)=f_{\omega}(t)

for almost all
\omega\in G
and for almost all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

In particular, if all
f_{n}
are polynomials and
D_{n}\in\mathcal{H}_{1}(\lambda)

are the Dirichlet polynomials associated to
f_{n}
under the Bohr transform, then
(1)
and
(2)
are equivalent to each of the
following two further statements:

(3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}D^{\omega}_{n}(0)=f(\omega)

for almost all
\omega\in G

(4)

\lim_{n\to\infty}D^{\omega}_{n}(it)=f_{\omega}(t)

for almost all
\omega\in G
and for almost all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Note that the question we formulated in (
11
) then reads: For which frequencies
\lambda

and for which
\lambda
-Dirichlet groups
(G,\beta)
is it true that for
every
D\in\mathcal{H}_{1}(\lambda)
we for
almost all
\omega\in G
have

(14)

\displaystyle D^{\omega}=\lim_{x\to\infty}R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(D^{\omega})

almost everywhere on the imaginary axis?

1.6.
The reflexive case – Dirichlet series

The following result is an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.1
and Lemma
1.4
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\lambda
be a frequency,
(G,\beta)
a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group, and
1<p<\infty
.
Then for every
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\in\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)

\Big(\int_{G}\sup_{x}\Big|\sum_{n=1}^{x}a_{n}h_{\lambda_{n}}(\omega)\Big|^{p}d\omega\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\text{CH}_{p}\,\|D\|_{p}.

In particular, almost all vertical limits
D^{\omega}

converge almost everywhere on the imaginary axis, and consequently also on the right half-plane.

For
p=2
Helson in
[
13
, §2]
proves convergence on
[Re>0]

under Bohr’s condition for
\lambda
, i.e.

\exists\penalty\ l>0\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ \delta>0\penalty\ \exists\penalty\ C>0\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\penalty\ \lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}\geq Ce^{-(l+\delta)\lambda_{n}}

(see also
[
14
, Theorem 9, p. 29]
),
and in the ordinary case and
1\leq p<\infty
this is done by Bayart
[
1
]
.
Still in the ordinary case, convergence on the imaginary axis
[Re=0]
for
p=2
has to be credited to Hedenmalm-Saksman
[
12
]
, and for the full scale
1<p<\infty
this is observed in
[
5
]
.

But for
p=1
the first two assertions of the preceding result are false. Otherwise by Lemma
1.4
we would see that all Fourier series of functions
f\in H_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})

converge pointwise almost everywhere which we know is false (even in the one variable case). The third statement we only know under the additional Landau condition
(LC)
for
\lambda
(see again
[
5
]
), i.e.

(15)

\forall\penalty\ \delta>0\penalty\ \exists\penalty\ C>0\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\penalty\ \lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}\geq Ce^{-e^{\delta\lambda_{n}}};

this in fact is a condition weaker than
(BC)
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\lambda
be a frequency
with
(LC)

and

(G,\beta)
a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group. Then
for
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\in\mathcal{H}_{1}(\lambda)

almost all vertical limits
D^{\omega}
of
D
converge on
[\text{Re}>0]
. More precisely, if
f\in H_{1}^{\lambda}(G)
is associated to
D
, then there is a null set
N\subset G
such that for
\omega\notin N
, all
u>0
and almost all
t\in\mathbb{R}

D^{\omega}(u+it)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}h_{\lambda_{n}}(\omega)e^{-\lambda_{n}(u+it)}=(f_{\omega}*P_{u})(t).

See also Section
2.2
, where we show that in the ordinary case Theorem
1.5
is false for
p=1
, even if we there replace summation of the series by the weaker Cesàro summation.
Alternative more adapted summation methods have to be taken into account which we describe now
(recall also the discussion from (
4
)).

1.7.
Riesz means – Dirichlet series

Here we repeat some fundamental definitions and results on Riesz-summability of general Dirichlet series from
[
10
]
.
Fix some frequency
\lambda
, some
k\geq 0
, and a
\lambda
-Dirichlet series
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
. The first
(\lambda,k)
-Riesz mean of
D
of length
x>0
is given by the Dirichlet polynomial

R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(D)(s):=\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}a_{n}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda_{n}}{x}\Big)^{k}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}.

We say that a Dirichlet series
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
is
(\lambda,k)
-Riesz summable at
s_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
, if the limit

\lim_{x\to\infty}R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(D)(s_{0})=\lim_{x\to\infty}\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}a_{n}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda_{n}}{x}\Big)^{k}e^{-\lambda_{n}s_{0}}

exists, and
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
is
(\lambda,k)
-Riesz summable in
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)

whenever this limit exists in
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
. As in (
5
), if
D
is (even)
(e^{\lambda},k)
-Riesz summable, then we have

(16)

\lim_{x\to\infty}R_{x}^{e^{\lambda},k}(D)(s)=\lim_{x\to\infty}R_{e^{x}}^{e^{\lambda},k}(D)(s)=\lim_{x\to\infty}S_{x}^{\lambda,k}(D)(s)\,,

where

S_{x}^{\lambda,k}(D)(s):=\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}a_{n}\Big(1-\frac{e^{\lambda_{n}}}{e^{x}}\Big)^{k}e^{\lambda_{n}s}

is what we call the second
(\lambda,k)
-Riesz mean of
D
of length
x>0
.

Let us again comment on the ordinary case
\lambda=(\log n)
. Then the first
(\lambda,k)
-Riesz mean of
D=\sum a_{n}n^{-s}
of length
x
is given by

R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(D)(s)=\sum_{\log n<x}a_{n}\Big(1-\frac{\log n}{x}\Big)^{k}n^{-s};

Hardy and Riesz in
[
10
]
call it the logarithmic mean of
D

of length
x
.
As in (
10
), we for
\lambda=(\log n)

and
x=N\in\mathbb{N}
obtain Cesàro means,

(17)

\displaystyle R_{N}^{e^{\lambda},k}(D)(s)=\sum_{n<N}a_{n}\big(1-\frac{n}{N}\big)a_{n}n^{-s}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sum_{n\leq k}a_{n}n^{-s}\,.

From Remark
1.3
we may deduce the following equivalence for the case
\lambda=(n)
.

Remark 1.7
.

Let
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
,
s_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
, and
k\geq 0
.
Then for
\lambda=(n)
the following are equivalent:

(1)

D
converges at
s_{0}

(2)

D
is
(e^{\lambda},k)
-summable at
s_{0}

Moreover, the limits coincide,
and the analog result holds true, whenever we replace convergence in
s_{0}
by convergence with respect to the

\mathcal{H}_{p}
-norm,
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

All results collected in Proposition
1.2
may be translated to

\lambda
-Dirichlet series. We do this in terms of Riesz-abscissas of convergence.
Define for
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}

\sigma_{c}^{\lambda,k}(D)=\inf\Big\{\sigma\in\mathbb{R}\colon\text{$D$ is $(\lambda,k)$-Riesz summable at $\sigma$}\Big\}\,.

Then it is proved in
[
10
, Theorem 24 and 25, p. 43]
that
D
converges on the half-plane
[Re>\sigma_{c}^{\lambda,k}(D)]

whereas it diverges on
[Re<\sigma_{c}^{\lambda,k}(D)]
. Moreover, the limit function is holomorphic (see
[
10
, Theorem 27, p. 44]
). Obviously, we have that

\sigma_{c}^{\lambda,0}(D)=\sigma_{c}(D)\,.

From Hardy and Riesz
[
10
, Theorem 16, p. 29 and Theorem 30, p. 45]
(see also again Proposition
1.2
) we know the following.

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
\lambda
be a frequency and
0\leq k<\ell
. Then for every
\lambda
-Dirichlet series
D
we have

(1)

\sigma_{c}^{\lambda,k}(D)\leq\sigma_{c}^{\lambda,\ell}(D)

(2)

\sigma_{c}^{e^{\lambda},k}(D)=\sigma_{c}^{\lambda,k}(D)

Finally, we note that for
f\in H_{1}^{\lambda}(G)
, where
(G,\beta)
is some
\lambda
-Dirichlet group
and
D\in\mathcal{H}_{1}(\lambda)
with
D:=\mathcal{B}(f)
, we for all
k,x>0
have

\mathcal{B}(R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f))=R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(D).

2.1.
Pointwise approximation by first Riesz means

Generally speaking, in order to obtain almost everywhere convergence of the Riesz means of some

f\in H^{\lambda}_{1}(G)
, it is sufficient to prove an adequate maximal inequality.

For that purpose recall that for some measure space
(\Omega,\mu)
the weak
L_{1}
-space

L_{1,\infty}(\mu)
is given by all measurable functions
f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
for which there is a constant
C>0
such that for all
\alpha>0
we have

(18)

m\big(\left\{\omega\in G\mid|f(\omega)|>\alpha\right\}\big)\leq\frac{C}{\alpha}.

Together with
\|f\|_{1,\infty}:=\inf C
the space
L_{1,\infty}(\mu)
becomes a quasi Banach space (see e.g.
[
9
, §1.1.1 and §1.4]
), where the triangle inequality holds with constant
2
.

The following maximal inequality forms the core of this article.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\lambda
be a frequency,
k>0
and
(G,\beta)
a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group. Then

R_{\max}^{\lambda,k}(f)(\omega):=\sup_{x>0}\big|R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f)(\omega)\big|=\sup_{x>0}\Big|\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}\widehat{f}(h_{\lambda_{n}})\Big(1-\frac{\lambda_{n}}{x}\Big)^{k}h_{\lambda_{n}}(\omega)\Big|

defines a bounded sublinear operator from
H_{1}^{\lambda}(G)
to
L_{1,\infty}(G)
, and from
H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)
to
L_{p}(G)
, whenever
1<p\leq\infty
.

By a standard argument (to be formalized in Lemma
3.6
), we deduce from Theorem
2.1

for each
f\in H_{1}^{\lambda}(G)
almost everywhere summability by first Riesz means of the Fourier series

\sum\widehat{f}(h_{\lambda_{n}})h_{\lambda_{n}}
,
but also of the translated Fourier series

\sum\widehat{f}(h_{\lambda_{n}})e^{-u\lambda_{n}}h_{\lambda_{n}}
.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
f\in H_{1}^{\lambda}(G)
and
k>0
. Then for almost all
\omega\in G
we have

(19)

\lim_{x\to\infty}R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f)(\omega)=f(\omega).

Moreover, there is a null set
N\subset G
such that for all
u>0
and all
\omega\notin N

(20)

\lim_{x\to\infty}R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f*p_{u})(\omega)=(f*p_{u})(\omega).

Note that for some fixed
u>0
the result from (
20
) is immediate from (
19
)
since
f*p_{u}\in H_{1}^{\lambda}(G)
,
but the point here is that the null set
N
in fact can be chosen to be independent of
u
.

In particular, (
19
) reproves (
3
) with the choice
\lambda=(n)
,
k=1
and the Dirichlet group
(\mathbb{T},\beta_{\mathbb{T}})
. Since first Riesz means of order
k=0
are precisely partial sums, Corollary
2.2
may fail for
k=0
(like it does for
\lambda=(n)
).
Let us also revisit the ordinary case
\lambda=(\log n)
. Then Theorem
2.1
gives the following substitute for the failure of (
4
).

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
f\in H_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
and
k>0
. Then for almost all
z\in\mathbb{T}^{\infty}

(21)

\lim_{x\to\infty}\sum_{\log(\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha})<x}\widehat{f}(\alpha)\Big(1-\frac{\log(\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha})}{x}\Big)^{k}z^{\alpha}=f(z),

that is,
f
almost everywhere is the pointwise limit of its logarithmic means.

Let us explain in which sense (
19
) is the limit case of (
20
).

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\lambda
be arbitrary. Then the operator

T(f)(\omega):=\sup_{u>0}|(f*p_{u})(\omega)|

defines a bounded sublinear operator from
L_{1}(G)
to
L_{1,\infty}(G)
, and from
L_{p}(G)
to
L_{p}(G)
, provided
1<p\leq\infty
. In particular, for almost all
\omega\in G

\lim_{u\to 0}(f*p_{u})(\omega)=f(\omega).

Obviously, Proposition
2.4
implies that for almost all
\omega\in G

\displaystyle\lim_{u\to 0}\lim_{x\to\infty}R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f*p_{u})(\omega)

\displaystyle=\lim_{u\to 0}(f*p_{u})(\omega)

\displaystyle=f(\omega)=\lim_{x\to\infty}\lim_{u\to 0}R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f*p_{u})(\omega),

explaining, why (
19
) is the limit case of (
20
).

For the
(n)
-Dirichlet group
(\mathbb{T},\beta_{\mathbb{T}})
all this is linked with Fatou’s famous theorem on radial limits. Observe that, if
f\in H_{1}(\mathbb{T})
and
0<r<1
, then with the choice
u:=-\log(r)
we have

f*p_{u}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(n)r^{n}z^{n},

and Proposition
2.4
implies, that for almost all
z\in\mathbb{T}

(22)

\lim_{r\to 1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(n)r^{n}z^{n}=f(z).

In the terminology of
[
16
, Theorem 11.23]
this says that
for every
f\in H_{p}(\mathbb{T})
the so-called Poisson integral

P[f](w):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(n)w^{n},w\in\mathbb{D}
has radial limits almost everywhere on
\mathbb{D}
. This is a crucial step of the proof of Fatou’s theorem, which states, that if
f\in H_{p}(\mathbb{D})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, then for almost all
z\in\mathbb{T}
the radial limits

g^{*}(z):=\lim_{r\to 1}g(rz)
exist and define a function from
H_{p}(\mathbb{T})
. So in this sense, Proposition
2.4
extends (
22
) from
H_{p}^{(n)}(\mathbb{T})
to
H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)
.

Let us again revisit Theorem
2.1
. If
p=1
, then the function
R_{\max}^{\lambda,k}(f)

for
f\in H^{\lambda}_{1}(G)
may fail to be in
L_{1}(G)
, like it does for
\lambda=(n)
and
(\mathbb{T},\beta_{\mathbb{T}})
. On the other hand, philosophically speaking, for
f\in H^{\lambda}_{1}(G)
a horizontal translation of
f
by
u>0
, that is

f_{u}=f\ast p_{u}\sim\sum\widehat{f}(h_{\lambda_{n}})e^{-u\lambda_{n}}h_{\lambda_{n}},

improves
f
considerably. Then, as shown in the following result,
R_{\max}^{\lambda,k}(f_{u})
for any
u,k>0
indeed belongs
to
L_{1}(G)
. Of course we we already know from Theorem
2.1
that
R_{\max}^{\lambda,k}(f_{u})\in L_{p}(G)
whenever
f\in H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)
and
1<p\leq\infty
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
u,k>0
. Then there is a constant
C=C(u,k)
such that for all
\lambda
,
1\leq p<\infty
and
f\in H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)
we have

\Big(\int_{G}\sup_{x>0}\Big|\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}\widehat{f}(h_{\lambda_{n}})e^{-u\lambda_{n}}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda_{n}}{x}\Big)^{k}h_{\lambda_{n}}(\omega)\Big|^{p}d\omega\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C\|f\|_{p}.

Compared with Theorem
2.1
the relevant part of this result is the case
p=1
. But also the case of arbitrary
p
’s seems interesting since in the above inequality
the constant
C=C(u,k)
does not depend on
p
.

Finally, we discuss Riesz summation of Dirichlet series. We use the ’bridge’ from Lemma
1.4
to rephrase Corollary
2.2
in terms of the summability of almost all vertical limits of
\mathcal{H}_{1}
-Dirichlet series by first Riesz means almost everywhere on the imaginary axis.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\lambda
be a frequency,
k>0
,
f\in H_{1}^{\lambda}(G)
,
and
D
its associated Dirichlet series in
\mathcal{H}_{1}(\lambda)
. Then
there is a null set
N\subset G
such that for all
\omega\notin N

(23)

\lim_{x\to\infty}R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(D^{\omega})(0)=f(\omega)\,,

(24)

\lim_{x\to\infty}R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(D^{\omega})(it)=f_{\omega}(t)\,\,\,\text{ for almost all $t\in\mathbb{R}$ },

and

(25)

\lim_{x\to\infty}R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(D^{\omega})(u+it)=f_{\omega}*P_{u}(t)\,\,\,\text{ for all $u>0$ and almost all $t\in\mathbb{R}$. }

Note that by Theorem
1.6
the case
k=0
in (
25
) is known, if
\lambda
satisfies
(LC)
.

In view of (
13
) the following maximal inequality may be considered as an
internal variant of Theorem
2.5
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
u,k>0
. Then there is a constant
C=C(u,k)
such that for all
\lambda
,
1\leq p<\infty
, and
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\in\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
we for almost all
\omega\in G
have

\lim_{T\to\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}\sup_{x>0}\Big|\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}a_{n}h_{\lambda_{n}}(\omega)\Big(1-\frac{\lambda_{n}}{x}\Big)^{k}e^{-\lambda_{n}(u+it)}\Big|^{p}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C\|D\|_{p}.

All proofs of this section are given in the Sections
3.2
,
3.3
,
3.4
,
and
3.5
.

2.2.
Failure of pointwise approximation by second Riesz means

Let
\lambda
be a frequency,
k>0
, and
(G,\beta)
a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group.
From Corollary
2.2
we know that the Fourier series of each
f\in H_{1}^{\lambda}(G)

is
(\lambda,k)
-Riesz summable with limit
f
, i.e.
f
is almost everywhere approximable by its first Riesz means
R^{\lambda,k}_{x}(f)
. Let us discuss whether in view of Proposition
1.2
, (2) the Fourier series of each
f\in H_{1}^{\lambda}(G)
is even
(e^{\lambda},k)
-Riesz summable with limit
f
, i.e.
f
is almost
everywhere approximable by its second Riesz means
S^{\lambda,k}_{x}(f)
(see (
6
)).

For
\lambda=(n)
and the
\lambda
-Dirichlet group
(\mathbb{T},\beta_{\mathbb{T}})

we know that the Fourier series of each
f\in H_{1}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{T})=H_{1}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{T})
is Cesáro summable almost everywhere with limit
f
, i.e.
it is
(\lambda,1)
-Riesz summable with limit
f
(as mentioned this is a particular case of
Corollary
2.2
). But it is definitely not
(e^{\lambda},1)
-Riesz summable since otherwise
by Proposition
1.3
each
f\in H_{1}(\mathbb{T})
would have an almost everywhere convergent Fourier series.

On the other hand look at
\lambda=(\log n)
and the
\lambda
-Dirichlet group
(\mathbb{T}^{\infty},\beta_{\mathbb{T}^{\infty}})
.
We have already mentioned Corollary
2.3
which shows that the Fourier series of every

f\in H_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})=H_{1}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
is
(\lambda,1)
-Riesz summable with limit
f
. Assume that the Fourier series of each such

f
may even be almost everywhere

(e^{\lambda},1)
-summable, i.e. it
is almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
approximable by its second Riesz means:

f=\lim_{x\to\infty}S_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f)=\lim_{x\to\infty}\sum_{\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha}<e^{x}}\hat{f}(\alpha)\big(1-\frac{\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha}}{e^{x}}\big)z^{\alpha}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\sum_{\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha}<x}\hat{f}(\alpha)\big(1-\frac{\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha}}{x}\big)z^{\alpha}\,.

Then for every
f\in H_{1}(\mathbb{T})\subset H_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}

f=\lim_{x\to\infty}\sum_{2^{j}<x}\hat{f}(j)\big(1-\frac{2^{j}}{x}\big)z^{j}\,.

In other words the Fourier series of every
f\in H_{1}(\mathbb{T})
is almost everywhere
((2^{j}),1)
-Riesz summable
with limit
f
. By Proposition
1.2
, this implies that each such Fourier series
is almost everywhere summable on
\mathbb{T}
, a contradiction.

We collect the preceding information in the following

Remark 2.8
.

Given a frequency
\lambda
and a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group
(G,\beta)
, it is in general not true,
that each
f\in H_{1}^{\lambda}(G)
has an almost everywhere
(e^{\lambda},1)
-summable Fourier series.
Counterexamples are
\lambda=(n)
and
(\mathbb{T},\beta_{\mathbb{T}})
, as well as
\lambda=(\log n)
and
(\mathbb{T}^{\infty},\beta_{\mathbb{T}^{\infty}})
.

Corollary
2.6
shows that, given a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group
(G,\beta)
and
k>0
, almost all vertical limits
D^{\omega}
of a Dirichlet series
D\in\mathcal{H}_{1}(\lambda)
are
(\lambda,k)
-Riesz summable
almost everywhere on the vertical line. Are they even
(e^{\lambda},k)
-Riesz summable? Remark
2.8
and
Lemma
1.4
show that the answer in general in no.

But something can be saved. By Corollary
2.6
and Proposition
1.8
almost all vertical limits of Dirichlet series
D\in\mathcal{H}_{1}(\lambda)
are
(\lambda,k)
-Riesz summable on
[Re>0]
, and so

(e^{\lambda},k)
-Riesz summable on
[Re>0]
. As a consequence of Theorem
2.5
we are going to deduce the following quantitative version of this fact.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
u,k>0
. Then there is a constant
C=C(u,k)
such that for all
\lambda
,
1\leq p<\infty
and
f\in H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)
we have

\left(\int_{G}\sup_{x>0}\Big|\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}\widehat{f}(h_{\lambda_{n}})e^{-u\lambda_{n}}\Big(1-e^{\lambda_{n}-x}\Big)^{k}h_{\lambda_{n}}(\omega)\Big|^{p}d\omega\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C\|f\|_{p}.

One could be tempted to believe that this maximal inequality is an immediate consequence of Theorem
2.5
(applying it to
e^{\lambda}
instead of
\lambda
). But this is not true since we here
consider functions in

H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)
and not
H_{p}^{e^{\lambda}}(G)
.
The proof is given in Section
3.5
.

2.3.
A Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator

One of the central tools needed for the proof of Theorem
2.1
is given a Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator adapted to Fourier analysis on Dirichlet groups. If
f\in L_{1}(G)
, where
(G,\beta)
is any Dirichlet group, then we define

(26)

\overline{M}(f)(\omega):=\sup_{I\subset\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}|f_{\omega}(t)|\penalty\ dt;

here
I
stands for any interval in
\mathbb{R}
and
|I|
for its Lebesgue measure. Recall that
f_{\omega}
for almost all
\omega\in G
is a locally integrable function on
\mathbb{R}
with
f_{\omega}(t)=f(\omega\beta(t))
for almost all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and so
\overline{M}(f)(\omega)
is defined almost everywhere.

Note that the definition of
\overline{M}
actually depends on the choice of the Dirichlet group
(G,\beta)
,
although for simplicity our notation does not indicate this. Moreover observe that (
26
) for
(\mathbb{T},\beta_{\mathbb{T}})
precisely gives the classical Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator on
\mathbb{T}
(see e.g.
[
16
, (3), p. 241]
).

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
(G,\beta)
be a Dirichlet group. Then the sublinear operator
\overline{M}
is bounded from
L_{1}(G)
to
L_{1,\infty}(G)
and from
L_{p}(G)
to
L_{p}(G)
, whenever
1<p\leq\infty.

In Section
3.2
we see that the proof of Theorem
2.1
reduces to Theorem
2.10
.
The following corollary is a consequence of independent interest.

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
f\in L_{1}(G)
. Then for almost all
\omega\in G
we have

(27)

\lim_{T\to 0}\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}f_{\omega}(t)dt=f(\omega),

and

(28)

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}f_{\omega}(t)\penalty\ dt=\widehat{f}(0).

Note that (
27
) may be interpret as a ’differentiation theorem’ for integrable functions on Dirichlet groups.

Given
1\leq p<\infty
, recall that the so-called Marcinkiewicz space
\mathcal{M}_{p}(\mathbb{R})
of all
f\in L_{loc}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
for which

\|f\|_{p}:=\limsup_{T\to\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}|f(t)|^{p}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

exists, contains all trigonometric polynomials of the form
q(t):=\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}e^{-itx_{n}}
with
x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
for
1\leq n\leq N
, and in this case we have that

\|f\|_{p}=\lim_{T\to\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}|f(t)|^{p}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

Then the Besicovitch space
\mathcal{B}_{p}(\mathbb{R})
is defined to be the closure of the trigonometric polynomials in
(\mathcal{M}_{p}(\mathbb{R}),\|\cdot\|_{p})
.
By density, we additionally have
\mathcal{B}_{p}(\mathbb{R})=L_{p}(\overline{\mathbb{R}})
. Moreover, from
[
4
]
we know that
L_{p}(G)\subset L_{p}(\overline{\mathbb{R}})
(isometrically) for all Dirichlet groups
(G,\beta)
, and so
L_{p}(G)\subset\mathcal{B}_{p}(\mathbb{R})
. Now given
f\in L_{p}(G)
, it seems difficult to determine the corresponding function in
\mathcal{B}_{p}(\mathbb{R})
. But we are going to deduce from
(
28
)
that at least for almost all
\omega\in G
the corresponding function for the translations
f(\omega\cdot)
is given by
f_{\omega}
.

Remark 2.12
.

Given
f\in L_{p}(G)
, then
f_{\omega}\in\mathcal{B}_{p}(\mathbb{R})
for almost all
\omega\in G
and

\|f_{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p}(\mathbb{R})}=\lim_{T\to\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}|f_{\omega}(t)|^{p}\penalty\ dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\|f\|_{L_{p}(G)}.

All proofs of this section are given in Section
3.1
and Section
3.3
.

2.4.
Norm approximation by first and second Riesz means

From
[
4
]
we know that for any frequency
\lambda
the sequence
(h_{\lambda_{n}})
forms a Schauder basis for
H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)
, provided
1<p<\infty
. Note that this
can also be seen as a straight forward consequence of the maximal inequality from Theorem
1.5
.

Consequently for
1<p<\infty
every function from
H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)
is approximated by its first and second Riesz means with respect to the norm. As shown by the next result, this for
p=1
and the first Riesz means is still true.

Theorem 2.13
.

Let
f\in H_{1}^{\lambda}(G)
. Then for all
k>0

(29)

\lim_{x\to\infty}R^{\lambda,k}_{x}(f)=f,

the limit taken with respect to the
H_{1}
-norm.

But for the frequencies
\lambda=(n)
and
\lambda=(\log n)
this result in general fails
if we replace first Riesz means by second Riesz means. This is shown exactly as in Remark
2.8
replacing almost everywhere convergence by convergence in the norm.

Remark 2.14
.

For
\lambda=(n)
and
\lambda=(\log n)
the Fourier series of some
f\in H_{1}^{\lambda}(G)
in general is not
(e^{\lambda},1)
-Riesz summable in the
H_{p}
-norm, i.e. the second
(\lambda,1)
-Riesz means do not approximate the function in the
H_{p}
-norm.

The proof of Theorem
2.13
is given in Section
3.6
.

2.5.
Uniformly almost periodic functions

Finally, we give an application of Corollary
2.6
(which follows from our main Theorem
2.1
) to uniformly almost periodic functions. We show that
H_{\infty}^{\lambda}(G)
may be identified isometrically and ’coefficient preserving’ with the space of all bounded and holomorphic functions on
[Re>0]
, which are uniformly almost periodic when restricted to any abscissa
[Re=\sigma],\sigma>0
.

Before we state the result, let us recall a few basic definitions and facts from the theory of almost periodic functions – we refer to
[
2
, Chapter III]
for more information.

A continuous function
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is said to be uniformly almost periodic (see
[
2
, pp.1-2]
)
if for every
\varepsilon>0
there is a number
l>0
such that for all intervals
I\subset\mathbb{R}
with
|I|=l
there is
\tau\in I
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|g(x+\tau)-g(x)|<\varepsilon.

Equivalently, a continuous function
g\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is uniformly almost periodic if and only if it is the uniform limit of trigonometric polynomials of the form
p(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{x_{n}}e^{-itx_{n}}
, where
x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
(see
[
2
,
2^{\circ}
Theorem, p. 29]
).

A holomorphic function
F
defined on an open strip
[\alpha<Re<\beta]
is said to be uniformly almost periodic (see
[
2
, pp.141-142]
)
if for every
\varepsilon>0
there is a number
l>0
such that for all intervals
I\subset\mathbb{R}
with
|I|=l
there is
\tau\in I
for which

\sup_{z\in[\alpha<Re<\beta]}|F(z+i\tau)-F(z)|<\varepsilon.

Obviously, this implies that for every

\alpha<\sigma<\beta
each of its restrictions
F_{\sigma}=F(\sigma+i\cdot):\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is uniformly almost periodic.

Moreover, in
[
2
,
4^{\circ}
Theorem, p. 142-143]
the following is proved: If a bounded and holomorphic function
F:[Re>0]\to\mathbb{C}

for some
\sigma_{0}>0

has a uniformly almost periodic restriction
F_{\sigma_{0}}=F(\sigma_{0}+i\cdot):\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
, then it is uniformly almost periodic on every possible smaller strip

[\alpha_{1}<Re<\beta_{1}]
with
0<\alpha_{1}<\beta_{1}<\infty
.

Fixing
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\sigma>0
, we call

a_{x}(F):=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}F_{\sigma}(t)e^{-(\sigma+it)x}dt

the
x
th Bohr coefficient of
F
; it appears that the definition of
a_{x}
is independent of the choice of
\sigma
(see
[
2
, p. 147]
). Moreover, it can be shown, that only at most countable many Bohr coefficients are non zero, and that
F
vanishes, whenever its Bohr coefficients vanish (see
[
2
, p. 148 and p. 18]
).

Note that finite sums of the form
F(z):=\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}
(all coefficients
\neq 0
) are typical examples of holomorphic, uniformly almost periodic functions, and then the coefficients
a_{k},\,1\leq k\leq N,
are precisely the (non-zero) Bohr coefficients of
F
.

Definition 2.15
.

Given a frequency
\lambda
, define
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\lambda}[Re>0]
to be the space of all bounded, holomorphic functions
F\colon[Re>0]\to\mathbb{C}
, which are uniformly almost periodic on all (or equivalently some) abscissa
[Re=\sigma],\sigma>0
and for which the Bohr coefficients
a_{x}(F)
vanish whenever
x\notin\lambda
.

Together with
\|F\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{[Re>0]}|F(z)|
the space
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\lambda}[Re>0]
becomes a Banach space, and we have
|a_{x}(F)|\leq\|F\|_{\infty}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Recall that, by
[
4
]
, if
\lambda
satisfies
(LC)
, then the space
H_{\infty}^{\lambda}(G)
equals
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
, which is the space of all
\lambda
-Dirichlet series
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
converging and defining a bounded function on
[Re>0]
. As proved in
[
19
]
, such Dirichlet series converge uniformly on ever half-plane
[Re>0],\sigma>0
, which implies that their limit functions belong to
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\lambda}[Re>0]
, i.e. under
(LC)
the Banach space
H_{\infty}^{\lambda}(G)
embeds isometrically into
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\lambda}[Re>0]
. Using Theorem
2.1
, we can show much more – for any
\lambda
both spaces may be identified ’coefficient preserving’.

Theorem 2.16
.

Let
\lambda
be an arbitrary frequency and
(G,\beta)
a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group. Then there is an onto isometry

\Psi\colon\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\lambda}[Re>0]\to H_{\infty}^{\lambda}(G),\penalty\ \penalty\ F\mapsto f,

which preserves Bohr and Fourier coefficients.

Note that Theorem
2.16
again proves that the definition of
H_{\infty}^{\lambda}(G)
is independent of the chosen
\lambda
-Dirichlet group
(G,\beta)
(see also
[
4
]
).

Remark 2.17
.

Recall from
[
19
]
that the space
\mathcal{D}^{ext}_{\infty}(\lambda)
denotes the space of all somewhere convergent Dirichlet series, which allow a bounded holomorphic extension to
[Re>0]
. By
[
4
]
, for any frequency
\lambda
there is an injective contraction
\Psi_{1}\colon\mathcal{D}^{ext}_{\infty}(\lambda)\hookrightarrow H_{\infty}^{\lambda}(G),\penalty\ \penalty\ D\mapsto f
, which preserves the Dirichlet and Fourier coefficients. On the other hand, by
[
19
]
the map
\Psi_{2}\colon\mathcal{D}^{ext}_{\infty}(\lambda)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\lambda}[Re>0],\penalty\ \penalty\ D\mapsto F,
where
F
is the limit function of
D
, defines an into isometry. Theorem
2.16
shows that
\Psi_{1}
is even isometric and that the mapping
\Psi
from Theorem
2.16
commutes with
\Psi_{1}
and
\Psi_{2}
, that is we have
\Psi_{1}=\Psi\circ\Psi_{2}.

Remark 2.18
.

To every function
F\in\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\lambda}[Re>0]
we may (formally) assign its Dirichlet series
D:=\sum a_{\lambda_{n}}(F)e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
. The question appears, under what conditions on
\lambda
does
D
converge on
[Re>0]
. Recall that, if
\lambda
satisfies Landau’s condition (
15
), then
D
even converges uniformly on
[Re>0]
(see e.g.
[
19
]
or
[
15
]
).

We finish with the following consequence of Theorem
2.16
. By
[
11
]
the Hardy space
H_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
isometrically equals the Banach space
H_{\infty}(B_{c_{0}})
of all bounded and holomorphic functions
F
on the open unit ball
B_{c_{0}}
of
c_{0}
, identifying Fourier and Taylor coefficients (see
[
3
, Theorem 5.1]
for details). Moreover, given
F\in H_{\infty}(B_{c_{0}})
, for every
u>0
the function

F_{u}(z):=F(\mathfrak{p}^{-u}z)\colon\mathbb{T}^{\infty}\to\mathbb{C}

is continuous and
\widehat{F_{u}}(\alpha)=c_{\alpha}(F)n^{-u}
whenever
n=\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha}
. Hence, if
f\in H_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
and
F\in H_{\infty}(B_{c_{0}})
are associated, then we have
f*p_{u}=F_{u}
, and so
f*p_{u}\in C(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
for every
u>0
. Theorem
2.16
extends this result to arbitrary
\lambda
-Dirichlet groups (compare also with Corollar
2.2
and Theorem
2.13
).

Corollary 2.19
.

Let
(G,\beta)
be a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group, and
f\in H_{\infty}^{\lambda}(G)
. Then
f*p_{u}\in C(G)
for all
u>0
, and for all
k>0

\lim_{x\to\infty}R_{x}^{\lambda,k}(f*p_{u})=f*p_{u}

uniformly on
G
.

The proof of Theorem
2.16
is in Section
3.6
.

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
2.10

Fixing
A>0
, consider the following ’graduated’
variant of
\overline{M}
, that is

(30)

\overline{M}_{A}(f)(\omega):=\sup_{I\subset[-A,A]}\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}|f_{\omega}(y)|dy,

where
f\in L_{1}(G)
.
Note that with this definition we have

(31)

\overline{M}(f)(\omega)=\sup_{I\subset\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}|f_{\omega}(t)|\penalty\ dt=\sup_{A>0}\overline{M}_{A}(f)(\omega)=\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{M}_{N}(f)(\omega).

Moreover, since for all intervals
I
the function

F(\omega,t):=\frac{1}{|I|}\chi_{I}(t)f(\omega\beta(t))\colon G\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

is measurable, by Fubini’s theorem for almost all
\omega\in G
the function

t\mapsto\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}f_{\omega}(t)dt

is measurable. Recall that the pointwise supremum of a countable family of measurable functions is again measurable. So, if we in the definition from (
30
) consider all intervals with rational boundary points, then
\overline{M}_{A}(f)
is measurable and it leads to the same operator. Indeed, if
I
is an non empty interval, then we are able to choose a sequence of subintervals
I_{n}\subset I
with rational boundary points, such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\big|I\setminus I_{n}\big|=0
. Then

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{1}{|I_{n}|}\int_{I_{n}}|f_{\omega}(t)|dt-\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}|f_{\omega}(t)|dt\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\frac{1}{|I_{n}|}\int_{I_{n}}|f_{\omega}(t)|dt-\frac{1}{|I|}\Big(\int_{I_{n}}|f_{\omega}(t)|dt+\int_{I\setminus I_{n}}|f_{\omega}(t)|dt\Big)\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\Big(\frac{1}{|I_{n}|}-\frac{1}{|I|}\Big)\int_{I_{n}}|f_{\omega}(t)|dt+\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I\setminus I_{n}}|f_{\omega}(t)|dt\Big|,

and so tending
n\to\infty
we obtain

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{|I_{n}|}\int_{I_{n}}f_{\omega}(t)dt=\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}f_{\omega}(t)dt.

Hence
\overline{M}_{A}(f)
is measurable for all
A>0
, and by
(\ref{friday})
we see that
\overline{M}(f)
is also measurable. Now Theorem
2.10
is a consequence of the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
f\in L_{1}(G)
. Then for all
A>0

m\Big(\left\{\omega\in G\mid\overline{M}_{A}(f)(\omega)>\alpha\right\}\Big)\leq\frac{10\|f\|_{1}}{\alpha}.

Proof.

Let
A>0
and fix
\alpha>0
. We define

\Omega(\alpha):=\left\{\omega\in G\mid\overline{M}_{A}(f)(\omega)>\alpha\right\}.

Then
\Omega(\alpha)
is measurable, since
\overline{M}_{A}(f)
is measurable. Moreover, for
\omega\in G
we define

\Omega_{\omega}(\alpha):=\{t\in[-A,A]\mid\omega\beta(t)\in\Omega(\alpha)\},

which is Lebesgue-measurable for almost all
\omega\in G
, since
\Omega(\alpha)
is measurable.
Hence by definition for all
t\in[-A,A]

\omega\beta(t)\in\Omega(\alpha)\Leftrightarrow t\in\Omega_{\omega}(t),

and so we obtain for all
A>0

\displaystyle m(\Omega(\alpha))

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\frac{1}{2A}\int_{-A}^{A}\chi_{\Omega(\alpha)}(\omega\beta(t))dtdm

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\frac{1}{2A}\int_{-A}^{A}\chi_{\Omega_{\omega}(\alpha)}(t)dtdm(\omega)=\int_{G}\frac{1}{2A}\eta(\Omega_{\omega}(\alpha))dm(\omega),

where
\eta
denotes the Lebesgue measure restricted to
[-A,A]
.
We now claim that

(32)

\eta(\Omega_{\omega}(\alpha))\leq\frac{5}{\alpha}\int_{-2A}^{2A}|f_{\omega}(t)|dt.

Indeed, if this estimate is verified, then we finally obtain

\displaystyle m(\Omega(\alpha))\leq\frac{10}{\alpha}\int_{G}\frac{1}{4A}\int_{-2A}^{2A}|f_{\omega}(t)|dtdm(\omega)=\frac{10}{\alpha}\|f\|_{1}.

So let us check (
32
). For every
t\in\Omega_{\omega}(\alpha)
(by definition) there is an open interval
I_{t}\in[-2A,2A]
containing
t
such that

(33)

\frac{1}{|I_{t}|}\int_{I_{t}}|f_{\omega}(y)|dy>\alpha.

By Vitali’s covering theorem (see e.g.
[
7
, Theorem 1.24, p. 36]
) there is a sequence
(t_{n})\subset\Omega_{\omega}(\alpha)
such that

\Omega_{\omega}(\alpha)\subset\bigcup_{t\in\Omega_{\alpha}}I_{t}\subset 5\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}I_{t_{n}},

where the latter union is disjoint. So by
(\ref{standardargument2})

\eta(\Omega_{\omega}(\alpha))\leq 5\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\eta\Big(I_{t_{n}}\Big)\leq\frac{5}{\alpha}\int_{\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}I_{t_{n}}}|f_{\omega}(y)|dy\leq\frac{5}{\alpha}\int_{-2A}^{2A}|f_{\omega}(y)|dy.\qed

Finally, we are ready to give the

Proof of Theorem
2.10
.

Take
f\in L_{1}(G)
and
\alpha>0
, and define for
N\in\mathbb{N}

\Omega(\alpha):=\{\omega\in G\mid\overline{M}(f)(\omega)>\alpha\},\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \Omega_{N}(\alpha):=\{\omega\in G\mid\overline{M}_{N}(f)(\omega)>\alpha\}.

Then

\Omega(\alpha)=\bigcup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\Omega_{N}(\alpha)

and, since
\Omega_{N}(\alpha)\subset\Omega_{N+1}(\alpha)
for all
N
, we by Lemma
3.1
have

m(\Omega(\alpha))=\lim_{N\to\infty}m(\Omega_{N}(\alpha))\leq\frac{10\|f\|_{1}}{\alpha}.

The case
p=\infty
follows directly from the fact, that
\|f_{\omega}\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}=\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(G)}
(see
[
4
, Lemma 3.10]
). Now Marcinkiewicz’s interpolation theorem (see e.g.
[
9
, Theorem 1.3.2., p. 34]
) gives the claim for
1<p<\infty
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\lambda
be a frequency and
(G,\beta)
a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group. Then
for every
k>0
there is a constant
C(k)>0

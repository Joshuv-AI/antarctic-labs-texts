Global Convergence of Hager–Zhang type Riemannian Conjugate Gradient Method
Hiroyuki Sakai, Hiroyuki Sato, Hideaki Iiduka

Abstract
This paper presents the Hager–Zhang (HZ)-type Riemannian conjugate gradient method that uses the exponential retraction. We also present global convergence analyses of our proposed method under two kinds of assumptions. Moreover, we numerically compare our proposed methods with the existing methods by solving two kinds of Riemannian optimization problems on the unit sphere. The numerical results show that our proposed method has much better performance than the existing methods, i.e., the FR, DY, PRP and HS methods. In particular, they show that it has much higher performance than existing methods including the hybrid ones in computing the stability number of graphs problem.

Introduction

Riemannian optimization has been widely researched along with the developments of real-world applications in various fields,
such as natural language processing
[

]
, signal processing
[

]
,
and computer vision

Many useful gradient methods
[
1
,
16
]
have been developed for Riemannian optimization that can be obtained by extending the existing methods in Euclidean space to a Riemannian manifold.
However, such extension is not always easy.
For example, in the Euclidean space setting, the
(k+1)
-th approximation of optimal solutions is
\bm{x}_{k+1}=\bm{x}_{k}+\alpha_{k}\bm{\eta}_{k}
,
where
\alpha_{k}>0
,
\bm{x}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a point at the
k

\bm{\eta}_{k}
is the search direction.
However, such an update cannot be defined for general Riemannian manifolds
because of nonlinear Riemannian geometric structure.
We can generalize Riemannian gradient methods using
retractions
and
transports
that are divided into various types, as described below.

Smith
[
18
]
proposed using
exponential retraction
and
parallel transport
to generalize the optimization methods from Euclidean space to a Riemannian manifold.
Absil, Mahony, and Sepulchre
[
1
]
proposed using a
general retraction
that approximates the exponential retraction
and a
vector transport
which approximates the parallel transport.
Note that a general retraction (resp. vector transport) is a generalization of the exponential retraction (resp. parallel transport).

We focus on
Riemannian conjugate gradient (RCG) methods
as they offer both theoretical and practical benefits.
A theoretical benefit of RCG methods is that we can show that they generate
sufficient descent search directions
,
which decrease an objective function at every iteration, and converge
globally
, i.e., without depending on the choice of the initial point.
A practical benefit of RCG methods is that they have efficient numerical performances, as shown in the previous studies
[
1
,
16
]
.

Contributions

This paper makes two contributions.

1.4
Difficulty to prove Theorem
3.3

A way to guarantee the global convergence property of the HZ-type ECG method in the Euclidean space is to assume the strong convexity of the objective function
f
.
We thus assume that there exists a constant
\mu>0
such that

(\nabla f(\bm{x})-\nabla f(\bm{y}))^{\top}(\bm{x}-\bm{y})\geq\mu\left\lVert{\bm{x}-\bm{y}}\right\rVert^{2}

(1)

holds for any
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which is equivalent to the condition that the smallest eigenvalue of the Hessian
\nabla^{2}f(\bm{x})
for any
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is not less than
\mu
.
Expression (
1
) is more useful in convergence analysis.

However, (
1
) cannot be directly generalized to the Riemannian case.
Instead, a natural definition of the strong convexity of
f
on a Riemannian manifold
M
is that there exists a constant
\mu>0
such that, for any
x\in M
, the smallest eigenvalue of the Riemannian Hessian
\Hess f(x)
is not less than
\mu
.
In Theorem
3.3
, we have to start with this condition and without a Riemannian counterpart of (
1
).

Noting that (
1
) is used to prove
(\nabla f(\bm{x}_{k+1})-\nabla f(\bm{x}_{k}))^{\top}(\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}_{k})\geq\mu\alpha_{k}\left\lVert{\bm{\eta}_{k}}\right\rVert^{2}
in Euclidean space,
we need to show the Riemannian counterpart of this inequality, not that of (
1
).
Fortunately, by imposing the Wolfe conditions on the step length, we can directly prove the desired inequality (
27
) from the assumption of the strong convexity of
f
, i.e., the condition that the eigenvalue of the Riemannian Hessian is uniformly lower bounded.

To generalize line search optimization algorithms to Riemannian manifolds, the notions of a retraction and a vector transport are used.

Definition 2.1

(Retraction)
.

A retraction (see
[
1
, Chapter 4, Definition 4.1.1]
) is a smooth map

that has the following properties.

•

R_{x}(0_{x})=x

•

\displaystyle(dR_{x})_{0_{x}}(\xi)=\xi

for all

and

denotes the restriction of

Definition 2.2

(Vector transport)
.

A vector transport (see
[
1
, Chapter 8, Definition 8.1.1]

that has the following properties.

•

There exists a retraction

, such that
\mathcal{T}_{\eta}(\xi)\in T_{R_{x}(\eta)}M
for all

\mathcal{T}_{0_{x}}(\xi)=\xi
for all

•

\mathcal{T}_{\eta}(a\xi+b\zeta)=a\mathcal{T}_{\eta}(\xi)+b\mathcal{T}_{\eta}(\zeta)

and for all

\left\langle{g_{0},\eta_{0}}\right\rangle_{x_{0}}=-\left\lVert{g_{0}}\right\rVert_{x_{0}}^{2}
.
Subsequently, we assume
k\geq 1
.
If
\beta_{k}=\beta_{k}^{\mathrm{HZ}}
, from
[

, (
21
) follows.
On the other hand, if
\beta_{k}\neq\beta_{k}^{\mathrm{HZ}}
, then
\beta_{k}^{\mathrm{HZ}}\leq\beta_{k}\leq 0
.
From (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\left\langle{g_{k},\eta_{k}}\right\rangle_{x_{k}}=-\left\lVert{g_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}^{2}+\beta_{k}\left\langle{g_{k},\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k-1}\eta_{k-1}}(\eta_{k-1})}\right\rangle_{x_{k}}.

If
\left\langle{g_{k},\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k-1}\eta_{k-1}}(\eta_{k-1})}\right\rangle_{x_{k}}\geq 0
, then (
21
) follows immediately since
\beta_{k}\leq 0
.
If
\left\langle{g_{k},\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k-1}\eta_{k-1}}(\eta_{k-1})}\right\rangle_{x_{k}}<0

\displaystyle\left\langle{g_{k},\eta_{k}}\right\rangle_{x_{k}}

\displaystyle=-\left\lVert{g_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}^{2}+\beta_{k}\left\langle{g_{k},\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k-1}\eta_{k-1}}(\eta_{k-1})}\right\rangle_{x_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq-\left\lVert{g_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}^{2}+\beta^{\mathrm{HZ}}_{k}\left\langle{g_{k},\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k-1}\eta_{k-1}}(\eta_{k-1})}\right\rangle_{x_{k}},

since
\beta_{k}^{\mathrm{HZ}}\leq\beta_{k}\leq 0
.
Hence, (
21
) follows by analysis as in
[
12
, Theorem 3.4]
.
∎

The following is the main theorem indicating that the HZ-type RCG method converges globally.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\{x_{k}\}_{k=0,1,\cdots}\subset M
be a sequence generated by Algorithm
3.1
with
\beta_{k+1}=\beta_{k+1}^{\mathrm{HZ}}

under Assumptions
3.1
and
3.2
.
Suppose that each step size
\alpha_{k}>0
satisfies Wolfe conditions (
10
) and (
11
).
Then either
\left\lVert{g_{k_{0}}}\right\rVert_{x_{k_{0}}}=0
for some
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, or

\displaystyle~\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert{g_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}=0.

(22)

Proof.

If
g_{k_{0}}=0
for some
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, then (
22
) obviously follows.
Assume that
g_{k}\neq 0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
From (
14
) and (
11
), we have

\displaystyle(c_{2}-1)\left\langle{g_{k},\eta_{k}}\right\rangle_{x_{k}}\leq\left\langle{g_{k+1}-\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}}(g_{k}),\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}}(\eta_{k})}\right\rangle_{x_{k+1}}.

Moreover, (
20
) and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality imply

\displaystyle\left\langle{g_{k+1}-\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}}(g_{k}),\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}}(\eta_{k})}\right\rangle_{x_{k+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{g_{k+1}-\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}}(g_{k})}\right\rVert_{x_{k+1}}\left\lVert{\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}}(\eta_{k})}\right\rVert_{x_{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert{g_{k+1}-\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}}(g_{k})}\right\rVert_{x_{k+1}}\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq L\alpha_{k}\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}^{2}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle(c_{2}-1)\left\langle{g_{k},\eta_{k}}\right\rangle_{x_{k}}\leq L\alpha_{k}\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}^{2},

which with Theorem
3.2
implies

\displaystyle~\alpha_{k}\geq\frac{1-c_{2}}{L}\frac{\left|{\left\langle{g_{k},\eta_{k}}\right\rangle_{x_{k}}}\right|}{\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}^{2}}.

(23)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\left\langle{y_{k},\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}}(\eta_{k})}\right\rangle_{x_{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle{g_{k+1}-\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}}(g_{k}),\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}}(\eta_{k})}\right\rangle_{x_{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle{g_{k+1},\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}}(\eta_{k})}\right\rangle_{x_{k+1}}-\left\langle{\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}}(g_{k}),\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}}(\eta_{k})}\right\rangle_{x_{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle{g_{k+1},\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}}(\eta_{k})}\right\rangle_{x_{k+1}}-\left\langle{g_{k},\eta_{k}}\right\rangle_{x_{k}}

\displaystyle\geq c_{2}\left\langle{g_{k},\eta_{k}}\right\rangle_{x_{k}}-\left\langle{g_{k},\eta_{k}}\right\rangle_{x_{k}}

\displaystyle=(c_{2}-1)\left\langle{g_{k},\eta_{k}}\right\rangle_{x_{k}},

(24)

where the third equation comes from (
14
), and the inequality comes from (
11
).
Furthermore, we define
\phi_{x,\mu}(t):=f(\exp_{x}(t\mu))
.
From Taylor’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle f(x_{k+1})-f(x_{k})

\displaystyle=f(\exp_{x_{k}}(\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}))-f(x_{k})

\displaystyle=\phi_{x_{k},\frac{\eta_{k}}{\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}}}(\alpha_{k}\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}})-\phi_{x_{k},\frac{\mu_{k}}{\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}}}(0)

\displaystyle=\phi^{\prime}_{x_{k},\frac{\eta_{k}}{\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}}}(0)\alpha_{k}\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}+\frac{1}{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}_{x_{k},\frac{\eta_{k}}{\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}}}(\theta)(\alpha_{k}\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}})^{2},

for some
\theta\in[0,\alpha_{k}\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}]
.
Moreover, by defining
c_{x,\mu}(t):=\exp_{x}(t\mu)
, we have

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime\prime}_{x_{k},\frac{\eta_{k}}{\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}}}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\Hess f(x_{k})\left[c^{\prime}_{x_{k},\frac{\eta_{k}}{\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}}}(\theta)\right],c^{\prime}_{x_{k},\frac{\eta_{k}}{\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}}}(\theta)}\right\rangle_{c_{x_{k},\frac{\eta_{k}}{\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}}}(\theta)}

\displaystyle\geq\mu\left\lVert{c^{\prime}_{x_{k},\frac{\eta_{k}}{\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}}}(\theta)}\right\rVert_{c_{x_{k},\frac{\eta_{k}}{\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}}}(\theta)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mu\left\lVert{\frac{\eta_{k}}{\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mu

from the strong convexity of
f
.
Using this evaluation of
\phi^{\prime\prime}
, we obtain

\displaystyle f(x_{k+1})-f(x_{k})

\displaystyle\geq\left\langle{g_{k},\frac{\eta_{k}}{\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}}}\right\rangle_{x_{k}}\alpha_{k}\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}+\frac{\mu}{2}(\alpha_{k}\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}})^{2}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}\left\langle{g_{k},\eta_{k}}\right\rangle_{x_{k}}+\frac{\mu}{2}(\alpha_{k}\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}})^{2}

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle~f(x_{k+1})-f(x_{k})\geq\alpha_{k}\left\langle{g_{k},\eta_{k}}\right\rangle_{x_{k}}+\frac{\mu}{2}(\alpha_{k}\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}})^{2}.

(25)

From (
10
) and (
25
), we have

\displaystyle~\left\langle{g_{k},\eta_{k}}\right\rangle_{x_{k}}\leq\frac{\mu}{2(c_{1}-1)}\alpha_{k}\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}^{2}.

(26)

Therefore, from (
24
) and (
26

\displaystyle~\left\langle{y_{k},\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}}(\eta_{k})}\right\rangle_{x_{k+1}}\geq\gamma\alpha_{k}\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}^{2},

(27)

where

\displaystyle\gamma:=\frac{\mu(1-c_{2})}{2(1-c_{1})}.

The assumption
g_{k}\neq 0
implies that
\eta_{k}\neq 0
,
which together with
\alpha_{k}>0
yield
\left\langle{y_{k},\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}}(\eta_{k})}\right\rangle_{x_{k+1}}\neq 0
.
From (
10
) and the lower boundedness of
f
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{1}\alpha_{k}\left\langle{g_{k},\eta_{k}}\right\rangle_{x_{k}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(f(x_{k+1})-f(x_{k}))

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to+\infty}f(x_{j})-f(x_{0})>-\infty,

which implies

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}\left\langle{g_{k},\eta_{k}}\right\rangle_{x_{k}}>-\infty.

Combining this with the lower bound for
\alpha_{k}
given in (
23
) and the sufficient descent property in Theorem
3.2
gives

\displaystyle~\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left\lVert{g_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}^{4}}{\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}^{2}}<\infty.

(28)

From (
20
), we obtain

\displaystyle~\left\lVert{y_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k+1}}=\left\lVert{g_{k+1}-\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}}(g_{k})}\right\rVert_{x_{k+1}}\leq L\alpha_{k}\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}.

(29)

From (
15
), (
16
), (
27
) and (
29
), we have

\displaystyle\left|{\beta_{k+1}^{\mathrm{HZ}}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|{\frac{\left\langle{g_{k+1},y_{k}}\right\rangle_{x_{k+1}}}{\left\langle{y_{k},\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}}(\eta_{k})}\right\rangle_{x_{k+1}}}-\mu\frac{\left\lVert{y_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k+1}}^{2}\left\langle{g_{k+1},\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}}(\eta_{k})}\right\rangle_{x_{k+1}}}{\left\langle{y_{k},\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}}(\eta_{k})}\right\rangle_{x_{k+1}}^{2}}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\lVert{g_{k+1}}\right\rVert_{x_{k+1}}\left\lVert{y_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k+1}}}{\gamma\alpha_{k}\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}^{2}}+\mu\frac{\left\lVert{y_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k+1}}^{2}\left\lVert{g_{k+1}}\right\rVert_{x_{k+1}}\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}}{\gamma^{2}\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}^{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L\alpha_{k}\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}\left\lVert{g_{k+1}}\right\rVert_{x_{k+1}}}{\gamma\alpha_{k}\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}^{2}}+\mu\frac{L^{2}\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}^{3}\left\lVert{g_{k+1}}\right\rVert_{x_{k+1}}}{\gamma^{2}\alpha_{k}^{2}\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}^{4}}

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\eta_{k+1}}\right\rVert_{x_{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert{-g_{k+1}+\beta_{k+1}^{\mathrm{HZ}}\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}}(\eta_{k})}\right\rVert_{x_{k+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{g_{k+1}}\right\rVert+\left|{\beta_{k+1}^{\mathrm{HZ}}}\right|\left\lVert{\eta_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left\lVert{g_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}^{2}<\infty,

3.3
Comparison of HZ-type ECG method with HZ-type RCG method

Let us consider
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then,
\beta_{k+1}^{\mathrm{HZ}}
defined by (
15
) can be expressed

\displaystyle\beta_{k+1}^{\mathrm{HZ}}

\displaystyle=\beta^{\mathrm{HS}}_{k+1}-\mu\frac{\left\lVert{y_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k+1}}^{2}\left\langle{g_{k+1},\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}}(\eta_{k})}\right\rangle_{x_{k+1}}}{\left\langle{y_{k},\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{k}\eta_{k}}(\eta_{k})}\right\rangle_{x_{k+1}}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\bm{y}_{k}^{\top}\bm{g}_{k+1}}{\bm{\eta}_{k}^{\top}\bm{y}_{k}}-\mu\frac{\|\bm{y}_{k}\|^{2}\bm{g}_{k+1}^{\top}\bm{\eta}_{k}}{(\bm{y}_{k}^{\top}\bm{\eta}_{k})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\bm{\eta}_{k}^{\top}\bm{y}_{k}}\left(\bm{y}_{k}-\mu\frac{\|\bm{y}_{k}\|^{2}}{\bm{\eta}_{k}^{\top}\bm{y}_{k}}\bm{\eta}_{k}\right)^{\top}\bm{g}_{k+1},

which implies that
\beta_{k+1}^{\mathrm{HZ}}
defined by (
15
) with
\mu=2
coincides with (1.3) in
[
4
]
used in the HZ-type ECG method.
Inequality (
21
) with
\mu=2
(see Theorem
3.2
) is the sufficient descent property of the HZ-type RCG method; i.e.,

\displaystyle\left\langle{g_{k},\eta_{k}}\right\rangle_{x_{k}}\leq-\frac{7}{8}\left\lVert{g_{k}}\right\rVert_{x_{k}}^{2},

which, together with
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, implies (1.9) in Theorem 1.1 of
[
4
]
:

\displaystyle\bm{g}_{k}^{\top}\bm{\eta}_{k}\leq-\frac{7}{8}\left\lVert{\bm{g}_{k}}\right\rVert^{2}.

Accordingly, Theorem
3.2
is a natural extended result of Theorem 1.1 in
[
4
]
to a Riemannian manifold.

Theorem 2.2 in
[
4
]
implies that the HZ-type ECG method converges globally if the Wolfe conditions hold and if

•

f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is strongly convex with a constant
c>0
and
\nabla f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

is Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
L>0
on the level set
\mathcal{L}:=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon f(\bm{x}_{0})\leq f(\bm{x})\}
.

Theorem
3.3
in this paper is satisfied under the Wolfe conditions and Assumptions
3.1
and
3.2
, i.e.,

(i)

f\colon M\to\mathbb{R}
is smooth and bounded below;
f\circ\exp_{x_{k}}\colon T_{x_{k}}M\to\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz continuously differentiable on
\mathrm{span}\{\eta_{k}\}
with uniform Lipschitz constant
L>0
;

(ii)

There exists a constant
L>0
such that, for all
x,y\in M
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\grad f(x)-\mathcal{T}_{X}(\grad f(y))}\right\rVert_{x}\leq Ld(x,y),

(30)

where
X\in T_{y}M
satisfies
x=\exp_{y}(X)
. Furthermore,
f
is strongly convex; i.e., there exists constant
\mu>0
such that, for all
x\in M
, the smallest eigenvalue of the Riemannian Hessian
\Hess f(x)
is not less than
\mu
.

Under the Euclidean space setting,
f
is strongly convex with a constant
c
if and only if the smallest eigenvalue of
\nabla^{2}f(\bm{x})
for any
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is not less than
c
.
Moreover, (
30
) in the Euclidean space setting is the same as the existence of
L>0
such that, for all
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla f(\bm{x})-\nabla f(\bm{y})\|\leq L\|\bm{x}-\bm{y}\|,

(31)

that is,
\nabla f
is Lipschitz continuous. Obviously, the strong convexity of
f
implies that
f
is bounded below.
Following Assumption 4.1 and Remark 4.1 in
[
15
]
, we can see that, in the Euclidean space setting,
the Lipschitz continuity of
f\circ\exp_{x_{k}}
(see (i)) is equivalent to (
31
). Therefore, Theorem
3.3
is a natural extended result of Theorem 2.2 in [3] to a Riemannian manifold.

5
Conclusion

We have presented a Hager–Zhang (HZ)-type Riemannian conjugate gradient method that uses exponential retraction and presented two global convergence properties under different assumptions.
We numerically compared the performance of the proposed method with those of existing Riemannian conjugate gradient methods for two Riemannian optimization problems on the unit sphere.
The results show that the HZ method has much better performance than the FR, DY, PRP, and HS methods.
In particular, we showed that the HZ method is suitable for the stability number of graphs problem.
It had much better performance than existing methods, including hybrid methods, for computing the stability number.

In a future paper, we will present the HZ method using a general retraction and its convergence analyses.

Abstract
We consider a reaction-diffusion model with a drift term in a bounded domain. Given a time T , \displaystyle T, we prove the existence and uniqueness of an initial datum that maximizes the total mass ∫ Ω u ⁡ ( T , x ) ​ 𝑑 x \displaystyle\textstyle{\int_{\Omega}u(T,x)\mathrm{d}x} in the presence of an advection term. In a population dynamics context, this optimal initial datum can be understood as the best distribution of the initial population that leads to a maximal the total population at a prefixed time T . \displaystyle T. We also compare the total masses at a time T \displaystyle T in two cases: depending on whether an advection term is present in the medium or not. We prove that the presence of a large enough advection enhances the total mass.



Keywords

optimal initial data, optimization in semilinear parabolic equations, maximal mass, enhancement by advection

Introduction and main results

In this paper, we study an optimization problem associated with a reaction-diffusion model that involves an advection term. The model reads

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u-\sigma\Delta u-q\cdot\nabla_{x}u=f(u)&\text{in $\displaystyle(0,T)\times\Omega$},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)&\text{in $\displaystyle\Omega,$}\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}(t,x)=0,&\text{for all $\displaystyle t\in(0,T)$ and $\displaystyle x\in\partial\Omega$},\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
\displaystyle\Omega
is a bounded, connected and smooth domain of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N},

\displaystyle\sigma>0
is a positive constant (standing for the diffusivity of the medium) and
\displaystyle\nu(x)
is the outward unit normal to
\displaystyle\partial\Omega

\displaystyle x.

Model (

Our present work aims to answer natural questions related to the maximization of the total mass
\displaystyle\textstyle{\int_{\Omega}u(T,x)~\rm{d}x}

\displaystyle T>0.
More precisely, at a prefixed time
\displaystyle T>0,
our goal is to maximize the functional

\mathcal{I}_{T}(u_{0}):=\int_{\Omega}u(T,x)\,\mathrm{d}x

Proof of Lemma

.

To prove (

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\int_{\Omega}1\,\,\mathrm{d}x=|\Omega|.

Hence,
\displaystyle u\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)\right)\cap L^{2}\left(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)\right).

\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{t}\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\mathrm{d}t+\sigma\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(q\cdot\nabla u)u\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\

\displaystyle U^{n}

\displaystyle\star

\displaystyle U
in
\displaystyle L^{\infty}\left(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)\right).
That is,

\begin{array}[]{c}\forall\varphi\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)\right),\\
\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}U^{n}(t,x)\varphi(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\to\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}U(t,x)\varphi(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\text{ as }n\rightarrow+\infty.\end{array}

Also, from (

such that
\displaystyle\partial_{t}U^{n}
converges, in the weak
\displaystyle\star
sense, to
\displaystyle v\in L^{2}\left(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega)\right).
That is, for all

We note that, for all

Passing to the limit as

leads to
\displaystyle\partial_{t}U=v.

For
\displaystyle\varphi\in H^{1}\left(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)\right),
such that
\displaystyle\varphi(0,x)=0
for all
\displaystyle x\in\Omega,
we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}U^{n}(t,x)\varphi(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\\

Passing to the limit as

\begin{array}[]{l}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}v(t,x)\varphi(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\\

as

Thus,

a.e in
\displaystyle\Omega.

For

Proof of Theorem

.

First, we will prove that
\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{T}
is strictly concave. Let
\displaystyle\lambda\in(0,1)
and let
\displaystyle U
be the solution of (
1
) with the initial condition
\displaystyle\lambda U_{1}^{0}(x)+(1-\lambda)U_{2}^{0}(x)
and let
\displaystyle v(t,x)=\lambda U_{1}(t,x)+(1-\lambda)U_{2}(t,x),
where
\displaystyle U_{1}
and
\displaystyle U_{2}
are the solutions of (
1
) with initial condition
\displaystyle U_{1}^{0}(x)
and
\displaystyle U_{2}^{0}(x)
respectively. As
\displaystyle f
is strictly concave, we have

\partial_{t}v-\sigma\Delta v-q\cdot\nabla_{x}v=\lambda f(U_{1})+(1-\lambda)f(U_{2})<f(\lambda U_{1}+(1-\lambda)U_{2})=f(v).

From the equation satisfied by
\displaystyle U
and the inequality satisfied by
\displaystyle v,
and since
\displaystyle f
is of class
\displaystyle C^{1},
it follows that
\displaystyle w:=v-U
satisfies

\partial_{t}w-\sigma\Delta w-q\cdot\nabla_{x}w<f(v(t,x))-f(U(t,x)):=b(t,x)w,

(29)

where
\displaystyle b(t,x)
is a bounded continuous function obtained from the fact that
\displaystyle f
is
\displaystyle C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
(hence Lipschitz) and
\displaystyle 0<v,U<1.
Namely,

b(t,x)=\begin{cases}\frac{f(v(t,x))-f(U(t,x))}{v(t,x)-U(t,x)},\quad\text{ if }\quad v(t,x)\neq U(t,x)\vskip 7.0pt\\
f^{\prime}(v(t,x))\text{ if }\quad v(t,x)=U(t,x).\end{cases}

Moreover,

w(0,x)=v(0,x)-U(0,x)=0\text{ for all }x\in\Omega,~\text{ and }\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}=0\text{ on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega.

Owing to the strong parabolic comparison principle on the function
\displaystyle w
(see Theorem 7 and the remark afterwords in
[
15
]
, for example), we conclude that
\displaystyle w\leq 0
in
\displaystyle[0,T]\times\Omega.
That is,
\displaystyle v\leq U
in
\displaystyle[0,T]\times\Omega.

Now, we claim that
\displaystyle v(T,x)<U(T,x)
in an open set contained in
\displaystyle\Omega.
Suppose to the contrary that
\displaystyle v(T,x)=U(T,x)
in
\displaystyle\Omega.
We have

\partial_{t}v-\sigma\Delta v-q\cdot\nabla_{x}v-f(v)<\partial_{t}u-\sigma\Delta U-q\cdot\nabla U-f(U)=0,

and since
\displaystyle v(T,x)=U(T,x),
we can reduce
\displaystyle f(U)
and
\displaystyle f(v)
for
\displaystyle t=T
to get

\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}v(T,x)-\sigma\Delta v(T,x)-q(x)\cdot\nabla_{x}v(T,x)\\
<\partial_{t}U(T,x)-\sigma\Delta U(T,x)-q(x)\cdot\nabla U(T,x).\end{array}

Again, since
\displaystyle v(T,x)=U(T,x),
we have

\Delta v(T,x)=\Delta U(T,x)\text{ and }\nabla_{x}v(T,x)=\nabla_{x}U(T,x).

This implies that

\partial_{t}v(T,x)<\partial_{t}U(T,x).

Hence,
\displaystyle\partial_{t}(u-v)(T,x)>0
which means that
\displaystyle(U-v)(T,x)
is increasing at
\displaystyle t=T.
Then, there exists
\displaystyle\varepsilon>0
such that

U(T-\varepsilon,x)-v(T-\varepsilon,x)<U(T,x)-v(T,x)=0.

This contradicts the fact that
\displaystyle v(t,x)\leq U(t,x)
and proves our claim.
Therefore,

\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathcal{I}_{T}(\lambda U_{1}^{0}+(1-\lambda)U_{2}^{0})&=\mathcal{I}_{T}(\lambda U_{1}^{0}(x)+(1-\lambda)U_{2}^{0}(x))\\
&=\int_{\Omega}U(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\vskip 5.0pt>\int_{\Omega}v(T,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\lambda\mathcal{I}_{T}(U_{1}^{0})+(1-\lambda)\mathcal{I}_{T}(U_{2}^{0}).\end{array}

This means that
\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{T}
is strictly concave and guarantees the uniqueness of the maximal element.

In order to prove the uniqueness of the above maximal element, we suppose that there exists
\displaystyle V_{1}^{0}
and
\displaystyle V_{2}^{0}
in
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{m},
such that both are maximizers of
\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{T}
in
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{m}.
Then,

Let
\displaystyle\mu
be such that
\displaystyle 0<\mu<1.
We then have

because
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}V(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=m.
Since
\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{T}
is strictly concave, it follows that

\mathcal{I}_{T}(V)>\mu\mathcal{I}_{T}(V_{1}^{0})+(1-\mu)\mathcal{I}_{T}(V_{2}^{0})=\sup_{u_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{m}}\mathcal{I}_{T}(u_{0}).

However, this contradicts the fact that
\displaystyle V_{1}^{0}
and
\displaystyle V_{2}^{0}
are maximizers of
\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{T}
in
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{m}.
Therefore, the maximizer of
\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{T}
is unique in the set
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{m}.

Remark 2

We mention that the right hand side of (

\displaystyle A

does not diverge to

when

bounded below by

and above by

as
\displaystyle 0\leq U_{A}\leq 1

. In fact, if
\displaystyle\{A_{n}\}_{n}
is a sequence that goes to

\displaystyle U_{A_{n}}

\displaystyle q

(

]
, for e.g.
)

The latter guarantees that the right hand side remains bounded even when the amplitude

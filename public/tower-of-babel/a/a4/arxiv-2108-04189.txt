Truncated Wigner approximation as a non-positive Kraus map
A.B. Klimov, I. Sainz, J.L. Romero

Abstract
We show that the Truncated Wigner Approximation developed in the flat phase-space is mapped into a Lindblad-type evolution with an indefinite metric in the space of linear operators. As a result, the classically evolved Wigner function corresponds to a non-positive operator R ^ ​ ( t ) \hat{R}(t) , which does not describe a physical state. The rate of appearance of negative eigenvalues of R ^ ​ ( t ) \hat{R}(t) can be efficiently estimated. The short-time dynamics of the Kerr and second harmonic generation Hamiltonains are discussed.

Introduction

The Liouvillian, or Truncated Wigner Approximation (TWA) is one of the most
popular semiclassical approximations, which has been widely used in numerous
applications (see

According to the general ideas of the phase-space mapping, every operator

\hat{f}\Leftrightarrow W_{f}(\Omega)

This procedure allows the computation of average values of any

by convoluting

with the symbol of the density matrix
W_{\rho}(\Omega)
(the Wigner function). The von Neumann evolution
equation for the density matrix is mapped into the Moyal equation

\partial_{t}W_{\rho}(\Omega|t)=\{W_{\rho}(\Omega|t),W_{H}(\Omega)\}_{M},\;

(2)

where

is the symbol of the Hamiltonian and

denote the
Moyal brackets. Equation (

derivatives on the phase-space coordinates, which makes its solution a
difficult task. Nevertheless, Eq.(

a semiclassical parameter

, and acquires the Liouvillian
form,

\partial_{t}W_{\rho}(\Omega)\approx\epsilon\{W_{\rho}(\Omega),W_{H}(\Omega)\}_{P},

(3)

to leading order in

is the Poisson bracket on

. The solution of Eq.(

exact Wigner function

as a “classically evolve”,

W_{\rho}(\Omega|t)\approx W_{\rho}(\Omega^{cl}(-t)),

where

denotes classical trajectories, is known as the
Truncated Wigner Approximation (TWA). This approximation describes the
propagation of every point of the initial distribution along the
corresponding classical trajectory. The evolution of average values are
computed by integrating symbols of operators with
W_{\rho}(\Omega^{cl}(-t))
,

\langle\hat{f}(t)\rangle\approx\int d\Omega W_{f}(\Omega)W_{\rho}(\Omega^{cl}(-t)).

(4)

Initially applied to the analysis of semiclassical dynamics of quantum
systems in the flat

distribution (interestingly, a similar behavior is also observed for some
dissipative scenarios

dynamic symmetry group the harmonic evolution is generated by Hamiltonians
linear in the group generators. In this case the initial distribution is
rigidly displaced (i.e. without distortion) in the corresponding phase-space

transformations.

Although formally speaking, TWA fails from the very beginning in case of
non-linear evolution

(since the classical dynamics preserves the
phase-space area,), it describes relatively well a short-time non-linear
dynamics of smooth and localized distributions (representing the so-called

In the present paper we will analyze the evolution equation for
\hat{R}(t)

and show that it has an indefinite Lindblad form, i.e. contains positive and
negative Lindblad-like terms. In other words, an anharmonic classical
dynamics, if viewed from the quantum point of view, corresponds to a very

quantum evolution which results in a

\hat{R}(t)

equation and the physical implications of its non-positivity. We show that
negative eigenvalues of the operator
\hat{R}(t)
appear already at
t=0^{+}

with the rate that depends both on the initial state and the degree of
non-linearity of the Hamiltonian.

Here we will focus only on the evolution in the flat phase-space, although a
similar approach can be applied to quantum systems with higher symmetries.

Appendix A

Here we show that the Liuovillian evolution in phase-space does not
correspond to quantum unitary dynamics.

The classical phase-space evolution of the Wigner function can be
symbolically described as

W_{\rho}\left(t\right)=e^{t\left\{W_{H},\cdot\right\}}W_{\rho}\left(0\right),

where
W_{H}(\alpha,\alpha^{\ast})
is the symbol of the Hamiltonian and
the flat space Poisson brackets has the form

\left\{\cdot,\cdot\right\}=i\left(\partial_{\alpha}\otimes\partial_{\alpha^{\ast}}-\partial_{\alpha^{\ast}}\otimes\partial_{\alpha}\right),

the symbol
\otimes
indicates the order of application of the derivatives.
The TWA evolution of the square of the density operator can be determined
in terms of the star-product as follows

W_{\rho^{2}}\left(t\right)=e^{-\frac{i}{2}\left\{\cdot,\cdot\right\}}\left(W_{\rho}\left(t\right)W_{\rho}\left(t\right)\right)=W_{\rho}\left(t\right)\ast W_{\rho}\left(t\right),

where
\ast
denotes the star-product operator.

Since
\left\{W_{H},\cdot\right\}
is a first-order differential operator
we can write

e^{t\left\{W_{H},\cdot\right\}}\left(W_{\rho}\left(0\right)\ast W_{\rho}\left(0\right)\right)=\hat{S}_{t}\left(W_{\rho}\left(\alpha(-t\right))W_{\rho}\left(\alpha(-t\right))\right),

(49)

where

W_{\rho}\left(\alpha(-t\right))=e^{t\left\{W_{H},\cdot\right\}}W_{\rho}(0),

is the classically evolved Wigner function (
11
) and

\hat{S}_{t}=\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}e^{t\left\{W_{H},\cdot\right\}}\left(\partial_{\alpha}\otimes\partial_{\alpha^{\ast}}-\partial_{\alpha^{\ast}}\otimes\partial_{\alpha}\right)e^{-t\left\{W_{H},\cdot\right\}}\right),

(50)

is the transformed star-product operator. For quadratic in
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\ast}
symbols
W_{H}(\alpha,\alpha^{\ast})
the operator (
50
) is invariant under transformation generated by
W_{H}
. This leads to
the well known result,

W_{\rho}\left(\alpha(-t\right))\ast W_{\rho}\left(\alpha(-t\right))=W_{\rho}\left(\alpha(-t\right)),

(51)

quadratic Hamiltonians.

In general case, by taking into account that

\displaystyle e^{t\left\{H,\cdot\right\}}\partial_{\alpha}e^{-t\left\{H,\cdot\right\}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}-it\left(\partial_{\alpha}^{2}W_{H}\partial_{\alpha^{\ast}}-\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha^{\ast}}W_{H}\partial_{\alpha}\right)+O(t^{2}),

\displaystyle e^{t\left\{H,\cdot\right\}}\partial_{\alpha^{\ast}}e^{-t\left\{H,\cdot\right\}}

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha^{\ast}}+it\left(\partial_{\alpha^{\ast}}^{2}W_{H}\partial_{\alpha}-\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha^{\ast}}W_{H}\partial_{\alpha^{\ast}}\right)+O(t^{2}),

\hat{S}=\exp\left[-\frac{i}{2}\left\{\cdot,\cdot\right\}+\frac{t}{2}\hat{d}\right],

\hat{d}\sim\partial_{\alpha}\otimes\alpha^{2}\partial_{\alpha}-\alpha^{2}\partial_{\alpha}\otimes\partial_{\alpha}+c.c.+2\left|\alpha\right|^{2}\partial_{\alpha}\otimes\partial_{\alpha^{\ast}}-\partial_{\alpha}\otimes 2\left|\alpha\right|^{2}\partial_{\alpha^{\ast}}+c.c.

a

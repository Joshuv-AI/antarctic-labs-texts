Abstract
In this work, we employ the ∂ ¯ \bar{\partial} steepest descent method in order to study the Cauchy problem of the cgNLS equations with initial conditions in weighted Sobolev space H 1 , 1 ( ℝ ) = { f ∈ L 2 ( ℝ ) : f ′ , x f ∈ L 2 ( ℝ ) } H^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}):f^{\prime},xf\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\} . The large time asymptotic behavior of the solution u ⁡ ( x , t ) u(x,t) and v ⁡ ( x , t ) v(x,t) are derived in a fixed space-time cone S ( x 1 , x 2 , v 1 , v 2 ) = { ( x , t ) ∈ ℝ 2 : x = x 0 + v t , x 0 ∈ [ x 1 , x 2 ] , v ∈ [ v 1 , v 2 ] } S(x_{1},x_{2},v_{1},v_{2})=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x=x_{0}+vt,~x_{0}\in[x_{1},x_{2}],~v\in[v_{1},v_{2}]\} . Based on the resulting asymptotic behavior, we prove the solution resolution conjecture of the cgNLS equations which contains the soliton term confirmed by | 𝒵 ⁡ ( ℐ ) | |\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{I})| -soliton on discrete spectrum and the t − 1 2 t^{-\frac{1}{2}} order term on continuous spectrum with residual error up to O ⁡ ( t − 3 4 ) O(t^{-\frac{3}{4}}) .

Introduction

The classical nonlinear Schrödinger(NLS) equation, i.e.,

\displaystyle iu_{t}\pm u_{xx}+2|u|^{2}u=0,

is a fundamental physical model and has been applied in various fields such as deep water waves
[
1
]
, plasma physics
[
2
,
3
]
, nonlinear optical fibers
[
4
,
5
]
, etc. Since it plays a significant role in the field of nonlinear science, lots of researchers and scholars do many works on the NLS equation and its extensions
[
6
]
-
[
11
]
. It is worth noting that in nonlinear science such as optical fibers, the NLS equation only describes the propagation of optical solitons in scalar fields of mono-mode fibers. However, because the change of nonlinear phase stems from the cross-phase modulation in multi-mode fibers or birefringent fibers, we need take the interaction of some field components at different frequencies or polarisations into consideration. The coupled nonlinear Schrödinger system came into being to describe the characteristics of such soliton. For example, the well-known Manakov system
[
12
]
, i.e.

\displaystyle iu_{t}\pm\frac{1}{2}u_{xx}+(|u|^{2}+|v|^{2})u=0,

\displaystyle iv_{t}\pm\frac{1}{2}v_{xx}+(|u|^{2}+|v|^{2})v=0,

can be used to describe the intense electromagnetic pulse propagation in birefringent fiber.

In this work, considering that there are more effects of nonlinear factors in practise, we derive a novel integrable coupled generalized nonlinear Schrödinger(cgNLS) equation that reads

\displaystyle\begin{split}iu_{t}-i\alpha u_{x}+u_{xx}-2u^{2}v+4\beta^{2}u^{3}v^{2}+4i\beta(uv)_{x}u+\gamma u=0,\\
iv_{t}-i\alpha v_{x}-v_{xx}+2uv^{2}-4\beta^{2}u^{2}v^{3}+4i\beta(uv)_{x}v-\gamma v=0,\end{split}

(1.1)

where
u(x,t)
and
v(x,t)
are complex function of variables
x
,
t
, and
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\gamma
are arbitrary constants. Additionally, we will derive the Lax pair, infinite conservation laws and Hamiltonian function associated with the cgNLS equation.
Furthermore, we investigate the soliton resolution of the cgNLS equation with the initial value condition

\displaystyle u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),v(x,0)=v_{0}(x)\in H^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}),

(1.2)

where
H^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})
is a weighted Sobolev space, i.e.,

\displaystyle H^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}):f^{\prime},xf\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\}.

(1.3)

In particular, some unique cases of (
1.1
) can be obtained by fixing the coefficients
\alpha
,
\beta
and
\gamma
.

1.

When
\alpha=\gamma=0
and
\beta\neq 0
, Eq.(
1.1
) degenerates into the coupled nonlinear Kundu-Eckhaus equation, i.e.,

\displaystyle iu_{t}+u_{xx}-2u^{2}v+4\beta^{2}u^{3}v^{2}+4i\beta(uv)_{x}u=0,

\displaystyle iv_{t}-v_{xx}+2uv^{2}-4\beta^{2}u^{2}v^{3}+4i\beta(uv)_{x}v=0,

which is a completely integrable system and possesses a Lax representation, the Hamiltonian structure and some other properties
[
13
]
-
[
15
]
.

2.

When
\alpha\neq 0
,
\gamma\neq 0
,
\beta=0
, and
v=-u^{*}
, Eq.(
1.1
) degenerates into the modified Landau-Lifshitz(mLL) equation, i.e.,

\displaystyle iu_{t}-i\alpha u_{x}+u_{xx}+2|u|^{2}u+\gamma u=0,

which can be employed to depict the dynamic behavior of local magnetization in electromagnetics. Also, a research for the mLL equation reveals the accumulation of energy plays an important role in the generation of magnetic rogue waves
[
16
]
. Furthermore, the long time asymptotic behavior of the mLL equation with nonzero boundary condition have been studied
[
17
]
.

3.

When
\alpha=\gamma=\beta=0
, and
v=-u^{*}
, Eq.(
1.1
) degenerates into the classical focusing nonlinear Schrödinger(NLS) equation, i.e.,

\displaystyle iu_{t}+u_{xx}+2|u|^{2}u=0,

which has played an important part in nonlinear science.

It is not hard to check that the cgNLS equation is not self-adjoint, so that the soliton solution will emerge for zero boundary conditions. Therefore, it is significant to consider the question that how to obtain the long time asymptotic behavior of the cgNLS equation in the domain of solution solutions.

In order to obtain the long time behavior of nonlinear evolution equations, researchers have done a series work. In 1974, using the inverse scattering method, the long time behavior of nonlinear wave equation was first carried out by Manakov
[
18
]
. In 1976, the long time asymptotic solutions of nonlinear Schrödinger(NLS) equation with decaying initial value was obtained by Zakharov and Manakov
[
19
]
. In 1993, a nonlinear steepest descent method was developed to study the modified Korteweg-de Vries(MKdV) equation and obtained the solution rigorously by Defit and Zhou
[
20
]
. Then, this approach has been applied widespread. However, it has been shown in literature
[
21
,
22
]
that if the initial value is smooth and decay fast enough then the error term is
o(\frac{\log t}{t})
. Then, after the hard work of many researchers
[
23
]
, the error term becomes
O(t^{-(\frac{1}{2}+\iota)})
for any
0<\iota<\frac{1}{4}
with the condition that the initial value belongs to the weighted Sobolev space (
1.3
).

In recent years, McLaughlin and Miller have made contributions to develop the method for the long time asymptotic analysis of Riemann-Hilbert problem(RHP). Combining steepest descent with
\bar{\partial}
-problem, they presented a
\bar{\partial}
steepest descent method which has been used to analyse the long time asymptotic behavior of series equations
[
24
,
25
]
. Since then, lots of significant works have been done by applying this method. For example, with the assumption of finite mass initial data, Dieng and McLaughlin studied the defocusing NLS equation
[
26
]
; with finite density initial data, the defocusing NLS equation was investigated by Cuccagna and Jenkins
[
27
]
; Borghese and Jenkins studied the Cauchy problem for the focusing NLS equation via applying this method
[
28
]
. Of course, there are a series of great work about this
\bar{\partial}
steepest descent method
[
29
]
-
[
33
]
.
Compared with the nonlinear steepest descent method, it is not necessary to study the delicate estimates involving
L^{p}
estimates of Cauchy projection operators by applying the
\bar{\partial}
steepest descent method. Also, it improves the error estimates, i.e., if the initial value belongs to the weighted Sobolev space (
1.3
), the error becomes
o(t^{-\frac{3}{4}})
which has been shown in
[
26
]
.

In this work, we are going to use the
\bar{\partial}
steepest descent method to study the long time asymptotic behavior of the cgNLS equation with the initial value
u_{0}(x)
and
v_{0}(x)
which belong to the weighted Sobolev space (
1.3
).

The outline of this work is as follows. In section 2, based on the Lax pair of the cgNLS equation, we introduce two kinds of eigenfunction and scattering matrix. Also, the analytical, symmetries and asymptotic properties are analyzed. Then the Riemann-Hilbert problem is constructed for the cgNLS equation with initial problem. In section 3, we introduce the matrix function

to separate the jump matric which is defined in (
2.2

and get a mixed
\bar{\partial}
-Riemann-Hilbert(RH) problem. In section 5, we decompose the mixed
\bar{\partial}
-RH problem into two parts which are a model RH problem with

and a pure
\bar{\partial}
-RH problem with
\bar{\partial}R^{(2)}\neq 0
, i.e.,

and

. In section 6, we solve the model RH problem

\bar{\partial}
-RH problem is studied. Finally, in section 8, we obtain the soliton resolution and long time asymptotic behavior of the cgNLS equation.

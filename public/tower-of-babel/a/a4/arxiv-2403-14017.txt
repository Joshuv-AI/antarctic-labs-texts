Linearized analysis of dissipative Two Axis Counter Twisting (TACT) squeezing for Metrology
Garry Goldstein 1

Abstract
In this work we analyze two axis twisting in the presence of depolarizing channel dissipation. We find that spin squeezing is only possible if the dissipation is parametrically weaker then the squeezing coupling. Squeezing may be used for meteorologically useful decrease of spin noise but only in the case where the squeezing occurs before measurement, in the case one squeezes as one measures one also squeezes the signal thereby making spin squeezing ineffective for metrological gain. The key mathematical advance made in this work is the observation that TACT in the presence of depolarizing noise is equivalent to TACT with reduced polarization and no noise. We find an exponential gain in signal to noise with the exponent proportional to the ratio between the squeezing strength and the depolarization rate.

II
Squeezing

We consider a spin ensemble with
N
spins with the initial density
matrix given by

\rho=\otimes_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\mathbb{I}}{2}+P\sigma_{z}^{i}\right)

(1)

Here
\sigma^{i}
are the Pauli matrices. This means that the spins
are uniformly polarized along the z-axis. We consider the following
Linblad evolution for the system:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathcal{L}\left(\rho\right)=\mathcal{L}_{1}\left(\rho\right)+\mathcal{L}_{2}\left(\rho\right)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}\left(\rho\right)

\displaystyle=-i\left[H_{Squ},\rho\right]

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2}\left(\rho\right)

\displaystyle=\Gamma\sum_{i}\left[\sigma_{x}^{i}\rho\sigma_{x}^{i}+\sigma_{z}^{i}\rho\sigma_{z}^{i}+\sigma_{y}^{i}\rho\sigma_{y}^{i}\right]-3\Gamma\rho

\displaystyle H_{Squ}

\displaystyle=J\sum_{i,j}\sigma_{x}^{i}\sigma_{x}^{j}-J\sum_{i,j}\sigma_{y}^{i}\sigma_{y}^{j}

(2)

It is given by a depolarizing channel with rate
\Gamma
and TACT
evolution with squeezing strength
J
. We now see that after time

T
:

\displaystyle\rho\left(T\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left[T\left(\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2}\right)\right]\rho

\displaystyle=\exp\left[T\mathcal{L}_{1}\right]\exp\left[T\mathcal{L}_{2}\right]\rho

(3)

Where we have used that:

\left[\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}\right]\cong 0+O\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)

(4)

Now we see that

\exp\left[T\mathcal{L}_{2}\right]\rho=\otimes_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\mathbb{I}}{2}+P\exp\left[-4\Gamma T\right]\sigma_{z}^{i}\right)

(5)

As such we have reduced the squeezing under decoherence problem to
a pure squeezing problem with finite initial polarization. To make
further analytical progress we consider the linearized Holstein Primakov
representation of spin:

\displaystyle S_{+}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{N-n_{b}}b\cong\sqrt{N\mathcal{P}}b

\displaystyle S_{-}

\displaystyle=b^{\dagger}\sqrt{N-n_{b}}\cong\sqrt{N\mathcal{P}}b^{\dagger}

\displaystyle S_{z}

\displaystyle=N-b^{\dagger}b

(6)

With

\mathcal{P}=P\exp\left[-4\Gamma T\right]

(7)

Now we write:

H_{Squ}=J\sum_{i,j}\sigma_{x}^{i}\sigma_{x}^{j}-J\sum_{i,j}\sigma_{y}^{i}\sigma_{y}^{j}=\frac{1}{2}JN\mathcal{P}\left[b^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}+bb\right]

(8)

Furthermore:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial b}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=i\left[H_{Squ},b\right]=iJN\mathcal{P}b^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial b^{\dagger}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=i\left[H_{Squ},b\right]=-iJN\mathcal{P}b

(9)

or

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}b\\
b^{\dagger}\end{array}\right)=JN\mathcal{P}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&i\\
-i&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}b\\
b^{\dagger}\end{array}\right)

(10)

There are two eigenvalues
\lambda_{\pm}=\pm JN\mathcal{P}
with
eigenvectors:

v_{+}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(b-ib^{\dagger}\right),\>v_{-}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(b+ib^{\dagger}\right)

(11)

As such we have that

\displaystyle b\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=b\mathrm{cosh}\left(JN\mathcal{P}t\right)-ib^{\dagger}\sinh\left(JN\mathcal{P}t\right)

\displaystyle b^{\dagger}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=ib^{\dagger}\cosh\left(JN\mathcal{P}t\right)-b\sinh\left(JN\mathcal{P}t\right)

(12)

As such after time
T
we have that:

\xi_{min}^{2}=\frac{\exp\left(-JNP\exp\left(-4\Gamma T\right)T\right)}{P\exp\left(-4\Gamma T\right)}

(13)

Writing
\Theta=4\Gamma T
and
\alpha\Theta=JNPT
we have that:

\xi_{min}^{2}=P^{-1}\exp\left(-\Theta\left[\alpha\exp\left(-\Theta\right)-1\right]\right)

(14)

As such its sufficient to optimize

\displaystyle\Theta\left[\alpha\exp\left(-\Theta\right)-1\right]

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\alpha>1

(15)

As such squeezing is only possible for

JNP>4\Gamma

(16)

Otherwise it is not worth to spend any time squeezing at all and measure
with unsqueezed states. The squeezing in Eq. (
13
)
can now be optimized numerically. For qualitative understanding we
now work in the strong squeezing limit,
\alpha\gg 1
which means
that
\Theta_{min}\cong 1
and

\xi_{min}^{2}=P^{-1}\exp\left(-\left[\alpha e^{-1}-1\right]\right)

(17)

This shows improved squeezing when the condition in Eq. (
16
)
is met.

III
Adding signal: metrology applications

III.1
Squeezing while measuring

Let us now add an external magnetic field. More precisely let us consider
the evolution:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathcal{L}\left(\rho\right)=\mathcal{L}_{1}\left(\rho\right)+\mathcal{L}_{2}\left(\rho\right)+\mathcal{L}_{3}\left(\rho\right)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{3}\left(\rho\right)

\displaystyle=i\left[B\sum_{i}\left[\sigma_{y}^{i}-\sigma_{x}^{i}\right],\rho\right]

(18)

Now we see that after time
T
:

\displaystyle\rho\left(T\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left[T\left(\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2}+\mathcal{L}_{3}\right)\right]\rho

\displaystyle=\exp\left[T\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{3}\right]\exp\left[T\mathcal{L}_{2}\right]\rho

(19)

Where we have used that:

\left[\mathcal{L}_{3},\mathcal{L}_{2}\right]=0

(20)

As such we see that squeezing while acquiring signal with depolarizing
channel decoherence is equivalent to squeezing while acquiring signal
in a partially polarized state. Therefore (using Eq. (
6
)
the total Hamiltonian for the squeezing while acquiring signal is
given by:

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=J\sum_{i,j}\sigma_{x}^{i}\sigma_{x}^{j}-J\sum_{i,j}\sigma_{y}^{i}\sigma_{y}^{j}-B\sum_{i}\left[\sigma_{y}^{i}-\sigma_{x}^{i}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}JN\mathcal{P}\left[b^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}+bb\right]-B\sqrt{N\mathcal{P}}\left(\frac{1+i}{2}b-\frac{1-i}{2}b^{\dagger}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}JN\mathcal{P}\left[\mathcal{B}^{\dagger}\mathcal{B}^{\dagger}+\mathcal{B}\mathcal{B}\right]+const

(21)

Where

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=b^{\dagger}-\frac{B}{2J\sqrt{N\mathcal{P}}}\left(1-i\right)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle=b-\frac{B}{2J\sqrt{N\mathcal{P}}}\left(1+i\right)

(22)

This means that after a time
t
:

\displaystyle v_{+}^{\mathcal{B}}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=v_{+}\exp\left(JN\mathcal{P}t\right)

\displaystyle v_{-}^{\mathcal{B}}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=v_{-}\exp\left(-JN\mathcal{P}t\right)

(23)

Where

v_{+}^{\mathcal{B}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\mathcal{B}-i\mathcal{B}^{\dagger}\right),\>v_{-}^{\mathcal{B}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\mathcal{B}+i\mathcal{B}^{\dagger}\right)

(24)

This means that

\displaystyle\left\langle v_{-}^{\mathcal{B}}\left(t\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\exp\left(JN\mathcal{P}t\right)\frac{B}{J\sqrt{N\mathcal{P}}}\left(1+i\right)

\displaystyle\left\langle v_{+}^{\mathcal{B}}\left(t\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=0

(25)

Here we have used the initial state in Eq. (
5
).
This means that

\displaystyle\left\langle v_{-}\left(t\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left[1-\exp\left(JN\mathcal{P}t\right)\right]\frac{B}{J\sqrt{N\mathcal{P}}}\left(1+i\right)

\displaystyle\left\langle v_{+}\left(t\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=0

(26)

Now the signal is given by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{SIG}

\displaystyle=\left\langle S_{x}+S_{y}\right\rangle=\frac{\sqrt{N\mathcal{P}}}{\left(1+i\right)}\left\langle v_{-}\left(t\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{B}{J}\left[1-\exp\left(-JN\mathcal{P}t\right)\right]

(27)

This means that for a total measurement time
\tau
with each measurement
taking a time of
T
for squeezing and the signal to noise is given
by:

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau}}\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}}{\partial B}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{J\sqrt{TN}}\frac{1-\exp\left(-JNP\exp\left(-4\Gamma T\right)T\right)}{\exp\left(-\left[JNPT-1\right]\exp\left(-4\Gamma T\right)\right)}

(28)

we see that because the squeezing also squeezes the signal spin squeezing
is ineffective for metrology.

III.2
Squeezing before measurement

Consider now squeezing for a time
T
and then acquiring signal for
a time
t
. That is we consider the following Linbladian evolution:

\displaystyle\rho\left(T+t\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\left[\mathcal{L}_{2}+\mathcal{L}_{3}\right]t\right)\exp\left(\left[\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2}\right]T\right)\rho

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\mathcal{L}_{3}t\right)\exp\left(\mathcal{L}_{1}T\right)\exp\left(\mathcal{L}_{2}\left(T+t\right)\right)\rho

(29)

Therefore we have that acquiring signal under decoherence after squeezing
is equivalent to acquiring signal with no decoherence with a state
thats squeezed as

\xi_{min}^{2}=\frac{\exp\left(-JNP\exp\left(-4\Gamma\left(T+t\right)\right)T\right)}{P\exp\left(-4\Gamma\left(T+t\right)\right)}

In this case we have that the signal to noise satisfies:

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau}}\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}}{\partial B}=\frac{t\sqrt{N}}{\sqrt{T+t}}\frac{P\exp\left(-4\Gamma\left(T+t\right)\right)}{\exp\left(-JNP\exp\left(-4\Gamma\left(T+t\right)\right)T\right)}

(30)

This can be optimized numerically however to gain intuition we now
work in the case where
\alpha\gg 1
in which case
4\Gamma\left(T+t\right)\cong 1

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau}}\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}}{\partial B}

\displaystyle\cong\frac{t\sqrt{N}}{\sqrt{T+t}}\frac{Pe^{-1}}{\exp\left(-\alpha e^{-1}\left(\frac{T}{t+T}\right)\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{N}}{\sqrt{4\Gamma}}\frac{Pe^{-1}}{\exp\left(-\alpha e^{-1}U\right)}\left(1-U\right)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau}}\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}_{max}}{\partial B}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{N}}{\sqrt{4\Gamma}}\frac{P\alpha^{-1}}{\exp\left(-\left[\alpha e^{-1}-1\right]\right)}

(31)

Where
U=4\Gamma T
and
U_{max}=\frac{\alpha e^{-1}-1}{\alpha e^{-1}}
.
We notice that without squeezing
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau}}\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}_{max}}{\partial B}\sim P\frac{\sqrt{N}}{\sqrt{\Gamma}}
,
which means the metrological improvement is given by:
\sim\frac{\exp\left(\alpha e^{-1}\right)}{\alpha}
.

IV
Conclusion

In this work we have studied TACT spin squeezing in the presence of
depolarizing channel noise. We have shown that in the case where the
squeezing Hamiltonian is parametrically greater then the depolarizing
decoherence rate meteorologically useful squeezing may be achieved
(see Eq. (
16
)). Spin squeezing while acquiring signal
on the other hand is not a promising pathway for sub shot noise meteorology
as the signal gets squeezed as well as the noise. However pre-squeezing
followed by signal acquisition even in the presence of a depolarizing
decoherence channel is a viable method to obtain sub shot noise meteorology.
These results further support TACT as a method of spin squeezing for
meteorological gain. The overall metrological improvement scales as

\sim\frac{\exp\left(\alpha e^{-1}\right)}{\alpha}
where
\alpha=\frac{JNP}{4\Gamma}
.

Appendix A
Argument why Eq. (
4
) is reasonable

We consider the case of a large number of spins. In which case we
have that for a term in the density matrix:

\rho=\sum_{\alpha}P_{\alpha_{i}}\prod\sigma_{\alpha_{i}}^{i},\quad\alpha_{i}=\mathbb{I},x,y,z

(32)

Because the polarization is non-zero there is a large number
\sim N

terms with
\alpha_{i}=x,y,z
. As such we have that

\mathcal{L}_{2}\left(\rho\right)=-4\Gamma\sum_{\alpha}P_{\alpha_{i}}\prod\sigma_{\alpha_{i}}^{i}\left(1-\delta_{\alpha_{i},\mathbb{I}}\right)

(33)

contains a large number of terms. Now we have that any terms of the
form:

\mathcal{L}_{1x/y}^{ij}\left(\rho\right):\sum_{i}\left(1-\delta_{\alpha_{i},\mathbb{I}}\right)\rightarrow\sum_{i}\left(1-\delta_{\alpha_{i},\mathbb{I}}\right)+\left(0/+1/-1\right)

(34)

where

\mathcal{L}_{1x/y}^{ij}\left(\rho\right)=J\sigma_{x/y}^{i}\sigma_{x/y}^{j}

(35)

As such the rate of decoherence does not significantly change for
a typical term in Eq. (
33
). Furthermore we know
that for
\alpha\gg 1
we have that
4\Gamma T=1
,
JPT=\alpha/N\ll 1

so that each individual term of the form
\mathcal{L}_{1x/y}^{ij}

need only act once during evolution (with corrections scaling as
1/N
)
so we have that:

\left[\mathcal{L}_{1x/y}^{ij},\mathcal{L}_{2}\right]\cong 0

(36)

which implies Eq. (
4
).

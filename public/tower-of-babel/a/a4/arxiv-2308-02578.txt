Notes on noncommutative ergodic theorems
Semyon Litvinov

Abstract
. Given a semifinite von Neumann algebra ℳ \mathcal{M} equipped with a faithful normal semifinite trace τ \tau , we prove that the spaces L 0 ​ ( ℳ , τ ) L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau) and ℛ τ \mathcal{R}_{\tau} are complete with respect to pointwise, almost uniform and bilaterally almost uniform, convergences in L 0 ​ ( ℳ , τ ) L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau) . Then we show that the pointwise Cauchy property for a special class of nets of linear operators in the space L 1 ​ ( ℳ , τ ) L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau) can be extended to pointwise convergence of such nets in any fully symmetric space E ⊂ ℛ τ E\subset\mathcal{R}_{\tau} , in particular, in any space L p ​ ( ℳ , τ ) L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau) , 1 ≤ p < ∞ 1\leq p<\infty . Some applications of these results in the noncommutative ergodic theory are discussed.

1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra equipped with a faithful normal semifinite trace
\tau
, and let
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
stand for the complete lattice of projections in
\mathcal{M}
; see
[
1
,
21
,
20
]
. If
\mathbf{1}
is the identity of
\mathcal{M}
and
e\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
, we write
e^{\perp}=\mathbf{1}-e
. In what follows, we assume that
\tau(\mathbf{1})=\infty
.

Denote by
L^{0}=L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
the algebra of
\tau
-measurable operators affiliated with
\mathcal{M}
. Let
L^{p}=L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
if
1\leq p<\infty
and
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{M},\tau)=\mathcal{M}

be the noncommutative
L^{p}
- spaces associated with
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. For detailed accounts on the spaces
L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
,
p\in\{0\}\cup[1,\infty)
, see
[
19
,
15
,
22
,
17
]
.

Let
(\Lambda,\leq)
be a directed set, and let
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
be the uniform norm in
\mathcal{M}
. The following definition presents two noncommutative counterparts of the classical pointwise (almost everywhere) convergence.

Definition 1.1
.

A net
\{x_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\subset L^{0}
is said to converge
almost uniformly
(a.u.) (
bilaterally almost uniformly
(b.a.u.)) to
\widehat{x}\in L^{0}
if for any given
\varepsilon>0
there exists a projection
e\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
such that
\tau(e^{\perp})\leq\varepsilon
and

\|(\widehat{x}-x_{\alpha})e\|_{\infty}\to 0\text{ \ (\,respectively,\ }\|e(\widehat{x}-x_{\alpha})e\|_{\infty}\to 0\,).

Remark 1.1
.

(1) A.u. convergence clearly implies b.a.u. convergence, while the converse in not true in general - see
[
6
, Example 3.1]
.

(2) Pointwise convergence in the space
L^{0}
is not topological in general, that is, it may not agree with any topology in
L^{0}
; see, for example, the note
[
16
]
.

First ergodic theorem in the space
L^{1}
equipped with b.a.u. convergence appeared in the groundbreaking paper
[
23
]
. Ergodic theorems in the spaces
L^{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
, endowed with b.a.u. or a.u. for
p\geq 2
convergence were comprehensively studied in another fundamental paper
[
11
]
. An alternative approach, based directly on
[
23
]
, to deriving ergodic theorems in the spaces
L^{p}
,
1<p<\infty
, was suggested in
[
14
]
.

Given
x\in L^{0}
and
t>0
, denote

\mu_{t}(x)=\inf\big\{\|xe\|_{\infty}:\,e\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M}),\,\tau(e^{\perp})\leq t\big\},

the
t
-th generalized singular number of
x
- see
[
10
]
. The function
\mu_{t}(x)
,
t>0
, is called the
non-increasing rearrangement
of
x
. It is worth mentioning that, as it follows from the proof of
[
10
, Proposition 2.2]
,

\mu_{t}(x)=\inf\big\{\|xe\|_{\infty}:\,e\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{A}),\,\tau(e^{\perp})\leq t\big\},

where
\mathcal{A}
is any subalgebra of
\mathcal{M}
containing the spectral family
\big\{e_{\lambda}(|x|)\big\}_{\lambda\geq 0}
of the absolute value

|x|=(x^{*}x)^{1/2}
of an operator
x\in L^{0}
. Thus,
\mu_{t}(x)
can be alternatively defined as

\mu_{t}(x)=\inf\big\{\lambda>0:\,\tau(e_{\lambda}(|x|)^{\perp})\leq t\big\}.

Now, let us denote

\mathcal{R}_{\tau}=\big\{x\in L^{1}+\mathcal{M}:\ \mu_{t}(x)\to 0\text{\,\ as\ }t\to\infty\big\}.

In view of
[
10
, Lemma 2.5]
,
\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
is a
\ast
-algebra. Besides, it is clear that

\mathcal{R}_{\tau}=\big\{x\in L^{1}+\mathcal{M}:\ \tau(e_{\lambda}(|x|)^{\perp})<\infty\,\ \forall\ \lambda>0\big\},

so
\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
is the noncommutative analogue of Fava’s space
\mathcal{R}_{0}
- see
[
9
, Section (2.2.15)]
.

Sequential completeness of the space
L^{0}
with respect to a.u. and b.a.u. convergences was proved in
[
7
, Theorem 2.3]
. In Section 2, we show that the space
L^{0}
and - more importantly for this article - the space
\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
are complete with respect to these convergences.

Section 3 is devoted to the description how the a.u. (b.a.u.) convergence of a generic class of nets of linear maps in any fully symmetric space
E\subset\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
- in particular, in any space
L^{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
- can be deduced from the a.u. (respectively, b.a.u.) Cauchy property for such nets in the space
L^{1}
.

In Section 4 we proceed to outline some possible applications of these results to the nocommutative ergodic theory.

2.
Pointwise completeness in
L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)

Here we aim to show that any almost uniform or bilaterally almost uniform Cauchy net converges in
L^{0}
and to derive the same for the space
\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
. We will employ the notion of measure topology in
L^{0}
- in which
L^{0}
is a complete metric space - and use the fact that a Cauchy net in a complete metric space is convergent.

The
measure topology

t_{\tau}
in
L^{0}
is given by the system

\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon,\delta)=\big\{x\in L^{0}:\ \exists\ e\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})\text{\,\ such that\,\ }\tau(e^{\perp})\leq\varepsilon\text{\,\ and\ }\|xe\|_{\infty}\leq\delta\big\}\,;

\varepsilon>0
,
\delta>0
, of (closed) neighborhoods of zero. The following is an immediate corollary of
[
15
, Theorem 1]
; note that it is understood that the space
L^{0}
is the completion of
\mathcal{M}
with respect to
t_{\tau}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

(L^{0},t_{\tau})
is a complete metrizable topological
\ast
-algebra.

Definition 2.1
.

A net
\{x_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\subset L^{0}
is said to converge to
\widehat{x}\in L^{0}

in measure (bilaterally in measure)

if for every
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta>0
there is
\gamma=\gamma(\varepsilon,\delta)\in I
such that, given
\alpha\in\Lambda
with
\alpha\geq\gamma
, there exists a projection
e_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
satisfying conditions

\tau(e^{\perp}_{\alpha})\leq\varepsilon
and

\|(\widehat{x}-x_{\alpha})e_{\alpha}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta\,\ (\text{respectively},\,\|e_{\alpha}(\widehat{x}-x_{\alpha})e_{\alpha}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta).

Proof of the following lemma - which idea is attributed to E. Nelson (see
[
18
, Appendix, Theorem B]
); note also the paragraph before
[
23
, Theorem 1]
- can be found in
[
7
, Lemma 2.1, Theorem 2.2]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
x\in L^{0}
and
e\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
be such that
exe\in\mathcal{M}
. Then there exists
f\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
such that

\tau(f^{\perp})\leq 2\tau(e^{\perp})\text{ \ and \ }\|xf\|_{\infty}\leq\|exe\|_{\infty}.

The following statement is a version of
[
7
, Theorem 2.2]
for nets. We provide a proof for the sake of the reader’s convenience.

Proposition 2.1
.

A net
\{x_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\subset L^{0}
is Cauchy in measure if and only if it is Cauchy bilaterally in measure.

Proof.

Clearly, if is sufficient to show the ”if” implication. So, let
\{x_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\subset L^{0}
be Cauchy bilaterally in measure and fix
\varepsilon>0
,
\delta>0
. Then there is
\gamma=\gamma(\varepsilon,\delta)
such that for any
\alpha,\beta\in I
with
\alpha,\beta\geq\gamma
there exists
e_{\alpha,\beta}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
for which

\tau(e_{\alpha,\beta}^{\perp})\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\text{ \ and \ }\|e_{\alpha,\beta}(x_{\alpha}-x_{\beta})e_{\alpha,\beta}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta.

Then, by Lemma
2.1
, for every
\alpha,\beta\geq\gamma
there exists
f_{\alpha,\beta}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
such that

\tau(f_{\alpha,\beta}^{\perp})\leq 2\tau(e_{\alpha,\beta}^{\perp})\leq\varepsilon\text{ \ and \ }\|(x_{\alpha}-x_{\beta})f_{\alpha,\beta}\|_{\infty}\leq\|e_{\alpha,\beta}(x_{\alpha}-x_{\beta})e_{\alpha,\beta}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta,

implying that the net
\{x_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\subset L^{0}
is Cauchy in measure.
∎

Now we are ready to prove main results of this section:

Theorem 2.2
.

The space
L^{0}
is complete with respect to a.u. and b.a.u. convergences.

Proof.

We will show that
L^{0}
is b.a.u. complete. Proof in the a.u. case is similar. Let
\{x_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\subset L^{0}
be b.a.u. Cauchy.
Then
\{x_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
is clearly Cauchy bilaterally in measure, which, by Proposition
2.1
, implies that it is Cauchy in measure.

Let
d
be a metric in
L^{0}
compatible with the measure topology
t_{\tau}
. Then for every
\delta>0
there is
\gamma\in I
such that

d(x_{\alpha},x_{\beta})\leq\delta
for all
\alpha,\beta\geq\gamma
.

So, pick
\gamma_{1}\in I
such that
d(x_{\alpha},x_{\beta})\leq 1
whenever
\alpha,\beta\geq\gamma_{1}
. Further, given
\gamma_{n}\in I
such that
\displaystyle d(x_{\alpha},x_{\beta})\leq\frac{1}{n}
for all
\alpha,\beta\geq\gamma_{n}
, choose
\gamma_{n+1}\in I
such that

\gamma_{n+1}\geq\gamma_{n}\text{\, \ and\, \ }d(x_{\alpha},x_{\beta})\leq\frac{1}{n+1}\ \ \forall\ \alpha,\beta\geq\gamma_{n+1}.

It follows that the sequence
\{x_{\gamma_{n}}\}
is Cauchy in measure, hence, in view of Theorem
2.1
, there exists
\widehat{x}\in L^{0}
such that
d(x_{\gamma_{n}},\widehat{x})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Given
\delta>0
, there is
n_{1}
such that
d(x_{\gamma_{n}},\widehat{x})\leq\delta/2
whenever
n\geq n_{1}
. In addition, there is
n_{2}
such that
\displaystyle d(x_{\alpha},x_{\beta})\leq\frac{\delta}{2}
whenever
\alpha,\beta\geq\gamma_{n_{2}}
. If
n_{0}=\max\{n_{1},n_{2}\}
, then

d(x_{\gamma_{n_{0}}},\widehat{x})\leq\frac{\delta}{2}\text{\ \ \ and, as\ }\gamma_{n_{0}}\geq\gamma_{n_{1}},\ d(x_{\alpha},x_{\gamma_{n_{0}}})\leq\frac{\delta}{2}\ \ \forall\ \alpha\geq\gamma_{n_{0}}.

Therefore
d(x_{\alpha},\widehat{x})\leq\delta
whenever
\alpha\geq\gamma_{n_{0}}
, implying that
x_{\alpha}\to\widehat{x}
in measure.

Finally, fix
\varepsilon>0
. Since
\{x_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
is b.a.u. Cauchy, there exists
e\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
with
\tau(e^{\perp})\leq\varepsilon
such that the net
\{ex_{\alpha}e\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
is
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
- Cauchy. Then, as
(\mathcal{M},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
is a complete metric space, we show as above that there is
x_{e}\in\mathcal{M}
such that
\|ex_{\alpha}e-x_{e}\|_{\infty}\to 0
, implying that
ex_{\alpha}e\to x_{e}
in measure. Also, as
x_{\alpha}\to\widehat{x}
in measure, it follows from Theorem
2.1
that
ex_{\alpha}e\to e\widehat{x}e
in measure, hence
x_{e}=e\widehat{x}e
, and we obtain

\|e(\widehat{x}-x_{\alpha})e\|_{\infty}=\|x_{e}-ex_{\alpha}e\|_{\infty}\to 0.

Therefore, it follows that
L^{0}
is b.a.u. complete.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

The space
\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
is complete with respect to a.u. and b.a.u. convergences.

Proof.

In view of Theorem
2.2
and, since
\mathcal{R}_{\tau}\subset L^{0}
and a.u. convergence implies b.a.u. convergence, it is sufficient to show that
\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
is b.a.u. closed.

Let a net
\{x_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}\subset\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
be such that
x_{\alpha}\to\widehat{x}\in L^{0}
b.a.u. Since
\mu_{t}(x)
is a non-increasing function of
t
, in order to show that
\widehat{x}\in\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
, that is, that
\mu_{t}(x)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
, it is sufficient to verify that for every
\delta>0
there is
t>0
such that
\mu_{t}(x)<\delta
. Since
\|xe\|_{\infty}<\delta
for some
e\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
with
\tau(e^{\perp})\leq t
implies that
\mu_{t}(x)<\delta
, this in turn will follow if we show that there exists
f\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
such that

\tau(f^{\perp})<\infty\text{ \ and \ }\|\widehat{x}f\|_{\infty}<\delta.

Since
x_{\alpha}\to\widehat{x}
b.a.u., given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exits
e_{n}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
such that

\tau(e_{n}^{\perp})\leq 2^{-n}\text{ \ and \ }\|e_{n}(\widehat{x}-x_{\alpha})e_{n}\|_{\infty}\xrightarrow[\alpha]{\ }0.

Therefore, for each
n
there is
x_{n}\in\{x_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}
for which
\|e_{n}(\widehat{x}-x_{n})e_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
, and let
N
be such that
\sum\limits_{n=N}^{\infty}2^{-n}\leq\varepsilon
. Then, with
e=\bigwedge\limits_{n\geq N}e_{n}
, we have

\tau(e^{\perp})\leq\varepsilon\text{ \ and \ }\|e(\widehat{x}-x_{n})e\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{n}\ \ \forall\ n\geq N,

implying that
\mathcal{R}_{\tau}\ni x_{n}\to\widehat{x}
b.a.u.

Now, let
\delta>0
. Since
x_{1}\in\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
, there is
e_{1}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
with
\tau(e_{1}^{\perp})<\infty
such that
\|x_{1}e_{1}\|_{\infty}<\delta
. As
x_{2}\in L^{0}
, there exists

\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})\ni h_{1}\leq e_{1}
such that
\tau(h_{1}^{\perp})<\infty
and
x_{2}h_{1}\in\mathcal{M}
. Next,
x_{2}\in\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
entails that there is
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})\ni e_{2}\leq h_{1}
such that

\tau(e_{2}^{\perp})<\infty
and
\|x_{2}e_{2}\|_{\infty}<\delta
. Repeating this process, one can construct a sequence
\{e_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
satisfying conditions

e_{n}\geq e_{n+1},\,\ \tau(e_{n}^{\perp})<\infty,\text{\ and \ }\|x_{n}e_{n}\|_{\infty}<\delta,\text{\,hence\ }\|e_{n}x_{n}e_{n}\|_{\infty}<\delta,\,\ \forall\ n.

Further,
x_{n}\to\widehat{x}
b.a.u. implies that there exists
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})\ni e\leq e_{1}
such that
\tau(e-e_{1})<\infty
, hence
\tau(e^{\perp})<\infty
, and
\|e(\widehat{x}-x_{n})e\|_{\infty}\to 0
, implying that

\|(e_{n}\land e)\widehat{x}(e_{n}\land e)\|_{\infty}-\|(e_{n}\land e)x_{n}(e_{n}\land e)\|_{\infty}\to 0.

Therefore, since
\|(e_{n}\land e)x_{n}(e_{n}\land e)\|_{\infty}<\delta
for every
n
, it follows that there exists
n_{0}
such that
\|(e_{n_{0}}\land e)\widehat{x}(e_{n_{0}}\land e)\|_{\infty}<\delta
. Then, by Lemma
2.1
, it is possible to find
f\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
such that

\tau(f^{\perp})\leq 2\tau((e_{n_{0}}\land e)^{\perp})\leq 2\tau(e_{n_{0}}^{\perp})+2\tau(e^{\perp})<\infty\text{ \ and \ }\|\widehat{x}f\|_{\infty}<\delta,

which completes the argument.
∎

3.
Pointwise convergence in fully symmetric spaces
E\subset\mathcal{R}_{\tau}

A non-zero Banach space
(E,\|\cdot\|_{E})\subset L^{0}
is called
fully symmetric
if conditions

x\in E,\ y\in L^{0},\text{\ and\ }\int_{0}^{s}\mu_{t}(y)dt\leq\int_{0}^{s}\mu_{t}(x)dt\ \ \forall\ s>0\text{\ (denoted\ }y\prec\prec x)

imply that
y\in E
and
\|y\|_{E}\leq\|x\|_{E}
.

It is well-known that the space
L^{p}=(L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau),\|\cdot\|_{p})
is fully symmetric and
L^{p}\subset\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
for each
1\leq p<\infty
. Besides, if
E\subset L^{0}
is a fully symmetric space, then
E\subset\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
if and only if
\mathbf{1}\notin E

[
6
, Proposition 2.2]
, implying that the noncommutative Orlicz, Lorentz and Marcinkiewicz spaces
E
on
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
with
\mathbf{1}\notin E
also lie in
\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
; see, for example,
[
4
, Section 7]
.

Remark 3.1
.

In contrast with Theorem
2.3
, even in the commutative case, a fully symmetric space may not be complete with respect to almost uniform convergence.
Indeed, denote by
\nu
Lebesgue measure on the interval
(0,\infty)
and define

\mathcal{R}_{\nu}=\big\{\,f\in L^{1}(\nu)+L^{\infty}(\nu):\ \nu\{|f|>\lambda\}<\infty\ \ \forall\ \lambda>0\,\big\},

the commutative precursor of
\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
, known as Fava’s space
\mathcal{R}_{0}
; see
[
3
]
for details.

Given
t\in(0,\infty)
, set

f_{n}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}2^{k}\cdot\chi_{(2^{-k},\,2^{-k+1}]}(t),\ \,n=1,2,\dots

It is easy to see that
\{f_{n}\}\subset L^{1}(\nu)
and that
\{f_{n}\}
converges almost uniformly to a function
f\in\mathcal{R}_{\nu}
such that
f\notin L^{1}(\nu)
. Therefore, the fully symmetric space
L^{1}(\nu)\subset\mathcal{R}_{\nu}
is not complete with respect to almost uniform convergence. However, for a special class of pointwise Cauchy nets in a fully symmetric space
E
the limits belong to
E
- see Theorem
3.1
below.

A linear operator
A:L^{1}+\mathcal{M}\to L^{1}+\mathcal{M}
is called a
Dunford-Schwartz operator
(denoting
A\in DS
) if
A(L^{1})\subset L^{1}
,
A(\mathcal{M})\subset\mathcal{M}
and

\|A(x)\|_{1}\leq\|x\|_{1}\,\ \forall\ x\in L^{1}\text{ \ and \ }\|A(x)\|_{\infty}\leq\|x\|_{\infty}\,\ \forall\ x\in\mathcal{M}.

The following fact is well-known in the commutative case; see, for example,
[
12
, Chapter II, Section 4]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
A\in DS
, then
A(x)\prec\prec x
for all
x\in L^{1}+\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

As it is proven right after
[
10
, Theorem 4.4]
- see also
[
8
, Proposition 2.5]
- given
x\in L^{1}+\mathcal{M}
and
s>0
, we have

\int_{0}^{s}\mu_{t}(x)ds=\inf\big\{\|y\|_{1}+s\|z\|_{\infty}:\ x=y+z,\ x\in L^{1},\ y\in\mathcal{M}\big\}.

Let
x\in L^{1}+\mathcal{M}
and
s>0
. As
x=y+z
, with
y\in L^{1}
and
z\in\mathcal{M}
, implies that
A(x)=A(y)+A(z)
, where

A(y)\in L^{1}
and
A(z)\in\mathcal{M}
, we can write

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{s}\mu_{t}(A(x))ds&=\inf\big\{\|y\|_{1}+s\|z\|_{\infty}:\ A(x)=y+z,\ x\in L^{1},\ y\in\mathcal{M}\big\}\\
&\leq\inf\big\{\|A(y)\|_{1}+s\|A(z)\|_{\infty}:\ A(x)=A(y)+A(z),\ x\in L^{1},\ y\in\mathcal{M}\big\}\\
&\leq\inf\big\{\|y\|_{1}+s\|z\|_{\infty}:\ A(x)=A(y)+A(z),\ x\in L^{1},\ y\in\mathcal{M}\big\}\\
&\leq\inf\big\{\|y\|_{1}+s\|z\|_{\infty}:\ x=y+z,\ x\in L^{1},\ y\in\mathcal{M}\big\}\\
&=\int_{0}^{s}\mu_{t}(x)ds,\end{split}

so the result follows.
∎

The following statement provides a useful characterization of the
*
-algebra
\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
x^{*}=x\in L^{0}
. Then
x\in\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
if and only if for any
\delta>0
there exist
y_{\delta}\in L^{1}
and
z_{\delta}\in\mathcal{M}
such that

(1)

\|z_{\delta}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta\text{ \ and \ }x=y_{\delta}+z_{\delta}.

Proof.

"\Rightarrow":\
Fix
\delta>0
. Since
|x|\in\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
, we have
0\leq x_{+}=\displaystyle\frac{|x|+x}{2}\in\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
and
0\leq x_{-}=\displaystyle\frac{|x|-x}{2}\in\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
. If
x_{+}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda de_{\lambda}
is the spectral decomposition of
x_{+}
, define
y_{\delta}^{+}=\int_{\delta/2}^{\infty}\lambda de_{\lambda}
and
z_{\delta}^{+}=\int_{0}^{\delta/2}\lambda de_{\lambda}
. Then we have

x_{+}=y_{\delta}^{+}+z_{\delta}^{+}\text{ \ and \ }\|z_{\delta}^{+}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\delta}{2}.

Next,
x_{+}\in\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
implies that
\tau(e_{\delta/2}^{\perp})<\infty
and
x_{+}=y^{+}+z^{+}
for some
y^{+}\in L^{1}
and
z^{+}\in\mathcal{M}
, hence

y_{\delta}^{+}=y_{\delta}^{+}e_{\delta/2}^{\perp}=y^{+}e_{\delta/2}^{\perp}+(z^{+}-z_{\delta}^{+})e_{\delta/2}^{\perp}\in L^{1}.

Similarly,
x_{-}=y_{\delta}^{-}+z_{\delta}^{-}
, where
y_{\delta}^{-}\in L^{1}
and
\|z_{\delta}^{-}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta/2
, so it follows that

x=(y_{\delta}^{+}-y_{\delta}^{-})+(z_{\delta}^{+}-z_{\delta}^{-}),\text{ \ where \ }y_{\delta}^{+}-y_{\delta}^{-}\in L^{1}\text{ \ and \ }\|z_{\delta}^{+}-z_{\delta}^{-}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta.

"\Leftarrow":\
Let
x^{*}=x\in L^{0}
be such that for any
\delta>0
there exist
y_{\delta}\in L^{1}
and
z_{\delta}\in\mathcal{M}
for which conditions (
1
) hold. Fix
\delta>0
. There exist
y_{\delta}\in L^{1}
and
z_{\delta}\in\mathcal{M}
such that
\|z_{\delta}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta/2
and

x=y_{\delta}+z_{\delta}
. Then, by
[
10
, Lemma 2.5]
, we have

\mu_{t}(x)\leq\mu_{t/2}(y_{\delta})+\mu_{t/2}(z_{\delta})\leq\mu_{t/2}(y_{\delta})+\frac{\delta}{2}

whenever
t>\delta
. Also, since
y_{\delta}\in L^{1}
, it follows that
\mu_{t}(y_{\delta})\to 0
as
t\to\infty
, so there is
t_{0}>0
such that
t\geq t_{0}
entails
\mu_{t}(y_{\delta})\leq\delta/2
. Thus, we conclude that
\mu_{t}(x)\leq\delta
for all
t>\max\{\delta,t_{0}\}
, implying that

\mu_{t}(x)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
, so
x\in\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
.
∎

We will call a map
A:L^{0}\to L^{0}

selfadjoint
if
A(x)^{*}=A(x)
whenever
x^{*}=x
. Here is an immediate consequence of Proposition
3.2
:

Corollary 3.1
.

If a linear selfadjoint map
A:L^{1}+\mathcal{M}\to L^{1}+\mathcal{M}
is such that
A(L^{1})\subset L^{1}
,

A(\mathcal{M})\subset\mathcal{M}
, and
\|A(x)\|_{\infty}\leq c\|x\|_{\infty}
for some
c>0
and any
x\in\mathcal{M}
, then
A(\mathcal{R}_{\tau})\subset\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
and fix
\delta>0
. By Proposition
3.2
, there exist
y_{\delta}\in L^{1}
and
z_{\delta}\in\mathcal{M}
such that

\|z_{\delta}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta/c
and
x=y_{\delta}+z_{\delta}
. Then
A(x)=A(y_{\delta})+A(z_{\delta})
, where
A(y_{\delta})\in L^{1}
and
\|A(z_{\delta})\|_{\infty}\leq\delta
, and Proposition
3.2
implies that
A(x)\in\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
.
∎

Theorem 3.1
.

Let a net of linear selfadjoint maps
A_{\alpha}:L^{1}+\mathcal{M}\to L^{1}+\mathcal{M}
,
\alpha\in\Lambda
, be such that

(1)

A_{\alpha}(L^{1})\subset L^{1}
for every
\alpha\in\Lambda
, and the net
\{A_{\alpha}(x)\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
is a.u. (b.a.u.) Cauchy for each
x\in L^{1}
;

(2)

A_{\alpha}(\mathcal{M})\subset\mathcal{M}
for every
\alpha\in\Lambda
, and there exists
c_{\infty}>0
such that

\sup_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\|A_{\alpha}(x)\|_{\infty}\leq c_{\infty}\|x\|_{\infty}\ \ \forall\ x\in\mathcal{M}.

Then
A_{\alpha}(x)\to\widehat{x}\in\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
a.u. (respectively, b.a.u.) for every
x\in\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
. If, in addition,
\|A_{\alpha}(x)\|_{1}\leq c_{1}\|x\|_{1}
for some
c_{1}>0
and all
x\in L^{1}
, then, given a fully symmetric space
E\subset\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
, we have
A_{\alpha}(E)\subset E
for every
\alpha\in\Lambda
and
A_{\alpha}(x)\to\widetilde{x}\in E
a.u. (respectively, b.a.u.) for every
x\in E
.

Proof.

We will prove the statement for a.u. convergence. Proof for b.a.u. convergence is similar. Assume, without loss of generality, that
x^{*}=x
and fix
\varepsilon>0
. By Proposition
3.2
, given
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist
y_{m}\in L^{1}
and
z_{m}\in\mathcal{M}
such that
\displaystyle\|z_{m}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{m}
and
x=y_{m}+z_{m}
. Since the net
\{A_{\alpha}(y_{m})\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
is a.u. Cauchy, there exist
e_{m}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
and
\gamma_{m}\in I
such that

\tau(e_{m}^{\perp})\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{m}}\text{ \ \ and \ \ }\|(A_{\alpha}(y_{m})-A_{\beta}(y_{m}))e_{m}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{m}\,\ \ \forall\ \alpha,\beta\geq\gamma_{m}.

Thus, letting
e=\bigwedge_{m}e_{m}
, we obtain

\tau(e^{\perp})\leq\varepsilon\text{ \ \ and \ \ }\|(A_{\alpha}(y_{m})-A_{\beta}(y_{m}))e\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{m}\,\ \ \forall\ \alpha,\beta\geq\gamma_{m}.

Therefore, given
\alpha,\beta\geq\gamma_{m}
, it follows that

\begin{split}\|(A_{\alpha}(x)-A_{\beta}(x))e\|_{\infty}&=\|(A_{\alpha}(y_{m})-A_{\beta}(y_{m}))e\|_{\infty}\\
&+\|(A_{\alpha}(z_{m})-A_{\beta}(z_{m}))e\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1+2c_{\infty}}{m},\end{split}

so the net
\{A_{\alpha}(x)\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
is a.u. Cauchy. Since, by Corollary
3.1
,
\{A_{\alpha}(x)\}_{\alpha\in I}\subset\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
, and, by Theorem
2.3
, the space
\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
is a.u. complete, we conclude that there exists
\widehat{x}\in\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
such that

A_{\alpha}(x)\to\widehat{x}\in\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
a.u.

Let now
E\subset\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
be a fully symmetric space, and assume, in addition, that
\|A_{\alpha}(x)\|_{1}\leq c_{1}\|x\|_{1}
for some
c_{1}>0
and all
x\in L^{1}
. Put
c=\max\{c_{\infty},c_{1}\}
and denote
\widetilde{A}_{\alpha}=c^{-1}A_{\alpha}(x)
,
\alpha\in\Lambda
. As
\{\widetilde{A}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}\subset DS
, we conclude, in view of Proposition
3.1
, that
\widetilde{A}_{\alpha}(x)\prec\prec x
for all
x\in L^{1}+\mathcal{M}
, hence
\widetilde{A}_{\alpha}(E)\subset E
, and so
A_{\alpha}(E)\subset E
for all
\alpha\in\Lambda
.

Next, let
x\in E
. We have
\widetilde{A}_{\alpha}(x)\to\widehat{x}\in\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
a.u. To simplify the argument, choose, as in the proof of Theorem
2.3
, a sequence
\{\widetilde{A}_{n}(x)\}\subset\{\widetilde{A}_{\alpha}(x)\}
with
\widetilde{A}_{n}(x)\to\widehat{x}
a.u. Then it is clear that
\widetilde{A}_{n}(x)\to\widehat{x}
in measure, which, by
[
10
, Lemma 3.4]
, implies that
\mu_{t}(\widetilde{A}_{n}(x))\to\mu_{t}(\widehat{x})
almost everywhere on
((0,\infty),\nu)
. Further, as
\widetilde{A}_{n}(x)\prec\prec x
, we have

\int_{0}^{s}\mu_{t}(\widetilde{A}_{n}(x))dt\leq\int_{0}^{s}\mu_{t}(x)dt\ \ \ \forall\ s>0,\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

Therefore, as
\mu_{t}(\widetilde{A}_{n}(x))\to\mu_{t}(\widehat{x})
almost everywhere on
((0,s),\nu)
, it follows from Fatou’s lemma that

\int_{0}^{s}\mu_{t}(\widehat{x})dt\leq\int_{0}^{s}\mu_{t}(x)dt\ \ \ \forall\ s>0,

that is
\widehat{x}\prec\prec x
. Since
E
is a fully symmetric space, we conclude that
\widehat{x}\in E
, hence
A_{\alpha}(x)\to c\,\widehat{x}\in E
a.u., completing the argument.
∎

4.
Applications

For the sake of convenience, let us state the following immediate corollary of Theorem
3.1
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
A_{\alpha}:L^{1}+\mathcal{M}\to L^{1}+\mathcal{M}
,
\alpha\in\Lambda
, be a net of linear selfadjoint maps such that

(1)

there exist
c_{1}>0
and
c_{\infty}>0
satisfying conditions

\|A_{\alpha}(x)\|_{1}\leq c_{1}\|x\|_{1}\ \ \forall\ x\in L^{1}\text{\, \ and\, \ }\|A_{\alpha}(x)\|_{\infty}\leq c_{\infty}\|x\|_{\infty}\ \ \forall\ x\in\mathcal{M};

(2)

the net
\{A_{\alpha}(x)\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
is a.u. (b.a.u.) Cauchy for each
x\in L^{1}
.

Then, given a fully symmetric space
E\subset L^{0}
with
\mathbf{1}\notin E
and
x\in E
, there exists
\widehat{x}\in E
such that

A_{\alpha}(x)\to\widehat{x}
a.u. (respectively, b.a.u.).

We will now present some applications of Proposition
4.1
.

Given
d\in\mathbb{N}
, denote

\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}=\{\,{\bf n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{d}):\,n_{1},\dots,n_{d}\in\mathbb{N}\cup{0}\}.

Let
\big\{{\bf n}_{\alpha}=(n_{1}(\alpha),\dots,n_{d}(\alpha))\big\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
be a net in
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
. We write
{\bf n}_{\alpha}\to\infty
if
n_{i}(\alpha)\to\infty
for each
1\leq i\leq d
. Denote
|{\bf n}_{\alpha}|^{\prime}=n_{1}(\alpha)\cdot\ldots\cdot n_{d}(\alpha)
, and let
|{\bf n}_{\alpha}|
stand for
|{\bf n}_{\alpha}|^{\prime}
, with
n_{i}(\alpha)
replaced by
1
whenever
n_{i}(\alpha)=0
.

Assume that
\{T_{1},\dots,T_{d}\}\subset DS^{+}
and let
x\in L^{1}+\mathcal{M}
. If
\alpha\in\Lambda
, set

(2)

A_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{1}{|{\bf n}_{\alpha}|}\sum_{k_{1}\leq n_{1}(\alpha),\dots,k_{d}\leq n_{d}(\alpha)}T_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot T_{d}^{k_{d}}(x).

Then
\{A_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
is a net of linear selfadjoint (positive) maps in
L^{1}+\mathcal{M}
that naturally satisfies condition (1) of Proposition
4.1
. To ensure that this net also satisfies condition (2) of Proposition
4.1
, we need an additional assumption that the net
{\bf n}_{\alpha}\to\infty
remains in a sector of
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
(see
[
13
, Ch. 6, § 6.2, 6.3]
for the sequential, commutative case):

Definition 4.1
.

A net
{\bf n}_{\alpha}\to\infty
is said to
remain in a sector
of
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
if there exists
0<c_{0}<\infty
such that

\displaystyle\frac{n_{i}(\alpha)}{n_{j}(\alpha)}\leq c_{0}\ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq i,j\leq d\text{\,\ and\,\ }\alpha\in\Lambda,

assuming that
n_{j}(\alpha)\neq 0
.

Now, straightforwardly adjusting to the net setting the argument in
[
6
]
that yields
[
6
, Theorem 5.3]
, we derive from Yeadon’s weak-type (1,1) maximal inequality
[
23
, Theorem 1]
a weak-type
(1,1)
maximal inequality for the net
\{A_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
in (
2
) and use it to show that it is b.a.u. Cauchy for every
x\in L^{1}
. Thus, as a corollary of Proposition
4.1
, we arrive at the following (cf.
[
6
, Theorem 5.6]
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\{T_{1},\dots,T_{d}\}\subset DS^{+}
be a commuting family, and let
E\subset\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
be a fully symmetric space, for example, any space
L^{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
. Then, given
x\in E
, the net
\{A_{\alpha}(x)\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
given by (
2
) converges b.a.u. to some
\widehat{x}\in E
, provided the net
{\bf n}_{\alpha}\to\infty
remains in a sector of
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
.

Theorem
4.1
for
d=1
yields the following version of
[
23
, Theorem 2]
(recall that the class of contractions
\alpha
considered in
[
23
]
coincides with the class
DS^{+}
modulo a unique extension - see
[
2
, Proposition 1.1]
).

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
T\in DS^{+}
, and let
\{n_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\subset\mathbb{N}
be a net such that
n_{\alpha}\to\infty
. Then, given
x\in L^{1}
, the net

A_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{1}{n_{\alpha}}\sum_{k=0}^{n_{\alpha}-1}T^{k}(x),\ \alpha\in\Lambda,

converges b.a.u. to some
\widehat{x}\in L^{1}
.

Next, let
\mathbb{C}_{1}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
. A function
p:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}
is called a
trigonomertic polynomial
if
p(t)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\lambda_{j}^{t}
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\{w_{j}\}_{1}^{n}\subset\mathbb{C}
, and
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{1}^{n}\subset\mathbb{C}_{1}
. A Lebesgue measurable function
\beta:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}
will be called a
bounded Besicovitch function
if
\|\beta\|_{\infty}<\infty
, and for every
\varepsilon>0
there is a trigonometric polynomial
p_{\varepsilon}
such that

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}|\beta(s)-p_{\varepsilon}(s)|ds<\varepsilon.

Let a semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}\subset DS
be
strongly continuous in

L^{1}
, that is,

\|T_{t}(x)-T_{t_{0}}(x)\|_{1}\to 0\text{ \ \ whenever\ \ }t\to t_{0}\text{ \ \ for all\ \ }x\in L^{1}.

Then, as it is shown in
[
5
]
, given a Lebesgue measurable function
\beta:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}
with
\|\beta\|_{\infty}<\infty
, for any
x\in L^{1}
and
t>0
there exists the Bochner integral

B_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)T_{s}(x)ds\in L^{1}.

Note that if
\|\beta\|_{\infty}\neq 0
, then for each
t>0
the operator

\|\beta\|_{\infty}^{-1}B_{t}:\,L^{1}\to L^{1}

can be uniquely extended to a Dunford-Schwartz operator - see
[
2
]
.

The following theorem was proved in
[
5
]
. Here we present an alternative, straightforward, argument.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}\subset DS^{+}
be a strongly continuous semigroup in
L^{1}
, and let
\beta(t)
be a bounded Besicovitch function. If
\mathbf{1}\notin E\subset L^{0}
is a fully symmetric space, then, given
x\in E
, the flow
\{B_{t}(x)\}_{t>0}
converges b.a.u. to some
\widehat{x}\in E
as
t\to\infty
. In particular,
B_{t}(x)\to\widehat{x}\in L^{p}
b.a.u. for every
x\in L^{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
.

Proof.

It is clear that the net
\{B_{t}\}_{t>0}
satisfies condition (1) of Proposition
4.1
. As the proof of
[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
shows that
\{B_{t}(x)\}_{t>0}
is b.a.u. Cauchy for all
x\in L^{1}
, that is, condition (2) of Proposition
4.1
is also satisfied, the assertion follows.
∎

Introduction and statement of result

A classical problem in stochastic geometry

[
1
,
3
]

is to determine
the probability of fully covering a region of Euclidean

d
-space
with a union of deterministic or random shapes centred on random points.
There are applications in topological data analysis, wireless communications,
and also in biology and chemistry, as discussed in e.g.
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
8
,
9
]
.
An asymptotic
answer to this question was provided in the 1980s by
Hall
[
2
]
and Janson
[
4
]
(Theorem
1.1
below).
In this paper we give a more quantitative version of that result.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
let
\mathbb{Q}
be a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}_{+}:=[0,\infty)
.
Given
t>0
, let
\xi_{t}
denote a Poisson process in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}

with intensity measure
t\lambda_{d}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
, where

\lambda_{d}
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Given also
r>0
, and
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
define the
k
-occupied set

Z_{k}(\xi_{t},r)
and

k
-vacant set

V_{k}(\xi_{t},r)
where for
any simple counting measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
r\geq 0
,
we define

Z_{k}(\mu,r):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\#\{(x,a)\in\mu:y\in B(x,ra)\}\geq k\};\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ V_{k}(\mu,r)=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus Z_{k}(\mu,r),

where for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
s>0
,
we set
B(x,s):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|y-x\|\leq s\}
, and

\|\cdot\|
is the Euclidean norm.
Then
Z_{k}(\xi_{t},r)
is the set of locations that are covered
at least
k
times by
a
spherical Poisson Boolean model

(SPBM)
consisting of balls (known as
grains
)
with independent
\mathbb{Q}_{r}
-distributed random radii centred
on the points of a homogeneous Poisson process of intensity
t
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Here
\mathbb{Q}_{r}
denotes the distribution of
rY
, and

Y
denotes a random variable with distribution
\mathbb{Q}
.

Let
A
denote a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with interior denoted

A^{o}
. Assume throughout
that
\lambda_{d}(A)=\lambda_{d}(A^{o})>0
.
This paper is concerned with the asymptotic as
t\to\infty
of
the
coverage threshold

R_{t,k}
defined by

\displaystyle R_{t,k}:=\inf\{r>0:A\subset Z_{k}(\xi_{t},r)\}.

Let

\theta_{d}:=\lambda_{d}(B_{1}(o))=\pi^{d/2}/\Gamma(1+d/2)
, the
volume of the
d
-dimensional unit ball.
Here
o
denotes the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The following result identifies the weak limit of
R_{t,k}
.

Theorem 1.1

(Hall, Janson)
.

Suppose
0<\mathbb{E}[Y^{d+\varepsilon}]<\infty
for some
\varepsilon>0
.
Set
\alpha=\theta_{d}\mathbb{E}[Y^{d}]
.
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and define
(r_{t})_{t>1}
taking values
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
by the relation

\displaystyle\alpha tr_{t}^{d}=\left(\log t+(d+k-2)\log\log t+\beta\right)\vee 0,

(1.1)

where we use
u\vee v
for the maximum of two real numbers

u,v
.
Then

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[R_{t,k}\leq r_{t}]=\exp(-c_{d,k,Y}\lambda_{d}(A)e^{-\beta})

where the constant
c_{d,k,Y}
is given by

\displaystyle c_{d,k,Y}:=\frac{1}{d!(k-1)!}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(1+d/2)}{\Gamma((d+1)/2)}\Big)^{d-1}\frac{(\mathbb{E}[Y^{d-1}])^{d}}{(\mathbb{E}[Y^{d}])^{d-1}}.

(1.2)

The case
k=1
of
Theorem
1.1
is immediate from
[
2
, Theorem 2]
. The
general case
can be derived from
the result in
[
4
]
; see

[
8
, Lemma 7.2]
and
[
9
, Lemma 3.2]
.

Another way to formulate this theorem is the convergence in distribution

\alpha tR_{t,k}^{d}-\log t-(d+k-2)\log\log t\to G+\log(c_{d,k,Y}\lambda_{d}(A)),

where
G
is a standard Gumbel random variable.

Previous proofs of Theorem
1.1
do not
provide any quantitative error bound for finite
t
, or any rate of convergence.
Our aim in this paper is to provide a new proof
(or at least, one with some new ingredients)
that gives a rate of convergence in the Hall-Janson
theorem. We do so under the extra assumption that
d\geq 2
;
when
d\geq 3
we also require
a stronger moment condition on
Y
than in Theorem
1.1
.

If
f(t)\in\mathbb{R}
and
g(t)\in(0,\infty)
are defined
for all large enough
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we say
f(t)=O(g(t))
as

t\to\infty
if

\limsup_{t\to\infty}(|f(t)|/g(t))<\infty
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
0<\mathbb{E}[Y^{2d-2}]<\infty
and

0<\mathbb{E}[Y^{d+\varepsilon}]<\infty
for some
\varepsilon>0
.
Assume that
d\geq 2
,
let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
and let
(r_{t})_{t>1}
taking values in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
be given
by the relation

\displaystyle\alpha tr_{t}^{d}=\left(\log t+(d+k-2)\log\log t+\beta+\frac{(d+k-2)^{2}\log\log t}{\log t}\right)\vee 0.

(1.3)

As
t\to\infty
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[R_{t,k}\leq r_{t}]-\exp(-c_{d,k,Y}\lambda_{d}(A)e^{-\beta})=O\Big(\lambda_{d}(A^{\prime}\setminus A^{\prime\prime})+\frac{1}{\log t}\Big),

(1.4)

where
A^{\prime}:=A^{\prime}(t):=A\oplus B(o,\sqrt{r_{t}})
and

\oplus
denotes the Minkowski sum,
while
A^{\prime\prime}:=A^{\prime\prime}(t):=\{x\in A:B(x,\sqrt{dr_{t}})\subset A\}
.

We note that if
A
has a smooth or polytopal boundary, then the
right hand side of (
1.4
) is simply
O(1/(\log t))
.

We sketch the main ideas of our proof. Let us refer to the closure of
each connected component of
V_{k}(\xi_{t},r_{t})
as a
vacant region
.
A percolation argument shows that with high probability, all vacant regions
intersecting
A
are confined to the slightly bigger region
A^{\prime}
.
The lowest point of each vacant region is a
local minimum of the closure of
V_{k}(\xi_{t},r_{t})
, and the mean number
of local minima in
A
or in
A^{\prime}

can be computed using the multivariate Mecke formula.
We divide
A
into moderately small boxes and
show by a second moment calculation (again using the Mecke formula)
that the probability of there being a box containing
a pair of local minima
decays like
1/\log t
(it is this step in the proof that takes the most work).
One can then use independence of
contributions of different boxes, and standard Poisson-binomial
approximation, to get the result.

For
k=1
one can
alternatively
use a version
of the Chen-Stein method
to show that the number of such local minima
in
A
is approximately Poisson;
see the earlier version of this paper
[
10
]
.
Unlike that method,
the box method here works for general
k
.

If we were to define
(r_{t})_{t>1}
by the simpler relation
(
1.1
) rather than by (
1.3
), we would
get (
1.4
) with

O((\log\log t)/\log t)
instead of

O(1/\log t)
on the right. This can be done by following
the proof of Theorem
1.2
with a few modifications,
mainly in the
proof of Proposition
3.1
.

We now identify various possible extensions to our result that
would be of interest.

We restrict attention to grains that are Euclidean balls of possibly
random radius. More general distributions for the shapes of the
grains in the Boolean model
are considered both by Hall
[
2
]
and by Janson
[
4
]
;
for example, a uniformly randomly rotated random polygon.
It would be natural to try to extend our result to these settings too.

Hall
[
2
]
shows that for
k=1
, the number
of vacant regions intersecting
A
is asymptotically Poisson
with parameter
c_{d,1,Y}\lambda_{d}(A)e^{-\beta}
.
It may be possible to extend our method
to give a bound on the rate of convergence in total variation distance.
Compared to the argument given here, the main missing step would be
to show that with high probability each vacant region within
A^{\prime}

has exactly one
local minimum (for our result here, all we need is the fact that each
vacant region has at least one local minimum).

Our method, in particular, the proof of Proposition
4.5
,
does not appear to work for
d=1
.

In
[
8
]
and
[
9
]
, analogous results to Theorem
1.1

are presented for the
restricted
spherical Poisson Boolean model where
one discards all grains centred outside
A
, and considers
coverage of
A
by
the remaining grains. The proof of these results relies heavily on
Theorem
1.1
. Using Theorem
1.2
, it may
be possible to obtain rates of convergence
in those results too, at least for
d\geq 3
.
(The coverage process on the boundary of
A
is
a
(d-1)
-dimensional SPBM, so we may need
a rate of convergence for the unrestricted SPBM in
d=1

to derive a rate for the restricted SPBM in
d=2
.)

Let
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be compact with
A\subset B^{o}
.
Janson
[
4
]
proves a variant of Theorem
1.1
where
the Poisson point process
\xi_{t}
is replaced by
a
binomial
point process in
B\otimes\mathbb{R}_{+}
, given by a collection of

n
random elements of
B\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
with probability
distribution
\lambda_{d}(B)^{-1}\lambda_{d}|_{B}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
, with
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
as
n\to\infty
with the scaling parameter
r
now depending on
n
.
It would be of interest to provide a bound on the
rate of convergence for this result too.

It follows from our result that if
A
has a nice boundary
then there exist positive finite constants
c^{\prime},t_{0}
such that

|\mathbb{P}[R_{t,k}\leq r_{t}]-\exp(-c_{d,k,Y}\lambda_{d}(A)e^{-\beta})|

is bounded by
c^{\prime}/\log t
for all
t>t_{0}
. In principle, one
could obtain numerical values for
c^{\prime}
and
t_{0}
by going through
our proof. For
d=2
,
Y\equiv 1
,
numerical upper and lower bounds
for the probability of coverage can be found in
[
3
,
5
]
.
Those bounds are not asymptotically sharp.

.

where we say a point

.
Also for any

,

Let

\displaystyle 0<\zeta<\frac{\mathbb{E}[\min(Y,1/2)^{d}]}{8d\mathbb{E}[Y^{d}]}.

(2.2)

We decompose
\xi_{t}

\xi^{\prime}_{t}\cup\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)

\xi^{\prime}_{t}:\xi\cap(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,t^{\zeta}]);\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \xi^{\prime\prime}_{t}:\xi\cap(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(t^{\zeta},\infty)).

\displaystyle F_{t}(D):=\sum_{\psi\subset\xi^{\prime}_{t}}h^{(r_{t})}_{D}(\psi){\bf 1}\{q^{(r_{t})}(\psi)\in V_{k}(\xi^{\prime}_{t}\setminus\psi,r_{t})\}.

\mathbb{P}[F_{t}(A^{o})=0]

Before starting on this,
we give two alternative characterisations of the ‘local minimum’

Given distinct

g(q,\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\})
to be 1, if and only if the following

)
; and

(ii) there is no hyperplane

such that all

.

Moreover, we define the indicator function
\tilde{g}(q,\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\})

,

.

Let

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{d}

\displaystyle=g(q^{(r)}(\{\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{d}\}),\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\})

\displaystyle=\tilde{g}(q^{(r)}(\{\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{d}\}),\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\}).

.
Then for

Given

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
a_{1},\dots,a_{d+1}\in(0,\infty)
.
Let
\ell\in[d+1]
.

(i) If
\ell\leq d
then for

\lambda_{d}^{\ell}
-almost all
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\ell}

the set
S_{\ell}:=S_{\ell}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}):=\cap_{i=1}^{\ell}\partial B(x_{i},a_{i})
is either
a
(d-\ell)
-dimensional sphere or the empty set.

(ii) For
\lambda_{d}^{d+1}
-almost
all
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d+1})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{d+1}

we have
S_{d+1}=\varnothing
.

Proof.

The result is clear for
\ell=1
.
Suppose
\ell=2
. Set
D=\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|
.

Suppose also
d=2
.
Then
S_{2}=\varnothing
if
D>a_{1}+a_{2}
or

D<|a_{1}-a_{2}|
. If
|a_{1}-a_{2}|<D<a_{1}+a_{2}
then

S_{2}
has two points and they are mirror to each other
in the line containing
x_{1},x_{2}
; this is geometrically obvious
and can be verified in the special case where

x_{1}=(0,0)
and
x_{2}=(D,0)
by solving the two
simultaneous equations for the two coordinates
of any point of intersection.

Now suppose
d\geq 3
and
\ell=2
. Suppose

|a_{1}-a_{2}|<D<a_{1}+a_{2}
, and assume without
loss of generality that
x_{1}=o
and
x_{2}=De_{1}
,
where we set
e_{1}:=(1,0,\ldots,0)
.
Given
u\in\mathbb{R}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
, we have

(u,z)\in S_{2}\Longleftrightarrow(u,\|z\|,0\ldots,0)\in S_{2}.

Therefore picking
u_{0}\in\mathbb{R},v>0
such that

(u_{0},v,0,\ldots,0)\in S_{2}
(by the case
d=2
already considered there
is just one such choice of
u_{0},v
),
we have that
S_{2}
is the intersection
of
\partial B(u_{0}e_{1},v)
with the hyperplane perpendicular
to
e_{1}
through
u_{0}e_{1}
, and therefore is a
(d-2)
-dimensional
sphere.

Now suppose
d\geq\ell=3
. Then
S_{3}=S_{2}\cap\partial B(x_{3},a_{3})

and by the case already considered, for almost all
(x_{1},x_{2})
,

S_{2}
is either empty or a
(d-2)
-dimensional sphere contained
in a
(d-1)
-dimensional affine subspace
H
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
In the latter case, since

\partial B(x_{3},a_{3})\cap H
is (for
\lambda_{d}
-almost all
x_{3}
)
either empty or a
(d-2)
-dimensional sphere
in
H
, by the case
\ell=2
its intersection with
S_{2}
is,
for almost all choices of
x_{3}
, either a
(d-3)
-dimensional
sphere or the empty set.

Continuing in this way (inductively in
\ell
) we obtain the result
(i). Part (ii) then follows
from the fact that
S_{d}
has at most two points for

\lambda_{d}^{d}
-almost all choices of
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
r>0
,
\ell\in[d]
.
Almost surely, for all
\ell
-tuples
of distinct
\mathbf{x}_{1}=(x_{1},a_{1}),\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{\ell}=(x_{\ell},a_{\ell})\in\xi_{t}
and
satisfying

, where we set

S_{\ell}:=S_{\ell}(\mathbf{x}_{1},...,\mathbf{x}_{\ell}):=\cap_{i=1}^{\ell}\partial B(x_{i},ra_{i}),

the set

is a
(d-\ell)
-dimensional sphere. Also,
almost surely,
S_{d+1}(\mathbf{x}_{1},...,\mathbf{x}_{d+1})=\varnothing
for distinct
\mathbf{x}_{1},...,\mathbf{x}_{d+1}\in\xi_{t}
.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
2.3

and application of the multivariate Mecke formula (see e.g.

[
6
]
) and Fubini’s theorem.
∎

Next we use a percolation argument to show that with high probability,
all components of
V_{k}(\xi^{\prime}_{t},r_{t})
intersecting

are confined
to the set
A^{\prime}:=A^{\prime}(t):=A\oplus B(o,\sqrt{r_{t}})

Lemma 2.5
.

Given
t>0
, let
\mathscr{V}_{k,t}

be the event that every connected component of the vacant set

V_{k}(\xi^{\prime}_{t},r_{t})
that intersects
A

is contained in
A^{\prime}
.
Then there exists
c>0
such that for all large enough

t
,
\mathbb{P}[\mathscr{V}_{k,t}]\geq 1-\exp(-ct^{1/(3d)})
.

Proof.

Choose
\delta>0
such that

\mathbb{P}[Y>\sqrt{d}\delta]>2\delta
.
Given
t
, partition
\mathbb{R}^{d}
into cubes
of side length
\delta r_{t}
indexed by
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such
that for each
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, the
cube indexed by
z
is centred on
\delta r_{t}z
.
We say a collection of cubes is a
path

if the indexing lattice points make a path
in the graph
(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\sim)
where for

x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we put
x\sim y
if and only if

\|x-y\|_{\infty}=1
(i.e., allow diagonal connections).
If
\mathscr{V}_{k,t}
does not occur, then there exists a
path of cubes
such that

1)

at least one constituting cube
Q
of the path intersects

A
;

2)

the cardinality of the path
is at least
n(t):=\lceil\sqrt{r_{t}}/(\sqrt{d}\delta r_{t})\rceil=\lceil r_{t}^{-1/2}/(\sqrt{d}\delta)\rceil
;

3)

each constituting cube
Q
of the animal satisfies
Q\cap V_{k}(\xi^{\prime}_{t},r_{t})\neq\varnothing
.

Item 3) implies that
\#(\xi^{\prime}_{t}\cap(Q\times[\sqrt{d}\delta,\infty)]))\leq(k-1)

because otherwise
Q\cap V_{k}(\xi^{\prime}_{t},r_{t})=\varnothing
.
Observe that provided
t
is sufficiently large,

t(\lambda_{d}\otimes\mathbb{Q})(Q\times[\sqrt{d}\delta,t^{\zeta}])\geq t\delta(\delta r_{t})^{d}
,
and thus by e.g.
[
7
, Lemma 1.2]

\mathbb{P}[\#(\xi^{\prime}_{t}\cap(Q\times[\sqrt{d}\delta,\infty)))\leq k-1]\leq\exp(-(t/2)\delta^{d+1}r_{t}^{d}).

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}

the number of paths of cardinality
n
starting from the origin
is at most

.
Hence there is a constant
c^{\prime}
such that
the number of paths satisfying 1) and 2)
is at most
c^{\prime}r_{t}^{-d}3^{dn(t)}
,
and hence for
t
large is at most

t3^{dn(t)}
.
Provided
t
is also large enough so that

\delta^{d+1}tr_{t}^{d}>4\log(3^{d})
and

r_{t}^{-1/2}/(d^{1/2}\delta)\geq t^{1/(3d)}
,
applying the union bound leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\mathscr{V}_{k,t}^{c}]

\displaystyle\leq t3^{dn(t)}\exp(-n(t)(t/2)\delta^{d+1}r_{t}^{d})

\displaystyle\leq t\exp(-(\log 3^{d})n(t))

\displaystyle\leq t\exp(-(\log 3^{d})t^{1/(3d)}),

and the result follows.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

For all
t
,
it holds that
\mathbb{P}[\{R_{t,k}>r_{t}\}\cap\mathscr{V}_{k,t}]\leq\mathbb{P}[F_{t}(A^{\prime})>0].

Proof.

Suppose that event
\mathscr{V}_{k,t}
occurs, and
R_{t,k}>r_{t}
.
Then we can and do choose

.
Let
V_{z}
denote the
closure of the connected component of the vacant set

V_{k}(\xi^{\prime}_{t},r_{t})
containing
z
. Then, since
we assume
\mathscr{V}_{k,t}
occurs,

V_{z}\subset A^{\prime}
.

Let
q
denote a lowest point of
V_{z}
, i.e.

\langle q,e_{d}\rangle\leq\langle x,e_{d}\rangle

for all
x\in V_{z}
.
Then
q\in A^{\prime}
.

For any simple counting measure
\mu

\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
and

, we
define SPBM

to be the collection of all
balls of the form

We claim that almost surely,

lies on the boundary of exactly
d
balls in

\mathrm{SPBM}(\xi^{\prime}_{t},r_{t})
, and in the interior of exactly
k-1
of these

It is clear that
q\in B^{o}
for at most
k-1
balls

and

;
otherwise either
q
lies in the interior of the
k
-occupied set

Z_{k}(\xi^{\prime}_{t},r_{t})
or
q
is not a lowest point of
V_{z}
.
Hence
q
has to lie on at least one sphere.

Suppose that
q
lies on exactly
m
spheres with
1\leq m\leq d-1
. In particular, it lies on the hyperplanes tangent to the spheres at
q
. Let
I
denote the intersection of these hyperplanes. Then the dimension of
I
is at least 1. For some small
\varepsilon>0
, any point in
(B(q,\varepsilon)\cap I)\setminus\{q\}

lies in the interior of
V_{z}
. Necessarily there exists a point

q^{\prime}\in B(q,\varepsilon)\cap I
that is as low as
q
such that for some

\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
, we have

.
It follows that the point

is strictly lower than

, a contradiction. Also, by Corollary
2.4

the intersection of
d+1
distinct spheres is empty.
Therefore
q
lies on the boundary of exactly
d

balls in SPBM
(\xi^{\prime}_{t},r_{t})
, denoted
B_{1},\ldots,B_{d}
say.
Moreover, if
q
lies in the interior
of fewer than
k-1
balls, denoted
say
B^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,B^{\prime}_{\ell}
with
\ell<k-1
,
then by repeating the above argument
for

we see there is a point
q^{\prime}
near
q
and lower than
q
that
lies in none of
B_{1},\ldots,B_{d-1}
and therefore in the interior
of at most
k-1
balls, namely
B^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,B^{\prime}_{\ell}
and

. This gives a contradiction, which
completes the proof of the claim.

By Corollary
2.4
with
\ell=d
and
r=r_{t}
,
there exist distinct

\mathbf{x}_{1},...,\mathbf{x}_{d}\in\xi_{t}
with
S_{d}(\mathbf{x}_{1},...,\mathbf{x}_{d})=\{q,p\}

is the mirror of
q
with respect to the affine

For

we write
\mathbf{x}_{i}=(x_{i},a_{i})
with

and

lies on exactly
d

Next we claim that
h^{(r_{t})}(\{\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{d}\})=1
.
Indeed, since

It follows from all the above that

3
Convergence of expectation

In this section we prove the convergence of the expectation of

Recall

Given
(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{d})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}

\displaystyle G(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}):=\frac{1}{d!}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d(d-1)}}h^{(1)}(\{(o,a_{1}),(x_{2},a_{2}),\ldots,(x_{d},a_{d})\})\lambda_{d}^{d-1}(d(x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})),

Proposition 3.1

Suppose for all

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[F_{t}(D(t))]-c_{0}\alpha^{1-d}((k-1)!)^{-1}\lambda_{d}(D(t))e^{-\beta}=O\Big(\frac{1}{\log t}\Big).

(3.2)

Proof.

Notice that for all

we have
that
\xi^{\prime}_{t}(\{(y,b):x\in B(y,r_{t}b)\})

is Poisson distributed with mean

t\int_{[0,t^{\zeta}]}\theta_{d}(br_{t})^{d}\mathbb{Q}(db)

and by the calculation at (

),

t\int_{[0,t^{\zeta}]}\theta_{d}(br_{t})^{d}\mathbb{Q}(db)=\alpha tr_{t}^{d}+O(t^{-\varepsilon\zeta/2})

and this probability does not depend on

.
Therefore
by (

) and
the multivariate Mecke formula, writing just

for

we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[F_{t}(D)]=\frac{t^{d}e^{-\alpha tr_{t}^{d}}(\alpha tr_{t}^{d})^{k-1}(1+O(\frac{1}{\log t}))}{d!(k-1)!}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb{Q}(da_{i})\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{d}}

\displaystyle h_{D}^{(r_{t})}(\{(x_{1},a_{1}),\ldots,(x_{d},a_{d})\})\lambda_{d}^{d}(d(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})).

\displaystyle\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{d}}h_{D}^{(r_{t})}(\{(x_{1},a_{1}),\ldots,(x_{d},a_{d})\})\lambda_{d}^{d}(d(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}))

\displaystyle=\lambda_{d}(D)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d(d-1)}}h^{(r_{t})}(\{(o,a_{1}),(x_{2},a_{2}),\ldots,(x_{d},a_{d})\})\lambda_{d}^{d-1}(d(x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})),

\displaystyle h^{(r_{t})}(\{(o,a_{1}),(x_{2},a_{2}),\ldots,(x_{d},a_{d})\})=h^{(1)}(\{(o,a_{1}),(r_{t}^{-1}x_{2},a_{2}),\ldots,(r_{t}^{-1}x_{d},a_{d})\}).

\displaystyle\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{d}}h_{D}^{(r_{t})}(\{(x_{1},a_{1}),\ldots,(x_{d},a_{d})\})\lambda_{d}^{d}(d(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}))=d!r_{t}^{d(d-1)}\lambda_{d}(D)G(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}).

)

)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[F_{t}(D)]

\displaystyle=t^{d}e^{-\alpha tr_{t}^{d}}r_{t}^{d(d-1)}\lambda_{d}(D)(\alpha tr_{t}^{d})^{k-1}((k-1)!)^{-1}c_{0}\Big(1+O\big(\frac{1}{\log t}\big)\Big)

The expected sum of

{S}_{\bar{\mathbf{x}},Q_{t,i}^{-}}^{(2a)}

is perhaps the most delicate one to deal with,

and before going into the details in the
proof of Lemma

below, we first provide some intuition
(it is easiest at first to keep in mind the case where

\mathbb{Q}
is the Dirac measure
\delta_{1}

When computing the probability that a pair

and
\bar{\mathbf{y}}
both contribute to
the sum, we get an extra exponential factor with
-t
times
the

-measure
of

in the exponent, due to vacancy in this set.
This measure is roughly proportional to

We shall make a change of variables so as
to integrate over
q_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}
and over

,
along with the polar coordinates
of the points of
\bar{\mathbf{x}}
relative to
q_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}

and the points of
\bar{\mathbf{y}}
relative to
q_{\bar{\mathbf{y}}}
.

When

is small the

-measure
of
B(q_{\bar{\mathbf{y}}})\setminus B(q_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}})

can be so small that our extra exponential factor
does not provide much decay. While the volume of the region of integration of

z
where
\|z\|
is small is itself small, it turns
out that the above argument is not quite enough on its own
to give the required decay for the expected sum of

S_{\mathbf{x},Q_{t,i}^{-}}^{(2a)}
,
and we shall require a further geometric argument that we now describe.

Given
q_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}
and
q_{\bar{\mathbf{y}}}
,
let
L
denote the hyperplane equidistant from these two points.
The condition
\bar{\mathbf{x}}\cap B(q_{\bar{\mathbf{y}}})=\bar{\mathbf{y}}\cap B(q_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}})=\varnothing
implies that
all points of
\bar{\mathbf{x}}
lie on one side of
L
and
all points of
\bar{\mathbf{y}}
lie on the other side of
L
.
Suppose
q_{\bar{\mathbf{y}}}
is lower than
q_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}
.
By the hyperplane condition in Lemma
2.1
,
at least one of the points of
\bar{\mathbf{x}}
must lie
below the hyperplane

that is parallel to
L

and contains
q_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}
, and hence
between
L
and
L^{\prime}
. Thus when
\|z\|
is small,
the spherical location of this point relative to
q_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}

is confined to a small region.
This extra constraint on the integrals provides an
extra factor of
(\log t)^{-1}
in our estimate
for the expected sum of

S_{\mathbf{x},Q_{t,i}^{-}}^{(2a)}
.

Lemma 4.5

, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[|(\bar{\mathbf{x}}\cup\xi^{\prime}_{t})\cap B(q_{\bar{\mathbf{y}}})|<k,|(\bar{\mathbf{y}}\cup\xi^{\prime}_{t})\cap B(q_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}})|<k]\leq w_{t}e^{-c_{2}tr_{t}^{d}}.

) to deduce that for any

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}\subset\xi^{\prime}_{t}}h^{(r_{t})}_{Q_{t,i}^{-}}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}){S}^{(2d)}_{\bar{\mathbf{x}},Q_{t,i}^{-}}(\xi^{\prime}_{t})\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t^{2d}}{d!^{2}}\int h^{(r_{t})}_{Q_{t,i}^{-}}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})h^{(r_{t})}_{Q_{t,i}^{-}}(\bar{\mathbf{y}})w_{t}e^{-c_{2}tr_{t}^{d}}{\bf 1}\{\|q_{\bar{\mathbf{y}}}-q_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}\|\in(r_{t},3t^{\zeta}r_{t}]\}(\lambda_{d}\otimes\mathbb{Q}^{\prime}_{t})^{2d}(d(\bar{\mathbf{x}},\bar{\mathbf{y}})).

Going into polar coordinates as before for
\bar{\mathbf{y}}
but not
\bar{\mathbf{x}}

gives us
a factor of

. Writing

,
we obtain that the last expression is
bounded by a constant times

\displaystyle t^{2d}w_{t}e^{-c_{2}tr_{t}^{d}}\Big(\int h^{(r_{t})}_{Q^{-}_{t,i}}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})(\lambda_{d}\otimes\mathbb{Q}^{\prime}_{t})^{d}(d\bar{\mathbf{x}})\Big)\times r_{t}^{d(d-1)}(t^{\zeta}r_{t})^{d}.

\displaystyle=\Big(t^{d}w_{t}\int h^{(r_{t})}_{Q_{t,i}^{-}}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})(\lambda_{d}\otimes\mathbb{Q}^{\prime}_{t})^{d}(d\bar{\mathbf{x}})\Big)\times t^{d\zeta}e^{-c_{2}tr_{t}^{d}}(tr_{t}^{d})^{d}.

The first factor (in large brackets), summed over

, equals

\mathbb{E}[F_{t}(\cup_{i\in{\cal I}(t)}Q_{t,i}^{-})]
, and
is therefore
O(1)
,
while by (
1.3
) we
have

for

Hence for some

, we have (

).
∎

The sum

{S}_{\bar{\mathbf{x}},Q_{t,i}^{-}}^{(3)}

is handled in Lemma
4.8
below.
The intuition is similar to that described for

{S}_{\bar{\mathbf{x}},Q_{t,i}^{-}}^{(2a)}

just before Lemma
4.5
, except that the final geometrical
argument described there no longer applies. What we have here instead,
since we are now considering pairs of non-disjoint
d
-tuples,
is that when we apply the Mecke formula here we are integrating
over

(d-j)
-tuples

\bar{\mathbf{y}}
for some

, rather than
d
-tuples as
the Lemma
4.5
. Having
j
fewer variables of integration
causes us to lose a factor of
(tr_{t}^{d})^{j}
,
which decreases the expectation by a factor of
(\log t)^{j}
.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times(\lambda_{d}\otimes\mathbb{Q}^{\prime}_{t})^{d-j}(d\bar{\mathbf{y}})(\lambda_{d}\otimes\mathbb{Q}^{\prime}_{t})^{d}(d\bar{\mathbf{x}}),

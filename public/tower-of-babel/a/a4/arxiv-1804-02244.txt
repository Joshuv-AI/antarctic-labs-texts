A fixed point theorem for plane homeomorphisms with the topological shadowing property.
Gonzalo Cousillas

Abstract
. In this paper we show that every homeomorphism of the plane with the topological shadowing property has a fixed point. Also, we show that a linear isomorphism of an Euclidean space has the topological shadowing property if and only if the origin is an attractor or a repeller.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
37E30, 37B20

1.
Introduction

The shadowing property was introduced in the works of Anosov and Bowen and plays an important role in several branches of dynamical systems’ theory. Roughly speaking, a system satisfies the shadowing property if every approximate orbit (pseudo-orbit) can be traced by a true one. Numerical simulations of dynamical systems always produce approximate orbits. Thus, systems with the shadowing property are those in which numerical simulations does not introduce unexpected behaviour, numerical orbits are followed by theoretical ones. For compact metric spaces the shadowing property does not depend on the choice of the metric.
But as will see in Remark
3.3
, even for a hyperbolic linear isomorphism the
shadowing property may depend on the metric.
In
[
2
]
it was introduced a notion of shadowing property for topological spaces, the idea is to change the constants in the definition by neighborhoods of the diagonal in the product space.
Even if the space is non neccesarily compact, this definition has the remarkable property of being a conjugacy invariant.

In this paper we study the topological shadowing property on euclidean spaces, mainly on the plane.
Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Every orientation preserving planar homeomorphism with the topological shadowing property has a fixed point.

The idea of the proof relies on Brouwer’s plane translation theorem, and the fact that a translation does not satisfy the topological shadowing property (see Lemma
4.1
).
The main problem we have to deal with is that even if a restriction of a homeomorphism to an invariant subset does not satisfy the shadowing property, the homeomorphism itself may satisfy it: it is possible that the orbit
shadowing a pseudo orbit belongs to the complement of the invariant set. Thus we adopt another approach and build around Brouwer’s lines and translation domains.

The paper is organized as follows, in Section
2
we set the definition of topological shadowing and some preliminary results. In Section
3
we exhibit explicit examples, we obtain that an homothety (by an homothety we mean the map
x\mapsto kx
,
|k|\neq 1
) satisfies the topological shadowing property but the map
f(x,y)=(2x,\tfrac{1}{2}y)
does not. In Section
4
we prove Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper we consider
X
a metric space and
f:X\to X
a homeomorphism. The orbit of
x\in X
is the set
\mathcal{O}(x)=\{f^{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
x\in X
is a fixed point for
f
if
f(x)=x
.
Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be positive real numbers. A sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset X
is a
\beta
-pseudo-orbit if it verifies
d(f(x_{n}),x_{n+1})<\beta
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. A sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is
\alpha
-shadowed by an orbit if there exists
y\in X
such that
d(f^{n}(y),x_{n})<\alpha
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
A homeomorphism
f:X\to X
satisfies the
metric shadowing property
if given
\alpha>0
, there exists
\beta>0
such that every
\beta
-pseudo-orbit is
\alpha
-shadowed by an orbit.

Denote by
\mathcal{C}^{+}
the set
\{\varepsilon:X\to\mathbb{R}^{+};\varepsilon\text{ is continuous}\}
.
Let
\varepsilon
and
\delta
be maps in
\mathcal{C}^{+}
, a sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset X
is a
\delta
-pseudo-orbit if it verifies
d(f(x_{n}),x_{n+1})<\delta(f(x_{n}))
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
A sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is
\varepsilon
-shadowed by an orbit if there exists
y\in X
such that
d(f^{n}(y),x_{n})<\varepsilon(x_{n})
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A homeomorphism
f:X\to X
satisfies the
topological shadowing property
if for every map
\varepsilon
in
\mathcal{C}^{+}
, there exists a map
\delta
in
\mathcal{C}^{+}
such that every
\delta
-pseudo-orbit is
\varepsilon
-shadowed by an orbit.
Denote

TSP:=\{f:X\to X,f\text{ satisfies the topological shadowing property}\}.

Proposition 2.2
.

If
X
is a locally compact metric space, definition
2.1
is equivalent to the definition given in
[
2
]
which is:

•

Given neighborhoods
E
and
D
of
\Delta_{X}
, a
D
-pseudo-orbit for
f
is a sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset X
such that
(f(x_{n}),x_{n+1})\in D
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
A
D
-pseudo-orbit is
E
shadowed by an orbit if there exists
y\in X
such that
(f^{n}(y),x_{n})\in E
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
A homeomorphism
f
satisfies the topological shadowing property if for every neighborhood
E
of
\Delta_{X}
there exists a neighborhood
D
of
\Delta_{X}
such that every
D
-pseudo-orbit is
E
shadowed by an orbit.

The idea for proving this proposition appears in
[
2
, Proposition 34]
.

Proof.

We claim that for every open neighborhood
E
of
\Delta_{X}
there exists a map
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{C}^{+}
such that
U_{\varepsilon}=\{(x,y)\in X\times X:d(x,y)<\varepsilon(x)\}\subset E
.

Let
E
be a neighborhood of
\Delta_{X}
such that
E[x]=\{y\in X:(x,y)\in E\}\subsetneq X
.
Define a function
h:X\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
by
h(x)=d(x,X-E[x])
.
Now define
\varepsilon:X\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
\varepsilon(x)=\inf\{h(y)+d(x,y):y\in X\}
.
By
[
7
, Theorem 67.2]

\varepsilon
is continuous.
Let us see that
U_{\varepsilon}[x]\subset E[x]
for every
x\in X
.
Let
y\in U_{\varepsilon}[x]
, then
d(x,y)<\varepsilon(x)=\inf\{h(z)+d(x,z):z\in X\}
.
Choose
z=x
, then
d(x,y)<\varepsilon(x)\leq h(x)=d(x,X-E[x])
.
This implies that
y\in E[x]
and the claim follows.
Conversely, a map
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{C}^{+}
defines a neighborhood of
\Delta_{X}
if we take the set
U_{\varepsilon}=\{(x,y)\in X\times X:d(x,y)<\varepsilon(x)\}
.

Taking this into account the equivalence is straightforward. ∎

Remark 2.3
.

In
[
5
]
for shadowing a pseudo-orbit the authors consider
d(f^{n}(y),x_{n})<\varepsilon(f^{n}(y))
i.e., the distance between
f^{n}(y)
and
x_{n}
depends on the value of
\varepsilon
in
f^{n}(y)
. We show it is equivalent if we consider
\varepsilon(x_{n})
instead of
\varepsilon(f^{n}(y))
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon
be an arbitrary element of
\mathcal{C}^{+}
, take
U_{\varepsilon}
defined above.
Note that for this
U_{\varepsilon}
there exists a symmetric neighborhood
\tilde{U}_{\varepsilon}
of
\Delta_{X}
such that
\tilde{U}_{\varepsilon}\subset U_{\varepsilon}
.
By the previous proposition there exists a neighborhood
D
of
\Delta_{X}
such that every
D
-pseudo-orbit is
\tilde{U}_{\varepsilon}
-shadowed.
Applying again the previous proposition, there exists
\delta\in\mathcal{C}^{+}
such that
U_{\delta}\subset D
.
Then every
\delta
-pseudo-orbit is
\tilde{U}_{\varepsilon}
-shadowed, i.e.,
(f^{n}(y),x_{n})\in\tilde{U}_{\varepsilon}
.
Since
\tilde{U}_{\varepsilon}
is symmetric, we have
(x_{n},f^{n}(y))\in\tilde{U}_{\varepsilon}
.
Thus
d(x_{n},f^{n}(y))<\varepsilon(f^{n}(y))
.
∎

Next proposition is a useful tool to create examples of homeomorphisms satisfying the topological shadowing property.

Proposition 2.4
.

[
2
, Proposition 13]

Let
X
be a first countable, locally compact, paracompact and Hausdorff space.

(1)

f\in TSP
if and only if
f^{k}\in TSP
for all nonzero
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

If
f\in TSP
and
f
is conjugate to
g
, then
g\in TSP
.

3.
Some examples on the plane

We show in this section that an homothety belongs to
TSP
, however a linear transformation with two different eigenvalues, one of module greater than
1
and another lower that
1
does not belong to
TSP
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
g:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be in the conjugacy class of the map
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
f(x,y)=(2x,\tfrac{1}{2}y)
. Then
g\notin TSP
.

Proof.

Since topological shadowing property is conjugacy invariant, we will take the map
f(x,y)=(2x,\tfrac{1}{2}y)
.
Let
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, consider
\|(x,y)\|=\max\{|x|,|y|\}
.
Let
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{C}^{+}
be such that
\varepsilon(x,y)=2^{-\|(x,y)\|}
.
Take
(x_{0},0)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
x_{0}>0
.
Given an arbitrary
\delta\in\mathcal{C}^{+}
take
(x_{0},y_{0})
,
y_{0}>0
, such that
d((x_{0},y_{0}),(x_{0},0))<\delta(x_{0},0)
.
Consider the following
\delta
-pseudo-orbit
\{(x_{n},y_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
:

(x_{n},y_{n})=\begin{cases}f^{n}(x_{0},0)&\mbox{ if }n\geq 0\\
f^{n}(x_{0},y_{0})&\mbox{ if }n<0\end{cases}.

Suppose that the shadowing property holds.
Let
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a point such that

d(f^{n}(\bar{x},\bar{y}),(x_{n},y_{n}))<\varepsilon(x_{n},y_{n}),\;n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Since
(x_{n},y_{n})=2^{n}(x_{0},0)
for
n>0
, we have

\displaystyle d(f^{n}(\bar{x},\bar{y}),(x_{n},y_{n}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(f^{n}(\bar{x},\bar{y}),2^{n}(x_{0},0))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{2^{n}|x_{0}-\bar{x}|,\tfrac{1}{2^{n}}|\bar{y}|\}.

If
\bar{x}\neq x_{0}
, then there exists
n_{0}
such that
d((x_{n},y_{n}),f^{n}(\bar{x},\bar{y}))=2^{n}|\bar{x}-x_{0}|
for
n>n_{0}
.
Since
\varepsilon(x,y)=2^{-\|(x,y)\|}
, we have that
\varepsilon(2^{n}(x_{0},0))=\tfrac{1}{2^{2^{n}\|(x_{0},0)\|}}
and it follows that
2^{n}|\bar{x}-x_{0}|<\tfrac{1}{2^{2^{n}\|(x_{0},0)\|}}
for every
n>n_{0}
.
Thus
\bar{x}=x_{0}
and
d((x_{n},0),f^{n}(\bar{x},\bar{y}))=\tfrac{1}{2^{n}}|\bar{y}|
.
So,

\tfrac{1}{2^{n}}|\bar{y}|=d(f^{n}(\bar{x},\bar{y}),(x_{n},y_{n}))<\varepsilon(2^{n}(x_{0},0))=\tfrac{1}{2^{2^{n}\|(x_{0},0)\|}},\;\;\text{for }n>0.

Then
|\bar{y}|<\tfrac{2^{n}}{2^{{2^{n}}\|(x_{0},0)\|}}\;
for
n>0
and this implies
\bar{y}=0
.
We conclude that
(\bar{x},\bar{y})=(x_{0},0)
and the only orbit that is capable of shadowing this pseudo-orbit is the orbit of
(x_{0},0)
.
Since the orbit of
(x_{0},0)
tends to
(0,0)
when
n
tends to
-\infty
and the pseudo-orbit tends to
\infty
when
n
tends to
-\infty
, we conclude that the orbit of
(x_{0},0)
cannot shadow
\{(x_{n},y_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
Thus
f\notin TSP
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

In higher dimensions a similar result can be obtained.
Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a linear isomorphism such that there exists
\lambda,\mu\in Spec(f)
,
\lambda>1
,
0<\mu<1
, then
f\notin TSP
.
We just need to create a pseudo-orbit that comes to
0
from the eigenspace asociated to
\mu
and jumps to the eigenspace asociated to
\lambda
, then the
\varepsilon
map in
\mathcal{C}^{+}
can be defined the same way as was done in the previous proposition.

Remark 3.3
.

With the usual metric a linear isomorphism satisfies the metric shadowing property (see
[
6
]
). However it is possible to choose another metric inducing the same topology such that the metric shadowing property does not hold. Take for instance
h:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
h(r)=r+r^{2}
, then consider the points in polar coordinates and define
d^{\prime}(p,q)=\|H(p)-H(q)\|
where
H(r,\theta)=(h(r),\theta)
. This way if a linear isomorphism is in the conditions of the previous theorem, following the same ideas of the previous proof it can be shown that for
(\mathbb{R}^{2},d^{\prime})
this map does not admit the metric shadowing property.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a homeomorphism.
We say that
f
satisfies the
forward topological shadowing property
if given
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{C}^{+}
, there exists
\delta\in\mathcal{C}^{+}
such that for every
\delta
-pseudo-orbit
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
there exists

such that
d(f^{n}(y),x_{n})\leq\varepsilon(x_{n})
,

.

The following lemma plays an important role for proving Theorem

.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a homeomorphism with the forward topological shadowing property. Then
f\in TSP
.

Proof.

Given
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{C}^{+}
, take
\delta\in\mathcal{C}^{+}
of the definition of the forward topological shadowing property and
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
a
\delta
-pseudo-orbit.
There exists
y_{0}
such that
d(x_{n},f^{n}(y_{0}))\leq\varepsilon(x_{n})
for
n\geq 0
, in particular
y_{0}\in\overline{B(x_{0},\varepsilon(x_{0}))}
.
Let
\{z_{n}^{(-1)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be the
\delta
-pseudo-orbit defined as
z^{(-1)}_{n}=x_{n-1}
.
Then there exists
y_{-1}
such that
d(z^{(-1)}_{n},f^{n}(y_{-1}))\leq\varepsilon(z^{(-1)}_{n})
for
n\geq 0
.
In particular
f(y_{-1})\in\overline{B(x_{0},\varepsilon(x_{0}))}
.
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\{z_{n}^{-k}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
defined by
z^{(-k)}_{n}=x_{n-k}
.
Then there exists
y_{-k}
such that

d(z^{(-k)}_{n},f^{n}(y_{-k}))<\varepsilon(z^{(-k)}_{n})\;\;\text{for }n\geq 0,\;\text{and }f^{k}(y_{-k})\in\overline{B(x_{0},\varepsilon(x_{0}))}.

Thus we have the sequence
\{f^{k}(y_{-k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\overline{B(x_{0},\varepsilon(x_{0}))}
.
Let
\tilde{y}
be a limit point of
\{f^{k}(y_{-k})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Let us show that the orbit of
\tilde{y}\;

\varepsilon
-shadows
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
Let
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
k_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that

.
Then

\displaystyle d(x_{l},f^{l}(\tilde{y}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(x_{l},f^{l}(\lim_{k_{i}\to\infty}f^{k_{i}}(y_{-k_{i}})))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{k_{i}\to\infty}d(x_{l},f^{l+k_{i}}(y_{-k_{i}}))

\displaystyle\varepsilon(x_{l})

This completes the proof.
∎

Although our main interest is to work in the plane, the next result is valid in

.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be in the conjugacy class of an homothety.
Then
g\in TSP
.

Proof.

Since the topological shadowing property is conjugacy invariant, we take
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
f(x)=2x
.
The main idea is to find
\delta\in\mathcal{C}^{+}
in order to obtain just two kinds of
\delta
-pseudo-orbits, those that remain close to the origin and those that tend (in norm) to
\infty
as
n
tends to
+\infty
.
We will forward shadow those pseudo-orbits and then apply Lemma

.

Formally, let
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{C}^{+}

r_{0}=\varepsilon(0)

m=\min\{\varepsilon(x):x\in\overline{B(0,r_{0})}\}

Take
\delta\in\mathcal{C}^{+}

(1)

\delta(x)<\varepsilon(x)\;\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\text{ and }

(2)

\delta(x)<\begin{cases}\;\;\;m&\mbox{ if }x\in\overline{B(0,r_{0})}\\

In this paragraph we are going to show that for this
\delta
, there exist only two kinds of
\delta
-pseudo-orbits
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}

x_{n}\in\overline{B(0,r_{0})}

, and the ones such that
\|x_{n}\|\to+\infty
as
n\to+\infty
.
Let us show the existence of the first kind of
\delta

Since
0
is fixed by
f

\delta

x_{0}\in\overline{B(0,r_{0})}

.
Then
x_{0}\in B(f(x_{0}),\delta(f(x_{0})))
.
We can choose
x_{1}
to be
x_{0}
.
Repeating the previous argument for every

x_{n}\in B(0,r_{0})
for all

.
For
n<0

.
Thus

for all

.
Now we show the existence of the other kind of
\delta

Suppose for simplicity

As

\tfrac{3}{2}\|x_{0}\|<\|x_{1}\|
, and

.
Similarly for
x_{n}

\left(\tfrac{3}{2}\right)^{n}\|x_{0}\|<\|x_{n}\|

0

\varepsilon

By a

4.
Main theorem: existence of a fixed point

In this section we prove the existence of a fixed point for an orientation preserving planar homeomorphism with the topological shadowing property.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

be such that

, where

. Then

.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{C}^{+}

\varepsilon

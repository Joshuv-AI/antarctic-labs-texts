Introduction

Let
G
be a finite, simple, undirected, and connected graph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
. The
distance
between two vertices
x,y\in V(G)
, denoted by
d(x,y)
, is the minimum number of edges on a path connecting
x
and
y
in
G
. The
diameter
,
\textnormal{diam}(G)
, of
G
is
\max\{d(x,y):x,y\in V(G)\}
. Let
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
denote the set of positive integers. For
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and for two vertices
x,y\in V(G)
, let
d_{k}(x,y)=\min\{d(x,y),k+1\}
.

Metric dimension, introduced in
[
14
]
and
[
22
]
, is a graph parameter that has been studied extensively. For distinct
x,y\in V(G)
, let
R\{x,y\}=\{z\in V(G):d(x,z)\neq d(y,z)\}
. A vertex subset
S\subseteq V(G)
is a
resolving set
of
G
if
|S\cap R\{x,y\}|\geq 1
for any pair of distinct
x,y\in V(G)
, and the
metric dimension

\dim(G)
of
G
is the minimum cardinality over all resolving sets of
G
. For
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and for distinct
x,y\in V(G)
, let
R_{k}\{x,y\}=\{z\in V(G):d_{k}(x,z)\neq d_{k}(y,z)\}
. A vertex subset
S\subseteq V(G)
is a
k
-truncated resolving set
(also called a distance-
k
resolving set) of
G
if
|S\cap R_{k}\{x,y\}|\geq 1
for any pair of distinct
x,y\in V(G)
, and the
k
-truncated metric dimension
(also called the distance-
k
dimension)
\dim_{k}(G)
of
G
is the minimum cardinality over all
k
-truncated resolving sets of
G
. Notice that
\dim_{k}(G)=\dim(G)
if
k\geq\textnormal{diam}(G)-1
. The metric dimension of a metric space
(V,d_{k})
is studied in
[
3
]
. The
k
-truncated metric dimension corresponds to the
(1,k+1)
-metric dimension in
[
7
,
8
]
. We note that
\dim_{1}(G)
is also called the adjacency dimension of
G
in
[
16
]
. For detailed results on
\dim_{k}(G)
, we refer to
[
11
]
. It is known that determining the metric dimension and the
k
-truncated metric dimension of a general graph are NP-hard problems; see
[
8
,
10
,
12
,
19
]
.

The fractionalization of various graph parameters has been extensively studied (see
[
21
]
). For definition and a formulation of fractional metric dimension as the optimal solution to a linear programming problem by relaxing a condition of the integer programming problem for metric dimension, see
[
5
,
9
]
. The fractional metric dimension of graphs was officially studied in
[
1
]
. For a function
g
defined on
V(G)
and for
U\subseteq V(G)
, let
g(U)=\sum_{s\in U}g(s)
. A real-valued function
g:V(G)\rightarrow[0,1]
is a
resolving function
of
G
if
g(R\{x,y\})\geq 1
for any distinct vertices
x,y\in V(G)
. The
fractional metric dimension
,
\dim_{f}(G)
, of
G
is
\min\{g(V(G)):g\mbox{ is a resolving function of }G\}
. Notice that
\dim_{f}(G)
reduces to
\dim(G)
if the codomain of resolving functions is restricted to
\{0,1\}
.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, a real-valued function
h:V(G)\rightarrow[0,1]
is a
k
-truncated resolving function
(also called a distance-
k
resolving function) of
G
if
h(R_{k}\{x,y\})\geq 1
for any pair of distinct
x,y\in V(G)
. The
fractional
k
-truncated metric dimension
(also called the fractional distance-
k
dimension) of
G
, denoted by
\dim_{k,f}(G)
, is
\min\{h(V(G)):h\mbox{ is a $k$-truncated resolving function of }G\}
. Note that
\dim_{k,f}(G)=\dim_{k}(G)
if the codomain of
k
-truncated resolving functions is restricted to
\{0,1\}
,
\dim_{k,f}(G)=\dim_{f}(G)
if
k\geq\textnormal{diam}(G)-1
, and
\dim_{k,f}(G)=\dim(G)
if
k\geq\textnormal{diam}(G)-1
and the codomain of
k
-truncated resolving functions is restricted to
\{0,1\}
.

In this paper, we initiate the study of the fractional
k
-truncated metric dimension of graphs. Let
P_{n}
,
C_{n}
and
K_{n}
, respectively, denote the path, the cycle and the complete graph on
n
vertices. In Section
2
, for any connected graph
G
of order
n\geq 2
and for any

, we show that

and we characterize
G
satisfying
\dim_{k,f}(G)
equals
1
and
\frac{n}{2}
, respectively. Along the way, we state some useful observations. In Section
3
, we examine
\dim_{k,f}(G)
when
G
is the Petersen graph, a wheel graph, a fan graph, a complete multipartite graph, a cycle, a grid graph or a tree. In Section

, we examine the relation among
\dim(G)
,
\dim_{f}(G)
,
\dim_{k}(G)
and
\dim_{k,f}(G)

and
\dim_{k,f}(G)\neq\dim_{k,f}(H)
. Based on the construction in

such that

can be arbitrarily large. We conclude the paper with some open problems.

2
Some observations and bounds on
\dim_{k,f}(G)

In this section, for any connected graph
G
of order
n\geq 2
and for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we show that
1\leq\dim_{k,f}(G)\leq\frac{n}{2}
; we characterize
G
satisfying
\dim_{k,f}(G)
equals
1
and
\frac{n}{2}
, respectively.

We begin with some observations. For
v\in V(G)
, let
N(v)=\{w\in V(G):vw\in E(G)\}
and
N[v]=N(v)\cup\{v\}
. More generally, for
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and for
v\in V(G)
, let
N_{k}[v]=\{w\in V(G):d(v,w)\leq k\}
and
N_{k}(v)=\{w\in V(G):d(v,w)=k\}
. Two vertices
x,y\in V(G)
are called
twins
if
N(x)-\{y\}=N(y)-\{x\}
. Hernando et al.
[
15
]
observed that the twin relation is an equivalence relation and that an equivalence class under it, called a twin equivalence class, induces either a clique or an independence set.

Observation 2.1
.

Let
x,y\in V(G)
with
x\neq y
, and let
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

(a)

[
24
]
If
x
and
y
are twins, then
g(x)+g(y)\geq 1
for any resolving function
g
of
G
.

(b)

If
x
and
y
are twins, then
h(x)+h(y)\geq 1
for any
k
-truncated resolving function
h
of
G
.

(c)

For any
k\geq 1
,
R_{k}\{x,y\}=(N_{k}[x]\cup N_{k}[y])-\cup_{i=1}^{k}(N_{i}(x)\cap N_{i}(y))
.

Observation 2.2
.

Let
G
be a non-trivial connected graph, and let
k,k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Then

(a)

[
1
]

\dim_{f}(G)\leq\dim(G)
;

(b)

[
3
,
7
]
if
k>k^{\prime}
, then
\dim(G)\leq\dim_{k}(G)\leq\dim_{k^{\prime}}(G)\leq\dim_{1}(G)
;

(c)

\dim_{f}(G)\leq\dim_{k,f}(G)\leq\dim_{k}(G)
;

(d)

if
k>k^{\prime}
, then
\dim_{f}(G)\leq\dim_{k,f}(G)\leq\dim_{k^{\prime},f}(G)\leq\dim_{1,f}(G)\leq\dim_{1}(G)
.

Observation 2.3
.

Let
G
be a connected graph with
\textnormal{diam}(G)=d
, and let
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

(a)

[
7
]
If
k\geq d-1
, then
\dim_{k}(G)=\dim(G)
.

(b)

If
k\geq d-1
, then
\dim_{k,f}(G)=\dim_{f}(G)
.

Next, we recall some results involving the bounds of the
k
-truncated metric dimension and the fractional metric dimension of graphs.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n\geq 2
, and let
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Then

(a)

[
7
]

1\leq\dim_{k}(G)\leq n-1
, and
\dim_{k}(G)=1
if and only if
G\in\cup_{i=2}^{k+2}\{P_{i}\}
;

(b)

[
1
,
17
]

1\leq\dim_{f}(G)\leq\frac{n}{2}
, and
\dim_{f}(G)=\frac{n}{2}
if and only if there exists a bijection
\phi:V(G)\rightarrow V(G)
such that
\phi(v)\neq v
and
|R\{v,\phi(v)\}|=2
for all
v\in V(G)
;

(c)

[
18
]

\dim_{f}(G)=1
if and only if
G=P_{n}
.

For the characterization of connected graphs
G
of order
n
satisfying
\dim_{k}(G)=n-2
and
\dim_{k}(G)=n-1
respectively, see
[
11
,
23
]
. For an explicit characterization of graphs
G
satisfying
\dim_{f}(G)=\frac{|V(G)|}{2}
, we recall the following construction from
[
2
]
. Let
\mathcal{K}=\{K_{a}:a\geq 2\}
and
\overline{\mathcal{K}}=\{\overline{K}_{b}:b\geq 2\}
, where
\overline{G}
denotes the complement of a graph
G
. Let
H[\mathcal{K}\cup\overline{\mathcal{K}}]
be the family of graphs obtained from a connected graph
H
by replacing each vertex
u_{i}\in V(H)
by a graph
H_{i}\in\mathcal{K}\cup\overline{\mathcal{K}}
, and each vertex in
H_{i}
is adjacent to each vertex in
H_{j}
if and only if
u_{i}u_{j}\in E(H)
.

Theorem 2.5
.

[
2
]

Let
G
be a connected graph of order at least two. Then
\dim_{f}(G)=\frac{|V(G)|}{2}
if and only if
G\in H[\mathcal{K}\cup\overline{\mathcal{K}}]
for some connected graph
H
.

Next, we obtain the bounds on
\dim_{k,f}(G)
.

Proposition 2.6
.

For any connected graph
G
of order
n\geq 2
and for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
1\leq\dim_{k,f}(G)\leq\frac{n}{2}
.

Proof.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, and let
G
be a connected graph of order
n\geq 2
. By definition,
\dim_{k,f}(G)\geq 1
. If
g:V(G)\rightarrow[0,1]
is a function defined by
g(v)=\frac{1}{2}
for each
v\in V(G)
, then
R_{k}\{x,y\}\supseteq\{x,y\}
and
g(R_{k}\{x,y\})\geq g(x)+g(y)=1
for any distinct
x,y\in V(G)
; thus,
g
is a
k
-truncated resolving function of
G
with
g(V(G))=\frac{n}{2}
. So,
\dim_{k,f}(G)\leq\frac{n}{2}
. ∎

Next, we characterize connected graphs
G
satisfying
\dim_{k,f}(G)=1
for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
G
be a non-trivial connected graph, and let
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Then
\dim_{k,f}(G)=1
if and only if
G\in\cup_{i=2}^{k+2}\{P_{i}\}
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n\geq 2
, and let
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

(
\Leftarrow
) Let
G\in\cup_{i=2}^{k+2}\{P_{i}\}
. Then
1=\dim_{f}(G)\leq\dim_{k,f}(G)\leq\dim_{k}(G)=1
by Observation
2.2
(c) and Theorem
2.4
(a)(c). So,
\dim_{k,f}(G)=1
.

(
\Rightarrow
) Let
\dim_{k,f}(G)=1
. By Observation
2.2
(c) and Theorem
2.4
(c),
\dim_{k,f}(G)\geq\dim_{f}(G)\geq 1
and
\dim_{f}(G)=1
if and only if
G=P_{n}
. So, if
G\neq P_{n}
, then
\dim_{k,f}(G)>1
. Now, suppose
G=P_{n}
, and let
P_{n}
be a path given by
u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n}
. Let
g:V(P_{n})\rightarrow[0,1]
be any minimum
k
-truncated resolving function of
P_{n}
. If
n\leq k+2
, then
\dim_{k,f}(P_{n})=1
as shown above. So, suppose
n\geq k+3
; we show that
\dim_{k,f}(P_{n})>1
.

First, let
k+3\leq n\leq 2k+3
. Then
R_{k}\{u_{1},u_{2}\}=\cup_{i=1}^{k+2}\{u_{i}\}
,
R_{k}\{u_{n-1},u_{n}\}=\cup_{i=n-(k+1)}^{n}\{u_{i}\}
, and
R_{k}\{u_{i-1},u_{i+1}\}=V(P_{n})-\{u_{i}\}
for each
i\in\{n-(k+1),\ldots,k+2\}
. So,
g(R_{k}\{u_{1},u_{2}\})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}g(u_{i})\geq 1
,
g(R_{k}\{u_{n-1},u_{n}\})\geq\sum_{i=n-(k+1)}^{n}g(u_{i})\geq 1
, and
g(R_{k}\{u_{i-1},u_{i+1}\})=g(V(P_{n}))-g(u_{i})\geq 1
for each
i\in\{n-(k+1),\ldots,k+2\}
. By summing over the
(6+2k-n)
inequalities, we have
(5+2k-n)g(V(P_{n}))\geq 6+2k-n
, i.e.,
g(V(P_{n}))\geq\frac{6+2k-n}{5+2k-n}
; thus,
\dim_{k,f}(P_{n})\geq\frac{6+2k-n}{5+2k-n}>1
(also see Theorem
3.11
(b)).

Second, let
n\geq 2k+4
. Then
R_{k}\{u_{1},u_{2}\}=\cup_{i=1}^{k+2}\{u_{i}\}
and
R_{k}\{u_{n-1},u_{n}\}=\cup_{i=n-k-1}^{n}\{u_{i}\}
. So,
g(R_{k}\{u_{1},u_{2}\})=\sum_{i=1}^{k+2}g(u_{i})\geq 1
and
g(R_{k}\{u_{n-1},u_{n}\})=\sum_{i=n-k-1}^{n}g(u_{i})\geq 1
. Since
n-k-1\geq 2k+4-k-1=k+3
,
g(V(P_{n}))\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k+2}g(u_{i})+\sum_{i=n-k-1}^{n}g(u_{i})\geq 2
, which implies
\dim_{k,f}(P_{n})\geq 2
. ∎

Let

Let
G
be a connected graph of order

.

(
\Leftarrow
) Let

for some connected graph

. Then
\dim_{1,f}(G)\geq\dim_{f}(G)=\frac{n}{2}
by Observation

(d) and Theorem

. Since
\dim_{1,f}(G)\leq\frac{n}{2}

,
\dim_{1,f}(G)=\frac{n}{2}
.

(
\Rightarrow
) Let

. It suffices to show that each twin equivalence class of

has cardinality at least two. Assume, to the contrary, that there exists a twin equivalence class

consisting of exactly one element; let

We recall some terminology. The

The

of a major vertex

for every other major vertex

in
T

, of a major vertex

in
T
, and an

is a major vertex that has positive terminal degree. Let

be the set of exterior major vertices of

. Let
M_{1}(T)=\{w\in M(T):ter(w)=1\}

,

.

Next, we examine

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

(a)

For

,

\dim_{k,f}(P_{n}+K_{1})=\dim_{f}(P_{n}+K_{1})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{n+1}{2}&\mbox{ if }n\in\{1,2,3\},\\
\frac{5}{3}&\mbox{ if }n\in\{4,5\},\\
\frac{n+1}{4}&\mbox{ if }n\geq 6\mbox{ and }n\equiv 1,3\pmod{4},\\
\frac{n+2}{4}&\mbox{ if }n\geq 6\mbox{ and }n\equiv 2\pmod{4}.\end{array}\right.

(b)

If

and

, then
\frac{n}{4}\leq\dim_{k,f}(P_{n}+K_{1})=\dim_{f}(P_{n}+K_{1})\leq\frac{n+2}{4}

Proof.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
n\geq 1
.
Since
\textnormal{diam}(P_{n}+K_{1})\leq 2
,
\dim_{k,f}(P_{n}+K_{1})=\dim_{f}(P_{n}+K_{1})
by Observation
2.3
(b). Let
G=P_{n}+K_{1}
such that
P_{n}
is given by
u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n}
and
w
is the vertex in
K_{1}
. Let
g:V(G)\rightarrow[0,1]
be any minimum (
k
-truncated) resolving function of
G
.

First, let
n\in\{1,2\}
; then
P_{n}+K_{1}=K_{n+1}
and
\dim_{k,f}(P_{n}+K_{1})=\dim_{f}(P_{n}+K_{1})=\frac{n+1}{2}
by Theorem
3.1
(e). Second, let
n=3
. Since
u_{1}
and
u_{3}
are twins and
u_{2}
and

are twins in

g(u_{1})+g(u_{3})\geq 1

(a)(b); thus
g(V(G))\geq 2
, and hence
\dim_{k,f}(G)=\dim_{f}(G)\geq 2

2.4

\dim_{k,f}(G)=\dim_{f}(G)=2
.

Third, let
n=4

R_{k}\{u_{1},u_{2}\}=R\{u_{1},u_{2}\}=V(G)-\{w,u_{4}\}

R_{k}\{u_{1},u_{3}\}=R\{u_{1},u_{3}\}=V(G)-\{w,u_{2}\}
,
R_{k}\{u_{2},u_{4}\}=R\{u_{2},u_{4}\}=V(G)-\{w,u_{3}\}
,
R_{k}\{u_{3},u_{4}\}=R\{u_{3},u_{4}\}=V(G)-\{w,u_{1}\}
,
R_{k}\{u_{2},w\}=R\{u_{2},w\}=V(G)-\{u_{1},u_{3}\}

R_{k}\{u_{3},w\}=R\{u_{3},w\}=V(G)-\{u_{2},u_{4}\}
. So,
g(V(G))-g(w)-g(u_{4})\geq 1
,
g(V(G))-g(w)-g(u_{2})\geq 1
,
g(V(G))-g(w)-g(u_{3})\geq 1
,
g(V(G))-g(w)-g(u_{1})\geq 1

3g(V(G))-3g(u_{1})-3g(u_{3})\geq 3
and
3g(V(G))-3g(u_{2})-3g(u_{4})\geq 3

6g(V(G))\geq 10
; thus,
\dim_{k,f}(G)=\dim_{f}(G)\geq\frac{5}{3}

h:V(G)\rightarrow[0,1]
is a function defined by

for each

, then
h
is a (
k
-truncated) resolving function of
G
with

; thus
\dim_{k,f}(G)=\dim_{f}(G)\leq\frac{5}{3}
. Therefore,
\dim_{k,f}(G)=\dim_{f}(G)=\frac{5}{3}
.

Fourth, let
n=5
. Then
R_{k}\{u_{1},u_{2}\}=R\{u_{1},u_{2}\}=V(G)-\{w,u_{4},u_{5}\}
,
R_{k}\{u_{4},u_{5}\}=R\{u_{4},u_{5}\}=V(G)-\{w,u_{1},u_{2}\}
,
R_{k}\{u_{2},w\}=R\{u_{2},w\}=V(G)-\{u_{1},u_{3}\}
,
R_{k}\{u_{3},w\}=R\{u_{3},w\}=V(G)-\{u_{2},u_{4}\}
and
R_{k}\{u_{4},w\}=R\{u_{4},w\}=V(G)-\{u_{3},u_{5}\}
. So,
g(V(G))-g(w)-g(u_{4})-g(u_{5})\geq 1
,
g(V(G))-g(w)-g(u_{1})-g(u_{2})\geq 1
,
g(V(G))-g(u_{1})-g(u_{3})\geq 1
,
g(V(G))-g(u_{2})-g(u_{4})\geq 1
and
g(V(G))-g(u_{3})-g(u_{5})\geq 1
. By summing over the five inequalities, we have
3g(V(G))\geq 5
; thus,
\dim_{k,f}(G)=\dim_{f}(G)\geq\frac{5}{3}
. On the other hand, if
h:V(G)\rightarrow[0,1]
is a function defined by
h(w)=0
and
h(u_{i})=\frac{1}{3}
for each

, then
h
is a (
k
-truncated) resolving function of
G
with
h(V(G))=\frac{5}{3}
; thus
\dim_{k,f}(G)=\dim_{f}(G)\leq\frac{5}{3}
. Therefore,
\dim_{k,f}(G)=\dim_{f}(G)=\frac{5}{3}
.

Next, let
n\geq 6
, and we consider the following cases.

Case 1:
n\equiv 1,3\pmod{4}
.
In this case,
n
is odd; let

. Note that
R_{k}\{u_{1},u_{2}\}=R\{u_{1},u_{2}\}=\{u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\}

for each odd

g(u_{1})+g(u_{2})+g(u_{3})\geq 1

Theorem 3.4

For any

Proof.

Let

. For

, let

be given by
u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{n-1},u_{0}
. Let

be any minimum

-truncated resolving function of

.

First, let

; then
\textnormal{diam}(C_{n})\leq\lfloor\frac{2k+3}{2}\rfloor=k+1
. By Observation

(b),
\dim_{k,f}(C_{n})=\dim_{f}(C_{n})
. So, by Theorem

(c),
\dim_{k,f}(C_{n})=\frac{n}{n-1}
for an odd

\dim_{k,f}(C_{n})=\frac{n}{n-2}
for an even

.

Second, let
n\geq 2k+4
. Note that, for each
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
,
R_{k}\{u_{i},u_{i+2}\}=\cup_{j=0}^{k}\{u_{i-j},u_{i+2+j}\}
, where the subscript it taken modulo
n
; thus
\sum_{j=0}^{k}(g(u_{i-j})+g(u_{i+2+j}))\geq 1
. By summing over

such inequalities, we have
2(k+1)g(V(C_{n}))\geq n
since each vertex appears

times in the

inequalities. So,
g(V(C_{n}))\geq\frac{n}{2(k+1)}

\dim_{k,f}(C_{n})\geq\frac{n}{2(k+1)}
. On the other hand, if we let
h:V(C_{n})\rightarrow[0,1]
be a function defined by
h(u_{i})=\frac{1}{2(k+1)}
for each
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
, then
h
is a
k
-truncated resolving function of
C_{n}
with
h(V(C_{n}))=\frac{n}{2(k+1)}
. To see this, for any distinct
x,y\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
, note that
|R_{k}\{u_{x},u_{y}\}|\geq 2(k+1)
and
h(R_{k}\{u_{x},u_{y}\})\geq\frac{1}{2(k+1)}\cdot 2(k+1)=1
. So,
\dim_{k,f}(C_{n})\leq h(V(C_{n}))=\frac{n}{2(k+1)}
. Therefore,
\dim_{k,f}(C_{n})=\frac{n}{2(k+1)}
. ∎

\dim_{1,f}(G)\leq g(V(G))=2
. Since
\dim_{1,f}(G)\geq\dim_{f}(G)=2
by Observation
2.2
(d) and Theorem
3.1
(f),
\dim_{1,f}(G)=\dim_{f}(G)=2
.

(
\Rightarrow
) Let
\dim_{1,f}(G)=\dim_{f}(G)
. Let the grid graph
G
be drawn in the
xy
-plane with the four corners at
(1,1)
,
(s,1)
,
(1,t)
and
(s,t)
with horizontal and vertical edges of equal lengths; we denote by
u_{i,j}
the vertex
(i,j)
. Let
g:V(G)\rightarrow[0,1]
be any
1
-truncated resolving function of
G
.

First, suppose
G=P_{s}\times P_{t}
for
s,t\geq 4
. Then
R_{1}\{u_{1,1},u_{2,2}\}=\{u_{1,1},u_{2,2},u_{2,3},u_{3,2}\}
,
R_{1}\{u_{1,2},u_{2,1}\}=\{u_{1,2},u_{1,3},u_{2,1},u_{3,1}\}
and
R_{1}\{u_{s-1,t},u_{s,t-1}\}=\{u_{s-2,t},u_{s-1,t},u_{s,t-1},u_{s,t-2}\}
; thus
g(u_{1,1})+g(u_{2,2})+g(u_{2,3})+g(u_{3,2})\geq 1
,

since
s,t\geq 4

\dim_{1,f}(G)\geq 3>2=\dim_{f}(G)

\dim_{1,f}(G)=\dim_{f}(G)
implies
t\leq 3
.

Second, suppose
G=P_{s}\times P_{3}
for

\dim_{1,f}(G)=2=\dim_{f}(G)
as shown above. If

R_{1}\{u_{1,1},u_{2,2}\}=\{u_{1,1},u_{2,2},u_{2,3},u_{3,2}\}

,

,

and
R_{1}\{u_{1,1},u_{4,3}\}=\{u_{1,1},u_{1,2},u_{2,1},u_{3,3},u_{4,2},u_{4,3}\}
; thus,
g(u_{1,1})+g(u_{2,2})+g(u_{2,3})+g(u_{3,2})\geq 1
,
g(u_{1,2})+g(u_{1,3})+g(u_{2,1})+g(u_{3,1})\geq 1
,
g(u_{2,3})+g(u_{3,3})+g(u_{4,2})+g(u_{4,1})\geq 1
,

2g(V(G))\geq 5
, and hence

. If
s\geq 5
, then
R_{1}\{u_{1,1},u_{2,2}\}=\{u_{1,1},u_{2,2},u_{2,3},u_{3,2}\}
,
R_{1}\{u_{1,2},u_{2,1}\}=\{u_{1,2},u_{1,3},u_{2,1},u_{3,1}\}

; thus,
g(u_{1,1})+g(u_{2,2})+g(u_{2,3})+g(u_{3,2})\geq 1
,
g(u_{1,2})+g(u_{1,3})+g(u_{2,1})+g(u_{3,1})\geq 1
and
g(u_{s-2,3})+g(u_{s-1,3})+g(u_{s,2})+g(u_{s,1})\geq 1
. By summing over the three inequalities, we have
g(V(G))\geq 3
since

, and hence
\dim_{1,f}(G)\geq 3>2=\dim_{f}(G)
.

Third, suppose

for

,

,

3.4
Trees

We examine
\dim_{k,f}(T)
for non-trivial trees
T
. For
n\geq 2
,
\dim_{k,f}(P_{n})=\dim_{f}(P_{n})
if and only if
n\in\{2,3,\ldots,k+2\}
by Theorems
2.4
(c) and
2.7
. We characterize trees
T
satisfying
\dim_{1,f}(T)=\dim_{f}(T)
. For trees
T
with
ex(T)=1
and for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we also characterize
T
satisfying
\dim_{k,f}(T)=\dim_{f}(T)
.

We first consider
\dim_{k,f}(P_{n})
.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
n\geq 2
.

(a)

If
n\leq k+2
, then
\dim_{k,f}(P_{n})=1
.

(b)

If
k+3\leq n\leq 2k+3
, then
\dim_{k,f}(P_{n})=\frac{6+2k-n}{5+2k-n}
.

(c)

Let
n\geq 2k+4
.

(i)

If
n\equiv 1\pmod{(2k+2)}
, then
\dim_{k,f}(P_{n})=\frac{n+k}{2k+2}
.

(ii)

If
n\equiv 2,3,\ldots,k+2\pmod{(2k+2)}
, then
\dim_{k,f}(P_{n})=\lceil\frac{n}{2k+2}\rceil
.

(iii)

If
n\equiv 0\pmod{(2k+2)}
or
n\equiv k+3,k+4,\ldots,2k+1\pmod{(2k+2)}
, then
\lceil\frac{n}{2k+2}\rceil\leq\dim_{k,f}(P_{n})\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2k+2}\rceil+\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. For
n\geq 2
, let
P_{n}
be given by
u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n}
. Let
g:V(P_{n})\rightarrow[0,1]
be any
k
-truncated resolving function of
P_{n}
. For (a), suppose
n\leq k+2
. Then
\dim_{k,f}(P_{n})=1
by Theorem
2.7
.

For (b), suppose
k+3\leq n\leq 2k+3
. Then
R_{k}\{u_{1},u_{2}\}=\cup_{i=1}^{k+2}\{u_{i}\}
,
R_{k}\{u_{n-1},u_{n}\}=\cup_{i=n-(k+1)}^{n}\{u_{i}\}
, and
R_{k}\{u_{i-1},u_{i+1}\}=V(P_{n})-\{u_{i}\}
for
u_{i}\in R_{k}\{u_{1},u_{2}\}\cap R_{k}\{u_{n-1},u_{n}\}
(i.e.,
n-k-1\leq i\leq k+2
). By summing over the
(6+2k-n)
inequalities, we have
(5+2k-n)g(V(P_{n}))\geq 6+2k-n
, i.e.,
g(V(P_{n}))\geq\frac{6+2k-n}{5+2k-n}
; thus,
\dim_{k,f}(P_{n})\geq\frac{6+2k-n}{5+2k-n}
. On the other hand, let
h:V(P_{n})\rightarrow[0,1]
be a function defined by

h(u_{i})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{5+2k-n}&\mbox{ if }u_{i}\in\{u_{1},u_{n}\}\cup(R_{k}\{u_{1},u_{2}\}\cap R_{k}\{u_{n-1},u_{n}\}),\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}.\\
\end{array}\right.

Then
h
is a
k
-truncated resolving function of
P_{n}
with
h(V(P_{n}))=\frac{6+2k-n}{5+2k-n}
; thus,
\dim_{k,f}(P_{n})\leq\frac{6+2k-n}{5+2k-n}
. Therefore,
\dim_{k,f}(P_{n})=\frac{6+2k-n}{5+2k-n}
for
k+3\leq n\leq 2k+3
.

For (c), let
n\geq 2k+4
, and we consider three cases.

Case 1:
n\equiv 1\pmod{(2k+2)}
. Note that
R_{k}\{u_{1},u_{2}\}=\cup_{i=1}^{k+2}\{u_{i}\}
,
R_{k}\{u_{n-1},u_{n}\}=\cup_{i=n-(k+1)}^{n}\{u_{i}\}
, and
R_{k}\{u_{i-1},u_{i+1}\}=\cup_{j=1}^{k+1}\{u_{i-j},u_{i+j}\}
for
i\equiv 1\pmod{(k+1)}
and
i\not\in\{1,n\}
. By summing over the
\lceil\frac{n}{k+1}\rceil
inequalities, we have
2g(V(P_{n}))\geq\lceil\frac{n}{k+1}\rceil=\frac{n-1}{k+1}+1=\frac{n+k}{k+1}
, i.e.,
g(V(P_{n}))\geq\frac{n+k}{2(k+1)}
; thus,
\dim_{k,f}(P_{n})\geq\frac{n+k}{2k+2}
. On the other hand, if we let
h:V(P_{n})\rightarrow[0,1]
be a function defined by

h(u_{i})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{2}&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 1\pmod{(k+1)},\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise},\\
\end{array}\right.

then
h
is a
k
-truncated resolving function of
P_{n}
with
h(V(P_{n}))=\frac{1}{2}\lceil\frac{n}{k+1}\rceil=\frac{n+k}{2k+2}
; thus,
\dim_{k,f}(P_{n})\leq\frac{n+k}{2k+2}
. Therefore,
\dim_{k,f}(P_{n})=\frac{n+k}{2k+2}
for
n\equiv 1\pmod{(2k+2)}
.

Case 2:
n\equiv 2,3,\ldots,k+2\pmod{(2k+2)}
. Note that
R_{k}\{u_{1},u_{2}\}=\cup_{i=1}^{k+2}\{u_{i}\}
,
R_{k}\{u_{n-1},u_{n}\}=\cup_{i=n-(k+1)}^{n}\{u_{i}\}
, and
R_{k}\{u_{(2k+2)i+1},u_{(2k+2)i+2}\}=\cup_{j=1-k}^{k+2}\{u_{(2k+2)i+j}\}
for
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,\lceil\frac{n}{2k+2}\rceil-2\}
. By summing over the
\lceil\frac{n}{2k+2}\rceil
inequalities, we have
g(V(P_{n}))\geq\lceil\frac{n}{2k+2}\rceil
; thus,
\dim_{k,f}(P_{n})\geq\lceil\frac{n}{2k+2}\rceil
. On the other hand, let
h:V(P_{n})\rightarrow[0,1]
be a function defined by

h(u_{i})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{2}&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 1\pmod{(k+1)}\mbox{ and }1\leq i\leq\frac{n}{2},\mbox{ or }n+1-i\equiv 1\pmod{(k+1)}\mbox{ and }\frac{n}{2}<i\leq n,\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}.\\
\end{array}\right.

Then
h
is a
k
-truncated resolving function of
P_{n}
with
h(V(P_{n}))=\lceil\frac{n}{2k+2}\rceil
; thus,
\dim_{k,f}(P_{n})\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2k+2}\rceil
. Therefore,
\dim_{k,f}(P_{n})=\lceil\frac{n}{2k+2}\rceil
for
n\equiv 2,3,\ldots,k+2\pmod{(2k+2)}
.

Case 3:
n\equiv 0\pmod{(2k+2)}
or
n\equiv k+3,\ldots,2k+1\pmod{(2k+2)}
. Note that
R_{k}\{u_{(2k+2)i+k+1},u_{(2k+2)i+k+2}\}=\cup_{j=1}^{2k+2}\{u_{(2k+2)i+j}\}
for each
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,\lceil\frac{n}{2k+2}\rceil-2\}
and
R_{k}\{u_{n-1},u_{n}\}=\cup_{i=n-(k+1)}^{n}\{u_{i}\}
. By summing over the
\lceil\frac{n}{2k+2}\rceil
inequalities, we have
g(V(P_{n}))\geq\lceil\frac{n}{2k+2}\rceil
; thus,
\dim_{k,f}(P_{n})\geq\lceil\frac{n}{2k+2}\rceil
. If
h:V(P_{n})\rightarrow[0,1]
is a function defined by

h(u_{i})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{2}&\mbox{ if }i=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil,\mbox{ or}\\
&\ \ \ 1\leq i<\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil\mbox{ and }i\equiv 1\!\!\!\pmod{(k+1)},\mbox{ or }\\
&\ \ \ \lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil<i\leq n\mbox{ and }n+1-i\equiv 1\!\!\!\pmod{(k+1)},\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise},\\
\end{array}\right.

then
h
is a
k
-truncated resolving function of
P_{n}
with
h(V(P_{n}))=\lceil\frac{n}{2k+2}\rceil+\frac{1}{2}
; thus,
\dim_{k,f}(P_{n})\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2k+2}\rceil+\frac{1}{2}
. Therefore,
\lceil\frac{n}{2k+2}\rceil\leq\dim_{k,f}(P_{n})=\lceil\frac{n}{2k+2}\rceil+\frac{1}{2}
for
n\equiv 0\pmod{(2k+2)}
or
n\equiv k+3,\ldots,2k+1\pmod{(2k+2)}
. ∎

Theorem
3.11
implies the following.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
n\geq 2
.

(a)

If
n\in\{2,3\}
, then
\dim_{1,f}(P_{n})=1
; if
n\in\{4,5\}
, then
\dim_{1,f}(P_{n})=\frac{8-n}{7-n}
.

(b)

If
n\geq 6
, then

\dim_{1,f}(P_{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{n+1}{4}&\mbox{ if }n\equiv 1\pmod{4},\\
\lceil\frac{n}{4}\rceil&\mbox{ if }n\equiv 2,3\pmod{4}.\end{array}\right.

(c)

If
n\geq 8
and
n\equiv 0\pmod{4}
, then
\frac{n}{4}\leq\dim_{1,f}(P_{n})\leq\frac{n+2}{4}
.

Next, we characterize non-trivial trees
T
satisfying
\dim_{1,f}(T)=\dim_{f}(T)
. We recall some terminology and notation. An
exterior degree-two vertex
is a vertex of degree
2
that lies on a path from a terminal vertex to its major vertex, and an
interior degree-two vertex
is a vertex of degree
2
such that the shortest path to any terminal vertex includes a major vertex. For
v\in M(T)
, let
T_{v}
be the subtree of
T
induced by
v
and all vertices belonging to the paths joining
v
with its terminal vertices. We begin with the following useful lemmas.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
T
be a tree with
ex(T)\geq 1
, and let
g:V(T)\rightarrow[0,1]
be any minimum resolving function of
T
. Then, for each
v\in M_{2}(T)
with
ter(v)=\alpha\geq 2
,
g(V(T_{v})-\{v\})=\frac{\alpha}{2}
and
\sum_{v\in M_{2}(T)}(g(V(T_{v}))-g(v))=\dim_{f}(T)
.

Proof.

Let
T
be a tree with
ex(T)\geq 1
, and let
g:V(T)\rightarrow[0,1]
be any minimum resolving function of
T
. Let
M_{2}(T)=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{a}\}
, where
a\geq 1
. For each
i\in\{1,2\ldots,a\}
, let
ter(v_{i})=\sigma_{i}\geq 2
, let
\ell_{i,1},\ell_{i,2},\ldots,\ell_{i,\sigma_{i}}
be the terminal vertices of
v_{i}
, and let
N(v_{i})=\{s_{i,1},s_{i,2},\ldots,s_{i,\sigma_{i}}\}
such that
s_{i,j}
lies on the
v_{i}-\ell_{i,j}
path and we denote by
P^{i,j}
the
s_{i,j}-\ell_{i,j}
path, where
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,\sigma_{i}\}
.

For each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,a\}
and for any distinct
x,y\in\{1,2,\ldots,\sigma_{i}\}
, note that
R\{s_{i,x},s_{i,y}\}=V(P^{i,x})\cup V(P^{i,y})
, and thus
g(V(P^{i,x}))+g(V(P^{i,y}))\geq 1
; by summing over the
\sigma_{i}\choose 2
inequalities, we have
(\sigma_{i}-1)\sum_{j=1}^{\sigma_{i}}g(V(P^{i,j}))\geq{\sigma_{i}\choose 2}
, which implies
g(V(T_{v_{i}}))-g(v_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{\sigma_{i}}g(V(P^{i,j}))\geq\frac{\sigma_{i}}{2}
. So,
\sum_{i=1}^{a}(g(V(T_{v_{i}}))-g(v_{i}))\geq\sum_{i=1}^{a}\frac{\sigma_{i}}{2}=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma(T)-ex_{1}(T))=\dim_{f}(T)
. Since
g
is a minimum resolving function of
T
,
g(V(T))=\dim_{f}(T)
; thus,
g(V(T_{v_{i}}))-g(v_{i})=\frac{\sigma_{i}}{2}
for each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,a\}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{a}(g(V(T_{v_{i}}))-g(v_{i}))=\dim_{f}(T)
. ∎

Lemma
3.13
implies the following

Corollary 3.14
.

Let
T
be a tree with
ex(T)\geq 1
, and let
g:V(T)\rightarrow[0,1]
be any minimum resolving function of
T
. If
x
is a major vertex, an interior degree-two vertex, or a vertex belonging to
T_{v}
for
v\in M_{1}(T)
, then
g(x)=0
.

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
T
be a tree with
ex(T)\geq 1
satisfying
\dim_{1,f}(T)=\dim_{f}(T)
.

(a)

If
v\in M_{2}(T)
, then every terminal vertex of
v
is adjacent to
v
in
T
.

(b)

T
contains no major vertex of terminal degree one.

(c)

T
contains neither a major vertex of terminal degree zero nor an interior degree-two vertex.

Proof.

Let
T
be a tree with
ex(T)\geq 1
satisfying
\dim_{1,f}(T)=\dim_{f}(T)
. Let
g:V(T)\rightarrow[0,1]
be any minimum resolving function of
T
, and let
h:V(T)\rightarrow[0,1]
be any minimum
1
-truncated resolving function of
T
.

(a) Let
v\in M_{2}(T)
with
ter(v)=\alpha\geq 2
. Let
\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{\alpha}
be the terminal vertices of
v
in
T
. Suppose
d(v,\ell_{i})\geq 2
for some
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,\alpha\}
. By relabeling the vertices of
T
if necessary, let
d(v,\ell_{1})\geq 2
.

First, let
\alpha\geq 3
. Let the
v-\ell_{1}
path be given by
v=s_{0},s_{1},\ldots,s_{d}=\ell_{1}
, where
d\geq 2
, and we denote by
P
the
v-\ell_{1}
path in
T
. Since
R_{1}\{s_{d-1},s_{d}\}=\{s_{d-2},s_{d-1},s_{d}\}\subseteq V(P)
,
h(V(P))\geq 1
. Since
\dim_{1,f}(T)=\dim_{f}(T)
and
\alpha\geq 3
,
h(V(T_{v}))-h(V(P))\geq\frac{\alpha-1}{2}
using the argument in the proof for Lemma
3.13
. So,
h(V(T_{v}))\geq\frac{\alpha+1}{2}>\frac{\alpha}{2}=g(V(T_{v}))
. By Lemma
3.13
and Corollary
3.14
,
\dim_{1,f}(T)>\dim_{f}(T)
. So,
\dim_{1,f}(T)=\dim_{f}(T)
implies that
d(v,\ell_{i})=1
for each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,\alpha\}
.

Second, let
\alpha=2
. Let the
v-\ell_{1}
path and the
v-\ell_{2}
path, respectively, be given by
v=s_{0},s_{1},\ldots,s_{d}=\ell_{1}
and
v=s_{0},s^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,s^{\prime}_{t}=\ell_{2}
, where
d\geq 2
; let
P^{1}
and
P^{2}
denote the
s_{1}-\ell_{1}
path and the
s^{\prime}_{1}-\ell_{2}
path, respectively. Note that
R_{1}\{s_{d-1},s_{d}\}\subseteq\{v\}\cup V(P^{1})
,
R_{1}\{s_{1},s^{\prime}_{1}\}\subseteq V(P^{1})\cup V(P^{2})
,
R_{1}\{v,s_{2}\}\subseteq(N[v]\cup V(P^{1}))-\{s_{1}\}
and
R_{1}\{v,s^{\prime}_{1}\}\subseteq N[v]\cup V(P^{2})
. So,
h(v)+h(V(P^{1}))\geq 1
,
h(V(P^{1}))+h(V(P^{2}))\geq 1
,
h(N[v]\cup V(P^{1}))-h(s_{1})\geq 1
and
h(N[v]\cup V(P^{2}))\geq 1
. Since each vertex in
N[v]\cup V(T_{v})
appears at most
3
times in the
4
inequalities, by summing over the
4
inequalities, we have
3h(N[v]\cup V(T_{v}))\geq 4
. Since each vertex in
N[v]-\{s_{1},s^{\prime}_{1}\}
is either a major vertex or an interior degree-two vertex in
T
,
g(N[v]-\{s_{1},s^{\prime}_{1}\})=0
by Corollary
3.14
. So,
h(N[v]\cup V(T_{v}))\geq\frac{4}{3}>1=g(N[v]\cup V(T_{v}))=g(V(T_{v})-\{v\})
. Thus,
\dim_{1,f}(T)=\dim_{f}(T)
implies that
d(v,\ell_{1})=1=d(v,\ell_{2})
.

(b) Let
v\in M_{1}(T)
, and let
\ell
be the terminal vertex of
v
in
T
such that the
v-\ell
path is given by
v=s_{0},s_{1},\ldots,s_{d}=\ell
. If
d(v,\ell)\geq 2
, then
R_{1}\{s_{d-1},s_{d}\}\subseteq V(T_{v})
and
h(V(T_{v}))\geq 1>0=g(V(T_{v}))
by Corollary
3.14
. If
d(v,\ell)=1
, then
R_{1}\{v,\ell\}=N[v]
and
h(N[v])\geq 1>0=g(N[v])
since each vertex in
N[v]
is a major vertex, an interior degree-two vertex, or a vertex belonging to
T_{v}
for
v\in M_{1}(T)
. So,
\dim_{1,f}(T)=\dim_{f}(T)
implies that
M_{1}(T)=\emptyset
.

(c) Suppose
x
is either an interior degree-two vertex or a major vertex of terminal degree zero in
T
. Then there exist distinct
w,w^{\prime}\in M_{2}(T)
such that
x
lies on the
w-w^{\prime}
path and that
w
and
w^{\prime}
are the only exterior major vertices on the
w-w^{\prime}
path. By relabeling the vertices of
T
if necessary, we may assume that
xw\in E(T)
. If
\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{\alpha}
are the terminal vertices of
w
, where
\alpha\geq 2
, then
\ell_{i}w\in E(T)
for each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,\alpha\}
by (a) of the current lemma.

For each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,\alpha\}
,
R_{1}\{\ell_{i},x\}=\{\ell_{i}\}\cup(N[x]-\{w\})
and
h(\ell_{i})+h(N[x]-\{w\})\geq 1
. By summing over the
\alpha
inequalities, we have
(\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha}h(\ell_{i}))+\alpha h(N[x]-\{w\})\geq\alpha
. Since each vertex in
N[x]-\{w\}
is either a major vertex or an interior degree-two vertex in
T
,
g(N[x]-\{w\})=0
by Corollary
3.14
. So, we have
(\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha}h(\ell_{i}))+\alpha h(N[x]-\{w\})\geq\alpha>\frac{\alpha}{2}=(\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha}g(\ell_{i}))+\alpha g(N[x]-\{w\})=g(V(T_{w})-\{w\})
. So,
\dim_{1,f}(T)=\dim_{f}(T)
implies that
T
contains neither a major vertex of terminal degree zero nor an interior degree-two vertex. ∎

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
T
be a non-trivial tree. Then
\dim_{1,f}(T)=\dim_{f}(T)
if and only if
T\in\{P_{2},P_{3}\}
, or
ex(T)\geq 1
and
V(T)=M_{2}(T)\cup L(T)
.

Proof.

Let
T
be a tree of order
n\geq 2
.

(
\Leftarrow
) If
T\in\{P_{2},P_{3}\}
, then
\dim_{1,f}(T)=1=\dim_{f}(T)
by Theorems
2.4
(c) and
2.7
. Now, suppose
ex(T)\geq 1
and
V(T)=M_{2}(T)\cup L(T)
. If
g:V(T)\rightarrow[0,1]
is a function defined by
g(u)=\frac{1}{2}
for each

and
g(w)=0
for each
w\in M_{2}(T)
, then
g
is a
1
-truncated resolving function of
T
with
g(V(T))=\frac{1}{2}\sigma(T)
; thus
\dim_{1,f}(T)\leq\frac{1}{2}\sigma(T)
. Since
\dim_{1,f}(T)\geq\dim_{f}(T)=\frac{1}{2}\sigma(T)
by Observation
2.2
(d) and Theorem
3.1
(a),
\dim_{1,f}(T)=\frac{1}{2}\sigma(T)=\dim_{f}(T)
.

(
\Rightarrow
) Let
\dim_{1,f}(T)=\dim_{f}(T)
. If
ex(T)=0
, then
T=P_{n}
and
\dim_{f}(P_{n})=1
by Theorem
2.4
(c). So,
\dim_{1,f}(P_{n})=\dim_{f}(P_{n})
implies
\dim_{1,f}(P_{n})=1
, and thus

by Theorem
2.7
.

Now, suppose
ex(T)\geq 1
. By Lemma
3.15
(b)(c),
T
contains neither an interior degree-two vertex nor a major vertex with terminal degree at most one. Moreover, by Lemma
3.15
(a),
T
contains no exterior degree-two vertex. So, each vertex in
T
is either a major vertex with terminal degree at least two, or a leaf that is adjacent to some exterior major vertex in
T
. ∎

For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, it’s an interesting yet a challenging task to characterize all connected graphs
G
satisfying
\dim_{k}(G)=\dim(G)
or
\dim_{k,f}(G)=\dim_{f}(G)
even when

is restricted to trees. We recall the following result on trees

\dim_{1}(T)=\dim(T)
.

Proposition 3.17
.

[

For any non-trivial tree
T

\dim_{1}(T)=\dim(T)
if and only if
T\in\{P_{2},P_{3}\}
or

(

edges exactly once.

Now, for trees
T
with
ex(T)=1
, we characterize

satisfying
\dim_{k,f}(T)=\dim_{f}(T)
.

Proposition 3.18
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, and let
T
be a tree with
ex(T)=1
such that
\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{\alpha}
are the terminal vertices of the exterior major vertex
v
in
T
. Then
\dim_{k,f}(T)=\dim_{f}(T)
if and only if
d(v,\ell_{i})\leq k
for each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,\alpha\}
.

Proof.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, and let
T
be a tree with
ex(T)=1
. Let
v
be the exterior major vertex of
T
,
N(v)=\{s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{\alpha}\}
, and let
\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{\alpha}
be the terminal vertices of
v
in
T
such that
s_{i}
lies on the
v-\ell_{i}
path for each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,\alpha\}
, where
\alpha\geq 3
. Let
P^{i}
denote the
s_{i}-\ell_{i}
path, where
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,\alpha\}
.

(
\Leftarrow
) Suppose
d(v,\ell_{i})\leq k
for each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,\alpha\}
. Let
g:V(T)\rightarrow[0,1]
be a function defined by

g(u)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{2}&\mbox{ if }u\in\{s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{\alpha}\},\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

We show that
g
is a
k
-truncated resolving function for
T
. Let
x
and
y
be distinct vertices in
T
. First, suppose
x
and
y
lie on the
v-\ell_{i}
path for some
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,\alpha\}
; let
d(v,x)<d(v,y)
without loss of generality. Since
d(x,s_{j})\leq k
and
d_{k}(x,s_{j})\neq d_{k}(y,s_{j})
for each
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,\alpha\}-\{i\}
,
R_{k}\{x,y\}\supseteq N(v)-\{s_{i}\}
and
g(R_{k}\{x,y\})\geq g(N(v))-g(s_{i})=\frac{\alpha-1}{2}\geq 1
since
\alpha\geq 3
. Second, suppose
x
lies on the
s_{i}-\ell_{i}
path and
y
lies on the
s_{j}-\ell_{j}
path for distinct
i,j\in\{1,2,\ldots,\alpha\}
. If
d(v,x)=d(v,y)
, then
R_{k}\{x,y\}=V(P^{i})\cup V(P^{j})
and
g(R_{k}\{x,y\})\geq g(V(P^{i}))+g(V(P^{j}))\geq g(s_{i})+g(s_{j})=1
. If
d(v,x)\neq d(v,y)
, say
d(v,x)<d(v,y)
, then
d(x,s_{t})\leq k
and
d_{k}(x,s_{t})\neq d_{k}(y,s_{t})
for each
t\in\{1,2,\ldots,\alpha\}-\{j\}
; thus,
R_{k}\{x,y\}\supseteq N(v)-\{s_{j}\}
and
g(R_{k}\{x,y\})\geq g(N(v))-g(s_{j})\geq\frac{\alpha-1}{2}\geq 1
. So,
g
is a
k
-truncated resolving function of
T
with
g(V(T))=\frac{\alpha}{2}
, and thus
\dim_{k,f}(T)\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}=\dim_{f}(T)
by Theorem
3.1
(a). Since
\dim_{k,f}(T)\geq\dim_{f}(T)
by Observation
2.2
(c), we have
\dim_{k,f}(T)=\frac{\alpha}{2}=\dim_{f}(T)
.

(
\Rightarrow
) Let
\dim_{k,f}(T)=\dim_{f}(T)
. Assume, to the contrary, that
d(v,\ell_{i})\geq k+1
for some
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,\alpha\}
. By relabeling the vertices of
T
if necessary, let
d(v,\ell_{1})\geq k+1
and let
v-\ell_{1}
path be given by
v=u_{0},s_{1}=u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{k},u_{k+1},\ldots,u_{z}=\ell_{1}
. Let
h:V(T)\rightarrow[0,1]
be a minimum
k
-truncated resolving function of
T
. Since
R_{k}\{u_{z-1},u_{z}\}\subseteq\{v\}\cup V(P^{1})
,
h(v)+h(V(P^{1}))\geq h(R_{k}\{u_{z-1},u_{z}\})\geq 1
. Note that, for any distinct
i,j\in\{2,3,\ldots,\alpha\}
,
R_{k}\{s_{i},s_{j}\}\subseteq V(P^{i})\cup V(P^{j})
and

; thus,
\sum_{i=2}^{\alpha}h(V(P^{i}))\geq\frac{\alpha-1}{2}
. So,
\dim_{k,f}(T)=h(V(T))=h(v)+\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha}h(V(P^{i}))\geq 1+\frac{\alpha-1}{2}=\frac{\alpha+1}{2}>\frac{\alpha}{2}=\dim_{f}(T)
, which contradicts the assumption that
\dim_{k,f}(T)=\dim_{f}(T)
. So,
d(v,\ell_{i})\leq k
for each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,\alpha\}
. ∎

4
Comparison on
\dim(G)
,
\dim_{f}(G)
,
\dim_{k}(G)
and
\dim_{k,f}(G)

In this section, we examine the relation among
\dim_{f}(G)
,
\dim_{k,f}(G)
,
\dim(G)
and
\dim_{k}(G)
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
in conjunction with Observation
2.2
. We show that, for two connected graphs
H
and
G
with
H\subset G
,
\frac{\dim_{k,f}(H)}{\dim_{k,f}(G)}
can be arbitrarily large. We also show the existence of non-isomorphic graphs
G
and
H
with
\dim_{k}(G)=\dim_{k}(H)
and
\dim_{k,f}(G)\neq\dim_{k,f}(H)
. We conclude the paper with some open problems.

It is known that metric dimension is not a monotone parameter on subgraph inclusion (see
[

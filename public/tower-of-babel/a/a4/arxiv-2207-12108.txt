Abstract




Abstract

In this paper, we study the averaging principle for distribution dependent stochastic differential equations
with drift in localized
L^{p}
spaces. Using Zvonkin’s transformation and estimates for solutions to Kolmogorov equations,
we prove that the solutions of the original system strongly and weakly converge to the solution of the
averaged system as the time scale
\varepsilon
goes to zero. Moreover, we obtain rates of the strong and
weak convergence that depend on
p
respectively.

1.
Introduction

Consider the following distribution dependent stochastic differential equation (in short, DDSDE):

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=b(t,X_{t},\mu_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\Sigma(t,X_{t},\mu_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t},

(1.1)

where
b:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
\Sigma:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
are measurable functions
and
\mu_{t}:={\mathcal{L}}(X_{t})
is the time marginal law of
X_{t}
.
Here
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
denotes the space of all probability measures over
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

and
(W_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is a
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motion on some stochastic basis

(\Omega,{\mathscr{F}},{\mathbf{P}};({\mathscr{F}}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0})
. The Kolmogorov operator of (
1.1
) is given by

{\mathscr{L}}_{t}\varphi(x)=A_{ij}(t,x,\mu_{t})\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\varphi(x)+b(t,x,\mu_{t})\cdot\nabla\varphi(x),

where
A_{ij}(t,x,\mu_{t}):=\frac{1}{2}\Sigma_{ik}\Sigma_{jk}(t,x,\mu_{t})
.
Here and below we use the usual Einstein
convention for summation.

Note that DDSDE (
1.1
) is also called mean-field SDE or McKean-Vlasov SDE in the literature if

b(t,x,\mu):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\hat{b}(t,x,y)\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y),\quad\Sigma(t,x,\mu):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\hat{\Sigma}(t,x,y)\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)

(1.2)

for all
(t,x,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where

\hat{b}:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
\hat{\Sigma}:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
are measurable functions.
It naturally appears in the studies on the limiting behavior of interacting particle systems and mean-field games. Roughly speaking,
DDSDE (or McKean-Vlasov SDE) describe stochastic systems whose evolution is determined not only by the microcosmic location of the particle, but also by the macrocosmic distribution. So it has attracted wide attention (see
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
22
,
33
,
43
]
and references therein).

When
b
and
\Sigma
satisfy some continuity assumptions, there are numerous results devoted
to studying the existence and uniqueness of this type of DDSDE (
1.1
)
(see
[
20
,
45
]
for examples). For the case that
b
is only measurable,
of at most linear growth and Lipschitz continuous with respect to
\mu
,
when
\Sigma
does not depend on
\mu
, is uniformly non-degenerate and Lipschitz continuous,
by using the classical Krylov estimates, Mishura and Veretenikov
[
34
]
obtained
the strong well-posedness of (
1.1
). After that, the strong well-posedness was extended
to local
L^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}
drift by Röckner and Zhang in
[
39
]
.
Moreover, by the relative entropy method and Girsanov’s theorem, Lacker
[
30
]
also
obtained some well-posedness results for linear growth cases (see also
[
29
]
).
Then, in the special case (
1.2
), Han obtained the well-posedness for
L^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}
drift based on
the relative entropy method in
[
21
]
.
In
[
49
]
, by some heat kernel estimates and Schauder-Tychonoff fixed point theorem,
Zhao established the well-posedness results for DDSDE in a more general case (
1.1
)
when
b
satisfies some
L^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}
condition and the derivatives of
\Sigma
with respect to

\mu
are Hölder continuous. It should be noted that by Zvonkin’s transformation of

[
52
]
(see also
[
47
]
and
[
48
]
) and entropy method,
the last two authors together with Zhang in
[
23
]
obtained the strong well-posedness
for (
1.1
), where
\Sigma
does not dependent on
\mu
and
b
is in mixed
L^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}
space. For existence and uniqueness results on weak solutions to Nemytskii-type (=density dependent) DDSDE with merely measurable coefficients we refer to
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
.

In this work, we are interested in investigating the averaging principle of the following
DDSDE with highly oscillating time component

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}^{\varepsilon}=b\left(\frac{t}{\varepsilon},X_{t}^{\varepsilon},\mu_{t}^{\varepsilon}\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma(X_{t}^{\varepsilon}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t},\ \ X_{0}^{\varepsilon}=\xi\in{\mathscr{F}}_{0},

(1.3)

where
\sigma:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a measurable function,
\mu^{\varepsilon}_{t}:={\mathcal{L}}(X^{\varepsilon}_{t})
is
the time marginal law of
X^{\varepsilon}_{t}
and the time scale
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
.

Usually, solving the original system (
1.3
) is
relatively difficult because of the highly oscillating time component. Therefore, it is desirable to find a simplified system which simulates and predicts the evolution of the original system over a long time scale.
As is well known, the highly oscillating time component can be “averaged” out to produce such a simplified system under some suitable conditions, which is called
averaging principle.

More exactly, let

\displaystyle\bar{b}(x,\mu):=\lim\limits_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}b(t,x,\mu){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t.

(1.4)

If
b
is independent of
\mu
,
b
is called
a
KBM-vector feld
(KBM stands for Krylov, Bogolyubov and Mitropolsky)
if the convergence (
1.4
) is uniformly with respect to
x
on
bounded subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see e.g.
[
40
]
).
The averaging principle states, as the time scale
\varepsilon
goes to zero, that the solution of the original equation (
1.3
) converges
to that of the following averaged equation on any finite time interval

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=\bar{b}(X_{t},\mu_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma(X_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t},\ \ X_{0}=\xi,

(1.5)

where
\mu_{t}
stands for the distribution of
X_{t}
.

The averaging principle was firstly established for deterministic systems by Krylov, Bogolyubov and
Mitropolsky
[
6
,
27
]
. Then it was extended to stochastic differential equations by
Khasminskii
[
25
]
. After that,
extensive work on the averaging principle for finite and infinite dimensional stochastic
differential equations was done; see e.g.

[
1
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
14
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
26
,
32
,
35
,
44
,
46
]
and the references therein.

Recall that the strong convergence rate of the averaging principle for slow-fast McKean-Vlasov SDE
was established by the techniques of time discretization and Poisson equation in
[
37
]
.
Furthermore, as discussed in
[
24
]
, the strong convergence rate of the averaging principle for slow-fast McKean-Vlasov SPDE was studied, based on the variational approach and the technique of time discretization.
Note that the coefficients of the slow equation with fast variables were globally Lipschitz continuous
with respect to the slow variable in the above results.
Recently, the strong convergence without a rate for DDSDE with highly oscillating time component
driven by fractional Brownian motion and standard Brownian motion was shown in
[
42
]
,
which requires that the drift term is continuous in the slow variable.

Obviously, Lipschitz or continuity assumptions on
b
are too strong for some applications.
There are a lot of interesting models from physics, only having bounded measurable or even singular
L^{p}
interaction kernels
b
and
\hat{b}
. For example, the rank-based interaction studied in
[
29
]
has an indicator kernel, which is discontinuous; the Biot-Savart law appearing in the vortex description of 2-dimensional incompressible Navier-Stokes equations has a singular kernel like
x^{\perp}/|x|^{2}
(see e.g.
[
51
]
). However, to the best of our knowledge, there is no result concerning the averaging principle both of DDSDE and SDE with
L^{p}
drift.

Following the above motivations, we consider the strong and weak convergence of the averaging principle
for DDSDE with
L^{p}
drift in the present paper. Moreover, we obtain the rate of the strong and weak convergence,
which is important for functional limit theorems in probability and homogenization in PDEs.
Throughout this paper we need the following conditions.

(H
{}_{b}^{1}
)

Let
p_{0}\in(d\vee 2,\infty)
and assume that there is a nonnegative constant
\kappa_{0}
such that for all
t\geqslant 0

and
\mu,\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b(t,\cdot,\mu)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p_{0}}+\frac{|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b(t,\cdot,\mu)-b(t,\cdot,\nu)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p_{0}}}{\|\mu-\nu\|_{var}}\leqslant\kappa_{0},

where

\|\mu-\nu\|_{var}:=\sup\limits_{A\in{\mathscr{B}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|
is total variation.

(H
{}_{b}^{2}
)

There are functions

\bar{b}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\omega:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and
H:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
such that for all
(T,t,x,\mu)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{T}\int_{t}^{T+t}(b(s,x,\mu)-\bar{b}(x,\mu)){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right|\leqslant\omega(T)H(x,\mu),

(1.6)

where
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\omega(t)=0
and
\sup\limits_{\mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|H(\cdot,\mu)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p_{0}}<\kappa_{0}
.
Here
p_{0}
and
\kappa_{0}
are as in
(H
{}_{b}^{1}
)
.

(H
σ
)

There are constants
p>d\vee 2
,
\kappa_{1}>1
and
\beta\in(0,1)
such that for all
x,y,\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}^{-1}|\xi|\leqslant|\sigma(x)\xi|\leqslant\kappa_{1}|\xi|,\quad|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\nabla\sigma|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}\leqslant\kappa_{1},

and

\|\sigma(x)-\sigma(y)\|_{HS}\leqslant\kappa_{1}|x-y|^{\beta},

where
\|\cdot\|_{HS}
is the Hilbert-Schmidt norm.

Please see the definition of the localized
L^{p}
norm
|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\cdot|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}
in Section
2.1
.

.

We Note that

Remark 1.4
.

(i)

Since we use the Zvonkin transformation using the parabolic equation,
when
\bar{b}=\bar{b}(t)=\bar{b}(\cdot,\mu_{t})
depends on the time variable

When

using the elliptic equation. Hence, the convergence rates in Theorems

and

(ii)

Noting that

\inf_{h>0}\left(\left(\omega\left(\frac{h}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)^{2}+h^{1-\delta}\right)^{\ell}

Remark 1.5
.

These averaging principle results are also applicable to the following system

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=\varepsilon b\left(t,X_{t},{\mathcal{L}}(X_{t})\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\sigma(X_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t}.

(1.9)

Define
{Z^{\varepsilon}_{t}}:=X_{t/\varepsilon}
and
W^{\varepsilon}_{t}:=\sqrt{\varepsilon}W_{t/\varepsilon}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. We rewrite (
1.9
) as

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}{Z^{\varepsilon}_{t}}=b\left(t/\varepsilon,{Z^{\varepsilon}_{t}},{\mathcal{L}}({Z^{\varepsilon}_{t}})\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma({Z^{\varepsilon}_{t}}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{\varepsilon}_{t}.

Then we can consider the following system

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}=b\left(t/\varepsilon,\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t},{\mathcal{L}}(\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t})\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma(\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t}.

Note that since the drift of both the DDSDE and SDE in this paper is locally
L^{p_{0}}_{x}
integrable,
we cannot use Gronwall’s lemma or the generalized Gronwall lemma directly to
prove the convergence of
X^{\varepsilon}
to
X
as in
[
24
,
42
]
. On the other hand, our system (
1.3
) can be rewritten in the following slow-fast system:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}^{\varepsilon}=b\left(Y_{t}^{\varepsilon},X_{t}^{\varepsilon},\mu_{t}^{\varepsilon}\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma(X_{t}^{\varepsilon}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t},\\
&{\mathord{{\rm d}}}Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t.\end{aligned}\right.

Since the Kolmogorov operator of the fast process
Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}=\frac{t}{\varepsilon},~t\geqslant 0
, does not have a second order elliptic part,
we cannot use the technique based on the Poisson equation as in
[
37
]
.
To overcome these difficulties, we use Zvonkin’s transformation to remove the drift
b

and employ the classical technique of time discretization.

More precisely, consider the following backward PDE for
t\in[0,T]
related to (
1.5
)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+a_{ij}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u-\lambda u+B\cdot\nabla u+B=0,\quad u(T)=0,

where
B(t,x):=\bar{b}(x,\mu_{t})
,
\lambda\geqslant 0
, is the dissipative term.
Under
(H
σ
)
and
(H
{}^{1}_{b}
)
, Xia et al
[
47
]

proved that for a sufficiently large number
\lambda
, there is a solution
u
such that

\displaystyle|\nabla u(t,x)|\leqslant\frac{1}{2},\quad t\in[0,T],x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Hence, if we define
\Phi_{t}(x):=x+u(t,x)
, then
x\to\Phi_{t}(x)
is a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
By Itô’s formula,
Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}:=\Phi_{t}(X^{\varepsilon}_{t})
and
Y_{t}:=\Phi_{t}(X_{t})
solve the following new SDEs:

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}=

\displaystyle\lambda u(t,\Phi^{-1}_{t}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+(\sigma^{*}\nabla\Phi_{t}(\Phi^{-1}_{t}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}))){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t}

\displaystyle+{\big(}b(t/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{t},\mu^{\varepsilon}_{t})-\bar{b}(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},\mu_{t}){\big)}\cdot\nabla\Phi_{t}{\big(}X^{\varepsilon}_{t}{\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t

and

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}Y_{t}=\lambda u(t,\Phi^{-1}_{t}(Y_{t})){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+(\sigma^{*}\nabla\Phi_{t})(\Phi^{-1}_{t}(Y_{t})){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t},

where
\sigma^{*}
is the transpose of
\sigma
and
\Phi^{-1}_{t}
is the inverse of
x\to\Phi_{t}(x)
.
Since these new systems have differentiable diffusion coefficients and the drifts are Lipshitz continuous,
we can use the stochastic Gronwall inequality. Please see the complete formulation in Section
5
.

The remaining part of the proof is about how to use the technique of time discretization to estimate the following crucial term

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}{\big(}b(\frac{s}{\varepsilon},X^{\varepsilon}_{s},\mu^{\varepsilon}_{s})-\bar{b}(X^{\varepsilon}_{s},\mu_{s}){\big)}\cdot\nabla\Phi_{s}{\big(}X^{\varepsilon}_{s}{\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\Big|^{2}\Big].

(1.10)

In particular, we need to estimate

\displaystyle\|\mu_{t}-\mu^{\varepsilon}_{t}\|_{var}

(1.11)

and the following difference for
B(t)=b(s/\varepsilon)\cdot\nabla\Phi_{t}
and
B(t)=\bar{b}\cdot\nabla\Phi_{t}
:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}{\big(}B(s,X^{\varepsilon}_{s},\mu^{\varepsilon}_{s})-B(s,X^{\varepsilon}_{\pi_{h}(s)},\mu^{\varepsilon}_{\pi_{h}(s)}){\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\Big|^{2},

(1.12)

where
\pi_{h}(s)
is defined by
\pi_{h}(s)=s
for
s\in[0,h)
and

\displaystyle\pi_{h}(s):=kh,\quad s\in[kh,(k+1)h),~~\forall k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Since
b
is not continuous, it is not an easy problem to give an estimate for (
1.12
).
Thanks to the important observation in
[
16
, Lemma 2.1]
,
we deal with this problem by considering the time regularity for the semi-group
generated by the solutions to (
1.3
). Basically, if
X^{\varepsilon}_{t}=W_{t}
is a standard Brownian motion and
f
is a discontinuous function, we have

\displaystyle\quad{\mathbb{E}}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}{\big(}f(W_{s})-f(W_{\pi_{h}(s)}){\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle=2{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}{\big(}f(W_{s})-f(W_{\pi_{h}(s)}){\big)}{\big(}f(W_{r})-f(W_{\pi_{h}(r)}){\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle=2{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t}{\big(}f(W_{s})-f(W_{\pi_{h}(s)}){\big)}\Big({\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathscr{F}}_{s}}\int_{s}^{t}{\big(}f(W_{r})-f(W_{\pi_{h}(r)}){\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\Big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s,

where
{\mathscr{F}}_{s}:=\sigma(W_{r},r\leqslant s)
and
{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathscr{F}}_{s}}
is the conditional expectation with respect to
{\mathscr{F}}_{s}
.
We note that by the Markov property,

\displaystyle\quad{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathscr{F}}_{s}}\int_{s}^{t}{\big(}f(W_{r})-f(W_{\pi_{h}(r)}){\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathscr{F}}_{s}}\int_{s}^{s+h}{\big(}f(W_{r})-f(W_{\pi_{h}(r)}){\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r+\int_{s+h}^{t}{\big(}P_{r-s}f(W_{s})-P_{\pi_{h}(r)-s}f(W_{s}){\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r,

where
P_{t}f(x):={\mathbb{E}}f(x+W_{t})
is the semi-group of Brownian motion.
Note that as
h\to 0
the first term converges to zero.
And the second term also converges to zero as
h\to 0
since
s\to P_{s}f
is continuous.
Please see Section
3
below for more details.
We mention that similar estimates are obtained in
[
31
]
by the stochastic sewing techniques.

To estimate (
1.11
), we employ a method based on the Kolmogorov equation which is also used in
[
36
]
.
Then, again by time discretization, we estimate the difference (
1.11
) and obtain (
1.7
) (see Section
4
).

Now we illustrate our results by some examples. Firstly, the following example shows that the function
\omega(t)

can be of the form
t^{-\alpha}
with any power
\alpha\in(0,1]
,
which can also be used in some systems with singular interactions.

Example 1.6
.

Consider the following DDSDE in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

2.1.

Let

Clearly, this space does not depend on

When

\widetilde{L}^{p}:=\widetilde{H}^{0,p}\quad{\text{and}}\quad|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}:=|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{0,p}.

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p}_{q}(T):=\widetilde{{\mathbb{H}}}^{0,p}_{q}(T).

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\mathbb{H}}}^{\alpha,p}(T):=L^{\infty}([0,T];\widetilde{H}^{\alpha,p}),\quad\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p}(T):=\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p}_{\infty}(T),\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}:=L^{\infty}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

\displaystyle(\lambda+\lambda_{0})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|u|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{H}}}^{\alpha,p}_{q^{\prime}}}+|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\partial_{t}u|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p}_{q}(T)}+|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\nabla^{2}u|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}_{q}^{p}(T)}\leqslant C\left(|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}_{q}^{p}(T)}+\lambda_{0}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|u|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}_{q}^{p}(T)}+|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\varphi|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{2,p}\right),

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|u(t)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}\leqslant C\left(|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}_{q}^{p}(T)}+|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\varphi|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{2,p}\right),

and complete the proof by Lemma

∎

2.3.
Elliptic equation

Now we consider the following second order elliptic PDE in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
:

\displaystyle a_{ij}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u-\lambda u+b\cdot\nabla u=f,

(2.8)

where
\lambda\geqslant 0
,

Firstly, we introduce the definition of a solution to PDE (

Definition 2.8
.

Let

,

and

. We call

\displaystyle a_{ij}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u(x)-\lambda u(x)+b(x)\cdot\nabla u(x)=f(x).

As a corollary of Lemma

, we have the following results.

Lemma 2.9
.

Assume

for some

. Then
there are constants
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(d,|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p},p,c_{0},\theta)

and

such that for any
\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{0}

and

, there exists a unique solution

u
to PDE (
2.8
) in the sense of Definition
2.8
such that

\displaystyle\lambda^{\frac{\beta}{2}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|u|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{H}^{\alpha,p^{\prime}}}+|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\nabla^{2}u|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}\leqslant C|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p},

(2.9)

where
\alpha\in[0,2),p\in[p,\infty],p^{\prime}\in[p,\infty]
with

\beta:=2-\alpha+\frac{d}{p^{\prime}}-\frac{d}{p}>0

Proof.

As usual, it suffices to show the a priori estimate (
2.9
).
Let
T>0
,
u
be a solution to (
2.8
)
and
\phi
be a nonnegative and nonzero smooth function on

Define

is a solution to the following parabolic

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{u}=a_{ij}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\tilde{u}-\lambda\tilde{u}+b\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}-\phi f+\phi^{\prime}u,\quad\tilde{u}(0)=0.

By (

with

\displaystyle\lambda^{\frac{\beta}{2}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\tilde{u}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{H}}}^{\alpha,p^{\prime}}(T)}+|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\nabla^{2}\tilde{u}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p}(T)}\leqslant C|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\phi^{\prime}u-\phi f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p}(T)},

\displaystyle\lambda^{\frac{\beta}{2}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|u|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{H}^{\alpha,p^{\prime}}}+|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\nabla^{2}u|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}\leqslant C\|\phi\|^{-1}_{\infty}\left(\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|u|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}+\|\phi\|_{\infty}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}\right),

where

. Noting that

(3.1)

Lemma 3.1

&+\int_{s}^{t}\nabla u(r,X_{s,r}(x)){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{r}.\end{split}

(3.4)

In this subsection, we consider the following case where

Z_{t}^{x}=x+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(Z_{s}^{x}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}.

Assume

σ

Proof.

Without loss of generality we assume that

in (

Combining Lemma
3.3
and Young’s convolution inequality, one sees that

\displaystyle|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\nabla^{k}P^{\sigma}_{t}f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{q}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(y)\nabla_{x}^{k}p_{t}^{\sigma}(\cdot,y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{q}

\delta\in(0,1)
, by a change of variables,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}{\big(}\eta^{*}_{T}{\big)}^{1-\delta}

\displaystyle=(1-\delta)\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-\delta}{\mathbb{P}}(\eta^{*}_{T}>\lambda){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-\delta}{\big(}1\wedge(\Xi_{h}\lambda^{-1}){\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim\Xi_{h}^{1-\delta}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-\delta}(1\wedge\lambda^{-1}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\lambda\lesssim(h\ln h^{-1})^{(1-\delta)}\|f\|_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p}(T)}^{2(1-\delta)}.

(3.23)

Combining (
3.22
) and (
3.23
), in view of Hölder’s inequality, for any
\delta>0
small enough, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\eta^{*}_{T}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[{\big(}\eta^{*}_{T}{\big)}^{1-\delta-\sqrt{\delta}}{\big(}\eta^{*}_{T}{\big)}^{\delta+\sqrt{\delta}}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\left[{\mathbb{E}}{\big(}\eta^{*}_{T}{\big)}^{1-\delta}\right]^{\frac{1-\delta-\sqrt{\delta}}{1-\delta}}\Big[{\mathbb{E}}{\big(}\eta^{*}_{T}{\big)}^{(\sqrt{\delta}+1)(1-\delta)}\Big]^{\frac{\sqrt{\delta}}{1-\delta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{1-\delta-\sqrt{\delta}}(\ln h^{-1})^{1-\delta-\sqrt{\delta}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p}(T)}^{2}

and complete the proof.

∎

Now, let us extend estimate (

\displaystyle X^{x}_{s,t}=x+\int_{s}^{t}B(r,X^{x}_{s,r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r+\int_{s}^{t}\sigma(X^{x}_{s,r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{r}.

(3.24)

Let

The following estimates follow from Girsanov’s transform and estimates for

Lemma 3.10
.

Assume (
3.1
) with

and

(ii)For any

(3.27)

(iv)For any
T>0
,
\delta>0

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{Z}^{x}
be a solution on a probability space
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{{\mathscr{F}}},(\widetilde{{\mathscr{F}}}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0},\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}})

\displaystyle\widetilde{Z}^{x}_{t}=x+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(\widetilde{Z}^{x}_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\widetilde{W}_{r},

where
\widetilde{W}_{t}

d

Since
p_{0}>2\vee d

B^{2}\in\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p_{0}/2}(T)

3.15
), one sees that for any
\gamma>0

\displaystyle\sup_{x}\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}}\exp\left(\gamma\int_{0}^{T}|\sigma^{-1}B(t,\widetilde{Z}^{x}_{t})|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)<\infty.

Hence, by Novikov’s criterion,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}_{T}:=\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{T}\sigma^{-1}B(t,\widetilde{Z}^{x}_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\widetilde{W}_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}|\sigma^{-1}B(t,\widetilde{Z}^{x}_{t})|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)

(3.29)

Define

\displaystyle\text{$\bar{W}_{t}:=\widetilde{W}_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{-1}B(s,\widetilde{Z}^{x}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s$ is a ${\mathbb{Q}}$-martingale}.

In other words,

\displaystyle\widetilde{Z}^{x}_{t}=x+\int_{0}^{t}B(s,\widetilde{Z}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(\widetilde{Z}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{W}_{s},\ \ {\mathbb{Q}}-a.e.

Therefore, by the uniqueness of (

\displaystyle{\mathbb{Q}}\circ(\widetilde{Z}^{x}_{\cdot})^{-1}={\mathbb{P}}\circ(X^{x}_{\cdot})^{-1}.

(3.30)

Now, we show (i)-(iv) one by one.

(i): In view of (
3.30
), one sees that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}f(X_{t}^{x})=\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}f(\widetilde{Z}^{x}_{t})=\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}}{\Big[}{\mathbb{Z}}_{T}f(\widetilde{Z}_{t}){\Big]}.

By Hölder’s inequality, (
3.29
) and (
3.13

r\in(1,p)
and
1/r^{\prime}+1/r=1

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}f(X^{x}_{t})\leqslant\left(\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}}|{\mathbb{Z}}_{T}|^{r^{\prime}}\right)^{1/r^{\prime}}\left(\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}}|f(\widetilde{Z}_{t})|^{r}\right)^{1/r}\lesssim\left(t^{-dr/(2p)}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}||f|^{r}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p/r}\right)^{1/r}\lesssim t^{-d/(2p)}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p},

which is (
3.25

(ii): Similarly to (i), by Hölder’s inequality and (

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\int_{s}^{t}f(u,X^{x}_{u}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}u

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}}|{\mathbb{Z}}_{T}|^{r^{\prime}}\right)^{1/r^{\prime}}\left(\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}}\left[\int_{s}^{t}f(u,\widetilde{Z}^{x}_{u}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}u\right]^{r}\right)^{1/r}

\displaystyle\lesssim(t-s)^{1-1/q-d/(2p)}\left(|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}||f|^{r}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p/r}_{q/r}}\right)^{1/r}\lesssim(t-s)^{1-1/q-d/(2p)}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p}_{q}}.

The term
{\mathbb{E}}\int_{s}^{t}f(u,X^{x}_{\pi_{h}(u)}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}u
can be estimated the same way.

(iii):
For (
3.27
), it again follows from Hölder’s inequality that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{T}f(t,X^{x}_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}}\left[{\mathbb{Z}}_{T}\exp{\big(}\int_{0}^{T}f(t,\widetilde{Z}^{x}_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t{\big)}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant{\big(}\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}}|{\mathbb{Z}}_{T}|^{r^{\prime}}{\big)}^{1/r^{\prime}}\left(\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}}\exp{\big(}r\int_{0}^{T}f(t,\widetilde{Z}^{x}_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t{\big)}\right)^{1/r}<\infty

by (
3.29
) and (
3.15
).

(iv): Let

\displaystyle A^{f}(h,X):=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}(f(s,X^{x}_{s})-f(s,X^{x}_{\pi_{h}(s)})){\mathord{{\rm d}}}u\Big|^{2}

and

\displaystyle A^{f}(h,\widetilde{Z}):=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\int_{{0}}^{t}(f(s,\widetilde{Z}^{x}_{s})-f(s,\widetilde{Z}^{x}_{\pi_{h}(s)})){\mathord{{\rm d}}}u\Big|^{2}.

For any
\delta\in(0,1)
, we note that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}A^{f}(h,X)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}}{\big(}{\mathbb{Z}}_{T}A^{f}(h,\widetilde{Z}){\big)}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}}{\big(}{\mathbb{Z}}_{T}|A^{f}(h,\widetilde{Z})|^{\delta}|A^{f}(h,\widetilde{Z})|^{1-\delta}{\big)}.

Based on Hölder’s inequality, (
3.29
), (
3.26
) and (
3.21
), for
1/r^{\prime}+1/r=1
with some

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}A^{f}(h,X)

\displaystyle\leqslant{\Big[}\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}}{\big(}|{\mathbb{Z}}_{T}|^{1/\delta}A^{f}(h,\widetilde{Z}){\big)}{\Big]}^{\delta}{\Big[}\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}}A^{f}(h,\widetilde{Z}){\Big]}^{1-\delta}

\displaystyle\leqslant{\big(}\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}}|{\mathbb{Z}}_{T}|^{r^{\prime}/\delta}{\big)}^{\delta/r^{\prime}}{\big(}\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}}|A^{f}(h,\widetilde{Z})|^{r}{\big)}^{\delta/r}{\big(}\widetilde{{\mathbb{E}}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}}A^{f}(h,\widetilde{Z}){\big)}^{1-\delta}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}||f|^{2r}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p/(2r)}(T)}^{\delta/r}h^{(1-\delta_{0})}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p}(T)}^{2(1-\delta)}

where

,
and complete the proof.

\sup_{x}\|\mu_{s}^{x}-\mu_{t}^{x}\|_{var}

To this end, we will use the relation between the PDE and the SDE.
For any
T>0
, consider the following backward PDE:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u^{T}+B\cdot\nabla u^{T}+a_{ij}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u^{T}=0,\quad u^{T}(T)=\varphi,

(3.31)

where
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{b}
. By Proposition

,
there exists a unique solution
u^{T}
to (

) in the sense of Definition
2.3

\displaystyle P^{X}_{s,t}f(x):={\mathbb{E}}f(X^{x}_{s,t}),\quad P^{X}_{t}:=P^{X}_{0,t}.

By (

), the domain of

includes
\widetilde{L}^{p}
for any

.
Then we have the following probabilistic representation.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
T>0
,
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
u^{T}
and
X^{x}_{s,t}

be the solutions to (

) and (

\displaystyle u^{T}(s,x)={\mathbb{E}}\varphi(X^{x}_{s,T})=P^{X}_{s,T}\varphi(x).

(3.32)

Proof.

It is straightforward to obtain (

) by applying the generalized Itô formula (
3.4
)
to the function

and taking expectation.

Apart from the probabilistic representation, by the generalized Itô formula, we have the following Duhamel formula.

Lemma 3.12

(Duhamel formula)
.

For any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\displaystyle P^{X}_{s,t}\varphi(x)=P^{\sigma}_{t-s}\varphi(x)+\int_{s}^{t}P^{X}_{s,r}{\big(}B(r)\cdot\nabla P^{\sigma}_{t-r}\varphi{\big)}(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r.

(3.33)

Proof.

For any
t\in[0,T]
, let
v^{t}=v^{t}(t,x)
be the solution to the following backward PDE:

\displaystyle\partial_{r}v^{t}+a_{ij}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}v^{t}=0,\quad v^{t}(t)=\varphi.

Based on (
3.32
), one sees that
v^{t}(r)=P^{\sigma}_{t-r}\varphi
.
By the generalized Itô formula (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}v^{t}(t,X^{x}_{s,t})=v^{t}(s,x)+{\mathbb{E}}\int_{s}^{t}(\partial_{r}v^{t}+a_{ij}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}v^{t}+B\cdot\nabla v^{t})(r,X^{x}_{s,r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r,

which implies that

\displaystyle P^{X}_{s,t}\varphi(x)

\displaystyle=P^{\sigma}_{t-s}\varphi+{\mathbb{E}}\int_{s}^{t}(B(r)\cdot\nabla P^{\sigma}_{t-r}\varphi)(X^{x}_{s,r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

\displaystyle=P^{\sigma}_{t-s}\varphi+\int_{s}^{t}P^{X}_{s,r}(B(r)\cdot\nabla P^{\sigma}_{t-r}\varphi)(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r,

and we complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.13
.

Assume (
3.1
). For any
T>0
and
q\in[p_{0},\infty)
, there is a constant
C=C(\Xi,q)
such that for any

\displaystyle\|P^{X}_{t}\varphi-P^{X}_{s}\varphi\|_{\infty}\leqslant C{\Big[}[(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{2q}}s^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{d}{2q}}]\wedge 1{\Big]}s^{-\frac{d}{2q}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\varphi|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{q}.

(3.34)

In particular, when
q=\infty
, for any
\delta>0
, there is a constant
C=C(\Xi,\delta)
such that for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\|\mu^{x}_{t}-\mu^{x}_{s}\|_{var}\leqslant C{\Big[}[(t-s)^{1/2-\delta}s^{-1/2}]\wedge 1{\Big]}.

(3.35)

Proof.

For simplicity, let

\displaystyle\alpha:=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{2q}.

From (
3.33
), one sees that

\displaystyle P^{X}_{t}\varphi-P^{X}_{s}\varphi=

\displaystyle{\big(}P^{\sigma}_{t}\varphi-P^{\sigma}_{s}\varphi{\big)}+\int_{s}^{t}P^{X}_{r}{\big(}B(r)\cdot\nabla P^{\sigma}_{t-r}\varphi{\big)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{s}P^{X}_{r}\Big[B(r)\cdot\nabla{\big(}P^{\sigma}_{t-r}-P^{\sigma}_{s-r}{\big)}\varphi\Big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle{\mathscr{I}}_{1}+{\mathscr{I}}_{2}+{\mathscr{I}}_{3}.

Based on (
3.16
), we have

\displaystyle\|{\mathscr{I}}_{1}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\Big[}[(t-s)s^{-1}]\wedge 1{\Big]}s^{-\frac{d}{2q}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\varphi|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{q}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\Big[}(t-s)^{\alpha}s^{-\alpha}{\Big]}s^{-\frac{d}{2q}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\varphi|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{q}.

By (
3.25
) and (
3.13
), we have

\displaystyle\|{\mathscr{I}}_{2}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{s}^{t}r^{-d/(2p_{0})}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|B(r)\cdot\nabla P^{\sigma}_{t-r}\varphi|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p_{0}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{s}^{t}r^{-d/(2p_{0})}\|\nabla P^{\sigma}_{t-r}\varphi\|_{\infty}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

\displaystyle\lesssim s^{-d/(2q)}\int_{s}^{t}r^{-d/(2p_{0})+d/(2q)}(t-r)^{-1/2-d/(2q)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\varphi|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{q}

\displaystyle:=s^{-d/(2q)}K(t,s)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\varphi|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{q},

\displaystyle K(t,s)

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(s^{-\frac{d}{2p_{0}}+\frac{d}{2q}}\int_{s}^{t}(t-r)^{\alpha-1}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right)\wedge\left((t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{2p_{0}}}\int_{0}^{1}r^{-\frac{d}{2p_{0}}+\frac{d}{2q}}(1-r)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{2q}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim(s^{-\alpha}(t-s)^{\alpha})\wedge 1

since
q\geqslant p_{0}>d
.

It remains to estimate
{\mathscr{I}}_{3}
. By (
3.25
) and (
3.16
), we have

\displaystyle\|{\mathscr{I}}_{3}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{s}r^{-d/(2p_{0})}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|B(r)\cdot\nabla{\big(}P^{\sigma}_{t-r}-P^{\sigma}_{s-r}{\big)}\varphi|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p_{0}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{s}r^{-d/(2p_{0})}\|\nabla{\big(}P^{\sigma}_{t-r}-P^{\sigma}_{s-r}{\big)}\varphi\|_{\infty}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{s}r^{-d/(2p_{0})}{\Big[}[(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}(s-r)^{-\frac{1}{2}}]\wedge 1{\Big]}(s-r)^{-1/2-d/(2q)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\varphi|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{q}.

We note that

\displaystyle{\mathscr{J}}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{s}r^{-d/(2p_{0})}{\Big[}[(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}(s-r)^{-\frac{1}{2}}]\wedge 1{\Big]}(s-r)^{-1/2-d/(2q)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{s}r^{-d/(2p_{0})}(s-r)^{-1/2-d/(2q)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\lesssim s^{1/2-d/(2p_{0})-d/(2q)}\lesssim s^{-d/(2q)},

(3.36)

since
p_{0}>d
. In addition, when
r\in(0,\frac{s}{2}]
, one sees that

\displaystyle{\Big[}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}(s-r)^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\Big]}\wedge 1\leqslant(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{2q}}(s-r)^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{d}{2q}}\leqslant(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{2q}}{\big(}\frac{s}{2}{\big)}^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{d}{2q}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle{\mathscr{J}}\lesssim

\displaystyle s^{-1/2-d/(2q)}\int_{0}^{s/2}r^{-d/(2p_{0})}{\Big[}[(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}(s-r)^{-\frac{1}{2}}]\wedge 1{\Big]}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

\displaystyle+s^{-d/(2p_{0})}\int_{s/2}^{s}{\Big[}[(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}(s-r)^{-{\frac{1}{2}}}]\wedge 1{\Big]}(s-r)^{-1/2-d/(2q)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle s^{-1}(t-s)^{1/2-d/(2q)}\int_{0}^{s/2}r^{-d/(2p_{0})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

\displaystyle+s^{-d/(2p_{0})}\int_{s/2}^{s}{\Big[}[(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}(s-r)^{-\frac{1}{2}}]\wedge 1{\Big]}(s-r)^{-1/2-d/(2q)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r.

\displaystyle{\mathscr{J}}\lesssim

\displaystyle s^{-d/(2p_{0})}(t-s)^{1/2-d/(2q)}

\displaystyle+s^{-d/(2p_{0})}(t-s)^{1-1/2-d/(2q)}\int_{0}^{s/(t-s)}{\Big[}(s-r)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\wedge 1{\Big]}(s-r)^{-1/2-d/(2q)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

\displaystyle s^{-d/(2p_{0})}(t-s)^{1-1/2-d/(2q)}\lesssim s^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{2q}}

since
q<\infty
and
p_{0}>d

\displaystyle{\mathscr{J}}\lesssim{\big(}s^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t-s)^{\alpha}{\big)}\wedge s^{-\frac{d}{2q}}=\Big([(t-s)^{\alpha}s^{-\alpha}]\wedge 1\Big)s^{-\frac{d}{2q}}.

Thus, we obtain (

).
In particular, noting that

\displaystyle|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\varphi|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{q}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{\infty},\quad\forall q<\infty,

by Lusin’s theorem and (

\displaystyle\|\mu^{x}_{t}-\mu^{x}_{s}\|_{var}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{b}}\frac{|P^{X}_{t}\varphi(x)-P^{X}_{s}\varphi(x)|}{\|\varphi\|_{\infty}}\lesssim[(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{2q}}s^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{d}{2q}}]s^{-\frac{d}{2q}}

Moreover, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\|\mu^{x}_{t}-\mu^{x}_{s}\|_{var}\leqslant 2,

which completes the proof.
∎

The following lemma is the distribution dependent version of (
3.28
).

Lemma 3.14
.

For any
T>0
,
p\in(d\vee 2,\infty)
, assume that
f:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\displaystyle\kappa_{f}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{\mu,\nu}\Big(|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f(t,\cdot,\mu)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}+\frac{|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f(t,\cdot,\mu)-f(t,\cdot,\nu)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}}{\|\mu-\nu\|_{var}}\Big)<\infty.

Then, for any
\delta>0
, there is a constant
C=C(\Xi,p,\delta)
such that for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
h\in(0,1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}(f(s,X^{x}_{s},\mu^{x}_{s})&-f(s,X^{x}_{\pi_{h}(s)},\mu^{x}_{\pi_{h}(s)})){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\Big|^{2}\Big)\leqslant C(\kappa_{f})^{2}h^{1-\delta}.\end{split}

(3.37)

Proof.

Lemma 3.16

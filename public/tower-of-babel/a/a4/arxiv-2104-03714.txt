The defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation with step-like oscillatory initial data
Samuel Fromm 1, Jonatan Lenells 1, Ronald Quirchmayr 2

Abstract
. We study the Cauchy problem for the defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation under the assumption that the solution vanishes as x → + ∞ x\to+\infty and approaches an oscillatory plane wave as x → − ∞ x\to-\infty . We first develop an inverse scattering transform formalism for solutions satisfying such step-like boundary conditions. Using this formalism, we prove that there exists a global solution of the corresponding Cauchy problem and establish a representation for this solution in terms of the solution of a Riemann–Hilbert problem. By performing a steepest descent analysis of this Riemann–Hilbert problem, we identify three asymptotic sectors in the half-plane t ≥ 0 t\geq 0 of the x ​ t xt -plane and derive asymptotic formulas for the solution in each of these sectors. Finally, by restricting the constructed solutions to the half-line x ≥ 0 x\geq 0 , we find a class of solutions with asymptotically time-periodic boundary values previously sought for in the context of the NLS half-line problem.

: 35Q55, 41A60, 35Q15.

Keywords
: Nonlinear Schrödinger equation, asymptotics, step-like initial data, Riemann–Hilbert problem, nonlinear steepest descent, initial-boundary value problem.

1.
Introduction

We study the Cauchy problem for the defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation

(1.1a)

\displaystyle iu_{t}+u_{xx}-2|u|^{2}u=0,

\displaystyle x\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad t>0,

(1.1b)

\displaystyle u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),

\displaystyle x\in{\mathbb{R}},

under the assumption that the solution
u(x,t)
vanishes as
x\to+\infty
and approaches a plane wave as
x\to-\infty
, i.e.,

(1.2)

u(x,t)\sim\begin{cases}\alpha\mathrm{e}^{2i\beta x+i\omega t},&x\to-\infty,\\
0,&x\to+\infty,\end{cases}

where
\alpha>0
and
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}
are two parameters. The constant
\omega\coloneqq-4\beta^{2}-2\alpha^{2}<0
in (
1.2
) is determined by the requirement that
\alpha\mathrm{e}^{2i\beta x+i\omega t}
should be a solution of (
1.1a
), which is necessary for consistency.
The phase invariance of (
1.1a
) implies that there is no loss of generality in assuming that
\alpha
is positive.
In order for the boundary conditions (
1.2
) to be consistent with (
1.1
), the initial data must satisfy

(1.3)

u_{0}(x)\sim\begin{cases}\alpha\mathrm{e}^{2i\beta x},&x\to-\infty,\\
0,&x\to+\infty.\end{cases}

The exact rates of convergence in (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) will be specified in the main theorems.

The NLS equation with non-vanishing boundary conditions at infinity has received plenty of attention in the literature, see e.g.

In this paper, we use Riemann–Hilbert (RH) methods to study the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) with the step-like boundary conditions (
1.2
). In particular, we prove that there exists a global solution of the problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) and establish a representation for this solution in terms of the solution of a RH problem. The formulation of the RH problem involves a reflection coefficient which is defined in terms of the initial data. We also obtain detailed asymptotic formulas for the long-time behavior of the solution by performing a Deift–Zhou steepest descent analysis
[
18
]
of the RH problem. It turns out that there are three main asymptotic sectors in the half-plane
t\geq 0
of the
xt
-plane separated by the two lines
x/t=4\beta-2\alpha
and
x/t=4\beta+4\alpha
; we refer to these three sectors as the left, middle, and right sectors, see Figure
1
. In the left sector (i.e., the sector adjacent to the negative
x
-axis), the leading term in the asymptotics is given by the plane wave
\alpha\mathrm{e}^{2i\beta x+i\omega t}
multiplied by a slowly varying factor which tends to
1
as
x/t\to-\infty
, in consistency with the boundary conditions (
1.2
). In the right sector (i.e., the sector adjacent to the positive
x
-axis), the leading term is of order
t^{-1/2}
and has a coefficient which vanishes as
x/t\to+\infty
, again in consistency with (
1.2
).
In the middle sector, which lies between the left and right sectors, the asymptotics has a different character. We will compute both the leading and the subleading terms in this sector. The leading term is generally of order
\mathcal{O}(1)
but it vanishes as
x/t
approaches
4\beta+4\alpha
, thus matching the leading asymptotics in the right sector. As
x/t
approaches
4\beta-2\alpha
, it tends, as expected, to the leading term in the left sector. The subleading term in the middle sector is of order
\mathcal{O}(t^{-1})
.

1.1.
Relation to earlier work

First results on the implementation of an Inverse Scattering Transform (IST) to the defocusing NLS with nonzero boundary conditions were obtained in
[
47
]
where solutions satisfying
|u(x,t)|\to c
as
x\to\pm\infty
for some constant
c>0
were considered. The asymmetric case where
u(x,t)
approaches plane waves of different amplitudes as
x\to+\infty
and
x\to-\infty
was considered in
[
8
]
.
We refer to
[
4
,
6
,
13
,
21
,
27
,
20
,
32
,
33
,
36
]
and the references therein for further works on the IST for NLS.
The implementation of the IST presented here can be compared to the implementation for the focusing NLS in
[
13
]
.

Studies of the long-time asymptotics for NLS with nonzero boundary conditions include
[
5
,
13
,
7
,
14
,
16
,
31
,
33
,
15
]
. Most of these studies concern the focusing NLS. Of particular interest to us is the work
[
33
]
, where Jenkins derived detailed formulas for the long-time asymptotics for the defocusing NLS equation with step-like initial data of the form

(1.4)

\displaystyle u_{0}(x)=\begin{cases}1,&x<0,\\
A\mathrm{e}^{-2i\mu x},&x>0,\end{cases}

for any choice of the constants
A>0
and
\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
If
u(x,t)
solves (
1.1a
), then the function
\tilde{u}(x,t)
defined by

\tilde{u}(x,t)\coloneqq au(a(x+4bt),a^{2}t)\mathrm{e}^{-2ib(x+2bt)},

also solves (
1.1a
) for any
a>0
and
b\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Moreover, if
u_{0}
satisfies (
1.4
), then
\tilde{u}_{0}(x)=\tilde{u}(x,0)
satisfies

(1.5)

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{0}(x)=\begin{cases}A_{1}\mathrm{e}^{-2iB_{1}x},&x<0,\\
A_{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2iB_{2}x},&x>0,\end{cases}

where
A_{1}=a
,
B_{1}=b
,
A_{2}=aA
, and
B_{2}=\mu a+b
.
Hence, whenever the initial data satisfy (
1.5
) with
A_{1}>0,A_{2}>0
, and
B_{1},B_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the asymptotics can be deduced from
[
33
]
. However, the nonzero boundary conditions (
1.2
) considered in this paper (for which
A_{2}=0
) fall outside the scope of
[
33
]
. It is therefore not surprising that the qualitative structure of the asymptotic picture we find is different from the one obtained in
[
33
]
.

A main difference between
[
33
]
and the present paper is that whereas
[
33
]
assumes that the initial data is
equal
to
1
and
A\mathrm{e}^{-2i\mu x}
for
x<0
and
x>0
, respectively, we only impose the boundary conditions (
1.2
)
asymptotically
as
x\to\pm\infty
. In fact, one of the main motivations behind this work was to understand how asymptotically imposed nonzero boundary conditions can be handled for an integrable PDE. The fact that the boundary conditions are only asymptotically imposed has rather far-reaching implications for the proof, because it means that instead of having an exact expression for the reflection coefficient
r(k)
at our disposal, we need to deduce the pertinent properties of
r(k)
from its definition in terms of solutions of Volterra equations and construct analytic approximations (or dbar extensions) for the asymptotic analysis. The construction of analytic approximations is challenging especially near the branch cut associated with the nonzero boundary conditions. We overcome this challenge by generalizing a construction in
[
26
]
.

We will show that there are no solitonic structures present in the long-time asymptotics for (
1.1a
) in the case of the boundary conditions (
1.2
). In particular, the discrete spectrum is always empty. In the case of nonzero backgrounds at both
+\infty
and
-\infty
of the same amplitudes, the discrete spectrum is generally non-empty, corresponding to the fact that the defocusing NLS supports dark solitons
[
47
]
(see also
[
20
]
). On the other hand, the discrete spectrum is always empty in the case of zero boundary conditions at both
\pm\infty
, corresponding to the fact that the equation admits no bright solitons, see
[
1
]
. It is interesting that vanishing boundary conditions at
+\infty
alone imply the absence of asymptotic solitonic structures.

The long-time asymptotics for the
focusing
NLS in the case when the left background is zero, i.e.,

(1.6)

\displaystyle u_{0}(x)=\begin{cases}0,&x<0,\\
A\mathrm{e}^{-2iBx},&x>0,\end{cases}

where
A>0
and
B\in{\mathbb{R}}
are constants, was analyzed in
[
14
]
. It was found that there are three asymptotic sectors in this case: a slow decay sector adjacent to the negative
x
-axis, a modulated plane wave sector adjacent to the positive
x
-axis, and a modulated elliptic wave (genus
1
) sector in between. This means that the asymptotic picture observed in
[
14
]
bears similarities to the one we find here for the defocusing NLS with the boundary conditions (
1.2
): in both cases there are three asymptotic sectors, and in both cases there is a slow decay sector and a modulated plane wave sector adjacent to the
x
-axis, as mandated by the boundary conditions. However, in the focusing case studied in
[
14
]
the middle sector is a genus
1
sector (i.e., the leading asymptotic term is expressed in terms of theta functions attached to a genus
1
Riemann surface), whereas in the defocusing case considered here the middle sector is a genus
0
sector (i.e., the underlying Riemann surface is of genus
0
).
As in
[
33
]
, it is assumed in
[
14
]
that the initial data is a pure step-like profile (meaning that
u_{0}(x)
is exactly equal to
0
for
x<0
and to
Ae^{-2iBx}
for
x>0
).
We expect that the techniques developed in this paper will be useful to handle the case of boundary conditions that are only asymptotically imposed also for the focusing NLS as well as other integrable PDEs.

1.2.
Application to the half-line problem

One motivation for studying step-like boundary conditions of the form (
1.2
) is that the restriction to the quarter-plane
\{x\geq 0,\,t\geq 0\}
of any solution of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) provides a solution of the half-line problem for (
1.1a
) with decay as
x\to\infty
. This initial-boundary value problem for NLS has been studied extensively (see e.g.
[
9
,
10
,
12
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
28
,
29
,
37
,
39
,
40
,
41
,
43
]
). Of particular interest is the case of (asymptotically) time-periodic boundary conditions
[
9
]
. The main difficulty when studying such boundary conditions is the construction of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map
[
40
]
. If both the Dirichlet and the Neumann boundary values are known, then an effective solution representation can be obtained
[
22
,
25
]
. However, for a well-posed problem only one of these boundary values can be independently prescribed, and the construction of the unknown boundary value (i.e., the construction of the generalized Dirichlet-to-Neumann map) is in general a highly nonlinear problem
[
23
]
. In the case of asymptotically time-periodic boundary conditions, progress can be made by considering the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map in the large
t
limit (which is enough for the calculation of long-time asymptotics for the solution for any
x\geq 0
)
[
12
,
40
]
. For example, assuming that the Dirichlet and Neumann values asymptote to periodic functions, the large
t
-behavior of the Neumann value can be effectively computed from the Dirichlet data to all orders in perturbation theory
[
41
]
.
It is particularly interesting to consider the situation when the boundary values asymptote to single exponentials. It was shown for the focusing NLS in
[
11
,
12
]
that there exists a solution
u
satisfying
u(0,t)\sim\alpha\mathrm{e}^{i\omega t}
and
u_{x}(0,t)\sim c\mathrm{e}^{i\omega t}
as
t\to\infty
if and only if the triplet of constants
(\alpha,\omega,c)
belongs to one of two explicitly given families, thereby characterizing the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map in this regime. A similar result was obtained for the defocusing NLS in
[
37
]
. However, in
[
37
]

five
families were found instead of
two
, and the fact that
(\alpha,\omega,c)
belongs to one of these families was (due to the lack of a vanishing lemma for the defocusing NLS on the half-line) only shown to be a
necessary
condition for the existence of a solution
u
with
u(0,t)\sim\alpha\mathrm{e}^{i\omega t}
and
u_{x}(0,t)\sim c\mathrm{e}^{i\omega t}
. Thus, to characterize the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map in this regime in the defocusing case, one needs to determine whether there really are solutions of the initial-boundary value problem on the half-line corresponding to each of the five families found in
[
37
]
. One of these five families is (see
[
37
, Eq. (2.3c)]
)

(1.7)

\displaystyle\Big\{\big(\alpha,\omega,c=i\alpha\sqrt{-2\alpha^{2}-\omega}\big)\colon\alpha>0,\omega<-3\alpha^{2}\Big\}.

We show in Theorem
2.6
that for each choice of
(\alpha,\omega,c)
in the family (
1.7
),
there is indeed a solution
u
of the defocusing NLS such that
u(0,t)\sim\alpha\mathrm{e}^{i\omega t}
and
u_{x}(0,t)\sim c\mathrm{e}^{i\omega t}
as
t\to\infty
. This answers affirmatively the question left open in
[
37
]
for this family. Moreover, Theorem
2.6
provides a natural interpretation of the family (
1.7
): boundary values corresponding to this family arise by restricting solutions of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) to the half-line
x\geq 0
whenever the
t
-axis belongs to the left asymptotic sector, i.e., whenever
\alpha>0
and
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}
are such that
4\beta-2\alpha>0
.

2.
Main results

Before stating the main results, we need to introduce some ingredients relevant for the direct and inverse scattering problems.

2.1.
Eigenfunctions and reflection coefficient

Equation (
1.1a
) admits the Lax pair

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\phi_{x}+ik\sigma_{3}\phi=\mathsf{U}\phi,\\
\phi_{t}+2ik^{2}\sigma_{3}\phi=\mathsf{V}\phi,\end{cases}

where
k\in{\mathbb{C}}
is the spectral parameter and

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathsf{U}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}0&u\\
\bar{u}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathsf{V}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}-i|u|^{2}&2ku+iu_{x}\\
2k\bar{u}-i\bar{u}_{x}&i|u|^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Suppose
\alpha>0
and
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
\omega\coloneqq-4\beta^{2}-2\alpha^{2}<0
and consider the following plane wave solution of (
1.1a
):

(2.3)

u^{b}\colon{\mathbb{R}}\times[0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{C}},\qquad u^{b}(x,t)\coloneqq\alpha\mathrm{e}^{2i\beta x+i\omega t}.

The corresponding “background” Lax pair is given by

(2.4)

\begin{cases}\phi^{b}_{x}+ik\sigma_{3}\phi^{b}=\mathsf{U}^{b}\phi^{b},\\
\phi^{b}_{t}+2ik^{2}\sigma_{3}\phi^{b}=\mathsf{V}^{b}\phi^{b},\end{cases}

with

\displaystyle\mathsf{U}^{b}(x,t)=\begin{pmatrix}0&\alpha\mathrm{e}^{2i\beta x+i\omega t}\\
\alpha\mathrm{e}^{-2i\beta x-i\omega t}&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\mathsf{V}^{b}(x,t)=\begin{pmatrix}-i\alpha^{2}&2\alpha(k-\beta)\mathrm{e}^{2i\beta x+i\omega t}\\
2\alpha(k-\beta)\mathrm{e}^{-2i\beta x-i\omega t}&i\alpha^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

2.1.1.
Background eigenfunction

Let
E_{1}\coloneqq-\beta-\alpha
and
E_{2}\coloneqq-\beta+\alpha
. We define the functions
X,\Omega\colon{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(E_{1},E_{2})\to{\mathbb{C}}
by

X(k)=\sqrt{(k-E_{1})(k-E_{2})},\qquad\Omega(k)=2\left(k-\beta\right)X(k),

where the branch of the square root is chosen such that

X(k)=\sqrt{\left(k+\beta\right)^{2}-\alpha^{2}}=k+\beta+\mathcal{O}(k^{-1})\qquad\text{as}\quad k\to\infty;

it follows that

\Omega(k)=2k^{2}+\frac{\omega}{2}+\mathcal{O}(k^{-1})
as
k\to\infty
.
We further consider the function

(2.5)

\Delta\colon{\mathbb{C}}\setminus[E_{1},E_{2}]\to{\mathbb{C}},\qquad\Delta(k)=\bigg(\frac{k-E_{2}}{k-E_{1}}\bigg)^{1/4},

where the branch of the fourth root is such that
\Delta(k)=1+\mathcal{O}(k^{-1})
as
k\to\infty
.

The background Lax pair (
2.4
) admits the explicit background eigenfunction

(2.6)

\displaystyle\phi_{1}^{b}\colon{\mathbb{R}}\times[0,\infty)\times\big({\mathbb{C}}\setminus[E_{1},E_{2}]\big)\to{\mathbb{C}}^{2\times 2},\quad\phi^{b}_{1}(x,t,k)=\mathrm{e}^{i\left({\beta}x+\frac{\omega}{2}t\right)\sigma_{3}}\,s^{b}(k)\,\mathrm{e}^{-i(X(k)x+\Omega(k)t)\sigma_{3}},

where
s^{b}\colon{\mathbb{C}}\setminus[E_{1},E_{2}]\to{\mathbb{C}}^{2\times 2}
is given by

(2.7)

\displaystyle s^{b}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\Delta+\Delta^{-1}&i(\Delta-\Delta^{-1})\\
-i(\Delta-\Delta^{-1})&\Delta+\Delta^{-1}\end{pmatrix},

and satisfies
\det s^{b}(k)=1
for
k\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus[E_{1},E_{2}]
.

2.1.2.
Reflection coefficient

Let
u^{b}_{0}(x)=\alpha\mathrm{e}^{2i\beta x}
and let
u_{0}\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be such that

(u_{0}-u^{b}_{0})|_{{\mathbb{R}}_{-}}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{-})\quad\text{and} \quad u_{0}|_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}).

Let
\mathsf{U}_{0}(x)=\mathsf{U}(x,0)
and
\mathsf{U}_{0}^{b}(x)=\mathsf{U}^{b}(x,0)
.
Define the eigenfunctions
\{\phi_{j}(x,k)\}^{2}_{j=1}
as the unique solutions of the Volterra integral equations

(2.8a)

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(x,k)

\displaystyle=\phi_{1}^{b}(x,0,k)+\int_{-\infty}^{x}\phi_{1}^{b}(x,0,k)(\phi_{1}^{b})^{-1}(x^{\prime},0,k)\left[(\mathsf{U}_{0}-\mathsf{U}_{0}^{b})(x^{\prime})\right]\phi_{1}(x^{\prime},k)\,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime},

(2.8b)

\displaystyle\phi_{2}(x,k)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-ikx\sigma_{3}}-\int^{\infty}_{x}\mathrm{e}^{ik(x^{\prime}-x)\sigma_{3}}\mathsf{U}_{0}(x^{\prime})\phi_{2}(x^{\prime},k)\,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}.

For each
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the map
\phi_{1}(x,\cdot)\colon({\mathbb{C}}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}_{-})\to{\mathbb{C}}^{2\times 2}
is analytic and has a continuous extension to
(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu{\mathbb{C}}_{-}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\setminus\{E_{1},E_{2}\}
, which is also denoted by
\phi_{1}
. This means that the first column of
\phi_{1}
is continuous on
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus\{E_{1},E_{2}\}
while the second column is continuous on
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu{\mathbb{C}}_{-}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus\{E_{1},E_{2}\}
.
For each
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the map
\phi_{2}(x,\cdot)\colon(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu{\mathbb{C}}_{-}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\to{\mathbb{C}}^{2\times 2}
is continuous and its restriction to
({\mathbb{C}}_{-},{\mathbb{C}}_{+})
is analytic.
Both
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
solve the
x
-part of the Lax pair (
2.1
) for
k\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{E_{1},E_{2}\}
and
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times[0,\infty)
. Hence there exists a unique matrix-valued function
s(k)
such that

(2.9)

s(k)=\phi^{-1}_{2}(x,k)\,\phi_{1}(x,k),\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}},~k\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{E_{1},E_{2}\}.

The symmetries of the Lax pair imply that
s
has the form

(2.10)

s(k)=\begin{pmatrix}a(k)&-b(k)\\
-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mub(k)\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu &\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mua(k)\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\end{pmatrix},\qquad k\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{E_{1},E_{2}\}.

We define the
reflection coefficient

r\colon{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{E_{1},E_{2}\}\to{\mathbb{C}}
by

(2.11)

r(k)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mub(k)\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}{a(k)},&k\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus[E_{1},E_{2}],\\

Lemma 5.2

(Analytic approximation of

for all

(ii)

\big|r_{a}(\xi,t,k)\big|\leq C\,\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{t}{4}|\text{Im\,}g(\xi,k)|}}{1+|k|^{2}}.

For all

\mathcal{O}(k^{-4}),&k\to-\infty.\end{cases}

In view of (
2.14
),
the coefficients

are rational functions of

with nonzero denominators of the form

In particular, we note that

\displaystyle Q_{1,-1}=-\frac{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muq_{1,0}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}{q_{1,0}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muq_{1,1}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muq_{1,0}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5muq_{1,1}}.

Next, we extend the function

to the interval

by setting

and define

by

(5.24)

\displaystyle h(\xi,k)\coloneqq\frac{p_{1}(\xi,k)}{X_{+}(\xi,k)}+p_{2}(\xi,k),

\displaystyle\frac{p_{1}(\xi,k)}{X_{+}(\xi,k)}=\begin{cases}\sum_{l\,\mathrm{odd}=-1}^{4}i^{l}{Q_{1,l}}(k-E_{1})^{l/2}+\mathcal{O}((k-E_{1})^{5/2}),&k\searrow E_{1},\\

and

(5.26)

\displaystyle p_{2}(\xi,k)=\begin{cases}\sum_{l\,\mathrm{even}=-1}^{4}i^{l}{Q_{1,l}}(k-E_{1})^{l/2}+\mathcal{O}((k-E_{1})^{5/2}),&k\searrow E_{1},\\

where the sums in (

The coefficients

.
By construction,

obeys the following properties:

(i)

uniformly for

,

as

;

(ii)

for every

such that

(iii)

the function

defined by

satisfies

\mathcal{O}(k^{-4}),&k\to-\infty,\\
\mathcal{O}(|k-E_{1}|^{5/2-n}),&k\to E_{1},\end{cases}

for

, uniformly for
\xi\in I_{\mathcal{M}}
;

(iv)

uniformly for
\xi\in I_{\mathcal{M}}
,

(5.28)

|r(k)h(\xi,k)+\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mur(k)h(\xi,k)\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+1|\in\mathcal{O}(|k-E_{1}|),\qquad(E_{1},\mu_{-})\ni k\searrow E_{1}.

Remark 5.5
.

The proof of Lemma
5.3
does not rely on an explicit expression of
r_{2}
on
(E_{1},E_{2})
in order to construct a function
r_{2,a}
with the desired properties. This generalizes a related construction in
[
26
]
, where
r_{2}
was defined on
(E_{1},E_{2})
in terms of
r
by
r_{2}(k)\coloneqq-r(k)/(1+r(k^{2}))
.
For consistency, additional assumptions were made in
[

]
, namely that
r\neq\pm i
on
(E_{1},E_{2})
and
r(E_{j})\in\{\pm i\}
,

. However, numerical experiments have shown that these assumptions are not satisfied in general. The above proof has the advantage that no additional assumptions are required.

for the Schwartz conjugate of a function

.
The regions

,

, and the jump contour

for

are depicted in Figure

.

5.1
,

, and

,
the function

satisfies the following RH problem for

:

•

is analytic in
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Gamma^{(3)}
;

•

m^{(3)}(x,t,\cdot)
has continuous boundary values on

satisfying the jump relation

\displaystyle m^{(3)}_{+}(x,t,k)=m^{(3)}_{-}(x,t,k)v^{(3)}(x,t,k)\quad\text{for}\;k\in\Gamma^{(3)}\setminus\{E_{1},\mu_{-},k_{0}\},

where
v^{(3)}=\mathcal{D}^{\sigma_{3}}_{-}\big(G^{(3)}_{-}\big)^{-1}\mathcal{D}^{-\sigma_{3}}_{-}\,v^{(2)}\,\mathcal{D}^{\sigma_{3}}_{+}G^{(3)}_{+}\mathcal{D}^{-\sigma_{3}}_{+}
;

•

m^{(3)}(x,t,k)=I+\mathcal{O}(k^{-1})
as
k\to\infty
;

•

m^{(3)}(x,t,k)=\mathcal{O}((k-E_{1})^{-1/4})
as
k\to E_{1}
;

•

m^{(3)}(x,t,k)=\mathcal{O}(1)
as
k\to k_{0}
;

•

m^{(3)}(x,t,k)=\mathcal{O}(1)
as
k\to\mu_{-}
.

Using (
5.7
), we can describe the behavior of
m^{(3)}
at
E_{1}
more precisely as follows:

(5.34)

\displaystyle m^{(3)}=\mathcal{D}_{\infty}^{\sigma_{3}}m^{(1)}G^{(3)}\times\begin{cases}(k-E_{1})^{-\sigma_{3}/4}\mathrm{e}^{-d_{0}\sigma_{3}}\big(I+\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{k-E_{1}})\big),&k\to E_{1},~k\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+},\\
(k-E_{1})^{\sigma_{3}/4}\mathrm{e}^{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mud_{0}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\sigma_{3}}\big(I+\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{k-E_{1}})\big),&k\to E_{1},~k\in{\mathbb{C}}_{-}.\end{cases}

Let us write
\Gamma^{(3)}=\cup_{j=1}^{8}\Gamma^{(3)}_{j}
, where
\Gamma^{(3)}_{j}
denotes the subcontour labeled by
j
in Figure
6
.
Letting
v^{(3)}_{j}
denote the restriction of
v^{(3)}
to
\Gamma^{(3)}_{j}
, we find

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} v^{(3)}_{1}&=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
-\mathcal{D}^{-2}r_{a}\mathrm{e}^{2itg}&1\end{pmatrix}&&v^{(3)}_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&\mathcal{D}_{+}\mathcal{D}^{-1}_{-}\mathrm{e}^{-2itg_{+}}\end{pmatrix}\\
v^{(3)}_{3}&=\begin{pmatrix}1&\mathcal{D}^{2}r^{*}_{a}\mathrm{e}^{-2itg}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}&&v^{(3)}_{4}=\begin{pmatrix}1-|r_{r}|^{2}&\mathcal{D}^{2}r^{*}_{r}\mathrm{e}^{-2itg}\\
-\mathcal{D}^{-2}r_{r}\mathrm{e}^{2itg}&1\end{pmatrix}\\
v^{(3)}_{5}&=\begin{pmatrix}1&\mathcal{D}^{2}_{+}r_{2,r}\mathrm{e}^{-2itg}\\
-\mathcal{D}^{-2}_{-}r^{*}_{2,r}\mathrm{e}^{2itg}&1-|r_{2,r}|^{2}(1-|r|^{2})^{2}\end{pmatrix}&&v^{(3)}_{6}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&\mathcal{D}_{+}\mathcal{D}^{-1}_{-}(rr_{2,a}+r^{*}r^{*}_{2,a}+1)\mathrm{e}^{-2itg_{+}}\end{pmatrix}\\
v^{(3)}_{7}&=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\mathcal{D}^{2}r_{2,a}\mathrm{e}^{-2itg}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}&&v^{(3)}_{8}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\mathcal{D}^{-2}r^{*}_{2,a}\mathrm{e}^{2itg}&1\end{pmatrix}\end{aligned}

where the simplification of the
(2,2)
-entry in
v^{(3)}_{6}
uses that

g_{+}=-g_{-}
,
\mathcal{D}_{+}\mathcal{D}_{-}=r
,
\mathcal{D}^{*}_{+}\mathcal{D}_{-}=1
, and
|r|\equiv 1
on
\Gamma^{(3)}_{6}
.

To leading order,
m^{(3)}
is approximated by a global parametrix (denoted by
m^{[E_{1},k_{0}]}
) with a constant off-diagonal jump across the branch cut
[E_{1},k_{0}]
. The sub-leading contribution stems from the local long-time behavior near the critical point
k_{0}
. It turns out that the local parametrix (denoted by
m^{k_{0}}
) can be constructed in terms of the solution of the well-known Airy RH problem.

5.4.
Global parametrix

On the branch cut
(E_{1},k_{0})
, the jump matrix
v^{(3)}
approaches

v^{[E_{1},k_{0}]}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad k\in(E_{1},k_{0}),

as
t\to\infty
, whereas
v^{(3)}\to I
for each fixed
k\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus[E_{1},k_{0}]
.
Thus for each
(x,t)\in\mathcal{M}
we consider the following RH problem:

•

m^{[E_{1},k_{0}]}(x,t,\cdot)
is analytic in
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus[E_{1},k_{0}]
;

•

m^{[E_{1},k_{0}]}(x,t,\cdot)
has continuous boundary values on
(E_{1},k_{0})
satisfying the jump relation

(5.35)

\displaystyle m^{[E_{1},k_{0}]}_{+}(x,t,k)=m^{[E_{1},k_{0}]}_{-}(x,t,k)v^{[E_{1},k_{0}]}(x,t,k)\quad\text{for}\;k\in(E_{1},k_{0});

•

m^{[E_{1},k_{0}]}(x,t,k)=I+\mathcal{O}(k^{-1})
as
k\to\infty
;

•

m^{[E_{1},k_{0}]}(x,t,k)=\mathcal{O}((k-E_{1})^{-1/4})
as
k\to E_{1}
;

•

m^{[E_{1},k_{0}]}(x,t,k)=\mathcal{O}((k-k_{0})^{-1/4})
as
k\to k_{0}
.

Let
\Delta_{0}\colon I_{\mathcal{M}}\times{\mathbb{C}}\setminus[E_{1},k_{0}]\to{\mathbb{C}}
be given by

(5.36)

\Delta_{0}(\xi,k)=\bigg(\frac{k-k_{0}}{k-E_{1}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{4}},

where the branch is such that
\Delta_{0}(k)=1+\mathcal{O}(k^{-1})
as
k\to\infty
.
The following result is standard.

Lemma 5.6
.

The unique solution of the RH problem (
5.35
) is given by

m^{[E_{1},k_{0}]}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\Delta_{0}+\Delta_{0}^{-1}&-i(\Delta_{0}-\Delta_{0}^{-1})\\
i(\Delta_{0}-\Delta_{0}^{-1})&\Delta_{0}+\Delta_{0}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}

Moreover, the following expansions hold uniformly for
\xi\in I_{\mathcal{M}}
:

(i)

As
k\to\infty
,

m^{[E_{1},k_{0}]}=I+\frac{i(k_{0}-E_{1})}{4k}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}+\mathcal{O}(k^{-2})
.

(ii)

As
k\to k_{0}
,

\displaystyle m^{[E_{1},k_{0}]}(\xi,k)

\displaystyle=\frac{(k_{0}-E_{1})^{1/4}}{2(k-k_{0})^{1/4}}\Bigg\{\begin{pmatrix}1&i\\
-i&1\end{pmatrix}+\frac{(k-k_{0})^{1/2}}{(k_{0}-E_{1})^{1/2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&-i\\
i&1\end{pmatrix}+\frac{k-k_{0}}{4(k_{0}-E_{1})}\begin{pmatrix}1&i\\
-i&1\\
\end{pmatrix}

(5.37)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(k-k_{0})^{3/2}}{4(k_{0}-E_{1})^{3/2}}\begin{pmatrix}-1&i\\
-i&-1\\
\end{pmatrix}+\mathcal{O}(|k-k_{0}|^{2})\Bigg\}.

5.5.
Local parametrix near the critical point

We will define the local parametrix with the help of the explicit solution to the classical Airy model RH problem.

5.5.1.
Airy model problem

Consider the contour
\Gamma^{\mathrm{Ai}}
consisting of the four components

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mathrm{Ai}}_{1}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\colon\arg z=2\pi/3\},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mathrm{Ai}}_{2}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\colon\arg z=\pi\},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mathrm{Ai}}_{3}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\colon\arg z=4\pi/3\},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mathrm{Ai}}_{4}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\colon\arg z=0\},

oriented as in Figure
8(a)
, which separate the sectors

\displaystyle S_{1}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\colon\arg z\in(0,2\pi/3)\},

\displaystyle S_{2}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\colon\arg z\in(2\pi/3,\pi)\},

\displaystyle S_{3}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\colon\arg z\in(\pi,4\pi/3,)\},

\displaystyle S_{4}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\colon\arg z\in(4\pi/3,2\pi)\}.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\mathrm{Ai}\colon{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
denote the complex Airy function and let
\zeta=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi i}{3}}
.
The function
m^{\mathrm{Ai}}\colon{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Gamma^{\mathrm{Ai}}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{2\times 2}
defined by

\displaystyle m^{\mathrm{Ai}}(z)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{cases}\mathcal{A}(z)\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2}{3}z^{3/2}\sigma_{3}},&z\in S_{1},\\
\mathcal{A}(z)\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
-1&1\end{pmatrix}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2}{3}z^{3/2}\sigma_{3}},\hskip-2.84544pt&z\in S_{2},\\
\mathcal{A}(z)\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2}{3}z^{3/2}\sigma_{3}},&z\in S_{3},\\
\mathcal{A}(z)\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2}{3}z^{3/2}\sigma_{3}},&z\in S_{4},\end{cases}\quad\mathcal{A}(z)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{Ai}(z)&\mathrm{Ai}(\zeta^{2}z)\\
\mathrm{Ai}^{\prime}(z)&\zeta^{2}\mathrm{Ai}^{\prime}(\zeta^{2}z)\end{pmatrix}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\pi i}{6}\sigma_{3}},\hskip-2.84544pt&z\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+},\\
\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{Ai}(z)&-\zeta^{2}\mathrm{Ai}(\zeta z)\\
\mathrm{Ai}^{\prime}(z)&-\mathrm{Ai}^{\prime}(\zeta z)\end{pmatrix}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\pi i}{6}\sigma_{3}},\hskip-2.84544pt&z\in{\mathbb{C}}_{-},\end{cases}

is analytic in
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Gamma^{\mathrm{Ai}}
and
m^{\mathrm{Ai}}_{+}(z)=m^{\mathrm{Ai}}_{-}(z)v^{\mathrm{Ai}}(z)
for all
z\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{Ai}}
, where
v^{\mathrm{Ai}}
is given by

v^{\mathrm{Ai}}(z)=\begin{cases}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
-\mathrm{e}^{\frac{4}{3}z^{3/2}}&1\end{pmatrix}&\text{on}\quad\Gamma^{\mathrm{Ai}}_{1}\cup\Gamma^{\mathrm{Ai}}_{3},\\
\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}&\text{on}\quad\Gamma^{\mathrm{Ai}}_{2},\\
\begin{pmatrix}1&-\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{4}{3}z^{3/2}}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}&\text{on}\quad\Gamma^{\mathrm{Ai}}_{4}.\end{cases}

The asymptotic behavior of
m^{\mathrm{Ai}}
as
z\to\infty
is given by

(5.38)

\displaystyle m^{\mathrm{Ai}}(z)\sim z^{-\frac{\sigma_{3}}{4}}N\bigg(I+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\mathrm{Ai}}_{j}}{z^{3j/2}}\bigg),\qquad z\to\infty,

where

N=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&i\\
i&1\end{pmatrix},\quad m^{\mathrm{Ai}}_{j}=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi i}{4}}}{\sqrt{2}}N^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-i\end{pmatrix}\bigg(\frac{3}{2}\bigg)^{j}\begin{pmatrix}(-1)^{j}u_{j}&u_{j}\\
-(-1)^{j}\nu_{j}&\nu_{j}\end{pmatrix}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\pi i}{4}\sigma_{3}},

and the real coefficients

u_{j},\nu_{j}
are defined by

u_{j}=\frac{(2j+1)(2j+3)\cdots(6j-1)}{(216)^{j}j!}\,,\quad\nu_{j}=\frac{6j+1}{1-6j}u_{j},\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 1}.

That is,

(5.39)

m^{\mathrm{Ai}}_{j}=-\frac{6^{-2j}(j+\frac{1}{2})_{2j}}{(6j-1)j!}\begin{pmatrix}(-1)^{j}&-6ij\\
(-1)^{j}6ij&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 1},

where the Pochhammer symbol
(a)_{j}
is defined by

(a)_{j}=\frac{\Gamma(a+j)}{\Gamma(a)}=a(a+1)(a+2)\cdots(a+j-1).

5.5.2.
Local transformation

Our aim is to show that the Airy solution
m^{\mathrm{Ai}}
yields a good approximation of
m^{(3)}
locally around
k_{0}
when considered in suitable coordinates.

Recalling the definition (
5.1
) of
g
,
we define the fractional power
g(\xi,k)^{2/3}
for
k\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(-\infty,k_{0}]
with the branch fixed by the requirement that

(g(k))^{2/3}>0
for
k>k_{0}
.
Let

f(\xi,k)\coloneqq-\bigg(\frac{3}{2}\,g(\xi,k)\bigg)^{\frac{2}{3}},\quad k\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(-\infty,k_{0}].

Then, uniformly for
\xi\in I_{\mathcal{M}}
,

(5.40)

\displaystyle f(\xi,k)=-3^{\frac{2}{3}}(k_{0}-E_{1})^{1/3}(k-k_{0})\bigg(1+\frac{k-k_{0}}{3(k_{0}-E_{1})}+\mathcal{O}\big((k-k_{0})^{2}\big)\bigg)

as
k\to k_{0}
,
k\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(-\infty,k_{0}]
, where
3^{\frac{2}{3}}(k_{0}-E_{1})^{1/3}>0
is bounded from below by a positive constant uniformly for
\xi\in I_{\mathcal{M}}
.
We introduce the new variable

(5.41)

z(\xi,t,k)\coloneqq t^{2/3}f(\xi,k).

Then

\frac{4}{3}z^{3/2}=\begin{cases}2itg(k),&\text{Im\,}k>0,\\
-2itg(k),&\text{Im\,}k<0,\\
2itg_{+}(k)=-2itg_{-}(k),&k<k_{0},\end{cases}

where the cut for
(\cdot)^{3/2}
runs along
{\mathbb{R}}_{-}
as in the Airy model problem.

5.5.3.
Definition and properties of
m^{k_{0}}

In the following we define the local parametrix
m^{k_{0}}
.
Let
D_{\varepsilon}(k_{0})
denote the open disc of radius
\varepsilon
around
k_{0}
and let
\varepsilon>0
be so small that

(5.42)

\bigcup^{4}_{j=1}\Big(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Gamma^{(3)}_{j}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\cap D_{\varepsilon}(k_{0})\Big)=\Gamma^{(3)}\cap D_{\varepsilon}(k_{0})

is satisfied for all
\xi\in I_{\mathcal{M}}
. The existence of such a uniform
\varepsilon
is guaranteed by the definition of
I_{\mathcal{M}}
. We denote the contour in (
5.42
) by
\Gamma^{k_{0},\varepsilon}

8(b)

Let
\mathfrak{r}\colon I_{\mathcal{M}}\times{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
denote the
(N-1)
-th degree Taylor polynomial of
r
at
k_{0}
:

(5.43)

\mathfrak{r}(\xi,k)\coloneqq\sum^{N-1}_{n=0}\frac{r^{(n)}(k_{0})}{n!}(k-k_{0})^{n}.

According to the assumptions on the initial datum
u_{0}
it holds that
r(k)=\mathfrak{r}(\xi,k)+\mathcal{O}(|k-k_{0}|^{N})
as
k\to k_{0}
for
1\leq N\leq 7
.
In order to obtain a good enough local approximation for
m^{(3)}
near
k_{0}
we need
N\geq 2
, thus we set
N\coloneqq 2
.
Furthermore, we define

\mathfrak{D}\colon I_{\mathcal{M}}\times\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muD_{\varepsilon}(k_{0})\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\setminus[k_{0}-\varepsilon,k_{0}+\varepsilon]\to{\mathbb{C}}
by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}(\xi,k)\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\mathcal{X}(\xi,k)}{2\pi i}\Big[\int^{k_{0}}_{E_{1}}\frac{\frac{1}{2}\log(\mathfrak{r}(\xi,s)/\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathfrak{r}(\xi,s)\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)}{\mathcal{X}_{+}(\xi,s)(s-k)}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{E_{2}}_{k_{0}}\frac{\log|\mathfrak{r}(\xi,s)|}{s-k}\,\mathrm{d}s-\sum^{N-1}_{n=0}c_{n}(\xi)(k-k_{0})^{n}\Big]}

where the real coefficients
\{c_{n}(\xi)\}^{N-1}_{n=0}
are uniquely determined by the requirement that

(5.44)

\mathcal{D}(\xi,k)=\mathfrak{D}(\xi,k)+\mathcal{O}(|k-k_{0}|^{N})\quad\text{as}\quad k\to k_{0}

uniformly for
\xi\in I_{\mathcal{M}}
, cf. Lemma
5.8
.

m^{k_{0}}\big(m^{[E_{1},k_{0}]}\big)^{-1}\to I

Lemma 5.8
.

The following assertions hold true:

(i)

there exist unique
\{c_{n}(\xi)\colon I_{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathbb{R}}\}^{N-1}_{n=0}
such that (
5.44
) holds with a uniform error term for all
\xi
;

(ii)

for each
\xi\in I_{\mathcal{M}}
and
t>0
the function
E(\xi,t,\cdot)\colon\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muD_{\varepsilon}(k_{0})\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\to{\mathbb{C}}
is analytic in
D_{\varepsilon}(k_{0})
.
Moreover,
E(\xi,t,k)
is invertible for all
(\xi,t,k)\in I_{\mathcal{M}}\times(0,\infty)\times\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muD_{\varepsilon}(k_{0})\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
.

Proof.

(
i
)
We note that additionally to
\mathfrak{r}(\xi,k)=r(k)+\mathcal{O}(|k-k_{0}|^{N})
as
k\to k_{0}
uniformly in
\xi\in I_{\mathcal{M}}
, we have that
|\mathfrak{r}(\xi,k)|=|r(k)|+\mathcal{O}(|k-k_{0}|^{N})
as
{\mathbb{R}}\ni k\to k_{0}
uniformly in
\xi\in I_{\mathcal{M}}
since
|r|\equiv 1
on
[E_{1},E_{2}]
. Consequently,

\log|\mathfrak{r}(\xi,k)|=\mathcal{O}(|k-k_{0}|^{N}),\quad{\mathbb{R}}\ni k\to k_{0}

uniformly in
\xi\in I_{\mathcal{M}}
.
In view of Lemma
A.1
and by noting that

\frac{1}{2}\log(\mathfrak{r}(\xi,s)/\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathfrak{r}(\xi,s)\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)=i\arg\mathfrak{r}(\xi,k)=\log\mathfrak{r}(\xi,k),

this means that there exist unique coefficients
\{c_{n}(\xi)\colon I_{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathbb{R}}\}^{N-1}_{n=0}
such that

\displaystyle\int^{k_{0}}_{E_{1}}\frac{\frac{1}{2}\log(\mathfrak{r}(\xi,s)/\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathfrak{r}(\xi,s)\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)}{\mathcal{X}_{+}(\xi,s)(s-k)}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{E_{2}}_{k_{0}}\frac{\log|\mathfrak{r}(\xi,s)|}{s-k}\,\mathrm{d}s-\sum^{N-1}_{n=0}c_{n}(\xi)(k-k_{0})^{n}

\displaystyle=\int^{E_{1}}_{-\infty}\frac{\log(1-|r(s)|^{2})}{\mathcal{X}(\xi,s)(s-k)}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{k_{0}}_{E_{1}}\frac{\log(r(s))}{\mathcal{X}_{+}(\xi,s)(s-k)}\,\mathrm{d}s+\mathcal{O}(|k-k_{0}|^{N}),

which implies (
5.44
).

(
ii
)
Let
(\xi,t)\in I_{\mathcal{M}}\times(0,\infty)
be arbitrary. It is clear from its definition that
E(\xi,t,\cdot)
is analytic in
D_{\varepsilon}(k_{0})\setminus(k_{0}-\varepsilon,k_{0}+\varepsilon)
, and that the one-sided limits
E_{\pm}(\xi,t,\cdot)
are well-defined along
(k_{0}-\varepsilon,k_{0}+\varepsilon)\setminus\{0\}
. Furthermore,
E(\xi,t,\cdot)
has no jump across
(k_{0},k_{0}+\varepsilon)
because
\frac{\mathfrak{D}_{-}}{\mathfrak{D}_{+}}|\mathfrak{r}|=1
for all
(\xi,k)\in I_{\mathcal{M}}\times(k_{0},k_{0}+\varepsilon)
, so that

\displaystyle E_{+}(k)

\displaystyle=m_{+}^{[E_{1},k_{0}]}(k)\,G^{-1}_{1,+}(k)\,N^{-1}(z(k))_{-}^{\frac{\sigma_{3}}{4}}

\displaystyle=m^{[E_{1},k_{0}]}_{-}(k)\,G^{-1}_{1,+}(k)\,N^{-1}\,i^{-\sigma_{3}}\,(z(k))_{+}^{\frac{\sigma_{3}}{4}}

\displaystyle=m_{-}^{[E_{1},k_{0}]}(k)\,G^{-1}_{2,-}(k)\,N^{-1}\,(z(k))_{+}^{\frac{\sigma_{3}}{4}}=E_{-}(k)

for all
(\xi,t,k)\in I_{\mathcal{M}}\times(0,\infty)\times(k_{0},k_{0}+\varepsilon)
.
Similarly,
E(\xi,t,\cdot)
has no jump across
(k_{0}-\varepsilon,k_{0})
because
\mathfrak{D}_{+}\mathfrak{D}_{-}\frac{\sqrt{\mathfrak{r}^{*}}}{\sqrt{\mathfrak{r}}}=1
for all
(\xi,k)\in I_{\mathcal{M}}\times(k_{0}-\varepsilon,k_{0})
, so that

\displaystyle E_{+}(k)

\displaystyle=m_{+}^{[E_{1},k_{0}]}(k)\,G^{-1}_{1,+}(k)\,N^{-1}\,z(k)^{\frac{\sigma_{3}}{4}}

\displaystyle=m^{[E_{1},k_{0}]}_{-}(k)\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}G^{-1}_{1,+}(k)\,N^{-1}z(k)^{\frac{\sigma_{3}}{4}}

\displaystyle=m^{[E_{1},k_{0}]}_{-}(k)\,G^{-1}_{2,-}(k)\,N^{-1}\,z(k)^{\frac{\sigma_{3}}{4}}=E_{-}(k)

for all

\displaystyle f_{0}(z)=-\pi\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{g_{0}^{(n)}(b)}{n!}(z-b)^{n-\frac{1}{2}}+\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}f_{0,n}(z-b)^{n}+o((z-b)^{N-\frac{1}{2}}),\qquad z\to b,

uniformly for
\arg(z-b)\in(-\pi,\pi)
, where the coefficients
f_{0,n}\in{\mathbb{C}}
are given by

(A.3)

\displaystyle f_{0,n}=\sum^{n}_{j=0}\frac{g_{0}^{(j)}(b)}{j!}h_{n-j}+C_{n},\qquad n=0,1,\dots,N-1,

with
C_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
h_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
defined by

(A.4)

C_{n}=\int^{b}_{a}\frac{g_{n+1}(s)}{\sqrt{b-s}}\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad h_{n}=\frac{2(-1)^{n}(b-a)^{-n-\frac{1}{2}}}{2n+1},\qquad n=0,1,\dots,N-1.

The functions
g_{n}\in\mathcal{C}^{N-n}([a,b],{\mathbb{C}})
in (
A.4
) are defined by

(A.5)

g_{n}(s)=\frac{g_{n-1}(s)-g_{n-1}(b)}{s-b}=\frac{g_{0}(s)-\sum^{n-1}_{j=0}\frac{g^{(j)}(b)}{j!}}{(s-b)^{n}},\qquad n=1,\dots,N.

Proof.

Define
h(z)
by

h(z)=\int_{a}^{b}\frac{1}{\sqrt{b-s}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s-z},\qquad z\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(-\infty,b].

For
s\in(a,b)
, we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\frac{\pi-2\arctan\left(\frac{\sqrt{z-b}}{\sqrt{b-s}}\right)}{\sqrt{z-b}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{b-s}(s-z)}.

Hence

h(z)=\frac{2\arctan\left(\frac{\sqrt{z-b}}{\sqrt{b-a}}\right)-\pi}{\sqrt{z-b}},\qquad z\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(-\infty,b].

Using that

\arctan z=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}z^{2k+1}}{2k+1},\qquad|z|<1,

we conclude that

(A.6)

\displaystyle h(z)\sim-\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{z-b}}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}h_{n}(z-b)^{n},\qquad z\to b,

where
\{h_{n}\}_{0}^{\infty}
are the coefficients defined in (
A.4
).

For
n=0,1,\dots,N
, let

\displaystyle f_{n}(z)\coloneqq\int_{a}^{b}\frac{g_{n}(s)}{\sqrt{b-s}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s-z}

with
\{g_{n}\}^{N}_{1}
being defined in (
A.5
).
Since

\frac{g_{n}(s)}{s-z}=\frac{g_{n+1}(s)}{s-z}(z-b)+\frac{g_{n}(b)}{s-z}+g_{n+1}(s),\qquad n=0,1,\dots,N-1,

we obtain

(A.7)

\displaystyle f_{n}(z)=f_{n+1}(z)(z-b)+g_{n}(b)h(z)+C_{n},\qquad n=0,1,\dots,N-1.

Recursive use of (
A.7
) gives

\displaystyle f_{0}(z)

\displaystyle=f_{1}(z)(z-b)+g_{0}(b)h(z)+C_{0}

\displaystyle=[f_{2}(z)(z-b)+g_{1}(b)h(z)+C_{1}](z-b)+g_{0}(b)h(z)+C_{0}

(A.8)

\displaystyle=\cdots=f_{N}(z)(z-b)^{N}+\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}(g_{n}(b)h(z)+C_{n})(z-b)^{n}.

Since
g_{N}
is Hölder continuous on
[a,b]
,
[
44
, Eq. (29.5)]
implies that

f_{N}(z)=-\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{z-b}}g_{N}(b)+o\big((z-b)^{-1/2}\big).

Hence, noting that

g_{n}(b)=\frac{g_{0}^{(n)}(b)}{n!},\qquad n=0,1,\dots,N,

the expansion (
A.2
) follows by expanding (
A.8
) as
z\to b
.
∎

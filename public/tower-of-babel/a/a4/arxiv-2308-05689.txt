Necessary and sufficient conditions for strong stability of explicit Runge–Kutta methods
Franz Achleitner, Anton Arnold, Ansgar Jüngel

Abstract
Strong stability is a property of time integration schemes for ODEs that preserve temporal monotonicity of solutions in arbitrary (inner product) norms. It is proved that explicit Runge–Kutta schemes of order p ∈ 4 ​ ℕ p\in 4{\mathbb{N}} with s = p s=p stages for linear autonomous ODE systems are not strongly stable, closing an open stability question from [Z. Sun and C.-W. Shu, SIAM J. Numer. Anal. 57 (2019), 1158–1182]. Furthermore, for explicit Runge–Kutta methods of order p ∈ ℕ p\in{\mathbb{N}} and s > p s>p stages, we prove several sufficient as well as necessary conditions for strong stability. These conditions involve both the stability function and the hypocoercivity index of the ODE system matrix. This index is a structural property combining the Hermitian and skew-Hermitian part of the system matrix.

Introduction

Explicit Runge–Kutta methods are standard tools in the numerical solution of ordinary differential equations and semi-discrete approximations of partial differential equations. Of particular interest are strongly stable schemes for which the norm of its numerical solution is nonincreasing in time, guaranteeing that the numerical error in each time step is not amplified during time integration. Surprisingly, the characterization of strongly stable explicit schemes is not complete. For instance, while it is well-known
SunShu19
that explicit Runge–Kutta methods for linear systems of order
p
are
not
strongly stable if
p\in 4{\mathbb{N}}_{0}+1
or
p\in 4{\mathbb{N}}_{0}+2
and that they are strongly stable for
p\in 4{\mathbb{N}}_{0}+3
, the case
p\in 4{\mathbb{N}}
is still open.

In this work, we show that the proof of strong stability can be reduced to testing strong stability with respect to (w.r.t.) the set of asymptotically stable, semi-dissipative matrices (see below for the definitions) and w.r.t. the set of purely imaginary scalars. We deduce that explicit Runge–Kutta methods for linear systems with order
p\in 4{\mathbb{N}}
and
p=s
stages are
not
strongly stable, thus filling the gap left open in
(
SunShu19
, Theorem 4.2)
. Furthermore, we prove that strong stability w.r.t. asymptotically stable, semi-dissipative matrices holds if the so-called hypocoercivity index of the system matrix is small enough.

We consider linear time-invariant systems of ordinary differential equations,

\frac{\,\operatorname{d}\!u}{\,\,\operatorname{d}\!t}=\mathbf{L}u,\quad t>0,\quad u(0)=u^{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n},

(1)

with Lyapunov stable matrices
\mathbf{L}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
; see
(
Be18

,
(
HadChe08
, p.172)
. In this paper, we use several notions of stability for matrices

•

\mathbf{L}
is
Lyapunov stable
if all eigenvalues have nonpositive real part and those eigenvalues with vanishing real part are non-defective (i.e., the algebraic and geometric multiplicities coincide)
(
Be18

•

\mathbf{L}

Dissipative matrices are asymptotically stable, and semi-dissipative matrices are Lyapunov stable. Lyapunov stability characterizes all system matrices
\mathbf{L}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
such that
u\equiv 0
is a Lyapunov stable solution to (
1

We use the following notation:
The conjugate transpose of a matrix
\mathbf{L}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}

Matrices

Positive definiteness (resp. semi-definiteness) of Hermitian matrices

It is known that

is Lyapunov stable if and only if there exists a positive definite Hermitian matrix
\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{H}_{n}^{>}

\mathbf{L}^{*}\mathbf{P}+\mathbf{P}\mathbf{L}\leq 0;

(2)

see, e.g.,
(

(

Under this condition, the solution

\frac{\,\operatorname{d}\!}{\,\,\operatorname{d}\!t}\|u(t)\|_{\mathbf{P}}^{2}=\frac{\,\operatorname{d}\!}{\,\,\operatorname{d}\!t}\langle u,\mathbf{P}u\rangle=\langle\mathbf{L}u,\mathbf{P}u\rangle+\langle u,\mathbf{P}\mathbf{L}u\rangle=\langle u,(\mathbf{L}^{*}\mathbf{P}+\mathbf{P}\mathbf{L})u\rangle\leq 0.

Stability of explicit Runge–Kutta schemes can be expected only under a restriction on the time step.

Let us summarize the main results of this paper:

•

An explicit Runge-Kutta scheme is strongly stable if and only if it is strongly stable w.r.t. the set
\mathcal{L}_{AS}
of asymptotically stable, semi-dissipative matrices and w.r.t. the set

of purely imaginary scalars, see Theorem

•

For this analysis, we derive novel necessary conditions for strong stability of explicit Runge–Kutta schemes using the precise short-time asymptotics for the spectral norm of the matrix exponential as derived in

•

Concerning strong stability w.r.t.

•

Main results

Before stating our main theorems, we recall that the Runge–Kutta scheme (
4
) for ODE systems (
1

u^{k}=R(\tau\mathbf{L})u^{k-1}
, where
R(z)

(
HaWa96

For linear time-invariant systems,
s=p

p

(
HaWa96
, § IV.2)

Generally, if an explicit Runge–Kutta method is of order
p

R(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{p}\frac{z^{j}}{j!}+\sum_{j=p+1}^{s}c_{j}\frac{z^{j}}{j!},\quad z\in{\mathbb{C}},\ c_{p+1}\neq 1,

(5)

with some constants
c_{p+1},\ldots,c_{s}\in{\mathbb{R}}

(
HaWa96

and
c_{j}=0
for
j\geq s+1

For an explicit Runge–Kutta scheme with
s=p

R(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{p}z^{j}/j!
; see, e.g.,
(
HaWa96

Our first main result shows that, to establish strong stability for an explicit Runge–Kutta scheme, it is (necessary and) sufficient to prove strong stability w.r.t. two distinct subsets of Lyapunov stable matrices.

Theorem 0.2.1

Consider an explicit Runge–Kutta method with stability function (
4
).
Then the Runge–Kutta scheme is strongly stable if and only if it is strongly stable w.r.t.

(a)

the set
\mathcal{L}_{AS}
of asymptotically stable, semi-dissipative matrices, and

(b)

the set
\mathcal{L}_{IM}
of purely imaginary scalars
\mathbf{L}\in{\rm i}{\mathbb{R}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
.

This means that the Runge–Kutta scheme is strongly stable if and only if for all linear systems (
1
) with
\mathbf{L}
either in
\mathcal{L}_{AS}
or
\mathcal{L}_{IM}
, for sufficiently small time steps, the numerical solution to (
1

\|u^{1}\|\leq\|u^{0}\|
for all initial data
u^{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}

The (strong) stability of Runge–Kutta schemes for scalar differential equations is well studied; see, e.g.,
KrSc92

In particular, strong stability w.r.t.
\mathcal{L}_{IM}
is equivalent to
local stability on the imaginary axis
(i.e., there exists

Therefore, we shall focus here on strong stability w.r.t. asymptotically stable, semi-dissipative matrices
\mathbf{L}\in\mathcal{L}_{AS}

We shall show that a Runge–Kutta method with

0.3
Proof of Theorem
0.2.1

The proof is based on a reduction of the matrix
\mathbf{L}
to block diagonal form. First, we recall some preparatory results and then detail the reduction strategy.

Preparations.

It is well-known that a similarity transformation of a Lyapunov stable matrix yields a semi-dissipative transformed matrix.

Lemma 1

Let
\mathbf{L}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
be Lyapunov stable. Then there exists
\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{H}_{n}^{>}
such that
\widehat{\mathbf{L}}:=\mathbf{P}^{1/2}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{P}^{-1/2}
is semi-dissipative and
\widehat{u}:=\mathbf{P}^{1/2}u
transforms (
1
) into the semi-dissipative ODE system
\mathrm{d}\widehat{u}/\mathrm{d}t=\widehat{\mathbf{L}}\widehat{u}
. Here,
\mathbf{P}^{-1/2}:=(\mathbf{P}^{-1})^{1/2}
.

Proof

Since
\mathbf{L}
is Lyapunov stable, there exists
\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{H}_{n}^{>}
such that
\mathbf{L}^{*}\mathbf{P}+\mathbf{P}\mathbf{L}\leq 0
. A congruence transformation with the Hermitian matrix
\mathbf{P}^{-1/2}
yields

0\geq\mathbf{P}^{-1/2}(\mathbf{L}^{*}\mathbf{P}+\mathbf{P}\mathbf{L})\mathbf{P}^{-1/2}=\mathbf{P}^{-1/2}\mathbf{L}^{*}\mathbf{P}^{1/2}+\mathbf{P}^{1/2}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{P}^{-1/2}=2(\mathbf{P}^{1/2}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{P}^{-1/2})_{H},

proving that
\widehat{\mathbf{L}}
is semi-dissipative. Finally, we multiply (
1
) from the left by
\mathbf{P}^{1/2}
to find that

\mathrm{d}\widehat{u}/\mathrm{d}t=\mathbf{P}^{1/2}\mathbf{L}u=(\mathbf{P}^{1/2}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{P}^{-1/2})\widehat{u}=\widehat{\mathbf{L}}\widehat{u}
.

\Box

We can characterize semi-dissipative matrices that are
not
asymptotically stable; see
(
MehMS16
, Lemma 3.1)
,

Reduction steps.

We prove the structural result in Theorem
0.2.1
by performing several reduction steps.

Step 1. Reduction to semi-dissipative matrices.

First, we show that strong stability of explicit Runge–Kutta schemes w.r.t. Lyapunov stable matrices
\mathbf{L}\in\mathcal{L}
is equivalent to strong stability w.r.t. semi-dissipative matrices
\widehat{\mathbf{L}}\in\mathcal{L}_{SD}
(as already stated in Remark
1
). To this end,
we consider a Lyapunov stable matrix
\mathbf{L}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
and
\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{H}_{n}^{>}
satisfying (
2
). By Lemma
1
,

\widehat{\mathbf{L}}:=\mathbf{P}^{1/2}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{P}^{-1/2}
is semi-dissipative. We define

\widehat{u}^{0}:=\mathbf{P}^{1/2}u^{0}
,
u^{1}:=R(\tau\mathbf{L})u^{0}
, and
\widehat{u}^{1}:=R(\tau\widehat{\mathbf{L}})\widehat{u}^{0}
. Then

\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbf{P}}=\|\mathbf{P}^{1/2}u^{0}\|=\|\widehat{u}^{0}\|
and

\displaystyle\|u^{1}\|_{\mathbf{P}}

\displaystyle=\|R(\tau\mathbf{L})u^{0}\|_{\mathbf{P}}=\|\mathbf{P}^{1/2}R(\tau\mathbf{L})u^{0}\|

\displaystyle=\|R(\tau\mathbf{P}^{1/2}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{P}^{-1/2})\mathbf{P}^{1/2}u^{0}\|=\|R(\tau\widehat{\mathbf{L}})\widehat{u}^{0}\|=\|\widehat{u}^{1}\|.

Hence,
\|u^{1}\|_{\mathbf{P}}\leq\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbf{P}}
if and only if
\|\widehat{u}^{1}\|\leq\|\widehat{u}^{0}\|
.

Step 2. Reduction to semi-dissipative matrices in block diagonal form.

Considering the unitary congruence transformation from Lemma
3
, we note that
\|\mathbf{V}u^{j}\|=\|u^{j}\|
, with the unitary matrix
\mathbf{V}
from (
10
).
Therefore, an explicit Runge–Kutta scheme is strongly stable if and only if it is strongly stable w.r.t. semi-dissipative matrices
\mathbf{L}
of the form (
10
).

Step 3. Reduction to two distinct subsets of semi-dissipative matrices.

Due to the block diagonal structure of
\mathbf{L}
in (
10
), an explicit Runge–Kutta scheme is strongly stable if and only if it is strongly stable w.r.t. two distinct subsets of semi-dissipative matrices, namely

(a)

asymptotically stable, semi-dissipative matrices
\mathbf{L}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
, and

(b)

skew-Hermitian matrices
\mathbf{L}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
(hence satisfying
\mathbf{L}+\mathbf{L}^{*}=0
).

Step 4. Reduction of skew-Hermitian matrices to (purely imaginary) scalar problems.

Skew-Hermitian matrices
\mathbf{L}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
are unitarily congruent to a diagonal matrix with purely imaginary eigenvalues, i.e., there exists a unitary matrix
\mathbf{U}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
such that
\mathbf{U}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{U}^{*}=\Lambda
with
\Lambda=\diag(\lambda_{1}^{\mathbf{L}},\ldots,\lambda_{n}^{\mathbf{L}})
, where
\lambda_{j}^{\mathbf{L}}\in{\rm i}{\mathbb{R}}
.
Therefore, in case (
b
), the analysis can be reduced to scalar ODEs of the form

\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}t}=\lambda u,\quad t\geq 0;\quad\lambda\in{\rm i}{\mathbb{R}}.

This finishes the proof of Theorem
0.2.1
.

satisfying
\|U_{j}\|_{2}\leq(2\|\mathbf{L}\|_{2})^{j}
for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.

First, we compute the stability function
R(\tau\mathbf{L})
in (
5
), setting
c_{k}=0
for
k>s
and
c_{k}=1
for
k\leq p
,

\displaystyle G(\tau)

\displaystyle=R(\tau\mathbf{L}^{*})R(\tau\mathbf{L})=\sum_{j=0}^{2s}\frac{1}{j!}\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{j}{k}c_{k}c_{j-k}(\tau\mathbf{L}^{*})^{k}(\tau\mathbf{L})^{j-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{2s}\frac{\tau^{j}}{j!}\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{j}{k}c_{k}c_{j-k}(\mathbf{L}^{*})^{k}\mathbf{L}^{j-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{p}\frac{\tau^{j}}{j!}U_{j}+\sum_{j=p+1}^{2s}\frac{\tau^{j}}{j!}\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{j}{k}c_{k}c_{j-k}(\mathbf{L}^{*})^{k}\mathbf{L}^{j-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{p}\frac{\tau^{j}}{j!}U_{j}+\frac{\tau^{p+1}}{(p+1)!}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\binom{p+1}{k}(\mathbf{L}^{*})^{k}\mathbf{L}^{p+1-k}

\displaystyle\phantom{xx}+c_{p+1}\mathbf{L}^{p+1}+c_{p+1}(\mathbf{L}^{*})^{p+1}\bigg)+\mathcal{O}(\tau^{p+2}).

We subtract

\displaystyle Q(\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{p}\frac{\tau^{j}}{j!}U_{j}+\frac{\tau^{p+1}}{(p+1)!}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\binom{p+1}{k}(\mathbf{L}^{*})^{k}\mathbf{L}^{p+1-k}

\displaystyle\phantom{xX}+\mathbf{L}^{p+1}+(\mathbf{L}^{*})^{p+1}\bigg)+\mathcal{O}(\tau^{p+2})

from
G(\tau)
and set
\widetilde{c}_{k}=c_{k}-1
to find that

G(\tau)-Q(\tau)=\frac{\tau^{p+1}}{(p+1)!}\widetilde{c}_{p+1}\big(\mathbf{L}^{p+1}+(\mathbf{L}^{*})^{p+1}\big)+\mathcal{O}(\tau^{p+2}).

(14)

Consequently, the Taylor expansions for
G(\tau)
and
Q(\tau)
differ starting from the
\tau^{p+1}
-term.
Now, consider an explicit Runge–Kutta scheme of order
p\in{\mathbb{N}}
with stability function
R(z)
, and matrices
\mathbf{L}\in\mathcal{L}_{AS}
with HC-index
{m_{HC}}
satisfying
2{m_{HC}}+1\leq p
.
Then, taking into account Proposition
2
, there exist
c
,
\epsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle\|R(\tau\mathbf{L})\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\|G(\tau)\|_{2}^{1/2}=\|Q(\tau)\|_{2}^{1/2}+\mathcal{O}(\tau^{p+1})

\displaystyle=\|e^{\tau\mathbf{L}}\|_{2}+\mathcal{O}(\tau^{p+1})=1-c\tau^{2{m_{HC}}+1}+\mathcal{O}(\tau^{2{m_{HC}}+2})

for all
\tau\in[0,\epsilon)
.
We conclude that
\|R(\tau\mathbf{L})\|_{2}\leq 1
for sufficiently small
\tau
, hence proving that all explicit Runge–Kutta schemes of order
p\in{\mathbb{N}}
are strongly stable w.r.t. matrices
\mathbf{L}\in\mathcal{L}_{AS}
with HC-index
{m_{HC}}
satisfying
2{m_{HC}}+1\leq p
.

where

The vector function

Since

{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\times[0,\infty)

\mathbf{L}

\displaystyle u_{0}^{*}\big(\mathbf{L}^{p+1}+(\mathbf{L}^{*})^{p+1}\big)u_{0}

\displaystyle=2(-1)^{{m_{HC}}}\ u_{0}^{*}(\mathbf{L}_{S}^{*})^{{m_{HC}}}\mathbf{L}_{H}(\mathbf{L}_{S})^{{m_{HC}}}u_{0}

\displaystyle=-2(-1)^{{m_{HC}}}\|(-\mathbf{L}_{H})^{1/2}\mathbf{L}_{S}^{{m_{HC}}}u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\neq 0,

(18)

where we used from (

\displaystyle G(\tau)u_{\tau}=\|u_{\tau}\|_{2}^{2}+g(u_{\tau};\tau)+u_{\tau}^{*}\big(G(\tau)-Q(\tau)\big)u_{\tau}

Proof of Theorem
0.2.4

As pointed out in
(
LeTa98
, §3.5)
, an explicit Runge–Kutta method of order
p=4
with
s=4
stages can be made strongly stable w.r.t.
\mathcal{L}_{AS}
when restricting to some adapted
\mathbf{P}
-norm.
Motivated by this observation, we prove Theorem
0.2.4
.

First, we observe that
for strong stability in weak form, the analogous statement as in Theorem
0.2.1
holds, i.e., an explicit Runge–Kutta method is strongly stable in weak form if and only if it is strongly stable in weak form w.r.t.
\mathcal{L}_{AS}
and
\mathcal{L}_{IM}
.

Second, let an asymptotically stable, semi-dissipative matrix
\mathbf{L}\in\mathcal{L}_{AS}
be given.
A matrix
\mathbf{L}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
is asymptotically stable if and only if there exists a matrix
\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{H}_{n}^{>}
such that

\mathbf{L}^{*}\mathbf{P}+\mathbf{P}\mathbf{L}<0,

(21)

see e.g.
(
Be18
, Corollary 15.10.1)
,
(
HadChe08
, Theorem 3.17)
.
Following the proof of Lemma
1
, we find that

\widehat{\mathbf{L}}:=\mathbf{P}^{1/2}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{P}^{-1/2}
is dissipative. Hence,
\widehat{\mathbf{L}}
has the HC-index
{m_{HC}}(\widehat{\mathbf{L}})=0
.
Consider the solution
(u^{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
in (
4
) in the weighted norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{P}}
with
\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{H}_{n}^{>}
satisfying (
21
). By Step 1 of the proof of Theorem
0.2.1
, strong stability in weak form for the Runge–Kutta scheme, i.e.
\|u^{1}\|_{\mathbf{P}}\leq\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbf{P}}
, is equivalent to

\|\widehat{u}^{1}\|\leq\|\widehat{u}^{0}\|,\quad\mbox{where }\widehat{u}^{1}=R(\tau\widehat{\mathbf{L}})\widehat{u}^{0}.

(22)

The inequality in (
22
) indeed holds due to Theorem
0.2.2
(a), since
\widehat{\mathbf{L}}
has the HC-index
{m_{HC}}=0
.
Thus,
\|u^{1}\|_{\mathbf{P}}\leq\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbf{P}}
holds without (further) constraints on
\mathbf{L}
.

Finally, consider strong stability w.r.t.
\mathcal{L}_{IM}
, the set of purely imaginary scalars
\lambda\in{\rm i}{\mathbb{R}}
.
Introducing a “weighted norm”
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{P}}
for some positive constant
\mathbf{P}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
does not change the stability criterion
\|u^{1}\|_{\mathbf{P}}\leq\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbf{P}}
on the considered explicit Runge–Kutta scheme.
This finishes the proof.

Example 3
(Continuation of Example
2
)

While the explicit Runge-Kutta scheme with
s=p=4
steps is
not
strongly stable (due to Corollary
1
), it is strongly stable in weak form (due to Theorem
0.2.4
, since it is strongly stable w.r.t.

Notice that the authors of

-\mathbf{L}=(\mathbf{W}^{*})^{-1}\mathbf{J}\mathbf{W}^{*}

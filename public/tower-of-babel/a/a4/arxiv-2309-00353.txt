Metrical properties of the product of partial quotients with geometric mean in continued fractions
Mumtaz Hussain, Bixuan Li, Nikita Shulga

Abstract
. The theory of uniform Diophantine approximation concerns the study of Dirichlet improvable numbers and the metrical aspect of this theory leads to the study of the product of consecutive partial quotients in continued fractions. It is known that the dimension of the set of Dirichlet non-improvable numbers depends upon the number of partial quotients in the product string. However, one can see that the Hausdorff dimension is the same for any number of consecutive partial quotients with a constant gap. This paper is aimed at a detailed analysis on how the Hausdorff dimension changes when there is a linear gap in indices and the number of partial quotients in the product grows. It is seen that the dimension is larger if d d is larger and t t has no contribution to the dimension.



1.
Introduction

It is well-known that every irrational number
x\in(0,1)
has a unique infinite continued fraction expansion. This expansion can be induced from the Gauss map
T:[0,1)\to[0,1)
defined by

T(0)=0,~T(x)=\frac{1}{x}-\left\lfloor\frac{1}{x}\right\rfloor\textmd{ for }x\in(0,1),

where
\lfloor x\rfloor
denotes the integer part of
x
. The continued fraction of
x
is denoted as
x:=[a_{1}(x),a_{2}(x),a_{3}(x),\ldots]
, with
a_{1}(x)=\lfloor 1/x\rfloor
and
a_{n}(x)=a_{1}(T^{n-1}(x))
for
n\geq 2
, referred to as the partial quotients of
x
.

The starting point of metric Diophantine approximation is Dirichlet theorem, that is the first non-trivial quantitative result. Landmark results of this field are Khintchine’s
[
12
]
and Jarník’s theorem with Lebesgue and Hausdorff measure. Both of them proved via continued fractions that is closely intertwined with the growth of the large partial quotients.

The classical Borel-Bernstein theorem (1912) states that the Lebesgue measure of the set

\mathcal{E}_{1}(\psi):=\left\{x\in[0,1):a_{n}(x)\geq\psi(n)\ {\rm for\ infinitely\ many}\ n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

is either zero or full depending upon the convergence or divergence of the series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi(n)^{-1}
respectively. Here and throughout
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to[1,\infty)
will be an arbitrary positive function. Taking
\psi(n)=n
, one can conclude that for almost all
x\in[0,1)
, with respect to Lebesgue measure,
a_{n}(x)\geq n
holds for infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This implies that the law of large numbers does not hold, that is

(1.1)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n<N}a_{n}(x)=\infty

for almost all
x
. Khintchine
[
13
]
showed that the weak law of large numbers holds for a suitable normalising sequence, that is,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(x)/n\log n
converges to
1/\log 2
with respect to Lebesgue measure. Philipp
[
21
]
proved that there is no reasonably regular function
\phi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(x)/\phi(n)
almost everywhere converges to a finite nonzero constant. However, Diamond and Vaaler
[
4
]
showed that the strong law of large numbers with the normalising sequence
n\log n
holds if the largest partial quotient
a_{k}(x)
is trimmed from the sum. Inspired by these conclusions, we are interested in studying the growth rate of partial quotients. Firstly, as a metric result, Wang and Wu
[
22
]
gave a Hausdorff dimension of the set
\mathcal{E}_{1}(\psi)
as follows.

Theorem 1.1

(Wang-Wu, 2008)
.

Let
\psi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{[}1,\infty)
. Suppose

(1.2)

\log B=\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\psi(n)}{n}\quad{and}\quad\log b=\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\log\psi(n)}{n}.

Then

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{E}_{1}(\psi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\inf\Big\{s:\mathsf{P}\left(-s\log\left|T^{\prime}\right|-s\log B\right)\leq 0\Big\}&{\rm if}\ \ 1\leq B<\infty;\\[12.91663pt]
\frac{1}{1+b}&{\rm if}\ \ B=\infty.\end{array}\right.

Here and throughout
T^{\prime}
denotes the derivative of the Gauss map
T
, and
\mathsf{P}
represents the pressure function defined in the Subsection
2.2
.

The product of two consecutive partial quotients also plays a significant role in Diophantine approximation. This was first pointed out by Kleinbock and Wadleigh in
[
15
]
, they showed that the set of Dirichlet non-improvable numbers can be written in terms of the growth of product of consecutive partial quotients. Moreover, they proved the Lebesgue measure of set

\mathcal{E}_{2}(\psi):=\left\{x\in[0,1):a_{n}(x)a_{n+1}(x)\geq\psi(n)\ {\rm for\ infinitely\ many}\ n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

is either full or null depending upon the divergence or convergence of the series
\sum_{n}\frac{-\log(1-n\psi(n))(1-n\psi(n))}{n}
respectively. The divergence of (
1.1
) induces the divergence of average of product of partial quotients, that is

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n<N}a_{n}(x)a_{n+1}(x)=\infty

for almost all
x
. Hu, Hussain and Yu
[
7
]
established the weak and strong laws of large numbers with a suitable sequence
n\log^{2}n
for the sums of product of partial quotients. In particular, they
showed that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(x)a_{i+1}(x)/n\log^{2}n
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(x)a_{i+1}(x)-\max_{1\leq i\leq n}a_{i}(x)a_{i+1}(x)/n\log^{2}n
converges to
1/(2\log 2)
with respect to Lebesgue measure.
A result of Aaronson
[
1
]
states that there is no normalisation
b_{n}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(x)a_{i+1}(x)/b_{n}
converges to a nonzero number for almost all
x
. These inspire us to study the growth rate of
a_{n}(x)a_{n+1}(x)
. With respect to the metric results of product of partial quotients, Hussain, Kleinbock, Wadleigh, and Wang
[
9
]
studied the Hausdorff measure of the set
\mathcal{E}_{2}(\psi)
, see also
[
3
,
14
]
. Huang, Wu and Xu
[
8
]
calculated the Lebesgue measure and Hausdorff dimension for a natural generalisation of the set
\mathcal{E}_{2}(\psi)
. Namely, for any
m\geq 1
, they considered

\mathcal{E}_{m}(\psi):=\left\{x\in[0,1):a_{n+1}(x)a_{n+2}(x)\cdots a_{n+m}(x)\geq\psi(n)\ {\rm for\ infinitely\ many}\ n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

and proved the following result.

Theorem 1.2

(Huang-Wu-Xu, 2020)
.

Let
\psi
be a positive function and
B
,
b
are defined as in (
1.2
). Then

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{E}_{m}(\psi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\inf\{s:\mathsf{P}(T,-f_{m}(s)\log B-s\log|T^{\prime}|)\leq 0\}&{\rm if}\ \ 1\leq B<\infty;\\[12.91663pt]
\frac{1}{1+b}&{\rm if}\ \ B=\infty,\end{array}\right.

where
f_{m}(s)
is given by the following iterative formula:

f_{1}(s)=s,\,\,\,\,\,f_{k+1}(s)=\frac{sf_{k}(s)}{1-s+f_{k}(s)}\text{ for }k\geq 1.

The main idea of estimating the lower bound of the dimension of this set is constructing a suitable subset by analysing the potential coverings. Then, defining a probability measure and using mass distribution principle to estimate the lower bound is a standard process. The specific value of partial quotients in subset grows with an exponential rate. It is worth noting that, the indices of the block
a_{n+1}(x)a_{n+2}(x)\cdots a_{n+m}(x)
has a constant gap among them. If we set the gap with any other constants, the partial quotients with indices in the gap does not make any contribution for the Hausdorff dimension through the analysis of potential coverings. More precisely, for any constant
c>0
and
m\geq 1
, the Hausdorff dimension of the set

\mathcal{E}_{mc}(\psi):=\left\{x\in[0,1):a_{n+c}(x)a_{n+2c}(x)\cdots a_{n+mc}(x)\geq\psi(n)\ {\rm for\ infinitely\ many}\ n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

is equal to the Hausdorff dimension of the set
\mathcal{E}_{m}(\psi)
, since
a_{n+ic+1},\ldots,a_{n+(i+1)c-1}
for any
1\leq i\leq m-1
in the Cantor subset construction is constant. More generally, let
\{c_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{m}
be any strictly increasing sequence of positive integers, the Cantor subset of the set

\mathcal{E}_{c_{m}}(\psi):=\left\{x\in[0,1):a_{n+c_{1}}(x)a_{n+c_{2}}(x)\cdots a_{n+c_{m}}(x)\geq\psi(n)\ {\rm for\ infinitely\ many}\ n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

is similar to the Cantor subset of
\mathcal{E}_{mc}
, so that the Hausdorff dimension for both of these sets is the same. Some results concerning the metrical theory of
\mathcal{E}_{m}(\psi)
can be found in
[
2
,
3
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
15
]
.

Thus, there are two natural problems: what kind of gap between the partial quotients can affect the dimension, and whether the dimension changes or not if the number of partial quotients in the product string is related to
n
. Integrating these two problems together gives rise to the following set. Throughout the paper, our main focus is on a general linear gap in the indices. Let
f(n):=dn+t
be a linear function, where
d\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
and
t\in\mathbb{N}
. Define the set

E_{f}(\psi):=\left\{x\in[0,1):\sqrt[n]{a_{f(n)}(x)a_{2f(n)}(x)\cdots a_{nf(n)}(x)}\geq\psi(n)\ {\rm for\ infinitely\ many}\ n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

The main result of the paper is as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\psi
be a positive function defined as

(1.3)

\log B=\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\psi\left(n\right)}{dn}\quad\text{ and }\quad\log b=\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\log\psi\left(n\right)}{dn^{2}}.

Then

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}E_{f}(\psi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\inf\Big\{s:\mathsf{P}\left(-s\log\left|T^{\prime}\right|-\left(2s-1\right)\log B\right)\leq 0\Big\}&{\rm if}\ \ 1\leq B<\infty;\\[12.91663pt]
\frac{1}{1+b}&{\rm if}\ \ B=\infty.\end{array}\right.

The main part of the proof of the above theorem will be focused on the Hausdorff dimensional of the set

E_{B}:=\left\{x\in[0,1):a_{f(n)}(x)a_{2f(n)}(x)\cdots a_{nf(n)}(x)\geq B^{dn^{2}}\ {\rm for\ infinitely\ many}\ n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

We prove the following result.

Theorem 1.4
.

For any
1\leq B<\infty
, we have

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}E_{B}=\inf\Big\{s:\mathsf{P}\left(-s\log\left|T^{\prime}\right|-\left(2s-1\right)\log B\right)\leq 0\Big\}.

Remark 1.5
.

Note that if we denote
d\log B=\log\tilde{B}
, then
d
appears in the pressure function. More precisely, the potential function then would be
-s\log\left|T^{\prime}\right|-d^{-1}\left(2s-1\right)\log\tilde{B}
. It can be seen that the dimension is larger if
d
is larger. In other words, a linear gap in indices of
n
partial quotients affects the dimension. As we analyzed above,
t
which is a constant gap does not make any contributions for the Hausdorff dimension. Substituting
d\log b=\log\tilde{b}
, dimension result is transformed into
(1+\tilde{b}^{d^{-1}})^{-1}
. The conclusion for a larger dimension with a larger linear gap is clearer.

1.1.
Modus Operandi

The most challenging part of this proof is how to find a suitable subset of
E_{B}
for the case
1\leq B<\infty
. For all other cases, the proofs are relatively easier. In proving the lower bound of Theorem
1.4
, we use the classical method, that is

•

define a good subset of
E_{B}
;

•

define a probability measure supported on a suitable subset (Cantor subset) of
E_{f}(\psi)
;

•

Calculate the Hölder exponent for the probability measure;

•

Use the mass distribution principle to calculate the lower bound of the Hausdorff dimension.

Now regarding the second question on whether the dimension changes if the number of partial quotients in the product string depends on the index ‘
n
’, when dealing with a growing number of product of partial quotients as in the set
E_{B}
, compared with the fixed length of partial quotients as in the sets
\mathcal{E}_{m}(\psi)
,
\mathcal{E}_{mc}(\psi)
, or
\mathcal{E}_{c_{m}}(\psi)
, the structure of the set
E_{B}
is more complicated with infinite number of variations. More precisely, partial quotients in Cantor subset of
[
8
]
are of exponential growth, but not in
E_{f}(\psi)
. The partial quotients
a_{f(n)}
shows an approximate exponential growth when
n
is large enough. So that, it is imperative to use this approximate property to define a probability measure supported on a Cantor subset. Then we use the method of mass distribution principle to obtain the lower bound. More details can be seen in Section 4.

Remark 1.6
.

It is worth remarking on the well-known Khintchine’s constant (1934) that states that the
\lim_{n\to\infty}(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})^{\frac{1}{n}}\to 2.685452001\ldots
, independent of
x
, except on a set of Lebesgue measure zero. It is not known yet that whether this constant is irrational or not. This raises a natural, but fundamental question whether the geometric mean of the mixed partial quotients
\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{if(n)}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}
converges or not, if it does, what is its limit?

The paper is organised as follows. In Section 2, we introduce some basic properties of continued fractions and some basic facts that we will used later. In Section 3, we will give a sketch of idea of construction of Cantor subset. In Section 4 and 5, we give the proof of Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
to analyze Hausdorff dimension.

\mathcal{H}^{s}
denotes the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure,

a\ll b
and

to respectively mean that

means

for unspecified constants
e_{1},e_{2}
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we will introduce some concepts and properties of continued fractions and pressure function.

2.1.
Continued fractions

Recall that the
n
th convergents of
x
are defined as

\displaystyle\frac{p_{n}\left(x\right)}{q_{n}\left(x\right)}=\left[a_{1}\left(x\right),\ldots,a_{n}\left(x\right)\right].

For simplicity, we write
p_{n}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)=p_{n}
,
q_{n}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)=q_{n}
when there is no ambiguity. The rule for constructing these sequences are as follows, for any
k\geq 2
we have

(2.1)

\begin{split}p_{k}&=a_{k}p_{k-1}+p_{k-2},\\
q_{k}&=a_{k}q_{k-1}+q_{k-2},\end{split}

where
p_{0}=0
,
q_{0}=1
.

For any
\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)
where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq i\leq n
, define
a cylinder

I_{n}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)

of order

n
as

\displaystyle I_{n}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right):=\left\{x\in\left[0,1\right):a_{1}\left(x\right)=a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\left(x\right)=a_{n}\right\}.

Then, we have

Proposition 2.1

(
[
12
]
)
.

For any
n\geq 1
and
\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, with
p_{k},q_{k}
being defined recursively by
\left(\ref{rule}\right)
where
0\leq k\leq n
, we have

(2.2)

I_{n}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left[\dfrac{p_{n}}{q_{n}},\dfrac{p_{n}+p_{n-1}}{q_{n}+q_{n-1}}\right)&\textrm{\rm if }\ \ n\textrm{ is even},\\[8.61108pt]
\left(\dfrac{p_{n}+p_{n-1}}{q_{n}+q_{n-1}},\dfrac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right]&\textrm{\rm if }\ \ n\textrm{ is odd}.\\
\end{array}\right.

The length of the
n
th cylinder is given by

(2.3)

\left|I_{n}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\right|=\dfrac{1}{q_{n}\left(q_{n}+q_{n-1}\right)}.

For a better estimation, we need the following results. For any
n\geq 1
and
\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, we have

Lemma 2.2

(
[
12
]
)
.

For any
1\leq k\leq n
, we have

(2.4)

\dfrac{a_{k}+1}{2}\leq\dfrac{q_{n}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)}{q_{n-1}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k-1},a_{k+1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)}\leq a_{k}+1.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
12
]
)
.

For any
k\geq 1
, we have

(2.5)

\begin{split}q_{n+k}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},a_{n+1},\ldots,a_{n+k}\right)&\geq q_{n}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\cdot q_{k}\left(a_{n+1},\ldots,a_{n+k}\right),\\
q_{n+k}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},a_{n+1},\ldots,a_{n+k}\right)&\leq 2q_{n}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\cdot q_{k}\left(a_{n+1},\ldots,a_{n+k}\right).\end{split}

For any
n\geq 1
, since
p_{n-1}q_{n}-p_{n}q_{n-1}=\left(-1\right)^{n}
, combined with simple calculation, then

(2.6)

q_{n}\geq 2^{\left(n-1\right)/2}.

Next, we introduce the mass distribution principle which is useful in calculating the lower bound of Hausdorff dimension.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
5
]
)
.

Let
E\subseteq\left[0,1\right)
be a Borel set and
\mu
be a measure with
\mu\left(E\right)>0
, suppose that for some
s>0
, there is a constant
c>0
such that for any
x\in\left[0,1\right)
one has

(2.7)

\mu\left(B\left(x,r\right)\right)\leq cr^{s},

where
B\left(x,r\right)
denotes an open ball centred at

and radius

, then

.

2.2.
Pressure Function

[
6
]
, Mauldin and Urbański
[

]

Consider a sub-system

where

denotes the ergodic sum
\phi(x)+\cdots+\phi(T^{n-1}x)
. For simplicity, when
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{N}
, we denote
\mathsf{P}(T,\phi)
by
\mathsf{P}_{\mathbb{N}}(T,\phi)
. We will use the notation of
n
th variation of
\phi
to explain the existence of limit (
2.8

\Var_{n}(\phi):=\sup\Big\{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|:I_{n}(x)=I_{y}(y)\Big\}.

In

16
]

Proposition 2.5

(
[
16
]
)

Let

\displaystyle\phi(x)=-s\log|T^{\prime}(x)|-(2s-1)\log B.

Next, denote

It is clear that

For any

.

3.
A suitable subset of
E_{B}

In this section, we mainly focus on how to construct a reasonable good subset of
E_{B}
that would act as a Cantor subset. We will prove some lemmas that we will use later. Recall that

E_{B}:=\left\{x\in[0,1):a_{f(n)}(x)a_{2f(n)}(x)\cdots a_{nf(n)}(x)\geq B^{dn^{2}}\ {\rm for\ infinitely\ many}\ n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

For any
n\geq 2
, let
\{A_{i}(n)\}_{1\leq i\leq n-1}
be a sequence of real numbers such that
1\leq A_{i}(n)\leq B^{dn}
for any
1\leq i\leq n-1
, and it will be optimized later. For simplicity, we write

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}(n)

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}(n)

\displaystyle=A_{i}(n)\alpha_{i-1}(n)\ \ \text{for any}\ 1\leq i\leq n-1,

\displaystyle w_{i}

\displaystyle:=(a_{if(n)+1},\ldots,a_{(i+1)f(n)-1})\ \ \text{for any}\ 0\leq i\leq n-1.

Define a set,

\displaystyle F(A):=\Big\{x\in\left[0,1\right):

\displaystyle A_{i}(n)^{n}\leq a_{if(n)}(x)\leq 2A_{i}(n)^{n},1\leq i\leq n-1;

\displaystyle\phantom{=\;\;}\left(\frac{B^{dn}}{A_{1}(n)\cdots A_{n-1}(n)}\right)^{n}\leq a_{nf(n)}(x)\leq 2\left(\frac{B^{dn}}{A_{1}(n)\cdots A_{n-1}(n)}\right)^{n}\textrm{ for i.m. }n\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}.

There is a natural cover of it, for each
n\geq 1
, let

\displaystyle\quad F_{n}:=\Big\{x:A_{i}(n)^{n}\leq a_{if(n)}(x)\leq 2A_{i}(n)^{n},1\leq i\leq n-1;

\displaystyle\phantom{=\;\;}\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\left(\frac{B^{dn}}{A_{1}(n)\cdots A_{n-1}(n)}\right)^{n}\leq a_{nf(n)}(x)\leq 2\left(\frac{B^{dn}}{A_{1}(n)\cdots A_{n-1}(n)}\right)^{n}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{if(n)+1},\ldots,a_{(i+1)f(n)-1}\in\mathbb{N},\\
0\leq i\leq n-1\end{subarray}}\Big\{x:a_{if(n)+\ell}(x)=a_{if(n)+\ell},0\leq i\leq n-1,1\leq\ell\leq f(n)-1;

\displaystyle\phantom{=\;\;}\quad A_{i}(n)^{n}\leq a_{if(n)}(x)\leq 2A_{i}(n)^{n},1\leq i\leq n-1;\left(\frac{B^{dn}}{A_{1}(n)\cdots A_{n-1}(n)}\right)^{n}\leq a_{nf(n)}(x)\leq 2\left(\frac{B^{dn}}{A_{1}(n)\cdots A_{n-1}(n)}\right)^{n}\Big\}

\displaystyle:=\bigcup_{w_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{f(n)-1},0\leq i\leq n-1}F_{n}(w_{0},w_{1},\ldots,w_{n-1}).

Subsequently, we find the most efficient cover of the set
F_{n}
in a way that the sequence
\{A_{i}(n)\}_{1\leq i\leq n-1}
is optimal, and that determines an effective cover of
F(A)
. More precisely,

\displaystyle F(A)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{N\geq 1}\bigcup_{n\geq N}F_{n}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{N\geq 1}\bigcup_{n\geq N}\bigcup_{w_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{f(n)-1},0\leq i\leq n-1}F_{n}(w_{0},w_{1},\ldots,w_{n-1}).

Fix
n\geq 1
, there are
n
potential covers for
F_{n}
.

•

Cover type
i
(
1\leq i\leq n-1
). For any integers
a_{jf(n)+1},\ldots,a_{(j+1)f(n)-1}\in\mathbb{N}
with
0\leq j\leq i-1
, define

J_{if(n)-1}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{if(n)-1})=\bigcup_{A_{i}(n)^{n}\leq a_{if(n)}\leq 2A_{i}(n)^{n}}I_{if(n)}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{if(n)}).

The length of this interval is

\displaystyle\left|J_{if(n)-1}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{if(n)-1})\right|=\sum_{A_{i}(n)^{n}\leq a_{if(n)}\leq 2A_{i}(n)^{n}}\left|\frac{p_{if(n)}}{q_{if(n)}}-\frac{p_{if(n)}+p_{if(n)-1}}{q_{if(n)}+q_{if(n)-1}}\right|\asymp\frac{1}{q_{if(n)-1}^{2}A_{i}(n)^{n}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{jf(n)+1},\ldots,a_{(j+1)f(n)-1}\in\mathbb{N},\\
0\leq j\leq i-1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{q_{if(n)-1}^{2}A_{i}(n)^{n}}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle\asymp\left(\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{f(n)-1}\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{q_{f(n)-1}^{2s}}\right)^{i}(A_{1}(n)\cdots A_{i-1}(n))^{(1-2s)n}A_{i}(n)^{-sn}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{f(n)-1}\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{q_{f(n)-1}^{2s}}\right)^{i}\alpha_{i-1}(n)^{(1-s)n}\alpha_{i}(n)^{-sn}.

•

Cover type
n
. For any integers
a_{jf(n)+1},\ldots,a_{(j+1)f(n)-1}\in\mathbb{N}
with
0\leq j\leq n-1
, define

J_{nf(n)-1}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{nf(n)-1})=\bigcup_{\left(\frac{B^{dn}}{\alpha_{n-1}(n)}\right)^{n}\leq a_{nf(n)}\leq 2\left(\frac{B^{dn}}{\alpha_{n-1}(n)}\right)^{n}}I_{nf(n)}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{nf(n)}).

The length of this interval is

\displaystyle\left|J_{nf(n)-1}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{nf(n)-1})\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left(\frac{B^{dn}}{\alpha_{n-1}(n)}\right)^{n}\leq a_{nf(n)}\leq 2\left(\frac{B^{dn}}{\alpha_{n-1}(n)}\right)^{n}}\left|\frac{p_{nf(n)}}{q_{nf(n)}}-\frac{p_{nf(n)}+p_{nf(n)-1}}{q_{nf(n)}+q_{nf(n)-1}}\right|

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{\alpha_{n-1}(n)^{n}}{q_{nf(n)-1}^{2}B^{dn^{2}}}.

With the similar estimates as above, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{jf(n)+1},\ldots,a_{(j+1)f(n)-1}\in\mathbb{N},\\
0\leq j\leq n-1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{\alpha_{n-2}(n)^{n}}{q_{nf(n)-1}^{2}B^{dn^{2}}}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle\asymp\left(\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{f(n)-1}\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{q_{f(n)-1}^{2s}}\right)^{n}(A_{1}(n)\cdots A_{n-1}(n))^{(1-2s)n}\frac{\alpha_{n-1}(n)^{sn}}{B^{sdn^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{f(n)-1}\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{q_{f(n)-1}^{2s}}\right)^{n}\alpha_{n-1}(n)^{(1-s)n}B^{-sdn^{2}}.

From the above, for any
n\geq 1
, the most effective cover relies on the minimum of the quantities

\min\Big\{\alpha_{1}(n)^{-s},(\alpha_{1}(n)^{(1-s)}\alpha_{2}(n)^{-s})^{\frac{1}{2}},\ldots,(\alpha_{n-2}(n)^{(1-s)}\alpha_{n-1}(n)^{-s})^{\frac{1}{n-1}},(\alpha_{n-1}(n)^{(1-s)}B^{-sdn})^{\frac{1}{n}}\Big\}.

Given any
\{A_{i}(n)\}_{i\geq 1,n\geq 1}
such that
1\leq A_{i}(n)\leq B^{dn}
, the quantities
\{\alpha_{i}(n)\}_{i\geq 1,n\geq 1}
can be determined accordingly so that

is a subset of

. Then the dimension of

is larger than a value which is determined by
\{\alpha_{i}(n)\}_{i\geq 1,n\geq 1}
. This leads us to maximize the sequence

to find an optimal cover for each
n\geq 1
. More precisely, we have the following.

Proposition 3.1
.

With notation as above, given any
n\geq 2
, the following supremum

(3.1)

\sup_{1\leq\alpha_{1}(n),\ldots,\alpha_{n-1}(n)\leq B^{dn}}\min\Big\{\alpha_{1}(n)^{-s},(\alpha_{1}(n)^{(1-s)}\alpha_{2}(n)^{-s})^{\frac{1}{2}},\ldots,(\alpha_{n-2}(n)^{(1-s)}\alpha_{n-1}(n)^{-s})^{\frac{1}{n-1}},(\alpha_{n-1}(n)^{(1-s)}B^{-sdn})^{\frac{1}{n}}\Big\}

is attained when all the terms are equal.

Proof of Proposition

We first look at the case when there are only two terms, then the supremum can be expressed as follows. Given

\displaystyle\sup_{1\leq\alpha_{1}(n)\leq B^{dn}}\min\left\{\alpha_{1}(n)^{-s},(\alpha_{1}(n)^{(1-s)}\alpha_{2}(n)^{-s})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\}.

As a function of

\displaystyle\sup_{1\leq\alpha_{1}(n),\ldots,\alpha_{n-1}(n)\leq B^{dn}}\min\Big\{\alpha_{1}(n)^{-s},\ldots,(\alpha_{n-2}(n)^{(1-s)}\alpha_{n-1}(n)^{-s})^{\frac{1}{n-1}},(\alpha_{n-1}(n)^{1-s}B^{-sdn})^{\frac{1}{n}}\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{1\leq\alpha_{n-1}(n)\leq B^{dn}}\sup_{1\leq\alpha_{1}(n),\ldots,\alpha_{n-2}(n)\leq B^{dn}}\min\Big\{\min\Big\{\alpha_{1}(n)^{-s},\ldots,(\alpha_{n-2}(n)^{(1-s)}\alpha_{n-1}(n)^{-s})^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\Big\},(\alpha_{n-1}(n)^{1-s}B^{-sdn})^{\frac{1}{n}}\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{1\leq\alpha_{n-1}(n)\leq B^{dn}}\min\Big\{\sup_{1\leq\alpha_{1}(n),\ldots,\alpha_{n-2}(n)\leq B^{dn}}\min\Big\{\alpha_{1}(n)^{-s},\ldots,(\alpha_{n-2}(n)^{(1-s)}\alpha_{n-1}(n)^{-s})^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\Big\},(\alpha_{n-1}(n)^{1-s}B^{-sdn})^{\frac{1}{n}}\Big\}.

By induction, the supremum

\sup_{1\leq\alpha_{1}(n),\ldots,\alpha_{n-2}(n)\leq B^{dn}}\min\Big\{\alpha_{1}(n)^{-s},\ldots,(\alpha_{n-2}(n)^{(1-s)}\alpha_{n-1}(n)^{-s})^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\Big\}

4.3.
Hölder exponent of the measure on fundamental intervals

Following these, we estimate the mass on fundamental intervals that will frequently use properties (

•

When

for some
1\leq i<r_{1}
, by the estimation of length of
J_{n}
in (
4.6
), we have

(4.8)

\begin{split}\mu(J_{if(N)})&\leq\prod_{t=1}^{i}\frac{1}{q_{f(N)}^{2s}(w_{t}^{(1,0)})B^{(2s-1)dN}}\leq 2^{2\left(i-1\right)}\cdot\frac{1}{q_{if(N)}^{2s}}\\
&\leq\left(\frac{1}{q_{if(N)}^{2}}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{f(N)}}\leq 6|J_{if(N)}|^{s-\frac{2}{f(N)}},\end{split}

where the third inequality holds by following

q_{if(N)}^{2}\geq 2^{if(N)-1}\Longrightarrow q_{if(N)}^{2\cdot\frac{2}{f(N)}}\geq 2^{2(i-1)}.

When

\begin{split}\mu(J_{f(n_{1})-1})&\leq\prod_{t=1}^{r_{1}}\frac{1}{q_{f(N)}^{2s}(w_{t}^{(1,0)})B^{(2s-1)dN}}\leq 2^{2\left(r_{1}-1\right)}\cdot\frac{1}{q_{f(n_{1})-1}^{2s}B^{(2s-1)r_{1}dN}}\\
&\leq\left(\frac{1}{q_{f(n_{1})-1}^{2}}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{f(N)}}\cdot\frac{1}{B^{(2s-1)r_{1}dN}}.\end{split}

Recall inequalities (
4.4
) and (
4.1
) and note that when
N
is large enough it implies that
n_{1}
is also large. So, we have

|J_{f(n_{1})-1}|^{s}\geq\frac{1}{2^{5}A_{1}(n_{1})^{sn_{1}}q_{f(n_{1})-1}^{2s}}\geq\frac{1}{2^{5}B^{dn_{1}(2s-1+s\varepsilon)}q_{f(n_{1})-1}^{2s}}.

Thus,

(4.9)

\begin{split}\mu(J_{f(n_{1})-1})&\leq\left(\frac{1}{q_{f(n_{1})-1}^{2}}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{f(N)}}\cdot\frac{1}{B^{(2s-1)(n_{1}d-r_{1}t-1)}}\\
&\leq 2^{5}B^{sn_{1}d\varepsilon+(2s-1)(r_{1}t+1)}\cdot|J_{f(n_{1})-1}|^{s-\frac{2}{f(N)}}\\
&\ll 2^{5}|J_{f(n_{1})-1}|^{s-\frac{2}{f(N)}-\frac{6(t+1)}{f(N)}\cdot\frac{\log B}{\log 2}}.\end{split}

The last inequality holds since, when
N

B^{sn_{1}d\varepsilon+2(2s-1)r_{1}t}\leq B^{n_{1}d\frac{1}{f(N)}+2\frac{f(n_{1})-1}{f(N)}t}=B^{\frac{(2t+1)dn_{1}+2t^{2}-2t}{f(N)}}\leq 2^{\frac{\log B}{\log 2}\cdot\frac{3(t+1)dn_{1}}{f(N)}}\textrm{ \ and \ }q_{f(n_{1})-1}^{2}\geq 2^{\frac{dn_{1}}{2}}.

for some

\displaystyle\mu(J_{jf(n_{1})})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{A_{j}(n_{1})^{n_{1}}}\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\frac{1}{A_{i}(n_{1})^{n_{1}}}\cdot\prod_{t=1}^{r_{1}}\frac{1}{q_{f(N)}^{2s}(w_{t}^{(1,i)})B^{(2s-1)dN}}\cdot\mu(J_{f(n_{1})-1})

is as follows,

\displaystyle|J_{jf(n_{1})}|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{6q_{jf(n_{1})}^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{6\cdot 2^{6j-2}(A_{1}(n_{1})\cdots A_{j}(n_{1}))^{2n_{1}}}\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}\left(\frac{1}{q_{f(n_{1})-1}^{2}(w_{1}^{(1,i)},\ldots,w_{r_{1}}^{(1,i)})}\right).

Furthermore, we will use the following inequalities. For any

(4.10)

\begin{split}q_{f(n_{\ell})-1}^{2}\geq 2^{f(n_{\ell})-2}\geq 2^{\frac{f(N)}{2}}\quad&\Longrightarrow\quad\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}q_{f(n_{\ell})-1}^{2}(w_{1}^{(\ell,i)},\ldots,w_{r_{1}}^{(\ell,i)})\geq 2^{\frac{jf(N)}{2}}\\
&\Longrightarrow\quad\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}q_{f(n_{\ell})-1}^{2\cdot\frac{12}{f(N)}}(w_{1}^{(\ell,i)},\ldots,w_{r_{1}}^{(\ell,i)})\geq 2^{6j},\end{split}

and

(4.11)

\begin{split}q_{f(n_{\ell})-1}^{2}\geq 2^{dn_{\ell}+t-2}\quad&\Longrightarrow\quad\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}q_{f(n_{\ell})-1}^{2\cdot\frac{1}{f(N)}}(w_{1}^{(\ell,i)},\ldots,w_{r_{1}}^{(\ell,i)})\geq 2^{\frac{dn_{\ell}j}{2f(N)}}\\

Thus, combined with (
3.2
) and (
4.1
), we have

(4.12)

\begin{split}\mu(J_{jf(n_{1})})&\leq\frac{A_{1}(n_{1})^{sjn_{1}}}{A_{j}(n_{1})^{(1-s)n_{1}}(A_{1}(n_{1})\cdots A_{j}(n_{1}))^{2sn_{1}}}\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}\left(\frac{1}{q_{f(n_{1})-1}^{2}(w_{1}^{(1,i)},\ldots,w_{r_{1}}^{(1,i)})}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{f(N)}}\frac{1}{B^{(2s-1)jdr_{1}N}}\\

&\leq 6\cdot 2^{6j-2}B^{jdn_{1}(2s-1+s\varepsilon)-(2s-1)j(dn_{1}+t-1-r_{1}t)}|J_{jf(n_{1})}|^{s-\frac{2}{f(N)}}\\
&\leq 6\cdot 2^{6j-2}2^{\frac{\log B}{\log 2}\frac{3(t+1)dn_{1}j}{f(N)}}|J_{jf(n_{1})}|^{s-\frac{2}{f(N)}}\\
&\leq 6|J_{jf(n_{1})}|^{s-\frac{15}{f(N)}-\frac{\log B}{\log 2}\cdot\frac{6(t+1)}{f(N)}}.\end{split}

\begin{split}\mu(J_{jf(n_{1})-1})&\leq\frac{1}{(A_{1}(n_{1})\cdots A_{j-1}(n_{1}))^{n_{1}}}\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}\left(\frac{1}{q_{f(n_{1})-1}^{2}(w_{1}^{(1,i)},\ldots,w_{r_{1}}^{(1,i)})}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{f(N)}}\frac{1}{B^{(2s-1)jdr_{1}N}}\\
&\leq\frac{A_{1}(n_{1})^{s(j-1)n_{1}}}{A_{j-1}(n_{1})^{(1-s)n_{1}}(A_{1}(n_{1})\cdots A_{j-1}(n_{1}))^{2sn_{1}}}\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\cdot\frac{1}{B^{(2s-1)j(dn_{1}+t-1-r_{1}t)}}\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}\left(\frac{1}{q_{f(n_{1})-1}^{2}(w_{1}^{(1,i)},\ldots,w_{r_{1}}^{(1,i)})}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{f(N)}}\\
&\leq 2^{6j-1}\frac{A_{1}(n_{1})^{s(j-1)n_{1}}A_{j}(n_{1})^{sn_{1}}B^{j(r_{1}t-t+1)}}{A_{j-1}(n_{1})^{(1-s)n_{1}}B^{(2s-1)jdn_{1}}}|J_{jf(n_{1})-1}|^{s-\frac{2}{f(N)}}\\
&\leq 2^{6j-1}\frac{A_{1}(n_{1})^{sjn_{1}}B^{j(r_{1}t-t+1)}}{B^{(2s-1)jdn_{1}}}|J_{jf(n_{1})-1}|^{s-\frac{2}{f(N)}}\\
&\leq 2^{6j-1}B^{\frac{3d(t+1)jn_{1}}{f(N)}}|J_{jf(n_{1})-1}|^{s-\frac{2}{f(N)}}\\
&\leq|J_{jf(n_{1})-1}|^{s-\frac{15}{f(N)}-\frac{\log B}{\log 2}\cdot\frac{6(t+1)}{f(N)}},\end{split}

where the last inequality holds by (

&\leq 2\left(\frac{1}{q_{m_{1}}^{2}}\right)^{s-\frac{15}{f(N)}-\frac{\log B}{\log 2}\cdot\frac{6(t+1)}{f(N)}}\\

(5.1)

\sum_{1\leq a_{f(n)}a_{2f(n)}\cdots a_{kf(n)}<\phi(n)}\left(\frac{1}{a_{f(n)}a_{2f(n)}\cdots a_{kf(n)}}\right)^{s}\ll\phi(n)^{1-s}(\log\phi(n))^{k-1}.

For the coverings of the set
E_{B}
, we note that
E_{B}:=\limsup_{n\to\infty}E_{n}
, where

\displaystyle E_{n}:

\displaystyle=\Big\{x\in\left[0,1\right):\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{if(n)}\left(x\right)\geq B^{dn^{2}}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}\Big\{x\in\left[0,1\right):\prod_{i=1}^{k}a_{if(n)}(x)\geq B^{dn^{2}},\ \prod_{i=1}^{k}a_{if(n)}(x)<B^{dn^{2}}\Big\}:=\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}F_{n,k}.

Then, fix an integer
1\leq k\leq n
, we consider the cover of
F_{n,k}
as

\displaystyle F_{n,k}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{kf(n)}:\prod_{i=1}^{k}a_{if(n)}\geq B^{dn^{2}},\\
\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}a_{if(n)}<B^{dn^{2}}\end{subarray}}I_{kf(n)}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{kf(n)}\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{kf(n)-1}:\\
\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}a_{if(n)}<B^{dn^{2}}\end{subarray}}\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{kf(n)}:\\
\prod_{i=1}^{k}a_{if(n)}\geq B^{dn^{2}}\end{subarray}}I_{kf(n)}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{kf(n)}\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{kf(n)-1}:\\
\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}a_{if(n)}<B^{dn^{2}}\end{subarray}}J_{kf(n)-1}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{kf(n)-1}\right).

Then the length of
J_{kf(n)-1}
is given by

|J_{kf(n)-1}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{kf(n)-1}\right)|\asymp\left(q_{kf(n)-1}^{2}\frac{B^{dn^{2}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}a_{if(n)}}\right)^{-1}.

Thus the
s
-volume of this cover of
F_{n,k}
is

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{kf(n)-1}:\\
\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}a_{if(n)}<B^{dn^{2}}\end{subarray}}\left|J_{kf(n)-1}\right|^{s}&\asymp\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{kf(n)-1}:\\
\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}a_{if(n)}<B^{dn^{2}}\end{subarray}}\left(q_{kf(n)-1}^{2}\frac{B^{dn^{2}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}a_{if(n)}}\right)^{-s}\\
&\leq\left(\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{f(n)-1}}\frac{1}{q_{f(n)-1}^{2s}}\right)^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{f(n)},\ldots,a_{(k-1)f(n)}:\\
\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}a_{if(n)}<B^{dn^{2}}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{a_{f(n)}a_{2f(n)}\cdots a_{(k-1)f(n)}}\right)^{s}\frac{1}{B^{dn^{2}s}}\\
&\ll n^{2(k-1)}(d\log B)^{k-1}\left(\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{f(n)-1}}\frac{1}{q_{f(n)-1}^{2s}}\right)^{k}B^{dn^{2}\left(1-s\right)}\frac{1}{B^{dn^{2}s}},\end{split}

where the last inequality holds by taking
\phi(n)=B^{dn^{2}}
in (
5.1
). Therefore, the
s
-volume of a cover of
E_{n}
is bounded by

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}n^{2(k-1)}(d\log B)^{k-1}\left(\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{f(n)-1}}\frac{1}{q_{f(n)-1}^{2s}}\right)^{k-1}B^{dn^{2}\left(1-s\right)}\frac{1}{B^{dn^{2}s}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\ll n^{2n-1}(d\log B)^{n-1}\left(\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{f(n)-1}}\frac{1}{q_{f(n)-1}^{2s}}\right)^{n}\frac{1}{B^{dn^{2}(2s-1)}}.

This implies that

(5.2)

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{s}\left(E_{B}\right)&\leq\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{n\geq N}\sum_{1\leq k\leq n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{kf(n)-1}:\\
\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}a_{if(n)}<B^{dn^{2}}\end{subarray}}\left|J_{kf(n)-1}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{kf(n)-1}\right)\right|^{s}\\
&\leq\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{n\geq N}n^{2n-1}(d\log B)^{n-1}\left(\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{f(n)-1}}\frac{1}{q_{f(n)-1}^{2s}}\right)^{n}\frac{1}{B^{dn^{2}(2s-1)}}.\end{split}

Finally, to complete the proof, we claim that the dimensional numbers

s_{0}^{\prime}:=\inf\left\{s:\sum_{n\geq N}\left(\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{f(n)-1}}\frac{1}{q_{f(n)-1}^{2s}}\right)^{n}\frac{1}{B^{dn^{2}\left(2s-1\right)}}<\infty\right\}

and

s_{0}:=\inf\left\{s:\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{dn}\log\left(\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{f(n)-1}}\frac{1}{q_{f(n)-1}^{2s}}\right)\frac{1}{B^{dn\left(2s-1\right)}}\leq 0\right\}

are the same.

Proposition 5.2
.

With the above notations
s_{0}=s_{0}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We first show that
s_{0}\geq s_{0}^{\prime}
. To this end, for any
\epsilon>0
, let
s^{\prime}:=s_{0}+2\epsilon
. For each
s^{\prime}>s_{0}
, we consider
s=(s_{0}+s^{\prime})/2
, then we have

\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{f(n)-1}}\frac{1}{q_{f(n)-1}^{2\cdot\frac{s^{\prime}+s_{0}}{2}}}\right)\frac{1}{B^{dn\left(2\cdot\frac{s^{\prime}+s_{0}}{2}-1\right)}}\leq\frac{d\epsilon}{\alpha},

where
\alpha>\frac{1}{2\log B}
is a constant. This implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{f(n)-1}}\frac{1}{q_{f(n)-1}^{2(s^{\prime}-\epsilon)}}\frac{1}{B^{dn(2(s^{\prime}-\epsilon)-1)}}\leq e^{n\frac{d\epsilon}{\alpha}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{f(n)-1}}\frac{1}{q_{f(n)-1}^{2s^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{B^{dn(2s^{\prime}-1)}}\leq\frac{e^{\frac{dn\epsilon}{\alpha}}}{B^{2dn\epsilon}}.

By the choice of
\alpha
we can conclude that

\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{f(n)-1}}\frac{1}{q_{f(n)-1}^{2s^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{B^{dn(2s^{\prime}-1)}}\right)^{n}<\infty,

and
s^{\prime}>s_{0}^{\prime}.
Therefore,
s_{0}\geq s_{0}^{\prime}
.

For the reverse inequality, for all
s<s_{0}
, there exists
\eta>0
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{dn}\log\left(\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{f(n)-1}}\frac{1}{q_{f(n)-1}^{2s}}\right)\frac{1}{B^{dn\left(2s-1\right)}}>\eta.

That is to say for large enough
n
, we have

\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{f(n)-1}}\frac{1}{q_{f(n)-1}^{2s}}\frac{1}{B^{dn\left(2s-1\right)}}>e^{\eta dn}.

Thus,

\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{f(n)-1}}\frac{1}{q_{f(n)-1}^{2s}}\frac{1}{B^{dn\left(2s-1\right)}}\right)^{n}>\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}e^{\eta dn^{2}}=\infty.

Hence
s<s_{0}^{\prime}
and as a result
s_{0}\leq s_{0}^{\prime}
.
∎

Therefore, the upper bound of the Hausdorff dimension of
E_{B}
is as follows

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}E_{B}\leq\inf\Big\{s:\textrm{P}(-s\log|T^{\prime}(x)|-(2s-1)\log B)\leq 0\Big\}.

6.
Hausdorff dimension of
E_{f}(\psi)

In this section we focus on the dimension of
E_{f}\left(\psi\right)
for a general function
\psi(n)
. First, we quote a well-known result of Lüczak
[
17
]
that will be used later.

Lemma 6.1
.

For any
b,c>1
,
\Big\{x\in[0,1):a_{n}\left(x\right)\geq c^{b^{n}}\textrm{ for infinitely many }n\Big\}
and
\Big\{x\in[0,1):a_{n}\left(x\right)\geq c^{b^{n}}\textrm{ for sufficiently large }n\Big\}
have the same Hausdorff dimension
1/\left(b+1\right)
.

Recall that

E_{f}(\psi):=\left\{x\in[0,1):\sqrt[n]{a_{f(n)}(x)a_{2f(n)}(x)\cdots a_{nf(n)}(x)}\geq\psi(n)\ {\rm for\ infinitely\ many}\ n\in\mathbb{N}\right\},

where

\log B=\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\psi\left(n\right)}{dn},\textrm{ }\log b=\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\log\psi\left(n\right)}{dn^{2}}.

The proof is split into several parts.

•

For the case
B=1
, we observe that

E_{f}\left(\psi\right)\supseteq\Big\{x\in\left[0,1\right):a_{nf(n)}\left(x\right)\geq\psi\left(n\right)^{n}\textrm{ for infinitely many }n\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}.

By Wang-Wu theorem
[
22
]
, we obtain that
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}E_{f}\left(\psi\right)=1
.

•

For the case
1<B<\infty
, for any
0<\varepsilon<B-1
we have
\psi\left(n\right)>\left(B-\varepsilon\right)^{dn}
when
n
is large enough. Thus, we have

E_{f}\left(\psi\right)\subseteq\Big\{x\in\left[0,1\right):\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{if(n)}\left(x\right)\geq\left(B-\varepsilon\right)^{dn^{2}}\textrm{ for infinitely many }n\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}.

On the other hand, we can choose a largely sparse integer sequence
\{n_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
such that for all
j\geq 1
,

\psi(n_{j})<\left(B+\varepsilon\right)^{dn_{j}}.

Choose large enough integers
N,M
, let
s<s_{B+\varepsilon}(M,N)
, similar as we discussed in Section 4, the difference in this case is
f(n_{j})-1
may not be a multiple of
f(N)
. So we define
\hat{r}_{j}
such that

\hat{r}_{j}f(N)\leq f(n_{j})-1<(\hat{r}_{j}+1)f(N).

For any
1\leq i\leq n_{j}-1
, let

o_{j}^{i}:=if(n_{j})+\hat{r}_{j}f(N),

one can see that
0\leq(i+1)f(n_{j})-o_{j}^{i}<f(N)
. Let
\hat{v}_{j}
be the largest integer such that
n_{j}f(n_{j})+\hat{v}_{j}f(N)\leq f(n_{j+1})-1
and denote
\hat{m}_{j}=n_{j}f(n_{j})+v_{j}f(N)
. Then we define a subset of
E_{f}(\psi)
,

\displaystyle\hat{F}:=\Big\{x\in\left[0,1\right)

\displaystyle:A_{i}(n_{j})^{n_{j}}\leq a_{if(n_{j})}(x)\leq 2A_{i}(n_{j})^{n_{j}},1\leq i\leq n_{j},

\displaystyle\phantom{=\;\;}a_{n}(x)=2\textrm{ for all }\hat{m}_{j}<n<f(n_{j+1}),j\geq 1,

\displaystyle\phantom{=\;\;}\textrm{ and }o_{j}^{i}<n<(i+1)f(n_{j}),1\leq i<n_{j};1\leq a_{n}\left(x\right)\leq M\textrm{ for other cases}\Big\}.

A rigorous proof of dimension of this set can be carried out with no more changes, one can conclude that

s_{B+\varepsilon}\leq\dim_{\mathcal{H}}E_{f}\left(\psi\right)\leq s_{B-\varepsilon}.

Letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we obtain
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}E_{f}\left(\psi\right)=s_{B}
.

•

For the case
B=\infty
, we consider the following three cases.

(i)

For
1<b<\infty
, for any
0<\varepsilon<b-1
we have
\psi\left(n\right)>e^{\left(b-\varepsilon\right)^{(1-\varepsilon)nf(n)}}
for sufficiently large
n
. We observe that

\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{if(n)}\left(x\right)\geq e^{n(b-\varepsilon)^{(1-\varepsilon)nf(n)}}

implies that there is an
1\leq i\leq n
such that

a_{if(n)}\left(x\right)\geq e^{(b-\varepsilon)^{(1-\varepsilon)if(n)}}

for any
x\in E_{f}\left(\psi\right)
. Thus,

E_{f}\left(\psi\right)\subseteq\Big\{x\in\left[0,1\right):a_{n}\left(x\right)\geq e^{\left(b-\varepsilon\right)^{(1-\varepsilon)n}}\textrm{ for infinitely many }n\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}.

Therefore, by Lemma
6.1
we have

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}E_{f}\left(\psi\right)\leq\frac{1}{1+(b-\varepsilon)^{1-\varepsilon}}.

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, we have

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}E_{f}\left(\psi\right)\leq\frac{1}{1+b}.

As for the lower bound, we observe that for any
\varepsilon>0
, we have
\psi\left(n\right)<e^{\left(b+\varepsilon\right)^{(1+\varepsilon)nf(n)}}
for infinitely many
n
. Let

\mathcal{G}_{b}=\Big\{n:\psi\left(n\right)<e^{\left(b+\varepsilon\right)^{(1+\varepsilon)nf(n)}}\Big\}.

Then we have

\displaystyle E_{f}\left(\psi\right)

\displaystyle\supseteq\Big\{x\in\left[0,1\right):\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{if(n)}\left(x\right)\geq e^{\left(b+2\varepsilon\right)^{(1+\varepsilon)nf(n)}}\textrm{ for infinitely many }n\in\mathcal{G}_{b}\Big\}

\displaystyle\supseteq\Big\{x\in\left[0,1\right):a_{if(n)}\left(x\right)\geq e^{\left(b+2\varepsilon\right)^{(1+\varepsilon)if(n)}},1\leq i\leq n,\textrm{ for infinitely many }n\in\mathcal{G}_{b}\Big\}

\displaystyle\supseteq\Big\{x\in\left[0,1\right):a_{n}\left(x\right)\geq e^{\left(b+2\varepsilon\right)^{(1+\varepsilon)n}},\textrm{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}.

Thus, by Lemma
6.1
and since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, we have

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}E_{f}\left(\psi\right)=\frac{1}{1+b}.

(ii)

For
b=1
, the proof of the lower bound is the same as in the case
1<b<\infty
, take
b=1
implies that
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}E_{f}\left(\psi\right)\geq 1/2
. As for the upper bound, using the fact that
B=\infty
, we have

\frac{\log\psi\left(n\right)}{dn}>\log B_{1}

for a sufficiently large
B_{1}>0
. This implies
\psi\left(n\right)>B_{1}^{dn}
holds for sufficiently large
n
. Then, we have

E_{f}\left(\psi\right)\subseteq\Big\{x\in\left[0,1\right):\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{if(n)}\left(x\right)\geq B_{1}^{dn^{2}}\textrm{ for infinitely many }n\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}.

Thus,

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}E_{f}\left(\psi\right)\leq s_{B_{1}}.

Letting
B_{1}\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain the desired result.

(iii)

For
b=\infty
, for a sufficiently large
b_{1}
such that
0<b_{1}<b
, by the first part in (i), we have

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}E\left(\psi\right)\leq\frac{1}{b_{1}+1}\rightarrow 0

as
b_{1}\rightarrow\infty
.

We have covered all potential cases, thus the theorem is proven.

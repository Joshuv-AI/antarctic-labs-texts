1.
Introduction

The question of solvability in the small and Schauder type estimates play an essential role in the theory of the Fredholmness of boundary value problems for elliptic equations in appropriate BFS. There is a vast number of papers and monographs dedicated to the Fredholmness of Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) in the frame of classical function classes as the Hölder and Sobolev spaces. The appearance of new function spaces and their intensive study had a very big impact on the regularity and existence theory for PDEs. The so-called non standard function spaces turn to be interesting not only from theoretical point of view but also for the applied mathematics and mathematical physics. Among the most studied spaces we can find the Morrey
L^{p,\lambda}
spaces, the weighted Lebesgue spaces and various generalizations, the variable Lebesgue spaces
L^{p(\cdot)},
the grand Lebesgue spaces
L^{p)},
the Orlicz spaces

In the present paper we are interested on elliptic differential operators of higher order in weighted general BFS

with Muckenhoupt type weight

with a smooth enough boundary. In order to obtain the desired a priori estimates we need that the Hardy-Littlewood maximal and the Calderón-Zygmund singular operators are bounded in

Then under certain conditions on the coefficients we obtain solvability in the small, that is local solvability, in Sobolev spaces

More over, we obtain interior Schauder type inequalities in

for the solutions of the linear uniformly elliptic equations under consideration. Such estimates play an exceptional role in the establishing of the Fredholmness of the corresponding elliptic operators. At the end, some examples of BFS are given.

2.
Definitions and auxiliary results

The question of existence and regularity of the solutions of linear elliptic equations is strongly related to the question of continuity of certain integral operators in the corresponding function spaces. For this goal we recall the definitions of these operators and introduce the function spaces that we are going to use.

Let
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
{\mathcal{M}}f
be the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator

(2.1)

{\mathcal{M}}f(x)=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{|{\mathcal{B}}_{r}(x)|}\int_{{\mathcal{B}}_{r}(x)}|f(y)|\,dy,

and

(2.2)

{\mathcal{K}}f(x)=p.v.\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{\omega(y)}{|x-y|^{n}}f(y)\,dy.

For each

(2.3)

Simple calculations show that we can estimate the Riesz potential via the maximal function (see

\int_{|y-x|\leq\delta}\frac{|f(y)|\,dy}{|y-x|^{n-\gamma}}\leq c\delta^{\gamma}{\mathcal{M}}f(x),\qquad\forall\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

Let

4.
Local existence of strong solutions

In the next section we are going to obtain some local results concerning solvability in weighted Sobolev-Banach space builded upon
X_{w}.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let (
3.2
), (

P

x
0

), the Property
1
and the condition (
E
) hold true in a ball
{\mathcal{B}}_{r}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega.
Then

(1)

If
\varphi\in W^{m}_{X_{w}}({\mathcal{B}}_{r}(x_{0}))
, then
\varphi={\mathcal{T}}_{x_{0}}\varphi+{\mathcal{S}}_{x_{0}}{\mathcal{L}}\varphi.

(2)

If for some
f\in X_{w}({\mathcal{B}}_{r}(x_{0}))
it holds
\varphi={\mathcal{T}}_{x_{0}}\varphi+{\mathcal{S}}_{x_{0}}f
, then
\varphi
is a strong solution of the equation
{\mathcal{L}}\varphi(x)=f(x)
for almost all
x\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r}(x_{0}).

Proof.

(1)
Without loss of generality we may assume that
x_{0}=0\in\Omega.

Because of (
2.9
)
there exists some
p_{0}>1
such that

W_{X_{w}}^{m}(\Omega)\subset W^{p_{0},m}(\Omega)
continuously.

Since

\varphi\in W^{p_{0},m}(r)
than we can consider
\varphi\in\overset{\circ\hskip 16.38895pt}{W^{m_{,}p_{0}}}(r+\delta)
for some
\delta>0

small enough
and

\varphi={\mathcal{T}}_{0}\varphi+{\mathcal{S}}_{0}{\mathcal{L}}\varphi,
(cf.
[
3
, Lemma A]
), i.e.
{\mathcal{I}d}={\mathcal{T}}_{0}+{\mathcal{S}}_{0}{\mathcal{L}}
, where
{\mathcal{I}d}
is the

identity operator
in
W^{p_{0},m}(r).

Then

(4.1)

\begin{split}{\mathcal{T}}_{0}\varphi={\mathcal{S}}_{0}({\mathcal{L}}-L_{0})\varphi={\mathcal{S}}_{0}\psi=\int_{{\mathcal{B}}(r+\delta)}J_{0}(x-y)\psi(y)\,dy,\end{split}

where
\psi=({\mathcal{L}}-L_{0})\varphi
and
x\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r+\delta}.

It is clear that
\psi\in L^{p_{0}}(r+\delta)
and from the classical theory (cf.
[
3
,
14
]
) we have the representation formula

(4.2)

D^{\beta}{\mathcal{T}}_{0}\varphi(x)=\int_{{\mathcal{B}}_{r+\delta}}D_{x}^{\beta}J_{0}(x-y)\psi(y)\,dy+C\psi(x),

for each
|\beta|=m
and
C
being a positive constant independent of
\psi.

Since
D_{x}^{m}J_{0}
is a Calderón-Zygmund type kernel, then from the boundedness of the singular operators
in
L^{p_{0}}(r+\delta)
it follows that the formula (
4.2
) holds also for

\psi\in L^{p_{0}}(r+\delta).

By the Property
1
and (
2.8
) it follows that (
4.2
) is valid also in
X_{w}(r+\delta)
and

(4.3)

\begin{split}\|D^{\beta}{\mathcal{T}}_{0}\varphi\|_{X_{w}(r)}&\leq\|D^{\beta}{\mathcal{T}}_{0}\varphi\|_{X_{w}(r+\delta)}\leq c\|\psi\|_{X_{w}(r+\delta)}\\
&\leq c\Big(\|L_{0}\varphi\|_{X_{w}(r+\delta)}+\|{\mathcal{L}}\varphi\|_{X_{w}(r+\delta)}\Big).\end{split}

By the assumptions on the coefficients we have
|a_{\alpha}(0)|\leq\|a_{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}(r+\delta)}
for all
|\alpha|=m
. Then from (
4.3
) it follows

\|D^{m}{\mathcal{T}}_{0}\varphi\|_{X_{w}(r)}\leq c\|\varphi\|_{W_{X_{w}}^{m}(r+\delta)}.

Since
W_{X_{w}}^{m}(\Omega)
verifies the Property
2
we have

\|D^{m}{\mathcal{T}}_{0}\varphi\|_{X_{w}(r)}\leq c\|\varphi\|_{W_{X_{w}}^{m}(r)}

and

\|{\mathcal{T}}_{0}\varphi\|_{W_{X_{w}}^{m}(r)}\leq c\|\varphi\|_{W_{X_{w}}^{m}(r)}

that implies
{\mathcal{T}}_{0}\in[W_{X_{w}}^{m}(r)]
.

Analogously
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\in[X_{w}(r);W_{X_{w}}^{m}(r)]
and hence
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}{\mathcal{L}}\in[W_{X_{w}}^{m}(r)].

Then

\varphi(x)={\mathcal{T}}_{0}\varphi(x)+{\mathcal{S}}_{0}{\mathcal{L}}\varphi(x)\quad\text{ for a.a. }x\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r},

in
L^{p_{0}}(r).

The estimate (
3.5
) holds also in
W_{X_{w}}^{m}(r)
, i.e.

(4.4)

{\mathcal{I}d}={\mathcal{T}}_{0}+{\mathcal{S}}_{0}{\mathcal{L}}\in[W_{X_{w}}^{m}(r)].

(2)

Let
f\in X_{w}(r)
such that
\varphi={\mathcal{T}}_{0}\varphi+{\mathcal{S}}_{0}f.
Hence

{\mathcal{L}}\varphi(x)=L_{0}{\mathcal{S}}_{0}{\mathcal{L}}\varphi(x)=L_{0}{\mathcal{S}}_{0}f(x)=f(x)\quad\text{ for a.a. }x\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r},

where we have used that

L_{0}{\mathcal{S}}_{0}={\mathcal{I}d}
in
X_{w}(r).

Then

{\mathcal{L}}\varphi(x)=f(x).

∎

Because of the equivalence of the norms (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) the Lemma
3.1
holds also in
W^{m}_{X_{w;r}}(r).

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\varphi\in W^{m}_{X_{w;r}}({\mathcal{B}}_{r}(x_{0})).
Under the conditions of Lemma
4.1
it holds

(1)

\varphi={\mathcal{T}}_{x_{0}}\varphi+{\mathcal{S}}_{x_{0}}{\mathcal{L}}\varphi,
a.e. in
{\mathcal{B}}_{r}(x_{0});

(2)

if for some
f\in X_{w}({\mathcal{B}}_{r}(x_{0}))
it holds
\varphi={\mathcal{T}}_{x_{0}}\varphi+{\mathcal{S}}_{x_{0}}f
, then
\varphi
is a solution of the equation
{\mathcal{L}}\varphi=f.

The previous lemma allows us to proof the following local existence result.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let (
3.2
), (

P

x
0

), and the Properties
1
and
2
hold.
Then the equation
{\mathcal{L}}u=f
admits a solution in
W_{X_{w}}^{m}({\mathcal{B}}_{r}(x_{0})),
for all
f\in X_{w}(\Omega),
and
r>0
small enough.

Proof.

By the Corollary
4.2
we can give the proof in the space
W^{m}_{X_{w;r}}(r),
taking
x_{0}=0\in\Omega.

Since
L_{0}{\mathcal{S}}_{0}={\mathcal{I}d}
in
X_{w}(r),
and keeping in mind (
3.7
) we have

(4.5)

L_{0}{\mathcal{T}}_{0}=L_{0}{\mathcal{S}}_{0}(L_{0}-{\mathcal{L}})={\mathcal{I}d}(L_{0}-{\mathcal{L}})=L_{0}-{\mathcal{L}}.

For any
u\in W_{X_{w;r}}^{m}(r)
we consider the equation
{\mathcal{L}}u=f.
By (
4.5
) we can rewrite it as follows

f={\mathcal{L}}u=(L_{0}-L_{0}{\mathcal{T}}_{0})u=L_{0}({\mathcal{I}d}-{\mathcal{T}}_{0})u,

where the identity operator here acts in
W_{X_{w;r}}^{m}(r).

Applying
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}
we obtain

{\mathcal{S}}_{0}f={\mathcal{S}}_{0}L_{0}({\mathcal{I}d}-{\mathcal{T}}_{0})u=({\mathcal{I}d}-{\mathcal{T}}_{0})u.

By Lemma

we have

\|{\mathcal{T}}_{0}\|_{[W_{X_{w};r}^{m}(r)]}=\sigma(r)\to 0,\qquad\text{ as }r\to 0.

Taking

we obtain that the operator
{\mathcal{I}d}-{\mathcal{T}}_{0}
is boundedly invertible in

and by Lemma

the function

is a solution of the equation
{\mathcal{L}}u=f
in

∎

5.
Interior Schauder type estimates

Our goal now is to obtain local interior Schauder type estimates for the solutions of
{\mathcal{L}}\varphi=f
in

W_{X_{w}}^{m}(\Omega).
For this purpose we need some auxiliary lemma.

Let
\omega\in C_{0}^{\infty}([0,1]
be such that

\omega(t)=\begin{cases}1&\quad 0\leq t<\frac{1}{3},\\
0&\quad\frac{2}{3}<t\leq 1.\end{cases}

Then we can define a cut-off function
\xi\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathcal{B}}_{r_{2}})
as

(5.1)

\xi(x)=\begin{cases}1&\quad|x|\leq r_{1},\\
\omega\Big(\frac{|x|-r_{1}}{r_{2}-r_{1}}\Big)&\quad r_{1}<|x|\leq r_{2},\end{cases}

for any
0<r_{1}<r_{2}\leq 1.
The norm of
\xi
is bounded, as it is proved in
[
5
]

\|\xi\|_{C^{m}(r_{2})}\leq c\Big(1-\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\Big)^{-m}

with a constant independent of
r_{1}
and
r_{2}.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let the conditions of Theorem
4.3
be fulfilled in
{\mathcal{B}}_{r_{2}}\Subset\Omega.
Then for any
0<r_{1}<r_{2}
as in (
5.1
) and
u\in W_{X_{w};r_{2}}^{m}(\Omega)
the following estimate holds

\|u\|_{W_{X_{w};r_{1}}^{m}(r_{1})}\leq c\Big(1-\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\Big)^{-m}\Big(\|{\mathcal{L}}u\|_{X_{w}(r_{2})}+\|u\|_{W_{X_{w};r_{2}}^{m-1}(r_{2})}\Big)

with a constant independent of
r_{1},r_{2},
and
u.

Proof.

Take
\varphi=\xi u\in W_{X_{w};r_{2}}^{m}(r_{2})
with a compact support in
{\mathcal{B}}_{r_{2}}.
Then by Corollary
4.2
we have

(5.2)

\varphi={\mathcal{T}}_{0}\varphi+{\mathcal{S}}_{0}{\mathcal{L}}\varphi

and by Lemma
3.1
there exists
r>0
such small that

\|{\mathcal{T}}_{0}\varphi\|_{W_{X_{w};r_{2}}^{m}(r_{2})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\varphi\|_{W_{X_{w};r_{2}}^{m}(r_{2})}

holds for all
r_{2}\in(0,r).
Then by (
5.2
) we obtain

(5.3)

\|\varphi\|_{W_{X_{w};r_{2}}^{m}(r_{2})}\leq 2\|{\mathcal{S}}_{0}{\mathcal{L}}\varphi\|_{W_{X_{w};r_{2}}^{m}(r_{2})}

where

(5.4)

{\mathcal{S}}_{0}{\mathcal{L}}\varphi(x)=\int_{{\mathcal{B}}_{r_{2}}}J_{0}(x-y){\mathcal{L}}\varphi(y)\,dy.

Calculating the higher order derivatives we obtain

D^{\beta}{\mathcal{S}}_{0}{\mathcal{L}}\varphi(x)=\int_{{\mathcal{B}}_{r_{2}}}D_{x}^{\beta}J_{0}(x-y){\mathcal{L}}\varphi(y)\,dy+c{\mathcal{L}}\varphi(x),\quad|\beta|=m

with a constant
c
independent of
\varphi.

The integral operator is of Calderón-Zygmund type and, by Property
1
, the following estimate holds

(5.5)

\|D^{m}{\mathcal{S}}_{0}{\mathcal{L}}\varphi\|_{X_{w}(r_{2})}\leq c\|{\mathcal{L}}\varphi\|_{X_{w}(r_{2})}.

For the lower order derivatives of (
5.4
) we have the following expression

D^{\beta}{\mathcal{S}}_{0}{\mathcal{L}}\varphi(x)=\int_{{\mathcal{B}}_{r_{2}}}D_{x}^{\beta}J_{0}(x-y){\mathcal{L}}\varphi(y)\,dy,\quad|\beta|<m.

In this case the kernel has a weak singularity and the integral operator is a Riesz type integral (
2.3
) that we can estimate as

(5.6)

r_{2}^{|\beta|}\|D^{\beta}{\mathcal{S}}_{0}{\mathcal{L}}\varphi\|_{X_{w}(r_{2})}\leq cr_{2}^{m}\|{\mathcal{L}}\varphi\|_{X_{w}(r_{2})}

with a constant independent of
r_{2}
and
\varphi.
Unifying (
5.5
) and (
5.6
) we obtain

(5.7)

\|{\mathcal{S}}_{0}{\mathcal{L}}\varphi\|_{W_{X_{w};r_{2}}^{m}(r_{2})}\leq cr_{2}^{m}\|{\mathcal{L}}\varphi\|_{X_{w}(r_{2})}.

On the other hand, it is easy to see that
{\mathcal{L}}\varphi
can be represented in the form

(5.8)

{\mathcal{L}}\varphi(x)=\xi(x){\mathcal{L}}u(x)+M(u;\xi),

where
M(u;\xi)
is a linear combination of derivatives
D^{\alpha}u
, the order of which does not exceed
(m-1)
, multiplied by the derivatives of
\xi
of order at most
m.
Precisely

M(u;\xi)=\sum_{\overset{|\alpha|+|\alpha^{\prime}|\leq m}{|\alpha|<m}}c_{\alpha}(x)D^{\alpha^{\prime}}\xi(x)D^{\alpha}u(x).

Following
[
5
]
we obtain analogously

(5.9)

r_{2}^{m}\|M(u;\xi)\|_{X_{w}(r_{2})}\leq c\|\xi\|_{C^{m}(r_{2})}\|u\|_{W_{X_{w};r_{2}}^{m-1}(r_{2})}.

Unifying (
5.9
) and (
5.7
), we obtain

(5.10)

\begin{split}\|{\mathcal{S}}_{0}{\mathcal{L}}u&\|_{W_{X_{w};r_{1}}^{m}(r_{1})}\leq\|{\mathcal{S}}_{0}{\mathcal{L}}\varphi\|_{W_{X_{w};r_{2}}^{m}(r_{2})}\\
&\leq cr_{2}^{m}\Big(\|\xi\|_{C^{m}(r_{2})}\|{\mathcal{L}}u\|_{X_{w}(r_{2})}+\|M(u,\xi)\|_{X_{w}(r_{2})}\Big)\\
&\leq c\|\xi\|_{C^{m}(r_{2})}\Big(\|{\mathcal{L}}u\|_{X_{w}(r_{2})}+\|u\|_{W_{X_{w};r_{2}}^{m-1}(r_{2})}\Big)\\
&\leq c\Big(1-\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\Big)^{-m}\Big(\|{\mathcal{L}}u\|_{X_{w}(r_{2})}+\|u\|_{W_{X_{w};r_{2}}^{m-1}(r_{2})}\Big).\end{split}

Then making use of (
5.3
) we obtain the desired estimate

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W_{X_{w};r_{1}}^{m}(r_{1})}

\displaystyle=\|\varphi\|_{W_{X_{w};r_{1}}^{m}(r_{1})}\leq 2\|{\mathcal{S}}_{0}{\mathcal{L}}\varphi\|_{W_{X_{w};r_{1}}^{m}(r_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq c\Big(1-\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\Big)^{-m}\Big(\|{\mathcal{L}}u\|_{X_{w}(r_{2})}+\|u\|_{W_{X_{w};r_{2}}^{m-1}(r_{2})}\Big).

∎

In order to establish interior Schauder’s estimate we need the following result.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose that the space
X_{w}(\Omega)
possesses the Properties
1
,
2
and
3
. Then

\|u\|_{W_{X_{w}}^{k}(\Omega)}\leq\varepsilon\|u\|_{W_{X_{w}}^{k+1}(\Omega)}+\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{p^{k}}}\|u\|_{X_{w}(\Omega)}

for some
p>1
and for all
k=1,\ldots,m-1.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
d_{\Omega}=1
(see
[
5
]
). Let
\Omega\Subset\Omega^{\prime}
, where

\Omega^{\prime}
is a bounded domain with sufficiently smooth boundary.

In order to simplify the calculus, we begin assuming
n=1,m=2
and then we extend the result via induction to more general situation.
Let
\Omega=(a,b)
be an interval of length
b-a=\varepsilon
for a fixed
\varepsilon
and suppose that
u\in C_{0}^{2}(\Omega^{\prime}),
then by

[
14
, Theorem 7.27]
we have

(5.11)

|u^{\prime}(x)|\leq\int_{a}^{b}|u^{\prime\prime}(t)|\,dt+\frac{18}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{a}^{b}|u(t)|\,dt,\quad\forall\,x\in(a,b).

By density arguments it follows that (
5.11
)
holds true also for any
u\in\overset{\circ}{W}{}^{2,1}(\Omega^{\prime}).
Since
\overset{\circ}{W}{}_{X_{w}}^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})\subset\overset{\circ}{W}{}^{2,1}(\Omega^{\prime})
, then it is evident that this inequality is true for all

u\in\overset{\circ}{W}{}_{X_{w}}^{2}(\Omega^{\prime}).

We can estimate the norm of
u^{\prime}w
by (
5.11
):

(5.12)

\begin{split}\|u^{\prime}w\|_{X(a,b)}\leq\|w\|_{X(a,b)}\Big(\int_{a}^{b}|u^{\prime\prime}(t)|\,dt+\frac{18}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{a}^{b}|u(t)|\,dt\Big).\end{split}

Then for an arbitrary interval
\Omega=(a,b)
we construct covering with disjoint intervals
I_{l}
with length
\varepsilon,
such that
\Omega=\bigcup_{l}I_{l}.

Since (
5.12
) holds for all
I_{l},
the Hölder inequality for
p>1
gives

(5.13)

\begin{split}\|u^{\prime}w\|_{X(I_{l})}^{p}\leq&\|w\|_{X(I_{l})}^{p}\Big[\Big(\int_{I_{l}}|u^{\prime\prime}(t)|\,dt\Big)^{p}+\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{2p}}\Big(\int_{I_{l}}|u(t)|\,dt\Big)^{p}\Big]\\
\leq&c\|w\|_{X(\Omega)}^{p}\Big(\varepsilon^{p-1}\int_{I_{l}}|u^{\prime\prime}(t)|^{p}\,dt+\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{p+1}}\int_{I_{l}}|u(t)|^{p}\,dt\Big),\end{split}

where we have used that for each
I_{l}\subset\Omega
we have

\|w\|_{X(I_{l})}\leq\|w\|_{X(\Omega)}.

Then

\sum_{l}\|u^{\prime}w\|_{X(I_{l})}^{p}\leq c\|w\|_{X(\Omega)}^{p}\Big(\varepsilon^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}|u^{\prime\prime}(x)|^{p}\,dx+\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{p+1}}\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\,dx\Big).

Let the Condition
1
holds. Then for each
p\in[1,p_{1}]
we have

\|u^{\prime}w\|_{X(\Omega)}^{p}\leq c\sum_{l}\|u^{\prime}w\|_{X(I_{l})}^{p}.

For any
p\leq\min\{p_{0},p_{1}\},
the Property
1
implies
X_{w}(\Omega)\subset L^{p}(\Omega)
and hence

(5.14)

\begin{split}\|u^{\prime}w\|_{X(\Omega)}&\leq c\|w\|_{X(\Omega)}\Big(\varepsilon^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}|u^{\prime\prime}(x)|^{p}\,dx+\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{p+1}}\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&\leq c\|w\|_{X(\Omega)}\Big(\varepsilon\|u^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\Big).\end{split}

If
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
then (
5.14
) holds for any partial derivative
D_{i}u
for
i=1,\ldots,n.
Moreover it holds also for the higher order derivatives, that is

\|D^{k}u\|_{X_{w}(\Omega)}\leq\|w\|_{X(\Omega)}\Big(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|D^{k+1}u\|_{X_{w}(\Omega)}+\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{2-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}}\|D^{k-1}u\|_{X_{w}(\Omega)}\Big),

with
1\leq k\leq m-1.
Summing up along
k
we obtain

\|u\|_{W_{X_{w}(\Omega)}^{s}}\leq\|w\|_{X_{w}}\Big(\epsilon\|u\|_{W_{X_{w}(\Omega)}^{s+1}}+\frac{c}{\epsilon^{2-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}}\|u\|_{W_{X_{w}}^{s-1}(\Omega)}\Big)

for all
s=1,\ldots,m-1.

Consider now the case when Condition
2
holds, hence there exists
p_{2}\in(1,\infty)
such that

\|u^{\prime}\|_{X_{w}(\Omega)}\leq c\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\qquad\forall\ p\in[p_{2},\infty).

As before, it is sufficient to consider the case of
n=1,

m=2
and
b-a=\varepsilon.

Taking into account (
5.11
) and
[
14
, Lemma 7.27]
(see also
[
1
,
21
]
) we have

(5.15)

\begin{split}\|u^{\prime}\|_{X_{w}(a,b)}&\leq c\Big(\int_{a}^{b}|u^{\prime}(x)|^{p}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq c\varepsilon\int_{a}^{b}|u^{\prime\prime}(t)|\,dt+\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{2p}}\int_{a}^{b}|u(t)|\,dt\end{split}

Taking an arbitrary interval
\Omega=(a,b)
we construct a covering with a family of disjoint intervals of length
\varepsilon,
that is,
\Omega=\bigcup_{l}I_{l}
, where
\Omega\bigcap I_{l}\neq\emptyset,

I_{i}\bigcap I_{j}=\emptyset,

i\neq j
. Since (
5.15
) holds for all
I_{l},
summing up these estimates with respect to
l
we obtain

\begin{split}\|u^{\prime}&\|_{X_{w}(\Omega)}\leq\varepsilon\|u^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\frac{c}{\varepsilon}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\\
&\ \leq\epsilon\int_{\Omega}|u^{\prime\prime}(x)|w(x)w^{-1}(x)\,dx+\frac{c}{\epsilon^{2p_{2}}}\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|w(x)w^{-1}(x)\,dx\Big)\\
&\ \leq\|w^{-1}\|_{X^{\prime}(\Omega)}\Big(\epsilon\|u^{\prime\prime}\|_{X_{w}(\Omega)}+\frac{c}{\epsilon^{2p_{2}}}\|u\|_{X_{w}(\Omega)}\int_{a}^{b}|u^{\prime\prime}(t)|\,dt+\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{a}^{b}|u(t)\,dt|\Big)\end{split}

Extending this to functions of
n
variables and taking
\varepsilon=\|w^{-1}\|_{X^{\prime}(\Omega)}\epsilon,
we obtain

(5.16)

\|u\|_{W_{X_{w}(\Omega)}^{k}}\leq\varepsilon\|u\|_{W_{X_{w}(\Omega)}^{k+1}}+\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{p}}\|u\|_{W_{X_{w}}^{k-1}(\Omega)},\quad\forall\ k=1,\ldots,m-1,

for any
p\geq 2p_{2}
and a constant
c
depending on known quantities and on the norms
\|w^{-1}\|_{X^{\prime}(\Omega)}
and

\|w\|_{X_{w}(\Omega)}.

Introducing the notion

A_{k}:=\|u\|_{W_{X_{w}(\Omega)}^{k}},
for all
k=0,\ldots,m,
we rewrite (
5.16
)

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{1}A_{2}+\frac{c_{1}}{\varepsilon_{1}^{p}}A_{0}

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{2}A_{3}+\frac{c_{2}}{\varepsilon_{2}^{p}}A_{1}\leq\varepsilon_{2}A_{3}+\frac{c_{1}c_{2}}{(\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{1})^{p}}A_{0}+\frac{c_{2}\varepsilon_{1}}{\varepsilon_{2}^{p}}A_{2}.

Taking
\varepsilon_{2}=\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon^{p+1}
with
\varepsilon c_{2}<1,
we get

A_{2}\leq\varepsilon A_{3}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{p^{2}}}A_{0}.

Repeating the same procedure we obtain a kind of interpolation inequality

A_{k}\leq\varepsilon A_{k+1}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{p^{k}}}A_{0},\qquad\forall\,k=1,\ldots,m-1

that is the assertion of the lemma.
∎

Theorem 5.3

(Interior Schauder type estimate)
.

Consider the uniformly elliptic equation
{\mathcal{L}}u=f
in a bounded domain
\Omega
with principal coefficients

a_{\alpha}\in C(\overline{\Omega}),
for all
|\alpha|=m
and
a_{\alpha}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
for
|\alpha|<m.

Suppose that the Properties
1
and
2
hold. Then for any domain
\Omega_{0}\Subset\Omega
the following a priori estimate holds

(5.17)

\|u\|_{W_{X_{w}}^{m}(\Omega_{0})}\leq C(\|{\mathcal{L}}u\|_{X_{w}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{X_{w}(\Omega)}).

Proof.

We can construct a finite cover of
\Omega_{0}
with balls whose closure belong to
\Omega.
That is way, without loss of generality we can consider
\Omega
and
\Omega_{0}
to be concentric balls of small radius centered at zero. Let
R>0
be sufficiently small, then we need to prove the following estimate

\|u\|_{W_{X_{w};r}^{m}(r)}\leq C\Big(1-\frac{r}{R}\Big)^{-m^{2}}(\|{\mathcal{L}}u\|_{X_{w}(R)}+\|u\|_{X_{w}(R)}).

Set

A=\sup_{0\leq r\leq R}\Big(1-\frac{r}{R}\Big)^{m^{2}}\|u\|_{W_{X_{w};r}^{m}(r)}.

Suppose that
\|u\|_{W_{X_{w};R}^{m}(R)}\neq 0
, otherwise, there is nothing to prove. It is easy to see that there exists
r_{1}\in(0,R)
, such that

A\leq 2\Big(1-\frac{r_{1}}{R}\Big)^{m^{2}}\|u\|_{W_{X_{w};r_{1}}^{m}(r_{1})}.

For a fixed
r_{2}\in(r_{1},R)
and Lemmata
5.1
and
5.2
we obtain

\displaystyle A\leq

\displaystyle\,c\Big(1-\frac{r_{1}}{R}\Big)^{m^{2}}\Big(1-\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\Big)^{-m}(\|{\mathcal{L}}u\|_{X_{w}(r_{2})}+\|u\|_{W_{X_{w};r_{2}}^{m-1}(r_{2})})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,c\Big(1-\frac{r_{1}}{R}\Big)^{m^{2}}\Big(1-\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\Big)^{-m}\Big(\|{\mathcal{L}}u\|_{X_{w}(r_{2})}+\varepsilon\|u\|_{W_{X_{w};r_{2}}^{m}(r_{2})}+\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{p^{m-1}}}\|u\|_{X_{w}(r_{2})}\Big).

Keeping in mind that

\Big(1-\frac{r_{2}}{R}\Big)^{m^{2}}\|u\|_{W_{X_{w};r_{2}}^{m}(r_{2})}\leq A,

we obtain

(5.18)

\begin{split}A\leq c&\Big(1-\frac{r_{1}}{R}\Big)^{m^{2}}\Big(1-\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\Big)^{-m}\|{\mathcal{L}}u\|_{X_{w}(r_{2})}\\
&+\varepsilon c\Big(1-\frac{r_{1}}{R}\Big)^{m^{2}}\Big(1-\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\Big)^{-m}\Big(1-\frac{r_{2}}{R}\Big)^{-m^{2}}A\\
&+\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{p^{m-1}}}\Big(1-\frac{r_{1}}{R}\Big)^{m^{2}}\Big(1-\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\Big)^{-m}\|u\|_{X_{w}(r_{2})}.\end{split}

Set
\delta=1-\frac{r_{1}}{R}
and choose
r_{2}
and
\varepsilon
in the following way

1-\frac{r_{2}}{R}=\frac{\delta}{2},\quad\varepsilon=2^{-1-4m^{2}}\delta^{2m}c^{-1}.

This implies

0<\delta<1,\quad\frac{\delta}{2}<1-\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}<\delta,\quad 0<\varepsilon<1

and, hence

\varepsilon c\Big(1-\frac{r_{1}}{R}\Big)^{m^{2}}\Big(1-\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\Big)^{-m}\Big(1-\frac{r_{2}}{R}\Big)^{-m^{2}}<\frac{1}{2}.

Making use of (
5.18
) we obtain the estimate

A<c\big(\|{\mathcal{L}}u\|_{X_{w}(r_{2})}+\|u\|_{X_{w}(r_{2})})\leq c\Big(\|{\mathcal{L}}u\|_{X_{w}(R)}+\|u\|_{X_{w}(R)}\Big),

that gives (
5.17
).
∎

6.
Some examples of Banach Function Spaces

Let
X(\Omega)
be correctly defined for any domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with a sufficiently smooth boundary
\partial\Omega,
and suppose that
\Omega_{0}
is a bounded domain such that

\Omega\subset\Omega_{0}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\theta_{k}\in[W_{X_{w}}^{k}(\Omega);W_{X_{w}}^{k}(\Omega_{0})]
be such that
\theta_{k}f\big|_{\Omega}=f
for all
k=0,\ldots,m.
Then there exists
\theta\in[W_{X_{w}}^{k}(\Omega);W_{X_{w}}^{k}(\Omega_{0})],
such that
\theta f\big|_{\Omega}=f,
for all
k=0,...,m
.

Proof.

Consider the following operator

\theta f=\begin{cases}\theta_{m}f&f\in W_{X_{w}}^{m}(\Omega),\\
\theta_{m-1}f&f\in W_{X_{w}}^{m-1}(\Omega)\backslash W_{X_{w}}^{m}(\Omega),\\
\ldots&\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\\
\theta_{1}f&f\in W_{X_{w}}^{1}(\Omega)\backslash W_{X_{w}}^{2}(\Omega),\\
\theta_{0}f&f\in X_{w}(\Omega)\backslash W_{X_{w}}^{1}(\Omega).\end{cases}

From the chain of embedding

X_{w}(\Omega)\supset W_{X_{w}}^{1}(\Omega)\supset\ldots\supset W_{X_{w}}^{m}(\Omega),

it follows that the linear operator
\theta\in[X_{w}(\Omega)]
is well defined.

Consider the case
m=1,
and extend the proof by induction to
m>1.
For simplicity we carry out the proof in the one dimensional case.
Supposing

\theta\in[W_{X_{w}}^{1}(\Omega);W_{X_{w}}^{1}(\Omega_{0})]\quad\text{ and }\quad\theta f\big|_{\Omega}=f,

we have to show that
\theta\in[X_{w}(\Omega);X_{w}(\Omega_{0})].
Indeed, if

f\in X_{w}(\Omega)\setminus W_{X_{w}}^{1}(\Omega),
then

\theta f=\theta_{0}f
and therefore

\|\theta f\|_{X_{w}(\Omega_{0})}\leq\|\theta_{0}\|\|f\|_{X_{w}(\Omega)}.

Let
f\in W_{X_{w}}^{1}(\Omega),
hence
\theta f=\theta_{1}f
.
Consider the function

F(x)=\int_{a}^{x}\theta_{1}f(t)\,dt\qquad\text{ for a.a. }\ x\in\Omega_{0},

where
a\in\Omega
is some fixed point.
Then

F\in W_{X_{w}}^{1}(\Omega_{0}),

\frac{dF(x)}{dx}=\theta_{1}f(x),
for a.a.
x\in\Omega_{0}
, and

\theta F=\theta_{1}F
that implies
F\in W_{X_{w}}^{1}(\Omega)
and

(6.1)

\|\theta_{1}F\|_{W_{X_{w}}^{1}(\Omega_{0})}\leq C_{1}\|F\|_{W_{X_{w}}^{1}(\Omega)},

where
C_{1}
is a constant, independent of
F.
It is obvious that
\frac{dF(x)}{dx}=f(x),
a.e. in
\Omega.
Moreover

\displaystyle\|\theta_{1}f\|_{X_{w}(\Omega_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\theta_{1}F\|_{X_{w}(\Omega_{0})}+\|\theta_{1}f\|_{X_{w}(\Omega_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\Big(\|f\|_{X_{w}(\Omega)}+\Big\|\int_{a}^{x}f(t)\,dt\Big\|_{X_{w}(\Omega)}\Big)\leq C\|f\|_{X_{w}(\Omega)}.

Considering functions of
n
variables, fixing all variables except one and applying the above procedure we can extend this result in the
n
-dimensional case.
∎

6.1.
The Marcinkiewicz spaces

The Banach space
M^{p,\lambda}(\Omega),p\in(1,\infty),\lambda\in(0,1),
consisting of all
functions
f\in L^{p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)

for which

\|f\|_{M^{p,\lambda}(\Omega)}=\sup_{E\subset\Omega}\Big(\frac{1}{|E|^{\lambda}}\int_{E}|f(x)|^{p}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty

is called
the Marcinkiewicz space
(see for instance
[
13
]
).

Let us note that in the definition of

the Morrey spaces

L^{p,\lambda}(\Omega)
the supremum is taken over all intersections
E={\mathcal{B}}\cap\Omega
where
{\mathcal{B}}
are balls in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.
This gives
the continuous embedding
M^{p,\lambda}(\Omega)\subset L^{p,\lambda}(\Omega)
for all
\lambda\in(0,1).

It is easy to see that
M^{p,\lambda}(\Omega)
is nonseparable,
moreover, it is a rearrangement invariant space (cf.
[
6
]
).

Let
T
be a quasi-linear operator of

(p,p;q,q)
-compatible weak type, (see
[
2
]
for the definition). Then we have the following results (cf.
[
2
,
6
]
).

Lemma 6.2
.

The Boyd indices of the Marcinkiewicz space
M^{p,\lambda}(\Omega)
are

\alpha_{X}=\beta_{X}=\frac{1-\lambda}{p},\qquad p\in(1,\infty),\ \lambda\in(0,1).

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
X
be a rearrangement invariant space on an nonatomic complete
\sigma
-ﬁnite space with infinite measure. Then any linear (quasilinear) operator
T
of
(p,p;q,q)
-compatible weak type is bounded in
X
; i.e.,
T\in X
if and only if the Boyd indices
\alpha_{X}
and
\beta_{X}
satisfy the inequality

\frac{1}{q}<\alpha_{X}\leq\beta_{X}<\frac{1}{p},\qquad 1\leq p<q\leq+\infty.

The Theorem
6.3
(cf.
[
2
]
) implies the validity of the Property
1
for
M^{q,\lambda}(\Omega)
for any
q\in(1,\infty),

\lambda\in(0,1).
The validity of Property
2
can be proved analogously as in the case of the Morrey spaces. Hence, by Theorem
4.3
we have the following result.

Corollary 6.4
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
6.3
there exists

a solution of

for all

In addition, by Theorem
5.3
we obtain.

Corollary 6.5
.

Under the conditions of Theorem

the following a priori estimate holds

\|u\|_{W_{M^{q,\lambda}}^{m}(\Omega_{0})}\leq c(\|{\mathcal{L}}u\|_{M^{q,\lambda}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{M^{q,\lambda}(\Omega)}).

6.3.
Variable Lebesgue spaces

Given a bounded domain
\Omega
and a Lebesgue measurable function
p(\cdot):\Omega\to[1,+\infty).
We say that
p(\cdot)
is
locally
log-Hölder continuous function
if there exists a constant
c_{0}
such that for all
x,y\in\Omega
verifying
|x-y|<1/2,
it holds

|p(x)-p(y)|\leq\frac{c_{0}}{-\log(|x-y|)}.

We denote this function set as
LH_{0}(\Omega).
It follows immediately (see
[
9
]
) that if
p(\cdot)\in LH_{0}(\Omega)
than it is uniformly continuous and
p(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega).

For a Lebesgue measurable function
f\in F(\Omega)
we define the
modular
associated with
p(\cdot)
by

I_{{p(\cdot),\Omega}}(f)=\int_{\Omega}|f(x)|^{p(x)}\,dx.

Then the
Variable Lebesgue spaces

L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega),

p(\cdot)\in LH_{0}(\Omega)
consist of all
f\in F(\Omega)
such that

for which the following norm is finite

(6.2)

\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}=\inf\Big\{\lambda>0:\ I_{p(\cdot),\Omega}\Big(\frac{f}{\lambda}\Big)\leq 1\Big\}.

The spaces
L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega),

p(\cdot)\in LH_{0}(\Omega),
endowed with
the norm (
6.2
) are BFSs.

The corresponding Sobolev spaces are denoted by
W^{p(\cdot),m}(\Omega)
and for a given weight

we can define also the weighted spaces

The weighted

Definition 6.8

We say that

Minor-closed classes of binary functions
Benjamin R. Jones

Abstract
Binary functions are a generalisation of the cocircuit spaces of binary matroids to arbitrary functions. Every rank function is assigned a binary function, and the deletion and contraction operations of binary functions generalise matroid deletion and contraction. We give excluded minor characterisations for the classes of binary functions with well defined minors, and those with an associated rank function. Within these classes, we also characterise the classes of binary functions corresponding to matroids, binary matroids and polymatroids by their excluded minors. This gives a new proof of Tutte’s excluded minor characterisation of binary matroids in the more generalised space of binary functions.

Introduction

A
binary function
on a finite
ground set

E
is a function
f\colon 2^{E}\to\mathbb{R}
with
f(\emptyset)=1
.

Binary functions were introduced by
Farr 1993
as a generalisation of cocircuit spaces of binary matroids. A rank transform assigns a (not necessarily matroid) rank function to any binary function. When
f
is the indicator function of a binary vector space, then the rank transform on
f
gives the rank function of the associated binary matroid. Aspects of the theory of binary matroids have been extended to binary functions, including deletion, contraction, duality and Tutte-Whitney polynomials by
Farr 1993
;
Farr 2004
;
Farr 2007a
;
Farr 2007b
.

Many important results in graph and matroid theory are characterisations of classes closed under deletion and contraction by a set of
excluded minors
. In graph theory, results range from the characterisation of planar graphs by
Wagner 1937
to the theorem of
Robertson and Seymour 2004
that any minor-closed class of graphs can be characterised by a finite set of excluded minors.

The first excluded minor characterisations for matroids were due to
Tutte 1958a
;
Tutte 1958b
;
Tutte 1959
, and included the classes of regular and graphic matroids.
A proof of Rota’s conjecture, that the number of excluded minors for matroids representable over any given finite field is finite, has been announced by
Geelen et al. 2014
.
The excluded minors for representability over finite fields have been found for the fields
GF(2)
by
Tutte 1958a
,
GF(3)
by
Bixby 1979
and
Seymour 1979
and
GF(4)
by
Geelen et al. 2000
.
Unlike graphs, minor-closed classes of matroids can have an infinite number of excluded minors. One of the first examples, proved by
Lazarson 1958
, is the class of matroids representable over any field.

In this paper, we establish the first excluded minor characterisations of classes of binary functions. We find excluded minor characterisations of binary functions for which no minor is undefined, and of those which have rank functions. We also find excluded minor characterisation for the class of binary functions corresponding to binary matroids.

We identify the classes of binary functions corresponding, via the rank transform, to matroids and polymatroids, and give excluded minor characterisations of these classes. By classifying matroids and binary matroids in the larger class of binary functions, we find a novel proof of Tutte’s characterisation of binary matroids among matroids.

2
Operations on binary functions

For a binary function
f\colon 2^{E}\to\mathbb{R}
, we have
f(\emptyset)=1
. Unless otherwise stated, we will assume that
f
is a binary function with ground set
E
. The
order
of a binary function is the size of its ground set.

Contraction and deletion operations on binary functions were introduced by
Farr 1993
.

The
contraction
of a set
X\subseteq E
from a binary function
f
gives the binary function
f/X\colon 2^{E\backslash X}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\displaystyle f/X(Y)=f(Y).

(1)

The
deletion
of
X\subseteq E
from
f
gives the binary function

f\backslash X\colon 2^{E\backslash X}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\displaystyle f\backslash X(Y)=\frac{\sum_{Z\subseteq X}f(Y\cup Z)}{\sum_{Z\subseteq X}f(Z)}.

(2)

Note that
f/\emptyset=f
and, since
f(\emptyset)=1
,
\sum_{Z\subseteq\emptyset}f(Z)=f(\emptyset)=1
, so
f\backslash\emptyset=f
.

We write
f/e
instead of
f/\{e\}
when contracting by a single element
e\in E
, and likewise write
f\backslash{e}
for
f\backslash\{e\}
. Two binary functions
f
and
f^{\prime}
, with ground sets
E
and
E^{\prime}
respectively, are
isomorphic
if there is a bijection
\phi:E\to E^{\prime}
such that, for all
X\subseteq E
,
f(X)=f^{\prime}(\{\phi(x):x\in X\})
.

We define a
minor
of a binary function
f
to be a binary function obtained from
f
by a sequence of contractions and deletions of ground set elements. The deletion and contraction operations commute (see
(
Farr 2004
, Lemma 4)
) in the sense that, for any disjoint
X,Y\subseteq E
,
f\backslash X\backslash Y=f\backslash Y\backslash X
,
f/X/Y=f/Y/X
and
f\backslash X/Y=f/Y\backslash X
. A minor of a binary function
f
is
proper
if it has order less than the order of
f
.

When
\sum_{Z\subseteq X}f(Z)=0
the minor
f\backslash X
is undefined. We call a binary function
f

stable
if, for all disjoint
X,Y\subseteq E
,
f/X\backslash Y
is defined,
i.e.
for every
X\subseteq E
,
\sum_{Z\subseteq X}f(Z)\neq 0
.

A class of stable binary functions is
minor-closed
if it closed under taking minors.

An
excluded minor
of a minor-closed class
\mathcal{C}
of binary functions is a binary function
f\notin\mathcal{C}
, such that all proper minors of
f
are in
\mathcal{C}
. Equivalently,
f
is an excluded minor of
\mathcal{C}
if
f\notin\mathcal{C}
and, for every
e\in E
,
f\backslash{e},f/e\in\mathcal{C}
.

There is only one binary function of order zero, namely
f:\{\emptyset\}\to\mathbb{R}
where
f(\emptyset)=1
.

When
|E|=1
, we define
f_{\alpha}\colon 2^{E}\to\mathbb{R}
to be the unique (up to isomorphism) order-one binary function with
f_{\alpha}(E)=\alpha
.

3
Stable binary functions and rank

The first step to understanding minor-closed classes of binary functions is to understand stable binary functions. It turns out that this class is minor-closed.

Proposition 3.1
.

The stable binary functions are a minor-closed class.

Proof.

Let
g=f\backslash X/Y
be a minor of a stable binary function
f
with ground set
E
, and
X^{\prime}\subseteq E\backslash(X\cup Y)
. As
f
is stable, both
\sum_{Z\subseteq X}f(Z)
and
\sum_{Z\subseteq(X\cup X^{\prime})}f(Z)
are non-zero. We then have

\begin{array}[]{rc>{\displaystyle}l}0&\neq&\frac{\sum\limits_{Z\subseteq X\cup X^{\prime}}f(Z)}{\sum\limits_{Z\subseteq X}f(Z)}\\
&=&\frac{\sum\limits_{V\subseteq X^{\prime}}\sum\limits_{W\subseteq X}f(V\cup W)}{\sum\limits_{Z\subseteq X}f(Z)}\\
&=&\sum\limits_{V\subseteq X^{\prime}}f\backslash X(V)\\
&=&\sum\limits_{V\subseteq X^{\prime}}f\backslash X/Y(V)\\
&=&\sum\limits_{V\subseteq X^{\prime}}g(V).\\
\end{array}

Hence for any subset
X^{\prime}
of the ground set of
g
we have
\sum_{V\subseteq X^{\prime}}g(V)\neq 0
, and so
g
is stable. Therefore any minor of a stable binary function is stable.
∎

Since this class is closed under taking minors, we can characterise it in terms of its excluded minors.

Theorem 3.2
.

A binary function is stable if and only if it has no minor isomorphic to
f_{-1}
.

Proof.

(\Leftarrow)
As
f_{-1}(\emptyset)+f_{-1}(E)=1-1=0
, we have that
f_{-1}\backslash E
is not defined, so
f_{-1}
is not stable. Therefore, any binary function that has
f_{-1}
as a minor is not stable.

(\Rightarrow)
Let
f
be a binary function with ground set
E
that is not stable. Then there is some minimum size
X\subseteq E
such that
\sum_{Z\subseteq X}f(Z)=0
. Note that
X\neq\emptyset
, as
f(\emptyset)=1
.

Let
e\in X
, and consider the minor
g=f\backslash(X\backslash\{e\})/(E\backslash X)
. As
X
is minimal,
\sum_{Z\subseteq X\backslash\{e\}}f(Z)\neq 0
and so
g
is defined on ground set
\{e\}
. So we have

\begin{array}[]{rc>{\displaystyle}l}g(\emptyset)&=&\frac{\sum\limits_{Z\subseteq X\backslash\{e\}}f(Z)}{\sum\limits_{Z\subseteq X\backslash\{e\}}f(Z)}=1,\end{array}

and

\displaystyle g(\{e\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sum\limits_{Z\subseteq X\backslash\{e\}}f(Z\cup\{e\})}{\sum\limits_{Z\subseteq X\backslash\{e\}}f(Z)}

(3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sum\limits_{Z\subseteq X}f(Z)-\sum\limits_{Z\subseteq X\backslash\{e\}}f(Z)}{\sum\limits_{Z\subseteq X\backslash\{e\}}f(Z)}

(4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{0-\sum\limits_{Z\subseteq X\backslash\{e\}}f(Z)}{\sum\limits_{Z\subseteq X\backslash\{e\}}f(Z)}

(5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-1.

(6)

Therefore
g=f_{1}
is a minor of
f
. ∎

The
rank transform

Q
takes a binary function
f
to its
rank function
Qf\colon 2^{E}\to\mathbb{R}
, defined in
Farr 1993
by

Qf(X):=\log_{2}\left(\frac{\sum\limits_{Y\subseteq E}f(Y)}{\sum\limits_{Y\subseteq E\backslash X}f(Y)}\right).

(7)

In the case
X=E
, we have
Qf(E)=\log_{2}\left(\sum_{Y\subseteq E}f(Y)\right)
. Note that
Qf
can be undefined for some binary functions, as the fraction in (
7
) may be undefined or nonpositive. We say a binary function
f
is
rankable
if
Qf
exists. We remark from
(
Farr 1993
, p. 270)
that if
f
is rankable then
Qf(\emptyset)=0
and that for any function
\rho\colon 2^{E}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\rho(\emptyset)=0
there is a binary function
f
with
Qf=\rho
.

We will now determine some properties of rankable binary functions.

Proposition 3.3
.

A binary function
f
on ground set
E
is rankable if and only if, for each
X\subseteq E
,

\sum\limits_{Y\subseteq X}f(Y)>0.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
Suppose
f
is rankable. Then for each
X\subseteq E

\begin{array}[]{rc>{\displaystyle}l}Qf(E)-Qf(E\backslash X)&=&\log_{2}\left(\frac{\sum\limits_{Y\subseteq E}f(Y)}{f(\emptyset)}\right)-\log_{2}\left(\frac{\sum\limits_{Y\subseteq E}f(Y)}{\sum\limits_{Y\subseteq X}f(Y)}\right)\\
&=&\log_{2}\left({\sum\limits_{Y\subseteq X}f(Y)}\right)\end{array}

exists, and hence
\sum\limits_{Y\subseteq X}f(Y)>0
.

(\Leftarrow)
Suppose for each
X\subseteq E
that
\sum\limits_{Y\subseteq X}f(Y)>0
. Then, for any
Z\subset E
,

Qf(Z)=\log_{2}\left(\frac{\sum\limits_{Y\subseteq E}f(Y)}{\sum\limits_{Y\subseteq E\backslash Z}f(Y)}\right)

is defined, so
f
is rankable.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

If
f
is rankable, then it is stable.

Proof.

We prove the contrapositive.
Suppose
f\colon 2^{E}\to\mathbb{R}
is a non-stable binary function. By Theorem
3.2
,
f
has
f_{-1}
as a minor, and so
E
can be partitioned as
E=\{e\}\cup X\cup Z
such that
f\backslash{X}/Z=f_{-1}
(on ground set
\{e\}
). Hence we have

\begin{array}[]{rl}-1=f\backslash{X}/Z(\{e\})&=\dfrac{\sum_{Y\subseteq X}f(Y\cup\{e\})}{\sum_{Y\subseteq X}f(Y)}\\
&\\
&=\dfrac{\sum_{Y\subseteq X\cup\{e\}}f(Y)}{\sum_{Y\subseteq X}f(Y)}-1,\end{array}

and so
\sum_{Y\subseteq X\cup\{e\}}f(Y)=0
. But by Proposition
3.3
we have that
f
is not rankable.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

The class of rankable binary functions is minor-closed.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.4
, every rankable binary function is stable. We only have to show that rankable binary functions are closed under taking minors.

Let
f\colon 2^{E}\to\mathbb{R}
be a rankable binary function, and
e\in E
. Consider contraction. For all
X\subseteq E\backslash\{e\}
,

\sum\limits_{Y\subseteq(E\backslash\{e\})\backslash X}f/e(Y)=\sum\limits_{Y\subseteq E\backslash(X\cup\{e\})}f(Y)>0,

and so, by Proposition
3.3
,
f/e
is rankable .

Now consider deletion. We first note that
1+f(\{e\})=\sum_{Y\subseteq E\backslash(E\backslash\{e\})}f(Y)>0
, so
f\backslash e
exists. We have, for any
X\subseteq E\backslash\{e\}
,

\sum\limits_{Y\subseteq(E\backslash\{e\})\backslash X}f\backslash e(Y)=\sum\limits_{Y\subseteq E\backslash(X\cup\{e\})}\frac{f(Y)+f(Y\cup\{e\})}{1+f(\{e\})}=\frac{1}{1+f(\{e\})}\sum\limits_{Y\subseteq E\backslash X}f(Y)>0,

and so
f\backslash e
is rankable. Hence, contraction and deletion of a rankable binary function gives a rankable binary function.
∎

We can now find the excluded minors of this class.

Theorem 3.6
.

A binary function is rankable if and only if it has no minor isomorphic to
f_{\alpha}
for any
\alpha\leq-1
.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
Let
\alpha\leq 1
, and suppose that
f
has a
f_{\alpha}
as a minor. By Proposition
3.3
the rank function of
f_{\alpha}
is undefined, as
\sum_{Y\subseteq E}f(Y)=1+\alpha\leq 0
. As rankable binary functions are minor-closed by Proposition
3.5
,
f
is not rankable.

(\Leftarrow)
let
f\colon 2^{E}\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-rankable binary function. Suppose for contradiction that
f
has no
f_{\alpha}
minor where
\alpha\leq-1
. Furthermore, let us take
f
with
|E|
minimal. Therefore, all proper minors of
f
are rankable.

As
f
is not rankable, by Proposition
3.3
there is some
X\subseteq E
such that

\sum\limits_{Y\subseteq X}f(Y)\leq 0.

As
f(\emptyset)=1
, we must have
X\neq\emptyset
, so let
e\in X
. Consider
f\backslash{e}
, which is rankable. By Proposition
3.3
, we have

0<\sum\limits_{Y\subseteq X\backslash\{e\}}f\backslash{e}(Y)=\sum\limits_{Y\subseteq X\backslash\{e\}}\frac{f(Y)+f(Y\cup\{e\})}{f(\emptyset)+f(\{e\})}=\frac{1}{1+f(\{e\})}\sum\limits_{Y\subseteq X}f(Y).

Hence
1+f(\{e\})<0
, and so
f/(E\backslash\{e\})\cong f_{\alpha}
, for some
\alpha=f(\{e\})<-1
. Therefore, if
f
is not rankable, it must have
f_{\alpha}
as a minor for some
\alpha\leq 1
.
∎

Rankable binary functions are of particular interest, as the deletion and contraction operations of binary functions correspond, via the rank transform, to the rank deletion and contraction operations of matroids. From
(
Farr 1993
, Lemma 4.3)
we have the following result.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
f\colon 2^{E}\to\mathbb{R}
be a rankable binary function, and let
X\subseteq E
and
Y\subseteq E\backslash X
. Then

\begin{array}[]{l}Q(f/X)(Y)=Qf(Y\cup X)-Qf(X),\\
Q(f\backslash X)(Y)=Qf(Y).\end{array}

Note here that deletion and contraction for these rank functions are the same operations as those used for rank functions of matroids and polymatroids.

4
Indicator functions of binary linear spaces

A binary function is
straight
if it is the indicator function of a binary vector space. A binary function
f
is straight if and only if
f
is
\{0,1\}
-valued and, for all
X,Y\subseteq E
, if
f(X)=f(Y)=1
then
f(X\Delta Y)=1
. A binary function is
crooked
if it is not straight.

When a binary function
f
is the indicator function of a binary vector space then
Qf
is the rank function of the binary matroid with that cocircuit space, see

(
Farr 1993
, Cor. 2.2)
and
(
Farr 2007b
, §6)
.
Conversely, for every binary matroid
M=(E,\rho)
, there is a straight binary function – namely, the indicator function of the cocircuit space of
M
– such that
\rho=Qf
. This relationship between the rank and the indicator function of the cocircuit space of a binary matroid was the motivation behind the introduction of binary functions and the rank transform
Q
by
Farr 1993
.

Deletion and contraction operations on binary functions were shown to correspond to matroid deletion and contraction in Proposition
3.7
. As binary matroids are closed under taking minors, the straight binary functions are a minor-closed class.

We have the following result on minors of crooked binary functions, which will be useful in finding the excluded minors of straight binary functions.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f
be a
\{0,1\}
-valued binary function. If
f
is crooked, then it has a crooked proper minor.

Proof.

As
f
is crooked, there is some
X,Y\subseteq E
such that
f(X)=f(Y)=1\text{, and }f(X\Delta Y)=0
. Let us choose such
X
and
Y
with minimum union.

Since
f(\emptyset)=1
,
X
and
Y
are distinct, so without loss of generality take
e\in X\backslash Y
. We have

\displaystyle f\backslash e(X\backslash\{e\})=\frac{1+f(X\backslash\{e\})}{1+f(\{e\})},

(8)

\displaystyle f\backslash e(Y)=\frac{1+f(Y\cup\{e\})}{1+f(\{e\})},

(9)

\displaystyle f\backslash e((X\backslash\{e\})\Delta Y)=\frac{f((X\backslash\{e\})\Delta Y)}{1+f(\{e\})}.

(10)

Suppose that
f(X\backslash\{e\})=1
. As
f(Y)=1
, and
|(X\backslash\{e\})\cup Y|<|X\cup Y|
, then by the minimality of
|X\cup Y|
, we have
f((X\backslash\{e\})\Delta Y)=1
. By (
8
) and (
10
) we have
f\backslash e(X\backslash\{e\})=2/(1+f(\{e\}))
and
f\backslash e((X\backslash\{e\})\Delta Y)=1/(1+f(\{e\}))
respectively. However, there is no
f(\{e\})\in\{0,1\}
such that
f\backslash e
is
\{0,1\}
-valued, and so
f\backslash e
is crooked.

Let us now consider the case where
f(X\backslash\{e\})=0
. Suppose for contradiction that
f\backslash e
is straight.

As
f\backslash e
is
\{0,1\}
-valued,
f(\{e\})=0
by (
8
), as
f(X\backslash\{e\})=0
. This in turn implies that
f(Y\cup\{e\})=0
by (
9
).

So by (
8
) and (
9
),
f\backslash e(X\backslash\{e\})=f\backslash e(Y)=1
, and so
f\backslash e((X\backslash\{e\})\Delta Y)=1
as
f\backslash e
is straight. Hence by (
10
)
f((X\Delta Y)\backslash\{e\})=1
.

We have
f(\left(X\backslash\{e\}\right)\Delta Y)=f(Y)=1
, and
|\left((X\backslash\{e\})\Delta Y\right)\cup Y|<|X\cup Y|
, and so by the minimality of
X
and
Y

we have
f(X\backslash\{e\})=1
, which is a contradiction. Therefore
f\backslash e
is crooked.
∎

Theorem
4.1
implies that any excluded minor for the class of straight binary functions is not
\{0,1\}
-valued. We now find the excluded minors for the straight binary functions.

Theorem 4.2
.

A binary function is straight if and only if it has no minor isomorphic to any of:

•

f_{\alpha}
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0,1\}
,

•

g\colon 2^{\{a,b\}}\to\mathbb{R}
, where

g(X)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}1&|X|=0,1\\
-1&|X|=2\end{array}\right.,\text{ and}

•

f_{\mathcal{U}_{2,4}}\colon 2^{\{a,b,c,d\}}\to\mathbb{R}
, where

f_{\mathcal{U}_{2,4}}(X)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}0&|X|=1,2\\
1&|X|=0,3\\
-1&|X|=4.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

There is only one binary function of order 0, and it is straight. Therefore, all crooked binary functions of order 1 will be excluded minors for the class of straight binary functions.

A binary function of order 1,
f_{\alpha}
, is straight only if and only if
\alpha\in\{0,1\}
, and so
f_{\alpha}
is an excluded minor for the class of straight binary functions for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\backslash{\{0,1\}}
.

We will now let
f
be an excluded minor for the class of straight binary functions of order at least two. By Proposition
4.1
,
f
is not
\{0,1\}
-valued. For any
X\subset E
,
f/(E\backslash X)
is a proper minor of
f
, and so it is straight. So
f(X)=f/(E\backslash X)(X)\in\{0,1\}
for all
X\subset E
. As
f
is not
\{0,1\}
-valued, we must have
f(E)\notin\{0,1\}
.

Let
e\in E
. Then

f\backslash e(E\backslash\{e\})=\frac{f(E\backslash\{e\})+f(E)}{1+f(\{e\})}.

(11)

Since
f\backslash e(E\backslash\{e\}),f(E\backslash\{e\}),f({e})\in\{0,1\}
, and
f(E)\notin\{0,1\}
, we must have
f(E)\in\{-1,2\}
by (
11
).

Case 1:
Suppose
f(E)=2
. By (
11
), we have
f(\{e\})=1
and
f(E\backslash\{e\})=0
for all
e\in E
. Take distinct
d,e\in E
, and consider the straight binary function
f\backslash e
. We have

\displaystyle f\backslash e(E\backslash\{e\})=\frac{f(E\backslash\{e\})+f(E)}{1+f(\{e\})}=\frac{0+2}{1+1}=1,

(12)

\displaystyle f\backslash e(\{d\})=\frac{f(\{d\})+f(\{d,e\})}{1+f(\{e\})}=\frac{1+f(\{d,e\})}{2},

(13)

\displaystyle f\backslash e(E\backslash\{d,e\})=\frac{f(E\backslash\{d,e\})+f(E\backslash\{d\})}{1+f(\{e\})}=\frac{f(E\backslash\{d,e\})+0}{2}.

(14)

As
f\backslash e(\{d\})\in\{0,1\}
, and
f(E)=2
, we must have
|E|\geq 3
by (
13
).
We must then have
f(\{d,e\})\in\{0,1\}
, as
\{d,e\}\subset E
, and so
f\backslash e(\{d\})=1
by (
13
). Likewise,
f\backslash e(E\backslash\{d,e\})=0
by (
14
), as
f(E\backslash\{d,e\})\in\{0,1\}
.

Using (
12
), we have
f\backslash e(E\backslash\{e\})=f\backslash e(\{d\})=1
, and
f\backslash e((E\backslash\{e\})\Delta\{d\})=f\backslash e(E\backslash\{d,e\})=0
. Hence
f\backslash e
is crooked, which is a contradiction. So
f
is not an excluded minor for the straight binary functions if
f(E)=2
.

Case 2:
Suppose then that
f(E)=-1
. Then for all
e\in E
,
f(E\backslash\{e\})=1
by (
11
), as
f\backslash e(E\backslash\{e\})\in\{0,1\}
. As
|E|\geq 2
we have, for any distinct
d,e\in E
,

f\backslash e(E\backslash\{d,e\})=\frac{f(E\backslash\{d,e\})+1}{1+f(\{e\})}.

(15)

As
f\backslash{e}
is straight,
f\backslash e(E\backslash\{d,e\})\in\{0,1\}
, and so
f(E\backslash\{d,e\})=f(\{e\})
, for any distinct
d,e\in E
. Hence if
f(E)=-1
, then
f(X)
is constant for all sets
X\subseteq E
of size 1 or
|E|-2
. This constant is either 0 or 1.

Subcase 2.1:
Suppose that
f(X)=1
for all
X\subseteq E
of size 1 or
|E|-2
.
If
|E|=2
, then there is a unique binary function
g
satisfying these conditions, namely

g(X)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}1,&|X|=0,1,\\
-1,&|X|=2.\end{array}\right.

Deletion and contraction of any element gives a straight binary function, and so
g
is an excluded minor for the class of straight binary functions.

For
|E|\geq 3
, take distinct
d,e\in E
. We have

\displaystyle f\backslash e(E\backslash\{d,e\})=\frac{f(E\backslash\{d,e\})+f(E\backslash\{e\})}{1+f(\{e\})}=\frac{1+1}{1+1}=1,

(16)

\displaystyle f\backslash e(\{d\})=\frac{f(\{d\})+f(\{d,e\})}{1+f(\{e\})}=\frac{1+f(\{d,e\})}{1+1},

(17)

\displaystyle f\backslash e(E\backslash\{e\})=\frac{f(E\backslash\{e\})+f(E)}{1+f(\{e\})}=\frac{1-1}{1+1}=0.

(18)

As
|E|\geq 3
,
\{d,e\}\neq E
and so
f(\{d,e\})\in\{0,1\}
. Therefore, as
f\backslash{e}
is
\{0,1\}
-valued,
f(\{d,e\})=1
and
f\backslash{e}(\{d\})=1
by (
17
).
But then
f\backslash{e}
is not straight, as
f\backslash e(E\backslash\{d,e\})=f\backslash e(\{d\})=1
, but
f\backslash e(E\backslash\{e\})=0
. Hence
f
is not an excluded minor for the class of straight binary functions.

Subcase 2.2:
Suppose that
f(X)=0
for all
X\subseteq E
of size 1 or
|E|-2
.

Note, there is no binary function of order 2 which satisfies this condition. For
|E|\geq 3
, take any distinct
d,e\in E
. We have

\displaystyle f\backslash e(E\backslash\{e\})=\frac{f(E\backslash\{e\})+f(E)}{1+f(\{e\})}=f(E\backslash\{e\})-1,

(19)

\displaystyle f\backslash e(E\backslash\{d,e\})=\frac{f(E\backslash\{d,e\})+f(E\backslash\{e\})}{1+f(\{e\})}=f(E\backslash\{e\}),

(20)

\displaystyle f\backslash e(\{d\})=\frac{f(\{d\})+f(\{d,e\})}{1+f(\{e\})}=f(\{d,e\}).

(21)

As
f\backslash{e}
is
\{0,1\}
-valued and
f(E\backslash\{e\})=1
, we must have
f\backslash{e}(E\backslash\{e\})=0
for all
e\in E
by (
19
). Therefore
f\backslash e(E\backslash\{d,e\})=1
by (
20
). As
f\backslash e
is straight, we must have
f\backslash e(\{d\})=f(\{d,e\})=0
by (
21
). Combining these results gives the following evaluations on
f

f(X)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}0,&|X|=1,2,|E|-2,\\
1,&|X|=0,|E|-1,\\
-1,&\phantom{|}X\phantom{|}=E.\end{array}\right.

If
|E|=3
, then no such function satisfies these conditions. If
|E|=4
, these conditions define a unique (up to isomorphism) binary function, which we will call
f_{\mathcal{U}_{2,4}}
. It is straightforward to confirm by (
1
) and (
2
) that deletion or contraction of any element from
f_{\mathcal{U}_{2,4}}
is straight. Hence
f_{\mathcal{U}_{2,4}}
is an excluded minor for the class of straight binary functions.

Finally, let us suppose that
|E|\geq 5
. Take distinct
a,b,c,d\in E
.

By (
20
) and (
21
) we have

\displaystyle f\backslash d(\{c\})=0,

(22)

\displaystyle f\backslash d(E\backslash\{a,d\})=1,

(23)

\displaystyle f\backslash d(E\backslash\{b,d\})=1.

(24)

As
f\backslash d
is straight and
f\backslash d(E\backslash\{a,d\})=f\backslash d(E\backslash\{b,d\})=1
, then
f\backslash d(\{a,b\})=1
, and so

1=f\backslash d(\{a,b\})=\frac{f(\{a,b\})+f(\{a,b,d\})}{1+f(\{d\})}=f(\{a,b,d\}).

(25)

We have
f\backslash c(\{a,b\})=1
by (
25
) and
f\backslash c(\{d\})=0
by (
22
), so
f\backslash c(\{a,b,d\})=0
as
f\backslash{c}
is straight. But,

0=f\backslash c(\{a,b,d\})=\frac{f(\{a,b,d\})+f(\{a,b,c,d\})}{f(\emptyset)+f(\{c\})}=\frac{1+f(\{a,b,c,d\})}{1+0},

and so
f(\{a,b,c,d\})=-1
. But as
|E|\geq 5
, we have
f(\{a,b,c,d\})\in\{0,1\}
. Therefore, no other excluded minors exist.
∎

5
Matroids and polymatroids

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, a binary function
f\colon 2^{E}\to\mathbb{R}
is
k
-polymatroidal
if
f
is rankable and
Qf\colon 2^{E}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the following:

R0)

\forall X\subseteq E,~Qf(X)\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},

R1)

\forall X\subseteq E,~Qf(X)\leq k|X|,

R2)

\forall X\subseteq E,\forall e\in E\backslash X,~Qf(X)\leq Qf(X\cup\{e\}),

R3)

\forall X\subseteq E,~\forall a,b\in E\backslash X\text{ and }a\neq b,~Qf(X)+Qf(X\cup\{a,b\})\leq Qf(X\cup\{a\})+Qf(X\cup\{b\}).

A function satisfying
R0
–
R3
is the rank function of a
k
-polymatroid (see, for example,
Oxley and Whittle 1993
), so
k
-polymatroidal binary functions are those with
k
-polymatroid rank functions. Let
\mathcal{M}_{k}
denote the class of
k
-polymatroidal binary functions.

Note that the deletion and contraction operations for polymatroid rank functions are the same as matroid deletion and contraction, and it is well known (cf.
Bonin and Chun 2020
) that
k
-polymatroid rank functions are minor-closed. Combined with Proposition
3.7
, this gives the following.

Remark 5.1
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathcal{M}_{k}
is closed under taking minors.

We can now determine the excluded minors of
\mathcal{M}_{k}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

A binary function is
k
-polymatroidal if and only if it has no minor isomorphic to any of:

•

f_{\alpha}
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0,1,3,\ldots,2^{k}-1\}
, and

•

f_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\colon 2^{\{a,b\}}\to\mathbb{R}
, where
0\leq\alpha\leq\beta\leq\gamma\leq k-1
are integers and

\left.\begin{array}[]{lcl}f_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(\emptyset)&=&1,\\
f_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(\{a\})&=&2^{\alpha+\gamma-\beta+1}-1,\\
f_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(\{b\})&=&2^{\gamma+1}-1,\\
f_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(\{a,b\})&=&2^{\alpha+\gamma+1}-2^{\gamma+1}-2^{\alpha+\gamma-\beta+1}+1.\\
\end{array}\right.

Proof.

We first consider
k
-polymatroidal binary functions of order 1. As the unique binary function of order 0 is
k
-polymatroidal, any order 1 binary function
f
is an excluded minor for
\mathcal{M}_{k}
if and only if
f\notin\mathcal{M}_{k}
. Again, we write
f_{\alpha}
as the binary function of order 1 where
f_{\alpha}(E)=\alpha
, which is unique up to isomorphism. We then have, where defined, that
Qf_{\alpha}(\emptyset)=0
and
Qf_{\alpha}(E)=\log_{2}(1+\alpha)
.

By Theorem
3.6
,
f_{\alpha}
is not rankable if
\alpha\leq-1
.

If
\alpha
is not of the form
2^{n}-1
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, then
Qf_{\alpha}(E)=\log_{2}(1+\alpha)\notin\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and so
Qf
does not satisfy
R0
.

If
\alpha>2^{k}-1
, then
Qf_{\alpha}
does not satisfy
R1
as
Qf_{\alpha}(E)>\log_{2}(1+(2^{k}-1))=k
.

If
-1<\alpha<0
, then
Qf_{\alpha}(E)=\log_{2}(1+\alpha)<0=Qf_{\alpha}(\emptyset)
, so
Qf_{\alpha}
does not satisfy
R2
.

As
Qf_{\alpha}
has order one it trivially satisfies
R3
.

Hence
f_{\alpha}
is
k
-polymatroidal only if
\alpha\in\{0,1,3,\ldots,2^{k}-1\}
. Finally, it is straightforward to check that
f_{\alpha}
is
k
-polymatroidal for each
\alpha\in\{0,1,3,\ldots,2^{k}-1\}
.

Hence the excluded minors of
\mathcal{M}_{k}
of order one are
f_{\alpha}
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0,1,3,\ldots,2^{k}-1\}
.

We now consider excluded minors
f
of order at least two.

By Theorem
3.6
, every binary function of order at least two which is not rankable has a proper minor that is not rankable. Hence any excluded minor of
\mathcal{M}_{k}
of order at least two is rankable.

Let
f
be an excluded minor of
\mathcal{M}_{k}
of order at least 2. Then
Qf
does not satisfy one of
R0
,
R1
,
R2
or
R3
.

Suppose
Qf
does not satisfy
R0
. Then there is some
X\subseteq E
such that
Qf(X)\notin\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.

As
Qf(\emptyset)=0
, we have that
X
is nonempty. Take
e\in X
, and as
f/e\in\mathcal{M}_{k}
, we know
Q(f/e)(X\backslash\{e\})\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. But
Q(f/e)(X\backslash\{e\})=Qf(X)-Qf(\{e\})
, and so
Qf(\{e\})\notin\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. But, as
|E|\geq 2
, there is some
d\in E\backslash\{e\}
, and
Q(f\backslash d)(\{e\})=Qf(\{e\})\notin\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, and so
f\backslash d\notin\mathcal{M}_{k}
.

Hence
Qf
must satisfy
R0
.

Suppose now that
Qf
does not satisfy
R1
. Then there exists
X\subseteq E
such that
Qf(X)>k|X|
.

Clearly,
X\neq\emptyset
, so let
e\in X
. Consider
f\backslash(E\backslash\{e\})\in\mathcal{M}_{k}
, which is a proper minor of
f
, and so
Qf(\{e\})=Q(f\backslash(E\backslash\{e\}))(\{e\})\leq k
.

But then
Q(f/e)(X\backslash\{e\})=Qf(X)-Qf(\{e\})>k|X|-k=k|X\backslash\{e\}|
, and so
f/e\notin\mathcal{M}_{k}
.

Hence
Qf
must satisfy
R1
.

Suppose now that
Qf
does not satisfy
R2
. That is, there exists
X\subset E
and
e\in E\backslash X
such that
Qf(X)>Qf(X\cup\{e\})
. Then we have
Q(f/X)(\{e\})=Qf(X\cup\{e\})-Qf(X)<0
, and so
f/X\notin\mathcal{M}_{k}
. But since
Qf
satisfies
R0
and
R1
,
X\neq\emptyset
. Hence
f/X
is a proper minor of
f
, so
f
is not an excluded minor of
\mathcal{M}_{k}
.

Hence
Qf
must satisfy
R2
.

Finally, suppose now that
Qf
does not satisfy
R3
. Therefore there exist distinct
a,b\in E
and
X\subseteq E\backslash\{a,b\}
such that

Qf(X)+Qf(X\cup\{a,b\})>Qf(X\cup\{a\})+Qf(X\cup\{b\}).

(26)

Suppose there is some
c\in X
. Then, as
f/c\in\mathcal{M}_{k}
and satisfies
R3
,

Q(f/c)(X\backslash\{c\})+Q(f/c)(X\backslash\{c\}\cup\{a,b\})\leq Q(f/c)((X\backslash\{c\})\cup\{a\})+Q(f/c)((X\backslash\{c\})\cup\{b\}).

This can be written as

Qf(X)+Qf(X\cup\{a,b\})-2Qf(\{c\})\leq Qf(X\cup\{a\})+Qf(X\cup\{b\})-2Qf(\{c\}),

which is equivalent to

Qf(X)+Qf(X\cup\{a,b\})\leq Qf(X\cup\{a\})+Qf(X\cup\{b\}).

This contradicts (
26
), and so we must have
X=\emptyset
, and (
26
) becomes

Qf(\emptyset)+Qf(\{a,b\})>Qf(\{a\})+Qf(\{b\}).

(27)

Suppose
c\in E\backslash{\{a,b\}}
. Then, as
f\backslash c\in\mathcal{M}_{k}
and satisfies
R3
,

Q(f\backslash c)(\emptyset)+Q(f\backslash c)(\{a,b\})\leq Q(f\backslash c)(\{a\})+Q(f\backslash c)(\{b\}),

which is equivalent to

Qf(\emptyset)+Qf(\{a,b\})\leq Qf(\{a\})+Qf(\{b\}).

This contradicts (
27
), and so
E\backslash\{a,b\}
is empty. Therefore,
f
must have order 2,
i.e.

E=\{a,b\}
, and

Qf(\{a,b\})>Qf(\{a\})+Qf(\{b\}).

(28)

We now know that our excluded minor
f
must have order 2 and its rank function must satisfy
R0
,
R1
,
R2
and (
28
). As
Qf
satisfies (
28
), we know
f\notin\mathcal{M}_{k}
, so it remains to consider the assumption that all proper minors of
f
are
k
-polymatroidal.

We know that
Q(f\backslash a)(\{b\})=Qf(\{b\})
is a nonnegative integer at most
k
as
Qf
satisfies
R0
,
R1
and
R2
. As
Q(f\backslash a)
has order one, it trivially satisfies
R3
. Hence,
f\backslash a\in\mathcal{M}_{k}
, and similarly
f\backslash b\in\mathcal{M}_{k}
.

We know that
Q(f/a)(\{b\})=Qf(\{a,b\})-Qf(\{a\})
is a nonnegative integer as
Qf
satisfies
R0
,
R1
and
R2
. Hence,
Q(f/a)
satisfies
R0
and
R2
. As
Q(f/a)
has order one, it trivially satisfies
R3
. We must then add the restriction that
Qf(\{a,b\})-Qf(\{a\})\leq k
in order that
Q(f/a)
satisfies
R1
and so
f/a\in\mathcal{M}_{k}
. Similarly, we have
Qf(\{a,b\})-Qf(\{b\})\leq k
as
f/b\in\mathcal{M}_{k}
.

Without loss of generality, let
Qf(\{a\})\leq Qf(\{b\})
. The necessary and sufficient conditions for
f
are

f\colon 2^{\{a,b\}}\to\mathbb{R},\text{ where }\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}Qf(\{a\}),Qf(\{b\}),Qf(\{a,b\})\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},\\
Qf(\{a\})\leq Qf(\{b\})\leq k,\\
Qf(\{a\})+Qf(\{b\})<Qf(\{a,b\})\leq k+Qf(\{a\}).\end{array}\right.

Setting
Qf(\{a\})=\alpha,Qf(\{b\})=\beta,
and
Qf(\{a,b\})=\alpha+\gamma+1
, these conditions are equivalent to
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
0\leq\alpha\leq\beta\leq\gamma\leq k-1
. So the excluded minors of
\mathcal{M}_{k}
of order two are of the form

\left.\begin{array}[]{lcl}f_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(\emptyset)&=&1,\\
f_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(\{a\})&=&2^{\alpha+\gamma-\beta+1}-1,\\
f_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(\{b\})&=&2^{\gamma+1}-1,\\
f_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(\{a,b\})&=&2^{\alpha+\gamma+1}-2^{\gamma+1}-2^{\alpha+\gamma-\beta+1}+1.\\
\end{array}\right.

∎

It is perhaps surprising that the excluded minors of
k
-polymatroidal binary functions have such a relatively simple structure. While there are an infinite number of excluded minors of order one, there are only
\binom{k+2}{3}
excluded minors of
\mathcal{M}_{k}
of order two.

Polymatroids and
k
-polymatroid rank functions are a generalisation of the matroid rank functions, which are the
1
-polymatroids. The above theorem can be applied to give the following excluded minor characterisation of binary functions with corresponding matroid rank functions.

Corollary 5.3
.

A binary function is 1-polymatroidal (
matroidal
) if and only if it has no minor isomorphic to any of:

•

f_{\alpha}
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0,1\}
, and

•

f\colon 2^{\{a,b\}}\to\mathbb{R}
, where

f(X)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}1,&|X|=0,1,\\
-1,&|X|=2.\end{array}\right.

Theorem
4.2
gave an excluded minor characterisation of the straight binary functions. As each straight binary function is matroidal, combining this theorem with Corollary
5.3
give the excluded minor characterisation of straight binary functions within the class of matroidal binary functions.

Theorem 5.4
.

A matroidal binary function is straight if and only if it has no minor isomorphic to
f_{\mathcal{U}_{2,4}}\colon 2^{\{a,b,c,d\}}\to\mathbb{R}
, where

f_{\mathcal{U}_{2,4}}(X)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}0,&|X|=1,2,\\
1,&|X|=0,3,\\
-1,&|X|=4.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Follows from Theorem
4.2
and Corollary
5.3
.
∎

The rank functions of the straight binary functions are the binary matroid rank functions, so this result is equivalent to an excluded minor characterisation of binary matroids within the class of matroids.
Here,
Qf_{\mathcal{U}_{2,4}}
is the rank function of the uniform matroid
{\mathcal{U}_{2,4}}
, and so Theorem
5.4
is equivalent to a classical result in structural matroid theory by
Tutte 1958a
;
Tutte 1958b
.

Theorem 5.5

(Tutte, 1958)
.

A matroid is binary if and only if it has no minor isomorphic to
{\mathcal{U}_{2,4}}
.

6
Further work

This paper lays the groundwork for using binary functions as a tool to investigate minor-closed classes of a number of types of structures.

Binary functions can be used to find new or shorter proofs for excluded minor characterisations of classes of graphs, matroids and other types of objects. In this paper we found a new proof of the characterisation of binary matroids among matroids. One candidate for further investigation are the binary polymatroids, which generalise the binary matroids. It is known from
Huynh 2010
and
Oxley et al. 2016
that the set of excluded minors of the binary polymatroids among polymatroids is infinite, but they have not been fully described. If the binary functions that represent binary polymatroids have a simple characterisation, then these could be used to investigate the class of binary polymatroids.

Binary functions admit an infinite number of minor operations by
Farr 2004
;
Farr 2007a
, and one could investigate classes of binary functions closed under minor operations other than deletion and contraction.

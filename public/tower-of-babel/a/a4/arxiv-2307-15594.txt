2.
Recent extensions of the classical GNS inequalities

2.1.
Historical remarks

The classical Sobolev inequalities from the 30’s of the last century which became the GNS inequalities in the 60’s of the same century have been improved in many directions. The purpose of this paper is to survey some recent improvements and extensions obtained by the authors in
[
HMP
]
. We begin by outlining the history of the GNS estimates in its different incarnations related to the context of
L^{p}
spaces and in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geq 2

(see the recent treatment
[
KLV
]
Section 3.2.).

2.1.1.
The origins

Our starting point is commonly referred to as the GNS inequalities which are ubiquitous in harmonic analysis and P.D.E. Hereafter, we will use the notation of
p^{*}=\frac{np}{n-p}
for the Sobolev exponent, often expressed in the following way

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{*}}=\frac{1}{n}.

Theorem A
.

(The classical GNS). Let
1\leq p<n
. Then

\|f\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c\,\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\quad f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(1)

One common approach to proving (
1
), typically only when
1<p<n
, is via the use of the well-known pointwise estimate (see
[
Sal
,
St
,
KLV
]
)

|f(x)|\leq c\,I_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x)

(2)

combined with the boundedness (a well-known classical result)

I_{1}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

(3)

for
p\in(1,n)
.
I_{1}
is the Riesz potential operator of order
{\alpha}=1
given by

I_{\alpha}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{n-{\alpha}}}\,dy,\quad 0<{\alpha}<n.

At the endpoint
p=1
and
1^{*}=\frac{n}{n-1}=n^{\prime}
,
I_{1}
only satisfies the weak endpoint boundedness

I_{1}:L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

yet the strong inequality (
1
) still holds in this case (more on this below).

2.1.2.
The 70’s, the influence of Muckenhoupt and Wheeden

A relevant extension of (
3
) and hence of (
1
) in the case
p>1
was obtained by B. Muckenhoupt and R. Wheeden
[
MW
]
.
They showed in this paper that
I_{1}
satisfies weighted bounds of the form

\|wI_{1}f\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c\,\|wf\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(4)

if and only if
w\in A_{p,p^{*}}
namely

[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}=\sup_{Q}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}w^{p^{*}}\right)\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}w^{-p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{p^{*}}{p^{\prime}}}<\infty.

Again, at the endpoint
p=1
only the weak boundedness holds, and Muckenhoupt and Wheeden proved that

\|I_{1}f\|_{L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(w^{n^{\prime}})}\leq c\,\|wf\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(5)

holds if and only if
w\in A_{1,n^{\prime}}
, i.e.

\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}w^{n^{\prime}}\right)\leq c\inf_{Q}(w^{n^{\prime}})

where the smallest constant
c
is denoted as
[w]_{A_{1,n^{\prime}}}
.

Combining (
2
) and (
4
) yields the following weighted GNS inequality.

Theorem B
.

(The weighted GNS) Let
1\leq p<n
and let
w\in A_{p,p^{*}}
. Then

\|wf\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c\,\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\qquad f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(6)

When
p=1
and
w\in A_{1,n^{\prime}}
, the operator
I_{1}
only satisfies a weak-type inequality, yet the strong bound holds for the weighted GNS as we will detail in the next section.

2.1.3.
The influence of modern weighted theory in harmonic analysis

Besides its dependence on the dimension and
p
, the constant
c
in (
6
) also depends on the constant
[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}
, and this relationship is of interest.

The sharp weighted bound for
I_{1}
was proven in
[
LMPT
]

\|wI_{1}f\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c_{p}\,[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}\max\{1,\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{*}}\}}\,\|wf\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},1<p<n,

(7)

which leads to the following quantitative weighted GNS inequality,

\|wf\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c_{p}\,[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}\max\{1,\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{*}}\}}\,\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\quad 1<p<n.

(8)

Inequality (
8
) can be improved and the sharp version of the GNS inequalities is contained in the next theorem which can also be found in
[
LMPT
]
.

Theorem C
.

(The precise weighted GNS) Let
1\leq p<n
and let
w\in A_{p,p^{*}}
. Then

\|wf\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c_{p}\,[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\,\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\quad 1\leq p<n.

(9)

Notice that inequality (
9
) improves (
8
)
in the exponent and holds at the endpoint
p=1
. In fact, it is the endpoint
p=1
that is crucial in proving (
9
).
We now briefly explain how to obtain the sharp inequality (
9
)
by using the theory of extrapolation of weights. First, using the pointwise inequality (
2
), we claim the weak type endpoint inequality for
p=1
and
w\in A_{1,n^{\prime}}
,

\|f\|_{L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(w^{n^{\prime}})}\leq c\,[w]_{A_{1,n^{\prime}}}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\,\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Given any measure
\mu
, the quantity
\|g\|_{L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(\mu)}
is equivalent to a norm since
n^{\prime}>1
.
Since
I_{1}
is a convolution operator with the kernel
k(x)=|x|^{1-n}
, we may use the Marcinkiewicz integral inequality for normed spaces to obtain

\displaystyle\|I_{1}f\|_{L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(\mu)}=\|k*f\|_{L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(y)|\big\||\cdot-y|^{1-n}\big\|_{L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(\mu)}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(y)|M\mu(y)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\,dy

where we have used the the fact that
\big\||\cdot-y|^{1-n}\big\|_{L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(\mu)}\approx M\mu(y)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}},
with
M
being the usual Hardy-Littlewood maximal function.
Thus
I_{1}
satisfies the weak bound

\|I_{1}f\|_{L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(w^{n^{\prime}})}\leq c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f|M(w^{n^{\prime}})^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\leq c\,[w]_{A_{1,n^{\prime}}}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\,\|wf\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(10)

Inequality (
10
) now implies

\|I_{1}f\|_{L^{p^{*},\infty}(w^{p^{*}})}\leq c_{p}\,[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\,\|wf\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\quad 1\leq p<n,

(11)

by the sharp extrapolation theorem from
[
LMPT
, Theorem 2.1]
which is based on the off-diagonal extrapolation theorem by
Harboure, Macías, and Segovia
[
HMS
]
. Inequality (
11
) now implies the weak Sobolev inequality

\|f\|_{L^{p^{*},\infty}(w^{p^{*}})}\leq c\|I_{1}(|\nabla f|)\|_{L^{p^{*},\infty}(w^{p^{*}})}\leq c_{p}[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(12)

The truncation method of Maz’ya
[
Maz
]
(see also the survey article
[
Haj
]
) applied to (
12
) now implies the strong inequality with the same dependence on
[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}
, namely, inequality (
9
).

The sharpness of (
9
) follows from the following example. Let

f_{\delta}(x)=\exp(-|x|^{\delta})\quad\text{and}\quad w_{\delta}(x)=|x|^{\frac{\delta-n}{p^{*}}}

where
\delta\in(0,1)
. Then,

\|w_{\delta}f_{\delta}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\approx\delta^{-\frac{1}{p^{*}}},[w_{\delta}]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}\approx\delta^{-1},\ \ \text{and}\ \|w_{\delta}\nabla f_{\delta}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\approx\delta^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

showing that the power
\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}
in (
9
) cannot be improved by taking
\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}
.

2.2.
The toy motivation: the maximal function case

We are interested in extending inequality (
9
) to an inequality with an operator on the left hand side. We begin with the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function defined by

Mf(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}|f(y)|\,dy.

The classical
A_{p}
theorem of Muckenhoupt showed that

M:L^{p}(w)\rightarrow L^{p}(w)

if and only if
w\in A_{p}
, i.e.

[w]_{A_{p}}=\sup_{Q}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}w\right)\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}w^{1-p^{\prime}}\right)^{p-1}<\infty.

There is an intimate connection between the
A_{p}
and
A_{p,p^{*}}
classes which will be very useful in what follows, namely

w\in A_{p,p^{*}}\ \ \mbox{if and only if}\ \ w^{p^{*}}\in A_{\frac{p^{*}}{n^{\prime}}}

and

[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}=[w^{p^{*}}]_{A_{\frac{p^{*}}{n^{\prime}}}}.

Hence, if
w\in A_{p,p^{*}}
, then the weight
w^{p^{*}}
belongs to
A_{p^{*}}
since
A_{\frac{p^{*}}{n^{\prime}}}\subset A_{p^{*}}
.

By Muckenhoupt’s theorem we obtain the following Sobolev inequality

\|wMf\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c\,\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

(13)

which is an extension of (
6
) since
Mf\geq|f|
, a.e. by the Lebesgue differentiation theorem.

Consider the problem of finding the dependence on
[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}
of the constant
c
in inequality (
13
). One approach is to use Buckley’s sharp bound in
[
Buc
]
which yields

\|Mf\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq cp^{\prime}\,[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}.

(14)

This inequality leads to the following calculation,

\|wMf\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|Mf\|_{L^{p^{*}}(w^{p^{*}})}\leq c_{p}\,[w^{p^{*}}]_{A_{p^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{p^{*}-1}}\,\|f\|_{L^{p^{*}}(w^{p^{*}})}\\
\leq c_{p}\,[w^{p^{*}}]_{A_{p^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{p^{*}-1}}\,[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\,\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}{\leq\,}c_{p}\,[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{p^{*}-1}+\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

using (
9
). We may improve these estimates by using the fact that the weight
w^{p^{*}}
belongs to the better class
A_{\frac{p^{*}}{n^{\prime}}}
. Here we will use the following observation by J. Duoandikoetxea
[
Duo
]
(see also
[
LPR
]
) who showed that if
w\in A_{q}
for
1\leq q<p
, namely a stronger condition, then

\|Mf\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq c_{p,q}\,[w]_{A_{q}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}.

This leads to

\|wMf\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c\,[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}+\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

(15)

using again (
9
). Observe that (
15
) also holds for
p=1
.
We do not know if the exponent is sharp as in the case of (
9
).

A similar argument works for iterations of
M
, namely

\|wM^{m}f\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c_{m,p}\,[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}^{\frac{m}{p^{*}}+\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\,\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\quad 1\leq p<n,

(16)

where
M^{m}
denotes the
m
-th iteration of the
Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator, namely
M^{m}=\underbrace{M\circ\cdots\circ M}_{(m\,times)}.

Recently, the estimates above have been significantly improved in
[
HMP
]
by using a different approach. In this work, it is shown that the bound

\|wMf\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c_{p}\,[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}\max\{1,\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{*}}\}}\,\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

(17)

holds when
p\in(1,n)
although the result is worse near
p=1
than (
15
).
This result follows applying
M
to (
2
) using the fact that
I_{1}f\in A_{1}
with dimensional constant, independent of
f
, namely

Mf(x)\leq c\,I_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x),\quad f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

The same pointwise bound holds for the iterated maximal operator, namely

M^{m}f(x)\leq c_{m}\,I_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x),\quad f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

and hence

\|wM^{m}f\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c_{m,p}\,[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}\max\{1,\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{*}}\}}\,\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\quad 1<p<n.

(18)

Observe that (
18
) is an extension of (
9
) since, again,
M^{m}f\geq|f|
, a.e. Furthermore, the exponent is better than (
16
) since it does not depend on
m
.

When
p=1
, we cannot use the truncation method mentioned earlier to derive the sharp estimate (
9
) since

M
and
M^{m}
are non-local operators. Nevertheless, (
18
) is also true, but with a worse constant, depending on
m
,
namely

\|wM^{m}f\|_{L^{n^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c_{m}\,[w]_{A_{1,n^{\prime}}}^{\frac{m+1}{n^{\prime}}}\,\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(19)

It is possible to further improve (
16
) and hence (
9
). Indeed, if we define

M_{r}{f}=\big[M(|f|^{r})\big]^{\frac{1}{r}}\quad\text{for }\,r\in(0,\infty),

then it is possible to prove in the range
p\in(1,n)
that

\|wM_{n^{\prime}}f\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c_{p}\,[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}\max\{1,\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{*}}\}}\,\|w\nabla f\|_{{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}},

(20)

by showing the pointwise estimate (see
[
HMP
]
)

M_{n^{\prime}}f(x)\leq c\,I_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x),

(21)

and using (
7
). Again, (
20
) improves the estimate that is obtained by using Buckley’s inequality (
14
), namely the less desirable bound,

\|wM_{n^{\prime}}f\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c_{p}\,[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{p^{*}-n^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\|w\nabla f\|_{{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}.

Moreover, we cannot use
A_{\frac{p^{*}}{n^{\prime}}}\subset A_{p^{*}}
because
A_{\frac{p^{*}}{n^{\prime}}}
is the precise class of weights for which
M_{n^{\prime}}
is bounded.

Observe that inequality (
20
) is better than inequality (
16
).
This follows from the fact that
M^{k}f\leq c_{r,k}\,M_{r}f
since
M_{r}f\in A_{1}
for
r>1
, with constant independent of
f
.

Inequality (
20
) holds in the range
1<p<n
, but it is false when
p=1
.
In this case we have two different replacements. First, we claim that

\|M_{n^{\prime}}f\|_{L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(w^{n^{\prime}})}\leq c\,{[w]_{A_{1,n^{\prime}}}^{\frac{2}{n^{\prime}}}}\|w\nabla f\|_{{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}},

using the classical
A_{1}
weak type
(1,1)
estimate of Fefferman-Stein,

\left\|Mf\right\|_{L^{1,\infty}(u)}\leq c\,\|f\|_{L^{1}(Mu)},

combined with (
9
) when
p=1
.

Alternatively, consider the quantity
\|wM_{n^{\prime}}f\|_{L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
which is very different from
\|M_{n^{\prime}}f\|_{L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(w^{n^{\prime}})}
. We claim that for a constant
c
depending on the dimension and
[w]_{A_{1,n^{\prime}}}
we have

\|wM_{n^{\prime}}f\|_{L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c\,\|w\nabla f\|_{{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}.

The difficulty is that the weight is inside the distribution level of the definition of the norm making the problem non-standard.
However, it is proved in
[
CMP
]
(improved in
[
LiOP
]
and then in
[
PR
]
) that the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator satisfies a Sawyer-type estimate of the form

\left\|\frac{Mf}{v}\right\|_{L^{1,\infty}(uv)}\leq c_{[u]_{A_{1}},[uv]_{A_{\infty}}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(u)},

(22)

where
u\in A_{1}
and
uv\in A_{\infty}
.

Applying this with
v=w^{-n^{\prime}}
and
u=\frac{1}{v}
, since
w^{n^{\prime}}\in A_{1}
we have

\displaystyle\|wM_{n^{\prime}}f\|_{L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|w^{n^{\prime}}M(|f|^{n^{\prime}})\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{[w]_{A_{1,n^{\prime}}}}\,\|w^{n^{\prime}}|f|^{n^{\prime}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=c_{[w]_{A_{1,n^{\prime}}}}\,\|wf\|_{L^{n^{\prime}}}\leq c_{[w]_{A_{1,n^{\prime}}}}\,\|w\nabla f\|_{{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}

for which we have to use the inequalities (
9
) and (
22
). The explicit dependence on the constant is not ideal and we do not state it here.

Interestingly enough, inequality (
21
) is false for the pointwise larger operator
M_{n^{\prime}+\epsilon}
, when
\epsilon>0
. It is possible to replace
M_{n^{\prime}}
by a slightly larger maximal operator defined in the scale of the Lorentz space
L^{n^{\prime},1}
. Namely, it is shown in
[
HMP
]
, that

M_{n^{\prime},1}f(x)\leq c\,I_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x),

(23)

which yields an improvement of (
20
) and hence of (
9
) when
p\in(1,n)
. Here we are using the normalized definition of the

M_{s,q}f(x)=\sup_{x\in Q}\frac{1}{|Q|^{1/s}}\mbox{$\left\|f\,\chi_{{}_{Q}}\right\|$}_{{{}_{L^{s,q}(dx)}}},

0<s,q\leq\infty
, where

\mbox{$\left\|f\right\|$}_{{}_{L^{s,q}(\mu)}}=\left[s\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(t\,\mu\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mbox{$|f(x)|$}>t\}^{1/s}\right)^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}\right]^{1/q}<\infty,

if
q<\infty
, and
\sup_{0<t<\infty}{t\,\mu\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mbox{$|f(x)|$}>t\}^{1/s}}<\infty,

when
q=\infty
. The proof of inequality (
23
) is based on the following improvement of the classical Poincaré-Sobolev inequality in Lorentz spaces due to O’Neil
[
ON
]
and Peetre
[
Pee
]
(see also
[
MP
]
),

\|f-f_{Q}\|_{L^{p^{*},p}(Q)}\leq c\,\ell(Q)\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}|\nabla f|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

for
1\leq p<n
, where we are using the normalized Lorentz average. In particular, when
p=1
, we have the following

\|f-f_{Q}\|_{L^{n^{\prime},1}(Q)}\leq c\,\ell(Q)\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}|\nabla f|

from which we can derive (
23
) easily.

A natural question would be to find the largest maximal function
M_{\Phi}
for which (
23
) holds, where

\Phi
is a Young function and
M_{\Phi}
is defined through a Luxemburg type norm.

2.3.
The case of singular integrals

Motivated by maximal operator results discussed above, we proved similar results for more complicated singular integral operators in
[
HMP
]
. This will be the case of several classical smooth convolution singular integrals but we will be considering operators beyond Calderón-Zygmund operators like the so-called rough singular integral operators
T_{\Omega}
. Surprisingly, we found Sobolev type estimate as (
17
) or (
20
) for these operators which are defined by the expression

T_{\Omega}f(x)=p.v.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\Omega(y^{\prime})}{|y|^{n}}f(x-y)\,dy

where
y^{\prime}=\frac{y}{|y|}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
which are well defined if
\Omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
and has zero average on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. We are not able to work with the assumption
\Omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
to obtain our results, but rather
\Omega\in L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
which properly contains the spaces
L^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
for
r\geq n
.

One of our main results from
[
HMP
]
is the following.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
HMP
]
)
.

Let
T_{\Omega}
be a rough singular integral convolution operator as above with
\Omega\in L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
. Then

|T_{\Omega}f(x)|\leq c\,\|\Omega\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\,I_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x),\quad f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(24)

Hence, if
p\in(1,n)
and if we let
w\in A_{p,p^{*}}
, we have

\|wT_{\Omega}f\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c_{p}\,\|\Omega\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\,[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}\max\{1,\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{*}}\}}\,\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(25)

and

\|T_{\Omega}f\|_{L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(w^{n^{\prime}})}\leq c\,\|\Omega\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\,[w]_{A_{1,n^{\prime}}}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\,\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Similar results hold for rough maximal type operators and more exotic maximal operators such as the spherical maximal operator.

We note here that all the singular integral operators for which we have proven (
24
) have a cancellation on the kernel.
We do not know if (
24
) also holds for Calderón-Zygmund operators of non-convolution type.
We do however have reasonable bounds for these operators.
Recall that these are a continuous linear operator
T:C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
which extend
to bounded operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and whose distributional
kernel coincides away from the diagonal
x=y
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with a function
K
so that

Tf(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}K(x,y)f(y)dy,\quad f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),x\not\in\mbox{supp}(f),

and satisfies the standard estimates, namely, the size estimate

|K(x,y)|\leq\frac{c}{|x-y|^{n}}

(26)

and the regularity condition: for some
\epsilon>0
,

|K(x,y)-K(z,y)|+|K(y,x)-K(y,z)|\leq c\,\frac{|x-z|^{\epsilon}}{|x-y|^{n+\epsilon}},

(27)

whenever
2\,|x-z|<|x-y|
.

By the well-known classical theory, we have

T:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

(28)

when
1<p<\infty
and

T:L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(29)

We will be using the following result due to Coifman-Fefferman
[
CF
]
,

\|Tf\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq c_{p,T}\,\,[w]_{A_{\infty}}\,\|Mf\|_{L^{p}(w)},

(30)

for
p\in(0,\infty)
and
w\in A_{\infty}
. The original proof does not yield this precise bound since some further ideas are required. See details in
[
OPR
]
. A similar result holds for the weak norm.

Now, arguing as in Section
2.2
, using the sharp weighted estimate for Calderón-Zygmund operators,
since
w^{p^{*}}\in A_{p^{*}}
, we have

\displaystyle\|wTf\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq c_{p,T}\,[w^{p^{*}}]_{A_{p^{*}}}^{\max\{1,\frac{1}{p^{*}-1}\}}\,\|wf\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq c_{p,T}\,[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}^{\max\{1,\frac{1}{p^{*}-1}\}+\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\,[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\,\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

in the case
p\in(1,n)
, which is certainly less optimal than (
25
). Moreover, we may use the fact that
A_{\frac{p^{*}}{n^{\prime}}}
is a proper subclass of
A_{p^{*}}
and the estimate of Duoandikoetxea
[
Duo
]
(see also
[
LM
]
)

\|Tf\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq c_{p,q,T}\,[w]_{A_{q}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)},\quad 1\leq q<p,

to obtain when
p\in(1,n)

\|wTf\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c_{p,T}\,[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}^{1+\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(31)

On the other hand, if we use (
30
) instead, we claim

\|wTf\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c_{p,T}\,[w^{p^{*}}]_{A_{\infty}}\,[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}\max\{1,\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{*}}\}}\,\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(32)

Indeed,

\displaystyle\|wTf\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|Tf\|_{L^{p^{*}}(w^{p^{*}})}\leq c_{p,T}\,[w^{p^{*}}]_{A_{\infty}}\,\|Mf\|_{L^{p^{*}}(w^{p^{*}})}

\displaystyle\leq c_{p,T}\,[w^{p^{*}}]_{A_{\infty}}\,[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}\max\{1,\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{*}}\}}\,\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

by (
17
), which is also a worse result than (
25
).

Finally, using the main result in
[
LOP
]
, we also have

\displaystyle\|Tf\|_{L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(w^{n^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle\leq c_{T}\,[w^{n^{\prime}}]_{A_{1}}\,\|f\|_{L^{n^{\prime}}(w^{n^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle\leq c_{T}\,[w]_{A_{1,n^{\prime}}}^{1+\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\,\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

which is a better result than (
31
).

3.
The linear commutators

Likely motivated by the work of A. P. Calderón on commutators, R. Coifman, R. Rochberg and G. Weiss introduced in
[
CRW
]
the operator

T_{b}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(b(x)-b(y))K(x,y)f(y)\,dy,

where
K
is a kernel satisfying the standard Calderón-Zygmund
estimates (see Section
2.3
) and where
b
is any locally integrable function. The
operator is called commutator since

T_{b}=[b,T]=bT-T(b\,\cdot)

where
T
is the Calderón-Zygmund singular
integral operator associated to
K
. The main result
from
[
CRW
]
states that
[b,T]
is a bounded operator on

L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if
1<p<\infty
and
b
is a

BMO
function. They proved that

\|T_{b}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c_{p,T}\,\|b\|_{BMO}.

Moreover, they provided a new and beautiful characterization of
BMO
in the case of
T
being the Hilbert transform or any Riesz transform
R_{j}
, i.e.

\|b\|_{BMO}\approx\|T_{b}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

These commutators have proved to be of great interest in many
situations, for instance, the theory of non divergence elliptic equations with discontinuous
coefficients
[
CF1
]

[
CF2
]

[
Dir
]
. We refer to
[
I
]
for a beautiful account.

A natural generalization of the commutator
[b,T]
is given by

T^{m}_{b}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(b(x)-b(y))^{m}K(x,y)f(y)\,dy,

where
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and the case
m=0
recaptures the
Calderón-Zygmund singular integral operator. Observing that for
m\geq 2
, the iterated commutator

T^{m}_{b}=\overbrace{[b,\cdots,[b,T]]}^{(m\,times)}

is also a bounded operator

T^{m}_{b}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

whenever
1<p<\infty
and
b
is a
BMO
function.

It was shown in
[
P1
]
that these operators are not of weak type
(1,1)
and that there is an intimate relationship with the iterated maximal function
M^{m}
We will need the following result: for any
0<p<\infty
and
any
w\in A_{\infty}
, we have

\|T^{m}_{b}f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq c_{m,p,T}\,[w]_{A_{\infty}}\,\|b\|_{BMO}^{m}\|M^{m+1}(f)\|_{L^{p}(w)}.

(33)

This inequality is sharp since
M^{m+1}
can not be
replaced by the smaller operator
M^{m}
.
[w]_{A_{\infty}}
denotes the usual
A_{\infty}
constant,

[w]_{A_{\infty}}:=\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}M(w\chi_{Q})\,dx.

On the other hand, it follows from
[
CPP
]
that

\displaystyle\|wT^{m}_{b}f\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|T^{m}_{b}f\|_{L^{p^{*}}(w^{p^{*}})}

\displaystyle\leq c_{m,p,T}\,[w^{p^{*}}]_{A_{p^{*}}}^{(m+1)\max\{1,\frac{1}{p^{*}-1}\}}\|b\|_{BMO}^{m}\|f\|_{L^{p^{*}}(w^{p^{*}})},

which combined with (
9
) yields

\|wT^{m}_{b}f\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c_{m,p,T}\,[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}^{(m+1)\max\{1,\frac{1}{p^{*}-1}\}}\|b\|_{BMO}^{m}[w]_{A_{p,{p^{*}}}}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

The above constant is worse than the one derived using (
33
)
[w^{p^{*}}]_{A_{\infty}}
times
[w]_{A_{p,{p^{*}}}}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}
) at least in the case
p
is close to
1
and
n
is large.

Reasoning as in the proof of (
32
), for
p\in(1,n)
we have

\|wT^{m}_{b}f\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c_{m,p,T}\,[w^{p^{*}}]_{A_{\infty}}^{m+1}\,[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}\max\{1,\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{*}}\}}\,\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

The case
p=1
has a different nature, observe that the exponent above blows up when
p\to 1
. We end this section with the following result whose proof is based on the
A_{1}
results for commutators by C. Ortiz in
[
O
]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
w\in A_{1,n^{\prime}}
. Then

\|T^{m}_{b}f\|_{L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(w^{n^{\prime}})}\leq c_{T}\,\|b\|_{BMO}^{m}\,[w]_{A_{1,n^{\prime}}}^{m+1+\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

4.
The nonlinear commutators

The second kind of commutators that we are going to consider were introduced by R.
Rochberg and G. Weiss in
[
RW
]
. These are nonlinear operators defined for
appropriate functions by

f{\mapsto}Nf=N_{T}(f):=T(f\,\log\mbox{$|f|$})-Tf\,\log\mbox{$|Tf|$}.

We will assume that
T
is a Calderón-Zygmund singular integral operator in this section.
It is of interest to study this operator due to its
relationship with the Jacobian mapping and with nonlinear P.D.E.
as shown in
[
IS
]

[
GI
]
(see also
[
M
]
).

The main result from
[
RW
]
is the following
L^{p}
bound

\mbox{$\left\|Nf\right\|$}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c_{p,T}\,\mbox{$\left\|f\right\|$}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\quad 1<p<\infty.

We have the following improvement from
[
P2
]
that uses a completely different method.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
0<p<\infty
and
w\in A_{\infty}
. Then,

\|Nf\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq c_{p,T}[w]_{A_{\infty}}\,\log\left(e+[w]_{A_{\infty}}\right)\,\|M^{2}f\|_{L^{p}(w)}.

We briefly sketch the proof below. The idea is to split the nonlinear commutator
N
into simpler pieces:

\displaystyle T(f\,\log\mbox{$|f|$})-Tf\,\log\mbox{$|Tf|$}

\displaystyle=T(f\,\log\frac{\mbox{$|f|$}}{Mf})+[\log Mf,T]f-Tf\,\log\frac{\mbox{$|Tf|$}}{Mf}

\displaystyle:=N_{1}f+N_{2}f+N_{3}f.

To estimate
N_{1}
, we write

N_{1}f=T\left(Mf\,\frac{f}{Mf}\,\log(\frac{\mbox{$|f|$}}{Mf})\right),

and we apply (
33
) with
m=0
. Since
w\in A_{\infty}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mbox{$|N_{1}f|$}^{p}\,w

\displaystyle\leq c\,[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{p}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(M\left(Mf\,\frac{f}{Mf}\,\log(\frac{\mbox{$|f|$}}{Mf})\right)\right)^{p}\,w

\displaystyle\leq c\,[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{p}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(M^{2}f)^{p}\,w

by utilizing the fact that
\mbox{$|t\,\log t|$}\leq\frac{1}{e}
when
0<t\leq 1
.

For
N_{2}
, we notice that it is of commutator type fixing
b_{f}=\log Mf\in BMO
with dimensional constant, namely independent of
f
and hence we can apply again (
33
) (see
[
P2
]
for details)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mbox{$|N_{2}f|$}^{p}\,w

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mbox{$|[\log Mf,T]f|$}^{p}\,w

\displaystyle\leq c\,\mbox{$\left\|\log Mf\right\|$}_{BMO}^{2p}\,[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{p}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(M^{2}f)^{p}\,w

\displaystyle\leq c\,[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{p}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(M^{2}f)^{p}\,w.

For
N_{3}
, we split
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in two disjoint sets
A
and

B
where

A=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mbox{$|Tf(y)|$}\leq Mf(y)\}

and
B=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus A.
Writing

N_{3}f=Mf\,\frac{\mbox{$|Tf|$}}{Mf}\,\log\frac{\mbox{$|Tf|$}}{Mf}

we have that
|N_{3}f|\leq Mf
on
A
, and on
B
we use
\log t\leq\,\frac{t^{\epsilon}}{\epsilon}
,
t>1
,
\epsilon>0
to get

\|N_{3}f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq\|N_{3}f\chi_{A}\|_{L^{p}(w)}+\|N_{3}f\chi_{B}\|_{L^{p}(w)}

\leq\|Mf\|_{L^{p}(w)}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\,\|\mbox{$|Tf|$}^{(\epsilon+1)}\,(Mf)^{-\epsilon}\|_{L^{p}(w)}

=\|Mf\|_{L^{p}(w)}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\,\|Tf\|_{L^{p(1+\epsilon)}(w(Mf)^{-\epsilon p})}^{1+\epsilon}

To apply (
33
) again with
m=0
, we must verify that
(Mf)^{-\epsilon\,p}\,w\in A_{\infty}
for
\epsilon
small enough and with a constant
independent of
f
. Being more precise, it can be shown that

[wu^{-1}]_{A_{\infty}}\leq[w]_{A_{\infty}}\,[u]_{A_{1}},

if
\epsilon
is chosen to be
\epsilon p<1
. Indeed, we have by the well-known Coifman-Rochberg theorem that if
0<\delta<1
,

u=(Mf)^{1-\delta}\in A_{1}
with
[(Mf)^{1-\delta}]_{A_{1}}\leq\frac{c_{n}}{\delta}
and then

[w(Mf)^{-\epsilon\,p}]_{A_{\infty}}\leq\frac{c_{n}}{1-\epsilon p}\,[w]_{A_{\infty}},

from which we have

\|N_{3}f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq\|Mf\|_{L^{p}(w)}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\,[w(Mf)^{-\epsilon p}]_{A_{\infty}}^{1+\epsilon}c_{p,T}^{1+\epsilon}\|Mf\|_{L^{p(1+\epsilon)}(w(Mf)^{-\epsilon p})}^{1+\epsilon}

\leq\|Mf\|_{L^{p}(w)}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\,[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{1+\epsilon}\,[(Mf)^{\epsilon p}]_{A_{1}}^{1+\epsilon}\,c_{p,T}^{1+\epsilon}\|Mf\|_{L^{p}(w)}

\leq\|Mf\|_{L^{p}(w)}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\,[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{1+\epsilon}\,(\frac{c_{n}}{1-p\epsilon})^{1+\epsilon}\,c_{p,T}^{1+\epsilon}\|Mf\|_{L^{p}(w)}

Optimizing this estimate with
\epsilon=\frac{1}{p\log(e^{2}+[w]_{A_{\infty}})}
we get finally

\|N_{3}f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq[w]_{A_{\infty}}\log\left(e+[w]_{A_{\infty}}\right)\,c_{p,T}\,\|Mf\|_{L^{p}(w)}

and hence

\|Nf\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq c_{p,T}\,[w]_{A_{\infty}}\log\left(e+[w]_{A_{\infty}}\right)\,\|M^{2}f\|_{L^{p}(w)}

(34)

This yields the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Using (
14
) we have as an immediate consequence the following corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
w\in A_{p}
. Then,

\|Nf\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq c_{p,T}\,[w]_{A_{\infty}}\log\left(e+[w]_{A_{\infty}}\right)\,[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}.

Another result that can be found in
[
P2
]
is the following

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose that
1<p<\infty
. Let
w
be any weight. Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mbox{$|Nf(x)|$}^{p}\,w(x)\,dx\leq c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mbox{$|f(x)|$}^{p}\,M^{[2p]+1}w(x)\,dx.

The method used in
[
P1
,
P2
]
cannot be applied here since the operator is non-linear. The splitting above has to be used.

Finally, combining (
34
) and the Sobolev type inequality (
18
) derived in
[
HMP
]

\|wNf\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c_{p,T}\,[w^{p^{*}}]_{A_{\infty}}\,\log\left(e+[w^{p^{*}}]_{A_{\infty}}\right)\,[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}\max\{1,\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{*}}\}}\,\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Observe that, as in the linear case, the exponent blows up when
p\to 1
.
We state now a conjecture motivated by Theorem
3.1
without such a blow up.

Conjecture 4.4
.

Let
w\in A_{1,n^{\prime}}
. Then

\|Nf\|_{L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(w^{n^{\prime}})}\leq c\,[w]_{A_{1,n^{\prime}}}^{2+\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\,\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

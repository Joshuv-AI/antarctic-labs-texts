Shape preserving approximation of periodic functions – Conclusion Thanks: AMS classification: 41A29, 42A10, 41A25, Keywords and phrases: Comonotone approximation by trigonometric polynomials, Degree of approximation, Jackson-type estimates.
D. Leviatan, I. O. Shevchuk

Abstract
We give here the final results about the validity of Jackson-type estimates in comonotone approximation of 2 ​ π 2\pi -periodic functions by trigonometric polynomials. For coconvex and the so called co- q q -monotone, q > 2 q>2 , approximations, everything is known by now. Thus, this paper concludes the research on Jackson type estimates of Shape Preserving Approximation of periodic functions by trigonometric polynomials. It is interesting to point out that the results for comonotone approximation of a periodic function are substantially different than the analogous results for comonotone approximation, by algebraic polynomials, of a continuous function on a finite interval.

3
Divided differences

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and let the function
g
be defined at the distinct points
t_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
0\leq i\leq m
. Denote
[t_{0};g]:=g(t_{0})
and by

[t_{0},t_{1},\dots,t_{m};g]:=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\frac{g(t_{i})}{\prod_{j=0,\,j\neq i}^{m}(t_{i}-t_{j})},

the divided difference of order
m
of
g
at the knots
t_{i}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{m}
. Assume that either

(3.1)

(-1)^{m-i}\bigl(g(t_{i})-g(t_{i-1})\bigr)\geq 0,\quad 1\leq i\leq m,

or

(3.2)

(-1)^{m-i}\bigl(g(t_{i})-g(t_{i-1})\bigr)\leq 0,\quad 1\leq i\leq m.

Then, respectively,

(3.3)

[t_{0},\dots,t_{m};g]\geq 0,\quad\text{or}\quad[t_{0},\dots,t_{m};g]\leq 0,

and

(3.4)

(t_{m}-t_{0})^{m}\bigl|[t_{0},t_{1},\dots,t_{m};g]\bigr|\geq\max_{0\leq i\leq m}g(t_{i})-\min_{0\leq i\leq m}g(t_{i}),

Therefore, for
m\geq 2
,

(3.5)

\bigl|[t_{0},\dots,t_{m};g]\bigr|=\frac{\bigl|[t_{1},\dots,t_{m};g]\bigr|+\bigl|[t_{0},\dots,t_{m-1};g]\bigr|}{t_{m}-t_{0}},

and if
2\leq r\leq m
, then

(3.6)

\bigl|[t_{0},\dots,t_{m};g]\bigr|\prod_{i=r}^{m}(t_{i}-t_{0})\geq\bigl|[t_{1},\dots,t_{r};g]\bigr|.

Proof.

Since all statements depend only on
g(t_{i})
,
0\leq i\leq m
, and there is an algebraic polynomial of degree
\leq m
, interpolating
g
at
t_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq m
, without loss of generality we assume that
g
is that polynomial. Then (
3.1
), respectively, (
3.2
) implies that
g^{\prime}(x)=a\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}(x-x_{i})
, where
a\geq 0
, respectively,
a\leq 0
, and
x_{i}\in(t_{0},t_{m})
,
1\leq i\leq m-1
. Note that
g^{(m)}(x)\equiv(m-1)!a
, so that
[t_{0},\dots,t_{m};g]=\frac{a}{m}
. Thus, (
3.3
) holds.

By the inequality between the arithmetic and geometric means,

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{m}}|g^{\prime}(x)|\,dx

\displaystyle=|a|\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}|x-x_{i}|\,dx\leq\frac{|a|}{m-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{m}}|x-x_{i}|^{m-1}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{|a|}{m(m-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\bigl((t_{m}-x_{i})^{m}+(x_{i}-t_{0})^{m}\bigr)\leq\frac{|a|}{m}(t_{m}-t_{0})^{m}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq i\leq m}g(t_{i})-\min_{0\leq i\leq m}g(t_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{m}}|g^{\prime}(x)|\,dx\leq\frac{|a|}{m}(t_{m}-t_{0})^{m}

\displaystyle=\bigl|[t_{0},t_{1},\dots,t_{m};g]\bigr|(t_{m}-t_{0})^{m}

Thus, (
3.4
) is proved. Finally, (
3.3
) readily implies (
3.5
), and (
3.6
) follows from (
3.5
) by induction on
m-r
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
0\leq l<m
. If
f\in C^{l}[a,b]
and
x_{i}\in[a,b]
,
0\leq i\leq m
, are distinct, then

(3.7)

|[x_{0},\dots,x_{m};f]|\leq\frac{c}{l!}\omega_{m-l}(f^{(l)},b-a;[a,b])\sum_{j=l}^{m}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=l,i\neq j}^{m}|x_{j}-x_{i}|},

where

Proof.

For
l\geq 1
, let
U_{l}:=\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{l}
and denote
f_{l}(x;U_{l}):=f^{(l)}\bigl(x_{0}+u_{1}(x_{1}-x_{0})+\cdots+u_{l}(x-x_{l-1})\bigr)
.

By virtue of
[
1
]
Chapter 4 (7.12) we have,

\displaystyle[x_{0},\dots,x_{l};f]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{u_{1}}\dots\int_{0}^{u_{l-1}}f_{l}(x_{l};U_{l})\,du_{l}\cdots du_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{u_{1}}\dots\int_{0}^{u_{l-1}}[x_{l};f_{l}(\cdot;U_{l})]\,du_{l}\cdots du_{1}.

Assume by induction that for some
m>l
,

(3.8)

[x_{0},\dots,x_{m-1};f]=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{u_{1}}\dots\int_{0}^{u_{l-1}}[x_{l},\dots,x_{m-1};f_{l}(\cdot;U_{l})]\,du_{l}\cdots du_{1},

we will prove (

Indeed,

\displaystyle[x_{0},\dots,x_{m};f]=\frac{[x_{0},\dots,x_{m-1};f]-[x_{0},\dots,x_{m-2},x_{m};f]}{x_{m-1}-x_{m}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{u_{1}}\dots\int_{0}^{u_{l-1}}\frac{[x_{l},\dots,x_{m-1};f_{l}(\cdot;U_{l})]-[x_{l},\dots,x_{m-2},x_{m};f_{l}(\cdot;U_{l})]}{x_{m-1}-x_{m}}\,du_{l}\cdots du_{1}

Hence,

(3.9)

\bigl|[x_{0},\dots,x_{m};f]\bigr|\leq\frac{\|f^{(l)}\|_{[a,b]}}{l!}\sum_{j=l}^{m}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=l,i\neq j}^{m}|x_{j}-x_{i}|}.

Finally, by Whitney’s inequality there is an algebraic polynomial

∎

Thus, combining with (
4.4

\displaystyle\frac{\bigl|[t_{0},\dots,t_{{2s}+k};\tilde{g}-g]\bigr|}{\bigl|[t_{0},\dots,t_{{2s}+k};\tilde{g}]\bigr|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(t_{2s}-t_{1})^{{2s}-1}(t_{2s}-t_{0})}{(t_{{2s}+1}-t_{2s})^{2s}}(2^{k}-1)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(2^{k}-1)(6sh)^{2s}}{(H-6sh))^{2s}}=\frac{1}{(2^{k}-1)^{{2s}-1}}<\frac{1}{2}.

Finally, by virtue of (

(4.5)

\displaystyle\bigl|[t_{0},\dots,t_{{2s}+k};g]\bigr|

\displaystyle\geq\bigl|[t_{0},\dots,t_{{2s}+k};\tilde{g}]\bigr|-\bigl|[t_{0},\dots,t_{{2s}+k};\tilde{g}-g]\bigr|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\bigl|[t_{0},\dots,t_{{2s}+k};\tilde{g}]\bigr|\geq\frac{c\|g\|}{h^{{2s}+k}}=\frac{c\|f-f(t_{2s})\|}{h^{{2s}+k}}.

Now, we combine (

Proof of Theorem

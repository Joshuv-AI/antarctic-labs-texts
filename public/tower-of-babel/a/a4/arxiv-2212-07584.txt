Abstract
. We give simple geometric proofs of Aprodu–Farkas–Papadima–Raicu–Weyman’s theorem on syzygies of tangent developable surfaces of rational normal curves and Raicu–Sam’s result on syzygies of K3 carpets. As a consequence, we obtain a quick proof of Green’s conjecture for general curves of genus g g over an algebraically closed field 𝐤 \mathbf{k} with char ⁡ ( 𝐤 ) = 0 \operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})=0 or char ⁡ ( 𝐤 ) ≥ ⌊ ( g − 1 ) / 2 ⌋ \operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})\geq\lfloor(g-1)/2\rfloor . We also show the arithmetic normality of tangent developable surfaces of arbitrary smooth projective curves of large degree.

1.
Introduction

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 3
over the field
\mathbf{C}
of complex numbers. If
\Cliff(C)\geq 1
, i.e.,
C
is nonhyperelliptic, then
K_{C}
is very ample. In this case, Noether’s theorem says that the canonical curve
C\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{g-1}
is projectively normal. Petri’s theorem states that if
\Cliff(C)\geq 2
, then the defining ideal
I_{C|\mathbf{P}^{g-1}}
of
C
in
\mathbf{P}^{g-1}
is generated by quadrics. To generalize classical theorems of Noether and Petri, in the early 1980’s, Green
[
13
, Conjecture 5.1]
formulated a very famous conjecture that predicts

K_{p,2}(C,K_{C})=0~~\text{ for $0\leq p\leq\Cliff(C)-1$}.

By Green–Lazarsfeld’s nonvanishing theorem
[
13
, Appendix]
, we have
K_{p,2}(C,K_{C})\neq 0
for
\Cliff(C)\leq p\leq g-3
. Then Green’s conjecture determines the shape of the minimal free resolution of the canonical ring
R(C,K_{C})=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbf{Z}}H^{0}(C,mK_{C})
(see
[
3
, Remark 4.19]
). Although the conjecture is still open, Voisin
[
26
,
27
]
resolved the general curve case in the early 2000’s. To prove generic Green’s conjecture, it suffices to exhibit one curve of each genus for which the assertion holds. If
S\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{g}
is a K3 surface of degree
2g-2
, then its general hyperplane section is a canonical curve
C\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{g-1}
and
K_{p,2}(S,\mathscr{O}_{S}(1))=K_{p,2}(C,K_{C})
. Based on the Hilbert scheme interpretation of Koszul cohomology, Voisin accomplished sophisticated cohomology computations on Hilbert schemes of K3 surfaces to show
K_{p,2}(S,\mathscr{O}_{S}(1))=0
. For an introduction to Voisin’s work, see
[
3
]
. Recently, Kemeny
[
15
]
gave a simpler proof of Voisin’s theorem for even genus case and a steamlined version of her arguments for odd genus case.

Prior to Voisin’s work, O’Grady and Buchweitz–Schreyer independently observed that one could use the
tangent developable surface

T\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{g}
of a rational normal curve of degree
g
to solve Green’s conjecture for general curves of genus
g
(see
[
9
]
). Note that
T\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{g}
is arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay as for a K3 surface. One can actually view
T
as a degenerate K3 surface (see
[
1
, Remark 6.6]
). A general hyperplane section of
T
is a canonically embedded
g
-cuspidal rational curve
\overline{C}\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{g-1}
of degree
2g-2
, which is degenerated to a general canonical curve
C\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{g-1}
with
\Cliff(C)=\lfloor(g-1)/2\rfloor
. By the upper semicontinuity of graded Betti numbers,
K_{p,2}(\overline{C},\mathscr{O}_{\overline{C}}(1))=K_{p,2}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))=0
implies
K_{p,2}(C,K_{C})=0
(see
[
1
, Section 6]
). The required vanishing of
K_{p,2}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))
for confirming generic Green’s conjecture was finally established by Aprodu–Farkas–Papadima–Raicu–Weyman
[
1
]
just a few years ago. Their important result gives not only an alternative proof of generic Green’s conjecture but also an extension to positive characteristic. This circle of ideas is surveyed in
[
6
]
. In this paper, we give a simple geometric proof of the main result of
[
1
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

be the tangent developable surface of a rational normal curve of degree

\mathbf{k}

K_{p,2}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))=0~~\text{ for $0\leq p\leq\lfloor(g-3)/2\rfloor$}.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})\geq\lfloor(g-1)/2\rfloor

\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})\neq 2

\mathbf{k}
.

Recall that Schreyer

verified Green’s conjecture for every curve of genus

is essentially different from that of

is degenerated to the tangent surface

.

be a smooth projective curve of genus

2.1.
Syzygies

Let
X
be a projective scheme,
B
be a coherent sheaf on
X
, and
L
be a very ample line bundle on
X
. The
Koszul cohomology

K_{p,q}(X,B;L)
is the cohomology of the Koszul-type complex

\begin{array}[]{l}\wedge^{p+1}H^{0}(X,L)\otimes H^{0}(X,B\otimes L^{q-1})\longrightarrow\wedge^{p}H^{0}(X,L)\otimes H^{0}(X,B\otimes L^{q})\longrightarrow\wedge^{p-1}H^{0}(X,L)\otimes H^{0}(X,B\otimes L^{q+1}).\end{array}

When
B=\mathscr{O}_{X}
, we put
K_{p,q}(X,L):=K_{p,q}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X};L)
and
\kappa_{p,q}(X,L):=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}K_{p,q}(X,L)
. Let
S:=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}S^{m}H^{0}(X,L)
, and view
R=R(X,B;L):=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}H^{0}(X,B\otimes L^{m})
as a graded
S
-module. If

\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{R\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces~\cdots~}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces E_{r}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces 0}

is a minimal free resolution of

, then
E_{p}=\bigoplus_{q}K_{p,q}(X,B;L)\otimes_{\mathbf{k}}S(-p-q)
. We may think that
K_{p,q}(X,B;L)
is the space of
p
-th syzygies of weight
q
. For a globally generated vector bundle

on
X
, we denote by

the kernel of the evaluation map

. The following is well-known.

Proposition 2.1

(cf.
[
19
, Proposition 2.1]
)
.

Assume that

for

and

. For

, we have

. If furthermore

.

2.2.
Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity

A coherent sheaf
\mathscr{F}
on
\mathbf{P}^{n}
is said to be
m

if
H^{i}(\mathbf{P}^{n},\mathscr{F}(m-i))=0
for
i>0
. By Mumford’s theorem
[
17

, if
\mathscr{F}
is
m
-regular, then
\mathscr{F}
is
(m+1)
-regular. The
Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity

\reg\mathscr{F}
is the minimum
m
such that
\mathscr{F}
is
m
-regular. If
\mathscr{F}
fits into an exact sequence
\cdots\to\mathscr{F}_{2}\to\mathscr{F}_{1}\to\mathscr{F}_{0}\to\mathscr{F}\to 0
of coherent sheaves on
\mathbf{P}^{n}
and
\mathscr{F}_{i}
is
(m+i)
-regular for each
i\geq 0
, then
\mathscr{F}
is
m
-regular (
[
17
, Example 1.8.7]
). If
\mathscr{F}
is
m
-regular and
E
is an
m^{\prime}
-regular vector bundle on
\mathbf{P}^{n}
, then
\mathscr{F}\otimes E
is
(m+m^{\prime})
-regular (
[
17
, Proposition 1.8.9]
). We can think of the regularity of a coherent sheaf
\mathscr{G}
on a closed subscheme
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{n}
by regarding
\mathscr{G}
as a sheaf on
\mathbf{P}^{n}
.

2.3.
Multilinear algebra

Let
V
be a finite dimensional vector space over
\mathbf{k}
. The symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
naturally acts on
V^{\otimes n}
by permuting the factors. The
divided power

D^{n}V
is the subspace
\{\omega\in V^{\otimes n}\mid\sigma(\omega)=\omega~\text{for all $\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}$}\}\subseteq T^{n}V
, and the
symmetric power

S^{n}V
is the quotient of
V^{\otimes n}
by the span of
\sigma(\omega)-\omega
for all
\omega\in V^{\otimes n}
and
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. We have a natural identification
D^{n}V=(S^{n}V^{\vee})^{\vee}
. By composing the inclusion of
D^{n}V
into
V^{\otimes n}
with the projection onto
S^{n}V
, we get a natural map
D^{n}V\to S^{n}V
. This is an isomorphism if
\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})=0
or
\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})>n
, but it may be neither injective nor surjective in general. The
wedge product

\wedge^{n}V
is the quotient of
V^{\otimes n}
by the span of
v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}
for all
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in V
with
v_{i}\neq v_{j}
for some
i\neq j
. We write
v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{n}
for the class of
v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{d}
in the quotient. There is a natural inclusion
\wedge^{n}V\to V^{\otimes n}
given by
v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{n}\mapsto\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\sgn(\sigma)\sigma(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n})
. This gives a splitting of the quotient map
V^{\otimes n}\to\wedge^{n}V
if and only if
\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})=0
or
\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})>n
. We refer to
[
1
, Section 3]
for more details.

.

Suppose that
\deg L\geq 3g+2
. Then the
2
-normality of
T\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
is equivalent to that
H^{1}(C\times C,(L\boxtimes L)(-3D))=0
.

Proof.

By (
4.1
), the
2
-normality of
T\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
is equivalent to that
H^{1}(C_{2},T_{L}(-4\delta))=0
. By
[
5
, Theorem 1 (i)]
,
H^{1}(C^{2},(L\boxtimes L)(-2D))=0
. It follows that
H^{1}(C_{2},T_{L}(-3\delta))=0
. Then
H^{1}(C_{2},T_{L}(-4\delta))=0
if and only if
H^{1}(C^{2},(L\boxtimes L)(-3D))=0
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

K_{p,3}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))=0
for
0\leq p\leq r-3
, and
\kappa_{r-2,3}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))=1
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.1
and Lemma
4.1
,
K_{p,3}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))=H^{2}\big(T,\wedge^{p+2}M_{\mathscr{O}_{T}(1)}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{T}(1)\big)
. Note that
\omega_{T}=\mathscr{O}_{T}
(Proposition
2.5
) and
\wedge^{p+2}M_{\mathscr{O}_{T}(1)}^{\vee}=\wedge^{r-p-2}M_{\mathscr{O}_{T}(1)}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{T}(1)
. By Serre duality, we have

H^{2}\big(T,\wedge^{p+2}M_{\mathscr{O}_{T}(1)}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{T}(1)\big)=H^{0}\big(T,\wedge^{p+2}M_{\mathscr{O}_{T}(1)}^{\vee}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{T}(-1)\big)^{\vee}=H^{0}\big(T,\wedge^{r-p-2}M_{\mathscr{O}_{T}(1)}\big)^{\vee}.

Then the proposition immediately follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By Lemma
4.1
, it suffices to show that
T\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
is
m
-normal for
m=1,2,3
. Indeed, this together with
\reg\mathscr{O}_{T}=3
implies
\reg\mathscr{I}_{T|\mathbf{P}^{r}}=4
, and thus,
T\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
is
m
-normal for
m\geq 1
. In view of (
4.1
),
T\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
is trivially
1
-normal. For the
2
-normality, we assume that
\deg L\geq 4g+3
. Then
[
5
, Theorem 1.7 (i)]
says that
H^{1}(C\times C,(L\boxtimes L)(-3D))=0
, so the
2
-normality of
T\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
follows from Lemma
4.2
. Now, the
3
-normality of
T\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
is equivalent to that
K_{0,3}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))=0
, but this is a special case of Proposition
4.3
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

In Theorem
1.3
, the degree condition
\deg L\geq 4g+3
is only used to show that
T\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
is
2
-normal. If
\deg L=4g+2
, then by Lemma
4.2
and
[
5
, Theorem 1.7 (ii), (iii)]
,
T\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
is
2
-normal (and arithmetically normal) if and only if
g\geq 3
and
C
is nonhyperelliptic. More generally, using the results in
[
5
, Section 1]
and
[
18
, Theorem 3.8 (b)]
, one can prove that if
\deg L\geq 4g+3-\Cliff(C)
, then
T\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
is arithmetically normal.

Corollary 4.5
.

Suppose that
\deg L\geq 4g+3
. Then the Hilbert function of
T\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
is given by
H_{T}(t)=(\deg L+g-1)t^{2}+2-2g
for
t\geq 1
. In particular,
\kappa_{1,1}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))=(r-2)(r-3)/2-6g
.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.3
,
H_{T}(t)
coincides with the Hilbert polynomial
P_{T}(t)
for
t\geq 1
.
We know that
\deg P_{T}(t)=2
and the leading coefficient of
P_{T}(t)
is
(\deg T)/2=\deg L+g-1
.
As
P_{T}(0)=\chi(\mathscr{O}_{T})=2-2g
and
P_{T}(1)=h^{0}(C,L)=\deg L-g+1
, we easily get the assertion. The last assertion follows from that
\kappa_{1,1}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))=h^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{r},\mathscr{I}_{T|\mathbf{P}^{r}}(2))={r+2\choose 2}-H_{T}(2)
.
∎

Observe that the tangent developable surface of an elliptic normal curve of degree
d
has the same Hilbert polynomial with an abelian surface of degree
2d
in
\mathbf{P}^{d-1}
.

Remark 4.6
.

Assume that
\deg L\geq 4g+3
. Notice that (cf. Proposition
4.3
)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}K_{p,0}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))\neq 0&\Longleftrightarrow&p=0~\text{ }~(\kappa_{0,0}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))=1);\\
K_{p,3}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))\neq 0&\Longleftrightarrow&p=r-2~\text{ }~(\kappa_{r-2,3}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))=1).\end{array}

It would be exceedingly interesting to study vanishing/nonvanishing behaviors of
K_{p,1}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))
and
K_{p,2}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))
as the positivity of
L
grows. For a possible generalization of Theorem
1.1
(see also Remark
3.4
), one may expect that

(4.2)

K_{p,1}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))=0~~\text{ for $p\geq\lfloor(\deg L-3g)/2\rfloor$}.

We have
\kappa_{p,1}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))\leq\kappa_{p,1}(C,L)
. Thus
K_{p,1}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))=0
at least for
p\geq r-1
(or for
p\geq r-\gon(C)+1
by
[
23
, Theorem 1.1]
). Then we find

\kappa_{r-2,2}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))=2g(r+1),~\kappa_{r-1,2}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))=2g.

If
(\ref{eq:expectK_{p,1}})
holds, then we can compute
\kappa_{p,2}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))
for
\lfloor(\deg L-3g-2)/2\rfloor\leq p\leq r-1
. Concerning vanishing of
K_{p,2}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))
, one may ask whether
K_{p,2}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))=0
as soon as
\deg L\geq 4g+3+2p
. Example
4.7
shows that this is not true for
g=1
and
p=1
but true for
g=2
and
p=1
. When
g=1
, it seems likely that

(4.3)

K_{p,2}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))=0~~\text{ for $p\leq\lfloor(\deg L-7)/3\rfloor$}.

In general, it is tempting to guess that
K_{p,2}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))=0
for
p\leq\lfloor(\deg L-4g-3)/(g+1)\rfloor
, but it is hard to make a precise prediction at this moment.

L p L^{p} maximal estimates for Weyl sums with k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 on 𝕋 \mathbb{T}
Xuezhi Chen, Changxing Miao, Jiye Yuan, Tengfei Zhao

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the L p L^{p} maximal estimates for the Weyl sums ∑ n = 1 N e 2 ​ π ​ i ​ ( n ​ x + n k ​ t ) \sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi i(nx+n^{k}t)} with higher-order k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 on 𝕋 \mathbb{T} , and obtain the positive and negative results. Especially for the case k = 3 k=3 , our result is sharp up to the endpoint. The main idea is to investigate the structure of the set where large values of Weyl sums are achieved by making use of the rational approximation and the refined estimate for the exponential sums.



Key Words:
Periodic Schrödinger equation; Weyl sums; Maximal estimate; Rational approximation; Super-level-set estimate.

AMS Classification:

42B25, 42B37, 35Q41.

1.
Introduction

In recent years, the pointwise convergence problem
for the solutions of the free dispersive equations has been widely studied. This problem was first posed by Carleson
[
10
]
for the free Schrödinger equation in the Euclidean spaces in 1980, which asks for the minimal
s
such that for any initial data
f(x)\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it holds that the free solution
e^{-it\Delta}f(x)
converges to

f(x)
almost everywhere as
t
tends to zero. And he obtained that
s\geq\frac{1}{4}
is a sufficient condition for the one-dimensional case by reducing the pointwise convergence problem to the maximal estimate for the Schrödinger operator
e^{-it\Delta}
, which becomes the general method to study this problem later. Then in 1981, Dahlberg and Kenig
[
12
]
proved that the result obtained by Carleson on
\mathbb{R}
is actually sharp. For the dispersive operator
e^{it(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}
with
\alpha>1
on
\mathbb{R}
, the corresponding sharp results have been obtained by Sjölin
[
22
]
in 1987. Recently, Bourgain
[
8
]
obtained the necessary condition
s\geq\frac{d}{2(d+1)}
for the Schrödinger operator and the dimension
d\geq 1
. Du-Guth-Li in
[
13
]
and Du-Zhang in
[
14
]
proved that the necessary condition due to Bourgain is also sufficient up to the endpoint in dimensions
d=2
and
d\geq 3
, respectively.

In the periodic case, the solution of
the free dispersive equation on
\mathbb{T}

\begin{cases}\frac{i}{2\pi}\partial_{t}u-\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}{\partial_{x}}\right)^{k}u=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}^{2},\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\,\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})\end{cases}

(1.1)

is given by

u(x,t)=\sum_{n}\widehat{u_{0}}(n)e^{2\pi i(n\cdot x+n^{k}t)}.

(1.2)

It is an interesting question to search for the optimal
s
such that
\lim_{t\to 0}u(x,t)=u_{0}(x)
almost everywhere whenever
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
. Similar to the Euclidean case, the strategy for studying the pointwise convergence problem in the periodic case is also to reduce it to the estimate of the corresponding maximal operator. In other words, we need an estimate like

\Big\|\sup_{0<t<1}\big|\sum_{|n|\leq N}a_{n}e^{2\pi i(n\cdot x+n^{k}t)}\big|\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}\lesssim N^{s_{p}}\Big(\sum_{|n|\leq N}|a_{n}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(1.3)

for some
s_{p}
. Using Strichartz estimates, Moyua and Vega in
[
19
]
proved that (
1.3
) holds for
k=2
,
1\leq p\leq 6
and
s_{p}>\frac{1}{3}
. One can see the corresponding results for dimension
d=2
in
[
23
]
and for higher dimensions
d\geq 3
in
[
11
]
.

The results for the periodic case is far from sharpness, and the pointwise convergence problem for the dispersive operator in the periodic case is still open. In the Euclidean case,
e^{-it\Delta}f(x)
decays to zero as the time
t
tends to
\infty
, which is the result of the local energy decaying of the solution to the dispersive equation. However in the periodic case, more difficulties in this problem arises, and the reason is that, at least formally, there are more resonances to be controlled, and there are not dispersive estimates and the local energy decaying for the dispersive operator
e^{-it\Delta}
in the periodic case.

The pointwise convergence problem for the dispersive operator can be reduced to the corresponding maximal estimate, which is related to harmonic analysis, PDEs and number theory. Consequently, tools from these variant fields can be employed to attack this problem.

For the case
k=2
, Barron in
[
7
]
and Baker in
[
5
]
used different methods to deal with the
L^{p}
maximal estimate of the quadratic Weyl sums
\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi i(nx+n^{2}t)}
which corresponds to (
1.3
) in the special case where
k=2
and
a_{n}=1
for all
1\leq n\leq N
.
And they obtained the sharp result
s>\frac{1}{4}
. And the corresponding sharp results of the higher dimension cases is due to the latter three authors and Barron
[
17
]
(with an appendix by Alex Barron). The maximal estimates for the Weyl sums
are of interest in their own right. The corresponding results reflect the constructive interference and the structure of the set on which Weyl sums obtain large values. And its application to the Talbot effect is related to the research for the diffraction of light in physics. Please see
[
15
]
for more details about the Talbot effect.

In this paper, we devote to this problem in the higher order cases
k\geq 3
on
\mathbb{T}
. Compared to the results for
k=2
in
[
5
]
and
[
7
]
,
the maximal estimates of the Weyl sums for
k\geq 3
are also difficult to obtain, due to the influence of the different geometric surface reflected by the parameter
k
. The following is the
L^{p}
maximal
estimates for Weyl sums.

Theorem 1.1
.

If the integer
k\geq 3
, then

\bigg\|\sup_{0<t<1}\Big|\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi i(nx+n^{k}t)}\Big|\bigg\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{2}+s_{p,k}+\epsilon}.

(1.4)

where for
k\geq 3

s_{p,k}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{k}},\quad&\textup{if}\,\,\,1\leq p\leq p_{k},\\
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p},\quad&\textup{if}\,\,\,p>p_{k},\end{cases}

(1.5)

where
p_{k}=\min\{2^{k-1},k(k-1)\}
.

Remark 1.2
.

(1) For the cases
k\geq 3
, we establish the maximal estimate (
1.4
) by investigating the structure of the set on which Weyl sums obtain large values and then getting the super-level-set estimates, which play a central role in obtaining Theorem
1.1
.

(2) Besides the results and methods for the case
k=2
in
[
7
]
, in order to obtain the
L^{p}
estimate for Weyl sums for the cases
k\geq 3
, we also utilize tools associated with the power-free and power-full properties of the integers, which can largely improve the results and is the key ingredient to get the sharp result for the case
k=3
.

Theorem 1.3

(Negative result)
.

For any integer
k\geq 3
, there holds for
N
large enough

\bigg\|\sup_{0<t<1}\Big|\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi i(nx+n^{k}t)}\Big|\bigg\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}\gtrsim N^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma_{p}}

(1.6)

where

\gamma_{p}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{4},\quad&\textup{if}\,\,1\leq p\leq 4,\\
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p},\quad&\textup{if}\,\,p>4.\end{cases}

(1.7)

Remark 1.4
.

(1) For the cases
k\geq 3
, we construct a nontrivial counterexample which gives the lower bound of the maximal estimates (
1.6
), which coincides with that in
[
5
]
and
[
7
]
for the case
k=2
.
We need the estimates of the general Gauss sums
\sum_{n=1}^{q}e^{2\pi i(\frac{a}{q}n^{k}+\frac{b}{q}n)}
for higher-order
k\geq 3
and count the number of the intervals for the set where Weyl sums obtain the lower value
\sqrt{q}
using pigeonholing and the prime number theorem.

(2) By Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
, we can see that the result of the maximal estimate for the case
k=3
is sharp up to the endpoint. However for the higher-order cases
k\geq 4
, this problem is left to be studied further to see that if more powerful tools than the Weyl’s differencing method and the decoupling method can be applied to the maximal estimates of Weyl sums.

(3) By Theorem
1.3
, we see that the lower bound obtained now is independent of the parameter
k
. It is unknown whether the lower bound is sharp or not.

Remark 1.5
.

Note that the possible values of
r_{k}
in (
1.5
), namely
2^{k-1}
and
k(k-1)
, are closely related to the pointwise estimate of the general Weyl sums. The value
2^{k-1}
is obtained by Weyl’s differencing method, while the value
k(k-1)
is obtained by Vinogradov’s mean value theorem established by Bourgain, Demeter and Guth
[
9
]
and Wooley
[
25
]
. It is conjectured that for any fixed integer
k\geq 2
and positive number
\epsilon>0
, if
P(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}x^{j}
with
|\alpha_{k}-a/q|\leq 1/q^{2}
and
(a,q)=1
, then

\sum_{n=1}^{N}e\left(P(n)\right)\lesssim_{k,\epsilon}N^{1+\epsilon}\left(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{q}{N^{k}}\right)^{1/k}.

(1.8)

As illustrated in Appendix
A
, if we assume the conjecture is valid, we could take
r_{k}=k+1
in (
1.5
), which is an potential improvement of Theorem
1.1
but still far from the lower bound given in Theorem
1.3
when
k\geq 4
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we give some preliminary tools and
lemmas. In Section
3
, we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
In Section
4
, we construct
the counterexample of the maximal estimates (
1.4
), which gives the proof of
Theorem
1.3
. And in Appendix
A
, we sketch a potential improvement of Theorem
1.1
assuming the Weyl sums estimate.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we present a series of lemmas that are instrumental in characterising the structure of the set of points for which the Weyl sum assumes a significant value.

For
\bm{u}=(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{k})
, define

S_{k}(\bm{u};N):=\sum_{n=1}^{N}e(u_{1}n+u_{2}n^{2}+\cdots+u_{k}n^{k}),

(2.1)

where
e(x):=e^{2\pi ix}
.
The following lemma gives an approximation of the Weyl sums.

Lemma 2.1

(Vaughan
[
24
]
, Theorem 7.2
)
.

Suppose that for integers
q
,
r_{1}
,
r_{2}
,
\ldots
,
r_{k}
satisfying

\gcd(q,r_{1},r_{2},\cdots,r_{k})=1,

we have

|u_{j}-\tfrac{r_{j}}{q}|\leq\xi_{j},\quad j=1,2,\cdots,k.

(2.2)

for some
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\cdots\xi_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

S_{k}(\bm{u},N)=q^{-1}S_{k}(q^{-1}\bm{r};q)I(\bm{\xi})+\Delta.

(2.3)

where
\bm{r}=(r_{1},r_{2},\cdots,r_{k})
,
\bm{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\cdots,\xi_{k})
,

I(\bm{\xi})=\int_{0}^{N}e(\xi_{1}z+\xi_{2}z^{2}+\cdots+\xi_{k}z^{k})\,\mathrm{d}z

(2.4)

and
\Delta
satisfies the bound

\Delta\lesssim q(1+|\xi_{1}|N+|\xi_{2}|N^{2}+\cdots+|\xi_{k}|N^{k}).

(2.5)

Remark 2.2
.

With the same notations as in Lemma
2.1
,
[
24
, Theorem 7.3]
tells us that the auxiliary
function
I(\xi)
satisfies

I(\xi)\lesssim N(1+\xi_{1}N+\cdots+\xi_{k}N^{k})^{-\frac{1}{k}},

(2.6)

which yields that

I(\xi)\lesssim N\min_{j=1,\cdots,k}\{1,\xi_{j}^{-\frac{1}{k}}N^{-\frac{j}{k}}\}.

(2.7)

Let

\omega_{N,k}(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi i(nx+n^{k}t)}

(2.8)

for
x\in\mathbb{T}
. It is easy to see that

\omega_{N,k}(x,t)=S_{k}(\bm{u};N)

with
u_{1}=x
,
u_{j}=0
for
2\leq j\leq k-1
and
u_{k}=t
.

To describe the structure of the set where large values of Weyl sums are achieved, we combine Theorem 4 in
[
4
]
with Theorem 3 in
[
2
]
to obtain the following result. For the strategy of the proof, one can see Appendix
A
and the reference therein.

Propostion 2.3
.

Let
k\geq 3
and some
\epsilon>0
. Suppose that

A>N^{1-\frac{1}{L}+\epsilon},

where

L=\min\{2^{k-1},k(k-1)\},

(2.9)

we have
|\omega_{N,k}(x,t)|\geq A
. Then there exist integers
q,r_{1},r_{k}
such that

1\leq q\leq(NA^{-1})^{k}N^{\epsilon},\quad\gcd(q,r_{1},r_{k})=1,

(2.10)

and

\displaystyle|x-\tfrac{r_{1}}{q}|\leq q^{-1}(NA^{-1})^{k}N^{-1+\epsilon},

(2.11)

\displaystyle|t-\tfrac{r_{k}}{q}|\leq q^{-1}(NA^{-1})^{k}N^{-k+\epsilon}.

(2.12)

For the case
k=2
of (
2.8
), Baker in
[
5
]
proved the sharp maximal estimates for Weyl sums using similar results as in Proposition
2.3

\bigg\|\sup_{0<t<1}\Big|\omega_{N,2}(x,t)\Big|\bigg\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}\lesssim N^{a(p)}(\log N)^{b(p)}

where

a(p)=\begin{cases}\frac{3p}{4},&(1\leq p\leq 4)\\
p-1,&(p>4)\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad b(p)=\begin{cases}1,&(p=4)\\
0,&(p\geq 1,p\neq 4).\end{cases}

However, to prove Theorem
1.1
, Proposition
2.3
is not enough to give the estimate (
1.4
). We also need some refined result (see
[
6
]
) for the Gauss sums to improve Proposition
2.3

as follows.

Definition 2.4

(
[
16
]
,
[
18
]
)
.

An integer number
m
is called

\bullet

r
-th power free
if any prime number
p\mid m
satisfies
p^{r}\nmid m
;

\bullet

r
-th power full
if any prime number
p\mid m
satisfies
p^{r}\mid m
.

We note that 1 is both
r
-th power free and
r
-th power full for any
r\in\mathbb{N}
.

For the numbers which are power-full, we recall the following result which shows the density of these numbers in the interval
[1,x]
with
x\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
16
]
)
.

For any integer
i\geq 2
, denote

\mathcal{F}_{i}=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:n\,\,\text{is}\,\,i\!-\!\text{th power full}\}\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,\mathcal{F}_{i}(x)=\mathcal{F}_{i}\cap[1,x].

(2.13)

Then we have

\#\mathcal{F}_{i}(x)\lesssim x^{\frac{1}{i}}.

(2.14)

Next, in order to estimate the Lebesgue measure of the set where large values of Weyl sums are achieved,
we recall two auxiliary lemmas.

The general bound
q^{1-\frac{1}{k}}
for the Gauss sum
S_{k,q}(\bm{b})=S_{k}(q^{-1}\bm{b};q)

defined by (
2.1
) is not enough to give the maximal estimate (
1.1
), thus
we utilize the new tools, that is associated to the notion for power-full and power-free, and give a new estimate
of the Gauss sum
S_{k,q}(\bm{b})
as follows.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
6
]
)
.

Write an integer
q\geq 1
as
q=q_{2}\cdots q_{k}
with
\gcd(q_{i},q_{j})=1
,
2\leq i<j\leq k
, such that

\bullet

q_{2}\geq 1
is cube free,

\bullet

q_{i}
is
i
-th power full but
(i+1)
-th power free when
3\leq i\leq k-1
,

\bullet

q_{k}
is
k
-th power full.

For
\bm{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
with

\gcd(q,b_{1},\cdots,b_{k})=1,

(2.15)

we have for
\epsilon>0

|S_{k,q}(\bm{b})|\leq\prod_{j=2}^{k}q_{j}^{1-\frac{1}{j}}q^{\epsilon}.

(2.16)

From the estimate for
S_{k,q}(\bm{b})
in Lemma
2.6
, we will see this estimate improves the general bound
q^{1-\frac{1}{k}}
, which will give an improved estimate for the Lebesgue measure of the set related to the large values of Weyl sums
\omega_{N,k}(x,t)
.

Using Lemma
2.6
,
we can improve the result of Proposition
2.3
.

Propostion 2.7
.

For
k\geq 3
and some
\epsilon>0
, suppose that

A>N^{1-\frac{1}{D}+\epsilon},

(2.17)

where

D=\min\{2^{k-1},k(k-1)\}.

(2.18)

If
|\omega_{N,k}(x,t)|\geq A
, then there exist positive integers
q_{2},\cdots,q_{k}
with

\gcd(q_{i},q_{j})=1
,
2\leq i<j\leq k
, such that

\bullet

q_{2}\geq 1
is cube free,

\bullet

q_{i}
is
i
-th power full but
(i+1)
-th power free when
3\leq i\leq k-1
,

\bullet

q_{k}
is
k
-th power full,

and

\prod_{j=2}^{k}q_{j}^{\frac{1}{j}}\leq N^{1+\epsilon}A^{-1}

(2.19)

and integers
r_{1},r_{k}
with

\gcd(q_{2}\cdots q_{k},r_{1},r_{k})=1

(2.20)

such that

\displaystyle|x-\tfrac{r_{1}}{q_{2}\cdots q_{k}}|\leq(NA^{-1})^{k}N^{-1+\epsilon}\prod_{j=2}^{k}q_{j}^{-\frac{k}{j}},

(2.21)

\displaystyle|t-\tfrac{r_{k}}{q_{2}\cdots q_{k}}|\leq(NA^{-1})^{k}N^{-k+\epsilon}\prod_{j=2}^{k}q_{j}^{-\frac{k}{j}}.

(2.22)

Proof.

Since
|\omega_{N,k}(x,t)|\geq A
, by Proposition
2.3
, then for
\epsilon>0
with (
2.17
) there exist integers
q,r_{1},r_{k}
such that

1\leq q\leq(NA^{-1})^{k}N^{\epsilon},\quad\gcd(q,r_{1},r_{k})=1,

(2.23)

and

\displaystyle|x-\tfrac{r_{1}}{q}|\leq q^{-1}(NA^{-1})^{k}N^{\epsilon},

(2.24)

\displaystyle|t-\tfrac{r_{k}}{q}|\leq q^{-1}(NA^{-1})^{k}N^{-k+\epsilon}.

(2.25)

By the approximation of Weyl sums as in Lemma
2.1

\omega_{N,k}(x,t)=q^{-1}S_{k}(q^{-1}\bm{r};q)I(\bm{\xi})+\Delta,

(2.26)

where
\bm{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\cdots,\xi_{k})
,

is given by (

),

and

are defined in Lemma

and

\displaystyle\xi_{1}=x-\tfrac{r_{1}}{q},\quad\xi_{k}=t-\tfrac{r_{k}}{q},

\displaystyle\xi_{j}=-\tfrac{r_{j}}{q}\,\,\text{with $r_{j}=0$ for}\,\,2\leq j\leq k-1.

Thus by triangle inequality, we have

(2.27)

By Lemma

3.
Positive result

For the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we use the following lemma which builds the relationship between the
L^{p}
norm of the integrable function and its level set estimate.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
6
]
, Lemma 3.1)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a metric space and
\nu
be a Radon measure on
\mathcal{X}
with

\nu(\mathcal{X})<\infty
. Let
M\leq N
be two positive numbers and
F:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow[0,N]
be
a function such that for any
M\leq A\leq N
,

\nu(\{x\in\mathcal{X}:F(x)\geq A\})\leq N^{a}A^{-b}.

(3.1)

Then for any
p>0
,

\int_{\mathcal{X}}F(x)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(x)\lesssim\nu(\mathcal{X})M^{p}+N^{a}M^{p-b}\log N+N^{p+a-b}.

(3.2)

For the convenience of readers, we give the outlines of the proof for Lemma
3.1
.
Split the integral region into two parts according to the large value and small value of
F(x)
, that is
\{x\in\mathcal{X}:F(x)<M\}
and
\{x\in\mathcal{X}:F(x)\geq M\}
. Then divide the region
\{x\in\mathcal{X}:F(x)\geq M\}
into
O(\log N)
regions by the dyadic values of
F(x)
. Taking the super-level-set estimate (
3.1
) for
F(x)
into account yields the result in Lemma
3.1
. See
[
6
]
for the details.

Using Lemma
3.1
, we see that to prove Theorem
1.1
and obtain the maximal estimate for Weyl sums (
1.4
), it is sufficient to
estimate the Lebesgue measure of the set where large values of Weyl sums
\omega_{N,k}(x,t)
are obtained.

With Proposition
2.7
and Lemma
2.5
, we have the following result.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that for some fixed
\epsilon>0

A>N^{1-\frac{1}{D}+\epsilon},

(3.3)

where

D=\min\{2^{k-1},k(k-1)\},\quad k\geq 3.

(3.4)

Let

M_{k}(A;N)=\Big\{x\in\mathbb{T}:\sup_{0<t<1}|\omega_{N,k}(x,t)|>A\Big\},

(3.5)

then

|M_{k}(A;N)|\lesssim N^{k+\epsilon}A^{-(k+1)}.

(3.6)

Proof.

Let
Q=(NA^{-1})^{k}
and

\mathcal{M}_{q_{2},\cdots,q_{k}}=\bigg\{x\in\mathbb{T}:|x-\tfrac{b}{q_{2}\cdots q_{k}}|\lesssim QN^{-1+\epsilon}\prod_{j=2}^{k}q_{j}^{-\frac{k}{j}},\,\,1\leq b\leq q_{2}\cdots q_{k}\bigg\}

(3.7)

with
q_{j}
for
2\leq j\leq k
satisfying the conditions
in Proposition
2.7
.

It is easy to see that

|\mathcal{M}_{q_{2},\cdots,q_{k}}|\lesssim(q_{2}\cdots q_{k})\cdot QN^{-1+\epsilon}\prod_{j=2}^{k}q_{j}^{-\frac{k}{j}},

(3.8)

and

M_{k}(A;N)\subset\bigcup_{(q_{2},\cdots,q_{k})\in\Omega}\mathcal{M}_{q_{2},\cdots,q_{k}},

(3.9)

where

\Omega=\bigg\{(q_{2},\cdots,q_{k})\in\mathbb{N}_{+}^{k-1}:q_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{j},3\leq j\leq k,\prod_{j=2}^{k}q_{j}^{\frac{1}{j}}\lesssim Q^{\frac{1}{k}}N^{\eta}\bigg\}

(3.10)

with
\eta>0
small.

By pigeonholing, we have

\begin{split}|M_{k}(A;N)|\lesssim&\max\bigg\{\vphantom{\prod_{j=2}^{k}}U(Q_{2},\cdots,Q_{k}):\text{dyadic}\,\,Q_{2},\cdots,Q_{k}\geq 1,\\
&\phantom{\max\{U(Q_{2},\cdots,}\prod_{j=2}^{k}Q_{j}^{\frac{1}{j}}\leq Q^{\frac{1}{k}}N^{\eta}\bigg\}(\log N)^{k},\end{split}

(3.11)

where

U(Q_{2},\cdots,Q_{k}):=\bigg|\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{j\sim Q_{j},2\leq j\leq k}\\
(q_{2},\cdots,q_{k})\in\Omega\end{subarray}}\mathcal{M}_{q_{2},\cdots,q_{k}}\bigg|.

(3.12)

From (
3.8
), (
3.12
) and Lemma
2.5
, we have

\begin{split}U(Q_{2},\cdots,Q_{k})&\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{j\sim Q_{j},2\leq j\leq k}\\
(q_{2},\cdots,q_{k})\in\Omega\end{subarray}}|\mathcal{M}_{q_{2},\cdots,q_{k}}|\\
&\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{j\sim Q_{j},2\leq j\leq k}\\
(q_{2},\cdots,q_{k})\in\Omega\end{subarray}}(q_{2}\cdots q_{k})\cdot QN^{-1+\epsilon}\prod_{j=2}^{k}q_{j}^{-\frac{k}{j}}\\
&\sim\Big(\sum_{q_{2}\sim Q_{2}}q_{2}^{1-\frac{k}{2}}\Big)QN^{-1+\epsilon}\prod_{j=3}^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{j}\sim Q_{j}\\
q_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{j}\end{subarray}}q_{j}^{1-\frac{k}{j}}\\
&\lesssim QN^{-1+\epsilon}\prod_{j=2}^{k}Q_{j}^{\beta_{j}},\end{split}

(3.13)

where

\beta_{j}=\begin{cases}1-\frac{k}{j}+1,\quad&\text{if}\,\,j=2,\\
1-\frac{k}{j}+\frac{1}{j},\quad&\text{if}\,\,3\leq j\leq k.\end{cases}

Since
k\geq 3
, we can see that
\beta_{j}\leq\tfrac{1}{j}
for
2\leq j\leq k
.
Thus we have

\prod_{j=2}^{k}Q_{j}^{\beta_{j}}\leq\prod_{j=2}^{k}Q_{j}^{\frac{1}{j}}\lesssim Q^{\frac{1}{k}}N^{\eta},

(3.14)

where the last inequality comes from the definition of
Q_{j}
in (
3.11
).

Combining (
3.11
), (
3.13
) with (
3.14
), we can obtain

\begin{split}|M_{k}(A;N)|&\lesssim QN^{-1+\epsilon}\cdot Q^{\frac{1}{k}}N^{\eta}\cdot(\log N)^{k}\\
&\sim(NA^{-1})^{k}\cdot N^{-1+\epsilon}\cdot(NA^{-1})\cdot N^{\eta}\cdot N^{\epsilon}\\
&\sim(NA^{-1})^{k+1}\cdot N^{-1+\epsilon}\\
&\sim N^{k+\epsilon}A^{-(k+1)}.\end{split}

(3.15)

∎

Now we will prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

For
D=\min\{2^{k-1},k(k-1)\}
with
k\geq 3
, then

D=\begin{cases}2^{k-1},\quad\text{if}\,\,3\leq k\leq 5\\
k(k-1),\quad\text{if}\,\,k\geq 6.\end{cases}

(3.16)

For the calculation of the maximal estimate for Weyl sums (
1.4
),
we only consider the cases
3\leq k\leq 5
, since
the calculation for the cases
k\geq 6
is similar.

Set

a=k,\quad b=k+1,\quad M=N^{1-\frac{1}{2^{k-1}}+\epsilon},

(3.17)

where
\epsilon
is taken small enough such that
M<N
.

Applying Proposition
2.7
, Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.1
to

I_{p}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sup_{0<t<1}|\omega_{N,k}(x,t)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x,

(3.18)

we obtain

I_{p}\lesssim M^{p}+N^{a}M^{p-b}\log N+N^{p+a-b}.

(3.19)

1^{\circ}
.

For
1\leq p\leq b
, we have
N^{a}M^{p-b}\geq N^{p+a-b}
.
Since for
k\geq 3
, there holds

1\geq\frac{k+1}{2^{k-1}},

then
M^{p}\geq N^{a}M^{p-b}
, which yields that

I_{p}\lesssim M^{p}\sim N^{(1-\frac{1}{2^{k-1}})p+\epsilon}.

(3.20)

2^{\circ}
.

For
p>b
, we have
N^{a}M^{p-b}<N^{p+a-b}
.

(a)

For
b<p\leq 2^{k-1}
, there holds
M^{p}\geq N^{p+a-b}
which yields that

I_{p}\lesssim M^{p}\sim N^{(1-\frac{1}{2^{k-1}})p+\epsilon}.

(3.21)

(b)

For
p>2^{k-1}
, there holds
M^{p}<N^{p+a-b}
which yields that

I_{p}\lesssim N^{p+a-b}\sim N^{p-1+\epsilon}.

(3.22)

Hence, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

∎

4.
Negative result

We first give a trivial lower bound for the
L^{p}
maximal estimates of
\omega_{N,k}(x,t)
,
which gives the sharp negative results in Theorem
1.3
for large
p
.

For
\forall\,\,p>1
, taking
E=[0,10^{-6}N^{-1}]
, by the constructive interference for
\omega_{N,k}(x,t)
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sup_{0<t<1}|\omega_{N,k}(x,t)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{E}\sup_{0<t<10^{-6}N^{-k}}|\omega_{N}(x,t)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle N\cdot|E|^{\frac{1}{p}}\gtrsim N\cdot N^{-\frac{1}{p}}=N^{1-\frac{1}{p}}.

Remark 4.1
.

From the estimate above and Theorem
1.1
, we can see that when
p\geq\min\{k(k-1),2^{k-1}\}
with
k\geq 3
, precisely when

p\geq\begin{cases}2^{k-1},&\quad\textup{for}\,\,3\leq k\leq 5,\\
k(k-1),&\quad\textup{for}\,\,k\geq 6,\end{cases}

the parameter
s_{p,k}
for the maximal estimate (
1.4
) in Theorem
1.1
is almost sharp
up to the
\epsilon
loss.

The simple example above gives the rough lower bound for the maximal estimates of Weyl sums, but is not good enough for this lower bound estimate for small
p
. Thus,
in order to give an example to obtain a better lower bound for (
1.6
) in Theorem
1.3
for small
p
,
we construct sets whose Lebesgue measure is greater than
O(1)
and on which
\omega_{N,k}
has nontrivial lower bound. For this purpose, we give some necessary tools.

First, we recall the Gauss sums.
For
a,q\in\mathbb{N}
and
b\in\mathbb{Z}
, define

S_{k}(a,b,q):=\sum_{n=1}^{q}e^{2\pi i(\frac{a}{q}n^{k}+\frac{b}{q}n)}.

(4.1)

It is easy to see that

S_{k}(a,b,q)=S_{k}(\tfrac{\bm{u}}{q};q)

with
\bm{u}=(b,0,\cdots,0,a)
which is
k
-dimensional.

Lemma 4.2

(
[
20
]
,
[
21
]
)
.

Let
a,q\in\mathbb{N}
and
b\in\mathbb{Z}
with
(a,q)=1
. Then, the following holds for the Gauss sums:

(1)

When
b
is even,

|S_{2}(a,b,q)|=\begin{cases}\sqrt{q},\quad&\text{if}\,\,q\,\,\text{is odd},\\
0,\quad&\text{if}\,\,q\equiv 2\,(\text{mod}\,\,4),\\
\sqrt{2q},\quad&\text{if}\,\,q\equiv 0\,(\text{mod}\,\,4).\end{cases}

(4.2)

(2)

When
b
is odd,

|S_{2}(a,b,q)|=\begin{cases}\sqrt{q},\quad&\text{if}\,\,q\,\,\text{is odd},\\
\sqrt{2q},\quad&\text{if}\,\,q\equiv 2\,(\text{mod}\,\,4),\\
0,\quad&\text{if}\,\,q\equiv 0\,(\text{mod}\,\,4).\end{cases}

(4.3)

For the case
k=2
in (
4.1
), which is the Schrödinger case, using the Gauss sums estimate which is Lemma
4.2
, Oh constructed a nontrivial example for the
L^{p}
maximal estimate for
\omega_{N,2}(x,t)
, which gives the lower bound for (
1.4
) with
k=2
. See
[
20
]
for the detail.

Now to construct the nontrivial counterexample for Theorem
1.3
, Lemma
4.2
is not enough to give the counterexample for the higher order cases
k\geq 3
, and we give the generalization of Lemma
4.2
to the high-degree cases,
which can be found in
[
1
]
. And we will see that the constructing of the counterexample for high-degree cases
k\geq 3
needs
q
to be of prime, which is different from the case
k=2
.

We now show that a positive proportion of choices for integral coefficients lead to a complete exponential sum modulo
q
of size
\gg q^{1/2}
.

Propostion 4.3

(An, Chu and Pierce
[
1
]
, Proposition 2.2)
.

Fix integers
k\geq 2
.
For each integer
q
and tuple
(a_{1},a_{2})
for
1\leq a_{j}\leq q
,
1\leq j\leq 2
,
there exist constants
0<\alpha_{1}<1
and
0<\alpha_{2}<1
with
\alpha_{2}
depending on
k
, such that for every prime
q\geq 3
with
q\nmid k
, at least
\alpha_{2}q^{2}
choices of
(a_{1},a_{2})
have
|S_{k}(a_{1},a_{2};q)|\geq\alpha_{1}q^{1/2}
. In fact, one can take
\alpha_{1}=1/2
and
\alpha_{2}=k^{-2}/4
.

In order to apply Proposition
4.3
to estimate the lower bound of
L^{p}
norm for
\sup_{0<t<1}|\omega_{N,k}(x,t)|
, we need the following corollary, which distinguishes the role of the highest-order coefficient.

Corollary 4.4
.

Fix integer
k\geq 2
.
Specify
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
to be as in Proposition
4.3
. For each prime
q
,
let
\mathcal{A}(q)
denote the set of
(a_{1},a_{2})
such that
1\leq a_{1},a_{2}\leq q
and
|S_{k}(a_{1},a_{2};q)|\geq\alpha_{1}q^{1/2}.

For each
a_{1},
define the “good set”

G(a_{1}):=\{a_{2}\in[1,q]:(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathcal{A}(q)\}.

Suppose that
q\geq 3
is a prime such that
q\nmid k
.
Then for at least one choice of
a_{1}(q)
depending on
q
, we have
\#G(a_{1}(q))\geq(\alpha_{2}/2)q.

Proof.

We prove this corollary by contradiction.
Assume for any
a_{1}\in[1,q]
, there holds
\#G(a_{1})<(\alpha_{2}/2)q
.

Then we see that

\#\mathcal{A}(q)\leq\sum_{a_{1}=1}^{q}\#G(a_{1})<(\alpha_{2}/2)q^{2}

which contradicts with the result of Proposition
4.3
,
\#\mathcal{A}(q)\geq\alpha_{2}q^{2}
. This fact implies the result in this corollary.
∎

Finally we give the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
k\geq 3
.
For
a,\,b,\,q\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
q\geq 3
is prime,
q\nmid k
, and
1\leq a<q
, define

J(q,a)=[\tfrac{a}{q}-\tfrac{1}{100N},\tfrac{a}{q}+\tfrac{1}{100N}]

(4.4)

and

\mathcal{M}_{k}(q,a,b)=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}^{2}:|x-\tfrac{a}{q}|\leq\tfrac{1}{100N},|t-\tfrac{b}{q}|\leq\tfrac{1}{100N^{k}}\}.

(4.5)

Since
q
is a prime, we have
\gcd(q,a,b)=1
with
1\leq a,b<q
.
Let

\bm{\beta}:=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})=\left(x-\frac{a}{q},t-\frac{b}{q}\right)

for
(x,t)\in\mathcal{M}_{k}(q,a,b)
.
Then by Lemma
2.1
, we have

\omega_{N,k}(x,t)=q^{-1}S_{k}(b,a,q)I(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})+\Delta

(4.6)

where

|\Delta|\lesssim q(1+|\beta_{1}|N+|\beta_{2}|N^{k})\lesssim q,

(4.7)

and

|I(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})|=\bigg|\int_{0}^{N}e^{2\pi i(\beta_{1}z+\beta_{2}z^{k})}\,\mathrm{d}z\bigg|\sim N.

(4.8)

By Proposition
4.3
,
there exist at least
\frac{1}{4k^{2}}q^{2}
choices of
(b,a)
with

1\leq a,b<q
, such that

|S_{k}(b,a,q)|\gtrsim\sqrt{q}.

(4.9)

Then by Corollary
4.4
, we have at least one choice of
b(q)\in\mathbb{Z}\cup[1,q)
, such that

\#G(b(q))\geq\tfrac{1}{8k^{2}}q.

(4.10)

This fact means that for a fixed
b(q)
, there exist at least
\tfrac{1}{8k^{2}}q
choices of
a\in\mathbb{N}\cup[1,q)

satisfying (
4.9
).

Taking prime number
q\in[c_{1}\sqrt{N},\sqrt{N}]
with
c_{1}
small,
it is easy to see that
J(q_{1},a_{1})
and

J(q_{2},a_{2})
are disjoint if
\frac{a_{1}}{q_{1}}\neq\frac{a_{2}}{q_{2}}
for
q_{1},q_{2}\in[c_{1}\sqrt{N},\sqrt{N}]
. By (
4.6
) -
(
4.9
), for
(x,t)\in\mathcal{M}_{k}(q,a,b)
, we have

|\omega_{N,k}(x,t)|\gtrsim q^{-1}|S_{k}(b,a,q)|\cdot|I(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})|\gtrsim\tfrac{N}{\sqrt{q}}\sim N^{\frac{3}{4}}.

(4.11)

Case 1,
1\leq p\leq 4
.

\begin{split}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sup_{0<t<1}|\omega_{N,k}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\geq&\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sup_{0<t<1}|\omega_{N,k}|\,\mathrm{d}x\\
\geq&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c_{1}\sqrt{N}\leq q\leq\sqrt{N},\\
q\,\,\text{prime}\end{subarray}}\sum_{a\in G(b(q))}\int_{J(q,a)}\sup_{0<t<1}|\omega_{N,k}|\,\mathrm{d}x\\
\gtrsim&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c_{1}\sqrt{N}\leq q\leq\sqrt{N},\\
q\,\,\text{prime}\end{subarray}}\sqrt{N}\cdot N^{\frac{3}{4}}N^{-1}\\
\gtrsim&\pi(\sqrt{N})\sqrt{N}\cdot N^{\frac{3}{4}}N^{-1}\\
\gtrsim&N^{\frac{3}{4}},\end{split}

(4.12)

where
b(q)
is chosen to be one fixed number such that (
4.10
) holds.

Case 2,
p>4
.

In this case, we can see that

\begin{split}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sup_{0<t<1}|\omega_{N,k}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\geq&\bigg(\int_{[0,10^{-6}N^{-1}]}\sup_{t\in[0,10^{-6}N^{-k}]}|\omega_{N,k}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
\gtrsim&N^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\geq N^{\frac{3}{4}}.\end{split}

(4.13)

The proof is completed.
∎

Appendix A
a potential improvement of Theorem
1.1
assuming the Weyl sums estimate

In this appendix, we will sketch how a better estimate of the Weyl sum (Conjecture
A.3
) can give a more precisely characterization of the set where the Weyl sums obtain significant values. The proof is mainly from Baker
[
3
, Chapter 4]
and one can also refer to Baker
[
2
,
4
]
.

Lemma A.1

(Dirichlet)
.

Let
\alpha
be a real number. Then for any
M\geq 1
, there exists a rational number
a/q
with
\gcd(a,q)=1
and
1\leq q\leq M
and

\Big|\alpha-\frac{a}{q}\Big|\leq\frac{1}{qM}.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
N\geq 1
and
k\geq 2
. Assume that the polynomial
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}x^{j}
satisfies

\alpha_{j}=\frac{a_{j}}{q}+\beta_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad|\beta_{j}|\leq\frac{1}{2k^{2}q}\frac{N}{N^{j}},\quad 1\leq j\leq k.

Denote that
e_{q}(x)=e^{2\pi ix/q}
. Let
d=\gcd(q,a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
and any integer
1\leq T\leq N
, we have

\sum_{n=1}^{T}e\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}n^{j}\Big)=q^{-1}\sum_{n=1}^{q}e_{q}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}n^{j}\Big)\int_{0}^{T}\!e\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}x^{j}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x+O(d^{\frac{1}{k}}q^{1-\frac{1}{k}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}}),

(A.1)

where

\int_{0}^{T}\!e\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}x^{j}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim N\min\left\{1,N^{-\frac{1}{k}}|\beta_{1}|^{-\frac{1}{k}},\ldots,N^{-1}|\beta_{k}|^{-\frac{1}{k}}\right\},

(A.2)

and

\sum_{n=1}^{q}e_{q}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}n^{j}\Big)=O(q^{1-(1/k)+\epsilon}d^{1/k}).

(A.3)

Conjecture A.3
.

For any fixed integer
k\geq 2
and number
\epsilon>0
, we have

\sum_{n=1}^{N}e\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}n^{j}\Big)\lesssim_{k,\epsilon}N^{1+\epsilon}\left(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{q}{N^{k}}\right)^{1/k},

(A.4)

if
|\alpha_{k}-a/q|\leq 1/q^{2}
with
(a,q)=1
.

Propostion A.4
.

Assume that Conjecture
A.3
holds. Let
N>N_{0}(k,\epsilon)
and suppose that

\Big|\sum_{n=1}^{N}e(tn^{k}+xn)\Big|\geq H>N^{1-\frac{1}{k}+\epsilon}

(A.5)

with
t,x\in[0,1)
.
Then there are integers
q,r_{1},r_{2}
such that

1\leq q\leq(NH^{-1})^{k}N^{\epsilon},\quad\gcd(q,r_{1},r_{2})=1,

(A.6)

and

\displaystyle\Big|t-\frac{r_{1}}{q}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq q^{-1}(NH^{-1})^{k}N^{-k+\epsilon},

(A.7)

\displaystyle\Big|x-\frac{r_{2}}{q}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq q^{-1}(NH^{-1})^{k}N^{-1+\epsilon}.

(A.8)

Proof of Proposition
A.4
.

By Dirichlet’s theorem (Lemma
A.1
), there exist
0\leq a\leq q_{0}\leq M=(NH^{-1})^{-k}N^{k-\epsilon}
with
\gcd(a,q_{0})=1
such that

\Big|t-\frac{a}{q_{0}}\Big|\leq\frac{1}{q_{0}M}\leq\frac{1}{q_{0}^{2}}.

(A.9)

Then Conjecture
A.3
yields that

\Big|\sum_{n=1}^{N}e(tn^{k}+xn)\Big|\lesssim_{k,\epsilon}N^{1+\epsilon/2k}\Big(\frac{1}{q_{0}}+\frac{q_{0}}{N^{k}}\Big)^{1/k}.

(A.10)

Comparing with (
A.5
), we have

(NH^{-1})^{-k}N^{-\epsilon/2}\lesssim\frac{1}{q_{0}}+\frac{q_{0}}{N^{k}}.

(A.11)

Note that the term

\frac{q_{0}}{N^{k}}\leq\frac{M}{N^{k}}=(NH^{-1})^{-k}N^{-\epsilon}

(A.12)

is much smaller than the left-hand side of the inequality (
A.11
) above. Hence we have

q_{0}\leq(NH^{-1})^{k}N^{\epsilon/2}.

(A.13)

Plugging
H>N^{1-\frac{1}{k}+\epsilon}
and
M=(NH^{-1})^{-k}N^{k-\epsilon}=H^{k}N^{-\epsilon}
into (
A.9
), we have

\Big|t-\frac{a}{q_{0}}\Big|\leq q_{0}^{-1}H^{-k}N^{\epsilon}<\frac{1}{N^{(k-1)\epsilon}q_{0}}\frac{N}{N^{k}}.

(A.14)

In order to apply Lemma
A.2
, we also need to give a rational approximation to
x
. By Lemma
A.1
again, there exist integers
1\leq q_{1}\leq 2k^{2}
and
b
with
\gcd(b,q_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}1}})=1
such that

\Big|q_{0}x-\frac{b}{q_{1}}\Big|\leq\frac{1}{2k^{2}q_{1}}.

(A.15)

Let
q=q_{0}q_{1}
,
r_{1}=aq_{1}
and
r_{2}=b
. Then (
A.14
) and (
A.15
) can be rewritten into

\Big|t-\frac{r_{1}}{q}\Big|<\frac{1}{2k^{2}q}\frac{N}{N^{k}},

(A.16)

and

\Big|x-\frac{r_{2}}{q}\Big|<\frac{1}{2k^{2}q}\frac{N}{N^{1}}.

(A.17)

Note that
\gcd(q,r_{1})=q_{1}\cdot\gcd(q_{0},a)=q_{1}
. By Lemma
A.2
, we have

\displaystyle H\leq\!\sum_{n=1}^{N}\!e(tn^{k}+xn)=\frac{1}{q}

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{q}\!e_{q}(r_{1}n^{k}+r_{2}n)\!\int_{0}^{N}\!\!e(\beta_{1}x^{k}+\beta_{2}x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+O(q^{1-\frac{1}{k}+\epsilon}q_{1}^{\frac{1}{k}}),

where
\beta_{1}=t-r_{1}/q
and
\beta_{2}=x-r_{2}/q
.
If
N
is sufficiently large, the error term

O(q^{1-\frac{1}{k}+\epsilon}q_{1}^{\frac{1}{k}})\leq\frac{1}{2}H,

(A.18)

which implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}H\leq\frac{1}{q}\sum_{n=1}^{q}\!e_{q}(r_{1}n^{k}+r_{2}n)\!\int_{0}^{N}\!\!e(\beta_{1}x^{k}+\beta_{2}x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

(A.19)

Note that (
A.2
) and (
A.3
) yields that

\int_{0}^{N}\!\!e(\beta_{1}x^{k}+\beta_{2}x)\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim N\min\Big\{1,N^{-1}|\beta_{1}|^{-1/k},N^{-1/k}|\beta_{2}|^{-1/k}\Big\},

(A.20)

and

\frac{1}{q}\sum_{n=1}^{q}\!e_{q}(r_{1}n^{k}+r_{2}n)=O(q_{1}^{1/k}q^{-{1/k}+\epsilon})=O_{k}(q^{-1/k}N^{\epsilon}).

(A.21)

Then we have

\max\{q,N|q\beta_{2}|,N^{k}|q\beta_{1}|\}\lesssim(NH^{-1})^{k}N^{k\epsilon}.

(A.22)

∎

This lemma is an analogy of Proposition
2.3
. Similarly, by introducing the definitions of power-free and power-full numbers, we can also get a refined rational approximation and a super-level-set estimate corresponding to Proposition
2.7
and Lemma
3.2
respectively with
D=k
instead of
\min\{2^{k-1},k(k-1)\}
. That is, for any
A>N^{1-\frac{1}{k}+\epsilon}
, we have the level set estimate

\Big|\Big\{x\in\mathbb{T}:\sup_{0<t<1}\big|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\!e(tn^{k}+xn)\big|>A\Big\}\Big|\lesssim N^{k+\epsilon}A^{-(k-1)}.

(A.23)

Then by Lemma
3.1
with
a=k+\epsilon
,
b=k+1
and
M>N^{1-\frac{1}{k}+\epsilon}
to be determined later, we obtain

\bigg\|\sup_{0<t<1}\Big|\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi i(nx+n^{k}t)}\Big|\bigg\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}\lesssim N^{\epsilon}(M+N^{\frac{k}{p}}M^{1-\frac{k+1}{p}}+N^{1-\frac{1}{p}}).

(A.24)

Take
M=N^{1-\frac{1}{k+1}}
, we get the optimal upper bound

\bigg\|\sup_{0<t<1}\Big|\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi i(nx+n^{k}t)}\Big|\bigg\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{2}+s_{p,k}+\epsilon}.

(A.25)

where for

s_{p,k}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{k+1},\quad&\textup{if}\,\,\,1\leq p\leq k+1,\\
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p},\quad&\textup{if}\,\,\,p>k+1.\end{cases}

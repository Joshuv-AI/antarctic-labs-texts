A Remark on the Rigidity of Delaunay Triangulated Plane
Song Dai 1

Abstract
. In [ 22 ] , under the uniformly acute condition, Wu showed the rigidity of the geodesic triangulated plane under Luo’s discrete conformality. In this article, by modifying Wu’s proof, we improve this result by weakening the uniformly acute condition to the uniformly Delaunay condition.



1.
Introduction

Let
T=(V,E,F)
be a triangulation of a surface
S
with or without boundary. Denote
|T|
as the underlying space of the complex
T
. A PL (piecewise linear) metric on
T
is a function,
l:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that for every
ijk\in F
,
ijk
forms a Euclidean triangle under the length
l
, denoted as
\triangle ijk
.
In
[
10
]
, Luo introduced the notion of the discrete conformality. Let
l,l^{\prime}
be two PL metrics on
T
. We call
l
is discrete conformal to
l^{\prime}
if there exists a function
u:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

l_{ij}=e^{(u_{i}+u_{j})/2}l^{\prime}_{ij},

denoted as
l^{\prime}=u*l
. The function
u
is called the conformal factor.

In recent years, the theory of Luo’s discrete conformality has developed in many directions, such as the prescribed curvature problems, the rigidity, the convergence, the numerical methods in computing conformal geometry and so on. One may refer to

. In this article, we focus on the rigidity of the triangulated plane.

For a triangle
\triangle ijk
, denote
\theta_{jk}^{i}
as the angle of
\angle jik
. A PL metric
l
is called

(1) uniformly nondegenerate if there is a constant
\epsilon>0
such that
\theta^{i}_{jk}\geq\epsilon
for every triangle
\triangle ijk\in F
;

(2) Delaunay if
\theta^{k}_{ij}+\theta^{l}_{ij}\leq\pi
for every adjacent triangles
\triangle ikj,\triangle ilj\in F
sharing the edge
ij\in E
;

(3) uniformly Delaunay if there is a constant
\epsilon>0
such that
\theta^{k}_{ij}+\theta^{l}_{ij}\leq\pi-\epsilon
for every adjacent triangles
\triangle ikj,\triangle ilj\in F
sharing the edge
ij\in E
;

(4) uniformly acute if there is a constant
\epsilon>0
such that
\theta^{i}_{jk}\leq\frac{\pi}{2}-\epsilon
for every triangle
\triangle ijk\in F
.

Remark 1.1
.

The Delaunay condition is equivalent to that for every adjacent triangles
\triangle ijk,\triangle ijl\in F
isometric embedding in
\mathbb{C}
, then
l\notin\text{int}(C)
, where
\text{int}(C)
is the interior of the circumscribed circle of
\triangle ijk
.

Remark 1.2
.

It is clear that under the uniformly nondegenerate condition with constant
\epsilon>0
,
\deg i
are uniformly bounded,
\deg i\leq\frac{2\pi}{\epsilon},\forall i\in V.

Denote
|T|^{o}
and
\partial|T|
be the interior and the boundary of
|T|
respectively. Denote
V^{o}=V\cap|T|^{o}
,
\partial V=V\cap\partial|T|
. Let
l
be a PL metric on
T
. For
i\in V^{o}
, denote
R_{i}
as the
1
-ring neighborhood of
i
, that is the subcomplex generated by
i
and
j
for every
ij\in E
. We abuse the notation
R_{i}
also as the underlying space
|R_{i}|
and the set of vertices
V(R_{i})
.
The curvature at
i
is defined as

K_{i}=2\pi-\sum\limits_{\triangle ijk\in F}\theta^{i}_{jk},

which only depends on the restriction of
l
on
R_{i}
.
Let
l^{\prime}=u*l
be also a PL metric discrete conformal to
l
. Then
K_{i}(u):\mathbb{R}^{\deg i+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is smooth with respect to
u
. From the direct calculation or
[
10
]
, one have

dK_{i}=-\sum\limits_{j:ij\in E}\eta_{ij}(du_{j}-du_{i}),

where

\eta_{ij}=\eta_{ij}(u)=\frac{1}{2}(\cot\theta^{k}_{ij}(u)+\cot\theta^{l}_{ij}(u))

for adjacent triangles
\triangle kij,\triangle lij\in F
sharing the edge
ij\in E
.
It is clear that under the Delaunay condition
\eta\geq 0
, and under the uniformly Delaunay condition
\eta\geq\epsilon>0.
We call
l
is flat if
K_{i}=0
for every
i\in V
.

Remark 1.3
.

For
i\in\partial V
, the curvature is defined as
K_{i}=\pi-\sum\limits_{jk:\triangle ijk\in F}\theta^{i}_{jk}.
It is clear that
K
is invariant under dilation, i.e.
u
being a constant.

A map
\phi:|T|\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is called a geodesic embedding if for every
ij\in E
,
\phi
maps
ij
to a segment connecting
\phi(i)
and
\phi(j)
, and
\phi
maps
|T|
homeomorphically to its image. If further
\phi
is surjective, we call
\phi
is a geodesic homeomorphism or a geodesic triangulated plane. It is clear that a geodesic embedding
\phi
gives a flat PL metric
l(\phi)
, or
l
for short, by using the Euclidean distance.

Let
\phi,\phi^{\prime}
be two geodesic triangulated planes with PL metric
l,l^{\prime}
. Suppose
l,l^{\prime}
are discrete conformal,
l^{\prime}=u*l
. The conformal factor
u
being a constant is clearly a solution. The rigidity problem is that whether
u
must be a constant. In
[
20
]
, Wu-Gu-Sun first gave an affirmative answer to this problem under the condition
T
being the standard hexagonal triangulation of the plane with
l\equiv 1
and
l^{\prime}
satisfying the uniformly acute condition. They also gave a counter-example for the case
l^{\prime}
being flat but not a geodesic triangulated plane. In
[
9
]
Luo-Sun-Wu improved the result above by showing that the rigidity of the standard hexagonal geodesic triangulated plane holds under the condition
l^{\prime}
satisfying the Delaunay condition. Dai-Ge-Ma also showed this result in
[
3
]
independently. Recently, Wu in
[
22
]
made a breakthrough to release the special case of the geodesic triangulated plane to general cases under certain conditions.

Theorem 1.4
.

(Theorem 1.2 in
[
22
]
)
Let
T
be a triangulated plane. Let
\phi:|T|\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a geodesic homeomorphism with the induced PL metric
l
. Let
\phi^{\prime}:|T|\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a geodesic embedding with the induced PL metric
l^{\prime}
. Suppose both
l,l^{\prime}
satisfy the uniformly nondegenerate condition and the uniformly acute condition. If
l
and
l^{\prime}
are discrete conformal,
l^{\prime}=u*l
, then
u
is a constant.

In this article, we modify Wu’s proof in
[
22
]
to improve this result by weakening the uniformly acute condition to the uniformly Delaunay condition.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
T
be a triangulated plane. Let
\phi:|T|\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a geodesic homeomorphism with the induced PL metric
l
. Suppose
l
satisfies the uniformly nondegenerate condition and the uniformly Delaunay condition. Let
\phi^{\prime}:|T|\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a geodesic embedding with the induced PL metric
l^{\prime}
. Suppose
l^{\prime}
satisfies the uniformly nondegenerate condition and the Delaunay condition. If
l
and
l^{\prime}
are discrete conformal,
l^{\prime}=u*l
, then
u
is a constant.

Remark 1.6
.

In
[
1
]
, Bobenko-Pinkall-Springborn observed the relation between Luo’s discrete conformality and the hyperbolic polyhedra in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
. In fact the rigidity problem in this article corresponds to the Cauchy rigidity of the ideal hyperbolic polyhedra and the Delaunay condition corresponds to the convexity of the hyperbolic polyhedra. One may also refer to
[
3
]
.

For the study of geodesic triangulations of other surfaces, one may refer to
[

,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
.

The article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we study the hyperbolic discrete conformality. In Section

2.
Hyperbolic discrete conformality

The notion of the hyperbolic discrete conformality was first introduced by Bobenko-Pinkall-Springborn in
[
1
]
. A hyperbolic PL metric on
T
is a function,
l^{h}:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that for every
ijk\in F
,
ijk
forms a hyperbolic triangle under the length
l^{h}
. Let
l^{h},l^{h\prime}
be two hyperbolic PL metrics on
T
. We call
l^{h}
is hyperbolic discrete conformal to
l^{h\prime}
if there exists a function
u^{h}:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\sinh\frac{l^{h}_{ij}}{2}=e^{(u^{h}_{i}+u^{h}_{j})/2}\sinh\frac{l^{h\prime}_{ij}}{2},

denoted as
l^{h\prime}=u^{h}*^{h}l^{h}
. The function
u^{h}
is called the hyperbolic conformal factor.
A hyperbolic PL metric is called Delaunay if for every adjacent hyperbolic triangles
\triangle ijk,\triangle ijl\in F
isometric embedding in the Poincaré disc
\mathbb{D}
, then
l\notin\text{int}(C)
, where
\text{int}(C)
is the interior of the circumscribed circle of
\triangle ijk
in the Euclidean sense.
The curvature is similarly defined by using the hyperbolic metric. A map
\phi^{h}:|T|\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is called a hyperbolic geodesic embedding if for every
ij\in E
,
\phi^{h}
maps
ij
to a hyperbolic geodesic segment connecting
\phi^{h}(i)
and
\phi^{h}(j)
, and
\phi^{h}
maps
|T|
homeomorphically to its image. It is clear that a hyperbolic geodesic embedding
\phi^{h}
gives a flat hyperbolic PL metric
l^{h}(\phi^{h})
, or
l^{h}
for short, by using the hyperbolic distance
d^{h}
,
l^{h}_{ij}=d^{h}(\phi^{h}(i),\phi^{h}(j))
.

Let
i\in V^{o}
and
R_{i}
be its
1
-ring neighborhood. Denote
D
as the unit disc with the Euclidean metric and
\mathbb{D}
as the unit disc with the hyperbolic metric. Let
\phi:R_{i}\rightarrow D\subset\mathbb{C}
be a geodesic embedding. It seems that
\phi
also induces a hyperbolic geodesic embedding
\phi^{h}:R_{i}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
by connecting the edges by the hyperbolic segments of
\mathbb{D}
and mapping the faces homeomorphically to its image. It is not true in general, whose reason will be clarified in the proof of the following lemma, while it indeed holds under certain conditions.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\phi:R_{i}\rightarrow D\subset\mathbb{C}
be a geodesic embedding such that
\theta_{ij}^{k}\geq\epsilon
for all
\triangle ijk\in R_{i}
, for some constant
\epsilon>0.
Suppose

l_{ij}<(1-|\phi(i)|^{2})\sin\epsilon,\quad\text{ for every }ij\in E.

Then
\phi
induces a hyperbolic geodesic embedding
\phi^{h}:R_{i}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
such that
\phi^{h}
coincides with
\phi
on the set of vertices, maps the edges to the hyperbolic segments and maps the faces homeomorphically to its image.

Proof.

Let
\deg i=m
. Let
z_{k}=\phi(j_{k})
,
j_{k}\in R_{i}\setminus\{i\}
,
k=1,\cdots,m
, be anti-clockwise on
\phi(R_{i})
, and
z_{0}=\phi(i)
.
Let
\exp_{z_{0}}
be the exponential map of the hyperbolic metric at
z_{0}
. Identifying
T_{z_{0}}\mathbb{D}
with
\mathbb{C}
by translating
z_{0}
to the origin, then for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, denote
v(z)=\exp_{z_{0}}^{-1}z\in\mathbb{C}
.
Then we only need to show the following claims.

(1)

\arg(\frac{v(z_{k+1})}{v(z_{k})})\in(0,\pi),\quad k=1,\cdots,m,

and

(2)

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\arg\big(\frac{v(z_{k+1})}{v(z_{k})}\big)=2\pi,

where
z_{m+1}=z_{1}
.
Fix
k\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
, denote

P=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\arg(\frac{z-z_{0}}{z_{k}-z_{0}})\in(0,\pi)\},

and

P_{h}=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:\arg(\frac{v(z)}{v(z_{k})})\in(0,\pi)\}.

We first show the claim (1). Since
\phi
is a geodesic embedding into
D
, we have
z_{k+1}\in P
. But if
z_{k+1}\in P\setminus P_{h}
, then
\phi^{h}
fails to be a hyperbolic geodesic embedding. So we need to rule out this possibility.

Let
\gamma_{h}
be the entire geodesic connecting
z_{0}
and
z_{k}
with respect to the hyperbolic metric. If
\gamma_{h}
is a straight line, then
z_{k+1}\in P_{h}
. Suppose
\gamma_{h}
is a circle, which is orthogonal to the boundary of the unit disc
D
. We denote
z_{*}
as its Euclidean center and
R
as its Euclidean radius. Then

R^{2}+1=|z_{*}|^{2}\leq(|z_{0}|+R)^{2}.

So

1-|z_{0}|^{2}\leq 2R|z_{0}|\leq 2R.

Then

\sin\frac{\angle z_{0}z_{*}z_{k}}{2}=\frac{l_{ij_{k}}}{2R}<\frac{(1-|z_{0}|^{2})\sin\epsilon}{2R}\leq\sin\epsilon.

So

\angle z_{0}z_{k+1}z_{k}\geq\epsilon>\frac{1}{2}\angle z_{0}z_{*}z_{k},

and

\angle z_{0}z_{k+1}z_{k}\leq\pi-\epsilon<\pi-\frac{1}{2}\angle z_{0}z_{*}z_{k}.

Then from the knowledge of the plane geometry, we see
z_{k}\in P_{h}
. We finish the proof of claim (
1
).

Next we show the claim (
2
). We have

(3)

\arg\big(\frac{v(z_{k+1})}{v(z_{k})}\big)+\arg\big(\frac{v(z_{k})}{z_{k}-z_{0}}\big)=\arg\big(\frac{z_{k+1}-z_{0}}{z_{k}-z_{0}}\big)+\arg\big(\frac{v(z_{k+1})}{z_{k+1}-z_{0}}\big)+2n\pi,

for some integer
n
. Notice that

\arg\big(\frac{v(z_{k})}{z_{k}-z_{0}}\big)\in(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}).

Then together with the claim (
1
), both the right hand side and the left hand side of the Equation (
3
) lies in
(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{3\pi}{2})
, so
n=0
. Then we obtain

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\arg\big(\frac{v(z_{k+1})}{v(z_{k})}\big)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\arg\big(\frac{z_{k+1}-z_{0}}{z_{k}-z_{0}}\big)=2\pi.

We finish the proof.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

In
[
22
]
, Wu showed that the acute condition also ensures that
\phi^{h}
is a hyperbolic geodesic embedding.

The discrete conformality and the hyperbolic discrete conformality are related as follows. One may refer to Lemma 5.1 in
[
22
]
for the proof.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\phi,\phi^{\prime}:|T|\rightarrow D\subset\mathbb{C}
be two geodesic embeddings with the induced PL metric
l,l^{\prime}
respectively. Suppose both
\phi,\phi^{\prime}
induce hyperbolic geodesic embeddings
\phi^{h},\phi^{h\prime}:|T|\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
with the induced hyperbolic PL metric
l^{h},l^{h\prime}
respectively. Then
l
and
l^{\prime}
are discrete conformal
l^{\prime}=u*l
with the conformal factor
u
if and only if
l
and
l^{\prime}
are hyperbolic discrete conformal
l^{h\prime}=u^{h}*^{h}l^{h}
with the hyperbolic conformal factor
u^{h}
, where
u
and
u^{h}
are related by

u^{h}_{i}=u_{i}+\log\frac{1-|z_{i}|^{2}}{1-|z_{i}^{\prime}|^{2}},

for
z_{i}=\phi(i)
,
z_{i}^{\prime}=\phi^{\prime}(i)
.

Remark 2.4
.

In fact the assumption that
\phi,\phi^{\prime}
induce hyperbolic geodesic embeddings is unnecessary if we consider the generalized (hyperbolic) PL metric.

3.
Maximum principles

Maximum principle plays a very important role in partial differential equations and geometric analysis. For the discrete conformal geometry, the curvature
K
, which is clearly nonlinear as an operator on functions on the set of vertices, also satisfies the maximum principle. The following lemma is a corollary of Theorem 3.1 in
[
9
]
. In
[
3
]
, there is another proof of the maximum principle for a special case.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
i\in V^{o}
and
R_{i}
be its
1
-ring neighborhood. Let
\phi,\phi^{\prime}:R_{i}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be two Delaunay geodesic embeddings with induced PL metric
l,l^{\prime}
respectively. Suppose
\phi
and
\phi^{\prime}
are discrete conformal,
l^{\prime}=u*l
. Then

\max\limits_{j\in R_{i}}|u_{j}|\leq\max\limits_{j\in\partial R_{i}}|u_{j}|.

Furthermore if
|u_{i}|=\max\limits_{j\in R_{i}}|u_{j}|
, then
u
is a constant on
R_{i}
.

As a direct corollary, we have the following maximum principle.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
T=(V,E,F)
be a triangulation of a closed surface with boundary. Let
\phi,\phi^{\prime}:|T|\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be two Delaunay geodesic embeddings with induced PL metric
l,l^{\prime}
respectively. Suppose
\phi
and
\phi^{\prime}
are discrete conformal,
l^{\prime}=u*l
. Then

\max\limits_{j\in V}|u_{j}|\leq\max\limits_{j\in\partial V}|u_{j}|.

Furthermore if
\max\limits_{j\in V^{o}}|u_{j}|=\max\limits_{j\in V}|u_{j}|
, then
u
is a constant on
V
.

For the hyperbolic setting, the minimum principle holds, even though we still call it “maximum principle”. The proof is in fact from Lemma 5.3 in
[
22
]
. We give a proof here to mention that the uniformly acute condition in Lemma 5.3 in
[
22
]
is just to ensure the hyperbolic geodesic embedding.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
i\in V^{o}
and
R_{i}
be its
1
-ring neighborhood. Let
\phi^{h},\phi^{h\prime}:R_{i}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be two Delaunay hyperbolic geodesic embeddings with induced hyperbolic PL metric
l^{h},l^{h\prime}
respectively. Suppose
\phi^{h}
and
\phi^{h\prime}
are hyperbolic discrete conformal,
l^{h\prime}=u^{h}*^{h}l^{h}
. Then

\min\limits_{j\in R_{i}}\{u^{h}_{j},0\}\geq\min\limits_{j\in\partial R_{i}}\{u^{h}_{j},0\}.

Furthermore if
u^{h}_{i}=\min\limits_{j\in R_{i}}\{u^{h}_{j},0\}
, then
u^{h}
is identically
0
on
R_{i}
.

Proof.

Since the hyperbolic discrete conformality and conformal factor are invariant under the hyperbolic isometric group, we may assume
\phi^{h}(i)=0
,
\phi^{h\prime}(i)=0
. Then
\phi,\phi^{\prime}
induce geodesic embeddings into
D\subset\mathbb{C}
with PL metric
l,l^{\prime}
respectively. Notice that the hyperbolic isometric group preserves circles, so
l,l^{\prime}
are also Delaunay. From Lemma
2.3
, since
l^{h\prime}=u^{h}*^{h}l^{h}
, we have
l
and
l^{\prime}
are also discrete conformal,
l^{\prime}=u*l
for

u_{j}=u^{h}_{j}-\log\frac{1-|z_{j}|^{2}}{1-|z_{j}^{\prime}|^{2}}.

In particular
u_{i}=u^{h}_{i}
. From the maximum principle Lemma
3.1
, we obtain there exists
j_{0}\in\partial R_{i}
such that
u_{j_{0}}\leq u_{i}
. Suppose
u^{h}_{i}\leq 0
. Then

|z^{\prime}_{j_{0}}|=l^{\prime}_{ij_{0}}=e^{(u_{i}+u_{j_{0}})/2}l_{ij_{0}}\leq l_{ij_{0}}=|z_{j_{0}}|.

Therefore

u_{j_{0}}^{h}=u_{j_{0}}+\log\frac{1-|z_{j_{0}}|^{2}}{1-|z_{j_{0}}^{\prime}|^{2}}\leq u_{i}+\log\frac{1-|z_{j_{0}}|^{2}}{1-|z_{j_{0}}^{\prime}|^{2}}\leq u_{i}=u_{i}^{h}.

So

\min\limits_{j\in R_{i}}\{u^{h}_{j},0\}\geq\min\limits_{j\in\partial R_{i}}\{u^{h}_{j},0\}.

Furthermore if
u^{h}_{i}=\min\limits_{j\in R_{i}}\{u^{h}_{j},0\}
, then
u^{h}_{i}=u_{i}\leq 0
and
u^{h}_{i}=u^{h}_{j_{0}}
for some
j_{0}\in\partial R_{i}
. Keep track the inequalities in the discussion above, from Lemma
3.1
we see
u\equiv 0
and then
u^{h}\equiv 0
on
R_{i}
.
∎

As a direct corollary, we have

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
T=(V,E,F)
be a triangulation of a closed surface with boundary. Let
\phi^{h},\phi^{h\prime}:|T|\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be two Delaunay hyperbolic geodesic embeddings with induced hyperbolic PL metric
l^{h},l^{h\prime}
respectively. Suppose
\phi^{h}
and
\phi^{h\prime}
are hyperbolic discrete conformal,
l^{h\prime}=u^{h}*^{h}l^{h}
. Then

\min\limits_{j\in V}\{u^{h}_{j},0\}\geq\min\limits_{j\in\partial V}\{u^{h}_{j},0\}.

Furthermore if
\min\limits_{j\in V^{o}}\{u^{h}_{j},0\}=\min\limits_{j\in V}\{u^{h}_{j},0\}
, then
u^{h}
is identically
0
on
V
.

4.
Modification of Wu’s Proof

In
[
22
]
, Wu showed Theorem
1.4
under the uniformly acute condition. Wu followed the strategy of He’s proof in his celebrated work
[
8
]
, where he showed the rigidity of infinite disc patterns. The proof has two steps, first to show the conformal factor
u
is bounded, second to show
u
is a constant.

In Section 3.1 of
[
22
]
, Wu showed the rigidity under the assumption of the boundedness of
u
, which is the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
T
be a triangulated plane. Let
\phi:|T|\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a geodesic homeomorphism with the induced PL metric
l
. Suppose
l
satisfies the uniformly nondegenerate condition and the uniformly Delaunay condition. Let
\phi^{\prime}:|T|\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a geodesic embedding with the induced PL metric
l^{\prime}
. Suppose
l^{\prime}
satisfies the Delaunay condition. If
l
and
l^{\prime}
are discrete conformal,
l^{\prime}=u*l
, with bounded conformal factor
u
, then
u
is a constant.

Notice that in Section 3.1 of
[
22
]
, the uniformly acute condition only plays the role to ensure that a small perturbation of
l
preserves the Delaunay condition. So the uniformly acute condition can be replaced by the uniformly Delaunay condition in Lemma
4.1
.

To show
u
is bounded, the following lemma plays a key role. In
[
22
]
, it is Lemma 2.9 except the uniformly acute condition is replaced by the uniformly Delaunay condition.
For
r>0
, denote
D_{r}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<r\}
be the open disc of radius
r
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
T=(V,E,F)
be a triangulated plane. Let
\phi,\phi^{\prime}:|T|\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be two geodesic embeddings with the induced PL metric
l,l^{\prime}
respectively. Suppose both
l,l^{\prime}
satisfy the uniformly nondegenerate condition with constant
\epsilon>0
and the Delaunay condition.
Suppose
l
and
l^{\prime}
are discrete conformal,
l^{\prime}=u*l
.
Let
r,r^{\prime}>0
. Let
T_{0}
be a subcomplex of
T
. Suppose

\phi(|T_{0}|)\subset D_{r},\quad D_{r^{\prime}}\subset\phi^{\prime}(|T_{0}|).

Then there is a constant
M=M(\epsilon)>0
such that for every
i\in V_{0}
satisfying
\phi^{\prime}(i)\in D_{r^{\prime}/2}
, we have

u_{i}\geq\log\frac{r^{\prime}}{r}-M.

We follow the idea of Wu’s proof in Section 5 of
[
22
]
, and modify the proof by an observation that the condition in Lemma
2.1
also implies the hyperbolic geodesic embedding. Before the proof of Lemma
4.2
, we show some basic estimates under the uniformly degenerate condition.

Lemma 4.3
.

For a triangle
\triangle ijk
satisfying the uniformly nondegenerate condition with constant
\epsilon>0
, then the length ratio is uniformly bounded,
\sin\epsilon\leq\frac{l_{ij}}{l_{ik}}\leq\sin^{-1}\epsilon
. As a corollary, for two PL metric on a triangle
ijk
satisfying the uniformly nondegenerate condition with constant
\epsilon>0
, which are discrete conformal with conformal factor
u
, then
u
has the gradient estimate
|u_{i}-u_{j}|\leq 4\log\sin\epsilon.

Proof.

For a triangle
\triangle ijk
, by the sine law, we have
\frac{l_{ij}}{l_{ik}}=\frac{\sin\angle\theta^{k}_{ij}}{\sin\angle\theta^{j}_{ik}}\geq\sin\angle\theta^{k}_{ij}\geq\sin\epsilon.
Let
l,l^{\prime}
be two PL metric on a triangle
ijk
satisfying the uniformly nondegenerate condition with constant
\epsilon>0
, and
l^{\prime}=u*l
. Then

e^{(u_{i}-u_{j})/2}=\frac{e^{(u_{i}+u_{k})/2}}{e^{(u_{j}+u_{k})/2}}=\frac{l^{\prime}_{ik}}{l_{ik}}\frac{l_{jk}}{l^{\prime}_{jk}}\leq\sin^{2}\epsilon.

So

u_{i}-u_{j}\leq 4\log\sin\epsilon.

∎

Now we prove the key estimate Lemma
4.2
.

Proof.

By approaching
D_{r^{\prime}}
by
D_{r^{\prime}-\delta}
, we may assume
\overline{D_{r^{\prime}}}\subset\phi^{\prime}(|T_{0}|)
. By scaling, we may assume
r=\frac{\sin^{3}\epsilon}{4}\leq\frac{1}{4}
and
r^{\prime}=1
.
Consider
V_{1}=\{i\in V:\phi^{\prime}(i)\in D=D_{1}\}
and
T_{1}
as the subcomplex generated by
V_{1}
. Then
\phi,\phi^{\prime}
map
|T_{1}|
into
D
.
We notice that
V_{1}
is finite.
In fact if
V_{1}
is infinite, then there exists
\{v_{n}\}_{n=1,2,\cdots}\in V_{1}
such that
v_{n}\rightarrow v_{\infty}\in\overline{D}
.
Since
v_{\infty}
lies in a triangle and the degree of a vertex is finite, it gives a contradiction. Let
z_{i}=\phi(i)
,
z_{i}^{\prime}=\phi^{\prime}(i)
.
Denote

u^{h}_{i}=u_{i}+\log\frac{1-|z_{i}|^{2}}{1-|z_{i}^{\prime}|^{2}}.

We claim
u_{i}^{h}\geq 0
for every
i\in V_{1}
. Let
i_{0}
attain the minimum of
u
in
V_{1}
.

(1) If
i_{0}\in V_{1}^{o}
and
l^{\prime}_{i_{0}j}<(1-|z^{\prime}_{i_{0}}|^{2})\sin\epsilon
, for every
i_{0}j\in E
, then from Lemma
2.1
,
\phi^{\prime}
induces a hyperbolic geodesic embedding
\phi^{h\prime}
from the
1
-ring neighborhood
R_{i_{0}}
of
i_{0}
into
\mathbb{D}
. Since
\phi^{\prime}
is Delaunay,
\phi^{h\prime}
is also Delaunay. Since
\phi(|V_{1}|)\subset D_{\sin^{3}\epsilon/4}
, for the same reason
\phi
also induces a Delaunay hyperbolic geodesic embedding
\phi^{h}
from
R_{i_{0}}
into
\mathbb{D}
. Then the hyperbolic maximum principle Lemma
3.3
implies
u_{i_{0}}^{h}\geq 0
.

(2) If
i_{0}\in V_{1}^{o}
and there exists
j_{0}\in V_{1}
,
i_{0}j_{0}\in E
such that
l^{\prime}_{i_{0}j_{0}}\geq(1-|z^{\prime}_{i_{0}}|^{2})\sin\epsilon
, then from Lemma
4.3

\displaystyle e^{u_{i_{0}}^{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{u_{i_{0}}}\cdot\frac{1-|z_{i_{0}}|^{2}}{1-|z_{i_{0}}^{\prime}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{l^{\prime}_{i_{0}j_{0}}}{l_{i_{0}j_{0}}}\cdot e^{(u_{i_{0}}-u_{j_{0}})/2}\cdot\frac{1-|z_{i_{0}}|^{2}}{1-|z_{i_{0}}^{\prime}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{l^{\prime}_{i_{0}j_{0}}}{l_{i_{0}j_{0}}}\cdot\sin^{2}\epsilon\cdot\frac{1-|z_{i_{0}}|^{2}}{1-|z_{i_{0}}^{\prime}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sin^{3}\epsilon\cdot\frac{1-|z_{i_{0}}|^{2}}{l_{i_{0}j_{0}}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sin^{3}\epsilon\cdot\frac{1/2}{2r}=1.

(3) If
i_{0}\in\partial V_{1}
, then there exists
j_{1}\in V
such that
j_{1}\notin D
.
Then
l^{\prime}_{i_{0}j_{1}}\geq 1-|z^{\prime}_{i_{0}}|.

As the estimates above, assume
\sin\epsilon\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we have

e^{u_{i_{0}}^{h}}\geq\frac{l^{\prime}_{i_{0}j_{1}}}{l_{i_{0}j_{1}}}\cdot\sin^{2}\epsilon\cdot\frac{1-|z_{i_{0}}|^{2}}{1-|z_{i_{0}}^{\prime}|^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{l_{i_{0}j_{1}}}\cdot\sin^{2}\epsilon\cdot\frac{1-|z_{i_{0}}|^{2}}{1+|z_{i_{0}}^{\prime}|}\geq\sin^{3}\epsilon\cdot\frac{1/2}{2r}=1.

So we show
u^{h}_{i}\geq 0
for every
i\in V_{1}
. Then for
i\in V_{0}
satisfying
\phi^{\prime}(i)\in D_{r^{\prime}/2}
, set
M=-\log\frac{\sin^{3}\epsilon}{2}
, we have

u_{i}=u_{i}^{h}-\log\frac{1-|z_{i}|^{2}}{1-|z_{i}^{\prime}|^{2}}\geq-\log\frac{1-|z_{i}|^{2}}{1-|z_{i}^{\prime}|^{2}}\geq\log(1-|z_{i}^{\prime}|^{2})\geq\log\frac{1}{2}=\log\frac{r^{\prime}}{r}-M.

∎

By using the estimate Lemma
4.2
and the conformal modulus, Wu showed the boundedness of
u
in Section 3.2 of

22
]
, which is the following lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
T

Blowing-up hermitian Yang–Mills connections
Andrew Clarke, Carl Tipler

Abstract
. We investigate hermitian Yang–Mills connections for pullback vector bundles on blow-ups of Kähler manifolds along submanifolds. Under some technical asumptions on the graded object of a simple and semi-stable vector bundle, we provide a necessary and sufficent numerical criterion for the pullback bundle to admit a sequence of hermitian Yang–Mills connections for polarisations that make the exceptional divisor sufficiently small, and show that those connections converge to the pulled back hermitian Yang-Mills connection of the graded object.

Primary: 53C07, Secondary: 53C55, 14J60

1.
Introduction

A cornerstone in gauge theory is the Hitchin–Kobayashi correspondence (
[
9
,
12
,
20
,
7
]
). This celebrated generalisation of the Narasimhan and Seshadri theorem asserts that a holomorphic vector bundle over a Kähler manifold carries an Hermite–Einstein metric if and only if it is polystable in the sense of Mumford and Takemoto (
[
14
,
19
]
). The interplay between the differential geometric side, in the form of the hermitian Yang–Mills connections (HYM for short) that originated from physics, and the algebro-geometric side, that of stability notions coming from moduli constructions, has had many applications and become a very fertile source of inspiration. Given that HYM connections are canonically attached to polystable vector bundles, it is natural to investigate their relations to natural maps between vector bundles, such as pullbacks. In this paper, we address the problem of pulling back HYM connections along blow-ups. While the similar problem for extremal Kähler metrics has seen many developments in the past ten years
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
18
,
17
,
5
]
, relatively little seems to be known about the behaviour of HYM connections under blow-ups
[
4
,
6
]
. In this paper, under some mild asumptions, we solve the problem for pullback of
semi-stable
vector bundles on blow-ups along smooth centers.

Let
\pi:X^{\prime}\to X
be the blow-up of a polarised Kähler manifold
(X,[\omega])
along a submanifold
Z\subset X
, and
E^{\prime}=\pi^{*}E
the pullback of a holomorphic vector bundle
E\to X
. For
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
,
L_{\varepsilon}:=\pi^{*}[\omega]-\varepsilon c_{1}(Z^{\prime})
defines a polarisation on
X^{\prime}
, where we denote by
Z^{\prime}=\pi^{-1}(Z)
the exceptional divisor. There are obstructions for
E^{\prime}
to admit HYM connections with respect to
\omega_{\varepsilon}\in c_{1}(L_{\varepsilon})
, with
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
. In particular,
E
should be
simple
and
semi-stable
with respect to
[\omega]
. In the latter case,
E
admits a Jordan–Holder filtration by semi-stable sheaves with polystable graded object
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
(see Section
2.2
for definitions). A further obstruction comes then from subsheaves of
E
arising from
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
. While those sheaves have the same slope as
E
, their pullbacks to
X^{\prime}
could destabilise
E^{\prime}
. Our main result asserts that, under some technical asumptions on
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
and on the dimension of
Z
, these are actually the only obstructions for
E^{\prime}
to carry a HYM connection. More precisely, from now on, and
until the end of the paper, we will assume that
Z\subset X
satisfies

\mathrm{codim}(Z)\geq 3.

Recall that a semi-stable holomorphic vector bundle
E\to(X,[\omega])
is said to be
sufficiently smooth
if its graded object
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
is locally free. We will assume further that the Jordan–Hölder filtration with locally-free subsheaves

0=\mathcal{F}_{0}\subset\mathcal{F}_{1}\subset\ldots\subset\mathcal{F}_{\ell}=E

is unique. For
1\leq i\leq\ell
, denote by
\mu_{L_{\varepsilon}}(\mathcal{F}_{i})=\frac{c_{1}(\pi^{*}\mathcal{F}_{i})\cdot L_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}}{\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{F}_{i})}
the slope of
\pi^{*}\mathcal{F}_{i}
on
(X^{\prime},L_{\varepsilon})
. In the following statements, when referring to a HYM connection
A
on
E
(resp. on
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
, or
\pi^{*}E
), we will implicitly assume that
A^{0,1}
is gauge equivalent to the holomorphic connection, or Dolbeault operator, of
E
(resp. of
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
, or
\pi^{*}E
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
E\to X
be a sufficiently smooth semi-stable holomorphic vector bundle on
(X,[\omega])
. Assume that the stable components of
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
are pairwise non-isomorphic and that
E
admits a unique Jordan–Holder filtration by locally free subsheaves.
Then, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and a sequence of HYM connections
(A_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})}
on
\pi^{*}E
with respect to
(\omega_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})}
if and only if

(1.1)

\forall\;i\in[\![1,\ell-1]\!],\>\mu_{L_{\varepsilon}}(\mathcal{F}_{i})\underset{\varepsilon\to 0}{<}\mu_{L_{\varepsilon}}(E).

In that case, if
A
denotes a HYM connection on
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
with respect to
\omega
, then
(A_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})}
can be chosen so that
A_{\varepsilon}\underset{\varepsilon\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\pi^{*}A
in any Sobolev norm.

The expression
\mu_{L_{\varepsilon}}(\mathcal{F})\underset{\varepsilon\to 0}{<}\mu_{L_{\varepsilon}}(E)
means that the first non-zero term in the
\varepsilon
-expansion for
\mu_{L_{\varepsilon}}(E)-\mu_{L_{\varepsilon}}(\mathcal{F})
is strictly positive. The conclusion of Theorem
1.1
can be rephrased as follows. If
\overline{\partial}_{\mathrm{Gr}(E)}
(resp.
\overline{\partial}_{E}
) stands for the Dolbeault operator of
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
(resp. of
E
) and if
h
is a Hermite–Einstein metric on
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
with respect to
\omega
, then there exist gauge transformations
(f_{\varepsilon})_{0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}}
on
X^{\prime}
such that the Chern connections of
(f_{\varepsilon}^{*}\pi^{*}\overline{\partial}_{E},\pi^{*}h)
are HYM with respect to
(\omega_{\varepsilon})_{0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}}
and converge to the Chern connection of
(\pi^{*}\overline{\partial}_{\mathrm{Gr}(E)},\pi^{*}h)
in any Sobolev norm.

Remark 1.2
.

The simplicity of
E
is implied by the hypotheses made on
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
(see
[
16
, Lemma 34]
). Semi-stability and condition (
1.1
) are also necessary to produce the connections
(A_{\varepsilon})
from Theorem
1.1
. The other three asumptions on
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
are technical. Assuming
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
to be locally free enables one to regard
E
as a smooth complex deformation of
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
and to work with the various connections on the same underlying complex vector bundle. We should warn the reader though that if one drops this asumption, Condition (
1.1
) may not be enough to ensure semi-stability of
\pi^{*}E
on
(X^{\prime},L_{\varepsilon})
(see the extra conditions in
[
15
, Theorem 1.10]
). On the other hand, the asumption on
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
having no pairwise isomorphic components is purely technical, and ensures that its automorphism group, which will provide obstructions in the perturbative theory, is abelian. Finally, the fact that the Jordan–Holder filtration is unique provides a special shape in the extension matrix from
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
to
E
, with non-zero terms above the diagonal. This is used to produce approximate solutions by induction on the number of stable components of
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
.

We now list some corollaries of Theorem
1.1
. First, the stable case :

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
E\to X
be a stable holomorphic vector bundle on
(X,[\omega])
and let
A
be a HYM connection on
E
with respect to
\omega
. Then, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and a sequence of HYM connections
(A_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})}
on
\pi^{*}E
with respect to
(\omega_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})}
such that
A_{\varepsilon}\underset{\varepsilon\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\pi^{*}A
in any Sobolev norm.

For the semi-stable case, Condition (
1.1
) reduces to a finite number of intersection product computations. One interesting feature comes from the second term in the expansion of
\mu_{L_{\varepsilon}}(E)
. It is the opposite of the slope of the restriction of
E
to
Z
. The following formula is proved in
[
15
, Section 4.1]
, where
m=\dim(Z)
:

(1.2)

\mu_{L_{\varepsilon}}(E)=\mu_{L}(E)-\dbinom{n-1}{m-1}\mu_{L_{|Z}}(E_{|Z})\varepsilon^{n-m}+O(\varepsilon^{n-m+1}).

We then have :

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
E\to X
be a sufficiently smooth semi-stable holomorphic vector bundle on
(X,[\omega])
. Assume that the stable components of
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
are pairwise non-isomorphic and that the Jordan–Holder filtration with locally free subsheaves is unique. Denote by
A
an HYM connection on
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
with respect to
\omega
. If

(1.3)

\forall\;i\in[\![1,\ell-1]\!],\>\mu_{L_{|Z}}(E_{|Z})<\mu_{L_{|Z}}((\mathcal{F}_{i})_{|Z}),

then, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and a sequence of HYM connections
(A_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})}
on
\pi^{*}E
with respect to
(\omega_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})}
converging to
\pi^{*}A
in any Sobolev norm.

Condition (
1.3
) was checked on explicit examples in
[
15
, Section 4.5]
to produce stable perturbations of tangent sheaves by blow-ups, and our result provides information on the associated connections and their asymptotic behaviour. Note that by the Mehta–Ramanathan theorem
[
13
]
, if
[\omega]=c_{1}(L)
is integral, and if
Z
is a generic intersection of divisors in linear systems
|L^{k}|
, then
E_{|Z}
is semi-stable as soon as
E
is. In that case, Condition (
1.3
) cannot be satisfied, and it seems unlikely that Condition (
1.1
) will hold true. Hence, blowing-up such subvarieties tend to destabilise a semi-stable bundle.

In general, we expect that it should not be too hard to obtain stability of sufficiently smooth pulled back bundles under condition (
1.1
) with purely algebraic methods. However, we emphasize that the Hitchin–Kobayashi correspondence doesn’t provide any information on the asymptotic behaviour of the associated HYM connections, which is then the main content of Theorem
1.1
. Nevertheless, we state the following corollary, that extends
[
15
, Theorem 1.10]
to a non-equivariant situation:

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
E\to X
be a sufficiently smooth semi-stable holomorphic vector bundle on
(X,[\omega])
. Assume that the stable components of
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
are pairwise non-isomorphic and that the Jordan–Holder filtration with locally free subsheaves is unique.
Then, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that
\pi^{*}E\to(X^{\prime},L_{\varepsilon})
is

(i)

stable if and only if for all
i\in[\![1,\ell-1]\!],\>\mu_{L_{\varepsilon}}(\mathcal{F}_{i})\underset{\varepsilon\to 0}{<}\mu_{L_{\varepsilon}}(E),

(ii)

semi-stable if and only if for all
i\in[\![1,\ell-1]\!],\>\mu_{L_{\varepsilon}}(\mathcal{F}_{i})\underset{\varepsilon\to 0}{\leq}\mu_{L_{\varepsilon}}(E),

(iii)

unstable otherwise.

Finally, we comment on previous related works. Theorem
1.1
extends results from
[
4
,
6
]
where blow-ups of HYM connections along points are considered. In the present paper, we consider blow-ups along any smooth subvariety, and also cover the semi-stable situation, which is technically more involved due to the presence of automorphisms of the graded object that obstruct the linear theory. While
[
6
]
is a gluing construction of a similar vein to the various solutions to the analogous problem of producing extremal Kähler metrics on blow-ups
[
2
,
3
,
18
,
17
,
5
]
, one of the key feature in our approach is to directly apply the quantitative implicit function theorem, closely following the method developed in
[
16
]
. The main new technical input is in Section
3
, where we perform a precise study of the linear operators involved in relation to the geometry of the blow-up. The rest of the argument, in Sections
4
and
5
, is more standard, and follows the work of Sektnan and the second author
[
16
]
.

Outline:

In Section
2
, we recall basic material about HYM connections and stability. We then perform in Section
3
the analysis of the linear theory on the blow-up. Relying on this, in Section
4
we explain how to produce approximate solutions to the HYM equations. This section, and the one that follows, rely on the treatment in
[
16
]
. Then, we perturb those approximate solutions in Section
5
to actual solutions, which concludes the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The corollaries are addressed in Section
5.3
.

Preliminaries

In Sections
2.1
and
2.2
we introduce the notions of HYM connections and slope stability, together with some general results, and refer the reader to
[
10
]
and
[
8
]
. From Section
3
we start to specialise the discussion to blow-ups. In particular, in Section
3.2
, we provide various asymptotic expressions for the linearisation of the HYM equation on the blow-up. Those results will be used in Section

2.1.
The hermitian Yang–Mills equation

Let
E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Kähler manifold
X
. A hermitian metric on
E
is
Hermite–Einstein
with respect to a Kähler metric with Kähler form
\omega
if the curvature
F_{h}\in\Omega^{2}\left(X,\End E\right)
of the corresponding Chern connection satisfies

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\omega}\left(iF_{h}\right)=c\Id_{E}

for some real constant
c
. Equivalently, if
h
is some hermitian metric on the smooth complex vector bundle underlying
E
, a hermitian connection
A
on
(E,h)
is said to be
hermitian Yang–Mills
if it satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}F_{A}^{0,2}&=&0,\\
\Lambda_{\omega}\left(iF_{A}\right)&=&c\Id_{E}.\end{array}\right.

The first equation of this system implies that the
(0,1)
-part of
A
determines a holomorphic structure on
E
, while the second that
h
is Hermite–Einstein for this holomorphic structure. We will try to find hermitian Yang–Mills connections within the complex gauge group orbit, which we now define. The
complex gauge group
is

\mathscr{G}^{\mathbb{C}}(E)=\Gamma\left(\GL\left(E,\mathbb{C}\right)\right).

We note that the set of unitary gauge transformations preserves the metric
h
so, taking account of the fibre-wise polar decomposition of an element of
\mathscr{G}^{\mathbb{C}}(E)
, we consider the bundle
\End_{H}(E,h)
of Hermitian endomorphisms of
E
and the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{C}}(E,h):=\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{C}}(E)\cap\Gamma(\End_{H}(E,h))=\{e^{s}\ :\ s\in\Gamma(\End_{H}(E,h))\}.

If
\bar{\partial}
is the Dolbeault operator defining the holomorphic structure on
E
, then
f\circ\bar{\partial}\circ f^{-1}
defines a biholomorphic complex structure on
E
. Let
d_{A}=\partial_{A}+\bar{\partial}_{A}
be the Chern connection of
(E,h)
with respect to the original complex structure (that is
\bar{\partial}_{A}=\bar{\partial}
). Then the Chern connection
A^{f}
of
h
with respect to
f\circ\bar{\partial}\circ f^{-1}
is

d_{A^{f}}=(f^{*})^{-1}\circ\partial_{A}\circ(f^{*})+f\circ\bar{\partial}\circ f^{-1}.

Solving the hermitian Yang–Mills equation is equivalent to solving

\Psi(s)=c\Id_{E}

where

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Psi:&\Gamma(\End_{H}(E,h))&\longrightarrow&\Gamma(\End_{H}(E,h)),\\
&s&\longmapsto&i\Lambda_{\omega}(F_{A^{\exp(s)}}),\end{array}

and where
\Gamma(\End_{H}(E,h))
is the tangent space to
\mathscr{G}^{\mathbb{C}}(E,h)
at the identity. For a connection
A
on
E
, the Laplace operator
\Delta_{A}
is

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Delta_{A}=i\Lambda_{\omega}\left(\bar{\partial}_{A}\partial_{A}-\partial_{A}\bar{\partial}_{A}\right).

If
A_{\End E}
denote the connection induced by
A
on
\End E
, then :

Lemma 2.1
.

If
A
is the Chern connection of
(E,\overline{\partial},h)
, the differential of
\Psi
at identity is

d\Psi_{\Id_{E}}=\Delta_{A_{\End E}}.

If moreover
A
is assumed to be hermitian Yang–Mills, then the kernel of
\Delta_{A_{\End E}}
acting on
\Gamma(\End(E))
is given by the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{aut}(E)
of the space of automorphisms
\Aut(E)
of
(E,\overline{\partial})
.

The last statement about the kernel follows from the Kähler identities and the Akizuki-Nakano identity that imply
\Delta_{A_{\End E}}=\partial^{*}_{A}\partial_{A}+\bar{\partial}_{A}^{*}\bar{\partial}_{A}
, the two terms of which are equal if
A
is Hermitian Yang-Mills. The operator
\Delta_{A_{\End E}}
being elliptic and self-adjoint,
\mathfrak{aut}(E)
will then appear as a cokernel in the linear theory for perturbations of hermitian Yang–Mills connections.

2.2.
Slope stability

We recall some basic facts about slope stability, as introduced by
[
14
,
19
]
, and refer the interested reader to
[
8
]
for a detailed treatment. We denote here
L:=[\omega]
the polarisation of the
n
-dimensional Kähler manifold
X
.

Definition 2.2
.

For
\mathcal{E}
a torsion-free coherent sheaf on
X
, the slope
\mu_{L}(\mathcal{E})\in\mathbb{Q}
(with respect to
L
) is given by the intersection formula

(2.3)

\mu_{L}(\mathcal{E})=\frac{\deg_{L}(\mathcal{E})}{\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E})},

where
\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E})
denotes the rank of
\mathcal{E}
while
\deg_{L}(\mathcal{E})=c_{1}(\mathcal{E})\cdot L^{n-1}
stands for its degree. Then,
\mathcal{E}
is said to be
slope semi-stable
(resp.
slope stable
) with respect to
L
if for any coherent subsheaf
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{E}
with
0<\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{F})<\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E})
, one has

\displaystyle\mu_{L}(\mathcal{F})\leq\mu_{L}(\mathcal{E})\>\textrm{( resp. }\mu_{L}(\mathcal{F})<\mu_{L}(\mathcal{E})).

A direct sum of slope stable sheaves of the same slope
is said to be
slope polystable
.

In this paper, we will often omit “slope” and simply refer to stability of a sheaf, the polarisation being implicit. We will make the standard identification of a holomorphic vector bundle
E
with its sheaf of sections, and thus talk about slope stability notions for vector bundles as well. In that case slope stability relates nicely to differential geometry via the Hitchin–Kobayashi correspondence :

Theorem 2.3

(
[
9
,
12
,
20
,
7
]
)
.

There exists a Hermite–Einstein metric on
E
with respect to
\omega
if and only if
E
is polystable with respect to
L

We will be mostly interested in semi-stable vector bundles. A
Jordan–Hölder filtration
for a torsion-free sheaf
\mathcal{E}
is a filtration by coherent saturated subsheaves:

(2.4)

\displaystyle 0=\mathcal{F}_{0}\subset\mathcal{F}_{1}\subset\ldots\subset\mathcal{F}_{\ell}=\mathcal{E},

such that the corresponding quotients,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{i}=\frac{\mathcal{F}_{i}}{\mathcal{F}_{i-1}},

for
i=1,\ldots,\ell
, are stable with slope
\mu_{L}(\mathcal{G}_{i})=\mu_{L}(\mathcal{E})
.
In particular, the graded object of this filtration

(2.6)

\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{E}):=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{l}\mathcal{G}_{i}

is polystable. From
[
8
, Section 1]
, we have the standard existence and uniqueness result:

Proposition 2.4
.

Any semi-stable coherent torsion-free sheaf
\mathcal{E}
on
(X,L)
admits a Jordan–Hölder filtration, and the double dual of the graded object
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{E})^{**}
of such filtrations is unique up to isomorphism.

When
E
is locally-free and semi-stable, we say that it is
sufficiently smooth
if
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
is locally-free. In that case, we denote
\mathfrak{E}_{[\omega]}
the set of holomorphic subbundles of
E
built out of successive extensions of some of the stable components of
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
. Equivalently,
\mathfrak{E}_{[\omega]}
is the set of holomorphic subbundles of
E
arising in a Jordan-Holder filtration for
E
. Finally, we recall that a necessary condition for
E
to be stable is simplicity, that is
\Aut(E)=\mathbb{C}^{*}\cdot\Id_{E}
.

3.
Geometry of the blow-up

We consider now
Z\subset X
a
m
-dimensional complex submanifold of codimension
r=n-m\geq 2
and the blow-up map

\pi:\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}(X)\to X.

We will denote by
X^{\prime}=\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}(X)
the blown-up manifold and by
Z^{\prime}=\pi^{-1}(Z)
the exceptional divisor. We denote by

L_{\varepsilon}:=\pi^{*}L-\varepsilon[Z^{\prime}]

a polarisation on
X^{\prime}
, for
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
. Let
E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle, and denote by
E^{\prime}=\pi^{*}E
the pulled back bundle. For any holomorphic subbundle
F\subset E
, the intersection numbers
\mu_{L_{\varepsilon}}(\pi^{*}E)-\mu_{L_{\varepsilon}}(\pi^{*}F)
admit expansions in
\varepsilon
, with first term given by
\mu_{L}(E)-\mu_{L}(F)
. For that reason, given the Hitchin–Kobayashi correspondence in Theorem
2.3
, semi-stability of
E
on
(X,L)
is a necessary condition for its pullback
E^{\prime}
to admit an HYM connection with respect to a Kähler metric in
L_{\varepsilon}
, for all
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
. Another necessary condition is simplicity of
E^{\prime}
, which, by Hartogs’ theorem, is equivalent to simplicity of
E
. Then, natural candidates to test for stability of
E^{\prime}
are given by the pullbacks of elements in
\mathfrak{E}_{[\omega]}
, and Condition (
1.1
) clearly is necessary for
E^{\prime}
to be stable in the polarisations we consider, and thus to admit an HYM connection. Hence, we will assume
E
to be simple, semi-stable, and to satisfy (
1.1
). We now turn back to the differential geometry of the blow-up.

3.1.
Decomposition on spaces of sections

We have a commutative diagramm:

\begin{array}[]{ccc}Z^{\prime}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\longrightarrow}}&X^{\prime}\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
Z&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&X\end{array}

where
\iota_{0}
and
\iota
denote the inclusions, while the vertical arrows are given by the projection map
\pi
. We then have a pullback map on sections

\pi^{*}:\Gamma(X,\End(E))\longrightarrow\Gamma(X^{\prime},\End(\pi^{*}E))

as well as a restriction map :

\iota^{*}:\Gamma(X^{\prime},\End(\pi^{*}E))\longrightarrow\Gamma(Z^{\prime},\End(\iota^{*}\pi^{*}E)).

Our goal now is to fit those maps in a short exact sequence, that will in the end split the space
\Gamma(X^{\prime},\End(\pi^{*}E))
.
If
N_{Z}=TX_{|Z}/TZ
denotes the normal bundle of
Z
in
X
, then
Z^{\prime}\simeq\mathbb{P}(N_{Z})
, and we can fix a
(1,1)
-form
\lambda\in c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(N_{Z})}(1))
that restricts to Kähler metrics on the fibers of
\mathbb{P}(N_{Z})\to Z
. We also fix a Kähler form
\omega\in c_{1}(L)
on
X
, and consider its restriction to
Z
. We then have a Kähler
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{r-1}
-fibration :

\pi:(Z^{\prime},\lambda)\longrightarrow(Z,\omega).

By averaging along fibers as described in
[
16
, Section 2.3]
, we obtain a splitting

(3.1)

\Gamma(Z^{\prime},\End(\iota^{*}\pi^{*}E))=\pi^{*}(\Gamma(Z,\End(\iota_{0}^{*}E)))\oplus\Gamma_{0}(Z^{\prime},\End(\iota^{*}\pi^{*}E)).

We will omit the
\iota^{*}
and
\pi^{*}
to simplify notation. Using the projection on the second factor

p_{0}:\Gamma(Z^{\prime},\End(E))\to\Gamma_{0}(Z^{\prime},\End(E))

in (
3.1
), we deduce a short exact sequence :

0\longrightarrow\Gamma(X,\End(E))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Gamma(X^{\prime},\End(E))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p_{0}\circ\iota^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Gamma_{0}(Z^{\prime},\End(E))\longrightarrow 0.

We can actually split this sequence by mean of a linear extension operator

\iota_{*}:\Gamma_{0}(Z^{\prime},\End(E))\longrightarrow\Gamma(X^{\prime},\End(E))

such that

p_{0}\circ\iota^{*}\circ\iota_{*}=\mathrm{Id}.

This can be done using bump functions and a standard partition of unity argument. The outcome is an isomorphism :

(3.2)

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Gamma(X^{\prime},\End(E))&\longrightarrow&\Gamma(X,\End(E))\oplus\Gamma_{0}(Z^{\prime},\End(E))\\
s&\longmapsto&(s-\iota_{*}\circ p_{0}\circ\iota^{*}s\;,\;p_{0}\circ\iota^{*}s),\end{array}

with inverse map
(s_{X},s_{Z})\mapsto(\pi^{*}s_{X}+\iota_{*}s_{Z})
. This splits the Lie algebra of gauge transformations, and will be used to identify contributions coming from
X
and from
Z^{\prime}
in the
\varepsilon
-expansion of the linearisation, which we describe in the next section. From now on, by abuse of notations, we will consider the spaces
\Gamma(X,\End(E))
and
\Gamma_{0}(Z^{\prime},\End(E))
as subspaces of
\Gamma(X^{\prime},\End(\pi^{*}E))
, and denote
s=s_{X}+s_{Z}
the decomposition of an element
s\in\Gamma(X^{\prime},\End(E))
.

3.2.
Decomposition of the Laplace operator

We extend
\lambda
to a closed
(1,1)
-form over
X^{\prime}
as in
[
21
, Section 3.3]
and consider the family of Kähler metrics on
X^{\prime}
:

\omega_{\varepsilon}=\pi^{*}\omega+\varepsilon\lambda\in c_{1}(L_{\varepsilon}),\>0<\varepsilon\ll 1.

Let
A
be a Hermitian connection on
E
,
which we pull back to
X^{\prime}
and extend to the bundle
\End(\pi^{*}E)
. We will now study the Laplace operator

\displaystyle\Delta_{\varepsilon}s=i\Lambda_{\varepsilon}(\bar{\partial}_{A}\partial_{A}-\partial_{A}\bar{\partial}_{A})s

acting on the various components of
s=s_{X}+s_{Z}\in\Gamma(X^{\prime},\End(E))
, where
\Lambda_{\varepsilon}
is the Lefschetz operator for the metric
\omega_{\varepsilon}
. For this, we need to introduce an operator on
Z^{\prime}
.

Definition 3.1
.

The
vertical Laplace operator
, denoted

\Delta_{\mathcal{V}}:\Gamma_{0}\left(Z^{\prime},\End(E)\right)\to\Gamma_{0}\left(Z^{\prime},\End(E)\right),

is the operator

\Delta_{\mathcal{V}}=i\Lambda_{\mathcal{V}}\big(\partial_{F}\bar{\partial}_{F}-\bar{\partial}_{F}\partial_{F}\big),

where

\Lambda_{\mathcal{V}}:\Omega^{1,1}\left(Z^{\prime},\End(E)\right)\to\Gamma_{0}\left(Z^{\prime},\End(E)\right)

is the
vertical contraction operator
defined on each fiber
F_{z}=\pi^{-1}(z)
of
\pi:Z^{\prime}\to Z
by

(\Lambda_{\mathcal{V}}\alpha)_{|F_{z}}\,\lambda_{|F_{z}}^{r-1}=(r-1)\alpha_{|F_{z}}\wedge\lambda_{|F_{z}}^{r-2}

and where

d_{F}=\partial_{F}+\bar{\partial}_{F}
is the flat connection along the fibres of
\pi:Z^{\prime}\to Z
.

The following lemma relies on
[
11
]
and was already observed in
[
16
, Lemma 4.6]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

The vertical Laplacian

\Delta_{\mathcal{V}}:\Gamma_{0}\left(Z^{\prime},\End(E)\right)\to\Gamma_{0}\left(Z^{\prime},\End(E)\right)

is an invertible linear map.

In the following statements, an expression of the form
O(\varepsilon^{j})
is to be understood as holding pointwise. Convergence considerations of those expressions with respect to various Sobolev space norms will be addressed in Section
5
.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
s_{Z}=\iota_{*}\sigma_{Z}
for
\sigma_{Z}\in\Gamma(Z^{\prime},\End(E))
, then

\displaystyle(p_{0}\circ\iota^{*})\Delta_{\varepsilon}(\iota_{*}\sigma_{Z})=\varepsilon^{-1}\Delta_{\mathcal{V}}\sigma_{Z}+\mathcal{O}(1).

Proof.

We introduce the operator
D
given by

\displaystyle Ds_{Z}=i(\bar{\partial}_{A}\partial_{A}-\partial_{A}\bar{\partial}_{A})s_{Z}.

The Laplacian
\Delta_{\varepsilon}
satisfies on
X^{\prime}
:

\Delta_{\varepsilon}s_{Z}\,\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}=nDs_{Z}\wedge\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n-1},

or equivalently

\displaystyle\Delta_{\varepsilon}s_{Z}=\frac{n\ Ds_{Z}\wedge(\omega+\varepsilon\lambda)^{n-1}}{(\omega+\varepsilon\lambda)^{n}}.

We note that
\omega
is a Kähler form on
X
, but on
X^{\prime}
is degenerate along the fibre directions of the submanifold
Z^{\prime}
. Then
(i^{*}\omega)^{m+1}=0\in\Omega^{2(m+1)}(Z^{\prime})
, and at
x\in Z^{\prime}\subseteq X^{\prime}
,
\omega^{m+2}=0
.
Then, expanding
(\omega+\varepsilon\lambda)^{n-1}
and
(\omega+\varepsilon\lambda)^{n}
gives

\displaystyle\iota^{*}\Delta_{\varepsilon}s_{Z}=(n-m-1)\varepsilon^{-1}\frac{Ds_{Z}\wedge\omega^{m+1}\wedge\lambda^{n-m-2}}{\omega^{m+1}\wedge\lambda^{n-m-1}}+\mathcal{O}(1).

Restricting to
Z^{\prime}
, the connection
1
-forms of
A
vanish, so
\iota^{*}Ds_{Z}=i\partial\bar{\partial}\sigma_{Z}
, acting on the coefficient functions of
\sigma_{Z}
. On the other hand, by considering a convenient orthonormal frame at
x\in Z^{\prime}
, we see that
\iota^{*}\Delta_{\varepsilon}\iota_{*}\sigma_{Z}=\varepsilon^{-1}\Delta_{\mathcal{V}}\sigma_{Z}+\mathcal{O}(1)
.
∎

In the next lemma, we denote
\Delta_{\varepsilon}s_{Z}=(\Delta_{\varepsilon}s_{Z})_{X}+(\Delta_{\varepsilon}s_{Z})_{Z}
the decomposition according to (
3.2
).

Lemma 3.4
.

For
s_{Z}=\iota_{*}\sigma_{Z}
with
\sigma_{Z}\in\Gamma(Z^{\prime},\End(E))
, we have

\displaystyle(\Delta_{\varepsilon}s_{Z})_{X}=\mathcal{O}(1).

Proof.

By definition,
(\Delta_{\varepsilon}s_{Z})_{X}=\pi^{*}\phi
for some
\phi\in\Gamma(X,\End(E))
. As we also have

\displaystyle(\Delta_{\varepsilon}s_{Z})_{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Id}-\iota_{*}(p_{0}\circ\iota^{*}))\Lambda_{\varepsilon}Ds_{Z},

we deduce that the section
\phi
is the continuous extension of
\pi_{*}(\mathrm{Id}-\iota_{*}(p_{0}\circ\iota^{*}))\Lambda_{\varepsilon}Ds_{Z}
across
Z\subseteq X
. On
X^{\prime}\setminus Z^{\prime}
we have

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\varepsilon}Ds_{Z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\frac{Ds_{Z}\wedge(\omega^{n-1}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon))}{\omega^{n}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)}=\mathcal{O}(1).

As
\pi_{*}(\mathrm{Id}-\iota_{*}(p_{0}\circ\iota^{*}))
is
\mathcal{O}(1)
, the result follows.
∎

From the previous two lemmas, in the decomposition

s=s_{X}+s_{Z},

\Delta_{\varepsilon}s_{Z}
also lies in the subspace
\Gamma_{0}(Z^{\prime},\End(E))\subseteq\Gamma(X^{\prime},\End(E))
to higher order in
\varepsilon
. For
s_{X}\in\Gamma(X,\End(E))
,

\Delta_{\varepsilon}s_{X}=(\Delta_{\varepsilon}s_{X})_{X}+(\Delta_{\varepsilon}s_{X})_{Z}

where
(\Delta_{\varepsilon}s_{X})_{Z}=\iota_{*}(p_{0}\circ\iota^{*})\Delta_{\varepsilon}s_{X}
. We first consider
\iota^{*}\Delta_{\varepsilon}s_{X}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

For
s_{X}=\pi^{*}\sigma_{X}\in\Gamma(X,\End(E))\subseteq\Gamma(X^{\prime},\End(E))
,

\displaystyle\iota^{*}\Delta_{\varepsilon}s_{X}=(m+1)\frac{Ds_{X}\wedge\omega^{m}\wedge\lambda^{n-m-1}}{\omega^{m+1}\wedge\lambda^{n-m-1}}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon).

Proof.

Firstly,
s_{X}=\pi^{*}\sigma_{X}
, and the connection
A
is pulled back from
X
, so
Ds_{X}
is basic for the projection to
X
and
Ds\wedge\omega^{m+1}=0
at points in
Z^{\prime}
. Secondly, we note that
\omega^{m+1}\wedge\lambda^{n-m-1}
is a volume form on
X^{\prime}
, in a neighbourhood of
Z^{\prime}
. Then, the result follows similarly to the previous lemma.
∎

For the final term
(\Delta_{\varepsilon}s_{X})_{X}
, we introduce
\Delta_{X}
the Laplace operator of
A
on
\End(E)\to(X,\omega)
:

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Delta_{X}:&\Gamma\left(X,\End(E)\right)&\to&\Gamma\left(X,\End(E)\right)\\
&\sigma&\mapsto&i\Lambda_{\omega}(\bar{\partial}_{A}\partial_{A}-\partial_{A}\bar{\partial}_{A})\sigma.\end{array}

Lemma 3.6
.

For
s_{X}=\pi^{*}\sigma_{X}\in\Gamma(X,\End(E))\subseteq\Gamma(X^{\prime},\End(E))
,

\displaystyle(\Delta_{\varepsilon}s_{X})_{X}=\pi^{*}(\Delta_{X}\sigma_{X})+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon).

Proof.

There is
\phi\in\Gamma(X,\End(E))
such that

(\Delta_{\varepsilon}s_{X})_{X}=\pi^{*}\phi
.
The element
\phi
can be identified as the lowest order term in the asymptotic expansion in
\varepsilon
of
(\Delta_{\varepsilon}\pi^{*}\sigma_{X})_{X}
. However, we have at
x\in X^{\prime}\setminus Z^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle\Delta_{\varepsilon}\pi^{*}\sigma_{X}=n\frac{D\pi^{*}\sigma_{X}\wedge(\omega+\varepsilon\lambda)^{n-1}}{(\omega+\varepsilon\lambda)^{n}}=n\pi^{*}\frac{D\sigma_{X}\wedge\omega^{n-1}}{\omega^{n}}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)

so we see that the lowest order term in the expansion of
(\Delta_{\varepsilon}\pi^{*}\sigma_{X})_{X}
is
\Delta_{X}\sigma_{X}
.
∎

Summarizing the above calculations, with respect to the decomposition
s=s_{X}+s_{Z}
produced by (
3.2
), the operator
\Delta_{\varepsilon}
takes the form

(3.3)

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\Delta_{X}&0\\
\mathcal{L}&\varepsilon^{-1}\Delta_{\mathcal{V}}\end{array}\right)

plus higher order terms, for some second order operator
\mathcal{L}
.

The goal of this section is to produce approximate solutions to the HYM equations. As in last section, expressions of the form

As from now on the methods and proofs follow very closely
[

. We will only sketch most of the steps, giving details when major differences occur and precisely quoting results in
[
16
]
otherwise. Note that in our work, the parameter
\varepsilon
plays the role of
k^{-1}
in
[

\Gamma(X^{\prime},\End E)=\Gamma(X,\End E)\oplus\Gamma_{0}(Z^{\prime},\End E)

4.1.
Fixing the complex deformation parameters

We start from a semi-stable and sufficiently smooth holomorphic vector bundle
E
on
(X,L)
, with
L=[\omega]
. Denote by
\mathrm{Gr}(E)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{G}_{i}
the associated polystable graded object, with stable components
\mathcal{G}_{i}
. We also assume that
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
has non-isomorphic stable quotients, and that the Jordan–Holder filtration with locally-free quotients is unique. We now explain the technical implications of these hypotheses. First, by
[
16
, Lemma 5.8]
,
E
is simple. The automorphism group
G:=\Aut(\mathrm{Gr}(E))
is a reductive Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{aut}(\mathrm{Gr}(E))
and compact form
K\subset G
, with
\mathfrak{k}:=\mathrm{Lie}(K)
. We have

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i,j}H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{G}_{i},\mathcal{G}_{j}).

As the
\mathcal{G}_{i}
’s are all stable, and non isomorphic, we deduce that

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathbb{C}\cdot\Id_{\mathcal{G}_{i}},

and in particular
\mathfrak{g}
is abelian. The upshot for us is that elements in
\mathfrak{k}
that will obstruct the linear theory all live on the diagonal in the matrix block decomposition induced by the decomposition
\mathrm{Gr}(E)=\oplus_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{G}_{i}
.
We let
\overline{\partial}_{0}
be the Dolbeault operator of
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
. The Dolbeault operator
\overline{\partial}_{E}
on
E
is given by

\overline{\partial}_{E}=\overline{\partial}_{0}+\gamma

where
\gamma\in\Omega^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{Gr}(E)^{*}\otimes\mathrm{Gr}(E))

can be written

\gamma=\sum_{i<j}\gamma_{ij}

for (possibly vanishing)
\gamma_{ij}\in\Omega^{0,1}(X,\mathcal{G}_{j}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{G}_{i})
. From the uniqueness hypothesis on the Jordan–Hölder filtration, we deduce that the terms
\gamma_{i,i+1}
, for
1\leq i\leq\ell-1
, are all non-zero, see
[
16
, Lemma 5.4]
. This will enable us to use induction on
\ell
, the number of stable components, in the construction of the approximate solutions.

Our starting point to produce HYM connections on
\pi^{*}E\to(X^{\prime},\omega_{\varepsilon})
will be a product Hermite–Einstein metric
h=h_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus h_{\ell}
on
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
(we can fix one by polystability), and the associated HYM connection
A_{0}
on
(\mathrm{Gr}(E),\overline{\partial}_{0})
with contracted curvature form

\Lambda_{\omega}iF_{A_{0}}=c_{0}\cdot\Id.

We then introduce the
L^{2}
projection

(4.1)

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Pi_{i\mathfrak{k}}:&\Gamma(X,\End_{H}(E,h))&\to&i\mathfrak{k}\\
&s&\mapsto&\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(\omega)}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{1}{\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{G}_{i})}(\int_{X}\mathrm{trace}_{\mathcal{G}_{i}}(s)\>\omega^{n})\>\Id_{\mathcal{G}_{i}}\end{array}

and the induced orthogonal decomposition:

\Gamma(X,\End_{H}(E,h))=i\mathfrak{k}\oplus\Gamma_{i\mathfrak{k}^{\perp}}(X,\End_{H}(E,h))

with respect to the pairing

(s_{1},s_{2})\mapsto\int_{X}\mathrm{trace}(s_{1}\cdot s_{2})\,\omega^{n}.

Denoting by
\Delta_{\mathrm{Gr}(E),0}
the Laplace operator of the Chern connection of
(\overline{\partial}_{0},h)
with respect to
\omega
, from Lemma
2.1
and self-adjointness, we have

Lemma 4.1
.

The following operator is invertible :

\Delta_{\mathrm{Gr}(E),0}:\Gamma_{i\mathfrak{k}^{\perp}}(X,\End_{H}(E,h))\to\Gamma_{i\mathfrak{k}^{\perp}}(X,\End_{H}(E,h)).

We will now make use of the gauge action of
G
to calibrate the complex deformation parameter
\gamma
with the metric variation parameter
\varepsilon
. We gauge fix
\gamma
by imposing

(4.2)

\overline{\partial}_{0}^{*}\gamma=0,

where the adjoint is with respect to the Kähler structure
\omega
. Elements

g:=g_{1}\Id_{\mathcal{G}_{1}}+\ldots,+g_{\ell}\Id_{\mathcal{G}_{\ell}}\in G,

for
(g_{i})\in(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{\ell}
, act on
\overline{\partial}_{E}
and produce isomorphic holomorphic vector bundles in the following way :

(4.3)

g\cdot\overline{\partial}_{E}=\overline{\partial}_{0}+\sum_{i<j}g_{i}g_{j}^{-1}\gamma_{ij}.

We assume from now on that for
1\leq i\leq\ell-1
,

\mu_{L_{\varepsilon}}(\mathcal{F}_{i})\underset{\varepsilon\to 0}{<}\mu_{L_{\varepsilon}}(E).

We introduce
q_{i}
to be the
discrepancy order
of
\mathcal{F}_{i}
for each

, that is the order

such that
\mu_{L_{\varepsilon}}(E)-\mu_{L_{\varepsilon}}(\mathcal{F}_{i})=\nu_{i}\varepsilon^{q_{i}}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{q_{i}+1})
for a constant
\nu_{i}>0
. The
discrepancy order

q
of
E
will be
q=\max(q_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\ell-1}
. Define now

\underline{m}=(m_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\ell-1}
where for all

,
m_{i}
satisfies
1
1

1

The formula differs from
[

]

, we don’t obtain an extra

-term (as was the case when contracting basic terms in

)

We then define
{\bf g}_{\underline{\upsilon},\underline{m}}\in G
by

where the constants

will be determined soon. Denote by

(4.4)

\gamma_{\varepsilon}:={\bf g}_{\underline{\upsilon},\underline{m}}\cdot\gamma.

Then, the operators
\overline{\partial}_{E}
and
\overline{\partial}_{\varepsilon}=\overline{\partial}_{0}+\gamma_{\varepsilon}

\underline{\upsilon}
. We denote by
A_{\varepsilon}
the Chern connection on
(\pi^{*}E,\pi^{*}(\overline{\partial}_{\varepsilon}),\pi^{*}h)
(and we will drop from now the

to ease notations). We will consider projections onto various components of
\mathfrak{g}
. For

and

4.2.
Inductive process and approximate solutions

To construct the approximate solutions to any desired order, we will perturb iteratively the connections
A_{\varepsilon}
by gauge transformations of the form
\exp(\varepsilon^{i}s_{i})
and use the properties of the associated Laplace operators. We denote by
\Delta_{\varepsilon}
(resp.
\Delta_{\mathrm{Gr}(E),\varepsilon}
) the associated Laplacian of
A_{\varepsilon}
(resp. of the Chern connection of
(\pi^{*}\overline{\partial}_{0},\pi^{*}h)
) with respect to
\omega_{\varepsilon}
. We also set

\Delta_{X}:=\Delta_{\mathrm{Gr}(E),0}

the Laplacian of
(\overline{\partial}_{0},h)
on

and

the second order operator associated to
(\overline{\partial}_{0},h)
as in the matrix Expression (

Proposition 4.3
.

Under the above hypotheses, there are expansions

(4.5)

\Delta_{\varepsilon}=\Delta_{\mathrm{Gr}(E),\varepsilon}+O(\varepsilon),

and for
s_{X}+s_{Z}\in\Gamma(X,\End E)\oplus\Gamma_{0}(Z^{\prime},\End E)
,

(4.6)

\Delta_{\mathrm{Gr}(E),\varepsilon}(s_{X}+\varepsilon s_{Z})=\Delta_{X}(s_{X})+\mathcal{L}(s_{X})+\Delta_{\mathcal{V}}(s_{Z})+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon).

The same expansions also hold at a Chern connection on
\pi^{*}E\to X^{\prime}
coming from a complex structure
f_{\varepsilon}\cdot\pi^{*}(\overline{\partial}_{0}+\gamma_{\varepsilon})
provided

for some

satisfies

and whose

-component

satisfies

Proof.

For the proof of (

(4.7)

F_{A_{\varepsilon}}=F_{A}+d_{A_{0}}(\gamma_{\varepsilon}-\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{*})+(\gamma_{\varepsilon}-\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{*})\wedge(\gamma_{\varepsilon}-\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{*}).

Upon contraction with
\omega_{\varepsilon}
, we obtain

\Lambda_{\varepsilon}(iF_{A_{\varepsilon}})=\Lambda_{\varepsilon}(iF_{A_{0}})+\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\left(\partial_{0}\gamma_{\varepsilon}-\overline{\partial}_{0}\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{*}\right)-2\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\left(\gamma_{\varepsilon}\wedge\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{*}\right).

Now, by choice of

and definition of
\gamma_{\varepsilon}
in Equation (

\gamma_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{\min(m_{i})}).

By definition,
m_{i}=\frac{q_{i}}{2}
. By Formula (

), for all

, we have

, and as we are blowing-up a submanifold, we must have

. Then,
q_{i}\geq 2
, and thus
m_{i}\geq 1
for all

.
Then,

\gamma_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon),

which gives

\Lambda_{\varepsilon}(iF_{A_{\varepsilon}})=\Lambda_{\varepsilon}(iF_{A_{0}})+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon).

Note that all the terms in
\left(\partial_{0}\gamma_{\varepsilon}-\overline{\partial}_{0}\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{*}\right)-2\left(\gamma_{\varepsilon}\wedge\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{*}\right)
are pulled back terms, and arguing as in Section

, we obtain that this estimate is preserved for first order variations, which gives

\Delta_{\varepsilon}=\Delta_{\mathrm{Gr}(E),\varepsilon}+O(\varepsilon).

The proof of (
4.6
) is a direct consequence of the various lemmas in Section

. Finally, we consider

∎

When producing the approximate HYM connections, we will use the mapping properties of
\Delta_{X}
and
\Delta_{\mathcal{V}}
. This will allow us to remove all error terms modulo elements in the cokernel of
\Delta_{X}=\Delta_{\mathrm{Gr}(E),0}
, which is
\mathfrak{g}
. By Chern-Weil theory, those remaining terms will be controlled by the expansions of
\mu_{L_{\varepsilon}}(\mathcal{F}_{i})
, and it is important in the argument that upon removing the other errors, they remain fixed. This is the content of the following lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
s\in\Gamma(\End(\mathcal{G}_{i}))
and
j\in[\![1,\ell]\!]
. Then

\Pi_{\langle\Id_{\mathcal{F}_{j}}\rangle}\Delta_{\varepsilon}(s)=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{q_{j}})

and

\Pi_{\langle\Id_{\mathcal{F}_{j}}\rangle}\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\left((\partial_{\varepsilon}s-\bar{\partial}_{\varepsilon}s)\wedge(\partial_{\varepsilon}s-\bar{\partial}_{\varepsilon}s)\right)=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{q_{j}}).

Let
\sigma\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{G}_{p},\mathcal{G}_{i}))
, with
i<p
, and
j\in[\![1,\ell]\!]
. Then

\Pi_{\langle\Id_{\mathcal{F}_{j}}\rangle}\Delta_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon^{m_{i}+\ldots+m_{p-1}+1}\sigma)=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{q_{j}+1}).

and

\Pi_{\langle\Id_{\mathcal{F}_{j}}\rangle}\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\left(\varepsilon^{2(m_{i}+\ldots+m_{p-1}+1)}\left((\partial_{\varepsilon}\sigma-\bar{\partial}_{\varepsilon}\sigma)\wedge(\partial_{\varepsilon}\sigma-\bar{\partial}_{\varepsilon}\sigma)\right)\right)=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{q_{j}+1}),

and similarily for
p<i
.

Proof.

Note that we only need to consider the
\Gamma(X,\End E)
components of the sections in the splitting (
3.2
), as we are only interested in the projection onto
\Id_{\mathcal{F}_{j}}
. Then, the proof follows exactly as in
[

, Lemma 5.20 and Lemma 5.21]
.
∎

The only errors that will then remain to remove are those in
\mathfrak{g}
. This is where we will use the following lemma, whose proof is a direct adaptation of
[

, proof of Lemma 5.23]
.

Lemma 4.5
.

For all
j\in[\![1,\ell-1]\!]
, there is a negative constant
a_{j,j+1}
such that

(4.8)

\Pi_{\mathfrak{g}}\Delta_{\varepsilon}(\Id_{\mathcal{F}_{j}})=a_{j,j+1}\varepsilon^{q_{j}}\Id_{j,j+1}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{q_{j}+1}),

where for
(p,l)\in[\![1,\ell]\!]^{2}
:

\Id_{pl}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{rank}\,\mathcal{G}_{p}}\Id_{\mathcal{G}_{p}}-\frac{1}{\mathrm{rank}\,\mathcal{G}_{l}}\Id_{\mathcal{G}_{l}}.

Note that as in Proposition
4.3
, the conclusions of Lemma
4.4
and
4.5
also hold for perturbed connections of the form
f_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}
for
f_{\varepsilon}=\Id_{E}+s_{\varepsilon,X}+s_{\varepsilon,Z}
with
s_{\varepsilon,X}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
and
s_{\varepsilon,Z}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2})
. We are now ready to prove the main proposition of this section.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
p\geq q
. Then, there exists gauge transformations
f_{\varepsilon}^{p}=\Id_{E}+s_{\varepsilon,X}^{p}+s_{\varepsilon,Z}^{p}
with
s_{\varepsilon,X}^{p}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
and
s_{\varepsilon,Z}^{p}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2})
, and constants
c_{\varepsilon}^{p}
, such that for all
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, we have

\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\left(F_{f_{\varepsilon}^{p}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}}\right)=c_{\varepsilon}^{p}\Id_{E}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{p}).

Proof.

The proof follows the strategy developped in
[
16
, Section 5.2 and Section 5.3.2]
, so we refer to those relevant sections and will only sketch the argument. We can expand in
\varepsilon
each term on the right hand side of the following identity :

\Lambda_{\varepsilon}(iF_{A_{\varepsilon}})=\Lambda_{\varepsilon}(iF_{A_{0}})+\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\left(\partial_{0}\gamma_{\varepsilon}-\overline{\partial}_{0}\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{*}\right)-2\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\left(\gamma_{\varepsilon}\wedge\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{*}\right).

As
A_{0}
is HYM, we have

\Lambda_{\varepsilon}(iF_{A_{0}})=c\Id_{E}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon).

By the gauge fixing condition
\overline{\partial}_{0}^{*}\gamma=\Lambda_{0}\partial_{0}\gamma=0
, the following off-diagonal terms have entries

(\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\left(\partial_{0}\gamma_{\varepsilon}-\overline{\partial}_{0}\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{*}\right))_{ip}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{m_{i}+\ldots+m_{p-1}+1}),

while the remaining terms will contribute to higher orders. Following
[
16
, Proposition 4.9 and Section 5.2]
, we start by perturbing inductively
A_{\varepsilon}
to remove all errors that live in
\Gamma_{i\mathfrak{k}^{\perp}}(X,\End_{H}(E,h))\oplus\Gamma_{0}(Z^{\prime},\End_{H}(E,h))
. This can be achieved by considering perturbations
\exp(\varepsilon^{i}(s_{X}+\varepsilon s_{Z})\cdot A_{\varepsilon})
thanks to Proposition
4.3
, together with Lemmas
3.2
and
4.1
. What remains are errors in
i\mathfrak{k}
. By Lemma
4.4
, and from the formula

\Lambda F_{f\cdot A}=\Lambda F_{A}+\Delta_{A}(f)+\Lambda\left((\partial_{A}f-\overline{\partial}_{A}f)\wedge(\partial_{A}f-\overline{\partial}_{A}f)\right),

the previous perturbations won’t affect any of the
\Id_{\mathcal{F}_{j}}
projections of the contracted curvatures. By proposition
4.2
, we then obtain a connection whose contracted curvature satisfies, for all
j\in[\![1,\ell-1]\!]
,

\Lambda_{\omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(iF_{A_{\varepsilon}}\right)=\mathcal{C}\,\mu_{L_{\varepsilon}}(E)\Id_{\mathcal{F}_{j}}+O(\varepsilon^{q_{j}+\frac{1}{2}}).

The end of the proof is then done by induction on
\ell
the number of stable components of
\mathrm{Gr}(E)
. As discussed in
[
16
, Section 5.3.2]
, using Lemma
4.5
, we can remove errors in
\mathfrak{g}
beyond the orders
q_{j}
, by induction on
\ell
, and eventually obtain the result.
∎

5.
The perturbation argument

We keep notations from the last section. We will now prove Theorem
1.1
, and its corollaries. Again, we follow closely
[
16
]
. The first step is to obtain an upper bound for the operator norm of the inverse of
\Delta_{\varepsilon}
. This is where the argument from
[
16
]
has to be adapted, given the different geometric settup that we address. Then, based on this bound and on the construction of approximate solutions, a quantitative version of the implicit function theorem is used in Section
5.2
.

For a Riemannian metric
g
on
X^{\prime}
, let
L^{2}_{d}(g)
denote the Sobolev space
W^{d,2}(X^{\prime},g)
of order
2
and
d
derivatives, with respect to the metric
g
. If
g
is Kähler with Kähler form
\eta
, we may write
L^{2}_{d}(\eta)
instead of
L^{2}_{d}(g)
. When

, we omit the subscript. For Sobolev spaces associated to
\pi^{*}E
or
\End\pi^{*}E
, we use the metric

(and the metric it induces on
\End\pi^{*}E
) on the bundle.

5.1.
Estimating the linearisation

Our main goal in this section is to prove:

Proposition 5.1
.

There exists
C>0
such that for all
s\in\Gamma(X^{\prime},\End E)
whose trace is average zero with respect to
\omega_{\varepsilon}
,

(5.1)

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{\varepsilon}(s)\|_{L^{2}_{d}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}\geq C\varepsilon^{q}\|s\|_{L^{2}_{d+2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}.

The same estimate also holds for the Laplacian of the perturbed connections built in Proposition
4.6
.

This will be done by steps. We first work out the case of a single stable component
\mathcal{G}:=\mathcal{G}_{i}\subset\mathrm{Gr}(E)
.

Lemma 5.2
.

There exists
C>0
such that for all
s\in\Gamma(X^{\prime},\End\mathcal{G})
that is
L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})
-orthogonal to
\Id_{\mathcal{G}}
,

(5.2)

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{\varepsilon}(s)\|_{L^{2}_{d}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}\geq C\varepsilon\|s\|_{L^{2}_{d+2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}.

The proof of Lemma
5.2
will require to compare various
L^{2}
-norms on
X^{\prime}
,
X
and
Z^{\prime}
. We gather those comparisons in the following lemmas.

Lemma 5.3
.

There are positive constants
C
and
C^{\prime}
independent on
\varepsilon
such that for any
\sigma\in\Gamma(X,\End\mathcal{G})
(or
\sigma\in\Omega^{1}(X,\End\mathcal{G})
), we have

(5.3)

C\|\pi^{*}\sigma\|_{L^{2}(X^{\prime},\omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(X,\omega)}\leq C^{\prime}\|\pi^{*}\sigma\|_{L^{2}(X^{\prime},\omega_{\varepsilon})}.

Proof.

The result follows from the facts that the
2
-forms
\omega_{\varepsilon}
vary in a bounded family (
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}]
), and that a pulled back section is constant on the fibers of
\pi:Z^{\prime}\to Z
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

There are positive constants
C
and
C^{\prime}
independent on
\varepsilon
such that for any
\sigma\in\Gamma_{0}(Z^{\prime},\End\mathcal{G})
(or
\sigma\in\Omega^{1}(Z^{\prime},\End\mathcal{G})
), we have

(5.4)

C\|\iota_{*}\sigma\|_{L^{2}(X^{\prime},\omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}\leq\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(Z^{\prime},\omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}\leq C^{\prime}\|\iota_{*}\sigma\|_{L^{2}(X^{\prime},\omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}.

Proof.

This can be done by a local and then patching argument. We may fix
W\subset X^{\prime}
an open set with
Z^{\prime}\subset W
, and such that
\iota_{*}\sigma
vanishes away from
W
. Then,

\|\iota_{*}\sigma\|_{L^{2}(X^{\prime},\omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}=\int_{W}\|\iota_{*}\sigma\|_{\varepsilon}^{2}\>\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}.

We may also consider an open finite covering of
W
by sets
U
of the form
U\simeq B(0,1)\times V
where the sets
V\subset Z^{\prime}
cover
Z^{\prime}
and such that
\iota_{*}\sigma
is given locally by
\rho\cdot\sigma
for a bump function
\rho
on
B(0,1)
. Then, from the facts that
\varepsilon
varies in a compact family, and that for any
v\in V
, the metrics
(\omega_{\varepsilon}(b,v))_{b\in B(0,1)}
on
B(0,1)\times\{v\}
are mutually bounded, by applying Fubini’s theorem, we obtain bounds :

C\int_{U}\|\iota_{*}\sigma\|_{\varepsilon}^{2}\>\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}\leq\int_{V}\|\sigma\|_{\varepsilon}^{2}\>\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}\leq C^{\prime}\int_{U}\|\iota_{*}\sigma\|_{\varepsilon}^{2}\>\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}.

A patching argument then provides the result.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

There exists a positive constant
C
independent on
\varepsilon
such that for any
s_{X}\in\Gamma(X,\End\mathcal{G})
and
s_{Z}\in\Gamma_{0}(Z^{\prime},\End\mathcal{G})
, we have

|\langle s_{X},s_{Z}\rangle_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}|\leq C\varepsilon^{3}||s_{X}||_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}\,||s_{Z}||_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}.

Proof.

We keep the notations from the proof of Lemma
5.4
.
Note that we can restrict to the open set
W
as
s_{Z}
vanishes away from
W
. Then, locally, we estimate

\int_{U}\mathrm{trace}(s_{X}\cdot s_{Z})\>\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}.

Take coordinates
(b,f,v)\in B(0,1)\times F\times V_{Z}
so that
U\simeq B(0,1)\times F\times V_{Z}
where this time
F
denote fibers of
\pi:Z^{\prime}\to Z
,
V_{Z}\subset Z
is an open set in
Z
and
B(0,1)
stands for normal coordinates. We may assume that
s_{X}
is independent on
f\in F
:

s_{X}=s_{X}(b,v)

and
s_{Z}
is of the form

s_{Z}=\rho(b)s_{Z}(f,w)

for
\rho
some cut off function used to produce the extension operator. Then, we compute with Fubini’s :

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\displaystyle\int_{U}\mathrm{trace}(s_{X}\cdot s_{Z})\>\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}&=&\displaystyle\int_{B\times F\times V_{Z}}\rho(b)\,\mathrm{trace}(s_{X}(b,w)\cdot s_{Z}(f,w))\,\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}\\
&=&\displaystyle\int_{B\times V_{Z}}\rho(b)\int_{F}\mathrm{trace}(s_{X}(b,w)\cdot s_{Z}(f,w))\,\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}.\end{array}

But to higher order in
\varepsilon
,

\displaystyle\int_{F}\mathrm{trace}(s_{X}(b,w)\cdot s_{Z}(f,w))\,\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}=

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{n-m-1}\omega^{m+1}\int_{F}\mathrm{trace}(s_{X}(b,w)\cdot s_{Z}(f,w))\,\lambda^{n-m-1}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{n-m})

and as
s_{Z}
is of average zero w.r.t.
\lambda^{n-m-1}
, we get

\int_{F}\mathrm{trace}(s_{X}(b,w)\cdot s_{Z}(f,w))\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{n-m}),

which is
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3})
as
\mathrm{codim}(Z)=n-m\geq 3
. The result then follows from Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, for a constant
C
that depends on
\omega,\lambda
and
\varepsilon_{0}
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.

The positive constants
C,C^{\prime},C_{i}
used in the proof might vary at several stages. We follow the strategy from
[
16
, Section 4.2]
. The key to obtain this estimate is the analogue of
[
16
, Lemma 4.12]
, as the rest of the argument follows as in
[
16
, Section 4.2]
. So our goal is to obtain the following Poincaré type inequality:
there exists
C>0
such that for any
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}]
, and all
s\in\Gamma(X^{\prime},\End\mathcal{G})
that is
L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})
-orthogonal to
\Id_{\mathcal{G}}
, we have :

\|d_{A_{\varepsilon}}(s)\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}\geq C\varepsilon\|s\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}.

As
A_{\varepsilon}=A_{0}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
, it is actually enough to obtain this Poincaré inequality for
A_{0}
:

\|d_{A_{0}}(s)\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}\geq C\varepsilon\|s\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}.

We will then prove this in three steps. First, for sections
s\in\Gamma(X,\End\mathcal{G})
, then for sections
s\in\Gamma_{0}(Z^{\prime},\End\mathcal{G})
and last for sums of such sections.

Step 1 :
For
\sigma\in\Gamma(X,\End\mathcal{G})
and
s=\pi^{*}\sigma\in\Gamma(X^{\prime},\End\mathcal{G})
, let

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathrm{rank}\,{\mathcal{G}}}\,\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(X,\omega)}\int_{X}\mathrm{trace}(\sigma)\,\omega^{n},

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathrm{rank}\,{\mathcal{G}}}\,\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(X^{\prime},\omega_{\varepsilon})}\int_{X^{\prime}}\mathrm{trace}(s)\,\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}.

Then,
s
is orthogonal to
\Id
on
X^{\prime}
if and only if
\alpha^{\prime}=0
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}-\alpha=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)\int_{X}\mathrm{trace}(\sigma)\,\omega^{n}.

Then, noting that
d_{A_{0}}\sigma=d_{A_{0}}(\sigma-\alpha\Id)
as
\Id
is parallel, we have

\displaystyle\|d_{A_{0}}(\pi^{*}\sigma)\|_{L^{2}(X^{\prime},\omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C_{1}\|\pi^{*}d_{A_{0}}\sigma\|_{L^{2}(X^{\prime},\omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2},

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C_{2}\|d_{A_{0}}\sigma\|_{L^{2}(X,\omega)}^{2},

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C_{3}\|\sigma-\alpha\Id\|_{L^{2}(X,\omega)}^{2},

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C_{4}\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(X,\omega)}^{2},\ \

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C_{5}\|s\|_{L^{2}(X^{\prime},\omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2},

where the second and fifth inequalities follow from Lemma
5.3
, the third one from Poincaré inequality for
A_{0}
on
(X,\omega)
, and the fourth inequality follows since
\alpha
is
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(\omega)})
.

Step 2 :
We next consider
s=\iota_{*}\sigma\in\Gamma_{0}(Z^{\prime},\End\mathcal{G})
. We set this time
\alpha^{\prime\prime}
to be the constant given by the
L^{2}(Z^{\prime},\omega_{\varepsilon})
-orthogonal projection of
\sigma
onto
\Id_{E}
:

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathrm{rank}\,\mathcal{G}\,\mathrm{vol}(Z^{\prime},\omega_{\varepsilon})}\int_{Z^{\prime}}\mathrm{trace}(\sigma)\,\omega^{n-1}_{\varepsilon}.

In general, for a given
\sigma\in\Gamma(Z^{\prime},\End\mathcal{G})
,
\alpha^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{O}(1)
with respect to
\varepsilon
. However, by definition of the subspace
\Gamma_{0}(Z^{\prime},\End\mathcal{G})\subset\Gamma(Z^{\prime},\End\mathcal{G})
, since
\sigma\in\Gamma_{0}(Z^{\prime},\End\mathcal{G})
is orthogonal to the identity on all fibres of
\pi:Z^{\prime}\to Z
, an argument similar to that of Lemma
5.5
, using the same system of coordinates
(b,f,v)\in B(0,1)\times F\times V_{Z}
, shows that
\alpha^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(Z^{\prime},\omega_{\varepsilon})})
. Then we find positive constants
C_{i}
such that

\displaystyle\|d_{A_{0}}(\iota_{*}\sigma)\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C_{1}\|\iota_{*}(d_{A_{0}}\sigma)\|_{L^{2}(X^{\prime},\omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C_{2}\|d_{A_{0}}\sigma\|_{L^{2}(Z^{\prime},\omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\>C_{3}\|\sigma-\alpha^{\prime\prime}\mathrm{Id}\|_{L^{2}(Z^{\prime},\omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\>C_{4}\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(Z^{\prime},\omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2},

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\>C_{5}\|\iota_{*}\sigma\|_{L^{2}(X^{\prime},\omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2},

where this time the first inequality follows from construction of the operator
\iota_{*}
, the second and last inequalities come from Lemma
5.4
, the third one follows from the corresponding
[
16
, Lemma 4.12]
, and the fourth one from the fact that
\alpha^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(Z^{\prime},\omega_{\varepsilon})})
.

Step 3 :
We need now to obtain similar estimates for sections
s=s_{X}+s_{Z}
. Note however that the splitting of (
3.2
) is
not
orthogonal with respect to the
L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})
inner product. What remains is to estimate

\langle d_{A_{0}}s_{X},d_{A_{0}}s_{Z}\rangle_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}=\langle\Delta_{A_{0},\varepsilon}(s_{X}),s_{Z}\rangle_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}=\langle s_{X},\Delta_{A_{0},\varepsilon}(s_{Z})\rangle_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})},

for
\Delta_{A_{0},\varepsilon}
the Laplacian of
A_{0}
with respect to
\omega_{\varepsilon}
.
We use the expansion of
\Delta_{\varepsilon}
from Section
3.2
. From Lemma
3.5
, Lemma
3.6
and formula (
3.3
) :

\langle\Delta_{A_{0},\varepsilon}(s_{X}),s_{Z}\rangle_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}=\langle\Delta_{X}s_{X},s_{Z}\rangle_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}+\langle\mathcal{L}(s_{X}),s_{Z}\rangle_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)

Then, from Lemma
5.5
,

|\langle\Delta_{X}s_{X},s_{Z}\rangle_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}\,|\leq C_{1}\,\varepsilon^{3}||\Delta_{X}s_{X}||_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}\,||s_{Z}||_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}.

Setting
s_{X}=\pi^{*}\sigma
, and using Lemma
5.3
, together with the continuity of the Green operator of
\Delta_{X}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\langle\Delta_{X}s_{X},s_{Z}\rangle_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}\,|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}\,\varepsilon^{3}||\Delta_{X}\sigma||_{L^{2}(\omega)}\,||s_{Z}||_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{3}\,\varepsilon^{3}||\sigma||_{L^{2}(\omega)}\,||s_{Z}||_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{4}\,\varepsilon^{3}||s_{X}||_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}\,||s_{Z}||_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})},

where again we dealt with the
L^{2}(X,\omega)
-projection of
\sigma
onto
\Id_{\mathcal{G}}
as in Step 1.
For the term
\langle\mathcal{L}(s_{X}),s_{Z}\rangle_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}
, we use local coordinates
(b,f,v)\in B\times F\times V_{Z}
as in the proof of Lemma
5.5
. Then, the expression of
\mathcal{L}
can be locally written

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(s_{X})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iota_{*}\circ p_{0}\circ\iota^{*}\left((m+1)\frac{Ds_{X}\wedge\omega^{m}\wedge\lambda^{n-m-1}}{\omega^{m+1}\wedge\lambda^{n-m-1}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(m+1)\frac{D_{b}s_{X}\wedge\omega^{m}\wedge\lambda^{n-m-1}}{\omega^{m+1}\wedge\lambda^{n-m-1}}

where
D_{b}
stands for derivatives in the
B(0,1)
-direction. We then compute the local contribution on
U
of the term
\langle\mathcal{L}(s_{X}),s_{Z}\rangle_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}
:

\int_{U}\mathrm{trace}(\mathcal{L}(s_{X})\cdot s_{Z})\>\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}=\int_{B\times F\times V_{Z}}\rho(b)\,\mathrm{trace}(\mathcal{L}(s_{X}(b,w))\cdot s_{Z}(f,w))\,\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n},

and by Fubini’s, Stokes theorem, and integration by parts, the relevant higher order term in
\varepsilon
is

\int_{U}D_{b}(\rho(b))\,\mathrm{trace}(s_{X}\cdot s_{Z})\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n},

which is
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3}\,||s_{X}||_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}\,||s_{Z}||_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})})
by a similar argument as in Lemma
5.5
. Gathering those estimates, we see that the mixed terms satisfy

\langle d_{A_{0}}s_{X},d_{A_{0}}s_{Z}\rangle_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}=\langle\Delta_{A_{0},\varepsilon}(s_{X}),s_{Z}\rangle_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3}\,||s_{X}||_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}\,||s_{Z}||_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}),

and together with steps 1 and 2, using Lemma
5.5
again, we obtain

\|d_{A_{0}}(s_{X}+s_{Z})\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}\geq C\varepsilon\|s_{X}+s_{Z}\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}.

Then, from this Poincaré inéquality, the result follows as in
[
16
, Section 4.2]
, using an analogous uniform Schauder estimate as in
[
16
, Proposition 4.13]
. The latter estimate can be obtained by patching local Schauder estimates (as in
[
16
, Lemma 4.14]
) that are easily derived away from the exceptional divisor, and can be obtained around a point in the exceptional divisor by adapting
[
16
, Proof of Lemma 4.14]
using local coordinates as in Lemma
5.5
.
∎

Once this bound settled, we can deal with the general case.

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

The proof for the estimate (
5.1
) can be established exactly as in
[
16
, Proposition 5.27]
, following
[
16
, Lemma 5.28, Lemma 5.29]
.
First, to obtain the estimate for elements in
\mathfrak{g}^{\perp}
,
one uses the full expansion in the linearisation :

(5.5)

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Delta_{\varepsilon}&=&\Delta_{\mathrm{Gr}(E),\varepsilon}\\
&+&i\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\left(\overline{\partial}_{0}([\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{*},\cdot])-[\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{*},\overline{\partial}_{0}\cdot]\right)\\
&+&i\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\left(\partial_{0}([\gamma_{\varepsilon},\cdot])-[\gamma_{\varepsilon},\partial_{0}\cdot]\right)\\
&+&i\Lambda_{\varepsilon}([\gamma_{\varepsilon},[\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{*},\cdot]]-[\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{*},[\gamma_{\varepsilon},\cdot]]).\end{array}

As
\gamma_{\varepsilon}
is a pulled back term, arguing as in Section
3.2
, we see that the
X
and
Z^{\prime}
contributions of the operator
\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\left(\partial_{0}([\gamma_{\varepsilon},\cdot])\right)
in the splitting (

. As argued before, from the choice of

and definition of
\gamma_{\varepsilon}
in Equation (

), we see that

(\gamma_{\varepsilon})_{ip}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{m_{i}}).

Now,
m_{i}=\frac{q_{i}}{2}\geq\frac{\mathrm{codim}(Z)}{2}
by Formula (

). Arguing similarily for the other terms in (

), we see that the contributions from
\Delta_{\varepsilon}-\Delta_{\mathrm{Gr}(E),\varepsilon}
will all come at order at least
\varepsilon^{\frac{\mathrm{codim}(Z)}{2}}
, and will be absorbed by the estimate in Lemma
5.2
, because we assumed
\mathrm{codim}(Z)\geq 3
. Arguing as in
[
16
, Lemma 5.28]
, we obtain the bound :

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{\varepsilon}(s)\|_{L^{2}_{d}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}\geq C\varepsilon\|s\|_{L^{2}_{d+2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})},

for

. Then, from here, the proof for the estimate for sections in
\mathfrak{g}
, or for sums of sections, follows as in
[

. Finally, the result for perturbed connections follows as in
[

, Proposition 5.30]
.
∎

5.2.
Perturbing to solutions

To conclude the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we refer to
[
16
, Section 4.2 and Section 5.4.2]
. It relies on a quantitative version of the implicit function theorem, applied to the operator

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\Psi_{p,\varepsilon}:&L^{2}_{d+2}(X^{\prime},\omega_{\varepsilon})\times\mathbb{R}&\to&L^{2}_{d}(X^{\prime},\omega_{\varepsilon})\\
&(s,\alpha)&\mapsto&i\Lambda_{\omega_{\varepsilon}}(F_{\exp(s)\cdot f_{\varepsilon}^{p}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}})-\alpha\Id_{E},\end{array}

where

are the connections built in Proposition
4.6
. Proposition
4.6
is the analogue of
[
16
, Proposition 4.9 and Section 5.3.2]
where approximate solutions to
\Psi_{p,\varepsilon}=0
are constructed, while Proposition
5.1
plays the role of
[
16
, Propositions 5.27 and 5.30]
and provides the required estimate on the linearisation of
\Psi_{p,\varepsilon}
, for

large enough, at the approximate solutions. Then, the implicit function theorem, as stated in
[
16
, Theorem 4.10]
, enables to conclude the existence of zeros for
\Psi_{p,\varepsilon}
, for

large and

small, which ends the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We comment now on the various corollaries stated in the introduction. First, Corollary

is a direct application of Theorem

, where

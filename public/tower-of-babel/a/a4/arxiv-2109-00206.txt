The perfection of local semi-flows and local random dynamical systems with applications to SDEs
Chengcheng Ling, Michael Scheutzow and Isabell Vorkastner

Abstract
. We provide a rather general perfection result for crude local semi-flows taking values in a Polish space showing that a crude semi-flow has a modification which is a (perfect) local semi-flow which is invariant under a suitable metric dynamical system. Such a (local) semi-flow induces a (local) random dynamical system. Then we show that this result can be applied to several classes of stochastic differential equations driven by semimartingales with stationary increments such as equations with locally monotone coefficients and equations with singular drift. For these examples it was previously unknown whether they generate a (local) random dynamical system or not. Keywords: (local) semi-flow, (local) random dynamical system, cocycle, perfection, stochastic differential equation, singular SDE, Brownian motion



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we investigate the relation between (local) semi-flows and (local) random dynamical systems.
Let us start with a very simple set-up (without measurability or topological assumptions) to illuminate this relation. To ease notation, we define

\Delta:=\{(s,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\,0\leqslant s\leqslant t\}.

Let
\Omega
be a non-empty set,
(H,\circ)
a semi-group with identity element
e_{H}
and let
\theta_{t}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
be a family of
maps from
\Omega
to itself such that
\theta_{0}=\mathrm{id}
and
\theta_{t+s}=\theta_{t}\theta_{s}
for
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\phi:\Delta\times\Omega\to H
and
\varphi:[0,\infty)\times\Omega\to H
be maps
and consider the following properties which
\phi
and
\varphi
may or may not satisfy.

(i)

\phi_{s,u}(\omega)=\phi_{t,u}(\omega)\circ\phi_{s,t}(\omega)
for all
0\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant u
,
\omega\in\Omega
,

(ii)

\phi_{s,s}(\omega)=e_{H}
for all
s\geqslant 0
,
\omega\in\Omega
,

(iii)

\phi_{s,s+t}(\omega)=\phi_{0,t}(\theta_{s}\omega)
,
s,t\geqslant 0
,
\omega\in\Omega
,

(iv)

\varphi_{t+s}(\omega)=\varphi_{t}(\theta_{s}\omega)\circ\varphi_{s}(\omega)
,
s,t\geqslant 0
,

\omega\in\Omega
,

(v)

\varphi_{0}(\omega)=e_{H}
,
\omega\in\Omega
.

Then it is straightforward to check that if
\phi
satisfies (i), (ii) and (iii), then

\varphi_{t}(\omega):=\phi_{0,t}(\omega)
,
t\geqslant 0
satisfies (iv) and (v). Conversely, if

\varphi
satisfies (iv) and (v), then
\phi_{s,t}(\omega):=\varphi_{t-s}(\theta_{s}\omega)
,
(s,t)\in\Delta
satisfies (i), (ii) and (iii). Properties (iv) and (v) are often referred to as the

cocycle property
and the relation just described shows that there is a one-to-one correspondence between semi-flows
\phi
(satisfying (i), (ii) and (iii)) and cocycles
\varphi
.

During the past decades, random dynamical systems (RDS), introduced by L. Arnold (
[
1
]
), have been studied in great detail. Many examples of RDS are generated by stochastic differential equations (SDEs) and it is therefore of interest to show that large classes of SDEs actually generate an RDS. By this we mean that the SDE has a solution map
\phi
which enjoys properties (i), (ii) and (iii) and hence generates a cocycle
\varphi
as outlined above (and satisfies further properties). We will provide precise definitions at the beginning of the following section.

If one wants to verify that an SDE (possibly infinite dimensional, e.g. an SPDE or a stochastic delay equation) generates a semi-flow
\phi
, then the problem comes up that in most cases solutions of an SDE are unique only up to sets of measure 0 and therefore, a solution map
\phi

will typically satisfy properties (i)-(iii) only up to sets of measure 0 and the exceptional sets
may depend on
s,t,u
and the initial condition
x
. The first issue is to find a modification of the
map
\phi
which satisfies at least properties (i) and (ii) and which is continuous with respect to the initial condition
x
. In many cases such a modification can be found using Kolmogorov’s continuity theorem
[
9
,
15
]
. If the SDE has time homogeneous coefficients,
\theta
is a metric dynamical system (see Definition
2.2
) and the SDE is driven by a process with stationary increments which is represented via
\theta
, then it is usually not hard to show that property (iii) holds up to a set of measure 0 which may however depend on
s
(one can usually get rid of the dependence of the exceptional null sets on
t
by (right) continuity properties of
t\mapsto\phi_{s,t}
). An important task is then to show that there exists a modification of
\phi
such that (iii) holds without exceptional null sets.
We will provide a
perfection
statement of this kind in Proposition
2.5
. We will formulate the result in such a way that it also applies to
local semi-flows, i.e. semi-flows which are only defined up to a random time, e.g. the blow-up time of
the SDE. Instead of performing the perfection on the level of semi-flows, one could also first
go from a crude semi-flow to a crude cocycle (defined like
\varphi
above) and then find a modification
of
\varphi
which is a (perfect) cocycle. This approach was taken in
[
2
,
12
,
14
]
. Here we follow
the approach in
[
7
]
by perfecting a crude semi-flow. Our result is more general than
[
7
]

since we also cover local semi-flows which require a different treatment at some places.

The paper is organized as follows. We first introduce the basic concepts. Then we formulate the mentioned perfection result for crude local semi-flows and show how a crude semi-flow generates a local RDS (Theorem
2.7
). In Section 3 we provide conditions on the semi-flow generated by an SDE on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which guarantee that it generates a local RDS and finally, in Section 4, we provide two classes of examples: SDEs with locally monotone coefficients and singular SDEs on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
both driven by
Brownian motion.

2.
Main results

We start by defining the concepts of (local) semi-flows and (local) RDS.
We will always assume that the state space is a Polish space
X
, i.e. a separable completely metrizable topological space and we denote its Borel
\sigma
-field
by
{\mathcal{X}}
. In the following, all random processes will be defined on a given probability space

Definition 2.1
.

A local semi-flow
(\phi,\Theta)

on a Polish space
X
consists of two measurable maps

\displaystyle\Theta:[0,\infty)\times X\times\Omega\rightarrow(0,\infty]

and

\displaystyle\phi:\left\{(s,t,x,\omega)\in[0,\infty)^{2}\times X\times\Omega:s\leqslant t<\Theta(s,x,\omega)\right\}\rightarrow X

such that, for each
\omega\in\Omega

(i)

(s,x)\mapsto\Theta(s,x)
is lower semi-continuous,

(ii)

s\mapsto\phi_{s,t}(x)
is right continuous on
[0,t)
for every
x\in X
and
0\leqslant t<\Theta(s,x)
,

(iii)

t\mapsto\phi_{s,t}(x)
is right continuous on
[s,\Theta(s,x))
for every
x\in X
and
s\geqslant 0
,

(iv)

for all
0\leqslant s\leqslant t<u
and
x\in X
, we have

iff both

and
u<\Theta(t,\phi_{s,t}(x))
.
In this case the following identity holds

\displaystyle\phi_{s,u}(x)=\phi_{t,u}(\phi_{s,t}(x)).

(v)

\phi_{s,s}(x)=x
for all
x\in X
and
s\geqslant 0
.

We call
(\phi,\Theta)
a
continuous local semi-flow
if, in addition,

x\mapsto\phi_{s,t}(x)\mbox{ is continuous for every }0\leqslant s\leqslant t<\Theta(s,x).

We call
(\phi,\Theta)
a (
global
)

if
\Theta(s,x,\omega)=\infty
for all
s\geqslant 0,x\in X
and
\omega\in\Omega
.

Next, we define the concepts of a metric dynamical system and a (local) random dynamical system.

Definition 2.2
.

A
metric dynamical system
(MDS for short)

\theta=(\Omega,\mathcal{F},{\mathbb{P}},\{\theta_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}})
is a
probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},{\mathbb{P}})
with a family of measure preserving transformations
\{\theta_{t}:\Omega\rightarrow\Omega,t\in\mathbb{R}\}
such that

(1)

\theta_{0}=\mathrm{id},\theta_{t}\circ\theta_{s}=\theta_{t+s}
for all

(2)

the map
(t,\omega)\mapsto\theta_{t}\omega
is measurable and
\theta_{t}{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 2.3

(local RDS)
.

A
local random dynamical system
(RDS)
(\theta,\varphi,\tau)

on a Polish space

consists of an MDS
\theta
and measurable maps
\tau:X\times\Omega\to(0,\infty]
and

\varphi:\left\{(s,x,\omega)\in[0,\infty)\times X\times\Omega:s<\tau(x,\omega)\right\}\rightarrow X

such that, for each
\omega\in\Omega
,

(1)

x\mapsto\tau(x)
is lower semi-continuous,

(2)

t\mapsto\varphi_{t}(x)
is right continuous on

for every

,

(3)

\tau(\varphi_{s}(x,\omega),\theta_{s}\omega)+s=\tau(x,\omega)

,

(4)

satisfies the following (perfect)

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}(.,\omega)=\mathrm{id},\hskip 10.00002pt\varphi_{t+s}(x,\omega)=\varphi_{t}(\varphi_{s}(x,\omega),\theta_{s}\omega)

for all
t,s\geqslant 0
,
x\in X
, and
\omega\in\Omega

.

We call

if, in addition, for each

We call

.

Remark 2.4

If

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}_{s,t}(x,\omega)=\begin{cases}\phi_{s,t}(x,\omega),&\text{if }t<\Theta(s,x)(\omega)\text{ and }x\in X,\\
\partial,&\text{else}.\end{cases}

Then, the continuity assumptions on

•

is right continuous on
[s,\infty)
for every

and

By property (v) of the local semi-flow, for all

and

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}_{s,u}(x)=\bar{\phi}_{t,u}(\bar{\phi}_{s,t}(x)).

Using our assumptions (

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}_{s,s+t}(x,\omega)=\bar{\phi}_{0,t}(x,\theta_{s}\omega)

for all

and
\omega\notin N_{s}

We can now apply Proposition

and obtain a map

satisfying all conclusions in Proposition

including (vii)
such that
\tilde{\phi}_{0,.}(.)
and

are indistinguishable. In particular,

\tilde{\phi}
satisfies the assumptions of the theorem with

.
Set

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}_{s+t}(x,\omega)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\phi}_{0,s+t}(x,\omega)=\tilde{\phi}_{s,s+t}(\tilde{\phi}_{0,s}(x,\omega),\omega)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\phi}_{0,t}(\tilde{\phi}_{0,s}(x,\omega),\theta_{s}\omega)=\hat{\varphi}_{t}(\hat{\varphi}_{s}(x,\omega),\theta_{s}\omega)

,

∎

3.
Application to SDEs

We can use the previous theorem to show that SDEs that admit a (local) stochastic flow also generate a (local) RDS.

Let us repeat the basic strategy: if an SDE (possibly infinite dimensional, e.g. an SPDE or a stochastic delay equation) with time-independent coefficients driven by a process with stationary increments has unique local solutions, then, in many cases, the solutions can be shown to admit a local
or global continuous semi-flow to which the previous theorem can be applied, so they generate an RDS.
Examples of results of this kind are contained in
[
2
]
where the processes driving a finite dimensional SDE
were continuous semimartingales (with stationary increments) and
[
7
, Theorem 5]
, in which the
driving semimartingales were just càdlàg (i.e. right continuous with left limits) but the coefficients of the SDE were assumed to satisfy a global Lipschitz condition (thus excluding the possibility of blow-up). For simplicity we now restrict to finite dimensional equations.

In the following we assume that
Z^{1},\dots,Z^{m}
are real-valued càdlàg semimartingales and that
f^{k}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
are measurable for
1\leqslant k\leqslant m
.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that that an SDE

\displaystyle\text{d}X_{t}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}f^{k}(X_{t-})\text{ d}Z^{k}_{t}

(3.1)

has a
(strong) local solution
if for each
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
s\geqslant 0
, there exists a stopping time
\tau=\tau_{s,x}>s
and an
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued adapted process
X_{t}
,
t\in[s,\tau)
with càdlàg paths such that
X_{t}=x+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{s}^{t}f^{k}(X_{u-})\text{d}Z^{k}_{u}
almost surely whenever
s\leqslant t<\tau
and

\limsup_{t\rightarrow\tau}|X_{t}|=\infty
almost surely on the set
\left\{\tau<\infty\right\}
.

We say that the local solution is
unique
if whenever
\tilde{X}_{t}
,
t\geqslant s
is another process with these properties with associated stopping time
\tilde{\tau}
, then
\tau=\tilde{\tau}
and
X=\tilde{X}
on
[s,\tau)
almost surely.

We say that (
3.1
) admits a
local semi-flow
, if it has a unique local solution which admits a modification
(\phi,\Theta)
which is a local semi-flow. In particular,
\limsup_{t\rightarrow\Theta(s,x)}|\phi_{s,t}(x)|=\infty
whenever
s\geqslant 0,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
\Theta(s,x)<\infty
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
Z^{1},\dots,Z^{m}
be real-valued càdlàg semimartingales with stationary increments in the sense that the law of
Z^{k}_{t+h}-Z^{k}_{t},\,h\geqslant 0,\,k\in\{1,...,m\}
does not depend on
t
. Moreover let
f^{k}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be measurable for
1\leqslant k\leqslant m
.
If the equation

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}f^{k}(X_{t-}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}Z^{k}_{t}

has a unique strong local solution which admits a continuous local semi-flow, then it also admits a continuous local RDS.

If the semi-flow is even global, then it admits a (global) RDS.

Proof.

All we have to do is to transfer the local semi-flow to a suitable MDS and to check that conditions
(
2.4
) and (
2.5
) hold. This is done similarly as in

[
2
]
and in
[
7
, Section 3]
.

We can and will assume that
Z^{1},\dots,Z^{m}
are defined on
\mathbb{R}
and are 0 at 0.

Step 1: Definition of MDS

Let
\Omega=\mathcal{D}_{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{m})
be the set of càdlàg functions from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}^{m}
which are
0
at
0

and equipped with the (local) Skorokhod topology and let
\mathcal{F}
be the Borel-
\sigma
-algebra of
\Omega
. Set
Z=(Z^{1},\dots,Z^{m})
and let
\mathbb{P}
be the law of
(Z_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
. Define a shift
\theta
on
\Omega
by

\displaystyle(\theta_{t}\omega)(s)=\omega(t+s)-\omega(t)

for all
\omega\in\Omega
and
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
. We now define the process
Z
on the MDS
\theta
by
Z^{k}_{t}(\omega):=\omega_{k}(t),\,t\in\mathbb{R},\,k\in\{1,...,m\}
(we will not use a new symbol).

Step 2: Time-invariance of stochastic flow

To apply Theorem
2.7
, it only remains to show that equations (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) hold for the continuous local semi-flow
(\phi_{s,t}(x),\Theta(s,x))
generated by the SDE on the MDS
\theta

Fix
s\geqslant 0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then

\displaystyle\phi_{s,s+t}(x,\omega)=x+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(\int_{(s,s+t]}f^{k}(\phi_{s,u-}(x,.))\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}Z^{k}_{u}\right)(\omega)

(3.2)

almost surely for all
t\in[0,\Theta(s,x)(\omega)-s)
. Further, we have

\displaystyle\phi_{0,t}(x,\theta_{s}\omega)=x+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(\int_{(0,t]}f^{k}(\phi_{0,u-}(x,.))\,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}Z^{k}_{u}\right)(\theta_{s}\omega)

(3.3)

almost surely for all

We want to show that

\displaystyle\phi_{s,s+t}(x,\omega)=x+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(\int_{(0,t]}f^{k}(\phi_{s,(s+u)-}(x,.))\text{ d}Z^{k}_{u}(\theta_{s}(.))\right)(\omega)

(3.4)

almost surely for all

.
Afterwards we can use the uniqueness of the solution.
Comparing the right-hand side of (

) holds true if

\displaystyle\left(\int_{(s,s+t]}g^{k}(u,.)\text{ d}Z^{k}_{u}\right)(\omega)=\left(\int_{(0,t]}g^{k}(u+s,.)\text{ d}Z^{k}_{u}(\theta_{s}(.))\right)(\omega)

(3.5)

for each

where

. One can check that (

and then using an approximation argument. Alternatively, we can derive (
3.5

[

.

Comparing (
3.3
) and (

), uniqueness of the local solution implies that
\Theta(s,x)(\omega)=\Theta(0,x)(\theta_{s}\omega)+s
and
\phi_{s,s+t}(x,\omega)=\phi_{0,t}(x,\theta_{s}\omega)
for all

and

. Here

is a null set that may depend on

and

. Indeed, choosing

, equation (

) holds since

is continuous in

. Then, for each

we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\rightarrow\Theta(s,x)(\omega)-s}|\phi_{0,t}(x,\theta_{s}\omega)|=\limsup_{t\rightarrow\Theta(s,x)(\omega)-s}|\phi_{s,s+t}(x,\omega)|=\infty

and

∎

Remark 3.3
.

Let us emphasize that if an SDE generates a continuous local semi-flow and if the SDE admits a global
solution for every initial condition then this does
not
imply that the local semi-flow is global. A counterexample of an SDE in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with bounded and infinitely differentiable coefficients can be found in
[
10
]
. SDEs which generate a continuous local semi-flow and admit a global
solution for every initial condition are often referred to as
weakly complete
while those generating a continuous global semi-flow are called
strongly
or
strictly complete

(sometimes this term is used if the solution map
\phi_{0,t}(x)
admits a modification which is
jointly continuous in
(t,x)
which is slightly weaker than assuming that it generates a continuous global semi-flow, see
[
15
, Definition 1.3]
). To prevent confusion about varying definitions of
strong or weak completeness we will avoid these terms in what follows.

Unlike the previous example we now focus on singular SDEs for which the drift

The price we have to pay is a non-degeneracy assumption on the diffusion part which is needed to ensure the existence of solutions. There are many papers on singular SDEs studying its well-posedness and properties of the solutions, see e.g.
[
8
]
,
[
17
]

[

]

[

We first introduce some notation for later use. For

for

[

We first state our result on the existence of a global semi-flow for singular SDEs.

Theorem 4.5

For

The result follows essentially by combining and extending

Theorem 4.8
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and some
p_{n}\in[2,\infty)
such that
\frac{d}{p_{n}}<1
, assume

(1)

|b|\in\tilde{\mathbb{L}}_{p_{n}}(B_{n})
where
B_{n}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|\leqslant n\}
;

(2)

|\nabla\sigma|\in\tilde{\mathbb{L}}_{p_{n}}(B_{n})
and there exists
K_{n}\geqslant 1
such that for all
x\in B_{n}
,

K_{n}^{-1}|\xi|^{2}\leqslant\langle\sigma\sigma^{*}(x)\xi,\xi\rangle\leqslant K_{n}|\xi|^{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

and
a:=\sigma\sigma^{*}
is uniformly continuous on
B_{n}
.

Then the SDE (
4.1
) admits a continuous local semi-flow.

Proof.

We adapt the localization technique (e.g. see
[
20
, Proof of Theorem 1.3]
,
[
15
, Proof of Theorem 2.4]
) to show the existence of a local semi-flow. Denote for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle b_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=b(x)\mathbbm{1}_{B_{n}}(x),

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\sigma\Big(z_{n+1}+\chi_{n}(x)(x-z_{n+1})\Big),\text{ for some }z_{n+1}\in B_{n+1},

where
\chi_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow[0,1]
is a nonnegative smooth function such that
\chi_{n}(y)=1
for all
y\in B_{n}
and
\chi_{n}(y)=0
for all
y\notin B_{n+1}
,
B_{n}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:|x|<n\}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. It can be verified that such
b_{n}
and
\sigma_{n}
fulfill the conditions in Theorem
4.5
. Therefore there exists a global continuous semi-flow
\psi^{n}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
following from Theorem
4.5
.
We define

\displaystyle\Theta(s,x,\omega):

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\{t\geqslant s:|\psi_{s,t}^{n}(x)|\geqslant n\},

\displaystyle\psi_{s,t}(x,\omega):

\displaystyle=\psi_{s,t}^{n}(x)\text{ for }t\in[s,\Theta(s,x,\omega)).

It is clear that
(\psi,\Theta)
is a local semi-flow of (
4.1
) following the argument from
[
15
, Proof of Theorem 2.4]
.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

Since our main interest are SDEs generating an RDS, we study singular SDEs with time homogeneous coefficients only. For the existence of a semi-flow, actually the above local/global results hold also when the coefficients are not time homogeneous. Then the required conditions are:
|b|\in{\mathbb{L}}_{q_{1}}([0,T],\tilde{\mathbb{L}}_{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
,
|\nabla\sigma|\in{\mathbb{L}}_{q_{2}}([0,T],\tilde{\mathbb{L}}_{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
for some
p_{i},q_{i}\in[2,\infty)
with
\frac{d}{p_{i}}+\frac{2}{q_{i}}<1
(known as ’LPS’ condition),
i=1,2
for the existence of a global semi-flow. For the existence of local semi-flow we assume
|b|,|\nabla\sigma|\in{\mathbb{L}}_{q_{n}}([0,n],{\mathbb{L}}_{p_{n}}(B_{n}))
for
p_{n},q_{n}\in[2,\infty)
with
\frac{d}{p_{n}}+\frac{2}{q_{n}}<1
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. In both cases it is necessary to assume the uniform ellipticity condition to hold globally and locally respectively.

Abstract
. We study the internal dynamics of multiply connected wandering domains of meromorphic functions. We do so by considering the sequence of injectivity radii along the orbit of a base point, together with the hyperbolic distortions along the same orbit. The latter sequence has previously been used in this context; the former introduces geometric information about the shape of the wandering domains that interacts with the dynamical information given by the hyperbolic distortions. Using this idea, we complete the description of the internal dynamics of any wandering domain of a meromorphic function, and also unify previous approaches to the question. We conclude that the internal dynamics of a wandering domain, from the point of view of hyperbolic geometry, can be classified into six different types. Five of these types were previously known and are realised by wandering domains of entire functions. The sixth type arises only for meromorphic functions: a locally but not globally eventually isometric wandering domain. We construct a meromorphic function with such a domain, demonstrating that this new phenomenon does in fact occur. Our results show that, on a local level, the dynamical behaviour of wandering domains of meromorphic functions is similar to that which occurs of entire functions. However, new global phenomena can arise in the non-entire case. In particular, we identify a category of multiply connected wandering domains – essentially thin and infinitesimally non-contracting domains – that naturally generalise multiply connected wandering domains of entire functions, and share many characteristics with these. We generalise a number of results of Bergweiler, Rippon and Stallard for the entire case to this more general setting. In particular, we show the existence of dynamically meaningful singular foliations provided by level lines of certain positive harmonic functions.

The contracting case

The case of an infinitesimally contracting wandering domain is the simplest from the point of view of the study of the sequence (

Ideas of the proofs

The most challenging part of our main theorem (Theorem
1.5
) is proving Theorems

and
1.9

[

,
24
]
, and rely on structural theorems available only to entire functions. Most crucially, in
[

]
, the authors use properties of the maximum modulus to obtain a harmonic function

that encodes (in a certain sense) the rate of growth of

relative to a base point

; see (

lead to the existence of an absorbing sequence of large (round) annuli, and the contracting foliation from
[

is given by the level lines of

For meromorphic functions, we do not have the maximum modulus to work with, and orbits in a multiply connected wandering domain need not tend to infinity. Hence the above strategy cannot be
applied, and new ideas are required to deal with the non-contracting thin case. To overcome this challenge,
we consider universal coverings of the domains
U_{n}
(Lemma
2.6
),
and show that the corresponding groups of
deck transformations converge to
a continuous one-dimensional limit group (Lemma
4.3
). The orbits of points under this limit group give rise to the desired contracting foliation. For large

, the point
f^{n}(z_{0})
is contained in an annulus of large modulus bounded by closed leaves in the contracting foliation. As we show in Section
5
,
the foliation is again given by the level lines of a positive harmonic function –
but here the function is obtained
a posteriori
from the contracting foliation.

(Another approach to the existence of attracting foliations in multiply connected wandering domains of entire functions was outlined by Shishikura
[
36
]
in unpublished work. His argument uses the existence of collars (annuli of large modulus) around short geodesics in
U_{n}
. Using this theorem and the Schwarz–Pick lemma, Shishikura is able to show the existence of an absorbing sequence of such collars. He then constructs the desired foliation as a limit of the
concentric foliations of the collars.
This strategy can
also be adapted to our setting. Instead, we construct the foliation directly;
the absorbing annuli
and their key properties appear naturally via the structure of this
foliation.)

The remainder of Theorem
1.5
is proved by applying or adapting known
techniques. In order to show the existence of locally but not globally eventually
isometric wandering domains (Theorem
1.6
), we first show that such an
example can be realised by a

sequence of
holomorphic covering maps between plane domains (see Definition
1.10
). Each of these covering maps is the restriction of a Blaschke product of the disc of degree two, chosen
(inductively) to be
sufficiently close to the identity on a large subset of the disc (Proposition
7.1
). We then
realise this model dynamics by a multiply connected wandering
domain of a meromorphic function, using a recent
result of Evdoridou, Martí-Pete, and the second author
[
20
]
.

We now review some important results and concepts in hyperbolic geometry, beginning with the definition of hyperbolic distortion.

Definition 2.2

(Hyperbolic distortion)
.

Let
f\colon U\to V
be a holomorphic map between hyperbolic Riemann surfaces, and let

z_{0}\in U
. The
hyperbolic distortion
of
f
at
z_{0}
is

(2.1)

\|\mathchoice{}{}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\mathinner{\mathrm{D}\hbox{}}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}f(z)\|_{U}^{V}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\lim_{z\to z_{0}}\frac{d_{V}(f(z),f(z_{0}))}{d_{U}(z,z_{0})}.

If
z
and
w
are local coordinates of
U
and
V
near
z_{0}
and
f(z_{0})
,
we can express the hyperbolic metrics of
U
and
V
as

\rho_{U}(z)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}z
and
\rho_{V}(w)\mathinner{\mathrm{d}}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}w
in these coordinates, with

\rho_{U}(z),\rho_{V}(w)>0
. In terms of these densities, the hyperbolic distortion of
f
at
z_{0}
can be expressed as

(2.2)

\|\mathchoice{}{}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\mathinner{\mathrm{D}\hbox{}}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}f(z)\|_{U}^{V}=\frac{\rho_{V}\left(f(z)\right)|f^{\prime}(z)|}{\rho_{U}(z)}

When
V=U
; i.e.,
f\colon U\to U
is a holomorphic
self-map of
U
,
we also write
\|\mathchoice{}{}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\mathinner{\mathrm{D}\hbox{}}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}f(z)\|_{U}
instead of

\|\mathchoice{}{}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\mathinner{\mathrm{D}\hbox{}}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}f(z)\|_{U}^{U}
.

The
Schwarz–Pick theorem

[
10
, Theorem 3.2]
states that holomorphic maps do not increase the hyperbolic metric.

Theorem 2.3

(Schwarz–Pick theorem)
.

Let
f\colon U\to V
be a holomorphic map between hyperbolic Riemann surfaces. Then

(2.3)

\|\mathchoice{}{}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\mathinner{\mathrm{D}\hbox{}}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}f(z)\|_{U}^{V}\leq 1\text{ for all $z\in U$},

with equality if and only if
f\colon U\to V
is an unbranched covering map.

Beardon
[
11
]
proved a version of the
Schwarz–Pick theorem for the hyperbolic distortion; see also
[
10
, Theorem 11.2]
.

Theorem 2.4

(Schwarz–Pick theorem for hyperbolic distortion)
.

Let
f\colon U\to V
be a holomorphic map between hyperbolic Riemann surfaces but not a covering map. Then, for all
z,w\in U
,

d_{\mathbb{D}}\bigl(\|\mathchoice{}{}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\mathinner{\mathrm{D}\hbox{}}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}f(z)\|_{U}^{V},\|\mathchoice{}{}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\mathinner{\mathrm{D}\hbox{}}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}f(z)\|_{U}^{V}\bigr)\leq 2d_{U}(z,w).

As mentioned above, we will prove all of our results for the general case of non-autonomous systems; the dynamics of meromorphic functions on an orbit of wandering domains is a special case. We use the following notation.

Definition 2.5

(Non-autonomous iteration)
.

Let
F=(U_{n},f_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a non-autonomous system on hyperbolic surfaces, and let
z_{0}\in U_{0}
. For
k,n\geq 0
, we define

f_{n}^{k}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=f_{n+k-1}\dots f_{n}\colon U_{n}\to U_{n+k}.

The sequence
(z_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
defined by
z_{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=f_{0}^{n}(z_{0})\in U_{n}
is called the
orbit
of
z_{0}
. As in (
1.2
), we define the sequence of hyperbolic distortions along the orbit of
z_{0}
by

\lambda_{n}(z_{0})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\lambda_{n}(z_{0},F)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\|\mathchoice{}{}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\mathinner{\mathrm{D}\hbox{}}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}f_{n}(z_{n})\|_{U_{n}}^{U_{n+1}}.

In the case of a meromorphic function
f
with an orbit
(U_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
of
wandering domains, we have
f_{n}^{k}=f^{k}|_{U_{n}}
, and
(z_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
is the
orbit of
z_{0}
in the usual sense.

It is often useful to lift the system
F
to the universal coverings of
the surfaces
U_{n}
. (See
[
26
, Lemmas 2.3 and 3.2]
.)

Lemma 2.6

(Preferred lifts)
.

Let
F=(U_{n},f_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a non-autonomous system on hyperbolic surfaces, and let

z_{0}\in U_{0}
with orbit
(z_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
.

There are sequences
(\pi_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
of universal covering maps

\pi_{n}\colon\mathbb{D}\to U_{n}
and non-constant holomorphic functions

g_{n}\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
with the following properties for
n\geq 0
:

(1)

\pi_{n}(0)=z_{n}
;

(2)

g_{n}(0)=0
;

(3)

f_{n}\circ\pi_{n}=\pi_{n+1}\circ g_{n}
;

(4)

if
z_{n}
is not a critical point of
f_{n}
, then
g_{n}^{\prime}(0)>0
.

We say that
G=(\mathbb{D},g_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
, which is a non-autonomous system on
\mathbb{D}
,
is a
preferred lift
of the
system
F
(via the universal coverings
(\pi_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
). The system
G
satisfies

(2.4)

g_{n}^{\prime}(0)=\lambda_{n}(0,G)=\lambda_{n}(z_{0},F)

for all
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

The construction of the system
G
proceeds recursively. Let
\pi_{0}\colon\mathbb{D}\to U_{0}
be any universal covering with
\pi_{0}(0)=z_{0}
.

Now suppose that
n\geq 0
, and that the maps
\pi_{k}
have been defined for
k\leq n
and that
g_{k}
has been defined for
k<n
. Let
\tilde{\pi}\colon\mathbb{D}\to U_{n}
be a universal covering with
\pi(0)=z_{n}
, and let
\tilde{g}\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be the corresponding lift of
f_{n}
; that is,
\tilde{g}(0)=0
and
\tilde{g}\circ\pi_{n}=\tilde{\pi}\circ f_{n}
. By the Schwarz–Pick theorem and the
chain rule, we have

\lvert(\tilde{g})^{\prime}(0)\rvert=\|\mathchoice{}{}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\mathinner{\mathrm{D}\hbox{}}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}\tilde{g}(0)\|_{\mathbb{D}}=\|\mathchoice{}{}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\mathinner{\mathrm{D}\hbox{}}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}f_{n}(z_{n})\rvert_{U_{n}}^{U_{n+1}}.

If
z_{n}
is a critical point of
f_{n}
, set
g_{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\tilde{g}
and
\pi_{n+1}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\tilde{\pi}
. Otherwise, postcompose
\tilde{g}
with a rotation to obtain
g_{n}
with
g_{n}^{\prime}(0)>0
and precompose
\tilde{\pi}
with the inverse of this rotation to obtain
\pi_{n+1}
.

This ensures properties
1
to
4
as well as (
2.4
) for
n
, and completes the recursive construction and the proof.
∎

Definition 2.7
.

Let
X
be a locally compact metrisable topological space, and let
\mathcal{C}(X)
denote the set of all closed subsets of

(C_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{C}(X)

(1)

and points

such that
x_{k}\to x
as

(2)

For any

(x_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}

We will apply this definition in the case where

\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
is that of uniform convergence; recall that the map
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{D})\to\mathbb{D}\times S^{1};\gamma\mapsto(\gamma(0),\arg\gamma^{\prime}(0))
is
a homeomorphism. In order to effectively use the geometric topology to our advantage, we need the following:

Theorem 2.8

(Geometric topology for closed subgroups
[
12
]
*Lemma E.1.4)
.

The subset
\mathcal{S}
of all closed subgroups of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
is compact in the geometric topology.

Theorem 2.9

(Continuous limit groups)
.

Suppose that
(\Gamma_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a sequence of torsion-free Fuchsian groups that converges to a group
\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}
. Furthermore suppose that there is a sequence
(\gamma_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
with
\gamma_{n}\in\Gamma_{n}\setminus\{\operatorname{id}\}
such that
\gamma_{n}\to\operatorname{id}
as
n\to\infty
.

Then
\Gamma
is a continuous one-parameter subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
. More precisely,
\Gamma
is conjugate either to the group of real translations on the upper half-plane, or to the group of real translations on a horizontal bi-infinite strip.

Proof.

By
[
31
, Theorem 7.1]
, the limiting group is Abelian. Since
\Gamma_{n}
is torsion-free and discrete, the orbit of
\gamma_{n}
is infinite. It follows easily that
\Gamma
contains elements arbitrarily close to the identity and is therefore non-discrete.

Two Möbius transformations commute if and only if they have the same set of fixed points. Hence
\Gamma
is either a one-parameter group of hyperbolic transformations, all sharing the same two fixed points on
\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}\mathbb{D}
, or a one-parameter group of parabolic transformations, all sharing the same single fixed point of
\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}\mathbb{D}
. By sending the two fixed points to the ends of a bi-infinite strip, or by sending the single fixed point to the boundary point of the upper half-plane at infinity, we obtain one of the two stated alternatives.

Finally, we discuss injectivity radii of hyperbolic Riemann surfaces.

Definition 2.10

(Injectivity radius)
.

Let
X
be a hyperbolic Riemann surface, and let
p\in X
. The
injectivity radius
of
X
at
p
is

\inj_{X}(p)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\inf\{\ell_{X}(\gamma)/2\colon\text{$\gamma\subset X$ is a non-trivial loop through $p$}\}.

If
X
is simply connected, we define
\inj_{X}(p)
to be infinite.

An equivalent way of stating Definition
2.10
(and one that better explains the name) is that
\inj(p)
is the supremum of
r>0
such that a universal covering
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to X
, normalised so that
\varphi(0)=p
, is injective in the hyperbolic ball
D_{\mathbb{D}}(0,r)
(cf.
[
37
, Section 4.5]
). We will use the following closely related characterisation.

Lemma 2.11

(Injectivity radii in terms of universal coverings)
.

Let
X
be a hyperbolic Riemann surface, let
p\in X
, and let
\pi\colon\mathbb{D}\to X
be a universal covering map such that
\pi(0)=p
. Let
\tilde{p}\in\pi^{-1}(0)\setminus\{0\}

be a point such that

d\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=d_{\mathbb{D}}(0,\tilde{p})=\min\{d_{\mathbb{D}}(0,\tilde{p})\colon\tilde{p}\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\},\pi(\tilde{p})=p\}.

Then
\inj_{X}(p)=d/2
.

Proof.

If
X
is simply connected, both
\inj_{X}(p)
and
d
are (by convention) infinite, and so we can assume that
X
is not simply connected. Join
0
to
\tilde{p}
by a geodesic arc
\gamma\in\mathbb{D}
; then
\pi(\gamma)
is
a non-trivial closed curve through
p
in
S
(recall that any trivial loop in
S
lifts to
a trivial loop in
\mathbb{D}
under
\pi
). So, by definition,

\inj_{X}(p)\leq\frac{1}{2}\ell_{X}(\pi(\gamma))=\frac{1}{2}\ell_{\mathbb{D}}(\gamma)=\frac{d}{2}.

For the reverse inequality, let
\gamma\in X
be some non-contractible closed curve passing through
p
. By the unique path-lifting property of
\pi

[
17
]
*Theorem 3.3, there
is a lift
\tilde{\gamma}\subset\mathbb{D}
of
\gamma
starting at
0
and terminating at some

\hat{p}\in\pi^{-1}(p)
distinct from zero. We have

d\leq d_{\mathbb{D}}(0,\hat{p})\leq\ell_{\mathbb{D}}(\tilde{\gamma})=\ell_{X}(\gamma);

thus,

\inj_{X}(p)=\frac{1}{2}\inf_{\gamma}\ell_{X}(\gamma)\geq\frac{d}{2}.\qed

3.
Uniformity at the infinitesimal level

In this section, we prove Propositions
1.1
and
1.4
, restated below in more general forms as Propositions
3.3
and
3.4
. In the proof of
Proposition
3.3
, we use the following formula for the hyperbolic distance between points in the unit disc (see
[
10
, p. 13]
).

Lemma 3.1

(Hyperbolic distances)
.

Let
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
be such that
0\leq z\leq w<1
. Then

d_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)=\log\left(\frac{1+w}{1-w}\cdot\frac{1-z}{1+z}\right).

Using Lemma
3.1
, we obtain the following bounds for the hyperbolic distortion:

Lemma 3.2

(Hyperbolic distortion and hyperbolic distance)
.

Let
g\colon X\to Y
be a holomorphic map between hyperbolic Riemann surfaces. For
p\in X
, let
\lambda(p)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\|\mathchoice{}{}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\mathinner{\mathrm{D}\hbox{}}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}g(p)\|_{X}^{Y}
. Then, for any
z,w\in X
,

e^{-2d_{X}(z,w)}(1-\lambda(w))\leq 1-\lambda(z)\leq e^{2d_{X}(z,w)}(1-\lambda(w)).

Proof.

First, if
\lambda(z)=1
, then by the Schwarz–Pick lemma
g
is a covering map, and so
\lambda(w)=1
for every
w
in
X
. Thus, in this case, the inequality is trivial.

Assume, for now, that
\lambda(z)\leq\lambda(w)<1
. Applying Lemma
3.1
gives us

\frac{1+\lambda(w)}{1-\lambda(w)}\cdot\frac{1-\lambda(z)}{1+\lambda(z)}=e^{d_{\mathbb{D}}(\lambda(z),\lambda(w))}.

Therefore,

1-\lambda(z)=e^{d_{\mathbb{D}}(\lambda(z),\lambda(w))}(1-\lambda(w))\frac{1+\lambda(z)}{1+\lambda(w)}\leq e^{d_{\mathbb{D}}(\lambda(z),\lambda(w))}(1-\lambda(w)),

where the inequality follows from the fact that
0\leq\lambda(z)\leq\lambda(w)<1
.

By Beardon’s Schwarz–Pick theorem for hyperbolic distortions (Theorem
2.4
),

1-\lambda(z)\leq e^{2d_{X}(z,w)}(1-\lambda(w)).

Reversing the roles of
z
and
w
in the proof gives the lower bound, and the proof is complete.
∎

We now prove Proposition
1.1
, in the following more general form
for the setting of non-autonomous systems.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
F=(U_{n},f_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a non-autonomous system on hyperbolic
surfaces. Then, for all
n\geq 0
and any
z,w\in U_{0}
,

e^{-2d_{U_{0}}(z,w)}(1-\lambda_{n}(w))\leq 1-\lambda_{n}(z)\leq e^{2d_{U_{0}}(z,w)}(1-\lambda_{n}(w)).

In particular, whether the series

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-\lambda_{n}(z))

converges, diverges, or has a finite number of non-zero terms does not depend on the choice of
z\in U_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
n\geq 0
and
z,w\in U_{0}
. Write
z_{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=f_{0}^{n}(z)
and

w_{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=f_{0}^{n}(w)
. By Lemma
3.2
,

e^{-2d_{U_{n}}(z_{n},w_{n})}(1-\lambda_{n}(w))\leq 1-\lambda_{n}(z)\leq e^{2d_{U_{n}}(z_{n},w_{n})}(1-\lambda_{n}(w)),

while by Theorem
2.3

d_{U_{n}}(z_{n},w_{n})\leq d_{U_{0}}(z,w)
.
∎

Next, we prove Proposition
1.4
, again restated here in more
general form.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
F=(U_{n},f_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a non-autonomous system on hyperbolic
surfaces. Let
z\in U_{0}
.
If
(n_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}
is a sequence such that
\delta_{n_{k}}(z)\to 0
, then
\delta_{n_{k}}(w)\to 0
for all
w\in U_{0}
.
(Recall from (
2.5
) that
\delta_{n}(w)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\inj_{U_{n}}(f_{0}^{n}(w))
for

n\geq 0
and
w\in U_{0}
.)

Proof.

Let
(\mathbb{D},g_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a preferred lift of the system
F
via universal
covering maps
\pi_{n}\colon\mathbb{D}\to U_{n}
.
For
n\geq 0
, set

z_{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=f_{0}^{n}(z)
and let

\tilde{z}_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
be a point of

\pi_{n}^{-1}(z)
closest to the origin, i.e. so that

|\tilde{z}_{n}|=\inf\{\lvert w\rvert\colon w\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\},\pi_{n}(w)=z\}.

For each
n\geq 0
, we find a deck transformation
\gamma_{n}\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
so that
\pi_{n}(0)=\tilde{z}_{n}
. By Lemma
2.11
, the hypothesis
\inj_{U_{n_{k}}}(z_{n_{k}})\to 0
implies that
\tilde{z}_{n_{k}}\to 0
as
k\to+\infty
, and we claim that this implies that the sequence
(\gamma_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges locally uniformly to the identity as
k\to+\infty
. First, since the autormorphisms of
\mathbb{D}
can be parametrised by their image and derivative at zero, we see (since
\tilde{z}_{n_{k}}=\gamma_{n_{k}}(0)\to 0
) that the sequence
(\gamma_{n_{k}})_{k=0}^{\infty}
is precompact in
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
, and that any of its limit functions is an automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
fixing the origin. Since no
\gamma_{n_{k}}
has fixed points in
\mathbb{D}
, it follows from Hurwitz’s theorem that the only possible limit function is the identity. Thus
\gamma_{n_{k}}\to\operatorname{id}
as
k\to+\infty
, uniformly in the Euclidean metric and locally uniformly in the hyperbolic metric of
\mathbb{D}
.

Now let
w\in U_{0}
and set
w_{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=f_{0}^{n}(w)

for
n\geq 0
. Then, for each
n
,
there exists
\tilde{w}_{n}\in\varphi^{-1}(w_{n})
such that

d_{\mathbb{D}}(0,\tilde{w}_{n})=d_{U_{n}}(w_{n},z_{n})\leq d_{U_{0}}(w,z),

where the inequality comes from the Schwarz–Pick lemma. By the definition of the injectivity radius,

\inj_{U_{n_{k}}}(w_{n_{k}})\leq\frac{1}{2}d_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\tilde{w}_{n_{k}},\gamma_{n_{k}}(\tilde{w}_{n_{k}})\right).

The conclusion now follows from the fact that
\gamma_{n_{k}}\to\operatorname{id}
as
k\to+\infty
.
∎

4.
Essential thinness, large annuli, and contracting foliations

Throughout this and the next section, we fix a non-autonomous system

F=(U_{n},f_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}

of holomorphic maps
f_{n}\colon U_{n}\to U_{n+1}
between hyperbolic Riemann surfaces (in the sense of Definition
1.10
). We also fix an orbit
(z_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
and a corresponding preferred lift

G=(\mathbb{D},g_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
of
F
as in Lemma
2.6
, via universal
coverings
(\pi_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
. For each
n\geq 0
, let
\Gamma_{n}\leq\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
be the group of
deck transformations of
\pi_{n}
. We begin with a general observation.

Lemma 4.1

(Limit functions)
.

For every
k\geq 0
, as
n\to\infty
the functions

g_{k}^{n}\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
converge locally uniformly to a holomorphic function

G_{k}\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
with
G_{k}(0)=0
.
Furthermore,
G_{k}=G_{k+1}\circ g_{k}
for all
k\geq 0
.

The systems
F
and
G
are
infinitesimally eventually isometric if and only if
G_{k}=\operatorname{id}

for all sufficiently large
k
. They are infinitesimally semi-contracting if and only if
G_{k}\to\operatorname{id}

locally uniformly but
G_{k}\neq\operatorname{id}
for all
k
. The systems are
infinitesimally
contracting if and only if
G_{k}(z)=0
for all
k\geq 0
and all
z\in\mathbb{D}
;
in this case, they are globally
contracting.

Proof.

The family
\mathcal{G}
of all holomorphic self-maps of
\mathbb{D}
with
g(0)=0
is compact with respect to the topology of locally uniform convergence. Indeed, the family is normal by (the weak version of) Montel’s theorem
[
3
]
*Theorem 5.15, and any limit function is again holomorphic by Weierstrass’s theorem
[
3
]
*Theorem 5.1.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{k}\subset\mathcal{G}
denote the set of all limit functions of

g_{k}^{n}
as
n\to\infty
. Then, an element
G\in\mathcal{G}
belongs to

\mathcal{G}_{k+1}
if and only if

G\circ g_{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{k}
. By the Schwarz–Pick theorem,
for every
z\in\mathbb{D}

the hyperbolic distortion
\|\mathchoice{}{}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\mathinner{\mathrm{D}\hbox{}}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}g_{k}^{n}(z)\|_{\mathbb{D}}

is non-increasing
as
n\to\infty
. If these distortions converge locally uniformly to
0
as

n\to\infty
, then every

\mathcal{G}_{k}
contains only the constant function
z\mapsto 0
, and
the systems
F
and
G
are
infinitesimally and globally contracting.

Otherwise, let
K\subset\mathbb{D}
be a closed disc centred at
0
. Then

(4.1)

\sup\{\|\mathchoice{}{}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\mathinner{\mathrm{D}\hbox{}}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}G(z)\|_{\mathbb{D}}\colon G\in\mathcal{G}_{k},z\in K\}\to 1

as
k\to\infty
. Suppose that

(G_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}

is a sequence with
G_{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{k}
for
k\geq 0
. Then it follows from (
4.1
)
that any limit function
\varphi
of

G_{k}
as
k\to\infty
is a conformal isomorphism with
\varphi(0)=0
;
since
G_{k}^{\prime}(0)\in[0,\infty)
, we must
have
\varphi=\operatorname{id}
.

So, for any
r>0
and all
sufficiently large
j
,
all functions in
\mathcal{G}_{j}

are close to the identity
on the hyperbolic disc
D_{\mathbb{D}}(0,r)
.
Let
k>0
. Any two limit functions
G,H\in\mathcal{G}_{k}

can be written as
G=G_{n}\circ g_{k}^{n}

and
H=H_{n}\circ g_{k}^{n}
for all

n\geq k
and some

G_{n},H_{n}\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
.
Since
G_{n}\to\operatorname{id}
and
H_{n}\to\operatorname{id}

as
n\to\infty
,
we conclude that
G=H
.
(Recall that
g_{k}^{n}(D_{\mathbb{D}}(0,r))\subset D_{\mathbb{D}}(0,r)
.)

We have proved that
\mathcal{G}_{n}
contains
a single element
G_{n}
, and hence

g_{k}^{n}\to G_{k}
locally uniformly as

n\to\infty
. We have also shown that

G_{k}\to\operatorname{id}
locally uniformly.
Recall that
F
and
G

are infinitesimally
eventually isometric if and only if
there is
k_{0}
such that

g_{k}=\operatorname{id}
for all
k\geq k_{0}
,
and hence
G_{k}=\operatorname{id}
for
k\geq k_{0}
.
Conversely, if
g_{k_{0}}\neq\operatorname{id}
for
some
k_{0}
, then
G_{k}\neq\operatorname{id}
for
k\leq k_{0}
, since

\|\mathchoice{}{}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\mathinner{\mathrm{D}\hbox{}}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}G_{k}(z)\|_{\mathbb{D}}\leq\|\mathchoice{}{}{\hbox{}}{\hbox{}}\mathinner{\mathrm{D}\hbox{}}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}g_{k_{0}}(g_{k}^{k_{0}-k}(z))\|_{\mathbb{D}}<1

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Hence, if

g_{k}\neq\operatorname{id}
for infinitely many

k
, then
G_{k}\neq\operatorname{id}
for all
k\geq 0
. This completes the proof.
∎

We note the following consequence of the preceding lemma, which is essentially
[
26
, Theorem 1.1(a)]
, and also follows by appplying
[
13
, Theorem 2.1]
to the system
G
.

Corollary 4.2

(Infinitesimally contracting systems are globally contracting)
.

A non-autonomus system
F
of holomorphic maps between hyperbolic Riemann surfaces is infinitesimally contracting if and only if it is globally contracting.

We now restrict to the case where
F
is non-contracting, so that the limit
functions
G_{k}
are non-constant, and
G_{k}\to\operatorname{id}
as
k\to\infty
.

Lemma 4.3

(Limit group)
.

Suppose that the system
F
is infinitesimally non-contracting. Then the groups
\Gamma_{n}
converge geometrically to a torsion-free group
\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}
. Moreover, for every
k\geq 0
, there are group homomorphisms
\psi_{k}\colon\Gamma_{k}\to\Gamma_{k+1}
and
\Psi_{k}\colon\Gamma_{k}\to\Gamma
such that
g_{k}\circ\gamma=\psi_{k}(\gamma)\circ g_{k}
and
G_{k}\circ\gamma=\Psi_{k}(\gamma)\circ G_{k}
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma_{k}
.

Either
\Gamma=\{\operatorname{id}\}
and

\delta_{n}(z_{0})=\inj_{U_{n}}(z_{n})\to\infty
,
or the sequence

\delta_{n}(z_{0})
is eventually finite
and non-increasing. The group
\Gamma
is discrete if and only if
F
is
essentially thick (i.e., if and only
if
\delta_{n}(z_{0})\not\to 0
).
Otherwise,
\Gamma
is a continuous one-parameter
group as in Theorem
2.9
.

If
F
is infinitesimally eventually isometric, then
\psi_{k}=\Psi_{k}=\operatorname{id}
for all sufficiently large
k
.

Proof.

The existence of the group homomorphisms
\psi_{k}
is a consequence of the basic properties of covering maps; see
[
1
]
*Proposition 1.6.14. For
k\geq 0
and
n\geq 0
, define
\psi_{k}^{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\psi_{k+n-1}\circ\dots\circ\psi_{k}\colon\Gamma_{k}\to\Gamma_{k+n}
. Then
g_{k}^{n}\circ\gamma=\psi_{k}^{n}(\gamma)\circ g_{k}^{n}
. Since
G
is infinitesimally non-contracting, we have
G_{k}^{n}\to\operatorname{id}
as
k\to\infty
. Since
(g_{k}^{n})^{\prime}(0)\geq(G_{k}^{n})^{\prime}(0)\to 1
for all
n\geq k
, we have
g_{k}^{n}\to\operatorname{id}
uniformly in
n\geq k
as
k\to\infty
.
We conclude that
\psi_{k}^{n}(\gamma)
is uniformly close to
\gamma
when
k
is large, independently of
n
. It follows that the sequence
(\psi_{k}^{n}(\gamma))_{n=0}^{\infty}
is Cauchy for every
k
, and hence converges to an element
\Psi_{k}(\gamma)
of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
as
n\to\infty
. Moreover, this convergence is uniform in the following sense: If
K\subset\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
is compact, then
\Psi_{k}(\gamma)
is uniformly close to
\gamma
for all sufficiently large
k
and all
\gamma\in K\cap\Gamma_{k}
.

Observe that
\Psi_{k}(\Gamma_{k})=\Psi_{k+1}(\psi_{k}(\Gamma_{k}))\subset\Psi_{k+1}(\Gamma_{k+1})
for all
k\geq 0
. Let
\Gamma
be the closure of the increasing union of all
\Psi_{k}(\Gamma_{k})
. We claim that
\Gamma_{k}\to\Gamma
. Indeed, let
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Then
there is a sequence
(\gamma_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}
such that

\Psi_{k}(\gamma_{k})\to\gamma
.
Moreover, each
\Psi_{k}(\gamma_{k})
is the limit of

\psi_{k}^{j}(\gamma_{k})\in\Gamma_{k+j}

as
j\to\infty
.
It follows that

\psi_{k(n)}^{n-k(n)}(\gamma_{k(n)})\to\gamma
as
n\to\infty
if

k(n)\to\infty
sufficiently slowly.
This establishes condition
2

of Definition
2.7
.
On the other hand,
suppose that a sequence
(\gamma_{n_{k}})_{k=0}^{\infty}
with

\gamma_{n_{k}}\in\Gamma_{n_{k}}

is given such that

\gamma_{n_{k}}\to\gamma\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
as
k\to\infty
. Then

K=\{\gamma_{n_{k}}\colon k\geq 0\}\cup\{\gamma\}\subset\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
is
compact. By the above
uniformity statement, we have

\Psi_{n_{k}}(\gamma_{n_{k}})\to\gamma
,
and hence
\gamma\in\Gamma
. This
establishes
1

of Definition
2.7
.
Hence
\Gamma_{k}\to\Gamma
, as claimed.

Next, recall that

\displaystyle 2\delta_{k}(z_{0})=2\inj_{U_{k}}(z_{k})

\displaystyle=\min\{d_{\mathbb{D}}(0,z)\colon z\in\mathbb{D}^{*},\pi_{k}(z)=z_{k}\}

\displaystyle=\min\{d_{\mathbb{D}}(0,\gamma(0))\colon\gamma\in\Gamma_{k}\setminus\{\operatorname{id}\}\}

by Lemma
2.11
.
Let
R>0
and let

K
be the closed hyperbolic disc
of radius
R
centred at
0
. There is

k_{0}
such that
g_{k}
is injective on
K

for all
k\geq k_{0}
. Suppose that

2\delta_{k}(z_{0})<R
for some
k\geq k_{0}
,
and let
\gamma\in\Gamma_{k}\setminus\{\operatorname{id}\}

such that
d_{\mathbb{D}}(0,\gamma(0))
is minimal.
Then

\psi_{k}(\gamma)(0)=\psi_{k}(\gamma)(g_{k}(0))=g_{k}(\gamma(0)).

By the Schwarz–Pick theorem,

2\delta_{k+1}(z_{0})\leq d_{\mathbb{D}}(0,g_{k}(\gamma(0)))\leq d_{\mathbb{D}}(0,\gamma(0))=2\delta_{k}(z_{0}).

We conclude that either
\delta_{k}(z_{0})\to\infty
, in which
case
\Gamma=\{\operatorname{id}\}
,
or the sequence
\delta_{k}(z_{0})
is
eventually non-increasing.

In particular,
F
is essentially thin if and only if
\delta_{k}(z_{0})\to 0
. In this case,
\Gamma
is a a continuous one-parameter subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
by Theorem
2.9
.

Otherwise,
F
is essentially thick,
\operatorname{id}
is an isolated element of
\Gamma
, and hence
\Gamma
is discrete.

If
F
is infinitesimally eventually isometric, then there is
k_{0}
such that
f_{k}
is a covering map, and hence
g_{k}=\operatorname{id}
, for all
k\geq k_{0}
. It follows that
\psi_{k}=\Psi_{k}=\operatorname{id}
for
k\geq k_{0}
.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

If the system
F
is infinitesimally eventually isometric and essentially thin, then, for all sufficiently large
n
,
U_{n}
is doubly connected and
f_{n}\colon U_{n}\to U_{n+1}
is a finite-degree covering map.

Proof.

For sufficiently large
n
,
\Psi_{n}=\operatorname{id}
, and hence
\Gamma_{n}
is a nontrivial discrete subgroup of the limit group
\Gamma
, which is isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}
. Hence
\Gamma_{n}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
, and
U_{n}
is doubly connected.

That
f_{n}
is a covering map is a simple consequence of the Schwarz–Pick theorem. Its degree is given by the index of
(f_{n})_{*}(\pi_{1}(U_{n}))\simeq\mathbb{Z}
as a subgroup of
\pi_{1}(U_{n+1})\simeq\mathbb{Z}
, which must be finite.
∎

Let us now assume that the system
F
is essentially thin, so that the limit group
\Gamma
is a continuous one-parameter group, conjugate to the group of translational isometries of either a finite horizontal strip (the
hyperbolic
case) or of the upper half-plane (the
parabolic
case). Let
\mathcal{F}
be the foliation of
\mathbb{D}
by the orbits of
\Gamma
. In the parabolic case
\mathcal{F}
consists of the horocycles based at the unique common fixed point of the elements of
\Gamma
, while in the hyperbolic case it consists of arcs of circles connecting the two common fixed points of the elements of
\Gamma
. Also, let
\mathcal{L}
be the foliation of
\mathbb{D}
perpendicular to
\mathcal{F}
. In the parabolic case, its leaves are the geodesics connecting the fixed point of
\Gamma
to other points of
\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}\mathbb{D}
. In the hyperbolic case, the leaves are the geodesics of
\mathbb{D}
perpendicular to the unique geodesic connecting the two fixed points of
\Gamma
.

Theorem 4.5
.

For every
k\geq 0
, the pull-back
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{k}
of
\mathcal{F}
under
G_{k}
is an analytic singular foliation that is invariant under
\Gamma_{k}
. Hence the image of
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{k}
under
\pi_{k}
is an analytic singular foliation
\mathcal{F}_{k}
of
U_{k}
, and
f_{k}
maps
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{k}
to
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{k+1}
.

Similarly, the pull-back
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{k}

of
\mathcal{L}
is an analytic
singular foliation of
\mathbb{D}
, and its
image
\mathcal{L}_{k}
under

\pi_{k}
is an analytic singular foliation
of
U_{k}
.

The foliations

\mathcal{F}_{k}

and
\mathcal{L}_{k}
depend
only on the system
F
, not on the choice
of
z_{0}
or
G
.

Proof.

The pull-back of any analytic singular
foliation under an analytic function
is again an analytic singular foliation.

The foliations
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{L}
are invariant under

\Gamma
. Hence, if
\gamma\in\Gamma_{k}
,
then
\Psi_{k}(\gamma)
maps leaves
of either foliation to leaves,
and it follows
that
\gamma
maps leaves of

\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{k}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{k}
to
leaves. This property
ensures that

\mathcal{F}_{k}
and
\mathcal{L}_{k}
are well-defined
singular foliations on
U_{k}
.

That the foliations of
U_{k}
depend only on the system
F
follows easily from the construction, since different choices of
G
and
z_{0}
would yield foliations
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{k}^{\prime}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{k}^{\prime}
conjugate to
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{k}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{k}
respectively.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
z,w\in U_{0}
be such that
z_{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=f_{0}^{n}(z)\neq f_{0}^{n}(w)=\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}w_{n}
for all
n\geq 0
. Set
d_{n}(z,w)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=d_{U_{n}}(z_{n},w_{n})\neq 0
.

Then
\lim_{n\to\infty}d_{n}(z,w)=0
if and only if
z_{n}
and
w_{n}
belong to the same leaf of
\mathcal{F}_{n}
for sufficiently large
n
.

If
F
and
G
are infinitesimally eventually isometric, the sequence
(d_{n}(z,w))_{n=0}^{\infty}
is eventually constant if and only if
z_{n}
and
w_{n}
belong to the same leaf of
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{n}
for sufficiently large
n
.

In all other cases,

d_{n}(z_{n},w_{n})
tends
to a positive limit as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\zeta_{0}
and
\omega_{0}
be
preimages of
z
and
w
under

\pi_{0}
, and set

\zeta_{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=g_{0}^{n}(\zeta)
,

\omega_{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=g_{0}^{n}(\omega)
.
Then

d_{n}(z,w)=d_{\mathbb{D}}(\Gamma_{n}(\zeta_{n}),\Gamma_{n}(\omega_{n}))
. Recall
that the sequence
d_{n}(z,w)
is
non-increasing by the Schwarz–Pick
theorem.

As
n\to\infty
,

\Gamma_{n}(\zeta_{n})
converges to the
orbit of
G_{0}(\zeta)
under
\Gamma
;
i.e., to the
leaf of
\mathcal{F}
containing
G_{0}(\zeta)
.
The same holds for
\omega
. So

d_{n}(z,w)
converges to
the hyperbolic distance between
the leaf containing
G_{0}(\zeta)

and that containing
G_{0}(\omega)
.

It follows that
d_{n}(z,w)\to 0
if and
only if
G_{0}(\zeta)
and
G_{0}(\omega)

belong to the same
\Gamma
-orbit.
On the other hand, two points on two
different

\Gamma
-orbits realise the hyperbolic
distance between these orbits
if and only if they belong to the
same leaf of
\mathcal{L}
(recall that
\mathcal{L}
is the foliation orthogonal to
\mathcal{F}
). So

d_{n}(z,w)
is eventually constant if
and only if
G_{0}(\zeta)
and
G_{0}(\omega)
belong
to the same leaf of
\mathcal{L}
. In all
other cases, the sequence converges
nontrivially to its non-zero limit. ∎

The existence of large annuli in
U_{n}
, for sufficiently large
n
, is a consequence of the following result.

Theorem 4.7

(Annuli)
.

Let
K\subset U_{0}
be compact and connected with non-empty interior, and not
contained in a leaf of
\mathcal{F}_{0}
. Then there is
n_{0}=n_{0}(K)\geq 0
such that,
for every
n\geq n_{0}
, all leaves of
\mathcal{F}_{n}
intersecting
f_{0}^{n}(K)
are simple closed curves. The union
A_{n}(K)\subset U_{n}
of all leaves of
\mathcal{F}_{n}
intersecting
f_{0}^{n}(K)
forms a closed annulus with
\Mod A_{n}(K)\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, and
f_{n}\colon A_{n}(K)\to A_{n+1}(K)
is a covering map for every
n\geq n_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
K\subset U_{0}
be as in the statement, and choose
R>\operatorname{diam}_{U_{0}}(K\cup\{\pi_{0}(0)\})>0
. Then
K\subset\pi_{0}(D_{R})
, where
D_{R}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=D_{\mathbb{D}}(0,R)
. By the Schwarz–Pick theorem,
f_{0}^{n}(K)\subset\pi_{n}(D_{R})
for all
k\geq 0
.

Since the limit group
\Gamma
is continuous, there is a sequence
(\gamma_{n})_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}
with
\gamma_{n}\in\Gamma_{n}\setminus\{\operatorname{id}\}
such that
\gamma_{n}\to\operatorname{id}
as
n\to\infty
. We may assume that
n_{0}
is chosen sufficiently large
to ensure that
\gamma_{n}(D_{R})\subset D_{R+1}
for
n\geq n_{0}
.
Set
\gamma_{n}^{*}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\Psi_{n}(\gamma_{n})
. Recall that

G_{n}\circ\gamma_{n}=\gamma_{n}^{*}\circ G_{n}
, and in particular

\gamma_{n}^{*}(0)=G_{n}(\gamma_{n}(0))
. Since

G_{n}\to\operatorname{id}
locally uniformly on
\mathbb{D}
, for sufficiently large
n
,

G_{n}
is injective on
D_{R+1}
.
In particular,
G_{n}(\gamma_{n}(0))\neq G_{n}(0)=0
for sufficiently large
n
, and
hence
\gamma_{n}^{*}\neq\operatorname{id}
.

Now let
n
be so large that, for every
z\in D_{R}
, the arc
\alpha^{*}(z)
of the leaf of
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}

connecting
G_{n}(z)
and
G_{n}(\gamma_{n}(z))=\gamma_{n}^{*}(G_{n}(z))
is
contained in
G_{n}(D_{R+1})
. Its preimage
\alpha(z)
under
G_{n}|_{D_{R+1}}
is an
analytic arc on a leaf of the foliation
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
connecting

z
and
\gamma_{n}(z)
. It follows that this leaf does not contain any singularity of the foliation, and that
the projection

\pi_{n}(\alpha(z))
is a simple closed curve in
U_{n}
(possibly covered several times by
\alpha(z)
), which is
a leaf of
\mathcal{F}_{n}
. By choice of
R
, this proves the first claim.

Since
K
is connected, the union
A_{n}(K)
of all the leaves of
\mathcal{F}_{n}
intersecting
f_{0}^{n}(K)
is connected. Since
K
is not contained in a single leaf, this union is a closed annulus bounded by two closed leaves of
\mathcal{F}_{n}
.
That the modulus of this annulus tends to infinity follows easily from the fact that
\gamma_{n}\to\operatorname{id}
. Moreover,
let
\hat{A}_{n}(K)
denote the union of all leaves of
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
that intersect
D_{R}\cap\pi_{n}^{-1}(A_{n}(K))
. It follows from the above that
\pi_{n}\colon\hat{A}_{n}(K)\to A_{n}(K)
is a universal covering, and that
g_{n}
maps

\hat{A}_{n}(K)
injectively onto
\hat{A}_{n+1}(K)
. This implies that
f_{n}\colon A_{n}(K)\to A_{n+1}(K)
is a finite-degree covering map of annuli.
∎

We now prove Theorems

and
1.9
.

and

.

First, to prove Theorem

, assume that

Now let us prove Theorem

.
To construct the annuli

such that

and such that

\mathcal{F}_{0}

We may choose a non-decreasing subsequence

∎

Remark
.

When the system

We conclude the section by discussing grand orbit classes. Let

essentially thick or essentially thin. Moreover, these grand orbits are always nowhere dense. As mentioned
in the introduction, neither of these facts is true for contracting systems.

Proposition 4.8

Suppose that

.
If

Remark
.

The equality (

Let

5.
Comparison with multiply connected domains of entire functions

In this section, we discuss our results in the context of the best understood multiply connected wandering domains, those of entire functions, while also generalising to other wandering domains with similar internal dynamics. Multiply connected wandering domains of entire functions were first constructed and studied by Baker. Already Töpfer
[
38
, p. 67]
observed that any multiply connected
Fatou component is necessarily escaping, while Baker
[
6
,
7
]
constructed the
first examples of transcendental entire functions with
multiply connected wandering domains. He also showed
[
8
]
*Theorem 3.1 that, for any
such wandering domain
U
, the Fatou component
U_{n}
containing
f^{n}(U_{n})
must
separate
U_{n-1}
from
\infty
for large
n
. In particular,
all
U_{n}
are bounded domains of the plane. Later, Kisaka and Shishikura
[
29
]
constructed the
first example of a doubly connected wandering domain and
showed that the eventual connectivity of a multiply connected wandering domain of a transcendental entire
function is always well-defined, and is either two or infinity.

In terms of the internal dynamics of these wandering domains, however, the greatest advances were made by Bergweiler, Rippon, and Stallard
[
15
]
. They showed that the dynamical behaviour of a multiply connected wandering domain
U
of an entire function
f
can be described using a dynamically defined sequence of harmonic functions
u_{n}\colon U_{n}\to(0,+\infty)
.
In
[
24
, Theorem 1.2]
, it was shown that the level sets of this harmonic function form
the leaves of a contracting foliation. We begin by noting that the contracting foliations constructed
in the previous section can also be described
a posteriori
as level sets of
harmonic functions.

Throughout this section,
F=(f_{n},U_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
is an essentially thin and non-contracting system (of which multiply connected wandering domains of entire functions are a special case). The notation and terminology are still the same as in Section
4
.

Theorem 5.1

(Harmonic functions for contracting foliations)
.

Let
F=(f_{n},U_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
be an essentially thin and non-contracting system of
holomorphic maps between hyperbolic Riemann surfaces.

Then there exists a sequence
u_{n}\colon U_{n}\to(0,\infty)
of positive harmonic functions such that
u_{n}=u_{n+1}\circ f_{n}
for all
n
, and such that the foliation of
U_{n}
by the level sets of
u_{n}
is the contracting foliation of
U_{n}
.

The function
u_{n}
is unique up to post-composition by a real-affine map
\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, and can be chosen such that its range is either
(0,1)
(in the hyperbolic case) or
(0,\infty)
(in the parabolic case).

Proof.

We use the results and notation from Section
4
. In particular, let

\mathcal{F}
be the foliation of
\mathbb{D}
by the limit group
\Gamma
. Recall that, for
n\geq 0
, the contracting foliation
\mathcal{F}_{n}
of
U_{n}
is the push-forward under the projection
\pi_{n}
of

\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n}=G_{n}^{-1}(\mathcal{F})
, where
G_{n}\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
are the functions
given by Lemma
4.1
.

Let
\varphi
be a conformal isomorphism from
\mathbb{D}
onto the upper half-plane
(in the parabolic case) or the strip
\{a+ib\colon b\in(0,1)\}
(in the hyperbolic case)
that takes the leaves of the foliation
\mathcal{F}
by orbits of the limit group

\Gamma
to horizontal lines. Define

\tilde{u}_{n}\colon\mathbb{D}\to(0,\infty);z\mapsto\operatorname{Im}(\varphi(G_{n}(z)))
. If

\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}
, then

\tilde{u}_{n}(\gamma(z))=\operatorname{Im}(\varphi(G_{n}(\gamma(z)))=\operatorname{Im}(\varphi(\Psi_{n}(\gamma)(G_{n}(z))).

Since
\Psi_{n}(\gamma)\in\Gamma
, the points
G_{n}(z)
and
\Psi_{n}(\gamma)(G_{n}(z))
belong to the
same leaf of
\mathcal{F}
, and hence

\tilde{u}_{n}(\gamma(z))=\operatorname{Im}(\varphi(G_{n}(z)))=\tilde{u}_{n}(z).

We conclude that

u_{n}\colon U_{n}\to(0,\infty);\quad u_{n}(\pi_{n}(z))\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\tilde{u}_{n}(z)

is a well-defined harmonic function on
U_{n}
with the desired properties.

Two harmonic functions
u
and
v
on a plane domain have the same family of level sets if and only if they differ by post-composition with a real-affine map. Indeed, near a regular point, we may write
v(z)=\alpha(u(z))
for a strictly monotone function

\alpha
. The chain rule, together with the fact that
u
and
v
are harmonic, implies that

\alpha^{\prime\prime}=0
. So
\alpha
is affine, and by the identity theorem
v(z)=\alpha(u(z))
on
U_{n}
. This proves
the final statement.
∎

In the case of a multiply connected wandering domain
U_{0}

of an entire function

.

Now let
F=(f_{n},U_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}

be a thin and non-contracting system as in the theorem; we continue to use the

.
In order to compare the construction from

(2)

(3)

(4)

.

(These can be achieved by applying

Let

as

. Define

Proposition 5.2

(Limit formula for

)
.

We have
h_{n}(z)\to u_{0}(z)/u_{0}(z_{0})-1

Sketch of proof.

Let
\gamma_{n}\in\Gamma_{n}
be an element minimising

Since

Which choice we take will be specified below.

If

Replacing

If

is parabolic

†

†

, we similarly let

Our normalisation above has assumed prior knowledge of the foliations
\mathcal{F}_{n}
and the functions
u_{n}
. It is not difficult to restate Proposition
5.2
in such a way as to avoid this; however, its
proof would still rely on existence of the foliation.
In contrast, in the entire case the existence of the foliation is obtained as a consequence of the convergence of the functions.

As mentioned in the introduction, the proof of convergence in the entire case relies on properties of the maximum modulus, particularly
Wiman–Valiron theory (which is available only for entire functions and certain meromorphic functions; see

[
14
]
). The ratio
b/a
mentioned above is
external
: it depends on the
way in which the domains
U_{n}
tend to infinity in the plane, rather than on their intrinsic geometry. Therefore no
analogue of this ratio exists in the general meromorphic setting.
However, whether this ratio is finite or infinite is an
internal
property: it distinguishes
whether the limit group
\Gamma
is hyperbolic or parabolic.

Another difference in the entire case is that each
U_{n}
has a distinguished
outer boundary component

\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{\out}U_{n}

\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}U_{n}
that separates

from

in

. The entire function must map
\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{\out}U_{n}
to
\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{\out}U_{n+1}

may take values close to

u_{n}
extends continuously to every

or

is (up to rescaling by a real-affine map) exactly given by the harmonic measure of

from

\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{\out}U_{n}
has zero harmonic measure
[

There is no distinguished outer boundary component in our more general setting. Nonetheless, some version of the
above results still hold for essentially thin non-contracting systems, if we impose the additional assumption
that the maps

are

; that is,

under

is compact. This condition is always satisfied for orbits of bounded wandering domains of transcendental meromorphic functions. In particular, it holds for multiply connected wandering domains of
entire functions.

We will use the notion of the set

.

The set of ends is non-empty if and only if

is non-compact.

is a compact connected topological (even metrisable)

is given by the domains

A proper map

Now let

Proposition 5.3

(Boundary components)

Let
F=(f_{n},U_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
be an essentially thin and non-contracting system of
holomorphic maps between hyperbolic Riemann surfaces, and assume that all maps

are proper.

Then, for every

, the sets
\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{-}U_{n}
and
\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{+}U_{n}

Proof.

The second claim follows from the fact that

is proper, together
with the functional

u_{n}=u_{n+1}\circ f_{n}

To prove the first statement, we observe that all regular leaves of the contracting

Next, we claim that, for every end

Let

Following

The harmonic measure of the boundary is
non-zero if and only if there exists
a Green’s function on
X

[
39
, Theorem X.1]
.
In the latter case
we say, following Tsuji
[
39
, Section X.1]
, that

X
has
positive boundary
. (Often, the term

non-polar boundary
is also used, particularly when

X\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.) This condition always holds for
our surfaces
U_{n}
.

Proposition 5.4

(Positive boundaries)
.

Let
F=(f_{n},U_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
be an essentially thin and non-contracting system of
holomorphic maps between hyperbolic Riemann surfaces.
Then every
U_{n}
has positive boundary.

In particular, if
n\geq 0
and

\pi_{n}\colon\mathbb{D}\to U_{n}
is a universal covering
map, then for almost every
\alpha\in\mathbb{D}
,
\pi_{n}(z)
tends to an end of

U_{n}
as
z\to\alpha
non-tangentially.

Proof.

Positive boundary, i.e., the existence of a Green’s
function on
X
, is equivalent
to
the existence of a non-constant positive harmonic function
on
X
;
see
[
39
, Theorem X.7]
. This holds for

U_{n}
by Theorem
5.1
.

The second statement follows from the first in two more steps. First, by
[
39
]
*XI.22, there exist full-measure subsets
E_{n}\subset\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}\mathbb{D}
such that, for
\zeta\in E_{n}
,
\pi_{n}(r\zeta)
converges to some end of
U_{n}
as
r\nearrow 1
. For universal coverings, having a radial limit at
\theta
is well-known to be equivalent to having a non-tangential limit. Indeed, points in a Stolz angle at
\zeta

are a bounded hyperbolic distance away from the
radial ray
\{r\zeta\colon r\in[0,1)\}
.
By the Schwarz–Pick lemma, the same is true for
their images under
\pi_{n}
. We refer the reader to
[
23
]
*Proposition 4.2 for details.
∎

Theorem 5.5

(Harmonic measure of boundary components)
.

Let
F=(f_{n},U_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
be an essentially thin and non-contracting system of holomorphic maps between hyperbolic Riemann surfaces. Additionally assume that all maps
f_{n}
are proper.

Then, for all
n\geq 0
and all
z\in U_{n}
,

(5.2)

\omega_{U_{n}}\left(z,\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{+}U_{n}\cup\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{-}U_{n}\right)=1.

Moreover, if the limit group
\Gamma
is hyperbolic, then

(5.3)

u_{n}(z)=\omega_{U_{n}}\left(z,\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{+}U_{n}\right)

for all
z\in U_{n}
. If the group is parabolic, then

(5.4)

\omega_{U_{n}}\left(z,\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{+}U_{n}\right)=0

for all
z\in U_{n}
.

Proof.

Set
b\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=1
in the hyperbolic case and
b\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\infty
in the parabolic case. Since the functions
f_{n}
are proper and each
U_{n}
has
positive boundary, the lifts
g_{n}\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
are inner functions;
that is, for almost every
\theta\in\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}\mathbb{D}
, the radial limit of
g_{n}

(which exists by Fatou’s theorem) is again on
\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}\mathbb{D}
.

Indeed, let
\theta\in\mathbb{D}
be such that the radial limit of
g_{n}
at
\theta

exists. By Proposition
5.4
, for almost every such
\theta
,

\pi_{n}(z)
tends to an end of
U_{n}
as
z\to\theta
non-tangentially
in
\mathbb{D}
. Since
f_{n}

is proper,
f_{n}(\pi_{n}(z))=\pi_{n+1}(g_{n}(z))
tends to an end of
U_{n+1}
. This means
that the radial limit of
g_{n}
at
\theta
must indeed lie on the unit circle. (Compare
[
22
]
*Proposition 2.10).

Consequently, by
[
25
]
*Theorem 1.1, the functions
G_{n}
given by Lemma
4.1
are also inner. It follows that the radial limit of
G_{n}
at almost every point
\theta\in\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}\mathbb{D}
exists, belongs to
\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}\mathbb{D}
, and is not a fixed point of the limit group

\Gamma
. In particular, by definition of the harmonic function
\tilde{u}_{n}
in the proof of Theorem
5.1
, the radial limit of
\tilde{u}_{n}
exists at almost every
\theta
and is equal to
0
or
b
. Moreover, in the parabolic case, this limit is equal to
0
almost everywhere.

By Proposition
5.4
, for almost every
\theta\in\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}\mathbb{D}
, the image under
\pi_{n}
of the radial line connecting
0
and
\theta
is a curve tending to an end of
U_{n}
.
This proves (
5.2
). In the parabolic case, (
5.4
) also follows.

In the hyperbolic case, let
C
be the subset of
\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}\mathbb{D}
bounded by the two fixed points of
\Gamma
such that
\tilde{u}_{n}(\zeta)\to 1
if and only if
G_{n}(\zeta)\to C
. Then
\tilde{u}_{n}(\zeta)=\omega_{\mathbb{D}}(G_{n}(\zeta),C)
for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
. Loewner’s lemma (see e.g.
[
18
]
*Corollary 1.5) implies that
G_{n}\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
and its boundary extension
G_{n}^{*}
(defined almost everywhere via radial limits) preserves harmonic measure. That is,

\tilde{u}_{n}(\zeta)=\omega_{\mathbb{D}}(G_{n}(\zeta),C)=\omega_{\mathbb{D}}(\zeta,(G_{n}^{*})^{-1}(C)).

As we saw above,
(G_{n}^{*})^{-1}(C)
and
(\pi_{n}^{*})^{-1}\left(\bigcup\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{+}U_{n}\right)
agree up to a set
of harmonic measure zero. Thus (
5.3
) follows.
∎

Remark
.

If the
U_{n}
are subdomains of the sphere, the notion of
harmonic measure of boundary components, described above,
coincides with the usual notion of harmonic measure on the
boundary of
U_{n}
.

In the entire case,
\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{+}U_{n}
contains only the outer boundary component
\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{\out}U_{n}
, while all other boundary components are in
\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{-}U_{n}
. In the meromorphic case, both
\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{+}U_{n}
and
\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{-}U_{n}
may consist of several boundary components. Moreover, it is plausible that there are examples
in which some ends of
U_{n}
do not belong to either

\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{+}U_{n}
or
\mathinner{\partial}\mathchoice{\mskip-3.0mu}{\mskip-3.0mu}{}{}{}_{-}U_{n}
. For such an end
e
, the function
u
would oscillate between values close to
0
and values close to
b\in\{1,\infty\}
along any curve approaching
e
.

6.
Eventual connectivity: The proof of Theorem
1.5

First, we restate Theorem
1.5
for non-autonomous systems.

Theorem 6.1

(Internal dynamics of non-autonomous systems on hyperbolic surfaces)
.

Let
F=(f_{n},U_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a non-autonomous
system of holomorphic maps between hyperbolic Riemann surfaces. Then
one of the following holds.

(1)

F
is globally contracting.

(2)

F
is essentially thick and globally semi-contracting. Either

\delta_{n}(z)\to\infty
for all
z\in U_{0}
, or for all
z\in U_{0}
,
\delta_{n}(z)
is eventually non-decreasing and tends to
a positive finite value as
n\to\infty
.

(3)

F
is essentially thin, infinitesimally semi-contracting and bimodal. Furthermore,

\delta_{n}(z)\to 0
for all
z\in U_{0}
.

(4)

F
is essentially thick and globally eventually isometric. In particular, there is

n_{0}
such that
\delta_{n}(z)=\delta_{n_{0}}(z)
for all
n\geq n_{0}
and all
z\in U_{0}
, and

F
has
finite or infinite eventual connectivity.

(5)

F
is essentially thick and locally, but not globally, eventually isometric.
The sequence
\delta_{n}(z)
is eventually constant for every
z\in U_{0}
, and
F
has eventual connectivity
\infty
.

(6)

F
is essentially thin, infinitesimally eventually isometric and trimodal.
F
has eventual connectivity
2
and
\delta_{n}(z)\to 0
for all
z\in U
.

Proof.

Most of the above follows from what we have already proved, except the essentially thick and non-contracting cases.

We start by taking a preferred lift

G=(\mathbb{D},g_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
of
F
via
universal covering maps
(\pi_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
. Then, we claim that for any compact subset
K
of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
there exists
N
such that the group homomorphisms
\psi_{n}\colon\Gamma_{n}\to\Gamma_{n+1}
given by Lemma
4.3
yield bijections between
\Gamma_{n}\cap K
and
\Gamma_{n+1}\cap K
whenever
n\geq N
. Indeed, if that were not the case, then we would have
\gamma_{n_{k}}\in\Gamma_{n_{k}}\cap K
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
\gamma_{n_{k}}\notin\psi_{n_{k}-1}(\Gamma_{n_{k}-1})
. The sequence
\Psi_{n_{k}}(\gamma_{n_{k}})
is now an infinite sequence of distinct elements of the discrete limit group
\Gamma
, all compactly contained in
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
(in a compact set possibly larger than
K
), which is a contradiction. Combining this observation with Lemma
4.1
, we see that for all
r>0
there exists
N
such that, for all
n\geq N
, the function
g_{n}
is injective in
D_{\mathbb{D}}(0,r)

and
there exists a well-defined bijection between
\Gamma_{n}(z)\cap D_{\mathbb{D}}(0,r)
and
\Gamma_{n+1}(z)\cap D_{\mathbb{D}}(0,r)
. Consequently, the commuting diagram
\pi_{n+1}\circ g_{n}=f_{n}\circ\pi_{n}
now implies that
f_{n}|_{|\pi_{n}(D_{\mathbb{D}}(0,r))}
is injective for
n\geq N
. This means that, when restricted to compact sets, the system
F
inherits its behaviour from the system
G
: if
G
(and thus
F
) is infinitesimally semi-contracting (resp. eventually isometric), then
F
is semi-contracting (resp. eventually isometric) on compact subsets. Being semi-contracting on compact subsets is the same as being globally semi-contracting, and we are left to prove the claims about the sequence
\delta_{n}
and infinite connectivity in the infinitesimally eventually isometric case. In this case,
\Gamma_{n}
injects into the (discrete) limit group
\Gamma
for all sufficiently large
n
. If
\Gamma_{n}=\Gamma
for some and hence all sufficiently large
n
, the system
F
is globally eventually isometric, and the stated claims easily follow. Otherwise,
\delta_{n}(z)
is eventually constant
for every
z\in U_{0}
by the characterisation of the injectivity radius

\delta_{n}=\inj_{U_{n}}(z_{n})
in terms of
\Gamma_{n}
. That all sufficiently large
U_{n}
must
be infinitely connected follows from the following topological fact. If
f\colon U\to V
is a covering map and
V
is finitely connected, then one of the following hold:

•

f
is a conformal isomorphism;

•

U
is simply connected and
f
is a universal covering map;

•

U
and
V
are both doubly connected, and
f
is a finite-degree covering map;

•

U
has greater connectivity than
V
.

If
f
has finite degree, this is a consequence of the Riemann–Hurwitz formula.
It remains to consider the case where
f
has infinite degree,
V
has connectivity at least
3
, and

U
is not simply connected.

In this case, there is some homotopically non-trivial closed curve
\gamma
in
V
(not necessarily primitive in
\pi_{1}(V)
) that lifts to a loop in
U
(since
U
is not simply connected and
f
provides an isomorphism from
\pi_{1}(U)
to a subgroup of
\pi_{1}(V)
). Now, either
f^{-1}(\gamma)
consists of infinitely many disjoint non-trivial loops in
U
, each mapped finitely many times onto
\gamma
, or there exists at least one component
\tilde{\gamma}
of
f^{-1}(\gamma)
that is not a loop, not compactly contained in
U
, and which maps onto
\gamma
with infinite degree. If it is the former, we are done –
U
is already infinitely connected. If it is the latter, we consider another non-trivial loop
\sigma\subset V
, with
\sigma\cap\gamma=\{p\}
and
\sigma
and
\gamma
not homotopic to each other. Consider a lift
\tilde{\sigma}
of
\sigma
; it is a curve that starts and ends at points in
f^{-1}(p)
, of which (by hypothesis) only finitely many lie outside of
\tilde{\gamma}
. Since
f
has infinite degree, this means that there are infinitely many instances of
f^{-1}(\sigma)
intersecting
\tilde{\gamma}
. No such instance can be trivial or homotopic to
\tilde{\gamma}
in
U
, for that would imply that
\sigma
is trivial in
V
or that
\sigma
and
\gamma
are homotopic. Therefore, again,
U
is infinitely connected.
∎

Before finally proving Theorem
1.5
, we recall the following fact about connectivities of wandering domains (see
[
40
]
*p. 219).

Lemma 6.2

(Connectivities of Fatou components)
.

Let
f
be a transcendental meromorphic function with a wandering domain
U
. Then, one of the following holds:

(1)

All
U_{n}
,
n\geq 0
, are infinitely connected;

(2)

There exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\geq 3
, such that
U
has eventual connectivity
k
;

(3)

For all large
k
,
c(U_{n})\in\{1,2\}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

We must prove the remaining statements on eventual connectivities. Let
U
be a wandering domain of a transcendental meromorphic function. By
[
24
, Theorem 1.1]
, if
U
is infinitesimally contracting or semi-contracting, then
U
cannot have a finite eventual connectivity greater than
1
. Hence, in view of Theorem
1.8
, together with Lemma
6.2
, to prove Theorem
1.5
, it only remains to show the following.

(1)

If
U
is locally, but not globally, eventually isometric, then it is infinitely connected.

(2)

If
U
is bimodal, then it is infinitely connected.

For
1
, notice that if
U_{n}
is finitely connected and infinitesimally eventually isometric, only the following possibilities can occur:

(1)

U_{n}
is simply connected,
f\colon U_{n}\to U_{n+1}
is a universal covering, and
c(U_{n+1})\geq 1
. If equality holds,
f
has degree one in
U_{n}
;

(2)

1<c(U_{n})<\infty
, and
c(U_{n+1})\leq c(U_{n})
. Indeed, this is an easy consequence of
[
16
]
*Theorem 3.

If case (ii) holds for all sufficiently large
n
, then
U
must have an eventual connectivity
k\geq 2
, whence it is globally eventually isometric by
[
24
]
*Theorem 1.1. On the other hand, if
c(U_{n})=1
for all large
n
, then
U
must once again be globally eventually isometric. Thus,
U
must either be infinitely connected, or have no eventual connectivity – which, by Lemma
6.2
, means having connectivity alternating between one and two. However, if
U
is infinitesimally eventually isometric, then
f
is a covering map from
U_{n}
to
U_{n+1}
for large
n
. This is impossible if
U_{n}
is doubly
connected and
U_{n+1}
is simply connected (since otherwise

f
would induce
an isomorphism from
\pi_{1}(U_{n})
to a subgroup of the trivial group
\pi_{1}(U_{n+1})
.)

For
2
, notice that, by Theorem
1.9
,
U_{n}
is already at least doubly connected for all sufficiently large
n
. By Lemma
6.2
and
[
24
]
*Theorem 1.1,
U
and all its iterates must be infinitely connected.
∎

Examples

Let us first consider the case of entire functions. Examples of simply connected globally contracting, globally semi-contracting and globally eventually isometric wandering domains are discussed in

]
. An entire function having a wandering domain of eventual connectivity

was first constructed by Baker
[
6
]
. A wandering domain with eventual connectivity

was constructed by Kisaka and Shishikura
[

]
. This covers all cases of Theorem
1.5
that can be realised by entire functions, and in particular gives examples of cases
3
and
6
of Theorem
1.5
.

Globally eventually isometric wandering domains of any eventual connectivity

are constructed in
[
5
]
; their existence also follows from Theorem

or
[
30

.

Examples of essentially thick globally contracting or semi-contracting wandering domains with eventual connectivity

are given in
[

and
[

We believe that the construction can be modified

3
).

It remains to prove Theorem
1.6
. To do so, we construct a suitable model map to which we can apply Theorem

.

Proposition 7.1

(A locally but not globally eventually isometric model)
.

There exist sequences
(b_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}

of degree
2
Blaschke products
\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}

and
(U_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
of subdomains of
\mathbb{D}
with
0\in U_{n}
for all
n\geq 0
such that:

(1)

b_{n}|_{U_{n}}\colon U_{n}\to U_{n+1}
is a degree two covering map and
b_{n}(0)=0
for all
n\geq 0
;

(2)

there is an increasing sequence
(r_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
with
r_{n}\nearrow 1

such that
b_{n}|_{D(0,r_{n})}
is injective, for all
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

For
0<a<1
, define

b_{a}(z)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=z\cdot\frac{z+a}{1+az}.

The maps
b_{n}
in the proposition will be of the form
b_{n}=b_{a_{n}}
, where

a_{n}\nearrow 1
is a suitable sequence of positive numbers.

The functions
b_{a}\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
have degree two, fix the origin,
and have a single critical point at

c_{a}=\frac{-1+\sqrt{1-a^{2}}}{a}.

The critical point approaches
-1
as
a\to 1
, as does its value
v_{a}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=b_{a}(c_{a})
.
Clearly
b_{a}
converges to the identity as
a\to 1
; in particular,
for any
r<1
,
b_{a}
is injective on
D(0,r)
for
a
sufficiently close to
1
. In fact, it is easy to see that
b_{a}
is injective on

D(0,\lvert c_{a}\rvert)
.

We now construct the numbers
a_{n}
inductively. As part of the construction, we also define the increasing sequence
r_{n}
, as well as compact subsets
A_{k}^{n}\subset\mathbb{D}
, for
0\leq k\leq n+1
, for
n\geq 0
, each of which is a finite union of topological discs. These are chosen to have the following properties for all
n\geq 0
.

(1)

b_{n}
is injective on
D(0,r_{n})
.

(2)

0\notin A_{k}^{n}
for all
k\leq n+1
and

c_{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=c_{a_{n}}\in A_{k}^{n}
for all
k\leq n
.

(3)

b_{k}^{-1}(A_{k+1}^{n})=A_{k}^{n}
for
0\leq k\leq n
.

(4)

If
n\geq 1
, every connected component of
A_{k}^{n-1}
is also
a connected component of
A_{k}^{n}
and

(A_{k}^{n+1}\setminus A_{k}^{n})\cap D(0,r_{n})=\emptyset

for
0\leq k\leq n
.

Suppose that
m\geq 0
is such, for all
n<m
, the numbers
a_{n}
and sets
A_{k}^{n}
have been defined with the above properties.
(This inductive hypothesis is empty
for
m=0
.) If
m=0
, set
r_{0}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=1/2
; otherwise,
choose
r_{m}\in(r_{m-1},1)
such that
r_{m}>1-1/(m+1)
and

A_{m}^{m-1}\subset D(0,r_{m})
. Then choose
a_{m}
close enough to
1
to ensure
that
c_{m}<-r_{m}
. With this choice,
b_{m}
is injective on
D(0,r_{m})
,
so
1
holds for
n=m
.

Since
c_{m}\notin D(0,r_{m})
, we have
v_{m}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=b_{m}(c_{m})\notin b_{m}(D(0,r_{m}))
(note that
b_{m}^{-1}(v_{m})=\{c_{m}\}
). Choose
\varepsilon>0
such that
D(v_{m},\varepsilon)\cap b_{m}(D(0,r_{m}))=\emptyset
. Define

(7.1)

A_{m+1}^{m}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\begin{cases}\overline{D(v_{m},\varepsilon)}\cup b_{m}(A_{m}^{m-1})&\text{if }m>0\\
\overline{D(v_{m},\varepsilon)}&\text{if }m=0\end{cases}

and define
A_{k}^{m}
recursively according to
3
for

k=m,m-1,\dots,0
.

Let us consider the connected components of
A_{m}^{m}=g_{m}^{-1}(A_{m+1}^{m})
.One such component is given by

, which is disjoint from

by our choice of

. Every other component of

is a connected component of

, where

is a connected component of

. Since

is injective on

, there are two such components:

itself, and a second component

. This establishes

for

. For

follows inductively from

and the Schwarz lemma.

Furthermore, since
b_{m}
is injective on

and

by the inductive hypothesis, we have

by (

).
By

, we conclude inductively that

for

(recall that all the functions

fix

, and by the inductive hypothesis and

for

For

, we define

U_{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathbb{D}\setminus\bigcup_{k=n}^{\infty}A_{n}^{k}.

Observe that, by
4
,
U_{n}
is a subdomain of
\mathbb{D}
, and furthermore
0\in U_{n}
,
c_{n}\notin U_{n}
, and
b_{n}^{-1}(U_{n+1})=U_{n}
. It follows that
b_{n}\colon U_{n}\to U_{n+1}
is indeed a degree two covering map. The proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Fix the sequences
(b_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
and
(U_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
from Proposition
7.1
and define

T_{n}(z)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=z+4n,\quad X_{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\overline{T_{n}(U_{n})}\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi(z)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=T_{n+1}(b_{n}((T_{n})^{-1}(z)))\ \text{for $z\in X_{n}$}.

Then
\varphi
is holomorphic on a neighbourhood of
X\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\bigcup_{j=0}^{\infty}X_{n}
.
By Theorem
2.1
, there exists a meromorphic function
f\colon\mathbb{C}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}

and a diffeomorphism
\theta\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
that is conformal on
\operatorname{int}(X)

such that

V_{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\theta(T_{n}(U_{n}))
is a
Fatou component of
f
for every
n
and

\theta\circ g=f\circ\theta
on
X
.
It follows that
f\colon V_{n}\to\widetilde{V}_{n+1}
is an unbranched covering of degree two for all
n\geq 0
. In particular,
(V_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
is an orbit
of infinitesimally isometric wandering domains that is not globally eventually isometric.
Since the
V_{n}
are infinitely connected, it follows from Theorem
1.5
that
this orbit of wandering domains is locally eventually isometric.
∎

Remark
.

It is also not difficult to show the final claim in the proof
(that the orbit of wandering domains is locally eventually isometric) directly, without referring to Theorem
1.5
.

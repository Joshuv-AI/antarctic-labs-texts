On the bilinear estimate of Ozawa and Rogers for the one-dimensional Klein-Gordon equation and some related lower Jacobian estimates
Shirong Chen, Yi C. Huang, Shaozhen Xu

Abstract
. We give a natural convexity proof of an elementary inequality used by Ozawa and Rogers in proving their bilinear estimate for the one-dimensional Klein-Gordon equation. This robust approach also enables us to derive the optimality of Ozawa-Rogers estimate and establish a new bilinear estimate. Our estimate is in sharp analogy with the bilinear estimate of Bez and Rogers for the wave equation. We also present an alternative proof of some lower Jacobian estimates used crucially by Ozawa and Rogers in proving their Fourier restriction results on the whole hyperbola. Based on interpolation, our proof is purely non-trigonometric.

2.
A new proof of Lemma
1.2

Fix
\xi_{1}
and consider the function

f(x)=\left(\xi_{1}^{2}+1\right)^{3/4}x\left(x^{2}+1\right)^{1/4}-\xi_{1}\left(\xi_{1}^{2}+1\right)^{1/4}\left(x^{2}+1\right)^{3/4}-(x-\xi_{1}),

thus
f(\xi_{1})=0
. Computing the derivative we have

f^{\prime}(x)=\left(\xi_{1}^{2}+1\right)^{3/4}\left(x^{2}+1\right)^{1/4}+\frac{\left(\xi_{1}^{2}+1\right)^{3/4}x^{2}}{2\left(x^{2}+1\right)^{3/4}}-\frac{3\xi_{1}\left(\xi_{1}^{2}+1\right)^{1/4}x}{2\left(x^{2}+1\right)^{1/4}}-1,

thus
f^{\prime}(\xi_{1})=0
. Computing the second-order derivative we have

f^{\prime\prime}(x)=\frac{3\left(\xi_{1}^{2}+1\right)^{1/4}\left(x^{2}+2\right)\left(\sqrt{\xi_{1}^{2}+1}x-\xi_{1}\sqrt{x^{2}+1}\right)}{4\left(x^{2}+1\right)^{7/4}}.

Now let
g(x)=\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}}
, we see that
g^{\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{(1+x^{2})^{3/2}}\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
Hence

\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}}\geq\frac{\xi_{1}}{\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}}},\qquad x\geq\xi_{1}.

Thus for all
x\geq\xi_{1}
, we have
f^{\prime\prime}(x)\geq 0
, thereby

f^{\prime}(x)\geq f^{\prime}(\xi_{1})=0,

and furthermore,

f(\xi_{2})\geq f(\xi_{1})=0,\qquad\xi_{2}\geq\xi_{1}.

This proves Lemma
1.2
.

If we replace the exponent

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(x)=

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

The proof of Theorem
1.4
uses the elegant machinery of Ozawa and Rogers
[
OR14
]
.
Since we can not do better, for completeness we have to “reload their arguments”.

The Ozawa-Rogers machinery starts with writing

\displaystyle e^{it\sqrt{1-\Delta}}

\displaystyle f_{1}(x)e^{it\sqrt{1-\Delta}}f_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi_{1})\widehat{f_{2}}(\xi_{2})e^{ix(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})+it\left(\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}}+\sqrt{1+\xi_{2}^{2}}\right)}d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}F(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})e^{ix(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})+it\left(\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}}+\sqrt{1+\xi_{2}^{2}}\right)}d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2},

where

F(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi_{1})\widehat{f_{2}}(\xi_{2})+\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi_{2})\widehat{f_{2}}(\xi_{1})\right).

By symmetry, we obtain

\displaystyle e^{it\sqrt{1-\Delta}}

\displaystyle f_{1}(x)e^{it\sqrt{1-\Delta}}f_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\xi_{2}\geq\xi_{1}}F(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})e^{ix(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})+it\left(\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}}+\sqrt{1+\xi_{2}^{2}}\right)}d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\xi_{2}\geq\xi_{1}}F(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})e^{ix\eta_{1}+it\eta_{2}}\frac{1}{|J(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|}d\eta_{1}d\eta_{2},

where we used the following change of variables

\eta_{1}=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\eta_{2}=\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}}+\sqrt{1+\xi_{2}^{2}},

with the Jacobian
J
given by

J(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\left|\begin{matrix}\frac{\partial\eta_{1}}{\partial\xi_{1}}&\frac{\partial\eta_{1}}{\partial\xi_{2}}\\
\frac{\partial\eta_{2}}{\partial\xi_{1}}&\frac{\partial\eta_{2}}{\partial\xi_{2}}\\
\end{matrix}\right|=\frac{\xi_{2}}{\sqrt{1+\xi_{2}^{2}}}-\frac{\xi_{1}}{\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}}},\quad\xi_{2}\geq\xi_{1}.

By Plancherel’s theorem and reversing the change of variables, we have

\displaystyle\left\|e^{it\sqrt{1-\Delta}}f_{1}e^{it\sqrt{1-\Delta}}f_{2}\right\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{1+1})}^{2}

(4.1)

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\xi_{2}\geq\xi_{1}}|F(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|^{2}\frac{1}{|J(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|^{2}}d\eta_{1}d\eta_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\xi_{2}\geq\xi_{1}}|F(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|^{2}\frac{1}{|J(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|}d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}.

At this point we need the following elementary inequality.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
\xi_{2}\geq\xi_{1}
,

\frac{\xi_{2}}{\sqrt{1+\xi_{2}^{2}}}-\frac{\xi_{1}}{\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}}}\geq 1-\frac{(1,\xi_{1})\cdot(1,\xi_{2})}{\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}}\sqrt{1+\xi_{2}^{2}}}.

(4.2)

Proof of (
4.2
) is deferred to next section.
Substituting (
4.2
) into (
4.1
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|e^{it\sqrt{1-\Delta}}f_{1}e^{it\sqrt{1-\Delta}}f_{2}\right\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{1+1})}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{4}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\xi_{2}\geq\xi_{1}}|F(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|^{2}\left(1-\frac{(1,\xi_{1})\cdot(1,\xi_{2})}{\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}}\sqrt{1+\xi_{2}^{2}}}\right)^{-1}d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{2}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|F(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|^{2}\left(1-\frac{(1,\xi_{1})\cdot(1,\xi_{2})}{\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}}\sqrt{1+\xi_{2}^{2}}}\right)^{-1}d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}.

Since
\text{\rm supp}\,(\widehat{f_{1}})\cap\text{\rm supp}\,(\widehat{f_{2}})=\emptyset
, we have

|F(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\left(|\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi_{1})|^{2}|\widehat{f_{2}}(\xi_{2})|^{2}+|\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi_{2})|^{2}|\widehat{f_{2}}(\xi_{1})|^{2}\right),

therefore

\displaystyle\left\|e^{it\sqrt{1-\Delta}}f_{1}e^{it\sqrt{1-\Delta}}f_{2}\right\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{1+1})}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{1}{2(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(|\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi_{1})|^{2}|\widehat{f_{2}}(\xi_{2})|^{2}+|\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi_{2})|^{2}|\widehat{f_{2}}(\xi_{1})|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left(1-\frac{(1,\xi_{1})\cdot(1,\xi_{2})}{\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}}\sqrt{1+\xi_{2}^{2}}}\right)^{-1}d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi_{1})|^{2}|\widehat{f_{2}}(\xi_{2})|^{2}\left(1-\frac{(1,\xi_{1})\cdot(1,\xi_{2})}{\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}}\sqrt{1+\xi_{2}^{2}}}\right)^{-1}d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}.

Theorem
1.4
is proved.

6.
On the Jacobian estimates of Ozawa and Rogers

For
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{*}={\mathbb{R}}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
, define

\chi(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\frac{|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|}{\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}}\sqrt{1+\xi_{2}^{2}}}

and

J(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\left|\frac{\xi_{1}}{\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}}}-\frac{\xi_{2}}{\sqrt{1+\xi_{2}^{2}}}\right|.

Here
\chi
is the chordal distance (on the extended complex plane
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
),
and it is used for example in defining the spherical derivative of meromorphic functions (see Zalcman
[
Zal98
]
).
As before,
J
is the Jacobian determinant for the change of variables

\eta_{1}=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\eta_{2}=\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}}+\sqrt{1+\xi_{2}^{2}}.

We have the following straightforward upper bounds:
\forall\,\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
,

\chi(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\leq 1\quad\text{and}\quad J(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\leq 2.

Following Ozawa and Rogers
[
OR14
]
we introduce

Definition 6.1
.

For
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
and
\alpha\in[1,2]
, let

\sigma_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\frac{|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{\alpha}}{(1+\xi_{1}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{4}}(1+\xi_{2}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{4}}}.

Note that
\sigma_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=0
when
\xi_{1}
agrees with
\xi_{2}
.
For our purpose below,
we also impose the constraint
\alpha\geq 1
on
\sigma_{\alpha}
(namely, continuity of order
\geq 1
).
As
\alpha\leq 2
,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})

\displaystyle=[\chi(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})]^{\alpha}\frac{1}{(1+\xi_{1}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}}(1+\xi_{2}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}}}

\displaystyle\leq[\chi(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})]^{\alpha}\leq 1,

and in particular,

\sigma_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=[\chi(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})]^{2}\leq 1.

Thus
\{\sigma_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in[1,2]}
can be interpreted as a scale of deformed chordal distances.

The following natural extension of Lemma
1.2
, which bound
J
from below with
\sigma_{\alpha}
,
was established by Ozawa and Rogers
[
OR14
]
, again via ‘‘an unlikely combination of five trigonometric identities”.
These inequalities are crucial, and actually critical

In their machinery, the power
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{4}

in proving their Fourier restriction results on the whole hyperbola.

For all

Proof.

We observe that the following interpolation (or, factorization) relation holds

\sigma_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=[\sigma_{1}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})]^{2-\alpha}[\sigma_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})]^{\alpha-1}.

Thus it suffices to prove Lemma
6.2
for
\alpha\in\{1,2\}
.

Note that the case
\xi_{1}=\xi_{2}
is trivial. The case
\xi_{1}=-\xi_{2}
is also simple as

\displaystyle\sigma_{\alpha}(-\xi_{2},\xi_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\alpha}|\xi_{2}|^{\alpha}}{(1+\xi_{2}^{2})^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\alpha-1}\frac{2|\xi_{2}|}{\sqrt{1+\xi_{2}^{2}}}=2^{\alpha-1}J(-\xi_{2},\xi_{2}).

In above inequality, we used
1+\xi_{2}^{2}\geq 1
,
\alpha\geq 1
, and
|\xi_{2}|/\sqrt{1+\xi_{2}^{2}}\leq 1
.

Now, let us first consider
\alpha=2
.
For
\xi_{1}\neq\pm\xi_{2}
, we start with reformulating
J
as

\displaystyle J(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\xi_{1}\sqrt{1+\xi_{2}^{2}}-\xi_{2}\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}}}{\sqrt{(1+\xi_{1}^{2})(1+\xi_{2}^{2})}}\right|

(6.2)

\displaystyle=\frac{|\xi_{1}^{2}-\xi_{2}^{2}|}{\left|\xi_{1}\sqrt{1+\xi_{2}^{2}}+\xi_{2}\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}}\right|\sqrt{(1+\xi_{1}^{2})(1+\xi_{2}^{2})}}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}(\xi_{1},

\displaystyle\xi_{2})[J(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|}{|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|}\frac{\left|\xi_{1}\sqrt{1+\xi_{2}^{2}}+\xi_{2}\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}}\right|}{\sqrt{(1+\xi_{1}^{2})(1+\xi_{2}^{2})}}.

Case 1
. If
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
have same sign, then

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}(\xi_{1},

\displaystyle\xi_{2})[J(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})]^{-1}

(6.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|}{|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|}\left|\frac{\xi_{1}}{\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}}}+\frac{\xi_{2}}{\sqrt{1+\xi_{2}^{2}}}\right|\leq 2.

Case 2
. If
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
have opposite sign, then

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}(\xi_{1},

\displaystyle\xi_{2})[J(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left|\xi_{1}\sqrt{1+\xi_{2}^{2}}+\xi_{2}\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}}\right|}{|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|}\frac{|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|}{\sqrt{(1+\xi_{1}^{2})(1+\xi_{2}^{2})}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|}{\left|\xi_{1}\sqrt{1+\xi_{2}^{2}}-\xi_{2}\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}}\right|}\frac{|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|}{\sqrt{(1+\xi_{1}^{2})(1+\xi_{2}^{2})}}\leq 1.

In the last step we used
\chi(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\leq 1
and the following elementary inequality

\frac{|A+B|}{\left|A\sqrt{1+B^{2}}+B\sqrt{1+A^{2}}\right|}\leq 1,\quad A\geq 0,\,B\geq 0.

Next, we consider
\alpha=1
.
Using the reformulation (
6.2
), it suffices to prove

\left|\xi_{1}\frac{(1+\xi_{2}^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}{(1+\xi_{1}^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}+\xi_{2}\frac{(1+\xi_{1}^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}{(1+\xi_{2}^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}\right|\leq|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|.

(6.4)

Without loss of generality we can assume
|\xi_{2}|\geq|\xi_{1}|
. By setting

s=\xi_{1},\quad t=\xi_{2},\quad\beta=\frac{(1+\xi_{2}^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}{(1+\xi_{1}^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}},

(
6.4
) becomes

s^{2}\beta^{2}+\frac{t^{2}}{\beta^{2}}\leq s^{2}+t^{2},

which is further equivalent to

(s^{2}\beta^{2}-t^{2})(\beta^{2}-1)\leq 0.

The last inequality is valid since

1\leq\beta^{2}\leq\frac{|\xi_{2}|}{|\xi_{1}|}\leq\frac{\xi_{2}^{2}}{\xi_{1}^{2}}=\frac{t^{2}}{s^{2}},

the second estimate involved being the monotonicity of
\xi/\sqrt{1+\xi^{2}}
.

This proves Lemma

(the constant

Linear Operator Approximate Message Passing (OpAMP)
Riccardo Rossetti, Bobak Nazer, Galen Reeves

Abstract
This paper introduces a framework for approximate message passing (AMP) in dynamic settings where the data at each iteration is passed through a linear operator. This framework is motivated in part by applications in large-scale, distributed computing where only a subset of the data is available at each iteration. An autoregressive memory term is used to mitigate information loss across iterations and a specialized algorithm, called projection AMP, is designed for the case where each linear operator is an orthogonal projection. Precise theoretical guarantees are provided for a class of Gaussian matrices and non-separable denoising functions. Specifically, it is shown that the iterates can be well-approximated in the high-dimensional limit by a Gaussian process whose second-order statistics are defined recursively via state evolution. These results are applied to the problem of estimating a rank-one spike corrupted by additive Gaussian noise using partial row updates, and the theory is validated by numerical simulations.

Introduction

Approximate message passing (AMP) refers to a family of iterative algorithms that has been applied to high-dimensional inference problems including regression, matrix estimation, and channel coding; for a comprehensive reference, see the recent tutorial
[
1

represents the collection of iterates up to time
t-1

One of the key features of the AMP framework is that the behavior can be tracked precisely in high-dimension settings provided that the data matrix satisfies certain distributional assumptions. In these settings, the coefficients
b_{ts}
can be specified in a way that both accelerates the overall convergence and guarantees that the process
\{x_{t}\}
can be closely approximated by a Gaussian process
\{y_{t}\}
whose mean and covariance can be efficiently computed via a recursion called state evolution (SE).
Over the last decade, the combination of AMP-based analysis with information-theoretic arguments has provided powerful new insights into the statistical-computational tradeoffs for wide variety of problems.

An important consideration for very large matrix operations is that the underlying computations are usually distributed across multiple servers. For instance, consider a scenario where each server is given a subset of the rows of the data matrix, and tasked to return the inner products of its rows with the current estimate. In practice, the server response times can have a long tail
[
2
]
, and waiting for the “stragglers” can significantly delay the next iteration. This delay can be mitigated via replication or coded computation
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
to ensure that the full matrix multiplication is available once enough servers respond, at the cost of additional computation per iteration to maintain a suitable erasure-correcting code.

However, for high-dimensional inference tasks, this level of redundancy may be too conservative, and it may be more efficient to proceed to the next iterate. For example, a simple approach is to update the coordinates corresponding to responding servers, while leaving those corresponding to straggling servers unchanged. Intuitively, after applying a well-chosen denoising function, the quality of the estimate should improve, despite the missing computations. One of the main contributions of this paper is an AMP framework that naturally captures this scenario, enabling a precise performance characterization via the associated state evolution. In fact, we provide theoretical guarantees for a much broader class of problems, where the matrix multiplication at each iteration is the result of passing the data matrix through a time-varying linear operator.

From another perspective, the goal is for the iterates to converge
efficiently
, e.g., using the fewest possible multiplications by the high-dimensional data matrix. An interesting by-product of our framework is that we can analyze the performance of AMP for carefully-scheduled computations. For instance, consider the following “round-robin” AMP algorithm: the rows of the data matrix are partitioned into
J
equally-sized subsets, and the
t
-th iteration only updates the coordinates corresponding to subset
t\bmod J
. As we demonstrate below in Sections
1.2
and
3.4
, for spiked matrix estimation, this update schedule can be more efficient than multiplying by the full matrix at each iteration.

Consider a scenario where the rows of the data matrix
M
are split up among multiple servers, which are tasked to return the inner products of their rows with the current estimate

. As motivated above, only a subset of the servers respond by the deadline. Alternatively, consider a scenario where only a subset of the rows are scheduled for each iteration, to reduce the computational load. Both of these scenarios can be modeled using (

\Pi_{t}^{\perp}=\mathrm{I}-\Pi_{t}
is used to retain signal memory via the term
\Pi_{t}^{\perp}x_{t-1}
.

Our formulation and results enables us to precisely characterize the effective signal-to-noise ratio at each iteration. As discussed below in Section
1.3
, various special cases of our framework have been previously studied in the literature, but, to the best of the authors’ understanding, the general framework for linear operators has not been considered directly. Based on the results of our simulations (Section
3.4
), we suggest that our projection method is potentially useful in accelerating convergence of standard AMP recursions, even in non-distributed settings, under certain models for the computational cost per iteration.

Asymptotic Gaussianity.

Beyond the algorithmic developments, a further contribution of this paper is the framework we use to describe the Gaussian approximation to the AMP iterates. Following the seminal work in
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
, most of the literature on AMP restricts to settings where the functions
f_{t}
are separable (i.e., the
i
-th output depends only on the
i
-th input) and then provides convergence guarantees with respect to the empirical measure of the iterates over a finite number of iterations; see
[
1
]
for more details.

In this paper, we build on the general framework for non-separable functions
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
, where (pseudo)-Lipschitz continuity is the only assumption placed on the function sequence and convergence is assessed in terms a of sequence of suitably normalized pseudo-Lipschitz test functions. This mode of convergence does not imply convergence in distribution for the underlying iterates because the space on which these test functions are applied is scaling with the problem size. Theorem
7
in the Appendix provides a dual interpretation for this mode of convergence, that is based on the existence of a coupling between the AMP iterates
\{x_{t}\}
and a Gaussian approximation
\{y_{t}\}
. Our main theorems establish conditions under which the normalized difference over a fixed number of iterates
T
convergence to zero in probability:

\displaystyle\frac{\|x_{\scriptscriptstyle{\leq T}}-y_{\scriptscriptstyle{\leq T}}\|}{\sqrt{n}}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathrm{p}}0

(6)

where
x_{\leq T}
denotes the concatenation of
x_{0},\dots x_{T}
and
\|\cdot\|
is the Euclidean norm. To the best of our understanding, this type of coupling argument has not appeared previously in the AMP literature.

1.2
Case Study: Power Iteration with Partial Updates

As an illustrative example of application of our results, we consider the problem of constructing an estimate for a random point
\theta\sim\mathsf{Unif}(\{\pm 1\}^{n}\})
given matrix observation
M
from a spiked Wigner model

\displaystyle M=\frac{\lambda}{n}\theta\theta^{\top}+Z

(7)

where
\lambda>0
and
Z
is an
n\times n
random matrix drawn independently of
\theta
from the Gaussian orthogonal ensemble
\mathsf{GOE}(n)
, i.e.,
Z
is symmetric with independent
\mathsf{N}(0,1/n)
entries above the diagonal and independent
\mathsf{N}(0,2/n)
entries on the diagonal.
A natural approach is to construct an estimator of
\theta
based on properties of the spectrum of
M\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,

\displaystyle M=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}v_{i}v_{i}^{\top},

(8)

for eigenvalues
|\xi_{1}|\geq|\xi_{2}|\geq\dots\geq|\xi_{n}|\in\mathbb{R}
sorted in order of magnitude. From standard results in random matrix theory
[
14
]
, the value of
\lambda
determines whether the angle
|\langle\theta,v_{1}\rangle|
is non-vanishing in the large-
n
limit. In particular,

\displaystyle|\langle\theta,v_{1}\rangle|^{2}\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\mathrm{a.s.}}\begin{cases}1-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}&\text{if $\lambda>1$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\qquad\xi_{1}\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\mathrm{a.s.}}\begin{cases}\lambda+\frac{1}{\lambda}&\text{if $\lambda>1$}\\
2&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(9)

Conversely, when
\lambda<1
, it is known that no procedure is capable of providing an estimate of
\theta
based on
M
that achieves non-vanishing correlation with the ground truth
[
15
]
. Thus, a reasonable approach to estimate
\theta
is to use the leading eigenvector of
M
,
v_{1}
, as our estimator.

A classical technique to recover
v_{1}
is the power method, or power iteration
[
16
]
. For some initial value
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the power iteration is given by the recursion

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}_{t}=\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\|x_{t}\|}x_{t},\qquad x_{t+1}=M\hat{\theta}_{t}

(10)

Let
\alpha\coloneqq\cos^{-1}(|\langle\frac{\hat{\theta}_{0}}{\sqrt{n}},v_{1}\rangle|)
be the angle between the initialization
x_{0}
and the leading eigenvalue
v_{1}
. Classical convergence bounds
[
16
, Theorem 8.2.1]
guarantee that

\displaystyle 1-|\langle v_{1},\hat{\theta}_{t}\rangle|^{2}\leq\tan^{2}(\alpha)\Big|\frac{\xi_{2}}{\xi_{1}}\Big|^{2t}\ .

(11)

Hence, under the mild condition
\alpha>0
(i.e.,
x_{0}
does not lie in the nullspace of
v_{1}
), the estimates
\hat{\theta}_{t}
converge to
v_{1}
geometrically fast as long as the spectral gap is greater than one. This result is general and holds for any symmetric matrix
M
.

Interpreting the projection onto the
\sqrt{n}
-sphere
x\mapsto\sqrt{n}x/\|x\|
to be a denoising function, a standard AMP correction term can be added to the power method to obtain the “AMP-adjusted” recursion

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}_{t}=\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\|x_{t}\|}x_{t},\qquad x_{t+1}=M\hat{\theta}_{t}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\|x_{t}\|}\hat{\theta}_{t-1}

(12)

with the convention that
\hat{\theta}_{-1}\equiv 0
. When
M
is sampled from a spiked matrix model, this iteration can be tracked accurately in the large-
n
limit via SE. Furthermore, when
\lambda>1
, the iterates
\{x_{t}\}
can be shown to converge to the fixed point
\sqrt{n}\lambda v_{1}
, a multiple of the leading eigenvector.

For applications with very large data matrices, it may only be possible to operate on a subset of the full data matrix
M
at each iteration. Specifically, consider the setting where only a fraction of the rows of
M
are available at each iteration
t
, i.e. only
\Pi_{t}M
is observed where
\Pi_{t}=\diag(\delta_{t})
and
\delta_{t}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
is a binary vector indicating which rows are updated in the
t
-th iteration.

for some diagonal 0–1 matrices
\{\Pi_{t}\}
. As discussed above, this may be due to straggler nodes in a distributed setting or deliberate scheduling. Based on the results in this paper, we propose the following AMP-corrected power method with partial updates:

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}_{t}=\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\|x_{t}\|},\quad x_{t+1}=\Pi_{t}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M\hat{\theta}_{t}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\|x_{t}\|}\sum_{s<t}\gtr\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Pi_{t-1}^{\perp}\dotsm\Pi_{s+1}^{\perp}\Pi_{s}}}\right)\hat{\theta}_{s}}}\right)+\Pi_{t}^{\perp}x_{t}

(13)

where the complementary projections are simply
\Pi_{t}^{\perp}=\mathrm{I}-\Pi_{t}
. This recursion is practical as it does not require knowledge of any of the model parameters and its computational cost per iteration
t
is dominated by the matrix multiply
\Pi_{t}Mx_{t}
, which involves
O(nk_{t})
operations, where
k_{t}=\operatorname{rank}(\Pi_{t})
(or the number of nonzero diagonal elements).

Despite the apparent complexity, the trace terms
\gtr\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Pi_{t-1}^{\perp}\dotsm\Pi_{s+1}^{\perp}\Pi_{s}}}\right)
can be computed efficiently in a recursive fashion in
O(nt)
operations for time step
t
.
This can be achieved via an equivalent recursion that uses the vectors
\gamma_{s}^{(t)}\in\{0,1\}^{n},0\leq s\leq t
to indicate the coordinates that, as of time
t
, were most recently updated at time
s
. Specifically, the
t
-th iterate is formed by

\displaystyle\gamma_{tt}=\Pi_{t}\mathsf{1}

\displaystyle\gamma_{ts}=\Pi_{t}^{\perp}\gamma_{t-1,s},\ 0\leq s<t

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}_{t}=\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\|x_{t}\|}x_{t},\quad x_{t+1}=\Pi_{t}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M\hat{\theta}_{t}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\|x_{t}\|}\sum_{s<t}\|\gamma_{t-1,s}\|\hat{\theta}_{s}}}\right)+\Pi_{t}^{\perp}x_{t}

(14)

where
\mathsf{1}
denotes the all-ones vector. Alternatively, see the implementation in Algorithm
1
.

In Section
3.2
, we provide single-letter formulas for the SE dynamics of (

) under some structural assumptions on the signal

\{\Pi_{t}\}
. Note that the AMP correction term is non-standard due to its long-term memory. Intuitively, this is due to coordinates that have not been updated for more than one time step. Formally, it follows from the correction term in our OpAMP framework.

Most of the work in the AMP literature has focused on finite-memory versions of (

) where each

depends only on a fixed number of previous iterates
[

,

,

,

.

A version of the projection AMP framework of this paper was studied by Çakmak et al.

[

. These works focus on the behavior of existing optimization techniques in scaling regimes where the number of rows updated at each iteration grows sublinearly with the problem dimension. By contrast, the main focus of this paper is to design algorithms that overcome the limitations of the dynamic data as expressed in (

), e.g., by optimizing the long-term memory as function of the linear operators.

In a slightly different direction, the special case of (

) where the linear operator is fixed for all iterations has been studied by a subset of the authors in the context of the matrix tensor product model
[
28
,
29
]
.

Beyond the setting of IID Gaussian matrices, AMP algorithms have also been proposed and analyzed for orthogonally-invariant random matrix ensembles

. These results impose a separability assumption on the functions

, which precludes the general linear transformations used in the proof of our OpAMP framework. Extending the results in this paper to other matrix ensembles is an interesting direction for future research.

We also note that there has been progress on finite sample analysis of AMP algorithms via concentration bounds
[

,

]
, local convexity properties
[

]
, and distributional decompositions

,

. However, all of these results are restricted to the setting of separable functions.

. By choosing

[

2
Linear Operator AMP

This section describes the linear operator AMP framework and states our main theoretical results. To keep the presentation streamlined, we focus on the centered version of the recursion given by

\displaystyle x_{t}=\mathcal{L}_{t}(Z)f_{t}(x_{<t})-\sum_{s<t}B_{ts}f_{s}(x_{<s})

(15)

where
Z
is an
n\times n
random matrix drawn from the Gaussian orthogonal ensemble
\mathsf{GOE}(n)
, i.e.,
Z
is symmetric with independent
\mathsf{N}(0,1/n)
entries above the diagonal and independent
\mathsf{N}(0,2/n)
entries on the diagonal. The extension to settings where the matrix has a low-rank signal component follows from standard arguments in the AMP literature.

These arguments are outlined in Appendix
D
for the spiked matrix model considered in Section
3
and full details for other problem settings (e.g., regression) can be found in
[
1
]
.

Each linear operator
\mathcal{L}_{t}
has a (non-unique) decomposition of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{t}(Z)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}L_{tk}ZR_{tk}

(16)

for
n\times n
matrices
\{L_{tk},R_{tk}:k=1,\dots,K\}
. We require that the both operator norm and the rank (i.e., the smallest
K
such that (
16
) holds) are bounded uniformly w.r.t. the problem dimension
n
.

Our theoretical results provide a connection between the distribution of the AMP iterates and a zero-mean Gaussian process
\{y_{t}\}
whose second-order statistics are described in terms of
\{\mathcal{L}_{t}\}
and
\{f_{t}\}
via a recursive process known as
state evolution
. Starting with
\cov(y_{0})=\frac{1}{n}\|f_{0}\|^{2}\mathrm{I}
, the covariance at time
t
is defined by the covariance up to

2.1
Autoregressive Linear Operator AMP

While the general formulation in (

\displaystyle x_{t}=\mathcal{L}_{t}(Z)f_{t}(x_{t-1})+\sum_{s<t}A_{ts}x_{s}-\sum_{s<t}B_{ts}f_{s}(x_{t-1})

Here, the
n\times n

To describe the SE, we define the collection of

\{C_{st}:0\leq s<t\}

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{I}_{n}&\\

).

2.2
Projection AMP

The projection AMP framework is a specialization of (
21
) where each linear operator is given by
\mathcal{L}_{t}(Z)=\Pi_{t}Z
for an
n\times n
projection matrix
\Pi_{t}
and the autoregressive linear memory term is the complementary projection matrix
\Pi_{t}^{\perp}=\mathrm{I}-\Pi_{t}
applied to the past iteration.

The projection AMP recursion can be expressed as

\displaystyle x_{t}

\displaystyle=\Pi_{t}\Big(Zf_{t}(x_{t-1})-\sum_{s<t}b_{ts}f_{s}(x_{s-1})\Big)+\Pi_{t}^{\perp}x_{t-1}

(27)

A useful property of this formulation is that the memory terms depend only on the projector sequence and the debiasing terms are described by scalars.

The additional structure of projection matrices also leads to simplifications for the SE. The covariance of the zero-mean Gaussian process
\{y_{t}\}
is given by

\displaystyle\cov(y_{s},y_{t})

\displaystyle=\sum_{s^{\prime}\leq s,t^{\prime}\leq t}\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\langle f_{s^{\prime}}(y_{s^{\prime}-1}),f_{t^{\prime}}(y_{t^{\prime}-1})\rangle}}\right]C_{ss^{\prime}}\Pi_{s^{\prime}}(\Pi_{t^{\prime}}C_{tt^{\prime}})^{\top}

(28)

where the matrices
C_{ts}
are defined by

\displaystyle C_{ts}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\mathrm{I}_{n}&s=t\\
\Pi_{t}^{\perp}\Pi_{t-1}^{\perp}\cdots\Pi_{s+2}^{\perp}\Pi_{s+1}^{\perp}&0\leq s<t\end{cases}

(29)

Theorem 3
.

Let
\{x_{t}\}
be generated by (
27
) and let
\{y_{n}\}
be the zero-mean Gaussian process defined by the SE. Suppose Assumption
1
holds,
Z\sim\mathsf{GOE}(n)
, and
the scalars
\{b_{ts}:0\leq s<t\}
are given by

\displaystyle b_{ts}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\gtr\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathsf{D}f_{t}(y_{t-1})}}\right]C_{t-1,s}\Pi_{s}}}\right)

(30)

Then, for any fixed number of iterations
T
, there exists a sequence (in
n
) of couplings between
x_{\scriptscriptstyle{\leq T}}
and
y_{\scriptscriptstyle{\leq T}}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\|x_{\scriptscriptstyle{\leq T}}-y_{\scriptscriptstyle{\leq T}}\|}{\sqrt{n}}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathrm{p}}0

(31)

The SE for projection AMP admits further simplifications for the special case of commuting orthogonal projections, i.e., each
\Pi_{s}
is symmetric and
\Pi_{s}\Pi_{t}=\Pi_{t}\Pi_{s}
for all
s,t
. Starting with (
28
) and then using the fact that the
\Pi_{t}
and
C_{ts}
commute, one finds that
\cov(y_{t})
satisfies the simple recursion

\displaystyle\cov(y_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\|f_{t}(y_{t-1})\|^{2}}}\right]\Pi_{t}+\cov(y_{t-1})\Pi_{t}^{\perp}

(32)

where
\cov(y_{t-1})\Pi_{t}^{\perp}=\Pi_{t}^{\perp}\cov(y_{t-1})
is symmetric. In particular, if
\Pi_{0}=\mathrm{I}_{n}
and every
f_{t}
is supported on the sphere of radius
\sigma\sqrt{n}
then it follows that
\cov(y_{t})=\sigma^{2}\mathrm{I}_{n}
is constant across iterations.

3
Matrix Estimation with Partial Updates

In this section, we show how our linear operator framework can be applied to settings where the entire data matrix cannot be applied at each iteration. For concreteness, we focus on the rank-one spiked matrix model

\displaystyle M=\frac{\lambda}{n}\theta\theta^{\top}+Z

(33)

where
\lambda\geq 0
is a positive scalar,
\theta=(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the unknown signal, and
Z\sim\mathsf{GOE}(n)
is additive noise. The goal is to recover
\theta
from
M
subject to the constraints that only a subset of the rows of
M
can be accessed at each iteration.

The update constraints are modeled using projection AMP with projections of the form
\Pi_{t}=\diag(\delta_{t})
where
\delta_{t}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
is a binary vector indicating which rows can be updated in the
t
-th iteration.
For a given sequence of “denoising” functions
\{f_{t}\}
, we construct a sequence of estimates
\{\hat{\theta}_{t}\}
using the following version of projection AMP:

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}_{t}

\displaystyle=f_{t}(x_{t-1})

\displaystyle x_{t}

\displaystyle=\delta_{t}\circ\Big(M\hat{\theta}_{t}-\sum_{s<t}b_{ts}\hat{\theta}_{s}\Big)+(\mathsf{1}-\delta_{t})\circ x_{t-1}

(34)

Here,
\circ
denotes the elementwise (Hadamard) product and
\mathsf{1}
denotes the all ones vector. The scalar debiasing coefficients
b_{ts}
are defined as a function of the SE according to (
40
). To circumvent some cumbersome details that arise with a generic initialization, we will assume throughout this section that every row of the matrix is updated in the the first time step, i.e.,
\delta_{0}\equiv\mathsf{1}
is the all-ones vector.

State evolution.

Combining Theorem
3
with the recentering arguments given in Appendix
D
, we find that the iterates from the recursion (
34
) are well approximated by a Gaussian process
\{y_{t}\}
, whose mean and covariance are defined by a two-parameter SE.
The SE is described in terms of the scalar overlap parameters

\displaystyle q_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\|f_{t}(y_{t-1})\|^{2}}}\right];\quad r_{t}=\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\langle\theta,f_{t}(y_{t-1})\rangle}}\right]

(35)

where
q_{0}=\|f_{0}\|^{2}/n,\ r_{0}=\langle\theta,f_{0}\rangle/n
are the overlaps arising from the initial estimate
\hat{\theta}_{0}=f_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Starting with
\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[y_{0}}}\right]=\lambda r_{0}\theta
and
\cov(y_{0})=q_{0}\mathrm{I}_{n}
, the mean and covariance of
\{y_{t}\}
are updated recursively according to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[y_{t}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\lambda r_{t}\delta_{t}\circ\theta+(1-\delta_{t})\circ\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[y_{t-1}}}\right]\ ,

\displaystyle\cov(y_{t})

\displaystyle=q_{t}\diag(\delta_{t})+\cov(y_{t-1})(\mathrm{I}_{n}-\diag(\delta_{t}))

(36)

Remark 2
.

State evolution is defined in terms of the ground truth signal
\theta
, that can be assumed to be either fixed or random but is generally unknown. Any computationally feasible AMP algorithm will not explicitly rely on knowledge of
\theta
, and instead will estimate
r_{t}
via concentration arguments based on structural information about theta (e.g., sparsity level, noise variance, limiting empirical distribution).

A useful property of the SE is that, for each time step
t
, the distribution of the
i
-th component of the Gaussian vector
y_{t}=(y_{1t},\dots,y_{tn})
depends only on the signal component
\theta_{i}
and the last time step in which
i
-th row of the matrix was updated. To see this, observe that the
\cov(y_{t})
is diagonal for all iterations, and thus each
y_{t}
has independent components. For a given indicator sequence
\{\delta_{t}\}
, the index for the most recent update of the
i
-th row before time step
t
is encoded by the function

\tau:\mathbb{N}\times\{1,\dots,n\}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0}
, defined by

\displaystyle\tau(t,i)\coloneqq\max\{s\in\{0,1,\dots,t-1\}:\delta_{si}=1\}

(37)

where
\delta_{si}
is the
i
-th element of the binary vector
\delta_{s}
.

From the recursive structure in (
36
), it follows that every component that was last updated at time step
s
is described by a scalar Gaussian noise model with parameters
(q_{s},r_{s})
. Specifically, the variables
y_{t1},\dots,y_{tn}
are distributed independently with

\displaystyle\tau(t+1,i)=s\quad\implies\quad y_{ti}\sim\mathsf{N}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\lambda r_{s}\theta_{i},q_{s}}}\right)

(38)

for all
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. We note that the function
\tau
provides an alternative representation of the indicator sequence
\{\delta_{t}\}
, which can be recovered via the correspondence
\delta_{ti}=\bm{1}\{\tau(t+1,i)=t\}
.

Debiasing coefficients.

The function
\tau
also provides a compact representation of the debiasing coefficients defined by (
30
). Observe that the
n\times n
matrix
C_{t-1,s}\Pi_{s}
appearing in the specification of
b_{ts}
is given by

\displaystyle C_{t-1,s}\Pi_{s}=\begin{cases}\diag((\mathsf{1}-\delta_{t-1})\circ(\mathsf{1}-\delta_{t-2})\cdots(\mathsf{1}-\delta_{s+1})\circ\delta_{s}),&s=0,1,\dots,t-2\\
\diag(\delta_{t-1})&s=t-1\end{cases}

(39)

This is a diagonal binary matrix with whose
i
-th diagonal entry is the indicator of the event that the
i
-th row was last updated at time step
s
, i.e., the event
\{\tau(t,i)=s\}
. Plugging (
39
) into the definition of projection AMP corrections (
30
), we see that the debiasing coefficients can be expressed as

\displaystyle b_{ts}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bm{1}\{\tau(t,i)=s\}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathsf{D}_{ii}f_{t}(y_{t-1})}}\right]

(40)

where
\mathsf{D}_{ii}
denotes the partial derivative of the
i
-th output
f_{ti}(y_{t-1})
with respect to the
i
-th input
y_{t-1,i}
.

3.1
Asymptotic State Evolution

In this section, we obtain a simplified characterization of the SE by focusing on the high-dimensional limit for sequence of problems, with increasing dimension
n
, where the signal
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, initialization
\hat{\theta}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and indicator sequence
\{\delta_{t}\}\in\{0,1\}^{n\times\mathbb{N}_{0}}
together satisfy a decoupling property.

Assumption 4
.

M
is given by (
33
) and the Projection AMP recursion (
34
) satisfies the following conditions:

1.

For each
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the joint empirical measure of
\{(\theta_{i},\hat{\theta}_{0i},\delta_{0i},\dots,\delta_{ti}):i\in[n]\}
converges in quadratic Wasserstein distance to a limiting probability measure the form
\mu\otimes\nu_{t}
where
\mu
is a distribution on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
whose marginals have unit second moments and
\nu_{t}
is is the distribution of the first
t
entries of a binary string drawn from a probability measure
\nu
on
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.

2.

For each
t\in\mathbb{N}
, the denoiser
f_{t}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is separable and is given by

\displaystyle f_{ti}(x_{t-1})=\eta_{t}(x_{t-1,i};\,q_{\tau(t,i)},\lambda r_{\tau(t,i)})

(41)

where
\eta_{t}\colon\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz continuous scalar denoiser that is fixed for all
n
and
\tau
is the index function defined in (
37
).

According to our convention that every row is updated at the first time step (
t=0
), the measure
\nu
is supported on binary strings whose first entry is one. For each
t\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
p_{t}
to the probability mass function for the position of the last non-zero entry occurring before time
t
, i.e.,

\displaystyle p_{t}(s)\coloneqq\nu_{t}\big(\{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{t}:\omega_{s}=1,\omega_{s+1}=\cdots=\omega_{t-1}=0\}\big)

(42)

for all
s\in\{0,1,\dots,t-1\}
. This probability mass function can also be defined directly via the limiting empirical measure as

\displaystyle p_{t}(s)\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bm{1}\{\tau(t,i)=s\}

(43)

where Assumption
4
.1 ensures that the limit exists.

We recall that
(q_{t},r_{t})
correspond to the finite-
n
overlap parameters of SE at iteration
t
(
35
). To define their asymptotic limits, we introduce the pairs
(\sigma^{2}_{t},\rho_{t})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
, that depend on the limiting measure
\mu\otimes\nu
and the denoiser
\eta
as follows. The initialization is given by

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}_{0}=\int\hat{u}^{2}\,d\mu(u,\hat{u}),\qquad\rho_{0}=\int u\hat{u}\,d\mu(u,\hat{u})

(44)

For
t\geq 1
, the update is given by

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}_{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s<t}\psi_{t}(\sigma^{2}_{s},\lambda\rho_{s})\,p_{t}(s),\qquad\rho_{t}=\sum_{s<t}\phi_{t}(\sigma^{2}_{s},\lambda\rho_{s})\,p_{t}(s)

(45)

where
the functions
\psi_{t},\phi_{t}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
are defined as

\displaystyle\psi_{t}(v,w)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[(\eta_{t}(wu+\sqrt{v}z;v,w))^{2}}}\right]\,d\mu(u,\hat{u})

\displaystyle\phi_{t}(v,w)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[u\,\eta_{t}(wu+\sqrt{v}z;v,w))}}\right]\,d\mu(u,\hat{u})

(46)

The expectation is taken with respect to a standard Gaussian variable
z\sim\mathsf{N}(0,1)
. From the product assumption on the limiting empirical measure
\mu\otimes\nu
, it follows that, for any
t\in\mathbb{N}
, the asymptotic SE
(\sigma^{2}_{t},\rho_{t})
corresponds to the large-
n
limit of the SE parameters
(q_{0},r_{0})
:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}q_{t}=\sigma^{2}_{t},\quad\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}r_{t}=\rho_{t}

(47)

We note that when applied to full matrix updates (i.e.,
\delta_{t}\equiv\mathsf{1}
) these SE update equations recover the usual form the SE appearing in AMP analysis where
p_{t}(s)
places all of its mass at the previous time step.

3.2
Power Iteration

In this section, we provide asymptotic guarantees on the limiting absolute empirical correlation of the projection AMP estimates
\{\hat{\theta}_{t}\}
when the denoisers
f_{t}
are chosen to be the projection onto the sphere of radius
\sqrt{n}
. The projection AMP algorithm takes the form

\displaystyle x_{t}=\delta_{t}\circ\Big(M\hat{\theta}_{t}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\|x_{t-1}\|}\sum_{s<t}w_{ts}\hat{\theta}_{s}\Big)+(\mathsf{1}-\delta_{t})\circ x_{t-1},\quad\hat{\theta}_{t+1}=\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\|x_{t}\|}x_{t}

(48)

for
w_{ts}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bm{1}\{\tau(t,i)=s\}
, the number of coordinates that were last updated at time
s
. We remark that this recursion encompasses as a special case the distributed power method (
13
) up to a scaling factor on
\theta
. Assumption
4
implies that
(q_{0},r_{0})
satisfy

\displaystyle q_{0}\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\|\hat{\theta}_{0}\|^{2}\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{}1,\quad r_{0}\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\langle\theta,\hat{\theta}_{0}\rangle\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{}\int u\hat{u}\,d\mu(u,\hat{u})\eqqcolon\rho_{0}

(49)

Starting with
\rho_{0}
, then, we define the recurrence relation

\displaystyle\rho_{t}=\frac{\lambda\sum_{s<t}\rho_{s}p_{t}(s)}{\sqrt{\sum_{s<t}(\lambda^{2}\rho_{s}^{2}+1)p_{t}(s)}}

(50)

The following result relates the sequence
\{\rho_{t}\}
and with the asymptotic correlation between the signal
\theta
and the distributed power method estimates
\{\hat{\theta}_{t}\}
described in (
48
), and is proved in Appendix
E
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
M
be a spiked matrix model (
7
),
(\theta,\hat{\theta}_{0})
satisfy Assumption
4
and
\|\hat{\theta}_{0}\|=\sqrt{n}
. Consider the estimate sequence
\{\hat{\theta}_{t}\}
produced by (
48
). Then, for each
t\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\frac{1}{n}\langle\theta,\hat{\theta}_{t}\rangle-\rho_{t}}}\right|\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\mathrm{p}}0

(51)

for
\{\rho_{t}\}
defined recursively as in (
50
).

Remark 3
.

The above result is asymptotic in nature and, according to Assumption
4
, it requires a form of warm start
\hat{\theta}_{0}
such that the limiting correlation
\rho_{0}
is strictly positive. In finite dimensions, any random initialization will achieve non-zero overlap
r_{0}
with high probability, and the AMP-adjusted power method described in (
13
) can be observed to converge to a non-trivial fixed point. The value of the corresponding fixed-point overlap can be approximated to a high degree of precision by considering the SE fixed point under a small initial perturbation of
\rho_{0}
about zero.

3.3
Bayes-Optimal Denoising

One of the key strengths of the AMP framework is that prior information about the unknown signal can be incorporated naturally via the specification of the denoising functions. Throughout this section, we consider the Bayesian setting where the signal entries
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n}
are drawn independently from a fixed probability measure
\pi
on
\mathbb{R}
with sub-Gaussian tails. Under this assumption, the sequence of overlap parameters
\{(q_{t},r_{t})\}
is random because it depends on the realization of
\theta
. However, following the concentration arguments outlined in
[
1
]
, it can be shown that these overlaps converge in the large-
n
limit to a non-random limit described by the asymptotic SE parameters
\{(\sigma^{2}_{t},\rho_{t})\}
defined by (
45
). Note that this limit depends only on the signal distribution
\pi
and the limiting empirical measure
\nu
of the projector sequence.

The practical upside of having an asymptotic SE that depends on the distribution
\pi
(instead of
\theta
) is the that the the asymptotic overlap parameters
\{(\sigma^{2}_{s},\rho_{s})\}_{s<t}
can be used to design the denoiser at time step
t
. In view of (
38
), we see that each comparison vector
y_{t}
can be seen as a collection of
n
independent additive Gaussian noise channel observations of the signal entries
\theta_{i}
given by

\displaystyle y_{ti}=\lambda\rho_{\tau(t,i)}\theta_{i}+\sigma_{\tau(t,i)}z_{i},\quad z_{i}\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\mathsf{N}(0,1).

(52)

For the prior
\theta\sim\pi^{\otimes n}
, the optimal estimator of
\theta
under mean square error is the conditional mean estimator. This is a separable function of the form given in (
41
) with

\displaystyle\eta(y;v,w)\coloneqq\frac{\int u\exp\{\frac{wuy}{v}-\frac{w^{2}u^{2}}{2v}\}\,d\pi(u)}{\int\exp\{\frac{wuy}{v}-\frac{w^{2}u^{2}}{2v}\}\,d\pi(u)}

(53)

From iterated expectations, it can be verified that
\psi(v,w)=\phi(v,w)=\Psi(w^{2}/v)
for all
(v,w)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
where
\Psi\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is defined by

\displaystyle\Psi(\gamma)\coloneqq\int\frac{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int u\exp\{\sqrt{\gamma}uy-\frac{1}{2}\gamma u^{2}\}\,d\pi(u)}}\right)^{2}}{\int\exp\{\sqrt{\gamma}uy-\frac{1}{2}\gamma u^{2}\}\,d\pi(u)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}y^{2}}\,dy

(54)

Consequently, the asymptotic overlap parameters satisfy
\rho_{t}=\sigma^{2}_{t}
for all
t
. With this observation, we conclude that the asymptotic SE is described by the one-parameter recursion

\displaystyle\rho_{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s<t}\Psi(\lambda^{2}\rho_{s})\,p_{t}(s).

(55)

Evaluating this recurrence relation in the special case where
p_{t}
is a delta function at
t-1
recovers the standard SE recursion for Bayes-optimal AMP with full matrix updates.

\displaystyle t\geq J:\quad p_{t}(s)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }s=0,\dotsc,t-J-1,\\

\displaystyle x_{t}=Zf_{t}(x_{<t})-\sum_{s<t}b_{ts}f_{s}(x_{<s})

\{x_{t}\}

.

1
holds,

are given by

\displaystyle b_{ts}=\frac{1}{n}\gtr(\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathsf{D}_{s}f_{t}(y_{<t})}}\right])

\displaystyle\frac{\|x_{\scriptscriptstyle{\leq T}}-y_{\scriptscriptstyle{\leq T}}\|}{\sqrt{n}}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathrm{p}}0

A.2.1
From matrix-valued AMP to full memory AMP

For a fixed number of iterations

A.2.2
Convergence along any subsequence

The prior work on AMP with non-separable functions
[
11
,
12
]
places assumptions on the
f_{t}
ensuring that the SE covariance has a well-defined limit. Specifically, for every
\Omega\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{T}
and
0\leq s,t\leq T
, the following limit exists and is finite

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\langle f_{s}(y_{<s}),f_{t}(y_{<t})\rangle}}\right]

(74)

where the expectation is taken with respect to
y_{<T}\sim\mathsf{N}(0,\Omega\otimes\mathrm{I}_{n})

In this section, we argue that these assumptions can be relaxed to require only that the sequence of (finite
n
) SE covariance matrices is uniformly bounded. Approximate Gaussianity of the AMP recursion is then established by showing that the normalized difference between the iterates
\{x_{t}\}
and the Gaussian approximation
\{y_{t}\}
converges to zero in the large-
n
limit.

This generalization is possible is because of the assumed Lipschitz continuity of each
f_{t}
function, which ensures that, if
y_{<T}\sim\mathsf{N}(0,\Omega\otimes\mathrm{I}_{n})
for
\Omega\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{T}
, then the element-wise overlap

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\langle f_{s}(y_{<s}),f_{t}(y_{<t})\rangle}}\right]

(75)

is continuous function of
\Omega
. It follows that, for any given
T
, the sequence of overlaps
\Sigma_{\leq T}
is defined on a compact subset of the cone positive semidefinite matrices. If the overlaps are bounded uniformly in
n
, it is then possible to partition any sequence of problems into a finite number of subsequences, each of which is eventually contained within an
\epsilon
-ball centered about a fixed quantization point. By continuity, closeness of SE covariance implies closeness of the expectations under the class of pseudo-Lipschitz functions. Hence, we can apply the result for converging sequences in
[
12
, Theorem 2]
to conclude that

\displaystyle\big|\psi_{n}(x_{\leq T})-\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\psi_{n}(y_{\leq T})}}\right]\big|\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathrm{p}}0

(76)

for any sequence of uniformly pseudo-Lipschitz functions where
y_{\leq T}\sim\mathsf{N}(0,\Sigma_{\leq T}\otimes\mathrm{I})
is defined with respect to the finite-
n
SE covariance. Finally, we appeal to the equivalence result in Theorem
7
to conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{\|x_{\scriptscriptstyle{\leq T}}-y_{\scriptscriptstyle{\leq T}}\|}{\sqrt{n}}\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\mathrm{p}}0

(77)

This concludes the proof Theorem
5
.

B.1
Proof of Theorem

The high-level idea of this proof is express the OpAMP recursion
\{x_{t}\}
in terms of a “lifted recursion”
\{w_{tk}\}
, indexed by

and

\displaystyle x_{t}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}L_{tk}w_{tk}

where the

are defined by the decomposition of

1
).
Making appropriate adjustments for the indexing, we apply Theorem

to obtain a SE and Gaussian approximation for
\{w_{tk}\}
. The desired result for the OpAMP recursion then follows straightforwardly from the linearity of the mapping from

B.1.1
Lifted recursion

We begin by introducing the recursion
\{w_{tk}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
indexed by the pair
(t,k)
with
t\in\mathbb{N}
and

\{g_{tk}\}
and a sequence of scalar debiasing terms

\displaystyle w_{tk}

\displaystyle=Zg_{tk}(w_{<t})-\sum_{s<t}\sum_{l=1}^{K}c_{tksl}\,g_{sl}(w_{<s})\ ,\quad\text{for $k=1,\dotsc,K$}

R_{tk}

\displaystyle g_{tk}(w_{<t})

\displaystyle=R_{tk}f_{t}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{K}L_{0k}w_{0k},\ldots,\sum_{k=1}^{K}L_{t-1,k}w_{t-1,k}\Big)

Next, we observe that the recursion defined by (

59

(t,k)
and a single iteration index

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1

\{g_{tk}\}
satisfies Assumption

has a Lipschitz constant that is bounded uniformly for all

\{w_{tk}\}

In order to describe the SE for the the lifted recursion, we follow the specification given in Theorem

\{u_{tk}\}
be a zero-mean Gaussian process whose covariance is defined recursively by the SE update equation

\displaystyle\cov(u_{sl},u_{tk})=\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\langle g_{sl}(u_{<s}),g_{tk}(u_{<t})}}\right]\,\mathrm{I}_{n},\quad 0\leq s\leq t,\quad k,l=1,\dots,K

The debiasing terms
\{c_{tksl}:0\leq s<t,k,l=1,\dots,K\}

\displaystyle c_{tksl}=\frac{1}{n}\gtr(\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathsf{D}_{sl}g_{tk}(u_{<t})}}\right])

where

B.1.2
Mapping back to OpAMP

The OpAMP recursion in (

\displaystyle w_{tk}

\displaystyle=ZR_{tk}f_{t}(x_{t-1})-\sum_{s<t}\sum_{l=1}^{K}c_{tksl}\,R_{sl}f_{s}(x_{s-1})\ ,\quad\text{for $k=1,\dotsc,K$}

\displaystyle B_{ts}=\sum_{k,l=1}^{K}c_{tksl}L_{tk}R_{sl}

\displaystyle\cov(u_{sl},u_{tk})

Theorem 6

Appendix D
Centering for the Spiked Matrix Model

This section outlines the centering arguments used for the spiked matrix model in Section
3
.
We recall the spiked matrix model and the projection AMP algorithm for the reader’s convenience.
The data matrix is a symmetric
n\times n
matrix drawn from the spiked matrix model

\displaystyle M=\frac{\lambda}{n}\theta\theta^{\top}+Z;\quad Z\sim\mathsf{GOE}(n),

(126)

where
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a fixed but unknown signal. A sequence of estimate-iterate pairs
\{(\hat{\theta}_{t},x_{t})\}
is generated according to the projection AMP algorithm

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}_{t}

\displaystyle=f_{t}(x_{t-1})\,

(127)

\displaystyle x_{t}

\displaystyle=\delta_{t}\circ\Big(M\hat{\theta}_{t}-\sum_{s<t}b_{ts}\hat{\theta}_{s}\Big)+(\mathsf{1}-\delta_{t})\circ x_{t-1}\ ,

(128)

with initialization
f_{0}\equiv\hat{\theta}_{0}
.

Our goal is to show that the sequence
\{x_{t}\}
can be compared with the Gaussian sequence
\{y_{t}\}
, whose (nonzero) mean and covariance are defined by the SE in (
36
).
Setting
\mu_{t}=\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[y_{t}}}\right]
and
\Sigma_{t}=\cov(y_{t})
, these SE update equations can be expressed compactly as

\displaystyle\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=\lambda r_{t}\delta_{t}\circ\theta+(\mathsf{1}-\delta_{t})\circ\mu_{t-1}

(129)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{t}

\displaystyle=\lambda q_{t}\diag(\delta_{t})+(\mathrm{I}_{n}-\diag(\delta_{t}))\Sigma_{t-1}.

(130)

where

\displaystyle q_{t}=\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\|f_{t}(y_{t-1})\|^{2}}}\right],\qquad r_{t}=\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\langle\theta,f_{t}(y_{t-1})}}\right]

The main difference between the projection AMP algorithm in (
D
) and the recursion appearing in Theorem
3
is that the the data matrix

has a nonzero mean. In order to apply the results in Theorem
3
we apply centering argument that is standard in the AMP literature; see
[
1
]
. Specifically, we show that that (
D
) can be approximated by a modified recursion that is applied directly to the noise matrix
Z
. We emphasize that this new recursion is introduced primarily as proof technique. The centering transformation cannot be applied in practice, because it depends on the unknown signal
\theta
.

While a variety of centering arguments can be applied, it is convenient to define the centered recursion in terms of the mean vector
\mu_{t}
defined by (
D

\{\tilde{x}_{t}\}
given by

\displaystyle\tilde{x}_{t}=\delta_{t}\circ\Big(Z\tilde{f}_{t}(\tilde{x}_{t-1})-\sum_{s<t}b_{ts}\tilde{f}_{s}(\tilde{x}_{s-1})\Big)+(\mathsf{1}-\delta_{t})\circ\tilde{x}_{t-1}

where

\{\tilde{y}_{t}\}

\{\tilde{y}_{t}\}
matched to the covariance of the process
\{y_{t}\}
. In particular,

From here, the remaining step is to argue that the shifted sequence defined by
\{\mu_{t}+\tilde{x}_{t}\}
is close in distribution to the sequence
\{x_{t}\}
generated by (

and leveraging the equivalence between convergence with respect to sequences of pseudo-Lipschitz test functions and the existence of a Gaussian coupling we show in Theorem

(143)

and therefore the summation

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda^{2}r_{\tau(t,i)}^{2}\theta_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\sum_{s<t}r_{s}^{2}\bm{1}\{\tau(t,i)=s\}\theta_{i}^{2}\Big)

(144)

can be seen as an integral over the empirical measure of
(\theta,\delta_{0},\dotsc,\delta_{t-1})
. Then, since by assumption this empirical measure converges to the product measure
\mu\otimes\nu_{t}
, in the limit the expectation factorizes and we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda^{2}r_{\tau(t,i)}^{2}\theta_{i}^{2}\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{}\lambda^{2}\sum_{s<t}r_{s}^{2}p_{t}(s)\int u\,d\mu(u,\hat{u})=\lambda^{2}\sum_{s<t}\rho_{s}^{2}p_{t}(s)

(145)

By a similar argument,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}q_{\tau(t,i)}\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{}\sum_{s<t}p_{t}(s)=1

(146)

Then, by the inductive hypothesis and continuity, we have

\displaystyle q_{t}=\frac{1}{\sum_{s<t}(\lambda^{2}r_{s}^{2}+q_{s})p_{t}(s)}\Big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda^{2}r_{\tau(t,i)}^{2}\theta_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}q_{\tau(t,i)}\Big)\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{}1

(147)

and

\displaystyle r_{t}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sum_{s<t}(\lambda^{2}r_{s}^{2}+q_{s})p_{t}(s)}}\Big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda r_{\tau(t,i)}\theta_{i}^{2}\Big)\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{}\frac{\lambda\sum_{s<t}\rho_{s}p_{t}(s)}{\sqrt{\sum_{s<t}(\lambda^{2}\rho_{s}^{2}+1)p_{t}(s)}}=\rho_{t}

(148)

and induction is complete. This implies that

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\frac{1}{n}\Big\langle\theta,\frac{\tilde{x}_{t}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\Big\rangle-\rho_{t}}}\right|\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\mathrm{p}}0

(149)

for all
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and therefore the separable AMP iterates
\{\tilde{x}_{t}\}
satisfy the convergence result of Theorem
4
. All that is left is to show that, for each
t
,
x_{t}
and
\tilde{x}_{t}
are asymptotically close, i.e.

\displaystyle\frac{\|x_{t}-\tilde{x}_{t}\|}{\sqrt{n}}\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow]{\mathrm{p}}0

(150)

For
t=0
, the claim is obvious due to the matched initialization. By way of induction, assume this holds until some time
t
. Then, for
t+1
, we get

\displaystyle\|x_{t+1}-\tilde{x}_{t+1}\|

\displaystyle=\Bigg\|\delta_{t}\circ\Big\{\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\|x_{t}\|}\Big(Mx_{t}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s\leq t}w_{st}\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\|x_{s}\|}x_{s}\Big)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t+1}}}\Big(M\tilde{x}_{t}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s\leq t}w_{st}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{s}}}\tilde{x}_{s}\Big)\Big\}

\displaystyle\qquad+(\mathsf{1}-\delta_{t})\circ(x_{t}-\tilde{x}_{t})\Bigg\|

(151)

\displaystyle\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\|x_{t}\|}\Big(Mx_{t}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s\leq t}w_{ts}\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\|x_{s}\|}x_{s}\Big)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t+1}}}\Big(M\tilde{x}_{t}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s\leq t}w_{st}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{s}}}\tilde{x}_{s}\Big)}}\right\|+\|x_{t}-\tilde{x}_{t}\|

(152)

\displaystyle\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\|x_{t}\|}\Big[\Big(Mx_{t}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s\leq t}w_{ts}\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\|x_{s}\|}x_{s}\Big)-\Big(M\tilde{x}_{t}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s\leq t}w_{st}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{s}}}\tilde{x}_{s}\Big)\Big]}}\right\|

(153)

\displaystyle\quad+\Big|\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\|x_{t}\|}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t+1}}}\Big|\,\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|M\tilde{x}_{t}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s\leq t}w_{st}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{s}}}\tilde{x}_{s}}}\right\|+\|x_{t}-\tilde{x}_{t}\|

(154)

As a direct consequence of the inductive hypothesis,
\|x_{t}-\tilde{x}_{t}\|/\sqrt{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Furthermore

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{1}{n}\|x_{t}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}[\|\tilde{y}_{t}\|^{2}]\Big|\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\mathrm{p}}0

(155)

which by continuity and (
147
) implies that

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\|x_{t}\|}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t+1}}}\Big|\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\mathrm{p}}0

(156)

Then, since

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Big\|M\tilde{x}_{t}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s\leq t}w_{st}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{s}}}\tilde{x}_{s}\Big\|

(157)

is bounded with high probability, we conclude that

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\|x_{t}\|}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t+1}}}\Big|\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|M\tilde{x}_{t}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s\leq t}w_{st}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{s}}}\tilde{x}_{s}}}\right\|\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\mathrm{p}}0

(158)

Finally, we use the inductive hypothesis and the fact that all terms
\sqrt{n}/\|x_{s}\|
are bounded in probability, for
s\leq t
, to establish

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\|x_{t}\|}\Big[\Big(Mx_{t}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s\leq t}w_{ts}\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\|x_{s}\|}x_{s}\Big)-\Big(M\tilde{x}_{t}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s\leq t}w_{st}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{s}}}\tilde{x}_{s}\Big)\Big]}}\right\|

(159)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\|x_{t}\|}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|M(x_{t}-\tilde{x}_{t})-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s\leq t}w_{ts}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\|x_{s}\|}x_{s}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{s}}}\tilde{x}_{s}\Big)}}\right\|

(160)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\|x_{t}\|}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\|M\|_{op}\|x_{t}-\tilde{x}_{t}\|+\sum_{s\leq t}\frac{w_{ts}}{n}\Big\|\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\|x_{s}\|}x_{s}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{s}}}\tilde{x}_{s}\Big\|}}\right\}\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\mathrm{p}}0

(161)

Thus,
\|x_{t+1}-\tilde{x}_{t+1}\|/\sqrt{n}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{p}}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. This concludes the induction and the proof of Theorem
4
. ∎

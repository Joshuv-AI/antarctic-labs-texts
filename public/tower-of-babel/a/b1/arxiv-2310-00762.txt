A note on the stabilizer formalism via noncommutative graphs
Roy Araiza, Jihong Cai, Yushan Chen, Abraham Holtermann, Chieh Hsu, Tushar Mohan, Peixue Wu, Zeyuan Yu

Abstract
In this short note we formulate a stablizer formalism in the language of noncommutative graphs. The classes of noncommutative graphs we consider are obtained via unitary representations of finite groups, and suitably chosen operators on finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. Furthermore, in this framework, we generalize previous results in this area for determining when such noncommutative graphs have anticliques.



1
Introduction

Since they were first abstractly characterized by Choi and Effros in
Choi
, operator systems have had profound applications on many areas of mathematics, and more recently, on quantum information theory, see e.g.
duan2012
;
weaver2017
;
Brannan2022
. Such a framework led to generalizations of Lovász’ famous theta function
\vartheta

duan2012
, nonlocal games
Brannan2020
, the study of correlation sets
Araiza1
;
Araiza2
, and extensions of classical graph invariants
Paulsen13
. Our work will focus on a special class of finite-dimensional operator systems called
noncommutative graphs
, which were first introducted in the seminal work of Duan-Severini-Winter in
duan2009
. Using this special class of operator systems, Duan,Severini and Winter were able to extend the notions of classical, quantum, and entanglement assisted capacities. Noncommutative graphs are obtained by taking a Kraus representation of a quantum channel
\Phi:\mathbb{B}(H_{1})\to\mathbb{B}(H_{2}),\Phi\rho=\sum E_{i}\rho E_{i}^{\dagger}
, and considering the subspace
\mathcal{V}:=span\{E_{i}^{\dagger}E_{j}:1\leq i,j\leq n\}.
Two important properties of
\mathcal{V}
are what characterizes an operator system. First note
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}^{\dagger},
which is to say
\mathcal{V}
is
self-adjoint
, and second is that
I\in\mathcal{V}
, which is to say that
\mathcal{V}
is unital. Due to the aforementioned paper of Choi-Effros, every operator system may be concretely represented as a self-adjoint unital subspace of
\mathbb{B}(H)
.
Though not considered in this framework, noncommutative graphs are the essential ingredient in the famous Knill-Laflamme subspace condition from quantum error correction (cite Knill-Laflamme). In particular, given a quantum channel
\Phi
with Kraus representation
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r},E_{i}\subset\mathbb{B}(H_{1}:H_{2})
, then
\Phi
is correctable if and only if

\displaystyle PE_{i}^{\dagger}E_{j}P=\lambda_{ij}P,

(1)

for every error
E_{i}\in\mathbb{B}(H_{1}:H_{2})
, where
P:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{C}
is the projection onto the codespace
\mathcal{C}
, and
\lambda=[\lambda_{ij}]\in\mathbb{M}_{r}
is a hermitian matrix.Equivalently, this may be expressed as

\displaystyle P\mathcal{V}P=\mathbb{C}P.

(2)

Given a noncommutative graph
\mathcal{V}
, if Equation (
1
) is satisfied, we will call
P
an
anticlique
for
\mathcal{V}
. With this framework in mind, a natural question is the following:
for which classes of noncommutative graphs do there exists anticliques?
This question led to a series of papers (
amosov2017
;
amosov2018_1
;
amosov2018_2
) in which the authors answer this question for particular examples of classes of noncommutative graphs. In particular, in
amosov2018_2
, the authors prove that
span\{U_{\varphi}M_{o}U_{\varphi}:\varphi\in\mathbb{T}\}
is a noncommutative graph with anticliques
\{P_{s}:1\leq s\leq d\}
, where
U_{\varphi}=\sum_{s=1}^{d}e^{i\varphi s}P_{s}
is a unitary representation of
\mathbb{T}
. In
weaver2017
, using combinatorial techniques, it was proven that for
\text{dim}\ \mathcal{H}=d
and
k\leq d
, if the noncommutative graph
\mathcal{V}
satisfies

\displaystyle\text{dim}\ \mathcal{V}(\text{dim}\ \mathcal{V}+1)\leq\frac{d}{k},

(3)

then there exists
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
with
\text{dim}\ \mathcal{C}=k
such that
\text{dim}\ P_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{V}P_{\mathcal{C}}=1
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{V}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}:=\text{span}\{E_{1},\cdots,E_{m}\},\ [E_{i},E_{j}]=0,\ \forall i,j\leq m

and

\dim\ \mathcal{V}\leq\frac{d-k}{k-1},

(4)

then there exists
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
with
\text{dim}\ \mathcal{C}=k
such that
\text{dim}\ P_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{V}P_{\mathcal{C}}=1
. In
amosov2017
it was pointed out that the above upper bound is not sharp when the underlying Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
has a tensor product structure. In fact, if
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}
with
\text{dim}\ \mathcal{H}_{1}=\text{dim}\ \mathcal{H}_{2}=n>2
, a concrete noncommutative graph
\mathcal{V}
with
\text{dim}\ \mathcal{V}=2n(n-1)+1
and a code space with dimension
n
were given, which violates the above bounds (
3
) and (
4
). We lend our hand in answering this question by weakening the assumptions in
amosov2018_2
and therefore constructing a larger class of noncommutative graphs which exhibit anticliques. To this end, given a finite group
G
, let
\pi:G\to\mathbb{B}(H)
be a unitary representation,
M_{o}\in\mathbb{B}(H)
, and consider the subspace
\mathcal{V}_{M_{o}}:=span\{\pi(g)M_{o}\pi(g):g\in G\}
. For any
g\in G
, let
P_{i_{g}}
be the projector onto the
i
-th eigenspace of
\pi(g)
. Then our first main result is as follows:

Theorem 1
.

Suppose
\mathcal{V}_{M_{o}}
is a noncommutative graph and
G
is Abelian. Then for any sequence
\{i_{g}\in J_{g}\}_{g\in G}
, if

P=\prod_{g\in G}P_{i_{g}}

has rank no less than 2, then
P
is an anticlique for
\mathcal{V}
.

Our next and final theorem is obtaining a stabilizer formalism in the language of noncommutative graphs. In what follows, let
\mathcal{P}_{n}
denote the Pauli group acting on
2^{n}
qubits.

Theorem 2
.

For any Abelian subgroup
G\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{n}
such that
-I_{2}^{\otimes n}\notin G
, and
M_{0}\in\mathbb{M}_{2^{n}}
, define

\mathcal{V}_{M_{0}}:=\text{span}\{gM_{0}g:g\in G\}.

(5)

Then the span of
\mathcal{V}_{M_{0}}
such that
\mathcal{V}_{M_{0}}
is an operator system coincides with all the correctable errors outside the normalizer of
G
plus identity. In other words,

\text{span}\{\mathcal{V}_{M_{0}}:M_{0}\ \text{is\ such\ that}\ \mathcal{V}_{M_{0}}\ \text{is\ an\ operator\ system}\}=\text{span}\{(\mathcal{P}_{n}\backslash N(G))\cup I_{2}^{\otimes n}\}.

(6)

The paper is structured as follows: In Section 2 we cover some preliminary material and prove Theorem 1. In Section 3 we review the stabilizer formalism and prove Theorem 2.

2
Noncommutative graph and quantum error-correcting codes

Suppose
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space and
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
is the set of bounded operators;
duan2012
first introduced the definition of a (quantum) noncommutative graph:

Definition 1
.

A noncommutative graph
\mathcal{V}
is a linear subspace of
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
, such that

•

v\in\mathcal{V}
implies
v^{*}\in\mathcal{V}
.

•

I\in\mathcal{V}
.

As mentioned in the introduction, noncommutative graphs are a special class of more general objects known as operator systems, whose theory as been greatly developed over the last few decades (see
Paulsen02
). A
quantum code
is defined as a subspace
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
. We have the following definition of a
quantum error-correcting code
a la Knill-Laflamme
KL97
:

Definition 2
.

We say that
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
is a quantum error-correcting code for the noncommutative graph
\mathcal{V}
if

\dim P_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{V}P_{\mathcal{C}}=1,

(7)

where
P_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{C}
is the projection onto
\mathcal{C}
.

Lending our hand in answering the main question stated in the introduction, we consider noncommutative graphs built from two ingredients:

•

A group
G
with a unitary representation
\pi:G\to\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

•

An operator
M_{0}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

A subspace
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
given by
(G,\pi)
and
M_{0}
may be defined as

\mathcal{V}:=\overline{span}\{\pi(g)M_{0}\pi(g)^{*}:g\in G\}.

(8)

We point out that in general, some restrictions on
(G,\pi)
and
M_{0}
are required for the subspace in (
8
) to be a concrete operator system, see Remark
1
for an example.

In this note, we study compact (finite)
G
with a projective unitary representation

\pi:G\to\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}),

\text{dim}\ \mathcal{H}=d<\infty
, and a prefixed
M_{0}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
such that

Assumption 1
.

\mathcal{V}
defined by (
8
) is an operator system.

Given a group
G
, we will always assume we have a fixed representation
\pi:G\to\mathbb{(}H)
, and
M_{0}
is chosen such that (
8
) is an operator system. At times we will denote
\mathcal{V}
as
\mathcal{V}_{M_{0}}
to emphasize the dependence on the operator
M_{0}
.

Remark 1
.

Note that in
amosov2018_1
;
amosov2018_2
, the authors start with a positive operator
M_{0}
and

\int_{G}\pi(g)M_{0}\pi(g)^{*}d\mu(g)=I,

(9)

where
\mu
is the Haar measure on
G
. On the other hand, our assumption is weaker. For example, if
G=\{I_{2},X\}\subseteq\mathbb{M}_{2}
is the group generated by Pauli-X operator where
X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}
, and the representation is given by
\pi:G\to U(2),g\mapsto g
. Then one can show by direct calculation that

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{M_{0}}\ \text{is\ an\ operator\ system}

(10)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle M_{0}=c_{0}I+c_{1}Y+c_{2}Z,c_{0}\neq 0,c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{C},\ \text{such\ that}\ \exists c\in\mathbb{C},c_{i}=c\overline{c_{i}},i=1,2.

Thus our class of
\mathcal{V}_{M_{0}}
is larger because
M_{0}
can even be non-self-adjoint.

In
(
amosov2018_2
, Proposition 1)
, for an Abelian group
G
, assuming all the unitaries have a common eigenspace, it was shown that the projection onto that eigenspace is an anticlique. We can generalize that result in our setting.

Suppose
G
is Abelian and
\pi:G\to\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a finite dimensional projective unitary representation with
\text{dim}\ \mathcal{H}=d
, then for any
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
, we have

\pi(g_{1})\pi(g_{2})=\pi(g_{2})\pi(g_{1}).

Therefore,
\{\pi(g):g\in G\}
can be diagonalized simultaneously, i.e., there exists a basis
\{\ket{e_j}:1\leq j\leq d\}
, such that

\pi(g)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\lambda_{j}(g)\ket{e_j}\bra{e_j}

(11)

For each
g\in G
, suppose
J_{g}
is the index set such that for any
r,s\in J_{g},r\neq s
, we have
\lambda_{r}(g)\neq\lambda_{s}(g)
, i.e., it is the index set of different eigenvalues. Then the spectral decomposition can be given as

\pi(g)=\sum_{i\in J_{g}}\lambda_{i}(g)P_{i}(g),

(12)

where

P_{i}(g)=\sum_{j:\lambda_{j}(g)=\lambda_{i}(g)}\ket{e_j}\bra{e_j}

are disjoint projections onto the eigenspace corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}(g)
.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose
\mathcal{V}_{M_{0}}
defined by (
8
) is an operator system and
G
is Abelian. Then for any sequence
\{i_{g}\in J_{g}\}_{g\in G}
, if

P=\prod_{g\in G}P_{i_{g}}

has rank no less than 2, then
P
is an anticlique for
\mathcal{V}
.

Proof.

We need to prove that for any
g\in G
, we have

P\pi(g)M_{0}\pi(g)^{*}P=c(g)P

for some constant
c(g)
only depending on
g
. In fact,

\displaystyle P\pi(g)M_{0}\pi(g)^{*}P=P\sum_{i\in J_{g}}\lambda_{i}(g)P_{i}(g)M_{0}\sum_{j\in J_{g}}\overline{\lambda_{j}(g)}P_{i}(g)P

Note that given
g\in G
, for
i,j\in J_{g}
, we have
P_{i}(g)P_{j}(g)=\delta_{i,j}P_{i}(g)
. Therefore, for any
i\in J_{g}
,

\displaystyle PP_{i}(g)=P_{i}(g)P=\begin{cases}P,\ \text{if}\ i=i_{g};\\
0,\ \text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then

\displaystyle P\sum_{i\in J_{g}}\lambda_{i}(g)P_{i}(g)M_{0}\sum_{j\in J_{g}}\overline{\lambda_{j}(g)}P_{i}(g)P=|\lambda_{i_{g}}(g)|^{2}PM_{0}P.

(13)

Since
I\in\mathcal{V}_{M_{0}}
and we are in a finite-dimensional setting, there exist
g_{1},\cdots g_{m}\in G
and
c_{1},\cdots,c_{m}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{m}c_{r}\pi(g_{r})M_{0}\pi(g_{r})^{*}=I.

Multiplying
P
from left and right and using the fact that

\displaystyle P\pi(g)M_{0}\pi(g)^{*}P=|\lambda_{i_{g}}(g)|^{2}PM_{0}P,\ \forall g\in G,

we have

\displaystyle P=\sum_{r=1}^{m}c_{r}P\pi(g_{r})M_{0}\pi(g_{r})^{*}P=\sum_{r=1}^{m}c_{r}|\lambda_{i_{g_{r}}}(g_{r})|^{2}PM_{0}P.

(14)

Plug (
14
) into (
13
), we have

\displaystyle P\pi(g)M_{0}\pi(g)^{*}P

\displaystyle=P\sum_{i\in J_{g}}\lambda_{i}(g)P_{i}(g)M_{0}\sum_{j\in J_{g}}\overline{\lambda_{j}(g)}P_{i}(g)P

\displaystyle=|\lambda_{i_{g}}(g)|^{2}PM_{0}P

\displaystyle=\frac{|\lambda_{i_{g}}(g)|^{2}}{\sum_{r=1}^{m}c_{r}|\lambda_{i_{g_{r}}}(g_{r})|^{2}}P=:c(g)P

which shows that
P
is a valid anticlique.
∎

3
Stabilizer formalism and noncommutative graphs

The
stabilizer formalism
first presented in
gottesman1997
involves an Abelian subgroup
G
of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
, which is the Pauli group on
n
qubits, such that
-I_{2}^{\otimes n}\notin G
. Denote

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}=I_{2},\sigma_{1}=Z=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},\sigma_{2}=X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\sigma_{3}=Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&i\\
-i&0\end{pmatrix}.

(15)

For simplicity of notation, and as is common in the field, we let
X_{j}
denote

\displaystyle X_{j}=I_{2}\otimes\cdots I_{2}\otimes\underbrace{X}_{j-th\ component}\otimes I_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes I_{2}

and similarly for
Y_{j},Z_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq n
. The Pauli group
\mathcal{P}_{n}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{n}

\displaystyle=\langle X_{j},Y_{j},Z_{j}:1\leq j\leq n\rangle

(16)

\displaystyle=\{c\sigma_{j_{1}}\otimes\sigma_{j_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\sigma_{j_{n}}:c=\pm 1,\pm i,0\leq j_{1},\cdots,j_{n}\leq 3\}

The stabilizer code is defined as follows:

Definition 3
.

For any Abelian subgroup
G\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{n}
such that
-I_{2}^{\otimes n}\notin G
, the stabilizer code
\mathcal{C}_{G}
is defined by

\mathcal{C}_{G}:=\text{span}\{\ket{\psi}:g\ket{\psi}=\ket{\psi}\forall g\in G\}.

(17)

The well-known theorem of stabilizer formalism is the following
gottesman1997
;
nielsen
:

Theorem 4
.

For any Abelian subgroup
G\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{n}
such that
-I_{2}^{\otimes n}\notin G
, let
E\in\mathbb{M}_{2^{n}}
is an operator and denote by
P
, the projection onto the stabilizer code
\mathcal{C}_{G}
. Then

PEP=c(E)P\iff E\in\text{span}\{(\mathcal{P}_{n}\backslash N(G))\cup G\},

(18)

where
N(G)=\{h\in\mathcal{P}_{n}:hGh^{-1}=G\}
.

In the framework of noncommutative graphs, the normalizer
N(G)
also plays a great role, and we can essentially recover the stabilizer formalism via the following theorem:

Theorem 5
.

For any Abelian subgroup
G\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{n}
such that
-I_{2}^{\otimes n}\notin G
, and
M_{0}\in\mathbb{M}_{2^{n}}
, define

\mathcal{V}_{M_{0}}:=\text{span}\{gM_{0}g:g\in G\}.

(19)

Then the span of
\mathcal{V}_{M_{0}}
such that
\mathcal{V}_{M_{0}}
is an operator system coincides with all the correctable errors outside the normalizer of
G
plus identity. In other words,

\text{span}\{\mathcal{V}_{M_{0}}:M_{0}\ \text{is\ such\ that}\ \mathcal{V}_{M_{0}}\ \text{is\ an\ operator\ system}\}=\text{span}\{(\mathcal{P}_{n}\backslash N(G))\cup I_{2}^{\otimes n}\}.

(20)

Remark 2
.

Our noncommutative graph can recover all the detectable errors
E
which are not commuting with
G
. The errors commuting with
G
cannot be reflected in
\mathcal{V}_{M_{0}}
.

Fix an Abelian subgroup
G\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{n}
such that
-I_{2}^{\otimes n}\notin G
, we choose the representation
\pi:G\to\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}),g\mapsto g
.
M_{0}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
is prefixed. Then the linear subspace
\mathcal{V}_{M_{0}}
is given by

\mathcal{V}_{M_{0}}:=\text{span}\{\pi(g)M_{0}\pi(g)^{*}:g\in G\}=\text{span}\{gM_{0}g:g\in G\},

(21)

where the last equality follows from the fact that if
-I_{2}^{\otimes n}\notin G
, then
\forall g\in G,g^{*}=g
. We will adopt the following the index sets given by

\mathcal{I}_{0}:=\{0,1\},\mathcal{I}_{1}:=\{2,3\}.

(22)

then the following characterization is realized:

Lemma 1
.

Suppose
G=\langle Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{s}\rangle
for some
1\leq s\leq n
. Then
\mathcal{V}_{M_{0}}
is an operator system if and only if

\displaystyle M_{0}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{00\cdots 0}I_{2}^{\otimes n}+\sum_{\exists 1\leq r\leq s,j_{r}\in\mathcal{I}_{1}}\sum_{j_{s+1},\cdots,j_{n}=0}^{3}\alpha_{j_{1}\cdots j_{n}}\sigma_{j_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\sigma_{j_{n}}

(23)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{00\cdots 0}I_{2}^{\otimes n}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\cdots,i_{s}=0\\
i_{1}+\cdots+i_{s}\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{r}\in\mathcal{I}_{i_{r}}\\
1\leq r\leq s\end{subarray}}\sum_{j_{s+1},\cdots,j_{n}=0}^{3}\alpha_{j_{1}\cdots j_{n}}\sigma_{j_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\sigma_{j_{n}},

for
\alpha_{00\cdots 0}\neq 0
, and

\displaystyle\forall i_{1},\cdots,i_{s}=0,1,\ i_{1}+\cdots+i_{s}\neq 0,\exists c_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{s}}\in\mathbb{C},s.t.,

(24)

\displaystyle\overline{\alpha_{j_{1}\cdots j_{n}}}=c_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{s}}\alpha_{j_{1}\cdots j_{n}},\forall j_{r}\in I_{i_{r}}:1\leq r\leq s;\ j_{s+1},\cdots,j_{n}=0,1,2,3.

Proof.

Assume
M_{0}
is given by

M_{0}=\ \sum_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{n}=0}^{3}\alpha_{j_{1}\cdots j_{n}}\sigma_{j_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\sigma_{j_{n}},\ \alpha_{j_{1}\cdots j_{n}}\in\mathbb{C}.

(25)

Necessity: suppose
\mathcal{V}_{M_{0}}
is an operator system.

Step I: Implication of
I_{2}^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal{V}_{M_{0}}
: Firstly,
I_{2}^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal{V}_{M_{0}}
implies that there exists
c_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}\in\mathbb{C},i_{1},\cdots,i_{s}=0,1
, such that

\displaystyle I_{2}^{\otimes n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{s}=0}^{1}c_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}(Z_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots Z_{s}^{i_{s}})M_{0}(Z_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots Z_{s}^{i_{s}})

(26)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{s}=0}^{1}c_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}\sum_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{s}=0}^{3}\sum_{j_{s+1},\cdots,j_{n}=0}^{3}\alpha_{j_{1}\cdots j_{n}}Z^{i_{1}}\sigma_{j_{1}}Z^{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes Z^{i_{s}}\sigma_{j_{s}}Z^{i_{s}}\otimes\sigma_{j_{s+1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\sigma_{j_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{s}=0}^{1}c_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}\sum_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{s}\in\mathcal{I}_{0}}\sum_{j_{s+1},\cdots,j_{n}=0}^{3}\alpha_{j_{1}\cdots j_{n}}\sigma_{j_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\sigma_{j_{n}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{s}=0}^{1}c_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}\sum_{\exists 1\leq r\leq s,j_{r}\in\mathcal{I}_{1}}\sum_{j_{s+1},\cdots,j_{n}=0}^{3}\alpha_{j_{1}\cdots j_{n}}Z^{i_{1}}\sigma_{j_{1}}Z^{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes Z^{i_{s}}\sigma_{j_{s}}Z^{i_{s}}\otimes\sigma_{j_{s+1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\sigma_{j_{n}}.

For the last equality, we used the fact that for
i=0,1,\ j=\mathcal{I}_{0}\cup\mathcal{I}_{1}
we have

Z^{i}\sigma_{j}Z^{i}=\begin{cases}\sigma_{j},&j\in\mathcal{I}_{0};\\
(-1)^{i}\sigma_{j},&j\in\mathcal{I}_{1}.\end{cases}

(27)

Moreover, for any given
i_{1},\cdots,i_{s}=0,1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\exists 1\leq r\leq s,j_{r}\in\mathcal{I}_{1}}\sum_{j_{s+1},\cdots,j_{n}=0}^{3}\alpha_{j_{1}\cdots j_{n}}Z^{i_{1}}\sigma_{j_{1}}Z^{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes Z^{i_{s}}\sigma_{j_{s}}Z^{i_{s}}\otimes\sigma_{j_{s+1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\sigma_{j_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\sum_{1\leq r_{1}<\cdots<r_{k}\leq s}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{r_{1}},\cdots,j_{r_{k}}\in\mathcal{I}_{1}\\
j_{r}\in\mathcal{I}_{0},r\neq r_{1},\cdots,r_{k}\end{subarray}}\sum_{j_{s+1},\cdots,j_{n}=0}^{3}(-1)^{i_{r_{1}}+\cdots i_{r_{k}}}\alpha_{j_{1}\cdots j_{n}}\sigma_{j_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\sigma_{j_{n}}.

Since
\{\sigma_{j_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\sigma_{j_{n}}:0\leq j_{1},\cdots,j_{n}\leq 3\}
form an orthonormal basis, we have

a\begin{aligned} &\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{s}=0}^{1}c_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}\alpha_{00\cdots 0}=1,\\
&\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{s}=0}^{1}c_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}\alpha_{j_{1}\cdots j_{n}}=0,\ \forall j_{1},\cdots,j_{s}\in\mathcal{I}_{0},j_{1}+\cdots+j_{s}\neq 0,\\
&\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{s}=0}^{1}c_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}(-1)^{i_{r_{1}}+\cdots i_{r_{k}}}\alpha_{j_{1}\cdots j_{s}j_{s+1}\cdots j_{n}}=0,\\
&\ \forall j_{r_{1}},\cdots,j_{r_{k}}\in\mathcal{I}_{1},j_{r}\in\mathcal{I}_{0},r\neq r_{1},\cdots,r_{k}\ \text{for\ some}\ 1\leq r_{1}<\cdots<r_{k}\leq s.\end{aligned}

(28)

For the existence of
c_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}\in\mathbb{C},i_{1},\cdots,i_{s}=0,1
, note that
\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{s}=0}^{1}c_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}\alpha_{00\cdots 0}=1
implies

Fixed points of generalized contractions on O-metric spaces
Hallowed Oluwadara Olaoluwa, Aminat Olawunmi Ige, Johnson Olajire Olaleru

Abstract
The class of O-metric spaces generalize several existing metric-types in literature including metric spaces, b-metric spaces, and ultra metric spaces. In this paper, we discuss the properties of the topology induced by an O-metric and establish in the setting, fixed point theorems of contractions and generalized contractions. The proofs of the theorems rely heavily on polygon o -inequalities which are a natural generalization of the triangle inequality, and the construction of which leads to the notion of o -series following a pattern of functions. AMS Subject Classification (2010): Primary 54E99; Secondary 54E350. Keywords: O-metrics; upward metrics; polygon inequalities; generalized series; contractive sequences.

Topology on O-metric spaces

An O-metric space
(X,d_{\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}},a)
is a topological space with the

\mathcal{T}_{d_{\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}}}=\left\{A\subset X:~\forall x\in A~\exists r>0,~B_{d_{\textnormal{{o}}}}(x,r)\subset A\right\},

(1)

where

B_{d_{\textnormal{{o}}}}(x,r)=\{y\in X:~|d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(x,y)-a|<r\}

(2)

is the

centered on

The terminology “open ball” is employed for consistency with literature on metric-types only: an “open ball” may not be an open set for
\mathcal{T}_{d_{\textnormal{{o}}}}

[
3
]

(see
[

]

2.1
Conditions for Metrizability

Theorem 2.2
.

[
16
]

Let
X
be a non-empty set,
a\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
a non-negative real number,
\textnormal{{o}}:[a,\infty)\times[a,\infty)\to[a,\infty)
a binary operation on
[a,\infty)
, and
\lambda:[a,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
a function such that the following properties are satisfied:

(E_{1})

\lambda
is increasing, with
\lambda(a)=0
;

(E_{2})

\lambda(\textnormal{{o}}(u,v))=\lambda(u)+\lambda(v)~\forall u,v\geq a.

(X,d_{\textnormal{{o}}},a)
is an upward O-metric space if and only if
(X,\lambda\circ d_{\textnormal{{o}}})
is a metric space. In such case, the O-metric topology on
(X,d_{\textnormal{{o}}},a)
and the metric topology on
(X,\lambda\circ d_{\textnormal{{o}}})
coincide.

Proof.

Let
(X,d_{\textnormal{{o}}},a)
be an upward O-metric space. Under condition
(E_{1})
,
\lambda(u)=0
if and only if
u=a
, for all
u\geq a
. Let
x,y,z\in X
; we have that:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\lambda(d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(x,y))=0\Leftrightarrow d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(x,y)=a\Leftrightarrow x=y,\\
\lambda(d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(x,y))=\lambda(d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(y,x)),\\
\lambda(d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(x,z))\leq\lambda\left(\textnormal{{o}}(d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(x,y),d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(y,z))\right)=\lambda(d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(x,y))+\lambda(d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(y,z)).\end{array}

Therefore,
\lambda\circ d_{\textnormal{{o}}}
is a metric on
X
.

The converse holds: if
\lambda\circ d_{\textnormal{{o}}}
is a metric on
X
, then,
d_{\textnormal{{o}}}
is an
a
-upward O-metric on
X
. Indeed, under conditions
(E_{1})
and
(E_{2})
, the function
\lambda:[a,\infty)\to{\rm Im}(\lambda)
is bijective,
\lambda^{-1}(\lambda(u))=u
, and
\lambda^{-1}(\lambda(u)+\lambda(v))=\textnormal{{o}}(u,v)
for all
u\geq a
; therefore, if
x,y,z\in X

Proposition 2.4

3

o
-inequalities and
o
-series

One of the beauties of the notion of O-metric spaces lies in the modification of the triangle inequality axiom of a metric space to accommodate other binary operations which are not necessarily associative. The triangle
\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}
-inequality obtained becomes interesting to study when applied to more than three points.

Let
(X,d_{\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}},a)
be an O-metric space. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

be a finite sequence of
n+2
points in
X

\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}
-inequality provides many upper bounds for
d_{\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}}(x_{0},x_{n+1})
as functions of exactly
d_{\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}}(x_{0},x_{1}),d_{\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}}(x_{1},x_{2}),\ldots,d_{\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}}(x_{n},x_{n+1})
with each of
d_{\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}}(x_{0},x_{1}),d_{\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}}(x_{1},x_{2}),\ldots,d_{\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}}(x_{n},x_{n+1})
occurring exactly once, without interchanging the order. In fact, if we view
o
as a binary operation, the upper bounds exactly correspond to the expressions
d_{\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}}(x_{0},x_{1})~\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}~d_{\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}}(x_{1},x_{2})~\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}~\cdots~\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}~d_{\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}}(x_{n},x_{n+1})
which depends on how parentheses are placed. The expression
d_{\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}}(x_{0},x_{1})~\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}~d_{\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}}(x_{1},x_{2})~\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}~\cdots~\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}~d_{\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}}(x_{n},x_{n+1})
has at most
C_{n}
values, where
C_{n}:=\frac{1}{n+1}{{2n}\choose{n}}
is the
n
-th Catalan number (see
[
6

22
]
). The lemma below immediately follows:

Lemma 3.1
.

Denote by
\Omega_{n,a}
(or simply
\Omega_{n}
when no confusion arises) the set (of order at most equal to
C_{n}
) of functions
\Delta_{n}:I_{a}^{n+1}\to I_{a}
such that
\Delta_{n}(t_{0},t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})=t_{0}~\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}~t_{1}~\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}\cdots\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}~t_{n}
. The triangle
\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}
-inequalities involving
n+2

in an O-metric space

become:

d_{\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}}(x_{0},x_{n+1})\leq\Delta_{n}\left(d_{\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}}(x_{0},x_{1}),d_{\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}}(x_{1},x_{2}),\ldots,d_{\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}}(x_{n},x_{n+1})\right)~\forall\Delta_{n}\in\Omega_{n},

(11)

or simply

d_{\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}}(x_{0},x_{n+1})\leq d_{\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}}(x_{0},x_{1})~\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}~d_{\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}}(x_{1},x_{2})~\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}\cdots\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}~d_{\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}}(x_{n},x_{n+1}),

(12)

and are called polygon
\mathnormal{\mathbf{o}}
-inequalities.

Example 3.1
.

Let

be a function defined for pairs of elements in the interval

\textnormal{{o}}(u,v)=u+2v

(13)

Definition 3.2

(

(15)

d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(x_{n},x_{n+1})=d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(Tx_{n-1},Tx_{n})\leq\alpha(x_{n-1},x_{n})d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(Tx_{n-1},Tx_{n})\leq\psi(d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(x_{n-1},x_{n})).

By induction,
d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(x_{n},x_{n+1})\leq\psi^{(n)}(d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(x_{0},x_{1}))
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

If
x_{0}=x_{1}
, then
x_{0}=Tx_{0}
is a fixed point of
T
. Suppose now that
x_{0}\neq x_{1}
.

Since
d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(x_{0},x_{1})>a
, from (
36
),
\lim_{n,i\to\infty}\overset{n+i}{\underset{j=n}{\textnormal{\large{O}}}}\psi^{(j)}(d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(x_{0},x_{1}))=a
. Fix
\epsilon>a
and let
N\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\overset{\infty}{\underset{j=N}{\textnormal{\large{O}}}}\psi^{(j)}(d(x_{0},x_{1}))<\epsilon
. Let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m>n>N
.
From the polygon
o
-inequality,
d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(x_{n},x_{m})\leq\overset{m-1}{\underset{j=n}{\textnormal{\large{O}}}}\psi^{(j)}(d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(x_{0},x_{1}))<\epsilon
. Thus
\{x_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence; it converges to some
x^{*}
from the completeness from
X
.

If
T
is sequentially continuous, then
x^{*}=\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n+1}=\lim_{n\to\infty}Tx_{n}=Tx^{*}
is a fixed point of
T
. Suppose now that (ii) holds. Then
\alpha(x_{n},x^{*})\geq 1
for
n
sufficiently large, and from the triangle
o
-inequality,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(Tx^{*},x^{*})&\leq&\textnormal{{o}}(d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(Tx^{*},Tx_{n}),d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(Tx_{n},x^{*}))\\
&\leq&\textnormal{{o}}(\alpha(x_{n},x^{*})d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(Tx_{n},Tx^{*}),d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(x_{n+1},x^{*}))\\
\end{array}

By taking the limit,
d_{\textnormal{{o}}}(Tx^{*},x^{*})=a
hence
x^{*}=Tx^{*}
is a fixed point of
T
.
∎

5.1
, Theorem
5.1

, since any

and

Corollary 5.4
.

Let

be a complete b-metric space,
\alpha:X\times X\to[0,\infty)
a mapping, and

a map that is
\alpha
-admissible and such that

A complete b-metric space

,

\textnormal{{o}}(u,v)=s(u+v)

, and such that
\textnormal{{o}}(0,0)=0

\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)

44

\overset{n+i}{\underset{j=n}{\textnormal{\large{O}}}}\varphi^{(j)}(\epsilon)

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\overset{n+i}{\underset{j=n}{\textnormal{\large{O}}}}\psi^{(j)}(\epsilon)&=&\overset{i}{\underset{j=0}{\textnormal{\large{O}}}}\psi^{(n+j)}(\epsilon)\\

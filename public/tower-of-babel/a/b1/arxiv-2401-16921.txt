Effect of repeated projective measurements on a two-qubit system undergoing dephasing Preprint: APS/123-QED
Hammas Hussain Ali, Muhammad Abdullah Ijaz, Fariha Hassan, Diya Batool, Adam Zaman Chaudhry

Abstract
The entanglement dynamics of an exactly solvable, pure dephasing model are studied. Repeated projective measurements are performed on the two-qubit system. Due to the system-environment interaction, system-environment correlations are established between each measurement. Consequently, the environment state keeps evolving. We investigate the effect of this changing environment state on the entanglement dynamics. In particular, we compare the dynamics with the case where the environment state is repeatedly reset.



I
Introduction

Rapid repeated measurements can slow down the temporal evolution of quantum systems
Misra and Sudarshan 2008
. This is called the Quantum Zeno effect (QZE) and it is of great theoretical and experimental interest, especially in practical implementations of emerging quantum technologies
Kwiat et al. 1999
;
Franson et al. 2004
;
Gordon et al. 2010
;
Zhu et al. 2014
;
Chaudhry and Gong 2014
;
Facchi and Ligabo 2010
;
Smerzi 2012
;
Facchi et al. 2000
;
Xu et al. 2011
;
Zhang and Xue 2011
;
McCusker et al. 2013
. Interestingly, if the repeated measurements are not rapid enough, the temporal evolution of the quantum system can accelerate. This is called the Quantum anti-Zeno effect (QAZE)
Kofman and Kurizki 2000
;
Facchi et al. 2001
;
Koshino and Shimizu 2005
. Both QZE and QAZE have been explored in many setups, such as superconducting qubits, Josephson junctions, and nanomechanical oscillators
Zhang and Xue 2011
;
Slichter et al. 2016
;
Cao et al. 2012
;
Chen et al. 2010
;
Barone et al. 2004
. There have been studies of QZE and QAZE for a single qubit system interacting with an environment that evolves as measurements on the system are made. The environment evolves because, especially with strong system-environment coupling strength, the system and the environment can establish significant correlations; as a result, any measurement performed thereafter on the system can significantly affect the environment state
Chaudhry and Gong 2014
;
Khalid and Chaudhry 2019
;
Khan et al. 2021
;
Khan et al. 2022
. In this paper, we aim to study the dynamics of a two-qubit system that interacts with its environment. In the presence of repeated projective measurements performed on the system, the environment is repeatedly projected onto a non-equilibrium state. The subsequent system dynamics are then highly non-trivial. We study the effect of this evolving environment state for different numbers of measurements, system-environment coupling strength, and different environment characteristics.

Entanglement is the fundamental quantity we use to study the dynamics of the central two-qubit system. As is well-known, entanglement is a crucial quantum-mechanical feature that enables the use and study of non-classical physics
Nielsen and Chuang 2010
;
Beck 2012
;
Waseem et al. 2020
. The measure for entanglement used within this study is concurrence
Wootters 1998
;
Qi et al. 2017
;
Schlosshauer 2007
. We model the environment of the two-qubit system as a collection of simple harmonic oscillators. Considering the dephasing timescales to be much shorter than dissipation, we consider only a pure dephasing type interaction between the system and the environment
Chaudhry and Gong 2014
;
Chaudhry 2016
;
Chaudhry 2017
;
Mirza et al. 2023
;
Schlosshauer 2007
;
Breuer and Petruccione 2002
. Such a study of the effect of the changing environment state on the two-qubit state is especially important as two-qubit systems can display physics (such as the entanglement between the qubits) entirely absent from single-qubit systems.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we introduce the two-qubit dephasing model we use and discuss its solution for the case where the environment state is repeatedly reset after each measurement. We then examine analytically the effect of the changing environment state. In Sec.
III
, we present results for the entanglement dynamics for the case where the environment changes upon measurements and compare this evolution to the case where the environment is repeatedly reset. We also look at the effect of using different measurement intervals on the evolution of the entanglement. Finally, we present our conclusions in Sec.
IV
. Technical details of the calculations are shown in the appendices.

II
Mathematical Framework:

II.1
Pure dephasing Model

We examine a pure dephasing model with the system comprised of two qubits. This means that the system and environment do not exchange energy, and there is decoherence without dissipation. The two qubits interact with a common environment of a collection of harmonic oscillators
Chaudhry and Gong 2014
. The total Hamiltonian,
H
, is comprised of the Hamiltonian of the system, environment, and the interaction (we use dimensionless units throughout with
\hbar
= 1),

H=H_{S}+H_{E}+H_{I},

(1)

where
H_{S}=\frac{\omega_{0}}{2}(\sigma_{z}^{(1)}+\sigma_{z}^{(2)})
,
H_{E}=\sum_{r}\omega_{r}b_{r}^{\dagger}b_{r}
, and
H_{I}=(\sigma_{z}^{(1)}+\sigma_{z}^{(2)})\sum_{r}(g_{r}^{*}b_{r}+g_{r}b_{r}^{\dagger})
. Here
g_{r}
is the coupling strength between the
r^{\text{th}}
mode of the environment with frequency
\omega_{r}
and the two-qubit system, and
\omega_{0}
is the energy difference between the ground and excited state of the two-level systems. We use the eigenstates of
\sigma_{z}^{(1)}
and
\sigma_{z}^{(2)}

\ket{k,l}
, defined as

\displaystyle\sigma_{z}^{(1)}\ket{k,l}=k\ket{k,l},

\displaystyle\sigma_{z}^{(2)}\ket{k,l}=l\ket{k,l},

with
k,l=\pm 1
. We consider the case in which the system and the environment initially form a product state
Mirza et al. 2023
,

\displaystyle\rho(0)

\displaystyle=\rho_{S}(0)\otimes\rho_{E}(0),

\displaystyle=\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}\otimes\frac{e^{-\beta H_{E}}}{Z_{E}},

where
Z_{E}=\text{Tr}_{E}[e^{-\beta H_{E}}]
and we assume that the system is initially in the pure state
\ket{\psi}
and the environment is in a thermal equilibrium state. Because we have a pure dephasing model, the off-diagonal elements of the system density matrix are of primary interest. The density matrix takes the form (see Appendices
A
and
B
)

\displaystyle[\rho_{S}(t)]_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime},kl}

\displaystyle=[\rho_{S}(0)]_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime},kl}e^{-i\frac{\omega_{0}}{2}(k^{\prime}+l^{\prime}-k-l)t}

\displaystyle\times e^{-i\frac{\Delta(t)}{2}(k^{\prime}l^{\prime}-kl)}e^{-\frac{1}{4}(k+l-k^{\prime}-l^{\prime})^{2}\gamma(t)}.

(2)

Here
[\rho_{S}(0)]_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime},kl}
are the matrix elements of the initial system state and

\displaystyle\gamma(t)=\sum_{r}\frac{4|g_{r}|^{2}}{\omega_{r}^{2}}[1-\cos(\omega_r t)]\coth(\frac{\beta\omega_{r}}{2})

is the environment-induced dephasing factor. On the other hand,

\displaystyle\Delta(t)=\sum_{r}\frac{4|g_{r}|^{2}}{\omega_{r}^{2}}[\sin(\omega_r t)-\omega_{r}t],

quantifies the indirect interaction between the qubits via the common environment. To calculate the effect of the environment, we replace the summation
\sum_{r}{|g_{r}|}^{2}(\ldots)
with the integral
\int\,d\omega\,J(\omega)(\ldots)

Breuer and Petruccione 2002
. Here, we take the spectral density to be
J(\omega)=G\omega^{s}\omega_{c}^{1-s}
, where
\omega_{c}
is the cutoff frequency of the environment, and
s
is the Ohmicity parameter.
s<1
represents the sub-Ohmic regime,
s>1
is the super-Ohmic regime, and
s=1
is the Ohmic regime. Our primary quantity of interest in this paper is the entanglement between the two qubits comprising the system. To quantify this, we use the concurrence defined as
Wootters 1998
,

\displaystyle R=\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho_{S}}\tilde{\rho}_{S}\sqrt{\rho_{S}}},

(3)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{S}=(\sigma_{y}\otimes\sigma_{y})\rho_{S}(\sigma_{y}\otimes\sigma_{y}),

and the concurrence is given by
C(\rho_{S})=\text{max}\{0,\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}\}
.
\lambda_{i}
are the eigenvalues of the Hermitian matrix
R
in descending order.

II.2
Repeated Projective Measurements

In this section, we aim to incorporate repeated projective measurements on the system. It is important to note that, after each measurement, the environment is generally not in a thermal equilibrium state. We consider the time interval between the equally spaced measurement to be
\tau
. We find that the density matrix of the system and the environment after the
(N-1)^{\text{th}}
and before the
N^{\text{th}}
measurement is given by

\displaystyle\rho\big[(N-1)\tau\leq t<N\tau\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{N-1}}U[t-\tau(N-1)]\big[P_{\psi}U(\tau)\big]^{N-1}\rho(0)\big[U^{\dagger}(\tau)P_{\psi}\big]^{N-1}U^{\dagger}[t-\tau(N-1)],

(4)

\displaystyle Z_{N-1}

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{S,E}\bigg\{\big[P_{\psi}U(\tau)\big]^{N-1}\rho(0)\big[U^{\dagger}(\tau)P_{\psi}\big]^{N-1}U^{\dagger}\big[t-\tau(N-1)\big]\bigg\}.

(5)

We can define
t^{\prime}=t-\tau(N-1)
for brevity and convenience. Here,
U(t)
is the combined time-evolution operator for the system and the environment, and
P_{\psi}
is the projection operator for the measurement. Now, we find that the time evolution operator
U(t)
can be written as

\displaystyle U(t)=U_{0}(t)U_{I}(t)=e^{-i\frac{\omega_{0}}{2}(\sigma_{z}^{(1)}+\sigma_{z}^{(2)})t}e^{-iH_{E}t}U_{I}(t),

(6)

where
U_{I}(t)
is the time-evolution operator associated with the interaction Hamiltonian (for details, see Appendix
A
). Using this time-evolution operator in Eq. (
4
), with the initial system-environment state
\rho(0)=\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}\otimes\rho_{E}(0)
, where
\rho_{E}(0)
is the initial thermal equilibrium state of the environment, we can massage the density matrix to the form

[\rho_{S}(t)]_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime},kl}=\frac{e^{\frac{i}{2}\Delta(t^{\prime})(kl-k^{\prime}l^{\prime})}}{Z_{N-1}}\text{T}_{\text{env}}\text{T}_{\text{sys}},

(7)

where

\displaystyle\text{T}_{\text{env}}=

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{E}\big\{\big[\bra{\psi}U(\tau)\ket{\psi}\big]^{N-1}\rho_{E}(0)

\displaystyle\times\big[\bra{\psi}U^{\dagger}(\tau)\ket{\psi}\big]^{N-1}e^{-R_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})}\big\},

\displaystyle\text{T}_{\text{sys}}=

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{S}\big\{P_{\psi}P_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}\}.

We have defined
P_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}=\ket{k,l}\bra{k',l'}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle R_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}(k+l-k^{\prime}-l^{\prime})\sum_{r}[\alpha_{r}(t^{\prime})b_{r}^{\dagger}-\alpha^{*}_{r}(t^{\prime})b_{r}],

where

\displaystyle\alpha_{r}(t^{\prime})=\frac{2g_{r}(1-e^{i\omega_{r}t^{\prime}})}{\omega_{r}}.

This result can be simplified as detailed in Appendix
C
. We can then study the dynamics of the system in the presence of repeated measurements. It must be noted that the environment state keeps changing throughout. This change is encapsulated in the form of the phase factors
\gamma_{ij},\mu_{ij},\epsilon_{ij}
and
\sigma_{ij}
which emerge from
T_{\text{Env}}
(see Appendix
C
). The complete form of
[\rho_{S}(t)]_{{k^{\prime}l^{\prime},kl}}
containing these factors is shown in Eq. (
28
). These factors accumulate with the number of measurements and show dependence on the time interval between measurements. This is to be contrasted with the scenario where we assume that the environment state is reset after each measurement on the system. In such a case, the system dynamics are given simply by Eq. (

IV
Conclusion

In this study, we explore the entanglement dynamics of a two-qubit system interacting with a harmonic oscillator environment, with repeated projective measurements performed on the system. To this end, we calculated the post-measurement system states for two cases. In one of the cases, the environment was reset to the thermal equilibrium state every time a measurement was made. The second case was where the environment did not remain in the thermal equilibrium state as measurements on the system were made. In fact, the system and the environment correlate as a consequence of the system-environment interaction. As a result, any measurement performed on the system affects the environment. Consequently, repeated measurements performed on the system keep changing the environment state. We simulated and compared the entanglement evolution for the scenario where the environment is repeatedly reset to the scenario where the aforementioned environment evolution is considered. We showed that the entanglement dynamics can be significantly different. By altering the measurement interval, we also investigated how the system dynamics are gradually frozen as we reduce the measurement interval. Our work is the first step towards understanding the role of system-environment correlations in studies of the quantum Zeno and anti-Zeno effects in multiqubit systems.

Appendix A
Unitary Time Evolution Operator

Here, we present a derivation of the unitary time evolution operator,
U(t)
, using the total Hamiltonian given by

H=H_{S}+H_{E}+H_{I},

where

H_{S}=\frac{\omega_{0}}{2}(\sigma_{z}^{(1)}+\sigma_{z}^{(2)}),

H_{E}=\sum_{k}\omega_{k}b_{k}^{\dagger}b_{k},

and

H_{I}=(\sigma_{z}^{(1)}+\sigma_{z}^{(2)})\sum_{r}(g_{r}^{*}b_{r}+g_{r}b_{r}^{\dagger}).

Let us first transform to the interaction picture. That is, with
U_{0}(t)=e^{-i(H_{E}+H_{S})t}
, we find
H_{I}(t)
to be

\displaystyle H_{I}(t)

\displaystyle=U_{0}(t)^{\dagger}H_{I}U_{0}(t)

\displaystyle=(\sigma_{z}^{(1)}+\sigma_{z}^{(2)})\sum_{r}(g_{r}^{*}b_{r}e^{-i\omega_{r}t}+g_{r}b_{r}^{\dagger}e^{i\omega_{r}t}).

We now move on to find the time evolution operator in the interaction picture,
U_{I}(t)
, using the Magnus expansion
Magnus 1954
;
Mirza et al. 2021
;
Blanes et al. 2010
,

U_{I}(t)=e^{\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}A_{i}(t)},

(10)

where

\displaystyle A_{1}=-i\int_{0}^{t}H_{I}(t_{1})dt_{1},

\displaystyle A_{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}dt_{1}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}dt_{2}\,[H_{I}(t_{1}),H_{I}(t_{2})].

It can be shown that

\displaystyle A_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{z}^{(1)}+\sigma_{z}^{(2)})\sum_{r}(\alpha_{r}(t)b_{r}^{\dagger}-\alpha^{*}_{r}(t)b_{r}),

\displaystyle A_{2}=-\frac{i}{2}(\mathds{1}+\sigma_{z}^{(1)}\sigma_{z}^{(2)})\Delta(t),

where

\displaystyle\Delta(t)=\sum_{r}\frac{4|g_{r}|^{2}}{\omega_{r}^{2}}[\sin(\omega_r t)-\omega_{r}t]

and

\alpha_{r}(t)=\frac{2g_{r}\left(1-e^{i\omega_{r}t}\right)}{\omega_{r}}.

One can also check that all subsequent terms,
A_{3},A_{4},...
, are zero.
Consequently, the total unitary time evolution operator takes the form

U(t)=U_{0}U_{I}(t)=e^{-i\frac{\omega_{o}}{2}(\sigma_{z}^{(1)}+\sigma_{z}^{(2)})t}e^{-iH_{E}t}U_{I}(t).

(11)

Appendix B
Reset The Environment

We now calculate the density matrix in the presence of measurements, but where the environment is repeatedly assumed to reset to the thermal equilibrium state. We start with

\rho_{S}(t)=\text{Tr}_{E}\left[U(t)\rho(0)U^{\dagger}(t)\right].

(12)

We want to express this density matrix in the eigenbasis of
\sigma_{z}^{(1)}
and
\sigma_{z}^{(2)}
given by
\sigma_{z}^{(1)}|k,l\rangle=k|k,l\rangle
and
\sigma_{z}^{(2)}|k,l\rangle=l|k,l\rangle
. To do this we introduce the operator
P_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}=\ket{k,l}\bra{k',l'}
. We can then write

\displaystyle[\rho_{S}(t)]_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime},kl}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}_{S,E}[U(t)\rho(0)U^{\dagger}(t)P_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}_{S,E}[\rho(0)P_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}(t)].

Here
P_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}(t)=U^{\dagger}(t)P_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}U(t)
. We now use the form of the unitary time evolution operator we found previously in Eq. (
11
) to show that
P_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}(t)=e^{i\frac{\omega_{0}}{2}\left(k+l-k^{\prime}-l^{\prime}\right)t}e^{i\frac{\Delta(t)}{2}\left(kl-k^{\prime}l^{\prime}\right)}e^{-R_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}(t)}P_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}
. Note that

\displaystyle R_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(k+l-k^{\prime}-l^{\prime}\right)\sum_{r}\left[\alpha_{r}(t)b_{r}^{\dagger}-\alpha_{r}^{*}(t)b_{r}\right].

Assembling all these pieces, we can write the density operator of the system as

\displaystyle\left[\rho_{S}(t)\right]_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime},kl}

\displaystyle=e^{-i\frac{\omega_{0}}{2}\left(k^{\prime}+l^{\prime}-k-l\right)t}e^{-i\frac{\Delta(t)}{2}\left(k^{\prime}l^{\prime}-kl\right)}

(13)

\displaystyle\times\operatorname{Tr}_{S,E}\left[\rho(0)e^{-R_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}(t)}P_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}\right].

As we have discussed before, we are interested in the case where
\rho(0)=\rho_{S}(0)\otimes\rho_{E}(0)
, where
\rho_{E}(0)=\rho_{E}=e^{-\beta H_{E}}/Z_{E}
(we assume the environment remains unchanged after each measurement), with
\beta
the inverse temperature and
Z_{E}=\operatorname{Tr}_{E}\left[e^{-\beta H_{E}}\right]
. This leads to

\displaystyle{\left[\rho_{S}(t)\right]_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime},kl}}

\displaystyle=\left[\rho_{S}(0)\right]_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime},kl}e^{-i\frac{\omega_{0}}{2}\left(k^{\prime}+l^{\prime}-k-l\right)t}

(14)

\displaystyle\times e^{-i\frac{\Delta(t)}{2}\left(k^{\prime}l^{\prime}-kl\right)}\operatorname{Tr}_{E}\left[\rho_{E}(0)e^{-R_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}(t)}\right].

Notice that
\operatorname{Tr}_{E}\left[\rho_{E}e^{-R_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}(t)}\right]=\left\langle e^{-R_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}(t)}\right\rangle
. This can be simplified as

\displaystyle\left\langle e^{-R_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}(t)}\right\rangle=e^{-\frac{1}{4}\left(k+l-k^{\prime}-l^{\prime}\right)^{2}\gamma(t)},

where

\gamma(t)=\sum_{r}\frac{4\left|g_{r}\right|^{2}}{\omega_{r}^{2}}\left[1-\cos\left(\omega_{r}t\right)\right]\operatorname{\coth}\left(\frac{\beta\omega_{r}}{2}\right).

Here, we have used the form of the Bose-Einstein distribution. We converted
\Delta(t)
and
\gamma(t)
into integrals using the spectral density function to perform numerical calculations. Finally, we can write the system density operator as

\displaystyle\left[\rho_{S}(t)\right]_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime},kl}

\displaystyle=\left[\rho_{S}(0)\right]_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime},kl}e^{-i\frac{\omega_{0}}{2}\left(k^{\prime}+l^{\prime}-k-l\right)t}

(15)

\displaystyle\times e^{-i\frac{\Delta(t)}{2}\left(k^{\prime}l^{\prime}-kl\right)}e^{-\frac{1}{4}\left(k+l-k^{\prime}-l^{\prime}\right)^{2}\gamma(t)}

The system density operator presented above is for the case where whenever a measurement is made, the system collapses to a projection state, and the environment is reset to its thermal equilibrium state. However, this is not the scenario we are interested in.
We want to study the more realistic scenario (especially in the strong system-environment coupling regime) in which the environment changes over time with measurements.

Appendix C
Keeping Track of The Changing Environment State

To start the calculation for the case where we keep track of the changing environment state with the measurements performed, we start with the fact that the total system-environment density matrix after
N-1
measurements is

\displaystyle\rho\big((N-1)\tau\leq t<N\tau\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{N-1}}U\big(t-\tau(N-1)\big)\big[P_{\psi}U(\tau)\big]^{N-1}\rho(0)\big[U^{\dagger}(\tau)P_{\psi}\big]^{N-1}U^{\dagger}\big(t-\tau(N-1)\big),

(16)

\displaystyle Z_{N-1}

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{S,E}\bigg\{\big[P_{\psi}U(\tau)\big]^{N-1}\rho(0)\big[U^{\dagger}(\tau)P_{\psi}\big]^{N-1}U^{\dagger}\big(t-\tau(N-1)\big)\bigg\}.

(17)

N
is an integer not less than one. Here,
U(t)
is the total system-environment time-evolution operator we derived previously, and
P_{\psi}
is the projection operator onto the state
\ket{\psi}
. We also define
t^{\prime}=t-(N-1)\tau
. Now, tracing out the environment, we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{S}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z_{N-1}}\text{Tr}_{E}\big\{U\big(t^{\prime}\big)\big[P_{\psi}U(\tau)\big]^{N-1}

(18)

\displaystyle\times\rho(0)\big[U^{\dagger}(\tau)P_{\psi}\big]^{N-1}U^{\dagger}\big(t^{\prime}\big)\big\}.

As we have done before, we would now like to extract the elements of the system density matrix by

\displaystyle[\rho_{S}(t)]_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime},kl}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z_{N-1}}\text{Tr}_{S,E}\big\{\big[P_{\psi}U(\tau)\big]^{N-1}\rho(0)

(19)

\displaystyle\times\big[U^{\dagger}(\tau)P_{\psi}\big]^{N-1}P_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\big\}.

Now, we assume that the initial density matrix is a product state initially, that is,

\rho(0)=P_{\psi}\otimes\rho_{E}(0)
, where
\rho_{E}(0)
is environment thermal equilibrium state. This does not mean the environment state will always be in this thermal equilibrium state. Now, by an inductive proof, one can show that Eq. (
19
) becomes

\displaystyle{[\rho_{S}(t)]}_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime},kl}=\frac{e^{\frac{i}{2}\Delta(t^{\prime})(kl-k^{\prime}l^{\prime})}}{Z_{N-1}}\text{Tr}_{S,E}\big\{\ket{\psi}\big[\bra{\psi}U(\tau)\ket{\psi}\big]^{N-1}\rho_{E}(0)\big[\bra{\psi}U^{\dagger}(\tau)\ket{\psi}\big]^{N-1}\bra{\psi}P_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}e^{-R_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})}\big\}.

We can express this as a product of a trace over the environment and a trace over the system,

[\rho_{S}(t)]_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime},kl}=\frac{e^{\frac{i}{2}\Delta(t^{\prime})(kl-k^{\prime}l^{\prime})}}{Z_{N-1}}\text{T}_{\text{env}}\text{T}_{\text{sys}},

(20)

where
\text{T}_{\text{env}}
takes the form,

\displaystyle\text{T}_{\text{env}}=

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{E}\big\{\big[\bra{\psi}U(\tau)\ket{\psi}\big]^{N-1}\rho_{E}(0)

\displaystyle\times\big[\bra{\psi}U^{\dagger}(\tau)\ket{\psi}\big]^{N-1}e^{-R_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})}\big\}.

On the other hand,
\text{T}_{\text{sys}}
takes the form,

\displaystyle\text{T}_{\text{sys}}=

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{S}\big\{P_{\psi}P_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}\}.

To make further progress, we note that

\bra{\psi}U(\tau)\ket{\psi}=e^{-iH_{E}\tau}X(\tau),

(21)

where
X(\tau)=\sum_{m,n}\left|\left\langle m,n|\psi\right\rangle\right|^{2}e^{-\frac{i}{2}(1+mn)\Delta(\tau)+R^{m,n}(\tau)}
and
R^{m,n}=\frac{1}{2}(m+n)\sum_{r}[\alpha_{r}(\tau)b_{r}^{\dagger}-\alpha^{*}_{r}(\tau)b_{r}]
. Note that this quantity is comprised of environment operators only.
Consequently, it follows,

[\rho_{S}(t)]_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime},kl}=\bra{\psi}\ket{k,l}\bra{k',l'}\ket{\psi}\frac{e^{\frac{i}{2}\Delta(t^{\prime})(kl-k^{\prime}l^{\prime})}}{Z_{N-1}}\text{Tr}_{E}\big\{\rho_{E}(0)\big[X^{\dagger}(\tau)e^{iH_{E}\tau}]^{N-1}e^{-R_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})}\big[e^{-iH_{E}\tau}X(\tau)]^{N-1}\big\}.

(22)

To proceed, we now deal with terms inside the trace in (
22
). We start with

\big[X^{\dagger}(\tau)e^{iH_{E}\tau}]^{N-1}
. This can be shown to equal,

e^{-iH_{E}\tau}X^{\dagger}_{1}(\tau)X^{\dagger}_{2}(\tau)\cdots X^{\dagger}_{N-1}e^{iNH_{E}\tau},

(23)

where

\displaystyle X^{\dagger}_{p}(\tau)

\displaystyle=e^{piH_{E}\tau}X^{\dagger}e^{-piH_{E}\tau}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n}|\bra{m,n}\ket{\psi}|^{2}e^{\frac{-i}{2}(1+mn)\Delta(\tau)+R^{m,n}_{p}(\tau)},

\displaystyle R^{m,n}_{p}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(m+n)\sum_{r}\big[\alpha_{r,p}(\tau)b_{r}^{\dagger}-\alpha_{r,p}^{*}(\tau)b_{r}\big],

and

\displaystyle\alpha_{r,p}(\tau)=\frac{2g_{r}e^{ip\omega_{r}\tau}\left(1-e^{i\omega_{r}\tau}\right)}{\omega_{r}}.

Similarly, we can deal with
\big[e^{-iH_{E}\tau}X(\tau)]^{N-1}
to get,

e^{-iNH_{E}\tau}X_{N-1}(\tau)X_{N-2}(\tau)\cdots X_{1}e^{iH_{E}\tau}.

(24)

We now use the two previous results and the cyclic invariance property of the trace to get,

\displaystyle[\rho_{S}(t)]_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime},kl}=

\displaystyle\psi^{*}_{k,l}\psi_{k^{\prime},l^{\prime}}\frac{e^{\frac{i}{2}\Delta(t^{\prime})(kl-k^{\prime}l^{\prime})}}{Z_{N-1}}

(25)

\displaystyle\times\text{Tr}_{E}\big\{\rho_{E}(0)X^{\dagger}_{1}(\tau)\cdots X^{\dagger}_{N-1}e^{iNH_{E}\tau}e^{-R_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})}e^{-iNH_{E}\tau}X_{N-1}(\tau)\cdots X_{1}\},

where for the sake of brevity
\bra{\psi}\ket{k,l}\bra{k',l'}\ket{\psi}=\psi^{*}_{k,l}\psi_{k^{\prime},l^{\prime}}
.

We also combine
e^{iNH_{E}\tau}e^{-R_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})}e^{-iNH_{E}\tau}
into a single exponential to get

e^{iNH_{E}\tau}e^{-R_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})}e^{-iNH_{E}\tau}=e^{-\tilde{R}_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime},N}(t^{\prime},\tau)},

where
\tilde{R}_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime},N}(t^{\prime},\tau)
is

\frac{1}{2}(k+l-k^{\prime}-l^{\prime})\sum_{r}(\tilde{\alpha}_{r,N}(t^{\prime}\tau)b_{r}^{\dagger}-\tilde{\alpha}^{*}_{r,N}(t^{\prime},\tau)b_{r})

and

\displaystyle\tilde{\alpha}_{r,N}(t^{\prime},\tau)=e^{i\omega_{r}N\tau}\alpha_{r}(t^{\prime}).

To proceed, we now attempt to combine
X^{\dagger}_{1}(\tau)\cdots X^{\dagger}_{N-1}(\tau)
into a single exponential. Combining each of these gives rise to phase factors of the form,

\displaystyle\frac{[R^{x,y}_{p}(\tau),R^{z,h}_{p^{\prime}}(\tau)]}{2}=\frac{i(x+y)(z+h)\mu_{pp^{\prime}}(\tau)}{2}.

We have that
\mu_{pp^{\prime}}(\tau)=\sum_{r}\frac{4|g_{r}|^{2}}{\omega_{r}}\sin[(p-p^{\prime})\omega_{r}\tau\big]\big[1-\cos(\omega_r \tau)\big]
.
Notice that
\mu_{pp^{\prime}}=-\mu_{p^{\prime}p}
and
\mu_{pp}=0
. Luckily, phase factors apart from the one above disappear because
\mu_{pp^{\prime}}
is a c-number.
Consequently, we have

\displaystyle X^{\dagger}_{1}(\tau)\cdots X^{\dagger}_{N-1}(\tau)=

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n}\cdots\sum_{u,v}e^{\frac{i}{2}\Delta(\tau)(mn+.....+uv)}\psi^{2}_{mn}\cdots\psi^{2}_{uv}e^{-R^{m,n}_{1}.....-R^{u,v}_{N-1}}

(26)

\displaystyle\times e^{\frac{i}{2}(p+q)(m+n)\mu_{12}+\frac{i}{2}(s+t)[(m+n)\mu_{13}+(p+q)\mu_{23}]+....+\frac{i}{2}(u+v)[(m+n)\mu_{1(N-1)}+....+(e+f)\mu_{(N-2)(N-1)}]},

where we have defined terms like
\left|\bra{m,n}\ket{\psi}\right|^{2}
as
\psi^{2}_{mn}
. This expression needs some explanation.
\mu_{pp^{\prime}}
is a factor picked up for each term combined in
X^{\dagger}_{1}(\tau)\cdots X^{\dagger}_{N-1}
and
p<p^{\prime}
always. Each combined term has associated indices that also appear in the phase factors. For example, for
X^{\dagger}_{1}(\tau)
and
X^{\dagger}_{N-1}
, we have the indices
(m,n)
and
(u,v)
. Similarly, we have

\displaystyle X_{N-1}(\tau)\cdots X_{1}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u^{\prime},v^{\prime}}\cdots\sum_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}e^{-\frac{i}{2}\Delta(\tau)(u^{\prime}v^{\prime}+....+m^{\prime}n^{\prime})}\psi^{2}_{u^{\prime}v^{\prime}}\cdots\psi^{2}_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}e^{R^{u^{\prime},v^{\prime}}_{N-1}+....+R^{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}_{1}}

(27)

\displaystyle\times e^{-\frac{i}{2}(u^{\prime}+v^{\prime})(b^{\prime}+c^{\prime})\mu_{(N-2)(N-1)}\cdots-\frac{i}{2}(m^{\prime}+n^{\prime})[(u^{\prime}+v^{\prime})\mu_{1(N-1)}+....+(p^{\prime}+q^{\prime})\mu_{12}]}.

Here, we have used primed indices when combining terms. The summations and phase factors can be taken out of the trace in Eq. (
25
); that leaves us with

\text{Tr}_{E}\big\{\rho_{E}(0)e^{-R^{m,n}_{1}....-R^{u,v}_{N-1}}e^{\tilde{R}_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime},N}}e^{R^{u^{\prime},v^{\prime}}_{N-1}+....+R^{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}_{1}}\big\}.

We combine the first exponent in the trace with the exponent term in the center. We will need to deal with phase factors of the form,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[-R_{p}^{w,x},-\tilde{R}_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime},N}].

This daunting-looking commutator takes the form,

\displaystyle(w+x)(k+l-k^{\prime}-l^{\prime})\sigma_{p,N}(t^{\prime},\tau)=

\displaystyle(w+x)(k+l-k^{\prime}-l^{\prime})\sum_{r}\frac{2i|g_{r}|^{2}}{w_{r}^{2}}\bigg\{-\sin[\omega_{r}(p-N)\tau-\omega_{r}t^{\prime}\big]

\displaystyle-\sin[\omega_{r}(p-N+1)\tau\big]+\sin[\omega_{r}(p-N)\tau\big]+\sin[\omega_{r}(p-N+1)\tau-\omega_{r}t^{\prime}\big]\bigg\}.

That leaves us with the following scenario inside the trace,

\text{Tr}_{E}\big\{\rho_{E}(0)e^{-R^{m,n}_{1}....-R^{u,v}_{N-1}+\tilde{R}_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime},N}}e^{R^{u^{\prime},v^{\prime}}_{N-1}+....+R^{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}_{1}}\big\}.

Note that we have not written down the phase factors for now. Finally, we combine the exponents in this expression as well. We must use the previously defined
\mu(\tau)
and
\sigma(t^{\prime},\tau)
. The form of the trace now becomes,

\text{Tr}_{E}\bigg\{\rho_{E}(0)e^{-R_{1}^{m,n}....-R_{N-1}^{u,v}+\tilde{R}_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime},N}+R_{N-1}^{u^{\prime},v^{\prime}}+....+R_{1}^{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}}\bigg\}.

Now, we group the primed and unprimed indices,

e^{(-R_{1}^{m,n}+R_{1}^{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}})+(-R_{2}^{p,q}+R_{2}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}).....(-R_{N-1}^{u,v}+R_{N-1}^{u^{\prime},v^{\prime}})-\tilde{R}_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime},N}}

and define, for example,
R_{1}^{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}-R_{1}^{m,n}=\frac{1}{2}[m^{\prime}+n^{\prime}-m-n]R_{1}
as
R_{1}^{m^{\prime},n^{\prime},m,n}
. This gives us

\text{Tr}_{E}\bigg\{\rho_{E}(0)e^{R_{1}^{m^{\prime},n^{\prime},m,n}+R_{2}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},p,q}+....R_{N-1}^{u^{\prime},v^{\prime},u,v}-\tilde{R}_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime},N}}\bigg\}.

For our purposes,
\rho_{E}(0)=\frac{e^{-\beta H_{E}}}{Z_{E}}
and
Z_{E}=\operatorname{Tr}_{E}\left[e^{-\beta H_{E}}\right]
. This allows us to carry out steps similar to the ones after Eq. (
14
). This gives us four terms
\big<R_{p}R_{p^{\prime}}+R_{p^{\prime}}R_{p}\big>
,
\big<R_{p}^{2}\big>
,
\big<\tilde{R}^{2}\big>
, and
\big<\tilde{R}R_{p}+R_{p}\tilde{R}\big>
, ignoring some indices. Note that all these terms have a sum over all modes. The first term
\big<R_{p}R_{p^{\prime}}+R_{p^{\prime}}R_{p}\big>
is defined as
-4\gamma_{pp^{\prime}}(\tau)
. We have that

\gamma_{pp^{\prime}}(\tau)=\sum_{r}\frac{4|g_{r}|^{2}}{w_{r}^{2}}\bigg\{[1-\cos(\omega_r\tau)]\cos[\omega_{r}\tau(p-p^{\prime})]\coth\bigg[\frac{\beta\omega_{r}}{2}\bigg]\bigg\}.

Observe that
\gamma_{pp^{\prime}}=\gamma_{p^{\prime}p}
. Next, we obtain
\big<R_{p}^{2}\big>
as,

\sum_{r}|\alpha_{r}^{2}|\coth\bigg\{\frac{\beta\omega_{r}}{2}\bigg\}\equiv 2\gamma(\tau).

Now, we have
\big<\tilde{R}^{2}\big>
which becomes,

-\sum_{r}|\tilde{\alpha}_{r,N}|\coth\bigg(\frac{\beta\omega_{k}}{2}\bigg)\equiv-2\gamma(t^{\prime}).

The final term in our expression,
\big<\tilde{R}_{N}R_{p}+R_{p}\tilde{R}_{N}\big>
is obtained as

\displaystyle-8\sum_{r}\frac{|g_{r}|^{2}}{\omega_{r^{2}}}\coth\bigg(\frac{\beta\omega_{r}}{2}\bigg)\bigg\{\cos[(p-N)\omega_{r}\tau]-

\displaystyle\cos[(p-N+1)\omega_{r}\tau]-\cos[(p-N)\omega_{r}\tau-\omega_{r}t^{\prime}]

\displaystyle+\cos[(p-N+1)\omega_{r}\tau-\omega_{r}t^{\prime}]\bigg\}.

We define this term as
-2\epsilon_{p,N}(t^{\prime},\tau)
.
The four terms above can be made into integrals using the spectral density, as was done previously.

Finally, we have all the ingredients to write the general expression of the density matrix of the two qubits at any time after the
(N-1)
th
measurement and before the
N
th
measurement:

\displaystyle[\rho_{S}(t)]_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime},k,l}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z_{N-1}}\psi_{k^{\prime},l^{\prime}}\psi^{*}_{k,l}e^{\frac{i}{2}\Delta(t^{\prime})(kl-k^{\prime}l^{\prime})}\sum_{mn}....\sum_{uv}\sum_{u^{\prime}v^{\prime}}....\sum_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}\psi_{mn}^{2}....\psi_{uv}^{2}\psi_{u^{\prime}v^{\prime}}^{2}....\psi_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}^{2}

(28)

\displaystyle\times e^{i\mu_{12}(\tau)[(m+n)(p+q)-(m^{\prime}+n^{\prime})(p^{\prime}+q^{\prime})+(m^{\prime}+n^{\prime})(p+q)-(m+n)(p^{\prime}+q^{\prime})]}....

\displaystyle\times e^{i\mu_{(N-2)(N-1)}(\tau)[(b+c)(u+v)-(b^{\prime}+c^{\prime})(u^{\prime}+v^{\prime})+(b^{\prime}+c^{\prime})(u+v)-(b+c)(u^{\prime}+v^{\prime})]}

\displaystyle\times e^{-\frac{1}{4}[(m^{\prime}+n^{\prime}-m-n)^{2}....(u^{\prime}+v^{\prime}-u-v)^{2}]\gamma(\tau)}e^{\frac{i}{2}\Delta(\tau)(mn-m^{\prime}n^{\prime}+....uv-u^{\prime}v^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\times e^{-\frac{1}{4}[(m^{\prime}+n^{\prime}-m-n)(p^{\prime}+q^{\prime}-p-q)\gamma_{12}(\tau)]}....e^{-\frac{1}{4}[(b^{\prime}+c^{\prime}-b-c)(u^{\prime}+v^{\prime}-u-v)]\gamma_{(N-2)(N-1)}(\tau)}

\displaystyle\times e^{-\frac{1}{4}[(k+l-k^{\prime}-l^{\prime})^{2}]\gamma(t^{\prime})}e^{-\frac{1}{4}(k+l-k^{\prime}-l^{\prime})[(m^{\prime}+n^{\prime}-m-n)\epsilon_{1,N}(t^{\prime},\tau)+....(u^{\prime}+v^{\prime}-u-v)\epsilon_{N-1,N}(t^{\prime},\tau)]}

\displaystyle\times e^{\frac{1}{2}(k+l-k^{\prime}-l^{\prime})[(m^{\prime}+n^{\prime}+m+n)\sigma_{1,N}(t^{\prime},\tau)+....(u^{\prime}+v^{\prime}+u+v)\sigma_{N-1,N}(t^{\prime},\tau)]},

where

\displaystyle Z_{N-1}=

\displaystyle\sum_{mn}....\sum_{uv}\sum_{u^{\prime}v^{\prime}}....\sum_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}\psi_{mn}^{2}....\psi_{uv}^{2}\psi_{u^{\prime}v^{\prime}}^{2}....\psi_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}^{2}

(29)

\displaystyle\times e^{i\mu_{12}(\tau)[(m+n)(p+q)-(m^{\prime}+n^{\prime})(p^{\prime}+q^{\prime})+(m^{\prime}+n^{\prime})(p+q)-(m+n)(p^{\prime}+q^{\prime})]}....

\displaystyle\times e^{i\mu_{(N-2)(N-1)}(\tau)[(b+c)(u+v)-(b^{\prime}+c^{\prime})(u^{\prime}+v^{\prime})+(b^{\prime}+c^{\prime})(u+v)-(b+c)(u^{\prime}+v^{\prime})]}

\displaystyle\times e^{-\frac{1}{4}[(m^{\prime}+n^{\prime}-m-n)^{2}....(u^{\prime}+v^{\prime}-u-v)^{2}]\gamma(\tau)}e^{\frac{i}{2}\Delta(\tau)(mn-m^{\prime}n^{\prime}+....uv-u^{\prime}v^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\times e^{-\frac{1}{4}[(m^{\prime}+n^{\prime}-m-n)(p^{\prime}+q^{\prime}-p-q)\gamma_{12}(\tau)]}....e^{-\frac{1}{4}[(b^{\prime}+c^{\prime}-b-c)(u^{\prime}+v^{\prime}-u-v)]\gamma_{(N-2)(N-1)}(\tau)}.

Let us examine closely the two final expressions. Remember that
(b,c)
and
(u,v)
correspond to the
(N-2)
th
and the
(N-1)
th
measurement respectively, and
(m,n)
and
(p,q)
correspond to the first and second measurements respectively. We have a sum over the indices
(m,n)
and
(p,q)
up to
(b,c)
and
(u,v)
. Similarly, there is a sum over the same indices but primed. There are projection factors like
\psi^{2}_{mn}
for all primed and non-primed indices. In terms like
\mu_{jk}
, the subscript is determined by forming pairs of numbers from 1 to
N-1
. This pair will be of the form
(j,k)
, where
j<k
. What about the factors multiplying the
\mu_{jk}
terms in the exponent? Consider
\mu_{12}
. The factors that get multiplied are primed and non-primed indices associated with “1” and “2” in the subscript, but these factors follow the pattern in the expressions above. This is how the
\gamma_{jk}
terms also operate. For
\epsilon_{jN}
and
\sigma_{jN}
, the second subscript,
N
, is fixed, but
j
can go from 1 to
N-1
. The factors that tag along with these terms are again comprised of their respective indices according to the pattern above. Essentially,
\gamma_{jk}
,
\epsilon_{jk}
,
\sigma_{jk}
, and
\mu_{jk}
allow us to take into account the impact of measurement on decoherence of the system as the environment keeps evolving due to measurement. The
\Delta(t^{\prime})
and
\Delta(\tau)
terms quantify the indirect interaction between the qubits due to the common environment. Finally, the normalization factor of
Z_{N-1}
is in the denominator. It has a sum over the same indices as the numerator and has the same terms as the numerator, except that the terms that depend on
t^{\prime}
are absent.

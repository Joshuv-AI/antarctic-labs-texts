Construction of ( σ , δ ) (\sigma,\delta) -cyclic codes over a non-chain ring and their applications in DNA codes
Ashutosh Singh, Priyanka Sharma and Om Prakash ∗

Abstract
For a prime p p and a positive integer m m , let 𝔽 p m \mathbb{F}_{p^{m}} be the finite field of characteristic p p , and ℜ l := 𝔽 p m ​ [ v ] / ⟨ v l − v ⟩ \mathfrak{R}_{l}:=\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}[v]/\langle v^{l}-v\rangle be a non-chain ring. In this paper, we study the ( σ , δ ) (\sigma,\delta) -cyclic codes over ℜ l \mathfrak{R}_{l} . Further, we study the application of these codes in finding DNA codes. Towards this, we first define a Gray map to find classical codes over 𝔽 p m \mathbb{F}_{p^{m}} using codes over the ring ℜ l \mathfrak{R}_{l} . Later, we find the conditions for a code to be reversible and a DNA code using ( σ , δ ) (\sigma,\delta) -cyclic code. Finally, this algebraic method provides many classical and DNA codes of better parameters.

Preliminary

Let
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
be the field of characteristic
p
where
p
is a prime and
m
is a positive integer. Consider the ring
\mathfrak{R}_{l}=\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}[v]/\langle v^{l}-v\rangle
, where
l\geq 2
. Thus
\mathfrak{R}_{l}
is a non-chain ring, and its elements are of the form
a_{0}+va_{1}+\cdots+v^{l-1}a_{l-1}
where
a_{i}
’s are in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
.

Given a primitive element
\omega
of the field
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
and
\varsigma=\omega^{\frac{p^{m}-1}{l-1}}
where
(l-1)|(p^{m}-1)
, consider

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}

\displaystyle=1-v^{l-1},

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{l-1}(v+v^{2}+\cdots+v^{l-2}+v^{l-1}),

\displaystyle\gamma_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{l-1}(\varsigma v+\varsigma^{2}v^{2}+\cdots+\varsigma^{l-2}v^{l-2}+v^{l-1}),

\displaystyle\gamma_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{l-1}(\varsigma^{2}v+(\varsigma^{2})^{2}v^{2}+\cdots+(\varsigma^{2})^{l-2}v^{l-2}+v^{l-1}),

\displaystyle\gamma_{l}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{l-1}(\varsigma^{l-2}v+(\varsigma^{l-2})^{2}v^{2}+\cdots+(\varsigma^{l-2})^{l-2}v^{l-2}+v^{l-1}).

Then, we get

\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j}=\begin{cases}\gamma_{i},&\text{if $i=j$}\\
0,&\text{if $i\neq j$}\end{cases}\text{ and }\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}=1\text{ for all $1\leq i,j\leq l$};

and the set
\{\gamma_{i}:1\leq i\leq l\}
contains pairwise orthogonal idempotent elements.

Therefore, by Chinese remainder theorem, we can decompose the ring
\mathfrak{R}_{l}
as follows

\mathfrak{R}_{l}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}\mathfrak{R}_{l}\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}.

Clearly,
\gamma_{i}\mathfrak{R}_{l}\cong\gamma_{i}\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
and any element
r
of the ring
\mathfrak{R}_{l}
can be uniquely written as
r=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}r_{i}
where
r_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
for all
i
.

Definition 1

A linear code
\mathcal{C}
over a ring
\mathcal{R}
of length
n
is defined as an
\mathcal{R}
-submodule of
\mathcal{R}^{n}
and elements of
\mathcal{C}
are called codewords.

The Hamming distance of two codewords
x
and
y
is defined as the minimum number of substitutions required to make
y
from
x
. The Hamming distance of a code is defined as the minimum of the distances between two distinct codewords.

Definition 2

Let
\theta
be an automorphism of a finite ring
\mathcal{R}
. Then a map
\delta:\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{R}
is called a
\theta
-derivation if the following conditions hold:

1.

\delta(r_{1}+r_{2})=\delta(r_{1})+\delta(r_{2})
and

2.

\delta(r_{1}r_{2})=\delta(r_{1})r_{2}+\theta(r_{1})\delta(r_{2})~~~~~~\text{ for all $r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathcal{R}$}.

Consider the set of all polynomials over ring
\mathfrak{R}_{l}
with an automorphism
\sigma
and an inner derivation
\delta
. Then this set forms a noncommutative ring under the usual addition of polynomials, and multiplication is defined with respect to
xr=\sigma(r)x+\delta(r)
. This ring is known as a skew polynomial ring and is denoted by
\mathfrak{R}_{l}[x;\sigma,\delta]
. As
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
is a division ring,
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}[x;\theta,\delta]
forms a right (resp. left) Euclidean ring. Throughout this paper, we take
\theta
as the Frobenius automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
, and we extend this automorphism to find an automorphism
\sigma
of the ring
\mathfrak{R}_{l}
. It is easy to verify that the order of
\theta
and
\sigma
is
2
, i.e.,
\theta^{2}=Id_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}}
and
\sigma^{2}=Id_{\mathfrak{R}_{l}}
.

Lemma 1

Let
\delta:\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{R}
be a map defined by
\delta(r)=\alpha(\theta(r)-r)

where
\alpha
is a non-zero element in
\mathcal{R}
. Then the map
\delta
is a
\theta
-derivation of
\mathcal{R}
.

Proof

Let
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathcal{R}
. Then for a nonzero
\alpha
in
\mathcal{R}
, we get

\displaystyle\delta(r_{1}+r_{2})

\displaystyle=\alpha(\theta(r_{1}+r_{2})-(r_{1}+r_{2}))

\displaystyle=\alpha(\theta(r_{1})-r_{1})+\alpha(\theta(r_{2})-r_{2})

\displaystyle=\delta(r_{1})+\delta(r_{2})

and

\displaystyle\delta(r_{1}r_{2})

\displaystyle=\alpha(\theta(r_{1}r_{2})-r_{1}r_{2})

\displaystyle=\alpha(\theta(r_{1}r_{2})-\theta(r_{1})r_{2}+\theta(r_{1})r_{2}-r_{1}r_{2})

\displaystyle=\alpha(\theta(r_{1})\theta(r_{2})-\theta(r_{1})r_{2})+\alpha(\theta(r_{1})r_{2}-r_{1}r_{2})

\displaystyle=\alpha\theta(r_{1})(\theta(r_{2})-r_{2})+\alpha(\theta(r_{1})-r_{1})r_{2}

\displaystyle=\delta(r_{1})r_{2}+\theta(r_{1})\delta(r_{2}).

\mathbb{F}_{p}

\displaystyle=\alpha(a^{p^{2}}+vb^{p^{2}}-a^{p}-vb^{p}),

\displaystyle\theta(\delta(r))

\delta(\theta(r))=\theta(\delta(r))

In this section, first we discuss the structure of
(\theta,\delta)
-cyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}

Definition 3

A
(\theta,\delta)
-linear code of length
n
is said to be a
(\theta,\delta)
-cyclic code if for any codeword
c=(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in\mathcal{C}
, we have
(\theta,\delta)
-cyclic shift
\tau_{(\theta,\delta)}(c)=(\theta(c_{n-1})+\delta(c_{0}),\theta(c_{0})+\delta(c_{1}),\ldots,\theta(c_{n-2})+\delta(c_{n-1}))\in\mathcal{C}
.

Theorem 3.1

A code
\mathcal{C}
of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
is a
(\theta,\delta)
-cyclic code if and only if code
\mathcal{C}
is a left
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}[x;\theta,\delta]
-submodule of
\frac{\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}[x;\theta,\delta]}{\langle x^{n}-1\rangle}
.

Proof

Since the code
\mathcal{C}
is a linear code, for any
c(x),c^{\prime}(x)\in\mathcal{C}
we get
c(x)+c^{\prime}(x)\in\mathcal{C}
. For a codeword
c(x)=c_{0}+c_{1}x+\cdots+c_{n-1}x^{n-1}
in the
(\theta,\delta)
-cyclic code
\mathcal{C}
,
xc(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}[\theta(c_{i-1})+\delta({c_{i}})]x^{i}\in\mathcal{C}
where
-1\equiv n-1.
Therefore, for a polynomial
a(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}[x;\theta,\delta]
and
c(x)\in\mathcal{C}
,
a(x)c(x)\in\mathcal{C}
and the code
\mathcal{C}
is a left
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}[x;\theta,\delta]
-submodule.

Conversely, suppose the code
\mathcal{C}
is a left
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}[x;\theta,\delta]
-submodule of
\frac{\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}[x;\theta,\delta]}{\langle x^{n}-1\rangle}
then by the definition of submodule it is linear. Now for any
c(x)\in\mathcal{C}
, take
a(x)=x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}[x;\theta,\delta]
, then
xc(x)\in\mathcal{C}
. Therefore, the code
\mathcal{C}
is a
(\theta,\delta)
-cyclic code.

Theorem 3.2

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a left submodule of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}[x;\theta,\delta]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
. Then
\mathcal{C}
is generated by a monic polynomial
g(x)
of minimum degree, where
g(x)
right divides
x^{n}-1
over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}[x;\theta,\delta]
.

Proof

Let
g(x)
be a monic polynomial of minimum degree in
\mathcal{C}
. Then for a polynomial
c(x)\in\mathcal{C}
, there exist unique polynomials
q(x)
and
r(x)
in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}[x;\theta,\delta]
such that

c(x)=q(x)g(x)+r(x)

where
\deg(r(x))<\deg(g(x))
or
r(x)=0
. Since polynomials
c(x)
and
g(x)
belong to
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}[x;\theta,\delta]
-submodule
\mathcal{C}
, polynomial
r(x)=c(x)-q(x)g(x)\in\mathcal{C}
and we get
r(x)=0
. Therefore, the code
\mathcal{C}
is generated by
g(x)
.

Now consider polynomial
x^{n}-1
in place of
c(x)
, then we get
x^{n}-1=q(x)g(x)
implies
g(x)|_{r}(x^{n}-1)
, that is, polynomial
g(x)
right divides
x^{n}-1
.

Codes given in the above theorem are free modules of dimension
k=n-\deg(g(x))
. And the rows of the generator matrix of these codes are given by
\tau_{\theta,\delta}^{i}(c)
where
0\leq i\leq k-1
.

\mathbb{F}_{7^{2}}=\mathbb{F}_{7}(t)

Theorem 3.3

Let
\mathcal{C}=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}\mathcal{C}_{i}
be a linear code over
\mathfrak{R}_{l}
where, for
i\in\{1,\ldots,l\}
,
\mathcal{C}_{i}
’s are linear codes over the field
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
. Then the code
\mathcal{C}
is
(\sigma,\delta)
-cyclic code over
\mathfrak{R}_{l}
if and only if
\mathcal{C}_{i}
’s are
(\theta,\delta)
-cyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
.

Proof

Let
\mathcal{C}=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}\mathcal{C}_{i}
be a
(\sigma,\delta)
-cyclic codes of length
n
over the ring
\mathfrak{R}_{l}
. Let
a=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}c_{i}
, where
c_{i}=(c_{i0},c_{i1},c_{i2},\ldots,c_{i(n-1)})\in\mathcal{C}_{i}
and
a=(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n-1})\in\mathcal{C}
. Then for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,l\}
we have

\sigma(a)=\sigma\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}c_{i}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}\theta(c_{i})

and

\displaystyle\delta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}c_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha\left(\sigma\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}c_{i}\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}c_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}\theta(c_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}c_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}(\theta(c_{i})-c_{i})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}\delta(c_{i}).

Now we get

\displaystyle\tau_{\sigma,\delta}(a)=

\displaystyle\tau_{\sigma,\delta}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}c_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg(\sigma\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}c_{i(n-1)}\right)+\delta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}c_{i0}\right),\sigma\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}c_{i0}\right)+\delta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}c_{i1}\right)

\displaystyle,\ldots,\sigma\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}c_{i(n-2)}\right)+\delta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}c_{i(n-1)}\right)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}\tau_{\theta,\delta}(c_{i}).

Therefore, the codes
\mathcal{C}_{i}
’s are
(\theta,\delta)
-cyclic codes.

Conversely, suppose the codes
C_{i}
’s are
(\theta,\delta)
-cyclic codes. Then for any
a=(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1})\in\mathcal{C}
, we get
a_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}c_{ij}
where
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}

and

\displaystyle\tau_{\sigma,\delta}(a)=

\displaystyle(\sigma(c_{n-1})+\delta(c_{0}),\sigma(c_{0})+\delta(c_{1}),\ldots,\sigma(c_{n-2})+\delta(c_{n-1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg(\sigma\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}c_{i(n-1)}\right)+\delta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}c_{i0}\right),\sigma\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}c_{i0}\right)+\delta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}c_{i1}\right)

\displaystyle,\ldots,\sigma\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}c_{i(n-2)}\right)+\delta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}c_{i(n-1)}\right)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}\theta(c_{i(n-1)})+\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}\delta(c_{i0}),\ldots,\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}\theta(c_{i(n-2)})+\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}\delta(c_{i(n-1)})\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}\gamma_{i}\tau_{\theta,\delta}(c_{i}).

Since
\mathcal{C}_{i}
’s are
(\theta,\delta)
-cyclic codes,
\tau_{\theta,\delta}(c_{i})\in\mathcal{C}_{i}
. Therefore, code
\mathcal{C}
is a
(\sigma,\delta)
-cyclic code.

in

Proof

Since

-cyclic code of length
n
over the field

4
Reversible and DNA codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}

In this section, we delve into the theory of reversible codes over a skew polynomial ring with a derivation. Reversible codes have several advantages over other types of error-correcting codes. This class of codes has a simple decoding algorithm that can be implemented efficiently. It is also robust to errors that occur in bursts, making them useful in situations where errors are likely to occur in clusters.

Definition 4

Let
g(x)=g_{0}+g_{1}x+\cdots+g_{m}x^{m}
be a polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{4}[x;\theta,\delta]
. Then
g(x)
is said to be a palindromic polynomial if
g_{i}=g_{m-i}
and
a
(\theta,\delta)
-palindromic if
g_{i}=\theta(g_{m-i})-\delta(g_{m-i+1})
where
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
.

Now, using the above definitions, we derive results for the reversible codes in
\mathbb{F}_{4}[x;\theta,\delta]
.

Theorem 4.1

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
(\theta,\delta)
-cyclic code of even length
n
generated by a polynomial
g(x)=g_{0}+g_{1}x+\cdots+g_{m}x^{m}
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
, where
m
is odd. Then
\mathcal{C}
will be reversible if
g(x)
is palindromic and
\delta(g((x))\in\mathcal{C}
.

Proof

Let
\mathcal{C}=\langle g(x)\rangle
be a
(\theta,\delta)
-cyclic code of even length
n
, where
g(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}g_{i}x^{i},

m
is odd. Let
c\in\mathcal{C}
be a codeword. Then
c=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}a_{j}x^{j}g(x)
, where
a_{j}
’s
\in\mathbb{F}_{4}
.

\displaystyle\noindent c=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}a_{j}x^{j}\sum_{i=0}^{m}g_{i}x^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}a_{j}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\theta^{j}(g_{i})x^{i+j}+\sum_{j=0}^{(k-3)/2}a_{2j+1}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\delta(g_{i})x^{i+2j}.

Now, we find the reverse of above codeword
c
, which is given by

\displaystyle c^{r}=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}a_{j}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\theta^{j}(g_{i})x^{n-1-(i+j)}+\sum_{j=0}^{(k-3)/2}a_{2j+1}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\delta(g_{i})x^{n-1-(i+2j)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{(k-1)/2}a_{2j}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{m}g_{i}x^{m-i}\right]x^{k-1-2j}+\sum_{j=0}^{(k-3)/2}a_{2j+1}x\left[\sum_{i=0}^{m}g_{i}x^{m-i}\right]x^{k-3-2j}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{(k-3)/2}a_{2j+1}\Bigg[\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{m}\delta(g_{i})x^{m-i}\Big)x^{k-1-2j}+\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m}\delta(g_{i})x^{m-i}\right)x^{k-3-2j}\Bigg].

Further, using the palindromic condition of the polynomial
g(x)
, we get

\displaystyle c^{r}=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{(k-1)/2}a_{2j}g(x)x^{k-1-2j}+\sum_{j=0}^{(k-3)/2}a_{2j+1}xg(x)x^{k-3-2j}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{(k-3)/2}a_{2j+1}\left[\delta(g(x))x^{k-1-2j}+\delta(g(x))x^{k-3-2j}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}a_{j}x^{k-1-j}g(x)+\sum_{j=0}^{(k-3)/2}a_{2j+1}\delta(g(x))\big[x^{k-1-2j}+x^{k-3-2j}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}a_{j}x^{k-1-j}g(x)+\sum_{j=0}^{(k-3)/2}a_{2j+1}\big[x^{k-1-2j}+x^{k-3-2j}\big]\delta(g(x)).

If
\delta(g(x))\in\mathcal{C}
, then
c^{r}\in\mathcal{C}
. Thus, the code
\mathcal{C}
is reversible.

Proof

This lemma can easily be proved by observing the correspondence of DNA nucleotides.

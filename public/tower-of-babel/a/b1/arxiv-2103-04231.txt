Quantum Interpolating Ensemble: Biorthogonal Polynomials and Average Entropies
Lu Wei, Nicholas Witte

Abstract
The density matrix formalism is a fundamental tool in studying various problems in quantum information processing. In the space of density matrices, the most well-known measures are the Hilbert-Schmidt and Bures-Hall ensembles. In this work, the averages of quantum purity and von Neumann entropy for an ensemble that interpolates between these two major ensembles are explicitly calculated for finite-dimensional systems. The proposed interpolating ensemble is a specialization of the θ \theta -deformed Cauchy-Laguerre two-matrix model and new results for this latter ensemble are given in full generality, including the recurrence relations satisfied by their associated bi-orthogonal polynomials when θ \theta assumes positive integer values.

3
Applications to Quantum Information Theory

In this section, we study a special case, relevant to quantum information theory, of the above discussed bi-orthogonal system that gives rise to the interpolating ensemble of interest. We first outline the corresponding correlation kernels before showing a new result on the kernel factorizations. We then perform analytical and numerical study on the average behavior of entanglement entropies over the interpolating ensemble.

3.1
Entanglement entropies and correlation kernels

For the quantum bipartite system introduced in Section
1

B

\lambda_{1}=1,~~\lambda_{2}=\dots=\lambda_{m}=0,

(90)

to the maximally-entangled state

\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\dots\lambda_{m}=\frac{1}{m}.

(91)

A standard one we consider here is quantum purity
[
2
]

S_{\rm{P}}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{2},

(92)

S_{\rm{P}}=1
and when
S_{\rm{P}}=1/m
, respectively. Quantum purity (

2
]

(93)

The von Neumann entropy (

We focus on the average entanglement entropies in this work, whereas the study of higher order moments would make full use of the results derived in Section

-\Sigma K_{01}(x_{j},x_{i})&\Delta K_{00}(x_{i},x_{j})\\
\end{array}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq k},

(108)

where the kernels

\displaystyle\Delta K_{00}(x,y)

\displaystyle=K_{00}(x,y)-K_{00}(y,x),

(109a)

\displaystyle\Sigma K_{01}(x,y)

\displaystyle=K_{01}(x,y)+K_{10}(y,x),

(109b)

\displaystyle\Delta K_{11}(x,y)

\displaystyle=K_{11}(x,y)-K_{11}(y,x),

(109c)

are written in terms of those of the
\theta
-deformed Cauchy-Laguerre bi-orthogonal ensemble
K_{00}(x,y)
,
K_{01}(x,y)
,
K_{10}(x,y)
, and
K_{11}(x,y)
. As a result, the computation of various statistical quantities of the quantum interpolating ensemble can be performed over those kernels. The kernels can be expressed via the following Fox H-functions
[

]

\displaystyle H_{q}(x)

\displaystyle=H_{2,3}^{1,1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}(-\alpha-m,1);(m,1)\\
(0,1);(-q,\theta),(-\alpha,1)\end{array}\Big|tx^{\theta}\Big.\right),

\displaystyle G_{q}(x)

\displaystyle=H_{2,3}^{2,1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}(-\alpha-m,1);(m,1)\\
(0,1),(-q,\theta);(-\alpha,1)\end{array}\Big|tx^{\theta}\Big.\right),

as

\displaystyle K_{00}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\theta\int_{0}^{1}t^{\alpha}H_{a}(x)H_{a+1}(y)\,\mathrm{d}t,

(111a)

\displaystyle K_{01}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\theta x^{2a+1}\int_{0}^{1}t^{\alpha}H_{a}(y)G_{a+1}(x)\,\mathrm{d}t,

(111b)

\displaystyle K_{10}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\theta y^{2a+1}\int_{0}^{1}t^{\alpha}H_{a+1}(x)G_{a}(y)\,\mathrm{d}t,

(111c)

\displaystyle K_{11}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\theta(xy)^{2a+1}\int_{0}^{1}t^{\alpha}G_{a+1}(x)G_{a}(y)\,\mathrm{d}t-\frac{x^{a}y^{a+1}}{x+y},

(111d)

where the definition

\alpha=\frac{2(a+1)}{\theta}-1.

(112)

follows from the notation (
11
). In general, the Fox H-function is defined through the following contour integral
[

]

\displaystyle H_{p,q}^{m,n}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}(a_{1},A_{1}),\ldots,(a_{n},A_{n});(a_{n+1},A_{n+1}),\ldots,(a_{p},A_{p})\\
(b_{1},B_{1}),\ldots,(b_{m},B_{m});(b_{m+1},B_{m+1}),\ldots,(b_{q},B_{q})\end{array}\Big|x\Big.\right)

(116)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\imath}\int_{\mathcal{L}}{\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma\left(b_{j}+B_{j}s\right)\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(1-a_{j}-A_{j}s\right)}{\prod_{j=n+1}^{p}\Gamma\left(a_{j}+A_{j}s\right)\prod_{j=m+1}^{q}\Gamma\left(1-b_{j}-B_{j}s\right)}}x^{-s}\,\mathrm{d}s,

where the contour
\mathcal{L}
separates the poles of
\Gamma\left(b_{j}+B_{j}s\right)
from the poles of
\Gamma\left(1-a_{j}-A_{j}s\right)
. In the special case
A_{1}=\dots=A_{p}=1
and
B_{1}=\dots=B_{q}=1
, the Fox H-function reduces to the Meijer G-function
[
47
]
. The integral forms of the kernel functions (

)–(
111d
) are useful for the computation of mean entropies, whereas several other distinct representations of the kernel functions exist
[

,

,
42
,

,

. In particular, we present the following bi-orthogonal polynomial forms
[

]
useful for a later discussion

\displaystyle K_{00}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}p_{k}\left(x^{\theta}\right)q_{k}\left(y^{\theta}\right),

(117a)

\displaystyle K_{01}(x,y)

\displaystyle=x^{a}e^{-x}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{v^{a+1}e^{-v}}{x+v}K_{00}(y,v)\,\mathrm{d}v,

(117b)

\displaystyle K_{10}(x,y)

\displaystyle=y^{a+1}e^{-y}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{w^{a}e^{-w}}{y+w}K_{00}(w,x)\,\mathrm{d}w,

(117c)

\displaystyle K_{11}(x,y)

\displaystyle=x^{a}y^{a+1}e^{-x-y}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{v^{a}e^{-v}}{y+v}\frac{w^{a+1}e^{-w}}{x+w}K_{00}(v,w)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}w-w(x,y),

(117d)

where the normalized bi-orthogonal polynomials

\displaystyle p_{j}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{j}\frac{\sqrt{2}(-1)^{k+j}\Gamma(k+j+\alpha+1)x^{k}}{\Gamma(\theta k+a+1)\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)(j-k)!k!},

(118a)

\displaystyle q_{j}\left(y\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{j}\frac{\sqrt{2}(-1)^{k+j}(\theta j+a+1)\Gamma(k+j+\alpha+1)y^{k}}{\Gamma(\theta k+a+2)\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)(j-k)!k!},

(118b)

are orthogonal with respect to the
b=a+1
specialization of the weight function (
12
) as given by the orthogonality condition (
14
). Here, we are using slightly different notations of the bi-orthogonal polynomials, cf. (

) and (

Before presenting the main results on the average entropies, we provide in the following lemma a generalization of the kernel factorization property from
\theta=1

[

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
\theta>0
, the correlation kernels (
117a
)–(
117d
) can be factorized

\displaystyle K_{00}(x,y)+K_{00}(y,x)

\displaystyle=u(x)u(y),

(119a)

\displaystyle K_{01}(x,y)-K_{10}(y,x)

\displaystyle=v(x)u(y),

(119b)

\displaystyle K_{11}(x,y)+K_{11}(y,x)

\displaystyle=-v(x)v(y),

(119c)

as the product of the functions

\displaystyle u(x)

\displaystyle=\theta\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}\Gamma(k+\alpha+m+1)x^{\theta k}}{\Gamma(\theta k+a+2)\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)\Gamma(m-k)k!},

(120a)

\displaystyle v(x)

\displaystyle=e^{-x}x^{a}-\theta x^{2a+1}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}\Gamma(k+\alpha+m+1)\Gamma(-\theta k-a,x)x^{\theta k}}{(\theta k+a+1)\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)\Gamma(m-k)k!},

(120b)

where
\Gamma(a,x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}t^{a-1}e^{-t}\,\mathrm{d}t
denoting the incomplete Gamma function.

Proof 3.2
.

The starting point of the proof is the bi-orthogonal polynomial forms of the kernels (
117a
)–(
117d
). To show (
119a
), we first represent (
117a
) via (
118a
) and (

) as

\displaystyle K_{00}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\frac{\theta(-1)^{i+k}x^{\theta k}y^{\theta i}}{\Gamma(\theta i+a+2)\Gamma(\theta k+a+1)\Gamma(i+\alpha+1)\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)i!k!}

(122)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{j=i}^{m-1}(2j+\alpha+1)\frac{\Gamma(j+i+\alpha+1)\Gamma(j+k+\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(j-i+1)\Gamma(j-k+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\frac{\theta(-1)^{i+k}x^{\theta k}y^{\theta i}}{\Gamma(\theta i+a+2)\Gamma(\theta k+a+1)\Gamma(i+\alpha+1)\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)i!k!}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Gamma(i+\alpha+m+1)\Gamma(k+\alpha+m+1)}{(i+k+\alpha+1)\Gamma(m-i)\Gamma(m-k)},

where the last step is obtained by Lemma 4.1 in
[
40
]
. We then have

\displaystyle K_{00}(x,y)+K_{00}(y,x)

(125)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\frac{\theta^{2}(-1)^{i+k}x^{\theta k}y^{\theta i}}{\Gamma(\theta i+a+2)\Gamma(\theta k+a+2)\Gamma(i+\alpha+1)\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)i!k!}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Gamma(i+\alpha+m+1)\Gamma(k+\alpha+m+1)}{(i+k+\alpha+1)\Gamma(m-i)\Gamma(m-k)}\left(i+k+\frac{2(a+1)}{\theta}\right),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{\theta(-1)^{k}\Gamma(k+\alpha+m+1)x^{\theta k}}{\Gamma(\theta k+a+2)\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)\Gamma(m-k)k!}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\frac{\theta(-1)^{i}\Gamma(i+\alpha+m+1)y^{\theta i}}{\Gamma(\theta i+a+2)\Gamma(i+\alpha+1)\Gamma(m-i)i!}=u(x)u(y),

which establishes (

). To show (

), we insert (

) into (

) and (

) that gives

\displaystyle K_{01}(x,y)-K_{10}(y,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{a}v^{a}e^{-x-v}}{x+v}\left(vK_{00}(y,v)-xK_{00}(v,y)\right)\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\frac{\theta(-1)^{i+k}\Gamma(i+\alpha+m+1)\Gamma(k+\alpha+m+1)\left(\Gamma(m-i)\Gamma(m-k)i!k!\right)^{-1}y^{\theta i}}{(i+k+\alpha+1)\Gamma(\theta i+a+2)\Gamma(\theta k+a+2)\Gamma(i+\alpha+1)\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)}

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{a}v^{a}e^{-x-v}}{x+v}v^{\theta k}\left((\theta i+a+1)v-x(\theta k+a+1)\right)\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\frac{\theta(-1)^{i}\Gamma(i+\alpha+m+1)e^{-x}x^{a}y^{\theta i}}{\Gamma(\theta i+a+1)\Gamma(i+\alpha+1)\Gamma(m-i)i!}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}\Gamma(k+\alpha+m+1)\left(\Gamma(m-k)k!\right)^{-1}}{(i+k+\alpha+1)(\theta k+a+1)\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)}

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\frac{\theta^{2}(-1)^{i}\Gamma(i+\alpha+m+1)x^{2a+1}y^{\theta i}}{\Gamma(\theta i+a+2)\Gamma(i+\alpha+1)\Gamma(m-i)i!}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}\Gamma(k+\alpha+m+1)\Gamma(-\theta k-a,x)x^{\theta k}}{(\theta k+a+1)\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)\Gamma(m-k)k!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(e^{-x}x^{a}-\theta x^{2a+1}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}\Gamma(k+\alpha+m+1)\Gamma(-\theta k-a,x)x^{\theta k}}{(\theta k+a+1)\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)\Gamma(m-k)k!}\right)

\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}\Gamma(k+\alpha+m+1)}{(i+k+\alpha+1)(\theta k+a+1)\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)\Gamma(m-k)k!}=\frac{1}{\theta i+a+1}.

(126)

This identity is established by the fact that the sum can be written in terms of a unit argument terminating hypergeometric function of Saalschützian type

{}_{4}F_{3}\left(1-m,\frac{\alpha+1}{2},\alpha+m+1,i+\alpha+1;\frac{\alpha+3}{2},\alpha+1,i+\alpha+2;1\right),

[

{}_{4}F_{3}(-n,b,c,d;b+1,c-l,d-m;1)=\frac{n!(b-c+1)_{l}(b-d+1)_{m}}{(b+1)_{n}(1-c)_{l}(1-d)_{m}},~~~~0\leq l+m\leq n,

(a)_{n}=\frac{\Gamma(a+n)}{\Gamma(a)},

(128)

denoting the Pochhammer’s symbol. This completes the proof of (
119b
). To show (
119c
), we insert (
117a
) into (

) that leads to

\displaystyle K_{11}(x,y)+K_{11}(y,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{a}y^{a}e^{-x-y}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{v^{a}w^{a}e^{-v-w}}{(x+w)(y+v)}\left(ywK_{00}(v,w)+xvK_{00}(w,v)\,\mathrm{d}w\,\mathrm{d}v\right)-1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\frac{\theta(-1)^{i+k}\Gamma(i+\alpha+m+1)\Gamma(k+\alpha+m+1)\left(\Gamma(m-i)\Gamma(m-k)\right)^{-1}}{(i+k+\alpha+1)\Gamma(\theta i+a+2)\Gamma(\theta k+a+1)\Gamma(i+\alpha+1)\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)i!k!}

\displaystyle\times x^{a}y^{a}e^{-x-y}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{v^{a}w^{a}e^{-v-w}\left(yv^{\theta k}w^{\theta i+1}+xw^{\theta k}v^{\theta i+1}\right)}{(x+w)(y+v)}\,\mathrm{d}w\,\mathrm{d}v-x^{a}y^{a}e^{-x-y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{\theta(-1)^{k}\Gamma(k+\alpha+m+1)\Gamma(-\theta k-a,x)x^{\theta k+2a+1}y^{a}e^{-y}}{\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)\Gamma(m-k)k!}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\frac{(-1)^{i}\Gamma(i+\alpha+m+1)}{(i+k+\alpha+1)(\theta i+a+1)\Gamma(i+\alpha+1)\Gamma(m-i)i!}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{\theta(-1)^{k}\Gamma(k+\alpha+m+1)\Gamma(-\theta k-a,y)y^{\theta k+2a+1}x^{a}e^{-x}}{\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)\Gamma(m-k)k!}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\frac{(-1)^{i}\Gamma(i+\alpha+m+1)}{(i+k+\alpha+1)(\theta i+a+1)\Gamma(i+\alpha+1)\Gamma(m-i)i!}

\displaystyle-\theta x^{2a+1}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}\Gamma(k+\alpha+m+1)\Gamma(-\theta k-a,x)x^{\theta k}}{(\theta k+a+1)\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)\Gamma(m-k)k!}

\displaystyle\times\theta y^{2a+1}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\frac{(-1)^{i}\Gamma(i+\alpha+m+1)\Gamma(-\theta i-a,y)y^{\theta i}}{(\theta i+a+1)\Gamma(i+\alpha+1)\Gamma(m-i)i!}-x^{a}y^{a}e^{-x-y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(e^{-x}x^{a}-\theta x^{2a+1}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}\Gamma(k+\alpha+m+1)\Gamma(-\theta k-a,x)x^{\theta k}}{(\theta k+a+1)\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)\Gamma(m-k)k!}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(e^{-y}y^{a}-\theta y^{2a+1}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}\Gamma(k+\alpha+m+1)\Gamma(-\theta k-a,y)y^{\theta k}}{(\theta k+a+1)\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)\Gamma(m-k)k!}\right)=-v(x)v(y),

where the second to last equality is obtained by applying twice the identity (

3.2
Average entropies over interpolating ensemble

With the above preparations, we now state the results on the average entropies over the interpolating ensemble. Our first result is the formula of average purity as summarized in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.3
.

The average value of quantum purity (
92
) under the quantum interpolating ensemble (
6
), for any
\theta>0
and
a>-1
, is given by

\mathbb{E}_{f}\!\left[S_{\rm{P}}\right]=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{(-1)^{k+m-1}\theta(\theta k+a+2)^{2}}{2d(d+1)(m-1-k)!k!}A_{\theta}(k),

(129)

where

A_{\theta}(k)=\frac{\Gamma\left(k+\frac{2(a+2)}{\theta}\right)\Gamma\left(k+m+\frac{2(a+1)}{\theta}\right)\Gamma\left(k+1+\frac{2}{\theta}\right)}{\Gamma\left(k+\frac{2(a+1)}{\theta}\right)\Gamma\left(k+m+\frac{2(a+2)}{\theta}\right)\Gamma\left(k+1+\frac{2}{\theta}-m\right)}.

(130)

Before proving Proposition
3.3
, two remarks on its special cases are in order.

Remark 3.4
.

In the special case
\theta=1
,
a=n-m-1/2
that corresponds to the Bures-Hall ensemble (
3
), by recognizing
A_{1}(m-2)
and
A_{1}(m-1)
as the only non-vanishing terms in (
130
) with

A_{1}(k)=\frac{(k+2n-2m+2)(k+2n-2m+1)\Gamma(k+3)}{(k+2n-m+2)(k+2n-m+1)\Gamma(k+3-m)},

(131)

the expression (
129
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{f}\!\left[S_{\rm{P}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=m-2}^{m-1}\frac{2(-1)^{k+m-1}(k+n-m+3/2)^{2}}{m(2n-m)(2mn-m^{2}+2)(m-1-k)!k!}A_{1}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{2n(2n+m)-m^{2}+1}{2n\left(2mn-m^{2}+2\right)}.

This recovers the mean purity formula of the Bures-Hall ensemble recently reported in
[
34
,
35
,
37
]
.

Remark 3.5
.

In the special case
\theta=2
,
a=n-m
that corresponds to the Hilbert-Schmidt ensemble (
1
),
by recognizing
A_{2}(m-1)
as the only non-vanishing term in (
130
) with

A_{2}(k)=\frac{(k+n-m+1)\Gamma(k+2)}{(k+n+1)\Gamma(k+2-m)},

(132)

the expression (
129
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{f}\!\left[S_{\rm{P}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=m-1}^{m-1}\frac{(-1)^{k+m-1}(2k+n-m+2)^{2}}{mn(mn+1)(m-1-k)!k!}A_{2}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{m+n}{mn+1}.

We recover the mean purity formula of the Hilbert-Schmidt ensemble obtained in
[
7
]
.

We now prove the Proposition
3.3
.

Proof 3.6
.

The essential task is to compute
\mathbb{E}_{h}\!\left[T_{\rm{P}}\right]
, which, after inserting into the moment relation (
102
), will establish the Proposition
3.3
.
The required single eigenvalue density
h_{1}(x)
of the unconstrained ensemble (
94
) can be read off from the correlation function (
108

2\times 2
matrix as

(133)

The computation now boils down to computing two integrals

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{h}\!\left[T_{\rm{P}}\right]

\displaystyle=m\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{2}h_{1}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{2}K_{01}(x,x)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{2}K_{10}(x,x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

(134)

The starting point to calculate the above integrals is the fact that the contour form (
3.1
) of the Fox H-function (
110
) admits a finite number of single poles, which by residue calculation gives a finite sum

H_{q}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}\Gamma(k+\alpha+m+1)\left(tx^{\theta}\right)^{k}}{\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)\Gamma(\theta k+q+1)(m-1-k)!k!}.

(135)

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{2}K_{01}(x,x)\,\mathrm{d}x

(136)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\theta x^{2a+3}\int_{0}^{1}t^{\alpha}H_{a}(y)G_{a+1}(x)\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}x

(137)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{\theta(-1)^{k}\Gamma(k+\alpha+m+1)}{\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)\Gamma(\theta k+a+1)(m-1-k)!k!}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!x^{\theta k+2a+3}\int_{0}^{1}\!t^{\alpha+k}G_{a+1}(x)\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}x

(138)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{(-1)^{k+m-1}\theta(\theta k+a+1)(\theta k+a+2)}{2(m-1-k)!k!}A_{\theta}(k)

(139)

where the integrals over

and
x
in (

) are evaluated respectively by the identity
[
47
]

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}x^{\rho-1}H_{p,q}^{m,n}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}(a_{1},A_{1}),\ldots,(a_{n},A_{n});(a_{n+1},A_{n+1}),\ldots,(a_{p},A_{p})\\
(b_{1},B_{1}),\ldots,(b_{m},B_{m});(b_{m+1},B_{m+1}),\ldots,(b_{q},B_{q})\end{array}\Big|\eta x\Big.\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{p+1,q+1}^{m,n+1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}(1-\rho,1),(a_{1},A_{1}),\ldots,(a_{n},A_{n});(a_{n+1},A_{n+1}),\ldots,(a_{p},A_{p})\\
(b_{1},B_{1}),\ldots,(b_{m},B_{m});(b_{m+1},B_{m+1}),\ldots,(b_{q},B_{q}),(-\rho,1)\end{array}\Big|\eta\Big.\right),

and the Mellin transform of the Fox H-function
[
47
]
, cf. (
3.1
),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}H_{p,q}^{m,n}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}(a_{1},A_{1}),\ldots,(a_{n},A_{n});(a_{n+1},A_{n+1}),\ldots,(a_{p},A_{p})\\
(b_{1},B_{1}),\ldots,(b_{m},B_{m});(b_{m+1},B_{m+1}),\ldots,(b_{q},B_{q})\end{array}\Big|\eta x\Big.\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

(149)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frac{\eta^{-s}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma\left(b_{j}+B_{j}s\right)\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma\left(1-a_{j}-A_{j}s\right)}{\prod_{j=n+1}^{p}\Gamma\left(a_{j}+A_{j}s\right)\prod_{j=m+1}^{q}\Gamma\left(1-b_{j}-B_{j}s\right)}}.

In the same manner, the second integral in (

) is evaluated to

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{2}K_{10}(x,x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{(-1)^{k+m-1}\theta(\theta k+a+2)(\theta k+a+3)}{2(m-1-k)!k!}A_{\theta}(k).

(150)

Putting together (

), (

), (

), and (

.

The next result on the average von Neumann entropy is presented in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.7

The average value of von Neumann entropy (

\mathbb{E}_{f}\!\left[S_{\rm{vN}}\right]=\psi_{0}(d+1)-\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{(-1)^{k+m-1}\left(\theta k+a+3/2\right)}{d(m-1-k)!k!}B_{\theta}(k),

where

4
Conclusions

In this work, we proposed and studied a generalized ensemble that interpolates between the two major measures of density matrices - the Hilbert-Schmidt ensemble and the Bures-Hall ensemble. In particular, we derived recurrence relations of the underlying bi-orthogonal polynomials of the ensemble useful in computing different statistical quantities. As an application, we computed the average entanglement entropies over the interpolating ensemble generalizing various known results in the literature. Numerical simulations show that the proposed ensemble provides additional power in estimating the degree of entanglement by varying the deformation parameters. Future work includes further study of the statistical information of the ensemble such as higher order moments of entropies, fidelity, and volumes as well as further study of the associated bi-orthogonal system.

Statistical inference for multi-regime threshold Ornstein − \mathit{-} Uhlenbeck processes
Yuecai Han, Dingwen Zhang

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the parameter estimation for threshold Ornstein − \mathit{-} Uhlenbeck processes. Least squares method is used to obtain continuous-type and discrete-type estimators for the drift parameters based on continuous and discrete observations, respectively. The strong consistency and asymptotic normality of the proposed least squares estimators are studied. We also propose a modified quadratic variation estimator based on the long-time observations for the diffusion parameters and prove its consistency. Our simulation results suggest that the performance of our proposed estimators for the drift parameters may show improvements compared to generalized moment estimators. Additionally, the proposed modified quadratic variation estimator exhibits potential advantages over the usual quadratic variation estimator with relatively small sample sizes. In particular, our method can be applied to the multi-regime cases ( m > 2 m>2 ), while the generalized moment method only deals with the two regime cases ( m = 2 m=2 ). The U.S. treasury rate data is used to illustrate the theoretical results.

Introduction

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a complete probability space equipped with a right continuous and increasing family of
\sigma
-algebra
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
and
W=\{W_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a one-dimensional Brownian motion adapted to
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. The threshold Ornstein
\mathit{-}
Uhlenbeck (OU) process
X=\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is defined as the unique solution to the following threshold stochastic differential equation (SDE)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathrm{d}X_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\beta_{j}-\alpha_{j}X_{t})I_{\{\theta_{j-1}\leq X_{t}<\theta_{j}\}}\mathrm{d}t+\sigma\mathrm{d}W_{t},\\
&X_{0}=x,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
\beta_{j},\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
are the drift parameters,
\Theta=(\theta_{0},\cdots,\theta_{m})
satisfy
-\infty=\theta_{0}<\theta_{1}<\cdots<\theta_{m}=\infty
are the so-called thresholds,
\sigma>0
is the diffusion parameter,
x\in\mathbb{R}
is the initial value of the process
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, and
I_{\{\cdot\}}
denotes the indicator function. Moreover, we assume that
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{m}
are larger than zero to ensure the ergodicity of
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. The existence and uniqueness of the solution to Eq. (
1.1
) follow, for example, from the results of
Bass and Pardoux 1987
.
Suppose that the discretely observed process is observed at the regularly spaced time points
\{t_{k}=kh\}_{k=0}^{n}
where
h
is the mesh size and
n
is the sample size. In this paper, we aim to propose the least squares estimators (LSEs) for the unknown parameters
\alpha_{j}
and
\beta_{j}
based on either the continuously observed process
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
or discretely observed process
\{X_{t_{kh}}\}_{k=0}^{n}
, respectively. Furthermore, we propose the quadratic variation estimator (QVE) and the modified quadratic variation estimator (MQVE) for the diffusion parameters.

As useful stochastic dynamics models, threshold diffusion processes have been extensively employed in numerous fields. The threshold autoregressive models are introduced to model the nonlinearities in nonlinear time series
(
Brockwell and Hyndman 1991
;
Brockwell and Hyndman 1992
, see, e.g.,)
. In finance,
Lejay and Pigato 2019
show that an exponential form of the threshold process generalizes the Black-Scholes model in a way to model leverage effects.
Gerber and Shiu 2006
model the surplus of a company after the payment of dividends, which are paid only if the profits of the company are higher than a certain threshold. The threshold processes also have numerous applications in physics
(
Ramirez et al. 2013
;
Sattin 2008
, see, e.g.)
, meteorology
(
Hottovy and Stechmann 2015
, see, e.g.)
, forecast
(
Brockwell and Hyndman 1992
, see, e.g.)
, and they are in close contact with the skew diffusion processes
(
Ding et al. 2021
;
Gairat and Shcherbakov 2017
, see, e.g.)
.

For threshold OU processes,
Chan 1993
proposes a LSE for a stationary ergodic threshold autoregressive model and proves its consistency and limiting distribution.
Su and Chan 2015
;
Su and Chan 2017
study the asymptotic behavior of the quasi-likelihood estimator of a diffusion with piecewise regular diffusivity and piecewise affine drift with an unknown threshold. They provide a hypothesis test to decide whether or not the drift is affine or piecewise affine.
Lejay and Pigato 2020
propose the maximum likehood estimator (MLE) for the drift parameters, with both drift and diffusion coefficients constant on the positive and negative axis, yet discontinuous at zero.
Kutoyants 2012
studies the asymptotic behavior of the maximum likelihood estimator and Bayesian estimator for the threshold parameter. He also discusses the possibility of the construction of the goodness-of-fit test.
Mazzonetto and Pigato 2020
discuss the (quasi)-MLE of the drift parameters, both assuming continuous and discrete time observations.
Hu and Xi 2022
propose a generalized moment estimator (GME) to estimate the drift parameter based on the discretely observed process. Their main theoretical basis is the celebrated ergodic theorem.
Han and Zhang 2023
propose the trajectory fitting estimators for the drift parameters and obtain the asymptotic behavior of the estimators. We also draw attention to a related piece of work by
Xing et al. 2020
, in which they propose an estimator for the drift parameter of a skew OU process.

Based on the celebrated Girsanov’s theorem for semimartingales, one could obtain the log-likehood function, and the MLE for the drift parameter is derived by minimizing the log-likehood function
(
Su and Chan 2015
;
Su and Chan 2017
;
Mazzonetto and Pigato 2020
)
. The generalized moment method could deal with low-frequency data such as the gross domestic product. However, it is significantly more complicated to explore the case
m>2

(
Hu and Xi 2022
, see)
. In this paper, we apply the least squares method to deal with the drift parameter estimation problem based on a continuously observed process and a discretely observed process, respectively.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce some basic facts on the stationary and ergodic properties of threshold OU processes. In Section
3
and
4
, we construct the LSEs for the drift parameters based on continuous sampling and discrete sampling. We prove the strong consistency and asymptotic normality of the proposed LSEs. In section
5
, we consider a general threshold OU process, and propose the QVEs and the MQVEs for the diffusion parameters
\sigma
. The results of our simulation studies are reported in Section
6

7

Preliminaries

In this section, we first introduce some basic facts on the stationary and ergodic properties of threshold OU processes.
Throughout this paper, we shall
use the notation “
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}
” to denote “almost sure convergence”, the notation “
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\longrightarrow}}
” to denote “convergence in probability”, and the notation “
X_{T}\sim\mathcal{N}(a,b)
” or “
X_{n}\sim\mathcal{N}(a,b)
” to denote “the convergence in distribution of
X_{T}
or
X_{n}
to
X
where
X
follows a normal distribution with mean
a
and variance
b
”. For the convenience of the following description, we make a notation
I_{\{\cdot,j\}}=I_{\{\theta_{j-1}\leq\cdot<\theta_{j}\}}
,
j=1,\cdots,m
.

The following proposition, adopt from Proposition 3.1 of
Brockwell and Hyndman 1991
, Section 1 of
Browne and Whitt 1995
, and Proposition 1 of
Hu and Xi 2022
, shows that the process
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
has a unique invariant density.

Proposition 1
.

If the drift parameters
\alpha_{1}
,
\alpha_{m}
,
\beta_{1}
, and
\beta_{m}
satisfy

\lim_{x\rightarrow-\infty}\left(-\alpha_{1}x^{2}+2\beta_{1}x\right)<0\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\left(-\alpha_{m}x^{2}+2\beta_{m}x\right)<0,

the unique invariant density of the process
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is given by

\pi(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\pi_{j}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}k_{j}\exp\left(\left(-\alpha_{j}x^{2}+2\beta_{j}x\right)/\sigma^{2}\right)I_{\{x,j\}},

(2.1)

where
k_{j}
are uniquely determined by the following equations

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\pi(x)dx=1\quad\text{ and }\quad\pi\left(\theta_{j}-\right)=\pi\left(\theta_{j}+\right),\quad j=1,2,\ldots,m-1.

The following proposition, based on basic stability theories of Markov processes, describes the ergodic properties of threshold OU processes
(
Hu and Xi 2022
, see Lemma 1 of)
.

Proposition 2
.

Assume that the drift parameters
\alpha_{1}
,
\alpha_{m}
,
\beta_{1}
, and
\beta_{m}
satisfy the conditions of Proposition
1
. Then the discretely observed process
\{X_{kh}\}_{k=0}^{n}
and continuously observed process
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
are ergodic, i.e., for any
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}f(X_{t_{kh}})=\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}f(X_{t})\mathrm{d}t=\mathbb{E}[f(X_{\infty})]=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)\pi(x)\mathrm{d}x,\quad a.s.,

(2.2)

where
\pi(x)
is defined in Eq. (
2.1
).

Remark 1
.

When
\beta_{j}=0
,
j=1,2,\cdots,m
, the sufficient and necessary condition for the ergodic properties of the threshold OU process is

\alpha_{1}>0\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha_{m}>0.

Let
\braket{X,Y}
be the covariation process of
X
and
Y
, and
\braket{X}=\braket{X,X}
. The following proposition, consider the representation of martingale, i.e., we treat the continuous local martingale as a time-changed Brownian motion
(
Revuz and Yor 1998
, see Theorem 1.6, Chapter 5, p.188 of)
.

Proposition 3
.

If
M
is a
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-continuous local martingale vanishing at
0
and such that
\braket{M}_{\infty}=\infty
, then we have the following results.

(1)

B_{t}=M_{\tau_{t}}
is the so-called Dambis, Dubins-Schwarz Brownian motion and
M_{t}=B_{\braket{M}_{t}}
, where

\tau_{t}=\inf\{s:\braket{M}_{s}>t\}.

(2)

The law of large numbers for continuous local martingale:

\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{M_{T}}{\braket{M}_{T}}=0,\quad a.s.

(3)

The central limit theorem for continuous local martingale:

\frac{M_{T}}{\sqrt{\braket{M}_{T}}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1),\quad\text{as}\quad T\rightarrow\infty.

(4)

The law of the iterated logarithm:

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{M_{T}}{\sqrt{2\braket{M}_{T}\log\log\braket{M}_{T}}}=1,

\displaystyle\varliminf_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{M_{T}}{\sqrt{2\braket{M}_{T}\log\log\braket{M}_{T}}}=-1,\quad a.s.

Remark 2
.

For the proof of (1) of Proposition
3
, one can refer to Theorem 1.6, Chapter 5, p.188 of
Revuz and Yor 1998
. While (2) of Proposition
3
follows from Corollary 1, Chapter 2, p.144 of
Liptser and Shiryayev 2011
.
And (3) of Proposition
3
can be obtained by the distribution of Brownian motion, i.e.,
B_{T}/\sqrt{T}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, for all
T>0
.
Furthermore, (4) of Proposition
3
is obtained by the law of the iterated logarithm
(
Schilling et al. 2012
, see Theorem 11.1, Section 11.1, p.165 of)
of Brownian motion, i.e.,

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{W_{T}}{\sqrt{2T\log\log T}}=1,

\displaystyle\varliminf_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{W_{T}}{\sqrt{2T\log\log T}}=-1,\quad a.s.

3
The LSE based on continuous sampling

In this section, we construct the continuous-type LSEs for the parameters
\alpha_{j}
and
\beta_{j}
,
j=1,\cdots,m
, and prove their consistency and asymptotic normality based on the continuous observations. We separate the discussion into two subsections: the first subsection considers that the drift term is known to be piecewise linear with
\beta_{j}=0
, while the second subsection considers that the drift term is known to be piecewise linear with unknown and not vanishing piecewise constants
\beta_{j}
.

3.1
Estimate
\alpha_{j}
for known
\beta_{j}=0
and
\Theta

(j=1,\cdots,m)

In this case, the SDE becomes

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathrm{d}X_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}-\alpha_{j}X_{t}I_{\{X_{t},j\}}\mathrm{d}t+\sigma\mathrm{d}W_{t},\\
&X_{0}=x.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.1)

When there is only one regime, i.e.,
X
is a standard OU process, the continuous-type LSE for
\alpha
is given by minimizing the following objective function
(see for example
Kutoyants 2004
;
Hu and Nualart 2010
, and
the references therein)

\int_{0}^{T}\left\lvert\dot{X}_{t}+\alpha X_{t}\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}t.

The minimum is achieved at

\hat{\alpha}_{T}=-\frac{\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}\mathrm{d}X_{t}}{\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{2}\mathrm{d}t}.

When there are multiple regimes,
the continuous-type LSE for
\alpha_{j}
is motivated by the following heuristic argument, which aims to minimize

\int_{0}^{T}\bigg|\dot{X}_{t}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}X_{t}I_{\{X_{t},j\}}\bigg|^{2}\mathrm{d}t,

where
\dot{X}_{t}
denotes the formal derivative of
X_{t}
with respect to
t
.
This is a quadratic function of
\alpha_{i}
,
i=1,\cdots,m
, although
\dot{X}_{t}
does not exist. The minimum is achieved at

\hat{\alpha}_{i,T}=-\frac{\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\left(\mathrm{d}X_{t}+\sum_{j\neq i}\alpha_{j}X_{t}I_{\{X_{t},j\}}\mathrm{d}t\right)}{\int_{0}^{T}X^{2}_{t}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t}.

(3.2)

By Eq. (
3.1
), the facts that
I_{\{X_{t},i\}}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}=1
and
I_{\{X_{t},i\}}I_{\{X_{t},j\}}=0
, for
i\neq j
, we have

\hat{\alpha}_{i,T}=-\frac{\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}X_{t}}{\int_{0}^{T}X^{2}_{t}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t}=\alpha_{i}-\sigma\frac{\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}W_{t}}{\int_{0}^{T}X^{2}_{t}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t}.

Recall the unique invariant density of
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, we define

M_{i}=\int_{\theta_{i-1}}^{\theta_{i}}k_{i}x^{2}\exp\left(\frac{-\alpha_{i}x^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}x,\quad\text{for}\quad i=1,\cdots,m.

(3.3)

The asymptotic behavior of our estimators
\hat{\alpha}_{i,T}
is given as follows.

Theorem 1
.

For
i=1,2,\cdots,m
, the continuous-type LSEs
\hat{\alpha}_{i,T}
defined in Eq. (
3.2
) have the following statistical properties.

(1)

The continuous-type LSEs
\hat{\alpha}_{i,T}
admit the strong consistency, i.e.,

\hat{\alpha}_{i,T}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}\alpha_{i},\quad\text{as}\quad T\rightarrow\infty.

(2)

The continuous-type LSEs
\hat{\alpha}_{i,T}
admit the asymptotic normality, i.e.,

\sqrt{T}(\hat{\alpha}_{i,T}-\alpha_{i})\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}M_{i}^{-1}),\quad\text{as}\quad T\rightarrow\infty,

where
M_{i}
is defined in Eq. (
3.3
).

Proof of Theorem
1
.
Let

\hat{\alpha}_{i,T}=\alpha_{i}-\sigma\frac{G_{i,T}}{F_{i,T}},

where

\displaystyle G_{i,t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}X_{s}I_{\{X_{s},i\}}\mathrm{d}W_{s}\quad\text{and}\quad F_{i,t}=\int_{0}^{t}X^{2}_{s}I_{\{X_{s},i\}}\mathrm{d}s.

To prove the strong consistency, it suffices to show that
G_{i,T}/F_{i,T}
converges to zero almost surely as
T\rightarrow\infty
.
It is obvious that the quadratic variation process of
G_{i,t}
is exactly
F_{i,t}
. By Eq. (
2.2
), we have

\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{F_{i,T}}{T}=\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X^{2}_{t}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t=M_{i},\quad a.s.

(3.4)

Note that
G_{i}
is a continuous local martingale.
Then by (2) of Proposition
3
and Eq. (
3.4
), we conclude

\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{G_{i,T}}{F_{i,T}}=0,\quad a.s.,

which completes the proof of the strong consistency.

By (3) of Proposition
3
, we have

\frac{G_{i,T}}{\sqrt{F_{i,T}}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1),\quad\text{as}\quad T\rightarrow\infty.

(3.5)

Furthermore, combining Eq. (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), and Slutsky’s theorem yields

\sqrt{T}\left(\hat{\alpha}_{i,T}-\alpha_{i}\right)\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,\sigma^{2}M_{i}^{-1}\right),\quad\text{as}\quad T\rightarrow\infty.

Thus, we complete the proof of the asymptotic normality.

\square

3.2
Estimate
\alpha_{j}
and
\beta_{j}
for known
\Theta

(j=1,\cdots,m)

In this case,
\beta_{j}\neq 0
and the SDE becomes

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathrm{d}X_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\beta_{j}-\alpha_{j}X_{t})I_{\{X_{t},j\}}\mathrm{d}t+\sigma\mathrm{d}W_{t},\\
&X_{0}=x.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.6)

The continuous-type LSEs for
\alpha_{j}
and
\beta_{j}
aim to minimize the following objective function

\int_{0}^{T}\bigg|\dot{X}_{t}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(\beta_{j}-\alpha_{j}X_{t}\right)I_{\{X_{t},j\}}\bigg|^{2}\mathrm{d}t,

which is a quadratic function of
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{i}

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}_{i,T}=

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{0}^{T}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}X_{t}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t-L_{i,T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}X_{t}}{L_{i,T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{2}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t-\left(\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{2}}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\hat{\beta}_{i,T}=

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{0}^{T}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}X_{t}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{2}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}X_{t}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t}{L_{i,T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{2}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t-\left(\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{2}},

For simplicity, we make some notations as follows

\displaystyle P_{i}=

\displaystyle\int_{\theta_{i-1}}^{\theta_{i}}\pi(x)\mathrm{d}x=\mathbb{P}\left(\theta_{i-1}\leq X_{t}<\theta_{i}\right),

(3.8)

\displaystyle K_{i}=

\displaystyle\int_{\theta_{i-1}}^{\theta_{i}}k_{i}x\exp\left(\frac{-\alpha_{i}x^{2}+2\beta_{i}x}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle R_{i}=

\displaystyle\int_{\theta_{i-1}}^{\theta_{i}}k_{i}x^{2}\exp\left(\frac{-\alpha_{i}x^{2}+2\beta_{i}x}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}x.

The asymptotic behavior of the proposed estimators
\hat{\alpha}_{i,T}
and
\hat{\beta}_{i,T}
is demonstrated in the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

For
i=1,\cdots,m
, the continuous-type LSEs
\hat{\alpha}_{i,T}
and
\hat{\beta}_{i,T}

(1)

and

3.7
)(

\displaystyle G_{i,T}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\left(\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t\int_{0}^{T}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}W_{t}-L_{i,T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}W_{t}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\left(L_{i,T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{2}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t-\left(\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{2}\right).

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{L_{i,T}}{T}=\int_{\theta_{i-1}}^{\theta_{i}}\pi(x)\mathrm{d}x=\mathbb{P}\left(\theta_{i-1}\leq X_{t}<\theta_{i}\right)=P_{i},

(3.10)

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\theta_{i-1}}^{\theta_{i}}k_{i}x\exp\left(\frac{-\alpha_{i}x^{2}+2\beta_{i}x}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}x=K_{i},

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{2}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\theta_{i-1}}^{\theta_{i}}k_{i}x^{2}\exp\left(\frac{-\alpha_{i}x^{2}+2\beta_{i}x}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}x=R_{i},\quad a.s.

On the one hand, by Eq. (
3.10

\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{F_{i,T}}{T}=P_{i}R_{i}-K_{i}^{2},\quad a.s.

By It
\hat{\rm{o}}

3.10
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\braket{G_{i}}_{T}}{T}=

\displaystyle K_{i}^{2}\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t+P_{i}^{2}\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}^{2}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-2P_{i}K_{i}\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{i}^{2}R_{i}-K_{i}^{2}P_{i},\quad a.s.

and

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\braket{H_{i}}_{T}}{T}=

\displaystyle R_{i}^{2}\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t+K_{i}^{2}\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X^{2}_{t}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-2R_{i}K_{i}\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{i}^{2}P_{i}-K_{i}^{2}R_{i},\quad a.s.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\braket{G_{i}}_{T}=\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\left(P_{i}^{2}R_{i}-K_{i}^{2}P_{i}\right)T=\infty,

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\braket{H_{i}}_{T}=\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\left(R_{i}^{2}P_{i}-K_{i}^{2}R_{i}\right)T=\infty.

By (2) of Proposition
3

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{G_{i,T}}{\braket{G_{i}}_{T}}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{H_{i,T}}{\braket{H_{i}}_{T}}=0,\quad a.s.

Hence, we have

\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{G_{i,T}}{F_{i,T}}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{H_{i,T}}{F_{i,T}}=0,\quad a.s.

(3.13)

This completes the proof of strong consistency.

Furthermore, by (3) of Proposition
3

\frac{G_{i,T}}{\sqrt{\braket{G_{i}}_{T}}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{H_{i,T}}{\sqrt{\braket{H_{i}}_{T}}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1),

as
T\rightarrow\infty

3.11

3.14

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma\sqrt{T}G_{i,T}}{F_{i,T}}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,\frac{\sigma^{2}P_{i}}{P_{i}R_{i}-K_{i}^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma\sqrt{T}H_{i,T}}{F_{i,T}}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,\frac{\sigma^{2}R_{i}}{P_{i}R_{i}-K_{i}^{2}}\right),

(3.15)

\displaystyle\sqrt{T}\left(\hat{\beta}_{i,T}-\beta_{i}\right)\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,\frac{\sigma^{2}R_{i}}{P_{i}R_{i}-K_{i}^{2}}\right),

as
T\rightarrow\infty
.
Thus, we complete the proof of the asymptotic normality.

Now we consider the joint distribution of

and

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\left(P_{i}R_{i}-K_{i}^{2}\right)^{2}T}\mathbb{E}\left[G_{i,T}H_{i,T}\right].

By Eq. (

Remark 6
.

When the observations are discrete,
Mazzonetto and Pigato 2020
propose the discretized likehood function by approximating the (stochastic) integral by its “Riemann-It
\hat{\rm{o}}
” sum. The least squares estimator is proprosed by discretizing the SDE and minimizing the sums of squares of error. The two methods yield the same formula for estimators, as well as the asymptotic behavior.

5
Parameter estimation for
\sigma

In
Lejay and Pigato 2018
, they study the asymptotic behavior of estimators for the oscillating Brownian motion, i.e., a two-valued, discontinuous diffusion coefficient of a SDE as follows

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathrm{d}X_{t}=\sigma^{+}I_{\{X_{t}\geq 0\}}\mathrm{d}W_{t}+\sigma^{-}I_{\{X_{t}<0\}}\mathrm{d}W_{t},\\
&X_{0}=x,\end{aligned}\right.

(5.1)

where
\sigma^{+},\sigma^{-}>0
.

Now, we consider a generalized SDE of the form

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathrm{d}X_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left\{\left(\beta_{j}-\alpha_{j}X_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\sigma_{j}\mathrm{d}W_{t}\right\}I_{\{X_{t},j\}},\\
&X_{0}=x,\end{aligned}\right.

(5.2)

where
\sigma_{j}>0
,
j=1,\cdots,m
are the diffusion parameters and the others keep the same as Eq. (
1.1
). The LSEs for
\alpha_{j}
and
\beta_{j}
proposed in Section
3
and
4
are suitable in this case when long-time observations are available. Hence, in this section, we focus on the problem of proposing estimators for
\sigma_{j}
. The SDE (
5.1
) is a special case of the above SDE (
5.2
), i.e., the drift term is zero,
m=2
, and the only threshold is zero. Motivated by
Lejay and Pigato 2018
, we shall propose the QVEs for the diffusion parameters
\sigma_{i}
.

Recall that QVE stands for quadratic variation estimator and MQVE stands for modified quadratic variation estimator. Based on the continuously observed process
\{X_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq\Lambda}
, we define the QVEs for
\sigma_{i}
as follows

\tilde{\sigma}_{i,\Lambda}=\sqrt{\frac{\braket{XI_{\{X,i\}}}_{\Lambda}}{L_{i,\Lambda}}},

(5.3)

where
L_{i,\Lambda}=\int_{0}^{\Lambda}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t
. We emphasize that the QVEs only need short time observations, i.e.,
0<\Lambda<\infty
.

Denote by
\ell_{Y,t}^{x}
the local time at a point
x
, which represents the time spent by
Y
at
x
until
t
, which means

\ell_{Y,t}^{x}=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}I_{\{x-\varepsilon\leq Y_{s}\leq x+\varepsilon\}}\mathrm{d}\braket{Y}_{s}.

The following theorem shows the strong consistency of the continuous type QVEs
\tilde{\sigma}_{i,\Lambda}
.

Theorem 5
.

For all
0<\Lambda<\infty
, the continuous-type QVEs of
\sigma_{i}
defined in Eq. (
5.3
) admit the strong consistency, i.e.,

\tilde{\sigma}_{i,\Lambda}=\sigma_{i}
, a.s.

Proof of Theorem
5
. Decompose the
m
regimes into three cases where
\theta_{j-1}<\theta_{j}<X_{0}
,
\theta_{j-1}\leq X_{0}\leq\theta_{j}
, and
X_{0}<\theta_{j-1}<\theta_{j}
. We give the proof of the third case
X_{0}<\theta_{j-1}<\theta_{j}
and the proof of the other two cases is similar.

Consider the following process

X^{(i)}_{t}=\left\{\begin{aligned} 0,\quad\quad\quad&\text{if}\quad X_{t}\in(-\infty,\theta_{i-1}),\\
X_{t}-\theta_{i-1},\quad&\text{if}\quad X_{t}\in[\theta_{i-1},\theta_{i}),\\
\theta_{i}-\theta_{i-1},\quad&\text{if}\quad X_{t}\in[\theta_{i},\infty).\end{aligned}\right.

Hence, we have

\mathrm{d}X^{(i)}_{t}=\left\{\begin{aligned} 0,\quad\quad&\text{if}\quad X_{t}\in(-\infty,\theta_{i-1}),\\
\mathrm{d}X_{t},\quad&\text{if}\quad X_{t}\in[\theta_{i-1},\theta_{i}),\\
0,\quad\quad&\text{if}\quad X_{t}\in[\theta_{i},\infty).\end{aligned}\right.

Using Meyer-Tanaka’s formula for
X_{t}^{(i)}
yields

X_{t}^{(i)}=\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}X_{t}^{(i)}+\ell^{0}_{X^{(i)},t}.

By the fact that
\mathrm{d}X_{t}^{(i)}=I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}X_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle X_{t}^{(i)}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}X_{t}+\ell^{\theta_{i-1}}_{X,t}/2=\xi_{1,t}^{(i)}+\xi_{2,t}^{(i)}+\ell^{\theta_{i-1}}_{X,t}/2,

.
Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\braket{X^{(i)}}_{\Lambda}=

\displaystyle\braket{\xi_{1}^{(i)}}_{\Lambda}+\braket{\xi^{(i)}_{2}}_{\Lambda}+\braket{\ell^{\theta_{i-1}}_{X}}_{\Lambda}/4+2\braket{\xi^{(i)}_{1},\xi^{(i)}_{2}}_{\Lambda}+\braket{\xi^{(i)}_{1},\ell^{\theta_{i-1}}_{X}}_{\Lambda}+\braket{\xi^{(i)}_{2},\ell^{\theta_{i-1}}_{X}}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\braket{\xi_{1}^{(i)}}_{\Lambda}+\braket{\xi_{2}^{(i)}}_{\Lambda}-\braket{\ell^{\theta_{i-1}}_{X}}_{\Lambda}/4-\braket{\xi_{1}^{(i)},\ell^{\theta_{i-1}}_{X}}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle+2\braket{\xi_{1}^{(i)},X^{(i)}}_{\Lambda}+\braket{\ell^{\theta_{i-1}}_{X},X^{(i)}}_{\Lambda}.

Note that
\xi_{2,\Lambda}^{(i)}

\braket{\xi_{2}^{(i)}}_{\Lambda}=\sigma_{i}^{2}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t,

which implies that
\braket{\xi_{2}^{(i)}}_{\Lambda}/L_{i,\Lambda}=\sigma_{i}^{2}
.
Furthermore, the local time
\ell_{X}^{\theta_{i-1}}
,
\xi_{1}^{(i)}
, and
X^{(i)}
are continuous, and
\xi_{1}^{(i)}
is of finite variation. Thus,
\braket{\xi_{1}^{(i)}}_{\Lambda}
,
\braket{\xi_{1}^{(i)},\ell_{X}^{\theta_{i-1}}}_{\Lambda}
,
\braket{\xi_{1}^{(i)},X^{(i)}}_{\Lambda}

, and

equal to zero almost surely, which implies

\frac{\braket{XI_{\{X,i\}}}}{L_{i,\Lambda}}=\sigma_{i}^{2}.

Hence, we complete the proof.

\square

For any finite time interval
[0,\Lambda]
, we consider the discretely observed process
\{X_{t_{k}}\}_{k=0}^{n}
in
[0,\Lambda]
given by
t_{k}=kh
and
h=\Lambda/n
. Let
[X,Y]_{\Lambda}^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(X_{t_{k+1}}-X_{t_{k}})(Y_{t_{k+1}}-Y_{t_{k}})
and
[X]_{\Lambda}^{n}=[X,X]_{\Lambda}^{n}
. Then we define the discrete-type QVE as follows

\tilde{\sigma}_{i,n}=\sqrt{\frac{[XI_{\{X,i\}}]_{\Lambda}^{n}}{L_{i,n}}},

where
L_{i,n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}I_{\{X_{t_{k}},i\}}h
.

The following theorem shows the weak consistency of the discrete type QVEs
\tilde{\sigma}_{i,n}
.

Theorem 6
.

Assume that
0<\Lambda<\infty
and
h=\Lambda/n
. Then the discrete-type QVEs
\tilde{\sigma}_{i,n}
admit the weak consistency, i.e.,

\tilde{\sigma}^{2}_{i,n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma^{2}_{i},\quad\text{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

Proof of Theorem
6
. Let
Q^{(i)}_{\Lambda}=\{k:t_{k}\in(0,\Lambda),X_{t_{k}}\in[\theta_{i-1},\theta_{i})\}
. By Eq. (
4.11
), we have

\displaystyle{}^{n}_{\Lambda}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in Q^{(i)}_{\Lambda}}\left(X_{t_{k+1}}-X_{t_{k}}\right)^{2}=\xi^{(i)}_{1,n}+\xi^{(i)}_{2,n}+2\xi^{(i)}_{3,n},

where

\displaystyle\xi^{(i)}_{1,n}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in Q^{(i)}_{\Lambda}}\left(\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\beta_{j}-\alpha_{j}X_{t})I_{\{X_{t},j\}}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{2},\quad\xi^{(i)}_{2,n}=\sum_{k\in Q^{(i)}_{\Lambda}}\left(\sigma_{i}\Delta W_{t_{k+1}}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle\xi^{(i)}_{3,n}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in Q^{(i)}_{\Lambda}}\left(\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\beta_{j}-\alpha_{j}X_{t})I_{\{X_{t},j\}}\mathrm{d}t\right)\left(\sigma_{i}\Delta W_{t_{k+1}}\right).

Recall the definition of
L_{i,n}
and
L_{i,\Lambda}
, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}L_{i,n}=L_{i,\Lambda}=\int_{0}^{\Lambda}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t,\quad a.s.

By some trivial calculations, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\xi_{1,n}^{(i)}}{L_{i,n}}=\mathcal{O}(h),\quad a.s.

(5.4)

Since the quadratic variation of a martingale can be approximated by the sum of squared increments over shrinking partitions, we have

\displaystyle\xi^{(i)}_{2,n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(\sigma_{i}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\Delta W_{t_{k+1}}\right)^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\longrightarrow}}\braket{\xi_{2}^{(i)}}_{\Lambda}=\sigma_{i}^{2}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t.

Then we have

\frac{\xi_{2,n}^{(i)}}{L_{i,n}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma_{i}^{2},\quad\text{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

(5.5)

By the scaling properties of the increments of the Brownian motion, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\xi_{3,n}^{(i)}}{L_{i,n}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\longrightarrow}}0,\quad\text{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

(5.6)

Combining Eq. (
5.4
)-(
5.6
) yields

\frac{[XI_{\{X,i\}}]_{\Lambda}^{n}}{L_{i,n}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma_{i}^{2},\quad\text{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

Thus, we complete the proof.

\square

Now we consider MQVEs for the diffusion parameters
\sigma_{i}
. The main idea is to obtain the consistent estimators for the drift parameters
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{i}
first. Then we could minimize the error made by the drift term as much as possible. Hence, we need the long-time observations.

When the continuously observed process
\{X_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq T}
is obtained, the continuous-type MQVEs for
\sigma_{i}
are defined as

\hat{\sigma}_{i,T}=\sqrt{\frac{\braket{Y^{(i)}}_{T}}{L_{i,T}}},

where
Y^{(i)}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}I_{\{X_{s},i\}}\mathrm{d}X_{s}-\hat{\beta}_{i,T}\int_{0}^{t}I_{\{X_{s},i\}}\mathrm{d}s+\hat{\alpha}_{i,T}\int_{0}^{t}X_{t}I_{\{X_{s},i\}}\mathrm{d}s
.

The following theorem shows the strong consistency of the continuous-type MQVEs
\tilde{\sigma}_{i,T}
.

Theorem 7
.

The continuous-type MQVEs
\hat{\sigma}_{i,T}
admit the strong consistency, i.e.,

\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{i,T}=\sigma^{2}_{i},\quad a.s.

Proof of Theorem
7
. According to (1) of Theorem
2
, we have

\left(\hat{\beta}_{i,T},\hat{\alpha}_{i,T}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}\left(\beta_{i},\alpha_{i}\right),\quad\text{as}\quad T\rightarrow\infty.

By Eq. (
1.1
), we have

\int_{0}^{t}I_{\{X_{s},i\}}\mathrm{d}X_{s}=\beta_{i}\int_{0}^{t}I_{\{X_{s},i\}}\mathrm{d}s+\alpha_{i}\int_{0}^{t}X_{s}I_{\{X_{s},i\}}\mathrm{d}s+\sigma_{i}\int_{0}^{t}I_{\{X_{s},i\}}\mathrm{d}W_{s}.

Hence, we have

Y_{t}^{(i)}=\sigma_{i}\int_{0}^{t}I_{\{X_{s},i\}}\mathrm{d}W_{s}.

Furthermore, the quadratic variation of
Y^{(i)}
is given by

\braket{Y^{(i)}}_{T}=\sigma^{2}_{i}\int_{0}^{T}I_{\{X_{t},i\}}\mathrm{d}t,

which yields

\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{\frac{\braket{Y^{(i)}}_{T}}{L_{i,T}}}=\sigma_{i},\quad a.s.

\square

When the long time discretely observed process
\{X_{t_{k}}\}_{k=0}^{n}
is obtained, i.e.,
h\rightarrow 0
,
n\rightarrow\infty
, and
nh\rightarrow\infty
, the discrete-type MQVEs for
\sigma_{i}
are defined as

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}_{i,n}=\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(X_{t_{k+1}}-X_{t_{k}}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(\hat{\beta}_{j,n}-\hat{\alpha}_{j,n}X_{t_{k}}\right)I_{\{X_{t_{k}},j\}}h\right)^{2}I_{\{X_{t_{k}},i\}}}{L_{i,n}}}.

In the following theorem, we demonstrate the consistency of the MQVE.

Theorem 8
.

Under the Hypothesis
1
, the discrete-type MQVEs
\hat{\sigma}_{i,n}
admit the weak convergence, i.e.,

\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{i,n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma^{2}_{i},\quad\text{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

Proof of Theorem
8
. Let
Q^{(i)}_{nh}=\{k:t_{k}\in(0,nh),X_{t_{k}}\in[\theta_{i-1},\theta_{i})\}
. By Eq. (
4.11
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(X_{t_{k+1}}-X_{t_{k}}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(\hat{\beta}_{j,n}-\hat{\alpha}_{j,n}X_{t_{k}}\right)I_{\{X_{t_{k}},j\}}h\right)^{2}I_{\{X_{t_{k}},i\}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in Q^{(i)}_{nh}}\left(X_{t_{k+1}}-X_{t_{k}}-(\hat{\beta}_{i,n}-\hat{\alpha}_{i,n}X_{t_{k}})h\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi^{(i)}_{1,n}+\xi^{(i)}_{2,n}+2\xi^{(i)}_{3,n},

where

\displaystyle\xi^{(i)}_{1,n}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in Q^{(i)}_{nh}}\left(\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\beta_{j}-\alpha_{j}X_{t})I_{\{X_{t},j\}}-(\hat{\beta}_{i,n}-\hat{\alpha}_{i,n}X_{t_{k}})\mathrm{d}t\right)^{2},\quad

\displaystyle\xi^{(i)}_{2,n}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in Q^{(i)}_{nh}}\left(\sigma_{i}\Delta W_{t_{k+1}}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle\xi^{(i)}_{3,n}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in Q^{(i)}_{nh}}\left(\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\beta_{j}-\alpha_{j}X_{t})I_{\{X_{t},j\}}-(\hat{\beta}_{i,n}-\hat{\alpha}_{i,n}X_{t_{k}})h\right)\left(\sigma_{i}\Delta W_{t_{k+1}}\right).

On the one hand, similar arguments as Eq. (
5.5
) and (
5.6
) yield

\frac{\xi_{2,n}^{(i)}}{L_{i,n}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma_{i}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\xi_{3,n}^{(i)}}{L_{i,n}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\longrightarrow}}0,\quad\text{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

On the other hand, we now consider the error term
\xi_{1,n}^{(i)}/L_{i,n}
, which is the main improvement of MQVEs
\hat{\sigma}_{i,n}
compared with QVEs
\tilde{\sigma}_{i,n}
.
Decomposing
I_{\{X_{t},i\}}
into three terms as Eq. (
4.6
) yields

\displaystyle\xi_{1,n}^{(i)}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(I_{\{X_{t_{k}},i\}}^{(1)}+I_{\{X_{t_{k}},i\}}^{(2)}+I_{\{X_{t_{k}},i\}}^{(3)}\right)\left[\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\beta_{j}I_{\{X_{t},j\}}-\hat{\beta}_{i,n}\right)\mathrm{d}t\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}X_{t}I_{\{X_{t},j\}}-\hat{\alpha}_{i,n}X_{t_{k}}\right)\mathrm{d}t\right]^{2}.

When
I_{\{X_{t_{k}},i\}}^{(2)}=1
, by Eq. (
4.12
), and (
4.21
)-(
4.22
), we have

\displaystyle\left[\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\beta_{j}I_{\{X_{t},j\}}-\hat{\beta}_{i,n}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}X_{t}I_{\{X_{t},j\}}-\hat{\alpha}_{i,n}X_{t_{k}}\right)\mathrm{d}t\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left[\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\left\lvert\beta_{i}-\hat{\beta}_{i,n}\right\rvert\mathrm{d}t+\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\left\lvert\alpha_{i}X_{t}-\alpha_{i}X_{t_{k}}+\alpha_{i}X_{t_{k}}-\hat{\alpha}_{i,n}X_{t_{k}}\right\rvert\mathrm{d}t\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left[\left\lvert\beta_{i}-\hat{\beta}_{i,n}\right\rvert h+\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\left(\lvert\alpha_{i}\rvert\lvert X_{t}-X_{t_{k}}\rvert+\lvert\alpha_{i}-\hat{\alpha}_{i,n}\rvert\lvert X_{t_{k}}\rvert\right)\mathrm{d}t\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{h\log\log nh}{n}\right),\quad a.s.

We thus have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{L_{i,n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}I_{\{X_{t_{k}},i\}}^{(2)}\left[\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\beta_{j}I_{\{X_{t},j\}}-\hat{\beta}_{i,n}\right)\mathrm{d}t\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}X_{t}I_{\{X_{t},j\}}-\hat{\alpha}_{i,n}X_{t_{k}}\right)\mathrm{d}t\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log\log nh}{n}\right),\quad a.s.

By Eq. (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{L_{i,n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(I_{\{X_{t_{k}},i\}}^{(1)}+I_{\{X_{t_{k}},i\}}^{(3)}\right)\left[\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\beta_{j}I_{\{X_{t},j\}}-\hat{\beta}_{i,n}\right)\mathrm{d}t\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}X_{t}I_{\{X_{t},j\}}-\hat{\alpha}_{i,n}X_{t_{k}}\right)\mathrm{d}t\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{2}{L_{i,n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(I_{\{X_{t_{k}},i\}}^{(1)}+I_{\{X_{t_{k}},i\}}^{(3)}\right)\left[\beta^{\ast}h+\alpha^{\ast}\sup_{s\in(t_{k},t_{k+1}]}|X_{s}|h\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{h^{3}\log\log(1/h)}\right),\quad a.s.

Hence, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\xi_{i,n}^{(1)}}{L_{i,n}}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log\log nh}{n}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{h^{3}\log\log(1/h)}\right),\quad a.s.

Hence, we complete the proof.

\square

Remark 7
.

The main improvement of MQVEs compared with QVEs is to give the consistent estimators for the drift parameters, which ensures us to eliminate the error term
\xi^{(i)}_{1,n}
.

6.2
An application in U.S. treasury rate

In the previous discussion we presented an efficient estimation procedure for the drift parameters of a threshold OU process.
Su and Chan 2015
apply the threshold OU process to the three-month U.S. treasury rate, which is a high-frequency observed process with data collected on a daily basis. The rates are published on business days and the data shall be treated as equally spaced. However, it is not clear to verify whether the information accumulates twice over two non-business days as fast as over a one-day gap between consecutive daily data.

Here, we consider the U.S. treasury rate based on the data sets from Federal Reserve Bank. We report the treasury rate process for four assets including the ten-year, five-year, two-year, and one-year US treasury rate, all of which are collected from “2002-1-2” to “2022-9-16”. We adopt the convention that the equal time interval for the “daily” rates is
h=0.046
while one unit in time represents one month.

We model the rate process using the following SDE

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathrm{d}X_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left[(\beta_{j}-\alpha_{j}X_{t})\mathrm{d}t+\sigma_{j}\mathrm{d}W_{t}\right]I_{\{X_{t},j\}},\\
&X_{0}=x.\end{aligned}\right.

Note that determining the number and values of thresholds (i.e., the values of
m
and
\theta_{j}
) is an essential problem. In real-world applications, we shall transform the model as the following piecewise linear model

Y_{t_{k}}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left[(\beta_{j}-\alpha_{j}X_{t_{k}})h+\sigma_{j}(W_{t_{k+1}}-W_{t_{k}})\right]I_{\{X_{t_{k}},j\}},

where
Y_{t_{k}}=X_{t_{k+1}}-X_{t_{k}}
. Then we can use the “
segmented
” function in “
segmented
” package of
\emph{R}
to obtain the number and values of thresholds. The “
segmented
” package is a tool designed for segmented regression analysis. This package facilitates the detection and fitting of breakpoints in data, enabling the modeling of distinct linear relationships in different segments. The “
segmented
” function within the “
segmented
” package is primarily employed for segmented regression analysis.

Figure
2
displays the U.S. treasury rate process and we set the thresholds as the horizontal dashed blue lines. We shall see from the figure that each of the treasury rate processes has only one threshold (
m=2
). The value of thresholds of 10-year and 5-year treasury rate processes are similar, as are 2-year and 1-year treasury rate processes. These are the ones that match the reality, since the trends of 10-year and 5-year treasury rate processes are about the same, while 2-year and 1-year treasury rate processes are about the same.

Table
5
summarizes the estimations of the drift and diffusion parameters where “value” is the estimation and
“CI” is the
95\%
confidence interval. Since the long rate is closely related to economic growth and inflation, the drift term does not have a significant slope with respect to its current treasury rate. The drift term in the first regime of the one-year and two-year treasury rate process is close to
0
, which shows that the short rate evolves as a martingale process until it hits the second regime. In the second regime, the drift term has a negative slope which ensures the ergodicity of the process. In general, the short-rate process has the same general trend as the long-rate process.

7
Disscussion

We have considered the parameter estimation problem for the parameters of a threshold Ornstein
\mathit{-}
Uhlenbeck process with multiple thresholds. Under the condition that the thresholds are known, we propose asymptotically consistent estimators for the drift and diffusion parameters. Our simulation results demonstrate that the proposed estimators are superior to the generalized moment estimators proposed by
Hu and Xi 2022
and the usual quadratic variation estimators.

There are several important directions for further research. We would like to study the least squares estimator for all parameters including the thresholds. Consider the following objective function

\displaystyle\min_{\alpha_{j},\theta_{j}}\int_{0}^{T}\bigg|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(\dot{X}_{t}+\alpha_{j}X_{t}\right)I_{\{X_{t},j\}}\bigg|^{2}\mathrm{d}t.

By the fact that
I_{\{X_{t},j\}}=I_{\{X_{t}<\theta_{j}\}}-I_{\{X_{t}<\theta_{j-1}\}}
, the above objective function is equivalent to

\displaystyle\min_{\alpha_{j},\theta_{j}}\int_{0}^{T}\bigg\lvert\sum_{j=2}^{m}(\alpha_{j-1}-\alpha_{j})X_{t}I_{\{X_{t}<\theta_{j-1}\}}+(\dot{X}_{t}+\alpha_{m}X_{t})\bigg\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}t.

It is almost impossible to obtain the closed-form formula of the estimator
\hat{\alpha}_{j}
and
\hat{\theta}_{j}
,
j=1,\cdots,n
. However, various specialized methods have been developed to demonstrate the consistency of the suggested estimators
(
Frydman 1980
;
van der Vaart 1998
, see, e.g.,)
.
Some other further research may include investigating the statistical inference for the generalized threshold diffusions.

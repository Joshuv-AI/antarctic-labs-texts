Global regularity results for non-homogeneous growth fractional problems
Jacques Giacomoni, Deepak Kumar, and Konijeti Sreenadh

Abstract
The main goal of this article is to show the global Hölder regularity of weak solutions to a class of problems involving the fractional ( p , q ) (p,q) -Laplacian, denoted by ( − Δ ) p s 1 + ( − Δ ) q s 2 (-\Delta)^{s_{1}}_{p}+(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}_{q} , for 1 < p , q < ∞ 1<p,q<\infty and s 1 , s 2 ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) s_{1},s_{2}\in(0,1) . We use a suitable Caccioppoli inequality and a local boundedness result in order to prove the weak Harnack inequality. Consequently, by employing a suitable iteration process, we establish the interior Hölder continuity result for local weak solutions. The global Hölder regularity result we prove expands and improves the regularity results of Giacomoni, Kumar and Sreenadh (arXiv: 2102.06080) to the subquadratic case (that is, q < 2 q<2 ) and to the more general right hand side, which requires a different and new approach. Moreover, we establish a nonlocal Harnack type inequality for weak solutions and a strong maximum principle for weak super-solutions, which are of independent interest. Key words: Fractional ( p , q ) (p,q) -Laplacian, non-homogeneous nonlocal operator, local boundedness result, weak Harnack inequality, interior and boundary Hölder continuity, Harnack inequality, strong maximum principle. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35J60, 35R11, 35B45, 35D30.

Introduction

In this paper, we establish the Hölder continuity results for the weak solutions to the following nonlocal problem:

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}_{p}u+(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}_{q}u=f\quad\text{in }\;\Omega,

(1.1)

where
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
C^{1,1}
boundary,
1<q,p<\infty
,
0<s_{2}\leq s_{1}<1
and
f\in L^{\gamma}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
with
\gamma\begin{cases}>N/(ps_{1})\quad\mbox{if }N>ps_{1},\\
\geq 1\quad\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The fractional
p
-Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}
is defined as

{(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u(x)}=2\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(x)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dy.

These kinds of nonlocal operators have their applications in real world problems, such as obstacle problems, the study of American options in finance, game theory, image processing and anomalous diffusion phenomena (see
[
18
]
for more details). Due to this reason, elliptic problems involving the fractional Laplacian have been extensively studied in the last two decades.

The leading operator,
(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}_{p}+(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}_{q}
, in problem (
1.1
) is known as the fractional
(p,q)
-Laplacian, which is non-homogeneous (unless
p=q
) in the sense that for any
t>0
, there does not exist any
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
such that
((-\Delta)^{s_{1}}_{p}+(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}_{q})(tu)=t^{\sigma}((-\Delta)^{s_{1}}_{p}u+(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}_{q}u)
holds for all
u\in W^{s_{1},p}(\Omega)\cap W^{s_{2},q}(\Omega)
.
The operator is the fractional analogue of the
(p,q)
-Laplacian (
-\Delta_{p}-\Delta_{q}
), which arises from the study of general reaction-diffusion equations with non-homogeneous diffusion and transport aspects. The problems involving these kinds of operators have applications in biophysics, plasma physics and chemical reactions, with double phase features, where the function
u
corresponds to the concentration term, and the differential operator represents the diffusion coefficient, for details, see
[
36
]
and references therein.

Regarding the regularity results for weak solutions to quasilinear elliptic equations, Lieberman in
[
33
]
proved
C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
regularity results for problems containing more general operators than the classical
p
-Laplacian. Subsequently, in
[
34
]
, the same author established the interior Hölder continuity of the gradient of weak solutions to problems where the leading operator has Orlicz type of growth condition. See
[
35
]
for more details on the regularity theory of quasilinear problems.

Concerning the regularity results for problems involving nonlocal operators, the case of the fractional Laplacian is well understood. In particular, in
[
11
]
Caffarelli and Silvestre obtained the interior regularity results, while Ros-Oton and Serra
[
38
]
proved the optimal boundary regularity. Precisely, in the latter work, the authors proved that weak solutions of the fractional Laplacian problem (i.e.,
p=q=2
and
s_{1}=s_{2}
) with a bounded right hand side and homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions, are in
C^{0,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. One can rely on
[
19
]
for regularity results of linear fractional problems involving a more general kernel. For the nonlinear and homogeneous operator case (
p=q\neq 2
and
s_{1}=s_{2}
), in
[
16
]
, Di Castro et al. established interior Hölder regularity results for nonlocal equations whose prototypes include the fractional
p
-Laplacian. Here, they established the Caccioppoli inequality and the Logarithmic lemma, and following De Giorgi’s iteration technique, proved the local Hölder continuity result. In
[
31
]
, Korvenpää et al. further obtained the interior and boundary Hölder regularity results for obstacle problems (also covering the Dirichlet problem) with
zero
right hand side.
We also mention the work of M. Cozzi
[
13
]
, for Hölder regularity results for minimizers of functional involving the fractional
p
-Laplacian energy in the fractional De Giorgi’s class.
Using a slightly different approach, Iannizzotto et al. in
[
26
]
, proved that the weak solutions of problem (
1.1
) with bounded right hand side, belong to the space
C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
, for some
\alpha\in(0,s_{1}]
. By constructing the barrier functions as a solution to some nonlocal equation on
B_{1}
(fractional torsion problem) and scaling it appropriately to get a sub-solution, they established a weak Harnack type inequality to prove the interior regularity results. Moreover, using the barrier arguments, in the spirit of Krylov’s approach (as in
[
38
]
), they obtained the boundary behavior of the weak solutions.
Subsequently, in
[
9
]
, Brasco et al. established the optimal Hölder interior regularity result and proved that the local weak solution
u\in W^{s,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
of problem (
1.1
) for the superquadratic and homogeneous case (i.e.,
2\leq p=q
and
s_{1}=s_{2}=s
) with
f\in L^{r}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
, for suitable
r>0
, is in
C^{0,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
, for all
\alpha<\min\{1,ps/(p-1)\}
.
Consequently, with the help of the boundary behavior of the solution from
[
26
]
, we get the
C^{s}
regularity result up to the boundary. We additionally refer to
[
32
]
, for regularity results for nonlocal problems involving measure data. In
[
27
]
, Iannizzotto et al. extended the results of
[
38
]
to the nonlinear setting and proved that the weak solution
u
of problem (
1.1
), again for the case
2\leq p=q
,
s_{1}=s_{2}=s\in(0,1)
and bounded right hand side, satisfies
\frac{u}{d^{s}}\in C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
, for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
, where
d(x):={\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
.

Due to the non-homogeneous and nonlinear nature of the fractional
(p,q)
-operators, the corresponding nonlocal problems have attracted the attention of many researchers in recent years. For instance, see
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
24
]
for the existence and multiplicity results for problems involving the fractional
(p,q)
-Laplacian, and we refer
[
37
]
for a survey of recent developments on non-standard growth problems. On the other hand, there is not much literature available regarding the regularity results. Particularly, in
[
24
]
, Goel et al. have obtained the
C^{0,\alpha}_{\rm loc}
regularity result, with some unspecified
\alpha\in(0,1)
, for weak bounded solutions in the superquadratic case. Further, we mention the recent work of
[
20
]
, where the global Hölder continuity results (in the spirit of
[
26
]
) for weak solutions to problems involving the fractional
(-\Delta)_{g}^{s}
-Laplacian, where
g
is a convex Young’s function, is proved. However, this does not include our problem, even for the case
s_{1}=s_{2}
, because of the power type of growth conditions (2.2) and (2.4), there. In
[
14
]
, the authors have obtained the interior Hölder regularity of viscosity solutions to a class of fractional double phase problems. Recently, in
[
21
]
, the authors of the present work have obtained almost optimal global Hölder continuity results for weak solutions to fractional
(p,q)
-problems in the superquadratic case (
p\geq q\geq 2
). To establish the optimal interior regularity result for local weak solutions (without the boundedness assumption), the authors proved a local boundedness result, which uses a new Caccioppoli type inequality for non-homogeneous operators. Then, employing Moser’s iteration technique on the discrete differential of the solution and exploiting the local boundedness of the function
f
, they proved suitable Besov space inclusion (which in turn gives Hölder continuity). However, the approach fails when either of the exponent is less than
2
. By establishing the control over the barrier functions involving the distance function, the authors further obtained almost optimal
d^{s_{1}}
boundary behavior of the weak solution. This coupled with the interior Hölder regularity result proves the almost optimal
s_{1}
-Hölder continuity result globally in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Concerning the Harnack type inequality, in
[
39
]
, Trudinger established Harnack and weak Harnack type estimates for weak solutions to general second order quasilinear problems. As an application to these, the author obtains Hölder continuity for weak solutions.
However, in the nonlocal case, the classical Harnack inequality fails, see for instance
[
29
]
. Subsequently,
in
[
28
]
, the same author proved the Harnack inequality (where the nonnegativity in the whole
\mathbb{R}^{N}
is not assumed) with nonlocal tails for problems involving the fractional Laplacian. For general
p
, we refer to
[
17
]
for Harnack and weak Harnack type inequality for minimizers as well as weak solutions. In this paper, the authors have used suitable Caccioppoli type estimates and a local boundedness result to achieve these aims.

Inspired from the above discussion, in this work, we answer the open question of Hölder regularity results for weak solutions to fractional
(p,q)
-problems, for the case
1<q<2
and
1<p<\infty
. We prove the interior regularity result for local weak solutions, as in Theorem
2.1
, by establishing a weak Harnack type inequality (see Proposition
4.1
) and then we complete the proof of Hölder continuity in the spirit of
[
24
, Theorem 2.10]
. As noted earlier, for the homogeneous fractional
p
-Laplacian case with
p<2
, to prove the weak Harnack inequality, in
[
26
]
a suitably scaled version of the solution to the fractional torsion problem is used for appropriate sub-solutions. Nevertheless, due to the lack of the scaling property, we can not rely on this method for our case. Moreover, we observe that by establishing the Logarithmic lemma (in accordance with
[
16
, Lemma 1.3]
and Lemma
3.1
, below), we can not prove the Hölder continuity result by applying De Giorgi’s iteration technique as in the proof of
[
16
, Theorem 1.2]
. This is due to the presence of non-homogeneous power of the parameter
\lambda
(taken in the place of
d
, there), which prevents from choosing the suitable constant
k
involved in the proof of
[
16
, Lemma 5.1]
. So, we follow the idea of
[
17
]
to prove our weak Harnack inequality. However, because of the non-homogeneous nature of the operator, we obtain two nonlocal tails with different behaviors, which require a technical care at several places. We overcome the difficulty raised by these nonlocal tails by further improving the parameters involved in the proofs (by adding an additional quantity of the form
r^{s_{1}p-s_{2}q}
). Our interior regularity result complements that of
[
16
]
to the case of non-homogeneous fractional
(p,q)
-problems with non-homogeneous right hand side in
L^{\gamma}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
, whereas in
[
21
]
in addition to
q\geq 2
,
f\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
is assumed. We strongly believe that this approach, while currently focused on a specific form of the operator, can be further applied for a wider class of nonlocal and non-homogeneous operators, see Remarks
2.2
and
2.4
in this regard.
It is also worth noting that we do not assume any boundedness condition on the solution in our interior regularity result, thanks to Proposition
3.1
, which extends that of
[
21
, Proposition 3.2]
. The proof of Proposition
3.1
relies on a Caccioppoli type inequality for non-homogeneous operators (see
[
21
, Lemma 3.1]
) and De Giorgi’s type iteration argument. The main obstacle in the proof is due to
f\in L^{\gamma}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
, for
\gamma<\infty
, as we can not use the Moser type iteration argument available for the homogeneous case in
[
10
]
. In this paper, clever convexity arguments proved that
u_{+}
is a sub-solution with right hand side
|f|
, whenever
u
is a solution to (
1.1
) (with
p=q
and
s_{1}=s_{2}
), but seem to be inefficient in presence of non-homogeneous operators. Subsequently, using the boundary behavior of
[
21
, Proposition 3.11]
, we obtain the global Hölder continuity result for weak solutions. Additionally, employing the approach of
[
17
, Theorem 1.1]
to our case, we obtain the Harnack inequality, as in Theorem
2.3
. Similar to the homogeneous case, our Harnack inequality is valid for sign changing solutions also and the sign changing behavior is displayed in terms of the nonlocal tails. As an application to our global Hölder regularity, we obtain a strong maximum principle for fractional
(p,q)
-problems, valid for all
p\geq q>1
. We observe that the proof of
[
21
, Theorem 2.6]
can not be generalized to this case, as our weak Harnack type inequality involves an additional constant term (
r^{\frac{ps_{1}-qs_{2}}{p-q}}
). So, we follow the approach of
[
15
]
and prove that continuous weak super-solutions are viscosity super-solutions of fractional
(p,q)
-problem with right hand side
zero
. Subsequently, we apply a suitable barrier function and the weak comparison principle to extend this result for problems involving more general nonlinearities (see Theorem
2.4
).

j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, define sequences
R_{j}=\frac{R_{0}}{4^{j}}
,
B_{j}=B_{R_{j}}
,
\frac{1}{2}B_{j}=B_{R_{j}/2}
.
We claim that there exist
\alpha>0
(a generic constant),
\omega>0
, a non-decreasing sequence
\{m_{j}\}
and a non-increasing sequence
\{M_{j}\}
such that

\displaystyle m_{j}\leq\inf_{B_{j}}\;u\leq\sup_{B_{j}}\;u\leq M_{j},\qquad M_{j}-m_{j}=\omega R_{j}^{\alpha}.

We will proceed by induction. For
j=0
, we set
M_{0}=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R_{0}})}
and
m_{0}=M_{0}-\omega R_{0}^{\alpha}
, where
\omega
satisfies

\omega\geq\frac{2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R_{0}})}}{R_{0}^{\alpha}}>0.

(5.1)

Hence,
m_{0}\leq\displaystyle\inf_{B_{0}}\;u\leq\displaystyle\sup_{B_{0}}\;u\leq M_{0}.
Suppose that the claim holds for all
i\in\{0,\dots,j\}
for some
j\in\mathbb{N}
. To prove the claim for
j+1
, we first consider the case
1<\frac{N(p-1)}{N-s_{1}p}
. Then

\displaystyle M_{j}-m_{j}

\displaystyle=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\frac{1}{2}B_{j+1}}(M_{j}-u(x))dx+\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\frac{1}{2}B_{j+1}}(u(x)-m_{j})dx

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\frac{1}{2}B_{j+1}}(M_{j}-u(x))^{t}dx\Big)^{1/t}+\Big(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\frac{1}{2}B_{j+1}}(u(x)-m_{j})^{t}dx\Big)^{1/t},

(5.2)

for some
1<t<\frac{N(p-1)}{N-s_{1}p}
. Employing Corollary
4.1
, for the choice
r=R_{j+1}
and
R=R_{j}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\varsigma(M_{j}-m_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{B_{j+1}}(M_{j}-u)+\inf_{B_{j+1}}(u-m_{j})+C\sum_{(\ell,s)}T_{\ell-1}((M_{j}-u)_{-};x_{0},R_{j})

\displaystyle\quad+C\sum_{(\ell,s)}T_{\ell-1}((u-m_{j})_{-};x_{0},R_{j})+CR_{j}^{\frac{ps_{1}-qs_{2}}{p-q}}+C\|f\|_{L^{\gamma}(B_{R_{0}})}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}R_{j}^{\frac{\gamma s_{1}p-N}{\gamma(p-1)}},

that is,

\displaystyle\underset{B_{j+1}}{\rm osc}\;u

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\varsigma\right)(M_{j}-m_{j})+C\sum_{(\ell,s)}T_{\ell-1}((M_{j}-u)_{-};x_{0},R_{j})+C\sum_{(\ell,s)}T_{\ell-1}((u-m_{j})_{-};x_{0},R_{j})

\displaystyle\quad+CR_{j}^{\frac{ps_{1}-qs_{2}}{p-q}}+C\|f\|_{L^{\gamma}(B_{R_{0}})}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}R_{j}^{\frac{\gamma s_{1}p-N}{\gamma(p-1)}}.

(5.3)

Now, we estimate the different tail terms appearing in the above expression:

\displaystyle T_{\ell-1}((u-m_{j})_{-};x_{0},R_{j})^{\ell-1}=R_{j}^{s\ell}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\int_{B_{k}\setminus B_{k+1}}\frac{(u(y)-m_{j})^{\ell-1}}{|x_{0}-y|^{N+s\ell}}dy+\int_{B_{0}^{c}}\frac{(u(y)-m_{j})^{\ell-1}}{|x_{0}-y|^{N+s\ell}}dy\right],

where
B_{0}^{c}:=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{0}
. By the induction hypothesis and
[
26
, Proof of Theorem 5.4]
, for
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\int_{B_{k}\setminus B_{k+1}}\frac{(u(y)-m_{j})^{\ell-1}}{|x_{0}-y|^{N+s\ell}}dy\leq c\omega^{\ell-1}R_{j}^{\alpha(\ell-1)-\ell s}S_{\ell}(\alpha),

where
S_{\ell}(\alpha):=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(4^{\alpha k}-1)^{\ell-1}}{4^{\ell sk}}\rightarrow 0
as
\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}
. For the second term, we set
Q(u;x_{0},R_{0}):=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{0})}+T_{p-1}(u;x_{0},R_{0})+T_{q-1}(u;x_{0},R_{0})
. By observing that
m_{j}\leq\inf_{B_{j}}u\leq\sup_{B_{j}}u\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{0})}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{0}}\frac{(u(y)-m_{j})^{\ell-1}}{|x_{0}-y|^{N+s\ell}}dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{0}}\frac{(|u(y)|+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{0})})^{\ell-1}}{|x_{0}-y|^{N+s\ell}}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{0})}^{\ell-1}+T_{\ell-1}(u;x_{0},R_{0})^{\ell-1}}{R_{0}^{sl}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle T_{\ell-1}((u-m_{j})_{-};x_{0},R_{j})^{\ell-1}\leq c\omega^{\ell-1}R_{j}^{\alpha(\ell-1)}S_{\ell}(\alpha)+cR_{j}^{s\ell}\frac{\big(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{0})}+T_{\ell-1}(u;x_{0},R_{0})\big)^{\ell-1}}{R_{0}^{s\ell}},

and an analogous estimate holds for
T_{\ell-1}((M_{j}-u)_{-};x_{0},R_{j})^{\ell-1}
also. Thus, from (
5
) and the inductive hypothesis, we get

\displaystyle\underset{B_{j+1}}{\rm osc}\;u

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\varsigma\right)\omega R_{j}^{\alpha}+c\omega R_{j}^{\alpha}S_{p}(\alpha)^{1/(p-1)}+cR_{j}^{\frac{s_{1}p}{p-1}}\frac{Q(u;x_{0},R_{0})}{R_{0}^{s_{1}p/(p-1)}}

\displaystyle\quad+c\omega R_{j}^{\alpha}S_{q}(\alpha)^{1/(q-1)}+cR_{j}^{\frac{s_{2}q}{q-1}}\frac{Q(u;x_{0},R_{0})}{R_{0}^{s_{2}q/(q-1)}}+CR_{j}^{\frac{ps_{1}-qs_{2}}{p-q}}+C\|f\|_{L^{\gamma}(B_{R_{0}})}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}R_{j}^{\frac{\gamma s_{1}p-N}{\gamma(p-1)}}.

Then, for
\alpha<\min\big\{\frac{ps_{1}}{p-1},\frac{qs_{2}}{q-1},\frac{ps_{1}-qs_{2}}{p-q},\frac{\gamma s_{1}p-N}{\gamma(p-1)}\big\}
, we have

\displaystyle\underset{B_{j+1}}{\rm osc}\;u

\displaystyle\leq 4^{\alpha}\left[1-\varsigma+cS_{p}(\alpha)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+cS_{q}(\alpha)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}\right]\omega R_{j+1}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{c4^{\alpha}}{R_{0}^{\alpha}}\left[Q(u;x_{0},R_{0})+R_{0}^{\frac{ps_{1}-qs_{2}}{p-q}}+\|f\|_{L^{\gamma}(B_{R_{0}})}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}R_{0}^{\frac{\gamma s_{1}p-N}{\gamma(p-1)}}\right]R_{j+1}^{\alpha},

(5.4)

where in the last line we have used
R_{j}=4^{\alpha}R_{j+1}\leq R_{0}
.
On account of
S_{\ell}(\alpha)\to 0
as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
, we can choose
\alpha<\min\big\{\frac{ps_{1}}{p-1},\frac{qs_{2}}{q-1},\frac{ps_{1}-qs_{2}}{p-q},\frac{\gamma s_{1}p-N}{\gamma(p-1)}\big\}
small enough such that

4^{\alpha}\left(1-\varsigma+cS_{p}(\alpha)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+cS_{q}(\alpha)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}\right)\leq 1-\frac{\varsigma}{4}

and set

\omega=\frac{4^{\alpha+1}\;c}{\varsigma\;R_{0}^{\alpha}}\Big(Q(u;x_{0},R_{0})+R_{0}^{\frac{ps_{1}-qs_{2}}{p-q}}+\|f\|_{L^{\gamma}(B_{R_{0}})}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}R_{0}^{\frac{\gamma s_{1}p-N}{\gamma(p-1)}}\Big).

(5.5)

Note that with the above choice of
\omega
, (
5.1
) is satisfied if the constant
c
, appearing in (
5
), is replaced by a bigger constant such that
4c/\varsigma\geq 2
. Thus, from (
5
), we have

\displaystyle\underset{B_{j+1}}{\rm osc}\;u\leq\omega R_{j+1}^{\alpha}.

Therefore, we pick
m_{j+1},\;M_{j+1}
such that

\displaystyle m_{j}\leq m_{j+1}\leq\inf_{B_{j}}\;u\leq\sup_{B_{j}}\;u\leq M_{j+1}\leq M_{j}\quad\mbox{and }M_{j+1}-m_{j+1}=\omega R_{j+1}^{\alpha}.

To finish the proof of the theorem, we fix
r\in(0,R_{0})
. Let
j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
be such that
R_{j+1}<r\leq R_{j}
, then taking into account (
5.5
) and
R_{j}\leq 4r
, we have

\displaystyle\underset{B_{r}}{\rm osc}\;u\leq\underset{B_{j}}{\rm osc}\;u\leq\omega R_{j}^{\alpha}\leq C\Big(Q(u;x_{0},R_{0})+R_{0}^{\frac{ps_{1}-qs_{2}}{p-q}}+\|f\|_{L^{\gamma}(B_{R_{0}})}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}R_{0}^{\frac{\gamma s_{1}p-N}{\gamma(p-1)}}\Big)\frac{r^{\alpha}}{R_{0}^{\alpha}}.

For the case
\frac{N(p-1)}{N-s_{1}p}\leq 1
(this forces
p<2
), using the induction hypothesis, we observe that

\displaystyle M_{j}-u\leq(M_{j}-m_{j})^{2-p}(M_{j}-u)^{p-1},\quad\mbox{in }B_{j},

and this still holds for
(u-m_{j})
. Hence,

\displaystyle M_{j}-m_{j}

\displaystyle\leq(M_{j}-m_{j})^{2-p}\Big[\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\frac{1}{2}B_{j+1}}(M_{j}-u(x))^{p-1}dx+\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\frac{1}{2}B_{j+1}}(u(x)-m_{j})^{p-1}dx\Big].

This implies that

\displaystyle M_{j}-m_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\Big[\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\frac{1}{2}B_{j+1}}(M_{j}-u(x))^{p-1}dx+\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\frac{1}{2}B_{j+1}}(u(x)-m_{j})^{p-1}dx\Big]^{1/(p-1)}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{2-p}{p-1}}\left[\Big(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\frac{1}{2}B_{j+1}}(M_{j}-u)^{p-1}dx\Big)^{1/(p-1)}+\Big(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\frac{1}{2}B_{j+1}}(u-m_{j})^{p-1}dx\Big)^{1/(p-1)}\right],

which is similar to (
5
), with
t=p-1
. Multiplying both the sides of the above expression by
\bar{\varsigma}:=\varsigma/2^{\frac{2-p}{p-1}}
, we can employ Corollary
4.1
. Then, the rest of the proof follows similarly as before with
\bar{\varsigma}
in place of
\varsigma
.
This completes the proof of the theorem.
\square

6
Strong maximum principle

The main goal of this section is to prove our strong maximum principle. We first recall the notion of viscosity solution (see e.g.,
[
30
]
). To this end, for
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
and
D\subset\Omega
, set the following:

\displaystyle N_{u}:=\{x\in\Omega:\ \nabla u(x)=0\},\quad d_{u}(x):=\mathrm{dist}(x,N_{u})\quad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle C^{2}_{\beta}(D):=\biggl\{u\in C^{2}(D):\ \sup_{x\in D}\Big(\frac{\min\{d_{u}(x),1\}^{\beta-1}}{|\nabla u(x)|}+\frac{|D^{2}u(x)|}{d_{u}(x)^{\beta-2}}\Big)<\infty\biggr\}.

Definition 6.1

A function
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to[-\infty,\infty]
is said to be a viscosity super-solution to
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s_{1}}u+(-\Delta)_{q}^{s_{2}}u\geq 0
in
\Omega
, if the following hold:

(i)

u
is lower semi-continuous in
\Omega
such that
u<\infty
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
u>-\infty
everywhere in
\Omega
;

(ii)

u_{-}\in L_{s_{1}p}^{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L_{s_{2}q}^{q-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
;

(iii)

if whenever
B_{r}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
and
\phi\in C^{2}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
are such that

\displaystyle\phi(x_{0})=u(x_{0})\quad\mbox{and }u(x)\geq\phi(x)\ \ \mbox{in }B_{r}(x_{0}),

and one of the following holds

(a)

p>\frac{2}{2-s_{1}}
and
q>\frac{2}{2-s_{2}}
or
\nabla\phi(x_{0})\neq 0
,

(b)

either
p\leq\frac{2}{2-s_{1}}
or
q\leq\frac{2}{2-s_{2}}
;
\nabla\phi(x_{0})=0
such that
x_{0}
is an isolated critical point of
\phi
and
\phi\in C^{2}_{\beta}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
, for some
\beta>\max\{\frac{s_{1}p}{p-1},\frac{s_{2}q}{q-1}\}
;

then
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s_{1}}\phi_{r}(x_{0})+(-\Delta)_{q}^{s_{2}}\phi_{r}(x_{0})\geq 0
, where

\displaystyle\phi_{r}(x)=\begin{cases}\phi(x)&\mbox{if }x\in B_{r}(x_{0}),\\
u(x)&\mbox{if }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}(x_{0}).\end{cases}

(6.1)

We recall the following weak comparison principle (e.g., see
[
24
]
).

Proposition 6.1

Let
\widetilde{W}^{s,p}(\Omega):=\{u\in L^{p}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\exists\;U\Supset\Omega\ \mbox{with }\ \|u\|_{W^{s,p}(U)}<\infty\}
.
Assume that
u,v\in\widetilde{W}^{s_{1},p}(\Omega)
are such that

\displaystyle\sum_{(\ell,s)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\frac{[u(x)-u(y)]^{\ell-1}(\phi(x)-\phi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+s\ell}}dxdy\leq\sum_{(\ell,s)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\frac{[v(x)-v(y)]^{\ell-1}(\phi(x)-\phi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+s\ell}}dxdy

for all
\phi\in W^{s_{1},p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
u\leq v
in
\Omega^{c}
. Then,
u\leq v
in
\Omega
.

Now, we prove that continuous weak super-solutions are viscosity super-solutions.

Lemma 6.1

Let
u\in W^{s_{1},p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
be a weak super-solution to
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s_{1}}u+(-\Delta)_{q}^{s_{2}}u\geq 0
in
\Omega
. Then,
u
is also a viscosity super-solution.

Proof.

From the assumption on
u
, it is clear that items (i) and (ii) of Definition
6.1
are satisfied. To prove (iii), on the contrary, assume that there exist
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
\phi\in C^{2}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
such that
\phi(x_{0})=u(x_{0})
,
u(x)\geq\phi(x)
in
B_{r}(x_{0})
, either (a) or (b) of Definition
6.1
(iii) holds and

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s_{1}}\phi_{r}(x_{0})+(-\Delta)_{q}^{s_{2}}\phi_{r}(x_{0})<0,

for some
r>0
and
\phi_{r}
given by (
6.1
). By
[
30
, Lemma 3.8]
, we have
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s_{1}}\phi_{r}(x)
and
(-\Delta)_{q}^{s_{2}}\phi_{r}(x)
are continuous at
x_{0}
. Therefore, there exist
\rho^{\prime}\in(0,r)
and
\eta>0
such that

\displaystyle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s_{1}}\phi_{r}(x)+(-\Delta)_{q}^{s_{2}}\phi_{r}(x)<-\eta\quad\mbox{for all }x\in B_{\rho^{\prime}}(x_{0}).

Following the proof of
[
30
, Lemma 3.9]
, there exist
\epsilon>0
,
\rho\in(0,\rho^{\prime}/2)
and
b\in C_{c}^{2}(B_{\rho/2}(x_{0}))
such that
b(x_{0})=1
with
0\leq b\leq 1
, and
\psi_{\epsilon}(x)=\phi_{r}(x)+\epsilon b(x)
satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|(-\Delta)_{p}^{s_{1}}\psi_{\epsilon}(x)-(-\Delta)_{p}^{s_{1}}\phi_{r}(x)|<\frac{\eta}{2}\quad\mbox{and }\sup_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|(-\Delta)_{q}^{s_{2}}\psi_{\epsilon}(x)-(-\Delta)_{q}^{s_{2}}\phi_{r}(x)|<\frac{\eta}{2}.

Consequently, we obtain

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s_{1}}\psi_{\epsilon}(x)+(-\Delta)_{q}^{s_{2}}\psi_{\epsilon}(x)\leq 0\quad\mbox{for all }x\in B_{\rho}(x_{0}).

Let
v\in W^{s_{1},p}_{0}(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))
be a non-negative function, then multiplying the above equation with it and upon integration, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{(\ell,s)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\frac{[\psi_{\epsilon}(x)-\psi_{\epsilon}(y)]^{\ell-1}(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+s\ell}}d\mu\leq 0\leq\sum_{(\ell,s)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\frac{[u(x)-u(y)]^{\ell-1}(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+s\ell}}d\mu,

where
d\mu=dxdy
. Further,
\psi_{\epsilon}\leq u
in
B_{\rho}(x_{0})^{c}
. Therefore, by the weak comparison principle, we obtain
\psi_{\epsilon}\leq u
in
B_{\rho}(x_{0})
. But this contradicts
\psi_{\epsilon}(x_{0})=\phi(x_{0})+\epsilon b(x_{0})=u(x_{0})+\epsilon>u(x_{0})
. Thus,
u
is a viscosity super-solution. This completes the proof of the lemma.
\square

Lemma 6.2

Suppose
1<q\leq p<\infty
. Let
u\in W^{s_{1},p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
be a weak super-solution to
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s_{1}}u+(-\Delta)_{q}^{s_{2}}u\geq 0
in
\Omega
and
u\geq 0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
. Then, either
u\equiv 0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
or
u>0
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

We will show that if
u\not\equiv 0
, then
u>0
in
\Omega
. By the weak comparison principle, we have
u\geq 0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Case I
: If
\Omega
is connected.

We proceed similarly to
[
8
, Theorem A.1]
.
Let
K\Subset\Omega
be any connected compact such that
u\not\equiv 0
in
K
. Then, we will show that
u>0
a.e. in
K
.
Since
K
is compact,
K\subset\{x\in\Omega:\ {\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)>2r\}
, for some
r>0
. Moreover, there exists a finite covering
\{B_{r/2}(x_{i})\}_{\{i=1,2\dots,n\}}
for
K
such that

\displaystyle|B_{r/2}(x_{i})\cap B_{r/2}(x_{i+1})|>0\quad\mbox{for all }i=1,2,\dots,n-1.

(6.2)

Suppose
u\equiv 0
on some subset of
K
with positive measure. Then, for some
i\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
,

\displaystyle|E:=\{x\in B_{r/2}(x_{i}):\ u(x)=0\}|>0.

For
\lambda>0
, set

\displaystyle U_{\lambda}(x):=\log\Big(1+\frac{u(x)}{\lambda}\Big),\quad\mbox{for all }x\in B_{r/2}(x_{i}).

By observing that
U_{\lambda}\equiv 0
on
E
, for
x\in B_{r/2}(x_{i})
and
y\in E
, we have

\displaystyle|U_{\lambda}(x)|^{q}=\frac{|U_{\lambda}(x)-U_{\lambda}(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N+qs_{2}}}|x-y|^{N+qs_{2}},

which upon integration with respect to
y\in E
and
x\in B_{r/2}(x_{i})
yields

\displaystyle|E|\int_{B_{r/2}(x_{i})}|U_{\lambda}(x)|^{q}dx\leq cr^{N+qs_{2}}\int_{B_{r/2}(x_{i})}\int_{B_{r/2}(x_{i})}\frac{|U_{\lambda}(x)-U_{\lambda}(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N+qs_{2}}}dxdy.

(6.3)

On account of

\displaystyle\bigg|\log\frac{u(x)+\lambda}{u(y)+\lambda}\bigg|^{q}=|U_{\lambda}(x)-U_{\lambda}(y)|^{q}

and (
3.2
), we deduce from (
6.3
) that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r/2}(x_{i})}\bigg|\log\Big(1+\frac{u(x)}{\lambda}\Big)\bigg|^{q}dx\leq c\frac{r^{N+s_{2}q}}{|E|}\big(r^{N-s_{2}q}+r^{N-s_{1}p}(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}+\lambda)^{p-q}\big),

where we have used that
u\geq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
(consequently,
u_{-}=0
). Passing to the limit as
\lambda\to 0
in the above expression, we get

\displaystyle u=0\quad\mbox{a.e. in }B_{r/2}(x_{i}).

By using the property (
6.2
), we can proceed similarly for balls
B_{r/2}(x_{i-1})
and
B_{r/2}(x_{i+1})
(note that
|E:=\{x\in B_{r/2}(x_{i+1}):\ u(x)=0\}|\geq|B_{r/2}(x_{i+1})\cap B_{r/2}(x_{i})|>0
) and so on for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, that is,
u=0
a.e. on
K
. This is a contradiction to our assumption that
u\not\equiv 0
in
K
. Thus
u>0
a.e. in
K
.

Since
\Omega
is open and connected (path connected), there exists a sequence of compact connected sets
\Omega_{n}\subset\Omega
such that

\displaystyle\Omega=\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Omega_{n},\quad\Omega_{n}\subset\Omega_{n+1}\quad\forall\ n\in\mathbb{N}\quad\mbox{and }u\not\equiv 0\quad\mbox{in }\Omega_{n},\quad\mbox{for all }n\geq n_{0}.

Then, proceeding as above, we get
u>0
a.e. in
\Omega_{n}
for all
n\geq n_{0}
. Therefore,
u>0
a.e. in
\Omega

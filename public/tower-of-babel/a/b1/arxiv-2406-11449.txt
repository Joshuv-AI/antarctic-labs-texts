Abstract
. This paper first investigates solvability of Hermitian-Einstein equation on a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle on the complement of an arbitrary closed subset in a compact Hermitian manifold. The uniqueness of Hermitian-Einstein metrics on a Zariski open subset in a compact Kähler manifold was only figured out by Takuro Mochizuki recently, for this model the second part of this paper gives an affirmative answer to a question proposed by Takuro Mochizuki and it leads to an alternative approach to the unique issue. We also prove stability from solvability of Hermitian-Einstein equation, which together with the classical existence result of Carlos Simpson in particular establish a Kobayashi-Hitchin bijective correspondence. The argument is also effective in more general settings, including basic models of Takuro Mochizuki, as well as non-Kähler and semi-stable contexts.

1.
Introduction

Let
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
be a holomorphic vector bundle on a compact Kähler manifold
(N,J,g)
, the Hermitian-Einstein (abbreviated as
\HE
, also called Hermitian-Yang-Mills because of its relation to Yang-Mills theory) equation reads as

(1.1)

\begin{split}\Lambda F_{H}^{\perp}\triangleq\Lambda F_{H}-\frac{\tr\Lambda F_{H}}{\rank(E)}\id_{E}=0,\end{split}

where
\Lambda
is the operator obtained as the adjoint of the multiplication of the associated fundamental form
\omega_{g}=g(J(\cdot),\cdot)
, and
F_{H}
denotes the Chern curvature of a Hermitian metric
H
on
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
. The
\HE
equation is one of the most important nonlinear partial differential equations in complex geometry and solving such an equation provides an effective approach to construct Yang-Mills connections on holomorphic vector bundles. A Hermitian metric satisfying the
\HE
equation will be called a
\HE
metric and a holomorphic vector bundle will be called indecomposable if it is not a direct sum of serval holomorphic vector bundles. Thanks to a series of works
including those of Narasimhan-Seshadri
[
19
]
, Kobayashi
[
8
]
, Lübke
[
15
]
, Metha-Ramanathan
[
16
]
, Donaldson
[
3
,
4
]
and Uhlenbeck-Yau
[
24
]
, the celebrated Kobayashi-Hitchin correspondence (also called Hitchin-Kobayashi correspondence or Donaldson-Uhlenbeck-Yau correspondence) states that for an indecomposable holomorphic vector bundle, the existence and uniqueness of
\HE
metrics is equivalent to the Mumford-Takemoto stability. It is a landmark in complex geometry and has been generalized in many different directions, the large majority of which themselves became very important results in complex geometry.

Furthermore, the semi-stable Kobayashi-Hitchin correspondence
[
7
,
9
,
10
]
indicates the equivalence between the existence of an approximate Hermitian-Einstein (abbreviated as
\AHE
) structure and the Mumford-Takemoto semi-stability, where an
\AHE
structure is a family of Hermitian metrics
\{H_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon>0}
such that the
\HE
equation can be solved in the sense of

(1.2)

\begin{split}\sup\limits_{N}|\Lambda F_{H_{\epsilon}}^{\perp}|_{H_{\epsilon}}\xrightarrow{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}0.\end{split}

In fact, to define stability and semi-stability, it is enough to pick a Gauduchon metric (that is,
\partial\overline{\partial}\omega_{g}^{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}N-1}=0
) as the reference metric. By a result of Gauduchon
[
6
]
, there exists on any compact complex manifold a Gauduchon metric in a fixed conformal class. Therefore, the stability and semi-stability also make sense on compact Hermitian manifolds and the above two mentioned results on
\HE
equations also hold on non-Kähler manifolds
[
2
,
12
,
22
]
indeed. For more related descriptions on this problem, the reader may also consult
[
13
,
14
]
.

It is a natural to ask under what assumptions
\HE
equations can be solved on noncompact Kähler manifolds and this question had been systematically investigated in various contexts, see
[
1
,
11
,
18
,
17
,
20
,
21
,
23
,
27
,
28
,
29
]
and references therein. From both the theoretic point of view and that of applications, it is important and highly desirable to establish a general existence theory for
\HE
equations on noncompact spaces with a few technical assumptions as possible, so that it covers a wide range of applications in different areas.

A first purpose of this paper is to study the above issue on a new but natural class of noncompact manifolds. We first have the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,J)
be a compact complex manifold and
\Sigma\subset M
be an arbitrary closed subset. Suppose
g
is a Hermitian metric on
M\setminus\Sigma
equivalent to a Riemannian metric on
M
and
\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\omega_{g}^{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M-1}\geq 0
. If
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},K)
is a stable Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle on
M\setminus\Sigma
with
|\Lambda F_{K}^{\perp}|\in L^{\infty}(M\setminus\Sigma)
, there exists a
\HE
metric compatible with
K
.

The general strategy in proving Theorem
1.1
is not so involved. In fact, one may first solve Dirichlet problems of
\HE
equations on an exhaustion series of compact submanifolds with boundaries. Secondly, once the zeroth estimate of a sequence of solutions to Dirichlet problems is established, the evolved metrics will converge to the desired
\HE
metric. Alternatively, the strategy in proving Theorem
1.2
below also applies to Theorem
1.1
, see Remark
3.5
.

We are further interested in the semi-stable context and prove

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,J)
be a compact complex manifold and
\Sigma\subset M
be an arbitrary closed subset. Suppose
g
is a Hermitian metric on
M\setminus\Sigma
equivalent to a Riemannian metric on
M
and
\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\omega_{g}^{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M-1}\geq 0
. If
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},K)
is a semi-stable Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle on
M\setminus\Sigma
with
|\Lambda F_{K}^{\perp}|\in L^{\infty}(M\setminus\Sigma)
, there exists an
\AHE
structure compatible with
K
.

In Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
, we require neither Kähler condition nor Gauduchon condition on
\omega_{g}
. We should mention that if
\Sigma\subset M
is Zariski closed and
g
is the restriction of a Kähler metric on
M
(we call it the model of Simpson since it satisfies the key assumptions in
[
23
]
), Theorem
1.1
was proved in
[
23
]
and Theorem
1.2
was obtained in
[
28
]
. Actually, the authors in
[
28
]
studied the case of noncompact Gauduchon manifolds satisfying Simpson’s type assumptions (see
[
23
, Section 2]
) and
|d\omega^{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}N-1}_{g}|\in L^{2}(N)
. Different from the standard method of exhaustion in stable case, the strategy we adopt in semi-stable case is to extend the method of continuity by Uhlenbeck-Yau
[
24
]
to noncompact case, meaning to solve a family of perturbed
\HE
equations
(\ref{epsilonequation})
on the noncompact space
M\setminus\Sigma
. The solvability of them was established on compact Gauduchon manifolds in
[
12
,
24
]
, on noncompact Gauduchon manifolds satisfying Simpson’s type assumptions and
|d\omega^{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}N-1}_{g}|\in L^{2}(N)
in
[
28
]
with the help of solving the following Poisson equation:

(1.3)

\begin{split}\sqrt{-1}\Lambda\partial\overline{\partial}f=-\frac{1}{\rank(E)}\tr\left(\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{K}-\frac{\int_{N}\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{K}\dvol_{g}}{\rank(E)\vol(N)}\id_{E}\right).\end{split}

However, the solvability of such a equation is not clearly since there is no enough information on both
\Sigma
and
\omega_{g}
. To get around this obstacle, the observation is a carefully new choice of a perturbed heat flow
(\ref{flow})
and the advantage is that, this flow preserves the property
\det H=\det K
. Finally, the existence of an
\AHE
structure will be proved by examining the limit of
\{H_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon>0}
when
\epsilon
goes to zero. In this step, one should note that again the generality of
\Sigma
and
\omega_{g}
may not imply suitable Stokes lemma on
M\setminus\Sigma
which is a pivotal point in establishing global integral estimates
[
23
,
28
]
, our argument removes this technical constraint.

Below we consider the uniqueness problem of
\HE
metrics on a stable holomorphic vector bundle. In compact case, it is not hard to know that
\HE
metrics are unique up to constant multiples, see
[
9
,
13
]
for example. However, the noncompact issue becomes more subtle. Even for the case that
\Sigma\subset M
is a Zariski closed subset, the uniqueness of
\HE
metrics is unknown until the recent work
[
17
]
of Mochizuki, while the existence of
\HE
metrics was established by Simpson
[
23
]
more than thirty years ago. Explicitly, we have

Theorem 1.3

(Mochizuki, Remark 2.13 in
[
17
]
)
.

Let
(M,J,g)
be a compact Kähler manifold and
\Sigma\subset M
be a Zariski closed subset. Suppose
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},K)
is a stable Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle on
M\setminus\Sigma
with
|\Lambda F_{K}|\in L^{\infty}(M\setminus\Sigma)
and
H_{1},H_{2}
are two
\HE
metrics compatible with
K
, then we have
H_{1}=H_{2}
.

In the same paper, Mochizuki also pointed out (see the paragraph above
[
17
, Remark 2.13]
) that the key point is, it is not clear whether the stability of
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},K)
implies the stability of
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},H)
for a
\HE
metric
H
and we need additional work. This nuisance does not appear in the case when the base space is compact since the stability condition on compact manifolds does not depend on the choice of metrics on a holomorphic vector bundle. In this paper, we answer Mochizuki’s question affirmatively for the model of Simpson as follows.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M,J,g)
be a compact Kähler manifold and
\Sigma\subset M
be a Zariski closed subset. Suppose
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},K)
is a stable Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle on
M\setminus\Sigma
with
|\Lambda F_{K}|\in L^{\infty}(M\setminus\Sigma)
and
H
is a
\HE
metric compatible with
K
, then
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},H)
is stable.

As a direct consequence of Theorem
1.4
, we shall present an alternative approach to the uniqueness issue of
\HE
metrics on noncompact manifolds in Section 4.

Next we focus on the necessary condition for the existence of
\HE
metrics on the model of Simpson. It is easy to see that the existence of a
\HE
metric
H
on an indecomposable holomorphic vector bundle
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
must imply the stability of
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},H)
. However, similar as Mochizuki’s question mentioned above, the stability with respect to the background data
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},K)
is generally unknown, hence a Kobayashi-Hitchin bijective correspondence between stability and existence of
\HE
metrics remains open. In present work, we are able to prove stability (resp. semi-stability) of
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},K)
from stability (resp. semi-stability) of
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},H)
, therefore establishing the opposite direction of Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
as follows.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(M,J,g)
be a compact Kähler manifold and
\Sigma\subset M
be a Zariski closed subset. Suppose
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},K)
is an indecomposable Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle on
M\setminus\Sigma
with
|\Lambda F_{K}|\in L^{\infty}(M\setminus\Sigma)
, then we have

(1)

If there exists a
\HE
metric compatible with
K
, then
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},K)
is stable.

(2)

If there exists an
\AHE
structure compatible with
K
, then
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},K)
is semi-stable.

Gathering Simpson’s existence result in
[
23
]
with the stability result in Theorem
1.5
, we conclude a Kobayashi-Hitchin bijective correspondence for the model of Simpson.

Corollary 1.6

(Kobayashi-Hitchin bijective correspondence)
.

Let
(M,J,g)
be a compact Kähler manifold and
\Sigma\subset M
be a Zariski closed subset. Suppose
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},K)
is an indecomposable Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle on
M\setminus\Sigma
with
|\Lambda F_{K}|\in L^{\infty}(M\setminus\Sigma)
, then

(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},K)
is stable
\Leftrightarrow
it admits a unique
\HE
metric compatible with
K
.

By Theorems
1.2
,
1.5
, we also obtain

Corollary 1.7

(Semi-stable Kobayashi-Hitchin bijective correspondence)
.

Let
(M,J,g)
be a compact Kähler manifold and
\Sigma\subset M
be a Zariski closed subset. Suppose
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},K)
is a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle on
M\setminus\Sigma
with
|\Lambda F_{K}|\in L^{\infty}(M\setminus\Sigma)
, then

(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},K)
is semi-stable
\Leftrightarrow
it admits an
\AHE
structure compatible with
K
.

It may be mentioned that Mochizuki’s discussion in
[
17
]
relies on certain crucial properties of the Donaldson functional on the space of Hermitian metrics and a priori estimate of
\HE
equation due to Simpson
[
23
]
, as well as the celebrated regularity result of weakly holomorphic subbundles due to Uhlenbeck-Yau
[
24
]
. In a different spirit, without the recourse to above deep results, our approach which is based on Theorem
1.4
, seems simpler and has a merit that the proof can be readily applied to the case where the base space is
\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{C}\times M
for a compact Kähler manifold
M
, and also to the non-Kähler context. In fact, we also build

(1)

Uniqueness of
\HE
metrics on
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{C}\times M
for a compact Kähler manifold
M
, and noncompact Gauduchon manifolds, see Theorems
4.7
,
4.8
,
4.10
.

(2)

Kobayashi-Hitchin bijective correspondence on
\mathbb{C}
and noncompact Gauduchon manifolds, see Theoremss
4.9
,
4.11
. We should note that the existence part on
\mathbb{C}
was previously given by Mochizuki
[
17
]
.

(3)

Semi-stable Kobayashi-Hitchin bijective correspondence on noncompact Gauduchon manifolds, see Theorem
4.12
.

We should remark that
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{C}\times M
for a compact Kähler manifold
M
are basic models satisfying Mochizuki’s assumption (see
[
17
, Assumption 2.1]
). Compared with that of Simpson, the model of Mochizuki allows the possibility of infinite volume. But due to the infinite volume, one needs additional work to investigate the semi-stable Kobayashi-Hitchin bijective correspondence on
\mathbb{C}
and it will be addressed elsewhere, see
[
27
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
be holomorphic vector bundle on a Hermitian manifold
(N,J,g)
. For any two Hermitian metrics
K
,
H
on
E
, we always denote by
h=K^{-1}H\in C^{\infty}(N,\End(E))
the section defined by
H(\cdot,\cdot)=K(h(\cdot),\cdot)
. Throughout this paper, unless otherwise mentioned, inner products and norms are always taken with respect to
K
and
g
.

Definition 2.1
.

We say
H
is compatible with
K
if

(2.1)

\begin{split}\det K=\det H,\ |h|\in L^{\infty}(N),\ |\overline{\partial}_{E}h|\in L^{2}(N).\end{split}

Let us recall the definition of stability in
[
23
]
. We define

(2.2)

\begin{split}\deg(E,K)=\int_{N}\tr\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{K}\dvol_{g},\end{split}

where
F_{K}=D_{K}^{2}
and
D_{K}
is the Chern connection. Given a saturated sub-sheaf
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{O}_{N}(E)
, there exists a closed complex analytic subset
Z(\mathcal{S})
of complex codimension at least two such that
\mathcal{S}|_{N\setminus Z(\mathcal{S})}
becomes a locally free
\mathcal{O}_{N}
-module inheriting the holomorphic structure and metric from
E
, and therefore

\deg(\mathcal{S},K)
can be naturally defined by integrating outside
Z(\mathcal{S})
.

Definition 2.2
.

We say
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},K)
is (semi-)stable if

(2.3)

\begin{split}\frac{\deg(\mathcal{S},K)}{\rank(\mathcal{S})}(\leq)<\frac{\deg(E,K)}{\rank(E)},\end{split}

for any nontrivial saturated sub-sheaf
\mathcal{S}
.

Remark 2.3
.

In fact, we have the Chern-Weil formula

(2.4)

\begin{split}\deg(\mathcal{S},K)=\int_{N\setminus Z(\mathcal{S})}(\tr(\sqrt{-1}\pi_{K}\circ\Lambda F_{K})-|\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{K}|^{2})\dvol_{g},\end{split}

where
\pi_{K}
denotes the projection onto
\mathcal{S}|_{N\setminus Z(\mathcal{S})}
with respect to
K
.

It is not hard to see

(2.5)

\begin{split}D_{H}=D_{K}+h^{-1}\partial_{K}h,\ F_{H}=F_{K}+\overline{\partial}_{E}(h^{-1}\partial_{K}h),\end{split}

(2.6)

\begin{split}\sqrt{-1}\Lambda\partial\overline{\partial}\tr h=(\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{K}-\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{H},h)+|\overline{\partial}_{E}h\circ h^{-\frac{1}{2}}|^{2},\end{split}

where
\partial_{K}
is the
(1,0)
-component of
D_{K}
. By
[
13
, Section 7.4]
and
[
23
, Section 4]
, for
x\in N
and a
K
-unitary basis
\{e_{a}\}_{a=1}^{\rank(E)}
, we set

(2.7)

\begin{split}\log h(x)=\sum\limits_{a=1}^{\rank(E)}\lambda_{a}e_{a}\otimes e^{a},\ \overline{\partial}_{E}\log h(x)=\sum\limits_{a,b=1}^{\rank(E)}A_{a}^{b}e^{a}\otimes e_{b},\end{split}

(2.8)

\begin{split}\Theta[\log h](\overline{\partial}_{E}\log h)(x)=\sum\limits_{a,b=1}^{\rank(E)}\Theta(\lambda_{a},\lambda_{b})A_{a}^{b}e^{a}\otimes e_{b},\end{split}

where
\Theta(x,y)=\frac{e^{y-x}-1}{y-x}
for
x\neq y
and
\Theta(x,y)=1
for
x=y
. Then we have

(2.9)

\begin{split}\sqrt{-1}\Lambda\partial\overline{\partial}|\log h|^{2}&=-2\sqrt{-1}\Lambda\overline{\partial}<\partial_{K}\log h,\log h>\\
&=-2\sqrt{-1}\Lambda\overline{\partial}<h^{-1}\partial_{K}h,\log h>\\
&=-2\sqrt{-1}\Lambda<\overline{\partial}_{E}(h^{-1}\partial_{K}h),\log h>+2\sqrt{-1}\Lambda<h^{-1}\partial_{K}h,\partial_{K}\log h>\\
&=2<\sqrt{-1}\Lambda(F_{K}-F_{H}),\log h>+2<\Theta[\log h](\overline{\partial}_{E}\log h),\overline{\partial}_{E}\log h>,\end{split}

where we have used

(2.10)

\begin{split}\tr(h^{-1}\partial_{K}h\circ\log h)=\tr(\log h\circ\partial_{K}\log h),\end{split}

and the fact (see
[
22
,
(3.12)
]
)

(2.11)

\begin{split}\sqrt{-1}\Lambda<h^{-1}\partial_{K}h,\partial_{K}\log h>=<\Theta[\log h](\overline{\partial}_{E}\log h),\overline{\partial}_{E}\log h>.\end{split}

We denote by
\nabla^{H}
the connection induced by
D_{H}
and the Chern connection
\nabla_{g}
on
TN
, by
\nabla
the connection induced by
D_{K}
and
\nabla_{g}
. In the local holomorphic coordinate
(z^{1},...,z^{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}N})
of
N
and the holomorphic frame
\{e_{1},...,e_{\rank E}\}
of
E
, we have

(2.12)

\begin{split}\sqrt{-1}\Lambda\partial\overline{\partial}|h^{-1}\partial_{K}h|^{2}_{H}&=|\nabla^{H}(h^{-1}\partial_{K}h)|^{2}_{H}+g^{\overline{j}i}<\nabla^{H}_{i}\nabla^{H}_{\overline{j}}(h^{-1}\partial_{K}h),h^{-1}\partial_{K}h>_{H}\\
&+g^{\overline{j}i}<h^{-1}\partial_{K}h,\nabla^{H}_{\overline{i}}\nabla^{H}_{j}(h^{-1}\partial_{K}h))>_{H}.\end{split}

Let
T_{\nabla_{g}}
denote the torsion of
\nabla_{g}
, it follows

(2.13)

\begin{split}\nabla^{H}_{i}\nabla^{H}_{\overline{j}}(h^{-1}\partial_{K,m}h)&=\nabla^{H}_{i}\overline{\partial}_{\overline{j}}(h^{-1}\partial_{K}h)_{m}\\
&=\nabla^{H}_{i}F_{H,m\overline{j}}-\nabla^{H}_{i}F_{K,m\overline{j}}\\
&=\nabla^{H}_{m}F_{H,i\overline{j}}+T_{im}^{l}F_{K,l\overline{j}}\\
&+T_{im}^{l}\overline{\partial}_{\overline{j}}(h^{-1}\partial_{K}h)_{l}-\nabla^{H}_{i}F_{K,m\overline{j}},\end{split}

where
T_{im}^{n}
is given by
T_{\nabla_{g}}(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{m}})=T_{im}^{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{l}}
and we have used the relation

(2.14)

\begin{split}0&=\nabla^{H}_{i}F_{H,m\overline{j}}-\nabla^{H}_{m}F_{H,i\overline{j}}+\nabla^{H}_{\overline{j}}F_{H,im}-T_{im}^{l}F_{H,l\overline{j}}\\

which comes from the Bianchi identity,
T^{1,1}_{\nabla_{g}}=0
and
T_{\nabla_{g}}(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{m}})\in T^{1,0}N
. Hence

(2.15)

\begin{split}&g^{\overline{j}i}<\nabla^{H}_{i}\nabla^{H}_{\overline{j}}(h^{-1}\partial_{K}h),h^{-1}\partial_{K}h>_{H}\\

&-C_{1}(|F_{K}|_{H}+|\Lambda F_{H}|_{H})|h^{-1}\partial_{K}h|^{2}_{H}\\
&-C_{1}|T||h^{-1}\partial_{K}h|_{H}|\nabla^{H}(h^{-1}\partial_{K}h)|_{H},\end{split}

where
C_{1}
is positive constant independent of
K
and

. Moreover, it holds that

(2.16)

\begin{split}\nabla^{H}_{\overline{i}}\nabla^{H}_{j}(h^{-1}\partial_{K}h)_{m\alpha}^{\beta}&=\nabla^{H}_{j}\nabla^{H}_{\overline{i}}(h^{-1}\partial_{K}h)_{m\alpha}^{\beta}-R_{j\overline{i}m}^{l}(h^{-1}\partial_{K}h)_{l\alpha}^{\beta}\\
&+F_{H,j\overline{i}\gamma}^{\beta}(h^{-1}\partial_{K}h)_{m\alpha}^{\gamma}-F_{H,j\overline{i}\alpha}^{\gamma}(h^{-1}\partial_{K}h)_{m\gamma}^{\beta},\end{split}

where

is given by
R_{\nabla_{g}}(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{j}},\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}^{i}})\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{m}}=R_{j\overline{i}m}^{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{l}}
. Hence there is positive constant
C_{2}
doesn’t depend on
K
and
H
such that

(2.17)

\begin{split}&g^{\overline{j}i}<h^{-1}\partial_{K}h,\nabla^{H}_{\overline{i}}\nabla^{H}_{j}(h^{-1}\partial_{K}h)>_{H}\\
&\geq-C_{2}(|\nabla F_{K}|_{H}+|T_{\nabla_{g}}||F_{K}|_{H}+|\nabla\Lambda F_{H}|_{H})|h^{-1}\partial_{K}h|_{H}\\
&-C_{2}(|R_{\nabla_{g}}|+|F_{K}|_{H}+|\Lambda F_{H}|_{H})|h^{-1}\partial_{K}h|^{2}_{H}\\
&-C_{2}|T||h^{-1}\partial_{K}h|_{H}|\nabla^{H}(h^{-1}\partial_{K}h)|_{H}.\end{split}

Combing
(\ref{AA})
,

,

Lemma 2.4
.

There is a positive constant
C_{3}
independent of
K
and
H
such that

(2.18)

\begin{split}&\sqrt{-1}\Lambda\partial\overline{\partial}|h^{-1}\partial_{K}h|^{2}_{H}\\
&\geq-C_{3}(|\nabla F_{K}|_{H}+|T_{\nabla_{g}}||F_{K}|_{H}+|\nabla\Lambda F_{H}|_{H})|h^{-1}\partial_{K}h|_{H}\\
&-C_{3}(|T_{\nabla_{g}}|^{2}+|R_{\nabla_{X}}|+|F_{K}|_{H}+|\Lambda F_{H}|_{H})|h^{-1}\partial_{K}h|^{2}_{H}\\
&+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla^{H}(h^{-1}\partial_{K}h)|^{2}_{H}.\end{split}

3.
Solvability of Hermitian-Einstein equations

Lemma 3.1
.

For a compact Hermitian manifold
(N,J,g)
with boundary and suppose
H
is a solution of the following perturbed heat flow

(3.1)

\begin{split}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}H^{-1}\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}=-2\left(\sqrt{-1}(\Lambda F_{H}-\frac{\tr\Lambda F_{K}}{\rank(E)}\id_{E})+\epsilon\log(K^{-1}H)\right),\\
H(0)=K,\ H|_{\partial N}=K|_{\partial N}.\end{array}\right.\end{split}

Then we have
\det K=\det H
.

Proof.

We compute

(3.2)

\begin{split}&(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-2\sqrt{-1}\Lambda\partial\overline{\partial})\left(e^{2\epsilon t}\tr(\sqrt{-1}(\Lambda F_{H}-\frac{\tr\Lambda F_{K}}{\rank(E)}\id_{E})+\epsilon\log(K^{-1}H))\right)\\
&=2\epsilon e^{2\epsilon t}\tr\sqrt{-1}(\Lambda F_{H}-\frac{\tr\Lambda F_{K}}{\rank(E)}\id_{E})+2\epsilon^{2}e^{2\epsilon t}\log\det(K^{-1}H)\\
&+e^{2\epsilon t}(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-2\sqrt{-1}\Lambda\partial\overline{\partial})\tr\sqrt{-1}(\Lambda F_{H}-\frac{\tr\Lambda F_{K}}{\rank(E)}\id_{E})\\
&+\epsilon e^{2\epsilon t}(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-2\sqrt{-1}\Lambda\partial\overline{\partial})\log\det(K^{-1}H)\\
&=e^{2\epsilon t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tr\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{H}-2e^{2\epsilon t}\sqrt{-1}\Lambda\partial\overline{\partial}\tr\sqrt{-1}(\Lambda F_{H}-\frac{\tr\Lambda F_{K}}{\rank(E)}\id_{E})\\
&-2\epsilon e^{2\epsilon t}\sqrt{-1}\Lambda\partial\overline{\partial}\log\det(K^{-1}H)\\
&=-2e^{2\epsilon t}\tr\left(\sqrt{-1}\Lambda\overline{\partial}_{E}\partial(\sqrt{-1}(\Lambda F_{H}-\frac{\tr\Lambda F_{K}}{\rank(E)}\id_{E})+\epsilon\log(K^{-1}H))\right)\\
&-2e^{2\epsilon t}\sqrt{-1}\Lambda\partial\overline{\partial}\tr\sqrt{-1}(\Lambda F_{H}-\frac{\tr\Lambda F_{K}}{\rank(E)}\id_{E})-2\epsilon e^{2\epsilon t}\sqrt{-1}\Lambda\partial\overline{\partial}\log\det(K^{-1}H)\\
&=0,\end{split}

where we have used

(3.3)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\log\det(K^{-1}H)&=\tr(H^{-1}\frac{\partial H}{\partial t})\\
&=-2\tr\sqrt{-1}(\Lambda F_{H}-\frac{\tr\Lambda F_{K}}{\rank(E)}\id_{E})-2\epsilon\tr\log(K^{-1}H)\\
&=-2\tr\sqrt{-1}(\Lambda F_{H}-\frac{\tr\Lambda F_{K}}{\rank(E)}\id_{E})-2\epsilon\log\det(K^{-1}H),\end{split}

and

(3.4)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial\tr F_{H}}{\partial t}&=\frac{\partial\tr\overline{\partial}_{E}\left((K^{-1}H)^{-1}\partial_{K}(K^{-1}H)\right)}{\partial t}\\
&=\tr\overline{\partial}_{E}\partial_{H}\left((K^{-1}H)^{-1}\frac{\partial K^{-1}H}{\partial t}\right)\\
&=-2\tr\overline{\partial}_{E}\partial_{H}\left(\sqrt{-1}(\Lambda F_{H}-\frac{\tr\Lambda F_{K}}{\rank(E)}\id_{E})+\epsilon\log(K^{-1}H)\right).\end{split}

In addition, we note on
N\times\{t=0\}
and
\overline{\partial}N\times[0,t)
,

(3.5)

\begin{split}e^{2\epsilon t}\tr\left(\sqrt{-1}(\Lambda F_{H}-\frac{\tr\Lambda F_{K}}{\rank(E)}\id_{E})+\epsilon\log(K^{-1}H)\right)=0,\end{split}

it follows from the maximum principle that on
N\times[0,t)
,

(3.6)

\begin{split}e^{2\epsilon t}\tr\left(\sqrt{-1}(\Lambda F_{H}-\frac{\tr\Lambda F_{K}}{\rank(E)}\id_{E})+\epsilon\log(K^{-1}H)\right)=0.\end{split}

Thus we finish the proof by noting

(3.7)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\log\det(K^{-1}H)&=-2\tr\left(\sqrt{-1}(\Lambda F_{H}-\frac{\tr\Lambda F_{K}}{\rank(E)}\id_{E})+\epsilon\log(K^{-1}H)\right)\\
&=0.\end{split}

∎

(3.41)

\begin{split}\log(\frac{\tr h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}}}{\rank(E)})\leq C_{11}(1+\int_{M_{j_{\epsilon}}}\log(\frac{\tr h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}}}{\rank(E)})\dvol_{g}),\end{split}

where we have used the fact that
g
is mutually bounded with a Hermitian metric on
M
. It is also easy to see
\log(\frac{\tr h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}}}{\rank(E)})\leq|\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}}|\leq\rank(E)^{\frac{3}{2}}\log\tr h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}}
and hence it follows

(3.42)

\begin{split}|\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}}|&\leq\rank(E)^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(\log(\frac{\tr h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}}}{\rank(E)})+\log\rank(E)\right)\\
&\leq\rank(E)^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(C_{11}+C_{11}\int_{M_{j_{\epsilon}}}\log(\frac{\tr h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}}}{\rank(E)})\dvol_{g}+\log\rank(E)\right)\\
&\leq\rank(E)^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(C_{11}+C_{11}\int_{M_{j_{\epsilon}}}|\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}}|\dvol_{g}+\log\rank(E)\right)\\
&=C_{12}(1+\int_{M_{j_{\epsilon}}}|\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}}|\dvol_{g}).\end{split}

By Lebesgue theorem, we then arrive at

(3.43)

\begin{split}|\log h_{\epsilon}|&=\lim\limits_{j_{\epsilon}\rightarrow\infty}|\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}}|\\
&\leq\lim\limits_{j_{\epsilon}\rightarrow\infty}C_{12}(1+\int_{M_{j_{\epsilon}}}|\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}}|\dvol_{g})\\
&=C_{12}(1+\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}|\log h_{\epsilon}|\dvol_{g}).\end{split}

In the remaining of the proof, we may assume
\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}|\log h_{\epsilon}|\dvol_{g}>1
and thus

(3.44)

\begin{split}|\log h_{\epsilon}|\leq A\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}|\log h_{\epsilon}|\dvol_{g},\end{split}

for a universal constant
A
. To prove the existence of an
\AHE
structure, it suffices to prove

(3.45)

\begin{split}\sup\limits_{M\setminus\Sigma}|\Lambda F_{H_{\epsilon}}^{\perp}|=\sup\limits_{M\setminus\Sigma}|\epsilon\log h_{\epsilon}|\xrightarrow{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}0.\end{split}

We may assume there exists a sequence
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
and a constant
B>0
such that

(3.46)

\begin{split}||\log h_{\epsilon}||_{L^{1}(M\setminus\Sigma)}\xrightarrow{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\infty,\end{split}

(3.47)

\begin{split}|\Lambda F_{H_{\epsilon}}^{\perp}|=|\epsilon\log h_{\epsilon}|\geq B.\end{split}

Set
\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon}}=||\log h_{\epsilon}||_{L^{1}(M\setminus\Sigma)}^{-1}\log h_{\epsilon}
and it follows from
(\ref{mean})
that

(3.48)

\begin{split}|\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon}}|\leq A,\ ||\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon}}||_{L^{1}(M\setminus\Sigma)}=1.\end{split}

Using
(\ref{identity})
, we have

(3.49)

\begin{split}&\epsilon||\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}}||_{L^{1}(M_{j_{\epsilon}})}^{-1}\int_{M_{j_{\epsilon}}}|\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}}|^{2}\dvol_{g}\\
&+\int_{M_{j_{\epsilon}}}||\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}}||_{L^{1}(M_{j_{\epsilon}})}<\Theta[\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}}](\overline{\partial}_{E}\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}}}),\overline{\partial}_{E}\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}}}>\dvol_{g}\\
&=-\int_{M_{j_{\epsilon}}}<\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{K}^{\perp},\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}}}>\dvol_{g},\end{split}

where
\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}}}=||\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}}||_{L^{1}(M_{j_{\epsilon}})}^{-1}\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}}
. Given any
\delta>0
, there exists large
J_{\delta}
such that if
j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}\geq j_{\epsilon}\geq J_{\delta}
, it holds

(3.50)

\begin{split}\vol(M_{j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}}\setminus M_{j_{\epsilon}})\leq\delta.\end{split}

We may assume
|\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}}}|\leq A
and therefore we deduce

(3.51)

\begin{split}&\epsilon||\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}}||_{L^{1}(M_{j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}})}^{-1}\int_{M_{j_{\epsilon}}}|\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}}|^{2}\dvol_{g}\\
&+\int_{M_{j_{\epsilon}}}||\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}}||_{L^{1}(M_{j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}})}<\Theta[\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}}](\overline{\partial}_{E}\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}}}),\overline{\partial}_{E}\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}}}>\dvol_{g}\\
&\leq-(\int_{M_{j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}}\setminus M_{j_{\epsilon}}}+\int_{M_{j_{\epsilon}}})<\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{K}^{\perp},\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}}}>\dvol_{g}\\
&\leq\vol(M_{j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}}\setminus M_{j_{\epsilon}})\sup\limits_{M_{j_{\epsilon}}}|\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}}}|\sup\limits_{M\setminus\Sigma}|\Lambda F_{K}^{\perp}|-\int_{M_{j_{\epsilon}}}<\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{K}^{\perp},\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}}}>\dvol_{g}\\
&\leq\delta A\sup\limits_{M\setminus\Sigma}|\Lambda F_{K}^{\perp}|-\int_{M_{j_{\epsilon}}}<\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{K}^{\perp},\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}}}>\dvol_{g}.\end{split}

Taking
j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}\rightarrow\infty
firstly and
j_{\epsilon}\rightarrow\infty
secondly, we can arrive at

(3.52)

\begin{split}&\epsilon||\log h_{\epsilon}||_{L^{1}(M\setminus\Sigma)}^{-1}\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}|\log h_{\epsilon}|^{2}\dvol_{g}\\
&+\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}||\log h_{\epsilon}||_{L^{1}(M\setminus\Sigma)}<\Theta[\log h_{\epsilon}](\overline{\partial}_{E}\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon}}),\overline{\partial}_{E}\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon}}>\dvol_{g}\\
&\leq\delta A\sup\limits_{M\setminus\Sigma}|\Lambda F_{K}^{\perp}|-\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}<\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{K}^{\perp},\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon}}>\dvol_{g}.\end{split}

Note that

(3.53)

\begin{split}&\epsilon||\log h_{\epsilon}||_{L^{1}(M\setminus\Sigma)}^{-1}\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}|\log h_{\epsilon}|^{2}\dvol_{g}\\
&\geq B|\log h_{\epsilon}|^{-1}||\log h_{\epsilon}||_{L^{1}(M\setminus\Sigma)}^{-1}\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}|\log h_{\epsilon}|^{2}\dvol_{g}\\
&\geq BA^{-1}||\log h_{\epsilon}||_{L^{1}(M\setminus\Sigma)}^{-2}\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}|\log h_{\epsilon}|^{2}\dvol_{g}\\
&\geq B(A\vol(M\setminus\Sigma))^{-1},\end{split}

this and the fact
\delta
is arbitrary furthermore give us

(3.54)

\begin{split}&B(A\vol(M\setminus\Sigma))^{-1}+\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}||\log h_{\epsilon}||_{L^{1}(M\setminus\Sigma)}<\Theta[\log h_{\epsilon}](\overline{\partial}_{E}\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon}}),\overline{\partial}_{E}\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon}}>\dvol_{g}\\
&\leq-\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}<\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{K}^{\perp},\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon}}>\dvol_{g}.\end{split}

Notice that
l\Theta(lx,ly)\rightarrow(x-y)^{-1}
for
x>y
and
l\Theta(lx,ly)\rightarrow+\infty
for
x\leq y
increases monotonically as
l\rightarrow+\infty
. It holds

(3.55)

\begin{split}&B(A\vol(M\setminus\Sigma))^{-1}+\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}(\rho[\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon}}](\overline{\partial}_{E}\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon}}),\overline{\partial}_{E}\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon}})\dvol_{g}\\
&\leq-\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}<\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{K}^{\perp},\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon}}>\dvol_{g},\end{split}

for
\rho:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
\rho(x,y)<\frac{1}{x-y}
whenever
x>y
and
\epsilon
small enough. Since
\{|\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon}}|\}_{\epsilon>0}
are uniformly bounded, by taking small
\rho
it follows that
\{\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon}}\}_{\epsilon>0}
are also uniformly bounded in
L_{1}^{2}
. We may assume
\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon}}\xrightarrow{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}u_{\infty}
weakly in
L_{1,\loc}^{2}
-topology and for large
j
,

(3.56)

\begin{split}\vol(M\setminus(\Sigma\cup M_{j}))\leq(2A)^{-1}.\end{split}

Therefore we have

(3.57)

\begin{split}\int_{M_{j}}|u_{\infty}|\dvol_{g}&=\lim\limits_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{M_{j}}|\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon}}|\dvol_{g}\\
&=\lim\limits_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}(\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}-\int_{M\setminus(\Sigma\cup M_{j})})|\widetilde{\log h_{\epsilon}}|\dvol_{g}\\
&\geq 1-A(2A)^{-1}\\
&=2^{-1},\end{split}

which indicates
u_{\infty}\neq 0
. Meanwhile, we also have

(3.58)

\begin{split}&B(A\vol(M\setminus\Sigma))^{-1}+\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}(\rho[u_{\infty}](\overline{\partial}_{E}u_{\infty},\overline{\partial}_{E}u_{\infty})\dvol_{g}\\
&\leq-\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}<\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{K}^{\perp},u_{\infty}>\dvol_{g}.\end{split}

Based on
(\ref{keysemistable})
and
[
23
, Lemma 5.5]
, we know that
u_{\infty}
has constant eigenvalues almost everywhere. Let
\lambda_{1}<...<\lambda_{k}
denote the distinct eigenvalues of
u_{\infty}
with
2\leq k\leq\rank(E)
. Define a family of smooth nonnegative functions
p_{i}
on
\mathbb{R}
such that
p_{i}(x)=1
if
x\leq\lambda_{i}
and
p_{i}(x)=0
if
x>\lambda_{i+1}
. Set
\pi_{i}=p_{i}[u_{\infty}]
which means
\pi_{i}
has eigenvalues
p_{i}(\lambda_{1}),...,p_{i}(\lambda_{k})
. Using
(\ref{keysemistable})
again, the regularity result of Uhlenbeck-Yau
[
24
]
and following
[
23
, pages 887-889]
, one further proves that each
\pi_{i}
represent a nontrivial saturated sub-sheaf
\mathcal{S}_{i}
and the negativity of

(3.59)

\begin{split}W&\triangleq\lambda_{k}\deg(E,K)-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k-1}(\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{i})\deg(\mathcal{S}_{i},K)\\
&=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k-1}(\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{i})\rank(\mathcal{S}_{i})(\frac{\deg(E,K)}{\rank(E)}-\frac{\deg(\mathcal{S}_{i},K)}{\rank(\mathcal{S}_{i})}),\end{split}

which we shall not repeat since there is no essential change. Hence there must exist at least one
\mathcal{S}_{i}
violating the semi-stability assumption. In a conclusion,
\{H_{\epsilon}\}
is an
\AHE
structure.

Finally for any
\epsilon>0
, we have
\det K=\det H_{\epsilon}
and it holds for
j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}\geq j_{\epsilon}
that

(3.60)

\begin{split}\int_{M_{j_{\epsilon}}}|\overline{\partial}_{E}\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}}|^{2}\dvol_{g}&\leq C_{\epsilon}\int_{M_{j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}}}\Theta[\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}}](\overline{\partial}_{E}\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}}),\overline{\partial}_{E}\log h_{\epsilon,j^{{}^{\prime}}_{\epsilon}}>\dvol_{g}\\
&\leq-C_{\epsilon}\int_{M_{j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}}}<\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{K}^{\perp},\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}}>\dvol_{g}\\
&\leq C_{\epsilon}\sup\limits_{M\setminus\Sigma}|\Lambda F_{K}^{\perp}|\sup\limits_{M\setminus\Sigma}|\log h_{\epsilon,j_{\epsilon}^{{}^{\prime}}}|\vol(M\setminus\Sigma)\\
&\leq\epsilon^{-1}C_{\epsilon}\sup\limits_{M\setminus\Sigma}|\Lambda F_{K}^{\perp}|^{2}\vol(M\setminus\Sigma),\end{split}

where
C_{\epsilon}
depends on
\epsilon
. Taking
j^{{}^{\prime}}_{\epsilon}\rightarrow\infty
and
j_{\epsilon}\rightarrow\infty
, we have

(3.61)

\begin{split}\int_{M}|\overline{\partial}_{E}\log h_{\epsilon}|^{2}\dvol_{g}\leq\epsilon^{-1}C_{\epsilon}\sup\limits_{M\setminus\Sigma}|\Lambda F_{K}^{\perp}|^{2}\vol(M\setminus\Sigma).\end{split}

Hence the proof of Theorem
1.2
is completed.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

To obtain the existence of a
\HE
metric, by
(\ref{keysemistable})
we have

(3.62)

\begin{split}\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}(\rho[u_{\infty}](\overline{\partial}_{E}u_{\infty},\overline{\partial}_{E}u_{\infty})\dvol_{g}&\leq-\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}<\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{K}^{\perp},u_{\infty}>\dvol_{g}.\end{split}

Nore this inequality follows from the assumption

(3.63)

\begin{split}||\log h_{\epsilon}||_{L^{1}(M\setminus\Sigma)}\xrightarrow{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\infty,\end{split}

and it implies the nonpositivity of
W
. As a consequence, there is at least one
\mathcal{S}_{i}
violating the stability assumption. Hence there must exist a
\HE
metric compatible with
K
.

4.
Uniqueness and stabilities of Hermitian-Einstein bundles

We recall following key assumptions of Simpson.

Proposition 4.1

(Proposition 2.2 in
[
23
]
)
.

Suppose
(M,J,g)
is a compact Kähler manifold and
\Sigma\subset M
is a Zariski closed subset, then
M\setminus\Sigma
satisfies:

(1)

It has finite volume.

(2)

It admits a nonnegative exhaustion function
\phi
such that
\Delta_{g}\phi\in L^{\infty}(M\setminus\Sigma)
.

(3)

If
0\leq f\in C^{\infty}(M\setminus\Sigma)\cap L^{\infty}(M\setminus\Sigma)
satisfies
\Delta_{g}f\geq-A
for a constant
A
, then

(4.1)

\begin{split}\sup\limits_{M\setminus\Sigma}f\leq C_{A}(1+\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}f\dvol_{g}),\end{split}

where
C_{A}
is a constant depending on
A
. Moreoover, if
\Delta_{g}f\geq 0
, then
f
is a constant.

The second assumption in Proposition
4.1
ensures

Proposition 4.2

(Lemma 5.2 in
[
23
]
)
.

Suppose a Riemannian manifold
(N,g)
has an exhaustion function
\phi
with
\Delta_{g}\phi\in L^{1}(N)
,
\eta
is a
\dim N-1
form with
|\eta|^{2}\in L^{1}(N)
, then

(4.2)

\begin{split}\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{N_{j}}d\eta=0,\end{split}

for a sequence of exhaustion subsets
\{N_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Remark 4.3
.

By Simpson’s proof, it is not hard to see that Proposition
4.2
also holds for a non-Kähler manifold
(N,J,g)
which satisfies
\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\omega_{g}^{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}N-1}\geq 0
and admits an exhaustion function
\phi
such that
\sqrt{-1}\Lambda\partial\overline{\partial}\phi\in L^{1}(N)
.

To prove the stability of
\HE
metrics, we first prove two lemmas.

Lemma 4.4
.

For two Hermitian metrics
H_{1},H_{2}
on a holomorphic vector bundle
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
and a holomorphic sub-bundle
S\subset E
, we denote by
\pi_{H_{1}}
the
H_{1}
-orthogonal projection onto
S
and
\overline{\partial}_{S}
the induced holomorphic structure on
S
, then we have

(1)

H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}H_{2}|_{S}=\pi_{H_{1}}\circ H_{1}^{-1}H_{2}\circ\pi_{H_{1}}
,
H_{2}|_{S}^{-1}H_{1}|_{S}=\pi_{H_{2}}\circ H_{2}^{-1}H_{1}\circ\pi_{H_{2}}
.

(2)

\overline{\partial}_{S}(H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}H_{2}|_{S})=\pi_{H_{1}}\circ\overline{\partial}_{E}(H_{1}^{-1}H_{2})\circ\pi_{H_{1}}+\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H_{1}}\circ(\id_{E}-\pi_{H_{1}})\circ(H_{1}^{-1}H_{2})\circ\pi_{H_{1}}
.

Proof.

For
u,v\in C^{\infty}(X,S)
, we have

(4.3)

\begin{split}H_{2}|_{S}(u,v)&=H_{1}(H_{1}^{-1}H_{2}(u),v)\\
&=H_{1}((\pi_{H_{1}}\circ H_{1}^{-1}H_{2})(u),v)\\
&=H_{1}|_{S}((\pi_{H_{1}}\circ H_{1}^{-1}H_{2})(u),v),\end{split}

and this proves (1). Let us write
E=S\oplus S^{\perp}
for the
H_{1}
-orthogonal decomposition and with respect to this decomposition, we have

(4.4)

\begin{split}\overline{\partial}_{E}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\overline{\partial}_{S}&-\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H_{1}}\\
0&\overline{\partial}_{S^{\perp}}\end{array}\right),\ H_{1}^{-1}H_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}H_{2}|_{S}&A\\
B&C\end{array}\right),\end{split}

where
\overline{\partial}_{S^{\perp}}
is the induced holomorphic structure on
S^{\perp}
. Then we deduce

(4.5)

\begin{split}&\overline{\partial}_{E}(H_{1}^{-1}H_{2})\\
&=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\overline{\partial}_{S}&-\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H_{1}}\\
0&\overline{\partial}_{S^{\perp}}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}H_{2}|_{S}&A\\
B&C\end{array}\right)\\
&-\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}H_{2}|_{S}&A\\
B&C\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\overline{\partial}_{S}&-\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H_{1}}\\
0&\overline{\partial}_{S^{\perp}}\end{array}\right)\\
&=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\overline{\partial}_{S}\circ H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}H_{2}|_{S}-\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H_{1}}\circ B&\overline{\partial}_{S}\circ A-\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H_{1}}\circ C\\
\overline{\partial}_{S^{\perp}}\circ B&\overline{\partial}_{S^{\perp}}\circ C\end{array}\right)\\
&-\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}H_{2}|_{S}\circ\overline{\partial}_{S}&-H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}H_{2}|_{S}\circ\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H_{1}}+A\circ\overline{\partial}_{S^{\perp}}\\
B\circ\overline{\partial}_{S}&-B\circ\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H_{1}}+C\circ\overline{\partial}_{S^{\perp}}\end{array}\right)\\
&=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\overline{\partial}_{S}(H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}H_{2}|_{S})&\overline{\partial}_{(S^{\perp})^{\ast}\otimes S}A\\
\overline{\partial}_{S^{\ast}\otimes S^{\perp}}B&\overline{\partial}_{S^{\perp}}C\end{array}\right)\\
&+\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H_{1}}\circ B&-\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H_{1}}\circ C+H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}H_{2}|_{S}\circ\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H_{1}}\\
0&B\circ\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H_{1}}\end{array}\right),\end{split}

and also note
B=(\id_{E}-\pi_{H_{1}})\circ(H_{1}^{-1}H_{2})\circ\pi_{H_{1}}
, this proves
(2)
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
be a holomorphic vector bundle on a Kähler manifold
(N,J,g)
admitting an exhaustion function
\phi
such that
\Delta_{g}\phi\in L^{1}(N)
and
S\subset E
be a holomorphic sub-bundle. Suppose
H_{1},H_{2}
are two Hermitian metrics on
E
compatible with each other and with
|\Lambda F_{H_{1}}|_{H_{1}},|\Lambda F_{H_{2}}|_{H_{2}}\in L^{1}(N)
. Denote by
\pi_{H_{1}}
,
\pi_{H_{2}}
the
H_{1}
-orthogonal,
H_{2}
-orthogonal projections onto
S
respectively, it holds for a sequence of exhaustion subsets
\{N_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
that

(4.6)

\begin{split}\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{N_{j}}\tr\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{H_{2}|_{S}}\dvol_{g}=\deg(S,H_{1}).\end{split}

Moreover,
|\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H_{1}}|_{H_{1}}\in L^{2}(N)
if and only if
|\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H_{2}}|_{H_{2}}\in L^{2}(N)
.

Proof.

We only prove
|\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H_{1}}|_{H_{1}}\in L^{2}(N)
implies
|\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H_{2}}|_{H_{2}}\in L^{2}(N)
. Recall

(4.7)

\begin{split}\deg(S,H_{1})&=\int_{N}\tr\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{H_{1}|_{S}}\dvol_{g}\\
&=\int_{N}\left(\tr(\sqrt{-1}\pi_{H_{1}}\circ\Lambda F_{H_{1}})-|\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H_{1}}|^{2}_{H_{1}}\right)\dvol_{g},\end{split}

it follows

(4.8)

\begin{split}\deg(S,H_{1})&\leq\int_{N}\left(\sqrt{\rank(S)}|\Lambda F_{H_{1}}|_{H_{1}}-|\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H_{1}}|^{2}_{H_{1}}\right)\dvol_{g}\\
&\leq\sqrt{\rank(S)}\int_{N}|\Lambda F_{H_{1}}|_{H_{1}}\dvol_{g}\\
&<+\infty,\end{split}

and also

(4.9)

\begin{split}\deg(S,H_{1})&\geq-\sqrt{\rank(S)}\int_{N}\left(|\Lambda F_{H_{1}}|_{H_{1}}+|\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H_{1}}|^{2}_{H_{1}}\right)\dvol_{g}\\
&>-\infty.\end{split}

By Lemma
4.4
and assumptions, we have

(4.10)

\begin{split}&||\tr((H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}K|_{S})^{-1}\partial_{K|_{S}}(H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}K|_{S}))\wedge\omega_{g}^{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}-1}||_{L^{2}(N)}\\
&=||\tr(\overline{\partial}_{S}(H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}K|_{S})(H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}K|_{S})^{-1})\wedge\omega_{g}^{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}-1}||_{L^{2}(N)}\\
&<\infty,\end{split}

thus Lemma
4.2
implies

(4.11)

\begin{split}&\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{N_{j}}\tr\sqrt{-1}\Lambda\overline{\partial}_{S}\left((H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}K|_{S})^{-1}\partial_{K|_{S}}(H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}K|_{S})\right)\dvol_{g}\\
&=\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{N_{j}}\tr\sqrt{-1}\overline{\partial}_{S}\left((H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}K|_{S})^{-1}\partial_{K|_{S}}(H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}K|_{S})\right)\wedge\frac{\omega_{g}^{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}N-1}}{(\dim_{\mathbb{C}}N-1)!}\\
&=\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{N_{j}}d\sqrt{-1}\left(\tr((H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}K|_{S})^{-1}\partial_{K|_{S}}(H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}K|_{S}))\wedge\frac{\omega_{g}^{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}N-1}}{(\dim_{\mathbb{C}}N-1)!}\right)\\
&=0.\end{split}

Then by
(\ref{finite1})
,
(\ref{finite2})
,
(\ref{difference1})
, we further deduce

(4.12)

\begin{split}&\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{N_{j}}\tr\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{H_{2}|_{S}}\dvol_{g}\\
&=\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{N_{j}}\left(\tr\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{H_{1}|_{S}}+\tr\sqrt{-1}\overline{\partial}_{S}((H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}H_{2}|_{S})^{-1}\partial_{K|_{S}}(H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}H_{2}|_{S}))\right)\dvol_{g}\\
&=\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{N_{j}}\tr\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{H_{1}|_{S}}\dvol_{g}\\
&+\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{N_{j}}\tr\sqrt{-1}\overline{\partial}_{S}((H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}H_{2}|_{S})^{-1}\partial_{K|_{S}}(H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}H_{2}|_{S}))\dvol_{g}\\
&=\deg(S,H_{1}),\end{split}

and therefore

(4.13)

\begin{split}\int_{N}|\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H_{2}}|_{H_{2}}^{2}\dvol_{g}&=\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{N_{j}}|\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H_{2}}|_{H_{2}}^{2}\dvol_{g}\\
&=\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{N_{j}}\left(\tr(\sqrt{-1}\pi_{H_{2}}\circ\Lambda F_{H_{2}})-\tr\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{H_{2}|_{S}}\right)\dvol_{g}\\
&\leq\sqrt{\rank(S)}\int_{X}|\Lambda F_{H_{2}}|_{H_{2}}\dvol_{g}+\deg(S,H_{1})\\
&<+\infty.\end{split}

∎

Now we are in a position to prove the stability of
\HE
metrics.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Since
\det K=\det H
and
|\Lambda F_{K}|\in L^{1}(M\setminus\Sigma)
, we have

(4.14)

\begin{split}\frac{\deg(E,H)}{\rank(E)}&=\frac{\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}\tr\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{H}\dvol_{g}}{\rank(E)}\\
&=\frac{\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}\tr\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{K}\dvol_{g}}{\rank(E)}\\
&\leq\frac{\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}|\Lambda F_{K}|_{K}|\id_{E}|\dvol_{g}}{\sqrt{\rank(E)}}\\
&<+\infty.\end{split}

For any any nontrivial saturated sub-sheaf
\mathcal{S}
and if
|\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H}|_{H}\notin L^{2}(M\setminus(\Sigma\cup Z(\mathcal{S})))
, we have

(4.15)

\begin{split}\frac{\deg(\mathcal{S},H)}{\rank(\mathcal{S})}&=\frac{\int_{M\setminus(\Sigma\cup Z(\mathcal{S}))}\left(\tr(\sqrt{-1}\pi_{H}\circ\Lambda F_{H})-|\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H}|^{2}_{H}\right)\dvol_{g}}{\rank(\mathcal{S})}\\
&=\frac{\int_{M\setminus(\Sigma\cup Z(\mathcal{S}))}\left(\tr(\sqrt{-1}\pi_{H}\circ\frac{\tr\Lambda F_{H}}{\rank(E)})-|\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H}|^{2}_{H}\right)\dvol_{g}}{\rank(\mathcal{S})}\\
&=\frac{\int_{M\setminus(\Sigma\cup Z(\mathcal{S}))}\left(\tr(\sqrt{-1}\pi_{H}\circ\frac{\tr\Lambda F_{K}}{\rank(E)})-|\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H}|^{2}_{H}\right)\dvol_{g}}{\rank(\mathcal{S})}\\
&=\frac{\deg(E,K)}{\rank(E)}-\frac{\int_{M\setminus(\Sigma\cup Z(\mathcal{S}))}|\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H}|^{2}_{H}\dvol_{g}}{\rank(\mathcal{S})}.\end{split}

and hence
\frac{\deg(\mathcal{S},H)}{\rank(\mathcal{S})}=-\infty<\frac{\deg(E,H)}{\rank(E)}
.
If
|\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H}|_{H}\in L^{2}(M\setminus(\Sigma\cup Z(\mathcal{S})))
,
\deg(\mathcal{S},H)
is finite and using Lemma
4.5
with
H_{1}=H,H_{2}=K
, we have

(4.16)

\begin{split}&\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X_{j}}\tr\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{K|_{S}}\dvol_{g}=\deg(S,H),\end{split}

for a sequence of exhaustion subsets
\{X_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
M\setminus(\Sigma\cup Z(\mathcal{S}))
.
Moreover, Lemma
4.5
also gives
|\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{K}|_{K}^{2}\in L^{2}(M\setminus(\Sigma\cup Z(\mathcal{S})))
and thus
\tr\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{K|_{S}}
is absolutely integrable since

(4.17)

\begin{split}&\int_{M\setminus(\Sigma\cup Z(\mathcal{S}))}|\tr\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{K|_{S}}|\dvol_{g}\\
&=\int_{M\setminus(\Sigma\cup Z(\mathcal{S}))}|\tr(\sqrt{-1}\pi_{K}\circ\Lambda F_{K})-|\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{K}|^{2}_{K}|\dvol_{g}.\end{split}

Hence we have

(4.18)

\begin{split}\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X_{j}}\tr\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{K|_{S}}\dvol_{g}&=\int_{M\setminus(\Sigma\cup Z(\mathcal{S}))}\tr\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{K|_{S}}\dvol_{g}\\
&=\deg(\mathcal{S},K).\end{split}

By the stability of
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},K)
,
(\ref{uniqueness1})
and
(\ref{uniqueness2})
, we deduce

(4.19)

\begin{split}\frac{\deg(\mathcal{S},H)}{\rank(\mathcal{S})}&=\frac{\deg(\mathcal{S},K)}{\rank(\mathcal{S})}\\
&<\frac{\deg(E,K)}{\rank(E)}\\
&=\frac{\deg(E,H)}{\rank(E)}.\end{split}

Therefore, we obtain stability of
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},H)
from stability of
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},K)
.
∎

Based on the stability of
\HE
metrics, we are able to prove the uniqueness of
\HE
metrics.

An alternative proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Firstly, we compute

(4.20)

\begin{split}\Delta_{g}\tr(H_{1}^{-1}H_{2})&=2\sqrt{-1}\Lambda\partial\overline{\partial}\tr(H_{1}^{-1}H_{2})\\
&=<\sqrt{-1}\Lambda(F_{H_{1}}-F_{H_{2}}),H_{1}^{-1}H_{2}>_{H_{1}}\\
&+\sqrt{-1}\Lambda<H_{2}^{-1}H_{1}\partial_{H_{1}}(H_{1}^{-1}H_{2}),\overline{\partial}_{H_{1}}(H_{1}^{-1}H_{2})>_{H_{1}}\\
&=|(H_{2}^{-1}H_{1})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{H_{1}}(H_{1}^{-1}H_{2})|^{2}_{H_{1}}.\end{split}

By Proposition
4.1
, we get
\partial_{H_{1}}(H_{1}^{-1}H_{2})=0
and
\overline{\partial}_{E}(H_{1}^{-1}H_{2})=0
since
H_{1}^{-1}H_{2}
is self-adjoint with respect to both
H_{1}
,
H_{2}
. Let
E=\oplus_{i=1}^{m}E_{i}
denote the eigendecomposition of
H_{1}^{-1}H_{2}
. Since
H_{1}^{-1}H_{2}
is
D_{H_{1}}
-parallel, the eigenvalues
\{c_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
are constants and therefore

(4.21)

\begin{split}H_{2}|_{E_{i}}=c_{i}H_{1}|_{E_{i}},\ i=1,...,m.\end{split}

For
v_{i}\in C^{\infty}(M\setminus\Sigma,E_{i}),v_{j}\in C^{\infty}(M\setminus\Sigma,E_{j})
, we have

(4.22)

\begin{split}H_{1}^{-1}H_{2}(\overline{\partial}_{E}v_{i})=\overline{\partial}_{E}((H_{1}^{-1}H_{2})(v_{i}))=c_{i}\overline{\partial}_{E}v_{i},\end{split}

(4.23)

\begin{split}H_{2}(v_{i},v_{j})=H_{1}(H_{1}^{-1}H_{2}(v_{i}),v_{j})=c_{i}H_{1}(v_{i},v_{j}),\end{split}

(4.24)

\begin{split}H_{2}(v_{i},v_{j})=H_{1}(v_{i},H_{1}^{-1}H_{2}(v_{j}))=c_{j}H_{1}(v_{i},v_{j}).\end{split}

Thus the decomposition splits in
H_{1}
-orthogonal,
H_{2}
-orthogonal and holomorphic senses.

It suffices to show
m=1
. Otherwise, each
E_{i}
is a nontrivial holomorphic sub-bundle and

(4.25)

\begin{split}\frac{\deg(E,H_{1})}{\rank(E)}&=\frac{\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}\tr\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{H_{1}}\dvol_{g}}{\rank(E)}\\
&=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}\tr\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{H_{1}|_{E_{i}}}\dvol_{g}}{\rank(E)}\\
&=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}\tr\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{H_{1}|_{E_{i}}}\dvol_{g}}{\rank(E_{i})}\frac{\rank(E_{i})}{\rank(E)}\\
&=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\deg(E_{i},H_{1})}{\rank(E_{i})}\frac{\rank(E_{i})}{\rank(E)}.\end{split}

On the other hand, recall that we have proved the stability of
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},H_{1})
in Theorem
1.4
, so it leads to the following contradiction

(4.26)

\begin{split}\frac{\deg(E,H_{1})}{\rank(E)}&<\frac{\deg(E,H_{1})}{\rank(E)}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\rank(E_{i})}{\rank(E)}=\frac{\deg(E,H_{1})}{\rank(E)},\end{split}

and therefore
H_{1}=H_{2}
.
∎

.

Since the proof only involves stabilities of

and
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},K)

|\overline{\partial}_{E}(K^{-1}H_{2})|\in L^{2}(M\setminus\Sigma)

\HE

\AHE

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

For (1), once
H
is a
\HE
metric, it is well-known that
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},H)
is a direct sum of some holomorphic vector bundles
(E_{i},\overline{\partial}_{E}|_{E_{i}},H|_{E_{i}})
and each of them is stable, see
[
8
,
15
]
for example. Since
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
is assumed to be indecomposable, we further find the stability of
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},H)
, from which and a similar argument in our proof Theorem
1.4
(only need to exchange the roles of
K
and
H
), we can conclude the the stability of
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},K)
.

Next we prove (2). Note in compact case, the semi-stability of a holomorphic vector bundle admitting an
\AHE
structure follows from a vanishing theorem
[
9
]
. It requires additional work to generalize the argument to noncompact context. The proof below bypasses the use of any vanishing theorem which seems more natural and directly. Let
\{H_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon>0}
be an
\AHE
structure compatible with
K
. Following
[
25
]
, we have for any saturated nontrivial sub-sheaf
\mathcal{S}
that

(4.27)

\begin{split}\frac{\deg(\mathcal{S},H_{\epsilon})}{\rank(\mathcal{S})}&=\frac{\int_{M\setminus(\Sigma\cup Z(\mathcal{S}))}\tr(\pi_{H_{\epsilon}}\circ\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{H_{\epsilon}})-|\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{H_{\epsilon}}|^{2}_{H_{\epsilon}}\dvol_{g}}{\rank(\mathcal{S})}\\
&\leq\frac{\int_{M\setminus(\Sigma\cup Z(\mathcal{S}))}\tr(\pi_{H_{\epsilon}}\circ\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{H_{\epsilon}})\dvol_{g}}{\rank(\mathcal{S})}\\
&=\frac{\int_{M\setminus(\Sigma\cup Z(\mathcal{S}))}\tr(\pi_{H_{\epsilon}}\circ\frac{\tr\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{H_{\epsilon}}}{\rank(E)})+\tr(\pi_{H_{\epsilon}}\circ\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{H_{\epsilon}}^{\perp})\dvol_{g}}{\rank(\mathcal{S})}\\
&\leq\frac{\deg(E,K)}{\rank(E)}+\frac{\int_{M\setminus(\Sigma\cup Z(\mathcal{S}))}|\pi_{H_{\epsilon}}|_{H_{\epsilon}}|\Lambda F_{H_{\epsilon}}^{\perp}|_{H_{\epsilon}}\dvol_{g}}{\rank(\mathcal{S})}\\
&\leq\frac{\deg(E,K)}{\rank(E)}+\frac{\epsilon\vol(M)}{\sqrt{\rank(\mathcal{S})}}.\end{split}

If
|\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{K}|_{K}\notin L^{2}(M\setminus(\Sigma\cup Z(\mathcal{S})))
, we have

(4.28)

\begin{split}\frac{\deg(\mathcal{S},K)}{\rank(\mathcal{S})}&\leq\frac{\int_{M\setminus(\Sigma\cup Z(\mathcal{S}))}\left(\sqrt{\rank(\mathcal{S})}|\Lambda F_{K}|-|\overline{\partial}_{E}\pi_{K}|_{K}^{2}\right)\dvol_{g}}{\rank(\mathcal{S})}\\
&=-\infty\\
&<\frac{\deg(E,K)}{\rank(E)}.\end{split}

If
|\overline{\partial}_{\theta}\pi_{K}|_{K}\in L^{2}(M\setminus(\Sigma\cup Z(\mathcal{S})))
, we know
\deg(\mathcal{S},K)
is finite. Note that

(4.29)

\begin{split}\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}|\Lambda F_{H_{\epsilon}}|_{H_{\epsilon}}\dvol_{g}&=\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}(|\Lambda F_{H_{\epsilon}}^{\perp}|_{H_{\epsilon}}+|\frac{\tr\Lambda F_{K}}{\rank(E)}\id_{E}|_{H_{\epsilon}})\dvol_{g},\end{split}

therefore
|\Lambda F_{H_{\epsilon}}|_{H_{\epsilon}}\in L^{1}(M\setminus\Sigma)
since
\{H_{\epsilon}\}
is an
\AHE
structure. Using Lemma
4.5
with
H_{1}=K,H_{2}=H_{\epsilon}
and that in the proof of Theorem
1.4
as before, we can conclude

(4.30)

\begin{split}\deg(\mathcal{S},H_{\epsilon})&=\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X_{j}}\tr\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F_{H_{\epsilon}|_{S}}\dvol_{g}=\deg(S,K),\end{split}

for a sequence of exhaustion subsets
\{X_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
M\setminus(\Sigma\cup Z(\mathcal{S}))
.
Hence we have

(4.31)

\begin{split}\frac{\deg(\mathcal{S},K)}{\rank(\mathcal{S})}&=\frac{\deg(\mathcal{S},H_{\epsilon})}{\rank(\mathcal{S})}\\
&\leq\frac{\deg(E,K)}{\rank(E)}+\frac{\epsilon\vol(M)}{\sqrt{\rank(\mathcal{S})}}\\
&\xrightarrow{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{\deg(E,K)}{\rank(E)}.\end{split}

The proof is completed.
∎

\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{C}\times M

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},K)
be a stable Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle on
\mathbb{C}
with
|F_{K}|\in L^{1}(\mathbb{C})
and
H_{1},H_{2}
be two
\HE
metrics compatible with
K
, then we have
H_{1}=H_{2}
.

Proof.

Tracking the proof, it is not hard to conclude Theorems
1.3
,
1.4
for the case where the base space is
\mathbb{C}
. In fact, instead of
(\ref{difference1})
in Lemma
4.5
, we note

(4.32)

\begin{split}&|\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}\int_{B_{2R}}\eta_{R}^{2}\tr\sqrt{-1}\overline{\partial}_{S}\left((H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}K|_{S})^{-1}\partial_{K|_{S}}(H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}K|_{S})\right)|\\
&=|\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}\int_{B_{2R}}\eta_{R}^{2}d\sqrt{-1}\left(\tr((H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}K|_{S})^{-1}\partial_{K|_{S}}(H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}K|_{S}))\right)|\\
&=2|\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}\eta_{R}d\eta_{R}\wedge\sqrt{-1}\left(\tr((H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}K|_{S})^{-1}\partial_{K|_{S}}(H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}K|_{S}))\right)|\\
&\leq 2\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}\vol(B_{2R}\setminus B_{R})|d\eta_{R}|^{2}||\tr((H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}K|_{S})^{-1}\partial_{K|_{S}}(H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}K|_{S}))||_{L^{2}(B_{2R}\setminus B_{R})}\\
&\leq 2C^{2}\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}R^{-2}\vol(B_{2R}\setminus B_{R})||\tr((H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}K|_{S})^{-1}\partial_{K|_{S}}(H_{1}|_{S}^{-1}K|_{S}))||_{L^{2}(B_{2R}\setminus B_{R})}\\
&=0,\end{split}

where
\{\eta_{R}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{2R})\}_{R\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of cut-off functions such that
0\leq\eta_{R}\leq 1
,
\eta_{R}=1
on
B_{R}
, and
|d\eta_{R}|\leq CR^{-1}
for constant
C
. Therefore it holds

(4.33)

\begin{split}\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}\int_{B_{2R}}\eta_{R}^{2}\tr\sqrt{-1}F_{H_{2}|_{S}}=\deg(S,H_{1}).\end{split}

Moreover, we also need to use the Liouville theorem for bounded sub-harmonic functions.
∎

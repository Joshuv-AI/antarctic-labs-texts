Nonemptiness of single affine Deligne-Lusztig varities
Dong Gyu Lim

Abstract
. Affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties with various level structures show up in the study of Shimura varieties and moduli spaces of shtukas. Among is the Iwahori level structure which is the most refined one. We study the nonemptiness problem of single affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties at Iwahori level in the basic case. Under a genericity condition (the “shrunken Weyl chambers” condition), an explicit criterion is known. However, no explicit criterion has been available without the condition even conjecturally. We conjecture a new criterion in full generality, and prove it except for finitely many cases. As an application, the nonemptiness problem for special cases and a new conjectural dimension formula are discussed.

Introduction

1.1.
Background

In the study of the special fibers of Shimura varieties, affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties show up naturally. In his seminal expository article
[
Rap05

affine Deligne-Lusztig variety

Many questions arise naturally including the geometric (or scheme) structure, the nonemptiness, the dimension formula, and the set of connected components, etc. These basic questions are not only interesting on their own but useful in the study of the aforementioned objects. For example, the set of connected components computed in

, 3]

[

1.4.
New Ideas and Sketch of Proof

The novelty of our work lies in the introduction of the set
W_{x}
which arises naturally in the following sense.

The idea is that a critical strip behaves as if it belongs to the shrunken Weyl chambers
adjacent
to the strip. Hence, if
x
lies in a critical strip, we can “embed”
x
into each shrunken Weyl chamber adjacent to the strip. Noting that, when
x
lies in a shrunken Weyl chamber, the rule for
X_{x}(b)\neq\emptyset
is
\supp_{\sigma}(\eta_{\sigma}(x))=\mathbb{S}
, we can presuppose that, in general, the rule for
X_{x}(b)\neq\emptyset
would be
\supp_{\sigma}(\eta_{\sigma}(x^{\prime}))=\mathbb{S}
for each embedding
x^{\prime}
of
x
into each shrunken Weyl chamber adjacent to the strips.

The set
W_{x}
is defined to be the set of elements in
W_{0}
that, intuitively, embed
x
into those shrunken Weyl chambers. Practically, for
x
in the shrunken Weyl chambers, we get
W_{x}=\{\id_{W_{0}}\}
and, for
x
in one critical strip (cf.
1.2
(1)), we have
W_{x}=\{\id_{W_{0}},s\}
where
s
is the simple reflection related to the critical strip containing
x
.

In order to define

, we make a new observation on a certain structure of the set of critical strips containing
x
(
3.4
). More precisely, let
\Phi
be the set of (relative) roots of
G
and, for simplicity,
x
lie in the dominant Weyl chamber. Next, denote by
\Phi_{x}
the set of positive roots whose critical strip contains
x
. Then,
\Phi_{x}
is “anti-closed” in the sense that if
\alpha+\beta\in\Phi_{x}
for two positive roots
\alpha
and
\beta
then
\alpha\in\Phi_{x}
or
\beta\in\Phi_{x}
. Alternatively, it is equivalent to that the complement of
\Phi_{x}
in the set of positive roots is closed.

Using this, we show that the set
W_{x}
is well-defined (
3.3
) and that
W_{x}
contains exactly the elements needed for the ‘
\sigma
-support test’ (cf.
3.7
). After that, we follow the strategy of
[
GHN15
]
as explained in
section

2.4
with more careful study on the

-action on dominant cocharacters. We also use the positivity of the coroot-coefficients of dominant cocharacters (see

) and work with the exact (positive) coefficients in the proof of

There has been an interesting coincidence happening while we have been working on this problem. Felix Schremmer (

1.5.2.
Future works on new dimension formulas

The following dimension formula (

]
) is known for

\dim X_{x}(b)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\ell(x)+\ell(\eta_{\sigma}(x))-\defe_{G}(b)\right).

On the contrary, outside of the shrunken Weyl chambers, even a conjectural formula for

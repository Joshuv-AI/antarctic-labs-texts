Abstract
. We prove a full Harnack inequality for local minimizers, as well as weak solutions to nonlocal problems with non-standard growth. The main auxiliary results are local boundedness and a weak Harnack inequality for functions in a corresponding De Giorgi class. This paper builds upon a recent work on regularity estimates for such nonlocal problems by the same authors.

35B65, 47G20, 35D30, 35B45, 35A15

1.
Introduction

The goal of the present work is to prove a full Harnack inequality for local minimizers and weak solutions to a class of nonlocal problems which exhibit non-standard growth.
This article builds upon the recent paper
[
CKW21
]
, in which we study regularity properties for local minimizers of nonlocal energy functionals, as well as weak solutions to nonlocal equations with non-standard growth. We prove that these objects satisfy a suitable fractional Caccioppoli inequality and therefore belong to corresponding De Giorgi classes. In this work, we show that any function in such De Giorgi class satisfies a full Harnack inequality.
As a consequence, we obtain the full Harnack inequality for local minimizers and weak solutions.

Before we state the main result of this paper, let us formulate the main assumptions and briefly present the energy functionals respectively the nonlocal operators considered in this work. We point out that the setup of this article is in align with

The Harnack inequality was originally proved for harmonic functions and later obtained for several elliptic and parabolic local operators. It is known to have important consequences such as a priori estimates in Hölder spaces or convergence theorems.
Therefore, it plays an important role in several mathematical fields such as geometric analysis, probability or analysis of partial differential equations. For an introduction to Harnack inequalities, their history and consequences, we refer the reader to the article by Kassmann
[

The appearance of the tail term on the right-hand side of (

It is shown in

The proof of the main result

follows from a weak Harnack inequality together with the local boundedness of functions in the De Giorgi class. Our approach roughly follows the ideas of Mascolo and Papi
[

]
, where they establish
a Harnack inequality for minimizers of functionals with non-standard growth in the local case.

We would like to point out that all results in the present paper are robust in the sense that the constants stay uniform as

, since they depend on

and are independent of the actual order of differentiability

[

,
ACPS21
]

The family of operators studied in this paper exhibits non-standard growth behavior.
The regularity theory has been intensively studied in recent years.
In

2.2.
Function spaces and De Giorgi classes

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open. We define the
Orlicz
and
Orlicz–Sobolev spaces
by

\begin{split}L^{f}(\Omega)&=\{u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}~\text{measurable}:\Phi_{L^{f}(\Omega)}(u)<\infty\},\\
W^{s,f}(\Omega)&=\{u\in L^{f}(\Omega):\Phi_{W^{s,f}(\Omega)}(u)<\infty\},\\
V^{s,f}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{d})&=\{u\in L^{f}(\Omega):\Phi_{V^{s,f}(\Omega)}(u)<\infty\},\end{split}

where
\Phi_{L^{f}(\Omega)}
,
\Phi_{W^{s,f}(\Omega)}
and
\Phi_{V^{s,f}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{d})}
are
modulars
defined by

\begin{split}\Phi_{L^{f}(\Omega)}(u)&=\int_{\Omega}f(|u(x)|)\,\mathrm{d}x,\\
\Phi_{W^{s,f}(\Omega)}(u)&=(1-s)\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}f\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{d}},\\
\Phi_{V^{s,f}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{d})}(u)&=(1-s)\iint_{(\Omega^{c}\times\Omega^{c})^{c}}f\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{d}}.\end{split}

Next, we define nonlocal tails, which capture the behavior of functions
u\in V^{s,f}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{d})
at large scales.
We define the nonlocal
f^{\prime}
-Tail by

(2.7)

\mathrm{Tail}_{f^{\prime}}(u;x_{0},R)=R^{s}(f^{\prime})^{-1}\left((1-s)R^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}f^{\prime}\left(\frac{|u(y)|}{|y-x_{0}|^{s}}\right)\frac{\textnormal{d}y}{|y-x_{0}|^{d+s}}\right).

Recall that the function
f^{\prime}
does not have to be invertible. Here
(f^{\prime})^{-1}
denotes the generalized inverse, see (
2.4
).
In our previous work
[
CKW21
]
, we prove that this expression is finite if
u\in V^{s,f}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
.

We are now ready to provide the definition of De Giorgi classes.

Definition 2.8

(De Giorgi classes)
.

Let
\Omega
be an open subset in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
s\in(0,1)
,
q>1
and
c>0
. We say that
u\in G_{+}(\Omega;q,c,s,f)
if
u\in V^{s,f}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{d})
and if for every
x_{0}\in\Omega
,
0<r<R\leq d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)
and
k\in\mathbb{R}
, it holds that

(2.8)

\begin{split}&\Phi_{W^{s,f}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}(w_{+})+(1-s)\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\int_{A_{k}^{-}}f^{\prime}\left(\frac{w_{-}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{w_{+}(x)}{|x-y|^{s}}\frac{\textnormal{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{d}}\\
&\leq c\left(\frac{R}{R-r}\right)^{q}\Phi_{L^{f}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}\left(\frac{w_{+}}{R^{s}}\right)\\
&\quad+c\left(\frac{R}{R-r}\right)^{d+sq}\|w_{+}\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}(1-s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})}f^{\prime}\left(\frac{w_{+}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{s}}\right)\frac{\textnormal{d}y}{|y-x_{0}|^{d+s}},\end{split}

where

and
A_{k}^{-}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:u(y)<k\}
. We say that

if (

) holds with

,
w_{-}
and
A_{k}^{-}
replaced by
w_{-}
,
w_{+}
and

, respectively. Moreover, we denote by

.

Let

\begin{split}f\left(\sup_{B_{\rho R}(x_{0})}\frac{u_{+}}{R^{s}}\right)&\leq C\frac{\delta^{(1-q)2d/s_{0}}}{(\tau-\rho)^{\gamma}}\fint_{B_{\tau R}(x_{0})}f\left(\frac{u_{+}(x)}{R^{s}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\delta f\left(\frac{\mathrm{Tail}_{f^{\prime}}(u_{+};x_{0},R/2)}{(R/2)^{s}}\right),\end{split}

Remark 3.2
.

In particular,

implies that functions

are locally bounded from above in

can be proved in the same way. Finiteness of

is a consequence of

[

Proof.

We may assume that
x_{0}=0
. For
j\geq 0
, we set

\begin{split}&R_{j}=\rho R+2^{-j}(\tau-\rho)R,\quad B_{j}=B_{R_{j}},\\
&k_{j}=(1-2^{-j})k,\quad\tilde{k}_{j}=(k_{j}+k_{j+1})/2,\\
&w_{j}=(u-k_{j})_{+},\quad\tilde{w}_{j}=(u-\tilde{k}_{j})_{+},\end{split}

where
k
is a positive number that will be determined later. Note that
R_{j+1}<R_{j}\leq 2R_{j+1}
,
k_{j}\leq\tilde{k}_{j}\leq k_{j+1}
and
w_{j+1}\leq\tilde{w}_{j}\leq w_{j}
. We denote by
A^{+}_{h,r}
the set
\{x\in B_{r}:u(x)>h\}
.

Let
\sigma=\max\{s_{0}/2,(3s-1)/2\}\in(0,s)
. Then, it is easy to check that

(3.1)

1-\sigma\leq\frac{3}{2}(1-s)\quad\text{and}\quad s-\sigma\geq\min\{s_{0}/2,(1-\sigma)/3\}.

First, by Hölder’s inequality we have

\begin{split}\fint_{B_{j+1}}f\left(\frac{w_{j+1}(x)}{R^{s}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x&\leq\frac{1}{|B_{j+1}|}\int_{A_{\tilde{k}_{j},R_{j+1}}^{+}}f\left(\frac{\tilde{w}_{j}(x)}{R^{s}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\frac{|A_{\tilde{k}_{j},R_{j+1}}^{+}|^{\frac{\sigma}{d}}}{2^{-d}|B_{j}|}\left(\int_{B_{j+1}}f\left(\frac{\tilde{w}_{j}(x)}{R^{s}}\right)^{\frac{d}{d-\sigma}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{d-\sigma}{d}}.\end{split}

By applying the fractional Sobolev inequality to
\tilde{w}_{j}/R^{s}
in

4.
Weak Harnack inequality

The goal of this section is to prove a weak Harnack inequality for functions
u\in G_{-}(\Omega;q,c,s,f)
. There exist several possible estimates in the literature, which go under the name “weak Harnack inequality”. They all
differ in the aspect that
\inf u
is estimated by different Lebesgue-norms of

. We will prove an estimate of the following type since it allows us to deduce a full Harnack inequality by combination with

3.3
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Omega
be an open subset in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
0<s_{0}\leq s<1
,

,

and assume that

satisfies (

). There exist constants

and

The constants

and

depend only on

,
s_{0}
,
q
and
c
.

Before we give the proof of

, we recall the following growth lemma from

]
:

.

[

, Theorem 4.1]

be an open subset in

,

,

,

,

Proof of

.

First of all, we claim that for any

\displaystyle\frac{|A^{+}_{t,R}|}{|B_{R}|}\leq\left(\frac{L}{\delta t}\right)^{a}.

The proof of (

q

From integration by parts and again (

f
q

), we see that

\displaystyle\int_{L/R^{s}}^{\infty}t^{-1-a}f^{\varepsilon}(t)\textnormal{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a}\left(\frac{L}{R^{s}}\right)^{-a}f^{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{L}{R^{s}}\right)-\frac{1}{a}\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{-a}f^{\varepsilon}(t)+\frac{\varepsilon}{a}\int_{L/R^{s}}^{\infty}t^{-a}f^{\varepsilon-1}(t)f^{\prime}(t)\textnormal{d}t

\delta f^{\varepsilon}\left(C\frac{\sup_{B_{R/2}}u+\mathrm{Tail}_{f^{\prime}}(u_{-};0,R/2)}{(R/2)^{s}}\right)\leq C\delta\left(f^{\varepsilon}\left(\sup_{B_{R/2}}\frac{u}{R^{s}}\right)+f^{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\mathrm{Tail}_{f^{\prime}}(u_{-};0,R)}{R^{s}}\right)\right).

By taking
\delta
sufficiently small so that
C\delta<1/2
, we have

f^{\varepsilon}\left(\sup_{B_{R/2}}\frac{u}{R^{s}}\right)\leq C\fint_{B_{R}}f^{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{u(x)}{R^{s}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+f^{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\mathrm{Tail}_{f^{\prime}}(u_{-};0,R)}{R^{s}}\right).

Thus, it follows from
Theorem

4.1
that

f^{\varepsilon}\left(\sup_{B_{R/2}}\frac{u}{R^{s}}\right)\leq Cf^{\varepsilon}\left(\inf_{B_{R/2}}\frac{u}{R^{s}}\right)+Cf^{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\mathrm{Tail}_{f^{\prime}}(u_{-};0,R)}{R^{s}}\right).

The desired inequality follows by using
(a+b)^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-1}(a^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}+b^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}})
, as well as the estimate
f(a)+f(b)\leq 2f(a+b)
and
Lemma

2.4
.

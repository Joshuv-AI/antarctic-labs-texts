CAT(0) spaces of higher rank
Stephan Stadler

Abstract
. Ballmann’s Rank Rigidity Conjecture predicts that a CAT(0) space of higher rank with a geometric group action is rigid – isometric to a Riemannian symmetric space, a Euclidean building, or splits as a direct product. We confirm this conjecture in rank 2. For CAT(0) spaces of higher rank n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 we prove rigidity if the space contains a periodic n n -flat and every complete geodesic lies in some n n -flat. This does not require a geometric group action.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Main results

A
Hadamard manifold
is a simply connected Riemannian manifold of non-positive sectional curvature.
Its
rank
is the maximal dimension of an isometrically embedded Euclidean space, a so-called
flat
.
The structure of manifolds of non-positive curvature and higher rank was clarified in the eighties, in the series of papers
[
BBE85
,
BBS85
]
,
culminating in Ballmann’s celebrated Rank Rigidity Theorem
[
Bal85
]
: The universal covering of a non-positively curved manifold of finite volume
and higher rank is either a Riemannian symmetric space or splits as a direct product.

The modern theory of non-positive curvature originates from Gromov’s seminal paper
[
Gro87
]
.
Nowadays,
CAT(0) spaces – the synthetic versions of Hadamard manifolds – play an important role in mathematics beyond geometry and topology.
They appear in many different fields such as geometric group theory,
representation theory, arithmetic and optimization.
For an account of the huge amount of literature on metric spaces with synthetic curvature bounds, see the bibliographies in
[
AKP19
,
Bal04
,
BH99
]
.
The following conjecture, formulated by Ballmann and Buyalo in
[
BB08
]
, is the main motivation for the present paper.

Conjecture

(Rank Rigidity)
.

Let
X
be a locally compact geodesically complete CAT(0) space. Suppose that
\Gamma
is a group of isometries of
X

with limit set
\Lambda(\Gamma)=\partial_{\infty}X
. Then
\operatorname{diam}(\partial_{T}X)=\pi
implies that
X
is a Riemannian symmetric space or a Euclidean
building of rank at least 2, or that
X
non-trivially splits as a direct product.

The condition on the diameter is equivalent to saying that any complete geodesic bounds a flat half-plane.
If a group
\Gamma
acts on a CAT(0) space
X
, then its
limit set

\Lambda(\Gamma)
is the set of accumulation points
of a
\Gamma
-orbit in the ideal boundary
\partial_{\infty}X
. The main example of group actions on CAT(0) spaces with full limit set are
geometric actions
, meaning
cocompact and properly discontinuous actions by isometries. In the smooth case, namely for Hadamard manifolds, it is enough to assume finite covolume instead of
cocompactness.

The conjecture is known for Hadamard manifolds if

\Gamma
acts properly discontinously and with finite covolume
[
Bal85
,
BS87a
]
, or if
\Gamma
satisfies the duality condition
[
Bal95
,
EH90
]
.
It is also known for homogeneous Hadamard manifolds
[
Heb93
]
.
For singular spaces, the conjecture is known to hold

•

for a 2-dimensional simplicial complex with a piecewise
smooth CAT(0) metric and a geometric group action
[
BB95
]
;

•

for a 3-dimensional simplicial complex with a piecewise Euclidean CAT(0) metric and a geometric group action
[
BB00
]
;

•

for a finite dimensional CAT(0) cube complex with a geometric group action

[
CS11
]
1
1

1

For cube complexes, the statement of rank rigidity takes the form of a splitting result, since
neither symmetric spaces nor Euclidean buildings carry a piecewise cubical CAT(0) metric
[
Lee00
]
.
;

•

for a locally compact and geodesically complete CAT(0) space such that its isometry group does not fix a point at infinity and
which supports a cocompact isometric action by an amenable locally compact group
[
CM15
]
.

•

for a space of rank 2 which supports a geometric group action by a group
\Gamma
and whose
Tits boundary contains a proper closed
\Gamma
-invariant subset
[
Ric19
]
.

Note that the interesting case in the next to last item concerns non-discrete groups since the only locally compact geodesically complete CAT(0)
space which admits a cocompact isometric action by an amenable group is the flat Euclidean space
[
AB98
, Corollary C]
.

We prove:

Theorem A
.

Let
X
be a locally compact and geodesically complete CAT(0) space of rank
n\geq 2
.
Suppose that every complete geodesic in
X
lies in an
n
-flat.
If
X
contains a periodic
n
-flat, then
X
is a Riemannian symmetric space or a Euclidean building, or
X
splits as a direct product.

Corollary A
.

Let
X
be a locally compact and geodesically complete CAT(0) space of rank
n\geq 2
.
Suppose that every complete geodesic in
X
lies in an
n
-flat.
If
X
contains a periodic
n
-flat, then
X
has a unique decomposition into irreducible factors

X=\mathbb{R}^{j}\times X_{1}\times\ldots\times X_{k}\times Y_{1}\times\ldots\times Y_{l}\times M_{1}\times\ldots\times M_{m}

where
X_{a}
are rank 1 spaces,
Y_{b}
are irreducible Euclidean buildings and
M_{c}
are
irreducible Riemannian symmetric space of higher rank.

Note that in the smooth case, rank
n
is expressed by the property that every complete geodesic admits
n
linearly independent parallel Jacobi fields.
In the presence of a geometric group action these Jacobi fields can then be “integrated” to
n
-flats
[
Bal95
, Section IV.4]
.
Hence, in the context of metric spaces, the assumption on the existence of
n
-flats in Theorem
A
is natural.
Recall that a flat is
periodic
, if its stabilizer contains a subgroup acting geometrically.
We point out that Theorem
A
does not assume any symmetries besides the existence of a single periodic
n
-flat.
Also, if
X
is a rank
n
symmetric space or Euclidean building, and
\Gamma
acts cocompactly on
X
, then
X

contains a periodic
n
-flat
[
BB95
, Theorem 8.9]
,
[
Mos73
, Lemma 8.3]
,
[
PR72
, Theorem 2.8]
.
We emphasize that some symmetry is required, since a geodesically complete CAT(0) space of higher rank and whose Tits boundary has diameter
\pi

does not have to be a product or a Euclidean building
[
BB00
]
.
Admittedly, it is unknown if a locally compact and geodesically complete CAT(0) space of rank
n
, where every complete geodesic lies in an
n
-flat,
and which supports a cocompact group action, has to contain a periodic
n
-flat.

For spaces of rank 2, we prove:

Theorem B
.

Let
X
be a locally compact and geodesically complete CAT(0) space of rank
2
.
Suppose that the diameter of its Tits boundary
\partial_{T}X
is equal to
\pi
.
If
X
admits a geometric group action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
, then
X
is a Riemannian symmetric space or a Euclidean building, or
X
non-trivially splits as a direct product.

This settles the Rank Rigidity Conjecture in rank 2, at least for spaces with a geometric group action.

We point out that we do not deduce Theorem
B
from Theorem
A
as we were unable to a priori show the existence of
a periodic 2-flat under the assumptions of Theorem
B
.
While the overall strategies for Theorems
A
and
B
are similar, the possible lack of a periodic 2-flat
in Theorem
B
leads to technical difficulties and the need for geometric measure theory methods.

1.2.
Strategy and predecessors

Our proofs rely on the study of the asymptotic geometry of CAT(0) spaces, in particular the Tits boundary as introduced by Gromov in

[
BGS85
]
. This approach is inspired by Mostow’s Strong Rigidity
[
Mos73
]
: Two irreducible locally symmetric spaces of noncompact type
and rank
\geq 2
with isomorphic fundamental groups are isometric up to scaling. Gromov generalized Mostow’s theorem to the case where only one of the two spaces is assumed to be locally symmetric
[
BGS85
]
, see also
[
BE87
,
Ebe83
]
. While in Mostow’s original proof, the Tits metric was only implicitly present, Gromov’s argument relies on a precise study of Tits boundaries. Putting complete focus on the asymptotics of a non-positively curved manifold, Burns and Spatzier introduced the notion of topological Tits buildings
[
BS87b
]
and were able to give an alternative proof of Ballmann’s
Rank Rigidity Theorem
[
BS87a
]
, adapting Gromov’s arguments. The counterpart of Mostow’s theorem for Euclidean buildings was proved by Prasad
[
Pra79
]
.
It is natural to wonder if asymptotic rigidity extends even further, from Hadamard manifolds to CAT(0) spaces, quasi from Riemannian geometry to Alexandrov geometry.
This is indeed true and the content of Leeb’s Rigidity Theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
Lee00
, Main Theorem]
)
.

Let
X
be a locally compact, geodesically complete CAT(0) space. If
\partial_{T}X
is a connected thick irreducible spherical building,
then
X
is either a symmetric space or a Euclidean building.

The proofs of Theorem
A
and
B
use Leeb’s result.
In order to show that the Tits boundary is a spherical building, we rely on Lytchak’s Rigidity Theorem.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
Lyt05
, Main Theorem]
)
.

Let
Z
be a finite dimensional geodesically complete
CAT(1) space. If
Z
has a proper closed subset
A
containing with each
point all of its antipodes, then
Z
is
a spherical join or a spherical building.

The strategy to approach rank rigidity in this way is well-known.

There are two main issues preventing one to implement this strategy.
First, we have to ensure geodesic completeness of the Tits boundary
\partial_{T}X
.
And secondly, we need to find
a proper closed set
A\subset\partial_{T}X
which is also closed under taking antipodes.

We overcome these obstacles as follows.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
A
any point at infinity is contained in a round
(n-1)
-sphere.
This implies geodesic completeness by a topological argument using local homology
[
Kle99
,
LS07
]
.
For Theorem
B
we first work with a geodesically complete subspace
\partial^{ess}_{T}X
called the
essential Tits boundary

which consists of the union of all simple closed geodesics in
\partial_{T}X
. We then show that
\partial^{ess}_{T}X
is a
spherical building and, in a second step, that it has to agree with the ordinary Tits boundary.

The second issue is more significant. We prove that the subset
O\subset\partial_{T}X

of
regular points
– points which have a neighborhood isometric to an open set in a round
(n-1)
-sphere –
is non-empty and closed under taking antipodes. As a consequence, either
\partial_{T}X
is equal to a round
(n-1)
-sphere,
or
A=\partial_{T}X\setminus O
is subject to Lytchak’s Theorem
1.2
.

1.3.
How to find regular points

The existence of regular points is the key to both theorems. In this section we provide an informal discussion of the arguments that yield regular points in Theorems
A
and
B
.

1.3.1.
Regular points at infinity of 2-dimensional spaces

Let us try to explain the idea for a locally compact CAT(0) space
X
of dimension 2. If the Tits boundary of our space has finite diameter, but
does not contain a regular point, then it basically branches everywhere. Intuitively this should contradict the local compactness of our space.
Now suppose that
F\subset X
is a periodic 2-flat. If its Tits boundary
\partial_{T}F
does not contain a regular point, then

\partial_{T}F
contains a dense set of branch points. Using periodicity, it is not hard to see that
each branch point at infinity yields a flat half-plane
H
orthogonal to
F
. Since
X
has dimension 2, any two such half-planes have to be orthogonal to each other.
By periodicity of
F
, there is a compact set
K\subset F
and an infinite sequence of pairwise orthogonal flat half-planes which intersect
K
. This does indeed contradict local compactness.

1.3.2.
The rank 2 case of Theorem
A

The actual proof of Theorem
A
in rank 2 is not much harder then the 2-dimensional case sketched above.
Again, from periodicity, one obtains families of orthogonal flat half-planes.
The key point is that these are again pairwise orthogonal (Lemma
6.2
). This orthogonality can be explained as follows.
Suppose
F
is a 2-flat in our rank 2 space
X
. For simplicity we assume that there is no 2-flat parallel to
F
in
X
.
Let
H^{\pm}
be two flat half-planes whose boundaries
\partial H^{\pm}\subset F
are not parallel.
Let
\eta^{\pm}\in\partial_{T}X
denote the centers of the half-circles
\partial_{T}H^{\pm}\subset\partial_{T}X
.
We think of
\partial H^{\pm}
to be the nearest point projections of
\eta^{\pm}
to
F
.
This is justified by the orthogonality of
H^{\pm}
to
F
which ensures that
\partial H^{\pm}
minimizes the relative distance to
\eta^{\pm}
on
F
.
Now if the angle between
H^{-}
and
H^{+}

would be strictly less than
\frac{\pi}{2}
, then by a first variation argument we could strictly decrease this relative distance (Busemann level)
and arrive at a contradiction.
(See Lemma
4.2
for more details.)

The case of rank larger than 2 is naturally a little more complicated but follows the same scheme.

1.3.3.
Regular points in Theorem
B

The setting of Theorem
B
awaits with a new difficulty. Since we do not assume the existence of a periodic flat,
we do not get orthogonal flat half-planes right away. However, a geometric group action still has a periodic 1-flat, an axis
g
.
Let us assume that
g
lies in a 2-flat
F
.
From the assumption on the Tits diameter, every branch point
\xi
in
\partial_{T}F
together with its antipode
\hat{\xi}
yields a sequence of Tits geodesics
in
\partial_{T}X\setminus\partial_{T}F
joining
\xi
to
\hat{\xi}
and whose lengths converge to
\pi
.
If we are able to extract a limit geodesic which does not run in
\partial_{T}F
, then we can produce an orthogonal flat half-plane corresponding to the branch point

\xi
and argue as before.
While everything up to now can be done purely geometrically, this part needs some analysis.
Intuitively, a geodesic
c
in
\partial_{T}X
of length approximately
\pi
which joins two antipodal points in
\partial_{T}F

should bound a surface in
X
which resembles a flat half-plane orthogonal to
F
.
We find such a surface by means of geometric measure theory, namely by solving an asymptotic Plateau problem relative to
F
.
More precisely, we find a minimal surface
S
whose boundary lies in
F
and which is asymptotic to
c
.
The relative minimality ensures that locally near every point in
\partial S
the surface has a definite distance from
F
.
This will serve as a substitute for orthogonality. Since the length of
c
is close to
\pi
, the surface
S
has almost Euclidean area growth.
We also show that the boundary
\partial S
has to intersect the axis
g
. Using the axial isometries we can then produce the required flat half-plane as
a limit of relative minimizers, appropriately translated by the axial isometry.

1.4.
Open questions

Recall (a special case of) Gromov’s Flat Closing Problem:

Question

(Flat Closing
[
Gro93
, 6.B
3
]
)
.

Let
X
be a locally compact geodesically complete CAT(0) space with a geometric group action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
.
Suppose that
X
contains an
n
-flat. Does this imply that
X
contains a
\Gamma
-periodic
n
-flat?

In general, the answer is expected to be no, cf.
[
KP99
,
SW11
]
. Yet, the Flat Closing Problem has a positive answer in a couple of cases, see the introduction to
[
KP99
]
.

As mentioned above, Flat Closing holds for Riemannian symmetric spaces and Euclidean buildings. Theorem
A
shows that
Flat Closing for rank
n
spaces where every complete geodesic lies in an
n
-flat is equivalent to Rank Rigidity (in the cocompact case).

Since Theorem
B
proves Rank Rigidity in rank 2 it also confirms Flat Closing for rank 2 spaces of Tits-diameter
\pi
.
We point out that if a group
\Gamma
acts geometrically on a CAT(0) space
X
, then
\Gamma
contains a hyperbolic element,
namely one that acts as an axial isometry on
X

[
Swe99
, Theorem 11]
. Formally, this corresponds to the
n=1
case of Flat Closing.
Our proof of Theorem
B

relies on this fact.

In view of Theorem
A
, we would like to state the following interesting special case of Gromov’s question.

Conjecture

(Higher Rank Flat Closing)
.

Let
X
be a locally compact geodesically complete CAT(0) space whose Tits-diameter is
\pi
and which supports a geometric group action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
.
If
X
has rank
n
, then
X
contains a periodic
n
-flat.

By the existence of asymmetric Euclidean buildings, we genuinely expect Theorem
A
to treat a more general setting than Rank Rigidity.
Actually, we obtain Theorem
A
as a consequence of the following.

Theorem C

(Theorem
5.7
)
.

Let
X
be a locally compact CAT(0) space of rank
n\geq 2
. If
X
contains a periodic
n
-flat
F
, then
\sigma=\partial_{T}F
contains a dense subset

U\subset\sigma
which is open in
\partial_{T}X
.

This theorem aims at to following general conjecture:

Conjecture

(Lytchak)
.

Let
X
be a locally compact CAT(0) space with a geometric group action.
Then
\partial_{T}X
contains a relatively compact open subset.

Again, if Gromov’s Flat Closing would hold for higher rank CAT(0) spaces, then Theorem
C
would confirm Lytchak’s conjecture.

1.5.
Organization

In Section
2
, we agree on notation and recall background on the geometry of spaces with upper curvature bounds.
In Section
3
, we recall metric characterizations of spherical and Euclidean buildings.
The central content of this section is a recognition theorem for buildings (Theorem
3.3
) which is later used in the proof of Theorem
A
.
The short Section
4
is included for the convenience of the reader. It illustrates the basic idea behind discreteness for branch points at infinity in
a simplified setting.
We begin Section
5
by recalling how branch points at infinity of periodic flats lead to flat half-planes orthogonal to the periodic flat.
The goal of this section is then to show that a periodic
n
-flat in a space of rank
n
leads to a non-empty set of regular points at infinity
(Theorem
5.7
). An important ingredient is Proposition
5.4
which constructs a flat 3-dimensional half-space from a sequence of flat
half-planes. We conclude this section with the proof of Theorem
A
.
Section
6
builds up to a proof of Theorem
B
assuming our key technical result, the
Half-Plane Lemma
.
Appenix
A
is devoted to geometric measure theory with the aim to provide a proof of the
Half-Plane Lemma
.
We also prove some folklore results, including monotonicity and volume rigidity of minimizing currents in CAT(0) spaces.
However, our approach is to find a streamlined path to the
Half-Plane Lemma
and we do not try to prove
required ingredients in their most general form.

2.1.
Metric spaces

Euclidean
n
-space with its flat metric will be denoted by
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The unit sphere
S^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
equipped with the induced metric will be referred to as a

round sphere
. Its intersection with a half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times[0,\infty)
is called a
round hemisphere
.
We denote the distance between two points
x
and
y
in a metric space
X
by
|x,y|
.
If
A\subset X
denotes a subset, then
|x,A|
refers to the greatest lower bound for distances from points in
A
to
x
.
For subsets
A,A^{\prime}\subset X
we denote the Hausdorff (pseudo-) distance by
|A,A^{\prime}|_{H}
.
For
x\in X
and
r>0
, we denote by
B_{r}(x)
and
\bar{B}_{r}(x)
the open and closed
r
-ball around
x
, respectively.
Similarly,
N_{r}(A)
and
\bar{N}_{r}(A)
denote the open and closed
r
-neighborhood of a subset
A\subset X
, respectively.
Moreover,
S_{r}(x)
denotes the
r
-sphere around
x
and by
\dot{B}_{r}(x)
we denote the punctured
r
-ball
B_{r}(x)\setminus\{x\}
.
A
geodesic

is an isometric embedding of an interval. It is called a
geodesic segment
, if it is compact.
The
endpoints
of a geodesic segment
c
are denoted by
\partial c
.
A geodesic segment
c

branches
at an endpoint
y\in\partial c=\{x,y\}
, if there are geodesics
c^{\pm}
starting in
x
which strictly contain
c

and such that
c^{-}\cap c^{+}=c
.

A
triangle
is a union of three geodesics connecting three points.

X
is
a geodesic metric space
if
any pair of points of
X

is connected by a geodesic.
It is
geodesically complete
if every geodesic segment is contained in a complete geodesic.

2.2.
Spaces with an upper curvature bound

For
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
, let
D_{\kappa}\in(0,\infty]
be the diameter of the complete, simply connected surface
M^{2}_{\kappa}

of constant curvature
\kappa
. A complete metric space is called a
CAT(
\kappa
) space

if any pair of its points with distance less than
D_{\kappa}
is connected by a geodesic and if
all triangles
with perimeter less than
2D_{\kappa}

are not thicker than
the
comparison triangle
in
M_{\kappa}^{2}
. In particular, geodesics between points of distance less than
D_{\kappa}

are unique. Hence,
X
is a CAT(
\kappa
) space, then we can define for every
p\in X
and every subset
A\subset B_{D_{\kappa}}(p)

the
geodesic cone
C_{p}(A)
.

For any CAT(
\kappa
) space
X
,
the angle between each pair of geodesics starting at the same point
is well defined.
Let
x,y,z
be three points at pairwise distance less than
D_{\kappa}
in a CAT(
\kappa
) space
X
.
Whenever
x\neq y
, the geodesic between
x
and
y
is unique and will be denoted
by
xy
. For
y,z\neq x
, the angle at
x
between
xy
and
xz

will be denoted by
\angle_{x}(y,z)
.

The
space of directions

\Sigma_{x}X
at
x\in X
, equipped with the angle metric,
is a CAT(1) space.
The Euclidean cone
over
\Sigma_{x}
is a CAT(0) space.
It is denoted by
T_{x}X
and called
the
tangent space
at
x
of
X
. Its tip will be denoted by
o_{x}
.
There are natural
logarithm maps

\log_{x}:\dot{B}_{x}(D_{\kappa})\to\Sigma_{x}X,\quad\quad\log_{x}:X\to T_{x}X.

A natural notion of dimension
\dim(X)
for a CAT(
\kappa
) space
X
was
introduced by
Kleiner in
[
Kle99
]
.
It is equal to the supremum of topological dimensions of compact subsets of
X
, satisfies

\dim(X)=\sup_{x\in X}\{\dim(\Sigma_{x}X)+1\}

and coincides with the homological dimension of
X
.

2.3.
CAT(1) spaces

For two CAT(1) spaces
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
we denote by
Z_{1}\circ Z_{2}
their
spherical join
.
It is a CAT(1) space of diameter
\pi
.
Note that every round subsphere
\sigma^{\prime}
in a round sphere
\sigma
yields a join decomposition
\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}\circ\sigma^{\prime\prime}

where
\dim(\sigma)=\dim(\sigma^{\prime})+\dim(\sigma^{\prime\prime})+1
. Also, every round hemisphere
\tau
decomposes as
\tau=\partial\tau\circ\zeta

where
\zeta
denotes the center of
\tau
. A CAT(1) space
Z
is called
irreducible
, if it does not admit a non-trivial
spherical join decomposition.

A subset
C
in a CAT(1) space
Z
is called
\pi
-convex

if for any pair of points
x,y\in Z
at distance less than
\pi
the unique geodesic
xy
is contained in
C
.
If
C
is closed, then it is CAT(1) with respect to the induced metric.
For instance, a ball of radius at most
\frac{\pi}{2}
is
\pi
-convex.
Let
C\subset Z
be a closed convex subset with radius
\operatorname{rad}(C)\geq\pi
. Then we define the set of
poles
for
C
by

\operatorname{pol}(C):=\{\eta\in Z|\ d(\eta,\cdot)|_{C}\equiv\frac{\pi}{2}\}.

If
\operatorname{diam}(C)>\pi
, then
C
has no pole. If
\operatorname{diam}(C)=\operatorname{rad}(C)=\pi
, then
\operatorname{pol}(C)
is closed and convex.
The convex hull of
C
and
\operatorname{pol}(C)
is canonically isometric to
C\circ\operatorname{pol}(C)
.
In case
C
consists of a pair of
antipodes

\xi^{\pm}
,
d(\xi^{-},\xi^{+})=\pi
, then the convex hull of

\{\xi^{-},\xi^{+}\}
and
\operatorname{pol}(\{\xi^{-},\xi^{+}\})
is isometric to a spherical suspension of
\operatorname{pol}(\{\xi^{-},\xi^{+}\})
.

A subset
A\subset Z
is called
spherical
, if it
embeds isometrically into a round sphere.
A
(spherical)

3.
Buildings

3.1.
Metric characterizations

Originally, buildings were introduced by Tits in order to provide geometric interpretations for algebraic groups.
A geometric treatment of spherical and Euclidean buildings from the perspective of CAT(
\kappa
)-spaces
was developed in
[
KL97
]
. Here we take the latter point of view.
The relevance of spherical and Euclidean buildings to Alexandrov geometry lies in the fact that they appear as Tits boundaries
and asymptotic cones, respectively, of non-positively curved symmetric spaces of higher rank
[
KL97
]
.
Instead of recalling the usual definitions of spherical and Euclidean buildings (which can be found in
[
KL97
]
)
we provide simple metric characterizations proved in
[
CL01
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a connected, piecewise spherical (respectively Euclidean)
complex of dimension
n\geq 2
satisfying

(1)

X
is CAT(1) (respectively CAT(0)).

(2)

Every
(n-1)
–cell is contained in at least two
n
-cells.

(3)

Links of positive dimension are connected.

(4)

Links of dimension 1 have diameter
\pi
.

Then
X
is isometric to a spherical building (respectively a Euclidean
building).

Definition 3.2
.

A
1-dimensional spherical building
is a 1-dimensional piecewise spherical CAT(1) space of diameter equal to
\pi

where every vertex has valence at least 2.
A
1-dimensional Euclidean building
is a geodesically complete tree.

Note that every building is geodesically complete. Moreover, in an
n
-dimensional spherical building (respectively Euclidean
building) any pair of points is contained in a round
n
-sphere (respectively an
n
-flat).
In particular, spherical buildings have diameter
\pi
and the rank of a Euclidean building coincides with its dimension.
However, a piecewise spherical CAT(1) spaces of diameter
\pi
does not have to be a spherical building
[
BB99
]
.
Similarly, a geodesically complete, piecewise Euclidean CAT(0) complex where every pair of points lies in a 2-flat
does not have to be a product or a Euclidean building
[
BB00
]
.

Examples of spherical buildings arise from projective planes, whereas
examples of (locally compact irreducible) Euclidean buildings
arise from simple algebraic groups
over non-Archimedean locally compact fields with a discrete valuation, see for instance
[
Lee00
, Section 2.3]
.

4.

This short section is illustrative. We present the key observation behind discreteness of branch points in a simplified setting.

The following basic fact will be used repeatedly throughout the paper.

Lemma 4.1

(
[

, Sublemma 2.3]
)
.

Let
F
be a flat in a CAT(0) space and let
C\subset X
be a closed convex subset so that
\partial_{\infty}F\subset\partial_{\infty}C
.
Then

contains a flat

parallel to

.

Proof.

The function

, the distance function to

, is a convex function on

. By assumption,

is bounded on

and therefore constant,
d_{C}\equiv a
.
By
[

, Flat Strip Lemma 2.3.5]
, there exists an isometric embedding
\psi:F\times[0,a]\to X

such that
\psi(\cdot,0)=\operatorname{id}_{F}
and
\psi(\cdot,a)=\pi_{C}|_{F}
. This implies the claim.
∎

In the upcoming section we will see how branch points at infinity of a flat

lead to flat half-planes orthogonal to
F
.
Hence the next lemma, which is not needed in the rest of the paper, explains the conflict between branch points at infinity and local compactness of a space.
The responsible geometric property is flatness of intersecting parallel sets, similar as in
[

, Theorem 2]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
X
be a CAT(0) space of rank 2 and let
F\subset X
be a 2-flat with
P(F)=F
.
Suppose that
H^{\pm}\subset X
are two flat half-planes, orthogonal to
F
.
If the boundaries
\partial H^{\pm}\subset F
are not parallel, then the angle between
H^{-}

and
H^{+}
is at least
\frac{\pi}{2}
.

Proof.

Set
h^{\pm}:=\partial H^{\pm}
and put

. Further, denote by
\eta^{\pm}
the centers of
\partial_{\infty}H^{\pm}
.
Since
H^{-}
is orthogonal to
F
, the Busemann function
b_{\eta^{+},p}
is non-negative on
F
.
If
\angle_{p}(\eta^{-},\eta^{+})<\frac{\pi}{2}
, then, by the first variation formula, there exists a point

such that
b_{\eta^{+},p}(q)<0
. Since
H^{-}\subset P(h^{-})
, we see
HB(\eta^{+},q)\cap P(h^{-})\neq\emptyset
.
Because
\partial_{\infty}h^{+}\subset\partial_{\infty}(HB(\eta^{+},q))\cap\partial_{\infty}P(h^{-}))=\partial_{\infty}(HB(\eta^{+},q)\cap P(h^{-}))
,
we infer from Lemma

that there is a complete geodesic
\tilde{h}^{-}\subset HB(\eta^{+},q)\cap P(h^{-})
which is parallel to
h^{-}
.
Since
h^{-}
is not parallel to

, there exists a 2-flat

which contains

.
Thus,
\partial_{\infty}\tilde{F}=\partial_{\infty}F
and therefore
\tilde{F}
is parallel to

. By assumption, we must have
\tilde{F}=F
.
This is a contradiction because

is bounded above on

by its value at

. In particular, it is negative on
\tilde{h}^{-}
.
But at the same time it is non-negative on

.
∎

6.4.
The Tits boundary is a building

Definition 6.6

For a CAT(0) space of rank 2, we define its

Lemma 6.7

Since

contains a 2-flat, the essential Tits boundary is non-empty.
Let
\xi
and
\hat{\xi}
be points in
\partial^{ess}_{T}X
. Then there is a geodesic

\pi

\partial_{T}X
.
We will show that this geodesic is contained in
\partial^{ess}_{T}X
. Since
\partial^{ess}_{T}X

\pi

to begin with. Let
\eta
be a point on

be such that

.
Now we extend
\alpha
beyond

to a point

∎

Lemma 6.8
.

Let

be a 1-dimensional CAT(1) space of diameter

.
Then the subset

of regular points is closed under taking antipodes.

Proof.

Let

be an antipode.
Then there exists

We may assume that the geodesic

Appendix A
The relative asymptotic Plateau problem after Kleiner–Lang

The primary goal of this section is to provide a proof of the
Half-Plane Lemma
.
Our proof requires a solution of an asymptotic Plateau problem relative to a flat.
Since this has not been done in the literature, we need some preparation.
The (non-relative) asymptotic Plateau problem has recently been solved by Kleiner–Lang
[
KL20
]

and we use their work extensively throughout this section. The idea to use solutions to a relative Plateau problem originated from
[
HKS22
]
.

In Section
A.1
, we agree on notation and proof monotonicity and volume rigidity of minimizing currents in CAT(0) spaces,
a result which is folklore.
In Section
A.2
, we solve the asymptotic Plateau problem relative to a 2-flat.
We provide basic properties of relative minimizers which are required for our proof of the
Half-Plane Lemma
.
At last, in Section
A.3
, we present the proof of the
Half-Plane Lemma
.
We have refrained from any kind of general treatment, in particular we only discuss spaces of rank 2.
This is all we need here and a broader discussion would get out of hand.

The general reference for this section is
[
KL20
]
.
See also
[

Lan11
]
for a thorough background on geometric measure theory in metric spaces.

\lambda
-Lipschitz map which
contracts radial geodesics relative
p
by the factor
\lambda\in(0,1)
, then we have
(c_{\lambda})_{\#}S\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>\bar{B}_{r}(p)=S\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>\bar{B}_{\lambda r}(p)
for
r\leq r_{0}
.
Moreover, since the characteristic set is closed,
\chi_{S}\cap\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(p)
agrees with
C_{p}(\chi_{S}\cap\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(p))
.
By monotonicity and the mass control, the multiplicity function
\theta
has to be equal to
1
in a neighborhood of
p
in
\chi_{S}
.
Since
S
is conical,
\theta\equiv 1
on all of
\chi_{S}\cap\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(p)
. This implies for all
r<r_{0}
,

\mathcal{H}^{n}(\chi_{S}\cap B_{r}(p))=\omega_{n}\cdot r^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\chi_{S}\cap S_{r}(p))=n\cdot\omega_{n}\cdot r^{n-1}.

Thus
c_{\lambda}
scales
\mathcal{H}^{n}
-measure on
\chi_{S}\cap B_{r_{0}}(p)
by the factor
\lambda^{n}
.
We claim that for every
x\in\operatorname{spt}(S)
with
a=|p,x|
and
r\leq r_{0}-a
we have
\|S\|(\bar{B}_{r}(x))=\omega_{n}\cdot r^{n}
.
Indeed, using the lower density bound at
x
, monotonicity and the effect of
c_{\lambda}
on
\mathcal{H}^{n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\omega_{n}\cdot r^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\|S\|(B_{r}(x))=\lambda^{-n}\cdot\|S\|(c_{\lambda}(B_{r}(x)))

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{-n}\cdot\|S\|(B_{\lambda r}(c_{\lambda}(x)))

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{-n}\cdot\left(\frac{\lambda r}{r_{0}-\lambda a}\right)^{n}\cdot\omega_{n}\cdot r_{0}^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lambda\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}\omega_{n}\cdot r^{n}.

We infer
\chi_{S}\cap\bar{B}_{r}(x)=C_{x}(\chi_{S}\cap\bar{B}_{r}(x))
. Therefore
\chi_{S}\cap\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(p)
is conical with respect to all of its points.
In particular, it is a closed convex subset of
X
. From the mass bound, it follows that
\chi_{S}\cap\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(p)
is isometric to an Euclidean
r_{0}
-ball.
∎

A.1
, we obtain:

be a locally compact CAT(0) space and

an area minimizer.
If

, then
\operatorname{spt}(S)
is an
n
-flat in

and

has constant multiplicity 1.

A.2.
The asymptotic Plateau problem relative to a 2-flat

We fix the following setup for this section. Let
X
be a locally compact CAT(0) space of rank 2 which contains a round sphere

\sigma\subset\partial_{T}X
in its Tits boundary. We fix a 2-flat
F\subset X
with
\partial_{T}F=\sigma
and a base point
o\in F
.
Moreover,
\alpha^{+}\subset\partial_{T}X
denotes an embedded local geodesic with
\alpha^{+}\cap\sigma=\partial\alpha^{+}
.
Our goal is to solve an asymptotic Plateau problem relative
F
. Informally speaking, we search for a minimal surface
S\subset X

whose boundary lies in
F
and which is asymptotic to
\alpha^{+}
. If the length of
\alpha^{+}
is close to
\pi
, then we expect
S

to behave almost like a flat half-plane orthogonal to
F
.
In order to achieve this, we employ doubling to reduce the problem to the ordinary asymptotic Plateau problem treated in
[
KL20
]
.
So we consider the double of
X
along
F
:

\hat{X}=X^{-}\cup_{F}X^{+}

where
X^{\pm}
are two isometric copies of
X
. Note that
\partial_{T}\hat{X}=\partial_{T}X^{-}\cup_{\sigma}\partial_{T}X^{+}
.
In particular,
\hat{X}
has rank 2. Let us denote by
\iota
the natural isometry of
\hat{X}
which interchanges
X^{-}

and
X^{+}
. For subsets
A^{+}\subset X^{+}
we set
A^{-}:=\iota(A^{+})\subset X^{-}
. We call a current
S\in{\mathbf{I}}_{2,loc}(\hat{X})

symmetric
, if

S=S^{+}-\iota_{\#}(S^{+})

where
S^{+}:=S\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>X^{+}
. Finally, we also double our boundary data, and define
\alpha:=\alpha^{+}\cup\iota(\alpha^{+})\subset\partial_{T}X
.

Definition A.3
.

For
S\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,loc}(\hat{X})
set
S^{+}:=S\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>X^{+}
. Then
S^{+}
is a
relative minimizer
if

{\mathbf{M}}(S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>B^{+})\leq{\mathbf{M}}(W^{+})

whenever
B^{+}\subset X^{+}
is a Borel set such that
S\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>(B^{+}\cup B^{-})\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,c}(\hat{X})
and
W^{+}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,c}(X^{+})

satisfies
\operatorname{spt}(\partial(S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>B^{+}-W^{+}))\subset F
.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
S^{+}\in{\mathscr{D}}_{2,loc}(X^{+})
be a current of locally finite mass and with
\operatorname{spt}(\partial S^{+})\subset F
.
Suppose that
\|S^{+}\|(F)=0
and that there is a sequence
t_{k}\to 0
, such that
S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>(X\setminus N_{t_{k}}(F))\in{\mathbf{I}}_{2,loc}(X^{+})
.
Then
\partial S^{+}
is a local flat chain in
F
,
\partial S^{+}\in{\mathscr{F}}_{1,loc}(F)
. Moreover, the double
S:=S^{+}-\iota_{\#}(S^{+})
is a locally integral cycle,

S\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{2,loc}(\hat{X})
.

Proof.

For a point
p\in X^{+}
we denote by
d_{p}
the distance function to
p
.
To see that
\partial S^{+}
is a local flat chain in
F
, it is enough to prove the following claim.
For any
p\in\operatorname{spt}(\partial S^{+})
there exists
r_{0}>0
such that
\langle S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>(X\setminus N_{t_{k}}(F)),d_{p},r_{0}\rangle\in{\mathbf{I}}_{1,c}(X^{+})
for every

k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(p)
is a flat limit of integral chains
T_{k}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{2,c}(X^{+})
.

Indeed, if the claim holds, then, since the nearest point projection
\pi_{F}:X^{+}\to F
is Lipschitz,
(\pi_{F})_{\#}(S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(p))\in{\mathscr{F}}_{2,c}(F)

and therefore
\partial(\pi_{F})_{\#}(S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(p))=(\pi_{F})_{\#}\partial(S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(p))\in{\mathscr{F}}_{1,c}(F)
.
Then, either
p\notin\operatorname{spt}(\partial S^{+}-(\pi_{F})_{\#}\partial(S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(p)))
or, there exists a large
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

p\notin\operatorname{spt}(\partial S^{+}-(\pi_{F})_{\#}\langle S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>(X\setminus N_{t_{k}}(F)),d_{p},r_{0}\rangle
. In any case, we see that
\partial S^{+}
is locally flat near
p
.

To prove the claim, we set
T_{k}:=S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>(X\setminus N_{t_{k}}(F))\in{\mathbf{I}}_{2,loc}(X^{+})
. By continuity of
S^{+}
as an extended functional

[
Lan11
, Theorem 4.4]
, we have
T_{k}\to S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>(X^{+}\setminus F)
weakly.
Since
\|S^{+}\|(F)=0
, we have
S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>(X^{+}\setminus F)=S^{+}

[
Lan11
, Lemma 4.7]
.
For
r>0
we infer from
[
Lan11
, Theorem 6.2 (3)]

\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\langle T_{k},d_{p},s\rangle\|(B_{r}(p))\ ds\leq\|T_{k}\|(B_{r}(p))\leq\|S^{+}\|(B_{r}(p)).

By monotone convergence, we conclude

\int_{0}^{\infty}\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\|\langle T_{k},d_{p},s\rangle\|(B_{r}(p))\ ds\leq\|S^{+}\|(B_{r}(p)).

Now choose
r_{0}\in(0,r)
such that
\langle T_{k},d_{p},r_{0}\rangle\in{\mathbf{I}}_{1,c}(X^{+})
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\|\langle T_{k},d_{p},r_{0}\rangle\|<\infty
.
Set
T_{k}^{\prime}:=T_{k}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(p)
. Because
\|S^{+}\|(F)=0
, we see
\lim\limits_{t\to 0}\|S^{+}\|(\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(p)\cap N_{t}(F))=0
. Then
for
k<l
we have

{\mathscr{F}}(T^{\prime}_{k},T^{\prime}_{l})\leq(\|S^{+}\|+\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\|\langle T_{k},d_{p},r_{0}\rangle\|)(\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(p)\cap\bar{N}_{t_{k}}(F))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k,l\to\infty}}{{\rightarrow}}0.

Hence
(T^{\prime}_{k})
is a Cauchy sequence with respect to flat distance.
Because flat convergence implies weak convergence, the limit coincides with
S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(p)
. This confirms the claim and therefore
\partial S^{+}\in{\mathscr{F}}_{1,loc}(F)
.

By the deformation theorem
[
FF60
,
Whi99a
]
, we can now find a locally polyhedral chain
P\in{\mathscr{P}}_{1,loc}(F)
and a locally flat
chain
V\in{\mathscr{F}}_{2,loc}(F)
such that
\partial V=P-\partial S^{+}
. Recall that since
V
is top-dimensional in
F
, it is canonically identified with a locally integrable
function with integer values. In particular,
S^{+}+V
is locally integral. Hence
S=(S^{+}+V)-\iota_{\#}(S^{+}+V)
is a locally integral cycle as claimed.
∎

Corollary A.5
.

Let
S\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{2,loc}(\hat{X})
be symmetric,
S=S\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>X^{+}-\iota_{\#}(S\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>X^{+})
.

is a local flat chain in
F
,
\partial S^{+}\in{\mathscr{F}}_{1,loc}(F)
.

Proof.

Note that since
S
is symmetric, we must have
\|S\|(F)=0
. Hence the claim follows from slicing and Lemma
A.4
.

Lemma A.6
.

There exists a minimizing
S\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{2,loc}(\hat{X})

.

.

Now the proof in

By

Lemma A.7

For every

Proof.

Let

For almost all

Lemma A.8
.

For every
\epsilon>0
there exists

.
After passing to a subsequence, we find an element

.
Hence

,
\partial S^{+}

A.3.
Producing flat half-planes as relative minimizers

Now we have all the necessary ingredients to prove:

Half-Plane Lemma.

Let
X
be a locally compact CAT(0) space of rank 2. Let
\sigma\subset\partial_{T}X
be a round sphere.
Further, let
g
be an axis of an axial isometry
\gamma
and assume
\partial_{\infty}g\subset\sigma
.
Let
\xi^{\pm}\in\sigma
be antipodes disjoint from
\partial_{\infty}g
.
Suppose that there is a sequence of local geodesics
\alpha^{+}_{k}\subset\partial_{T}X
such that

•

\partial\alpha^{+}_{k}=\{\xi^{-}_{k},\xi^{+}_{k}\}
and
\xi^{\pm}_{k}\to\xi^{\pm}
with respect to the Tits metric;

•

the lengths of
\alpha_{k}^{+}
converges to
\pi
as
k\to\infty
.

Then there exists a 2-flat
\tilde{F}\subset P(g)
, and a flat half-plane
\tilde{H}
with boundary
\tilde{h}:=\partial\tilde{H}\subset\tilde{F}
such that the following properties hold.

(1)

g\subset N_{a}(\tilde{F})
if
g\subset N_{a}(F)
for
a>0
;

(2)

|\partial_{\infty}\tilde{h},\partial_{\infty}g|=|\{\xi^{-},\xi^{+}\},\partial_{\infty}g|
;

(3)

\partial_{\infty}\tilde{H}\cap\partial_{\infty}\tilde{F}=\partial_{\infty}\tilde{h}
.

Proof.

Choose a 2-flat
F
with
\partial_{\infty}F=\sigma
and a base point
o\in F
.
As before, we consider the double of
X
along
F
,
\hat{X}=X^{-}\cup_{F}X^{+}

and put
\alpha_{k}:=\alpha_{k}^{+}\cup\iota(\alpha_{k}^{+})
.
By Lemma
A.6
, we find a symmetric minimizer
S_{k}\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{2,loc}(\hat{X})
with
F_{\infty}(S_{k}-C_{o}(\alpha_{k}))=0

and
\Theta_{\infty}(S_{k})=\frac{\operatorname{length}(\alpha_{k})}{2}
. Let
g_{0}
be a complete geodesic in
F
parallel to
g
. By Lemma
A.9
,
there exists a point
p_{k}\in\operatorname{spt}(S_{k})\cap g_{0}
.

Now let us choose numbers
m_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that the points
\gamma^{m_{k}}(p_{k})
stay in a fixed compact set. Put
\gamma_{k}:=\gamma^{m_{k}}
.
After passing to subsequences, we have convergences with respect to pointed Hausdorff topology

\gamma_{k}(C_{p_{k}}(\alpha^{+}_{k}),p_{k})\to(\tilde{H},p_{\infty})\quad\text{ and }\quad\gamma_{k}(F,p_{k})\to(\tilde{F},p_{\infty})

where
\tilde{H}
is a flat half-plane,
\tilde{F}
is a 2-flat, and
\tilde{h}:=\partial\tilde{H}\subset\tilde{F}
.
We will now show that
\tilde{F}
and
\tilde{H}
satisfy the three required properties.

(1) Since
\gamma
preserves
g
, we have
g\subset N_{a}(\tilde{F})
for
a>0
whenever
g\subset N_{a}(F)
.

(2) Let
\partial_{\infty}\tilde{h}=\{\tilde{\xi}^{-},\tilde{\xi}^{+}\}
.
Since
\xi^{\pm}_{k}\to\xi^{\pm}
with respect to the Tits metric, and since
\gamma
preserves
g
, we have

|g(\pm\infty),\gamma_{k}\xi_{k}^{+}|=|g(\pm\infty),\xi_{k}^{+}|
and
|g(\pm\infty),\gamma_{k}\xi_{k}^{-}|=|g(\pm\infty),\xi_{k}^{-}|
.
Hence the statement follows from lower semi-continuity of the Tits metric with respect to the cone topology

[
KL97
, Lemma 2.3.1]
.

(3) Note that if this fails, then we must have
\tilde{H}\subset\tilde{F}
since
\partial\tilde{H}\subset\tilde{F}
.

Denote by
\tilde{F}^{\pm}\subset\tilde{F}
the two half-planes determined by
\partial\tilde{H}
.
Further, denote by
\sigma_{k}^{\pm}\subset\sigma
the two closed arcs determined by
\{\xi_{k}^{-},\xi_{k}^{+}\}
.
Then, as currents, we can write
F=F_{k}^{+}-F_{k}^{-}
where
F_{k}^{\pm}=C_{p_{k}}(\sigma_{k}^{\pm})\in{\mathbf{I}}_{2,loc}(\hat{X})
.
From
[
KL20
, Proposition 4.5]
, we conclude that for every
\epsilon>0

there exists
a_{\epsilon}^{\prime}>0
such that
F_{p_{k},r}(S_{k}-C_{p_{k}}(\alpha_{k}))\leq\frac{\epsilon}{3}
for all
r\geq a_{\epsilon}^{\prime}
.
On the other hand, Lemma
A.7
implies
F_{p_{k},r}(S_{k}-F)\geq\frac{\pi}{3}
for all
r>0
.
Hence,
F_{p_{k},r}(F-C_{p_{k}}(\alpha_{k}))\geq\frac{\pi-\epsilon}{3}
and thus, by symmetry of
C_{p_{k}}(\alpha_{k})
, for
r\geq a_{\epsilon}^{\prime}
,

F_{p_{k},r}(F_{k}^{\pm}-C_{p_{k}}(\alpha^{+}_{k}))\geq\frac{\pi-\epsilon}{6}.

Since
\operatorname{length}(\alpha_{k}^{+})\to\pi
, we have the uniform mass bound

(\|C_{p_{k}}(\alpha^{+}_{k})\|+\|\partial C_{p_{k}}(\alpha^{+}_{k})\|)(B_{r}(p_{k}))\leq\pi r^{2}+2r.

Using
[
KL20
, Theorem 2.3]
, we may assume that
(\gamma_{k})_{\#}(C_{p_{k}}(\alpha^{+}_{k}))
converges to a limit
C_{\infty}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{2,loc}(X^{+})
with respect to local flat topology. By
[
Wen11
, Proposition 2.2]
, the support of
C_{\infty}
is contained in
\tilde{H}
.
Because
(\gamma_{k})_{\#}\partial(C_{p_{k}}(\alpha^{+}_{k}))\to\partial\tilde{H}
weakly, we have
\partial C_{\infty}=\partial\tilde{H}
. But
C_{\infty}

is a top-dimensional locally integral current in
\tilde{H}
, hence
C_{\infty}=\tilde{H}

[
Lan11
, Theorem 7.2]
.

Now suppose for contradiction, that
\tilde{H}=\tilde{F}^{+}
. Set
T_{k}=F_{k}^{+}-C_{p_{k}}(\alpha^{+}_{k})\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{2,loc}(X^{+})
. Because
(\gamma_{k})_{\#}F_{k}^{+}\to\tilde{F}^{+}
locally flat, we infer
(\gamma_{k})_{\#}(T_{k})\to 0
locally flat. Since
\partial T_{k}=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

[
LW11
, Proposition 3.2]
implies
F_{p_{\infty},r}((\gamma_{k})_{\#}(T_{k}))\to 0
for every
r>0
.
Since
\gamma_{k}(p_{k})\to p_{\infty}
, this contradicts (
\star
). Hence
\tilde{H}
cannot be contained in
\tilde{F}
and the proof is complete.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Extreme values and moments of the Riemann zeta function

The value distribution of the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
on the line
\Re(s)=1/2
is of fundamental importance in number theory. It is tightly linked to the distribution of its zeros which, in turn, encode the distribution of prime numbers (see, for instance, Theorem 9.3 in
[
38
]
). A classical result in this direction is Selberg’s Central Limit Theorem
[
34
,
35
]
, which states that at a typical point
s=1/2+it
,
\log\zeta(s)
behaves like a complex Gaussian random variable for large
t
. More specifically, this theorem implies that if
\tau
is uniformly distributed on
[T,2T]
, then

\mathbb{P}\left\{\log|\zeta(1/2+i\tau)|>\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}\log\log T}\cdot y\right\}\sim\int_{y}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-z^{2}/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathrm{d}z,\quad y\in\mathbb{R}

as
T\to\infty
, suggesting an average value of the order of
\sqrt{\log\log T}
on such large intervals. This behaviour was also shown to persist in the large deviations regime
[
1
,
2
]
.

As was first noticed by Bourgade
[
11
]
, logarithmic correlations between
\zeta(1/2+it+ih)
for different values of
h
begin to appear when
|h-h^{\prime}|\leq 1
. While these correlations do not seem affect
macroscopic
quantities (e.g. related to intervals of length
T
) they can play a significant role at the
mesoscopic scale
where intervals of length
\log^{\theta}T
(for
\theta\in(-1,0]
) are being considered.

For instance, using connections with log-correlated processes, Fyodorov-Hiary-Keating
[
19
]
and Fyodorov-Keating
[
20
]
formulated the following striking conjecture about the local maximum of the zeta function:

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
20
]
, Section 2(c)(ii))
.

Let
\theta\in(-1,0]
. Then

\displaystyle\max_{h\leq\log^{\theta}T}\log\big|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i\tau+ih)\big|

\displaystyle\sim(1+\theta)\log\log T-\frac{3}{4}\log\log\log T+\sqrt{\tfrac{|\theta|}{2}\log\log T}\cdot\mathcal{N}(0,1)+\mathcal{M},

where
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
a standard Gaussian random variable and
\mathcal{M}
a random variable that is
O(1)
in probability, independent of
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
and has a tail of the form
Cye^{-2y}
.

Analogous conjectures were formulated for the characteristic polynomial of random matrices of the
cue
ensemble. In that case, Fyodorov–Keating give the precise distribution for
\mathcal{M}
, which was recognized in
[
37
]
to be that of a sum of two independent Gumbel random variables.

Even at
\theta=0
, the logarithmic correlations appear through the coefficient
3/4
in the second–order term. Indeed, it can be shown (e.g. using the functional equation for the zeta function, as in
[
17
]
) that the analysis of the maximum of
\zeta(1/2+it+ih)
over
|h|\leq\log^{\theta}T
can be be reduced to a discrete set of
(\log T)^{(1+\theta)}
points, which are approximately Gaussian by Selberg’s theorem. If we assume independence (which would correspond to Derrida’s
Random Energy Model

[
15
]
), a straightforward calculation gives a subleading coefficient of
1/4
(see, e.g.,
[
21
]
) meaning a larger maximum, as one should expect by Slepian’s lemma.

This conjecture has seen significant progress since its formulation, particularly in the
\theta=0
case. Following initial progress by Najnudel
[
27
]
and Arguin, Bourgade, Belius, Radziwiłł and Soundararajan
[
4
]
, sharp upper and lower bounds were established by Arguin, Bourgade and Radziwiłł in a series of papers
[
6
,
7
]
using methods from the theory of branching random walks. On the random matrix theoretic side, the leading order was proved in
[
3
]
, and the subleading order by Paquette and Zeitouni
[
29
]
and Chhaibi, Madaule and Najnudel
[
14
]
independently. This culminated in a recent work of Paquette and Zeitouni
[
30
]
, in which they established distributional convergence of the maximum to a sum of a Gumbel random variable and a “derivative martingale”.

As for
\theta<0
, the leading order for the maximum was established by one of the authors, Ouimet and Radziwiłł in
[
9
]
. In this paper, we improve their upper bound to
O(1)
precision, obtaining a tail of
y\exp\big(-2y-y^{2}/((1+\theta)\log\log T)\big)
. More specifically, let
\tau
be uniformly distributed in
[T,2T]
, and

(1.1)

S_{k}(h)=\sum_{\log p\leq e^{k}}\Re\left(\frac{1}{p^{1/2+i(\tau+h)}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{p^{1+2i(\tau+h)}}\right),\quad k\leq t

(where the sum is taken over primes
p
). This is essentially a second order Taylor approximation to
\log|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(\tau+h))|
when
k\approx\log\log T
. Then we establish the following result.

Theorem 1.2

(Upper bound for the local maximum)
.

Let
C>0
be an absolute constant and
\theta\in(-1,0]
.
Then uniformly in
1\leq y<C{(\log T)^{(1+\theta)}}/{\log\log((\log T)^{(1+\theta)})}
and
T\geq 3
, we have

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\max_{|h|\leq\log^{\theta}T}\big|\zeta\big(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(\tau+h)\big)\big|&>e^{S_{t_{\theta}}(0)+y}\frac{(\log T)^{(1+\theta)}}{\big(\log\log((\log T)^{(1+\theta)}\big)^{3/4}}\bigg\}\\
&\leq C^{\prime}ye^{-2y-y^{2}/((1+\theta)\log\log T)}\end{split}

for some absolute constant
C^{\prime}>0
, where
t_{\theta}:=|\theta|\log\log T
.

Remark 1.3
.

It follows by a moment computation (e.g., the proof of Selberg’s theorem in
[
24
]
) that
S_{t_{\theta}}(0)/\sqrt{(|\theta|/2)\log\log T}
is distributed like a standard normal for large
T
. Theorem
1.2
thus settles a strong form of conjecture
1.1
while allowing us to make sense of it in a precise way: to estimate the maximum in given interval on the critical line of size
\log^{\theta}T
, one computes
e^{S_{t_{\theta}}}
at the midpoint and then multiplies it by

\frac{(\log T)^{(1+\theta)}}{\big(\log\log((\log T)^{(1+\theta)}\big)^{3/4}}.

The maximum is then within
O(1)
of this quantity.

Remark 1.4
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
also gives the claim for
\theta
depending on
T
, provided that
(1+\theta)>(\log\log T)^{-1}
.

The conjecture for
\theta<0
has yet to be studied explicitly in the case of random matrices. Using a classical expansion due to Diaconis and Evans
[
16
]
, we expect from our conclusions in Theorem
1.2
that

\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{|h|\leq\log^{\theta}T}\big|\mathrm{P}_{N}(e^{ih})\big|>e^{X_{\theta}+y}\frac{N^{(1+\theta)}}{\log\log(N^{(1+\theta)})}\right\}\leq Cye^{-2y-y^{2}/((1+\theta)\log N)}.

where
\mathrm{P}_{N}(z)
is the characteristic polynomial of an
N\times N
Haar-distributed unitary matrix and

X_{\theta}:=-\sum_{j=1}^{N^{|\theta|}}\frac{\text{Tr}(U_{n}^{j})}{j}.

Here, we used the correspondence
N\leftrightarrow\log T
which is standard in this context (see
[
3
]
for more details).

The
moments
of the zeta function on short intervals

(1.3)

\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}^{(\theta)}:=\int_{|h|\leq\log^{\theta}T}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(\tau+h))|^{\beta}\mathrm{d}h

are closely related to the maximum. Upper and lower bounds for the leading order of
\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}^{(\theta)}
were established in
[
9
]
for
\theta>-1
, with the former holding unconditionally for all
\beta>0
, and the latter assuming the Riemann hypothesis when
\theta\leq 3
. Further, they showed that a
freezing transition
(which had been conjectured in
[
20
]
) occurs at
\beta=2\sqrt{1+\min{(\theta,0)}}
. These bounds were later improved by one of the authors and Bailey
[
1
]
for
\theta\in[0,3)
and
\beta>2\sqrt{1+\theta}
, using the measure of high points of
\log|\zeta|
. In the specific case
\theta=0,\beta=2
, a sharp upper bound was obtained by Harper
[
22
]
, who showed that

\int_{|h|\leq 1}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(\tau+h))|^{2}\mathrm{d}h\leq A\frac{\log T}{\sqrt{\log\log T}}

for all
t\in[T,2T]
, except possibly on a subset of Lebesgue measure bounded by a constant times
A^{-1}\min\{\log A,\sqrt{\log\log T}\}
.

In
[
20
]
(Section 4(b)), Fyodorov and Keating also made conjectures regarding moments when
\theta<0
, arguing that asymptotically,
\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}^{(\theta)}
splits into a product of two uncorrelated random variables. The first is conjectured to be a log-normal random variable with parameters
(0,2|\theta|\log\log T)
, while the moments of the second were computed explicitly, revealing a leading order of
(1+\theta)\log\log T
.

Towards this conjecture, our main result is the following precise upper bound on moments in mesoscopic intervals. In it, both the previously mentioned log-normal factor, as well as a square root correction term like in Harper’s result appear (the latter is recovered by taking
\theta=0
).

Theorem 1.5

(Upper bound for the second moment)
.

Let
A>1
be a constant and
\theta\in(-1,0]
. Define

\mathcal{Z}^{(\theta)}_{2}(\tau):=\frac{1}{\log^{\theta}T}\int_{|h|\leq\log^{\theta}T}\big|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(\tau+h))\big|^{2}\mathrm{d}h.

Then there exist absolute constants
C,C^{\prime}>0
such that if
\theta\neq 0
,

\mathbb{P}\left\{\mathcal{Z}^{(\theta)}_{2}(\tau)\geq C\cdot A\frac{\log^{(1+\theta)}T}{\sqrt{(1+\theta)\log\log T}}\cdot e^{2u}\,\bigg|\,S_{t_{\theta}}(0)=u\right\}\leq C^{\prime}\frac{e^{-2u}}{A}

and if
\theta=0
,

\mathbb{P}\left\{\mathcal{Z}^{(0)}_{2}(\tau)\geq C\cdot A\frac{\log T}{\sqrt{\log\log T}}\right\}\leq C^{\prime}\frac{1}{A}.

The proof takes a different approach from
[
22
]
. We make use of the typical measure of level sets of
\log|\zeta|
, similarly to the argument in section 4 of
[
1
]
except that theirs could not be made rigorous. To achieve that, we make use of a novel “partial barrier” for Dirichlet sums, which we explain in section
1.3
.

1.2.
Related results.

Comparing the subleading order for the maximum in
[
1
]
(for
\theta>0
) and
[
6
]
(
\theta=0
), we find a discontinuity as
\theta\downarrow 0
representing a transition from log-correlated to IID statistics. By analyzing a model of the zeta function, Dubach, Hartung and one of the authors
[
8
]
showed that this discontinuity can be resolved by approaching zero at an appropriate rate, revealing a “hybrid” regime. Upper and lower bounds for the maximum of the same model were recently obtained by Chang
[
13
]
in this regime. All of these works can also be related to the vast literature on extrema of log-correlated fields (e.g.
[
33
,
12
,
18
,
31
,
23
,
39
,
28
,
32
]
), for which an in-depth discussion can be found in a recent survey of Bailey and Keating
[
10
]
.

Notation.
Throughout our arguments,
\tau
will always denote a random variable that is uniformly distributed in
[T,2T]
. For simplicity, we will use the notation
\zeta_{\tau}(h):=\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(\tau+h))
. As was done above, we will let

t=\log\log T\text{ and }t_{\theta}=t|\theta|.

We will use standard asymptotic notation, writing
f(T)=o(g(T))
to mean that
|f(T)/g(T)|\to_{T\to\infty}0
and
f(T)=O(g(T))
or
f(T)\ll g(T)
to mean that

\limsup_{T\to\infty}|f(T)/g(T)|
is bounded. A subscripted parameter next to

or
\ll
means that the implicit constant is allowed to depend on said parameter.
Lastly, we will use standard number theoretic notation for the following arithmetical functions:
\omega(n)
is the number distinct primes dividing
n
,
\Omega(n)
the number of such primes counted with multiplicity, and
\mu
is the Möbius function, which equals
-1
(resp.
1
) on squarefree integers with an odd (resp. even) number of prime factors, and zero otherwise.

1.3.
Branching random walk model and proof outlines

Our arguments are all centered around a random walk model for the zeta function, which we now introduce. Note that this is the same model as in
[
9
,
4
]
. For any
k\leq t
, consider the process

(S_{k}(h),\quad|h|\leq\log^{\theta}T)

where
S_{k}
is as defined previously. Then by a standard estimate (see, e.g. lemma 3.4 in
[
9
]
), the covariance of this process is equal to

\mathbb{E}\left\{S_{k}(h)S_{k}(h^{\prime})\right\}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\log p\leq e^{k}}\frac{\cos(|h-h^{\prime}|\log p)}{p}+O\left(\frac{\exp(e^{k})}{T}\right),

and analyzing the cosine factor, we see that these sums are highly correlated if
k\leq\exp(|h-h^{\prime}|^{-1})
and decorrelate quickly for larger
k
. A simple application of Mertens’ estimate reveals that this sum is approximately proportional to
\log|h-h^{\prime}|^{-1}
, revealing the aforementioned logarithmic correlation structure.

Now recall that
S_{k}(h)
approximates
\log|\zeta_{\tau}(h)|:=\log|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(\tau+h))|
when
k\approx t
. Viewing each
S_{t}(h)
as a random walk, we can split it the walk increments
(S_{k}-S_{k-1})(h)
that each have variance
1/2+o(1)
, and note that any two walks
S_{t}(h),S_{t}(h^{\prime})
are essentially perfectly correlated up to
k\approx\log|h-h^{\prime}|^{-1}
and branch out afterwards. This naturally leads to the branching random walk model illustrated in figure
1
, where a full binary tree of height
t
is considered, with edges corresponding to the increments
(S_{k}-S_{k-1})(h)
and leaves corresponding to points
h\in[-1,1]
.

Proof for the maximum.
On the interval
|h|\leq\log^{\theta}T
, all of the
h

k\approx t_{\theta}
(see lemma
3.2
). The maximum of
\log|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(\tau+h))|
on this interval should therefore roughly equal the sum of two random variables: the first is
S_{t_{\theta}}(0)
(which is roughly Gaussian), and the second comes from the maximum of a branching random walk starting at time
t_{\theta}
, which we denote by
(\mathscr{S}_{k}(h))_{|h|\leq\log^{\theta}T}

\mathscr{S}_{k}:={S}_{t_{\theta}+k}-S_{t_{\theta}}.

On figure
1

which explains the leading order in Theorem
1.2

To prove this theorem, our strategy will therefore be to subtract

4

\theta<0

\mathscr{S}_{k}
is a longer Dirichlet polynomial than

Proof for the second moment.

By Theorem

Theorem 1.6

(Large deviations with constraints on a prime interval)

\theta\in(-1,0]

V\sim\alpha t(1+\theta)
where
0<\alpha<2
,
\mathfrak{s}=2\cdot 10^{6}/\alpha^{2}(2-\alpha)^{2}
, and
t_{1}=t(1+\theta)-\mathfrak{s}\log\big(t(1+\theta)\big)
. For any
A\geq 1
, let
U_{A}(k)
be the barrier

U_{A}(k)=A+k\bigg(1-\frac{3}{4}\frac{\log(t(1+\theta))}{t(1+\theta)}\bigg)+10^{3}\log(t(1+\theta)-k),\quad k\leq t_{1}.

Then for any
h\in[-\log^{\theta}T,\log^{\theta}T]

(1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\big\{\log|\zeta_{\tau}e^{-S_{t_{\theta}}}(h)|>V,\mathscr{S}_{k}(h)&\leq U_{A}(k)\,\forall k\leq t_{1}\big\}\\
&\ll\frac{A\left(U_{A}(t_{1})-V(t_{1}/t(1+\theta))\right)}{t_{1}}\frac{e^{-V^{2}/t(1+\theta)}}{\sqrt{t(1+\theta)}},\end{split}

where the implicit constant can be taken uniform in

Lastly, to bound
\mathcal{Z}_{2}^{(\theta)}
when
\theta<0

Theorem 1.7

(Correlation of primes smaller than
\exp(e^{|\theta|t}
))
.

Let
\theta\in(-1,0]
. Then there exist absolute constants
c,C>0
such that

\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{|h|\leq\log^{\theta}T}|S_{t_{\theta}}(h)-S_{t_{\theta}}(0)|>x\right\}\leq Ce^{-x^{2}/c}.

The rest of the argument is then identical to the one used for
\mathcal{Z}^{(0)}_{2}
, considering the shifted walk
\mathscr{S}_{k}
instead of
S_{k}
.

Proof of Theorem

We first prove the claim for
\theta=0
, beginning with the introduction of some notation. In what follows, for any
k\geq 0
,

m(k)=k\bigg(1-\frac{3}{4}\frac{\log t}{t}\bigg)

is the deterministic centering of
S_{k}(h)
, which we recall can be thought of as a Gaussian random walk for fixed
h
. For simplicity, we let
\mathcal{Z}_{2}=\mathcal{Z}_{2}^{(0)}
. For any
A
, consider the following set of “good”
h

G_{A}=\{h\leq 1:S_{k}(h)\leq U_{A}(k)\text{ for all }k\leq t_{1}\}.

where
t_{\ell}=t-\mathfrak{s}\log_{\ell}t
for any
\ell\geq 1
, and
\mathfrak{s},U_{A}(k)
are as in the statement of Theorem
1.6
. We’re interested in the normalized measure of high points of the zeta function on this good set, namely

\mathcal{S}(V):=\frac{1}{2}\text{meas}\{|h|\leq 1:\log|\zeta_{\tau}(h)|>V,h\in G_{A}\},

which we note is a random variable taking values in
[0,1]
.

The proof follows the strategy outlined in
[
1
]
(proof of Corollary 1.4), with the main difference being that we use one–point large deviation estimates for zeta in the set
G_{A}
(given by Theorem
1.6
) to get an additional factor of
{(t(1+\theta))}^{-1/2}
. We begin by integrating by parts, which yields

\mathcal{Z}_{2}=-e^{2V}\mathcal{S}(V)\bigg|^{+\infty}_{-\infty}+2\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{2V}\mathcal{S}(V)\mathrm{d}V.

The boundary term at
-\infty
is equal to zero, since
\mathcal{S}(V)\leq 1
by definition. To deal with the limit at
+\infty
, we place ourselves on the following event

E_{A}:=\{\forall h\in[-1,1],\,\log|\zeta_{\tau}(h)|>V\text{ and }h\in G_{A}\},

noting that
\mathbb{P}\left\{E_{A}^{c}\right\}
is bounded by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{|h|\leq 1}\log|\zeta_{\tau}(h)|>m(t)+A\right\}+\mathbb{P}\left\{S_{k}(h)>U_{A}(k)\text{ for some $k\leq t_{1},|h|\leq 1$}\right\}

and that both of these terms are
\ll Ae^{-2A}
by Theorem
1.2
and
the proof of proposition
4.3
respectively. This leaves us with the task of bounding

\int_{-\infty}^{m(t)+A}e^{2V}\mathcal{S}(V)\mathrm{d}V.

Using the fact that
\mathcal{S}(V)\leq 1
once again, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{m(t)+A}e^{2V}\mathcal{S}(V)\mathrm{d}V

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{2V}\mathrm{d}V+\int_{0}^{t/4}e^{2V}\mathrm{d}V+\int_{t/4}^{m(t)+A}e^{2V}\mathcal{S}(V)\mathrm{d}V

\displaystyle\leq 1+e^{t/2}+\int_{t/4}^{m(t)+A}e^{2V}\mathcal{S}(V)\mathrm{d}V,

and what’s left to show is that the rightmost integral is of the desired order. This requires us to control the values of
\mathcal{S}(V)
on that range. To that end, we partition said range into sub-intervals of length of the order of
\sqrt{t}
, letting
(V_{j},1\leq j\leq J)
be an enumeration of the elements of
\left[\frac{t}{4},m(t)+A\right]\cap(\sqrt{t}\mathbb{Z})
and defining
V_{0}=V_{1}-\sqrt{t}
and
V_{J+1}=V_{J}+\sqrt{t}
. We then partition the integral into

I_{j}=\int_{V_{j}}^{V_{j+1}}e^{2V}\mathcal{S}(V)\mathrm{d}V

for each
0\leq j\leq J
, and let
E_{j}
be the event that the integral
I_{j}
does not exceed a constant
a_{j}
times its expectation, namely

E_{j}:=\left\{I_{j}\leq a_{j}\mathbb{E}\left\{I_{j}\right\}\right\}

where

a_{j}:=A\cdot\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\big(1+{\big(V_{j}-t\big)^{2}}/{t}\big)&\mbox{if }V_{j}>t\\
\big(1+{\big(V_{j+1}-t\big)^{2}}/{t}\big)&\mbox{if }V_{j}\leq t\mbox{ and }V_{j+1}\leq t\\
1&\mbox{if }V_{j}\leq t\mbox{ and }V_{j+1}>t\\
\end{array}\right..

This choice of
a_{j}
ensures that on
[V_{j},V_{j+1}]
,

a_{j}\leq A\bigg(1+\frac{(V-t)^{2}}{t}\bigg)

while also making the
a_{j}^{-1}
summable. Indeed, by Markov’s inequality and a union bound, the good event

G=E\cap\bigg(\bigcap_{j}E_{j}\bigg)

is such that

\mathbb{P}\left\{G^{c}\right\}\ll\sum_{j}a_{j}^{-1}+Ae^{-2A}\ll 1/A.

We can therefore place ourselves on this event, where we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{2}\ll\int_{t/4}^{m(t)+A}e^{2V}\mathcal{S}(V)\mathrm{d}V

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}a_{j}\int_{V_{j}}^{V_{j+1}}e^{2V}\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathcal{S}(V)\right\}\mathrm{d}V

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t/4}^{m(t)+A}A\bigg(1+\frac{(V-t)^{2}}{t}\bigg)e^{2V}\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathcal{S}(V)\right\}\mathrm{d}V.

By Fubini’s theorem and Theorem
1.6
,

\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathcal{S}(V)\right\}\ll\frac{A\left(U_{A}(t_{1})-V(t_{1}/t)\right)}{t_{1}}\frac{e^{-V^{2}/t}}{\sqrt{t}}

and we therefore have that

\displaystyle\ll A^{2}e^{t}\int_{t/4}^{m(t)+A}\bigg(1+\frac{(V-t)^{2}}{t}\bigg)\frac{\left(U_{A}(t_{1})-V(t_{1}/t)\right)}{t_{1}}\frac{e^{-(V-t)^{2}/t}}{\sqrt{t}}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\ll A^{2}\frac{e^{t}}{\sqrt{t}}\int_{t/4}^{m(t)+A}\bigg(1+\frac{(V-t)^{2}}{t}\bigg)\bigg(1-\frac{(V-t)}{\sqrt{t}}\bigg)\frac{e^{-(V-t)^{2}/t}}{\sqrt{t}}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\ll A^{2}\frac{e^{t}}{\sqrt{t}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+u^{2})(1-u)e^{-u^{2}}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\ll_{A}\frac{e^{t}}{\sqrt{t}}.

To prove the claim for
\theta<0
, we first note that the proof above applies
mutatis mutandis
to give

\frac{1}{2e^{t\theta}}\int_{|h|\leq e^{\theta t}}|\zeta_{\tau}e^{-S_{t_{\theta}}}(h)|^{2}dh\ll_{A}\frac{e^{t(1+\theta)}}{\sqrt{t(1+\theta)}}

with probability
1-O(e^{-2u}/A)
.

Now using proposition
1.7
we further place ourselves on an event where

\max_{|h|\leq\log^{\theta}T}|S_{t_{\theta}}(h)-S_{t_{\theta}}(0)|\leq A,

which occurs with probability
1-O(e^{-A^{2}/C})
by Theorem
1.7
. This yields

\displaystyle e^{-2S_{t_{\theta}}(0)}\mathcal{Z}_{2}^{(\theta)}\ll_{A}\exp\big({\max_{h}|S_{t_{\theta}}(h)-S_{t_{\theta}}(0)|}\big)\frac{e^{t(1+\theta)}}{\sqrt{t(1+\theta)}},

and the claim follows.

3.
Correlation of primes up to
\exp(e^{t|\theta|})

This section proves that the contribution to maxima of the zeta function in
|h|\leq e^{t\theta}
coming from primes up to
\exp(e^{t|\theta|})
is roughly the same for all
h
’s. More specifically, we prove the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1

(Correlation of primes up to
\exp(e^{t|\theta|})
)
.

Let
\theta\in(-1,0]
and
0<x<4^{t_{\theta}}
be arbitrary. Then there exists an absolute constant
c>0
such that

\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{|h|\leq e^{t\theta}}|S_{t_{\theta}}(h)-S_{t_{\theta}}(0)|>x\right\}\ll e^{-x^{2}/c}.

The claim is trivial for
\theta=0
. Otherwise, we use a chaining argument as in
[
5
]
, viewing
(S_{t_{\theta}}(h),-\log^{\theta}T<h<\log^{\theta}T)
as a stochastic process indexed by
h
.

For any positive integer
k
, let
\mathcal{H}_{k}
be the set of
\tfrac{1}{2^{k}}\mathbb{Z}
dyadic rationals, making
(\mathcal{H}_{k})_{k\geq 1}
a nested sequence of sets whose respective intersections with
[0,1)
each contain
2^{k}
points.

Let
k
be the largest integer for which
[-\log^{\theta}T,\log^{\theta}T]\subseteq[-2^{-k-1},2^{-k-1}]:=A_{k}
. Now consider a dyadic sequence
(h_{i})_{i\geq 0}
such that
h_{0}=0,h_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{k+i}\cap A_{k}
and
\lim_{i\to\infty}h_{i}=h
, so that
|h_{i+1}-h_{i}|\in\{0,2^{-k-i-1}\}
for all
i
. Noting that
h\to S_{t_{\theta}}(h)
is almost surely continuous, we have

S_{t_{\theta}}(h)-S_{t_{\theta}}(0)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\big(S_{t_{\theta}}(h_{i+1})-S_{t_{\theta}}(h_{i})\big)

and the right-hand side converges almost surely since the partial sum up to
l
equals
S_{t_{\theta}}(h_{l+1})-S_{t_{\theta}}(0)
, which tends to
S_{t_{\theta}}(h)-S_{t_{\theta}}(0)
by (almost sure) continuity of
h\mapsto S_{t_{\theta}}(h)
.

We therefore get that
\big\{\max_{h\in A_{k}}S_{t_{\theta}}(h)-S_{t_{\theta}}(0)\geq x\big\}
is included in

\displaystyle\bigcup_{i\geq 0}\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}h_{1}\in\mathcal{H}_{k+i}\cap A_{k}\\
h_{2}=h_{1}\pm 2^{-k-i-1}\end{subarray}}\bigg\{S_{t_{\theta}}(h_{2})-S_{t_{\theta}}(h_{1})\geq\frac{x}{2(i+1)^{2}}\bigg\},

(ignoring
h_{1}=h_{2}
) and in turn, using
|\mathcal{H}_{k+i}\cap A_{k}|\leq c2^{i}
for a constant
c>0
that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\max_{|h|\leq e^{t\theta}}

\displaystyle|S_{t_{\theta}}(h)-S_{t_{\theta}}(0)|>x\bigg\}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{i\geq 0}2^{i}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}h_{1}\in\mathcal{H}_{k+i}\cap A_{k}\\
h_{2}=h_{1}\pm 2^{-k-i-1}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left\{S_{t_{\theta}}(h_{2})-S_{t_{\theta}}(h_{2})>\frac{x}{2(i+1)^{2}}\right\}.

To estimate the supremum, we use the following Gaussian tail estimate.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists an absolute constant
c>0
such that for any
h,h^{\prime}\in[-e^{t\theta},e^{t\theta}],h\neq h^{\prime}
and
0<x<4^{t_{\theta}}
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left\{S_{t_{\theta}}(h)-S_{t_{\theta}}(h^{\prime})>x\right\}\ll\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{c\cdot\big(e^{t_{\theta}}|h-h^{\prime}|\big)^{2}}\right).

Applying this inequality to each term in
3.1
, we get a bound of

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{i\geq 0}2^{i}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}h_{1}\in\mathcal{H}_{k+i}\cap A_{k}\\
h_{2}=h_{1}\pm 2^{-k-i-1}\end{subarray}}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{c\cdot\big(e^{t_{\theta}}|h_{1}-h_{2}|2(i+1)^{2}\big)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{i\geq 0}2^{i}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{c\cdot(i+1)^{4}}\frac{4^{k+i}}{e^{2t_{\theta}}}\right)

\displaystyle\ll\exp(-x^{2}/c).

where the last inequality uses the fact that
k\asymp t\theta
. It remains to prove lemma
3.2
.

3.1.
Proof of lemma
3.2

We begin by introducing some notation. Let
(Z_{p},\text{$p$ prime})
be a sequence of independent and identically distributed random variables that are uniformly distributed on
|z|=1
. For any integer
n
with prime factorization
\prod_{i}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
(where the
p_{i}
are distinct for different
i
), let

Z_{n}:=\prod_{i=1}Z_{p_{i}}^{\alpha_{i}},

noting that
\mathbb{E}\left\{Z_{n}\overline{Z_{m}}\right\}=\mathbf{1}_{[n=m]}
.

Consider the random variables

X_{p}(h)=\text{Re}\left(Z_{p}p^{-1/2-ih}+\tfrac{1}{2}Z_{p}^{2}p^{-1-2ih}\right),\text{ $p$ prime, $h\in[-2,2]$.}

The sums
\sum_{p\leq t_{\theta}}X_{p}(h)-X_{p}(h^{\prime})
will serve as a random model for
S_{t_{\theta}}(h)-S_{t_{\theta}}(h)
; we will show that the former’s moments approximate the latter’s, and that they are roughly Gaussian. This is achieved using the following (classical) mean-value theorem estimate for Dirichlet polynomials

Lemma 3.3

(Mean–value theorem for Dirichlet polynomials)
.

Let
T>0
and
\tau
be uniformly distributed in
[T,2T]
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\Big|\sum_{n\leq N}a(n)n^{-i\tau}\Big|^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\bigg(1+O\Big(\frac{N}{T}\Big)\bigg)\sum_{n\leq N}|a(n)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigg(1+O\Big(\frac{N}{T}\Big)\bigg)\mathbb{E}\left\{\Big|\sum_{n\leq N}a(n)Z_{n}\Big|^{2}\right\}.

Proof.

This follows from corollary 3 in
[
25
]
.
∎

Using this with
S_{t_{\theta}}(h)-S_{t_{\theta}}(h^{\prime})
, we have that for any
q>0
satisfying
2q\leq e^{t(1+\theta)}
and
\lambda<|h-h^{\prime}|^{-1}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{|S_{t_{\theta}}(h)-S_{t_{\theta}}(h^{\prime})|^{2q}\right\}\ll\mathbb{E}\left\{\Big(\sum_{\log p\leq e^{t_{\theta}}}X_{p}(h)-X_{p}(h^{\prime})\Big)^{2q}\right\}.

We can then bound the right hand side using a similar argument to that of lemma 16 in
[
6
]
. Namely, let

\mathcal{S}_{1}=\sum_{\log p\leq e^{t|\theta|}}\text{Re}\bigg(Z_{p}\cdot\frac{\Delta(p)}{p^{1/2}}\bigg),\quad\mathcal{S}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\log p\leq e^{t|\theta|}}\text{Re}\bigg(Z_{p}^{2}\cdot\frac{\Delta(p)}{p}\bigg),

where
\Delta(p):=|p^{-ih}-p^{-ih^{\prime}}|
, and similarly

\mathcal{G}_{1}=\sum_{\log p\leq e^{t|\theta|}}\bigg(Y_{p}\cdot\frac{\Delta(p)}{p^{1/2}}\bigg),\quad\mathcal{G}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\log p\leq e^{t|\theta|}}\bigg(Y_{p}^{2}\cdot\frac{\Delta(p)}{p}\bigg),

where the
(Y_{p})_{p\text{ prime}}
are centered, independent Gaussian random variables of variance
1/2
. Noting that
\mathbb{E}\left\{(\text{Re}Z)^{2m}\right\}\leq\mathbb{E}\left\{Y^{2m}\right\}
for any integer
m
, the binomial theorem gives

\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{2q}\right\}\leq\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathcal{G}_{i}^{2q}\right\}\text{ for $i\in\{1,2\}$}

and we can therefore write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\Big(\sum_{\log p\leq e^{t_{\theta}}}X_{p}(h)-X_{p}(h^{\prime})\Big)^{2q}\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left\{\big(\mathcal{S}_{1}+\mathcal{S}_{2}\big)^{2q}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathcal{S}_{1}^{2q}\right\}^{1/2q}+\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathcal{S}_{2}^{2q}\right\}^{1/2q}\bigg)^{2q}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathcal{G}_{1}^{2q}\right\}^{1/2q}+\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathcal{G}_{2}^{2q}\right\}^{1/2q}\bigg)^{2q}.

Applying the binomial theorem and using the estimate
\cos(x)=1+O(x^{2})
shows that the right hand side is no greater than

\leq\frac{(2q)!}{2^{q}q!}\left(c|h-h^{\prime}|^{2}\sum_{\log p\leq e^{t|\theta|}}\frac{(\log p)^{2}}{p}\right)^{q}

for some constant
c>0
, which by the quantitative prime number theorem (see, e.g., theorem 6.9 in
[
26
]
) is

\ll\frac{(2q)!}{2^{q}q!}\left(c\cdot e^{t|\theta|}|h-h^{\prime}|\right)^{2q}.

The lemma then follows by Chebyshev’s inequality on
\mathbb{P}\left\{S_{t_{\theta}}(h)-S_{t_{\theta}}(h^{\prime})\right\}
with the choice
2q=\lceil x^{2}/(ce^{t|\theta||h-h^{\prime}|})\rceil
, together with Stirling’s formula.

4.1.
Notation and initial reductions

We begin by introducing notation pertaining to the recursive scheme.

The lemma then follows by proceeding as in the previous section starting from equation (

6.
Constrained large deviation estimates

We now focus on Theorem

As in section

We begin by redefining the good sets

as follows. For

(noting the dependence on

for

in this section, and for simplicity write

Consider the events

\displaystyle A_{0}=B_{0}=C_{0}=D_{0}=[T,2T]\text{ (the sample space of $\tau$)}

and for

\displaystyle A_{\ell}=A_{\ell-1}\cap\{|\tilde{\mathscr{S}_{t_{\ell}}}-\tilde{\mathscr{S}}_{t_{\ell-1}}<\mathcal{A}(t_{\ell}-t_{\ell-1})\}

\displaystyle C_{\ell}=C_{\ell-1}\cap\{\mathscr{S}_{t_{\ell}}>\kappa t_{\ell}-\mathcal{C}\log_{\ell}t\}

\displaystyle D_{\ell}=D_{\ell-1}\cap\{|\zeta_{\tau}e^{-S_{t_{\ell}+t_{\theta}}}|\leq c_{\ell}|\zeta_{\tau}\mathcal{M}_{1}\cdots\mathcal{M}_{\ell}|+e^{-\mathcal{D}(t_{\ell}-t_{\ell-1})}\}

,

\mathcal{B}=\frac{3\cdot 10^{6}}{2\alpha(2-\alpha)^{2}}+\frac{1}{4\alpha},\text{ and }\mathcal{C}=\frac{3\cdot 10^{6}}{2\alpha^{2}(2-\alpha)}+\frac{1}{4(2-\alpha)}.

The choice of these constants is explained in section 2 of
[
1
]
, and ensures that the following inequalities (needed for technical reasons) hold

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}>\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}+\frac{\alpha\mathcal{C}}{2\mathfrak{s}}+2,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\alpha}+\frac{10^{6}}{\alpha(2-\alpha)^{2}}<\mathcal{B}<\frac{2\cdot 10^{6}}{\alpha(2-\alpha)^{2}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(2-\alpha)}(1+(2-\alpha)^{2}\mathfrak{s})<\mathcal{C}<(2-\alpha)\mathfrak{s}.

As one would come to expect, we let
G_{\ell}=A_{\ell}\cap B_{\ell}\cap C_{\ell}\cap D_{\ell}
for
\ell\geq 0
.

Let

and

Similarly to the proof of Theorem

, we decompose

over the

, where

is as before (see equation (

More precisely, we prove the following three propositions.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\theta\in(-1,0]

,

.

Let
\theta\in(-1,0]

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

It suffices to prove the claim for

, and the latter follows from the decomposition

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{H^{\theta}\cap G_{A}\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left\{H^{\theta}\cap G_{A}\cap G_{1}^{c}\right\}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\mathcal{L}-1}\mathbb{P}\left\{H^{\theta}\cap G_{A}\cap G_{\ell}\setminus G_{\ell+1}\right\}+\mathbb{P}\left\{H^{\theta}\cap G_{A}\cap G_{\mathcal{L}}\right\}

followed by an application of the propositions above.
∎

A few heuristics are in order prior to proving these results. As in section 4, we approximate
\log|\zeta_{\tau}e^{-S_{t_{\theta}}}|
by
\mathscr{S}_{t(1+\theta)}
, which can intuitively be thought of as a Gaussian random walk of length
t(1+\theta)
(as suggested by Selberg’s central limit theorem and its generalizations to the large deviations regime, e.g.
[
1
]
). The probability that such a walk’s endpoint is in
[V,V+1]
should roughly equal
e^{-V^{2}/t(1+\theta)}/\sqrt{t(1+\theta)}
.

The additional factor multiplying the Gaussian term comes from the event
G_{A}
, which restricts the walk to lie under a barrier (around
A>0
) up to time
t_{1}
and ends at
u
. Viewing
\mathscr{S}_{t_{1}}
as Gaussian, the probability of this happening is

\ll\frac{A\big(U_{A}(t_{1})-V\big(\tfrac{t_{1}}{t(1+\theta)}\big)\big)}{t_{1}^{3/2}}e^{-u^{2}/t_{1}}

by the ballot theorem in
[
6
]
. Being supported on different primes,
\mathscr{S}_{t_{1}}
and
\mathscr{S}_{t(1+\theta)}-\mathscr{S}_{t_{1}}
should behave independently, and if viewed as Gaussian increments, the probability of
H^{\theta}\cap G_{A}
should therefore be

\ll\frac{A\big(U_{A}(t_{1})-V\big(\tfrac{t_{1}}{t(1+\theta)}\big)\big)}{t_{1}}\int_{u\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{e^{-u^{2}/t_{1}}}{\sqrt{t_{1}}}\frac{e^{-(V-u)^{2}/(t(1+\theta)-t_{1})}}{\sqrt{t(1+\theta)-t_{1}}}\mathrm{d}u.

By observation, the integral above equals
e^{-V^{2}/t(1+\theta)}/\sqrt{t(1+\theta)}
and we recover the right hand side in Theorem
1.6
. The remainder of this section is devoted to making this argument rigorous.

6.1.
Proof of proposition
6.1

A union bound gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{H\cap G_{1}^{c}\cap G_{A}\right\}

\displaystyle\ll\mathbb{P}\left\{A_{1}^{c}\right\}+\mathbb{P}\left\{A_{1}\cap D_{1}^{c}\right\}+\mathbb{P}\left\{B_{1}^{c}\cap G_{A}\right\}+\mathbb{P}\left\{H\cap C_{1}^{c}\cap A_{1}\cap D_{1}\cap G_{A}\right\}.

Lemma 6.4

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big\{\mathscr{S}_{t_{1}}

Lastly, we estimate

\leq\sum_{\mathcal{A}(t_{1}-t_{0})<u<\kappa t_{1}-\mathcal{C}\log t_{1}}\mathbb{P}\left\{\big\{\mathscr{S}_{t_{1}}\in(u,u+1],|\zeta_{\tau}e^{-S_{t_{1}+t_{\theta}}}|>e^{V-u-1}\big\}\cap D_{1}\cap G_{A}^{\theta}\right\}.

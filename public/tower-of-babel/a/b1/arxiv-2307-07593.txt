1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field of order
q
and characteristic
p
, and let
\ell
be a prime different from
p
. In the
\ell
-modular representation theory of finite groups such as
\gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, the importance of tools such as Brauer theory and Deligne–Lusztig varieties is well-established
[
FS82
,
DJ86
]
. In this paper, we investigate a different tool, analogous to a construction in the local Langlands program for
p
-adic groups: gamma factors. While they first arose in the context of complex representations, they have been fruitful in studying mod-
\ell
representations of
\gl_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
(
[
HM18
,
Mos21
]
).

Fix a nontrivial character
\psi:\mathbb{F}_{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Rankin–Selberg gamma factors
\gamma(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},\psi)
have been defined for pairs
\pi
,
\pi^{\prime}
where
\pi
is a complex representation of
\gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
\pi^{\prime}
is a complex representation of
\gl_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, and both
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are assumed to be irreducible and
\psi
-generic
[
Pia83
,
Rod10
,
Nie14
,
Ye19
,
YZ21
,
Zel22
,
SZ23
]
. In this context, there are converse theorems analogous to the
p
-adic setting, which describe sets of
\pi^{\prime}
such that
\gamma(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},\psi)
uniquely determine
\pi
. In this paper we construct gamma factors and prove the simplest possible
\ell
-modular
converse theorem.

1.1.
Results

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
\ell
, now allowing
\ell
to possibly be zero. Irreducible complex representations of
\gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
are defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, they admit stable
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}[\frac{1}{p}]
-lattices, and the mod
\ell
reductions of such lattices are equivalent up to semisimplification. All irreducible
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
-representations arise through this reduction mod
\ell
procedure. Since the complex gamma factor
\gamma(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},\psi)
lies in
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}
, a first candidate for the mod
\ell
gamma factor is the mod
\ell
-reduction
\text{red}_{\ell}(\gamma(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},\psi))
. It follows from our mod
\ell
functional equation (
Section 1.1
) that
\red_{\ell}(\gamma(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},\psi))
is indeed the unique gamma factor satisfying the functional equation for
\red_{\ell}(\pi)
and
\red_{\ell}(\pi^{\prime})
. When
\ell\nmid\#\gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, the mod
\ell
representation theory of
\gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is essentially no different than the complex setting and our
Section 1.1
below implies
\red_{\ell}(\gamma(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},\psi))
indeed satisfies a mod
\ell
converse theorem under this restriction. However, it fails to satisfy a mod
\ell
converse theorem for general
\ell
in several examples when
n=2
. Using SAGE computations (
Appendix A
), we found the mod
\ell
converse theorem for
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
fails when
(\ell,q)=(2,5),(2,17),(3,7),(3,19),(5,11),(11,23),(23,47),(29,59),
though we verify it holds for all other pairs
(\ell,q)
with
\ell\leq 11
and
q=p\leq 23
. In all the counterexamples we found,
q
has the form
2\ell^{k}+1
, and we conjecture that these are the only cases in which it can fail. In analogy with the results in the
p
-adic setting in
[
Mos21
]
, the point of failure in the classical proof is the failure of so-called “
L^{2}
-completeness” of the Whittaker space.

This raises the question of how to construct a “new” gamma factor for any
\ell\neq p
that does satisfy a converse theorem, and which returns the classical gamma factor when
\ell\nmid\#\gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

The first step in constructing a “new” gamma factor is establishing a functional equation over arbitrary
\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p},\zeta_{p}]
-algebras
A
, where
\zeta_{p}
is a
p
-th root of unity. Bernstein and Zelevinsky developed a theory of “derivatives” for complex representations of
\gl_{n}(\mathcal{F})

[
BZ77
]
with respect to a fixed additive character
\psi
on
\mathcal{F}
. Fixing
\psi:\mathbb{F}_{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p},\zeta_{p}]^{\times}
, Vignéras observed derivatives work equally well for
\gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
-representations on
A
-modules. If
\pi
is an
A[\gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})]
-module its “
i
-th derivative”
\pi^{(i)}
is a representation of
\gl_{n-i}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, and the restriction
\pi|_{P_{n}}
to the mirabolic subgroup
P_{n}
(matrices with bottom row
(0,\dots,0,1)
) is glued from
\pi^{(1)},\dots,\pi^{(n)}
in a simple way. The top derivative
\pi^{(n)}
is equivalent to the
(N_{n},\psi)
-coinvariants, where
N_{n}
is the unipotent upper triangular subgroup, to which
\psi
is extended in a natural way. Thus
(\pi^{(n)})^{\vee}
is the space of Whittaker models of
\pi
(Frobenius reciprocity). The starting point of our construction is to restrict our attention to
\pi
of
Whittaker type
, meaning
\pi^{(n)}
(and hence
(\pi^{(n)})^{\vee}
) is free of rank one over
A
, which generalizes the “irreducible generic” hypothesis ubiquitous in the
A=\mathbb{C}
case. In particular, this allows one to speak of the Whittaker model
\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi_{A})\subset\Ind_{N}^{G}\psi_{A}
, where
\psi_{A}=\psi\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p},\zeta_{p}]}A
.

To state our first main result, we define an
A[\gl_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{q})]
-module
\pi^{\prime}
to be
exceptional
for
\pi
if there exists
k=1,\dots,m
such that

\Hom_{A[\GL_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{q})]}\left(\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi_{A})^{(n-k)},\left(\mathcal{W}(\pi^{\prime},\psi^{-1}_{A})^{(m-k)}\right)^{\vee}\right)\neq 0.

Theorem A
.

Suppose
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are Whittaker type
A[\gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})]
- and
A[\gl_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{q})]
-modules, respectively, and
\pi^{\prime}
is not exceptional for
\pi
. There exists a unique element
\gamma(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},\psi)
of
A^{\times}
such that

(1)

\left(\sum_{x\in N_{m}\backslash G_{m}}W\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&0\\
0&I_{n-m}\end{smallmatrix}\right)W^{\prime}(x)\right)\gamma(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},\psi)\\
=\sum_{x\in N_{m}\backslash G_{m}}\sum_{y\in M_{m,n-m-1}}W\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1&0\\
0&0&I_{n-m-1}\\
x&0&y\end{smallmatrix}\right)W^{\prime}(x)

for all
W\in\mathcal{W}(V,\psi_{A})
,
W^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}(V^{\prime},\psi_{A}^{-1})
.

Note that when
A=k
is a field, a representation
\pi
of
\gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is cuspidal if and only
\pi^{(n)}
is its only non-zero derivative, in which case there are no exceptional representations
\pi^{\prime}
.

Upon specializing to
k=\mathbb{C}
we obtain a functional equation that is more general than any appearing in the literature because
\pi
need not be cuspidal, nor even irreducible. The functional equation is known to fail without the non-exceptional hypothesis on
\pi^{\prime}
(
[
Rod10
, Theorem 3.1]
).

Our second main result is a converse theorem for new gamma factors of irreducible cuspidal
k
-representations. The novelty of these gamma factors is that they are valued in certain finite dimensional local
k
-algebras instead of
k
itself, which necessitates the level of generality on the coefficients that
Section 1.1
provides. These finite-dimensional
k
-algebras arise as the endomorphism rings of projective envelopes of generic representations. More precisely, given
\pi^{\prime}
an irreducible generic
k
-representation of
\gl_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
with projective cover
P(\pi^{\prime})
, we take
A=R(\pi^{\prime})=\End_{k[\gl_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{q})]}(P(\pi^{\prime}))
and set

\widetilde{\gamma}(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},\psi)=\gamma((\pi\otimes_{k}R(\pi^{\prime}))\times P(\pi^{\prime}),\psi)\in R(\pi^{\prime})^{\times}\ ,

which satisfies the following converse theorem.

Theorem B
.

Let
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
be irreducible cuspidal
k
-representations of
\gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and suppose

\widetilde{\gamma}(\pi_{1}\times\pi^{\prime},\psi)=\widetilde{\gamma}(\pi_{2}\times\pi^{\prime},\psi)

for all irreducible generic
k
-representations
\pi^{\prime}
of
\gl_{n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Then
\pi_{1}\cong\pi_{2}
.

When
\Char(k)\nmid\#\gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
P(\pi^{\prime})=\pi^{\prime}
and
R(\pi^{\prime})=k
, so
\widetilde{\gamma}(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},\psi)=\gamma(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},\psi)
. In particular,
Section 1.1
reduces to a finite field version of Henniart’s
n\times(n-1)
converse theorem
[
Hen93
]
when
k=\mathbb{C}
.

In
Appendix B
, we propose an alternative “new gamma factor” for
n=2
and
m=1
, which also specializes to the classical one for
\ell\nmid\#\gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, but which is an element of the base field
k
and does not involve exotic
k
-algebras. Remarkably, it satisfies a functional equation and converse theorem for cuspidals. This method shares some similarities with
[
Vig00
]
, including the fact that it does not appear to generalize beyond
n=2
.

1.2.
Future directions

1.2.1.
Macdonald correspondence in families

In
[
Mac80
]
, Macdonald established an analogue of the local Langlands correspondence for
\gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. If
\mathcal{F}
is a nonarchimedean local field with residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, it can be formulated as a bijection between complex irreducible representations of
\gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and tame inertial classes of complex representations of the Weil group of
\mathcal{F}
. This bijection preserves gamma factors, which (following
[
Spr75
]
) Macdonald defined analogously to the Godement–Jacquet factors for representations of
\gl_{n}(\mathcal{F})
. Later, Vignéras found a similar but more subtle bijection in the mod
\ell
setting (
[
Vig94
, Section 3]
), but she did not consider gamma factors. More recently, a local Langlands correspondence “in families” has been established for
\gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. If
\mathcal{O}
is a complete discrete valuation ring with residue field
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
, it takes the form of an isomorphism of commutative rings
B_{q,n}\xrightarrow{\sim}\End_{\mathcal{O}[\gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})]}(\ind_{N_{n}}^{\gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\psi_{\mathcal{O}})
, where
B_{q,n}
is the ring of functions on a natural moduli space of tame
\ell
-adically continuous
\mathcal{O}
-valued inertial classes, and
\psi_{\mathcal{O}}:N_{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}^{\times}
is a nondegenerate character on the unipotent upper triangular subgroup
N_{n}
. The first approach to proving the existence of such an isomorphism was to deduce it as a consequence of the local Langlands correspondence in families for
\gl_{n}(\mathcal{F})

[
HM18
,
Hel20
]
(see also
[
Sho22
]
) which, in turn, requires gamma factors, converse theorems, and the classical local Langlands correspondence for
\gl_{n}(\mathcal{F})
, as an input. More recently, Li and Shotton found a remarkable second proof of “finite fields local Langlands in families,” which works for any reductive group
G(\mathbb{F}_{q})
whose dual group has simply connected derived subgroup. Their proof uses purely finite fields methods (
[
LS22
,
Li23
]
), but they do not consider gamma factors. The present paper is a first step toward understanding how Rankin–Selberg gamma factors for
\gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
fit into the
\ell
-modular correspondence and the families correspondence.

In future work, the authors plan to apply the converse theorems proved here to address the question of whether the Macdonald bijection and its mod
\ell
analog are the unique sequence of bijections (one for each
n
) matching the Rankin–Selberg gamma factors
\gamma(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},\psi)
defined here with Deligne’s
\epsilon_{0}
factors of the tensor product of the corresponding inertial classes of
W_{\mathcal{F}}
-representations. To show the local Langlands correspondence for
\gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
in families preserves our new gamma factors (and is uniquely characterized by this property), one would need to establish a compatibility between Curtis homomorphisms, which were used in
[
Hel20
]
and
[
LS22
]
to construct the local Langlands in families, and the Rankin–Selberg gamma factors. It seems that a multiplicativity property for our gamma factors would be needed here.

1.2.2.
Converse theorem for generic irreducibles

Our proof of
Section 1.1
only works for cuspidals because cuspidal representations
\pi
have no exceptional representations
\pi^{\prime}
. When
\pi^{\prime}
is exceptional for
\pi
, the gamma factor
\gamma(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},\psi)
can no longer be defined using a functional equation, but in the complex setting it is traditionally defined using Bessel vectors (
[
Rod10
,
Nie14
]
). Very recently, Soudry and Zelingher
[
SZ23
]
proved a multiplicativity property for
\gamma(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},\psi)
thus defined, and used it to deduce a converse theorem applying to all irreducible generic representations
\pi
. In future work we plan to investigate the question of whether this remains true in the mod
\ell
setting, either by establishing a generalization of the Bessel vector construction and the multiplicativity property to characteristic
\ell>0
, or by using the functional equation even while excluding exceptional
\pi^{\prime}
.

1.2.3.
Jacquet’s conjecture in the mod
\ell
setting

In the complex setting, it has been proved that
\pi
is characterized by gamma factors
\gamma(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},\psi)
where
\pi^{\prime}
ranges over irreducible generic representations of
\gl_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
for
m=1,\dots,\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
(
[
Nie14
]
for cuspidal
\pi
, and
[
SZ23
]
in general). It is natural to ask whether our
Section 1.1
remains true with
m\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
; the answer is probably yes (c.f.
[
Mos21
]
in the
p
-adic setting), but we do not to address this here.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we collect some basic facts about the representation theory of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

2.1.
Subgroups of
\gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

Let
p
be a prime,
q
be a power of
p
, and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the field with
q
elements. For
n
a positive integer, let

G_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\gl_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}).

Denote by
\mat_{m_{1},m_{2}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
the vector space of
m_{1}\times m_{2}
matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
The mirabolic subgroup of
G_{n}
is

P_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{\begin{pmatrix}g&y\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\in G_{n}:g\in G_{n-1},y\in\mat_{n-1,1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\right\},

with unipotent radical

U_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{\begin{pmatrix}I_{n-1}&y\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}:y\in\mat_{n-1,1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\right\}\leq P_{n},

so that

P_{n}=U_{n}\rtimes G_{n-1}
. We also denote

N_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&*&\ldots&*\\
0&1&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&*\\
0&\ldots&0&1\end{array}\right)

the subgroup of unipotent upper-triangular matrices in
G_{n}
. We consider a sequence of subgroups interpolating between
U_{n}
and
N_{n}
: for
-1\leq m\leq n-1
, define

U_{n,k}:=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}I_{n-k}&z\\
0&y\end{pmatrix}\ :\ z\in\text{Mat}_{n-k,k}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),\ y\in N_{k}\right\}.

Note that

I_{n}=U_{n,0},\quad U_{n}=U_{n,1},\quad N_{n}={U_{n,n-1}}=U_{n,n}.

2.2.
Representations

Let
G
be a finite group. In this article, our coefficient rings
R
will always be assumed to be algebras over
\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p},\zeta_{p}]
. Let
\Rep_{R}(G)
denote the category of
R
-linear representations of
G
, or, equivalently, of
R[G]
-modules. We warn the reader that we will often use the letter
V\in\Rep_{R}(G)
, even if
V
is not necessarily free as an
R
-module. If
H\leq G
is a subgroup, the induction functor
\Ind_{H}^{G}:\Rep_{R}(H)\rightarrow\Rep_{R}(G)
sends
(\pi,V)
to the representation

\Ind_{H}^{G}(\pi)=\left\{f:G\rightarrow V:f(hg)=\pi(h)f(g),\;h\in H\right\},

with its natural left
G
-action by right multiplication on
G
. Frobenius reciprocity is the statement that induction is a left-adjoint to restriction: given
\rho\in\Rep_{R}(H)
and
\pi\in\Rep_{R}(G)
,

\Hom_{G}(\Ind_{H}^{G}\rho,\pi)\simeq\Hom_{H}(\rho,\pi|_{H}).

The group ring
R[G]
is equipped with a natural left
H
-action, which makes
\Ind_{H}^{G}(\pi)
naturally isomorphic to
\Hom_{R[H]}(R[G],\pi)
as left
R[G]
-modules, which some authors call “coinduction”. However the distinction is unimportant because of the isomorphism given by

\displaystyle R[G]\otimes_{R[H]}\pi

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\sim}\Ind_{H}^{G}(\pi)

\displaystyle 1\otimes v

\displaystyle\mapstochar\rightarrow f_{v},

where
v
is an element in the space of
\pi
and
f_{v}
is the function supported on
H
such that
f_{v}(h)=\pi(h)v
,
h\in H
. In particular, induction is also a right adjoint to restriction.

If
N<G
is a subgroup such that
\left\lvert N\right\rvert
is invertible in
R
, and
\psi:N\rightarrow R^{\times}
is a character, we define a projector to the submodule
\pi^{N,\psi}
of elements on which
N
acts via
\psi
:

(2)

\displaystyle\pi

\displaystyle\rightarrow\pi^{N,\psi}

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle\mapstochar\rightarrow|N|^{-1}\sum_{n\in N}\psi(n)^{-1}\pi(n)v.

The kernel of this projector equals the submodule
V(N,\psi)
generated by
\{\pi(n)v-\psi(n)v:n\in N,v\in V\}
, so
\pi^{N,\psi}
is canonically isomorphic to the
(N,\psi)
-coinvariants
\pi_{N,\psi}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}V/V(N,\psi)
.

Lemma 2.2.1
.

Let
N
be a subgroup whose cardinality is invertible in
R
and let
\psi:N\rightarrow R^{\times}
be a character. Then
(\pi_{N,\psi})^{\vee}\cong(\pi^{\vee})_{N,\psi^{-1}}
.

Proof.

We make use of the fact that for a finite group
G
,
\Hom_{R[G]}(V,W^{\vee})\cong\Hom_{R[G]}(W,V^{\vee})
(
[
Vig96
, I.4.13]
), where
(-)^{\vee}
denotes the
R
-linear dual equipped with its natural
G
-action. Applying this for
G=N
we have the following identifications

\displaystyle(\pi_{N,\psi})^{\vee}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{def}}{=}\Hom_{R}(\pi_{N,\psi},R)

\displaystyle=\Hom_{R[N]}(\pi,\psi)

\displaystyle=\Hom_{R[G]}(\pi,\Ind_{N}^{G}\psi)

\displaystyle=\Hom_{R[G]}(\Ind_{N}^{G}\psi^{-1},\pi^{\vee})\ \ \ \text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{vig_book}{}{}, I.5.11]}}

\displaystyle=\Hom_{R[N]}(\psi^{-1},\pi^{\vee})

\displaystyle=(\pi^{\vee})^{N,\psi^{-1}}

\displaystyle=(\pi^{\vee})_{N,\psi^{-1}}\qed

Given a nontrivial partition
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}
of
n
, there is an associated standard parabolic subgroup
P_{n_{1},...,n_{r}}
with Levi subgroup
G_{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times G_{n_{r}}
. If
\sigma_{i}
is a representation of
G_{n_{i}}
, then the parabolic induction

\sigma_{1}\times\cdots\times\sigma_{r}:=\ind_{P_{n_{1},...,n_{r}}}^{G_{n}}\sigma_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\sigma_{r}

is obtained by first inflating
\sigma_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\sigma_{r}
to a representation of
P_{n_{1},...,n_{r}}
by letting its unipotent radical act trivially, and inducing the resulting representation to
G_{n}
.

The corresponding “parabolic restriction” functors are known as Jacquet functors. Given a partition as above, the functor
J^{G_{n}}_{P_{n_{1},...,n_{r}}}:\Rep_{R}(G_{n})\rightarrow\Rep_{R}(G_{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times G_{n_{r}})
takes a representation
(\pi,V)\in\Rep_{R}(G)
to its coinvariants under the unipotent radical of
P_{n_{1},...,n_{r}}
.
The functor
J^{G_{n}}_{P_{n_{1},...,n_{r}}}
is both left- and right-adjoint to parabolic induction.

We say that
(\rho,V)\in\Rep_{R}(G_{n})
is
cuspidal
if its image under the Jacquet functor
J^{G_{n}}_{P_{n_{1},\dots,n_{r}}}
is zero for every non-trivial partition. This is equivalent to asking that there are no nonzero morphisms from
\rho
to a parabolic induction.

2.3.
Multilinear forms

Gamma factors are defined as the constants of proportionality between certain multilinear forms, once the spaces of such forms are shown to be one-dimensional. We define those spaces now.

Definition 2.3.1
.

If
G
is a group,
(\rho,V),(\rho^{\prime},V^{\prime}),(\rho^{\prime\prime},V^{\prime\prime})\in\Rep_{R}(G)
and
\chi:G\rightarrow R^{\times}
is a character, let

\displaystyle\bil_{G}(V,V^{\prime},\chi)

\displaystyle:=\Hom_{R[G]}(V\otimes_{R}V^{\prime},\chi)

\displaystyle=\{\text{bilinear functions }B:V\times V^{\prime}\rightarrow R\ |\ B(gv,gv^{\prime})=\chi(g)B(v,v^{\prime})\}.

and let

\displaystyle\tril_{G}(V,V^{\prime},V^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle:=\Hom_{R[G]}(V\otimes_{R}V^{\prime}\otimes_{R}V^{\prime\prime},1)

\displaystyle=\{\text{$G$-invariant trilinear functions }B:V\times V^{\prime}\times V^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow R\}.

In the above definitions
G_{n}
acts diagonally on the tensor products.

2.4.
Derivative functors

Let

2.4.1.
Properties of derivative functors

Bernstein–Zelevinsky established some basic properties of these functors over
p
-adic general linear groups, and Vignéras has observed that the proofs work equally well in the case of finite general linear groups
[

, III.1.3]

(1)

They are all exact.

(2)

\Psi^{-}
is left adjoint to
\Psi^{+}

(3)

\Phi^{+}
is left adjoint to
\Phi^{-}
and
\Phi^{-}
is left adjoint to
\Phi^{+}
.

(4)

\Phi^{-}\Phi^{+}\cong\id
and
\Psi^{-}\Psi^{+}\cong\id

(5)

\Phi^{-}\Psi^{+}=0
and
\Psi^{-}\Phi^{+}=0

(6)

There is a canonical exact sequence

0\rightarrow\Phi^{+}\Phi^{-}\rightarrow\id\rightarrow\Psi^{+}\Psi^{-}\rightarrow 0.

We note the following additional property:

(7)

All the functors commute with arbitrary base change. In other words, if
R\rightarrow R^{\prime}
is a map of rings, then
\Phi^{+}(V\otimes_{R}R^{\prime})=\Phi^{+}(V)\otimes_{R}R^{\prime}
, and the same for all the other functors.

Definition 2.4.2

4.

Let

4.1.
Projective envelopes

Recall that
N_{n}
denotes the subgroup of unipotent upper triangular matrices. Since the order of
N_{n}
is relatively prime to
\ell
, the character
\psi_{k}:N_{n}\rightarrow k^{\times}
is a projective
k[N_{n}]
-module. Since
\Ind_{N_{n}}^{G_{n}}
is left-adjoint to an exact functor, it takes projective objects to projective objects and therefore
\Ind_{N_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{k}
is a projective
k[G_{n}]
-module. We can then decompose
\Ind_{N_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{k}
as a direct sum

\Ind_{N_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{k}=P_{1}^{\oplus e_{1}}\oplus\dots\oplus P_{r}^{\oplus e_{r}},

where each
P_{i}
is indecomposable and projective, and
P_{i}\not\cong P_{j}
for

. However, we know, see
[
Zha21
, Corollary 1.7]
, that
\End_{G_{n}}(\Ind_{N_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{k})
is a commutative ring, so

for all

. The commutativity of
\End_{G_{n}}(\Ind_{N_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{k})
also implies that
\Hom_{G_{n}}(P_{i},P_{j})=0
.

There is a bijection
[
Vig88
, I.A.7]
between isomorphism classes of irreducible representations of
G_{n}
and isomorphism classes of indecomposable projective
k[G_{n}]
-modules:

\displaystyle\{\text{irreducible $k[G_{n}]$-modules}\}

\displaystyle\leftrightarrow\{\text{indecomposable projective $k[G_{n}]$-modules}\}

\displaystyle\pi

\displaystyle\mapstochar\rightarrow P(\pi)

\displaystyle\soc(P)

\displaystyle\mapsfrom P,

where
P(\pi)
denotes the projective envelope of
\pi
and
\soc(P)
denotes the socle (i.e. the largest semisimple subrepresentation) of
P
. Note also that, by duality,
\pi
also occurs as a quotient of

and is in fact the only irreducible quotient of
P(\pi)

[
Ser77
, Chapter 14]
. In other words,
\pi
is not only the socle of
P(\pi)
but also its cosocle (i.e. the largest semisimple quotient).

Since
P_{i}
is not isomorphic to
P_{j}
for

, the bijectivity above implies that
\soc(P_{i})
is not isomorphic to
\soc(P_{j})
. On the other hand, being contained in
\Ind_{N_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{k}
, each
\soc(P_{i})
is irreducible and generic, and every irreducible generic representation must occur as a (the) submodule of some
P_{i}
. Thus in restricting to generic objects we have a bijection of isomorphism classes:

\displaystyle\{\text{irreducible \emph{generic} $k[G_{n}]$-modules}\}

\displaystyle\leftrightarrow\{P_{1},P_{2},\dots,P_{r}\}

\displaystyle\pi

\displaystyle\mapstochar\rightarrow P(\pi)

\displaystyle\soc(P)

\displaystyle\mapsfrom P,

Let

for an irreducible generic representation

. Since

is indecomposable projective,

is a local ring.

is commutative because it is contained in

. Note that since

is finite-dimensional over

, the ring

is a finite-dimensional

-algebra.

4.2.
Duality and derivative of

Recall that we let
{}^{\iota}g:={{}^{t}g^{-1}}

Now let us consider the representation

Proposition 4.2.2

Let

\displaystyle P^{(n)}

\displaystyle\cong\Hom_{k[N_{n}]}(\psi_{k},P)

4.3.
Whittaker model of
P(\pi)

We first note that

is of
\psi
-Whittaker type: from

,

is an
R(\pi)

1

\Hom_{R(\pi)}(P^{(n)},R(\pi))=R(\pi).

This allows us to consider the Whittaker model of
P(\pi)
in
\Ind_{N_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{R(\pi)}
, which entails choosing an element
\eta\in\Hom_{R(\pi)}(P^{(n)},R(\pi))
corresponding to a unit in
R(\pi)
. If we identify
P(\pi)^{(n)}\cong R(\pi)
under the isomorphism from
Section 4.2
, and thus identify

\Hom_{R(\pi)}(P^{(n)},R(\pi))\cong\Hom_{R(\pi)}(R(\pi),R(\pi))=R(\pi),

we might as well choose
\eta
corresponding to correspond to the identity under this identification. The Whittaker model
\mathcal{W}(P(\pi),\psi_{R(\pi)})
is then, by definition, the image of the map

\displaystyle P(\pi)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Ind_{N_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{R(\pi)}

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapstochar\rightarrow W_{f}\

defined as follows. If
\lambda:P(\pi)\rightarrow P(\pi)^{(n)}\cong R(\pi)
denotes the natural quotient map, Frobenius reciprocity gives the formula

W_{f}(g):=\lambda(gf),\ \ g\in G.

Next, we will compute a natural section of the above map from
P(\pi)
to its Whittaker model. There is a canonical map of
k[N_{n}]
-modules

\Ind_{N_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{k}\rightarrow\psi_{k}

given by evaluation at the identity.
For each irreducible generic representation
\pi
, we can restrict this to a map
P(\pi)\rightarrow\psi
, which must factor through the
(N_{n},\psi)
-coinvariants to give a map

of

-vector spaces. In other words, for

an element of
\Ind_{N_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{k}
that lives in

, we have
\theta(\lambda(f))=f(1)
.

Let

be the
R(\pi)
-valued Whittaker function of

in
\mathcal{W}(P(\pi),\psi_{R(\pi)}^{-1})
. We have

(\theta\circ W_{f})(g)=\theta(\lambda(gf))=(gf)(1)=f(g),\ \ \ g\in G_{n}

Thus a section of our chosen map

P(\pi)\rightarrow\Ind_{N_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{R(\pi)}

is given by composing with
\theta
. We record these observations in the following corollary.

Corollary 4.3.1
.

The representation
P=P(\pi)
is of
\psi
-Whittaker type and essentially
\psi
-generic, i.e., embeds in its Whittaker model.

4.3.2.
Alternative view of the Whittaker model of
P(\pi)

In this subsection we attempt to illustrate why
\mathcal{W}(P(\pi),\psi_{R(\pi)})
is more useful that
P(\pi)
itself: it sees the natural action of
R(\pi)
on
P(\pi)
in both its
G_{n}
-structure and its
R(\pi)
-structure coming from multiplying
R(\pi)
-valued functions by elements of
R(\pi)
. We will not use these results in the rest of the paper, but include them to give a more conceptual understanding of the map
\theta
from the previous subsection.

By extending scalars along the natural inclusion
k\subset R(\pi)
we get an embedding
\Ind_{N_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{k}\hookrightarrow\Ind_{N_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{R(\pi)}
, which restricts to an inclusion on the summand
P=P(\pi)

P\hookrightarrow P\otimes_{k}R(\pi).

The module
P\otimes_{k}R(\pi)
has two distinct
R(\pi)
-module structures, both of which commute with the
G_{n}
-action, namely the one defined on simple tensors by

\phi*(f\otimes\phi^{\prime})=\phi(f)\otimes\phi^{\prime},

and the one defined by

\phi\cdot(f\otimes\phi^{\prime})=f\otimes(\phi\cdot\phi^{\prime}).

There is a natural projection of
k[G_{n}]
-modules

\varpi:P\otimes_{k}R(\pi)\rightarrow P\otimes_{R(\pi)}R(\pi)\cong P

given by taking the quotient by the
k
-subspace
\ker(\varpi)
generated by tensors of the form
f\otimes\phi-\phi(f)\otimes 1
.

Since
P
is a projective
k[G_{n}]
-module,
\varpi
is a split surjection, and there exists a section
\eta:P\rightarrow P\otimes_{k}R(\pi)
giving a decomposition into a direct sum of
k[G_{n}]
-modules

P\otimes_{k}R(\pi)=\eta(P)\oplus\ker(\varpi).

However, by commutativity of
R(\pi)
we have

\displaystyle\phi*(f\otimes\phi^{\prime}-\phi^{\prime}(f)\otimes 1)

\displaystyle=\phi(f)\otimes\phi^{\prime}-\phi^{\prime}(\phi(f))\otimes 1

\displaystyle\phi\cdot(f\otimes\phi^{\prime}-\phi^{\prime}(f)\otimes 1)

\displaystyle=(f\otimes\phi\phi^{\prime}-\phi\phi^{\prime}(f)\otimes 1)-(\phi^{\prime}(f)\otimes\phi-\phi(\phi^{\prime}(f))\otimes 1),

so
\ker(\eta)
is stable under
R(\pi)
for both actions, and thus so is
\eta(P)
. We conclude the above splitting is in fact a splitting of
R(\pi)[G_{n}]
-modules for both
R(\pi)
actions. Furthermore, given
f\in P
, we must have that
\phi*\eta(f)-\phi\cdot\eta(f)
is an element of
\ker(\varpi)\cap\eta(P)=\{0\}
, which shows
\phi*\eta(f)=\phi\cdot\eta(f)
.

We conclude that each splitting
\eta
gives rise to a Whittaker model

P\hookrightarrow\Ind_{N_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{R(\pi)},

for which
\varpi
is a canonical section, and whose image lands in the subset

\{W\in P\otimes_{k}R(\pi)\ :\ \phi*W=\phi\cdot W\ ,\ \phi\in R(\pi)\}.

From this perspective, the relation

\theta\circ W_{f}=f

of the previous subsection amounts to the fact that the composite map

P\hookrightarrow P\otimes_{k}R(\pi)\xrightarrow{\varpi}P

is equivalent to the identity.

4.5.
Completeness of Whittaker models

To prove the converse theorem we need a so-called “
L^{2}
-completeness of Whittaker models” statement. The point of passing to
R(\pi)
coefficients instead of
k
coefficients is to recover such a completeness statement.
Appendix A
discusses counterexamples to the converse theorem for
k
-valued gamma factors: they arise because of the failure of completeness of Whittaker models (which for
G_{1}
reduces to the dual to linear independence of characters.)

Fix an irreducible generic
k
-representation
\pi
of
G_{n}
.

Theorem 4.5.1
.

Let
H
be an element of
\Ind_{N_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{k}
. If

\sum_{x\in{N_{n}}\backslash G_{n}}H(x)W(x)=0

for every
W\in\mathcal{W}(P(\pi),\psi^{-1}_{R(\pi)})
, for every irreducible generic representation
\pi
of
G_{n}
, then
H
is identically zero.

Proof.

By replacing
\psi
with
\psi^{-1}
in
Section 4.3
we can make a choice of isomorphism
P(\pi)^{(n,\psi^{-1})}\cong R(\pi)
for each irreducible
\psi^{-1}
-generic representation
\pi
to get a Whittaker model

\displaystyle P(\pi)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Ind_{N_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi^{-1}_{R(\pi)}

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapstochar\rightarrow W_{f}.

Recall from
Section 4.3
there is a map
\theta:R(\pi)\cong P^{(n,\psi^{-1})}\rightarrow k
arising from
f\mapstochar\rightarrow f(1)
such that
f=\theta\circ W_{f}
.

Thus for every such
f\in\Ind_{N_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{k}^{-1}
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\theta\left(\sum_{x\in U\backslash G}H(x)W_{f}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in U\backslash G}H(x)\theta(W_{f}(x))

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in U\backslash G}H(x)f(x).

We established in
Section 4.1
that
\Ind_{N_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi
is the multiplicity-free direct sum of the
P(\pi)
for
\pi
irreducible generic, and as such is spanned by
f\in P(\pi)
. Since

\displaystyle\Ind_{U}^{G}\psi_{k}\times\Ind_{U}^{G}\psi_{k}^{-1}

\displaystyle\rightarrow k

\displaystyle(H,f)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\sum_{x\in U\backslash G}H(x)f(x)

is a nondegenerate duality pairing, we conclude that
H
is identically zero.
∎

4.6.
Proof of converse theorem

We finally arrive at the proof of
Section 1.1
. Our strategy is inspired by the proof of the converse theorem in
[
Hen93
]
.

If
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
are cuspidal
k
-representations of
G_{n}
, set

S(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\psi):=\left\{(W_{1},W_{2})\in\mathcal{W}(\rho_{1},\psi_{k})\times\mathcal{W}(\rho_{2},\psi_{k}):W_{1}|_{P_{n}}=W_{2}|_{P_{n}}\right\}.

There is a diagonal action of
P_{n}
on
\mathcal{W}(\rho_{1},\psi_{k})\times\mathcal{W}(\rho_{2},\psi_{k})
and the subspace
S(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\psi)
is stable under this action by its definition. We will show it is in fact
G_{n}
-stable if we suppose that
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
have the same gamma factors.

Proposition 4.6.1
.

Let
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
be cuspidal
k
-representations of
G_{n}
and suppose that

\tilde{\gamma}(\rho_{1}\times\pi,\psi)=\tilde{\gamma}(\rho_{2}\times\pi,\psi)

for all irreducible generic representations
\pi
of
G_{n-1}
. Then
S(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\psi)
is stable under the diagonal action of
G_{n}
.

Proof.

The restriction of a Whittaker function to
P_{n}
is determined by its values on
G_{n-1}
(embedded in
G_{n}
in the top left). Therefore:

\displaystyle(W_{1},W_{2})\in S(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\psi)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow W_{1}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)=W_{2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\text{ for all }x\in G_{n-1}

\displaystyle\xLeftrightarrow{\text{\autoref{thm:completeness}}}\sum_{x\in U_{n-1}\backslash G_{n-1}}W_{1}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)W^{\prime}(x)=\sum_{x\in U_{n-1}\backslash G_{n-1}}W_{2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)W^{\prime}(x)

for all
W^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}(P(\pi),\psi_{R(\pi)}^{-1})
, for all
\pi

\displaystyle\xLeftrightarrow{\text{equality of $\tilde{\gamma}$'s}}\sum_{x\in U_{n-1}\backslash G_{n-1}}W_{1}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
x&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)W^{\prime}(x)=\sum_{x\in U_{n-1}\backslash G_{n-1}}W_{2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
x&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)W^{\prime}(x)

for all
W^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}(P(\pi),\psi_{R(\pi)}^{-1})
, for all
\pi

\displaystyle\xLeftrightarrow{\text{\autoref{thm:completeness}}}W_{1}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
{{}^{\iota}x}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)=W_{2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
{{}^{\iota}x}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\text{ for all }x\in G_{n-1}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\widetilde{W_{1}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\widetilde{W_{2}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\text{ for all }x\in G_{n-1}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(\widetilde{W_{1}},\widetilde{W_{2}})\in S(\rho_{1}^{\vee},\rho_{2}^{\vee},\psi^{-1})

Now if
\overline{p}\in{{}^{t}P_{n}}
we have, for
i=1,2
,

\widetilde{\overline{p}W_{i}}(g)=({{}^{\iota}\overline{p}}\widetilde{W_{i}})(g).

Thus if
(W_{1},W_{2})\in S(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\psi)
, then since
S(\rho_{1}^{\vee},\rho_{2}^{\vee},\psi^{-1})
is
P_{n}
-stable we have

(\widetilde{\overline{p}W_{1}},\widetilde{\overline{p}W_{2}})=({{}^{\iota}\overline{p}}\widetilde{W_{1}},{{}^{\iota}\overline{p}}\widetilde{W_{2}})\in S(\rho_{1}^{\vee},\rho_{2}^{\vee},\psi^{-1}).

The above equivalences then imply that
(\overline{p}W_{1},\overline{p}W_{2})
is in
S(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\psi)
. Thus we have shown that
S(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\psi)
is stable under both
P_{n}
and
{}^{t}P_{n}
. Since these two groups generate
G_{n}
we conclude that
S(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\psi)
is stable under
G_{n}
.
∎

.

Suppose
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}

[

using the constructions of

]
to recover

for

A.2.
The algorithm

The algorithm executes two tasks:

(1)

The function

computes gamma factors.

(2)

The function

detects equalities between gamma factors.

The function
gammafactor
.

Let

be prime. To compute the Gauss sums, we exploit that all groups in sight are cyclic. We have the following variables:

•

\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}
,

•

\_
m
(resp.
coprime
\_
n
) is the largest divisor of
q^{2}-1
(resp.
q-1
) coprime to
\ell
.

•

is a choice of primitive root of unity of order
coprime
\_
m

•

is a choice of primitive root of unity of order
coprime
\_
n

•

is a choice of primitive
q^{\text{th}}
root of unity, and we fix the additive character
\psi:\mathbb{F}_{q}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{\times}
so that
\psi(1)=\texttt{psi}
.

This allows us to identify characters of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
with integers in the relevant ranges as follows:

•

For

, the character
\nu_{i}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}
is defined by

We will denote the cuspidal representation

•

For

The input of the function

\texttt{f}=\texttt{nu}\,\hat{}\,(\texttt{Integer}(\texttt{m}/\texttt{2}))
, the function returns

\texttt{gf}:=\texttt{f}*\texttt{sum}(\texttt{nu}\,\hat{}\,(\texttt{i}*\texttt{k})*\texttt{omega}\,\hat{}\,(\texttt{j}*\texttt{k})*\texttt{psi}\,\hat{}\,(\texttt{w}\,\hat{\,}\texttt{k}+\texttt{w}\,\hat{}\,(\texttt{q}*\texttt{k}))\texttt{ for}\;\texttt{k}\;\texttt{in }[\texttt{0}..\texttt{m-1}])

This function compares the output of the function

for different values of

and

. First recall that,
\rho_{i}\simeq\rho_{i^{\prime}}
for

precisely when

The function

first runs over all isomorphism classes of cuspidal representations
\rho_{j}
, removes duplicates, and records a list

of integers

corresponding to a list of non-duplicate

in the list

and
i’
have the same gamma factor

\_

. Finally, the function returns an array

of

in

\_
duplicates
and

\_

-1
]
.

The function

then runs the utility function

Currently, the speed of the algorithm is restricted by the actual computations of the Gauss sums, which runs at least in

In this appendix we construct an “

Lemma B.1.1
.

The group generated by
P_{2}^{\ell}
and
\overline{P}_{2}^{\ell}
is
G_{2}^{\ell}
.

Proof.

Let
H
be the subgroup of
G_{2}
generated by
P_{2}^{\ell}
and
\overline{P}_{2}^{\ell}
. Clearly
P_{2}^{\ell}\subset G_{2}^{\ell}
and
\overline{P}_{2}^{\ell}\subset G_{2}^{\ell}
, and thus
H\subset G_{2}^{\ell}
.

For the opposite inclusion we argue as follows. By row reduction,
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is generated by the elementary matrices with
1

\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is generated by
N_{2}
and
\overline{U}_{2}
. But
N_{2}\subset P_{2}^{\ell}
and
\overline{U}_{2}\subset\overline{P}_{2}^{\ell}
so

. We are done if for every element

we can find an element

such that

(for then

∎

Theorem B.1.2

(Clifford’s Theorem
[
CR81
, 11.1]
)
.

If
G
is a finite group,
H\triangleleft G
and

is an irreducible representation of

\rho_{i}\not\sim\rho_{j}
for

. The isotypic components

are permuted transitively under conjugation by

\rho=\Ind_{\Stab_{G}(\rho_{i})}^{G}(\rho_{i}^{e})

.

Since
G
acts by conjugation on the set

it follows that

. But
G
acts transitively so

[G:H]
.

.

If

By

Proposition B.2.2
.

\Ind_{N_{2}}^{P_{2}^{\ell}}\psi

Corollary B.2.3
.

The composition

By construction it is a morphism of

-representations. Both

Corollary B.2.4
.

r=[P_{2}:P_{2}^{\ell}]

Proof.

r=\frac{\dim_{k}\rho}{\dim_{k}\rho_{\psi}}=\frac{\dim_{k}\Ind_{N_{2}}^{P_{2}}\psi}{\dim_{k}\Ind_{N_{2}}^{P_{2}^{\ell}}\psi}=\frac{[P_{2}:N_{2}]}{[P_{2}^{\ell}:N_{2}]}=[P_{2}:P_{2}^{\ell}].

∎

Next we prove the key one-dimensionality result that lets us deduce the existence of the gamma factor as the ratio between two linear functionals in a functional equation. Note that because

Corollary B.2.5
.

For any
\omega:G_{1}\rightarrow k^{\times}

\dim_{k}\bil_{G_{1}^{\ell}}(\mathcal{W}^{\ell}(\rho_{\psi})\otimes\omega,\mathbf{1})=1.

Proof.

Note

\bil_{G_{1}^{\ell}}(\mathcal{W}^{\ell}(\rho_{\psi})\otimes\omega,\mathbf{1})=\Hom_{G_{1}^{\ell}}(V_{\psi},\omega^{-1}).

By
Section B.2
we have
V_{\psi}|_{P_{2}^{\ell}}\cong\Ind_{N_{2}}^{P_{2}^{\ell}}\psi
. The map

\displaystyle\Ind_{N_{2}}^{P_{2}^{\ell}}\psi

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\sim}k[G_{1}^{\ell}]

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapstochar\rightarrow\left(x\mapstochar\rightarrow f\begin{pmatrix}x&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right)

gives an isomorphism with the regular representation. But
G_{1}^{\ell}
is a cyclic group of order prime to

contains every

with multiplicity one.
∎

Definition B.2.6
.

Following

, for

and

a character we define

\displaystyle I^{\ell}(W,\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in G_{1}^{\ell}}W\begin{pmatrix}x&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\omega^{-1}(x)

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}^{\ell}(W,\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in G_{1}^{\ell}}W\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
x&0\end{pmatrix}\omega^{-1}(x)

Then

are two nonzero elements. But in view of

Definition B.2.7
.

For
\omega:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\rightarrow k^{\times}

\gamma^{\ell}(\rho\times\omega,\psi)\in k

B.3.
Converse theorem

We now show that the
\ell
-regular factor satisfies a converse theorem; our strategy mirrors that of
Section 4.6
. Suppose
(\rho_{1},V_{1})
and
(\rho_{2},V_{2})
are two irreducible generic cuspidal
k
-linear representations of
G_{2}
and further suppose that

\gamma^{\ell}(\rho_{1}\times\omega,\psi)=\gamma^{\ell}(\rho_{2}\times\omega,\psi)

for all
\omega:G_{1}^{\times}\rightarrow k^{\times}
. Let
\mathcal{W}_{1}^{\ell}=\mathcal{W}^{\ell}(V_{1,\psi})
and
\mathcal{W}_{2}^{\ell}=\mathcal{W}^{\ell}(V_{2,\psi})
.

Definition B.3.1
.

Let

S(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\psi):=\left\{(W_{1},W_{2})\in\mathcal{W}^{\ell}_{1}\times\mathcal{W}^{\ell}_{2}:W_{1}|_{P_{2}^{\ell}}=W_{2}|_{P_{2}^{\ell}}\right\}.

By definition there is a diagonal action of
G_{2}^{\ell}
on
S(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\psi)
and
S(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\psi)
is
P_{2}^{\ell}
-stable for this action.

Lemma B.3.2
.

If
g\in G_{2}^{\ell}
and
(W_{1},W_{2})\in S(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\psi)
, then

(gW_{1},gW_{2})\in S(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\psi)
.

Proof.

First note that since
W_{1},W_{2}
are Whittaker functions,

\displaystyle(W_{1},W_{2})\in S(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\psi)

\displaystyle\iff W_{1}\begin{pmatrix}x&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}=W_{2}\begin{pmatrix}x&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\text{ for all }x\in G_{1}^{\ell}

\displaystyle\xLeftrightarrow{\text{Artin's Lemma}}I^{\ell}(W_{1},\omega)=I^{\ell}(W_{2},\omega)\text{ for all }\omega

\displaystyle\xLeftrightarrow{\text{equality of $\gamma^{\ell}$}}\widetilde{I}^{\ell}(W_{1},\omega)=\widetilde{I}^{\ell}(W_{2},\omega)\text{ for all }\omega

\displaystyle\xLeftrightarrow{\text{Artin's Lemma}}W_{1}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
x&0\end{pmatrix}=W_{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
x&0\end{pmatrix}\text{ for all }x\in G_{1}^{\ell}

\displaystyle\iff\widetilde{W_{1}}\begin{pmatrix}x&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}=\widetilde{W_{2}}\begin{pmatrix}x&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\text{ for all }x\in G_{1}^{\ell}

\displaystyle\iff(\widetilde{W_{1}},\widetilde{W_{2}})\in S(\rho_{1}^{\vee},\rho_{2}^{\vee},\psi^{-1})

Here Artin’s Lemma (the

, provided that
\mathrm{char}(k)\nmid\left\lvert H\right\rvert
, see
[
Pia83
, §1]
. Now if
\overline{p}\in\overline{P}_{2}^{\ell}
and

(for

), then for all

\displaystyle\widetilde{\overline{p}W}(g)

\displaystyle=(\overline{p}W)(\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}(^{\iota}g))

\displaystyle=W(\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}(^{\iota}g)\overline{p})

\displaystyle=W(\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}(^{\iota}(g(^{\iota}\overline{p}))))

\displaystyle=\widetilde{W}(g(^{\iota}\overline{p}))=(^{\iota}\overline{p}\widetilde{W})(g).

Thus if
(W,W^{\prime})\in S(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\psi)
then

(\widetilde{\overline{p}W},\widetilde{\overline{p}W^{\prime}})=(^{\iota}\overline{p}\widetilde{W},^{\iota}\overline{p}\widetilde{W^{\prime}})\in S(\rho^{\vee},\sigma^{\vee},\psi^{-1})

since
S(\rho^{\vee},\sigma^{\vee},\psi^{-1})
is
P_{2}^{\ell}
-stable and
{}^{\iota}\overline{p}\in P_{2}^{\ell}
. By the above equivalences we see that
(\overline{p}W,\overline{p}W^{\prime})\in S(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\psi)
. We conclude by noting that
P_{2}^{\ell}
and
\overline{P}_{2}^{\ell}
generate
G_{2}^{\ell}
.
∎

If

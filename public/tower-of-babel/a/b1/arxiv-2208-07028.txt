Introduction

The role of double categories as a framework for abstract or formal
category theory has been promoted by several authors
(notably by Bob Paré and Mike Shulman) and seems by now widely accepted.
Indeed, it is well known that many categorical notions and properties can be treated
in any “virtual equipment”, playing the role of the double category
\mathbb{C}\rm{at}

of functors and profunctors.

Furthermore, “all” generalized multicategories
can be seen as instances of
T-
monoids in some virtual double category

\mathbb{D}
, where
T
is a monad on
\mathbb{D}

(see in particular
[
Leinster, 2003
]
and
[
Cruttwell & Shulman, 2010
]
).
While this approach has the advantage of bringing under the same umbrella
many sorts of multicategories, at the same time it may result too general
when one is interested in some specific cases.
Indeed, the relevant aspects are in a sense hidden in the monad
T
,
and the monad itself often looks rather involved or unmanageable.
This is the case in particular for one of the most commonly arising sort
of multicategories, namely the symmetric ones, also known as (coloured) operads.

The thesis of the present paper is that the theory of double categories is actually
more directly involved in summarizing some aspects of (symmetric) multicategories
than the above mentioned perspective could suggest.
In fact, classical coloured operads
(in their non-skeletal form) are simply product-preserving (double lax) functors

M:(\mathbb{P}\rm{b}\,\Set_{f})^{\rm op}\to\mathbb{S}\rm{et}

(1)

to the double category of mappings and spans.
Thus
(\mathbb{P}\rm{b}\,\Set_{f})^{\rm op}
, the horizontal dual of the double category of
pullback squares in finite sets, is, in a sense, the “double theory” for operads.
This can be seen as a generalization of the fact that product-preserving functors

M:(\mathbb{P}\rm{b}\,\Set_{f})^{\rm op}\to\mathbb{S}\rm{q}\,\Set

(2)

are commutative monoids, where
\Set
can be replaced by any
finite-product category
{\cal S}
.

By the universal property of the module construction (see
[
Cruttwell & Shulman, 2010
]
),
which applied to
\mathbb{S}\rm{et}
gives
\mathbb{C}\rm{at}
,
one can equivalently consider, instead of (
1
), normal functors

M:(\mathbb{P}\rm{b}\,\Set_{f})^{\rm op}\to\mathbb{C}\rm{at}

(3)

While the first perspective is more suitable to treat the fibered aspects,
this one has technical advantages and is preferred in this work.

Of course, the present approach points toward a notion of generalized operad
rather different from the monad-based ones;
namely we are led to consider normal product-preserving functors

M:(\mathbb{P}\rm{b}\,{\cal C})^{\rm op}\to\mathbb{C}\rm{at}

(4)

(or to
\mathbb{C}\rm{at}\,{\cal S}
) as the basic concept.
We call such a functor a “DF operad” (on
{\cal C}
), where the prefix “DF”
stands for “double functor” and will accompany
also the various concepts generalized in this context.

Thus, the horizontal part
M_{h}
of a DF operad
M
consists
of an indexed category
M:{\cal C}^{\rm op}\to\Cat

(giving the
I
-“families”
MI
which are the domains of the “operations”)
such that

M(\Sigma I)\,\cong\,\Pi(MI)

(5)

while the vertical part
M_{v}
gives the operations themselves and their
compositions.

Among DF operads we isolate the “DF (symmetric) monoidal categories”
(in which the profunctors
M_{v}f
are representable and the associated
Beck-Chevalley condition holds)
and the “DF fibrations” (in which
M_{v}f
is co-represented by
M_{h}f
).

Typically, DF monoidal categories arise from an indexed categories

M:{\cal C}^{\rm op}\to\Cat
satisfying (
5
) and having indexed sums or products;
that is, such that substitution (or reindexing) functors
f^{*}=M_{h}f

have left or right adjoint

f_{!}\dashv f^{*}\quad\quad;\quad\quad f^{*}\dashv f_{*}

satisfying the Beck-Chevalley law.
In the case of indexed sums, the corresponding DF monoidal categories
are also DF fibrations, since profunctors represented by
f_{!}
are also
co-represented by
f^{*}
.

In the classical case
{\cal C}=\Set_{f}
,
M_{h}
is the standard indexing

{\cal A}^{I}
for a category
{\cal A}
. If
{\cal A}
has finite sums or products,
the above mentioned DF monoidal categories are of course the
usual cocartesian or cartesian monoidal categories.
On the other and, if the category
{\cal A}
has all small (co)products, we can
use
\Set
in place of
\Set_{f}
as the indexing category
{\cal C}

and we still get a (genuinely generalized) DF operad.
In this perspective, the role of the Beck-Chevalley condition is to assure that
by taking isomorphism classes of a DF monoidal category one gets
a (genuinely generalized) DF monoid

M:(\mathbb{P}\rm{b}\,{\cal C})^{\rm op}\to\mathbb{S}\rm{q}\,\Set

(6)

(recall (
2
) and see section
3.1
).
This makes it precise the idea that also infinite categorical sums or products
give a well defined algebraic structure.

The present work originates from
[
Pisani, 2022
]
, whose emphasis was on the double
fibered form of operads (see also
[
Lambert, 2021
]
).

Some remarks on double categories

We assume the basic notions about double categories and lax functors
(see for instance
[
Paré, 2011
]
,
[
Paré, 2018
]
and
[

]
).
In the present section we fix notations and highlight the facts that will be
useful in the sequel; some of them (especially those in section

)
seem to be new, or at least not widely known.

2.1
Notations and conventions

All double categories will be written with the first letter in a blackboard style.
Since a double category

are called “vertical arrows” or “proarrows”
and the arrows of
A_{1}
are the “cells” or “squares”.

\textstyle{K}

(8)

the vertical or “external” composition

]

(but not reversing

) in
\mathbb{A}
corresponds to the following cell in

:

\textstyle{I\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}

Following Paré,
\mathbb{S}\rm{et}=\mathbb{S}\rm{pan}\,\Set
is the double category of mappings
and spans and
\mathbb{C}\rm{at}
is the double category of functors and profunctors.
More generally, for a category
{\cal S}
with pullbacks,
\mathbb{C}\rm{at}\,{\cal S}

is the double category of internal categories, functors and profunctors,
obtained by applying the module construction to the double category
\mathbb{S}\rm{pan}\,{\cal S}
.
For a category
{\cal C}
,
\mathbb{S}\rm{q}\,{\cal C}
is the double category of commutative squares in
{\cal C}
,
and
\mathbb{P}\rm{b}\,{\cal C}
is the double category of pullback squares in
{\cal C}
.

2.2
Double categories with products

Following
[
Paré, 2018
]
we say that a double category
\mathbb{A}
has
(finite) products if both
A_{0}
and
A_{1}
have (finite) products
and the source and target functors (

) preserve them.

So, for any pair of proarrows
f:I\to J
and
g:K\to L
we have a diagram

\textstyle{I\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{I\times K\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{p_{2}}

\textstyle{\pi_{2}}

\textstyle{J\times L\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{q_{2}}
\textstyle{L}

(9)

encompassing a product diagram in
A_{1}
along with its source and target

which also are product diagrams.

.

There are also stronger notions of product in a double category;
this one is best suited to our needs.

Similarly, if
\mathbb{A}
has sums we have diagrams

\textstyle{I\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{i_{1}}
\scriptstyle{f}

\textstyle{\iota_{2}}

\textstyle{J+L}
\textstyle{L\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{j_{2}}

Clearly,
\mathbb{A}
has sums if and only if
\mathbb{A}^{\rm op}
has products.

Proposition 1
.

If
{\cal C}
has (finite) products, then
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{S}\rm{q}\,{\cal C}
also as (finite) products;
the same holds true for sums.

Proof.

Indeed, in
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{S}\rm{q}\,{\cal C}
the central vertical arrow of diagram (
9
)
is necessarily

f\times g
, the unique arrow making the diagram commute in
{\cal C}
,
and it gives the product in
A_{1}
(the arrow category of
{\cal C}
).
The same proof holds for sums. Note that we cannot refer naively
to duality, since
\mathbb{S}\rm{q}\,({\cal C}^{\rm op})\neq(\mathbb{S}\rm{q}\,{\cal C})^{\rm op}
; rather,

\mathbb{S}\rm{q}\,({\cal C}^{\rm op})
is the vertical dual of
(\mathbb{S}\rm{q}\,{\cal C})^{\rm op}
(
s
and
t
are also reversed).
But taking the vertical dual does not affect the existence of products or sums.
∎

Remark 2
.

Actually, the proposition is true for all those double categories
\mathbb{A}

constructed from a bipointed category
\sigma,\tau:1\to D
with

A_{0}={\cal C}
,
A_{1}={\cal C}^{D}

and with

induced by
\sigma
and
\tau
.
If
{\cal C}
has (finite) products, then
\mathbb{A}
has (finite) products, since
products in
{\cal C}^{D}
are computed pointwise.
If

is the “free arrow”

\textstyle{\bullet\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\bullet}
we get
\mathbb{S}\rm{q}\,{\cal C}
,
while if

is the “free span”

\textstyle{\bullet}

\textstyle{\bullet}
,
we get

.
Note that while the vertical structure for
\mathbb{S}\rm{q}\,{\cal C}
is induced by the pushout
(in
\Cat

\mathbb{S}\rm{pan}\,{\cal C}
.
It would be interesting to find general procedures to construct the vertical structure
for

for some sorts of bipointed categories

.

We say that a functor
F:\mathbb{A}\to\mathbb{B}
preserves products
(or that it is product-preserving) if both its components

F_{h}:A_{0}\to B_{0}
and

For instance, both the “inclusion” functors

preserve products, as can be easily seen directly;
alternatively, one may note that these functor are induced by each of the two
bipoint-preserving functors from the free span to the free arrow.

.

If the horizontal component

\scriptstyle{p_{2}}
\textstyle{K\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g}
\textstyle{\pi_{1}}
\textstyle{\pi_{2}}
\textstyle{J}
\textstyle{J\times L\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q_{1}}
\scriptstyle{q_{2}}

2.3
The double category
\mathbb{P}\rm{b}\,{\cal C}
and Mackey functors

Recall that an extensive category is a category
{\cal C}
with finite sums that
interact nicely with pullbacks (see
[

]
):
given a commutative diagram in
{\cal C}

\textstyle{I\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

.

If
{\cal C}
is extensive, then
\mathbb{P}\rm{b}\,{\cal C}
has sums and the inclusion
\mathbb{P}\rm{b}\,{\cal C}\to\mathbb{S}\rm{q}\,{\cal C}

By the extensivity of
{\cal C}
, the following is a sum diagram both in
\mathbb{S}\rm{q}\,{\cal C}

and in
\mathbb{P}\rm{b}\,{\cal C}
:

\textstyle{I\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{i_{1}}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{I+K\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f+g}
\textstyle{K\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g}
\scriptstyle{i_{2}}
\textstyle{\rm pb}
\textstyle{\rm pb}
\textstyle{J\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{j_{1}}
\textstyle{J+L}
\textstyle{L\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{j_{2}}

∎

Given two categories
{\cal C}
and
{\cal D}
, a Mackey functors

in the sense of

consists of two functors which coincide on objects

\scriptstyle{g}

\textstyle{J\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{l}
\textstyle{K}

\textstyle{FI\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F_{!}f}
\textstyle{FL\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

The following proposition is then an easy consequence of the definitions.

Proposition 4
.

For an extensive category
{\cal C}
, the Mackey functors

correspond to the finite-product-preserving functors

The correspondence is obviously given by

, preservation of products

2.4
The double category
\mathbb{C}\rm{at}

Recall that
\mathbb{C}\rm{at}
is the double category of functors and profunctors.
Following
[
Joyal, 2022
]
and
[
Paré, 2011
]
, a profunctor
\Phi:{\cal C}\to{\cal D}
is a functor
{\cal C}^{\rm op}\times{\cal D}\to\Set
,
rather than
{\cal D}^{\rm op}\times{\cal C}\to\Set
as many authors prefer.
One reason for this choice is that profunctors
{\cal C}\to{\cal D}

can be seen as categories over the free arrow category

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{1}
(the “barrels” of
[
Joyal, 2022
]
),
and in this way the domain and the codomain of the profunctor
are projected respectively on the domain and the codomain of the free arrow.

A cell in
\mathbb{C}\rm{at}

\textstyle{{\cal A}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi}
\scriptstyle{F}
\textstyle{{\cal C}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Psi}
\textstyle{{\alpha}}
\textstyle{{\cal B}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{G}
\textstyle{\cal D}

is a natural transformation
\alpha:\Phi\to\Psi(F-,G-)
, that is a family of mappings

\alpha_{X,Y}:\Phi(X,Y)\to\Psi(FX,GY)\quad\quad;\quad\quad X\in{\cal C},\,\,Y\in{\cal D}

satisfying the usual conditions.
Equivalently, it is a morphism of barrels, that is of categories over the free arrow.

A profunctor
\Phi:{\cal C}\to{\cal D}
is “representable” if it isomorphic to

\Phi_{F}={\cal D}(F-,-)
for a functor
F:{\cal C}\to{\cal D}
.
As a barrel,
\Phi
is representable if it is an opfibration over the free arrow.
The assignment
F\mapsto\Phi_{F}
defines a functor
\mathbb{S}\rm{q}\,\Cat\to\mathbb{C}\rm{at}
.
For any square in
\Cat
, we get a cell
\alpha
in
\mathbb{C}\rm{at}

\textstyle{{\cal A}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Remark 7
.

By proposition
7
, DF monoids in
\Cat

correspond to commutative monoids in
\Cat

It would be nice if general symmetric monoidal categories
could be captured by functors

M^{\prime}:(\mathbb{P}\rm{b}\,\Set_{f})^{\rm op}\to\mathbb{S}\rm{q}\,^{*}\Cat

where
\mathbb{S}\rm{q}\,^{*}\Cat

3.4
DF fibrations

A
DF fibration
is a DF operad
M:(\mathbb{P}\rm{b}\,{\cal C})^{\rm op}\to\mathbb{C}\rm{at}

such that the profunctors
\Phi_{f}=M_{v}f:MI\to MJ
are co-represented
by
f^{*}=M_{h}f
, for any
f:I\to J
in
{\cal C}
:

\Phi_{f}(X,Y)\,\cong\,MI(X,f^{*}Y)

Thus, as expected, a DF fibration is determined by its horizontal component,
that is the indexed category

I\mapsto MI\quad\quad;\quad\quad f\mapsto f^{*}

The vertical component, which as a normal lax functor
{\cal C}\to\Prof
corresponds
to a category over
{\cal C}
, embodies the associated fibration.

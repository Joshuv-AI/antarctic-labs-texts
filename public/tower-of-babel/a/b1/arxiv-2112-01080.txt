Analogs of Bol operators for 𝔭 ​ 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( a + 1 | b ) ⊂ 𝔳 ​ 𝔢 ​ 𝔠 ​ 𝔱 ​ ( a | b ) {\mathfrak{pgl}}(a+1|b)\subset{\mathfrak{vect}}(a|b)
Sofiane Bouarroudj a∗ , Dimitry Leites a,b , Irina Shchepochkina c

Abstract
. Bol operators (Bols for short) are differential operators invariant under the projective action of 𝔭 ​ 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) ≃ 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) \mathfrak{pgl}(2)\simeq\mathfrak{sl}(2) between spaces of weighted densities on the 1-dimensional manifold. Here, we described analogs of Bols: 𝔭 ​ 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( a + 1 | b ) \mathfrak{pgl}(a+1|b) -invariant differential operators between spaces of tensor fields on ( a | b ) (a|b) -dimensional supermanifolds with irreducible, as 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( a | b ) \mathfrak{gl}(a|b) -modules, fibers of arbitrary, even infinite, dimension for certain “key” values of a a and b b — the ones for which the solution is describable. We discovered many new operators for ( a | b ) = ( 2 | 0 ) , ( 0 | 3 ) (a|b)=(2|0),(0|3) and for the case of 1 | 1 1|1 -dimensional general superstring which looks like a most natural superization of Bol’s result, additional to the cases of super analogs of Bols between spaces of weighted densities on the 1 | n 1|n -dimensional superstrings with a contact structure we classified in arXiv:2110.10504. In the case of fibers of dimension > 1 >1 , there are ( a + b − 1 ) (a+b-1) -parameter families of Bols, whereas there are no non-scalar non-zero differential operators between spaces of weighted densities. These two extreme answers justify the selection of cases here.

Restricted and unrestricted duals

Let

Recall the following isomorphisms of the dual spaces

We will also need the dual spaces of tensor fields expressed in the same terms (for example, spaces of integrable forms dual to the spaces of differential forms). Researchers who, for whatever reason, would like to express the dual of the space of polynomials as a space of polynomials, not power series, introduced

of

f(y)\mathop{\mathrm{{v\hskip-0.43057pto\hskip-0.08612ptl\/}}}\nolimits(y)\mapsto f(y(x))\cdot\det\left(\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.25pt}{$\dfrac{\partial y}{\partial x}$}}{\raisebox{0.25pt}{$\dfrac{\partial y}{\partial x}$}}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{$\tfrac{\partial y}{\partial x}$}}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{$\tfrac{\partial y}{\partial x}$}}\right)\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{{v\hskip-0.43057pto\hskip-0.08612ptl\/}}}\nolimits(y(x)).

2.2.
On invariant operators and singular vectors

To every invariant differential operator

, where
X
and
Y
are
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
-modules with lowest weight vectors, there corresponds the dual over the ground field operator

since

and

. Then, to a non-zero

; the

-highest weight vector of this submodule is called

. So, a non-zero vector

of degree
i
in a basis of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}
— we will also say that
i
is the
level
of

— is singular if and only if it is
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
-highest weight vector, and
{\mathfrak{g}}_{>}(f_{i})=0
.

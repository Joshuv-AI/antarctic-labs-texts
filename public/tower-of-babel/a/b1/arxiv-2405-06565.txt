Theory of interacting vector dark energy and fluid
Masroor C. Pookkillath, Kazuya Koyama

Abstract
In this work, we study interaction between dark energy and dark matter, where dark energy is described by a massive vector field, and dark matter is modelled as a fluid. We present a new interaction term, which affects only perturbations and can give interesting phenomenology. Then we present a general Lagrangian for the interacting vector dark energy with dark matter. For the dark energy, we choose Proca theory with G 3 G_{3} term to study its phenomenological consequence. For this model, we explore both background and perturbation dynamics. We also present the no-ghost condition for tensor modes, vector modes and scalar modes. Subsequently, we also study the evolution of the overdensities of both baryon and cold dark matter in the high − k -k limit. We show that the effective gravitational coupling is modified for cold dark matter and baryon. We also choose a simple concrete model and numerically show a suppression of the effective gravitational coupling for cold dark matter. However, in this simple model, the suppression of the effective gravitational coupling does not result in a suppression of the matter overdensity compared to that in the Λ \Lambda CDM model due to the modified background expansion.

II
Interacting fluid description

The theory of an interacting fluid with the scalar field (dark energy) is discussed in
[
54
]
, where the cold dark matter fluid is described by a “pull-back” formalism (for the details of pull-back formalism, refer to
[
55
]
). For the interacting fluid with a scalar field dark energy, the general action is

L=L(n_{\alpha\beta\gamma},u^{\mu},\phi,\nabla_{\nu}\phi)\,,

(1)

where
n_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
is a full anti-symmetric tensor, which is dual to the number density of the dark matter fluid
n^{\alpha}
,

n_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}n^{\delta}\,,

(2)

and,
u^{\mu}
is the fluid velocity and
\phi
is the scalar field, which describes the dark energy. In this section, we develop an interaction of the fluid with the dark energy field, which originated from a broken
U(1)
gauge symmetry vector field.

II.1
Building blocks for interacting vector dark energy

Since we are interested in a dark energy model as a vector theory, the above Lagrangian (
1
) can be rewritten assuming no additional changes in the fluid sector.

L=L(n_{\alpha},u^{\mu},A_{\nu},\nabla_{\mu}A_{\nu},\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta})\,,

(3)

where we have included the
\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
since we will use
F_{\mu\nu}
, which is an anti-symmetric term,

F_{\mu\nu}\equiv\nabla_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\nabla_{\nu}A_{\mu}\,.

(4)

From the combination of
\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
and
F_{\mu\nu}
, we can define the following dual tensor

\tilde{F}_{\alpha\beta}\equiv\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\,.

(5)

We can also have the dual fluid number density given by Eq. (
2
). Here, we explicitly present all the possible interactions that could be possible with the vector dark energy with dark matter.

The idea is to construct invariants from the basic fields that the Lagrangian is depending on. First, let us look at the following invariant from the combinations of
A_{\mu}
and
u^{\mu}
.

\mathcal{Z}\equiv-A_{\mu}u^{\mu}\,.

(6)

This is equivalent to the term considered in
[
54
]
with a scalar field.
Since we have the tensor
F_{\mu\nu}
which is anti-symmetric, we can further have other invariants from the combination of
F_{\mu\nu}
,
n_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
, and
u^{\alpha}

\mathcal{A}_{p}\equiv F^{\beta\gamma}n_{\alpha\beta\gamma}u^{\alpha}\,.

(7)

The above interaction term is parity violating. Including the
\epsilon
tensor, we can have the following invariants as well

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\beta\gamma\delta\varepsilon}F^{\beta\gamma}n_{\alpha}{}^{\delta\varepsilon}u^{\alpha}=4F_{\alpha\beta}n^{\alpha}u^{\beta},

(8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\alpha\gamma\delta\varepsilon}F^{\beta\gamma}n_{\beta}{}^{\delta\varepsilon}u^{\alpha}=4F_{\alpha\beta}n^{\alpha}u^{\beta}\,,

(9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\beta\gamma\delta\varepsilon}F_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}n^{\gamma\delta\varepsilon}u^{\alpha}=-6F_{\alpha\beta}n^{\alpha}u^{\beta}\,.

(10)

Instead of

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle n_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\tilde{F}^{\beta\gamma}u^{\alpha}=4F_{\alpha\beta}n^{\alpha}u^{\beta}\,.

(11)

Now adding the
\epsilon

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{p1}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\beta\gamma\delta\varepsilon}n^{\gamma\delta\varepsilon}\tilde{F}_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}u^{\alpha}=\epsilon_{\beta\gamma\delta\alpha}F^{\gamma\delta}n^{\alpha}u^{\beta}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{p2}

\displaystyle\equiv

,

, and

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle A^{\alpha}\epsilon_{\beta\gamma\delta\varepsilon}F^{\beta\gamma}n_{\alpha}{}^{\delta\varepsilon}=-4A^{\alpha}F_{\alpha\beta}n^{\beta}\,\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{2}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle A^{\alpha}\epsilon_{\alpha\gamma\delta\varepsilon}F^{\beta\gamma}n_{\beta}{}^{\delta\varepsilon}=-2A^{\alpha}F_{\alpha\beta}n^{\beta}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{3}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle A^{\alpha}\epsilon_{\beta\gamma\delta\varepsilon}F_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}n^{\gamma\delta\varepsilon}=6A^{\alpha}F_{\alpha\beta}n^{\beta}\,.

Considering the dual tensor
\tilde{F}_{\alpha\beta}

\mathcal{F}\equiv A^{\alpha}n_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\tilde{F}^{\beta\gamma}=-4A^{\alpha}F_{\alpha\beta}n^{\beta}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{p1}

\displaystyle A^{\alpha}\epsilon_{\beta\gamma\delta\varepsilon}n^{\gamma\delta\varepsilon}\tilde{F}_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}=6A^{\alpha}\epsilon_{\alpha\gamma\delta\beta}F^{\gamma\delta}n^{\beta}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{p3}

\displaystyle\equiv

III
An interacting Lagrangian

Now we can write the interacting Lagrangian that does not violate the parity as

L=L\left(\tilde{n},\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{E}\right)\,,

(28)

where

\tilde{n}\equiv n^{2}=\frac{1}{3!}n^{\alpha\beta\gamma}n_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\,,

(29)

and is called the fluid number density.

The Lagrangian that we have considered is completely general. In the following, we assume that the dark energy is described by the
G_{3}
Proca theory, together with fluid interaction as described above, and explore the physical consequences of the new interaction terms. With matter minimal coupling to gravity, we have the following action.

S_{\rm tot}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{M_{\rm P}^{2}}{2}R-\frac{1}{4}F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}+G_{3}(\mathcal{Y})\nabla_{\mu}A^{\mu}+f(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{E})\right]+S_{\rm c}+S_{\rm m}\,,

(30)

In the above action Eq. (
30
), the kinetic terms are separated for each propagating mode, and then an interacting action is also added.
Note that we can generalise the action Eq. (
30

G_{3}
term in generalized Proca theory
[

,
47
]

(31)

where
n_{\text{m}}
are defined as

\displaystyle n_{\text{m}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{-g_{\mu\nu}J_{\text{m}}^{\mu}J_{\text{m}}^{\nu}}\,,

(32)

and

J^{\mu}\equiv n_{\rm m}u_{\rm m}^{\mu}\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad u_{{\rm m}\alpha}u_{\rm m}^{\alpha}=-1\,.

(33)

To model the interacting dark sector, we assume that the dark matter is described via the dust fluid as introduced in

IV.1
Background

Let us look at the background cosmology. We assume an isotropic and homogeneous flat background, Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker(FLRW) space-time with linear perturbations,

\displaystyle{\rm d}s^{2}=g_{\mu\nu}{\rm d}x^{\mu}{\rm d}x^{\nu}=a^{2}\left[-(1+2\alpha){\rm d}t^{2}+2\frac{1}{a}\partial_{i}\chi{\rm d}x^{i}{\rm d}t+\left\{(1+2\zeta)\delta_{ij}+2\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\left(E/a^{2}\right)\right\}{\rm d}x^{i}{\rm d}x^{j}\right]\,.

(36)

For the Proca field
A_{\mu}
we define

A^{\mu}=\left[\frac{1}{a}(\phi+\delta\phi),\frac{1}{a^{2}}\delta^{ij}\partial_{j}\delta\phi_{s}\right]\,.

(37)

Now for the cold dark matter fluid part we have the fields
n^{\alpha}
and

n_{c}^{\alpha}=\left[\frac{1}{a}n_{0}(1+\delta n_{0}),\frac{1}{a^{2}}\delta^{ij}\partial_{j}n_{s}\right]\,,\qquad K=K_{0}+\delta K\,.

(38)

Finally, we have other matter action for baryons and photons

\displaystyle J_{\text{m}}^{\nu}=\left[\frac{J_{\text{m}}}{a}\left(1+\delta J_{\text{m}}\right),\frac{1}{a^{2}}\delta^{ij}\partial_{j}\delta j_{\text{m}}\right]\,,\qquad l_{\text{m}}=\bar{l}_{\text{m}}+\delta l_{\text{m}}\,.

(39)

For the cold dark matter sector, we have

n_{0}=\frac{N_{\rm c}}{a^{3}}\,,

(40)

K_{0}=-2\int a(\eta)n_{0}(\eta)\frac{\partial\rho_{c}}{\partial\tilde{n}}{\rm d}\eta\,.

(41)

For the matter sector (from

\displaystyle J_{\rm m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{N_{\rm m}}{a^{3}}\,,

(42)

\displaystyle\bar{l}_{\text{m}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\rho\left(n_{\rm m}\right)_{,n_{\text{m}}}a\left(\eta\right)\,\text{d}\eta\,.

(43)

Having substituted the matter equations in the action, we can get the Friedmann equations. The first Friedmann equation,

\displaystyle 3M_{\rm P}^{2}H^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{\rm m}+\rho_{\rm c}-f-3H\phi^{3}G_{3,\mathcal{Y}}-\phi f_{,\mathcal{Z}}-2\mathcal{Y}f_{,\mathcal{Y}}\,,

\displaystyle 2M_{\rm P}^{2}\frac{\dot{H}}{a}

\displaystyle-n_{\rm m}\rho_{,n_{\rm m}}+2n_{0}^{2}\rho_{c,\tilde{n}}+\phi^{2}\frac{\dot{\phi}}{a}G_{3,\mathcal{Y}}-\left(3H\phi^{3}G_{3,\mathcal{Y}}+\phi f_{,\mathcal{Z}}+2\mathcal{Y}f_{,\mathcal{Y}}\right),

(45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\rho_{\rm m}+P_{\rm m})-\rho_{\rm c}-(\rho_{\rm DE}+P_{\rm DE}),\,

where we have used the relation

n_{\rm m}\rho_{,n_{\rm m}}=\rho_{\rm m}+P_{\rm m}\,,\qquad 2n_{0}^{2}\rho_{c,\tilde{n}}=\rho_{c},

(46)

where
P_{\rm m}
is the pressure of matter. The density of DE is

\rho_{\rm DE}=-f-3H\phi^{3}G_{3,\mathcal{Y}}-\phi f_{,\mathcal{Z}}-2\mathcal{Y}f_{,\mathcal{Y}}\,,

(47)

and the sum of DE density and pressure is

\rho_{\rm DE}+P_{\rm DE}=-\phi^{2}\frac{\dot{\phi}}{a}G_{3,\mathcal{Y}}+\left(3H\phi^{3}G_{3,\mathcal{Y}}+\phi f_{,\mathcal{Z}}+2\mathcal{Y}f_{,\mathcal{Y}}\right).

(48)

Finally, we have the equation for the field
\phi
, which is given as

3H\phi^{3}G_{3,\mathcal{Y}}+\phi f_{,\mathcal{Z}}+2\mathcal{Y}f_{,\mathcal{Y}}=0\,.

(49)

Notice that from the equations of motion for
\phi
, for the dark energy content
\rho_{\rm DE}+P_{\rm DE}=-\phi^{2}G_{3,\mathcal{Y}}\tfrac{\dot{\phi}}{a}
, and
\rho_{\rm DE}=-f
. In other words, the dark energy equation of state is given by

w_{\rm DE}=-1+\frac{\phi^{2}G_{3,\mathcal{Y}}}{f}\frac{\dot{\phi}}{a}\,.

(50)

On top of it, it is interesting to notice that at the background level we do not see the consequences of the interaction,
\mathcal{E}
. However, we need to see how the interactions influence the linear perturbation sector.

IV.2
Perturbation

In this section we study the ghost instability condition and the Laplace instability conditions for tensor, vector and scalar modes for the theory we are considering Eq. (
30
).

IV.2.1
Tensor modes

In this subsection, we study the propagation of the tensor mode in this theory. We consider the tensor mode perturbation around the FLRW background,

{\rm d}s^{2}=a(t)^{2}\left[-{\rm d}t^{2}+\left(\delta_{ij}+\sum_{\lambda=+,\times}\epsilon_{ij}^{\lambda}h_{\lambda}\right){\rm d}x^{i}{\rm d}x^{j}\right]\,,

(51)

where the tensor mode perturbations obey standard trace-less and divergence free gauge condition
\delta_{ij}\epsilon_{ij}=0
, and
\epsilon_{ij}^{\lambda}\delta^{jl}\partial_{l}h_{\lambda}=0
, and also it obeys the normalisation condition
\epsilon_{ij}^{+}\epsilon_{kl}^{+}\delta^{ik}\delta^{jl}=\epsilon_{ij}^{\times}\epsilon_{kl}^{\times}\delta^{ik}\delta^{jl}=1
, with
\epsilon_{ij}^{+}\epsilon_{kl}^{\times}\delta^{ik}\delta^{jl}=0
. Now expanding the action Eq. (
30
) with the above metric Eq. (
51
), and substituting the background equations of motion, we get the following reduced action.

S_{\rm T}=\frac{M_{\rm P}^{2}}{4}\sum_{\lambda=+,\times}\int{\rm d}^{4}x\,a^{4}\left[\frac{\dot{h}_{\lambda}^{2}}{a^{2}}-\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}h_{\lambda}^{2}\right]\,,

(52)

where we have changed the action into Fourier space, and
k
is the Fourier mode.

Hence, the above expression proves that the tensor mode propagation is not affected.
We note that the new term in the dark matter fluid Lagrangian,
n^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}K
, which corresponds to the conservation equation, is crucial to obtain this result. Without this term, we can show that the tensor mode acquires a mass term incorrectly.

IV.2.2
Vector mode

In this part, we study the no-ghost condition and Laplace instability condition for the vector mode. The theory we are considering is an interaction between dark energy and dark matter, where dark energy described by a massive vector field. We need to check any new non-trivial stability conditions emerges from the propagation of the vector modes. To study the vector mode, we consider a flat-FLRW metric with vector perturbation

{\rm d}s^{2}=-a^{2}{\rm d}t^{2}+a^{2}\delta_{ij}{\rm d}x^{i}{\rm d}x^{j}+2a^{2}V_{i}{\rm d}x^{i}{\rm d}t\,.

(53)

The vector field
A^{\mu}
is given by

A^{\mu}=\left(\frac{\phi}{a},\frac{\delta^{ij}}{a^{2}}E_{j}\right)\,.

(54)

For the dark matter fluid we define

n_{\rm c}^{\mu}=\left(\frac{n_{0}}{a},\frac{\delta_{ij}}{a^{2}}W_{j}{}^{\rm c}\right)\,,

(55)

and for the standard matter we define

J^{\mu}=\left(\frac{J_{\rm m}}{a},\frac{\delta_{ij}}{a^{2}}W_{j}{}^{\rm m}\right)\,.

(56)

The quantities
V_{i}
,
E_{i}
,
W_{i}{}^{\rm c}
, and
W_{i}{}^{\rm m}
obeys the transverse conditions

\partial_{i}V^{i}=\partial_{i}E^{i}=\partial_{i}W^{i}{}^{\rm c}=\partial_{i}W^{i}{}^{\rm m}=0\,.

(57)

For the convenience of the calculations, we assume the vector fields depends on
t
and
z
only, such that we can keep the third component of the vector fields to zero. We also choose,

\mathcal{A}_{1}{}^{\rm c,m}=\delta\mathcal{A}_{1}{}^{\rm c,m}(t,z)\,,\quad\,\mathcal{A}_{2}{}^{\rm c,m}=\delta\mathcal{A}_{2}{}^{\rm c,m}(t,z)\,,\quad\,\mathcal{B}_{1}{}^{\rm c,m}=x+\delta\mathcal{B}_{1}{}^{\rm c,m}(t,z)\,,\quad\,\mathcal{B}_{2}{}^{\rm c,m}=y+\delta\mathcal{B}_{2}{}^{\rm c,m}(t,z)\,.

(58)

Due to the transverse conditions, the third component of
\mathcal{A}_{i}{}^{\rm c,m}\nabla\mathcal{B}^{i}{}^{\rm c,m}
,
\mathcal{A}_{3}\nabla_{\mu}\mathcal{B}_{3}{}^{\rm c,m}
can be removed since it is redundant.

Now we can expand the action up to second order. Then varying it with respect to
W_{i}{}^{\rm m}
we get

W_{i}{}^{\rm m}=-an_{\rm m}V_{i}+\frac{n_{\rm m}}{\rho(n_{\rm m})_{,n_{\rm m}}}\mathcal{A}_{i}{}^{\rm m}\,.

(59)

Substituting back the
W_{i}{}^{\rm m}
to the action and varying the action with respect to the field
\mathcal{A}_{i}{}^{\rm m}
we get

\mathcal{A}_{i}{}^{\rm m}=a(V_{i}-\dot{\mathcal{B}}_{i}{}^{\rm m})\rho(n_{\rm m})_{n_{\rm m}}\,,

(60)

and varying with respect to the field
\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{\rm m}
we get

\dot{\mathcal{A}}_{i}{}^{\rm m}=0\,.

(61)

This show that
\mathcal{A}_{i}{}^{\rm m}
is constant that is

a(V_{i}-\dot{\mathcal{B}}_{i}{}^{\rm m})\rho(n_{\rm m})_{n_{\rm m}}=\text{constant}\,.

(62)

Now from the dark matter sector varying the action with respect to the field
W_{i}{}^{\rm c}
we get

\mathcal{A}_{i}{}^{\rm c}=2an_{\rm c}V_{i}+2W_{i}{}^{\rm c}+\left(\frac{Y_{i}}{n_{\rm c}}-\frac{a\phi}{n_{\rm c}}V_{i}-\frac{\phi}{n_{\rm c}{}^{2}}W_{i}{}^{\rm c}\right)f_{,\mathcal{Z}}+\frac{\phi}{n_{\rm c}}\frac{\dot{Y}_{i}}{a}f_{,\mathcal{E}}\,,

(63)

where

Y_{i}\equiv E_{i}+a\phi V_{i}\,.

(64)

Following similar procedure for the standard matter fluid as done before, we substitute
W_{i}{}^{\rm c}
from the above constraint equation into the action. Then we can find the equation of motion for the field

\mathcal{A}_{i}{}^{\rm c}=2n_{\rm c}aV_{i}\rho(n_{\rm c})_{,n_{\rm c}}-2n_{\rm c}a\dot{\mathcal{B}}_{i}{}^{\rm c}+\left(\frac{Y_{i}}{n_{\rm c}}-\frac{a\phi}{n_{\rm c}}V_{i}+\frac{a\phi}{n_{\rm c}}\dot{\mathcal{B}}_{i}{}^{\rm c}\right)f_{,\mathcal{Z}}+\frac{\phi}{n_{\rm c}}\frac{\dot{Y}_{i}}{a}f_{,\mathcal{E}}\,.

(65)

Now varying the action with respect to the field
\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{c}
we get

\dot{\mathcal{A}}_{i}{}^{\rm c}=0\,.

(66)

This shows that
\mathcal{A}_{i}{}^{\rm c}
is constant. That is

(67)

By varying the action with respect to the field
V_{i}
we get

\frac{1}{2}M_{\rm P}^{2}a^{2}k^{2}V_{i}+2a^{4}\tilde{n}^{2}\left(V_{i}-\delta\dot{\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{\rm c}}\right)+2a^{4}n_{\rm m}\left(V_{i}-\delta\dot{\mathcal{B}}_{i}{}^{\rm m}\right)-\phi a^{4}\left(V_{i}-\delta\dot{\mathcal{B}}_{i}{}^{\rm c}\right)f_{,\mathcal{Z}}+a^{3}f_{,\mathcal{Z}}Y_{i}+a^{2}\phi\dot{Y}_{i}f_{,\mathcal{E}}=0\,.

(68)

On substituting back the
V_{i}

(k\rightarrow\infty)
we can find the action contains

S_{V}{}^{(2)}\simeq\sum_{i=1,2}\int{\rm d}t{\rm d}^{3}xa^{2}\left[Q_{V}\left(\frac{\dot{Y}_{i}^{2}}{a^{2}}-c_{V}^{2}\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}Y_{i}^{2}\right)\right]\,,

(69)

where

(70)

This shows that, there is no additional vector mode present, but the kinetic term is modified. Hence, the condition for no-ghost is given by,

Q_{V}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rho_{\rm c}+\phi\left(f_{,\mathcal{Z}}-\phi f_{,\mathcal{E}}\right)}{\rho_{\rm c}+\phi\left(f_{,\mathcal{Z}}\right)}>0\,.

(71)

This also ensures the avoidance of Laplacian instabilities for the propagating vector modes

. Notice that we have started from the vector fields, whose
z
component is zero. Hence, we have two vector modes that propagating in the
z
direction.

VIII
Conclusion

In this work, we have constructed a theory of interacting dark energy and dark matter fluid (Section
II
). The dark energy is described by a massive vector field, and the dark matter is described by a perfect fluid. We found a new interaction,
\mathcal{E}=A^{\alpha}F_{\alpha\beta}u^{\beta}
, which have not been explored. On top of this interaction, we also find the interactions that break the parity symmetry. On the basis of the parity-preserving interaction terms, we construct a general Lagrangian in Section
III
. Then, we introduced a toy model to understand the consequence of newly introduced interactions, where the dark energy action is a cubic generalised Proca theory.

Then we studied the background evolution and linear perturbation equations of motion in Section
IV
. We also derived the no ghost condition for the tensor, vector and scalar modes. Due to the term
\nabla_{\mu}Kn^{\mu}
in the dark matter action, this model does not modify the continuity equation, but it modifies the Euler equation. This indicates that the interaction introduces only a momentum transfer. Then, in Section
V
, we derived the linear equations of motion in Newtonian gauge.

To understand the phenomenological features of the theory, we study the evolution of baryon and cold dark matter in the high
k
limit in Section
VI
. We show that the evolution of cold dark matter is modified as a result of the interaction. In particular, the effective gravitational coupling to the cold dark matter
G_{\rm cc}
is modified. In the limit of
G_{3}\rightarrow 0
, we do not have any modification. For the evolution of baryons, we show that there are modifications similar to that of cold dark matter, particularly effective gravitational coupling
G_{\rm bb}
is modified.

We also introduced a simple concrete model for the free function
f
introduced in the Lagrangian in Section
VII
, and studied the ghost conditions and speed of propagation of the dark energy scalar mode and vector modes. We also showed the behaviour of the modified effective gravitational couplings
G_{\rm bb}
and
G_{\rm cc}
. We showed that the effective gravitational coupling
G_{\rm cc}
was reduced. However, the effective gravitational coupling to baryons
G_{\rm bb}
increases. The difference in the behaviour of the effective gravitational coupling to baryon and dark matter, when dark energy interacts only with dark matter, is a generic feature of interacting dark energy models as these models violate the equivalence principle
[
66
,
29
,
53
]
. We numerically solved the evolution equation of baryon and cold dark matter over-densities and showed that the evolution of the cold dark matter was suppressed due to the interaction, as a result the total matter overdensity
\delta_{M}
is also suppressed. To understand whether this model can address the
S_{8}
tension or not, we also plotted the evolution of the total matter density in the
\Lambda
CDM model. We noticed that,
\delta_{M}
in this model, is enhanced in comparison with the
\Lambda
CDM model with the same
\Omega_{\rm DE,0}
. This is due to the modification in the background cosmology. To understand this behaviour better, we also show in Figure
6
, the purple line, which corresponds to the overdensity
\delta_{m}
in the interacting Proca model in the background without modifications to the effective Newton constants. The modification in the background changes the coefficient of the fiction term in the second order matter overdensity equation, which causes the enhancement with respect to the total matter density in
\Lambda
CDM (the black line in the figure). The total matter overdensity in this model is suppressed due to the suppression of the effective Newton constant for cold dark matter (the green line in the figure), but this suppression is not sufficient. The observable
\sigma_{8}(S_{8})
is determined by the total matter overdensity
\delta_{\rm M}
as gravitational lensing is determined by the gravitational potential, which is sourced by the total matter density. Since the evolution of
\delta_{M}
of this model is not suppressed with respect to that of
\Lambda
CDM, as shown in Figure
6
, to address the
S_{8}
tension, we need to extend the concrete model to see if this model can further suppress the growth of structure to address the
S_{8}
tension. We note that Ref.
[
53
]
considered an extension of the
\mathcal{Z}
interaction to the simplest one considered in this paper and showed that it is possible to suppress the growth compared with
\Lambda
CDM. We also need to constrain
\Omega_{\rm DE,0}
from the background observations to make a fair comparison with the
\Lambda
CDM prediction. We leave this for future work. We stress that all the formulae that are necessary to check the stability conditions and the modifications to the effective Newton constants were derived for the general Lagrangian given by Eq. (
30
) in this paper.

There are several future directions based on the current work. It is also interesting to study the implications of the interaction between the vector dark energy and dark matter with parity-violating interacting terms.
It will also be interesting to see if this model can address the Hubble and large-scale structure
S_{8}
tension simultaneously by extending the concrete model, modelling this as the early interacting dark sector. Another interesting direction is to study non-linear cosmology, which is important in the light of the upcoming cosmological surveys like Euclid
[
67
]
, Square Kilometer Array
[
68
]
, and Rubin observatory’s LSST
[
69
]
. It is also interesting to find new interactions with vector dark energy models with a disformal coupling to dark matter
[
70
,
71
]
.

(180)

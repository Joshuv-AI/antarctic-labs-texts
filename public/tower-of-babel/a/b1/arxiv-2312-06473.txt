Pathwise Uniqueness for Multiplicative Young and Rough Differential Equations Driven by Fractional Brownian Motion
Toyomu Matsuda, Avi Mayorcas

Abstract
We show pathwise uniqueness of multiplicative SDEs, in arbitrary dimensions, driven by fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter H ∈ ( 1 / 3 , 1 ) H\in(1/3,1) with volatility coefficient σ \sigma that is at least γ \gamma -Hölder continuous for γ > 1 2 ​ H ∨ 1 − H H \gamma>\frac{1}{2H}\vee\frac{1-H}{H} . This improves upon the long-standing results of [ Lyo94 , Lyo98 , Dav08 ] which cover the same regime but require σ \sigma to be at least 1 H \frac{1}{H} -Hölder continuous. Our central innovation is to combine stochastic averaging estimates with refined versions of the stochastic sewing lemma, due to [ Lê20 , Ger22 , MP22 ] . Keywords and phrases. Stochastic differential equations, fractional Brownian motion, rough paths, stochastic sewing, regularisation by noise. MSC 2020 . 60H10, 60G22, 60L20, 60H50.

Introduction

This paper is concerned with
pathwise
uniqueness of solutions to multiplicative SDEs driven by fractional Brownian motion (fBm),

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}X_{t}

\displaystyle=\sigma(X_{t})\,\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}^{H},\quad X_{0}=x\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}},

(1)

where
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\ni x\mapsto\sigma(x)
takes values in the space of
d_{1}\times d_{2}
matrices and

B^{H}
is a
d_{2}
-dimensional fBm with Hurst parameter
H\in(1/3,1)
. We interpret (
1
) as either a Young differential equation, when
H\in(1/2,1)
or a rough differential equation when
H\in(1/3,1/2]
. The tools developed herein naturally extend to the even rougher case
H\in(1/4,1/3)
, however, for the sake of concision they are not presented, see Rem.
1.4
for a further discussion. Our main result is to obtain
pathwise
uniqueness of Young (resp. rough) solutions to (
1
) for uniformly elliptic, volatility functions
\sigma
which are
\gamma
-Hölder continuous for all

\gamma>\max\left\{\frac{1}{2H},\frac{1-H}{H}\right\}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2H},\quad&\text{if }H\in(1/2,1),\\
\frac{1-H}{H},&\text{if }H\in(1/3,1/2].\end{cases}

The ellipticity condition on
\sigma
is natural since both the Young and rough interpretation of (
1
) are natural extensions of the Stratonovich interpretation, applicable when
H=1/2
, which also requires ellipticity on
\sigma
in order to generically guarantee well-posedness. See Rem.
1.3
for a further discussion of this assumption. We also refer the reader to Rem.
1.7
, Def.
3.5
and Def.
4.5
for further discussion and precise definition of
pathwise
uniqueness in this context, for
H\neq 1/2
.
At this stage, we simply note that
pathwise
uniqueness under the above condition is the first major improvement on well-posedness theory for multiplicative SDEs driven by fBm since
[
Lyo94
,
Lyo98
,
Dav08
]
, which together required
\gamma\geq 1/H
. Hence, our result significantly reduces the regularity requirement on
\sigma
in arbitrary dimensions. In fact, our present work improves the recent result
[
MP22
, Thm. 5.2]
, which treats the regime
H\in(1/2,1)
and assumes the technical condition
\gamma>\frac{(1-H)(2-H)}{H(3-H)}
.

Our work fits into the growing literature on the topic of
regularisation by noise
. This is the phenomenon by which rapidly oscillating and typically random data lead to improved existence, uniqueness or other desirable properties of equations which would otherwise be ill-posed or behave pathologically in some manner,
[
Fla11
,
Tzv16
,
Fla17
,
GT20
,
FL21
,

]
.

In recent years there has been significant progress in the study of SDEs with irregular coefficients and driven by noise lying outside of the Brownian (or even martingale) class, for which we give a non-exhaustive list of references
[
CG16
,
BM19
,
ABM20
,
Lê20
,
HP21
,
MP22
,
Ger22
,
DG22
,
GG22
,

]
. More precisely, our work fits within this latter programme and considers an, as yet, less studied regime for regularisation by noise; namely the interaction between noise and irregular multiplicative coefficients.
To the best of the authors’ knowledge, the only other work in this direction is
[
HTV22
]
in which equations with more general noise are studied but under more stringent dimensional and structural assumptions on the volatility coefficient. Although not as directly related, we also highlight the work
[
GH22
]
which obtained regularisation by noise for SDEs with multiplicative Brownian noise under the inclusion of an additive fBm.
More widely, we point out that equations of the type (
1
) are of interest in a number of applications, most notably in the study of stochastic volatility
[
CR96
,
CCR12
,
FSW22
]
, turbulent fluids
[

When
H=1/2
there are two natural interpretations of (
1
), either as a stochastic, Itô equation or Stranovich equation. These correspond to two interpretations of the deterministically, ill-posed integration of
\sigma(X_{t})
against
\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}\coloneqq\,\mathrm{d}B^{1/2}_{t}
. Crucially, Itô solutions are martingales while Stratonovich integration respects the chain rule but does not produce martingale solutions. The notion of
pathwise
uniqueness is equally applicable to both, however, since both interpretations produce solutions adapted to the natural filtration of the Brownian motion. In Itô’s theory it is readily seen that
pathwise
uniqueness holds for (
1
) (with
H=1/2
) provided
\sigma
is Lipschitz continuous, see for example
[
RY99
]
. This condition provides a natural sanity check on our condition as they agree at
H=1/2
. We mention here that the result for Itô solutions does not require any ellipticity assumption on
\sigma
, while the same statement holds for Stratonovich solutions, under the additional requirement of ellipticity on
\sigma
, see Rem.
1.3
. As will be remarked below, this structural condition in the Stratonovich case can be relaxed at the price of requiring
\sigma
to be twice differentiable and bounded.

When
H\neq 1/2
Itô’s theory no longer applies to (
1
) since the fBm is not a martingale process, see Sec.
2.5
. However, the Stratonovich interpretation naturally extends beyond the martingale case. Note that for
H>1/2
no probabilistic theory is actually required to make sense of the equation, since, under sufficient regularity assumptions on
\sigma
, one expects
t\mapsto\sigma(X_{t})
to carry the same Hölder regularity as the fBm, in this case larger than
1/2
and so Young integration theory classically applies; see Sec.
2.2
. In our case the regularity assumption on
\sigma
is too weak to naively apply this theory, however, our main contribution in this regime is to carefully exploit a combination of deterministic analysis and probabilistic averaging tools to indeed construct a sufficiently regular integral, see Sec.
3.1
. For
H<1/2
the natural extension of the Stratonovich integral is provided by
[
Lyo98
,
CQ02
]
.

Remark 1.3

(Ellipticity Assumption)
.

In Itô’s theory, applicable in the case
H=1/2
, if
\sigma
is at least Lipschitz continuous, then no ellipticity is required in order to obtain existence and uniqueness of solutions. However, if one treats the SDE (
1
) as a Stratonovich equation, the elliptic condition becomes necessary to obtain existence and uniqueness under the same regularity assumption. Alternatively, one can remove ellipticity at the price of increased regularity on
\sigma
, this corresponds to the
\gamma>\frac{1}{H}
threshold discussed earlier. Since our treatment, beyond the Brownian case, relies on either Young or rough integration both of which generalise the Stratonovich integral and aims for improved regularity assumptions, we also rely on an ellipticity assumption for
\sigma
.

For simplicity of presentation, in Thm.
1.1
(similarly Thm.
3.8
and Thm.
4.10
) this is stated as uniform ellipticity and our proofs only explicitly treat this case. However, a simple stopping time argument easily allows one to relax this assumption to obtain well-posedness on any fixed time interval to simple ellipticity. We refer to the proof of
[
MP22
, Thm. 5.2]
for an exposition of this idea in a similar setting. Since our main focus is the regularity of
\sigma
we do not repeat the argument in our paper.

Remark 1.4

(Rougher and Smoother Noise Regimes)
.

It is natural to expect that Thm
1.1
can be extended to the case
H\in(1/4,1/3]
. However, since this would require the treatment of 3
rd
order iterated integrals (as opposed to 2
nd
order as required in Sec.
4
) it would significantly increase the length technicality of our proofs. Therefore, in the interests of keeping the paper somewhat concise we do not present this further extension. It is worth noting that
1/4
is likely to be a significant barrier, since even the canonical lift of an fBm to a rough path is not defined below this threshold, see
[
CQ02
]
.

Similarly, one could consider the extended Young regime, i.e.
H\in[1,\infty)\setminus\mathbb{N}
(see for example
[
DG22
,
GG22
]
). Again, in order to clarify presentation and avoid additional technicalities we have chosen not to explicitly consider this case here. However, we do not expect significant challenge, in this regime the fBm is classically differentiable so that some arguments may even simplify. We conjecture that in this regime the threshold, coming from our tools, would become
\gamma>1-\frac{1}{2H}
.

Remark 1.5
.

The condition of
\gamma
-Hölder regularity for
\sigma
with
\gamma>\frac{1-H}{H}
is necessary to interpret
the SDE (
1
) as a Young differential equation in the case
H>1/2
or as rough differential equation when
H\leq 1/2
.
This condition is sufficient for weak existence when
H\in(1/3,1)
, and also for pathwise uniqueness when
H\in(1/3,1/2]
.
When
H>1/2
, there is a gap between the condition of weak existence and that of pathwise uniqueness.
We conjecture that pathwise uniqueness does not hold in general for
\gamma<\frac{1}{2H}
, although
we are not currently able to establish a proof.

Due to comparison with Brownian case, we expect that pathwise uniqueness also holds for
\gamma=\frac{1}{2H}
when
H>1/2
, however,
our current methods cannot cover this case either.

(Time Dependence
\sigma
)
.

As in
[
GG22
]
, one could naturally consider time dependent
\sigma
, for which we expect that the shifted stochastic sewing lemma (Lem.
2.4
) with controls would be necessary.

Remark 1.7

(
Path-by-Path
and
Weak
Uniqueness)
.

Despite its name the notion of
pathwise
uniqueness is a purely probabilistic one, which in this work we combine with truly pathwise notions of existence (see Def.
3.5
and Def.
4.5
). A stronger and more genuinely pathwise notion of uniqueness was coined by Davie,
[
Dav08
]
, as
path-by-path
uniqueness. In our case, this property would hold if, on a given probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
carrying an fBm
B^{H}
, there exists a
\mathbb{P}
-null set in
\Omega
, which may depend on
\sigma
and the initial data such that the Young differential equation (resp. rough differential equation) for
H>1/2
(resp.
H\leq 1/2
),

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}y_{t}=\sigma(y_{t})\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}^{H}(\omega),\quad y_{0}=x\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}

has a unique solution. In particular, these solutions are not required to be adapted to any given filtration. We refer the reader to
[
SW22
]
and the references therein for a more thorough discussion of the distinction between pathwise and path-by-path uniqueness.

It is not currently clear to the authors what the natural assumptions on
\sigma
are to ensure
path-by-path
uniqueness for solutions to (
1
). In
[
Dav08
, Thm. 4.8]
an example of (
1
) is studied for
H=1/2
with
\sigma
that is
2-\varepsilon
-Hölder continuous, for any
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
which has infinitely many solutions. However, this is not a counter example to
path-by-path
uniqueness since the
\sigma
here is in fact allowed to be random (almost surely of the required Hölder continuity) and may depend on the realisation of the Brownian motion.

Similarly, it is not clear to the authors which assumptions should be required of
\sigma
in order to prove weak uniqueness of solutions to (
1
) (see Def.
3.2
and Def.
4.2
).

Remark 1.8

(SDEs with Drift)
.

A natural question following from Thm.
1.1
is to obtain similar results for fBm driven SDEs with drift and multiplicative noise, of the form

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X_{t}=b(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{t}.

(3)

If
b
is sufficiently regular, for example Lipschitz continuous, it seems clear that the arguments of the current paper should adapt without difficulty to prove pathwise uniqueness, under the assumptions of Thm.
1.1
.
Conversely, in
[
DG22
]
the authors consider (
3
) with regular
\sigma
but irregular drift term,
b
.
Building on the current work, it would be of interest to study the case when both
\sigma
and
b
are irregular.
Such situation has been studied in the Brownian case, e.g.
[
Kry23
]
.

1.2
Overview of the Proof

We give a brief and formal description of our proof strategy for Thm.
1.1
. In both cases for
H\in(1/3,1/2]
and
H\in(1/2,1)
our argument is derived from a strategy given in
[
Lê20
]
, which relates pathwise uniqueness to construction of an irregular stochastic integral. In our case, let us consider two (weak) solutions
X,\,Y
to (
1
) defined on the same, filtered, probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
carrying an fBm
B^{H}
with Hurst parameter
H\in(1/4,1)
. Then, letting
d_{1},\,d_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
and assuming
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}}
is smooth for now, the difference
X-Y
satisfies,

X_{t}-Y_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sigma(X_{r})-\sigma(Y_{r})\right)\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}^{H}.

(4)

Applying the mean value theorem we see that
\sigma(X_{r})-\sigma(Y_{r})
is a
d_{1}\times d_{2}
matrix whose components are given by

\sigma_{ij}(X_{r})-\sigma_{ij}(Y_{r})=(X_{r}-Y_{r})\cdot\int_{0}^{1}\nabla\sigma_{ij}(\theta X_{r}+(1-\theta)Y_{r})\,\mathrm{d}\theta,\quad i=1,\ldots d_{1},\,j=1,\ldots,d_{2}.

Thus, we can formally write,

X_{t}-Y_{t}=\sum_{k=1}^{d_{1}}\int_{0}^{t}(X_{r}^{k}-Y_{r}^{k})\,\mathrm{d}G^{k}_{r},

(5)

where the components
(G^{k})_{k=1}^{d_{1}}
are defined by setting

G^{k}_{t}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{k}\sigma(\theta X_{r}+(1-\theta)Y_{r})\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r}.

(6)

Here the product between
\partial_{k}\sigma
and
\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}
is understood in the usual manner as a
d_{1}\times d_{2}
vector acting on a vector of length
d_{2}
. Thus, we aim to view the difference
X-Y
as a solution to the linear, integral equation

Z_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}Z_{t}\cdot\,\mathrm{d}G_{t}.

(7)

Since (
7
) clearly has unique solutions (if they exist), the question of pathwise uniqueness of solutions to (
1
) boils down to rigorous construction of the integral on the right hand side of (
5
). Of course, the challenge is to do so for irregular
\sigma
and this requires careful use of a priori properties which we obtain for solutions to (
1
). Here the technical steps of our proof differ in the regimes
H\in(1/2,1)
and
H\in(1/3,1/2]
, however, the spirit is the same in both cases.

Remark 1.9
.

It seems reasonable to expect that using similar arguments as ours one could show the existence of a continuous, adapted, semi-flow
\Phi:[0,T]^{2}_{\leq}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
defined such that

\Phi_{s,t}(x)\coloneqq X^{s;x}_{t}=x+\int_{s}^{t}\sigma(X^{s;x}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r}.

(8)

Continuity here means weak convergence (as stochastic processes) of
\Phi_{s,\,\cdot\,}(x)-\Phi_{s,\,\cdot\,}(y)
to zero as
x\to y
. One challenge here would be to obtain uniqueness of solutions for all initial data simultaneously, while
relevant null sets in our arguments may (in principle) depend on the initial data
x
. A common resolution to this issue is to first obtain stability of the solution in the initial data, see for example
[
CLL13
,
GG22
]
. It is not currently clear if our tools allow us to obtain similar stability estimates. This question is also related to stability of the solution in the coefficient
\sigma
, which in turn would directly allow one to consider McKean–Vlasov SDEs of the form,

\,\mathrm{d}X_{t}=(\sigma\ast\mu_{t})(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{t},\quad\mu_{t}=\text{Law}(X_{t}),

(9)

under similar conditions on
\sigma
as Thm.
1.1
. See for example
[
GHM22
,
GG22
]
for treatments of McKean–Vlasov equations with additive noise and singular drifts as well as
[
BCD20
,
DS23
,
DS22
]
for treatments of multiplicative, rough, McKean–Vlasov equations in the case of smooth volatilities. Stability estimates with respect to
\sigma
are also related to convergence rates of numerical schemes for these and similar equations, see
[
FSW22
,
LL21
,
GL23
]
.

Young case:
H\in(1/2,1)

In this regime we expect to have solutions
X,\,Y
which are
\alpha
-Hölder continuous for any
\alpha\in(1/2,H)
and in turn we expect the map
t\mapsto G_{t}^{k}
(as defined by (
6
)) to be at least
1/2
-Hölder continuous. Thus we expect to be able to make sense of the integral on the right hand side of (
5
) as a Young integral, see Sec.
3.1
below. However, the limiting factor is the regularity of the composition
\partial_{k}\sigma(\theta X_{r}+(1-\theta)Y_{r})
inside
G
. If we allow
\sigma
to be
\gamma
-Hölder continuous then one expects
r\mapsto\partial_{k}\sigma(\theta X_{r}+(1-\theta)Y_{r})
to be
\alpha(\gamma-1)
-Hölder continuous. Thus, in order to naively define
G
we would require
\alpha(\gamma-1)+\alpha>1\,\,\iff\,\,\gamma>\frac{1}{\alpha}>\frac{1}{H}
, (recall this is the condition of
[
Lyo94
,
Dav08
]
). To go below this threshold we make more detailed use of the fact that one expects
\theta X+(1-\theta)Y
to be
controlled
in a suitable probabilistic sense by
B^{H}
(see Def.
3.6
).
This allows us to exploit averaging properties of the fBm in order to obtain sharper stochastic estimates on the composition
\partial_{k}\sigma(\theta X_{r}+(1-\theta)Y_{r})
. These estimates are obtained by Prop.
3.7
giving control (essentially) of the form

\Big\|\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{r})\mathrm{d}B_{r}^{H}\Big\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim_{\beta,l,p,\gamma,X,T}\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\gamma-1}}\big((t-s)^{\gamma H}+(t-s)^{\left(1+\frac{1}{2H}+(\gamma-1)\right)H}\big),

(10)

for
f:\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}}
that is at least
(\gamma-1)
-Hölder continuous and any
X

probabilistically controlled
by
B^{H}
with remainder that is at least
1/2
-Hölder continuous. In order to apply the sewing lemma (see Lem.
2.3
) we therefore require
\gamma H\wedge\left(\frac{1}{2H}+\gamma\right)H\geq\frac{1}{2}\iff\gamma>\frac{1}{2H}
. The proof of this estimate is carried out by considering the germ,

A_{s,t}\vcentcolon=\mathbb{E}_{s-(t-s)}\left[\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{s-(t-s)}+X^{\prime}_{s-(t-s)}B_{s-(t-s),r}^{H})\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s-(t-s)}\right],

where
X^{\prime}
denotes the Gubinelli derivative of
X
and applying Gerencsér’s shifted, stochastic, sewing lemma
[
Ger22
, Lem. 2.2]
(see Sec.
2.4
). The significant challenge here is to treat the stochastic integral inside the germ
A
, this is handled by somewhat involved Gaussian computations, carried out in App.
B
. The remainder of Sec.
3
essentially consists of applying this abstract control to construct
G
as defined by (
6
) by considering a smooth approximation
\sigma_{n}\to\sigma
converging in any
\gamma^{\prime}
-Hölder topology for
\frac{1}{2H}<\gamma^{\prime}<\gamma
, see the proof of Thm.
3.8
in Sec.
3.2
. Prior to this final step we also show that all weak solutions to (
1
) are
probabilistically controlled
by
B^{H}
in a suitable sense, see Lem.
3.9
, which allows us to apply Prop.
3.7
.

Rough case:
H\in(1/3,1/2]

In the rough case, our strategy is essentially the same, in that we reduce our problem to constructing the integral (
6
) and solving the linearised equation (
5
). However, in this case we can no longer appeal to Young integration theory for either task. Concerning the second, since we cannot expect
X,\,Y
to be more regular than the noise, i.e.
\alpha
-Hölder continuous for any
\alpha\in(1/3,H)
, even if we assume
\sigma
to be smooth, we cannot expect
r\mapsto G_{r}
(with
G
as in (
6
)) to be more regular than the noise either and so the integral of
X-Y
against
\,\mathrm{d}G
is not defined as a Young integral since we no longer have
2\alpha>1
. This difficult is relatively easily circumvented by instead appealing to rough integration. Assuming for now that we can construct
G
, the natural solution is to define the joint lift of
(B^{H},G)
to a rough path

\left(B^{H},G^{k},\int B^{H}\otimes\,\mathrm{d}B^{H},\int B^{H}\otimes\,\mathrm{d}G^{k},\int G^{k}\otimes\,\mathrm{d}B^{H},\int G^{k}\otimes\,\mathrm{d}G^{l}\right)_{k,\,l\,=1}^{d_{1}}.

(11)

Since
X,\,Y
can both be shown to be
controlled
by
B^{H}
(in the sense of Gubinelli’s controlled rough paths, see
[
Gub04
]
and Def.
2.2
) the integration of
X-Y
against this joint rough path follows from standard results. The construction of the joint lift for smooth
\sigma
is also relatively standard, the central challenge is to obtain this construction for
\sigma
satisfying the assumptions of Thm.
1.1
.

It turns out that this construction relies on the same estimates that allow us to construct
G
in the first place. As in the Young case the core of the proof is to obtain precise, probabilistic estimates on integrals of singular functions against fBm, in this case these are of the form

\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r},\quad\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{r})B^{H}_{s,r}\otimes\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r}\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{s}^{t}\int_{s}^{r_{2}}g(Y_{r_{1}})\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r_{1}}\otimes f(X_{r_{2}})\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r_{2}}.

As before, we take
f,\,g
to be
\gamma-1
-Hölder continuous, playing the role of
\nabla\sigma
, this time, however, we assume
X,\,Y
are deterministically
controlled
by
B^{H}
, see Def.
2.2
. Control on these integrals, similar in spirit to (
10
) are obtained progressively through Prop.
4.7
, Prop.
4.8
and Prop.
4.9
. These estimates are also more technical than in the Young case and we are required to apply the stochastic sewing lemma twice, first in the fully shifted sense of
[
MP22
]
and then in the original form given by
[
Lê20
]
. We refer the reader to the proofs of the above propositions for details, simply remarking here that the main innovation is to use the knowledge that
X
and
Y
are controlled by
B^{H}
to again obtain improved regularising effects through averaging.

Once sufficient control on the above integrals is obtained we are again able to pass along a smooth approximation
\sigma_{n}
to
\sigma
, converging in any
\gamma^{\prime}
-Hölder topology for
\frac{1-H}{H}<\gamma^{\prime}<\gamma
. As before, we are then in position to appeal to uniqueness of solutions to the linear equation (
7
) and conclude.

When

2.3
Rough Integration

When the regularity of the driver
g

g

Definition 2.1

(Rough Paths and Geometric Rough Paths -
[
Lyo98
]
)
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha\in(1/3,1/2]
,
g\in\mathcal{C}_{T}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mathbb{G}\colon[0,T]_{\leq}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The pair
(g,\mathbb{G})
is called an
\alpha
-Hölder
rough path
if

•

the pair satisfies the following Hölder regularity estimate

\displaystyle\lVert(g,\mathbb{G})\rVert_{\alpha}\vcentcolon=\sup_{[s,t]\in[0,T]_{\leq}}\frac{\lvert g_{t}-g_{s}\rvert}{\lvert t-s\rvert^{\alpha}}+\frac{\lvert\mathbb{G}_{s,t}\rvert}{\lvert t-s\rvert^{2\alpha}}<\infty,

•

and the following algebraic relationship; for
(s,u,t)\in[0,T]_{\leq}^{3}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{s,t}=\mathbb{G}_{s,u}+\mathbb{G}_{u,t}+G_{s,u}\otimes G_{u,t}.

(18)

We say that a rough path is
geometric
if there exists a sequence of smooth functions
(g^{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}

such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\lVert g-g^{n}\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}_{T}}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{[s,t]\in[0,T]_{\leq}^{2}}\frac{\lvert\mathbb{G}_{s,t}-\mathbb{G}^{n}_{s,t}\rvert}{\lvert t-s\rvert^{2\alpha}}=0,

where
\mathbb{G}^{n}_{s,t}\vcentcolon=\int_{s}^{t}g^{n}_{s,r}\otimes\dot{g}^{n}_{r}\,\mathrm{d}r
.

Definition 2.2

(Space of Controlled Paths -
[

]
)
.

Let

,
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\beta\in(\alpha\vee(1-\alpha),1)
and

such that

\displaystyle\lVert(f,f^{\prime})\rVert_{\mathscr{D}_{g}^{\beta}}\vcentcolon=\lVert f^{\prime}\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta-\alpha}_{T}}+\sup_{(s,t)\in[0,T]_{\leq}^{2}}\frac{\lvert f_{s,t}-f^{\prime}_{s}g_{s,t}\rvert}{\lvert t-s\rvert^{\beta}}<\infty.

(19)

The map
f^{\prime}
is called the

of

.
We write
\mathscr{D}^{\beta}_{g}
for the space of
\beta

g

.

The definition of the space of controlled rough paths allows us to give meaning to the rough integral against a given path. For
\alpha\in(1/3,1)
and

\Big\lvert\int_{s}^{t}f_{r}\,\mathrm{d}g_{r}-f_{s}g_{s,t}-f^{\prime}_{s}\mathbb{G}_{s,t}\Big\rvert\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta}\lVert(f,f^{\prime})\rVert_{\mathscr{D}_{g}^{\beta}}\lVert(g,\mathbb{G})\rVert_{\alpha}(t-s)^{\alpha+\beta}.

Lemma 2.3

(Sewing Lemma -
[

]

Let

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{A}_{t}-\mathcal{A}_{s}-A_{s,t}\rVert_{E}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}C\lvert t-s\rvert^{1+\varepsilon},\quad\forall\,(s,t)\in[0,T]_{\leq}^{2}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{t}-\mathcal{A}_{s}=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\text{ is a partition of }[s,t],\\

(24)

under the additional condition that at least one of the Riemann sum approximations converges.

Similarly, in the setting of the stochastic sewing lemma, if

\lVert A^{1}_{s,t}-A^{2}_{s,t}\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon_{1}},\quad\lVert\mathbb{E}[A^{1}_{s,t}-A^{2}_{s,t}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim(s-v)^{-\alpha}(t-s)^{1+\varepsilon_{2}}

with
\alpha\in[0,1/2+\varepsilon_{2})
, then the identity (
24
) holds in
L^{p}(\Omega)
.
If the latter estimate also holds with
\alpha=0
, then one only needs to check the bound for
v=s
or
v=s-(t-s)
.

We will apply an appropriate version of the result depending on the problem at hand.
In our paper, we use the three versions of the stochastic sewing lemma: in Sec.
3
we use Gerencsér’s version,
[
Ger22
, Lem. 2.2]
(see proof of Prop.
3.7
),
while in Sec.
4
we use both Lê’s version
[
Lê20
, Thm. 2.1]
(see proof of Prop.
4.7
)
and the fully shifted version (see proof of Lem.
4.6
).

2.5
Fractional Brownian Motion

We collect some necessary background on the fractional Brownian motion, referring to
[
Nua06
, Sec. 5]
and
[
Gal22
, App. A.1]
for more details. As specified in the notation sections we fix a
d_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
and will consider noise taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
. We also fix a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
H\in(0,1)
. The
fractional Brownian motion
(fBm) with Hurst parameter
H
, started at
0
, is a centred Gaussian process

B^{H}=(B^{H,i})_{i=1}^{d_{2}}:[0,\infty)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}

such that
B^{H}_{0}=0
and for all
i,\,j=1,\ldots,d_{2}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[B^{H,i}_{t}B^{H,j}_{s}]=\frac{\delta_{ij}}{2}(s^{2H}+t^{2H}-(t-s)^{2H}),\quad\forall\,0\leq s<t<\infty,

where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta.

It will be useful to have a more concrete representation for the fBm.
Given a Brownian motion
W
, the formally written process

\displaystyle B^{H}_{t}\vcentcolon=(\mathcal{K}_{H}\,\mathrm{d}W)_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}K_{H}(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}

(25)

defines an fBm with Hurst parameter
H
. We refer the reader to
[
Nua06
, Sec. 5.1.3]
for more details. In the case
H>1/2
the kernel
K_{H}
is explicitly given by

\displaystyle K_{H}(t,s)\vcentcolon=c_{H}s^{\frac{1}{2}-H}\int_{s}^{t}(u-s)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}u^{H-\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}u,

(26)

while for
H<1/2
one has

K_{H}(t,s)\vcentcolon=c_{H}\left(\Big(\frac{t}{s}\Big)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}(t-s)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}-\Big(H-\frac{1}{2}\Big)s^{\frac{1}{2}-H}\int_{s}^{t}u^{H-\frac{3}{2}}(u-s)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}u\right).

(27)

In both cases, the constant
c_{H}
ensures that
B^{H}
has the correct covariance.

The main benefit of this representation is that since the operator
\mathcal{K}_{H}
admits an inverse onto its domain of definition, given an fBm
B^{H}
we can recover the associated Brownian motion
W_{t}\vcentcolon=(\mathcal{K}_{H}^{-1}B^{H})_{t}
. An explicit formula for the inverse is given, for example, by
[
Gal22
, Eq. A.7]
.
Recall that a Brownian motion
W
is called an
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-Brownian motion if for every
s<t
the random variable
W_{t}-W_{s}
is
independent of
\mathcal{F}_{s}
.

Definition 2.9
.

Given a complete filtered space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
, an adapted process
(B^{H}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is called
an
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-
fractional Brownian motion
if the process
\mathcal{K}_{H}^{-1}B^{H}
is
an
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-Brownian motion.

When
H\in(1/3,1/2]
, the process
B^{H}
can be lifted to a second order geometric rough path, we state this fact without proof below, for details see
[
FH20
, Ch. 10]
.

Lemma 2.10

(Canonical Lift of fBm,
[
FH20
, Ch. 10]
)
.

Let
H\in(1/3,1/2]

and
(B^{H,(n)})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a piecewise linear approximation of the fBm
B^{H}
. Then
the canonical lift of
B^{H,(n)}

\left(B^{H,(n)},\mathbb{B}^{H,(n)}\right)\vcentcolon=\left(B^{H,(n)},\left(\int_{s}^{t}B^{H,(n)}_{s,r}\otimes\dot{B}^{H,(n)}_{r}\,\mathrm{d}r\right)_{0\leq s<t<\infty}\right)

converges in the space of

\alpha
-Hölder rough paths for any
\alpha\in(1/3,H)

to some geometric rough path
(B^{H},\mathbb{B}^{H})
, called the
canonical lift of the fBm
B^{H}
.

As the stochastic sewing lemma is a key tool in our proofs, we will need to understand the behaviour of the fBm under conditioning.
Let
B^{H}
be an
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-fBm.
Recalling the Volterra representation (
25
), for
s<t
we observe the decomposition

B^{H}_{t}=\mathbb{E}\left[B^{H}_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]+\left(B^{H}_{t}-\mathbb{E}\left[B^{H}_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]\right)=\int_{0}^{s}K_{H}(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}W_{r}+\int_{s}^{t}K_{H}(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}W_{r},

where the first term is measurable with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{s}
and the second term is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{s}
.
In view of this decomposition,
we define the processes
Y^{H,s}=(Y^{H,s,i})_{i=1}^{d_{2}}
and
\tilde{B}^{H,s}=(\tilde{B}^{H,s,i})_{i=1}^{d_{2}}
by

\displaystyle Y^{H,s}_{t}

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=\int_{0}^{s}K_{H}(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}W_{r},

(28)

\displaystyle\tilde{B}^{H,s}_{t}

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=\int_{s}^{t}K_{H}(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}W_{r}.

(29)

Informally, we refer to
Y
as the
history process
and
\tilde{B}
as the
update process
.
When,
H=1/2
, by convention we set
Y^{H,s}\vcentcolon=0
and
\tilde{B}^{H,s}\vcentcolon=B^{H}
.
We additionally set

\displaystyle\rho^{2}_{H}(s,t)\vcentcolon=\mathbb{E}[(\tilde{B}^{H,s,i}_{t})^{2}]=\int_{s}^{t}K_{H}(t,r)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r

(30)

and have the following, two sided bound, for all
H\in(0,1)

\displaystyle(t-s)^{2H}\lesssim\rho^{2}_{H}(s,t)\leq(t-s)^{2H}.

(31)

The upper bound of (
31
) follows trivially from (
26
) and (
27
). In particular one readily checks

\int_{s}^{t}K_{H}(t,r)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\leq\int_{0}^{s}\left(K_{H}(t,r)-K_{H}(s,r)\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{s}^{t}K_{H}(t,r)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r=(t-s)^{2H}.

The lower bound of (
31
), whose simple proof can be found for example as
[
Gal22
, App. A.1]
, expresses the so-called
local nondeterminism
property of fBm, see also
[
Ber73
]
.

The function
t\mapsto\rho_{H}(s,t)
is increasing, whence we can consider the Riemann-Stieltjes integral
\int_{a}^{b}f(r)\rho_{H}(s,\mathrm{d}r)
.
Since
\rho_{H}(s,\cdot)
is differentiable, one actually has
\rho_{H}(s,\mathrm{d}r)=\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho_{H}(s,r)}{\mathrm{d}r}\,\mathrm{d}r
. In particular,

\int_{u}^{t}\rho_{H}^{\gamma}(s,r)\rho_{H}(s,\mathrm{d}r)=\frac{1}{\gamma+1}\int_{u}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho_{H}^{\gamma+1}(s,r)}{\mathrm{d}r}\,\mathrm{d}r\leq\frac{\max\{\rho_{H}^{\gamma+1}(s,t),\rho_{H}^{\gamma+1}(s,u)\}}{\lvert\gamma+1\rvert}.

(32)

A similar remark holds for
\rho^{2}_{H}(s,\mathrm{d}r)
.

We record the following lemmas on
Y^{H,s}
and
\tilde{B}^{H,s}
, whose proofs will be given in App.
B
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
H\in(1/3,1)
and we define
Y^{H,s}
by (
28
).
The process
Y^{H,s}
is smooth in
(s,\infty)
. Furthermore, for every
p\in[1,\infty)
and

(u,t)\in[s,\infty)_{\leq}^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert\dot{Y}^{H,s}_{t}\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim_{p}(t-s)^{H-1},\quad\lVert Y^{H,s}_{u,t}\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim_{p}(t-s)^{H}-(u-s)^{H}.

Proof.

The proof is given in App,
B.1
.
∎

The following crucial lemma, shows that for
H\in(1/3,1/2)
, the
update process
of the Volterra, fBm can be lifted to a second order rough path.

.

Given
H\in(1/3,1/2)
, then the pair

is a geometric rough path of order
\alpha
for any

\tilde{\mathbb{B}}^{H,s}_{u,t}\vcentcolon=\mathbb{B}^{H}_{u,t}-\int_{u}^{t}Y^{H,s}_{u,r}\otimes\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r}-\int_{u}^{t}B^{H}_{u,r}\otimes\dot{Y}^{H,s}_{r}\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{u}^{t}Y^{H,s}_{u,r}\otimes\dot{Y}^{H,s}_{r}\,\mathrm{d}r.

The second integral is well-defined as Riemann–Stieltjes integral, since

.

Proof.

The proof is given in App.
B.1
.

is understood as

and as

.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let

,

,

3
Young Case:
H\in(1/2,1)

In this section we prove Thm.
1.1
for the regime
H\in(1/2,1)
. In this parameter range we are able to appeal to Young integration theory in order to give a pathwise definition of solutions to the equation.

\,\mathrm{d}X_{t}=\sigma(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{t},\quad X_{0}=x\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}

(34)

To make this precise we recall the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

Given
T>0
,
d_{1},\,d_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
a path
g\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})
and a map
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\to\mathcal{M}(d_{1},d_{2})
. We say that a path
y:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
is a
solution to the Young differential equation

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}y_{t}=\sigma(y_{t})\,\mathrm{d}g_{t},\quad y_{0}=x\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}

(35)

on
[0,T]
if
y
is such that
\sigma(y)\in\mathcal{C}^{\beta}([0,T];\mathcal{M}(d_{1},d_{2}))
for some
\beta>0
such that
\beta>1-\alpha
and for all
t\in[0,T]
one has

\displaystyle y_{t}=x+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(y_{r})\,\mathrm{d}g_{r},

where the integral on the right hand side is understood as a Young integral as described in Sec.
2.2
.

Since we are interested in probabilistic notions of uniqueness we develop the following series of probabilistic definitions for solutions to (
34
).

Definition 3.2

(Existence and Uniqueness of Weak-Young Solutions)
.

We say that a tuple

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P},B^{H},X),

of a filtered probability space, an fBm of Hurst parameter
H
and an
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-adapted process
X
is a weak-Young solution to (
34
) if
B^{H}
is an
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
fBm (in the sense of Def.
2.9
) and for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e
\omega\in\Omega
, the process
X(\omega)
solves the differential equation (
34
) in the sense of Young (Def.
3.1
).

We say that weak uniqueness holds for (
34
) in the Young sense, if for any two weak solutions
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P},B^{H},X)
and
(\tilde{\Omega},\tilde{\mathcal{F}},(\tilde{\mathcal{F}_{t}})_{t\geq 0},\tilde{\mathbb{P}},\tilde{B}^{H},\tilde{X})
as above, the processes
X,\,\tilde{X}
are equal in law, i.e.

X_{\#}\mathbb{P}=\tilde{X}_{\#}\tilde{\mathbb{P}}\in\mathcal{P}(C([0,+\infty);\mathbb{R})).

Remark 3.3
.

As mentioned in the text following Prop.
1.2
, a standard compactness argument (given in
[
MP22
, App. B]
) allows for the construction of weak solutions in the case
H\in(1/2,1)
and
\sigma\in\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}};\mathcal{M}(d_{1},d_{2}))
for
\gamma>\frac{1-H}{H}
.

Note that since a posteriori one has
y\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
for any
\alpha\in(0,H)
, we have
\sigma(y)\in\mathcal{C}^{\gamma\alpha}([0,T];\mathcal{M}(d_{1},d_{2}))
. Hence, the conditions
\alpha<H
and
\gamma>\frac{1-H}{H}
imply that
\alpha\gamma+\alpha=(1+\gamma)\alpha>\frac{\alpha}{H}>1
, hence the Young integral in (
35
) is well-defined.

In the regime
H\in(1/2,1)
our main result (Thm.
1.1
) obtains pathwise uniqueness for
\sigma
that is
\gamma
-Hölder continuous for
\gamma>\frac{1}{2H}
and are uniformly elliptic; in this case note that
\frac{1}{2H}>\frac{1-H}{H}
, so that a fortiori we obtain pathwise uniqueness (under the assumption of uniform ellipticity) for these weak solutions and hence strong existence due to Prop.
1.2
.

Definition 3.4

(Existence of Strong-Young Solutions)
.

Given a probability space,
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
carrying an fBm
B^{H}
, we say that
X
is a strong-Young solution to (
34
) if for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
, the process
X(\omega)
solves (
34
) in the sense of Young (Def.
3.1
) and
X
is adapted to the natural filtration generated by
B^{H}
.

Definition 3.5

(Pathwise Uniqueness of Young Solutions)
.

We say that
pathwise-uniqueness
holds for (
34
), in the Young sense, if for any two, weak-Young solutions,
X
and
\tilde{X}
(in the sense of Def
3.2
), on the same probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, it holds that
X(\omega)=\tilde{X}(\omega)
for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
.

Pathwise uniqueness for (
34
) is proven in Sec.
3.2
.

3.1
Young Integral with Irregular Integrand

As explained in Sec.
1.2
, the key to obtaining pathwise uniqueness is to obtain suitable, stochastic estimates on integrals of the form,

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r}

(36)

for an irregular
f
and a path
X
in a suitable class. Since we will eventually take
X
to be any weak solution to the SDE (
34
), a natural class to take are those paths which are stochastically controlled by
B^{H}
. It turns out that this class is also sufficient for our purposes. The following definition is given in the full range
H\in(0,1)
since we also make use of it in Sec.
4
where we treat the case
H\in(1/3,1/2]
.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
T>0
,
d_{1},\,d_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
,
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
be a filtered probability space and
B^{H}
be an
\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
dimensional,
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-fBm with Hurst parameter
H\in(1/3,1)
.
Then, for
\beta\in(0,1]
,
l\in(1,\infty)
and a pair of (
\mathcal{F}_{t}
)-adapted processes
(X,X^{\prime}):[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathcal{M}(d_{1},d_{2})
, we say that
X
is
controlled
by
B^{H}
and write
(X,X^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{D}^{\beta,l}_{B^{H}}
if

•

the
derivative process
,
X^{\prime}
satisfies the analytic bound,

\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\frac{||X^{\prime}_{s,t}||_{L^{p}_{\omega}}}{(t-s)^{\beta H}}<\infty\quad\text{for all }\,p\in[1,\infty);

•

the remainder

\displaystyle R_{s,t}\vcentcolon=X_{s,t}-X^{\prime}_{s}B^{H}_{s,t},

(37)

defined for all
0\leq s<t\leq T
, satisfies the bound

\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\frac{||R_{s,t}||_{L^{p}_{\omega}}}{(t-s)^{(1+\beta)H}}<\infty,\quad\text{for all }\,p\in[1,\infty);

•

and for all
t\geq 0
,
X^{\prime}(X^{\prime})^{\top}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(d_{2})
, i.e. there exists an
l>0
such that

l^{-1}\leq X^{\prime}_{t}(X^{\prime}_{t})^{\top}\leq l
.

Given
(X,X^{\prime})
satisfying these conditions and
p\in[1,\infty)
, we set

\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}X\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{p,\beta}\vcentcolon=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|X^{\prime}_{t}\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}+\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\frac{||X^{\prime}_{s,t}||_{L^{p}_{\omega}}}{(t-s)^{\beta H}}+\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\frac{||R_{s,t}||_{L^{p}_{\omega}}}{(t-s)^{(1+\beta)H}}.

(38)

The main result of this subsection is the following proposition, which gives an analogue of the fundamental estimate of Young integration for integrals of the form (
36
).

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
T>0
,
d_{1},\,d_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
,
B^{H}
be an
\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
-fBm, with Hurst parameter
H\in(1/2,1)
, adapted to a given filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
,
\beta\in(\frac{1}{2H},1]
and
l\in[1,\infty)
.
Then, given
(X,X^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{D}^{\beta,l}_{B^{H}}
,

f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}},\mathbb{R})
,
p\in[2,\infty)
,
\tilde{\gamma}\in(\frac{1}{2H}-1,0)
and
(s,t)\in[0,T]^{2}_{\leq}
, it holds that

\Big\|\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{r})\mathrm{d}B_{r}^{H}\Big\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim_{\beta,l,p,\tilde{\gamma},T}\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\big((t-s)^{(1+\tilde{\gamma})H}+\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}X\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{2p,\beta}(t-s)^{(1+\beta+\tilde{\gamma})H}\big).

Proof.

For simplicity, in the remainder of the proof we write
B\vcentcolon=B^{H}
and do not keep track of dependencies on
\beta,l,p,\tilde{\gamma},T
.
Our strategy of proof is to apply Gerencsér’s shifted stochastic sewing,
[
Ger22
, Lem. 2.2]
(equivalently Lem.
2.4
with
\alpha=0
and
v=s-(t-s)
) to the germ

A_{s,t}\vcentcolon=\mathbb{E}_{s-(t-s)}\left[\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{s-(t-s)}+X_{s-(t-s)}^{\prime}B_{s-(t-s),r})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}\right].

(39)

As we demonstrate below, the shifted conditional, expectation and coefficients, along with the shifted stochastic sewing lemma, allow us to obtain the claimed estimate. We note that the shift is necessary, for instance to bound (
54
) below.
We decompose the proof into six steps. First demonstrate that the integral
\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r}
can be realised as the (shifted) stochastic sewing of (
39
).

Step 1.

This is equivalent to the claim that the following limit holds in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\text{ is a partition of }[s,t],\\
\lvert\pi\rvert\to 0\end{subarray}}\sum_{[s^{\prime},t^{\prime}]\in\pi}A_{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}.

(40)

Namely, this shows that the germ
(A_{s,t})
provides a correct approximation to the integral, (
36
).
To prove this identity, we first set
v\vcentcolon=s-(t-s)
and define the auxiliary germ

\displaystyle A^{0}_{s,t}\vcentcolon=\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{v}+X_{v}^{\prime}B_{v,r})\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r}.

(41)

It is directly clear that the Riemann sums of
A^{0}
and
A
coincide. To wit, appealing to the uniqueness element of the stochastic sewing lemma, as in Rem.
2.7
, it suffices to check that

\displaystyle\lVert A_{s,t}-A^{0}_{s,t}\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim\lvert t-s\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon_{1}}\quad\forall(s,t)\in[0,T]_{\leq}^{2},

(42)

and

\left\|\mathbb{E}_{s-(t-s)}\left[A_{s,t}-A^{0}_{s,t}\right]\right\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim|t-s|^{1+\varepsilon_{2}},\quad\forall(s,t)\in[0,T]_{\leq}^{2}.

The latter is immediate since
\mathbb{E}_{s-(t-s)}\left[A_{s,t}-A^{0}_{s,t}\right]=0
. The former also follows rather straightforwardly, we apply the triangle inequality, the fundamental estimate of Young integration (
17
) and the assumptions on
f,\,(X,X^{\prime})
to find,

\|A_{s,t}-A^{0}_{s,t}\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\leq\|A_{s,t}\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}+\|A^{0}_{s,t}\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim_{p,f,(X,X^{\prime}),T}|t-s|^{H}.

(43)

Hence, (
42
) follows since we here we only consider
H>1/2
. Therefore, in order to obtain (
40
) it is enough to show that

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\text{ is a partition of }[s,t],\\
\lvert\pi\rvert\to 0\end{subarray}}\sum_{[s^{\prime},t^{\prime}]\in\pi}A^{0}_{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}.

+\lvert f(X_{s})B_{s,t}-f(X_{v}+X^{\prime}_{v}B_{v,s})B_{s,t}\rvert.

By the remainder estimate of Young integration (
17
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\lVert\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}-f(X_{s})B_{s,t}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{C^{1}}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}X\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{2p,\beta}(t-s)^{(1+\beta)H},

\displaystyle\lVert A^{0}_{s,t}-f(X_{v}+X^{\prime}_{v}B_{v,s})B_{s,t}\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{C^{1}}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}X\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{2p,\beta}(t-s)^{(1+\beta)H}.

In addition, since

\displaystyle\lvert f(X_{s})-f(X_{v}+X^{\prime}_{v}B_{v,s})\rvert\leq\lVert f\rVert_{C^{1}}\lvert R_{v,s}\rvert,

where

is defined by (
37
),
we get

\displaystyle\lVert f(X_{s})B_{s,t}-f(X_{v}+X^{\prime}_{v}B_{v,s})B_{s,t}\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{C^{1}}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}X\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{2p,\beta}(t-s)^{(2+\beta)H}.

Combining these estimates, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\lVert\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}-A^{0}_{s,t}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{C^{1}}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}X\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{2p,\beta}(t-s)^{(1+\beta)H}.

As
(1+\beta)H>1
, appealing this time to the uniqueness element of the deterministic sewing lemma (see Rem.
2.7
), the identity (
44
) is established.

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}X\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{p,\beta}\left[(v_{2}-v_{1})^{(1+\beta)H}\rho^{\tilde{\gamma}}(v_{2},s)+(v_{2}-v_{1})^{\beta H}\rho^{\tilde{\gamma}+1}(v_{2},u)\right]

Applying the estimate (
31
) for
\rho(v_{2},r)
, we get

\displaystyle\lVert I_{3,2,1}\rVert\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}X\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{p,\beta}(t-s)^{(1+\beta+\tilde{\gamma})H}.

We finally estimate

. Using the bounds (
53
) and (
32
),

\displaystyle\lvert I_{3,2,3}\rvert\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\left|X_{v_{1},v_{2}}^{\prime}\right|\int_{s}^{u}\rho^{\tilde{\gamma}}(v_{2},r)\,\rho(v_{2},\mathrm{d}r)\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\left|X_{v_{1},v_{2}}^{\prime}\right|\rho^{\tilde{\gamma}+1}(v_{2},u).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\lVert I_{3,2,3}\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}X\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{p,\beta}(t-s)^{(1+\beta+\tilde{\gamma})H},

yielding the estimate (

), which in turn gives us (

), completing the proof.
∎

3.2
Pathwise Uniqueness

Thanks to Prop.
3.7
, we are ready to prove our main result in the case
H\in(1/2,1)
.

Theorem 3.8

(Pathwise Uniqueness in the Young regime)
.

Let
T>0
,
d_{1},\,d_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
,
B^{H}
be an fBm with Hurst parameter
H\in(1/2,1)
,
\gamma\in(\frac{1}{2H},1)
and
\sigma\in\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}};\mathcal{M}(d_{1},d_{2}))
be such that
\sigma\sigma^{\top}
is uniformly elliptic, i.e. there exists an
l\in[1,\infty)
such that

l^{-1}\leq\sigma(x)\sigma^{\top}(x)\leq l
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
.
Then, pathwise uniqueness holds
for (
34
) in the sense of Def.
3.5
.

Before giving the proof of Thm.
3.8
, we
prepare the following lemma.

Lemma 3.9
.

In the setting of Thm.
3.8
,
let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P},X,B^{H})
be a weak-Young solution to (
34
) (see Def.
3.2
). Then, we have

(X,\sigma(X))\in\mathfrak{D}^{\gamma,l}_{B^{H}}
as given by Def.
2.2
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha<H
, but sufficiently close to
H
.
We recall the seminorm
\llbracket\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}_{[s,t]}}
from (
13
).
Since

\displaystyle X_{s,t}=\int_{s}^{t}\sigma(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r},

the remainder estimate of Young integration, estimate (
17
), gives

\displaystyle\lvert X_{s,t}-\sigma(X_{s})B^{H}_{s,t}\rvert\lesssim_{\alpha,\gamma}\lVert\sigma\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}_{x}}\llbracket X\rrbracket_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}_{[s,t]}}^{\gamma}\left\|B^{H}\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}_{[s,t]}}(t-s)^{(1+\gamma)\alpha}.

(55)

By
[
FV10
, Lem. 10.7]
, we have

\displaystyle\llbracket X\rrbracket_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}_{T}}\lesssim_{\alpha,\gamma,T}(1+\lVert\sigma\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}_{x}}\lVert B^{H}\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}_{T}})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

(56)

Now, due to Gaussianity of
B^{H}
and as a consequence of the Kolmogorov continuity criterion, for all
p<\infty
,
there exists a
C\coloneqq C(p,\alpha,T,\lVert\sigma\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\gamma}})>0
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\llbracket X\rrbracket_{\mathcal{C}_{T}^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\leq C.

(57)

Combining (
55
) and (
57
), we get

\lVert X_{s,t}\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim(t-s)^{H}
.
In particular, we have
\lVert\sigma(X)_{s,t}\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim(t-s)^{\gamma H}
.
To get an estimate of the remainder

R_{s,t}=X_{s,t}-\sigma(X_{s})B_{s,t}^{H}
,
it suffices to apply the sewing lemma in
L^{p}_{\omega}
with germ

A_{s,t}\vcentcolon=\sigma(X_{s})B_{s,t}^{H}
,
since

\lVert\delta A_{s,u,t}\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}=\left\|\sigma(X)_{s,u}B^{H}_{u,t}\right\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim(t-s)^{(1+\gamma)H}.

∎

We are now ready to give the proof of Thm.
3.8
.

Proof of Thm.
3.8
.

Let
\alpha\in(1/2,H)
be arbitrarily close to
H
and

(X,B^{H})
and
(Y,B^{H})
be two weak solutions defined on the same filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t}),\mathbb{P})
and driven by the same
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-fBm,
B^{H}
.
Since the requirement on
\gamma\in(\frac{1}{2H},1)
is given by an open interval it is always possible to find a
\gamma^{\prime}\in(\frac{1}{2H},\gamma)
. We then let
(\sigma_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a smooth approximation to
\sigma
converging in
\mathcal{C}^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}};\mathcal{M}(d_{1},d_{2}))
(which exists by the compact embedding between Hölder–Besov spaces).
The remainder estimate of Young integration (
17
) gives

\displaystyle\Big\lvert\int_{s}^{t}\sigma(Z_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r}-\sigma(Z_{s})B^{H}_{s,t}\Big\rvert\lesssim_{\alpha,\gamma^{\prime}}\lVert\sigma\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\gamma^{\prime}}}\lVert Z\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{T}^{\alpha}}^{\gamma^{\prime}}\left\|B^{H}\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}_{T}}(t-s)^{(1+\gamma^{\prime})\alpha}

(58)

for
Z\in\{X,Y\}
.

From the estimate (
58
) and convergence of the
\sigma_{n}
in
\mathcal{C}^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}};\mathcal{M}(d_{1},d_{2}))
, it follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sigma_{n}(Z_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r}=\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sigma(Z_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r}

uniformly in
[0,T]
. As the computation (
5
) shows,
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sigma_{n}(X_{r})-\sigma_{n}(Y_{r})\right)\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r}=\sum_{k}\int_{0}^{t}(X^{k}_{r}-Y^{k}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}V^{k}_{n}(r),

where

\displaystyle V^{k}_{n}(r)\vcentcolon=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{r}\partial_{k}\sigma_{n}(\theta X_{u}+(1-\theta)Y_{u})\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{u}\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle X_{t}-Y_{t}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k}\int_{0}^{t}(X^{k}_{r}-Y^{k}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}V^{k}_{n}(r).

(59)

Now our task is to prove the convergence of
V_{n}
.
By Lem.
3.9
, for
(s,t)\in[0,T]^{2}_{\leq}
we have

\displaystyle\theta X_{s,t}+(1-\theta)Y_{s,t}=(\theta\sigma(X_{s})+(1-\theta)\sigma(Y_{s}))B_{s,t}+R^{\theta}_{s,t}

with

\displaystyle\lVert R_{s,t}^{\theta}\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim_{p,\gamma,x,\sigma}(t-s)^{(1+\gamma)H}.

One technical problem is that
the Gubinelli derivative
\theta\sigma(X)+(1-\theta)\sigma(Y)
might be degenerate, that is,
the matrix

\displaystyle\left[\theta\sigma(X)+(1-\theta)\sigma(Y)\right]\left[\theta\sigma(X)+(1-\theta)\sigma(Y)\right]^{\top}

could have an eigenvalue
0
.
(In the end, we will show that
X=Y
, hence it must be non-degenerate; but a priori we do not know this.)
Because of this technical problem, we proceed as follows.

Step 1.
To clarify our argument, we first assume that

\displaystyle\sup_{x,\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert\sigma(x)-\sigma(y)\rvert\leq\frac{l^{-2}}{4}.

(60)

This condition implies that
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
and
\theta\in(0,1)
we have

\displaystyle\left[\theta\sigma(x)+(1-\theta)\sigma(y)\right]

\displaystyle\left[\theta\sigma(x)+(1-\theta)\sigma(y)\right]^{\top}

\displaystyle=[\sigma(x)+(1-\theta)(\sigma(y)-\sigma(x))][\sigma(x)+(1-\theta)(\sigma(y)-\sigma(x))]^{\top}

\displaystyle\geq l^{-1}-\frac{l^{-1}}{2}-\frac{l^{-2}}{4}\geq\frac{l^{-1}}{4}.

Therefore, in combination of Lem.
3.9
, we see that

\displaystyle(\theta X+(1-\theta)Y,\theta\sigma(X)+(1-\theta)\sigma(Y))\in\mathfrak{D}^{\gamma,l/4}_{B^{H}}.

Prop.
3.7
and standard embedding of Hölder–Besov spaces, yields

\Big\lVert\int_{s}^{t}\partial_{k}(\sigma_{n}-\sigma_{m})(\theta X_{r}^{1}+(1-\theta)X_{r}^{2})\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\\
\lesssim_{p,\gamma^{\prime},x,\sigma}\lVert\sigma_{n}-\sigma_{m}\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\gamma^{\prime}}}(t-s)^{\gamma^{\prime}H}.

By Kolmogorov’s continuity theorem, we see that
there exists a process
V^{k}
such that for any
\beta<\gamma H
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\lVert\lVert V^{k}-V^{k}_{n}\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}_{T}}\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}=0.

Since
\gamma^{\prime}H>1/2
, we may take
\beta>1/2
. Therefore, recalling (
59
) we observe

\displaystyle X_{t}-Y_{t}=\sum_{k}\int_{0}^{t}(X^{k}_{r}-Y^{k}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}V^{k}_{r}\quad\text{a.s.}

As this shows that
X-Y
solves the linear Young differential equation

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}x_{t}=\sum_{k}x^{k}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}V^{k}_{t},\quad x_{0}=0,

we see that
X-Y=0
a.s.

Step 2.
Now we do not assume (
60
). The new ingredient is a stopping time argument.
We choose
\varepsilon>0
so that

\displaystyle\sup_{x,y:\lvert x-y\rvert\leq\varepsilon}\lvert\sigma(x)-\sigma(y)\rvert\leq\frac{l^{-2}}{5},

and we set
T^{(0)}\vcentcolon=0
and inductively

\displaystyle T^{(i)}\vcentcolon=\inf\{\,t\geq T^{(i-1)}:\lvert X_{t}-X_{T^{(i-1)}}\rvert\geq\varepsilon/2\text{ or }\lvert Y_{t}-Y_{T^{(i-1)}}\rvert\geq\varepsilon/2\,\}.

Using the notation
\llbracket X\rrbracket_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}_{T}}
from (
13
), if
T^{(i)}\leq T
,

\frac{\varepsilon}{2}=\max\left\{\lvert X_{T^{(i)}}-X_{T^{(i-1)}}\rvert,\lvert Y_{T^{(i)}}-Y_{T^{(i-1)}}\rvert\right\}\leq\max\left\{\llbracket X\rrbracket_{\mathcal{C}_{T}^{\alpha}},\llbracket Y\rrbracket_{\mathcal{C}_{T}^{\alpha}}\right\}(T^{(i)}-T^{(i-1)})^{\alpha}.

Hence, the a priori estimate (
56
) implies that

\displaystyle T^{(i)}-T^{(i-1)}\gtrsim_{\alpha,\gamma,T}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}(1+\lVert\sigma\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}_{x}}\|B^{H}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}_{T}})^{\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}}

(61)

uniformly over
i
, as long as
T^{(i)}\leq T
.

To see that
X=Y
a.s. up to time
T^{(1)}
,
let
\sigma^{(1)}
be a
\gamma
-Hölder map such that
\sigma^{(1)}=\sigma
in an
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of
the initial condition
x
and such that

\displaystyle\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}\lvert\sigma^{(1)}(x)-\sigma^{(1)}(y)\rvert\leq\frac{l^{-2}}{4}.

We set

\displaystyle X^{(1)}_{t}\vcentcolon=x+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{(1)}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r,\quad Y^{(1)}_{t}\vcentcolon=x+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{(1)}(Y_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r

and

\displaystyle V^{(1),k}_{n}(t)\vcentcolon=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{k}\sigma_{n}(\theta X^{(1)}_{r}+(1-\theta)Y^{(1)}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r}.

Up to time
T^{(1)}
, we have

\displaystyle X_{t}-Y_{t}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k}\int_{0}^{t}(X^{k}_{r}-Y^{k}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}V^{(1),k}_{n}(r).

Due to our choice of
\sigma^{(1)}
, the argument of Step 1 shows that
V^{(1),k}_{n}
converges to some
V^{(1),k}

in
\mathcal{C}_{T}^{\beta}
, and that
X=Y
up to time
T^{(1)}
.

Step 3.

Obviously we want to repeat the operation of Step 2, but now there is a small problem that
X_{T^{(1)}}
is random.
For this sake, let
(x_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
be a countable dense set of
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
, and
let
\sigma^{m}
be a
\gamma
-Hölder map such that

\sigma^{m}=\sigma
in an
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of
x_{m}
and such that

\displaystyle\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}\lvert\sigma^{m}(x)-\sigma^{m}(y)\rvert\leq\frac{l^{-2}}{4}.

(62)

For each
m
and
Z\in\{X,Y\}
,
we define the approximation,

\Sigma^{Z,m}(t)\vcentcolon=\begin{cases}\sigma(Z_{t})\mathbf{1}_{\{t\leq T^{(1)}\}}+\sigma^{m}(Z_{t})\mathbf{1}_{\{t>T^{(1)}\}},&\text{if }\lvert x_{m}-X_{T^{(1)}}\rvert=\lvert x_{m}-Y_{T^{(1)}}\rvert\leq\varepsilon/2,\\
\sigma(Z_{\min\{t,T^{(1)}\}}),&\text{otherwise },\end{cases}

along with the cut-off equations,

\displaystyle X^{(2,m)}_{t}\vcentcolon=x+\int_{0}^{t}\Sigma^{X,m}(r)\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r},\qquad Y^{(2,m)}_{t}\vcentcolon=x+\int_{0}^{t}\Sigma^{Y,m}(r)\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r}

and approximate linearisation,

\displaystyle V^{(2,m),k}_{n}(t)\vcentcolon=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{k}\sigma_{n}(\theta X^{(2,m)}_{r}+(1-\theta)Y^{(2,m)}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r}.

Notice that
X^{(2,m)}
,
Y^{(2,m)}
are both adapted.
We claim that, for
Z\in\{X,Y\}
,

\displaystyle\lvert Z_{s,t}^{(2,m)}-\Sigma^{Z,m}(s)B^{H}_{s,t}\rvert\lesssim(\lVert\sigma\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\gamma}}+\lVert\sigma^{m}\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\gamma}})\lVert Z\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{T}^{\alpha}}^{\gamma}\left\|B^{H}\right\|_{\mathcal{C}_{T}^{\alpha}}(t-s)^{(1+\gamma)\alpha}.

(63)

Indeed, by the remainder estimate of Young’s integral (
17
),
the estimate (
63
) is obvious if
t\leq T^{(1)}
or if
s\geq T^{(1)}
. Suppose that
s<T^{(1)}<t
.
If

, then

\displaystyle\lvert Z_{s,T^{(1)}}^{(2,m)}-\sigma(Z_{s})B^{H}_{s,T^{(1)}}\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\sigma\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\gamma}}\lVert Z\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{T}^{\alpha}}^{\gamma}\left\|B^{H}\right\|_{\mathcal{C}_{T}^{\alpha}}(t-s)^{(1+\gamma)\alpha},

\displaystyle\lvert Z_{T^{(1)},t}^{(2,m)}-\sigma^{m}(Z_{T^{(1)}})B^{H}_{T^{(1)},t}\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\sigma^{m}\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\gamma}}\lVert Z\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{T}^{\alpha}}^{\gamma}\left\|B^{H}\right\|_{\mathcal{C}_{T}^{\alpha}}(t-s)^{(1+\gamma)\alpha}.

Since
\lvert x_{m}-X_{T^{(1)}}\rvert=\lvert x_{m}-Y_{T^{(1)}}\rvert\leq\varepsilon/2
, we have

\displaystyle\lvert\sigma^{m}(Z_{T^{(1)}})-\sigma(Z_{s})\rvert=\lvert\sigma(Z_{T^{(1)}})-\sigma(Z_{s})\rvert\leq\lVert\sigma\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\gamma}}\lVert Z\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{T}^{\alpha}}^{\gamma}(T^{(1)}-s)^{\gamma\alpha},

and the estimate (
63
) follows.
The case where
\lvert x_{m}-X_{T^{(1)}}\rvert>\varepsilon/2
is similar.

Now we check that the Gubinelli derivative

is non-degenerate. Indeed,
for
t\leq T^{(1)}
we have

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\theta,m}(t)\Sigma^{\theta,m}(t)^{\top}=\sigma(X_{t})\sigma(X_{t})^{\top}\geq l^{-1},

and for

the condition (
62
) implies that

\Sigma^{\theta,m}(t)\Sigma^{\theta,m}(t)^{\top}\geq l^{-1}/4
.
Therefore, Prop.
3.7
and the Kolmogorov continuity theorem show that

converges to some
V^{(2,m)}
in
L^{p}_{\omega}
. By the diagonalization argument, we may suppose that

and
\delta\in(0,\gamma H)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\lVert V^{(2,m)}-V^{(2,m)}_{n}\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{T}^{\gamma H-\delta}}=0.

Since
(x_{m})
is dense, we can find a random

. We then have

\displaystyle X_{t}-Y_{t}=\sum_{k}\int_{0}^{t}(X^{k}_{r}-Y^{k}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}V^{(2,m),k}(r)

up to

, hence

on
[0,T^{(2)}]
.
It is now clear that this algorithm can be continued, and at some point we must have

due to (

).
∎

4
Rough Case:
H\in(1/3,1/2]

In this section we prove Thm.
1.1
for the regime
H\in(1/3,1/2]
. As discussed at the opening of Sec.
2.3
, for Hurst parameters below
1/2
we can no longer appeal to Young integration theory to make sense of
\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{s}
in (
1
). Here we recall the fact that for
H\in(1/3,1/2]
we can
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. canonically lift
B^{H}
to a geometric rough path
(B^{H},\mathbb{B}^{H})
(see Lem.
2.10
) as well as the definition of
\mathscr{D}^{\beta}_{B^{H}}
; the space of
\beta
-regular, rough paths, controlled by
B^{H}
(see Def.
2.2
). These preparations are enough to give a pathwise definition of solutions to rough differential equations (RDEs).

Definition 4.1

(Solutions to RDEs)
.

Given
T>0
,
d_{1},\,d_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
,
H\in(1/3,1/2]
,
(B^{H}(\omega),\mathbb{B}^{H}(\omega))
an almost sure realisation of the canonical, geometric rough path lift for the fBm with Hurst parameter
H
(see Lem.
2.10
) and a map
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\to\mathcal{M}(d_{1},d_{2})
, we say that a path
(y,y^{\prime})
controlled by
(B^{H}(\omega),\,\mathbb{B}^{H}(\omega))
is a
solution to the rough differential equation

\mathrm{d}y_{t}=\sigma(y_{t})\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}^{H}(\omega),\quad y_{0}=x,

(64)

on
[0,T]
, if the path
(\sigma(y),\nabla\sigma(y)y^{\prime})\in\mathscr{D}^{\gamma\alpha}_{B^{H}(\omega)}
for some
\alpha\in(1/3,H)
and
\gamma>\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}
and if for every
t\in[0,T]
one has

\displaystyle y_{t}=x+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(y_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}^{H}(\omega),

where the integral is understood as a rough integral as described in Sec.
2.3
.

Note that the assumption
(\sigma(y),\nabla\sigma(y)y^{\prime})\in\mathscr{D}^{\gamma\alpha}_{B^{H}(\omega)}
for
\gamma>\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}>1
is the minimum required to make sense of the integral in (
64
) as a rough integral.
If
(y,y^{\prime})
is a solution to (
64
), the remainder estimate of rough integrals (
20
) shows that
the map
t\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(y_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r}(\omega)
is again controlled by
B^{H}(\omega)

with Gubinelli derivative
\sigma(y)
.
Due to this observation, it in fact holds that

(y,\sigma(y))\in\mathscr{D}^{2\alpha}_{B^{H}(\omega)}
,
hence appealing to
[
FH20
, Prop. 6.4]
and
true roughness
of
B^{H}
one sees that for all solutions in the sense of Def.
4.1
, it holds that
y^{\prime}=\sigma(y)
.

As in the Young case, for an fBm on a given probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, Def.
4.1
is an
\omega
-wise definition. We therefore give the following probabilistic definitions, which are analogues of Def.
3.2
,
3.4
and
3.5
for solutions to

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X_{t}=\sigma(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{t},\quad X_{0}=x.

(65)

Definition 4.2

(Weak-Rough Solutions)
.

Given
T>0
, say that a tuple

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P},B^{H},X,X^{\prime}),

of a filtered probability space, an fBm of Hurst parameter
H
and an
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-adapted, controlled rough path,
(X,X^{\prime})
is a weak-rough solution to (
65
), if
B^{H}
is an
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
fBm (in the sense of Def.
2.9
) and for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e
\omega\in\Omega
, the process
(X(\omega),X^{\prime}(\omega))
is a rough solution to (
65
)in the sense of Def.
4.1
.

We say that weak uniqueness holds for (
65
) in the rough sense, if for any two weak solutions
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P},B^{H},X,X^{\prime})
and
(\tilde{\Omega},\tilde{\mathcal{F}},(\tilde{\mathcal{F}_{t}})_{t\geq 0},\tilde{\mathbb{P}},\tilde{B}^{H},\tilde{X},\tilde{X}^{\prime})
as above, the processes
(X,X^{\prime}),\,(\tilde{X},\tilde{X}^{\prime})
are equal in law, i.e. one has

(X,X^{\prime})_{\#}\mathbb{P}=(\tilde{X},\tilde{X}^{\prime})_{\#}\tilde{\mathbb{P}}\quad\text{as elements of}\quad\mathcal{P}\left(C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})\times C\left([0,T];\mathcal{M}(d_{1},d_{2})\right)\right)

Remark 4.3
.

As mentioned in the Young case (Rem.
3.3
) a standard compactness argument (a la
[
MP22
, Appendix B]
) allows one to construct weak-rough solutions in the case
\sigma\in\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}};\mathcal{M}(d_{1},d_{2}))
for
\gamma>\frac{1-H}{H}
. Note that in contrast to Def.
4.1
this is explicitly an assumption on the coefficient
\sigma
which in turn ensures the required regularity of the controlled path
(\sigma(X),\nabla\sigma(X),X^{\prime})
for some
\alpha\in(1/3,H)
sufficiently close to
H
.

In the regime
H\in(1/3,1/2)
our main result (Thm.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let

,

,

\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}

Then, for

\Big\lVert\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}^{H}-f(X_{s})B_{s,t}^{H}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim_{p,l,\tilde{\gamma},T}\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}(1+\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}X\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{2p,1})(t-s)^{(1+\tilde{\gamma})H}.

Proof.

For concision, throughout the proof we simply write
B\vcentcolon=B^{H}
. Let us set

\displaystyle A_{s,t}\vcentcolon=\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{s}+X_{s}^{\prime}B_{s,r})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}.

(66)

The integral is understood as rough integral: the path

r\mapsto f(X_{s}+X_{s}^{\prime}B_{s,r})

B
with Gubinelli derivative
\nabla f(X_{s}+X_{s}^{\prime}B_{s,r})X^{\prime}_{s}
.
In particular, the estimate (
20

\displaystyle\lvert A_{s,t}-f(X_{s})B_{s,t}-\nabla f(X_{s})X^{\prime}_{s}\mathbb{B}_{s,t}\rvert\lesssim(t-s)^{3\alpha}

. Hence, the uniqueness part of the sewing lemma (see Rem.
2.7
) implies that

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}=\lim_{\lvert\pi\rvert\to 0}\sum_{[s^{\prime},t^{\prime}]\in\pi}A_{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}},

where
\pi
is a partition of
[s,t]
.
As in Prop.
3.7
, we apply the stochastic sewing to

A_{s,t}
. This time, Lê’s original version
[
Lê20
]
is sufficient (that is
\alpha=0
and
v=s

in Lem.
2.4
).
Precisely, we prove the estimates

\displaystyle\lVert\delta A_{s,u,t}\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\leq\Gamma_{1}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon_{1}},\quad\lVert\mathbb{E}[\delta A_{s,u,t}|\mathcal{F}_{s}]\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\leq\Gamma_{2}(t-s)^{1+\varepsilon_{2}}

for
s<u<t
with appropriate
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}>0
and proportional constants
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}
.
We split the rest of the proof into three steps.

Step 1.
We estimate
\lVert\delta A_{s,u,t}\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}
.
Writing
Y\vcentcolon=Y^{H,u}
and
\tilde{B}\vcentcolon=\tilde{B}^{H,u}
as in (
28
) and (
29
) respectively,
we observe

\displaystyle\delta A_{s,u,t}=

\displaystyle\int_{u}^{t}\left(f(X_{s}+X_{s}^{\prime}B_{s,r})-f(X_{u}+X_{u}^{\prime}B_{u,r})\right)\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{u}^{t}\left(f(X_{s}+X_{s}^{\prime}B_{s,r})-f(X_{u}+X_{u}^{\prime}B_{u,r})\right)\dot{Y_{r}}\,\mathrm{d}r

(67)

\displaystyle+\int_{u}^{t}\left(f(X_{s}+X_{s}^{\prime}B_{s,r})-f(X_{u}+X_{u}^{\prime}B_{u,r})\right)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{B}_{r},

(68)

The integral (
67
), by the triangle inequality, is bounded by

\displaystyle\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\int_{u}^{t}\lvert X_{s}+X_{s}^{\prime}B_{s,r}-(X_{u}+X_{u}^{\prime}B_{u,r})\rvert^{\tilde{\gamma}}\lvert\dot{Y}_{r}\rvert\,\mathrm{d}r.

Defining the remainder
R
as in Def.
3.6
, we have

\displaystyle X_{s}+X_{s}^{\prime}B_{s,r}-(X_{u}+X_{u}^{\prime}B_{u,r})=-R_{s,u}-X^{\prime}_{s,u}B_{u,r},

and since, due to Lem.
2.11
we have
\lVert\dot{Y}_{r}\rVert_{L^{2p}_{\omega}}\lesssim_{p}(r-u)^{H-1}
, it follows that

\Bigg\|\int_{u}^{t}\left(f(X_{s}+X_{s}^{\prime}B_{s,r})-f(X_{u}+X_{u}^{\prime}B_{s,r})\right)\dot{Y_{r}}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\Bigg\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\\
\leq\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\int_{u}^{t}\lVert\lvert R_{s,u}+X^{\prime}_{s,u}B_{u,r}\rvert^{\tilde{\gamma}}\rVert_{L^{2p}_{\omega}}\lVert\dot{Y}_{r}\rVert_{L^{2p}_{\omega}}\,\mathrm{d}r\lesssim_{p}\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}X\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{2p,1}^{\tilde{\gamma}}(t-s)^{(1+2\tilde{\gamma})H}.

To estimate (
68
),
for

we apply Lem.
4.6
with

\displaystyle\tau=u,\quad\xi=(X_{v}+X_{v}^{\prime}B_{v,u},X_{v}^{\prime}),\quad F((x,x^{\prime}),y)=f(x+x^{\prime}y)

\Big\lVert\int_{u}^{t}f(X_{v}+X_{v}^{\prime}B_{v,r})\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{B}_{r}-\frac{f(X_{v}+X^{\prime}_{v}B_{v,u})+f(X_{v}+X^{\prime}_{v}B_{v,t})}{2}\tilde{B}_{u,t}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\\

Since

\Big\lvert\frac{f(X_{s}+X^{\prime}_{s}B_{s,u})+f(X_{s}+X^{\prime}_{s}B_{s,t})}{2}-\frac{f(X_{u})+f(X_{u}+X^{\prime}_{u}B_{u,t})}{2}\Big\rvert\\

we see that the

-norm of the integral (

is bounded by (up to constant)

\displaystyle\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}(t-s)^{(1+\tilde{\gamma})H}+\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}X\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{2p,1}^{\tilde{\gamma}}(t-s)^{(1+2\tilde{\gamma})H}.

\displaystyle\lVert\delta A_{s,u,t}\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim_{p,l,\tilde{\gamma}}\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}(t-s)^{(1+\tilde{\gamma})H}+\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}X\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{2p,1}^{\tilde{\gamma}}(t-s)^{(1+2\tilde{\gamma})H}.

(69)

Step 2.
Next we estimate
\mathbb{E}[\delta A_{s,u,t}|\mathcal{F}_{s}]
,
which is essentially done in Prop.
3.7
.
We define
Y
and
\tilde{B}
as in Step 1, and we write
\mathbb{E}_{u}[\,\cdot\,]\vcentcolon=\mathbb{E}[\,\cdot\,|\mathcal{F}_{u}]

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{u}\left[\delta A_{s,u,t}\right]=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{u}\left[\int_{u}^{t}\left(f(X_{s}+X_{s}^{\prime}B_{s,r})-f(X_{u}+X_{u}^{\prime}B_{s,r})\right)\dot{Y_{r}}\,\mathrm{d}r\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}_{u}\left[\int_{u}^{t}\left(f(X_{s}+X_{s}^{\prime}B_{s,r})-f(X_{u}+X_{u}^{\prime}B_{s,r})\right)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{B}_{r}\right].

For

, we observe

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbb{E}_{s}[\delta A_{s,u,t}]\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim_{p,l,\tilde{\gamma},T}\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}X\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{2p,1}(t-s)^{(2+\tilde{\gamma})H}.

(70)

) and (

as in Prop.
4.7

These two propositions as well as Prop.

Since we already have access to Prop.
4.7

follows from the deterministic sewing lemma (Lem.

We recall again the notations
\mathfrak{D}^{1,l}_{B^{H}}
and
\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}\,\cdot\,\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{p,1}

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
H\in(1/3,1/2]
,
p\in[2,\infty)
,
l\in[1,\infty)
,
(X,X^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{D}^{1,l}_{B^{H}}
,

and
\tilde{\gamma}\in\left(\frac{1-H}{H}-1,1\right)
.
Then, for
(s,t)\in[0,T]_{\leq}^{2}
and

we have

\Big\lVert\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{r})B^{H,i}_{s,r}\,\mathrm{d}B^{H,j}_{r}-f(X_{s})\mathbb{B}^{H,i,j}_{s,t}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim_{p,l,\tilde{\gamma},T}\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}(1+\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}X\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{4p,1})(t-s)^{(2+\tilde{\gamma})H}.

Proof.

For concision, we write
B\vcentcolon=B^{H}
and
\mathbb{B}\vcentcolon=\mathbb{B}^{H}
.
We fix a
\tau\leq s
, and we set

\bar{A}_{s,t}\vcentcolon=B^{i}_{\tau,s}\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B^{j}_{r}+f(X_{s})\mathbb{B}_{s,t}^{i,j}.

Step 1.
We show that
\bar{A}_{s,t}
is a correct approximation to the iterated integral

\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{r})B_{\tau,r}^{i}\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}^{j}
.
For this sake, we set

\bar{A}^{\prime}_{s,t}\vcentcolon=f(X_{s})B^{i}_{\tau,s}B^{j}_{s,t}\\
+f(X_{s})\mathbb{B}^{i,j}_{s,t}+\sum_{k,l}B^{i}_{\tau,s}\partial_{k}f(X_{s})(X^{\prime}_{s})^{kl}\mathbb{B}_{s,t}^{lj}.

By definition of the rough integral, we have

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{r})B^{i}_{\tau,r}\,\mathrm{d}B^{j}_{r}=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\text{ is a partition of }[s,t],\\
\lvert\pi\rvert\to 0\end{subarray}}\sum_{[u,v]\in\pi}\bar{A}^{\prime}_{u,v}.

In view of the uniqueness part of the sewing lemma (see Rem.
2.7
),
our goal is to prove
\lvert\bar{A}_{s,t}-\bar{A}^{\prime}_{s,t}\rvert\lesssim_{f,X,X^{\prime},\omega}(t-s)^{3H}
.
We compute

\displaystyle\bar{A}_{s,t}-\bar{A}^{\prime}_{s,t}=B^{i}_{\tau,s}\Big(\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}-f(X_{s})B^{j}_{s,t}-\sum_{k,l}\partial_{k}f(X_{s})(X^{\prime}_{s})^{kl}\mathbb{B}_{s,t}^{lj}\Big)

By the remainder estimate of the rough integral (
20
), we have

\displaystyle\Big\lvert\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}-f(X_{s})B^{j}_{s,t}-\sum_{k,l}\partial_{k}f(X_{s})(X^{\prime}_{s})^{kl}\mathbb{B}_{s,t}^{lj}\Big\rvert\lesssim_{f,X,X^{\prime},\omega}(t-s)^{3H},

whence
\bar{A}
is a correct approximation.

Step 2.
This time we do not need the stochastic sewing; the deterministic sewing (Lem.
2.3
)
in
L^{p}_{\omega}
suffices. We compute

\displaystyle\delta\bar{A}_{s,u,t}=-B^{i}_{s,u}\int_{u}^{t}f(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B^{j}_{r}+f(X_{s})(\mathbb{B}_{s,t}^{i,j}-\mathbb{B}_{s,u}^{i,j})-f(X_{u})\mathbb{B}_{u,t}^{i,j}.

Using Chen’s relation

\mathbb{B}^{i,j}_{u,t}=\mathbb{B}^{i,j}_{s,t}-\mathbb{B}^{i,j}_{s,u}-B^{i}_{s,u}B^{j}_{u,t}
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\delta A_{s,u,t}=-B^{i}_{s,u}\Big(\int_{u}^{t}f(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}^{j}-f(X_{u})B^{j}_{u,t}\Big)-f(X)_{s,u}(\mathbb{B}^{i,j}_{s,t}-\mathbb{B}^{i,j}_{s,u}).

Since

\lVert B^{i}_{s,u}\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim_{p}(t-s)^{H},\,\,\lVert\mathbb{B}^{i,j}_{s,t}\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim_{p}(t-s)^{2H},\,\,\lVert f(X)_{s,u}\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim_{p,\tilde{\gamma}}\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}X\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{p\tilde{\gamma},1}^{\tilde{\gamma}}(t-s)^{\tilde{\gamma}H},

and by Prop.
4.7

\displaystyle\Big\lVert\int_{u}^{t}f(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}^{j}-f(X_{u})B^{j}_{u,t}\Big\rVert_{L^{2p}_{\omega}}\lesssim_{p,l,\tilde{\gamma},T}\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}(1+\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}X\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{4p,1})(t-s)^{(1+\tilde{\gamma})H},

using Cauchy–Schwarz inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert\delta A_{s,u,t}\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim_{p,l,\tilde{\gamma},T}\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}(1+\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}X\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{4p,1})(t-s)^{(2+\tilde{\gamma})H}.

As
(2+\tilde{\gamma})H>1
, the sewing lemma in
L^{p}_{\omega}
gives

\Big\lVert\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{r})B_{\tau,r}^{i}\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}^{j}-\bar{A}_{s,t}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim_{p,l,\tilde{\gamma},T}\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}(1+\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}X\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{4p,1})(t-s)^{(2+\tilde{\gamma})H}.

It remains to set
\tau=s
.
∎

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
H\in(1/3,1/2]
,
p\in[2,\infty)
,
l\in[1,\infty)
,
(X,X^{\prime}),(Y,Y^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{D}^{1,l}_{B^{H}}
,

f,g\in C^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\tilde{\gamma}\in\left(\frac{1-H}{H}-1,1\right)
.
Then, for every
(s,t)\in[0,T]_{\leq}^{2}
and
i,j\in\{1,2,\ldots,d_{2}\}

we have

\Big\lVert\int_{s}^{t}\Big(\int_{s}^{r_{2}}g(Y_{r_{1}})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r_{1}}^{H,i}\Big)f(X_{r_{2}})\,\mathrm{d}B^{H,j}_{r_{2}}-g(Y_{s})f(X_{s})\mathbb{B}_{s,t}^{H,i,j}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\\
\lesssim_{p,l,\tilde{\gamma},T}\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\lVert g\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}(1+\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}X\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{8p,1})(1+\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}Y\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{8p,1})(t-s)^{(2+\tilde{\gamma})H}.

Proof.

For concision, throughout the proof we write
B\vcentcolon=B^{H}
and
\mathbb{B}\vcentcolon=\mathbb{B}^{H}
.
We fix a
\tau\in[0,s]
. To estimate the iterated integral

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\Big(\int_{\tau}^{r_{2}}g(Y_{r_{1}})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r_{1}}^{i}\Big)f(X_{r_{2}})\,\mathrm{d}B^{j}_{r_{2}}

our choice of the germ is

\tilde{A}_{s,t}\vcentcolon=\Big(\int_{\tau}^{s}g(Y_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B^{i}_{r}\Big)\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B^{j}_{r}+g(Y_{s})\int_{s}^{t}B^{i}_{s,r}f(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B^{j}_{r}.

Step 1.
To see that
\tilde{A}_{s,t}
is a correct approximation, we set

\tilde{A}^{\prime}_{s,t}\vcentcolon=\Big(\int_{\tau}^{s}g(Y_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}^{i}\Big)f(X_{s})B^{j}_{s,t}+g(Y_{s})f(X_{s})\mathbb{B}^{i,j}_{s,t}\\
+\sum_{k,l}\Big(\int_{\tau}^{s}g(Y_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B^{i}_{r}\Big)\partial_{k}f(Y_{s})(Y^{\prime}_{s})^{kl}\mathbb{B}_{s,t}^{l,j}.

By definition of the rough integral, we have

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\Big(\int_{\tau}^{r_{2}}g(Y_{r_{1}})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r_{1}}^{i}\Big)f(X_{r_{2}})\,\mathrm{d}B^{j}_{r_{2}}=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\text{ is a partition of }[s,t],\\
\lvert\pi\rvert\to 0\end{subarray}}\sum_{[u,v]\in\pi}\tilde{A}^{\prime}_{u,v}.

In view of the uniqueness part of the sewing lemma (see Rem.
2.7
),
our goal is to prove
\lvert\tilde{A}_{s,t}-\tilde{A}^{\prime}_{s,t}\rvert\lesssim(t-s)^{3H}
. We observe

\tilde{A}_{s,t}-\tilde{A}^{\prime}_{s,t}=\Big(\int_{\tau}^{s}g(Y_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}^{i}\Big)\Big(\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B^{j}_{r}-f(X_{s})B^{j}_{s,t}-\sum_{k,l}\partial_{k}f(Y_{s})(Y^{\prime}_{s})^{kl}\mathbb{B}_{s,t}^{l,j}\Big)\\
+g(Y_{s})\Big(\int_{s}^{t}B^{i}_{s,r}f(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B^{j}_{r}-f(X_{s})\mathbb{B}_{s,r}^{i,j}\Big).

By the remainder estimate of the rough integral (
20
), we have

\displaystyle\Big\lvert\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B^{j}_{r}-f(X_{s})B^{j}_{s,t}-\sum_{k,l}\partial_{k}f(Y_{s})(Y^{\prime}_{s})^{kl}\mathbb{B}_{s,t}^{l,j}\Big\rvert\lesssim(t-s)^{3H},

\displaystyle\Big\lvert\int_{s}^{t}B^{i}_{s,r}f(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B^{j}_{r}-f(X_{s})\mathbb{B}_{s,r}^{i,j}\Big\rvert\lesssim(t-s)^{3H}.

Hence,
\tilde{A}_{s,t}
is a correct approximation.

Step 2.
Again we do not need the stochastic sewing; the deterministic sewing lemma (Lem.
2.3
) in
L^{p}_{\omega}
suffices. We compute

\delta\tilde{A}_{s,u,t}=-\Big(\int_{s}^{u}g(Y_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B^{i}_{r}-g(Y_{s})B^{i}_{s,u}\Big)\int_{u}^{t}f(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B^{j}_{r}-g(X)_{s,u}\int_{u}^{t}f(X_{r})B^{i}_{u,r}\,\mathrm{d}B^{j}_{r}.

By Prop.
4.7
and Prop.
4.8
,

\displaystyle\Big\lVert\int_{s}^{u}g(Y_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B^{i}_{r}-g(Y_{s})B^{i}_{s,u}\Big\rVert_{L^{2p}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert g\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}(1+\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}X\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{4p,1})(t-s)^{(1+\tilde{\gamma})H},

\displaystyle\Big\lVert\int_{u}^{t}f(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B^{j}_{r}\Big\rVert_{L^{2p}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}(1+\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}Y\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{4p,1})(t-s)^{H},

\displaystyle\lVert g(X)_{s,u}\rVert_{L^{2p}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert g\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}X\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{4p,1}(t-s)^{\tilde{\gamma}H},

\displaystyle\Big\lVert\int_{u}^{t}f(X_{r})B^{i}_{u,r}\,\mathrm{d}B^{j}_{r}\Big\rVert_{L^{2p}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}(1+\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}Y\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{8p,1})(t-s)^{2H}.

Since
(2+\tilde{\gamma})H>1
, we are in the setting of the sewing lemma (Lem.
2.3
).
The remainder estimate yields

\Big\lVert\int_{s}^{t}\Big(\int_{s}^{r_{2}}g(Y_{r_{1}})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r_{1}}^{i}\Big)f(X_{r_{2}})\,\mathrm{d}B^{j}_{r_{2}}-\tilde{A}_{s,t}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\\
\lesssim\lVert g\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}(1+\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}X\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{8p,1})(1+\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}Y\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{8p,1})(t-s)^{(2+\tilde{\gamma})H}.

Setting
\tau=s
, we get

\Big\lVert\int_{s}^{t}\Big(\int_{s}^{r_{2}}g(Y_{r_{1}})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r_{1}}^{i}\Big)f(X_{r_{2}})\,\mathrm{d}B^{j}_{r_{2}}-g(Y_{s})\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{r})B^{i}_{s,r}\,\mathrm{d}B^{j}_{r}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\\
\lesssim\lVert g\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}(1+\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}X\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{8p,1})(1+\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}Y\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{8p,1})(t-s)^{(2+\tilde{\gamma})H}.

By Prop.
4.8
,

\Big\lVert\int_{s}^{t}f(X_{r})B^{i}_{s,r}\,\mathrm{d}B^{j}_{r}-f(X_{s})\mathbb{B}^{i,j}_{s,t}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\tilde{\gamma}}}(1+\mathopen{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}Y\mathclose{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|}_{4p,1})(t-s)^{(2+\tilde{\gamma})H},

and the proof is complete.
∎

4.3
Pathwise Uniqueness

Our final result is the following, which is a restatement of Thm.
1.1
in the regime
(1/3,1/2]
.

Theorem 4.10

(Pathwise Uniqueness in the Rough Regime)
.

Let
T>0
,
d_{1},\,d_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
,
B^{H}
be an fBm with Hurst parameter
H\in(1/3,1/2]
,
\gamma\in((1-H)/H,2)
and
\sigma\in\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}};\mathcal{M}(d_{1},d_{2}))
be such that
\sigma\sigma^{\top}
is uniformly elliptic, i.e. there exists an
l\in[1,\infty)
such that

l^{-1}\leq\sigma(x)\sigma^{\top}(x)\leq l
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
.
Then, pathwise uniqueness holds for (
65
) in the sense of Def.
4.5
.

Before giving the proof of Thm.
4.10
, we require some preparations. Let
\gamma\in(\frac{1-H}{H},2)
be as above and fix an
\alpha\in(1/3,H)
such that

\gamma+\gamma\alpha>1
.
Let
\rho\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}};\mathcal{M}(d_{1},d_{2}))
(which we will eventually think of as a smooth approximation to
\sigma
)
and
(X,X^{\prime}),(Y,Y^{\prime})\in\mathscr{D}^{\gamma\alpha}_{B^{H}}
(see Def.
2.2
).
Then, we set

\displaystyle G_{t}^{k}\vcentcolon=G[\rho]^{k}_{t}\vcentcolon=\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{k}\rho(\theta X_{r}+(1-\theta)Y_{r})\,\mathrm{d}\theta\Big)\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}^{H},\quad\text{for}\,k=1,\ldots,d.

(74)

The integral is understood as a rough integral; since
\rho
was assumed to be smooth for now, the path

\displaystyle t\mapsto\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{k}\rho(\theta X_{t}+(1-\theta)Y_{t})\,\mathrm{d}\theta

is controlled by
B^{H}
with Gubinelli derivative

\displaystyle t\mapsto\int_{0}^{1}\nabla\partial_{k}\rho(\theta X_{t}+(1-\theta)Y_{t})(\theta X_{t}^{\prime}+(1-\theta)Y_{t}^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

We furthermore set for
k=1,\ldots,d_{1}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{s,t}^{k}\vcentcolon=\mathcal{G}[\rho]_{s,t}^{k}=\int_{s}^{t}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{k}\rho(\theta X_{t}+(1-\theta)Y_{t})\,\mathrm{d}\theta\Big)B_{s,r}^{H}\otimes\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}^{H},

(75)

and define the vector of paths

\mathbf{G}^{k}\vcentcolon=(G^{k},\mathcal{G}^{k})
. Note that
(\mathbf{G}^{k})_{k=1}^{d}
does not satisfy Chen’s relation (see (
77
) below) so it is not strictly speaking a rough path, however, the same analytic tools may be applied to it. By the remainder estimate of rough integration (
20
) and assumed regularity of
\rho
, we may define the following finite quantity,

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{G}^{k}\|_{\alpha,\gamma}\vcentcolon=\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\left(\frac{\lvert G^{k}_{s,t}\rvert}{(t-s)^{\alpha}}+\frac{\lvert\mathcal{G}^{k}_{s,t}\rvert}{(t-s)^{\gamma\alpha}}\right)<\infty.

(76)

Finally, we observe the following
modified
Chen’s relation

\mathcal{G}_{s,t}^{k}=\mathcal{G}_{s,u}^{k}+\mathcal{G}_{u,t}^{k}+B_{s,u}^{H}\otimes G_{u,t}^{k}.

(77)

More abstractly, we can consider any pair
\mathbf{G}=(G,\mathcal{G})
satisfying the analytic condition (
76
) and
the algebraic condition (
77
).
Let
Z
be a path controlled by
B^{H}
.
It is not difficult to see (simply by repeating the arguments in the usual rough path setting, e.g.
[
FH20
, Thm. 4.10]
) that
the integral

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}Z_{r}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{G}_{r}\vcentcolon=\lim_{\lvert\pi\rvert\to 0}\sum_{[u,v]\in\pi}(Z_{u}G_{u,v}+Z^{\prime}_{u}\mathcal{G}_{u,v})

(78)

exists, and we have the quantitative remainder estimate

\displaystyle\Big\lvert\int_{s}^{t}Z_{r}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{G}_{r}-Z_{s}G_{s,t}-Z_{s}^{\prime}\mathcal{G}_{s,t}\Big\rvert\lesssim_{\gamma,\alpha}\lVert Z\rVert_{\mathscr{D}_{B^{H}}^{\gamma\alpha}}\lVert\mathbf{G}\rVert_{\alpha,\gamma}(t-s)^{(1+\gamma)\alpha}.

(79)

Furthermore, as in
[
FH20
, Thm. 4.17]
, we have the following stability estimate: by defining the metric

\displaystyle d_{\alpha,\gamma}(\mathbf{G},\bar{\mathbf{G}})\vcentcolon=\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\left(\frac{\lvert G_{s,t}-\bar{G}_{s,t}\rvert}{(t-s)^{\alpha}}+\frac{\lvert\mathcal{G}_{s,t}-\bar{\mathcal{G}}_{s,t}\rvert}{(t-s)^{\gamma\alpha}}\right),

(80)

and provided that

\lVert\mathbf{G}\rVert_{\alpha,\gamma}+\lVert\bar{\mathbf{G}}\rVert_{\alpha,\gamma}\leq M

for some
M\geq 1
,
we have

\displaystyle\Big\lvert\int_{s}^{t}Z_{r}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{G}_{r}-\int_{s}^{t}Z_{r}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mathbf{G}}_{r}\Big\rvert\lesssim_{\gamma,\alpha,M}\lVert Z\rVert_{\mathscr{D}^{\gamma\alpha}_{B^{H}}}d_{\alpha}(\mathbf{G},\bar{\mathbf{G}})(t-s)^{\alpha}.

(81)

With the above in place and continuing to treat
\rho
as smooth, we obtain the following linearisation, as laid out in Sec.
1.2
.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\gamma\in((1-H)/H,2)
,
\alpha\in(1/3,H)
be such that
\gamma+\gamma\alpha>1
,
\rho\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}};\mathcal{M}(d_{1},d_{2}))
,
(X,X^{\prime}),(Y,Y^{\prime})\in\mathscr{D}^{\gamma\alpha}_{B^{H}}

and for
k=1,2,\ldots,d_{1}
let us set
\mathbf{G}^{k}[\rho]=(G^{k}[\rho],\mathcal{G}^{k}[\rho])
as in (
74
) and (
75
). Then it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left(\rho(X_{r})-\rho(Y_{r})\right)\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}^{H}=\sum_{k=1}^{d_{1}}\int_{0}^{t}(X^{k}_{r}-Y^{k}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{G}_{r}^{k}[\rho],

where the integral with respect to
\mathbf{G}^{k}[\rho]
is understood in the same sense as (
78
).

Proof.

The proof is completely pathwise.
We set

\displaystyle A^{1}_{s,t}

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=\left(\rho(X_{s})-\rho(Y_{s})\right)B^{H}_{s,t}+\left(\nabla\rho(X_{s})X_{s}^{\prime}-\nabla\rho(Y_{s})Y_{s}^{\prime}\right)\mathbb{B}^{H}_{s,t},

\displaystyle A^{2}_{s,t}

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=\sum_{k=1}^{d_{1}}(X^{k}_{s}-Y^{k}_{s})G_{s,t}^{k}+\left((X^{\prime}_{s})^{k}-(Y^{\prime}_{s})^{k}\right)\mathcal{G}^{k}_{s,t},

where as usual
X^{k}
denotes the
k^{\text{th}}
element of
X\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
and
(X^{\prime})^{k}
denotes the
k^{\text{th}}
row of the matrix
X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}(d_{1},d_{2})
, similarly for
Y,\,Y^{\prime}
.
In addition, the second term in
A^{1}_{s,t}
should be understood as a
d_{1}
-dimensional vector whose
i^{\text{th}}
component is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{j,k,l}\left(\partial_{k}\rho^{ij}(X_{s})(X^{\prime}_{s})^{kl}-\partial_{k}\rho^{ij}(Y_{s})(Y^{\prime}_{s})^{kl}\right)\mathbb{B}_{s,t}^{H,lj}.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\big(\,\rho(X_{r})-\rho(Y_{r})\big)\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\text{ is a partition of }[0,t],\\
\lvert\pi\rvert\to 0\end{subarray}}\sum_{[u,v]\in\pi}A^{1}_{u,v},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{d_{1}}\int_{0}^{t}\big(X^{k}_{r}-Y^{k}_{r}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{G}_{r}^{k}[\rho]

\displaystyle=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\text{ is a partition of }[0,t],\\
\lvert\pi\rvert\to 0\end{subarray}}\sum_{[u,v]\in\pi}A^{2}_{u,v}.

Therefore, by the uniqueness part of the deterministic sewing lemma (Rem.
2.7
), it suffices to show
\lvert A^{1}_{s,t}-A^{2}_{s,t}\rvert\lesssim(t-s)^{3H}
.
It follows from the mean value theorem that we have both

\displaystyle\rho(X_{s})-\rho(Y_{s})=\sum_{k=1}^{d_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{k}\rho(\theta X_{s}+(1-\theta)Y_{s})(X^{k}_{s}-Y^{k}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}\theta

(82)

and

\nabla\rho(X_{s})X_{s}^{\prime}-\nabla\rho(Y_{s})Y_{s}^{\prime}=\sum_{k=1}^{d_{1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}(X^{k}_{s}-Y^{k}_{s})\partial_{k}\nabla\rho(\theta X_{s}+(1-\theta)Y_{s})\\
\times(\theta X_{s}^{\prime}+(1-\theta)Y_{s}^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\theta+\int_{0}^{1}(X_{s}^{\prime}-Y_{s}^{\prime})^{k}\partial_{k}\rho(\theta X_{s}+(1-\theta)Y_{s})\,\mathrm{d}\theta\Big).

(83)

By (
82
) and (
83
), we compute

\displaystyle A^{2}_{s,t}-A^{1}_{s,t}=\sum_{k=1}^{d_{1}}\left((X^{k}_{s}-Y^{k}_{s})\Delta^{1,k}_{s,t}+\left((X^{\prime}_{s})^{k}-(Y^{\prime}_{s})^{k}\right)\Delta^{2,k}_{s,t}\right),

where

\displaystyle\Delta^{1,k}_{s,t}\vcentcolon=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{k}\rho(\theta X_{r}+(1-\theta)Y_{r})\,\mathrm{d}\theta\Big)\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r}-\left(\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{k}\rho(\theta X_{s}+(1-\theta)Y_{s})\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)B^{H}_{s,t}

\displaystyle-\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{k}\nabla\rho(\theta X_{s}+(1-\theta)Y_{s})(\theta X_{s}^{\prime}+(1-\theta)Y_{s}^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\theta\Big)\mathbb{B}^{H}_{s,t}

and

\Delta^{2,k}_{s,t}\vcentcolon=\int_{s}^{t}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{k}\rho(\theta X_{r}+(1-\theta)Y_{r})\,\mathrm{d}\theta\Big)B^{H}_{s,r}\otimes\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r}\\
-\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{k}\rho(\theta X_{s}+(1-\theta)Y_{s})\,\mathrm{d}\theta\Big)\mathbb{B}^{H}_{s,t}.

By the remainder estimate of the rough integral (
20
) and assumed regularity of
\rho
,
both
\Delta^{1,k}_{s,t}
and
\Delta^{2,k}_{s,t}
are bounded by
(t-s)^{3H}
up to constant,
and the proof is complete.
∎

4.10
, the key step being
to use Prop.
4.7
,
4.8
&
4.9
to prove the convergence of
the rough path lifts from
(B^{H},\int\partial_{k}\sigma_{n}(X_{r})\otimes\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r})

), where
the sequence
(\sigma_{n})
of smooth maps approximates to

.

Proof of Thm.
4.10
.

We assume that (
60
) holds, as the general case is handled by stopping time arguments as exhibited in the proof of Thm.
3.8
.
Let
(X,B^{H})
and
(Y,B^{H})
be two weak solutions to (
65
) defined on the same filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
and driven by the same
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-fBm,
B^{H}
.
Since the requirement on

\gamma^{\prime}\in((1-H)/H,\gamma)

Y
.
In view of (

), we then have

\displaystyle X_{t}-Y_{t}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sigma_{n}(X_{r})-\sigma_{n}(Y_{r})\right)\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{r},

and so by Lem.
4.11
,

\displaystyle X_{t}-Y_{t}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{d_{1}}\int_{0}^{t}(X^{k}_{r}-Y^{k}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{G}^{k}_{r}[\sigma_{n}].

By repeating the argument of Lem.
3.9
, we can show that

(X,\sigma(X))\in\mathfrak{D}^{1,l}_{B^{H}}
and similarly for
Y
.
Furthermore, by the condition (
60
), as checked in Step 1 of the proof of Thm.
3.8
, we have

\displaystyle\theta X+(1-\theta)Y\in\mathfrak{D}^{1,l/4}_{B^{H}},\quad\forall\theta\in[0,1].

Therefore, by Prop.
4.7
&
4.8
(with

),
and the Kolmogorov criterion for rough paths
(
[

, Thm. 3.1]
), the sequence of the lifts

converges in

to some limit

\mathbf{G}^{k}[\sigma]=(G^{k}[\sigma],\mathcal{G}^{k}[\sigma])
in the metric

defined by (
80
).
Namely, for all

and

,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\lVert d_{\alpha,\gamma^{\prime}}(\mathbf{G}^{k}[\sigma_{n}],\mathbf{G}^{k}[\sigma])\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}=0

The stability estimate (
81
) yields

\displaystyle X_{t}-Y_{t}=\sum_{k=1}^{d_{1}}\int_{0}^{t}(X^{k}_{r}-Y^{k}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{G}^{k}_{r}[\sigma]\quad\text{a.s.}

The remainder estimate (
79
) shows that
X-Y
is controlled by
(G^{k})_{k=1}^{d_{1}}
with
Gubinelli derivative
X-Y
.
Moreover, similarly to
\mathbf{G}^{k}[\sigma]
, by Prop.
4.9
(with
f=\partial_{k}\sigma_{n}-\partial_{k}\sigma_{m}
,
g=\partial_{l}\sigma_{n}
and
f=\partial_{k}\sigma_{n}
,
g=\partial_{l}\sigma_{n}-\partial_{l}\sigma_{m}
) and the Kolmogorov criterion for rough paths, the sequence of the rough paths

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{G}}[\sigma_{n}]\vcentcolon=\Big((G^{k}[\sigma_{n}])_{k=1}^{d_{1}},\Big(\int G^{k}[\sigma_{n}]\otimes\,\mathrm{d}G^{l}[\sigma_{n}]\Big)_{k,l=1}^{d_{1}}\Big)

converges to some limit
\hat{\mathbf{G}}[\sigma]
.
We claim that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(X^{k}_{r}-Y^{k}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{G}^{k}_{r}[\sigma]=\int_{0}^{t}(X^{k}_{r}-Y^{k}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}\hat{\mathbf{G}}^{k}_{r}[\sigma]\quad\text{a.s.}

(84)

We note that the integral in the right-hand side is defined as rough integral with respect to
\hat{\mathbf{G}}[\sigma]
, which is well-defined since

X-Y
is controlled by
(G^{k})_{k=1}^{d_{1}}
with Gubinelli derivative
X-Y
.
To see (
84
), we again rely on the uniqueness part of the sewing lemma (Rem.
2.7
). By definition we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(X^{k}_{r}-Y^{k}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{G}^{k}_{r}-\int_{0}^{t}(X^{k}_{r}-Y^{k}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}\hat{\mathbf{G}}^{k}_{r}=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\text{ is a partition of }[0,t],\\
\lvert\pi\rvert\to 0\end{subarray}}\sum_{[u,v]\in\pi}\Delta_{u,v},

where

\displaystyle\Delta_{s,t}\vcentcolon=

\displaystyle\,(\sigma(X_{s})-\sigma(Y_{s}))\mathcal{G}^{k}_{s,t}[\sigma]-\sum_{l=1}^{d_{1}}(X^{l}_{s}-Y^{l}_{s})\int_{s}^{t}G^{l}_{s,r}[\sigma]\otimes\,\mathrm{d}G_{r}^{k}[\sigma]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{d_{1}}(X^{l}_{s}-Y^{l}_{s})\left(\left(\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{l}\sigma(\theta X_{s}+(1-\theta)Y_{s})\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)\mathcal{G}^{k}_{s,t}[\sigma]-\int_{s}^{t}G^{l}_{s,r}[\sigma]\otimes\,\mathrm{d}G_{r}^{k}[\sigma]\right).

By Prop.
4.8
&
4.9
,

\Big\lVert\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{l}\sigma(\theta X_{s}+(1-\theta)Y_{s})\,\mathrm{d}\theta\Big)\mathcal{G}^{k}_{s,t}-\int_{s}^{t}G^{l}_{s,r}\otimes\,\mathrm{d}G_{r}^{k}\,\Big\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim(t-s)^{(1+\gamma^{\prime})H},

and as
(1+\gamma)H>1
the identity (
84
) is established.

Hence,
X-Y
solves the linear rough differential equation

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}Z_{t}=\sum_{k=1}^{d_{1}}Z_{t}^{k}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{\mathbf{G}}_{t}^{k},\quad Z_{0}=0,

and its uniqueness implies that for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega
we have
X(\omega)=Y(\omega)
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Appendix A
Properties of Heat Semi-Groups

We collect some standard, useful results concerning heat semi-groups with positive definite conductances.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
c\in(1,\infty)
and
\Gamma\in\mathcal{M}_{+}
such that
c^{-1}\leq\Gamma\leq c
.
Then,
for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
with
\beta\geq 0\vee\alpha
and
t\in(0,T)
one has

\lVert\mathcal{P}_{t\Gamma}f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}_{x}}\lesssim_{c,\alpha,\beta}(t\wedge 1)^{-\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}_{x}}.

Proof.

We write

\Lambda_{\Gamma}f(x)\vcentcolon=f(\sqrt{\Gamma}x).

(85)

Then
\mathcal{P}_{t\Gamma}f=\Lambda_{\Gamma^{-1}}\mathcal{P}_{t}\Lambda_{\Gamma}f
.
Since

\lVert\Lambda_{\Gamma}f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\gamma}}\lesssim_{c,\gamma}\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\gamma}}\lesssim_{c,\gamma}\lVert\Lambda_{\Gamma}f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\gamma}},

we can assume that
\Gamma=I_{d}
. Then the claim follows from
[
DGL23
, Lem. 2.3]
.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
c\in(1,\infty)
and let
\Gamma_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}

(i=1,2)
be such that
c^{-1}\leq\Gamma_{i}\leq c
. Then,
for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
with
\beta\geq\alpha\vee 0
and
t\in(0,T)
we have

\lVert(\mathcal{P}_{t\Gamma_{1}}-\mathcal{P}_{t\Gamma_{2}})f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\beta}}\lesssim_{c,\alpha,\beta}(t\wedge 1)^{-\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}\lvert\Gamma_{1}-\Gamma_{2}\rvert\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\alpha}}.

Proof.

We first claim

\lVert(\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{1}t}-\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{2}t})f\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim_{c}\lvert\Gamma_{1}-\Gamma_{2}\rvert\lVert f\rVert_{L^{\infty}}.

(86)

Indeed,
we have

(\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{1}t}-\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{2}t})f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}\left(p_{\Gamma_{1}t}(y)-p_{\Gamma_{2}t}(y)\right)f(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

By
[
DGL23
, Prop. 2.7]
,

\lvert p_{\Gamma_{1}t}(y)-p_{\Gamma_{2}t}(y)\rvert\lesssim_{c}\lvert\Gamma_{1}-\Gamma_{2}\rvert(p_{\Gamma_{1}t/2}(y)+p_{\Gamma_{2}t/2}(y)),

(87)

which yields the estimate (
86
).

Next we prove the estimate (
16
) for
\alpha=\beta\notin\mathbb{N}
.
The proof uses the characterization of Besov spaces by Littlewood–Paley blocks
[
BCD11
]
.
The case for
\alpha=\beta=0
is proved by (
86
).
Let
\{\Delta_{j}\}_{j=-1}^{\infty}
be Littlewood–Paley blocks.
Since
\Delta_{j}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{i}t}
commute, the estimate (
86
) yields

\lVert\Delta_{j}(\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{1}t}-\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{2}t})f\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim_{c}\lvert\Gamma_{1}-\Gamma_{2}\rvert\lVert\Delta_{j}f\rVert_{L^{\infty}}.

Therefore,

\lVert(\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{1}t}-\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{2}t})f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}_{x}}\lesssim_{c}\lvert\Gamma_{1}-\Gamma_{2}\rvert\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}_{x}}.

Next we prove the estimate (
16
) for
\alpha=\beta\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since we only need the case
\alpha=\beta=1
, we only prove that case. We have

\lvert(\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{1}t}-\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{2}t})f(x)-(\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{1}t}-\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{2}t})f(y)\rvert\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}\lvert p_{\Gamma_{1}t}(z)-p_{\Gamma_{2}t}(z)\rvert\lvert f(x-z)-f(y-z)\rvert\,\mathrm{d}z.

By (
87
),

\lvert(\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{1}t}-\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{2}t})f(x)-(\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{1}t}-\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{2}t})f(y)\rvert\\
\lesssim_{c}\lvert\Gamma_{1}-\Gamma_{2}\rvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}\lvert p_{\Gamma_{1}t/2}(z)+p_{\Gamma_{2}t/2}(z)\rvert\lvert f(x-z)-f(y-z)\rvert\,\mathrm{d}z,

which is then bounded by

2\lvert\Gamma_{1}-\Gamma_{2}\rvert\lVert f\rVert_{C^{1}}\lvert x-y\rvert
.

Finally, we prove the general case. Since

\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{1}t}-\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{2}t}=(\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{1}t/2}-\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{2}t/2})\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{1}t/2}+\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{2}t/2}(\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{1}t/2}-\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{2}t/2}),

we have

\lVert(\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{1}t}-\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{2}t})f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}_{x}}\leq\lVert(\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{1}t/2}-\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{2}t/2})\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{1}t/2}f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}_{x}}+\lVert\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{2}t/2}(\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{1}t/2}-\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{2}t/2})f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}_{x}}.

As for the first term, the estimate (
16
) with
\alpha=\beta
and Lem.
A.1

imply

\lVert(\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{1}t/2}-\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{2}t/2})\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{1}t/2}f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}_{x}}\lesssim_{c}\lvert\Gamma_{1}-\Gamma_{2}\rvert\lVert\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{1}t/2}f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}_{x}}\lesssim_{c,\alpha,\beta}\lvert\Gamma_{1}-\Gamma_{2}\rvert(t\wedge 1)^{-\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\alpha}}.

The estimate of the second term is similar.
∎

Lemma B.1

\displaystyle=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\text{ is a partition of }[u,t],\\
\lvert\pi\rvert\to 0\end{subarray}}\sum_{[s^{\prime},t^{\prime}]\in\pi}\mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{c_{s^{\prime}}\tilde{B}^{s,i}_{s^{\prime}}+c_{t^{\prime}}\tilde{B}^{s,i}_{t^{\prime}}}{2}\tilde{B}^{s,i}_{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{u}^{t}c_{r}\,\mathrm{d}(\tilde{B}^{s,i}_{r})^{2}\Big]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{u}^{t}c_{r}\rho_{H}^{2}(s,\mathrm{d}r),

and we obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{u}^{t}g(a\tilde{B}^{s}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{B}^{s,i}_{r}\right]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{u}^{t}\mathbb{E}[g(a\tilde{B}_{r}^{s})\tilde{B}_{r}^{s,i}]\frac{\rho_{H}^{2}(s,\mathrm{d}r)}{\rho_{H}^{2}(s,r)}.

(95)

Gaussian integration by parts yields

\mathbb{E}[g(a\tilde{B}_{r}^{s})\tilde{B}_{r}^{s,i}]=\rho_{H}^{2}(s,r)\sum_{j=1}^{d_{1}}a^{ji}\mathbb{E}[\partial_{j}g(a\tilde{B}^{s}_{r})]=\rho_{H}^{2}(s,r)\sum_{j=1}^{d_{1}}a^{ji}[\partial_{j}\mathcal{P}_{\rho^{2}_{H}(s,r)aa^{\mathrm{T}}}g](0).

(96)

Combining (
95
) and (
96
), we obtain the desired identity.
∎

However, unlike
[

Lemma B.4
.

Let
H\in(1/3,1/2)
and we define
K_{H}
by (
27
).
Then, for
0\leq v<s\leq t
, we have

\displaystyle\Big\lvert\int_{0}^{v}K_{H}(s,r)\partial_{t}K_{H}(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}r\Big\rvert\lesssim(s-v)^{2H-1}.

Proof.

Recalling (
27
) and observing

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}K_{H}(t,s)=c_{H}\Big(H-\frac{1}{2}\Big)\Big(\frac{t}{s}\Big)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}(t-s)^{H-\frac{3}{2}},

it suffices to prove

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{v}\Big(\frac{s}{r}\Big)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}(s-r)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{t}{r}\Big)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}(t-r)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}r\lesssim(s-v)^{2H-1},

(97)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{v}\Big(\frac{t}{r}\Big)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}(t-r)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}r^{\frac{1}{2}-H}\Big(\int_{r}^{s}u^{H-\frac{3}{2}}(u-r)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}u\Big)\,\mathrm{d}r\lesssim(s-v)^{2H-1}.

(98)

The estimate (
97
) is straightforward. Indeed, noting that
r/t\leq 1
,
r/s\leq 1
and
s-r\leq t-r
, the
left-hand side is bounded by

(s-v)^{2H-1}
up to constant.

To prove (
98
), using again
r/t\leq 1
and also for
r\in[0,v]

\displaystyle(t-r)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}=(t-r)^{2H-1}(t-r)^{-H-\frac{1}{2}}\leq(s-v)^{2H-1}(t-r)^{-H-\frac{1}{2}},

the left-hand side of (
98
) is bounded by
(s-v)^{2H-1}I
, where

\displaystyle I\vcentcolon=\int_{0}^{v}(t-r)^{-H-\frac{1}{2}}r^{\frac{1}{2}-H}\Big(\int_{r}^{t}u^{H-\frac{3}{2}}(u-r)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}u\Big)\,\mathrm{d}r.

Therefore, it suffices to show
I\lesssim 1
.
Applying the bound

\displaystyle u^{H-\frac{3}{2}}=u^{-1}u^{H-\frac{1}{2}}\leq r^{-1}(u-r)^{H-\frac{1}{2}},\quad u\in[r,t],

we get

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{v}(t-r)^{-H-\frac{1}{2}}r^{\frac{1}{2}-H}r^{-1}\Big(\int_{r}^{t}(u-r)^{2H-1}\,\mathrm{d}u\Big)\,\mathrm{d}r\lesssim\int_{0}^{v}(t-r)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}r^{-\frac{1}{2}-H}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}r^{-\frac{1}{2}-H}\,\mathrm{d}r=\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}s^{-\frac{1}{2}-H}\,\mathrm{d}s.\qed

Lemma B.5
.

Let
H\in(1/3,1/2]
, and we define
\tilde{B}^{H,v}
by (
29
) and
\rho_{H}(v,t)
by (
30
).
Let
v<s<t
.

1.

For
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,d_{2}\}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{B}_{s}^{H,v,i}\tilde{B}_{s,t}^{H,v,i}\right]\right|+\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{B}_{t}^{H,v,i}\tilde{B}_{s,t}^{H,v,i}\right]\right|\lesssim(t-s)^{2H}+(s-v)^{2H-1}(t-s).

2.

We have
\lvert\rho_{H}^{2}(v,t)-\rho_{H}^{2}(v,s)\rvert\lesssim(t-s)^{2H}+(s-v)^{2H-1}(t-s)
.

Proof.

When
H=1/2
the claim is trivial, so we suppose that
H<1/2
.

Proof of 1.
By (
31
), we have

\mathbb{E}[\lvert B^{H,v}_{s,t}\rvert^{2}]\lesssim(t-s)^{2H}
.
Hence, it suffices to estimate
\mathbb{E}[\tilde{B}^{H,v,i}_{s}\tilde{B}^{H,v,i}_{s,t}]
.
Recall
Y^{H,v}
from (
28
). Since
Y^{H,v}
and
\tilde{B}^{H,v}
are independent and
B^{H}=Y^{H,v}+\tilde{B}^{H,v}
,
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[B_{s}^{H,i}B_{s,t}^{H,i}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{s}^{H,v,i}Y_{s,t}^{H,v,i}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{B}_{s}^{H,v,i}\tilde{B}_{s,t}^{H,v,i}\right].

As for
B^{H}
, the computation is explicit, and it is easy to see that the left-hand side is bounded by
(t-s)^{2H}
up to constant.
Therefore, it suffices to prove
\lvert\mathbb{E}[Y_{s}^{H,v,i}Y_{s,t}^{H,v,i}]\rvert\lesssim(s-v)^{2H-1}(t-s)
.
However, this follows from the bound

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left[Y^{H,v,i}_{s}\dot{Y}^{H,v,i}_{t}\right]\right|\lesssim(s-v)^{2H-1},

which in turn follows from Lem.
B.4
.

Proof of 2.
We have

\displaystyle\rho^{2}_{H}(v,t)-\rho^{2}_{H}(v,s)+\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(Y^{H,v,i}_{t}\Big)^{2}\Big]-\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(Y^{H,v,i}_{s}\Big)^{2}\Big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(B_{t}^{H,i}\Big)^{2}\Big]-\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(B_{s}^{H,i}\Big)^{2}\Big].

Since

\displaystyle 0\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(B_{t}^{H,i}\Big)^{2}\Big]-\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(B_{s}^{H,i}\Big)^{2}\Big]=t^{2H}-s^{2H}\leq(t-s)^{2H},

it suffices to prove
\lvert\mathbb{E}[(Y^{H,v,i}_{t})^{2}]-\mathbb{E}[(Y^{H,v,i}_{s})^{2}]\rvert\lesssim(s-v)^{2H-1}(t-s)
. However, this follows from the bound

\displaystyle\Big\lvert\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(Y^{H,v,i}_{t}\Big)^{2}\Big]\Big\rvert=2\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big[Y^{H,v,i}_{t}\dot{Y}^{H,v,i}_{t}\Big]\Big|\lesssim(t-v)^{2H-1},

which follows from Lem.
B.4
.
∎

Lemma B.6
.

Let
H\in(1/3,1/2]
and
B^{H}
be an
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-fBm.
We fix a
\tau\in[0,T]
and define
\tilde{B}^{H,\tau}
by (
29
).
Let
\xi
be an
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
-measurable random variable valued in some measurable space
E
.
Suppose that
F\colon E\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies

\displaystyle\lvert F(x,y_{1})-F(x,y_{2})\rvert\leq M(x)\lvert y_{1}-y_{2}\rvert^{\gamma}

for all
x\in E
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
,
with
\gamma\in(\frac{1}{2H}-1,1]
.
We set

\displaystyle A_{s,t}\vcentcolon=(F(\xi,B^{H}_{s})+F(\xi,B^{H}_{t}))\tilde{B}_{s,t}^{H,\tau,i}.

For
\tau\leq v<s<u<t
with
t-s\leq s-v
, we then have

\displaystyle\lVert\delta A_{s,u,t}\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,\gamma}\lVert M(\xi)\rVert_{L^{2p}_{\omega}}(t-s)^{(1+\gamma)H},

(99)

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbb{E}[\delta A_{s,u,t}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,\gamma}\lVert M(\xi)\rVert_{L^{2p}_{\omega}}\Big(\frac{t-s}{s-v}\Big)^{(3-\gamma)H}(t-s)^{(1+\gamma)H}.

(100)

Proof.

We essentially repeat the argument of
[
MP22
, Prop. 3.5]
.
For simplicity, we drop scripts on
H
.
We first consider the estimate (
99
).
We compute

\displaystyle\delta A_{s,u,t}=F(\xi,B)_{u,t}\tilde{B}^{\tau,i}_{s,u}-F(\xi,B)_{s,u}\tilde{B}^{\tau,i}_{u,t},

and

\displaystyle\lvert\delta A_{s,u,t}\rvert\leq M(\xi)\big[\lvert B_{u,t}\rvert^{\gamma}\lvert\tilde{B}^{\tau}_{s,u}\rvert+\lvert B_{s,u}\rvert^{\gamma}\lvert\tilde{B}^{\tau}_{u,t}\rvert\big].

In view of (
31
), it is easy to see the estimate (
99
).

We turn to the estimate (
100
).
We recall
Y^{v}
from (
28
) and
Y^{\tau,v}
from (
89
), and we recall the decomposition

B=Y^{v}+\tilde{B}^{v}
and
\tilde{B}^{\tau}=Y^{\tau,v}+\tilde{B}^{v}
,
where
Y^{v}
and
Y^{\tau,v}
are measurable with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{v}
and
\tilde{B}^{v}
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{v}
.
We set

a_{0}(s)\vcentcolon=\mathbb{E}[F(x,y_{s}+\tilde{B}^{v}_{s})]|_{x=\xi,y=Y^{v}},\quad a_{i}(s)\vcentcolon=\rho^{-2}(v,s)\mathbb{E}[F(x,y_{s}+\tilde{B}^{v}_{s})\tilde{B}^{v,i}_{s}]|_{x=\xi,y=Y^{v}}

with
\rho=\rho_{H}

As in
[

, Prop. 3.5]

\mathbb{E}[\delta A_{s,u,t}|\mathcal{F}_{v}]=D^{0}_{s,u,t}+D^{i}_{s,u,t}

where

\displaystyle D^{0}_{s,u,t}

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=(a_{0}(t)-a_{0}(u))Y_{s,u}^{\tau,v,i}+(a_{0}(s)-a_{0}(u))Y_{u,t}^{\tau,v,i},

\displaystyle D^{i}_{s,u,t}

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=(a_{i}(t)-a_{i}(u))\mathbb{E}[\tilde{B}_{t}^{v,i}\tilde{B}_{s,t}^{v,i}]+(a_{i}(s)-a_{i}(u))\mathbb{E}[\tilde{B}_{s}^{v,i}\tilde{B}_{s,t}^{v,i}]

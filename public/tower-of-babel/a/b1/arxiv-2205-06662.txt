Polarization Tracking in the Presence of PDL and Fast Temporal Drift
Mohammad Farsi, Christian Häger, Magnus Karlsson, Erik Agrell

Abstract
In this paper, we analyze the effectiveness of polarization tracking algorithms in optical transmission systems suffering from fast state of polarization (SOP) rotations and polarization-dependent loss (PDL). While most of the gradient descent (GD)-based algorithms in the literature may require step size adjustment when the channel condition changes, we propose tracking algorithms that can perform similarly or better without parameter tuning. Numerical simulation results show higher robustness of the proposed algorithms to SOP and PDL drift compared to GD-based algorithms, making them promising candidates to be used in aerial fiber links where the SOP can potentially drift rapidly, and therefore becomes challenging to track.



I
Introduction

Advanced digital signal processing enables the use of multilevel modulation formats and polarization-division multiplexed transmission to achieve high spectral efficiency
[
1
]
. However, spectrally-efficient systems have a lower tolerance to fiber impairments such as state of polarization drift and polarization-dependent loss, which need to be adaptively estimated and tracked at the receiver
[
2
]
.

In an optical link, the random state of polarization drift originates from the random variation of environmental conditions (mechanical/thermal stress, weather conditions, splices, etc.) and internal imperfections (asymmetry of the core, manufacturing process errors, etc.) of the fiber. Experimental measurements show that the state of polarization drift can be extremely slow (days and hours) in buried fibers
[
3
]
and very fast (microseconds) in aerial fibers
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
. For instance, field measurements of an aerial fiber link have revealed that the rotation rate of state of polarization might get up to 5.1 Mrad/s due to lightning strikes
[
7
]
. In the literature, the state of polarization fluctuation is often modeled as randomly chosen rotations without drift
[
8
,
9
]
or cyclic/quasi-cyclic deterministic rotation
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
. Recently, an experimentally verified model was proposed in
[
13
]
, where state of polarization drift is described by a random walk on the Poincaré sphere.

In a long-haul fiber, several polarization-dependent loss-inducing components (e.g., isolators, amplifiers, multiplexers, and couplers) are in place, and the overall polarization-dependent loss might aggregate to several dBs. Historically, polarization-dependent loss has been modeled as a concatenation of many randomly oriented polarization-dependent loss elements along with the fiber, and its statistics have been extensively studied in
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
, describing the aggregated polarization-dependent loss with a Maxwellian distribution. The impact of the average polarization-dependent loss on the performance of the optical link is studied in
[
18
,
19
]
. The interplay of polarization-dependent loss with polarization-mode dispersion and Kerr nonlinearity has been studied in
[
20
]
, and
[
21
]
, respectively.

The state of polarization drift accompanied by polarization-dependent loss not only results in a time-varying power imbalance between the two polarizations, but also it causes optical signal-to-noise ratio fluctuations and breaks the orthogonality between the two polarizations
[
22
]
. While it would be feasible to resolve the static state of polarization and polarization-dependent loss, the time-varying nature of state of polarization and the aggregated polarization-dependent loss makes the polarization tracking challenging at the receiver.

The gradient descent-based algorithms has been widely applied in both wireless and optical fields for adaptive tracking, and the most popular one is the constant modulus algorithm
[
23
]
and its variants known as modified CMA
[
24
]
and multi-modulus algorithm
[
25
]
. Although constant modulus algorithm is immune to phase noise and has low computational complexity, it is modulation dependent and suffers from phase ambiguity and the so-called singularity problem
[
26
]
, where the equalizer converges only on one of two polarizations.
The decision-directed least mean squares algorithm
[
27
,
28
]
removes the modulation format dependence of the blind algorithms
[
11
]
, and the phase ambiguity can be resolved by differential encoding/decoding at the expense of a performance degradation
[
29
, Sec. 2.6.1]
.
Although many have tried to overcome the singularity problem of constant modulus algorithm
[
30
,
31
]
, its solution remains an open research problem. The pilot-aided hybrid algorithms
[
32
,
33
]
ensure reliable and fast convergence by proper tap initialization of the blind algorithms.
For instance, in
[
33
]
, a pilot-based filter tap initialization using the least mean squares
[
34
]
algorithm has been proposed to address the singularity problem. For a linear time-invariant system with stationary noise, the least mean squares solution converges to the well-known Wiener filter solution
[
35
]
,
[
36
, Ch. 13.2]
.

The memoryless dual-polarization channel in the presence of state of polarization drift and a negligible amount of polarization-dependent loss can be described by a time-varying complex unitary matrix. Most of the tracking algorithms in the literature (e.g., constant modulus algorithm, multi-modulus algorithm, decision-directed least mean squares, etc.) are designed for a general complex channel matrix without constraining their channel estimation matrix to be unitary. The tracking performance can be improved by explicitly taking the unitary nature of the SOP rotations into account when designing tracking algorithms (even if the overall channel is not exactly unitary). For example, the block-wise decision-directed Kabsch algorithm was proposed in
[
37
]
to address the unitary constraints of the channel.
In
[
38
]
, a unitary gradient descent-based decision-directed joint phase noise and state of polarization tracking algorithm was proposed, where the authors have shown a higher polarization drift tolerance than the Kabsch and constant modulus algorithm-based algorithms. We refer to the algorithm in
[
38
]
as decision-directed Czegledi.

This paper, which extends the conference paper
[
39
]
, proposes two polarization tracking algorithms called sliding window Kabsch and sliding window least squares, described in Sections
III-A
and
III-B1
, respectively.
When the polarization-dependent loss is negligible, sliding window Kabsch shows higher state of polarization drift tolerance than sliding window least squares and the gradient descent-based algorithms (e.g., decision-directed Czegledi, constant modulus algorithm and its variants). However, considering polarization-dependent loss, the channel is no longer unitary, and sliding window least squares shows the highest state of polarization drift tolerance. Moreover, unlike gradient descent-based algorithms that require step size adjustment, the proposed algorithms require no further parameter tuning, making them potential candidates for memoryless fast drifting optical systems.

Although the literature is replete with time-invariant polarization-dependent loss models
[
15
,
16
]
, the time-varying nature of polarization-dependent loss is not well-studied. Inspired by the previous work
[
13
]
, we introduce a channel model called dual-polarization PDL (described in Section
II
) that accounts for memoryless time-varying polarization-dependent loss.

II
System Model

We consider dual-polarization transmission in the presence of polarization-dependent loss, state of polarization drift, and amplified spontaneous emission noise at the receiver. We also assume that nonlinearities are negligible and chromatic dispersion is compensated. We particularly study the memoryless dual-polarization channel where polarization-mode dispersion is not considered. To model the channel, we take the experimentally verified state of polarization drift model in
[
13
]
and combine it with concatenated polarization-dependent loss elements. This model can be regarded as a spherical analogy of a two-dimensional Wiener process.

The transmitted signal at time
k
is a
2
-dimensional random vector
\mathbf{\underline{s}}_{k}
, which takes on values from a set
\mathcal{S}=\{\underline{c}_{1},\underline{c}_{2},...,\underline{c}_{M}\}
of complex, zero-mean, equiprobable constellation points. The vector of received complex samples

\mathbf{\underline{x}}_{k}=\mathbf{H}_{k}\mathbf{\underline{s}}_{k}+\mathbf{\underline{z}}_{k},

(1)

where

k
is shown in Fig.
1
, where a link with
N
segments, each consisting of a state of polarization element
\mathbf{J}_{k,n}
and a polarization-dependent loss element
\mathbf{\Gamma}_{n}
, where

is the segment index. The dual-polarization PDL channel matrix can be described by a
2\times 2
complex-valued matrix as

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{k}=\mathbf{\Gamma}_{N}\mathbf{J}_{k,N}\cdots\mathbf{\Gamma}_{1}\mathbf{J}_{k,1}=\prod_{n=1}^{N}\mathbf{\Gamma}_{n}\mathbf{J}_{k,n},

(2)

where
\mathbf{\Gamma}_{n}
is a
2\times 2
positive real-valued diagonal matrix modeling the power imbalance induced by polarization-dependent loss. For the sake of simplicity, we assume that a time-invariant deterministic matrix can describe each polarization-dependent loss component as
[
15
]

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Gamma}_{n}=\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{1+\gamma_{n}}&0\\
0&\sqrt{1-\gamma_{n}}\\
\end{bmatrix}

(3)

where

is each segment’s polarization-dependent loss ratio indicating that in the extreme case, only one active polarization will remain (
\gamma_{n}=1
). Moreover,
\mathbf{J}_{k,n}
is a random
2\times 2
unitary matrix accounting for state of polarization drift defined in
[
13
]
as

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}_{k+1,n}=\exp(-j\bm{\underline{\alpha}}_{k,n}\cdot\overrightarrow{\sigma})\mathbf{J}_{k,n},

(4)

where
\exp{(\cdot)}
is the matrix exponential and

\displaystyle\bm{\underline{\alpha}}_{k,n}\sim\mathcal{N}(\underline{0},\sigma_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}\mathrm{I}_{3}),

(5)

where

and

is referred to as the

determining the speed of the state of polarization drift and

is the symbol duration. Finally,
\overrightarrow{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})
is a tensor of the Pauli spin matrices
[

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix},\quad\sigma_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix},\quad\sigma_{3}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-j\\
j&0\end{bmatrix}.

(6)

The total polarization linewidth scales with
N

\displaystyle\Delta\mathrm{p}_{\text{tot}}=N\cdot\Delta\mathrm{p}.

(7)

For the dual-polarization PDL channel, we define the segment-wise polarization-dependent loss in dB as

\displaystyle{\varphi}_{n}=10\log_{10}\left(\frac{1+\gamma_{n}}{1-\gamma_{n}}\right),

(8)

and the aggregated polarization-dependent loss ratio at time

\displaystyle\bm{\rho}_{k}=\frac{\left\lVert\bm{\lambda}_{k}^{\max}\right\rVert^{2}}{\left\lVert\bm{\lambda}_{k}^{\min}\right\rVert^{2}},

(9)

where
\bm{\lambda}_{k}^{\max}
and

are the singular values of
\mathbf{H}_{k}
. The average aggregated polarization-dependent loss over
K

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}=10\log_{10}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{H}}\left[\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\bm{\rho}_{k}\right]\right).

(10)

III
Proposed Algorithms

Since the received data suffers from a time-varying state of polarization drift, adaptive channel tracking is needed to recover the transmitted data. This section describes the proposed channel tracking algorithms. First, an adaptive unitary tracking algorithm is proposed, which performs best when polarization-dependent loss is negligible (e.g.,
\mathbf{\Gamma}_{n}=\mathrm{I}_{2}\quad\forall{n}
); after that, a data-aided estimation algorithm is proposed that estimates and tracks both state of polarization and polarization-dependent loss of the dual-polarization PDL channel.

III-A

Decision-directed Sliding Window Kabsch Algorithm

In this section, we introduce a modification to the decision-directed Kabsch algorithm
[
37
]
and make it suitable for polarization tracking in fast drifting channels. The key idea is to add a sliding window called
equalization window
with a size of
L
and update the channel estimate for each window.
We define

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}_{k}

\displaystyle=[\mathbf{\underline{x}}_{k},\mathbf{\underline{x}}_{k+1},\dots,\mathbf{\underline{x}}_{k+L-1}],

\displaystyle\mathbf{\hat{S}}_{k}

\displaystyle=[\mathbf{\underline{\hat{s}}}_{k},\mathbf{\underline{\hat{s}}}_{k+1},\dots,\mathbf{\underline{\hat{s}}}_{k+L-1}],

(11)

where
\mathbf{X}_{k}
is a
2\times L
matrix of received symbols and
\mathbf{\hat{S}}_{k}
is a
2\times L
matrix of estimated received symbols.
Ignoring the polarization-dependent loss, the channel matrix can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{k+1}=\mathbf{R}_{k}\mathbf{H}_{k},

(12)

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{k}=\mathbf{J}_{k,N}\mathbf{J}_{k,N-1}\cdots\mathbf{J}_{k,1},

(13)

is obtained from (
2
) by substituting
{\mathbf{\Gamma}_{n}=\mathrm{I}_{2}}
.

Although the channel
\mathbf{H}_{k}
changes for every
k
, a constant estimated channel
\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{k}
is used over an equalization window of length
L
to obtain the required decision-directed symbols for the tracking algorithm. This can be justified when the speed of the drift is not extremely high, and
L
is small. Then the transmitted symbols in an equalization window can be estimated by the previous estimate of the channel
\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{k}
and using the minimum Euclidean distance criterion

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{\underline{s}}}_{k}=\arg\min\limits_{\underline{c}\in\mathcal{S}}\left\lVert\mathbf{\hat{H}}^{-1}_{k}\mathbf{\underline{x}}_{k}-\underline{c}\right\rVert^{2}.

(14)

Then, we define the channel estimation problem as

\displaystyle\arg\min\limits_{\mathbf{\hat{R}}_{k}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}_{k}-\mathbf{\hat{R}}_{k}\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{k}\mathbf{\hat{S}}_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\quad\text{subject to}\quad{\mathbf{\hat{R}}_{k}}{\mathbf{\hat{R}}_{k}}^{\dagger}=\mathrm{I}_{2},

(15)

which is known as the orthogonal procrustes problem. The optimal solution is given in
[
41
,
42
,
43
]
as

\displaystyle\mathbf{\hat{R}}_{k}=\mathbf{U}_{k}\mathbf{V}_{k}^{\dagger},

(16)

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{k}\Sigma_{k}\mathbf{V}_{k}^{\dagger}=\text{svd}(\mathbf{X}_{k}\mathbf{\hat{S}}_{k}^{\dagger}\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{k}^{\dagger}),

(17)

where svd(
\cdot
) stands for singular value decomposition and
\Sigma_{k}
is a positive definite diagonal matrix (not used in the algorithm) containing the singular values. Note that
\mathbf{\hat{R}}_{k}
is not an estimate of
\mathbf{R}_{k}
. Instead it is an averaged estimation of all the rotations in one equalization window. Finally, initializing the estimated channel matrix as
\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{0}=\mathrm{I}_{2}
, the next estimated channel will be updated by

\displaystyle\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{k+\nu}=\mathbf{\hat{R}}_{k}\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{k},

(18)

where
\nu
is the sliding stride of the equalization window, meaning that the equalization window slides
\nu
symbols at a time. The complexity of the algorithm is roughly
L/\nu
times higher than the decision-directed Kabsch algorithm in
[
37
]
.

Pilot-Aided Algorithms

Sliding Window Least Square Algorithm (SW-LS)

The key idea is similar to sliding window Kabsch. Note that the standard least squares algorithm is designed to estimate deterministic channels; however, we combine LS with a decision-directed sliding window enabling us to track the dual-polarization PDL channel adaptively. Considering the polarization-dependent loss and state of polarization drift, we can write the next instance of the channel matrix as

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{k+1}=\mathbf{G}_{k}\mathbf{H}_{k},

(20)

where
\mathbf{G}_{k}
is
2\times 2
a complex matrix accounting for the overall rotations and aggregated polarization-dependent loss since time instance
k
. The channel estimation problem can be defined as

\displaystyle\arg\min\limits_{\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}_{k}-\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{k}\mathbf{\hat{S}}_{k}\right\rVert^{2},

(21)

where the decision-directed transmitted symbols can be estimated by (
14
). Hence,
\mathbf{\hat{S}}_{k}
and
\mathbf{X}_{k}
can be formed in the same way as (
III-A
). The optimal solution of (
21

\displaystyle\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}=\mathbf{X}_{k}\mathbf{\hat{S}}_{k}^{\dagger}\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{k}^{\dagger}\left(\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{k}\mathbf{\hat{S}}_{k}\mathbf{\hat{S}}_{k}^{\dagger}\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{k}^{{\dagger}}\right)^{-1}.

(22)

Initializing the estimated channel matrix as
\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{0}=\mathrm{I}_{2}
, the estimated channel
\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{k}
then is updated by

\displaystyle\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{k+\nu}=\mathbf{\hat{G}}_{k}\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{k}.

(23)

Since

is not hand-picked and comes from the received data symbols, the matrix inverse at the right-hand side of the (

A way to deal with this problem is to use the minimum mean square error criterion, which regularize the inverse matrix using he covariance matrix of the amplified spontaneous emission noise. However, we assume that the receiver has no knowledge of the amplified spontaneous emission noise power, and hence we do not consider the minimum mean square error estimator. Besides, we observed that for high order modulations (

, the chance of running to singularity is low.

The complexity of the proposed algorithm is approximately

times higher than least squares and can be controlled by proper selection of

III-B
2
Pilot-Aided Hybrid Algorithms

We consider a dual-polarization PDL channel with constant polarization-dependent loss components
\Gamma_{n}
. We assume that for a short enough transmission block length
K_{\mathrm{s}}
, the position of the maximum singular value of the channel matrix
\mathbf{H}_{k}
remains the same, and that the aggregated polarization-dependent loss ratio
\bm{\rho}_{k}
is almost constant.
Thus, one can argue that after compensating for the channel using the pilots, the residual channel matrix is almost unitary, which can be tracked using an arbitrary unitary channel tracking algorithm. Therefore, considering the frame structure in Fig.
3
, we propose two hybrid algorithms, which are described in the following.

Since the frame structure is periodic, the algorithms are described for the first frame, where the same procedure is repeated for the subsequent frames. The first
K_{\mathrm{p}}
transmitted symbols are pilots as defined in (
19
) and the corresponding
2\times K_{\mathrm{p}}
matrix of received symbols is
\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{p}}=[\mathbf{\underline{x}}_{0},\dots,\mathbf{\underline{x}}_{K_{\mathrm{p}}-1}]
. We define the set of payload indices as
\mathcal{P}=\{K_{\mathrm{p}}+1,\dots,K_{\mathrm{s}}\}
. In the first stage of the hybrid algorithms, a coarse estimation of the channel is obtained by applying the least squares estimator as

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{0}=\frac{1}{\delta}\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{S^{p}}.

(24)

The compensated payload symbols can be obtained according to

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}_{\text{c}}=\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{0}^{-1}\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{d}},

(25)

where
\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{d}}=[\mathbf{\underline{x}}_{K_{\mathrm{p}}},\dots,\mathbf{\underline{x}}_{K_{\mathrm{p}}+K_{\mathrm{s}}-1}]
is the matrix of payload symbols. The residual channel matrix at time
k\in\mathcal{P}
can be written as

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{r}}_{k}=\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{0}^{-1}\mathbf{H}_{k}.

(26)

For a short
K_{\mathrm{s}}
, the aggregated polarization-dependent loss ratio
\bm{\rho}_{k}
for
k\in\mathcal{P}
is assumed to be constant. Thus, assuming that the aggregated polarization-dependent loss is compensated by
\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{0}^{-1}
, the residual channel matrix
\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{r}}_{k}
can be regarded as an almost unitary matrix. Therefore, in the second stage, given
\mathbf{X}_{\text{c}}
, a unitary adaptive algorithm is used to track the residual channel matrix
\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{r}}_{k}
for
k\in\mathcal{P}
.

In this paper, using least squares for the first stage, we propose the least squares-sliding window Kabsch and least squares-decision-directed Czegledi hybrid algorithms where sliding window Kabsch and decision-directed Czegledi
[
38
]
are used in the second stage, respectively.

IV
Results

IV-A

Simulation Setup

Polarization-multiplexed
16
quadrature amplitude modulation at a symbol rate of
\smash{R_{s}=28}
Gbaud (i.e.,
T=1/Rs_{s}
) is considered. A random sequence of
\smash{K=10^{5}}
symbols is transmitted on each polarization where the initial matrices
\mathbf{J}_{0,1},\dots,\mathbf{J}_{0,N}
are drawn from the set of all
2\times 2
unitary matrices. Thereby the state of polarization gets a uniform distribution over the Poincaré sphere. The presented results are evaluated by averaging over
10^{5}
such sequences. For the dual-polarization PDL channel, it is assumed that the link has
\smash{N=20}
segments where all the segments have identical polarization linewidth
\Delta\mathrm{p}
and segment-wise polarization-dependent loss ratio
\gamma_{n}
. The launch power in each polarization is
P=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{\underline{s}}_{k}^{\dagger}\mathbf{\underline{s}}_{k}]
and the signal-to-noise ratio per polarization is defined as
\text{SNR}=P/\sigma^{2}_{z}
. The performance is assessed by estimating the symbol error rate for different setups in the presence of state of polarization drift, polarization-dependent loss, and amplified spontaneous emission noise.

IV-A
1
Blind / Decision-Directed Algorithms Setup

For comparison, the results obtained from the modified CMA
[
24
]
, decision-directed Kabsch
[
37
]
, decision-directed Czegledi
[
38
]
algorithms are presented as benchmarks. The decision-directed Kabsch algorithm operates in a decision-directed block-wise fashion where it uses a block size of
L_{\text{Kabsch}}=16
, which gives the best polarization drift tolerance for this algorithm. To ensure the convergence of the decision-directed Czegledi and modified CMA algorithms, they are implemented with two stages of convergence where the first stage uses a larger tracking step size
\mu
than the one is used in the second stage; for detailed implementation, refer to
[
38
, Algorithm 1]
. To compare the sliding window Kabsch algorithm with the best version of the benchmarks, the tracking step size
\mu
of the decision-directed Czegledi and modified CMA algorithms is optimized as a function of state of polarization drift speed and signal-to-noise ratio.

Moreover, coherent differential coding is used to resolve the four-fold phase ambiguity of the polarization-multiplexed
16
quadrature amplitude modulation constellation for blind and decision-directed algorithms. Deploying differential coding induces an signal-to-noise ratio penalty meaning that an extra signal-to-noise ratio is needed to attain a given symbol error rate compared to nondifferential schemes
[
46
]
. For comparison, differential coding is used even when the channel is perfectly known (i.e.,
\mathbf{\hat{H}}_{k}=\mathbf{H}_{k}
). Both decision-directed and blind algorithms may swap the equalized channels yielding polarization ambiguity. The polarization ambiguity may be resolved by inserting a few pilots in the data load. In most communication systems, it is common to use pilots for different purposes such as timing synchronization, carrier frequency estimation, etc. Therefore, in this paper, a genie-aided ambiguity resolution is used for both benchmarks and the proposed algorithm in our simulation, whereas we expect real deployed receivers to use pilots.

The sliding window Kabsch operates with a sliding window of size
\smash{L=24}
. The sliding stride is
\smash{\nu=6}
, which results in
4
times higher computational complexity than the decision-directed Kabsch algorithm. The parameters
L
and
\nu
are chosen such that sliding window Kabsch outperform the benchmarks with relatively low complexity. Note that optimizing
\nu
and
L
as a function of state of polarization drift might alter the performance of the algorithm for a fixed computational complexity.

Pilot-aided Algorithms Setup

The pilot length is chosen to be
\smash{K_{\mathrm{p}}=16}
, and the block length is assumed to be
\smash{K_{\mathrm{s}}=1016}

1.6\%

Note that optimizing
K_{\mathrm{p}}
and
K_{\mathrm{s}}
as a function of the polarization linewidth,
\bar{\rho}

K_{\mathrm{p}}
and
K_{\mathrm{s}}

The sliding window least squares algorithm is set to use an equalization window of
\smash{L=24}
with a
\nu=6
to get a fair performance while keeping the complexity low.

As for the benchmarks, a hybrid algorithm called least squares-decision-directed least mean squares is used, where the decision-directed least mean squares algorithm is initialized with a coarse estimate of the channel obtained by applying least squares on the pilot sequence. The maximum likelihood detection for a known channel at the receiver is also used to serve as a benchmark.

IV-B

Polarization Drift Tolerance

This part evaluates the channel tracking ability of the proposed and conventional algorithms for the dual-polarization PDL channel. The polarization drift sensitivity is measured by sweeping
\Delta\mathrm{p}
while the signal-to-noise ratio is fixed. The polarization movement between two consecutive symbols on the Poincaré sphere can be quantitatively characterized by the angle of the polarization movement
\theta_{\mathrm{SOP}}

[
7
, Eq. (2)]
. For a fixed symbol rate of
R_{s}=28
Gbaud (i.e.,
T=35.7~\mathrm{ps}
), we sweep
\Delta\mathrm{p}_{\text{tot}}\cdot T
from
10^{-8}
to
10^{-3}
which gives an average
\theta_{\mathrm{SOP}}
from
5\cdot 10^{-4}
to
1.57\cdot 10^{-1}
rad/symbol, respectively. Note that
\theta_{\mathrm{SOP}}
does not correspond to the angular velocity of the state of polarization movement in rad/s. The angular velocity is quantified as the time derivative of
\theta_{\mathrm{SOP}}
, which is undefined for our model (i.e., the time derivative of a Wiener process is undefined).

To investigate the step size sensitivity of the gradient descent-based algorithms, the symbol error rate of modified CMA and decision-directed Czegledi for fixed and optimal
\mu

\mu=10^{-3}

While the tracking capability of the gradient descent-based algorithms is highly dependent on the proper adjustment of the tracking step size, sliding window Kabsch requires no parameter tuning. This could be advantageous in bursty channels where the state of polarization drift speed does not remain constant during the whole transmission. Thus, an algorithm that is tailor-made for a specific state of polarization drift speed may fail to track sudden changes in the channel.

IV-D

Computational Complexity vs. Performance

In
[
38
, Table I]
, it has been shown that decision-directed Czegledi is almost twice as complex as decision-directed Kabsch for a dual-polarization channel. Therefore, taking decision-directed Kabsch as a reference, a rough complexity comparison of the proposed algorithm is presented.
Compared to decision-directed Kabsch, the sliding window Kabsch algorithm has an additional sliding window, resulting in approximately
L/\nu
times higher complexity. The sliding window least squares algorithm has a matrix inverse instead of the singular value decomposition in sliding window Kabsch, which essentially has the same complexity. Therefore, sliding window least squares is also at least
L/\nu
times more complex than decision-directed Kabsch. While the complexity of the proposed algorithms is tractable for low-dimensional channels (e.g., dual-polarization PDL channel, few-mode fiber, etc.), it becomes challenging for high-dimensional channels since the complexity of singular value decomposition and matrix inversion operations increases cubically with the number of dimensions.

The complexity of the algorithms can be decreased at the expense of performance. For instance, reducing the
L/\nu
ratio decreases the complexity of the proposed algorithms but also degrades the performance. Besides, adjusting the
L/\nu
ratio as a function of channel parameters might reduce the complexity of the algorithms for certain channel conditions (e.g., slowly drifting channels).

V
Conclusion

We have proposed sliding window Kabsch and sliding window least squares algorithms to track memoryless dual-polarization PDL channels. Both proposed algorithms use a sliding window in a decision-directed fashion, processing multiple symbols at a time. Numerical simulations are used to evaluate and compare the performance of the proposed algorithms with the conventional tracking algorithms, including modified CMA which is the most popular tracking algorithm in the literature. Results show that the proposed algorithms are more robust to polarization drift than the benchmarks. While parameter adjustment is required for gradient descent-based benchmarks (e.g., modified CMA, decision-directed least mean squares, decision-directed Czegledi, etc.), the proposed algorithms need no such adjustments and still outperform the benchmarks. Besides, unlike the decision-directed Czegledi algorithm
[
38
]
, which cannot be scaled for higher dimensional channels, the proposed algorithm can be applied to any number of dimensions at the expense of increasing the computational complexity.

The proposed algorithms are analyzed assuming negligible polarization-mode dispersion, which is not the case in more realistic fiber channels. Although it is not possible to compensate for polarization-mode dispersion and state of polarization separately, the proposed algorithms can be used in a hybrid fashion as in
[
47
]
, where polarization-mode dispersion is compensated in the frequency domain and state of polarization is tracked in the time domain.
The effect of polarization-mode dispersion is left for future work.

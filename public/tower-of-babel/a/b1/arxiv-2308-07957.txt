Supermoduli space with Ramond punctures is not projected
Ron Donagi, Nadia Ott

Abstract




Supergeometry is the study of spaces described by a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded sheaf of functions,
\mathcal{O}_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{X,+}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X,-}
whose sections obey the rule of signs,

ab=(-1)^{|a||b|}ba.

Every superspace
X
determines an ordinary space
M
which naturally embeds into
X
, and one can ask if this embedding
M\subset X
has a projection
X\to M
. If such a projection exists, then
X
is said to be
projected
. If, in addition, the projection
X\to M
makes
X
into a vector bundle over
M
, then
X
is said to be
split
. One important class of examples of superspaces are the moduli spaces of super Riemann surfaces, known as
supermoduli spaces
and denoted by
\mathfrak{M}_{g,n_{S},n_{R}}
. Here
g
is the genus,
n_{S}
is the number of Neveu-Schwarz punctures, and
n_{R}
the (always even) number of Ramond punctures. In physics, integrals over supermoduli space calculate important quantities like the partition function and scattering amplitude. So far, the most successful computations of these quantities have relied upon the splitness of the associated supermoduli space; notably, D’Hoker and Phong’s computation of the scattering amplitude in genus
g=2

[
3
]
.
However, most supermoduli spaces are not split or projected,
[
6
]
. Specifically, the cited work demonstrates that
\mathfrak{M}_{g,n_{S},0}
is not projected for all
g\geq 5
when
n_{S}=0
, and for all
g\geq n_{S}+1\geq 2
when
n_{S}\geq 1
. This paper’s main result is that most supermoduli spaces with Ramond punctures
\mathfrak{M}_{g,0,n_{r}}
are not projected.

.

Let
r>0

\mathfrak{M}_{g,0,2r}

Preliminaries: Split and projected supermanifolds

Let
X=(|X|,\mathcal{O}_{X})
be a supermanifold and let
J\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
denote the ideal sheaf generated by
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{-}
.
Any supermanifold
X
determines an ordinary manifold
X_{bos}:=(|X|,\mathcal{O}_{X}/J)
called its
bosonic reduction
.
Henceforth, we set
M:=X_{bos}
.

Given an ordinary manifold
M
and a locally free sheaf
\mathcal{X}
on
M
, we can always construct a supermanifold, denoted by
S(M,\mathcal{X})
, by applying the parity reversing-functor
\Pi
to
\mathcal{X}
and defining
S(M,\Pi\mathcal{X}):=\operatorname{Spec}_{M}\operatorname{Sym}^{\bullet}\Pi\mathcal{X}
.

Example 1
.

The supermanifold
S(M,J/J^{2})
determined by the pair
(M,J/J^{2})
is the normal bundle,
N_{M/X}
, to the embedding
M\subset X
. To see this, note that
J/J^{2}
is the conormal sheaf associated to the embedding
M\subset X
.

We say that
X
is
split
if there exists an isomorphism
\phi:X\cong N_{M/X}
of supermanifolds such that the restriction
\phi|_{M}
is equal to the identity on
M
. A weaker condition than being split is being projected: The supermanifold
X
is said to be
projected
if the embedding
M\subset X
has a projection
X\to M
.
In terms of coordinates and transition functions, a supermanifold is projected if its even coordinates modulo nilpotents transform like coordinates on
M
, and
it is split if, in addition, its odd coordinates transform like sections of the normal bundle
N_{M/X}
.

Example 2
.

All affine superspaces
\mathbb{A}^{m|n}
and all projective superspaces
\mathbb{P}^{m|n}
are split. Specifically,

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}^{m|n}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathbb{A}^{m}}\operatorname{Sym}^{\bullet}\Pi\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{m}}^{\oplus n}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{m|n}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathbb{P}^{m}}\operatorname{Sym}^{\bullet}\Pi\mathcal{O}(1)^{\oplus n}.

Example 3
.

Every supermanifold of dimension
(m|1)
is split.
More generally, the
first truncation

X^{1}:=(|X|,\mathcal{O}_{X}/J^{2})
of any supermanifold
X
is split.

In general, a projected supermanifold need not be split. However, there are some special cases where the two notions are equivalent:

Example 4
.

A supermanifold of dimension
(m|2)
is split if and only if it is projected. More generally, the second truncation
X^{2}:=(|X|,\mathcal{O}_{X}/J^{3})
of any supermanifold
X
is split if and only if projected.
This is (roughly) because the transformation laws for the odd coordinates on
X^{2}
remain unchanged from those on the split supermanifold
X^{1}
when “adding back” variables in
J^{2}/J^{3}
.

The truncations
X^{1}
and
X^{2}
are the first two terms in a finite sequence of truncations,
X^{i}:=(|X|,\mathcal{O}_{X}/J^{i+1})
which approximate
X
in the sense of the embeddings

M\subset X^{1}\subset\cdots X^{i}\subset\cdots\subset X.

A supermanifold
X
is projected or split if and only if all of its truncations
X^{i}
are projected or split.
Since
X^{1}
is always split, the first interesting case is
X^{2}
, the second truncation.
X^{2}
determines a class
\omega_{2}\in H^{1}(M,\mathcal{T}_{M}\otimes J^{2}/J^{3})
called the
first obstruction to splitting
. Indeed, the surjection
\mathcal{O}_{X}/J^{3}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}/J^{2}=\mathcal{O}_{M}\oplus J/J^{3}
making
X^{2}
a square-zero extension of
X^{1}
is an isomorphism on the odd components, and the conormal sequence associated to
(\mathcal{O}_{X}/J^{3})^{+}\to\mathcal{O}_{M}
,

0\to J^{2}/J^{3}\to\Omega_{X^{2}}^{1}|_{M}\to\Omega_{M}^{1}\to 0,

identifies
X^{2}
with a class
\omega_{2}
in
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\Omega_{M}^{1},J^{2}/J^{3})\cong H^{1}(M,\mathcal{T}_{M}\otimes J^{2}/J^{3})
such that
\omega_{2}=0
if and only if
0\to J^{2}/J^{3}\to(\mathcal{O}_{X}/J^{3})^{+}\to\mathcal{O}_{M}\to 0
is split exact. In this case,
X^{2}
is projected, and thus also split by example
4
.

It is not difficult to find examples of supermanifolds which are
not
projected.

Example 5
.

The superconic defined by the equation
z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}+z_{3}^{2}+\theta_{1}\theta_{1}
in
\operatorname{Proj}\mathbb{C}[z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}|\theta_{1},\theta_{2}]
is not projected.

Example 6
.

Every non-trivial
odd
first order deformation of a super Riemann surface supermanifold is not projected, see
[
6
]
.

The following two Corollaries from
[
6
]
can be used to construct a large class of non-projected supermanifolds.

Corollary 3

(Corollary 2.8)
.

Let
\pi:\tilde{X}\to X
be a finite cover of supermanifolds. If
\omega_{2}(X)\neq 0
, then
\omega_{2}(\tilde{X})\neq 0
. Furthermore,
X
is split if and only if
\tilde{X}
is split.

Corollary 4

(Corollary 2.11)
.

Let
X
be a supermanifold and suppose
X^{\prime}\subset X
is a sub-supermanifold with bosonic reduction
M^{\prime}\subset M
such that the normal sequence,

0\to TM\to TM|_{M^{\prime}}\to N_{M^{\prime}/M}\to 0

is split exact. If
X^{\prime}
is not projected, then
X
is not projected.

Example.
Let us apply Corollary
4
to the superconic
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{2|2}
from example
5
. Since
X
is not projected, the normal sequence associated to the embedding
X_{bos}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
is not split exact by Corollary
4
.

Preliminaries: Supercurves, divisors on supercurves, super Riemann surfaces and their punctures

2.1
Supercurves

A
supercurve

S
is a compact, connected supermanifold of dimension
(1|1)
. The bosonic reduction of a supercurve is an ordinary projective curve.

Example 7
.

The superprojective line
\mathbb{P}^{1|1}
is a supercurve whose bosonic reduction is the ordinary projective line,
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

A
family of supercurves
over a supermanifold
T
is a smooth, proper morphism of supermanifolds
\pi:S\to T
of relative dimension
(1|1)
. The bosonic reduction of
\pi:S\to T
is the family of ordinary curves,
\pi_{bos}:S_{bos}\to T_{bos}
.

Example 8
.

An example of a family of supercurves is
\mathbb{P}^{1|1}\times T\to T
, where
T
can be any supermanifold. Its bosonic reduction is
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times T_{bos}\to T_{bos}
.

In general, we write
S
for a family of supercurves and make no explicit mention of the base supermanifold. We will sometimes use the phrase “in the split case” to indicate that the base of a family is an ordinary scheme. If the base is
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
, then we will refer to
S
as a “single” supercurve.

Any topological invariant assigned to a supermanifold is determined by the bosonic reduction of that supermanifold. In particular, the genus of a supercurve is the genus of its bosonic reduction.

2.2
Divisors on supercurves

Our treatment of divisors on supermanifolds follows the style of Chapter 6 in
[
5
]
.

Let
X
be a supermanifold and let
\mathcal{K}
denote the sheaf of total quotient superrings (see
[
5
]
for the ordinary definition).
A
Cartier divisor
on
X
is a global section of
\mathcal{K}^{*}/\mathcal{O}_{X}^{*}
, where
\mathcal{K}^{*}\subset\mathcal{K}
is the subsheaf of invertible elements in
\mathcal{K}
. As in the ordinary case, a Cartier divisor on
X
can be described by a collection
f_{i}\in\Gamma(U_{i},\mathcal{K}^{*})
on an open cover
U_{i}
of
X
such that for all
i,j
,
f_{i}/f_{j}\in\Gamma(U_{i}\cap U_{j},\mathcal{O}_{X}^{*})
.

Effective and prime divisors
A Cartier divisor is said to be
effective
if
f_{i}\in\Gamma(U_{i},\mathcal{O}_{U_{i},+})
for all
i
. Every effective Cartier divisor on
X
has an associated closed sub-supermanifold
\mathcal{P}\subset X
of codimension
1|0
defined by the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{P}}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
locally generated by the
f_{i}
. We say that
\mathcal{P}\subset X
is a
prime divisor
if its bosonic reduction
P=\mathcal{P}_{bos}
is a prime divisor on
M=X_{bos}
.

Example 9
.

Let
(z,\theta)
be a choice of local coordinates on a supercurve
S
over a base with one odd coordinate
\eta
. The divisor defined by the equation
z-z_{0}-\theta\eta=0
is a prime divisor on
S
.

Lemma 5
.

On any supermanifold
X
,
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{*}=\mathcal{O}_{X,+}^{*}
and
\mathcal{K}^{*}=\mathcal{K}_{+}^{*}
. In particular, a one-to-one correspondence exists between Cartier divisors on
X
and Cartier divisors on
X_{ev}
.

Proof.

A section
f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}

is invertible if and only if its image
\overline{f}
in
\mathcal{O}_{X}/J
is invertible. Therefore, if
f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}^{*}
, then
\overline{f}\neq 0
and, by homogeneity,
f
is even since
\overline{f}\neq 0
is even.
∎

Corollary 6
.

Let
S
be a family of supercurves over an ordinary scheme. Then there is a one-to-one correspondence between Cartier divisors on
S
and Cartier divisors on the bosonic reduction
C
of
S
. In particular, every Cartier divisor on
C
extends uniquely to a Cartier divisor on
S
.

Proof.

Over an ordinary scheme,
S_{bos}=S_{ev}
since
\mathcal{O}_{S,+}=\mathcal{O}_{S}/J
. Now apply Lemma
5
.

2.2.1
Points and divisors

A
marked point
on a family of supercurves
\pi:S\to T
is the image of a section
s:T\to S
. When
T=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
, marked points are the same as closed points.

Example 10
.

Consider the (trivial) family
\pi:\mathbb{A}^{1|1}\times\mathbb{A}^{0|1}\to\mathbb{A}^{0|1}
, and choose coordinates

\mathbb{A}^{1|1}\cong\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}[z,\theta],\ \ \mathbb{A}^{0|1}\cong\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}[\eta]

where the isomorphism on the right is for both copies of
\mathbb{A}^{0|1}
.
A marked point
s:\mathbb{A}^{0|1}\to\mathbb{A}^{1|1}\times\mathbb{A}^{0|1}
is a morphism of superrings

\mathbb{C}[\eta][z,\theta]\to\mathbb{C}[\eta]:\eta\mapsto\eta,z\mapsto a,\theta\mapsto b\eta

for some
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
.

Lemma 7
.

Let
S
be a family of supercurves over a purely bosonic scheme. Then there is a one-to-one correspondence between prime divisors on
S
and marked points on
S
.

Proof.

Suppose
T
is purely bosonic. Then any marked point
s:T\to S
on
S
must factor through
C=S_{bos}
, and so we have a one-to-one correspondence between marked points on
C
and marked points on
S
. On the other hand, marked points on
C
are the same as prime divisors on
C
, and every prime divisor on
C
extends uniquely to
S
by Corollary
6
.
∎

However, there is
no
duality between prime divisors and marked points on a supercurve over a general superscheme. In the next section we will show that the duality is restored, regardless of the base superscheme, in the presence of a superconformal structure.

2.3
Super Riemann surfaces

A super Riemann surface
X=(S,\mathcal{D})
is a supercurve
S
equipped with a rank
(0|1)
distribution
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{T}_{S}
which is maximally non-integrable in the sense of the following short exact sequence,

0\to\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{T}_{S/T}\to\mathcal{D}^{\otimes 2}\to 0.

(4)

An isomorphism of super Riemann surfaces
\phi:X\to X^{\prime}
is an isomorphism
\phi:S\to S^{\prime}
of supercurves such that
\phi^{*}\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\mathcal{D}
.

The distribution
\mathcal{D}
is called a superconformal structure, and there always exists a special choice of local coordinates
(z,\theta)
on
S
such that

\mathcal{D}
is locally generated by the odd vector field,

D=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial z}.

(5)

Duality.
In the last section we define a duality between points and divisors on a supercurve over a purely bosonic scheme, and noted that this duality does not hold over general superschemes. It turns out that on super Riemann surfaces the defined duality holds
in general
, i.e., with no condition on the base superscheme. This is because the maximal non-integrability condition on
\mathcal{D}
ensures that there exists a unique point on each divisor tangent to
\mathcal{D}
.

More precisely, let
p=(z_{0},\theta_{0})
be a point on
S
, and let
v_{p}:=D|_{p}
be the restriction of the local generator (
5
) of
\mathcal{D}
to the point
p
. The pair
(p,v_{p})
determines a prime divisor
P
on
S
which is locally defined by the equation
z-z_{0}-\theta\theta_{0}=0
. By construction,
\mathcal{D}
is tangent to
P
at
p\in P
, and the maximal non-integrability condition on
\mathcal{D}
ensures that
p
is the unique point on
P
tangent to
\mathcal{D}
.

Superconformal vector fields.
Let
X=(S,\mathcal{D})
be a super Riemann surface, and let
I
be an
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-module.
A superderivation
\delta:\mathcal{O}_{S}\to I
is a
k
-linear map, satisfying the super Leibniz rule

\delta(ab)=\delta(a)b+(-1)^{|\delta||a|}\ a\delta(b),\ a,b\in I

(6)

We can organize the set of derivations into a sheaf of super vector spaces on
S
,

\underline{\operatorname{Der}}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{S},I)=\operatorname{Der}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{S},I)\oplus\operatorname{Der}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\Pi I).

where
\operatorname{Der}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{S},I)
is the space of all grading-preserving derivations, and
\operatorname{Der}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\Pi I)
is the space of grading-reversing derivations. The sheaf
\underline{\operatorname{Der}}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\mathcal{O}_{S})
is the tangent sheaf
\mathcal{T}_{S}
on
S
, and
\mathcal{T}_{S}\otimes I=\underline{\operatorname{Der}}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{S},I)
where

(\mathcal{T}_{S}\otimes I)^{+}=\operatorname{Der}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{S},I),\ \text{and}\ (\mathcal{T}_{S}\otimes I)^{-}=\operatorname{Der}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\Pi I).

On the super Riemann surface
X
, we can consider the subsheaf
\underline{\operatorname{Der}}_{k}^{scf}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X})\subset\underline{\operatorname{Der}}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X})=\mathcal{T}_{X}
of derivations preserving the superconformal structure
\mathcal{D}
. A derivation
V\in\mathcal{T}_{X}
is in
\underline{\operatorname{Der}}_{k}^{scf}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X})
if and only if

[V,\mathcal{D}]\in\mathcal{D}
. Henceforth we set
\mathcal{A}_{X}:=\underline{\operatorname{Der}}_{k}^{scf}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X})
. The maximal non-integrability condition on
\mathcal{D}
implies that
V\in\mathcal{T}_{X}/\mathcal{D}
, and

\mathcal{A}_{X}\cong\mathcal{T}_{X}/\mathcal{D}\cong\mathcal{D}^{2}.

(7)

If
I
is an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module, then
\underline{\operatorname{Der}}^{scf}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{X},I)\cong\mathcal{A}_{X}\otimes I
, where

(\mathcal{A}_{X}\otimes I)^{+}=\operatorname{Der}(\mathcal{O}_{X},I),\ \text{and}\ (\mathcal{A}_{X}\otimes I)^{-}=\operatorname{Der}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\Pi I).

Here we are using the fact that
\mathcal{A}_{X}
inherits the structure of a sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules from
\mathcal{D}^{2}
, and so the tensor product
\mathcal{A}_{X}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}-
is well-defined.

Lemma 8
.

I\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus\Pi\mathcal{O}_{X}
, then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Der}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{X},I)

\displaystyle\cong\underline{\operatorname{Der}}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X})\cong\mathcal{T}_{X}

\displaystyle\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{scf}(\mathcal{O}_{X},I)

\displaystyle\cong\underline{\operatorname{Der}}_{k}^{scf}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X})\cong\mathcal{A}_{X}

Proof.

\displaystyle\operatorname{Der}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{X},I)

\displaystyle\cong(\mathcal{T}_{X}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus\Pi\mathcal{O}_{X}))_{+}=(\mathcal{T}_{X}\oplus\Pi\mathcal{T}_{X})_{+}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{X}^{+}\oplus(\Pi T_{X})^{+}=T_{X}^{+}\oplus\mathcal{T}_{X}^{-}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{X}

∎

A first order deformation of a super Riemann surface
X
is a family of super Riemann surfaces
f:X^{\prime}\to\operatorname{Spec}D
over the Spec of the super dual numbers
D=\mathbb{C}[t,\eta]/(t^{2},\eta t),|t|=0,|\eta|=1

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}

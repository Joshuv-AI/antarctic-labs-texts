Abstract:

In this paper we establish
commmutator estimates for the Dirichlet-to-Neumann Map associated to a divergence form elliptic operator
in the upper half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}:=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)\}
,
with uniformly complex elliptic,
L^{\infty}
,
t
-independent coefficients. By a standard pull-back mechanism,
these results extend corresponding results of Kenig, Lin and Shen for the Laplacian in a Lipschitz domain,
which have application to the theory of homogenization.

Keywords:

Commutator; Dirichlet-to-Neumann map; Divergence form elliptic operator;
Dahlberg’s bilinear estimate; Layer potentials.

1
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{L}:=-\mbox{div}(A\nabla)
, defined in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}(n\geq 1)
, where
A=A(x)
is a
n+1\times n+1
matrix with complex-valued, bounded and
t
-independent coefficients satisfying the uniform (complex)-ellipticity condition

\gamma|\xi|^{2}\leq\mbox{Re}\left\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\right\rangle=\mbox{Re}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n+1}A_{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\overline{\xi_{j}}\right),\quad\quad\left\|A\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\gamma^{-1},

[formula]

for some
\gamma\in(0,1],
and all
\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1},\;x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}.
Moreover, throughout our paper, we shall further assume that there exists
A_{0}
, a uniformly elliptic,
t
-independent matrix as above, which in addition is real and symmetric, such that

\|A-A_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\epsilon,

[formula]

where
\epsilon
depends only on
n,\gamma
.

If we assume
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, then the Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathcal{L}u=0\quad\;\mbox{in}\;{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}&\\
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}u(\cdot,t)=f,\quad\;&\end{aligned}\right.

[formula]

has a unique solution
u\in\dot{W}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1})
, the space of functions modulo constants with seminorm given by the norm of
\nabla u

in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1})
,
and the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map,
defined by

f\to\Lambda(f):=\frac{\partial{u}}{\partial{\nu_{A}}}=\partial_{\nu_{A}}u:=-e_{n+1}\cdot A(\nabla u)\biggl|_{t=0}\,,

extends to
a mapping from
\dot{H}^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cong I_{1/2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
to

\dot{H}^{-1/2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, where
\dot{H}^{-1/2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
denotes the dual space of the
fractional Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{1/2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
(see
[
HKMP
]
); here
I_{1/2}
denotes the usual 1/2
order homogeneous fractional integral operator (i.e., Riesz potential).
We also define the homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{L}^{2}_{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to be the completion of
C^{\infty}_{0}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
with respect to the seminorm
\|\nabla f\|_{2}
. For convenience, we set
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\dot{L}^{2}_{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, and we define the
inhomogeneous version by

H^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})=L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\cap\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
For
\epsilon>0
small enough, depending only on
n
and
\gamma
,
we obtain that

\Lambda:\dot{H}^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\rightarrow L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}).

[formula]

In fact, (1.4) is an immediate consequence of the solution of the Regularity problem given in

[
AAAHK
, Theorem 1.14]
.

We let
C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the space of Lipschitz functions, with norm

\|g\|_{C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,x\neq y}\frac{|g(x)-g(y)|}{|x-y|}\,.

Now we can state our main results as follows. The first generalizes the classical commutator theorem of
A. P. Calderón
[
Ca
]
.

Theorem 1.1

Suppose that
A
satisfies (1.1) and (1.2) with
\epsilon
sufficiently small,
depending on dimension and ellipticity.
Then, for any
f\in L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
g\in C^{0,1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}),

\|[\Lambda,g](f)\|_{L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\|g\|_{C^{0,1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})},

[formula]

where the constant
C
depends only on
n
and
\gamma
.

Theorem 1.2

Suppose that
A
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem 1.1. Then, for any

f\in L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
g\in\dot{H}^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}),

\|[\Lambda,g](f)\|_{L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\|g\|_{\dot{H}^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})},

[formula]

where the constant
C
depends only on
n
and
\gamma
.

Analogous results were previously obtained in
[
KLS
]
, for the Laplacian
in a Lipschitz domain, as part of the authors’ study of homogenization.
Our results, with
A
real and symmetric, include this case, by a well-known pullback
mechanism. A different
generalization of the results in
[
KLS
]
has been obtained in
[
Sh
]
, for elliptic systems in
Lipschitz domains, with Hölder continuous coefficients. Neither our work nor that of
[
Sh
]
subsumes the
other. The approach to these commutator results in both papers
[
KLS
]
and
[
Sh
]
, is based on
a bilinear estimate of Dahlberg
[
D
]
, and it’s extension in
[
Sh
]
to certain variable coefficient elliptic systems.
In
[
H
]
, the first author of this paper established Dahlberg’s bilinear estimate
for the class of second-order elliptic operators enjoying the same assumptions
that we impose here, i.e., the matrix
A
satisfies (1.1) and (1.2) with
\epsilon
small enough, in
the upper half-space. The latter result, along with layer potential technology for
the operators under consideration, will allow us to follow the strategy
in
[
KLS
]
and
[
Sh
]
, to obtain the stated theorems.

Remark 1.
In our Theorem 1.2, as compared to its analogue

[
Sh
, Theorem 1.2]
, we obtain an estimate in terms of
the
L^{\infty}
norm of the boundary data, as opposed to that of the solution

[

The paper is organized as follows. In the next section we discuss certain preliminaries. In Section 3, we prove
Theorem 1.1. In Section 4, we establish an Agmon-Miranda maximum principle for the class of operators under
consideration, which we then use in Section 5 to give the proof of Theorem 1.2.

|u(x,t)-u_{k}(x,t)|\,\lesssim\,\left(\fint_{t/2}^{2t}\fint_{|x-y|<t}|u(y,s)-u_{k}(y,s)|^{2}\,dyds\right)^{1/2}\\[4.0pt]
\lesssim\,\left(t^{-n}\fint_{t/2}^{2t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(y,s)-u_{k}(y,s)|^{2}\,dyds\right)^{1/2}\,\to\,0\,,\quad\mbox{as}\;k\rightarrow\infty.

Similarly, for any fixed cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\iint_{R_{2Q}}|u-u_{k}|^{2}\leq\int_{0}^{\ell(Q)}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(y,s)-u_{k}(y,s)|^{2}\,dyds\,\to\,0\,,\quad\mbox{as}\;k\rightarrow\infty.

We conclude that (4.2) holds for
u
.

\Box

Lemma 4.4.

Let

be as in Corollary 4.3.
Let

, and

, with

Suppose that

Let

with boundary data

.
Then there exists a constant

[formula]

uniformly in
R^{\prime}
, for

\alpha>0
is the exponent in Corollaries 4.2 and 4.3.

Proof.
Set

\Delta_{k}=\Delta_{k}(x):=\Delta(x,2^{k}):=\{y\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:|x-y|<2^{k}\},\;k=0,1,2,...,

and

We are now able to prove the following.

Proposition 4.5.

Let
A
,
A_{0}
and
\mathcal{L}
be as in Corollary 4.3.
Let
f\in L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
. Then there is a solution
u
of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}
such that
u(\cdot,0)=f
in the sense of non-tangential convergence, satisfying the Agmon-Miranda maximum principle

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})},

where
C=C(n,\gamma)
.

Proof.
Given a point
x_{0}\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
, we define the dyadic surface balls centered at
x_{0}
on

{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\cong{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\times\{0\}=\partial{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}
by

\Delta_{k}=\Delta_{k}(x_{0}):=\Delta(x_{0},2^{k}):=\{y\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:|x_{0}-y|<2^{k}\},\;k\in{\mathbb{Z}}

and set

S_{k}=\Delta_{k+1}\setminus\Delta_{k},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

so that
\cup_{k}S_{k}={{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\setminus\{0\}
.

We let
f_{k}:=f1_{S_{k}}
and
u_{k}
be the solution of
(D)^{\mathcal{L}}_{2}
with boundary data
f_{k}
.
Define

f^{N}:=\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{N}f_{k},\quad u^{N}:=\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{N}u_{k}.

Clearly,
u^{N}
is the unique solution
of
(D)^{\mathcal{L}}_{2}
with boundary data
f^{N}
.
To prove the proposition, we will show that

u:=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}u^{N}

exists at
each point of
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}
and satisfies the conclusion of the theorem. Moreover,
u
is well-defined,
in the sense that if
u^{\prime}
is
constructed in the same way as
u
, but for a different center
x^{\prime}_{0}

u=u^{\prime}.

we fix a point
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}
and suppose that

2^{M}\geq 2^{N}\gg t+|x-x_{0}|

f^{N}

{\rm supp}(f^{M}-f^{N})\subset S_{R,R^{\prime}}(x)\,,

where
R\approx 2^{N}
and
R^{\prime}\approx 2^{M}
.
By Lemma 4.4,

|u^{N}(x,t)-u^{M}(x,t)|\leq C\left(\frac{t}{2^{N}}\right)^{\alpha}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\rightarrow 0,\quad\mbox{as}\;N,M\rightarrow 0.

[formula]

Thus,
u^{N}
converges pointwise, and in fact, uniformly on compacta, in
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}
, hence also in
L^{2}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1})
.
By Caccioppoli’s inequality applied to

u^{N}-u^{M}
, we further
see that
u^{N}
converges in
W^{1,2}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1})
,
whence the limit
u
also solves
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}.

Let us now show that
u
satisfies the required properties.

Definition of
u
is independent of center
x_{0}
.
Fix two distinct points
x_{1},x_{2}\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and construct the corresponding
f^{N,i},u^{N,i},i=1,2
, and
u^{i}=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}u^{N,i},i=1,2
as above, with

x_{1},x_{2}
in place of
x_{0}
. Let
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}
and consider
M,N
such that

2^{M}\geq 2^{N}\gg t+|x-x_{1}|+x-x_{2}|\,.

Then

{\rm supp}(f^{M,1}-f^{N,2})\subset S_{R,R^{\prime}},

where

R\approx 2^{N}
and
R^{\prime}\approx 2^{M}
. Again we invoke Lemma 4.4 to get

|u^{N,2}(x,t)-u^{M,1}(x,t)|\leq C\left(\frac{t}{2^{N}}\right)^{\alpha}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\rightarrow 0,\quad\mbox{as}\;N,M\rightarrow 0.

[formula]

Therefore,
u^{N,2}
and
u^{M,1}
converge to the
same limit
u
.

Non-tangential convergence to
f
.
Fix
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and build
f^{N}
and
u^{N}
as above,
relative to the center
x_{0}
.
By
(D)^{\mathcal{L}}_{2}
, each
u^{N}
converges
non-tangentially to
f^{N}
, for a.e.
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
. Thus, there is a set
Z=\cup_{N}Z_{N}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
, of
measure zero, such that

u^{N}
converges non-tangentially to
f^{N}
for every
N
, and
at every point
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\setminus Z
.
Fix such an
x\neq x_{0}
and let
\epsilon>0
. Consider the truncated cone at
x
of
height
\epsilon:

\Gamma^{\epsilon}(x):=\{(y,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}:|x-y|<t<\epsilon\}.

Observe that (4.4)
continues to hold with
y
in place of
x
, for
(y,t)\in\Gamma^{\epsilon}(x)
, with
\epsilon
small, and
N,M
large. We therefore have for such

|u(y,t)-u^{N}(y,t)|=\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}|u^{M}(y,t)-u^{N}(y,t)|\lesssim\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2^{N}}\right)^{\alpha}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}.

[formula]

On the other hand, if we fix
N
so large that

and use that

converges non-tangentially to

at
x
, then for

|u^{N}(y,t)-f(x)|=o(1),\quad\mbox{as}\;\epsilon\rightarrow 0.

Letting

, we see that
u(y,t)\rightarrow f(x)
non-tangentially.

Agmon-Miranda maximum principle.
Let
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}
.
We seek
to show that

with
C=C(n,\gamma)
. Since the definition of

is
independent of the choice of

We then define

\Delta_{k}=\Delta_{k}(x),\,S_{k}=S_{k}(x),\,f_{k}=f1_{S_{k}}
and

as above. Choose

such that

u=\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k(t)+10}u_{k}+\displaystyle\sum_{k=k(t)+11}^{\infty}u_{k}=:U_{1}+U_{2}.

By Moser local boundedness and

|U_{1}(x,t)|\lesssim\left(\fint_{t/2}^{2t}\fint_{|x-y|<t}|U_{1}(y,s)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\left(t^{-n}\int_{|x-y|<C2^{k(t)}}|f|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}},

Proposition 4.6.

Let
A
,
A_{0}
and
\mathcal{L}
be as in Proposition 4.5 (i.e., as in

Let

We then have the Carleson measure estimate

\|\mu\|_{\mathit{c}}\,:=\,\sup_{\Delta}\frac{1}{|\Delta|}\iint_{R_{\Delta}}|t\nabla u(x,t)|^{2}\,\frac{dxdt}{t}\,\leq\,C\|f\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,,

Proof.
Given our preceding work in this section,
the argument is standard, but we include it here for the sake of completeness. Fix a
surface ball
\Delta_{0}:=\Delta(x_{0},r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, set
\Delta_{k}:=\Delta(x_{0},2^{k}r)
,
and
S_{k}:=\Delta_{k+1}\setminus\Delta_{k}
. Now define
f_{k}=f1_{S_{k}}
, let
u_{k}
solve

(D)^{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}
(equivalently,
(D)^{\mathcal{L}}_{2}
, see Remark 4)
with boundary data
f_{k}
, and as in the proof of Proposition 4.5, set

f^{N}:=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{N}f_{k},\quad u^{N}:=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{N}u_{k},

[formula]

so that

is the solution
of
(D)^{\mathcal{L}}_{2}
(and of
(D)^{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}

.
As noted above,

in

\int_{\delta}^{r}\!\!\int_{\Delta_{0}}|t\nabla u^{N}(x,t)|^{2}\,\frac{dxdt}{t}\,\to\,\int_{\delta}^{r}\!\!\int_{\Delta_{0}}|t\nabla u(x,t)|^{2}\,\frac{dxdt}{t}\,,\quad\text{as }N\to\infty.

Thus, it is enough to show that

r^{-n}\int_{0}^{r}\!\!\int_{\Delta_{0}}|t\nabla u^{N}(x,t)|^{2}\,\frac{dxdt}{t}\,\lesssim\,\|f\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,,

[formula]

u^{N}\,=\,\sum_{k=-\infty}^{0}u_{k}\,+\,\sum_{k=1}^{N}u_{k}\,=\,u^{0}\,+\,\sum_{k=1}^{N}u_{k}\,,

\left(\int_{0}^{r}\!\!\int_{\Delta_{0}}|t\nabla u^{N}(x,t)|^{2}\,\frac{dxdt}{t}\right)^{1/2}\\[4.0pt]

5
Proof of Theorem 1.2

In this section, we focus on the proof of Theorem 1.2, which, together with the
results in the previous section, comprise
the main new contributions of this paper.
The proof will be split into two parts. In Part 1,
we present a suitable definition
of the commutator
[\Lambda,g](f)
, under the assumptions that
f\in L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and

g\in\dot{H}^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
. In Part 2, we prove a variant of
Dahlberg’s bilinear estimate by a more refined version of the procedure used to prove
Lemma 3.1. The conclusion of the theorem then follows.
As in the preceding section, we let

\Delta(x,r):=\{y\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:|x-y|<r\}
denote
the surface ball of radius
r
and center
x
, on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Part 1: definition of
\|[\Lambda,g](f)\|_{L^{2}}
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem 1.2, we have from Theorem 2.2 that both
(D)_{2}
and
(R)_{2}
are solvable for
\mathcal{L}
, and its adjoint
\mathcal{L}^{*}
.

We let
\Lambda^{*}
to denote the adjoint of

\Lambda
. Observe that
\Lambda^{*}
is the the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map for the adjoint operator
\mathcal{L}^{*}
,
as may be seen by the Gauss-Green formula.

For
f\in L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, let
u
be the solution of
(D)^{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}
with boundary data
f
,
as constructed in section 4. We may assume that
g\in C_{0}^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, which is dense in
\dot{H}^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
.

For
0<\delta\ll 1
, and
1\ll R<\infty
, set
f_{\delta}:=u(\cdot,\delta)
, and choose
\eta_{R}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B(0,2R))
with
\eta_{R}\equiv 1
on
B(0,R)
. Let
u_{\delta,R}
be the solution of
(D)^{\mathcal{L}}_{2}

(equivalently, the solution of
(D)^{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}
; see Remark 4)
with boundary data
f_{\delta}\eta_{R}
. By the Agmon-Miranda maximum principle proved
in section 4,
f_{\delta}:=u(\cdot,\delta)
satisfies that

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}f_{\delta}(x)=f(x),\;\mbox{a.e.}\;x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\,,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sup_{\delta>0}\|f_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}.

[formula]

For any
h\in C_{0}^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, we shall prove the following estimate

|I_{\delta,R}|:=\biggl|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left[\Lambda(f_{\delta}\eta_{R}g)\overline{h}-g\Lambda(f_{\delta}\eta_{R})\overline{h}\right]\biggl|\lesssim\|f_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|h\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla_{\|}g\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|h\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla_{\|}g\|_{L^{2}},

[formula]

uniformly in
\delta
and
R
; in fact, the implicit constants depend only on
n,\gamma
, provided
\epsilon
is small enough, with the same dependence. Observe that we have used (5.1) in the last step.

Taking (5.2) for granted momentarily,
we seek to extend estimate (5.2) to the limiting case as

\delta\rightarrow 0
and
R\rightarrow\infty
.
To this end, we define

\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left[\Lambda(fg)\overline{h}-g\Lambda(f)\overline{h}\right]:=\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left[\Lambda(f_{\delta}\eta_{R}g)\overline{h}-g\Lambda(f_{\delta}\eta_{R})\overline{h}\right]=:\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}I_{\delta,R}.

[formula]

Let us show that this definition is reasonable, and in particular that the limit exists. We observe that at least formally,

\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left[\Lambda(fg)\overline{h}-g\Lambda(f)\overline{h}\right]=\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left[fg\overline{\Lambda^{*}(h)}-f\overline{\Lambda^{*}(\overline{g}h)}\right],

[formula]

so our goal is to show that
the limit in (5.3) exists, and is equal to the right hand of (5.4).

By
[
AAAHK
, Theorem 1.14]
(solvability of
(R)_{2}^{\mathcal{L}}
),
the analogue of (5.4) does hold for any
\delta>0
and
R<\infty
, i.e., we can write
I_{\delta,R}
as

I_{\delta,R}=\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left[f_{\delta}\eta_{R}\left(g\overline{\Lambda^{*}(h)}-\overline{\Lambda^{*}(\overline{g}h)}\right)\right].

[formula]

By the solvability of
(R)^{\mathcal{L}}_{2}
, we know that

\Lambda^{*}(h),\Lambda^{*}(\overline{g}h)\in L^{2}
(recall that we have taken
g,h\in C^{\infty}_{0}
by density).
Consequently, by (5.1) we may use dominated convergence to obtain

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}I_{\delta,R}=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left[f_{\delta}\eta_{R}\left(g\overline{\Lambda^{*}(h)}-\overline{\Lambda^{*}(\overline{g}h)}\right)\right]=\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left[f\eta_{R}\left(g\overline{\Lambda^{*}(h)}-\overline{\Lambda^{*}(\overline{g}h)}\right)\right]:=I_{R}.

Since (as we shall prove) (5.2) holds uniformly in

\delta>0
, we also have that

|I_{R}|\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|h\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla_{\|}g\|_{L^{2}}.

[formula]

Set

\Psi=\Psi(g,h):=\left(g\overline{\Lambda^{*}(h)}-\overline{\Lambda^{*}(\overline{g}h)}\right).

Since (5.6) holds for any
f\in L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, we have

\sup_{R<\infty}\int_{|x|<R}|\Psi(x)|\,dx\,\lesssim\,\|h\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla_{\|}g\|_{L^{2}},

by the definition of
\eta_{R}
, and thus using monotone convergence theorem we also have that

\|\Psi\|_{L^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\lesssim\|h\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla g\|_{L^{2}}.

Consequently, we obtain the desired limit

\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}I_{\delta,R}=\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}f\eta_{R}\Psi(g,h)=\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}f\Psi(g,h)

by dominated convergence theorem. This completes Part 1.
It remains to prove (5.2).

Part 2: the proof of (5.2).

We now fix
0<\delta\ll 1
, and
1\ll R<\infty
, let
f_{\delta}
and
\eta_{R}
be defined as in Part 1 above,
and for notational convenience,
we set
f=f_{\delta}\eta_{R}
. Recall also that by density, we may assume that
g,h\in C_{0}^{\infty}
.
Then qualitatively, with this revised notation,
fg\in H^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
f\in H^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
.
Of course, by hypothesis, we also have a quantitative
L^{\infty}
bound for
f
, and moreover

f
now has compact support.
We let
u
be the solution of

(D)^{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}
(equivalently, the solution of

(D)^{\mathcal{L}}_{2}
; see Remark 4) with boundary data
f
, and as above, we let

H
be the solution of
(D)^{\mathcal{L}^{*}}_{2}
with boundary data
h
,
and set
V(x,t)=\varphi_{t}*g=P_{t}\,g
, where
P_{t}
is a nice approximate identity
with a smooth, radial, compactly supported kernel
\varphi_{t}
.

Thus, (5.2) will follow immediately once we establish the following estimate:

\biggl|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left[\Lambda(fg)\overline{h}-g\Lambda(f)\overline{h}\right]\biggl|\,\lesssim\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|\nabla_{\|}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,,

for all
g,h\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and every

f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
compact support.

Exactly as in
(3.3)
, we have

\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left[\Lambda(fg)\overline{h}-g\Lambda(f)\overline{h}\right]\,=\,\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}}u\nabla V\overline{A^{*}\nabla H}\,-\,\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}}A\nabla u\nabla V\overline{H}:=I+J\,.

[formula]

By Lemma 3.1, and the solvability of
(D)^{\mathcal{L}^{*}}_{2}
,

|I|\,\lesssim\,\sup_{t>0}\|H(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\bigg(\|N_{*}(u\nabla V)\|_{L^{2}}\,+\,\interleave t\nabla(u\nabla V)\interleave\bigg)\\[4.0pt]
\lesssim\,\|h\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|N_{*}(u\nabla V)\|_{L^{2}}\,+\,\interleave t\nabla u\cdot\nabla V\interleave\,+\,\interleave tu\nabla^{2}V\interleave\right)\\[4.0pt]
=:\,\|h\|_{L^{2}}\,\big({\bf M}_{1}+{\bf M}_{2}+{\bf M}_{3}\big)\,.

In turn, to handle term
I
, it is therefore enough to show that

{\bf M}_{1}+{\bf M}_{2}+{\bf M}_{3}\,\lesssim\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|\nabla_{\|}g\|_{L^{2}}\,.

To this end, let us note that
by Proposition 4.6,

d\mu=|t\nabla u(x,t)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}\;\mbox{is a Carleson measure on }\;{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}\;\mbox{with norm}\;\|\mu\|_{\mathit{c}}\lesssim\|f\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,.

[formula]

Recall that
V=P_{t}g
, so that by (3.2),
N_{*}(\nabla V)\lesssim\mathcal{M}(\nabla_{\|}g)
, where

\mathcal{M}
denotes the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator. Moreover,

t\nabla^{2}V=Q_{t}(\nabla_{\|}g)
, where
Q_{t}
satisfies the classical Littlewood-Paley estimate

\interleave\!Q_{t}F\interleave\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

for arbitrary
F\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Consequently,

\|N_{*}(\nabla V)\|_{L^{2}}\,+\,\interleave t\nabla^{2}V\interleave\,\lesssim\,\|\nabla_{\parallel}g\|_{L^{2}}

[formula]

With these observations in hand, by the Agmon-Miranda maximum principle and (5.9), we have

{\bf M}_{1}\,\leq\,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1})}\,\|N_{*}(\nabla V)\|_{L^{2}}\,\lesssim\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|\nabla_{\|}g\|_{L^{2}}\,.

By (5.8), Carleson’s lemma, and (5.9),

{\bf M}_{2}\,\lesssim\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|N_{*}(\nabla V)\|_{L^{2}}\,\lesssim\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|\nabla_{\|}g\|_{L^{2}}\,,

and by the Agmon-Miranda maximum principle and (5.9),

{\bf M}_{3}\,\leq\,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1})}\interleave t\nabla^{2}V\interleave\,\lesssim\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|\nabla_{\|}g\|_{L^{2}}\,.

This concludes out treatment of term
I
.

It remains to
estimate term
J
(see (5.7) above), which is the heart of the matter.
The basic strategy will be that of Lemma 3.1, but in the present setting we shall
need to proceed more carefully.
As in the proof of Lemma 3.1, it suffices to prove

\displaystyle\sup_{0<\rho\ll 1}\fint_{\rho}^{2\rho}\bigg|\int^{1/\theta}_{\theta}\!\!\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}A(x)\nabla u(x,t)\cdot\nabla V(x,t)\,\overline{H(x,t)}\,dxdt\bigg|d\theta\,\lesssim\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|\nabla_{\parallel}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

For any
\rho>0
small, integrating by parts in
t
, we have the following

\displaystyle\int^{1/\theta}_{\theta}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}A(x)\nabla u(x,t)\cdot\nabla V(x,t)\overline{H}dxdt\\[4.0pt]
=\,\left(\displaystyle\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}A\,t\nabla u(\cdot,t)\cdot\nabla V(\cdot,t)\overline{H}\,dx\right)\bigg|_{t=\theta}^{t=1/\theta}\,-\,\displaystyle\int^{1/\theta}_{\theta}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}A\nabla\partial_{t}u\cdot\nabla V\,\overline{H}\,dx\,tdt\\[4.0pt]
-\,\displaystyle\int^{1/\theta}_{\theta}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}A\,t\nabla u\cdot\nabla\partial_{t}V\,\overline{H}\,dx\,dt\ \,-\,\displaystyle\int^{1/\theta}_{\theta}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}A\,t\nabla u\cdot\nabla V\,\overline{\partial_{t}H}\,dx\,dt\\[4.0pt]
\,=:\,J_{1}-J_{2}-J_{3}-J_{4}.

We start with the last of these. Uniformly in
\theta
, and hence in
\rho

|J_{4}|\,\lesssim\,\left(\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}}\big|t\nabla u(x,t)\cdot\nabla V(x,t)\big|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|t\partial_{t}H(x,t)\big|^{2}\,\frac{dxdt}{t}\right)^{1/2}\\[4.0pt]
\lesssim\,\interleave t\nabla u\cdot\nabla V\interleave\interleave t\nabla H\interleave\,\lesssim\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\nabla_{\parallel}g\|_{L^{2}}\|h\|_{L^{2}},

where we have used (5.8), (5.9) (as for the term
{\bf M}_{2}
above),
along with (2.12) for the adjoint solution
H
, and the solvability of
(D)^{\mathcal{L}^{*}}_{2}
.

By Cauchy-Schwarz,

|J_{3}|\,\lesssim\,\interleave\,|t\nabla u|\,\,|H(\cdot,t)|\,\interleave\interleave t\nabla^{2}V\interleave\\[4.0pt]
\lesssim\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|N_{*}H\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|\nabla_{\|}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\lesssim\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|\nabla_{\|}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,,

as desired, uniformly in
\theta
(hence also in
\rho
),
where we have used (5.8) and Carleson’s lemma, (5.9), and the fact that
H
solves

(D)_{2}^{\mathcal{L}}
with data
h
.

The boundary terms
J_{1}
are handled as follows:

\fint_{\rho}^{2\rho}|J_{1}|\,d\theta\,\lesssim\,\sup_{r>0}\fint_{r}^{2r}\!\!\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|t\nabla u(x,t)|\,|\nabla V(x,t)|\,|H(x,t)|\,dx\,dt\\[4.0pt]
\lesssim\,\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|t\nabla u(x,t)|^{2}\,|\nabla V(x,t)|^{2}\,\frac{dxdt}{t}\right)^{1/2}\|N_{*}(H)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\[4.0pt]
\lesssim\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|\nabla_{\|}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,,

uniformly in
\rho
,
by (5.8), Carleson’s lemma and (5.9), and the fact that
H
solves

(D)_{2}^{\mathcal{L}^{*}}
with data
h
.

It remains to treat

. To this end, we begin by recording the following
generalization of
(5.8), which follows from the latter by the

for any

d\mu_{m}=|t^{m}\nabla\partial_{t}^{m-1}u(x,t)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}\;\mbox{is a Carleson measure on }\;{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}\;\mbox{with norm}\;\|\mu_{m}\|_{\mathit{c}}\lesssim\|f\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

[formula]

with implicit constant depending of course on
m
, as well as on dimension and ellipticity.

To control the term

,
we integrate by parts up to a total of
N+1
times in
t
(that is,
N
additional times:
we have already done so once),
for some suitably large integer

to be chosen,
stopping the first time that a

either
\nabla V
or

In either of the latter two cases, the result is a term of

or
J_{4}
, along with boundary terms of the same form as

Using (5.10) in lieu of (5.8), we may handle these terms exactly like their counterparts with

already treated above. The one scenario that remains to be considered is that which occurs when all

N+1

fall upon

, i.e., it remains only to show that

\displaystyle\sup_{0<\rho\ll 1}\fint_{\rho}^{2\rho}\bigg|\displaystyle\int^{1/\theta}_{\theta}\!\!\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}A(x)\nabla\partial_{t}^{N+1}u(x,t)\cdot\nabla V(x,t)\overline{H(x,t)}\,dx\,t^{N+1}dt\bigg|d\theta\\[4.0pt]
=:\,\displaystyle\sup_{0<\rho\ll 1}\fint_{\rho}^{2\rho}|\Omega(\theta)|\,d\theta\,\lesssim\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\nabla_{\parallel}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

(3)

provided that

in particular, it will be enough to take

in the sequel.

To prove (5.11), we shall follow the outline of the
argument in Section 3. We first make the change of variable

,
to obtain

\Omega(\theta)\,=\,C_{N}\displaystyle\int^{1/\theta}_{\theta}\!\!\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}A(x)\nabla\partial_{t}^{N+1}u(x,2t)\cdot\nabla V(x,2t)\overline{H(x,2t)}\,dx\,t^{N+1}dt\,,

and then we use the Green formula (3.10) (bearing in mind our qualitative assumptions on
u
),
and set
s=t
, to get the following generalization of (3.11):

\left(\nabla\partial^{N+1}_{t}u\right)(\cdot,2t)=-\left(\nabla\partial^{N}_{t}\mathcal{D}_{t}\right)(f_{t})\,-\,\left(\nabla\partial^{N}_{t}\big(\mathcal{S}_{t}\nabla_{\parallel}\big)\right)\left(\big(A\nabla u(\cdot,t)\big)_{\parallel}\right)\,,

where as before,
f_{t}:=\partial_{t}u(\cdot,t)
.
We may then set
\mathcal{W}(\cdot,t):=\overline{\nabla V(\cdot,t)}H(\cdot,t)
, and
use (2.6) and (2.7) to write

\left|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}A(x)\nabla\partial_{t}^{N+1}u(x,2t)\cdot\nabla V(x,2t)\overline{H(x,2t)}\,dx\right|\,\leq\,K_{N}(t)+L_{N}(t)\,,

where

K_{N}(t):=\left|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}A(x)\left(\nabla\partial^{N}_{t}\mathcal{D}_{t}\right)(f_{t})(x)\cdot\overline{\mathcal{W}(x,2t)}\,dx\right|\,=\,\left|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\partial_{t}u(\cdot,t)\,\overline{\left(\partial_{\nu_{A^{*}}}\partial_{t}^{N}(\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{L}^{*}}_{-t}\nabla)\right)\big(A^{*}\mathcal{W}(\cdot,2t)\big)}\,dx\right|\,,

and

L_{N}(t):=\left|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}A(x)\left(\nabla\partial^{N}_{t}\big(\mathcal{S}_{t}\nabla_{\parallel}\big)\right)\left(\big(A\nabla u(\cdot,t)\big)_{\parallel}\right)(x)\cdot\overline{\mathcal{W}(x,2t)}\,dx\right|\\[4.0pt]
=\,\left|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\big(A\nabla u(\cdot,t)\big)_{\parallel}\cdot\overline{\left(\nabla_{\parallel}\partial_{t}^{N}(\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{L}^{*}}_{-t}\nabla)\right)(A^{*}\mathcal{W}(\cdot,2t))}\,dx\right|.

Using (2.9), we observe that these expressions make sense, by virtue of our qualitative assumptions on

u
, and the fact that
H(\cdot,t)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

(qualitatively, because the data
h\in C_{0}^{\infty}
; see Remark 4), for each fixed
t>0
,
and therefore
\mathcal{W}(\cdot,t)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(again qualitatively).
Note that

K_{N}(t)+L_{N}(t)\,\lesssim\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|\nabla u(\cdot,t)\big|\,\left|\left(\nabla\partial_{t}^{N}(\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{L}^{*}}_{-t}\nabla)\right)(A^{*}\mathcal{W}(\cdot,2t))\right|\,dx\,,

hence, plugging this bound into the definition of
\Omega(\theta)
, and in turn
into (5.11), it suffices to prove that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|t\nabla u(\cdot,t)\big|\,\left|\Theta_{t}\left(A^{*}\mathcal{W}(\cdot,2t)\right)\right|\,\frac{dxdt}{t}\,\lesssim\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|\nabla_{\parallel}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,,

[formula]

where

\Theta_{t}:=t^{N+1}\nabla\partial_{t}^{N}(\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{L}^{*}}_{-t}\nabla)

(note that the operator
\Theta_{t}
defined in Section 3 was exactly the same, but with
N=2
).
Let
P_{t}
be the nice approximation of the identity
with a smooth, compactly supported kernel, introduced previously.
Just as in Section 3, we then write

\Theta_{t}\big(A^{*}\mathcal{W}(\cdot,2t)\big)(x)\,=\,\Theta_{t}\,A^{*}(x)\,P_{t}\mathcal{W}(\cdot,2t)(x)\,+\,\mathcal{R}_{t}\mathcal{W}(\cdot,2t)(x)\,,

where
for a function
{\bf f}
valued in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
(in particular, for
{\bf f}=W(\cdot,2t)
with
t

momentarily fixed), we define

\mathcal{R}_{t}\,{\bf f}(x)\,:=\,\Theta_{t}\left(A^{*}{\bf f}\right)(x)\,-\,\Theta_{t}\,A^{*}(x)\,P_{t}\,{\bf f}(x)\,.

We first consider the contribution of
\Theta_{t}\,A^{*}(x)\,P_{t}\mathcal{W}(\cdot,2t)(x)
in (5.12).
Note that

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{0}^{l(Q)}\!\!\int_{Q}\big|t\nabla u(x,t)\big|\,\big|\Theta_{t}(A^{*})\big|\,\frac{dxdt}{t}\\[4.0pt]
\leq\,\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{0}^{l(Q)\!\!}\int_{Q}\big|t\nabla u(x,t)\big|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{0}^{l(Q)}\!\!\int_{Q}\big|\Theta_{t}(A^{*})\big|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}\right)^{1/2}\\[4.0pt]
\lesssim\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|A\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\approx\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,,

uniformly in
Q
, by (2.10), (5.8) and ellipticity.
Recall that
\mathcal{W}(\cdot,t):=\overline{\nabla V(\cdot,t)}H(\cdot,t)
, so
by Carleson’s lemma, we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|t\nabla u(x,t)\big|\,\left|\Theta_{t}\,A^{*}(x)\,P_{t}\mathcal{W}(\cdot,2t)(x)\right|\,\frac{dxdt}{t}\,\\[4.0pt]
\lesssim\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|N_{*}\big(P_{t}\mathcal{W}(\cdot,2t)\big)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|N_{*}(\nabla V)\|_{L^{2}}\|N_{*}H\|_{L^{2}}\\[4.0pt]
\lesssim\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|\nabla_{\parallel}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,,

as desired.

Last, we deal with the remainder term
\mathcal{R}_{t}
. We begin by recording two facts, for future reference.
The first
entails precise quantitative dependence on the aperture of the cones used to define
the non-tangential maximal function:

\|N_{*}^{\beta}(f)\|_{L^{2}}\,\lesssim\beta^{n/2}\|N_{*}(f)\|_{L^{2}}

[formula]

for any
f\in L^{2}
and
\beta\geq 1
; the proof can be found in
[
FS
, Lemma 1, p. 166]
.
The second indicates the off-diagonal decay for
\Theta_{t}
, and hence for
\mathcal{R}_{t}
:
for every cube
Q
and all
t\lesssim\ell(Q)
,

\|\mathcal{R}_{t}(\mathbf{f}1_{2^{j+1}Q\setminus 2^{j}Q})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q)}\lesssim 2^{-nj}2^{2j}\left(\frac{t}{2^{j}\ell(Q)}\right)^{2N+2}\|\mathbf{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(2^{j+1}Q\setminus 2^{j}Q)},\quad\forall j\geq 1,

[formula]

for any
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1})
. For
\Theta_{t}
, the latter estimate is simply (2.11) for
\mathcal{L}^{*}
, in the lower
half-space, with
m=N+1
.
As in Section 3, where we considered the case
N=2
(see (3.12) above), we may use
(2.8),
[
AAAHK
, Lemma 3.11]
and the definition of
P_{t}
, to
extend the estimate to
\mathcal{R}_{t}
, which is (5.14).
As in Section 3, we may then define
\mathcal{R}_{t}{\bf 1}
as an
element of
L^{2}_{{\rm loc}}
, where
{\bf 1}
denotes the

(n+1)\times(n+1)
identity matrix,
and by construction
\mathcal{R}_{t}{\bf 1}=0
.

As above, let
\mathbb{D}_{k}
denote the grid of dyadic cubes on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of length
\ell(Q)=2^{k}
.
Let
Q\in\mathbb{D}_{k}
, suppose that
t\in(2^{k},2^{k+1}]
, and for
i\geq 1
, set

[\mathcal{W}]_{2^{i}Q}:=\fint_{2^{i}Q}\mathcal{W}(\cdot,2t)
.
For
j\geq 1
, since
t\approx\ell(Q)
,
we then have

\left(\displaystyle\int_{2^{j+1}Q}\big|\mathcal{W}(x,2t)-[\mathcal{W}]_{2Q}\big|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\\[4.0pt]
\lesssim\left(\displaystyle\int_{2^{j+1}Q}\big|W(x,2t)-[\mathcal{W}]_{2^{j+1}Q}\big|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\,+\,\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(2^{jn}|Q|\,\big|[\mathcal{W}]_{2^{i+1}Q}-[\mathcal{W}]_{2^{i}Q}\big|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\\[4.0pt]
\lesssim\,2^{j}\left(\int_{2^{j+1}Q}|t\nabla_{x}\mathcal{W}(x,2t)|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\,+\,\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(2^{(j-i)n}\,2^{2i}\displaystyle\int_{2^{i+1}Q}|t\nabla_{x}W(x,2t)|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\\[4.0pt]
\lesssim\,j\,2^{jn/2}2^{j}\,\left(\int_{2^{j+1}Q}|t\nabla_{x}\mathcal{W}(x,2t)|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\,,

by Poincaré’s inequality.
Thus, since
\mathcal{R}_{t}{\bf 1}=0
, and
t\approx\ell(Q)
, we see from (5.14) that

\left(\int_{Q}\left|\mathcal{R}_{t}\mathcal{W}(\cdot,2t)(x)\right|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\,\leq\,\left(\int_{Q}\,\left|\mathcal{R}_{t}\,\bigg(\big(\mathcal{W}(\cdot,2t)(x)-[\mathcal{W}]_{2Q}\big)1_{2Q}\bigg)\right|\,dx\right)^{1/2}\\[4.0pt]
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\,\,\,\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{Q}\,\left|\mathcal{R}_{t}\,\bigg(\big(\mathcal{W}(\cdot,2t)(x)-[\mathcal{W}]_{2Q}\big)1_{2^{j+1}Q\setminus 2^{j}Q}\bigg)\right|\,dx\right)^{1/2}\\[4.0pt]
\lesssim\,\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j\,2^{-j(N-1)}\left(\int_{2^{j+1}Q}|t\nabla_{x}\mathcal{W}(x,2t)|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}

(4)

We shall now use the preceding estimate to establish the following.

Claim
. Define the conical square function

\mathcal{A}\mathcal{W}(x):=\left(\iint_{|x-y|<t}\big|\mathcal{R}_{t}\mathcal{W}\big(\cdot,2t\big)(y)\big|^{2}\,\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}.

We then have

\|\mathcal{A}\mathcal{W}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\lesssim\,\|\nabla_{\|}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,.

[formula]

Proof of Claim
. Using (5.15), we find that for some purely dimensional constant
M
,

\mathcal{A}\mathcal{W}(x)\,\leq\,\left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\,\sum_{Q\in\mathbb{D}_{k}:\,\text{dist}(x,Q)<2^{k+1}}\,\int_{2^{k}}^{2^{k+1}}\!\!\!\int_{Q}\big|\mathcal{R}_{t}\mathcal{W}\big(\cdot,2t\big)(y)\big|^{2}\,\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}\\[4.0pt]
\lesssim\,\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j\,2^{-j(N-1)}\left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\,\sum_{Q\in\mathbb{D}_{k}:\,\text{dist}(x,Q)<2^{k+1}}\,\int_{2^{k}}^{2^{k+1}}\!\!\!\int_{2^{j+1}Q}|t\nabla_{y}\mathcal{W}(y,2t)|^{2}\,\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}\\[4.0pt]
\lesssim\,\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j\,2^{-j(N-1)}\left(\iint_{|x-y|<M2^{j}t}|t\nabla_{y}\mathcal{W}(y,2t)|^{2}\,\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}\,.

Recall that in the present context,

\mathcal{W}(\cdot,t)=\overline{\nabla V(\cdot,t)}H(\cdot,t)
.
For notational convenience, we set

g_{1}:=\nabla_{\|}g,\quad G_{1}(x,t):=\nabla V(\cdot,2t)(x),\qquad g_{2}:=h,\quad G_{2}(x,t):=H(x,2t)\,,

so that

|t\nabla_{\|}\mathcal{W}(\cdot,2t)|\,\lesssim\,|t\nabla G_{1}|\,|G_{2}|\,+\,|t\nabla G_{2}|\,|G_{1}|\,.

Note that by (5.9), (2.12), and the solvability of
(D)_{2}^{\mathcal{L}^{*}}
,

\|N_{*}(G_{i})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,+\,\interleave t\nabla G_{i}\interleave\,\lesssim\,\|g_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\qquad i=1,2\,.

Thus, to prove the claim, it suffices to show that

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j\,2^{-j(N-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\iint_{|x-y|<M2^{j}t}|t\nabla G_{1}(y,t)|^{2}\,|G_{2}(y,t)|^{2}\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}dx\,\lesssim\interleave t\nabla G_{1}\interleave\,\|N_{*}(G_{2})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,,

[formula]

along with a similar estimate with the roles of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
reversed.
Since the roles of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are symmetrical, we need only treat the version
stated in (5.17). Note that for
|x-y|<M2^{j}t
,
we have

|G_{2}(y,t)|\leq N_{*}^{M2^{j}}G_{2}(x)\,,

i.e., the non-tangential maximal function defined with respect to a cone of aperture

M2^{j}
. Thus, the left hand side of (5.17) is bounded by

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j\,2^{-j(N-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}N_{*}^{M2^{j}}G_{2}(x)\,\left(\iint_{|x-y|<M2^{j}t}|t\nabla G_{1}(y,t)|^{2}\,\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}dx\\[4.0pt]
\leq\,\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j\,2^{-j(N-1)}\,\|N_{*}^{M2^{j}}G_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\iint_{|x-y|<M2^{j}t}|t\nabla G_{1}(y,t)|^{2}\,\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}dx\right)^{1/2}\\[4.0pt]
\lesssim\,\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j\,2^{-j(N-1)}\,2^{jn/2}\,\|N_{*}G_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,2^{jn/2}\,\interleave t\nabla G_{1}\interleave\,,

where in the last step we have used (5.13), along with the following
estimate, obtained via Fubini’s theorem:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\iint_{|x-y|<M2^{j}t}|t\nabla G_{1}(y,t)|^{2}\,\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}dx\,=\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|t\nabla G_{1}(y,t)|^{2}\,t^{-n}\int_{|x-y|<M2^{j}t}dx\,\frac{dydt}{t}\,\approx\,2^{jn}\interleave t\nabla G_{1}\interleave^{2}.

We now choose
N=n+2
, to obtain (5.17), and hence the claim.

\Box

With (5.16) in hand, and using the Carleson measure estimate (5.8),
we then obtain

\|\mu\|^{1/2}_{\mathit{c}}\,\|\mathcal{A}\mathcal{W}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\nabla_{\|}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|h\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,.

[formula]

We also claim that

{\bf K}\,:=\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|t\nabla u(y,t)\big|\,\left|\mathcal{R}_{t}\mathcal{W}(\cdot,2t)(y)\right|\,\frac{dydt}{t}\,\lesssim\,\|\mu\|^{1/2}_{\mathit{c}}\,\|\mathcal{A}\mathcal{W}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,.

[formula]

Momentarily taking (5.19) for granted, we
then immediately obtain the desired estimate (5.12) for the contribution of the
\mathcal{R}_{t}
term,
by combining (5.18)-(5.19). The conclusion of Theorem 1.2 follows.

It remains only to discuss (5.19). In fact, the latter is actually
a classical estimate of Fefferman
(see
[
FS
, pp. 148-149]
), but for the reader’s convenience, we shall reproduce
the argument here. To this end, for
0<h<\infty
, set

\mathcal{A}_{h}\mathcal{W}(x):=\left(\iint_{|x-y|<t\leq h}\big|\mathcal{R}_{t}\mathcal{W}\big(\cdot,2t\big)(y)\big|^{2}\,\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2},\quad\mathcal{A}_{h}(t\nabla u)(x):=\left(\iint_{|x-y|<t\leq h}\big|t\nabla u(y,t)\big|^{2}\,\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}\,.

(thus, for all
h\in(0,\infty)
,
\mathcal{A}_{h}\mathcal{W}\leq\mathcal{A}\mathcal{W}
as defined above).
By (5.8) (i.e., Proposition 4.6), for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and all
h\in(0,\infty)
,

\int_{|y-x|<h}\big(\mathcal{A}_{h}(t\nabla u)(x)\big)^{2}dx\,\leq\,C_{0}\|\mu\|_{\mathit{c}}\,h^{n}\,,

[formula]

with
C_{0}
depending only on dimension.
Set

h(x):=\sup\left\{h\geq 0:\,\mathcal{A}_{h}(t\nabla u)(x)\leq C_{1}\|\mu\|^{1/2}_{\mathit{c}}\right\}\,,

with
C_{1}
a sufficiently large dimensional constant to be chosen momentarily.
Note that in particular,

\mathcal{A}_{h(x)}(t\nabla u)(x)\leq C_{1}\|\mu\|^{1/2}_{\mathit{c}}\,.

[formula]

Then for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there is a uniform constant
c
such that

|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,|x-y|<h\leq h(x)\}|\geq ch^{n}\,.

[formula]

Indeed, by definition, if
h(x)<h
, then
\mathcal{A}_{h}(t\nabla u)(x)>C_{1}\|\mu\|^{1/2}_{\mathit{c}}
,
so that by Tchebychev’s inequality

\left|\big\{x:\,|x-y|<h\text{ and }h>h(x)\big\}\right|\leq\left|\big\{x:\,|x-y|<h\text{ and }\mathcal{A}_{h}(t\nabla u)(x)>C_{1}\|\mu\|^{1/2}_{\mathit{c}}\big\}\right|\\[4.0pt]
\leq\frac{1}{C_{1}^{2}\|\mu\|_{\mathit{c}}}\int_{|x-y|<h}\left(\mathcal{A}_{h}(t\nabla u)(x)\right)^{2}\,dx\,\leq\,\frac{1}{2}\left|\big\{x:\,|x-y|<h\big\}\right|\,,

by (5.20), provided that
C_{1}
is chosen large enough, depending on
C_{0}
.
Consequently, using (5.22), we see that

{\bf K}\,\lesssim\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|t\nabla u(y,t)\big|\,\left|\mathcal{R}_{t}\mathcal{W}(\cdot,2t)(y)\right|\,t^{-n}\int_{|x-y|<t<h(x)}dx\,\frac{dydt}{t}\\[4.0pt]
=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\iint_{|x-y|<t<h(x)}\big|t\nabla u(y,t)\big|\,\left|\mathcal{R}_{t}\mathcal{W}(\cdot,2t)(y)\right|\,\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}\,dx\\[4.0pt]
\lesssim\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\iint_{|x-y|<t<h(x)}\big|t\nabla u(y,t)\big|^{2}\,\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}\,\left(\iint_{|x-y|<t<h(x)}\left|\mathcal{R}_{t}\mathcal{W}(\cdot,2t)(y)\right|^{2}\,\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}dx\\[4.0pt]
\leq\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{A}_{h(x)}(t\nabla u)(x)\,\mathcal{A}\mathcal{W}(x)\,dx\,\lesssim\,\|\mu\|^{1/2}_{\mathit{c}}\,\|\mathcal{A}\mathcal{W}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,,

by (5.21), so that (5.19) holds.

This concludes the proof of Theorem 1.2.

\Box

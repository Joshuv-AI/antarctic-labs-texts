Euler-Lagrange Equations for Multiobjective Calculus of Variations Problems via Set Optimization
D. Visetti, F. Heyde

Abstract
The problem of minimizing an integral functional of a vector-valued Lagrangian on a set of admissible arcs with given endpoints is considered. The problem is tackled by embedding it into a set-optimization problem such that the image space becomes a complete lattice. This procedure allows to define the concepts of minimizer and of infimizer, as two completely different notions. An infimizer is proved to contain minimizers or at least minimizing sequences for the linearly scalarized problems, with the scalarizing parameters being elements in the dual of the ordering cone. In this way set-valued Euler-Lagrange equations are obtained and weak solutions are defined for these equations. Following the guidelines of the classical results, under suitable convexity and coercivity hypotheses an existence result for an infimizer is proved. In addition to the unconstrained problem, Euler-Lagrange equations are provided for the case with isoperimetric constraints. Finally, the results are applied to the optimization of the shape of energy-saving buildings.



Keywords:
multicriteria calculus of variations, multiobjective optimization,
set-valued Euler-Lagrange equations, isoperimetric problems, Lagrange multipliers.

49J53

Introduction

The problem of minimizing a vector-valued functional on a set of admissible arcs
with given endpoints is a multicriteria calculus of variations problem. Similar
problems of optimal control type have been called multi-index optimization
problems and already studied in the 1960s and 70s, see for example
[
16
]
,
[
18
]
. Solving such a problem is usually understood as looking for a minimal element
in the set of the images with respect to a vector order generated by a convex cone
C
. The standard
example is when
C
is the positive orthant, which generates the component-wise
order. However, this point of view does not lead to satisfactory results for example
when it comes to value functions or duality since these concepts involve infima and suprema which may not exist with respect to vector orders or, even if they do, they
do not provide useful information. Therefore, a new approach is proposed which
consists of two steps: first, extend the problem to one with values in a complete
lattice of sets and secondly, apply concepts and tools from set optimization theory as surveyed in
[
6
]
. In particular, the set optimization solution concept is applied here which has two features: on the one hand, one looks for sets which
generate the infimum of the set extension of the functional (called infimizers), on the other hand, one looks for minimal elements with respect to a set relation. This is a new approach: the goal of this paper are Euler-Lagrange equations characterizing infimizers.

The underlying order relation will be generated by a closed convex cone which can
be different from the positive orthant. This vector order will then be used to construct the image spaces of the set-valued extension of the problem. This procedure and basic concepts from the theory of ordered vector spaces and the complete-lattice approach to set optimization will be recalled in the next section.

Multi-objective optimization has many applications in engineering, see, e.g.,
[
14
]
and the bibliography therein. For example, it
is employed in the design, planning and operation of chemical process industries (see
[
15
]
). More references
about the use of multiple objectives in engineering system design and reliability optimization can be found in
[
12
]
: they range from a residual heat removal safety system for a boiling water reactor, to the selection of technical specifications and maintenance activities at nuclear power plants to increase reliability, availability and maintainability of safety-related equipment or to minimizing total wire length and minimizing the failure rate in the printed circuit board design.

Infimizers are sets, but it is easier to deal with points. If one passes from the
set-valued function
f
to be minimized to the inf-translation
of
f
by
M
, the infimizer, which was a set, becomes a set formed by a single
point:
\{0\}
.
Using this fact and the scalarizations with respect to elements of the dual cone,
we prove that an infimizer
M
must contain the minimizers of the scalarized
problems or at least their minimizing sequences (in the considered direction).
For each direction
\zeta
, set-valued Euler-Lagrange equations are derived. It is
also possible to write an equation of sets, that contains the information of all the
scalarizations corresponding to the dual cone.

Like in the well-known real-valued results, under hypotheses of coercivity and
convexity an existence result is proved: the set formed by all the solutions of the scalarized problems is an infimizer. In order to obtain the existence result we
must enlarge the set of the admissible arcs from the space
C^{1}
to a
Sobolev space. As a consequence, a concept of weak solutions for set-valued
Euler-Lagrange equations is proposed.

Since constrained problems are important for applications, we
considered the isoperimetric problem. Also here an infimizer
M
must
contain the minimizers of the scalarized problems or at least their minimizing
sequences. The set-valued Euler-Lagrange equations are provided.

Finally, an application to optimization of the shape of energy-saving buildings,
taken from
[
13
]
, is studied.

2
Complete Lattices of Sets: Basic Concepts

In this section, basic concepts and constructions for the complete-lattice approach to vector and set optimization are recalled. The reader may compare

[
6
]
for more details and references.

A nonempty closed convex cone
\emptyset\neq C\subsetneq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
generates a vector preorder on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(i.e., a reflexive and transitive relation which is compatible with addition and multiplication by non-negative numbers) by means of

y\leq_{C}z\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad z-y\in C

for
y,z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. The (positive) dual of the cone
C
is

C^{+}=\{\zeta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\mid\forall z\in C\,,\ \zeta\cdot z\geq 0\}.

The bipolar theorem yields
C=C^{++}:=(C^{+})^{+}
under the given assumptions.

Let
\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
denote the power set of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. On
\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
the Minkowski
sum of two non-empty sets
A,B
is defined as
A+B=\{a+b\mid a\in A,\;b\in B\}
.
It can be extended to the whole power set by
A+\emptyset=\emptyset+A=\emptyset
. Furthermore, we consider the sum

A\oplus B=\mbox{cl}\,(A+B)\,,

where
\mbox{cl}\,(\cdot)
denotes the closure with respect to the usual topology on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

The two sets

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)

\displaystyle=\{A\in\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\mid A=A\oplus C\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)

\displaystyle=\{A\in\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\mid A=\mbox{cl\,co}\,(A+C)\}

comprise the closed and the closed convex subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, respectively, which are stable under addition of
C
. Clearly,
\mathcal{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)\subseteq\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)
. The pairs
(\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C),\supseteq)
,
(\mathcal{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C),\supseteq)
are complete lattices with the following formulas for infimum and supremum (see, for example,
[
6
]
) for a collection
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C),\mathcal{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)
:

\displaystyle\inf\mathcal{A}=\mbox{cl}\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{A}}A,\quad

\displaystyle\sup\mathcal{A}=\bigcap_{A\in\mathcal{A}}A,

(1)

\displaystyle\inf\mathcal{A}=\mbox{cl\,co}\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{A}}A,\quad

\displaystyle\sup\mathcal{A}=\bigcap_{A\in\mathcal{A}}A,

(2)

respectively. Moreover,
\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)
as well as
\mathcal{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)
are ordered conlinear spaces in the sense that their order
\supseteq
is compatible with the addition
\oplus
and multiplication with non-negative scalars defined by
sA=\{sa\mid a\in A\}
for
A\neq\emptyset
,
s\emptyset=\emptyset
whenever
s>0
, and
0A=C
for all
A\in\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)
,
\mathcal{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)
. Finally,
\mathcal{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)
with these operations is a conlinear subspace of
\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)
. For references on conlinear spaces see

[
6
]
.

For
\zeta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
, we denote

H^{+}(\zeta)=\{z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\mid\zeta\cdot z\geq 0\}\,.

The
\zeta
-difference for
\zeta\in C^{+}\backslash\{0\}
of two sets
A,B\in\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)
is the set

A-_{\zeta}B=\{z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\mid z+B\subseteq A\oplus H^{+}(\zeta)\}

which can also be written as

A-_{\zeta}B=\{z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\mid\zeta\cdot z+\inf_{b\in B}\zeta\cdot b\geq\inf_{a\in A}\zeta\cdot a\}

(3)

(see Remark 2.1 in
[
9
]
). The
\zeta
-difference is always an element of

\mathcal{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)
and does not change if
A
and
B
are replaced by their closed
convex hulls. It is a closed half-space or the empty set or all the space.

Let
\{A_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of sets in
\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)
, we
denote by
\lim_{n\to\infty}A_{n}
the following set:

\lim_{n\to\infty}A_{n}=\left\{z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\ |\ \forall n\in{\mathbb{N}},\exists z_{n}\in A_{n},\lim_{n\to\infty}z_{n}=z\right\}.

This definition of limit coincides with the upper limit of Painlevé-Kuratowski
(see
[
1
]
,
\mathrm{Liminf}_{n\to\infty}A_{n}=\left\{z\in Z\ |\ \lim_{n\to\infty}d(z,A_{n})=0\right\}
).

Let
\{A_{s}\}_{s\in S}
with
S\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
,
S=[s_{1},s_{0})
or
S=(s_{0},s_{2}]
,

s_{0},s_{1},s_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, be a family of sets in
\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)
. We
denote by
\lim_{s\to s_{0}}A_{s}
the set which satisfies that for any sequence

\{s_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subseteq S
with
s_{n}\to s_{0}
one has

\lim_{s\to s_{0}}A_{s}=\lim_{n\to\infty}A_{s_{n}}
.

Let
X
be a vector space and
f\colon X\to\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)
be a
set-valued function. The graph of
f
is the set

\mbox{graph}\,f=\{(x,z)\in X\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\mid z\in f(x)\}\subseteq X\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

and its (effective) domain is the set

\mbox{dom}\,f=\{x\in X\mid f(x)\neq\emptyset\}\subseteq X\,.

The function
f
is called convex if and only if
\mbox{graph}\,f
is a convex set.
This is equivalent to the following inclusion: for any
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
and

\lambda\in(0,1)

f(\lambda x_{1}+(1-\lambda)x_{2})\supseteq\lambda f(x_{1})+(1-\lambda)f(x_{2})\,.

A convex function
f\colon X\to\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)
automatically maps into

\mathcal{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)
, i.e., it has convex values.

For
M\subseteq X
, we set

f[M]=\{f(x)\mid x\in M\}\,.

A derivative concept that is most compatible with the complete lattice approach and also the solution concept introduced in the next section is as follows.

Let
\zeta\in C^{+}\backslash\{0\}
and
v\in X
. The directional derivative with
respect to
\zeta
of
f\colon X\to\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)
at
x_{0}\in X
in direction

v
is

f^{\prime}_{\zeta}(x_{0})(v)=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s}\left(f(x_{0}+sv)-_{\zeta}f(x_{0})\right)

(4)

(see
[
8
]
and
[
9
]
for a motivation and many features including a
discussion of the “improper” function values
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
\emptyset
).

We can also consider the derivative of
f
along a curve
\gamma:[s_{1},s_{2}]\to X
with respect to
\zeta
at
s_{0}\in[s_{1},s_{2}]
:

\left.\frac{d\ }{ds}f_{\zeta}(\gamma(s))\right|_{s=s_{0}}=\lim_{s\to s_{0}}\frac{1}{s-s_{0}}\left(f(\gamma(s))-_{\zeta}f(\gamma(s_{0}))\right).

(5)

In the set-valued theory, a special type of function replaces linear functionals.
Let
X
be a topological vector space and
X^{\prime}
its dual space. For
\xi\in X^{\prime}

and
\zeta\in C^{+}\backslash\{0\}
, the function
S_{(\xi,\zeta)}\colon X\to\mathcal{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)
is defined by

S_{(\xi,\zeta)}(x)=\{z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\mid\zeta\cdot z\geq\xi(x)\}\,.

(6)

For each
\xi\in X^{\prime}
and
\zeta\in C^{+}\backslash\{0\}
,
S_{(\xi,\zeta)}
is positively homogeneous and additive, hence in particular convex (see
[
6
]
).

3
Set-valued Euler-Lagrange Equation

Let
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
be two real numbers with
a<b
,
n,d
two positive integers
and
L\colon[a,b]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, where
d
is greater than 1, be
a function of class
C^{1}
. We consider the functional

J(y)=\int_{a}^{b}L(t,y(t),\dot{y}(t))\,dt

(7)

on a set
\mathcal{X}
of admissible arcs, taking values in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and with
given endpoints:

\mathcal{X}=\{y\in C^{1}([a,b];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\mid y(a)=A,\ y(b)=B\}\,,

(8)

for
A,B\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

In this section, we first clarify in what sense the problem of minimizing the
functional (
7
) on the set
\mathcal{X}
, shall be solved. The set-valued
extension of the problem is established by extending
L
and
J
to functions into

\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)
. The function

\overline{L}\colon[a,b]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\to\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)
is simply defined by

\overline{L}(t,y,p)=L(t,y,p)+C

and the extension of
J
is
\overline{J}\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)
defined by

\overline{J}(y)=\int_{a}^{b}\overline{L}(t,y(t),\dot{y}(t))\,dt

where the integral is understood in the Aumann sense (see
[
2
]
),
but one may just note that
\overline{J}(y)=J(y)+C
. Clearly, since
L
and
J
are vector-valued and
C
is closed and convex,
the two extensions actually map into
\mathcal{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)
. However, they will be considered
as
\mathcal{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)
-valued only if convexity is assumed,
otherwise as
\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)
-valued. The choice influences the formula for the
infimum as one may see from (
1
), (
2
).

In this paper, the following solution concept for the problem

\text{minimize}\quad\overline{J}(y)\quad\text{subject to}\quad y\in\mathcal{X}

is used. It is an adaption of the concept from
[
10
]
which is, for more abstract problems, due to
[
8
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

A set
M\subseteq\mathcal{X}
is called an infimizer of (P) if

\inf\overline{J}[M]=\inf\overline{J}[\mathcal{X}].

(9)

An element
y_{\zeta}\in\mathcal{X}
is called a
\zeta
-solution of (P) with

\zeta\in C^{+}\backslash\{0\}
if

\zeta\cdot J(y_{\zeta})=\inf\{\zeta\cdot\overline{J}(y)\mid y\in\mathcal{X}\}.

(10)

An infimizer
M\subseteq\mathcal{X}
of (P) which only includes
\zeta
-solutions
with
\zeta\in C^{+}\backslash\{0\}
is called a
C^{+}
-solution of (P).

It is immediate that
M=\mathcal{X}
is an infimizer, and that if
M
is
an infimizer of (P), then so is any other set
M^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
with
M^{\prime}\supseteq M
. Since an infimizer is a subset of
\mathcal{X}
, it may seem hard to
formulate necessary conditions for a set to be an infimizer. The following concept
reduces infimizers to singletons. It was introduced in
[
8
]
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
M\subseteq\mathcal{X}
be a non-empty set of feasible arcs. The function

\widehat{J}(\cdot;M)\colon C^{1}_{0}([a,b];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)

defined by

\widehat{J}(y;M)=\inf_{v\in M}\overline{J}(y+v)

(11)

is called the inf-translation of
\overline{J}
by
M
.

The infimum in (
11
) is understood in
(\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C),\supseteq)
. Note that
\widehat{J}(\cdot;M)
is not convex in general even if
\overline{J}
is. On the other hand, it is easy to see that a set
M
is an infimizer for
\overline{J}
if and only if
\{0\}\subset C_{0}^{1}([a,b];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is an infimizer for
\widehat{J}(\cdot;M)
(see
[
6
]
). In
[
7
]
the link between optimization problems
and the inf-translation is studied.

It is important to realize that even though the original problem is vector-valued, its inf-translation

\text{minimize}\quad\widehat{J}(y;M)\quad\text{subject to}\quad y\in C^{1}_{0}([a,b];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})

is a genuine set-valued problem.

Remark 3.3
.

Since the boundary conditions are essentially linear constraints, the inf-translation
can be considered as a function on the linear space
C_{0}^{1}([a,b];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
which
“absorbs” the boundary conditions, since for
v\in\mathcal{X}
and
y\in C_{0}^{1}([a,b];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
one has
y+v\in\mathcal{X}
.

In (
10
), linear scalarizations of the vector-valued problems were used. This is extended to
\widehat{J}
by considering the function
\varphi_{\zeta,M}\colon C^{1}_{0}([a,b];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}:={\mathbb{R}}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
defined by

\varphi_{\zeta,M}(v)=\inf_{z\in\widehat{J}(v;M)}\zeta\cdot z

(12)

with
\zeta\in C^{+}
.

Since the following equalities hold

\displaystyle\varphi_{\zeta,M}(v)

\displaystyle=\inf_{z\in\widehat{J}(v;M)}\zeta\cdot z=\inf\left\{\zeta\cdot z\mid z\in\inf_{y\in M}\overline{J}(y+v)=\mbox{cl}\bigcup_{y\in M}\overline{J}(y+v)\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\zeta\cdot z\mid z\in\bigcup_{y\in M}J(y+v)\right\},

one has

\varphi_{\zeta,M}(v)=\inf_{y\in M}\zeta\cdot J(y+v)\,.

(13)

The relation between an infimizer of the set-valued function
\overline{J}
and the
corresponding extended real-valued function
\varphi_{\zeta,M}
is explained in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
M\subset\mathcal{X}
is an infimizer of
\overline{J}
, then
0\in C^{1}_{0}([a,b];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is a minimizer of
\varphi_{\zeta,M}
for every
\zeta\in C^{+}\backslash\{0\}
.

If
\bar{J}
is convex as a function into
\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},C)
and
M
is a convex set,
then
M
is an infimizer if and only if
0\in C^{1}_{0}([a,b];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is a minimizer
of
\varphi_{\zeta,M}
for every
\zeta\in C^{+}\backslash\{0\}
.

Proof.

If
M\subset\mathcal{X}
is an infimizer for
\overline{J}
, using (
12
),
we have for all
v\in C_{0}^{1}[a,b]

\displaystyle\varphi_{\zeta,M}(v)

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\zeta\cdot z\mid z\in\widehat{J}(v;M)\right\}=\inf\left\{\zeta\cdot z\mid z\in\inf_{y\in M}\overline{J}(y+v)\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\inf\left\{\zeta\cdot z\mid z\in\inf_{y\in\mathcal{X}}\overline{J}(y)\right\}=\inf\left\{\zeta\cdot z\mid z\in\inf_{y\in M}\overline{J}(y)\right\}=\varphi_{\zeta,M}(0)\,.

To prove the second part of the statement, let us suppose that
M
is not an
infimizer. Then there exists
y_{*}\in\mathcal{X}
such that

J(y_{*})\not\in\inf_{y\in M}\overline{J}(y)=:\Gamma_{M},

since otherwise
\bigcup_{y\in\mathcal{X}}\{J(y)\}\subseteq\inf_{y\in M}\overline{J}(y)
and consequently

\inf_{y\in X}\overline{J}(y)={\rm cl\,}\left(\bigcup_{y\in\mathcal{X}}\{J(y)\}+C\right)\subseteq{\rm cl\,}\left(\inf_{y\in M}\overline{J}(y)+C\right)=\inf_{y\in M}\overline{J}(y)

thus
M
would be an infimizer.

Since
\Gamma_{M}
is nonempty closed and convex,
J(y_{*})
can be strongly
seperated form
\Gamma_{M}
, i.e. there is
\xi
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}

such that

\xi\cdot J(y_{*})<\inf_{z\in\Gamma_{M}}\xi\cdot z.

(14)

Since
M
is nonempty there exists
\hat{y}\in M
. For all
c\in C
,
J(\hat{y})+c\in\Gamma_{M}
. We can conclude that
\xi\in C^{+}
, since otherwise
\xi\cdot\hat{c}<0
for some
\hat{c}\in C
and consequently

\inf_{z\in\Gamma_{M}}\xi\cdot z\leq\inf_{t>0}\xi\cdot\left(J(\hat{y})+t\hat{c}\right)=\xi\cdot J(\hat{y})+\inf_{t>0}t\left(\xi\cdot\hat{c}\right)=-\infty,

contradicting (
14
).

With
v:=y_{*}-\hat{y}\in C_{0}^{1}([a,b];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
we obtain from (
14
):

\varphi_{\xi,M}(v)\leq\xi\cdot J(v+\hat{y})=\xi\cdot J(y_{*})<\inf_{z\in\Gamma_{M}}\xi\cdot z=\varphi_{\xi,M}(0),

a contradiction to the assumptions, which completes the proof.
∎

To simplify the notation, we denote

L_{\zeta}(t,y,p)=L(t,y,p)\cdot\zeta\,,

while
D_{y}L_{\zeta}(t,y,p)
,
D_{p}L_{\zeta}(t,y,p)
are the vectors in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of the
derivatives with respect to
y_{i}
and with respect to

p_{i}
for
i=1,2,\dots,n
. Analogously, we write

J_{\zeta}(y)=\zeta\cdot J(y)=\int_{a}^{b}L_{\zeta}(t,y,\dot{y})dt\,.

Remark 3.5
.

If
M
is an infimizer for
\overline{J}
, from the definition of infimizer, for
every
\zeta\in C^{+}\backslash\{0\}
, we have

\inf_{z\in\inf_{y\in M}\overline{J}(y)}\zeta\cdot z=\inf_{z\in\inf_{y\in\mathcal{X}}\overline{J}(y)}\zeta\cdot z\,.

As in the previous lemma, this equation can be written as

\inf_{y\in M}J_{\zeta}(y)=\inf_{y\in\mathcal{X}}J_{\zeta}(y)\,.

We can deduce that for every
\zeta\in C^{+}\backslash\{0\}

M
must contain
either a
\zeta
-solution, i.e. an arc
y_{\zeta}
such that

J_{\zeta}(y_{\zeta})=\inf_{y\in\mathcal{X}}J_{\zeta}(y)

(15)

or an infimizing sequence
\{y_{m,\zeta}\}_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
such that

\lim_{m\to+\infty}J_{\zeta}(y_{m,\zeta})=\inf_{y\in\mathcal{X}}J_{\zeta}(y)
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
L:[a,b]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be of class
C^{1}
. Let
M\subset\mathcal{X}

be an infimizer for
\overline{J}
. Given
\zeta\in C^{+}\backslash\{0\}
, suppose that there
exists a
\zeta
-solution
y_{\zeta}\in M
.
Then
\varphi_{\zeta,M}\colon C_{0}^{1}([a,b];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}
is
Fréchet differentiable at
0
and

\varphi_{\zeta,M}^{\prime}(0)(v)=\int_{a}^{b}\left[D_{y}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})\cdot v+D_{p}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})\cdot\dot{v}\right]dt\,.

(16)

Proof.

For any
v\in C_{0}^{1}([a,b];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, the following inequalities hold:

\displaystyle\varphi_{\zeta,M}(v)-\varphi_{\zeta,M}(0)

\displaystyle=\inf_{y\in M}\int_{a}^{b}L_{\zeta}(t,y+v,\dot{y}+\dot{v})dt-\int_{a}^{b}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{a}^{b}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta}+v,\dot{y}_{\zeta}+\dot{v})dt-\int_{a}^{b}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})dt\,.

By the Fréchet differentiability of the functional
J_{\zeta}:C^{1}([a,b];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to{\mathbb{R}}
one can conclude that

\displaystyle J_{\zeta}(y_{\zeta}+v)-J_{\zeta}(y_{\zeta})

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta}+v,\dot{y}_{\zeta}+\dot{v})dt-\int_{a}^{b}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})dt

\displaystyle=J_{\zeta}^{\prime}(y_{\zeta})(v)+o(\|v\|_{C^{1}})

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\left[D_{y}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})\cdot v+D_{p}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})\cdot\dot{v}\right]dt+o(\|v\|_{C^{1}})

and we have proved that

\varphi_{\zeta,M}(v)-\varphi_{\zeta,M}(0)\leq\int_{a}^{b}\left[D_{y}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})\cdot v+D_{p}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})\cdot\dot{v}\right]dt+o(\|v\|_{C^{1}})\,.

(17)

On the other hand, by Lemma

, we have that

\varphi_{\zeta,M}(v)-\varphi_{\zeta,M}(0)\geq 0
. By this and (
17
),
the following inequality holds

\int_{a}^{b}\left[D_{y}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})\cdot v+D_{p}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})\cdot\dot{v}\right]dt+o(\|v\|_{C^{1}})\geq 0\,.

Using the same argument with
-v
, one obtains that

\int_{a}^{b}\left[D_{y}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})\cdot v+D_{p}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})\cdot\dot{v}\right]dt+o(\|v\|_{C^{1}})\leq 0

and so

\int_{a}^{b}\left[D_{y}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})\cdot v+D_{p}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})\cdot\dot{v}\right]dt=o(\|v\|_{C^{1}})\,.

Then also the other inequality holds:

\varphi_{\zeta,M}(v)-\varphi_{\zeta,M}(0)\geq\int_{a}^{b}\left[D_{y}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})\cdot v+D_{p}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})\cdot\dot{v}\right]dt+o(\|v\|_{C^{1}[a,b]})\,.

(18)

Now by (
17

∎

Example 3.10

In this section we consider a bigger set of admissible arcs, in order to obtain results

The admissible set is

We assume that all the components of the Lagrangian satisfy the inequality

for some constant

and all

,

and

In this section the functional is

23
) ensures that the

Remark 4.5
.

For the Lagrangian of Example

To fix the ideas, let the cone be

. Except for the projection along
the horizontal direction, the Lagrangian

is not coercive and is not even
bounded from below. All the same, considering the arcs in

and using Poincaré inequality, it is possible to prove that

with

constants. In fact, let

.
Then

and there exist

constants such

\displaystyle\leq\|y-y_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\|y_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq K\|\dot{y}-\dot{y}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\|y_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq K\|\dot{y}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+K^{\prime}\,.

From (

\alpha>0
and

such that

(J(y))_{2}\geq\alpha\|\dot{y}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}-\beta

With this observation we can see that, even if

is not coervive in

is coercive in

In this section we want to consider isoperimetric problems. See also
[

]
and

[

we derive a vector-valued result.

The considered problem is the minimization of the functional

\overline{J}(y)=\int_{a}^{b}\overline{L}(t,y(t),\dot{y}(t))\,dt

(34)

on the set

\mathcal{X}_{2}=\left\{y\in W^{1,q}([a,b];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\mid y(a)=A,\ y(b)=B,\ \int_{a}^{b}G(y(t))\,dt=0\right\},

(35)

where
q>1
and
G:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is such that every component verifies

|\nabla G_{i}(w)|\leq K\left(|w|^{q-1}+1\right)

(36)

for some constant
K
.

The following real-valued existence result of a constrained minimizer holds
(this result is more general than the one in
[
3
]
).

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume that
L
satisfies the coercivity inequalities (
28
) for any
\zeta\in C^{+}\backslash\{0\}
such that
|\zeta|=1
and that
\overline{L}(t,y,p)
is convex in

p
. Assume that
G
satisfies
(
36
). Moreover, assume that
\mathcal{X}_{2}
is not empty. For any

\zeta\in C^{+}\backslash\{0\}
, there exists a
\zeta
-solution
y_{\zeta}\in\mathcal{X}_{2}
satisfying

J_{\zeta}(y_{\zeta})=\inf_{y\in\mathcal{X}_{2}}J_{\zeta}(y)\,.

(37)

Proof.

Let
\{y_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathcal{X}_{2}
be a minimizing sequence:

\lim_{k\to+\infty}J_{\zeta}(y_{k})=\inf_{y\in\mathcal{X}_{2}}J_{\zeta}(y)=m_{0}
.
It is possible to see that the hypothesis of coercivity implies that
\{y_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is bounded in
W^{1,q}([a,b];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. There exists a
subsequence, that we still denote
\{y_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, and a function

y_{0}\in W^{1,q}([a,b];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that
y_{k}\rightharpoonup y_{0}
in

W^{1,q}([a,b];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
Since the functional
J_{\zeta}
is weakly lower semicontinuous

J_{\zeta}(y_{0})\leq\lim_{k\to+\infty}J_{\zeta}(y_{k})=m_{0}
.
We want to prove that
y_{0}\in\mathcal{X}_{2}
. By Rellich-Kondrachov theorem

W^{1,q}([a,b];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is compactly embedded in
C([a,b];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
Consequently, both
y_{k}
and
G(y_{k})
tend pointwise to
y_{0}
and
G(y_{0})
,
respectively. From (
36
), we easily deduce that

|G_{i}(w)|\leq K^{\prime}\left(|w|^{q}+1\right)
, where
K^{\prime}
is a positive constant. Since

|G_{i}(y_{k})|\leq K^{\prime}\left(|y_{k}|^{q}+1\right)\leq K^{\prime\prime}\left(|y_{0}|^{q}+1\right),

with
K^{\prime\prime}
positive constant, by the dominated convergence theorem

0=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{a}^{b}G_{i}(y_{k}(t))dt=\int_{a}^{b}G_{i}(y_{0}(t))dt\,.

Then
y_{0}\in\mathcal{X}_{2}
and coincides with
y_{\zeta}
.
∎

Such a minimizer of the real-valued problem (
37
) is a solution of the
following Lagrange multiplier problem.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
L:[a,b]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be of class
C^{1}
and satisfy
(
23
) and (
24
). Assume that
G
satisfies
(
36
). Let
y_{\zeta}\in\mathcal{X}_{2}
be such that (
37
) holds. Suppose
that there exist
w_{i}\in W_{0}^{1,q}([a,b];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, with
1\leq i\leq m
such that

\det\left(\int_{a}^{b}\nabla G_{i}(y_{\zeta})\cdot w_{j}\,dt\right)\neq 0\,.

(38)

Then there exists
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
for any
v\in W_{0}^{1,q}([a,b];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})

\int_{a}^{b}\left[D_{y}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})\cdot v+D_{p}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})\cdot\dot{v}\right]dt-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\int_{a}^{b}\nabla G_{i}(y_{\zeta})\cdot v\,dt=0\,.

(39)

Proof.

Let us denote by
W
the matrix
m\times m
, whose elements are

W_{ij}=\left(\int_{a}^{b}\nabla G_{i}(y_{\zeta})\cdot w_{j}\,dt\right)

and by
h:{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
the map

h(s,\sigma)=\int_{a}^{b}G\left(y_{\zeta}(t)+sv(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_{i}w_{i}(t)\right)dt\,.

Since
h(0,0)=0
and
\det\frac{\partial h}{\partial\sigma}(0,0)\neq 0
,
it is possible to apply the implicit function theorem and there exists
\sigma:[0,\delta)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
such that
\sigma(0)=0
,
h(s,\sigma(s))=0
for all
s\in[0,\delta)

and

\sigma^{\prime}(0)=-\left(\frac{\partial h}{\partial\sigma}(0,0)\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}(0,0)=-W^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}(0,0)\,,

where the components of the vector
\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}(0,0)
are

\int_{a}^{b}\nabla G_{i}(y_{\zeta})\cdot v\,dt
.
We denote by
w
the curve
w:[0,\delta)\to W_{0}^{1,q}([a,b];{\mathbb{R}}^{n})

as

w(s)=sv+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_{i}(s)w_{i}\,.

(40)

The curve
y_{\zeta}+w(s)
is in
\mathcal{X}_{2}

37

\left.\frac{d}{ds}J_{\zeta}(y_{\zeta}+w(s))\right|_{s=0}=0
and this gives

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}

\displaystyle\left[D_{y}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})\cdot v+D_{p}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})\cdot\dot{v}\right]dt

\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\left(W^{-1}\right)_{ij}\int_{a}^{b}[D_{y}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})\cdot w_{i}+D_{p}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})\cdot\dot{w}_{i}]dt\left(\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}(0,0)\right)_{j}=0\,.

The previous equation can be written as

\int_{a}^{b}\left[D_{y}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})\cdot v+D_{p}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})\cdot\dot{v}\right]dt-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}\int_{a}^{b}\nabla G_{j}(y_{\zeta})\cdot v\,dt=0\,,

with

\lambda_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(W^{-1}\right)_{ij}\int_{a}^{b}[D_{y}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})\cdot w_{i}+D_{p}L_{\zeta}(t,y_{\zeta},\dot{y}_{\zeta})\cdot\dot{w}_{i}]dt\,.

∎

6
Shape Optimization of Energy-saving Buildings

Following the idea in
[
13
]
, we consider the problem of optimizing the
shape of a building of volume
V
and height
h
by minimizing building costs
and yearly heating costs. The plan of the building is defined by two curves

y_{1}(t)
and
y_{2}(t)
, with
y_{1}(t)\geq y_{2}(t)
. Here, in the plane
x,y
, the
positive
y
direction coincides with south. The building is assumed to
extend in the east-west direction from
-a
to
a
, so that
-a\leq t\leq a
.

The construction cost can be represented by the following
functional

J_{1}(y_{1},y_{2})=\int_{-a}^{a}\left[\alpha_{1}\sqrt{1+\dot{y}_{1}^{2}}+\alpha_{2}\sqrt{1+\dot{y}_{2}^{2}}+\alpha_{3}\right]dt\,,

while the annual heating cost can be represented by

J_{2}(y_{1},y_{2})=\int_{-a}^{a}\left[\beta_{1}\sqrt{1+\dot{y}_{1}^{2}}+\beta_{2}\sqrt{1+\dot{y}_{2}^{2}}+\beta_{3}-\beta_{4}\theta(\dot{y}_{1})\sqrt{1+\dot{y}_{1}^{2}}\right]dt\,.

For the detailed explanations of the above functionals and some technical
motivations see
[
13
]
.
Here
\theta
is the heat gain due to the solar radiation during the heating period,
but it is chosen in a different way from
[
13
]
, more precisely
we choose
\theta(p)=\theta_{1}+(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\frac{p}{\sqrt{1+p^{2}}}
,
where
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
are the average sums of the total solar radiation on the
wall facing south (
\theta_{1}
) and on the walls facing east and west (
\theta_{2}
).

The curves must fulfill the boundary conditions
y_{1}(-a)=y_{2}(-a)=0
and
y_{1}(a)=y_{2}(a)=0
. Moreover, there is a constraint because the volume must be
V
:

\int_{-a}^{a}(y_{1}(t)-y_{2}(t))\,dt=\frac{V}{h}\,.

If
J=(J_{1},J_{2})
, our problem is to minimize the functional
\overline{J}(y)
on the set

\mathcal{X}_{3}=\left\{y\in W^{1,2}([-a,a];{\mathbb{R}}^{2})\mid y(\pm a)=(0,0),\ \int_{-a}^{a}G(y)\,dt=0\right\},

where
G:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is defined by
G(y_{1},y_{2})=y_{1}-y_{2}-\frac{V}{2ah}
.

The Lagrangian associated to the functional
J
is
L:[-a,a]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

defined by

L(t,p_{1},p_{2})=\left(\alpha_{1}\sqrt{1+p_{1}^{2}}+\alpha_{2}\sqrt{1+p_{2}^{2}}+\alpha_{3},\beta_{1}^{\prime}\sqrt{1+p_{1}^{2}}+\beta_{2}\sqrt{1+p_{2}^{2}}+\beta_{3}-\beta_{4}^{\prime}p_{1}\right),

with
\beta_{1}^{\prime}=\beta_{1}-\beta_{4}\theta_{1}
and
\beta_{4}^{\prime}=\beta_{4}(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})
.
Let
C
be
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+}
. If
\zeta=(\zeta_{1},1-\zeta_{1})
for
\zeta_{1}\in[0,1]
,
the projection of the Lagrangian in the direction
\zeta
is

\displaystyle L_{\zeta}(t,p_{1},p_{2})=

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}\left(\alpha_{1}\sqrt{1+p_{1}^{2}}+\alpha_{2}\sqrt{1+p_{2}^{2}}+\alpha_{3}\right)

\displaystyle+(1-\zeta_{1})\left(\beta_{1}^{\prime}\sqrt{1+p_{1}^{2}}+\beta_{2}\sqrt{1+p_{2}^{2}}+\beta_{3}-\beta_{4}^{\prime}p_{1}\right).

In case there are solutions that are
C^{2}
, we consider the Euler-Lagrange
equations:

-\frac{d}{dt}D_{p}L_{\zeta}(t,\dot{y})-\lambda\nabla G(y)=0

and we obtain

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle-[\zeta_{1}\alpha_{1}+(1-\zeta_{1})\beta_{1}^{\prime}]\frac{\ddot{y}_{1}}{\sqrt{(1+\dot{y}_{1}^{2})^{3}}}-\lambda=0\\
\\
\displaystyle-[\zeta_{1}\alpha_{2}+(1-\zeta_{1})\beta_{2}]\frac{\ddot{y}_{2}}{\sqrt{(1+\dot{y}_{2}^{2})^{3}}}+\lambda=0\end{array}\right.

(44)

From these ordinary differential equations it is possible to see that if
\lambda
is
zero, the solutions
y_{1},y_{2}
would be straight lines, which is not an interesting
solution to the problem proposed. We look for solutions with
\lambda\neq 0
.
For simplicity we write
A_{1,\zeta}=\zeta_{1}\alpha_{1}+(1-\zeta_{1})\beta_{1}^{\prime}
and

A_{2,\zeta}=\zeta_{1}\alpha_{2}+(1-\zeta_{1})\beta_{2}
.
By integrating (
44
), one obtains

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle A_{1,\zeta}\frac{\dot{y}_{1}(t)}{\sqrt{1+\dot{y}_{1}^{2}(t)}}=K_{1}-\lambda t\\
\\
\displaystyle A_{2,\zeta}\frac{\dot{y}_{2}(t)}{\sqrt{1+\dot{y}_{2}^{2}(t)}}=K_{2}+\lambda t\end{array}\right.

where
K_{1},K_{2}
are constants, and this gives

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\dot{y}_{1}(t)=\frac{K_{1}-\lambda t}{\sqrt{A_{1,\zeta}^{2}-(K_{1}-\lambda t)^{2}}}\\
\\
\displaystyle\dot{y}_{2}(t)=\frac{K_{2}+\lambda t}{\sqrt{A_{2,\zeta}^{2}-(K_{2}+\lambda t)^{2}}}\end{array}\right.

A second integration, using the boundary condition in
-a
, gives

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle y_{1}(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\sqrt{A_{1,\zeta}^{2}-(K_{1}-\lambda t)^{2}}-\sqrt{A_{1,\zeta}^{2}-(K_{1}+\lambda a)^{2}}\right)\\
\\
\displaystyle y_{2}(t)=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\sqrt{A_{2,\zeta}^{2}-(K_{2}+\lambda t)^{2}}-\sqrt{A_{2,\zeta}^{2}-(K_{2}-\lambda a)^{2}}\right)\end{array}\right.

Using the boundary condition in
a
, we obtain
K_{1}=K_{2}=0
. So the
components of the solution
y_{\zeta}
are

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle y_{1}(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\sqrt{A_{1,\zeta}^{2}-\lambda^{2}t^{2}}-\sqrt{A_{1,\zeta}^{2}-\lambda^{2}a^{2}}\right)\\
\\
\displaystyle y_{2}(t)=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\sqrt{A_{2,\zeta}^{2}-\lambda^{2}t^{2}}-\sqrt{A_{2,\zeta}^{2}-\lambda^{2}a^{2}}\right)\end{array}\right.

(45)

See Figure
3
for an example of such a solution.

Figure 3:

45
) gives a building with this shape.

The constraint can be written

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{A_{1,\zeta}^{2}}{\lambda}\arctan\frac{\lambda a}{\sqrt{A_{1,\zeta}^{2}-\lambda^{2}a^{2}}}-a\sqrt{A_{1,\zeta}^{2}-\lambda^{2}a^{2}}\right.

(46)

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{A_{2,\zeta}^{2}}{\lambda}\arctan\frac{\lambda a}{\sqrt{A_{2,\zeta}^{2}-\lambda^{2}a^{2}}}-a\sqrt{A_{2,\zeta}^{2}-\lambda^{2}a^{2}}\right)=\frac{V}{h}\,.

Since the limit of the left-hand side of (
46
) for
\lambda
tending to
zero is zero, a solution
\lambda>0
exists for example if

and

\frac{a}{A_{1,\zeta}}\left(\frac{A_{1,\zeta}a\pi}{2}+\frac{A_{2,\zeta}^{2}a}{A_{1,\zeta}}\arctan\frac{A_{1,\zeta}}{\sqrt{A_{2,\zeta}^{2}-A_{1,\zeta}^{2}}}-a\sqrt{A_{2,\zeta}^{2}-A_{1,\zeta}^{2}}\right)\geq\frac{V}{h}\,.

Reasoning as in
[
17
, section 3.5]
, it is possible to conclude that the solution
(
45
) is the minimizer in the direction
\zeta
. In fact, the Lagrangian

L_{\zeta}(t,p_{1},p_{2})-\lambda G(y_{1},y_{2})

is convex in
(y_{1},y_{2},p_{1},p_{2})
and strictly convex in
(p_{1},p_{2})
for any

\lambda
. Then the solution (
45
) is the unique minimizer of class
C^{1}

of the functional

\int_{-a}^{a}L_{\zeta}(t,\dot{y}_{1},\dot{y}_{2})\,dt
on
\mathcal{X}_{3}
.

Equation (
43
) is in this case

S_{\left(-\frac{d}{dt}D_{p}L_{\zeta}(t,y,\dot{y})-\lambda\nabla G(y),\zeta\right)}(v)=H^{+}(\zeta)

and we can propose the set of all the arcs
y_{\zeta}
for
\zeta=(\zeta_{1},1-\zeta_{1})
,

\zeta_{1}\in[0,1]
, as a candidate infimizer.

7
Conclusions

A new approach to multi-criteria calculus of variations problems based on set
optimization methods is proposed. In particular, the problem is extended to a
problem with an objective function mapping into a complete lattice of sets. The
corresponding solution concept is twofold: in addition to more traditional minimal
solutions (also called efficient or non-dominated in multi-criteria/vector
optimization), infimizer sets are searched for. Thus, optimality conditions are
needed which characterize infimizers.

In this paper, conditions are presented which characterize infimizers consisting of arcs which in turn solve weighted sum scalarizations of the original problem and thus satisfy the known optimality conditions for scalarized problems. This works well in the convex case. The extension to the non-convex case is very desirable and could be addressed using nonlinear scalarizations of translative or oriented distance type (see for
example
[
19
]
,
[
5
]
and
[
11
]
).

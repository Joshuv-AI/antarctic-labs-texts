In the year

A finite matrix or a singly infinite matrix or a doubly infinite matrix is a Hankel matrix if its entries are constant along each skew diagonal. That is, the matrix

The notion of Hankel operators have been generalized by many mathematicians. Avendano

initiated the idea of Toeplitz and slant Toeplitz operators in the polydisk and discussed several properties in 2019. Recently, Datt and Pandey
[

discussed the Slant Toeplitz operators on the Lebesgue space of n-dimensional Torus. For adequate literature on Toeplitz, slant Toeplitz, and the concepts of polydisk, one is referred to

Definition 2.2
.

For an integer
k

\geq 2
, a slant Hankel operator
\mathcal{S}_{\phi}^{k,n}

k^{th}
order which is induced by
\phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})

is given by
\mathcal{S}_{\phi}^{k,n}=\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n}M_{\phi}
, where
M_{\phi}
is the Multiplication operator on

and
\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n}
is a linear operator defined on
{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
by

\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n}\;e_{m}=\begin{cases}e_{\frac{-m}{k}}&\mbox{if}\;k\mid m;\\
0&{\rm otherwise},\forall\;m\;\in\;\mathbb{Z}^{n}.\par\end{cases}

Let
\displaystyle h(z)=\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{m}z^{m}\in{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, then
\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n}\,h(z)=\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{km}z^{-m}
.
Further, the adjoint of
\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n}
is given by
(\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n})^{\ast}\;e_{m}=e_{-km}
for each
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
So,
(\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n})^{\ast}\,h(z)=\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}z^{-km}
. Then
\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n}\,(\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n})^{\ast}=I
and
(\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n})^{\ast}\,\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n}={P}_{e}
, where
{P}_{e}
is the projection on the closed span of
\{e_{km}\;\;\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\;m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}
in
{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Thus
\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n}
is the co-isometry on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and isometry on
{P}_{e}({L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
.
In fact,
\|\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n}\|=1
. Let
\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
be the algebra of all bounded linear operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and
\Omega\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\mapsto\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
be defined as

then

is linear and
1-1

Lemma 2.1
.

A bounded linear operator

on

is a
k^{th}
order slant Hankel operator iff

Lemma 2.2
.

M_{z_{j}}S=SM_{z_{j}^{-k}}
iff
M_{z^{m}}S=SM_{z^{-km}}\;\forall 1\leq j\leq n\;and\;m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
for a bounded linear operator

on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

Proof.

Let
M_{z^{m}}S=SM_{z^{-km}}\forall m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Taking
m=\epsilon_{j}\;{\rm for}1\leq j\leq n
implies
M_{z_{j}}S=SM_{z_{j}^{-k}}
. For the converse part consider
m=(m_{1},m_{2},\cdots,m_{n})
, then

M_{z^{m}}S=SM_{z_{1}^{m_{1}},\cdots,z_{n}^{m_{n}}}S=SM_{z^{-km}}

.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

A bounded linear operator

Theorem 2.1
.

If

Proof.

M_{z^{m}}(M_{\phi}\mathcal{S}_{\psi}^{k,n})=M_{\phi}M_{z^{m}}\mathcal{S}_{\psi}^{k,n}=(M_{\phi}\mathcal{S}_{\psi}^{k,n})M_{z^{-km}}\;\forall m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.
Therefore by Lemma
2.3

M_{\phi}\mathcal{S}_{\psi}^{k,n}
is a
k^{th}
order slant Hankel operator.
\mathcal{S}_{\psi}^{k,n}
is a
k^{th}
order slant Hankel operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
implies
M_{z^{m}}\mathcal{S}_{\psi}^{k,n}=\mathcal{S}_{\psi}^{k,n}M_{z^{-km}}\;\forall m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. This in turn implies
M_{\phi(z)}\mathcal{S}_{\psi}^{k,n}=\mathcal{S}_{\psi}{k,n}M_{\phi(z^{-k})}\;\forall\phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}).
Now,
M_{\phi}\mathcal{S}_{\psi}^{k,n}=\mathcal{S}_{\psi}^{k,n}M_{\phi(z^{-k})}=\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n}M_{\psi}M_{\phi(z^{-k})}=\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n}M_{\psi(z)\phi(z^{-k})}=\mathcal{S}_{\psi(z)\phi(z^{-k})}^{k,n}.

∎

Theorem 2.2
.

\mathcal{S}_{\phi}^{k,n}M_{\psi}=M_{\psi}\mathcal{S}_{\phi}^{k,n}
iff
\phi(z)\psi(z)=\phi(z)\psi(z^{-k})\;\forall\;z\in\mathbb{T}^{n}

\ \mathcal{S}_{\phi}^{k,n}M_{\psi}=M_{\psi}\mathcal{S}_{\phi}^{k,n}

Proof.

By Theorem

,

(1)

\mathcal{S}_{\phi}^{k,n}M_{\psi}=\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n}M_{\phi}M_{\psi}=\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n}M_{\phi\psi}=\mathcal{S}_{\phi\psi}^{k,n}{\rm and}\;M_{\psi}\mathcal{S}_{\phi}^{k,n}=\mathcal{S}_{\psi(z^{-k})\phi(z)}^{k,n}

Using equation (
1

If

For converse part, let

Lemma 2.4
.

If

We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\phi}^{k,n}\mathcal{S}_{\psi}^{k,n}-\mathcal{S}_{\psi}^{k,n}\mathcal{S}_{\phi}^{k,n}=

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n}\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n}\big(M_{\phi(z^{-k})\psi(z)}-M_{\psi(z^{-k})\phi(z)}

(13)

Theorem 3.8
.

(14)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n})^{2}(\mathcal{S}_{\phi}^{k,n}\mathcal{S}_{\psi}^{k,n})^{*}e_{m}(z)=

(15)

\displaystyle\;(\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n})^{2}M_{\overline{\psi}\overline{\phi}(z^{-k})}\big\{(\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n})^{*}\big\}^{2}z^{m}

\displaystyle\;(\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n})^{2}\big(\overline{\psi}\overline{\phi}(z^{-k})z^{k^{2}m}\big)

(17)

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n}\big\{z^{-km}\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n}\big(\overline{\psi}\overline{\phi}(z^{-k})\big)\big\}

(18)

\displaystyle\;(\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n})^{2}\big(\overline{\psi}\overline{\phi}(z^{-k})\big)z^{m}

(19)

\mathcal{S}_{\phi}^{k,n}\mathcal{S}_{\psi}^{k,n}

\mathcal{S}_{\phi}^{k,n}\mathcal{S}_{\psi}^{k,n}M_{z^{t}}

\displaystyle\;(\mathcal{V}_{k}^{n})^{2}M_{\overline{z}^{t}}(\mathcal{S}_{\psi}^{k,n})^{*}(\mathcal{S}_{\phi}^{k,n})^{*}

Theorem 3.11
.

k^{th}

n
,

Proof.

Let

(23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\phi}^{k,n}(\mathcal{S}_{\phi}^{k,n})^{*}e_{p}(z)=

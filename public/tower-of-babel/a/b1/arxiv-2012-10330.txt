On monophonic position sets in graphs Journal: Discrete Applied Mathematics
Elias John Thomas, Ullas Chandran S. V, James Tuite, Gabriele Di Stefano

Abstract
The general position problem in graph theory asks for the largest set S S of vertices of a graph G G such that no shortest path of G G contains more than two vertices of S S . In this paper we consider a variant of the general position problem called the monophonic position problem , obtained by replacing ‘shortest path’ by ‘induced path’. We prove some basic properties and bounds for the monophonic position number of a graph and determine the monophonic position number of some graph families, including unicyclic graphs, complements of bipartite graphs and split graphs. We show that the monophonic position number of triangle-free graphs is bounded above by the independence number. We present realisation results for the general position number, monophonic position number and monophonic hull number. Finally we discuss the complexity of the monophonic position problem.

In 1900 Dudeney, famous for his mathematical puzzles, posed the following question

[

[

,

,

,

,

Some interesting variants of the general position problem have been considered in the literature. In

[

2
Monophonic position sets in graphs

Recall that a set
S
of vertices in a graph
G
is a
general position set
if no shortest path in
G
contains more than two vertices of
S
; by convention a
gp-set
is a largest general position set of
G
. The number of vertices in a largest general position set of
G
is called the
general position number
of
G
and is denoted by
{\rm gp}(G)
. We have the following result for the general position number of graphs with diameter two.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
2
]
)
.

If
{\rm diam}(G)=2
, then
{\rm gp}(G)=\max\{\omega(G),\eta(G)\},
where
\eta(G)
is the maximum order of an induced complete multipartite subgraph of the complement of
G
.

We now introduce the following variant of the gp-number.

Definition 2.2
.

A set
M\subseteq V(G)
is a
monophonic position set
or
mp-set
of
G
if no three vertices of
M
lie on a common monophonic path in
G
. The
monophonic position number
or
mp-number

{\rm mp}(G)
of
G
is the number of vertices in a largest mp-set of
G
.

For an example of these concepts see Figure
1
. Observe that every monophonic position set
S
of a graph
G
is also in general position; it follows that
{\rm mp}(G)\leq{\rm gp}(G)
. Any pair of vertices is in monophonic position, so for graphs with order
n\geq 2
we have
2\leq{\rm mp}(G)\leq n
. It is easily seen that a connected graph satisfies
{\rm mp}(G)=n
if and only if
G\cong K_{n}
and the only connected graphs
G
with
{\rm mp}(G)=n-1
are a) the joins of
K_{1}
with a disjoint union of cliques and b) graphs formed from a clique by deleting between one and
n-2
edges incident to a given vertex. Also the mp-number of the cycle
C_{n}
is given by
{\rm mp}(C_{n})=2
for
n\geq 4
.

We begin by noting that for a wide class of graphs the monophonic position number coincides with the general position number. A graph
G
is
distance-hereditary
if for any connected induced subgraph
H
of
G
and vertices
u,v\in H
we have
d_{H}(u,v)=d_{G}(u,v)
, where
d_{G}(u,v)
is the distance between
u
and
v
in
G
and
d_{H}(u,v)
is the distance between
u
and
v
in the subgraph
H
. Distance-hereditary graphs have been characterised by Howorka
[
19
]
and many works in the literature are dedicated to them as well as to their generalisations or specialisations (see e.g.
[
7
,
9
,
10
,
20
]
). In a distance-hereditary graph a path is a geodesic if and only if it is induced; hence the definitions of general position and monophonic position coincide for such graphs.

Observation 2.3
.

For any distance-hereditary graph
G
we have
{\rm mp}(G)={\rm gp}(G)
.

Distance-hereditary graphs
G
are not characterised by the relation
{\rm mp}(G)={\rm gp}(G)
; for example, we have
{\rm mp}(C_{5}\odot K_{1})={\rm gp}(C_{5}\odot K_{1})=5
, but this graph is not distance-hereditary. Since the class of distance-hereditary graphs includes other well studied classes of graphs such as cographs, block graphs, trees and Ptolemaic graphs, results provided for these classes can be seen as a consequence of Observation
2.3
. Hence by Theorem
2.1
and the result of
[
27
]
we immediately have the following corollaries.

Corollary 2.4
.

For any block graph
G
with
{\rm s}(G)
simplicial vertices,
{\rm mp}(G)={\rm s}(G)
. In particular, for any tree
T
with
\ell(T)
leaves, we have
{\rm mp}(T)=\ell(T)
.

Corollary 2.5
.

For integers
r_{1}\geq r_{2}\geq\dots\geq r_{t}
the mp-number and gp-number of the complete multipartite graph
K_{r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{t}}
are given by

{\rm gp}(K_{r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{t}})={\rm mp}(K_{r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{t}})=\max\{r_{1},t\}.

We now provide some bounds on the monophonic position number of a graph. It was shown in
[
6
]
that the gp-number of a graph with diameter
D
is bounded above by
n-D+1
; there is an analogous upper bound for the mp-number of a graph in terms of the length of its longest induced path.

Proposition 2.6
.

If
G
is a graph with order
n
and the longest induced path in
G
has length
L
, then then
{\rm mp}(G)\leq n-L+1
. This bound is sharp.

Proof.

Let
P
be a longest monophonic path in
G
with length
L
and let
M
be a maximum mp-set. The path
P
can contain at most two vertices of
M
, so that at least
L-1
vertices of
G
do not lie in
M
. The bound is sharp for cliques and paths.
∎

In
[
27
]
the authors bound the
{\rm gp}
-number of a graph
G
using the
isometric-path number
, which is the smallest number of geodesics in
G
such that each vertex of
G
is contained in exactly one of the geodesics. There is a similar upper bound for the
{\rm mp}
-number in terms of the
induced path number

\rho(G)
, which is defined to be the smallest number of induced paths in
G
such that each vertex of
G
is contained in exactly one of the paths. This parameter was introduced in
[
8
]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

The mp-number of a graph
G
is bounded above by
{\rm mp}(G)\leq 2\rho(G)
.

Proof.

Consider a partition of
V(G)
into
\rho(G)
induced paths and let
M
be a largest
{\rm mp}
-set.
M
can intersect each path in the partition in at most two vertices, so that
|M|\leq 2\rho(G)
.
∎

It is shown in
[
1
]
that the induced path number of any connected cubic graph
G
with order
n\geq 7
is at most
\rho(G)\leq\frac{n-1}{3}
. Thus Lemma
2.7
has the following interesting corollary.

Corollary 2.8
.

For any connected cubic graph with order
n\geq 7
, the monophonic position number is at most
{\rm mp}(G)\leq\left\lfloor\frac{2(n-1)}{3}\right\rfloor
.

Corollary
2.8
is tight; for example the cube has order eight and
{\rm mp}
-number four. However, the authors conjecture that the coefficient
\frac{2}{3}
is not best possible asymptotically.

Conjecture 2.9
.

The the largest possible monophonic position number of a cubic graph with order
n
is
\frac{n}{3}+O(n)
.

We can also bound
{\rm mp}(G)
above in terms of the number of cut-vertices in
G
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
G
be any connected graph of order
n
and
c(G)
cut-vertices. Then
G
has a maximum monophonic position set that does not contain any cut-vertices. Thus
{\rm mp}(G)\leq n-c(G)
.

Proof.

Let
M
be a maximum mp-set of
G
that contains as few cut-vertices of
G
as possible. Suppose for a contradiction that
M
contains a cut-vertex
v
of
G
. Let
W_{1},W_{2},\dots,W_{k},

k\geq 2
, be the components of
G\setminus\{v\}
. Then
M
intersects at most one component, say
W_{1}
, or else there will be a monophonic path between vertices of
M
lying in different components that must pass through
v
. Let
W
be the subgraph of
G
induced by
W_{2}\cup\{v\}
and
P
be a longest path in
W
with initial vertex
v
. It is easily seen that the terminal vertex
u
of
P
is not a cut-vertex of
G
. The set
M^{\prime}=(M\setminus\{v\})\cup\{u\}
is also a maximum mp-set of
G
, but contains fewer cut-vertices of
G
than
M
, contradicting the definition of
M
.
∎

Lemma
2.10
is sharp for block graphs. We now give a lower bound for
{\rm mp}(G)
in terms of the number of simplicial vertices in
G
.

Lemma 2.11
.

For any graph
G
we have
{\rm mp}(G)\geq{\rm s}(G)
.

Proof.

Let
S
be the set of simplicial vertices of
G
. Suppose that
u,v,w\in S
and that
P
is a monophonic
u,v
-path containing
w
(in particular, this requires
{\rm s}(G)\geq 3
). The vertex
w
has two neighbours
w^{\prime}
and
w^{\prime\prime}
on
P
; however, by definition, we must have
w^{\prime}\sim w^{\prime\prime}
, so that
P
is not induced, a contradiction.
∎

Corollary
2.4
shows that the lower bound in Lemma
2.11
is also sharp for block graphs. Finally we present two results that show the effect on monophonic position sets of adding a leaf to a graph. Recall that for any set
M\subset V(G)
a vertex
u
of
G
is in the interior
M^{0}
of
M
if and only if there are vertices
x,y\in M\setminus\{u\}
such that
u
lies on a monophonic
x,y
-path.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
G^{\prime}
be a graph obtained from a graph
G
by adding a pendant edge
uv
at a vertex
v
of
G
. Then
{\rm mp}(G)\leq{\rm mp}(G^{\prime})\leq{\rm mp}(G)+1
. Moreover,
{\rm mp}(G^{\prime})={\rm mp}(G)+1
if and only if there exists a maximum mp-set
M
of
G
with
v\not\in M
such that
\big(M\cup\{v\}\big)^{0}=\{v\}
.

Proof.

Since every mp-set of
G
is also an mp-set of
G^{\prime}
, it follows that
{\rm mp}(G)\leq{\rm mp}(G^{\prime})
; conversely, if
M^{\prime}
is a maximum mp-set of
G^{\prime}
, then
M^{\prime}\setminus\{u\}
is an mp-set of
G
, so that
|M^{\prime}\setminus\{u\}|\leq{\rm mp}(G)
and hence
{\rm mp}(G^{\prime})=|M^{\prime}|\leq{\rm mp}(G)+1
.

Suppose that
{\rm mp}(G^{\prime})={\rm mp}(G)+1
with a largest mp-set
M^{\prime}
; then
{\rm mp}(G^{\prime})\geq 3
and
u\in M^{\prime}
. Since
|M^{\prime}|\geq 3
, it follows that
v\not\in M^{\prime}
, for otherwise
v
would lie on a monophonic path from
u
to another member of
M^{\prime}
in
G^{\prime}
. Let
M=(M^{\prime}\cup\{v\})\setminus\{u\}
.
M
is too large to be an mp-set of
G
, so there must exist three vertices
x,y,z
of
M
that lie on a common monophonic path in
G
. Since
M^{\prime}\setminus\{u\}=M\setminus\{v\}
is an mp-set of
G
, one of these vertices, say
x
, must be
v
. If
y\in K[v,z]
in
G
, then we would have
y\in K[u,z]
in
G^{\prime}
, contradicting the fact that
M^{\prime}
is an mp-set of
G^{\prime}
. Therefore
v\in K[y,z]
and so
v
is the only vertex in
M
such that
v\in M^{0}.
Thus
M\setminus\{v\}
is the required set.

Conversely, assume that there exists a maximum mp-set
M
of
G
with
v\not\in M
and
\big(M\cup\{v\}\big)^{0}=\{v\}
. We claim that
M^{\prime}=M\cup\{u\}
is an mp-set of
G^{\prime}
. Suppose that there exist
x,y,z\in M^{\prime}
such that
x\in K[y,z]
in
G^{\prime}
. As
u
is a leaf,
x\not=u
, so we can set
z=u
. Then
x\in K[y,v]
and hence
x\in\big(M\cup\{v\}\big)^{0},
which is impossible. Thus
M^{\prime}
is an mp-set in
G^{\prime}
with
|M^{\prime}|={\rm mp}(G)+1
.
∎

Proposition 2.13
.

If
G^{\prime}
is a graph obtained from
G
by adding a pendant vertex
u
to a simplicial vertex
v
in
G
, then
{\rm mp}(G)={\rm mp}(G^{\prime})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.12

{\rm mp}(G)\leq{\rm mp}(G^{\prime})\leq{\rm mp}(G)+1
and if
{\rm mp}(G^{\prime})={\rm mp}(G)+1
, then there exists an mp-set
M
of
G
with
|M|={\rm mp}(G)
such that
v\not\in M
and
\big(M\cup\{v\}\big)^{0}=\{v\}
. However, as a simplicial vertex
v
cannot be an interior vertex of any monophonic path in
G
, we have
{\rm mp}(G)={\rm mp}(G^{\prime})
.
∎

3
Monophonic position in graph families

In this section we discuss the monophonic position numbers of some common graph families. In Theorem
3.2
we give a sharp bound for the mp-numbers of triangle-free graphs. We then give exact expressions for the mp-numbers of unicyclic graphs and graphs formed as the join or corona product of two graphs.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a connected graph and
M\subseteq V(G)
be an mp-set. Then
G[M]
is a disjoint union of
k
cliques
G[M]=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}W_{i}
. If
k\geq 2
, then for
1\leq i\leq k
any two vertices of
W_{i}
have a common neighbour in
G\setminus M
.

Proof.

Let
W_{1},W_{2},\dots,W_{k}
be the components of
G[M]
. If some
W_{i}
is not a clique, then it would contain an induced path of length two, which is impossible; hence
M
is an independent union of cliques. Let
k\geq 2
and suppose for a contradiction that there is a component, say
W_{1}
, with
u,v\in V(W_{1})
such that
u
and
v
have no common neighbour in
G\setminus M
. Let
w
be any vertex in
W_{2}
and let
P
be a
u,w
-monophonic path
u,u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{\ell}=w
in
G
. Then
\ell\geq 2
. Since
M
is an mp-set, it follows that
P
together with the edge
uv
is not a monophonic path in
G
. This shows that
v
must be adjacent to
u_{j}
for some
j
with
1\leq j\leq\ell-1
. It follows from the choice of
u
and
v
that
j\geq 2
; let
j
be the largest suffix for which
v
is adjacent to
u_{j}
. Then the
u_{j},w
-subpath of
P
together with the 2-path
u_{j},v,u
forms a monophonic path containing three points of
M
; hence
u
and
v
must have a common neighbour in
G\setminus M
.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

The mp-number of a connected triangle-free graph
G
with order
n\geq 3
satisfies
{\rm mp}(G)\leq\alpha(G)
. Moreover, if the length of any monophonic path is at most three, then
{\rm mp}(G)=\alpha(G)
.

Proof.

Let
M
be a maximum mp-set in a triangle-free graph
G
. By Lemma
3.1

M
is an independent union of cliques
W_{1},W_{2},\dots,W_{k}
. If
k=1
, then
G[M]
is a clique and so
{\rm mp}(G)=|M|\leq\omega(G)=2\leq\alpha(G),
so assume that
k\geq 2.
If any component
W_{i}
of
G[M]
contains distinct vertices
u,v
, then by Lemma
3.1

u
and
v
have a common neighbour in
G\setminus M
, so that
G
would contain a triangle. It follows that each component of
G[M]
consists of a single vertex, so that
M
is an independent set of
G
and
{\rm mp}(G)\leq\alpha(G)
.

Let
T
be a maximum independent set of
G
. If three vertices of
T
lie on a common monophonic path
P
, then the length of
P
must be at least four. Therefore if the longest monophonic path of
G
has length at most three, then we have equality in the bound and any maximum independent set of
G
will be a maximum mp-set.
∎

The bound in Theorem
3.2
is sharp; it is met by the caterpillar formed by adjoining one leaf to every internal vertex of a path, complete bipartite graphs
K_{r,r}
and the corona product
C_{n}\odot K_{1}
for
n\geq 4
. Hence this bound can be achieved by graphs with arbitrarily large diameter, minimum degree and girth.

Theorem
3.2
can be used to find the monophonic position numbers of some graphs with large girth. This problem is particularly interesting for the cage graphs. The unique cubic cages with girths five, six and seven are the Petersen graph, the Heawood graph and the McGee graph respectively. We omit the lengthy case argument used to prove the following result, which was checked computationally by Erskine
[
16
]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

The monophonic position numbers of the Petersen graph, the Heawood graph and the McGee graph are three, three and two respectively.

This motivates the following conjecture.

Conjecture 3.4
.

For sufficiently large
g
, the monophonic position number of a
(d,g)
-cage

{\rm mp}(G)<d
.

We now determine the mp-numbers of unicyclic graphs; as we have seen, such graphs can meet the upper bound in Theorem
3.2
. We will identify the vertex set of the unique cycle
C
of a unicyclic graph
G
with
\mathbb{Z}_{s}
, where the length of the cycle is
s\geq 3
, where
i\sim i+1
for
0\leq i\leq s-1\pmod{s}
. We denote by
R
the set of vertices of
C
that have degree
\geq 3
. We will call any vertex in
R
a
root
and if
i\in R
we write
T_{i}
for the tree in
G\setminus\{i-1,i+1\}
that contains
i
. The leaf number of the tree
T_{i}
is
\ell(T_{i})
and we write
\ell^{\prime}(T_{i})
for the number of leaves of
T_{i}
that are also leaves of
G
. We have already dealt with the case that

is a cycle (
{\rm mp}(K_{3})=3
,
{\rm mp}(C_{s})=2
for

), so we assume that

.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
G
be a unicyclic graph that is not a cycle. Then

{\rm mp}(G)={\begin{cases}\ell(G)+2,&\text{ if }r=1,\\
\ell(G)+1,&\text{ if }r=2,R=\{i,j\}\text{ and either }T_{i}\text{ or }T_{j}\text{ is a path},\\
\ell(G)+1,&\text{ if }r=2\text{ and }R=\{i,i+1\}\text{ for some }0\leq i\leq s-1,\\
\ell(G),&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}}

Proof.

Let
M
be a maximum mp-set of
G
; by Lemma
2.10
we can assume that
M
contains no cut-vertices, so that
M\cap T_{i}
consists of leaves for any tree
T_{i}
(in particular
M\cap(C\setminus R)=\emptyset
). It follows from Corollary
2.4
and the mp-numbers of the cycles that
\ell(G)\leq{\rm mp}(G)\leq\ell(G)+2
. If
|R|=1
, then the leaves of the unique tree
T_{i}
and the vertices
\{i-1,i+1\}
form an mp-set, so that the upper bound is achieved, so we can take
|R|\geq 2
.

Suppose that
{\rm mp}(G)=\ell(G)+2
. Then
M
must contains
\ell^{\prime}(T_{i})
vertices in each tree
T_{i}
for
i\in R
, as well as two vertices of
C\setminus R
. As we are assuming that
|R|\geq 2
, this is not possible, for either there is a monophonic path from a vertex of
M
in a tree
T_{i}
through a section of

containing two vertices of

, or else a monophonic path from a vertex of
M
in a tree
T_{i}
to a vertex of
M
in a tree

,

, through a vertex of
M
on

. Thus for
|R|\geq 2
we have
{\rm mp}(G)\leq\ell(G)+1
.

Suppose that
{\rm mp}(G)=\ell(G)+1
. Then either
|M\cap(C\setminus R)|=1
and
|M\cap T_{i}|=\ell^{\prime}(T_{i})
for each
i\in R
, or else
|C\setminus R|=2
and there is one tree
T_{i}
with
|M\cap T_{i}|=\ell^{\prime}(T_{i})-1
. If
|M\cap(C\setminus R)|=2
, then there is a unique tree
T_{i}
that contains a vertex of
M
and the vertices of
M\cap(C\setminus R)
are
\{i-1,i+1\}
, so that, since no other tree contains a vertex of
M
, we must have
|R|=2
and the other tree
T_{j}
is a path, in which case equality holds in
{\rm mp}(G)=\ell(G)+1
. Hence assume that every tree
T_{i}
has
|M\cap T_{i}|=\ell^{\prime}(T_{i})
and
|M\cap(C\setminus R)=1
. If there are two trees
T_{i},T_{j}
such that
i\not\sim j
on
C
, then there would be a monophonic path from
M\cap T_{i}
to
M\cap T_{j}
through the vertex of
M
on

, so either
|R|=2
or
s=|R|=3
; in the latter case trivially
{\rm mp}(G)=\ell(G)
. If
R=\{i,i+1\}
, then the leaves of
T_{i}
and
T_{i+1}
together with the vertex

of

constitute an
{\rm mp}
-set of

, so that we have
{\rm mp}(G)=\ell(G)+1
in this case; otherwise
{\rm mp}(G)=\ell(G)
.
∎

In
[
17
]
the authors determined the gp-number of the join and corona product of graphs.

Theorem 3.6

(
[
17
]
)
.

For graphs

and

the general position number of the join

and the corona product

are given by

{\rm gp}(G\vee H)=\max\{\omega(G)+\omega(H),\alpha^{\omega}(G),\alpha^{\omega}(H)\}

and if

is connected and has order

, then

{\rm gp}(G\odot H)=n(G)\alpha^{\omega}(H).

We now determine the corresponding relation for the monophonic position numbers.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
G
be a connected graph with order
n(G)
and let
H
be any graph. Then
{\rm mp}(G\odot H)=n(G){\rm mp}(H)
.

Proof.

Let
V(G)=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
and let
H_{1},\dots,H_{n}
be the corresponding copies of
H
in
G\odot H
. Let
M
be a maximum mp-set of
G\odot H
. By Lemma
2.10
we can assume that
M
does not contain any cut-vertices of
G\odot H
. Observe that every vertex of
G
is a cut-vertex in

, so that we can take

to lie entirely in
H_{1}\cup H_{2}\cup\dots\cup H_{n}
. It is easily seen that each set
M\cap V(H_{i})
must be in monophonic position; it follows that
|M|\leq n(G){\rm mp}(H)
. Conversely, if
S
is a maximum mp-set of

and for

the corresponding set in

is
S_{i}
, then
S_{1}\cup S_{2}\cup\dots\cup S_{n}
is in monophonic position. Therefore
{\rm mp}(G\odot H)=n(G){\rm mp}(H)
.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

The monophonic position number of the join
G\vee H
of graphs
G
and
H
is related to the monophonic position numbers of
G
and
H
by

{\rm mp}(G\vee H)=\max\{\omega(G)+\omega(H),{\rm mp}(G),{\rm mp}(H)\}.

Proof.

It is evident that an mp-set in
G
is an mp-set in
G\vee H
, so that
{\rm mp}(G\vee H)\geq{\rm mp}(G)
and likewise
{\rm mp}(G\vee H)\geq{\rm mp}(H)
. Also the union of a clique in
G
and a clique in
H
is a clique in
G\vee H
and is hence an mp-set in
G\vee H
, so it follows that
{\rm mp}(G\vee H)\geq\max\{\omega(G)+\omega(H),{\rm mp}(G),{\rm mp}(H)\}
. For the opposite direction, let
M
be a maximum mp-set in
G\vee H
and set
M_{1}=M\cap V(G)
and
M_{2}=M\cap V(H)
. If
M_{1}=\emptyset
or
M_{2}=\emptyset
, then trivially
{\rm mp}(G\vee H)\leq\max\{{\rm mp}(G),{\rm mp}(H)\}
, so suppose that
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are both non-empty. Suppose that there exist distinct
x_{1},x_{2}\in M_{1}
such that
x_{1}\not\sim x_{2}
; in this case if
y\in M_{2}
, then
x_{1},y,x_{2}
would be a monophonic path in

, a contradiction. Therefore
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
must both induce cliques in

and
H
respectively, so that
{\rm mp}(G\vee H)\leq\omega(G)+\omega(H)
.
∎

4
Complements of bipartite graphs and split graphs

Cographs
are induced
P_{4}
-free graphs. As cographs are distance-hereditary, the mp- and gp-numbers of these graphs are equal by Observation
2.3
. It is therefore of interest to study the mp-numbers of induced
P_{5}
-free graphs; in this class equality between the
{\rm gp}
- and
{\rm mp}
-numbers does not hold in general, as shown by the cycle
C_{5}
, for which
{\rm mp}(C_{5})=2<3={\rm gp}(C_{5})
. We therefore consider two well-known classes of induced
P_{5}
-free graphs, namely the complement of connected bipartite graphs and split graphs. Since the complement of the complete bipartite graph
K_{m,n}
is disconnected, clearly
{\rm mp}(\overline{K}_{m,n})=m+n
.

Let
G
be a connected bipartite graph with bipartition
(A,B)
and let
S\subseteq V(G)
. Fix
S_{A}=S\cap A
and
S_{B}=S\cap B
. A set
X\subseteq A
(or
B
) is
uniform
in
G
if
N(u)=N(v)
for all
u,v\in X
; we call
S\subseteq V(G)
a
uniform set
if both
S_{A}
and
S_{B}
are uniform in
G
. Let
\psi(G)
denote the number of vertices in a largest uniform set in
G
.

Theorem 4.1
.

If
G
is a connected bipartite graph with bipartition
(A,B)
, then
{\rm mp}(\overline{G})=\max\{\alpha(G),\psi(G)\}.

Proof.

Let
S
be a maximum
{\rm mp}
-set in
\overline{G}
. Then both
S_{A}
and
S_{B}
are cliques in
\overline{G}
. If
S_{A}=\emptyset
or
S_{B}=\emptyset
, then it is clear that
|S|\leq\omega(\overline{G})=\alpha(G)
, so assume that
S_{A}\not=\emptyset
and
S_{B}\not=\emptyset
. If there is an edge between
S_{A}
and
S_{B}
in
\overline{G}
, then each vertex of
S_{A}
must be adjacent to all vertices of
S_{B}
, so that
S
induces a clique in
\overline{G}
and again
|S|\leq\alpha(G)
. Hence we can assume that there is no edge between
S_{A}
and
S_{B}
in
\overline{G}
; thus
S
induces a complete bipartite subgraph in
G
.

Next we claim that
S
is uniform. Assume to the contrary that there exist vertices
u,v\in S_{A}
and
w\in B
such that
w
is adjacent to
v
but not to
u
in
G
. Then
w\notin S_{B}
. Choose
x\in S_{B}
arbitrarily. Then the path
v,u,w,x
is a
v,x
-monophonic path in
\overline{G}
containing the vertex
u
, a contradiction to the fact that
S
is an mp-set of
\overline{G}
. Thus
S
must be uniform in
G
and so
|S|\leq\psi(G)
. Therefore we have
{\rm mp}(\overline{G})\leq\max\{\alpha(G),\psi(G)\}
.

On the other hand, it is clear that
{\rm mp}(\overline{G})\geq\omega(\overline{G})=\alpha(G)
. We now show that every maximum uniform set
S\subseteq V(G)
gives an
{\rm mp}
-set in
\overline{G}
. Both
S_{A}
and
S_{B}
are cliques in
\overline{G}
. If there is no edge between
S_{A}
and
S_{B}
in
G
, then
S
induces a clique in
\overline{G}
, so that
S
is an
{\rm mp}
-set and
{\rm mp}(\overline{G})\geq|S|=\psi(G)
. Hence we can assume that there is an edge between
S_{A}
and
S_{B}
in
G
; as
S
is uniform,
G
contains all edges between
S_{A}
and
S_{B}
, so that there are no edges between
S_{A}
and
S_{B}
in
\overline{G}
. Also by uniformity, there are sets
X\subseteq A\setminus S_{A}
and
Y\subseteq B\setminus S_{B}
such that
N(x)=Y
for each
x\in S_{A}
and
N(y)=X
for each
y\in S_{B}
; from this it is simple to see that
S
is in monophonic position in
\overline{G}
, so that
{\rm mp}(\overline{G})\geq|S|=\psi(G)
, concluding the proof.
∎

Let
T
be a tree with order
n\geq 2
and suppose that
S
is a uniform set of
T
with
|S|>\alpha(T)
. Suppose that
|S_{A}|\geq 2
; then each vertex in
S_{A}
is a leaf, for if
d(u)\geq 2
for some
u\in S_{A}
, then for any
v\in S_{A}\setminus\{u\}
the vertices
u
and
v
would have at least two common neighbours, which is impossible. As the same reasoning applies to
S_{B}
and
\alpha(T)\geq\ell(T)
, we conclude that
S_{A}
is a set of leaves and
|S_{B}|=1
, say
S_{B}=\{w\}
. If
S_{A}\cup S_{B}=V(T)
, then
T
is a star,
V(T)
is a uniform set and
{\rm mp}(T)=n(T)
; otherwise, considering the endpoint of a longest path to a vertex of
V(T)\setminus S
, we see that
T
contains a leaf that does not belong to

, so that after all

. This proves the following corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

If
T
is a tree with order

, then

{\rm mp}(\overline{T})={\begin{cases}n,&\text{ if }T\text{ is a star},\\
\alpha(T),&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}}

A similar argument yields the following results.

Corollary 4.3
.

If
n,m\geq 2,
then

{\rm mp}(\overline{P_{n}\Box P_{m}})={\begin{cases}4,&\text{ if }n=m=2,\\
\alpha({P_{n}\Box P_{m}})=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil\lceil\frac{m}{2}\rceil+\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\rfloor,&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}}

Corollary 4.4
.

If
k\geq 3
, then
{\rm mp}(\overline{Q_{k}})=2^{k-1}
, where
Q_{k}
is the hypercube with order
2^{k}
.

A graph is a
split graph
if the vertex set can be partitioned into a clique
C
and an independent set
I
. If there is a vertex
v
that is adjacent to every vertex of
C\setminus\{v\}
and non-adjacent with every vertex of
I\setminus\{v\}
we will say that
v
is
divided
. Obviously there cannot be divided vertices in both
C
and
I
. If there are no divided vertices, then
\omega(G)=|C|
and
\alpha(G)=|I|
. If
G
contains a divided vertex
v
, then by moving
v
from
C
into
I
if necessary, we can assume that any divided vertices lie in
I
and
\omega(G)=|C|+1
and
\alpha(G)=|I|
. We define a
separated subgraph

(C^{\prime},I^{\prime})
of the split graph
G=(C,I)
as follows.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
C^{\prime}\subseteq C
and
I^{\prime}\subseteq I
. Then
G^{\prime}=(C^{\prime},I^{\prime})
is a
separated subgraph
of the split graph
G=(C,I)
if and only if either

1.

(
Type A
)
there is no edge from
C^{\prime}
to
I^{\prime}
in
G
, or

2.

(
Type B
)
there is a vertex
v\in I^{\prime}
that is adjacent to every vertex of
C^{\prime}
, there are no edges from
I^{\prime}\setminus\{v\}
to
C^{\prime}
and for any
w\in I^{\prime}\setminus\{v\}
we have
N(w)\subset N(v)
.

Let
\phi(G)
denote the order of a largest separated subgraph of
G
.

Trivially the clique induced by
C
is separated, as is the independent set induced by
I
. If there is a divided vertex
v
in
I
, then
(C,\{v\})
is separated. This shows that we always have
\phi(G)\geq\max\{\omega(G),\alpha(G)\}
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
G=(C,I)
be a connected split graph. Then
{\rm mp}(G)=\phi(G)
.

Proof.

Let
(C^{\prime},I^{\prime})
be a separated subgraph of
G
. Suppose for a contradiction that
P
is a monophonic path with endpoints
w_{1},w_{3}\in C^{\prime}\cup I^{\prime}
that passes through a vertex
w_{2}\in(C^{\prime}\cup I^{\prime})\setminus\{w_{1},w_{3}\}
. We cannot have
w_{1},w_{3}\in C^{\prime}
, as
w_{1}\sim w_{3}
. Also
w_{2}
cannot lie in
I^{\prime}
, as the neighbours of
w_{2}
on
P
would both be in
C
and so would be adjacent. Suppose that
w_{1},w_{3}\in I^{\prime},w_{2}\in C^{\prime}
. If
(C^{\prime},I^{\prime})
is a separated subgraph of Type A, then the monophonic path
P
would have to include at least three vertices from
C
, which is impossible. Hence
(C^{\prime},I^{\prime})
is a separated subgraph of Type B. Now, if
w_{1}=v
, then
P
cannot be monophonic, as we would have
N(w_{3})\subset N(w_{1})
, but
w_{3}\not\sim w_{2}
. Hence
w_{1},w_{2}\in I^{\prime}\setminus\{v\}
; however, in this case
P
would contain at least three vertices from
C
, which is again impossible. Finally suppose that
w_{1},w_{2}\in C^{\prime},w_{3}\in I^{\prime}
. Since
P
has exactly two vertices from
C
, it follows that
(C^{\prime},I^{\prime})
is Type
B
and
w_{3}=v
; this is a contradiction, as we would have
v\sim w_{1}
. Hence we conclude that any separated subgraph is in monophonic position.

Conversely, we now show that any subgraph
(C^{\prime},I^{\prime})
that is in monophonic position must be separated. If
(C^{\prime},I^{\prime})
is not Type A, then there is a vertex
v\in I^{\prime}
with an edge to a vertex
u\in C^{\prime}
. Suppose that there is a vertex
u^{\prime}\in C^{\prime}\setminus N(v)
; then
v,u,u^{\prime}
would be a monophonic path in
(C^{\prime},I^{\prime})
, a contradiction. Hence
v
is adjacent to every vertex of
C^{\prime}
. If there is a vertex
v^{\prime}\in I^{\prime}\setminus\{v\}
that is adjacent to a vertex
x
in
C^{\prime}
, then
v,x,v^{\prime}
would be an induced path in
(C^{\prime},I^{\prime})
, so there are no edges from
C^{\prime}
to
I^{\prime}\setminus\{v\}
. Finally suppose that there is an edge
u^{\prime}\sim v^{\prime}
, where
u^{\prime}\in C\setminus C^{\prime}
,
v^{\prime}\in I^{\prime}\setminus\{v\}
, such that
v\not\sim u^{\prime}
; in this case

would be an induced path containing three vertices of
(C^{\prime},I^{\prime})
; we conclude that
N(v^{\prime})\subset N(v)
for any
v^{\prime}\in I^{\prime}\setminus\{v\}
. Hence
(C^{\prime},I^{\prime})
is a separated subgraph.
∎

We noted before that for any split graph

we have
{\rm mp}(G)=\phi(G)\geq\max\{\omega(G),\alpha(G)\}
. We now characterise the cases in which equality holds. For
X\subseteq V(C)
write
N_{I}(X)=I\cap(\bigcup_{x\in X}N(x))
. By a matching between
C
and
I
in

, we will mean a matching such that no edge of the matching has both endpoints in
C
.

Theorem 4.7
.

If
G=(C,I)
is a connected split graph, then if there are no divided vertices in

we have
{\rm mp}(G)=\phi(G)=\max\{\omega(G),\alpha(G)\}
if and only if there exists a matching in
G
between
C
and
I
that saturates either
C
or
I
. The same conclusion holds if
G
has a divided vertex, unless
G
is formed from a clique
A
of order

and an independent set
B
of order
>r
by adding a set
E^{\prime}
of edges between
A^{\prime}
and
I
, where
A^{\prime}\subset A
,
|A^{\prime}|=r
, such that
E^{\prime}
contains a matching saturating

, in which case
{\rm mp}(G)=\alpha(G)+1>\omega(G)
.

Proof.

First suppose that there is no matching between
C
and
I
that saturates
C
or
I
; we will show that
\phi(G)>\max\{\omega(G),\alpha(G)\}
. By Hall’s Theorem
[

]
, as there is no matching between

and
I
that saturates
C
, there is a subset

such that
|N_{I}(K)|<|K|
. Then
(K,I\setminus N_{I}(K))
is a separated subgraph with order
>|I|=\alpha(G)
. Similarly, considering matchings from

, we see that there is subset

such that
|N(J)|<J
, implying that
(C\setminus N(J),J)
is a separated subgraph with order
|(C\setminus N(J),J)|>|C|
. If

has no divided vertices, then

and we are done, so suppose that

is a divided vertex in

. If

, then

, so we have

and hence

. However, the separated set
(K,I\setminus N_{I}(K))

>\alpha(G)
and hence suffices. Otherwise, we can move

from

to

to obtain a separated subset of Type B with order

.

Now suppose that there is a matching

between

and

that saturates

or

. If

are non-empty. If

.

Thus assume that

Now suppose that

. But now it follows that each vertex of

and

is divided. Hence if there are no divided vertices we have equality in the lower bound.

Let

be any bipartite graph with bipartition
(A,B)
such that a)
|B|\geq|A|+1
and b) there is a matching in

that saturates

as follows: add two new vertices

and add every edge between vertices in

. The original matching from

to

in

gives the required matching. Also the set

is a largest mp-set, so that

; in fact the previous discussion shows that a split graph

has
{\rm mp}(G)=\alpha(G)+1>\omega(G)
if and only if it belongs to this family.
∎

5
Realisation results

In this paper we have presented some properties of the monophonic position number of graphs. In some respects this graph parameter behaves like the more widely studied general position number. It is therefore of interest to ask whether there is a relation between the two numbers other than the trivial inequality

For given

6
Computational complexity

In this section, we study the computational complexity of the problem of finding the monophonic position number of a general graph. To this end, we formally define the decision version of the problem:

Definition 6.1
.

Monophonic position set

Instance
: A graph
G
, a positive integer
k\leq|V(G)|
.

Question
: Is there a monophonic position set
S
for
G
such that
|S|\geq k
?

The problem is hard to solve as shown by the next theorem.

Theorem 6.2
.

The
Monophonic position set
problem is NP-hard.

Proof.

We prove that the
Clique
problem polynomially reduces to
Monophonic position set
.
An instance of
Clique
is given by a graph
G
and a positive integer
k\leq|V|
.
The
Clique
problem asks whether
G
contains a clique
K\subseteq V(G)
of order
k
or more.

The NP-completeness of
Clique
is well known, as it is one of the original list of 21 NP-complete problems presented in
[
22
]
. We polynomially transform an instance
(G,k)
of
Clique
to an instance
(G^{\prime},k^{\prime})
of
Monophonic position set
so that
G
has a clique of order
k
or more if and only if
G^{\prime}
has a monophonic position set of order
k^{\prime}
or more.

Given an instance
(G,k)
, the graph
G^{\prime}
is built as follows:

\displaystyle V(G^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\{v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime}~|~v\in V(G)\}

\displaystyle E(G^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\{u^{\prime}v^{\prime}~|~uv\in E(G)\}\cup\{u^{\prime}v^{\prime\prime}~|~u,v\in V(G)\}\cup\{u^{\prime\prime}v^{\prime\prime}~|~u,v\in V(G)\}

In words,
G^{\prime}
contains a subgraph
H
isomorphic to
G
and a clique graph
H^{\prime}
of order
n=|V(G)|
such that
G^{\prime}=H\vee H^{\prime}
. As for
k^{\prime}
, we set
k^{\prime}=n+k
.

As a preliminary results, note that
\omega(G^{\prime})=\omega(G)+n
. Indeed, if
S
is any clique in
G
, then
S^{\prime}=\{s^{\prime}~|~s\in S\}
is a clique in
H
, and
S^{\prime}\cup V(H^{\prime})
is a clique in
G^{\prime}
, since any vertex in
H^{\prime}
is a universal vertex of
G^{\prime}
. Then
\omega(G^{\prime})\geq\omega(G)+n
.
On the contrary, if
S
is any clique of
G^{\prime}
, then
|S\cap V(H)|\leq\omega(G)
and
|S\cap V(H^{\prime})|\leq n
. Hence
\omega(G^{\prime})\leq\omega(G)+n
.

By Proposition
3.8
,

{\rm mp}(G^{\prime})={\rm mp}(H\vee H^{\prime})=\max\{\omega(H)+\omega(H^{\prime}),{\rm mp}(H),{\rm mp}(H^{\prime})\}
. We have that
\omega(H^{\prime})={\rm mp}(H^{\prime})=n
for
H^{\prime}
is a clique;
{\rm mp}(H)={\rm mp}(G)
and
\omega(H)=\omega(G)\leq n
for
H
is isomorphic to
G
. Then
{\rm mp}(G^{\prime})=\max\{\omega(H)+n,{\rm mp}(H),n\}=\omega(H)+n
, being
{\rm mp}(H)\leq n
.

Now assume that an instance
(G,k)
of
Clique
has a positive answer, then
\omega(G)=\omega(H)\geq k
. Then
{\rm mp}(G^{\prime})=\omega(H)+n\geq k+n=k^{\prime}
, and hence
Monophonic position set
has a positive answer. On the other hand, if the instance
(G,k)
of
Clique
has a negative answer then
\omega(G)=\omega(H)<k
, which in turn implies that
Monophonic position set
has a negative answer, since
{\rm mp}(G^{\prime})=\omega(H)+n<k+n
.
∎

By the proof of Theorem
6.2
, it is not clear if
Monophonic position set
is NP-complete. However if we restrict the problem to instances
(G,k)
with
k>|V(G)|/2
and
G=H\vee K
, where
H
is a generic graph and
K
is a clique graph having the same order of
H
, the problem is NP-complete. Indeed, given a solution, it can be tested in polynomial time if it is a clique and if its order is larger than
k
.

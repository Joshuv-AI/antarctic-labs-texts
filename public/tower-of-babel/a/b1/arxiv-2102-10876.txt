Abstract
. For a finite group G G and an inverse-closed generating set C C of G G , let Aut ⁡ ( G , C ) {\rm Aut}(G;C) consist of those automorphisms of G G which leave C C invariant. We define an Aut ⁡ ( G , C ) {\rm Aut}(G;C) -invariant normal subgroup Φ ⁡ ( G , C ) \Phi(G;C) of G G which has the property that, for any Aut ⁡ ( G , C ) {\rm Aut}(G;C) -invariant normal set of generators for G G , if we remove from it all the elements of Φ ⁡ ( G , C ) \Phi(G;C) , then the remaining set is still an Aut ⁡ ( G , C ) {\rm Aut}(G;C) -invariant normal generating set for G G . The subgroup Φ ⁡ ( G , C ) \Phi(G;C) contains the Frattini subgroup Φ ⁡ ( G ) \Phi(G) but the inclusion may be proper. The Cayley graph Cay ⁡ ( G , C ) {\rm Cay}(G,C) is normal edge-transitive if Aut ⁡ ( G , C ) {\rm Aut}(G;C) acts transitively on the pairs { c , c − 1 } \{c,c^{-1}\} from C C . We show that, for a normal edge-transitive Cayley graph Cay ⁡ ( G , C ) {\rm Cay}(G,C) , its quotient modulo Φ ⁡ ( G , C ) \Phi(G;C) is the unique largest normal quotient which is isomorphic to a subdirect product of normal edge-transitive graphs of characteristically simple groups. In particular, we may therefore view normal edge-transitive Cayley graphs of characteristically simple groups as building blocks for normal edge-transitive Cayley graphs whenever we have Φ ⁡ ( G , C ) \Phi(G;C) trivial. We explore several questions which these results raise, some concerned with the set of all inverse-closed generating sets for groups in a given family. In particular we use this theory to classify all 4 4 -valent normal edge-transitive Cayley graphs for dihedral groups; this involves a new construction of an infinite family of examples, and disproves a conjecture of Talebi.

1.
Introduction and the main results

Let
G
be a nontrivial finite group (that is,
|G|>1
), and let
{\rm Aut}(G)
denote its automorphism group. A non-empty subset
C
of
G\setminus\{1\}
is said to be
inverse-closed
if for each
c\in C
, its inverse
c^{-1}
also lies in
C
.
We will be concerned with inverse-closed generating sets
C
for
G
, and for such a set we let
{\rm Aut}(G;C)
denote the subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(G)
consisting of those automorphisms

\sigma
which leave
C
invariant, that is to say,
C^{\sigma}=C
. A subgroup
N\leq G
is
{\rm Aut}(G;C)
-invariant if
N^{\sigma}=N
for each
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(G;C)
. We will study

(1)

\mathcal{A}(G;C)=\{N\mid N\unlhd G,\ N\neq G,\ \mbox{and $N$ is ${\rm Aut}(G;C)$-invariant}\}

and in particular its subset of maximal elements

(2)

\mathcal{A}_{\max}(G;C)=\{N\in\mathcal{A}(G;C)\mid\ \mbox{$N$ is maximal by inclusion}\}.

Since
|G|>1
, the identity subgroup lies in
\mathcal{A}(G;C)
, and it follows that
\mathcal{A}_{\max}(G;C)
is non-empty. Thus the following subgroup

(3)

\Phi(G;C)=\bigcap\{N\mid N\in\mathcal{A}_{\max}(G;C)\}

is a well defined element of
\mathcal{A}(G;C)
. We may have
\Phi(G;C)=1
; this is the case, for example, if
G
is simple so that
\mathcal{A}_{\max}(G;C)=\mathcal{A}(G;C)=\{\{1\}\}
.
The definition of
\Phi(G;C)
as the intersection of a certain family of subgroups of
G
is reminiscent of the definition of the Frattini subgroup
\Phi(G)
as the intersection of all maximal (proper) subgroups of
G
. Indeed
\Phi(G;C)
contains
\Phi(G)

(see Lemma
2.1
), but the inclusion can be proper (see Examples
2.3
and
2.4
).

An important property of the Frattini subgroup
\Phi(G)
is that, if
X
is a generating set for
G
and
X
contains an element
y\in\Phi(G)
, then
X\setminus\{y\}
still generates
G
(see, for example,
[
7
, Satz III.3.2]
). It turns out that
\Phi(G;C)
has a similar property. We say that a subset
X
of
G
is
normal
C
-closed
if for each
x\in X,g\in G,\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(G;C)
, the image
(x^{g})^{\sigma}
also lies in
X
. Clearly an arbitrary subset
X\subseteq G
may be enlarged to a normal
C
-closed subset by adding to it each of the elements
(x^{g})^{\sigma}
; the set thus obtained is called the
normal
C
-closure
of
X
. We show that from each normal
C
-closed generating set we may remove all elements of
\Phi(G;C)
(if any) and maintain these properties.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
C
a generating set for
G
.

(a)

If
X
is a normal
C
-closed generating set for
G
, then
X\setminus(X\cap\Phi(G;C))
is also a normal
C
-closed generating set.

(b)

The subgroup
\Phi(G;C)
is the set of all elements
y\in G
which satisfy the following property:

for
X\subseteq G
, if the normal
C
-closure of
X\cup\{y\}
generates
G
,

then the normal
C
-closure of
X
also generates
G
.

We will show that this Frattini-like subgroup
\Phi(G;C)
plays a key role in describing the structure of normal edge-transitive Cayley graphs.

We briefly summarise the key concepts:

for a group
G
, and a non-empty, inverse-closed subset
C\subseteq G\setminus\{1\}
, the
(undirected)

Cayley graph
{\rm Cay}(G;C)

of
G
with respect to
C
is the graph with
vertex set
G
and edge set
E(G;C)
consisting of all
pairs
\{g,h\}
such that
gh^{-1}\in C
(or equivalently
hg^{-1}\in C
since
C
is inverse-closed). The graph
\Gamma:={\rm Cay}(G;C)
is connected if and only if the
connection set

C
generates
G
. For each

g\in G
, the right multiplication map
\rho_{g}:h\mapsto hg
is an automorphism of

\Gamma
, and the group
G_{R}:=\{\rho_{g}\mid g\in G\}
(called
the right regular representation of
G
) is a subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
acting regularly on vertices (that is,
G_{R}
is transitive with trivial stabilisers). In addition
{\rm Aut}(G;C)
is a subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
, and the semidirect product
N(G;C):=G_{R}\rtimes{\rm Aut}(G;C)
is the full normaliser of
G_{R}
in
{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
(see
[
6
]
). As in

[
11
]
,
\Gamma
is said to be
normal edge-transitive
if
N(G;C)

is transitive on the edge-set
E(G;C)
. In
[
11
, Theorem 3]
it was shown that, for each
N\in\mathcal{A}(G;C)
, the normal quotient, modulo
N
, of a connected normal edge-transitive Cayley graph
{\rm Cay}(G;C)
is again a connected normal edge-transitive Cayley graph, namely

{\rm Cay}(G/N;CN/N)
. In particular if
N\in\mathcal{A}_{\max}(G;C)
, then the group
G/N
is characteristically simple, and we refer to
{\rm Cay}(G/N;CN/N)
as a
characteristically simple normal quotient
of
{\rm Cay}(G;C)
.

We denote the set of connected normal edge-transitive Cayley graphs for finite characteristically simple groups by
{\sf NormCay{CS}}
.
A (still open) question from
[
11
, Question 2]
asks
under what conditions
{\rm Cay}(G;C)
is determined by its proper normal quotients (that is, quotients modulo subgroups
N\in\mathcal{A}_{\max}(G;C)
with
N\neq 1
). Our results suggest that, provided
\Phi(G;C)=1
,
{\rm Cay}(G;C)
is determined to a large extent by its normal quotients lying in
{\sf NormCay{CS}}
.
Our main result relies on certain graph constructions which we define in Subsection
3.1
, namely the direct product
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}
of a family of graphs
\Gamma_{i}
, and a full subdirect product of a graph direct product. A different graph theoretical construction was used recently in
[
8
]
to study normal edge-transitive Cayley graphs
{\rm Cay}(G;C)
of groups
G
admitting a direct decomposition
G=M\times N
with both
M,N\in\mathcal{A}(G;C)
, but the analysis in
[
8
]
does not apply in our general situation.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a nontrivial finite group with inverse-closed generating set
C
such that
\Gamma={\rm Cay}(G,C)
is normal edge-transitive, and let
\Phi:=\Phi(G;C)
(as in
(
3
)
). Then
there exist
k\geq 1
and
N_{1},\dots,N_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{\max}(G;C)
for which the following conditions hold, where
\Gamma_{i}:={\rm Cay}(G/N_{i};CN_{i}/N_{i})
.

(i)

\Phi=\cap_{i=1}^{k}N_{i}
, and the map
\zeta:g\Phi\rightarrow(gN_{1},\cdots,gN_{k})
is an isomorphism
G/\Phi\rightarrow\prod_{i=1}^{k}G/N_{i}
;

(ii)

for each
i\leq k
,
\Gamma_{i}\in{\sf NormCay{CS}}
,
\mathcal{A}(G/N_{i};CN_{i}/N_{i})=\{1\}
, and
\zeta
induces a graph isomorphism from
{\rm Cay}(G/\Phi;C\Phi/\Phi)
onto a full subdirect product of
\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma_{i}
.

Remark 1.3
.

Theorem
1.2
constructs a normal quotient
{\rm Cay}(G/\Phi(G;C);C\Phi(G;C)/\Phi(G;C))
of a given normal edge-transitive Cayley graph

{\sf NormCay{CS}}
. If
\Phi(G;C)=1
this gives a structural description of the original graph

.

Theorem
1.2
follows fairly easily from a more general result Theorem

which considers general normal quotients in

.
We prove Theorem
1.1
in Section

, and Theorem
1.2
in Section

.

2.
The Frattini-like subgroup
\Phi(G;C)

We first prove the assertion that
\Phi(G;C)
contains the Frattini subgroup.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a nontrivial finite group with an inverse-closed generating set
C\subseteq G\setminus\{1\}
. Let
\Phi(G;C)
be as in
(
3
)
, and let
\Phi(G)
be the Frattini subgroup. Then

(a)

\Phi(G)\leq\Phi(G;C)
;

(b)

for
N\in\mathcal{A}_{\max}(G;C)
and
N\leq M<G
with
M
maximal in
G
, we have

N=\cap_{g\in G,\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(G;C)}(M^{g})^{\sigma}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be the set of maximal proper subgroups of
G
, so by definition,
\Phi(G)=\cap_{M\in\mathcal{M}}M
. For each
M\in\mathcal{M}
, and each
g\in G,\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(G;C)
, the image
(M^{g})^{\sigma}
is also a subgroup in
\mathcal{M}
, and so
\Phi(G)\leq N_{M}
,
where
N_{M}:=\cap_{g\in G,\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(G;C)}(M^{g})^{\sigma}
. Note that, by definition,
N_{M}\in\mathcal{A}(G;C)
.

Now let
N\in\mathcal{A}_{\max}(G;C)
, and let
M\in\mathcal{M}
such that
N\leq M
(note that such an
M
exists since
G
is finite). Then
N\leq N_{M}
since
N
is normal in
G
and
{\rm Aut}(G;C)
-invariant. By the maximality of
N
, it now follows that
N=N_{M}
, proving part (b).
Since this holds for all
N\in\mathcal{A}_{\max}(G;C)
, it follows that
\Phi(G)\leq\cap_{N\in\mathcal{A}_{\max}(G;C)}N=\Phi(G;C)
, proving part (a).
∎

Sometimes equality holds in Lemma
2.1
(a), for example when
G
is a cyclic group (Example
2.2
). It is also easy to construct examples for which equality does not hold, for example, when
G
is a dihedral group (Example
2.3
), or is almost simple but not simple (Example
2.4
). Example
2.3
shows how
\Phi(G;C)
can vary for different inverse-closed generating sets
C
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
G=\langle a\mid a^{n}=1\rangle\cong Z_{n}
with
n\geq 2
. Each subgroup of
G
is normal and
{\rm Aut}(G)
-invariant. In particular the maximal subgroups of
G
are the cyclic subgroups
M_{p}=\langle a^{p}\rangle\cong Z_{n/p}
, for primes
p
dividing
n
. These are also the maximal normal
{\rm Aut}(G;C)
-invariant subgroups for each inverse-closed generating set
C
of
G
. Thus
the Frattini subgroup (which is the intersection of all the maximal subgroups of
G
), and also
\Phi(G;C)
, are equal to

\Phi(G)=\Phi(G;C)=\langle a^{m}\rangle,\ \mbox{where $m$ is the product of the distinct prime divisors of $n$.}

In particular,
\Phi(G)=\Phi(G;C)=1
if and only if
n
is square-free.

The next example is for the dihedral group

(4)

G=\langle a,b\mid a^{n}=b^{2}=1,bab=a^{-1}\rangle\cong D_{2n}

of order
2n
. This group
G
admits automorphisms
\varphi,\tau_{k}
, for positive integers
k<n
such that
\gcd(k,n)=1
, defined on the generators

as follows:

(5)

\varphi:a\to a,\ b\to ba;\quad\tau_{k}:a\to a^{k},\ b\to b,

and we have

(6)

{\rm Aut}(G)=A\rtimes T,\ \mbox{where}\ A=\langle\varphi\rangle\cong Z_{n},\ \mbox{and}\ T=\{\tau_{k}\mid 1\leq k\leq n-1,\ \gcd(k,n)=1\}\cong{\rm Aut}(Z_{n}).

Example 2.3
.

Let
G\cong D_{2n}
, as in (
4
), with

. If
n
is odd then the
n
involutions in
G
form a single
G
-conjugacy class. On the other hand, if
n
is even then the
n
non-central involutions form two
G
-conjugacy classes of size

, with representatives
b,ba
, and
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)

The maximal subgroups of

. Secondly, for each prime

M_{p}:=\langle a^{p},b\rangle\cong D_{2n/p}\ \mbox{and also, if $n$ is even and $p=2$,}\ M_{2}^{\prime}:=\langle a^{2},ba\rangle\cong D_{n}.

If

, and

\Phi(G)=\langle a^{m}\rangle,\ \mbox{where $m$ is the product of the distinct prime divisors of $n$.}

For example,
\Phi(G)=1
if and only if

is square-free.
Now we consider
\Phi(G;C)
for two particular inverse-closed generating sets

, namely

In each case, to determine

, and then we find all subgroups

{\rm Aut}(G;C)
-invariant. It follows from (

{\rm Aut}(G;C_{1})=\langle\tau_{n-1}\rangle\cong Z_{2}
,

.
Let

, or one if the
M_{p}
or

Example 2.4
.

Let
G
be an almost simple group with unique minimal normal subgroup

, a nonabelian simple group. If

, then the only proper normal subgroup of

\Phi(G)\leq\cap_{g\in G}M^{g}=1
, that is,
\Phi(G)=1
. On the other hand, each maximal proper normal subgroup

, and for any generating set

{\rm Aut}(G;C)
-invariant. Thus

.

-closed, and hence also

is normal

-closed. This implies that
K:=\langle Y\rangle
is a normal
{\rm Aut}(G;C)
-invariant subgroup of
G
. Suppose that
K\neq G
. Then
K\in\mathcal{A}(G;C)
, and there exists
N\in\mathcal{A}_{\max}(G;C)
such that
K\leq N
. By definition
Y\subseteq K\subseteq N
, and also
X\cap\Phi(G;C)\subseteq\Phi(G;C)\subseteq N
by (
3
). Thus
X\subseteq N
, which is a contradiction since
X
generates
G
. Thus
K=G
, and hence
Y
is a normal
C
-closed generating set.

(b) Let
S
be the subset consisting of all elements
y
of
G
which satisfy the condition in Theorem
1.1
(b). Then by part (a),
\Phi(G;C)\subseteq S
. Now let
y\in S
and let

N\in\mathcal{A}_{\max}(G;C)
. We claim that
y\in N
. Suppose to the contrary that
y\not\in N
.
Let
\widehat{N}=N\cup\{(y^{g})^{\sigma}\mid\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(G;C),g\in G\}
(the normal

-closure of
N\cup\{y\}
), and let
M=\langle\widehat{N}\rangle
. Since

is normal in

and

{\rm Aut}(G;C)
-invariant, the subgroup

is also normal in

and
{\rm Aut}(G;C)
-invariant. Then since

is properly contained in

satisfies the condition in Theorem

(b), the normal

∎

2.1.
Transitive inverse-closed generating sets

For our application to normal edge-transitive Cayley graphs, we are interested in generating sets
C
for
G
which have the following property (see
[
11
, Lemma 1]
).

Definition 2.1
.

An inverse-closed generating set
C
for a nontrivial group
G
is said to be
transitive
if either
the
{\rm Aut}(G;C)
-action on
C
is transitive, or there are two orbits
C_{+}
and
C_{-}
in this action such that
C=C_{+}\cup C_{-}
and
C_{-}=(C_{+})^{-1}
, where
(C_{+})^{-1}=\{c^{-1}\mid c\in C_{+}\}
.

Transitive generating sets have some useful properties.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be a nontrivial finite group with transitive inverse-closed generating set
C
. Then

(a)

C\cap\Phi(G;C)=\emptyset
;

(b)

there is a positive integer
\ell
such that
|C\cap y\Phi(G;C)|\in\{0,\ell\}
for each
y\in G
. Moreover, if both
C\cap y\Phi(G;C)\neq\emptyset
and
C\cap y^{\prime}\Phi(G;C)\neq\emptyset
, then there exists
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(G;C)
such that
(y^{\prime}\Phi(G;C))^{\sigma}
is either
y\Phi(G;C)
or

y^{-1}\Phi(G;C)
.

Proof.

(a) Suppose that
c\in C\cap\Phi(G;C)
. Then, since
\Phi(G;C)
is
{\rm Aut}(G;C)
-invariant, each element of the orbit containing
c
, under the natural action of
{\rm Aut}(G;C)
, lies in
\Phi(G;C)
. By
Definition
2.1
, this means that for each
c^{\prime}\in C
, either
c^{\prime}
or its inverse lies in
\Phi(G;C)
. Then since
\Phi(G;C)
is closed under forming inverses (as it is a subgroup), we have
C\subseteq\Phi(G;C)
. Hence
G=\langle C\rangle\leq\Phi(G;C)
, which is a contradiction, since
\Phi(G;C)
is a proper subgroup.

(b) Suppose that
C\cap y\Phi(G;C)\neq\emptyset
and
C\cap y^{\prime}\Phi(G;C)\neq\emptyset
. Then there exist
c,c^{\prime}\in C
such that
y\Phi(G;C)=c\Phi(G;C)
and
y^{\prime}\Phi(G;C)=c^{\prime}\Phi(G;C)
. By
Definition
2.1
, there exists
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(G;C)
such that
(c^{\prime})^{\sigma}
is equal to either
c
or

c^{-1}
, and hence, since
\Phi(G;C)
is
{\rm Aut}(G;C)
-invariant,
(y^{\prime}\Phi(G;C))^{\sigma}=(c^{\prime}\Phi(G;C))^{\sigma}
is either
c\Phi(G;C)=y\Phi(G;C)
or

c^{-1}\Phi(G;C)=y^{-1}\Phi(G;C)
. Note that, since
\Phi(G;C)
is a normal subgroup of
G
, we have
(c\Phi(G;C))^{-1}=c^{-1}\Phi(G;C)
. Then, since
C
is inverse-closed,
(C\cap c\Phi(G;C))^{-1}=C^{-1}\cap(c\Phi(G;C))^{-1}=C\cap c^{-1}\Phi(G;C)
.
Thus, the cardinality
|C\cap y^{\prime}\Phi(G;C)|=|C\cap y\Phi(G;C)|
is constant for all non-empty intersections.
∎

3.
Quotients of normal edge-transitive graphs

In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
, as a consequence of a more general result, Theorem
3.2
. First we summarise our notation for graphs and their homomorphisms.

3.1.
Graph concepts

We will only consider finite simple undirected graphs. That is, a
graph

\Gamma=(V,E)
will consist of a finite set
V
of vertices, and a set
E
of edges, where each edge is an unordered pair of distinct vertices. We often denote the vertex set and the edge set of
\Gamma
by
V(\Gamma)
and
E(\Gamma)
, respectively.

A
subgraph

\Gamma^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
of
\Gamma=(V,E)
, is a graph with
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
,
E^{\prime}\subseteq E
, and each
\{u,v\}\in E^{\prime}
is a subset of
V^{\prime}
. A
homomorphism
from
\Gamma_{1}=(V_{1},E_{1})
to
\Gamma_{2}=(V_{2},E_{2})
is a map
f:V_{1}\to V_{2}
such that, for each
\{u,v\}\in E_{1}
, the image
\{u^{f},v^{f}\}\in E_{2}
. A graph homomorphism
f
is called

•

an
epimorphism
, if
f
is onto (on vertices);

•

a
full homomorphism
, if the induced map
E_{1}\to E_{2}
is onto;

•

a
full epimorphism
, if
f
is both an epimorphism and a full homomorphism;

•

an
isomorphism
if
f
is a full homomorphism and is a bijection on vertices; in this case
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
are said to be
isomorphic
, written
\Gamma_{1}\cong\Gamma_{2}
.

For graphs
\Gamma_{i}=(V_{i},E_{i})
, where
1\leq i\leq n
, their
direct product

is the graph
(V,E)
with
V=V_{1}\times\dots\times V_{n}
and
E
consisting of all the pairs
\{u,v\}\subset V
such that
\{u_{i},v_{i}\}\in E_{i}
for each
i\leq n
. We denote this graph by
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}
. A
subdirect product
of a family of graphs
\{\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{n}\}
is a subgraph
\Gamma
of the direct product
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}
such that, for each
i\leq n
, the natural map
\pi_{i}:\Gamma\to\Gamma_{i}
(where
\pi_{i}:u\to u_{i}
) is a graph epimorphism. It is called a
full subdirect product
if each
\pi_{i}
is a full epimorphism (see
[
2
]
).

Our main graph theoretical result Theorem
1.2
relies in an essential way on the following fact about products of Cayley graphs.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer
n\geq 2
, and let
G_{1},\dots,G_{n}
be nontrivial finite groups with inverse-closed generating sets
C_{1},\dots,C_{n}
, respectively.
Consider the Cayley graphs

\Gamma:={\rm Cay}(G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{n},C_{1}\times\dots\times C_{n}),\ \text{and}\ \Gamma_{i}:={\rm Cay}(G_{i},C_{i}),\ \text{for}\ i=1,\dots,n.

Then
\Gamma
is the direct product graph
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}
.

Proof.

By the definition of a Cayley graph, for each
i
, the edge set of
\Gamma_{i}
is the set of all pairs of the form
\{g_{i},c_{i}g_{i}\}
with
g_{i}\in G_{i}
and
c_{i}\in C_{i}
. Similarly, the edge-set of
\Gamma
is the set of all pairs of the form
\{g,cg\}
where
g=(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}G_{i}
and
c=(c_{1},\dots,c_{n})\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}
. In such a pair the element
cg=(c_{1}g_{1},\dots,c_{n}g_{n})
, and hence these pairs
\{g,cg\}
are precisely the pairs for which
\{g_{i},c_{i}g_{i}\}
is an edge of
\Gamma_{i}
, for each
i=1,\dots,n
. By the definition of a direct product graph,
\Gamma
is the direct product
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{i}
.
∎

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Now we state and prove our main decomposition result for normal edge-transitive Cayley graphs. Recall that, for an inverse-closed generating set
C
for a group
G
,
{\rm Cay}(G;C)
is normal edge-transitive if and only if
G_{R}\rtimes{\rm Aut}(G;C)
is transitive on edges and, by
[
11
, Proposition 1(b)]
, this is equivalent to the set

being transitive in the sense of Definition
2.1
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
G
be a nontrivial finite group with inverse-closed generating set
C
such that

\Gamma={\rm Cay}(G;C)
is normal edge-transitive. Then
there exist
k\geq 1
and
N_{1},\dots,N_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{\max}(G;C)
for which the following conditions hold for the subgroup

and quotient graphs
\Gamma_{i}:={\rm Cay}(G/N_{i};CN_{i}/N_{i})
.

(i)

and the map
\zeta:gX\rightarrow(gN_{1},\cdots,gN_{k})
is a group isomorphism
G/X\rightarrow\prod_{i=1}^{k}G/N_{i}
;

(ii)

for each
i\leq k
,
\Gamma_{i}\in{\sf NormCay{CS}}
, and
\zeta
induces a graph isomorphism from

(iii)

Moreover the unique smallest

for which both (i) and (ii) hold is

Proof.

We prove parts (i) and (ii) by induction on

. These assertions hold if
\mathcal{A}_{\max}(G;C)=\{\{1_{G}\}\}
, since in this case
G
is characteristically simple and so
{\rm Cay}(G;C)\in{\sf NormCay{CS}}
, and we take

and

is simple. Suppose inductively that

|G|
.

Choose

is characteristically simple and since the graph

[
11

\Gamma_{1}\in{\sf NormCay{CS}}
.
Let

and suppose that

N_{1}H
is a proper normal subgroup of

{\rm Aut}(G;C)

N_{1}H\in\mathcal{A}(G;C)

N_{1}
, it follows that
N_{1}H=N_{1}
, that is to say,
H\leq N_{1}
. If this is true for all
H\in\mathcal{A}(G;C)
, then
N_{1}
is the unique maximal element of
\mathcal{A}(G;C)
, and hence
\mathcal{A}_{\max}(G;C)=\{N_{1}\}
,
\Phi(G;C)=N_{1}
, and parts (i) and (ii) hold with
k=1,X=\Phi(G;C)=N_{1}
.

Thus we may assume that there exists
H\in\mathcal{A}(G;C)
such that
N_{1}H=G
. Since

N_{1}\neq G
, we have
H\neq 1
so
|G/H|<|G|
, and by induction parts (i) and (ii) hold for

\overline{\zeta}:\overline{G}/\overline{K}\rightarrow\prod_{i=2}^{k}\overline{G}/\overline{N}_{i}

\overline{\zeta}
induces a graph isomorphism from

Note that each

for a unique

such that
H\leq N_{i}
. Moreover,
\overline{G}/\overline{N_{i}}\cong G/N_{i}
and the natural map
\nu_{i}:xN_{i}\to\overline{x}\overline{N}_{i}

\overline{x}=xH\in\overline{G}=G/H

\overline{x}=xH\in(CN_{i})/H=\overline{CN_{i}}

to

for

. Moreover the map

Setting

\overline{K}=K/H\in\mathcal{A}(\overline{G};\overline{C})
and
K\in\mathcal{A}(G;C)
, and a natural isomorphism

\mu:xK\to\overline{x}\overline{K}

The composition
\zeta^{\prime}:=\mu\circ\overline{\zeta}\circ\nu

Proof of part (i).
Let

Proof of part (ii).

Proof.

of Theorem

.

3.3.
Transitive inverse-closed generating sets for a given group

Inverse-closed generating sets for a group
G
, that are transitive in the sense of Definition
2.1
, are often fairly natural subsets. For example we may take a full conjugacy class of involutions in a nonabelian simple group
G
.
Let
\mathcal{T}(G)
denote the set of all transitive, inverse-closed generating sets for
G
.
First we observe some simple properties of
\mathcal{T}(G)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G,H
be nontrivial finite groups. Then

(a)

\mathcal{T}(G)
is
{\rm Aut}(G)
-invariant; and

(b)

if
C\in\mathcal{T}(G)
and

, and if either
{\rm Aut}(G;C)
is transitive on
C
or
{\rm Aut}(H;D)

, then
C\times D\in\mathcal{T}(G\times H)

Proof.

(a) It is easy to check that, for
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(G)
and
C\in\mathcal{T}(G)
, the image

C^{\sigma}
is an inverse-closed generating set for
G
. Further,
C^{\sigma}
is
{\rm Aut}(G;C^{\sigma})
-invariant (since
C
is
{\rm Aut}(G;C)
-invariant), and the fact that

is transitive implies that

is transitive. Thus

.

(b) Suppose that

and

. Then

is inverse closed, and generates

. Also

. If both

is transitive on

is transitive on

, then

is transitive on

, and hence

. Suppose next that

is transitive on

and

has two orbits on

, namely

and

. Then

and

are the two orbits of

in

since

is inverse-closed. So again

, and the same argument works in the case where

is transitive on

but

is not transitive on

.
∎

We note that the argument in the proof of part (b) does not extend to the case where both

is not transitive on

and

is not transitive on

, since in this case

has four orbits in

.

We now give a family of examples to illustrate that a group may have several transitive inverse-closed generating sets, and in particular that some may be properly contained in others.

Example 3.4
.

Let
G=\langle a,b\mid a^{n}=b^{2}=1,bab=a^{-1}\rangle
, the dihedral group

of order

, as in (

is given in (

(a)

As we saw in Example

(b)

By Lemma

4.

Lemma 4.8
.

There are infinitely many odd primes which are not Mersenne primes.

Proof.

This follows easily from the number theoretic result known as Bertrand’s Postulate (see for example
[
9
, Theorem 8.7]
) that, for all integers
m\geq 3
there exists a prime
p
such that
m<p<2m
.
Taking
m=2^{r}-1\geq 3
, we obtain a prime satisfying
2^{r}-1<p<2^{r+1}-2
. Such a prime
p
is odd and is not a Mersenne prime, and the primes obtained for distinct integers
r\geq 2
are pairwise distinct.
∎

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
p
be an odd prime that is not a Mersenne prime, and let
q
be any odd prime divisor of
p+1
.
Define
n,i,j,k,\ell,m
as follows:

n=2pq,\ i=p,\ j=q,\ k=p+q,\ \ell=2p+1,\ m=n-\ell=2pq-2p-1.

Then all the conditions of Example
4.5
hold for
i,j,k,\ell,m
, and in particular
C=\{b,ba^{i},ba^{j},ba^{k}\}
arises in Example
4.5
for the group
G=D_{2n}
.

Proof.

Since
p,q
are distinct odd primes it follows that
i,j,k
are pairwise coprime, so condition (i) of Example
4.5
holds. Next

\gcd(i,n)=p,\quad\gcd(j,n)=q,\quad\gcd(k,n)=2,

and hence condition (ii) of Example
4.5
holds. Now we check condition (iii)
of Example
4.5
. Note that
p
divides
\ell-1
and
q
divides
2(p+1)=\ell+1
,
and hence, since
\ell
is odd,
n=2pq
divides
\ell^{2}-1
, that is,
\ell^{2}\equiv 1\pmod{n}
.
Also
m^{2}\equiv 1\pmod{n}
since
m\equiv-\ell\pmod{n}
. By definition,
\ell,m\not\equiv 1\pmod{n}
, and we have
\ell m\equiv-\ell^{2}\equiv-1\not\equiv 1\pmod{n}
. Next
i(\ell+1)=p(2p+2)=2p(p+1)\equiv 0\pmod{n}
since
q
divides
p+1
, and
j(m+1)=q(n-2p)\equiv-2pq\equiv 0\pmod{n}
. Also
j\ell+i=q(2p+1)+p\equiv q+p=k\pmod{n}
. Finally

im+j=p(n-2p-1)+q\equiv-2p(p+1)+p+q\equiv p+q=k\pmod{n}
, since
n
divides
2p(p+1)
.
Thus all the conditions of Example
4.5
hold, so
C=\{b,ba^{i},ba^{j},ba^{k}\}
arises in Example
4.5
for the group
G=D_{2n}
.
∎

5.
Graphs which are not normal edge-transitive

For an arbitrary inverse-closed generating set
C
of a group
G
, it is of course possible to define normal quotients of the Cayley graph
{\rm Cay}(G;C)
relative to
{\rm Aut}(G;C)
-invariant normal subgroups of
G
. One may wonder to what extent Theorem
1.2
might extend to this general situation, that is to say, whether or not the assumption that
{\rm Cay}(G;C)
is normal edge-transitive is needed. Here we consider a small example which pin-points one of the problems which may cause the graph isomorphism in Theorem
1.2
(ii) to fail if
{\rm Cay}(G;C)
is not normal edge-transitive. Recall that
{\sf NormCay{CS}}
is the class of connected normal edge-transitive Cayley graphs for finite characteristically simple groups.

Example 5.1
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
so that
{\rm Aut}(G)={\rm GL}(4,2)
, and define
C=\{a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\}
, where

a_{1}=(1,0,1,1),\ a_{2}=(0,1,1,1),\ b_{1}=(1,0,1,0),\ b_{2}=(0,1,0,1),\ b_{3}=(1,1,1,1),

and set
A=\{a_{1},a_{2}\}
and
B=\{b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\}
, so
C=A\cup B
. Define

\displaystyle\tau_{1}

\displaystyle:(x,y,z,w)\to(y+z+w,x+z+w,z,w),

\displaystyle\tau_{2}

\displaystyle:(x,y,z,w)\to(x+z+w,x+w,w,x+y),\ \text{and}

\displaystyle\tau_{3}

\displaystyle:(x,y,z,w)\to(y,x,w,z).

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
G,C
be as in Example
5.1
, and let
A_{0}={\rm Aut}(G;C)
. Then

(a)

C
is an inverse-closed generating set for
G
, and
A_{0}=\langle\tau_{1}\rangle\times\langle\tau_{2},\tau_{1}\tau_{3}\rangle\cong{\rm Sym}(A)\times{\rm Sym}(B)=S_{2}\times S_{3}
. In particular
A_{0}
fixes each of
A
and
B
setwise, and
C\not\in\mathcal{T}(G)
, that is,
C
is not transitive.

(b)

\mathcal{A}_{\max}(G;C)
contains
M:=\langle A\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
and
N:=\langle b_{1},b_{2},a_{1}+a_{2}\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3}
, and
\Phi(G;C)=M\cap N=\langle a_{1}+a_{2}\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

(c)

The graph
\Gamma:={\rm Cay}(G;C)
is not normal edge-transitive; its normal quotient graphs

\Gamma_{M}:={\rm Cay}(G/M,(C+M)/M),\ \Gamma_{N}:={\rm Cay}(G/N,(C+N)/N)\in{\sf NormCay{CS}},

and moreover
\Gamma_{M}\cong K_{4}
,
\Gamma_{N}\cong K_{2}
, while for
\Phi:=\Phi(G;C)
,
\Gamma_{\Phi}:={\rm Cay}(G/\Phi,(C+\Phi)/\Phi)
is the
4
-valent complement of the cube
Q_{3}
.

(d)

The map
g\to(gM,gN)
induces a group isomorphism
G/\Phi(G;C)\to G/M\times G/N
, but does not induce a graph isomorphism from
\Gamma_{\Phi}
to a subgraph of the direct product
\Gamma_{M}\times\Gamma_{N}
. For example, a ‘
b_{3}
-type’ edge such as
\{0+\Phi,b_{3}+\Phi\}
of
\Gamma_{\Phi}
does not map to an edge of
\Gamma_{M}\times\Gamma_{N}
as it projects to a loop of
\Gamma_{N}
.

Proof.

(a) The first assertion follows since each element of
G
is equal to its inverse, and since
C
spans
G
as a vector space. Next, to simplify our later arguments, we check directly from their definitions that

\displaystyle\tau_{1}

\displaystyle:a_{1}\leftrightarrow a_{2},\ b_{1}\to b_{1},\ b_{2}\to b_{2},\ b_{3}\to b_{3}

\displaystyle\tau_{2}

\displaystyle:a_{1}\to a_{1},\ a_{2}\to a_{2},\ b_{1}\to b_{2}\to b_{3}\to b_{1}

\displaystyle\tau_{3}

\displaystyle:a_{1}\leftrightarrow a_{2},\ b_{1}\leftrightarrow b_{2},\ b_{3}\to b_{3}.

In particular each
\tau_{i}
maps the spanning set
C
bijectively to itself and hence each
\tau_{i}\in{\rm GL}(4,2)
. Again, since each
\tau_{i}
fixes
C
setwise (and moreover each
\tau_{i}
fixes each of
A
and
B
setwise), it follows that each
\tau_{i}\in A_{0}
. Now
A_{0}
acts faithfully as a permutation group on
C
, and hence it follows easily from the displayed actions of the
\tau_{i}
on
C
that the subgroup they generate is
H=\langle\tau_{1}\rangle\times\langle\tau_{2},\tau_{1}\tau_{3}\rangle\cong{\rm Sym}(A)\times{\rm Sym}(B)=S_{2}\times S_{3}
.
Moreover, this subgroup
H
is a maximal subgroup of
{\rm Sym}(C)=S_{5}
. If
A_{0}
is strictly larger than
H
, then we would have
A_{0}\cong{\rm Sym}(C)
, and hence
A_{0}
would contain an element
\varphi:b_{1}\to b_{1},\ b_{2}\to b_{2},\ b_{3}\to a_{1}\to a_{2}\to b_{3}
. However such an element
\varphi
must in particular lie in
{\rm GL}(4,2)
and hence

\varphi(b_{3})=\varphi(b_{1}+b_{2})=\varphi(b_{1})+\varphi(b_{2})=b_{1}+b_{2}=b_{3},

which is a contradiction. Hence
H=A_{0}
. Thus
A_{0}
has orbits in
C
of lengths
2
and
3
, and hence
C\not\in\mathcal{T}(G)
, that is,
C
is not transitive. This proves part (a).

(b) Let
M_{B}=\langle B\rangle=\{0,b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\}
. Then
G=M+M_{B}
and
M\cap M_{B}=0
. In particular each nontrivial coset of
M
in
G
is of the form
b_{i}+M
for some
i\leq 3
. Thus if
K
is an
A_{0}
-invariant normal subgroup of
G
properly containing
M
then
K=G
since
A_{0}
is transitive on
B
by part (a). It follows that
M\in\mathcal{A}_{\max}(G;C)
. Next we deal with
N
. We see by its definition that
N=\{(x+z,y+z,x,y)\mid x,y,z\in\mathbf{F}_{2}\}
. It follows from the actions of the
\tau_{i}
displayed above that
\tau_{1}
fixes
N
pointwise, and that both
\tau_{2}
and
\tau_{3}
leave
N
invariant. Hence
N
is an
A_{0}
-invariant normal subgroup of
G
, and as
N
is maximal in
G
we conclude that
N\in\mathcal{A}_{\max}(G;C)
. We note that
M\cap N=\langle a_{1}+a_{2}\rangle=\{0,(1,1,0,0)\}
since
M\cap M_{B}=0
. Hence,
\Phi(G;C)\leq M\cap N
, by (
3
), so to complete the proof of part (b) it remains for us to show that
a:=a_{1}+a_{2}=(1,1,0,0)\in\Phi(G;C)
.

To do this, we need to check that the criterion of Theorem
1.1
(b) holds for
a
. So suppose that
X\subseteq G
and that the normal
C
-closure of
X\cup\{a\}
generates
G
, that is,
G
is generated by

\widehat{X}:=\{(x^{g})^{\sigma}|x\in X\cup\{a\},g\in G,\sigma\in A_{0}\}
. Since
G
is abelian, and since
a=a_{1}+a_{2}
is fixed by each element of
A_{0}
(by part (a)),
\widehat{X}=\{a\}\cup Y
, where
Y=\{(x^{g})^{\sigma}|x\in X,g\in G,\sigma\in A_{0}\}
, that is,
Y
is the normal
C
-closure of
X
. We therefore need to prove that
Y
generates
G
. Suppose to the contrary that
W:=\langle Y\rangle
is a proper subgroup of
G
. Since
Y\cup\{a\}
generates
G
it follows that
G=W+\langle a\rangle
and
W\cap\langle a\rangle=0
. In particular there exists
w\in W
such that
w+a=a_{1}
. This means that
W
contains
w=a+a_{1}=a_{2}
, and since
Y
is
C
-closed and generates
W
, also
a_{1}=a_{2}^{\tau_{1}}
lies in
W
, and therefore
a=a_{1}+a_{2}\in W
, which is a contradiction. Thus we have proved that
Y
generates
G
, and hence that the criterion of Theorem
1.1
(b) holds for
a
. Therefore
a:=(1,1,0,0)\in\Phi(G;C)
. and part (b) is proved.

(c) By
[
11
, Proposition 1(c)]
, the graph
\Gamma:={\rm Cay}(G;C)
is not normal edge-transitive since the set
C
is not transitive (by part (a)). Since
(C+M)/M=(B+M)/M
(as
A\subset M
), and since
A_{0}
is transitive on
B
by part (a), it follows that
\Gamma_{M}
is normal edge-transitive (by
[
11
, Proposition 1(c)]
), and hence
\Gamma_{M}\in{\sf NormCay{CS}}
. Similarly
\Gamma_{N}\in{\sf NormCay{CS}}
since
(C+N)/N=(A+N)/N
. Now
\Gamma_{M}\cong K_{4}
and
\Gamma_{N}\cong K_{2}
, since
(C+M)/M
and
(C+N)/N
consist of all the nontrivial cosets of
G/M
and
G/N
, respectively. On the other hand,
(C+\Phi)/\Phi
has size 4, since
a_{1}+\Phi=a_{2}+\Phi
and this element is distinct from each of the
b_{i}+\Phi
, which themselves are pairwise distinct. Thus
\Gamma_{\Phi}
has valency
4
, and it is not difficult to check that
\Gamma_{\Phi}
is the complement of the
3
-dimensional cube graph
Q_{3}
.

(d) The map
\phi:g\to(gM,gN)
is a group homomorphism
\phi:G\to G/M\times G/N
with kernel
M\cap N=\Phi(G;C)
(by part (b)). Thus the image of
\phi
is isomorphic to
G/\Phi(G;C)=G/(M\cap N)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3}
. Since
G/M\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
and
G/N\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, it follows that
\phi
is onto and
\phi
induces a group isomorphism
G/\Phi(G;C)\cong G/M\times G/N
. This bijection on vertices does not map all edges of
\Gamma_{\Phi}
to edges of the direct product
\Gamma_{M}\times\Gamma_{N}
, as a ‘
b_{3}
-type’ edge
\{x+\Phi,x+b_{3}+\Phi\}
is mapped to the vertex-pair
\{(x+M,x+N),(x+b_{3}+M,x+N)\}
(since
b_{3}\in N
), and this is not an edge of the direct product
\Gamma_{M}\times\Gamma_{N}
. Put more simply,

\Gamma_{\Phi}
has valency
4
and so is not isomorphic to a subgraph of the valency
3
direct product graph
\Gamma_{M}\times\Gamma_{N}
.
∎

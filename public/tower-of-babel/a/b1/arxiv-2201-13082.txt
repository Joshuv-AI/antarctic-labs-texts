Asymptotic issues for porous media systems with linear multiplicative gradient-type noise via state constrained arguments
Ioana Ciotir, Dan Goreac, Ionuţ Munteanu

Abstract
The aim of the present paper is to provide necessary and sufficient conditions to maintain a stochastic coupled system, with porous media components and gradient-type noise in a prescribed set of constraints by using internal controls. This work is a continuation of the results in [ 13 ] , as we consider the case of divergence type noise perturbation. On the other hand, it provides a different framework in which the quasi-tangency condition can be obtained with optimal speed. In comparison with the aforementioned result, here we transform the stochastic system into a random deterministic one, via the rescaling approach, then we study the viability of random sets. As an application, conditions for the stabilization of the stochastic porous media equations are obtained.



Keywords
: stochastic porous media equation, control system, divergence Stratonovich noise

Introduction

We focus on the following Stratonovich stochastic controlled system

(1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\begin{aligned} dX^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s)=&\Delta\beta_{1}(X^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s))ds+f_{1}(X^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s),Y^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s),u(s))ds\\
&+B_{1}X^{t,\xi,\eta,u}\circ dW(s)\text{ in }(t,\infty)\times\mathcal{O}_{1},\end{aligned}\\
\begin{aligned} dY^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s)=&\Delta\beta_{2}(Y^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s))ds+f_{2}(X^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s),Y^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s),u(s))ds\\
&+B_{2}Y^{t,\xi,\eta,u}\circ dW(s)\text{ in }(t,\infty)\times\mathcal{O}_{2},\end{aligned}\\
X^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s)=0\text{ on }\partial\mathcal{O}_{1},\ Y^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s)=0\text{ on }\partial\mathcal{O}_{2},\\
X^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(t)=\xi,\ Y^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(t)=\eta,\end{array}\right.\

where, for
i\in\left\{1,2\right\}:

\mathcal{O}_{i}
is an open, bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d},\ d\geq 2,
with smooth boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}_{i}
;
\beta_{i}
and
f_{i}
are nonlinear Lipschitz continuous functions (see below);
B_{i}\phi=b_{i}\cdot\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\phi,
where
b_{i}:\overline{\mathcal{O}_{i}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the coefficient field and
W
denotes a one-dimensional violetian motion on a filtered probability space
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\left\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\right\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P}\right)
obeying the usual assumptions. The initial datum is chosen as
\left(\xi,\eta\right)\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathbb{P};H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{1}\right)\times H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{2}\right)).
These equations are controlled with a progressively measurable process
u
taking its values in a compact metric space
U
. The class of such control policies is denoted by
\mathcal{U}
. The existence and the uniqueness of solutions for the (coupled) system (
1
) follows in a standard way (see
[
6
, Chapter 2]
,
[
7
]
or the recent work
[
16
]
for more general coupled systems). For more details about systems of the same type as (
1
) see
[
13
]
and the references therein.

We are interested here on the following problem: under which conditions a given set
K\subset H^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{1})\times H^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
is viable with respect to equation (
1
). To be more precise, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 1

Given a closed set
K\subset H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{1}\right)\times H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{2}\right)
, it is said to be
nearly viable
with respect to (
1
) on the finite interval
\left[0,T>0\right]
if, for every initial time
t\in\left[0,T\right)
and every initial pair
\left(\xi,\eta\right)\in\mathbb{K}_{t}:=\mathbb{L}^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathbb{P};K\right)
, we have

(2)

\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\sup_{s\in\left[t,T\right]}d\left(\left(X^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s),Y^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s)\right),\mathbb{K}_{s}\right)=0.

The distance is meant in
\mathbb{L}^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P};H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{1}\right)\times H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{2}\right)\right)
. If an optimal control in (
2
) exists, then near viability is equivalent with viability i.e.
(X^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s),Y^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s))\in K
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. and for all
s\in\left[t,T\right]
. A notion of
\mathbb{L}^{2}
-near viability
can be introduced when
\left(\xi,\eta\right)\in\mathbb{L}^{2}\left({\mathcal{O}_{{1}}}\right)\times\mathbb{L}^{2}\left({\mathcal{O}_{{2}}}\right)
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s., by replacing
\mathbb{K}_{s}
with
\mathbb{K}_{s}\cap\mathbb{L}^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{s},\mathbb{P};\mathbb{L}^{2}\left({\mathcal{O}_{{1}}}\right)\times\mathbb{L}^{2}\left({\mathcal{O}_{{2}}}\right)\right)
.

The literature on state-constrained systems is vast. It originates from the work
[
17
]
, then it has been extended to tangency concepts to stochastic finite-dimensional systems by
[
1
]
. See also the pioneering works
[
2

]
and the monograph
[

]
. The results in
[

]
offer an important semi-group-based method dealing with a wide class of deterministic PDEs. Let us also mention the application of such methods to purely deterministic porous media equations in
[

. Their setting, however is fundamentally different than ours.

As already mentioned, we shall follow, in the stochastic case, some of the ideas in
[
13
]
. We emphasize that, in
[
13
]
, the authors study the same problem for a porous media-type system (as (

)) but with

The fundamental idea is to replace the solution with a constrained Euler-like scheme and provide good estimates of the distance between the original solution and its approximating scheme. While the best local in time estimate for deterministic systems may be expected to be of order

,
[
13
, Sections 3.2, 3.3 ]
only exhibit speeds of order
t^{1-\frac{\lambda}{2}}
for every
\lambda>0
. One way of obtaining the estimate for
\lambda=0
for the "deterministic" part (i.e. conditional expectations) is to project the system on convenient finite-dimensional spaces, cf.
[
13
, Section 3.4]
. We provide here another way of overcoming these technical difficulties and obtain optimal speed when the system can be transformed into a pseudo-deterministic one.

Partly based on the techniques in
[
13
]
, we provide necessary and sufficient conditions for a set to be viable with respect to (
1

H^{-1}
-divergence type noise. The choice of a Stratonovich stochastic integral is motivated both by the physical interpretation of the equation and by the particular affinity to exponential transformations. One consequence is the fact that the Stratonovich integral is stable with respect to changes in the random term. See
[
18
]
for more details.

The rescaling method which is used in this work was first introduced for stochastic porous media equations in a pioneer paper of Barbu-Röckner (see
[
8
]
).
Recently, this technique was intensively used for different stochastic partial differential equations under different sets of assumptions. See e.g.
[
7
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
15
]
.

In the present work, via the rescaling argument, we rewrite (
1

[
13

As an application of the theoretical results, at the end of the paper, we will provide some necessary and sufficient conditions for the exponential asymptotic stabilization of the aforementioned stochastic porous media equation. For other results on long time behaviour of the solution to stochastic porous media equation, see
[
14

2
Notations and assumptions

For
i\in\left\{1,2\right\}
:
\mathbb{L}^{p}\left({\mathcal{O}_{{i}}}\right)
,
p\geq 1
is the standard Banach space of real-valued
p
-power (Lebesgue-) integrable functions on
\mathcal{O}_{i}
. We set
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\mathcal{O}_{i})}
for the classical Lebesgue norm, and set
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>_{\mathbb{L}^{2}\left({\mathcal{O}_{{i}}}\right)}
for the classical scalar product.

H_{0}^{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right)
is the space of
\mathbb{L}^{2}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right)
functions that vanish on
\partial\mathcal{O}_{i}
and such that the distributional derivative of first order belongs to
\mathbb{L}^{p}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right)
. The norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{H_{0}^{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right)}
is given by
\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{H_{0}^{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right)}^{2}:=\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}\left({\mathcal{O}_{{i}}}\right)}.

The dual, with pivot space
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{i})
, of the aforementioned space is assumed to be
H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right)
. The associated norm is
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right)}
and the associated product
\left\langle\phi,\psi\right\rangle_{H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right)}:=\left<(-\Delta)^{-1}\phi,\psi\right>_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{i})}.

Let us introduce
\mathcal{B}_{i}
, the set of all functions
b=(b^{1},b^{2},...,b^{d}),\ b^{j}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\ j=1,2,...,d,
, such that
b^{j}\in C^{2}(\overline{\mathcal{O}_{i}})
,
\text{div}\ b=0
and
b
is tangent to the boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}_{i}
, of the domain
\mathcal{O}_{i}
. Let any
b\in\mathcal{B}_{i}
. We associate the operator
B:\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{i})\rightarrow H^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{i})
, defined as

(3)

B\phi:=b\cdot\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\phi,\ \phi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{i}).

It is proven in
[
7
, Section 4.3]
that
B
is densely defined and skew-symmetric in
H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right)
such that
B\in\mathcal{L}(H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right);\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{i}))
. Furthermore,
B
is the infinitesimal generator of a contraction
C_{0}-
group in
H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right)
, denoted by
\left\{e^{sB},\ s\in\mathbb{R}\right\}
. This group leaves
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{i})
invariant.

In this paper, we assume that
b_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{i}
for
i\in\left\{1,2\right\}
. For
\phi,\psi\in H^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{i}),\ s\in\mathbb{R},
we have

(4)

\left<e^{sB_{i}}\phi,\psi\right>_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{i})}=\left<\phi,e^{-sB_{i}}\psi\right>_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{i})}.

The contraction property reads

(5)

\|e^{sB_{i}}\phi\|_{H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right)}\leq\|\phi\|_{H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right)}.

Concerning the nonlinearities in (
1
), we will work under the assumption

(6)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(i)\ &\beta_{i}\text{ is real-valued, Lipschitz continuous of Lipschitz constant }[\beta_{i}]_{i}>0;\\
(ii)\ &\textnormal{There exist }\alpha_{i}>0\textnormal{ s.t. }\left(\beta_{i}(r)-\beta_{i}(s)\right)\left(r-s\right)\geq\alpha_{i}(r-s)^{2},\ \forall r,s\in\mathbb{R};\\
(iii)\ &f_{i}:H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{1}\right)\times H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{2}\right)\times U\rightarrow H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right),\\
&\textnormal{There exist }[f_{i}]_{1}>0\textnormal{ such that }\forall x,x^{\prime}\in H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{1}\right),\ y,y^{\prime}\in H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{2}\right),\ u\in\mathcal{U},\\
&\left\lVert f_{i}(x,y,u)-f_{i}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},u)\right\rVert_{H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right)}\leq\left[f_{i}\right]_{1}\left(\left\lVert x-x^{\prime}\right\rVert_{H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{1}\right)}+\left\lVert y-y^{\prime}\right\rVert_{H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{2}\right)}\right);\\
&\underset{u\in\mathcal{U}}{\sup}\left\lVert f_{i}(0,0,u)\right\rVert_{H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right)}<\infty;\\
(iv)\ &{\textnormal{ the restriction }f_{i}:\mathbb{L}^{2}\left({\mathcal{O}_{{1}}}\right)\times\mathbb{L}^{2}\left({\mathcal{O}_{{2}}}\right)\times U\rightarrow\mathbb{L}^{2}\left({\mathcal{O}_{{i}}}\right)\textnormal{ enjoys the same properties}.}\end{cases}

3
The equivalent formulation and the main result

Let us introduce
\Gamma_{i},\ i=1,2,
the stochastic exponential
\Gamma_{i}(t,s)=e^{[W(s)-W(t)]B_{i}},\ i=1,2.
Following the ideas in
[
7
]
, by the rescaling
X^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s)=\Gamma_{1}(t,s)x^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s)
and
Y^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s)=\Gamma_{2}(t,s)y^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s),
we equivalently rewrite (
1
) as a random-deterministic system

(7)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\begin{aligned} \partial_{s}x^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s)=&\Gamma_{1}(s,t)\Delta\beta_{1}(\Gamma_{1}(t,s)x^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s))\\
&+\Gamma_{1}(s,t)f_{1}(\Gamma_{1}(t,s)x^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s),\Gamma_{2}(t,s)y^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s),u(s))\text{ in }(t,\infty)\times\mathcal{O}_{1},\end{aligned}\\
\begin{aligned} \partial_{s}y^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s)=&\Gamma_{2}(s,t)\Delta\beta_{2}(\Gamma_{2}(t,s)y^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s))\\
&+\Gamma_{2}(s,t)f_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(t,s)x^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s),\Gamma_{2}(t,s)y^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s),u(s))\text{ in }(t,\infty)\times\mathcal{O}_{2},\end{aligned}\\
x^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s)=0\text{ on }\partial\mathcal{O}_{1},\ y^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s)=0\text{ on }\partial\mathcal{O}_{2},\\
x^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(t)=\xi,\ y^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(t)=\eta.\end{array}\right.

One can easily adapt Definition
1
in order to precise what a viable set to system (
7
) means. Then,
owing to (
4
)-(
5
), it is enough to study the viability of equation (
7
) only, because

\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\sup_{s\in\left[t,T\right]}d\left(\left(X^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s),Y^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s)\right),\mathbb{K}_{s}\right)\leq\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\sup_{s\in\left[t,T\right]}d\left(\left(x^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s),y^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s)\right),\hat{\mathbb{K}}_{t,s}\right)

where
\hat{\mathbb{K}}_{t,s}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\Gamma_{1}(s,t)&0\\
0&\Gamma_{2}(s,t)\end{array}\right)\mathbb{K}_{s}
. Here,
d
is the same distance as in Definition
1
. Since we are interested in norm considerations to evaluate an asymptotic behaviour, and in order to avoid moving sets, we assume that
\hat{\mathbb{K}}_{t,s}=\mathbb{K}_{s}
is no-longer time dependent (except for unavoidable adaptedness issues). But, since we deal with a random-deterministic equation, and due to the particular structure of
\Gamma_{i}
depending only on the increment of the Brownian, adaptedness issues can be abandoned altogether. We will simply write
\mathbb{K}
instead of
\mathbb{K}_{T}
.

Before moving on, let us recall that
B_{i}\in\mathcal{L}(H^{-1},\mathbb{L}^{2})
, such that

\left\lVert e^{sB_{i}}-\mathbb{I}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}\left(H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right);\mathbb{L}^{2}\left({\mathcal{O}_{{i}}}\right)\right)}\leq e^{\left\lvert s\right\rvert\left\lVert B_{i}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}\left(H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right);\mathbb{L}^{2}\left({\mathcal{O}_{{i}}}\right)\right)}}-1.

One recalls that, for the folded normal distribution, one has

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(e^{\delta\left\lvert r\right\rvert}-1\right)\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{N}(0,1)}(dr)=2e^{\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\delta}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{l^{2}}{2}}dl-1:=\omega(\delta).

As a consequence, whenever
\zeta\in\mathbb{L}^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathbb{P};H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right)\right)
, and
s\geq t
, by using the independence of
W(s)-W(t)
of
\mathcal{F}_{t}
and the stationarity of the increments, one has

(8)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\Gamma_{i}(t,s)\zeta-\zeta\right\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}\left({\mathcal{O}_{{i}}}\right)}^{2}\right]\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(e^{\left\lvert r\right\rvert\left\lVert B_{i}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}\left(H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right);\mathbb{L}^{2}\left({\mathcal{O}_{{i}}}\right)\right)}}-1\right)^{2}\mathbb{P}_{W(s-t)}(dr)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\zeta\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right)}\right]\\
\leq&\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(e^{2\left\lvert r\right\rvert\left\lVert B_{i}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}\left(H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right);\mathbb{L}^{2}\left({\mathcal{O}_{{i}}}\right)\right)}}-1-2\left(e^{\left\lvert r\right\rvert\left\lVert B_{i}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}\left(H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right);\mathbb{L}^{2}\left({\mathcal{O}_{{i}}}\right)\right)}}-1\right)\right)\mathbb{P}_{W(s-t)}(dr)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\zeta\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right)}\right]\\
=&\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\zeta\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right)}\right]\left[\omega(2\delta)-2\omega(\delta)\right]\mid_{\delta=\sqrt{s-t}\left\lVert B_{i}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}\left(H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{1}\right);\mathbb{L}^{2}\left({\mathcal{O}_{{1}}}\right)\right)}}.\end{split}

The reader is invited to note that
\frac{\omega(2\delta)-2\omega(\delta)}{\delta^{2}}
is bounded (arround
0+
). Indeed,

\displaystyle\omega(\delta)=O(\delta^{2})+2\int_{-\infty}^{\delta}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{l^{2}}{2}}dl-2\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{l^{2}}{2}}dl

\displaystyle=O\left(\delta^{2}\right)+\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\delta}\left(1-\frac{l^{2}}{2}\right)dl=O\left(\delta^{2}\right)+\frac{2\delta}{\sqrt{2\pi}}.

As a consequence,
\omega(2\delta)-2\omega(\delta)=O(\delta^{2})
(with the obvious use of Landau notation), and we get the existence of a generic universal constant
C>0
(independent of time and the initial datum
\zeta
) for which

(9)

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\Gamma_{i}(t,s)\zeta-\zeta\right\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}\left({\mathcal{O}_{{i}}}\right)}^{2}\right]\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\zeta\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right)}\right](s-t),\ \forall s\geq t\textnormal{ small enough}.

Let us consider the fundamental solution (acting as an Euler-type scheme) associated to (
7
)

(10)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\begin{aligned} \partial_{s}\hat{x}^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s)=&\Gamma_{1}(s,t)\Delta\beta_{1}(\Gamma_{1}(t,s)\hat{x}^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s))+f_{1}(\xi,\eta,u(s)),\end{aligned}\\
\begin{aligned} \partial_{s}\hat{y}^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s)=&\Gamma_{2}(s,t)\Delta\beta_{2}(\Gamma_{2}(t,s)\hat{y}^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s))+f_{2}(\xi,\eta,u(s)),\end{aligned}\\
\hat{x}^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s)=0\text{ on }\partial\mathcal{O}_{1},\ \hat{y}^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s)=0\text{ on }\partial\mathcal{O}_{2},\ s\geq t,\\
\hat{x}^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(t)=\xi\in\mathbb{L}^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathbb{P};H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{1}\right)\right),\ \hat{y}^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(t)=\eta\mathbb{L}^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathbb{P};H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{2}\right)\right).\end{array}\right.

In order to state the main result of this paper, let us introduce below the definition of quasi-tangent sets

Definition 2

Let us fix the finite interval
\left[0,T\right]
and
t\in\left[0,T\right)
. A closed set
K\subset H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{1}\right)\times H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{2}\right)
satisfies the
quasi-tangency condition
with respect to the control system (
7
) at
\left(\xi,\eta\right)\in\mathbb{K}_{t}
if

\displaystyle\underset{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0+}{\lim\inf}\inf\big\{

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\hat{x}^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(t+\varepsilon)-\theta_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{1}\right)}^{2}+\left\lVert\hat{y}^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(t+\varepsilon)-\theta_{2}\right\rVert_{H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{2}\right)}^{2}\right]:

\displaystyle\left(\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\right)\in\mathbb{K}_{t+\varepsilon}\big\}=0,\ \mathbb{P}-\text{a.s.}.

If this condition holds for every
t\in\left[0,T\right)
and every
\left(\xi,\eta\right)\in H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{1}\right)\times H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{2}\right)
, then we will simply say that
K
satisfies the quasi-tangency condition. This corresponds to
[
13
, Definition 4]
with
\lambda=0
.

The main theoretical result is the following.

Theorem 3

Under the above assumptions, the closed set
K
is near viable with respect to (
7
) if and only if
K
is quasi-tangent for every
t\in[0,T)
and every initial data
\xi,\eta
belonging to the space
\mathbb{K}_{t}
.

Proof.
The sufficiency follows immediately by invoking the results in
[
13
, Theorem 9 and Theorem 18]
, forwardly adapted to the random-deterministic case. For our readers’ sake, we provide the adaptation of the proof to this framework in the Appendix
5.1
. For the necessity, which constitutes the whole contribution of this paper, we invite the reader to take a look at Proposition
4
and Proposition
5
below.

First, we will need some estimates on the right-continuity of schemes which are sharper than the ones obtained in
[
13
, Proposition 5]
.

Proposition 4

Let the time horizon
T>0
be fixed. Then, there exists a constant
C>0
(generic, dependent of
T
), but not on the initial data such that, for every
\xi\in\mathbb{L}^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathbb{P};H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{1}\right)\right)
and every
\eta\in\mathbb{L}^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathbb{P};H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{2}\right)\right)
, and every
t\leq s\leq T
, we have

(11)

\begin{cases}&\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\hat{x}^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s)-\xi\right\rVert_{H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{1}\right)}^{2}\right]\leq C\left(1+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\beta_{1}\left(\xi\right)\right\rVert_{H_{0}^{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{1}\right)}^{2}+\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{1}\right)}^{2}+\left\lVert\eta\right\rVert_{H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{2}\right)}^{2}\right]\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}(s-t)^{\frac{3}{2}},\\
&\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\hat{y}^{t,\xi,\eta,u}(s)-\eta\right\rVert_{H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{1}\right)}^{2}\right]\leq C\left(1+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\beta_{2}\left(\eta\right)\right\rVert_{H_{0}^{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{2}\right)}^{2}+\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{1}\right)}^{2}+\left\lVert\eta\right\rVert_{H^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{2}\right)}^{2}\right]\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}(s-t)^{\frac{3}{2}}.\end{cases}

Proof.
By (
10
) and (
4
), we have

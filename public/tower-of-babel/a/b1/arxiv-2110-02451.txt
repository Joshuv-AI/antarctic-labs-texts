On the instability of the Ruf-Sani solitons for the NLS with exponential nonlinearity Thanks: Atanas Stefanov acknowledges partial support from NSF-DMS, # 1908626.
Hichem Hajaiej, Atanas G. Stefanov

Abstract
. We study the two dimensional non-linear Schrödinger equation with two types of exponential non-linearities. It is well-known by a work of Ruf - Sani, [ 12 ] , that such models support solitary wave solutions, which are solutions of some constrained minimization problem. We show that these Ruf - Sani solitons, are spectrally unstable as well as unstable by blow-up.

.

If
n({\mathcal{L}}_{+})=1
and there exists
\Psi\perp\phi_{\omega}
, so that
\langle{\mathcal{L}}_{+}\Psi,\Psi\rangle<0
, then the wave
\phi_{\omega}
is spectrally unstable, with exactly one unstable real mode.

3.2.
Proof of the spectral instability of the waves

Per the results of Corollary
3.1
, it suffices to construct
\Psi\perp\phi_{\omega}
, so that
\langle{\mathcal{L}}_{+}\Psi,\Psi\rangle<0
. To this end, set

\Psi:=x\cdot\nabla\phi_{\omega}+\phi_{\omega}.

A direct calculation shows
\Psi\perp\phi_{\omega}
. Indeed,

\langle\Psi,\phi_{\omega}\rangle=\langle x\cdot\nabla\phi_{\omega},\phi_{\omega}\rangle+\|\phi_{\omega}\|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}x_{j}\partial_{j}\phi_{\omega}^{2}+\|\phi_{\omega}\|^{2}=0.

It remains to calculate
\langle{\mathcal{L}}_{+}\Psi,\Psi\rangle
. Since
-\Delta(x\cdot\nabla f)=-x\cdot\nabla\Delta f-2\Delta f
and using the profile equation (
1.2
), we compute

{\mathcal{L}}_{+}(x\cdot\nabla\phi_{\omega})=-2\Delta\phi_{\omega}.

So,

\displaystyle\langle{\mathcal{L}}_{+}\Psi,\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\mathcal{L}}_{+}(x\cdot\nabla\phi_{\omega}),x\cdot\nabla\phi_{\omega}+\phi_{\omega}\rangle+\langle\phi_{\omega},{\mathcal{L}}_{+}(x\cdot\nabla\phi_{\omega})\rangle+\langle{\mathcal{L}}_{+}\phi,\phi\rangle=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\langle\Delta\phi_{\omega},x\cdot\nabla\phi_{\omega}\rangle+4\|\nabla\phi_{\omega}\|^{2}-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}g^{\prime}(\phi_{\omega}^{2})\phi_{\omega}^{4}dx=4\|\nabla\phi_{\omega}\|^{2}-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}g^{\prime}(\phi_{\omega}^{2})\phi_{\omega}^{4}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(4f(\phi_{\omega})\phi_{\omega}-8F(\phi_{\omega})-2g^{\prime}(\phi_{\omega}^{2})\phi_{\omega}^{4}\right)dx=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[e^{4\pi\phi^{2}}\left(8\pi\phi_{\omega}^{4}+\frac{1}{\pi}-4\phi_{\omega}^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{\pi}\right]dx.

where we have used that
\langle\Delta\phi_{\omega},x\cdot\nabla\phi_{\omega}\rangle=0
,
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}\phi_{\omega}=-2\phi_{\omega}^{3}g^{\prime}(\phi^{2})
and the Pohozaev identities (
1.11
) and
(
1.12
). We will show momentarily that the integrand function,

(3.4)

e^{4\pi z^{2}}\left(8\pi z^{4}+\frac{1}{\pi}-4z^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{\pi}\geq 0

whence

\langle{\mathcal{L}}_{+}\Psi,\Psi\rangle<0.

According to Corollary
3.1
, this implies the spectral instability of
\phi_{\omega}
.

It remains to prove (
3.4
). Indeed, it suffices to show that
\chi(x):=8\pi x^{2}+\frac{1}{\pi}-4x-\frac{e^{-4\pi x}}{\pi}\geq 0
for all
x\geq 0
. However, note that
f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)=0
, while
f^{\prime\prime}(x)=16\pi(1-e^{-4\pi x})\geq 0
, whence
f(x)>0
for all
x>0
.

4.
Instability by blow up

Before tackling the strong instability of the standing waves, we need to make sure that the following Cauchy problem:

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}iu_{t}+\triangle u+f_{\mu}(u)=0,\ (t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\\
\\
u(t,0)=u_{0}(x)\end{array}\right.

where
u_{0}
is an initial data in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, has a unique solution for a time
T>0
.

(\ref{3.1})
has been resolved in
[
3
]
. More precisely, the authors have proved the following result.

Lemma 4.1

(Theorem 1.10,
[
3
]
)
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{0}<1
, then there exists a time
T>0
and a unique solution to the Cauchy problem
(\ref{3.1})
in the space
C_{T}(H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
with initial data
u_{0}
. Moreover
u\in L^{4}_{T}(C^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
, where
C^{\alpha}
is the space of
\alpha
-Hölder continuous functions endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{C^{\alpha}}=\|u\|_{\infty}+\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}.

Additionally, if
T^{*}
denote the maximal time, i.e,

T^{*}=\sup\left\{T>0,\ (\ref{3.1})\ \text{has a solution on}\ [0,T]\right\},

we say that a solution blows up at
T^{*}
, if
\lim\limits_{t\nearrow T^{*}}\|\nabla u(t,.)\|^{2}_{2}=1.

We also have the mass and energy conservation for the solutions of (
4.1
), which take the form

(4.2)

M(u(t,.))=M(u_{0}),E_{\mu}(u(t,.))=E(u_{0})

For
v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we define the action functional in the following way:

S_{\mu}(v)=E_{\mu}(v)+\frac{1}{2}M(v)=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|v\|_{2}^{2}-\int F_{\mu}(v)dx.

Note that a ground state as defined by
(1.3)
minimizes the action functional in the following way:

S_{\mu}(\Phi)=\inf\left\{S_{\mu}(v):\ v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\setminus\{0\},\ P_{\mu}(v)=0\right\}.

The following two quantities also play a crucial role in the study of strong instability:

P_{\mu}(v)=\frac{1}{2}\|v\|^{2}_{2}-\int F_{\mu}(v)dx.

and

I_{\mu}(v)=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{2}-\int vf_{\mu}(v)dx=2E_{\mu}(v)-\int vf_{\mu}(v)-4F_{\mu}(v)dx.

Note that
S_{\mu}(\Phi)
can also be rewritten in the following way :

S_{\mu}(\Phi)=\inf\left\{S_{\mu}(v):\ v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\setminus\{0\},\ I_{\mu}(v)=0\right\}.

A virial property has also been shown in
[
4
]
, More precisely, the authors showed that if
u
is a solution to
(\ref{3.1})
, then

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\|xu\|^{2}_{2}=8I_{\mu}(u),\ \ \forall\ t\in[0,T^{*}).

Lastly, we introduce two classical sets that are important in the study of orbital stability as they have the desired invariance with respect to the flow of
(\ref{3.1})
:

K^{-}_{\mu}=\left\{v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\backslash\{0\}:\ S_{\mu}(v)<S_{\mu}(\Phi),\ I_{\mu}(v)<0\right\}

and

K^{+}_{\mu}=\left\{v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\backslash\{0\}:\ S_{\mu}(v)<S_{\mu}(\Phi),\ I_{\mu}(v)>0\right\}

The following lemma provides sufficient conditions for a finite time blow-up.

Lemma 4.2

(Lemma 3.9,
[
4
]
)
.

Let
\mu\in\{0,1\}
and
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be such that
\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}<1
and
E_{\mu}(u_{0})\geq 0.
If

u_{0}\in K^{-}_{\mu},\ \ u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap L^{2}(|x|^{2}dx),

then the corresponding solution to
(\ref{3.1})
blows up in finite time.

The next result, also proved in
[
4
]
, shows that appropriate and close rescales of the ground state satisfy the requirements of Lemma
4.2
and hence provide the instability by blow up claimed in Theorem
1
.

Lemma 4.3

(Lemma 3.13,
[
4
]
)
.

Let
\mu\in\{0,1\}
,
\Phi_{\lambda}(x):=\lambda\Phi(\lambda x)
. There exists
\epsilon>0
, so that for all
\lambda:1<\lambda<1+\epsilon
, the following holds true:

(4.3)

\displaystyle E_{\mu}(\Phi_{\lambda})>0,\ I_{\mu}(\Phi_{\lambda})<0,\ \ S_{\mu}(\Phi_{\lambda})<S_{\mu}(\Phi),

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|\triangledown\Phi_{\lambda}\|^{2}_{2}<1,\ \ \Phi_{\lambda}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap L^{2}(|x|^{2}dx)

Remark:
The precise statement of Lemma 3.13 in
[
4
]
requires a slight modification, but the result quoted here holds true due to the arguments presented there.

We are now ready to complete the proof of the strong instability of the waves
\Phi
. Consider
\Phi_{n}(x):=\lambda_{n}\Phi(\lambda_{n}x)
, for a sequence
\lambda_{n}\to 1+
. Clearly,
\lim_{n}\|\Phi_{n}-\Phi\|_{H^{1}}=0
. Also, the sequence
\Phi_{n}
satisfies all the assumptions in Lemma
4.2
, due to Lemma
4.3
. Therefore, all solutions with initial data
\Phi_{n}
blow up in finite time, whence we conclude the strong instability of the waves
\Phi
.

On off-critical zeros of lattice energies in the neighborhood of the Riemann zeta function
Laurent Bétermin, Ladislav Šamaj, Igor Travěnec

Abstract
The Riemann zeta function ζ ⁡ ( s ) := ∑ n = 1 ∞ 1 / n s \zeta(s):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}1/n^{s} can be interpreted as the energy per point of the lattice ℤ \mathbb{Z} , interacting pairwisely via the Riesz potential 1 / r s 1/r^{s} . Given a parameter Δ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] \Delta\in(0,1] , this physical model is generalized by considering the energy per point E ⁡ ( s , Δ ) E(s,\Delta) of a periodic one-dimensional lattice alternating the distances between the nearest-neighbour particles as 2 / ( 1 + Δ ) 2/(1+\Delta) and 2 ​ Δ / ( 1 + Δ ) 2\Delta/(1+\Delta) , keeping the lattice density equal to one independently of Δ \Delta . This energy trivially satisfies E ⁡ ( s , 1 ) = ζ ⁡ ( s ) E(s,1)=\zeta(s) at Δ = 1 \Delta=1 , it can be easily expressed as a combination of the Riemann and Hurwitz zeta functions, and extended analytically to the punctured s s -plane ℂ ∖ { 1 } \mathbb{C}\setminus\{1\} . In this paper, we perform numerical investigations of the zeros of the energy { ρ = ρ x + i ρ y } \{\rho=\rho_{x}+{\rm i}\rho_{y}\} , which are defined by E ⁡ ( ρ , Δ ) = 0 E(\rho,\Delta)=0 . The numerical results reveal that in the Riemann limit Δ → 1 − \Delta\to 1^{-} theses zeros include the anticipated critical zeros of the Riemann zeta function with ℜ ⁡ ( ρ x ) = 1 2 \Re(\rho_{x})=\frac{1}{2} as well as an unexpected – comparing to the Riemann Hypothesis – infinite series of off-critical zeros. The analytic treatment of these off-critical zeros shows that their imaginary components are equidistant and their real components diverge logarithmically to − ∞ -\infty as Δ → 1 − \Delta\to 1^{-} , i.e., they become invisible at the Riemann’s Δ = 1 \Delta=1 .

2
The generalized one-dimensional model

2.1
Definition of the model

Given
\Delta\in(0,1]
, we consider the infinite set of points

L_{\Delta}\subset\mathbb{R}
given by

L_{\Delta}:=2\mathbb{Z}\cup\left(2\mathbb{Z}+\frac{2\Delta}{1+\Delta}\right),

which is the unit density periodic configuration with alternating distances
2/(1+\Delta)

and
2\Delta/(1+\Delta)
, since
2-2/(1+\Delta)=2\Delta/(1+\Delta)
. Assuming that each pair of points in
L_{\Delta}
interacts via the Riesz potential
1/r^{s}
,
s>1
, the total energy per point of this system is therefore

E(s,\Delta):=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k\in\left\{0,\frac{2\Delta}{1+\Delta}\right\}}\sum_{p\in L_{\Delta}\atop p\neq k}\frac{1}{|p-k|^{s}}.

The following proposition shows how to write this energy in terms of Riemann and Hurwitz zeta functions.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
s>1
and any
\Delta\in(0,1]
, we have

E(s,\Delta)=\frac{1}{2^{s}}\zeta(s)+\frac{1}{2^{s+1}}\left[\zeta\left(s,\frac{1}{1+\Delta}\right)+\zeta\left(s,\frac{\Delta}{1+\Delta}\right)\right].

(3)

Proof.

We simply compute the above double sum as follows:

\displaystyle E(s,\Delta):

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k\in\left\{0,\frac{2\Delta}{1+\Delta}\right\}}\sum_{p\in L_{\Delta}\atop p\neq k}\frac{1}{|p-k|^{s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k\in\left\{0,\frac{2\Delta}{1+\Delta}\right\}}\left(\sum_{p\in 2\mathbb{Z}\atop p\neq k}\frac{1}{|p-k|^{s}}+\sum_{p\in 2\mathbb{Z}+\frac{2\Delta}{1+\Delta}\atop p\neq k}\frac{1}{|p-k|^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\atop n\neq 0}\frac{1}{|2n|^{s}}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\atop 2n\neq\frac{2\Delta}{1+\Delta}}\frac{1}{\left|2n-\frac{2\Delta}{1+\Delta}\right|^{s}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\atop 2n\neq-\frac{2\Delta}{1+\Delta}}\frac{1}{\left|2n+\frac{2\Delta}{1+\Delta}\right|^{s}}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\atop n\neq 0}\frac{1}{|2n|^{s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{s+1}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}\frac{1}{|n|^{s}}+\frac{1}{2^{s+2}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\left|n+\frac{\Delta}{1+\Delta}\right|^{s}}+\frac{1}{2^{s+2}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\left|n-\frac{\Delta}{1+\Delta}\right|^{s}}.

We now split the two last sums in order to get two Hurwitz zeta functions and two rests that we write again in terms of the same Hurwitz zeta functions:

\displaystyle E(s,\Delta):

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{s}}\zeta(s)+\frac{1}{2^{s+2}}\left[\zeta\left(s,\frac{1}{1+\Delta}\right)+\zeta\left(s,\frac{\Delta}{1+\Delta}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2^{s+2}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\left|-n+\frac{\Delta}{1+\Delta}\right|^{s}}+\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\left|-n+\frac{1}{1+\Delta}\right|^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{s}}\zeta(s)+\frac{1}{2^{s+2}}\left[\zeta\left(s,\frac{1}{1+\Delta}\right)+\zeta\left(s,\frac{\Delta}{1+\Delta}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2^{s+2}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\left(n-\frac{\Delta}{1+\Delta}\right)^{s}}+\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\left(n-\frac{1}{1+\Delta}\right)^{s}}\right).

Since we have, by the change of variables
n=k+1
,

\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\left(n-\frac{\Delta}{1+\Delta}\right)^{s}}=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\left(k+\frac{1}{1+\Delta}\right)^{s}}\ \textnormal{and}\ \sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\left(n-\frac{1}{1+\Delta}\right)^{s}}=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\left(k+\frac{\Delta}{1+\Delta}\right)^{s}},

we obtain

\displaystyle E(s,\Delta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{s}}\zeta(s)+\frac{2}{2^{s+2}}\left[\zeta\left(s,\frac{1}{1+\Delta}\right)+\zeta\left(s,\frac{\Delta}{1+\Delta}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{s}}\zeta(s)+\frac{1}{2^{s+1}}\left[\zeta\left(s,\frac{1}{1+\Delta}\right)+\zeta\left(s,\frac{\Delta}{1+\Delta}\right)\right]

and the proof is complete.
∎

Notice that the energy satisfies the required symmetry relation

E(s,\Delta)=E(s,1/\Delta)
.

Remark 2.2

(
Crystallization result
)
.

It is known (see e.g.
[

]
), by a convexity argument
(or by the so-called one-dimensional “Universal Optimality" of

,

\min_{\Delta\in(0,1]}E(s,\Delta)=E(s,1)=\zeta(s),

2.2
The Hurwitz zeta function and the analytic continuation of
E(\cdot,\Delta)

The Hurwitz zeta function (
2
) is a generalization of
the Riemann zeta-function (
1
) via a shift
a>0
.
In particular,

\zeta(s,1)=\zeta(s).

(4)

In the symbolic computer language
Mathematica
, the Riemann
and Hurwitz zeta functions are tabulated under the symbols Zeta[
s
]
and Zeta[

The Hurwitz zeta function satisfies two easily verifiable important equalities,

\forall x\in\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right],\forall s>1,\quad\zeta(s,x)+\zeta(s,1/2+x)=2^{s}\zeta(s,2x),

(5)

and the multiplication theorem

k\in\mathbb{N},\forall s>1,\quad k^{s}\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{k}\zeta\left(s,\frac{n}{k}\right).

(6)

The second relation easily implies that

\zeta(s,1/3)+\zeta(s,2/3)=(3^{s}-1)\zeta(s)

(7)

as well as

\zeta(s,1/2)=(2^{s}-1)\zeta(s).

(8)

From (
5
) with
x=1/4
and (
8
), we therefore obtain

\zeta(s,1/4)+\zeta(s,3/4)=(4^{s}-2^{s})\zeta(s).

(9)

From (
3
) and (
8
), it is also straightforward to check
that
E(s,1)=\zeta(s)
.

Furthermore, it is clear that (
3
) holds for all
s\in\mathbb{C}
such
that
\Re(s)>1
and we get the following result by directly applying
the classical one (see e.g.
[
11
,
15
,
26
]
)
on the analytic continuation of the Riemann and Hurwitz zeta functions.

Proposition 2.3
.

For all
\Delta\in(0,1]
, the function
s\mapsto E(s,\Delta)
has
an analytic continuation on
\mathbb{C}\backslash\{1\}
.
Furthermore, we have, for all
s

\Re(s)<1
and all

\Delta\in(0,1]

\displaystyle\pi^{-\frac{s}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)E(s,\Delta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{s+1}}\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\vartheta\left(\frac{1}{1+\Delta},it\right)-1\right]t^{\frac{-1-s}{2}}{\rm d}t+f(s)\right\},

\displaystyle\vartheta(z,it)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\pi n^{2}t}e^{2i\pi nz}

is the Jacobi theta function defined for
t>0

z\in\mathbb{C}
, and

\displaystyle f(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\vartheta(0,it)-1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\right]t^{\frac{s}{2}-1}{\rm d}t\qquad\mbox{for $0<\Re(s)<1$,}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\vartheta(0,it)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\right]t^{\frac{s}{2}-1}{\rm d}t\qquad\mbox{for $\Re(s)<0$.}

Proof.

Recall that, for
\Delta\in(0,1]
and
\Re(s)>1
, we have

E(s,\Delta)=\frac{1}{2^{s}}\zeta(s)+\frac{1}{2^{s+1}}\left[\zeta\left(s,\frac{1}{1+\Delta}\right)+\zeta\left(s,\frac{\Delta}{1+\Delta}\right)\right].

It has been shown in
[
11
,
15
,
26
]
that

s\mapsto\zeta(s)
and
s\mapsto\zeta(s,a)
,
a>0
, admit an analytic
continuation to
\mathbb{C}\backslash\{1\}
, which implies the same for

s\mapsto E(s,\Delta)
.
Furthermore, writing
z=\frac{1}{1+\Delta}
, we have

E(s,\Delta)=\frac{1}{2^{s}}\zeta(s)+\frac{1}{2^{s+1}}\left[\zeta\left(s,z\right)+\zeta\left(s,1-z\right)\right].

Considering the analytic continuation of
\zeta(1-\alpha,a)
,
a>0
,
the following formula is well-known
[
11
,
15
]
for all

\alpha
such that
\Re(\alpha)>0
:

\pi^{-\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\right)\left[\zeta\left(1-\alpha,z\right)+\zeta\left(1-\alpha,1-z\right)\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}[\vartheta(z,it)-1]t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}

(10)

holding for
z\notin\mathbb{Z}
(see the remark in Theorem 12.6 on page 257
of
[
1
]
).
Replacing
\alpha
by
s=1-\alpha
(so that
\Re(s)<1
for
\Re(\alpha)>0
),
we write

\displaystyle\pi^{-\frac{s}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)E(s,\Delta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{-\frac{s}{2}}}{2^{s}}\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\zeta(s)+\frac{\pi^{-\frac{s}{2}}}{2^{s+1}}\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\left[\zeta\left(s,z\right)+\zeta\left(s,1-z\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi^{-\frac{s}{2}}}{2^{s+1}}\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\zeta(s)+\frac{\pi^{-\frac{s}{2}}}{2^{s+1}}\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\left[\zeta\left(s,z\right)+\zeta\left(s,1-z\right)\right].

Next we replace

\pi^{-\frac{s}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\left[\zeta\left(s,z\right)+\zeta\left(s,1-z\right)\right]

by the integral given in (
10
) and

2\pi^{-\frac{s}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\zeta(s)

by the

integral in Eq. (21) of
[

]
for

and
by the

[
26
]
for

to complete the proof.
∎

Therefore, we can consider the zeros of

in

2.3
Factorization and zeros of the energy for special values of
\Delta

There exist special values of
\Delta
for which the energy
E(s,\Delta)

factorizes itself into a product of the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
and
some simple functions of
s
, by using the previously presented relations
(
5
) and (
7
).
For these cases, both critical and off-critical zeros can be found easily.
The most obvious choice of
\Delta
is
\Delta=1
for which we have

E(s,1)=\zeta(s).

(11)

In the cases
\Delta\in\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{3}\right\}
we have the following result giving the zeros of
E(s,\Delta)
as well as the factorization of the energy.

Proposition 2.4
.

For all
s\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{1\}
, we have

E(s,1/2)=\frac{1}{2^{s+1}}(1+3^{s})\zeta(s)\quad\textnormal{and}\quad E(s,1/3)=\frac{1}{2^{s+1}}(2-2^{s}+4^{s})\zeta(s).

(12)

Furthermore, the zeros of
E(s,1/2)
and
E(s,1/3)
are

\displaystyle Z_{\frac{1}{2}}=Z_{1}\cup\left\{\frac{(2k+1){\rm i}\pi}{\ln 3}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}},

\displaystyle Z_{\frac{1}{3}}=Z_{1}\cup\left\{\frac{1}{\ln{2}}\left[\ln\left(\frac{1\pm{\rm i}\sqrt{7}}{2}\right)+2\pi{\rm i}k\right]\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}.

Proof.

For
\displaystyle\Delta=\frac{1}{2}
, one has

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+\Delta}=\frac{2}{3}
and

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta}{1+\Delta}=\frac{1}{3}
and therefore,
using (
7
), it holds that

E(s,1/2)=\frac{1}{2^{s+1}}(1+3^{s})\zeta(s).

(13)

where the function
1+3^{s}
yields an infinite sequence of (purely imaginary)
off-critical zeros

\rho_{k}=\frac{(2k+1){\rm i}\pi}{\ln{3}},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

(14)

Furthermore, for
\displaystyle\Delta=\frac{1}{3}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+\Delta}=\frac{3}{4}
and

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta}{1+\Delta}=\frac{1}{4}
and therefore,
applying (
9
), it holds that

E(s,1/3)=\frac{1}{2^{s+1}}(2-2^{s}+4^{s})\zeta(s).

(15)

The function
2-2^{s}+4^{s}
yields the following zeros

\rho_{k}=\frac{1}{\ln{2}}\left[\ln\left(\frac{1\pm{\rm i}\sqrt{7}}{2}\right)+2\pi{\rm i}k\right],\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

(16)

∎

.

\Delta=1/3

4
Analytic results in the limit
\Delta\to 1^{-}

Approaching
\Delta\to 1^{-}
, with regard to Eq. (
11
) one anticipates
the presence of critical zeros of the Riemann zeta function for
E(s,\Delta)
.
Surprisingly, as was already indicated, there are also additional curves of
off-critical zeros.

To derive coordinates of these off-critical zeros, we set

\Delta=1-\varepsilon
in (
3
) and expand the energy in Taylor series
in the small positive
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
up to the order
\varepsilon^{5}
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{1\}
, then, as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
,

\displaystyle E(s,1-\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\zeta(s)+\frac{2^{2+s}-1}{2^{5+s}}s(1+s)\zeta(2+s)\left(\varepsilon^{2}+\varepsilon^{3}+\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon^{4}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{5}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{3}\frac{2^{4+s}-1}{2^{11+s}}s(1+s)(2+s)(3+s)\zeta(4+s)\left(\varepsilon^{4}+2\varepsilon^{5}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{6}).

Proof.

It directly follows from the Taylor expansion of the Hurwitz zeta function
(see e.g.
[
27
]
): for
|a|<1
,

\zeta(s,a)=\frac{1}{a^{s}}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-a)^{n}\binom{s+n-1}{n}\zeta(s+n),

(18)

where the binomial coefficient for a complex
s
has to be understood as

\binom{s+n-1}{n}=\frac{s(s+1)(s+2)\cdots(s+n-1)}{n!},

(19)

as well as the analytic continuation of
s\mapsto E(s,\Delta)
.
∎

It is clear that in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
the zeros of

E(s,1-\varepsilon)
coincide trivially with the critical ones
of the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
.
Let us compute the
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
asymptotics of the other
nontrivial zeros.

Theorem 4.7
.

The nontrivial off-critical zeros of
E(s,1-\varepsilon)
are given, as

\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, by
\{\rho(k)=\rho_{x}(k)+{\rm i}\rho_{y}(k)\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}

where

\displaystyle\rho_{x}(k)=\frac{2}{\ln{2}}\ln\varepsilon+\left(-3+\frac{2}{\ln 2}\ln\pi\right)+\frac{1}{\ln 2}\varepsilon

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\ln 2}\left(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{7\pi^{2}}{24}\right)\varepsilon^{2}+\frac{1}{\ln 2}\left(\frac{1}{12}+\frac{7\pi^{2}}{24}\right)\varepsilon^{3}

\displaystyle+\frac{8}{3\ln 2}\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}\right)^{\frac{\ln 3}{\ln 2}}\cos\left[\frac{\ln 3}{\ln{2}}(2k+1)\pi\right]\varepsilon^{2\frac{\ln 3}{\ln 2}}+o\left(\varepsilon^{2\frac{\ln 3}{\ln 2}}\right),

(20)

\displaystyle\rho_{y}(k)=\frac{1}{\ln{2}}(2k+1)\pi+\frac{8}{3\ln 2}\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}\right)^{\frac{\ln 3}{\ln 2}}\sin\left[\frac{\ln 3}{\ln{2}}(2k+1)\pi\right]\varepsilon^{2\frac{\ln 3}{\ln 2}}

(21)

\displaystyle+o\left(\varepsilon^{2\frac{\ln 3}{\ln 2}}\right).

(22)

In particular,

1.

Vanishing of off-critical zeros: we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\rho_{x}(k)=-\infty
;

2.

Asymptotic crystallization of their imaginary parts: at first order,
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, the imaginary parts of off-critical zeros are
equidistributed on the lattice
\displaystyle\frac{(2\mathbb{Z}+1)\pi}{\ln 2}
.

Proof.

The other nontrivial zeros
\{\rho\}
, besides the one of the Riemann
zeta function, correspond to solutions of the equation

\displaystyle 2^{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-2^{2+\rho}}{2^{5}}\frac{\rho(1+\rho)\zeta(\rho+2)}{\zeta(\rho)}\left(\varepsilon^{2}+\varepsilon^{3}+\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon^{4}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{5}\right)

(23)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{3}\frac{1-2^{4+\rho}}{2^{11}}\frac{\rho(1+\rho)(2+\rho)(3+\rho)(4+\rho)\zeta(4+\rho)}{\zeta(\rho)}\left(\varepsilon^{4}+2\varepsilon^{5}\right)

\displaystyle+O(\varepsilon^{6}).

As will be showed later, the component
\rho_{x}
of
\rho=\rho_{x}+{\rm i}\rho_{y}

goes to
-\infty
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.
To simplify our computations, one applies the well known duality transformation

\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\zeta(s)=\pi^{(s-1)/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1-s}{2}\right)\zeta(1-s)

(24)

to each Riemann zeta function in (
23
).
Using then the formula
\Gamma(x+1)=x\Gamma(x)
,
one ends up with the result

\displaystyle 2^{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1-2^{2+\rho}}{2^{3}}\pi^{2}\frac{\zeta(-1-\rho)}{\zeta(1-\rho)}\left(\varepsilon^{2}+\varepsilon^{3}+\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon^{4}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{5}\right)

(25)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{3}\frac{1-2^{4+\rho}}{2^{7}}\pi^{4}\frac{\zeta(-3-\rho)}{\zeta(1-\rho)}\left(\varepsilon^{4}+2\varepsilon^{5}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{6}).

In the limit
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, the r.h.s. of this equation vanishes
and, consequently, the component
\rho_{x}
of
\rho=\rho_{x}+{\rm i}\rho_{y}

must go to
-\infty
as indicated before.
In the limit
\rho_{x}\to-\infty
, the ratios of Riemann zeta functions
in (
25
) can be expanded as follows

\frac{\zeta(-1-\rho)}{\zeta(1-\rho)}=\frac{1+2^{\rho+1}+3^{\rho+1}+\sum_{k\geq 4}k^{\rho+1}}{1+2^{\rho-1}+3^{\rho-1}+\sum_{k\geq 4}k^{\rho-1}}=1+\frac{3}{2}2^{\rho}+\frac{8}{3}3^{\rho}+O(4^{\rho})

(26)

and

\frac{\zeta(-3-\rho)}{\zeta(1-\rho)}=\frac{1+2^{\rho+3}+3^{\rho+3}+\sum_{k\geq 4}k^{\rho+3}}{1+2^{\rho-1}+3^{\rho-1}+\sum_{k\geq 4}k^{\rho-1}}=1+\frac{15}{2}2^{\rho}+O(3^{\rho}).

(27)

In the leading order of the smallness parameter
\varepsilon
, it holds that

2^{\rho_{x}+{\rm i}\rho_{x}}=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}\varepsilon^{2}+o(\varepsilon^{2}),

(28)

Since the right-hand side of this equation is real and negative,
the leading order of the
\rho_{y}
-component is given by

2^{{\rm i}\rho_{y}}=-1+o(1)
, or, equivalently,

\rho_{y}(k)=\frac{1}{\ln{2}}(2k+1)\pi+o(1),\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

(29)

This means that in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
there exists an infinite
sequence of equidistant zero components along the
\rho_{y}
axis.
As follows from (
28
), the
x
-component of these zeros diverges
logarithmically as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
:

\rho_{x}(k)=\frac{2}{\ln{2}}\ln\varepsilon+\left(-3+\frac{2}{\ln 2}\ln\pi\right)+O(\varepsilon).

(30)

Note that the leading terms are the same for any value of
k
.
This behavior can be seen in Figure
2
.

Higher orders of the expansion of
\rho_{y}(k)
and
\rho_{x}(k)

in
\varepsilon
can be obtained by inserting the leading order expressions
(
29
) and (
30
) directly into the basic relation (
25
).
Performing the expansion procedure in
\varepsilon
it is important to
realize that

3^{s}=\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}\varepsilon^{2}\right)^{\frac{\ln 3}{\ln 2}}\exp\left[{\rm i}\frac{\ln 3}{\ln 2}(2k+1)\pi\right]+o\left(\varepsilon^{2\frac{\ln 3}{\ln 2}}\right)

(31)

is of order
2\ln 3/\ln 2\approx 3.17>3
.
After simple algebra one obtains the desired asymptotics for
\rho_{x}(k)

and
\rho_{y}(k)
.
∎

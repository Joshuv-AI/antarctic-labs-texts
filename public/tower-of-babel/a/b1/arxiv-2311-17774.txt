Enumeration of Minimum Weight Codewords of Pre-Transformed Polar Codes by Tree Intersection This work is supported by the German Federal Ministry of Education and Research (BMBF) within the project Open6GHub (grant no. 16KISK019).
Andreas Zunker, Marvin Geiselhart and Stephan ten Brink

Abstract
\Acp PTPC form a class of codes that perform close to the finite-length capacity bounds. The minimum distance and the number of minimum weight codewords are two decisive properties for their performance. In this work, we propose an efficient algorithm for determining the number of minimum weight codewords of general PTPC that eliminates all redundant visits to nodes of the search tree, thus reducing the computational complexity typically by several orders of magnitude compared to state-of-the-art algorithms. This reduction in complexity allows, for the first time, the minimum distance properties to be directly considered in the code design of PTPC . The algorithm is demonstrated for randomly pre-transformed RM ( RM ) codes and PAC ( PAC ) codes. Furthermore, we design optimal polynomials for PAC codes with this algorithm, minimizing the number of minimum weight codewords.

I
Introduction

While asymptotically achieving the channel capacity
[
1
]
, the performance of polar codes in the finite block length regime remains limited by their suboptimal achievable weight spectrum, i.e., the minimum distance
\dmin
and the large number of minimum weight codewords
A_{\dmin}
, which are the main source of non-correctable errors.
A key advancement to improve the distance spectrum is the removal of low-weight codewords through the introduction of precoding. The resulting code is a polar subcode
[
2
]
and will be referred to as a
PTPC
hereafter.
Proper precoder design results in very powerful coding schemes that can be decoded by
SCL
(
SCL
) with little additional complexity overhead.
Several precoders have been proposed in the past:
In
[
3
]
,
CRC
(
CRC
) codes have been suggested as precoders to prevent incorrect decisions of the
SCL
decoder. Similarly, the authors of
[
2
]
propose parity checks that can be seen as dynamic frozen bits.
Outer convolutional codes have been proposed in
[
4
]
, resulting in so-called
PAC
codes that show performance close to the finite block length bounds.

These advancements went hand in hand with the development of algorithms for counting minimum weight codewords of polar codes and
PTPC
.
The statistical method proposed in
[
5
]
investigates the improvement of the distance spectrum by ensembles of random precoders. This method is extended to an asymptotic analysis in
[
6
]
.
In contrast, deterministic/exact methods analyze a concrete realization of a plain or pre-transformed polar code.
For plain polar codes,
[
7
]
proposes to use symmetries (permutations) of polar codes to generate all minimum weight codewords from minimum weight generators. While of low complexity, the algorithm is limited to polar codes following the partial order.
This method is extended to obtain the full weight distribution of polar codes up to length 128
[
8
]
.

[
9
]
proposes to count minimum weight codewords by finding combinations of rows that generate them.
Although originally designed for plain polar codes and related to
[
7
]
as shown in
[
10
]
, this method is also applicable to
PTPC
such as
PAC
codes.
While more complex, algorithms for more terms of the weight distribution are proposed in
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
.
Due to the unfavorable scaling of these algorithms, however, it was not yet feasible to compute the number of minimum weight codewords for long polar codes
[
6
]
.
Our contributions are:

•

We propose an improved algorithm for counting the minimum weight codewords of
PTPC
.
Compared to
[
9
]
, the proposed algorithm eliminates all redundant visits to each coset in the search tree and is therefore significantly less complex.
Moreover, the proposed algorithm supports arbitrary upper-triangular precoding rather than only convolutional precoding.

•

We find bounds on the minimum distance
\dmin
and the number of minimum weight codewords
\Admin
of a
PTPC
based on its rate-profile.

•

We demonstrate our low-complexity weight-enumeration algorithm by providing numerical results for very long codes (such as pre-transformed
\RM(10,21)
codes with
{N=2097152}
), for which only probabilistic estimates existed
[
6
]
, and list optimal polynomials for
PAC
codes.

(

]

P^{\mathrm{ML}}_{\mathrm{FE}}\leq P^{\mathrm{UB}}_{\mathrm{FE}}=\sum\limits_{w}A_{w}\cdot Q\left(\sqrt{2w\cdot R\cdot\frac{E_{\mathrm{b}}}{N_{0}}}\right),

(1)

II-C

Polar Codes

Polar codes are constructed on the basis of the
n
-fold application of the polar transform, resulting in polarized synthetic bit channels
[
1
]
. Reliable bit channels, denoted by the information set
\mathcal{I}
, are used for information transmission, while the remaining unreliable bit channels
\mathcal{F}=\mathbb{Z}_{N}\setminus\mathcal{I}
(frozen set) are set to zero.
The generator matrix
\boldsymbol{G}=(\boldsymbol{G}_{N})_{\mathcal{I}}
of an
(N,K)
polar code
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I})
is formed by those
K
rows of the matrix
\boldsymbol{G}_{N}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{bmatrix}^{\otimes n}
indicated by
\mathcal{I}
, where
(\cdot)^{\otimes n}
denotes the
n
-th Kronecker power and
N=2^{n}
.
Let
\boldsymbol{m}=\boldsymbol{u}_{\mathcal{I}}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{K}
denote the message and
\boldsymbol{u}_{\mathcal{F}}=\boldsymbol{0}
the frozen bits. Then, polar encoding can be written as
\boldsymbol{c}=\boldsymbol{m}\cdot\boldsymbol{G}=\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{G}_{N}
.

Although the optimal code design
\mathcal{I}
depends on the transmission channel, some synthetic bit channels are always more reliable than others
[
7
]
.
Thus, the bit channels exhibit a
partial order
, in which
i\preccurlyeq j
denotes that channel
j
is at least as reliable as channel
i
, defined by:

1.

Left swap
: If
i_{(l)}>j_{(l)}
, and
i_{(l+1)}<j_{(l+1)}
for
{l\in\mathbb{Z}_{n-1}}
, while all other
i_{(l^{\prime})}=j_{(l^{\prime})}
,
l^{\prime}\notin\{l,l+1\}
, then
i\preccurlyeq j
.

2.

Binary domination
: If
i_{(l)}\leq j_{(l)}
for all
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, then
i\preccurlyeq j
.

All remaining relations are derived from transitivity.
A polar code
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I})
is called a
decreasing monomial code
and said to comply with the partial order “
\preccurlyeq
” if and only if

\mathcal{I}
fulfills the partial order as
\forall i\in\mathcal{I},\;\forall j\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}
with
i\preccurlyeq j\implies j\in\mathcal{I}
.

RM
codes are a special case of decreasing monomial codes.
The
RM
code of order
r
and length
{N=2^{n}}
, denoted by
\RM(r,n)
, is exactly the plain polar code
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I})
with information set
{\mathcal{I}=\left\{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}\,\middle|\,\w(i)\geq n-r\right\}}
. An
\RM(r,n)
code has minimum distance
{d_{\mathrm{min}}=2^{n-r}}
.

III
Counting Minimum Weight Codewords of Pre-Transformed Polar Codes

Our goal is to find the minimum distance
\dmin
and the corresponding number of
\dmin
-weight codewords
\Admin
of a
PTPC

\C
.
For this, we will exploit the underlying structure of polar codes.
Thus, our method is best suited for
PTPC

\C
with polar-like rate-profile
\mathcal{I}
and upper-triangular pre-transformation matrix
\boldsymbol{T}
.
To simplify the problem, the general approach is to split the code
\C=\{\boldsymbol{0}\}\cup\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\Ci
into
K=|\mathcal{I}|

{\Ci=\left\{\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{G}_{N}\,\middle|\,\boldsymbol{u}\in\Qi\right\}}
which
are obtained by the one-to-one polar transform mapping
\boldsymbol{G}_{N}
of the pre-transformed messages

\Qi=\Big\{\boldsymbol{t}_{i}\oplus\bigoplus_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\boldsymbol{t}_{h}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{i+1}\Big\}.

In other words,
\Ci
contains all codewords whose corresponding messages
\Qi
have their first non-zero bit in position
i
.
The row
\boldsymbol{g}_{i}
is called the
coset leader
of
\Ci
.
Instead of examining the whole code at once, each coset can be considered individually.
We subsequently denote
plain
(i.e., without pre-transform) and
universal
(i.e., without considering a specific rate-profile) polar cosets by
{\mathcal{P}_{i}(\mathcal{I})=\mathcal{P}_{i}(\mathcal{I},\boldsymbol{T}=\boldsymbol{I}_{N})}
and
\mathcal{P}_{i}=\mathcal{P}_{i}(\mathcal{I}=\mathbb{Z}_{N})
, respectively.
Their corresponding messages are denoted by
\mathcal{Q}_{i}(\mathcal{I})=\mathcal{Q}_{i}(\mathcal{I},\boldsymbol{I}_{N})
and
\mathcal{Q}_{i}=\mathcal{Q}_{i}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
.

III-A

Minimum Distance of
PTPC

To find the number of
\dmin
-weight codewords, the minimum distance must be considered first.
For this, we review a lower bound on the minimum distance
\dmin(\C)
of a
PTPC
.
As shown in
[
10
]
, any codeword in a universal coset
\mathcal{P}_{i}
has at least the Hamming weight of the coset leader
\boldsymbol{g}_{i}
, i.e.,

\w\Big(\boldsymbol{g}_{i}\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\boldsymbol{g}_{h}\Big)\geq\w(\boldsymbol{g}_{i}),

(2)

for all
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{i+1:N}
.
From
\mathcal{P}_{i}(\mathcal{I})\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{i}
and
\boldsymbol{g}_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{i}(\mathcal{I})
follows that the minimum weight of the codewords of
\mathcal{P}_{i}(\mathcal{I})
is
\w(\boldsymbol{g}_{i})
.
The Hamming weight of a row
\boldsymbol{g}_{i}
can be efficiently calculated as
{\w(\boldsymbol{g}_{i})=2^{\w(i)}}

[
10
]
.
Consequently, the minimum distance of a plain polar code
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I})
is found as

\dmin(\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I}))=\min_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\left\{\w(\boldsymbol{g}_{i})\right\}=\min_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\big\{2^{\w(i)}\big\}.

For an upper-triangular pre-transformation matrix
\boldsymbol{T}
follows
\Ci\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{i}
.
Therefore, we find with (
2
) that the codewords of
\Ci
have at least the Hamming weight
\w(\boldsymbol{g}_{i})
of the coset leader
\boldsymbol{g}_{i}
.
Hence, a lower bound on the minimum distance of
PTPC
can be derived as

\dmin(\C)\geq\dmin(\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I}))=\wmin.

(3)

In the following, the lowest potential codeword weight
\wmin
is a constant dependent only on the (fixed) rate-profile
\mathcal{I}
of the given
PTPC

\C
.

III-B

Minimum Weight Codewords of
PTPC

Following the lower bound in (
3
), two cases occur for the minimum distance
\dmin
of a
PTPC

\C
:

1.

\dmin(\C)=\wmin
:

The minimum distance is equal to the lower bound and thus
\Awmin(\C)>0
.
We consider this case and propose an enumeration method that counts the codewords with weight
\wmin
of a
PTPC

\C
.

2.

\dmin(\C)>\wmin
:

The minimum distance is greater than the lower bound, as all
\wmin
-weight codewords have been eliminated by the pre-transform
\boldsymbol{T}
.
In this case, the proposed algorithm will just find
\Awmin(\C)=0
.
Consequently, more general methods as introduced in
[
11
]
or
[
12
]
have to be applied to find
\dmin
and
\Admin
of
\C
.

Our method is based on the construction of
\wmin
-weight codewords of polar codes introduced in
[
10
]
.
Following this formalism, the enumeration algorithm proposed in
[
9
]
counts the codewords of weight
\wmin
of
PAC
codes.
We will generalize this method to arbitrary
PTPC
and reduce the complexity by eliminating all redundant operations.
From
{\Ci\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{i}}
and (
2
) follows that only cosets with coset index

i\in\mathcal{I}_{\wmin}=\left\{j\in\mathcal{I}\,\middle|\,\w(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})=\wmin\right\}

can contain codewords of weight
\wmin
.
Hence, only these cosets or the respective message sets which generate them have to be considered for the computation of
A_{\wmin}(\C)
.
The set of (pre-transformed) messages that form the
\wmin
-weight codewords
\mathcal{P}_{i,\wmin}(\mathcal{I},\boldsymbol{T})
of a coset
\Ci
is defined as

\mathcal{Q}_{i,\wmin}(\mathcal{I},\boldsymbol{T})=\left\{\boldsymbol{u}\in\Qi\,\middle|\,\w(\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{G}_{N})=\wmin\right\}.

Consequently,
the number of
\wmin
-weight codewords of a
PTPC
can be found as

A_{\wmin}(\C)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\wmin}}\left|\mathcal{Q}_{i,\wmin}(\mathcal{I},\boldsymbol{T})\right|.

(4)

The formalism introduced in
[
10
]
allows to find all messages
{\mathcal{Q}_{i,\wmin}=\mathcal{Q}_{i,\wmin}(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\boldsymbol{I}_{N})}
that form the codewords with weight
\wmin=\w(\boldsymbol{g}_{i})
of a universal polar coset
\mathcal{P}_{i}
.
Since both
\mathcal{Q}_{i,\wmin}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{i}
and
\Qi\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{i}
, we can find the messages that generate the
\wmin
-weight codewords of a polar coset
\Ci
by the intersection of the two considered message sets as

\mathcal{Q}_{i,\wmin}(\mathcal{I},\boldsymbol{T})=\Qi\cap\mathcal{Q}_{i,\wmin}.

The set of “1” bit positions
\operatorname{supp}(\boldsymbol{u})
of a message
\boldsymbol{u}
directly corresponds to the row combination
\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{G}_{N}=\bigoplus_{h\in\operatorname{supp}(\boldsymbol{u})}\boldsymbol{g}_{h}
.
The construction of codewords of a universal polar coset
\mathcal{P}_{i}
with weight

\w(\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{G}_{N})=\w\Big(\underbrace{\boldsymbol{g}_{i}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{coset}\\
\text{leader}\end{subarray}}\oplus\underbrace{\bigoplus\limits_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\boldsymbol{g}_{j}}_{\text{core rows}}\oplus\underbrace{\bigoplus\limits_{m\in\mathcal{M}_{i}(\mathcal{J})}\boldsymbol{g}_{m}}_{\text{balancing rows}}\Big)=\w(\boldsymbol{g}_{i})

can be formulated by the combination of the coset leader
\boldsymbol{g}_{i}
with a set of
core rows

\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{i}
and dependent

\mathcal{M}_{i}(\mathcal{J})\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}
.
Thus, the set of “1” bit positions of a message
\boldsymbol{u}
that forms a codeword of weight
\wmin=\w(\boldsymbol{g}_{i})
of
\mathcal{P}_{i}
is

\operatorname{supp}(\boldsymbol{u}(i,\mathcal{J}))=\mathcal{U}_{i}(\mathcal{J})=\{i\}\cup\mathcal{J}\cup\mathcal{M}_{i}(\mathcal{J}).

(5)

Hence, the set of all messages
\boldsymbol{u}
that form the
\wmin
-weight codewords of a universal polar coset
\mathcal{P}_{i}
is

\mathcal{Q}_{i,\wmin}=\mathcal{Q}_{i,\wmin}(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\boldsymbol{I}_{N})=\left\{\boldsymbol{u}(i,\mathcal{J})\,\middle|\,\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{i}\right\}.

The core rows
\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{i}
are found as subsets of the (subsequent) rows that individually do not increase the weight of the coset leader
\boldsymbol{g}_{i}
as

\mathcal{K}_{i}=\left\{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{i+1:N}\,\middle|\,\w(\boldsymbol{g}_{i}\oplus\boldsymbol{g}_{j})=\w(\boldsymbol{g}_{i})\right\}.

(6)

The corresponding complementary set is
\mathcal{K}_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}=\mathbb{Z}_{i+1:N}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{i}
.
As shown in
[
10
]
, the Hamming weight of the sum of two rows can be computed as

\w(\boldsymbol{g}_{i}\oplus\boldsymbol{g}_{j})=2^{\w(i)}+2^{\w(j)}-2^{\w(i\land j)+1}.

(7)

For
i<j
,
\w(j)-\w(i~\land~j)=\w(\bar{i}~\land~j)
is fulfilled.
Thus, with (
7
) we find for
i<j
that

\begin{split}j\in\mathcal{K}_{i}\Leftrightarrow\w(\boldsymbol{g}_{i}\oplus\boldsymbol{g}_{j})=\w(\boldsymbol{g}_{i})&\Leftrightarrow\w(\bar{i}\land j)=1,\\
j\in\mathcal{K}_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}\Leftrightarrow\w(\boldsymbol{g}_{i}\oplus\boldsymbol{g}_{j})>\w(\boldsymbol{g}_{i})&\Leftrightarrow\w(\bar{i}\land j)>1,\end{split}

(8)

efficiently checking whether
j\in\mathcal{K}_{i}
or
j\in\mathcal{K}_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}
.
As each core row-combination
\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{i}
forms one
\wmin
-weight codeword, the number of these codewords in
\mathcal{P}_{i}
is

\left|\mathcal{Q}_{i,\wmin}\right|=\sum_{j=0}^{\left|\mathcal{K}_{i}\right|}\binom{\left|\mathcal{K}_{i}\right|}{j}=2^{\left|\mathcal{K}_{i}\right|}.

(9)

In addition to a set of core rows
\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}
, the balancing rows
{\mathcal{M}_{i}(\mathcal{J})\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}}
are required to obtain a
\wmin
-weight codeword.
Following
[
9
, Algorithm 2]
, the balancing row set

Our approach is to represent the message sets

A

Definition 1

(
DBT
(

A

1.

\mathcal{L}_{1}

All nodes do not have a sibling.

2.

The value of each node is either “0” or “1”, where a left child node is always “0” and a right child node is always “1”. If all leaf nodes are at the last level, the tree is called a

A set of binary vectors

IV
Theoretical Findings

If we find
\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{K}_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}=\emptyset
for a coset
\Ci
where
i\in\mathcal{I}_{\wmin}
, it follows that
f_{i}^{*}(\mathcal{I})=i
and thus
\mathcal{K}_{i}^{\circ}(\mathcal{I})=\mathcal{K}_{i}
.
Consequently, from (
16
) we can derive that
\left|\Qiwmin\right|=2^{\left|\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{K}_{i}\right|}
, meaning the number of
\wmin
-weight codewords of coset
\Ci
is independent of
\boldsymbol{T}
, and therefore
\Awmin(\Ci)=\Awmin(\mathcal{P}_{i}(\mathcal{I}))
.
Subsequently, as introduced in
[
9
]
, we distinguish two types of cosets with indices
i\in\mathcal{I}_{\wmin}
, based on whether
\Awmin(\Ci)
can be reduced by choosing a suitable pre-transformation
\boldsymbol{T}
:

\Ci\mathrel{\widehat{=}}\begin{cases}\emph{``pre-transformable"}&\text{
if }\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{K}_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}\neq\emptyset\\
\emph{``nonpre-transformable"}&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Correspondingly, the set of indices of pre-transformable cosets is
\mathcal{I}^{*}_{\wmin}=\left\{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\wmin}\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{K}_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}\neq\emptyset\right\}
and of non pre-transformable cosets is
\mathcal{I}^{\circ}_{\wmin}=\mathcal{I}_{\wmin}\setminus\mathcal{I}^{*}_{\wmin}
.
Using this classification and the previous findings, we can characterize a
PTPC

\C
based on the chosen rate-profile
\mathcal{I}
.

Firstly, we consider the minimum distance
\dmin
of a
PTPC

\C
with decreasing and with
\RM(r,n)
rate-profile
\mathcal{I}
.

Theorem 1
.

A pre-transformed polar code
\C
with a decreasing rate-profile
\mathcal{I}
has the same minimum distance as the corresponding plain polar code
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I})
.

Proof.

As
\mathcal{I}
is decreasing, from the
left swap
rule follows that
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I})
includes the most reliable
\wmin
-weight row
\boldsymbol{g}_{i}
with index
{i=\max\left(\mathcal{I}_{\wmin}\right)=N-\nicefrac{{N}}{{\wmin}}}

as
i\succcurlyeq j\in\mathcal{I}_{\wmin}
.
Subsequently, we find
\mathbb{Z}_{i:N}=\left\{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}\,\middle|\,i\preccurlyeq j\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{I}
by
following the
binary domination
rule.
Thus, there are no frozen rows
\boldsymbol{g}_{f}
with
f>i
to add to
\boldsymbol{g}_{i}
, implying that
\boldsymbol{g}_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I},\boldsymbol{T})
.
With the lower bound (
3
), it follows that
\dmin(\C)=\dmin(\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I}))=\wmin
.
∎

Corollary 1
.

A pre-transformed polar code
\C
with an
\RM(r,n)
rate-profile
\mathcal{I}
has minimum distance
\dmin=2^{n-r}
.

Proof.

From the
\RM(r,n)
rate-profile
\mathcal{I}
follows that
\mathcal{I}
is decreasing and that
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I})=\RM(r,n)
.
Thus, with Theorem
1
we find
\dmin\left(\C\right)=\dmin\left(\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I})\right)=2^{n-r}
.
∎

Secondly, we examine the number of minimum weight codewords
\Awmin
of a
PTPC

\C
with arbitrary and with
\RM(r,n)
rate-profile
\mathcal{I}
.

Lemma 3
.

(
[
9
, Lemma 3]
)
The number of
\wmin
-weight codewords of a pre-transformed polar code
\C
with arbitrary rate-profile
\mathcal{I}
is lower bounded as

\Awmin(\C)\geq A_{\wmin}^{\mathrm{LB}}(\mathcal{I})=\sum\limits_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\wmin}^{\circ}}2^{\left|\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{K}_{i}\right|}.

Proof.

As we saw above, for a cosets with index
i\in\mathcal{I}^{\circ}_{\wmin}
holds
{\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{K}_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}=\emptyset}
and thus
\left|\Qiwmin\right|=2^{\left|\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{K}_{i}\right|}
. By only considering these non pre-transformable cosets in (
4
), we obtain a lower bound on
\Awmin(\C)
.
∎

For an
\RM(r,n)
rate-profile
\mathcal{I}
we can find a closed form solution of the lower bound on the number of
\wmin
-weight codewords of a
PTPC

\C
given in Lemma
3
.
First, we consider which cosets are non pre-transformable.

Lemma 4
.

A coset
\Ci
with
\RM(r,n)
rate-profile
\mathcal{I}
of order
r\leq n-2
is non pre-transformable if the support of its index
i\in\mathcal{I}_{\dmin}
is
\mathcal{S}_{i}=\{x,y\}\cup\mathbb{Z}_{r+2:n}
, where
0\leq x<y<r-2
.

Proof.

Following the
\RM(r,n)
rate-profile
\mathcal{I}
, for
f\in\mathcal{F}
holds
\w(f)<n-r
.
To fulfill
f>i
, the support of
f
is found as
\mathcal{S}_{f}=\{z\}\cup\mathbb{Z}_{r+2:n}
with
z>y
.
Subsequently,
\mathcal{S}_{f}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{i}=\{z\}
and thus
\w\left(\bar{i}\land f\right)=1
, implying that
\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{K}_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}=\emptyset
.
By definition,
\Ci
is non pre-transformable.
∎

Knowing which cosets are non pre-transformable, we can now find a closed form lower bound on
\Admin(\C)
for an
\RM(r,n)
rate-profile
\mathcal{I}
.

Theorem 2
.

The number of codewords with weight
\dmin=2^{n-r}
of a pre-transformed polar code
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I},\,\boldsymbol{T})
with
\RM(r,n)
rate-profile
\mathcal{I}
of order
r\leq n-2
is lower bounded as

A_{\dmin}^{\mathrm{LB}}(r)=\frac{8\cdot 2^{3r}-6\cdot 2^{2r}+2^{r}}{3}.

(17)

Proof.

From Corollary
1
we know that
\dmin(\C)=2^{n-r}
for an
\RM(r,n)
rate-profile
\mathcal{I}
.
Following Lemma
4
, for order
r\leq n-2
,
cosets having an index support
\mathcal{S}_{i}=\{x,y\}\cup\mathbb{Z}_{r+2:n}
, where
0\leq x<y<r+2
, are non pre-transformable.
As we have determined above,
\Admin(\Ci)=2^{\left|\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{K}_{i}\right|}
holds for these cosets
i\in\mathcal{I}_{\smash{\dmin}}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{\smash{\dmin}}^{\circ}
.
As shown in
[
9
]
, for a decreasing (and thus also for
RM
) rate-profile
\mathcal{I}
follows

\left|\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{K}_{i}\right|=\left|\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}\right|+\sum_{l\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}\left|\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{l}\right|,

simply put, the number of “0” bits of coset index
i
plus the number of more significant “0” bits for each “1” bit.
For
{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\smash{\dmin}}^{\prime}}
follows
\left|\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}\right|=n-\left|\mathcal{S}_{i}\right|=r
.
Since
{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{r+2}}
and
{l\geq r+2}
imply that
\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{l}=\emptyset
, we find for
i\in\mathcal{I}^{\prime}_{\smash{\dmin}}
that

\sum_{l\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}\left|\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{l}\right|=\left|\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{x}\right|+\left|\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{y}\right|.

Further, the number of “0” bit positions larger than
x
and
y
is found as
{\left|\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{x}\right|=r-x}
and
{\left|\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{y}\right|=r-y+1}
, respectively.
Thus, we obtain
\left|\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{K}_{i}\right|=3r+1-x-y
, and find with Lemma
3
the lower bound

\Admin(\C)\geq\sum\limits_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\dmin}^{\circ}}2^{\left|\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{K}_{i}\right|}\geq\sum\limits_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\dmin}^{\prime}}2^{\left|\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{K}_{i}\right|}=A_{\dmin}^{\mathrm{LB}}(r),

where

A_{\dmin}^{\mathrm{LB}}(r)=\sum_{y=1}^{r+1}\sum_{x=0}^{y-1}2^{3r+1-x-y}=2^{3r+1}\cdot\sum_{y=1}^{r+1}\frac{1}{2^{y}}\cdot\sum_{x=0}^{y-1}\frac{1}{2^{x}}.

By employing the geometric series, we obtain the closed-form solution

\begin{split}A_{\dmin}^{\mathrm{LB}}(r)&=2^{3r+2}\cdot\sum_{y=1}^{r+1}\frac{1}{2^{y}}\cdot\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{y}}\right)=2^{3r+2}\cdot\left(\sum_{y=0}^{r+1}\frac{1}{2^{y}}-\sum_{y=0}^{r+1}\frac{1}{2^{2y}}\right)\\
&=2^{3r+2}\cdot\left(\frac{2}{3}-\frac{1}{2^{r+1}}+\frac{1}{3\cdot 2^{2r+2}}\right)=\frac{8\cdot 2^{3r}-6\cdot 2^{2r}+2^{r}}{3}.\end{split}

∎

Proposition 5
.

The lower bound
A_{\dmin}^{\mathrm{LB}}(r)
given in Theorem
2
is tight for a pre-transformed polar code
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I},\,\boldsymbol{T})
with
\RM(r,n)
rate-profile
\mathcal{I}
of order
r=n-2
.

Proof.

For order
r=n-2
holds
\w(i)=n-r=2
with
i\in\mathcal{I}_{\dmin}
. In this case, Lemma
4
gives that a coset with index support
\mathcal{S}_{i}=\{x,y\}
, where
0\leq x<y<n
, is non pre-transformable. Since this describes all coset indices
{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\dmin}}
, we find with
\Admin(\Ci)=2^{\left|\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{K}_{i}\right|}
that
\Admin(\C)=A_{\dmin}^{\mathrm{LB}}(r)
.
∎

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed a novel, low-complexity algorithm for enumerating minimum-weight codewords of
PTPC
based on tree intersection.
The algorithm shows significantly lower complexity than the state-of-the-art methods described in
[
9
]
and
[
12
]
.
Therefore, it enables for the first time to directly take the actual number of minimum-weight codewords into account in the code design. As an application, optimal convolutional polynomials for
PAC
codes have been determined through a now possible exhaustive search.

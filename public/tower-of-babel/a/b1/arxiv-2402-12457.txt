1.
Introduction

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we will say that a function
w:\mathbb{Z}_{+}\to[0,\infty)
,
w\nequiv 0
,
is
universally
L^{p}
-good
if for every
measure preserving system
(X,\mu,T)
, and every
f\in L^{p}(\mu)
, the averages

\frac{1}{W(N)}\sum_{n\leq N}w(n)f(T^{n}x)

converge almost everywhere, where
W(N)=\sum_{n\leq N}w(n)
.
The classical pointwise ergodic theorem of Birkhoff asserts
that the function
w(n)\equiv 1
is universally
L^{1}
-good. Establishing this
property for functions with non-trivial arithmetic structure is more challenging.
Here, the archetypal results are the celebrated theorems of Bourgain, and
extended by Weirdl, Mirek and Trojan, showing that the indicator function of the square numbers
or the primes is universally
L^{p}
-good for all
1<p\leq\infty

[
2
,
3
,
17
,
20
]
. Buczolich and Mauldin, and subsequently La Victoire, showed that this range of
p
is sharp by excluding
p=1

[
4
,
16
]
. We refer to the papers
[
1
,
18
,
19
]
for other results of
this type and alternative presentations of the relevant arguments. An arithmetic
function of particular importance is the divisor function, which counts
the number of positive divisors of an integer
n
. In 2017, Cuny and
Weber proved that it is universally
L^{p}
-good for all
1<p\leq\infty

[
6
]
.

1.1.
Main theorems

The aim of this paper is to study the averaging properties of the divisor function

d
in the Gaussian integers
\mathbb{Z}[i]=\{a+ib\,:\,a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, where

d(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\,:\,d|n\end{subarray}}1,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}[i].

We begin with some definitions. We say that
(X,\mu,T)
is a measure
preserving
\mathbb{Z}[i]
-action if
T=(T_{1},T_{2})
is a pair of commuting, invertible,
measure preserving transformations of a finite measure space
(X,\mu)
. In this
case, for
n=a+ib\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
we write
T^{n}:=T_{1}^{a}T_{2}^{b}
. As before, we say that
w:\mathbb{Z}[i]\to[0,\infty)
,
w\nequiv 0
, is
universally
L^{p}
-good
if for every measure preserving

\mathbb{Z}[i]
-action
(X,\mu,T)
, and every
f\in L^{p}(\mu)
, the averages

(1.1)

M_{N}^{T}(w,f)(x):=\frac{1}{W(N)}\sum_{\mathcal{N}(n)\leq N}w(n)f(T^{n}x)

converge as
N\to\infty
for
\mu
-a.e
x\in X
, where
\mathcal{N}(a+ib)=a^{2}+b^{2}

is the algebraic norm of
a+ib
and
W(N)=\sum_{\mathcal{N}(n)\leq N}w(n)
. Our first
main result is the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

The divisor function
d
is universally
L^{p}
-good for all
1<p\leq\infty
.

By the Calderón transference principle, Theorem
1.2
is reduced
to analysis of the averages

(1.3)

A_{N}f(x)=\frac{1}{D(N)}\sum_{\mathcal{N}(n)\leq N}d(n)f(x-n),

where
D(N)=\sum_{\mathcal{N}(n)\leq N}d(n)
is the divisor summatory function.
Our other main results, Theorems
1.4
and
1.6
below,
give a scale free,
\ell^{p}
improving estimate for these operators, as well as
maximal bounds in weighted
\ell^{p}
spaces. These results are in the realm
of discrete harmonic analysis, an area motivated by pointwise ergodic theory
since its introduction. We point to the survey
[
14
]
for an
exposition of relevant ideas.

Before stating the results, we need to introduce
some additional notation. For a function
f
on
\mathbb{Z}[i]
, and a finite set

E\subset\mathbb{Z}[i]
, we write the
p
-norm average of
f
on
E
as

\langle f\rangle_{E,p}=\left(\frac{1}{|E|}\,\sum_{x\in E}|f(x)|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

If
E
is a disk, we let
2E
be the disk with the same centre and
twice the radius.
Finally, if
1\leq p\leq\infty
then
p^{\prime}
will denote its conjugate
exponent, so that
\dfrac{1}{p}+\dfrac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
.

Theorem 1.4
.

For
1<p<2
, there exists
C_{p}>0
such that for all positive integers

N
, all disks
E
of radius
\sqrt{N}
, and all functions
f
,

(1.5)

\langle A_{N}f\rangle_{E,p^{\prime}}\leq C_{p}\,\langle f\rangle_{2E,p}.

A collection
\mathcal{S}
of boxes or balls is called sparse if for every
I\in\mathcal{S}

there exists a set
E_{I}\subset I
so that
|E_{I}|>|I|/2
and
E_{I}
for
I\in\mathcal{S}
are pairwise disjoint. In addition, the maximal operator associated
to the sequence
A_{N}
is defined in the usual way:

A^{*}f(x)=\sup_{N}A_{N}f(x).

Theorem 1.6
.

For
1<r,s<2
, the maximal operator
A^{*}
is of
(r,s)
-sparse type – that is, there exists
C>0
such that for all compactly supported functions
f
and
g
, there exists a sparse collection
\mathcal{S}
so that the
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}[i])
inner product satisfies

(1.7)

|(A^{*}f,g)|\leq C\sum_{I\in\mathcal{S}}|I|\langle f\rangle_{I,r}\langle g\rangle_{I,s}

It is well-established that a sparse bound as the one in Theorem
1.6
, implies not only
\ell^{p}
boundedness for the maximal operator, but also weighted inequalities for weights
w
for which the ordinary Hardy-Littlewood maximal function is bounded.

Corollary 1.8
.

For any
1<p<\infty
and all weights
w
in the Muckenhoupt class
A_{p}
, the maximal operator
A^{*}:\ell^{p}(w)\to\ell^{p}(w)
is a bounded operator.

The analogues of Theorems
1.4
and
1.6
have been
proved for many other averaging operators. Indeed,
[
10
]
established
these for the divisor function in the usual integers. However, a particular
novelty of our results is the setting, and to the best of our knowledge the
only other improving and maximal type estimates in the Gaussian integers are
in
[
8
]
.

1.2.
Overview of our approach

Our approach is based on the following, well-established idea: we work
on the Fourier side and use the Hardy-Littlewood circle method to analyse
our operators. In particular, we approximate our multiplier
\widehat{A_{N}}

by one which is localised near the major arcs. In addition, these
approximating multipliers behave like the usual averages on their
supports. The precise statement is Lemma
4.6
(i)
below.

For the improving and sparse bounds, we use a high/low decomposition.
The
low
part is the main term in the approximation of our multiplier.
We show that this satisfies an
\ell^{p}\to\ell^{\infty}
estimate for all
1<p\leq 2
. The
high
part is the error term, which satisfies a
good
\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}
estimate. These bounds are leveraged in the proof
of Theorems
1.4
and
1.6
.

For the pointwise ergodic theorem, our approach is based on ideas of
Bourgain and Lacey (see e.g.
[
19
]
). Since our
sparse bound implies an
\ell^{p}
maximal inequality, it suffices to
prove an oscillation inequality (Theorem
5.27
).
This is achieved by showing that, in an appropriate sense,
\widehat{A_{N}}
is
close to a sum of bump functions and then appealing to Bourgain’s
multifrequency lemma.

B_{q}:=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}[i]\,:\,0\leq\langle x,q\rangle<\mathcal{N}(q)\ \ \text{and}\ \ 0\leq\langle x,iq\rangle<\mathcal{N}(q)\}.

For a positive integer

We also need the following estimate for exponential sums on a sector:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\omega\subseteq[0,2\pi]
be a an interval. Then for all
N\geq 1

we have

(2.5)

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left\lvert\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{N}(\omega+t)}e(\langle n,\alpha\rangle)\right\rvert\;dt\lesssim N^{1/2}+N^{1/4}\|\alpha\|^{-3/2}.

The implied constant is independent of
\omega
.

Proof.

We can suppose that
\mathcal{N}(\alpha)=\|\alpha\|^{2}\leq 1/2
.
For
n\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
, let

be the square in

with

(2.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{N}(\omega)}\int_{I_{n}}e(\langle\xi,\alpha\rangle)\;d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{I_{0}}e(\langle\xi,\alpha\rangle)\;d\xi\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{N}(\omega)}e(\langle n,\alpha\rangle)

(2.7)

\displaystyle=\widecheck{\mathbbm{1}_{I_{0}}}(\alpha)\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{N}(\omega)}e(\langle n,\alpha\rangle).

Note that
\widecheck{\mathbbm{1}_{I_{0}}}(\alpha)\simeq 1
for
\mathcal{N}(\alpha)\leq 1/2
.
Also, the measure of the symmetric difference of
\Gamma_{N}(\omega)

and
\bigcup_{n\in\Gamma_{N}(\omega)}I_{n}
is
O(N^{1/2})
. It follows that

(2.8)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{N}(\omega+t)}e(\langle n,\alpha\rangle)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\simeq\left\lvert\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{N}(\omega+t)}\int_{I_{n}}e(\langle\xi,\alpha\rangle)\;d\xi\right\rvert

(2.9)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\int_{\Gamma_{N}(\omega+t)}e(\langle\xi,\alpha\rangle)\;d\xi\right\rvert+O(N^{1/2})

(2.10)

\displaystyle=N\left\lvert\int_{\Gamma_{1}(\omega+t)}e(\langle\xi,N^{1/2}\alpha\rangle)\;d\xi\right\rvert+O(N^{1/2}).

Moreover, for each
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
,

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{\Gamma_{1}(\omega+t)}e(\langle\xi,\beta\rangle)\;d\xi\;dt=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Gamma_{1}(\omega)}e(\langle\xi,\beta_{t}\rangle)\;d\xi\;dt,

where

, and so it suffices to show that the last

. Indeed, if this is the case, then

(2.11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left\lvert\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{N}(\omega+t)}e(\langle n,\alpha\rangle)\right\rvert\;dt

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{1/2}+N\mathcal{N}\left(N^{1/2}\alpha\right)^{-3/4}

(2.12)

\displaystyle=N^{1/2}+N^{1/4}\|\alpha\|^{-3/2}.

Let
\gamma
be the boundary of
\Gamma_{1}(\omega)
. Then by the divergence theorem

(2.13)

\int_{\Gamma_{1}(\omega)}e(\langle\xi,\beta_{t}\rangle)\;d\xi=\frac{i}{2\pi\mathcal{N}(\beta)}\int_{\gamma}e(\langle\xi,\beta_{t}\rangle)\langle\nu(\xi),\beta_{t}\rangle\;d\sigma(\xi),

. Observe that

, where
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
are

and
\gamma_{3}
is an arc of the unit circle. We
can suppose
\gamma_{1}
is the interval
[0,1]
. Then

(2.14)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{\gamma_{1}}e(\langle\xi,\beta_{t}\rangle)\langle\nu(\xi),\beta_{t}\rangle\;d\sigma(\xi)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq|\beta|\left\lvert\int_{0}^{1}e(x|\beta|\cos(2\pi t))\;dx\right\rvert

(2.15)

\displaystyle\lesssim|\beta|\left\lvert\frac{\sin(\pi|\beta|\cos(2\pi t))}{\pi|\beta|\cos(2\pi t)}\right\rvert.

Clearly the same is true for
\gamma_{2}
and so

(2.16)

\int_{0}^{1}\left|\int_{\gamma_{1}\cup\gamma_{2}}e(\langle\xi,\beta_{t}\rangle)\langle\nu(\xi),\beta_{t}\rangle\;d\sigma(\xi)\right|\;dt\lesssim|\beta|\int_{0}^{1}\left\lvert\frac{\sin(\pi|\beta|\cos(2\pi t))}{\pi|\beta|\cos(2\pi t)}\right\rvert\;dt\lesssim\log|\beta|.

We now consider the integral over

For
\xi\in\gamma_{3}
, write

3.

\mathbb{Z}[i]

Throughout this section, we set

S_{N}=D(N)A_{N}=\sum_{\mathcal{N}(n)\leq N}d(n)\;\mathbbm{1}_{n}.

Let
\omega\subseteq[0,2\pi]

be an interval and
0\leq t<2\pi
. It will also be convenient to define

S_{N,t}^{\omega}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{N}(n)\leq N\\
\arg(n)\in\omega+t\end{subarray}}d(n)\;\delta_{n}.

3.1.
Rational frequencies

We begin by using the Dirichlet hyperbola method to evaluate

\widehat{S_{N,t}^{\omega}}
at rational points.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\omega\subseteq[0,2\pi]
be an interval and
0\leq t<2\pi
. For
each
a,q\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
with
q\neq 0
and
a\in\mathbb{A}_{q}
, and each integer
N\geq 1
,

(3.2)

\widehat{S_{N,t}^{\omega}}(a/q)=\frac{|\omega|\pi N}{2\mathcal{N}(q)}\Big(\log N-2\log\mathcal{N}(q)+2\kappa-1\Big)+E_{N}^{\omega}(a/q,t),

where
E_{N}^{\omega}(a/q,t)
satisfies

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|E_{N}^{\omega}(a/q,t)\right|\;dt\lesssim(N^{3/4}+N^{1/4}\mathcal{N}(q)^{3/4})\log(1+\mathcal{N}(q)).

The implied constant is independent of
\omega
.

Remark 3.3
.

Inspection of the proof shows that in the case that
\mathcal{N}(q)=1
and
a=0
,
the error estimate above holds for all
t
uniformly rather than just on average. In particular, for any interval
\omega
we have the formula

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{N}(n)\leq N\\
\arg(n)\in\omega\end{subarray}}d(n)=\frac{|\omega|\pi N}{2}\left(\log N+2\kappa-1\right)+O(N^{3/4}).

Proof.

For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}[i]\setminus\{0\}
and
t\in\mathbb{T}
, set

(3.4)

\displaystyle D_{1}(n,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\mathcal{N}(m)\leq N/\mathcal{N}(n)\\
\arg(mn)\in\omega+t\end{subarray}}e(\langle mn,a/q\rangle),

(3.5)

\displaystyle D_{2}(n,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\mathcal{N}(m)\leq\sqrt{N}\\
\arg(mn)\in\omega+t\end{subarray}}e(\langle mn,a/q\rangle),

so that

(3.6)

\widehat{S_{N,t}^{\omega}}(a/q)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{N}(mn)\leq N\\
\arg(mn)\in\omega+t\end{subarray}}e(\langle mn,a/q\rangle)=\sum_{1\leq\mathcal{N}(n)\leq\sqrt{N}}(2D_{1}(n,t)-D_{2}(n,t)).

We consider the cases
\mathcal{N}(q)\leq\sqrt{N}
and
\mathcal{N}(q)>\sqrt{N}
separately.

Case 1:

\mathcal{N}(q)\leq\sqrt{N}
.
If
n\neq 0
is a multiple of
\overline{q}
, then

(3.7)

\displaystyle D_{1}(n,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{|\omega|N}{2\mathcal{N}(n)}+O\left(\frac{N^{1/2}}{\mathcal{N}(n)^{1/2}}\right).

Then summing each term on the right over all such

, using
(
2.3
), and applying Cauchy-Schwartz, we get

(3.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\mathcal{N}(n)\leq\sqrt{N}\\
n\equiv 0\mod\overline{q}\end{subarray}}\frac{|\omega|N}{2\mathcal{N}(n)}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\omega|N}{2\mathcal{N}(q)}\sum_{k=1}^{\sqrt{N}/\mathcal{N}(q)}\frac{r_{2}(k)}{k}

(3.9)

\displaystyle=\frac{|\omega|\pi N}{4\mathcal{N}(q)}\left(\log N-2\log\mathcal{N}(q)+2\kappa\right)+O(N^{-1/2}),

and

(3.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\mathcal{N}(n)\leq\sqrt{N}\\
n\equiv 0\mod\overline{q}\end{subarray}}\frac{N^{1/2}}{\mathcal{N}(n)^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{1\leq\mathcal{N}(n)\leq\sqrt{N}}\frac{N^{1/2}}{\mathcal{N}(n)^{1/2}}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{1\leq\mathcal{N}(n)\leq\sqrt{N}}\frac{N^{1/2}}{\mathcal{N}(n)^{1/2}}\lesssim N^{3/4}.

Combining these we find that

(3.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\mathcal{N}(n)\leq\sqrt{N}\\
n\equiv 0\mod\overline{q}\end{subarray}}D_{1}(n,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{|\omega|\pi N}{4\mathcal{N}(q)}\left(\log N-2\log\mathcal{N}(q)+2\kappa\right)+O(N^{3/4}).

The same reasoning also gives

(3.13)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\mathcal{N}(n)\leq\sqrt{N}\\
k\equiv 0\mod\overline{q}\end{subarray}}D_{2}(n,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{|\omega|\pi N}{2\mathcal{N}(q)}+O(N^{3/4}),

and hence

E_{N}^{\omega}(a/q,t)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\mathcal{N}(n)\leq\sqrt{N}\\
n\nequiv 0\mod\overline{q}\end{subarray}}(2D_{1}(n,t)-D_{2}(n,t))+O(N^{3/4}).

If
\overline{n}\equiv s\mod q
for some
s\in B_{q}\setminus\{0\}
, then

\mathcal{N}(n)\gtrsim\|sa/q\|^{2}
. Then by Lemma
2.4
,

(3.14)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|D_{1}(n,t)\right|\;dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\mathcal{N}(m)\leq N/\mathcal{N}(n)\\
\arg(mn)\in\omega+t\end{subarray}}e(\langle m,sa/q\rangle)\right|\;dt

(3.15)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{N^{1/2}}{\mathcal{N}(n)^{1/2}}+\frac{N^{1/4}}{\mathcal{N}(n)^{1/4}\|sa/q\|^{3/2}}

(3.16)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N^{1/2}}{\mathcal{N}(n)^{1/2}}+\frac{N^{1/4}}{\|sa/q\|^{2}}.

As before, we have that

(3.17)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\mathcal{N}(n)\leq\sqrt{N}\\
\overline{n}\nequiv 0\mod q\end{subarray}}\frac{N^{1/2}}{\mathcal{N}(n)^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{3/4}.

Note that the map
s\mapsto sa\mod q
is a bijection of
B_{q}
, and so

(3.18)

\displaystyle\sum_{s\in B_{q}\setminus\{0\}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\mathcal{N}(n)\leq\sqrt{N}\\
\overline{n}\equiv s\mod q\end{subarray}}\frac{N^{1/4}}{\|sa/q\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{s\in B_{q}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{N^{3/4}}{\mathcal{N}(q)\|sa/q\|^{2}}

(3.19)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s\in B_{q}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{N^{3/4}}{\mathcal{N}(s)}\lesssim N^{3/4}\log(1+\mathcal{N}(q)).

We conclude that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\mathcal{N}(n)\leq\sqrt{N}\\
n\nequiv 0\mod\overline{q}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|D_{1}(n,t)\right|\;dt\lesssim N^{3/4}\log(1+\mathcal{N}(q)).

The same computations give an analogous bound for
D_{2}(n,t)
,
which concludes the proof in the case that
\mathcal{N}(q)\geq\sqrt{N}
.

Case 2:

\mathcal{N}(q)>\sqrt{N}
.
In this case there are no non-zero
n
such that
\mathcal{N}(n)\leq\sqrt{N}
and
n
is a multiple of
\overline{q}
. However,

(3.20)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\frac{|\omega|\pi N}{4\mathcal{N}(q)}\left(\log N-2\log\mathcal{N}(q)+2\kappa-1\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{1/2}\log(1+\mathcal{N}(q)),

and so

E_{N}^{\omega}(a/q,t)=\widehat{S_{N,t}^{\omega}}(a/q)+O(N^{1/2}\log(1+\mathcal{N}(q)))

Observe that for each
s\in B_{q}
the number of
n
such that

\mathcal{N}(n)\leq\sqrt{N}
and
\overline{n}\equiv s\mod q
is at most 4. It follows from
this and Hölder’s inequality that

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq\mathcal{N}(n)\leq\sqrt{N}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|D_{1}(n,t)\right|\;dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{s\in B_{q}\setminus\{0\}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\mathcal{N}(n)\leq\sqrt{N}\\
\overline{n}\equiv s\mod q\end{subarray}}\frac{N^{1/4}}{\mathcal{N}(n)^{1/4}\|sa/q\|^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{s\in B_{q}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{N^{1/4}}{\mathcal{N}(s)^{1/4}\|sa/q\|^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{1/4}\log(1+\mathcal{N}(q))^{1/4}\left(\sum_{s\in B_{q}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{\|sa/q\|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{1/4}\mathcal{N}(q)^{3/4}\log(1+\mathcal{N}(q)).

The same holds for
D_{2}(n,t)
. Combining these completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Major and minor arcs

Let
\delta>0
be a small constant and let
a,q\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
be
such that
q\neq 0
and
a\in\mathbb{A}_{q}
. Then for

N\geq 1
, we define

(3.21)

\mathfrak{M}_{N,\delta}(a/q)=\left\{\alpha\in\mathbb{T}^{2}\,:\,\mathcal{N}(\alpha-a/q)\leq N^{-1+\delta/2}\mathcal{N}(q)^{-1}\right\}.

The
major arcs

\mathfrak{M}_{N,\delta}
consist of

\mathfrak{M}_{N,\delta}(a/q)
with
\mathcal{N}(q)\leq N^{\delta}
and the

minor arcs

\mathfrak{m}_{N,\delta}
are the complement of
the major arcs:

\mathfrak{M}_{N,\delta}=\bigcup_{\mathcal{N}(q)\leq N^{\delta}\,}\bigcup_{\;a\in\mathbb{A}_{q}}\mathfrak{M}_{N,\delta}(a/q),\qquad\mathfrak{m}_{N,\delta}=\mathbb{T}^{2}\setminus\mathfrak{M}_{N,\delta}.

The next lemma describes the behaviour of
\widehat{S_{N}}
on major arcs.

Proposition 3.22
.

Let
0<\delta\leq 1/20
and
N\geq 1
. For each
a,q\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
such that

1\leq\mathcal{N}(q)\leq N^{\delta}
and
a\in\mathbb{A}_{q}
, and each

\alpha\in\mathfrak{M}_{N,\delta}(a/q)
we have

(3.23)

\widehat{S_{N}}(\alpha)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{N}(n)\leq N\end{subarray}}\frac{\pi}{\mathcal{N}(q)}\left(\log\mathcal{N}(n)-2\log{\mathcal{N}(q)}+2\kappa\right)e(\langle n,\alpha-a/q\rangle)+O(N^{1-\delta}).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a partition of
[0,2\pi]
into intervals of equal
length
\lambda\simeq N^{-2\delta}
. For each integer
j\geq 0
, set

N_{j}=(1+\lambda)^{j}\sqrt{N}
and let
j_{0}
be such that
N_{j_{0}}\leq N<N_{j_{0}+1}
. For
0<t<2\pi
,
P\in\mathcal{P}
and

j=0,1\dots,j_{0}
we set

R(j,P,t)=\Big\{n\;\colon\;N_{j}\leq\mathcal{N}(n)<N_{j+1},\;\arg(n)\in P+t\Big\}.

Note that

|R(j,P,t)|=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}N_{j-1}+O\left(N_{j}^{1/2}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{n\in R(j,P,t)}\,d(n)\lesssim N^{-4\delta}N_{j}\log N_{j}.

Choose
\beta=\beta(P,t)\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathcal{N}(\beta)=1
and

\arg(\beta)\in P+t
. Then for each
n\in R(j,P,t)
,

\left|1-e(\langle n-\beta\sqrt{N_{j}},\alpha-a/q\rangle)\right|\leq\mathcal{N}(n-\beta\sqrt{N_{j}})^{1/2}\;\mathcal{N}(\alpha-a/q)^{1/2}\lesssim N^{-\delta},

and so

(3.24)

\displaystyle D_{j,t}^{P}(\alpha):

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in R(j,P,t)}d(n)e(\langle n,\alpha\rangle)

(3.25)

\displaystyle=D_{j,t}^{P}(a/q)\;e(\langle\beta\sqrt{N_{j}},\alpha-a/q\rangle)+O(N^{-5\delta}N_{j}).

Next, for each
j
set

\Delta_{j}:=\frac{\lambda\pi N_{j}}{2\mathcal{N}(q)}\left(\log N_{j}-2\log\mathcal{N}(q)+2\kappa-1\right).

Then for each
j\geq 1
we have

(3.26)

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}-\Delta_{j-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda\pi}{2\mathcal{N}(q)}\left[(N_{j}-N_{j-1})(\log N_{j}-2\log\mathcal{N}(q)+2\kappa-1)+N_{j-1}\log(1+\lambda)\right]

(3.27)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda\pi}{2\mathcal{N}(q)}(N_{j}-N_{j-1})(\log N_{j}-2\log\mathcal{N}(q)+2\kappa)+O(N^{-6\delta}N_{j})

(3.28)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{\mathcal{N}(q)}\sum_{n\in R(j,P,t)}(\log\mathcal{N}(n)-2\log\mathcal{N}(q)+2\kappa)+O(N^{-6\delta}N_{j}).

It follows that

(3.29)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\sum_{j=1}^{j_{0}}(\Delta_{j}-\Delta_{j-1})e(\langle\beta\sqrt{N_{j}},\alpha-a/q\rangle)

(3.30)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{\pi}{\mathcal{N}(q)}\sum_{j=1}^{j_{0}}\sum_{n\in R(j,P,t)}(\log\mathcal{N}(n)-2\log\mathcal{N}(q)+2\kappa)e(\langle\beta\sqrt{N_{j}},\alpha-a/q\rangle)+O(N^{1-4\delta})

(3.31)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{\pi}{\mathcal{N}(q)}\sum_{j=1}^{j_{0}}\sum_{n\in R(j,P,t)}(\log\mathcal{N}(n)-2\log\mathcal{N}(q)+2\kappa-1)e(\langle n,\alpha-a/q\rangle)+O(N^{1-3\delta})

(3.32)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{\pi}{\mathcal{N}(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{N}(n)\leq N\\
\arg(n)\in P+t\end{subarray}}(\log\mathcal{N}(n)-2\log\mathcal{N}(q)+2\kappa-1)e(\langle n,\alpha-a/q\rangle)+O(N^{1-3\delta}).

Since
|\widehat{S_{\sqrt{N},t}^{P}}(\alpha)|\lesssim N^{1/2}\log N
, using
(
3.24
), we can write

(3.33)

\displaystyle\widehat{S_{N,t}^{P}}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{j_{0}}D_{j,t}^{P}(a/q)e(\langle\beta\sqrt{N_{j}},\alpha-a/q\rangle)+O(N^{1-3\delta})

(3.34)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{j_{0}}(D_{j,t}^{P}(a/q)-\Delta_{j}+\Delta_{j-1})e(\langle\beta\sqrt{N_{j}},\alpha-a/q\rangle)

(3.35)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j=1}^{j_{0}}(\Delta_{j}-\Delta_{j-1})e(\langle\beta\sqrt{N_{j}},\alpha-a/q\rangle)+O(N^{1-3\delta}).

Let
E_{N}^{P}(a/q,t)
be as in Lemma
3.1
. Then using
summation by parts, we can bound the first term on the right as follows:

(3.36)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\sum_{j=1}^{j_{0}}(D_{j,t}^{P}(a/q)-\Delta_{j}+\Delta_{j-1})e(\langle\beta\sqrt{N_{j}},\alpha-a/q\rangle)\right\rvert

(3.37)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\left\lvert\sum_{j=1}^{j_{0}}[E_{N_{j}}^{P}(a/q,t)-E_{N_{j-1}}^{P}(a/q,t)]e(\langle\beta\sqrt{N_{j}},\alpha-a/q\rangle)\right\rvert

(3.38)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\left\lvert\sum_{j=1}^{j_{0}}E_{N_{j}}^{P}(a/q,t)(1-e(\langle\beta(\sqrt{N_{j}}-\sqrt{N_{j+1}}),\alpha-a/q\rangle))\right\rvert

(3.39)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+|E_{N_{j_{0}}}^{P}(a/q,t)|+|E_{\sqrt{N}}^{P}(a/q,t)|

(3.40)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq N^{-\delta}\sum_{j=1}^{j_{0}}|E_{N_{j}}^{P}(a/q,t)|+|E_{N_{j_{0}}}^{P}(a/q,t)|+|E_{\sqrt{N}}^{P}(a/q,t)|.

Combining this with (
3.29
) and (
3.33
), then
summing over all
P\in\mathcal{P}
gives

(3.41)

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{S_{N}}(\alpha)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{N}(n)\leq N\end{subarray}}\frac{\pi}{\mathcal{N}(q)}\left(\log\mathcal{N}(n)-2\log{\mathcal{N}(q)}+2\kappa\right)e(\langle n,\alpha-a/q\rangle)\right|

(3.42)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim N^{-\delta}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\sum_{j=1}^{j_{0}}|E_{N_{j}}^{P}(a/q,t)|+\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}|E_{N_{j_{0}}}^{P}(a/q,t)|+\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}|E_{\sqrt{N}}^{P}(a/q,t)|+N^{1-\delta}

Finally, averaging over
t
and applying Lemma
3.1
gives the
conclusion.
∎

We now provide estimates for the complementary minor arcs:

Proposition 3.43
.

Assume that
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}
. Then

\left|\widehat{S_{N}}(\alpha)\right|\lesssim N^{1-\delta/4}\log N.

Proof.

By Dirichlet’s Approximation Theorem there exist
a,q\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
with
a\in\mathbb{A}_{q}
such that

\mathcal{N}(\alpha-a/q)\leq N^{-1+\delta/2}\mathcal{N}(q)^{-1}
. Since
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}
,
we have
\mathcal{N}(q)>N^{\delta}
, and so
\mathcal{N}(\alpha-a/q)\leq N^{-1-\delta/2}
.
Then

\left|\widehat{S_{N}}(\alpha)-\widehat{S_{N}}(a/q)\right|\lesssim\sum_{\mathcal{N}(n)\leq N}d(n)\mathcal{N}(n)^{1/2}\mathcal{N}(\alpha-a/q)^{1/2}\leq N^{1-\delta/4}\log N.

∎

4.
Approximation

4.1.
Approximating multipliers

We will establish an approximation result for the kernel
of our operator, on the Fourier side, by utilizing Section

3.2
. To this end, we introduce multipliers

L_{N,q}
defined by

(4.1)

\displaystyle\widehat{L_{N,q}}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{N\log N}\frac{\pi}{\mathcal{N}(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{N}(n)\leq N\end{subarray}}\left(\log\mathcal{N}(n)-2\log{\mathcal{N}(q)}+2\kappa\right)e(\langle n,\alpha\rangle).

Using the estimates in Remark
2.18
, one easily sees that for
all
\alpha
we have the bound

(4.2)

|\widehat{L_{N,q}}(\alpha)|\lesssim\min\left\{\;1,\mathcal{N}(q)^{-1}(N^{-1/2}+N^{-3/4}\mathcal{N}(\alpha)^{-3/4})\right\}.

We will also need the Fourier kernel of the usual averaging operators:

(4.3)

\displaystyle\widehat{M}_{N}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{N}(n)\leq N\end{subarray}}e(\langle n,\alpha\rangle).

Next, we introduce a cut-off by considering a Schwartz function
\eta

such that

\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{N}(x)\leq\frac{1}{16}}\leq\eta\leq\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{N}(x)\leq\frac{1}{8}}.

Then for an integer
s\geq 0
, we set
\eta_{s}(\alpha)=\eta(2^{s}\alpha)
and

\mathcal{R}_{s}=\{a/q:\;a\in\mathbb{A}_{q},\;2^{s}\leq\mathcal{N}(q)<2^{s+1}\}.

An important observation that we will use frequently is that the
functions
\eta_{s}(\cdot-a/q)
,
a/q\in\mathcal{R}_{s}
, have pairwise
disjoint supports. We can now define our approximating multipliers

K_{N,s}
and
K^{\prime}_{N,s}
as follows:

(4.4)

\displaystyle\widehat{K_{N,s}}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a/q\in\mathcal{R}_{s}}\widehat{L_{N,q}}(\alpha-a/q)\,\eta_{s}(\alpha-a/q);

(4.5)

\displaystyle\widehat{K^{\prime}_{N,s}}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a/q\in\mathcal{R}_{s}}\frac{\pi^{2}}{\mathcal{N}(q)}\widehat{M_{N}}(\alpha-a/q)\,\eta_{s}(\alpha-a/q).

Lemma 4.6
.

For each
0<\delta\leq 1/20
, the following estimates hold:

(i)

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{A_{N}}-\sum_{s:2^{s}\leq N^{\delta}}\widehat{K_{N,s}}\right\|_{\infty}\lesssim N^{-\delta/4};

(ii)

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{A_{N}}-\sum_{s:2^{s}\leq N^{\delta}}\widehat{K^{\prime}_{N,s}}\right\|_{\infty}\lesssim\frac{1}{\log N}.

Proof.

(i)

Fix
\alpha\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
and
N
large. By Dirichlet’s Approximation Theorem there
exist
a_{0},q_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
with
a_{0}\in\mathbb{A}_{q_{0}}
and
\mathcal{N}(q_{0})\leq N^{1-\delta/2}

such that
\mathcal{N}(\alpha-a_{0}/q_{0})\leq N^{-1+\delta/2}\mathcal{N}(q_{0})^{-1}
. Suppose
2^{s_{0}}\leq\mathcal{N}(q_{0})<2^{s_{0}+1}
. We consider to cases depending on
s_{0}
.

Case 1:

2^{s_{0}}\leq N^{\delta}
. In this case
\eta_{s_{0}}(\alpha-a_{0}/q_{0})=1
and
so
\widehat{K_{N,s_{0}}}(\alpha)=\widehat{L_{N,q_{0}}}(\alpha-a_{0}/q_{0})
. Then by Proposition

3.22
,

(4.7)

|\widehat{A_{N}}(\alpha)-\widehat{L_{N,q_{0}}}(\alpha-a_{0}/q_{0})|\lesssim N^{-\delta}.

For any
s\neq s_{0}
and
a/q\in\mathcal{R}_{s}
,

\mathcal{N}(\alpha-a/q)\gtrsim\mathcal{N}(a/q-a_{0}/q_{0})-\mathcal{N}(\alpha-a_{0}/q_{0})\geq\mathcal{N}(1/qq_{0})\gtrsim N^{-\delta}\mathcal{N}(q)^{-1}.

Using (
4.1
), and noting that
\delta
is small,
we see that

|\widehat{L_{N,q}}(\alpha)|\lesssim\mathcal{N}(q)^{-1}(N^{-1/2}+N^{-3/4}N^{3\delta/4}\mathcal{N}(q)^{3/4})\lesssim 2^{-s/4}N^{-\delta},

and so

(4.8)

\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s:2^{s}\leq N^{\delta}\\
s\neq s_{0}\end{subarray}}\widehat{K_{N,s}}(\alpha)\right|\lesssim N^{-\delta}.

Combining (
4.7
) and (
4.8
) completes the proof in this case.

Case 2:

2^{s_{0}}>N^{\delta}
. Then for any
s
such that
2^{s}\leq N^{\delta}
and
a/q\in\mathcal{R}_{s}
,
\mathcal{N}(\alpha-a/q)\gtrsim N^{-1+\delta/2}\mathcal{N}(q)^{-1}
. The argument above
shows that

(4.9)

\left|\sum_{s:2^{s}\leq N^{\delta}}\widehat{K_{N,s}}(\alpha)\right|\lesssim N^{-\delta}.

In addition, by Proposition
3.43
, we have that
|\widehat{A_{N}}(\alpha)|\leq N^{-\delta/4}
.
Combining these completes the proof of
(i)
.

(ii)

This follows from
(i)
upon observing that for each
s
,

\|\widehat{K_{N,s}}(\alpha)-\widehat{K^{\prime}_{N,s}}(\alpha)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\frac{1}{\log N}.

∎

4.2.
The High and Low Parts

We now decompose
A_{N}
into
high
and
low
parts as follows:
for
\delta>0
, we write

\widehat{A_{N}}=\widehat{\textup{Lo}_{N,\delta}}+\widehat{\textup{Hi}_{N,\delta}},\quad\text{where}\quad\widehat{\textup{Lo}_{N,\delta}}(\alpha)=\sum_{s:2^{s}\leq N^{\delta}}\widehat{K_{N,s}}(\alpha).

Lemma
4.6
(i)
states that for
\delta

sufficiently small,

(4.10)

\|\widehat{\textup{Hi}_{N,\delta}}\|_{\infty}\lesssim N^{-\delta/4}.

This is sufficient for our purposes and so we turn our attention to the
low part. Notice that

(4.11)

\displaystyle\textup{Lo}_{N,\delta}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s:\,2^{s}\leq N^{\delta}}\sum_{a/q\in\mathcal{R}_{s}}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\widehat{L_{N,q}}(\cdot-a/q)\;\eta_{s}(\cdot-a/q)\right)(n)

(4.12)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s:\,2^{s}\leq N^{\delta}}\sum_{a/q\in\mathcal{R}_{s}}\Big(L_{N,q}*\widecheck{\eta_{s}}\Big)(n)\,e(\langle n,a/q\rangle)

(4.13)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathcal{N}(q)<2N^{\delta}}\Big(L_{N,q}*\widecheck{\eta_{s}}\Big)(n)\,\tau_{q}(n)

where
\tau_{q}(n)
is a
Ramanujan sum

\tau_{q}(n):=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{A}_{q}}e(\langle n,a/q\rangle).

Thus to control the Low part, one needs to first be able to control Ramanujan sums. The following result can be easily adapted from
[
8
]
*Prop 3.11.

Lemma 4.14
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
and all integers
N,Q,k>2
such that
N>Q^{k}
, we have

\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathcal{N}(n)\leq N}\left(\sum_{\mathcal{N}(q)<Q}\frac{|\tau_{q}(n)|}{\mathcal{N}(q)}\right)^{k}\right)^{1/k}\lesssim_{k,\varepsilon}Q^{\varepsilon}.

Using this, we can now prove
\ell^{p}\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}
bound
for
\textup{Lo}_{N,\delta}
.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
1<p<2
, let
N>2
be an integer and let
E
be a disk of radius
\sqrt{N}
.
Then for all
\delta\leq 1/p^{\prime}
and all functions
f
supported on
2E
, we have

\langle\textup{Lo}_{N,\delta}f\rangle_{E,\infty}\lesssim_{p}N^{\delta/p^{\prime}}\langle f\rangle_{2E,p}.

Proof.

It suffices to consider the case when
p^{\prime}
is an integer.

We begin by observing that

|(L_{N,q}\ast\widecheck{\eta}_{s})(x)|\leq\|L_{N,q}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\|\widecheck{\eta}_{s}\|_{\ell^{1}}\lesssim\frac{1}{N\mathcal{N}(q)}.

Consequently, for
x\in E
we have that

\displaystyle|\textup{Lo}_{N,\delta}f(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\mathcal{N}(y)<4N}\sum_{\mathcal{N}(q)<2N^{\delta}}|\tau_{q}(y)|L_{N,q}\ast\widecheck{\eta}_{s}(y)||f(x-y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathcal{N}(y)<9N}\sum_{\mathcal{N}(q)<2N^{\delta}}\frac{|\tau_{q}(y)|}{\mathcal{N}(q)}|f(x-y)|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathcal{N}(n)<9N}\left(\sum_{\mathcal{N}(q)<2N^{\delta}}\frac{|\tau_{q}(n)|}{\mathcal{N}(q)}\right)^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathcal{N}(y)<9N}|f(x-y)|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

The conclusion now follows from Lemma
4.14
with

Q=\lfloor 2N^{\delta}\rfloor
,
k=p^{\prime}
and
\varepsilon=1/p^{\prime}
.
∎

5.
Proof of the main theorems

As is expected for these types of questions,
[
11
,
10
,
9
,
8
]
,
a multifrequency type of inequality is required, especially for the sparse bound. One can easily follow the steps of Lacey’s proof of Bourgain’s inequality
[
15
]
to obtain the following:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a smooth function that satisfies:

\displaystyle|\phi(x)|\lesssim|x|^{-3},

\displaystyle|\hat{\phi}(\alpha)-1|\lesssim|\alpha|,

\displaystyle|\hat{\phi}(\alpha)|\lesssim|\alpha|^{-2}

Assume that
K\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
and
\mathcal{N}(K)>1
. Let
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{L}
be in
2^{-j_{0}}K^{-1}\mathbb{Z}[i]
. Then

(5.2)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sup_{j\geq j_{0}}\left|\sum_{\ell=1}^{K}\textup{Mod}_{\lambda_{\ell}}\,\left[\phi_{j}\ast\textup{Mod}_{-\lambda_{\ell}}f\right]\right|\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}\lesssim\log\log(L+\mathcal{N}(K))\lVert f\rVert_{\ell^{2}},

where
\phi_{j}(x)=2^{-j}\phi(2^{-j}x)
and
\textup{Mod}_{\theta}f(x)=e(\theta\cdot x)f(x)
.

5.1.
Fixed scale estimate

First we focus on a fixed scale estimate.
It has been well-established in the literature that it suffices to obtain a
restricted weak-type estimate for all
p
sufficiently close to
1
. Then
the result follows by real interpolation. (see e.g.
[
13
, Chapter 1
§2]
). Thus, the proof of Theorem
1.4
boils down to the following Lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
1<p<2
, let
N\geq 1
be an integer and let
E
be a disk of radius
\sqrt{N}
. Then for
f=\mathbbm{1}_{F}
supported on
2E
and
g=\mathbbm{1}_{G}
supported on
E
, we have

(5.4)

\frac{1}{N}|(A_{N}f,g)|\lesssim\langle f\rangle_{2E,p}\langle g\rangle_{E,p}.

Remark 5.5
.

We emphasize that Lemma
5.3
fails for
p=1
, making the endpoint sharp. Indeed, for
N\geq 4
, let
M=\lfloor\log^{2}N\rfloor
and set
f(x)=\mathbbm{1}_{[M,2M]}\big(d(x)\big)
and
g(x)=\mathbbm{1}_{0}(x)
. Then one can easily verify that

(5.6)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}|(A_{N}f,g)|

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{N^{2}\log N}\left|\sum_{\mathcal{N}(n)\leq N}f(n)d(n)\right|

(5.7)

\displaystyle>\frac{\log N}{N^{2}}\sum_{\mathcal{N}(n)\leq N}|f(n)|

(5.8)

\displaystyle=\log N\;\langle f\rangle_{2E,1}\langle g\rangle_{E,1}.

Proof.

Take
1<q<2
and
0<\varepsilon\leq 1/100q^{\prime 2}
. Note that if

(5.9)

\langle f\rangle_{2E,q^{\prime}}\langle g\rangle_{E,q^{\prime}}\leq N^{-\varepsilon},

then using a trivial estimate for
A_{N}f
, we get that for all
p\geq q
,

\frac{1}{N}(A_{N}f,g)\lesssim N^{\varepsilon}\langle f\rangle_{2E,1}\langle g\rangle_{E,1}\leq\langle f\rangle_{2E,p}\langle g\rangle_{E,p}.

Now consider the case when (
5.9
) fails, i.e when

(5.10)

\min\{\langle f\rangle_{2E,1},\langle g\rangle_{E,1}\}>N^{-\varepsilon q^{\prime}}.

Then using (
4.10
) and Lemma
4.15
we see that for
all
0<\delta<\min\{1/20,1/q^{\prime}\}
,

(5.11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}(A_{N}f,g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}(\textup{Hi}_{N,\delta}f,g)+\frac{1}{N}(\textup{Lo}_{N,\delta}f,g)

(5.12)

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\delta/4}\langle f\rangle_{2E,1}^{1/2}\langle g\rangle_{E,1}^{1/2}+N^{\delta/q^{\prime}}\langle f\rangle_{2E,1}^{1/q}\langle g\rangle_{E,1}.

The two terms on the right are equal if we choose
\delta
such that

N^{\delta(q^{\prime}+4)/4q^{\prime}}=\langle f\rangle_{2E,1}^{1/2-1/q}\langle g\rangle_{E,1}^{-1/2}.

Observe that this choice of
\delta
is within the allowed range. Indeed,
(
5.10
) implies that

\delta\leq\frac{4\varepsilon q^{\prime 2}}{q(q^{\prime}+4)}<\frac{1}{25q^{\prime}}.

Then in this case, the right hand side of (
5.11
) becomes

2\left(\langle f\rangle_{2E,1}\langle g\rangle_{E,1}\right)^{\frac{q^{\prime}+2}{q^{\prime}+4}}\langle f\rangle_{2E,1}^{\frac{-1}{q^{\prime}+4}}\leq\left(\langle f\rangle_{2E,1}\langle g\rangle_{E,1}\right)^{\frac{q^{\prime}+1}{q^{\prime}+4}},

which gives (
5.4
) for
p=(q^{\prime}+4)/(q^{\prime}+1)
. Then letting
q\to 1

concludes the proof.
∎

5.2.
Sparse bounds

Traditionally, analogous proofs in the literature depend upon the Kesler, Lacey, Mena recursive argument from
[
12
]
*Theorem 1.2, that allows them to reduce the proof of a sparse domination result to a simpler estimate for indicators. Our reduced version takes the following form.

Lemma 5.13
.

Fix
E=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}[i]\;:\;\mathcal{N}(x)\leq N\}
. For all admissible stopping times
T
, and for all
1<p<2
we have that

(5.14)

(A_{T}f,g)\lesssim\big[\langle f\rangle_{E,1}\;\langle g\rangle_{E,1}\big]^{1/p}\,|E|,

where a stopping time
T
is called admissible if and only if for any
I\subseteq E
such that

\langle f\rangle_{3I,1}>100\langle f\rangle_{E,1}
,
we have
\inf\{T(x)\,:\,x\in I\}>|I|
.

Before we discuss the proof of Lemma
5.13
, we shall revisit the proof of Kelser, Lacey and Mena, to verify that it still holds in our adjusted setting. More specifically,

Lemma 5.15
.

Suppose that for every
E=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}[i]\,:\,\mathcal{N}(x)\leq N\}
, and all admissible stopping times
T
, a family of operators
K_{N}
satisfies the estimate in equation (
5.14
). Then there exists a sparse family of balls
\mathcal{S}
so that the operators are dominated by the corresponding
(p,p)
-sparse form.

Proof.

Pick indicator functions
f
and
g
supported on a fixed ball
E
. Let
\mathcal{Q}_{E}
be the collection of all balls
Q
that are maximally contained inside our ball
E
, for which we have
\langle f\rangle_{Q}>C\langle f\rangle_{E}
. We have for our admissible stopping times:

\displaystyle\Big(\sup_{n\leq|E|}K_{n}f,g\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\left(K_{T}f,g\right)+\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{E}}\Big(\sup_{n\leq|Q|}K_{n}f\mathbbm{1}_{Q},g\mathbbm{1}_{Q}\Big)

The first term above satisfies our desired bound by assumption.
Note that, because of the admissibility condition and the fact that
f
is a non-negative function, we must have

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{E}}|Q|=\left|\bigcup_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{E}}Q\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}|E|.

For the second term, one can repeat the argument for each ball
Q
. Using this recursion, we can deduce the desired sparse domination result, with the associated sparse family be the collection of all possible
\mathcal{Q}_{E}
’s. The sparse condition is satisfied thanks to our observation for the size of
Q
above, and the respective exceptional set defined in the standard way
\mathcal{E}_{E}=E\setminus\mathcal{Q}_{E}
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
5.13
.

Note that Hölder, along with a trivial bound produces

(5.16)

\displaystyle(A_{T}f,g)

\displaystyle\leq\langle A_{T}f\rangle_{E,\infty}\langle g\rangle_{E,1}\,|E|\leq\sup_{x\in E}\big\{T^{\delta}(x)\big\}\;\langle f\rangle_{E,1}\,\langle g\rangle_{E,1}|E|.

for
0<\delta<\frac{1}{100(p^{\prime}+1)}
. Let
B_{J}=\Big\{x\in\mathbb{Z}[i]\>:\>T^{\delta}(x)\lesssim J^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\langle f\rangle^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}_{E,1}\;\langle g\rangle^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}_{E,1}\Big\}
, and notice that when restricted on
B_{J}
, (
5.14
) holds trivially. So we devote the rest of the proof in studying what happens when restricting to
x\notin B_{J}
.

We follow a slight modification of the fixed scale approach. Specifically, this time decompose our operator into three pieces
\widehat{A}_{T}f\lesssim\widehat{H}_{T,1}f+\widehat{H}_{T,2}f+\widehat{L}_{T,0}f
, and provide their estimates separately. Once again we rely on (
4.10
) and Lemma
4.15
. In the end we bring them together and optimize our parameters as in the fixed scale proof.

First, we look at
\widehat{H}_{T,1}:=\widehat{\operatorname{Hi}}_{T,3p^{\prime}\,\delta}
, where the inequalities below come from (
4.10
), an elementary square function argument, and Parseval

(5.17)

\displaystyle\langle H_{T,1}*f\rangle_{E,2}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{\mathcal{N}(n)>J^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}\;\delta}}}n^{-2p^{\prime}\,\delta}\langle f\rangle_{E,2}^{2}\lesssim J^{-1+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\langle f\rangle_{E,2}^{2}.

Next, we define the
H_{T,2}
term as

(5.18)

\displaystyle\widehat{H}_{T,2}:=\sum_{k=\lfloor\log K\rfloor}^{\log J}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2^{k}\leq N(q)<2^{k+1}\end{subarray}}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{A}_{q}}\widehat{L}_{N,q}(\alpha-\frac{a}{q})\;\eta_{s}(\alpha-\frac{a}{q}),

and appeal to Theorem
5.1
for the next estimate

(5.19)

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{N}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2^{k}\leq N(q)<2^{k+1}\end{subarray}}\big(L_{N,q}*\widecheck{\eta}_{s}*f\big)\;\tau_{q}\right|\right\|_{\ell^{2}}\lesssim k2^{-k}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

Thus, summing over
k
and using Parseval, we can verify that

(5.20)

\displaystyle\langle H_{T,2}*f\rangle_{E,2}\lesssim J^{-1+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\;\langle f\rangle_{E,2}

Finally, we define our last term

(5.21)

\displaystyle\widehat{L}_{T,0}:=\widehat{\operatorname{Lo}}_{K,3p^{\prime}\,\delta},

and, using Lemma
4.15
, one can easily verify that

(5.22)

\displaystyle\langle L_{T,0}*f\rangle_{E,\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim J^{1/p^{\prime}}\langle f\rangle^{1/p}_{E,1}

Putting everything together, we have shown that

(5.23)

\displaystyle\langle A_{T}f\rangle_{E,2}

\displaystyle\lesssim J^{-1+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\langle f\rangle_{E,2}

(5.24)

\displaystyle\langle A_{T}f\rangle_{E,\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim J^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\langle f\rangle_{E,p}

Last, we optimize with respect to
J
, so that

(5.25)

\displaystyle J^{-1+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\;\langle f\rangle^{1/2}_{E,1}\;\langle g\rangle^{1/2}_{E,1}\sim J^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\;\langle f\rangle^{1/p}_{E,1}\;\langle g\rangle_{E,1}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad J\sim\langle f\rangle_{E,1}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\;\langle g\rangle_{E,1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}

Since this is well-within the allowed range, we have established the desired estimate.
∎

5.3.
The Pointwise Ergodic Theorem

Let
(X,\mu,T)
be a measure preserving
\mathbb{Z}[i]
-action and recall
the definition of
M_{N}^{T}=M_{N}^{T}(d,f)
in (
1.1
).
For
R\geq 1
, set
I_{R}=\{\,\lfloor 2^{k/R}\rfloor:\;k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\,\}
. The
following Lemma shows that in order to establish Theorem
1.2
,
it suffices to prove that for each
R\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
M_{N}^{T}f
converges as

N\to\infty
with
N\in I_{R}
.

Lemma 5.26
.

Let
f\geq 0
be a measurable function on
X
. Suppose that for each
R\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
the sequence
\{M_{N}^{T}f:N\in I_{R}\}
converges almost everywhere. Then the
sequence
\{M_{N}^{T}f:N\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}
converges almost everywhere.

Proof.

We begin by observing that each sequence
I_{R}
contains
\{2^{k}:\;k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}

as a subsequence and so the limit of
M_{N}^{T}f
as
I_{R}\ni N\to\infty

does not depend on
R
; denote this limit by
L
.

Write
\rho_{k}=\lfloor 2^{k/R}\rfloor
. For
N\geq 1
, choose
k

such that
\rho_{k}\leq N<\rho_{k+1}
. Then one can easily check that

\frac{D(\rho_{k})}{D(\rho_{k+1})}\,M_{\rho_{k}}^{T}f\leq M_{N}^{T}f\leq\frac{D(\rho_{k+1})}{D(\rho_{k})}M_{\rho_{k+1}}^{T}f.

Since
D(\rho_{k+1})/D(\rho_{k})\to 2^{1/R}
as
k\to\infty
, we conclude that

2^{-1/R}L\leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}M_{N}^{T}f\leq\limsup_{N\to\infty}M_{N}^{T}f\leq 2^{1/R}L.

Letting
R\to\infty
completes the proof.
∎

For
f\in L^{p}(\mu)
, let
M_{*}^{T}f
be the maximal function

M_{*}^{T}f(x)=\sup_{N\geq 1}\left\lvert M_{N}^{T}f(x)\right\rvert.

In light of the Calderón Transference Principle
[
5
,
7
]
, Corollary

1.8

implies that
M_{*}^{T}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mu)
for all
1<p<\infty
. It then
follows, by the Banach Principle, that the set

\left\{f\in L^{p}(\mu):\;\lim_{N\to\infty}M_{N}^{T}f\;\;\text{exists $\mu$-a.e}\right\}

is closed in
L^{p}(\mu)
. Thus it remains show this set is dense. We do
this with the following oscillation inequality.

Theorem 5.27
.

For each
R\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, each sequence of integers
\{N_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
such that

N_{k+1}\geq 2N_{k}
, and
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
, we have

(5.28)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{k}\leq N<N_{k+1}\\
N\in I_{R}\end{subarray}}|M_{N}^{T}f-M_{N_{k+1}}^{T}f|\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}\lesssim_{R}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}.

Firstly, let us note that Theorem
5.27
implies the almost
everywhere convergence of
M_{N}^{T}f
for all
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
. Indeed if

M_{N}^{T}f
does not converge
\mu
-a.e, there must be some
\varepsilon>0
,
R\geq 1
,
and a sequence
\{N_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
with
N_{k+1}\geq 2N_{k}
such that for all

k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

(5.29)

\mu\left(\left\{\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{k}\leq N<N_{k+1}\\
N\in I_{R}\end{subarray}}|M_{N}^{T}f-M_{N_{k+1}}^{T}f|>\varepsilon\right\}\right)>\varepsilon.

But if
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
, Markov’s inequality and Theorem
5.27

give

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu\left(\left\{\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{k}\leq N<N_{k+1}\\
N\in I_{R}\end{subarray}}|M_{N}^{T}f-M_{N_{k+1}}^{T}f|>\varepsilon\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{k}\leq N<N_{k+1}\\
N\in I_{R}\end{subarray}}|M_{N}^{T}f-M_{N_{k+1}}^{T}f|\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{R}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2},

which contradicts (
5.29
).

Secondly, by the transference principle, it suffices to prove Theorem

5.27
in the case that
X=\mathbb{Z}[i]
,
\mu
is counting measure
and
T
is the standard shift, i.e. for
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
,
T^{n}(m)=n+m
. In fact, we focus on proving the following inequality

(5.30)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{k}\leq N<N_{k+1}\\
N\in I_{R}\end{subarray}}|A_{N}f-A_{N_{k+1}}f|\right\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\lesssim_{R}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}.

For
N\geq 1
and
s
such that
2^{s+1}\leq N^{1/20}
, we define multipliers

V_{N,s}
and
U_{N,s}
on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}[i])
by

\widehat{V_{N,s}}(\alpha)=\sum_{a/q\in\mathcal{R}_{s}}\frac{\pi^{2}}{\mathcal{N}(q)}\eta(\sqrt{N}(\alpha-a/q)),\quad\widehat{U_{N,s}}(\alpha)=\sum_{a/q\in\mathcal{R}_{s}}\eta(\sqrt{N}(\alpha-a/q)).

In addition, we define
U_{N,s}=V_{N,s}=0
when
2^{s+1}>N^{1/20}
and set

V_{N}=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}V_{N,s},\quad U_{N}=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}U_{N,s}.

Observe that these multipliers have the following properties:

(1)

If
N\geq 2M
, then
U_{M,s}U_{N,s}=U_{N,s}U_{M,s}=U_{N,s}
;

(2)

For
f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}[i])
,
V_{N,s}f=U_{N,s}\tilde{f}
, where

(5.31)

\widehat{\tilde{f}}(\alpha)=\sum_{a/q\in\mathcal{R}_{s}}\frac{\pi^{2}}{\mathcal{N}(q)}\eta_{s}(\alpha-a/q)\widehat{f}(\alpha).

Lemma 5.32
.

Let
\rho>1
. For each sequence of integers
\{N_{k}\}
such that

N_{k+1}\geq\rho N_{k}
, and each
f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}[i])
we have

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|A_{N_{k}}f-V_{N_{k}}f\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\lesssim_{\rho}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}.

Proof.

We need to prove the bound

\left\|\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\widehat{A_{N_{k}}}-\widehat{V_{N_{k}}}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{\infty}\lesssim_{\rho}1.

Recall the definition of
K^{\prime}_{N,s}
in (
4.5
). For simplicity, we redefine

K^{\prime}_{N,s}
to be
0
when
2^{s+1}>\sqrt{N}
and set

K_{N}=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}K^{\prime}_{N,s}.

Fix
\alpha\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
. Then

(5.33)

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\widehat{A_{N_{k}}}(\alpha)-\widehat{V_{N_{k}}}(\alpha)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(5.34)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\widehat{A_{N_{k}}}(\alpha)-\widehat{K_{N_{k}}}(\alpha)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\;\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\widehat{K_{N_{k}}}(\alpha)-\widehat{V_{N_{k}}}(\alpha)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(5.35)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\widehat{A_{N_{k}}}(\alpha)-\widehat{K_{N_{k}}}(\alpha)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\;\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\widehat{K^{\prime}_{N_{k},s}}(\alpha)-\widehat{V_{N_{k},s}}(\alpha)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

By Lemma
4.6
, the first term satisfies

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\widehat{A_{N_{k}}}(\alpha)-\widehat{K_{N_{k}}}(\alpha)|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(\log N_{k})^{-2}\lesssim_{\rho}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}}.

To bound the second term we note that if
|\widehat{K^{\prime}_{N_{k},s}}(\alpha)-\widehat{V_{N_{k},s}}(\alpha)|\neq 0
, there is a unique
a/q\in\mathcal{R}_{s}
such that

(5.36)

|\widehat{K^{\prime}_{N_{k},s}}(\alpha)-\widehat{V_{N_{k},s}}(\alpha)|\\
=\frac{\pi^{2}}{\mathcal{N}(q)}\left|\frac{1}{\pi N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{N}(n)\leq N\end{subarray}}e(\langle n,\alpha-a/q\rangle)-\eta(\sqrt{N_{K}}(\alpha-a/q))\right|.

Choose
k_{0}
such that
N_{k_{0}+1}^{-1}<\mathcal{N}(\alpha-a/q)\leq N_{k_{0}}^{-1}
.
If
k\leq k_{0}
and
\eta(\sqrt{N_{K}}(\alpha-a/q))=1
(
5.36
) becomes

(5.37)

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{\mathcal{N}(q)}\left|\frac{1}{\pi N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{N}(n)\leq N\end{subarray}}e(\langle n,\alpha-a/q\rangle)-1\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}(q)}((N_{k}/N_{k_{0}})^{1/2}+N_{k}^{-1/2})

(5.38)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-s}(\rho^{-(k_{0}-k)/2}+\rho^{-k/2}).

For the remaining
k\leq k_{0}
, we trivially bound (
5.36
) by
2^{-s}
.
Note that there are at most
O((\log\rho)^{-1})
such
k
. It follows that

(5.39)

\sum_{k\leq k_{0}}|\widehat{K^{\prime}_{N_{k},s}}(\alpha)-\widehat{V_{N_{k},s}}(\alpha)|^{2}\lesssim 2^{-2s}\left((\log\rho)^{-1}+\sum_{k\leq k_{0}}(\rho^{-(k_{0}-k)/2}+\rho^{-k/2})\right)^{2}\lesssim_{\rho}2^{-2s}.

For
k>k_{0}
, we have that
\eta(\sqrt{N_{K}}(\alpha-a/q))=0
, so
(
5.36
) becomes

(5.40)

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{\mathcal{N}(q)}\left|\frac{1}{\pi N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{N}(n)\leq N\end{subarray}}e(\langle n,\alpha-a/q\rangle)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}(q)}((N_{k}/N_{k_{0}})^{-3/4}+N_{k}^{-1/2})

(5.41)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-s}(\rho^{-3(k-k_{0})/4}+\rho^{-k/2}),

and so

(5.42)

\sum_{k>k_{0}}|\widehat{K^{\prime}_{N_{k},s}}(\alpha)-\widehat{V_{N_{k},s}}(\alpha)|^{2}\lesssim 2^{-2s}\left(\sum_{k>k_{0}}(\rho^{-3(k-k_{0})/4}+\rho^{-k/2})\right)^{2}\lesssim_{\rho}2^{-2s}.

Combining (
5.39
) and (
5.42
) then summing over
s

completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.27
.

As before, we have

(5.43)

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{k}\leq N<N_{k+1}\\
N\in I_{R}\end{subarray}}|A_{N}f-A_{N_{k+1}}f|\right\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(5.44)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{k}\leq N<N_{k+1}\\
N\in I_{R}\end{subarray}}|V_{N,s}f-V_{N_{k+1},s}f|\right\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\;\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|A_{N_{k}}f-V_{N_{k}}f\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

In light of Lemma
5.32
, it only remains to bound
the first term on the right.

Take
f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}[i])
and define
\tilde{f}
as in (
5.31
).
Then for each
s
and
N_{k}\leq N<N_{k+1}
,

(5.45)

\displaystyle|V_{N,s}f-V_{N_{k+1},s}f|

\displaystyle=|U_{N,s}\tilde{f}-U_{N_{k+1},s}\tilde{f}|

(5.46)

\displaystyle\leq|U_{N,s}(U_{N_{k-1},s}-U_{N_{k+1},s})\tilde{f}|+|(U_{N_{k+2},s}-U_{N_{k+1},s})\tilde{f}|.

Using this followed by Theorem
5.1
we see that

(5.47)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{k}\leq N<N_{k+1}\\
N\in I_{R}\end{subarray}}|V_{N,s}f-V_{N_{k+1},s}f|\right\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|\max_{N\in I_{R}}|U_{N,s}(U_{N_{k},s}-U_{N_{k+1},s})\tilde{f}|\right\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}

(5.48)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\log s)^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|(U_{N_{k},s}-U_{N_{k+1},s})\tilde{f}\right\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}

(5.49)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\log s)^{2}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\widehat{U_{N_{k},s}}-\widehat{U_{N_{k+1},s}}|^{2}\right\|_{\infty}\|\tilde{f}\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}

(5.50)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\log s)^{2}2^{-2s}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}.

In the last step, we used the estimate
\|\tilde{f}\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq 2^{-s}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}
and the fact that for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
, there
are at most 2 values of
k
such that

|\widehat{U_{N_{k},s}}(\alpha)-\widehat{U_{N_{k+1},s}}(\alpha)|\neq 0
,
and so the sum is uniformly bounded. Then summing over
s
produces the desired result.
∎

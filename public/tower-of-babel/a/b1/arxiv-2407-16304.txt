Lipschitz multivalued perturbations of integro-differential prox-regular sweeping processes
Tahar Haddad, Sarra Gaouir, Lionel Thibault

Abstract
. Integro-differential sweeping processes with prox-regular sets in Hilbert spaces have been the subject of various recent studies. Diverse applications of such differential inclusions to complementarity problems, electrical circuits, frictionless contact, can be found in the literature. Here we provide a general theorem of existence of solution for such processes perturbed by a Lipschitz multimapping with nonconvex values.



1.
Introduction

The Moreau’s sweeping process differential inclusion

-\frac{dx}{dt}(t)\in N_{C(t)}x(t),\quad\text{with}\;x(0)=x_{0}\in C(T_{0})

(1.1)

modeling in mechanics a point

swept by a moving set
C(t)
(depending on time

[
21

are prox-regular (resp. convex), it is generally said that (

) is the formulation of the
prox-regular (resp. convex) sweeping process
. Under the absolutely continuous variation assumption, the well-posedness in the Hilbert setting of the prox-regular sweeping process has been shown in
[
11
]
. In finite dimensions, perturbations of the ACV prox-regular sweeping process with scalarly upper semicontinuous multimappings with compact convex values were studied in
[
25
]
. Under the inclusion of such a multivalued perturbation into a fixed compact set, the ACV study was extended to Hilbert spaces in
[
5
]
; see also
[
7
,
19
,
22
]
. The paper
[
14
]
developed in Hilbert spaces
the RCBV case with multivalued perturbations satisfying suitable compact growth conditions; see also
[
22
]
. The well-posedness of perturbed ACV prox-regular sweeping processes in Hilbert spaces has been established in
[
15
]
for Lipschitz single-valued perturbations and this well-posedness has been extended in
[
1
,
23
]
under the RCBV property of the moving set

.

Recently, the integro-differential prox-regular sweeping process

\begin{cases}-\frac{dx}{dt}(t)\in N_{C(t)}x(t)+f_{1}(t,x(t))+\int_{T_{0}}^{t}f_{2}(t,s,x(s))\,ds\\
x(T_{0})=x_{0}\in C(T_{0})\end{cases}

has been studied in

,

with prox-regular sets

\begin{cases}-\frac{dx}{dt}(t)\in N_{C(t)}x(t)+f_{1}(t,x(t))+\int_{T_{0}}^{t}f_{2}(t,s,x(s))\,ds+F(t,x(t))\\

2.
Preliminaries

Let
H
be a (real) Hilbert space and let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
be its inner product and
\|\cdot\|
its associated norm. The closed ball of
H
with center
x\in H
and radius
\delta>0
is denoted

while
\mathbb{B}
stands for the closed unit ball

of

. The distance function

to a subset

of

is given by
d_{S}(x):=\inf_{y\in S}\|x-y\|
.
For any cone
P
of
H
(i.e.,
tP\subset P
for any real
t>0
), the polar of
P
is defined as

P^{\circ}:=\{\varsigma\in H:\langle\varsigma,u\rangle\leq 0,\;\forall u\in P\}.

Let
S
be a nonempty closed subset of
H
. For
x\in S
, a vector

\varsigma\in H
is a
proximal normal
to
S
at

provided that there exists a real
\eta>0
such that
x\in\mathrm{Proj}_{S}(x+\eta\varsigma)
, where
\mathrm{Proj}_{S}(y)
is the set of all nearest points from
S
to

. The cone
N_{S}(x)
of all proximal normals to
S
at

is called the

to
S
at
x
(see, e.g.,
[
26
]
). For
x\not\in S
, one puts
N_{S}(x)=\emptyset
.

When a certain uniformity of
\eta>0
holds, in the sense that there exists an extended real
R>0
such that for any
x\in S
and any
\varsigma\in N_{S}(x)
with
\|\varsigma\|\leq 1

x\in\mathrm{Proj}_{S}(x+t\varsigma)\quad\text{for all}\;t\in]0,R[,

one says that the set
S
is
R
-prox-regular (see, e.g.,
[
24
,
13
,
27
]
). For prox-regular sets all basic normal cones in variational analysis coincide. The closed set
S
is convex if and only if it is
\infty
-prox-regular. Sets which are
R
-prox-regular are known to enjoy a very long list of fundamental variational regularity properties, for which we refer the reader to,
e.g.,
[
24
,
13
,
27
]
.

One of those variational regularity properties needed in the paper is the so-called hypomonotonicity of the normal cone (see, e.g.,
[
24
,
13
,
27
]
) as stated in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

Given
R>0
a nonempty closed set
S
in
H
is
R
-prox-regular if and only if for any
x_{i}\in S
,
\varsigma_{i}\in N_{S}x_{i}
with
i=1,2
one has
the hypomonotonicity property

\langle\varsigma_{2}-\varsigma_{1},x_{2}-x_{1}\rangle\geq-\dfrac{1}{2}\bigg(\dfrac{\lVert\varsigma_{2}\rVert+\lVert\varsigma_{1}\rVert}{R}\bigg)\lVert x_{2}-x_{1}\rVert^{2}.

Another important property of a prox-regular set
S
in
H
is that its proximal normal cone
N_{S}x
at
x\in S
coincides with its Clarke normal cone
\mathcal{N}_{S}x
as said in part above, see, e.g.,
[
26
,
27
]
for the definition and the property; in particular,
N_{S}x
is closed and convex.
Further, for any point

in the prox-regular set

it is known that the directional derivative of its distance function

at

d^{\prime}_{S}(x;u):=\lim_{\tau\downarrow 0}\tau^{-1}d_{S}(x+\tau{u})

(2.1)

exists for every

(see, e.g.,
[

, Theorem 8.98]
), so the Bouligand-Peano tangent cone

T_{S}x
to

at

(see, e.g.,
[
26
]
for the definition) can be written as

T_{S}x=\{u\in H:d^{\prime}_{S}(x;u)=0\},

and hence the coincidence of the proximal normal cone
N_{S}x
with the corresponding Clarke normal cone
along with the inclusions
N_{S}x\subset(T_{S}x)^{\circ}\subset\mathcal{N}_{S}x
entail that

N_{S}x=(T_{S}x)^{\circ}=\{u\in H:d^{\prime}_{S}(x;u)=0\}^{\circ}.

(2.2)

Now let us recall the following lemma which is a consequence of Gronwall’s lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
I
be an interval of
\mathbb{R}
and let
T_{0}\in I
. Let
\rho:I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a nonnegative locally absolutely continuous function and let
b_{1},b_{2},a:I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
be non-negative locally Lebesgue integrable functions. Assume that

\dot{\rho}(t)\leq a(t)+b_{1}(t)\rho(t)+b_{2}(t)\int_{T_{0}}^{t}\rho(s)\,ds,\hskip 8.5359pta.e.\,\,t\in I.

(2.3)

Then for all
t\in I
, one has

\displaystyle\rho(t)\leq\rho(T_{0})\,\exp\bigg(\int_{T_{0}}^{t}(b(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\bigg)+\int_{T_{0}}^{t}a(s)\,\exp\bigg(\int_{s}^{t}(b(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\bigg)\,ds,

where
b(t):=\max\{b_{1}(t),b_{2}(t)\}
for
t\in I
.

We will also need the following Gronwall-like inequality.

Lemma 2.3

(Gronwall-like differential inequality)
.

Let
I
be an interval of
\mathbb{R}
, let
T_{0}\in I
and let
\rho:I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a non-negative locally absolutely continuous function. Let
K_{1},K_{2},K_{3}:I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
be non-negative locally Lebesgue integrable functions such that

\dot{\rho}(t)\leq K_{1}(t)\rho(t)+K_{2}(t)\sqrt{\rho(t)}+K_{3}(t)\sqrt{\rho(t)}\int_{T_{0}}^{t}\sqrt{\rho(s)}\,ds,\hskip 8.5359pta.e.\,\,t\in I.

(2.4)

Then for all
t\in I
, one has with

K(t):=\max\bigg\{\dfrac{K_{1}(t)}{2},\dfrac{K_{3}(t)}{2}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\sqrt{\rho(t)}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\rho(T_{0})}\,\exp\bigg(\int_{T_{0}}^{t}(K(s)+1)\,ds\bigg)\!+\!\int_{T_{0}}^{t}\dfrac{K_{2}(s)}{2}\exp\bigg(\int_{s}^{t}(K(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\bigg)ds.

Proof.

Considering the locally absolutely continuous function
\rho_{\epsilon}(\cdot):=\rho(\cdot)+\varepsilon
, we see that the assumption (
2.4
) is fulfilled with
\rho_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
in place of
\rho(\cdot)
. We may then suppose that
\rho(\cdot)>0
on
I
. Putting
\sigma(t):=\sqrt{\rho(t)}
for all
t\in I
, the function
\sigma
is locally absolutely continuous on
I
, and for a.e.
t\in I
we have

\dot{\sigma}(t)=\dfrac{\dot{\rho}(t)}{2\sqrt{\rho(t)}}=\dfrac{\dot{\rho}(t)}{2\sigma(t)},\quad\text{so}\;2\sigma(t)\dot{\sigma}(t)=\dot{\rho}(t).

Further,
\sigma(t)^{2}=\rho(t)
for all
t\in I
. Then, from (
2.4
) we have for a.e.
t\in I

2\sigma(t)\dot{\sigma}(t)\leq K_{1}(t)\sigma(t)^{2}+K_{2}(t)\sigma(t)+K_{3}(t)\sigma(t)\int_{T_{0}}^{t}\sigma(s)\,ds.

Therefore, for a.e.
t\in I
we have

\dot{\sigma}(t)\leq\dfrac{K_{2}(t)}{2}+\dfrac{K_{1}(t)}{2}\sigma(t)+\dfrac{K_{3}(t)}{2}\int_{T_{0}}^{t}\sigma(s)\,ds.

(2.5)

Letting
K(t):=\max\bigg\{\dfrac{K_{1}(t)}{2},\dfrac{K_{3}(t)}{2}\bigg\}
,
a(t):=\dfrac{K_{2}(t)}{2}
, Lemma
2.2
furnishes the desired inequality of the lemma.

3.
Main Results

Throughout the rest of the paper,
H
will be a (real) separable Hilbert space. Given the interval
I:=[T_{0},T]
, we consider the following nonconvex integro-differential sweeping process with initial condition

x_{0}\in C(T_{0})

\!\!\mathcal{P}_{f_{1},f_{2}}(x_{0},F)\!\!\begin{cases}-\dot{x}(t)\!\in\!N_{C(t)}x(t)\!+\!f_{1}(t,x(t))\!+\!\!\int_{T_{0}}^{t}\!f_{2}(t,s,x(s))ds\!+\!F(t,x(t))\,\text{a.e.}\,t\!\in\!I\\
x(T_{0})=x_{0},\end{cases}

where
f_{1}:I\times H\to H
and
f_{2}:I\times I\times H\to H
are given mappings and

C:I\rightrightarrows H
and
F:I\times H\rightrightarrows H
are given multimappings.

Our objective in this section is to state and establish the main result of existence of solutions for the integro-differential sweeping process
\big(\mathcal{P}_{f_{1},f_{2}}(x_{0},F)\big)
under appropriate hypotheses. As usual, given a closed bounded set
P
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the Lebesgue measure on
P
will be denoted
\lambda
and
L^{1}(P,H)
will be the space of Bochner

-integrable mappings from
P
into
H
. The
integral of any mapping on
P
with values in
H
in the paper will be the Bochner integral with respect
to the Lebesgue measure. Similarly,
\mathcal{C}(P,H)
will be the space of continuous mappings from
P
into
H
endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
of uniform convergence on
P
.

3.1.
Hypotheses on the data

H
as a separable real Hilbert space and we consider the interval
I=[T_{0},T]
. As in Section

\mathcal{H}(C)
.
We will take

as a multimapping with nonempty and closed values, and we will consider the following conditions :

(\mathcal{H}^{C}_{1})

(\mathcal{H}^{C}_{2})

there exists

.

\mathcal{H}(F)
.
The multimapping

(\mathcal{H}_{0}^{F})

For every
x\in H

is Lebesgue-measurable;

(\mathcal{H}_{1}^{F})

there exists a non-negative function
k(\cdot)\in L^{1}(I,\mathbb{R}_{+})

F(t,y)\subset F(t,x)+k(t)\|x-y\|\mathbb{B}\quad\text{for all}\;t\in I\;\text{and}\;x,y\in H;

(3.1)

(\mathcal{H}_{2}^{F})

there exists a non-negative function

Lemma 3.4
.

Under notation above one has

\int_{T_{0}}^{t}\Phi(T_{0},T)k(s)\int_{T_{0}}^{s}\dfrac{[c(s)-c(\tau)]^{i-1}}{(i-1)!}\gamma(\tau)d\tau ds=\int_{T_{0}}^{t}\dfrac{[c(t)-c(s)]^{i}}{i!}\gamma(s)ds.

Proof.

Put
k_{0}(t):=\Phi(T_{0},T)k(t)
, so
{k}_{0}(t)=\dot{c}(t)
since
c(t)=\Phi(T_{0},T)\int_{T_{0}}^{t}k(s)ds
. Denoting
I(t)
the integral in the left-hand side of the statement of the lemma, we have

I(t)=I_{1}(t)-I_{2}(t)
for every
t\in I
, where

I_{1}(t)=\int_{T_{0}}^{t}k_{0}(s)(\int_{T_{0}}^{t}\frac{[c(s)-c(\tau)]^{i-1}}{(i-1)!}\gamma(\tau)d\tau)ds,

I_{2}(t)=\int_{T_{0}}^{t}k_{0}(s)(\int_{s}^{t}\frac{[c(s)-c(\tau)]^{i-1}}{(i-1)!}\gamma(\tau)d\tau)ds.

Let us write by Fubini theorem

\displaystyle I_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{T_{0}}^{t}\left(\int_{T_{0}}^{t}k_{0}(s)\frac{[c(s)-c(\tau)]^{i-1}}{(i-1)!}ds\right)\gamma(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{T_{0}}^{t}\frac{[c(t)-c(\tau)]^{i}}{i!}\gamma(\tau)d\tau-\int_{T_{0}}^{t}\frac{[c(T_{0})-c(\tau)]^{i}}{i!}\gamma(\tau)d\tau.

Using again the Fubini theorem, we also have

\displaystyle I_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{T_{0}}^{t}\left(\int_{s}^{t}k_{0}(s)\frac{[c(s)-c(\tau)]^{i-1}}{(i-1)!}\gamma(\tau)d\tau\right)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{T_{0}}^{t}\left(\int_{T_{0}}^{\tau}k_{0}(s)\frac{[c(s)-c(\tau)]^{i-1}}{(i-1)!}ds\right)\gamma(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle=-\int_{T_{0}}^{t}\frac{[c(T_{0})-c(\tau)]^{i}}{i!}\gamma(\tau)d\tau.

Coming back to the equality
I(t)=I_{1}(t)-I_{2}(t)
, we deduce the desired

I(t)=\int_{T_{0}}^{t}\frac{[c(t)-c(\tau)]^{i}}{i!}\gamma(\tau)d\tau.

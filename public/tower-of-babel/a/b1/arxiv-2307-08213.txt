Lipschitz Continuous Algorithms for Covering Problems
Soh Kumabe, Yuichi Yoshida

Abstract
Combinatorial algorithms are widely used for decision-making and knowledge discovery, and it is important to ensure that their output remains stable even when subjected to small perturbations in the input. Failure to do so can lead to several problems, including costly decisions, reduced user trust, potential security concerns, and lack of replicability. Unfortunately, many fundamental combinatorial algorithms are vulnerable to small input perturbations. To address the impact of input perturbations on algorithms for weighted graph problems, Kumabe and Yoshida (FOCS’23) recently introduced the concept of Lipschitz continuity of algorithms. This work explores this approach and designs Lipschitz continuous algorithms for covering problems, such as the minimum vertex cover, set cover, and feedback vertex set problems. Our algorithm for the feedback vertex set problem is based on linear programming, and in the rounding process, we develop and use a technique called cycle sparsification, which may be of independent interest.

1.1
Lipschitz Continuity

In this section, we formally define the Lipschitz continuity of an algorithm.
We explain the definition using graph algorithms that generate a vertex set as its output, but the definition can be easily generalized to other combinatorial problems, such as the set cover problem (See Section
3
).

Consider a graph algorithm
\mathcal{A}
, that takes a graph
G=(V,E)
and a weight vector
w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{V}
, and outputs a vertex set
S\subseteq V
.
To define the Lipschitz continuity of
\mathcal{A}
, we need to define distances between input weights and output sets.
For the input weights, we use the
\ell_{1}
distance.
For the output sets, we follow the approach in
[
13
]
.
Namely, we map a vertex set
S\subseteq V
to a vector
\sum_{v\in S}w_{v}\bm{1}_{v}
, where
\bm{1}_{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
is the characteristic vector of
v\in V
, that is,
\bm{1}_{v}(u)=1
if
u=v
and
0
otherwise.
Then, we use the
\ell_{1}
distance between the mapped vectors to measure the distance between two outputs.
Specifically, for two vertex sets
S,S^{\prime}\subseteq V
and weight vectors
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{V}
, we use the distance defined as

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{w}}((S,w),(S^{\prime},w^{\prime})):=\left\|\sum_{v\in S}w_{v}\bm{1}_{v}-\sum_{v\in S^{\prime}}w^{\prime}_{v}\bm{1}_{v}\right\|_{1}=\sum_{v\in S\cap S^{\prime}}|w_{v}-w^{\prime}_{v}|+\sum_{v\in S\setminus S^{\prime}}w_{v}+\sum_{v\in S^{\prime}\setminus S}w^{\prime}_{v}.

Unfortunately, it is known that there is no deterministic Lipschitz continuous algorithm with respect to
d_{\mathrm{w}}
for most problems
[
13
]
.
Hence, our focus is on randomized algorithms, and we need to define the distance between distributions over sets.
For two probability distributions
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
over subsets of
V
, the
earth mover’s distance
between
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is defined as

\mathrm{EM}_{\mathrm{w}}\left((\mathcal{S},w),(\mathcal{S}^{\prime},w^{\prime})\right):=\min_{\mathcal{D}}\mathop{\mathbf{E}}_{(S,S^{\prime})\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[d_{\mathrm{w}}\left((S,w),(S^{\prime},w^{\prime})\right)\right],

where the minimum is taken over
couplings
of
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, that is, distributions over pairs of sets such that its marginal distributions on the first and second coordinates are equal to
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, respectively.
We note that
\mathrm{EM}_{\mathrm{w}}
coincides with
d_{\mathrm{w}}
if the distributions
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
are supported by single vertex sets.

For a randomized algorithm
\mathcal{A}
, a graph
G=(V,E)
, and a weight vector
w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{V}
, let
\mathcal{A}(G,w)
denote the (random) output of
\mathcal{A}
on
G
and
w
.
Abusing the notation, we often identify the output with its distribution.
Then, we define the Lipschitz constant of a randomized algorithm as follows:

Definition 1.1

(Lipschitz constant of a randomized algorithm
[
13
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a randomized algorithm that, given a graph
G=(V,E)
and a weight vector
w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{V}
, outputs a (random) vertex set
\mathcal{A}(G,w)\subseteq V
.
Then, the
Lipschitz constant
of the algorithm
\mathcal{A}
on a graph
G=(V,E)
is

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}w,w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{V},\\
w\neq w^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathrm{EM}_{\mathrm{w}}\left((\mathcal{A}(G,w),w),(\mathcal{A}(G,w^{\prime}),w^{\prime})\right)}{\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{1}}.

We say that
\mathcal{A}
is
Lipschitz continuous
if its Lipschitz constant is bounded for any graph
G=(V,E)
.

It is not hard to show that, if there exists an
\alpha
-approximation algorithm for a minimization problem on graphs, then for any
\epsilon>0
, we can transform it to a
(1+\epsilon)\alpha
-approximation algorithm with Lipschitz constant
\epsilon^{-1}|V|
(see Appendix
A
for details).
Hence, the question is whether we can design algorithms with Lipschitz constant sublinear in
|V|
.

Although Definition
1.1
is natural, it may not be apparent whether bounding the Lipschitz constant of a randomized algorithm is practically relevant because, even if the output distributions for two weighted graphs
(G,w)
and
(G,w^{\prime})
are close, the outputs obtained by running the algorithm on
(G,w)
and
(G,w^{\prime})
independently may be completely different.
Therefore, we consider randomized algorithms such that the outputs for two similar weighted graphs are close in expectation over the internal randomness.
More specifically, for a randomized algorithm
\mathcal{A}
, let
A_{\pi}
denote the deterministic algorithm obtained from
\mathcal{A}
by fixing its internal randomness to
\pi
.
Then, we define the Lipschitz constant with shared randomness of
\mathcal{A}
as follows:

Definition 1.2

(Lipschitz constant of a randomized algorithm with shared randomness
[
13
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a randomized algorithm that, given a graph
G=(V,E)
and a weight vector
w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{V}
, outputs a (random) vertex set
\mathcal{A}(G,w)\subseteq V
.
Then, the
Lipschitz constant under shared randomness
of the algorithm
\mathcal{A}
on a graph
G=(V,E)
is

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}w,w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{V},\\
w\neq w^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\mathop{\mathbf{E}}_{\pi}\left[\frac{d_{\mathrm{w}}\left((\mathcal{A}_{\pi}(G,w),w),(\mathcal{A}_{\pi}(G,w^{\prime}),w^{\prime})\right)}{\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{1}}\right],

where
\pi
represents the internal randomness of
\mathcal{A}
.
We say that
\mathcal{A}
is
Lipschitz continuous under shared randomness
if its Lipschitz constant under shared randomness is bounded for any graph
G=(V,E)
.

Note that if a randomized algorithm has a Lipschitz constant
L
under shared randomness, then it has Lipschitz constant
L
, and hence the former is a stronger property.

1.3
Proof Overview

If an algorithm comprises multiple steps and the later steps depend on the results of earlier steps, the Lipschitz constant generally increases at each step, eventually reaching a large value. To achieve a small Lipschitz constant, the Lipschitz constant should not increase at each step or we should break the dependencies between steps.
We provide an overview of our algorithm and how we achieve a small Lipschitz constant (without shared randomness) for each problem.
Then we discuss modifications required to achieve a small Lipschitz constant under shared randomness.

Vertex Cover

Our algorithm is a primal-dual algorithm based on the following LP:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{llll}\text{Primal}(G,w):=&\text{minimize}&\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V}w_{v}x_{v},\\
&\text{subject to}&x_{u}+x_{v}\geq 1&\forall e=(u,v)\in E,\\
&&x_{v}\geq 0&\forall v\in V.\end{array}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{llll}\text{Dual}(G,w):=&\text{maximize}&\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E}y_{e},\\
&\text{subject to}&\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y_{e}\leq w_{v}&\forall v\in V,\\
&&y_{e}\geq 0&\forall e\in E.\end{array}

Here
\delta(v)
denotes the set of edges incident to the vertex
v
.
The algorithm uses the concept of time to obtain feasible solutions for the dual problem. A vertex
v\in V
is called
tight
when it satisfies
\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y_{e}=w_{v}
. At time
0
, we set
y_{e}=0
for all edges
e\in E
, and simultaneously increment the value of
y_{e}
by
1
per unit of time as long as neither of the endpoints of
e
is tight.
Once all edges are incident to tight vertices, the algorithm adds each vertex
v
to the output with probability
\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y_{e}/w_{v}
. The approximation guarantee of
2
follows from weak LP duality.

The main technical part lies in the analysis of the Lipschitz constant. Let
w
and
w^{\prime}
be two different weight vectors. We denote the value of
y_{e}
at time
t
when the algorithm is executed with weights
w
and
w^{\prime}
as
y_{e}(t)
and
y^{\prime}_{e}(t)
, respectively. We define the
residual distance
as

\mathrm{RD}(t):=\sum_{v\in V}\left|\left(w_{v}-\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y_{e}(t)\right)-\left(w^{\prime}_{v}-\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y^{\prime}_{e}(t)\right)\right|.

We can prove that the residual distance does not increase over time.
It implies that for any given time
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,
\sum_{e\in E}|y_{e}(t)-y^{\prime}_{e}(t)|\leq|w-w^{\prime}|
, which can be used to bound the Lipschitz constant.

Naive algorithm for Set Cover

Since the minimum vertex cover problem is a special case of the minimum set cover problem, it is natural to expect that our Lipschitz continuous algorithm for the former can be extended to the latter.
However, the proof of the monotone non-increasingness of the residual distance heavily relies on the fact that each element is included in exactly two sets, so this approach does not succeed.
Therefore, we need to design new algorithms specific to the minimum set cover problem.
We design multiple algorithms, and here we overview the simplest one.

We start with the observation that the algorithm that selects, for each element
e\in U
, a set
S\ni e
with the minimum weight and outputs them is a trivial
s
-approximation algorithm, where
s
is the maximum set size in
\mathcal{F}
.
We make slight modifications to obtain a Lipschitz continuous algorithm. Specifically, instead of selecting the set with the minimum weight that contains each
e\in U
, we sample a set uniformly at random from the family of sets with their weight less than a constant times the minimum weight independently from others.
By setting the constant to achieve an approximation ratio of
s+\epsilon
, we obtain the Lipschitz constant of
O(\epsilon^{-1}s^{2})
using the independence of the sampling process.

Greedy-based algorithm for Set Cover

A well-known greedy algorithm for the minimum set cover problem is as follows: Start with an empty solution. At each step, let
R\subseteq U
be the set of vertices still not covered, select the set
S\in\mathcal{F}
that minimizes
w_{S}/|R\cap S|
, and add it to the solution. This algorithm is an
H_{s}
-approximation algorithm, where
H_{s}:=\sum_{i=1}^{s}(1/i)
.

We modify this algorithm to obtain a Lipschitz continuous algorithm.
First, we rewrite the operations of the original algorithm as follows: Define a multifamily
\mathcal{F}^{\downarrow}
as
\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{F}}(2^{S}\setminus\{\emptyset\})
, and define the weight
w^{\downarrow}_{A}
for each element
A\in\mathcal{F}^{\downarrow}
as the weight
w_{S}
of the corresponding
S\in\mathcal{F}
.
By this rephrasing, each step of the greedy algorithm can be seen as the operation of selecting the set
A\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{\downarrow}
of the minimum relative weight
w^{\downarrow}_{A}/|A|
that has no intersection with already covered elements.
Then, we introduce a positive integer constant
M
and round
w^{\downarrow}_{A}
to an integer power of
s^{1/M}
.

The main trick of our algorithm is to apply the greedy algorithm after discarding some sets of
\mathcal{F}^{\downarrow}
.
Specifically, we introduce a positive integer constant
K
and discard a set
A\in\mathcal{F}^{\downarrow}
if
\log_{s}w^{\downarrow}_{A}
belongs to a specific interval of width
1
modulo
K
.
Then, whether to include
A
in the solution only affects choices of having elements with weights less than
s^{K}\cdot w^{\downarrow}_{A}
, which breaks the dependencies between steps in the greedy algorithm.
Because we round the weights, there are at most
sKM
possible values for
w^{\downarrow}_{A}/|A|
.
If we randomly decide whether to include elements with equal (rounded) weights in the solution, we can prove that the Lipschitz constant can be bounded by a constant determined by
s
and
f
raised to the power of
sKM
.

LP-based algorithm for Set Cover

The general approach of solving an LP relaxation and then rounding the obtained fractional solution is powerful in designing approximation algorithms.
Unfortunately, it was not known whether this approach can be made Lipschitz continuous (with respect to the weighted distance).
In this work, we show a Lipschitz continuous algorithm that solves a covering LP and then use it to design Lipschitz continuous algorithms for various settings.

Let us begin by recalling the standard LP-based algorithm for the minimum set cover problem.
Given an instance
(U,\mathcal{F})
of the minimum set cover problem and a weight vector
w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathcal{F}}
, we consider the following LP relaxation:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\text{minimize}&\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}w_{S}x_{S},\\
\text{subject to}&\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}:e\in S}x_{S}\geq 1&\forall e\in U,\\
&0\leq x_{S}\leq 1&\forall S\in\mathcal{F},\end{array}

where
n=|U|
is the number of elements.
Let
\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}
denote the optimal value of LP (
1.3
) and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{F}}
be the corresponding solution.
Then for each
S\in\mathcal{F}
, we independently add it to the solution with probability
x_{S}
, repeating this process
O(\log n)
times to ensure that the output solution becomes a set cover with a high probability.
Clearly, the expected total weight of the obtained set cover is
O(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}\cdot\log n)
.

Now, we explain the modification required to make the algorithm above Lipschitz continuous.
Suppose we have two weight vectors
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathcal{F}}
and their corresponding solutions to LP (
1.3
) are denoted by
x
and
x^{\prime}
, respectively.
To achieve Lipschitz continuity in the rounding part, we need to ensure that
\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}|w_{S}x_{S}-w^{\prime}_{S}x^{\prime}_{S}|
is small.
To accomplish this, we introduce two regularizers in the objective function:

\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}w_{S}x_{S}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}x_{S}^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}\left(\frac{w_{S}x_{S}}{2W}+\frac{1}{2m}\right)\log\left(\frac{w_{S}x_{S}}{2W}+\frac{1}{2m}\right),

where
m=|\mathcal{F}|
is the number of sets,
\lambda=\Theta(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}/m)
,
\kappa=\Theta(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}/\log m)
, and
W=\Theta(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}})
1
1

1

We denote the optimal LP value as
\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}
without specifying the weights, but this is an inaccurate representation since the optimal LP values for
w
and
w^{\prime}
are different. However, we consider the situation where
w
and
w^{\prime}
are close, and thus two optimal values are also close. Therefore, in this overview, we simply write
O(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}})
to represent the amount proportional to both optimal values.
.
The addition of the first type of regularizer has been utilized in
[
17
]
to design an algorithm for the
s
-
t
minimum cut problem with low average sensitivity, and in
[
13
]
to design a Lipschitz continuous algorithm for the bipartite maximum matching problem, where the distance between two matchings is computed without using the respective weights (as opposed to
\mathrm{EM}_{\mathrm{w}}
).

To control the stability with respect to the weighted distance
d_{\mathrm{w}}(\cdot,\cdot)
, we also incorporate the second type of regularizer, which is the negative entropy of the vector
(\frac{w_{S}x_{S}}{2W}+\frac{1}{2m})_{S\in\mathcal{F}}
.
Exploiting strong convexity of these regularizers, respectively, we can show that
\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}|w_{S}x_{S}-w^{\prime}_{S}x^{\prime}_{S}|=O(\sqrt{m\log m}\cdot\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{1})
while the additional error in the objective value is
O(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}})
.

An intuitive reason that we have a factor of
O(\sqrt{m\log m})
in the above bound is as follows:
Assuming that the weight vector
w^{\prime}
is obtained from
w
by increasing
w_{S}
by
\delta>0
and
\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}=\Theta(1)
, the first regularizer guarantees that we have
\lambda(x_{S}-x^{\prime}_{S})^{2}\leq\lambda\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}=O(\delta(x_{S}-x^{\prime}_{S}))
, and hence we have
x_{S}-x^{\prime}_{S}=O(\delta/\lambda)
.
Then, the second regularizer guarantees that
\kappa(\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}|w_{S}x_{S}-w^{\prime}_{S}x^{\prime}_{S}|)^{2}=O(\delta(x_{S}-x^{\prime}_{S}))
, and hence we have
\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}|w_{S}x_{S}-w^{\prime}_{S}x^{\prime}_{S}|=O(\delta/\sqrt{\kappa\lambda})=O(\delta\sqrt{m\log m})
.

We can show that the Lipschitz constant for a single iteration of the rounding part is
\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}|w_{S}x_{S}-w^{\prime}_{S}x^{\prime}_{S}|/\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{1}=O(\sqrt{m\log m})
using the independence of the sampling process.
Since we repeat the iteration
O(\log n)
times, the final Lipschitz constant becomes
O(\sqrt{m\log m}\log n)
, while the expected total weight of the output is
O(\log n\cdot\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}})=O(\log n\cdot\mathrm{OPT})
.
Also, we note that we can improve the Lipschitz constant to
O(\sqrt{\min\{n,m\}\log m}\log n)
by exploiting the fact that there exists an optimal solution with at most
\min\{n,m\}
sets in
\mathcal{F}
.

By replacing the rounding scheme, we can also obtain a
(f+\epsilon)
-approximation algorithm with Lipschitz constant
O(\epsilon^{-1}f^{3}\sqrt{m\log m}\log n)
, where
f
is the maximum frequency of an element, which shows the versatility of the linear programming approach.

Feedback Vertex Set

The minimum feedback vertex set problem can be represented as a minimum set cover problem, where vertices correspond to sets and cycles correspond to elements.
Therefore, given a graph
G=(V,E)
and a weight vector
w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{V}
, we consider the LP relaxation (
1.3
), and we solve it using our algorithm for the minimum set cover problem with
\lambda=\Theta(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}/n)
and
\kappa=\Theta(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}/\log n)
, where
n:=|V|
is the number of vertices (corresponding to
m
in the set cover case).
This will yield an LP solution
x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{V}
such that
\sum_{v\in V}w_{v}x_{v}=O(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}})
and
\sum_{v\in C}x_{v}\geq 1
for every cycle
C
in
G
. (For technical reasons, we consider a tighter LP relaxation in the formal analysis.)
In addition, for LP solutions
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
constructed from weight vectors
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
, respectively, we have
\sum_{v\in V}|w_{v}x_{v}-w^{\prime}_{v}x^{\prime}_{v}|=O(\sqrt{n\log n}\cdot\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{1})
.

However, an issue arises in the rounding part.
To ensure that the output is a feedback vertex set, the independent sampling process needs to be repeated
O(\log|\mathcal{C}|)
times, where
\mathcal{C}
is the set of simple cycles in
G
.
However, because
|\mathcal{C}|
can be exponential in the number of vertices in
G
, this rounding approach leads to polynomial approximation ratio and Lipschitz constant, which are excessively large.

To address this issue, we introduce a technique called
cycle sparsification
and use it to round the LP solution.
For two weight vectors
z,\tilde{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
, we say that
(G,\tilde{z})
is an
\epsilon
-cycle sparsifier
of
(G,z)
if

(1-\epsilon)z(C)\leq\tilde{z}(C)\leq(1+\epsilon)z(C)

holds for every cycle
C
in
G
, where
z(C)=\sum_{v\in C}z_{v}
and
\tilde{z}(C)=\sum_{v\in C}\tilde{z}_{v}
.
A similar notion for cuts has been extensively studied under the name of
cut sparsifier

[
2
,
8
]
.

To obtain a cycle sparsifier, we modify the cut sparsification algorithm for integer-weighted graphs proposed by Fung et al.
[
8
]
.
In their algorithm, they compute the
edge connectivity

k_{e}
of each edge
e\in E
, which is the minimum weight of a cut that separates the endpoints of
e
.
Then for each edge
e\in E
with weight
z_{e}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, they sample a number
c_{e}
from the binomial distribution
\mathcal{B}(z_{e},p_{e})
, where
p_{e}=O(\log n/k_{e})
, and assign the weight
c_{e}/p_{e}
to it.
To construct a cycle sparsifier, we first compute the
girth

g_{v}
of a vertex
v\in V
, the minimum weight of a cycle passing through the vertex.
Then for each vertex
v\in V
with weight
z_{e}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we sample a number
c_{v}
from the binomial distribution
\mathcal{B}(z_{v},p_{v})
, where
p_{v}=O(\log n/g_{v})
, and assign the weight
c_{v}/p_{v}
to it.

In the analysis of the cut sparsifier proposed by Fung et al.
[
8
]
, they used a cut counting lemma, which asserts that, for any edge set
F\subseteq E
, the number of subsets of
F
induced by small cuts is small.
We need a counterpart of this lemma for cycles, that is, we want to show that for any vertex set
S\subseteq V
, the number of subsets of
S
induced by small cycles is small.
We prove this by generalizing the known argument for
S=V

[
16
]
.

Now, we explain how we use the cycle sparsification result for rounding.
Consider an LP solution
x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{V}
for the minimum feedback vertex set problem.
Note that in the weighted graph
(G,x)
, every vertex has a girth of at least one.
To round the LP solution
x
, we first construct an integer vector
z\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{V}
by scaling it by a factor
n^{2}
.
Then, we apply cycle sparsification to
z
using the girth lower bound of
n^{2}
.
The resulting vector
\tilde{z}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{V}
is a cycle sparsifier with a high probability.
This implies that the set
S=\{v\in V:\tilde{z}_{v}>0\}
is a feedback vertex set because every cycle has a positive weight in
\tilde{z}
.
Furthermore, we can show that the total weight of
S
is
O(\sum_{v\in V}w_{v}x_{v}\log n)
, which is
O(\log n\cdot\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}})
.

Now let us consider Lipschitz continuity of the algorithm.
Let
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
be the LP solutions for weight vectors
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
, respectively.
Then, considering that our cycle sparsification algorithm samples weights of vertices independently, we can show that it amplifies the Lipschitz constant merely by
O(\log n)
.
Combined with the bound
\sum_{v\in V}|w_{v}x_{v}-w^{\prime}_{v}x^{\prime}_{v}|=O(\sqrt{n\log n}\cdot\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{1})
, we can show that the Lipschitz constant of the entire algorithm is
O(\sqrt{n}\log^{3/2}n)
.

Lipschitz Continuity under Shared Randomness

Our algorithms can straightforwardly be made Lipschitz continuous even in the shared randomness setting.
Specifically, we replace the random sampling processes in our algorithms with stable sampling procedures developed by Kumabe and Yoshida
[
13
]
to ensure Lipschitz continuity under shared randomness.

1.4
Related Work

As mentioned earlier, Kumabe and Yoshida introduced the concept of Lipschitzness for graph algorithms
[
13
]
.
They devised Lipschitz continuous algorithms for the minimum spanning tree, shortest path, and maximum matching problems.
This work expands the scope of Lipschitz continuous algorithms to covering problems.
Although different possible notions of Lipschitz continuity were considered, the ones used in this work (Definitions
1.1
and
1.2
) are arguably the most natural.

The average sensitivity of algorithms, introduced by Varma and Yoshida
[
17
]
, is a concept closely related to Lipschitz continuity.
The
average sensitivity
of an algorithm on an (unweighted) instance
X=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
consisting of
n
elements is the average earth mover’s distance between the output for
X
and that for
X\setminus\{x_{i}\}
, where we take the expectation over
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.
They showed algorithms for the minimum spanning tree, minimum cut, and maximum matching problems with low average sensitivity.
Since then algorithms with low average sensitivity have been proposed for various problems, including the minimum cut and maximum matching problems
[
20
]
,
k
-means
[
19
]
, decision tree learning
[
9
]
, spectral clustering
[
15
]
.
We note that bounding the Lipschitz constant is usually more challenging than bounding the average sensitivity because it considers the worst-case input modification and requires dealing with arbitrarily small yet non-zero perturbations.

For a (continuous or discrete) set

For a vector

The goal of this section is to show the following:

Theorem 2.1
.

.

We first look at the primal and dual LP formulations for the minimum vertex cover problem.
In the primal LP, the variable

for a vertex

represents whether it belongs to a vertex cover.

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{llll}\text{Primal}(G,w):=&\text{minimize}&\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V}w_{v}x_{v},\\

&&x_{v}\geq 0&\forall v\in V.\end{array}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{llll}\text{Dual}(G,w):=&\text{maximize}&\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E}y_{e},\\
&\text{subject to}&\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y_{e}\leq w_{v}&\forall v\in V,\\
&&y_{e}\geq 0&\forall e\in E.\end{array}

(

Lipschitz continuous algorithm for the minimum vertex cover problem

Our algorithm,

.
In the while loop starting from Line

, we call a vertex

.
This algorithm is nearly identical to the standard primal-dual algorithm (see, e.g., Section 7.1 of

2.1
Approximation Ratio

The following holds.

Lemma 2.2
.

VertexCover
is a
2
-approximation algorithm.

Proof.

Since removing vertices
v\in V
with
w_{v}=0
does not affect the algorithm and the optimal value, we can assume
w_{v}>0
for every
v\in V
to analyze approximation ratio without loss of generality.
At the end of each iteration in the while loop starting at Line
1
,
A
forms a set of edges such that at least one of the endpoints is tight.
By the termination condition of the loop, at the end of the loop, at least one endpoint of each edge is tight.
Since the output must include all tight vertices, Algorithm
1
outputs a vertex cover.

Note that the value of
y_{e}
corresponding to an edge
e\in E
incident to a tight vertex does not increase further.
Therefore, at the end of the algorithm, for every vertex
v\in V
, we have
\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y_{e}\leq w_{v}
.
In particular,
y
is a feasible solution for
\mathrm{Dual}(G,w)
.
Let
\bm{C}=\textsc{VertexCover}(G,w)
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbf{E}}\left[\sum_{v\in\bm{C}}w_{v}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V}\left(w_{v}\cdot\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y_{e}}{w_{v}}\right)=\sum_{v\in V}\left(\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y_{e}\right)=2\sum_{e\in E}y_{e}\leq 2\cdot\mathrm{OPT},

where the last inequality is because
y
is a feasible solution for
\mathrm{Dual}(G,w)
and the weak duality theorem of linear programming.
∎

2.2
Lipschitz Continuity

Now we evaluate the Lipschitz continuity.
For two weight vectors
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{V}
, let us evaluate

\displaystyle\mathrm{EM}_{\mathrm{w}}\left((\textsc{VertexCover}(G,w),w),(\textsc{VertexCover}(G,w^{\prime}),w^{\prime})\right).

Let
y_{e}
and
y^{\prime}_{e}
denote the values of the variable
y_{e}
in
VertexCover
(
G,w
) and
VertexCover
(
G,w^{\prime}
), respectively.
We introduce the concept of
time
to the loop starting from Line
1
and regard that the algorithm continuously increments the value of
y_{e}
for each edge
e\in E
by
1
per unit time unless one of the endpoints is tight.
Let
y_{e}(t)
and
y^{\prime}_{e}(t)
be the value of
y_{e}
and
y^{\prime}_{e}
at time
t
during the execution of
VertexCover
(
G,w
) and
VertexCover
(
G,w^{\prime}
), respectively. It can be easily observed that, for every
e\in E
, there exists a time
t_{e}
such that
y_{e}(t)
is expressed as follows:

\displaystyle y_{e}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}t&(t\leq t_{e})\\
t_{e}&(t_{e}<t).\end{array}\right.

We call the value

\mathrm{RD}(t)=\sum_{v\in V}\left|\left(w_{v}-\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y_{e}(t)\right)-\left(w^{\prime}(v)-\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y^{\prime}_{e}(t)\right)\right|

the
residual distance
between
y_{e}
and
y^{\prime}_{e}
at time
t
.
Let us evaluate how the residual distance changes during the loop starting at Line
1
.
At the beginning of the loop, the residual distance is
\mathrm{RD}(0)=\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{1}
.

For a function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, we denote the right derivative of
f
by
\partial_{+}f(t)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\partial_{+}f(t):=\lim_{x\rightarrow+0}\frac{f(t+x)-f(t)}{x}.

The following lemma is the heart of our Lipschitz continuity analysis.

Lemma 2.3
.

\partial_{+}\mathrm{RD}(t)\leq 0
.

Proof.

Treating
y_{e}(t)
’s and
y^{\prime}_{e}(t)
’s as functions of
t
, using an integer function
c_{e}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}
defined for each
e\in E
, the right derivative of
\mathrm{RD}(t)
can be represented as

\displaystyle\partial_{+}\mathrm{RD}(t)=\sum_{e\in E}c_{e}(t)\left(\partial_{+}y_{e}(t)-\partial_{+}y^{\prime}_{e}(t)\right),

where
c_{e}(t)
takes only values from
\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}
because for each edge
e\in E
, the term
y_{e}(t)-y^{\prime}_{e}(t)
appears twice in the expression of
\mathrm{RD}(t)
.

Let
A(t)
and
A^{\prime}(t)
denote the set
A
at time
t
in
\textsc{VertexCover}(G,w)
and
\textsc{VertexCover}(G,w^{\prime})
, respectively.
Then,
\partial_{+}y_{e}(t)
takes the value
1
if
e\in A(t)
and
0
otherwise.
Similarly,
\partial_{+}y^{\prime}_{e}(t)
takes the value
1
if
e\in A^{\prime}(t)
and
0
otherwise. Using these facts, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{+}\mathrm{RD}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E}c_{e}\left(\partial_{+}y_{e}(t)-\partial_{+}y^{\prime}_{e}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in A(t)\cap A^{\prime}(t)}c_{e}\cdot 0+\sum_{e\in A(t)\setminus A^{\prime}(t)}c_{e}\cdot 1+\sum_{e\in A^{\prime}(t)\setminus A(t)}c_{e}\cdot(-1)+\sum_{e\in E\setminus(A(t)\cap A^{\prime}(t))}c_{e}\cdot 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in A(t)\setminus A^{\prime}(t)}c_{e}-\sum_{e\in A^{\prime}(t)\setminus A(t)}c_{e}.

Let
e=(u,v)\in A(t)\setminus A^{\prime}(t)
. From the definitions of
A(t)
and
A^{\prime}(t)
, neither
u
nor
v
is tight at time
t
in
VertexCover
(
G,w
), while in
VertexCover
(
G,w^{\prime}
), at least one of them, say
u
, is tight. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{+}\left|\left(w_{u}-\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y_{e}(t)\right)-\left(w^{\prime}_{u}-\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y^{\prime}_{e}(t)\right)\right|=\partial_{+}\left|\left(w_{u}-\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y_{e}(t)\right)-0\right|

\displaystyle=\partial_{+}\left(w_{u}-\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y_{e}(t)\right)=-\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}\partial_{+}y_{e}(t).

In particular, at least once in the sum of
\partial_{+}\mathrm{RD}(t)
, the term
\partial_{+}y_{e}(t)
makes a negative contribution.
Additionally, because
y_{e}(t)-y^{\prime}_{e}(t)
appears twice in the expression of
\mathrm{RD}(t)
, we have
c_{e}\leq 0
.

By a similar argument, we obtain
c_{e}\geq 0
for
e\in A^{\prime}(t)\setminus A(t)
. Therefore, the claim holds.
∎

The following is immediate from Lemma
2.3
.

Lemma 2.4
.

At the end of the loop starting at Line
1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V}\left|\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y_{e}-\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y^{\prime}_{e}\right|\leq 2\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{1}.

Proof.

Let
T
be the later of the times
\textsc{VertexCover}(G,w)
and
\textsc{VertexCover}(G,w^{\prime})
terminate.
Then, from Lemma

, we have
\mathrm{RD}(T)\leq\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V}\left|\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y_{e}(T)-\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y^{\prime}_{e}(T)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in V}\left(\left|\left(w_{v}-\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y_{e}(t)\right)-\left(w^{\prime}_{v}-\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y^{\prime}_{e}(t)\right)\right|+\left|w_{v}-w^{\prime}_{v}\right|\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{RD}(T)+\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{1}\leq 2\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{1}.\qed

Lemma 2.5
.

VertexCover

4
.

Proof.

By Lemma
1.3
, we can assume there exists a vertex

such that
w_{v}=w^{\prime}_{v}
holds for

and
w_{u}<w^{\prime}_{u}

To bound the earth mover’s distance between the output vertex covers for
(G,w)
and
(G,w^{\prime})
,
we consider the coupling
\mathcal{W}
between
\bm{z}
and
\bm{z}^{\prime}

\bm{z}^{\prime}=z

When

\displaystyle\mathrm{EM}_{\mathrm{w}}\left((\textsc{VertexCover}(G,w),w),(\textsc{VertexCover}(G,w^{\prime}),w^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in V\setminus\{u\}\colon w_{v}>0}\left|\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y_{e}}{w_{v}}-\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y^{\prime}_{e}}{w_{v}}\right|\cdot w_{v}+\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y^{\prime}_{e}}{w^{\prime}_{u}}\cdot w^{\prime}_{u}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in V\setminus\{u\}\colon w_{v}>0}\left|\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y_{e}-\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y^{\prime}_{e}\right|+1\cdot w^{\prime}_{u}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in V}\left|\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y_{e}-\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y^{\prime}_{e}\right|+\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{1}\leq 3\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{1},

where the first inequality is because
w_{v}\neq w^{\prime}_{v}
holds for
u=v
and
w_{u}=0
.
Otherwise, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{EM}_{\mathrm{w}}\left((\textsc{VertexCover}(G,w),w),(\textsc{VertexCover}(G,w^{\prime}),w^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in V\setminus\{u\}\colon w_{v}>0}\left|\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y_{e}}{w_{v}}-\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y^{\prime}_{e}}{w_{v}}\right|\cdot w_{v}

\displaystyle\quad+\min\left(\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y_{e}}{w_{u}},\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y^{\prime}_{e}}{w^{\prime}_{u}}\right)\cdot(w^{\prime}_{u}-w_{u})+\left|\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y_{e}}{w_{u}}-\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y^{\prime}_{e}}{w^{\prime}_{u}}\right|\cdot w^{\prime}_{u}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in V\setminus\{u\}\colon w_{v}>0}\left|\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y_{e}-\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y^{\prime}_{e}\right|+1\cdot(w^{\prime}_{u}-w_{u})+\left|\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y_{e}}{w_{u}}-\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y^{\prime}_{e}}{w^{\prime}_{u}}\right|\cdot w^{\prime}_{u}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in V\setminus\{u\}\colon w_{v}>0}\left|\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y_{e}-\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y^{\prime}_{e}\right|+(w^{\prime}_{u}-w_{u})

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\left|\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y_{e}}{w_{u}}-\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y_{e}}{w^{\prime}_{u}}\right|+\left|\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y_{e}}{w^{\prime}_{u}}-\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y^{\prime}_{e}}{w^{\prime}_{u}}\right|\right)\cdot w^{\prime}_{u}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in V\setminus\{u\}\colon w_{v}>0}\left|\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y_{e}-\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y^{\prime}_{e}\right|+(w^{\prime}_{u}-w_{u})+\left|\frac{w_{u}}{w_{u}}-\frac{w_{u}}{w^{\prime}_{u}}\right|\cdot w^{\prime}_{u}+\left|\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y_{e}-\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y^{\prime}_{e}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in V\colon w_{v}>0}\left|\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y_{e}-\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}y^{\prime}_{e}\right|+2(w^{\prime}_{u}-w_{u})

\displaystyle\leq 2\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{1}+2\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{1}=4\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{1},

where the first inequality is because
w_{v}=w^{\prime}_{v}
holds for
v\neq u
.
The contribution of vertex
u
to the earth mover’s distance is

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y_{e}}{w_{u}}\cdot|w^{\prime}_{u}-w_{u}|+\left(\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y^{\prime}_{e}}{w^{\prime}_{u}}-\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y_{e}}{w_{u}}\right)\cdot w^{\prime}_{u}

\displaystyle\leq\min\left(\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y_{e}}{w_{u}},\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y^{\prime}_{e}}{w^{\prime}_{u}}\right)\cdot(w^{\prime}_{u}-w_{u})+\left|\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y_{e}}{w_{u}}-\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y^{\prime}_{e}}{w^{\prime}_{u}}\right|\cdot w^{\prime}_{u}

for
\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y_{e}}{w_{u}}\leq\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y^{\prime}_{e}}{w^{\prime}_{u}}
and

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y^{\prime}_{e}}{w^{\prime}_{u}}\cdot|w^{\prime}_{u}-w_{u}|+\left(\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y_{e}}{w_{u}}-\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y^{\prime}_{e}}{w^{\prime}_{u}}\right)\cdot w_{u}

\displaystyle\leq\min\left(\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y_{e}}{w_{u}},\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y^{\prime}_{e}}{w^{\prime}_{u}}\right)\cdot(w^{\prime}_{u}-w_{u})+\left|\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y_{e}}{w_{u}}-\frac{\sum_{e\in\delta(u)}y^{\prime}_{e}}{w^{\prime}_{u}}\right|\cdot w^{\prime}_{u}

otherwise.

Therefore, the Lipschitz constant of Algorithm
1
is

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}w,w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{V},\\
w\neq w^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathrm{EM}_{\mathrm{w}}\left((\textsc{VertexCover}(G,w),w),(\textsc{VertexCover}(G,w^{\prime}),w^{\prime})\right)}{\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{1}}\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}w,w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{V},\\
w\neq w^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{4\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{1}}{\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{1}}=4.

Hence, the claim holds.

follows by combining Lemmas

.

2.3
Lower Bound

Here, we give a lower bound on the Lipschitz constant for the minimum vertex cover problem.

Lemma 2.6
.

For
\epsilon>0
, any (not necessarily polynomial-time) exact algorithm for the minimum vertex cover problem has Lipschitz constant
\Omega(|V|)
.

Proof.

Let
n
be a positive integer and
G=(V,E)
be a complete bipartite graph, where each part
V_{1},V_{2}
has size

.
Clearly, the minimum vertex cover for the weight vector
w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{V}
is the part with the smaller total weight. Let
v_{1}\in V_{1}
and
v_{2}\in V_{2}
.
Now, consider the weight vectors
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{V}
given by

\displaystyle w_{v}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1&(v\neq v_{1})\\
0&(v=v_{1})\end{array}\right.,\quad w^{\prime}_{v}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1&(v\neq v_{2})\\
0&(v=v_{2})\end{array}\right..

Since any exact algorithm outputs
V_{1}
for the weight
w
and
V_{2}
for the weight

, the Lipschitz constant is at least

\frac{\|\bm{1}_{V_{1}\setminus\{v_{1}\}}-\bm{1}_{V_{2}\setminus\{v_{2}\}}\|_{1}}{\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{1}}=\frac{2n-2}{2}\geq\Omega(|V|).\qed

We first formally define the Lipschitz constant of an algorithm for the set cover problem:

Definition 3.1

(Lipschitz constant of a randomized algorithm for the set cover problem)

Let
\mathcal{A}

(U,\mathcal{F})

This process allows us to construct a maximal set packing. The result in Censor-Hillel, Haramaty, and Karnin states the following.

Lemma 4.2

where we abuse notation

4.2
Approximation Ratio

Let us analyze the approximation ratio.
We say that a set
S\in\mathcal{F}
is
hashed
(with respect to
\bm{i}_{S}
) if
\bm{i}_{S}\bmod KM<M\log_{s}|S|
.
When a set
S
is hashed, only singleton subsets of
S
are added to the set
\bm{Q}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

LipschitzGreedy
(
U,\mathcal{F},w,K,M
)
has an approximation ratio
H_{s}\cdot\left(1+\frac{s-1}{K}\right)\cdot s^{1/M}
.

Proof.

Let
\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{hash}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
be the set of hashed sets.
Let
\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{F}}}
be the family of sets over
U
defined by

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{F}}}=(\mathcal{F}\setminus\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{hash}})\cup\{\{e\}:S\in\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{hash}},e\in S\}.

The weight
\widehat{\bm{w}}_{S}
of a set
S\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{hash}}
is defined as
s^{K\bm{b}+(\bm{i}_{S}+1)/M}
, and the weight
\widehat{\bm{w}}_{\{e\}}
for
e\in S\in\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{hash}}
is defined as
s^{K\bm{b}+(\bm{i}_{S}+1)/M}
.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{*}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
be a minimum set cover of the instance
(U,\mathcal{F},w)
. Let
\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{C}}}^{*}
be the family of sets in
\mathcal{F}
defined by

\displaystyle(\mathcal{C}^{*}\setminus\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{hash}})\cup\{\{e\}:S\in\mathcal{C}^{*}\cap\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{hash}},e\in S\}.

It is clear that
\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{C}}}^{*}
is a set cover of
(U,\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{F}}},\widehat{\bm{w}})
. Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbf{E}}\left[\mathrm{OPT}_{U,\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{F}}},\widehat{\bm{w}}}\right]\leq\mathop{\mathbf{E}}\left[\widehat{\bm{w}}\left(\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{C}}}^{*}\right)\right]\leq s^{1/M}\mathop{\mathbf{E}}\left[w\left(\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{C}}}^{*}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=s^{1/M}\left(\mathop{\mathbf{E}}\left[w(\mathcal{C}^{*}\setminus\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{hash}})+s\cdot w(\mathcal{C}^{*}\cap\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{hash}})\right]\right)

\displaystyle=s^{1/M}\left(w(\mathcal{C}^{*})+(s-1)\mathop{\mathbf{E}}\left[w(\mathcal{C}^{*}\cap\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{hash}})\right]\right)

\displaystyle=s^{1/M}\left(w(\mathcal{C}^{*})+(s-1)\int_{0}^{1}\left(\sum_{S\in\mathcal{C}^{*}\colon\text{hashed w.r.t.\ }\bm{i}_{S}}w_{S}\right)\mathrm{d}b\right)

\displaystyle\leq s^{1/M}\left(w(\mathcal{C}^{*})+(s-1)\int_{0}^{1}\left(\sum_{S\in\mathcal{C}^{*}\colon\text{hashed w.r.t.\ }\bm{i}_{S}}w_{S}\right)\mathrm{d}b\right)

\displaystyle=s^{1/M}\left(w(\mathcal{C}^{*})+\frac{s-1}{K}w(\mathcal{C}^{*})\right)\leq s^{1/M}\left(1+\frac{s-1}{K}\right)\mathrm{OPT}_{U,\mathcal{F},w}.

Here,
\mathrm{OPT}_{U,\mathcal{F},w}
and
\mathrm{OPT}_{U,\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{F}}},\widehat{\bm{w}}}
represent the optimal values for
(U,\mathcal{F},w)
and
(U,\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{F}}},\widehat{\bm{w}})
, respectively.

Recalling that
LipschitzGreedy
simulates
ClassicalGreedy
(
U,\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{F}}},\widehat{\bm{w}}
), we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbf{E}}\left[w\left(\textsc{LipschitzGreedy}(U,\mathcal{F},w,K,M)\right)\right]\leq H_{s}\mathop{\mathbf{E}}\left[\mathrm{OPT}_{U,\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{F}}},\widehat{\bm{w}}}\right]\leq H_{s}\cdot s^{1/M}\left(1+\frac{s-1}{K}\right)\mathrm{OPT}_{U,\mathcal{F},w}.

Therefore the lemma is proved.
∎

4.3
Lipschitz Continuity

Now we analyze the Lipschitz constant.
By Lemma

, it suffices to evaluate

\displaystyle\mathrm{EM}_{\mathrm{w}}\left((\textsc{LipschitzGreedy}(U,\mathcal{F},w,K,M),w),(\textsc{LipschitzGreedy}(U,\mathcal{F},w+\delta\bm{1}_{T},K,M),w+\delta\bm{1}_{T})\right).

\bm{b}

\bm{b}
sampled in

LipschitzGreedy

We use similar notations for

,
\bm{Q}

To bound the earth mover’s distance, we consider the coupling

between

Firstly, we evaluate the probability that

Lemma 4.4
.

If

\Pr\left[\bm{i}_{T}\neq\bm{i}^{\prime}_{T}\right]\leq\frac{\delta M}{w_{T}\log s}

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\Pr_{\mathcal{W}}\left[\bm{i}_{T}\neq\bm{i}^{\prime}_{T}\right]

Consider the case

The following is easy but important.

Lemma 4.5
.

For

Proof.

.
Assume

. Then, we have

\displaystyle x_{1}<\left(\bm{l}(S_{1})+1\right)KM\leq\bm{l}(S_{2})KM\leq x_{2},

where the last inequality is because in Line

From Lemma

Since the sets of elements covered by

We can assume without loss of generality that there exists no set

In particular, any set that differs between

\bm{l}(T)
or

\displaystyle\bm{X}=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}\bigcup_{x=\left\lceil{M\log_{s}i}\right\rceil}^{KM-1}\left\{\bm{l}(T)KM+x-M\log_{s}i,\bm{l}^{\prime}(T)KM+x-M\log_{s}i\right\}

when

\displaystyle\bm{X}=\{-\infty\}\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}\bigcup_{x=\left\lceil{M\log_{s}i}\right\rceil}^{KM-1}\left\{\bm{l}^{\prime}(T)KM+x-M\log_{s}i\right\}

For

Lemma 4.6
.

Let

. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{W}}\left[\left|\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{k}\;\triangle\;\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{k}\right|\right]\leq(2^{s}sf+1)^{k+3}.

Proof.

We prove this by induction on

.
Let
\bm{D}_{k}
(resp.
\bm{D}^{\prime}_{k}
) be the set of tuples
(x,A,S)
in
\bm{Q}
with

holds when

(
U,\mathcal{F},w,K,M
) (resp.,

(

(\tilde{x},\tilde{A},\tilde{S})
in
\bm{Q}
with

.
Furthermore, let
\bm{R}_{k}
(resp.
\bm{R}^{\prime}_{k}
) be
\bm{R}
at that time.
From Lemma

, we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{W}}\left[\left|\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{k}\;\triangle\;\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{k}\right|\right]\leq 2\cdot\mathop{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{W}}\left[\left|\bm{D}_{k}\;\triangle\;\bm{D}^{\prime}_{k}\right|\right].

Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{W}}\left[\left|\bm{R}_{k+1}\;\triangle\;\bm{R}^{\prime}_{k+1}\right|\right]\leq s\cdot\mathop{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{W}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left|\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{k}\;\triangle\;\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{k}\right|\right].

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{W}}\left[\left|\bm{D}_{k+1}\;\triangle\;\bm{D}^{\prime}_{k+1}\right|\right]\leq f\cdot 2^{s}\cdot\mathop{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{W}}\left[\left|\bm{R}_{k+1}\;\triangle\;\bm{R}^{\prime}_{k+1}\right|\right]+2\cdot 2^{s},

where the last term counts the number of tuples

with

.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{W}}\left[\left|\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{k+1}\;\triangle\;\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{k+1}\right|\right]\leq 2\cdot\mathop{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{W}}\left[\left|\bm{D}_{k+1}\;\triangle\;\bm{D}^{\prime}_{k+1}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq f\cdot 2^{s}\cdot\mathop{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{W}}\left[\left|\bm{R}_{k+1}\;\triangle\;\bm{R}^{\prime}_{k+1}\right|\right]+2\cdot 2^{s}\leq f\cdot 2^{s}\cdot s\cdot\mathop{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{W}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left|\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{k}\;\triangle\;\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{k}\right|\right]+2\cdot 2^{s}.

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{W}}\left[\left|\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{k}\;\triangle\;\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{k}\right|\right]\leq\left(2^{s}sf+1\right)^{k+3}-2\cdot 2^{s}

by induction. Therefore the lemma holds.
∎

Since there are at most

Lemma 4.7
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{W}}\left[\left|\bm{\mathcal{C}}\;\triangle\;\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\right|\right]\leq\left(2^{s}sf+1\right)^{2sKM+4}.

Proof.

We have

\mathop{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{W}}\left[\left|\bm{\mathcal{C}}\;\triangle\;\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\right|\right]=\sum_{k=1}^{|X|}\mathop{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{W}}\left[\left|\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{k}\;\triangle\;\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{k}\right|\right]\leq\sum_{k=1}^{|X|}(2^{s}sf+1)^{k+3}\leq(2^{s}sf+1)^{|X|+4}\leq\left(2^{s}sf+1\right)^{2sKM+4}.\qed

The following is straightforward.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
S\in\mathcal{F}
and assume
S
has level
\bm{l}(T)
or
\bm{l}^{\prime}(T)
.
Then,
w_{S}\leq s^{K}(w_{T}+\delta)
.

Proof.

Since
\bm{l}(S)\leq\bm{l}^{\prime}(T)
, we have

\displaystyle w_{S}

\displaystyle\leq s^{Kb+(i_{S}+1)/M}\leq s^{Kb+(KM\cdot\bm{l}(S)+KM-1+1)/M}\leq s^{K}\cdot s^{Kb+(KM\cdot\bm{l}(S))/M}

\displaystyle\leq s^{K}\cdot s^{Kb+(KM\cdot\bm{l}^{\prime}(T))/M}\leq s^{K}\cdot w^{\prime}(T)=s^{K}(w_{T}+\delta).\qed

Therefore, the following holds.

Lemma 4.9
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathrm{EM}_{\mathrm{w}}\left(\left(\textsc{LipschitzGreedy}(U,\mathcal{F},w,K,M),w\right),\left(\textsc{LipschitzGreedy}(U,\mathcal{F},w+\delta\bm{1}_{T},K,M),w+\delta\bm{1}_{T}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq 3M\left(2^{s}sf+1\right)^{5sKM}\cdot\delta.

Proof.

If
w_{T}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{EM}_{\mathrm{w}}\left(\left(\textsc{LipschitzGreedy}(U,\mathcal{F},w,K,M),w\right),\left(\textsc{LipschitzGreedy}(U,\mathcal{F},w+\delta\bm{1}_{T},K,M),w+\delta\bm{1}_{T}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{\mathbf{E}}\left[\left|\bm{\mathcal{C}}\;\triangle\;\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\right|\right]\cdot s^{K}(0+\delta)\leq\left(2^{s}sf+1\right)^{2sKM+4}\cdot s^{K}\cdot\delta\leq\left(2^{s}sf+1\right)^{2sKM+4+K}\cdot\delta\leq\left(2^{s}sf+1\right)^{5sKM}\cdot\delta,

where the second inequality is from Lemmas

and
4.8

.
Otherwise, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{EM}_{\mathrm{w}}\left(\left(\textsc{LipschitzGreedy}(U,\mathcal{F},w,K,M),w\right),\left(\textsc{LipschitzGreedy}(U,\mathcal{F},w+\delta\bm{1}_{T},K,M),w+\delta\bm{1}_{T}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Pr\left[\bm{i}_{T}\neq\bm{i}^{\prime}_{T}\right]\cdot\mathop{\mathbf{E}}\left[\left|\bm{\mathcal{C}}\;\triangle\;\bm{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\right|\right]\cdot s^{K}(w_{T}+\delta)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta M}{w_{T}\log s}\cdot\left(2^{s}sf+1\right)^{2sKM+4}\cdot s^{K}\cdot 2w_{T}\leq 3M\left(2^{s}sf+1\right)^{2sKM+4+K}\cdot\delta\leq 3M\left(2^{s}sf+1\right)^{5sKM}\cdot\delta,

where the second inequality is from Lemmas

,
4.7
,
4.8
and

, and the third inequality is from

.

.

Next, we prove the main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let
K=\left\lceil{4\epsilon^{-1}H_{s}}\right\rceil(s-1)
and

, the approximation ratio is

\displaystyle H_{s}\cdot\left(1+\frac{s-1}{K}\right)\cdot s^{1/M}\leq H_{s}\cdot\left(1+\frac{\epsilon}{4H_{s}}\right)\cdot 2^{\frac{\epsilon}{4H_{s}}}

\displaystyle\leq H_{s}\cdot\left(1+\frac{\epsilon}{4H_{s}}\right)\cdot\left(1+\frac{\epsilon}{4H_{s}}\right)\leq H_{s}\cdot\left(1+\frac{\epsilon}{H_{s}}\right)=H_{s}+\epsilon,

where the second inequality is from

, and the third inequality is from

. In this setting of

and

, the Lipschitz constant of

is at most

\displaystyle 3M\left(2^{s}sf+1\right)^{5sKM}\leq 3\cdot 5\epsilon^{-1}H_{s}\log s\cdot\left(2^{s}sf+1\right)^{5s\cdot\left(5\epsilon^{-1}H_{s}\right)^{2}\cdot(s-1)\log s}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{O\left(\log H_{s}+\log\epsilon^{-1}+\log\log s+(s+\log s+\log f)\cdot s\cdot H_{s}^{2}\cdot\epsilon^{-2}\cdot s\log s\right)}\leq 2^{O\left(\epsilon^{-2}\left(s+\log f\right)s^{2}\log^{3}s\right)},

where the first inequality is from
K,M\leq 5\epsilon^{-1}
follows from

.
∎

5
LP-based
O(\log n)
-Approximation Algorithm for Set Cover

In this section, we design a Lipschitz-continuous algorithm for the minimum set cover problem that has a sublinear Lipschitz constant for any instance.
Specifically, we show the following:

Theorem 5.1
.

There exists a polynomial-time randomized algorithm for the minimum set cover problem that, given a set cover instance
(U,\mathcal{F})
on
n
elements and
m
sets and a weight vector
w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathcal{F}}
, outputs a family of sets
\bm{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
with the following properties:

•

\bm{\mathcal{S}}
is a feasible solution with probability
1-1/\mathrm{poly}(n)
,

•

the total weight of
\bm{\mathcal{S}}
satisfies
\mathop{\mathbf{E}}[\sum_{S\in\bm{\mathcal{S}}}w_{S}]=O(\log n\cdot(\mathrm{OPT}+c))
, where
c>0
is an arbitrarily small constant, and

•

the algorithm has a Lipschitz constant
O(\sqrt{\min\{n,m\}\log m}\log n)
.

Our algorithm first solves a (regularized) LP relaxation and then rounds the obtained solution so that the whole process is Lipschitz continuous.
These two steps are explained and analyzed in Sections
5.1
and
5.2
, respectively.
Then, we provide the proof of Theorem
5.1
in Section
5.3
.

5.1
LP Relaxation

We consider the following natural LP relaxation for the set cover problem:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\text{minimize}&\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}w_{S}x_{S},\\
\text{subject to}&\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}:e\in S}x_{S}\geq 1&\forall e\in U,\\
&\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}x_{S}\leq n,\\
&0\leq x_{S}\leq 1&\forall S\in\mathcal{F}.\end{array}

Note that the second constraint is valid because there is always an optimal solution that uses at most
n
elements.
However, the solution to this LP relaxation may not be Lipschitz continuous with respect to the weight vector
w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathcal{F}}
.
Hence, we consider the following optimization problem with additional regularizers and constraints.

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathrm{minimize}&\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}w_{S}x_{S}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}x_{S}^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}\left(\frac{w_{S}x_{S}}{2W}+\frac{1}{2m}\right)\log\left(\frac{w_{S}x_{S}}{2W}+\frac{1}{2m}\right)\\
\text{subject to}&\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}:e\in S}x_{S}\geq 1&\forall e\in U,\\
&\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}x_{S}\leq n,\\
&0\leq x_{S}\leq 1&\forall S\in\mathcal{F},\end{array}

where
\lambda,\kappa,W>0
are parameters.
The first regularizer is simply a squared
\ell_{2}
norm of
x
.
The second regularizer is the negative entropy of the vector
(w_{S}x_{S}/(2W)+1/(2m))_{S\in\mathcal{F}}
.
We will choose
W\approx\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}
, where
\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}
is the optimal value of LP (
5.1
), so that
\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}(w_{S}x_{S}/2W+1/(2m))\leq 1
, and hence the negative entropy term contributes by
\log m
.

Let
RegularizedLP
denote the algorithm that takes an instance
(U,\mathcal{F},w)

Proof of the first part of Lemma

be the optimal solution to LP (

Then, the objective function is bounded as

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}w_{S}x_{S}

Henceforth, we focus on analyzing the Lipschitz constant of

Fix
\kappa,\lambda,W>0

denote the objective function of (

.

Fix a weight vector
w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathcal{F}}
and a set
S\in\mathcal{F}
.
Let
w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathcal{F}}
be a vector obtained from
w

w_{S}
by

.
By a similar argument to the proof of Lemma

w
and

Let

be the optimal solutions to (

) for the instances

We consider the following quantity:

where the inequality holds because

and
x^{\prime}

For notational simplicity, for a vector

H(x)=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}x_{S}\log x_{S}
as the negative entropy function.
Also for two vectors

be their Hadamard product, i.e.,
(x\circ y)_{S}=x_{S}y_{S}

The following gives an upper bound on

:

Lemma 5.3
.

We have

D\leq\delta(x_{S}-x^{\prime}_{S})\left(1+\frac{\kappa}{2W}\right).

In particular,
x_{S}\geq x^{\prime}_{S}
holds because

.

Proof.

We first note that

\displaystyle D=f_{w}(x^{\prime})-f_{w}(x)+f_{w^{\prime}}(x)-f_{w^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=w_{S}x^{\prime}_{S}-w_{S}x_{S}+w^{\prime}_{S}x_{S}-w^{\prime}_{S}x^{\prime}_{S}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\kappa}{2}\left(H\left(\frac{w\circ x^{\prime}}{2W}+\frac{\bm{1}}{2m}\right)-H\left(\frac{w\circ x}{2W}+\frac{\bm{1}}{2m}\right)+H\left(\frac{w^{\prime}\circ x}{2W}+\frac{\bm{1}}{2m}\right)-H\left(\frac{w^{\prime}\circ x^{\prime}}{2W}+\frac{\bm{1}}{2m}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\delta(x_{S}-x^{\prime}_{S})

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\kappa}{2}\left(H\left(\frac{w\circ x^{\prime}}{2W}+\frac{\bm{1}}{2m}\right)-H\left(\frac{w\circ x}{2W}+\frac{\bm{1}}{2m}\right)+H\left(\frac{w^{\prime}\circ x}{2W}+\frac{\bm{1}}{2m}\right)-H\left(\frac{w^{\prime}\circ x^{\prime}}{2W}+\frac{\bm{1}}{2m}\right)\right)

(12)

Now, we evaluate the second term.
Let
h(\hat{w}_{S},\hat{x})=H((w-w_{S}\bm{1}_{S}+\hat{w}_{S}\bm{1}_{S})\circ\hat{x}/(2W)+\bm{1}/(2m))
.
Then, by applying the mean-value theorem twice, we can show that there exist
w_{S}\leq\tilde{w}_{S}\leq w^{\prime}_{S}
and a convex combination
\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{F}}
of
x
and
x^{\prime}
such that

\displaystyle H\left(\frac{w\circ x^{\prime}}{2W}+\frac{\bm{1}}{2m}\right)-H\left(\frac{w\circ x}{2W}+\frac{\bm{1}}{2m}\right)+H\left(\frac{w^{\prime}\circ x}{2W}+\frac{\bm{1}}{2m}\right)-H\left(\frac{w^{\prime}\circ x^{\prime}}{2W}+\frac{\bm{1}}{2m}\right)

\displaystyle=h(w_{S},x^{\prime})-h(w_{S},x)+h(w^{\prime}_{S},x)-h(w^{\prime}_{S},x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(w^{\prime}_{S}-w_{S})\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{w}_{S}}(h(\cdot,x)-h(\cdot,x^{\prime}))(\tilde{w}_{S})

\displaystyle=(w^{\prime}_{S}-w_{S})\cdot(x-x^{\prime})^{\top}\frac{\partial^{2}h}{\partial\hat{w}_{S}\partial\hat{x}}(\tilde{w}_{S},\tilde{x})

\displaystyle=(w^{\prime}_{S}-w_{S})(x_{S}-x^{\prime}_{S})\frac{1}{2W}\left(2-\frac{W}{W+m\tilde{w}_{S}\tilde{x}}+\log\left(\frac{1}{2m}+\frac{\tilde{w}_{S}\tilde{x}_{S}}{2W}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{2W}(x_{S}-x^{\prime}_{S})\left(2-\frac{W}{W+m\tilde{w}_{S}\tilde{x}_{S}}+\log\left(\frac{1}{2m}+\frac{\tilde{w}_{S}\tilde{x}_{S}}{2W}\right)\right).

The third factor is an increasing function in
\tilde{w}_{S}\tilde{x}_{S}
, and hence it can be bounded from above by replacing
\tilde{w}_{S}\tilde{x}_{S}
with
W
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle H\left(\frac{w\circ x^{\prime}}{W}+\frac{\bm{1}}{2m}\right)-H\left(\frac{w\circ x}{W}+\frac{\bm{1}}{2m}\right)+H\left(\frac{w^{\prime}\circ x}{W}+\frac{\bm{1}}{2m}\right)-H\left(\frac{w^{\prime}\circ x^{\prime}}{W}+\frac{\bm{1}}{2m}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta}{2W}(x_{S}-x^{\prime}_{S})\left(2-\frac{1}{1+m}+\log\frac{1+m}{2m}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta(x_{S}-x^{\prime}_{S})}{W}.

Combining this with (
12
), the the claim holds.
∎

Now using this upper bound, we analyze the difference between
x
and
x^{\prime}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

We have

x_{S}-x^{\prime}_{S}\leq\frac{\delta}{\lambda}\left(1+\frac{\kappa}{2W}\right).

Proof.

As
x\mapsto\|x\|_{2}^{2}/2
is
1
-strongly convex with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{2}
and so is the objective function of (
5.1
), we have

\displaystyle f_{w}(x^{\prime})-f_{w}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\langle\nabla f_{w}(x),x^{\prime}-x\rangle+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|x^{\prime}-x\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{\lambda}{2}\|x^{\prime}-x\|_{2}^{2},\text{ and}

\displaystyle f_{w^{\prime}}(x)-f_{w^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq\langle\nabla f_{w^{\prime}}(x),x-x^{\prime}\rangle+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{\lambda}{2}\|x^{\prime}-x\|_{2}^{2}.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle D\geq\lambda\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}

(13)

Combining Lemma
5.3
and (
13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|x^{\prime}-x\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{\delta(x_{S}-x^{\prime}_{S})}{\lambda}\left(1+\frac{\kappa}{2W}\right).

In particular, this implies

x_{S}-x^{\prime}_{S}\leq\frac{\delta}{\lambda}\left(1+\frac{\kappa}{2W}\right).\qed

Combining Lemmas
5.3
and
5.4
, the following holds.

Corollary 5.5
.

We have

D\leq\frac{\delta^{2}}{\lambda}\left(1+\frac{\kappa}{2W}\right)^{2}.

Proof of the second part of Lemma
5.2
.

Note that
x\mapsto H(x)
is
1
-strongly convex with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{1}
, given that
x\geq 0
and
\|x\|\leq 1
.
Then, using the fact that
\max\{w^{\top}x,w^{\top}x^{\prime},(w^{\prime})^{\top}x,(w^{\prime})^{\top}x^{\prime}\}\leq W
, we have the following:

\displaystyle f_{w}(x^{\prime})-f_{w}(x)\geq\langle\nabla f_{w}(x),x^{\prime}-x\rangle+\frac{\kappa}{2}\left\|\left(\frac{w\circ x^{\prime}}{W}+\frac{\bm{1}}{2m}\right)-\left(\frac{w\circ x}{W}+\frac{\bm{1}}{2m}\right)\right\|_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\kappa}{2}\left\|\frac{w\circ x^{\prime}}{W}-\frac{w\circ x}{W}\right\|_{1}^{2}=\frac{\kappa}{2W^{2}}\left\|w\circ(x^{\prime}-x)\right\|_{1}^{2},\text{ and}

\displaystyle f_{w^{\prime}}(x)-f_{w^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})\geq\langle\nabla f_{w^{\prime}}(x),x-x^{\prime}\rangle+\frac{\kappa}{2}\left\|\left(\frac{w^{\prime}\circ x}{W}+\frac{\bm{1}}{2m}\right)-\left(\frac{w^{\prime}\circ x^{\prime}}{W}+\frac{\bm{1}}{2m}\right)\right\|_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\kappa}{2}\left\|\frac{w^{\prime}\circ x^{\prime}}{W}-\frac{w^{\prime}\circ x}{W}\right\|_{1}^{2}=\frac{\kappa}{2W^{2}}\left\|w^{\prime}\circ(x^{\prime}-x)\right\|_{1}^{2}.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle D\geq\frac{\kappa}{2W^{2}}\left(\left\|w\circ(x^{\prime}-x)\right\|_{1}^{2}+\left\|w^{\prime}\circ(x^{\prime}-x)\right\|_{1}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa}{2W^{2}}\left\|w^{\prime}\circ x^{\prime}-w\circ x-\delta x^{\prime}_{S}\bm{1}_{S}\right\|_{1}^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2W^{2}}\left\|w^{\prime}\circ x^{\prime}-w\circ x+\delta x_{S}\bm{1}_{S}\right\|_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\kappa}{2W^{2}}\left(\|w^{\prime}\circ x^{\prime}-w\circ x\|_{1}^{2}-2\|w^{\prime}\circ x^{\prime}-w\circ x\|_{1}\|\delta x^{\prime}_{S}\bm{1}_{S}\|_{1}+\|\delta x^{\prime}_{S}\bm{1}_{S}\|_{1}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\kappa}{2W^{2}}\left(\|w^{\prime}\circ x^{\prime}-w\circ x\|_{1}^{2}-2\|w^{\prime}\circ x^{\prime}-w\circ x\|_{1}\|\delta x_{S}\bm{1}_{S}\|_{1}+\|\delta x_{S}\bm{1}_{S}\|_{1}^{2}\right)

(by the inverse triangle inequality)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\kappa}{W^{2}}\|w^{\prime}\circ x^{\prime}-w\circ x\|_{1}^{2}-\frac{2\delta\kappa}{W^{2}}\|w^{\prime}\circ x^{\prime}-w\circ x\|_{1}.

(14)

Combining Corollary
5.5
and (
14
), we have

\frac{\kappa}{W^{2}}\|w^{\prime}\circ x^{\prime}-w\circ x\|_{1}^{2}-\frac{2\delta\kappa}{W^{2}}\|w^{\prime}\circ x^{\prime}-w\circ x\|_{1}\leq\frac{\delta^{2}}{\lambda}\left(1+\frac{\kappa}{2W}\right)^{2}

Solving this inequality for
\|w^{\prime}\circ x^{\prime}-w\circ x\|_{1}^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|w^{\prime}\circ x^{\prime}-w\circ x\|_{1}\leq\delta+\frac{\delta}{2}\sqrt{\frac{(2W+\kappa)^{2}+4\kappa\lambda}{\kappa\lambda}}=O\left(\delta(\kappa+W)\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{\kappa\lambda}}\right)

Hence, the claim holds.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
(U,\mathcal{F})
be a set cover instance on
n

w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathcal{F}}
be a weight vector.
Let
\bm{\mathcal{S}}=\textsc{RoundingSC}(U,\mathcal{F},x)
, where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{F}}
is a feasible solution to LP (
5.1
).
Then,
\bm{\mathcal{S}}
is a feasible set cover with probability

, and

\mathop{\mathbf{E}}\left[\sum_{S\in\bm{\mathcal{S}}}w_{S}\right]=O\left(\log n\cdot\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}w_{S}x_{S}\right).

Let
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathcal{F}}
be weight vectors and
x,x^{\prime}\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{F}}
be feasible solutions to LP (
5.1
).
For
\bm{\mathcal{S}}=\textsc{RoundingSC}(U,\mathcal{F},x)

\bm{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}=\textsc{RoundingSC}(U,\mathcal{F},x^{\prime})
, we have

\mathrm{EM}_{\mathrm{w}}((\bm{\mathcal{S}},w),(\bm{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime},w^{\prime}))=O\left(\left(\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{1}+\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}|w_{S}x_{S}-w^{\prime}_{S}x^{\prime}_{S}|\right)\log n\right).

Proof.

We first show that
\bm{\mathcal{S}}
is feasible with high probability.
The probability that an element

is not covered is

\displaystyle\prod_{S\in\mathcal{F}:e\in S}(1-x_{S})^{K}\leq\prod_{S\in\mathcal{F}:e\in S}\exp(-Kx_{S})=\exp\left(-K\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}:e\in S}x_{S}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-K\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}:e\in S}x_{S}\right)\leq\exp(-K)\leq\frac{1}{\mathrm{poly}(n)}

by setting the hidden constant in
K
to be large enough.
By a union bound over elements in
U
, the probability that the output is infeasible is at most
1/\mathrm{poly}(n)
.

It is clear that the expected total weight of
\bm{\mathcal{S}}
is as stated.

Now, we consider the Lipschitz continuity.
For
S\in\mathcal{F}
, let
\delta_{S}=w^{\prime}_{S}-w_{S}
.
For
k\in[K]
, let
\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{k}
and
\bm{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{k}
be the family of sets added to
\bm{\mathcal{S}}
and
\bm{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
, respectively, at step

.
We note that

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}|w_{S}x_{S}-w^{\prime}_{S}x^{\prime}_{S}|=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}|w_{S}x_{S}-(w_{S}+\delta_{S})x^{\prime}_{S}|\geq\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}\left(|x_{S}-x^{\prime}_{S}|w_{S}-|\delta_{S}|x^{\prime}_{S}\right).

(15)

Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{EM}_{\mathrm{w}}((\bm{\mathcal{S}},w),(\bm{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime},w^{\prime}))\leq\sum_{k\in[K]}\mathrm{EM}_{\mathrm{w}}((\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{k},w),(\bm{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{k},w^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in[K]}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}\left(\min\{x_{S},x^{\prime}_{S}\}\cdot|w_{S}-w^{\prime}_{S}|+\max\{x_{S}-x^{\prime}_{S},0\}\cdot w_{S}+\max\{x^{\prime}_{S}-x_{S},0\}\cdot w^{\prime}_{S}\right)

\displaystyle=K\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}\Bigl(\min\{x_{S},x^{\prime}_{S}\}|\delta_{S}|+\max\{x_{S}-x^{\prime}_{S},0\}w_{S}+\max\{x^{\prime}_{S}-x_{S},0\}(w_{S}+\delta_{S})\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq K\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}\Bigl(\max\{x_{S},x^{\prime}_{S}\}|\delta_{S}|+|x_{S}-x^{\prime}_{S}|w_{S}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq K\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}\Bigl(\max\{x_{S},x^{\prime}_{S}\}|\delta_{S}|+\bigl(|w_{S}x_{S}-w^{\prime}_{S}x^{\prime}_{S}|+x^{\prime}_{S}|\delta_{S}|\bigr)\Bigr)

(by (
15
))

\displaystyle\leq K\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}\Bigl(2|\delta_{S}|+|w_{S}x_{S}-w^{\prime}_{S}x^{\prime}_{S}|\Bigr)

\displaystyle=O\left(K\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{1}+K\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}|w_{S}x_{S}-w^{\prime}_{S}x^{\prime}_{S}|\right).\qed

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbf{E}}\left[\sum_{S\in\bm{\mathcal{S}}}w_{S}\right]=O(\log n\cdot(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}+c))=O(\log n\cdot(\mathrm{OPT}+c)).

(17)

Next, we analyze the Lipschitz continuity.
Fix a weight vector

and a set

.
Let

\mathcal{L}
between

\displaystyle\Pr_{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\sim\mathcal{L}}[\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}]\leq 1-\Pr[\lambda^{\prime}\in[\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}/\min\{n,m\},2\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}/\min\{n,m\}]]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}^{\prime}+c)/\min\{n,m\}-2(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}+c)/\min\{n,m\}}{2(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}^{\prime}+c)/\min\{n,m\}-(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}^{\prime}+c)/\min\{n,m\}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}+c+\delta)-2(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}+c)}{2(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}^{\prime}+c)-(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}^{\prime}+c)}=O\left(\frac{\delta}{\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}^{\prime}+c}\right).

(20)

For

,

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}w_{S}x^{\prime}_{S}\leq\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}w^{\prime}_{S}x^{\prime}_{S}\leq 3(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}^{\prime}+c)\leq 3(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}+2c)\leq\bm{W}

Hence, the requirement of Lemma

Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{EM}_{\mathrm{w}}((\bm{\mathcal{S}},w),(\bm{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime},w^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathbf{E}}_{((\lambda,\kappa,W),(\lambda^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime},W^{\prime}))\sim\mathcal{D}}\mathrm{EM}_{\mathrm{w}}((\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{\lambda,\kappa,W},w),(\bm{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{\lambda^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime},W^{\prime}},w^{\prime}))

In this section, we give a proof sketch of the fact that, if there exists an

The algorithm

.

d_{v}
robots at each vertex at time 0).
These robots then move through the network at a speed of one unit.
Whenever a robot reaches a vertex of the graph, it splits instantaneously into several copies, leaving the vertex in different directions.
The effect of repeated splitting is to increase the number of robots in the network; however, distinct robots traverse distinct trajectories.
We require that (i) the trajectory of any given robot be a light cycle and (ii) if a robot reaches a point in the trajectory of another robot and the set of nodes in
S
that the former robot had passed through is different from that of the latter robot (when it was at that point);
any robot that cannot meet these conditions immediately destroys itself.
A robot that completes its cycle waits there;
clearly, each robot completes its cycle by time
kg
and passes through a different set of nodes in
S
.
It follows that the quantity to count equals the number of robots that are alive at time
kg
.

Let
g^{-}
denote a quantity that is infinitesimally smaller than
g
.
Let
j
be any nonnegative integer.
We prove the following claim by induction on
j
: there are at most
(2m)^{j+1}
robots alive at time
jg^{-}/2
.
The case
j
= 0 is clear because
\sum_{v\in S}d_{v}\leq 2m
.
We now assume that the claim is true for
j-1
and establish it for
j
.

The idea is to focus on any one robot alive at time
(j-1)g^{-}/2
, and to show that at time
jg^{-}/2
this robot has split into at most
2m
copies.
Consider the trajectories of all the above copies during the interval
[(j-1)g^{-}/2,jg^{-}/2]
.
Once the trajectories of two copies separate and meet again, only one survives, for otherwise, either of the following holds: (i) we have a cycle including a vertex in
S
with a weight less than
g
, or (ii) one of the two copies destroys itself because the sets of nodes in
S
in these trajectories are the same at the meeting point.
We may assume that each robot copy is on some edge of the network at time
jg^{-}/2
.
This is because our robots traverse vertices instantly; if a robot happens to be exactly on a vertex at time
jg^{-}/2
, we retrace its step by an infinitesimal amount.
Now observe that the structure of the trajectories forbids two copies heading the same way on any edge of the network at time
jg^{-}/2
.
It follows that there are at most
2m
copies, thus establishing the correctness of the inductive step and hence the claim.

The claim implies that there are at most
(2m)^{2k}
robots alive at time
(2k-1)g^{-}/2
.
Let us focus on any one of these robots.
We claim that this robot will not split during the interval
[(2k-1)g^{-}/2,kg^{-}]
.
This is because at time
kg^{-}
, the robot must be back at its starting vertex.
Using the same trajectory argument as before, it follows that at most one robot survives during the interval
[(2k-1)g^{-}/2,kg^{-}]
.
Therefore the total number of successful robots alive at time
kg^{-}
is at most
(2m)^{2k}
.
These robots remain at their respective edges until time
kg
.
∎

Search for the decay of the Higgs boson to a pair of light pseudoscalar bosons in the final state with four bottom quarks in proton-proton collisions at s = 13 ​ \TeV \sqrt{s}=13\TeV


Abstract
A search is presented for the decay of the 125 \GeV Higgs boson ( \PH ) to a pair of new light pseudoscalar bosons ( \Pa ), followed by the prompt decay of each a boson to a bottom quark-antiquark pair, \PH → \Pa ​ \Pa → \bbbar ​ \bbbar \PH\to\Pa\Pa\to\bbbar\bbbar . The analysis is performed using a data sample of proton-proton collisions collected with the CMS detector at a center-of-mass energy of 13 \TeV , corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 138 \fbinv . To reduce the background from standard model processes, the search requires the Higgs boson to be produced in association with a leptonically decaying \PW or \PZ boson. The analysis probes the production of new light bosons in a 15 < m \Pa < 60 ​ \GeV 15<m_{\Pa}<60\GeV mass range. Assuming the standard model predictions for the Higgs boson production cross sections for \Pp ​ \Pp → \PW ​ \PH \Pp\Pp\to\PW\PH and \PZ ​ \PH \PZ\PH , model independent upper limits at 95% confidence level are derived for the branching fraction ℬ ⁡ ( \PH → \Pa ​ \Pa → \bbbar ​ \bbbar ) \mathcal{B}(\PH\to\Pa\Pa\to\bbbar\bbbar) . The combined \PW ​ \PH \PW\PH and \PZ ​ \PH \PZ\PH observed upper limit on the branching fraction ranges from 1.10 for m \Pa = 20 ​ \GeV m_{\Pa}=20\GeV to 0.36 for m \Pa = 60 ​ \GeV m_{\Pa}=60\GeV , complementing other measurements in the \PGm ​ \PGm ​ \PGt ​ \PGt \PGm\PGm\PGt\PGt , \PGt ​ \PGt ​ \PGt ​ \PGt \PGt\PGt\PGt\PGt and \Pb ​ \Pb ​ ℓ ​ ℓ \Pb\Pb\ell\ell ( ℓ = \PGm , \PGt \ell=\PGm,\PGt ) channels.

0.1
Introduction

A Higgs boson with a mass of 125
\GeV
was
discovered
[
1
,
2
,
3
]

in 2012 by the ATLAS and CMS Collaborations at the CERN LHC.
Studies
[
4
,
5
]
of this particle,
here denoted
\PH
,
have indicated its compatibility with the Higgs boson of
the standard model (SM) of particle physics.
Notwithstanding this compatibility,
present experimental limits
allow a significant branching fraction of the
\PH
boson to beyond-the-SM
particles up to 16%
[
5
]
.
Observation of such
\PH
boson decays could point the way
for the SM to be extended to a more fundamental theory.

Many models of physics beyond the SM predict an extended Higgs sector.
Examples include two-Higgs-doublet models (2HDM)
[
6
]
,
the 2HDM with a scalar singlet field (2HDM+S)
[
7
]
,
and the next-to-minimal supersymmetric SM (NMSSM)
[
8
]
.
In the 2HDM+S model,
the Higgs sector contains seven physical scalar and pseudoscalar particles.
One of the scalar states can be identified with the 125
\GeV
\PH
boson
while one of the pseudoscalar states, denoted
\Pa
,
can be light enough to allow
\PH\to\Pa\Pa
decays.
These decays can occur with a significant branching fraction (
\gtrsim
10%)
if they are kinematically allowed
[
7
]
.
For virtually all 2HDM+S scenarios,
the dominant pseudoscalar decay mode is
\Pa\to\bbbar

(with
\PQb
a bottom quark)
so long as the pseudoscalar mass
m_{\Pa}
is at least twice the bottom quark mass.

In this paper,
we present a search for the Higgs boson decay
\PH\to\Pa\Pa\to\bbbar\bbbar
.
The decays are assumed to be prompt,
i.e., not leading to long-lived new particles.
The analysis is based on a sample of proton-proton (
\Pp\Pp
) collision events at
a center-of-mass energy of 13
\TeV
recorded with the CMS detector in 2016–2018,
corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 138
\fbinv
.
The search makes use of
\PV=\PW
or
\PZ
boson associated production,
in which the
\PV
boson decays leptonically,

\PW\to\ell\nu
or
\PZ\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
,
with
\ell
an electron or muon.
The leptonic signature suppresses SM background processes
such as those with four
\PQb
jets produced
through quantum chromodynamics (QCD) interactions (multijet background).

The
\PH\to\Pa\Pa\to\bbbar\bbbar

channel was previously explored by the
ATLAS Collaboration
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
.
The present work represents the first search in this channel from the CMS Collaboration.
The ATLAS results
[
10
,
11
]

were based on a data sample of about 36
\fbinv
at
\sqrt{s}=13\TeV
.
Thus, the present study utilizes a data sample that is approximately four times larger.
Beyond
\PH\to\Pa\Pa\to\bbbar\bbbar
,
searches for prompt
\PH\to\Pa\Pa
decays have been performed in many other channels.
At
\sqrt{s}=7
or 8
\TeV
,
these include

\PH\to\Pa\Pa\to\Pe\Pe\Pe\Pe

[
12
]
,

\PGm\PGm\PGm\PGm

[
12
,
13
,
14
]
,

\Pe\Pe\PGm\PGm

[

]
,

\PGm\PGm\PGt\PGt

[
15
]
,

\PGt\PGt\PGt\PGt

[
16
,
15
]
,

\PGg\PGg\PGg\PGg

[
17
]
,
and
\Pb\Pb\PGm\PGm

[
15
]
.
At
\sqrt{s}=13\TeV
,
these include

\PH\to\Pa\Pa\to\Pe\Pe\Pe\Pe

[
18
]
,

\PGm\PGm\PGm\PGm

[
18
,
19
]
,

\Pe\Pe\PGm\PGm

[
18
]
,

\PGm\PGm\PGt\PGt

[
20
,
21
,
22
]
,

\PGg\PGg\PGg\PGg

[
23
,
24
,
25
]
,

\Pb\Pb\PGm\PGm

[

,

\Pb\Pb\PGt\PGt

,
and
\gamma\gamma\,\mathrm{j}\,\mathrm{j}
(where
\mathrm{j}
is a jet)

These searches for the new particle
\Pa
cover a mass range
0.2<m_{\Pa}<62.5\GeV
.
The sensitivity of these searches as a
function of
m_{\Pa}
has recently been summarized in Ref.
[

.
Searches in the
\Pb\Pb\PGt\PGt
,
\PGt\PGt\PGt\PGt

\Pb\Pb\Pb\Pb

1

for

.

Backgrounds to
\PH\to\Pa\Pa\to\bbbar\bbbar
from SM processes arise from several sources.
The dominant background arises from
{\ttbar}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
production in which one or both top quarks decay leptonically.
The next-largest backgrounds are from
{\PZ}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
and
{\PW}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
production
with a
\PV
boson that decays leptonically: the main background process
in this case is from
\PV+\Pb\Pb
production plus one or two mistagged
\Pb
jets.
Smaller backgrounds arise from various electroweak processes.
These include events with diboson
\PV\PV^{(\prime)}
or triboson
\PV\PV\PV^{(\prime)}
production,
events with a single top quark,
and rare electroweak processes such as events with
\ttbar\PV
or
\ttbar\gamma
production.
The SM associated production of the Higgs boson in the
\PV\PH
channel, with
\PH\to\bbbar
,
is also expected to comprise a background component in this search.
Background from multijet production arises when jets or other reconstructed
objects are erroneously identified as leptons
or when genuine leptons are produced through hadron decays.

0.2
Detector and trigger

A detailed description of the CMS detector,
along with a definition of the coordinate system and
pertinent kinematic variables,
is presented in Ref.
[
33
]
.
Briefly,
a cylindrical superconducting solenoid with an inner diameter of 6
\unit
m
provides a 3.8
\unit
T axial magnetic field.
Within the solenoid volume
are a silicon pixel and strip tracker,
a lead tungstate crystal electromagnetic calorimeter (ECAL),
and a brass and scintillator hadron calorimeter (HCAL).
The tracking detectors cover the pseudorapidity range
\abs{\eta}<2.5
.
The ECAL and HCAL,
each with a barrel and two endcap sections,
cover
\abs{\eta}<3.0
.
Forward calorimeters extend the coverage to
\abs{\eta}<5.0
.
Muons are detected within
\abs{\eta}<2.4
by gas-ionization chambers
embedded in the steel flux-return yoke outside the solenoid.
The detector is nearly hermetic,
permitting accurate measurements of missing transverse momentum.

The CMS trigger system is described in Refs.
[
34
,
35
]
.
For this analysis,
signal event candidates are selected using a combination of
triggers requiring either at least one electron or muon,
or,
with lower transverse momentum (
\pt
) thresholds on the leptons,
at least two electrons or muons.
The trigger efficiency for signal events is measured in data and is found
to exceed 95% for events satisfying the event selection criteria
described below.

0.3
Event simulation

With the exception of the multijet background,
which is determined from data,
the background from SM processes is evaluated from simulation
using Monte Carlo (MC) event generators.
The
{\ttbar}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
background is simulated at next-to-leading order (NLO) accuracy
with the
\POWHEG
2.0
[
36
,
37
,
38
]
generator.
A reweighting is applied to the top quark
\pt
to improve the modeling in the
{\ttbar}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
simulation.
The reweighting factors are defined as the ratio of the top quark
\pt
measured in data,
unfolded to the parton level,
with respect to the theory predictions at NNLO,
and are taken from Ref.
[
39
]
.
The
{\PV}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
background is simulated at leading order (LO) accuracy with the

\MGvATNLO
generator
(version 2.2.2 for the simulation of the 2016 data
and version 2.4.2 for the 2017–2018 data)
[
40
]
,
with cross sections normalized to NLO.
For the
{\PV}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
processes,
we apply NLO electroweak corrections as a function of the
\PV
boson
\pt
at the generator level,
as documented in Ref.
[
41
]
.
Diboson (triboson) processes are simulated at LO (NLO) accuracy
with the
\PYTHIA
8.212 (
\POWHEG
2.0) generator.
Single top quark production and the rare electroweak processes
are simulated at NLO with a combination of the
\POWHEG
2.0 and
\MGvATNLO
generators.
Samples generated at NLO with the
\MGvATNLO
generator
employ the FxFx matching and merging scheme
[
42
]
,
while those generated at LO with the same generator
use the MLM matching and merging scheme
[
43
]
.

Signal events,
namely events with associated
\PV\PH
production followed by the decay
\PH\to\Pa\Pa
,
are simulated at LO using the
\MGvATNLO
(version 2.6.5) generator.
The cross sections are normalized to the values presented in
Refs.
[
44
,
45
,
46
,
47
]

and include NNLO QCD and NLO electroweak corrections.
The pseudoscalar bosons are required to decay via
\Pa\to\bbbar
,
while the
\PW
and
\PZ
bosons are required to decay
through the
\Pe
,
\PGm
, or
\PGt
leptonic channels.
The leptonic decays of the
\PGt
lepton are treated as a signal contribution.
We require at least one lepton (
\Pe
or
\PGm
) with
\pt
greater than 10
\GeV
and
\abs{\eta}
less than 2.5:
this results in a filter efficiency of about 61% for the
\PW\PH
channel
and 75% for the
\PZ\PH
channel.
Signal event samples are generated for pseudoscalar mass values

m_{\Pa}=15
, 20, 25, 30, 40, 50, and 60
\GeV
.

The parton distribution function (PDF) set used
in the simulations is NLO NNPDF3.1 for the NLO samples
and LO NNPDF3.1 for the LO samples
[
48
]
.
Parton showering and hadronization are described for all MC samples
using the
\PYTHIA
8 generator.
For the simulation of the 2016 data,
the CUETP8M1 tune
[
49
,
50
]
is used.
For the 2017–2018 data, we use the CP5 tune
[
51
]
.
The detector response is evaluated using
\GEANTfour

[
52
]
.
The contribution of additional
\Pp\Pp
interactions
within the same or nearby bunch crossing (pileup)
is modeled by adding simulated minimum bias events to the MC samples,
with the distribution of additional interactions per event adjusted to agree with observation.

0.4
Event reconstruction

The particle-flow (PF) algorithm
[
53
]

is used to reconstruct and identify individual photons, electrons, muons, charged hadrons,
and neutral hadrons (PF candidates) by combining information from individual subdetectors in an optimal fashion.
To improve the quality of the reconstruction,
requirements beyond the PF criteria are imposed on electron and muon candidates.
For electron candidates,
these requirements are based on the shower shape of the associated energy cluster in the ECAL,
the angular matching of the electron track candidate with the cluster,
and the consistency of the track with originating from the
primary
\Pp\Pp
interaction vertex (PV)
[
54
]
.
Electrons compatible with photon conversion in the detector material are removed.
For muon candidates,
these requirements are based on the number of energy deposits
recorded for the candidate in the silicon tracker and muon system,
the fit quality of the candidate,
and the consistency of the candidate with
originating from the PV
[
55
]
.
Electron candidates are restricted to
\abs{\eta}<2.5

and muon candidates to
\abs{\eta}<2.4
.

The PV is taken to be the vertex corresponding
to the hardest scattering in the event,
evaluated using tracking information alone as described in Section 9.4.1 of Ref.
[
56
]
.
The PV is required to lie within 24
\cm
of the center of the detector
in the direction along the beam axis and within 2
\cm
in the plane
transverse to that axis.

Leptons from
\PV
boson decays are generally isolated from the jets in an event,
unlike leptons from sources such as hadron decays.
We therefore require electron and muon candidates to be isolated.
An isolation variable
I_{\mathrm{iso}}
is defined as the sum of the scalar
\pt
of charged hadron,
neutral hadron, and photon PF candidates within a cone of radius

\Delta R=\sqrt{\smash[b]{(\Delta\eta)^{2}+(\Delta\phi)^{2}}}=0.3

around the electron or muon candidate direction,
where
\phi
is the azimuthal angle.
A correction is applied to account for the contribution of pileup.
For electrons,
this correction is performed using the jet area method
described in Ref.
[
57
]
.
For muons,
the correction is performed by subtracting half the sum of scalar
\pt
of charged-particle tracks within the cone,
but using only those tracks that do not originate from the PV.
We require the ratio of
I_{\mathrm{iso}}
to the
\pt
value of the muon
to be less than 0.15.
For electrons, the
I_{\mathrm{iso}}
selection requirements are optimized via a multivariate analysis,
and are chosen to be 0.059 (
0.029+0.506\pt
)
and 0.057 (
0.044+0.963\pt
) in the ECAL barrel and endcap, respectively,
for the 2016 (2017–2018) data-taking period.

The combined reconstruction, identification, and isolation efficiency
for electrons and muons is determined in data using
\PZ
boson decays
with a tag-and-probe method
[
58
]
.
The efficiencies range from 70 to 90% for electrons
and from 90 to 97% for muons,
depending on the lepton
\pt
and
\eta
.

Jets are reconstructed by clustering PF candidates using
the anti-
\kt
jet algorithm
[
59
,
60
]

with a distance parameter of 0.4.
Charged-particle tracks not associated with the PV
are excluded from the clustering.
The jet energies are corrected as explained in Ref.
[
61
]

to account for the nonlinear response of the detector and
for the expected contributions of neutral particles from pileup.
The jets are required to satisfy
\pt>20\GeV
and
\abs{\eta}<2.5
.
The number of selected jets in an event is denoted
N_{\text{j}}
.

Bottom quark jets are identified by applying
the
DeepCSV
algorithm
[
62
]
to the selected jets
(the
\pt
threshold is maintained at 20
\GeV
).
The medium working point of this algorithm is used,
for which the tagging efficiency is 68% for
\PQb
quark jets with
\pt\approx 30\GeV
.
The corresponding misidentification probability for light jets
(
\ie
, gluon and light quark jets) is 1.6%,
while that for charm quark jets is 13%.
The number of
\PQb\text{-tagged}
jets in an event is denoted
N_{\PQb}
.

The missing transverse momentum vector
\ptvecmiss
is defined as the projection
onto the plane perpendicular to the beam axis of the negative vector sum of the
momenta of all reconstructed PF candidates in an event.
Its magnitude is referred to as
\ptmiss
.
Calibration and pileup effects are accounted for
as described in Ref.
[
63
]
.
The
\ptmiss
variable is used to measure the undetected
momentum in an event,
such as is carried by neutrinos in leptonic
\PW
boson decays
or as can arise spuriously from the misreconstruction of jet
\pt
.

For the
\PW\PH
channel
we also consider the transverse mass
\mT
, defined by

\mT=\sqrt{2\ptmiss p_{\text{T},\ell}(1-\cos\phi_{\ptmiss,\ell})},

(1)

where
p_{\text{T},\ell}
is the transverse momentum of the lepton
and
\phi_{\ptmiss,\ell}
is the azimuthal angle between
\ptvecmiss
and the
p_{\text{T},\ell}
vector.
For
\PW\PH
events,

\mT
represents the
\PW
boson mass calculated
using transverse momentum only
[
64
]
.

0.5
Analysis procedure

0.5.1
Baseline selection

A set of baseline selection criteria are applied in order to identify events
that are consistent with the signal processes considered.

Single-lepton triggers are used to identify events in the
\PW\PH
channel.
The
\pt
threshold for electrons was 27
\GeV
in 2016 and 32
\GeV
in 2017–2018.
The corresponding thresholds for muons were 24 and 27
\GeV
, respectively.
Dilepton triggers are used to identify events in the
\PZ\PH
channel.
For electrons, the
\pt
thresholds on the two leptons were 23 and 12
\GeV
,
while for muons they were 17 and 8
\GeV
,
for all data-taking periods.
For muons,
the triggers in 2017–2018 differ from those in 2016
by the additional requirement that the dimuon invariant mass be larger than 3.8
\GeV
.
The triggers require the leptons to satisfy stringent identification criteria
and to be isolated from other tracks
and energy deposits in the calorimeters.

For the
\PW\PH
channel,
events must contain exactly one electron or muon, and
no other electron (muon) with
\pt
above 15 (10)
\GeV
.
For the 2016 (2017–2018) data,
electrons must have
\pt>30
(35)
\GeV
and muons
\pt>25
(30)
\GeV
.
Higher thresholds are used for the 2017–2018 data to account for the
increase in the online trigger thresholds.
To suppress the multijet background,

\PW\PH
events must satisfy
\ptmiss>25\GeV
and
\mT>50\GeV
.

For the
\PZ\PH
channel,
events must contain exactly one
\EE
or
\MM
pair
and no other electron or muon with
\pt
above 15 (10)
\GeV
.
For electron pairs,
one of the two legs must have
\pt>25\GeV
and the other
\pt>15\GeV
.
For muon pairs,
the corresponding requirements are
\pt>20\GeV
and
\pt>10\GeV
.
To reduce the background from
{\ttbar}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
events,
the invariant mass of the lepton pair must lie within
20
\GeV
of the nominal
\PZ
boson mass.
No requirements are imposed on
\ptmiss
or
\mT
.

For both
\PW\PH
and
\PZ\PH
events, we require
N_{\text{j}}\geq 3
and
N_{\PQb}\geq 2
.

0.5.2
Analysis overview

The selected events are categorized in terms of
N_{\PQb}
and
N_{\text{j}}
.
Events in the signal region (SR) are divided into two categories depending on whether
they have
N_{\PQb}=3
(denoted the 3b category) or
N_{\PQb}=4
(denoted the 4b category).
The 4b category corresponds to events in which all four
\Pb
jets in

\PH\to\Pa\Pa\to\bbbar\bbbar
decays
have been reconstructed.
The 3b category accounts for events in which two
\Pb
quarks are merged in a single
jet or one of the
\Pb
quarks is rather soft and fails to be reconstructed.
The latter case is expected to have a small impact on the Higgs boson kinematics.

For
\PW\PH
events, the signal region is defined by
(
N_{\PQb}
,
N_{\text{j}})=(3
b, 3–4j) and (4b, 4j),
where 3–4j refers to events with either exactly three or exactly four jets.
For
\PZ\PH
events,
the SR is defined by (3b,
\geq
3j) and (4b,
\geq
4j).
These regions are summarized in Table
0.5.2
.
The
\PH
boson is formed from the four
\PQb\text{-tagged}
jets
in the 4b category and from the three
\PQb\text{-tagged}
jets in the 3b category.
For
\PW\PH
events,
the
\PW
boson is defined by the electron or muon
and the
\ptvecmiss
vector.
For
\PZ\PH
events,
the
\PZ
boson is defined by the
\EE
or
\MM
pair.

the minimum difference between two dijet masses formed
from all possible combinations with the four
\PQb\text{-tagged}
jets.
The four
\PQb\text{-tagged}
jets can be grouped in three different ways to form a pair of
\Pa\to\bbbar
candidates.
The
\Delta m_{\bbbar}^{\text{min}}
variable represents the grouping that results in the smallest difference
between the masses of the two pseudoscalar candidates.

The BDTs are trained on signal events, combining all pseudoscalar boson mass points,
and on background events, combining all considered SM processes.
The shapes of the BDT distributions
are not strongly dependent on the assumed value of
m_{\Pa}
,
allowing the same BDT to be used for all signal mass values.

We perform a maximum likelihood fit of the signal and background
components to the observed distribution of the BDT discriminant.
The fit is performed simultaneously across the SRs and control regions (CRs).
The CRs are described in Section
0.5.3
.
The templates for signal events are derived from simulation.
The templates for background events are determined as
described in Section
0.5.3
.

0.5.3
Background modeling and control regions

A major source of background in our study is from
{\ttbar}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
production,
as mentioned in the introduction.
To evaluate this background,
simulated
{\ttbar}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
events are categorized based on
the parton-level flavor content of additional particle-level jets not originating
from the decay of the
{\ttbar}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
system.
Events that contain at least one additional particle-level jet matched
to a
\PQb
hadron are labeled as
\ttbar\!+\!\bbbar
.
Events that contain at least one additional particle jet matched to a
\PQc
hadron
and no
\PQb
hadron are labeled as
\ttbar\!+\!\ccbar
.
The
\ttbar\!+\!\ccbar
and
\ttbar\!+\!\bbbar
categories, with heavy-flavor (HF) production,
are referred to as the
“
{\ttbar}\!+\!{\text{HF}}
”
background.
This background mostly arises from events with gluon splitting,
\Pg\to\ccbar
or
\bbbar
.
The remaining
{\ttbar}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
events are referred to as the
“
{\ttbar}\!+\!{\text{LF}}
”
background,
where “LF” refers to light flavor.
The
{\ttbar}\!+\!{\text{LF}}
category also includes events
with no additional particle-level jets. One source of this background is

\ttbar\!+\!\qqbar
(
\PQq=\PQu
,
\PQd
,
\PQs
) events in which light-flavored jets are
erroneously identified as
\PQb
jets.

The template for the
{\ttbar}\!+\!{\text{LF}}
background is taken from simulation,
both for its shape and normalization. In contrast to
{\ttbar}\!+\!{\text{LF}}
events,
the rate of
{\ttbar}\!+\!{\text{HF}}
events is not well predicted by simulation because
it is dominated by the gluon splitting process,
which is not yet well understood to adequate precision
[
66
]
.
Therefore, while the shapes of the templates and the relative rate of
\ttbar\!+\!\bbbar
to
\ttbar\!+\!\ccbar
production are taken from simulation, the overall normalization is
determined from the fit to data.

The
{\PW}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
and
{\PZ}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
backgrounds mostly arise
when a
\PW
or
\PZ
boson is produced in association with
\PQb
quark jets.
Like
{\ttbar}\!+\!{\text{HF}}
production,
the rates of these processes are difficult to model
[
67
,
68
]
.
Thus,
for the
{\PW}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
background to the
\PW\PH
channel
and the
{\PZ}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
background to the
\PZ\PH
channel,
the shapes of the templates are taken from simulation while the normalizations
are determined in the fit.
There is a small
{\PZ}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
background to the
\PW\PH
channel.
The template for this background is taken from simulation
for both the shape and normalization.
The
{\PW}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
background to the
\PZ\PH
channel is negligible and is therefore ignored.

The CRs used to constrain the normalizations of the
{\ttbar}\!+\!{\text{HF}}
,
{\PW}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
, and
{\PZ}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
processes
are obtained by selecting events with one less
\PQb\text{-tagged}
jet with respect to the SRs.
This requirement enables a collection of events predominantly
governed by these background processes,
while simultaneously depleting the signal content but closely aligning with the SRs.
The (
N_{\PQb}
,
N_{\text{j}}
) criteria for the CRs are as listed in Table
0.5.2
.
In the (2b, 3j) and (2b, 4j) categories,
any variable that depends on
N_{\PQb}
is constructed by adding “fake”
\Pb
tags
using the untagged jet(s) with highest
DeepCSV
discriminator value(s).
For the
\PW\PH
channel,
we define a
\PW
/
{\ttbar}\!+\!{\text{LF}}
CR in the
(2b, 3j)
category and a
{\ttbar}\!+\!{\text{LF}}
CR in the (2b, 4j) category.
For the
\PZ\PH
channel,
we define a
{\PZ}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
CR in the (2b, 3j) and (2b, 4j) categories.
The modeling of the
{\ttbar}\!+\!{\text{HF}}
background is validated in signal-depleted regions of the SR bins
corresponding to BDT scores
\lesssim
0.1.
The modeling of the various backgrounds has been studied through comparison with observations in the CRs.
The degree of agreement for shape variations
between CR data and pre-fit estimates is validated to within
\approx
10%.

For the small backgrounds from diboson, triboson,
single-top quark, rare electroweak processes,
and the SM
\PV\PH
process with
\PH\to\bbbar
,
both the shape and normalization are taken from simulation.
Because of the presence of two isolated leptons,
the multijet background to the
\PZ\PH
channel is negligible and is ignored.
In the
\PW\PH
channel,
for which only one isolated lepton is required,
the multijet background is expected to be small.
We estimate this background contribution from data,
as described below.

To evaluate the multijet background to the
\PW\PH
channel,
we select data events by inverting the lepton isolation requirements
of Section
0.4
.
All other selection criteria are the same as for signal events.
We call this sample the inverted-isolation sample.
The multijet background template is given by the measured BDT distribution
of events in the inverted-isolation sample, normalized by a scale factor
\mathcal{F}
calculated in a low-
\ptmiss
data sideband.
The low-
\ptmiss
sideband is
defined by
\ptmiss<25\GeV
and
\mT<50\GeV
.
We choose a sideband in
\ptmiss
and
\mT
because,
for multijet background events,
these variables are largely uncorrelated with the lepton isolation variable.

We thus consider the following four quantities:

•

D_{\mathrm{A}}
: The BDT distribution of multijet background events in the SR;

•

D_{\mathrm{B}}
: The BDT distribution of multijet background events in the inverted-isolation sample;

•

N_{\mathrm{C}}
: The number of multijet background events in the low-
\ptmiss
sideband that
satisfy the lepton isolation requirements;

•

N_{\mathrm{D}}
: The number of multijet background events in the low-
\ptmiss
sideband that
fail the lepton isolation requirements.

The multijet background estimate is given by

D_{\mathrm{A}}=\mathcal{F}\,D_{\mathrm{B}}
with
\mathcal{F}\equiv N_{\mathrm{C}}/N_{\mathrm{D}}
.
The contributions of nonmultijet background events to

D_{\mathrm{B}}
,
N_{\mathrm{C}}
, and
N_{\mathrm{D}}
are subtracted using simulation before calculating
D_{\mathrm{A}}
.
Because the multijet background is expected to be small compared to other backgrounds,
and because the statistical precision in this background estimate is limited,
the estimate is assigned a conservative uncertainty of 50%.
The closure or self-consistency of the estimate is tested in the 3b and 4b CRs,
where the contribution of multijet events is small.
The predicted multijet background in these CRs is found to be in reasonable agreement
with the data,
to within the assigned uncertainty,
once the nonmultijet contributions in data have been subtracted.

The same procedure is used to account for the multijet background contributions
to the
{\ttbar}\!+\!{\text{HF}}
and
{\PW}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
CRs for the
\PW\PH
channel.

0.5.4
Systematic uncertainties

The overall uncertainty in the determination of the integrated luminosity
over the 2016–2018 data-taking period
is 1.6%
[
69
,
70
,
71
]
.
Systematic uncertainties related to the event simulation include those
associated with the PDFs,
the value of the strong coupling strength
\alpS
,
and the renormalization and factorization scales.
We evaluate these uncertainties by altering the PDFs and scales of the simulated background
samples according to the PDF4LHC prescription of Ref.
[
72
]
.
Their effect on the yields is estimated to be 4–5%.
The differences between the NNPDF3.1 and PDF4LHC uncertainties are found to be small
compared to the statistical uncertainties in the background templates.
The uncertainties in the signal cross section arising from the perturbative QCD calculations,
PDF, and the
\alpS
scales have a negligible effect on the shape of the signal.
Their effects on the yield correspond to 0.7 and 1.9%, respectively, for
\PW\PH
production,
and to 3.8 and 1.6%,
respectively, for
\PZ\PH
production
[
44
]
,
and are taken into account by introducing nuisance parameters with
log-normal distributions into the fit.
We assign the following theoretical uncertainties on
the cross sections for those simulated backgrounds
whose normalization is not determined in the fit:

{\ttbar}\!+\!{\text{LF}}
events (6%)
[
73
]
, single top quark events (5%)
[
74
,
75
]
,
diboson and triboson events (50%)
[
76
,
77
]
,

{\PZ}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
events in the
\PW\PH
channel (2%)
[
78
]
,
and rare electroweak processes (15%).

Tables
0.5.4
and
0.5.4

summarize the other systematic uncertainties that are considered in the analysis.
For those, the shape variations are taken into account in the template fit, while their effect
on the background and signal yield has been evaluated for illustration purposes.
For signal events, the estimate of these uncertainties are shown for two choices
of the pseudoscalar mass:
m_{\Pa}=20
and 60
\GeV
.

The uncertainties related to the trigger efficiency
and the lepton identification and isolation requirements are evaluated using data
(this latter uncertainty, at 2%,
is the same for electrons and muons).
The uncertainties associated with the lepton energy scale, the jet energy scale,
and the jet energy resolution are evaluated as a function of lepton or jet
\pt
and
\eta
.
The uncertainty associated with the lepton energy scale is found to be negligible.
In Tables
0.5.4
and
0.5.4
,
the uncertainty quoted for the jet energy scale is the
maximum that is found for the various
\pt
and
\eta
bins.
A related uncertainty,
relevant for
\PW\PH
channel,
is in the energy scale of unclustered
\ptmiss
,
where unclustered
\ptmiss
refers to signals in the detector
that are not associated to reconstructed physics objects, namely muons, electrons,
photons, hadronically decaying taus and jets
[
63
]
.
The uncertainties in the efficiencies for
\PQb
tagging
and for the mistagging of
\PQc
quark and light-flavored jets
are evaluated as described in Ref.
[
62
]
.
Sources of systematic uncertainty that affect the data-to-simulation corrections
of the
\Pb
-tagging efficiency and misidentification rate
are the jet energy scale,
the cross contamination between light flavor or gluon jets with heavy flavor jets,
and the statistical fluctuations in the data and MC.
These uncertainties are split into 15
\pt
and
\eta
bins
and consist of 9 independent sources.
Except for their statistical component,
they are treated as correlated between the three data-taking periods.
To evaluate the uncertainty associated with pileup,
the total inelastic
\Pp\Pp
cross section is varied by
\pm
5%
[
79
]
.
The uncertainty from the mismodeling of the top quark
\pt
is assessed
for the
{\ttbar}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
MC by comparing results with and without reweighting.
This uncertainty is negligible compared to the other uncertainties in the
\PZ\PH
channel.

0.5.5
Maximum likelihood fit

A maximum likelihood fit of the signal and background terms to the observed
distribution of the BDT discriminant is performed
simultaneously across the SRs and CRs.
The fits are performed separately for the
\PW\PH
and
\PZ\PH
channels
and also with the
\PW\PH
and
\PZ\PH
channels combined.

For the
\PW\PH
channel,
there are two SRs and two CRs,
as indicated in the upper portion of Table
0.5.2
,
for a total of four independent data regions.
In addition,
the electron and muon samples are treated as separate event categories in the fit.
We also treat each of the three years of data collection separately.
There are therefore 24 data regions that are fitted simultaneously.
The fitted parameters are the signal strength modifier,
a global scale factor for
{\ttbar}\!+\!{\text{HF}}
production,
a global scale factor for
{\PW}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
production,
and various nuisance parameters,
where the nuisance parameters represent the systematic uncertainties from Section
0.5.4
.
The signal strength modifier
\mu
is defined by
the equation:
\mathrm{s}=\mu\mathrm{s_{0}}
, where
\mathrm{s}
is the observed signal yield
and
\mathrm{s_{0}}
is the expected signal yield for a SM
\PV\PH
production cross section
times a “benchmark” branching fraction
\mathcal{B}(\PH\to\Pa\Pa\to\bbbar\bbbar)=1
.

For the
\PZ\PH
channel,
there are also two SRs and two CRs,
as indicated in the lower portion of Table
0.5.2
,
leading to 24 independent data regions in the fit as for the
\PW\PH
channel.
The fitted parameters are the signal strength modifier,
a global scale factor for
{\ttbar}\!+\!{\text{HF}}
production,
a global scale factor for
{\PZ}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
production with three
\PQb\text{-tagged}
jets,
a global scale factor for
{\PZ}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
production with four
\PQb\text{-tagged}
jets,
and several nuisance parameters.
We implement separate scale factors for
{\PZ}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
production
with three and four
\PQb\text{-tagged}
jets
because the relative rates of
\PZ
boson production in association
with two or more
\PQb
quarks
are not yet well established
[
67
,
68
]
.
For
{\PW}\!+\!{\text{jets}}
events,
a common scale factor is used
for the three and four
\PQb\text{-tagged}
categories
because the large statistical uncertainties do not allow discrimination
between the two categories.

Systematic uncertainties in the signal and background normalizations
are treated as log-normal distributions. The SR and CR distributions
in data and the background estimates are
used in the maximum likelihood fit to search for
\PH\to\Pa\Pa\to\bbbar\bbbar
decays.

Model independent upper limits are placed, at 95% confidence level (
\CL

on the branching fraction
\mathcal{B}(\PH\to\Pa\Pa\to\bbbar\bbbar)
as a function of
m_{\Pa}
.
The results for the signal are normalized to the NNLO SM cross sections of the Higgs boson production

\sigma(\Pp\Pp\to\PW\PH)=1.37\unit{pb}
and
\sigma(\Pp\Pp\to\PZ\PH)=0.86\unit{pb}
.
The results are dominated by the statistical uncertainties.
The dominant systematic uncertainties are associated with the
\Pb

For
m_{\Pa}=60
\GeV

the
\Pb
-tagging uncertainties on the yield vary between 6 and 9% for the major background processes.
For the signal process, this uncertainty is around 8%.
Other uncertainties lie below 5%.

Summary

A search for exotic decays of the 125
\GeV
Higgs boson (
\PH
)
to a pair of new light pseudoscalar bosons (
\Pa
),
followed by decay to four
\Pb
quark jets,
\PH\to\Pa\Pa\to\bbbar\bbbar
,
is presented, using data recorded with the CMS detector.
The analysis is based on an integrated luminosity of 138
\fbinv
collected at a center-of-mass energy of 13
\TeV
in 2016–2018.
The search is performed in the context of the associated
\PW\PH
and
\PZ\PH
production
in which the
\PW
or
\PZ
boson decays leptonically,

\PW\to\ell\nu
or
\PZ\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
,
with
\ell
an electron or muon.
No evidence for the targeted decay mode is observed.
The analysis obtains model independent upper limits at 95% confidence level
on the branching fraction
\mathcal{B}(\PH\to\Pa\Pa\to\bbbar\bbbar)
of a SM-like Higgs boson.
The combined result for the associated
\PW\PH
and
\PZ\PH
Higgs boson production excludes
branching fractions as low as 0.36 in the mass range
m_{\Pa}
between about 25
\GeV
and 60
\GeV
, assuming the SM

\PW\PH
and
\PZ\PH
cross-sections.
These results provide enhanced sensitivity,
in the mass range
m_{\Pa}\gtrsim 20
\GeV
, in complementary regions of the 2HDM+S model parameter space
compared to CMS searches in the

\PGm\PGm\PGt\PGt
and
\Pb\Pb\ell\ell
(
\ell=\PGm,\PGt
) final states.

Nambu-Jona-Lasinio model correlation functions from QCD
Marco Frasca, Anish Ghoshal, Stefan Groote

Abstract
We treat quantum chromodynamics (QCD) using a set of Dyson-Schwinger equations derived, in differential form, with the Bender-Milton-Savage technique. In this way, we are able to derive the low energy limit that assumes the form of a non-local Nambu-Jona-Lasinio model with all the parameters properly fixed by the QCD Lagrangian and the determination of the mass gap of the gluon sector.

Introduction

Adding quarks to the Yang-Mills Lagrangian makes the theory not exactly treatable.
Notwithstanding such a difficulty, full QCD can be handled with Dyson-Schwinger equations even if some approximations are needed to get the low-energy limit.
Such approximations entail both the strong coupling limit and ’t Hooft limit
N\rightarrow\infty,\ \ Ng^{2}=constant\gg 1
.
In this way, we will recover, in the low-energy limit, the equations for the correlation functions of a non-local NJL model.

Yang-Mills set of Dyson-Schwinger equations can be solved through a class of exact solutions of the 1-point function, similarly to the
\phi^{4}

Frasca:2015yva

Frasca:2017slg
. Adding quarks to the theory makes it unsolvable but, also in this case, our approach could be proven meaningful to derive the proper low energy limit of QCD.

Our idea is to provide a method to derive the full hierarchy of Dyson-Schwinger equations also for QCD, retaining their full differential form. This is possible provided we use a technique devised by Bender, Milton and Savage

2
Bender-Milton-Savage technique

This technique can be better explained referring to a scalar field. We consider the following partition function

Z[j]=\int[D\phi]e^{iS(\phi)+i\int d^{4}xj(x)\phi(x)}.

(1)

To derive 1P-function, one has

\left\langle\frac{\delta S}{\delta\phi(x)}\right\rangle=j(x),

(2)

assuming

\left\langle\ldots\right\rangle=\frac{\int[D\phi]\ldots e^{iS(\phi)+i\int d^{4}xj(x)\phi(x)}}{\int[D\phi]e^{iS(\phi)+i\int d^{4}xj(x)\phi(x)}}

(3)

By setting
j=0
one obtains the equation for th 1P-function. Next, we derive this equation again with respect to
j
to get the equation for the 2P-function. We are taking for the nP-functions the following definition

\langle\phi(x_{1})\phi(x_{2})\ldots\phi(x_{n})\rangle=\frac{\delta^{n}\ln(Z[j])}{\delta j(x_{1})\delta j(x_{2})\ldots\delta j(x_{n})}.

(4)

This implies

\frac{\delta G_{k}(\ldots)}{\delta j(x)}=G_{k+1}(\ldots,x).

(5)

Such a procedure can be iterated to whatever order giving, in principle, all the hierarchy of the Dyson-Schwinger equations in PDE form. Going to higher orders could imply complicated computations but this approach shows itself to be very useful when some known solutions are given for 1P- and 2P-functions as in our case.

3
1P and 2P functions for QCD

We work in the Landau gauge as can make some computations simpler as seen in
Frasca:2015yva
.

The Bender-Milton-Savage method yields for the 1P-functions

\displaystyle\partial^{2}G_{1\nu}^{a}(x)+gf^{abc}(\partial^{\mu}G_{2\mu\nu}^{bc}(0)+

\displaystyle\partial^{\mu}G_{1\mu}^{b}(x)G_{1\nu}^{c}(x)-\partial_{\nu}G_{2\mu}^{\nu bc}(0)

\displaystyle-\partial_{\nu}G_{1\mu}^{b}(x)G_{1}^{\mu c}(x))

\displaystyle+gf^{abc}\partial^{\mu}G_{2\mu\nu}^{bc}(0)+gf^{abc}\partial^{\mu}(G_{1\mu}^{b}(x)G_{1\nu}^{c}(x))

\displaystyle+g^{2}f^{abc}f^{cde}(G_{3\mu\nu}^{\mu bde}(0,0)+G_{2\mu\nu}^{bd}(0)G_{1}^{\mu e}(x)

\displaystyle+G_{2\nu\rho}^{eb}(0)G_{1}^{\rho d}(x)+G_{2\mu\nu}^{de}(0)G_{1}^{\mu b}(x)+

\displaystyle G_{1}^{\mu b}(x)G_{1\mu}^{d}(x)G_{1\nu}^{e}(x))

\displaystyle=g\sum_{q,i}\gamma_{\nu}T^{a}S_{q}^{ii}(0)+g\sum_{q,i}{\bar{q}}_{1}^{i}(x)\gamma_{\nu}T^{a}q_{1}^{i}(x),

(6)

and for the quarks

(i\not{\partial}-m_{q})q_{1}^{i}(x)+g{\bm{T}}\cdot\not{\bm{G}}_{1}(x)q_{1}^{i}(x)+g{\bm{T}}\cdot\not{\bm{W}}^{i}_{q}(x,x)=0.

(7)

Here and in the following Greek indexes (
\mu,\nu,\ldots
) are for the space-time and Latin index (
a,b,\ldots
) for the gauge group. A usual for the Dyson-Schwinger set, the equations for the lower order nP-functions depend on the higher order correlation functions. We will see how to treat this aspect in the following.

At this stage, we apply the re-mapping idea to such equations as done in
Frasca:2015yva
. So, we assume

G_{1\nu}^{a}(x)\rightarrow\eta_{\nu}^{a}\phi(x)

(8)

being
\phi(x)
a scalar field. Let us introduce the
\eta
-symbols as follows

\displaystyle\eta_{\mu}^{a}\eta^{a\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N^{2}-1.

\displaystyle\eta_{\mu}^{a}\eta^{b\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{ab},

\displaystyle\eta_{\mu}^{a}\eta_{\nu}^{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(g_{\mu\nu}-\delta_{\mu\nu}\right)/2.

(9)

All this permits to get the reduced equations

\displaystyle\partial^{2}\phi(x)+2Ng^{2}\Delta(0)\phi(x)+Ng^{2}\phi^{3}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{2}-1}\left[g\sum_{q,i}\eta^{a\nu}\gamma_{\nu}T^{a}S_{q}^{ii}(0)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+g\sum_{q,i}{\bar{q}}_{1}^{i}(x)\eta^{a\nu}\gamma_{\nu}T^{a}q_{1}^{i}(x)\right]

\displaystyle(i\not{\partial}-m_{q}^{i})q_{1}^{i}(x)+g{\bm{T}}\cdot\not{\eta}\phi(x)q_{1}^{i}(x)=0.

(10)

We do the same for the 2P-functions. In the Landau gauge, the gluon 2P-function takes the form

G_{2\mu\nu}^{ab}(x-y)=\left(\eta_{\mu\nu}-\frac{\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}}{\partial^{2}}\right)\Delta_{\phi}(x-y)

(11)

being
\eta_{\mu\nu}
the Minkowski metric, and

\phi

\displaystyle\partial^{2}\Delta_{\phi}(x-y)+2Ng^{2}\Delta_{\phi}(0)\Delta_{\phi}(x-y)+3Ng^{2}\phi^{2}(x)\Delta_{\phi}(x-y)

\displaystyle=g\sum_{q,i}{\bar{Q}}^{ia}_{\nu}(x-y)\gamma^{\nu}T^{a}q_{1}^{i}(x)

\displaystyle+g\sum_{q,i}{\bar{q}}_{1}^{i}(x)\gamma^{\nu}T^{a}Q^{ia}_{\nu}(x-y)+\delta^{4}(x-y)

\displaystyle\partial^{2}P^{ad}_{2}(x-y)=\delta_{ad}\delta^{4}(x-y)

\displaystyle(i\not{\partial}-m_{q}^{i})S^{ij}_{q}(x-y)

\displaystyle+g{\bm{T}}\cdot\not{\eta}\phi(x)S^{ij}_{q}(x-y)=\delta_{ij}\delta^{4}(x-y)

\displaystyle\partial^{2}W_{q\nu}^{ai}(x-y)+2Ng^{2}\Delta_{\phi}(0)W_{q\nu}^{ai}(x-y)+3Ng^{2}\phi^{2}(x)W_{q\nu}^{ai}

\displaystyle=g\sum_{j}{\bar{q}}_{1}^{j}(x)\gamma_{\nu}T^{a}S^{ji}_{q}(x-y)

\displaystyle+gT^{a}\gamma_{\mu}\Delta_{\phi}(x-y)q_{1}^{i}(x)=0.

(12)

4
’t Hooft limit

’t Hooft limit means to solve the theory assuming
tHooft:1973alw
,
tHooft:1974pnl

N\rightarrow\infty,\qquad Ng^{2}=constant,\qquad Ng^{2}\gg 1.

(13)

In our case, the gauge group is SU(N) being
N
is the number of colors. To evaluate our equations in such a limit, we need a perturbation series for a very large coupling. We proposed such a technique in Ref.
Frasca:2013tma
. We do a rescaling,
x\rightarrow\sqrt{Ng^{2}}x
, and write the equation for the gluon field as follows

\displaystyle\partial^{2}\phi(x^{\prime})+2\Delta_{\phi}(0)\phi(x^{\prime})+3\phi^{3}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

(14)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{Ng^{2}}\sqrt{N}(N^{2}-1)}\left[\sum_{q,i}\eta\cdot\gamma\cdot TS_{q}^{ii}(0)+\right.

\displaystyle\left.\sum_{q,i}{\bar{q}}_{1}^{i}(x^{\prime})\eta\cdot\gamma\cdot Tq_{1}^{i}(x^{\prime})\right].

In the ’t Hooft limit, we get at the leading order for the 1P-functions

\displaystyle\partial^{2}\phi_{0}(x)+2Ng^{2}\Delta_{\phi}(0)\phi_{0}(x)+3Ng^{2}\phi_{0}^{3}(x)=0,

\displaystyle(i\not{\partial}-m_{q}^{i}){\hat{q}}_{1}^{i}(x)+g{\bm{T}}\cdot\not{\eta}\phi(x)q_{1}^{i}(x)=0.

(15)

At the leading order the only effect is seen on masses. For the quark field, this will be clearer below. We can solve the equation for the gluon field taking

\displaystyle\phi_{0}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2\mu^{4}}{m^{2}+\sqrt{m^{4}+2Ng^{2}\mu^{4}}}}\times

\displaystyle{\rm sn}\left(p\cdot x+\chi,\kappa\right),

(16)

being sn a Jacobi elliptical function,
\mu
and
\chi
arbitrary integration constants and
m^{2}=2Ng^{2}\Delta_{\phi}(0)
. We have

\kappa=\frac{-m^{2}+\sqrt{m^{4}+2Ng^{2}\mu^{4}}}{-m^{2}-\sqrt{m^{4}+2Ng^{2}\mu^{4}}}.

(17)

This is true provided that the following dispersion relation holds

p^{2}=m^{2}+\frac{Ng^{2}\mu^{4}}{m^{2}+\sqrt{m^{4}+2Ng^{2}\mu^{4}}}.

(18)

For the equations of the 2P-functions one has

\displaystyle\partial^{2}\Delta_{\phi}(x,y)+2Ng^{2}\Delta_{\phi}(0)\Delta(x-y)+3Ng^{2}\phi_{0}^{2}(x)\Delta_{\phi}(x-y)

\displaystyle=g\sum_{q,i}{\bar{Q}}^{ia}_{\nu}(x,y)\gamma^{\nu}T^{a}{\hat{q}}_{1}^{i}(x)

\displaystyle+g\sum_{q,i}{\bar{\hat{q}}}_{1}^{i}(x)\gamma^{\nu}T^{a}Q^{ia}_{\nu}(x,y)+\delta^{4}(x-y)

\displaystyle\partial^{2}P^{ad}_{2}(x-y)=\delta_{ad}\delta^{4}(x-y)

\displaystyle(i\not{\partial}-m_{q}^{i}){\hat{S}}^{ij}_{q}(x-y)+g{\bm{T}}\cdot\not{\eta}\phi(x)S^{ij}_{q}(x-y)=\delta_{ij}\delta^{4}(x-y)

\displaystyle\partial^{2}W_{q\nu}^{ai}(x,y)+2Ng^{2}\Delta_{\phi}(0)W_{q\nu}^{ai}(x,y)+3Ng^{2}\phi_{0}^{2}(x)W_{q\nu}^{ai}(x,y)

\displaystyle=g\sum_{j}{\bar{\hat{q}}}_{1}^{j}(x)\gamma_{\nu}T^{a}{\hat{S}}^{ji}(x-y)

\displaystyle(i\not{\partial}-{\hat{M}}_{q}^{i}){\hat{Q}}^{ia}_{\mu}(x,y)+gT^{a}\gamma_{\mu}\Delta_{\phi}(x-y){\hat{q}}_{1}^{i}(x)=0.

(19)

To solve these equations, let us consider

\displaystyle\partial^{2}\Delta_{0}(x-y)+[m^{2}+3Ng^{2}\phi_{0}^{2}(x)]\Delta_{0}(x-y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta^{4}(x-y).

(20)

In momenta space, the solution of this equation is given by

,

\displaystyle\Delta_{0}(p)=M{\hat{Z}}(\mu,m,Ng^{2})\frac{2\pi^{3}}{K^{3}(\kappa)}\times

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\frac{e^{-(n+\frac{1}{2})\pi\frac{K^{\prime}(\kappa)}{K(\kappa)}}}{1-e^{-(2n+1)\frac{K^{\prime}(\kappa)}{K(\kappa)}\pi}}\times

\displaystyle(2n+1)^{2}\frac{1}{p^{2}-m_{n}^{2}+i\epsilon}

(21)

and

a given constant. The spectrum is given by

5
QCD in the low energy limit

Using the technique devised in
Frasca:2013tma
, the next-to-leading order term is given by

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(x)=g\frac{1}{N^{2}-1}\int d^{4}x^{\prime}\Delta_{0}(x-x^{\prime})\left[\sum_{q,i}\eta\cdot\gamma\cdot TS_{q}^{ii}(0)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\sum_{q,i}{\bar{q}}_{1}^{i}(x^{\prime})\eta\cdot\gamma\cdot Tq_{1}^{i}(x^{\prime})\right].

(23)

Given equation for the quark 1P-function

(i\not{\partial}-m_{q})q_{1}^{i}(x)+g{\bm{T}}\cdot\not{\eta}\phi(x)q_{1}^{i}(x)=0,

(24)

one has

\displaystyle(i\not{\partial}-m_{q})q_{1}^{i}(x)+g{\bm{T}}\cdot\not{\eta}\phi_{0}(x)q_{1}^{i}(x)

\displaystyle+g^{2}\frac{1}{N^{2}-1}\int d^{4}x^{\prime}\Delta_{0}(x-x^{\prime})\sum_{q,k}{\bar{q}}_{1}^{k}(x^{\prime}){\bm{T}}\cdot\not{\eta}q_{1}^{k}(x^{\prime})\times

\displaystyle{\bm{T}}\cdot\not{\eta}q_{1}^{i}(x)=0.

(25)

’t Hooft limit implies that the
\phi_{0}
term can be neglected with respect to the second one and we get

\displaystyle(i\not{\partial}-m_{q})q_{1}^{i}(x)

\displaystyle+g^{2}\frac{1}{N^{2}-1}\int d^{4}x^{\prime}\Delta_{0}(x-x^{\prime})\sum_{q,k}{\bar{q}}_{1}^{k}(x^{\prime}){\bm{T}}\cdot\not{\eta}q_{1}^{k}(x^{\prime})\times

\displaystyle{\bm{T}}\cdot\not{\eta}q_{1}^{i}(x)=0.

(26)

For the quark propagator one has instead

(i\not{\partial}-m_{q})S^{ij}_{q}(x-y)+g{\bm{T}}\cdot\not{\eta}\phi(x)S^{ij}_{q}(x-y)=\delta_{ij}\delta^{4}(x-y)

(27)

therefore

\displaystyle(i\not{\partial}-m_{q})S^{ij}_{q}(x-y)

\displaystyle+g{\bm{T}}\cdot\not{\eta}\phi_{0}(x)S^{ij}_{q}(x-y)

\displaystyle+g^{2}\frac{1}{N^{2}-1}\int d^{4}x^{\prime}\Delta_{0}(x-x^{\prime})\sum_{q,k}{\bar{q}}_{1}^{k}(x^{\prime}){\bm{T}}\cdot\not{\eta}q_{1}^{k}(x^{\prime})\times

\displaystyle{\bm{T}}\cdot\not{\eta}S^{ij}_{q}(x-y)=\delta_{ij}\delta^{4}(x-y).

(28)

Again, by the ’t Hooft limit we can neglect the
\phi_{0}
term with respect to the second one and we get

\displaystyle(i\not{\partial}-m_{q})S^{ij}_{q}(x-y)

\displaystyle+g^{2}\frac{1}{N^{2}-1}\int d^{4}x^{\prime}\Delta_{0}(x-x^{\prime})\sum_{q,k}{\bar{q}}_{1}^{k}(x^{\prime}){\bm{T}}\cdot\not{\eta}q_{1}^{k}(x^{\prime})\times

\displaystyle{\bm{T}}\cdot\not{\eta}S^{ij}_{q}(x-y)

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}\delta^{4}(x-y).

(29)

We can recognize here the equations for the 1P- and 2P-functions of a non-local Nambu-Jona-Lasinio model. These are not generally treatable. They should be solved straightforwardly being already quantum averaged. In order to obtain a gap equation, we need to recover the Nambu-Jona-Lasinio-model from which they can be obtained
doing some kind of backtracking. Only in this way a gap equation is derived.
Indeed, such a model has the Lagrangian

\displaystyle L_{NJL}=\sum_{i,q}\left[{\bar{q}}_{i}(x)(i\not{\partial}-m_{q})q_{i}(x)+\right.

\displaystyle\left.\frac{g^{2}}{N^{2}-1}{\bar{q}}_{i}(x)\int d^{4}x^{\prime}\Delta_{0}(x-x^{\prime})\sum_{k,q^{\prime}}{\bar{q}^{\prime}}_{k}(x^{\prime}){\bm{T}}\cdot\not{\eta}q^{\prime}_{k}(x^{\prime}){\bm{T}}\cdot\not{\eta}q_{i}(x)\right].

(30)

From this, one can get a quark gap equation that is identical to the one given in
Frasca:2019ysi
as proven in
Frasca:2021yuu
.

6
Conclusions

We derived the set of Dyson-Schwinger equations, till to 2P-functions, for QCD with the Bender-Milton-Savage technique. We treated them in the ’t Hooft limit. The low-energy limit is a nonlocal-Nambu-Jona-Lasinio model. This was shown obtaining the corresponding 1P- and 2P-equations for its correlation functions. The Lagrangian of the model is also given.

Work is ongoing to get the gap equation and to analyze its properties in view of recent g-2 Fermilab measurement
Frasca:2021yuu
.

Search for Dark Matter Produced in Association with a Higgs Boson Decaying to b ​ b ¯ b\bar{b} using 36 fb -1 of p ​ p pp collisions at s = 13 \sqrt{s}=13 TeV with the ATLAS Detector
The ATLAS Collaboration

Abstract
Several extensions of the Standard Model predict associated production of dark-matter particles with a Higgs boson. Such processes are searched for in final states with missing transverse momentum and a Higgs boson decaying to a b ​ b ¯ b\bar{b} pair with the ATLAS detector using 36.1 fb -1 of p ​ p pp collisions at a center-of-mass energy of 13 TeV at the LHC. The observed data are in agreement with the Standard Model predictions and limits are placed on the associated production of dark-matter particles and a Higgs boson.

Events are selected by an
E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}
trigger based on calorimeter
information
[
60
]
.
Its threshold was
110\penalty\ \text{Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
for most of the data taking period, and lower in the first third.
Events are required to have at least one
pp
collision
vertex reconstructed from at least two inner detector (ID) tracks
with
p_{\text{T}}^{\mathrm{track}}>0.4\penalty\ \text{Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
. The primary vertex (PV) for
each event is the vertex with the highest

\sum(p_{\text{T}}^{\mathrm{track}})^{2}
.

Reconstruction of muons (
\mu
) incorporates tracks or track segments
found in the muon spectrometer and matched ID tracks. Identified
muons must satisfy the “loose” quality
criteria
[
61
]
and have
|\eta|<2.7
. Electrons (
e
) are
reconstructed by matching an ID track to a cluster of energy in the
calorimeter. Electron candidates are identified through a likelihood-based
method
[
62
]
and must satisfy the
“loose” operating point and be within
|\eta|<2.47
. Muon and
electron candidates must have
p_{\text{T}}>7\penalty\ \text{Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
and are required to be
isolated by limiting the sum of
p_{\text{T}}
for tracks within a cone in

\Delta R
around the lepton direction, as in
Ref.
[
31
]
.

Jets reconstructed from three-dimensional clusters of calorimeter cells
[
63
]
with the anti-
k_{t}
algorithm
[
64

are used to identify the
h\to b\bar{b}
decay. For small to moderate
h
momenta, the decay products can be resolved using jets with a radius parameter
R=0.4
(small-
R
jets or
j
). The decay products of high-momenta

become collimated and are reconstructed using a single jet with
R=1.0
(large-
R
jet or
J
).
Small-
R
jets with
|\eta|<2.5
must
satisfy
p_{\text{T}}>20\penalty\ \text{Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
and are called “central”, while those with

2.5<|\eta|<4.5
must have
p_{\text{T}}>30\penalty\ \text{Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
and are called
“forward”. Small-
R
jets are corrected for pileup
[
65
]
, and central small-
R
jets with

20\penalty\ \text{Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}<p_{\text{T}}<60\penalty\ \text{Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
and
|\eta|<2.4
are additionally required to be identified as originating from the PV using associated tracks
[
66
]
. Small-
R

jets closer than
\Delta R=0.2
to an electron candidate
are rejected.
Large-
R
jets are reconstructed independently of small-
R
jets and trimmed
[
67
,
68
]
to reduce the effects of
pileup and the underlying event.
Furthermore, large-
R
jets must fulfill

p_{\text{T}}>200\penalty\ \text{Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
and
|\eta|<2.0
. To improve the resolution and
minimize uncertainties, the mass of large-
R
jets is determined by
the resolution-weighted mean of the mass measured using
calorimeter information alone and the track-assisted
jet mass
[
69
]
.
The latter is obtained by scaling
the mass determined using ID tracks alone by the ratio of jet
p_{\text{T}}
measured in the
calorimeter and in the ID.

Multivariate algorithms are used to identify jets containing

b
-hadrons (
b
-tagging), which are expected in
h\to b\bar{b}

decays
[
68
,
70
]
. These
algorithms are applied directly to small-
R
jets, while for large-
R

jets they are applied to track-jets matched to large-
R

jets. Track-jets are reconstructed from ID tracks matched to the PV
using the anti-
k_{t}
algorithm with

, and must fulfill
p_{\text{T}}>10\penalty\ \text{Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
and
|\eta|<2.5
.

The
\vec{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}
observable is calculated as the negative of the vector sum
of the transverse momenta of
e
,
\mu
, and jet candidates in the event. The transverse momenta
not associated with any
e
,
\mu
, or jet candidates are accounted for using ID
tracks
[
71
,
72
]
. Similarly,

\vec{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\mathrm{miss,trk}}
is defined as the negative of the
vector sum of the transverse momenta of tracks with
p_{\text{T}}>0.5\penalty\ \text{Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
associated with the PV
and within
|\eta|<2.5
.

The signal is characterized by high
E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}
, no isolated leptons, and an
invariant mass of the
h
candidate
m_{h}
compatible with the observed
Higgs boson mass of 125
GeV

[
73
]
. In the signal
region (SR) described below, the dominant backgrounds from

Z(\nu\nu)+\mathrm{jets}
,
W+\mathrm{jets}
, and
t\bar{t}
production contribute respectively
30–60%, 10–25%, and 15–50% of the total
background, depending on
E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}
and the
b
-tag multiplicity. The
models for
V\!+\!\mathrm{jets}
and
t\bar{t}
are constrained using two control
regions (CR): the single-muon control region (1
\mu
-CR) is designed to
constrain the
t\bar{t}
and
W\!+\!\mathrm{jets}
backgrounds, while the two-lepton
control region (2

Z\!+\!\mathrm{jets}

The SR requires
E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}>150\,\text{Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
, and no isolated

\mu

The multijet background contributes due to mismeasured jet momenta. To suppress it, additional selections are required:
\min\left[\Delta\phi\left(\vec{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}},\vec{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{j}\right)\right]>\pi/9
for the three highest-

(leading) small-

\Delta\phi\left(\vec{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}},\vec{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,\mathrm{miss,trk}}\right)<\pi/2
, and

for events with fewer than two central
b
-tagged small-
R
jets. The requirements using
p_{\text{T}}^{\mathrm{miss,trk}}
also reduce non-collision backgrounds.

In the “resolved” regime, defined by
E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}<500\penalty\ \text{Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}

candidate is reconstructed from two leading

-tagged central small-
R

-tagged central small-

A separation in

\vec{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}
of more than

To improve the trigger efficiency modeling, events are retained only if the
scalar sum
H_{\mathrm{T}}
of the

of the two (three) leading jets fulfills

H_{\mathrm{T},2j}>120\penalty\ \text{Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
(

if two (more than two) central jets are present. Further optimization
of the event selection described below provides an additional

Ref.
[

, for a small signal loss.
Events with a hadronic
\tau
-lepton candidate, identified either by an

[

In the “merged” regime, defined by

In summary, a search for DM produced in association with a Higgs boson
in final states with
E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}
and a
b\bar{b}
pair from the
h\to b\bar{b}
decay was conducted using
36.1\penalty\ \mbox{fb${}^{-1}$}
of
pp
collisions at
\sqrt{s}=13\penalty\ \text{Te\kern-1.00006ptV}
recorded by the ATLAS detector at the LHC. The results are
in agreement with SM predictions, and a substantial
region of the parameter space of a representative
Z^{\prime}
-2HDM model is excluded,
significantly improving upon previous results. Stringent limits are
also placed on the production cross-section of non-SM events with large

E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}
and a Higgs boson
without extra model assumptions.

Nijenhuis operators on low dimensional pre-Lie algebras and solutions of CYBE
Xiang Gao, Chuangchuang Kang, Jiafeng Lü, Shizhuo Yu

Abstract
. We first classify all Nijenhuis operators on 2-dimensional complex pre-Lie algebras and 3-dimensional complex associative algebras. Moreover, applying the obtained classification, we provide solutions of classical Yang-Baxter equation of the semi-direct product sub-adjacent Lie algebras.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
17B60, 16T25, 17B81

1.
Introduction

Nijenhuis torsion was first introduced by Nijenhuis in 1951 (
[
20
]
).
Let
M
be a smooth
n
-dimensional manifold,
N
a smooth operator field on it, and

v
,
u
smooth vector fields. Then the Nijenhuis torsion
(\mathcal{T}_{N})_{~jk}^{i}
of
N
is a tensor of type (1, 2) given by the formula

\mathcal{T}_{N}(v,u)=[Nv,Nu]+N^{2}[v,u]-N[Nv,u]-N[v,Nu],

or in local coordinates
\mathbf{x}=(x^{1},...,x^{n})
,

(\mathcal{T}_{N})_{~jk}^{i}=N_{~j}^{l}\frac{\partial N_{~k}^{i}}{\partial x^{l}}-N_{~k}^{l}\frac{\partial N_{~j}^{i}}{\partial x^{l}}-N_{~l}^{i}\frac{\partial N_{~k}^{l}}{\partial x^{j}}+N_{~l}^{i}\frac{\partial N_{~j}^{l}}{\partial x^{k}}.

An operator
N
is called Nijenhuis operator if
\mathcal{T}_{N}=0
. The vanishing of the Nijenhuis torsion is a necessary (but not
sufficient) condition for integrability of an operator field (
[
4
]
). Moreover, the Nijenhuis operator plays a central role in various fields such as complex differential geometry and complex Lie algebra theory (
[
27
]
), deformation of associative (resp. Lie) algebra (
[
13
,
21
]
) and pairs of Dirac structures (
[
10
,
8
]
).
And numerous interesting generalizations have been made regarding the Nijenhuis operator, including its application to pre-Lie algebras (
[
25
]
),
n
-Lie algebras (
[
18
]
), Hom-Lie algebras (
[
9
]
) and Courant algebroids with Nijenhuis structures (
[
16
]
).

Pre-Lie algebras, also known as left-symmetric algebras, were first introduced by Cayley in the context of rooted tree algebras (
[
7
]
), which have been applied in study homogeneous convex cones (
[
24
]
), affine manifolds
and affine structures on Lie group (
[
19
,
17
]
). The author proved in
[
26
]
that an operator, which linearly depends on the local coordinates, is a Nijenhuis operator if and only if the coefficients are structure constants of a pre-Lie algebra.

In
[
25
]
, the authors defined the concept of a Nijenhuis operator on pre-Lie algebras, which generates a trivial deformation of pre-Lie algebras. The local behavior of Nijenhuis operators at singular points plays an important role in the study of complex integrable systems (
[
5
]
).
Up to now, only a few Nijenhuis operators on (simple) pre-Lie algebras are explicitly known (see
[
6
,
2
,
15
,
14
]
for references) and
a complete classification of simple pre-Lie algebras has not yet been achieved.

Let
A
be a pre-Lie algebra (product of

x
and
y
is denoted by
xy
) over a field
\mathbb{C}
,
a linear map
R:A\rightarrow A
is called a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
on
A
if

(1.1)

R(x)R(y)=R(R(x)y+xR(y)+\lambda xy),~\forall~x,y\in A.

If
N
is a Nijenhuis operator on a pre-Lie algebra that satisfies
N^{2}=0
, then
N
is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight zero on
A
(see Proposition
5.1
).
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra, a linear map
R:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}

is called an operator form of the
classical Yang-Baxter equation
(CYBE) if,

(1.2)

[R(x),R(y)]=R([R(x),y]+[x,R(y)]),~\forall~x,y\in\mathfrak{g},

which is also called a Rota-Baxter operator on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. If
R
is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight zero on a pre-Lie algebra
A
, then
R
is
a Rota-Baxter operator of weight zero on the sub-adjacent Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}(A)
(see Proposition
5.2
). Therefore, we can obtain a Rota-Baxter operator of weight zero on its sub-adjacent Lie algebra from a Nijenhuis operator on a pre-Lie algebra.

The CYBE originated from the
study of inverse scattering theory, which is recognized as the
“semi-classical
limit” of the quantum Yang-Baxter equation (
[
11
,
12
,
3
]
). Recall that
r=\sum\limits_{i}a_{i}\otimes b_{i}\in{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}
is called a solution of CYBE in
\mathfrak{g}
if

(1.3)

[r_{12},r_{13}]+[r_{12},r_{23}]+[r_{13},r_{23}]=0\;\;{\rm in}\;U({\mathfrak{g}}),

where
U({\mathfrak{g}})
is the universal
enveloping algebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
and

r_{12}=\sum\limits_{i}a_{i}\otimes b_{i}\otimes 1,\quad r_{13}=\sum\limits_{i}a_{i}\otimes 1\otimes b_{i},\quad r_{23}=\sum\limits_{i}1\otimes a_{i}\otimes b_{i}.

Semonov-Tian-Shansky (
[
23
]
) proved that if a nondegenerate symmetric invariant bilinear
form exists on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and if
r
is a skew-symmetric solution of the CYBE, then (
1.2
) is equals to (
1.3
). C. Bai (
[
1
]
) proved that a linear map
R:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a Rota-Baxter operator if and only if

r=R-R^{21}
is a skew-symmetric solution of CYBE
in
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{{\rm ad}^{\ast}}\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
. In
[
22
]
, the authors determined all Rota-Baxter operators (of weight zero) on
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
under
the Cartan-Weyl basis and found solutions of the classical Yang-Baxter equation in the
six-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})\ltimes_{\ad^{*}}\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})^{*}
. In this paper we obtain solutions of CYBE in a semidirect product sub-adjacent Lie algebra from a Nijenhuis operator on a pre-Lie algebra.

The goal of this paper is to classify Nijenhuis operators on 2-dimensional complex pre-Lie algebras and 3-dimensional complex associative algebras. Applying the classification, we also provide solutions of the classical Yang-Baxter equation in the semi-direct product sub-adjacent Lie algebras.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall some basic concepts and known results about pre-Lie algebras and Nijenhuis operators (
[
6
,
2
]
). In Section 3, we present all Nijenhuis operators on 2-dimensional complex pre-Lie algebras (Proposition
3.1
and
3.2
). Section 4 provides all
Nijenhuis operators on 3-dimensional complex associative algebras (Proposition

4.1
and
4.2
).
In Section 5, we give Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras
{\mathfrak{g}}(A)
that are derived from Nijenhuis operators on pre-Lie algebras
A
. Furthermore, for these Rota-Baxter operators on
{\mathfrak{g}}(A)
, we give the induced solutions
of CYBE in the Lie algebras
{\mathfrak{g}}(A)\ltimes_{{\rm ad}^{\ast}}{\mathfrak{g}}(A)^{\ast}
.

Throughout this paper, all pre-Lie algebras are of finite dimension over
\mathbb{C}
and all associative algebras are of finite (Krull) dimension over
\mathbb{C}
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we will provide some preliminaries and classification results of 2-dimensional and 3-dimensional pre-Lie algebras in
[
6
]
and
[
2
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A
be a vector space over

\mathbb{C}
with a bilinear product
\cdot:A\times A\rightarrow A
. Then
(A,\cdot)
is called
a
pre-Lie algebra
if for any
x,y,z\in A
,

(2.4)

(x\cdot y)\cdot z-x\cdot(y\cdot z)=(y\cdot x)\cdot z-y\cdot(x\cdot z).

For a pre-Lie algebra
(A,\cdot)
, the commutator

(2.5)

[x,y]=x\cdot y-y\cdot x,

defines a Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}(A)
, which is called the

sub-adjacent Lie algebra
of
(A,\cdot)
.

Proposition 2.2
.

(
[
6
]
)
Let
\left(A,\cdot\right)
be a 2-dimensional commutative pre-Lie algebra and
\left\{e_{1},e_{2}\right\}
be a basis of
A
. Then
(A,\cdot)
must be isomorphic to one of the
following algebras:

(A_{1})

e_{1}\cdot e_{1}=e_{1},~e_{2}\cdot e_{2}=e_{2}
;

(A_{2})

e_{1}\cdot e_{1}=e_{1},~e_{1}.e_{2}=e_{2}\cdot e_{1}=e_{2}
;

(A_{3})

e_{1}\cdot e_{1}=e_{1}
;

(A_{4})

e_{i}\cdot e_{j}=0,~i,j=1,2
;

(A_{5})

e_{1}\cdot e_{1}=e_{2}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

(
[
6
]
)
Let
\left(B,\cdot\right)
be a 2-dimensional non commutative pre-Lie algebra and
\left\{e_{1},e_{2}\right\}
be a basis of
B
. Then
(B,\cdot)
must be isomorphic to one of the
following algebras:

(B_{1})

e_{2}\cdot e_{1}=-e_{1},~e_{2}\cdot e_{2}=e_{1}-e_{2}
;

(B_{2})

e_{2}\cdot e_{1}=-e_{1},~e_{2}\cdot e_{2}=-e_{2}
;

(B_{3})

e_{2}\cdot e_{1}=-e_{1},~e_{2}\cdot e_{2}=ke_{2},~k\neq-1
;

(B_{4})

e_{1}\cdot e_{2}=e_{1},~e_{2}\cdot e_{2}=e_{2}
;

(B_{5})

e_{1}\cdot e_{2}=le_{1},~e_{2}\cdot e_{1}=(l-1)e_{1},~e_{2}\cdot e_{2}=e_{1}+le_{2},~l\neq 0
;

(B_{6})

e_{1}\cdot e_{1}=2e_{1},~e_{1}\cdot e_{2}=e_{2},~e_{2}\cdot e_{2}=e_{1}
.

Proposition 2.4
.

(
[
2
]
)
Let
\left(C,\cdot\right)
be a 3-dimensional commutative associative algebra and
\left\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\right\}
be a basis of
C
. Then
(C,\cdot)
must be isomorphic to one of the
following algebras:

(C_{1})

e_{3}\cdot e_{3}=e_{1}
;

(C_{2})

e_{i}\cdot e_{j}=0,~i,j=1,2,3
;

(C_{3})

e_{2}\cdot e_{2}=e_{1},~e_{3}\cdot e_{3}=e_{1}
;

(C_{4})

e_{2}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}\cdot e_{2}=e_{1},~e_{3}\cdot e_{3}=e_{2}
;

(C_{5})

e_{1}\cdot e_{1}=e_{1},~e_{2}\cdot e_{2}=e_{2},~e_{3}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}
;

(C_{6})

e_{2}\cdot e_{2}=e_{2},~e_{3}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}
;

(C_{7})

e_{1}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}\cdot e_{1}=e_{1},~e_{2}\cdot e_{2}=e_{2},~e_{3}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}
;

(C_{8})

e_{3}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}
;

(C_{9})

e_{1}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}\cdot e_{1}=e_{1},~e_{3}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}
;

(C_{10})

e_{1}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}\cdot e_{1}=e_{1},~e_{2}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}\cdot e_{2}=e_{2},~e_{3}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}
;

(C_{11})

e_{1}\cdot e_{1}=e_{2},~e_{3}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}
;

(C_{12})

e_{1}\cdot e_{1}=e_{2},~e_{1}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}\cdot e_{1}=e_{1},~e_{2}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}\cdot e_{2}=e_{2},~e_{3}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

(
[
2
]
)
Let
\left(D,\cdot\right)
be a 3-dimensional non-commutative associative algebra and
\left\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\right\}
be a basis of

. Then
(D,\cdot)
must be isomorphic to one of the

(D_{1})

e_{1}\cdot e_{2}=\frac{1}{2}e_{3},~e_{2}\cdot e_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}e_{3}
;

(D_{2})

e_{2}\cdot e_{1}=-e_{3}
;

(D_{3})

e_{1}\cdot e_{1}=e_{3},~e_{1}\cdot e_{2}=e_{3},~e_{2}\cdot e_{2}=\lambda e_{3},~\lambda\neq 0
;

(D_{4})

e_{3}\cdot e_{2}=e_{2},~e_{3}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}
;

(D_{5})

e_{2}\cdot e_{3}=e_{2},~e_{3}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}
;

(D_{6})

e_{1}\cdot e_{1}=e_{1},~e_{3}\cdot e_{2}=e_{2},~e_{3}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}
;

e_{1}\cdot e_{3}=e_{1},~e_{3}\cdot e_{1}=e_{1},~e_{3}\cdot e_{2}=e_{2},~e_{3}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}

e_{1}\cdot e_{1}=e_{1},~e_{1}\cdot e_{2}=e_{2}\cdot e_{1}=e_{2},~e_{1}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}\cdot e_{1}=e_{3},~e_{3}\cdot e_{2}=e_{2},~e_{3}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}
;

(D_{10})

e_{3}\cdot e_{1}=e_{1},~e_{3}\cdot e_{2}=e_{2},~e_{3}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}
;

e_{1}\cdot e_{3}=e_{1},~e_{2}\cdot e_{3}=e_{2},~e_{3}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}
;

(D_{12})

e_{3}\cdot e_{1}=e_{1},~e_{2}\cdot e_{3}=e_{2},~e_{3}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3}
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a pre-Lie algebra. A linear map
N:A\rightarrow A
is called a
Nijenhuis operator
if

(2.6)

N(x)\cdot N(y)=N(N(x)\cdot y+x\cdot N(y)-N(x\cdot y)),\quad\forall~x,y\in A.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a pre-Lie algebra and
\left\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\right\}
be a basis of
A
. A linear map
N:A\rightarrow A
is a Nijenhuis operator if

(2.7)

N(e_{i})\cdot N(e_{j})=N(N(e_{i})\cdot e_{j}+e_{i}\cdot N(e_{j})-N(e_{i}\cdot e_{j})),\quad\forall~1\leq i,j\leq n.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a pre-Lie algebra and
\left\{e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n}\right\}
be a basis of
A
. For all positive integers
i,j,t=1,2,\cdots,n
and structural constants
C_{ij}^{t}\in\mathbb{C}
, set

(2.8)

e_{i}\cdot e_{j}=\sum_{t=1}^{n}C_{ij}^{t}e_{t}.

If
N:A\rightarrow A
is a linear map given by

(2.9)

N\left(e_{i}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}n_{ij}e_{j},\quad~n_{ij}\in\mathbb{C},

then
N
is a Nijenhuis operator on
A
if and only if
n_{ij}
satisfies the following equations:

(2.10)

\sum_{k,l,m,t=1}^{n}\left(C_{kl}^{m}n_{ik}n_{jl}+C_{ij}^{t}n_{tl}n_{lm}-C_{kj}^{l}n_{ik}n_{lm}-C_{il}^{k}n_{jl}n_{km}\right)=0.

Proof.

For all
e_{i},e_{j}\in\left\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\right\}
, let

N\left(e_{i}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}n_{ik}e_{k},~N\left(e_{j}\right)=\sum_{l=1}^{n}n_{jl}e_{l},~e_{i}\cdot e_{j}=\sum_{t=1}^{n}C_{ij}^{t}e_{t}.

By calculating the left-hand side of (
2.7
), we have:

N(e_{i})\cdot N(e_{j})=\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}n_{ik}n_{jl}e_{k}\cdot e_{l}=\sum_{k,l,m=1}^{n}C_{kl}^{m}n_{ik}n_{jl}e_{m}.

By calculating the right-hand side of (
2.7
), we have:

\displaystyle N(N(e_{i})\cdot e_{j}+e_{i}\cdot N(e_{j})-N(e_{i}\cdot e_{j}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N(\sum_{k=1}^{n}n_{ik}e_{k}\cdot e_{j}+e_{i}\cdot\sum_{l=1}^{n}n_{jl}e_{l}-N(\sum_{t=1}^{n}C_{ij}^{t}e_{t}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N(\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}C_{kj}^{l}n_{ik}e_{l}+\sum_{l,k=1}^{n}C_{il}^{k}n_{jl}e_{k}-\sum_{t,l=1}^{n}C_{ij}^{t}n_{tl}e_{l})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l,m,t=1}^{n}(C_{kj}^{l}n_{ik}n_{lm}e_{m}+C_{il}^{k}n_{jl}n_{km}e_{m}-C_{ij}^{t}n_{tl}n_{lm}e_{m}).

If
N
is a Nijenhuis operator, then

\displaystyle N(e_{i})\cdot N(e_{j})-N(N(e_{i})\cdot e_{j}+e_{i}\cdot N(e_{j})-N(e_{i}\cdot e_{j}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l,m=1}^{n}C_{kl}^{m}n_{ik}n_{jl}e_{m}-\sum_{k,l,m,t=1}^{n}(C_{kj}^{l}n_{ik}n_{lm}e_{m}+C_{il}^{k}n_{jl}n_{km}e_{m}-C_{ij}^{t}n_{tl}n_{lm}e_{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l,m,t=1}^{n}(C_{kl}^{m}n_{ik}n_{jl}e_{m}+C_{ij}^{t}n_{tl}n_{lm}e_{m}-C_{kj}^{l}n_{ik}n_{lm}e_{m}-C_{il}^{k}n_{jl}n_{km}e_{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Therefore, (
2.10
) holds.
∎

3.1.
Nijenhuis operators on 2-dimensional commutative pre-Lie algebras

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
N:A_{i}\rightarrow A_{i}
be a linear map on pre-Lie algebras
(A_{i},\cdot)
defined by (
2.9
). Then the Nijenhuis operators on
2
-dimensional commutative pre-Lie algebras are given as follows:

(1)
The Nijenhuis operators on the pre-Lie algebra
(A_{1},\cdot)
are:

N_{A_{1}}^{1}(e_{1})=n_{11}e_{1},~~N_{A_{1}}^{1}(e_{2})=n_{21}e_{1}+(n_{11}+n_{21})e_{2},~n_{21}\neq 0.

N_{A_{1}}^{2}(e_{1})=n_{11}e_{1},~~N_{A_{1}}^{2}(e_{2})=n_{22}e_{2}.

N_{A_{1}}^{3}(e_{1})=n_{11}e_{1}+n_{12}e_{2},~~N_{A_{1}}^{3}(e_{2})=(n_{11}-n_{12})e_{2},~n_{12}\neq 0.

(2)
The Nijenhuis operators on the pre-Lie algebra
(A_{2},\cdot)
are:

N_{A_{2}}^{1}(e_{1})=n_{11}e_{1},~~N_{A_{2}}^{1}(e_{2})=n_{22}e_{2}.

N_{A_{2}}^{2}(e_{1})=n_{11}e_{1}+n_{12}e_{2},~~N_{A_{2}}^{2}(e_{2})=n_{11}e_{2},~n_{12}\neq 0.

(3)
The Nijenhuis operators on the pre-Lie algebra
(A_{3},\cdot)
are:

N_{A_{3}}^{1}(e_{1})=n_{11}e_{1},~~N_{A_{3}}^{1}(e_{2})=n_{22}e_{2}.

N_{A_{3}}^{2}(e_{1})=n_{11}e_{1}+n_{12}e_{2},~~N_{A_{3}}^{2}(e_{2})=n_{11}e_{2},~n_{12}\neq 0.

(4)
The Nijenhuis operators on the pre-Lie algebra
(A_{4},\cdot)
are:

N_{A_{4}}^{1}(e_{1})=n_{11}e_{1}+n_{12}e_{2},~~N_{A_{4}}^{1}(e_{2})=n_{21}e_{1}+n_{22}e_{2}.

(5)
The Nijenhuis operators on the pre-Lie algebra
(A_{5},\cdot)
are:

N_{A_{5}}^{1}(e_{1})=n_{11}e_{1}+n_{12}e_{2},~~N_{A_{5}}^{1}(e_{2})=n_{11}e_{2}.

Proof.

Let
\left\{e_{1},e_{2}\right\}
be a basis of 2-dimensional commutative pre-Lie algebras
(A_{i},\cdot)
. Set

N(e_{1})=n_{11}e_{1}+n_{12}e_{2},~N(e_{2})=n_{21}e_{1}+n_{22}e_{2}.

In order to show that
N
is a Nijenhuis operator, we only need to check

(3.11)

\displaystyle N(e_{1})\cdot N(e_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N(N(e_{1})\cdot e_{1}+e_{1}\cdot N(e_{1})-N(e_{1}\cdot e_{1})),

(3.12)

\displaystyle N(e_{1})\cdot N(e_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N(N(e_{1})\cdot e_{2}+e_{1}\cdot N(e_{2})-N(e_{1}\cdot e_{2})),

(3.13)

\displaystyle N(e_{2})\cdot N(e_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N(N(e_{2})\cdot e_{1}+e_{2}\cdot N(e_{1})-N(e_{2}\cdot e_{1})),

(3.14)

\displaystyle N(e_{2})\cdot N(e_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N(N(e_{2})\cdot e_{2}+e_{2}\cdot N(e_{2})-N(e_{2}\cdot e_{2})).

Calculating the left-hand side of (
3.11
) for the pre-Lie algebra
(A_{1},\cdot)
, we have:

(3.15)

\displaystyle N(e_{1})\cdot N(e_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n_{11}e_{1}+n_{12}e_{2})(n_{11}e_{1}+n_{12}e_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{n_{11}}^{2}e_{1}+{n_{12}}^{2}e_{2}.

Calculating the right-hand side of (
3.11
), we have:

(3.16)

\displaystyle N(N(e_{1})\cdot e_{1}+e_{1}\cdot N(e_{1})-N(e_{1}\cdot e_{1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N((n_{11}e_{1}+n_{12}e_{2})\cdot e_{1}+e_{1}\cdot(n_{11}e_{1}+n_{12}e_{2})-N(e_{1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N(2n_{11}e_{1}-n_{11}e_{1}-n_{12}e_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N(n_{11}e_{1}-n_{12}e_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{n_{11}}^{2}e_{1}+n_{11}n_{12}e_{2}-n_{12}n_{21}e_{1}-n_{12}n_{22}e_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n_{11}^{2}-n_{12}n_{21})e_{1}+(n_{11}n_{12}-n_{12}n_{22})e_{2}.

Comparing the coefficients in (
3.15
) and (
3.16
), we have:

(3.17)

n_{{11}}^{2}=n_{11}^{2}-n_{12}n_{21},~{n_{12}}^{2}=n_{11}n_{12}-n_{12}n_{22}.

Similarly, by calculating (
3.12
)-(
3.14
), we have:

(3.18)

\displaystyle n_{11}n_{21}=n_{11}n_{21}+n_{12}n_{21},~n_{12}n_{22}=n_{12}n_{21}+n_{12}n_{22}.

(3.19)

\displaystyle{n_{21}}^{2}=n_{21}n_{22}-n_{11}n_{21},~{n_{22}}^{2}={n_{22}}^{2}-n_{12}n_{21}.

Simplifying (

)-(

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}n_{12}n_{21}=0,\\

{n_{21}}^{2}=n_{21}n_{22}-n_{11}n_{21}.\end{array}\right.

To solve the quadratic equations, we distinguish the two cases depending on whether
or not
n_{12}=0
.

\mathbf{Case~1}
: If
n_{12}=0
, then (
3.20
) implies
n_{21}(n_{21}-n_{22}+n_{11})=0
. There are two subcases:
n_{21}=0
and
n_{21}\neq 0
. If
n_{21}\neq 0
, we have
n_{11}+n_{21}=n_{22}
. Then we get Nijenhuis operators

:

N_{A_{1}}^{1}(e_{1})=n_{11}e_{1},~~N_{A_{1}}^{1}(e_{2})=n_{21}e_{1}+(n_{11}+n_{21})e_{2},~n_{21}\neq 0.

If
n_{21}=0
, then we get Nijenhuis operators
N_{A_{1}}^{2}
:

N_{A_{1}}^{2}(e_{1})=n_{11}e_{1},~~N_{A_{1}}^{1}(e_{2})=n_{22}e_{2}.

\mathbf{Case~2}
: If
n_{12}\neq 0
, we have
n_{21}=0,~n_{22}=n_{11}-n_{12}
. Then we get Nijenhuis operators
N_{A_{1}}^{3}
:

N_{A_{1}}^{3}(e_{1})=n_{11}e_{1}+n_{12}e_{2},~~N_{A_{1}}^{3}(e_{2})=(n_{11}-n_{12})e_{2},~n_{12}\neq 0.

Therefore, we obtain all Nijenhuis operators on the pre-Lie algebra
(A_{1},\cdot)
. Similarly, we can obtain all Nijenhuis operators on other 2-dimensional commutative pre-Lie algebras.
∎

5.
Rota-Baxter operators on sub-adjacent Lie algebras
{\mathfrak{g}}(A)
and solutions of CYBE

In the previous sections, we classified Nijenhuis operators
N
on 2-dimensional pre-Lie algebras and some 3-dimensional pre-Lie algebras
(A,\cdot)
. Using these results, we first obtain Rota-Baxter operators
R
of weight zero on the sub-adjacent Lie algebras
{\mathfrak{g}}(A)
in this section. Then, the solutions of CYBE in the Lie algebras

5.1.
Rota-Baxter operators on sub-adjacent Lie algebras
{\mathfrak{g}}(A)

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\left(A,\cdot\right)
be a pre-Lie algebra and
N:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
a linear map.
If
N^{2}=0
, then
N
is a Nijenhuis operator if and only if
N
is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight zero on
(A,\cdot)
.

Proof.

Let
N:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a linear map and
N^{2}=0
. If
N
is a Nijenhuis operator, then

\displaystyle N(x)\cdot N(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N(N(x)\cdot y+x\cdot N(y)-N(x\cdot y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N(N(x)\cdot y+x\cdot N(y))-N^{2}(x\cdot y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N(N(x)\cdot y+x\cdot N(y)),\quad\forall~x,y\in A.

Therefore
N
is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight zero on
(A,\cdot)
. Proof of the converse part is similar.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

If R is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight zero on a pre-Lie algebra
(A,\cdot)
, then
R
is
a Rota-Baxter operator of weight zero on its sub-adjacent Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}(A)
.

Proof.

Suppose
R
is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight zero on a pre-Lie algebra
(A,\cdot)
. Then for all
x,y\in A
, we
have

\displaystyle R(x)\cdot R(y)=R(R(x)\cdot y+x\cdot R(y)),

\displaystyle R(y)\cdot R(x)=R(R(y)\cdot x+y\cdot R(x)).

Therefore,

\displaystyle[R(x),R(y)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R(x)\cdot R(y)-R(y)\cdot R(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R(R(x)\cdot y+x\cdot R(y))-R(R(y)\cdot x+y\cdot R(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R(R(x)\cdot y-y\cdot R(x)+x\cdot R(y)-R(y)\cdot x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R([R(x),y]+[x,R(y)]),\quad\forall~x,y\in A.

Hence
R
is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight zero on its sub-adjacent Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}(A).

∎

Let

\displaystyle[e_{1},e_{1}]=e_{1}\cdot e_{1}-e_{1}\cdot e_{1}=0,\quad\quad[e_{1},e_{2}]=e_{1}\cdot e_{2}-e_{2}\cdot e_{1}=e_{1},

\displaystyle[e_{2},e_{1}]=e_{2}\cdot e_{1}-e_{1}\cdot e_{2}=-e_{1},\quad\quad[e_{2},e_{2}]=e_{2}\cdot e_{2}-e_{2}\cdot e_{2}=0.

5.2.
Solutions of CYBE in Lie algebras
{\mathfrak{g}}(A)\ltimes_{{\rm ad}^{\ast}}{\mathfrak{g}}(A)^{\ast}

Let
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
be a representation of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
On the vector space
\mathfrak{g}\oplus V
,
we can define a Lie algebra structure
(denoted by
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho}V
) given by

(5.23)

[x_{1}+v_{1},x_{2}+v_{2}]=[x_{1},x_{2}]+\rho(x_{1})v_{2}-\rho(x_{2})v_{1},\quad\forall~x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathfrak{g},v_{1},v_{2}\in V.

Define a linear map
\rho^{\ast}:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast})
by

(5.24)

\langle\rho_{x}^{*}(\xi),y\rangle=-\langle\xi,\rho_{x}(y)\rangle,\;\;\forall~x,y\in\mathfrak{g},\xi\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}.

Then
\rho^{\ast}
is a representation of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. It is
the dual representation of
\rho
.

Proposition 5.14
.

(
[
1
]
)
If
R:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a linear map, then
R
can be viewed
as an element in
\mathfrak{g}\otimes V^{\ast}\subset(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho^{\ast}}V^{\ast})\otimes(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho^{\ast}}V^{\ast})
.

Proof.

Let
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}
be a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Let
\{v_{1},\cdots,v_{m}\}
be a basis of
V
and

\{v^{\ast}_{1},\cdots,v^{\ast}_{m}\}
be its dual basis,
that is
v^{\ast}_{i}(v_{j})=\delta_{ij}
.
Set
R(v_{i})=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}e_{j},

i=1,2,\cdots,n
.
Since as vector spaces,
{\rm Hom}(V,\mathfrak{g})\cong\mathfrak{g}\otimes V^{\ast}
, we have

(5.25)

R=\sum_{i=1}^{m}R(v_{i})\otimes v_{i}^{\ast}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}e_{j}\otimes v_{i}^{\ast}\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes V^{\ast}\subset(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho^{\ast}}V^{\ast})\otimes(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho^{\ast}}V^{\ast}).

Hence
R
can be viewed
as an element in
\mathfrak{g}\otimes V^{\ast}\subset(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho^{\ast}}V^{\ast})\otimes(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho^{\ast}}V^{\ast})
.
∎

For any tensor element
r=\sum_{i}a_{i}\otimes b_{i}\in V\otimes V
, denote
r^{21}=\sum_{i}b_{i}\otimes a_{i}
.

Lemma 5.15
.

(
[
1
]
)
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra. A linear map
R:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a Rota-Baxter operator if and only if

r=R-R^{21}
is a skew-symmetric solution of CYBE
in
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{{\rm ad}^{\ast}}\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}

Proposition 5.16
.

Let
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
be a basis of
{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}
and

\{e^{\ast}_{1},e^{\ast}_{2}\}
be its dual basis. Then
{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}\ltimes_{{\rm ad}^{\ast}}{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}^{\ast}
has a basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{1}^{\ast},e_{2}^{\ast}\}
with respect to which the non-zero products are given by the following:

[e_{1},e_{2}]=e_{1},\quad\quad[e_{1},e^{\ast}_{1}]=-e^{\ast}_{2},\quad\quad[e_{2},e^{\ast}_{1}]=e^{\ast}_{1}.

The solutions of CYBE in the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}\ltimes_{{\rm ad}^{\ast}}{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}^{\ast}
are given by

r_{1}=n_{21}e_{1}\otimes e_{2}^{\ast}-e_{2}^{\ast}\otimes n_{21}e_{1}.

Proof.

Let
\rho=\ad^{\ast}
. By (
5.24
),
the dual representation
\ad^{\ast}:{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}({\mathfrak{g}_{1}}^{\ast})
is defined by

(5.26)

\langle\ad_{x}^{*}(\xi),y\rangle=-\langle\xi,\ad_{x}(y)\rangle,\;\;\forall~x,y\in\mathfrak{g}_{1},~\xi\in{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}^{*}.

By (
5.23
) we have

[e_{1},e_{1}]=0,\quad\quad[e_{1},e_{2}]=-[e_{2},e_{1}]=e_{1},\quad\quad[e_{2},e_{2}]=0.

[e^{\ast}_{1},e^{\ast}_{1}]=0,\quad\quad[e^{\ast}_{1},e^{\ast}_{2}]=-[e^{\ast}_{2},e^{\ast}_{1}]=0,\quad\quad[e^{\ast}_{2},e^{\ast}_{2}]=0.

[e_{1},e^{\ast}_{1}]=-[e^{\ast}_{1},e_{1}]=\ad_{e_{1}}^{*}e^{\ast}_{1},\quad\quad[e_{1},e^{\ast}_{2}]=-[e^{\ast}_{2},e_{1}]=\ad_{e_{1}}^{*}e^{\ast}_{2},

[e_{2},e^{\ast}_{1}]=-[e^{\ast}_{1},e_{2}]=\ad_{e_{2}}^{*}e^{\ast}_{1},\quad\quad[e_{2},e^{\ast}_{2}]=-[e^{\ast}_{2},e_{2}]=\ad_{e_{2}}^{*}e^{\ast}_{2}.

For
\ad_{e_{1}}^{*}(e^{\ast}_{1})
, set
\ad_{e_{1}}^{*}(e^{\ast}_{1})=k_{11}e^{\ast}_{1}+k_{12}e^{\ast}_{2},~k_{11},~k_{12}\in\mathbb{C}.

Then

\langle\ad_{e_{1}}^{*}(e^{\ast}_{1}),{e_{1}}\rangle=-\langle e^{\ast}_{1},\ad_{e_{1}}(e_{1})\rangle=-\langle e^{\ast}_{1},0\rangle=0,\;\;

(k_{11}e^{\ast}_{1}+k_{12}e^{\ast}_{2})({e_{1}})=k_{11}.

This implies that
k_{11}=0
.

\langle\ad_{e_{1}}^{*}(e^{\ast}_{1}),{e_{2}}\rangle=-\langle e^{\ast}_{1},\ad_{e_{1}}(e_{2})\rangle=-\langle e^{\ast}_{1},{e_{1}}\rangle=-1,\;\;

(k_{11}e^{\ast}_{1}+k_{12}e^{\ast}_{2})({e_{2}})=k_{12}.

We obtain
k_{12}=-1
. Therefore,
\ad_{e_{1}}^{*}(e^{\ast}_{1})=-e^{\ast}_{2}
.

For
\ad_{e_{1}}^{*}(e^{\ast}_{2})
, set
\ad_{e_{1}}^{*}(e^{\ast}_{2})=k_{21}e^{\ast}_{1}+k_{22}e^{\ast}_{2},~k_{21},~k_{22}\in\mathbb{C}.

We have

\langle\ad_{e_{1}}^{*}(e^{\ast}_{2}),{e_{1}}\rangle=-\langle e^{\ast}_{2},\ad_{e_{1}}(e_{1})\rangle=-\langle e^{\ast}_{2},0\rangle=0,\;\;

(k_{21}e^{\ast}_{1}+k_{22}e^{\ast}_{2})({e_{1}})=k_{21}.

This implies that
k_{21}=0
.

\langle\ad_{e_{1}}^{*}(e^{\ast}_{2}),{e_{2}}\rangle=-\langle e^{\ast}_{2},\ad_{e_{1}}(e_{2})\rangle=-\langle e^{\ast}_{2},{e_{1}}\rangle=0,\;\;

(k_{21}e^{\ast}_{1}+k_{22}e^{\ast}_{2})({e_{2}})=k_{22}.

We obtain
k_{22}=0
. Hence
\ad_{e_{1}}^{*}(e^{\ast}_{2})=0
.

For
\ad_{e_{2}}^{*}(e^{\ast}_{1})
, set
\ad_{e_{2}}^{*}(e^{\ast}_{1})=k_{31}e^{\ast}_{1}+k_{32}e^{\ast}_{2},~k_{31},~k_{32}\in\mathbb{C}.

We have

\langle\ad_{e_{2}}^{*}(e^{\ast}_{1}),{e_{1}}\rangle=-\langle e^{\ast}_{1},\ad_{e_{2}}(e_{1})\rangle=-\langle e^{\ast}_{1},-{e_{1}}\rangle=1,\;\;

(k_{31}e^{\ast}_{1}+k_{32}e^{\ast}_{2})({e_{1}})=k_{31}.

Then, we obtain
k_{31}=1
.

\langle\ad_{e_{2}}^{*}(e^{\ast}_{1}),{e_{2}}\rangle=-\langle e^{\ast}_{1},\ad_{e_{2}}(e_{2})\rangle=-\langle e^{\ast}_{2},0\rangle=0,\;\;

(k_{31}e^{\ast}_{1}+k_{32}e^{\ast}_{2})({e_{2}})=k_{32}.

Thus
k_{32}=0
. Therefore, we have
\ad_{e_{2}}^{*}(e^{\ast}_{1})=e^{\ast}_{1}
.

For
\ad_{e_{2}}^{*}(e^{\ast}_{2})
, set

Representation theory of the group of automorphisms of a finite rooted tree.
Fabio Scarabotti

Abstract
We construct the ordinary irreducible representations of the group of automorphisms of a finite rooted tree and we get a natural parametrization of them. To achieve these goals, we introduce and study the combinatorics of tree compositions, a natural generalization of set compositions but with new features and more complexity. These combinatorial structures lead to a family of permutation representations which have the same parametrization of the irreducible representations. Our trees are not necessarily spherically homogeneous and our approach is coordinate free. 1 1 1 AMS 2020 Math. Subj. Class. Primary: 20C15. Secondary: 05E10, 20B25, 20C30. Keywords: Automorphisms of rooted trees, Symmetric groups, Representation theory, Clifford theory, Mackey theory.

Introduction

The symmetric group
S_{n}
may be seen as the group of automorphisms of a rooted tree
\mathcal{T}
with just one level, that is an
n
-star. Similarly, the wreath product of two symmetric groups
S_{m}\wr S_{n}
is the group of automorphisms of a rooted tree with two levels: just add
m
children to each leaf of the
n
-star; we may continue: the iterated wreath product of
h
symmetric groups is naturally the group of automorphisms of a spherically homogeneous rooted tree
\mathcal{T}
with
h
levels. This observation was the starting point of the joint paper with Tullio Ceccherini-Silberstein and Filippo Tolli
[
6
]
, where, in particular, we analyzed the permutation representations on the varieties of spherically homogeneous subtrees, showing that they decomposes without multiplicity. This is a clear analogy with the
h=1
case, where they correspond to the permutation representations
M^{n-k,k}
of the symmetric group.

In the present paper we push the analogy further. We introduce a theory of tree compositions, which generalizes the usual theory of set compositions, and we use it to construct a family of permutation representations of
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})
, the group of automorphisms of a finite rooted tree
\mathcal{T}
(which is not necessarily spherically homogeneous).
These permutation representations are the natural generalization of the
M^{\lambda}
usually introduced for the symmetric group (see
[
9
,
19
,
25
]
) and we parametrize them by means of what we have called trees of partitions. Then we show that these combinatorial objects also yields a natural parametrization of the conjugacy classes. The irreducible representations of
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})
are constructed in two independent ways: by means of iterated Clifford theory and then by means of Mackey theory. The latter approach stress further the analogies with the representation theory of the symmetric group: we show that we can use one of the classical approach for
S_{n}
, namely that in
[
19
, Chapter 2]
, in such a way that the representation theory of
S_{n}
becomes a particular case of that of
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})
. In both cases, the irreducible representations are naturally parametrized by means of the trees of partitions previously introduced.

This is the plan of the paper.

In Section
2
we define the fundamental notion of tree composition. It is a natural generalization of the composition of a set but it has two peculiarities. First of all, it is presented in a coordinate free fashion, by means of surjective maps between rooted trees. Moreover, these maps must satisfy a regularity condition imposed by the representation theory.
In Section
3
we define the quotient of a tree composition. Its name derives from its construction: we start by introducing a suitable equivalence relation and its quotient yields the desired composition.
In Section
4
we introduce the type of a tree composition: it is a generalization, in our setting, of the usual notion of integer composition. Then we introduce the notion of tree of partitions: it is the generalization, in our setting, of the notion of integer partition and we show that tree of partitions parametrizes the equivalence classes of tree compositions.

In Section
5
we describe and parametrize the conjugacy classes of
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})
by means equivalence classes of tree compositions which in turn are parametrized by the tree of partitions associated to
\mathcal{T}
. Similar results were obtained in
[
16
]
.
In Section
6
we give a first construction of the irreducible representations of
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})
by iterating Clifford theory, showing also that they are naturally subspaces of the permutation representations given by the action of the group on the tree compositions. This way, we are naturally lead to the parametrization by means of the trees of partitions.

In Section
7
we construct a theory of common refinements of two tree compositions. It is the key ingredient to parametrize diagonal actions of
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})
on products of spaces of tree compositions and therefore to apply Mackey theory.
In Section
8
we introduce a total order between tree of partitions that generalizes the lexicographic order for integer partitions. Then we generalizes, in our setting, two basic combinatorial and algebraic facts which are fundamental ingredient in the representation theory of the symmetric group, namely the existence and uniqueness of the
0-1
matrix in
[
19
, 2.1.1]
and the easier implication of the Gale-Ryser Theorem
[
19
, 1.4.17]
, with the dominance order replaced by the total order. These results are quite easy for set compositions but in the context of tree compositions they require more technical proofs.
In Section
9
we finally give a second construction of the ordinary irreducible representations of
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})
, exactly as in
[
19
, Theorem 2.1.11]
. This approach does not use the irreducible representation of the symmetric group and, with respect to the Clifford approach, it requires just induction of the trivial and alternating representations from two subgroups.

Other references on the approach to the representation theory of the symmetric group that we follow in Section

Definition 4.3
.

A

is a partitional labeling of a rooted tree
\mathcal{Q}
such that: (i) among the children of an internal vertex of

are

.

and

9
The irreducible representations via Mackey theory

Now we set the stage for the application of Mackey theory of induced representations for which we refer to
[
4
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
29
]
.

If
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{M}
are compositional trees for
\mathcal{T}
we denote by
\mathsf{I}(\mathcal{T};\mathcal{P},\mathcal{M})
the set of all equivalence classes of coarsest common refinements of couples
(\varphi,\psi)\in\mathsf{C}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{P})\times\mathsf{C}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{M})
; cf. (
7.7
) and Definition
7.7
.
We denote by
[\mathcal{R}]
an element of
\mathsf{I}(\mathcal{T};\mathcal{P},\mathcal{M})
. If
(\varphi,\psi)\in\mathsf{C}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{P})\times\mathsf{C}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{M})
then we denote by
[\mathcal{R}](\varphi,\psi)
the equivalence classes in
\mathsf{I}(\mathcal{T};\mathcal{P},\mathcal{M})
containing the coarsest common refinement of
(\varphi,\psi)
. The diagonal action of
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})

on
\mathsf{C}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{P})\times\mathsf{C}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{M})
is given by:

g(\varphi,\psi)\coloneqq(\varphi\circ g^{-1},\psi\circ g^{-1}),\qquad\text{for }(\varphi,\psi)\in\mathsf{C}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{P})\times\mathsf{C}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{M}),\quad g\in\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T});

cf. (
6.1
).
From Proposition
7.8
it follows that
\mathsf{I}(\mathcal{T};\mathcal{P},\mathcal{M})
parametrizes the strict equivalence classes of the couples
(\varphi,\psi)
, that is the orbits of
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})

on
\mathsf{C}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{P})\times\mathsf{C}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{M})
.

Definition 9.1
.

If
\varphi\in\mathsf{C}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{P})
and
\psi\in\mathsf{C}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{M})
are fixed tree compositions we denote by
\mathsf{I}(\mathcal{T};\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert,\lvert\psi^{-1}\rvert)
the set of all equivalence classes of coarsest common refinements of couples
(\varphi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})\in(\mathcal{P},\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert)\times(\mathcal{P},\lvert\psi^{-1}\rvert)
(cf. (
6.3
)).

Clearly
\mathsf{I}(\mathcal{T};\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert,\lvert\psi^{-1}\rvert)
parametrizes the diagonal orbits of
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})
on
(\mathcal{P},\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert)\times(\mathcal{P},\lvert\psi^{-1}\rvert)
and the
K_{\varphi}
-orbits on
(\mathcal{P},\lvert\psi^{-1}\rvert)
; see
[
11
, Corollary 10.4.13]
. Then for each
[\mathcal{R}]\in\mathsf{I}(\mathcal{T};\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert,\lvert\psi^{-1}\rvert)
we can choose
\psi_{[\mathcal{R}]}\in(\mathcal{P},\lvert\psi^{-1}\rvert)
such that
[\mathcal{R}]\left(\varphi,\psi_{[\mathcal{R}]}\right)=[\mathcal{R}]
and then
t_{[\mathcal{R}]}\in\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})
such that
t_{[\mathcal{R}]}\psi=\psi_{[\mathcal{R}]}
. It follows that
t_{[\mathcal{R}]}K_{\psi}t_{[\mathcal{R}]}^{-1}=K_{\psi_{[\mathcal{R}]}}
. Moreover,

H_{[\mathcal{R}]}\coloneqq K_{\varphi}\cap\left(t_{[\mathcal{R}]}K_{\psi}t_{[\mathcal{R}]}^{-1}\right)\;\text{ is the stabilizer in }\;K_{\varphi}\;\text{ of }\;\psi_{[\mathcal{R}]}

(9.1)

and the orbits of
K_{\varphi}
on
(\mathcal{P},\lvert\psi^{-1}\rvert)
are
\left\{k\psi_{[\mathcal{R}]}\colon k\in K_{\varphi}\right\}\equiv\left\{kt_{[\mathcal{R}]}\psi\colon k\in K_{\varphi}\right\}
,
[\mathcal{R}]\in\mathsf{I}(\mathcal{T};\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert,\lvert\psi^{-1}\rvert)
.
Finally,

\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})=\bigsqcup_{[\mathcal{R}]\in\mathsf{I}(\mathcal{T};\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert,\lvert\psi^{-1}\rvert)}K_{\varphi}t_{[\mathcal{R}]}K_{\psi}

(9.2)

is the decomposition of
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})
into double
K_{\varphi}-K_{\psi}
cosets, cf.
[
11
, Section 11.3]
.

We define the
sign representation

\text{sign}\colon\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}
of
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})
inductively as follows. If
H(\mathcal{T})=1
then it is the usual
sign
representation of the symmetric group. If
H(\mathcal{T})>1
then we use the notation in Example
3.5
, we assume that sign is defined on the groups
\mbox{\rm Aut}\left(\mathcal{T}_{u_{c}}\right)
,
c\in V_{\mathcal{C}}\setminus W_{\mathcal{C}}
and we set

\text{sign}(g)\coloneqq\text{sign}\left(g\rvert_{V_{\mathcal{T}}}\right)\cdot\prod_{c\in V_{\mathcal{C}}\setminus W_{\mathcal{C}}}\prod\limits_{v\in\text{\rm tv}^{-1}(c)}\text{sign}\left(\tau_{u_{c},g(v)}\circ g_{v}\circ\tau_{v,u_{c}}\right),

(9.3)

where we have used a system of
\tau
’s for the trivial composition (cf. Definition
3.2
). Clearly
\tau_{u_{c},g(v)}\circ g_{v}\circ\tau_{v,u_{c}}\in\mbox{\rm Aut}\left(\mathcal{T}_{u_{c}}\right)
.

Lemma 9.2
.

The function
sign
in (
9.3
) is a representation of
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})
.

Proof.

If
g,g^{\prime}\in\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})
then

\begin{split}\text{sign}(g^{\prime}g)=&\text{\rm sign}\left[(g^{\prime}g)\rvert_{V_{\mathcal{T}}}\right]\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in V_{\mathcal{C}}\setminus W_{\mathcal{C}}\\
v\in\text{\rm tv}^{-1}(c)\end{subarray}}\text{sign}\left[\tau_{u_{c},g^{\prime}g(v)}\circ(g^{\prime}g)_{v}\circ\tau_{v,u_{c}}\right]\\
(\text{by }\eqref{firstldell2}\text{ and }\eqref{proptau2b})\quad=&\text{sign}\left(g^{\prime}\rvert_{V_{\mathcal{T}}}\right)\cdot\text{sign}\left(g\rvert_{V_{\mathcal{T}}}\right)\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in V_{\mathcal{C}}\setminus W_{\mathcal{C}}\\
v\in\text{\rm tv}^{-1}(c)\end{subarray}}\Bigl\{\text{sign}\left[\tau_{u_{c},g^{\prime}g(v)}\circ g^{\prime}_{g(v)}\circ\tau_{g(v),u_{c}}\right]\cdot\\
&\qquad\cdot\text{sign}\left[\tau_{u_{c},g(v)}\circ g_{v}\circ\tau_{v,u_{c}}\right]\Bigr\}\\
(\text{by }\eqref{squaresigmap17})\quad=&\text{sign}(g^{\prime})\cdot\text{sign}(g).\end{split}

∎

We denote by
\iota_{G}
the
trivial representation
of the group
G
. In the notation of Definition
6.1
, we have
M^{\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert}=\mbox{\rm Ind}_{K_{\varphi}}^{\text{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})}\iota_{K_{\varphi}}
. Similarly, we set
\widetilde{M}^{\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert}\coloneqq\mbox{\rm Ind}_{K_{\varphi}}^{\text{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})}\text{sign}
.

Proposition 9.3
.

If
\varphi
and
\psi
are equivalent as compositions of
\mathcal{T}
then
\widetilde{M}^{\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert}
and
\widetilde{M}^{\lvert\psi^{-1}\rvert}
are equivalent as
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})
-representations.

Proof.

Indeed,
\widetilde{M}^{\lvert\psi^{-1}\rvert}
is the space of all functions
f\colon\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that
f(g\circ k)=\text{sign}(k^{-1})f(g)
, for all
g\in\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})
,
k\in K_{\psi}
, with the left regular representation. From (
6.4
) it follows that the map
T\colon\widetilde{M}^{\lvert\psi^{-1}\rvert}\rightarrow\widetilde{M}^{\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert}
given by
[Tf](g)\coloneqq f(g\circ\tau^{-1})
, for all
g\in\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})
, is an equivalence between the induced representations.
∎

Denote by
\mathsf{I}_{0}(\mathcal{T};\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert,\lvert\psi^{-1}\rvert)
the set of all
[\mathcal{R}]\in\mathsf{I}(\mathcal{T};\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert,\lvert\psi^{-1}\rvert)
which represent equivalence classes of
trivial
common refinements.

Proposition 9.4
.

If
\varphi
and
\psi
are tree compositions of
\mathcal{T}
then

\text{\rm dim}\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\text{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})}\left(M^{\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert},M^{\lvert\psi^{-1}\rvert}\right)=\left\lvert\mathsf{I}(\mathcal{T};\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert,\lvert\psi^{-1}\rvert)\right\rvert

(9.4)

and

\text{\rm dim}\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\text{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})}\left(M^{\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert},\widetilde{M}^{\lvert\psi^{-1}\rvert}\right)=\left\lvert\mathsf{I}_{0}(\mathcal{T};\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert,\lvert\psi^{-1}\rvert)\right\rvert.

(9.5)

Proof.

The identity in (
9.4
) follows from (
9.2
) and Mackey intertwining numbers Theorem (cf.
[
11
, Corollary 11.4.5]
,
[
29
, p. 64]
). Similarly, Mackey formula for the invariants (cf.
[
11
, Corollary 11.4.4]
,
[
29
, p. 164]
) yields

\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\text{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})}\left(M^{\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert},\widetilde{M}^{\lvert\psi^{-1}\rvert}\right)=\bigoplus_{[\mathcal{R}]\in\mathsf{I}(\mathcal{T};\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert,\lvert\psi^{-1}\rvert)}\mbox{\rm Hom}_{H_{[\mathcal{R}]}}\left(\iota,\text{sign}\right),

where
\iota
and
sign
are respectively the restriction of trivial and of the sign representations to
H_{[\mathcal{R}]}
. But if
\mathcal{R}
is not trivial then
H_{[\mathcal{R}]}
contains some
g
with
\text{sign}(g)=-1
; for instance, from the inductive definition (
9.3
) it follows that the sign of the
g
in (
7.16
) is
\,-1
and from Lemma
7.10
and (
9.1
) it follows that this
g
belongs to
H_{[\mathcal{R}]}
. If this is the case then the trivial and the sign representations are not equivalent and therefore the corresponding
Hom
space is trivial. By contrast, if
\mathcal{R}
is trivial then also
H_{[\mathcal{R}]}
is trivial and the corresponding
Hom
space is one-dimensional and this ends the proof of (
9.5
).
∎

Corollary 9.5
.

The
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})
-representations
M^{\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert}
and
\widetilde{M}^{\lvert(\varphi^{t})^{-1}\rvert}
have a unique irreducible common constituent, which is contained in both of them with multiplicity one.

Proof.

From Theorem
8.6
it follows that if
\varphi^{t}
is a transpose of
\varphi
then
\left\lvert\mathsf{I}_{0}(\mathcal{T};\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert,\lvert(\varphi^{t})^{-1}\rvert)\right\rvert=1
and then we may apply (
9.5
).
∎

Definition 9.6
.

In the notation of Definition
4.8
, let
\varphi\in\mathsf{C}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{P})
and denote by
\Lambda
the associated tree of partitions. Then we define
S^{\Lambda}
as the unique irreducible common constituent of
M^{\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert}
and
\widetilde{M}^{\lvert(\varphi^{t})^{-1}\rvert}
(cf. Corollary
9.5
). By Theorem
4.9
(
a
), Proposition
6.2
, Theorem
8.1
(
a
) and Proposition
9.3
,
S^{\Lambda}
does not depend on the choice of
\varphi
and of its transposed
\varphi^{t}
but just on
\Lambda
.

Theorem 9.7
.

The set
\left\{S^{\Lambda}:\Lambda\in\mathsf{Par}(\mathcal{T})\right\}
is a complete list of pairwise, inequivalent representations of the group
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})
.

Proof.

First of all, we prove that if
(\Lambda,\mathcal{Q}),(\Xi,\mathcal{N})\in\mathsf{Par}(\mathcal{T})
are not isomorphic, then
S^{\Lambda}
and
S^{\Xi}
are not equivalent. Indeed, if
\varphi\in\mathsf{C}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{P})
is associated to
(\Lambda,\mathcal{Q})
and
\psi\in\mathsf{C}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{M})
to
(\Xi,\mathcal{N})
, by (
9.5
) and Definition
9.6
, the equivalence of
S^{\Lambda}
and
S^{\Xi}
would lead to
\left\lvert\mathsf{I}_{0}(\mathcal{T};\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert,\lvert(\psi^{t})^{-1}\rvert)\right\rvert\neq 0
, so that, by Theorem
8.5
and recalling that
\psi
is a transpose of
\psi^{t}
,
\varphi<\psi
; by symmetry, we get also
\psi<\varphi
and this is impossible. Therefore the irreducible representations in the statement are pairwise inequivalent and, by Corollary
5.6
, this system is also complete.
∎

From the proof of Theorem
9.7
, we can deduce immediately the following Corollary.

Corollary 9.8
.

If
(\Lambda,\mathcal{Q})
is the tree of partitions of
\varphi
and
S^{\Lambda}
is contained in
M^{\lvert\psi^{-1}\rvert}
then
\varphi\geqq\psi
.

Example 9.9
.

In the notation of Example
3.5
we have:
K_{\text{\rm id}_{\mathcal{T}}}=\{1_{\text{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})}\}
and
K_{\text{\rm tv}_{\mathcal{T}}}=\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})
. Therefore
M^{\lvert\text{\rm id}_{\mathcal{T}}^{-1}\rvert}
is the regular representation of
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})
,
M^{\lvert\text{\rm tv}_{\mathcal{T}}^{-1}\rvert}
coincides with the trivial representation
\iota_{\text{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})}
and
\widetilde{M}^{\lvert\text{\rm tv}_{\mathcal{T}}^{-1}\rvert}
is the sign representation. If we denote by
\Lambda_{\text{\rm id}}
and
\Lambda_{\text{\rm tv}}
the trees of partitions respectively of the identity and trivial composition (see Example
4.7
for the case of a spherically homogeneous tree), taking Example
8.4
into account, we have

S^{\Lambda_{\text{\rm tv}}}\text{ is the trivial representation},\qquad S^{\Lambda_{\text{\rm id}}}\text{ is the sign representation}.

Finally, arguing as in
[
19
, p. 36]
, we can prove that, for every tree composition
\varphi
of
\mathcal{T}
with associated tree of partitions
\Lambda
, we have

S^{\Lambda_{\text{\rm id}}}\otimes M^{\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert}\sim\widetilde{M}^{\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert}\quad\text{ and }\quad S^{\Lambda_{\text{\rm id}}}\otimes S^{\Lambda}\sim S^{\Lambda^{t}}

where
\Lambda^{t}
is the tree of partitions associated to any transpose
\varphi^{t}
of
\varphi
. Indeed, the first equivalence follows from an easy application of
[
11
, Corollary 11.1.17]
; the second equivalence follows from the fact that
S^{\Lambda_{\text{\rm id}}}\otimes S^{\Lambda}
is contained both in
S^{\Lambda_{\text{\rm id}}}\otimes M^{\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert}\sim\widetilde{M}^{\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert}
and in
S^{\Lambda_{\text{\rm id}}}\otimes\widetilde{M}^{\lvert(\varphi^{t})^{-1}\rvert}\sim M^{\lvert(\varphi^{t})^{-1}\rvert}
, so that it coincides with
S^{\Lambda^{t}}
.

Finally, we prove that both Clifford theory and Mackey theory lead to the same parametrization of the irreducible representations of
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})
.

Theorem 9.10
.

With the notation in Theorem
6.7
and Theorem
9.7
,
Z^{\Lambda}
and
S^{\Lambda}
are equivalent
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})
irreducible representations. Moreover, they coincide as multiplicity one subspaces of
M^{\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert}
if
Z^{\Lambda}
is constructed by means of the realization of
\varphi
.

Proof.

First of all, by induction on
H(\mathcal{T})
, we prove that
S^{\Lambda_{\text{\rm id}}}\otimes Z^{\Lambda}\sim Z^{\Lambda^{t}}
; cf. Example
9.9
. For
H(\mathcal{T})=1
it is a well known fact in the representation theory of the symmetric group. Denote by
Z^{\prime}
and
S^{\prime}
the representations in (
6.14
) but constructed by means of
\Lambda^{t}
; recall that, by Theorem
8.1
(
a
),
\Lambda^{t}
has the same realization
\delta
of
\Lambda
, and therefore in the application of (
6.14
) we may use the same group
I
in (
6.5
). After that, for
g\in I
we set

\widetilde{\text{sign}}(g)\coloneqq\prod_{c\in V_{\mathcal{C}}\setminus W_{\mathcal{C}}}\prod\limits_{v\in\text{\rm tv}^{-1}(c)}\text{sign}\left(\tau_{u_{c},g(v)}\circ g_{v}\circ\tau_{v,u_{c}}\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad\overline{\text{sign}}(g)\coloneqq\text{sign}\left(g\rvert_{V_{\mathcal{T}}}\right).

Clearly,
\widetilde{\text{sign}}
is an extension of
sign
from
N
to
I
while
\overline{\text{sign}}
is the inflation of
sign
from
I/N
to
I
. From (
9.3
) it follows that
\mbox{\rm Res}^{\text{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})}_{I}\text{\rm sign }=\widetilde{\text{sign}}\otimes\overline{\text{sign}}
and therefore

\displaystyle\text{\rm sign}\otimes Z^{\Lambda}=

\displaystyle\text{\rm sign }\otimes\mbox{\rm Ind}_{I}^{\text{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})}\left(\widetilde{Z}\otimes\overline{S}\right)

\displaystyle(\text{by }\eqref{irredClifford})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Ind}_{I}^{\text{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})}\left[\left(\mbox{\rm Res}_{I}^{\text{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})}\text{\rm sign}\right)\otimes\left(\widetilde{Z}\otimes\overline{S}\right)\right]

\displaystyle(\text{by }\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{book4}{}{}, \rm Theorem 11.1.16]})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Ind}_{I}^{\text{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})}\left[\left(\widetilde{\text{\rm sign }\otimes Z}\right)\otimes\left(\overline{\text{\rm sign }\otimes S}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Ind}_{I}^{\text{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{T})}\left(Z^{\prime}\otimes S^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle(\text{inductive hypothesis})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z^{\Lambda^{t}}

We deduce that
S^{\Lambda_{\text{\rm id}}}\otimes Z^{\Lambda}
is contained both in
S^{\Lambda_{\text{\rm id}}}\otimes M^{\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert}\sim\widetilde{M}^{\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert}
(cf. Example
9.9
) and in
M^{\lvert(\varphi^{t})^{-1}\rvert}
, so that
S^{\Lambda_{\text{\rm id}}}\otimes Z^{\Lambda}\sim S^{\Lambda^{t}}
and therefore

Z^{\Lambda}\sim S^{\Lambda_{\text{\rm id}}}\otimes S^{\Lambda^{t}}\sim S^{\Lambda}
. Actually,
S^{\Lambda}
and
Z^{\Lambda}
coincide as subspaces of
M^{\lvert\varphi^{-1}\rvert}
, because both have multiplicity one.
∎

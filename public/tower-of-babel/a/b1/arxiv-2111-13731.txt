Splitting rates in QCD plasmas from a non-perturbative determination of the momentum broadening kernel C ⁡ ( q ⟂ ) C(q_{\perp})
Sören Schlichting, Ismail Soudi

Abstract
We exploit a recent non-perturbative determination of the momentum broadening kernel C ⁡ ( b ⊥ ) C(b_{\bot}) in impact parameter space Moore et al. 2021 , to determine the momentum space broadening kernel C ⁡ ( q ⊥ ) C(q_{\bot}) in high-temperature QCD plasmas. We show how to use the non-pertubatively determined kernel C ⁡ ( q ⊥ ) C(q_{\bot}) to compute the medium-induced splitting rates in a QCD plasma of finite size. We compare the resulting in-medium splitting rates to the results obtained with leading-order and next-to-leading order perturbative determinations of C ⁡ ( q ⊥ ) C(q_{\bot}) , as well as with various approximations of the splitting employed in the literature. Generally, we find that the differences in the splitting rates due to the momentum broadening kernel are larger than the errors associated with approximations of the splitting rate.



I
Introduction

One of the clearest signals for the formation of a Quark Gluon Plasma (QGP) in heavy ion collisions is the suppression of the yields of highly energetic particles.
When highly energetic partons or jets traverse the medium, they interact with the constituents of the QGP leading to a loss of energy, commonly referred to as jet quenching
Bjorken 1982
.
While for highly energetic partons only a small fraction of the energy is lost due to elastic interactions with the medium, the interactions of hard partons with the medium constituents also induce additional “medium-induced“ radiation
Gyulassy and Wang 1994
;
Baier et al. 1995
;
Zakharov 1996
, which provides the dominant energy loss mechanism for highly energetic particles. Studies of medium-induced radiation in QCD plasmas date back to the early determinations of the Landau-Pomeranchuk-Migdal effect
Landau and Pomeranchuk 1953
;
Migdal 1956
in QCD
Baier et al. 1997a
;
Baier et al. 1997b
;
Zakharov 1996
;
Zakharov 1997
, and include determinations of the radiation rate in QCD plasmas of infinite
Arnold et al. 2002
and finite spatial extent
Caron-Huot and Gale 2010
;
Andres et al. 2020
. Beyond the development of different theoretical formalism to determine the in-medium splitting rates
Gyulassy and Wang 1994
;
Zakharov 1996
;
Zakharov 1997
;
Zakharov 1998
;
Baier et al. 1995
;
Baier et al. 1997a
;
Baier et al. 1997b
;
Wiedemann and Gyulassy 1999
;
Gyulassy et al. 2000a
;
Salgado and Wiedemann 2003
;
Blaizot et al. 2013
;
Apolinário et al. 2015
;
Sievert et al. 2019
;
Andres et al. 2020
, there have also been ongoing efforts to construct suitable approximations of the medium-induced splitting rates in various different limits
Gyulassy et al. 2000a
;
Wiedemann and Gyulassy 1999
;
Gyulassy et al. 2000b
;
Mehtar-Tani 2019
;
Mehtar-Tani and Tywoniuk 2020
;
Barata and Mehtar-Tani 2020
;
Andres et al. 2021
.

In all the different formalisms to obtain splittings rates, the interaction of hard partons with the medium are described using the rate of transverse momentum broadening

\displaystyle C(q_{\perp})

\displaystyle\equiv(2\pi)^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}{\mathrm{d}^{2}\bm{q}_{\perp}}\;,

(1)

which defines the rate to exchange transverse momentum
\bm{q}_{\perp}
with the QCD medium. While perturbative broadening kernels have been used to successfully predict observables (see e.g.
Mehtar-Tani et al. 2013
;
Cao and Wang 2020
for recent reviews) it is known that due to the infrared (IR) problem, the perturbative expansion breaks down in the IR regime even at small coupling
Linde 1980
. Nevertheless, effective field theories coupled with lattice calculations can be used to evade the IR problem
Braaten and Nieto 1995
. Specifically, for the momentum broadening kernel
C(q_{\perp})
, one can define the zero-subtracted Fourier transform

\displaystyle C(b_{\perp})

\displaystyle\equiv\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\bm{q}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\left(1-e^{i\bm{q}_{\perp}\cdot{{\bm{b}}_{\perp}}}\right)C(q_{\perp})\;.

(2)

which can be defined non-perturbatively in terms of certain light-like Wilson loops
Casalderrey-Solana and Teaney 2007
. For temperatures well above the critical temperature
T_{c}
these light-like Wilson loops can be recast in the dimensionally reduced long-distance effective theory for QCD, 3D Electrostatic QCD (EQCD)
Caron-Huot 2009
.
In an earlier study
Moore et al. 2021
we showed how the short distance behavior of the broadening kernel
C_{\mathrm{EQCD}}({{\bm{b}}_{\perp}})
determined from non-perturbative lattice simulations of EQCD
Panero et al. 2014
;
D’Onofrio et al. 2014
;
Moore and Schlusser 2019
;
Moore and Schlusser 2020a
, can be matched to obtain a non-perturbative determination of
C_{\mathrm{QCD}}({{\bm{b}}_{\perp}})
in QCD at all scales. In this study the broadening kernel was computed in impact parameter
({{\bm{b}}_{\perp}})
space, which is favorable for the calculation of medium-induced radiation in an infinite medium
Arnold et al. 2003a
. However, in order to extend the framework to a QCD medium of finite size,
it is highly favorable to work in momentum
(\bm{q}_{\perp})
space
Caron-Huot and Gale 2010
.

Central objective of this paper is to employ the non-perturbative determination of
C_{\mathrm{QCD}}({{\bm{b}}_{\perp}})
in
Moore et al. 2021
, to determine the medium-induced radiation rates in the physically relevant situation of a finite medium. First, we Fourier transform the non-perturbative kernel to momentum space in Sec.
II
.
Subsequently in Sec.
III
, we recapitulate the formalism of
Caron-Huot and Gale 2010
to obtain the splitting rates in a finite medium and introduce the opacity expansion
Wiedemann 2000
;
Gyulassy et al. 2000a
together with an expansion around the multiple soft scattering limit
Mehtar-Tani 2019
;
Mehtar-Tani and Tywoniuk 2020
;
Barata and Mehtar-Tani 2020
and a resummed opacity expansion method
Andres et al. 2021
. Numerical results for the splitting rates are presented in Sec.
IV
, where we compare our calculations with the results obtained using leading order (LO) and next-to-leading order (NLO) perturbative determinations of the broadening kernels, and investigate the quality of various approximations. We conclude with a summary of our important findings in Sec.
V
.

We now proceed to perform the Fourier transform of the non-perturbative broadening kernel
Moore et al. 2021
back to momentum space.
In principle, the inverse Fourier transform is standard and should be straightforward to compute. However, due to the spareness of the data points, the divergent behavior of the kernel at large impact parameter and the highly oscillatory nature of the integrals involved, performing the numerical integral is actually rather challenging. To avoid these difficulties, we found that it is best to Fourier transform the coordinate space derivative
\frac{\mathrm{d}C({{\bm{b}}_{\perp}})}{\mathrm{d}b_{\perp}}
of the momentum broadening kernel. Using Eq. (
2
), one can write

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}C({{\bm{b}}_{\perp}})}{\mathrm{d}b_{\perp}}=

\displaystyle\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}q_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-i\bm{q}_{\perp}\hskip-1.0pt\cdot\,{{\bm{b}}_{\perp}}}\left[\frac{i\bm{q}_{\perp}\hskip-1.0pt\cdot\,{{\bm{b}}_{\perp}}}{b_{\perp}}C(q_{\perp})\right]\,.

(8)

Exploiting the fact that
C(\bm{q}_{\perp})
does not depend on the direction
{{\bm{b}}_{\perp}}/|b_{\perp}|
then leads to the following Hankel transform,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}C({{\bm{b}}_{\perp}})}{\mathrm{d}b_{\perp}}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}q_{\perp}}{(2\pi)}~q_{\perp}J_{1}(b_{\perp}\,q_{\perp})\left[q_{\perp}C(q_{\perp})\right]\;,

(9)

where
J_{1}(x)
is the Bessel function of the first kind of order
1
. By use of the inverse Hankel transform, one then obtains the broadening kernel in momentum space as

\displaystyle C(q_{\perp})=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{q_{\perp}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}b_{\perp}~b_{\perp}J_{1}(b_{\perp}\,q_{\perp})\frac{\mathrm{d}C(b_{\perp})}{\mathrm{d}b_{\perp}}\,.

(10)

While the integral in Eq. (
10
) is still highly oscillatory, it can be computed numerically as long as the integrand is sufficiently well behaved at the integration boundaries. In order to ensure numerical convergence, we therefore subtract the leading asymptotic behavior at large distances

\frac{\mathrm{d}C^{\rm IR}({{\bm{b}}_{\perp}})}{\mathrm{d}b_{\perp}}=\sigma_{\mathrm{EQCD}}\;.

(11)

and only perform a numerical Hankel transform of the remainder

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}b_{\perp}}\Delta C_{\rm QCD}({{\bm{b}}_{\perp}})=\frac{\mathrm{d}C_{\rm QCD}({{\bm{b}}_{\perp}})}{\mathrm{d}b_{\perp}}-\frac{\mathrm{d}C^{\rm IR}_{\rm QCD}({{\bm{b}}_{\perp}})}{\mathrm{d}b_{\perp}}\;,

(12)

which by construction vanishes for large impact parameters. By numerically performing the Hankel transform

\displaystyle\Delta C_{\rm QCD}(q_{\perp})=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{q_{\perp}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}b_{\perp}~b_{\perp}J_{1}(b_{\perp}\,q_{\perp})\frac{d}{db_{\perp}}\Delta C_{\rm QCD}(b_{\perp})\,,

(13)

and supplying it with the analytic result for the Hankel transform of
C^{\rm IR}({{\bm{b}}_{\perp}})
, given by (c.f. Appendix.
A

C^{\rm IR}_{\rm QCD}(q_{\perp})=\frac{2\pi}{q_{\perp}^{3}}\sigma_{\mathrm{EQCD}}\;.

(14)

we obtain the full momentum broadening kernel as

\displaystyle C_{\rm QCD}(q_{\perp})=\Delta C_{\rm QCD}(q_{\perp})+C^{\rm IR}_{\rm QCD}(q_{\perp})\;,

(15)

We note that, due to the fact that the Bessel function is highly oscillatory for large momenta
q_{\perp}
, sufficient care should be taken in performing the integral, and we describe the procedure we employ in Appendix.
A

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{\infty}dt~G(t,{\bm{q}};t_{1},{\bm{p}})

\displaystyle\frac{1}{-i\delta E({\bm{q}})}\left[G(\infty,{\bm{q}};t_{1},{\bm{p}})-G(t_{1},{\bm{q}};t_{1},{\bm{p}})\right]

III.0.1
Expressing the rate using wave function

By introducing the wave function

\displaystyle\vec{\psi}({\bm{p}},t,t_{1})=\int_{{\bm{q}}}\frac{i{\bm{q}}}{\delta E({\bm{q}})}\Gamma_{3}(t)\circ G(t,{\bm{q}};t_{1},{\bm{p}})\;,

(35)

we may further compactify the expression for the rate as follows

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma^{a}_{bc}}{dz}(P,z,t)

Exploiting the linearity of the evolution equation (

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{t_{1}}-i\delta E({\bm{p}})-\Gamma_{3}(t_{1})\circ\right]\vec{\psi}({\bm{p}},t,t_{1})=

\displaystyle 0\;,

which needs to be solved backward in time

for

\displaystyle\vec{\psi}({\bm{p}},t,t_{1})=

\displaystyle\int_{\bm{q}}\frac{i{\bm{q}}}{\delta E({\bm{q}})}\Gamma_{3}\circ(2\pi)^{2}\delta^{2}({\bm{p}}-{\bm{q}})\;,

(38)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma_{3}(t)\circ\frac{i{\bm{p}}}{\delta E({\bm{p}})}\;,

(39)

While the above re-arrangements can always be performed, we will in the following consider the radiative emission rates in a static QCD plasma, where

We proceed to factor out the physical scales by defining the dimensionless variables

\displaystyle=\tilde{{\bm{p}}}^{2}+(1-z)\frac{m_{z}^{2}}{m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}}+z\frac{m_{1-z}^{2}}{m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}}-z(1-z)\frac{m_{1}^{2}}{m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}}\;.

(42)

By factoring out the parametric dependencies of the broadening kernel as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{3}=g^{2}T~\tilde{\Gamma}_{3}\;.

Now, expressing the wave function as

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{\Delta\tilde{t}}+\delta\tilde{E}(\tilde{{\bm{p}}})+\lambda\,\tilde{\Gamma}_{3}(t)\circ\right]\vec{\tilde{\psi}}(\tilde{\bm{p}},\Delta\tilde{t})=

\displaystyle 0\;,

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma^{a}_{bc}}{dz}(P,z,\tilde{t})=

\displaystyle\frac{g^{4}TP^{a}_{bc}(z)}{\pi}{\rm Re}~\int_{0}^{\tilde{t}}d{\Delta\tilde{t}}~\int_{\tilde{{\bm{p}}}}\tilde{{\bm{p}}}\cdot\vec{\tilde{\psi}}(\tilde{\bm{p}},\Delta\tilde{t})\;.

\displaystyle 0\;,

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma^{a}_{bc}}{dz}=

\displaystyle\frac{g^{4}TP^{a}_{bc}(z)}{\pi}{\rm Re}~\int_{0}^{\tilde{t}}d{\Delta\tilde{t}}~\int_{\tilde{{\bm{p}}}}e^{-i\delta\tilde{E}(\tilde{p})\Delta\tilde{t}}\tilde{\psi}_{I}(\tilde{p},\Delta\tilde{t})\;,

Opacity expansion

Simplification to the rate occur, when the medium is short

2

III.3
Harmonic Oscillator expansion

When the typical energy evolved in the radiation is much larger than the medium temperature
(Pz(1-z)\gg T)
, the formation time is large so that multiple soft scatterings have to be resummed. By treating the multiple soft scatterings in diffusion approximation, the evolution equation for the Green’s function can be re-cast into the form of a harmonic oscillator type equation which can be solved analytically
Arnold 2009
.
Rather than using this approximation only, we will make use of recent calculations which go beyond this simple harmonic oscillator limit by treating the hard scatterings as a perturbative correction on top of the resummed infinitely many soft scatterings
Mehtar-Tani 2019
;
Mehtar-Tani and Tywoniuk 2020
;
Barata and Mehtar-Tani 2020
. Here, we will only compute the first correction, i.e. a single hard scattering in addition to many soft scatterings.

Starting with the short-distance behavior defined in Eq. (
II.1
), one can introduce a scale
Q^{2}
to evaluate the logarithm and separate it as follows

\displaystyle\bar{C}(b_{\perp})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{R}}C(b_{\perp})

(64)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g^{4}T^{3}}{16\pi}\mathcal{N}b_{\perp}^{2}\ln\left(\frac{4Q^{2}}{\xi m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}}\right)+\frac{g^{4}T^{3}}{16\pi}\mathcal{N}b_{\perp}^{2}\ln\left(\frac{1}{Q^{2}b_{\perp}^{2}}\right)

(65)

where
\xi=4\frac{g^{4}T^{2}}{m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}}e^{-4\pi\frac{\hat{q}_{0}}{C_{R}g^{4}T^{3}\mathcal{N}}}\simeq 0.1702
for
T=500
MeV as denoted in Tab.
1

65

\displaystyle\bar{C}^{HO}(b_{\perp})=

\displaystyle\frac{g^{4}T^{3}}{16\pi}\mathcal{N}b_{\perp}^{2}\ln\left(\frac{4Q^{2}}{\xi m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}}\right)\;,

(66)

which is used to calculate the Green’s function subject to multiple soft-scatterings, while the remainder is treated perturbatively.
Instead of using only the short-distance limit, i.e.
\bar{C}^{\rm pert}(b_{\perp})=\frac{g^{4}T^{3}}{16\pi}\mathcal{N}b_{\perp}^{2}\ln\left(\frac{1}{Q^{2}b_{\perp}^{2}}\right)

\bar{C}^{\rm pert}(b_{\perp})=\bar{C}(b_{\perp})-\bar{C}^{HO}(b_{\perp})\;,

(67)

where we use the full definition of
\bar{C}(b_{\perp})

The radiation spectrum will also be separate to the sum of the HO and the first correction

\displaystyle\frac{dI^{NLO}}{dz}(P,z,t)=\frac{dI^{HO}}{dz}(P,z,t)+\frac{dI^{(1)}}{dz}(P,z,t)\;,

\displaystyle Q^{2}(P,z)=

\displaystyle\sqrt{Pz(1-z)\hat{q}_{\rm eff}(Q^{2})}\;,

\displaystyle\hat{q}_{\rm eff}(Q^{2})=

\displaystyle\frac{g^{4}T^{3}}{4\pi}\mathcal{N}\left[C_{1}+C_{z}z^{2}+C_{1-z}(1-z)^{2}\right]

V
Conclusion

Building on the determination of the collisional broadening kernel
C_{\rm QCD}({{\bm{b}}_{\perp}})
in
Moore et al. 2021
, we performed a Fourier transform of
C_{\rm QCD}({{\bm{b}}_{\perp}})
, to determine the non-perturbative broadening kernel
C_{\rm QCD}(\bm{q}_{\perp})
in momentum space in order to compute radiative emissions rates in a QCD medium of finite extent.

We presented results for the in-medium splitting rates obtained with the non-perturbative collision kernel and compared them to the results obtained with leading and next-to-leading order perturbative collision kernels, as well as with different approximations of the in-medium splitting rates, which are commonly employed in the literature. While approximations to the splitting rate calculation are quite effective in reproducing the rate within their respective range of validity, differences between the LO kernel, which is usually used in phenomenological studies of jet quenching, and the non-perturbative kernel can easily be on the order of
30\%
. We conclude that, while for sophisticated numerical simulations one can reconstruct the full rate to obtain precise results, for (semi-)analytical calculations a combination of the resummed opacity and NLO-HO rates is likely sufficient, as theoretical improvements mostly rely on the precise knowledge of the collisional broadening kernel.

With regards to the phenomenological applications of our work, we note that the collisional broadening kernel and in-medium splitting rates obtained in this paper can be incorporated into a study of jet quenching either using a kinetic approach
Mehtar-Tani and Schlichting 2018
;
Adhya et al. 2020
;
Schlichting and Soudi 2021b
or with a MonteCarlo simulations
Caucal et al. 2019
;
Chen et al. 2018
;
Putschke et al. 2019
;
Schenke et al. 2009
. Similarly, one could also utilize the same broadening kernel to include non-perturbative contributions to the elastic scatterings. We finally note that a recent study using the same EQCD setting obtained non-perturbative contributions to the thermal masses
Moore and Schlusser 2020b
and it would be interesting to investigate their impact on the rate calculation specifically and jet quenching in general.

Appendix A
Hankel transformation

Below we provide details of the procedure followed to obtain the Hankel transform of the momentum broadening kernel.

A.1
Numerical implementation of the Hankel transformation

To perform the numerical integration in Eq. (
13
), we split the integral using the zeros
\{x_{i}\}
of the Bessel function
(J_{1}(x_{i})=0)
as follows

\displaystyle\Delta C^{\rm NP}(q_{\perp})=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{q_{\perp}}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\int_{x_{i}/q_{\perp}}^{x_{i+1}/q_{\perp}}\!\mathrm{d}b_{\perp}~b_{\perp}J_{1}(b_{\perp}\,q_{\perp})

\displaystyle\times\frac{d}{db_{\perp}}\Delta C^{\rm NP}(b_{\perp})\,.

(80)

Let us define the series

\displaystyle A_{n}=\int_{x_{i}/q_{\perp}}^{x_{i+1}/q_{\perp}}\!\mathrm{d}b_{\perp}~b_{\perp}J_{1}(b_{\perp}\,q_{\perp})\frac{d}{db_{\perp}}\Delta C^{\rm NP}(b_{\perp})\;.

(81)

The Hankel transform is then given by the sum
A=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}
, however, this sum is slowly convergent. The convergence can be accelerated using a method known as Shanks transformation
Shanks 1955
, where one defines the series

\displaystyle A=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}S(A_{n})\;,

(82)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}A_{n+1}-\frac{(A_{n+1}-A_{n})^{2}}{(A_{n+1}-A_{n})-(A_{n}-A_{n-1})}\;.

(83)

The result is then obtained by truncating the sum when we obtain convergence up to a small tolerance threshold, i.e. when
\left|\frac{S(A_{n+1})}{S(A_{n})}-1\right|\leq 10^{-8}
.

A.2
Transformation of the short-distance behavior

At short-distances the broadening kernel follows a similar behavior to the LO kernel, which is the short-distance limit of

\displaystyle C(b_{\perp})=\frac{C_{\mathrm{R}}g^{4}T^{3}\mathcal{N}}{8\pi m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}}[\gamma_{e}+\log(m_{\mathrm{D}}b_{\perp}/2)+K_{0}(m_{\mathrm{D}}b_{\perp})]\;.

(84)

In momentum space this broadening kernel is given by
Ghiglieri et al. 2013

\displaystyle C(q_{\perp})=\frac{C_{\mathrm{R}}g^{4}T^{3}\mathcal{N}}{m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}}\frac{m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}}{q_{\perp}^{2}(q_{\perp}^{2}+m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2})}\;.

(85)

Since we are only interested in the leading UV behavior, provided by the
b^{2}\log(b)
term, the non-perturbative broadening kernel will follow the same behavior as the LO kernel

\displaystyle C^{\rm UV}(q_{\perp})=\frac{C_{\mathrm{R}}g^{4}T^{3}\mathcal{N}}{8\pi}\frac{8\pi}{q_{\perp}^{4}}\;.

(86)

A.3
Transformation of the long-distance behavior

We proceed to transform the long-distance behavior given in Eq. (
II.1
).
Let us first consider the Hankel transformation of the linear function

\displaystyle C(b_{\perp})=A+Bb_{\perp}\;.

(87)

The constant term leads to a delta function
\delta^{(2)}(q_{\perp})
in momentum space which can be discarded, and the linear term will lead to

\displaystyle C(q_{\perp})=B\frac{2\pi}{q_{\perp}^{3}}\;.

(88)

Note that in order to verify this identity, it is actually easier to compute the following inverse transform

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{2}q_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{2\pi}{q_{\perp}^{3}}(1-e^{iq_{\perp}\cdot{{\bm{b}}_{\perp}}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dy~\frac{1}{(x^{2}+y^{2})^{3/2}}(1-e^{ixb_{\perp}})\;,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx~\frac{2}{x^{2}}(1-\cos{xb_{\perp}})=b_{\perp}\;.

(89)

On the Frobenius fields of abelian varieties over number fields
Ashay A. Burungale, Haruzo Hida, Shilin Lai

Abstract
. Let A A be a non-CM simple abelian variety over a number field K K . For a place v v of K K such that A A has good reduction at v v , let F ⁡ ( A , v ) F(A,v) denote the Frobenius field generated by the corresponding Frobenius eigenvalues. 1.
Introduction

For an abelian variety over a number field and a place of good reduction, a basic invariant is the Frobenius field generated by the corresponding Frobenius eigenvalues. In this paper we study its connection with the arithmetic of the abelian variety.

For a CM abelian variety, in view of the CM theory of Shimura–Taniyama–Weil, the Frobenius fields are contained in a fixed number field and equal to it for a set of places of Dirichlet density one. A natural question: to explore the upper Dirichlet density of the set of places at which the Frobenius field of a non-CM abelian variety coincides with a given number field up to an isomorphism. The question has been studied in the literature in various low-dimensional cases, and the primary goal of this paper is to consider the general case via a uniform approach.

We show that the density is zero under a mild connected-ness hypothesis (see Theorem
1.1
). Moreover, assuming the GRH, we provide a power saving upper bound on the size of the set of places with bounded norm
at which the Frobenius field of a non-CM abelian variety coincides with a given number field (see Theorem
1.3
).

About the proof

Our approach is based on the compatible system of Galois representations associated to the abelian variety and some group theory.
To begin, we recast the problem in terms of (a variant of) Frobenius tori in the algebraic monodromy group of the abelian variety.
A volume computation of conjugacy classes arising from these tori and large Galois image results for
A
are keys of the proof.

We now describe the strategy in more detail.
To begin, for primes
p
which split completely in
M
, we show that if
v\in S_{A,M}
, then the image of
\mathrm{Frob}_{v}
in the associated mod
p
monodromy group lies in a
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-rational Borel subgroup. Then we give a volume upper bound for the union of all such subgroups (cf. Corollary
3.4
), perhaps of independent interest. On the other hand, since
A
is non-CM, the associated Galois representations have large image, thanks to the work of Wintenberger
[
35
]
and Hui–Larsen
[
14
]
(cf. Theorem
2.5
). In light of the image lower bound and the volume upper bound, the Chebotarev density theorem implies that
\mathrm{ud}(S_{A,M})
is bounded above by a constant
c
less than
1
, which is independent of the prime
p
. This deduction relies on basic properties of reductive groups, several of which require the group to be connected.

Next, we synthesise the analysis at different primes via the product Galois representation
\prod\rho_{A,p}
. Ideally, we expect

(1.1)

\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\big(\prod_{p}\rho_{A,p}\big)=\prod_{p}\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits(\rho_{A,p}),

which implies: for different choices of

1.1

As for Theorem
1.3

•

To refine exponents naturally appearing in the analysis, we make the following improvements:

•

Instead of the

4.3.2.
Background on effective Chebotarev

For any number field
k
, let
n_{k}=[k:\mathbb{Q}]
and
\Delta_{k}
be its absolute discriminant. Given a finite extension
l/k
, let
\mathcal{D}(l/k)
denote the set of rational primes that lie below the ramified finite places of
l/k
. Define

M(l/k)=\left|G\right|\Delta_{k}^{\frac{1}{n_{k}}}\prod_{p\in\mathcal{D}(l/k)}p.

Suppose that
l/k
is Galois with Galois group
G
. Let
C\subseteq G
be a union of conjugacy classes. For any positive real number
X
, define

\pi(X,C,l/k):=|\{\mathfrak{p}\,|\,\mathfrak{p}\text{ is unramified in }l,\ \mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}\in C,\ \mathbf{N}_{k/\mathbb{Q}}\mathfrak{p}\leq X\}|.

We are interested in the error term

R(X,C,l/k):=\pi(X,C,l/k)-\frac{\left|C\right|}{\left|G\right|}\mathrm{Li}(X).

The following result is equation (
20_{\mathrm{R}}
) in
[
30
, §2.4]
, which is an improvement to Lagarias–Odlyzko’s original result
[
19
]
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Assume the GRH holds for the Artin
L
-functions of irreducible representations of
l/k
, then

\left|R(X,C,l/k)\right|\ll\left|C\right|n_{k}X^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\log X+\log M(l/k)\big).

Assuming the AHC, Murty–Murty–Saradha obtained the following improvement
[
25
, Corollary 3.10]
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of
G
such that the GRH and AHC hold for the Artin
L
-functions of the irreducible characters of
H
. Suppose
H
meets every conjugacy class contained in
C
, then

R(X,C,l/k)\ll\left|C\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}[G:H]^{\frac{1}{2}}n_{k}X^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\log X+\log M(l/k)\big).

If
\left|C\right|
is of roughly the same size as
\left|G\right|
, then this estimate saves a factor of
\left|H\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}
compared to Theorem
4.7
. We therefore want to take
\left|H\right|
as large as possible.

Remark 4.9
.

This theorem is a corollary of a precise result
[
25
, Proposition 3.9]
. While it suffices for our purpose, we expect that a finer analysis of centralizers in finite reductive groups can give a better exponent.

In view of Lemma

Now,

\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_{i})
. Therefore,

\frac{\log|\Delta_{F(A,v)}|}{[F(A,v):\mathbb{Q}]}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\frac{\log|\Delta_{\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_{i})}|}{[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_{i}):\mathbb{Q}]}.

Let
q=\mathbf{N}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}v
, then all the roots of
P(T)
have complex absolute value
q^{\frac{1}{2}}
, so

\log\left|\Delta_{\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)}\right|\leq\log\left|\mathop{\rm disc}\nolimits P(T)\right|=\sum_{i\neq j}\log\left|\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}\right|\leq((2g)^{2}-2g)\log\big(2q^{\frac{1}{2}}\big).

Combining the above two inequalities gives the claim.
∎

Theorem 4.19
.

Assume the GRH. For any
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\left|S_{A,M}(X)\right|\ll_{A,K,\varepsilon}X^{1-\frac{1}{4\gamma+2}+\varepsilon},

where
\gamma=\frac{1}{4}(3\dim\mathbf{G}^{\mathrm{ss}}-\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits\mathbf{G}^{\mathrm{ss}})
from Proposition
4.14
.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
, then Proposition
4.5
gives an equality

\left|S_{A,M}(X)\right|=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\bigg|E_{\mathtt{d}}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathtt{d}}^{(i)})-\bigcup_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}^{(\mathtt{d})}}E_{\mathtt{d}p}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathtt{d}}^{(i)}\times\mathcal{C}_{p}^{(i)})\bigg|.

Fix an index
i\in\mathcal{I}
. We will apply Theorem
4.6
to the corresponding term in the above sum. Take
{\mathcal{A}}=E_{\infty}(g_{i}H_{\infty})
, sieving primes
{\mathcal{P}}^{(\mathtt{d})}
, and excluded subsets
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}=E_{\mathtt{d}p}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathtt{d}}^{(i)}\times\mathcal{C}_{p}^{(i)})
. By combining Propositions
4.13
and
4.14
, we get the estimate

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{A}}_{d}|=\bigg|\bigcap_{p|d}E_{\mathtt{d}p}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathtt{d}}^{(i)}\times\mathcal{C}_{p}^{(i)})\bigg|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{I}\right|}\alpha_{\mathtt{d}}^{(i)}\beta_{d}^{(i)}\mathrm{Li}(X)+O_{A,K}\big((2C)^{\omega(d)}d^{\gamma}X^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log X+\log d)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{I}\right|}\alpha_{\mathtt{d}}^{(i)}\beta_{d}^{(i)}\mathrm{Li}(X)+O_{A,K,\varepsilon}\big(d^{\gamma+\varepsilon}X^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log X+\log d)\big).

By definition, the function
d\mapsto\beta_{d}^{(i)}
is multiplicative. Let
p\in{\mathcal{P}}^{(\mathtt{d})}
, then

\beta_{p}^{(i)}=\frac{|\mathcal{C}_{p}\cap g_{i,p}H_{p}|}{\left|H_{p}\right|}=1-\frac{|\mathcal{B}_{p}\cap g_{i,p}H_{p}|}{\left|H_{p}\right|}.

Recall that
H_{p}
contains
I_{p}
, so each
g_{i,p}H_{p}
is a union of cosets of
I_{p}
. Over each such coset, Proposition
3.6
gives uniform upper and lower bounds for its intersection with
\mathcal{B}_{p}
. It follows that
\beta_{p}^{(i)}
is uniformly bounded away from 0 and 1, and the bounds only depend on the dimension
g
.

Having verified all of the hypotheses, Theorem
4.6
gives the estimate

\bigg|E_{\mathtt{d}}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathtt{d}}^{(i)})-\bigcup_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}^{(\mathtt{d})}}E_{\mathtt{d}p}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathtt{d}}^{(i)}\times\mathcal{C}_{p}^{(i)})\bigg|\\
\ll_{A,K,\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{I}\right|}\alpha_{\mathtt{d}}^{(i)}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{Li}(X)}{\pi_{{\mathcal{P}}^{(\mathtt{d})}}(z)}+\left(\frac{z^{1+\varepsilon}}{\pi_{{\mathcal{P}}^{(\mathtt{d})}}(z)}\right)^{2}X^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1},d_{2}\leq z\\
d_{1},d_{2}\in{\mathcal{R}}^{(\mathtt{d})}\end{subarray}}\frac{[d_{1},d_{2}]^{\gamma+\varepsilon}}{d_{1}d_{2}}(\log X+\log[d_{1},d_{2}]).

In the sum for the second term, use the trivial bound
[d_{1},d_{2}]\leq d_{1}d_{2}
and extend the sum to all pairs
d_{1},d_{2}\leq z
. In particular,
\log[d_{1},d_{2}]\leq\log(z^{2})
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1},d_{2}\leq z\\
d_{1},d_{2}\in{\mathcal{R}}^{(\mathtt{d})}\end{subarray}}\frac{[d_{1},d_{2}]^{\gamma+\varepsilon}}{d_{1}d_{2}}(\log X+\log[d_{1},d_{2}])

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{d_{1},d_{2}\leq z}(d_{1}d_{2})^{\gamma-1+\varepsilon}(\log X+2\log z)

\displaystyle=(\log X+2\log z)\Big(\sum_{d\leq z}d^{\gamma-1+\varepsilon}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll_{\gamma}z^{2\gamma+2\varepsilon}(\log X+\log z).

which gives

(4.6)

\bigg|E_{\mathtt{d}}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathtt{d}}^{(i)})-\bigcup_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}^{(\mathtt{d})}}E_{\mathtt{d}p}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathtt{d}}^{(i)}\times\mathcal{C}_{p}^{(i)})\bigg|\ll_{A,K,\varepsilon}\frac{X}{\pi_{{\mathcal{P}}^{(\mathtt{d})}}(z)}+\left(\frac{z^{1+\varepsilon}}{\pi_{{\mathcal{P}}^{(\mathtt{d})}}(z)}\right)^{2}X^{\frac{1}{2}}z^{2\gamma+2\varepsilon}\log(Xz).

It remains to bound
\pi_{{\mathcal{P}}^{(\mathtt{d})}}(z)
from below. By definition,

\pi_{{\mathcal{P}}^{(\mathtt{d})}}(z)=\pi(z,\{1\},M/\mathbb{Q})-O_{A,K}(1)

By the effective Chebotarev theorem (Theorem
4.7
, also cf.
[
30
, Equation (14
R
)]
), we have

\pi(z,\{1\},M/\mathbb{Q})=\frac{1}{[M:\mathbb{Q}]}\mathrm{Li}(z)+O_{g}\big(z^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log z+\log|\Delta_{M}|)\big).

We may assume
\log|\Delta_{M}|\ll_{g}\log X
, since otherwise
S_{A,M}(X)=\emptyset
by Lemma
4.18
. Now choose
z=X^{\beta}
, where
\beta=\frac{1}{4\gamma+2}
, then

\pi_{{\mathcal{P}}^{(\mathtt{d})}}(z)=\frac{1}{[M:\mathbb{Q}]}\mathrm{Li}(X^{\beta})+O_{g,\beta}(X^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\log X)-O_{A,K}(1)\gg_{A,K,\varepsilon}X^{\beta-\varepsilon}.

Substituting this in equation
4.6
and summing over all
i\in\mathcal{I}
gives the desired result.
∎

Remark 4.20
.

We only needed the error term in the effective Chebotarev theorem on average. The situation is analogous to the Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem, except that it deals with the family of abelian extensions
\{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})/\mathbb{Q}\}
, and we have a family of non-abelian Lie-extensions
\{K_{d}/K\}
. There are some works when the Galois group is fixed (cf.
[
26
]
), but we are not aware of any work in our setting.

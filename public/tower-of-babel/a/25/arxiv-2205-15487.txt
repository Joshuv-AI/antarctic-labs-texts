Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce multi-layer quiver and show how to construct an ( n + 1 ) (n+1) -slice algebras of infinite type from an n n -slice algebra of infinite type using the bound quivers. This leads to constructing ( n + 1 ) (n+1) -slice algebras of infinite type as matrix algebra and as tensor algebra of an n n -slice algebra and equivalences of their module categories as the module categories of diagram of some quiver of type A ~ n + 1 \widetilde{A}_{n+1} .

2.1.
Algebras and bound quivers

Throughout this paper,
k
is a fixed field.
A bound quiver
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1},\rho)
is a triple of the vertex set
Q_{0}
, the arrow set
Q_{1}
and a relation set
\rho
which is a set of
k
-linear combinations of paths in
Q
.
In this paper the vertex set
Q_{0}
may be infinite and the arrow set
Q_{1}
is assumed to be locally finite.
The arrow set
Q_{1}
is usually defined with two maps
\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t}
from
Q_{1}
to
Q_{0}
to assign an arrow
\alpha
its starting vertex
\mathfrak{s}(\alpha)
and its terminating vertex
\mathfrak{t}(\alpha)
.
We also write
\mathfrak{s}(p)
for the starting vertex and
\mathfrak{t}(p)
for its terminating vertex of a path
p
in
Q
.

A bound quiver is related to an algebra over
Q
, that is, the quotient algebra
kQ/(\rho)
of the path algebra
kQ
modulo the ideal
(\rho)
generated by the relation set
\rho
.
A path
p
in
Q
is called a

if its image in
kQ/(\rho)
is non-zero.

Let
\Lambda=\Lambda_{0}+\Lambda_{1}+\cdots
be a graded algebra over
k
with
\Lambda_{0}
direct sum of copies of
k
such that
\Lambda
is generated by
\Lambda_{0}
and
\Lambda_{1}
.
Such algebra is determined by a bound quiver
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1},\rho)

[
13
]
.

Let
S=\Lambda_{0}=\bigoplus\limits_{i\in Q_{0}}k_{i}
, with
k_{i}\simeq k
as algebras, and let
e_{i}
be the image of the identity of
k
under the canonical embedding of the
k_{i}
into
S
.
Then
\{e_{i}\mid i\in Q_{0}\}
is a complete set of orthogonal primitive idempotents in
S
and
V=\Lambda_{1}=\bigoplus\limits_{i,j\in Q_{0}}e_{j}\Lambda_{1}e_{i}
as
S
-
S
-bimodules.
Fix a basis
Q_{1}^{ij}
of
e_{j}\Lambda_{1}e_{i}
for any pair
i,j\in Q_{0}
, take the elements of
Q_{1}^{ij}
as arrows from
i
to
j
, and let
Q_{1}=\cup_{(i,j)\in Q_{0}\times Q_{0}}Q_{1}^{ij}.

Let
Q_{t}
be the set of the paths of length
t
in
Q
and let
kQ_{t}
be the
k
-space spanned by
Q_{t}
, then
\Lambda_{t}=kQ_{t}/(\rho)_{t}
, here
(\rho)_{t}
is the subspace of
(\rho)
spanned by the linear combination of the paths of length
t
.
The relation set
\rho
is a set of linear combinations of paths of length
\geq 2
.
We assume that it is normalized such that each element in
\rho
is a linear combination of paths starting at the same vertex and ending at the same vertex, that is
\rho=\cup_{i,j\in Q_{0}}\rho(i,j)
, where
\rho(i,j)
is the set of relations which are a linearly combination of paths from
i
to
j
.
We also assume that these paths are of the same length since we deal with graded algebra.

Let
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1},\rho)
be an bound quiver and
\Lambda
be the algebra given by the bound quiver
Q
.
The quiver

is called

contains no oriented cycle, the algebra

is called

is acyclic.

Q
is called

if relations are quadratics, that is

.
If

is a quadratic bound quiver, the quotient algebra

The quadratic dual quiver

has the same vertex set
Q_{0}=Q_{0}
as

, has the dual basis
Q_{1}
of
Q_{1}
as arrow set, and has a basis
\rho^{\perp}
in the orthogonal subspace

of

is identified with

itself (see
[
14
]
).
The algebra

is called the

of
\Lambda
.

2.2.
The

-properly-graded quiver and nicely-graded quiver

A bound quiver

is called

if all the maximal bound paths in

A sequence

of paths in

\mathfrak{t}(p_{2h+1})=\mathfrak{t}(p_{2h})

, conventionally

A walk

Define

, and call it

(i,w)
-grade
of

Q

to
j
along the walk

We have the following obvious result.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any two vertices

in
Q
, we have

(1)

The
(i,w)
-grade of

are independent of the walk

if and only if for any cyclic walk

on

.

(2)

If

is connected and

Q
, then for any vertex
j

Q

\mathfrak{u}_{i,w}(j)
is independent of
w
if and only if for any cyclic walk with

,
\mathfrak{u}_{i,w^{\prime}}(i)=0
.

A quiver with

for any vertex
i
and any cyclic walk starting at

is called a

in this paper.
In this case, if

is connected, for any

, write
\mathfrak{u}_{i}(j)=\mathfrak{u}_{i,w}(j)
for any walk from

to

Clearly, we have that if

\mathfrak{u}_{i,w}(i)=0
for any vertex
i
in the walk

The following result follows from Proposition
2.1
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let

be a connected quiver.
Then

is nicely-graded if and only if there is a map

from

to

such that

So our definition of nicely-graded quiver coincide with the one given in

]

Clearly, a nicely-graded quiver is acyclic.
A bound quiver

is called an

-properly-graded.

So
(p^{\prime},\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{s}(p^{\prime})),\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{s}(p^{\prime}))+r+1)(\gamma_{\mathfrak{t}(q)},\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{t}(q)),\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{t}(q))+r)(q,\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{s}(q)),\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{s}(q))+r)(p^{\prime\prime},\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{s}(p^{\prime\prime})),\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{s}(p^{\prime\prime}))+r)

is a bound path of length
n+1
in
\widehat{Q}
.
If for some
q\in\mathcal{M}
,
\hat{p}=(p^{\prime},\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{s}(p^{\prime})),\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{s}(p^{\prime}))+n+1)(\beta_{q},\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{t}(q)),\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{t}(q)))(p^{\prime\prime},\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{s}(p^{\prime\prime})),\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{s}(p^{\prime\prime})))
,
then there is a path
q^{\prime}
in
Q
, such that
l(p^{\prime\prime}q^{\prime}p^{\prime})=n
and
q^{*}(p^{\prime\prime}q^{\prime}p^{\prime})\neq 0
, thus
(p^{\prime},\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{s}(p^{\prime})),\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{s}(p^{\prime}))+n+1)(\beta_{q},\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{t}(q)),\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{t}(q)))(p^{\prime\prime},\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{s}(p^{\prime\prime})),\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{s}(p^{\prime\prime})))(q^{\prime},\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{s}(q^{\prime})),\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{s}(q^{\prime})))
is a bound path of length
n+1
in
\widehat{Q}
.

This proves that
\widehat{Q}
is an
(n+1)
-properly-graded quiver.

For a cyclic walk
\hat{w}
with a vertex
(i,\mathfrak{u}(i),\mathfrak{u}(i)+r)
in
\widehat{Q}
, its projection on the floor
Q[0]
is a cyclic walk
w(0)
in
Q[0]
with a vertex
i
.

\hat{w}
is obtained from
w(0)
by breaking
w(0)
into pieces and put these pieces in different floors, and then connect them with arrows of type
(\gamma_{i},\mathfrak{u}(i),\mathfrak{u}(i)+r)
.
The arrows of type
(\gamma_{j},\mathfrak{u}(j),\mathfrak{u}(j)+h)
appear in pairs to connect the pieces into a cyclic walk.
For each such pair of arrows of type
(\gamma_{j},\mathfrak{u}(j),\mathfrak{u}(j)+h)
, when we go from a vertex
(i,\mathfrak{u}(i),\mathfrak{u}(i)+r)

along the walk
\hat{w}
to itself, the arrows are passed in opposite direction.
So
\mathfrak{u}_{(i,\mathfrak{u}(i),\mathfrak{u}(i)+r),\hat{w}}(i,\mathfrak{u}(i),\mathfrak{u}(i)+r)=0
.

Thus
\widehat{Q}
is nicely-graded and so
\widehat{Q}
is an
(n+1)
-nicely-graded quiver.
∎

Given an

\widetilde{Q}
be its returning arrow quiver.
Since

have the same length
n+1

We are going to show that

is nicely-graded when

is nicely-graded, so it is isomorphic to the connected components of

By Lemma 2.1 of
[
18
]

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\overline{Q}
be a stable
n
-translation quiver.

(1)

If
p_{t}
and
p_{t+1}
are two bound paths with
\mathfrak{t}(p_{t})=\mathfrak{t}(p_{t+1})=\bar{i}
, then there are bound paths
p^{\prime}_{t+1}
and
p^{\prime}_{t+2}
with
\mathfrak{s}(p^{\prime}_{t+1})=\mathfrak{s}(p^{\prime}_{t+2})=\tau\bar{i}

and
\mathfrak{t}(p^{\prime}_{t+2})=\mathfrak{s}(p_{t+1})

If
p_{t}
and
p_{t+1}
are two bound paths with
\mathfrak{s}(p_{t})=\mathfrak{s}(p_{t+1})=\bar{j}

l(p_{t})+l(p^{\prime}_{t+1})=n+1=l(p_{t+1})+l(p^{\prime}_{t+2}).

Lemma 4.2

Let
\overline{Q}

n
-translation quiver.

(1)

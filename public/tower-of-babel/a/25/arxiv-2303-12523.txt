Variations on a result of Granville about polynomial invariance
Labib Haddad and Charles Helou

Abstract




Abstract

In 2004, Andrew Granville proved the following: A polynomial in variables
x,y,z,
invariant under the map
\varphi:z\to-(x+y+z)
is a polynomial in
b=z(x+y+z)
. We tell, curtly, its history and then give a variation on this theme.

History

In March 2004, the second author received an inquiry from a colleague about the factorization of some symmetric polynomials, with power monomials, in two or three variables. There was a statement about a factorization in
\mathbb{Z}[x,y]
of the polynomial
F_{p}(x,y)=(x+y)^{p}-x^{p}-y^{p}
, for a prime number
p>3
, namely

F_{p}(x,y)=pxy(x+y)\left(x^{2}+xy+y^{2}\right)^{e}C_{p}(x,y),

where
e=1\ \text{or}\ 2
according to whether
p\equiv-1\ \text{or}\ 1\pmod{6}
, respectively, and
C_{p}(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}[x,y]
is a so-called Cauchy polynomial, described as a well-known elementary result. In fact,
F_{p}(x,y)
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
p
, which, upon division by
y^{p}
, gives a polynomial
K_{p}(t)
, of degree
p-1
in
t=\frac{x}{y}
, namely

\frac{F_{p}(x,y)}{y^{p}}=\left(\frac{x}{y}+1\right)^{p}-\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)^{p}-1=(t+1)^{p}-t^{p}-1=\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\binom{p}{k}t^{k}=K_{p}(t).

Clearly,
K_{p}(0)=K_{p}(-1)=0
and
K^{\prime}_{p}(0)=p,\ K^{\prime}_{p}(-1)=-p\neq 0
, so
0
and
1
are simple roots of
K_{p}
, so that
K_{p}
is divisible by
t(t+1)
in
\mathbb{Z}[t]
, and also by
p
since the binomial coefficients
\binom{p}{k}
are divisible by
p
for
1\leq k\leq p-1
. Moreover, letting
\zeta_{3}=\exp\left(\frac{2i\pi}{3}\right)
be a primitive cubic root of unity, we have

K_{p}(\zeta_{3})=(\zeta_{3}+1)^{p}-\zeta_{3}^{p}-1=-(1+\zeta_{3}^{p}+\zeta_{3}^{2p})=0

and

K^{\prime}_{p}(\zeta_{3})=p((\zeta_{3}+1)^{p-1}-\zeta_{3}^{p-1})=p\zeta_{3}^{p-1}(\zeta_{3}^{p-1}-1),

so that
K^{\prime}_{p}(\zeta_{3})=0
if and only if
p\equiv 1\pmod{3}
, i.e.,
p\equiv 1\pmod{6}
. Thus
\zeta_{3}
is a root of
K_{p}(t)
, and it’s a double root if and only if
p\equiv 1\pmod{6}
, i.e.,
K_{p}(t)
is divisible by
t^{2}+t+1
if
p\equiv-1\pmod{6}
and by
(t^{2}+t+1)^{2}
if
p\equiv 1\pmod{6}
, in
\mathbb{Z}[t]
. Thus

K_{p}(t)=pt(t+1)(t^{2}+t+1)^{e}C_{p}(t),

where

e=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if $p\equiv-1\pmod{6}$}\\
2,&\text{if $p\equiv 1\pmod{6}$},\end{cases}

and
C_{p}(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
, of degree
p-3-2e
, is called a Cauchy polynomial, because it was introduced by A. L. Cauchy in 1839 and reconsidered in 1841 (
[
2
,
3
,
5
]
). The Cauchy polynomials were studied by D. Mirimanoff in 1903 (
[
4
]
), who conjectured their irreducibility over
\mathbb{Q}
, a still open conjecture. For more details, one can consult the 1989 paper of G. Terjanian (
[
6
]
). This factorization of
K_{p}(t)
implies the factorization of
F_{p}(x,y)
cited above. To be accurate,
C_{p}(t)
should be
C_{p}(t,1)
in the factorization of
K_{p}(t)
, but by “abuse of notation”, we identified the two.

This leads to introduce the polynomial

E_{2}=\frac{(x+y)^{p}-x^{p}-y^{p}}{pxy(x+y)}=\left(x^{2}+xy+y^{2}\right)^{e}C_{p}(x,y),

which lies in
Z[x,y]
, and, by analogy, in the case of 3 variables
x,y,z
, the polynomial

E_{3}=\frac{(x+y+z)^{p}-x^{p}-y^{p}-z^{p}}{p(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)},

which lies in
Z[x,y,z]
. Indeed, letting

H_{p}(x,y,z)=(x+y+z)^{p}-x^{p}-y^{p}-z^{p}=\sum\Sb 0\leq j,k,l<p\\j+k+l=p\frac{p!}{j!k!l!}x^{j}y^{k}z^{l},

it is easily seen that all the coefficients of
H_{p}
are divisible by
p
, and that
H_{p}(x,-x,z)=H_{p}(x,y,-y)=H_{p}(x,y,-x)=0
, so that
H_{p}
is divisible by
p
,
x+y
,
y+z
and
z+x
, in
Z[x,y,z]
, which are pairwise coprime. Therefore
H_{p}
is divisible by their product
p(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)
, i.e.,
E_{3}
has its coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover, it is noted that

E_{3}=E_{2}+Q(x,y,z),

where
Q\in z\mathbb{Z}[x,y,z]
. Indeed,

\displaystyle E_{3}-E_{2}=\frac{(x+y+z)^{p}-x^{p}-y^{p}-z^{p}}{p(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)}-\frac{(x+y)^{p}-x^{p}-y^{p}}{pxy(x+y)}=

\displaystyle=\frac{xy((x+y+z)^{p}-x^{p}-y^{p}-z^{p})-(y+z)(z+x)((x+y)^{p}-x^{p}-y^{p})}{pxy(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)}=

\displaystyle=\frac{xy\left((\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\binom{p}{k}(x+y)^{k}z^{p-k}+(x+y)^{p}-x^{p}-y^{p}\right)-(xy+xz+yz+z^{2})((x+y)^{p}-x^{p}-y^{p})}{pxy(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)}=

\displaystyle=\frac{xy\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\binom{p}{k}(x+y)^{k}z^{p-k}-z(x+y+z)((x+y)^{p}-x^{p}-y^{p})}{pxy(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)},

which is a polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[x,y,z]
all of whose terms are multiples of
z
.

Then, came the main question. Setting
b=z(x+y+z)
, my correspondent made the observation that

E_{3}=a_{n}+b(a_{n-1}+b(a_{n-2}+\cdots+b(a_{2}+b(a_{1}+b))\cdots))=b^{n}+a_{1}b^{n-1}+\cdots a_{n-2}b^{2}+a_{n-1}b+a_{n},

where
n=\frac{p-3}{2}
and all the
a_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}[x,y]
, with
a_{n}=E_{2}
. This amounts to stating that
E_{3}
is a polynomial in
b
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}[x,y]
, and asking for a proof.

In April 2004, a second message came, extending the first one. In it, he makes the observation that
a_{n}+xya_{n-1}=(x+y)^{p-3}
, and he conjectures that, for
0\leq m\leq n-1
, where
n=\frac{p-3}{2}
,

a_{n-m}+xya_{n-m-1}=a_{n-m}^{(1)}(x+y)^{p-2m-3},

where
a_{k}^{(j)}
is the coefficient of the
j
-th term in the polynomial
a_{k}
. Then it is claimed that a result of E. Catalan, cited in L. E. Dickson’s
History of the Theory of Numbers, vol. II
, gives the coefficients of
C_{p}(x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{p-3-2e}c_{k}x^{p-3-2e-k}y^{k}
, namely (with a correction)

c_{k}=\frac{1}{p}\left(\binom{p-1}{k}-(-1)^{k}\right).

He then claims to deduce that
a_{n-1}^{(1)}=a_{n}^{(2)}
and
a_{n}^{(2)}=\frac{p-3}{2}
. All computations were done, using
Mathematica
.

In May 2004, a third message came saying that he sent his query to Andrew Granville, who replied that any polynomial
F(x,y,z)
which is invariant under the map

\varphi:z\mapsto-(x+y+z)
(as is
E_{3}
) can be written as a polynomial in
b=z(x+y+z)
, providing a simple proof by induction on the degree of
F
. Indeed, setting
a_{0}=F(x,y,0)
, Granville notes that
a_{0}
and
F(x,y,z)-a_{0}
are invariant under
\varphi
. But
F(x,y,z)-a_{0}
is divisible by
z
, and, applying
\varphi
, it is also divisible by
-(x+y+z)
, and therefore it is divisible by
b
. Noting that
b
is also invariant under
\varphi
, he deduces that
G(x,y,z)=\frac{F(x,y,z)-a_{0}}{b}
is invariant under
\phi
, and has lower degree than
F
. Thus, by the induction hypothesis,
G
can be written as a polynomial in
b
, and therefore so can
F
.

It is this brilliantly simple argument of Granville that we attempt to generalize below.

Remarks.
The polynomials denoted by
E_{2}
and
E_{3}
will henceforth be designated by
E_{2,p}
and
E_{3,p}
to emphasize their dependence on
p
.
Note that

\displaystyle\frac{(x+y)^{p}-(x^{p}+y^{p})}{x+y}

\displaystyle=(x+y)^{p-1}-\frac{x^{p}+y^{p}}{x+y}=\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\binom{p-1}{k}x^{k}y^{p-1-k}-\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{k}x^{k}y^{p-1-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\left(\binom{p-1}{k}-(-1)^{k}\right)x^{k}y^{p-1-k}=\sum_{k=1}^{p-2}\left(\binom{p-1}{k}-(-1)^{k}\right)x^{k}y^{p-1-k},

since
\binom{p-1}{k}-(-1)^{k}=0
for
k=0
and
k=p-1
. Hence

\displaystyle E_{2,p}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{(x+y)^{p}-(x^{p}+y^{p})}{pxy(x+y)}=\frac{1}{pxy}\sum_{k=1}^{p-2}\left(\binom{p-1}{k}-(-1)^{k}\right)x^{k}y^{p-1-k}=

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{p-2}\frac{\binom{p-1}{k}-(-1)^{k}}{p}x^{k-1}y^{p-k-2},

where the coefficients
\frac{\binom{p-1}{k}-(-1)^{k}}{p}
are integers, for
1\leq k\leq p-2
, since

\binom{p-1}{k}=\frac{(p-1)(p-2)\cdots(p-k)}{k!}\equiv\frac{(-1)^{k}k!}{k!}\equiv(-1)^{k}\pmod{p}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle H_{p}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=(x+y+z)^{p}-(x^{p}+y^{p}+z^{p})=\sum_{m=0}^{p}\binom{p}{m}(x+y)^{m}z^{p-m}-(x^{p}+y^{p}+z^{p})=

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{p}\binom{p}{m}(x+y)^{m}z^{p-m}-(x^{p}+y^{p}),

so that

\displaystyle E_{3,p}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{H_{p}(x,y,z)}{p(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)}=\frac{1}{p(y+z)(z+x)}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{p}\binom{p}{m}(x+y)^{m-1}z^{p-m}-\frac{x^{p}+y^{p}}{x+y}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p(y+z)(z+x)}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{p}\binom{p}{m}(x+y)^{m-1}z^{p-m}-\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{k}x^{k}y^{p-1-k}\right)=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p(y+z)(z+x)}\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\left(\binom{p}{k+1}(x+y)^{k}z^{p-k-1}-(-1)^{k}x^{k}y^{p-k-1}\right),

the last equality being obtained by setting
k=m-1
in the first of the two sums.

Furthermore, multiplying and dividing by
(x+y)
, distributing in the numerator, and setting
l=k+1
, we get

\displaystyle E_{3,p}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{p}\binom{p}{l}(x+y)^{l}z^{p-l}+\sum_{l=1}^{p}(-1)^{l}x^{l}y^{p-l}-\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{k}x^{k}y^{p-k}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)}\left(\sum_{l=0}^{p}\binom{p}{l}(x+y)^{l}z^{p-l}-x^{p}-y^{p}-z^{p})\right).

Expanding the binomial
(x+y)^{l}
, and replacing all binomial coefficients by their defining expressions, then reversing the order of summations, and factoring out the common factors, we get

E_{3,p}(x,y,z)=\frac{1}{p(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)}\left(p!z^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{p}\frac{1}{k!}\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)^{k}\sum_{l=k}^{p}\frac{1}{(p-l)!(l-k)!}\left(\frac{y}{z}\right)^{l}-x^{p}-y^{p}-z^{p}\right).

Hence the following two expressions for
E_{3,p}
, established above,

\displaystyle E_{3,p}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p(y+z)(z+x)}\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\left(\binom{p}{k+1}(x+y)^{k}z^{p-k-1}-(-1)^{k}x^{k}y^{p-k-1}\right)=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)}\left(p!z^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{p}\frac{1}{k!}\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)^{k}\sum_{l=k}^{p}\frac{1}{(p-l)!(l-k)!}\left(\frac{y}{z}\right)^{l}-x^{p}-y^{p}-z^{p}\right).

Similarly, exchanging
x+y
and
y+z
, then
x+y
and
x+z
, gives, respectively,

\displaystyle E_{3,p}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p(x+y)(x+z)}\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\left(\binom{p}{k+1}(y+z)^{k}x^{p-k-1}-(-1)^{k}y^{k}z^{p-k-1}\right)=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)}\left(p!x^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{p}\frac{1}{k!}\left(\frac{y}{z}\right)^{k}\sum_{l=k}^{p}\frac{1}{(p-l)!(l-k)!}\left(\frac{z}{x}\right)^{l}-x^{p}-y^{p}-z^{p}\right),

and

\displaystyle E_{3,p}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p(x+y)(y+z)}\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\left(\binom{p}{k+1}(x+z)^{k}y^{p-k-1}-(-1)^{k}x^{k}z^{p-k-1}\right)=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)}\left(p!y^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{p}\frac{1}{k!}\left(\frac{z}{x}\right)^{k}\sum_{l=k}^{p}\frac{1}{(p-l)!(l-k)!}\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)^{l}-x^{p}-y^{p}-z^{p}\right).

The variation

Let

Take

[formula]

This transformation

B=\{F\in A:TF=F\}.

[formula]

Of course,

[formula]

How can the elements of

What is needed for

The case

We shall say that polynomial

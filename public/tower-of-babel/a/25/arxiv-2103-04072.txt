On a Conjecture Concerning the Approximates of Complete Elliptic Integral of the First Kind by Inverse Hyperbolic Tangent
Song-Liang Qiu, Qi Bao, Xiao-Yan Ma, Hong-Biao Jiang

Abstract




Abstract:
. Let
\mathscr{K}
be the complete elliptic integral of the first kind. In this paper, the authors prove that the function
r\mapsto r^{-2}\{[\log(2\mathscr{K}(r)/\pi)]/\log(({\rm{arth}}r)/r)-3/4\}
is strictly increasing from
(0,1)
onto
(1/320,1/4)
, so that
[({\rm{arth}}r)/r]^{3/4+r^{2}/320}<2\mathscr{K}(r)/\pi<[({\rm{arth}}r)/r]^{3/4+r^{2}/4}
for
r\in(0,1)
, in which all the coefficients of the exponents of the two bounds are best possible, thus proving a conjecture raised by Alzer and Qiu to be true, and giving better bounds of
\mathscr{K}(r)
than those they conjectured and put in an open problem. Some other analytic properties of the complete elliptic integrals, including other kind of approximates for
\mathscr{K}(r)
, are obtained, too.

Keywords
: The complete elliptic integrals; inverse hyperbolic tangent; monotonicity; convexity; inequality

MSC (2010):
33C75, 33E05, 33F05

Introduction

Throughout this paper, we let
\mathbb{N}
(
{\rm{arth}}
) denote the set of positive integers (the inverse hyperbolic tangent, respectively),
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, and let
r^{\thinspace\prime}=\sqrt{1-r^{2}}
for each
r\in[0,1]
. For
r\in(0,1)
,
the complete elliptic integrals of the first and second kinds
are defined as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathscr{K}=\mathscr{K}(r)=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\left(1-r^{2}\sin^{2}t\right)^{-1/2}dt,\\
\mathscr{K}^{\prime}=\mathscr{K}^{\prime}(r)=\mathscr{K}(r^{\thinspace\prime}),\\
\mathscr{K}(0)=\pi/2,\mathscr{K}(1)=\infty,\end{cases}

(1.1)

and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathscr{E}=\mathscr{E}(r)=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\left(1-r^{2}\sin^{2}t\right)^{1/2}dt,\\
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}=\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(r)=\mathscr{E}(r^{\thinspace\prime}),\\
\mathscr{E}(0)=\pi/2,\mathscr{E}(1)=1,\end{cases}

(1.2)

respectively, which have wide applications and are the particular cases of the Gaussian hypergeometric function

\displaystyle F(a,b;c;x)={}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{n}(b)_{n}}{(c)_{n}n!}x^{n}~(|x|<1)

(1.3)

for complex numbers
a,b
and
c
with
c\neq 0,-1,-2,...
, where
(a)_{n}=a(a+1)(a+2)\cdots(a+n-1)=\Gamma(a+n)/\Gamma(a)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
(a)_{0}=1
, while
\Gamma(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{x-1}e^{-t}dt
is the Euler gamma function. As a matter of fact, it is well known that

\displaystyle\mathscr{K}(r)=\frac{\pi}{2}F\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2};1;r^{2}\right)\mbox{~and~}\mathscr{E}(r)=\frac{\pi}{2}F\left(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2};1;r^{2}\right)

(1.4)

(cf.
[
4
, 17.3.9–17.3.10]
). Moreover, by
[
4
, 15.1.4]
,

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm{arth}}r}{r}=F\left(\frac{1}{2},1;\frac{3}{2};r^{2}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2n+1}r^{2n}.

(1.5)

One of the topics of studying the properties of the complete elliptic integrals is to approximate
\mathscr{K}(r)
by means of the function
r\mapsto({\rm{arth}}r)/r
. Following this topic, G.D. Anderson et al. proved in
[
8

that the function
r\mapsto r\mathscr{K}(r)^{2}/{\rm{arth}}r
(
r\mapsto r\mathscr{K}(r)/{\rm{arth}}r
) is strictly increasing (decreasing) from
(0,1)
onto
(\pi^{2}/4,\infty)
(
(1,\pi/2)
, respectively), so that

\displaystyle\max\left\{\left(\frac{{\rm{arth}}r}{r}\right)^{1/2},\frac{2}{\pi}\frac{{\rm{arth}}r}{r}\right\}<\frac{2}{\pi}\mathscr{K}(r)<\frac{{\rm{arth}}r}{r}

(1.6)

for
r\in(0,1)
. In
[
3

, Alzer and Qiu improved (

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\frac{{\rm{arth}}r}{r}\right)^{\alpha_{1}}<\mathscr{K}(r)<\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\frac{{\rm{arth}}r}{r}\right)^{\beta_{1}}

(1.7)

with best possible exponents
\alpha_{1}=3/4
and

3

Open Problem 1.1
.

What is the largest (smallest) constant

(
\beta^{\ast}
, respectively) such that the double inequality

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\frac{{\rm{arth}}r}{r}\right)^{3/4+\alpha^{\ast}r}<\mathscr{K}(r)<\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\frac{{\rm{arth}}r}{r}\right)^{3/4+\beta^{\ast}r}

(1.8)

r\in(0,1)

The double inequality (
1.8

\displaystyle\alpha^{\ast}<\frac{1}{r}\left[G(r)-\frac{3}{4}\right]<\beta^{\ast},\mbox{~ where ~}G(r)=\frac{\log(2\mathscr{K}(r)/\pi)}{\log(({\rm{arth}}r)/r)}

3

Conjecture 1.2

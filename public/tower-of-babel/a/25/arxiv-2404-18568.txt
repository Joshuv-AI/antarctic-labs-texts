Fei Xu, Manting Xie, Meiling Yue

Abstract
In this paper, a novel multigrid method based on Newton iteration is proposed to solve nonlinear eigenvalue problems. Instead of handling the eigenvalue λ \lambda and eigenfunction u u separately, we treat the eigenpair ( λ , u ) (\lambda,u) as one element in a product space ℝ × H 0 1 ​ ( Ω ) \mathbb{R}\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega) . Then in the presented multigrid method, only one discrete linear boundary value problem needs to be solved for each level of the multigrid sequence. Because we avoid solving large-scale nonlinear eigenvalue problems directly, the overall efficiency is significantly improved. The optimal error estimate and linear computational complexity can be derived simultaneously. In addition, we also provide an improved multigrid method coupled with a mixing scheme to further guarantee the convergence and stability of the iteration scheme. More importantly, we prove convergence for the residuals after each iteration step. For nonlinear eigenvalue problems, such theoretical analysis is missing from the existing literatures on the mixing iteration scheme.

Introduction

Solving large-scale nonlinear eigenvalue problems is a basic and challenging task in the field of scientific and engineering computing.
Various practical problems reduce to solve nonlinear eigenvalue problems.
For example, the Schrödinger-Newton equation which has been widely used to model the quantum state reduction
[
19
]
,
the Gross-Pitaevskii equation which describes Bose-Einstein condensates
[
4
]
, and the Kohn-Sham equation
which is used to describe the electronic structure in quantum chemistry
[
10
,
11
]
.
In this paper, we will study a class of nonlinear eigenvalue models with convex energy functional.
It is quite difficult to solve such nonlinear eigenvalue problems whose computational work grows exponentially as the problem size increases.
Additionally, there are far fewer available numerical methods for nonlinear eigenvalue problems than those for the boundary value problems.

In this study, we resort to the multigrid method to improve the solving efficiency for nonlinear eigenvalue problems.
The multigrid method was first proposed by Fedorenko
[
17
]
in 1961. Later, Brandt
[
6
]
demonstrated the efficiency of the multigrid method,
which was then a widely examined subject in the computational mathematics field.
Subsequently, Hackbusch, Xu, and many others began to use the tool of functional analysis to analyze this algorithm
[
18
,
31
,
29
]
,
which made the multigrid method experience rapid development.
As we know, the multigrid method is able to derive an approximate solution possessing the optimal error estimates with
the linear computational complexity.
For eigenvalue problems, the study on the multigrid method is relatively limited.
Xu and Zhou
[
32
]
first designed a two-grid method for the linear eigenvalue problem. This algorithm requires solving a small-scale eigenvalue problem on
a coarse mesh and a large-scale boundary value problem on a fine mesh. When the mesh sizes of the coarse mesh (
H
) and fine mesh (
h
) have an appropriate proportional relation (
H=\sqrt{h}
),
the optimal estimate for the approximate solution can be derived.
Later, Chen and Liu et al.
[
13
]
extended the two-grid method to solve nonlinear eigenvalue problems using specially selected coarse and fine meshes.
However, owing to the strict constrains on the ratio (i.e.,
H=\sqrt{h}
), the two-grid method only performs on two mesh levels and cannot be used in the multigrid sequence.

To solve the nonlinear eigenvalue problems via the multigrid technique, the Newton iteration is adopted.
In this paper, the eigenvalue
\lambda
and
eigenfunction
u
are treated as one element in a product space
\mathbb{R}\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.
Then, the nonlinear eigenvalue equation can be viewed as a special nonlinear equation defined
in
\mathbb{R}\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. Next, based on the Newton iteration technique, we simply solve a linear
boundary value problem in each layer of the multigrid sequence. Solving the large-scale nonlinear eigenvalue problem is avoided in our algorithm. Besides, the involved
linearized boundary value problems can be solved quickly by many mature numerical algorithms, such as the classical multigrid method.
Hence, the presented algorithm can significantly
improve
the solving efficiency for nonlinear eigenvalue problems.
The well-posedness for these linearized boundary value problems and the convergence for our entire algorithm
have
been rigorously
analyzed in this paper.

For the above-mentioned multigrid method, we find that the Newton iteration scheme may fail to converge for some complicated nonlinear eigenvalue models.
In fact, this problem exists widely during the self-consistent field iteration for nonlinear eigenvalue problems.
To derive a convergence result, the mixing scheme (or damping method) is introduced.
The most widely used mixing scheme is Anderson acceleration, which is used to improve the convergence rate for fixed-point iterations.
Anderson acceleration was first designed by D. G. Anderson in 1965 to solve the integral equation
[
2
]
.
Subsequently, this technique was used to solve various models, such as, the self-consistent field iteration in electronic structure computations
[
16
]
,
flow problems
[
25
,
27
]
, molecular interaction
[
28
]
, etc.
Anderson acceleration adopts the mixing scheme to make the fixed-point iterations converge for general equations.
Despite its widespread applications, the first mathematical convergence
result for linear and nonlinear problems did not appear until 2015 in
[
30
]
.
For nonlinear eigenvalue problems, there is still no strictly theoretical analysis.
In general, the mixing scheme is used in fixed-point iterations to accelerate the convergence rate.
Thus, we use the mixing scheme in the Newton iteration scheme to design a more efficient multigrid method.
By treating the nonlinear eigenvalue problem as a special nonlinear equation, we just solve a series of linearized boundary value problems derived from the
Newton iteration scheme defined
on a multigrid space sequence. Next, we mix the approximate solution in each iteration step to generate a more accurate approximation.
More importantly, we can rigorously
prove convergence
for the residuals of the nonlinear eigenvalue problem after a one-time iteration step.
This may provide some inspiration to prove the theoretical conclusions of the self-consistent field iteration for nonlinear eigenvalue problems.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, some preliminaries about the nonlinear eigenvalue problems and the corresponding finite element method are presented.
In Section 3, we use the Newton iteration to solve the nonlinear eigenvalue problems and provide several useful error estimates.
In Section 4, the multigrid method based on Newton iteration is introduced.
In Section 5, we adopt the mixing scheme to provide an improved multigrid algorithm. Numerical experiments are presented in Section 6 to verify the
theoretical results derived in this paper. Some concluding remarks are presented in the last section.

In this paper, we focus on the nonlinear eigenvalue problems arising from the following variational problem:

\displaystyle I=\inf\{E(v),v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\int_{\Omega}v^{2}d\Omega=1\},

(1)

where the energy functional
E
is defined by

\displaystyle E(v)=\dfrac{1}{2}a(v,v)+\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}F(v^{2})d\Omega,

\displaystyle a(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{A}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+Vuv)d\Omega.

To simplify the notation, let us define
f(t)=F^{\prime}(t)

v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)

E^{\prime}(v)=A_{v}v

\displaystyle A_{v}=-{\rm div}(\mathcal{A}\nabla\cdot)+V+f(v^{2}).

(2)

Next, applying the Lagrange multiplier method to (

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}A_{u}u&=&\lambda u,\ \ \text{ in }\Omega,\\
u&=&0,\ \ \ \ \text{ on }\partial\Omega,\\
\int_{\Omega}u^{2}d\Omega&=&1,\end{array}\right.

(3)

\lambda

For future theoretical analysis, we assume that the following conditions hold true (see

•

•

•

\exists\ 0\leq m<2\ \text{and}\ C\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\ \text{s.t.}\ \forall t\geq 0,\ |F^{\prime}(t)|\leq C(1+t^{m})

•

•

\exists\ 1<r\leq 2

\forall a>0,\exists\ C\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\ \text{s.t.}\ \forall\ 0<t_{1}\leq a,\ \forall\ t_{2}\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle|f(t_{2}^{2})t_{2}-f(t_{1}^{2})t_{1}-2f^{\prime}(t_{1}^{2})t_{1}^{2}(t_{2}-t_{1})|\leq C(1+|t_{2}|^{s})|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{r};

•

\exists\ 0\leq q<1\ \text{and}\ C\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\ \text{s.t.}\ \forall t\geq 0,\ |f^{\prime}(t)|+|f^{\prime\prime}(t)t|\leq C(1+t^{q})

Newton iteration for nonlinear eigenvalue problems

To introduce Newton iteration for nonlinear eigenvalue problem (
7

Let us define

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{F}(\lambda,u),v\rangle=(\mathcal{A}\nabla u,\nabla v)+(Vu+f(u^{2})u-\lambda u,v),\quad\forall v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

Theorem 3.2

For any

Obviously,

\displaystyle\eta(0)=\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{(0)},u^{(0)}),(\sigma,\eta)\rangle\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \eta(1)=\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{(1)},u^{(1)}),(\sigma,\eta)\rangle.

(41)

The first order derivative and the second order derivative of

\displaystyle\eta^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}((\lambda^{(0)},u^{(0)})+t\{(\lambda^{(1)},u^{(1)})-(\lambda^{(0)},u^{(0)})\})((\lambda^{(1)},u^{(1)})-(\lambda^{(0)},u^{(0)})),(\sigma,\eta)\big\rangle

(42)

4
Multigrid method for nonlinear eigenvalue problems based on Newton iteration

In this section, a novel multigrid method is proposed for solving the nonlinear eigenvalue problems based on the Newton iteration.
To design the multigrid method, let us construct a nested
sequence of finite element spaces
based on a nested multigrid sequence in the first step.
The sequence of meshes is denoted by
\{\mathcal{T}_{h_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{n}
. For any two consecutive meshes
\mathcal{T}_{h_{k}}
and
\mathcal{T}_{h_{k-1}}(k\geq 2)
,

\mathcal{T}_{h_{k}}
is produced through a one-time uniform refinement form
\mathcal{T}_{h_{k-1}}
. The corresponding mesh sizes satisfy the following conditions:

\displaystyle h_{k}\approx\dfrac{1}{\beta}h_{k-1},\quad k=2,\cdots,n,

(47)

where the refinement index
\beta>1
. Meanwhile, the following approximate relationship holds true:

\displaystyle\delta_{h_{k}}(u)\approx\dfrac{1}{\beta}\delta_{h_{k-1}}(u),\quad k=2,\cdots,n.

(48)

Based on the multigrid sequence
\{\mathcal{T}_{h_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{n}
, we denote the corresponding
sequence of finite element spaces
by:

\displaystyle V_{h_{1}}\subset V_{h_{2}}\subset\cdots\subset V_{h_{n}}\subset H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

(49)

and denote the
sequence of tensor product finite element spaces
by:

\displaystyle X_{h_{1}}\subset X_{h_{2}}\subset\cdots\subset X_{h_{n}}\subset X.

(50)

4.1
One step of the multigrid method

To describe the multigrid method more clearly, we first introduce how to perform one step
of the Newton iteration.

For two consecutive finite element spaces
X_{h_{k}}
and
X_{h_{k+1}}
, assume that we have obtained an approximate solution
(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})\in X_{h_{k}}
,
Algorithm
4.1
shows the procedure for obtaining a new approximate solution
(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}})\in X_{h_{k+1}}
.

Algorithm 4.1

One step of the multigrid method

1.

Assume that we have obtained an approximate solution
(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})\in X_{h_{k}}
.

2.

Solve the linearized equation derived from Newton iteration in the finite element space
X_{h_{k+1}}
:
Find
(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}})\in X_{h_{k+1}}
such that for any
(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\in X_{h_{k+1}}
, there holds

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle

(51)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle+\langle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle.

The equation (
51
) can be solved by the mixed finite element method in the following form: Find
(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}})\in\mathbb{R}\times V_{h_{k+1}}

such that for any
(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\in\mathbb{R}\times V_{h_{k+1}}
, there holds

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}a(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}};u^{h_{k+1}},v_{h_{k+1}})+b(u^{h_{k}};v_{h_{k+1}},\lambda^{h_{k+1}})&=&\langle\mathcal{F}_{u}^{\prime}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})u^{h_{k}},v_{h_{k+1}}\rangle\\

b(u^{h_{k}};u^{h_{k+1}},\mu)&=&-\mu/2-\mu(u^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})/2.\end{array}\right.

In order to simplify the notation, we use the following symbol to denote the above two solving steps:

\displaystyle(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}})=Newton_{-}Iteration(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}},X_{h_{k+1}}).

Next, we can prove that the new approximate solution
(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}})\in X_{h_{k+1}}

derived by Algorithm
4.1
has a better accuracy than
(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})\in X_{h_{k}}
.
The detailed
conclusion is
presented in Theorem
4.1
.

Theorem 4.1

After implementing Algorithm
4.1
, the new approximate solution
(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}})
has the following error estimate

\displaystyle\|(\lambda-\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u-u^{h_{k+1}})\|_{X}\lesssim\delta_{h_{k+1}}(u)+\|(\lambda-\lambda^{h_{k}},u-u^{h_{k}})\|_{X}^{2}.

Proof

We use
(\lambda_{h_{k+1}},u_{h_{k+1}})
to denote the standard finite element solution of (
9
), that is
(\lambda_{h_{k+1}},u_{h_{k+1}})
satisfies

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}\nabla u_{h_{k+1}},\nabla v_{h_{k+1}})+(Vu_{h_{k+1}},v_{h_{k+1}})+(f(u_{h_{k+1}}^{2})u_{h_{k+1}},v_{h_{k+1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{h_{k+1}}(u_{h_{k+1}},v_{h_{k+1}}),\quad\forall v_{h_{k+1}}\in V_{h_{k+1}},

or equivalently

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda_{h_{k+1}},u_{h_{k+1}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle=0,\quad\forall(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\in X_{h_{k+1}}.

(53)

Combining (
51

53

\displaystyle\big\langle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})(\lambda_{h_{k+1}}-\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u_{h_{k+1}}-u^{h_{k+1}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\big\rangle

(54)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\big\rangle+\big\langle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})(\lambda_{h_{k+1}}-\lambda^{h_{k}},u_{h_{k+1}}-u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\big\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda_{h_{k+1}},u_{h_{k+1}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\big\rangle+\big\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\big\rangle

\displaystyle-R((\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),(\lambda_{h_{k+1}},u_{h_{k+1}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})).

Combining (

3.2

\displaystyle\|(\lambda_{h_{k+1}}-\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u_{h_{k+1}}-u^{h_{k+1}})\|_{X}\lesssim\|(\lambda_{h_{k+1}}-\lambda^{h_{k}},u_{h_{k+1}}-u^{h_{k}})\|_{X}^{2}.

(55)

Using the standard error estimates in Lemma
2

\displaystyle\|(\lambda-\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u-u^{h_{k+1}})\|_{X}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|(\lambda-\lambda_{h_{k+1}},u-u_{h_{k+1}})\|_{X}+\|(\lambda_{h_{k+1}}-\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u_{h_{k+1}}-u^{h_{k+1}})\|_{X}

4.2
Multigrid method based on Newton iteration

In this subsection, we introduce the multigrid method to solve the nonlinear eigenvalue problems.
We use the following nested
sequence of finite element spaces
:

\displaystyle V_{h_{1}}\subset V_{h_{2}}\subset\cdots\subset V_{h_{n}}\subset H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

(56)

and

\displaystyle X_{h_{1}}\subset X_{h_{2}}\subset\cdots\subset X_{h_{n}}\subset X.

(57)

The multigrid method is presented in Algorithm
4.2
. The new algorithm requires solving the nonlinear eigenvalue problem directly in the initial
space to obtain an initial value; then performing Algorithm
4.1
in the subsequent spaces. The approximate solution derived from the last space
is used as the initial iterative value in the current space.

Algorithm 4.2

Multigrid method based on Newton iteration

1.

Solve the following nonlinear eigenvalue problem directly in the initial space
V_{h_{1}}
: Find
(\lambda^{h_{1}},u^{h_{1}})\in\mathbb{R}\times V_{h_{1}}
such that
\|u^{h_{1}}\|_{0}=1
and

(\mathcal{A}\nabla u^{h_{1}},\nabla v_{h_{1}})+(Vu^{h_{1}},v_{h_{1}})+(f((u^{h_{1}})^{2})u^{h_{1}},v_{h_{1}})=\lambda^{h_{1}}(u^{h_{1}},v_{h_{1}}),\quad\forall v_{h_{1}}\in V_{h_{1}}.

2.

For
k=1,\cdots,n-1
, obtain a new approximate solution
(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}})\in X_{h_{k+1}}
through

\displaystyle(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}})=Newton_{-}Iteration(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}},X_{h_{k+1}}),

End for.

3.

Finally, we obtain the approximate solution
(\lambda^{h_{n}},u^{h_{n}})\in X_{h_{n}}
.

The error estimate for the final approximate solution
(\lambda^{h_{n}},u^{h_{n}})\in X_{h_{n}}
derived by Algorithm
4.2

is
presented in Theorem
4.2
.

Theorem 4.2

After performing Algorithm
4.2
, the final approximate solution
(\lambda^{h_{n}},u^{h_{n}})\in X_{h_{n}}
satisfies the following error estimate

\displaystyle\|(\lambda-\lambda^{h_{n}},u-u^{h_{n}})\|_{X}\lesssim\delta_{h_{n}}(u).

(58)

Proof

We prove this theorem by the mathematical induction method.
Since we solve the nonlinear eigenvalue problem directly in the initial space, using Lemma
2
leads to

\displaystyle\|(\lambda-{\lambda}^{h_{1}},u-{u}^{h_{1}})\|_{X}\lesssim\delta_{h_{1}}(u).

Assume that (
58
) holds true for
(\lambda^{h_{n-1}},{u}^{h_{n-1}})\in X_{h_{n-1}}
.
According to Theorem
4.1
, we can derive

\displaystyle\|(\lambda-{\lambda}^{h_{n}},u-{u}^{h_{n}})\|_{X}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\delta_{h_{n}}(u)+\|(\lambda-\lambda^{h_{n-1}},u-u^{h_{n-1}})\|_{X}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\delta_{h_{n}}(u)+\delta_{h_{n-1}}^{2}(u)

4.3
Work estimate of Algorithm

In this subsection, we end Section 4 by estimating the computational work of Algorithm

N_{k}
to denote the dimensions of

5
Multigrid method for nonlinear eigenvalue problems based on mixing scheme

In this section, an improved multigrid method is designed.
The motivation is that when solving the nonlinear equation (
18
) by Newton iteration, we may encounter
the non-convergence issue for some complicated models. This issue is the same as that of
the classical self-consistent field iteration for the nonlinear eigenvalue problems.
In order to overcome this difficulty, the mixing theme (damping method) is introduced to solve
the nonlinear eigenvalue problems.
The most widely used mixing scheme is Anderson acceleration
[
2
]
, which is used to improve the convergence rate for fixed-point iterations.
Although Anderson acceleration was widely used for solving various models, the first mathematical convergence
result for linear and nonlinear problems did not appear until 2015 in
[
30
]
.
For nonlinear eigenvalue problems, there is still no strictly theoretical analysis.
In general, the mixing scheme is used in fixed-point iterations to accelerate the convergence rate.
In this section, we use the mixing scheme in Newton iteration to design an improved multigrid method for nonlinear eigenvalue problems.
Most importantly, we can prove that the norm of the residual decreases in each step of the Newton iteration.
This may provide some inspiration to prove the theoretical conclusions for the
more general mixing scheme for nonlinear eigenvalue problems.

Assume that we have obtained an approximate solution
(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})\in X_{h_{k}}
, we introduce a
novel iteration step in Algorithm
5.1
to obtain a better approximation
(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}})\in X_{h_{k+1}}

on the basis of Newton iteration and the mixing scheme.

Algorithm 5.1

One step of the mixing scheme

1.

Given a parameter
\theta_{k+1}\in(0,1)
and an approximate solution
(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})\in X_{h_{k}}
.

2.

Solve the linearized equation derived from Newton iteration in the finite element space
X_{h_{k+1}}
:
Find
(\widehat{\lambda}^{h_{k+1}},\widehat{u}^{h_{k+1}})\in X_{h_{k+1}}
such that for any
(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\in X_{h_{k+1}}
, there holds

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})(\widehat{\lambda}^{h_{k+1}},\widehat{u}^{h_{k+1}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle

(59)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle+\langle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle.

The equation (
59
) can be solved by the mixed finite element method in the following form: Find
(\widehat{\lambda}^{h_{k+1}},\widehat{u}^{h_{k+1}})\in\mathbb{R}\times V_{h_{k+1}}

such that for any
(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\in\mathbb{R}\times V_{h_{k+1}}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}a(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}};\widehat{u}^{h_{k+1}},v_{h_{k+1}})+b(u^{h_{k}};v_{h_{k+1}},\widehat{\lambda}^{h_{k+1}})&=&\langle\mathcal{F}_{u}^{\prime}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})u^{h_{k}},v_{h_{k+1}}\rangle\\
&&-\langle\mathcal{F}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),v_{h_{k+1}}\rangle-\lambda^{h_{k}}(u^{h_{k}},v_{h_{k+1}}),\\

Set

\displaystyle(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}})=(1-\theta_{k+1})(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})+\theta_{k+1}(\widehat{\lambda}^{h_{k+1}},\widehat{u}^{h_{k+1}}).

Next, we can prove that the value of the residual
|\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle|

monotonically decreases after performing Algorithm
5.1

Theorem 5.1

Assume that we have obtained an approximate solution
(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})
, then, there exists a sufficiently small

(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}})

has the following
convergence

\displaystyle|\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle|\leq(1-\frac{\theta_{k+1}}{2})|\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle|,\ \ \forall(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\in X_{h_{k+1}}.

Proof

From the following mixing scheme

\displaystyle(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}})=(1-\theta_{k+1})(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})+\theta_{k+1}(\widehat{\lambda}^{h_{k+1}},\widehat{u}^{h_{k+1}}),

used in Algorithm
5.1
, we can derive

\displaystyle(\lambda^{h_{k+1}}-\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k+1}}-u^{h_{k}})=\theta_{k+1}(\widehat{\lambda}^{h_{k+1}}-\lambda^{h_{k}},\widehat{u}^{h_{k+1}}-u^{h_{k}}).

(61)

Combining (
61
) and the mean value theorem leads to

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle-\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle

(62)

\displaystyle-\langle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})(\lambda^{h_{k+1}}-\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k+1}}-u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\widetilde{\lambda}^{h_{k}},\widetilde{u}^{h_{k}})(\lambda^{h_{k+1}}-\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k+1}}-u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})(\lambda^{h_{k+1}}-\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k+1}}-u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta_{k+1}\langle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\widetilde{\lambda}^{h_{k}},\widetilde{u}^{h_{k}})(\widehat{\lambda}^{h_{k+1}}-\lambda^{h_{k}},\widehat{u}^{h_{k+1}}-u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle

\displaystyle-\theta_{k+1}\langle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})(\widehat{\lambda}^{h_{k+1}}-\lambda^{h_{k}},\widehat{u}^{h_{k+1}}-u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle,

where
(\widetilde{\lambda}^{h_{k}},\widetilde{u}^{h_{k}})=(\alpha\lambda^{h_{k+1}}+(1-\alpha)\lambda^{h_{k}},\alpha u^{h_{k+1}}+(1-\alpha)u^{h_{k}})
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

From (
61
), we know that when
\theta_{k+1}\rightarrow 0
, there holds

\displaystyle(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}})\rightarrow(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),

and thus

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\lambda}^{h_{k}},\widetilde{u}^{h_{k}})\rightarrow(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),

which also indicates

\displaystyle\big\langle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\widetilde{\lambda}^{h_{k}},\widetilde{u}^{h_{k}})(\widehat{\lambda}^{h_{k+1}}-\lambda^{h_{k}},\widehat{u}^{h_{k+1}}-u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\big\rangle

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\big\langle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})(\widehat{\lambda}^{h_{k+1}}-\lambda^{h_{k}},\widehat{u}^{h_{k+1}}-u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\big\rangle.

Hence, we can choose a sufficiently small
\theta_{k+1}
, such that

\displaystyle\big|\langle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\widetilde{\lambda}^{h_{k}},\widetilde{u}^{h_{k}})(\widehat{\lambda}^{h_{k+1}}-\lambda^{h_{k}},\widehat{u}^{h_{k+1}}-u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle

(63)

\displaystyle-\langle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})(\widehat{\lambda}^{h_{k+1}}-\lambda^{h_{k}},\widehat{u}^{h_{k+1}}-u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big|\langle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})(\widehat{\lambda}^{h_{k+1}}-\lambda^{h_{k}},\widehat{u}^{h_{k+1}}-u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big|\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle\big|.

Based on (
62
) and (
63
), we can derive

\displaystyle\big|\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle-\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle

(64)

\displaystyle-\langle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})(\lambda^{h_{k+1}}-\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k+1}}-u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\theta_{k+1}}{2}|\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle\big|.

Besides, using (
59
) and (
61
), we can also derive

\displaystyle\big|\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle-\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle

(65)

\displaystyle-\langle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})(\lambda^{h_{k+1}}-\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k+1}}-u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big|\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle-\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle

\displaystyle-\theta_{k+1}\langle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})(\widehat{\lambda}^{h_{k+1}}-\lambda^{h_{k}},\widehat{u}^{h_{k+1}}-u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big|\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle-\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle

\displaystyle+\theta_{k+1}\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big|\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle-(1-\theta_{k+1})\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle\big|.

Combining (
64
) and (
65
) leads to

\displaystyle\big|\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle-(1-\theta_{k+1})\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\theta_{k+1}}{2}|\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle|.

That is

\displaystyle|\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\frac{\theta_{k+1}}{2})|\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle|.

Then we complete the proof.

In order to give a practical algorithm, there are two remaining problems. The first one is to choose an appropriate parameter
\theta_{k+1}
.
The second one is to compute the residual after each iteration step.

For the first problem, we provide an adaptive strategy for choosing
\theta_{k+1}
in Algorithm
5.2
, which gradually reduces the value of
\theta_{k+1}
.
For the second problem, in order to compare the following two values
|\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle|
and
|\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle|

for any
(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\in X_{h_{k+1}}
,
we define the residual for the nonlinear eigenvalue problem (
3
) directly in the following way:

\displaystyle Resi(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})=\|A_{u^{h_{k}}}u^{h_{k}}-\lambda^{h_{k}}u^{h_{k}}\|_{1}+\frac{1}{2}|1-\|u^{h_{k}}\|_{0}^{2}|.

Then next, we use
Resi(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})
to judge the error reduction.

The modified mixing scheme and the corresponding multigrid method are presented in Algorithms
5.2
and
5.3
, respectively.

Algorithm 5.2

One step of the modified mixing scheme

1.

Assume that we have obtained an approximate solution
(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})\in X_{h_{k}}
.

2.

Set
\theta_{k+1}=1
.

3.

Solve the linearized equation derived from Newton iteration in the finite element space
X_{h_{k+1}}
:
Find
(\widehat{\lambda}^{h_{k+1}},\widehat{u}^{h_{k+1}})\in X_{h_{k+1}}
such that for any
(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\in X_{h_{k+1}}
, there holds

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})(\widehat{\lambda}^{h_{k+1}},\widehat{u}^{h_{k+1}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\rangle.

The above equation can be solved by the mixed finite element method in the following form: Find
(\widehat{\lambda}^{h_{k+1}},\widehat{u}^{h_{k+1}})\in\mathbb{R}\times V_{h_{k+1}}

such that for any
(\mu,v_{h_{k+1}})\in\mathbb{R}\times V_{h_{k+1}}
, there holds

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}a(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}};\widehat{u}^{h_{k+1}},v_{h_{k+1}})+b(u^{h_{k}};v_{h_{k+1}},\widehat{\lambda}^{h_{k+1}})&=&\langle\mathcal{F}_{u}^{\prime}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})u^{h_{k}},v_{h_{k+1}}\rangle\\
&&-\langle\mathcal{F}(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}}),v_{h_{k+1}}\rangle-\lambda^{h_{k}}(u^{h_{k}},v_{h_{k+1}}),\\
b(u^{h_{k}};\widehat{u}^{h_{k+1}},\mu)&=&-\mu/2-\mu(u^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})/2.\end{array}\right.

4.

Set

\displaystyle(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}})=(1-\theta_{k+1})(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})+\theta_{k+1}(\widehat{\lambda}^{h_{k+1}},\widehat{u}^{h_{k+1}}).

5.

If
Resi(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}})\leq Resi(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}})
, stop. Else set
\theta_{k+1}=\theta_{k+1}/2
and goto Step 4.

In order to simplify the notation, we use the following symbol to denote the above solving steps:

\displaystyle(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}})=Mixing_{-}Iteration(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}},\theta_{k+1},X_{h_{k+1}}).

Algorithm 5.3

Multigrid method based on mixing scheme

1.

Solve the following nonlinear eigenvalue problem directly in the initial space
V_{h_{1}}
: Find
(\lambda^{h_{1}},u^{h_{1}})\in\mathbb{R}\times V_{h_{1}}
such that
\|u^{h_{1}}\|_{0}=1
and

(\mathcal{A}\nabla u^{h_{1}},\nabla v_{h_{1}})+(Vu^{h_{1}},v_{h_{1}})+(f((u^{h_{1}})^{2})u^{h_{1}},v_{h_{1}})=\lambda^{h_{1}}(u^{h_{1}},v_{h_{1}}),\quad\forall v_{h_{1}}\in V_{h_{1}}.

2.

For
k=1,\cdots,n-1
, obtain a new approximate solution
(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}})\in X_{h_{k+1}}
through

\displaystyle(\lambda^{h_{k+1}},u^{h_{k+1}})=Mixing_{-}Iteration(\lambda^{h_{k}},u^{h_{k}},\theta_{k+1},X_{h_{k+1}}),

End for.

3.

Finally, we obtain the approximate solution
(\lambda^{h_{n}},u^{h_{n}})\in X_{h_{n}}
.

7
Concluding remarks

In this study, we designed a novel multigrid method to solve the nonlinear eigenvalue problem
on the basis of the Newton iteration. The novel scheme transforms the nonlinear eigenvalue problem into
a series of linearized boundary value problems defined in a
sequence of product finite element spaces
.
Because of avoiding solving large-scale nonlinear eigenvalue problems directly, the overall solving efficiency is significantly improved.
Besides, the optimal error estimate and linear
complexity can be derived simultaneously. In addition, an improved multigrid method coupled with the mixing scheme is also
introduced. The improved scheme can make the iteration scheme converge for more complicated models.
More importantly, a convergence result is derived which is missing in the existing literature on the mixing scheme for nonlinear eigenvalue problems.

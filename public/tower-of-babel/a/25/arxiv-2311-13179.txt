On the universal local and global properties of positive solutions to Δ p ​ v + b ​ | ∇ v | q + c ​ v r = 0 \Delta_{p}v+b|\nabla v|^{q}+cv^{r}=0 on complete Riemannian manifolds
Jie He, Youde Wang

Abstract
. In this paper we study the positive solutions to a nonlinear elliptic equation Δ p ​ v + b ​ | ∇ v | q + c ​ v r = 0 \Delta_{p}v+b|\nabla v|^{q}+cv^{r}=0 defined on a complete Riemannian manifold ( M , g ) (M,g) with Ricci curvature bounded from below, where p > 1 p>1 , q , r , b q,\,r,\,b and c c are some real constants. If p > 1 p>1 is given and b ​ c ≥ 0 bc\geq 0 , we provide a new routine to give some regions of ( q , r ) (q,r) such that the Cheng-Yau’s logarithmic gradient estimates hold true exactly on such given regions. In particular, we derive the upper bounds of the constants c ⁡ ( n , p , q , r ) c(n,p,q,r) in the Cheng-Yau’s gradient estimates for the entire solutions to the above equation. As applications, we reveal some universal local and global properties of positive solutions to the equation. On the other hand, we extend some results due to [ 26 ] to the case the domain of the equation is a complete manifold and obtain wider ranges of ( q , r ) (q,r) for Liouville properties.



1.
Introduction

Liouville property states that entire solutions to an elliptic equation must be constants under certain boundedness conditions. The earliest Liouville theorem can be traced back to Cauchy. In 1844, Cauchy proved that any bounded entire holomorphic function must be a constant. The Cauchy’s theorem was extended at once to real harmonic functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
which are bounded only on one side.
The Liouville theorem has had a deep impact across many fields, such as complex analysis, PDE and differential geometry (see
[
11
]
).

In the past half century, one trend about the study of Liouville theorem is to study the Liouville property of semilinear and quasi-linear elliptic equations defined on Riemannian manifolds (see for example
[
10
,
12
,
13
,
22
,
24
,
37
,
39
,
40
]
). Up to now, it is well-known that the curvature of a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
could affects some global properties of solution to partial differential equations on
M
and global quantities like the eigenvalues of the Laplacian with respect to
g
on
M
. So, the situation is more complicated than the case
M\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For instance, the so-called Cheng-Yau type gradient estimate, a more profound property of solutions to elliptic equations on manifolds, is of obvious geometric significance and plays important role on the local and global properties of solutions to a linear or nonlinear elliptic equation on a complete manifold.

Let’s recall that Cheng and Yau
[
10
]
in 1975 used Bochner’s formula on Riemannian manifolds and employed cut-off functions via the Laplacian comparison theorem under the condition of Ricci curvature being bounded below to prove that any positive harmonic function
v
defined on a complete non-compact Riemannian manifold with
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}\geq-(n-1)\kappa g
satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{R/2}(o)}\frac{|\nabla v|}{v}\leq C_{n}\frac{1+\sqrt{\kappa}R}{R}.

(1.1)

The above elegant logarithmic estimate is also holds for the eigenvalue problem (see
[
23
]
). Now, such an estimate is called as Cheng-Yau’s gradient estimate. An important feature of Cheng-Yau’s estimate is that the RHS of estimate (
1.1
) depends only on
n
,
\kappa
and
R
, it does not depend on the injective radius or global system. If
(M,g)
is a non-compact complete manifold with non-negative Ricci curvature, then Liouville theorem for harmonic functions follows immediately from Cheng-Yau’s gradient estimate. Moreover, Harnack inequality can also be concluded from the estimate (
1.1
).

Usually, the above estimate is also called as universal a priori estimates. By using the word “universal” here, we mean that our bounds are not only independent of any given solution under consideration but also do not require, or assume, any boundary conditions whatsoever. Actually, to derive universal a priori estimates for solutions of some partial differential equations is also one’s principal purpose to explore local and global properties of these solutions (see
[
17
,
14
,
28
,
30
]
).

Let
(M,g)
be a complete non-compact Riemannian manifold with
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}\geq-(n-1)\kappa g
. We say an elliptic equation defined on
M
satisfies Cheng-Yau type gradient estimate if for any geodesic ball
B_{R}(o)\subset M
and any (positive) solution
v
of the equation on
B_{R}(o)
, there holds the above estimate (
1.1
).

A natural question is for which equations on a complete non-compact Riemannian manifold with
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}\geq-(n-1)\kappa g
the Cheng-Yau’s estimate holds true exactly?

Indeed, one has shown that for some equations, for instances
p
-harmonic functions, Lane-Emden equation etc., such a priori estimates hold true (see
[
37
,
39
,
20
,
19
]
).

In this paper, we intend to using directly Nash-Moser iteration to establish such a gradient estimate and Liouville properties of positive solutions to the following equation:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta_{p}v+b|\nabla v|^{q}+cv^{r}=0;\\
p>1,\,\,b,\,c,\,q,\,r\in{\mathbb{R}},\end{cases}

(1.2)

on a complete Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
with
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}\geq-(n-1)\kappa g
. It is worthy to point out that, in order to obtain Harnack inequalities and universal boundedness estimates, although one usually needs to employ the classical Nash-Moser iteration, as adopted by Gidas-Spruck
[
17
]
or Serrin-Zou
[
30
]
, or blow-up analysis method via Liouville theorems inspired by Dancer
[
14
]
or Poláčcik-Quittner-Souplet
[
28
]
, neither of these methods can be directly applied to the above equations (
1.2
) on a complete Riemannian manifold. So, here we will adopt a new way to apply Nash-Moser technique to approach these estimates and provide a new routine to explore the deep relationship between these estimates and Riemannian curvature.

When equation (
1.2
) is defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it has been studied by many mathematicians, for details we refer to
[
4
,
5
,
16
,
26
]
and references therein. Obviously, the equation (
1.2
) is related to many classical equations. For instance, in the case

, equation (
1.2
) reduces to the
p
-Laplace harmonic function equation. In 2011, Wang and Zhang
[
37

proved any positive

-harmonic function

(M,g)
with
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}\geq-(n-1)\kappa g
satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{R/2}(o)}\frac{|\nabla v|}{v}\leq C_{n,p}\frac{1+\sqrt{\kappa}R}{R}.

Wang-Zhang’s results generalized Cheng-Yau’s result
[
40
]
of
p=2
to any
p>1
and improved Kotschwar-Ni’s results
[
22
]
by weakening sectional curvature condition to the Ricci curvature condition. Later in 2014, Sung and Wang
[
33
]
studied the sharp gradient estimate for eigenfunctions of
p
-Laplace operator.

In the case
b=-1
and
c=0
, then equation (
1.2
) reduces to the so called quasi-linear Hamilton-Jacobi equation,

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}v-|\nabla v|^{q}=0.

(1.3)

For the case
(M,g)
is the Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we refer to
[
3
,
25
]
for some Liouville type results of global solutions of equation (
1.3
). In particular, Bidaut-Véron, Garcia-Huidobro and Véron
[

]
proved that, if
n\geq p>1
and

, then any solution
u
of equation (
1.3
) defined on a domain

satisfies

\displaystyle|\nabla v(x)|\leq c(n,p,q)\left(d(x,\partial\Omega)\right)^{-\frac{1}{q+1-p}},\quad\forall x\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.4)

In
[
3
]
, the authors also extended their estimates to equations on complete non-compact manifolds with sectional curvature bounded from below. Recently, Han, He and Wang
[
18
]
also studied the nonlinear Hamilton-Jacobi equation on manifolds
(M,g)
with
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}\geq-(n-1)\kappa g
. They gave a unified Cheng-Yau type gradietn estimate for the solutions of equation (
1.3
) with
q>p-1
defined on
M
. Concretely, for any solution
u
to (
1.3
) there holds the following universal estimate

\sup_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}(o)}|\nabla v|\leq C_{n,p,q}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{\kappa}R}{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-p+1}}.

If
b=0
and
c=1
, then equation (
1.2
) reduces to the Lane-Emden equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta_{p}v+cv^{r}=0,\quad&\text{in}\,M;\\
v>0,\quad&\text{in}\,M.\end{cases}

(1.5)

In the case
M={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
r>0
and
1<p<n
, there have been extensive studies on the equation (

In the case

and

, Wang-Wei

derived Cheng-Yau type gradient estimates for positive solutions to (

For any

We refer to

-Laplace Lichnerowicz operator.

Now we turn to the case where

and

are both non-vanishing. The following theorem was proved by Bidaut-Véron, Garcia-Huidobro and Véron(

Theorem
.

(Filippucci, Sun and Zheng
[
16
]
) Let
v
be a positive solution to the following equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta_{p}v+M|\nabla v|^{q}+v^{r}=0,\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\\
p>1,\,\,M>0.\end{cases}

(1.6)

If
(q,r)
satisfies

\displaystyle 1<\frac{q}{p-1}<\frac{n+2}{n}\quad\text{and}\quad 1<\frac{r}{p-1}<\frac{n+3}{n-1},

(1.7)

then there exist positive constants

such that

\displaystyle|\nabla v^{a}(x)|\leq c(n,p,q,r)\left(d(x,\partial\Omega)\right)^{-\frac{pa}{r-p+1}-1},\quad\forall x\in\Omega.

(1.8)

Naturally, one wants to know if the similar gradient estimate with that established in the above theorem for the case

also holds true for the same equation defined on a complete Riemannian manifold?

Furthermore, one would like to ask whether or not any positive solution to equation (
1.2
) on a complete non-compact Riemannian manifold with Ricci curvature bounded from below satisfies the exact Cheng-Yau type gradient estimates?

It turns out that the answer is yes. What’s more, the Cheng-Yau type estimate can be established in a wider range of
(q,r)
than the range in (
1.7
).
Now we are in a position to state our results.

1.2.

We also note that Saloff-Coste’s Sobolev inequality implies

Thus, we can infer from the above Saloff-Coste’s Sobolev inequality and (

) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\beta_{0}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}+\frac{a_{3}}{t}e^{-c_{0}(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R)}V^{\frac{2}{n}}R^{-2}\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}}^{2}\\
\leq&\ 2(n-1)\kappa\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+t+\frac{p}{2}-1}\eta^{2}+\frac{a_{4}}{t}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+t+\frac{p}{2}-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2}+\frac{a_{3}}{t}\int_{\Omega}R^{-2}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}.\end{split}

(3.12)

Now we set

\displaystyle t_{0}=c_{n,p,q,r}\left(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R\right)\quad\text{and}\quad c_{n,p,q,r}=\max\left\{c_{0}+1,\frac{a_{1}^{2}}{a_{2}\beta_{n,p,q,r}}\right\},

(3.13)

and pick
t
such that
t\geq t_{0}
. Since

\displaystyle 2(n-1)\kappa R^{2}\leq\frac{2(n-1)}{c_{n,p,q,r}^{2}}t_{0}^{2}\quad\text{ and}\quad\frac{a_{3}}{t}\leq\frac{a_{3}}{c_{n,p,q,r}},

then, there exists
a_{5}=a_{5}(n,p,q,r)>0
such that

\displaystyle 2(n-1)\kappa R^{2}+\frac{a_{3}}{t}\leq a_{5}t_{0}^{2}=a_{5}c^{2}_{n,p,q,r}\left(1+\sqrt{\kappa}R\right)^{2}.

(3.14)

Hence, it follows from (
3.12
) and (
3.14
) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\beta_{0}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t}\eta^{2}+\frac{a_{3}}{t}e^{-t_{0}}V^{\frac{2}{n}}R^{-2}\left\|f^{\frac{\alpha_{0}+t-1}{2}+\frac{p}{4}}\eta\right\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}}^{2}\\
\leq\ &a_{5}t_{0}^{2}R^{-2}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}\eta^{2}+\frac{a_{4}}{t}\int_{\Omega}f^{\alpha_{0}+\frac{p}{2}+t-1}|\nabla\eta|^{2}.\end{split}

(3.15)

Thus, we finish the proof.

Proof.

For any

, by Theorem
1.1
, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla\ln v(x)|\leq c(n,p,q)\frac{1+\sqrt{\kappa}R}{R}.

(3.33)

v
is an entire positive solution of equation (
1.2
) on
M
. Letting
R\to\infty
in (
3.33
), we obtain that

|\nabla\ln v(x)|\leq C(n,p,q,r)\sqrt{\kappa},\quad\forall x\in M.

\gamma(t)
with arc length parameter connecting
x
and

:

\gamma:[0,d]\to M,\quad\gamma(0)=x,\quad\gamma(d)=y,

where
d=d(x,y)
is the distance from
x
to

. We have

\displaystyle\ln v(y)-\ln v(x)=\int_{0}^{d}\frac{d}{dt}\ln v\circ\gamma(t)dt.

(3.36)

, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d}{dt}\ln v\circ\gamma(t)\right|\leq|\nabla\ln v||\gamma^{\prime}(t)|=C(n,p,q,r)\sqrt{\kappa}.

In order to give the proof of Theorem

we need to establish the following lemma:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a non-compact complete manifold with
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}\geq 0
. Assume that
v>0
satisfies the following differential inequality with
1<p<n

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}v+v^{r}\leq 0\quad

\displaystyle\text{in}\,\,M.

(3.39)

If

p-1<r<\frac{n(p-1)}{n-p},

then there holds true

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(o)}v^{r}\leq C_{1}(n,p,r)R^{\frac{(n-p)r-(p-1)n}{r-p+1}},

(3.40)

where
B_{R}(o)\subset M
is a geodesic ball and
C_{1}(n,p,r)
ia a positive constant depending on
n
,
p
and
r
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in C_{0}^{1}(M)
be a standard non-negative cutoff function that will be specified later. Let
\alpha<0
be a parameter that will also be chosen later. Multiplying (
4.3
) by
\varphi v^{\alpha}
and then integrating by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{M}v^{r+\alpha}\varphi\leq&\int_{M}\alpha v^{\alpha-1}|\nabla v|^{p}\varphi+v^{\alpha}|\nabla v|^{p-2}\langle\nabla v,\nabla\varphi\rangle\\
\leq&\int_{M}\alpha v^{\alpha-1}|\nabla v|^{p}\varphi+v^{\alpha}|\nabla v|^{p-1}|\nabla\varphi|.\end{split}

(3.41)

Young inequality implies

\displaystyle\int_{M}v^{\alpha}|\nabla v|^{p-1}|\nabla\varphi|\leq\frac{(p-1)\epsilon^{\frac{p}{p-1}}}{p}\int_{M}|\nabla v|^{p}v^{\alpha-1}\varphi+\frac{1}{p\epsilon^{p}}\int_{M}\varphi^{1-p}v^{\alpha+p-1}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}.

If we denote

\theta_{\epsilon}=|\alpha|-\frac{(p-1)\epsilon^{\frac{p}{p-1}}}{p}>0\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\theta_{\epsilon}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{p\epsilon^{p}},

then we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}v^{r+\alpha}\varphi+\theta_{\epsilon}\int_{M}v^{\alpha-1}|\nabla v|^{p}\varphi\leq\theta_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\int_{M}\varphi^{1-p}v^{\alpha+p-1}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}.

Now we set
\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}=1
. Using Young inequality again yields

\displaystyle\int_{M}v^{r+\alpha}\varphi+\theta_{\epsilon}\int_{M}v^{\alpha-1}|\nabla v|^{p}\varphi\leq\theta_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}\int_{M}v^{(\alpha+p-1)s}\varphi+\theta_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime\prime}\int_{M}|\nabla\varphi|^{pt}\frac{1}{\varphi^{pt-1}}.

(3.42)

If we choose

s=\frac{r+\alpha}{\alpha+p-1}

(here we need require
\alpha>1-p
), then we have

\theta^{\prime\prime}_{\epsilon}=\frac{\delta^{s}}{sp\epsilon^{p}}\quad\text{and}\quad\theta^{\prime\prime\prime}_{\epsilon}=\frac{\delta^{-t}}{tp\epsilon^{p}}.

We neglect the first term in the right hand side of (
3.42
) and obtain

\displaystyle C_{\epsilon}\int_{M}v^{r+\alpha}\varphi+\theta_{\epsilon}\int_{M}v^{\alpha-1}|\nabla v|^{p}\varphi\leq\theta_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime\prime}\int_{M}|\nabla\varphi|^{pt}\frac{1}{\varphi^{pt-1}}.

(3.43)

Now, let us estimate the integral
\int_{M}v^{r}\varphi
. Multiplying
\varphi
on the both sides of (
4.3
) and integrating over

yield

\displaystyle\int_{M}v^{r}\varphi\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla v|^{p-1}|\nabla\varphi|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(|\nabla v|^{p-1}v^{\frac{(\alpha-1)(p-1)}{p}}\varphi^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\right)\left(v^{\frac{(1-\alpha)(p-1)}{p}}\varphi^{-\frac{p-1}{p}}|\nabla\varphi|\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}|\nabla v|^{p}v^{\alpha-1}\varphi\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{M}v^{(1-\alpha)(p-1)}\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{p}}{\varphi^{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}|\nabla v|^{p}v^{\alpha-1}\varphi\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{M}v^{a(1-\alpha)(p-1)}\varphi\right)^{\frac{1}{pa}}\left(\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{pa^{\prime}}}{\varphi^{pa^{\prime}-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{pa^{\prime}}},

where the constant
a
is determined by

a(1-\alpha)(p-1)=r+\alpha\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{a^{\prime}}=1.

It follows from the above inequality and (
3.43
)

\displaystyle\int_{M}v^{r}\varphi\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}|\nabla v|^{p}v^{\alpha-1}\varphi\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{M}v^{r+\alpha}\varphi\right)^{\frac{1}{pa}}\left(\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{pa^{\prime}}}{\varphi^{pa^{\prime}-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{pa^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\theta_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{\theta_{\epsilon}^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\frac{\theta_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{C_{\epsilon}}\right)^{\frac{1}{pa}}\left(\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{pt}}{\varphi^{pt-1}}\right)^{\beta}\left(\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{pa^{\prime}}}{\varphi^{pa^{\prime}-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{pa^{\prime}}},

where

\beta=\frac{p-1}{p}+\frac{(1-\alpha)(p-1)}{p(r+\alpha)}.

Now, we choose
\gamma\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{2R}(o))
such that

\begin{cases}\gamma(x)\equiv 1,&\quad\text{in}\quad B_{R}(0);\\
1\geq\gamma(x)\geq 0,\,\,\,\,|\nabla\gamma(x)|\leq\frac{C}{R},&\quad\text{in}\quad B_{2R}(0),\end{cases}

and let
\varphi=\gamma^{l}
. It is easy to see that

\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{pt}}{\varphi^{pt-1}}=l^{pt}\gamma^{l-pt}|\nabla\gamma|^{pt}\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{pa^{\prime}}}{\varphi^{pa^{\prime}-1}}=l^{pa^{\prime}}\gamma^{l-pa^{\prime}}|\nabla\gamma|^{pa^{\prime}}.

Now we choose
l
large enough such that
l>pt
and
l>pa^{\prime}
. Since
\mathrm{Ric}\geq 0
, the volume comparison theorem implies

\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{pt}}{\varphi^{pt-1}}\leq(lC)^{pt}\int_{M}R^{-pt}\gamma^{l-pt}\leq(lC)^{pt}\int_{B_{2R}(o)}R^{-pt}\leq 2^{n}(lC)^{pt}R^{n-pt},

and

\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{pa^{\prime}}}{\varphi^{pa^{\prime}-1}}\leq(lC)^{pa^{\prime}}\int_{M}R^{-pa^{\prime}}\gamma^{l-pa^{\prime}}\leq 2^{n}(lC)^{pa^{\prime}}R^{n-pa^{\prime}}.

Hence, it follows

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(o)}v^{r}\leq\int_{M}v^{r}\varphi\leq C_{1}R^{\sigma},

(3.44)

where

Thus, we complete the proof of this lemma.
∎

n

(1)

(b,c,p,q)\in W_{1}

\omega=(f-y_{1}-\delta)^{+}

(2)

b_{1}^{\prime}

b_{2}^{\prime}

(3)

In the case

and
\omega=(f-y_{3}-\delta)^{+}

\Omega_{3}=\{f\geq y_{3}+\delta\}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}(\omega^{\alpha})\geq 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}\left(b_{1}^{\prime\prime}\omega-b_{2}^{\prime\prime}|\nabla\omega|\right),

b_{1}^{\prime\prime}
and
b_{2}^{\prime\prime}

\alpha>0

(4)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}(\omega^{\alpha})\geq 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}\left(b_{1}^{\prime\prime\prime}\omega-b_{2}^{\prime\prime\prime}|\nabla\omega|\right),

b_{1}^{\prime\prime\prime}
and
b_{2}^{\prime\prime\prime}

Assume that

may vary in different
W_{i}
where
i=1,2,3,4

If
(b,c,p,q)\in W_{1}
, we denote that
y_{1}=(n-1)^{2}\kappa
. Then, for any
\delta>0
we define
\omega=(f-y_{1}-\delta)^{+}
. Hence, on
\{f\geq y_{1}+\delta\}
we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}(\omega^{\alpha})={\rm div}(\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}A(\nabla f))=\alpha(\alpha-1)\omega^{\alpha-2}\langle\nabla\omega,A(\nabla f)\rangle+\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}{\mathcal{L}}(f).

In the above expression, it is easy to see that
\nabla\omega=\nabla f
in

, but
\nabla\omega
causes a distribution on

\alpha>2

\nabla\omega
on

is eliminated by
\omega

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}(\omega^{\alpha})=

\displaystyle\ \alpha(\alpha-1)\omega^{\alpha-2}\langle\nabla f,A(\nabla f)\rangle+\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}{\mathcal{L}}(f)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ \omega^{\alpha-1}\left(\alpha(\alpha-1)f^{-1}\langle\nabla f,A(\nabla f)\rangle+\alpha{\mathcal{L}}(f)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\omega^{\alpha-1}}{f^{\alpha-1}}{\mathcal{L}}(f^{\alpha}).

It follows from the above inequality and (

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})\geq

\displaystyle\ 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}f^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\left(\frac{f^{2}}{n-1}-(n-1)\kappa f-\frac{a_{1}}{2}f^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla\omega|\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}f^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(f^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{f}{n-1}-(n-1)\kappa\right)-\frac{a_{1}}{2}|\nabla\omega|\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}f^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(f^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\delta}{n-1}-\frac{a_{1}}{2}|\nabla\omega|\right).

Using the fact

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}(f^{\alpha})\geq 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}\left(b_{1}\omega-b_{2}|\nabla\omega|\right),\quad\forall\alpha>2,

where
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
are two positive constants.

If
(b,c,p,q)\in W_{2}

\displaystyle\frac{f^{2-\alpha-\frac{p}{2}}}{2\alpha}\mathcal{L}(f^{\alpha})\geq

B^{1}_{n,p,q,r,\alpha}:=\frac{1}{n-1}-\frac{\left(\frac{n+1}{n-1}-\frac{r}{p-1}\right)^{2}}{\frac{4}{n-1}-\frac{4d_{2}}{2\alpha-1}}>0

and
\alpha
is large enough such that

Denote

\displaystyle y_{2}=\frac{4\kappa}{\left(\frac{n+3}{n-1}-\frac{r}{p-1}\right)\left(\frac{r}{p-1}-1\right)},

and let’s define

\omega=(f-y_{2}-\delta)^{+}

where
\delta>0
is any positive real number. Then, by taking a computation we have on
\{f\geq y_{2}+\delta\}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}(\omega^{\alpha})\geq

\displaystyle\ 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}f^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(f^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(B^{1}_{n,p,q,r,\alpha}f-(n-1)\kappa\right)-\frac{a_{1}}{2}|\nabla\omega|\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}f^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(f^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\delta}{2(n-1)}-\frac{a_{1}}{2}|\nabla\omega|\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}\left(b_{1}^{\prime}\omega-b_{2}^{\prime}|\nabla\omega|\right).

If
(b,c,p,q)\in W_{3}
, we denote

y_{3}=\frac{4\kappa}{\left(\frac{n+3}{n-1}-\frac{q}{p-1}\right)\left(\frac{q}{p-1}-1\right)}

\omega=(f-y_{3}-\delta)^{+}

\delta>0

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}(\omega^{\alpha})\geq

\displaystyle 2\alpha\omega^{\alpha-1}\left(b_{1}\omega-b_{2}|\nabla\omega|\right)

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dim(
n\geq 2
) complete Riemannian manifold satisfying
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}\geq-(n-1)\kappa g
for some constant
\kappa\geq 0
. Let
v\in C^{1}(M)
be an entire positive solution of (
1.2
). For some
y>0
, we define
\omega=(f-y)^{+}
. If
\omega
satisfies the following inequality

{\mathcal{L}}(\omega^{\alpha})\geq\omega^{\alpha-1}(b_{1}\omega-b_{2}|\nabla\omega|)

where
b_{1}
,
b_{2}
and
\alpha
are some positive constants, then
\omega\equiv 0
, i.e.,
f\leq y
.

Proof.

Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
be a cut-off function to be determine later. We choose
\eta^{2}\omega^{\gamma}
as test function, then we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}{\mathcal{L}}(\omega^{\alpha})\omega^{\gamma}\eta^{2}\geq\int_{M}2\alpha\omega^{\alpha+\gamma-1}\left(b_{1}\omega-b_{2}|\nabla\omega|\right)\eta^{2}.

We omit the term
f^{\frac{p}{2}-1}
since
f
is uniform bounded on
M
. By integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{M}2\alpha\omega^{\alpha+\gamma-1}\left(b_{1}\omega-b_{2}|\nabla\omega|\right)\eta^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\alpha\gamma\omega^{\alpha+\gamma-2}\eta^{2}(|\nabla\omega|^{2}+(p-2)f^{-1}\langle\nabla\omega,\nabla u\rangle^{2})

\displaystyle-\int_{M}2\alpha\eta\omega^{\alpha+\gamma-1}\left(\langle\nabla\omega,\nabla\eta\rangle+(p-2)f^{-1}\langle\nabla\omega,\nabla\eta\rangle\langle\nabla u,\nabla\eta\rangle\right).

It follows

\displaystyle\int_{M}2\omega^{\alpha+\gamma-1}\left(b_{1}\omega-b_{2}|\nabla\omega|\right)\eta^{2}+a_{1}\int_{M}\gamma\omega^{\alpha+\gamma-2}\eta^{2}|\nabla\omega|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2(p+1)\int_{M}\omega^{\alpha+\gamma-1}|\nabla\omega||\nabla\eta|\eta.

Cauchy inequality implies

\displaystyle 2b_{2}\omega^{\alpha+\gamma-1}|\nabla\omega|\eta^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\ b_{2}\eta^{2}\omega^{\alpha+\gamma-2}\left(\frac{|\nabla\omega|^{2}}{\epsilon}+\epsilon\omega^{2}\right);

\displaystyle 2\eta(p+1)\omega^{\alpha+\gamma-1}|\nabla\omega||\nabla\eta|\leq

\displaystyle\ (p+1)\omega^{\alpha+\gamma-2}\left(\frac{\eta^{2}|\nabla\omega|^{2}}{\epsilon}+\epsilon|\nabla\eta|^{2}\omega^{2}\right).

If we choose
\epsilon
such that

\displaystyle\frac{b_{2}+p+1}{\epsilon}=a_{1}\gamma,

then we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}2b_{1}\omega^{\alpha+\gamma}\eta^{2}\leq\int_{M}\epsilon(p+1)\omega^{\alpha+\gamma}|\nabla\eta|^{2}+\int_{M}\epsilon b_{2}\omega^{\alpha+\gamma}\eta^{2}.

Picking
\gamma
large enough such that
\epsilon b_{2}<b_{1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle b_{1}\int_{M}\omega^{\alpha+\gamma}\eta^{2}\leq\int_{M}\epsilon(p+1)\omega^{\alpha+\gamma}|\nabla\eta|^{2}.

Now, if
\omega\neq 0
, without loss of generality we may assume that
\omega|_{B_{1}}\neq 0
for some geodesic ball
B_{1}
with radius 1, it follows that there always holds true

\int_{B_{1}}\omega^{\alpha+\gamma}>0.

By choosing
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{k+1})
,
\eta\equiv 1
in
B_{k}
and
|\nabla\eta|<4
, then from the above argument we have

\displaystyle b_{1}\int_{B_{k}}\omega^{\alpha+\gamma}\leq 4\epsilon(p+1)\int_{B_{k+1}}\omega^{\alpha+\gamma}.

By iteration on
k
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{k}}\omega^{\alpha+\gamma}\geq\left(\frac{C}{\epsilon}\right)^{k}\int_{B_{1}}\omega^{\alpha+\gamma}.

Since
\omega
is uniformly bounded and
{\rm{vol}}(B_{k})\leq e^{(n-1)k\sqrt{\kappa}}
by volume comparison theorem, we have

\displaystyle C_{1}^{\alpha+\gamma}e^{(n-1)k\sqrt{\kappa}}\geq e^{k\ln\frac{C}{\epsilon}}.

We choose
\gamma
such that

\ln\frac{C}{\epsilon}>2(n-1)\sqrt{\kappa}+2

and then choose
k
large enough, immediately we get a contradiction. This means
\omega\equiv 0
and we finish the proof of this lemma.
∎

5.2.
Some further comments and open problems

Although the arguments presented in the above subsection give the upper bounds of constants
c(n,p,q,r)
for different cases by obvious
formula, we can not confirm whether these bounds are sharp or not.

In the case the coefficients
b
and
c
associated with equation (
1.2
) satisfy
bc<0
, i.e.,
b
and
c
are of different signs, it seems that the method employed here is not valid. However, from the results of Theorem E in
[
4
]
we know that some Liouville properties hold true for positive solutions to (
1.2
) on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 3
if
|b|
is small enough,
c=1
,
p=2
,
1<r<\frac{n+2}{n-2}
and
q=\frac{2r}{r+1}
(also see
[
16
]
).

Naturally, one wants to know whether or not the Cheng-Yau’s logarithmic gradient estimate or Liouville property still holds true for positive solutions to (
1.2
) with
bc<0
on a complete Riemannian manifold?

In fact, Bidaut-Véron, Garcia-Huidobro and Véron
[
6
]

studied recently the properties of nonnegative functions satisfying (E)
-\Delta u+m|\nabla u|^{q}-u^{r}=0

in a domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
when
r>1
,
m>0
and
1<q<r
.
They concentrate their analysis on the solutions of (E) with an isolated singularity, or in an exterior domain, or in the whole space.

The existence of such solutions and their behaviours depend strongly on the values of the exponents
r
and
q
.

Very recently, Bidaut-Véron and Véron in
[
7
]
also studied the local properties of positive solutions of the equation
\Delta u-m|\nabla u|^{q}+u^{r}=0
in a punctured domain
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or in an exterior domain
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(0)
in the range
\min\{r,q\}>1
and
m>0
. They proved a series of a priori estimates depending
r
and
q
.

But, it seems that the methods adopted in
[
6
,
7
]
are not valid for the case
(M,g)
is a Riemannian manifold.

Abstract
. Bounded oscillation ( BO {\rm BO} ) operators were recently introduced and studied in [ 15 ] . Those are operators running on abstract measure spaces equipped with a ball-basis. It was proved that many operators in harmonic analysis (Calderón-Zygmund operators and their maximally modulations, Carleson type operators, martingale transforms, Littlewood-Paley square functions, maximal function, etc) are BO {\rm BO} operators. Various properties of BO {\rm BO} operators were studied in [ 16 , 15 ] . A multilinear version of BO {\rm BO} operators recently was considered in [ 6 ] , recovering series of results of papers [ 16 , 15 ] in multilinear setting. A main line of these works are so called sparse dominations results, a subject of intensive study of recent years, which is closely related to sharp weighted norm and exponential decay estimates of various operators. First sparse domination results were proved for the Calderón-Zygmund operators in [ 22 , 9 , 18 ] , providing a simplified proof to the A 2 A_{2} -conjecture that was earlier solved in [ 12 ] . In this paper we will consider a generalized version of BO {\rm BO} operators, involving new parameters in the definition, as well as considering the operators on vector-valued function spaces. With this definition we will capture some more operators to the class of BO {\rm BO} operators. We prove sparse domination and exponential decay estimates, with various applications in harmonic analysis operators. We provide a new simplified approach, separating certain set theoretic proposition, which become a basic tool in the proofs of the main results. For a ”narrowed” class of BO {\rm BO} operators we also obtain new type of sparse estimation, involving mean oscillation instead of integral averages in the definition of sparse operators. Among with new corollaries we recover also series of results obtained in recent years. In particular, we prove the boundedness of maximally modulated Calderón-Zygmund operators on spaces BMO {\rm BMO} .

(1.1)

\displaystyle B^{*}=\bigcup_{A\in\mathscr{B}:\,\mu(A)\leq 2\mu(B),\,A\cap B\neq\varnothing}A\subset[B],

(1.2)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\mu\left([B]\right)\leq\mathcal{K}\mu(B),

A ball-basis

1.2.
Sparse domination by fractional means

A classical problem in harmonic analysis is the control of an operator by means of simple operators. A model example of such a phenomenon is the technique of domination by so-called sparse operators, which became a powerful tool to obtain sharp weighted norm inequalities in the series of recent works. The sparse operators was first explicitly appeared in Lerner’s work
[
22
]
towards an alternative proof of the
A_{2}
-theorem due to Hytönen
[
12
]

[
22
]
a norm estimation of classical Calderón–Zygmund operators by sparse operators. This was later improved to a pointwise estimate independently by Conde-Alonso and Rey
[
9

and by Lerner and Nazarov
[
20
]

[
18

obtained the same pointwise domination for general

[

elaborated the proof of Lacey
[

[

obtained also a pointwise sparse domination for general martingale transforms (including non-doubling cases of filtrations), giving a short and elementary proof of the

-theorem for martingale transforms due to Thiele, Treil and Volberg in

Finally note that multilinear versions of many results of papers

1.3.
Sparse domination by mean oscillations

For certain subclass of
{\rm BO}
operators and their maximally modulations we prove a new type of sparse domination and exponential decay inequalities, involving mean oscillation instead of fractional means. Using these results we obtain boundedness of maximally modulated Calderón-Zygmund and some other operators on spaces

r\geq 1
and suppose our ball-basis is doubling.
Define the volume distance between a point
x\in X
and a ball
B
by

(1.25)

d(x,B)=\inf_{A\in\mathscr{B}:\,A\supset B\cup\{x\}}\mu(A).

Definition 1.9
.

Let
\omega:(0,1)\to(0,1)
be a modulus of continuity, that is a monotone increasing and subadditive function. A family of non-negative real valued functions
\phi=\{\phi_{B}:\,B\in\mathscr{B}\}\subset L^{1}(X)
is said to be
\omega
-regular if

(1)

\|\phi_{B}\|_{L^{1}(X)}=1
,

(2)

there is an increasing function
\gamma:[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
such that

(1.26)

\phi_{B}(x)\leq\gamma\left(\frac{\mu(A)}{\mu(B)}\right)\phi_{A}(x)

for every two balls
B\subset A
.

(3)

there are positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}
such that for any ball
B
we have the inequalities

(1.27)

c_{1}\cdot\frac{\textbf{1}_{B}(x)}{\mu(B)}\leq\phi_{B}(x)\leq c_{2}\cdot\omega\left(\frac{\mu(B)}{d(x,B)}\right)\frac{1}{d(x,B)},\quad x\in X.

Definition 1.10
.

A family of balls
{\mathscr{G}\,}(x)\subset\mathscr{B}
is said to be complete at a point
x\in X
if

(1)

x\in B
for any
B\in{\mathscr{G}\,}(x)
,

(2)

for any ball
A\ni x
(not necessarily from
{\mathscr{G}\,}(x)
) there is a ball
\bar{A}\in{\mathscr{G}\,}(x)
with
\bar{A}\supset A,\quad\mu(\bar{A})\leq\eta\mu(A)
,

where
\eta\geq 1
is a constant.

Let
{\mathscr{G}\,}=\cup_{x\in X}{\mathscr{G}\,}(x)
, where each
{\mathscr{G}\,}(x)
is a family of balls complete at
x
and let
\phi=\{\phi_{B}:\,B\in\mathscr{B}\}
be a
\omega
-regular family of functions on
X
. For
f\in L^{r}(X,\mathbb{U})
and a measurable set
B
we define

(1.28)

\langle f\rangle_{\phi_{B}}=\int_{X}\|f\|_{\mathbb{U}}\phi_{B},

and consider the maximal function

(1.29)

\mathcal{M}^{\phi,{\mathscr{G}\,}}f(x)=\sup_{B\in\mathscr{B}(x)}\langle f\rangle_{\phi_{B}},

which is an operator acting from
L^{r}(X,\mathbb{U})
to
L^{0}(X,\mathbb{R})
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathscr{B}
be a doubling ball-basis. If a modulus of continuity satisfies

(1.30)

\|\omega\|=1+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\omega(t)\log(1/t)}{t}dt<\infty,

then for any function
f\in L^{r}(X,\mathbb{U})
and a ball
B

(1.31)

{\rm OSC}_{B,\alpha}(\mathcal{M}^{\phi,{\mathscr{G}\,}}(f))\lesssim\|\omega\|(1-\alpha)^{-1/r}\langle f\rangle^{*}_{\#,B},\quad B\in\mathscr{B},

for any
0<\alpha<1
.

Thus we can state a corollary analogous to Theorem
1.2
.

Corollary 1.1
.

If a ball-basis
\mathscr{B}
is doubling, then for any
f\in L^{r}(X,\mathbb{U})
and a ball
B
there exist a ball
B^{\prime}\supset B
, a sparse-collection of balls
\mathscr{A}
and a martingale-collection of measurable sets
\bar{}\mathscr{A}\Subset\mathscr{A}
satisfying (

1.15
) such that

(1.32)

|\mathcal{M}^{\phi,{\mathscr{G}\,}}f(x)-m(B)|\lesssim\|\omega\|\sum_{G\in\mathscr{A}}\langle f\rangle_{\#,G}^{*}\textbf{1}_{\bar{G}}(x),\quad x\in B,

where

is a median of the real function
\mathcal{M}^{\phi,{\mathscr{G}\,}}(f)
on

(see Definition

).

Corollary 1.2

Under the assumptions of Theorem

the maximal function

is bounded from

(1.33)

\|\mathcal{M}^{\phi,{\mathscr{G}\,}}(f)\|_{{\rm BMO}(X,\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\rm BMO}(X,\mathbb{U})}

for any function

(1.34)

2.
Preliminary properties of ball bases

Let
\mathscr{B}
be a ball-basis in the measure space
(X,\mu)
. From B4) condition it follows that if balls
A,B
satisfy
A\cap B\neq\varnothing
,
\mu(A)\leq 2\mu(B)
, then
A\subset B^{*}
. This property will be called two balls relation. The notation
B^{*}
in (
1.2
) can be also used for arbitrary measurable set
B
. In particular, the notation
B^{**}
will stand for the
(B^{*})^{*}
. We say a set
E\subset X
is bounded if
E\subset B
for a ball
B\in\mathscr{B}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
(X,\mu)
is a measure space equipped with a ball-basis
\mathscr{B}
and
\mu(X)<\infty
, then
X\in\mathscr{B}
.

Proof.

Fix an arbitrary point
x_{0}\in X
and let
\mathscr{A}\subset\mathscr{B}
be the family of balls containing
x_{0}
. By B2) condition we can write
X=\cup_{A\in\mathscr{A}}A
. Then there exists a
B\in\mathscr{A}
such that
\mu(B)>\frac{1}{2}\sup_{A\in\mathscr{A}}\mu(A)
and using B4) condition we obtain

X=\cup_{A\in\mathscr{A}}A\subset B^{*}\subset[B].

Thus
X=[B]\in\mathscr{B}
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a measure space equipped with a ball-basis
\mathscr{B}
. Then there exists a sequence of balls
G_{1}\subset G_{2}\subset\ldots\subset G_{n}\subset\ldots
such that
X=\cup_{k}G_{k}
. Moreover, for any ball
B
there is a ball
G_{n}\supset B
.

Proof.

Fix an arbitrary
x_{0}\in X
and let
\mathscr{A}\subset\mathscr{B}
be the family of balls containing the point
x_{0}
. Choose a sequence
\eta_{n}\nearrow\eta=\sup_{A\in\mathscr{A}}\mu(A)
, where
\eta
can also be infinity. Let us see by induction that there is an increasing sequence of balls
G_{n}\in\mathscr{A}
such that
\mu(G_{n})>\eta_{n}
and
G_{n}^{*}\subset G_{n+1}
. The base of induction is trivial. Suppose we have already chosen the first balls
G_{k}
,
k=1,2,\ldots,l
. There is a ball
A\in\mathscr{A}
so that
\mu(A)>\eta_{l+1}
. Let
A_{l}
be the biggest in measure among two balls
A
and
G_{l}
. By property B4) we have
A\cup G_{l}\subset A_{l}^{*}
and there exists a ball
G_{l+1}\supset A_{l}^{**}
. This implies
\mu(G_{l+1})\geq\mu(A)>\eta_{l+1}
and
G_{l+1}\supset G_{l}^{*}
, completing the induction.
Let us see that
G_{n}
is our desired sequence of balls. Indeed, let
B
be an arbitrary ball. By B2) property there is a ball
A
containing
x_{0}
, such that
A\cap B\neq\varnothing
. Then by property B4) we may find a ball
C\supset A\cup B
. For some
n
we will have
\mu(C)\leq 2\mu(G_{n})
and so once again using B4), we get
B\subset C\subset G_{n}^{*}\subset G_{n+1}
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a measure space with a ball bases
\mathscr{B}
. If
E\subset X
is bounded and a family of balls
{\mathscr{G}\,}
is a covering of
E
, i.e.
E\subset\bigcup_{G\in{\mathscr{G}\,}}G
, then there exists a finite or infinite sequence of pairwise disjoint balls
G_{k}\in{\mathscr{G}\,}
such that
E\subset\bigcup_{k}G_{k}^{*}
.

Proof.

The boundedness of
E
implies
E\subset B
for some
B\in\mathscr{B}
. If there is a ball
G\in{\mathscr{G}\,}
so that
G\cap B\neq\varnothing
,
\mu(G)>\mu(B)
, then by two balls relation we will have
E\subset B\subset G^{*}
. Thus our desired sequence can be formed by a single element
G
. Hence we can suppose that every
G\in{\mathscr{G}\,}
satisfies
G\cap B\neq\varnothing
,
\mu(G)\leq\mu(B)
and again by two balls relation
G\subset B^{*}
. Therefore,
\bigcup_{G\in{\mathscr{G}\,}}G\subset B^{*}
.
Choose
G_{1}\in{\mathscr{G}\,}
, satisfying
\mu(G_{1})>\frac{1}{2}\sup_{G\in{\mathscr{G}\,}}\mu(G)
. Then, suppose by induction we have already chosen elements
G_{1},\ldots,G_{k}
from
{\mathscr{G}\,}
. Choose
G_{k+1}\in{\mathscr{G}\,}
disjoint with the balls
G_{1},\ldots,G_{k}
such that

(2.1)

\mu(G_{k+1})>\frac{1}{2}\sup_{G\in{\mathscr{G}\,}:\,G\cap G_{j}=\varnothing,\,j=1,\ldots,k}\mu(G).

If for some
n
we will not be able to determine
G_{n+1}
the process will stop and we will get a finite sequence
G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{n}
. Otherwise our sequence will be infinite. We shall consider the infinite case of the sequence (the finite case can be done similarly). Since the balls
G_{n}
are pairwise disjoint and
G_{n}\subset B^{*}
, we have
\mu(G_{n})\to 0
. Choose an arbitrary
G\in{\mathscr{G}\,}
with
G\neq G_{k}
,
k=1,2,\ldots
and let
m
be the smallest integer satisfying

\mu(G)\geq 2\mu(G_{m+1})
.
So we have
G\cap G_{j}\neq\varnothing

for some
1\leq j\leq m
, since otherwise by (
10.20
),
G
had to be chosen instead of
G_{m+1}
. Besides, we have
\mu(G)<2\mu(G_{j})
because of the minimality property of
m
, and so by two balls relation
G\subset G_{j}^{*}
. Since
G\in{\mathscr{G}\,}
was chosen arbitrarily, we get
E\subset\bigcup_{G\in{\mathscr{G}\,}}G\subset\bigcup_{k}G_{k}^{*}
.
∎

Definition 2.1
.

For a measurable set
E\subset X
a point
x\in E
is said to be density point if for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a ball
B\ni x
such that

\mu(B\cap E)>(1-\varepsilon)\mu(B).

We say a ball basis satisfies the density property if for any measurable set
E
almost all points
x\in E
are density points.

Lemma 2.4
.

Every ball basis
\mathscr{B}
satisfies the density condition.

Proof.

Applying Lemma
2.2
one can check that it is enough to establish the density property for the bounded measurable sets. Suppose to the contrary there exist a bounded measurable set
E\subset X
together its subset
F\subset E
(maybe non-measurable) with an outer measure

(2.2)

\mu^{*}(F)=\inf_{A\supset F,\,A\in\mathfrak{M}}\mu(A)>0

such that

(2.3)

\mu(B\setminus E)>\alpha\mu(B)\text{ whenever }B\in\mathscr{B},\,B\cap F\neq\varnothing,

where
0<\alpha<1
. According to the definition of the outer measure one can find a measurable set
\bar{F}
such that

(2.4)

F\subset\bar{F}\subset E,\quad\mu(\bar{F})<\mu^{*}(F)+\varepsilon.

By B3)-condition there is a sequence of balls
B_{k}
,
k=1,2,\ldots
, such that

(2.5)

\mu\left(\bar{F}\bigtriangleup(\cup_{k}B_{k})\right)<\varepsilon.

We discuss two kind of balls
B_{k}
, satisfying either
B_{k}\cap F=\varnothing
or
B_{k}\cap F\neq\varnothing
. For the first collection we have

\mu^{*}(F)\leq\mu\left(\bar{F}\setminus\bigcup_{k:\,B_{k}\cap F=\varnothing}B_{k}\right)\leq\mu(\bar{F})<\mu^{*}(F)+\varepsilon,

which implies

\mu\left(\bar{F}\bigcap\left(\bigcup_{k:\,B_{k}\cap F=\varnothing}B_{k}\right)\right)=\mu(\bar{F})-\mu\left(\bar{F}\setminus\bigcup_{k:\,B_{k}\cap F=\varnothing}B_{k}\right)<\varepsilon.

Thus, combining also (
2.5
) we obtain

(2.6)

\mu\left(\bigcup_{k:\,B_{k}\cap F=\varnothing}B_{k}\right)<2\varepsilon.

For the balls
B_{k}\cap F\neq\varnothing
, using (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) we have

(2.7)

\mu(B_{k}\setminus\bar{F})\geq\mu(B_{k}\setminus E)>\alpha\mu(B_{k}),\quad k=1,2,\ldots.

Since
E
and so
\bar{F}
are bounded, applying Lemma
2.3
and (
2.5
), one can find a subsequence of pairwise disjoint balls
\tilde{B}_{k}
,
k=1,2,\ldots
, such that

\mu\left(\bar{F}\setminus\cup_{k}\tilde{B}_{k}^{*}\right)<\varepsilon.

Thus, from B4)-condition, (
2.5
), (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mu^{*}(F)<\mu(\bar{F})

\displaystyle\leq\mu\left(\cup_{k}\tilde{B}_{k}^{*}\right)+\varepsilon\leq\mathcal{K}\sum_{k}\mu(\tilde{B}_{k})+\varepsilon

\displaystyle=\mathcal{K}\mu\left(\bigcup_{k:\,\tilde{B}_{k}\cap F=\varnothing}\tilde{B}_{k}\right)+\mathcal{K}\sum_{k:\,\tilde{B}_{k}\cap F\neq\varnothing}\mu(\tilde{B}_{k})+\varepsilon

\displaystyle<2\mathcal{K}\varepsilon+\frac{\mathcal{K}}{\alpha}\sum_{k}\mu(\tilde{B}_{k}\setminus\bar{F})+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq(2\mathcal{K}+1)\varepsilon+\frac{\mathcal{K}}{\alpha}\mu\left(\bar{F}\bigtriangleup(\cup_{k}B_{k})\right)<\varepsilon\left(2\mathcal{K}+1+\frac{\mathcal{K}}{\alpha}\right).

Since
\varepsilon
can be arbitrarily small, we get
\mu(F)=0
and so a contradiction.
∎

Denote by
\#E
the number of elements of a finite set
E
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{B}
and
{\mathscr{G}\,}
be a family of pairwise disjoint balls such that each
G\in{\mathscr{G}\,}
satisfies the relations

(2.8)

\displaystyle G^{*}\cap A\neq\varnothing,

(2.9)

\displaystyle 0<c_{1}\leq\mu(G)\leq c_{2}.

Then
{\mathscr{G}\,}
is finite and for the number of its elements we have
\#{\mathscr{G}\,}\lesssim\max\{c_{2},\mu(A)\}/c_{1}
.

Proof.

Suppose
G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{N}
are some elements of
{\mathscr{G}\,}
with hull-balls satisfying
\mu([G_{1}])\geq\max_{j}\mu([G_{i}])
. Consider the ball

\displaystyle B=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}A&\hbox{ if }\mu(A)>\mu([G_{1}]),\\
\left[G_{1}\right]&\hbox{ if }\mu(A)\leq\mu([G_{1}]).\\
\end{array}\right.

Using (
2.8
), one can check that for both cases in (
2
) we have
G_{k}\subset[G_{k}]\subset B^{**}
,
k=1,\ldots,N
. Thus, since
G_{k}
are pairwise disjoint, from (
2.9
) we obtain

(2.12)

\displaystyle N\cdot c_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\mu\left(\cup_{k}G_{k}\right)\leq\mu(B^{**})

(2.13)

\displaystyle\leq\max\{\mu(A^{**}),\mu([G_{1}]^{**})\}\lesssim\max\{c_{2},\mu(A)\},

which completes the proof of lemma.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

One can prove a similar lemma with
G^{**}\cap A\neq\varnothing
instead of (

).

Lemma 2.6
.

If
\mathscr{B}
is a doubling ball-basis, then for any ball
B\in\mathscr{B}
with
B^{*}\neq X
there is a ball
B^{\prime}
, satisfying

(2.14)

B\subsetneq B^{\prime},\quad 2\mu(B)\leq\mu(B^{\prime})\leq\eta\mathcal{K}\mu(B).

Proof.

Let
B\in\mathscr{B}
,
B^{*}\neq X
. Suppose to the contrary there is no ball
B^{\prime}
satisfying (
2.14
) and denote

\mathscr{A}=\{A\in\mathscr{B}:\,B\subseteq A,\,\mu(A)\leq\eta\mathcal{K}\mu(B)\}.

According to the assumption every
A\in\mathscr{A}
should satisfy the bound
\mu(A)<2\mu(B)
. This and B4) imply

\bigcup_{A\in\mathscr{A}}A\subset B^{*}\subset[B]\in\mathscr{B}
, where
\mu([B])\leq\mathcal{K}\mu(B)
. Thus, we have
[B]\in\mathscr{A}
and so

\bigcup_{A\in\mathscr{A}}A=B^{*}=[B]
. On the other hand, according to the doubling condition, there is a ball

A^{\prime}\supsetneq[B]

such that
\mu(A^{\prime})\leq\eta\cdot\mu([B])\leq\eta\mathcal{K}\mu(B)
. The latter implies
A^{\prime}\in\mathscr{A}
and therefore
A^{\prime}\subset[B]
that gives a contradiction. Lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mathscr{B}
be a doubling ball basis. Then for any balls

there exists a sequence of balls
A=A_{0}\subset A_{1}\subset\ldots\subset A_{n}=[B]
such that
\mu(A_{k+1})\lesssim\mu(A_{k})
.

The case of
\mu(B)\leq 2\mu([A])
is trivial, we may simply choose

and

. So we can suppose
\mu(B)>2\mu([A])

we may find a sequence of balls

. Obviously, we may define our desired sequence by

,

and

,

.
∎

3.

For two measurable sets

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
F
and

be measurable sets, where
E
is bounded as well. Then there exists a countable family of balls
{\mathscr{G}\,}={\mathscr{G}\,}(E)
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle E\subset_{a.e.}\bigcup_{G\in{\mathscr{G}\,}}G,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{G\in{\mathscr{G}\,}}\mu(G)\leq 2\mathcal{K}\mu(F).

Besides, for any ball

we have

(3.3)

\mu(G^{\prime}\cap F)<\mu(G^{\prime})/2.

If
G
is doubling, then (
3.3
) (with the constant
2\eta\mathcal{K}
on the right-hand side) holds also for

.

Proof.

First consider a general ball-basis. Let
D
be the density points set of

(see Definition

the family of balls

{\mathscr{C}}_{x}=\{C\in\mathscr{B}:\,x\in C,\,\mu(C\cap F)\geq\mu(C)/2\}

is nonempty and therefore there is a ball

such that
\mu(B_{x})>\frac{1}{2}\sup_{C\in{\mathscr{C}}_{x}}\mu(C)

From the two ball relation it follows that
\cup_{C\in{\mathscr{C}}_{x}}C\subset B_{x}^{*}
.
Thus, if a ball
G^{\prime}
satisfies

(3.4)

\mu(G^{\prime}\cap F)<\mu(G^{\prime})/2.

Since

is a ball-covering for

, applying Lemma

, we find sequence of pairwise disjoint balls
\{B_{x_{k}}\}
such that

(3.5)

E\cap D\subset\cup_{k}B_{x_{k}}^{*}.

Choose a hull-ball
G_{k}\supset B_{x_{k}}^{*}
such that

. We claim that the family

satisfies the conditions of lemma. Indeed, (

(3.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\mu(G_{k})\leq\mathcal{K}\sum_{k}\mu(B_{x_{k}})<2\mathcal{K}\sum_{k}\mu(B_{x_{k}}\cap F)\leq 2\mathcal{K}\mu(F)

that gives (

satisfying

) for

.

Definition 3.1
.

A family of balls
\mathscr{A}
is said to be a tree-collection with a root-ball

if for each

there is an attached children-collection

(it can also be empty),

with

,

. The relation

otherwise will be denoted by

, meaning that
A
is the parent of
G
. We say
G
is in the generation of a ball

if
G\in{\mathfrak{Ch}}^{n}(A)
for some

. The generation of a ball

will be denoted by
{\mathfrak{Gen}}(A)=\cup_{n\geq 1}{\mathfrak{Ch}}^{n}(A)
. By the definition for the root-ball we have

.

The notation

(
n\gg m
) for two integers

denotes

(
n>m+1
) and

will stand for the condition
|m-n|\leq 1
.

.

Let

, where

. Then for any ball
A_{0}
one can find a countable sparse-tree-collection of balls

containing a double-hull ball

as a root-ball, such that

G\in{\mathfrak{Ch}}(A)\,\Rightarrow\,G\subset A

\bar{B}_{1}\subset B_{2}^{*}
and therefore

\langle f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus B^{*}}\rangle_{\bar{B}_{1}}\leq\left(\frac{\mu(B_{2}^{*})}{\mu(\bar{B}_{1})}\right)^{\rho}\langle f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus B^{*}}\rangle_{B_{2}^{*}}\leq(\gamma\mathcal{K})^{\rho}\langle f\rangle^{*}_{B}.

Otherwise, if
\mu(\bar{B}_{1})\geq\mu(B_{2})
, then we will have

B\subset B_{2}\subset\bar{B}_{1}^{*}
and then

(4.9)

\langle f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus B^{*}}\rangle_{\bar{B}_{1}}\leq\mathcal{K}^{\rho}\langle f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus B^{*}}\rangle_{\bar{B}_{1}^{*}}\leq\mathcal{K}^{\rho}\langle f\rangle_{B}^{*}.

Thus we obtain (
4.6
).
∎

Definition 4.1
.

Let
T
be a sublinear operator. Given measurable sets
A
and
B\supset A
we denote

(4.10)

\Delta(A,B)=\Delta_{T}(A,B)=\sup_{x\in A,\,f\neq 0\in L^{r}(X,\mathbb{U})}\frac{\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{B^{*}\setminus A^{*}})(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}}{\langle f\rangle_{B^{*}}}.

Notice that T2)-condition for a sublinear operator
T
means that for any
A\in\mathscr{B}
there exists a ball
B\supsetneq A
such that
\Delta(A,B)\leq\mathcal{L}_{2}
. The following lemma clearly follows from definition (
4.10
).

Lemma 4.2
.

If
T
is an arbitrary sublinear operator, then for any balls
A
,
B
and
C
satisfying
A\subset B\subset C
we have

(4.11)

\Delta(A,B)\leq\Delta(A,C).

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{r}(X,\mathbb{U})
,
A\subset B\subset C
and
x\in B
. We have

(4.12)

\displaystyle\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{B^{*}\setminus A^{*}})(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}

\displaystyle=\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{B^{*}\setminus A^{*}}\cdot\textbf{1}_{C^{*}\setminus A^{*}})(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}

(4.13)

\displaystyle\leq\Delta(A,C)\langle f\cdot\textbf{1}_{B^{*}\setminus A^{*}}\rangle_{C^{*}}\leq\Delta(A,C)\langle f\rangle_{B^{*}}

that completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

If a sublinear operator
T
satisfies T0) and T1)-conditions, then for any
\gamma
-balls
A,B
and
C
, satisfying
A\subset B\subset C
, we have

(4.14)

\Delta(A,C)\lesssim\left(\frac{\mu(C)}{\mu(B)}\right)^{\rho}\left(\mathcal{L}_{0}(T)+\gamma^{\rho}\mathcal{L}_{1}(T)+\Delta(A,B)\right).

Proof.

First consider the case when
A=B
and so
\Delta(A,B)=0
. Applying (
1.9
) with

(4.15)

\lambda=\mathcal{L}_{0}\cdot\left(\frac{2\mu(C^{*})}{\mu(B)}\right)^{\rho}\langle f\rangle_{C^{*}}=\mathcal{L}_{0}\cdot\left(\frac{2}{\mu(B)}\right)^{\rho}\left(\int_{C^{*}}\|f|\|_{\mathbb{U}}^{r}\right)^{\varrho}

we get

\displaystyle\mu\left\{x\in B:\,\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{C^{*}\setminus B^{*}})(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}>\mathcal{L}_{0}\cdot\left(\frac{2\mu(C^{*})}{\mu(B)}\right)^{\rho}\langle f\rangle_{C^{*}}\right\}\leq\frac{\mu(B)}{2}

and so we find a point
x_{0}\in B
such that

(4.16)

\displaystyle\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{C^{*}\setminus B^{*}})\}(x_{0})\|_{\mathbb{V}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}_{0}\cdot\left(\frac{2\mu(C^{*})}{\mu(B)}\right)^{\rho}\langle f\rangle_{C^{*}}

(4.17)

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{L}_{0}\cdot\left(\frac{\mu(C)}{\mu(B)}\right)^{\rho}\langle f\rangle_{C^{*}}.

According to T1)-condition and Lemma
4.1
, for any
x\in B
we also have

(4.18)

\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{C^{*}\setminus B^{*}})(x)-T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{C^{*}\setminus B^{*}})(x_{0})\|_{\mathbb{V}}\leq\gamma^{\rho}\mathcal{L}_{1}\langle f\cdot\textbf{1}_{C^{*}\setminus B^{*}}\rangle^{*}_{B}.

By the definition of
\langle f\rangle^{*}_{B}
there is a ball
G\supset B
such that

(4.19)

\langle f\cdot\textbf{1}_{C^{*}\setminus B^{*}}\rangle^{*}_{B}<2\langle f\cdot\textbf{1}_{C^{*}\setminus B^{*}}\rangle_{G}.

If
\mu(G)\leq\mu(C)
, then we have
G\subset C^{*}
and therefore

(4.20)

\displaystyle\langle f\cdot\textbf{1}_{C^{*}\setminus B^{*}}\rangle_{G}\leq\left(\frac{\mu(C^{*})}{\mu(G)}\right)^{\rho}\langle f\cdot\textbf{1}_{C^{*}\setminus B^{*}}\rangle_{C^{*}}\lesssim\left(\frac{\mu(C)}{\mu(B)}\right)^{\rho}\cdot\langle f\rangle_{C^{*}}.

If
\mu(G)>\mu(C)
, then
C^{*}\subset G^{**}
. Hence we get

(4.21)

\displaystyle\langle f\cdot\textbf{1}_{C^{*}\setminus B^{*}}\rangle_{G}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\mu(G^{**})}{\mu(G)}\right)^{\rho}\langle f\cdot\textbf{1}_{C^{*}\setminus B^{*}}\rangle_{G^{**}}

(4.22)

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle f\cdot\textbf{1}_{C^{*}\setminus B^{*}}\rangle_{C^{*}}\leq\langle f\rangle_{C^{*}}.

The combination of (
4.19
), (
4.20
) and (
4.22
) imply the inequality

\langle f\cdot\textbf{1}_{C^{*}\setminus B^{*}}\rangle^{*}_{B}\lesssim\left(\frac{\mu(C)}{\mu(B)}\right)^{\rho}\cdot\langle f\rangle_{C^{*}},

which together with (
4.17
) and (
4.18
) gives

\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{C^{*}\setminus B^{*}})(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}\lesssim\left(\frac{\mu(C)}{\mu(A)}\right)^{\rho}(\mathcal{L}_{0}+\gamma^{\rho}\mathcal{L}_{1})\langle f\rangle_{C^{*}},\quad x\in A.

The latter implies

\Delta(B,C)\lesssim\left(\frac{\mu(C)}{\mu(B)}\right)^{\rho}(\mathcal{L}_{0}+\gamma^{\rho}\mathcal{L}_{1})

that is (
4.14
) if
A=B
. Now let
A
be arbitrary. Thus, for
f\in L^{r}(X,\mathbb{U})
and
x\in A
we have

\displaystyle\|T(f\cdot

\displaystyle\textbf{1}_{C^{*}\setminus A^{*}})(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}

\displaystyle\leq\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{C^{*}\setminus B^{*}})(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}+\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{B^{*}\setminus A^{*}})(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Delta(B,C)\langle f\rangle_{C^{*}}+\Delta(A,B)\langle f\rangle_{B^{*}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{\mu(C)}{\mu(B)}\right)^{\rho}(\mathcal{L}_{0}+\gamma^{\rho}\mathcal{L}_{1})\langle f\rangle_{C^{*}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\frac{\mu(C^{*})}{\mu(B^{*})}\right)^{\rho}\Delta(A,B)\langle f\rangle_{C^{*}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{\mu(C)}{\mu(B)}\right)^{\rho}(\mathcal{L}_{0}+\gamma^{\rho}\mathcal{L}_{1}+\Delta(A,B))\langle f\rangle_{C^{*}},

which is the full version of (
4.14
).
∎

Remark 4.1
.

We will often use the following particular case of inequality (
4.14
), that is

(4.23)

\Delta(A,B)\lesssim\left(\frac{\mu(B)}{\mu(A)}\right)^{\rho}\left(\mathcal{L}_{0}+\gamma^{\rho}\mathcal{L}_{1}\right).

Lemma 4.4
.

If
T
is a
{\rm BO}
operator, then for any
\gamma
-ball
A\in\mathscr{B}
(
A^{*}\neq X
) there exists a ball
B\supsetneq A
such that

(4.24)

\Delta(A,B)\lesssim\gamma^{\rho}\mathcal{L}_{0}+\gamma^{2\rho}\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2},

whenever
f\in L^{r}(X,\mathbb{U})
.

Proof.

Let
A
be a
\gamma
-ball that is
A_{1}\subset A\subset A_{2}
,
\mu(A_{2})\leq\gamma\mu(A_{1})
for some balls
A_{1},A_{2}
. If
A=A_{2}
, then by T2) we will have (
4.24
) with a constant
\mathcal{L}_{2}
. If
A\neq A_{2}
, then, applying (
4.23
) we obtain

(4.25)

\Delta(A,A_{2})\lesssim\left(\frac{\mu(A_{2})}{\mu(A)}\right)^{\rho}\left(\mathcal{L}_{0}+\gamma^{\rho}\mathcal{L}_{1}\right)\leq\gamma^{\rho}\mathcal{L}_{0}+\gamma^{2\rho}\mathcal{L}_{1}.

Thus we get (
4.24
) with
B=A_{2}
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Taking into account Lemmas
4.1
and
4.4
, in the definition of
{\rm BO}
operators we can equivalently consider
\lambda
-balls instead of real balls.

For any a ball
A\in\mathscr{B}
,
A\neq X
, denote

\phi(A)=\inf_{B\in\mathscr{B}:\,B\supsetneq A}\mu(B).

From Lemma
2.2
it easily follows that the set of balls
B
satisfying
B\supsetneq A
is nonempty and so the number
\phi(A)
is precisely determined as
A\neq X
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
T
is a
{\rm BO}
operator and
A
be a ball. Then for any ball
B\supsetneq A
such that
\mu(B)\leq 2\phi(A)
we have

(4.26)

\Delta(A,B)\lesssim\mathcal{L}_{0}(T)+\mathcal{L}_{1}(T)+\mathcal{L}_{2}(T).

Proof.

Let balls
B\supsetneq A
satisfy the conditions of lemma. Using T2)-condition, we find a ball
C\supsetneq A
such that

(4.27)

\Delta(A,C)\leq\mathcal{L}_{2}(T).

If
\mu(C)>\mu(B)
, then we have
B\subset C^{*}
. Thus, applying Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
, we obtain

(4.28)

\displaystyle\Delta(A,B)\leq\Delta(A,C^{*})

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{L}_{0}(T)+\mathcal{L}_{1}(T)+\Delta(A,C)

(4.29)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}_{0}(T)+\mathcal{L}_{1}(T)+\mathcal{L}_{2}(T).

In the case
\mu(C)\leq\mu(B)
we have
C\subset B^{*}
, and since
C\supsetneq A
, we obtain

\mu(B)\gtrsim\mu(B^{*})\geq\mu(C)\geq\phi(A)\geq\frac{\mu(B)}{2}.

Thus, once again applying Lemmas
4.2
,
4.3
and (
4.27
), we conclude

(4.30)

\displaystyle\Delta(A,B)

\displaystyle\leq\Delta(A,B^{*})\lesssim\mathcal{L}_{0}(T)+\mathcal{L}_{1}(T)+\Delta(A,C)

(4.31)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}_{0}(T)+\mathcal{L}_{1}(T)+\mathcal{L}_{2}(T).

This completes the proof of lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
T
be a
{\rm BO}
operator. For any balls
A
and
B
such that
A\cap B\neq\varnothing
and
\mu(A)<\mu(B)
there exists a ball
\tilde{A}
such that
A\subsetneq\tilde{A}
,
\tilde{A}^{*}\subset B^{*}
and

(4.32)

\Delta(A,\tilde{A})\lesssim\mathcal{L}_{0}+\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2}

One can also write
A^{*}
and
\tilde{A}^{*}
in (
4.32
) instead of

and
\tilde{A}
respectively.

Proof.

Let a pair
(G,A)
of balls

satisfy T2) condition, that is
\Delta(A,G)\leq\mathcal{L}_{2}
. If for a hull-ball
[G]
we have

, then

and one can choose

4.3.
Truncated
{\rm BO}
operators

For a
{\rm BO}
operator (
1.4
) we consider the truncated operator

T^{*}f(x)=\sup_{B\in\mathscr{B}:\,x\in B}\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus B^{*}})(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}.

Theorem 4.2
.

If a sublinear operator
T
satisfies T0) and T1)-conditions, then

(4.33)

\mu\{x\in X:\,T^{*}f(x)>\lambda,\,\mathcal{M}f(x)<\delta\lambda\}\lesssim\mu\{x\in X:\,\|Tf(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}>\lambda/2\}

with a constant
\delta\sim(\mathcal{L}_{1}(T)+\mathcal{L}_{0}(T))^{-1}
.

Proof.

Denote by
E=E_{\lambda}
the set on the left of (
4.33
), which can also be non-measurable. By the definition of
T^{*}
for any
x\in E
there is a ball
B(x)\in\mathscr{B}
such that

(4.34)

x\in B(x),\quad\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus B^{*}(x)})(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}>\lambda.

One can check that
E\subset\cup_{x\in E}B(x)
and

(4.35)

\langle f\rangle_{B(x)}^{*}\leq\mathcal{M}f(x)<\delta\lambda,\quad x\in E.

Given ball
G
apply Lemma
2.3
, we find a sequence
x_{k}\in E
such that the balls
\{B_{k}=B(x_{k})\}
are pairwise disjoint and

(4.36)

E\cap G\subset\cup_{k}B^{*}_{k}=Q(G).

Since
T
satisfies T1)-condition, by (
4.35
) we obtain

\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus B^{*}_{k}})(x_{k})-T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus B^{*}_{k}})(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}\leq\mathcal{L}_{1}\cdot\langle f\rangle^{*}_{B_{k}}\leq\mathcal{L}_{1}\delta\lambda,\quad x\in B_{k}.

Thus, we conclude from (
4.34
) that

(4.37)

\displaystyle\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus B^{*}_{k}})(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}

\displaystyle\geq\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus B^{*}_{k}})(x_{k})\|_{\mathbb{V}}

(4.38)

\displaystyle\qquad-\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus B^{*}_{k}})(x_{k})-T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus B^{*}_{k}})(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}

(4.39)

\displaystyle\geq\lambda(1-\mathcal{L}_{1}\delta),\quad x\in B_{k}.

For a
\beta>0
we define measurable sets

(4.40)

\tilde{B}_{k}=\{x\in B_{k}:\,\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{B_{k}^{*}})(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}\leq\beta\cdot\langle f\rangle^{*}_{B_{k},r}\}.

Using T0)-condition, the measure of the complement of
\tilde{B}_{k}
is estimated by

\displaystyle\mu(\tilde{B}_{k}^{c})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\mathcal{L}_{0})^{1/\rho}}{(\beta\cdot\langle f\rangle^{*}_{B_{k},r})^{1/\rho}}\cdot\left(\int_{B_{k}^{*}}|f|^{r}\right)^{\varrho/\rho}\leq\left(\frac{\mathcal{L}_{0}}{\beta}\right)^{1/\rho}\mu(B_{k}^{*})

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{\mathcal{L}_{0}}{\beta}\right)^{1/\rho}\mu(B_{k}).

Using also (
4.35
), for an appropriate constant
\beta\sim\mathcal{L}_{0}
we have

(4.41)

\displaystyle\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{B_{k}^{*}})(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}\leq\beta\delta\lambda,\quad x\in\tilde{B}_{k},

(4.42)

\displaystyle\mu(\tilde{B}_{k})\geq\mu(B_{k})-\mu(\tilde{B}_{k}^{c})\geq\mu(B_{k})/2.

If
x\in\tilde{B}_{k}
, then, using sublinearity of
T
together with relations (
4.37
) and (
4.41
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|Tf(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus B_{k}^{*}})(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}-\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{B_{k}^{*}})(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}

\displaystyle\geq\lambda(1-\mathcal{L}_{1}\delta-\beta\delta)\geq\lambda/2,

where the last inequality can be satisfied for
\delta=1/2(\mathcal{L}_{1}+\beta)\sim(\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{0})^{-1}
.
Hence we conclude
\cup_{k}\tilde{B}_{k}\subset\{\|Tf(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}>\lambda/2\}

and taking into account also (
4.36
) and (
4.42
), we get

\displaystyle\mu(E\cap G)\leq\mu(Q(G))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k}\mu(B_{k}^{*})\leq\mathcal{K}\cdot\sum_{k}\mu(B_{k})

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathcal{K}\cdot\sum_{k}\mu(\tilde{B}_{k})\leq 2\mathcal{K}\mu\{\|Tf(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}>\lambda/2\}.

Since
G
is arbitrary, applying Lemma
2.2
we complete the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

If
T
is a
{\rm BO}
operator, then
T^{*}
is also a
{\rm BO}
operator. Moreover, we have

(4.43)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}(T^{*})\lesssim\mathcal{L}_{0}(T)+\mathcal{L}_{1}(T),

(4.44)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}(T^{*})\lesssim\mathcal{L}_{0}(T)+\mathcal{L}_{1}(T),

(4.45)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2}(T^{*})=\mathcal{L}_{2}(T),

Proof.

Weak type inequality (
1.9
) or condition (
4.43
) easily follows from Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
. One can also check that for any balls
A,B
satisfying
A\subset B
we have

\Delta_{T^{*}}(A,B)=\Delta_{T}(A,B).

Thus, if balls
A
and
B
satisfy T2)-condition for the operator
T
, then the same conditions hold also for
T^{*}
with
\mathcal{L}_{2}(T^{*})=\mathcal{L}_{2}(T)
.
To prove (
4.44
), let
B\in\mathscr{B}
and
f\in L^{r}(X,\mathbb{U})
satisfy

(4.46)

{\rm supp\,}f\in X\setminus B^{*}.

Choose arbitrary points
x,x^{\prime}\in B
and let estimate
|T^{*}f(x)-T^{*}f(x^{\prime})|
. If
T^{*}f(x)=T^{*}f(x^{\prime})
, then the estimation is trivial. So we can suppose that
T^{*}f(x)>T^{*}f(x^{\prime})
. Using the definition of
T^{*}f(x)
, we find a ball
A\ni x
such that

(4.47)

\frac{T^{*}f(x)+T^{*}f(x^{\prime})}{2}<\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus A^{*}})(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}.

First suppose that
\mu(A^{*})>\mu(B)
. So we can say that
x^{\prime}\in B\subset A^{**}\subset A^{\prime}
, where
A^{\prime}
is a ball satisfying
\mu(A^{\prime})\leq\mathcal{K}^{2}\mu(A)
. By definition of
T^{*}
we can write

(4.48)

T^{*}f(x^{\prime})\geq\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus A^{\prime*}})(x^{\prime})\|_{\mathbb{V}},

which together with (
4.47
) yields

(4.49)

\displaystyle|T^{*}f(x)-T^{*}f(x^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=T^{*}f(x)-T^{*}f(x^{\prime})

(4.50)

\displaystyle<2\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus A^{*}})(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}-2T^{*}f(x^{\prime})

(4.51)

\displaystyle\leq 2\bigg(\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus A^{*}})(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}-\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus A^{\prime*}})(x^{\prime})\|_{\mathbb{V}}\bigg).

Besides, since
x\in A
, from (
4.23
) (or Lemma
4.3
) and (
4.46
) it follows that

(4.52)

\displaystyle\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{A^{\prime*}\setminus A^{*}})(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}

\displaystyle\leq\Delta(A,A^{\prime})\langle f\cdot\textbf{1}_{A^{\prime*}\setminus A^{*}}\rangle_{A^{\prime*}}

(4.53)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{\mu(A^{\prime})}{\mu(A)}\right)^{\rho}(\mathcal{L}_{0}(T)+\mathcal{L}_{1}(T))\langle f\rangle_{A^{\prime*}},

(4.54)

\displaystyle\lesssim(\mathcal{L}_{0}(T)+\mathcal{L}_{1}(T))\langle f\rangle^{*}_{B},

Thus, applying the T1)-condition for
T
, from (
4.54
) and (
4.51
) we conclude

\displaystyle|T^{*}f(x)-T^{*}f(x^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq 2\bigg(\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus A^{\prime*}})(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}+\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{A^{\prime*}\setminus A^{*}})(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}

\displaystyle\qquad-\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus A^{\prime*}})(x^{\prime})\|_{\mathbb{V}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus A^{\prime*}})(x)-T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus A^{\prime*}})(x^{\prime})\|_{\mathbb{V}}

\displaystyle\qquad+(\mathcal{L}_{0}(T)+\mathcal{L}_{1}(T))\langle f\rangle^{*}_{B}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\mathcal{L}_{0}(T)+\mathcal{L}_{1}(T))\langle f\rangle^{*}_{B}

that implies (
4.44
).
Now suppose
\mu(A^{*})\leq\mu(B)
and therefore
A^{*}\subset B^{*}
. Then by (
4.46
) we get
f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus A^{*}}=f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus B^{*}}
. Thus, using the same argument, we can write

(4.55)

\displaystyle|T^{*}f(x)-T^{*}f(x^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=T^{*}f(x)-T^{*}f(x^{\prime})

(4.56)

\displaystyle\leq 2\bigg(\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus B^{*}})(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}-\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus B^{*}})(x^{\prime})\|_{\mathbb{V}}\bigg)

(4.57)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}_{1}(T)\langle f\rangle^{*}_{B}

that again gives (
4.44
).
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

If
\{T_{\alpha}\}
is a family of
{\rm BO}
operators with uniformly bounded characteristic constants
\mathcal{L}_{j}(T_{\alpha})
, then the maximal operator

(4.58)

Tf(x)=\sup_{\alpha}\|T_{\alpha}f(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}

satisfies the bounds

(4.59)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}(T)\lesssim\sup_{\alpha}\mathcal{L}_{1}(T_{\alpha}),

(4.60)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2}(T)\lesssim\sup_{\alpha}(\mathcal{L}_{0}(T_{\alpha})+\mathcal{L}_{1}(T_{\alpha})+\mathcal{L}_{2}(T_{\alpha})).

Proof.

Let
A\in\mathscr{B}
be an arbitrary ball and
{\rm supp\,}f\subset X\setminus A^{*}
.
Take arbitrary points
x,x^{\prime}\in A
and suppose that
Tf(x)\geq Tf(x^{\prime})
.
According to the definition of
T
, for any
\delta>0
there exists an index
\alpha_{0}
such that
Tf(x)\leq\|T_{\alpha_{0}}f(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}+\delta
.
On the other hand for the same
\alpha_{0}
we have
Tf(x^{\prime})\geq\|T_{\alpha_{0}}f(x^{\prime})\|_{\mathbb{V}}
. Thus, applying T1) property of
T_{\alpha_{0}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|Tf(x)-Tf(x^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=Tf(x)-Tf(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\|T_{\alpha_{0}}f(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}+\delta-\|T_{\alpha_{0}}f(x^{\prime})\|_{\mathbb{V}}

\displaystyle\leq\|T_{\alpha_{0}}f(x)-T_{\alpha_{0}}f(x^{\prime})\|_{\mathbb{V}}+\delta

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\alpha}\mathcal{L}_{1}(T_{\alpha})\cdot\langle f\rangle^{*}_{A}+\delta.

Since
\delta>0
can be arbitrarily small, we get (
4.59
). (
4.60
) follows from Lemma
4.5
, according which we can choose a ball
B
independent of the operators
T_{\alpha}
such that

(4.61)

\Delta_{T_{\alpha}}(A,B)\lesssim\mathcal{L}_{0}(T_{\alpha})+\mathcal{L}_{1}(T_{\alpha})+\mathcal{L}_{2}(T_{\alpha})

for any
\alpha
. This implies (
4.60
), completing the proof of theorem.
∎

Corollary 4.1
.

If
\{T_{\alpha}\}
is a family of
{\rm BO}
operators with uniformly bounded characteristic constants
\mathcal{L}_{j}(T_{\alpha})
and the maximal operator
T
in (
4.58
) satisfies condition T0), then

6.

Denote the

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon+\|Tf_{1}(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}+\|Tf_{1}(x^{\prime})\|_{\mathbb{V}}+\|T_{\alpha}f_{2}(x)-T_{\alpha}f_{2}(x^{\prime})\|_{\mathbb{V}}

(7.21)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1-\beta)^{-1/r}\mathcal{L}_{0}(T)\langle f\rangle^{*}_{\#,B}

(7.22)

\displaystyle\qquad+\|T_{\alpha}f_{2}(x)-T_{\alpha}f_{2}(x^{\prime})\|_{\mathbb{V}}.

Applying T1)-condition, we get

(7.23)

\displaystyle\|T_{\alpha}f_{2}(x)-T_{\alpha}f_{2}(x^{\prime})\|_{\mathbb{V}}

\displaystyle\leq{\rm OSC}_{B}(T_{\alpha}(f_{2}))

(7.24)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}_{1}(T_{\alpha})\sup_{C\in\mathscr{B}\,C\supset B}\log^{-1}\left(1+\frac{\mu(C)}{\mu(B)}\right)\langle f_{2}\rangle_{C}

(7.25)

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathcal{L}_{1}(T_{\alpha})\log^{-1}\left(1+\frac{\mu(A)}{\mu(B)}\right)\langle f_{2}\rangle_{A}

for some ball
A\supset B
.
Clearly we can suppose that
\mu(A)>2\mu(B)
, since otherwise we would have
A\subset B^{*}
that means
\langle f_{2}\rangle_{A,r}=0
and so the left hand side of (
7.25
) is zero. Thus, applying Lemma
7.2
, we obtain

(7.26)

\displaystyle\|T_{\alpha}f_{2}(x)-T_{\alpha}f_{2}(x^{\prime})\|_{\mathbb{V}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathcal{L}_{1}(T_{\alpha})\log^{-1}\left(1+\frac{\mu(A)}{\mu(B)}\right)\langle f-f_{B}\rangle_{A}

(7.27)

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathcal{L}_{1}(T_{\alpha})\log^{-1}\left(1+\frac{\mu(A)}{\mu(B)}\right)(\langle f-f_{A}\rangle_{A}+|f_{A}-f_{B}|)

(7.28)

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{L}_{1}(T_{\alpha})\log^{-1}\left(1+\frac{\mu(A)}{\mu(B)}\right)\log\left(1+\frac{\mu(A)}{\mu(B)}\right)\langle f\rangle^{*}_{\#,B}

(7.29)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{1}(T_{\alpha})\langle f\rangle^{*}_{\#,B}.

Thus, combining (
7.22
) and (
7.29
), we obtain (
1.24
).
∎

Proof of Theorem

Let

Combining (

)

8.
Exponential inequalities

In this section we provide applications of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
in exponential decay inequalities as a special types of good-lambda inequalities.
First good-lambda inequalities arreared in the 1970’s and till are significant tools in the study of operators in harmonic analysis. Originally the good-lambda technique was introduced by Burkholder and Gundy in
[
4
]
,
[
3
]
, where the method was applied to operators generated by martingales. Later, Coifman and Fefferman
[
8
]
proved a good-lambda inequality involving the truncated Calderón-Zygmund operator
T^{*}
and the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Namely they established

(8.1)

w\{T^{*}(f)>2\lambda,\,M(f)<\beta\lambda\}|\leq c(\beta)w\{T^{*}(f)>\lambda\},\quad\lambda>0,

where
w
is an arbitrary
A_{\infty}
measure and
c(\beta)\to 0
as
\beta\to 0
. This inequality provides a control of
T^{*}
by mean of the maximal function
M
, namely one can easily deduce the bound
\|T^{*}f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq c\|Mf\|_{L^{p}(w)}
.
The classical proof of (
8.1
) is based on a local type good-lambda inequality

(8.2)

|\{x\in B:\,T^{*}f(x)>2\lambda,\,Mf(x)<\beta\lambda\}|\leq c\beta^{\delta}|B|,\quad\lambda>0,

where
B
is an Euclidean cube in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In fact, to deduce (
8.1
) from (
8.2
) one just need to apply (
8.2
) to the cubes of the Whitney decomposition of the set
\{T^{*}(f)>\lambda\}
. Buckley in
[
2
]
improved the constant on the right-hand side of (
8.2
) replaced it by
c_{1}\exp(-c_{2}/\beta)
. Buckley proved this estimate using as a model a more classical inequality due to Hunt
[
Hunt
]
for the conjugate function. Buckley’s proof is based on an approach given
[
Hunt
]
. Both proofs use the Calderón-Zygmund standard
\lambda
-decomposition of the function to a sum of a bounded function
g
and a function
h
supported on the intervals outside of the set
\{Mf(x)<\beta\lambda\}\}
. The exponential decay of
\{T(g)>\lambda\}
is a classical inequality. As for
T(h)
it has an exponential distribution on the set
\{Mf(x)<\beta\lambda\}\}
, since
T(h)
admits a pointwise estimate by the
\Delta
-function used in Carleson’s celebrated paper (
[
7
]
, Lemma 5). Improving Buckley’s inequality, Karagulyan
[
14
]
established a new type of exponential inequality

(8.3)

|\{x\in B:\,T^{*}f(x)>\lambda Mf(x)\}|\leq c_{1}\exp(-c_{2}\lambda)|B|,

which proof uses rather different approach than papers
[
2
,
8
]
provide. It applies a careful covering of level sets of
T^{*}(f)/M(f)
by exponentially sparsening collections of balls. Furthermore such exponential type inequalities involving other operators (Littlewood-Paley square functions, multilinear Calderón-Zygmund operators and commutators) were obtained by Ortiz-Caraballo, Pérez and Rela
[
26
]
. In this context one can also consider the recent paper of Canto and Pérez
[
5
]
, where authors give two interesting extensions of John-Nirenberg theorem in weighted setting.

Karagulyan in
[
16
]
gave a general approach to good-
\lambda
inequalities, providing domination conditions, which imply good-
\lambda
inequalities for couples of measurable functions. As an immediate corollary of Proposition
6.1
, we recover one of two main inequalities of paper
[
16
]
for vector-valued functions.

Definition 8.1
.

Let
f,g\in L^{0}(X,\mathbb{U})
be measurable functions. The function
f
is said to be strongly dominated by
g
if for any
0<\alpha<1
there exists a number
\beta=c(\alpha)>0
such that

(8.4)

{\rm OSC}_{B,\alpha}(f)<\beta\cdot{\rm INF}_{B}(g).

Corollary 8.1
.

If the ball-basis
\mathscr{B}
in a measure space
(X,\mu)
is doubling and measurable functions
f,g\in L^{0}(X,\mathbb{U})
satisfy strong domination condition (
8.4
), then for any ball
B\in\mathscr{B}
we have

(8.5)

\mu\{x\in B:\,\|f(x)-m_{f}(B)\|_{\mathbb{U}}>\lambda\|g(x)\|_{\mathbb{U}}\}\lesssim\exp(-c\cdot\lambda)\mu(B),\quad\lambda>0,

where
c>0
is an admissible constant and
m_{f}(B)
is a median of
f
on the ball
B
.

The following two inequalities are immediate consequences of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. Note that the first one is for
{\rm BO}
operators in most general settings, while in the second one our ball-basis is doubling and we have the restricted version of
{\rm BO}
operators. Hence, given a function
f\in L^{r}(X)
and a ball
B\in\mathscr{B}
. Applying sparse bound (
1.16
) of Theorem
1.1
, we can write

(8.6)

\|Tf(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}\lesssim\mathcal{M}f(x)\sum_{G\in\mathscr{A}}\textbf{1}_{\bar{G}}(x),\quad x\in B,

then, using also (
1.15
), we immediately arrive to the following exponential estimate.

Corollary 8.2
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be measure space equipped with a ball-basis
\mathscr{B}
(which can also be non-doubling) and
T
be a general
{\rm BO}
-operator. Then the inequality

(8.7)

\mu\{x\in B:\,\|Tf(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}>t\cdot\mathcal{M}f(x)|\}\lesssim\exp(-t/c(T))\mu(B),\quad t>0,

holds for any function
f\in L^{r}(X,\mathbb{U})
and a ball
B
, where
c(T)\sim\mathcal{L}_{0}(T)+\mathcal{L}_{1}(T)+\mathcal{L}_{2}(T)
.

Now let

(8.8)

\mathcal{M}_{\#}f(x)=\sup_{B\in\mathscr{B},\,B\ni x}\langle f\rangle_{\#,B}=\sup_{B\in\mathscr{B},\,B\ni x}\langle f\rangle_{\#,B}^{*}

denote the sharp maximal function.
Similarly to (
8.6
) from Theorem
1.2
it follows that

(8.9)

\|Tf(x)-m_{T(f)}(B)\|_{\mathbb{V}}\lesssim\mathcal{M}_{\#}f(x)\sum_{G\in\mathscr{A}}\textbf{1}_{\bar{G}}(x),\quad x\in B,

for any
f\in L^{r}(X)
and a ball
B\in\mathscr{B}
. Then, using also (
1.14
), we obtain

Corollary 8.3
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
for any
f\in L^{r}(X,\mathbb{U})
and a ball
B
the maximal operator
Tf=\sup_{\alpha}|T_{\alpha}f|

satisfies

(8.10)

\mu\{x\in B:\,|Tf(x)-m_{T(f)}(B)|>t\mathcal{M}_{\#}f(x)|\}\lesssim\exp(-ct)\mu(B),\,t>0.

Let denote by
{\rm BMO}(X,\mathbb{U})
the space of functions
f\in L^{0}(X,\mathbb{U})
such that

(8.11)

\|f\|_{\rm BMO}=\sup_{B\in\mathscr{B}}\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}|f-f_{B}|<\infty.

Corollary 8.4
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
the maximal operator
Tf=\sup_{\alpha}|T_{\alpha}f|
is bounded on
{\rm BMO}
. More precisely,

(8.12)

\|T(f)\|_{{\rm BMO}(X,\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\rm BMO}(X,\mathbb{U})}

for any function

(8.13)

f\in L^{r}(X,\mathbb{U})\cap{\rm BMO}(X,\mathbb{U}).

Proof.

First apply Corollary
8.3
for the identical operator
T(f)=f
, which is obviously a restricted type of
{\rm BO}
operator with parameter
r=1
. We have

\mathcal{M}_{\#}f(x)\leq\|f\|_{{\rm BMO}}
for all
x\in X
, where we suppose
r=1
in the definition of
\mathcal{M}_{\#}
. Thus, applying (
8.10
), we obtain the John-Nirenberg inequality

(8.14)

\mu\{x\in B:\,\|f(x)-m\|_{\mathbb{U}}>t\cdot\|f\|_{\rm BMO}\}\lesssim\exp(-c\cdot t)\mu(B),\quad t>0,

where by a standard argument
m
can be also replaced by
f_{B}
. Returning to the general case of
r\geq 1
from (
8.14
) it follows that

(8.15)

\mathcal{M}_{\#}f(x)\lesssim\|f\|_{\rm BMO},\quad x\in X.

Now let
T
be the operator in Corollary
8.3
. From (
8.10
) and (
8.15
) we obtain

(8.16)

\mu\{x\in B:\,\|Tf(x)-m_{T(f)}(B)\|_{\mathbb{V}}>t\cdot\|f\|_{\rm BMO}\}\lesssim\exp(-c\cdot t)\mu(B),\quad t>0,

then by the same standard argument we get
\|T(f)\|_{\rm BMO}\lesssim\|f\|_{\rm BMO}
.
∎

Finally observe that
{\rm BO}
operators satisfying a strong-sublinearity condition

(8.17)

\|Tf(x)-Tg(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}\leq\|T(f-g)(x)\|_{\mathbb{V}}

boundedly map
L^{\infty}
to
{\rm BMO}
. Clearly, (
8.17
) implies the sublinearity of
T
and all linear operators as well as positive sublinear operators are strong-sublinear. Hence the following statement is true.

Theorem 8.1
.

Every strong-sublinear
{\rm BO}
operator
T
boundedly maps
L^{\infty}
into
{\rm BMO}
. Moreover,

\|T(f)\|_{{\rm BMO}(X,\mathbb{V})}\lesssim(\mathcal{L}_{0}(T)+\mathcal{L}_{1}(T)+\mathcal{L}_{2}(T))\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{U})}.

Proof.

Sparse domination (
1.16
) implies boundedness of
T
on
L^{p}
for any
r<p<\infty
. Namely we can write

(8.18)

\|T\|_{L^{p}(X,\mathbb{U})\to L^{p}(X,\mathbb{V})}\lesssim(\mathcal{L}_{0}(T)+\mathcal{L}_{1}(T)+\mathcal{L}_{2}(T)).

Take a function
f\in L^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{U})
and for
B\in\mathscr{B}
denote

(8.19)

\alpha_{B}=\left(T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus B^{*}})\right)_{B}.

Strong-subadditivity of
T
implies

\|Tf-T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus B^{*}})\|_{\mathbb{V}}\leq\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{B^{*}})\|_{\mathbb{V}}.

Hence, applying T1)-condition and (
8.18
), we get

\displaystyle\langle Tf-\alpha_{B}\rangle_{B}

\displaystyle=\left\langle Tf-T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus B^{*}})+T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus B^{*}})-\alpha_{B}\right\rangle_{B}

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{B^{*}})\right\rangle_{B}+{\rm OSC}_{B}(T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{X\setminus B^{*}}))

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\langle T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{B^{*}}\right\rangle_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{1}(T)\langle f\rangle_{B}^{*}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{X}\|T(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{B^{*}}\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{p}\right)^{1/p}+\mathcal{L}_{1}(T)\|f\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|T\|_{L^{p}\to L^{p}}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{X}\|f\cdot\textbf{1}_{B^{*}}\|_{\mathbb{U}}^{p}\right)^{1/p}+\mathcal{L}_{1}(T)\|f\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq(\mathcal{L}_{0}(T)+\mathcal{L}_{1}(T)+\mathcal{L}_{2}(T))\|f\|_{\infty}.

∎

9.1.
Maximally modulated Calderón-Zygmund operators

It was proved in
[
15
]
that Calderón-Zygmund operators on metric measure spaces of homogeneous type are
{\rm BO}
operators. Here we consider Calderón-Zygmund operators, which are restricted type
{\rm BO}
operators, namely satisfy R1)-R5). For the sake of simplicity we state our results on Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. A Calderón-Zygmund operator
T
is a linear operator such that

(9.1)

Tf(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x,y)f(y)dy,\quad x\notin{\rm supp\,}f,

for any compactly supported continuous function
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where the kernel
K
satisfies the conditions

(9.2)

\displaystyle|K(x,y)|\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|^{d}}\text{ if }x\neq y,

(9.3)

\displaystyle|K(x,y)-K(x^{\prime},y)|\leq\omega\left(\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|}{|x-y|}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d}},

(9.4)

\displaystyle\text{ whenever }|x-y|>2|x-x^{\prime}|.

Here
\omega:(0,1)\to(0,1)
is non-decreasing and satisfies the Dini condition
\int_{0}^{1}\omega(t)/tdt<\infty
. Also it is supposed that
T
can be boundedly extended on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Given function
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
consider the operator
T_{g}(f)=T(gf)
.

Lemma 9.1
.

If a Calderón-Zygmund kernel satisfies logarithmic Dini condition

(9.5)

[\omega]=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\omega(t)\log(1/t)}{t}dt<\infty,

then the operators
T_{g}
satisfy (
1.17
) uniformly with respect to functions
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\|g\|_{\infty}\leq 1
.

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
B=B(x_{0},R)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an Euclidean ball of center
x_{0}
and radius
R
. One can check that
B^{*}=B(x_{0},R^{*})
, where
R^{*}=(1+2\cdot 2^{1/d})R\in[R,5R]
. If any point
x\in B=B(x_{0},R)
, using (
9.4
) and (
9.5
), we get

(9.6)

\displaystyle|T_{g}(f\cdot

\displaystyle\textbf{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B^{*}})(x)-T_{g}(f\cdot\textbf{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B^{*}})(x_{0})|

(9.7)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{|y-x_{0}|>R^{*}}|K(x,y)-K(x_{0},y)||f(y)|dy

(9.8)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{2^{k+1}R^{*}\geq|y-x_{0}|>2^{k}R^{*}}\omega\left(\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{|x_{0}-y|}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{|x_{0}-y|^{d}}|f(y)|dy

(9.9)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\omega(2^{-k})\frac{1}{k|B(x_{0},2^{k+1}r)|}\int_{B(x_{0},2^{k+1}r)}|f(y)|dy

(9.10)

\displaystyle\lesssim[\omega]\sup_{A\supset B}\log^{-1}\left(1+\frac{\mu(A)}{\mu(B)}\right)\langle f\rangle_{A},

where
\sup
is taken over all the balls
A\supset B
. Thus we get R4).
∎

One can similarly prove the following

Lemma 9.2
.

If

(9.11)

[K]=\sup_{R,R^{\prime}:\,R^{\prime}>2R}\,\sup_{t\in B(x,R)}\left|\int_{B(x,R^{\prime})}K(x,y)-K(t,y)dy\right|<\infty,

then the operators
T_{g}

, satisfy restriction condition R5) uniformly.

9.2.

Let

.

Vector-valued versions of operators (

) and (

) are
{\rm BO}
operators with parameters
\varrho=\rho=r=1
and absolute constants
\mathcal{L}_{0}
,
\mathcal{L}_{1}=0
,
\mathcal{L}_{2}
. If the ball-basis (martingal filtration) is doubling, then both operators became restricted type
{\rm BO}

Remarks:

(1)

If
T
is one of operators (

) or (
9.19
) based on general ball-basis
\mathfrak{B}
, then we have exponential estimate (
8.7
), where
\mathcal{M}
is the maximal function corresponding to
\mathfrak{B}
. In
[
16
]
the same inequality for (
9.18
) was proved in the case of doubling ball-basis
\mathfrak{B}
and real valued functions.

(2)

If the ball-basis is doubling, then
T
satisfies sparse bound (
1.22
) and so exponential inequality (
8.10
). Exponential inequality (
8.10
) for martingale transforms gives another extension of the mentioned inequality of
[
16
]
, replacing the maximal function by the sharp maximal function.

(3)

So in the case of doubling ball-basis we have operators (
9.18
) and (
9.19
) are bounded on
{\rm BMO}(X,\mathbb{U})
.

(4)

Using the sparse domination (
1.16
) we can write a sharp weighted norm estimate

(9.20)

\|T(f)\|_{L^{p}(X,w,\mathbb{V})}\leq C[w]_{A_{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(X,w,\mathbb{U})}.

for both operators (
9.18
) and (
9.19
), where

(9.21)

[w]_{A_{p}}=\sup_{B\in\mathfrak{B}}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}w\right)\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}w^{-1/(p-1)}\right)^{p-1}<\infty.

is the Muckenhaupt characteristic of the weight
w
.

9.3.
Riesz potentials

For
0<\alpha<n
the fractional integral operator or Riesz potential
I_{\alpha}
is defined by

(9.22)

I_{\alpha}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dy.

The related fractional maximal operator
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
is given by

(9.23)

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}f(x)=\sup_{B\ni x}\frac{1}{|B|^{1-\alpha/n}}\int_{B}|f(y)|dy.

Theorem 9.3
.

Operators (
9.22
) and (
9.23
) are
{\rm BO}
operators with parameters

(9.24)

\varrho=r=1,\quad\rho=1-\alpha/n.

Proof.

The case of
\alpha=0
for the maximal operator
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
was proved in
[
15
]
and the general case may be proved similarly. So let us consider the operator (
9.22
). We need to prove only conditions T0) and T1), since the Euclidean ball-basis is doubling. It is well-known that
I_{\alpha}
is of weak
(1,q)
type with
q
satisfying
q^{-1}=\rho=1-\alpha/n
. This implies T0) condition with parameters (
9.24
). Then suppose
B=B(x_{0},R)
and
x,x^{\prime}\in B
,
y\in B^{*}
, are arbitrary points. One can check that

(9.25)

\left|\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}-\frac{1}{|x^{\prime}-y|^{n-\alpha}}\right|\lesssim\frac{R}{|x-y|^{n+1-\alpha}}.

Thus for
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we obtain

(9.26)

|I_{\alpha}f(x)-I_{\alpha}f(x^{\prime})|\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\cdot\frac{1}{|B(x_{0},2^{k}R)|^{\rho}}\int_{B(x_{0},2^{k}R)}|f(y)|dy\lesssim\langle f\rangle_{B}^{*},

which implies T1) condition.
∎

Weighted inequalities for operators (
9.22
) and (
9.23
) and more general potential operators have been studied in depth. See e.g. the works of Muckenhoupt and Wheeden
[
25
]
, Sawyer
[
32
,
31
]
, Gabidzashvili and Kokilashvili
[
10
]
, Sawyer and Wheeden
[
30
]
, and Pérez
[
28
,
29
]
. Classical results of
[
25
]
assert that operators (
9.22
) and (
9.23
) boundedly map
L^{q}(w^{q})
into
L^{p}(w^{p})
if and only if a weight
w
satisfies

(9.27)

[w]_{A_{p,q}}=\sup_{B\in\mathscr{B}}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}w^{q}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}w^{-p^{\prime}}\right)^{q/p^{\prime}}<\infty.

The following sharp weighted estimates were proved by Lacey, Moem, Pérez and Torres in
[
17
]
:

(9.28)

\displaystyle\|I_{\alpha}(f)\|_{L^{q}(w^{q})\to L^{p}(w^{p})}\lesssim[w]_{A_{p,q}}^{(1-\alpha/n)\max\{1,p^{\prime}/q\}},

(9.29)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(f)\|_{L^{q}(w^{q})\to L^{p}(w^{p})}\lesssim[w]_{A_{p,q}}^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{q}(1-\alpha/n)},

where parameters
q>p>1
satisfy

(9.30)

p^{-1}-q^{-1}=\frac{\alpha}{d}.

Applying Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the following sparse domination inequality.

Corollary 9.1
.

For any function
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
{\rm supp\,}f\subset B\in\mathscr{B}
there is a sparse family of balls
\mathcal{S}
such that

(9.31)

|I_{\alpha}f(x)|\lesssim C(\alpha)\sum_{G\in\mathcal{S}}\left\langle f\right\rangle_{G}\textbf{1}_{G}(x),\text{ a.e. }x\in B,

where

(9.32)

\langle f\rangle_{G}=\frac{1}{|G|^{\rho}}\int_{G}|f|,\quad\rho=1-\alpha/d.

Consider an abstract measure space
(X,\mu)
with a ball-basis
\mathscr{B}
satisfying the Besicovitch condition.

Definition 9.1
.

Let
\mathscr{B}
be a family of sets of an arbitrary set
X
. We say a family of balls
\mathscr{B}
satisfies the Besicovitch
D
-condition with a constant
D\in\mathbb{N}
, if for any collection
{\mathscr{G}\,}\subset\mathscr{B}
one can find a subscollection
{\mathscr{G}\,}^{\prime}\subset{\mathscr{G}\,}
such that

\displaystyle\bigcup_{G\in{\mathscr{G}\,}}G=\bigcup_{G\in{\mathscr{G}\,}^{\prime}}G,

\displaystyle\sum_{G\in{\mathscr{G}\,}^{\prime}}\textbf{1}_{G}(x)\leq D.

We say
\mathscr{B}
is martingale system if
D=1
.

Consider a sparse operator

(9.33)

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{\mathcal{S}}f(x)=\sum_{B\in\mathcal{S}}\langle f\rangle_{B}\cdot\textbf{1}_{B}(x),\quad f\in L^{1}_{loc}(X)=L^{1}_{loc}(X,\mathbb{R})

(9.34)

\displaystyle\text{ where }\langle f\rangle_{B}=\frac{1}{\mu(B)^{\rho}}\int_{B}|f|,\quad 0<\rho\leq 1,

corresponding to a sparse collection of balls
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathscr{B}
. The method used in
[
17
]
can be applied to prove weighted estimate (
9.28
) for sparse operators. Namely, we can state the following.

Theorem 9.4
.

If a ball-basis
\mathscr{B}
satisfies Besicovitch’s condition and
w
is an
A_{p,q}
weight with parameters parameters
q>p>1
satisfying

(9.35)

p^{-1}-q^{-1}=1-\rho,

then the sparse operator (
9.33
) satisfies the sharp bound

(9.36)

\|\mathscr{A}\|_{L^{q}(w^{q})\to L^{p}(w^{p})}\lesssim[w]_{A_{p,q}}^{\rho\max\{1,p^{\prime}/q\}}.

Having (
9.31
) and (
9.36
), clearly we can obtain the weighted estimate (
9.28
) of
[
17
]
. Such a conclusion could be interesting if we had a straightforward simple proof of (
9.36
). In fact, the suggested proof of (
9.36
) uses the same deep method of paper
[
17
]
. So it is not consistent to state the proof of (
9.36
) in the present paper. Anyway the inequality (
9.36
) is itself interesting, probably we will do it later in another note. Note that a straightforward proof of (
9.36
) in the case of
\rho=1
is known from
[
15
]
. To the best of our knowledge the method of
[
15
]
is not applicable to the general case.

10.
Proofs of Theorem
1.4
and corollaries

Lemma 10.1
.

If
A\subset B
are balls and
x\notin B^{*}
, then
d(x,A)\geq\mu(B)
.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that
d(x,A)<\mu(B)
, which means there is a ball
A^{\prime}\supset A
such that
x\in A^{\prime}
and
\mu(A^{\prime})<\mu(B)
. Then we get
A^{\prime}\subset B^{*}
, which is in contradiction with
x\notin B^{*}
.
∎

Lemma 10.2
.

For any ball
B\in\mathscr{B}
and a function
f\in L^{r}(X,\mathbb{U})
it holds the inequality

(10.1)

\langle f\rangle^{*}_{\#,B}\leq{\rm INF}_{B}(\mathcal{M}_{\#}(f))\lesssim\langle f\rangle^{*}_{\#,B}.

Proof.

The proof of the left hand side of the inequality is straightforward. To prove the right hand side inequality, we will need the following standard inequalities

(10.2)

\displaystyle\langle f\rangle_{\#,B}\leq\langle f-c\rangle_{B}+\|f_{B}-c\|_{\mathbb{U}}\leq 2\langle f-c\rangle_{B},\quad c\in\mathbb{U},

(10.3)

\displaystyle\langle f\rangle_{\#,B}\leq 2\langle f-f_{B^{*}}\rangle_{B}\leq 2\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B^{*}}\|f-f_{B^{*}}\|_{\mathbb{U}}^{r}\right)^{1/r}\lesssim\langle f\rangle_{\#,B^{*}},

where
f\in L^{r}(X,\mathbb{U})
and
B
is an arbitrary ball. For any
x\in B
there exists a ball
B(x)\ni x
such that

(10.4)

\langle f\rangle_{\#,B(x)}>\mathcal{M}_{\#}f(x)/2\geq{\rm INF}_{B}(\mathcal{M}_{\#}(f))/2=\lambda.

Applying Lemma
2.3
, we find a sequence of pairwise disjoint balls
\{B_{k}\}\subset\{B(x):\,x\in B\}
such that
\cup_{k}B_{k}^{*}\supset B
. If some
B_{k}
satisfies
\mu(B_{k})>\mu(B)
, then we have
B\subset B_{k}^{*}
and, using (
10.3
), we get

\displaystyle\langle f\rangle^{*}_{\#,B}\geq\langle f\rangle_{\#,B_{k}^{*}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\langle f\rangle_{\#,B_{k}}>\lambda.

If
\mu(B_{k})\leq\mu(B)
for every
k
, then
\cup_{k}B_{k}\subset B^{*}
. Therefore by (
10.2
), (
10.4
) and the pairwise disjointness of
B_{k}
we obtain

\displaystyle\langle f\rangle^{*}_{\#,B}

\displaystyle\geq\langle f\rangle_{\#,B^{*}}\geq\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B^{*})}\sum_{k}\int_{B_{k}}\|f-f_{B^{*}}\|_{\mathbb{U}}^{r}\right)^{1/r}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B^{*})}\sum_{k}\int_{B_{k}}\|f-f_{B_{k}}\|_{\mathbb{U}}^{r}\right)^{1/r}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B^{*})}\sum_{k}\mu(B_{k})(\langle f\rangle_{\#,B_{k}})^{r}\right)^{1/r}

\displaystyle\geq\lambda\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B^{*})}\sum_{k}\mu(B_{k})\right)^{1/r}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\lambda\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\sum_{k}\mu(B_{k}^{*})\right)^{1/r}\geq\lambda,

which gives the right inequality in (
10.1
).
∎

Lemma 10.3
.

Let
\omega
satisfy (
1.30
) and a family
\phi=\{\phi_{B}:\,B\in\mathscr{B}\}
be
\omega
-regular. Then for any function
f\in L^{r}(X,\mathbb{U})
and balls
A\subset B
we have

(10.5)

\int_{X}\|f-f_{\phi_{A}}\|_{\mathbb{U}}\phi_{B}\lesssim(\|\omega\|+\log(\mu(B)/\mu(A)))\cdot\langle f\rangle_{\#,A}^{*}.

Proof.

Applying the doubling condition we find a sequence of balls

(10.6)

\displaystyle B=B_{0}\subset B_{1}\subset\ldots\subset B_{n}\subset\ldots,

(10.7)

\displaystyle 2\mu(B_{n-1})\leq\mu(B_{n})\leq\eta\mu(B_{n-1}).

For
x\in B_{n}^{*}\setminus B_{n-1}^{*}
by Lemma
10.1
we have

(10.8)

d(x,B)\geq\mu(B_{n-1}^{*})\gtrsim\mu(B_{n}^{*})\gtrsim 2^{n}\mu(B).

Thus, using (
1.27
), we obtain

(10.9)

\phi_{B}(x)\lesssim\frac{\textbf{1}_{B^{*}}(x)}{\mu(B^{*})}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\omega(2^{-n})}{\mu(B_{n}^{*})}\textbf{1}_{B_{n}^{*}\setminus B_{n-1}^{*}}(x).

Then by Lemma
7.2
we have

(10.10)

\displaystyle\|f_{B_{n}^{*}}-f_{A}\|_{\mathbb{U}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{B_{n}^{*}}-f_{B}\|_{\mathbb{U}}+\|f_{B}-f_{A}\|_{\mathbb{U}}

(10.11)

\displaystyle\lesssim(n+\log(\mu(B)/\mu(A)))\langle f\rangle_{\#,A}^{*}.

Thus we obtain

(10.12)

\displaystyle\int_{X}\|f-f_{A}\|_{\mathbb{U}}\phi_{B}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\mu(B^{*})}\int_{B^{*}}\|f-f_{A}\|_{\mathbb{U}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\omega(2^{-n})}{\mu(B_{n}^{*})}\int_{B_{n}^{*}\setminus B_{n-1}^{*}}\|f-f_{A}\|_{\mathbb{U}}

(10.13)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mu(B^{*})}\int_{B^{*}}\|f-f_{B^{*}}\|_{\mathbb{U}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\omega(2^{-n})}{\mu(B_{n}^{*})}\int_{B_{n}^{*}}\|f-f_{B_{n}^{*}}\|_{\mathbb{U}}

(10.14)

\displaystyle\qquad+\|f_{B^{*}}-f_{A}\|_{\mathbb{U}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\omega(2^{-n})\|f_{B_{n}^{*}}-f_{A}\|_{\mathbb{U}}

(10.15)

\displaystyle\lesssim(\|\omega\|+\log(\mu(B)/\mu(A)))\cdot\langle f\rangle_{\#,A}^{*}.

Applying this inequality with
B=A
we get

(10.16)

\|f_{\phi_{A}}-f_{A}\|_{\mathbb{U}}=\left\|\int_{X}(f-f_{A})\phi_{A}\right\|_{\mathbb{U}}\lesssim\|\omega\|\cdot\langle f\rangle_{\#,A}^{*}

then, combining (
10.15
) and (
10.16
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{X}\|f-f_{\phi_{A}}\|_{\mathbb{U}}\phi_{B}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}\|f-f_{A}\|_{\mathbb{U}}\phi_{B}+\|f_{\phi_{A}}-f_{A}\|_{\mathbb{U}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\|\omega\|+\log(\mu(B)/\mu(A)))\cdot\langle f\rangle_{\#,A}^{*}.

∎

Remark 10.1
.

We will also use the inequality

(10.17)

\|f_{\phi_{A}}-f_{\phi_{B}}\|_{\mathbb{U}}\lesssim(\|\omega\|+\log(\mu(B)/\mu(A)))\cdot\langle f\rangle_{\#,A}^{*},

which immediately follows from (
10.5
).

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
f\in L^{r}(X,\mathbb{U})
be a nontrivial function and
B
be an arbitrary ball. Set
g=(f-f_{\phi_{B}})\cdot\textbf{1}_{[B]}
and
E=E_{B,\lambda}=\{y\in B:\,\mathcal{M}g(y)\leq\lambda\}
, where
\mathcal{M}
is the maximal function in (
4.1
) with parameters
\varrho=\rho=1/r
,
r\geq 1
. According to the weak-
L^{r}
bound of
\mathcal{M}
we have

(10.18)

\mu(B\setminus E)=\mu\{y\in B:\,\mathcal{M}g(y)>\lambda\}\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda^{r}}\cdot\int_{[B]}|g|^{r}.

So for an appropriate number
\lambda\sim(1-\alpha)^{-1/r}\langle g\rangle_{[B]}
we have
\mu(B\setminus E)<(1-\alpha)\mu(B)
and therefore,

(10.19)

\mu(E)>\alpha\mu(B).

Applying left hand side of (
1.27
), as well as (
10.5
) and (
10.17
), for any
y\in E
we can write

(10.20)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}g(y)\lesssim(1-\alpha)^{-1/r}\langle g\rangle_{[B]}=(1-\alpha)^{-1/r}\langle f-f_{\phi_{B}}\rangle_{[B]}

(10.21)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\lesssim(1-\alpha)^{-1/r}\left(\langle f-f_{\phi_{[B]}}\rangle_{\phi_{[B]}}+\|f_{\phi_{B}}-f_{\phi_{[B]}}\|_{\mathbb{U}}\right)

(10.22)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\,\lesssim(1-\alpha)^{-1/r}\|\omega\|\langle f\rangle^{*}_{\#,B},\quad y\in E.

Take arbitrary points
x,x^{\prime}\in E
. Without loss of generality we can suppose that
\mathcal{M}^{\phi,{\mathscr{G}\,}}f(x)\geq\mathcal{M}^{\phi,{\mathscr{G}\,}}f(x^{\prime})
. For any
\delta>0
there is a ball
A\in{\mathscr{G}\,}(x)
(so
A\ni x
) such that

(10.23)

\mathcal{M}^{\phi,{\mathscr{G}\,}}f(x)\leq\langle f\rangle_{A}+\delta.

If
\mu(A)>\mu(B)
, then we have
x^{\prime}\in B\subset[A]
and so there is a ball
\bar{A}\in\mathscr{B}(x^{\prime})
such that

(10.24)

\bar{A}\supset[A],\quad\mu(\bar{A})\leq\eta\mu([A])\lesssim\mu(A).

Then by definition of
\mathcal{M}^{\phi,{\mathscr{G}\,}}
we can write

(10.25)

\mathcal{M}^{\phi,{\mathscr{G}\,}}f(x^{\prime})\geq\langle f\rangle_{\phi_{\bar{A}}}.

Using left hand side of (
1.27
) together with relations (
10.5
), (
10.23
), (
10.24
) and (
10.25
), we obtain

(10.26)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\phi,{\mathscr{G}\,}}f(x)-\mathcal{M}^{\phi,{\mathscr{G}\,}}f(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\langle f\rangle_{A}-\langle f\rangle_{\phi_{\bar{A}}}+\delta

(10.27)

\displaystyle\leq\langle f-f_{\phi_{\bar{A}}}\rangle_{A}+|f_{\phi_{\bar{A}}}|+\langle f-f_{\phi_{\bar{A}}}\rangle_{\phi_{\bar{A}}}-|f_{\phi_{\bar{A}}}|+\delta

(10.28)

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle f-f_{\phi_{\bar{A}}}\rangle_{\phi_{\bar{A}}}+\langle f-f_{\phi_{\bar{A}}}\rangle_{\phi_{\bar{A}}}+\delta

(10.29)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\omega\|\langle f\rangle^{*}_{\#,B}+\delta.

If
\mu(A)\leq\mu(B)
, then
x^{\prime}\in A\subset[B]
and

(10.30)

\mathcal{M}^{\phi,{\mathscr{G}\,}}f(x^{\prime})\geq\langle f\rangle_{\phi_{\bar{B}}}

for a ball
\bar{B}\in\mathscr{B}(x^{\prime})
with
\mu(\bar{B})\leq\eta\mu([B])\lesssim\mu(B)
. Thus, using also (
10.20
) and (
10.23
), we obtain

(10.31)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\phi,{\mathscr{G}\,}}f(x)-\mathcal{M}^{\phi,{\mathscr{G}\,}}f(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\langle f\rangle_{A}-\langle f\rangle_{\phi_{\bar{B}}}+\delta

(10.32)

\displaystyle\leq\langle f-f_{\phi_{\bar{B}}}\rangle_{A}+\|f_{\phi_{\bar{B}}}\|_{\mathbb{U}}+\langle f-f_{\phi_{\bar{B}}}\rangle_{{\phi_{\bar{B}}}}-\|f_{\phi_{\bar{B}}}\|_{\mathbb{U}}+\delta

(10.33)

\displaystyle\leq\langle f-f_{\phi_{B}}\rangle_{A}+\|f_{\phi_{B}}-f_{\phi_{\bar{B}}}\|_{\mathbb{U}}+\langle f-f_{\phi_{\bar{B}}}\rangle_{{\phi_{\bar{B}}}}+\delta

(10.34)

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle g\rangle_{A}+\|\omega\|\langle f\rangle^{*}_{\#,B}+\delta

(10.35)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{M}g(x)+\|\omega\|\langle f\rangle^{*}_{\#,B}+\delta

(10.36)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1-\alpha)^{-1/r}\|\omega\|\langle f\rangle^{*}_{\#,B}+\delta.

Since
\delta
can be arbitrary small, from (
10.29
) and (
10.31
) we conclude

|\mathcal{M}^{\phi,{\mathscr{G}\,}}f(x)-\mathcal{M}^{\phi,{\mathscr{G}\,}}f(x^{\prime})|\lesssim(1-\alpha)^{-1/r}\|\omega\|\langle f\rangle^{*}_{\#,B},\quad x,x^{\prime}\in E.

This implies

(10.37)

{\rm OSC}_{E}(\mathcal{M}^{\phi,{\mathscr{G}\,}}f)\lesssim(1-\alpha)^{-1/r}\|\omega\|\langle f\rangle^{*}_{\#,B}.

Combining (
10.19
) and (
10.37
), we deduce (
1.31
), completing the proof of theorem.
∎

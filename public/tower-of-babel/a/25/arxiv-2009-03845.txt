Existence, nonexistence, and asymptotic behavior of solutions for N \displaystyle N -Laplacian equations involving critical exponential growth in the whole ℝ N \displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}

Abstract

The nonlinearity considered involves critical Trudinger-Moser growth. Our approach is non-variational, and in this way we can address a wide range of problems not yet contained in the literature. Introduction

Let
\displaystyle W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
,
\displaystyle\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\displaystyle N\geq 2
, be the Sobolev space endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla u|^{p}+|u|^{p})dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

If
\displaystyle p<N
, the critical growth
\displaystyle p^{*}=Np/(N-p)
means that
\displaystyle\|u\|_{W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)}\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega)
,
\displaystyle N\leq p\leq p^{*}
. The case
\displaystyle p=N
is a borderline case in the sense of Sobolev embeddings.
As is well-known, one has
\displaystyle\|u\|_{W_{0}^{1,N}(\Omega)}\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega)
,
\displaystyle q\geq N
, but a function in
\displaystyle W_{0}^{1,N}(\Omega)
may have a local singularity and this causes the failure of the embedding
\displaystyle W^{1,N}(\Omega)\not\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. If
\displaystyle\Omega
is a bounded domain, another kind of maximal growth was proved by Yudovič
[
38
]
, Pohožaev
[
35
]
and Trudinger
[
36
]
. They proved, in an independent way,
that

u\in W^{1,N}_{0}(\Omega)\Longrightarrow\int_{\Omega}e^{|u|^{N^{\prime}}}dx<\infty,

(1)

where
\displaystyle N^{\prime}=\frac{N}{N-1}
. Moreover, for any higher growth, the corresponding integral can be infinite for a suitable choice of
\displaystyle u
. After that, Moser
[
30
]
improved this assertion, showing that if
\displaystyle u\in W_{0}^{1,N}(\Omega)
, then

\sup_{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{N}(\Omega)}\leq 1}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha|u|^{N^{\prime}}}dx\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\leq c|\Omega|,&\mbox{ if }\alpha\leq\alpha_{N},\\
=\infty,&\mbox{ if }\alpha>\alpha_{N},\end{array}\right.

(2)

where
\displaystyle\alpha_{N}=N\omega_{N-1}^{1/(N-1)}
,
\displaystyle c
is a constant which depends on
\displaystyle N
, and
\displaystyle\omega_{N-1}
is the measure of the unit sphere in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Inequality
(
2
)
is now called Moser-Trudinger inequality and the term
\displaystyle e^{\alpha_{N}|u|^{N^{\prime}}}
is known as critical Moser-Trudinger growth.

This well-known Moser-Trundinger inequality has been generalized in many ways. In the case
\displaystyle\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
, B. Ruf when
\displaystyle N=2
in
[
33
]
, and Y.X. Li and B. Ruf in
[
25
]
for
\displaystyle N>2
, proved the following assertion

\sup_{\|u\|_{W^{1,N}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq 1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi_{N}(\alpha|u|^{\frac{N}{N-1}})dx\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\leq C(\alpha,N)^{N^{\prime}},&\mbox{ if }\alpha\leq\alpha_{N},\\
=\infty,&\mbox{ if }\alpha>\alpha_{N},\end{array}\right.

(3)

where
\displaystyle\alpha_{N}>0
is a constant and

\phi_{N}(t)=e^{t}-\sum_{j=0}^{N-2}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}=\sum_{j=N-1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{j}}{j!},\,\,t\geq 0.

(4)

Remark 1
.

Notice that
\displaystyle\phi_{N}(t)
is a increasing function.

In this paper, we consider the existence, nonexistence, and asymptotic behavior of positive solutions for a class of

The solution of (
5

As in
[

Such a lemma, together Brouwer’s fixed point theorem, allows us, in the scheme of the Galerkin method, to work in general Banach spaces of finite dimensions with general norms.

Due to the presence of the critical term
\displaystyle\phi_{N}(\alpha|u|^{\frac{N}{N-1}})

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}

\displaystyle f

Another delicate point in our approach is the regularity needed to ensure that the solution, obtained in the limit, does not vanish.
In the bounded domain, we obtain regularity up to the boundary for the auxiliary problems considering approximate functions in the sense of Strauss
[

. For the domain-wide solution case, a kind of a priori estimate in the sup norm is presented, see Proposition

Lemma 2.3

Let
\displaystyle F:(\mathbb{R}^{d},\|\cdot\|_{d})\rightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{d},\|\cdot\|_{d})
be a continuous function such that
\displaystyle\left\langle F(\xi),\xi\right\rangle\geq 0
for every
\displaystyle\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\displaystyle\|\xi\|_{d}=\varrho
for some
\displaystyle\varrho>0
, and
\displaystyle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle^{1/2}=|\cdot|_{2}
. Then, there exists
\displaystyle z_{0}

\displaystyle\overline{B}^{d}_{\varrho}(0):=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d};\|z\|_{d}\leq\varrho\}
such that
\displaystyle F(z_{0})=0
.

Proof.

Firstly, there exists
\displaystyle c(d)>0
such that

\|x\|_{d}\leq c(d)|x|_{2},\,\,\ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(26)

Suppose,
\displaystyle F(x)\neq 0

\displaystyle x\in\overline{B}^{d}_{\varrho}(0)

g:(\mathbb{R}^{d},\|\cdot\|_{d})\rightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{d},\|\cdot\|_{d})

g(x)=-\frac{\varrho}{\|F(x)\|_{d}}F(x)

which maps
\displaystyle\overline{B}^{d}_{\varrho}(0)
into itself and is continuous. Hence it has a fixed point
\displaystyle x_{0}
, by Brouwer’s Fixed Point Theorem. Since
\displaystyle x_{0}=g(x_{0})
, we have
\displaystyle\|x_{0}\|_{d}=\|g(x_{0})\|_{d}=\varrho>0
. But then by (
26
)

0<\varrho^{2}=\|x_{0}\|^{2}_{d}\leq c(d)^{2}|x_{0}|^{2}_{2}=c(d)^{2}\left\langle x_{0},x_{0}\right\rangle=c(d)^{2}\left\langle x_{0},g(x_{0})\right\rangle

=-c(d)^{2}\frac{\varrho}{\|F(x_{0})\|_{d}}\left\langle x_{0},F(x_{0})\right\rangle\leq 0,

by assumptions, which is a contradiction.

3
Approximating functions

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we approximate
\displaystyle f
by Lipschitz functions
\displaystyle f_{k}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

f_{k}(s)=\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}-k[G(-k-\frac{1}{k})-G(-k)],&\textup{if}&s\leq-k,\\
-k[G(s-\frac{1}{k})-G(s)],&\textup{if}&-k\leq s\leq-\frac{1}{k},\\
k^{2}s[G(-\frac{2}{k})-G(-\frac{1}{k})],&\textup{if}&-\frac{1}{k}\leq s\leq 0,\\
k^{2}s[G(\frac{2}{k})-G(\frac{1}{k})],&\textup{if}&0\leq s\leq\frac{1}{k},\\
k[G(s+\frac{1}{k})-G(s)],&\textup{if}&\frac{1}{k}\leq s\leq k,\\
k[G(k+\frac{1}{k})-G(k)],&\textup{if}&s\geq k,\\
\end{array}\right.

(28)

where
\displaystyle G(s)=\int_{0}^{s}f(\xi)d\xi
.

The following approximation result was proved in
[
34
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\displaystyle f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function such that
\displaystyle sf(s)\geq 0
for every
\displaystyle s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then there exists a sequence
\displaystyle f_{k}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
of continuous functions satisfying

(i)
\displaystyle sf_{k}(s)\geq 0
for every
\displaystyle s\in\mathbb{R}
;

(ii)
\displaystyle\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\exists\,c_{k}>0
such that
\displaystyle|f_{k}(\xi)-f_{k}(\eta)|\leq c_{k}|\xi-\eta|
for every
\displaystyle\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}
;

(iii)
\displaystyle f_{k}
converges uniformly to
\displaystyle f
in bounded subsets of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
.

The sequence
\displaystyle f_{k}
of the previous lemma has some additional properties presented below. Here, we present a suitable improvement in the estimates of the approximating functions
\displaystyle f_{k}
by comparing it with
[
10
, Lemma
2.2.]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\displaystyle f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function satisfying (
6
) for every
\displaystyle s\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then the sequence
\displaystyle f_{k}
of Lemma
3.1
satisfies

(i)
\displaystyle\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\displaystyle 0\leq sf_{k}(s)\leq C_{1}|s|^{p}\phi_{N}(2^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\alpha\,|s|^{\frac{N}{N-1}})
for every
\displaystyle|s|\geq\frac{1}{k}
;

(ii)
\displaystyle\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\displaystyle 0\leq sf_{k}(s)\leq C_{2}\frac{1}{k^{p-2}}|s|^{2}
for every
\displaystyle|s|\leq\frac{1}{k}
,

where
\displaystyle C_{1}
and
\displaystyle C_{2}
are positive constants independent of
\displaystyle k
.

Proof.

Everywhere in this proof, the constant
\displaystyle a_{1}
is the one of (
6
).

First step
. Suppose that
\displaystyle-k\leq s\leq-\frac{1}{k}
.

By the mean value theorem, there exists
\displaystyle\eta\in(s-\frac{1}{k},s)
such that

f_{k}(s)=-k[G(s-\frac{1}{k})-G(s)]=-kG^{\prime}(\eta)(s-\frac{1}{k}-s)=f(\eta)

and

sf_{k}(s)=sf(\eta).

Since
\displaystyle s-\frac{1}{k}<\eta<s<0
and
\displaystyle f(\eta)<0
, we have
\displaystyle sf(\eta)\leq\eta f(\eta)
. Therefore, by using Remark
1
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{rcl}sf_{k}(s)\leq\eta f(\eta)&\leq&a_{1}|\eta|^{p}\phi_{N}(\alpha\,|\eta|^{\frac{N}{N-1}})\\
&\leq&a_{1}|s-\frac{1}{k}|^{p}\phi_{N}(\alpha\,|s-\frac{1}{k}|^{\frac{N}{N-1}})\\
&\leq&a_{1}(|s|+\frac{1}{k})^{p}\phi_{N}(\alpha\,(|s|+\frac{1}{k})^{\frac{N}{N-1}})\\
&\leq&a_{1}(2|s|)^{p}\phi_{N}(\alpha\,(2|s|)^{\frac{N}{N-1}})\\
&=&a_{1}2^{p}|s|^{p}\phi_{N}(2^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\alpha\,|s|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}).\end{array}

Second step
. Assume
\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\leq s\leq k
.

By the mean value theorem, there exists
\displaystyle\eta\in(s,s+\frac{1}{k})
such that

f_{k}(s)=k[G(s+\frac{1}{k})-G(s)]=kG^{\prime}(\eta)(s+\frac{1}{k}-s)=f(\eta)

and

sf_{k}(s)=sf(\eta).

Since
\displaystyle 0<s<\eta<s+\frac{1}{k}
and
\displaystyle f(\eta)>0
, we have
\displaystyle sf(\eta)\leq\eta f(\eta)
. Therefore,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}sf_{k}(s)\leq\eta f(\eta)&\leq&a_{1}|\eta|^{p}\phi_{N}(\alpha\,|\eta|^{\frac{N}{N-1}})\\
&\leq&a_{1}|s+\frac{1}{k}|^{p}\phi_{N}(\alpha\,|s+\frac{1}{k}|^{\frac{N}{N-1}})\\
&\leq&a_{1}(2|s|)^{p}\phi_{N}(\alpha\,(2|s|)^{\frac{N}{N-1}})\\
&=&a_{1}2^{p}|s|^{p}\phi_{N}(2^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\alpha\,|s|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}).\end{array}

Third step
. Suppose that
\displaystyle|s|\geq k
, then

f_{k}(s)=\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}-k[G(-k-\frac{1}{k})-G(-k)],&\textup{if}&s\leq-k\\
k[G(k+\frac{1}{k})-G(k)],&\textup{if}&s\geq k.\\
\end{array}\right.

(29)

If
\displaystyle s\leq-k
, by the mean value theorem, there exists
\displaystyle\eta\in(-k-\frac{1}{k},-k)
such that

f_{k}(s)=k[G(-k-\frac{1}{k})-G(-k)]=-kG^{\prime}(\eta)(-k-\frac{1}{k}-(-k))=f(\eta)

and

sf_{k}(s)=sf(\eta).

Since
\displaystyle-k-\frac{1}{k}<\eta<-k<0
and
\displaystyle k<|\eta|<k+\frac{1}{k}
, we conclude that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}sf_{k}(s)=\frac{s}{\eta}\eta f(\eta)&\leq&\frac{|s|}{|\eta|}a_{1}|\eta|^{p}\phi_{N}(\alpha\,|\eta|^{\frac{N}{N-1}})=a_{1}|s||\eta|^{{p}-1}\phi_{N}(\alpha\,|\eta|^{\frac{N}{N-1}})\\
&\leq&a_{1}|s|(k+\frac{1}{k})^{{p}-1}\phi_{N}(\alpha\,(k+\frac{1}{k})^{\frac{N}{N-1}})\\
&\leq&a_{1}|s|(|s|+\frac{1}{k})^{{p}-1}\phi_{N}(\alpha\,(|s|+\frac{1}{k})^{\frac{N}{N-1}})\\
&\leq&a_{1}|s|(2|s|)^{{p}-1}\phi_{N}(\alpha\,(2|s|)^{\frac{N}{N-1}})\\
&\leq&a_{1}2^{{p}}|s|^{p}\phi_{N}(2^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\alpha\,|s|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}).\end{array}

(30)

If
\displaystyle s\geq k
, by the mean value theorem, there exists
\displaystyle\eta\in(k,k+\frac{1}{k})
such that

f_{k}(s)=k[G(k+\frac{1}{k})-G(k)]=kG^{\prime}(\eta)(k+\frac{1}{k}-k)=f(\eta).

By computations similar to conclude (
30
) one has

sf_{k}(s)=sf(\eta)=\frac{s}{\eta}\eta f(\eta)\leq\frac{|s|}{|\eta|}a_{1}|\eta|^{p}\phi_{N}(\alpha\,|\eta|^{\frac{N}{N-1}})\leq a_{1}2^{{p}}|s|^{p}\phi_{N}(2^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\alpha\,|s|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}).

Fourth step
. Assume
\displaystyle-\frac{1}{k}\leq s\leq\frac{1}{k}
, then

f_{k}(s)=\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}k^{2}s[G(-\frac{2}{k})-G(-\frac{1}{k})],&\textup{if}&-\frac{1}{k}\leq s\leq 0\\
k^{2}s[G(\frac{2}{k})-G(\frac{1}{k})],&\textup{if}&0\leq s\geq\frac{1}{k}.\\
\end{array}\right.

(31)

If
\displaystyle-\frac{1}{k}\leq s\leq 0
, by the mean value theorem, there exists
\displaystyle\eta\in(-\frac{2}{k},-\frac{1}{k})
such that

f_{k}(s)=k^{2}s[G(-\frac{2}{k})-G(-\frac{1}{k})]=k^{2}sG^{\prime}(\eta)(-\frac{2}{k}-(-\frac{1}{k}))=-ksf(\eta).

Therefore

\begin{array}[]{lll}sf_{k}(s)&=&-ks^{2}f(\eta)=-k\frac{s^{2}}{\eta}\eta f(\eta)\leq k\frac{s^{2}}{|\eta|}\eta f(\eta)\\
&\leq&a_{1}k|s|^{2}|\eta|^{{p}-1}\phi_{N}(\alpha\,|\eta|^{\frac{N}{N-1}})\leq a_{1}k|s|^{2}(\frac{2}{k})^{{p}-1}\phi_{N}(\alpha\,|\eta|^{\frac{N}{N-1}})\\
&\leq&a_{1}2^{{p}-1}\frac{|s|^{2}}{k^{p-2}}\phi\left(\alpha\,\left(\frac{2}{k}\right)^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\right)\\
&\leq&a_{1}2^{{p}-1}\exp(2^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\alpha)\frac{1}{{k^{p-2}}}|s|^{2}.\end{array}

(32)

If
\displaystyle 0\leq s\leq\frac{1}{k}
, by the mean value theorem, there exists
\displaystyle\eta\in(\frac{1}{k},\frac{2}{k})
such that

f_{k}(s)=k^{2}s[G(\frac{2}{k})-G(\frac{1}{k})]=k^{2}sG^{\prime}(\eta)(\frac{2}{k}-\frac{1}{k})=ksf(\eta).

By similar computations to conclude (
32
) one obtains

sf_{k}(s)=ks^{2}f(\eta)=k\frac{s^{2}}{|\eta|}\eta f(\eta)\leq a_{1}2^{{p}-1}\exp(2^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\alpha)\frac{1}{{k^{p-2}}}|s|^{2}.

The proof of the lemma follows by taking
\displaystyle C_{1}=a_{1}2^{p}
ad
\displaystyle C_{2}=a_{1}2^{{p}-1}\exp(2^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\alpha)
, where
\displaystyle a_{1}
is given in (
6
). ∎

Lemma 4.2
.

There exists
\displaystyle\lambda^{*}>0
and
\displaystyle n^{*}\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle(PD_{n})

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=\{w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{n},\dots\}

5

\displaystyle(W_{0}^{1,N}(D),\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,N}(D)})

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle m

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\left\langle F(\xi),\xi\right\rangle_{P}&\geq&\displaystyle\int_{D^{+}_{n}}|\nabla u|^{N}dx+\int_{D^{+}_{n}}|u|^{N}dx-\lambda\frac{K_{1}}{2}\|\tilde{u}\|^{q}_{W^{1,N}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
&&-\displaystyle K_{2}C(\alpha,N)\|\tilde{u}\|^{p}_{W^{1,N}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}-\frac{K_{3}}{2n}\|\tilde{u}\|_{W^{1,N}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.\end{array}

(41)

Remark 2

Notice that the constants

.

-\Delta_{N}u_{\lambda}+u^{N-1}_{\lambda}=\lambda a(x)u_{\lambda}^{q-1}+f(u_{\lambda})\quad{\rm in}\ B_{m},\quad u_{\lambda}\geq 0\quad{\rm on}\ \partial B_{m}.

By using (
24
) and since
\displaystyle C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is dense in
\displaystyle W^{1,N}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, by a diagonal argument, there exist a relabeled subsequence of
\displaystyle\{\tilde{u}_{n}\}
and a function
\displaystyle u_{\lambda}\in W^{1,N}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

\tilde{u}_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{\lambda}\,\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\,W^{1,N}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

\tilde{u}_{n}(x)\rightarrow u_{\lambda}(x)\,\,\,\mbox{for a.e }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

These convergence properties, and some iteration process, ensure that
\displaystyle u_{\lambda}
is a solution of problem
(
5
)
, that belongs to
\displaystyle C^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
(see (
100
)).

The next step in the proof is to show that
\displaystyle u_{\lambda}
does not vanish in

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Indeed, Lemma
2.2
provides a
solution
\displaystyle u_{\lambda,m}
of the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta_{N}u+|u|^{N-2}u=\lambda a_{m}|u|^{q-2}u&\mbox{in}&B_{m}\\
u>0&\mbox{in}&B_{m}\\
u(x)=0&\mbox{on}&\partial B_{m},\end{array}\right.

where

a_{m}=\inf_{B_{m}}a(x).

Since
\displaystyle\lambda a(x)t^{q-1}+f(t)\geq\lambda a_{m}t^{q-1}
for all
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
We are thus in a position to apply Proposition
2
to the
functions
\displaystyle u_{\lambda,m}
and
\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{n}
with
\displaystyle n>m
, in place of
\displaystyle u_{1}=u_{\lambda,m}
and

\displaystyle u_{2}=\tilde{u}_{n}
, respectively, which renders
\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{n}\geq u_{\lambda,m}
in
\displaystyle B_{m}
for
every
\displaystyle n>m
. This enables us to deduce that
\displaystyle u_{\lambda}\geq u_{\lambda,m}
in
\displaystyle B_{m}
, so

\displaystyle u_{\lambda}(x)>0
for every
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, because
\displaystyle m
was arbitrary chosen.

Furthermore, since
\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{n}
weakly converges to
\displaystyle u_{\lambda}
in
\displaystyle W^{1,N}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, by (

) we have

\|u_{\lambda}\|_{W^{1,N}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\tilde{u}_{n}\|_{W^{1,N}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\tilde{u}_{n}\|_{N,n}\leq\tilde{\varrho},

(107)

and then, we obtain
\displaystyle u_{\lambda}\in W^{1,N}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and

\|u_{\lambda}\|_{W^{1,N}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\rightarrow 0,\,\mbox{ as }\lambda\rightarrow 0.

(108)

Thus, the proof
of Theorem

5.3
Proof of Proposition
1

In this proof, we borrowed some ideas from
[
6
]
.
For the sake of contradiction, suppose that
\displaystyle\lambda^{*}=\infty
. Thus, there is a sequence
\displaystyle\lambda_{n}\to\infty
and corresponding solutions
\displaystyle u_{\lambda_{n}}>0
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}
given by Theorem
1.1
. In what follows, define
\displaystyle a_{R}=\inf_{B_{R}(0)}a(r)
, for some
\displaystyle R>0
fixed. Define also

\mathcal{P}(x,t)=\lambda a(x)t^{q-1}+t^{p-1}\phi_{N}(\alpha|t|^{\frac{N}{N-1}})

and

P_{1}(t)=\Lambda t^{q-1}+t^{p-1}\phi_{N}(\alpha|t|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}),

where
\displaystyle\Lambda=\lambda\ a_{R}
.

We claim that there exists a constant
\displaystyle C_{\Lambda}>0
such that

P(x,t)\geq C_{\Lambda}t^{N-1}\mbox{ for every }\ t>0.

Indeed, define the function
\displaystyle Q(t)=P_{1}(t)t^{-(N-1)}
. Then, since
\displaystyle q<N<p
,
\displaystyle Q(t)\to\infty
as
\displaystyle t\to 0^{+}
and as
\displaystyle t\to\infty
. The minimum value is
\displaystyle Q(t_{1})=C_{\Lambda}
, where
\displaystyle t_{1}>0
is the unique solution of

\phi_{N}(\alpha t^{\frac{N}{N-1}})(p-N)t^{p-q}+\alpha\frac{N}{N-1}t^{\frac{(N-1)(p-q+1)+1}{N-1}}\phi_{N-1}(\alpha t^{\frac{N}{N-1}})=\Lambda(N-q).

Indeed, let us define

H(t):=\phi_{N}(\alpha t^{\frac{N}{N-1}})(p-N)t^{p-q}+\alpha\frac{N}{N-1}t^{\frac{(N-1)(p-q+1)+1}{N-1}}\phi_{N-1}(\alpha t^{\frac{N}{N-1}}).

Note that
\displaystyle H(0)=0
and
\displaystyle H(t)\to\infty
as
\displaystyle t\to\infty
. Since

\begin{array}[]{rcl}H^{\prime}(t)&=&(p-N)(p-q)t^{p-q-1}\phi_{N}(\alpha t^{\frac{N}{N-1}})\\
&+&\frac{\alpha\,N(p-N)}{N-1}t^{p-q+\frac{1}{N}}\phi_{N-1}(\alpha t^{\frac{N}{N-1}})\\
&+&\frac{N\alpha}{N-1}\left[\frac{(N-1)(p-q+1)+1}{N-1}\right]t^{\frac{(N-1)(p-q+1)+1}{N-1}-1}\phi_{N-1}(\alpha t^{\frac{N}{N-1}})\\
&+&\frac{\alpha^{2}N^{2}}{(N-1)^{2}}t^{\frac{(N-1)(p-q+1)+1}{N-1}+\frac{1}{N-1}}\phi_{N-2}(\alpha t^{\frac{N}{N-1}})>0,\,\,\,\forall t\geq 0,\end{array}

hence
\displaystyle H
is increasing. Therefore, there is a unique point
\displaystyle t_{1}>0
such that
\displaystyle H(t_{1})=\Lambda(N-q)
. Consequently, by the arguments before,
\displaystyle t_{1}>0
is the unique point of minimum of
\displaystyle Q
, with
\displaystyle Q(t_{1})=C_{\Lambda}
.

Denote by
\displaystyle\sigma_{1}>0
and
\displaystyle\varphi_{1}>0
, respectively, the principal eigenvalue and corresponding eigenfunction of the eigenvalue
problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta_{N}(\varphi_{1})=\sigma_{1}|\varphi_{1}|^{N-2}\varphi_{1}&\mbox{in}&B_{R}(0)\\
\varphi_{1}=0&\mbox{on}&\partial B_{R}(0).\end{array}\right.

Since
\displaystyle C_{\Lambda}
increases as
\displaystyle\lambda_{n}
increases, for
\displaystyle\delta>0
small enough, there is
\displaystyle\lambda_{0}
in this sequence such that the corresponding constant satisfies
\displaystyle C_{\Lambda_{0}}\geq\sigma_{1}+\delta+1
. Hence the solution
\displaystyle u_{\lambda_{0}}
of (
5
) associated to
\displaystyle\lambda_{0}
satisfies
\displaystyle u_{\lambda_{0}}>0
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}
and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta_{N}u_{\lambda_{0}}\geq(C_{\Lambda_{0}}-1)|u_{\lambda_{0}}|^{N-2}u_{\lambda_{0}}\geq(\sigma_{1}+\delta)|u_{\lambda_{0}}|^{N-2}u_{\lambda_{0}}&\mbox{in}&B_{R}(0)\\
u_{\lambda_{0}}\geq 0&\mbox{on}&\partial B_{R}(0).\end{array}\right.

Otherwise, taking
\displaystyle\varepsilon>0
small enough we obtain
\displaystyle\varepsilon\varphi_{1}<u_{\lambda_{0}}
in
\displaystyle B_{R}(0)
and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta_{N}(\varepsilon\varphi_{1})=\sigma_{1}|\varepsilon\varphi_{1}|^{N-2}(\varepsilon\varphi_{1})\leq(\sigma_{1}+\delta)|\varepsilon\varphi_{1}|^{N-2}(\varepsilon\varphi_{1})&\mbox{in}&B_{R}(0)\\
\varphi_{1}=0&\mbox{on}&\partial B_{R}(0).\end{array}\right.

By the method of subsolution and supersolution, see
[
6
, Theorem 2.1]
, there is a solution
\displaystyle\varepsilon\varphi_{1}\leq\omega\leq u_{\lambda_{0}}
in
\displaystyle B_{R}(0)
of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta_{N}\omega=(\sigma_{1}+\delta)|\omega|^{N-2}\omega&\mbox{in}&B_{R}(0)\\
\omega=0&\mbox{on}&\partial B_{R}(0).\end{array}\right.

Hence there is a contradiction to the fact that
\displaystyle\sigma_{1}
is isolated (see
[
28
]
). Therefore,
\displaystyle\lambda^{*}<\infty
.

Equivariant Khovanov homotopy types
Jakub Paliga

Abstract
. We investigate external group actions on homotopy coherent diagrams. This is used to prove an equivalence between realizations of equivariant cubical flow categories and external actions on Burnside functors. In particular, the results imply that the equivariant Khovanov homotopy types defined by [ BPS21 ] and [ SZ18 ] are equivariantly stably homotopy equivalent.



Khovanov homology was introduced in
[
Kho00
]
as a categorification of the Jones polynomial, with decategorification by way of graded Euler characteristic. Building on the work of Cohen-Jones-Segal in
[
CJS95
]
, Lipshitz and Sarkar defined in
[
LS14
]
a space-level refinement of Khovanov homology. This takes the form of a CW spectrum
\mathcal{X}_{Kh}(L)=\bigveeop\displaylimits_{j}\mathcal{X}^{j}_{Kh}(L)
, such that for any
j
, the cellular cochain complex of
\mathcal{X}^{j}_{Kh}(L)
is isomorphic to the Khovanov complex
\ckh^{\bullet,j}(L)
in quantum grading
j
. Among its uses, it allows for the definition of a stronger s-invariant.
[
LS14a
]

In the case of links equipped with symmetries, it is expected that the spectra
\mathcal{X}(L)
carry additional data. For periodic links, an equivariant Khovanov homotopy type was defined by
[
BPS21
]
, who introduced equivariant cubical flow categories for the purpose. At the same time,
[
SZ18
]
proposed a different notion of equivariant Khovanov homotopy type of a periodic link, using external actions on Burnside functors. Both approaches furnish localization results relating the Khovanov homotopy type of a periodic link to the annular Khovanov homotopy type of its quotient, resulting in periodicity criteria. A difference persists in that
[
BPS21
]
identified the Borel cohomology of their spectrum with equivariant Khovanov homology as defined by Politarczyk in
[
Pol19
]
. It has been an open question whether the equivariant spectra defined in
[
BPS21
]
and
[
SZ18
]
are equivalent.

This paper answers the question in the affirmative. Namely, given a periodic link diagram
D
, consider the equivariant spectra
\mathcal{X}_{SZ}(D)
and
\mathcal{X}_{BPS}(D)
defined by
[
SZ18
]
and
[
BPS21
]
, respectively. We show the following.

Theorem 1
.

There is an equivariant stable homotopy equivalence

\mathcal{X}_{SZ}(D)\to\mathcal{X}_{BPS}(D).

The paper’s structure is as follows.

introduces the cube category and the prerequisites on equivariant topology. In
Section

2
we recall several definitions of homotopy coherent diagrams and relate to them the concept of external action introduced in
[

]
.
Section

3
serves to describe equivariant cubical flow categories. In particular, in
Section

we identify the equivariant cube flow category as the free topological category on the equivariant cube

. Burnside functors together with a notion of external action appropriate to them are introduced in
Section

.
In
Section

5
we introduce configurations of stars and via a Pontrjagin-Thom-type construction associate to them equivariant maps of spheres. Those configurations are used to define geometric realizations of external actions on Burnside functors. This follows the constructions of
[

]
, albeit allowing for more general shapes.

In
Section

6
we compare the definitions of external action on Burnside functors due to
[

]
and
[
SZ18
]
. In a series of comparison results, we relate those to the notion of an equivariant cubical flow category, culminating in

{restatable*}

The data of an equivariant cubical flow category

We finish in

1.
Prerequisites

1.1.
The cube category

Let
\underline{2}=\underline{2}^{1}
denote the poset
\{0>1\}
, or the category with objects
0
and
1
and a single non-identity morphism
1\to 0.
For
n\in\mathbb Z
,
n>1
, we let
\underline{2}^{n}=\underline{2}^{1}\times\underline{2}^{n-1}.
If
u\geq v,
we will denote the single element of
\hom_{\underline{2}^{n}}(u,v)
by
\phi_{u,v}
. For
u\in\underline{2}^{n}
, we denote by
\lvert u\rvert
the
L^{1}
-norm of
u
, so that
\lvert u\rvert=u_{1}+\dotsb+u_{n}.
If
u\geq v
and
\lvert v\rvert-\lvert u\rvert=k
, we write
u\geq_{k}v.

The category
\underline{2}^{n}
will sometimes be considered as a
2
-category with no non-identity
2
-morphisms.

A group
G
can be understood as a category with one object
\ast
and morphisms
\hom_{G}(\ast,\ast)=G
with composition defined by the group law of
G
.
Then, an action of a group
G
on a small category
\mathcal{C}
is a functor
\gamma\colon G\to\cats
with
\gamma(\ast)=\mathcal{C}
.

Although we state some results in greater generality, in application to Khovanov homology of periodic links the setting is that of a particular action of a cyclic group on a cube category.
Given integers
n
and
m
, the identification
\underline{2}^{nm}\cong(\underline{2}^{n})^{m}
establishes a left action of
\mathbb Z_{m}
on
\underline{2}^{nm}
by cyclic permutation of the
\underline{2}^{n}
-factors; so that the generator
1\in\mathbb Z_{m}
acts by

1.(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m})=(x_{m},x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m-1}),\quad x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m}\in\underline{2}^{n}.

In the setting of a group
G
acting on a poset
\mathcal{C}
, one can speak of the fixed-point category
\mathcal{C}^{H}
(for any subgrop
H\subseteq G
). The fixed points of the action of
\mathbb Z_{m}
on
\underline{2}^{nm}
as above, the fixed-point category
(\underline{2}^{nm})^{\mathbb Z_{m}}
is identified with
\underline{2}^{n}
itself. Likewise, if
H\subseteq\mathbb Z_{m}
is the single subgroup of index
k
, we fix an identification
(\underline{2}^{nm})^{H}\cong\underline{2}^{nk}
.

1.2.
G
-cell complexes and the equivariant Spanier-Whitehead category

The definitions are classical, and in notation we follow
[
BPS21
, Sections 3.1, 3.2, 3.3]
.

An orthogonal representation of a finite group
G
is a homomorphism
\rho\colon G\to O(V)
, with
V
a real linear space equipped with an inner product. We will only consider finite-dimensional orthogonal representations and will call them “representations” for short. A morphism of representations
(\rho_{V}\colon G\to O(V))\to(\rho_{W}\colon G\to O(W))
is a linear map
f\colon V\to W
such that

\forall g\in G\kern 5.0pt\rho_{W}(g)\circ f=f\circ\rho_{V}(g).

Representations of
G
make up a monoid under the direct sum operation
\oplus
. We will often consider virtual representations, which arise by applying the Grothendieck construction to the monoid of representations of
G
. Namely, let

\representationring(G)=\{V-W\mathrel{|}\text{$V$ and $W$ are representations of $G$}\}/{\sim},

where
V_{1}-W_{1}\sim V_{2}-W_{2}
whenever
V_{1}\oplus W_{2}
and
V_{2}\oplus W_{1}
are isomorphic representations of
G
. Together with multiplication induced by
\otimes
,
\representationring(G)
becomes a ring.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
H\subseteq G
be a subgroup,
V
an
H
-representation. Denote by
B_{R}(V)
the ball of radius
R
in
V
, which inherits the group action. A
G
-cell of type
(H,V)
is a topological space

E(H,V)=G\times_{H}B_{R}(V)=G\times B_{R}(V)/{[(gh,x)\sim(g,hx)]}

(for some
R>0
), considered as a
G
-space via
g^{\prime}.[g,x]=[g^{\prime}g,x]
.
A
G
-cell complex
is a topological space with filtation
X_{0}\subseteq X_{1}\subseteq\dotsb
such that:

•

X_{0}
is a disjoint union of orbits
G/{H}
,

•

X_{n}=X_{n-1}\cup_{f}E(H_{n},V_{n})
, where
f\colon\partial E(H_{n},V_{n})\to X_{n-1}
is
G
-equivariant,

•

X=\colim_{n}X_{n}
.

Note that, should
V
already be a
G
-representation, the cell
E(H,V|_{H})
is equivariantly homeomorphic to
G/{H}\times B_{R}(V).

A
G
-cell complex in which all cells are modeled on trivial representations, meaning that all cells are of the form
G/{H}\times D^{n+1}
, is called a
G
-CW complex
. Any
G
-cell complex is
G
-homotopy equivalent to a
G
-CW complex (see
[
BPS21
, Proposition 3.3]
).

2.
Homotopy coherent diagrams

Homotopy coherent diagrams represent a relaxed notion of a diagram of topological spaces, one that is required to commute only up to coherent homotopies. In this section we present one approach of introducing group actions on homotopy coherent diagrams.

2.1.
Homotopy coherent diagrams

In the following, every indexing category
\mathcal{C}
will have the property that for any two objects
c,d\in\ob(\mathcal{C})
, there are only finitely many chains of morphisms starting at
c
and ending at
d
. In particular, the category
\mathcal{C}
does not have any non-identity isomorphisms. This last condition simplifies the formulas defining homotopy coherent diagrams and their homotopy colimits, allowing us to consider only non-identity morphisms (see
[
LLS20
, Observation 4.12]
).

The formalism for homotopy coherent diagrams that we use has been described by
[
Vog73
]
, already in the case of an arbitrary small indexing category. A slightly different realization, in the case of strictly commutative diagrams, can be found in
[
Seg68
]
, and a different approach was introduced by
[
BK72
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
denote a small category and assume that
\mathcal{C}
does not have non-identity isomorphisms. A
homotopy coherent diagram

F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Top}_{*}
consists of assignments: to each
x\in\mathcal{C}
, a pointed topological space
F(x)\in\mathrm{Top}_{*}
, and to each sequence
x_{0}\xrightarrow{f_{1}}x_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{2}}\dotsb\xrightarrow{f_{n}}x_{n}
of composable non-identity morphisms in
\mathcal{C}
, of a pointed continuous map

F(f_{n},\dotsc,f_{1})\colon[0,1]^{n-1}\times F(x_{0})\to F(x_{n}).

These maps are required to satisfy the following conditions:

•

F(f_{n},\dotsc,f_{i+1})(t_{i+1},\dotsc,t_{n-1})\circ F(f_{i},\dotsc,f_{1})(t_{1},\dotsc,t_{i-1})=F(f_{n},\dotsc,f_{1})(t_{1},\dotsc,t_{i-1},0,t_{i+1},\dotsc,t_{n-1}),

•

F(f_{n},\dotsc,f_{i+1}\circ f_{i},\dotsc,f_{1})(t_{1},\dotsc,t_{i-1},t_{i},\dotsc,t_{n-1})=F(f_{n},\dotsc,f_{1})(t_{1},\dotsc,t_{i-1},1,t_{i+1},\dotsc,t_{n-1}).

We will sometimes say that a homotopy coherent diagram
F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Top}_{*}
is
of shape
\mathcal{C}
.

Denote by
\mathcal{C}_{n+1}(x_{0},x_{n})
the set of composable chains of non-identity morphisms in
\mathcal{C}
of length
n
, starting at
x_{0}
and ending at
x_{n}
:

\mathcal{C}_{n+1}(x_{0},x_{n})\coloneqq\{x_{0}\xrightarrow{f_{1}}\dotsb\xrightarrow{f_{n}}x_{n}\mathrel{|}\forall i\kern 5.0ptf_{i}\neq\text{id}_{x_{i}}\}.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a homotopy coherent diagram
F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Top}_{*}
, the homotopy colimit of
F
is the pointed topological space

(1)

\hocolim F=\{\ast\}\disjoint\coprodop\displaylimits_{n\geq 0}\coprodop\displaylimits_{x_{0},x_{n}\in\ob(\mathcal{C})}\mathcal{C}_{n+1}(x_{0},x_{n})\times[0,1]^{n}\times F(x_{0})/{\sim},

with
\sim
defined as follows, for
f_{i}\colon x_{i-1}\to x_{i}
,
t_{i}\in[0,1]
,
p\in F(x_{0})
:

•

(f_{n},\dotsc,f_{1};t_{1},\dotsc,t_{n};\ast)\sim\ast,

•

(f_{n},\dotsc,f_{i+1};t_{i+1},\dotsc,t_{n};F(f_{i},\dotsc,f_{1})(t_{1},\dotsc,t_{i-1};p))\sim(f_{n},\dotsc,f_{1};t_{1},\dotsc,t_{i-1},0,t_{i+1},\dotsc,t_{n};p),

•

(f_{n},\dotsc,f_{i+1}\circ f_{i},\dotsc,f_{1};t_{1},\dotsc,t_{i-1},t_{i+1},\dotsc,t_{n};p)\sim(f_{n},\dotsc,f_{1};t_{1},\dotsc,t_{i-1},1,\dotsc,t_{i+1},\dotsc,t_{n};p)
,
i<n
,

•

(f_{n-1},\dotsc,f_{1};t_{1},\dotsc,t_{n-1};p)\sim(f_{n},\dotsc,f_{1};t_{1},\dotsc,t_{n-1},1;p)
.

Definition 2.3
.

A
homomorphism
of homotopy coherent diagrams
F_{1},F_{0}\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Top}_{*}
is a collection of (pointed, continuous) maps
\phi_{x}\colon F_{1}(x)\to F_{0}(x)
for each
x\in\ob(\mathcal{C})
such that

F_{0}(f_{n},\dotsc,f_{1})(t_{1},\dotsc,t_{n-1})\circ\phi_{x}=\phi_{y}\circ F_{1}(f_{n},\dotsc,f_{1})(t_{1},\dotsc,t_{n-1})

for all sequences
x\xrightarrow{f_{1}}\dotsb\xrightarrow{f_{n}}y
of morphisms in
\mathcal{C}
and all
(t_{1},\dotsc,t_{n-1})\in[0,1]^{n-1}
.

A homomorphism of homotopy coherent diagrams
\varphi\colon F_{1}\to F_{2}
induces a continuous map
\hocolim\varphi\colon\hocolim F_{1}\to\hocolim F_{2}
in
Equation

1
, via

(f_{n},\dotsc,f_{1};t_{1},\dotsc,t_{n};p)\mapsto(f_{n},\dotsc,f_{1};t_{1},\dotsc,t_{n};\varphi_{x_{0}}(p)),

where
p\in F(x_{0})
and
x_{0}
is the target of
f_{n}
.

A more relaxed notion of map between homotopy coherent diagrams takes the form of a larger homotopy coherent diagram, as in the definition below.

Definition 2.4
.

A
natural transformation
of homotopy coherent diagrams
F_{1},F_{0}\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Top}_{*}
is a homotopy coherent diagram
\eta\colon\underline{2}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Top}_{*}
with

\eta|_{\{i\}\times\mathcal{C}}=F_{i}

for
i=0,1
.

The data of a natural transformation also contains maps
\eta_{x}\colon F_{1}(x)\to F_{0}(x)
for
x\in\mathcal{C}
. If they are all weak homotopy equivalences, we call
\eta
a
weak equivalence
(of homotopy coherent diagrams). However, the
\eta_{x}
do not immediately commute with the identifications in
Equation

1
; rather, the comparison map on homotopy colimits is defined up to homotopy, and the following holds.

Proposition 2.5
.

(
[
Vog73
]
)
Let
F_{0},F_{1}\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Top}_{*}
be homotopy coherent diagrams and
\eta\colon F_{1}\to F_{0}
a natural transformation. Then there is a map
\hocolim\eta\colon\hocolim F_{1}\to\hocolim F_{0}
, well defined up to homotopy. If the components
\eta_{x}\colon F_{1}(x)\to F_{0}(x)
are all weak homotopy equivalences, so is
\hocolim\eta
.

2.2.
Homotopy coherent diagrams as topological diagrams

[
Vog73
]
provides another description of homotopy coherent diagrams, which is of use to us. The following again supposes that
\mathcal{C}
has no non-identity isomorphisms. We work in the category
\mathrm{Top}_{*}
of compactly generated topological spaces.

A
topological category
is a topologically enriched small category; that is, one in which the morphism sets carry a topology and composition is a continuous map. If
\mathcal{D}
is a topological category, a
topological diagram

F\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathrm{Top}_{*}
consists of a function

F_{0}\colon\ob(\mathcal{D})\to\ob(\mathrm{Top}_{*})

along with continuous maps

F_{A,B}\colon\mathcal{D}(A,B)_{+}\wedge F_{0}(A)\to F_{0}(B)

satisfying

•

F_{A,B}(\text{id}_{A};-)=\text{id}_{F_{0}(A)}
,

•

for all pairs of composable morphisms
A\xrightarrow{f}C\xrightarrow{g}B
,

F_{A,B}(g\circ f;-)=F_{C,B}(g;F_{A,C}(f;-)).

Given an ordinary small category
\mathcal{C}
, one can treat its morphism sets as discrete spaces, whereby topological diagrams are the same as commutative diagrams
\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Top}_{*}
. A more interesting construction is the following.

Definition 2.6
.

The
free topological category
associated to
\mathcal{C}
is the topological category
\free\mathcal{C}
wih
\ob(\free\mathcal{C})=\ob(\mathcal{C})
and morphism spaces

\free\mathcal{C}(A,B)=\disjoint_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{C}_{n+1}(A,B)\times I^{n}/{\sim}

where

\sim
is generated by

(f_{n},\dotsc,f_{0};t_{1},\dotsc,t_{i-1},1,t_{i},\dotsc,t_{n})\sim(f_{n},\dotsc,f_{i}\circ f_{i-1},\dotsc,f_{0};t_{1},\dotsc,t_{i-1},t_{i+1},\dotsc,t_{n}).

Composition in
\free\mathcal{C}
is defined by the formula

(f_{n},\dotsc,f_{0};t_{1},\dotsc,t_{n})\circ(g_{m},\dotsc,g_{0};u_{1},\dotsc,u_{m})=(g_{n},\dotsc,f_{0},g_{m},\dotsc,g_{0};u_{1},\dotsc,u_{m},0,t_{1},\dotsc,t_{n}).

The assignment is functorial; given a functor of small categories
\eta\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
, there is an induced continuous functor
\free\eta\colon\free\mathcal{C}\to\free\mathcal{D}
given by

\free\eta(f_{n},\dotsc,f_{0};t_{1},\dotsc,t_{n})=(\eta f_{n},\dotsc,\eta f_{0},t_{1},\dotsc,t_{n}).

The free topological category construction allows us to redefine homotopy coherent diagrams as follows.

Definition 2.7
.

A homotopy coherent diagram
F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Top}_{*}
is defined as a topological diagram
F\colon\free\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Top}_{*}
.

Given a category
\mathcal{C}
, let
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
denote the ’cone category over
\mathcal{C}
’, that is the category with objects
\ob(\tilde{\mathcal{C}})=\ob(\mathcal{C})\cup\{\ast\}
,
\ast
being made a terminal object of
\mathcal{C}
.
Define the topological functor
\phi_{\bullet}\colon\free\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Top}_{*}
by

\phi_{\bullet}(c)=\free\tilde{\mathcal{C}}(c,\ast).

Then the formula for the homotopy colimit in
Equation

1
can be restated as follows:

(2)

\hocolim F=\coeq\left(\coprodop\displaylimits_{u,v\in\mathcal{C}}\phi_{\bullet}(d)\wedge\free\mathcal{C}(c,d)\wedge F(c)\rightrightarrows\coprodop\displaylimits_{u\in\ob(\mathcal{C})}\phi_{\bullet}(c)\wedge F(c)\right).

This description has been pointed out by
[
Ste11
]
, who also defines homotopy colimits using a universal property. Namely, suppose we are given a homotopy coherent diagram
F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Top}_{*}
and its extension to a homotopy coherent diagram
G\colon\tilde{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathrm{Top}_{*}
. Then, any pointed continuous map
\varphi\colon G(\ast)\to Z
induces another extension of
F
to a homotopy coherent diagram
G^{\prime}\colon\tilde{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathrm{Top}_{*}
with
G^{\prime}(\ast)=Z
. Consider then the category with objects extensions of
F
to a coherent diagram
G\colon\tilde{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathrm{Top}_{*}
, and morphisms
G\to G^{\prime}
the maps
\varphi\colon G(\ast)\to G^{\prime}(\ast)
such that
G^{\prime}
is induced from
G
by
\varphi
. The
homotopy colimit
of
F
can be defined as an initial object in this category; indeed,
[
Ste11
, Proposition 3.2]
verifies that
Equation

2
satisfies this universal property.

A natural transformation of homotopy coherent diagrams
\eta\colon F_{1}\rightrightarrows F_{0}
of shape
\mathcal{C}
is then a topological functor
\eta\colon\free(\underline{2}\times\mathcal{C})\to\mathrm{Top}_{*}
with
F_{i}=\eta\circ\free(\iota_{i})
, where
\iota_{i}\colon\mathcal{C}\to\underline{2}\times\mathcal{C}
denotes the inclusions for
i=1,0
. Respectively, a homomorphism
F_{1}\to F_{2}
is a topological functor
\theta\colon\underline{2}\times\free{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathrm{Top}_{*}
.

2.3.
External actions on homotopy coherent diagrams

Equivariant diagrams of spaces have been studied i.e. in
[
JS01
]
,
[
VF23
]
, who consider the notion of an action of a group
G
on a diagram
X\colon I\to\mathcal{C}
in the case that
I
carries an action by
G
;
[
DM16
]
and
[
Dot16
]
have further considered properties of homotopy colimits of such functors. We instead require a notion of a “
G
-homotopy coherent diagram”, and one such has been proposed in
[
SZ18
]
.

Recall that we consider a group
G
as a category with one object and morphisms the elements of
G
, and an action of
G
on a category
\mathcal{C}
is a functor

\gamma\colon G\to\cats

with
\gamma(\ast)=\mathcal{C}
.
Likewise, if
\mathcal{C}
is a topological category, an action of
G
on
\mathcal{C}
is a functor

\gamma\colon G\to\cats_{\mathrm{Top}}

that picks out
\mathcal{C}
.

Observe that in the presence of a group action of a group
G
on a small category
\mathcal{C}
, the free topological category
\free\mathcal{C}
carries an induced action of
G
which on objects agrees with the action on
\mathcal{C}
and on morphism spaces is defined by

g.(f_{n},\dotsc,f_{0};t_{1},\dotsc,t_{n})=(g.f_{n},\dotsc,g.f_{0};t_{1},\dotsc,t_{n}).

The following definition extends
[
JS01
, Definition 2.2]
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category with an action
\gamma\colon G\to\cats
and let
F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Top}_{*}
be a homotopy coherent diagram.

An
external action
of
G
on
F
compatible with
\gamma
consists of a family of homomorphisms of homotopy coherent diagrams

\{\psi_{g}\colon F\rightrightarrows F\circ\gamma_{g}\mathrel{|}g\in G\}

satisfying:

(1)

\psi_{e}=\text{id}_{F}
,

(2)

for any
g,h\in G
,
\psi_{hg}=(\psi_{h},\text{id}_{g})\circ\psi_{g}.

An equivalent definition is
[
SZ18
, Definition 5.1]
, which calls for a homomorphism
\psi\colon G\to\homeo(\vee_{c\in\ob(\mathcal{C})}F(c))
compatible with
\gamma
and such that

g.F(f_{n},\dotsc,f_{0};t_{1},\dotsc,t_{n};p)=F(g.f_{n},\dotsc,g.f_{0};t_{1},\dotsc,t_{n};g.p).

In the presence of an external action, the homotopy colimit realized as the coequalizer of
Equation

2
carries an action of
G
by

g.(t,x)=(gt,gx)
,
as the two defining maps are equivariant. In terms of
Equation

1
, the action reads

(3)

g.(f_{n},\dotsc,f_{1};t_{1},\dotsc,t_{n};p)=(g.f_{n},\dotsc,g.f_{1};t_{1},\dotsc,t_{n};\psi_{g}(p)).

2.4.
Fixed point diagrams

Suppose that

acts on a small category
\mathcal{C}
and

has objects and morphisms those fixed by

is defined analogously. The two topological categories

and

Moreover, the fixed point set

.

(
[

, Lemma 5.6]

Definition 3.4
.

A

3.2.
Permutohedra and group actions

Let
n\geq 1
be a natural number. The symmetric group
\Sigma_{n}
acts on
n
by

\sigma.(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})=(x_{\sigma^{-1}(1)},\dotsc,x_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}).

Suppose
S=(s_{1},\dotsc,s_{n})\in\real^{n}
is an increasing sequence of real numbers. The
S
-permutohedron

\permutohedron_{S}
is the convex hull of
\Sigma_{n}
-translations of
S
;
if
[n]=(1,\dotsc,n)
, we write
\permutohedron_{n-1}=\permutohedron_{[n]}
.

For
i=1,\dotsc,n,
let
\tau_{i}=\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}.
If
P\subseteq S
is a non-empty subset, consider the half-space

H_{P}=\left\{(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})\in\real^{n}\mathrel{|}\sumop\displaylimits_{i\in P}x_{i}\geq\tau_{\#{P}}\right\}.

Then the permutohedron
\permutohedron_{S}
can equivalently be defined as

\permutohedron_{S}=(\setintersection_{\emptyset\neq P\subsetneq S}H_{P})\cap\partial H_{S}

(see e.g.
[
BP15
, Theorem 1.5.7]
).
In fact, for any ordered partition
P_{1}\cup\dotsb\cup P_{r}
of
S
, the subset

\permutohedron_{P_{1},\dotsc,P_{r}}=\permutohedron_{S}\cap\setintersection_{i=1,\dotsc,r-1}\partial H_{P_{1}\cup\dotsb\cup P_{i}}

is an
(n-r+1)
-dimensional face of
\permutohedron_{S}.

In particular, each
\permutohedron_{P,S\setminus P}
is a facet in the sense of
Section

3.1
. Moreover,
\permutohedron_{P_{1},\dotsc,P_{r}}
is a facet of each of the facets
\permutohedron_{P_{1},\dotsc,P_{i-1}\cup P_{i},P_{i+1},\dotsc,P_{r}}
,
i=1,\dotsc,r-1.
It is the intersection of any two of them; indeed all intersections of the sets
\permutohedron_{P_{1},\dotsc,P_{r}}
arise in this way, and the following holds.

Proposition 3.10
.

(
[
LLS20
, Lemma 3.14]
)

The polyhedron
\permutohedron_{S}
becomes an
(n-1)
-dimensional
\langle n\rangle
-manifold by letting

\partial_{i}\permutohedron_{S}=\coprodop\displaylimits_{\#{P}=i}\permutohedron_{P,S\setminus P}.

For any face
\permutohedron_{P_{1},\dotsc,P_{k+1}}
, there are
2^{k}
faces that contain it, all of the form
\permutohedron_{P_{1},\dotsc,P_{i}\cup\dotsb\cup P_{j},\dotsc,P_{k+1}}
; for the sake of this statement, we treat
\permutohedron_{S}
as the ’face’ corresponding to the trivial partition. Denote by
C_{P_{1},\dotsc,P_{k+1}}
the convex hull of barycentra of all the faces containing
\permutohedron_{P_{1},\dotsc,P_{k+1}}

Lemma 3.11
.

[
LLS20
, cf. Lemma 3.15]

Each of the
C_{P_{1},\dotsc,P_{k+1}}
is combinatorially equivalent to a
k
-dimensional cube, and these cubes form a cubical subdivision of
\permutohedron_{S}.

The poset of faces of
\permutohedron_{S}
is isomorphic to the poset of internal chains in
\{0,1\}^{n}
: to the face
\permutohedron_{P_{1},\dotsc,P_{k+1}}
one associates the chain
u^{1}>\dotsb>u^{k}
with

u^{j}_{i}=\begin{cases}1,&s_{i}\in P_{1}\cup\dotsb\cup P_{j},\\
0,&s_{i}\in P_{j+1}\cup\dotsb\cup P_{k+1}.\end{cases}

Consider now the action of a cyclic subgroup
\langle\sigma\rangle\subseteq\Sigma_{n}
on
n
. The sets
\permutohedron_{S}
and
\{0,1\}^{n}
are invariant, and so carry the induced action. The poset of faces of
\permutohedron_{S}
also carries this action,
via the corresponding action on the poset of internal chains in
\{0,1\}^{n}
:

g.(u^{1}>\dotsb>u^{k})=(g.u^{1}>\dotsb>g.u^{k}).

Now, the fixed point poset of the latter action is isomorphic to the poset of internal chains in some other
\{0,1\}^{n^{\prime}}.
As the
\langle\sigma\rangle
-action descends also to the set of barycentra of faces of
\permutohedron_{S}
, the following holds.

Proposition 3.12
.

The fixed-point set
\permutohedron_{S}^{\langle\sigma\rangle}
is a cubical subcomplex, combinatorially equivalent to a lower-dimensional permutohedron.

Indeed,
[
BPS21
, Proposition B.18]
establishes that this can be realized as a diffeomorphism of
(n^{\prime}-1)
-dimensional
\langle n^{\prime}-1\rangle
-manifolds.

3.3.
The cube flow category

The free topological category construction applied to a poset
\mathcal{C}
yields a topological category
\free\mathcal{C}
whose morphism spaces are naturally decomposed as cubical complexes. Abouzaid and Blumberg
[
AB21
, Section 2.1]
used this in the case that
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{P}
is a
finite-dimensional poset
in the sense that between for any two elements
p,q\in\mathcal{P}
, lengths of chains in
\mathcal{P}
starting at
p
and ending at
q
form a finite set. The upshot is that moduli spaces of
\free\mathcal{P}
are finite-dimensional and have a well-defined boundary.

Consider the topological category
C(n)\coloneqq\free\underline{2}^{n}
as defined in
Section

2.3
. We require a description of morphisms spaces of
C(n)
; one such description appears as early as
[
Lei74
]
, see also
[
Blo11
]
.

Following the definitions in
Section

, given

, the space of morphisms

is

\disjoint_{m\geq 0}\underline{2}^{n}(u,v)_{m}\times[0,1]^{m}/{\sim},

where
\underline{2}^{n}(u,v)_{m}
is the set of chains in
\underline{2}^{n}
of length
m
, lying entirely between
u
and
v
. Here, for
P\subseteq S
two chains between
u
and
v
,
\sim
identifies the cube
[0,1]^{P}
corresponding to

to the subset of
[0,1]^{S}
obtained by inserting

s at

-coordinates.
Thus,
C(n)(u,v)
is isomorphic as a cubical complex to the permutohedron
\permutohedron^{u\Delta v}
as described in
Lemma

3.11
; here,

u\Delta v=\{i\in\{1,\dotsc,n\}\mathrel{|}u_{i}=1,v_{i}=0\}.

Likewise, the composition maps in
C(n)
recover the multifacets
\partial C(n)(u,v)
making up the boundary of
C(n)(u,v)
via

\partial_{i}C(n)(u,v)=\coprodop\displaylimits_{\lvert v\rvert-\lvert w\rvert=i}\circ(C(n)(v,w)\times C(n)(u,v))\subseteq\partial C(n)(u,v).

Definition 3.13
.

The

is the topological category

.

(
[

, Proposition 3.10]

(F(gu,gv,gv^{\prime},gw)\circ_{2}\text{id})\circ(\text{id}\circ\psi{g,u,v})\circ(\psi_{g,v,w}\circ\text{id})=(\text{id}\circ\psi_{g,u,v^{\prime}})\circ(\psi_{g,v^{\prime},w}\circ\text{id})\circ(\text{id}\circ F(u,v,v^{\prime},w)).

Then there exists a Burnside functor

admitting an external
\mathbb Z_{m}
-action, uniquely up to
\mathbb Z_{m}
-equivariant isomorphism.

5.
Spatial refinements

The aim of this chapter is to, given a Burnside functor (with external action), produce homotopy coherent diagrams with the property that in the homotopy colimit, vertices of the diagram correspond to cells and the Burnside functor describes degrees of attaching maps.

5.1.
Stars and star maps

The constructions presented in this sections realise a version of the “charge map”, associating to a configuration of points in
n
a map of spheres
S^{n}\to S^{n}
(see
[
Seg73
, Section 1]
). The approach taken here (after
[
LLS20
]
) allows for composing such maps between (wedges of) spheres along a Burnside functor, in the end furnishing a homotopy coherent diagram. This was already done equivariantly in
[
SZ18
, Section 4.4]
, using spaces of little disks; for our purposes, a wider family of shapes must be used, contatining both disks and products of disks.

In the scope of this section,
V
will denote an orthogonal representation of a group
G
.

For
X
a finite
G
-set, the
configuration space
of points of
X
in a
G
-space
Y
is the space

\conf_{X}(Y)=\{\{p_{x}\}_{x\in X}\in Y^{k}\mathrel{|}\forall x\neq y\in X\kern 5.0ptp_{x}\neq p_{y}\}.

Equivalently, a configuration can be seen as an embedding
f\colon X\to Y
, whereby
\conf_{X}(Y)
carries an action of
G
by

(g.f)(x)=g.f(g^{-1}.x).

We aim to describe one of the possible equivariant versions of the Pontryagin-Thom collapse map, associating a map between spheres to a configuration. This entails replacing the points of a configuration by “little stars”, as expanded upon below.

Let
\sphere(V)
denote the unit sphere in
V
. Let
f\colon\sphere(V)\to\real_{+}
be a continuous map. By a
star
in
V
we will mean the set

B(p,f)=p+\{\alpha\cdot v\in V\mathrel{|}v\in V,\kern 5.0pt\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq(v)\}\subseteq V

for some
f
as above and
p\in V
. The point
p
is then called the center point of
B(p,f)
, and a star is understood to come with a distinguished center point. If
f
is
G
-invariant, we call
B(0,f)
an
invariant star
in
V
. Any star is a star-shaped subset of
V
; an invariant star in
V
is a
G
-invariant subset of
V
, and as such becomes a
G
-space.

Let
\mathbb A\colon\underline{2}\to\burnside
be a Burnside functor with external action by
G
, subordinate to the trivial action of
G
on
\underline{2}
. Per
6.1
, this consists of a correspondence
X\xleftarrow{s}A\xrightarrow{t}Y
along with actions
\phi_{g,X}\colon X\to X
,
\phi_{g,Y}\colon Y\to Y
and
\phi_{g,A}\colon A\to A
, which commute with the source- and target-maps.

Suppose
\{B_{x}\}_{x\in X}
is a set of
G
-invariant stars in
V
. We fix the radial homeomorphisms
{\varphi_{x,y}\colon B_{x}\to B_{y}}
for all
x,y
; these satisfy
\varphi_{x,z}=\varphi_{y,z}\circ\varphi_{x,y}
. The space
B(X,V)\coloneqq\disjoint_{x\in X}B_{x}
carries an action of
G
by
g.(x,v)=(g.x,g.\phi_{x,g.x}(v)).

The space
\conf(\{B_{x}\},s)
is defined as the space of embeddings
\gamma\colon A\to B(X,V)
satisfying
\gamma(a)\in B_{s(a)}.
Topologically, this is the same as
\disjoint_{x\in X}\conf_{s^{-1}(x)}(B_{x}).
The space
\conf(\{B_{x}\},s)
carries an action of
G
by

(g.\gamma)(a)=g.\varphi_{g,g.x}\gamma(g^{-1}.a).

Replacing the points of

by little stars, we consider the space
\mathrm{Stars}(\{B_{x}\},s)
of embeddings of

-labeled stars in

, again with

. This is topologized as a subset of
{\conf(\{B_{x}\},s)\times\maps(X\times S(V),\real^{+})}
, and again carries an action by

(g.\gamma)(a,v)=g.\varphi_{g,g.x}\gamma(g^{-1}.a,v).

A configuration of stars with centers
\{p_{x}\}_{x\in X}
can be deformed to one whose stars are all spheres with radius

\frac{1}{3}\min_{x,y\in X}(d(p_{x},p_{y}),d(p_{x},V\setminus B)).

This establishes a strong equivariant deformation retraction from
\mathrm{Stars}(\{B_{x}\},s)
to a bundle of points over

. The map is also equivariant and induces homotopy equivalences of fixed-point sets, implying the following.

Lemma 5.1
.

The spaces
\mathrm{Stars}(\{B_{x}\},s)
and
\conf(\{B_{x}\},s)
are
G
-homotopy equivalent.

Note that the target map of the correspondence played no role in the definition of
\mathrm{Stars}(\{B_{x}\}_{X},s)
; rather, it becomes relevant in the definition of the associated map of spheres. Let
S^{V}
denote the one-point compactification

, considered as a
G
-space.

Definition 5.2
.

(
[

6.1.
Musyt’s formalism

Definition 6.1
.

Fix a Burnside functor

Say there exists an action of
G
by
\phi
on
\underline{2}^{n}.
An

consists of the following data:

(1)

a collection of bijections

\{\phi_{g,v}\colon F(v)\to F(gv)\mathrel{|}g\in G,\kern 5.0ptv\in\underline{2}^{n}\},

(2)

(2)

(3)

6.2.
Musyt and SZ

Essentially, Musyt’s formalism corresponds to that of Stoffregen-Zhang by exchanging

for bijections of sets, and some of the

Construction 6.2
.

From Musyt data of external action we produce Stoffregen-Zhang external action as follows.

(1)

From a bijection
\phi_{g,u}\colon F(u)\to F(gu),
we produce a correspondence
\psi_{g,u}\colon F(u)\to F(gu)

\psi_{g,u}\coloneqq F(u)\xleftarrow{\text{id}}F(u)\xrightarrow{\phi_{g,u}}F(gu).

(2)

They are

(3)

From bijection

where

for

Just the same, the Burnside functor

8.2.
Periodic links

An
m
-periodic link
is one invariant under a rotation of the sphere of order
m
, and disjoint from the axis of that rotation. We will give a digest of the constructions of equivariant Khovanov homotopy types due to
[
SZ18
]
and
[
BPS21
]
. For a given link, there may be more than one such rotation, defining to distinct equivariant spectra; hence, the rotation is fixed at the outset.

For the following, let
D
be a link diagram with
N=nm
crossings, invariant under a rotation of the plane
\rho
of order
m
, such that
\rho(D)=D.
Consequently, there is an action on the cube of resolutions, which upon numbering the crossings takes the form of the natural
\mathbb Z_{m}
-action on
(\underline{2}^{n})^{m}\cong\underline{2}^{nm}
as in
Section

1.1
.

[
SZ18
, Proposition 6.2]
construct an external action of
\mathbb Z_{m}
on the Khovanov-Burnside functor
F_{Kh}\colon\underline{2}^{n}\to\burnside
using
Lemma

4.9
. The construction is forced in almost all cases by the group action on
(\underline{2}^{n})^{m}
and the non-equivariant
F
itself. The exceptional case is that of ladybug configurations, and the well-definedness of the action follows from the fact that ladybug configurations are invariant under planar isotopy. In parallel,
[
BPS21
, Proposition 4.6]
use a simplification result
[
BPS21
, Lemma 3.8]
and construct moduli spaces inductively, with all steps but the one pertaining ladybug configurations already forced.

It is clear that the two constructions are related by the equivalences presented here in
Section

6
. The results of
Section

7
imply the following.

Theorem 2
.

The equivariant stable homotopy types
\lvert\lvert\mathcal{C}\rvert\rvert
and
\hocolim\tilde{F}_{V}^{+}
associated to a periodic link by
[
BPS21
]
and
[
SZ18
]
, respectively, are equivariantly stably homotopy equivalent. The equivalence can be realised as
\Sigma^{\infty}\Psi
, where
\Psi
is a cellular map depending on a choice of extended equivariant cubical framed embedding
\tilde{\iota}
of
\mathcal{C}
.

Let
\mathbb F
be a field. Up to chain homotopy, the Khovanov complex
\ckh(D;\mathbb{F})
can be equipped with an action of
\mathbb Z_{m}
, whereby it can be seen as a
\mathbb{F}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
-module. In
[

]

-module

\ekh^{j,q}(D;M)=\ext^{j}_{\mathbb{F}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]}(M;\ckh^{\bullet,q}(D;\mathbb{F})).

In
[
BPS21

it is proved that
\lvert\lvert\mathcal{C}\rvert\rvert
realizes this notion of equivariant Khovanov homology via Borel cohomology.

Corollary 8.1
.

Let

m
-periodic link diagram and

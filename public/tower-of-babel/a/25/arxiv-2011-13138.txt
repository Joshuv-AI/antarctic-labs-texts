Irreducible components of two-row Springer fibers for all classical types
Mee Seong Im, Chun-Ju Lai, and Arik Wilbert

Abstract
. We give an explicit description of the irreducible components of two-row Springer fibers for all classical types using cup diagrams. Cup diagrams can be used to label the irreducible components of two-row Springer fibers. We use these diagrams to explicitly write down relations between the vector spaces of the flags contained in a given irreducible component. This generalizes results by Stroppel–Webster and Fung for type A to all classical types.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a connected complex reductive group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Given a nilpotent element
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, the corresponding Springer fiber
\mathcal{B}_{x}
is defined as the variety of all Borel subgroups
B\subseteq G
whose Lie algebra contains
x
. If
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
(type A), the Springer fiber
\mathcal{B}_{x}
can be realized more concretely as the variety of all complete flags
\{0\}=F_{0}\subseteq F_{1}\subseteq F_{2}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq F_{n}=\mathbb{C}^{n}
satisfying
xF_{i}\subseteq F_{i-1}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. For the other classical groups
\mathrm{SO}_{n}
(types B and D) and
\mathrm{Sp}_{n}
(type C) one additionally imposes the condition
F_{n-i}^{\perp}=F_{i}
for all
0\leq i\leq n
. Here, the orthogonal complement is taken with respect to some fixed nondegenerate symmetric (types B and D) or symplectic (type C) bilinear form compatible with the nilpotent
x\in\mathfrak{g}
. The varieties
\mathcal{B}_{x}
naturally appear as the fibers under the Springer resolution and play an important role in classical geometric representation theory,
[
Spr76
,
Spr78
]
. Their geometric properties and combinatorial structure remain largely mysterious. In general, Springer fibers are not smooth and decompose into many equidimensional irreducible components.

In this article, we study Springer fibers for classical groups
G
, where the nilpotent element
x\in\mathfrak{g}
has two Jordan blocks. Two-row Springer fibers are known to have some desirable geometric properties, e.g., the irreducible components are all smooth,
[
Fun03
,
FM10
,
ES16a
]
. Moreover, they are known to have remarkable connections to representation theory and low-dimensional topology.
For
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, it was shown by Khovanov in
[
Kho04
]
that the cohomology ring of a two-row Springer fiber is isomorphic to the center of the arc algebra appearing in his construction of link homology (see
[
Kho02
]
). Arc algebras and their generalizations in
[
BS11a
,
CK14
]
have shown intriguing connections to representation theory, e.g., they describe interesting categories of representations of the general Lie algebras,
[
BS11b
]
, nonsemisimple walled Brauer algebras,
[
BS12a
]
, and the general linear Lie superalgebras,
[
BS12b
]
. In
[
SW12
]
, Stroppel–Webster constructed the entire arc algebra (and their generalizations from
[
BS11a
,
CK14
]
) as a convolution algebra using two-row Springer fibers.

For two-row Springer fibers, the irreducible components can be classified in terms of so-called cup diagrams,
[
Fun03
,
ES16a
]
. This diagrammatic tool does not only provide a direct connection to the combinatorics of the arc algebra, but it is particularly useful in writing down explicit relations characterizing all flags contained in a given irreducible component. For
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, such an explicit description goes back to
[
SW12
]
based on
[
Fun03
]
(see Theorem
2.8
). The goal of this article is to extend this result to two-row Springer fibers for all classical groups (see Theorem
2.16
).

Studying the geometric, topological, and combinatorial properties of Springer fibers is an active field of research. Our work focusing on two-row Springer fibers is largely motivated by the fact that the center of the arc algebras of types B and D constructed in
[
ES16b
]
are isomorphic to cohomology rings of two-row Springer fibers of types C and D,
[
ES16a
,
SW19
]
. This relates these Springer fibers to representation theory of orthogonal Lie algebras,
[
ES16b
]
, nonsemisimple Brauer algebras,
[
ES16c
]
, and orthosymplectic Lie superalgebras,
[
ES16d
]
.

Before this article was written, the only explicit algebro-geometric construction of irreducible components of two-row Springer fibers outside of type A required an intricate inductive procedure, see
[
ES16a
, §6]
, based on
[
Spa82
,
vL89
]
. Other than that, only topological models were available,
[
ES16a
,
Wil18
]
.

This article is structured as follows. In Section 2 we provide the basic definitions, introduce the diagrammatic tools, state our main result, and discuss examples. It should be pointed out that our main theorem is not a straightforward generalization of the type A result from
[
SW12
]
and
[
Fun03
]
. In fact, due to the appearance of the bilinear form, quite a couple of new phenomena appear when generalizing the known results for type A to the other classical types. Our main theorem is proved in Section 3. The key idea is to prove that the explicit subvarieties we claim to be the irreducible components are iterated fiber bundles over
\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1}
.

2.
The main result

In this section, we recall some basic facts about Springer fibers, state our main theorem, and provide examples.

2.1.
Nilpotent orbits and Springer fibers

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
be a positive integer and let
V
be an
n
-dimensional complex vector space. Let
G=\mathrm{GL}(V)
be the group of all linear automorphisms of
V
. The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}(V)
of
G
consists of all linear endomorphisms of
V
.
Let
\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathfrak{gl}(V)
be the variety of all linear endomorphisms which are nilpotent. It is well known that the orbits under the conjugation-action of
G
on
\mathcal{N}
can be parametrized by partitions of
n
. Recall that a partition
\lambda
of
n
, denoted by
\lambda\vdash n
, is a finite sequence
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{r}
,
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, of weakly decreasing positive integers
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{r}
summing up to
n
. Given a nilpotent orbit, the associated partition
\lambda
encodes the Jordan type of the endomorphisms in that orbit, i.e., the parts of
\lambda
encode the sizes of the different Jordan blocks.

We fix
\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
together with a non-degenerate bilinear form
\beta_{\epsilon}
on
V
satisfying
\beta_{\epsilon}(v,w)=\epsilon\beta_{\epsilon}(w,v)
for all
v,w\in V
. Let
G_{\epsilon}
be the group of all linear automorphisms of
V
preserving
\beta_{\epsilon}
. Let
\mathfrak{g}_{\epsilon}
be the Lie algebra of
G_{\epsilon}
, i.e., the subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
consisting of all linear endomorphisms
x
of
V
satisfying
\beta_{\epsilon}(x(v),w)=-\beta_{\epsilon}(v,x(w))
for all
v,w\in V
. We have isomorphisms
G_{1}\cong\textup{O}(n,\mathbb{C})
and
G_{-1}\cong\textup{Sp}(n,\mathbb{C})
. Given
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we refer to the groups
\mathrm{GL}(m,\mathbb{C})
,
\textup{O}(2m+1,\mathbb{C})
,
\textup{Sp}(2m,\mathbb{C})
, and
\textup{O}(2m,\mathbb{C})
as the classical groups of types A, B, C, and D, respectively.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}(n)
be the subset of all partitions of
n
defined by

(2.1)

\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}(n)=\{\lambda\vdash n\mid\#\{i\mid\lambda_{i}=j\}\in 2\mathbb{Z}\textup{ for all }j\text{ with }(-1)^{j}=\epsilon\}.

The following result classifies the nilpotent orbits.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
Ger61
,
Wil37
]
)
.

The orbits under the conjugation-action of
G_{\epsilon}
on the variety
\mathcal{N}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\epsilon}
of all nilpotent elements in
\mathfrak{g}_{\epsilon}
are in bijective correspondence with the partitions contained in
\mathcal{P}_{\epsilon}(n)
. The parts of the partition associated with the orbit of an endomorphism encode the sizes of the Jordan blocks in Jordan normal form.

A complete flag in
V
is a sequence
F_{\bullet}
of nested vector subspaces
\{0\}=F_{0}\subsetneq F_{1}\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq F_{n}=V
. We write
\mathcal{B}
to denote the set of all flags in
V
. A complete flag
F_{\bullet}
is called isotropic (with respect to
\beta_{\epsilon}
) if
F_{n-i}=F_{i}^{\perp}
for all
i\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
. Here, the orthogonal complement is taken with respect to
\beta_{\epsilon}
. The set of all complete isotropic flags is denoted by
\mathcal{B}^{\beta_{\epsilon}}
. Both
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\beta_{\epsilon}}
can be identified with a homogeneous space, thereby giving these sets the structure of a smooth projective variety.

Definition 2.2
.

The Springer fiber
\mathcal{B}_{x}
corresponding to
x\in\mathcal{N}
is the subvariety of
\mathcal{B}
consisting of all complete flags which satisfy the condition
xF_{i}\subseteq F_{i-1}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. The Springer fiber
\mathcal{B}^{\beta_{\epsilon}}_{x}
associated with
\beta_{\epsilon}
and
x\in\mathcal{N}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\epsilon}
is the subvariety of
\mathcal{B}^{\beta_{\epsilon}}
consisting of all isotropic flags
F_{\bullet}
which satisfy the conditions
xF_{i}\subseteq F_{i-1}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Remark 2.3
.

We would like to point out that we work with the orthogonal group instead of the special orthogonal group in this article. This is mainly for technical reasons. In particular, the corresponding flag varieties and Springer fibers in type D decompose into two connected components (see e.g.
[
vL89
, §1.4]
for details).

In this article, we focus on two-row Springer fibers, i.e., the nilpotent endomorphism has Jordan type
\lambda=(n-k,k)
. Note that by the classification of nilpotent orbits in Proposition
2.1
, there is no two-row Springer fiber of type B. Moreover, we have the following result which reduces the problem of describing two-row Springer fibers for type C to the type D case.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
Wil18
, Theorem B]
,
[
Li19
, Theorem 8.4.1]
)
.

Let
x\in\mathcal{N}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{1}
be of Jordan type
(n-k,k)
and
y\in\mathcal{N}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
be of Jordan type
(n-k-1,k-1)
. Let
\overline{\mathcal{B}}^{\beta_{1}}_{x}
be one of the two isomorphic connected components of the Springer fiber
\mathcal{B}^{\beta_{1}}_{x}
. Then we have an isomorphism of algebraic varieties
\overline{\mathcal{B}}^{\beta_{1}}_{x}\cong\mathcal{B}^{\beta_{-1}}_{y}
.

For the remainder of this article, we use the following explicit conventions for Springer fibers of types A and D: given a two-row partition
\lambda=(n-k,k)
of
n
, we define an
n
-dimensional complex vector space
V_{\lambda}
with (ordered) basis

(2.2)

\{e^{\lambda}_{1},e^{\lambda}_{2},\ldots,e^{\lambda}_{n-k},f^{\lambda}_{1},f_{2}^{\lambda},\ldots,f^{\lambda}_{k}\}.

Let
x_{\lambda}
be the nilpotent endomorphism given by

(2.3)

e^{\lambda}_{n-k}\mapsto e^{\lambda}_{n-k-1}\mapsto\ldots\mapsto e^{\lambda}_{1}\mapsto 0,\quad f^{\lambda}_{k}\mapsto\ldots\mapsto f^{\lambda}_{1}\mapsto 0.

Usually, when there is no ambiguity, we omit the superscripts
\lambda
in (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) and we denote the endomorphism
x_{\lambda}
by
x
. The type A Springer fiber corresponding to these data is denoted by
\mathcal{B}_{n-k,k}
.

For the type D case, we assume that
n=2m
is even,
\epsilon=1
, and
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
, i.e.,
\lambda
is of the following form:

(2.4)

\lambda=(m,m),\quad\textup{or}\quad\lambda=(n-k,k)\in(2\mathbb{Z}+1)^{2}.

We also fix the non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form
\beta_{\lambda}:V_{\lambda}\times V_{\lambda}\to\mathbb{C}
,
whose associated matrix with respect to the ordered basis (
2.2
) is given by

(2.5)

M^{\lambda}=\begin{cases}\small\begin{array}[]{ *{8}{c} }&\{e^{\lambda}_{i}\}&\{f^{\lambda}_{i}\}\\
\{e^{\lambda}_{i}\}&0&J_{m}\\
\{f^{\lambda}_{i}\}&J^{t}_{m}&0\\
\end{array}~~~\normalsize&\>\>\textup{if }n-k=k;\\
\small\begin{array}[]{ *{8}{c} }&\{e^{\lambda}_{i}\}&\{f^{\lambda}_{i}\}\\
\{e^{\lambda}_{i}\}&J_{n-k}&0\\
\{f^{\lambda}_{i}\}&0&J_{k}\\
\end{array}~~~\normalsize&\>\>\textup{if }n-k>k,\end{cases}\quad\textup{where}\quad J_{i}=\small\begin{pmatrix}&&&1\\
&&-1&\\
&\iddots&&\\
(-1)^{i-1}\end{pmatrix}\normalsize.

As above, we usually omit the superscripts in (
2.5
) when the partition is fixed. The type D Springer fiber corresponding to the above data is denoted by
\mathcal{B}^{\textup{D}}_{n-k,k}
.

2.2.
Irreducible components of type A two-row Springer fibers

In
[
Spa76
,
Var79
]
, the irreducible components of type A Springer fibers were classified in terms of Young tableaux. For two-row Springer fibers, it is convenient to label the irreducible components by so-called cup diagrams,
[
Fun03
]
. For the rest of this subsection, we fix a two-row partition
\lambda=(n-k,k)
of
n
.

Definition 2.5
.

We fix a rectangle in the plane together with
n
evenly spaced vertices on the top horizontal edge of the rectangle. A cup diagram is a non-intersecting arrangement of cups and vertical rays inside the rectangle such that each vertex is connected to exactly one endpoint of either a cup or a ray. We write
I_{n-k,k}
to denote the set of all cup diagrams on
n
vertices with
k
cups.

2.3.
Irreducible components of type D two-row Springer fibers

For types B, C, and D, the irreducible components of Springer fibers can be classified in terms of so-called signed domino tableaux,
[
vL89
,
Spa82
]
. We now give an explicit characterization of the irreducible components of two-row Springer fibers for all classical types which generalizes the result for two-row Springer fibers of type A in Theorem
2.8
. Recall that by Proposition
2.4
it suffices to treat the type D case. In this subsection, we assume that
n=2m
is even and we fix a partition
\lambda
of the form as in (
2.4
).

In order to describe the irreducible components of type D two-row Springer fibers, we generalize Definition
2.5
to also include so-called marked cup diagrams.

Definition 2.10
.

We call a cup or ray in a cup diagram accessible from the right if it can be connected to the right vertical edge of the rectangle by a path which does not intersect any other cup or ray. A marked cup diagram is a cup diagram in which any cup or ray that is accessible from the right is allowed to be decorated with a single marker (a small black square). We write
I_{n-k,k}^{\textup{D}}
to denote the set of all marked cup diagrams on
m
vertices with
\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor
cups. This set decomposes as a disjoint union
I^{\textup{D}}_{n-k,k}=I_{n-k,k}^{\mathrm{even}}\sqcup I_{n-k,k}^{\mathrm{odd}}
, where
I_{n-k,k}^{\mathrm{even}}
(resp.
I_{n-k,k}^{\mathrm{odd}}
) consists of all marked cup diagrams with an even (resp. odd) number of markers.

Remark 2.12
.

Note that the marked cup diagrams only have
m=n/2
vertices. This comes from the fact that marked cup diagrams in
I^{\textup{D}}_{n-k,k}
are produced by folding certain symmetric cup diagrams on
n
vertices in
I_{n-k,k}
, see
[
LS13
]
. The marked cup corresponds to a pair of unmarked cups crossing the axis of symmetry. More on this (including an algorithm on how to pass from ordinary cup diagrams to marked cup diagrams) can also be found in an unpublished manuscript
[
ILW19
]
.

In type D, it is useful to distinguish between a two-row partition with equal and unequal parts. We start by considering the case in which
\lambda=(m,m)=(k,k)\vdash n=2m
. We use the conventions for the type D Springer fiber
\mathcal{B}^{\textup{D}}_{n-k,k}
as discussed at the end of Subsection
2.1
omitting to indicate the partition
\lambda
in the notation.

Definition 2.13
.

Given
a\in{I}^{\textup{D}}_{k,k}
,
define
K_{a}\subseteq\mathcal{B}^{\textup{D}}_{k,k}
as the subset consisting of all complete flags
F_{\bullet}
satisfying the following conditions imposed by the diagram
a
:

(i)

If vertices
i<j
are connected by a cup without a marker, then

F_{j}=x^{-\frac{1}{2}(j-i+1)}F_{i-1}.

(ii)

If vertices
i<j
are connected by a marked cup, then

F_{i-1}+x^{\frac{1}{2}(j-i+1)}F_{j}=F_{i}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptF_{j}^{\perp}=x^{-\frac{1}{2}(n-2j)}F_{j}.

(iii)

If vertex
i
is connected to a marked ray, then

F_{i}=\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{\frac{1}{2}(i-1)},f_{1},\ldots,f_{\frac{1}{2}(i+1)}\rangle.

(iv)

If vertex
i
is connected to a ray without a marker, then

F_{i}=\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{\frac{1}{2}(i+1)},f_{1},\ldots,f_{\frac{1}{2}(i-1)}\rangle.

The key new phenomenon in type D when compared to type A is the appearance of the marked cup relation in Definition
2.13
(ii). We point out that this relation is in fact a pair of equations instead of a single equation. The purpose of the following example is to show in detail how to work with the equations for a marked cup.

Example 2.14
.

Let

In this section, we prove Theorem

3.1.
Some small cases

We first consider the case
n=2m\leq 4
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
a\in{I}^{\textup{D}}_{\lambda}
for some
\lambda=(n-k,k)
,
n\leq 4
. Then
K_{a}
is irreducible.

Proof.

For each of the partitions
(1,1)
,
(2,2)
, and
(3,1)
, the set
a\in{I}^{\textup{D}}_{\lambda}
contains two diagrams. It is easy to see that the subvariety
K_{a}
is just a (geometric) point for each of the diagrams if the partition is either
(1,1)
or
(3,1)
. For
(2,2)
, the two subvarieties are both isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1}
. In particular, they are irreducible.
∎

3.2.
Iterated fiber bundles over
\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1}

In order to prove the irreducibility of
K_{a}
for
a\in B^{\textup{D}}_{n-k,k}
in the general case, we recall the definition of an iterated fiber bundle over
\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1}

Definition 3.2
.

A space
X
is called an
iterated fiber bundle over
\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1}
of length
\ell
if there exist spaces

X=X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{\ell},X_{\ell+1}=\textup{pt}
and maps
\pi_{i}:X_{i}\to\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1}
such that
\pi_{i}
is a fiber bundle with typical fiber
X_{i+1}
for
1\leq i\leq\ell
.
A point is considered an iterated bundle over
\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1}
of length 0.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
a\in{I}^{\textup{D}}_{n-k,k}
be a marked cup diagram with
\ell
cups.
Then

K_{a}
is an iterated fiber bundle over
\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1}
of length
\ell
.

n\leq 4

3.3

3.1

3.3.
Quadratic spaces

In order to go through Cases I-III, we will need certain isomorphisms of
quadratic spaces
, i.e., vector spaces equipped with a symmetric bilinear form.
For
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\vdash n
, we denote by
V_{\lambda}
the quadratic space
\mathbb{C}^{n}\cong\langle e_{1}^{\lambda},\ldots,e^{\lambda}_{\lambda_{1}},f^{\lambda}_{1},\ldots,f^{\lambda}_{\lambda_{2}}\rangle
equipped with the bilinear form
\beta_{\lambda}
introduced in Subsection
2.1
(see (
2.2
)–(
2.5
)).

For
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}),\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
such that
\lambda_{1}\geq\mu_{1},\lambda_{2}\geq\mu_{2}
, we define a projection

(3.2)

P^{\lambda}_{\mu}:V_{\lambda}\to V_{\mu},\quad e^{\lambda}_{i}\mapsto\begin{cases}e^{\mu}_{i}&\textup{if }1\leq i\leq\mu_{1};\\
0&\textup{otherwise},\end{cases}\quad f^{\lambda}_{i}\mapsto\begin{cases}f^{\mu}_{i}&\textup{if }1\leq i\leq\mu_{2};\\
0&\textup{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We also define the inverse map of its restriction on
\langle e^{\lambda}_{i},f^{\lambda}_{j}~|~1\leq i\leq\mu_{1},1\leq j\leq\mu_{2}\rangle
by

(3.3)

P^{\mu}_{\lambda}:V_{\mu}\to V_{\lambda},\quad e^{\mu}_{i}\mapsto e^{\lambda}_{i},\quad f^{\mu}_{j}\mapsto f^{\lambda}_{j}.

Now we consider an isotropic subspace
W\subseteq V_{\lambda}
. Let
\{x^{\lambda}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{x^{\lambda}_{j}\}_{j\in J}
be bases of
W
and
W^{\perp}
, respectively, such that
I\subsetneq J
.
We write
\bar{x}^{\lambda}_{j}=x^{\lambda}_{j}+W
, and hence

\{\bar{x}^{\lambda}_{j}\}_{j\in J\setminus I}
forms a basis of
W^{\perp}/W
.
Denote the quotient map by
\psi=\psi(W)
by

(3.4)

\psi:W^{\perp}\to W^{\perp}/W.

Moreover,
W^{\perp}/W
is a quadratic space equipped with the induced bilinear form
\bar{\beta}_{\lambda}
given by
\bar{\beta}_{\lambda}(x+W,y+W)=\beta_{\lambda}(x,y)
for all
x,y\in W^{\perp}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
a,c
be defined as in Definition

.
Recall
\Omega
from (

) using

from (

).

\Omega(K_{a})\subseteq K_{c}
.

Proof.

We first the the unmarked cup relations hold in
\Omega(K_{a})
.
In other words, for any

and any pairs of vertices

connected by an unmarked cup in

,

(3.20)

Q(F_{\ell+j}/W)=x_{\nu}^{\frac{-1}{2}(j-i+1)}Q(F_{\ell+i-1}/W),\quad\textup{for all}\quad i\in V^{c}_{L}.

Note that
(i-\ell,j-\ell)

(3.21)

F_{j-\ell}=x_{\lambda}^{\frac{-1}{2}(j-i+1)}F_{i-\ell},\quad\textup{for all}\quad i\in V^{c}_{L}.

Thus, (
3.27
) follows from combining (
3.28
) and the former part of Corollary
3.5
.

A ray connected to vertex
i
in

corresponds to the five flag conditions as in Definition
2.13
(iii)–(iv) and Definition

(iii)–(v).
Since
b
contains exactly

unmarked cups,

and so

. A case-by-case analysis shows that the new ray relations hold.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
a,b
be defined as in Definition
3.6
.
Then the map below is well-defined:

(3.22)

\pi_{a,b}:K_{a}\to K_{b},\quad F_{\bullet}\mapsto F^{\prime}_{\bullet},

where
F^{\prime}_{i}=P^{\lambda}_{\mu}(F_{i})
if
1\leq i\leq 2t
, and that
F^{\prime}_{2t+1},\ldots,F^{\prime}_{4t-1}
are uniquely determined by
F^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,F^{\prime}_{2t}
under the isotropy condition with respect to
\beta_{\mu}
.

Proof.

We split the proof into three steps: firstly we show that
F^{\prime}_{\bullet}
is isotropic under
\beta_{\mu}
. Secondly, we show that
F^{\prime}_{\bullet}
sits inside the Springer fiber
\mathcal{B}^{\textup{D}}_{x_{\mu}}
.
Finally, we show that
F^{\prime}_{\bullet}
lies in the irreducible component
K_{b}
.

Step 1:

It suffices to show that
F^{\prime}_{2t}
is isotropic. Since the first
2t
vertices in
a
are all connected by unmarked cups,
F_{2t}=\langle e^{\lambda}_{1},\ldots,e^{\lambda}_{t},f^{\lambda}_{1},\ldots,f^{\lambda}_{t}\rangle
. Hence,

(3.23)

F^{\prime}_{2t}=\langle e^{\mu}_{1},\ldots,e^{\mu}_{t},f^{\mu}_{1},\ldots,f^{\mu}_{t}\rangle.

It then follows directly from (
2.5
) that
(F^{\prime}_{2t})^{\perp}=F^{\prime}_{2t}
.

Step 2:

It suffices to check that
x_{\mu}F^{\prime}_{i}\subseteq F^{\prime}_{i-1}
for all
i
.
Note that a direct computation shows that

(3.24)

P_{\mu}^{\lambda}x_{\lambda}=x_{\mu}P_{\mu}^{\lambda}.

Hence, when
i\leq 2t
, we have

(3.25)

x_{\mu}F^{\prime}_{i}=x_{\mu}P^{\lambda}_{\mu}(F_{i})=P_{\mu}^{\lambda}x_{\lambda}(F_{i})\subseteq P_{\mu}^{\lambda}(F_{i-1})=F^{\prime}_{i-1}.

Next, since now
x_{\mu}F^{\prime}_{i+1}\subseteq F^{\prime}_{i}
for
i<2t
, we have

F^{\prime}_{i+1}\subseteq x_{\mu}^{-1}F^{\prime}_{i}
, and hence

(3.26)

F^{\prime}_{4t-i-1}=(F^{\prime}_{i+1})^{\perp}\supseteq(x_{\mu}^{-1}F^{\prime}_{i})^{\perp}=x_{\mu}F^{\prime}_{4t-i}.

Step 3:

We need to verify the conditions

(3.27)

P^{\lambda}_{\mu}(F_{\sigma(i)})=x_{\mu}^{\frac{-1}{2}(\sigma(i)-i+1)}(P^{\lambda}_{\mu}(F_{i-1})),\quad\textup{for all}\quad i\in V^{b}_{L}.

Note that
V^{b}_{L}\subseteq V^{a}_{L}
, hence the unmarked cup relations in
a
hold, i.e.,

(3.28)

F_{\sigma(i)}=x_{\lambda}^{\frac{-1}{2}(\sigma(i)-i+1)}F_{i-1},\quad\textup{for all}\quad i\in V^{b}_{L}.

Thus, (
3.27
) follows from applying
P^{\lambda}_{\mu}
to (
3.28
),
thanks to (
3.24
).

∎

Finally, we are in a position to show that
K_{a}
is irreducible.

Proposition 3.9
.

Using the notations of Lemmas
3.7
–
3.8
, the assignment

F_{\bullet}\mapsto(\pi_{a,b}(F_{\bullet}),\Omega(F_{\bullet}))

Proof.

It suffices to check that the given assignment is an isomorphism with inverse given by

(3.29)

That

almost follows from construction, except for that we need to check if

is a
2t+1

F_{2t}
.
∎

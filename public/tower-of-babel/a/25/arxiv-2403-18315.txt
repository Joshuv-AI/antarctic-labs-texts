Topological Vector Spaces: a non-standard approach with monads and galaxies.
Niels Charlier, Hans Vernaeve

Abstract
A new and extensive formalism is developed for monads and galaxies in non-standard enlargements. It is shown that monads and galaxies can be manipulated using order-preserving and order-reversing set-to-set maps, and that set properties associated with these maps can be extended not only to internal sets but to all monads and galaxies. An abstract theory of Intersections of Galaxies is introduced. These concepts are applied to basic topology as well (locally convex) topological vector spaces, their various properties and completions, allowing these to be effortlessly defined and characterized. Duality theory is studied in this framework, allowing in particular to formulate new brief and insightful proofs for the theorems of Mackey-Arens and Grothendieck completeness without any technicalities.



Preliminaries

Several proposed non-standard theories of topological vector spaces can be found in literature (
Stroyan and Luxemburg 1976
;
Henson and Moore 1972
;
Young 1972
). In this paper we introduce a new approach with new notations and more generalized definitions. The reader is only assumed to be familiar with the basic principles of non-standard analysis, in particular with saturated models.

1.1
Directedness and cofinality

Definition 1.1
.

A
preordered set
is a pair
(X,\leq)
where
X
is
non-empty
set and
\leq
a reflexive, transitive relation on
J=X
. If the equivalence relation defined by
\leq\wedge\geq
coincides with
=
, it is a
(partial) order
. If for any
a,b\in X:\exists c\in X:c\geq a,b
, the (pre-)order is
directed
. If for any
a,b\in J
,
a\leq b\vee a\geq b
, the (pre-)order is
total
. The
tails; heads; finite and infinite points
of
(X,\leq)
are (
a\in[{}^{\star}]{X}
):

\begin{split}[{}^{\star}]{X}_{\leq a}:=\{x\in[{}^{\star}]{X}:x\leq a\}~~~&~~~[{}^{\star}]{X}_{\geq a}:=\{x\in[{}^{\star}]{X}:x\geq a\}\\
{}^{\star}{X}_{\mathfrak{f}}:=\{x\in{}^{\star}{X}:\exists a\in{}^{\sigma}{X}:x\leq a\}\supseteq{}^{\sigma}{X};~~~&~~~{}^{\star}{X}_{\infty}:=\{\omega\in{}^{\star}{X}:\forall x\in{}^{\sigma}{X}:\omega\geq x\}.\end{split}

If the greatest elements
X_{\top}:=\{x\in{X}:\forall a\in X:x\geq a\}=\emptyset
then
{}^{\star}{X}_{\infty}\cap{}^{\star}{X}_{\mathfrak{f}}=\emptyset
and both sets are external. Otherwise
{}^{\star}{X}={}^{\star}{X}_{\mathfrak{f}}\supseteq{}^{\star}{X}_{\infty}={}^{\star}{X_{\top}}
.

Definition 1.2
.

Given preordered sets
(X,\leq)
and
(Y,\leq)
and a mapping
\psi:X\rightarrow Y
. Then
\psi
is
order preserving
if for any
x,x^{\prime}\in X:y\geq y^{\prime}\Rightarrow\psi(x)\geq\psi(x^{\prime})
. It is
order reversing
if for any
x,x^{\prime}\in X:x\geq x^{\prime}\Rightarrow\psi(x)\leq\psi(x^{\prime})
.

Proposition 1.3
.

Assume we are using a
\kappa^{+}
-saturated model. Given a pre-ordered set
(X,\leq)
with
|X|\leq\kappa
. Then
X
is directed iff
{}^{\star}{X}_{\infty}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Since
{}^{\star}{X}_{\infty}=\bigcap_{x\in X}{}^{\star}{X}_{\geq x}
, saturation implies that
{}^{\star}{X}_{\infty}\neq\emptyset
iff
\{{}^{\star}{X}_{\geq x}\}_{x\in X}
has non-empty finite intersections, which is equivalent to directedness.
∎

Proposition 1.4
.

(Finite overspill) Assume we are using a
\kappa^{+}
-saturated model. Given a directed set
X
with
|X|\leq\kappa
and an internal
A\subseteq{}^{\star}{X}
such that
{}^{\star}{X}_{\infty}\subseteq A
. Then there exists an
a\in X
such that
{}^{\star}{X}_{\geq a}\subseteq A
.

Proof.

Since
\bigcap_{x\in X}{}^{\star}{X}_{\geq x}\subseteq A
, i.e.
\bigcap_{x\in X}{}^{\star}{X}_{\geq x}\setminus A=\emptyset
, saturation implies
\{{}^{\star}{X}_{\geq x}\setminus A\}_{x\in X}
has a non-empty finite intersection, which due to the directedness of
X
implies the existence of a
x\in X
such that
{}^{\star}{X}_{\geq x}\setminus A=\emptyset
, i.e.
{}^{\star}{X}_{\geq a}\subseteq A
.
∎

Corrolary 1.5
.

(Infinite overspill) Assume we are using a
\kappa^{+}
-saturated model. Given a directed set
X
with
|X|\leq\kappa
and an internal
A\subseteq{}^{\star}{X}
such that
{}^{\star}{X}_{\mathfrak{f}}\subseteq A
. Then there exists an
\omega\in{}^{\star}{X}_{\infty}
such that
{}^{\star}{X}_{\leq\omega}\subseteq A
.

Proof.

Consider the internal set
Z=\{\iota\in{}^{\star}{X}:{}^{\star}{X}_{\leq\iota}\not\subseteq A\}
. Suppose
{}^{\star}{X}_{\infty}\subseteq Z
, then there exists a
a\in X
such that
{}^{\star}{X}_{\geq a}\subseteq Z
, i.e.
{}^{\star}{X}_{\leq a}\not\subseteq A
, contradicting the assumption. Hence there exists an
\omega\in{}^{\star}{X}_{\infty}\setminus Z
, i.e.
{}^{\star}{X}_{\leq\omega}\subseteq A
.
∎

Proposition 1.8
.

Assume we are using a
\kappa^{+}
-saturated model. Given a pre-ordered set

{}^{\sigma}{J}

Proposition 1.9
.

Given a pre-ordered set
(X,\leq)
and standard-indexed internal families
\mathscr{A}=(a_{j})_{j\in{}^{\star}{J}},\mathscr{B}=(b_{k})_{k\in{}^{\star}{K}}

X

(i)

\mathscr{A}_{\infty}\cap P

(ii)

\mathscr{A}_{\mathfrak{f}}\cap P
cofinal in
\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{f}}\cap Q

(
if

, this is equivalent to
\mathscr{A}_{\sigma}
cofinal in
\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
)
.

Proof.

Consider the pre-order
\leq
on
J
(resp.
K
) induced by
\mathscr{A}
(resp.
\mathscr{B}
); i.e.
\forall j,j^{\prime}\in J:j\leq j^{\prime}\iff a_{j}\leq a_{j^{\prime}}
(resp.
\forall k,k^{\prime}\in K:k\leq k^{\prime}\iff b_{k}\leq k_{k^{\prime}}
). Note that
\mathscr{A}_{\infty}=\{a_{j}\}_{j\in{}^{\star}{J}_{\infty}}
and
\mathscr{B}_{\infty}=\{b_{k}\}_{k\in{}^{\star}{K}_{\infty}}
.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): For any

,
{}^{\star}{J}_{\infty}\subseteq\{j\in{}^{\star}{J}:\exists p\in P:q\leq p\leq a_{j}\}
, so that because of overspill there exists a

, which implies

.

(ii)
\Rightarrow

{}^{\star}{J}_{\mathfrak{f}}\subseteq\{j\in{}^{\star}{J}:\exists p\in P:a_{j}\leq p\leq q\}
, so that because of overspill there exists an

and

. .
∎

Corrolary 1.10
.

Given a pre-ordered set
(X,\leq)
and directed
V,W\subseteq X
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

V
cofinal in
W
.

(ii)

{}^{\star}{V}_{\infty}
coinitial in
{}^{\star}{W}_{\infty}
;

If

this is equivalent to
{}^{\star}{V}_{\infty}\subseteq{}^{\star}{W}_{\infty}

{}^{\star}{V}_{\infty}={}^{\star}{V}\cap{}^{\star}{W}_{\infty}

For the last statement, notice that

always, hence

. Now assume this to be the case and suppose there exists a

{}^{\star}{V}\cap{}^{\star}{X}_{\infty}=\emptyset
, a contradiction because
{}^{\star}{V}_{\infty}
cannot be empty. Hence

and thus
{}^{\star}{V}_{\infty}\subseteq_{[\mathrm{coi}]}{}^{\star}{W}_{\infty}
.
∎

1.2
Monads and Galaxies

From now on we work with a space called
E
. We will use indexed families - with cardinality limited by some
\kappa
- of sets in
E
, accompanied by either one of two induced orders
\subseteq
or
\supseteq
; as well as standard-indexed internal indexed families, denoted:

\begin{split}\mathfrak{B}_{\kappa}(E,\supseteq):=\bigcup_{|J|\leq\kappa,J\neq\emptyset}(\mathcal{P}(E),\supseteq)^{J};~~~&~~~{}^{\filleddiamond}\mathfrak{B}_{\kappa}(E,\supseteq):=\bigcup_{|J|\leq\kappa,J\neq\emptyset}{}^{\star}(\mathcal{P}(E),\supseteq)^{J};\\
\mathfrak{B}_{\kappa}(E,\subseteq):=\bigcup_{|J|\leq\kappa,J\neq\emptyset}(\mathcal{P}(E),\subseteq)^{J};~~~&~~~{}^{\filleddiamond}\mathfrak{B}_{\kappa}(E,\subseteq):=\bigcup_{|J|\leq\kappa,J\neq\emptyset}{}^{\star}(\mathcal{P}(E),\subseteq)^{J}.\end{split}

We may omit the subscript
\kappa

.

Given
\mathcal{U}=(U_{j})_{j\in{}^{\star}{J}}\in{}^{\filleddiamond}\mathfrak{B}(E,\supseteq)
, resp.
{}^{\filleddiamond}\mathfrak{B}(E,\subseteq)

is the
\cap
-closure
, resp.

of

Note that

is always directed. For

,
\bigcap\vec{\mathcal{U}}_{\sigma}=\bigcap\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}
,

Definition 1.12
.

Given
\mathcal{U}\in\mathfrak{B}(E,\supseteq)

Proposition 1.13
.

(Cauchy principle) Given directed
\mathcal{U}\in{}^{\filleddiamond}\mathfrak{B}(E,\supseteq)
, resp.
{{}^{\filleddiamond}\mathfrak{B}(E,\subseteq)}
, then

\begin{split}\forall A\in{}^{\star}{\mathcal{P}}(E):~~~&\bigcup\mathcal{U}_{\infty}\subseteq A\iff\exists U\in\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}:U\subseteq A;\\
resp.~~&A\subseteq\bigcap\mathcal{U}_{\infty}\iff\exists U\in\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}:A\subseteq U.\end{split}

Proof.

This follows from finite overspill.
∎

Proposition 1.14
.

(Nucleus principle) Given directed
\mathcal{U}\in{}^{\filleddiamond}\mathfrak{B}(E,\supseteq)
, resp.
{}^{\filleddiamond}\mathfrak{B}(E,\subseteq)
, then

\begin{split}\forall A\in{}^{\star}{\mathcal{P}}(E):~~~&A\subseteq\bigcap\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}\iff\exists U\in\mathcal{U}_{\infty}:A\subseteq U;\\
resp.~~&\bigcup\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}\subseteq A\iff\exists U\in\mathcal{U}_{\infty}:U\subseteq A.\end{split}

Proof.

This follows from infinite overspill.
∎

Proposition 1.15
.

Given directed
\mathcal{U}\in{}^{\filleddiamond}\mathfrak{B}(E,\supseteq)
, resp.
{{}^{\filleddiamond}\mathfrak{B}(E,\subseteq)}
. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\bigcap\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}\subseteq\bigcap\mathcal{V}_{\sigma}
, resp.
\bigcup\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}\supseteq\bigcup\mathcal{V}_{\sigma}
;

(ii)

\bigcup\mathcal{U}_{\infty}\subseteq\bigcup\mathcal{V}_{\infty}
, resp.
\bigcap\mathcal{U}_{\infty}\supseteq\bigcap\mathcal{V}_{\infty}
;

(iii)

\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}
cofinal in
\mathcal{V}_{\sigma}
;

(iv)

\mathcal{V}_{\infty}
coinitial in
\mathcal{U}_{\infty}
.

Proof.

The equivalence (iii)
\iff
(iv) follows from proposition
1.9
. The implications (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i) and (iv)
\Rightarrow
(ii) are clear. The implications (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) and (i)
\Rightarrow
(iv) follow from the Cauchy, resp. nucleus principle.
∎

Proposition 1.16
.

Given directed
\mathcal{U}\in{}^{\filleddiamond}\mathfrak{B}(E,\supseteq)
, resp.
{{}^{\filleddiamond}\mathfrak{B}(E,\subseteq)}
.

\bigcap\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}=\bigcap\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{f}}=\bigcup\mathcal{U}_{\infty}\textnormal{, resp. }\bigcup\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}=\bigcup\mathcal{U}_{f}=\bigcap\mathcal{U}_{\infty}.

Proof.

The inclusion
\bigcap\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}\subseteq\bigcup\mathcal{U}_{\infty}
follows from the nucleus principle;
\bigcap\mathcal{U}_{\infty}\subseteq\bigcup\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}
from the Cauchy principe (each time applied to a singleton). The other inclusions are clear.
∎

A

(E,F)
are two vector spaces

and
F
over a field

and a bilinear mapping
\langle,\rangle:E\times F\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
such that both spaces are separated by each other:

\forall e\in E\setminus\{0\}:\exists f\in F:\langle f,e\rangle\neq 0;~~~\forall f\in F\setminus\{0\}:\exists e\in E:\langle f,e\rangle\neq 0.

For instance, a vector space
E
over
\mathbb{K}
and its algebraic dual
E^{*}
form a dual pair. In fact, fixing
E
, any possible
F
may be identified with a subset of
E^{*}
using the isomorphic embedding
f\rightarrow\langle\cdot,f\rangle
. Note that the definition of a dual pair is symmetric, so that we can flip the roles of
E
and
F
in any definition or proposition. Given a dual pair
(E,F)
, Given
V\subseteq E
, its

is

V^{\circ}:=\{f\in F:\sup_{e\in V}|\langle e,f\rangle|\leq 1\}\subseteq F.

The

of

is
V^{\circ\circ}:=(V^{\circ})^{\circ}
. It can be proven that for

:

•

U\subseteq V\Rightarrow U^{\circ}\supseteq V^{\circ}
hence
(U\cap V)^{\circ}\supseteq U^{\circ}\cup V^{\circ}
;

•

(U\cup V)^{\circ}=U^{\circ}\cap V^{\circ}
;

•

V\subseteq V^{\circ\circ}
hence
V^{\circ\circ\circ}=V^{\circ}
.

The non-standard extensions
{}^{\star}{E}
and

form a dual pair of vector spaces over

{}^{\star}{(V^{\circ})}=({}^{\star}{V})^{\circ}

In particular, if

|\langle e,f\rangle|\leq|\langle\hat{e},f\rangle|+|\langle e-\hat{e},f\rangle|\approx 0.

∎

Corrolary 3.30
.

For any topology on
E
, on equicontinuous sets of
F
the topology of uniform convergence on compact sets coincides with the weak topology.

Proof.

Indeed, since

\begin{split}\mathbf{m}_{E}(0)^{\circ}\cap\mathrm{comp}({}^{\star}{E})^{\circ}&\supseteq\mathbf{m}_{E}(0)^{\circ}\cap\mathrm{ns}({}^{\star}{E})^{\circ}\\
&=\mathbf{m}_{E}(0)^{\circ}\cap\mathbf{m}_{E}(0)^{\circ}\cap{}^{\sigma}{E}^{\circ}\\
&=\mathbf{m}_{E}(0)^{\circ}\cap\mathbf{m}_{\sigma}(0).\end{split}

∎

Corrolary 3.31
.

For any polar
\tau
,
\mathrm{ns}_{\tau}({}^{\star}{E})^{\circ\circ}=\mathrm{pns}_{\tau}({}^{\star}{E})^{\circ\circ}=\mathrm{pns}_{\tau}({}^{\star}{E}).

In other words, the bipolar identity is only valid for the near-standard points in spaces where completeness and the convex compactness property coincide. Since there exist spaces that have this property but are not complete (for instance quasi-complete spaces that are not complete
Khaleelulla 1982
), the bipolar identity is not always valid for the near-standard points. Hence, this is a demonstration of two order-preserving maps that coincide on standard and internal sets,
⋆
monads and
⋆
galaxies but not on all external sets.

3.3
Compatibility with the duality and completeness

In this section we work with a duality
(E,F)
with
\tau
being a locally convex Hausdorff topology on
E
. Identifying
E
as a subset of
F^{*}
, we may extend the definition of the
\mathrm{st~}\cdot
map to
\mathrm{Fin}_{\sigma}({}^{\star}{E})=\mathrm{pns}_{\sigma}({}^{\star}{E})
as follows:

\langle\hat{\mathrm{st}}_{\sigma}~e,f\rangle:=\mathrm{st~}\langle e,f\rangle\textnormal{ for }f\in{}^{\sigma}{F}.

Then
\hat{\mathrm{st}}_{\tau}~\cdot
is a restriction of this map to
\mathrm{pns}_{\tau}({}^{\star}{E})
. Indeed, since
\hat{\mathrm{st}}_{\sigma}~e\approx_{\sigma}e
and the topology of
E
is finer than the weak topology, this extension is entirely compatible with the previous definition of
\hat{\mathrm{st}}~
.

Lemma 3.32
.

Given
V\subseteq E
a convex balanced set. Then

(\mathbf{m}_{E,\tau}(0)\cap{}^{\star}{V})^{\circ}\subseteq{}^{\sigma}{({V_{\tau}}^{\prime}\cap E^{*}})+\mathbf{m}(0)=\mathrm{ns}_{\sigma}({}^{\star}{({V_{\tau}}^{\prime}\cap E^{*})}).

In other words, any member of
{}^{\star}{F}
that is
s
continuous on
V
is in the weak topological monad (for the space
E^{*}
) of a functional that is continuous on
V
.

Proof.

Take
f\in({}^{\star}{V}\cap\mathbf{m}_{E}(0))^{\circ}
. Because of the Cauchy principle, there exists a
U\in\mathrm{fil~}\mathbf{m}_{E}(0)
so that
f\in{}^{\star}(V\cap U)^{\circ}
. Let
\hat{f}:=\hat{\mathrm{st}}_{\sigma}~f\in E^{*}
. Then we have for any
e\in U
that
|\langle e,\hat{f}\rangle|\approx|\langle e,f\rangle|\leq 1
so that
|\langle e,\hat{f}\rangle|\leq 1
since it has a standard value, i.e.
\hat{f}\in(V\cap U)^{\circ}\subseteq V^{\prime}
.
∎

Lemma 3.33
.

Given
f\in{}^{\sigma}{(V_{\tau})^{\prime}\cap E^{*}}
with
V\subseteq E
a convex balanced set, there exists an
f^{\prime}\in(\mathbf{m}_{E,\tau}(0)\cap{}^{\star}{V_{\tau}})^{\circ}
such that that
f^{\prime}\approx_{\sigma}f
.
In other words, any functional in
E^{*}
that is continuous for
V
, is in the weak topological monad of a member of
{}^{\star}{F}
that is
s
continuous on
V
.

Proof.

Notice that
f\in(\mathbf{m}_{E}(0)\cap{}^{\star}{V_{\tau}})^{\bullet}
, where
\bullet
is the polar for the duality
(E,E^{*})
. Since
\mathbf{m}_{E}(0)^{\bullet}=\mathbf{m}_{E}(0)^{\circ\circ\bullet}=\mathbf{m}_{E}(0)^{\circ\bullet\bullet}
, this is in fact the weak closure in
{}^{\star}{E^{*}}
(for the duality
(E,E^{*})
) of
\mathbf{m}_{E}(0)^{\circ}
, implying the result.
∎

Theorem 3.34
.

\tau
is compatible with the duality iff
\tau\geq\sigma
and

\mathbf{m}_{E,\tau}(0)^{\circ}\subseteq\mathrm{ns}_{\sigma}({}^{\star}{F}).

Proof.

F\subseteq E^{\prime}\iff{}^{\sigma}{F}\subseteq\mathbf{m}_{E}(0)^{\circ}\iff\mathbf{m}_{E}(0)\subseteq{}^{\sigma}{F}^{\circ}=\mathbf{m}_{E,\sigma}(0).

We must then only proof that
\mathbf{m}_{E,\tau}(0)^{\circ}\subseteq\mathrm{ns}_{\sigma}({}^{\star}{F})
is equivalent with
E^{\prime}\subseteq F
. The
\Leftarrow
-implication is lemma
3.32
(with
V=E
). The
\Rightarrow
-implication follows from lemma
3.33
. Indeed, given
f\in E^{\prime}
, we find a
f^{\prime}\in\mathbf{m}_{E,\tau}(0)^{\circ}
so that
f\approx_{\sigma}f
which implies that
\mathrm{st~}f^{\prime}=f
and therefore
f\in F
.
∎

Corrolary 3.35
.

(Alaoglu-Bourbaki) The equicontinuous sets of
E^{\prime}
are weakly relatively compact.

Corrolary 3.36
.

(Mackey-Arens)
A locally convex Hausdorff topology
\tau
on
E
is compatible with the duality iff
\tau
is a topology of uniform convergence on covering of convex, balanced, weakly relatively compact sets.

The theorem is thus the non-standard characterization of both Alaoglu-Bourbaki and Mackey-Arens which now appear to be trivially equivalent. The most coarse topology that is compatible with the duality is the weak topology. Now we know from section
2.4
that there exists a greatest galaxy and therefore a smallest monad or finest topology
\tau(E,F)
that satisfies this condition, namely
\mathbf{m}_{E,\tau}(0)=\mathrm{coco}_{\sigma}({}^{\star}{F})^{\circ}
, the
Mackey-topology
.

Theorem 3.37
.

\mathrm{pns}_{\tau}({}^{\star}{E})=(\mathbf{m}_{E,\tau}(0)^{\circ}\cap{}^{\sigma}{E}^{\circ})^{\circ}
.

Proof.

This follows from propositions
3.25
and
3.27
.
∎

Corrolary 3.38
.

Given
\mathcal{U}
a subbase of

. Then

e
is weakly
s

∎

.

\mathcal{U}

\mathbf{m}_{E}(0)^{\circ}
.

A General Construction of Permutation Polynomials of 𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} Thanks: * The research of Vincenzo Palozzi Lavorante was partially supported by the Italian National Group for Algebraic and Geometric Structures and their Applications (GNSAGA - INdAM)
Xiang-dong Hou, Vincenzo Pallozzi Lavorante *

Abstract
. Let r r be a positive integer, h ​ ( X ) ∈ 𝔽 q 2 ​ [ X ] h(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X] , and μ q + 1 \mu_{q+1} be the subgroup of order q + 1 q+1 of 𝔽 q 2 ∗ \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*} . It is well known that X r ​ h ​ ( X q − 1 ) X^{r}h(X^{q-1}) permutes 𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} if and only if gcd ​ ( r , q − 1 ) = 1 \text{gcd}(r,q-1)=1 and X r ​ h ​ ( X ) q − 1 X^{r}h(X)^{q-1} permutes μ q + 1 \mu_{q+1} . There are many ad hoc constructions of permutation polynomials of 𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} of this type such that h ​ ( X ) q − 1 h(X)^{q-1} induces monomial functions on the cosets of a subgroup of μ q + 1 \mu_{q+1} . We give a general construction that can generate, through an algorithm, all permutation polynomials of 𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} with this property, including many which are not known previously. The construction is illustrated explicitly for permutation binomials and trinomials.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
denote the finite field with
q
elements. A polynomial
f(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
is called a
permutation polynomial
(PP) of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if it induces a permutation of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
r
be a positive integer,
d\mid q-1
, and
h(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
. It is well known
[
9
,
11
,
15
]
that
X^{r}h(X^{(q-1)/d})
is a PP of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if
\text{gcd}(r,(q-1)/d)=1
and
X^{r}h(X)^{(q-1)/d}
permutes the multiplicative group
\mu_{d}:=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}:x^{d}=1\}
. (In general, we use
\mu_{m}
to denote a multiplicative group of order
m
of a finite field.) Replacing
q
with
q^{2}
and
d
with
q+1
, we see that for
h(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
,
X^{r}h(X^{q-1})
is a PP of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
if and only
\text{gcd}(r,q-1)=1
and
X^{r}h(X)^{q-1}
permutes
\mu_{q+1}
. To facilitate the constructions of
\mu_{q+1}
of the form
X^{r}h(X)^{q-1}
, the following idea has been used by several authors
[
1
,
5
,
6
,
10
,
14
]
: Let
H
be a subgroup of
\mu_{q+1}
of small index. Construct a polynomial
h(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
such that
h(X)^{q-1}
induces monomial functions on each coset of
H
in
\mu_{q+1}
. With such a property,
X^{r}h(X)^{q-1}
permutes
\mu_{q+1}
if and only if some simple number theoretic conditions on the parameters are satisfied. This method has produced many results. However, these results only deal with specific situations, leaving a unified treatment to be desired.

In the present paper, we take a general approach to the question. The main result is an algorithm (Algorithm
2.4
) that produces
all
PPs of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
of the form
X^{r}h(X^{q-1})
such that
h(X)^{q-1}
induces monomial functions on the cosets of a subgroup in
\mu_{q+1}
. Let
d\mid q+1
and
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
be such that
o(\epsilon)=d
. Define

(1.1)

A_{k}=\{x\in\mu_{q+1}:x^{(q+1)/d}=\epsilon^{k}\},\quad 0\leq k<d.

Then
A_{0}=\mu_{(q+1)/d}
, and
A_{0},\dots,A_{d-1}
are the cosets of
\mu_{(q+1)/d}
in
\mu_{q+1}
, whence

(1.2)

\mu_{q+1}=\bigsqcup_{k=0}^{d-1}A_{k}.

Since
X^{n_{1}(q-1)}\equiv X^{n_{2}(q-1)}\pmod{X^{q^{2}-1}-1}
whenever
n_{1}\equiv n_{2}\pmod{q+1}
, it suffices to consider
h\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
with
\deg h\leq q
. Write

(1.3)

h(X)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i<(q+1)/d\cr 0\leq j<d\end{subarray}}a_{ij}X^{i+j(q+1)/d}.

The objective is to find conditions on
a_{ij}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
such that for every
0\leq k<d
,

(1.4)

x^{r}h(x)^{q-1}=\lambda_{k}x^{e_{k}}\ \text{for all}\ x\in A_{k},

where
e_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\lambda_{k}\in\mu_{q+1}
, say
\lambda_{k}\in A_{\pi(k)}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that (
1.4
) is satisfied for all
0\leq k<d
. Then
X^{r}h(X)^{q-1}
permutes
\mu_{q+1}
if and only if

\text{\rm gcd}\Bigl(e_{k},\frac{q+1}{d}\Bigr)=1,\quad 0\leq k<d,

and

k\mapsto\pi(k)+e_{k}k

is a permutation of
\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

By (
1.4
),
X^{r}h(X)^{q-1}
maps
A_{k}
to
A_{\pi(k)+e_{k}k}
. This map is one-to-one on
A_{k}
if and only if
\text{gcd}(e_{k},(q+1)/d)=1
. Hence the conclusion.
∎

Therefore, the crucial question is to determine the polynomials
h(X)
satisfying (
1.4
). In Section 2, we will resolve this question and we will describe an algorithm that produces all PPs of the form
X^{r}h(X^{q-1})
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
satisfying (
1.4
). In Section 3, we determine all permutation binomials of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
resulting from this algorithm and it turns out that these permutation binomials were all known previously. In Section 4, we determine all permutation trinomials of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
resulting from the algorithm. There are four classes such permutation trinomials, excluding those that were previously known. These four classes, in their generality, appear to be new, although many special cases have been discovered by other authors. Additional examples of the algorithm are given in Section 5. Overall, this approach reveals many PPs that were not known previously.

Remark.
In the present paper, we investigate polynomials
h(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
such that
h(X)^{q-1}
induces monomial functions on the cosets of a subgroup of
\mu_{q+1}
and permutes
\mu_{q+1}
as a whole. Before this approach became popular in recent years, people had explored a similar method for PPs of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Several authors
[
2
,
8
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
had studied PPs of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
which induce monomial functions on the cosets of a subgroup of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
.

2.
The Construction

For
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, define
\bar{a}=a^{q}
; for
f(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}X^{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
with
a_{n}\neq 0
, define

\bar{f}(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\bar{a}_{i}X^{i}

and

\tilde{f}(X)=X^{n}\bar{f}(X^{-1})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\bar{a}_{i}X^{n-i}.

Obviously,
\bar{\bar{f}}=f
and
\tilde{\tilde{f}}=f
. If
\tilde{f}=cf
for some
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
,
f
is said to be
self-dual
; in this case, it is necessary that
c\in\mu_{q+1}
. Self-dual polynomials were first introduced in
[
4
]
for a different purpose; they will also play an important role in the present paper.

We follow the notation of Section 1. Let
h(X)
be given in (
1.3
) and assume that
h
has no root in
\mu_{q+1}
. For
x\in A_{k}
, where
0\leq k<d
, we have

(2.1)

h(X)=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\epsilon^{jk}x^{i}=\sum_{i}M_{ik}x^{i},

where

(2.2)

M_{ik}=\sum_{j}a_{ij}\epsilon^{jk}.

Note that the
((q+1)/d)\times d
matrices
[M_{ik}]
and
[a_{ij}]
are related by the
d\times d
Vandermonde matrix
[\epsilon^{jk}]
:

[M_{ik}]=[a_{ij}]\,[\epsilon^{jk}],\qquad[a_{ij}]=\frac{1}{d}\,[M_{ik}]\,[\epsilon^{-kj}].

By (
2.1
), for
x\in\mu_{q+1}
,

(2.3)

x^{r}h(x)^{q-1}=x^{r}\frac{h(x)^{q}}{h(x)}=x^{r}\,\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{i}\overline{M}_{ik}\,x^{-i}}{\displaystyle\sum_{i}M_{ik}\,x^{i}}.

Write

(2.4)

\sum_{i}M_{ik}X^{i}=X^{s}L(X),

where
L(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
,
L(0)\neq 0
,
\deg L=t
,
s+t<(q+1)/d
. Then (
2.4
) becomes

(2.5)

x^{r}h(x)^{q-1}=x^{r}\,\frac{x^{-s}\bar{L}(x^{-1})}{x^{s}L(x)}=x^{r-2s-t}\frac{\tilde{L}(x)}{L(x)}.

The following lemma is crucial.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
L(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
be such that
L(0)\neq 0
,
\deg L=t<(q+1)/d
, and
L(X)
has no root in
A_{k}
.

(i)

Assume that there exist
0\leq\tau<(q+1)/d
and
\lambda\in\mu_{q+1}
such that

(2.6)

\frac{\tilde{L}(x)}{L(x)}=\lambda x^{\tau}\quad\text{for all}\ x\in A_{k}.

Then either
\tau=0
or
(q+1)/d-t\leq\tau\leq t
.

(ii)

When
\tau=0
, (
2.6
) is satisfied if and only if

(2.7)

\tilde{L}(X)=\lambda L(X).

(iii)

When
(q+1)/d-t\leq\tau\leq t
, (
2.6
) is satisfied if and only if

(2.8)

L(X)=P(X)+X^{(q+1)/d-\tau}Q(X),

where
P,Q\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
,
\deg P=t-\tau
,
\tilde{P}=\lambda P
,
\deg Q=\tau+t-(q+1)/d
,
\tilde{Q}=\lambda\epsilon^{k}Q
.

Proof.

(ii) Since
\deg(\tilde{L}-\lambda L)\leq t<(q+1)/d=|A_{k}|
,

\tilde{L}(x)-\lambda L(x)\ \text{for all}\ x\in A_{k}\ \Leftrightarrow\ \tilde{L}(X)-\lambda L(X)=0.

(iii) (
\Rightarrow
) We have
X^{(q+1)/d}-\epsilon^{k}\mid\tilde{L}(X)-\lambda X^{\tau}L(X)
, say

(2.9)

\tilde{L}(X)-\lambda X^{\tau}L(X)=g(X)(\epsilon^{k}-X^{(q+1)/d}),

where
g(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
with
\deg g=\tau+t-(q+1)/d
. In (
2.9
),

\displaystyle\widetilde{\tilde{L}-\lambda X^{\tau}L}\,

\displaystyle=X^{\tau+t}\bigl(\bar{\tilde{L}}(X^{-1})-\bar{\lambda}X^{-\tau}\bar{L}(X^{-1})\bigr)

\displaystyle=X^{\tau}\tilde{\tilde{L}}-\bar{\lambda}\tilde{L}

\displaystyle=X^{\tau}L-\bar{\lambda}\tilde{L}

\displaystyle=-\bar{\lambda}(\tilde{L}-\lambda X^{\tau}L).

Hence

\widetilde{g(X)(\epsilon^{k}-X^{(q+1)/d})}=-\bar{\lambda}g(X)(\epsilon^{k}-X^{(q+1)/d}),

i.e.,

\tilde{g}(X)(\epsilon^{-k}X^{(q+1)/d}-1)=-\bar{\lambda}g(X)(\epsilon^{k}-X^{(q+1)/d}),

hence
\tilde{g}=\bar{\lambda}\epsilon^{k}g
. Therefore, (
2.9
) becomes

(2.10)

\tilde{L}-\lambda X^{\tau}L=\lambda\tilde{g}-X^{(q+1)/d}g.

Let
L=a_{0}+\cdots+a_{t}X^{t}
and
g=b_{0}+\cdots+b_{v}X^{v}
, where
v=\deg g=\tau+t-(q+1)/d
. The coefficients of
\tilde{L}-\lambda X^{\tau}L
and
\lambda\tilde{g}-X^{(q+1)/d}g
are illustrated in Figure
1
. It follows from (
2.10
) and Figure
1
that
a_{i}=0
for
t-\tau<i<t-v
,
a_{t-\tau}\neq 0
, and

\displaystyle L\,

\displaystyle=a_{0}+\cdots+a_{t-\tau}X^{t-\tau}+X^{(q+1)/d-\tau}(a_{t-v}+\cdots+a_{t}X^{v})

\displaystyle=P(X)+X^{(q+1)/d-\tau}Q(X),

where
P(X)=a_{0}+\cdots+a_{t-\tau}X^{t-\tau}
, which satisfies
\tilde{P}=\lambda P
, and
Q(X)=a_{t-v}+\cdots+a_{t}X^{v}=-\bar{\lambda}g(X)
, which satisfies
\tilde{Q}=-\lambda\tilde{g}=-\lambda\bar{\lambda}\epsilon^{k}g=\lambda\epsilon^{k}Q
.

\text{gcd}(h,X^{q+1}-1)=1
. Then
X^{r}h(X^{q-1})
is a PP of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. This construction does not seem to have appeared in the literature.

As an explicit instance of Example
5.2
, let’s consider the following situation: Let
q\geq 5
be odd,
t_{0}=(q+5)/2
,
\tau_{0}=(q+1)/2
,
\text{gcd}(r,q-1)=\text{gcd}(r-2,q+1)=1
,
P=1+X^{2}
,
Q=a^{q}+aX^{2}
, where
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
is such that
(1+a)^{(q^{2}-1)/2}=(1-a)^{(q^{2}-1)/2}=(-1)^{(q-1)/2}
. The number of such elements
a
is
(q^{2}-1)/4
; see Lemma
5.3
below. Let

h(X)=P(X)+X^{(q+1)/2}Q(X)=1+X^{2}+X^{(q+1)/2}(a^{q}+aX^{2}).

We claim that
\text{gcd}(h(X),X^{q+1}-1)=1
. Assume to the contrary that
h(X)
and
X^{q+1}-1
have a common root
x
. Then

x^{(q+1)/2}=-\frac{1+x^{2}}{a^{q}+ax^{2}},

whence

1=x^{q+1}=\Bigl(\frac{1+x^{2}}{a^{q}+ax^{2}}\Bigr)^{2}.

Thus

\frac{1+x^{2}}{a^{q}+ax^{2}}=\pm 1,

giving

x^{2}=-\frac{1\mp a^{q}}{1\mp a}=-(1\mp a)^{q-1}.

Therefore,

1=(x^{2})^{(q+1)/2}=\bigl(-(1\mp a)^{q-1}\bigr)^{(q+1)/2}=(-1)^{(q+1)/2}(1\mp a)^{(q^{2}-1)/2}=-1,

which is a contradiction.

Therefore,

X^{r}h(X^{q-1})=X^{r}(1+X^{2(q-1)}+a^{q}X^{(q^{2}-1)/2}+aX^{(q+5)(q-1)})

is a PP of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
q
be odd and

\mathcal{A}=\{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}:(1+a)^{(q^{2}-1)/2}=(1-a)^{(q^{2}-1)/2}=(-1)^{(q-1)/2}\}.

Then
|\mathcal{A}|=(q^{2}-1)/4
.

Proof.

Choose
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
such that
u^{(q^{2}-1)/2}=(-1)^{(q-1)/2}
, and let

\mathcal{X}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{2}:x^{2}+y^{2}=2u\}.

By
[
3
, Lemma 6.55]
or
[
7
, Lemma 6.24]
,
|\mathcal{X}|=q^{2}-1
. Note that for
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
,

\displaystyle(1+a)^{(q^{2}-1)/2}=(1-a)^{(q^{2}-1)/2}=(-1)^{(q-1)/2}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\

\displaystyle\begin{cases}1+a=u^{-1}x^{2}\cr 1-a=u^{-1}y^{2}\end{cases}\ \text{for some}\ (x,y)\in\mathcal{X}.

If
u
is a nonsquare of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, then

|\mathcal{A}|=\frac{1}{4}|\mathcal{X}|=\frac{1}{4}(q^{2}-1).

If
u
is a square of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, partition
\mathcal{X}
as
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}_{1}\sqcup\mathcal{X}_{2}\sqcup\mathcal{X}_{3}
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{1}\,

\displaystyle=\{(x,y)\in\mathcal{X}:x^{2}\neq 0,u;\ y^{2}\neq 0,u\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{2}\,

\displaystyle=\{(x,y)\in\mathcal{X}:x^{2}=y^{2}=u\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{3}\,

\displaystyle=\{(x,y)\in\mathcal{X}:x=0\ \text{or}\ y=0\}.

Then
|\mathcal{X}_{1}|+|\mathcal{X}_{2}|+|\mathcal{X}_{3}|=q^{2}-1
,
|\mathcal{X}_{2}|=4
and
|\mathcal{X}_{3}|=4
. Hence

|\mathcal{A}|=\frac{1}{4}|\mathcal{X}_{1}|+\frac{1}{2}|\mathcal{X}_{3}|=\frac{1}{4}(q^{2}-1-8)+2=\frac{1}{4}(q^{2}-1).

∎

Equidimensional quiver representations and their U U -invariants
A.N. Panov

Abstract
For an arbitrary equidimensional quiver representation, we proposed the method of construction of a system of free generators of the field of U U -invariants. The construction of the section and system of generators depends on the choice of a map that assign to each vertex one of the arrows incident to it.

Introduction

A quiver is a directed graph. A quiver is given by a pair
Q=(V,A)
, where
V
is the set of vertices and
A
is the set of arrows. Each arrow
\alpha
starts at its source

s(\alpha)
and terminates at its target
t(\alpha)
. Multiple arrows and loops are allowed.

Let
K
be a field. A representation of the quiver
Q
assigns to each vertex
v\in V
a linear space
\{W_{v},~v\in V\}
defined over the field
K
, and to each arrow
\alpha
a linear map
W_{s(\alpha)}\to W_{t(\alpha)}
. Each representation defines the dimensional vector
n_{Q}=(n_{v})
, where
n_{v}=\dim W_{v}
.

We identify each linear space
W_{v}
with the coordinate space
K^{n_{v}}
and the space of linear maps
{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha}
from
W_{s(\alpha)}
to
W_{t(\alpha)}
with the space of matrices
{\mathrm{Mat}}(n_{t(\alpha)},n_{s(\alpha)},K)
of size

n_{t(\alpha)}\times n_{s(\alpha)}
.

For each vertex
v\in V
, we consider the group
{\mathrm{GL}}_{v}={\mathrm{GL}}(n_{v})
with entries in the field
K
, and its unitriangular group
U_{v}={\mathrm{UT}}(n_{v})
that consists of all upper triangular
(n_{v}\times n_{v})
-matrices with ones on the diagonal.
Consider the direct product
{\mathrm{GL}}_{Q}=\prod_{v\in V}{\mathrm{GL}}_{v}
, its subgroup
U_{Q}=\prod_{v\in V}U_{v}
, and the linear space

{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}_{Q}=\oplus_{\alpha\in A}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha}.

The group
{\mathrm{GL}}_{Q}
acts on
{\mathcal{H}}
by the formula

g.h=(g_{t(\alpha)}X_{\alpha}g_{s(\alpha)}^{-1})_{\alpha\in A},\quad g=(g_{v})\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{Q},\quad h=(X_{\alpha})\in{\mathcal{H}}.

The action of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{Q}
on
{\mathcal{H}}
determines the representation
\rho_{g}
of the group
{\mathrm{GL}}_{Q}
in the space of regular functions

K[{\mathcal{H}}]
by the formula

\rho_{g}f(h)=f(g^{-1}.h).

This representation is extended to the action of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{Q}
on the field of rational functions
K({\mathcal{H}})
.
For any subgroup
G
of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{Q}
one can define the algebra of invariants
K[{\mathcal{H}}]^{G}
and the field of invariants
K({\mathcal{H}})^{G}
.

The partial case is the action of the group
{\mathrm{GL}}(n)
on the systems of matrices
g.(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})=(gX_{1}g^{-1},\ldots,gX_{n}g^{-1})
. This representation is associated with the quiver with the set of loops
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
with the common vertex. The problem of description of the algebra of
{\mathrm{GL}}(n)
-invariants is solved in the framework of classical invariant theory in tensors (see
[
1
,
2
,
4
]
). The algebra of
{\mathrm{GL}}(n)
-invariants is generated by the system of polynomials
\sigma_{t}(M)
, where
M
is an arbitrary monomial of
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
, and
\{\sigma_{t}:~~1\leqslant t\leqslant n\}
are coefficients of the characteristic polynomial.

For an arbitrary quiver
Q
and a representation of an arbitrary dimension
(n_{v})_{v\in V}
, the algebra of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{Q}
-invariants is generated by the polynomials
\sigma_{t}(X_{\beta_{1}}\cdots X_{\beta_{s}})
, where
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{s}
is an arbitrary directed closed path of the quiver. This theorem in the case of zero characteristic was solved in 1990 in the paper
[
3
]
of L. Le Bruyn, C. Procesi, and in the case of arbitrary characteristic in 1994 in
[
6
]
of S. Donkin.

In the present paper, we consider the action of the subgroup
U_{Q}
on
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}_{Q}
. The problem of construction of a system of generators with their relations
is considered extremely difficult even for the case of coadjoint action of
{\mathrm{UT}}(n)
on the space of
{\mathrm{Mat}}(n)
(this is associated with the quiver
of one loop and one vertex).
We consider the action of
U_{Q}
on the field
K({\mathcal{H}})
. We refer to the field of invariants of this action as the field of
U
-invariants and denote
K({\mathcal{H}})^{U}
. According to the K. Miyata theorem
[
5
]
, the field of invariants with respect to an action of a group of inipotent transformations is rational. Therefore, the field
K({\mathcal{H}})^{U}
is rational, i.e. it is a pure transcendental extension of the main field
K
.

Definition
. We say that a quiver representation is equidimensional if
n_{v}=n
for each
v\in V
.

In the case of equidimensional representation, the linear space
{\mathcal{H}}
is decomposed into a direct sum of the subspaces
{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in A
, which is identified with the space of
(n\times n)
-matrices
{\mathrm{Mat}}(n)
defined over the field
K
.

We aim to construct the system of free generators of the field of
U
-invariants of the equidimensional representation
{\mathcal{H}}
,
n_{v}=n
, for an arbitrary quiver
Q
. The main result (see Theorem
3
) is formulated in terms of a fixed map
\psi:V\to A
that assign to each vertex
v\in V
one of the arrows
\psi(v)\in A
incident to it. The constructed section
{\mathcal{S}}
and the system of free generators
{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in A
, depend on the choice of the map
\psi
. Earlier in the papers
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
, the author constructed such systems for the adjoint representation and representations on the matrix tuples.

We introduce some notations. For positive integers
a
and
b
, we denote by
[a,b]
the set of all integers
a\leqslant i\leqslant b
.
For any integer
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
, let
i^{\prime}
stand for the symmetric number to
i
with respect to the center of the segment
[1,n]
. We have
i^{\prime}=n-i+1
.

Consider the
(n\times n)
-matrix
X=(x_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
with variables
\{x_{ij}\}
. For each
1\leqslant k\leqslant n
, we denote by
D_{k}(X)
the left lower corner minor of order
k^{\prime}
for
X
. In particular,
D_{1}(X)=\det X
.

We denote by
{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}
the subfield of
K({\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha})
generated by
D_{k}(X_{\alpha})
,
1\leqslant k\leqslant n
.
The polynomial
D_{k}(X)
is invariant with respect to the left and right multiplication by
{\mathrm{UT}}(n)
. Therefore for each
\alpha\in A
, the polynomials
\{D_{k}(X_{\alpha})\}
are
U
-invariants, i.e.

\{D_{k}(X_{\alpha})\}
belongs to
K[{\mathcal{H}}]^{U}
.
Consider the subfield
{\mathcal{F}}
of
K({\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha})^{U}
generated by
{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in A
.

We say that a matrix is anti-diagonal if its entries above and below the anti-diagonal are zeros. We denote by
\Lambda(n)
the subspace of all anti-diagonal
(n\times n)
-matrices over the field
K
. We consider the subspace of upper anti-triangular matrices
{\mathcal{S}}^{+}(n)
over the field
K

(their entries below the anti-diagonal are zeros) and the subspace of lower anti-triangular matrices
{\mathcal{S}}^{-}(n)
(their entries above the anti-diagonal are zeros).

We introduce a linear order on the set of all pairs

\{(a,b)\}
,
1\leqslant a,b\leqslant n
, as follows:
(a,b)\prec(i,k)
, if
b<k
or
b=k
and
a>i
. Then
(n,1)\prec(n-1,1)\prec\ldots\prec(1,1)\prec(n,2)\prec\ldots\prec(1,n)
.

Definition
. Let
X=(x_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
be the matrix with variables
\{x_{ij}\}
.

1) We say that a polynomial
f(X)
is obtained by a triangular transformation from the variable
x_{ik}
if
f(X)=f_{0}(X)x_{ik}+f_{1}(X)
, where
f_{0}(X)
and
f_{1}(X)
are polynomials in variables
x_{ab}
,
(a,b)\prec(i,k)
defined over the same field as
f(X)
, and
f_{0}(X)\neq 0
.

2) We say that the system of polynomials
\{f_{ik}(X):~1\leqslant i,k\leqslant n\}
, is obtained by a triangular transformation from the system
\{x_{ik}:~1\leqslant i,k\leqslant n\}
, if each
f_{ik}(X)
is obtained by a triangular transformation from
x_{ik}
.

Remark
. If
\{f_{ik}(X):~1\leqslant i,k\leqslant n\}
is obtained by a a triangular transformation from
\{x_{ik}:~1\leqslant i,k\leqslant n\}
, then the first system of polynomials (and the second also) freely generate the field of rational functions
K(X)
.

Aparajita Dasgupta, Vishvesh Kumar, Shyam Swarup Mondal, Michael Ruzhansky

Introduction

In this work, we are interested in the study of the Cauchy problem for the semilinear damped wave equation with power-type nonlinearities:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{tt}+\mathcal{R}u+u_{t}=|u|^{p},&x\in\mathbb{G},~t>0,\\
u(0,x)=\varepsilon u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{G},\\
u_{t}(0,x)=\varepsilon u_{1}(x),&x\in\mathbb{G},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
1<p<\infty
,
\mathcal{R}
is a positive Rockland operator of homogeneous degree
\nu\geq 2
on a graded Lie group
\mathbb{G}
, and the initial data
(u_{0},u_{1})
with its size parameter
\varepsilon>0
belongs to homogeneous Sobolev spaces of negative order
\dot{H}^{-\gamma}(\mathbb{G})\times\dot{H}^{-\gamma}(\mathbb{G})
with
\gamma>0
. Here we note that the commutative group
(\mathbb{R}^{n},+),
the Heisenberg group, Engel groups, Cartan groups, and more generally, stratified Lie groups are examples of graded Lie groups.

Müller and Stein
[
28
]
initiated the analysis of wave equations associated with sub-Laplacian on the Heisenberg group and proved
L^{p}
-estimates of wave kernels. Later, Müller and Seeger
[
29
]
proved sharp
L^{p}
-estimates of wave kernels on groups of Heisenberg type.
Bahouri, Geŕard, and Xu
[
2
]
began a systematic study of damped wave equations involving sub-Laplacian on the Heisenberg group to prove a weak type decay rate in the dispersive estimate and local Strichartz estimates, which are mainly based on the analysis in Besov type spaces. In
[
1
]
, the authors extended these results in the setting of step 2 stratified Lie groups and showed that the decay rate of the solution might be influenced by the dimension of the center of the group. Nevertheless, a better decay rate can be achieved when the wave equation for the full Laplacian on the Heisenberg group is taken into account, see
[
12
,
25
]
. See also
[
41
]
for decay estimates for a class of wave equations on the Heisenberg group.
We also refer to
[
22
,
23
]
, where damped hyperbolic equations were studied using attractors.

In
[
14
]
, Georgiev and Palmieri investigated the global existence and nonexistence results for the Cauchy problem for the semilinear damped wave equation on the Heisenberg group with the power type nonlinear (such as
|u|^{p}
) by finding the critical exponent of Fujita-type. The meaning of critical exponent is the threshold condition on the exponent
p
for the global-in-time Sobolev solutions and the blow-up of local-in-time weak solutions with small data. Its counterpart in the Euclidean framework, the critical exponent for the semilinear damped wave equation with the power type nonlinear, can be found in
[

,
26
,
47
,
44
]
and references therein. Moreover, Palmieri
[
33
]
derived the
L^{2}
-decay estimates for solutions as well as for their time derivative, and their horizontal gradient of solution to the homogeneous linear damped wave equation on the Heisenberg group. In
[
38
]
, the authors considered the Cauchy problem for the semilinear damped wave equation for the sub-Laplacian on the Heisenberg group with power-like nonlinearities and proved global in time well-posedness for small data in the case of the positive mass and damping term. Moreover, the estimates for the linear viscoelastic type damped wave equation on the Heisenberg group can be found in
[
24
]
. We also refer to
[

In recent years, considerable attention has been devoted by several researchers to find new critical exponents for the semilinear damped wave equations in different frameworks. For instance, in the Euclidean setting, for the semilinear damped wave equations with initial data belonging to
L^{m}
-regular data instead of
L^{1}
-data with
m\in(1,2)
, the critical exponent is changed from
p_{\text{Crit}}(n)=1+\frac{2}{n}
to the new modified critical exponent
p_{\text{Crit}}(n):=p_{\text{Crit }}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)=1+\frac{2m}{n}
, see
[
19
,
18
,
31
]
. Furthermore, for initial data additionally belonging to homogeneous Sobolev spaces
\dot{H}^{-\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of negative order
-\gamma
, the new critical exponent becomes
p_{\text{Crit}}(n,\gamma):=1+\frac{4}{n+2\gamma}
for some
\gamma\in(0,\frac{n}{2})
, see
[
4
]
for more detail. In the non-Euclidean settings, specifically, for the semilinear damped wave equation associated with sub-Laplacian on the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, we
[
7
]
proved that the critical exponent
1+\frac{2}{Q}
for
L^{1}
-regular data is changed into the modified critical exponent
p_{\text{Crit}}(Q,\gamma):=1+\frac{4}{Q+2\gamma}
for some
\gamma\in(0,\frac{Q}{2})
if initial data is localized in the homogeneous Sobolev spaces of negative order
\dot{H}^{-\gamma}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, where
Q:=2n+2
is the homogeneous dimension on
\mathbb{H}^{n}.

One of the important features in the negative order Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{-\gamma},\gamma>0,
is that the corresponding Sobolev norm of initial data enhances the faster decay rate of the solution compared to the
L^{2}
-decay.
The study of different kinds of partial differential equations in the framework of Sobolev space of negative order is not a new topic. For example, one can see

The main aim of this paper is to find a critical exponent for the semilinear hypoelliptic damped wave equation (
1.1
) associated with a positive Rockland operator
\mathcal{R}
on a graded Lie group
\mathbb{G}
with power type nonlinearity
|u|^{p}
and initial data taken from negative order homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{-\gamma}(\mathbb{G}),\gamma>0
.

Recall that a connected and simply connected Lie group
\mathbb{G}
is a graded Lie group if its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is
graded
, that is,
\mathfrak{g}
admits a vector space decomposition of the form
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{g}_{i},
for which all but finitely many
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
’s are
\{0\}
such that
[\mathfrak{g}_{i},\mathfrak{g}_{j}]\subset\mathfrak{g}_{i+j}
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{N}.
We refer to Section
2
for a detailed description of the graded Lie groups. If the first stratum
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
generates the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
as an algebra, the group
\mathbb{G}
is called a stratified Lie group. In this case, the sum of squares of a basis of vector fields in
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
gives a sub-Laplacian on
\mathbb{G}
. This immediately shows that every stratified Lie group is graded. However, if the group
\mathbb{G}
is non-stratified, then it may not have a homogeneous sub-Laplacian or Laplacian but they always posses Rockland operators. A Rockland operator on
\mathbb{G}
is a left-invariant hypoelliptic differential operator of a positive homogeneous degree
\nu
, see Subsection
2.3
for an overview. The Heisenberg group, more generally,
H
-type groups, Engel groups, and Cartan groups are examples of graded Lie groups. The following are some examples of graded Lie groups with a Rockland operator which are included in the analysis of this paper.

•

When
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{n},+),
a Rockland operator
\mathcal{R}
can be any positive homogeneous elliptic differential operator with constant coefficients, for example, we can consider

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}=(-\Delta)^{m}\text{ or }\mathcal{R}=(-1)^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\right)^{2m},\quad a_{j}>0,m\in\mathbb{N},

(1.2)

which are Rockland operators with homogeneous degree
2m
when the commutative group
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is equipped with isotropic dilations.

•

When
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{H}^{n},
the Heisenberg group, we can consider the Rockland operator of the homogeneous degree
2m
as

\mathcal{R}=(-\mathcal{L})^{m}\text{ or }\mathcal{R}=(-1)^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(a_{j}X_{j}^{2m}+b_{j}Y_{j}^{2m}\right),\quad a_{j},b_{j}>0,m\in\mathbb{N},

where
X_{j}=\partial_{x_{j}}-\frac{y_{j}}{2}\partial_{t},Y_{j}=\partial_{y_{j}}+\frac{x_{j}}{2}\partial_{t}
are the left-invariant vector fields for its algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathcal{L}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(X_{j}^{2}+Y_{j}^{2}\right)
is the sub-Laplacian on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

•

When
\mathbb{G}
is a stratified Lie group, then
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{G}}
, defined in (
2.4
)
is a positive Rockland operator with homogeneous degree
\nu=2
.

•

When
\mathbb{G}
is a graded Lie group with dilation weights
\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n}
, if
\nu_{0}
is any common multiple of
\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n}
, then the operators given by

\mathcal{R}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{\frac{v_{0}}{v_{j}}}a_{j}X_{j}^{2\frac{v_{0}}{v_{j}}},\quad\text{with}\,\,a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}>0,

(1.3)

are positive Rockland operators of homogeneous degree
\nu=2\nu_{0}
for any strong Malcev basis
\{X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}\}
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.

For the semilinear hypoelliptic damped wave equation (
1.1
) for a positive Rockland operator
\mathcal{R}
of homogeneous degree
\nu
with power type nonlinearity
|u|^{p}
on the graded Lie group
\mathbb{G}
, we show that
p_{\text{Crit}}(Q,\gamma,\nu):=1+\frac{2\nu}{Q+2\gamma}
is the critical exponent for some
\gamma\in(0,\frac{Q}{2})
when the initial data are additionally taken from homogeneous Sobolev space of negative order
\dot{H}^{-\gamma}(\mathbb{G})
. We note that our analysis on this new critical exponent
p_{\text{Crit}}(Q,\gamma,\nu):=1+\frac{2\nu}{Q+2\gamma}
generalizes several known results. For example,

•

When
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathcal{R}=-\Delta

coincides with the critical exponent

for some

•

When

and a finite time blow-up of weak solutions for
1<p<p_{\text{Crit}}(Q,\gamma,\nu)
to (
1.1
). We also derive a sharp estimate for the lifespan in the sub-critical case:
1<p<p_{\text{Crit}}(Q,\gamma,\nu)
.

2.1.
Graded Lie groups

A graded Lie group
\mathbb{G}
is a connected and simply connected Lie group whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is
graded
, that is,
\mathfrak{g}
admits a vector space decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{g}_{i},
for which all but finitely many
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
’s are
\{0\}
and satisfy the identities
[\mathfrak{g}_{i},\mathfrak{g}_{j}]\subset\mathfrak{g}_{i+j}
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{N}.
Such a decomposition of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is called
gradation
of
\mathfrak{g}.
A graded Lie algebra is
stratifiable
if there exists a gradation of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{i}]=\mathfrak{g}_{i+1}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}.
A Lie group
\mathbb{G}
whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is stratifiable is called a stratified Lie group. This immediately shows that every stratified Lie group is graded. The Heisenberg group, more generally,
H
-type groups, Engel groups and Cartan groups are examples of graded Lie groups.

We define a family of dilations
D_{r}^{\mathfrak{g}},\,r>0,
on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
as the vector space automorphisms of
\mathfrak{g}
of the form
D_{r}^{\mathfrak{g}}:=\exp(\ln(r)A)
for some diagonalisable matrix
A\sim\text{diag}[\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\ldots,\nu_{n}]
with positive eigenvalues
0<\nu_{1}\leq\nu_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\nu_{n}
on
\mathfrak{g}
such that

D_{r}^{\mathfrak{g}}[X,Y]=[D_{r}^{\mathfrak{g}}X,D_{r}^{\mathfrak{g}}Y],

for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
and
r>0.
The positive eigenvalues
0<\nu_{1}\leq\nu_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\nu_{n}
of
A
are called the dilations’ weights of
\mathfrak{g}.
A Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
homogeneous
if there exists a family of dilations
D_{r}^{\mathfrak{g}},\,r>0,
on
\mathfrak{g}.
It is well known that the existence of a family of dilations on
\mathfrak{g}
implies that
\mathfrak{g}
is a nilpotent Lie algebra. The Lie group
\mathbb{G}:=\exp{\mathfrak{g}},
which is connected and simply connected, is called
homogeneous
if
\mathfrak{g}
is a homogeneous Lie algebra. The family of dilations
\{D_{r}^{\mathfrak{g}}:r>0\}
on Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
induces a family of dilations
\{D_{r}:r>0\}
on the group
\mathbb{G}
by
D_{r}:=\exp\circ D_{r}^{\mathfrak{g}}\circ\exp^{-1},\,\,r>0.

It is easy to note that for a given graded Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{g}_{i}
, the sequence of subspaces
\mathfrak{I}_{k}:=\bigoplus_{i=k}^{\infty}\mathfrak{g}_{i}
forms a finite nested sequence of ideals in
\mathfrak{g}.
Thus, using these ideals
\mathfrak{I}_{k},
any basis
\{X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}\}
given as the union of the bases
\{X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n_{i}}\}
is necessarily a strong Malcev basis of
\mathfrak{g}.
Such a basis of a graded Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
gives rise to a family of dilations
D_{r}^{\mathfrak{g}},\,r>0,
on
\mathfrak{g}
using the matrix given by
AX_{j}=iX_{j}
for every
X_{j}\in\mathfrak{g}_{i},
that is,
D_{r}^{\mathfrak{g}}X_{j}=r^{i}X_{j}.

We may identify
\mathbb{G}
with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n=\text{dim}~\mathfrak{g}
via the exponential map
\exp:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow G
given by
x=\exp(x_{1}X_{1}+x_{2}X_{2}+\cdots+x_{n}X_{n})\in\mathbb{G}
having a basis of
\mathfrak{g}.
Using this identification, we can naturally identify a function on
\mathbb{G}
as a function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
These exponential coordinates allow us to represent the action of
D_{r}
on
\mathbb{G}
explicitly:

D_{r}(x)=rx:=\exp(r^{\nu_{1}}X_{1}+r^{\nu_{2}}X_{2}+\cdots+r^{\nu_{n}}X_{n})=(r^{\nu_{1}}x_{1},r^{\nu_{2}}x_{2},\ldots,r^{\nu_{n}}x_{n}),

for
x:=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{G},\,\,r>0.
This notion of dilation on
\mathbb{G}
is crucial to define the notion of homogeneity for functions, measures, and operators. For examples, the bi-invariant Haar measure
dx
on
\mathbb{G},
which is just a Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n},
is
Q
-homogeneous in the sense that

d(D_{r}(x))=r^{Q}dx,

where
Q:=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}i\dim\mathfrak{g}_{i}=\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}+\ldots+\nu_{n}
is called the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{G}.
It is customary to jointly rescale weights so that
\nu_{1}=1.
This also shows that
Q\geq n.

Identifying the elements of
\mathfrak{g}
with the left-invariant vector fields, each
X_{j}
is a homogeneous differential operator of degree
\nu_{j}.
For every multi-index
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}
, we set
X^{\alpha}=X_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}X_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\ldots X_{r}^{\alpha_{n}}
in the universal enveloping algebra
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}.
Then
X^{\alpha}
is of homogeneous degree
[\alpha]:=\alpha_{1}\nu_{1}+\alpha_{2}\nu_{2}+\ldots+\alpha_{n}\nu_{n}.

A
homogeneous quasi-norm
on a homogeneous group
\mathbb{G}
is a continuous function
|\cdot|:\mathbb{G}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that it satisfies the following properties:

•

|x|=0
if and only if
x=e_{\mathbb{G}}.

•

|x^{-1}|=|x|

•

|D_{r}x|=r|x|
for

There always exists a homogeneous quasi-norm in any homogeneous group
\mathbb{G}
(
[

). One can show the existence of a homogeneous quasi-norm on
\mathbb{G}
, which is
C^{\infty}
-smooth on
\mathbb{G}\backslash\{e_{\mathbb{G}}\}.
Every homogeneous quasi-norm satisfies the following triangle inequality with the constant
C\geq 1:

|xy|\leq C(|x|+|y|)\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{G}.

In fact, it is always possible to choose a homogeneous quasi-norm on any homogeneous group that satisfies the triangle inequality with constant
C=1.
Any two homogeneous quasi-norms on
\mathbb{G}
are equivalent.

Similar to the Euclidean space, there is a notion of polar decomposition on a homogeneous group
\mathbb{G}
for the homogenous quasi-norm

Let

\mathfrak{S}:={x\in G:\quad|x|=1}

be the unite sphere with respect to the homogenous quasi-norm
|\cdot|.
Then there exists a unique Radon measure
\sigma
on
\mathfrak{S}
such that for all
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G}),
we have

\int_{G}f(x)dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}f(ry)r^{Q-1}d\sigma(y)\,dr.

2.3.
Positive Rockland operators on graded Lie groups

Now it is time to introduce the main object of the discussion, namely, Rockland operators. To define them, we first need to fix some notation for the continuous unitary representations of the group. Let
(\pi,\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
be a continuous unitary representation of a graded Lie group
\mathbb{G}.
Denote the set of equivalence classes of all strongly continuous unitary representations of
\mathbb{G}
by
\widehat{\mathbb{G}}.
Here, the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
denotes the representation space of
\pi.
We also denote the space of all smooth vectors of
\pi
by
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty},
which is a subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}.

The infinitesimal representation of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and its extension to the universal enveloping Lie algebra
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})
will also be denoted by
\pi.
We note here that the space of left-invariant vector fields and the algebra of left-invariant differential operators on
\mathbb{G}
can be identified with
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g}),
respectively. For a left-invariant differential operator
T
, let us denote by
\pi(T),
the infinitesimal representation
d\pi(T)
associated with
\pi\in\widehat{\mathbb{G}}.

A left-invariant differential operator
\mathcal{R}
on a homogeneous group
\mathbb{G}
called the
Rockland operator
if it is homogeneous of positive degree
\nu,
that is,

\mathcal{R}(f\circ D_{r})=r^{\nu}(\mathcal{R}f)\circ D_{r},\quad r>0,\,\,f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}),

and
the operator
\pi(\mathcal{R})
is injective on
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\pi}
for every nontrivial representation
\pi\in\widehat{\mathbb{G}},
that is,

\forall v\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\pi}\quad\pi(\mathcal{R})v=0\implies v=0.

(2.2)

The condition (
2.2
) is known as the Rockland condition. The Rockland condition for
\mathcal{R}
is equivalent to the
hypoellipticity
of
\mathcal{R},
that is locally,
\mathcal{R}f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\implies f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}).
This equivalence is commonly known as the
Rockland conjecture
(see
[
36
]
) and was resolved in
[
16
]
(see also
[
27
]
).
A Rockland operator is
positive
when

\int_{\mathbb{G}}\mathcal{R}f(x)\overline{f(x)}dx\geq 0,\quad\forall f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{G}).

It is a celebrated result of ter Elst and Robinson
[
43
]
which says that if there is a Rockland operator on a homogeneous Lie group
\mathbb{G}
, then the group
\mathbb{G}
must be graded.
On the other hand, an infinite family of positive Rockland operators can be created for any graded Lie group
\mathbb{G}.
Indeed, the operators given by

\mathcal{R}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{\frac{\nu_{0}}{\nu_{j}}}a_{j}X_{j}^{2\frac{\nu_{0}}{\nu_{j}}},\quad\text{with}\,\,a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}>0

(2.3)

for any strong Malcev basis
\{X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}\}
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and any common multiple
\nu_{0}
of
\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\ldots,\nu_{n},
are positive Rockland operators of homogeneous degree
\nu=2\nu_{0}.
It is easy to see that if
\mathcal{R}
is a positive Rockland operator, then its powers
\mathcal{R}^{k},\,k\in\mathbb{N},
and complex conjugate
\overline{\mathcal{R}}
are also Rockland operators.

Throughout this paper, we will always assume that a Rockland operator is always positive and essentially self-adjoint on
L^{2}(\mathbb{G}).

In the stratified case, assume that
\{X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n_{1}}\}
is a basis of the first stratum
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
of the stratified Lie algebra. Then any left-invariant
sub-Laplacian
(with analyst sign convention) on
\mathbb{G}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{G}}:=-(X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+\cdots+X_{n_{1}}^{2})

(2.4)

is a positive Rockland operator of the homogeneous degree
\nu=2.

On
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{R}^{n},
with the trivial stratification and canonical family of dilation
D_{r}(x)=rx,\,\,r>0,
on the group
(\mathbb{R}^{n},+)
, the Laplace operator operator
-\Delta_{x}:=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{i}}^{2}
is a particular case of a positive sub-Laplacian. By equipping the group
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{n},+)
with another isotropic or anisotropic family of dilations with the dilations’ weights
\mathbb{R}^{n}\ni(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\ldots,\nu_{n})\neq(1,1,\ldots,1)
determined by the canonical basis of
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{R}^{n},
the operator

\mathcal{R}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{\frac{\nu_{0}}{\nu_{j}}}a_{j}\partial_{j}^{2\frac{\nu_{0}}{\nu_{j}}},\quad\text{with}\,\,a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}>0

(2.5)

is a positive Rockland operator of Homogeneous degree
\nu:=2\nu_{0}
on
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{n},+)
provided
\nu_{0}
is any common multiple of
\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\ldots,\nu_{n}.

2.4.
Fourier transform on graded Lie groups

One of the important tools to deal with PDEs on graded Lie groups is the operator-valued group Fourier transform on
\mathbb{G}
. The group Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{G}}(f)(\pi):\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
of
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{G})\cong\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
at
\pi\in\widehat{\mathbb{G}},
is a linear mapping that can be represented by an infinite matrix once we choose a basis for
the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\pi},
and defined by

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{G}}(f)(\pi)=\widehat{f}(\pi):=\int_{\mathbb{G}}f(x)\pi(x)^{*}dx=\int_{\mathbb{G}}f(x)\pi(x^{-1})\,dx.

(2.6)

For
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{G}),
the operator
\widehat{f}(\pi)
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator on
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
for each
\pi\in\widehat{G}.
Moreover, there exists a measure
\mu
on
\widehat{G}
such that the following inversion formula

f(x)=\int_{\widehat{\mathbb{G}}}\text{Tr}(\pi(x)\widehat{f}(\pi))d\mu(\pi)

holds for every
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{G})
and
x\in\mathbb{G}.

Additionally, the following
Plancherel identity
is also true for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{G}):

\int_{\mathbb{G}}|f(x)|^{2}\,dx=\int_{\widehat{G}}\|\widehat{f}(\pi)\|_{\text{HS}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})}^{2}\,d\mu(\pi).

(2.7)

Furthermore, the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{G}}
extends uniquely to a unitary isomorphism from
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
onto the space
L^{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{G}}),
where the space
L^{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{G}})
is defined as the direct integral of Hilbert spaces of measurable fields of operators

L^{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{G}}):=\int_{\widehat{G}}^{\oplus}\text{HS}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})d\mu(x)

with the norm

\|\tau\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{G}})}=\left(\int_{\widehat{\mathbb{G}}}\|\tau_{\pi}\|_{\text{HS}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})}^{2}\,d\mu(\pi)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The measure
\mu
is called the Plancherel measure on
\widehat{\mathbb{G}}
.

Moreover, for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
, we have

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{G}}(\mathcal{R}f)(\pi)=\pi(\mathcal{R})\widehat{f}(\pi).

The authors in
[
17
]
proved that the spectrum of the operator
\pi(\mathcal{R})
with
\pi\in\widehat{\mathbb{G}}\backslash\{1\}
, is discrete and lies in
(0,\infty)
. Thus we can choose an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
such that the infinite matrix associated
to the self-adjoint operator
\pi(\mathcal{R})
has the following representation

\displaystyle\pi(\mathcal{R})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\pi_{1}^{2}&0&\cdots&\cdots\\
0&\pi_{2}^{2}&0&\cdots\\
\vdots&0&\ddots&\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\ddots\end{array}\right)

where
\pi_{i},\,i=1,2,\ldots,
are strictly positive real numbers and
\pi\in\widehat{\mathbb{G}}\backslash\{1\}
.

2.5.
Sobolev spaces on graded Lie groups and interpolation inequalities

The Sobolev spaces on graded Lie groups were systematically studied by Fischer and the third author in
[
10
,
9
]
.

The inhomogeneous Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\mathbb{G}):=H^{s}_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathbb{G}),s\in\mathbb{R}
, associated to positive Rockland operator
\mathcal{R}
of homogeneous degree
\nu
, is defined as

H^{s}\left(\mathbb{G}\right):=\left\{f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{G}\right):(I+\mathcal{R})^{s/\nu}f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{G}\right)\right\},

with the norm

\|f\|_{H^{s}\left(\mathbb{G}\right)}:=\left\|(I+\mathcal{R})^{s/\nu}f\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{G}\right)}.

Similarly, we define the homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{p,s}_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathbb{G}):=\dot{H}^{p,s}(\mathbb{G})
on
\mathbb{G}
as the space of all
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{G})
such that

\mathcal{R}^{{s}/{\nu}}f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{G})
with the norm

\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{p,s}(\mathbb{G})}:=\left\|\mathcal{R}^{s/\nu}f\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{G}\right)}.

The reason for omitting the subscript
\mathcal{R}
is that these Sobolev spaces are independent of the choice of a Rockland operator
\mathcal{R}
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a graded Lie group with homogeneous dimension
Q
. Then we use the following inequalities developed in
[
38
,
9
,
10
]
throughout this work.

•

Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality

[
9
,
10
]
: Let
s>0
and
1<p<q<\infty
be such that

\frac{s}{Q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}.

Then

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{L}^{q}(\mathbb{G})}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\dot{H}}^{p,s}(\mathbb{G})}\simeq\|\mathcal{R}^{\frac{s}{\nu}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}.

(2.12)

•

Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

[
38
]
:
Let

s\in(0,1],1<r<\frac{Q}{s},\text{ and }~2\leq q\leq\frac{rQ}{Q-sr}.
Then

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{G})}\lesssim\|u\|_{{\dot{H}}^{r,s}(\mathbb{G})}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{G})}^{1-\theta},

(2.13)

for
\theta=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right)/{\left(\frac{s}{Q}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\in[0,1]
, provided that
\frac{s}{Q}+\frac{1}{2}\neq\frac{1}{r}
.

3.
Philosophy of our approach

In this section, we will describe our approach to prove the main results of this paper. First, we provide the notion of mild solutions to (
1.1
) in our framework. Consider the inhomogeneous system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{tt}+\mathcal{R}u+u_{t}=F(t,x),&x\in\mathbb{G},~t>0,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{G},\\
u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),&x\in\mathbb{G}.\end{cases}

(3.1)

By applying Duhamel’s principle, the solution to the above system can be written as

u(t,x)=u_{0}*E_{0}(t,x)+u_{1}*E_{1}(t,x)+\int_{0}^{t}F(s,x)*E_{1}(t-s,x)\;ds,

where
*
denotes the group convolution product on
\mathbb{G}
with respect to the
x
variable, and
E_{0}
and
E_{1}
represent the propagators to (
3.1
) in the homogeneous case
F=0
with initial data
\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)=\left(\delta_{0},0\right)
and
\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)=

\left(0,\delta_{0}\right)
, respectively. Our main interest lies in considering power-type nonlinearities, that is,
F(t,x)=|u(t,x)|^{p}
, and this will always be the case throughout the paper.

A function
u
is said to be a
mild solution
to (
1.1
) on
[0,T]
if
u
is a fixed point for the integral operator
N:u\in X_{s}(T)\mapsto Nu(t,x),
given by

\displaystyle Nu(t,x):=u^{\text{lin}}(t,x)+u^{\text{non}}(t,x),

(3.2)

in the energy evolution space
X_{s}(T)\doteq\mathcal{C}\left([0,T],H^{s}(\mathbb{G})\right),s\in(0,1]
,
equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}

\displaystyle:=\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left((1+t)^{\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}+(1+t)^{\frac{s+\gamma}{\nu}}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\right)

(3.3)

with
\gamma>0,

where

u^{\text{lin}}(t,x)=u_{0}*E_{0}(t,x)+u_{1}*E_{1}(t,x)

is the solution to the corresponding linear Cauchy problem (
1.1
), and

u^{\text{non}}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}F(s,x)*E_{1}(t-s,x)\;ds.

We aim to prove the global-in-time existence and uniqueness of small data Sobolev solutions of low regularity to the semilinear damped wave equation (
1.1
) with
the help of the Banach’s fixed point theorem argument. We find a unique fixed point (say)
u^{*}
of the operator
N
, that means,
u^{*}=Nu^{*}\in X_{s}(T)
for all positive
T
. More precisely, to find such a unique fixed point, we will establish two crucial inequalities of the form

\displaystyle\|Nu\|_{X_{s}(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s}}+\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p},

(3.4)

and

\displaystyle\|Nu-Nv\|_{X_{s}(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u-v\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left[\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right],

(3.5)

for any
u,v\in X_{s}(T)
with initial data space
\mathcal{A}^{s}:=\ (H^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}\ )\times\ (L^{2}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}\ )
.
We will consider sufficiently small
\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s}}<\varepsilon
so that combining (
3.4
) with (
3.5
) we can apply Banach’s fixed point theorem to ensure that there exists a global-in-time small data unique Sobolev solution
u^{*}=Nu^{*}\in X_{s}(T)
for all
T>0
, which also gives the solution to (
1.1
). Here we want to note that the treatment of the power-type nonlinear term in
\dot{H}^{-\gamma}
is based on the applications of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality and the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality on the graded Lie group
\mathbb{G}
.

Before considering the semilinear Cauchy problem, first, we will determine the
\dot{H}^{s}
-norm estimate for the solution to the linear problem (
1.4
) via the group Fourier transform on the graded Lie group
\mathbb{G}
concerning the spatial variable.

On the other hand, to prove a blow-up (in-time) result in the subcritical case to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) for certain ranges of
p
regardless of the size of the initial data, we need to introduce a suitable notion of a weak solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
).

For any
T>0
, a weak solution of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) in
[0,T)\times\mathbb{G}
is a function
u\in L_{\text{loc }}^{p}\left([0,T)\times\mathbb{G}\right)
that satisfies the following integral relation:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{G}}u(t,x)\left(\partial_{t}^{2}\phi(t,x)-\mathcal{R}\phi(t,x)-\partial_{t}\phi(t,x)\right)dx\;dt-\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}u_{0}(x)\phi(0,x)dx

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}u_{1}(x)\phi(0,x)dx+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{G}}u_{0}(x)\partial_{t}\phi(0,x)dx=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(t,x)|^{p}\phi(t,x)dx\;dt,

(3.6)

for any
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\mathbb{G})
and any
t\in(0,T)
.
If
T=\infty
, we call
u
to be a global-in-time weak solution to (
1.1
), otherwise
u
is said to be a local-in-time weak solution to (
1.1
).
We obtain blow-up of weak solutions even for small
data in the case
1<p<p_{\text{Crit}}(Q,\gamma,\nu)
by employing the test function method.

4.
Main results

This section is devoted to present the proofs of the main results of this paper. In particular, in the setting of negative order Sobolev spaces, we show that
p_{\text{Crit}}(Q,\gamma,\nu)=1+\frac{2\nu}{Q+2\gamma}
is the new critical exponent for some
\gamma\in(0,\frac{Q}{2})
to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) by proving global-in-time existence of small data Sobolev solutions of lower regularity for
p>p_{\text{Crit}}(Q,\gamma,\nu)
and a finite time blow-up of weak solutions for
1<p<p_{\text{Crit}}(Q,\gamma,\nu)
. We begin this section with the following
\dot{H}^{s}
- estimates for solutions to the homogeneous Cauchy problem (
1.4
), which will be crucial to proving the global-in-time existence of small data Sobolev solutions.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Applying the group Fourier transform (

\displaystyle=(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}(s\theta_{1}+\gamma)}\left\{(1+\kappa)^{\frac{(s+\gamma)}{\nu}}\|u(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{G})}\right\}^{p\theta_{1}}\left\{(1+\kappa)^{\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\|u(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{G})}\right\}^{p(1-\theta_{1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}\left[{Q}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{mp})+\gamma\right]}\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2})+\frac{1}{\nu}\left({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2}\right)}\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p},

(4.42)

for
\kappa\in[0,T]
provided
\theta_{1}=\frac{Q}{s}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{mp})\in[0,1]
. Since
\theta_{1}\in[0,1]
, we have another restriction
p
as

\displaystyle\frac{2}{m}\leq p\leq\frac{2Q}{m(Q-2s)}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle 1+\frac{2\gamma}{Q}\leq p\leq\frac{Q+2\gamma}{Q-2s}\quad\text{ if }Q>2s,

(4.43)

where we used
m=\frac{2Q}{Q+2\gamma}
. In conclusion, from (
4.40
) and (
4.43
), if we consider

\displaystyle 1+\frac{2\gamma}{Q}\leq p\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}<\infty&\text{ if }Q\leq 2s,\\
\leq\frac{Q}{Q-2s}&\text{ if }Q>2s,\end{array}\right.

then from the estimates (
4.39
) and (
4.42
), it immediately follows that

\displaystyle\left\||u(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{L^{2}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}}\lesssim(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2})+\frac{1}{\nu}\left({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2}\right)}\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p}

(4.44)

for
\kappa\in[0,T]
. In order to estimate the solution itself in
L^{2}
, we first see that

\displaystyle\left\|u^{\mathrm{non}}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\bigg\|\int\limits_{0}^{t}|u(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}*E_{1}(t-\kappa,\cdot)d\kappa\bigg\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{0}^{t}\||u(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}*E_{1}(t-\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}d\kappa.

(4.45)

Next, note that
\tilde{u}(t,x):=(|u(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}*E_{1}(t,\cdot))(x)
is also a solution to the linear system (
1.4
) with initial data
u_{0}=0
,
u_{1}=|u(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}
. To estimate Duhamel’s term on the interval
[0,t]
, we apply
(L^{2}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma})-L^{2}
estimate (
4.37
) for
\tilde{u}
in (
4.45
) to get

\displaystyle\left\|u^{\mathrm{non}}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\limits_{0}^{t}(1+t-\kappa)^{-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\||u(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}\|_{L^{2}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}}d\kappa

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\kappa)^{-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2})+\frac{1}{\nu}\left({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2}\right)}d\kappa~\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2})+\frac{1}{\nu}\left({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2}\right)}d\kappa~\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad+(1+t)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2})+\frac{1}{\nu}\left({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2}\right)}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(1+t-\kappa)^{-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\mathrm{~d}\kappa\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p},

(4.46)

where we used the estimate (
4.44
) for the nonlinear term.

Now we estimate separately the two integrals on the right-hand side of (
4.46
). Notice that the first integral in (
4.46
)

\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2})+\frac{1}{\nu}\left({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2}\right)}d\kappa

converges uniformly over
\left[0,\frac{t}{2}\right]
for
p>1+\frac{2\nu}{Q+2\gamma}
. For the integral over
[\frac{t}{2},t]
, we see that

\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(1+t-\kappa)^{-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\mathrm{~d}\kappa\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(1+t)^{1-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}&\text{ if }\gamma<\nu,\\
\ln(\mathrm{e}+t)&\text{ if }\gamma=\nu,\\
1&\text{ if }\gamma>\nu.\end{array}\right.

•

Considering
p>1+\frac{2\nu}{Q+2\gamma}
if
\gamma\leq\nu
, we see that

\displaystyle(1+t)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2})+\frac{1}{\nu}\left({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2}\right)}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(1+t-\kappa)^{-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}{~d}\kappa

\displaystyle\leq(1+t)^{-1}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(1+t-\kappa)^{-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}{~d}\kappa

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-1}\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(1+t)^{1-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}&\text{ if }\gamma<\nu,\\
\ln(\mathrm{e}+t)&\text{ if }\gamma=\nu,\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}.

(4.49)

•

Considering
p>1+\frac{2\gamma}{Q+2\gamma}
if
\gamma>\nu
, we get

\displaystyle(1+t)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2})+\frac{1}{\nu}\left({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2}\right)}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(1+t-\kappa)^{-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}{~d}\kappa

\displaystyle\leq(1+t)^{-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(1+t-\kappa)^{-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}{~d}\kappa\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}.

(4.50)

Thus from (
4.46
), (
4.49
), and (
4.50
), we finally get

\displaystyle(1+t)^{\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\|u^{\text{non}}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p}.

(4.51)

Again, in order to estimate the solution itself in
\dot{H}^{s}
, using
\left(L^{2}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}\right)-\dot{H}^{s}
estimate (
4.36
) for the interval
\left[0,\frac{t}{2}\right]
and the
L^{2}-\dot{H}^{s}
estimate (
4.35
) for the interval
\left[\frac{t}{2},t\right]
, we get

\displaystyle\left\|u^{\mathrm{non}}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{0}^{t}\||u(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}*E_{1}(t-\kappa,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}d\kappa

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+t-\kappa)^{-\frac{(s+\gamma)}{\nu}}(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2})+\frac{1}{\nu}\left({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2}\right)}d\kappa~\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(1+t-\kappa)^{-\frac{s}{\nu}}(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2})+\frac{Q}{2\nu}}\mathrm{~d}\kappa\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{(s+\gamma)}{\nu}}\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2})+\frac{1}{\nu}\left({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2}\right)}d\kappa~\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad+(1+t)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2})+\frac{Q}{2\nu}}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(1+t-\kappa)^{-\frac{s}{\nu}}\mathrm{~d}\kappa\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p}.

(4.52)

For
s\in(0,1]
and
p>1+\frac{2\nu}{Q+2\gamma}
,
following the same computations performed earlier, we can write the following:

\displaystyle(1+t)^{\frac{s+\gamma}{\nu}}\left\|u^{\text{non}}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\lesssim\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p}.

(4.53)

Therefore, combining (
4.51
) and (
4.53
), it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|u^{\text{non}}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}\lesssim\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p},

(4.54)

provided the exponent
p
satisfies the following criteria:

1<p\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}<\infty&\text{ if }Q\leq 2s,\\
\leq\frac{Q}{Q-2s}&\text{ if }Q>2s,\end{array}\right.\quad
and

\quad p\begin{cases}>1+\frac{2\nu}{Q+2\gamma}&\text{if}~\gamma\leq\nu,\\
>1+\frac{2\gamma}{Q+2\gamma}&\text{if $\gamma>\nu,$}\\
\geq 1+\frac{2\gamma}{Q}.\end{cases}

First, we examine the scenario when
\gamma>\nu
. Subsequently, its easy to see that

\max\left\{1+\frac{2\gamma}{Q+2\gamma},1+\frac{2\gamma}{Q}\right\}=1+\frac{2\gamma}{Q}.

Next, we consider
\gamma\leq\nu
. In this case, we have to compare between
1+\frac{2\nu}{Q+2\gamma}
and
1+\frac{2\gamma}{Q}
. Observe that the expressions
1+\frac{2\nu}{Q+2\gamma}
and
1+\frac{2\gamma}{Q}
intersect at a specific point
\tilde{\gamma}
, which is nothing but the positive root of the quadratic equation
2\gamma^{2}+Q\gamma-\nu Q=0
. Moreover, it is important to highlight that the positive root
\tilde{\gamma}<\nu
for any
Q\geq 1.
This implies that

•

\max\left\{1+\frac{2\nu}{Q+2\gamma},1+\frac{2\gamma}{Q}\right\}=1+\frac{2\nu}{Q+2\gamma}
for
\gamma\leq\tilde{\gamma}
,

•

\max\left\{1+\frac{2\nu}{Q+2\gamma},1+\frac{2\gamma}{Q}\right\}=1+\frac{2\gamma}{Q}
for
\tilde{\gamma}<\gamma\leq\nu
.

Summarizing, for any
\gamma\in(0,\frac{Q}{2})
, the assumption for the exponent
p
is reduced to

\displaystyle p\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}>1+\frac{2\nu}{Q+2\gamma}=p_{\text{Crit }}(Q,\gamma,\nu)&\text{ if }\gamma\leq\tilde{\gamma},\\
\geq 1+\frac{2\gamma}{Q}&\text{ if }\gamma>\tilde{\gamma}.\end{array}\right.

Therefore, the condition for the exponent
p
reduces to (
1.2
). Moreover, from (
4.38
) and (
4.54
), we get our required first inequality (
4.32
), i.e.,

\displaystyle\|Nu\|_{X_{s}(T)}\lesssim\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s}}+\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p}.

(4.55)

To establish (
4.33
), our next objective is to evaluate
\|Nu-Nv\|_{X_{s}(T)}
. First we notice that

\|Nu-Nv\|_{X_{s}(T)}=\left\|\int_{0}^{t}E_{1}(t-\kappa,\cdot)*\left(|u(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}-|v(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}\right){d}\kappa\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}.

Now the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality (
2.12
) yields

\displaystyle\left\||u(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}-|v(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-\gamma}}\lesssim\left\||u(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}-|v(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{L^{m}}

with
\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{\gamma}{Q}
provided
\gamma\in(0,Q)
and
m\in(1,2)
.

Now using the fact that
||u|^{p}-|v|^{p}|\leq p|u-v|(|u|^{p-1}+|v|^{p-1})
along with Hölder’s inequality on the right-hand side of the above inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\||u(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}-|v(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{L^{m}}\lesssim\|u(\kappa,\cdot)-v(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{mp}}\left(\|u(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{mp}}^{p-1}+\|v(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{mp}}^{p-1}\right).

(4.56)

Our next aim is to estimate three terms on the right-hand side of the previous inequality (
4.56
). Applying the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
2.13
) for each term that appeared on the right-hand side of (
4.56
), we get

\displaystyle\left\|u(\kappa,\cdot)-v(\kappa,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{mp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u(\kappa,\cdot)-v(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{\theta_{1}}\|u(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{(1-\theta_{1})}

\displaystyle=(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{1}{\nu}\left({\gamma}+{s\theta_{1}}\right)}\left\{(1+\kappa)^{\frac{(s+\gamma)}{\nu}}\|u(\kappa,\cdot)-v(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\right\}^{\theta_{1}}\left\{(1+\kappa)^{\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\|u(\kappa,\cdot)-v(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\right\}^{(1-\theta_{1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{1}{\nu}\left({\gamma}+{s\theta_{1}}\right)}\left\|u-v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}

(4.57)

and

\displaystyle\left\|u(\kappa,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{mp}}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}^{(p-1)\theta_{1}}\|u(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}^{(p-1)(1-\theta_{1})}

\displaystyle=(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{(p-1)}{\nu}(s\theta_{1}+\gamma)}\left\{(1+\kappa)^{\frac{(s+\gamma)}{\nu}}\|u(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\right\}^{(p-1)\theta_{1}}\left\{(1+\kappa)^{\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\|u(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\right\}^{(p-1)(1-\theta_{1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{(p-1)}{\nu}(s\theta_{1}+\gamma)}\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1},

(4.58)

for
\kappa\in[0,T]
with
\theta_{1}=\frac{Q}{s}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{mp})\in[0,1]
.
Therefore, combining (
4.56
), (
4.57
), and (
4.58
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\||u(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}-|v(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-\gamma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{1}{\nu}\left({\gamma}+{s\theta_{1}}\right)}(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{(p-1)}{\nu}(s\theta_{1}+\gamma)}\left\|u-v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\kappa)^{-\left(\frac{p-1}{\nu}+\frac{1}{\nu}\right)(\gamma+{Q}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{mp}))}\left\|u-v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right)

\displaystyle=(1+\kappa)^{-\left(\frac{p-1}{\nu}+\frac{1}{\nu}\right)(\gamma+\frac{Q}{2}-\frac{1}{p}(\frac{Q}{2}+{\gamma}))}\left\|u-v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right)

\displaystyle=(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}(1-\frac{1}{p})(\gamma+\frac{Q}{2})}\left\|u-v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}\left({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{\nu}({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2})}\left\|u-v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right).

Finally, using the given range of
p
, we see that
-\frac{p}{\nu}\left({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{\nu}({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2})\leq 0
and therefore

\displaystyle\|Nu-Nv\|_{X_{s}(T)}\leq A\|u-v\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right),

(4.59)

for any
u,v\in X_{s}(T)
and for some positive constat
A.

Also from (
4.55
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|Nu\|_{X_{s}(T)}\leq B\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s}}+B\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p},

(4.60)

for some positive constant
B
with initial data space
\mathcal{A}^{s}:=(H^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma})\times(L^{2}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma})
.
Consequently, for some
r>1
, we choose
R_{0}=rB\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s}}
with sufficiently small
\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s}}<\varepsilon
so that

BR_{0}^{p}<\frac{R_{0}}{r}\quad\text{and}\quad 2AR_{0}^{p-1}<\frac{1}{r}.

Then (
4.59
) and (
4.60
) reduce to

\displaystyle\|Nu\|_{X_{s}(T)}\leq\frac{2R_{0}}{r}

(4.61)

and

\displaystyle\|Nu-Nv\|_{X_{s}(T)}\leq\frac{1}{r}\|u-v\|_{X_{s}(T)},

(4.62)

respectively for all
u,v\in\mathcal{B}(R_{0}):=\{u\in X_{s}(T):\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}\leq R_{0}\}
.

Since
\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s}}<\varepsilon
is sufficiently small, (
4.61
) implies that
Nu\in X_{s}(T)
, that is,
N
maps
X_{s}(T)
into itself.
On the other hand, (
4.62
) implies that the map
N
is a contraction mapping on the ball
\mathcal{B}(R_{0})
around the origin in the Banach space
X_{s}(T)
. Using Banach’s fixed point theorem, we can say that there exists a uniquely determined fixed point
u^{*}
of the operator
N
, which means
u^{*}=Nu^{*}\in X_{s}(T)
for all positive
T
. This implies that there exists a global-in-time small data Sobolev solution
u^{*}
of the equation
u^{*}=Nu^{*}
in
X_{s}(T)
, which also gives the solution to the semilinear damped wave equation (
1.1
) and this completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

4.2.
Blow-up result

Now we prove blow-up of weak solutions in the subcritical case
1<p<p(Q,\gamma,\nu)
to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
).

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We apply the so-called test function method on the graded Lie group
\mathbb{G}
in order to prove this result. For
R>1
, let us consider the test function as

\varphi_{R}(t,x)=\Phi\left(\frac{|x|}{R}\right)\Phi\left(\frac{t}{R^{\nu}}\right),

where
\Phi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow[0,1]
is the smooth cut-off function such that

\Phi(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&0\leq r\leq 1\\
\searrow,&1\leq r\leq 2\\
0,&r\geq 2.\end{array}\right.

By changing variables
x=R\tilde{x}
and
t=R^{\nu}\tilde{t}
and the fact that
R>1
, we obtain the following estimates on
\Omega_{T}:=(0,T)\times\mathbb{G},
(see
[
46
]
)

•

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\Omega_{T}}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi_{R}(t,x)\right|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\varphi_{R}(t,x)^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}dxdt\lesssim R^{-\frac{\nu p}{p-1}+Q+\nu},

•

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\Omega_{T}}\left|\mathcal{R}\varphi_{R}(t,x)\right|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\varphi_{R}(t,x)^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}dxdt\lesssim R^{-\frac{\nu p}{p-1}+Q+\nu},

•

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\Omega_{T}}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\varphi_{R}(t,x)\right|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\varphi_{R}(t,x)^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}dxdt\lesssim R^{-\frac{2\nu p}{p-1}+Q+\nu}\leq R^{-\frac{\nu p}{p-1}+Q+\nu}.

For
a,b\geq 0
, using
\varepsilon
-Young’s inequality

ab\leq\varepsilon a^{r}+C(\varepsilon)b^{r^{\prime}},\quad\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}=1,

from the relation (
3.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(t,x)|^{p}\varphi_{R}(t,x)\;dx\;dt+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left(u_{0}(x)+u_{1}(x)\right)\varphi_{R}(0,x)\;dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(t,x)|\left(|\partial_{t}^{2}\varphi_{R}(t,x)|+|\mathcal{R}\varphi_{R}(t,x)|+|\partial_{t}\varphi_{R}(t,x)\right)|\;dx\;dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi_{R}(t,x)^{\frac{1}{p}}|u(t,x)|\left(|\partial_{t}^{2}\varphi_{R}(t,x)|+|\mathcal{R}\varphi_{R}(t,x)|+|\partial_{t}\varphi_{R}(t,x)|\right)\varphi_{R}(t,x)^{-\frac{1}{p}}\;dx\;dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi_{R}(t,x)|u(t,x)|^{p}\;dx\;dt

\displaystyle\quad+C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left(|\partial_{t}^{2}\varphi_{R}(t,x)|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}+|\mathcal{R}\varphi_{R}(t,x)|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}+|\partial_{t}\varphi_{R}(t,x)|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)\varphi_{R}(t,x)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\;dx\;dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi_{R}(t,x)|u(t,x)|^{p}\;dx\;dt+CR^{-\frac{\nu p}{p-1}+Q+\nu},

(4.63)

where we used the fact that
\partial_{t}\varphi_{R}\leq 0
. Thus from (
4.63
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(t,x)|^{p}\varphi_{R}(t,x)\;dx\;dt\leq CR^{-\frac{\nu p}{p-1}+Q+\nu}-\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{G}}(u_{0}(x)+u_{1}(x))\varphi_{R}(0,x)\;dx.

(4.64)

Now we find estimate for

\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left(u_{0}(x)+u_{1}(x)\right)\varphi_{R}(0,x)\;dx

using the assumption (
1.9
). Prior to that, under the assumptions (
1.9
), we must guarantee that the set of all initial data
\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)
in
\dot{H}^{-\gamma}\times\dot{H}^{-\gamma}
is not empty. To prove this, first we claim that
\mathcal{D}_{Q,\gamma}\cap\left(L^{\frac{2Q}{Q+2\gamma}}\times L^{\frac{2Q}{Q+2\gamma}}\right)\neq\emptyset
for
\frac{2Q}{Q+2\gamma}>1
, where the set
\mathcal{D}_{Q,\gamma}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{Q,\gamma}

\displaystyle:=\{\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{G})\times L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{G}):u_{0}(x)+u_{1}(x)\geq C_{1}\langle x\rangle^{-Q\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{Q}\right)}(\log(e+|x|))^{-1}\}.

In particular, consider the functions

\displaystyle u_{0}(x)=u_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=C_{1}\langle x\rangle^{-Q\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{Q}\right)}(\log(e+|x|))^{-1}.

Then using the polar decomposition (
2.1
) on
\mathbb{G}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u_{0}(x)|^{\frac{2Q}{Q+2\gamma}}{d}x

\displaystyle=C_{1}^{\frac{2Q}{Q+2\gamma}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\langle x\rangle^{-Q\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{Q}\right)\times\frac{2Q}{Q+2\gamma}}(\log(e+|x|))^{-\frac{2Q}{Q+2\gamma}}\;dx

\displaystyle=C_{1}^{\frac{2Q}{Q+2\gamma}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\langle x\rangle^{-Q}(\log(e+|x|))^{-\frac{2Q}{Q+2\gamma}}\;dx

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{1}^{\frac{2Q}{Q+2\gamma}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle r\rangle^{-Q}r^{Q-1}(\log({e}+r))^{-\frac{2Q}{Q+2\gamma}}dr

\displaystyle=C_{1}^{\frac{2Q}{Q+2\gamma}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle r\rangle^{-1}(\log({e}+r))^{-\frac{2Q}{Q+2\gamma}}dr<\infty,

for
\frac{2Q}{Q+2\gamma}>1
.
This shows that
u_{0},u_{1}\in L^{\frac{2Q}{Q+2\gamma}}
. Since
\frac{Q+2\gamma}{2Q}-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{\gamma}{Q}
with
\gamma\in\left(0,\frac{Q}{2}\right)
, according to the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality (
2.12
), we have
L^{\frac{2Q}{Q+2\gamma}}\subset\dot{H}^{-\gamma}
. Consequently, in any case, we may claim that

\mathcal{D}_{Q,\gamma}\cap\left(\dot{H}^{-\gamma}\times\dot{H}^{-\gamma}\right)\neq\emptyset,\quad\text{for}~\gamma\in\left(0,\frac{Q}{2}\right).

Now from our assumption (
1.9
), for
R\gg 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left(u_{0}(x)+u_{1}(x)\right)\varphi_{R}(0,x)\;dx

\displaystyle\geq C_{1}\int_{|x|\leq R}\langle x\rangle^{-Q\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{Q}\right)}(\log(e+|x|))^{-1}dx

\displaystyle\geq C_{1}R^{Q-Q(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{Q})}(\log R)^{-1}=C_{1}R^{\frac{Q}{2}-\gamma}(\log R)^{-1}.

(4.65)

By denoting

I_{R}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|u(t,x)|^{p}\varphi_{R}(t,x)\;dx\;dt,

from (
4.64
) and (
4.65
), we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\frac{I_{R}}{2}\leq CR^{-\frac{\nu p}{p-1}+Q+\nu}-C_{1}\varepsilon R^{\frac{Q}{2}-\gamma}(\log R)^{-1}.

(4.66)

By the assumption
1<p<p_{\text{Crit }}(Q,\gamma,\nu)=1+\frac{2\nu}{Q+2\gamma},
we have
Q+\nu-\nu p^{\prime}-(\frac{Q}{2}-\gamma)<0
and consequently, it is easy to see that

R^{Q+\nu-2p^{\prime}-\frac{Q}{2}+\gamma}\log R<\frac{\varepsilon C_{1}}{C},

(4.67)

for large
R\gg 1.
Thus from (
4.67
) and (
4.66
), we obtain

0\leq\frac{I_{R}}{2}\leq CR^{-\frac{\nu p}{p-1}+Q+\nu}-C_{1}\varepsilon R^{\frac{Q}{2}-\gamma}(\log R)^{-1}<0,

which is a contradiction and this completes the proof of the blow-up part. ∎

Remark 4.1
.

In order to ensure that the function
\varphi_{R}
belongs to the space
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\mathbb{G})
, the scaling factor
R^{\nu}
in the bump function with respect to the time variable must be dominated by the lifespan
T_{w,\varepsilon}
of the weak solution. To obtain an upper bound estimate for the lifespan, we consider increasing
R\uparrow T_{w,\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{\nu}}
in (
4.66
). Consequently, similar to (
4.66
), a contradiction exists if we have

{T_{w,\varepsilon}^{\frac{Q+\nu}{\nu}-p^{\prime}}}<C\varepsilon T_{w,\varepsilon}^{\frac{{\frac{Q}{2}-\gamma}}{\nu}}(\log T_{w,\varepsilon})^{-1},

i.e.,

\displaystyle T_{w,\varepsilon}\leq C\varepsilon^{-\left(\frac{1}{p-1}-\left(\frac{Q}{2\nu}+\frac{\gamma}{\nu}\right)\right)^{-1}},

(4.68)

where
C
is positive constant and independent of
\varepsilon
and
p
. Therefore (
4.68
) offers an upper bound estimate for the lifespan for the local in time weak solutions to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
).

4.3.
Sharp lifespan estimate

From (
4.68
) we have an upper bound estimate for the lifespan for the local in time weak solutions to (
1.1
). In order to find a sharp estimate for the lifespan, our next aim is to find the lower bound estimates of lifespan. We will employ several notations, namely, the particular data space
\mathcal{A}^{1}
and the particular evolution space
X_{1}(T)
, which were considered in Section
3
. Let
T_{m,\varepsilon}
be the lifespan of a mild solution
u
to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
). Then, we have the following result regarding the lower bound for the lifespan of mild solution.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
{\mathbb{G}}
be a graded Lie group of homogeneous dimension
Q
and let
\mathcal{R}
be a positive Rockland operator of homogeneous degree
\nu\geq 2.

Let
\gamma\in(0,\tilde{\gamma})
and let the exponent
p
satisfy
1<p<p_{\text{Crit }}(Q,\gamma,\nu)
such that

\displaystyle 1+\frac{2\gamma}{Q}\leq p\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}<\infty&\text{ if }Q\leq 2,\\
\leq\frac{Q}{Q-2}&\text{ if }Q>2.\end{array}\right.

We also assume that
\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\in\mathcal{A}^{1}
such that
\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{A}^{1}}<\varepsilon
. Then, there exists a constant
\varepsilon_{0}
such that for every
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\varepsilon_{0}\right]
, the lifespan
T_{m,\varepsilon}
of mild solutions
u
to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) satisfies the following lower bound condition:

T_{m,\varepsilon}\geq C\varepsilon^{-\left(\frac{1}{p-1}-\left(\frac{Q}{2\nu}+\frac{\gamma}{\nu}\right)\right)^{-1}},

where the positive constant
C
is independent of
\varepsilon
, but may depends on
p,Q,\gamma
as well as
\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}^{1}}
.

Proof.

Here, we observe that local-in-time mild solutions can be directly expressed and estimated using the definition of mild solutions (
3.2
). By following a similar approach as the one used to derive estimate (
4.46
) in Section
4
, we obtain

\displaystyle(1+t)^{\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}^{1}}+\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2})+\frac{1}{\nu}\left({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2}\right)}d\kappa~\left\|u\right\|_{X_{1}(T)}^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad+(1+t)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2})+\frac{1}{\nu}\left({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2}\right)+\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(1+t-\kappa)^{-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\mathrm{~d}\kappa\|u\|_{X_{1}(T)}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}^{1}}+\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2})+\frac{1}{\nu}\left({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2}\right)}d\kappa~\left\|u\right\|_{X_{1}(T)}^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad+(1+t)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2})+\frac{1}{\nu}\left({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2}\right)+1}\|u\|_{X_{1}(T)}^{p},

(4.71)

because of
\gamma\in(0,\min\{\nu,\frac{Q}{2}\})
. Note that, from (
4.43
), the restriction on
p
as

1+\frac{2\gamma}{Q}\leq p\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}<\infty&\text{ if }Q\leq 2,\\
\leq\frac{Q}{(Q-2)}&\text{ if }Q>2,\end{array}\right.

is due to the application of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality.
Due to the fact that
1<p<p_{\text{Crit }}(Q,\gamma,\nu)=1+\frac{2\nu}{Q+2\gamma}
, we immediately get

-\frac{p}{\nu}({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2})+\frac{1}{\nu}\left({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2}\right)>-\frac{2\gamma+Q}{2\nu}\left(1+\frac{2\nu}{Q+2\gamma}\right)+\frac{2\gamma+Q}{2\nu}=-1

and from (
4.71
), we have

\displaystyle(1+t)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}^{1}}+(1+t)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2})+\frac{1}{\nu}\left({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2}\right)+1}\|u\|_{X_{1}(T)}^{p}.

(4.72)

Choosing
s=1
in (
4.52
), we get

\displaystyle(1+t)^{\frac{1+\gamma}{\nu}}\left\|u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\lesssim\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}^{1}}+(1+t)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2})+\frac{Q}{2\nu}+\frac{\gamma}{\nu}+1}\|u\|_{X_{1}(T)}^{p}.

(4.73)

Thus from (
4.72
) and (
4.73
), we can write

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X_{1}(T)}\leq C\varepsilon+D(1+t)^{\alpha(p,Q,\gamma,\nu)}\|u\|_{X_{1}(T)}^{p},

(4.74)

where
\alpha(p,Q,\gamma,\nu):=-\frac{p}{\nu}({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2})+\frac{Q}{2\nu}+\frac{\gamma}{\nu}+1>0
and
C,D
are two positive constants independent of
\varepsilon
and
T
.

Now for a sufficiently large constant
M>0
(to be choosen later), let us introduce

T^{*}:=\sup\left\{T\in\left[0,T_{m,\varepsilon}\right)\text{ such that }\mathcal{X}(T):=\|u\|_{X_{1}(T)}\leq M\varepsilon\right\}.

Using the fact that
\mathcal{X}\left(T^{*}\right)\leq M\varepsilon
, from (
4.74
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}\left(T^{*}\right)=\|u\|_{X_{1}(T^{*})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(C\varepsilon+D\left(1+T^{*}\right)^{\alpha(p,Q,\gamma,\nu)}M^{p}\varepsilon^{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\left(C+D\left(1+T^{*}\right)^{\alpha(p,Q,\gamma,\nu)}M^{p}\varepsilon^{p-1}\right)<M\varepsilon,

(4.75)

for sufficiently large
M
, provided
2C<M
and

2D\left(1+T^{*}\right)^{\alpha(p,Q,\gamma,\nu)}M^{p-1}\varepsilon^{p-1}<1.

It is crucial to observe that the function
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}(T)
is continuous for
T\in\left(0,T_{m,\varepsilon}\right)
. However, inequality (
4.75
) provides the existence of a time
T_{0}\in\left(T^{*},T_{m,\varepsilon}\right)
such that
\mathcal{X}\left(T_{0}\right)\leq M\varepsilon
. This contradicts the fact that
T^{*}
is the supremum.
In other words, we need to ensure strict compliance with the following requirement:

D\left(1+T^{*}\right)^{\alpha(p,Q,\gamma,\nu)}M^{p-1}\varepsilon^{p-1}\geq 1,

that is

1+T^{*}\geq D^{-\frac{1}{\alpha(p,Q,\gamma,\nu)}}M^{-\frac{p-1}{\alpha(p,Q,\gamma,\nu)}}\varepsilon^{-\frac{p-1}{\alpha(p,Q,\gamma,\nu)}}.

Consequently, we can deduce the estimated time of the blow-up as

\displaystyle T_{m,\varepsilon}\geq C\varepsilon^{-\frac{p-1}{\alpha(p,Q,\gamma,\nu)}}=C\varepsilon^{-\frac{\nu(p-1)}{(1-p)({\gamma}+\frac{Q}{2})+\nu}}=C\varepsilon^{-\left(\frac{1}{p-1}-\left(\frac{Q}{2\nu}+\frac{\gamma}{\nu}\right)\right)^{-1}},

(4.76)

which is the required lower-bound estimate for the lifespan of a mild solution. ∎

\hbar
-dependent KP hierarchy

In
[
7
,
8
]
, the authors K. Takasaki and T. Takebe defined the
\hbar
-dependent KP hierarchy (
\hbar
-KP hierarchy for short) by introduced a formal parameter
\hbar
. When
\hbar\rightarrow 0
, the
\hbar
-KP hierarchy becomes the dispersionless KP hierarchy, and when
\hbar\rightarrow 1
, it becomes the KP hierarchy. The tau functions and the wave function in
\hbar
-KP hierarchy are functions of parameters
x,t_{1},t_{2},\cdots
, while
x
is only emerged in
x+t_{1}
. In this paper, we want the parameters are
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots)
, which correspond to
(t_{1},t_{2},\cdots)

[

,
8
]
.

Consider a pseudodifferential operator

L=\hbar\partial+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}f_{j}(\hbar\partial)^{-j},

(2)

and the corresponding eigenvalue problem

Lw=zw,

(3)

where
\partial=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
.

We consider a formal solution

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{x_{j}}{\hbar}z^{j}}(1+\frac{w_{1}}{z}+\frac{w_{2}}{z^{2}}+\cdots)

(4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+{w_{1}}{(\hbar\partial)^{-1}}+{w_{2}}{(\hbar\partial)^{-2}}+\cdots)e^{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{x_{j}}{\hbar}z^{j}}.

(5)

Then let

M=1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{w_{j}}{(\hbar\partial)^{-j}}.

(6)

Consider the linear system of equations

\hbar\frac{\partial w}{\partial x_{j}}=B_{j}w,\ \ \text{with}\ \ B_{j}=(L^{j})_{+},

(7)

where
(L^{j})_{+}
is the differential operator part of
L^{j}
, that is,
(L^{j})_{+}
includes the terms
\partial^{k},\ k\geq 0
in
L^{j}
.

The compatibility condition between (
3
) and (
7
) gives

\hbar\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{j}}=[B_{j},L].

(8)

This is called the
\hbar
-dependent KP hierarchy, the
\hbar
-KP hierarchy for short. It is clear that when
\hbar\rightarrow 1
, it become the classical KP hierarchy.

Substituting (
5
) into (
3
), we get

L=M\cdot(\hbar\partial)\cdot M^{-1}.

(9)

This equation gives the relations between
w_{1},w_{2},\cdots
and
f_{1},f_{2},\cdots
. The compatibility condition (
8
) shows that these unknown functions can be written in terms of a single function
\tau(x)
by the following relation

w=\frac{\tau(x-\hbar[z^{-1}])}{\tau(x)}e^{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{x_{j}}{\hbar}z^{j}},

(10)

where

[z^{-1}]=(\frac{1}{z},\frac{1}{2z^{2}},\frac{1}{3z^{3}},\cdots).

Then the
\hbar
-KP hierarchy is an infinite set of nonlinear differential equations in a function
\tau
of infinitely many variables
x_{1},x_{2},\cdots
. As in the classical KP hierarchy, let

u=2\hbar^{2}\partial^{2}\text{log}\tau.

(11)

We get the first nonlinear differential equation in the
\hbar
-KP hierarchy

\frac{3}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{2}^{2}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{3}}-\frac{3}{2}u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4}\frac{\partial^{3}u}{\partial x^{3}}\right).

(12)

This equation is called the
\hbar
-KP equation. When
\hbar\rightarrow 1
, it become the KP equation, and when
\hbar\rightarrow 0
, it become the dispersionless KP equation.

4
The Hirota equation and vertex operators

In this section, we will show that the
\hbar
-dependent KP hierarchy is exactly the integrable hierarchy defined in (
78
). The calculation is similar to that of KP hierarchy in
[
4
]
.

Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots)
and
y=(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots)
. The Hirota derivative
D_{x_{k}}^{j}
is defined to be
[
4
]

D_{x_{k}}^{j}f(x)\cdot g(x)=\partial_{y_{k}}^{j}f(x+y)g(x-y)|_{y=0}

(84)

The
\hbar
-KP equation (
12
) becomes the following Hirota equation

\hbar^{2}D_{x_{1}}^{4}\tau(x)\cdot\tau(x)-4D_{x_{1}}D_{x_{3}}\tau(x)\cdot\tau(x)+3D_{x_{2}}^{2}\tau(x)\cdot\tau(x)=0.

(85)

Set

P(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})=\hbar^{2}k_{1}^{4}+3k_{2}^{2}-4k_{1}k_{3}.

The solutions of the equation
P(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})=0
are

(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})=(\frac{1}{\hbar}p-\frac{1}{\hbar}q,\frac{1}{\hbar}p^{2}-\frac{1}{\hbar}q^{2},\frac{1}{\hbar}p^{3}-\frac{1}{\hbar}q^{3})

for any
z_{1},z_{2}
. Take two solutions

\displaystyle(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\frac{1}{\hbar}p_{1}-\frac{1}{\hbar}q_{1},\frac{1}{\hbar}p_{1}^{2}-\frac{1}{\hbar}q_{1}^{2},\frac{1}{\hbar}p_{1}^{3}-\frac{1}{\hbar}q_{1}^{3}),

\displaystyle(k_{1}^{\prime},k_{2}^{\prime},k_{3}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\frac{1}{\hbar}p_{2}-\frac{1}{\hbar}q_{2},\frac{1}{\hbar}p_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{\hbar}q_{2}^{2},\frac{1}{\hbar}p_{2}^{3}-\frac{1}{\hbar}q_{2}^{3}),

we have

-\frac{P(k_{1}-k_{1}^{\prime},k_{2}-k_{2}^{\prime},k_{3}-k_{3}^{\prime})}{P(k_{1}+k_{1}^{\prime},k_{2}+k_{2}^{\prime},k_{3}+k_{3}^{\prime})}=\frac{(p_{1}-p_{2})(q_{1}-q_{2})}{(p_{1}-q_{2})(q_{1}-p_{2})}.

Suppose

\displaystyle\xi_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(p_{i}^{j}-q_{i}^{j})\frac{x_{j}}{\hbar},

\displaystyle a_{ii^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(p_{i}-p_{i^{\prime}})(q_{i}-q_{i^{\prime}})}{(p_{i}-q_{i^{\prime}})(q_{i}-p_{i^{\prime}})},

then for
I=\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
,

\tau=\sum_{J\subset I}\left(\prod_{i\in J}c_{i}\right)\left(\prod_{i,i^{\prime}\in J,i<i^{\prime}}a_{ii^{\prime}}\right)\exp\left(\sum_{i\in J}\xi_{i}\right)

(86)

gives the
n
-soliton solution of
\hbar
-KP hierarchy. Introduce
the vertex operator

X(p,q)=\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(p^{j}-q^{j})\frac{x_{j}}{\hbar}\right)\exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\hbar}{j}(p^{-j}-q^{-j})\partial_{x_{j}}\right),

(87)

then the
n
-soliton solution
\tau
above can be written as

\tau=e^{c_{1}X(p_{1},q_{1})}\cdots e^{c_{n}X(p_{n},q_{n})}\cdot 1.

(88)

The tau functions satisfy the following bilinear identity. For any
x
and
x^{\prime}
, let

\xi=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{x_{j}}{\hbar}z^{j},\ \ \xi^{\prime}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{x_{j}^{\prime}}{\hbar}z^{j}.

the bilinear identity holds:

\oint\frac{dz}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}e^{\xi-\xi^{\prime}}\tau(x-\hbar[z^{-1}])\tau(x^{\prime}+\hbar[z^{-1}])=0.

(89)

w^{*}(x,z)=\frac{\tau(x+\hbar[z^{-1}])}{\tau(x)}e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{x_{j}}{\hbar}z^{j}},

(90)

it has the following form

w^{*}(x,z)=e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{x_{j}}{\hbar}z^{j}}\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{w_{j}^{*}}{z^{j}}\right).

Then the bilinear identity (
89

\oint\frac{dz}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}w(x,z)w^{*}(x^{\prime},z)=0.

(91)

From this relation, the linear system (
7
) can be obtained. Let

Q=\hbar\partial_{x_{j}}-(L^{j})_{+},\ \ \tilde{w}(x,k)=Qw(x,k).

Then
\tilde{w}(x,k)
has the following form

\tilde{w}(x,k)=e^{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{x_{j}}{\hbar}z^{j}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\tilde{w}_{j}}{z^{j}}\right)

and satisfy

\oint\frac{dz}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}\tilde{w}(x,z)w^{*}(x^{\prime},z)=0,

which show
\tilde{w}_{j}=0
for
j=1,2,\cdots
. Therefore,
Qw(x,z)=0
, that is (
7
) is obtained.

and
\alpha=\hbar^{2}

\displaystyle c_{(2,2)}c_{\emptyset}-c_{(2,1)}c_{(1)}+c_{(2)}c_{(1,1)}=0.

(95)

In fact, the coefficients
c_{\lambda}
satisfy the classical Plücker relations since
S_{\lambda}(\hbar\partial_{p})S_{\mu}\left(\frac{p}{\hbar}\right)|_{p=0}=\delta_{\lambda\mu}
, which is the same with that in
[
18
]
. For
c_{\lambda}^{\prime}
, since

\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(\partial_{p})\tilde{J}_{\mu}(p)|_{p=0}=\delta_{\lambda\mu}\langle\tilde{J}_{\lambda},\tilde{J}_{\lambda}\rangle,

we obtain the relations of
c_{\lambda}^{\prime}
. For example,

\displaystyle\frac{4}{(1+\alpha)^{2}}c_{(2,2)}^{\prime}c_{\emptyset}^{\prime}+\frac{6(\alpha-1)}{(2+\alpha)(3+\alpha)}c_{(2,1,1)}^{\prime}c_{\emptyset}^{\prime}+\frac{2(\alpha-1)\alpha}{(2+\alpha)(3+\alpha)}c_{(1^{4})}^{\prime}c_{\emptyset}^{\prime}-\frac{2(\alpha-1)}{2+\alpha}c_{(1^{3})}^{\prime}c_{(1)}^{\prime}

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{1+\alpha}c_{(2,1)}^{\prime}c_{(1)}^{\prime}+\frac{2}{(1+\alpha)}c_{(2)}^{\prime}c_{(1^{2})}^{\prime}+\frac{(\alpha-1)}{1+\alpha}c_{(1^{2})}^{\prime}c_{(1^{2})}^{\prime}=0.

(96)

The set of coefficients
\{c_{\lambda}\}
can be represented linearly by the set
\{c_{\lambda}^{\prime}\}
and vice versa. In fact,
\{c_{\lambda}\}
satisfies the classical Plücker relations if and only if
\{c_{\lambda}^{\prime}\}
satisfies its Plücker realtions. For example,
\{c_{\lambda}\}
satisfies (

) if and only if
\{c_{\lambda}^{\prime}\}
satisfies (

).

5
The Boson-Fermion correspondence for
\hbar
-KP hierarchy

Let
p_{n}=nx_{n}
. For Schur functions
S_{\lambda}(x)
and Jack polynomials
\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(x)
, we have

\displaystyle e^{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}z^{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}S_{(n)}(x)z^{n},

(97)

\displaystyle e^{-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}z^{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}S_{(1^{n})}(x)z^{n}.

(98)

Then we have

\displaystyle\exp\left({\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{x_{n}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}z^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}S_{(n)}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\right)z^{n}=\frac{1}{\langle\tilde{J}_{(n)},\tilde{J}_{(n)}\rangle\sqrt{\alpha}^{n}}\tilde{J}_{(n)}(x)z^{n},

(99)

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\sum_{n\geq 1}x_{n}\sqrt{\alpha}z^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}S_{1^{n}}(\sqrt{\alpha}x)z^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}\sqrt{\alpha}^{n}}{\langle\tilde{J}_{(1^{n})},\tilde{J}_{(1^{n})}\rangle}\tilde{J}_{(1^{n})}(p)z^{n}.

(100)

The Cauchy formula is

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{p_{n}p_{n}^{\prime}}{n}\right)=\exp(\sum_{n\geq 1}nx_{n}x_{n}^{\prime})

(101)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda}S_{\lambda}(x)S_{\lambda}(x^{\prime})=\sum_{\lambda}\frac{1}{\langle\tilde{J}_{\lambda},\tilde{J}_{\lambda}\rangle}\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(x)\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(x^{\prime}).

Note that
\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(x)
is not equal to
S_{\lambda}(x)
(or multiplied by a constant).

The Fermions
\psi_{j}
and
\psi_{j}^{*}
are defined as usual. For
j\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}
,
\psi_{j}
and
\psi_{j}^{*}
satisfy
[
2
]

[\psi_{i},\psi_{j}]_{+}=0,\ [\psi_{i}^{*},\psi_{j}^{*}]=0,\ [\psi_{i},\psi_{j}^{*}]=\delta_{i+j,0},

(102)

where
[A,B]_{+}=AB-BA
. Particularly,

\psi_{j}^{2}=0,\ \ \psi_{j}^{*2}=0.

The Fermionic Fock space
\mathcal{F}
is the space of Maya diagrams
[
2
]
. A Maya diagram can be discribed as an increasing sequence of half-integers

|{\bf u}\rangle=|u_{1},u_{2},\cdots\rangle,\ \ \text{with}\ \ u_{1}<u_{2}<\cdots,

and
u_{j+1}=u_{j}+1
for all sufficiently large
j
.

The actions of Fermions
\psi_{j},\psi^{*}_{j}
on Maya diagrams are determined by

\displaystyle\psi_{j}|{\bf u}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(-1)^{i-1}|\cdots,u_{i-1},u_{i+1},\cdots\rangle&\text{ if }u_{i}=-j\ \text{for some}\ i,\\
0&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

(103)

\displaystyle\psi^{*}_{j}|{\bf u}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(-1)^{i}|\cdots,u_{i},j,u_{i+1},\cdots\rangle&\text{ if }u_{i}<j<u_{i+1}\ \text{for some}\ i,\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

(104)

The generating functions of Fermions are

\psi(z)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}+1/2}\psi_{j}z^{-j-1/2},\ \psi^{*}(z)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}+1/2}\psi^{*}_{j}z^{-j-1/2}.

The normal order is defined as usual. For Maya diagrams
|{\bf u}\rangle
and
|{\bf v}\rangle
, the pair
\langle{\bf v}|{\bf u}\rangle
is defined by the formula

\langle{\bf v}|{\bf u}\rangle=\delta_{v_{1}+u_{1},0}\delta_{v_{2}+u_{2},0}\cdots.

Let

H_{n}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}+1/2}:\psi_{-j}\psi_{j+n}^{*}:.

(105)

It satisfy
[
2
]

[H_{n},\psi_{j}]=\psi_{n+j},\ [H_{n},\psi_{j}^{*}]=-\psi_{n+j}^{*},

and

[H_{n},H_{m}]=n\delta_{n+m,0}.

Then we show the Boson-Fermion correspondence in the
\hbar
-KP hierarchy. Define

H(x)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{x_{n}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}H_{n}.

(106)

For any element
|{\bf{u}}\rangle\in\mathcal{F}
, define the map

\Phi(|u\rangle)=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}z^{l}\langle l|e^{H(x)}|{\bf u}\rangle.

(107)

Then
\Phi(z)
is in the space
\mathbb{C}(\alpha)[z,z^{-1},x_{1},x_{2},\cdots]
, and the correspondence
\Phi
is an isomorphism of the vector spaces
\mathcal{F}
over
\mathbb{C}(\alpha)
and
\mathbb{C}(\alpha)[z,z^{-1},t_{1},t_{2},\cdots]
. Moreover, for
n>0
,

\Phi(H_{n}|{\bf u}\rangle)=\sqrt{\alpha}\partial_{x_{n}}\Phi(|{\bf u}\rangle),\ \ \Phi(H_{-n}|{\bf u}\rangle)=n\frac{x_{n}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\Phi(|{\bf u}\rangle).

(108)

From the commutation relations above, we get

\displaystyle[H(x),\psi(z)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{x_{n}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}z^{n}\right)\psi(z),

(109)

and

[H(x),\psi^{*}(z)]=\left(-\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{x_{n}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}z^{n}\right)\psi^{*}(z),

(110)

which show

\displaystyle e^{H(x)}\psi(z)e^{-H(x)}=e^{\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{x_{n}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}z^{n}}\psi(z),

(111)

\displaystyle e^{H(x)}\psi(z)^{*}e^{-H(x)}=e^{-\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{x_{n}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}z^{n}}\psi^{*}(z).

(112)

Then

\displaystyle e^{H(x)}\psi_{j}e^{-H(x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_{j+n}S_{(n)}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\right),

(113)

\displaystyle e^{H(x)}\psi_{j}^{*}e^{-H(x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_{j+n}S_{(n)}\left(-\frac{x}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\right).

(114)

From these formulas, we polynomials
\Phi(|{\bf u}\rangle)
can be determined. For example, let
|{\bf u}\rangle=\psi_{-5/2}|\text{vac}\rangle
,

\displaystyle\Phi(\psi_{-5/2}|\text{vac}\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z\langle 1|e^{H(x)}\psi_{-5/2}|\text{vac}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z\langle\text{vac}|\psi_{1/2}^{*}e^{H(x)}\psi_{-5/2}e^{-H(x)}|\text{vac}\rangle

\displaystyle zS_{(2)}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z\frac{1}{\langle\tilde{J}_{(2)},\tilde{J}_{(2)}\rangle\sqrt{\alpha}^{2}}\tilde{J}_{(2)}(x).

Let the operators
z^{H_{0}}
and
e^{K}
are defined the same as that in

]

(115)

\displaystyle=

(116)

They give the realization of the Fermioinic generating functions

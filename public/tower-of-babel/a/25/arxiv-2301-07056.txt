Abstract
. Given a set of distinct points X = { P 1 , … , P r } X=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{r}\} in ℙ 𝐤 n , \mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathbf{k}}}, in this paper we characterize being X X arithmetically Gorenstein through the “special” structure of the inverse system of the defining ideal I ⁡ ( X ) ⊆ R = 𝐤 ⁡ [ X 0 , … , X n ] . I(X)\subseteq R={\mathbf{k}}[X_{0},\dots,X_{n}]. We describe the corresponding Zariski locally closed subset of ( ℙ 𝐤 n ) r \mathbb{(}\mathbb{P}_{{\mathbf{k}}}^{n})^{r} parametrized by the coordinates of the points. Several examples are given to show the effectiveness of the results. As a consequence of the main result we characterize the Artinian Gorenstein rings which are the Artinian reduction of Gorenstein points. The problem is of interest in several areas of research, among others the G G -linkage, the Waring decomposition, the smoothability of Artinian algebras and the identifiability of symmetric tensors.

Introduction

Let
X=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{r}\}
be a set of non degenerate distinct points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathbf{k}}}
where
{\mathbf{k}}
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.
The defining ideal
I(X)\subseteq R={\mathbf{k}}{}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]
is saturated and
R/I(X)
is a Cohen-Macaulay one-dimensional standard graded
{\mathbf{k}}
-algebra.
Little is known how to obtain a reduced zero-dimensional scheme
X
which is arithmetically Gorenstein, especially if
X
is not a complete intersection.
Many authors give geometric constructions of some particular families of arithmetically Gorenstein schemes (e.g.
[
2
]

[
3
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
39
]
,
[

]
,
[

Notice that a set of
r

generic

.
Recall that was noted by Eisenbud and Popescu (
[
14

X
of
2n+2
points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathbf{k}}}
is arithmetically Gorenstein if and only if they are self-associated. This is the case of an hyperplane sections of canonically embedded curves.
In general, a geometric
or algebraic description of arithmetically Gorenstein reduced schemes is not understood.
For instance this lack is one of the main obstacles in the Gorenstein liaison (or
G
-linkage) of zero-dimensional schemes.
Recall that two schemes X and Y in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathbf{k}}}

[

[

We can say that our main purpose would be to “construct” Gorenstein points through the structure of Macaulay’s inverse system, rather than to prove that a given set of points is arithmetically Gorenstein.

As a continuation of
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
, taking advantage of the knowledge on the inverse system of one-dimensional Gorenstein
{\mathbf{k}}
-algebras, in this paper we describe Macaulay’s inverse system of Gorenstein points in terms of their coordinates, see Theorem
3.14
.
We will prove, by analogy with the Artinian case, that the inverse system of Gorenstein points can be characterized in terms of a suitable homogeneous polynomial, despite the fact that
I(X)^{\perp}
is a not finitely generated
R
-module.
It is clear that for being Gorenstein the points must be somehow “special” and we describe the corresponding Zariski locally closed subset (eventually empty) of
(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathbf{k}}}^{n})^{r}
parametrizing the coordinates of the points, see Proposition
4.3
and Remark

Beside the
G
-linkage, the problem we face in this paper is also related to the study of the Waring problem and to the identifiability of symmetric tensors which plays a crucial role in Algebraic Statistic (see

This because the Artinian reduction of

The inverse system of any Artinian reduction of

By Matlis duality the dual module is generated by a homogeneous form

.
This is the connection with the celebrated Big Waring Problem and though we will not discuss here about this topic, see for instance
[

]
and
[
32
]
. Inverse systems of Artinian graded rings coming from points are put to great use also in the theory of splines approximation (e.g.

[

2.
Preliminaries

Let
V
be a
{\mathbf{k}}
-vector space of dimension
n

with basis
\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle,
then we denote by
V^{*}=\langle y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\rangle
the dual basis.
Let
R=Sym_{\cdot}^{{\mathbf{k}}}V=\oplus_{i\geq 0}Sym_{i}^{{\mathbf{k}}}V
be the standard graded polynomial ring in
n
variables over
{\mathbf{k}}
and
\Gamma=D_{\bf\cdot}^{{\mathbf{k}}}(V^{*})=\oplus_{i\geq 0}D_{i}^{{\mathbf{k}}}(V^{*})=\oplus_{i\geq 0}{\rm Hom}_{{\mathbf{k}}}(R_{i},{\mathbf{k}})
be the graded
R
-module of graded
{\mathbf{k}}
-linear homomorphisms from
R
to
{\mathbf{k}},
hence

\Gamma\simeq{\mathbf{k}}_{DP}[y_{1},\dots,y_{n}]
the divided power ring.

We recall that
\Gamma
is an
R
-module with
R
acting on
\Gamma
by
derivation
denoted by
\circ
. We will think the elements of
R
as representing partial differential operators and
the polynomials of
\Gamma
as the “real” polynomials on which the differential operators act.
This action is sometimes called the “apolarity” action of
R
on
\Gamma
. We begin with a precise
definition of this action by

\begin{array}[]{ cccc}\circ:&R\times\Gamma&\longrightarrow&\Gamma\\
&(f,g)&\to&f\circ g=f(\partial_{y_{1}},\dots,\partial_{y_{n}})(g)\end{array}

where
\partial_{y_{i}}
denotes the partial derivative with respect to
y_{i}.
This action can be defined bilinearly from that for monomials.
If we denote by
x^{\alpha}=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
then

x^{\alpha}\circ y^{\beta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\beta!}{(\beta-\alpha)!}\;y^{\beta-\alpha}&\text{ if }\beta_{i}\geq\alpha_{i}\text{ for }i=1,\dots,n\\
\\
0&\text{ otherwise}\end{array}\right.

\frac{\beta!}{(\beta-\alpha)!}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\beta_{i}!}{(\beta_{i}-\alpha_{i})!}
.
Notice that the apolarity action induces a non-singular
{\mathbf{k}}
-bilinear pairing:

\begin{array}[]{ cccc}\circ:&R_{j}\times\Gamma_{j}&\longrightarrow&{\mathbf{k}}\end{array}

for every
j\geq 0.

Recall that the injective hull
E_{R}({\mathbf{k}})
of
{\mathbf{k}}
as an
R
-module is isomorphic as an
R
-module to the divided power ring
\Gamma
(see
[
21
]
,
[
42
]
).
For detailed information see also
[
12
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
32
, Appendix A]
.
If
I\subset R
is an ideal of
R,
then
(R/I)^{\vee}={\rm Hom}_{R}(R/I,E_{R}({\mathbf{k}}))
is the
R
-submodule of
\Gamma

{I^{\perp}}=\{F\in\Gamma\ |\ I\circ F=0\ \}.

This submodule of
\Gamma
is called
Macaulay’s inverse system of
I
.

Given an
R
-submodule
W
of
\Gamma,
then the dual
W^{\vee}={\rm Hom}_{R}(W,E_{R}({\mathbf{k}}))
is the ring
R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(W)
where

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(W)=\{g\in R\ \mid\ g\circ F=0\ \mbox{ for \ all \ }F\in W\}

is an ideal of
R
.

If
I
is a homogeneous ideal of
R
(resp.
W
is generated by homogeneous polynomials of
\Gamma
), then
I^{\perp}
is generated by homogeneous polynomials of
\Gamma
(resp.
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(W)
is an homogeneous ideal of
R
) and

I^{\perp}=\oplus I_{j}^{\perp}
where
I_{j}^{\perp}=\{F\in\Gamma_{j}\mid\ g\circ F=0\ \ \mbox{ for \ all \ }g\in I_{j}\}
, i.e.
(I^{\perp})_{j}=I_{j}^{\perp}
, see
[
22
]
, Proposition 2.5.
Notice that, in particular, the Hilbert function of
R/I
can be computed by
I^{\perp},
see for instance
[
16
, Section 2]
.

Macaulay in
[
38
, IV]
proved a particular case of Matlis duality, called Macaulay’s correspondence,
between the
ideals
I\subseteq R
such that
R/I
is an Artinian local ring and
R
-submodules
W=I^{\perp}
of
\Gamma
which are finitely generated.
Macaulay’s correspondence is an effective method for computing Gorenstein Artinian rings, see
[
6
]
, Section 1,
[
31
]
,
[
22
]
and
[
32
]
. Macaulay proved that Artinian Gorenstein
{\mathbf{k}}
-algebras
A=R/I
of socle degree
s
correspond to cyclic
R
-submodules of
\Gamma
generated by a polynomial
F\neq 0
of degree
s
.
See
[
18
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
32
]
for more details on inverse systems.

The authors extended Macaulay’s correspondence characterizing the
d
-dimensional local Gorenstein
{\mathbf{k}}
-algebras in terms of suitable submodules of
\Gamma
, see
[
16
]
. This result in the one-dimensional case will be our starting point and for completeness we include here the statement.

In the following given a family
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{j};j\in J\}
of elements of
\Gamma
we denote by

\langle F_{j};j\in J\rangle
(resp.
\langle F_{j};j\in J\rangle_{{\mathbf{k}}}
) the
R
-submodule (resp.
{\mathbf{k}}
-vector subspace) of
\Gamma
generated by
\mathcal{F}.

Definition 2.1
.

An
R
-submodule
M
of
\Gamma
is called
G
-admissible if it admits a countable system of generators
\{F_{l}\}_{l\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
satisfying the following conditions

(1)

There exists a linear form
z\in R
such that for all
l\in\mathbb{N}_{+}

z\circ F_{l}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}F_{l-1}&\text{ if }l>1\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

(2)

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\langle F_{l}\rangle)\circ F_{l+1}=\langle F_{1}\rangle
for all
l\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
.

If this is the case, we say that
M=\langle F_{l},l\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\rangle
is a

G
-admissible
R
-submodule of
\Gamma
with respect to the linear form
z\in R
.

We present the main result of
[
16
]
in the one-dimensional case of interest in this paper.

Theorem 2.2
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence
\mathcal{C}
between the following sets:

(i)
one-dimensional Gorenstein
{\mathbf{k}}
-algebras
A=R/I
,

(ii) non-zero
G
-admissible
R
-submodules
M=\langle F_{l},l\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\rangle
of
\Gamma
.

In particular, given an ideal
I\subset R
with
A=R/I
satisfying
(i)
and
z
a linear regular element modulo
I,
then

\mathcal{C}(A)=I^{\perp}=\langle F_{l},l\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\rangle\subset\Gamma\ \ {\text{with}}\ \ \langle F_{l}\rangle=(I+(z^{l}))^{\perp}

is
G
-admissible.
Conversely, given an
R
-submodule
M
of
\Gamma
satisfying (ii), then

\mathcal{C}^{-1}(M)=R/I\ \ {\text{with}}\ \ I=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(M)=\bigcap_{l\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\langle F_{l}\rangle).

In the next result we prove that the condition
(1)
of the Definition
2.1
suffices for a system of generators of a Gorenstein ring to be
G
-admissible.
This result holds for arbitrary dimension, we give here the proof in the one-dimensional case.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
A=R/I
be a one-dimensional Gorenstein ring with inverse system

I^{\perp}
containing a set
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{l}\}_{l\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
.
Let
z
be a non-zero divisor of
A
.
If
\mathcal{F}
satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

for all
l\in\mathbb{N}_{+}

z\circ F_{l}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}F_{l-1}&\text{ if }l>1\\
0&\text{ otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

(2)

\operatorname{deg}(F_{1})
agrees with the socle degree of
A.

then there exist non-zero elements

\alpha_{l}\in{\mathbf{k}}^{*}
,
l\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, such that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\{\frac{1}{\alpha_{l}}F_{l};l\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\}
is
G
-admissible with respect to
z
; in particular
\mathcal{F}
( and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
) is a system of generators of
I^{\perp}
.

Proof.

Let
\{H_{l}\}_{l\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
be a
G
-admissible system of generators of
\mathcal{C}(A)
with respect to
z
in Theorem
2.2
above.
Since
\operatorname{deg}(H_{1})
agrees with the socle degree of
A
, from
(2)
we get
\operatorname{deg}(F_{1})=\operatorname{deg}(H_{1})
.
Furthermore, again by
(1)
we deduce that

\operatorname{deg}(F_{l})=\operatorname{deg}(H_{l})
for all
l\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
.
On the other hand, since
\operatorname{Ann}(H_{l})=I+(z^{l})\subset\operatorname{Ann}{F_{l}}
we deduce
F_{l}\in\langle H_{l}\rangle
.
From
\operatorname{deg}(F_{l})=\operatorname{deg}(H_{l})
we deduce that
there is
\alpha_{l}\in{\mathbf{k}}
such that
F_{l}=\alpha_{l}H_{l}
.
If
\alpha_{l}=0
then by
(1)
we get
F_{1}=0
, this is not possible because
\operatorname{deg}(F_{1})
is the socle degree of
A
. Hence
H_{l}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{l}}F_{l}
and from this we get the claim.
∎

3.
The inverse system of Gorenstein points

Given a set of distinct points
X=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{r}\}
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathbf{k}}}
not contained in a hyperplane, consider the corresponding defining ideal
I(X)\subseteq R={\mathbf{k}}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}].

Recall that
R/I(X)
is a Cohen-Macaulay one-dimensional graded ring and
I(X)^{\perp}
as graded
R
-submodule of
\Gamma={\mathbf{k}}[y_{0},\dots,y_{n}]
is not finitely generated. We have
(I(X)^{\perp})_{j}=(I(X)_{j})^{\perp}
for every
j\geq 0
and

{\operatorname{H\!F}}_{X}(j)=\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbf{k}}}(R_{j}/I(X)_{j})=\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbf{k}}}(I(X)^{\perp})_{j},

see
[
22
, Proposition 2.5]
where we denote by
{\operatorname{H\!F}}_{X}
the Hilbert Function of
R/I(X).
Let
z
be a linear form in
R
such that
z(P_{i})\neq 0
for every
i=1,\dots,r,
that is
z
is a not zero divisor in
R/I(X).
Then we will say that
R/I+(z)
is
an Artinian reduction of
X
.
With this,
h_{t}=\Delta{\operatorname{H\!F}}_{X}(t)={\operatorname{H\!F}}_{X}(t)-{\operatorname{H\!F}}_{X}(t-1)
is the Hilbert Function in degree
t
of any Artinian reduction of
X.
In particular
h_{t}=0
for every
t>s
where
s
is called
the socle degree
of
R/I(X).
We recall that
s
equals also the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of
R/I(X).
The vector
(h_{0},\dots,h_{s})
is
the
h
-vector of
R/I(X)
or, for short,
the
h
-vector of
X.
Recall that
\sum_{i=0}^{s}h_{i}=r
the number of points or equivalently the degree of
R/I(X).

Recall that
R/I(X)
is Gorenstein iff it is Cohen-Macaulay and the last (free) nonzero module in its minimal graded resolution is of rank
1
.
It is known that if
X
is arithmetically Gorenstein (that is
R/I(X)
is a Gorenstein ring), then the
h
-vector of
R/I(X)
is symmetric, but the symmetry of the
h
-vector is only a necessary condition for being Gorenstein.

Example 3.1
.

Let us consider
X=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{5}\}
where
P_{1}=[1,0,0,1]
,
P_{2}=[0,1,0,1]
,
P_{3}=[0,0,1,1]
,
P_{4}=[0,0,0,1]
,
P_{5}=[-1,0,0,1]
in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{{\mathbf{k}}}
.
The points do not lie on
x_{3}=0
and by an easy computation we see that the socle degree of
R/I(X)
is two. Then the scheme
X
is not arithmetically Gorenstein, even if the h-vector
(1,3,1)
is symmetric.

We recall here an important result due to Davis, Geramita and Orecchia (
[
9
]
) characterizing Gorenstein points in terms of the Hilbert function, via a Cayley-Bacharach condition.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
X=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{r}\}
be a set of
r
distinct points of
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathbf{k}}}
and let
s
be the socle degree of
R/I(X)
.
Then
X
is arithmetically Gorenstein if and only if:

(1)

\Delta{\operatorname{H\!F}}_{X}(t)=\Delta{\operatorname{H\!F}}_{X}(s-t)

for all
t\geq 0
,

(2)

{\operatorname{H\!F}}_{X}(t)={\operatorname{H\!F}}_{Y}(t)
for all
t=0,\dots,s-1
and for all subset
Y
of
r-1
points of
X
.

In this paper we are mainly interested in the construction of Gorenstein points, rather than verifying that a given a set of points is Gorenstein. We hope to achieve this aim through an effective characterization of the inverse system.

Given a point
P=[a_{0},\dots,a_{n}]\in\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathbf{k}}}
we define the dual linear form
L:=a_{0}y_{0}+\cdots+a_{n}y_{n}
in
\Gamma
and we say that
L

is the linear form associated to

P.

We present a well known result concerning the inverse system of the ideal of any set of points, see
[
20
, Theorems IIA and IIB]
,
[
22
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
X=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{r}\}
be a set of distinct points of
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathbf{k}}}
and let
L_{1},\dots,L_{r}
be the corresponding associated linear forms.
Then for all
j\geq 0

(I(X)^{\perp})_{j}=\langle L_{1}^{j},\dots,L_{r}^{j}\rangle_{{\mathbf{k}}}.

It is clear that for being Gorenstein the number of points has to be compatible with a symmetric
h
-vector. But this is only a necessary condition. If we consider
n+2
generic points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathbf{k}}},
(
h
-vector
(1,n,1)
), then the defining ideal is always Gorenstein, but this is the only case where generic points work. Next case of interest is a set
X
of
2n+2
points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathbf{k}}}
with
h
-vector
(1,n,n,1)
. To construct
2n+2
Gorenstein points, a first naive idea could be to add a suitable point to a set
Y
of
2n+1
generic points. Unfortunately this idea does not work if
n>3.
Let
P\in\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathbf{k}}}
a point and let
X=Y\cup\{P\}
where
Y
consistes of
2n+1
generic points. It is known that
{\operatorname{H\!F}}_{Y}(2)=2n+1
and
I(Y)
is generated by quadrics, as we explain later. Now since
I(X)\subsetneq I(Y),
then necessarily
{\operatorname{H\!F}}_{X}(2)=2n+2>{\operatorname{H\!F}}_{Y}(2)=2n+1
and hence the
h
-vector of
X
is
(1,n,n+1),
not symmetric. In particular
X
is not aritmetically Gorenstein.
We explain why
I(Y)
is generated by quadrics. This was expected by the “Ideal Generation Conjecture” stated by Geramita and Orecchia
[
24
]
and Lorenzini
[
37
]
.
They proved that there exists a Zariski open set where the conjecture holds true.
Hence for proving the conjecture it is enough to prove that this open set is not empty.
This conjecture is still open in general, but it holds true for
2n+1
generic points. This follows by Corollary 5.1. in
[
26
]
for all integers
n\geq 7.
For
n=4,5,6
it can be verified by a straightforward computation, for instance by
[
27
]
.

Recall that it was noted by Eisenbud and Popescu (
[
14
]
, Theorem 7.3) that a set
X
of
2n+2
points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathbf{k}}}
being arithmetically Gorenstein is equivalent to be self-associated, that is any quadric containing all but one of the points of
X
must also contain the remaining point. Self-associated sets of points arise naturally as hyperplane sections of canonically embedded curves (
[
11
]
Ex. 5, p. 47). More
precisely, let
C\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{g-1}_{{\mathbf{k}}}
be the canonical embedding of a non-bielliptic curve
C
of
genus
g
, and let
X=C\cap H
be any transverse hyperplane section. By the Riemann-
Roch theorem,
X
fails by 1 to impose independent conditions on quadrics, while any
proper subset of
X
imposes independent conditions. Thus,
X
is a self-associated set of points
in
\mathbb{P}^{g-1}_{{\mathbf{k}}}.

See also
[
7
]
Theorem 5.3 for a discussion on the topic.

Combining Proposition

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
X=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{r}\}
be a set of distinct points of
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathbf{k}}}
of socle degree
s
, and let
L_{1},\dots,L_{r}
be the corresponding associated linear forms.
For all
i=1,\dots,r
, there exists
an homogeneous form
\sigma
of degree
s
such that

(1)

\sigma\circ L_{i}^{t}\neq 0
for all
t\geq s
, and

(2)

\sigma\circ L_{j}^{t}=0
for all
t\geq 1
and
j\neq i
.

Proof.

We may assume that
i=1
.
We write
Y=\{P_{2},\dots,P_{r}\}
.
Since
Y\subsetneq X
we have that

{\operatorname{H\!F}}_{Y}(s)<{\operatorname{H\!F}}_{X}(s)=r.

This implies that there exist

\sigma\in I(Y)_{s}\setminus I(X)_{s}
, so

\sigma(P_{1})\neq 0
and
\sigma(P_{j})=0
for all
j\geq 2
.

From
\sigma\in I(P_{j})
for
j\geq 2
we deduce that

\sigma\circ L_{j}^{t}=0
for all
t\geq 1
and
j\geq 2
(by Proposition
3.3
).

Since
\sigma\notin I(P_{1})
there exist an integer
t_{0}
such that
\sigma\circ L_{1}^{t_{0}}\neq 0
.
Assume
\sigma\circ L_{1}^{t_{0}+m}=0
for some integer
m\geq 0
and
let
l\in\{0,...,n\}
be such that
x_{l}\circ L_{1}=c\neq 0
(this means that the
l
-th homogeneous coordinate of the point
P_{1}
is nonzero), then

[c^{m}(t_{0}+m)\cdots(t_{0}+1)](\sigma\circ L_{1}^{t_{0}})=\sigma\circ(x_{l}^{m}\circ L_{1}^{t_{0}+m})=x_{l}^{m}\circ(\sigma\circ L_{1}^{t_{0}+m})=x_{l}^{m}\circ 0=0,

so
\sigma\circ L_{1}^{t_{0}}=0
; contradiction.
Hence
\sigma\circ L_{1}^{t}\neq 0
for all
t\geq t_{0}
.
Take
t_{0}
to be the minimum with the above property. Obviously
t_{0}\geq s,
since otherwise
\sigma\circ L_{1}^{t_{0}}=0,
as deg
(\sigma)=s.

Assume
t_{0}>s
. Then,
\sigma\circ L_{1}^{t_{0}}
is a non-constant homogeneous polynomial in
\Gamma
and so, at least one of its first partial derivatives is nonzero. So there exists
l\in\{0,...,n\}
such that
x_{l}\circ(\sigma\circ L_{1}^{t_{0}})\neq 0.
Then

0\neq x_{l}\circ(\sigma\circ L_{1}^{t_{0}})=\sigma\circ(x_{l}\circ L_{1}^{t_{0}})=\sigma\circ(t_{0}a_{l}L_{1}^{t_{0}-1})=t_{0}a_{l}(\sigma\circ L_{1}^{t_{0}-1})

where
L_{1}=a_{0}y_{0}+\dots+{a_{n}}y_{n}
.
Hence
\sigma\circ L_{1}^{t_{0}-1}\neq 0,
a contradiction with the minimality of
t_{0}.
So
t_{0}=s
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{r}\}
be a set of distinct points of
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathbf{k}}}
of socle degree
s
and let
L_{1},\dots,L_{r}
be the associated linear forms.
Given an integer
u\geq 1
and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\in{\mathbf{k}}^{*}
let us consider a family of polynomials

F_{j}=\frac{1}{(j+u)!}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}L_{i}^{j+u}
,
j\geq 1
.
For all integers
w\geq 1
,
i=1,\dots,r

and
t\geq\operatorname{max}\{s,s+w-u\}
there exist a homogeneous form
\beta_{i}\in R
of degree
t+u-w
such that

L_{i}^{w}=\beta_{i}\circ F_{t}

Proof.

Fix an integer
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
and consider a homogeneous form
\sigma\in R
of degree
s
under the hypothesis of Lemma
3.4
.
If
t\geq s,
then

\sigma\circ F_{t}=\sigma\circ\left(\frac{1}{(t+u)!}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}L_{i}^{t+u}\right)=\frac{1}{(t+u)!}\alpha_{i}(\sigma\circ L_{i}^{t+u}).

Since
\sigma\circ L_{i}^{t+u}
is a degree
t+u-s\geq 1
non-zero homogeneous element of
I(P_{i})^{\perp}
there exist
c_{i}\in{\mathbf{k}}^{*}

such that

\sigma\circ L_{i}^{t+u}=c_{i}L_{i}^{t+u-s}.

By hypothesis
\alpha_{i}\neq 0,
so

L_{i}^{t+u-s}=\frac{(t+u)!}{\alpha_{i}c_{i}}\sigma\circ F_{t}.

Given an integer
w\geq 1
we consider an integer
t
such that
t\geq s
and
t+u-s\geq w
.
Let
l
be an integer such that
x_{l}\circ L_{i}\neq 0
(the point
P_{i}
has
l
-th homogeneous coordinate
\neq 0
). So there is
c\in{\mathbf{k}}^{*}
such that

L_{i}^{w}=cx_{l}^{t+u-s-w}\circ L_{i}^{t+u-s}.

From the last identity we get

L_{i}^{w}=cx_{l}^{t+u-s-w}\circ L_{i}^{t+u-s}=\left(\frac{c(t+u)!}{\alpha_{i}c_{i}}x_{l}^{t+u-s-w}\sigma\right)\circ F_{t},

and we take

\beta_{i}=\frac{c(t+u)!}{\alpha_{i}c_{i}}x_{l}^{t+u-s-w}\sigma.

∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
X=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{r}\}
be a set of distinct points of
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathbf{k}}}
and let
L_{1},\dots,L_{r}
be the associated linear forms.
Given

and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\in{\mathbf{k}}^{*}

\Gamma

(2)

Lemma 3.10
.

Let

be a set of distinct points of

\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathbf{k}}}\setminus\{x_{n}=0\}
.
Let
\{F_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}

satisfying the condition
(1)
of

G
-admissibility with respect to

Then for all

it holds

\langle F_{1}\rangle\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}(F_{1})\circ F_{2}\subseteq\dots\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}(F_{t})\circ F_{t+1}\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}(F_{t+1})\circ F_{t+2}\subseteq(I(X)+(x_{n}))^{\perp}

Proof.

Since
F_{1}=x_{n}\circ F_{2}
we get

F_{1}=x_{n}\circ F_{2}\in\operatorname{Ann}(F_{1})\circ F_{2}

so
\langle F_{1}\rangle\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}(F_{1})\circ F_{2}
.

For all

we have

(x_{n}\operatorname{Ann}(F_{i}))\circ F_{i+1}=\operatorname{Ann}(F_{i})\circ(x_{n}\circ F_{i+1})=\operatorname{Ann}(F_{i})\circ F_{i}=0.

Hence we obtain

\langle F_{1}\rangle\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}(F_{1})\circ F_{2}\subseteq\dots\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}(F_{t})\circ F_{t+1}\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}(F_{t+1})\circ F_{t+2}.

\operatorname{Ann}(F_{t+1})\circ F_{t+2}\subseteq I(X)^{\perp}
.
On the other hand

x_{n}\circ(\operatorname{Ann}(F_{t+1})\circ F_{t+2})=\operatorname{Ann}(F_{t+1})\circ(x_{n}\circ F_{t+2})=\operatorname{Ann}(F_{t+1})\circ F_{t+1}=0.

Hence

\operatorname{Ann}(F_{t+1})\circ F_{t+2}\subseteq(x_{n})^{\perp}
.
Recall that
(I(X)+(x_{n}))^{\perp}=I(X)^{\perp}\cap(x_{n})^{\perp}
, so

\operatorname{Ann}(F_{t+1})\circ F_{t+2}\subseteq(I(X)+(x_{n}))^{\perp}.

.

\mathcal{F}=\{F_{t};t\geq 1\}

\operatorname{Ann}(F_{t})\circ F_{t+1}=(I(X)+(x_{n}))^{\perp}.

Since
\mathcal{F}
satisfies the condition
(1)
of

-admissibility with respect to

(2)

Next result answers one of the main question of this paper. Notice that we characterize the inverse system of Gorenstein points (a not finitely generated
R
-module!) in terms of one single polynomial of
\Gamma
by analogy with the Artinian case. For this reason the result can be considered as a strengthening of
[
44
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
X=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{r}\}
be a set of points of
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathbf{k}}}\setminus\{x_{n}=0\}
of socle degree
s
and let
L_{1},\dots,L_{r}
be the associate linear forms.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

X
is arithmetically Gorenstein,

(2)

there exists a polynomial
F=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}L_{i}^{s}
with

\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\in{\mathbf{k}}
such that

(a)

\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}L_{i}^{s-1}=0
,

(b)

\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbf{k}}}\langle F\rangle\geq r.

If this is the case, then
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\in{\mathbf{k}}^{*}
are uniquely determined (up to scalars in
{\mathbf{k}}^{*}
),

I(X)^{\perp}=\langle F_{t};t\geq 1\rangle

where
F_{t}=\frac{1}{(t+s-1)!}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}L_{i}^{t+s-1}
, in particular
F_{1}=\frac{1}{s!}F
and
the family
\{F_{t};t\geq 1\}
is
G
-admissible.

Proof.

Assume that
X
is arithmetically Gorenstein.
Then by Corollary
3.9
there exist
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\in{\mathbf{k}}^{*}
such that the
R
-submodule
M
of
\Gamma
generated by
F_{t}=\frac{1}{(t+s-1)!}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}L_{i}^{t+s-1}
for
t\geq 1
is
G
-admissible and
I(X)^{\perp}=M.

It is enough to take
F=s!F_{1}
. In fact by Proposition
3.13
we have
(a)
and the equality
\langle F_{1}\rangle=\langle F\rangle=\operatorname{Ann}(F_{s})\circ F_{s+1}.
Hence by Proposition
3.12
we have
\langle F_{1}\rangle=\langle F\rangle=\operatorname{Ann}(F_{s})\circ F_{s+1}=(I(X)+(x_{n})^{\perp}.
Now
R/I(X)+(x_{n})
is an Artinian ring since
x_{n}
is a non-zero divisor in
R/I(X)
, hence of finite dimension as
{\mathbf{k}}
-vector space. Its length equals the dimension and

\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbf{k}}}R/(I(X)+(x_{n}))=r.

Hence
\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbf{k}}}\langle F\rangle=\operatorname{Length}(R/(I(X)+(x_{n})))=r.

Conversely, by Proposition
3.13
it is enough to prove that
\langle F_{1}\rangle=\operatorname{Ann}(F_{s})\circ F_{s+1}.
This is clear because
\langle F_{1}\rangle\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}(F_{s})\circ F_{s+1}
and by
(b)

\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbf{k}}}\langle F\rangle\geq r=\operatorname{Length}(R/I(X)+(x_{n}))=\operatorname{Length}(\operatorname{Ann}(F_{s})\circ F_{s+1}).

The last equality follows again by Proposition
3.12
.
∎

Remark 3.15
.

Let
X=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{r}\}
be a set of points of
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathbf{k}}}
of socle degree
s
and let
L_{1},\dots,L_{r}
be the associate linear forms. Denote
P_{i}=[a_{0,i},\dots,a_{n,i}]
for
i=1,\dots,r.

Along the paper we assumed for simplicity that
x_{n}
is a non-zero divisor of
R/I(X),
that is
x_{n}(P_{i})=a_{n,i}\neq 0
for all
i=1,\dots,r.
In particular we assumed
a_{n,i}=1,
hence
x_{n}\circ L_{i}=1
for all
i=1,\dots,r
.

In general, if

is any linear form in
R
such that
z
is a regular element of
R/I(X),
it means that
z(P_{i})=z\circ L_{i}=b_{0}a_{0,i}+\cdots+b_{n}a_{n,i}\neq 0
for all
i=1,\dots,r.
We remark that
(1)
and
(2)
in Theorem
3.14
can be rephrased as follows:

(1)

X
is arithmetically Gorenstein,

(2)

there exists a polynomial

with

\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\in{\mathbf{k}}
such that

(a)

\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}L_{i}^{s-1}=0
,

(b)

\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbf{k}}}\langle F\rangle\geq r.

If this is the case, then
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\in{\mathbf{k}}^{*}
are uniquely determined, modulo
{\mathbf{k}}^{*}
,

where

, in particular

and
the family

is

-admissible with respect to

.

4.
Effective construction of Gorenstein Points

Using Theorem
3.14
and Remark
3.15
, in this section we give an explicit description of the Zarisky locally closed locus of the points

X=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{r}\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathbf{k}}}
which are arithmetically Gorenstein.

Given a linear form
z=b_{0}x_{0}+\cdots+b_{n}x_{n}
in
R
and
L=a_{0}y_{0}+\cdots+a_{n}y_{n}
in
\Gamma,
we denote by
z\cdot L
the scalar
z\cdot L:=b_{0}a_{0}+\cdots b_{n}a_{n}=z\circ L
.
Notice that if

is the linear form associated to a point
P\in\mathbb{P}_{{\mathbf{k}}}^{n},
then

.

Definition 4.1
.

Given integers
2\leq s\leq r
, we define the set
W_{r,s}
of linear forms

\{L_{1},\dots,L_{r}\}
in
\Gamma
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

\operatorname{rank}(L_{1}^{s-1},\dots,L^{s-1}_{r})=\operatorname{rank}(L_{1}^{s-1},\dots,\widehat{L^{s-1}_{i}},\dots,L^{s-1}_{r})=r-1
, ,
i=1,\dots,r.

(2)

let
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\in{\mathbf{k}}^{*}
the elements uniquely determined (up to scalars in
{\mathbf{k}}^{*}
), by the identity
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}L_{i}^{s-1}=~0
given by
(1).

Let
z
be a linear form such that
z\cdot L_{i}\neq 0
for
i=1,\dots,r
.
If
F=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{\alpha_{i}}{z\cdot L_{i}}L_{i}^{s},
then
\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbf{k}}}\langle F\rangle\geq r.

Assume
L_{1},\dots,L_{r}
linear forms associated to
X=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{r}\},
we remark that (1) in the above definition is equivalent
X
having the Cayley-Bacharach property, for details see
[
8
]
and the proof of Theorem 20 in
[
44
]
.

Remark 4.2
.

We remark that
\mathbb{P}(W_{r,s})
defines a Zariski locally closed subset of
(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathbf{k}}}^{n})^{r}
described as it follows.
Let
L_{1},\dots,L_{r}
be linear forms in
\Gamma={\mathbf{k}}[y_{0},\dots,y_{n}]
and consider the base
B
of the monomials of degree
s-1
in
y_{0},\dots,y_{n}
.
Let
M_{r,s}
be the
\binom{s-1+n}{n}\times r
matrix whose the columns are respectively the coefficients of
L_{1}^{s-1},\dots,L^{s-1}_{r}
with respect to the base
B.
Hence the columns of
M_{r,s}
are the monomials of degree
s-1
in the coefficients of the
r
linear forms.

The condition
\operatorname{rank}(L_{1}^{s-1},\dots,L^{s-1}_{r})\leq r-1
is satisfied by choosing the coefficients of the linear forms in the irreducible Zariski closed set defined by the vanishing of all
r\times r
minors of
M_{r,s}
, say
V_{r,s-1}.
Consider now a submatrix
N
of
M_{r,s}
defined by
r-1
rows of
M_{r,s}
. The condition
\operatorname{rank}(L_{1}^{s-1},\dots,\widehat{L^{s-1}_{i}},\dots,L^{s-1}_{r})\geq r-1
is satisfied if all the
r-1\times r-1
minors
N_{i}
of
N
do not vanish. Set
\alpha_{i}:=(-1)^{i}N_{i}\neq 0,
then
\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbf{k}}}\langle F\rangle\geq r
imposes an other open condition on the coefficients of the linear forms.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
s\leq r
.
A set of points
X=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{r}\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathbf{k}}}
is arithmetically Gorenstein with socle degree
s
if and only if
(L_{1},\dots,L_{r})\in\mathbb{P}(W_{r,s})
where
L_{i}
is the linear form defined by
P_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,r
.

Proof.

Let
X=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{r}\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathbf{k}}}
be a Gorenstein set of points with socle degree
s
.
Let
z\in\Gamma
be a linear form defining a non-zero divisor of
R/I(X)
.

From Theorem
3.14
, see Remark
3.15
, there exists a polynomial

F=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{\alpha_{i}}{z\cdot L_{i}}L_{i}^{s}
with

\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\in{\mathbf{k}}^{*}
uniquely determined, up to scalars in
{\mathbf{k}}^{*}
, such that

H_{1}=\frac{1}{s!}F
is a generator of
(I(X)+(z))^{\perp}
.
Hence, Proposition
3.3
,

r-1={\operatorname{H\!F}}_{X}(s-1)=\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbf{k}}}\langle L_{1}^{s-1},\dots,L_{r}^{s-1}\rangle=\operatorname{rank}\langle L_{1}^{s-1},\dots,L_{r}^{s-1}\rangle,

i.e. we get condition
(1)
of Definition
4.1
.
Condition
(2)(b)
of Remark
3.15
implies
(2)
. Hence
(L_{1},\dots,L_{r})\in\mathbb{P}(W_{r,s}).

Conversely, let
(L_{1},\dots,L_{r})\in\mathbb{P}(W_{r,s})
be
r
linear forms satisfying conditions
(1)
and
(2)
.
Let
P_{i}
be the point defined by
L_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,r
.
Notice that
z
defines a non-zero divisor of
R/I(X)
.
Let
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\in{\mathbf{k}}^{*}
be the elements uniquely determined, up to scalars in
{\mathbf{k}}^{*}
, by condition
(1)
, i.e.
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}L^{s-1}=0
.
If we write
F=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{\alpha_{i}}{z\cdot L_{i}}L_{i}^{s}
we have
z\circ F=s(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}L_{i}^{s-1})=0
, so we get condition
(2)(a)
of Remark
3.15
.
Condition
(2)
implies
(2)(b)
of Remark
3.15
.

We have to prove that the socle degree of

is
s
to conclude that

is arithmetically Gorenstein.
From Proposition
3.3
we deduce that
F\in I(X)^{\perp}\cap(z)^{\perp}=(I(X)+(z))^{\perp}
.
Hence, since
z
is a non-zero divisor of

, we get

r\leq\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbf{k}}}\langle F\rangle\leq\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbf{k}}}(I(X)+(z))^{\perp}=r

and then
\langle F\rangle=(I(X)+(z))^{\perp}
.
Being

a form of degree

we conclude that the socle degree of

is
s
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Since

5.
Artinian reduction of Gorenstein points

Let
R={\mathbf{k}}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]
be and let
B=R/J
be an Artinian Gorenstein graded
{\mathbf{k}}
-algebra of length
r
and socle degree
s.
The following natural question comes up: under which conditions an Artinian Gorenstein ring is the Artinian reduction of a Gorenstein reduced set
X
of points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathbf{k}}}
?
Since
J^{\perp}=\langle F\rangle
with
F\in\Gamma
of degree
s,
the question can be rephrased in terms of
F.

This was the main aim of the paper by Tohaneanu
[
44
]
. A similar problem was studied in the work of Cho and Iarrobino (
[
6
, Proposition 1.13 and Theorem 3.3]
) in the less restrictive situation of a general zero-dimensional scheme.
Boij in
[
4
]
describes the possible Gorenstein Artinian quotients of the coordinate ring of a set of points in projective space through the corresponding canonical module.

Observe that condition
(2)
of Theorem
3.14
establishes some restrictions on a form
F
of degree
s
to be a generator of the inverse system of an Artinian reduction of
R/I(X).

On the other hand, Theorem
2.2
and the definition of
G
-admissible submodules of
\Gamma
are based in the lifting of a minimal reduction of the one-dimensional Gorenstein ring
R/I
with respect to a linear non-zero divisor
z
. In fact if
M=\langle H_{l},l\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\rangle
is a
G
-admissible
R
-submodule of
\Gamma,
equivalently it is the inverse system of a Gorenstein ring
R/I
of socle degree
s,
then
(I+(z))^{\perp}=\langle H_{1}\rangle.

As a consequence of Theorem
3.14
, in the next result we give a complete answer to our question, see also
[
44
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
X=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{r}\}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathbf{k}}}\setminus\{x_{n}=0\}
be a set of Gorenstein points of socle degree
s
and let
L_{1},\dots,L_{r}
be the associate linear forms.
Let
B=R/J
be a Gorenstein Artin algebra of length
r
and socle degree
s
and let
J^{\perp}=\langle F\rangle.

The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

B
is the Artinian reduction of
X
with respect to
x_{n}
,

(2)

there exist
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\in{\mathbf{k}}^{*}
(unique up scalars) such that
F=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}L_{i}^{s}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}L_{i}^{s-1}=0.

Proof.

Assume that
B
is the Artinian reduction of
X
with respect to
x_{n},
then
I(X)+(x_{n})=J.
Let
\{H_{t};t\geq 1\}
be a
G
-admissible generating system of
I(X)^{\perp}
as in Theorem Theorem
3.14
.
Since
\langle H_{1}\rangle=(I(X)+(x_{n}))^{\perp}
, then
F=s!H_{1}
satisfies
(2)
.

Assume there exist
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\in{\mathbf{k}}^{*}
such that
F=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}L_{i}^{s}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}L_{i}^{s-1}=0.
Hence
x_{n}\circ F=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}L_{i}^{s-1}=0
and by Proposition
3.3

F\in I(X)^{\perp}.

Then

J^{\perp}=\langle F\rangle\subset I(X)^{\perp}\cap(x_{n})^{\perp}=(I(X)+(x_{n}))^{\perp}.

Hence
I(X)+(x_{n})\subseteq J
and they are Gorenstein ideals with the same socle
s.
By the socle lemma by Kustin and Ulrich,
[
36
]
, it follows
J=I(X)+(x_{n})
, then
B=R/J
is the Artinian reduction of
X
with respect to
x_{n}
.
∎

It was known that there are Artinian Gorenstein algebras which are not Artinian reductions of Gorenstein points. As J. Jelisiejew mentioned to us in a private communication, every Artinian reduction of the defining ring of a set of points is smoothable. Hence the non-smoothabillity of a general Artinian algebras implies that it is not such a reduction. For instance in the case of the Artinian Gorenstein algebras with socle degree
s=3
, we may use the results on non-smoothability of
(1,n,n,1)
for
n\geq 6,
known in a range since Iarrobino-Kanev, but proved recently by Szafarczyk in
[
43
]
.
By following these results the first case of non-smoothability with
s=3
is for
n=8.

Proposition
5.1
allows to find new examples of Artinian Gorenstein algebras which are not Artinian reductions of Gorenstein points (hence non-smoothables).

Remark 5.2
.

Since

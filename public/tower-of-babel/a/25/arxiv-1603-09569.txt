On Jacobi Inversion Formulae for Telescopic Curves
Takanori AYANO

Abstract
For a hyperelliptic curve of genus g g , it is well known that the symmetric products of g g points on the curve are expressed in terms of their Abel–Jacobi image by the hyperelliptic sigma function (Jacobi inversion formulae). Matsutani and Previato gave a natural generalization of the formulae to the more general algebraic curves defined by y r = f ⁡ ( x ) y^{r}=f(x) , which are special cases of ( n , s ) (n,s) curves, and derived new vanishing properties of the sigma function of the curves y r = f ⁡ ( x ) y^{r}=f(x) . In this paper we extend the formulae to the telescopic curves proposed by Miura and derive new vanishing properties of the sigma function of telescopic curves. The telescopic curves contain the ( n , s ) (n,s) curves as special cases.

Introduction

The theory of the elliptic function was the one of the main subjects of the research of mathematics in the nineteenth century. Now the beautiful theory of the elliptic function is constructed and is applied to many fields such as mathematical physics, integrable system, number theory, engineering, and cryptography. In integrable system, it is well known that the elliptic function gives an exact solution of some nonlinear differential equations. In cryptography, the cryptosystem using the elliptic curves is used widely. Recently, with the scientific development, we have to analyze many complicated nonlinear phenomena and it is necessary to give exact solutions of many nonlinear differential equations in order to analyze the phenomena precisely. In cryptography, it is necessary to make a wider class of algebraic curves available to the cryptosystem for assuring the safety of cryptosystem. Therefore it is very important to construct the basic theory of the Abelian function, which is a generalization of the elliptic function to several variables. The sigma function plays an important role in the theory of the Abelian function.

The multivariate sigma function is introduced by F. Klein
[
13
,
14
]
for hyperelliptic curves as a generalization of the Weierstrass’s elliptic sigma function. Recently, the hyperelliptic sigma function is generalized to the more general plane algebraic curves called
(n,s)

[

Further the sigma function has some remarkable algebraic properties that it is directly related with the defining equations of an algebraic curve. From these algebraic properties, the sigma function is expected to have many applications in mathematical physics etc.
[

. Further the sigma function is useful to describe a solution of the inversion problem of algebraic integrals. The Jacobi inversion problem for hyperelliptic curves is described as follows.

\displaystyle\wp_{1,i}\big(u^{[g]}\big)=(-1)^{i-1}e_{i},\qquad 1\leq i\leq g,

Fundamental differential of second kind

A fundamental differential of second kind plays an important role in the theory of the sigma function. We recall its definition.

Definition 3.1
.

A two form
\omega(P,Q)
on
X\times X
is called a fundamental differential of second kind if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

\omega(P,Q)=\omega(Q,P)
,

(ii)

\omega(P,Q)
is holomorphic except
\{(R,R)\,|\,R\in X\}
where it has a double pole,

(iii)

for
R\in X
, take a local coordinate
t
around
R
, then the expansion around
(R,R)
is of the form

\displaystyle\omega(P,Q)=\left(\frac{1}{(t_{P}-t_{Q})^{2}}+\text{regular}\right)dt_{P}dt_{Q}.

A fundamental differential of second kind exists but is not unique. Let
\omega_{1}(P,Q)
be a fundamental differential of second kind. Then a two form
\omega_{2}(P,Q)
is a fundamental differential of second kind if and only if there exists
\{c_{ij}\}_{i,j=1,\dots,g}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
c_{ij}=c_{ji}
and

\displaystyle\omega_{2}(P,Q)=\omega_{1}(P,Q)+\sum_{i,j=1}^{g}c_{ij}dv_{i}(P)dv_{j}(Q),

where
\{dv_{i}\}_{i=1}^{g}
is a basis of holomorphic one forms on
X
.

4

Let
X
be a telescopic curve of genus

associated with
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})

\displaystyle\widehat{\omega}(P,Q)=d_{Q}\Omega(P,Q)+\sum_{i=1}^{g}du_{i}(P)dr_{i}(Q),

\displaystyle dr_{1}(Q)=-\frac{\varphi_{g+1}(Q)}{\det G_{1}(Q)}dy_{1}.

(4.2)

This choice is possible from Proposition

(see
[
2

21

Take a symplectic basis

\displaystyle 2\omega_{1}=\left(\int_{\alpha_{j}}du_{i}\right),\qquad\!2\omega_{2}=\left(\int_{\beta_{j}}du_{i}\right),\qquad\!-2\eta_{1}=\left(\int_{\alpha_{j}}dr_{i}\right),\qquad\!-2\eta_{2}=\left(\int_{\beta_{j}}dr_{i}\right).

The normalized period matrix is given by

Let
\delta=\tau\delta^{\prime}+\delta^{\prime\prime}

The sigma function

\displaystyle\sigma(u)=C\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}{}^{t}u\eta_{1}\omega_{1}^{-1}u\right)\theta[\delta]\left((2\omega_{1})^{-1}u,\tau\right),

\displaystyle\wp_{i,j}(u)=-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial u_{i}\partial u_{j}}\log\sigma(u).

Then we have

\displaystyle\wp_{i,j}(u)=\frac{\sigma_{i}(u)\sigma_{j}(u)-\sigma_{i,j}(u)\sigma(u)}{\sigma(u)^{2}},

(4.5)

where
\sigma_{i}(u)=\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}\sigma(u)
and
\sigma_{i,j}(u)=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial u_{i}\partial u_{j}}\sigma(u)
.

We have

\displaystyle d_{P}d_{Q}\log\sigma\left(\int_{Q}^{P}du-\sum_{j\neq i}\int_{\infty}^{P_{j}}du\right)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{g}\wp_{k,\ell}\left(\int_{Q}^{P}du-\sum_{j\neq i}\int_{\infty}^{P_{j}}du\right)\frac{\varphi_{g+1-k}(P)\varphi_{g+1-\ell}(Q)}{\det G_{1}(P)\det G_{1}(Q)}dx_{1}dy_{1}.

From Proposition
4.3
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{F(P,Q)}{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}}=\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{g}\wp_{k,\ell}\left(\int_{Q}^{P}du-\sum_{j\neq i}\int_{\infty}^{P_{j}}du\right)\varphi_{g+1-k}(P)\varphi_{g+1-\ell}(Q),

(4.6)

as a meromorphic function of
(P,Q)\in X^{2}
. This formula is an analogue of the formula of Klein (cf.
[
8
, Theorem 3.4]
).

5
Frobenius–Stickelberger matrix

For
P_{1},\dots,P_{k},P\in X
, we define the matrix (Frobenius–Stickelberger matrix) as in the case of
[
17
]

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}\varphi_{1}(P_{1})&\varphi_{2}(P_{1})&\cdots&\varphi_{k+1}(P_{1})\\
\varphi_{1}(P_{2})&\varphi_{2}(P_{2})&\cdots&\varphi_{k+1}(P_{2})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\varphi_{1}(P_{k})&\varphi_{2}(P_{k})&\cdots&\varphi_{k+1}(P_{k})\\
\varphi_{1}(P)&\varphi_{2}(P)&\cdots&\varphi_{k+1}(P)\end{matrix}\right).

(5.1)

For
1\leq i\leq k+1
, let
\psi_{k+1}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k};P)
and
\psi_{k}^{(i)}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})
be the determinants of the matrix (
5.1
) and the matrix obtained by deleting the last row and the
i
-th column from (
5.1
), respectively. Note that
\psi_{k}^{(i)}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})
does not vanish identically as a meromorphic function of
P_{1},\dots,P_{k}
. We define
\mu_{k+1}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k};P)
by

\displaystyle\mu_{k+1}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k};P)=\frac{\psi_{k+1}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k};P)}{\psi_{k}^{(k+1)}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})}

and
\mu_{k,i}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})
by

\displaystyle\mu_{k+1}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k};P)=\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{k+1-i}\mu_{k,i}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})\varphi_{i}(P)

and
\mu_{k,i}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})=0
for
i\geq k+2
. Then, for
1\leq i\leq k+1
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{k,i}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})=\frac{\psi_{k}^{(i)}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})}{\psi_{k}^{(k+1)}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})}.

Note that
\mu_{k,i}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})
can be regarded as a meromorphic function on
X^{k}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
z_{k}
be the local parameter of
P_{k}
around
\infty
satisfying (
2.3
). Then, as a meromorphic function of
P_{1},\dots,P_{k}
, we have the expansion

\displaystyle\mu_{k,i}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})=\mu_{k-1,i}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k-1})z_{k}^{N(k)-N(k+1)}+O\big(z_{k}^{N(k)-N(k+1)+1}\big),

where
1\leq i\leq k
and
N(n)=\operatorname{ord}_{\infty}(\varphi_{n})
for a positive integer
n
.

Proof.

As a meromorphic function of
P_{1},\dots,P_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{k,i}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})=\frac{\psi_{k}^{(i)}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})}{\psi_{k}^{(k+1)}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})}=\frac{\varphi_{k+1}(P_{k})\psi_{k-1}^{(i)}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k-1})+O\big(z_{k}^{-N(k+1)+1}\big)}{\varphi_{k}(P_{k})\psi_{k-1}^{(k)}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k-1})+O\big(z_{k}^{-N(k)+1}\big)}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\mu_{k,i}(P_1,\ldots,P_k)}{}=\frac{\big\{z_{k}^{-N(k+1)}+O\big(z_{k}^{-N(k+1)+1}\big)\big\}\psi_{k-1}^{(i)}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k-1})+O\big(z_{k}^{-N(k+1)+1}\big)}{\big\{z_{k}^{-N(k)}+O\big(z_{k}^{-N(k)+1}\big)\big\}\psi_{k-1}^{(k)}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k-1})+O\big(z_{k}^{-N(k)+1}\big)}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\mu_{k,i}(P_1,\ldots,P_k)}{}=\frac{z_{k}^{-N(k+1)}}{z_{k}^{-N(k)}}\cdot\frac{\psi_{k-1}^{(i)}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k-1})+O(z_{k})}{\psi_{k-1}^{(k)}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k-1})+O(z_{k})}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\mu_{k,i}(P_1,\ldots,P_k)}{}=\mu_{k-1,i}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k-1})z_{k}^{N(k)-N(k+1)}+O\big(z_{k}^{N(k)-N(k+1)+1}\big).

∎

6
Riemann’s singularity theorem

Let
X
be a telescopic curve of genus
g\geq 1
. For a divisor
D
, let
L(D)
be the vector space consisting of meromorphic functions
f
on
X
such that
\operatorname{div}(f)+D\geq 0
and the zero function on
X
, and
\ell(D)
the dimension of
L(D)
.

For
1\leq k\leq g-1
and
P_{1},\dots,P_{k}\in X\backslash\infty
, let

\displaystyle u^{[k]}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\infty}^{P_{k}}du

and

\displaystyle n_{k}=\ell(P_{1}+\cdots+P_{k}+(g-k-1)\infty).

Then the following theorem holds.

Theorem 6.1

(Riemann’s singularity theorem, cf.
[
1
,
18
,
20
]
)
.

1.

For every multi-index
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m})
with
\alpha_{i}\in\{1,\dots,g\}
and
m<n_{k}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial u_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial u_{\alpha_{m}}}\sigma\big(u^{[k]}\big)=0.

2.

There exists a multi-index
(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n_{k}})
, which in general depends on
P_{1}+\cdots+P_{k}
, such that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{n_{k}}}{\partial u_{\beta_{1}}\cdots\partial u_{\beta_{n_{k}}}}\sigma\big(u^{[k]}\big)\neq 0.

The following proposition is stated for the hyperelliptic curves in
[
24
]
and for the curves
y^{r}=f(x)
in
[
17
,
18
]
. The same statement is also satisfied for telescopic curves. The proof is similar to
[
24
, Proposition 5.2]
.

Proposition 6.2
.

If
\psi_{k}^{(k+1)}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})\neq 0
, then we have
n_{k}=\sharp\{n\,|\,0\leq N(n)\leq g-k-1\}
, where
N(n)=\operatorname{ord}_{\infty}(\varphi_{n})
for a positive integer
n
and
\sharp
means the number of elements.

7
Jacobi inversion formulae for telescopic curves

For
1\leq k\leq g
and
P_{1},\dots,P_{k}\in X\backslash\infty
, let

\displaystyle u^{[k]}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\int_{\infty}^{P_{j}}du.

7.1
\boldsymbol{k=g}

Theorem 7.1
.

As a meromorphic function of
P_{1},\dots,P_{g}
, we have

\displaystyle\wp_{1,i}\big(u^{[g]}\big)=(-1)^{i-1}\mu_{g,g+1-i}(P_{1},\dots,P_{g}),\qquad 1\leq i\leq g.

(7.1)

Proof.

Let
S
be the set of
(P_{1},\dots,P_{g})\in(X\backslash\infty)^{g}
such that
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{g}P_{i}
is a general divisor and
P_{i}\neq P_{j}
for any
i
,
j

(i\neq j)
. First we prove the equation (
7.1
) for
(P_{1},\dots,P_{g})\in S
. From Lemma
3.2
(ii), we have
\operatorname{ord}_{\infty}(\det H(P,Q))\leq\sum\limits_{i=2}^{m}(d_{i-1}/d_{i}-1)a_{i}
and
\operatorname{ord}_{\infty}(x_{1}\frac{\partial\det H}{\partial y_{k}}(P,Q))\leq\sum\limits_{i=2}^{m}(d_{i-1}/d_{i}-1)a_{i}-a_{k}+a_{1}
with respect to
P
. On the other hand, we have
\operatorname{ord}_{\infty}(x_{1}^{2}\varphi_{g}(P))=-1+a_{1}+\sum\limits_{i=2}^{m}(d_{i-1}/d_{i}-1)a_{i}
. Since
a_{i}\geq 2
for any
i
, we have
\operatorname{ord}_{\infty}(\det H(P,Q))<\operatorname{ord}_{\infty}(x_{1}^{2}\varphi_{g}(P))
and
\operatorname{ord}_{\infty}(x_{1}\frac{\partial\det H}{\partial y_{k}}(P,Q))<\operatorname{ord}_{\infty}(x_{1}^{2}\varphi_{g}(P))
with respect to
P
. We let
P\to\infty
after dividing the both sides of (
4.6
) by
\varphi_{g}(P)
and
Q=P_{i}
. Then, from (
4.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\varphi_{g+1}(P_{i})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{g}\wp_{1,\ell}\big(u^{[g]}\big)\varphi_{g+1-\ell}(P_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{g}\wp_{1,g+1-j}\big(u^{[g]}\big)\varphi_{j}(P_{i}),

where we use the fact that
\wp_{1,\ell}(u)
is an even function from Proposition
4.2
. From
(P_{1},\dots,P_{g})\in S
, we have
\psi_{g}^{(g+1)}(P_{1},\dots,P_{g})\neq 0
(cf.
[
1
, p. 154]
). From
\mu_{g+1}(P_{1},\dots,P_{g};P_{i})=0
for any
i
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{g+1}(P_{1},\dots,P_{g};P_{i})=\varphi_{g+1}(P_{i})+\sum_{j=1}^{g}(-1)^{g+1-j}\mu_{g,j}(P_{1},\dots,P_{g})\varphi_{j}(P_{i})=0.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{g}\wp_{1,g+1-j}\big(u^{[g]}\big)\varphi_{j}(P_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{g}(-1)^{g-j}\mu_{g,j}(P_{1},\dots,P_{g})\varphi_{j}(P_{i}).

Therefore we have

\displaystyle A\cdot\left(\begin{matrix}\alpha_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\alpha_{g}\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{matrix}\right),

where
A=(\varphi_{j}(P_{i}))_{1\leq i,j\leq g}
and
\alpha_{j}=\wp_{1,g+1-j}\big(u^{[g]}\big)-(-1)^{g-j}\mu_{g,j}(P_{1},\dots,P_{g})
. From
(P_{1},\dots,P_{g})

\in S
, we have
\det A\neq 0
. Therefore we have
\alpha_{j}=0
, i.e.,
\wp_{1,g+1-j}\big(u^{[g]}\big)=(-1)^{g-j}\mu_{g,j}(P_{1},\dots,P_{g})
for any
j
. We set
i=g+1-j
, then we have
\wp_{1,i}(u^{[g]})=(-1)^{i-1}\mu_{g,g+1-i}(P_{1},\dots,P_{g})
.

Let
T
be the set of
(P_{1},\dots,P_{g})\in(X\backslash\infty)^{g}
such that
\psi_{g}^{(g+1)}(P_{1},\dots,P_{g})\neq 0
. Then we have
S=T
(cf.
[
1
, p. 154]
).
Since the equation (
7.1
) holds for any
(P_{1},\dots,P_{g})\in T
, it holds as a meromorphic function of
P_{1},\dots,P_{g}
.
∎

Remark 7.2
.

As discussed in
[
17
]
, for a hyperelliptic curve,
\mu_{g,g+1-i}
is equal to the symmetric polynomial
e_{i}
.
Therefore Theorem
7.1
is a natural generalization of the Jacobi inversion formulae for hyperelliptic curves to telescopic curves.

7.2
\boldsymbol{k\leq g-1}

Let
a=\min\{a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\}
. Hereafter we assume
g-a\leq k\leq g-1
.

Theorem 7.3
.

\sigma_{g-k}\big(u^{[k]}\big)
does not vanish identically with respect to
P_{1},\dots,P_{k}
and we have, as a meromorphic function of
P_{1},\dots,P_{k}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{i}\big(u^{[k]}\big)}{\sigma_{g-k}\big(u^{[k]}\big)}=(-1)^{k+i-g}\mu_{k,g+1-i}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k}),\qquad 1\leq i\leq g.

Proof.

First we prove for
k=g-1
. Let
z_{g}
be the local parameter of
P_{g}
around
\infty
satisfying (
2.3
). From
[
3
, Theorem 2]
,
\sigma_{1}\big(u^{[g-1]}\big)
does not vanish identically with respect to
P_{1},\dots
,
P_{g-1}
and we have the expansion

\displaystyle\sigma\left(u^{[g-1]}+\int_{\infty}^{P_{g}}du\right)=\sigma_{1}\big(u^{[g-1]}\big)z_{g}+O\big(z_{g}^{2}\big).

From Theorem
7.1
and (
4.5
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{i}\left(u^{[g-1]}+{\int_{\infty}^{P_{g}}du}\right)\sigma_{1}\left(u^{[g-1]}+{\int_{\infty}^{P_{g}}du}\right)-\sigma_{1,i}\left(u^{[g-1]}+{\int_{\infty}^{P_{g}}du}\right)\sigma\left(u^{[g-1]}+{\int_{\infty}^{P_{g}}du}\right)}{\sigma\left(u^{[g-1]}+{\int_{\infty}^{P_{g}}du}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=(-1)^{i-1}\mu_{g,g+1-i}(P_{1},\dots,P_{g}),

as a meromorphic function of
P_{1},\dots,P_{g}
. Therefore, from Proposition
5.1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\big\{\sigma_{i}\big(u^{[g-1]}\big)+O(z_{g})\big\}\cdot\big\{\sigma_{1}\big(u^{[g-1]}\big)+O(z_{g})\big\}+O(z_{g})}{\sigma_{1}\big(u^{[g-1]}\big)^{2}z_{g}^{2}+O(z_{g}^{3})}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=(-1)^{i-1}z_{g}^{-2}\mu_{g-1,g+1-i}(P_{1},\dots,P_{g-1})+O\big(z_{g}^{-1}\big).

By comparing the coefficient of
z_{g}^{-2}
of the above equation, we find that
\sigma_{i}\big(u^{[g-1]}\big)
does not vanish identically with respect to
P_{1},\dots,P_{g-1}
and we have, as a meromorphic function of
P_{1},\dots,P_{g-1}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{i}\big(u^{[g-1]}\big)}{\sigma_{1}\big(u^{[g-1]}\big)}=(-1)^{i-1}\mu_{g-1,g+1-i}(P_{1},\dots,P_{g-1}).

Next we prove Theorem
7.3
for
g-a\leq k\leq g-2
by induction of
k
as in the case of
[
17
]
. Assume that Theorem
7.3
holds for
k
satisfying

. Then, for
i\geq g-k
,
\sigma_{i}\big(u^{[k]}\big)
does not vanish identically with respect to
P_{1},\dots,P_{k}
. From the assumption of induction, we have, as a meromorphic function of
P_{1},\dots,P_{k}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{g-k+1}\big(u^{[k]}\big)}{\sigma_{g-k}\big(u^{[k]}\big)}=-\mu_{k,k}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k}).

From Proposition
5.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{k,k}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})=z_{k}^{N(k)-N(k+1)}+O\big(z_{k}^{N(k)-N(k+1)+1}\big).

By the assumption of induction we have, as a meromorphic function of
P_{1},\dots,P_{k}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{i}\big(u^{[k]}\big)}{\sigma_{g-k}\big(u^{[k]}\big)}=(-1)^{k+i-g}\mu_{k,g+1-i}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k}).

(7.2)

By multiplying the both sides of (
7.2
) by
\sigma_{g-k}\big(u^{[k]}\big)/\sigma_{g-k+1}\big(u^{[k]}\big)
, we have, as a meromorphic function of
P_{1},\dots,P_{k}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{g-k}\big(u^{[k]}\big)}{\sigma_{g-k+1}\big(u^{[k]}\big)}\cdot\frac{\sigma_{i}\big(u^{[k]}\big)}{\sigma_{g-k}\big(u^{[k]}\big)}=(-1)^{k+i-g}\frac{\sigma_{g-k}\big(u^{[k]}\big)}{\sigma_{g-k+1}\big(u^{[k]}\big)}\cdot\mu_{k,g+1-i}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k}).

Therefore, from Proposition
5.1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{i}\big(u^{[k]}\big)}{\sigma_{g-k+1}\big(u^{[k]}\big)}=(-1)^{k-1+i-g}\big\{z_{k}^{N(k+1)-N(k)}+O\big(z_{k}^{N(k+1)-N(k)+1}\big)\big\}\cdot\mu_{k,g+1-i}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})

\displaystyle\hphantom{\frac{\sigma_{i}\big(u^{[k]}\big)}{\sigma_{g-k+1}\big(u^{[k]}\big)}}{}=(-1)^{k-1+i-g}\mu_{k-1,g+1-i}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k-1})+O(z_{k}).

(7.3)

Since
\psi_{k-1}^{(k)}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k-1})
does not vanish identically with respect to

, there exist
\tilde{P}_{1},\dots,\tilde{P}_{k-1}\in X\backslash\infty

and Proposition

For

,

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{2}\big(u^{[3]}\big)}{\sigma_{1}\big(u^{[3]}\big)}=-\frac{\left|\begin{matrix}1&x_{1}(P_{1})&x_{2}(P_{1})\\

For
k=2

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{3}\big(u^{[2]}\big)}{\sigma_{2}\big(u^{[2]}\big)}=\frac{x_{3}(P_{1})-x_{3}(P_{2})}{x_{1}(P_{2})-x_{1}(P_{1})},\qquad\frac{\sigma_{4}\big(u^{[2]}\big)}{\sigma_{2}\big(u^{[2]}\big)}=\frac{x_{1}(P_{1})x_{3}(P_{2})-x_{1}(P_{2})x_{3}(P_{1})}{x_{1}(P_{2})-x_{1}(P_{1})}.

For

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{4}\big(u^{[1]}\big)}{\sigma_{3}\big(u^{[1]}\big)}=-x_{1}(P_{1}).

9
Vanishing of
\boldsymbol{\sigma_{i}}

In
[
3
,
22
]
, the vanishing and the expansion of the sigma functions of
(n,s)
curves and telescopic curves on the Abel–Jacobi image are studied.
In this section, we show that from Theorem
7.3
we can derive some new vanishing properties of
\sigma_{i}
for telescopic curves immediately.

Corollary 9.1
.

If
g-a\leq k\leq g-1
and
i\geq g-k
, then
\sigma_{i}\big(u^{[k]}\big)
does not vanish identically with respect to
P_{1},\dots,P_{k}
.

Proof.

For
i\geq g-k
,
\mu_{k,g+1-i}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})
does not vanish identically with respect to
P_{1},\dots,P_{k}
.
Therefore Corollary
9.1
follows from Theorem
7.3
.
∎

For
g-a\leq k\leq g-1
and
i>g-k
, we consider the expansion

\displaystyle\sigma_{g-k}\left(u^{[k-1]}+\int_{\infty}^{P_{k}}du\right)=C_{k}\big(u^{[k-1]}\big)z_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}+O\big(z_{k}^{\alpha_{k}+1}\big)

and

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}\left(u^{[k-1]}+\int_{\infty}^{P_{k}}du\right)=C_{k,i}\big(u^{[k-1]}\big)z_{k}^{\beta_{k,i}}+O\big(z_{k}^{\beta_{k,i}+1}\big),

where
C_{k}\big(u^{[k-1]}\big)
and
C_{k,i}\big(u^{[k-1]}\big)
do not vanish identically with respect to
P_{1},\dots,P_{k-1}
.

Corollary 9.2
.

(i)

We have
\alpha_{k}=\beta_{k,i}+N(k+1)-N(k)
. In particular, if
g-a<k\leq g-1
and
i>g-k
, then we have
\beta_{k,i}=0
and
\alpha_{k}=N(k+1)-N(k)
.

(ii)

We have, as a meromorphic function of
P_{1},\dots,P_{k-1}
,

\displaystyle\frac{C_{k,i}\big(u^{[k-1]}\big)}{C_{k}\big(u^{[k-1]}\big)}=(-1)^{k+i-g}\mu_{k-1,g+1-i}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k-1}).

(9.1)

Proof.

From Theorem
7.3
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{i}\big(u^{[k]}\big)}{\sigma_{g-k}\big(u^{[k]}\big)}=(-1)^{k+i-g}\mu_{k,g+1-i}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k}).

Therefore we have

\displaystyle\frac{C_{k,i}\big(u^{[k-1]}\big)z_{k}^{\beta_{k,i}}+O\big(z_{k}^{\beta_{k,i}+1}\big)}{C_{k}\big(u^{[k-1]}\big)z_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}+O\big(z_{k}^{\alpha_{k}+1}\big)}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=(-1)^{k+i-g}\mu_{k-1,g+1-i}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k-1})z_{k}^{N(k)-N(k+1)}+O\big(z_{k}^{N(k)-N(k+1)+1}\big).

Therefore we obtain
\beta_{k,i}-\alpha_{k}=N(k)-N(k+1)
and (
9.1
). On the other hand, if
g-a<k\leq g-1
and
i>g-k
, then from Corollary
9.1

\sigma_{i}\big(u^{[k-1]}\big)
does not vanish identically with respect to
P_{1},\dots,P_{k-1}
. Therefore, if
g-a<k\leq g-1
and
i>g-k
, then
\beta_{k,i}=0
.
∎

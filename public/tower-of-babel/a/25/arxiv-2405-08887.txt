Non-Abelian Fractional Chern Insulators and Competing States in Flat Moiré Bands
Hui Liu, Zhao Liu, Emil J. Bergholtz

Abstract
Breakthrough experiments have recently realized fractional Chern insulators (FCIs) in moiré materials. However, all states observed are Abelian, the possible existence of more exotic non-Abelian FCIs remains controversial both experimentally and theoretically. Here, we investigate the competition between charge density wave order, gapless composite fermion liquids and non-Abelian Moore-Read states at half-filling of a moiré band. Although groundstate (quasi-)degeneracies and spectral flow is not sufficient for distinguishing between charge order and Moore-Read states, we find evidence using entanglement spectroscopy that both these states of matter can be realized with realistic Coulomb interactions. In a double twisted bilayer graphene model transitions between these phases by tuning the coupling strength between the layers: at weak coupling there is a composite Fermi liquid phase and at strong coupling a low entanglement state signaling CDW order emerges. Remarkably, however, there is compelling evidence for a non-Abelian Moore-Read FCI phase at intermediate coupling.

We focus on the Moore-Read states, a well-known non-Abelian topological phase that emerges at

Moore-Read states.
—

By switching the interaction from a screened to a full long range Coulomb, we find compelling evidence for the presence of the true non-Abelian MR states. As shown in Fig.

Transition between CDWs and Moore-Read states.
—

Having provided evidence for both possibilities of the CDW and the MR states in moiré systems, in the following, we revisit and provide more details on the phase transition between them by tuning the coupling strength
\gamma
between two layers of TBGs as was already shown in Fig.
1
. Here, we fix
\kappa=0
, which corresponds to the unscreened Coulomb interaction, and instead tune the strength of the coupling,
\gamma
, between the bilayers.
We then choose
\gamma\in[3,5.5]
, where the two layers of TBGs are tight coupled and the middle valence flat band remains isolated.
We identify the phase transition between the charge density wave and the Moore-Read states through two criteria.
One is the many-body energy spectrum gap
\Delta_{E}
between the sixfold degenerated states and excitation states. It reveals a phase transition around
\gamma\approx 4.7
.
Our second tool is the entanglement spectrum gap both for the CDW and MR states.
Here, the entanglement spectrum gap is defined as
\Delta_{\text{PES}}=E_{\text{PES}}^{(m+1)}-E_{\text{PES}}^{(m)}
, where
E_{\text{PES}}^{(m)}
represents the
m
-th eigenvalue of
\rho_{A}
with
m
being the correct counting number of quasihole statistics for the CDW and the MR states, respectively.
It then shows a consistent behavior as
\Delta_{E}
.
Before the phase transition, the entanglement gap for the CDW is roughly
0
, while the gap for MR states is quite large, about
\Delta_{\text{PES}}^{\text{MR}}\approx 0.6
, which serves as a supplementary confirmation of MR states.
Upon crossing the phase transition point, we observe a rapid decrease to
0
in the entanglement gap for MR states, while the charge density wave gap undergoes a pronounced jump, attaining a finite value.
This serves as compelling evidence for the phase transition from MR states to the CDW.

Composite Fermi liquids.
—
A third phase in these systems is comprised by gapless CFLs.
It originally occurs in the lowest LL at half or quarter band filling
Jain 1989
.
Within our system, this phenomenon arises at weak coupling strength
\gamma
between the two twisted bilayers.
As when
\gamma\rightarrow 0
, the full system decouples into two independent TBGs, each resembling the characteristic behaviors of the original cTBG, and it has been verified that the flat band of cTBG perfectly captures the lowest LL physics
Ledwith et al. 2020
.
This then offers a convincing explanation for the emergence of gapless CFLs.
To further confirm the existence of these phases, we examine both the many-body spectrum and the structure factor.
The many-body spectrum reveals a gapless structure and the ground states match with the recently observations in tMoTe
2
and the lowest LL
Goldman et al. 2023
;
Dong et al. 2023
, while the structure factor shows the absence of a conventional electronic Fermi surface at momentum
k_{F}
, instead, revealing a new Fermi surface at
2k_{F}
(see THE SM for details on this
Sup
).
Increasing
\gamma
drives the middle flat band from capturing the lowest LL physics to mimicking the higher LL physics, which then results in a transition from gapless CFL to MR states.

Discussion.
—
In this work we have strongly corroborated recent claims that non-Abelian MR states may be realized in realistic moiré systems. At the same time our detailed entanglement spectroscopy highlights the fact that ground state degeneracies and momentum quantum numbers alone is insufficient for establishing the presence of this exotic phase of matter. In particular our analysis demonstrates that competing CDW-like order can be the underlying reason for states that at first glance appear to be MR states.

We have also identified parameters that lead to the respective phases. By switching the Coulomb interaction to infinite range, we uncover genuine MR states, albeit with a ground state degeneracy that at these small system sizes appear less favorable than in the charge density wave scenario.
Furthermore, by tuning the coupling strength between two layers of TBGs, we identify the possibility for a direct phase transition between the charge density wave and the Moore-Read states, where the entanglement spectrum exhibits a consistent behavior with the energy gap closing and reopening.

Our work serves as a prototypical example of the interplay between Moore-Read states and competing states in moiré systems, offering compelling avenues for future research.
Firstly, while non-Abelian topological states have long been theorized, their (likely) realization has been limited to systems with strong magnetic field and low temperatures, such as LL systems
Nayak et al. 2008
. Our findings extend the possibility of observing these states in a more favourable setting in moiré materials without the need for external fields which has recently been utilized for realizing Abelian topological states at zero magnetic field and relatively high temperatures.

From a theoretical standpoint, although the MR states represent the simplest manifestation of non-Abelian topological states, our work can be readily adapted to more complicate scenarios, such as the Read-Rezayi states at fractional fillings
2/5
and
3/5

Read and Rezayi 1999
.
Secondly, while evidence of charge density wave is primarily observed in the particle-cut entanglement spectrum side, the absence of prominent peaks in structure factor calculations suggests the potential existence of novel phases of matter. Finally, the transition between the CFL and MR states warrants further investigation.

VIII
quasihole counting for Moore-Read states and charge density waves

Here, we detail how to do the quasihole counting. In the thin-torus limit, the ground states of non-Abelian fractional topological states at band filling
\nu=k/(kM+2)
should fulfill the rules that, any
kM+2
consecutive sites occupy exactly
k
particles, and the distance between two particles is at least
M

Ardonne et al. 2008
.
For Fermionic Moore-Read states at half-filling,
where
k=2
and
M=1
, this yields ground states exhibiting a sixfold degeneracy for an even number of electrons and a twofold degeneracy for an odd number of electrons.
A quasihole excitation state is then characterized by one string of
(kM+2)
consecutive sites carrying particles less than
k
.
For the quasihole counting statistics, it is just the number of how many such states are allowed when putting
N_{A}
particles in
N
sites with
N_{A}/N<\nu
.

We below give a concrete example. For Moore-Read states with a system size of
N

in subsystem

•

No
0110

10
and zeros,

\displaystyle n_{1}=N\cdot\frac{(N-8+4-1)!}{(N-8)!\cdot 4!}.

(6)

•

One
0110
string. The permissible states are the combinations of one string of
01100
, two strings of
10
, and zeros,

\displaystyle n_{2}=N\cdot\frac{(N-9+3-1)!}{(N-9)!\cdot 2!}.

(7)

•

Two
0110

0110
and zeros,

\displaystyle n_{3}=N\cdot\frac{(N-8+2-1)!}{(N-8)!\cdot 2!}.

(8)

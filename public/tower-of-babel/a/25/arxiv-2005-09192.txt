1
Introduction

We consider rough differential equations in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of the form

dY_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}V_{i}(Y_{t})d\boldsymbol{X}^{i}_{t}+V_{0}(Y_{t})dt,\;Y_{0}=y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;t\in[0,1].

(1)

Over the past decade, rough differential equations driven by Gaussian processes (i.e.,
\boldsymbol{X}_{t}
is a Gaussian rough path) have been extensively studied. The existence and smoothness of the density of
Y_{t}
is among the most important questions and have attracted a lot attentions.

The case where
\boldsymbol{X}_{t}
is given by the Stratonavich Brownian rough path is equivalent to the classical probabilistic Hörmander’s theorem studied by P. Malliavin
[
16
]
. In this case, it is possible to prove that
Y_{t}
admits a smooth density is equivalent to the hypoellipticity of the differential operator given by

L=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}V_{i}^{2}+V_{0}.

L. Hörmander
[
13
]
was the first to formulate a sufficient condition on
\{V_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq d}
to ensure the hypoellipticity of
L
, known today as the parabolic Hörmander’s condition. The parabolic Hörmander’s condition, which we introduce now, has become a fundamental setting in many areas including probability, geometry and PDE.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\{V_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq d}
be a collection of smooth vector fields on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{0}

\displaystyle=\big\{V_{1},\cdots,V_{d}\big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\cup_{0\leq i\leq d}\big\{[V,V_{i}]\;,\;V\in\mathcal{W}^{k}\big\}\;,\;k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}.

We say
\{V_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq d}
satisfy the parabolic Hörmander’s condition if we can find an integer
k_{0}\geq 0
such that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

Span\left\{\cup_{0\leq k\leq k_{0}}\mathcal{W}^{k}(x)\right\}=\mathbb{R}^{d}.

With the previous definition, the probabilistic Hörmander’s theorem by P. Malliavin
[
16
]
can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
W_{t}
be a standard Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and consider

dY_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}V_{i}(Y_{t})\circ dW^{i}_{t}+V_{0}(Y_{t})dt,\;Y_{0}=y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;t\in[0,1].

Assume that
\{V_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq d}
are smooth vector fields with bounded derivatives of all orders. If
\{V_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq d}
satisfy the parabolic Hörmander’s condition, then for all
t\in(0,1]
,
Y_{t}
admits a smooth density with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Remark 1.3
.

Theorem
1.2
is still true when the Stratonovich integral is replaced by Itô’s integral in the equation.

Under the same assumptions as theorem
1.2
with
W_{t}
replaced by a fractional Brownian motion (fBm) with Hurst parameter
H>1/2
, F. Baudoin and M. Hairer
[
2
]
proved that
Y_{t}
admits a smooth density. With recent developments on Gaussian rough paths, we can consider (
1
) with
\boldsymbol{X}_{t}
given by a general non-degenerate Gaussian rough path. It was proved in
[
3
]
, that the
Y_{t}
admits a density if
\{V_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq d}
satisfy the parabolic Hörmander’s condition. Smoothness of the density was proved later in
[
4
]
after the tail estimate for the associated Jacobian process and a deterministic Norris’s lemma were established (see
[
5
]
,
[
11
]
).

The major tool that all above-mentioned results heavily rely on is the Malliavin calculus, which is a successful application of differential measure theory to Radon Gaussian measures. Despite being sufficiently flexible to obtain related results for a number of extensions of the original problem, Malliavin calculus cannot be applied to problems without an underlying Gaussian structure. As a result, contrary to the rapid developments of its Gaussian counterpart, the study of rough differential equations driven by Markovian rough paths progresses rather slowly. In
[
6
]
, I. Chevyrev and M. Ogrodnik used analysis on manifolds to prove that
Y_{t}
admits a density with respect to any smooth measure with assumptions strictly stronger than parabolic Hörmander’s condition on the vector fields. The question of the smoothness of the density is still open.

In this paper, we consider (
1
) with
\boldsymbol{X}_{t}
given by a Markovian rough path. Our goal is to prove that, with the parabolic Hörmander’s condition assumption on the vector fields,
Y_{t}
admits a smooth density with a Gaussian type upper bound. Unlike Gaussian processes, a general Markov process
X_{t}
may not have a smooth density. Thus, certain regularity assumption is necessary for the coefficients of its generator. We will see very soon that this extra regularity assumption provides an underlying Gaussian structure, which, in turn, makes Malliavin calculus applicable.

Now we can introduce our basic settings. We fix two constants
0<\lambda<\Lambda
. Let
a(x)
be a non-constant measurable function from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to the space of symmetric matrices which are uniformly elliptic with respect to
\lambda
and bounded by
\Lambda
, i.e.,

\lambda\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert}^{2}\leq\langle\xi,a(x)\xi\rangle\leq\Lambda\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert}^{2}

(2)

for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert}
is the Euclidean norm. We use
\Xi^{\lambda,\Lambda}
to denote all the functions that satisfy (
2
). Define the associated differential operator

L=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}(a_{i,j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}})

(3)

with domain
Dom(L)=\{f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\mid Lf\in L^{2}\}
. Let
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be the Markov process generated by
L
and define
Y_{t}
as the solution to

dY_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}V_{i}(Y_{t})d\boldsymbol{X}^{i}_{t}+V_{0}(Y_{t})dt,\;Y_{0}=y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;t\in[0,1].

We can now state our assumptions. First two are standard.

Assumption 1.4
.

The vector fields
\{V_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq d}
are smooth and bounded together with all their derivatives.

Assumption 1.5
.

The vector fields
\{V_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq d}
satisfy the parabolic Hörmander’s condition.

Our next assumption is about the regularity of
a(x)
that we mentioned earlier.

Assumption 1.6
.

The function
a(x)
is smooth and bounded together with all their derivatives.

The smoothness assumption on
a(x)
, though seems a bit restrictive at the first look, is actually necessary. Indeed, we can let
\{V_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq d}
be the columns of the identity matrix on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
V_{0}=0
. Obviously
\{V_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq d}
satisfy
Assumptions

1.4
and
1.5
. If we aim for a Hörmander’s type theorem we must have that
Y_{t}=X_{t}
has a smooth density. On the other hand, we know from classical analysis that, to obtain
k
th-order derivative on the density of
X_{t}
, one needs
a(x)
to be at least
(k-1)
th-order continuously differentiable. Consequently, the smoothness of
a(x)
is a necessary.

It is well-known that if
a(x)
is smooth,
X_{t}
is a diffusion process, whose stochastic differential equation can be written as follows:

dX_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}A_{i}(X_{t})dW^{i}_{t}+B(X_{t})dt,

where
A=\sqrt{a}
and
B=\nabla\cdot a
.

Central to all previous cases is the non-degeneracy of the driving signal
X_{t}
. It is not surprising that
A(x)
, which itself is an elliptic system, guarantees that
X_{t}
is non-degenerate in certain sense. However, we will see later that, exclusive to the non-Gaussian case, the non-degeneracy of the Malliavin derivative of
X_{t}
is also needed (see remark
3.5
). This motivates our final assumption. We want to emphasis that the next assumption is not necessary if
X_{t}
generated by (
3
) is a Gaussian process (e.g., when
a(x)
is constant). Hence, it constitutes the biggest difference between non-Gaussian case and Gaussian case.

Assumption 1.7
.

Let
A(x)
be the unique square root of
a(x)
. For a fixed positive constant
C_{J}
, the differential of
A(x)
, which is a smooth map
dA(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, does not vanish and satisfy the following uniform non-degenerate condition

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}\cdot dA(x)\right\rvert}^{2}\geq C_{J}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v\right\rvert}^{2},\;\forall x,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The main result of this paper is the following:

Theorem 1.8
.

Assume
a\in\Xi^{\lambda,\Lambda}
, let
X_{t}
be the Markov process whose generator is given by (
3
) with canonical rough lift
\boldsymbol{X_{t}}
. Consider the rough differential equation

Y_{t}=y_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}V_{i}(Y_{s})d\boldsymbol{X}^{i}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}V_{0}(Y_{s})ds,\;\;y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;t\in[0,1].

Suppose that
a(x)
is
not constant
and
Assumptions
1.4
,
1.5
,
1.6
and
1.7
are satisfied. Then for any
t\in(0,1]
,
Y_{t}
has a smooth density
p_{Y_{t}}(y)
with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Moreover,
p_{Y_{t}}(y)
has the following Gaussian type upper bound,

p_{Y_{t}}(y)\leq C_{1}(t)\exp(-\frac{C_2 (y-y_0)^2}{t}).

The fundamental argument for our proof is again the classical Malliavin calculus adapted to the rough paths theory, which involves the study of the Malliavin derivative of
Y_{t}
and a small ball estimate for
X_{t}
. The main difficulty in the our case is the extra integral structure in the Malliavin derivative of
Y_{t}
, which never appeared in any previous cases as explained in remark
3.4
. We will tackle this by developing a non-degenerate property of the Jacobian process of
X_{t}
.

There is another important point that we would like to emphasis. We shall see later that the majority of our proofs can be completely carried out in the language of classical stochastic calculus. This is due to the fact that
X_{t}
and its Malliavin derivative are both diffusion and rough integrals against diffusion coincide with the corresponding Stratonovich integrals. We will frequently take advantage of this fact in our proofs. From this perspective, our results can be viewed as an application of stochastic calculus to a rough path problem. However, we will continue to present our results in the rough paths setting. The reason is twofold. On one hand, formula (
8
), which is crucial for future studies in more general settings, can only be generalized as rough integrals. On the other hand, results like proposition
4.2
are, in fact, deterministic and point-wise, which are stronger than their stochastic counterparts. We prefer to keep them in this stronger form.

2
Preliminary material

2.1
Rough paths

For
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}]
, we define the space of
\alpha
-Hölder rough path on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, in symbols
\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
, as those pairs
(X,\mathbb{X})=\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\boldsymbol{X}\in C([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d}\oplus(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes 2})
such that

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X\right\rVert}_{\alpha}=\sup_{s\neq t\in[0,1]}\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert X_{s,t}\right\rvert}}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert}^{\alpha}}<+\infty,\;\;\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathbb{X}\right\rVert}_{2\alpha}=\sup_{s\neq t\in[0,1]}\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathbb{X}_{s,t}\right\rvert}}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert}^{2\alpha}}<+\infty,

where
X_{s,t}=X_{t}-X_{s}
and similarly for
\mathbb{X}_{s,t}
. Moreover, for
0\leq s\leq u\leq t\leq 1
,

\mathbb{X}_{s,t}=\mathbb{X}_{s,u}+\mathbb{X}_{u,t}+X_{s,u}\otimes X_{u,t}.

We equip
\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
with the rough metric

\rho_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{X},\boldsymbol{Y})=\sup_{s\neq t\in[0,1]}\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert X_{s,t}-Y_{s,t}\right\rvert}}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert}^{\alpha}}+\sup_{s\neq t\in[0,1]}\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathbb{X}_{s,t}-\mathbb{Y}_{s,t}\right\rvert}}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert}^{2\alpha}},

and define
\rho_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{X})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\rho_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{X},0)
. Note that if
X\in BV([0,1];\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\mathbb{X}
can be canonically defined as

\mathbb{X}_{s,t}=\int_{s}^{t}X_{s,r}dX_{r},

where the integral is understood as Riemann–Stieltjes integral and
(X,\mathbb{X})\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
\alpha\in(0,1]
. A rough path
\boldsymbol{X}\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
is said to be a geometric
\alpha
-Hölder rough path if we can find a sequence
\{X^{k}\}_{k\geq 1}\in BV([0,1];\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\rho_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{X},\boldsymbol{X}^{k})\rightarrow 0.

(4)

Another important notion is the so called controlled rough path. Let
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}]
and
X_{t}\in C^{\alpha}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
be an
\alpha
-Hölder continuous path. For a Banach space
W
, we say
Y_{t}\in C^{\alpha}([0,1],W)
, is controlled by
X_{t}
if we can find
Y^{\prime}_{t}\in C^{\alpha}([0,1],L(\mathbb{R}^{d},W))
such that the remainder term
R^{Y}
given by

Y_{s,t}=Y^{\prime}_{s}X_{s,t}+R^{Y}_{s,t},

satisfies
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert R^{Y}\right\rVert}_{2\alpha}<+\infty
, where
L(\mathbb{R}^{d},W)
is the space of all linear operators from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to
W
. We denote the space of all
W
-valued controlled paths
\mathscr{D}^{2\alpha}_{X}(W)
, and endow it with the semi-norm

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert Y,Y^{\prime}\right\rVert}_{X,2\alpha}=\mathinner{\!\left\lVert Y^{\prime}\right\rVert}_{\alpha}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert R^{Y}\right\rVert}_{2\alpha}.

\mathscr{D}^{2\alpha}_{X}
becomes a Banach space with the norm
(Y,Y^{\prime})\mapsto\mathinner{\!\left\lvert Y_{0}\right\rvert}+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert Y^{\prime}_{0}\right\rvert}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert Y,Y^{\prime}\right\rVert}_{X,2\alpha}
. Controlled paths are stable under composition with with regular functions. In fact, it is easy to check that if
f\in C^{2}_{b}(W)
, then
f(Y_{t})\in\mathscr{D}^{2\alpha}_{X}
with

f(Y_{t})^{\prime}=f^{\prime}(Y_{t})Y_{t}^{\prime}.

If
Y_{t}
is controlled by
X_{t}
with
W=L(\mathbb{R}^{d},V)
for a Banach space
V
. Then the rough integral of
Y_{t}
against
X_{t}
is defined as

\int_{0}^{t}Y_{s}d\boldsymbol{X}_{s}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lim_{{\Delta_{n}}\rightarrow 0}\sum_{i}Y_{t_{n_{i}}}\cdot X_{t_{n_{i}},t_{n_{i+1}}}+Y^{\prime}_{t_{n_{i}}}\cdot\mathbb{X}_{t_{n_{i}},t_{n_{i+1}}},

where
\{t_{n_{i}}\}_{i\geq 1}
is a sequence of partitions of
[0,t]
with mesh
\Delta_{n}
. Note that we use the canonical injection
L(\mathbb{R}^{d},L(\mathbb{R}^{d},V))\xhookrightarrow{}L(\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d},V)
in writing
Y^{\prime}_{t_{n_{i}}}\cdot\mathbb{X}_{t_{n_{i}},t_{n_{i+1}}}
. The rough integral can be seen as a map from
\mathscr{D}^{2\alpha}_{X}(W)
to
\mathscr{D}^{2\alpha}_{X}(V)
. Since

\left(\int_{0}^{t}Y_{s}d\boldsymbol{X}_{s},Y_{t}\right)\in\mathscr{D}^{2\alpha}_{X}(V).

This map is in fact continuous and we have the estimate

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\left(\int_{0}^{t}Y_{s}d\boldsymbol{X}_{s},Y_{t}\right)\right\rVert}_{X,2\alpha}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert Y\right\rVert}_{\alpha}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert Y^{\prime}_{t}\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathbb{X}\right\rVert}_{2\alpha}+C\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X\right\rVert}_{\alpha}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert R^{Y}\right\rVert}_{2\alpha}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert Y^{\prime}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\alpha}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathbb{X}\right\rVert}_{2\alpha}\right),

where
C
is a positive constant depends on
\alpha
.

Finally, let
f\in C^{2}_{b}(V,L(\mathbb{R}^{d},V))
. We may consider the rough differential equation driven by
\boldsymbol{X}
given by

Z_{t}=z_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}f(Z_{s})d\boldsymbol{X}_{s}.

(5)

A process
Z_{t}\in V
is said to be a solution (
5
) if
Z_{t}\in\mathscr{D}^{2\alpha}_{X}(V)
and the integral in (
5
) holds as a rough integral. We may also consider more general equations like

dY_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}V_{i}(Y_{t})d\boldsymbol{X}^{i}_{t}+V_{0}(Y_{t})dt,\;Y_{0}=y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;t\in[0,1].

For simplicity, We will follow the usual convention and write

Y_{t}=\pi_{V}(0,y_{0};\boldsymbol{X})(t),\ t\in[0,1].

The map
\pi
is called the Itô-Lyons map. Moreover, if
\boldsymbol{X}
is a geometric
\alpha
-Hölder rough path, then we have

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\pi_{V}(0,y_{0};\boldsymbol{X})-\pi_{V}(0,y_{0};\boldsymbol{X}^{k})\right\rVert}_{\alpha}=0,

where
\{\boldsymbol{X}^{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
is any sequence such that (
4
) is satisfied.

2.2
Markovian rough paths

Central to our purpose is the Markovian rough paths. Let
X_{t}
be the Markov process generated by (
3
). For Let
t\in(0,1]
and
\{D_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of increasing partitions of interval
[0,t]
with
\Delta_{n}\rightarrow 0
. Define

K^{n}_{i,j}(X)_{t}=\sum_{t_{k}\in D_{n}}\frac{X^{i}_{t_{k+1}}+X^{i}_{t_{k}}}{2}(X^{j}_{t_{k+1}}-X^{j}_{t_{k}}),

then
K_{i,j}(X)_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}K^{n}_{i,j}(X)_{t}
exists in probability, and the couple
\boldsymbol{X}_{t}=(X_{t},K_{t})
is a geometric rough path in
\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
(see
[
15
]
).
\boldsymbol{X}_{t}
is called the canonical rough lift of

.

Remark 2.1
.

It is worth mentioning that there are other equivalent ways to construct the rough lift of
X_{t}
, see for example
[
8
]
.

2.3
Malliavin calculus

We collect some basic materials in Malliavin calculus and refer to
[
18
]
for a complete exploration.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{t}
be the filtration generated by a Brownian motion
W_{t}
and
\mathcal{H}=W^{1,2}_{0}([0,1])
be the Cameron-Martin space of
W_{t}
. A
\mathcal{F}_{1}
-measurable random variable
F
is said to be cylindrical if it has the form

F=f(W_{t_{1}},W_{t_{2}},\cdots,W_{t_{n}}),

where
f\in C^{\infty}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
and
\{t_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}\in[0,1]
. We denote the collection of all cylindrical random variables
\mathcal{S}
.

The Malliavin derivative of
F
is defined as

DF=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}f(W_{t_{1}},W_{t_{2}},\cdots,W_{t_{n}})1_{[0,t_{i}]},

where
1_{[0,t_{i}]}
is the indicator function of interval
[0,t_{i}]
. For
h\in\mathcal{H}
, we have the following relation between directional derivatives of
F
and
DF
:

D_{h}F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\langle DF,\dot{h}\rangle_{L^{2}([0,1];\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{f(W_{t_{1}}+\epsilon h_{t_{1}},\cdots,W_{t_{n}}+\epsilon h_{t_{n}})-f(W_{t_{1}},\cdots,W_{t_{n}})}{\epsilon},

where
\dot{h}
is the derivative of
h
. It is often useful to use the canonical isometry between
\mathcal{H}
and
L^{2}([0,1])
and take
DF
as an element in
\mathcal{H}
. (In fact, on abstract Wiener spaces, Malliavin derivatives are defined to be random variables in the corresponding Cameron-Martin space; the usual setting for Brownian motion is a very special case.) One could then iterate the previous definition the define the
n
-th Malliavin derivative of
D^{n}F
which takes value in
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
.

For any
p\geq 1
, it is possible to prove that
D^{n}
is closable from
\mathcal{S}
to
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n})
. We denote by
\mathbb{D}^{n,p}
the closure of
\mathcal{S}
with respect to the norm

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert F\right\rVert}_{n,p}=\left(\mathbb{E}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert F\right\rvert}^{p}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert D^{i}F\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes i}}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and
\mathbb{D}^{\infty}=\cap_{n\geq 1}\cap_{p\geq 1}\mathbb{D}^{n,p}
.

If the vector fields
\{A_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq d},B\in C^{\infty}_{b}
, then for any
t\in[0,1]
,
X_{t}
defined by

dX_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}A_{i}(X_{t})dW^{i}_{t}+B(X_{t})dt,X_{0}=x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

belongs to
\mathbb{D}^{\infty}
. Moreover, let
A=(A_{1},A_{2},\cdots,A_{d})
then
DX_{t}
satisfies

D_{r}X_{t}=A(X_{r})+\int_{0}^{t}DA_{i}(X_{s})\cdot D_{r}X_{s}dW^{i}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}DB(X_{s})\cdot D_{r}X_{s}ds,

for
r\leq t
and
D_{r}X_{t}=0
for
s>t
a.s.

Recall the Jacobian process associated with
X_{t}
is given by

J^{X}_{t\leftarrow 0}=I_{d\times d}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}DA_{i}(X_{s})\cdot J^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}dW^{i}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}DB(X_{s})\cdot J^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}ds,\ t\in[0,1].

The inverse of
J^{X}_{t\leftarrow 0}
, written as
J^{X}_{0\leftarrow t}
, is well defined, and we have the following composition property:

J^{X}_{t\leftarrow u}\cdot J^{X}_{u\leftarrow s}=J^{X}_{t\leftarrow s},

for
s,u,t\in[0,1]
. The following integrability result is well-known and we refer to theorem 7.2 and remark 7.3 of
[
4
]
for a proof.

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose that
\{A_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq d},B\in C^{\infty}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Define
\Phi_{t}=(X_{t},J^{X}_{t\leftarrow 0},J^{X}_{0\leftarrow t})
. Then for any
\gamma\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, we have
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi\right\rVert}_{\gamma}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for any
p\geq 1
. In particular,
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi\right\rVert}_{\infty}=\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\Phi(t)\right\rvert}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for any
p\geq 1
.

By uniqueness, it is straightforward to see that, for any
r\leq t
we have

D_{r}X_{t}=J^{X}_{t\leftarrow r}A(X_{r}).

(6)

Another fundamental object in Malliavin calculus is given by the next

Definition 2.3
.

Let
F=(F^{1},F^{2},\cdots,F^{d})
be a random vector with whose components belong to
\mathbb{D}^{1,1}
. The Malliavin matrix
\Gamma(F)
is defined as

\Gamma(F)=(\langle DF^{i},DF^{j}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}})_{1\leq i,j\leq d}.

An estimate on the Malliavin matrix allows one to prove the existence and smoothness of density.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
F=(F^{1},F^{2},\cdots,F^{d})
be a random vector whose components belong to
\mathbb{D}^{\infty}
. If
(\det\Gamma(F))^{-1}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for all
p\geq 1
, then

F
has a smooth density with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

3
Malliavin derivative of
Y_{t}

This section is devoted to the computation of the Malliavin derivative of
Y_{t}
defined in theorem
1.8
. Let
\{D_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of increasing partitions of
[0,t]
, with the mesh
\Delta_{n}\rightarrow 0
. For any process
Z
defined on
[0,t]
, we use
Z^{n}
to represent the piece-wise linear approximation of
Z
along
D_{n}
.

Similar to
X_{t}
, the associated Jacobian process
J^{Y}_{t\leftarrow 0}
is a
d\times d
matrix-valued process given by

dJ^{Y}_{t\leftarrow 0}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}DV_{i}(Y_{t})J^{Y}_{t\leftarrow 0}d\boldsymbol{X}^{i}_{t}+DV_{0}(Y_{t})J^{Y}_{t\leftarrow 0}dt,\;J^{Y}_{0\leftarrow 0}=I_{d\times d},\;t\in[0,1].

Now let us write

Y_{t}(\omega)=y_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}V_{i}(Y_{s})d\boldsymbol{X}_{s}^{i}(\omega)+\int_{0}^{t}V_{0}(Y_{s})ds,\;y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;t\in[0,1],

where
\omega
represents the underlying Brownian paths. For every
h\in\mathcal{H}
, we have by definition

D_{h}Y^{j}_{t}=\langle DY^{j}_{t},\dot{h}\rangle_{L^{2}([0,1])}=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{Y^{j}_{t}(\omega+\epsilon h)-Y^{j}_{t}(\omega)}{\epsilon},\;\;1\leq j\leq d.

(7)

Here

Y_{t}(\omega+\epsilon h)=y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}V_{0}(Y_{s}(\omega+\epsilon h))ds+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}V_{i}(Y_{s}(\omega+\epsilon h))d\boldsymbol{X}(\omega+\epsilon h)^{i}_{s}.

Note that
X(\omega+\epsilon h)
is the diffusion given by

X_{t}(\omega+\epsilon h)=x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}A_{i}(X_{s}(\omega+\epsilon h))d(W+\epsilon h)^{i}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}B(X_{s}(\omega+\epsilon h))ds.

Hence, the rough lift
\boldsymbol{X}(\omega+\epsilon h)_{t}
is well defined.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
J^{Y}_{t\leftarrow 0}
be the Jacobian process of
Y_{t}
. Under assumptions of theorem
1.8
and for all
t\in[0,1]
,
h\in\mathcal{H}
, we have

D_{h}Y_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}J^{Y}_{t\leftarrow 0}\int_{0}^{t}J^{Y}_{0\leftarrow s}V_{i}(Y_{s})d\boldsymbol{D_{h}X}^{i}_{s}.

almost surely.

Remark 3.2
.

Although this result is straightforward, to the author’s best knowledge it is not written in any standard reference. We include a proof for completeness.

Proof.

We first note that,
X^{n}
has bounded variation almost surely. Thus,
\pi_{V}(0,y_{0};\boldsymbol{X^{n}})
is just the classical Itô map and we will simply write it as
\pi_{V}(0,y_{0};X^{n})
. We know that
\pi_{V}(0,y_{0};\boldsymbol{X})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{V}(0,y_{0};X^{n})
uniformly. As a result

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{Y_{t}(\omega+\epsilon h)-Y_{t}(\omega)}{\epsilon}=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\pi_{V}(0,y_{0};(X+\epsilon h)^{n})(t)-\pi_{V}(0,y_{0};X^{n})(t)}{\epsilon}.

On the other hand, we have

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{(X+\epsilon h)^{n}-X^{n}}{\epsilon}=D_{h}X^{n}.

By Duhamel’s principle of ODE (see for example
[
7
]
section 4.1 and 4.2), we have

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{\pi_{V}(0,y_{0};(X+\epsilon h)^{n})(t)-\pi_{V}(0,y_{0};X^{n})(t)}{\epsilon}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}J^{Y_{n}}_{t\leftarrow s}V_{i}(Y_{n}(s))d(D_{h}X^{n})^{i}_{s},

where
Y_{n}(t)=\pi_{V}(0,y_{0};X^{n})(t)
. Finally, sending
n
to infinity gives

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{Y_{t}(\omega+\epsilon h)-Y_{t}(\omega)}{\epsilon}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}J^{Y}_{t\leftarrow s}V_{i}(Y_{s})d\boldsymbol{D_{h}X}^{i}_{s}.

∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
J^{Y}_{t\leftarrow 0}
be the Jacobian process of
Y_{t}
. Under assumptions of theorem
1.8
and for all
t\in[0,1]
and
1\leq j\leq d
, we have

D^{j}_{r}Y_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}J^{Y}_{t\leftarrow 0}\int_{0}^{t}J^{Y}_{0\leftarrow s}V_{i}(Y_{s})d\boldsymbol{D}^{j}_{r}\boldsymbol{X}^{i}_{s}.

(8)

Proof.

By definition we have

D_{h}X^{i}_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle D^{j}_{r}X^{i}_{t},\dot{h}^{j}_{r}\rangle_{L^{2}([0,1])}.

With same notations as previous proposition, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}J^{Y_{n}}_{t\leftarrow s}V_{i}(Y_{n}(s))d(D_{h}X^{n})^{i}_{s}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}J^{Y_{n}}_{t\leftarrow s}V_{i}(Y_{n}(s))d(D^{j}_{r}X^{n})^{i}_{s},\dot{h}^{j}_{r}\rangle_{L^{2}([0,1])}.

Taking the limit and we immediately have

D^{j}_{r}Y_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}J^{Y}_{t\leftarrow 0}\int_{0}^{t}J^{Y}_{0\leftarrow s}V_{i}(Y_{s})d\boldsymbol{D}^{j}_{r}\boldsymbol{X}^{i}_{s}.

∎

Remark 3.4
.

We can make a quick comparison between (
8
) and (
6
). Note that if
a(x)\equiv I_{d\times d}
, then
X_{t}
is a standard Brownian motion and
D_{r}X_{t}=I_{d\times d}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{[0,t]}(r)
. If we fix
r
,
D_{r}X_{t}
is diagonal and a pure jump process with a jump at
r
. By (
8
) we have

D^{j}_{r}Y_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}J^{Y}_{t\leftarrow 0}\int_{0}^{t}J^{Y}_{0\leftarrow s}V_{i}(Y_{s})d\boldsymbol{D}^{j}_{r}\boldsymbol{X}^{i}_{s}=J^{Y}_{t\leftarrow r}V_{j}(Y_{r}),

(9)

which recovers (
6
). We see that in (
9
), the rough integral degenerates into evaluation at a single point and this happens when
X_{t}
is Gaussian; because Malliavin derivatives of Gaussian processes are always pure jump processes when
r
is fixed. Our goal is to study the more general rough integral formula (
8
); that is why we assume that
a(x)
is not constant.

Remark 3.5
.

Now is a good time tp further discuss the motivation of
Assumptions

1.7
. One crucial step in the proof of a Hörmander’s type theorem is the implication that

\left\{\inf_{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v\right\rVert}=1}v^{T}\cdot\langle DY_{t},DY_{t}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\cdot v\leq\epsilon\right\}\Rightarrow\left\{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J^{Y}_{0\leftarrow s}V_{i}(Y_{s})\right\rVert}_{\infty}\leq\epsilon^{\alpha}\right\},

(10)

for some
\alpha>0
and
1\leq i\leq d
. When
X_{t}
is Brownian motion, this step was done using a non-property of the
L^{2}
norm (see lemma A.3 of
[
9
]
). For the case where
X_{t}
is a non-degenerate Gaussian process, this is done by an interpolation inequality (see theorem 6.9 of
[
4
]
). Since we have
D_{r}Y_{t}=J^{Y}_{t\leftarrow r}V(Y_{r})
when
X_{t}
is Gaussian, in all previous cases (
10
) can be roughly understood as

\left\{\inf_{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v\right\rVert}=1}v^{T}\cdot\langle DY_{t},DY_{t}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\cdot v\leq\epsilon\right\}\Rightarrow\left\{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert DY_{t}\right\rVert}_{\infty}\leq\epsilon^{\alpha}\right\}.

However, in our case, due to the integral representation (
8
), same type of argument only gives

\left\{\inf_{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v\right\rVert}=1}v^{T}\cdot\langle DY_{t},DY_{t}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\cdot v\leq\epsilon\right\}\Rightarrow\left\{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}J^{Y}_{0\leftarrow s}V_{i}(Y_{s})d\boldsymbol{D}_{r}\boldsymbol{X}^{i}_{s}\right\rVert}_{\infty}\leq\epsilon^{\beta}\right\},

for some
\beta>0
,
1\leq i\leq d
. We still need to further justify that

\left\{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}J^{Y}_{0\leftarrow s}V_{i}(Y_{s})d\boldsymbol{D}_{r}\boldsymbol{X}^{i}_{s}\right\rVert}_{\infty}\leq\epsilon^{\beta}\right\}\Rightarrow\left\{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J^{Y}_{0\leftarrow s}V_{i}(Y_{s})\right\rVert}_{\infty}\leq\epsilon^{\alpha}\right\}.

(11)

To prove (
11
), it requires
D_{r}X_{s}=J^{X}_{s\leftarrow r}A(X_{r})
to be non-degenerate. Since
A
is an elliptic system, the behavior of
D_{r}X_{s}
essentially depends on the Jacobian process of
X_{t}
. Recall

dJ^{X}_{t\leftarrow 0}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}DA_{i}(X_{t})J^{X}_{t\leftarrow 0}dW^{i}_{t}+DB(X_{t})J^{X}_{t\leftarrow 0}dt.

Thus, a control on
(DA_{1},DA_{2},\cdots,DA_{d})=dA
will be sufficient to give the desired non-degeneracy. From this perspective,
Assumptions

1.7
is very natural.

4
Small ball estimate

This section is devoted to developing technical tools necessary for the proof of our main result.

4.1
Hölder roughness of diffusion

Norris’ type lemmas are crucial in the proofs of Hörmander’s type theorems. In classical stochastic analysis theory, it is nothing but a quantitative version of the Doob-Meyer decomposition of semi-martingales. In the context of rough differential equations, we are going to use a deterministic version of Norris’ lemma, which first appeared in
[
11
]
and was improved in
[
4
]
. We start with a definition.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1)
. A path
X\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called
\theta
-Hölder rough if there exists a constant
c>0
such that for every
s
in
[0,1]
, every
\epsilon
in
(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, and every
\phi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\phi\right\rvert}=1
, there exists
t
in
[0,1]
such that
\epsilon/2<\mathinner{\!\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert}<\epsilon
and

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\langle\phi,X_{s,t}\rangle\right\rvert}>c\epsilon^{\theta}.

The largest such constant is called the modulus of
\theta
-roughness, and is denoted by
L_{\theta}(X)
.

Now we can state the Norris type result that we are going to use and refer to
[
4
]
for a proof.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\boldsymbol{X}
be a geometric
\alpha
-Hölder rough path and assume that
X
is a
\theta
-Hölder rough with
2\alpha>\theta
. Let
Y
be a
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued path controlled by
X
, and set

Z_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}Y^{i}_{s}d\boldsymbol{X}^{i}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}b_{s}ds,

where
b
is an
\alpha
-Hölder continuous function. Then there exists constants
l>0
and
q>0
such that, setting

\mathcal{A}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=1+L_{\theta}(X)^{-1}+\rho_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{X})+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert Y\right\rVert}_{\alpha}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert Y^{\prime}\right\rVert}_{\alpha}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert b\right\rVert}_{\alpha},

one has the bound

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert Y\right\rVert}_{\infty}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert b\right\rVert}_{\infty}\leq M\mathcal{A}^{q}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert Z\right\rVert}^{l}_{\infty}

where
M
depends on the dimension
d
and
Y
.

We aim to prove Hölder roughness for
X_{t}
in this subsection. Let
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v\right\rvert}=1
, then
v^{T}X_{s,t}
is given by

v^{T}X_{s,t}=\int_{s}^{t}v^{T}A_{i}(X_{l})dW^{i}_{l}+\int_{s}^{t}v^{T}B(X_{l})dl,\ \forall t\in[s,1].

We are able to prove the following small ball estimate.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
\{A_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq d}
form an elliptic system, then for any
s\in[0,1)
and
k\in(0,1)
, we can find
\epsilon_{0}>0
and constants
C,C^{\prime}>0
such that for all
0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
and
\delta\in(0,1-s)

\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v\right\rVert}=1}\sup_{t\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}X_{s,t}\right\rvert}\leq\epsilon\right)\leq C\exp\left\{-C^{\prime}\frac{\delta}{\epsilon^{2-2k}}\right\}.

Proof.

For a fixed
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
s\in[0,1)
, define
M_{s,r}(v)=v^{T}X_{s,r}
for
s\leq r
. Then

\sup_{t\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}X_{s,t}\right\rvert}=\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert M_{s,r}\right\rvert}.

Let
M^{m}_{s,r}
be the local martingale part of
M_{s,r}
. By uniform ellipticity, we can find a constant
C_{1}>0
such that

\langle M^{m}_{s,r},M^{m}_{s,r}\rangle_{s,s+\delta}\geq\delta\inf_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}A_{i}(X_{r})\right\rvert}^{2}\geq\delta C_{1},

(12)

where the bracket means the quadratic variation. We can therefore deduce that

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert M_{s,r}\right\rvert}\leq\epsilon\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert M_{s,r}\right\rvert}\leq\epsilon,\ \langle M^{m}_{s,r},M^{m}_{s,r}\rangle_{s,s+\delta}\geq C_{1}\delta\right).

By Itô’s formula, we have

M^{2}_{s,r}=2\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{s}^{r}M_{s,l}\cdot v^{T}A_{i}(X_{l})dW^{i}_{l}+2\int_{s}^{r}M_{s,l}\cdot v^{T}B(X_{l})dl+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{s}^{r}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}A_{i}(X_{l})\right\rvert}^{2}dl.

(13)

Since
\{A_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq d},B\in C^{\infty}_{b}
, we can find
C_{B}>0
such that

\left\{\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert M_{s,r}\right\rvert}\leq\epsilon\right\}\Rightarrow\left\{\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{s}^{r}M_{s,l}\cdot v^{T}B(X_{l})dl\right\rvert}\leq\delta C_{B}\epsilon\right\}\ \&\ \left\{\int_{s}^{s+\delta}M_{s,l}^{2}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}A_{i}(X_{l})\right\rvert}^{2}dl\leq\delta C^{2}_{B}\epsilon^{2}\right\}.

(14)

By the second term of the last implication we have the following decomposition for
k\in(0,1)
and
1\leq i\leq d

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert M_{s,r}\right\rvert}^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert M_{s,r}\right\rvert}^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2},\int_{s}^{s+\delta}M_{s,l}^{2}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}A_{i}(X_{l})\right\rvert}^{2}dl\leq\delta C^{2}_{B}\epsilon^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert M_{s,r}\right\rvert}^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2},\int_{s}^{s+\delta}M_{s,l}^{2}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}A_{i}(X_{l})\right\rvert}^{2}dl\leq\delta C^{2}_{B}\epsilon^{2},\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{s}^{r}M_{s,l}\cdot v^{T}A_{i}(X_{l})dW^{i}_{l}\right\rvert}>\delta\epsilon^{k}\right)

(15)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert M_{s,r}\right\rvert}^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2},\int_{s}^{s+\delta}M_{s,l}^{2}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}A_{i}(X_{l})\right\rvert}^{2}dl\leq\delta C^{2}_{B}\epsilon^{2},\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{s}^{r}M_{s,l}\cdot v^{T}A_{i}(X_{l})dW^{i}_{l}\right\rvert}\leq\delta\epsilon^{k}\right).

(16)

For (
15
), we have by the exponential inequality for martingales (see,
[
19
]
, p. 153 ) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\left(\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert M_{s,r}\right\rvert}^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2},\int_{s}^{s+\delta}M_{s,l}^{2}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}A_{i}(X_{l})\right\rvert}^{2}dl\leq\delta C^{2}_{B}\epsilon^{2},\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{s}^{r}M_{s,l}\cdot v^{T}A_{i}(X_{l})dW^{i}_{l}\right\rvert}>\delta\epsilon^{k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\int_{s}^{s+\delta}M_{s,l}^{2}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}A_{i}(X_{l})\right\rvert}^{2}dl\leq\delta C^{2}_{B}\epsilon^{2},\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{s}^{r}M_{s,l}\cdot v^{T}A_{i}(X_{l})dW^{i}_{l}\right\rvert}>\delta\epsilon^{k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp{-\frac{ \delta}{C^2_B\epsilon^{2-2k} }}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\left(\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert M_{s,r}\right\rvert}^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2},\int_{s}^{s+\delta}M_{s,l}^{2}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}A_{i}(X_{l})\right\rvert}^{2}dl\leq\delta C^{2}_{B}\epsilon^{2},\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{s}^{r}M_{s,l}\cdot v^{T}A_{i}(X_{l})dW^{i}_{l}\right\rvert}\leq\delta\epsilon^{k}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert M_{s,r}\right\rvert}^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2}\right)-2\exp{-\frac{ \delta}{C^2_B\epsilon^{2-2k} }}.

(17)

On the other hand, by using (
14
) and (
13
) we see that

\displaystyle\left\{\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert M_{s,r}\right\rvert}^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2},\int_{s}^{s+\delta}M_{s,l}^{2}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}A_{i}(X_{l})\right\rvert}^{2}dl\leq\delta C^{2}_{B}\epsilon^{2},\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{s}^{r}M_{s,l}\cdot v^{T}A_{i}(X_{l})dW^{i}_{l}\right\rvert}\leq\delta\epsilon^{k}\right\}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\left\{\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert M_{s,r}\right\rvert}^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2},\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{s}^{r}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}A_{i}(X_{l})\right\rvert}^{2}dl\leq\epsilon^{2}+2C_{B}\delta\epsilon+d\delta\epsilon^{k}\right\}.

(18)

From (
4.1
) and (

) we can deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\left(\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert M_{s,r}\right\rvert}^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2},\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{s}^{r}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}A_{i}(X_{l})\right\rvert}^{2}dl\leq\epsilon^{2}+2C_{B}\delta\epsilon+d\delta\epsilon^{k}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert M_{s,r}\right\rvert}^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2}\right)-2\exp{-\frac{ \delta}{C^2_B\epsilon^{2-2k} }}.

(19)

By (
12
), there exists
\epsilon_{1}>0
such that when

\displaystyle\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{s}^{r}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}A_{i}(X_{l})\right\rvert}^{2}dl=\langle M^{m}_{s,r},M^{m}_{s,r}\rangle_{s,s+\delta}\geq\delta C_{1}>\epsilon^{2}+2C_{B}\delta\epsilon+d\delta\epsilon^{k}.

(20)

Combining (

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert M_{s,r}\right\rvert}\leq\epsilon,\ \langle M^{m}_{s,r},M^{m}_{s,r}\rangle_{s,s+\delta}\geq\delta C_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert M_{s,r}\right\rvert}^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2},\sup_{r\in[s,s+\delta]}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{s}^{r}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}A_{i}(X_{l})\right\rvert}^{2}dl>\epsilon^{2}+2C_{B}\delta\epsilon+d\delta\epsilon^{k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\exp{-\frac{ \delta}{C^2_B\epsilon^{2-2k} }}.

Up to this point, all of our computations are done with

[
17
]
(page 127). Observe that if

is Lipschitz as a function of

. Moreover, since the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is compact, we can cover it with balls of radius

and the number of these ball can be chosen to be less than

for some constant

.
So, we have for any

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v\right\rVert}=1}\sup_{t-s\leq\delta}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}X_{s,t}\right\rvert}\leq\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{2}\theta^{d}}{\epsilon^{d}}\sup_{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v\right\rVert}=1}\mathbb{P}\bigg(\sup_{t-s\leq\delta}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}X_{s,t}\right\rvert}\leq 2\epsilon\bigg)+\mathbb{P}\bigg(\sup_{t-s\leq\delta}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert X_{s,t}\right\rvert}>\theta\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{2}\theta^{d}}{\epsilon^{d}}\exp{-\frac{ \delta}{C^2_B\epsilon^{2-2k} }}+C_{4}\exp{-C_5\theta^{\tau}}.

We used the exponential integrability of
\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert X_{s}\right\rvert}
in the last inequality (see for example proposition 2.9 of
[
1
]
). Finally, set

\theta=\frac{\sigma^{\frac{1}{\tau}}}{\epsilon^{\frac{2-2k}{\tau}}},

then we can find
\epsilon_{0}\leq\epsilon_{1}
such that for
\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v\right\rVert}=1}\sup_{t-s\leq\delta}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}X_{s,t}\right\rvert}\leq\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{2}\delta^{\frac{d}{\tau}}}{\epsilon^{\frac{(2-2k+\tau)d}{\tau}}}\exp{-\frac{ \delta}{C^2_B\epsilon^{2-2k} }}+C_{4}\exp{-C_5\frac{ \delta}{\epsilon^{2-2k} }}

\displaystyle\leq C_{6}\exp{-C_7\frac{ \delta}{\epsilon^{2-2k} }}.

∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Under the assumptions of the previous lemma, for any
\theta>\frac{1}{2}
, and
k\in(0,1)
such that
\theta>\frac{1}{2-2k}
, we have

\mathbb{P}(L_{\theta}(X)<\epsilon)\leq C_{1}\exp(-C_2\epsilon^{2k-2}).

In particular,
L^{-1}_{\theta}(X)\in L^{P}(\Omega)
, for any
p\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let us define

D_{\theta}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\inf_{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v\right\rVert}=1}\inf_{n\geq 1}\inf_{l\leq 2^{n}}\sup_{s,t\in I_{l,n}}\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}X_{s,t}\right\rvert}}{2^{-n\theta}};

and

\hat{D}_{\theta}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\inf_{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v\right\rVert}=1}\inf_{n\geq 1}\inf_{l\leq 2^{n}}\sup_{s=\frac{l}{2^{n}},t\in I_{l,n}}\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}X_{s,t}\right\rvert}}{2^{-n\theta}},

where

I_{l,n}=[\frac{l}{2^{n}},\frac{l+1}{2^{n}}].

Obviously we have
D_{\theta}(X)\geq\hat{D}_{\theta}(X)
. The exact same argument of lemma 3 of
[
11
]
can be applied here, from which we can deduce

L_{\theta}(X)>\frac{1}{2\cdot 8^{\theta}}D_{\theta}(X)\geq\frac{1}{2\cdot 8^{\theta}}\hat{D}_{\theta}(X).

Thus, it suffices to give estimate for
\hat{D}_{\theta}(X)
. By definition, we have

\mathbb{P}(\hat{D}_{\theta}(X)<\epsilon)\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}-1}\mathbb{P}(\inf_{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v\right\rVert}=1}\sup_{s=\frac{l}{2^{n}},t\in I_{l,n}}\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}X_{s,t}\right\rvert}}{2^{-n\theta}}<\epsilon).

When
\epsilon
is sufficiently small, we can apply the previous lemma to get

\mathbb{P}(\hat{D}_{\theta}(X)<\epsilon)\leq C_{1}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{n}\exp{-C_2\epsilon^{2k-2}2^{n(\theta(2-2k)-1) }}.

Since
\theta>\frac{1}{2-2k}
, we can find
C_{3},C_{4}>0
uniformly over
\epsilon\leq 1,\;n\geq 1
, such that

2^{n}\exp{-C_2\epsilon^{2k-2}2^{n(\theta(2-2k)-1) }}\leq\exp{C_3-C_4n\epsilon^{2k-2} }.

Thus,

\mathbb{P}(\hat{D}_{\theta}(X)<\epsilon)\leq C_{1}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\exp{C_3-C_4n\epsilon^{2k-2} }\leq C_{5}\exp{-C_6\epsilon^{2k-2} },

and the proof is finished.

∎

4.2
Non-degenerate property of Jacobian processes

Now, we move on to the study of non-degenerate property of the Jacobian process of
X_{t}
. For the sake of conciseness, we adopt the notions from
[
10
]
and introduce the following

Definition 4.5
.

A family of sets
\{E_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon\in[0,1]}\in\mathcal{F}
is said to be “almost true” if for any
p\geq 1
, we can find
C_{p}>0
such that

\mathbb{P}(E_{\epsilon})\geq 1-C_{p}\epsilon^{p}.

Similarly for “almost false”. Given two
such families of events A and B, we say that “A almost implies B” and we write
A\Rightarrow_{\epsilon}B
if
A\setminus B
is almost false.

It is straightforward to check that these “almost” implications are transitive and invariant under any reparametrisation of the form
\epsilon\mapsto\epsilon^{\alpha}
for
\alpha>0
.

Remark 4.6
.

A typical situation where this definition naturally appears is as follows. Suppose that
Z
is a random variable in some probability space such that
\mathbb{E}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert Z\right\rvert}^{p}<\infty
for any
p\geq 1
, then

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert Z\right\rvert}>\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)\leq\mathbb{E}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert Z\right\rvert}^{p}\epsilon^{p}.

In other words,
\left\{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert Z\right\rvert}\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right\}
is almost true. We will simply write it as
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert Z\right\rvert}\leq_{\epsilon}\frac{1}{\epsilon}
.

Our next result establishes a non-degenerate property of Jacobian process
J^{X}
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
f(s)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a diffusion controlled by
J^{X}
, such that for any
\gamma\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
we have
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{\gamma}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for any
p\geq 1
. Then

\left\{\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{0}^{r}f(s)^{T}d\boldsymbol{J^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}}\right\rvert}\leq\epsilon\right\}\Rightarrow_{\epsilon}\left\{\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f(r)\right\rvert}\leq C\epsilon^{\alpha}\right\},

for some constant
C>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Since
J^{X}
is a semi-martingale, we can consider

M_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{0}^{t}f(s)^{T}\circ dJ^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}.

One has the following representation (recall
f^{m}
means the local martingale part of
f
).

M_{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{r}f(s)^{T}\cdot DA_{i}(X_{s})J^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}dW^{i}_{s}+\int_{0}^{r}f(s)^{T}\cdot DB(X_{s})J^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}ds+\frac{1}{2}\langle f^{m},M^{m}\rangle_{[0,r]}.

By our assumptions and proposition
2.2
, it is easy to see
M_{r}
verifies the assumption of lemma 4.11 of
[
12
]
, from which we have

\left\{\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert M_{r}\right\rvert}\leq\epsilon\right\}\Rightarrow_{\epsilon}\left\{\max_{1\leq i\leq d}\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f(s)^{T}\cdot DA_{i}(X_{s})J^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}\right\rvert}<\epsilon^{\alpha^{\prime}}\right\}.

(21)

for some
\alpha^{\prime}\in(0,1)
. Moreover, since

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert J^{X}_{0\leftarrow s}\right\rvert}=\left\{\inf_{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v\right\rvert}=1}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert J^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}\cdot v\right\rvert}\right\}^{-1},

we have, by proposition
2.2
, for any
\eta>0
that

\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{s\in[0,1]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}\cdot J^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}\right\rvert}<\epsilon^{\eta}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v\right\rvert}\bigg)=\mathbb{P}\bigg(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi\right\rVert}_{\infty}>\frac{1}{\epsilon^{\eta}}\right)\leq C_{p}\epsilon^{\eta p}.

Thus, for
s\in[0,t]
we have

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f(s)^{T}\cdot DA_{i}(X_{s})J^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}\right\rvert}^{2}\geq_{\epsilon}\epsilon^{2\eta}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f(s)\cdot DA_{i}(X_{s})\right\rvert}^{2}.

(22)

Finally,
Assumption

1.7
gives

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f(s)^{T}\cdot DA_{i}(X_{s})\right\rvert}^{2}\geq C_{J}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f(s)\right\rvert}^{2}.

(23)

Combining (
21
) (
22
) (
23
) and use the fact that
\eta
is arbitrary, we deduce

\left\{\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{0}^{r}f(s)^{T}d\boldsymbol{J^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}}\right\rvert}\leq\epsilon\right\}\Rightarrow_{\epsilon}\left\{\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f(r)\right\rvert}\leq C\epsilon^{\alpha}\right\}

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

∎

With the previous proposition, we can show the desired non-degenerate property of
D_{r}X_{s}
as a family of processes index by
r
.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
f(s)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a diffusion controlled by
D_{r}X
for any
r\in[0,1]
and that for any
\gamma\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, we have
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{\gamma}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for all
p\geq 1
. Then for any
t\in[0,1]
we have

\left\{\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{0}^{t}f(s)^{T}d\boldsymbol{D}_{r}\boldsymbol{X}_{s}\right\rvert}\leq\epsilon\right\}\Rightarrow_{\epsilon}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f(s)\right\rvert}\leq\epsilon^{\alpha}.

Proof.

As before, we can write

\int_{0}^{t}f(s)^{T}\boldsymbol{D}_{r}\boldsymbol{X}_{s}=\int_{r}^{t}f(s)^{T}\circ dJ^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}\cdot J^{X}_{0\leftarrow r}A(X_{r}).

Since
A
is an elliptic system, we see immediately

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{r}^{t}f(s)^{T}\circ dJ^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}\cdot J^{X}_{0\leftarrow r}A(X_{r})\right\rvert}\geq C_{1}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{r}^{t}f(s)^{T}\circ dJ^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}\cdot J^{X}_{0\leftarrow r}\right\rvert}.

(24)

Moreover, we have

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert J^{X}_{0\leftarrow r}\right\rvert}=\left\{\inf_{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v\right\rvert}=1}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert J^{X}_{r\leftarrow 0}\cdot v\right\rvert}\right\}^{-1},

which implies that for any
\beta>0

\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v\right\rvert}=1}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert J^{X}_{r\leftarrow 0}\cdot v\right\rvert}\leq\epsilon^{\beta}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi\right\rVert}_{\infty}\geq\frac{1}{\epsilon^{\beta}})\leq C_{p}\epsilon^{p}.

By taking transpose, we have that

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{r}^{t}f(s)^{T}\circ dJ^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}\cdot J^{X}_{0\leftarrow r}\right\rvert}\geq_{\epsilon}\epsilon^{\beta}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{r}^{t}f(s)^{T}\circ dJ^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}\right\rvert}.

(25)

Combining (
24
) and (
25
) gives

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{0}^{t}f(s)^{T}\boldsymbol{D}_{r}\boldsymbol{X}_{s}\right\rvert}\geq_{\epsilon}C_{1}\epsilon^{\beta}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{r}^{t}f(s)^{T}\circ dJ^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}\right\rvert}.

As a result, we have for any
p\geq 1
that

\left\{\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{0}^{t}f(s)^{T}\boldsymbol{D}_{r}\boldsymbol{X}_{s}\right\rvert}\leq\epsilon\right\}\Rightarrow_{\epsilon}\left\{\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{r}^{t}f(s)^{T}\circ dJ^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}\right\rvert}\leq C_{1}\epsilon^{1-\beta}\right\}\Rightarrow\left\{\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{0}^{r}f(s)^{T}\circ dJ^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}\right\rvert}\leq C_{2}\epsilon^{1-\beta}\right\}.

From proposition
4.7
, we deduce

\left\{\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{0}^{r}f(s)^{T}\circ dJ^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}\right\rvert}\leq C_{2}\epsilon^{1-\beta}\right\}\Rightarrow_{\epsilon}\left\{\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f(r)\right\rvert}\leq C_{3}\epsilon^{(1-\beta)\alpha^{\prime}}\right\}

for some
\alpha^{\prime}\in(0,1)
. Since
\beta
is arbitrary and almost true implications are transitive, our result follows.

∎

We prepare another lemma for next section.

Lemma 4.9
.

If
f(s)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is a diffusion process controlled by
D_{r}X_{s}
such that

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{\infty},\ \mathinner{\!\left\lVert f^{M}\right\rVert}_{\infty}\in L^{p}(\Omega),\ \forall p\geq 1.

Then

F(r)=\int_{0}^{t}f(s)d\boldsymbol{D}_{r}\boldsymbol{X}_{s}

is
\gamma
-Hölder continuous for any
\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
and
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert F\right\rVert}_{\gamma}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for all
p\geq 1
.

Proof.

We have

F(r)=\int_{0}^{t}f(s)d\boldsymbol{D}_{r}\boldsymbol{X}_{s}=\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\circ dD_{r}X_{s}=\int_{r}^{t}f(s)\circ dD_{r}X_{s}\ a.s.

where we used the property that
D_{r}X_{s}=0
a.s. for
s<r
.
As a result, for
0\leq u\leq v\leq t
we have

F(u)-F(v)=\int_{v}^{t}f(s)\circ d(D_{u}X_{s}-D_{v}X_{s})+\int_{u}^{v}f(s)\circ dD_{u}X_{s}.

The first term on the right hand side can be written as

\Gamma_{1}=\int_{v}^{t}f(s)\circ dJ^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}\cdot(J^{X}_{0\leftarrow u}A(X_{u})-J^{X}_{0\leftarrow v}A(X_{v})).

Similarly, We can write the second term as

\Gamma_{2}=\int_{u}^{v}f(s)\circ dJ^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}\cdot J^{X}_{0\leftarrow u}A(X_{u}).

For
\Gamma_{1}
we have for any
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
p\geq 1

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\Gamma_{1}\right\rvert}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left\{\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\circ dJ^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}\right\rvert}^{p}\left(C_{B}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi\right\rVert}_{\alpha}(u-v)^{\alpha}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi\right\rVert}_{\infty}C_{B}(v-u)^{\alpha}\right)^{p}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2p-1}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left\{\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\circ dJ^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}\right\rvert}^{p}(C_{B}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi\right\rVert}_{\infty}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi\right\rVert}_{\alpha})^{p}\right\}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v-u\right\rvert}^{\alpha p}.

For
\Gamma_{2}
we have for any
p\geq 1

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\Gamma_{2}\right\rvert}^{p}\leq\mathbb{E}\left\{C_{B}^{p}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi\right\rVert}^{p}_{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{u}^{v}f(s)\circ dJ^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}\right\rvert}^{p}\right\}\leq C_{B}^{p}\mathbb{E}(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi\right\rVert}^{2p}_{\infty})^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{u}^{v}f(s)\circ dJ^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}\right\rvert}^{2p}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality, standard computation gives that for any
q\geq 2

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{u}^{v}f(s)\circ dJ^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}\right\rvert}^{q}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{u}^{v}f(s)d(J^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0})^{m}+\int_{u}^{v}f(s)d(J^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0})^{b}+\frac{1}{2}\langle f^{m},(J^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0})^{m}\rangle_{u,v}\right\rvert}^{q}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C_{q}\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{\infty}^{q}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi\right\rVert}_{\infty}^{q}C_{B}^{q}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v-u\right\rvert}^{\frac{q}{2}}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{\infty}^{q}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi\right\rVert}_{\infty}^{q}C_{B}^{q}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v-u\right\rvert}^{q}\right\}

\displaystyle+C^{\prime}_{q}(\mathbb{E}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f^{m}\right\rVert}_{\infty}^{2q})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{E}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi\right\rVert}_{\infty}^{2q}C_{B}^{2q})^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v-u\right\rvert}^{\frac{q}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq P_{q}(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{\infty},\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f^{m}\right\rVert}_{\infty},\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi\right\rVert}_{\infty},C_{B},)\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v-u\right\rvert}^{\alpha q},

where
P_{q}
is some polynomial depends on
q
. It is also relative easy to check that, under our assumptions

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\circ dJ^{X}_{s\leftarrow 0}\right\rvert}\in L^{p}(\Omega),\ \ \forall p\geq 1.

Hence, combining our estimate for
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
gives

\mathbb{E}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert F(v)-F(u)\right\rvert}^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}(\mathbb{E}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\Gamma_{1}\right\rvert}^{p}+\mathbb{E}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\Gamma_{2}\right\rvert}^{p})\leq C_{p}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v-u\right\rvert}^{\alpha p}.

We conclude with Kolmogorov continuity theorem and Besov–Hölder embedding (see theorem A.10 of
[
7
]
).
∎

Existence of smooth density and Gaussian type upper bound

5.1
Malliavin smoothness and integrability

Let
X_{t},Y_{t}
be the processes defined in theorem
1.8
. We need to show
Y_{t}\in\mathbb{D}^{\infty}
before we can apply proposition
2.4
. In general, it is not an easy task to show the Malliavin smoothness of solution to a rough differential equation (see
[
4
]
, proposition 7.5 and the note after). This result has been obtained for Gaussian rough paths in
[
14
]
, but the technique used there is very difficult to generalize to non-Gaussian rough paths. It would be interesting to investigate further in this direction.

For our purpose, however, we can again use the fact that rough integrals against
\boldsymbol{X_{t}}
coincide with Stratonovich integrals against
X_{t}
to our advantage. Indeed, the couple process
(X_{t},Y_{t})
is solution to a stochastic differential equation driven by Brownian motion. Since the vector fields

\frac{\partial Y_{t}}{\partial X_{0}}&J^{Y}_{t\leftarrow 0}\end{pmatrix}

with inverse

J^{X,Y}_{0\leftarrow t}=\begin{pmatrix}J^{X}_{0\leftarrow t}&0\\[6.0pt]
-J^{Y}_{0\leftarrow t}\frac{\partial Y_{t}}{\partial X_{0}}J^{X}_{0\leftarrow t}&J^{Y}_{0\leftarrow t}\end{pmatrix}.

Define

U_{t}=(X_{t},Y_{t},J^{X,Y}_{t\leftarrow 0},J^{X,Y}_{0\leftarrow t}),

then by proposition
2.2
, we know for any
0<\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
the

-Hölder constant
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert U_{t}\right\rVert}_{\gamma}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for all
p\geq 1
. Since

is solution to a rough differential equation driven by

with

vector fields,

is automatically a rough path controlled by
X_{t}
. Along the same lines of proposition 8.1 and corollary 8.2 of
[
4
]
, we know
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert Y,Y^{\prime}\right\rVert}_{X,2\gamma}
is in
L^{p}(\Omega)
for any
p\geq 1
. So we have

Proposition 5.1
.

Under the assumptions of theorem
1.8
. For any
\gamma\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, define

\mathcal{L}_{\theta}=\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}1+L_{\theta}(X)^{-1}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert M_{t}\right\rVert}_{\gamma}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert Y,Y^{\prime}\right\rVert}_{X,2\gamma}+\rho_{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{X}),

Proof of main results

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert[V_{i},V_{0}]\right\rVert}_{\infty}\;\&\;\;\mathinner{\!\left\lVert[V_{j},V_{i}]\right\rVert}_{\infty}\leq M\mathcal{L}_{\theta}^{q}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f_{v}\right\rVert}_{\infty}^{l}.

By induction, we can see

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v^{T}J^{Y}_{0\leftarrow s}W(Y_{t})\right\rVert}_{\infty}\leq C\mathcal{L}_{\theta}^{m(k)}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f_{v}\right\rVert}_{\infty}^{n(k)}

for all
W\in\mathcal{W}_{k}
, where
m(k),n(k)
are constants only depend on
k
.
Since
\{V_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq d}
satisfy the parabolic Hörmander’s condition, we can find
a_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle a_{0}=\inf_{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v\right\rVert}=1}\sum_{W\in\cup_{0\leq k\leq k_{0}}\mathcal{W}_{k}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v^{T}W(y_{0})\right\rvert}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v\right\rVert}=1}\sum_{W\in\mathcal{W}_{k}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v^{T}J^{Y}_{0\leftarrow s}W(Y_{s})\right\rVert}_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v\right\rVert}=1}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v^{T}J^{Y}_{0\leftarrow s}W(Y_{t})\right\rVert}_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v\right\rVert}=1}C\mathcal{L}_{\theta}^{m(k)}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f_{v}\right\rVert}_{\infty}^{n(k)}.

(28)

From (
27
) and (
5.2
) we have

\left\{\inf_{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v\right\rVert}=1}v^{T}C_{t}(Y)v<\epsilon\right\}\Rightarrow_{\epsilon}\left\{a_{0}\leq C\mathcal{L}_{\theta}^{m(k)}(2\mathcal{R}^{\frac{2\gamma}{2\gamma+1}}+2)^{\alpha n(k)}\epsilon^{\frac{\alpha n(k)}{2\gamma+1}}\right\}.

(29)

On the other hand, since
\mathcal{L}_{\theta},\mathcal{R}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for any
p\geq 1
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left\{a_{0}\leq C\mathcal{L}_{\theta}^{m(k)}(2\mathcal{R}^{\frac{2\gamma}{2\gamma+1}}+2)^{\alpha n(k)}\epsilon^{\frac{\alpha n(k)}{2\gamma+1}}\right\}\leq C_{p}\epsilon^{p}.

(30)

Hence, combining (
29
) (
30
), we see that when
\epsilon
is sufficiently small

\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v\right\rVert}=1}v^{T}C_{t}(Y)v\leq\epsilon\right)\leq C_{p}\epsilon^{p}.

(31)

This finishes the proof of existence of a smooth density.

Let
p_{Y_{t}}(y)
be the density of
Y_{t}
, we have the following upper bound (see proposition 2.1.4 and 2.1.5 in
[
18
]
)

p_{Y_{t}}(y)\leq C\cdot\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert Y_{s}-y_{0}\right\rvert}>\mathinner{\!\left\lvert y-y_{0}\right\rvert}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\det(\Gamma(Y_t))^{-1}\right\rVert}^{m}_{L^{k}(\Omega)}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert DY_{t}\right\rVert}^{l}_{h,\rho},

for some constants
m,l,h,\rho,k
. Consider
(X_{t},Y_{t})
as solution to a stochastic differential equation driven by Brownian motion, then by proposition 2.10 from
[
1
]
we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert Y_{s}-y_{0}\right\rvert}>\mathinner{\!\left\lvert y-y_{0}\right\rvert}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\exp(-\frac{C (y-y_0)^2}{t}).

We have just proved that
\det(\Gamma(Y_t))^{-1}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for all
p\geq 1
. Finally, for
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert DY_{t}\right\rVert}^{l}_{h,\rho}
the exact argument of lemma 4.1 in
[
1
]
applies to
(X_{t},Y_{t})
. Therefore
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert DY_{t}\right\rVert}^{l}_{h,\rho}<C(t)
, for some positive constant
C(t)
, and the proof is finished. ∎

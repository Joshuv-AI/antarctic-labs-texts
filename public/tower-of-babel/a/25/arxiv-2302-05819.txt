New Clebsch–Gordan-type integrals involving threefold products of complete elliptic integrals


Abstract
Multiple elliptic integrals related to the generalized Clebsch–Gordan (CG) integral are of importance in many areas in physics and special functions theory. Zhou has introduced and applied Legendre function-based techniques to prove symbolic evaluations for integrals of CG form involving twofold and threefold products of complete elliptic integral expressions, and this includes Zhou’s remarkable proof of an open problem due to Wan. The foregoing considerations motivate the results introduced in this article, in which we prove closed-form evaluations for new CG-type integrals that involve threefold products of the complete elliptic integrals K and E . Our methods are based on the use of fractional derivative operators, via a variant of a technique we had previously referred to as semi-integration by parts .



John M. Campbell

Keywords: multiple elliptic integral; complete elliptic integral; Clebsch–Gordan integral; closed form

2
Clebsch–Gordan theory

The CG coefficients are typically defined via the phenomenon of angular momentum coupling. Following
[
1
]
, we express that the CG
coefficients that have zero magnetic quantum numbers satisfy the following identity:

\left(C_{i0b0}^{c0}\right)^{2}=\frac{2c+1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}P_{i}(x)P_{b}(x)P_{c}(x)\,dx,

(18)

letting the orthogonal family of Legendre polynomials
be denoted as per usual. A common definition for Legendre polynomials
is via the binomial sum indicated as follows:
P_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{2}(x+1)^{k}(x-1)^{n-k}
.
In view of (
18
), and
as in the Zhou article
[
28
]
that is the main source of motivation behind our new results,
we may define
generalized Clebsch–Gordan integrals
to be of the form

\int_{-1}^{1}P_{\mu}(x)P_{\nu}(x)P_{\nu}(-x)\,dx

(19)

for
\mu
and
\nu
in
\mathbb{C}
,
and this terminology is also used in the article
[
10
]
relevant to much of our work.

The CG coefficients naturally arise in both the decomposition of a product of two spherical harmonics into spherical harmonics and, equivalently, in the
decomposition of a product of Legendre polynomials into Legendre polynomials
[
12
]
. So, by taking a product of three Legendre polynomials
and integrating this product, the CG coefficients naturally arise according to this latter decomposition.
From the product formula for
Y_{\ell_{1}}^{m_{1}}(\theta,\varphi)Y_{\ell_{2}}^{m_{2}}(\theta,\varphi)
for spherical harmonics in terms of Legendre polynomials, we may write

\displaystyle P_{\ell_{1}}^{m_{1}}(x)P_{\ell_{2}}^{m_{2}}(x)=

(20)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{(\ell_{1}+m_{1})!(\ell_{2}+m_{2})!}{(\ell_{1}-m_{1})!(\ell_{2}-m_{2})!}}\sum_{\ell_{12}}\sqrt{\frac{(\ell_{12}-m_{12})!}{(\ell_{12}+m_{12})!}}C_{m_{1},m_{2},m_{12}}^{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{12}}C_{0,0,0}^{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{12}}P_{\ell_{12}}^{m_{12}}(x),

(21)

referring to
[
12
]
for details.

Integrals of threefold products of Legendre polynomials of the form shown in (
19
) arise in the context of the evaluation of CG
coefficients in much the same way as in the classic identity in (
18
), and hence the appropriateness as to how series and integral
evaluations arising from or otherwise directly relating to integrals as in (
18
) and (
19
) may be referred to as being of CG
type, especially in view of the product identity shown in (
20
)–(
21
). From Fourier–Legendre expansions such as

\text{{\bf K}}\left(\sqrt{x}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2}{2n+1}P_{n}\left(2x-1\right),

(22)

the integration of products of complete elliptic-type expressions
often gives rise to CG coefficients via identities as in (
20
)–(
21
).

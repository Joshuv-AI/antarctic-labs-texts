Abstract
. This paper identifies certain interesting mathematical problems of stochastic quantization type in the modeling of Laser propagation through turbulent media. In some of the typical physical contexts the problem reduces to stochastic Schrödinger equation with space-time white noise of Gaussian, Poisson and Lévy type. We identify their mathematical resolution via stochastic quantization. Nonlinear phenomena such as Kerr effect can be modeled by stochastic nonlinear Schrodinger equation in the focusing case with space-time white noise. A treatment of stochastic transport equation, the Korteweg-de Vries Equation as well as a number of other nonlinear wave equations with space-time white noise is also given. Main technique is the S-transform (we will actually use closely related Hermite transform) which converts the stochastic partial differential equation with space time white noise to a deterministic partial differential equation defined on the Hida-Kondratiev white noise distribution space. We then utlize the inverse S-transform/Hermite transform known as the characterization theorem combined with the infinite dimensional implicit function theorem for analytic maps to establish local existence and uniqueness theorems for path-wise solutions of these class of problems. The particular focus of this paper on singular white noise distributions is motivated by practical situations where the refractive index fluctuations in propagation medium in space and time are intense due to turbulence, ionospheric plasma turbulence, marine-layer fluctuations, etc. Since a large class of partial differential equations that arise in nonlinear wave propagation have polynomial type nonlinearities, white noise distribution theory is an effective tool in studying these problems subject to different types of white noises.



Key words:
Laser propagation, stochastic nonlinear Schrödinger equation, stochastic quantization, space-time white noise, Wick product, paraxial equation, Korteweg-de Vries Equation, white noise calculus, S-transform, Benjamin-Ono equation, Schrödringer-Hartree equation, Zakharov system, Davey-Stewartson equation.

2.
Derivation of the paraxial equation from the Maxwell equations

In this section we will give a heuristic derivation of the paraxial equation starting from the Maxwell equation. The paraxial equation we derive is a very well-known model widely used in the literature on acoustic wave and electromagnetic wave propagation in random media
[
65
,
53
,
64
,
60
,
32
]
. Let
E,H,B,D
denote respectively the electric field, magnetizing field, magnetic field and the displacement field.The Maxwell equations are:

(2.1)

\nabla\times E=\frac{\partial B}{\partial t}

(2.2)

\nabla\times H=\frac{\partial D}{\partial t}

(2.3)

D=\epsilon E\mbox{ and }B=\mu H.

Here
\epsilon
is the permitivity and
\mu
is the permiability of the medium.
Taking curl of the first equation and taking time derivative of the second equation and substituting in the first we get upon using the vector identity
\nabla\times\nabla\times E=-\Delta E+\nabla(\nabla\cdot E)

(2.4)

\Delta E-\mu\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}(\epsilon E)=\nabla(\nabla\cdot E)

In the absence of free charge
\nabla\cdot D=0
and hence
E\cdot\nabla\epsilon+\epsilon\nabla\cdot E=0
and substituting we get

(2.5)

\Delta E-\mu\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}(\epsilon E)=-\nabla(E\cdot\nabla(log\epsilon))

We have noting
\epsilon(x,t)=\epsilon_{0}n^{2}(x,t)
where
n
is the refractive index and
c^{2}=1/(\mu\epsilon_{0})
with
c
the light speed, we arrive at

(2.6)

\Delta E(x,t)-\frac{1}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}(n^{2}(x,t)E(x,t))=-2\nabla(E(x,t)\cdot\nabla log(n(x,t))).

We assume that the time scale of fluctuations in the medium is much slower than the light speed and invoke further simplifications based on this assumption. Thus neglecting the right hand side and also
n^{2}
term out of time derivative we arrive at

(2.7)

\Delta E(x,t)-\frac{n^{2}(x,t)}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}E(x,t)=0.

Substituting a plane wave solution
E(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},t)=\psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},t)\exp(ikx_{3}-i\omega t)
and neglecting the back-scatter term
\frac{\partial^{2}\psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},t)}{\partial x_{3}^{2}}
(using simple scaling argument, see for example
[
53
]
) we arrive at the paraxial equation:

(2.8)

2ik\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x_{3}}+[\Delta_{\bot}+k^{2}\{\frac{n^{2}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},t)}{n_{0}^{2}}-1\}]\psi=0.

where
\Delta_{\bot}
is the two dimensional Laplacian in variables
x_{2},x_{3}
.
Renaming the time-like variable
x_{3}
as
t
and suppressing the actual time variable
t
we end up with the
two dimensional
linear or nonlinear stochastic Schrödinger equation:

(2.9)

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi(x,t,\omega)+[\Delta+V(x,t,\omega,\psi)]\psi(x,t,\omega)=0.

In this paper
V
will be modeled as a space-time Gaussian white noise
[
35
,
49
]
, Poisson white noise
[
38
,
39
,
4
]
or Levy type white noise
[
36
]
and the paraxial equation will then be framed as a stochastic quantization problem.

The dual space of

Theorem 3.1
.

A necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a probability measure

\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
and a functional
F
on
\mathcal{S}
such that

(3.3)

\int_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}e^{i<\omega,\phi>}\mu(d\omega)=F(\phi),\hskip 7.22743pt\forall\phi\in\mathcal{S}

is that

(1)

(2)

\sum_{j,l=1}^{n}z_{j}\bar{z}_{l}F(\phi_{j}-\phi_{l})\geq 0,\hskip 7.22743pt\forall z_{k}\in\mathbb{C},\forall\phi_{k}\in\mathcal{S},k=1,\cdots,n,

We will also utilize three particular cases:

(i) Gaussian measure

\int_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}e^{i<\omega,\phi>}\mu_{G}(d\omega)=\exp\{-\frac{1}{2}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\},\hskip 7.22743pt\forall\phi\in\mathcal{S},

Definition 3.1
.

(1)

The continuous version of the mapping

Definition 3.2
.

Each

3.2.

We will start with the characterization of the classical embedding of the Schwartz space in the space of tempered distributions as

(3.30)

Here

are the Fourier coefficients of

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}^{2}(\delta^{(j)})^{\gamma}<\infty,

for all
d
-dimensional multi-indices
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{d})
.

(ii) A distribution
T\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathrm{R}^{d})
is characterized by the expansion

(3.32)

(3.33)

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}b_{j}^{2}(\delta^{(j)})^{-\theta}<\infty,

for some
d
-dimensional multi-index

.

.

Let

. We say

3.3.
Wick products and properties

The Wick product is defined as below:

Definition 3.5

The Wick product

The fact that Wick product gives a distribution

follows from the estimate below (see

Now rewriting

(3.42)

F\diamond G:=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}a_{\alpha}\cdot b_{\beta}H_{\alpha+\beta}=\sum_{\gamma}(\sum_{\alpha+\beta=\gamma}a_{\alpha}\cdot b_{\beta})H_{\gamma},

and then setting

and

we have by Cauchy-Schwartz inequality:

\sum_{\gamma}(2\mathbb{N})^{-q\gamma}|c_{\gamma}|^{2}\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sum_{\gamma}(2\mathbb{N})^{-k\gamma}}}\right)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sum_{\alpha}|a_{\alpha}|^{2}(2\mathbb{N})^{-q_{1}\alpha}}}\right)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sum_{\beta}|b_{\beta}|^{2}(2\mathbb{N})^{-q_{1}\beta}}}\right)<\infty.

The first term on the right is finite for

due to a Lemma by Zhang
[
71
]
and the other two terms are finite by definition of the distributions

4.
Kato theory of quasilinear abstract evolution equations

All the problems considered in this paper, after Wick-quantization followed by Hermit (or

4.1.
Analytic Mappings between Banach Spaces and Inverse Mapping Theorem

Definition 4.2
.

A map
\Phi:Z_{1}\rightarrow Z_{2}
between Banach spaces

is called analytic in

(4.11)

\Phi(u)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Phi_{k}(u,u,\cdots,u),

where

\|\Phi_{k}(\cdot)\|=\sup\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\|\Phi_{k}(u,\cdots,u)\|_{Z_{2}};\|u\|_{Z_{1}}\leq 1}}\right\}<\infty,

-norm for

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|\Phi_{k}\|\rho^{k}<\infty,\hskip 7.22743pt\mbox{ for }0<\rho\leq\delta.

We will now state the analytic inverse function theorem

,

,

]

.

Suppose that

and that its Frechét derivative at the origin

is an isomorphism from

. Then locally

.

(\Omega,\Sigma,m)

(5.1)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(x,t,\omega)+n(x,t,\omega)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}u(x,t,\omega)=c(x,t,\omega)u(x,t,\omega)+d(x,t,\omega).

Here the wave speed

and forcing

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(x,t,\omega)+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\frac{d}{dt}\beta(t,\omega)+b(t,x)}}\right\}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}u(x,t,\omega)

(5.2)

Here

Funaki also considered the n-dimensional scalar random transport model and we first write with Smooth noise:

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(x,t,\omega)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}n_{i}(x,t,\omega)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u(x,t,\omega)

(5.3)

=c(x,t)u(x,t,\omega)+d(x,t).

The Funaki’s temporal white noise model takes the form:

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(x,t,\omega)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\sum_{j}^{n}a_{ij}(t,x)\frac{d}{dt}\beta_{j}(t,\omega)+b_{i}(x,t)}}\right\}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u(x,t,\omega)

(5.4)

=c(x,t)u(x,t,\omega)+d(x,t).

Here
\beta_{j}(t),j=1,\cdots,n
are independent 1-D Brownian motions.

Define Stratenovich differential equation

(5.5)

dX_{t}=a(t,X_{t})\circ dB_{t}+b(t,X_{t})dt,\hskip 7.22743ptt\in(r,T),

(5.6)

X_{r}=x.

This is equivalent to Ito differential equation of the form:

(5.7)

dX_{t}=a(t,X_{t})dB_{t}+\tilde{b}(t,X_{t})dt,\mbox{ where }

(5.8)

\tilde{b}(x,t)=b(t,x)+\frac{1}{2}(a^{\prime}a)(t,x),

(5.9)

(a^{\prime}a)(t,x)_{i}=\sum_{k,j}\frac{\partial a_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}a_{kj}.

Lemma 5.1
.

For each
r,t
with
0\leq r\leq t\leq T
,
X(r,t,\cdot)
is a homeomorphism of
\mathrm{R}^{n}
on to
\mathrm{R}^{n}

Define time reversed process

(5.10)

Consider:

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(x,t,\omega)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\sum_{j}^{n}a_{ij}(t,x)\frac{d}{dt}\beta_{j}(t,\omega)+b_{i}(x,t)}}\right\}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u(x,t,\omega)

(5.11)

=c(x,t)u(x,t,\omega)+d(x,t),

(5.12)

u(x,0)=\phi(x),\hskip 7.22743ptx\in G\mbox{ and }u(x,t)=\psi(x,t),\hskip 7.22743pt(x,t)\in\partial G\times[0,T].

The probabilistic solution to the stochastic transport equation by Funaki is:

u(x,t,\omega)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\phi(Y(0,t,x))+\psi(Y(\sigma(t,x),t,x))}}\right\}\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\int_{0}^{t}c(s,Y(s,t,x))ds}}\right\}

Here

5.2.
Stochastic transport model with space-time white noise

We will begin with the following simple model (similar model with temporal white noise was considered by Funaki

(5.14)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(x,t,\omega)+\Gamma(x,t,\omega)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}u(x,t,\omega)=0,\hskip 7.22743pt(x,t)\in\mathrm{R}\times[0,T],

(5.15)

We quantize this problem as:

(5.16)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(x,t,\omega)+\Gamma(x,t,\omega)\diamond\frac{\partial}{\partial x}u(x,t,\omega)=0,\hskip 7.22743pt(x,t)\in\mathrm{R}\times[0,T],

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{u}(x,t,z)+[{\mathcal{H}}\Gamma](x,t,z)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\tilde{u}(x,t,z)=0,

\tilde{u}(x,0,z)=\phi(x).

We write down the equation of characteristic:

(5.20)

\frac{d\tilde{u}}{ds}=\frac{\partial\tilde{u}(x,t,z)}{\partial t}\frac{dt}{ds}+\frac{\partial\tilde{u}(x,t,z)}{\partial x}\frac{dx}{ds}

(5.21)

\frac{d\tilde{u}}{ds}=0,\hskip 7.22743pt\frac{dt}{ds}=1,\mbox{ and }\frac{dx}{ds}=[{\mathcal{H}}\Gamma](x,t,z).

Solving we get
x=\zeta_{t}(x_{0})=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}[{\mathcal{H}}\Gamma](x,r,z)dr
as the equation of characteristics and
\tilde{u}(x,0,z)=\phi(x_{0})=\phi(\zeta_{t}^{-1}(x))

(5.22)

\tilde{u}(x,t,z)=\phi(\zeta_{t}^{-1}(x))=\phi(x-\int_{0}^{t}[{\mathcal{H}}\Gamma](x,r,z)dr).

Hence

(5.23)

u(x,t,\omega)={\mathcal{H}}^{-1}\phi(\zeta_{t}^{-1}(x))={\mathcal{H}}^{-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\phi(x-\int_{0}^{t}[{\mathcal{H}}\Gamma](x,r,z)dr)}}\right).

We will now turn to the multidimensional stochastic transport model with space-time white noise:

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(x,t,\omega)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\sum_{j}^{n}a_{ij}(t,x)\Gamma_{j}(x,t,\omega)+b_{i}(x,t)}}\right\}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u(x,t,\omega)

(5.24)

=c(x,t)u(x,t,\omega)+d(x,t).

We will also study the quantized random transport model:

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(x,t,\omega)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\sum_{j}^{n}a_{ij}(t,x)\Gamma_{j}(x,t,\omega)}}\right\}\diamond\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u(x,t,\omega)+\sum_{i}^{n}b_{i}(x,t)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u(x,t,\omega)

=c(x,t)u(x,t,\omega)+d(x,t).

Taking the Hermite transform: For
z\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}
, and denoting
{\mathcal{H}}u
as

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{u}(x,t,z)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\sum_{j}^{n}a_{ij}(t,x)[{\mathcal{H}}\Gamma]_{j}(x,t,z)+b_{i}(x,t)}}\right\}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\tilde{u}(x,t,z)

We now consider a multidimensional quasilinear stochastic transport model with space-time white noise:

s\geq 1
. Hence using inverse Hermite transform we obtain:

Proposition 6.2
.

For the Gaussian white noise initial data
\varphi(\cdot,0,\cdot)=\Gamma(\cdot,\cdot)\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}(H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
,
s\geq 1
, there exists a unique solution to stochastic Benjamin-Ono equation as
\varphi\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}(C([0,T];H^{s})),

s\geq 1.
For Poisson and Lévy cases the unique correspondence map
{\mathcal{U}}
gives unique solution
\varphi\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}(L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s})),

s\geq 1.

7.
Stochastic reaction diffusion equation and quantization

Parisi and Wu
[
54
]
initiated the subject of stochastic quantization
[
20
]
which takes the view that (Euclidean) quantum fields can be constructed by studying stochastic partial differential equations. Stochastic reaction diffusion equation with space-time Gaussian noise has been studied by many authors
[
24
]
with results such as construction of invariant measures
[
5
,
27
]
as well as pathwise strong solutions
[
2
,
21
]
. In this section we will treat this class of problems with white noise theory.

7.1.
Stochastic heat equation with multiplicative noise and the KPZ Equation

Kardar, Parisi and Zhang
[
41
]
studied the stochastic model (which has come to be known as the KPZ equation):

(7.1)

\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}=\nu\Delta h+\frac{\lambda}{2}(\nabla h)^{2}+\Gamma(x,t).

As pointed out in this paper, in the case of smooth noise the above stochastic PDE can be formally transformed in to two other well-known stochastic PDEs (ignoring some constants). In fact
v=-\nabla h
gives the stochastic Burgers equation:

(7.2)

\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}+\lambda v\cdot\nabla v=\nu\Delta v+\nabla\Gamma(x,t),

and the transform
\varphi=\exp((\frac{\lambda}{2\nu})h)
converts the KPZ equation to

(7.3)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi(x,t,\omega)=\nu\Delta\varphi(x,t,\omega)+\frac{\lambda}{2\nu}\varphi(x,t,\omega)\Gamma(x,t,\omega),

with spatial white noise initial data:

\varphi(x,0,\omega)=\Gamma_{0}(x,\omega).

We quantize this problem as (after setting
\nu=\sigma^{2}/2
and
\lambda=\sigma^{2}
for simplicity):

(7.4)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi(x,t,\omega)=\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\Delta\varphi(x,t,\omega)+\varphi(x,t,\omega)\diamond\Gamma(x,t,\omega).

Applying the Hermite transform gives

(7.5)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{\varphi}(x,t,z)-[\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\Delta+[{\mathcal{H}}\Gamma](x,t,z)]\tilde{\varphi}(x,t,z)=0,

\tilde{\varphi}(x,0,z)=[{\mathcal{H}}\Gamma_{0}](x,z).

We write the solution formally using the propagator
{\mathcal{Z}}(t,r)
generated by
[
55
]
the unbounded time dependent operator
\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\Delta+[{\mathcal{H}}\Gamma](x,t,z)
:

(7.6)

\tilde{\varphi}(x,t,z)={\mathcal{Z}}(t,0)[{\mathcal{H}}\Gamma_{0}](x,z).

The solution is also probabilistically expressed by the Feynman-Kac formula using a Brownian motion
B_{t}
independent of
\Gamma(x,t,\omega)
and
\Gamma_{0}(x,\omega)
:

(7.7)

\tilde{\varphi}(x,t,z)=E_{x}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[[{\mathcal{H}}\Gamma_{0}](\sigma B_{t},z)\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\int_{0}^{t}[{\mathcal{H}}\Gamma](\sigma B_{s},t-s,z)ds}}\right\}}}\right].

Alternatively, we can consider the solution as a fixed point problem for the heat semigroup:

(7.8)

\tilde{\varphi}(x,t,z)=e^{\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}t\Delta}[{\mathcal{H}}\Gamma_{0}](x,z)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}(t-\tau)\Delta}{\mathcal{H}}[\Gamma](x,\tau,z)\tilde{\varphi}(x,\tau,z)d\tau.

Heat equation with singular and non-autonomous potentials have been studied in the literature
[
30
,
31
]
and for a fixed
z
as necessary using smoothing operator of the form
(I-\Delta_{x,t})^{-\gamma}
for the noise we can conclude that the above Hermite transformed problem has a unique solution in
\tilde{\varphi}\in C([0,T];L^{\infty})
and hence by inverse Hermite transform we obtain:

Proposition 7.1
.

For the Gaussian white noise
\Gamma(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}(L^{\infty}_{\mbox{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}))
, and initial data
\varphi(\cdot,0)\in L^{\infty}_{\mbox{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists a unique solution to the stochastic heat equation
\varphi\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}(C([0,T];L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))).
For the Poisson and Lévy white noises the unique correspondence map
{\mathcal{U}}
gives a unique solution to the stochastic heat equation
\varphi\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}(L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))).

The solution to the quantized problem is also probabilistically expressed by the Feynman-Kac formula:

(7.9)

\varphi(x,t,\omega)=E_{x}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Gamma_{0}(\sigma B_{t},\omega)\diamond\exp^{\diamond}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\int_{0}^{t}\Gamma(\sigma B_{s},t-s,\omega)ds}}\right\}}}\right],

where the Wick exponential of
X\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}
is defined by:

\exp^{\diamond}X=\sum_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}X^{\diamond n}.

We also note here that instead of the multiplicative noise term if we have a stochastic heat equation with space-time white noise as an additive noise term or initial data as a spatial white noise then after Hermite transform we end up with heat equation with singular initial data or forcing term and the problem can be resolved using the fundamental solution of the heat equation followed by inverse Hermite transform.

7.2.
Stochastic nonlinear heat equation with white noise Initial Data

We will now consider:

(7.10)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi(x,t,\omega)+\varphi(x,t,\omega)^{p}=\Delta\varphi(x,t,\omega),\hskip 7.22743ptp=2,3,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

\varphi(x,0,\omega)=\Gamma(x,\omega),x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We will quantize this equation as:

(7.11)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi(x,t,\omega)+\varphi(x,t,\omega)^{\diamond p}=\Delta\varphi(x,t,\omega),

Applying the Hermite transform gives

(7.12)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{\varphi}(x,t,z)-[\Delta-\tilde{\varphi}(x,t,z)^{p-1}]\tilde{\varphi}(x,t,z)=0,

(7.13)

\tilde{\varphi}(x,0,z)=[{\mathcal{H}}\Gamma](x,z).

Nonlinear heat equations with measure initial data has been studied in the literature with positive as well as negative results. When the initial data is a Dirac measure it has been shown in
[
8
]
that this problem with the nonlinearity selected above
(p=2
and
3)
has no solution in any space dimension
n\geq 1
. This means that we need to smooth the measure by an operator of the form
(I-\Delta)^{-\gamma}
so that we can use results for slightly less singular but still measure data such as that presented in
[
9
]
with initial data in
L^{q},1\leq q<\infty
. This provides a unique short time solution to the Hermite transformed problem as
\tilde{\varphi}\in C([0,T^{\prime}];L^{q})
and we then inverse Hermite transform to obtain:

Proposition 7.2
.

There exists a unique solution
\varphi\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}(C([0,T^{\prime}];L^{q}))
to the stochastic nonlinear heat equation with spatial white noise initial data
\Gamma(\cdot,\cdot)\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}(L^{q})
.

8.
Stochastic linear and nonlinear Schrödinger equations with space-time white noise

In this section we will address the stochastic linear and nonlinear Schrödinger equations with space-time white noise that arise in the Laser propagation problems as discussed earlier. Once again we will utilize the Hermite transform to convert the problem to deterministic linear and nonlinear Schrodinger equations parameterized by an infinite sequence of complex variables
z
. There is a wealth of literature on linear Schrödinger semigroup with a range of potentials
[
59
,
6
]
and nonlinear Schrödinger equations
[
12
,
62
]
which will enable the solvability of the deterministic problems obtained by Hermite transforms as we discuss below.

8.1.
Strichartz estimates

We recall the following estimates for the Schrödinger free propagator
[
61
,
46
]
:

Lemma 8.1
.

For
(q,r)
and
(\tilde{q},\tilde{r})
such that for
d\geq 1
both exponent pair satisfying:

2\leq q,r\leq\infty,\frac{1}{q}=\frac{d}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})\hskip 7.22743pt(q,r,d)\neq(2,\infty,2),

we have:

(8.1)

\|e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

and

(8.2)

\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}F(s,\cdot)ds\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}_{t}L^{\tilde{r}^{\prime}}_{x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

8.2.
Stochastic linear Schrödinger equation with additive space-time white noise

(8.3)

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi(x,t,\omega)+\Delta\psi(x,t,\omega)=\Gamma(x,t,\omega),\hskip 7.22743pt(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},

with spatial white noise initial data:

(8.4)

\psi(x,0,\omega)=\Gamma_{0}(x,\omega),\hskip 7.22743ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Applying Hermite transform we get the Schrödinger equation in free space:

(8.5)

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{\psi}(x,t,z)+\Delta\tilde{\psi}(x,t,z)={\mathcal{H}}[\Gamma](x,t,z),\hskip 7.22743ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{d},z\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}},

(8.6)

\tilde{\psi}(x,0,z)={\mathcal{H}}[\Gamma_{0}](x,z),\hskip 7.22743ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{d},z\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}.

The solution is written formally using the free Schrödinger propagator
e^{it\Delta}
as

(8.7)

\tilde{\psi}(x,t,z)=e^{it\Delta}{\mathcal{H}}[\Gamma_{0}](x,z)-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta}{\mathcal{H}}[\Gamma](x,\tau,z)d\tau.

The d-dimensional Schrödinger kernel is:

(8.8)

K_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{(4\pi it)^{d/2}}e^{i\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}.

Hence we also have:

(8.9)

\tilde{\psi}(x,t)=\frac{1}{(4\pi it)^{d/2}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\int_{\mathrm{R}^{d}}e^{i\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}}{\mathcal{H}}[\Gamma_{0}](y,z)dy-i\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathrm{R}^{d}}e^{i\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}{\mathcal{H}}[\Gamma](x,\tau,z)dyd\tau}}\right].

This equation makes sense for a fixed
z\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}
if
{\mathcal{H}}[\Gamma_{0}](x,z)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
{\mathcal{H}}[\Gamma](\cdot,\cdot,z)\in L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
due to the Strichartz estimates recalled above. Hence inverse Hermite transform gives:

Proposition 8.1
.

For initial data noise distribution
\Gamma_{0}\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and noise forcing
\Gamma\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}(L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))
there exists a unique solution
\psi\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}(L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))
to the stochastic Schrödinger equation.

8.3.
Stochastic linear Schrödinger equation with multiplicative space-time white noise

Consider the linear stochastic Schrödinger equation with multiplicative space-time noise: For
\omega\in\Omega,x\in\mathrm{R}^{d},d\geq 1,t>0

(8.10)

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi(x,t,\omega)+[\Delta+\Gamma(x,t,\omega)]\psi(x,t,\omega)=0,

and spatial white noise initial data:

(8.11)

\psi(x,0,\omega)=\Gamma_{0}(x,\omega),\hskip 7.22743ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Here the time-like coordinate
t
is the propagation direction,
\varphi
is the (complex) electric field and the potential
V
in general depends on the refractive index of the medium.

We consider the quantized linear Schrödinger equation with multiplicative white noise:

(8.12)

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi(x,t,\omega)+\Delta\psi(x,t,\omega)+\psi(x,t,\omega)\diamond\Gamma(x,t,\omega)=0.

Applying Hermite transform we get the Schrödinger equation with a potential
V(x,t,z)={\mathcal{H}}[\Gamma](x,t,z)
:

(8.13)

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{\psi}(x,t,z)+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Delta+{\mathcal{H}}[\Gamma](x,t,z)}}\right)\tilde{\psi}(x,t,z)=0.

(8.14)

\tilde{\psi}(x,0,z)={\mathcal{H}}[\Gamma_{0}](x,z),\hskip 7.22743ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{d},z\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}.

We write the solution formally using the propagator
{\mathcal{Z}}(t,r)
generated by
[
59
,
55
]
the unbounded Hamiltonian time dependent
(\Delta+V(x,t,z))
:

(8.15)

\tilde{\psi}(x,t,z)={\mathcal{Z}}(t,0)\tilde{\psi}(x,0,z).

Alternatively, we can consider the solution as a fixed point problem for the free propagator:

(8.16)

\tilde{\psi}(x,t,z)=e^{it\Delta}\tilde{\psi}(x,0,z)-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta}{\mathcal{H}}[\Gamma](x,\tau,z)\tilde{\psi}(x,\tau,z)d\tau.

Schrödinger equations with time dependent unbounded singular potential have been studied in the literature
[
70
]
where it is shown that the two parameter propagator
{\mathcal{Z}}(t,r)
is unitary in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
(actually K. Yajima’s results holds in any dimension.) With an introduction to a suitable smoothing for the noise in the form of
(I-\Delta_{x,t})^{-\gamma}
we can fit the Hermite transformed problem above in Yajima’s framework and deduce a unique solution
\tilde{\psi}\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
and hence inverse Hermite transform gives:

Proposition 8.2
.

For Gaussian initial data noise distribution
\Gamma_{0}\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and Gaussian white noise forcing
\Gamma\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}(L^{\infty}_{\mbox{loc}}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, there exists a unique solution to the stochastic Schrödinger equation with multiplicative space-time white noise as
\psi\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}(C([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})))
. For Gaussian and Lévy cases unique correspondence map
{\mathcal{U}}
gives a unique solution
\psi\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}(L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})))

8.4.
Nonlinear Schrödinger equation with multiplicative space-time white noise

Nonlinear defocusing cubic Stochastic Schrödinger equation with multiplicative space-time white noise:

(8.17)

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi(x,t,\omega)+\Delta\psi(x,t,\omega)+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Gamma(x,t,\omega)-|\psi|^{2}}}\right)\psi(x,t,\omega)=0,\hskip 7.22743ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{3},t>0

and spatial white noise initial data:

(8.18)

\psi(x,0,\omega)=\Gamma_{0}(x,\omega),\hskip 7.22743ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

We consider the quantized nonlinear Schrodinger equation with multiplicative white noise:

(8.19)

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi(x,t,\omega)+\Delta\psi(x,t,\omega)+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Gamma(x,t,\omega)-\psi(x,t,\omega)\diamond\psi^{*}(x,t,\omega)}}\right)\diamond\psi(x,t,\omega)=0.

Here (in the Gaussian white noise case) we take

\psi(x,t,\omega)=\sum_{\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}(x,t)H_{\alpha}(\omega)\mbox{ and its complex conjugate }\psi^{*}(x,t,\omega)=\sum_{\alpha}\Phi^{*}_{\alpha}(x,t)H_{\alpha}(\omega),

Applying Hermite transform we get a deterministic nonlinear Schrödinger equation:

(8.20)

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{\psi}(x,t,z)+[(\Delta+{\mathcal{H}}[\Gamma](x,t,z)-\tilde{\psi}(x,t,z)\tilde{(\psi^{*})}(x,t,z)]\tilde{\psi}(x,t,z)=0,

where

\tilde{\psi}(x,t,\omega)=\sum_{\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}(x,t)z^{\alpha}\mbox{ and }\tilde{(\psi^{*})}(x,t,z)=\sum_{\alpha}\Phi^{*}_{\alpha}(x,t)z^{\alpha}.

(8.21)

\tilde{\psi}(x,0,z)={\mathcal{H}}[\Gamma_{0}](x,z),\hskip 7.22743ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{3},z\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}.

Nonlinear Schrödinger equation with time dependent potential of the above type (with fixed
z
) is studied in
[
15
]
. With an introduction to a suitable smoothing for the noise in the form of
(I-\Delta_{x,t})^{-\gamma}
the Hermite transformed problem above in the framework of
[
15
]
(in particular Assumption 1.3 regarding the potential in that paper) and deduce a unique solution
\tilde{\psi}\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{8/3}([0,T];L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
and hence inverse Hermite transform gives:

Proposition 8.3
.

For Gaussian initial data noise distribution
\Gamma_{0}\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and Gaussian noise forcing
\Gamma\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}(L^{\infty}_{\mbox{loc}}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, there exists a unique solution to the stochastic nonlinear Schrödinger equation with multiplicative space-time white noise as
\psi\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}(C([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{8/3}([0,T];L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})))
. For Poisson and Lévy cases unique correspondence map
{\mathcal{U}}
gives a unique solution
\psi\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}(L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{8/3}([0,T];L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})))

Concluding remarks

In this paper we have casted a number of Laser propagation problems in random media in the white noise distribution theory framework to stimulate further research in their mathematical structure. Some of the most prominent problems are selected for our discussion and a number of other nonlinear wave equations that arise in Laser interaction with plasma such as the Schrodinger-Hartree equation
[

[

and Davey-Stewartson equation

(1) Random (quantized) Schrödinger-Hartree equation:

(9.1)

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi+\Delta\varphi=\pm[|x|^{n}\star(\varphi^{*}\diamond\varphi)]\diamond\varphi,\hskip 7.22743ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\hskip 7.22743pt0<n<d,

(9.2)

\varphi(x,\omega,0)=\Gamma(x,\omega).

Upon Hermite transform this system (for a fixed

[
37

[
7

67

Proposition 9.1

Proposition 9.2

Suppose the initial data Gaussian white noise distribution satisfy

then there exists a unique local-in-time solution to stochastic Zakharov equation with white noise initial data such that

(9.6)

(\varphi,n,\partial_{t}\varphi)\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(C([0,T];H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}}\right).

Proposition 9.3

For the Gaussian/Poisson/Lévy initial data white noise distribution
\Gamma\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))

(9.10)

u\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(C([0,T^{*}[;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))\cap L^{4}((0,T^{*})\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}}\right),

(9.11)

\nabla\varphi\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(L^{2}((0,T^{*})\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}}\right).

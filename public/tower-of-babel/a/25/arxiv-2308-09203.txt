Invariant Geometric Structures on Complex Almost Abelian Lie Groups
Zhirary Avetisyan, Abigail Brauer, Oderico-Benjamin Buran, Jimmy Morentin, Tianyi Wang

Abstract
. An almost Abelian group is a non-Abelian Lie group with a codimension 1 Abelian subgroup. This paper investigates invariant Hermitian and Kähler structures on connected complex almost Abelian groups. We find explicit formulas for the left and right Haar measures, the modular function, and left and right generator vector fields on simply connected complex almost Abelian groups. From the generator fields, we obtain invariant vector and tensor field frames, allowing us to find an explicit form for all invariant tensor fields. Namely, all such invariant tensor fields have constant coefficients in the invariant frame. From this, we classify all invariant Hermitian forms on complex simply connected almost Abelian groups, and we prove the nonexistence of invariant Kähler forms on all such groups. Via constructions involving the pullback of the quotient map, we extend the explicit description of invariant Hermitian structures and the nonexistence of Kähler structures to connected almost Abelian groups.



1.
Introduction

An almost Abelian Lie algebra is a non-Abelian Lie algebra with a codimension one Abelian ideal. In analogy, an almost Abelian Lie group is a Lie group with a codimension 1 Abelian subgroup; almost Abelian Lie groups have almost Abelian Lie algebras. The present paper only considers complex almost Abelian Lie groups and algebras.

Almost Abelian groups include important classes of groups including groups having Lie algebras of type Bi(II)-Bi(VII) and the Heisenberg group. Almost Abelian groups also arise in cosmological models, dynamical systems, integrable systems, PDEs, and solvmanifolds. This last application is of particular relevance to this paper, and, furthermore, almost Abelian solvmanifolds have seen extensive study in recent years (in particular complex almost Abelian solvmanifolds); see:
[
And+11
]
,
[
AO17
]
,
[
Fre11
]
,
[
FP22
]
,
[
FP21
]
, and
[
Sta21
]
.

A solvmanifold is a quotient
G/H
of a simply connected solvable Lie group
G
and a discrete subgroup
H
. Since (complex) connected almost Abelian Lie groups are solvable, we can consider almost Abelian solvmanifolds. This paper studies invariant structures on complex simply connected almost Abelian groups and generalizes them to connected almost Abelian groups (being quotients of the simply connected groups by discrete subgroups), and we hope to eventually generalize these results to almost Abelian solvmanifolds.

The following results are found in this paper. Let
G
be a simply connected complex almost Abelian group. In Prop
3.1
, the explicit formula for the left and right Haar measures and the associated modular function are found. In Corollary
3.2
we find explicit forms for the left and right generator fields of
G
, from which we obtain explicit invariant tensor and vector field frames for
G
. In Prop
3.6
it is shown that all invariant tensor fields on
G
have constant coefficients in the invariant frame. In particular, all invariant Hermitian forms on
G
have constant coefficients in the invariant frame (Corollary
3.7
). These results are used to prove our main theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

There are no left-invariant Kähler metrics on a simply connected almost Abelian group.

Lastly, these results are generalized in §
5
to complex connected almost Abelian groups.

2.
Preliminaries and Basic Properties

We briefly recall some fundamentals of (complex) almost Abelian Lie algebras, and we refer the reader to
[
Ave18
]
for more details. An almost Abelian Lie algebra is a Lie algebra containing a codimension 1 Abelian subalgebra. We use the formal device of an
\N
-graded multiplicity function to completely capture the data of a finite dimensional complex almost Abelian Lie algebra.

Let
\F
be a field, and let
\sigma_{\F}\subset\F[X]
denote the set of monic irreducible polynomials with coefficients in
\F
. A
\N
-graded multiplicity function
is a map
\aleph:\sigma_{\F}\times\N\to\mathcal{C}
, where
\mathcal{C}
denote the class of cardinals. In the case where
\F=\C
, every monic irreducible polynomial
p_{\mu}\in\sigma_{\C}
is of the form
p_{\mu}(X)=X-\mu
where
\mu\in\C
. Therefore we can identify
\sigma_{\C}
with
\C
and view
\aleph:\C\times\N\to\mathcal{C}
.

We define

\mathrm{supp}\left(\aleph\right)=p\in\quantity{p\in\C[X]: \exists n\in\N\text{ such that }\aleph(p,n)\neq 0}[X]:\exists n\in\N\text{ such that }\aleph(p,n)\neq 0.

Then, for a polynomial
p_{\mu}\in\sigma_{\C}
, we define
J(p,n)=\mu\mathds{1}+N_{n}
, where
N_{n}\in M_{n\times n}(\C)
with 1’s on the super diagonal and zeros everywhere else. Then we may define

J(\aleph)=\bigoplus_{p\in\mathrm{supp}\left(\aleph\right)}\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigoplus_{\aleph(p,n)}J(p,n).

It is shown in
[
Ave18
]
that a finitely supported
\N
-graded multiplicity function
\aleph
uniquely and completely determines the structure of a complex almost Abelian Lie algebra by determining the Jordan matrix
J(\aleph)
which serves as a matrix representation for
\ad_{e_{0}}\in\text{End}(\C^{d})
, where
e_{0}=(0,1)\in\C^{d}\rtimes\C
.

Thus, the data of a finitely supported
\N
-graded multiplicity function is the same as the data of a finite-dimensional almost Abelian Lie algebra. Namely, a finitely supported multiplicity function
\aleph
is the same as the data of the almost Abelian Lie algebra
{{\scriptstyle{}^{\scriptstyle a}}\!\!\mathcal{A}}(\aleph)=\mathbf{V}\rtimes_{\ad_{e_{0}}}\C e_{0}
, where
\ad_{e_{0}}=J(\aleph)
and
\mathbf{V}=\C^{\dim_{\C}(\aleph)}
.

Having established the formal apparatus of multiplicity functions, we recall Proposition 3.3 from
[
Ave+23
]
that the simply connected representation of a complex almost Abelian group with finitely supported multiplicity function
\aleph
is given by:

Proposition 2.1
.

For a finitely supported multiplicity function
\aleph
, let

G\coloneqq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
v&e^{tJ(\aleph)}&0\\
t&0&1\end{pmatrix}\>\middle|\ (v,t)\in\C^{d}\oplus\C\right\}

Then
G
is a complex simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra isomorphic to
{{\scriptstyle{}^{\scriptstyle a}}\!\!\mathcal{A}}(\aleph)
.

In order to abbreviate our notations, we will use the notation
(v,t)
for an element of a complex almost Abelian Lie algebra
{{\scriptstyle{}^{\scriptstyle a}}\!\!\mathcal{A}}(\aleph)=\mathbf{V}\rtimes\C
, and we will use the notation
[v,t]
to denote the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
v&e^{tJ(\aleph)}&0\\
t&0&1\end{pmatrix}

from the simply connected representation of Prop.
2.1
; i.e.,
[v,t]
is an element of the simply connected group with Lie algebra
{{\scriptstyle{}^{\scriptstyle a}}\!\!\mathcal{A}}(\aleph)
. Using these convenient representations, we recall Remark 3.5 from
[
Ave+23
]
:

Remark 2.2
.

Let
G
be the simply connected group that has Lie algebra
{{\scriptstyle{}^{\scriptstyle a}}\!\!\mathcal{A}}(\aleph)
. It follows that on the Abelian Lie subalgebra
\ker(J(\aleph))\oplus\C
the exponential map
\exp:{{\scriptstyle{}^{\scriptstyle a}}\!\!\mathcal{A}}(\aleph)\to G
associated with
G
is given by:

\exp((v,t))=[v,t],\qquad\forall(v,t)\in\ker(J(\aleph))\oplus\C

We recall the following standard fact from Lie theory:

Lemma 2.3

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an arbitrary Lie algebra,
G
be a connected matrix Lie group that has Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, and let
\exp_{G}:\mathfrak{g}\to G
be the corresponding exponential map (specific to

\exp_{G}(Z(\mathfrak{g}))\subseteq Z(G)

We will also need a result on the description of the center of a complex simply connected almost Abelian group. This is Proposition 4.2 from

The center of the simply connected almost Abelian Lie group

\displaystyle Z(G)

\displaystyle=\exp_{G}[Z({{\scriptstyle{}^{\scriptstyle a}}\!\!\mathcal{A}}(\aleph))]\times T_{\aleph}=\exp_{G}[Z({{\scriptstyle{}^{\scriptstyle a}}\!\!\mathcal{A}}(\aleph))\times T_{\aleph}]

\displaystyle=\{(u,s)\in\C^{d}\rtimes\C\divides u\in\ker(J(\aleph)),\ e^{sJ(\aleph)}=\mathds{1}\}

Also, the preimage under the exponential map (associated with

3.
Invariant Measures and Frames

We now find the left and right Haar measures
\mu_{L}
and
\mu_{R}
, and the modular function
\Delta
, on a simply connected almost Abelian group
G
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Denote the left Haar measure by
\mu_{L}
, the right Haar measure by
\mu_{R}
, and the modular function associated to the Haar measures by
\Delta
. Then:

d\mu_{L}(v,t)=e^{-2\tr[\real(tJ(\aleph))]}dv\,dt,\quad d\mu_{R}(v,t)=dv\,dt,\quad\Delta(v,t)=e^{-2\tr[\real(tJ(\aleph))]}.

Proof.

In order to check the left and right Haar measures, we simply need to check that they are indeed invariant under left and right multiplication, respectively. So, fix an arbitrary
[u,s]\in G
. First consider left multiplication:

(1)

\Phi_{[u,s]}([v,t]):=[u,s][v,t]=[u+e^{sJ(\aleph)}v,t+s]=:[v^{\prime},t^{\prime}].

The Jacobian matrix of the coordinate transformation
(v,t)\mapsto(v^{\prime},t^{\prime})
, denoted here by
\mathcal{J}(v^{\prime},t^{\prime};v,t)
, can be seen from (
1
) to be:

\mathcal{J}_{\C}(v^{\prime},t^{\prime};v,t)\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\partialderivative{v'}{v}&\partialderivative{v'}{t}\\
\partialderivative{t'}{v}&\partialderivative{t'}{t}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{sJ(\aleph)}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},

when
v^{\prime},t^{\prime},v,t
are considered as complex vectors. But we are interested in transforming the Lebesgue measure. Let
\mathcal{J}_{\R}
represent the corresponding real Jacobian. We use the known (see
[
Ran98
]
, pg. 19) equality
\mathcal{J}_{\det(\mathcal{J}_{\R})}=\mathcal{J}_{\det(\mathcal{J}_{\C} \mathcal{J}_{\C}^*)}\mathcal{J}_{\C}^{*}
. Thus

\mathcal{J}_{\det\big(\mathcal{J}_{\R}\big)}\big.=\det\big( (e^{sJ(\aleph)}) (e^{sJ(\aleph)})^* \big)=e^{2\tr[\real(sJ(\aleph))]}.

Then we calculate for our candidate of the left Haar measure:

\displaystyle d\mu_{L}\big(\Phi_{[u,s]}([v,t])\big)

\displaystyle=d\mu_{L}([v^{\prime},t^{\prime}])

\displaystyle=e^{-2\tr[\real(t^{\prime}J(\aleph))]}dv^{\prime}\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle=e^{-2\tr[\real(t^{\prime}J(\aleph))]}\mathcal{J}_{\absolutevalue{\mathcal{J}_{\R}(v',t';v,t)}}(v^{\prime},t^{\prime};v,t)dv\,dt

\displaystyle=e^{-2\tr[\real((t+s)J(\aleph))]}e^{2\tr[\real(sJ(\aleph))]}dv\,dt

\displaystyle=e^{-2\tr[\real(tJ(\aleph))]}dv\,dt

\displaystyle=d\mu_{L}([v,t]).

Now consider right multiplication for a fixed arbitrary
[u,s]\in G
:

\Psi_{[u,s]}([v,t]):=[v,t][u,s]=[v+e^{tJ(\aleph)}u,t+s]=:[v^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}].

Then

\displaystyle d\mu_{R}\big(\Psi_{[u,s]}([v,t])\big)

\displaystyle=d\mu_{R}([v^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}])

\displaystyle=dv^{\prime\prime}\,dt^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}_{\absolutevalue{\mathcal{J}_{\R}(v'', t'', v,t)}}(v^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime},v,t)dv\,dt

\displaystyle=dv\,dt

\displaystyle=d\mu_{R}([v,t]),

where the complex Jacobian matrix
\mathcal{J}_{\C}(v^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime};v,t)
is calculated to be:

\mathcal{J}_{\C}(v^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime};v,t)=\begin{pmatrix}1&J(\aleph)e^{tJ(\aleph)}u\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},

from which it is clear the real Jacobian is also 1.

Lastly, we calculate that the modular function is

\Delta(v,t)=\frac{d\mu_{L}(v,t)}{d\mu_{R}(v,t)}=e^{-2\tr[\real(tJ(\aleph)\big)]}.

∎

Let
G
be a simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra
{{\scriptstyle{}^{\scriptstyle a}}\!\!\mathcal{A}}(\aleph)
. Recall that the value of a vector field
X\in C^{\infty}(TG)
evaluated at a point
[v,t]\in G
can be written in terms of the coordinate basis vectors:

X_{[v,t]}=X^{i}([v,t])\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\bigg|_{[v,t]}.

Working with the global chart
(v,t)\in\C^{d}\rtimes\C=G
as in Prop.
2.1
, a vector
X\in T_{[v,t]}G
in these coordinates will take the form of a
d+1
dimensional row such that

(u,s)\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}u^{\top}&s\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}X^{1}([v,t])&X^{2}([v,t])&\dots&X^{d+1}([v,t])\end{pmatrix}.

We sometimes will also identify
X
directly with the
d+1
dimensional row
\begin{pmatrix}u^{\top}&s\end{pmatrix}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

For every tangent vector
X\in T_{[0,0]}G
at the identity, the corresponding left and right generator vector fields are given by

\vL_{X}(v,t)=X\begin{pmatrix}\mathds{1}&0\\
v^{\top}J(\aleph)^{\top}&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\vR_{X}(v,t)=X\begin{pmatrix}e^{tJ(\aleph)^{\top}}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad(v,t)\in G.

Proof.

Let
(-1,1)\ni\tau\mapsto(u(\tau),s(\tau))\in G
be a smooth curve with
(u(0),s(0))=(0,0)
such that
(u^{\prime}(0),s^{\prime}(0))=X
. Consider the smooth 1-parameter family of biholomorphisms

[v,t]\mapsto[u(\tau),s(\tau)][v,t]=[u(\tau)+e^{s(\tau)J(\aleph)}v,s(\tau)+t]=[v(\tau),t(\tau)],\quad\forall(v,t)\in G,\quad\tau\in(-1,1).

Then,

\vL_{X}(v,t)=\frac{d}{d\tau}[v(\tau),t(\tau)]\bigg|_{\tau=0}=\frac{d}{d\tau}[u(\tau)+e^{s(\tau)J(\aleph)}v,s(\tau)+t]\bigg|_{\tau=0}=(u^{\prime}(0)+s^{\prime}(0)J(\aleph)v,s^{\prime}(0))

=\begin{pmatrix}{(u^{\prime}(0)+s^{\prime}(0)J(\aleph)v)}^{\top}&s^{\prime}(0)\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}{u^{\prime}(0)}^{\top}&{s^{\prime}(0)}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mathds{1}&0\\
v^{\top}J(\aleph)^{\top}&1\end{pmatrix}=X\begin{pmatrix}\mathds{1}&0\\
v^{\top}J(\aleph)^{\top}&1\end{pmatrix},

as desired. Next consider the smooth 1-parameter family of biholomorphisms given by

[v,t]\mapsto[v,t][u(\tau),s(\tau)]=[v+e^{tJ(\aleph)}u(\tau),t+s(\tau)]=[v(\tau),t(\tau)],\quad\forall(v,t)\in G,\quad\tau\in(-1,1).

Then,

\vR_{X}(v,t)=\frac{d}{d\tau}[v(\tau),t(\tau)]\bigg|_{\tau=0}=\frac{d}{d\tau}[v+e^{tJ(\aleph)}u(\tau),t+s(\tau)]\bigg|_{\tau=0}=(e^{tJ(\aleph)}u^{\prime}(0),s^{\prime}(0))

=\begin{pmatrix}{(e^{tJ(\aleph)}u^{\prime}(0))}^{\top}&s^{\prime}(0)\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}{u^{\prime}(0)}^{\top}&{s^{\prime}(0)}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{e^{tJ(\aleph)}}^{\top}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}=X\begin{pmatrix}{e^{tJ(\aleph)}}^{\top}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Again, as desired.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

A global frame of left- and right- invariant vector fields on the simply connected almost Abelian Lie group
G
as above can be formed by columns of the matrices

\begin{pmatrix}e^{tJ(\aleph)}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\begin{pmatrix}\mathds{1}&J(\aleph)v\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

respectively. A global frame of left- and right- invariant 1-forms on the simply connected almost Abelian Lie group
G
as above can be formed by rows of the matrices

\begin{pmatrix}e^{-tJ(\aleph)}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\begin{pmatrix}\mathds{1}&-J(\aleph)v\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

respectively.

Proof.

The first statement follows from the expression of
\mathcal{L}_{X}
and
\mathcal{R}_{X}
in the previous proposition. Since the right generator vector fields are precisely left-invariant vector fields, finding a global frame of left-invariant vector fields is equivalent to finding a basis of the right generator vector fields. Hence by letting
X
run over a standard basis of
T_{[0,0]}G
, which we identify as a standard basis of
\C^{d+1}
, we get the first statement.

For the second statement, define the dual frame
\{X^{i}\}_{i=1}^{d+1}
of
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d+1}
by
X^{i}(X_{j})=\delta_{j}^{i}
. Then clearly, if
X_{i}
is the columns of a matrix, then
X^{i}
are the rows of the inverse matrix. This concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Let
\{X^{j}\}_{j=1}^{d+1}
be the above left-invariant frame of holomorphic covector fields, where
X^{j}=X_{a}^{j}dz^{a}
and each
X_{a}^{j}
is holomorphic for each
a
and
j
. Define
\{\overline{X}^{j}\}_{j=1}^{d+1}
, where
\overline{X}^{j}=\overline{X}_{a}^{j}d\overline{z}^{a}
. Then
\{\overline{X}^{j}\}_{j=1}^{d+1}
is a frame of antiholomorphic covector fields.

Proof.

Let
\overline{W}:=\overline{W_{j}X^{j}}
be an antiholomorphic covector field. Let
\{X_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d+1}
be the left invariant holomorphic vector field frame we found in Corollary
3.3
, and let
\{X^{j}\}_{j=1}^{d+1}
be the corresponding holomorphic covector field frame. For each
1\leq j\leq d+1
let
X^{j}=X_{a}^{j}dz^{a}
, and recall that
X^{j}(X_{k})=\delta_{k}^{j}
. Let
p\in G
be an arbitrary element of our simply connnected almost Abelian Lie group.
Now we calculate as follows:

\displaystyle\left(\overline{W}(\overline{X_{k}})\right)_{p}

\displaystyle=\overline{\overline{\overline{W_{p}}\left(\overline{X_{k}}\right)_{p}}}=\overline{W_{p}\left(X_{k}\right)_{p}}=\overline{\left(W_{j}(p)X_{p}^{j}\right)\big((X_{k})_{p}\big)}=\overline{W_{k}(p)}\overline{\left(X^{j}X_{k}\right)_{p}}=\overline{W_{k}(p)}

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\overline{W_{j}}\,\overline{X^{j}}\right)\big(\overline{X_{k}}\big)\right)_{p}.

∎

We display a formula for left-invariant tensor fields, but it applies to right-invariant tensor fields similarly.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
T
be a smooth section of
TG^{(m,n)}\otimes\overline{TG^{(p,q)}}
. Then
T
can be expanded in the left invariant tensor frame derived above as follows:

(2)

T([v,t])=T^{i_{1},\dots,i_{m},k_{1},\dots,k_{p}}_{j_{1},\dots,j_{n},\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{q}}X_{i_{1}}\cdots X_{i_{m}}\overline{X}_{k_{1}}\cdots\overline{X}_{k_{p}}X^{j_{1}}\cdots X^{j_{n}}\overline{X}^{\ell_{1}}\cdots\overline{X}^{\ell_{q}}

for all

, where
\left\{X_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{d+1}
and
\left\{X^{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{d+1}
are the mutually dual frames of left-invariant holomorphic vector fields and covector fields, respectively, and
\left\{\overline{X}_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{d+1}
and
\left\{\overline{X}_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{d+1}
are their antiholomorphic counterparts.

Proposition 3.6
.

The mixed tensor field
T
as in (
2
) is left-invariant if and only if the coefficient functions

T^{i_{1},\dots,i_{m},k_{1},\dots,k_{p}}_{j_{1},\dots,j_{n},\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{q}}

are all constant.

Proof.

For every
g\in G
, define
\Phi_{g}:G\to G
by
\Phi_{g}(x)=gx
. Abusing notation, we let
\Phi_{g}^{*}
denote both the pullback and pushforward of
\Phi_{g}
.

Since the frames
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d+1}
,
\{X^{i}\}_{i=1}^{d+1}
,
\{\overline{X}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d+1}
, and
\{\overline{X}^{j}\}_{j=1}^{d+1}
are all left-invariant, we know that
\Phi_{g}^{*}X_{j}=X_{j}
,
\Phi_{g}^{*}X^{j}=X^{j}
,
\Phi_{g}^{*}\overline{X}_{j}=\overline{X}_{j}
, and
\Phi_{g}^{*}\overline{X}^{j}=\overline{X}^{j}
for each
1\leq j\leq d+1
and for all
g\in G
. Then we may say that
T
is left-invariant if and only if

\displaystyle\Phi_{g}^{*}T

\displaystyle=\Phi_{g}^{*}(T^{i_{1},\dots,i_{m},k_{1},\dots,k_{p}}_{j_{1},\dots,j_{n},\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{q}})\Phi_{g}^{*}(X_{i_{1}}\cdots X_{i_{m}}\overline{X}_{k_{1}}\cdots\overline{X}_{k_{p}}X^{j_{1}}\cdots X^{j_{n}}\overline{X}^{\ell_{1}}\cdots\overline{X}^{\ell_{q}})

\displaystyle=\Phi_{g}^{*}(T^{i_{1},\dots,i_{m},k_{1},\dots,k_{p}}_{j_{1},\dots,j_{n},\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{q}})X_{i_{1}}\cdots X_{i_{m}}\overline{X}_{k_{1}}\cdots\overline{X}_{k_{p}}X^{j_{1}}\cdots X^{j_{n}}\overline{X}^{\ell_{1}}\cdots\overline{X}^{\ell_{q}}

\displaystyle=T^{i_{1},\dots,i_{m},k_{1},\dots,k_{p}}_{j_{1},\dots,j_{n},\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{q}}X_{i_{1}}\cdots X_{i_{m}}\overline{X}_{k_{1}}\cdots\overline{X}_{k_{p}}X^{j_{1}}\cdots X^{j_{n}}\overline{X}^{\ell_{1}}\cdots\overline{X}^{\ell_{q}}=T,

i.e., iff the scalar functions

T^{i_{1},\dots,i_{m},k_{1},\dots,k_{p}}_{j_{1},\dots,j_{n},\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{q}}

∎

A hermitian form
h
is a non-degenerate sesquilinear form with hermitian symmetry, written locally that is

h=h_{ab}dz^{a}\otimes d\overline{z}^{b},

where
h_{ab}
is a hermitian matrix. Furthermore a hermitian metric (or structure) is a positive-definite hermitian form. In light of Corollary
3.5
we may write any hermitian metric
h
on a simply connected group
G
as

h=h_{ij}X^{i}\overline{X}^{j},

where
h_{ij}
is a positive definite matrix. We are now prepared to classify all (left) invariant hermitian metrics on a simply connected complex almost Abelian groups.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
h={h}_{jk}X^{j}\overline{X}^{k}
be a Hermitian metric. Then
h
is left-invariant if and only if
\widehat{h}_{jk}
is a constant matrix.

Proof.

For every
g\in G
, define
\Phi_{g}:G\to G
by
\Phi_{g}(x)=gx
.

Since the frames
\{X^{i}\}_{i=1}^{d+1}
, and
\{\overline{X}^{j}\}_{j=1}^{d+1}
are left-invariant, we know that
\Phi_{g}^{*}X^{j}=X^{j}
, and
\Phi_{g}^{*}\overline{X}^{j}=\overline{X}^{j}
for each
1\leq j\leq d+1
and for all
g\in G
. Then we may say that
h
is left-invariant if and only if

\displaystyle\Phi_{g}^{*}h

\displaystyle=\Phi_{g}^{*}(\widehat{h}_{jk})\Phi_{g}^{*}(X^{j}\overline{X}^{k})

\displaystyle=\Phi_{g}^{*}(\widehat{h}_{jk})X^{j}\overline{X}^{k}

\displaystyle=\widehat{h}_{jk}X^{j}\overline{X}^{k}=h,

i.e., iff the scalar functions
\widehat{h}_{jk}
are invariant under the left action of
G
. Since this action is transitive, invariance is equivalent to being constant.
∎

Lastly, the following Lemma is used implicitly (without mention) for the remainder of this paper.

Lemma 3.8
.

If
h
is a Hermitian form on
G
, by definition we have
h(Y,\overline{Z})=\overline{h(Z,\overline{Y})}\foreach Y,Z\in T_{p}G,\ p\in G
. Then this implies for arbitrary
V,W\in T_{p}^{(1,1)}G
that
h(V,W)=\overline{h(\overline{W},\overline{V})}
.

Proof.

Let
V=V_{1}+V_{2}
and
W=W_{1}+W_{2}
, where
V_{1},W_{1}\in T_{p}G
and
V_{2},W_{2}\in\overline{T_{p}G}
. Then we may express:

\displaystyle h(V,W)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\in\{1,2\}}h(V_{i},W_{j})

\displaystyle\overline{h(\overline{W},\overline{V})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\in\{1,2\}}\overline{h(\overline{W_{j}},\overline{V_{i}})}

But then note that:

\displaystyle h(V_{1},W_{1})

\displaystyle=0=\overline{h(\overline{W_{1}},\overline{V_{1}})}

\displaystyle h(V_{1},W_{2})

\displaystyle=\overline{h(\overline{W_{2}},\overline{V_{1}})}

\displaystyle h(V_{2},W_{1})

\displaystyle=0=\overline{h(\overline{W_{1}},\overline{V_{2}})}

\displaystyle h(V_{2},W_{2})

\displaystyle=0=\overline{h(\overline{W_{2}},\overline{V_{2}})}

Thus indeed

h(V,W)=\overline{h(\overline{W},\overline{V})}.

∎

4.
Nonexistence of Invariant Kähler Metrics

We first recall some definitions.

Definition 4.1

(Def. 3.1.1 in
[
HB05
]
, pg 114)
.

We define the
fundamental form
of a Hermitian metric
h=h_{ab}dz^{a}\otimes d\overline{z}^{b}
to be

\omega\coloneqq\frac{i}{2}h_{ab}dz^{a}\wedge d\overline{z}^{b}

Definition 4.2

(Def 3.1.6 in
[
HB05
]
)
.

A
Kähler structure
is a hermitian structure
h
for which the fundamental form
\omega
is closed, i.e.
d\omega=0
. In this case the fundamental
\omega
form is called the Kähler form.

We now state the fundamental form of a left-invariant Hermitian metric in terms of our left-invariant frame.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
h=\widehat{h}_{ab}X^{a}\overline{X}^{b}
be a left-invariant Hermitian metric on a simply connected almost Abelian Lie group
G
. Then the fundamental form of
h
will be

\omega=\frac{i}{2}\widehat{h}_{ab}X^{a}\wedge\overline{X}^{b}.

Proof.

From definition
4.1
we have
\frac{i}{2}h_{ab}dz^{a}\wedge d\overline{z}^{b}
. Recall (see §3.7 in
[
Tu07
]
) that for one forms, say
dz^{a}
and
d\overline{z}^{b}
, the wedge product is

\displaystyle(dz^{a}\wedge d\overline{z}^{b})(V_{1},V_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2}}\sgn(\sigma)dz^{a}(V_{\sigma(1)})d\overline{z}^{b}(V_{\sigma(2)})

\displaystyle=dz^{a}(V_{1})d\overline{z}^{b}(V_{2})-d\overline{z}^{b}(V_{1})dz^{a}(V_{2}).

i.e.,
dz^{a}\wedge d\overline{z}^{b}=dz^{a}\otimes d\overline{z}^{b}-d\overline{z}^{b}\otimes dz^{a}
. We then calculate the following expression for
\omega
,

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}h_{ab}dz^{a}\wedge d\overline{z}^{b}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}[h_{ab}dz^{a}\otimes d\overline{z}^{b}-h_{ab}d\overline{z}^{b}\otimes dz^{a}]

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}[h_{ab}dz^{a}\otimes d\overline{z}^{b}-\overline{\overline{h_{ab}}dz^{b}\otimes d\overline{z}^{a}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}[h_{ab}dz^{a}\otimes d\overline{z}^{b}-\overline{h_{ba}dz^{b}\otimes d\overline{z}^{a}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}[h_{ab}dz^{a}\otimes d\overline{z}^{b}-\overline{h_{ab}dz^{a}\otimes d\overline{z}^{b}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}[h-\overline{h}].

Note that
h=\widehat{h}_{ab}X^{a}\overline{X}^{b}
, so we further have

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}[h-\overline{h}]

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}[\widehat{h}_{ab}X^{a}\overline{X}^{b}-\overline{\widehat{h}_{ab}X^{a}\overline{X}^{b}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}[\widehat{h}_{ab}X^{a}\overline{X}^{b}-\overline{\widehat{h}_{ab}}\overline{X}^{a}X^{b}]

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}[\widehat{h}_{ab}X^{a}\overline{X}^{b}-\widehat{h}_{ba}\overline{X}^{a}X^{b}]

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}[\widehat{h}_{ab}X^{a}\overline{X}^{b}-\widehat{h}_{ab}\overline{X}^{b}X^{a}].

Therefore,
\omega=\frac{i}{2}\widehat{h}_{ab}X^{a}\wedge\overline{X}^{b}
and is the desired form.
∎

We now state the main result for this section.

Theorem 1.1

(Nonexistence of Kähler Metrics)

There are no left-invariant Kähler metrics on a simply connected almost Abelian group.

5.
Generalization to Connected Groups

We use the fact that every connected Lie group
G
is the quotient of a unique simply connected Lie group
\widetilde{G}
(having the same Lie algebra) by a discrete subgroup
\Gamma
in order to generalize our results on Hermitian and Kähler structures to connected complex almost Abelian groups. Namely, we know that the covering map is a quotient map by a discrete subgroup
\Gamma
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\widetilde{G}
be a discrete normal (equiv. central) subgroup. Let
q_{\Gamma}:\widetilde{G}\to G\coloneqq\widetilde{G}/\Gamma
be the standard quotient map. Then the pullback of any Hermitian metric
h
on
G
is a right-
\Gamma
-invariant Hermitian metric
\widetilde{h}
on
\widetilde{G}
, and locally the pullback under
q_{\Gamma}\inv
of any right-
\Gamma
-invariant Hermitian metric
\widetilde{h}
on
\widetilde{G}
yields a Hermitian metric on
G
.

Proof.

Since
q_{\Gamma}
is a smooth covering map, it is a local diffeomorphism. Thus
dq_{\Gamma}
is an isomorphism between tangent spaces at each point, i.e., an invertible linear transformation.
Let
h
be an arbitrary Hermitian structure on
G
.
Then define
\widetilde{h}(Y,Z)\coloneqq q_{\Gamma}^{*}h
. Then

\displaystyle\overline{\widetilde{h}(Y,Z)}

\displaystyle=\overline{h((q_{\Gamma})_{*}Y,(q_{\Gamma})_{*}Z)}

\displaystyle=h((q_{\Gamma})_{*}Z,(q_{\Gamma})_{*}Y)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{h}(Z,Y).

Thus
\widetilde{h}
has Hermitian symmetry at each point. Note that
\widetilde{h}=q_{\Gamma}^{*}h
implies that
\widetilde{h}
is smooth.

We denote elements of
G
as equivalence classes
[p]
of elements
p\in\widetilde{G}
. Now suppose there exists a vector
Y\in T_{p}^{(1,1)}\widetilde{G}
such that
\widetilde{h}(Y,Z)=0
for each
Z\in T_{p}^{(1,1)}\widetilde{G}
. Then we must have
h((q_{\Gamma})_{*}Y,W)=0
for all
W\in T_{[p]}^{(1,1)}\widetilde{G}
(because
(q_{\Gamma})_{*}Z=[dq_{\Gamma}(p)]Z
, and
[dq_{\Gamma}(p)]
was established to be invertible for all
p\in\widetilde{G}
). Thus
(q_{\Gamma})_{*}Y=0
by the non-degeneracy of
h
. But then since once again
(q_{\Gamma})_{*}Y=[dq_{\Gamma}(p)]Y
and
[dq_{\Gamma}(p)]
is invertible, we must have
Y=0
. Thus
\widetilde{h}
is non-degenerate. It is apparent
\widetilde{h}
is positive by definition, and thus it is indeed positive definite. Thus
\widetilde{h}
is a Hermitian metric, and so the pullback of any Hermitian metric on
G
yields a Hermitian metric on
\widetilde{G}
.

For all right-
\Gamma
-invariant Hermitian metrics
\widetilde{h}
on
\widetilde{G}
, define

h(Y_{[p]},Z_{[p]})\coloneqq\widetilde{h}\left([dq_{\Gamma}(p)]\inv Y_{[p]},[dq_{\Gamma}(p)]\inv Z_{[p]}\right),

where it is understood that
[dq_{\Gamma}(p)]\inv:T_{[p]}^{(1,1)}\widetilde{G}\longrightarrow T_{p}^{(1,1)}\widetilde{G}
.
Now note that by the equivariance of
q_{\Gamma}
with respect to the action of right multiplication by
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we have:

\displaystyle h(Y_{[p\gamma]},Z_{[p\gamma]})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{h}\left([dq_{\Gamma}(p\gamma)]\inv Y_{p\gamma},[dq_{\Gamma}(p\gamma)]\inv Z_{p\gamma}\right)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{h}\left([dq_{\Gamma}(p)]\inv Y_{[p]},[dq_{\Gamma}(p)]\inv Z_{[p]}\right)

\displaystyle=h(Y_{[p]},Z_{[p]}).

Therefore,
h
is indeed well-defined. Then

\displaystyle\overline{h(Y_{[p]},Z_{[p]})}

\displaystyle=\overline{\widetilde{h}([dq_{\Gamma}(p)]\inv Y_{[p]},[dq_{\Gamma}(p)]\inv Z_{[p]})}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{h}([dq_{\Gamma}(p)]\inv Z_{[p]},[dq_{\Gamma}(p)]\inv Y_{[p]})

\displaystyle=h(Z_{[p]},Y_{[p]}).

Thus
h
has Hermitian symmetry at each point. Furthermore,
h=\widetilde{h}\circ([dq_{\Gamma}(p)]\inv,[dq_{\Gamma}(p)]\inv)
is smooth because it is the composition of smooth functions.

Now suppose there exists
Y_{[p]}\in T_{[p]}^{(1,1)}\widetilde{G}
such that
h(Y_{[p]},Z_{[p]})=0\foreach Z_{[p]}\in T_{[p]}^{(1,1)}\widetilde{G}
. Similarly as earlier, we must have
\widetilde{h}([dq_{\Gamma}(p)]\inv Y_{[p]},W)=0\foreach W\in T_{p}^{(1,1)}\widetilde{G}
, which occurs if and only if
[dq_{\Gamma}(p)]\inv Y_{[p]}=0
. But then since
dq_{\Gamma}
is invertible, we have
Y_{[p]}=0
, and so
h
is non-degenerate at each point. Thus
h
is a Hermitian metric on
G
.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\widetilde{G}
be a discrete normal (equiv. central) subgroup. Let
q_{\Gamma}:\widetilde{G}\to G\coloneqq\widetilde{G}/\Gamma
be the standard quotient map. Then the pullback of any left-
G
-invariant Hermitian metric
h
on
G
is a right-
\Gamma
-invariant, left-
\widetilde{G}
-invariant Hermitian metric
\widetilde{h}
on
\widetilde{G}
, and locally the pullback under
q_{\Gamma}\inv
of any right-
\Gamma
-invariant, left-
\widetilde{G}
-invariant Hermitian metric
\widetilde{h}
on
\widetilde{G}
yields a left-
G
-invariant Hermitian metric on
G
.

Proof.

By Prop.
5.1
, all that there is to check is that the metric obtained on
G
by a left-
\widetilde{G}
-invariant, right-
\Gamma
-invariant Hermitian metric
\widetilde{h}
on
\widetilde{G}
down to a left-invariant metric on
G
. But then this is apparent from the fact that there is a left action of
\widetilde{G}
on the quotient by
\Gamma
, namely right
\Gamma
multiplication on left coset representatives, and that
q_{\Gamma}
is clearly equivariant with respect to this action.
∎

Similarly for invariant Kähler metrics we have:

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\widetilde{G}
be a discrete normal (equiv. central) subgroup of the simply connected group
\widetilde{G}
. Let
q_{\Gamma}:\widetilde{G}\to G\coloneqq\widetilde{G}/\Gamma
be the standard quotient map. Then all left-
G
-invariant Kähler metrics on
G
pullback to left-
\widetilde{G}
-invariant Kähler metrics on
\widetilde{G}
. Consequentially, there are no left-
G
-invariant Kähler metrics on
G
.

Proof.

Let
h
be a Hermitian metric on
G
with corresponding fundamental form
\omega
. Assume
h
is Kähler, and so
d\omega=0
. Now since the pullback commutes with the exterior derivative, we have that
0=q_{\Gamma}^{*}0=q_{\Gamma}^{*}d\omega=d(q_{\Gamma}^{*}\omega)
.

Let
\widetilde{h}\coloneqq q_{\Gamma}^{*}h
be the Hermitian metric on
\widetilde{G}
induced by the pullback (we know this is indeed a Hermitian metric by Prop.
5.1
). Let
\widetilde{\omega}
be the corresponding fundamental form of
\widetilde{h}
. Thus if
q_{\Gamma}^{*}\omega=\widetilde{\omega}
, then we will have established that
d\widetilde{\omega}=0
, implying
\widetilde{h}
is a Kähler metric on
\widetilde{G}
.

Written locally, we have
\tilde{h}=q_{\Gamma}^{*}h=(h_{ab}\circ q_{\Gamma})q_{\Gamma}^{*}(dz^{a})\otimes q_{\Gamma}^{*}(d\overline{z}^{b})
. Therefore the fundamental form
\widetilde{\omega}
of
\widetilde{h}
will be

\widetilde{\omega}=\frac{i}{2}(h_{ab}\circ q_{\Gamma})q_{\Gamma}^{*}(dz^{a})\wedge q_{\Gamma}^{*}(d\overline{z}^{b}).

Considering the pullback of
\omega
we have

\displaystyle q_{\Gamma}^{*}\omega

\displaystyle=q_{\Gamma}^{*}(\frac{i}{2}h_{ab}dz^{a}\wedge d\overline{z}^{b})

\displaystyle=q_{\Gamma}^{*}\left(\frac{i}{2}h_{ab}(dz^{a}\otimes d\overline{z}^{b}-d\overline{z}^{b}\otimes dz^{a})\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{i}{2}h_{ab}\circ q_{\Gamma}\right)\left[q_{\Gamma}^{*}(dz^{a})\otimes q_{\Gamma}^{*}(d\overline{z}^{b})-q_{\Gamma}^{*}(d\overline{z}^{b})\otimes q_{\Gamma}^{*}(dz^{a})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}(h_{ab}\circ q_{\Gamma})q_{\Gamma}^{*}(dz^{a})\wedge q_{\Gamma}^{*}(d\overline{z}^{b})=\widetilde{\omega}.

∎

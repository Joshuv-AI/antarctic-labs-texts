On the eigenvalues and energy of the A α A_{\alpha} -matrix of graphs
Nijara Konch, A. Bharali, S. Pirzada

Abstract
For a graph G G , the generalized adjacency matrix A α ​ ( G ) A_{\alpha}(G) is the convex combination of the diagonal matrix D ⁡ ( G ) D(G) and the adjacency matrix A ⁡ ( G ) A(G) and is defined as A α ​ ( G ) = α ​ D ​ ( G ) + ( 1 − α ) ​ A ​ ( G ) A_{\alpha}(G)=\alpha D(G)+(1-\alpha)A(G) for 0 ≤ α ≤ 1 0\leq\alpha\leq 1 . This matrix has been found to be useful in merging the spectral theories of A ⁡ ( G ) A(G) and the signless Laplacian matrix Q ⁡ ( G ) Q(G) of the graph G G . The generalized adjacency energy or A α A_{\alpha} -energy is the mean deviation of the A α A_{\alpha} -eigenvalues of G G and is defined as E ⁡ ( A α ​ ( G ) ) = ∑ i = 1 n | p i − 2 ​ α ​ m n | E(A_{\alpha}(G))=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|p_{i}-\frac{2\alpha m}{n}| , where p i p_{i} ’s are A α A_{\alpha} -eigenvalues of G G . In this paper, we investigate the A α A_{\alpha} -eigenvalues of a strongly regular graph G G . We observe that A α A_{\alpha} -spectral radius p 1 p_{1} satisfies δ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ p 1 ≤ Δ ⁡ ( G ) \delta(G)\leq p_{1}\leq\Delta(G) , where δ ⁡ ( G ) \delta(G) and Δ ⁡ ( G ) \Delta(G) are, respectively, the smallest and the largest degrees of G G . Further, we show that the complete graph is the only graph to have exactly two distinct A α A_{\alpha} -eigenvalues. We obtain lower and upper bounds of A α A_{\alpha} -energy in terms of order, size and extremal degrees of G G . We also discuss the extremal cases of these bounds. On
A_{\alpha}
-eigenvalues of a graph

First, we obtain the
A_{\alpha}
-eigenvalues of a strongly regular graph.

Theorem 3.1
.

If
G
is a strongly regular graph with parameters
(n,r,a,c)
having
m
edges, then the
A_{\alpha}
-eigenvalues of
G
are

(i)

r
with multiplicity 1.

(ii)

\dfrac{\{2r\alpha+(1-\alpha)(a-c)\}+\sqrt{d}}{2}
with multiplicity

\dfrac{1}{2}\left[(n-1)+\dfrac{\{2r\alpha+(1-\alpha)(a-c)\}(n-1)+2(r-2\alpha m)}{\sqrt{d}}\right]

(iii)

\dfrac{\{2r\alpha+(1-\alpha)(a-c)\}-\sqrt{d}}{2}
with multiplicity

\dfrac{1}{2}\left[(n-1)-\dfrac{\{2r\alpha+(1-\alpha)(a-c)\}(n-1)+2(r-2\alpha m)}{\sqrt{d}}\right]

where
d=\{2r\alpha+(1-\alpha)(a-c)\}+4\{(r-c)(1-\alpha)^{2}-r\alpha(1-\alpha)(a-c)-r^{2}\alpha^{2}\}
.

Proof.

Throughout the proof, we use
A_{\alpha}
and
D
to denote the
A_{\alpha}(G)
and
D(G)
respectively. We have

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\alpha D+(1-\alpha)A)^{2}=\alpha^{2}D^{2}+(1-\alpha)^{2}A^{2}+2\alpha(1-\alpha)DA

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}r^{2}I+(1-\alpha)^{2}[rI+aA+c(J-I-A)]+2r\alpha(1-\alpha)A

\displaystyle=\left\{\alpha^{2}r^{2}+(1-\alpha)^{2}r-c(1-\alpha)^{2}\right\}I

\displaystyle+\left\{(1-\alpha)^{2}a+2r\alpha(1-\alpha)-c(1-\alpha)^{2}\right\}\frac{1}{(1-\alpha)}\left\{A_{\alpha}-\alpha D\right\}+c(1-\alpha)^{2}J.

This implies that

A_{\alpha}^{2}-\left\{2r\alpha+(1-\alpha)(a-c)\right\}A_{\alpha}-\left\{(r-c)(1-\alpha)^{2}-r\alpha(1-\alpha)(a-c)-r^{2}\alpha^{2}\right\}I=c(1-\alpha)^{2}J.

Since
G
is
r
-regular, so
r
is an eigenvalue of
A_{\alpha}
with eigenvector
1
(i.e.,
n\times 1
column vector with all entries 1). Let
z
be another eigenvector of
A_{\alpha}
corresponding to eigenvalue
\theta\neq r
such that
A_{\alpha}\theta=\theta z
. Then

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}^{2}z-\left\{2r\alpha+(1-\alpha)(a-c)\right\}A_{\alpha}z-\left\{(r-c)(1-\alpha)^{2}-r\alpha(1-\alpha)(a-c)-r^{2}\alpha^{2}\right\}Iz

\displaystyle=c(1-\alpha)^{2}Jz=0

Therefore, as
z\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\theta^{2}-\left\{2r\alpha+(1-\alpha)(a-c)\right\}\theta-\left\{(r-c)(1-\alpha)^{2}-r\alpha(1-\alpha)(a-c)-r^{2}\alpha^{2}\right\}=0

(3.1)

Thus, the remaining eigenvalues of
A_{\alpha}
distinct from
r
which satisfy the equation

\displaystyle x^{2}-\left\{2r\alpha+(1-\alpha)(a-c)\right\}x-\left\{(r-c)(1-\alpha)^{2}-r\alpha(1-\alpha)(a-c)-r^{2}\alpha^{2}\right\}=0

are
\dfrac{\{2r\alpha+(1-\alpha)(a-c)\}+\sqrt{d}}{2}=\theta_{1}
(say) and
\dfrac{\{2r\alpha+(1-\alpha)(a-c)\}-\sqrt{d}}{2}=\theta_{2}
(say), where the discriminant of the quadratic equation is

d=\{2r\alpha+(1-\alpha)(a-c)\}+4\{(r-c)(1-\alpha)^{2}-r\alpha(1-\alpha)(a-c)-r^{2}\alpha^{2}\}.

Using these given parameters for a strongly regular graph, the multiplicities of
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
can be attained. If
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are the multiplicities of
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
, then

m_{1}+m_{2}+1=n
and
m_{1}\theta_{1}+m_{2}\theta_{2}+r=2\alpha m
.
Solving these two equations and replacing
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
, we get

m_{1}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left[(n-1)+\dfrac{\{2r\alpha+(1-\alpha)(a-c)\}(n-1)+2(r-2\alpha m)}{\sqrt{d}}\right]

and

m_{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left[(n-1)-\dfrac{\{2r\alpha+(1-\alpha)(a-c)\}(n-1)+2(r-2\alpha m)}{\sqrt{d}}\right].

For example,
A_{\alpha}
-eigenvalues of the strongly regular graph
(5,2,0,1)
, that is, of the cycle of length

2,\dfrac{1}{2}\left(-1+5\alpha-\sqrt{5}\sqrt{1-2\alpha+\alpha^{2}}\right),\dfrac{1}{2}\left(-1+5\alpha+\sqrt{5}\sqrt{1-2\alpha+\alpha^{2}}\right)
with multiplicities

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
G
be a graph with degree sequence
\{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}\}
. For
0\leq\alpha<1
, the
A_{\alpha}
-spectral radius of
G
satisfies
\delta\leq p_{1}\leq\Delta
. If
G
is connected, then equality in either case holds if and only if
G
is regular.

Proof.

Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})^{T}
be a non-negative eigenvector corresponding to
p_{1}
. Since
(\alpha D+(1-\alpha)A)x=\lambda x
, therefore, for
1\leq k\leq n
, we have

\displaystyle p_{1}x_{k}

\displaystyle=\alpha d_{k}x_{k}+(1-\alpha)\sum_{k\sim j}x_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\Delta x_{k}+(1-\alpha)\Delta x_{k}=\Delta x_{k}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle p_{1}x_{k}

\displaystyle=\alpha d_{k}x_{k}+(1-\alpha)\sum_{k\sim j}x_{j}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\delta x_{k}+(1-\alpha)\delta x_{k}=\delta x_{k}.

That is,
\delta x\leq p_{1}x\leq\Delta x
and therefore, by Proposition
2.9
, we get
\delta\leq p_{1}\leq\Delta
. If
G
is connected and
r
-regular, then
p_{1}=r=\delta=\Delta
. Conversely, if
p_{1}=\delta=\Delta
, then

\displaystyle\delta x_{k}

\displaystyle=\alpha d_{k}x_{k}+(1-\alpha)\sum_{k\sim j}x_{j}=\Delta x_{k},\,\,1\leq k\leq n

which gives
x_{1}=x_{2}=\dots=x_{n}
and
d_{1}=d_{2}=\dots=d_{n}
. Therefore,
G
is a regular graph.

Let
G
be a graph with
A_{\alpha}
-eigenvalues
\beta,\gamma,\tau
with multiplicities say
l,m,k
respectively. Throughout our work it is represented using the notation
\{[\beta]^{l},[\gamma]^{m},[\tau]^{k}\}
. The set of eigenvalues of
A_{\alpha}(G)
called spectrum of

or

-spectrum is denoted by

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
G
be a connected
r
- regular graph with
n
vertices and
m
edges. For
\alpha\in[0,1)
, if
Spec~(A_{\alpha}(G))
=
\{r,[r\alpha+(1-\alpha)]^{a},[r\alpha-(1-\alpha)]^{b}\}
for some non-negative integers

and

, then
a=0
,
b=r=n-1
and
G\cong K_{r+1}
.

Proof.

Here
1+a+b=n
and using Lemma
2.2
, we get

\displaystyle r+a\{r\alpha+(1-\alpha)\}+b\{r\alpha-(1-\alpha)\}=\alpha nr

\displaystyle r^{2}+a\{r\alpha+(1-\alpha)\}^{2}+b\{r\alpha-(1-\alpha)\}^{2}=\alpha^{2}nr^{2}+(1-\alpha)^{2}nr

and hence after simplifying these equations, we obtain
a=0
and
b=r=n-1
. Therefore, spectrum of
A_{\alpha}(G)
reduces to
\{n-1,[n\alpha-1]^{n-1}\}
. Hence
G\cong K_{r+1}=K_{\frac{2m}{n}+1}

Theorem 3.4
.

For
0\leq\alpha<1
, a simple connected graph
G
with order
n\geq 2
has exactly two
A_{\alpha}
-eigenvalues if and only if
G
is isomorphic to complete graph
K_{n}
.

Proof.

If
G\cong K_{n}
, then by Lemma
2.1
, the
A_{\alpha}
-spectrum of
K_{n}
is
\{[n-1]^{1},[\alpha n-1]^{n-1}\}
and hence the sufficient part automatically holds.

Conversely, if possible let
G
posses two distinct eigenvalues say
\beta
and
\gamma
( where
\beta>\gamma
without loss of generality), and is not complete. This hypothesis is not at all true for
n=2
and hence result holds trivially. For
n>2
, connectedness of
G
guarantees that
Spec~(A_{\alpha}(G))
=
\{[\beta]^{1},[\gamma]^{n-1}\}
. Since
G
is not complete, it contains at least one pair of distinct vertices
u,v\in V(G)
, where
d(u)\geq d(v)
, but
uv\notin E(G)
. Therefore, the vertices
u
and
v
induces a principal submatrix of the form
M=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha d(u)&0\\
0&\alpha d(v)\end{bmatrix}
that will be contained in
A_{\alpha}(G)
. Therefore, by Interlacing theorem
2.10
, we have

\gamma=\lambda_{2}(M)=\alpha d(v).
Also, as
G
is connected, there exist some
u^{\prime}\in V(G)
such that
u^{\prime}v\in E(G)
. Thus we can have another principal submatrix
M^{\prime}
of
A_{\alpha}(G)
induced by
u^{\prime}
and
v
, where
M^{\prime}=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha d(v)&1-\alpha\\
1-\alpha&\alpha d(u^{\prime})\end{bmatrix}.

Now, the characteristic polynomial of
M^{\prime}
is

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}-\lambda\alpha\{d(v)+d(u^{\prime})\}+\alpha^{2}d(v)d(u^{\prime})-(1-\alpha)^{2}=0

This implies that

\displaystyle\lambda=\dfrac{\alpha(d(v)+d(u^{\prime}))\pm\sqrt{\alpha^{2}\{d(v)+d(u^{\prime})\}^{2}-4\alpha^{2}d(v)d(u^{\prime})}}{2}.

From Interlacing theorem, we get
\lambda_{n}(A_{\alpha}(G))\leq\lambda_{2}(M^{\prime})\leq\lambda_{2}(A_{\alpha}(G))
.
Therefore

\displaystyle\alpha d(v)=\dfrac{\alpha(d(v)+d(u^{\prime}))-\sqrt{\alpha^{2}\{d(v)+d(u^{\prime})\}^{2}-4\alpha^{2}d(v)d(u^{\prime})}}{2},

implying that

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}(d(v)-d(u^{\prime}))^{2}+4(1-\alpha)^{2}=\alpha^{2}(d(u^{\prime})-d(v))^{2},

which is a contradiction unless
\alpha=1
and hence the proof.

Theorem 4.1
.

(Some modification of Theorem 3. 1 in
[
19
]
)

If
G
is a connected graph of order
n\geq 2
and edges

, then for
\alpha\in[0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle E(A_{\alpha}(G))>\sqrt{2\left(\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\dfrac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}\right)},

where equality can not be attained for connected graph.

Proof.

As
s_{i}=|p_{i}-\dfrac{2\alpha m}{n}|,i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
, we have

E(A_{\alpha}(G))^{2}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}\right)^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}^{2}+2\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}s_{i}s_{j}

(4.2)

Also,

\displaystyle 2\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}s_{i}s_{j}=2\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\bigg|p_{i}-\frac{2\alpha m}{n}\bigg|

\displaystyle\bigg|p_{j}-\frac{2\alpha m}{n}\bigg|=2\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\bigg|\left(p_{i}-\frac{2\alpha m}{n}\right)\left(p_{j}-\frac{2\alpha m}{n}\right)\bigg|

\displaystyle\geq 2\bigg|\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\left(p_{i}-\frac{2\alpha m}{n}\right)\left(p_{j}-\frac{2\alpha m}{n}\right)\bigg|

(4.3)

\displaystyle=2\bigg|\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}p_{i}p_{j}-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}(n-1)}{2n}\bigg|

4.16
is also obvious from Theorem
4.2
for
s_{n}>0
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
G
be a graph with order
n
and size
m
. Then, for
\alpha\in[0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle E(A_{\alpha}(G))\leq\sqrt{\frac{y}{n}}+\sqrt{(n-1)\left(y-\frac{y}{n}\right)},

where
y=\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}
and equality holds if and only if
G\cong K_{2}
or
G\cong\frac{n}{2}K_{2}.

Proof.

Let
\eta_{i}=p_{i}-\frac{2\alpha m}{n}
for
i\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, where
\eta_{1}\geq\eta_{2}\geq\dots\geq\eta_{n}
. For
\alpha\in[0,1)
, we have
p_{1}\geq\frac{2m}{n}>\frac{2\alpha m}{n}
and thus by using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we get

\displaystyle E(A_{\alpha}(G))

\displaystyle=\big|\eta_{1}\big|+\sum_{i=2}^{n}\big|\eta_{i}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\eta_{1}+\sqrt{(n-1)\sum_{i=2}^{n}\eta_{i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\eta_{1}+\sqrt{(n-1)\left(\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}-\eta_{1}^{2}\right)}\,\,(\text{using Lemma \ref{lemma 2}(iii)})

Now, consider the function
f(x)=x+\sqrt{(n-1)\left(\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}-x^{2}\right)}
on the defined domain
0<x\leq\sqrt{\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}}
where the variable
x
stands for
\eta_{1}
. Then

f^{\prime}(x)=1-\dfrac{x\sqrt{n-1}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}-x^{2}}}

and so
f
is decreasing on

U=\Bigg\{x\bigg|\sqrt{\dfrac{\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}}{n}}\leq x\leq\sqrt{\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}}\Bigg\}

and is increasing on

V=\Bigg\{x\bigg|0<x\leq\sqrt{\dfrac{\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}}{n}}\Bigg\}.

Let
y=\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}
. Now, if
\eta_{1}\in V
, we have

\displaystyle E(A_{\alpha}(G))

\displaystyle\leq f(\eta_{1})\leq f(\sqrt{\dfrac{y}{n}})

(4.17)

\displaystyle=f(\sqrt{\dfrac{y}{n}}+\sqrt{(n-1)(y-\dfrac{y}{n})})

(4.18)

Therefore, the first part of Equation
4.17
is an equality if and only if
\eta_{2}=\eta_{3}=\dots=\eta_{n}
and the second part of Equation
4.17
is an equality if and only if
\eta_{1}=\sqrt{\dfrac{\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}}{n}}
. Finally, Equation
4.17
is an equality if and only if
\eta_{1}=\sqrt{\dfrac{\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}}{n}}
and
\eta_{i}=p_{i}-\dfrac{2\alpha m}{n}=\pm\sqrt{\dfrac{\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}}{n}}
(using Lemma
2.2
(iii)) for
i\in\{2,3,\dots,n\}.

that is,
p_{1}=\sqrt{\dfrac{\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}}{n}}+\dfrac{2\alpha m}{n}
and
p_{i}\in\Bigg\{\sqrt{\dfrac{\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}}{n}}+\dfrac{2\alpha m}{n},-\sqrt{\dfrac{\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}}{n}}+\dfrac{2\alpha m}{n}\Bigg\}
for
i\in\{2,3,\dots,n\}
.

Case 1.

G
is connected, that is, the spectral radius of
G
has multiplicity 1. So
Spec~(G)=\Bigg\{\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}}{n}}+\frac{2\alpha m}{n},\Bigr[-\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}}{n}}+\frac{2\alpha m}{n}\Bigr]^{n-1}\Bigg\}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\Biggl(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}}{n}}+\frac{2\alpha m}{n}\Biggr)+(n-1)\Biggl(-\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}}{n}}+\frac{2\alpha m}{n}\Biggr)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}}{n}}(2-n)+2\alpha m,

which will satisfy Lemma
2.2
(i) provided
n=2
. Therefore, for a connected graph, equality in Equation
4.18
happens if and only if
G\cong K_{2}
.

Case 2.
If
G
is disconnected, then there exists some natural number
l
, where
2\leq l<n
such that
A_{\alpha}
- spectrum of
G
is
\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}}{n}}+\frac{2\alpha m}{n},-\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}}{n}}+\frac{2\alpha m}{n}
with multiplicity
l
and
n-l
respectively. Then we have

\displaystyle l\Biggl(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}}{n}}+\frac{2\alpha m}{n}\Biggr)+(n-l)\Biggl(-\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}}{n}}+\frac{2\alpha m}{n}\Biggr)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}}{n}}(2l-n)+2\alpha m,

which will satisfy Lemma
2.2
(i) provided
2l+n=0
, that is,
l=\frac{n}{2}
, which implies that
n
is even natural such that
n\geq 2
. Since
G
is disconnected and so it is obvious that
G\cong\frac{n}{2}K_{2}
.

Again, if
\eta_{1}\in U
, that is

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}}{n}}\leq\eta_{1}\leq\sqrt{\alpha^{2}M_{1}+(1-\alpha)^{2}2m-\frac{4\alpha^{2}m^{2}}{n}}

and hence same as Equation
4.18
. Therefore the conclusion follows as above.

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
G
be a connected graph with
n
vertices and
m
edges. Then

\displaystyle E(A_{\alpha}(G))\leq n\sqrt{\frac{2m(1-\alpha)^{2}}{n}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}(\Delta-\delta)^{2}},

where equality holds if and only if
G\cong K_{2}.

Proof.

Here the inequality can be obtained by using Lemma
2.12
. Also equality holds if and only if
G
is regular and
n=2
which implies
G\cong K_{2}.

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
G
be a regular graph with order
n
and size
m
. Then

\displaystyle E(A_{\alpha}(G))\leq n(1-\alpha)\sqrt{\frac{2m}{n}}

with equality if and only if
G\cong K_{2}
.

Proof.

This can be attained from Theorem
4.8
considering
G
to be regular.

5
Conclusion

In this present work, we have obtained
A_{\alpha}
-spectra of strongly regular graphs with given parameters and some results on
A_{\alpha}
-eigenvalues of a graph. We have given a modification of a lower bound of
A_{\alpha}
-energy of a graph
G
, obtained in
[
19
]
. Further, we have obtained some bounds of
A_{\alpha}
-energy of graphs in terms of given order, size, Zagreb index and extremal degrees of
G
. Equality cases are also analyzed and extremal graphs are obtained.

A new Discrete Analysis Of Fourth Order Elliptic Variational Inequalities
Yahya Alnashri

Abstract
. This paper applies the gradient discretisation method (GDM) for fourth order elliptic variational inequalities. The GDM provides a new formulation of error estimates and a complete discrete analysis of different approximating schemes. We show that the convergence is unconditional. Classical assumptions on data are only sufficient to establish the convergence results. These results are applicable to all schemes that fall in the framework of GDM.

2.
Continuous and discrete setting

Let
{\Omega}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(
d>1
) be a bounded connected domain with boundary
\partial{\Omega}
,
f\in L^{2}({\Omega})
, the barrier
\psi\in C^{2}({\Omega})\cap C(\overline{{\Omega}})
and
\psi\geq 0
on
\partial{\Omega}
. In this paper, we study here the following fourth order variational inequality: Seek
\bar{c}\in\mathcal{K}
satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Delta\bar{c}(\boldsymbol{x})\Delta(\bar{c}-\varphi)(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\leq\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(\boldsymbol{x})(\bar{c}(\boldsymbol{x})-\varphi(\boldsymbol{x}))\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x},\quad\forall\varphi\in{\mathcal{K}},

(2.1)

where the non empty closed convex set
{\mathcal{K}}
is defined by

{\mathcal{K}}:=\{\varphi\in H_{0}^{2}({\Omega})\;:\;\varphi\leq\psi\mbox{ in }{\Omega}\}.

Note that the standard theory founded in
[
16
]
shows that the above problem is well-posed. We see that the model can be formulated by the following equivalent energy minimization equation:

\mbox{Find $\bar{c}\in{\mathcal{K}}$ such that },\quad\argminB_{\varphi\in{\mathcal{K}}}\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Delta\bar{c}(\boldsymbol{x})\Delta\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(\boldsymbol{x})\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}.

Here, we define the discrete elements (called gradient discretisation) to construct the approximation scheme for our problem.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
{\Omega}
be an open domain of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(
d>1
). A gradient discretisation
{\mathcal{D}}
for fourth order obstacle problems is give by a family
{\mathcal{D}}=(X_{{\mathcal{D}},0},\Pi_{\mathcal{D}},\nabla_{\mathcal{D}},\Delta_{\mathcal{D}})
, where:

•

The discrete set
X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}
is a finite-dimensional vector space on
{\mathbb{R}}
, dealing with the unknowns of the method.

•

The linear operator
\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}:X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}\to L^{2}({\Omega})
is the reconstruction of the approximate function.

•

The linear operator
\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}:X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}\to L^{2}({\Omega})^{d}
is the reconstruction of the gradient of the function.

•

\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}:X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}\to L^{2}({\Omega})
is a linear mapping to construct a discrete of the bi-harmonic
\Delta
form, and must be defined so that
\|\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}\cdot\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}
is a norm on
X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a gradient discretisation. The discrete version of the priblem(
2.1
) is given by

\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}&\mbox{ Find $c\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{D}}:=\{\varphi\in X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}\;:\;\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi\leq\psi\}$ such that, for all $\varphi\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{D}}$},\\
&\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}c(\boldsymbol{x})\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}(c-\varphi)(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\leq\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(\boldsymbol{x})\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}(c(\boldsymbol{x})-\varphi(\boldsymbol{x}))\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}.\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

The accuracy of this approximation can be measured by the following three indicators; the first one is the constant
{\mathbb{C}}_{\mathcal{D}}
, which measures the coercivity and is defined by

{\mathbb{C}}_{\mathcal{D}}=\displaystyle\max_{\omega\in X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}\setminus\{0\}}\left(\frac{\|\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\omega\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}}{\|\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}\omega\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}},\;\frac{\|\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}\omega\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})^{d}}}{\|\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}\omega\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}}\right).

(2.3)

It implies the discrete Poincaré inequalities, for any
w\in X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}
,

\|\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\omega\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}\leq{\mathbb{C}}_{\mathcal{D}}\|\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}\omega\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}\mbox{ and }\|\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}\omega\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})^{d}}\leq{\mathbb{C}}_{\mathcal{D}}\|\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}\omega\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}.

(2.4)

The second quantity is the function
{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathcal{D}}:{\mathcal{K}}\to{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{D}}
, which measures the interpolation error and it is given by: For all
v\in{\mathcal{K}}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathcal{D}}(v)=\displaystyle\min_{\omega\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{D}}}\Big(\|\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\omega

\displaystyle-v\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}+\|\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}\omega-\nabla v\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})^{d}}

(2.5)

\displaystyle+\|\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}\omega-\Delta v\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}\Big).

The last one is the function
{\mathbb{W}}_{\mathcal{D}}:H_{\Delta}({\Omega})\to{\mathbb{R}}
, which refers to the limit–conformity and it is defined by: For
v\in H_{\Delta}({\Omega})
,

{\mathbb{W}}_{\mathcal{D}}(v)=\displaystyle\max_{\omega\in X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{\|\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}\omega\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big(\Delta v\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\omega-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}v\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}\omega\big)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x},

(2.6)

where
H_{\Delta}({\Omega}):=\left\{v\in L^{2}({\Omega})\;:\;\Delta v\in L^{2}({\Omega})\right\}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
({\mathcal{D}}_{m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of a gradient discretisation in the sense of definition
2.1
. We say that

•

({\mathcal{D}}_{m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is coercive if there exists
{\mathbb{C}}_{P}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
such that
{\mathbb{C}}_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}\leq{\mathbb{C}}_{P}
for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

•

({\mathcal{D}}_{m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is consistent if

\mbox{for all }v\in{\mathcal{K}},\quad\lim_{m\to\infty}{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathcal{D}}(v)=0.

•

({\mathcal{D}}_{m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is limit–conforming if

\mbox{for all }v\in H_{\Delta}({\Omega}),\quad\lim_{m\to\infty}{\mathbb{W}}_{\mathcal{D}}(v)=0.

•

({\mathcal{D}}_{m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is compact if for any sequence
(\omega_{m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
X_{{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}},0}
, such that
\|\Delta_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}\omega_{m}\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}
is a bounded, then there exists
\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}({\Omega})
, such that the sequence
(\Pi_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}\omega_{m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges strongly to
\varphi
in
L^{2}({\Omega})
, as
m\to\infty
.

3.
Main results

Let us now introduce the main results; the general error estimates and the convergence results. We begin with defining the continuous interpolant
I_{\mathcal{D}}:{\mathcal{K}}\to{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{D}}
by

\displaystyle I_{\mathcal{D}}h=\argminB_{\omega\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{D}}}\big(\|\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\omega-h\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}

\displaystyle+\|\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}\omega-\nabla h\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})^{d}}

(3.1)

\displaystyle+\|\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}w-\Delta h\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}\big).

Thus, from the definition of
{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathcal{D}}
, we have, for any
v\in{\mathcal{K}}
,

\|\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}I_{\mathcal{D}}v-v\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}+\|\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}I_{\mathcal{D}}v-\nabla v\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})^{d}}+\|\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}I_{\mathcal{D}}v-\Delta v\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}\leq{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathcal{D}}(v).

(3.2)

Theorem 3.1

(Convergence rates)
.

Assume that
{\Omega}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\;(d>1)
is a bounded domain with the boundary
\partial{\Omega}
,
f\in L^{2}({\Omega})
,
\psi\in C^{2}({\Omega})\cap C(\overline{{\Omega}})
,
\psi\geq 0
on
\partial{\Omega}
, and
\bar{c}
be the solution to (
2.1
). Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a gradient discretisation such that
{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{D}}\neq\Phi
, then the discrete scheme (
2.2
) has a unique solution
c\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{D}}
. Moreover, if it is assumed that
\Delta^{2}\bar{c}\in L^{2}({\Omega})
, then:

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}c

\displaystyle-\bar{c}\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{C}}_{\mathcal{D}}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}{\mathbb{W}}_{\mathcal{D}}(\Delta\bar{c})+({\mathbb{C}}_{\mathcal{D}}\frac{\sqrt{2}+2}{2}+1){\mathbb{S}}_{\mathcal{D}}(\bar{c})+{\mathbb{C}}_{\mathcal{D}}R_{\mathcal{D}}(\bar{c})^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}c

\displaystyle-\nabla\bar{c}\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})^{d}}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{C}}_{\mathcal{D}}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}{\mathbb{W}}_{\mathcal{D}}(\Delta\bar{c})+({\mathbb{C}}_{\mathcal{D}}\frac{\sqrt{2}+2}{2}+1){\mathbb{S}}_{\mathcal{D}}(\bar{c})+{\mathbb{C}}_{\mathcal{D}}R_{\mathcal{D}}(\bar{c})^{\frac{1}{2}},

\|\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}c-\Delta\bar{c}\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}W_{\mathcal{D}}(\Delta\bar{c})+\frac{\sqrt{2}+2}{2}{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathcal{D}}(\bar{c})+R_{\mathcal{D}}(\bar{c})^{\frac{1}{2}},

(3.5)

where
R_{\mathcal{D}}(\bar{c}):=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\Delta^{2}\bar{c}+f)(\psi-\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}
.

Proof.

The existence and uniqueness of the discrete solution follow from Stampacchia’s theorem
[
16
]
, thanks to the assumption that
{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{D}}
is a non empty set. Under the regularity
\Delta^{2}\bar{c}\in L^{2}({\Omega})
, we can apply (
2.6
) to
v:=\Delta\bar{c}\in H_{\Delta}({\Omega})
with taking

to deduce

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Delta\bar{c}(\boldsymbol{x})\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}(I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}-c)(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Delta^{2}\bar{c}(\boldsymbol{x})\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}(I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}-c)(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}(I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}-c)\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}{\mathbb{W}}_{\mathcal{D}}(\Delta\bar{c}),

where
I_{\mathcal{D}}
is the interpolant defined by (
3.1
). Since
c
is the solution to (
2.2
), the above inequality implies

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}(I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}-c)(\boldsymbol{x})(\Delta\bar{c}(\boldsymbol{x})-\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}c(\boldsymbol{x}))\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Delta^{2}\bar{c}(\boldsymbol{x})\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}(I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}-c)(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq\|\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}(I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}-c)\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}{\mathbb{W}}_{\mathcal{D}}(\Delta\bar{c})-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(\boldsymbol{x})\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}(I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}-c)(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}.

Thanks to the regularity assumption
\Delta^{2}\bar{c}\in L^{2}({\Omega})
, taking
\varphi:=\bar{c}-v
(with a non negative
v\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\Omega})
) as a generic function in (
2.1
) shows that
(f+\Delta^{2}\bar{c})\geq 0
for a.e. in
{\Omega}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Delta^{2}\bar{c}(\boldsymbol{x})\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}(I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}-c)(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\Delta^{2}\bar{c}(\boldsymbol{x})+f(\boldsymbol{x}))\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}(I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}-c)(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(\boldsymbol{x})\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}(I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}-c)(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\Delta^{2}\bar{c}(\boldsymbol{x})+f(\boldsymbol{x}))(\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}(\boldsymbol{x})-\psi(\boldsymbol{x}))\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\Delta^{2}\bar{c}(\boldsymbol{x})+f(\boldsymbol{x}))(\psi(\boldsymbol{x})-\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}(\boldsymbol{x}))

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(\boldsymbol{x})\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}(I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}-c)(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle\geq\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\Delta^{2}\bar{c}(\boldsymbol{x})+f(\boldsymbol{x}))(\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}(\boldsymbol{x})-\psi(\boldsymbol{x}))\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(\boldsymbol{x})\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}(I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}(\boldsymbol{x})-c(\boldsymbol{x}))\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x},

since the quantity
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\Delta^{2}\bar{c}(\boldsymbol{x})+f(\boldsymbol{x}))(\psi(\boldsymbol{x})-\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}(\boldsymbol{x}))\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\geq 0
. Substituting the above relation in (
3.6
) yields

\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}(I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}(\boldsymbol{x})-c(\boldsymbol{x}))(\Delta\bar{c}(\boldsymbol{x})-\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}c(\boldsymbol{x}))\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\leq\|\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}(I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}-c)\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}{\mathbb{W}}_{\mathcal{D}}(\Delta\bar{c})+R_{\mathcal{D}}(\bar{c}).

Introduce the term
\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}
and apply the Cauchy–Schwarz’s inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}(I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}

\displaystyle-c)\|^{2}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}(I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}(\boldsymbol{x})-c(\boldsymbol{x}))(\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}(\boldsymbol{x})-\Delta\bar{c}(\boldsymbol{x}))\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle+\|\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}(I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}-c)\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}{\mathbb{W}}_{\mathcal{D}}(\Delta\bar{c})+R_{\mathcal{D}}(\bar{c})^{+}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}(I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}-c)\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}\big(\|\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}-\Delta\bar{c}\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}+{\mathbb{W}}_{\mathcal{D}}(\Delta\bar{c})\big)

\displaystyle+R_{\mathcal{D}}(\bar{c})^{+},

which leads to, thanks to (

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}(I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}-c)\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}^{2}\leq\|\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}(I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}-c)\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}\big({\mathbb{W}}_{\mathcal{D}}(\Delta\bar{c})+{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathcal{D}}(\bar{c})\big)+R_{\mathcal{D}}(\bar{c})^{+}.

Applying Young’s inequality to this relation gives

\|\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}I_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{c}-\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}c\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}\leq\big(\frac{1}{2}\left({\mathbb{W}}_{\mathcal{D}}(\Delta\bar{c})+{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathcal{D}}(\bar{c})\right)^{2}+R_{\mathcal{D}}(\bar{c})^{+}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.8)

This inequality with the use of triangle inequality, the fact that
\forall a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+},\;(a+b)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq a^{\frac{1}{2}}+b^{\frac{1}{2}}
and (
3.2

Applying

again establishes Estimate (
3.5
). Estimate (
3.4

Theorem 3.2

(Convergence)
.

Assume that
{\Omega}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\;(d>1)
is a bounded domain with the boundary
\partial{\Omega}
,
f\in L^{2}({\Omega})
,
\psi\in C^{2}({\Omega})\cap C(\overline{{\Omega}})
,
\psi\geq 0
on
\partial{\Omega}
, and let
({\mathcal{D}}_{m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of a gradient discretisation, that is coercive, consistent, limit–conforming and compact. Let

be the solution to (
2.1
). If

) (with

), and, as

\Pi_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}c_{m}
converges strongly to
\bar{c}
in

,

Proof.

Let

and

in (
2.2
), where

is defined by (

) with

. Use the discrete relation (
2.4
) to obtain

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}c_{m}\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}\|\Pi_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}(c_{m}-I_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}\bar{c})\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}

\displaystyle+\|\Delta_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}c_{m}\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}\|\Delta_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}I_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}\bar{c}\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{C}}_{P}\|f\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}\|\Delta_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}(c_{m}-I_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}\bar{c})\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}

\displaystyle+\|\Delta_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}c_{m}\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}\|\Delta_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}I_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}\bar{c}\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})},

where

(3.9)

where

In order to obtain the strong convergence of

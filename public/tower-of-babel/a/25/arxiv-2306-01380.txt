q -crossed modules and q -capability of Lie algebras
Emzar Khmaladze 1, Manuel Ladra 2

Abstract
. Given a non-negative integer q , we study two different notions of the q -capability of Lie algebras via the non-abelian q -exterior product of Lie algebras. The first is related to the q -crossed modules and inner q -derivations, and the second is the Lie algebra version of the q -capability of groups proposed by Ellis in 1995.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
18G10, 18G50.

Introduction

1.1.
A brief overview of mod
q
theories

Since the 1980s, many important articles have been published devoted to studying modulo
q
(also called finite) versions of algebraic and topological theories. Neisendorfer
[
20
]
developed the homotopy theory with coefficients in the abelian group
\mathbb{Z}/{\textsl{q}}\mathbb{Z}
(primary homotopy theory) and studied such essential
topics as mod
q
Hurewicz theorems, exponents of mod
q
homotopy groups, mod
q
Samelson products and so on.
Browder
[
5
]
investigated the algebraic

-theory with
\mathbb{Z}/{\textsl{q}}\mathbb{Z}
coefficients (the mod
q
algebraic
K
-theory).
Suslin and Voevodsky
[
26
]
calculated the mod
2
algebraic
K
-theory of the integers due to Voevodsky’s solution of the Milnor conjecture. Karoubi and Lambre
[
15
]
constructed the Dennis trace map from mod
q
algebraic
K
-theory to mod
q
Hochschild homology, having an unexpected relationship with number theory.

A considerable number of papers are devoted to investigating the non-abelian mod
q
tensor and exterior products of groups; see, for example,
[
6
,
8
,
11
,
12
]
. It is essential to note the applications of these constructions in group homologies with
\mathbb{Z}/{\textsl{q}}\mathbb{Z}
coefficients and in the description of the universal
q
-central extensions. Furthermore, they are used in developing the mod
q
(co)homology theory of groups by Conduché, H. Inassaridze, and N. Inassaridze
[
7
]
.

The mod
q
versions of Lie algebras’s non-abelian tensor and exterior products
[
10
,
9

were developed in
[
17
,
18
]
. Among other results, applications of the mod

non-abelian tensor and exterior products to the Lie algebra homologies with coefficients in the ground ring modulo

, and to the description of the universal

-central relative extensions of Lie algebras are obtained.

1.2.
Shortly on capability and
q
-capability of groups

The capability of groups was first studied by Baer
[
1
]
, who classified all capable groups among the finitely generated abelian groups. Regarding the classification of prime-power groups, Hall
[
13
]
found it important and interesting to investigate what condition a group must satisfy to be isomorphic to a central quotient group of another group, or equivalently, to be isomorphic to an inner automorphism group of another group.
This laid the foundation for introducing and investigating the concept of capable groups. We say that
a group
G
is capable
if there exists a group
H
such that
G\cong H/\Z(H)
, or equivalently,
G\cong\operatorname{InnAut}(H)
, where
\Z(H)
denotes the center and
\operatorname{InnAut}(H)
represents the group of inner automorphisms of
H
. An overview of the basic theory on the capability of groups is given in
[
4
]
. In our interest, it is also essential to mention the article
[
3
]
, where the notion of the epicenter
\Z^{*}(G)
of a group
G
is introduced and proved that
G
is capable if and only if
\Z^{*}(G)
is the trivial subgroup of
G
.

Later on, using the non-abelian exterior square of groups, Ellis introduced the exterior center
\Z^{\wedge}(G)
of a group
G
in
[
11
]
and proved that
G
is capable if and only if
\Z^{\wedge}(G)\cong\{1\}
. In fact, he worked more generally in the modulo q setting, and the result mentioned is the case when
{\textsl{q}}=0
. More concretely, for any non-negative integer
q
, he introduced the
q
-capability of a group and gave its necessary and sufficient condition via the non-abelian
q
-exterior square introduced in
[
12
]
.

Recently, a different notion of a
q
-capable group was introduced and studied in
[
2
]
, which seems more natural to us than the one in
[
11
]
. The ideas of
[
2
]
inspired us to export similar results for Lie algebras in the modulo
q
setting.

2.
q
-crossed modules of Lie algebras

Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
be two Lie algebras.
A Lie (left-)action
of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}

is a
R
-bilinear map
\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}
,
(g,h)\mapsto{}^{g}h
such that the following conditions hold

•

{}^{[g,g^{\prime}]}h={}^{g}(^{g^{\prime}}h)-{}^{g^{\prime}}(^{g}h)
,

•

{}^{g}[h,h^{\prime}]=[{}^{g}h,h^{\prime}]+[h,{}^{g}h^{\prime}]
.

For example, if
\mathfrak{h}
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
, then the Lie bracket in
\mathfrak{g}
yields a Lie action of

\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
.

Let

0\to\mathfrak{h}\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}\mathfrak{e}\overset{\pi}{\longrightarrow}\mathfrak{g}\to 0

be a central extension of Lie algebras, i.e.
\Ker\pi
is contained in the center

\Z(\mathfrak{e})=\big\{e\in\mathfrak{e}\mid[e,x]=0\>\text{for each}\>x\in\mathfrak{e}\big\}

of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{e}
. Take
g\in\mathfrak{g}
and choose
e_{g}\in\mathfrak{e}
such that
\pi(e_{g})=g
. Then, for any
h\in\mathfrak{h}
and
e\in\mathfrak{e}
one sets

{}^{g}h=i^{-1}\big([e_{g},i(h)]\big)\quad\text{and}\quad{}^{g}e=[e_{g},e].

These elements are independent on the choice of
e_{g}
, and we get Lie actions of
\mathfrak{g}
on both
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{e}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra.

A
q
-crossed
\mathfrak{g}
-module

(\mathfrak{h},\mu)
is a homomorphism of Lie algebras
\mu\colon\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
with an action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
satisfying

(i)

\mu({}^{g}h)=[g,\mu(h)]
,

(ii)

{}^{\mu(h)}h^{\prime}=[h,h^{\prime}]
,

(iii)

{\textsl{q}}k=0

for all
g\in\mathfrak{g}
,
h,h^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{h}
and
k\in\Ker\mu
.

Obviously, for
{\textsl{q}}=0
, this is just the definition of
a crossed
\mathfrak{g}
-module
, see e.g.
[
16
]
.

A morphism of
q
-crossed
\mathfrak{g}
-modules

\varphi\colon(\mathfrak{h},\mu)\to(\mathfrak{h}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-equivariant homomorphism of Lie algebras
\varphi\colon\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
, i.e.
\varphi({}^{g}h)={}^{g}{\varphi(h)}
for all
g\in\mathfrak{g}
and
h\in\mathfrak{h}
, such that
\mu^{\prime}\varphi=\mu
.

It follows immediately from Definition
2.1
the following

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}\mathfrak{e}\overset{\pi}{\longrightarrow}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow 0

be a
q
-central extension of Lie algebras, that is,
\Ker\pi
is contained in the
q
-center

\Z_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{e})=\big\{e\in\Z(\mathfrak{e})\mid{\textsl{q}}e=0\big\}

of
\mathfrak{e}
(see
[
17
]
). Then
\pi\colon\mathfrak{e}\to\mathfrak{g}
with the above-described Lie action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{e}
, is a
q
-crossed
\mathfrak{g}
-module.

Conversely, given a
q
-crossed
\mathfrak{g}
-module
\mu\colon\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
, it follows from the definition that
\Ker\mu
is a
q
-central ideal of
\mathfrak{h}
. Thus, if
\mu
is an epimorphism, then it gives rise to a
q
-central extension

0\longrightarrow\Ker\mu{\longrightarrow}\mathfrak{e}\overset{\mu}{\longrightarrow}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow 0.

The existence of universal
q
-central extensions of Lie algebras was studied in
[
17
]
, where additionally they are described via the non-abelian exterior product modulo
q
. This gives us another important example of
q
-crossed modules of Lie algebras and will be presented below in this section.

First, we need to recall that, given a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and two crossed
\mathfrak{g}
-modules
\mathfrak{m}\to\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{n}\to\mathfrak{g}
,
the non-abelian
q
-tensor and

\mathfrak{m}\otimes^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{n}
and

\mathfrak{n}\wedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{m}
are defined and studied in

In this paper, we need just to use these notions for the identity

and the inclusion
\mathfrak{h}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
crossed
\mathfrak{g}
-modules, where
\mathfrak{h}
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Definition 2.3
.

(
[
17
,
18
]
)
The non-abelian
q
-tensor product
of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and its ideal
\mathfrak{h}
, for
{\textsl{q}}\geq 1
,
is the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}\otimes^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}
generated by symbols
h\otimes g
and
\{h\}

with
g\in\mathfrak{g}
,
h\in\mathfrak{h}
subject to the following relations for
h,h^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{h}
,
g,g^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}
and
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\textsf{R}
:

\displaystyle\lambda(h\otimes g)=\lambda h\otimes g=h\otimes\lambda g,

(1)

\displaystyle(h+h^{\prime})\otimes g=h\otimes g+h^{\prime}\otimes g,

(2)

\displaystyle h\otimes(g+g^{\prime})=h\otimes g+h\otimes g^{\prime},

(3)

\displaystyle[h,h^{\prime}]\otimes g=h\otimes[h^{\prime},g]-h^{\prime}\otimes[h,g],

(4)

\displaystyle h\otimes[g,g^{\prime}]=[g^{\prime},h]\otimes g-[g,h]\otimes g^{\prime},

(5)

\displaystyle[h\otimes g,h^{\prime}\otimes g^{\prime}]=[h,g]\otimes[h^{\prime},g^{\prime}],

(6)

\displaystyle[\{h^{\prime}\},h\otimes g]=[{\textsl{q}}h^{\prime},h]\otimes g+h\otimes[{\textsl{q}}h^{\prime},g],

(7)

\displaystyle\{\lambda h+\lambda^{\prime}h^{\prime}\}=\lambda\{h\}+\lambda^{\prime}\{h^{\prime}\},

(8)

\displaystyle[\{h\},\{h^{\prime}\}]={\textsl{q}}h\otimes{\textsl{q}}h^{\prime},

(9)

\displaystyle\{[h,g]\}={\textsl{q}}(h\otimes g).

(10)

For
{\textsl{q}}=0
, we define
\mathfrak{h}\otimes^{0}g
to be the non-abelian tensor product

,

Further,
the non-abelian
q
-exterior product
,
\mathfrak{h}\wedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}
,
{\textsl{q}}\geq 0
, of
\mathfrak{g}
and its ideal
\mathfrak{h}
is the quotient of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}\otimes^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}
by the relation

h\otimes h=0,\quad h\in\mathfrak{h}.

(11)

Let us denote by
h\wedge g
the coset of

in
\mathfrak{h}\wedge^{q}\mathfrak{g}
.
There is a natural epimorphism of Lie algebras

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be an ideal of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.

i)

There is a Lie homomorphism

\xi^{\otimes}\colon\mathfrak{h}\otimes^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}

defined on generators by

\xi^{\otimes}(h\otimes g)=[h,g]\quad\text{and}\quad\xi^{\otimes}(\{h\})={\textsl{q}}h.

Moreover,
\xi^{\otimes}
factors through the non-abelian
q
-exterior product
\mathfrak{h}\wedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}
, and there is an induced Lie homomorphism

\xi^{\wedge}\colon\mathfrak{h}\wedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
.

ii)

If
x\in\mathfrak{h}\otimes^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}
(resp.
x\in\mathfrak{h}\wedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}
) then
\{\xi^{\otimes}(x)\}={\textsl{q}}x
(resp.
\{\xi^{\wedge}(x)\}={\textsl{q}}x
).

Proof.

i) It is readily seen that
\xi^{\otimes}
commutes with all defining relations
1
–(
11
).

ii) First, note that
\Img\xi^{\otimes}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}
(resp.
\Img\xi^{\wedge}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}
).

For the generators
h\otimes g,\{h\}\in\mathfrak{h}\otimes^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\displaystyle\{\xi^{\otimes}(h\otimes g)\}=\{[h,g]\}={\textsl{q}}(h\otimes g)\quad\text{by \eqref{t10}},

\displaystyle\{\xi^{\otimes}({\{h\}})\}=\{{\textsl{q}}h\}={\textsl{q}}\{h\}\quad\text{by \eqref{t8}}.

Now, if
x\in\mathfrak{h}\otimes^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}
(resp.
x\in\mathfrak{h}\wedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}
) is any element, the defining relations (
6
)–(
9
) guarantee that the identity

\{\xi^{\otimes}(x)\}={\textsl{q}}x
(resp.
\{\xi^{\wedge}(x)\}={\textsl{q}}x
) holds.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

There is a Lie action of

\displaystyle{}^{g^{\prime}}(h\otimes g)=[g^{\prime},h]\otimes g+h\otimes[g^{\prime},g],\quad{}^{g}\{h\}=\{[g,h]\}

-crossed

Proof.

The verification that the given equations define the Lie action of

3.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an abelian Lie algebra, i.e.
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=0
.
Then
\mathfrak{g}\otimes^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}
(resp.
\mathfrak{g}\wedge^{q}\mathfrak{g}
) is an abelian Lie algebra and there is an isomorphism:

\mathfrak{g}\otimes^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{g}\times\big((\mathfrak{g}/{\textsl{q}}\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\textsf{R}_{{\textsl{q}}}}(\mathfrak{g}/{\textsl{q}}\mathfrak{g})\big)

(\text{resp.}\quad\mathfrak{g}\wedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{g}\times\big((\mathfrak{g}/{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g})\wedge_{\textsf{R}_{{\textsl{q}}}}(\mathfrak{g}/{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g})\big)\ ).

Proof.

From the defining relations, it is easy to see that
\mathfrak{g}\otimes^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}
is abelian. Moreover,

{\textsl{q}}(h\otimes g)=\{[h,g]\}=\{0\}=0,

for each
g,h\in\mathfrak{g}
. Therefore, there is the following diagram of (abelian) Lie algebras

\textstyle{\mathfrak{g}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sigma_{1}\ \ }
\textstyle{\mathfrak{g}\otimes^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{1}\ \ }
\scriptstyle{\pi_{2}\ \ \ \ \ \ }
\textstyle{(\mathfrak{g}/{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\textsf{R}_{{\textsl{q}}}}(\mathfrak{g}/{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces,}
\scriptstyle{\sigma_{2}\ \ \ \ \ \ }

where

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(g)=\{g\};

\displaystyle\pi_{1}\{g\}=g,\ \pi_{1}(g\otimes h)=0;

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}\big((g+{\textsl{q}}\mathfrak{g})\otimes(h+{\textsl{q}}\mathfrak{g})\big)=g\otimes h;

\displaystyle\pi_{2}(g\otimes h)=(g+{\textsl{q}}\mathfrak{g})\otimes(h+{\textsl{q}}\mathfrak{g}),\ \pi_{2}\{g\}=0.

One easily checks that
\pi_{1}\sigma_{1}=1_{\mathfrak{g}}
,

\pi_{2}\sigma_{2}=1_{(\mathfrak{g}/{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\textsf{R}_{{\textsl{q}}}}(\mathfrak{g}/{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g})}
and

\pi_{1}\sigma_{1}+\pi_{2}\sigma_{2}=1_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}}
.
Hence the first isomorphism holds. The proof is essentially the same for the second isomorphism.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

There is a well-defined homomorphism of

i\colon\Gamma\big(\mathfrak{g}/(\mathfrak{g}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g})\big)\to\mathfrak{g}\otimes^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g},\quad\text{given by}\quad i\big(\gamma(g+\mathfrak{g}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g})\big)=g\otimes g,

(i)

The sequence

\Gamma\big(\mathfrak{g}/(\mathfrak{g}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g})\big)\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}\mathfrak{g}\otimes^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}\wedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow 0

is exact;

(ii)

Consider
\Gamma\big(\mathfrak{g}/(\mathfrak{g}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g})\big)
as an abelian Lie algebra.
Then
i
is a homomorphism of Lie algebras;

(iii)

If
\mathfrak{g}/(\mathfrak{g}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g})
is a free
\textsf{R}_{{\textsl{q}}}
-module, then

is a monomorphism.

Proof.

(i) Let
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
denote the non-abelian tensor square of
\mathfrak{g}

[
10
]
.
Then, there is a well-defined homomorphism
\Gamma\big(\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\big)\to\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
,

\gamma(g+[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}])\mapsto g\otimes g
,
[
10
]
. Therefore, to show that

also is well defined, it suffices
to check that
i\big(\gamma(g+{\textsl{q}}h)\big)=i\big(\gamma(g)\big)
for each
g,h\in\mathfrak{g}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle i\big(\gamma(g+qh)\big)

\displaystyle=(g+qh)\otimes(g+qh)

\displaystyle=g\otimes g+q(g\otimes h)+q(h\otimes g)+q^{2}(h\otimes h)

\displaystyle=g\otimes g+\{[g,h]\}+\{[h,g]\}+q\{[h,h]\}

\displaystyle=g\otimes g+\{[g,h]+[h,g]+q[h,h]\}=g\otimes g=i\big(\gamma(g)\big).

(ii) We must show that

for each

(iii) Consider the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{\Gamma\big(\mathfrak{g}/(\mathfrak{g}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g})\big)\ \ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{g}\otimes^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

where the right vertical arrow is the natural homomorphism. By Proposition

is a free

-module, the lower horizontal arrow is a

. Hence, the upper horizontal arrow is a monomorphism too.

Given an epimorphism of Lie algebras
\partial\colon\mathfrak{f}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
, one easily observes that the induced homomorphism

\bar{\partial}\colon\mathfrak{f}/(\Ker(\partial)\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{f})\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{g}

is a

-central extension of Lie algebras. In particular, if
\partial\colon\mathfrak{f}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{g}

is a free presentation of
\mathfrak{g}
, i.e.
\mathfrak{f}
is a free Lie algebra, then

\bar{\partial}\colon\mathfrak{f}/(\Ker(\partial)\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{f})\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{g}

will be called
the
q
-central extension induced by this free presentation
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose that the ground ring
R
is a
q
-torsion-free ring.

Let

be a free presentation of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
Denote
\mathfrak{c}=\mathfrak{f}/(\mathfrak{r}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{f})
. Let
\bar{\partial}\colon\mathfrak{c}\to\mathfrak{g}
be the
q
-central extension induced by the given free
presentation of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then there is a natural isomorphism of Lie algebras

\mathfrak{c}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{c}\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}\mathfrak{g}\wedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g},\quad\text{given by}\quad[c_{1},c_{2}]\mapsto\bar{\partial}(c_{1})\wedge\bar{\partial}(c_{2})\quad\text{and}\quad{\textsl{q}}c\mapsto\{\bar{\partial}(c)\}.

Proof.

We have an isomorphism

\mathfrak{c}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{c}\cong(\mathfrak{f}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{f})/(\mathfrak{r}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{f})
.
By Proposition
3.2
we get the exact sequence of Lie algebras

\mathfrak{r}\wedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{f}\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}\mathfrak{f}\wedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{f}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}\wedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow 0
and so

(\mathfrak{f}\wedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{f})/{i(\mathfrak{r}\wedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{f})}\cong\mathfrak{g}\wedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}
.
On the other hand, since
\mathfrak{f}
is a free Lie algebra, by Lemma

, we have the isomorphism

\xi^{\wedge}\colon\mathfrak{f}\wedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{f}\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}\mathfrak{f}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{f}

∎

4.
q
-capability of Lie algebras

In complete analogy to the notion of capable Lie algebras, we formulate the following definition of
q
-capable Lie algebras.

Definition 4.1
.

A Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is called
q
-capable
if there exists a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{m}
such that

\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{m}/\Z_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{m}).

It is well-known that the notion of capable Lie algebra is related to inner derivations. Namely, a Lie algebra is capable if and only if it is isomorphic to the Lie algebra of inner derivations of a Lie algebra. Below we examine a similar result for the modulo
q
setting. Before, we need to recall some notations.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a Lie algebra.
A derivation
of
\mathfrak{m}
is a linear self-map
d\colon\mathfrak{m}\to\mathfrak{m}
such that
d[m,m^{\prime}]=[d(m),m^{\prime}]+[m,d(m^{\prime})]
for all
m,m^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{m}
. For example, for any
m\in\mathfrak{m}
, the map
d_{m}\colon\mathfrak{m}\to\mathfrak{m}
, given by
d_{m}(m^{\prime})=[m,m^{\prime}]
is a derivation, called
the inner derivation
defined by
m\in\mathfrak{m}
.

Denote by
\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{m})
the set of all derivations of
\mathfrak{m}
. It is a Lie algebra with the bracket
[d,d^{\prime}]=dd^{\prime}-d^{\prime}d
. The set of all inner derivations
\operatorname{IDer}(\mathfrak{m})=\{d_{m}\mid m\in\mathfrak{m}\}
is an ideal of
\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{m})
, and it is called
the Lie algebra of inner derivations
.

Given a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{m}
, let us consider the Lie algebra structure on
\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{m})\times{\textsl{q}}\mathfrak{m}
given by

[(d,{\textsl{q}}m),(d^{\prime},{\textsl{q}}m^{\prime})]=\big([d,d^{\prime}],{\textsl{q}}[m,m^{\prime}]\big).

It is easy to check that there is a Lie action of
\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{m})\times{\textsl{q}}\mathfrak{m}
on
\mathfrak{m}
defined by

{}^{(d,{\textsl{q}}m)}m^{\prime}=d(m^{\prime}).

There is a Lie homomorphism

\partial\colon\mathfrak{m}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{m})\times{\textsl{q}}\mathfrak{m},\quad\partial(m)=(d_{m},{\textsl{q}}m),

the image of which is the Lie subalgebra

\operatorname{IDer}(\mathfrak{m},{\textsl{q}})=\{(d_{m},{\textsl{q}}m)\mid m\in\mathfrak{m}\}

of
\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{m})\times{\textsl{q}}\mathfrak{m}
, and the kernel is the
q
-center
\Z_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{m})
of
\mathfrak{m}
. We call
\operatorname{IDer}(\mathfrak{m},{\textsl{q}})

the Lie algebra of inner
q
-derivations
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Given a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{m}
, there is a short exact sequence of Lie algebras

0\longrightarrow\Z_{q}({\mathfrak{m}})\longrightarrow\mathfrak{m}\overset{\partial}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{IDer}(\mathfrak{m},{\textsl{q}})\longrightarrow 0,

where
\partial
is a
q
-crossed
\operatorname{IDer}(\mathfrak{m},{\textsl{q}})
-module with the Lie action of
\operatorname{IDer}(\mathfrak{m},{\textsl{q}})
on
\mathfrak{m}
given by

{}^{(d_{m},{\textsl{q}}m)}m^{\prime}=[m,m^{\prime}].

(12)

Proof.

It is easy to check that the sequence is a short exact sequence of Lie algebras, and (
12
) indeed defines a Lie action.
To show that
\partial
is a
q
-crossed module, we only need to verify the first two conditions of Definition
2.1
.

Thus, we will show that
\partial(^{(d_{m},{\textsl{q}}m)}m^{\prime})=[(d_{m},m),\partial(m^{\prime})]
. In fact, we have

\displaystyle\partial(^{(d_{m},{\textsl{q}}m)}m^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\partial([m,m^{\prime}])=(d_{[m,m^{\prime}]},{\textsl{q}}[m,m^{\prime}])=([d_{m},d_{m^{\prime}}],{\textsl{q}}[m,m^{\prime}])

\displaystyle=[(d_{m},{\textsl{q}}m),(d_{m^{\prime}},{\textsl{q}}m^{\prime})]=[(d_{m},m),\partial(m^{\prime})].

Here we have used the obvious equality
d_{[m,m^{\prime}]}=[d_{m},d_{m^{\prime}}]
for inner derivations.
And finally, we see that

\displaystyle{}^{\partial({m})}m^{\prime}={}^{(d_{m},{\textsl{q}}m)}m^{\prime}=[m,m^{\prime}].

∎

Corollary 4.3
.

A Lie algebra is
q
-capable if and only if it is isomorphic to the Lie algebra of inner
q
-derivations
\operatorname{IDer}(\mathfrak{m},{\textsl{q}})
for some Lie algebra
\mathfrak{m}
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra. The
q
-
exterior center
of
\mathfrak{g}
is defined by

\Z^{\wedge}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})=\Big\{g\in\mathfrak{g}\mid\{g\}=0_{\mathfrak{g}\wedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}}\>\text{and}\>g\wedge x=0_{\mathfrak{g}\wedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}}\>\text{for each}\>x\in\mathfrak{g}\Big\}.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\bar{\partial}\colon\mathfrak{c}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a
q
-central extension induced by a free

. Then

\bar{\partial}(c)\in\Z^{\wedge}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Proof.

If

, then
{{\textsl{q}}}c=0
and
[c,x]=0
for each

. By Lemma

\bar{\partial}(c)\wedge\bar{\partial}(x)=0_{\mathfrak{g}\wedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}}
and

\{\bar{\partial}(c)\}=0_{\mathfrak{g}\wedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}}
. Since
\bar{\partial}
is an epimorphism,

\bar{\partial}(c)\in Z^{\wedge}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Conversely, if
\bar{\partial}(c)\in\Z^{\wedge}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})

for each

because the isomorphic

5.

In this section, we introduce the tensor analog of the

-exterior center of a Lie algebra and prove their coincidence under additional conditions. The idea comes from the work

.

To show the converse inclusion
\Z^{\otimes}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\Z^{\wedge}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})\cap(\mathfrak{g}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g})
, it suffices to prove that
\Z^{\otimes}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\mathfrak{g}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}
.
Take
g\in\Z^{\otimes}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})
. Then
\{g\}=0_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}}
. Hence,

\{\bar{g}\}=0_{(\mathfrak{g}/(\mathfrak{g}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}))\otimes^{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g}/(\mathfrak{g}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}))}
,
where
\bar{g}=g+\mathfrak{g}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}
. Since
\mathfrak{g}/(\mathfrak{g}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g})
is a free

\textsf{R}_{{\textsl{q}}}
-module, by Proposition
3.1

\big(\mathfrak{g}/(\mathfrak{g}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g})\big)\otimes^{{\textsl{q}}}\big(\mathfrak{g}/(\mathfrak{g}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g})\big)\cong\big(\mathfrak{g}/(\mathfrak{g}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g})\big)\times\Big(\big(\mathfrak{g}/(\mathfrak{g}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g})\big)\otimes_{\textsf{R}_{{\textsl{q}}}}\big(\mathfrak{g}/(\mathfrak{g}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g})\big)\Big).

Using this isomorphism we deduce that

\{\bar{g}\}=0_{(\mathfrak{g}/(\mathfrak{g}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}))\otimes^{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g}/(\mathfrak{g}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}))}

if and only if
\bar{g}=0_{\mathfrak{g}/(\mathfrak{g}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g})}
. Thus,
g\in\mathfrak{g}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}
.

Finally, by using Proposition
3.1
again (for the non-abelian
q
-exterior product), one can repeat the reasoning given in the previous paragraph to show that
\Z^{\wedge}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\mathfrak{g}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}
. This completes the proof.
∎

6.
Strongly
q
-capable Lie algebras

Our definition of a
q
-capable Lie algebra for
{\textsl{q}}=0
gives the notion of a capable Lie algebra. So, it is a modulo
q
generalization of the usual capability of Lie algebras.

In the case of groups, according to Ellis’ definition in
[
11
]
, a group
G
is
q
-capable if there exists a group
M
whose center and
q
-center coincide and the quotient of
M
by its center (
q
-center) is isomorphic to
G
.
Now we give the Lie algebra version of the
q
-capability in the sense of Ellis, which differs from our notion of
q
-capability.

Definition 6.1
.

We say that a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is
strongly
q
-capable
(or
q
-
capable
in the sense of Ellis) if there exists a Lie algebra

\mathfrak{m}
such that
\Z(\mathfrak{m})=\Z_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{m})
and
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{m}/\Z(\mathfrak{m})
.

Note that any strongly
q
-capable Lie algebra is
q
-capable at the same time.

In the rest of this section, we describe a necessary and sufficient condition of strongly
q
-capability of a Lie algebra via the non-abelian
q
-exterior product.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra, and
\mathfrak{h}
be an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Let
\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
(resp.
\mathfrak{h}\wedge\mathfrak{g}
) denote the non-abelian tensor (resp. exterior)
product of
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{g}
(see
[
10
]
). We have the natural homomorphisms

\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}\otimes^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}
and

\mathfrak{h}\wedge\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}\wedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}
. We introduce the following notations:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}\boxtimes\mathfrak{g}=\Img\big(\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}\otimes^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}\big),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}\curlywedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}=\Img\big(\mathfrak{h}\wedge\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}\wedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}\big).

The proof of the following proposition is standard.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
0\to\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\to 0
be an extension of Lie algebras.
Then we have the following exact sequence

\mathfrak{h}\curlywedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}\curlywedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\curlywedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\longrightarrow 0.

In what follows, the ground ring
R
is assumed to be
q
-torsion-free.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{f}
be a free Lie algebra.
Assume that
\xi^{\wedge}\colon\mathfrak{f}\wedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{f}\to\mathfrak{f}
is defined as in Lemma
2.4
. Then

\xi^{\wedge}
induces an isomorphism
\mathfrak{f}\curlywedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{f}\cong[\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{f}]
.

Proof.

Straightforward from Lemma
3.3
.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra and
\bar{\partial}\colon\mathfrak{c}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be the
q
-central extension induced by a free
presentation
0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{r}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{f}\overset{\partial}{\longrightarrow}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow 0
of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then there is a natural isomorphism of Lie algebras

[\mathfrak{c},\mathfrak{c}]\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}\mathfrak{g}\curlywedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g},\quad[c_{1},c_{2}]\mapsto\bar{\partial}(c_{1})\wedge\bar{\partial}(c_{2}).

Proof.

Straightforward from Lemma
3.5
.
∎

Definition 6.5
.

The
q
-
tensor
and
q
-
exterior centers
in the sense of Ellis of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
are defined by

\displaystyle\Z^{\boxtimes}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})=\Big\{g\in\mathfrak{g}\mid g\otimes x=0_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}}\>\text{for each}\>x\in\mathfrak{g}\Big\},

\displaystyle\Z^{\curlywedge}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})=\Big\{g\in\mathfrak{g}\mid g\wedge x=0_{\mathfrak{g}\wedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}}\>\text{for each}\>x\in\mathfrak{g}\Big\}.

Remark 6.6
.

For arbitrary Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, we have that

\Z^{\otimes}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\Z^{\boxtimes}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})
and

\Z^{\wedge}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\Z^{\curlywedge}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})
. In general, the inverse inclusions are not true. For instance, if
\mathfrak{g}=\textsf{R}
is considered as an abelian Lie algebra, then
\Z^{\curlywedge}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\textsf{R})=\textsf{R}
. Whilst
\Z^{\wedge}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\textsf{R})=\{0\}
, since
R
is a
q
-torsion-free ring.

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
\bar{\partial}\colon\mathfrak{c}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a
q
-central extension induced by a free
presentation of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then
c\in\Z(\mathfrak{c})
if and only if
\bar{\partial}(c)\in\Z^{\curlywedge}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Proof.

Straightforward from Lemma
6.4
.
∎

Theorem 6.8
.

A Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is strongly
q
-capable if and only if

\Z^{\curlywedge}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})=\{0\}
.

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as that of Theorem
4.6
. But still, we are going to give full details
for the reader’s convenience.

First, suppose that
\Z^{\curlywedge}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})=0
. Consider a
q
-central extension

\bar{\partial}\colon\mathfrak{c}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
induced by a free presentation of
\mathfrak{g}
. It suffices to show that
\Ker\bar{\partial}=\Z_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{c})=\Z(\mathfrak{c})
.
Since
\bar{\partial}\colon\mathfrak{c}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a
q
-central extension, we have

\Ker\bar{\partial}\subseteq\Z_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{c})\subseteq\Z(\mathfrak{c})
. To prove the inverse inclusions it suffices to show that

\Z(\mathfrak{c})\subseteq\Ker\bar{\partial}
. Indeed, if
x\in\Z(\mathfrak{c})
, then by Corollary
6.7
,

\bar{\partial}(x)\in\Z^{\curlywedge}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})=\{0\}
and so
x\in\Ker\bar{\partial}
.

Conversely, let
\mathfrak{g}
be a strongly
q
-capable Lie algebra. Then there is a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{m}

such that
\Z_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{m})=\Z_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{m})
and
\mathfrak{m}/\Z(\mathfrak{m})\cong\mathfrak{g}
. Hence,
there is an epimorphism of Lie algebras
\pi\colon\mathfrak{m}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
such that

\Ker\pi=\Z(\mathfrak{m})=\Z_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{m})
.
Let
0\to\mathfrak{s}\to\mathfrak{f}\overset{\tau}{\to}\mathfrak{m}\to 0
be a free presentation of
\mathfrak{m}
.
Consider the free presentation
0\to\mathfrak{r}\to\mathfrak{f}\overset{\partial}{\to}\mathfrak{g}\to 0
of
\mathfrak{g}
,
where
\partial=\pi\tau
and
\mathfrak{r}
denotes the kernel of
\partial
. Then we have the following commutative diagram
with exact rows

\textstyle{0\ \ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{r}\ \ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{f}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\partial}
\scriptstyle{\tau}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{g}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{0\ \ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Z(\mathfrak{m})=\Z_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{m})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{m}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{g}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0.}

We have
\tau(\mathfrak{r}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{f})=0
. Indeed, for each
r\in\mathfrak{r}
and
f\in\mathfrak{f}
,

\tau[r,f]=[\tau(r),\tau(f)]=0
because
\tau(r)\in\Z(\mathfrak{m})=\Ker\pi
. Moreover, for each
r\in\mathfrak{r}
,

\tau({\textsl{q}}r)={\textsl{q}}\tau(r)=0
because
\tau(r)\in\Z_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{m})
. Denote
\mathfrak{f}/(\mathfrak{r}\#_{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{f})

by
\mathfrak{c}
. Then we have the following induced commutative triangle of Lie algebras

\textstyle{\mathfrak{c}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\bar{\partial}}
\scriptstyle{\bar{\tau}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{m}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{g}}

Since
\bar{\tau}
is an epimorphism,
\bar{\tau}(\Z(\mathfrak{c}))\subseteq\Z(\mathfrak{m})=\Ker\pi
, therefore

\bar{\partial}(\Z(\mathfrak{c}))=0
, i.e.
\Z(\mathfrak{c})\subseteq\Ker\bar{\partial}
. On the other hand, since

\bar{\partial}\colon\mathfrak{c}\to\mathfrak{g}
is a
q
-central extension,

\Ker\bar{\partial}\subseteq\Z_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{c})\subseteq\Z(\mathfrak{c})
.
We get that
\Z(\mathfrak{c})=\Ker\bar{\partial}
. Then, by Corollary
6.7
we have

\Z^{\curlywedge}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})=\bar{\partial}(\Z(\mathfrak{c}))=\bar{\partial}(\Ker\bar{\partial})=0.

∎

Proposition 6.9
.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is a perfect Lie algebra, i.e.
\mathfrak{g}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, then

\Z^{\boxtimes}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})=\Z^{\curlywedge}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})=\Z(\mathfrak{g}).

So, if
R
is a
q
-torsion-free ring, then
\mathfrak{g}=\textsf{R}
is a
q
-capable but not a strongly
q
-capable Lie algebra.

Proof.

We have well-defined homomorphisms

\mathfrak{g}\boxtimes^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}\curlywedge^{{\textsl{q}}}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, given by
g\otimes h\mapsto g\wedge h
and
g\wedge h\mapsto[g,h]
, respectively. They imply the
inclusions

\Z^{\boxtimes}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\Z^{\curlywedge}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\Z(\mathfrak{g}).

Therefore, it suffices to show that
\Z(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\Z^{\boxtimes}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})
.
Let
x\in\Z(\mathfrak{g})
and
g\in\mathfrak{g}
. Then there are
g_{i},h_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}
and
\lambda_{i}\in\textsf{R}
,
for
i=1,\dots,n
, such that
g=\overset{n}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}\lambda_{i}[g_{i},h_{i}]
. Then

x\otimes g=x\otimes\overset{n}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}\lambda_{i}[g_{i},h_{i}]=\overset{n}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}\lambda_{i}\big(x\otimes[g_{i},h_{i}]\big)=\overset{n}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}\lambda_{i}\big([h_{i},x]\otimes g_{i}-[g_{i},x]\otimes h_{i}\big)=0,

which means that
x\in\Z^{\boxtimes}_{{\textsl{q}}}(\mathfrak{g})
and the proof is completed.
∎

Corollary 6.10
.

A perfect Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is
q
-capable if and only if
\Z(\mathfrak{g})=0
.

About penalty-duality methods in fluid-structure interactions
Philippe Destuynder, Erwan Liberge

Abstract
In fluid-structure interaction problems, some people use a penalty method for positioning the structure inside the fluid. This is usually performed by considering that the fluid is very stiff or/and very heavy at the place occupied by the structure. These methods are very convenient for the programming point of view but lead to ill conditioned operators. This is a drawback in the numerical solution methods. In particular the forces applied to the structure by the surrounding flow are not accurately estimated because the penalty parameter -which is a very large number- appears in their expressions. We suggest in this paper a mathematical analysis of the difficulties encountered and we discuss how the penalty-duality method of D. Bertsekas can be an interesting alternative to overcome them.

3.1
The definitions of the penalty models

The velocity

\\
\hbox{where:}\\
\\
\hskip-5.69046ptJ^{\varepsilon}({\mathbb{v}})\hskip-1.42271pt=\displaystyle\hskip-1.42271pt\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[\hskip-1.42271pt\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\hskip-2.84544pt\sigma({\mathbb{v}}).\gamma({\mathbb{v}})\hskip-1.42271pt+\hskip-1.42271pt\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\varepsilon}\hskip-1.42271pt\displaystyle\int_{\partial S}\hskip-4.26773pt||{\mathbb{v}}\hskip-1.42271pt-\hskip-1.42271pt{\mathbb{u}}_{0}||^{2}\hskip-1.42271pt+\hskip-1.42271pt\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\varepsilon}\hskip-1.42271pt\displaystyle\int_{S}\hskip-1.42271pt\sigma({\mathbb{v}}){.\gamma({\mathbb{v}})}\hskip-1.42271pt+\hskip-1.42271pt\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{\varepsilon}\hskip-1.42271pt\displaystyle\int_{S}\hskip-1.42271pt||{\mathbb{v}}\hskip-1.42271pt-\hskip-1.42271pt{\mathbb{u}}_{0}||^{2}\right]\\

Remark 7
.

Let us now consider the case where

The existence and uniqueness are relevant of the general A.K. Aziz and I. Babuska Theorem
[

that we recall hereafter for sake of convenience.

Theorem 5.1
.

[Aziz-Babuska] Let
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
be two Hilbert spaces,
b
a bilinear and continuous form on
H_{1}\times H_{2}
and
j
a linear and continuous form on
H_{2}
. It is asuumed that the following properties hold:

#

1 if
q\in H_{2}
is such that:
\forall\mu\in H_{1},\;b(\mu,q)=0
then
q=0
;

#

2 there exists a constant
\delta>0
such that
\forall\mu\in H_{1},\;\displaystyle\sup_{q\in H_{2}}\displaystyle\frac{b(\mu,q)}{||q||_{H_{2}}}\geq\delta||\mu||_{H_{1}}.

Then there exists a unique element
\lambda\in H_{1}
such that:

\forall q\in H_{2},\;b(\lambda,q)=j(q).

(50)

Let us now apply this Theorem to our case.

Theorem 5.2
.

Existence and uniqueness of a solution to (
49
). Let
r>0
and
\alpha>0
. The function
{\mathbb{g}}
is assumed as previously to be in the space
{\Huge[}L^{2}(\Gamma_{1}){\Huge]}^{2}
. Hence the system (
46
) has a unique solution
({\mathbb{u}}^{r},\lambda)
in the space
W_{0}\times\left[H^{-1/2}(\partial S)\right]^{2}
. Furthermore
{\mathbb{u}}^{r}={\mathbb{u}}
solution of the initial problem (
3
).

Proof.

If
({\mathbb{u}}^{r},\lambda)\in W_{0}\times\left[H^{-1/2}(\partial S)\right]^{2}
is a solution it should satisfy the following relation:

\forall{\mathbb{v}}\in W_{00},\;\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sigma({\mathbb{u}}^{r}).\gamma({\mathbb{v}})=\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{1}}{\mathbb{g}}.{\mathbb{v}},\;\hbox{ and necessarily:\;}{\mathbb{u}}^{r}={\mathbb{u}}_{0}\hbox{ on }\partial S.

(51)

Therefore
{\mathbb{u}}^{r}
is the initial solution of equation (
3
) which is also the term
{\mathbb{u}}^{0}
computed in the asymptotic method(s) applied to the penalty method at section
3
(for both
\alpha>0,\;\beta=0
or
\alpha>0,\beta>0
). It also implies that it is unique and equal to the rigid body velocity
{\mathbb{u}}_{0}
on the whole domain
S
and not only on its boundary
\partial S
.

Finally, in order to complete the proof of Theorem
5.2
it is sufficient to prove the existence and uniqueness of
\lambda
using Aziz-Babuska Theorem
5.1
.

Let us set:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}H_{1}=\left[H^{-1/2}(\partial S)\right]^{2},\;H_{2}=\left[H^{-1/2}(\partial S)\right]^{2},\\
\\
\forall\mu\in H_{1},\;\forall{\mathbb{v}}\in H_{2},\;b(\mu,{\mathbb{v}})=\alpha\displaystyle\int_{\partial S}\mu.{\mathbb{v}},\\
\\
\forall{\mathbb{v}}\in H_{2},\;j({\mathbb{v}})=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{S}}\sigma({\mathbb{u}}^{r}).\gamma({\mathbb{v}})-\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{1}}{\mathbb{g}}.{\mathbb{v}}.\end{array}\right.

(52)

The first requirement of the Aziz-Babuska Theorem is obvious because of the duality between

and
H_{2}
. The second one is satisfied with

because of the definition of the norm in

The only point to be verified is the definition and the continuity of

on

First of all

is clearly linear. It depends on the value of
{\mathbb{v}}
on the boundary
\partial S
because of the definition of

. Hence, for a given element

in
H_{2}
one can associate any element in the space
V_{0}
denoted also by
{\mathbb{v}}
and which gives the same value of

. Let us recall that there exists a linear and continuous operator -say

- from

into

[
29
]
such that:

\exists c_{9}>0,\hbox{ independent on }{\mathbb{v}}\in H_{1}\hbox{ suh that: }||R({\mathbb{v}})||_{1,\Omega_{S}}\leq c_{9}||{\mathbb{v}}||_{1/2,\partial S}.

(53)

Hence:

|j({\mathbb{v}})|=\displaystyle|\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{S}}\sigma(R{\mathbb{v}}).\gamma(R{\mathbb{v}})-\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{1}}{\mathbb{g}}.R({\mathbb{v}})|\leq c_{10}||{\mathbb{v}}||_{1/2,\partial S},

(54)

Hence the assumptions of Theorem
5.1
are satisfied and we can conclude the proof of Theorem

6
The numerical algorithm and its solution method

In this section we focus on the Uzawa algorithm
[
21
]
[
11
]
for solving (
49
). Let us start from a given value for
\lambda^{n_{0}}\in{\Huge[}H^{-1/2}(\partial S){\Huge]}^{2}
(the most classical choice is to start from
\lambda^{n_{0}}=0
). For each
n\geq n_{0}
, we define
{\mathbb{u}}^{n}\in W_{0}
solution of:

\forall{\mathbb{v}}\in W_{0},\;\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sigma({\mathbb{u}}^{n}).\gamma({\mathbb{v}})+{r}\displaystyle\int_{\partial S}({\mathbb{u}}^{n}-{\mathbb{u}}_{0}).{\mathbb{v}}=\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{1}}{\mathbb{g}}.{\mathbb{v}}-\displaystyle\int_{\partial S}\lambda^{n}.{\mathbb{v}}.

(55)

Then we upgrade
\lambda^{n}
by setting:

\lambda^{n+1}=\lambda^{n}+r({\mathbb{u}}^{n}-{\mathbb{u}}_{0})\;\hbox{ on }\partial S.

(56)

An important point is that if the first multiplier
\lambda^{n}
belongs to the space
{\Huge[}L^{2}(\partial S){\Huge]}^{2}
all the sequence
\lambda^{n}
belongs to this space. But in general the solution
\lambda
of (
49
) doesn’t belongs to this space unless one has a regularity property. In fact, one has (interpretation of (
49
)),

\lambda=-\sigma({\mathbb{u}}^{r}).\nu
on
\partial S
. This regularity assumption is exactly the one used in the asymptotic analysis of the penalty model for
\alpha>0
and
\beta=0
. It is convenient (but not necessary) in the following to adopt this hypothesis (the norm
L^{2}(\partial S)
is more convenient in the writings than the one of
H^{-1/2}(\partial S)
).

The convergence of the algorithm is classical
[
9
]
. But for sake of clarity let us summarize the proof hereafter with few remarks concerning the regularity of the multiplier
\lambda
which are only valid for our case.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
({\mathbb{u}}^{n},\lambda^{n})\in W_{0}\times{\Huge[}H^{-1/2}(\partial S){\Huge]}^{2}
be the sequence defined by the algorithm (
55
)-(
56
). We assume, just for sake of convenience in the writings, that:

\lambda=-\sigma({\mathbb{u}}^{r}).\nu\in{\Huge[}L^{2}(\partial S){\Huge]}^{2}.

Then for any
r>0
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\;{\mathbb{u}}^{n}={\mathbb{u}}^{r}={\mathbb{u}}\hbox{ solution of (\ref{eq3})},\\
\\
\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\;\lambda^{n}=\lambda\hbox{ solution with ${\mathbb{u}}^{r}$ of (\ref{eq27})}.\end{array}\right.

(57)

Proof.

Let us introduce the
gap
variables:

\underline{\mathbb{u}}^{n}={\mathbb{u}}^{n}-{\mathbb{u}}^{r},\;\;\underline{\lambda}^{n}=\lambda^{n}-\lambda.

One has the following equalities:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\forall{\mathbb{v}}\in W_{0},\;\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sigma(\underline{\mathbb{u}}^{n}).\gamma(\underline{\mathbb{u}}^{n})+{r}\displaystyle\int_{\partial S}|\underline{\mathbb{u}}^{n}|^{2}+\displaystyle\int_{\partial S}\underline{\mathbb{u}}^{n}\underline{\lambda}^{n}=0,\\
\\
|\underline{\lambda}^{n+1}|^{2}=|\underline{\lambda}^{n}|^{2}+{r^{2}}|\underline{\mathbb{u}}^{n}|^{2}+{2r}\underline{\lambda}^{n}.\underline{\mathbb{u}}^{n}.\end{array}\right.

We deduce that:

\displaystyle\int_{\partial S}|\underline{\lambda}^{n}|^{2}-\displaystyle\int_{\partial S}|\underline{\lambda}^{n+1}|^{2}=r{\Huge[}-2\displaystyle\int_{\partial S}\underline{\mathbb{u}}^{n}\underline{\lambda}^{n}-r\displaystyle\int_{\partial S}|\underline{\mathbb{u}}^{n}|^{2}{\Huge]},

or else:

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{\partial S}|\underline{\lambda}^{n}|^{2}-\displaystyle\int_{\partial S}|\underline{\lambda}^{n+1}|^{2}=r{\Huge[}2\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sigma(\underline{\mathbb{u}}).\gamma(\underline{\mathbb{u}}^{n})+2r\displaystyle\int_{\partial S}|\underline{\mathbb{u}}^{n}|^{2}-r\displaystyle\int_{\partial S}|\underline{\mathbb{u}}^{n}|^{2}{\Huge]}\\
\\
=r{\Huge[}2\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sigma_{ij}(\underline{\mathbb{u}}).\gamma(\underline{\mathbb{u}}^{n})+r\displaystyle\int_{\partial S}|\underline{\mathbb{u}}^{n}|^{2}{\Huge]}\geq 0.\end{array}

Because the real and positive sequence of numbers
q^{n}=\displaystyle\int_{\partial S}|\underline{\lambda}^{n}|^{2}
is decreasing, it is convergent and therefore it is a Cauchy sequence. Hence
\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}q^{n}-q^{n+1}=0.
As a consequence (using the
H^{1}(\Omega)
-coerciveness of the bilinear form
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sigma({\mathbb{u}}).\gamma({\mathbb{v}})
):

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}||{\mathbb{u}}^{n}-{\mathbb{u}}_{0}||_{0,\partial S}=0,\\
\\
\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}||{\mathbb{u}}^{n}-{\mathbb{u}}^{r}||_{1,\Omega}=0.\end{array}\right.

The second convergence result reinforces the first one because of the continuity of the trace mapping from
H^{1}(\Omega)
into
H^{1/2}(\partial S)
:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}||{\mathbb{u}}^{n}-{\mathbb{u}}_{0}||_{1/2,\partial S}=0.

The final step consists in proving the convergence of the multiplier
\lambda^{n}
. From:

\displaystyle\int_{\partial S}\underline{\lambda}^{n}.{\mathbb{v}}=-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sigma(\underline{\mathbb{u}}^{n}).\gamma({\mathbb{v}})-r\displaystyle\int_{\partial S}\underline{\mathbb{u}}^{n}.{\mathbb{v}}-\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{1}}{\mathbb{g}}.{\mathbb{v}},

and because of the property
\#
2 mentioned in Theorem
5.1
, one has:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}||\lambda^{n}-\lambda||_{-1/2,\partial S}=0.

Even if we assumed the regularity
\lambda\in{\huge[}L^{2}(\partial S){\Huge]}^{2}
the standard convergence is only in the space
{\huge[}H^{-1/2}(\partial S){\Huge]}^{2}
.
∎

Conclusion

In this paper we have first studied several penalty methods in a simple fluid-structure model assuming the case of a small reduce frequency in order to partially decouple the inertia terms between the fluid and the structure. We discussed the possibilities of several penalty terms inside the structure (
L^{2}
or/and

Our goal has been to focus on the mathematical difficulties which can occur in the numerical schemes due to the ill conditioning of the penalty models.We compare the methods from the theoretical point of view and on a very simple 1D model. We also introduced in this framework the Bertsekas penalty-duality algorithm for the ensuring the velocity continuity at the fluid-structure interface. It appears that the penalty-duality algorithm applied to this quasi-static model without inertia terms is very efficient compared to other penalty methods. Furthermore it leads to a much better condition number of the numerical scheme as far as it not necessary to use a large value of the penalty coefficient.

In a second step we compared the various possibilities discussed in the first step on two very simple 1D fluid-structure models. The first one is the advection-diffusion equation with a prescribed movement of an immersed structure and the second one is a similar model but with Burgers equation for the fluid. In this case there a convection term (linear for the advection and non linear for Burgers equation). The numerical tests show that the
L^{2}
penalty term inside the structures is very efficient as far as the penalty parameter is very small. The coupling with the penalty-duality algorithm involving the velocity continuity at the interface enables to improve very slightly the numerical tests as far as the inner penalty parameter is very small. But it is much more efficient if this parameter is more moderate. This can be therefore an interesting improvement in cases where the condition numerber is an important point in the numerical scheme. One can forcast that this is mainly the case for for 2D and more for 3D models.

Because this paper is mainly theoretically oriented, we focused on the mathematical analysis of the penalty and penalty-duality methods for Stokes equations coupled with a rigid structure in movement.

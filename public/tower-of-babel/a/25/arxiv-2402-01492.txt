FFLV polytopes are string polytopes
Ester Cleusters and Ghislain Fourier and Felix Lerner

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish that FFLV polytopes, which describe monomial bases compatible with the PBW filtration on finite-dimensional simple modules for 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 n , 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 n \mathfrak{sl}_{n},\mathfrak{sp}_{n} , are actually string polytopes as described by Littelmann and Berenstein-Zelevinsky for Demazure modules of higher rank Lie algebras.



1.
Introduction

Degenerations of flag varieties have been studied in various contexts.
The PBW degenerate flag variety
\mathcal{F}l^{a}
was introduced by Evgeny Feigin
[
Fei12
]
in the context of PBW degenerate
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-modules.
He demonstrated that
\mathcal{F}l^{a}
is a flat degeneration of the classical flag variety and provided a description in terms of sequences of subspaces similarly to the description of the classical flag variety:

\mathcal{F}l^{a}=\{(U_{i})\mid U_{i}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n},\dim U_{i}=i,\text{pr}_{i}(U_{i})\subset U_{i+1}\}

Subsequently, Giovanni Cerulli Irelli and Martina Lanini
[
CIL15
]
showed that this variety, also defined through an abelianization of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
is a natural object in Lie theory, specifically isomorphic to a Schubert variety in a partial flag variety.
Precisely, they considered the element
w=\ldots(s_{3}s_{4}s_{5})(s_{2}s_{3})s_{1}
in
S_{2n}
and the associated Schubert variety in

\mathcal{F}l=\{(U_{i})\mid U_{i}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2n},\dim U_{i}=2i-1,U_{i}\subset U_{i+1}.\}

Following this, they showed together with Littelmann in
[
CILL16
]
that this isomorphism can be interpreted in terms of modules for Lie algebras.
Specifically, they established that the aforementioned PBW degenerate module for a degenerate
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
is isomorphic to a Demazure module for
\mathfrak{sl}_{2n}
.
To be precise, if
\lambda=\sum m_{i}\omega_{i}
is the highest
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
-weight, then
V^{a}(\lambda)
(the PBW degenerate module) is,
as a module for the degenerate Lie algebra, isomorphic to
V_{w}(\tilde{\lambda})
, where
\tilde{\lambda}=\sum m_{i}\omega_{2i-1}
.

The aim of this paper is to present a combinatorial shadow of these isomorphisms. Specifically, in
[
FFL11
]
, the FFLV-polytope was introduced.
Its lattice points parametrize a monomial basis of
V^{a}(\lambda)
and, consequently, of
V(\lambda)
.
This basis, particularly for
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
with
n\geq 6
, is distinct from the well-known string bases of Littelmann
[
Lit98
]
and Berenstein-Zelevinsky
[
BZ01
]
, the bases constructed by Lusztig
[
Lus90
,
Cal02
]
, and those by Nakashima-Zelevinsky
[
NZ97
]
.
The Gelfand-Tsetlin basis
[
GC50
]
is, in fact, a Nakashima-Zelevinsky basis for a specific reduced expression of the longest Weyl group element. All these bases can be parametrized by lattice points in convex polytopes, such as string polytopes.

Conversely, the non-degenerate object, the Demazure module, has known monomial bases, like the string bases
[
Cal02
]
, derived from the canonical basis using a reduced expression of
w
. Denoting the string polytope for
\tilde{\lambda}
and
w
with the given reduced expression as
Q_{\underline{w}}(\tilde{\lambda})
, and the FFLV polytope for
\lambda

FFLV(\lambda)
, the main result of this paper is stated as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

The FFLV polytope
FFLV(\lambda)
is unimodularly equivalent to the string polytope

Q_{\underline{w}}(\tilde{\lambda})
.

Furthermore, in subsequent works (
[
FFL14
,
FFL11a
]
), PBW degenerate objects and compatible bases were also constructed for the symplectic Lie algebra. We extend our results to this setup as well:

Theorem 1.2
.

The symplectic FFLV-polytope is isomorphic to a string polytope for a symplectic Demazure module.

Considering the other classical types is more delicate. Something is wrong? No FFLV-Polytopes are known for an abelianization of orthogonal Lie algebras yet.
Nevertheless, Igor Makhlin has provided a polytope describing monomial bases for an almost abelian Lie algebra in type
B
, where the short root vectors are not abelianized
[
Mak19
]
.
A geometric interpretation of this will be presented in the upcoming work
[
Enu+
]
.
The combinatorial shadow of this is also part of an upcoming project, as our current methods do not readily apply to this setup.

In fact, we might want to change the point of view and pull back a string polytope for Demazure modules to a candidate for an FFLV polytope in type
B
and
D
, as the results in
[
Enu+
]
suggest a suitable Weyl group element and reduced expression.

Another application we are pursuing involves tableau combinatorics, such as the Littlewood-Richardson rules.
Although the FFLV polytopes are translated to PBW semistandard tableaux
[
Fei12
,
Bal23
]
, the usual tableaux combinatorics are yet to be developed.
We aim to develop a Littlewood-Richardson rule that is compatible with the PBW filtration.

In
[
Gor15
]
, similar bases have been provided for type
G_{2}
.
Although there are existing geometric interpretations, as noted in
[
Enu+
]
and
[
Enu22
]
, computational experiments indicate that the polytope proposed in
[
Gor15
]
does not correspond to a string polytope for a Demazure module of affine Lie algebras.

It should be mentioned that this combinatorial picture can only be a shadow of the geometric construction of desingularizations and Newton-Okounkov bodies, following the concept of birational sequences
[
FFL17
]
. This still has to be explored.

The paper is structured as follows: We recall definitions and foundations on Lie algebras, monomial bases and degenerations in Section
2
. Then we provide the unimodular equivalence for the two main theorems and the outline of their proofs in Section
3
. In Section
4
we give the proof while leaving out the considerations of the two cases to Section
5
and Section
6
.

2.
Preliminaries

We fix here definitions, notations and recall results, that will be used throughout this paper.

2.1.
Notations

We introduce a few standard definitions and notations, we follow here
[
Hum72
]
. We denote
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}^{+}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{-}
a simple, finite-dimensional complex Lie algebra with a fixed triangular decomposition.
We denote the set of (positive) roots
R
(resp.
R^{+}
), the set of (dominant) integral weights
P
(resp.
P^{+}
).
For each positive root
\alpha
, we fix root vectors
e_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{n}^{+},f_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{n}^{-}
and
h_{\alpha}:=[e_{\alpha},f_{\alpha}]
.
If
n=\text{rk }\mathfrak{g}
, then
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
denote the simple roots, we set
f_{i}:=f_{\alpha_{i}}
, the fundamental weights are denoted
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n}
.
The height of a positive root is the sum of the coefficients when written in terms of the simple roots.

The Weyl group is denoted
W
, the simple reflection corresponding to
\alpha_{i}
is denoted
s_{i}
, the longest element of
W
is denoted
w_{0}
.

Let
\lambda\in P^{+}
, the finite-dimensional simple module of highest weight
\lambda
is denoted
V(\lambda)
and we fix a generator of the highest weight line
v_{\lambda}
. Similar, we fix for each
w\in W
a generator
v_{w(\lambda)}
of the line of weight
w(\lambda)
.
Let
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{n}^{+}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
.
With the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})=U(\mathfrak{n}^{-})U(\mathfrak{b})
one has
V(\lambda)=U(\mathfrak{n}^{-}).v_{\lambda}
.
For fixed
\lambda\in P^{+},w\in W
, we denote the Demazure module
V_{w}(\lambda):=U(\mathfrak{b}).v_{w(\lambda)}
.

2.2.
Monomial bases

Since
V(\lambda)=U(\mathfrak{n}^{-}).v_{\lambda}
and
V_{w}(\lambda):=U(\mathfrak{b}).v_{w(\lambda)}
one can ask for monomial bases of
V(\lambda)
(resp.
V_{w}(\lambda)
) within
U(\mathfrak{n}^{-})
(resp.
U(\mathfrak{n}^{+})
) .
A general framework on how to construct bases which are suitable within a Lie theoretic context is described in
[
FFL17a
]
using birational sequences.
One orders
\dim\mathfrak{n}^{-}
roots such that the product of their one-dimensional root subgroups is birational to
\mathfrak{n}^{-}
, then ordered monomials in the root vectors form a spanning set of
U(\mathfrak{n}^{-})
. Fixing a monomial ordering provides a basis of
U(\mathfrak{n}^{-})
and of course also of
V(\lambda)
.
The monomials are parametrized by their exponents and we denote
S(\lambda)
, the set of integer lattice points.
Then we have the general fact

Proposition 2.3

(
[
FFL17a
]
)
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in P^{+}
, then

S(\lambda_{1})+S(\lambda_{2})\subseteq S(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}).

The construction of monomial bases, of course, also works for Demazure modules using a birational sequence for
U(\mathfrak{n}^{+})
and a fixed monomial ordering.

Famous examples of monomial bases of
V(\lambda)
that fit into the framework of birational sequences are the Gelfand-Tsetlin bases
[
GC50
]
, the string bases of Littelmann and Berenstein-Zelevinsky
[
Lit98
,
BZ01
]
, the Lusztig bases
[
Lus90
,
Cal02
]
, the Nakashima-Zelevinsky bases
[
NZ97
]
, the FFLV bases
[
FFL11
,
FFL11a
]
and the Makhlin basis
[
Mak19
]
. We recall here briefly two of them, the string bases and the FFLV bases.

String bases
:

Let
w\in W
and we fix a reduced expression
\underline{w}_{0}=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{k}}
of the longest Weyl group element. Then
(\alpha_{i_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{k}})
is a birational sequence and the following is a spanning set of
V(\lambda)

\{f_{i_{1}}^{t_{1}}\cdots f_{i_{k}}^{t_{k}}.v_{\lambda}\mid t_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}\}

For the string bases, one fixes the
neglex
ordering, then the bases obtained through the birational sequence and
neglex
are described in
[
Lit98
,
BZ01
]
.
In fact, the latter provides a convex polytope
Q_{\underline{w}_{0}}(\lambda)
whose lattice points parametrize the monomials (via their exponents), e.g.
Q_{\underline{w}_{0}}(\lambda)^{\mathbb{Z}}=S_{\underline{w}_{0}}(\lambda)
.
Moreover, let
w\in W
and
V_{w}(\lambda)
be the associated Demazure module,
w=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{\ell}}
be a reduced expression, extended by
s_{i_{\ell+1}}\cdots s_{i_{k}}
to a reduced expression
\underline{w}_{0}
of
w_{0}
, then a monomial basis of
V_{w}(\lambda)
is parametrized by the lattice points in the projection of
Q_{\underline{w}_{0}}(\lambda)
to the first
\ell
coordinates (
[
Cal02
]
).
We denote this convex polytope
Q_{\underline{w}}(\lambda)
.

FFLV
:

A
good ordering
of the positive roots is an ordering such that
\beta\prec\alpha\Rightarrow
the height of
\beta
is smaller than the height of
\alpha
.
We fix a good ordering on the positive roots, starting with the highest root (and consequently ending with an arbitrary ordering of the simple roots), this provides a birational sequence and the FFLV-type bases are obtained from here by fixing the
degrevlex
(the homogeneous reverse lexicographic) order.
For type
A
and
C
they are described in
[
FFL11
]
and
[
FFL11a
]
, while for type
G_{2}
they are described in
[
Gor15
]
.
In fact, in loc. cit., convex polytopes are provided whose lattice points parameterize the bases, in type
A
and
C
these polytopes
P(\lambda)
are lattice polytopes for all
\lambda
. Moreover, one has for
\lambda=\sum a_{i}\omega_{i}\in P^{+}

P(\lambda)=\sum a_{i}P(\omega_{i}),

where
+
denotes the Minkowski sum.

In the 1990’s several families of monomial bases were constructed, mostly depending on the canonical (global) bases combinatorics. In this perspective, the upcoming remark is crucial for our interest in the topic.

Remark 2.4
.

In general (for type
A
,
n\geq 4
,
\lambda
regular, other types similarly), there is no reduced expression of
w_{0}
such that
P(\lambda)
is unimodular equivalent to
Q_{\underline{w_{0}}}(\lambda)
. In fact, the number of facets in the FFLV polytope is in general strictly larger than the number of facets in any string polytope for
V(\lambda)
. The FFLV-type polytope does not belong to the world of string polytopes (consequently also Lusztig polytopes) obtained from canonical bases.

It should also be mentioned here, that the FFLV polytopes are instances of marked chain polytopes
[
ABS11
]
while the Gelfand-Tsetlin polytopes are instances of marked order polytopes. So by
[
HL16
]
they are in general (beyond the natural representations) not unimodular equivalent. For more on this framework we refer to
[
Fan+20
]
.

2.5.
Degenerations

The PBW filtration on
U\mathfrak{(}\mathfrak{n}^{-})
induces a filtration on
V(\lambda)
, the associated graded module
V^{a}(\lambda)
is then a module for the associated graded (abelian) Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}^{-,a}
. Since the PBW filtration on
V(\lambda)
is stable under
U(\mathfrak{b})
,
V^{a}(\lambda)
is a module for the semi direct product
\mathfrak{b}\rtimes\mathfrak{n}^{-,a}
. In type
A
and
C
, the FFLV bases are monomial bases for this degenerate module as they are compatible with the PBW degree.

The degenerate modules we used in
[
Fei12
]
to define the PBW degenerate flag variety in type
A
, later generalized in
[
FFL14
]
and
[
FFL17a
]
to other types. Shortly after, in
[
CIL15
]
, it was shown that this degenerate flag variety (in type
A
and
C
) is actually a Schubert variety in a partial flag variety (in higher rank).
This surprising result, the variety obtained by forgetting the Lie structure on the module, is still a reasonable object in Lie theory, has also an interpretation in terms of modules.
Namely, there is an Lie algebra isomorphism (shown in
[
CILL16
]
for type
A
and
C
) between
\mathfrak{b}\times\mathfrak{n}^{-,a}
and a subquotient on the Borel subalgebra in
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
, the Lie algebra of the same type as
\mathfrak{g}
but of rank
2n-1
.
This translates to an isomorphism between
V^{a}(\lambda)
and
V_{\underline{w}}(\tilde{\lambda})
for a specific
\underline{w}
and
\tilde{\lambda}
.
In
[
Enu+
]
, this is even generalized to all classical type up to some modification on the filtration.

3.
Main theorem

We are giving a combinatorial shadow of the
\mathfrak{n}^{-,a}
-module isomorphism between
V^{a}(\lambda)
and
V_{\underline{w}}(\tilde{\lambda})
. That is, we will show that the FFLV-type polytopes for
V(\lambda)
are unimodularly equivalent to the string polytopes for
V_{\underline{w}}(\tilde{\lambda})
. As mentioned in Remark
2.4
, the FFLV-type polytopes for
V(\lambda)
are in general not isomorphic to string polytopes for
V(\lambda)
. The isomorphism we will construct is to string polytopes of Demazure modules.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be of type
X_{n}
(here
X
is
A
or
C
) and
\lambda=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\omega_{i}\in P^{+}
Denote
P(\lambda)
the FFLV polytope. We set for
X_{2n-1}

\tilde{\lambda}=\sum_{i}a_{i}\omega_{2i-1}

and we fix
\underline{w}
in the various types as

A
:

\underline{w}=\sigma_{n}\sigma_{n-1}\cdots\sigma_{3}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}

C
:

\underline{w}=\tau_{2n-1}\tau_{2n-2}\dots\tau_{n+1}\sigma_{n}\sigma_{n-1}\cdots\sigma_{3}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}

where

\displaystyle\tau_{j}=s_{j}s_{j+1}\dots s_{2n-1}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{j}=s_{j}s_{j+1}\dots s_{2j-1}

Theorem 3.1
.

The polytopes
P(\lambda)
and
Q_{\underline{w}}(\tilde{\lambda})
are isomorphic.

Corollary 3.2
.

Restricted to the lattice points, the equivalence is weight preserving (up to a twist and shift).

Remark 3.3
.

There are defining inequalities for the FFLV type polytope provided in
[
FFL11
,
FFL11a
,
Mak19
]
, so using the isomorphism one obtains defining inequalities for these particular string polytopes.

We provide here the outline of the proof for both types and leave the technical differences to the next sections.

Proof.

For all types
X_{n}
and
\lambda\in P^{+}
, the rational polytopes
P(\lambda)

[
FFL11
,
FFL11a
]
and
Q_{\underline{w}}(\tilde{\lambda})

[
BZ01
,
Lit98
]
are convex polytopes in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Claim:

There is an affine map
T_{X_{n},\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
with

T_{X_{n},\lambda}(y)=\mathcal{X}_{n}(y)+t_{X_{n},\lambda}

where
\mathcal{X}_{n}
is a linear map with determinant
\pm 1
, depending on
X_{n}
but not on
\lambda
,
t_{X_{n},\lambda}
a constant vector, depending linearly on
\lambda
, e.g.
t_{X_{n},-}
is a linear assignment
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then

T_{X_{n},\lambda}(P(\lambda))\subseteq Q_{\underline{w}}(\tilde{\lambda}).

Proof: We prove the claim for
\lambda
being a fundamental weight
\omega_{i}
in Lemma
4.7
.

Let
\lambda\in P^{+}
with a decomposition
\lambda=\sum a_{i}\omega_{i}
. Then

P(\lambda)=\sum a_{i}P(\omega_{i})

and hence, by construction of the map
T_{X_{n},\lambda}

\begin{array}[]{rcl}T_{X_{n},\lambda}(P(\lambda))&=&\mathcal{X}_{n}(P(\lambda))+t_{X_{n},\lambda}\\
&=&\sum a_{i}\mathcal{X}_{n}(P(\omega_{i}))+\sum a_{i}t_{X_{n},\omega_{i}}\\
&=&\sum a_{i}(\mathcal{X}_{n}(P(\omega_{i}))+\sum t_{X_{n},\omega_{i}})\end{array}

With Lemma
4.7
one deduces

T_{X_{n},\lambda}(P(\lambda))\subseteq\sum a_{i}Q_{\underline{w}}(\tilde{\omega_{i}})\subseteq Q_{\underline{w}}(\tilde{\lambda})

while the last relation is due to Proposition
2.3
. This proves the claim.

Claim:

For
\lambda\in P^{+}
we have
T_{X_{n},\lambda}(P(\lambda))=Q_{\underline{w}}(\tilde{\lambda})
.

Proof:
For fixed
\lambda\in P^{+}
,
Q_{\underline{w}}(\tilde{\lambda})
is a rational polytope. Let
k>0
be minimal, such that
kQ_{\underline{w}}(\tilde{\lambda})
is a lattice polytope. Then we have

\displaystyle T_{X_{n},k\lambda}(P(k\lambda))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle kT_{X_{n},\lambda}(P(\lambda))

previous claim

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle kQ_{\underline{w}}(\tilde{\lambda})

Proposition

2.3

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle Q_{\underline{w}}(\tilde{k\lambda})

We can consider for all polytopes the lattice points and obtained the very same inequalities with
Z
. Since for all
\lambda
,
V(\lambda)
has the same dimension as
V_{w}(\tilde{\lambda})
(the degenerate module is isomorphic to the Demazure module), we have

\sharp P(k\lambda)^{\mathbb{Z}}=\sharp Q_{\underline{w}}(\tilde{k\lambda})^{\mathbb{Z}}

and since
P(\lambda)
is a lattice polytope

\sharp k(P(\lambda)^{\mathbb{Z}})=\sharp(kQ_{\underline{w}}(\tilde{\lambda}))^{\mathbb{Z}}

This implies that all lattice points of
kQ_{\underline{w}}(\tilde{\lambda})
are already in
kT_{X_{n},\lambda}(P(\lambda))
. Since by assumption,
kQ_{\underline{w}}(\tilde{\lambda})
is a lattice polytope, it is spanned by the lattice points in the
k
-times Minkowski sum of
Q_{\underline{w}}(\tilde{\lambda})
and hence
k=1
and
Q_{\underline{w}}(\tilde{\lambda})
is a lattice polytope.

∎

Corollary 3.4
.

For all
\lambda
, the string polytope
Q_{\underline{w}}(\tilde{\lambda})
is a lattice polytope.

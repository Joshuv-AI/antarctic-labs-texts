Magnetic Charges for the Graviton Preprint: Imperial-TP-2023-CH-03
C.M. Hull

Abstract
Symmetries and conserved charges are investigated for linearised gravity and its dual formulation in terms of the dual graviton field. Conserved charges are constructed for the dual graviton theory that are associated with invariances of the dual graviton theory. These invariances arise for gauge parameters that are certain generalised Killing tensors. These electric-type charges for the dual graviton are then shown to give magnetic-type charges for the graviton. One of the magnetic-type gravitational charges obtained in this way is the linearisation of the gravitational charge in d ≥ 5 d\geq 5 dimensions that arises as a central charge in the supersymmetry algebra and is carried by Kaluza-Klein monopoles. Solutions of linearised gravity carrying the magnetic gravitational charges are discussed. The application of the approach used here to other gauge theories is discussed.

Introduction and Overview

One way of understanding magnetic charge in electromagnetism or antisymmetric tensor gauge theories is as the electric charge of the dual theory.
In this paper magnetic-type charges will be introduced for linearised gravity as the electric-type charges of the dual formulation in terms of the dual graviton
Hull:2000zn
.
The corresponding magnetic charges for non-linear gravity
will be discussed elsewhere. In this introduction, an overview of the construction and the results will be given, while the remainder of the paper will fill in the details.

Magnetic charge for gravity plays an important role in M-theory. Kaluza-Klein monopoles are an essential part of the spectrum of BPS states in M-theory
Hull:1994ys
and the charge that they carry can be viewed as a magnetic gravitational charge. On toroidal compactification, the gravitational field gives vector gauge fields (graviphotons)
and the magnetic monopoles for these graviphotons arise from Kaluza-Klein monopoles in the higher dimensional theory. Then
the magnetic charge for the graviphotons should arise from a charge carried by the Kaluza-Klein monopoles. The BPS charge carried by Kaluza-Klein monopoles is a
d-5
form charge that was introduced in
Hull:1997kt
,
where it was
shown to agree with the central term in the superalgebra for Kaluza-Klein monopole solutions. (The explicit form of this charge is given in equation (
288
).)
This charge can be viewed as a gravitational magnetic charge that arises in dimensions
d\geq 5
, while the standard ADM momentum and angular momentum
Abbott:1981ff
can be regarded as gravitational electric charges. It was seen in
Hull:2000zn
;
Hull:2001iu
that the Kaluza-Klein monopole charge can be thought of as a charge for the dual graviton.

The aim of the present work is to further investigate such charges in gravity. In this paper it will be seen that the magnetic-type charges for linearised gravity that arise as electric-type charges for the dual graviton include the Kaluza-Klein monopole charge that appears in the superalgebra but also include further charges.
The case of 4 dimensions is rather different from that of
d>4
– for example, Kaluza Klein monopoles and their associated magnetic charges only arise for
d>4
.
In the full non-linear theory, turning on magnetic charges such as the Kaluza Klein monopole charge can change the topology of the spacetime,
whereas in the linear theory of fluctuations about a Minkowski metric such complications can be avoided.

\partial_{(\mu_{1}}K_{\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{s})}=0

The gauge field couples to a source
\Theta_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{s}}=\Theta_{(\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{s})}
that is conserved,

\partial^{\mu_{1}}\Theta_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{s}}=0
. Then the following Noether current is conserved:

j_{\nu}=K^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{s-1}}\Theta_{\nu\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{s-1}}

This can then be re-expressed as a surface integral of an integrand that depends on
H
and
K
.

For any free gauge theory, the 1-form Noether current
j
is conserved. There is an associated 2-form current
J
with

*j=d*J

(30)

so that
J
is a conserved 2-form current in any region in which
j=0
, which will be referred to here as a
secondary Noether current
. This then can be integrated to give a charge

Q^{\prime}=\int_{S}*J

and
Q=Q^{\prime}
if
S
is the boundary of
\Sigma
.
In the Euclidean theory,
S
can be any closed
d-2
surface and
\Sigma
can be taken to be any
d-1
dimensional subspace with boundary
S
. If
\Sigma
has no boundary, the charge will vanish if
*j
is exact, so that
J
is globally well-defined.
The charge defined by
\int_{\Sigma}*j
for a
d-1
surface
\Sigma
is topological in the sense that it is invariant under any smooth deformation of
\Sigma
.
Similarly,
the charge defined by

\int_{S}*J
for a
d-2
surface
S
is topological in the sense that it is invariant under any smooth deformation of
S

that does not involve passing through a region in which
j\neq 0
.

For a given current
j
, (
30
) only determines
J
up to the addition of a co-closed form. For any
J
satisfying (
30
), further solutions are given by
J^{\prime}=J+d^{\dagger}K
for any 3-form
K
, where
d^{\dagger}=*d*
and
J^{\prime}=J+L
where
d^{\dagger}L=0
.

The current

corresponds to the charge density for the source of the gauge field – for electromagnetism,
j_{0}[\sigma]
is the electric charge density,
j_{0}[\tilde{\sigma}]
is the magnetic charge density and for gravity (with
k^{\mu}=\delta^{\mu}_{0}
the timelike Killing vector)

j_{0}[k]=T_{00}
is the mass density. If
\Sigma
is chosen to be a region of space at fixed time, then
\int_{\Sigma}*j
gives the total charge in that region and this can be re-expressed as
\int_{S}*J
where
S
is the boundary of
\Sigma
.
The regions in which
j=0
are then regions in which there is no local charge. For localised systems
all the charge is confined to some region of spacetime, and outside this region

.

On general grounds

;
Wald:1999wa
;
Barnich:1994db
;
Barnich:2001jy
;
Barnich:2000zw
, the 2-form current
J
has a local expression in terms of the gauge field and the Killing tensor. While the Noether current

is typically gauge invariant, the secondary Noether current

is often not, changing by a co-exact term

\delta*J=d\Delta

for some
\Delta
.
Then in such cases
*J
can be regarded as a
d-2
form gauge field
B=*J
with gauge transformation

\delta B=d\Delta
and which is flat,
dB=0
, in regions in which
j=0
. Then, for the charge
Q^{\prime}
, the operator

\exp\left(iQ^{\prime}\right)
is associated with the surface operator
\exp\left(i\int_{S}B\right)
.

In this way, the integrals of
j
and
J
define ‘electric’ charges of a given gauge theory.
In any region in which
j=0
so that
J
is conserved, the free gauge theory has a dual formulation in terms of a dual gauge field. Then
J
can be regarded as a current in the dual theory whose integral can be viewed as a
‘magnetic’ charge of the dual theory.
As will be seen, in some cases the current
J
can be written as a local expression in terms of the dual fields and in some cases it can’t.
This will be applied to the dual graviton theory here: the gauge symmetries of the dual graviton give Noether currents
j
and secondary currents
J
. Then, in regions without sources for the dual graviton, the 2-form current
J
can be regarded as a conserved current for the dual of the dual graviton theory, which is the theory of the graviton
h_{\mu\nu}
. Then the integral of these currents give magnetic charges for the graviton.

2.1
Linearised Gravity

The linearised graviton in
d
-dimensional Minkowski space with a flat background metric

\eta_{\mu\nu}
(which is used to raise and lower indices) is a free
symmetric tensor gauge field
h_{\mu\nu}

which has
the gauge symmetry

\delta h_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{(\mu}\xi_{\nu)}

(31)

The invariant field strength is the linearised Riemann tensor
5
5

5

The square brackets on indices denotes antisymmetrisation with strength one, so that e.g.
T_{[\mu\nu]}=\frac{1}{2}(T_{\mu\nu}-T_{\nu\mu})
.

R_{\mu\nu\,\sigma\tau}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\sigma}h_{\nu\tau}+\ldots)=-2\partial_{[\mu}h_{\nu][\sigma,\tau]}

(32)

which satisfies

R_{\mu\nu\,\sigma\tau}=R_{\sigma\tau\,\mu\nu}

(33)

together with
the first Bianchi identity

R_{[\mu\nu\,\sigma]\tau}=0

(34)

and the second Bianchi identity

R_{\mu\nu\,[\rho\sigma,\tau]}=0

(35)

The free field equation in
d\geq 4
is the linearised
Einstein equation

R_{\mu\,\nu}=0

(36)

where the linearised Ricci tensor is

R_{\mu\,\nu}=R^{\sigma}{}_{\mu\,\sigma\nu}

(37)

This, together with the second Bianchi identity (
35
), implies

\partial^{\mu}R_{\mu\nu\,\sigma\tau}=0

(38)

where indices are raised and lowered with the flat background metric

\eta_{\mu\nu}
.

It will be useful to introduce a linearised connection

\Gamma_{\mu\nu\,\tau}=\partial_{[\mu}h_{\nu]\tau}

(39)

in terms of which the curvature is

(40)

This formula for the curvature is invariant under

\Gamma_{\mu\nu\,\tau}\to\Gamma^{\prime}_{\mu\nu\,\tau}=\Gamma_{\mu\nu\,\tau}+\partial_{\tau}V_{\mu\nu}

(41)

for any

, which need not be antisymmetric. In particular, choosing
V_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}h_{\mu\nu}

gives a
\Gamma^{\prime}_{\mu\nu\,\tau}
which is the linearised Christoffel connection

(42)

Note that the torsion given by the antisymmetric part of the connection vanishes:

T_{\mu\nu\tau}\equiv\Gamma_{[\mu\nu\,\tau]}=0

(43)

The connection also satisfies

\partial_{[\rho}\Gamma_{\mu\nu]\,\tau}=0

2.2
Global Structure and Topological Charges

Gravity linearised around a background spacetime with background metric
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
is obtained
from the non-linear theory by writing the metric as
g_{\mu\nu}=\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}
and linearising in the fluctuation

h_{\mu\nu}
. The theory is invariant under diffeomorphisms under which

x^{\mu}{\rightarrow x^{\prime}}^{\mu}
and tensor gauge transformations with finite one-form
parameter
\xi_{\mu}
with the fields transforming as

\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}\rightarrow\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}^{\prime},\quad h_{\mu\nu}\rightarrow h_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}+2\bar{\nabla}_{(\mu}\xi_{\nu)}

(45)

where

\bar{g}^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}(x^{\prime})=\bar{g}_{\rho\sigma}(x)\frac{\partial x^{\rho}}{{\partial x^{\prime}}^{\mu}}\frac{\partial x^{\sigma}}{{\partial x^{\prime}}^{\nu}},\qquad h^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}(x^{\prime})=h_{\rho\sigma}(x)\frac{\partial x^{\rho}}{{\partial x^{\prime}}^{\mu}}\frac{\partial x^{\sigma}}{{\partial x^{\prime}}^{\nu}}

(46)

For infinitesimal diffeomorphisms under which
x^{\mu}{\rightarrow x^{\prime}}^{\mu}=x^{\mu}-\zeta^{\mu}
with infinitesimal parameter
\zeta_{\mu}
these
become

\delta\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}=\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}=2\bar{\nabla}_{(\mu}\zeta_{\nu)},\quad\delta h_{\mu\nu}=\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}h_{\mu\nu}+2\bar{\nabla}_{(\mu}\xi_{\nu)}

(47)

Similarly, a 1-form gauge field
A_{\mu}
on same the background spacetime transforms under diffeomorphisms and
gauge transformations as

A_{\mu}\rightarrow A^{\prime}_{\mu}+\partial_{\mu}\lambda

(48)

with

A^{\prime}_{\mu}(x^{\prime})=A_{\rho}(x)\frac{\partial x^{\rho}}{{\partial x^{\prime}}^{\mu}}

(49)

The background spacetime will in general be covered by a set of coordinate
patches with fields defined in each patch. In the overlaps between coordinate
patches the fields
A_{\mu},\bar{g}_{\mu\nu},h_{\mu\nu}
in the two
patches are related by transition functions that are diffeomophisms combined
with gauge transformations of the form (
45
),(
48
).

If the transition functions for a particular configuration of the field

A_{\mu}
are all just diffeomorphisms with no gauge transformations, then

A_{\mu}
are the components of a globally defined 1-form and the gauge bundle
is trivial. In particular, the magnetic charge
\int dA
, or (
3
) which gives

Q[\tilde{\sigma}]=\int_{S}d(\tilde{\sigma}\wedge A)
,
is then a
total derivative of a globally defined form and so is zero when integrated
over a closed surface. The magnetic charge can only be non-zero if the transition
functions involve gauge transformations and
A_{\mu}
is a connection on a
non-trivial bundle.

The situation is similar for
h_{\mu\nu}
. If the transition functions for a
particular configuration of the graviton field
h_{\mu\nu}
are all just
diffeomorphisms with no gauge transformations, then
h_{\mu\nu}
are the
components of a globally defined tensor and magnetic charges such as (
25
) or (
27
)
are zero. To obtain non-zero magnetic charges, it is necessary that
h_{\mu\nu}
is not a globally defined tensor and has transition functions with
non-trivial gauge transformations. See
Hull:2023dgp
for further discussion of transition functions for symmetric tensor gauge fields.

In this paper, the background is taken to be Minkowski space, or Minkowski
space with a region or regions removed, with a globally defined Cartesian coordinate
system and metric
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}
. Then there are no
diffeomorphisms in the transition functions (as the coordinates are defined over the whole space) and magnetic charges require that
the gauge fields
A_{\mu}
or
h_{\mu\nu}
are defined in patches with
non-trivial gauge transformations relating the gauge fields in different
patches. Alternatively, if one tried to define a gauge field
A_{\mu}
or

h_{\mu\nu}
with magnetic charge globally in Minkowski space (with regions removed)
without
introducing different patches, then the gauge field would have a Dirac
string singularity. The removed regions are associated with the locations of magnetic sources.

S_{[\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n+1}\,|\,\nu]\rho}=0,\qquad S_{\sigma[\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu\rho]}=0

S^{\prime}_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu}=0

where

S^{\prime}_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu}=S_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\rho\,|\,\nu}{}^{\rho}

The double-trace gives an
(n-1)

, together with the second Bianchi identity (

), implies

\partial^{\sigma}S_{\sigma\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu\rho}=0,\qquad\partial^{\rho}S_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n+1}\,|\,\nu\rho}=0

There are two kinds of dual connection that can be introduced for the dual graviton.

\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n+1}\,\nu}=\partial_{[\mu_{1}}D_{\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n+1}]\,\nu}

S_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n+1}}{}_{\nu\sigma}=\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n+1}\,[\nu,\sigma]}

S_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n+1}}{}^{\sigma\tau}=\partial_{[\mu_{1}}\hat{\Gamma}_{\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\ldots\mu_{n+1}]}{}^{\sigma\tau}

(75)

2.5
Gravitational Duality

Gravitational duality
Hull:2000zn
;
Hull:2001iu
results from identifying
S(D)
with the dual of
R(h)
,

S=*R

so that

S_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n+1}\;|\,\nu\rho}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n+1}\alpha\beta}R^{\alpha\beta}{}_{\nu\rho}

(76)

Then the graviton theory and the dual graviton theory give dual equivalent descriptions of the same system (in the absence of sources), with field equations of one formulation becoming the Bianchi identities of the other.
The field equation
R_{\mu\,\nu}=0
for
h
becomes the first Bianchi identity for
D
, which is

S_{[\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n+1}\,\nu]\rho}=0
, while the first Bianchi identity for
h
,
R_{[\mu\nu\,\sigma]\tau}=0
, becomes the field equation for
D
, which is
S^{\prime}_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,\nu}=0
.
Furthermore,

\partial_{[\rho}R_{\mu\nu]\,\sigma\tau}=0
becomes

\partial^{\sigma}S_{\sigma\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu\rho}=0,\qquad S_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n+1}\,|\,[\nu\rho,\sigma]}=0

(77)

while
\partial^{\mu}R_{\mu\nu\,\sigma\tau}=0
becomes

\partial_{[\sigma}S_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n+1}]\,|\,\nu\rho}=0,\qquad\partial^{\rho}S_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n+1}\,|\,\nu\rho}=0

(78)

The fields
h
and
D
are non-locally related by the duality constraint, although the curvatures
R,S
are locally related by (
76
). This then implies that, up to gauge transformations, the connections
\Gamma(h)
given by (
39
) and
\tilde{\Gamma}(D)
given by (
72
) can be taken to be dual,

\Gamma_{\nu\gamma\,\lambda}(h)=-\frac{1}{2}(*\tilde{\Gamma})_{\nu\gamma\,\lambda}(D)

(79)

where
(*\tilde{\Gamma})_{\tau}{}^{\mu\nu}(D)
is the dual of the connection
\tilde{\Gamma}
given by

\tilde{\Gamma}^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n+1}}{}_{\tau}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n+1}}{}_{\mu\nu}\,(*\tilde{\Gamma})^{\mu\nu}{}_{\tau}

(80)

Choosing the connections to satisfy the duality condition (
80
) amounts to imposing a gauge condition, as will now be shown.
The dual connection
\tilde{\Gamma}(D)
defined by (
72
) satisfies

\tilde{\Gamma}_{[\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n+1}\,\nu]}=0

(81)

so that (
79
) implies that
\Gamma(h)
is traceless

\Gamma_{\nu\gamma}{}^{\nu}(h)=0

(82)

which implies the gauge condition

\partial_{\mu}(h^{\mu\nu}-\eta^{\mu\nu}h)=0

(83)

Thus the duality condition (
79
) implies the gauge choice (
83
). Similarly,
\Gamma_{[\nu\gamma\,\lambda]}(h)=0
implies that

\tilde{\Gamma}(D)
is traceless

\tilde{\Gamma}^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n+1}}{}_{\mu_{1}}=0

(84)

giving a similar gauge condition for
D
.

3
Sources for Linearised Gravity and Dual Gravity

For linearised gravity, the key to coupling to a source is the
existence of the Einstein tensor

G_{\mu\nu}=R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}R

(85)

which is identically conserved

\partial^{\mu}G_{\mu\nu}=0

(86)

as a result of the Bianchi identities.
This can be seen by writing

G_{\mu}{}^{\alpha}=-\frac{3}{2}\delta_{\mu\nu\rho}^{\alpha\beta\gamma}\,R^{\nu\rho}{}_{\beta\gamma}

(87)

so that (
86
) immediately follows from (
35
).
The graviton can be consistently coupled to an energy-momentum tensor source

T_{\mu\nu}
through the linearised Einstein equation

G_{\mu\nu}=T_{\mu\nu}

(88)

provided
T_{\mu\nu}
is conserved

\partial^{\mu}T_{\mu\nu}=0

(89)

The corresponding linearised Einstein-Hilbert action can be written as
deMedeiros:2002qpr

S=\int d^{d}x\left(\frac{1}{2}h^{\mu\nu}G_{\mu\nu}-h^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}\right)

(90)

and is invariant under the gauge transformation (
31
) as a result of (
86
) and (
89
).

For dual gravity, there is an Einstein-like tensor
deMedeiros:2002qpr

E_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu}=-\frac{2}{n+2}\left(S^{\prime}_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu}-\frac{n}{2}S^{\prime\prime}_{[\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n-1}}\eta_{\mu_{n}]\nu}\right)

(91)

(with
S^{\prime},S^{\prime\prime}
defined in (
69
),(
70
))
which is identically conserved

\partial^{\mu_{1}}E_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu}=0,\qquad\partial^{\nu}E_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n-1}\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu}=0

(92)

as a consequence of (
66
),(
67
).
This can be seen by rewriting
E
in a form similar to (
87
):

E^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}|}{}_{\nu}=\,\delta^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\alpha\beta}_{\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\ldots\rho_{n+1}\nu}\,\,S^{\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\ldots\rho_{n+1}\,|\,}{}_{\alpha\beta}

(93)

so that (
92
) immediately follows from (
67
). Note that
E_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu}
is an
[n,1]
tensor satisfying

E_{[\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu]}=0

(94)

Then the dual graviton can be coupled to an
[n,1]
tensor source

U_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu}

satisfying

U_{[\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu]}=0

(95)

through

E_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu}=U_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu}

(96)

provided the source is conserved

\partial^{\mu_{1}}U_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu}=0,\qquad\partial^{\nu}U_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu}=0

(97)

The action is
deMedeiros:2002qpr

S=\int d^{d}x\left(\frac{1}{2}D^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu}E_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu}-D^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu}U_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu}\right)

(98)

and is invariant under the gauge transformations (
63
) as a result of (
92
) and (
97
).
It will be useful to introduce the
trace
U^{\prime}
of the current
U
which is an
(n-1)
-form

U^{\prime}_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n-1}}=U_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu}\,\eta^{\nu\mu_{n}}

(99)

Then (
96
) implies

S^{\prime\prime}_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n-1}}=-U^{\prime}_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n-1}}

(100)

Using the duality relation (
76
), the current
U
gives a source for
R_{[\mu\nu\,|\,\sigma]\tau}
so that the graviton Bianchi identity
(
34
) no longer holds:

R_{[\mu\nu\,|\,\sigma]\tau}-R_{[\mu\nu\,|\,\sigma\tau]}=\frac{1}{n!}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\sigma}{}^{\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\dots\rho_{n}}U_{\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\dots\rho_{n}\,|\,\tau}

(101)

or equivalently

R_{[\mu\nu\,|\,\sigma]\tau}=\frac{1}{n!}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\sigma}{}^{\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\dots\rho_{n}}\bar{U}_{\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\dots\rho_{n}\,|\,\tau}

(102)

where

\bar{U}_{\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\dots\rho_{n}\,|\,\tau}=-(-1)^{n}\frac{2}{3(n+2)}\left(U_{\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\dots\rho_{n}\,|\,\tau}-\frac{n}{2}U^{\prime}_{[\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\dots\rho_{n-1}}\eta_{\rho_{n}]\tau}\right)

(103)

with

U^{\prime}
given by (
99
).
Thus the current
U
can be regarded as a magnetic source that leads to a violation of a Bianchi identity.
Similarly,
T_{\mu\nu}
is, via the duality relation (
76
), a magnetic source for the dual gauge field
D
giving a violation of the first Bianchi identity in
(
66
).

Generally, the case in which both sources
T,U
are non-zero can be formulated in terms of a field strength
{\cal R}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
in the (reducible)
[2,0]\times[2,0]
representation in which the trace
{\cal R}_{\mu\nu}
is determined by
T
and
{\cal R}_{[\mu\nu\rho]\sigma}
is determined by
U
.
In regions in which
U=0
,
{\cal R}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
can be expressed in terms of a gauge field
h_{\mu\nu}
and the theory can be formulated as conventional linearised gravity, while in regions in which
T=0
it can be expressed in terms of a gauge field
D_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu}
giving the dual graviton theory.
In regions in which both sources are zero, both formulations are possible and are dual to each other. The
d=4
theory with both sources has been discussed in
Hull:2000rr
;
Bunster:2006rt
;
Barnich:2008ts
; the general case will be discussed elsewhere.

4
Conserved Charges

4.1
Conserved Charges for the Graviton

The graviton is invariant (
\delta h_{\mu\nu}=0
) under gauge transformations (
31
) where the parameter
\xi
is a Killing vector of Minkowski space
\xi_{\mu}=k_{\mu}
satisfying

\partial_{(\mu}k_{\nu)}=0

(104)

Corresponding to the invariance under transformations generated by a given Killing vector
k
, there is a corresponding conserved charge
Q[k]
.
Indeed,

j_{\mu}[k]=T_{\mu\nu}k^{\nu}

(105)

is a conserved current (using
\partial^{\mu}T_{\mu\nu}=0
)

\partial^{\mu}j_{\mu}=0

(106)

and so can be integrated over a constant time slice
\Sigma
to give a conserved charge

Q[k]=\int_{\Sigma}j_{\mu}[k]\,d\Sigma^{\mu}

(107)

Using the field equation (
88
)
the current (
105
) becomes

j_{\mu}[k]=G_{\mu\nu}k^{\nu}

(108)

so that the charge (
107
) can be written as

Q[k]=\int_{\Sigma}G_{\mu\nu}k^{\nu}\,d\Sigma^{\mu}

(109)

4.2
Conserved Charges for the Dual Graviton

The dual graviton
D_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu}
is invariant (
\delta D=0
) under gauge transformations (
63
) with parameter
\alpha

given by a generalised Killing tensor
\kappa_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n-1}\,|\,\rho}
which is in the
[n-1,1]
representation and satisfies

\partial_{[\mu}\kappa_{\nu\ldots\sigma]\,|\rho}=0

(110)

or transformations (
63
) with parameter
\beta

given by a tensor
\lambda_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n}}
in the
[n]
representation satisfying

\partial_{\rho}\lambda_{\mu\nu\ldots\sigma}-\partial_{[\rho}\lambda_{\mu\nu\ldots\sigma]}=0

(111)

so that
\lambda_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n}}

is a Killing-Yano tensor.

For each Killing tensor
\kappa
there is a current

j_{\rho}[\kappa]=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\kappa^{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n-1}\,|\,\nu}U_{\rho\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n-1}\,|\,\nu}

(112)

which is conserved (
\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}=0
) as a consequence of (
97
),(
110
), and for each Killing-Yano tensor
\lambda
there is a current

j_{\nu}[\lambda]=\frac{1}{n!}\lambda^{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n}}U_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu}

(113)

which is conserved as a consequence of (
95
),(
97
),(
111
).
Integrating these over a constant time slice gives conserved charges
Q[\kappa],Q[\lambda]
:

Q[\kappa]=\int_{\Sigma}\,j_{\rho}[\kappa]\,d\Sigma^{\rho}

(114)

Q[\lambda]=\int_{\Sigma}\,j_{\rho}[\lambda]\,d\Sigma^{\rho}

(115)

The 1-form current

j_{\rho}[\kappa]

can be viewed as the Noether current for the 0-form symmetry with parameter

\varepsilon

given by (
63
) with

\alpha_{\nu\ldots\sigma\,|\,\rho}=\varepsilon\kappa_{\nu\ldots\sigma\,|\,\rho}

while the 1-form current

j_{\rho}[\lambda]

can be viewed as the Noether current for the 0-form symmetry
with parameter
\epsilon

given by (
63
) with
\beta_{\mu\nu\ldots\sigma}=\epsilon\lambda_{\mu\nu\ldots\sigma}
.
Then
Q[\kappa],Q[\lambda]
are the corresponding Noether charges.

Using the field equation (
96
), the currents can be written as

j_{\rho}[\kappa]=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\kappa^{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n-1}\,|\,\nu}E_{\rho\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n-1}\,|\,\nu}

(116)

and

j^{\nu}[\lambda]=\frac{1}{n!}\lambda_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n}}E^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\,|\,\nu}

(117)

The relation (
93
) gives

j^{\rho}[\kappa]=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\,\delta^{\rho\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n-1}\alpha\beta}_{\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\ldots\rho_{n+1}\nu}\,\,\kappa_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n-1}}{}^{\nu}\,S^{\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\ldots\rho_{n+1}}{}_{\alpha\beta}

(118)

The dual of the Killing tensor

\tilde{\kappa}^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta\,|\,\nu}=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\kappa_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n-1}\,|}{}^{\nu}\,\epsilon^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n-1}\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}

(119)

satisfies

\tilde{\kappa}^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta\,|\,\nu}=\tilde{\kappa}^{[\alpha\beta\gamma\delta]\,|\,\nu},\qquad\partial_{\alpha}\tilde{\kappa}^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta\,|\,\nu}=0,\qquad\tilde{\kappa}^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}{}_{\alpha}=0

(120)

Note that
\tilde{\kappa}^{[\alpha\beta\gamma\delta\,|\,\nu]}
need not vanish.
The current (
118
) can be rewritten (using (
55
) with
r=1
) as

j^{\alpha}[\kappa]=-(-1)^{n}\frac{1}{d-1}\,\tilde{\kappa}^{\alpha\nu\rho\sigma|\mu}\,\tilde{S}_{\mu[\nu\rho\sigma]}

(121)

where
\tilde{S}(D)

is the dual of
S(D)

S_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n+1}\;\nu\rho}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n+1}\alpha\beta}\tilde{S}^{\alpha\beta}{}_{\nu\rho}

(122)

Gravitational duality amounts to identifying
\tilde{S}(D)
with
R(h)
, or equivalently
S(D)
with
*R(h)
.

Next consider the current (
117
)
which can be rewritten using (
93
) as

j_{\nu}[\lambda]=\frac{1}{n!}\lambda_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n}}\delta^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}\alpha\beta}_{\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\ldots\rho_{n+1}\nu}\,\,S^{\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\ldots\rho_{n+1}\,|\,}{}_{\alpha\beta}

(123)

Introducing the dual of the Killing-Yano
n
-form
\lambda
,

\tilde{\lambda}^{\alpha\beta\gamma}=\frac{1}{n!}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n}}}\lambda_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n}}

(124)

the current can be written as

j_{\nu}[\lambda]=(-)^{n}\frac{1}{d-1}\tilde{\lambda}^{\alpha\beta\gamma}\,\,\tilde{S}_{\nu[\alpha\beta\gamma]}

(125)

4.3
Killing Vectors

The general Killing vector in Minkowski space is of the form

k_{\mu}=V_{\mu}+\Lambda_{\mu\nu}x^{\nu}

(126)

for a constant vector
V_{\mu}
and constant 2-form,
\Lambda_{\mu\nu}=-\Lambda_{\nu\mu}
.
Then

Q[k]=V_{\mu}P^{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{\mu\nu}J^{\mu\nu}

(127)

where
P^{\mu}
is the momentum and
J^{\mu\nu}
is the angular momentum. In particular,
P^{0}
is the mass.
As the symmetries (
31
) are abelian, the corresponding algebra of the charges
P^{m},J^{mn}
is abelian in the linearised theory.

4.4
Killing Tensors for the Dual Graviton

A Killing-Yano tensor
\lambda_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n}}
in the
[n]
representation satisfies

\partial_{\rho}\lambda_{\mu\nu\ldots\sigma}-\partial_{[\rho}\lambda_{\mu\nu\ldots\sigma]}=0

(128)

The general Killing-Yano tensor satisfying (
128
) in Minkowski space
is

\lambda_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n}}=a_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n}}+b_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n}\nu}x^{\nu}

(129)

where
a_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n}}
,
b_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n+1}}
are constant totally antisymmetric tensors, and so consist of an
n
-form and an

(n+1)
-form.
This then gives an

n
-form charge
\hat{P}_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n}}
and an
n+1
-form charge
\hat{J}_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n+1}}

given by

Q[\lambda]=\frac{1}{n!}a_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n}}\hat{P}^{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n}}+\frac{1}{(n+1)!}b_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n+1}}\hat{J}^{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n+1}}

(130)

Consider now the generalised Killing tensor
\kappa_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n-1}\,|\,\rho}
in the
[n-1,1]
representation satisfying

\partial_{[\mu}\kappa_{\nu\ldots\sigma]\rho}=0

(131)

A particular class of ‘closed’
[n-1,1]
Killing tensors
\kappa
satisfying (
131
) are those that are ‘exact’, i.e. of the form

\kappa_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n-1}\,|\,\rho}=\partial_{[\mu_{1}}\sigma_{\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{n-1}]\,|\,\rho}

(132)

for
n>2
.
Such ‘exact’ tensors automatically satisfy (
131
) for any

[n-2,1]
tensor
\sigma_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n-2}\,|\,\rho}
.
As
\sigma_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n-2}\,|\,\rho}
is an

[n-2,1]
tensor it satisfies

\sigma_{[\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n-2}\,|\,\rho]}=0
and so
(
132
) satisfies

\kappa_{[\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n-1}\,|\,\rho]}=0
and as a result is an

[n-1,1]
tensor.
For such Killing tensors, the charge
(
114
) becomes a boundary term

For

5
Surface Integral Charges

Each of the currents considered in section
4
can be written as a surface integral by first using the field equations to express them in terms of gauge fields and then finding a secondary 2-form current
J_{\mu\nu}

such that

j_{\mu}=\partial^{\nu}J_{\mu\nu}

so the corresponding charge can be written as an integral over the

(d-2)
dimensional boundary
S=\partial\Sigma

Q=\int_{\Sigma}j_{\mu}\,d\Sigma^{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{S}\,J_{\mu\nu}\,d\Sigma^{\mu\nu}

The existence of secondary 2-form currents and their explicit form follows from the arguments of
Barnich:2001jy
;
Lee:1990nz
;
Wald:1999wa
, but for the linearised theory it is straightforward to find them directly, following
Abbott:1981ff
.
Note that
j_{\mu}=\partial^{\nu}J_{\mu\nu}

does not determine
J
uniquely. Given any solution
J
, another is given by

{J^{\prime}}_{\mu\rho}=J_{\mu\rho}+\partial^{\sigma}\Lambda_{\mu\rho\sigma}

where
\Lambda_{\mu\rho\sigma}=\Lambda_{[\mu\rho\sigma]}
is an arbitrary 3-form. The corresponding charge is independent of
\Lambda_{\mu\rho\sigma}
as
Q=\int_{S}*J=\int_{S}*J^{\prime}
.

the curvature
S=\tilde{R}
is the dual of
R
,

\tilde{R}(\tilde{h})=*R(h)
,
so that

\tilde{R}_{\mu\sigma\nu\rho}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\mu\sigma\alpha\beta}R^{\alpha\beta}{}_{\nu\rho}

(190)

The dual connection is

\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu\,\tau}=\partial_{[\mu}\tilde{h}_{\nu]\tau}

(191)

and the duality relation (
190
) implies that one can choose a gauge in which this connection is dual to the connection (
39
):

\tilde{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu\,\tau}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\mu\nu}{}^{\alpha\beta}\Gamma_{\alpha\beta\,\tau}

(192)

The source is a symmetric dual stress tensor
U_{\mu\nu}=\tilde{T}_{\mu\nu}
, with field equation given by

\tilde{G}_{\mu\nu}=\tilde{T}_{\mu\nu}

(193)

where
\tilde{G}_{\mu\nu}
is the linearised Einstein tensor for the dual graviton
\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu}
.

The Killing tensors from gauge transformations preserving
\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu}
are given by parameters
\tilde{\xi}_{\nu}
that are Killing vectors satisfying (
104
).
The current

\tilde{j}_{\mu}[k]=\tilde{T}_{\mu\nu}k^{\nu}

(194)

is then conserved, giving a conserved charge

\tilde{Q}[k]=\int_{\Sigma}\tilde{j}_{\mu}[k]\,d\Sigma^{\mu}

(195)

The structure of the currents is of the same form as that described in sections
4.1
and
5.1
, but with
h_{\mu\nu}
replaced by the dual graviton
\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu}
.
Using the field equation (
193
) for
\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu}
,
the current can be written as
\tilde{j}_{\mu}[k]=\partial^{\nu}\tilde{J}_{\mu\nu}[k]

where

\tilde{J}^{\mu\rho}[k]=(\partial_{\sigma}\tilde{K}^{\mu\rho\,|\,\nu\sigma})k_{\nu}-\tilde{K}^{\mu\sigma\,|\,\nu\rho}\partial_{\sigma}k_{\nu}

(196)

\tilde{K}^{\mu\rho\,|\,\nu\sigma}=-6\eta^{\mu\rho\alpha|\nu\sigma\beta}\tilde{h}_{\alpha\beta}

(197)

The corresponding charge is

\tilde{Q}[k]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{S}\,\,d\Sigma_{\mu\rho}\left[(\partial_{\sigma}\tilde{K}^{\mu\rho\,|\,\nu\sigma})k_{\nu}-\tilde{K}^{\mu\sigma\,|\,\nu\rho}\partial_{\sigma}k_{\nu}\right]

(198)

For constant Killing vectors
k

the charge can be written in the form (
154
), but with the connection
\Gamma
replaced by the dual connection
\tilde{\Gamma}

\tilde{Q}[k]=-\frac{3}{2}\int_{S}\delta_{\mu\nu\rho}^{\alpha\beta\gamma}\,k^{\mu}\,\tilde{\Gamma}^{\nu\rho}{}_{\gamma}\,d\Sigma_{\alpha\beta}

(199)

This can then be rewritten in terms of
\Gamma(h)
using (
55
),(
192
) as

\tilde{Q}[k]=-\int_{S}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}k^{\mu}\,\Gamma_{\mu\delta\,\gamma}\,d\Sigma_{\alpha\beta}

(200)

Using
\Gamma_{[\gamma|\mu\delta]}=0
, this becomes

\tilde{Q}[k]=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{S}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}k^{\mu}\,\Gamma_{\gamma\delta\,\mu}\,d\Sigma_{\alpha\beta}=-\int_{S}k^{\mu}\,(*\Gamma)_{\alpha\beta\,\mu}\,d\Sigma^{\alpha\beta}

(201)

The charges
\tilde{P}^{\mu}
defined for constant
k^{\mu}
by

\tilde{Q}[k]=k_{\mu}\tilde{P}^{\mu}
is the linearised version of the dual momentum or NUT 4-momentum introduced for general relativity in
Ramaswamy
;
Ashtekar
. The charge can be written as

\tilde{Q}[k]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{S}\,\partial^{\rho}Z_{\mu\nu\rho}(k)\,d\Sigma^{\mu\nu}

(202)

where

Z_{\mu\nu\rho}(k)=-\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}k^{\tau}h_{\tau}{}^{\sigma}

(203)

and so is a topological charge that is zero if
h_{\mu\nu}
is a globally defined tensor.
It can be written as

\tilde{Q}[k]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{S}\,d(*Z)

(204)

where

*Z
is the one-form

*Z=k^{\tau}h_{\tau\sigma}\,dx^{\sigma}

(205)

This is a linearisation of the Komar-type form for the NUT 4-momentum that was discussed in
Bossard:2008sw
.

For the non-constant Killing vectors
k_{\mu}=\Lambda_{\mu\nu}x^{\nu}

(generating Lorentz transformations), the dual angular-momentum charge

Q[k]=\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\Lambda}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{J}^{\mu\nu}

can be written locally in terms of
\tilde{h}
and this is non-local when expressed in terms of

h
and
k_{\mu}
. An alternative local expression will be discussed in
Hutt
.

8
Kaluza Klein Monopoles and Other Solutions

In this section some explicit solutions of linearised gravity will be considered and their electric and magnetic charges found.
Of particular interest is the solution
that results from the linearisation of the Kaluza-Klein monopole solution of the full non-linear gravity or supergravity in
d
dimensions. In the linear theory, this results from the superposition of a solution carrying mass and one carrying gravitational magnetic charge, so that these solutions can be considered separately.
It will be seen that, on dimensional reduction, the magnetic gravitational charge
Q[\kappa]
reduces to the magnetic charge of the graviphoton.

8.1
Kaluza-Klein Ansatz

The background spacetime in
d
dimensions will be taken to be the product of

d^{\prime}=d-1
-dimensional Minkowski space with
coordinates
x^{m}
and a circle of radius
r
with periodic coordinate
y
.
On Kaluza Klein reduction on the circle to
d^{\prime}
dimensions,

gives a
d^{\prime}
-dimensional graviton
h_{mn}
, a
d^{\prime}
-dimensional graviphoton
A_{m}=h_{my}
and a scalar
\phi=h_{yy}
.
In this section, the solutions that will be discussed are ones in which all fields are independent of
y

(Note that one can also consider the
y
-independent system in
d
dimensional Minkowski space with the
y
direction non-compact, which can also be formulated in terms of the same fields in
d^{\prime}
dimensions.)

In
linearised gravity, one can consider solutions with, say, just
A_{m}=h_{my}
non-zero. Then a solution
A_{m}
of
d^{\prime}
-dimensional Maxwell theory can be lifted to a solution of
d
-dimensional linearised gravity and the electric and magnetic charges lift to gravitational charges in
d
dimensions.
The electric charge
q
in
d^{\prime}
dimensions lifts to the
y

P^{y}
of the momentum (
127

p_{m_{1}\dots m_{d^{\prime}-4}}
lifts to the component
\tilde{P}_{m_{1}\dots m_{d^{\prime}-4}y\,|\,y}
of the gravitational charge
Q[\kappa]
defined by (

Consider the ansatz for the curvature

R_{mnpy}=\partial_{p}F_{mn}

(206)

If
\partial_{[p}F_{mn]}=0
, which implies that locally there is a 1-form potential
A
, with
F=dA
, then (
206

32
) for

h_{my}=2A_{m}

(207)

with
h_{mn}=0
,
h_{yy}=0

Then

so

T_{ny}=j_{n}

(209)

The connection (
39

\Gamma_{mn\,|y}=\partial_{[m}h_{n]y}=2\partial_{[m}A_{n]}=F_{mn}

(210)

\Gamma_{ny\,|m}=\partial_{n}A_{m}

(211)

If there are magnetic sources
\tilde{j}=*dF
, then

R_{[mnp]y}=\partial_{[p}F_{mn]}=(*\tilde{j})_{mnp}

Magnetic monopoles in
d^{\prime}
dimensions have
\tilde{j}\neq 0
at the locations of the monopoles and so give a violation of the gravitational Bianchi identity
R_{[mnp]y}=0
in
d=d^{\prime}+1
dimensions.
The dual (
76
) of the curvature is

S_{m_{1}m_{2}\ldots m_{d-3}y|py}=\partial_{p}\widetilde{F}_{m_{1}m_{2}\ldots.m_{d-3}}

(213)

with trace

S^{\prime}_{m_{1}m_{2}\ldots.m_{d-4}y|y}=\partial^{p}\widetilde{F}_{m_{1}m_{2}\ldots m_{d-4}p}

(214)

so that the dual gravitational source is

U_{m_{1}m_{2}\ldots m_{d-2}y|y}=\tilde{j}_{m_{2}\ldots.m_{d-2}}

(215)

The potential
A
and the graviton

are only defined in regions where
\tilde{j}=0
so that
U=0

Consider a magnetic current
\tilde{j}
that is a delta-function so that the source is a magnetic monopole
if
d^{\prime}=d-1=4
and is a magnetic
d^{\prime}-4
brane if
d^{\prime}>4
.
Removing the source location from the spacetime leaves a space
{\cal M}
with non-trivial topology that can can support magnetic charge, with
A
a gauge connection of a bundle on
{\cal M}
.

The Killing tensor given by (
136
) with
V^{\mu}
the unit vector in the
y
direction,
V^{\mu}=(V^{m},V^{y})=(0,1)
, so that

\kappa_{m_{1}\ldots m_{n-2}y\,|\,y}=\alpha_{m_{1}\ldots m_{n-2}}

(216)

with
\alpha(x^{m})
a closed
n-2
form.
Then the charge (
137
),(
139
) is

Q[\kappa]=Q[V,\alpha]=(-)^{n}\frac{1}{4}\frac{1}{d-1}\,\int_{S}\,\tilde{\kappa}^{mnpq\,|\,y}\Gamma_{pq\,|y}d\Sigma_{mn}

(217)

and using (

Q[V,\alpha]=(-)^{n}\frac{1}{d-1}\,\frac{1}{4}\int_{S}\,(*\alpha)^{mnpqy}F_{pq}d\Sigma_{mn}

This is precisely the magnetic charge for Maxwell theory in
d^{\prime}=d-1
dimensions (
3
) with

\tilde{\sigma}=(-)^{n}\frac{1}{d-1}\,\alpha

(219)

For constant
\alpha
, (
139
) gives the charge
\tilde{P}^{m_{1}\ldots m_{n-2}y|y}
with

Q[V,\alpha]=\frac{1}{(n-2)!}\tilde{P}^{m_{1}\ldots m_{n-2}y|y}\alpha_{m_{1}\ldots m_{n-2}}

(220)

Choosing instead

\kappa_{m_{1}\ldots m_{n-2}p\,|\,q}=-\alpha_{[m_{1}\ldots m_{n-2}}\eta_{p]q}

(221)

gives the same charge

, a reflection of the fact that Killing tensors of the form (

8.2

This and the following subsections will discuss solutions in

in a background spacetime given by the product of a circle of radius

In this subsection, the only non-vanishing component of the graviton is

The electrically charged solution has

(222)

The 4-d field strength is

\Delta W=4\pi\sum_{s}{q_{s}}\delta(x-x_{s})

This is a solution to the equations of motion with energy-momentum tensor

(227)

Dimensionally reducing on the
y
circle to 4 dimensions gives
an electric current

The charge

(228)

and agrees with the total electric charge in

dimensions.

There is a similar solution with magnetic charges given by

h_{iy}=2A_{i}

(229)

with all other components vanishing. Here
A_{i}(x^{j})
is a vector potential on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with field strength

F_{ij}=\partial_{i}A_{j}-\partial_{j}A_{i}

satisfying

F_{ij}=\epsilon_{ijk}\partial_{k}\widetilde{W}

(231)

with

\widetilde{W}(x^{i})=-\sum_{s}\frac{p_{s}}{|x-x_{s}|}

Dimensionally reducing on the

circle to 4 dimensions gives

at the points

in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

For a single charge at

The non-vanishing components of the curvature are

R_{ijky}=\partial_{k}F_{ij}

(233)

(235)

Then

of the monopoles.

In 5-dimensions, the Killing tensor

8.3
Solution with electric mass

Again in the 5-dimensional set-up of the previous subsection, consider the ansatz

8.6
Global Structure

A magnetic monopole of charge
p
at the origin in 4 dimensions has
potential

\widetilde{W}(x^{i})=-p\frac{1}{|x|}

(261)

so that the
field strength
(
231
)
written in spherical polar coordinates is the 2-form

F=p\sin\theta d\theta\wedge d\phi

(262)

Two choices of gauge potential 1-form are
A_{\pm}
with

A_{\pm}=p(\pm 1-\cos\theta)d\phi

(263)

Here
A_{+}
has a Dirac string singularity at
\theta=\pi
and

A_{-}
has a Dirac string singularity at
\theta=0
.
If there is no charged matter coupling to
A
, then the Dirac strings are unobservable and either potential
A_{\pm}
can be used.
If there is charged matter, the Dirac strings are unobservable only if the electric charges all satisfy the Dirac quantisation condition.

The same results can also be understood in terms of the topology of the gauge bundle. Removing the origin from
\mathbb{R}^{3}
leaves a topologicially non-trivial space

(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}\times S^{1}
that can be covered by two patches,

H_{\pm}
. Here
H_{+}
is the region with
0\leq\theta<\pi/2+\epsilon

and
H_{-}
is the region with

\pi/2-\epsilon<\theta\leq\pi
. Then
A_{\pm}
has no singularity in
H_{\pm}
so
A_{+}
can be used in
H_{+}
and
A_{-}
can be used in
H_{-}
.
In the overlap, the potentials are related by

A_{+}-A_{-}=2pd\phi

(264)

In the absence of charged matter, the free Maxwell theory has a symmetry under shifts of the potential by a closed 1-form:

A\to A+\alpha,\qquad d\alpha=0

(265)

The two potentials
A_{\pm}
differ by a closed form (
264
) and so in this case the gauge potentials are related by a gauge transformation of this type.
If there is charged matter, then the gauge symmetry is broken to shifts by

A\to A+d\lambda

(266)

The difference between the gauge potentials
A_{\pm}
is then a gauge transformation with

\lambda=2p\phi

(267)

which requires that
\lambda
be a periodic variable with

\lambda\sim\lambda+4\pi p
, so that the gauge group is
U(1)
.
A charged field
\psi
of charge
q

will transform under gauge transformations as
\psi\to e^{iq\lambda}\psi
.
The fields
\psi_{\pm}
in
H_{\pm}
are related by the gauge transformation

\psi_{+}=e^{iq\lambda}\psi_{-}

(268)

with
\lambda=2p\phi
, and this is well-defined provided
that the charges satisfy the Dirac quantization condition

pq=\frac{N}{2}

(269)

for some integer
N
.

Now consider the magnetic solution of subsection
8.2
with
h_{iy}=A_{i}

satisfying (
230
),(
231
) with a single centre potential (
261
).
Here
A
can be taken to be
A_{+}
or
A_{-}
, both of which have Dirac strings, giving rise to Dirac strings in the graviton field
h_{\mu\nu}
.
Electrically charged particles in 4 dimensions lift to particles carrying momentum
P^{y}
in the
y
direction and if there are no particles carrying this momentum then the Dirac strings are unobservable.

Instead, removing the origin and taking the two patches
H_{\pm}
with gravitons
h_{\pm}

9
Discussion

Invariances of a gauge theory – i.e. gauge transformations that leave the gauge field (plus any other fields in the theory) invariant – can be regarded as global symmetries and have corresponding Noether charges. For free theories, this gives a rich set of charges as there are invariances for
any
field configuration. For interacting theories, however, there are typically no such invariances as invariances only arise for special field configurations. For example, for general relativity the invariances are isometries of the spacetime, and most spacetimes do not have any isometries. Such charges are then only defined for those spacetimes with isometries.
Fortunately, the construction generalises to spaces with asymptotic isometries:
for asymptotically flat or asymptotically AdS spacetimes,
if suitable boundary conditions are satisfied, then an asymptotic invariance is sufficient for there to be conserved ADM charges
Abbott:1981ff
. For the free theories with exact invariances there is a charge conservation and charges can be defined as an integral over any closed
d-2
surface. On the other hand, for the interacting theories with asymptotic invariances the surface must be a sphere at infinity and there is no local charge – e.g. for linearised gravity there is a local concept of energy but for general relativity energy is non-local and the ADM mass is defined by a sphere at infinity. Here, attention has been restricted to exact invariances and the case of asymptotic invariances will be discussed elsewhere.

The discussion here extends readily to tensor fields with Young tableaux with
N
columns. For a gauge field in such a representation there are
N
independent symmetries if the all the columns have different lengths (if some of the lengths are equal, there will be less independent symmetries)
Curtright:1980yk
-
Dubois-Violette:1999rd
,
deMedeiros:2002qpr
and for each symmetry a charge can be defined in the way discussed here. The differential calculus of multiforms introduced in
deMedeiros:2002qpr
;
Bekaert:2002dt
provides an elegant way of formulating the theory.

The dual formulation of the theory will have its own electric type (Noether) symmetries and these in turn will have associated conserved charges. The electric-type charges of the dual theory can then be interpreted as magnetic-type charges for the original theory.
This has been seen in detail for the free graviton and dual graviton theories but the method applies more generally.

In 4 dimensions the electric gravitational charges are the momentum
P^{\mu}
and the angular momentum
J^{\mu\nu}
while the magnetic ones discussed in section

7

are the dual
momentum
\tilde{P}^{\mu}
and angular momentum
\tilde{J}^{\mu\nu}
.
The expression found for the angular momentum

depends explicitly on the graviton field
h_{\mu\nu}
and so cannot be written locally for the dual graviton theory, so that it can be thought of as a non-local charge there.
Similarly, the dual
angular momentum
\tilde{J}^{\mu\nu}
depends explicitly on the dual graviton field
\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu}
and so cannot be written locally for the graviton theory, so that it can be viewed as a non-local charge for linearised gravity.
The linearised Lorentzian Taub-NUT solution carries the dual mass
\tilde{P}^{0}
, often referred to as the NUT charge.

In
d>4

the electric gravitational charges are again the momentum

while for constant

These charges for the full non-linear supergravity theory can be written as follows.

In the non-linear theory, gravitational energy is non-local, so that only the total energy in the spacetime can be defined. Moreover, introducing magnetic charges changes the topology of spacetime (or introduces Dirac string singularities) and the non-trivial topology leads to some issues in the definition of the charges, as discussed in e.g.

The linear theory has the advantage that energy is local and the charge contained within any surface

In the linear theory there is no longer a relation between the graviton field and the geometry and topology of spacetime and one can work in a flat space background, so that the nature of the magnetic charges is clearer than it is in the full interacting theory.

An alternative approach to constructing charges for a given theory from the conserved charges of a dual formulation of that theory was given in
Nguyen:2022nnx

Nguyen:2022nnx

There is a parent action that gives rise to both the graviton and dual graviton theories

On the Transcendence of Power Towers of Liouville Numbers
Diego Marques, Marcelo Oliveira, Pavel Trojovský

Abstract
. In this paper, among other things, we explicit a G δ G_{\delta} -dense set of Liouville numbers, for which the triple power tower of any of its elements is a transcendental number.

Primary 11J81; Secondary 11J86

1.
Introduction

The beginning of the transcendental number theory happened in 1844 when Liouville
[
10
]
showed that real algebraic numbers are not “well-approximated” by rational numbers. By using this fact, Liouville explicitly, for the first time, provided examples of transcendental numbers (known as Liouville numbers now). We recall that a real number
\xi
is called a
Liouville number
if there exist infinitely many rational numbers
p_{k}/q_{k}
, with
q_{k}>1
, such that

\displaystyle\left|\xi-\frac{p_{k}}{q_{k}}\right|<q_{k}^{-\omega_{k}}
,
\forall k
,

for some sequence of positive real numbers
(\omega_{k})_{k}
which tends to
\infty
as
k\to\infty
. The first example of a Liouville number (and consequently, of a transcendental number) is the known
Liouville constant
defined by
\ell:=\sum_{n\geq 1}10^{-n!}
(i.e., the decimal with
1
’s in each factorial position and
0
’s otherwise). The set of Liouville numbers is denoted by
\mathbb{L}
.

In his pioneering book, Maillet
[
14
, Chapitre III]
discussed some arithmetic properties of Liouville numbers. One of them is that given a non-constant rational function
f
, with rational coefficients, if
\xi
is a Liouville number, then so is
f(\xi)
. Thus, a natural problem concerns the possible arithmetic behavior of
f(\xi)
when
\xi\in\mathbb{L}
and
f
is a transcendental function. In particular, it remains unsolved the question raised by Mahler
[
13
]
about the existence of a transcendental entire function taking
\mathbb{L}
into itself. However, there are some results on this subject. For instance, Chalebgwa and Morris
[
4
]
proved, that
e^{\xi}
is transcendental whenever
\xi\in\mathbb{L}
. Also, Marques and Oliveira
[
16
]
showed that
\alpha^{\xi}
is transcendental, for all Liouville number
\xi
and all algebraic number
\alpha\not\in\{0,1\}
. By using Baire’s theorem (see
[
9
]
), one has that for any non-constant function
f
, there is a
G_{\delta}
-dense set
\mathcal{L}
of Liouville numbers such that
f(\mathcal{L})\subseteq\mathbb{L}
.

Set, inductively,
\exp[n](x)=\exp(\exp[n-1](x))
and
\exp[0](x)=x
. In 2015, Marques and Moreira
[
15
]
defined an
ultra-Liouville number

\xi
as a real number for which, for every positive integer
k
, there exist infinitely many rational numbers
p/q
, with
q>1
, such that

0<\left|\alpha-\frac{p}{q}\right|<\frac{1}{\exp[k](q)}.

They proved the existence of a transcendental entire function
f
for which
f(\mathbb{L}_{\text{ultra}})\subseteq\mathbb{L}_{\text{ultra}}
(where
\mathbb{L}_{\text{ultra}}
denotes the set of ultra-Liouville numbers).

Very recently, Marques and Oliveira
[
16
]
showed that
\xi^{\xi}
is a transcendental number whenever
\xi
is an ultra-Liouville number (indeed, they provided a larger class of Liouville numbers with this property).

We now define sub-classes of Liouville numbers
\xi
(which satisfy (
1
)) depending on the growth of the map
\nu\mapsto\lim_{k\to\infty}\omega_{k}/q_{k}^{\nu}
. More specifically, we have the following definition:

Definition 1.1
.

A real number
\xi
is called a
\nu
-Liouville number if
\nu
is the infimum (possibly infinite) of the non-negative real numbers
\nu^{*}
for which there exists an infinite sequence of rational numbers
(p_{k}/q_{k})_{k}
(with
q_{k}>1
) and a sequence of positive real numbers
(\omega_{k})_{k}
such that

\displaystyle\left|\xi-\frac{p_{k}}{q_{k}}\right|<q_{k}^{-\omega_{k}}
,
\forall k
,

and
\omega_{k}/q_{k}^{\nu^{*}}
tends to
\infty
as
k\to\infty
. The set of all
\nu
-Liouville numbers is denoted by
\mathbb{L}^{(\nu)}
.

We note that
0
-Liouville numbers are precisely the Liouville numbers and
\mathbb{L}^{(\nu)}\subseteq\mathbb{L}
, for any
\nu\in[0,\infty]
. Also,
\mathbb{L}^{(\infty)}
is a
G_{\delta}
-dense set since it contains the set of ultra-Liouville numbers (indeed, one has that
|\xi-p_{k}/q_{k}|<\exp(-\exp(q_k))=q_{k}^{-\omega_{k}}
, where
\omega_{k}:=\exp(q_k)/\log q_{k}
, satisfies
\lim_{k\to\infty}\omega_{k}/q_{k}^{\nu}=\infty
,
\forall\nu\geq 0
). For topological reasons, the set
\mathbb{L}^{(\nu)}
is not
G_{\delta}
-dense whenever
\nu\in(0,\infty)
. So a natural question arises: are the sets
\mathbb{L}^{(\nu)}
non-empty, for all
\nu\in(0,\infty)
? Our first result answers this question positively. More precisely,

Theorem 1.1
.

The set
\mathbb{L}^{(\nu)}
is uncountable, for any
\nu>0
.

The main goal of this paper is to provide some results on the arithmetic behavior of a triple power tower of numbers related to the previously defined sets. In order to state some conditions succinctly, we introduce the following terminology: a triple of polynomials
(P(X),Q(X),R(X))
is said to be
\mathbb{K}
-admissible
(where
\mathbb{K}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
), when

(i)

P(X),Q(X)\in\mathbb{K}[X]
and
R(X)\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
;

(ii)

P(X)\not\equiv 0
,
Q(X)\not\equiv 0
and
P(X)\not\equiv 1
;

(iii)

P^{\prime}(X)\equiv 0
implies
Q^{\prime}(X)\not\equiv 0
and
R(X)\not\equiv 0
;

(iv)

Q^{\prime}(X)\equiv 0
implies
P^{\prime}(X)\not\equiv 0
;

(v)

P^{\prime}(X)\equiv 0,\ Q^{\prime}(X)\equiv 0
implies
R^{\prime}(X)\not\equiv 0
.

where, as usual,
T(X)\equiv c
(resp.,
T(X)\not\equiv c
) means that
T(X)=0
, for all
X
(resp.,
T(X)\neq c
, for some
X
).

Thus, our main result can be stated as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(P(X),Q(X),R(X))
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-admissible triple of polynomials. If
\xi
is a
\nu
-Liouville number such that
\nu>6\deg R
, then

P(\xi)^{Q(\xi)^{R(\xi)}}

is a transcendental number.

Remark 1
.

As discussed before, by supposing that the function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
, defined by
f(X):=P(X)^{Q(X)^{R(X)}}
is well-defined and non-constant in some open interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, then
f(\xi)
is transcendental, for any
\xi
belonging to an uncountable set of Liouville numbers. However, there is no information on the approximation structure of this set. Thus, our Theorem
1.2
provides such a structure that depends only on the degree of
R(X)
.

As immediate consequences, we have

Corollary 1.1
.

If
\xi
is an
\infty
-Liouville number, then
P(\xi)^{Q(\xi)^{R(\xi)}}
is a transcendental number, for any non-constant polynomials
P(X),Q(X),R(X)\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
.

Corollary 1.2
.

If
\xi\in\mathbb{L}_{\text{ultra}}
, then
\xi^{\xi^{\xi}}
is a transcendental number.

Furthermore, by exploring the admissibility conditions, we obtain

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\nu>6
be a real number. Then, for any
\nu
-Liouville number
\xi
and for any integer
n\geq 2
, all the numbers

n^{n^{\xi}},\ n^{\xi^{\xi}},\ n^{\xi^{n}},\ \xi^{n^{\xi}},\xi^{\xi^{n}}

are transcendental.

The proofs presented here combine some algebraic and analytic tools together with a bit of Diophantine approximation and lower bounds for linear forms in logarithms.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

For a given
\nu>0
, let
(s_{k})_{k}
be the sequence defined by
s_{1}=4
and
s_{k+1}=\lfloor 10^{\nu s_{k}}\rfloor s_{k}+1
, for
k\geq 1
. We claim that the number
\xi:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}10^{-s_{n}}
is a
\nu
-Liouville number.

In order to prove that, we start by defining coprime integers
A_{k}:=\sum_{n=1}^{k}10^{s_{k}-s_{n}}
and
B_{k}:=10^{s_{k}}
. Since
A_{k}/B_{k}
is a truncation of the series which defines
\xi
, we get

(1)

\absolutevalue{\xi-\frac{A_k}{B_k}}=\sum_{n\geq k+1}10^{-s_{n}}<\frac{2}{10^{s_{k+1}}}=\frac{2}{B_{k}^{s_{k+1}/s_{k}}}<\frac{1}{B_{k}^{\lfloor 10^{\nu s_{k}}s_{k}\rfloor}}.

Note that
\lfloor 10^{\nu s_{k}}\rfloor/B_{k}^{\nu}=\lfloor B_{k}^{\nu}s_{k}\rfloor/B_{k}^{\nu}
tends to infinite as
k\to\infty
yielding that
\xi
belongs to
\mathbb{L}^{(\tau)}
, for some
\tau\geq\nu
. To finish the proof, it suffices to show that
\xi
is not a
(\nu+\epsilon)
-Liouville number, for all
\epsilon>0
. Indeed, by (
1
), one has

\absolutevalue{\xi-\frac{A_k}{B_k}}<\frac{1}{B_{k}^{\lfloor 10^{\nu s_{k}}s_{k}\rfloor}}<\frac{1}{2B_{k}^{2}},

where we used that
\lfloor 10^{\nu s_{k}}s_{k}\rfloor\geq 10^{\nu s_{k}}s_{k}-1\geq s_{k}-1\geq 3
. Hence, a criterion due to Legendre yields that
A_{k}/B_{k}
is a convergent, say
p_{r(k)}/q_{r(k)}
, of the continued fraction of
\xi=[b_{0};b_{1},b_{2},\ldots]
. Note that
q_{r(k)}=B_{k}=10^{s_{k}}
. Also, for
r(k-1)<m\leq r(k)
, it holds that

b_{m}\leq\displaystyle\prod_{i=r(k-1)+1}^{r(k)}b_{i}<\dfrac{q_{r(k)}}{q_{r(k-1)}}=q_{r(k-1)}^{-1+s_{k}/s_{k-1}}\leq q_{m-1}^{-1+s_{k}/s_{k-1}}.

Thus, for any convergent
p_{m}/q_{m}
of the continued fraction of
\xi
, we have

(2)

\left|\xi-\dfrac{p_{m}}{q_{m}}\right|>\frac{1}{3b_{m+1}q_{m}^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{q_{m}^{1+s_{k+1}/s_{k}}}.

Aiming for a contradiction, suppose that
\xi\in\mathbb{L}^{(\nu+\epsilon)}
, then there exists infinitely many rational numbers
c_{i}/d_{i}
, with
d_{i}\geq 2
and a sequence
(\theta_{i})_{i}
, with
\theta_{i}\geq 3
, such that

(3)

\left|\xi-\dfrac{c_{i}}{d_{i}}\right|<\dfrac{1}{d_{i}^{\theta_{i}}},\ \forall i,

where
\lim_{i\to\infty}\theta_{i}/d_{i}^{\nu+\epsilon}=\infty
. Since
1/d_{i}^{\theta_{i}}\leq 1/(2d_{i}^{2})
, then
c_{i}/d_{i}
must be a convergent of the continued fraction of
\xi
, say
p_{m(i)}/q_{m(i)}
, where
r(k)<m(i)\leq r(k+1)
, for some
k
(note that
d_{i}=q_{m(i)}
). Therefore, by combining (
2
) and (
3
), we arrive at

\frac{1}{3q_{m(i)}^{s_{k+1}/s_{k}}}<\left|\xi-\dfrac{p_{m(i)}}{q_{m(i)}}\right|<\dfrac{1}{q_{m(i)}^{\theta_{i}}}

and so
\theta_{i}<2s_{k+1}/s_{k}
. By reordering indexes if necessary, we may suppose that
i\mapsto m(i)
and
k\mapsto r(k)
are increasing functions (thus
i
tends to infinity if and only if
k
does as well). By dividing both sides of the previous inequality by
d_{i}^{\nu+\epsilon}
, we obtain

\frac{\theta_{i}}{d_{i}^{\nu+\epsilon}}<\frac{2\cdot 10^{\nu s_{k}}s_{k}}{10^{s_{k}(\nu+\epsilon)}}=\frac{2s_{k}}{10^{\epsilon s_{k}}},

where we used that
d_{i}=q_{m(i)}>q_{r(k)}=10^{s_{k}}
. However, the inequality
\theta_{i}/d_{i}^{\nu+\epsilon}<2s_{k}/10^{\epsilon s_{k}}
leads to a contradiction, since its left-hand side tends to infinity while its right-hand side tends to zero as
i
and so
k
tends to
\infty
. Therefore,
\xi
is a
\nu
-Liouville number as desired. An easy modification of this proof shows that
\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}10^{-s_{n}}\in\mathbb{L}^{(\nu)}
, for any choice of
a_{n}\in\{1,2\}
. Hence, there exist uncountably many
\nu
-Liouville numbers, which completes the proof.∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

3.1.
Auxiliary results

We start by stating two ingredients for the proof (in all that follows, we use the notation
[a,b]=\{a,a+1,\ldots,b\}
, for integers
a<b
).

The first one is due to Íçen
[
6
]
, which provides an inequality relating the
height
of algebraic numbers (the height of
\gamma\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, denoted by
H(\gamma)
is the maximum of the absolute values of the coefficients of the minimal polynomial of
\gamma
(over
\mathbb{Z}
)).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
be algebraic numbers belonging to a
g
-degree number field
K
, and let
F(y,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be a polynomial with integer coefficients and with degree
d>1
in the variable
y
. If
\eta
is an algebraic number such that
F(\eta,\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})=0
, then the degree of
\eta
is at most
dg
, and

(4)

H(\eta)\leq 3^{2dg+(l_{1}+\cdots+l_{k})g}\cdot H^{g}\cdot H(\alpha_{1})^{l_{1}g}\cdots H(\alpha_{n})^{l_{n}g},

where
H
is the maximum of the absolute values of the coefficients of
F
(the height of
F
),
l_{i}
is the degree of
F
in the variable
x_{i}
, for
i\in[1,n]
.

Also, the lower bounds for linear form in logarithms
à la Baker
lie at the heart of our approach. Among the many extensions (and variations) of the original Baker’s bounds, we choose to use the lower bound due to Mignotte and Waldschmidt (see Remark after Theorem in
[
18
]
), which is the most convenient for our purpose. For the sake of preciseness, we state it as a lemma:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\beta,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
be nonzero algebraic numbers and let
D=[\mathbb{Q}(\beta,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}):\mathbb{Q}]
. Set
A_{j}\geq H(\alpha_{j})
(
j=1,2
) and
B\geq\max\{H(\beta),e^{D}\}
. If
\Lambda=\beta\log\alpha_{1}-\log\alpha_{2}
is not zero, then

|\Lambda|>\exp\left(-5\cdot 10^{10}D^{4}(\log A_{1})(\log A_{2})T^{2}\right),

where
T:=\log B+\log\log A_{1}+\log\log A_{2}+\log D
.

Throughout this text,
\log z
denotes the main value of the complex logarithm function.

In the next section, each given inequality holds for all sufficiently large
k
(unless stated otherwise), which makes valid all the previous ones.

3.2.
The proof

First, we note that if
R(X)\equiv 0
, then
P(\xi)^{Q(\xi)^{R(\xi)}}=P(\xi)
is transcendental, for all
\xi\in\mathbb{L}^{(\nu)}
(since a non-constant polynomial takes the set of transcendental numbers into itself).

Aiming for a contradiction, suppose that
P(\xi)^{Q(\xi)^{R(\xi)}}
is an algebraic number, say
\gamma
. In particular,
Q(\xi)^{R(\xi)}\log P(\xi)=\log\gamma
(here we used that
\gamma\neq 0
, since
P(\xi)\neq 0
, because
\xi\not\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
). In what follows,
m,n
and
r
denote the degree of the polynomials
P(x),Q(x)
and
R(x)
, respectively.

Since
\xi
is a
\nu
-Liouville number, then there exist infinitely many rational numbers
p_{k}/q_{k}
(with
q_{k}>1
) and an increasing sequence
(\omega_{k})_{k}
belonging to
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that

(5)

0<\absolutevalue{\xi- \frac{p_k}{q_k}}<\frac{1}{q_{k}^{\omega_{k}}},

for all
k\geq 1
, where
\lim_{k\to\infty}\omega_{k}/q_{k}^{\nu}=\infty
. We may suppose (by reordering the indexes if necessary) that
p_{k}/q_{k}\in\Omega:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:P(z)Q(z)R(z)\neq 0\ \mbox{and}\ P(z)\neq 1\}
, for all
k\geq 1
. Now, we claim

\Lambda_{k}:=Q(p_{k}/q_{k})^{R(p_{k}/q_{k})}\log P(p_{k}/q_{k})-\log{\gamma}

is nonzero for all sufficiently large
k
. In fact, on the contrary, i.e., if
\Lambda_{k}=0
for all
k
belonging to an infinite set of positive integers, say
N^{\prime}
, then the function
h:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
given by
h(z):=Q(z)^{R(z)}\log P(z)-\log\gamma
vanishes in the set
\{p_{k}/q_{k}:k\in N^{\prime}\}
which has an accumulation point (namely
\xi
). By the Identity Theorem for Analytic Functions, one has that
h(z)=0
, for all
z\in\Omega
, which implies that the function
\tilde{h}(z):=h(z)+\log\gamma
is the constant function. On the other hand, either
\Omega^{+}:=\{z\in\Omega\cap\mathbb{Z}_{>0}:R(z)\geq 1\}
or
\Omega^{-}:=\{z\in\Omega\cap\mathbb{Z}_{>0}:R(z)\leq-1\}
must be an infinite set (actually, only one of them has this property). If
\Omega^{+}
is infinite, we use that

|\tilde{h}(z)|\geq|Q(z)|^{R(z)}(\log|P(z)|+i\Arg(P(z)))\geq|Q(z)|(\log|P(z)|-2\pi),

to infer that
|\tilde{h}(z)|
tends to infinity as
z\to\infty
(
z\in\Omega^{+}
). This contradicts the fact that
\tilde{h}(z)
is constant. For the case in which
\Omega^{-}
is an infinite set, we use that
|P(z)|<|z|^{m+1}
and
|Q(z)|>(|c|/2)|z|^{n}
for all sufficiently large
z\in\Omega^{-}
(here
c
is the leading coefficient of
Q(z)
). Thus

|\tilde{h}(z)|\leq\frac{1}{|Q(z)|^{-R(z)}}|\log P(z)|\leq\dfrac{(m+1)\log z+2\pi}{|c/2|z^{n}}

yielding that
|\tilde{h}(z)|
tends to
0
as
z\to\infty
(
z\in\Omega^{-}
). Therefore
\tilde{h}(z)
must be the zero function which is absurd, since
\tilde{h}(z)\neq 0
, for all
z\in\Omega
.

In conclusion,
\Lambda_{k}
is nonzero, for all sufficiently large
k
. Hence, all hypotheses of Lemma
3.2
are fulfilled for
\Lambda_{k}
and therefore

(6)

\absolutevalue{\Lambda_k}>\exp(-5\cdot 10^{10}D_k^4(\log A_{1,k})(\log A_{2,k})T_k^2),

where
A_{1,k}\geq H(P(p_{k}/q_{k})),A_{2,k}:=H(\gamma)
,
T_{k}\geq\log B_{k}+\log\log A_{1,k}+\log\log A_{2,k}+\log D_{k}
(and
B_{k}=H(Q(p_{k}/q_{k})^{R(p_{k}/q_{k})})
). Now, we need to make some estimates in order to choose
A_{1,k},T_{k}
, and
D_{k}
.

First, in Lemma
3.1
, set
F(Y,X):=Y-P(X)\in\mathbb{Z}[X,Y]
, then
F(P(p_{k}/q_{k}),p_{k}/q_{k})=0
and so, by that lemma, one deduces that
H(P(p_{k}/q_{k}))\leq 3^{m+2}(1+|\xi|)^{m}H(P)q_{k}^{m}
. Thus, we can take
A_{k}:=c_{0}q_{k}^{m}
, where
c_{0}=3^{m+2}(1+|\xi|)^{m}H(P)
.

Next, by setting
\theta_{k}:=q_{k}^{r}R(p_{k}/q_{k})
(which is an integer), we note that
Q(p_{k}/q_{k})^{R(p_{k}/q_{k})}
is a root of the polynomial
q_{k}^{n\theta_{k}}X^{q_{k}^{r}}-(q_{k}^{n}Q(p_{k}/q_{k}))^{\theta_{k}}\in\mathbb{Q}[X]
and so it has degree at most
q_{k}^{r}
. Thus, we can choose
D_{k}:=q_{k}^{r}
(since
P(p_{k}/q_{k})\in\mathbb{Q}
). Furthermore, one of the polynomials
q_{k}^{n\theta_{k}}X^{q_{k}^{r}}-(q_{k}^{n}Q(p_{k}/q_{k}))^{\theta_{k}}
and
(q_{k}^{n}Q(p_{k}/q_{k}))^{\theta_{k}}X^{q_{k}^{r}}-q_{k}^{n\theta_{k}}
has integers coefficients (depending on the sign of
R(p_{k}/q_{k})
). Thus, this polynomial is divisible by the minimal polynomial of
Q(p_{k}/q_{k})^{R(p_{k}/q_{k})}
(over
\mathbb{Z}
). In particular, after some manipulations, we arrive at

H(Q(p_{k}/q_{k})^{R(p_{k}/q_{k})})\leq q_{k}^{c_{1}q_{k}^{r}},

where
c_{1}:=3n(1+|\xi|)^{mnr}\max\{H(P),H(Q),H(R)\}
. Thus, we can choose
B_{k}:=q_{k}^{c_{1}q_{k}^{r}}
and
T_{k}:=c_{2}q_{k}^{r}\log q_{k}
(where
c_{2}:=c_{1}+\log c_{0}+m+\log H(\gamma)+r
).

By using these estimates in (
6
), we deduce (after some straightforward calculations) that

(7)

\absolutevalue{\Lambda_k}>\exp(-c_3q_k^{6r}(\log q_k)^3),

where
c_{3}:=5\cdot 10^{10}mc_{2}^{2}(\log c_{0})(\log H(\gamma))
.

On the other hand, we have

(8)

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\Lambda_k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{Q(\pq)^{R(\pq)}\log P(\pq)-Q(\xi)^{R(\xi)}\log P(\xi)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\log P(\pq)}\absolutevalue{Q(\xi)^{R(\xi)}-Q(\pq)^{R(\pq)}}

\displaystyle+\absolutevalue{Q(\xi)^{R(\xi)}}\absolutevalue{\log P(\xi)-\log P(\pq)}

where we used that
Q(\xi)^{R(\xi)}\log P(\xi)=\log\gamma
together with the triangle inequality. It is well-known that for a holomorphic function
\phi:U\to\mathbb{C}
, if
z_{1},z_{2}\in U
are such that the the line segment with endpoints
z_{1},z_{2}
, denoted by,
[z_{1},z_{2}]
, belongs to
U
, then
|\phi(z_{1})-\phi(z_{2})|\leq|z_{1}-z_{2}|\cdot\max\{|\phi^{\prime}(\zeta)|:\zeta\in[z_{1},z_{2}]\}
. By using this fact for the holomorphic functions
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
and
g:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
given by

f(z):=\log P(z)
and
g(z):=Q(z)^{R(z)}
,

we obtain

\absolutevalue{\log P(\xi)-\log P(\pq)}\leq c_{4}\absolutevalue{\xi- \frac{p_k}{q_k}}
and
\absolutevalue{\log P(\xi)-\log P(\pq)}\leq c_{5}\absolutevalue{\xi- \frac{p_k}{q_k}}
,

where
c_{4}:=\sup_{\zeta\in I_{k}}|f^{\prime}(\zeta)|
and
c_{5}:=\sup_{\zeta\in I_{k}}|g^{\prime}(\zeta)|
(here
I_{k}
is the closed interval with endpoints
\xi
and
p_{k}/q_{k}
). So, we get from (
8
) that

(9)

\absolutevalue{\Lambda_k}<c_{6}\absolutevalue{\xi- \frac{p_k}{q_k}}<\dfrac{c_{6}}{q_{k}^{\omega_{k}}},

for all sufficiently large
k
(say
k\geq\kappa_{1}
), where
c_{6}:=|\log P(\xi)|+|Q(\xi)^{R(\xi)}|+c_{4}+c_{5}+1
.

Now, by combining (
7
) and (
9
), we obtain

c_{6}q_{k}^{-\omega_{k}}>\exp(-c_3q_k^{6r}(\log q_k)^3)

yielding

\omega_{k}<2c_{3}q_{k}^{6r}(\log q_{k})^{2}

for all sufficiently large
k
. However, there exist
\epsilon>0
such that
\nu=6r+\epsilon
and so the last inequality can be rewritten as

\dfrac{\omega_{k}}{q_{k}^{6r+\epsilon}}<2c_{3}\dfrac{(\log q_{k})^{2}}{q_{k}^{\epsilon}}

This leads to an absurd, since the left-hand side of the previous inequality tends to infinity, while its right-hand side tends to zero when
k\to\infty
. In conclusion, the number
P(\xi)^{Q(\xi)^{R(\xi)}}
is transcendental and the proof is complete.
∎

4.
Further comments, results, and a conjecture

We close this article by addressing a discussion on some more technical results and possible problems for further research. In what follows, the implied constants in
\ll
and
\gg
do not depend on
k
.

First, we point out that when
P(X)
is a constant, it can be replaced by some classes of transcendental numbers. Indeed, let us define the set
\mathcal{T}
by

\mathcal{T}:=\{e^{\beta_{0}}\alpha_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots\alpha_{n}^{\beta_{n}}:n\geq 1\ \mbox{and}\ (\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{n},\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{2n+1}\}

Note that the preceding set contains all algebraic numbers (case
(n,\beta_{0},\beta_{1})=(1,0,1)
) as well as the families of transcendental numbers proved by Hermite and Lindemann (case
(n,\alpha_{1})=(1,1)
), Gelfond and Schneider (case
(n,\beta_{0})=(1,0)
) and Baker (
n>1
, under some mild hypotheses).

We have the following result:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(P(X),Q(X),R(X))
be a
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-admissible triple of polynomials with
R(X)\in\mathbb{Q}[X]
. If
\zeta\in\mathcal{T}
, then the numbers

\zeta^{Q(\xi)^{R(\xi)}}
and
P(\xi)^{Q(\xi)^{R(\xi)}}

do not belong to
\mathcal{T}
, for all
\xi\in\mathbb{L}^{(\infty)}
. In particular,
\xi^{\alpha},\xi^{\xi^{r}},e^{\xi^{\xi}}
and
\beta^{\alpha\xi^{\xi}}
are transcendental numbers, whenever
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\backslash\{0\}
,
\beta\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\backslash\{0,1\},r\in\mathbb{Q}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{L}_{\text{ultra}}
.

Before the proof, we state the following lower bound for linear forms due to Baker (see
[
2
]
). For the sake of preciseness, we state it as a lemma:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
n>1
be an integer and let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},\beta_{0},\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}
be nonzero algebraic numbers. Set
A=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\log(\max\{H(\a_i), 4\})
and
B=\max_{i\in[1,n]}\{H(\beta_{i}),4\}
. Let
D=[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{n}):\mathbb{Q}]
. Then,

|\beta_{0}+\beta_{1}\log\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\beta_{n}\log\alpha_{n}|>(AB)^{-(16nD)^{200n}A\log A},

provided that the previous linear form does not vanish.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

We shall proceed along the same lines as in the proof of Theorem
1.2
. In fact, striving for a contradiction, suppose the existence of
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in\mathcal{T}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{L}^{(\infty)}
such that
\zeta_{1}^{Q(\xi)^{R(\xi)}}=\zeta_{2}
. By proceeding along the same lines as before, we obtain that
|\Lambda_{k}|\ll q_{k}^{-\omega_{k}}
, where
\Lambda_{k}:=Q(p_{k}/q_{k})^{R(p_{k}/q_{k})}\log\zeta_{1}-\log\zeta_{2}
is a linear form in logarithms of algebraic numbers. By Lemma
4.1
, after some calculations, one has
|\Lambda_{k}|\gg\exp(-q_k^{O(1)})
. We then combine the upper and lower bounds for
|\Lambda|
to obtain
\omega_{k}\ll q_{k}^{O(1)}/\log q_{k}
which contradicts the fact that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\omega_{k}/q_{k}^{\nu}=\infty
, for all
\nu>0
(since
\xi
is an
\infty
-Liouville number).

For the transcendence of
P(\xi)^{Q(\xi)^{R(\xi)}}
, we simply mimic the same approach. The novelty here lies
in the fact that
P(X)
and
Q(X)
have algebraic coefficients. So, it suffices to prove that the degrees and heights of the numbers
P(p_{k}/q_{k})
and
\gamma_{k}:=Q(p_{k}/q_{k})^{R(p_{k}/q_{k})}
have the reasonable growth order compared to the case of integer coefficients (by reasonable, we mean that if the integer case is
\leq f(k)^{g(k)}
, then the algebraic one is
O(f(k)^{O(g(k))})
). To prove that, let
\mathbb{K}
be the
d
-degree number field which contains all the coefficients of
P(X)
and
Q(X)
. Clearly, the degree of
P(p_{k}/q_{k})
is at most
d
. By supposing, without loss of generality, that the integer
q_{k}^{r}R(p_{k}/q_{k})
is positive, then
\gamma_{k}^{q_{k}^{r}}
has degree at most
d
and so
\gamma_{k}
has degree
\leq dq_{k}^{r}
. For the heights, we shall use the following relations (which can be found, for instance, in
[
17
, p. 325]
): Let
y_{1},\ldots,y_{\ell}
be algebraic numbers of degree
m_{1},\ldots,m_{\ell}
, respectively. Then

•

(2^{m_{1}n}\sqrt{2m_{1}})^{-1}H(y_{1})^{n}\leq H(y_{1}^{n})\leq e^{O(n)}H(y_{1})^{n}
;

•

H(y_{1}y_{2})\ll(H(y_{1})H(y_{2}))^{m_{1}m_{2}}
;

•

H(y_{1}+\cdots+y_{\ell})\leq e^{O(\ell)}(H(y_{1})\cdots H(y_{\ell}))^{m_{1}\cdots m_{\ell}}
,

where the implied constants depend only on the degree of the algebraic numbers. Therefore, by using these estimates, it is straightforward to show that
H(P(p_{k}/q_{k}))\ll q_{k}^{m^{2}d^{m+2}}
and
H(\gamma_{k})\ll q_{k}^{O(q_{k}^{r})}
. Hence, we confirm that these estimates are reasonable (in our sense) which ensures that the condition of
\xi
being an
\infty
-Liouville number is enough to complete the proof (we leave the details to the reader).
∎

We now continue by recalling the following definitions:

Definition 4.1
.

Let
k\geq 1
be an integer. The power tower function of order
k
,
h_{k}(x)
, is defined for all real number
x>0
as

h_{k}(x)=x^{\,x^{\,\cdot^{\cdot^{\cdot^{x}}}}}
(
k
times)

For all
x\in[e^{-e},e^{1/e}]
,
h_{k}(x)
converges (as
k\to\infty
) to a function
h(x)
which is named as the infinite power tower function (see Eisenstein
[
7
]
). In other words,

h(x)=x^{\,x^{\,x^{\,\cdot^{\cdot^{\cdot}}}}}.

The previous definitions imply in the following functional equations

(10)

h(x)=x^{\,h(x)},\ h_{k+1}(x)=x^{h_{k}(x)}\ \mbox{and}\ h_{k+2}(x)=x^{x^{h_{k}(x)}}.

Remark 2
.

We remark that
h_{1}(\xi),h_{2}(\xi)
and
h_{3}(\xi)
are transcendental numbers whenever
\xi\in\mathbb{L}_{\text{ultra}}
(by
[
16
, Prop. 3.2]
and Corollary
1.2
).

Turning back to our problems, we point out that there are at least two directions in order to generalize the previous results, namely, by increasing the power tower of Liouville numbers or by replacing Liouville numbers by
U_{m}
-numbers (we refer to
[
3
]
for their definition). However, our argument is invalid to deal even with the transcendence of
h_{4}(\xi)
and
h_{3}(\zeta)
, where
\xi
is a Liouville number and
\zeta
is a
U_{m}
-number (with
m>1
). Indeed, in both cases, at least one of the coefficients of the linear forms will be
h_{3}(r)
and
h_{2}(\alpha)
, for non-integer rational numbers
r
and non-rational algebraic numbers
\alpha
. Since
h_{2}(r)\not\in\mathbb{Q}
, for any
r\in\mathbb{Q}\backslash\mathbb{Z}
, the Gelfond-Schneider theorem implies in the transcendence of both
h_{3}(r)
and
h_{2}(\alpha)
. Therefore, the linear form
\Lambda
will not have algebraic coefficients, so it is useless in the sense of Baker’s theory.

However, there are some consequences of Proposition
4.1
concerning the previous functions:

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
\xi
be a positive
\infty
-Liouville number. We have:

(i)

If
\xi\in[e^{-e},e^{1/e}]
, then
h(\xi)
is a transcendental number;

(ii)

For any
k\geq 1
, at least one of the numbers
h_{k}(\xi)
or
h_{k+1}(\xi)
is transcendental.

(iii)

If
h_{k}(\xi)
is a rational number, then both
h_{k+1}(\xi)
and
h_{k+2}(\xi)
are transcendental numbers.

Proof.

The proofs are direct combinations of Proposition
4.1
and (
10
). In fact, if
h(\xi)=\gamma\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\backslash\{0\}
, then
\xi^{\gamma}
is transcendental contradicting the fact that
\xi^{\gamma}=\gamma
(this proves (i)). For (ii), we use that if
h_{k}(\xi)=\gamma
is algebraic (and so nonzero), then
h_{k+1}(\xi)=\xi^{\gamma}
is transcendental. To prove (iii), if
h_{k}(\xi)=r
is a rational number, then
h_{k+1}(\xi)
is transcendental (by item (ii)) as well as
h_{k+2}(\xi)=\xi^{\xi^{r}}
.
∎

We finish by posing a conjecture, which we do believe to be true.

Conjecture 4.1
.

If
\xi
is an ultra-Liouville number, then
h_{k}(\xi)
is transcendental, for all
k\geq 1
.

A partial evidence to support this conjecture is that Corollary
4.1
(ii) implies in the existence of infinitely many transcendental numbers among
h_{4}(\xi),h_{5}(\xi),\ldots
whenever
\xi\in\mathbb{L}^{(\infty)}
.

Zeros of the Selberg zeta function for symmetric infinite area hyperbolic surfaces
Mark Pollicott, Polina Vytnova

Abstract
In the present paper we give a simple mathematical foundation for describing the zeros of the Selberg zeta functions Z X Z_{X} for certain very symmetric infinite area surfaces X X . For definiteness, we consider the case of three funneled surfaces. We show that the zeta function is a complex almost periodic function which can be approximated by complex trigonometric polynomials on large domains (in Theorem 4.2 ). As our main application, we provide an explanation of the striking empirical results of Borthwick [ 6 ] (in Theorem 1.5 ) in terms of convergence of the affinely scaled zero sets to standard curves 𝒞 \mathcal{C} . The approach of Ruelle provides an approximation of

geodesics on the

\tilde{r}_{n}\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}\qquad\tilde{r}_{n}(\xi)=\ell(\gamma_{\xi_{[n/2]}\xi_{[n/2]+1}}),

\gamma_{\xi}
is a closed geodesic corresponding to the cutting sequence

\xi

\gamma_{\xi_{[n/2]}\xi_{[n/2]+1}}

and

R_{\xi_{[n/2]+1}}
which lies on a longer segment passing consecutively through the

In particular, any geodesic is uniquely defined by its cutting sequence and
therefore if the word length

\ell(\gamma_{\xi})=\sum_{j=0}^{\omega(\gamma)-1}\tilde{r}_{n}(\sigma^{j}\xi);\mbox{
and }

(2.7)

\ell(\gamma_{\xi})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\omega(\gamma)-1}\tilde{r}_{n}(\sigma^{j}\xi),

(2.8)

\sigma(\xi_{n})=(\xi_{n+1})

We know that periodic sequences in

A^{n}_{i,j}=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{ if }\xi^{i}_{k+1}=\xi^{j}_{k};\mbox{ for }k=1,\ldots,n-1\\
0,&\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

(2.9)

We use the transition matrix
A^{n}
for the subshift to define a family of
matrices

whose elements depend on the length of geodesics segments corresponding to
transitions. Namely, we introduce a matrix-valued complex function

A\colon\mathbb{C}\to Mat(N,N)\qquad A_{i,j}(s)=\exp(-s\tilde{r}_{n}(\xi))\cdot A^{n}_{i,j},

Note that

The following fact is very useful and relatively well-known

[

Lemma 2.8
.

Using the notation introduced above the following
equality holds

\prod_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\gamma=\mbox{ \scriptsize primitive }}}{{\mbox{\scriptsize closed
geodesic}}}}\left(1-e^{-s\ell(\gamma)}\right)^{2}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\det\left(I_{N}-A^{2}(s)\right);

(2.11)

where
I_{N}
is the
N\times N
identity matrix.

Proof .
Observe that on the domain of convergence the right hand side

\displaystyle\log\left(\det\left(I_{N}-A^{2}(s)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j}\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits\left(A^{2j}(s)\right)=-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j}\sum_{\sigma^{2j}\xi=\xi}e^{-s(\tilde{r}_{n}(\xi)+\tilde{r}_{n}(\sigma\xi)+\cdots+\tilde{r}_{n}(\sigma^{2j-1}\xi))}

we may rewrite the latter term as

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{jm}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma^{2j}\xi=\xi}}{{\xi\mbox{ \scriptsize primitive}}}}e^{-sm(\tilde{r}_{n}(\xi)+\tilde{r}_{n}(\sigma\xi)+\cdots+\tilde{r}_{n}(\sigma^{2j-1}\xi))},

where the inner summation is taken over primitive fixed points for

\sigma^{2j}

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\{\xi,\sigma\xi,\sigma^{2}\xi,\cdots,\sigma^{2j-1}\xi\}}}{{\mbox{ \scriptsize primitive}}}}e^{-sm(\tilde{r}_{n}(\xi)+\tilde{r}_{n}(\sigma\xi)+\cdots+\tilde{r}_{n}(\sigma^{2j-1}\xi))},

where the inner summation is taken over primitive closed orbits
of period
2j

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\{\xi,\sigma\xi,\sigma^{2}\xi,\cdots,\sigma^{2j-1}\xi\}}}{{\mbox{ \scriptsize primitive}}}}\log\left(1-e^{-s(\tilde{r}_{n}(\xi)+\tilde{r}_{n}(\sigma\xi)+\cdots+\tilde{r}_{n}(\sigma^{2j-1}\xi))}\right),

\displaystyle=-2\log\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\prod_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\{\xi,\sigma\xi,\sigma^{2}\xi,\cdots,\sigma^{2j-1}\xi\}}}{{\mbox{ \scriptsize primitive}}}}\left(1-e^{-s(\tilde{r}_{n}(\xi)+\tilde{r}_{n}(\sigma\xi)+\cdots+\tilde{r}_{n}(\sigma^{2j-1}\xi))}\right).

Using (
2.8
) and swapping the limits, we get the result, since
there is a bijection between oriented primitive closed geodesics and primitive
periodic cutting sequences.

\blacksquare

We apply the method described in the previous paragraph in a simple case

In other words, we will be computing the length of segments of closed geodsics

In order to compute

The result follows by expanding the both sides in

\blacksquare

Now we are ready to describe

Lemma 2.10

Assume that

Proof .
We need to estimate the length of the shortest of the segments of closed geodesics
passing through

,
\beta_{\xi_{2}}

\beta_{\xi_{3}}

Error estimates for approximations of the function

[

3.1
Nuclear Operators

The convergence of the series (
2.2
) in Theorem
2.4
will follow from
estimates of Ruelle
[
28
]
, after Grothendieck
[
11
]
. We summarize below the general theory.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a Banach space.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that a linear operator
T:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}
is nuclear
if there exist for each
n\geq 1

1.

w_{n}\in\mathcal{B}
, with
\|w_{n}\|_{\mathcal{B}}=1
;

2.

\nu_{n}\in\mathcal{B}^{*}
, with
\|\nu_{n}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{*}}=1
;

3.

\lambda_{n}\in\mathord{\mathbb{R}}
, with
0<\lambda<1
,
C>0
satisfying
|\lambda_{n}|\leq C\lambda^{n}

such that

Tf=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}w_{n}\nu_{n}(f).

(3.1)

Lemma 3.2

(after Grothendieck)
.

A nuclear operator on a Banach space is trace class, and we can write

\det(I-zT)=\exp\Bigl(-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n}\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits T^{n}\Bigr).

where the infinite series on the right hand side converges in a small disk

|z|<\varepsilon
and the equality between analytic continuations then holds
on
\mathbb{C}
.

As the left hand side is an analytic function we may expand it in a power series
at
z=0
:

\det(I-zT)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n},

where

a_{n}=\sum\limits_{j_{1}<\cdots<j_{n}}\det\left([\nu_{j_{k}}(w_{j_{l}})]_{k,l=1}^{n}\right)\lambda_{j_{1}}\ldots\lambda_{j_{n}}.

(3.2)

Applying estimates of Grothendieck and Ruelle, we obtain an explicit bound.

|a_{n}|\leq C^{n}n^{n/2}\lambda^{n(n+1)/2},

(3.3)

where
n^{n/2}
bounds the supremum
norm of the matrix
6
6

6

We follow Ruelle in including the term
n^{n/2}
although
this can be improved upon by looking at Hilbert spaces of analytic functions.
For instance, O. Bandtlow and O. Jenkinson
[
2
]
have shown
that we can supress
n^{n/2}
by working with Hardy spaces, but then

\lambda
would be different, too.

[\nu_{j_{k}}(w_{j_{l}})]_{k,l=1}^{n}
and
C\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant.

For completeness, in this Appendix we describe a slight strengthening of a

Notation C.1
.

A compact part of the critical strip of width

The sets

Abstract
. Here α , β \alpha,\beta are positive constants and Λ s = ( − Δ ) s / 2 \Lambda^{s}=(-\Delta)^{s/2} is the fractional Laplacian of order s s . We establish global existence of weak solutions of the Stokes-Magneto system for any initial data in L 2 L_{2} when α \alpha , β \beta satisfy 1 / 2 < α < ( d + 1 ) / 2 1/2<\alpha<(d+1)/2 , β > 0 \beta>0 , and min ⁡ { α + β , 2 ​ α + β − 1 } > d / 2 \min\{\alpha+\beta,2\alpha+\beta-1\}>d/2 . 1.
Introduction

For several decades, many authors have studied linear and nonlinear equations with fractional diffusions from a mathematical point of view and applied them
to diverse fields such as biology
[
5
]
, financial mathematics
[
2
]
, and probability
[
30
]
. Particularly important models from fluid mechanics are generalized Navier-Stokes equations (see e.g
[
24
,
25
,
12
,
33
]
) and surface quasi-geographic equations (see e.g.
[
11
,
9
]
).

In this paper, we consider the following initial value problem for a Stokes-Magneto system with fractional diffusions in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \nu\Lambda^{2\alpha}\bm{u}+\nabla{p_{*}}&=(\bm{b}\cdot\nabla)\bm{b}&&\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty),\\
\partial_{t}\bm{b}+\eta\Lambda^{2\beta}\bm{b}+(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla)\bm{b}&=(\bm{b}\cdot\nabla)\bm{u}&&\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty),\\
\Div\bm{u}=\Div\bm{b}&=0&&\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty),\\
\bm{b}(\cdot,0)&=\bm{b}_{0}&&\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here
\bm{u}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the velocity field,
\bm{b}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the magnetic field, and
{p_{*}}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
denotes the total pressure:
{p_{*}}=p+\frac{1}{2}|\bm{b}|^{2}
with
p
being the pressure on the fluid. The positive constants
\nu
and
\eta
stand for the viscosity constant and the magnetic diffusivity, respectively. For
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\Lambda^{s}=(-\Delta)^{s/2}
denotes the fractional Laplacian
of order
s
(see Section 2 for more details).

Our model (
1.1
) was motivated by Moffatt
[
27
,
28
]
who proposed the magnetic relaxation method to construct magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) equilibria from a given magnetic field
\bm{b}_{0}
. More precisely, Moffatt
[
27
]
first suggested the method of magnetic relaxation to construct a magnetic equilibrium from a solution of ideal MHD equations by letting
t\rightarrow\infty
. Later, in 2009, Moffatt suggested another possible way to construct an MHD equilibrium by introducing the system (
1.1
) with
\alpha=1
and
\eta=0
. See Beekie-Friedlander-Vicol
[
4
]
, Constantin-Pasqualotto
[
13
]
, and references therein for further motivation of magnetic relaxation.

In comparison to (
1.1
), we recall the generalized MHD system:

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\bm{u}+\nu\Lambda^{2\alpha}\bm{u}+(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla)\bm{u}+\nabla{p_{*}}&=(\bm{b}\cdot\nabla)\bm{b}&&\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty),\\
\partial_{t}\bm{b}+\eta\Lambda^{2\beta}\bm{b}+(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla)\bm{b}&=(\bm{b}\cdot\nabla)\bm{u}&&\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty),\\
\Div\bm{u}=\Div\bm{b}&=0&&\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty),\\
\bm{u}(\cdot,0)=\bm{u}_{0},\quad\bm{b}(\cdot,0)&=\bm{b}_{0}&&\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{aligned}\right.

When
\nu,\eta>0
and
\alpha=\beta=1
, Duvaut-Lions
[
15
]
proved global existence of weak solutions of (
1.2
) with
L_{2}
-initial data. Later, Wu
[
37
]
extended this result to arbitrary
\alpha,\beta>0
and also proved existence of global classical solutions for smooth initial data when
\alpha,\beta
satisfy
\alpha,\beta\geq d/4+1/2
. The existence results in
[
37
]
were later improved by the same author
[
38
]
when
\alpha\geq d/4+1/2
,
\beta>0
, and
\alpha+\beta\geq d/2+1
. In addition to these results, many authors proved global regularity of classical solutions of (
1.2
) in two-dimensional spaces under various hypotheses on
\alpha
and
\beta
; see Yamazaki
[
39
]
and references therein. On the other hand, when
\nu=0
and
\eta>0
, Kozono
[
23
]
proved local existence and uniqueness of classical solutions of (
1.2
) when
\beta=1
. This result was extended to any
\beta>0
by Wu
[
38
]
for sufficiently smooth initial data. Recently, Fefferman et.al
[
16
,
17
]
established local existence of strong solutions of (
1.2
) when
\nu>0
,
\eta=0
, and
\alpha=1
, which was extended by Kim-Zhou
[
22
]
to general
\alpha
.

In contrast to the generalized MHD system, there are few results for the Stokes-Magneto system with or without fractional diffusions. When
\nu,\eta>0
,
\alpha=\beta=1
, and
d=2,3
, McCormick-Robinson-Rodrigo
[
26
]
proved global existence of weak solutions of (
1.1
). For the two-dimensional case, they also proved uniqueness and regularity of weak solutions. Recently, Ji-Tan
[
21
]
proved global existence of strong solutions of (
1.1
) when
d=3
,
\alpha=1
, and
\beta\geq 3/2
. On the other hand, when
\nu>0
,
\eta=0
, and
\alpha=0
, Brenier
[
7
]
proved global existence of dissipative weak solutions on the two-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. When
\nu>0
,
\eta=0
, and
\alpha=1
, Fefferman et.al.
[
16
]
proved local existence and uniqueness of strong solutions of (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
. Recently, when
\nu>0
,
\eta=0
, and
\alpha>d/2+1
, Beekie-Friedlander-Vicol
[
4
]
established global existence of strong solutions of (
1.1
) on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
,
d=2,3
. Moreover, they also investigated 2D stability and 3D instability as well as the long-time behavior of solutions.

In this paper, we establish global existence and uniqueness of weak solutions of the Stokes-Magneto system (
1.1
) with fractional diffusions. To elaborate a motivation of our notion of weak solutions, we multiply the first equation in (
1.1
) by
\bm{u}
and the second equation by
\bm{b}
, and then integrate the resulting equations on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Using the divergence-free condition on
\bm{u}
and
\bm{b}
, we have

\nu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\Lambda^{\alpha}\bm{u}|^{2}\,d{x}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\bm{b}\otimes\bm{b}):\nabla\bm{u}\,d{x}

and

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\bm{b}|^{2}\,d{x}+\eta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\Lambda^{\beta}\bm{b}|^{2}\,d{x}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\bm{b}\cdot\nabla)\bm{u}\cdot\bm{b}\,d{x}.

Adding these two identities and integrating it in time, we derive the energy identity

(1.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\bm{b}(t)|^{2}\,d{x}+2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\nu|\Lambda^{\alpha}\bm{u}(s)|^{2}+\eta|{\Lambda^{\beta}\bm{b}(s)}|^{2}\right)\,d{x}\,d{s}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\bm{b}_{0}|^{2}\,d{x}

for all
t\geq 0
. Furthermore, it will be shown in Subsection
3.2
that if
1/2<\alpha<(d+1)/2
, then
\bm{u}
is given by

(1.4)

\bm{u}=\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}*(\bm{b}\otimes\bm{b})

where
\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}
is a matrix-valued function satisfying

|\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}(x)|\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{d+1-2\alpha}}

for some constant
C
depending only on
d
and
\alpha
. By Young’s convolution inequality in weak spaces (Lemma
2.5
), we deduce that

(1.5)

\|{\bm{u}(t)}\|_{L_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}}\leq C\|{\bm{b}(t)\otimes\bm{b}(t)}\|_{L_{1}}\leq C\|{\bm{b}(t)}\|_{L_{2}}^{2}

for some constant
C=C(d,\alpha)
. Here
L_{p}
and
L_{p,q}
denote the Lebesgue spaces and Lorentz spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, respectively, where
1\leq p<\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
. For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\dot{H}^{s}
be the homogeneous Sobolev spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see Section
2
for details). Then motivated by (
1.3
) and (
1.5
), we define weak solutions of (
1.1
) as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
1/2<\alpha<(d+1)/2
and
0<\beta<\infty
. Suppose that
\bm{b}_{0}\in L_{2}
,
{\rm div}\,\bm{b}_{0}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
0<T\leq\infty
. Then a pair
(\bm{u},\bm{b})
of vector fields satisfying

\displaystyle\bm{u}

\displaystyle\in L_{\infty}(0,T;L_{{d}/{(d+1-2\alpha)},\infty})\cap L_{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{\alpha}),

\displaystyle\bm{b}

\displaystyle\in L_{\infty}(0,T;L_{2})\cap L_{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{\beta})

is called a
weak solution
of (
1.1
) in
[0,T)
if the following are satisfied:

(1)

For all
\Phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\Div\Phi=0
,

(1.6)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nu\Lambda^{\alpha}\bm{u}(t)\cdot\Lambda^{\alpha}\Phi\,d{x}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{b}(t)\otimes\bm{b}(t):\nabla\Phi\,d{x}

for almost all
t\in(0,T)
.

(2)

For all
\Phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{b}\cdot(-\partial_{t}\Phi+\eta\Lambda^{2\beta}\Phi)\,d{x}dt

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\bm{u}\otimes\bm{b}-\bm{b}\otimes\bm{u}):\nabla\Phi\,d{x}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{b}_{0}\cdot\Phi(0)\,dx.

(3)

For all
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}(t)\cdot\nabla\psi\,d{x}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{b}(t)\cdot\nabla\psi\,d{x}=0

for almost all
t\in(0,T)
.

A weak solution of (
1.1
) in
[0,\infty)
will be called a
global weak solution
.

Now we are ready to present the main results of the paper. We first establish existence of global weak solutions of (
1.1
) under a suitable assumption on
\alpha
and
\beta
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
satisfy

\frac{1}{2}<\alpha<\frac{d+1}{2},\quad\beta>0,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\min\{\alpha+\beta,2\alpha+\beta-1\}>\frac{d}{2}.

Then for any
\bm{b}_{0}\in L_{2}
with
{\rm div}\,\bm{b}_{0}=0
, there exists at least one global weak solution of (
1.1
).

Next, we prove the time-continuity, energy identity, and uniqueness of weak solutions of (
1.1
) for larger values of
\alpha
and
\beta
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
satisfy

\frac{1}{2}<\alpha<\frac{d+1}{2},\quad\beta\geq 1,\quad\text{and}\quad\min\{\alpha+\beta,2\alpha+\beta-1\}\geq\frac{d}{2}+1.

Then for any
\bm{b}_{0}\in L_{2}
with
{\rm div}\,\bm{b}_{0}=0
, there exists a unique global weak solution
(\bm{u},\bm{b})
of (
1.1
). Moreover, the solution
(\bm{u},\bm{b})
is continuous in
L_{{d}/{(d+1-2\alpha)},\infty}\times L_{2}
and satisfies the energy identity (
1.3
) for any
t\geq 0
.

Remark
.

(a) Assume that
\alpha=1
and
\beta>d/2-1
. Then it follows from Theorem
1.2
that for any
\bm{b}_{0}\in L_{2}
with
{\rm div}\,\bm{b}_{0}=0
, there exists a global weak solution
(\bm{u},\bm{b})
of (
1.1
).
This result extends the existence result of McCormick-Robinson-Rodrigo
[
26
]
who considered the case when
d=2,3
and
\beta=1
.
Moreover, by Theorem
1.3
, weak solutions of (
1.1
) are unique if
\beta\geq d/2
. Hence it follows from the existence theorem for strong solutions due to Ji-Tan
[
21
]
that if
d=3
and
\bm{b}_{0}\in H^{1}
, then
(\bm{u},\bm{b})
satisfies the following strong regularity properties:

\bm{u}\in C([0,T];H^{3/2})\cap L_{2}(0,T;H^{2}),\quad\bm{b}\in C([0,T];H^{1})\cap L_{2}(0,T;H^{1+\beta})

for any
T<\infty
.

(b) Assume that
\beta=1
and
\max\{1/2,d/2-1,d/4\}<\alpha<(d+1)/2
. Then it follows from Theorem
1.2
that for any
\bm{b}_{0}\in L_{2}
with
{\rm div}\,\bm{b}_{0}=0
, the problem (
1.1
) has global weak solutions. Moreover, by Theorem
1.3
, weak solutions of (
1.1
) are unique if
\alpha\geq d/2
in addition.

(c) Assume that
2\leq d\leq 4
and
(d+2)/6<\alpha=\beta<(d+1)/2
. Then it follows from Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
that for any
\bm{b}_{0}\in L_{2}
with
{\rm div}\,\bm{b}_{0}=0
, the problem (
1.1
) has global weak solutions, which are unique if
(d+2)/4\leq\alpha=\beta<(d+1)/2
.

One of our main tools to prove Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
is the following product estimate (see Lemma
2.8
): if
1/2<\alpha<(d+1)/2
,
\beta>0
, and
\alpha+\beta>d/2
, then there exist constants
0<\theta_{1},\theta_{2}<1
such that

(1.8)

\|{fg}\|_{{2}}\apprle\|{f}\|_{{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}}^{1-\theta_{1}}\|{f}\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}^{\theta_{1}}\|{g}\|_{{2}}^{1-\theta_{2}}\|{g}\|_{\dot{H}^{\beta}}^{\theta_{2}}

for all
f\in L_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}\cap\dot{H}^{\alpha}
and
g\in{H}^{\beta}
. Moreover, if
\alpha+(1-\mu)\beta\geq d/2+1
for some
0\leq\mu\leq 1
, we can choose
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
so that
(1+\mu)\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+1/\beta\leq 2
. This additional condition enables us to establish uniqueness of weak solutions of (
1.1
). The estimate (
1.8
) will be derived from generalized Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequalities that were obtained recently by Byeon-Kim-Oh
[
8
]
and Wang-Wei-Ye
[
36
]
.

To prove existence of weak solutions (Theorem
1.2
) of (
1.1
), we first construct a family of solutions
\{(\bm{u}_{R},\bm{b}_{R})\}
of the
R
-truncated problems (
5.4
). Moreover, it is shown that these solutions satisfy uniform bounds. Then by a standard Aubin-Lions compactness argument combined with the estimate (
1.8
), we construct a sequence of functions
\{\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\}
that has strong convergence in
L_{2}(0,T;L_{2}(K))
for every finite
T>0
and compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. To show strong convergence of
\bm{u}_{R_{k}}
, we utilize the solution formula (
1.4
) for the fractional Stokes system that will be derived in Section 3 (Theorem
3.9
). Very weak solutions of the fractional Stokes system are introduced to apply the solution formula (
1.4
) for
L^{1}
-data.
Finally, making crucial use of the assumption
2\alpha+\beta>d/2+1
, we show that the nonlinear terms
\bm{u}_{R_{k}}\otimes\bm{b}_{R_{k}}
converge strongly in
L_{1,\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty))
. The strong convergence properties of
\bm{u}_{R_{k}}
and
\bm{b}_{R_{k}}
enable us to complete the proof of existence of weak solutions of (
1.1
) by passing to the limit
k\to\infty
. To prove uniqueness of weak solutions (Theorem
1.3
), we first study weak solutions of perturbed fractional heat equations. Then applying a standard Gronwall-type argument combined with product estimates (Lemmas
2.8
and
2.9
), we show that weak solutions are unique.

This paper proceeds in six sections and three appendix sections. In Section
2
, we introduce our notations, the fractional Laplacian, homogeneous Sobolev spaces, and Lorentz spaces. We also provide proofs of product estimates that play crucial roles in proving existence and uniqueness of weak solutions. In Section
3
, we prove the solution formula (
1.4
) for the fractional Stokes system. The notion of very weak solutions is also introduced. In Section
4
, we prove existence and regularity properties of weak solutions of perturbed fractional heat equations that will be used in the proof of uniqueness of weak solutions of (
1.1
). The main theorems are proved in Sections
5
and
6
. Finally we give somewhat detailed proofs of some technical results in Appendices.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notations

Let
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
be the set of nonnegative integers. For
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}
and a function
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we write

D^{\gamma}u=D_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}\cdots D_{d}^{\gamma_{d}}u\quad\mbox{and}\quad|\gamma|=\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{d}.

We write
\nabla u=(D_{1}u,\dots,D_{d}u)
for the gradient of
u
.

We use bolditalic to denote vector fields, e.g.,
\bm{u}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Similarly we use boldroman to denote 2-tensors, e.g.,
\mathbf{F}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
. For two vector fields
\bm{u}=(u^{1},\dots,u^{d})
and
\bm{v}=(v^{1},\dots,v^{d})
, let
\bm{u}\otimes\bm{v}
be the
2
-tensor whose
(i,j)
component is
u^{i}v^{j}
for
1\leq i,j\leq d
. Also,
(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla)\bm{v}
is the vector field defined by

[(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla)\bm{v}]^{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}u^{j}\frac{\partial v^{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\quad(1\leq i\leq d).

For two 2-tensors
\mathbf{F}=[F^{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{d}
and
\mathbf{G}=[G^{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{d}
, their inner product is defined by

\mathbf{F}:\mathbf{G}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}F^{ij}G^{ij}

For
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
L_{p}(\Omega)
denotes the
L_{p}
-space over
\Omega
with the norm
\|{\cdot}\|_{{p};\Omega}
. For
1\leq p<\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
, we denote by
L_{p,q}(\Omega)
the Lorentz space over
\Omega
with the quasi-norm
\|{\cdot}\|_{{p,q};\Omega}
. Let
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
denote the space of all smooth functions (or vector fields) that have compact supports in
\Omega
. We denote by
C_{c,\sigma}^{\infty}(\Omega)
the set of all vector fields in
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
that are divergence-free. For the sake of convenience, we drop
\Omega
in function spaces and their (quasi-)norms if
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, e.g.,
L_{p}=L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\|\cdot\|_{p}=\|\cdot\|_{p;\mathbb{R}^{d}}
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
, let
B_{r}(x)
denote the open ball of radius
r
centered at
x
. We write
B_{r}
for open balls centered at the origin.

For two nonnegative quantities
A
and
B
, we write
A\apprle_{\alpha,\beta,\ldots}B
if
A\leq CB
for some positive constant
C
depending on the parameters
\alpha
,
\beta
, …. If the dependence is evident, we usually omit the subscripts and simply write
A\apprle B
. We write
A\approx B
if
A\apprle B
and
B\apprle A
.
For two topological vector spaces
X
and
Y
with
X\subset Y
, we write
X\hookrightarrow Y
if
X
is continuously embedded into
Y
, that is, the embedding
x\mapsto x
is continuous. For a topological vector space
X
, we denote by
X^{*}
the (topological) dual space of
X
. The dual pairing between
X^{*}
and
X
is denoted by
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>
.

Let
\mathscr{S}
and
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
be the Schwartz class and the space of tempered distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, respectively.
For an integrable function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote by
\hat{f}
and
f^{\vee}
the Fourier transform and the inverse Fourier transform, respectively, of
f
:

\hat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}f(x)\,d{x}\quad\text{and}\quad f^{\vee}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}f(\xi)\,d{\xi}.

If
f
is a tempered distribution, then
\hat{f}
and
f^{\vee}
are defined via duality by

\langle{\hat{f},\phi}\rangle=\langle{f,\hat{\phi}}\rangle\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left<f^{\vee},\phi\right>=\left<f,\phi^{\vee}\right>\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,\phi\in\mathscr{S}.

2.2.
Sobolev spaces

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1<p<\infty
, the
inhomogeneous Sobolev space

{H}^{s}_{p}
is defined by

{H}^{s}_{p}=\{f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}:J^{s}f\in L_{p}\},

where
J^{s}=(I-\Delta)^{s/2}:\mathscr{S}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
is defined via the Fourier transform by

\widehat{J^{s}f}=\left(1+4\pi^{2}|\cdot|^{2}\right)^{s/2}\hat{f}\quad\text{for }f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}.

For
p=2
, we write
{H}^{s}={H}^{s}_{2}
. Then the Sobolev space
{H}^{s}
is indeed a Hilbert space equipped with the inner product

(u,v)_{{H}^{s}}=\left(J^{s}u,J^{s}v\right)

with
(\cdot,\cdot)
denoting the inner product on
L_{2}
. By the Plancherel theorem, we have

(u,v)_{{H}^{s}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(1+4\pi^{2}|\xi|^{2}\right)^{s}\hat{u}(\xi)\overline{\hat{v}(\xi)}\,\,d{\xi}.

To define homogeneous Sobolev spaces, we need to introduce the following subspace of
\mathscr{S}
:

\mathscr{S}_{0}=\{\phi\in\mathscr{S}:D^{\gamma}\hat{\phi}(0)=0\quad\text{for all }\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}\}.

Let
\mathscr{S}_{0}^{\prime}
be the dual space of
\mathscr{S}_{0}
. Then since
\mathscr{S}_{0}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{S}\hookrightarrow L_{1}\cap L_{\infty}
, we have

L_{1}+L_{\infty}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{S}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{S}_{0}^{\prime}.

Moreover, it follows from the Hahn-Banach theorem that every
f\in\mathscr{S}_{0}^{\prime}
can be extended to a tempered distribution
F
. Such an
F
is unique up to additive polynomials, thanks to the following simple lemma (see e.g.
[
19
, Proposition 1.1.3]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f
be a tempered distribution. Then
f
is a polynomial if and only if
\left<f,\psi\right>=0
for all
\psi\in\mathscr{S}_{0}
.

The fractional Laplacian
\Lambda^{s}:\mathscr{S}_{0}\rightarrow\mathscr{S}_{0}
of order
s\in\mathbb{R}
is defined by

\Lambda^{s}\phi=\left((2\pi|\cdot|)^{s}\hat{\phi}\right)^{\vee}\quad\text{for all }\phi\in\mathscr{S}_{0}.

Then since
\Lambda^{s+t}=\Lambda^{s}\Lambda^{t}
for any
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
, it follows that
\Lambda^{s}:\mathscr{S}_{0}\rightarrow\mathscr{S}_{0}
is an isomorphism with the inverse
\Lambda^{-s}
. Hence for each
u\in\mathscr{S}_{0}^{\prime}
, we define
\Lambda^{s}u\in\mathscr{S}_{0}^{\prime}
via duality by

\left<\Lambda^{s}u,\phi\right>=\left<u,\Lambda^{s}\phi\right>\quad\text{for all }\phi\in\mathscr{S}_{0}.

Note also that
\Lambda^{s}:\mathscr{S}_{0}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathscr{S}_{0}^{\prime}
is an isomorphism.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1<p<\infty
, the
homogeneous Sobolev space

\dot{H}^{s}_{p}
is defined by

\dot{H}^{s}_{p}=\{f\in\mathscr{S}_{0}^{\prime}:\Lambda^{s}f\in L_{p}\}.

For
p=2
, we write
\dot{H}^{s}=\dot{H}^{s}_{p}
. The space
\dot{H}^{s}
is a Hilbert space equipped with the inner product

(u,v)_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\left(\Lambda^{s}u,\Lambda^{s}v\right).

It should be remarked that
\Lambda^{t}:\dot{H}^{s+t}\rightarrow\dot{H}^{s}
is an isometric isomorphism for any
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
. It is standard that
{H}^{s}=L_{2}\cap\dot{H}^{s}
for any
s\geq 0
(see e.g.
[
6
, Theorem 6.3.2]
).

The following density results will be used several times in the paper.

Lemma 2.2
.

(1)

C_{c}^{\infty}
is dense in
\mathscr{S}
.

(2)

For each
\phi\in\mathscr{S}
, there exists a sequence
\{\phi_{k}\}
in
\mathscr{S}_{0}
such that
\phi_{k}\rightarrow\phi
in
{H}^{s}
for any
s\geq 0

(3)

\mathscr{S}_{0}
is dense in both
{H}^{s}
and
\dot{H}^{s}
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

(4)

\mathscr{S}_{0}
is dense in
L_{p}\cap\dot{H}^{s}
for
1<p<\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

To prove Parts (1) and (2), we choose
\psi\in C^{\infty}
such that
0\leq\psi\leq 1
,
\psi=0
in
B_{1}
, and
\psi=1
outside
B_{2}
. Given
\phi\in\mathscr{S}
, we define
\phi_{k}(x)=\left[1-\psi(x/k)\right]\phi(x)
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\phi_{k}\in C_{c}^{\infty}
and
\phi_{k}\rightarrow\phi
in
\mathscr{S}
. Moreover, if
\psi_{k}=\left(\psi(k\cdot)\hat{\phi}\right)^{\vee}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\psi_{k}\in\mathscr{S}_{0}
and

\|{\psi_{k}-\phi}\|_{{H}^{s}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(1+4\pi^{2}|\xi|^{2}\right)^{s}\left|\psi(k\xi)-1\right|^{2}|\hat{\phi}(\xi)|^{2}\,\,d{\xi}\rightarrow 0

for any
s\geq 0
, by the dominated convergence theorem.

Suppose that
f\in\dot{H}^{s}
and
u=\Lambda^{s}f
. Then since
\mathscr{S}
is dense in
L_{2}
, it follows from Part (2) that there exists a sequence
\{u_{k}\}
in
\mathscr{S}_{0}
such that
u_{k}\to u
in
L_{2}
. If
f_{k}=\Lambda^{-s}u_{k}
, then
f_{k}\in\mathscr{S}_{0}
and
\|{f_{k}-f}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\|{u_{k}-u}\|_{2}\to 0
. This proves that
\mathscr{S}_{0}
is dense in
\dot{H}^{s}
. Similarly, using
J^{s}
instead of
\Lambda^{s}
, one easily shows that
\mathscr{S}_{0}
is dense in
{H}^{s}
. This proves Part (3). A proof of (4) will be given later in Appendix
A
.
∎

An immediate consequence of Lemma
2.2
(3) is that every
f\in\dot{H}^{-s}
can be extended to a bounded linear functional on
\dot{H}^{s}
in such a way that the inequality

(2.1)

\left<f,g\right>\leq\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}}\|g\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

holds for all
g\in\dot{H}^{s}
. Moreover, since
L^{2}
is self-dual and
\Lambda^{t}:\dot{H}^{t}\to L^{2}
is an isomorphism for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, it is easily shown that
\dot{H}^{-s}
is the dual space of
\dot{H}^{s}
.

Assume now that
s>0
. Then it can be shown (see e.g.
[
20
, Lemma 1]
) that
\Lambda^{s}
is well-defined on
\mathscr{S}
and maps
\mathscr{S}
to
\mathscr{S}_{s}
, where

\mathscr{S}_{s}=\left\{f\in C^{\infty}:\left(1+|\cdot|^{d+s}\right)D^{\gamma}\psi\in L_{\infty}\,\,\text{ for all }\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\right\}.

In particular,
\Lambda^{s}
is bounded from
\mathscr{S}
to
L_{1}\cap L_{\infty}
. Hence for each
u\in L_{1}+L_{\infty}
, we can extend
\Lambda^{s}u
to a tempered distribution by defining

\left<\Lambda^{s}u,\phi\right>=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\Lambda^{s}\phi\,d{x}\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,\phi\in\mathscr{S}.

2.3.
Lorentz spaces

Let
\Omega
be any domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For a measurable function
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, the
decreasing rearrangement

f^{*}
of
f
is defined by

f^{*}(t)=\inf\{s>0:d_{f}(s)\leq t\}\quad(t>0),

where
d_{f}(s)=|\{x\in\Omega:|f(x)|>s\}|
. For
1\leq p<\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
, we define

\|{f}\|_{{p,q};\Omega}=\begin{cases}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}[t^{1/p}f^{*}(t)]^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/q}&if $q<\infty$,\\
\sup_{t>0}t^{1/p}f^{*}(t)&if $q=\infty$.\end{cases}

The set of all
f
satisfying
\|{f}\|_{{p,q};\Omega}<\infty
is denoted by
L_{p,q}(\Omega)
and called the
Lorentz space
over
\Omega
with indices
p
and
q
. It is well-known that
L_{p,q}(\Omega)
is a complete quasi-normed space and is normable if
1<p<\infty
(see e.g.
[
32
, Theorem 3.22]
). Moreover, for
1\leq p<\infty
, we have

L_{p,1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{p,p}(\Omega)=L_{p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{p,\infty}(\Omega).

Using Lorentz spaces, we can refine several well-known inequalities in analysis. The first one is Hölder’s inequality in Lorentz spaces which was proved by O’Neil
[
29
, Theorems 3.4, 3.5]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
1\leq p,p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
and
1\leq q,q_{1},q_{2}\leq\infty
satisfy

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}.

Then for all
f\in L_{p_{1},q_{1}}(\Omega)
and
g\in L_{p_{2},q_{2}}(\Omega)
,

\|{fg}\|_{{p,q};\Omega}\apprle\|{f}\|_{{p_{1},q_{1}};\Omega}\|{g}\|_{{p_{2},q_{2}};\Omega}.

The second one is the following refinement of Sobolev’s inequality (see e.g.
[
1
, Theorem 7.34]
and
[
6
, Theorem 6.5.1]
).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
0<s<d/2
. Then for all
u\in\dot{H}^{s}
, we have

\|{u}\|_{{{2d}/{(d-2s)},2}}\apprle\|{u}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}.

The third one is the following generalization of Young’s convolution inequality due to O’Neil
[
29
, Theorem 2.6]
(see also
[
18
, Theorems 1.2.13 and 1.4.24]
).

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
1<p,p_{1}<\infty
and
1\leq p_{2}<\infty
satisfy

\frac{1}{p}+1=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}.

Then for all
f\in L^{p_{1},\infty}
and
g\in L^{p_{2}}
, we have

\displaystyle\|f*g\|_{p}

\displaystyle\apprle\|f\|_{p_{1},\infty}\|g\|_{p_{2}}\quad\mbox{if}\,\,1<p_{2}<\infty;

\displaystyle\|f*g\|_{p,\infty}

\displaystyle\apprle\|f\|_{p_{1},\infty}\|g\|_{1}\quad\mbox{if}\,\,p_{2}=1.

Recall the following classical Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequality (see e.g.
[
8
, Theorem 5.10]
).

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
0\leq s_{0}<s
,
1<p,p_{1}<\infty
, and
0<\theta<1
satisfy

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{s_{0}}{d}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{1}}+\theta\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{s}{d}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\theta\geq\frac{s_{0}}{s}.

Then for all
f\in L_{p_{1}}\cap\dot{H}^{s}
, we have

\|{\Lambda^{s_{0}}f}\|_{{p}}\apprle\|{f}\|_{{p_{1}}}^{1-\theta}\|{\Lambda^{s}f}\|_{{2}}^{\theta}.

Particularly, if
s_{0}=0
and
p_{1}=2
in Proposition
2.6
, we deduce the well-known Sobolev embedding

H^{s}\hookrightarrow L^{p}
for
s>0
and
2\leq p<\infty
satisfying
1/p\geq 1/2-s/d
.

The Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequality can be refined by using Lorentz spaces. The following is a special case of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequalities in Sobolev-Lorentz spaces (see
[
36
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
8
, Theorem 5.17]
).

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
s>0
,
1<p,p_{1}<\infty
, and
0<\theta<1
satisfy

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{1}}+\theta\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{s}{d}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{p_{1}}\neq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{s}{d}.

Then for all
f\in L_{p_{1},\infty}\cap\dot{H}^{s}
, we have

\|{f}\|_{{p,1}}\apprle\|{f}\|_{{p_{1},\infty}}^{1-\theta}\|{\Lambda^{s}f}\|_{{2}}^{\theta}.

2.4.
Product estimates

In this subsection, we prove product estimates that will be used in the paper. The first product estimate plays a crucial role in the proof of existence and uniqueness of weak solutions of (
1.1
).

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\alpha,\beta>0
satisfy
1/2<\alpha<(d+1)/2
and
\alpha+\beta>d/2
. Then there exist
0<\theta_{1},\theta_{2}<1
such that

\|{fg}\|_{{2}}\apprle\|{f}\|_{{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}}^{1-\theta_{1}}\|{f}\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}^{\theta_{1}}\|{g}\|_{{2}}^{1-\theta_{2}}\|{g}\|_{\dot{H}^{\beta}}^{\theta_{2}}

for all
f\in L_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}\cap\dot{H}^{\alpha}
and
g\in{H}^{\beta}
. Moreover, if
\alpha+(1-\mu)\beta\geq d/2+1
for some
0\leq\mu\leq 1
, then
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
can be chosen so that
(1+\mu)\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+1/\beta\leq 2
.

Proof.

We will show that there exists
2<p<\infty
satisfying

(2.2)

\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{p}<\alpha<\frac{d+1}{2}-\frac{d}{2p}\quad\text{and}\quad\beta>\frac{d}{p}.

For such
p
, we define

\theta_{1}=\frac{d+1-2\alpha-d/p}{{d}/{2}+1-\alpha}\quad\text{and }\quad\theta_{2}=\frac{d}{p\beta}.

Then since

(2.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}

\displaystyle=(1-\theta_{1})\frac{d+1-2\alpha}{d}+\theta_{1}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{d}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{p-2}{2p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\theta_{2}}{2}+\theta_{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\beta}{d}\right),\quad\mbox{and}\quad 0<\theta_{1},\theta_{2}<1,

it follows from Hölder’s inequality and Proposition
2.7
that

\displaystyle\|{fg}\|_{{2}}\leq\|{f}\|_{{p}}\|{g}\|_{{2p/(p-2)}}\apprle\|{f}\|_{{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}}^{1-\theta_{1}}\|{f}\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}^{\theta_{1}}\|{g}\|_{{2}}^{1-\theta_{2}}\|{g}\|_{\dot{H}^{\beta}}^{\theta_{2}}.

Moreover, since

{\beta\theta_{2}}=\frac{d}{p}=(1-\theta_{1})\left({d+1-2\alpha}\right)+\theta_{1}\left(\frac{d}{2}-{\alpha}\right),

we deduce that if
\alpha+(1-\mu)\beta\geq d/2+1
for some
0\leq\mu\leq 1
, then

\displaystyle\beta\left[(1+\mu)\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}-2\right]+1

\displaystyle=\left[\alpha+(1+\mu)\beta-\frac{d}{2}-1\right]\theta_{1}-2\alpha-2\beta+d+2

\displaystyle\leq-\alpha-(1-\mu)\beta+\frac{d}{2}+1\leq 0.

Therefore it remains to prove existence of
2<p<\infty
satisfying (
2.2
).

First, if
{d}/{2}\leq\alpha<{(d+1)}/{2}
and
\beta>0
, then choosing a sufficiently large
2<p<\infty
, we obviously have

\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{p}<\frac{d}{2}\leq\alpha<\frac{d+1}{2}-\frac{d}{2p}\quad\text{and}\quad\beta>\frac{d}{p}.

Suppose next that
{1}/{2}<\alpha<{d}/{2}
and
\beta>d/2-\alpha
.
Then

\max\left\{2,\frac{d}{\beta},\frac{d}{d+1-2\alpha}\right\}<\frac{d}{{d}/{2}-\alpha}<\infty.

Choosing any
p
such that

\max\left\{2,\frac{d}{\beta},\frac{d}{d+1-2\alpha}\right\}<p<\frac{d}{{d}/{2}-\alpha},

we have

2<p<\infty,\quad\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{p}<\alpha<\frac{d+1}{2}-\frac{d}{2p},\quad\text{and}\quad\beta>\frac{d}{p}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
2.8
.
∎

To prove uniqueness of weak solutions of (
1.1
) particularly when
1/2<\alpha<1
, we also need the following product estimate.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\gamma>d/2
and
0<s\leq\gamma
. Then for all
f\in{H}^{s}
and
g\in{H}^{\gamma}
,

\|{\Lambda^{s}(fg)}\|_{{2}}\leq C\|{f}\|_{{H}^{s}}\|{g}\|_{{H}^{\gamma}},

where
C=C(d,\gamma,s)>0
.

Proof.

A proof is given in
[
22
, Lemma 3.2]
. However, we provide a detailed proof of the lemma for the sake of convenience.

Suppose that
0<s<\gamma
. Then since
\gamma>d/2
, there exists
2<p<\infty
such that

\max\left\{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{s}{d},0\right\}<\frac{1}{p}<\min\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{\gamma-s}{d}\right\}.

By the Sobolev embedding theorem, we have

\|{f}\|_{{p}}\apprle\|{f}\|_{{H}^{s}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|{J^{s}g}\|_{{{2p}/{(p-2)}}}\apprle\|{J^{s}g}\|_{{H}^{\gamma-s}}.

Therefore, it follows from the fractional Leibniz rule (see e.g.
[
20
, Theorem 1]
) and Sobolev embedding theorem that

\displaystyle\|{\Lambda^{s}(fg)}\|_{{2}}

\displaystyle\apprle\|{f}\|_{{p}}\|{\Lambda^{s}g}\|_{{{2p}/{(p-2)}}}+\|{\Lambda^{s}f}\|_{{2}}\|{g}\|_{{\infty}}

\displaystyle\apprle\|{f}\|_{{H}^{s}}\|{J^{s}g}\|_{{H}^{\gamma-s}}+\|{\Lambda^{s}f}\|_{{2}}\|{g}\|_{{H}^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\apprle\|{f}\|_{{H}^{s}}\|{g}\|_{{H}^{\gamma}}.

Similarly, if
s=\gamma
, then

\displaystyle\|{\Lambda^{s}(fg)}\|_{{2}}

\displaystyle\apprle\|{f}\|_{{\infty}}\|{\Lambda^{s}g}\|_{{2}}+\|{\Lambda^{s}f}\|_{{2}}\|{g}\|_{{\infty}}

\displaystyle\apprle\|{f}\|_{{H}^{s}}\|{g}\|_{{H}^{\gamma}}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
2.9
.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
satisfy either one of the following conditions:

(1)

\alpha<1
,
\beta>d/2
, and
\alpha+\beta\geq 1
.

(2)

1\leq\alpha<d/2+1
and
\alpha+2\beta\geq d/2+1
.

Then for all
f,g\in{H}^{\beta}
,

\|{fg}\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\alpha}}\leq C\|{f}\|_{{H}^{\beta}}\|{g}\|_{{H}^{\beta}},

where
C=C(d,\alpha,\beta)>0
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\alpha<1
,
\beta>d/2
, and
\alpha+\beta\geq 1
. Then since
0<1-\alpha\leq\beta
, it immediately follows from Lemma
2.9
that

\|{fg}\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\alpha}}\leq C\|{f}\|_{{H}^{1-\alpha}}\|{g}\|_{{H}^{\beta}}\leq C\|{f}\|_{{H}^{\beta}}\|{g}\|_{{H}^{\beta}}.

Suppose next that
1\leq\alpha<d/2+1
and
\alpha+2\beta\geq d/2+1
. Recall then that

\dot{H}^{1-\alpha}=\left(\dot{H}^{\alpha-1}\right)^{*}.

Let
2\leq r<\infty
be defined by

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha-1}{d}.

Note that

\beta>0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{1}{2r^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha-1}{d}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\beta}{d}.

Hence by the Sobolev embedding theorem (see Lemma
2.4
and Proposition
2.6
with
p_{1}=2
e.g.), we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(fg)\phi\,d{x}\leq\|{f}\|_{{2r^{\prime}}}\|{g}\|_{{2r^{\prime}}}\|{\phi}\|_{{r}}\apprle\|{f}\|_{{H}^{\beta}}\|{g}\|_{{H}^{\beta}}\|{\phi}\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha-1}}

for any
\phi\in\dot{H}^{\alpha-1}
. This completes the proof of Lemma
2.10
.
∎

3.
Fractional Stokes equations

In this section, we consider the fractional Stokes equations in
\mathbb{R}^{d},d\geq 2
:

(3.1)

\Lambda^{2\alpha}\bm{u}+\nabla{p_{*}}=\Div\mathbf{F},\quad\Div\bm{u}=0\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\alpha
is a real number. Here
\mathbf{F}=[F^{jk}]_{1\leq j,k\leq d}
is a given matrix-valued function and
\Div\mathbf{F}
is the vector field whose
j
-th component is given by

(\Div\mathbf{F})^{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}D_{k}F^{jk};

hence

\Div\Div\mathbf{F}=\sum_{j,k=1}^{d}D_{jk}F^{jk}

and

\widehat{\Div\Div\mathbf{F}}(\xi)=(2\pi i\xi)\otimes(2\pi i\xi):\widehat{\mathbf{F}}(\xi)=-4\pi^{2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{d}\xi_{j}\xi_{k}\widehat{F}^{jk}(\xi).

This section consists of three subsections. In Subsection
3.1
, we prove solvability results in Sobolev spaces for (
3.1
). In Subsection
3.2
, we derive a solution formula for the problem (
3.1
) that will be used in several places in the rest of the paper. Finally, very weak solutions of (
3.1
) are studied in Subsection
3.3
.

3.1.
Solutions in Sobolev spaces

The problem (
3.1
) is first solved in the class
\mathscr{S}_{0}
by decoupling the fractional Stokes system into two elliptic equations.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\alpha
be any real number. Then for each
\mathbf{F}\in\mathscr{S}_{0}
, there exists a unique solution
(\bm{u},{p_{*}})\in\mathscr{S}_{0}\times\mathscr{S}_{0}
of (
3.1
).

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathbf{F}\in\mathscr{S}_{0}
. Then since
\Div\mathbf{F}\in\mathscr{S}_{0}
and
\Delta=-\Lambda^{2}
is an isomorphism from
\mathscr{S}_{0}
onto itself, there exists a unique
{p_{*}}\in\mathscr{S}_{0}
such that

(3.2)

\Delta{p_{*}}=\Div\Div\mathbf{F}\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Let
\mathbf{I}
denote the identity matrix. Then since
\Lambda^{2\alpha}:\mathscr{S}_{0}\rightarrow\mathscr{S}_{0}
is an isomorphism, there exists a unique
\bm{u}\in\mathscr{S}_{0}
such that

(3.3)

\Lambda^{2\alpha}\bm{u}=\Div(\mathbf{F}-{p_{*}}\mathbf{I})\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Since
\Div({p_{*}}\mathbf{I})=\nabla{p_{*}}
, we have

\Lambda^{2\alpha}\bm{u}+\nabla{p_{*}}=\Div\mathbf{F}\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Moreover,
taking the divergence in (
3.3
), we have

\Lambda^{2\alpha}(\Div\bm{u})=\Div\Div\mathbf{F}-\Delta{p_{*}}=0\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Since
\Div\bm{u}\in\mathscr{S}_{0}
and
\Lambda^{2\alpha}:\mathscr{S}_{0}\rightarrow\mathscr{S}_{0}
is an isomorphism, it follows that
\Div\bm{u}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This proves existence of a solution in
\mathscr{S}_{0}\times\mathscr{S}_{0}
of (
3.1
).

To prove the uniqueness assertion, let
(\bm{u},{p_{*}})\in\mathscr{S}_{0}\times\mathscr{S}_{0}
be a solution of (
3.1
) with the trivial data
\mathbf{F}={\mathbf{0}}
. Then since
\Div\bm{u}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\Lambda^{2\alpha}\Div=\Div\Lambda^{2\alpha}
in
\mathscr{S}_{0}
, we take the divergence in (
3.1
) to obtain

\Delta{p_{*}}=0\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Since
\Delta:\mathscr{S}_{0}\rightarrow\mathscr{S}_{0}
is an isomorphism, it follows that
{p_{*}}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then since
\Lambda^{2\alpha}\bm{u}=\mathbf{0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we conclude that
\bm{u}=\mathbf{0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This completes the proof of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Using the decomposition of (
3.1
) into (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we prove unique solvability of (
3.1
) in homogeneous Sobolev spaces of arbitrary orders.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\alpha,s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then for each
\mathbf{F}\in\dot{H}^{s}
, there exists a unique solution
(\bm{u},{p_{*}})
in
\dot{H}^{2\alpha+s-1}\times\dot{H}^{s}
of (
3.1
).
Moreover, we have

\|{\bm{u}}\|_{\dot{H}^{2\alpha+s-1}}+\|{{p_{*}}}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\apprle\|{\mathbf{F}}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathbf{F}\in\dot{H}^{s}
. Then since
\Div\Div\mathbf{F}\in\dot{H}^{s-2}
and
\Delta
is an isometric isomorphism from
\dot{H}^{t+2}
to
\dot{H}^{t}
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists a unique solution
{p_{*}}\in\dot{H}^{s}
of (
3.2
), which satisfies

\|{{p_{*}}}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\apprle\|{\mathbf{F}}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}.

Moreover, since
\Lambda^{2\alpha}
is an isomorphism from
\dot{H}^{2\alpha+s-1}
to
\dot{H}^{s-1}
, there exists a unique
\bm{u}\in\dot{H}^{2\alpha+s-1}
satisfying (
3.3
). Note also that

\|{\bm{u}}\|_{\dot{H}^{2\alpha+s-1}}\apprle\|{\mathbf{F}}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\|{{p_{*}}}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\apprle\|{\mathbf{F}}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}.

Finally, since
\Div\bm{u}\in\dot{H}^{2\alpha+s-2}
and
\Lambda^{2\alpha}(\Div\bm{u})=0
in
\dot{H}^{s-2}
, it follows that
\Div\bm{u}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This proves the existence assertion of the theorem.

To show the uniqueness, suppose that
(\bm{u},{p_{*}})\in\dot{H}^{2\alpha+s-1}\times\dot{H}^{s}
satisfies (
3.1
) with
\mathbf{F}=\mathbf{0}
. Since
\Div\bm{u}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\Lambda^{2\alpha}\Div=\Div\Lambda^{2\alpha}
in
\mathscr{S}_{0}^{\prime}
, it follows from (
3.1
) that
\Delta{p_{*}}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Since
\Delta
is an isomorphism, we then deduce that
{p_{*}}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. From (
3.1
) again, we have
\Lambda^{2\alpha}\bm{u}=\mathbf{0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Since
\Lambda^{2\alpha}
is an isomorphism, we then conclude that
\bm{u}=\mathbf{0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
3.2
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\alpha
be any real number. Then for each
\mathbf{F}\in\dot{H}^{1-\alpha}
, there exists a unique solution
(\bm{u},{p_{*}})
in
\dot{H}^{\alpha}\times\dot{H}^{1-\alpha}
of (
3.1
).
Moreover, we have

\|{\bm{u}}\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}+\|{{p_{*}}}\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\alpha}}\apprle\|{\mathbf{F}}\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\alpha}}.

In addition,
\bm{u}
satisfies the energy identity

(3.4)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\Lambda^{\alpha}\bm{u}|^{2}\,d{x}=\left<\Div\mathbf{F},\bm{u}\right>.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.2
, it remains to show that the solution
(\bm{u},{p_{*}})
in
\dot{H}^{\alpha}\times\dot{H}^{1-\alpha}
of (
3.1
) satisfies the energy identity (
3.4
).
Note first that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda^{\alpha}\bm{u}\cdot\Lambda^{\alpha}\Phi\,d{x}-\left<{p_{*}},\Div\Phi\right>=\left<\Lambda^{2\alpha}\bm{u},\Phi\right>+\left<\nabla{p_{*}},\Phi\right>=\left<\Div\mathbf{F},\Phi\right>

for all
\Phi\in\mathscr{S}_{0}
.
By Lemma
2.2
, there exists a sequence
\{\bm{u}_{k}\}
in
\mathscr{S}_{0}
such that
\|{\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}}\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}\rightarrow 0
. For each
k
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda^{\alpha}\bm{u}\cdot\Lambda^{\alpha}\bm{u}_{k}\,d{x}-\left<{p_{*}},\Div\bm{u}_{k}\right>=\left<\Div\mathbf{F},\bm{u}_{k}\right>.

Hence by the duality inequality (
2.1
),
\bm{u}
satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\Lambda^{\alpha}\bm{u}|^{2}\,d{x}-\left<{p_{*}},\Div\bm{u}\right>=\left<\Div\mathbf{F},\bm{u}\right>.

Since
\Div\bm{u}=0
, we get the energy identity (
3.4
).
This completes the proof of Theorem
3.3
.
∎

3.2.
A solution formula

Given
\mathbf{F}=[F^{jk}]_{1\leq j,k\leq d}\in\mathscr{S}_{0}
, let
(\bm{u},{p_{*}})\in\mathscr{S}_{0}\times\mathscr{S}_{0}
be the solution of (
3.1
). First, taking the Fourier transform in (
3.2
), we have

-(2\pi|\xi|)^{2}\widehat{{p_{*}}}(\xi)=(2\pi i\xi)\otimes(2\pi i\xi):\widehat{\mathbf{F}}(\xi)\quad\mbox{for all }\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

or equivalently

\widehat{{p_{*}}}(\xi)=\frac{\xi\otimes\xi}{|\xi|^{2}}:\widehat{\mathbf{F}}(\xi)\quad\mbox{for all }\xi\neq 0.

Next, taking the Fourier transform in (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle(2\pi|\xi|)^{2\alpha}\widehat{u}^{j}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{d}(2\pi i\xi_{l})\widehat{F}^{jl}(\xi)-(2\pi i\xi_{j})\widehat{{p_{*}}}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}(2\pi i\xi_{l})\left(\delta_{jk}-\frac{\xi_{j}\xi_{k}}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)\widehat{F}^{kl}(\xi)

and so

\widehat{u}^{j}(\xi)=\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\frac{(2\pi i\xi_{l})}{(2\pi|\xi|)^{2\alpha}}\left(\delta_{jk}-\frac{\xi_{j}\xi_{k}}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)\widehat{F}^{kl}(\xi)

for
j=1,\ldots,d
.

Assuming that
2\alpha<d+1
, let
U_{\alpha,j}^{kl}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
be the inverse Fourier transform of the locally integrable function

\frac{(2\pi i\xi_{l})}{(2\pi|\xi|)^{2\alpha}}\left(\delta_{jk}-\frac{\xi_{j}\xi_{k}}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)

in
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
. The following lemma will be proved later in Appendix
B
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose that
j,k,l\in\{1,...,d\}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, and
\psi\in\mathscr{S}
. Let
\Gamma
be the Gamma function.

(1)

If
1<\operatorname{Re}\lambda<d+1
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{-2\pi i\xi_{j}}{|\xi|^{\lambda}}{\psi}^{\vee}(\xi)\,d\xi=\frac{2\Gamma((d+2-\lambda)/2)}{\Gamma(\lambda/2)\pi^{d/2-\lambda}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{x_{j}}{|x|^{d+2-\lambda}}\psi(x)\,dx.

(2)

If
2<\operatorname{Re}\lambda<d+2
, then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(-2\pi i\xi_{j})(-2\pi i\xi_{k})}{|\xi|^{\lambda}}{\psi}^{\vee}(\xi)\,d\xi

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{2\Gamma((d+2-\lambda)/2)}{\Gamma(\lambda/2)\pi^{d/2-\lambda}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(d+2-\lambda)x_{j}x_{k}-\delta_{jk}|x|^{2}}{|x|^{d+4-\lambda}}{\psi}(x)\,dx.

(3)

If
3<\operatorname{Re}\lambda<d+3
, then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(-2\pi i\xi_{j})(-2\pi i\xi_{k})(-2\pi i\xi_{l})}{|\xi|^{\lambda}}{\psi}^{\vee}(\xi)\,d\xi

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{4\Gamma((d+4-\lambda)/2)}{\Gamma(\lambda/2)\pi^{d/2-\lambda}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[-D_{l}\left(\frac{x_{j}x_{k}}{|x|^{d+4-\lambda}}\right)-\frac{\delta_{jk}x_{l}}{|x|^{d+4-\lambda}}\right]{\psi}(x)\,d{x}.

Assume that
1<2\alpha<d+1
.
Then using Lemma
3.4
, we can find
U_{\alpha,j}^{kl}
explicitly. First of all, it follows from Lemma
3.4
(1) that the inverse Fourier transform of
(2\pi i\xi_{l})(2\pi|\xi|)^{-2\alpha}
is

-\frac{2\Gamma(1+d/2-\alpha)}{2^{2\alpha}\pi^{d/2}\Gamma(\alpha)}\frac{x_{l}}{|x|^{d+2-2\alpha}}.

Next, by Lemma
3.4
(3) with
\lambda=2\alpha+2
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{2\pi i\xi_{j}\xi_{k}\xi_{l}}{|\xi|^{2\alpha+2}}{\psi}^{\vee}(\xi)\,d\xi

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{\Gamma(1+d/2-\alpha)}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)\pi^{d/2-2\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[-D_{l}\left(\frac{x_{j}x_{k}}{|x|^{d+2-2\alpha}}\right)-\frac{\delta_{jk}x_{l}}{|x|^{d+2-2\alpha}}\right]{\psi}(x)\,d{x}

for all
\psi\in\mathscr{S}
. Hence the inverse Fourier transform of
(2\pi i\xi_{l}\xi_{j}\xi_{k})(2\pi|\xi|)^{-2\alpha}|\xi|^{-2}
is

\frac{\Gamma(1+d/2-\alpha)}{2^{2\alpha}\pi^{d/2}\Gamma(1+\alpha)}\left[-D_{l}\left(\frac{x_{j}x_{k}}{|x|^{d+2-2\alpha}}\right)-\frac{\delta_{jk}x_{l}}{|x|^{d+2-2\alpha}}\right].

Therefore,

(3.5)

U_{\alpha,j}^{kl}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(1+d/2-\alpha)}{2^{2\alpha}\pi^{d/2}\Gamma(1+\alpha)}\left[D_{l}\left(\frac{x_{j}x_{k}}{|x|^{d+2-2\alpha}}\right)-\frac{\delta_{jk}(2\alpha-1)x_{l}}{|x|^{d+2-2\alpha}}\right].

Note that

U_{\alpha,j}^{kl}\in L_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}.

Now, setting
\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}=[U_{\alpha,j}^{kl}]_{1\leq j,k,l\leq d}
, we have

\bm{u}=\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}*\mathbf{F}

or componentwise

u^{j}=\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}U_{\alpha,j}^{kl}*F^{kl}\quad(j=1,\ldots,d).

3.3.
Very weak solutions

The notion of very weak solutions is introduced to solve the fractional Stokes system (
3.1
) for
\mathbf{F}\in L_{1}
. Recall that if
s\geq 0
, then
\Lambda^{s}
is bounded from
\mathscr{S}
to
L_{1}\cap L_{\infty}
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\alpha\geq 0
. Suppose that
\mathbf{F}
is a distribution. Then we say that
\bm{u}\in L_{1}+L_{\infty}
is a
very weak solution
of (
3.1
) if

(3.6)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}\cdot\left(\Lambda^{2\alpha}\Phi+\nabla\psi\right)dx=-\left<\mathbf{F},\nabla\Phi\right>

for all
\Phi\in C_{c,\sigma}^{\infty}
and
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}
.

Indeed, taking
\Phi=0
in (
3.6
), we deduce that if
\bm{u}
is a very weak solution of (
3.1
), then it is divergence-free:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\psi\,d{x}=0\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}.

Existence of a pressure associated with a very weak solution of (
3.1
) is an easy consequence of the following more or less standard result, which will be proved later in Appendix
C
.

Lemma 3.6
.

If
\bm{u}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
satisfies
\left<\bm{u},\Phi\right>=0
for all
\Phi\in C_{c,\sigma}^{\infty}
, then there exists
p\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
such that
\bm{u}=\nabla p
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\alpha\geq 0
. If
\bm{u}
is a very weak solution of (
3.1
) with
\mathbf{F}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
, then
there exists
{p_{*}}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}\cdot\left(\Lambda^{2\alpha}\Phi+\nabla\psi\right)dx-\left<{p_{*}},\Div\Phi\right>=-\left<\mathbf{F},\nabla\Phi\right>

for all
(\Phi,\psi)\in\mathscr{S}
.

Proof.

Since
C_{c}^{\infty}
is dense in
\mathscr{S}
, we easily have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\psi\,d{x}=0\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,\psi\in\mathscr{S}.

Note that
\Lambda^{2\alpha}\bm{u}-\Div\mathbf{F}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
. Moreover, for all
\Phi\in C_{c,\sigma}^{\infty}
, we have

\left<\Lambda^{2\alpha}\bm{u}-\Div\mathbf{F},\Phi\right>=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}\cdot\Lambda^{2\alpha}\Phi\,d{x}+\left<\mathbf{F},\nabla\Phi\right>=0.

Hence by Lemma
3.6
, there exists
{p_{*}}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}\cdot\Lambda^{2\alpha}\Phi\,d{x}+\left<\mathbf{F},\nabla\Phi\right>=\left<\Lambda^{2\alpha}\bm{u}-\Div\mathbf{F},\Phi\right>=\left<{p_{*}},\Div\Phi\right>

for all
\Phi\in\mathscr{S}
. This completes the proof of Lemma
3.7
.
∎

We are now able to prove uniqueness of very weak solutions of (
3.6
).

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\alpha\geq 0
. If
\bm{u}\in L_{1}+L_{\infty}
is a very weak solution of (
3.1
) with
\mathbf{F}=\mathbf{0}
, then
\bm{u}
is constant. In addition, if
\bm{u}\in L_{q,\infty}
for some
1<q<\infty
, then
\bm{u}
is identically zero.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.7
, there exists
{p_{*}}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}\cdot\Lambda^{2\alpha}\Phi\,d{x}-\left<{p_{*}},\Div\Phi\right>=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\psi\,d{x}=0

for all
(\Phi,\psi)\in\mathscr{S}
.

Let
\psi\in\mathscr{S}_{0}
be given. Then since
\nabla\psi\in\mathscr{S}_{0}
,
\Lambda^{2\alpha}\psi\in\mathscr{S}_{0}
, and
\Lambda^{2\alpha}\nabla\psi=\nabla\Lambda^{2\alpha}\psi
, we have

\left<{p_{*}},\Delta\psi\right>=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}\cdot\Lambda^{2\alpha}\nabla\psi\,d{x}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}\cdot\nabla(\Lambda^{2\alpha}\psi)\,d{x}=0.

Since
\Delta=-\Lambda^{2}:\mathscr{S}_{0}\rightarrow\mathscr{S}_{0}
is an isomorphism, it follows from Lemma
2.1
that
{p_{*}}
is a polynomial. Hence, for all
\Phi\in\mathscr{S}_{0}
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}\cdot\Lambda^{2\alpha}\Phi\,d{x}=\left<{p_{*}},\Div\Phi\right>=0.

Therefore, by Lemma
2.1
again,
\bm{u}
is a polynomial. It is easy to check that polynomials in
L_{1}+L_{\infty}
are constant and the only constant function belonging to
L_{q,\infty}
is the zero function. This completes the proof of Lemma
3.8
.
∎

The following theorem shows existence and uniqueness of very weak solutions in
L_{q}
of (
3.1
) when
\mathbf{F}\in L_{p}
, where
1<p<d/(2\alpha-1)
and
1/q=1/p-(2\alpha-1)/d
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
1/2<\alpha<(d+1)/2
,
1<p<d/(2\alpha-1)
, and
1/q=1/p-(2\alpha-1)/d
.
Then for each
\mathbf{F}\in L_{p}
, there exists a unique very weak solution
\bm{u}
in
L_{q}
of (
3.1
). Moreover, the solution
\bm{u}
is given by
\bm{u}=\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}*\mathbf{F}
and satisfies

\|{\bm{u}}\|_{{q}}\apprle\|{\mathbf{F}}\|_{{p}}.

In addition, if
\mathbf{F}\in\dot{H}^{1-\alpha}
, then
\bm{u}
belongs to
\dot{H}^{\alpha}
and satisfies the energy identity (
3.4
).

Proof.

Define
\bm{u}=\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}*\mathbf{F}
. Then since
\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}\in L_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}
, it follows from Lemma
2.5
that
\bm{u}\in L_{q}
and
\|{\bm{u}}\|_{{q}}\apprle\|{\mathbf{F}}\|_{{p}}
.

We now show that
\bm{u}
is a very weak solution of (
3.1
).
To show this, choose a sequence
\{\mathbf{F}_{l}\}
in
\mathscr{S}_{0}
such that
\mathbf{F}_{l}\rightarrow\mathbf{F}
in
L_{p}
.
By Lemma
3.1
, there exists a unique solution
(\bm{u}_{l},p_{*,l})\in\mathscr{S}_{0}\times\mathscr{S}_{0}
of (
3.1
) with
\mathbf{F}=\mathbf{F}_{l}
. Moreover, it was shown in Subsection 3.2 that

\bm{u}_{l}=\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}*\mathbf{F}_{l}.

For all
(\Phi,\psi)\in\mathscr{S}_{0}
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}_{l}\cdot\Lambda^{2\alpha}\Phi\,d{x}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\Lambda^{2\alpha}\bm{u}_{l})\cdot\Phi\,d{x}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mathbf{F}_{l}:\nabla\Phi+\nabla p_{*,l}\cdot\Phi)\,d{x}

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}_{l}\cdot\nabla\psi\,d{x}=0.

Since
\bm{u}_{l},{p_{*}}_{l},\mathbf{F}_{l}\in\mathscr{S}_{0}
, it follows from Lemma
2.2
that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}_{l}\cdot\left(\Lambda^{2\alpha}\Phi+\nabla\psi\right)dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mathbf{F}_{l}:\nabla\Phi+\nabla p_{*,l}\cdot\Phi)\,d{x}

for any
(\Phi,\psi)\in\mathscr{S}
. Hence for all
\Phi\in C_{c,\sigma}^{\infty}
and
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}_{l}\cdot\left(\Lambda^{2\alpha}\Phi+\nabla\psi\right)dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{F}_{l}:\nabla\Phi\,d{x}.

Since
\mathbf{F}_{l}\rightarrow\mathbf{F}
in
L_{p}
, it follows from Lemma
2.5
that
\bm{u}_{l}\rightarrow\bm{u}
in
L_{q}
. Therefore, for all
\Phi\in C_{c,\sigma}^{\infty}
and
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}\cdot\left(\Lambda^{2\alpha}\Phi+\nabla\psi\right)dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{F}:\nabla\Phi\,d{x}.

Hence
\bm{u}
is a very weak solution of (
3.1
), which is unique due to Lemma
3.8
.

To complete the proof, suppose in addition that
\mathbf{F}\in\dot{H}^{1-\alpha}
. Then by Lemma
2.2
, there is a sequence
\{\mathbf{G}_{l}\}
in
\mathscr{S}_{0}
such that
\mathbf{G}_{l}\rightarrow\mathbf{F}
in both
L_{p}
and
\dot{H}^{1-\alpha}
. By Lemma
3.1
, there exists a unique solution
(\bm{v}_{l},\tilde{p}_{*,l})\in\mathscr{S}_{0}\times\mathscr{S}_{0}
of (
3.1
) with
\mathbf{F}=\mathbf{G}_{l}
. Moreover, by Theorem
3.3
,
\{\bm{v}_{l}\}
converges in
\dot{H}^{\alpha}
to some
\bm{v}\in\dot{H}^{\alpha}
satisfying

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\Lambda^{\alpha}\bm{v}|^{2}\,d{x}=\left<\Div\mathbf{F},\bm{v}\right>.

On the other hand, it was already shown that

\bm{v}_{l}=\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}*\mathbf{G}_{l}
and
\bm{v}_{l}\to\bm{u}
in
L_{q}
.
Hence, for all
\Phi\in\mathscr{S}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}\cdot\Lambda^{\alpha}\Phi\,dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{l\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{v}_{l}\cdot\Lambda^{\alpha}\Phi\,dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{l\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda^{\alpha}\bm{v}_{l}\cdot\Phi\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda^{\alpha}\bm{v}\cdot\Phi\,dx,

which implies that
\Lambda^{\alpha}\bm{u}=\Lambda^{\alpha}\bm{v}
in
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
. Since
\Lambda^{\alpha}:\mathscr{S}_{0}\rightarrow\mathscr{S}_{0}
is an isomorphism, we also have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}\cdot\Phi\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{v}\cdot\Phi\,dx\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,\Phi\in\mathscr{S}_{0}.

Therefore,

\left<{\rm div}\,\mathbf{F},\bm{u}\right>=\lim_{l\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}\cdot{\rm div}\,\mathbf{G}_{l}\,dx=\lim_{l\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{v}\cdot{\rm div}\,\mathbf{G}_{l}\,dx=\left<{\rm div}\,\mathbf{F},\bm{v}\right>.

This shows that
\bm{u}
satisfies the energy identity (
3.4
). The proof of Theorem
3.9
is completed.
∎

The following theorem shows existence and uniqueness of very weak solutions in
L_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}
of (
3.1
) when
\mathbf{F}\in L_{1}
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
1/2<\alpha<(d+1)/2
. Then for each
\mathbf{F}\in L_{1}
,
there exists a unique very weak solution
\bm{u}
of (
3.1
) in
L_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}
. Moreover, the solution
\bm{u}
is given by

\bm{u}=\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}*\mathbf{F}
and satisfies

\|{\bm{u}}\|_{{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}}\apprle\|{{\mathbf{F}}}\|_{{1}}.

Proof.

Given
\mathbf{F}\in L_{1}
, we define
\bm{u}=\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}*\mathbf{F}
. Then it follows from Lemma
2.5
that
\bm{u}\in L_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}
and

\|{\bm{u}}\|_{{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}}\apprle\|{{\mathbf{F}}}\|_{{1}}
. Hence by Lemma
3.8
, it remains to show that
\bm{u}
is a very weak solution of (
3.1
).

Fixing any
r
with
1<r<d/(2\alpha-1)
, we choose a sequence
\{\mathbf{G}_{l}\}
in
L_{1}\cap L_{r}
such that
\mathbf{G}_{l}\rightarrow\mathbf{F}
in
L_{1}
. Then by Theorem
3.9
,
\bm{v}_{l}=\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}*\mathbf{G}_{l}
satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{v}_{l}\cdot(\Lambda^{2\alpha}\Phi+\nabla\psi)\,d{x}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{G}_{l}:\nabla\Phi\,d{x}

for all
\Phi\in C_{c,\sigma}^{\infty}
and
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}
. By Lemma
2.5
again,
\bm{v}_{l}\rightarrow\bm{u}
in
L_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}
. Hence for all
\Phi\in C_{c,\sigma}^{\infty}
and
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}
, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}\cdot(\Lambda^{2\alpha}\Phi+\nabla\psi)\,d{x}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{F}:\nabla\Phi\,d{x}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
3.10
.
∎

4.
Perturbed fractional heat equations

For
0<T<\infty
, we consider the following initial value problem for perturbed fractional heat equations in
\mathbb{R}^{d},d\geq 2
:

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\bm{b}+\Lambda^{2\beta}\bm{b}+\Div\hskip 1.00006pt(\bm{u}\otimes\bm{b})&=\Div\mathbf{F}&&\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T),\\
\bm{b}(\cdot,0)&=\bm{b}_{0}&&\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\beta
is a positive number and
\bm{u}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a given vector field with
\Div\bm{u}=0
.

By a
weak solution
of (
4.1
), we mean a vector field
\bm{b}\in L_{\infty}(0,T;L_{2})\cap L_{2}(0,T;{H}^{\beta})
satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{b}\cdot(-\partial_{t}\Phi+\Lambda^{2\beta}\Phi)\,d{x}dt\,

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\bm{u}\otimes\bm{b}):\nabla\Phi\,d{x}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{F}:\nabla\Phi\,d{x}dt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{b}_{0}\cdot\Phi(0)\,dx

for all
\Phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T))
, provided that the integrals are all well-defined. Note that if
\beta\geq 1
, then by Proposition
2.6
,

(4.2)

\|{\nabla\bm{b}}\|_{{2}}\apprle\|{\bm{b}}\|_{{2}}^{1-1/\beta}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\bm{b}}\|_{{2}}^{1/\beta}.

Hence if
\beta\geq 1
, then every weak solution of (
4.1
) belongs to
L_{2\beta}(0,T;\dot{H}^{1})
.

This section consists of two parts. We first show that if
\mathbf{F}\in L_{2\beta/(2\beta-1)}(0,T;L_{2})
,
\beta\geq 1
, and
\bm{b}_{0}\in L_{2}
, then there exists a unique weak solution of (
4.1
) with
\bm{u}=0
satisfying the energy identity. Next, extending this result to nonzero
\bm{u}
, we prove that if
\beta\geq 1
and
\bm{u}\in L_{\infty}(0,T;L_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty})\cap L_{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{\alpha})
, then for every
\mathbf{F}\in L_{2\beta/(2\beta-1)}(0,T;L_{2})
and
\bm{b}_{0}\in L_{2}
, there exists a unique weak solution
\bm{b}
of (
4.1
). Moreover, the solution
\bm{b}
satisfies the energy identity, which will be used to prove uniqueness of weak solutions of the original nonlinear problem (
1.1
).

To prove existence and uniqueness of weak solutions of (
4.1
) when
\bm{u}=0
, we introduce the Fourier truncation operators
\mathcal{S}_{R}
(
R>0
) defined by

\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{R}f}(\xi)=\chi_{B_{R}}(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)\quad(f\in L_{2}),

where
\chi_{B_{R}}
is the characteristic function of
B_{R}
. The following properties of
\mathcal{S}_{R}
are quite elementary.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
0\leq s<t
.

(1)

If
f\in L_{2}
, then

\mathcal{S}_{R}f\in H^{s}
and
\|{\mathcal{S}_{R}f}\|_{{H}^{s}}\apprle(1+R)^{s}\|{f}\|_{{2}}
.

(2)

If
f\in{H}^{s}
, then
\mathcal{S}_{R}f\rightarrow f
in
{H}^{s}
as
R\rightarrow\infty

(3)

If
f\in H^{t}
, then

\|{\mathcal{S}_{R}f-f}\|_{{H}^{s}}\apprle(1+R)^{-(t-s)}\|{f}\|_{{H}^{t}}
.

For
R>0
, we denote by
V_{R}
the space of all vector fields
\bm{b}\in L_{2}
such that
\supp\hat{\bm{b}}\subset\overline{B_{R}}
. Note that
V_{R}
is a closed subspace of
L_{2}
. Moreover, since
\mathcal{S}_{R}\bm{b}=\bm{b}
for any
\bm{b}\in V_{R}
, it follows from Lemma
4.1
that
V_{R}
is continuously embedded into
{H}^{s}
for any
s\geq 0
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose that
\beta\geq 1
and
\gamma=2\beta/(2\beta-1)
. Then for every
\mathbf{F}\in L_{\gamma}(0,T;L_{2})
and
\bm{b}_{0}\in L_{2}
, there exists a unique weak solution
\bm{b}
of (
4.1
) with
\bm{u}=0
. Moreover,
\bm{b}
belongs to
C([0,T];L_{2})
and satisfies the energy identity:

\|{\bm{b}(t)}\|_{{2}}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\bm{b}(s)}\|_{{2}}^{2}\,ds=-2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{F}:\nabla\bm{b}\,d{x}ds+\|{\bm{b}_{0}}\|_{2}^{2}

for
0\leq t\leq T
.

Proof.

Choose a sequence
\{\mathbf{F}_{k}\}
in
C([0,T];L_{2})
such that
\mathbf{F}_{k}\rightarrow\mathbf{F}
in
L_{\gamma}(0,T;L_{2})
and
\|{\mathbf{F}_{k}(t)}\|_{{2}}\leq\|{\mathbf{F}(t)}\|_{{2}}
for all
t
and
k
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the following truncated problem for
\bm{b}_{k}\in C^{1}([0,T);V_{k})
:

(4.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\bm{b}_{k}+\Lambda^{2\beta}\bm{b}_{k}&=\Div\mathcal{S}_{k}\mathbf{F}_{k}&&\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T)\\
\bm{b}_{k}(\cdot,0)&=\mathcal{S}_{k}\bm{b}_{0}&&\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{aligned}\right.

Since
\Lambda^{2\beta}:V_{k}\rightarrow V_{k}
is a bounded linear operator, it follows from a standard theory of linear differential equations (see e.g. Cartan
[
10
, Theorem 1.9.1]
) in Banach spaces that the problem (
4.3
) has a unique solution
\bm{b}_{k}\in C^{1}([0,T];V_{k})
.

Given
k,m\in\mathbb{N}
, write
\tilde{\bm{b}}=\bm{b}_{k}-\bm{b}_{m}
,
\tilde{\mathbf{F}}=\mathcal{S}_{k}\mathbf{F}_{k}-\mathcal{S}_{m}\mathbf{F}_{m}
, and
\tilde{\bm{b}}_{0}=\mathcal{S}_{k}\bm{b}_{0}-\mathcal{S}_{m}\bm{b}_{0}
. Then
\tilde{\bm{b}}
satisfies

(4.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\tilde{\bm{b}}+\Lambda^{2\beta}\tilde{\bm{b}}&=\Div\tilde{\mathbf{F}}&&\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T)\\
\tilde{\bm{b}}(\cdot,0)&=\tilde{\bm{b}}_{0}&&\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{aligned}\right.

Multiplying (
4.4
) by
\tilde{\bm{b}}
and then integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,t]
, we have

\frac{1}{2}\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}(t)}\|_{{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\tilde{\bm{b}}(s)}\|_{{2}}^{2}\,\,d{s}=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\mathbf{F}}:\nabla\tilde{\bm{b}}\,\,d{x}ds+\frac{1}{2}\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}_{0}}\|_{{2}}^{2}

for all
0\leq t\leq T
. By Lemma
4.1
, (
4.2
), and Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\mathbf{F}}:\nabla\tilde{\bm{b}}\,d{x}

\displaystyle\leq C\|{\tilde{\mathbf{F}}}\|_{{2}}\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{1-1/\beta}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{1/\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C\|{\tilde{\mathbf{F}}}\|_{{2}}^{\gamma}\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{2(\beta-1)/(2\beta-1)}+\frac{1}{2}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{2}.

Since
0\leq{(\beta-1)}/{(2\beta-1)}<1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}(t)}\|_{{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\tilde{\bm{b}}(s)}\|_{{2}}^{2}\,d{s}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}_{0}}\|_{{2}}^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}\|{\tilde{\mathbf{F}}(s)}\|_{{2}}^{\gamma}\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}(s)}\|_{{2}}^{2(\beta-1)/(2\beta-1)}\,d{s}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}_{0}}\|_{{2}}^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}\|{\tilde{\mathbf{F}}(s)}\|_{{2}}^{\gamma}\left(1+\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}(s)}\|_{{2}}^{2}\right)ds

for all
0\leq t\leq T
. Hence by Gronwall’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|{\bm{b}_{k}(t)-\bm{b}_{m}(t)}\|_{{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}(\bm{b}_{k}-\bm{b}_{m})(t)}\|_{{2}}^{2}\,d{t}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\|\mathcal{S}_{k}\bm{b}_{0}-\mathcal{S}_{m}\bm{b}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+C\int_{0}^{T}\|{\mathcal{S}_{k}\mathbf{F}_{k}(t)-\mathcal{S}_{m}\mathbf{F}_{m}(t)}\|_{{2}}^{\gamma}\,d{t}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\exp\left(C\int_{0}^{T}\|{\mathcal{S}_{k}\mathbf{F}_{k}(t)-\mathcal{S}_{m}\mathbf{F}_{m}(t)}\|_{{2}}^{\gamma}\,d{t}\right)

for some constant
C
depending only on
d
,
\beta
, and
T
.
By the dominated convergence theorem, we easily show that
\mathcal{S}_{k}\mathbf{F}_{k}\rightarrow\mathbf{F}
in
L_{\gamma}(0,T;L_{2})
. Therefore, the sequence
\{\bm{b}_{k}\}
converges in
C([0,T];L_{2})\cap L_{2}(0,T;{H}^{\beta})
to a function
\bm{b}
. It is easy to check that
\bm{b}
is a weak solution of (
4.1
) with
\bm{u}=0
. To show that
\bm{b}
satisfies the energy identity, we multiply (
4.3
) by
\bm{b}_{k}
to obtain

(4.5)

\frac{1}{2}\|{{\bm{b}}_{k}(t)}\|_{{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}{\bm{b}}_{k}(s)}\|_{{2}}^{2}\,\,d{s}=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{S}_{k}\mathbf{F}_{k}:\nabla{\bm{b}}_{k}\,d{x}ds+\frac{1}{2}\|{\mathcal{S}_{k}\bm{b}_{0}}\|_{{2}}^{2}.

By the interpolation inequality (
4.2
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|{\nabla(\bm{b}_{k}-\bm{b})}\|_{{2}}^{2\beta}\,d{t}\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\|{\bm{b}_{k}-\bm{b}}\|_{{2}}^{2(\beta-1)}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}(\bm{b}_{k}-\bm{b})}\|_{{2}}^{2}\,d{t}\rightarrow 0.

Hence the energy identity follows from (
4.5
) by letting
k\rightarrow\infty
.

Finally, to show the uniqueness, let
\bm{b}
be a weak solution of (
4.1
) with
(\bm{u},\mathbf{F})=(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0})
. Then the truncations
\bm{b}_{k}=\mathcal{S}_{k}\bm{b}
satisfy

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\bm{b}_{k}+\Lambda^{2\beta}\bm{b}_{k}&=\mathbf{0}&&\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T),\\
\bm{b}_{k}(\cdot,0)&=\mathbf{0}&&\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{aligned}\right.

It is easy to see that
\bm{b}_{k}
is identically zero. Since
\bm{b}_{k}\to\bm{b}
in
L_{2}(0,T;L_{2})
, we conclude that
\bm{b}
is identically zero. This completes the proof of Lemma
4.2
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
satisfy

\frac{1}{2}<\alpha<\frac{d+1}{2},\quad\beta\geq 1,\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha+\beta\geq\frac{d}{2}+1.

(1)

For all
\bm{u}\in L_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}\cap\dot{H}^{\alpha}
and
\bm{v},\bm{w}\in{H}^{\beta}
, we have

(4.6)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\bm{u}|\,|\bm{v}|\,|\nabla\bm{w}|\,d{x}\apprle(\|{\bm{u}}\|_{{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}}+\|{\bm{u}}\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}})\|{\bm{v}}\|_{{H}^{\beta}}\|{\bm{w}}\|_{{H}^{\beta}}.

Moreover, if
\Div\bm{u}=0
, then

(4.7)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}\otimes\bm{v}:\nabla\bm{w}\,d{x}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla)\bm{v}\cdot\bm{w}\,d{x}.

(2)

If
\bm{u}\in L_{\infty}(0,T;L_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty})\cap L_{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{\alpha})
and
\bm{b}\in L_{\infty}(0,T;L_{2})\cap L_{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{\beta})
, then

\bm{b}\in L_{2\beta}(0,T;\dot{H}^{1})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\bm{u}\otimes\bm{b}\in L_{2\beta/(2\beta-1)}(0,T;L_{2}).

Moreover, if
\Div\bm{u}=0
, then

(4.8)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}(t)\otimes\bm{b}(t):\nabla\bm{b}(t)\,d{x}=0\quad\text{for almost all }t\in(0,T)

Proof.

(1) Let
\gamma=2\beta/(2\beta-1)
. Then by Lemma
2.8
,
there exist
0<\theta_{1},\theta_{2}<1
with
\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}\leq 2-1/\beta=2/\gamma
such that

(4.9)

\|{|\bm{u}|\,|\bm{v}|}\|_{{2}}\apprle\|{\bm{u}}\|_{{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}}^{1-\theta_{1}}\|{\bm{u}}\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}^{\theta_{1}}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{{2}}^{1-\theta_{2}}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{\dot{H}^{\beta}}^{\theta_{2}}.

By Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\bm{u}|\,|\bm{v}|\,|\nabla\bm{w}|\,d{x}

\displaystyle\leq\|{|\bm{u}|\,|\bm{v}|}\|_{{2}}\|{\nabla\bm{w}}\|_{{2}}

\displaystyle\apprle(\|{\bm{u}}\|_{{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}}+\|{\bm{u}}\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}})\|{\bm{v}}\|_{{H}^{\beta}}\|{\bm{w}}\|_{{H}^{\beta}},

which proves (
4.6
).

To show (
4.7
), choose sequences
\{\bm{v}_{k}\}
and
\{\bm{w}_{k}\}
in
C_{c}^{\infty}
satisfying
\bm{v}_{k}\rightarrow\bm{v}
and
\bm{w}_{k}\rightarrow\bm{w}
in
{H}^{\beta}
. Since
\Div\bm{u}=0
, it follows that

0=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}\cdot\nabla(\bm{v}_{k}\cdot\bm{w}_{k})\,d{x}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla)\bm{v}_{k}\cdot\bm{w}_{k}\,d{x}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}\otimes\bm{v}_{k}:\nabla\bm{w}_{k}\,d{x}

for all
k
. Hence the identity (
4.7
) is derived by letting
k\rightarrow\infty
and using (
4.6
).

(2) It immediately follows from (
4.2
) that
\bm{b}\in L_{2\beta}(0,T;\dot{H}^{1})
. Let
1<r\leq\infty
be defined by

\frac{1}{r}=1-\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}\right).

Then by (
4.9
) and Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|{\bm{u}\otimes\bm{b}}\|_{{2}}^{\gamma}\,d{t}

\displaystyle\apprle\int_{0}^{T}\|{\bm{u}}\|_{{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}}^{\gamma(1-\theta_{1})}\|{\bm{u}}\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}^{\gamma\theta_{1}}\|{\bm{b}}\|_{{2}}^{\gamma(1-\theta_{2})}\|{\bm{b}}\|_{\dot{H}^{\beta}}^{\gamma\theta_{2}}\,d{t}

\displaystyle\apprle\int_{0}^{T}\|{\bm{u}}\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}^{\gamma\theta_{1}}\|{\bm{b}}\|_{\dot{H}^{\beta}}^{\gamma\theta_{2}}\,d{t}

\displaystyle\apprle T^{1/r}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|{\bm{u}}\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}^{2}\,d{t}\right)^{\gamma\theta_{1}/2}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|{\bm{b}}\|_{\dot{H}^{\beta}}^{2}\,d{t}\right)^{\gamma\theta_{2}/2}.

Finally, the identity (
4.8
) is obtained by taking
\bm{v}=\bm{w}=\bm{b}
in (
4.7
). This completes the proof of Lemma
4.3
.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
satisfy

\frac{1}{2}<\alpha<\frac{d+1}{2},\quad\beta\geq 1,\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha+\beta\geq\frac{d}{2}+1.

Suppose that

\bm{u}\in L_{\infty}(0,T;L_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty})\cap L_{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{\alpha})
and
\Div\bm{u}=0
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T)
. Then for every
\mathbf{F}\in L_{2\beta/(2\beta-1)}(0,T;L_{2})
and
\bm{b}_{0}\in L_{2}
, there exists a unique weak solution
\bm{b}
of (
4.1
). Moreover, the solution
\bm{b}
belongs to
C([0,T];L_{2})
and satisfies the energy identity:

(4.10)

\|{\bm{b}(t)}\|_{{2}}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\bm{b}(s)}\|_{{2}}^{2}\,ds=-2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{F}:\nabla\bm{b}\,d{x}ds+\|{\bm{b}_{0}}\|_{{2}}^{2}

for
0\leq t\leq T
.

Proof.

We first show that every weak solution
\bm{b}
of (
4.1
) belongs to
C([0,T];L_{2})
and satisfies the energy identity (
4.10
). Let
\bm{b}
be a weak solution of (
4.1
) with
\mathbf{F}\in L_{2\beta/(2\beta-1)}(0,T;L_{2})
. Then
\bm{b}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\bm{b}+\Lambda^{2\beta}\bm{b}&=\Div\tilde{\mathbf{F}}&&\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T),\\
\bm{b}(\cdot,0)&=\bm{b}_{0}&&\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\tilde{\mathbf{F}}=\mathbf{F}-\bm{u}\otimes\bm{b}
. By Lemma
4.3
, we have

\tilde{\mathbf{F}}\in L_{2\beta/(2\beta-1)}(0,T;L_{2})\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\mathbf{F}}:\nabla\bm{b}\,d{x}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{F}:\nabla\bm{b}\,d{x}.

Hence it follows from Lemma
4.2
that
\bm{b}
belongs to
C([0,T];L_{2})
and satisfies the energy identity (
4.10
).

Uniqueness of weak solutions of (
4.1
) is an immediate consequence of the energy identity. Indeed, if
\bm{b}
is a weak solution of (
4.1
) with
(\mathbf{F},\bm{b}_{0})=(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0})
, then since it satisfies the energy identity, we have

\|{\bm{b}(t)}\|_{{2}}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\bm{b}(s)}\|_{{2}}^{2}\,\,d{s}=0

for all
0\leq t\leq T
, which implies that
\bm{b}
is identically zero.

To complete the proof, it remains to prove existence of a weak solution of (
4.1
). To prove this, we may consider truncated problems as in the proof of Lemma
4.2
. The resulting truncated problems are still linear but non-autonomous with coefficients being singular in time. To avoid this difficulty, we apply the Galerkin approximation method. Choose a sequence
\{\Phi_{k}\}
in
\mathscr{S}
such that the span of
\{\Phi_{k}\}
is dense in
{H}^{\beta}
and
\{\Phi_{k}\}
is orthonormal in
L_{2}
. Then for a fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we seek a function
\bm{b}_{m}
of the form

\bm{b}_{m}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}c_{k}^{m}(t)\Phi_{k}

that satisfies

\bm{b}_{m}(0)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(\bm{b}_{0},\Phi_{j}\right)

and

(4.11)

\left(\partial_{t}\bm{b}_{m}(t),\Phi_{j}\right)+\left(\Lambda^{\beta}\bm{b}_{m}(t),\Lambda^{\beta}\Phi_{j}\right)-\left(\bm{u}(t)\otimes\bm{b}_{m}(t),\nabla\Phi_{j}\right)=-\left(\mathbf{F}(t),\nabla\Phi_{j}\right)

for
1\leq j\leq m
. Equivalently, since
\{\Phi_{k}\}
is orthonormal in
L_{2}
, we look for a vector function
\bm{c}^{m}=(c_{1}^{m},\dots,c_{m}^{m}):[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
satisfying

(4.12)

\frac{d}{dt}\bm{c}^{m}(t)+A(t)\bm{c}^{m}(t)=\bm{f}(t)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\bm{c}^{m}(0)=\bm{c}_{0},

where
A(t)=[A_{jk}(t)]_{1\leq j,k\leq m}
,
\bm{f}(t)=(f_{1}(t),\ldots,f_{m}(t))
, and
\bm{c}_{0}=(c_{0,1},\ldots,c_{0,m})
are defined by

A_{jk}(t)=\left(\Lambda^{\beta}\Phi_{k},\Lambda^{\beta}\Phi_{j}\right)-\left(\bm{u}(t)\otimes\Phi_{k},\nabla\Phi_{j}\right),

f_{j}(t)=-\left(\mathbf{F}(t),\nabla\Phi_{j}\right),\quad\mbox{and}\quad c_{0,j}=\left(\bm{b}_{0},\Phi_{j}\right)

for
1\leq j,k\leq m
.
By Lemma
4.3
, we have

|A(t)|\apprle\left(1+\|{\bm{u}(t)}\|_{{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}}+\|{\bm{u}(t)}\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}\right)\|{\Phi_{k}}\|_{H^{\beta}}\|{\Phi_{j}}\|_{H^{\beta}},

which implies that
A\in L_{2}(0,T)
. Moreover, since
\bm{f}\in L_{2\beta/(2\beta-1)}(0,T)
, it follows from a standard theory of ordinary differential equations (ODEs) that there exists a unique absolutely continuous function
\bm{c}^{m}:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
satisfying (
4.12
).

Multiplying (
4.11
) by
c_{j}^{m}
and taking summation over
1\leq j\leq m
, we get

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|{\bm{b}_{m}}\|_{{2}}^{2}+\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\bm{b}_{m}}\|_{{2}}^{2}-\left(\bm{u}\otimes\bm{b}_{m},\nabla\bm{b}_{m}\right)=-\left(\mathbf{F},\nabla\bm{b}_{m}\right).

By Lemma
4.3
, (
4.2
), and Young’s inequality,

\left(\bm{u}\otimes\bm{b}_{m},\nabla\bm{b}_{m}\right)=0

and

\displaystyle-\left(\mathbf{F},\nabla\bm{b}_{m}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\|{\mathbf{F}}\|_{{2}}\|{\bm{b}_{m}}\|_{{2}}^{1-1/\beta}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\bm{b}_{m}}\|_{{2}}^{1/\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C\|{\mathbf{F}}\|_{{2}}^{\gamma}\|{\bm{b}_{m}}\|_{{2}}^{2(\beta-1)/(2\beta-1)}+\frac{1}{2}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\bm{b}_{m}}\|_{{2}}^{2}.

Since
0\leq(\beta-1)/(2\beta-1)<1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|{\bm{b}_{m}}\|_{{2}}^{2}+\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\bm{b}_{m}}\|_{{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2C\|{\mathbf{F}}\|_{{2}}^{\gamma}\left(1+\|{\bm{b}_{m}}\|_{{2}}^{2}\right).

Hence, by Gronwall’s inequality,

\displaystyle\sup_{0<t<T}\|{\bm{b}_{m}(t)}\|_{{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\bm{b}_{m}(t)}\|_{{2}}^{2}\,\,d{t}\leq\|{\bm{b}_{0}}\|_{{2}}^{2}\,\exp\left(C\int_{0}^{T}\|{\mathbf{F}(t)}\|_{{2}}^{\gamma}\,\,d{t}\right)

for some constant
C
depending only on
d
,
\beta
, and
T
. This shows that the sequence
\{\bm{b}_{m}\}
is uniformly bounded in
L_{\infty}(0,T;L_{2})\cap L_{2}(0,T;{H}^{\beta})
. Therefore, by weak-* compactness, there exist a subsequence
\{\bm{b}_{m_{k}}\}
and a function
\bm{b}
such that

\bm{b}_{m_{k}}\rightarrow\bm{b}\quad\text{weakly-* in }L_{\infty}(0,T;L_{2})\cap L_{2}(0,T;{H}^{\beta}).

It is quite standard to show that
\bm{b}
is a weak solution of (
4.1
).
The proof of Theorem
4.4
is complete.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

This section is fully devoted to proving Theorem
1.2
.
Throughout the remaining sections, we assume that
\nu=\eta=1
for simplicity.

5.1.
Approximation of the problem by truncations

Let
\mathbb{P}
be the Leray projection defined by

\widehat{\mathbb{P}(\bm{u})}(\xi)=\left(I-\frac{\xi\otimes\xi}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)\widehat{\bm{u}}(\xi).

It is well-known that the Leray projection
\mathbb{P}
has the following properties:

(1)

\Div\mathbb{P}(\bm{u})=0
for all
\bm{u}\in\mathscr{S}
;

(2)

\mathbb{P}(\nabla\phi)=0
for all
\phi\in\mathscr{S}
;

(3)

\mathbb{P}(\bm{u})=\bm{u}
for all
\bm{u}\in\mathscr{S}
with
\Div\bm{u}=0
;

(4)

\mathbb{P}[\mathbb{P}(\bm{u})]=\mathbb{P}(\bm{u})
for all
\bm{u}\in\mathscr{S}
;

(5)

\mathbb{P}
is bounded from
L_{q}
to itself for
1<q<\infty
.

Note also that
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{S}_{R}(\bm{u})\in V_{R,\sigma}
for all
\bm{u}\in L_{2}
, where
V_{R,\sigma}
is the space of all vector fields
\bm{v}\in V_{R}
such that
\Div\bm{v}=0
.

The following lemma is necessary to construct approximate solutions of (
1.1
).

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
1/2<\alpha<(d+1)/2
. Given
\bm{b}\in L_{2}
, we define

\bm{u}=\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}*(\bm{b}\otimes\bm{b}),

where
\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}=[U_{\alpha,j}^{kl}]_{1\leq j,k,l\leq d}
is defined by (
3.5
). Then

(5.1)

\bm{u}\in L_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|{\bm{u}}\|_{{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}}\apprle\|{\bm{b}}\|_{{2}}^{2}.

Moreover, if
\bm{b}\in V_{R}
, then for any
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
and
d/(d+1-2\alpha)<q<\infty
,

(5.2)

D^{\gamma}\bm{u}\in L_{q},\quad\|{D^{\gamma}\bm{u}}\|_{q}\apprle_{R}\|{\bm{b}}\|_{{2}}^{2},

and

(5.3)

\|{D^{\gamma}(\bm{u}\otimes\bm{b})}\|_{{2}}+\|{D^{\gamma}(\bm{b}\otimes\bm{u})}\|_{{2}}\apprle_{R}\|{\bm{b}}\|_{{2}}^{3}.

Proof.

Set
\mathbf{F}=\bm{b}\otimes\bm{b}
. Then since
\mathbf{F}\in L_{1}
and
\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}\in L_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}
, the estimate (
5.1
) immediately follows from Lemma
2.5
. Suppose in addition that
\bm{b}\in V_{R}
. Since
V_{R}\hookrightarrow{H}^{s}
and
{H}^{d+s}\hookrightarrow L_{\infty}
for any
s>0
, it follows from Hölder’s inequality that

\|D^{\gamma}\mathbf{F}\|_{L_{1}\cap L_{\infty}}\apprle_{R}\|{\bm{b}}\|_{{2}}^{2}\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,\gamma\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}.

Let
\gamma\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}
be fixed. Then since
D^{\gamma}\bm{u}=\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}*D^{\gamma}\mathbf{F}
,
it follows from Lemma
2.5
that

\|D^{\gamma}\bm{u}\|_{q}\apprle\|D^{\gamma}\mathbf{F}\|_{L_{1}\cap L_{\infty}}\apprle_{R}\|{\bm{b}}\|_{{2}}^{2}

for any
d/(d+1-2\alpha)<q<\infty
. Finally, since there exist
p
and
q
such that

2<p<\infty,\quad\frac{d}{d+1-2\alpha}<q<\infty,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q},

the estimate (
5.3
) is easily deduced from (
5.2
) by using Hölder’s inequality.
This completes the proof of Lemma
5.1
.
∎

By virtue of Lemma
5.1
, we may consider the following
truncated problem for
\bm{b}_{R}\in C^{1}([0,\infty);V_{R,\sigma})
:

(5.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\bm{b}_{R}+\Lambda^{2\beta}\bm{b}_{R}&=\mathbb{P}\mathcal{S}_{R}[\Div\hskip 1.00006pt(\bm{b}_{R}\otimes\bm{u}_{R}-\bm{u}_{R}\otimes\bm{b}_{R})]&&\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty),\\
\bm{u}_{R}&=\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}*(\bm{b}_{R}\otimes\bm{b}_{R})&&\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty),\\
\bm{b}_{R}(\cdot,0)&=\mathbb{P}\mathcal{S}_{R}\bm{b}_{0}&&\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\bm{b}_{0}\in L_{2}
with
{\rm div}\,\bm{b}_{0}=0
is given. Note here that if
\bm{b}_{R}\in C^{1}([0,T);V_{R,\sigma})
,
0<T\leq\infty
, and
\bm{u}_{R}=\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}*(\bm{b}_{R}\otimes\bm{b}_{R})
, then by Lemma
5.1
,

\bm{u}_{R}\in C([0,T);L_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty})

and

(5.5)

\|{\bm{u}_{R}(t)}\|_{{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}}\apprle\|{\bm{b}_{R}(t)}\|_{{2}}^{2}\quad\mbox{for}\,\,0\leq t<T.

Moreover, it follows from Theorem
3.10
that for each
t\in(0,T)
,
\bm{u}_{R}(t)
is a very weak solution of the fractional Stokes system

(5.6)

\Lambda^{2\alpha}\bm{u}_{R}(t)+\nabla{p_{*}}(t)=\Div\hskip 1.00006pt(\bm{b}_{R}(t)\otimes\bm{b}_{R}(t)),\quad\Div\bm{u}_{R}(t)=0\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{d};

that is,
\bm{u}_{R}(t)
satisfies

(5.7)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{u}_{R}(t)\cdot\left(\Lambda^{2\alpha}\Phi+\nabla\psi\right)dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{b}_{R}(t)\otimes\bm{b}_{R}(t):\nabla\Phi\,d{x}

for all
(\Phi,\psi)\in\mathscr{S}
with
\Div\Phi=0
. We shall show that for each
R>0
, there exists a unique solution
\bm{b}_{R}\in C^{1}([0,\infty);V_{R,\sigma})
of the
R
-truncated problem (
5.4
) and then that some sequence
\{(\bm{u}_{R_{k}},\bm{b}_{R_{k}})\}
converges to a weak solution of the original problem (
1.1
).

5.2.
Existence of approximate solutions

Let
0<R<\infty
be fixed. For
\bm{b}\in V_{R,\sigma}
, we define

G_{R}(\bm{b})=-\Lambda^{2\beta}\bm{b}+\mathbb{P}\mathcal{S}_{R}[\Div\hskip 1.00006pt(\bm{b}\otimes\bm{u}-\bm{u}\otimes\bm{b})],

where
\bm{u}=\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}*(\bm{b}\otimes\bm{b})
. By Lemma
5.1
,
G_{R}
is a well-defined mapping from
V_{R,\sigma}
into
V_{R,\sigma}
. Therefore, the
R
-truncated problem (
5.4
) can be rewritten as the following Cauchy problem on an ODE in the Hilbert space
V_{R,\sigma}
:

(5.8)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\bm{b}_{R}(t)&=G_{R}(\bm{b}_{R}(t))\quad(0<t<\infty),\\
\bm{b}_{R}(0)&=\mathbb{P}\mathcal{S}_{R}\bm{b}_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\bm{b}_{0}\in L_{2}
with
{\rm div}\,\bm{b}_{0}=0
is given.

Suppose that
\bm{b}^{i}\in V_{R,\sigma}
and
\bm{u}^{i}=\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}*(\bm{b}^{i}\otimes\bm{b}^{i})
for
i=1,2
. Then since
V_{R,\sigma}\hookrightarrow{H}^{s}\hookrightarrow\dot{H}^{s}
for any
s\geq 0
, we have

\|\Lambda^{2\beta}\bm{b}^{1}-\Lambda^{2\beta}\bm{b}^{2}\|_{2}\apprle_{R}\|\bm{b}^{1}-\bm{b}^{2}\|_{2}.

Following the proof of Lemma
5.1
, we also obtain

\displaystyle\|{\Div\hskip 1.00006pt(\bm{b}^{1}\otimes\bm{u}^{1}-\bm{b}^{2}\otimes\bm{u}^{2})}\|_{{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|{\Div\hskip 1.00006pt((\bm{b}^{1}-\bm{b}^{2})\otimes\bm{u}^{1})}\|_{{2}}+\|{\Div\hskip 1.00006pt(\bm{b}^{2}\otimes(\bm{u}^{1}-\bm{u}^{2}))}\|_{{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\apprle_{R}\|\bm{b}^{1}-\bm{b}^{2}\|_{2}\|\bm{b}^{1}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\bm{b}^{2}\|_{2}\left(\|\bm{b}^{1}\|_{2}+\|\bm{b}^{2}\|_{2}\right)\|\bm{b}^{1}-\bm{b}^{2}\|_{2}

and

\displaystyle\|{\Div\hskip 1.00006pt(\bm{u}^{1}\otimes\bm{b}^{1}-\bm{u}^{2}\otimes\bm{b}^{2})}\|_{{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|{\Div\hskip 1.00006pt((\bm{u}^{1}-\bm{u}^{2})\otimes\bm{b}^{1})}\|_{{2}}+\|{\Div\hskip 1.00006pt(\bm{u}^{2}\otimes(\bm{b}^{1}-\bm{b}^{2}))}\|_{{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\apprle_{R}\left(\|\bm{b}^{1}\|_{2}+\|\bm{b}^{2}\|_{2}\right)\|\bm{b}^{1}-\bm{b}^{2}\|_{2}\|\bm{b}^{1}\|_{2}+\|\bm{b}^{2}\|_{2}^{2}\|\bm{b}^{1}-\bm{b}^{2}\|_{2}.

Hence by the
L_{2}
-boundedness of
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{S}_{R}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|G_{R}(\bm{b}^{1})-G_{R}(\bm{b}^{2})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\apprle_{R}\left(1+\|\bm{b}^{1}\|_{2}+\|\bm{b}^{2}\|_{2}\right)^{2}\|\bm{b}^{1}-\bm{b}^{2}\|_{2},

which implies that
G_{R}
is locally Lipschitz continuous on
V_{R,\sigma}
. Therefore it follows from Picard’s theorem for ODEs in infinite dimensional spaces that for every
\bm{b}_{0}\in L_{2}
with
{\rm div}\,\bm{b}_{0}=0
, the
R
-truncated problem (
5.4
) has a unique local classical solution
\bm{b}_{R}\in C^{1}([0,T_{R});V_{R,\sigma})
. Here
0<T_{R}\leq\infty
denotes the maximal existence time of the local solution
\bm{b}_{R}
. Indeed,
\bm{b}_{R}
exists globally, that is,
T_{R}=\infty
. To show this, let
\bm{u}_{R}=\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}*(\bm{b}_{R}\otimes\bm{b}_{R})
. Since
V_{R,\sigma}\hookrightarrow{H}^{s}\hookrightarrow\dot{H}^{s}
and
(\dot{H}^{s})^{*}=\dot{H}^{-s}
for any
s\geq 0
, it follows that

\bm{b}_{R}\otimes\bm{b}_{R}\in C([0,T_{R});\dot{H}^{1-\alpha}).
Hence by Theorem
3.9
,
\bm{u}_{R}
belongs to
C([0,T_{R});\dot{H}^{\alpha})
and satisfies the energy identity

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\Lambda^{\alpha}\bm{u}_{R}(t)|^{2}\,d{x}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{b}_{R}(t)\otimes\bm{b}_{R}(t):\nabla\bm{u}_{R}(t)\,dx

for
0<t<T_{R}
. Multiply the
\bm{b}_{R}
-equation in (
5.4
) by
\bm{b}_{R}
and using the divergence-free condition, we get

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|{\bm{b}_{R}(t)}\|_{{2}}^{2}\right)+\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\bm{b}_{R}(t)}\|_{{2}}^{2}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{b}_{R}(t)\otimes\bm{u}_{R}(t):\nabla\bm{b}_{R}(t)\,dx

for
0<t<T_{R}
. Adding these two identities and using the divergence-free condition, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|{\bm{b}_{R}(t)}\|_{{2}}^{2}\right)+\|{\Lambda^{\alpha}\bm{u}_{R}(t)}\|_{{2}}^{2}+\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\bm{b}_{R}(t)}\|_{{2}}^{2}=0,

from which we derive the global energy estimate

(5.9)

\|{\bm{b}_{R}(t)}\|_{{2}}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\|{\Lambda^{\alpha}\bm{u}_{R}(\tau)}\|_{{2}}^{2}\,d\tau+2\int_{0}^{t}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\bm{b}_{R}(\tau)}\|_{{2}}^{2}\,d\tau\leq\|{\bm{b}_{0}}\|_{{2}}^{2}

for
0\leq t<T_{R}
.
Therefore, by a standard ODE theory, we conclude that
T_{R}=\infty
.

The following lemma will be used to construct weak solutions of (
1.1
) by applying the Aubin-Lions compactness lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

There exist
r>1
and
s\geq 1
such that
\{\partial_{t}\bm{b}_{R}\}
is bounded in
L_{r}(0,T;H^{-s})
for any
0<T<\infty
.

Proof.

To show this, let
s=\max\{\beta,1\}
. Then since
-s+1\leq 0
and
2\beta-s\leq\beta
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left<\partial_{t}\bm{b}_{R},\Phi\right>

\displaystyle=\left<-\Lambda^{2\beta}\bm{b}_{R},\Phi\right>-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\bm{b}_{R}\otimes\bm{u}_{R}-\bm{u}_{R}\otimes\bm{b}_{R}):\nabla(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{S}_{R}\Phi)\,d{x}

\displaystyle\apprle\|{\Lambda^{2\beta}\bm{b}_{R}}\|_{{H}^{-s}}\|{\Phi}\|_{{H}^{s}}+\|{|\bm{u}_{R}||\bm{b}_{R}|}\|_{{H}^{-s+1}}\|{\nabla(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{S}_{R}\Phi)}\|_{{H}^{s-1}}

\displaystyle\apprle\left(\|{\bm{b}_{R}}\|_{{H}^{2\beta-s}}+\|{|\bm{u}_{R}||\bm{b}_{R}|}\|_{{H}^{-s+1}}\right)\|{\Phi}\|_{{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\apprle\left(\|{\bm{b}_{R}}\|_{{H}^{\beta}}+\|{|\bm{u}_{R}||\bm{b}_{R}|}\|_{{2}}\right)\|{\Phi}\|_{{H}^{s}}

for all
\Phi\in\mathscr{S}
. Therefore,

(5.10)

\|{\partial_{t}\bm{b}_{R}(t)}\|_{{H}^{-s}}\apprle\|{\bm{b}_{R}}\|_{{H}^{\beta}}+\|{|\bm{u}_{R}||\bm{b}_{R}|}\|_{{2}}.

By Lemma
2.8
, there exist
0<\theta_{1},\theta_{2}<1
such that

\|{|\bm{u}_{R}||\bm{b}_{R}|}\|_{2}\apprle\|{\bm{u}_{R}}\|_{{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}}^{1-\theta_{1}}\|{\bm{u}_{R}}\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}^{\theta_{1}}\|{\bm{b}_{R}}\|_{{2}}^{1-\theta_{2}}\|{\bm{b}_{R}}\|_{\dot{H}^{\beta}}^{\theta_{2}}.

Setting
r_{0}=2/(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2})>1
and using Young’s inequality, we have

\|{|\bm{u}_{R}||\bm{b}_{R}|}\|_{2}\apprle\left(\|{\bm{u}_{R}}\|_{{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}}^{1-\theta_{1}}\|{\bm{u}_{R}}\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}^{\theta_{1}}\right)^{2/r_{0}\theta_{1}}+\left(\|{\bm{b}_{R}}\|_{{2}}^{1-\theta_{2}}\|{\bm{b}_{R}}\|_{\dot{H}^{\beta}}^{\theta_{2}}\right)^{2/r_{0}\theta_{2}}

and so

\|{|\bm{u}_{R}||\bm{b}_{R}|}\|_{2}^{r_{0}}\apprle\|{\bm{u}_{R}}\|_{{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}}^{2(1-\theta_{1})/\theta_{1}}\|{\Lambda^{\alpha}\bm{u}_{R}}\|_{{2}}^{2}+\|{\bm{b}_{R}}\|_{{2}}^{2(1-\theta_{2})/\theta_{2}}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\bm{b}_{R}}\|_{{2}}^{2}.

By (
5.5
) and (
5.9
), we thus obtain

\int_{0}^{\infty}\|{|\bm{u}_{R}||\bm{b}_{R}|}\|_{2}^{r_{0}}\,dt\apprle\|{\bm{b}_{0}}\|_{{2}}^{4/\theta_{1}-2}+\|{\bm{b}_{0}}\|_{{2}}^{2/\theta_{2}}.

Now, let
r=\min\{2,r_{0}\}
. Then by (
5.9
) and (
5.10
), we have

\displaystyle\|{\partial_{t}\bm{b}_{R}}\|_{L_{r}(0,T;{H}^{-s})}

\displaystyle\apprle T^{1/r-1/2}\|{\bm{b}_{R}}\|_{L_{2}(0,T;{H}^{\beta})}+T^{1/r-1/r_{0}}\|{\bm{u}_{R}\otimes\bm{b}_{R}}\|_{L_{r_{0}}(0,T;L_{2})}

\displaystyle\leq C(d,\alpha,\beta,T,\|{\bm{b}_{0}}\|_{{2}}),

which proves that
\{\partial_{t}\bm{b}_{R}\}
is bounded in
L_{r}(0,T;{H}^{-s})
for any
0<T<\infty
.
∎

5.3.
Completion of the proof of Theorem
1.2

We have shown that for each
0<R<\infty
, the
R
-truncated problem (
5.4
) has a unique global solution
\bm{b}_{R}\in C^{1}([0,\infty);V_{R,\sigma})
. By (
5.5
) and (
5.9
), the families
\{\bm{b}_{R}\}_{R>0}
and
\{\bm{u}_{R}\}_{R>0}
are bounded in
L_{\infty}(0,\infty;L_{2})\cap L_{2}(0,\infty;\dot{H}^{\beta})
and
L_{\infty}(0,\infty;L_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty})\cap L_{2}(0,\infty;\dot{H}^{\alpha})
, respectively.

By the weak-* compactness result and (
5.9
), there exists a sequence
\{R_{k}\}
with
R_{k}\rightarrow\infty
such that

\bm{u}_{R_{k}}\rightarrow\bm{u}\quad\mbox{weakly-* in}\,\,L_{\infty}(0,\infty;L_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty})\cap L_{2}(0,\infty;\dot{H}^{\alpha})

and

\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\rightarrow\bm{b}\quad\mbox{weakly-* in}\,\,L_{\infty}(0,\infty;L_{2})\cap L_{2}(0,\infty;\dot{H}^{\beta})

for some
(\bm{u},\bm{b})
satisfying

(5.11)

\|\bm{b}\|_{L_{\infty}(0,\infty;L_{2})}^{2}+\|\bm{b}\|_{L_{2}(0,\infty;\dot{H}^{\beta})}^{2}+\|\bm{u}\|_{L_{2}(0,\infty;\dot{H}^{\alpha})}^{2}\leq 2\|{\bm{b}_{0}}\|_{{2}}^{2}.

By Lemma
5.2
and the Aubin-Lions compactness lemma, there exists a subsequence of
\{\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\}
, denoted still by
\{\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\}
, such that

(5.12)

\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\rightarrow\bm{b}\quad\mbox{in}\,\,L_{2}(K\times(0,T))

for any compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and finite
T>0
.
Moreover, by (
5.9
), we have

\displaystyle\|{\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\otimes\bm{b}_{R_{k}}-\bm{b}\otimes\bm{b}}\|_{{1};K}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\otimes(\bm{b}_{R_{k}}-\bm{b})}\|_{{1};K}+\|{(\bm{b}_{R_{k}}-\bm{b})\otimes\bm{b}}\|_{{1};K}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|{\bm{b}_{0}}\|_{{2}}\|{\bm{b}_{R_{k}}-\bm{b}}\|_{{2};K}

and so

(5.13)

\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\otimes\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\rightarrow\bm{b}\otimes\bm{b}\quad\mbox{in}\,\,L_{2}(0,T;L_{1}(K))

for any compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and finite
T>0
.

Next, we show that

(5.14)

\bm{u}_{R_{k}}\rightarrow\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}*(\bm{b}\otimes\bm{b})\quad\mbox{in}\,\,L_{2}(0,T;L_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}(K))

for any finite
T
and compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which automatically implies that

(5.15)

\bm{u}=\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}*(\bm{b}\otimes\bm{b});

hence
\bm{u}
satisfies (
1.6
).

Let
\bm{v}=\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}*(\bm{b}\otimes\bm{b})
. Fixing

0<r,T<\infty
, we write

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{R_{k}}(x,t)-\bm{v}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{M+r}}\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}(x-y):(\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\otimes\bm{b}_{R_{k}}-\bm{b}\otimes\bm{b})(y,t)\,d{y}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{M+r}}\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}(x-y):(\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\otimes\bm{b}_{R_{k}}-\bm{b}\otimes\bm{b})(y,t)\,d{y}

\displaystyle=\bm{I}_{1}^{k}(x,t)+\bm{I}_{2}^{k}(x,t)

for
(x,t)\in B_{r}\times(0,T)
, where
M>1
is a large number to be determined later. Recall from (
3.5
) that

|\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}(x)|\apprle\frac{1}{|x|^{d+1-2\alpha}}.

Hence by Lemma
2.5
and (
5.13
), we have

\displaystyle\|{\bm{I}_{1}^{k}}\|_{L_{2}(0,T;L_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty})}

\displaystyle\apprle\|{\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}}\|_{{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}}\|{\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\otimes\bm{b}_{R_{k}}-\bm{b}\otimes\bm{b}}\|_{L_{2}(0,T;L_{1}(B_{M+r}))}

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as}\,\,k\rightarrow\infty.

To estimate
|\bm{I}_{2}^{k}|
, we choose
0<\theta\leq 1/2
and
2<p<{2d}/{(2\alpha-1)}

such that

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\theta\beta}{d}.

Then by Proposition
2.6
,

\|\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\|_{p}\apprle\|\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\|_{2}^{1-\theta}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\bm{b}_{R_{k}}}\|_{{2}}^{\theta}.

Hence setting
q=2/\theta
, we have

\|{\bm{b}_{R_{k}}}\|_{L_{q}(0,\infty;L_{p})}\apprle\|{\bm{b}_{R_{k}}}\|_{L_{\infty}(0,\infty;L_{2})}^{1-\theta}\|{\bm{b}_{R_{k}}}\|_{L_{2}(0,\infty;\dot{H}^{\beta})}^{\theta}\apprle\|\bm{b}_{0}\|_{2}

and similarly

\|{\bm{b}}\|_{L_{q}(0,\infty;L_{p})}\apprle\|\bm{b}_{0}\|_{2}.

On the other hand, since
d+1-2\alpha-{d(p-2)}/{p}>0
, it follows that if
|x|\leq r
, then

\displaystyle|\bm{I}_{2}^{k}(x,t)|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{M}}|\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}(z)|^{(p/2)^{\prime}}dz\right)^{1/(p/2)^{\prime}}\|{[\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\otimes\bm{b}_{R_{k}}-\bm{b}\otimes\bm{b}](t)}\|_{{p/2}}

\displaystyle\apprle M^{-[d+1-2\alpha-d(p-2)/p]}\left(\|{\bm{b}_{R_{k}}(t)}\|_{{p}}^{2}+\|{\bm{b}(t)}\|_{{p}}^{2}\right).

Therefore, noting that
q\geq 4
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|{\bm{u}_{R_{k}}-\bm{v}}\|_{L_{2}(0,T;L_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}(B_{r}))}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\bm{I}_{1}^{k}}\|_{L_{2}(0,T;L_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}(B_{r}))}

\displaystyle\quad+C(r,T)M^{-[d+1-2\alpha-d(p-2)/p]}\|\bm{b}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}

for some constant
C(r,T)
independent of
k
and
M
.

Now, let
\delta>0
be given. Then choosing
M>1
so large that

C(r,T)M^{-[d+1-2\alpha-d(p-2)/p]}\|\bm{b}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}<\delta,

and then letting
k\to\infty
, we have

\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|{\bm{u}_{R_{k}}-\bm{v}}\|_{L_{2}(0,T;L_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}(B_{r}))}\leq\delta,

which proves (
5.14
).

Finally, we show that

(5.16)

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{R_{k}}\otimes\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\rightarrow\bm{u}\otimes\bm{b}\quad\mbox{in}\,\,L_{1}(K\times(0,T)),

\displaystyle\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\otimes\bm{u}_{R_{k}}\rightarrow\bm{b}\otimes\bm{u}\quad\mbox{in}\,\,L_{1}(K\times(0,T))

for any compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and finite
T>0
. By symmetry, it suffices to show the first assertion. We write

\bm{u}_{R_{k}}\otimes\bm{b}_{R_{k}}-\bm{u}\otimes\bm{b}=(\bm{u}_{R_{k}}-\bm{u})\otimes\bm{b}_{R_{k}}+\bm{u}\otimes(\bm{b}_{R_{k}}-\bm{b}).

First, since
1/2<\alpha<(d+1)/2
,
\beta>0
, and
2\alpha+\beta>d/2+1
, there exists
2<p_{1}<\infty
such that

\max\left\{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\beta}{d},0\right\}<\frac{1}{p_{1}}<\min\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{2\alpha-1}{d}\right\}.

Then by Proposition
2.7
,

\|\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\|_{{p_{1},1}}\apprle\|\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\|_{2}^{1-\theta_{1}}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\bm{b}_{R_{k}}}\|_{{2}}^{\theta_{1}},

where
0<\theta_{1}<1
is defined by
1/p_{1}=1/2-(\theta_{1}\beta)/d
. Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be compact. Then since
1/p_{1}>1/2-\beta/d
and
d/(2\alpha-1)<p_{1}
, it follows from Lemma
2.3
and the Sobolev embedding theorem that

\displaystyle\|(\bm{u}_{R_{k}}-\bm{u})\otimes\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\|_{1;K}

\displaystyle\apprle\|\bm{u}_{R_{k}}-\bm{u}\|_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty;K}\|\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\|_{d/(2\alpha-1),1;K}

\displaystyle\apprle_{K}\|\bm{u}_{R_{k}}-\bm{u}\|_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty;K}\|\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\|_{H^{\beta}}.

Hence by (
5.9
), (
5.14
) and (
5.15
), we have

\displaystyle\|(\bm{u}_{R_{k}}-\bm{u})\otimes\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\|_{L_{1}(0,T;L_{1}(K))}

\displaystyle\apprle_{K,T}\|{\bm{u}_{R_{k}}-\bm{u}}\|_{L_{2}(0,T;L_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}(K))}\|\bm{b}_{0}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as}\,\,k\to\infty

for any finite
T
. To estimate
\bm{u}\otimes(\bm{b}_{R_{k}}-\bm{b})
, we choose
2<p_{2}<\infty
such that

\max\left\{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{d},0\right\}<\frac{1}{p_{2}}<\min\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{d+1-2\alpha}{d}\right\}.

By Proposition
2.7
again,

\|\bm{u}\|_{2;K}\apprle_{K}\|\bm{u}\|_{p_{2}}\apprle\|\bm{u}\|_{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}^{1-\theta_{2}}\|\Lambda^{\alpha}\bm{u}\|_{2}^{\theta_{2}}

for some
0<\theta_{2}<1
. Hence by (
5.11
), (
5.12
), and (
5.15
), we have

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}\otimes(\bm{b}_{R_{k}}-\bm{b})\|_{L_{1}(0,T;L_{1}(K))}

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{u}\|_{L_{2}(0,T;L_{2}(K))}\|\bm{b}_{R_{k}}-\bm{b}\|_{L_{2}(0,T;L_{2}(K))}

\displaystyle\apprle_{K,T}\left(\|\bm{b}_{0}\|_{2}+\|\bm{b}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\right)\|{\bm{b}_{R_{k}}-\bm{b}}\|_{L_{2}(0,T;L_{2}(K))}

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as}\,\,k\to\infty,

which proves (
5.16
).

We have shown strong convergence for the nonlinear terms
\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\otimes\bm{b}_{R_{k}}
,
\bm{u}_{R_{k}}\otimes\bm{b}_{R_{k}}
, and
\bm{b}_{R_{k}}\otimes\bm{u}_{R_{k}}
. Hence by a standard argument, we can show that
(\bm{u},\bm{b})
satisfies (
1.7
). It is obvious that both
\bm{u}
and
\bm{b}
are divergence-free.
This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
\square

.
By Theorem

and its proof, there exists a global weak solution

of (

), which also satisfies

in

Since

. Adding (

for any

, where

.
Hence by Theorem
4.4
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{2}+\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\bm{b}}\otimes\bm{u}^{1}:\nabla\tilde{\bm{b}}\,d{x}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(-\bm{b}^{2}\otimes\tilde{\bm{u}}+\tilde{\bm{u}}\otimes\bm{b}^{2}\right):\nabla\tilde{\bm{b}}\,d{x}

for some constant

, where

\phi(t)=1+\|{\Lambda^{\alpha}\bm{u}^{1}(t)}\|_{{2}}^{2}+\|{\Lambda^{\alpha}\bm{u}^{2}(t)}\|_{{2}}^{2}+\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\bm{b}^{1}(t)}\|_{{2}}^{2}+\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\bm{b}^{2}(t)}\|_{{2}}^{2}.

2.8
; that is, we choose

with
\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+1/\beta\leq 2

(6.4)

\|{\bm{u}^{i}\otimes\bm{b}^{j}}\|_{{2}}\apprle\|{\bm{u}^{i}}\|_{{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}}^{1-\theta_{1}}\|{\Lambda^{\alpha}\bm{u}^{i}}\|_{{2}}^{\theta_{1}}\|{\bm{b}^{j}}\|_{{2}}^{1-\theta_{2}}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\bm{b}^{j}}\|_{{2}}^{\theta_{2}}

for

. Note also that if
\alpha<1
and
\mu={(1-\alpha)}/{\beta}

\alpha+(1-\mu)\beta\geq d/2+1
, the numbers
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
can be chosen so that
(1+\mu)\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+1/\beta\leq 2
.

Now, by (
6.4
) and Proposition
2.6
, we obtain

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}\otimes\bm{u}^{1}}\|_{{2}}\|{\nabla\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}

\displaystyle\apprle\|{\bm{u}^{1}}\|_{{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}}^{1-\theta_{1}}\|{\Lambda^{\alpha}\bm{u}^{1}}\|_{{2}}^{\theta_{1}}\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{1-\theta_{2}}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{\theta_{2}}\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{1-1/\beta}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{1/\beta}.

and

(6.5)

II\apprle\|{\tilde{\bm{u}}}\|_{{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}}^{1-\theta_{1}}\|{\Lambda^{\alpha}\tilde{\bm{u}}}\|_{{2}}^{\theta_{1}}\|{\bm{b}^{2}}\|_{{2}}^{1-\theta_{2}}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\bm{b}^{2}}\|_{{2}}^{\theta_{2}}\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{1-1/\beta}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{1/\beta}.

It is easy to estimate

. Indeed, by Lemma
5.1
, the energy identity (
1.3
), and Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\apprle\|{\bm{b}_{0}}\|_{{2}}^{2(1-\theta_{1})}\left(\|{\Lambda^{\alpha}{\bm{u}}^{1}}\|_{{2}}^{\theta_{1}}\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{2-\theta_{2}-1/\beta}\right)\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{\theta_{2}+1/\beta},

\displaystyle\leq C\|{\Lambda^{\alpha}\bm{u}^{1}}\|_{{2}}^{\frac{2\theta_{1}}{2-\theta_{2}-1/\beta}}\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\phi(t)^{\frac{\theta_{1}}{2-\theta_{2}-1/\beta}}\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{2}

for some

. Since
\theta_{1}/(2-\theta_{2}-1/\beta)\leq 1

I\leq C\phi(t)\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{2}.

To estimate

, we recall that

\tilde{\bm{u}}=\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}*(\tilde{\bm{b}}\otimes\bm{b}^{1}+\bm{b}^{2}\otimes\tilde{\bm{b}}).

By Theorems
3.9
and
3.10
, we have

(6.6)

\|{\tilde{\bm{u}}}\|_{{d/(d+1-2\alpha),\infty}}\apprle\left(\|{\bm{b}^{1}}\|_{{2}}+\|{\bm{b}^{2}}\|_{{2}}\right)\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}\apprle\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}

and

(6.7)

\|{\Lambda^{\alpha}\tilde{\bm{u}}}\|_{{2}}\apprle\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}\otimes\bm{b}^{1}+\bm{b}^{2}\otimes\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\alpha}}.

Suppose first that

. Then

where

{1}/{r}={1}/{2}-{(\alpha-1)}/{d}
.
Fix any

. Then by Hölder’s inequality and Proposition
2.6
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\tilde{\bm{b}}\otimes\bm{b}^{1}+\bm{b}^{2}\otimes\tilde{\bm{b}}\right):\Phi\,d{x}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\left(\|{\bm{b}^{1}}\|_{{2r^{\prime}}}+\|{\bm{b}^{2}}\|_{{2r^{\prime}}}\right)\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2r^{\prime}}}\|{\Phi}\|_{{r}}

\displaystyle\quad\apprle\left(\|{\bm{b}^{1}}\|_{{2r^{\prime}}}+\|{\bm{b}^{2}}\|_{{2r^{\prime}}}\right)\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{2r^{\prime}}\|{\Phi}\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha-1}}

\displaystyle\quad\apprle\left(\|{\bm{b}^{1}}\|_{2}^{1-\lambda}\|{\bm{b}^{1}}\|_{\dot{H}^{\beta}}^{\lambda}+\|{\bm{b}^{2}}\|_{2}^{1-\lambda}\|{\bm{b}^{2}}\|_{\dot{H}^{\beta}}^{\lambda}\right)\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{2}^{1-\lambda}\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{\dot{H}^{\beta}}^{\lambda}\|{\Phi}\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha-1}},

Hence by (
6.7

(6.8)

\displaystyle\|{\Lambda^{\alpha}\tilde{\bm{u}}}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\apprle\left(\|{\bm{b}^{1}}\|_{2}^{1-\lambda}\|{\bm{b}^{1}}\|_{\dot{H}^{\beta}}^{\lambda}+\|{\bm{b}^{2}}\|_{2}^{1-\lambda}\|{\bm{b}^{2}}\|_{\dot{H}^{\beta}}^{\lambda}\right)\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{2}^{1-\lambda}\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{\dot{H}^{\beta}}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle\apprle\phi(t)^{\lambda/2}\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{2}^{1-\lambda}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{\lambda}.

Combining (

), (
6.6
), and (

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle\leq C\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{1-\theta_{1}}\left(\phi(t)^{\lambda/2}\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{1-\lambda}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{\lambda}\right)^{\theta_{1}}\phi(t)^{\theta_{2}/2}\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{1-1/\beta}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{1/\beta}

\displaystyle=C\phi(t)^{(\lambda\theta_{1}+\theta_{2})/2}\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{2}^{2-\lambda\theta_{1}-1/\beta}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{\lambda\theta_{1}+1/\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C\phi(t)^{\frac{\lambda\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}}{2-\lambda\theta_{1}-1/\beta}}\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{2}.

Since

and

\frac{\lambda\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}}{2-\lambda\theta_{1}-1/\beta}\leq 1.

Therefore,

II\leq C\phi(t)\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{2}.

\alpha<1

\displaystyle\|{\Lambda^{\alpha}\tilde{\bm{u}}}\|_{{2}}\apprle\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}\otimes\bm{b}^{1}+\bm{b}^{2}\otimes\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\alpha}}\apprle\left(\|{\bm{b}^{1}}\|_{{H}^{1-\alpha}}+\|{\bm{b}^{2}}\|_{{H}^{1-\alpha}}\right)\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{H}^{\beta}}.

\displaystyle\|{\bm{b}^{i}}\|_{{H}^{1-\alpha}}

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle\leq C\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{1-\theta_{1}}\left[\phi(t)^{\mu/2}\left(\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}+\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{\dot{H}^{\beta}}\right)\right]^{\theta_{1}}\phi(t)^{\theta_{2}/2}\|{\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{1-1/\beta}\|{\Lambda^{\beta}\tilde{\bm{b}}}\|_{{2}}^{1/\beta}

This completes the proof of (
6.3
). By Gronwall’s inequality, we conclude that
\bm{b}^{1}=\bm{b}^{2}
on
[0,\infty)
. Moreover, it follows from (

Appendix A
Completion of the proof of Lemma
2.2

To complete the proof of Lemma
2.2
, it remains to prove the following result.

Lemma A.1
.

\mathscr{S}_{0}
is dense in
L_{p}\cap\dot{H}^{s}
for
1<p<\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma
A.1
will be proved by using Littlewood-Paley operators as in the proofs of
[
3
, Proposition 2.27]
and
[
34
, 5.1.5/Theorem]
. Let
\psi
and
\varphi
be radial
C^{\infty}
-functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

{\rm supp}\,\psi\subset B_{4/3},\quad{\rm supp}\,\varphi\subset\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:3/4\leq|\xi|\leq 8/3\},

\psi(\xi)+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

and

\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,\xi\neq 0.

For
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\psi_{j}(\xi)=\psi(2^{-j}\xi)
and
\varphi_{j}(\xi)=\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)
. Then the Littlewood-Paley operators
S_{j}
and
\Delta_{j}
are defined by

S_{j}u=(\psi_{j}\hat{u})^{\vee}=\psi_{j}^{\vee}*u\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta_{j}u=(\varphi_{j}\hat{u})^{\vee}=\varphi_{j}^{\vee}*u

for
u\in\mathscr{S}_{0}^{\prime}
. It immediately follows from Young’s convolution inequality that

(A.1)

\|{S_{j}u}\|_{{p}}+\|{\Delta_{j}u}\|_{{p}}\leq C\|{u}\|_{{p}}\quad(1\leq p\leq\infty)

for some constant
C
independent of
j
.

The following inequalities of Bernstein type are also easily proved by using Young’s convolution inequality (see e.g.
[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
or
[
36
, Lemma 2.4]
).

Proposition A.2
.

Let
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and
0<R<\infty
. Then for all
u\in L_{p}
with
\supp\hat{u}\subset B_{R}
, we have

\|{D^{k}u}\|_{{q}}:=\max_{|\beta|=k}\|{D^{\beta}u}\|_{{q}}\apprle R^{k+d(1/p-1/q)}\|{u}\|_{{p}}.

Moreover, if
u\in L_{p}
and
\supp\hat{u}\subset B_{R}\setminus B_{R/2}
, then

\|{D^{k}u}\|_{{p}}\approx R^{k}\|{u}\|_{{p}}.

In particular, for all
u\in L_{p}
, we have

(A.2)

\displaystyle\|{\Delta_{j}u}\|_{{q}}

\displaystyle\apprle 2^{jd(1/p-1/q)}\|{\Delta_{j}u}\|_{{p}},

(A.3)

\displaystyle\|{S_{j}u}\|_{{q}}

\displaystyle\apprle 2^{jd(1/p-1/q)}\|{S_{j}u}\|_{{p}},

(A.4)

\displaystyle\|{D^{k}(\Delta_{j}u)}\|_{{p}}

\displaystyle\approx 2^{jk}\|{\Delta_{j}u}\|_{{p}}.

The following is the classical Littlewood-Paley characterization of the homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{s}_{p}
(see e.g.
[
19
, Theorem 6.2.7]
).

Proposition A.3
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1<p<\infty
. For all
u\in\dot{H}^{s}_{p}
, we
have

\|{u}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}_{p}}\approx\left\|\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{2js}|{\Delta}_{j}u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{p}.

Proof of Lemma
A.1
.

Suppose that
u\in L_{p}\cap\dot{H}^{s}
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
be given. Then by Proposition
A.3
with
s=0
and
p=2
, respectively, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\|{u_{N}-u}\|_{{p}}+\|{u_{N}-u}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

where
u_{N}=\sum_{|j|\leq N}\Delta_{j}u
.
Note that
u_{N}
belongs to
L_{p}\cap L_{2}
and has support in an annulus. Hence it follows from Proposition
A.2
that
u_{N}\in H_{2}^{k}\cap H_{p}^{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Choose
\theta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{2})
with
\theta=1
in
B_{1}
, and let
\theta_{R}(x)=\theta(x/R)
for
R>0
. Fixing an integer
M>N
(say,
M=N+1
), we define

u_{N}^{R}=(I-S_{-M})(\theta_{R}u_{N}).

Note then that

(A.5)

u_{N}^{R}\in\mathscr{S}_{0}\quad\mbox{and}\quad u_{N}^{R}-u_{N}=(I-S_{-M})((\theta_{R}-1)u_{N}).

By (
A.1
), we have

\|{u_{N}^{R}-u_{N}}\|_{{p}}\apprle\|{(\theta_{R}-1)u_{N}}\|_{{p}}.

Since
u_{N}\in L_{p}
and
(\theta_{R}-1)u_{N}\rightarrow 0
as
R\rightarrow\infty
, it follows from the dominated convergence theorem that

\|{u_{N}^{R}-u_{N}}\|_{{p}}\rightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as}\,\,R\rightarrow\infty.

Let
k=\max\,\{0,\lfloor s\rfloor+2\}
, where
\lfloor s\rfloor
is the greatest integer less than or equal to
s
. If
j\geq 0
, then by (
A.5
), (
A.4
), and (
A.1
), we have

\displaystyle 2^{js}\|{\Delta_{j}(u_{N}^{R}-u_{N})}\|_{{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{j(k-1)}\|{\Delta_{j}(I-S_{-M})((\theta_{R}-1)u_{N})}\|_{{2}}

\displaystyle\approx 2^{-j}\|{D^{k}[\Delta_{j}[(I-S_{-M})((\theta_{R}-1)u_{N})]]}\|_{{2}}

\displaystyle\apprle 2^{-j}\|{D^{k}((\theta_{R}-1)u_{N})}\|_{{2}}.

If
-M-1\leq j\leq-1
, then by (
A.1
) and (
A.5
),

2^{js}\|{\Delta_{j}(u_{N}^{R}-u_{N})}\|_{{2}}\apprle 2^{js}\|{(\theta_{R}-1)u_{N}}\|_{{2}}.

Finally, noting that

\Delta_{j}(u_{N}^{R}-u_{N})=0\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,j\leq-M-2,

we obtain

\displaystyle\|{u_{N}^{R}-u_{N}}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle\approx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{2js}|\Delta_{j}(u_{N}^{R}-u)|^{2}\right)\,d{x}

\displaystyle\apprle\sum_{j=-M-1}^{-1}2^{2js}\|{(\theta_{R}-1)u_{N}}\|_{{2}}^{2}+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j}\|{D^{k}((\theta_{R}-1)u_{N})}\|_{{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\apprle\|{(\theta_{R}-1)u_{N}}\|_{{H}^{k}}^{2}.

Hence by the dominated convergence theorem again, we have

\|{u_{N}^{R}-u_{N}}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\,\,R\rightarrow\infty.

This completes the proof of Lemma
A.1
.

Proof of Lemma

Our proof of Lemma
3.4
is based on the following well-known result (see e.g.
[
31
, Chapter V, Lemma 2]
and
[
18
, Theorem 2.4.6]
).

Lemma B.1
.

If
0<\operatorname{Re}\lambda<d
, then

(B.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{|\xi|^{\lambda}}\phi^{\vee}(\xi)\,d\xi=\frac{\Gamma((d-\lambda)/2)}{\Gamma(\lambda/2)\pi^{d/2-\lambda}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{|x|^{d-\lambda}}\phi(x)\,dx

for all
\phi\in\mathscr{S}
.

We will also use the method of analytic continuation. The following result is useful to show the analyticity of some functions defined by integrals.

Lemma B.2
.

Fix
\phi\in\mathscr{S}
and
\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}
. Then the function

f(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}x^{\beta}|x|^{z}\phi(x)\,d{x}

is holomorphic in the region
H=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\operatorname{Re}z>-d-|\beta|\}
.

Proof.

Let
z\in H
be fixed. Choose
\varepsilon>0
such that
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:|w-z|\leq\varepsilon\}\subset H
.

Suppose that
x\neq 0
. Then the function
|x|^{w}=\exp(w\ln|x|)
is entire and

\frac{d}{dw}\left[|x|^{w}\right]=(\log|x|)|x|^{w}.

Hence for all
w\in\overline{B_{\varepsilon}(z)}
with
w\neq z
,

\left|\frac{|x|^{w}-|x|^{z}}{w-z}\right|\leq|\log|x||\max_{|\eta-z|\leq\varepsilon}||x|^{\eta}|=|\log|x|||x|^{\operatorname{Re}z+\varepsilon}.

Moreover, since
\operatorname{Re}z+\varepsilon>-d-|\beta|
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|x^{\beta}\log|x||x|^{\operatorname{Re}z+\varepsilon}\phi(x)\right|\,d{x}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\log|x|||x|^{\operatorname{Re}z+\varepsilon+|\beta|}|\phi(x)|\,d{x}<\infty.

Hence it follows from the dominated convergence theorem that

\lim_{w\rightarrow z}\frac{f(w)-f(z)}{w-z}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}x^{\beta}(\log|x|)|x|^{z}\phi(x)\,dx.

This completes the proof of Lemma
B.2
.
∎

Recall that

(D^{\beta}\phi)^{\vee}(\xi)=(-2\pi i\xi)^{\beta}{\phi}^{\vee}(\xi),\quad D^{\beta}\widehat{\phi}(\xi)=\left((-2\pi ix)^{\beta}\phi\right)^{\wedge}(\xi)

for
\phi\in\mathscr{S}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
.

Proof of Lemma
3.4
(1).

Assume that
0<\operatorname{Re}\lambda<d
. Set
\phi=D_{j}\psi
.
Then since
{\phi}^{\vee}(\xi)=(-2\pi i\xi_{j}){\psi}^{\vee}(\xi)
, it follows from Lemma
B.1
that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{-2\pi i\xi_{j}}{|\xi|^{\lambda}}{\psi}^{\vee}(\xi)\,d\xi=\frac{\Gamma((d-\lambda)/2)}{\Gamma(\lambda/2)\pi^{d/2-\lambda}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{|x|^{d-\lambda}}D_{j}\psi(x)\,dx.

By the fundamental property of the Gamma function,

(d-\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{-2\pi i\xi_{j}}{|\xi|^{\lambda}}{\psi}^{\vee}(\xi)\,d\xi=\frac{2\Gamma((d+2-\lambda)/2)}{\Gamma(\lambda/2)\pi^{d/2-\lambda}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{|x|^{d-\lambda}}D_{j}\psi(x)\,dx,

which holds for
0<\operatorname{Re}\lambda<d+1
, by analytic continuation based on Lemma
B.2
.

Assume next that
1<\operatorname{Re}\lambda<d+1
.
Then performing the integration by parts, we easily obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{|x|^{d-\lambda}}D_{j}\psi(x)\,dx=(d-\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{x_{j}}{|x|^{d+2-\lambda}}\psi(x)\,dx.

Hence, if
d<\operatorname{Re}\lambda<d+1
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{-2\pi i\xi_{j}}{|\xi|^{\lambda}}{\psi}^{\vee}(\xi)\,d\xi=\frac{2\Gamma((d+2-\lambda)/2)}{\Gamma(\lambda/2)\pi^{d/2-\lambda}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{x_{j}}{|x|^{d+2-\lambda}}\psi(x)\,dx.

This identity holds for
1<\operatorname{Re}\lambda<d+1
, by Lemma
B.2
and analytic continuation again. This completes the proof of Lemma
3.4
(1).
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.4
(2).

Assume that
1<\operatorname{Re}\lambda<d+1
.
Then by Lemma
3.4
(1),

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{-2\pi i\xi_{j}}{|\xi|^{\lambda}}{\phi}^{\vee}(\xi)\,d\xi=\frac{2\Gamma((d+2-\lambda)/2)}{\Gamma(\lambda/2)\pi^{d/2-\lambda}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{x_{j}}{|x|^{d+2-\lambda}}\phi(x)\,dx.

for all
\phi\in\mathscr{S}
.
Setting
\phi=D_{k}\psi
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(-2\pi i\xi_{j})(-2\pi i\xi_{k})}{|\xi|^{\lambda}}{\psi}^{\vee}(\xi)\,d\xi=\frac{2\Gamma((d+2-\lambda)/2)}{\Gamma(\lambda/2)\pi^{d/2-\lambda}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{x_{j}}{|x|^{d+2-\lambda}}D_{k}\psi(x)\,dx,

which holds for
1<\operatorname{Re}\lambda<d+2
, by analytic continuation.
Moreover, if
d+1<\operatorname{Re}\lambda<d+2
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{x_{j}}{|x|^{d+2-\lambda}}D_{k}\psi(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(d+2-\lambda)x_{j}x_{k}-\delta_{jk}|x|^{2}}{|x|^{d+4-\lambda}}{\psi}(x)\,d{x}.

Therefore, the proof of Lemma
3.4
(2) is completed by analytic continuation.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.4
(3).

Assume that
2<\operatorname{Re}\lambda<d+2
.
Then by Lemma
3.4
(2),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(-2\pi i\xi_{j})(-2\pi i\xi_{k})}{|\xi|^{\lambda}}{\phi}^{\vee}(\xi)\,d\xi

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{2\Gamma((d+2-\lambda)/2)}{\Gamma(\lambda/2)\pi^{d/2-\lambda}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(d+2-\lambda)x_{j}x_{k}-\delta_{jk}|x|^{2}}{|x|^{d+4-\lambda}}{\phi}(x)\,dx

for all
\phi\in\mathscr{S}
. Setting
\phi=D_{l}\psi
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(-2\pi i\xi_{j})(-2\pi i\xi_{k})(-2\pi i\xi_{l})}{|\xi|^{\lambda}}{\psi}^{\vee}(\xi)\,d\xi

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{2\Gamma((d+2-\lambda)/2)}{\Gamma(\lambda/2)\pi^{d/2-\lambda}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(d+2-\lambda)x_{j}x_{k}-\delta_{jk}|x|^{2}}{|x|^{d+4-\lambda}}D_{l}{\psi}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{4\Gamma((d+4-\lambda)/2)}{(d+2-\lambda)\Gamma(\lambda/2)\pi^{d/2-\lambda}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(d+2-\lambda)x_{j}x_{k}-\delta_{jk}|x|^{2}}{|x|^{d+4-\lambda}}D_{l}{\psi}(x)\,dx,

which holds for
2<\operatorname{Re}\lambda<d+3
with
\lambda\neq d+2
, by the analytic continuation.
Moreover, if
3<\operatorname{Re}\lambda<d+3
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{|x|^{d+2-\lambda}}D_{l}{\psi}(x)\,d{x}=(d+2-\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{x_{l}}{|x|^{d+4-\lambda}}\psi(x)\,dx.

Hence for
d+2<\operatorname{Re}\lambda<d+3
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(-2\pi i\xi_{j})(-2\pi i\xi_{k})(-2\pi i\xi_{l})}{|\xi|^{\lambda}}{\psi}^{\vee}(\xi)\,d\xi

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{4\Gamma((d+4-\lambda)/2)}{\Gamma(\lambda/2)\pi^{d/2-\lambda}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[-D_{l}\left(\frac{x_{j}x_{k}}{|x|^{d+4-\lambda}}\right)-\frac{\delta_{jk}x_{l}}{|x|^{d+4-\lambda}}\right]{\psi}(x)\,d{x}.

The proof of Lemma
3.4
(3) is completed by analytic continuation.
∎

Appendix C
Proof of Lemma
3.6

This section is devoted to proving Lemma
3.6
. It is a classical result due to de Rham
[
14
]
that if a distribution
\bm{u}
satisfies

(C.1)

\left<\bm{u},\Phi\right>=0\quad\text{ for all }\Phi\in C_{c,\sigma}^{\infty},

then
\bm{u}=\nabla p
for some distribution
p
. We prove that if
\bm{u}
is a tempered distribution, then
p
is also a tempered distribution by following the idea of Wang
[
35
]
. It will be first shown (see Lemma
C.2
below) that for each
g\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g\,d{x}=0
, there exists
\bm{w}_{g}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\Div\bm{w}_{g}=g\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The following lemma is necessary to show that
\bm{w}_{g}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the mapping
g\mapsto\bm{w}_{g}
is linear and continuous.

Lemma C.1
.

For a fixed
1\leq j\leq d
, let
\mathscr{S}_{\#}^{(j)}
be the subspace of
\mathscr{S}
that consists of all
u\in\mathscr{S}
satisfying

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}u(x_{1},\dots,x_{j-1},t,x_{j+1},\dots,x_{d})\,d{t}=0

for all
(x_{1},...,x_{j-1},x_{j+1},...,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
. For each
u\in\mathscr{S}_{\#}^{(j)}
, we define
T^{(j)}(u):\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

T^{(j)}(u)(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x_{j}}u(x_{1},\dots,x_{j-1},t,x_{j+1},\dots,x_{d})\,d{t}\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Then
T^{(j)}
is a linear operator from
\mathscr{S}_{\#}^{(j)}
into
\mathscr{S}
. Moreover, if
u_{k}\in\mathscr{S}_{\#}^{(j)}
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
u_{k}\rightarrow 0
in
\mathscr{S}
, then
T^{(j)}(u_{k})\rightarrow 0
in
\mathscr{S}
.

Proof.

We prove the lemma only for the case when
d\geq 2
and
j=1
.

Suppose that

u\in\mathscr{S}_{\#}^{(1)}
and
v=T^{(1)}(u)
.
Obviously,
v
is smooth on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Moreover for all
\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta^{\prime})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
, we have

D^{\beta}v(x)=\begin{cases}\int_{-\infty}^{x_{1}}D^{\beta^{\prime}}_{x^{\prime}}u(t,x^{\prime})\,d{t}&\quad\text{if }\beta_{1}=0,\\
D^{\beta_{1}-1}_{1}D^{\beta^{\prime}}_{x^{\prime}}u(x_{1},x^{\prime})&\quad\text{if }\beta_{1}>0.\end{cases}

Hence to show that
v\in\mathscr{S}
, it remains to show that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|x^{\alpha}D^{\beta^{\prime}}_{x^{\prime}}v(x)\right|<\infty

for every
\beta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d-1}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
.

Let
\beta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d-1}
and
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha^{\prime})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
be fixed. Then since

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}D^{\beta^{\prime}}_{x^{\prime}}u(t,x^{\prime})\,d{t}=D^{\beta^{\prime}}_{x^{\prime}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}u(t,x^{\prime})\,d{t}=0,

it follows that

x^{\alpha}D^{\beta^{\prime}}_{x^{\prime}}v(x)=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\int_{-\infty}^{x_{1}}(x^{\prime})^{\alpha^{\prime}}D^{\beta^{\prime}}_{x^{\prime}}u(t,x^{\prime})\,d{t}=-x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\int_{x_{1}}^{\infty}(x^{\prime})^{\alpha^{\prime}}D^{\beta}_{x^{\prime}}u(t,x^{\prime})\,d{t}

for all
x=(x_{1},x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
On the other hand, since
u\in\mathscr{S}
, there exists a constant
C
such that

\sup_{(t,x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|(1+|t|)^{\alpha_{1}+2}(x^{\prime})^{\alpha^{\prime}}D^{\beta^{\prime}}_{x^{\prime}}u(t,x^{\prime})|\leq C.

Hence for all
x=(x_{1},x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
x_{1}\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\left|x^{\alpha}D^{\beta^{\prime}}_{x^{\prime}}v(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\int_{x_{1}}^{\infty}\left|(x^{\prime})^{\alpha^{\prime}}D^{\beta^{\prime}}_{x^{\prime}}u(t,x^{\prime})\right|\,d{t}

\displaystyle\leq x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\int_{x_{1}}^{\infty}\frac{C}{(1+|t|)^{\alpha_{1}+2}}dt\leq C(\alpha,\beta^{\prime}).

Similarly, if
x=(x_{1},x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
x_{1}\leq 0
, then

\left|x^{\alpha}D_{x^{\prime}}^{\beta^{\prime}}v(x)\right|\leq|x_{1}|^{\alpha_{1}}\int_{-\infty}^{x_{1}}\left|(x^{\prime})^{\alpha^{\prime}}D_{x^{\prime}}^{\beta^{\prime}}u(t,x^{\prime})\right|dt\leq C(\alpha,\beta^{\prime}).

This proves that
v\in\mathscr{S}
.

We have shown that
T^{(1)}
maps
\mathscr{S}_{\#}^{(1)}
to
\mathscr{S}
. Linearity of
T^{(1)}
is obvious from the very definition of
T^{(1)}
. Hence to complete the proof, it remains to prove the continuity property of
T^{(1)}
.

Suppose that
u_{k}\in\mathscr{S}_{\#}^{(1)}
and
u_{k}\rightarrow 0
in
\mathscr{S}
. Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}
be fixed. Then since
u_{k}\rightarrow 0
in
\mathscr{S}
, it follows that if
\beta_{1}>0
, then

x^{\alpha}D^{\beta}T^{(1)}(u_{k})(x)=x^{\alpha}D^{\beta_{1}-1}_{1}D^{\beta^{\prime}}_{x^{\prime}}u_{k}(x)\rightarrow 0

uniformly on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Moreover, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|(1+|x_{1}|)^{\alpha_{1}+2}(x^{\prime})^{\alpha^{\prime}}D^{\beta^{\prime}}_{x^{\prime}}u_{k}(x)\right|<\varepsilon

for all
k\geq N
. If
x=(x_{1},x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
x_{1}\geq 0
, then for all
k\geq N
,

\left|x^{\alpha}D^{\beta^{\prime}}_{x^{\prime}}T^{(1)}(u_{k})(x)\right|\leq\varepsilon x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\int_{x_{1}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+|t|)^{\alpha_{1}+2}}dt<C\varepsilon.

Similar estimates hold for
x_{1}\leq 0
. This implies that
T^{(1)}(u_{k})\rightarrow 0
in
\mathscr{S}
, which completes the proof of Lemma
C.1
.
∎

Lemma C.2
.

Fix
\phi_{i}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{i}\,d{x}=1
(
i=1,2,\dots,d
) and define

\phi(x)=\phi_{1}(x_{1})\cdots\phi_{d}(x_{d})\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Then there exists a continuous linear operator
\mathcal{B}
from
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

(C.2)

\Div\mathcal{B}(g)=g-\phi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g\,d{x}\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{d}

for all
g\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

For the sake of notational simplicity, we mainly focus on the case
d=3
. The general case can be proved in a similar way.

For a fixed
g\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we define

\overline{g}=g-\phi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}g\,d{x}.

Also for
1\leq j\leq 3
, let
S^{(j)}(g):\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by

\displaystyle S^{(1)}(g)(x)

\displaystyle=\overline{g}(x)-\phi_{1}(x_{1})\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{g}(s_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\,d{s_{1}},

\displaystyle S^{(2)}(g)(x)

\displaystyle=\phi_{1}(x_{1})\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{g}(s_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\,d{s_{1}}-\phi_{2}(x_{2})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\overline{g}(s_{1},s_{2},x_{3})\,d{s_{1}}ds_{2}\right],

\displaystyle S^{(3)}(g)(x)

\displaystyle=\phi_{1}(x_{1})\phi_{2}(x_{2})\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\overline{g}(s_{1},s_{2},x_{3})\,d{s_{1}}ds_{2}\right].

Then it is easy to check that
S^{(j)}
is a linear operator from
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
into
\mathscr{S}_{\#}^{(j)}
and that if
g_{k}\rightarrow 0
in
\mathscr{S}
, then
S^{(j)}(g_{k})\rightarrow 0
in
\mathscr{S}
.

Now, for each
g\in\mathscr{S}
, we define

\mathcal{B}(g)=\left(T^{(1)}S^{(1)}(g),T^{(2)}S^{(2)}(g),T^{(3)}S^{(3)}(g)\right).

Then by Lemma
C.1
,
\mathcal{B}
is a continuous linear operator from
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
to
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Moreover, by a direct computation,

\Div\mathcal{B}(g)=S^{(1)}(g)+S^{(2)}(g)+S^{(3)}(g)=\overline{g}\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{3}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
C.2
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.6
.

Suppose that
\bm{u}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
satisfies
\left<\bm{u},\Phi\right>=0
for all
\Phi\in C_{c,\sigma}^{\infty}
. Then by a simple density argument, we have

(C.3)

\left<\bm{u},\Phi\right>=0\quad\mbox{for all }\Phi\in\mathscr{S}\,\,\mbox{with}\,\,\Div\Phi=0.

Let
\mathcal{B}:\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the operator defined in Lemma
C.2
.
Then since
\mathcal{B}
is linear and continuous, the mapping

g\mapsto-\left<\bm{u},\mathcal{B}(g)\right>

defines a continuous linear functional
p
on
\mathscr{S}
.

Given
\Phi\in\mathscr{S}
, we define
g=\Div\Phi
and
\Psi=\mathcal{B}(g)-\Phi
. Then
\Psi\in\mathscr{S}
. Moreover, since
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g\,d{x}=0
, we have

\Div\Psi=\Div\mathcal{B}(g)-\Div\Phi=0\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Hence it follows from (
C.3
) that
\left<\bm{u},\Psi\right>=0
.
Therefore,

\left<\bm{u},\Phi\right>=\left<\bm{u},\mathcal{B}(g)\right>=-\left<p,g\right>=-\left<p,\Div\Phi\right>.

Since
\Phi\in\mathscr{S}
is arbitrary, we deduce that
\bm{u}=\nabla p
.
This completes the proof of Lemma
3.6
.
∎

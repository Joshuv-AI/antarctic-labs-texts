Exploring lepton flavor violation phenomena of the Z Z and Higgs bosons at electron-proton colliders Preprint: CTPU-PTC-23-18
Adil Jueid, Jinheung Kim, Soojin Lee, Jeonghyeon Song, Daohan Wang

Abstract
We comprehensively study the potential for discovering lepton flavor violation (LFV) phenomena associated with the Z Z and Higgs bosons at the LHeC and FCC-he. Our meticulous investigation reveals the remarkable suitability of electron-proton colliders for probing these rare new physics signals. This is due to the distinct advantages they offer, including negligible pileups, minimal QCD backgrounds, electron-beam polarization P e P_{e} , and the capability of distinguishing the charged-current from neutral-current processes. In our pursuit of LFV of the Z Z boson, we employ an innovative indirect probe, utilizing the t t -channel mediation of the Z Z boson in the process p ​ e − → j ​ τ − pe^{-}\to j\tau^{-} . For LFV in the Higgs sector, we scrutinize direct observations of the on-shell decays of H → e + ​ τ − H\to e^{+}\tau^{-} and H → μ ± ​ τ ∓ H\to\mu^{\pm}\tau^{\mp} through the charged-current production of H H . Focusing on H → e + ​ τ − H\to e^{+}\tau^{-} proves highly efficient due to the absence of positron-related backgrounds in the charged-current modes at electron-proton colliders. Through a dedicated signal-to-background analysis with the boosted decision tree algorithm, we demonstrate that the LHeC with the total integrated luminosity of 1 ​ ab − 1 1{\,{\rm ab}^{-1}} can put significantly lower 2 ​ σ 2\sigma bounds than the HL-LHC with 3 ​ ab − 1 3{\,{\rm ab}^{-1}} . Specifically, we find Br ⁡ ( Z → e ​ τ ) < 2.2 × 10 − 7 {\rm{Br}}(Z\to e\tau)<2.2\times 10^{-7} , Br ⁡ ( H → e ​ τ ) < 1.7 × 10 − 4 {\rm{Br}}(H\to e\tau)<1.7\times 10^{-4} , and Br ⁡ ( H → μ ​ τ ) < 1.0 × 10 − 4 {\rm{Br}}(H\to\mu\tau)<1.0\times 10^{-4} . Furthermore, our study uncovers the exceptional precision of the FCC-he in measuring the LFV signatures of the Z Z and Higgs bosons, which indicates the potential for future discoveries in this captivating field.

In this section,

The Monte Carlo event generation procedure is as follows.
First, we employ

To simulate the detector effects, we utilized version 3.5.0 of

to align the particle efficiencies, momentum smearing, and isolation parameters with the default values specified in the LHeC Concept Design Report

The
{\tau_{\rm h}}
-tagging plays a crucial role in detecting the LFV signals of the
Z
and Higgs bosons.
Let us elaborate on our strategy for choosing the
{\tau_{\rm h}}
-tagging efficiency in more detail.
The
{\tau_{\rm h}}
-tagging efficiency is not fixed but rather a parameter that we can choose depending on our target process.
When selecting the
{\tau_{\rm h}}
-tagging efficiency, it is essential to strike a balance between retaining the
\tau
-related signal events and suppressing the background events.
To find this balance, we take into account the feature that as the tagging efficiency increases, the mistagging rate also increases.

For the LFV
Z
channel, the primary background is
jj\nu
.
Given that this background overwhelmingly dominates the signal, our primary objective is to effectively suppress the QCD background.
To achieve this, we chose a relatively small
{\tau_{\rm h}}
-tagging efficiency.
By doing so, we reduce the mistagging probability, thereby minimizing contamination from QCD jets and enhancing the sensitivity to the LFV
Z
signal.
In our analysis,
we have specifically chosen
P_{{\tau_{\rm h}}\to{\tau_{\rm h}}}=0.4
for the
{\tau_{\rm h}}
-tagging efficiency and
P_{j\to{\tau_{\rm h}}}=0.001
for the mistagging probability.
These values are based on the default settings in the
Delphes
cards for the LHeC and FCC-he.

On the other hand, in the LFV
H
channel, the dominant background arises from

pe^{-}\to Z(\to\tau_{e,\mu}\tau)+j_{\rm f}\nu
processes.
Unlike the LFV
Z
channel, this background does not overwhelm the signal.
Consequently, choosing a small
{\tau_{\rm h}}
-tagging efficiency would not be advantageous since it would decrease both the signal and background event yields.
To maximize the signal significance in the presence of a manageable background,
we opt for a larger
{\tau_{\rm h}}
-tagging efficiency. This choice allows us to retain more
\tau
-related signal events while still keeping the background under control.
For the LFV
H
analysis, we have adopted
P_{{\tau_{\rm h}}\to{\tau_{\rm h}}}=0.85
for the
{\tau_{\rm h}}
-tagging efficiency and
P_{j\to{\tau_{\rm h}}}=0.05

ATLAS:2022aip
.

We compute the signal significance
\mathcal{S}
taking into account the uncertainty in the background,
defined by
Cowan:2010js

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=\left[2(N_{\rm s}+N_{\rm b})\log\left(\frac{(N_{\rm s}+N_{\rm b})(N_{\rm b}+\delta_{\rm bg}^{2})}{N_{\rm b}^{2}+(N_{\rm s}+N_{\rm b})\delta_{\rm bg}^{2}}\right)-\frac{2N_{\rm b}^{2}}{\delta_{b}^{2}}\log\left(1+\frac{\delta_{\rm bg}^{2}N_{\rm s}}{N_{\rm b}(N_{\rm b}+\delta_{\rm bg}^{2})}\right)\right]^{1/2},

(18)

where
N_{\rm s}
is the number of signal events,
N_{\rm b}
is the number of background events,
and
\delta_{\rm bg}=\Delta_{\rm bg}N_{\rm b}
is the uncertainty in the background yields.

.
Therefore, let us consider the specific case of

in our discussion.

III.2
Results of the LFV Higgs boson

Exploring the LFV of the Higgs boson at electron-proton colliders poses challenges
primarily due to the inherently low cross section of the Higgs production.
As summarized in Table
2

the highest possible cross section via the CC production with

(

Despite our concerted efforts in a cut-based analysis to enhance the signal significance, we were only able to achieve a modest significance of

To address these limitations, we turned to a multivariate analysis approach,

IV
Conclusions

We have conducted a comprehensive investigation into the potential of the LHeC and FCC-he for detecting lepton flavor violation (LFV) phenomena
of the
Z
and Higgs bosons.
For our analysis, we considered collision energies of
E_{e}=50{\,{\rm GeV}}
and
E_{p}=7{\;{\rm TeV}}
for the LHeC
and
E_{e}=60{\,{\rm GeV}}
and
E_{p}=50{\;{\rm TeV}}
for the FCC-he.
We found that electron-proton colliders are well-suited for detecting LFV phenomena
thanks to negligible pileups, small QCD backgrounds, and the feasibility to distinguish the charged-current
from neutral current processes.

For the
Z
LFV study,
we focused on the indirect probe
pe^{-}\to j_{\rm f}\tau^{-}
mediated by the
Z
boson in the
t
-channel.
Employing a conventional cut-based analysis and considering a full detector simulation with 10% background uncertainty,
we have demonstrated that the LHeC with
\mathcal{L}_{\rm tot}=1{\,{\rm ab}^{-1}}
and electron beam polarization of
P_{e}=0

can yield a significance of about 7.08 if
C_{\tau e}^{L}=C_{\tau e}^{R}=10^{-3}
, while the FCC-he with
\mathcal{L}_{\rm tot}=1{\,{\rm ab}^{-1}}
has a significance of about 8.04.
We have extended the analysis employing a dedicated multivariate analysis utilizing the BDT algorithm with the
XGBoost
package,
which enhances the significances by about 74% (54%) at the LHeC (FCC-he) with
\mathcal{L}_{\rm tot}=1{\,{\rm ab}^{-1}}
.
We obtained
2\sigma
bounds of
{\rm{Br}}(Z\to e\tau)\leq 2.22~(2.27)\times 10^{-7}

\mathcal{L}_{\rm tot}=1{\,{\rm ab}^{-1}}

For the LFV studies involving the Higgs boson,
we have concentrated on the direct observation of on-shell decays.
Specifically, we have emphasized
H\to e^{+}\tau^{-}
to avoid the electron-related backgrounds.
To address the challenges associated with the small production cross sections of the Higgs boson at the LHeC and FCC-he,
we employed a multivariate analysis utilizing the BDT algorithm with the
XGBoost
package.
Our analysis yielded the
2\sigma
bounds at the LHeC (FCC-he) with
\mathcal{L}_{\rm tot}=1{\,{\rm ab}^{-1}}
and
P_{e}=-80\%

such that
{\rm{Br}}(H\to e^{\pm}\tau^{\mp})<1.7\times 10^{-4}\left(6.3\times 10^{-5}\right)
and
{\rm{Br}}(H\to\mu^{\pm}\tau^{\mp})<1.0\times 10^{-4}\left(4.5\times 10^{-5}\right)
.
These bounds surpass the projected sensitivities of the HL-LHC with
\mathcal{L}_{\rm tot}=3{\,{\rm ab}^{-1}}
.

Our investigation into the LFV signatures of the
Z
and Higgs bosons
has unveiled the high potential of the LHeC and FCC-he in observing these rare processes.
Given the impressive outcomes of our study, we strongly endorse and support the LHeC and FCC-he programs.

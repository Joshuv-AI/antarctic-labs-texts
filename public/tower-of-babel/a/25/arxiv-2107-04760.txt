Leptin densities in amenable groups
Felix Pogorzelski, Christoph Richard, Nicolae Strungaru

Abstract
. Consider a positive Borel measure on a locally compact group. We define a notion of uniform density for such a measure, which is based on a group invariant introduced by Leptin in 1966. We then restrict to unimodular amenable groups and to translation bounded measures. In that case our density notion coincides with the well-known Beurling density from Fourier analysis, also known as Banach density from dynamical systems theory. We use Leptin densities for a geometric proof of the model set density formula, which expresses the density of a uniform regular model set in terms of the volume of its window, and for a proof of uniform mean almost periodicity of such model sets.

2.
Delone measures

2.1.
Definition of Delone measures

Instead of counting the number of points in locally finite sets, we use the more general setting of (positive) Borel measures. For some of our results we restrict to translation bounded measures, a notion which encompasses uniformly discrete point sets. See the discussion below.
It is somewhat common in works on non-abelian aperiodic order to define uniform discreteness and relative denseness for point sets via left translations, cf. e.g.
[
46
,
27
,
10
,
11
,
8
,
6
]
. Deviating from this convention we will work with the (of course equivalent) setting of uniform discreteness and relative denseness from the right. In the presence of a metric this means describing the topology by means of a right-invariant instead of a left-invariant metric. The reason for this choice is the interplay between uniform counting of points along asymptotically invariant sets. For amenable groups, counting quantities defined via approximation by left-asymptotically invariant (left-Følner) sets show desirable uniformity properties with respect to translations from the right. This phenomenon has also been observed in the theory of aperiodic order, see e.g.
[
46
,
49
,
5
]
. In the present paper, we decide to work with left-asymptotic invariance, which is also standard in the literature, and we consequently accept the topological structure to be from the right.

Throughout the paper, we denote by
G
a locally compact topological group. When we speak of a locally compact group
G
in this work, we will implicitly assume that
G
also satisfies the Hausdorff property, i.e., any two points in
G
can be separated by disjoint open neighborhoods.
For simplicity we will call a positive Borel measure a measure in the following.

Definition 2.1

(Delone measure)
.

Let
\nu
be a measure on a locally compact group
G
.

(a)

We call
\nu
upper translation bounded if there exist
C_{u}<\infty
and a compact symmetric unit neighborhood
B_{u}\subseteq G
such that
\sup_{x\in G}\nu(B_{u}x)\leq C_{u}
.

(b)

We call
\nu
lower translation bounded if there exist
C_{l}>0
and a compact symmetric unit neighborhood
B_{l}\subseteq G
such that
\inf_{x\in G}\nu(B_{l}x)\geq C_{l}
.

(c)

We call
\nu
a Delone measure if
\nu
it is both upper and lower translation bounded.

Remark 2.2
.

In the above definition, by convenience we restricted to compact symmetric unit neighborhoods. Using covering arguments, it is readily seen that
\nu
is upper translation bounded iff
\sup_{x\in G}\nu(Kx)<\infty
for every compact
K\subseteq G
. In particular, upper translation boundedness implies local finiteness, i.e.,
\nu(A)
is finite for every compact
A
. For a complex measure, upper and lower translation boundedness can be defined via its (positive) variation measure. Upper translation boundedness is traditionally called translation boundedness
[
1
]
. The name Delone measure is motivated from weak Delone sets
[
31
]
, i.e., from point sets that are weakly uniformly discrete and relatively dense. For point sets
\Lambda
, weak uniform discreteness is equivalent to upper translation boundedness of the associated point measure defined via
\nu(A)=\mathrm{card}(\Lambda\cap A)
. Relative denseness is equivalent to lower translation boundedness of the associated point measure. For point sets, uniform discreteness is also called separatedness, and weak uniform discreteness is also called relative separatedness, see e.g.
[
18
,
42
]
.

In this article, we will analyse various notions of density of a measure. Upper translation boundedness will result in finite upper density. Lower translation boundedness will result in positive lower density.
Examples of Delone measures are any (left or right) Haar measure on a unimodular locally compact group
G
and, for a uniform lattice
L
in
G
, the Haar measure on
L
viewed as a measure on
G
.

2.2.
Standard estimates on unimodular groups

For later use, we provide standard estimates for Delone measures on unimodular groups. These are based on the following observation from
[
47
]
. In the sequel,
AB
denotes the Minkowski product of
A,B\subseteq G
, i.e., we have
AB=\{ab:a\in A,b\in B\}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be a unimodular locally compact group with Haar measure
m
.
Consider any compact
A\in\mathcal{K}
and any unit neighborhood
B
. Take
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}\subseteq A
maximal such that
Ba_{i}\cap Ba_{j}=\varnothing
for
i\neq j
. Then

\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}Ba_{i}\subseteq BA\ ,\qquad A\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}B^{-1}Ba_{i}\ .

where the union on the lhs is disjoint.
In particular we have

n\cdot m(B)\leq m(BA)\ ,\qquad m(A)\leq n\cdot m(B^{-1}B)\ .

Proof.

The first inclusion is obvious. For the second inclusion, assume
a\in A
satisfies
a\notin\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}B^{-1}Ba_{i}
. Then for arbitrary fixed
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
we have
a\notin B^{-1}Ba_{i}
. Hence
ba\neq b^{\prime}a_{i}
for all
b,b^{\prime}\in B
, which implies
Ba\cap Ba_{i}=\varnothing
. As
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
was arbitrary, this contradicts maximality. The inequalities for the Haar measure follow from unimodularity.
∎

We denote by
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}(G)
the collection of nonempty compact subsets of
G
.
For an upper translation bounded measure we have the following upper bound.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G
be a unimodular locally compact group with Haar measure
m
.
Let
\nu
be an upper translation bounded measure on
G
. Take a compact symmetric unit neighborhood
B_{u}\subseteq G
and a finite number
C_{u}
such that
\nu(B_{u}^{2}x)\leq C_{u}
for all
x\in G
. We then have for any
A\in\mathcal{K}
the estimate

\nu(A)\leq\frac{C_{u}}{m(B_{u})}\cdot m(B_{u}A)\ .

Proof.

Fix any
A\in\mathcal{K}
and consider the setting of Lemma
2.3
with
B=B_{u}
. We then have

\nu(A)\leq\nu\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}B_{u}^{2}a_{i}\right)\leq n\cdot\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\nu(B_{u}^{2}a_{i})\leq\frac{C_{u}}{m(B_{u})}\cdot m(B_{u}A)\ .

∎

For a measure that is lower translation bounded, we have the following lower bound.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be a unimodular locally compact group with Haar measure
m
.
Let
\nu
be a lower translation bounded measure on
G
. Take a compact symmetric unit neighborhood
B_{l}\subset G
and a positive number
C_{l}
such that
\nu(B_{l}x)\geq C_{l}
for all
x\in G
. We then have for any compact set
A\in\mathcal{K}
the estimate

\nu(B_{l}A)\geq\frac{C_{l}}{m(B_{l}^{2})}\cdot m(A)\ .

Proof.

Fix any
A\in\mathcal{K}
and consider the setting of Lemma
2.3
with
B=B_{l}
. We then have

\nu(B_{l}A)\geq\nu\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}B_{l}a_{i}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nu(B_{l}a_{i})\geq n\cdot C_{l}\geq\frac{C_{l}}{m(B_{l}^{2})}\cdot m(A)\ .

∎

3.
Leptin density of a measure

We will define a certain uniform density that is based on the group invariant
I(G)
introduced by Leptin
[
36
]
, see also
[
48
, Cor. 4.14]
. Our notion is inspired by the work of Gröchenig, Kutyniok and Seip in
[
22
]
, see Appendix C for a discussion of the connection.

3.1.
Definition

Let
G
be a locally compact group. Take any left Haar measure
m
on
G
. Let
\mathcal{K}
denote the collection of nonempty compact subsets of
G
, and let
\mathcal{K}_{p}\subseteq\mathcal{K}
denote the collection of
compact subsets of
G
of positive left Haar measure. Consider

I(G)=\sup_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\inf_{A\in\mathcal{K}_{p}}\frac{m(KA)}{m(A)}\ .

Note that in the definition of
I(G)
we may restrict to
K
being a compact symmetric unit neighborhood without loss of generality.
The quantity
I(G)
is a group invariant as it does not depend on the choice of the left Haar measure.
It exhibits the following dichotomy.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group with left Haar measure
m
. Then either
I(G)=1
or
I(G)=\infty
.

Proof.

Note that
I(G)\geq 1
by definition.
For any compact unit neighborhood
K\in\mathcal{K}_{p}
consider

C(K)=\inf_{A\in\mathcal{K}_{p}}\frac{m(KA)}{m(A)}

and note that
C(K^{2})\geq C(K)^{2}
. Assume now that
I(G)>1
. Then there exist a compact unit neighborhood
K_{0}\in\mathcal{K}_{p}
and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that
C(K_{0})>1+\varepsilon_{0}>1
. This implies
I(G)\geq C(K_{0}^{n})\geq C(K_{0})^{n}\to\infty
. Thus
I(G)=\infty
.
∎

Recall that a locally compact group
G
is
amenable
if it admits a left-invariant mean on
L^{\infty}(G)
. The following important result is well known, see e.g.
[
16
,
21
]
and the monograph
[
48
]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
48
, Cor. 4.14]

Let
G
be a locally compact group. Then
G
is amenable if and only if
I(G)=1
. ∎

We now define the notion of Leptin density of a measure. Although our definition is given in a general setting, the notion of Leptin density seems most useful in amenable unimodular locally compact groups. Moreover it will be infinite on measures that are not upper translation bounded. This will be discussed below.

Definition 3.3

(Leptin densities)
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group. Let
m
be a left Haar measure on
G
. For a measure
\nu
on
G
consider

L_{\nu}^{-}=\sup_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\inf_{A\in\mathcal{K}_{p}}\frac{\nu(KA)}{m(A)}\ ,\qquad L_{\nu}^{+}=\inf_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\sup_{A\in\mathcal{K}_{p}}\frac{\nu(A)}{m(KA)}\ .

We call
L_{\nu}^{-}
and
L_{\nu}^{+}
the lower Leptin density respectively the upper Leptin density of the measure
\nu
. If
L_{\nu}^{-}=L_{\nu}^{+}=L_{\nu}
, we call
L_{\nu}
the Leptin density of
\nu
.

Remark 3.4
.

In the above definition we may assume
e\in K
without loss of generality, due to left invariance of the Haar measure.
The attribution to Leptin is motivated by
L^{-}_{m}=I(G)
and
L^{+}_{m}=I(G)^{-1}
. Note that the left Haar measure
m
has Leptin density
1
if and only if
G
is amenable, as a consequence of Theorem
3.2
.

3.2.
Some properties of Leptin densities

Let us analyse how Leptin densities behave with respect to translation. For a given measure
\nu
on
G
we consider its left translation
\nu\mapsto\delta_{t}*\nu
and its right translation
\nu\mapsto\nu*\delta_{t}
for
t\in G
, where
(\delta_{t}*\nu)(A)=\nu(t^{-1}A)
and
(\nu*\delta_{t})(A)=\nu(At^{-1})
.
The following result is obvious.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\nu
be a measure on a locally compact group
G
. Then the Leptin densities of
\nu
are invariant under left translation, i.e., we have for all
t\in G
that

L^{-}_{\delta_{t}*\nu}=L^{-}_{\nu}\ ,\qquad L^{+}_{\delta_{t}*\nu}=L^{+}_{\nu}\ .

If
G
is assumed to be unimodular, then the Leptin densities of
\nu
are also invariant under right translation, i.e., we have for all
t\in G
that

L^{-}_{\nu*\delta_{t}}=L^{-}_{\nu}\ ,\qquad L^{+}_{\nu*\delta_{t}}=L^{+}_{\nu}\ .

∎

Leptin densities reflect amenability of the underlying group. Whereas they are degenerate for Delone measures in non-amenable groups, they are well behaved in the amenable case.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\nu
be a measure on a locally compact group
G
. If
G
is amenable, then
L^{-}_{\nu}\leq L^{+}_{\nu}
.

Proof.

Assume
L^{+}_{\nu}<L^{-}_{\nu}
and pick
a<b
such that

L^{+}_{\nu}<a<b<L^{-}_{\nu}
.
Since
b<L^{-}_{\nu}
, there exists
K_{b}\in{\mathcal{K}}
such that for all
A\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}
we have

b\cdot m(A)\leq\nu(K_{b}A)
. Since
a>L^{+}_{\nu}
, there exists
K_{a}\in{\mathcal{K}}
such that for all
A\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}
we have

a\cdot m(K_{a}A)\geq\nu(A)
. Now fix arbitrary
A\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}
. We thus have

b\cdot m(A)\leq\nu(K_{b}A)\leq a\cdot m(K_{a}K_{b}A)
.
Therefore, for all
A\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}
we have

\frac{m(K_{a}K_{b}A)}{m(A)}\geq\frac{b}{a}>1\,.

Using the notation of the proof of Lemma
3.1
, this implies
C(K_{a}K_{b})>1
. Hence
I(G)=\infty
, and
G
is not amenable by Theorem
3.2
.
∎

For a converse of the above statement in the unimodular case, see Lemma
3.9
.

3.3.
Leptin densities of Delone measures in unimodular groups

In this subsection, we restrict to unimodular locally compact groups. The standard estimates of Section
2.2
have the following consequence.

Lemma 3.7

(Standard estimates)
.

Let
G
be a unimodular locally compact group. Let
\nu
be any Delone measure on
G
. Take compact symmetric unit neighborhoods
B_{l},B_{u}\subseteq G
and positive finite constants
C_{l},C_{u}
such that

C_{l}\leq\nu(B_{l}x)\ ,\qquad\nu(B_{u}^{2}x)\leq C_{u}

for all
x\in G
. Then the following estimates hold.

\begin{split}\frac{C_{l}}{m(B_{l}^{2})}\cdot I(G)&\leq L_{\nu}^{-}\leq\frac{C_{u}}{m(B_{u})}\cdot I(G)\ ,\\
\frac{C_{l}}{m(B_{l}^{2})}\cdot I(G)^{-1}&\leq L_{\nu}^{+}\leq\frac{C_{u}}{m(B_{u})}\cdot I(G)^{-1}\ .\end{split}

Here the upper estimates rely on upper translation boundedness, and the lower estimates rely on lower translation boundedness.

Proof.

We consider
L_{\nu}^{-}
first and use the estimate in Lemma
2.5
to obtain

L_{\nu}^{-}\geq\sup_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\inf_{A\in\mathcal{K}_{p}}\frac{\nu(B_{l}KA)}{m(A)}\geq\frac{C_{l}}{m(B_{l}^{2})}\cdot\sup_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\inf_{A\in\mathcal{K}_{p}}\frac{m(KA)}{m(A)}=\frac{C_{l}}{m(B_{l}^{2})}\cdot I(G)\ .

For an upper estimate, we use the estimate in Lemma
2.4
to obtain

L_{\nu}^{-}=\sup_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\inf_{A\in\mathcal{K}_{p}}\frac{\nu(KA)}{m(A)}\leq\frac{C_{u}}{m(B_{u})}\cdot\sup_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\inf_{A\in\mathcal{K}_{p}}\frac{m(B_{u}KA)}{m(A)}\leq\frac{C_{u}}{m(B_{u})}\cdot I(G)\ .

The proofs of the assertions on
L_{\nu}^{+}
are analogous and use Lemma
2.4
.
∎

The following lemma relates the notions of translation boundedness and Leptin density. This also motivates the notion of Delone measure. Part (a) of the lemma is analogous to
[
23
, Cor. 2]
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
G
be a unimodular locally compact group, and let
\nu
be any measure on
G
. Then the following hold.

(a)

\nu
is upper translation bounded if and only if
L_{\nu}^{+}
is finite.

(b)

\nu
is lower translation bounded if and only if
L_{\nu}^{-}
is positive.

(c)

\nu
is a Delone measure if and only if
L_{\nu}^{-}
is positive and
L_{\nu}^{+}
is finite.

Proof.

(c) follows from (a) and (b). The claims (a
\Rightarrow
) and (b
\Rightarrow
) follow from Lemma
3.7
. In order to prove the remaining claims, fix a Haar measure
m
on
G
.

(a
\Leftarrow
) Assume
L_{\nu}^{+}<\infty
. Then there exist a compact set
K
and a finite constant
C
such that
\nu(A)\leq Cm(KA)
for all compact sets
A
of positive measure. Choosing
A=Bx
for some compact symmetric unit neighbouhood
B
, the claim follows from unimodularity of
G
.

(b
\Leftarrow
) Assume that
L_{\nu}^{-}>0
. Then there exist a compact set
K
and a positive constant
C
such that
\nu(KA)\geq Cm(A)
for all compact sets
A
of positive measure. Choosing
A=Bx
for some compact symmetric unit neighbouhood
B
, the claim follows from unimodularity of
G
.
∎

Due to the above standard estimates, Leptin densities of Delone measures characterise amenability of unimodular locally compact groups.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
G
be a unimodular locally compact group. Let
\nu
be any Delone measure on
G
. Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

G
is amenable.

(ii)

L^{-}_{\nu}\leq L^{+}_{\nu}
.

(iii)

L^{-}_{\nu}
is finite.

(iv)

L^{+}_{\nu}
is positive.

Proof.

The implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) is Lemma
3.6
. For the implication (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) assume
L^{-}_{\nu}=\infty
. Then (ii) implies
L^{+}_{\nu}=\infty
. Hence by Lemma
3.7
we have
I(G)=0
and thus
L^{-}_{\nu}=0
, which is a contradiction. For the implication (iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv), note that (iii) excludes the case
I(G)=\infty
by Lemma
3.7
. In view of Lemma
3.1
, we have
I(G)=1
.
But then
L^{+}_{\nu}>0
by Lemma
3.7
. For the implication (iv)
\Rightarrow
(i), note that (iv) excludes the case
I(G)=\infty
by Lemma
3.7
, hence
I(G)=1
by Lemma
3.1
. Thus,
G
is amenable by Theorem
3.2
.
∎

4.
Leptin densities and Beurling densities

4.1.
Leptin densities and Beurling densities

Often uniform densities are used, which are invariant under shifting the averaging sequence or the measure. These densities are sometimes called Beurling densities or Beurling-Landau densities, see
[
7
, p. 346]
and
[
32
, p. 47]
for
G=\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In ergodic theory they are known by the name Banach densities. In fact these densities can be defined without resorting to an averaging sequence. For discrete groups, this is studied in
[
15
]
. The case
G=\mathbb{R}^{d}
is considered in
[
19
]
by a linear functional approach.

Definition 4.1

(Beurling densities)
.

Assume that
G
is a unimodular locally compact group with left Haar measure
m
.
Let
\nu
be a measure on
G
and consider

B^{-}_{\nu}=\sup_{A\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}}\inf_{s\in G}\frac{\nu(As)}{m(A)}\ ,\qquad B^{+}_{\nu}=\inf_{A\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}}\sup_{s\in G}\frac{\nu(As)}{m(A)}\ .

We call
B_{\nu}^{-}
and
B_{\nu}^{+}
the lower Beurling density respectively the upper Beurling density of the measure
\nu
. If
B_{\nu}^{-}=B_{\nu}^{+}=B_{\nu}
, we call
B_{\nu}
the Beurling density of
\nu
.

In the following, we will suppress the subscript
\nu
if the reference to the measure is clear.

Remark 4.2
.

Note that
B^{+}_{\nu}<\infty
if
\nu
is upper translation bounded, as a consequence of Lemma
2.4
.
The attribution to Beurling is motivated by Proposition
5.13
below, which states that, for unimodular groups, the above densities coincide with a definition based on averaging.

Let us discuss the relation between Beurling densities and Leptin densities.
Beyond the amenable situation, these notions differ. Indeed, for any left Haar measure
m
on a unimodular non-amenable group
G
, we have
B^{+}_{m}=B^{-}_{m}=1
, but
L^{-}_{m}=\infty
and
L^{+}_{m}=0
.
It follows from the next theorem that, for a locally finite measure
\nu
on a unimodular amenable group, the apriori different notions of Beurling density and Leptin density coincide. Precisely, the lower Beurling density of
\nu
coincides with its lower Leptin density and the upper Beurling density of
\nu
coincides with its upper Leptin density.

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume that
G
is a unimodular locally compact group. Consider any locally finite measure
\nu
on
G
. For the densities associated to
\nu
we then have

B^{-}_{\nu}\leq L^{-}_{\nu}\leq B^{-}_{\nu}\cdot I(G)\ ,\qquad I(G)^{-1}\cdot B^{+}_{\nu}\leq L^{+}_{\nu}\leq B^{+}_{\nu}\ .

In particular if
G
is additionally amenable, then we have
B^{-}_{\nu}=L^{-}_{\nu}\leq L^{+}_{\nu}=B^{+}_{\nu}
.

4.2.
Proof of Theorem
4.3

We will treat the four inequalities separately. The inequalities
L^{-}\leq B^{-}\cdot I(G)
and
B^{+}\leq L^{+}\cdot I(G)
follow from elementary estimates.

Proof of
L^{-}\leq B^{-}\cdot I(G)
.

Consider any
A\in\mathcal{K}_{p}
, any nonempty
K\in\mathcal{K}
and any
s\in G
. We have

\frac{\nu(KAs)}{m(A)}=\frac{m(KA)}{m(A)}\cdot\frac{\nu(KAs)}{m(KA)}\ .

Taking the infimum over
A\in\mathcal{K}
on the lhs and using unimodularity of
G
, we see that the lhs is independent of
s\in G
. Hence we may take the infimum over
s
on the rhs and arrive at

\inf_{A\in\mathcal{K}_{p}}\frac{\nu(KA)}{m(A)}\leq\frac{m(KA)}{m(A)}\cdot\inf_{s\in G}\frac{\nu(KAs)}{m(KA)}\leq\frac{m(KA)}{m(A)}\cdot B^{-}\ .

As the lhs is independent of
A\in\mathcal{K}_{p}
, we may take the infimum over
A
on the rhs and the supremum over
K
on both sides to conclude

L^{-}=\sup_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\inf_{A\in\mathcal{K}_{p}}\frac{\nu(KA)}{m(A)}\leq\sup_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\inf_{A\in\mathcal{K}_{p}}\frac{m(KA)}{m(A)}\cdot B^{-}\leq I(G)\cdot B^{-}\ .

∎

Proof of
I(G)^{-1}\cdot B^{+}\leq L^{+}
.

Consider any
A\in\mathcal{K}_{p}
, nonempty
K\in\mathcal{K}
and any
s\in G
and note that by unimodularity of
G
we have

\frac{m(A)}{m(KA)}\cdot\frac{\nu(As)}{m(A)}=\frac{\nu(As)}{m(KA)}\leq\sup_{A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}_{p}}\frac{\nu(A^{\prime})}{m(KA^{\prime})}\ ,

where the rhs is independent of
s\in G
. Hence we may take the supremum over
s
on the lhs and arrive at

\frac{m(A)}{m(KA)}\cdot B^{+}\leq\frac{m(A)}{m(KA)}\cdot\sup_{s\in G}\frac{\nu(As)}{m(A)}\leq\sup_{A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}_{p}}\frac{\nu(A^{\prime})}{m(KA^{\prime})}\ .

As the rhs is independent of
A\in\mathcal{K}_{p}
,
we may take the supremum over
A
on the lhs and the infimum over
K
on both sides to conclude

I(G)^{-1}\cdot B^{+}\leq L^{+}
.
∎

The proofs of
B^{-}\leq L^{-}
and of
L^{+}\leq B^{+}
are more delicate. We will follow ideas from
[
15
, Lem. 2.9]
. Note the following two elementary results.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
G
be a unimodular locally compact group. Fix a left Haar measure
m
on
G
and let
\nu
be a locally finite measure on
G
.
Then we have for any compact sets
A,B\subseteq G
that

(4.1)

\int_{A}\nu(aB)\,{\rm d}m(a)=\int_{B}\nu(Ab)\,{\rm d}m(b)\ .

Proof.

We evaluate the lhs and use Tonelli’s theorem, which is applicable as
\nu
is assumed to be locally finite, and
A
and
B
are compact sets. We obtain

\begin{split}\int_{A}\nu(aB)\,{\rm d}m(a)&=\int_{A}\int_{aB}{\rm d}\nu(x)\,{\rm d}m(a)=\int_{G}\int_{A\cap xB^{-1}}\!\!\!\!\!{\rm d}m(a)\,{\rm d}\nu(x)=\int_{G}m(A\cap xB^{-1})\,{\rm d}\nu(x)\ ,\end{split}

where we used
x\in aB
iff
a\in xB^{-1}
.
Evaluating the rhs and using Tonelli’s theorem yield

\begin{split}\int_{B}\nu(Ab)\,{\rm d}m(b)&=\int_{B}\int_{Ab}{\rm d}\nu(x)\,{\rm d}m(b)=\int_{G}\int_{B\cap A^{-1}x}\,{\rm d}m(b)\,{\rm d}\nu(x)=\int_{G}m(B\cap A^{-1}x)\,{\rm d}\nu(x)\ ,\end{split}

where we used
x\in Ab
iff
b\in A^{-1}x
.
Now equality follows from unimodularity and left invariance of the Haar measure. Indeed we have

m(B\cap A^{-1}x)=m(B^{-1}\cap x^{-1}A)=m(xB^{-1}\cap A)\ .

∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
G
be a unimodular locally compact group. Fix a left Haar measure
m
on
G
and let
\nu
be a locally finite measure on
G
.
Let
A,B\subseteq G
be compact sets. Assume that there is a positive finite constant
C=C(A,B)
such that for all
b\in B
we have

\nu(Ab)\geq C\cdot m(A)\ .

Then there exists
a\in A
such that

\nu(aB)\geq C\cdot m(B)\ .

Remark 4.6
.

In the two inequalities of the above lemma, replace “
\geq
” by “
\leq
”. Then the resulting statement remains true, as the following proof shows.

Proof.

Let
A,B
be compact sets and assume that the assertion of the lemma is wrong. Then we have for all
a\in A
that
\nu(aB)<C\cdot m(B)
. Integrate this inequality to obtain

(4.2)

\int_{A}\nu(aB)\,{\rm d}m(a)<C\cdot m(A)\cdot m(B)\ .

On the other hand, the inequality in the assumption of the lemma can be integrated to yield

(4.3)

\int_{B}\nu(Ab)\,{\rm d}m(b)\geq C\cdot m(A)\cdot m(B)\ .

But the left hand sides of the above two inequalities are equal due to Lemma
4.4
. This is contradictory.
∎

Proof of
B^{-}\leq L^{-}
.

Recall from Definition
4.1
that

B^{-}=\sup_{A\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}}\inf_{s\in G}\frac{\nu(As)}{m(A)}\ ,\qquad L^{-}=\sup_{K\in{\mathcal{K}}}\inf_{A\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}}\frac{\nu(KA)}{m(A)}\ .

Fix arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
and take
A\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}
such that for all
s\in G
we have

B^{-}-\varepsilon\leq\frac{\nu(As)}{m(A)}\ .

Let now
S\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}
be arbitrary. Recall that
m(A)\cdot(B^{-}-\varepsilon)\leq\nu(As)
for all
s\in S
. Hence by Lemma
4.5
there exists
a\in A
such that
m(S)\cdot(B^{-}-\varepsilon)\leq\nu(aS)\leq\nu(AS)
. Hence we have

B^{-}-\varepsilon\leq\frac{\nu(AS)}{m(S)}\ .

As
S\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}
and
\varepsilon>0
were arbitrary, the claim follows.
∎

Proof of
L^{+}\leq B^{+}
.

Recall from Definition
4.1
that

L^{+}=\inf_{K\in{\mathcal{K}}}\sup_{A\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}}\frac{\nu(A)}{m(KA)}\ ,\qquad B^{+}=\inf_{A\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}}\sup_{s\in G}\frac{\nu(As)}{m(A)}\ .

Assume without loss of generality that
B^{+}<\infty
. Fix arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
and take
A\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}
such that for all
s\in G
we have

\frac{\nu(As)}{m(A)}\leq B^{+}+\varepsilon\ .

Let now
S\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}
be arbitrary. Since
\nu(At)\leq(B^{+}+\varepsilon)\cdot m(A)
for all
t\in A^{-1}S
, by Remark
4.6
there exists
a\in A
such that
\nu(aA^{-1}S)\leq(B^{+}+\varepsilon)\cdot m(A^{-1}S)
. Since
S\subseteq aA^{-1}S
, we have

\frac{\nu(S)}{m(A^{-1}S)}\leq B^{+}+\varepsilon\ .

As
S\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}
and
\varepsilon>0
were arbitrary, the claim follows.
∎

5.
Følner conditions and Følner nets

As for Følner sequences or nets in locally compact groups there are various definitions in the literature depending on the definition of boundary. In this section we clarify some subtle connections, most of which can be found in the literature.

5.1.
Various boundaries

The geometric intuition behind amenability is that, for suitable sets
A
, the measure of the boundary of
A
is small when compared to the measure of
A
. Traditionally this is formalised using the so-called Følner boundaries

\delta^{K}A:=KA\,\triangle\,A=\big(KA\cap A^{c}\big)\cup\big((KA)^{c}\cap A\big)\ .

Here and also in the sequel of this text,
\triangle
denotes the symmetric set difference, i.e.,
A\,\triangle\,B=(A\cap B^{c})\cup(A^{c}\cap B)
. We write
A^{c}:=G\setminus A
for the complement set of
A
.

As we discuss in the following sections, it is often useful to consider different notions of boundaries such as the
van Hove boundary
, defined as

\partial^{K}A:=(KA\cap\overline{A^{c}})\cup(K^{-1}\overline{A^{c}}\cap A)

or the
strong Følner boundary
, defined as

\partial_{K}A:=K^{-1}A\cap K^{-1}A^{c}=\{g\in G:Kg\cap A\neq\varnothing\wedge Kg\cap A^{c}\neq\varnothing\}

for sets
K,A\subseteq G
.

Remark 5.1
.

The attribution to van Hove is due to Schlottmann
[
55
]
. Van Hove boundaries are often used in model set analysis, see
[
3
]
and
[
46
, Sec. 2]
for further background.
Strong Følner boundaries have been introduced and used by Ornstein and Weiss in
[
47
]
.
The name strong Følner boundary is coined in
[
49
]
. Also note the slightly different definition in
[
25
]
, which is more in line with the notion of van Hove boundary, compare
[
25
, Rem. 2.2(ii)]
.
Such boundaries naturally arise in tiling problems leading to sub- or almost additive convergence results beyond Euclidean space, cf. e.g.
[
37
,
30
,
13
,
49
,
25
]
.

Note that the van Hove boundary and the strong Følner boundary are both monotonic in
K
, in contrast to the classical Følner boundary. Strong Følner boundaries additionally satisfy the simple relation
L\partial_{K}A\subseteq\partial_{KL^{-1}}A
, which is very convenient in calculations. Let us collect some relations between the three types of boundary for later use.

Lemma 5.2

(Boundary comparison)
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group.
Let
K\subseteq G
be any symmetric unit neighborhood and
A\subseteq G
be arbitrary. Then

\partial_{K}A\subseteq\partial^{K}A\subseteq\partial_{K^{2}}A\ ,\qquad\delta^{K}A\subseteq\partial_{K}A\subseteq K\delta^{K}A\ ,\qquad\partial_{K}(KA)\subseteq\delta^{K^{2}}A\ .

Remark 5.3
.

The statements comparing the boundaries
\partial_{K}
and
\delta^{K}
can be found in
[
30
, Lem. 2.2]
. The inclusion
\partial_{K}(KA)\subseteq\delta^{K^{2}}A
has been observed in
[
47
]
and leads to the uniform Følner condition (UFC) in
[
47
, p. 19]
. We provide the proofs for the sake of self-containment.

Proof.

Let
K=K^{-1}
be any symmetric unit neighborhood.
We have to show five inclusions which we enumerate by (i)–(v) in its order of appearance in the statement.

(i) To show
\partial_{K}A\subseteq\partial^{K}A
, let
x\in KA\cap KA^{c}
and assume
x\notin KA\cap\overline{A^{c}}
. Then
x\in A
, which implies
x\in KA\cap KA^{c}\cap A=A\cap KA^{c}\subseteq A\cap K\overline{A^{c}}\subseteq\partial^{K}A
.

(ii) Noting
\overline{A^{c}}\subseteq KA^{c}
we have
KA\cap\overline{A^{c}}\subseteq KA\cap KA^{c}\subseteq K^{2}A\cap K^{2}A^{c}
. Likewise we have
K\overline{A^{c}}\cap A\subseteq KKA^{c}\cap KA\subseteq K^{2}A^{c}\cap K^{2}A
. We thus have
\partial^{K}A\subseteq\partial_{K^{2}}A
.

(iii) As
(KA)^{c}\subseteq KA^{c}
, we have

\delta^{K}A=(KA\cap A^{c})\cup((KA)^{c}\cap A)\subseteq(KA\cap A^{c})\cup(KA^{c}\cap A)\subseteq\partial_{K}A
.

(iv) We have
\partial_{K}A=KA\cap KA^{c}\cap(A\cup A^{c})=(A\cap KA^{c})\cup(KA\cap A^{c})
. As we have
A\cap KA^{c}\subseteq K(KA\cap A^{c})
, this implies
\partial_{K}A\subseteq K\delta^{K}A
.

(v) We have
\partial_{K}(KA)=K(KA)\cap K(KA)^{c}=K^{2}A\cap K(KA)^{c}\subseteq K^{2}A\cap A^{c}\subseteq\delta^{K^{2}}A
.
∎

Let us denote by
\mathcal{K}_{s0}\subseteq\mathcal{K}
the collection of compact sets which are symmetric and contain the identity. The following proposition characterizes amenability. It is well-known, we give a proof for the convenience of the reader.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group with left Haar measure
m
. Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

For all
K\in\mathcal{K}
we have
\inf_{A\in\mathcal{K}_{p}}m(KA)/m(A)=1
.

(ii)

For all
K\in\mathcal{K}
we have
\inf_{A\in\mathcal{K}_{p}}m(\delta^{K}A)/m(A)=0
.

(iii)

For all
K\in\mathcal{K}
we have
\inf_{A\in\mathcal{K}_{p}}m(\partial_{K}A)/m(A)=0
.

(iv)

For all
K\in\mathcal{K}
we have
\inf_{A\in\mathcal{K}_{p}}m(\partial^{K}A)/m(A)=0
.

In fact the claimed equivalences continue to hold if in any of the four statements (i) to (iv) the condition “
K\in\mathcal{K}
” is replaced by “
K\in\mathcal{K}_{s0}
”.

Proof.

We will first prove the claimed equivalences for
K\in\mathcal{K}_{s0}
.
The equivalence of (i) and (ii) is a direct consequence of
A\,\dot{\cup}\,\delta^{K}A=KA
, which holds as
e\in K
.
For (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii), let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary and choose
A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}_{p}
such that
m(\delta^{K^{2}}A^{\prime})/m(A^{\prime})<\varepsilon
. Letting
A=KA^{\prime}
, we then estimate

\partial_{K}A=\partial_{K}(KA^{\prime})\subseteq\delta^{K^{2}}A^{\prime}
.
As we have
m(A)=m(KA^{\prime})\geq m(A^{\prime})
, we obtain
m(\partial_{K}A)/m(A)\leq m(\delta^{K^{2}}A^{\prime})/m(A^{\prime})<\varepsilon
. As
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, then claim follows.

The implications (iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv) and (iv)
\Rightarrow
(ii) follow from the comparison lemma
5.2
. Indeed that lemma states
\partial^{K}A\subseteq\partial_{K^{2}}A
and
\delta^{K}A\subseteq\partial^{K}A
.

Consider now the general case
K\in\mathcal{K}
in the above statements. Then statements
(i)
,
(iii)
,
(iv)
hold if these statements hold for
K\in\mathcal{K}_{0s}
, as the corresponding measure ratios are monotonic in
K
. With respect to
(ii)
, note
\delta^{K}A\subseteq\partial_{K^{-1}\cup\{e\}}A
. Hence
(ii)
follows from
(iii)
, which is already established. ∎

5.2.
Følner nets

Consider a Delone measure
\nu
on an amenable locally compact group
G
. In that case, one might ask whether there exists a sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of compact sets in
G
such that the Leptin density
L_{\nu}^{-}
is realised on that sequence, instead of taking the infimum over all
A\in\mathcal{K}_{p}
. Natural candidates are Følner sequences, which exist in any
\sigma
-compact amenable group. For general locally compact groups, amenability is characterized by the existence of Følner nets. We refer to
[
48
, Ch. 4]
for the construction of Følner nets.
For the convenience of the reader, background about nets is collected in Appendix
A
.

Definition 5.5

(Følner net)
.

Let
G
be an amenable locally compact group with left Haar measure
m
. Let
(\mathbb{I},\prec)
be a directed poset and let
(A_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\in\mathcal{(}\mathcal{K}_{p})^{\mathbb{I}}
. Then
(A_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
is called a Følner net if

\sup_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\lim_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{m(\delta^{K}A_{i})}{m(A_{i})}=0\ .

Remark 5.6
.

Note that any subnet of a Følner net is a Følner net.
If
G
is unimodular and
(A_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
is a Følner net, then
(A_{i}x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
is a Følner net for every net
(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\in G^{\mathbb{I}}
. This is a direct consequence of right-invariance of the Haar measure in that case.
For
\sigma
-compact amenable
G
, there always exists a Følner sequence. See the proof of
[
48
, Thm. 4.16]
.

Clearly, one can define alternative Følner nets or sequences using
\partial^{K}
or
\partial_{K}
as a notion of boundary. This has lead to the terminology of
van Hove sequences
or
strong Følner sequences
in the literature. Let us introduce these notions in the general context of nets. Namely,
(A_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\in(\mathcal{K}_{p})^{\mathbb{I}}
is called
strong Følner net
, respectively a
van Hove net
if

\sup_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\lim_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{m\big(\partial_{K}A_{i}\big)}{m(A_{i})}=0,\quad\mbox{ respectively }\quad\sup_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\lim_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{m\big(\partial^{K}A_{i}\big)}{m(A_{i})}=0.

It is obvious from the boundary comparison lemma
5.2
that the notions of strong Følner net and van Hove net coincide, a fact that has already been remarked in
[
25
, Rem. 2.2(ii)]
. In the remainder, we will mostly work with strong Følner boundaries.
It is easy to see that every strong Følner net is a Følner net, since
\delta^{K}A\subseteq\partial_{K^{-1}\cup\{e\}}A
for all
K\in\mathcal{K}
and each
A\in\mathcal{K}_{p}
. An example of a Følner sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
G=\mathbb{R}
that is not a strong Følner sequence can be provided by compact nowhere dense sets
A_{n}\subset[0,n]
of Lebesgue measure
n-1/n
, see
[
61
]
. An example in
G=\mathbb{R}^{d}
has been given in
[
59
, Appendix, Ex. 3.4]
. The next proposition characterizes strong Følner nets. Whereas parts of it are known, we give a proof for the convenience of the reader.

Proposition 5.7

(Characterization of strong Følner nets)
.

Let
G
be an amenable locally compact group with left Haar measure
m
.
Let
(A_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\in\mathcal{K}_{p}^{\mathbb{I}}
.
Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

(A_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
is a strong Følner net.

(ii)

(A_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
is a Følner net and there is an open unit neighborhood
O
such that

\lim_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{m\big(\bigcap_{o\in O}oA_{i}\big)}{m(A_{i})}=1.

(iii)

(A_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
is a Følner net and for every compact symmetric unit neighborhood
K

\lim_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{m\big(\bigcap_{k\in K}kA_{i}\big)}{m(A_{i})}=1.

(iv)

For all
K,L\in\mathcal{K}
we have
\lim_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{m(L\delta^{K}A_{i})}{m(A_{i})}=0
.

(v)

For all
K,L\in\mathcal{K}
we have
\lim_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{m(L\partial^{K}A_{i})}{m(A_{i})}=0
.

(vi)

For all
K,L\in\mathcal{K}
we have
\lim_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{m(L\partial_{K}A_{i})}{m(A_{i})}=0
.

In fact the same equivalences (iv), (v) and (vi) hold if “
K,L\in\mathcal{K}
” is replaced by “
K,L\in\mathcal{K}_{s0}
” in any of the above statements.
In particular,
(A_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
is a van Hove net (sequence) if and only if it is a strong Følner net (sequence).
If
G
is additionally assumed to be discrete, then every Følner net (sequence) is also a strong Følner net (sequence).

Remark 5.8
.

The equivalence (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii) already appears in
[
59
, Appendix, (3.K)]
. It shows that Følner nets are strong Følner nets if the they display some asymptotic invariance with respect to small topological pertubations. In this situation, one gets with (iii) asymptotic invariance for the “inner part”
A_{i}\setminus\mathrm{Int}_{K}(A_{i})
, where
\mathrm{Int}_{K}(A)=\{g\in A:\,Kg\subseteq A\}
for a compact symmetric unit neighborhood
K
and a set
A
.
The observation that for discrete groups, the notions of a Følner net (or sequence) and a strong Følner net (or sequence) are equivalent has also been observed in
[
30
, Prop. 2.3]
and
[
13
, Prop. 2.4]
. Note also that the authors deal in
[
13
]
with semigroups and with boundary defined as the inner part of the strong Følner boundary, i.e.
\widetilde{\partial}_{K}A:=A\setminus\mathrm{Int}_{K}(A)
.

Proof.

We have indicated above that every strong Følner net is a Følner net. In order to complete the proof of the implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii), let
O
be an open relatively compact, symmetric unit neighborhood.
Since
A_{i}\setminus\bigcap_{o\in O}oA_{i}\subseteq\partial_{O}(A_{i})
, and since
(A_{i})
is a strong Følner net by (i), we obtain

\liminf_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{m\big(\bigcap_{o\in O}oA_{i}\big)}{m(A_{i})}\geq\Bigg(1-\limsup_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{m\big(\partial_{O}A_{i}\big)}{m(A_{i})}\Bigg)=1.

We turn to the proof of (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii). Let
K
be any compact symmetric unit neighborhood.
We find a finite cover of
K
by left-translates
s_{j}O
. Then

\displaystyle\limsup_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{m\big(A_{i}\setminus\bigcap_{k\in k}kA_{i}\big)}{m(A_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{m\big(A_{i}\setminus\bigcap_{j}\bigcap_{o\in O}s_{j}oA_{i}\big)}{m(A_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\sum_{j}\frac{m\big(s_{j}^{-1}A_{i}\setminus\bigcap_{o\in O}oA_{i}\big)}{m(A_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}\Bigg(\limsup_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{m\big(s_{j}^{-1}A_{i}\,\triangle\,A_{i}\big)}{m(A_{i})}+\limsup_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{m\big(A_{i}\setminus\bigcap_{o\in O}oA_{i}\big)}{m(A_{i})}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=0.

Note that we used for the last equality that
(A_{i})
is a Følner net and the assumption on
O
. This shows the assertion (iii). We turn to the proof of the implication (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i). By Lemma
5.2
and the fact that
(A_{i})
is a Følner net,

\displaystyle\limsup_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{m\big(\partial_{K}A_{i}\big)}{m(A_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{m\big(K(KA_{i}\setminus A_{i}))}{m(A_{i})}\leq\limsup_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{m\big(K^{2}A_{i}\setminus\bigcap_{k\in K}kA_{i}\big)}{m(A_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{m(K^{2}A_{i}\setminus A_{i})}{m(A_{i})}+\limsup_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{m(A_{i}\setminus\bigcap_{k\in K}kA_{i})}{m(A_{i})}

\displaystyle=0.

Due to the monotonicity of the strong Følner boundary in
K
, the same is true for general
K\in\mathcal{K}
. Hence
(A_{i})
is a strong Følner net. The equivalence of (i) and (vi) is due to the relation
L\partial_{K}A\subseteq\partial_{KL^{-1}}A
for general compact sets
K,L,A
. If restricting oneself to compact symmetric unit neighborhoods
K
and
L
, the equivalences (iv)
\Leftrightarrow
(v)
\Leftrightarrow
(vi) follow from the boundary comparison lemma, Lemma
5.2
. Due to monotonicity, these assertions are equivalent when formulated for general
K,L\in\mathcal{K}
. Assuming in addition that
G
is discrete, then the implication (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) shows that every Følner net is a strong Følner net.
∎

It is well known that existence of a Følner sequence implies existence of a strong Følner sequence, cf.
[
49
, Lem. 2.6]
or
[
61
, Lem. 2.2]
. Here we describe how a strong Følner net can be constructed from any Følner net by a simple “thickening procedure”.

Proposition 5.9

(Construction of strong Følner nets)
.

Let
G
be an amenable locally compact group with left Haar measure
m
. If
(A_{i})
is a Følner net and
L
is a compact symmetric unit neighborhood, then the net
(A_{i}^{\prime})
with
A_{i}^{\prime}:=LA_{i}
is a strong Følner net.

Proof.

We first note that
(A_{i}^{\prime})
is a Følner net which follows from the Følner property of
(A_{i})
and the inclusion

A_{i}^{\prime}\,\triangle\,KA_{i}^{\prime}\subseteq\big(LA_{i}\,\triangle\,A_{i}\big)\cup\big(A_{i}\setminus KA_{i}\big)\cup\big(KLA_{i}\setminus A_{i}\big)

for arbitrary
K\in\mathcal{K}
.
Further, take an open symmetric unit neighborhood
O\subseteq L
. We claim that
A_{i}\subseteq\bigcap_{o\in O}oA_{i}^{\prime}
. Indeed, for any
a\in A_{i}
and each
o\in O
, we have
a=oo^{-1}a\subseteq oA_{i}^{\prime}
. Using that
(A_{i})
is a Følner net, we obtain

\liminf_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{m\big(\bigcap_{o\in O}oA_{i}^{\prime}\big)}{m(A_{i}^{\prime})}\geq\liminf_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{m(A_{i})}{m(LA_{i})}=1.

Now the claim follows from the previous proposition, equivalence (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii).
∎

The following lemma shows that (left)-asymptotic invariance observed via strong Følner nets carries over to upper translation bounded measures.
For
\sigma
-compact LCA groups, weaker versions of it are well known, see e.g.
[
34
, Prop. 9.1]
and
[
46
, Lem. 2.2, Prop. 5.1]
.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
G
be a unimodular amenable locally compact group with Haar measure
m
. Let
(\mathbb{I},\prec)
be a directed poset and let
(A_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
be a strong Følner net. Let
\nu
be a measure on
G
that is upper translation bounded. We then have

\sup_{K,L\in\mathcal{K}}\lim_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\sup_{s\in G}\frac{\nu(L\delta^{K}A_{i}s)}{m(A_{i})}=\sup_{K,L\in\mathcal{K}}\lim_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\sup_{s\in G}\frac{\nu(L\partial_{K}A_{i}s)}{m(A_{i})}=\sup_{K,L\in\mathcal{K}}\lim_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\sup_{s\in G}\frac{\nu(L\partial^{K}A_{i}s)}{m(A_{i})}=0\ .

Proof.

We use Lemma
2.4
to transfer the properties in Lemma
5.7
to the measure
\nu
. For instance, for the strong Følner boundary this yields

\lim_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\sup_{s\in G}\frac{\nu(L\partial_{K}A_{i}s)}{m(A_{i})}\leq\lim_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{C_{u}}{m(B_{u})}\frac{m(B_{u}L\partial_{K}A_{i})}{m(A_{i})}=0\ ,

where we used right invariance of the Haar measure in the estimate.
∎

5.3.
Densities on Følner nets in unimodular groups

In this section, we restrict ourselves to amenable locally compact groups that are unimodular. We define densities of a measure via averaging with respect to a Følner net.

Definition 5.11

(
{\mathcal{A}}
-densities)
.

Let
G
be a unimodular amenable locally compact group with left Haar measure
m
.
Let
\nu
be a measure on
G
, and let
{\mathcal{A}}=(A_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
be a strong Følner net in
G
. We define the upper and lower densities of
\nu
with respect to
{\mathcal{A}}
by

\begin{split}D^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}=\liminf_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{1}{m(A_{i})}\nu(A_{i})\ ,&\qquad D^{+}_{\mathcal{A}}=\limsup_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{1}{m(A_{i})}\nu(A_{i})\\
B^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}=\liminf_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{1}{m(A_{i})}\inf_{s\in G}\nu(A_{i}s)\ ,&\qquad B^{+}_{\mathcal{A}}=\limsup_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{1}{m(A_{i})}\sup_{s\in G}\nu(A_{i}s)\ .\end{split}

If
D^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}=D^{+}_{\mathcal{A}}=D_{\mathcal{A}}
, we say that
\nu
has
{\mathcal{A}}
-density
D_{\mathcal{A}}
.

Remark 5.12
.

We have
0\leq B^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}\leq D^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}\leq D^{+}_{\mathcal{A}}\leq B^{+}_{\mathcal{A}}\leq\infty
by definition. If
\nu
is upper translation bounded, we have
B^{+}_{\mathcal{A}}<\infty
. If
\nu
is lower translation bounded, we have
B^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}>0
.

Proposition 5.13
.

Assume that
G
is a unimodular amenable locally compact group with left Haar measure
m
.
Let
\nu
be an upper translation bounded measure on
G
. Take any strong Følner net
{\mathcal{A}}=(A_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
in
G
. We then have

B^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}=\lim_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{1}{m(A_{i})}\inf_{s\in G}\nu(A_{i}s)\ ,\qquad B^{+}_{\mathcal{A}}=\lim_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{1}{m(A_{i})}\sup_{s\in G}\nu(A_{i}s)\ .

Moreover we have

0\leq L^{-}=B^{-}=B^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}\leq D^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}\leq D^{+}_{\mathcal{A}}\leq B^{+}_{\mathcal{A}}=B^{+}=L^{+}<\infty\ ,

where
L^{-},L^{+}
are the Leptin densities from Definition
3.3
, and where
B^{-},B^{+}
are the Beurling densities from Definition
4.1
.
In particular, the above
\mathcal{A}
-densities do not depend on the choice of the strong Følner sequence.

Remark 5.14
.

In the following proof, note that upper translation boundedness and the strong version of the Følner property only enter in showing that
B^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}=B_{-}
. Assuming only local finiteness of
\nu
we still have
B^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}\leq B^{-}=L^{-}\leq L^{+}=B^{+}\leq B^{+}_{\mathcal{A}}
.

Proof.

Consider

C^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}=\limsup_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{1}{m(A_{i})}\inf_{s\in G}\nu(A_{i}s)\ ,\qquad C^{+}_{\mathcal{A}}=\liminf_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{1}{m(A_{i})}\sup_{s\in G}\nu(A_{i}s)\ .

(i) Note that for each
i\in\mathbb{I}
we have

\frac{1}{m(A_{i})}\inf_{s\in G}\nu(A_{i}s)\leq\sup_{A\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}}\frac{1}{m(A)}\inf_{s\in G}\nu(As)=B^{-}\ ,

which gives
C^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}\leq B^{-}
. If
L^{-}\leq C^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}
, then
C^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}=B^{-}=L^{-}
by Theorem
4.3
. To show
L^{-}\leq C^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}
, we take arbitrary
d<L^{-}
. Take
K\in\mathcal{K}
such that for all
A\in\mathcal{K}_{p}
we have
d\leq\nu(KA)/m(A)
.
Choosing
A=A_{i}s
for
i\in\mathbb{I}
we obtain the estimate

d\leq\frac{\nu(KA_{i}s)}{m(A_{i})}\leq\frac{\nu(A_{i}s)}{m(A_{i})}+\frac{\nu((\delta^{K}A_{i})s)}{m(A_{i})}\,,

where we used the estimate
KAs\subseteq As\cup(\delta^{K}A)s
, and the unimodularity of
G
. Now upper translation boundedness of
\nu
, right invariance of the Haar measure and the strong Følner property of
{\mathcal{A}}
yield
d\leq C^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}
, cf. Lemma
5.10
. As
d<L^{-}
was arbitrary, we infer
L^{-}\leq C^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}
. Therefore, we have
C^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}=B^{-}=L^{-}
.

(ii)
Since for all
i\in\mathbb{I}
we have

\frac{1}{m(A_{i})}\sup_{s\in G}\nu(A_{i}s)\geq\inf_{A\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}}\frac{1}{m(A)}\sup_{s\in G}\nu(As)=B^{+}\,,

we get
B^{+}\leq C^{+}_{\mathcal{A}}
. If
C^{+}_{\mathcal{A}}\leq L^{+}
, then
C^{+}_{\mathcal{A}}=B^{+}=L^{+}
by Theorem
4.3
, which uses local finiteness of
\nu
. Let us thus show
L^{+}\geq C^{+}_{\mathcal{A}}
. Assume without loss of generality
L^{+}<\infty
. Assume
d>L^{+}
for some finite
d
. Take
K\in\mathcal{K}
such that for all
A\in\mathcal{K}_{p}
we have
d\geq\nu(A)/m(KA)
. Choosing
A=A_{i}s
for
i\in\mathbb{I}
we obtain with the unimodularity of
G
the estimate

d\geq\frac{\nu(A_{i}s)}{m(KA_{i})}\ .

By the Følner property of
{\mathcal{A}}
this implies
d\geq C^{+}_{\mathcal{A}}
. As
d>L^{+}
was arbitrary, we infer
L^{+}\geq C^{+}_{\mathcal{A}}
. Therefore, we have
C^{+}_{\mathcal{A}}=B^{+}=L^{+}
.

(iii) By (i) and (ii), the values
C^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}
and
C^{+}_{\mathcal{A}}
are independent of the choice of the strong Følner net. This means that
C^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}
and
C^{+}_{\mathcal{A}}
are limits. In particular this implies
C^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}=B^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}\leq D^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}\leq D^{+}_{\mathcal{A}}\leq B^{+}_{\mathcal{A}}=C^{+}_{\mathcal{A}}
. Also note
B^{+}<\infty
by upper translation boundedness of
\nu
, see Remark
4.2
.
∎

6.
Leptin densities for lattice point counting

We say that
\Gamma\subseteq G
is a uniform lattice in a locally compact group
G
if
\Gamma
is a countable discrete cocompact subgroup of
G
.
Recall that existence of a uniform lattice in
G
implies that
G
is unimodular, see e.g.
[
14
, Thm. 9.1.6]
. A lattice is both left-uniformly discrete and right-uniformly discrete.
Analogously, a uniform lattice
\Gamma
in
G
is both left-relatively dense and right-relatively dense. Hence it admits a measurable, relatively compact left-fundamental domain and also a measurable, relatively compact right-fundamental domain. For a proof, see Theorem 1 and Remark 6 in
[
17
]
.
Since we are only interested in the covolume of a fundamental domain we can make this choice freely and fix a relatively compact measurable left-fundamental domain
F
, i.e., we have
\Gamma F=G
and
F^{-1}\gamma_{1}\cap F^{-1}\gamma_{2}\neq\varnothing
for some
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
implies
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}
.
Moreover, for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we observe that

(6.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{card}(\Gamma\cap F^{-1}\gamma)=\operatorname{card}(\Gamma\gamma^{-1}\cap F^{-1})=\operatorname{card}(\Gamma\cap F)=1.

This fact will be used below in the proofs of the density formulas.

When identified with the Delone measure
\delta_{\Gamma}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\delta_{\gamma}
,
uniform lattices
\Gamma
have a positive finite Leptin density, which equals the reciprocal of its covolume
\mathrm{covol}(\Gamma)
. Recall that the covolume is defined as the Haar measure of any measurable relatively compact (left or right) fundamental domain.

Proposition 6.1

(Density of a uniform lattice)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a uniform lattice in a unimodular amenable locally compact group
G
. Then
\Gamma
has positive finite Leptin density

L_{\Gamma}:=L_{\delta_{\Gamma}}=\frac{1}{m(F)}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{covol}(\Gamma)}\ ,

where
F
is any measurable relatively compact (left or right) fundamental domain for
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Fix a measurable relatively compact left-fundamental domain
F
of
\Gamma
.
Define the compact set
K=\overline{F^{-1}F}
. Fix an arbitrary compact set
A\subseteq G
and consider

\Gamma_{A}=\{\gamma\in\Gamma:A\cap F^{-1}\gamma\neq\varnothing\}\ ,\qquad A_{F}=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{A}}F^{-1}\gamma\ .

Note that
\Gamma_{A}
is a finite set and
A_{F}
is a disjoint union. We have

(6.2)

A\subseteq A_{F}\subseteq KA\ .

For the second inclusion, assume that
x\in A_{F}
. Then
x\in F^{-1}\gamma
for some
\gamma\in\Gamma_{A}
and there exists some
a\in A
such that
a\in F^{-1}\gamma
. Hence
x\in F^{-1}\gamma\subseteq F^{-1}Fa\subseteq F^{-1}FA\subseteq KA
.
With equality (
6.1
) we thus have

\begin{split}m(A)&\leq m(A_{F})=m(F)\cdot\mathrm{card}(\Gamma_{A})=m(F)\cdot\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{A}}\mathrm{card}(\Gamma\cap F^{-1}\gamma)=m(F)\cdot\mathrm{card}(\Gamma\cap A_{F})\\
&\leq m(F)\cdot\mathrm{card}(\Gamma\cap KA)=m(F)\cdot\delta_{\Gamma}\big(KA\big).\end{split}

By Definition
3.3
this implies
L^{-}\geq 1/m(F)
. We can similarly estimate

\begin{split}m(KA)&\geq m(A_{F})=m(F)\cdot\mathrm{card}(\Gamma_{A})=m(F)\cdot\mathrm{card}(\Gamma\cap A_{F})\geq m(F)\cdot\delta_{\Gamma}(A)\ .\end{split}

This leads to
L^{+}\leq 1/m(F)
by Definition
3.3
. Finally, since
G
is amenable, we can use Lemma
3.6
in order to conclude
L^{-}=L^{+}
. This and the fact that the expression
1/m(F)
does not depend on
F
show that
\Gamma
has Leptin density
1/\mathrm{covol}(\Gamma)=1/m(F)
.
∎

The next result, whose proof uses a standard argument
[
58
, Lem. 2]
, will be needed in the following section.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group and let
\Gamma
be a uniform lattice in
G
. Consider any measurable relatively compact set
U\subseteq G
such that
\Gamma U=G
. Then
U
contains a measurable relatively compact left-fundamental domain for
\Gamma
in
G
.

Proof.

Let
F
be a measurable relatively compact left-fundamental domain for
\Gamma
in
G
. By relative compactness of both
F
and
U
, the set
\Gamma_{U}=\{\gamma\in\Gamma:U\cap\gamma F\neq\varnothing\}
is finite, and we can thus write
\Gamma_{U}=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\}
.
Define

F_{k}=F\cap\gamma_{k}^{-1}U\subseteq F
and note
F=\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}F_{k}
as
\Gamma U=G
.
Define
F_{1}^{\prime}=\gamma_{1}F_{1}
and

F_{k}^{\prime}=(\gamma_{k}F_{k})\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{k-1}(\Gamma F_{j})\subseteq U
for
k\geq 2
and note that
F_{U}:=\dot{\bigcup}_{k=1}^{n}F_{k}^{\prime}
is a disjoint union. Furthermore,
F_{U}
is a measurable relatively compact set which satisfies
F_{U}\subseteq U
.
We show that
F_{U}
is a left-fundamental domain for
\Gamma
. Consider any
z\in G
and choose
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
z\in\Gamma F_{k}
but
z\notin\Gamma F_{j}
for all
j<k
. Then
z\in\Gamma F_{k}^{\prime}\subseteq\Gamma F_{U}
and thus
\Gamma F_{U}=G
. Assume
F_{U}^{-1}\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\cap F_{U}^{-1}\gamma_{2}^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
for some
\gamma_{1}^{\prime},\gamma_{2}^{\prime}\in\Gamma
. Then
F_{r}^{\prime-1}\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\cap F_{s}^{\prime-1}\gamma_{2}^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
for some
r
and some
s
. We may assume
r\leq s
without loss of generality. By construction of
F_{s}^{\prime}
we then have
r=s
. Since
F_{r}^{\prime}\subseteq\gamma_{r}F
, we have
F^{-1}\gamma_{r}^{-1}\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\cap F^{-1}\gamma_{r}^{-1}\gamma_{2}^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
. Since
F
is a left-fundamental domain for
\Gamma
, this implies
\gamma^{\prime}_{1}=\gamma^{\prime}_{2}
.
∎

7.
Uniform model sets in amenable groups

We analyse the upper and lower Leptin densities of weak model sets in amenable groups. As an application we give a simple geometric proof of the density formula for regular model sets.

7.1.
Definition and elementary properties

Let
(G,H,{\mathcal{L}})
be a
cut-and-project scheme
, i.e., both
G
and
H
are unimodular locally compact groups, and
{\mathcal{L}}
is a uniform lattice in
G\times H
, which projects injectively to
G
and densely to
H
. Let us denote the canonical projections by
\pi^{G}
and
\pi^{H}
. If
W\subseteq H
is relatively compact, then the set
\Lambda_{W}=\pi^{G}({\mathcal{L}}\cap(G\times W))
is called a
weak model set
. Any weak model set is left-uniformly discrete and right-uniformly discrete. A
model set
is a weak model set which satisfies
\mathring{W}\neq\varnothing
. Here and in the sequel of this paper, we denote by
\mathring{S}
the interior of a set
S
in a topological space.
A model set is both right-relatively dense and left-relatively dense. We call a model set
regular
if
W
is Riemann measurable, i.e., if
m_{H}(\partial W)=0
, a nomenclature that is nowadays common in the abelian case
[
2
, Def. 7.2]
. Recall that the topological boundary of set
S
in a topological spaces is defined as
\partial S:=\overline{S}\cap\overline{S^{c}}
. Note that in other contexts, the definition of regular model set can be slightly different. For instance, in
[
10
,
11
]
, regular model sets are defined for non-abelian groups and homogeneous spaces arising from them via cut-and-project schemes with a slightly more restrictive notion of window but also for possibly non-uniform lattices.
We refer to
[
43
,
44
,
2
]
for further background on model sets in mostly abelian situations.

As
\pi^{H}({\mathcal{L}})
is dense in
H
, the lattice
{\mathcal{L}}
admits fundamental domains in
G\times H
that are “closely aligned to
G
”. This is formalised in the following lemma, which combines
[
27
, Lem. 2.3]
and Lemma
6.2
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
(G,H,\mathcal{L})
be a cut-and-project scheme and consider any non-empty, open and relatively compact set
U\subseteq H
. Then there exists an open relatively compact set
V\subseteq G
satisfying

\mathcal{L}(V\times U)=G\times H.

Moreover
V\times U
contains a measurable relatively compact left-fundamental domain for
{\mathcal{L}}
. ∎

7.2.
Leptin density of weak model sets

The above property allows to derive bounds on the strong upper and lower densities of any weak model sets in the same way as in the lattice case.

Theorem 7.2

(Leptin density estimates for weak model sets)
.

Let
(G,H,{\mathcal{L}})
be a cut-and-project scheme, where
G
is amenable.
Fix a relatively compact set
W\subseteq H
. We then have

\frac{m_{H}(\mathring{W})}{\mathrm{covol}(\mathcal{L})}\leq L^{-}_{\Lambda_{\mathring{W}}}\leq L^{-}_{\Lambda_{W}}\leq L^{+}_{\Lambda_{W}}\leq L^{+}_{\Lambda_{\overline{W}}}\leq\frac{m_{H}(\overline{W})}{\mathrm{covol}(\mathcal{L})}\ .

In particular, if
W
is Riemann measurable, then

L_{\Lambda_{W}}=\frac{m_{H}(W)}{\mathrm{covol}(\mathcal{L})}\ .

Remark 7.3
.

The so-called density formula
L_{\Lambda_{W}}=m_{H}(W)/\mathrm{covol}(\mathcal{L})
for Riemann measurable windows has been proven in the literature in various contexts, compare the discussion in the introduction. In
[
10
, Sec. 4.5]
the notion of covolume of a regular model set over locally compact second countable groups is defined. We emphasize that regular model sets as of
[
10
,
11
]
are defined via cut-and-project schemes with additional assumptions on
W
, but
\mathcal{L}
does not need to be uniform, and the construction works beyond the realm of amenable groups.
In the language of the present paper, the covolume of a regular model set is the inverse of its Leptin density. Sticking to the framework of
[
10
]
, a density approximation formula for regular model sets in amenable locally compact second countable groups can be derived by combining Prop. 4.13 with the ergodic theorem in Cor. 5.4 of
[
10
]
.

Proof.

(i) Consider any compact unit neighborhood
U\subseteq H
. By Lemma
7.1
, there exists a compact set
F
such that
{\mathcal{L}}(F\times U)=G\times H
, and we can pick a relatively compact measurable left-fundamental domain
{\mathcal{F}}_{U}\subseteq F\times U
.
Define
{\mathcal{K}}_{U}={\mathcal{F}}_{U}^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}_{U}
, write
K=\overline{F^{-1}F}
and note that
{\mathcal{K}}_{U}\subseteq K\times U^{-1}U
.
For an arbitrary compact set
{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq G\times H
, consider

{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathcal{A}}=\{\ell\in{\mathcal{L}}:{\mathcal{A}}\cap{\mathcal{F}}_{U}^{-1}\ell\neq\varnothing\}\ ,\qquad{\mathcal{A}}_{U}=\bigcup_{\ell\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathcal{A}}}{\mathcal{F}}_{U}^{-1}\ell\ .

Then
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathcal{A}}
is a finite set and
{\mathcal{A}}_{U}
is a disjoint union.
We have

{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{U}\subseteq{\mathcal{K}}_{U}{\mathcal{A}}\ .

For the second implication, assume that
x\in{\mathcal{A}}_{U}
. Then
x\in{\mathcal{F}}_{U}^{-1}\ell
for some
\ell\in{\mathcal{L}}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
and there exists some
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
such that
a\in{\mathcal{F}}_{U}^{-1}\ell
. Hence
x\in{\mathcal{F}}_{U}^{-1}\ell\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}_{U}^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}_{U}a\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}_{U}^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}_{U}{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq{\mathcal{K}}_{U}{\mathcal{A}}
.

(ii) We first prove the last two upper inequalities. Fix arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
.
Choose
U
in (i) sufficiently small such that
m_{H}(U^{-1}U\overline{W})\leq(1+\varepsilon)\cdot m_{H}(\overline{W})
, which is possible due to continuity of the Haar measure on
H
. For arbitrary compact
A\subseteq G
and
{\mathcal{A}}=A\times\overline{W}
we estimate with equality (
6.1
)

\begin{split}m_{G\times H}({\mathcal{K}}_{U}{\mathcal{A}})&\geq m_{G\times H}({\mathcal{A}}_{U})=m_{G\times H}({\mathcal{F}}_{U})\cdot\mathrm{card}({\mathcal{L}}_{\mathcal{A}})=m_{G\times H}({\mathcal{F}}_{U})\cdot\sum_{\ell\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathcal{A}}}\mathrm{card}({\mathcal{L}}\cap{\mathcal{F}}_{U}^{-1}\ell)\\
&=m_{G\times H}({\mathcal{F}}_{U})\cdot\mathrm{card}({\mathcal{L}}\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{U})\geq m_{G\times H}({\mathcal{F}}_{U})\cdot\mathrm{card}({\mathcal{L}}\cap{\mathcal{A}})\ .\end{split}

Combining the latter estimate with
m_{G\times H}({\mathcal{K}}_{U}{\mathcal{A}})\leq m_{G}(KA)\cdot m_{H}(U^{-1}U\overline{W})
, we arrive at

(7.1)

\begin{split}(1+\varepsilon)\cdot\frac{m_{H}(\overline{W})}{m_{G\times H}({\mathcal{F}}_{U})}&\cdot m_{G}(KA)\geq\frac{m_{H}(U^{-1}U\overline{W})}{m_{G\times H}({\mathcal{F}}_{U})}\cdot m_{G}(KA)\geq\mathrm{card}({\mathcal{L}}\cap{\mathcal{A}})\\
&\geq\mathrm{card}(\Lambda_{\overline{W}}\cap A)\geq\mathrm{card}(\Lambda_{W}\cap A)\ .\end{split}

Now the last two upper inequalities claimed in the theorem follow by definition of the upper Leptin density.

(iii) To show the first two lower inequalities, fix arbitrary

(iv) Recall that

Now the “In particular”-part of the theorem follows from

8.
Almost periodicity of regular model sets

8.1.
Almost periodicity for point sets and measures

With respect to mathematical diffraction theory, almost periodicity of the underlying point set has become a central notion in recent years. For upper translation bounded measures in
\sigma
-compact locally compact abelian groups, this is analysed in
[
35
]
. In particular, pure point diffraction of a measure is shown to be equivalent to so-called mean almost periodicity of the measure
[
35
, Thm. 2.13]
, see also below. The discussion is based on density notions using van Hove sequences. Here we will work with the stronger uniform version of mean almost periodicity, thereby avoiding averaging sequences or nets.

Definition 8.1
.

A model set
\Lambda
in an amenable locally compact group
G
is called right-uniformly mean almost periodic if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a both left- and right-relatively dense set
T\subseteq G
such that
L^{+}_{\Lambda\,\triangle\,(\Lambda t)}\leq\varepsilon
for every
t\in T
.

Remark 8.2
.

Left-uniformly mean almost periodic model sets can be defined analogously.
Recall that groups containing a model set are necessarily unimodular. Due to invariance of the Leptin densities with respect to translations, see Lemma
3.5
, the set
T
in the above definition can be chosen to be symmetric, i.e.,
T=T^{-1}
. In this situation,
T
is right-relatively dense if and only it if left-relatively dense.

Note that, for
\sigma
-compact locally compact abelian groups, the latter is a weaker notion than Weyl almost periodicity
[
35
, Def. 4.1]
, the latter being defined via approximation by trigonometric polynomials. Also note that Meyer’s and Guihéneuf’s definition of almost periodic pattern
[
24
, Def. 7]
,
[
23
, Def. 4]
and almost periodic measure
[
24
, Def. 10]
coincide with Weyl almost periodicity. Note finally that the notion of Weyl almost periodicity is based on the Weyl seminorm
[
35
, Sec. 1.3]
, which can be rephrased using the Leptin upper density, without resorting to a van Hove sequence or net.

8.2.
Uniform mean almost periodicity and model sets

We analyse (right-)uniform mean almost periodicity for regular model sets.

Theorem 8.3
.

Any regular model set in an amenable locally compact group is right-uniformly mean almost periodic.

This is an immediate consequence of the following result, which may be seen as weak variant of Lemma 3 from
[
24
]
, however without resorting to an averaging net.

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
\Lambda_{W}
be a regular model set. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a compact symmetric unit neighborhood
U\subseteq H
such that
L^{+}_{\Lambda_{(WU)\cap(W^{c}U)}}\leq\varepsilon
, and
t\in\Lambda_{U}
implies
\Lambda_{W}\,\triangle\,(\Lambda_{W}t)\subseteq\Lambda_{(WU)\cap(W^{c}U)}
.

Proof.

Take any compact symmetric unit neighborhood
U\subseteq H
. Consider
t\in\Lambda_{U}
, write
t=\pi^{G}(\ell)
for
\ell\in{\mathcal{L}}\cap(G\times U)
and note

(\Lambda_{W}t)\,\triangle\,\Lambda_{W}=\Lambda_{(W\pi^{H}(\ell))}\,\triangle\,\Lambda_{W}\subseteq\Lambda_{(WU)\cap(W^{c}U)}\ .

Now note that
m_{H}(\overline{WU\cap W^{c}U})\to m_{H}(\partial W)=0
as
U\to\{e\}
due to continuity of
m_{H}
. We thus have by Theorem
7.2
that
L^{+}_{\Lambda_{WU\cap W^{c}U}}\leq\mathrm{covol}(\mathcal{L})^{-1}m(\overline{WU\cap W^{c}U})\to 0
as
U\to\{e\}
.
∎

To put this result into perspective, take any strong Følner net
(A_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
in
G
. We then have the estimate

d(t):=\limsup_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{\text{card}((\Lambda_{W}t)\,\triangle\,\Lambda_{W}\cap A_{i})}{m(A_{i})}\leq L^{+}_{\Lambda_{(WU)\cap(W^{c}U)}}\ ,

see Proposition
5.13
. In particular, the set
P_{\varepsilon}(\Lambda_{W})=\{t\in G:d(t)\leq\varepsilon\}
is both right- and left-relatively dense for all
\varepsilon>0
.

In the context of
\sigma
-compact locally compact abelian groups, the property of
P_{\varepsilon}(\Lambda_{W})
being relatively dense for all
\varepsilon>0
is called mean almost periodicity of
\Lambda_{W}
, see
[
35
, Thm. 2.18]
. Note that for
\Lambda
being an arbitrary Meyer set, mean almost periodicity of
\Lambda
is equivalent to pure point diffraction of
\Lambda
. Moreover, any regular model set
\Lambda_{W}
is a Meyer set and thus,
\Lambda_{W}
has pure point diffraction. Whereas this has been known since
[
4
, Thm. 5]
and
[
20
, Thm. 1.1]
, it has recently been put into broader perspective in
[
35
, Thms. 2.18, 2.13]
.
We also point out that uniform mean almost periodicity of
\Lambda
is a stronger notion than mean almost periodicity, that typically results in continuous dynamical eigenfunctions, compare
[
33
, Thm. 5]
. In light of these results, it might also be interesting to study variants and aspects of uniformly mean almost periodic point sets beyond the abelian situation.

Appendix A
Nets and convergence

We briefly explain our definition of net and collect some basic properties. We adopt the setting of Moore-Smith convergence as described in
[
29
, Ch. 2]
.

A.1.
Directed sets

Let
\mathbb{I}
be a set, and let
\prec
be a binary relation on
\mathbb{I}
. Recall the following properties that
(\mathbb{I},\prec)
might have.

•

reflexiveness: For all
i\in\mathbb{I}
we have
i\prec i
.

•

anti-symmetry: For all
i,j\in\mathbb{I}
, we have that
i\prec j
and
j\prec i
implies
i=j
.

•

transitivity: For all
i,j,k\in\mathbb{I}
we have that
i\prec j
and
j\prec k
implies
i\prec k
.

•

directedness: For all
i,j\in\mathbb{I}
there exists
k\in\mathbb{I}
such that
i\prec k
and
j\prec k
.

We say that
(\mathbb{I},\prec)
is a
partial order
if
\prec
is reflexive, transitive and antisymmetric. In that case we call
\mathbb{I}
a partially ordered set or poset. Examples of directed partial orders are
(\mathbb{N},\leq)
,
(\mathbb{R},\leq)
,
(\mathbb{R},\geq)
,
(\mathcal{P}(X),\subseteq)
and
(\mathcal{P}(X),\supseteq)
, where
\mathcal{P}(X)
is the collection of subsets of the set
X
.

A.2.
Nets, cluster points and limits

Let
(\mathbb{I},\prec)
a directed poset and let
X
be a topological space. A
net
in
X
is a map
\mathbb{I}\to X
. We denote nets by
(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
. The
range of a net
is the set
\{x_{i}:i\in\mathbb{I}\}\subseteq X
. We call
x\in X
a
cluster point
of a net
(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
if for every neighborhood
U
of
x
and for every
i_{0}\in\mathbb{I}
there exists
i\succ i_{0}
such that
x_{i}\in U
.
A net
(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}

converges
to
x\in X
if for every neighborhood
U
of
x
there exists
i_{0}\in\mathbb{I}
such that
x_{i}\in U
for all
i\succ i_{0}
. In this case we say that
x
is a
limit point
of
(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
. Any limit point is a cluster point. Limit points are unique if and only if
X
is a Hausdorff space
[
29
, Ch. 2, Th, 3]
.

A.3.
Subnets and cluster points

Let
X
be a topological space and let
(\mathbb{I},\prec)
and
(\mathbb{J},\prec)
be directed preorders. Then a net
(y_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
in
X
is a
subnet
of
(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
in
X
if
y_{j}=x_{\phi(j)}
for some function
\phi:\mathbb{J}\to\mathbb{I}
which is
strictly cofinal
, i.e., for every
i_{0}\in\mathbb{I}
there exists
j_{0}\in\mathbb{J}
such that
j\succ j_{0}
implies
\phi(j)\succ i_{0}
. Note that if
(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
converges to
x
, then every subnet
(y_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
converges to
x
. This is a direct consequence of the subnet definition. Also note that the composition of two cofinal maps is a cofinal map. Hence a subnet of a subnet is a subnet of the original net. The following characterisation of a cluster point is standard
[
29
, Ch. 2, Thm. 6]
.

Proposition A.1
.

A point
x\in X
is a cluster point of a net
(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
if and only if there exists a subnet
(x_{\phi(j)})_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
that converges to
x
. ∎

A.4.
Nets in
\overline{\mathbb{R}}

Let us consider nets in the affinely extended real numbers
\overline{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty,-\infty\}
.
A net
(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
in
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is
increasing
if
x_{i}\leq x_{j}
for all
i\prec j
. It is
decreasing
if
x_{i}\geq x_{j}
for all
i\prec j
. As in the sequence case, we have the following result.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
be an increasing net in
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
. Then
(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
converges to its supremum
x=\sup\{x_{i}:i\in\mathbb{I}\}\leq\infty
. ∎

Let
(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
be a net in
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
. Then the net
(s_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
where
s_{i}=\inf\{x_{j}:j\succ i\}
is increasing, and the net

(t_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
where
t_{i}=\sup\{x_{j}:j\succ i\}
is decreasing. Note
s_{i}\leq x_{i}\leq t_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbb{I}
as
\prec
is reflexive. As the nets
(s_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
and
(t_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
both have a unique limit point, we can define

(A.1)

\begin{split}\liminf_{i\in\mathbb{I}}x_{i}&=\lim_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\inf_{j\succ i}x_{j}=\sup_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\inf_{j\succ i}x_{j}\geq-\infty\ ,\qquad\limsup_{i\in\mathbb{I}}x_{i}=\lim_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\sup_{j\succ i}x_{j}=\inf_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\sup_{j\succ i}x_{j}\leq\infty\ .\end{split}

As in the sequence case it is seen that
\liminf_{i\in\mathbb{I}}x_{i}
is the smallest cluster point of
(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
. Moreover one may easily construct a subnet
(x_{\phi(n)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
which is a sequence and converges to
\liminf_{i\in\mathbb{I}}x_{i}
. Analogous results hold for
\limsup_{i\in\mathbb{I}}x_{i}
.

Appendix B
Box decompositions in
\sigma
-compact groups

Some
\sigma
-compact locally compact groups admit Følner sequences of hierarchically nested monotiles. Such sequences can be used for box decomposition arguments. We indicate in this section how to prove equality of uniform Leptin and Beurling densities via this method, as was done in
[
22
, Sec. 7]
for
G=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\nu=\delta_{\Lambda}
being a Dirac comb over a uniformly discrete set
\Lambda
.

In a
\sigma
-compact group we call a compact set
A
a
right monotile for
G
if there is a countable set
T
such that
A\cdot T=G
and for each
s,t\in T
with
s\neq t
, we have
\mathring{A}s\cap\mathring{A}t=\varnothing
, where as above,
\mathring{A}
denotes the interior of the set
A
. Analogously, one defines left monotiles for
G
. In the following we will exclusively deal with right monotiles and refer to those as
monotiles for
G
.

Definition B.1
.

We call a Følner sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
a monotile Følner sequence if for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the set
A_{n}
a is a monotile for
G
. We say that a monotile Følner sequence is topologically nested if for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is some
s_{n}\in G
such that
A_{n}\subseteq\mathring{A}_{n+1}s_{n}
.

Monotile Følner sequences can be found in many amenable groups. In
[
60
]
, Weiss has shown that all countable linear amenable groups and all residually finite amenable groups admit such sequences. In the abelian situation, Emerson used in
[
16
, Thm. 5]
the structure theorem for compactly generated LCA groups for finding monotile Følner sequences. By passing to a subsequence if necessary one can make sure that these sequences are topologically nested.
The construction of Emerson has been used in the context of mathematical quasicrystals, see for example the proof of Lem. 1.1 in
[
55
]
.
Moreover, topologically nested monotile Følner sequences can be constructed explicitly for many homogeneous Lie groups such as the Heisenberg group.

Example B.2
.

Let
G=H_{3}(\mathbb{R})
be the
3
-dimensional Heisenberg group with its group multiplication given by

(a,b,c)\cdot(x,y,z)=(a+x,b+y,c+z+ay).

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the set
F_{n}:=[0,n)^{2}\times[0,n^{2})
is a fundamental domain for the (uniform) lattice

\Gamma_{n}:=\{(k,l,m):k,l\in n{\mathbb{Z}},\,m\in n^{2}{\mathbb{Z}}\big\}.

Now it is easy to see that by setting
A_{n}:=\overline{F}_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, one obtains a monotile strong Følner sequence for
G
. Moreover, we have
A_{n}\cdot t_{n}\subseteq\mathring{A}_{n+1}
when setting

t_{n}=(\varepsilon,\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
, with
0<\varepsilon<1
.
Therefore,
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is also topologically nested.

For illustration, let us give a box decomposition proof of
B^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}\leq L^{-}
when
{\mathcal{A}}=(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a topologically nested monotile strong Følner sequence in a
\sigma
-compact amenable unimodular group.

Proof of
B^{-}_{\mathcal{A}}\leq L^{-}
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}=(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a Følner sequence of monotiles as of Definition
B.1
and suppose that
{\mathcal{A}}
is also a strong Følner sequence. We show

L^{-}=\sup_{K\in{\mathcal{K}}}\inf_{A\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}}\frac{\nu(KA)}{m(A)}\geq B_{\mathcal{A}}^{-}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\inf_{s\in G}\frac{\nu(A_{n}s)}{m(A_{n})}\ .

Setting
B_{n}:=\mathring{A}_{n}=A_{n}\setminus\partial A_{n}
we note that we have

B_{\mathcal{A}}^{-}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\inf_{s\in G}\frac{\nu(B_{n}s)}{m(A_{n})}\ .

Indeed, this follows from standard estimates, using
A_{n}s\setminus B_{n}s=\partial A_{n}s\subseteq\partial_{U}A_{n}s
for any relatively compact unit neighborhood
U
, and using the Følner property together with

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{s\in G}\frac{\nu(\partial_{U}A_{n}s)}{m(A_{n})}=0\ ,

cf. Lemma
5.10
. Fix arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
. Take
n\in\mathbb{N}
large enough such that for all
s\in G
we have

\frac{\nu(B_{n}s)}{m(A_{n})}\geq B_{\mathcal{A}}^{-}-\varepsilon\ .

Consider an arbitrary compact set
A\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}
. Since
B_{n}t_{n}=\mathring{A}_{n}t_{n}\supseteq A_{n-1}
for some
t_{n}\in G
, by assumption on a topologically nested monotile Følner sequence, we find a finite set
I
and
s_{i}\in G
for
i\in I
such that

A\subseteq\dot{\bigcup_{i\in I}}B_{n}s_{i}\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I}A_{n}s_{i}\subseteq KA,

where
K={A_{n}A_{n}^{-1}}
, compare the argument for the inclusions (
6.2
) in the proof of Proposition
6.1
. Now we can estimate for
n
large enough

\begin{split}\nu(KA)&\geq\nu\left(\bigcup_{i\in I}B_{n}s_{i}\right)=\sum_{i\in I}\nu(B_{n}s_{i})\geq|I|\cdot m(A_{n})\cdot(B_{\mathcal{A}}^{-}-\varepsilon)\\
&\geq m\left(\bigcup_{i\in I}A_{n}s_{i}\right)\cdot(B_{\mathcal{A}}^{-}-\varepsilon)\geq m(A)\cdot(B_{\mathcal{A}}^{-}-\varepsilon)\ .\end{split}

As
A
was arbitrary, we infer

\sup_{K\in{\mathcal{K}}}\inf_{A\in{\mathcal{K}}_{p}}\frac{\nu(KA)}{m(A)}\geq B_{\mathcal{A}}^{-}-\varepsilon\ .

As
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we get
L^{-}\geq B_{\mathcal{A}}^{-}
.
∎

Appendix C
The densities of Gröchenig, Kutyniok, Seip

We review the density notion from
[

]
, which has inspired our definition of Leptin density. Consider any locally finite measure
\nu
on
G
.
For example,
\nu
might be the counting measure on some right uniformly discrete point set in

.
Assume that

admits a uniform lattice. Then densities of

\Gamma
.

Definition C.1
.

Let
G
be a unimodular locally compact group. Assume that
\Gamma
is a uniform lattice in
G
. For locally finite measures
\mu,\nu
on

we write
\mu\leq\nu

(1-\varepsilon)\cdot\mu(A)\leq\nu(KA)

{\mathcal{D}}_{\nu}^{-}=\sup\{\alpha\in[0,\infty):\alpha\delta_{\Gamma}\leq\nu\}\ ,\qquad{\mathcal{D}}_{\nu}^{+}=\inf\{\alpha\in[0,\infty):\nu\leq\alpha\delta_{\Gamma}\}\ .

Here
\delta_{\Gamma}
is the point counting measure associated to
\Gamma

\delta_{\Gamma}(A)=\mathrm{card}(A\cap\Gamma)

Proposition C.2
.

Let
G
be a unimodular locally compact group which admits a uniform lattice
\Gamma
. Let
\nu
be any locally finite measure on
G

L_{\Gamma}\cdot{\mathcal{D}}_{\nu}^{-}=L^{-}_{\nu}\ ,\qquad L_{\Gamma}\cdot{\mathcal{D}}_{\nu}^{+}=L^{+}_{\nu}\ ,

where
L_{\Gamma}
denotes the Leptin density of the lattice
\Gamma
.

Proof.

We argue
L_{\Gamma}\cdot{\mathcal{D}}^{-}=L^{-}
, the proof for the upper density is analogous.

(i) We collect some prerequisites from the proof of Proposition
6.1
. Let
F
be a measurable relatively compact left-fundamental domain for
\Gamma
and note
L_{\Gamma}\cdot m(F)=1
. Defining
K=\overline{F^{-1}F}
, we have for any
A\in{\mathcal{K}}
the estimates

m(F)\cdot\delta_{\Gamma}(A)\leq m(KA)\ ,\qquad m(F)\cdot\delta_{\Gamma}(KA)\geq m(A)\ .

(ii) To show
{\mathcal{D}}^{-}\leq m(F)\cdot L^{-}
, we assume without loss of generality
{\mathcal{D}}^{-}>0
.
Consider any
0<d<{\mathcal{D}}^{-}
. Then by Definition
C.1
there exists
K^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{K}}
such that for all
A\in{\mathcal{K}}
we have
d\,\delta_{\Gamma}(A)\leq\nu(K^{\prime}A)
. This implies

\nu(K^{\prime}KA)\geq d\,\delta_{\Gamma}(KA)\geq d\cdot m(A)/m(F)
. We thus have
d/m(F)\leq L^{-}
. As
0<d<{\mathcal{D}}^{-}
was arbitrary, we conclude
{\mathcal{D}}^{-}\leq m(F)\cdot L^{-}
.

(iii) To show
m(F)\cdot L^{-}\leq{\mathcal{D}}^{-}
, we assume without loss of generality
L^{-}>0
.
Take any
0<d<L^{-}
. Then by definition of the lower Leptin density, cf. Definition
3.3
, there exists
K^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{K}}
such that for all
A\in{\mathcal{K}}
we have
d\cdot m(KA)\leq\nu(K^{\prime}KA)
.
This implies
m(F)\cdot d\,\delta_{\Gamma}(A)\leq d\cdot m(KA)\leq\nu(K^{\prime}KA)
. We thus obtain
m(F)\cdot d<{\mathcal{D}}^{-}
. As
0<d<L^{-}
was arbitrary, we conclude
m(F)\cdot L^{-}\leq{\mathcal{D}}^{-}
.
∎

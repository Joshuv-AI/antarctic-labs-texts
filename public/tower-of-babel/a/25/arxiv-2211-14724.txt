On the experimental verification of the uncertainty principle of position and momentum
Thomas Schürmann, Ingo Hoffmann, Winfrid Görlich

Abstract
Historically, Kennard was the first to choose the standard deviation as a quantitative measure of uncertainty, and neither he nor Heisenberg explicitly explained why this choice should be appropriate from the experimental physical point of view. If a particle is prepared by a single slit of spatial width Δ ​ x \Delta x , it has been shown that a finite standard deviation σ p < ∞ \sigma_{p}<\infty can only be ensured if the wave-function is zero at the edge of Δ ​ x \Delta x , otherwise it does not exist TS09 . Under these circumstances the corresponding sharp inequality is σ p ​ Δ ​ x ≥ π ​ ℏ \sigma_{p}\Delta x\geq\pi\hbar . This bound will be reconsidered from the mathematical point of view in terms of a variational problem in Hilbert space and will furthermore be tested in a 4f-single slit diffraction experiment of a laser beam. Our results will be compared with a laser-experiment recently given by Fernández-Guasti (2022) G22 .



I
1. Introduction

The diffraction of particles by a single slit has often been discussed as an illustration of Heisenberg’s uncertainty relations and their role in the process of measurement. In the case of particles passing through a slit of width
\Delta x
in a diaphragm of some experimental arrangement, the diffraction by the slit of the wave implies a spread in the momentum of the particles, which is greater the narrower the slit. This phenomenon is an example of the famous Heisenberg principle
H27
H30
.
The most familiar formalization of the uncertainty principle is in terms of standard deviations
K27

\displaystyle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{p}\geq\hbar/2

(1)

with the reduced Planck constant
\hbar=h/2\pi
.

However, for particles passing through a slit of width
\Delta x
, the diffraction of the incoming wave function
\psi
has to be considered as a preparation corresponding to the ordinary
von Neumann-Lüders projection
. This approach is often applied in the actual experimental design of the uncertainty relation
S69
Le69
Z03
. The advantage is that the localization of the particles is simply given by the width
\Delta x
of the slit. Unfortunately this approach cannot be considered as a rigorous experimental test of Kennard’s expression (
1
) because
\Delta x
and
\sigma_{x}
are quite different measures of localization. An obvious interrelation between
\Delta x
and
\sigma_{x}
is given by the Popoviciu inequality for variances
Po

\displaystyle\sigma_{x}\leq\frac{\Delta x}{2}

(2)

while equality holds precisely when half of the probability is concentrated at each of the two boundary points of
\Delta x
. From (
1
) and (
2
), the following inequality is immediately implied

\displaystyle\sigma_{p}\Delta x>\hbar

(3)

However, the bound
\hbar
on the right-hand side can never be reached. This is because a finite value of
\sigma_{p}<\infty

can only be ensured if the wave-function is zero at the edge of
\Delta x
, otherwise it does not exist. According to this condition the following sharp inequality has been introduced
TS09

\displaystyle\sigma_{p}\Delta x\geq\pi\hbar.

(4)

Since the standard deviation
\sigma_{p}
is (by definition) corresponding only to the ”one-sided” deviation of the probability density it is sometimes obvious to measure the momentum uncertainty
\Delta p
according to the definition

\displaystyle\Delta p=2\sigma_{p}.

(5)

With this definition, the inequalities (
3
) and (
4
) can equivalently be rewritten as

\displaystyle\Delta x\Delta p>2\hbar

(6)

and

\displaystyle\Delta x\Delta p\geq 2\pi\hbar.

(7)

Actually, the sharp inequality (
7
) can be considered as a mathematically rigorous version of the heuristic expression
\Delta x\Delta p\gtrsim h
originally introduced by the measurement process of Heisenberg in
H27
H30
, before Kennard came with the alternative measurement approach corresponding to inequality (
1
).

Recently it has been mentioned that there is a quantum optical realization of the position and momentum uncertainty relation in the quantum limit
G22
. Interference of two non-collinear photon modes with different frequencies are space and time resolved and the detection is performed in the same space-time region. It is argued that evaluation of the photon momentum from the position versus time interferograms makes this procedure similar to the mechanical momentum construct. The measured uncertainty
\Delta p_{x}
in
G22
is applied to approximate twice as much of the standard deviation
\sigma_{p}
in order to verify the inequalities (
6
) and (
7
). The experimental factors corresponding to the right-hand side of (
6
) and (
7
) are given by
1.128\hbar
,
2.464\hbar
and
2.723\hbar
. That is, inequality (
6
) is experimentally falsified for the first measurement and expression (
7
) for all three cases. This conclusion seems at least questionable, such that a closer look at the measurement in
G22
seems appropriate at this point. To do so, let us consider the experimental determination of
\Delta p_{x}
given in
G22
by

\displaystyle\Delta p_{x}=\hbar\,\frac{\delta\omega}{2}\cdot\frac{\dot{x}_{\text{max}}-\dot{x}_{\text{min}}}{\dot{x}_{\text{max}}\,\dot{x}_{\text{min}}}.

(8)

The notation
\dot{x}=dx/dt
is the velocity by which the fringes of the interferograms are displaced in time and
\delta\omega
the frequency shift of the incoming modes expressed in Figs. 3, 4 and 5 of
G22
. In the experimental approach
\Delta p_{x}
is considered to be proportional to the difference of nearby maximum and minimum slopes. Actually, the slopes extrema were drawn by hand prior to any calculation to avoid statistical bias. An arbitrary dark fringe was chosen and parallel slopes to the median were drawn (the distance between them was roughly half the distance between fringe maxima). The slopes extrema have been obtained from the diagonals of the corresponding parallelogram.

As far as can be seen it is neither obvious nor proven in
G22
that expression (
8

5

2\sigma_{p}

\sigma_{p}
has been mentioned, but this cannot be sufficient for the conclusion given in

II
2.  Variation problem in Hilbert space

Let us consider particles in one spatial dimension described by a wave function
\psi
which is an element of the Hilbert space
{\cal H}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, the space of square integrable functions on
\mathbb{R}
. The scalar product in Hilbert space will be denoted by angular brackets, that is to write
\langle\phi|\psi\rangle
for the scalar product of two state vectors
\phi,\psi\in{\cal H}
. Accordingly, the norm of
\psi
is given by
||\psi||\equiv\sqrt{\langle\psi|\psi\rangle}
. The point-spectrum of the momentum eigenvalue equation

\displaystyle\hat{p}|\varphi_{n}\rangle=p_{n}|\varphi_{n}\rangle

(23)

on the interval
[-\frac{\Delta x}{2},\frac{\Delta x}{2}]
of length
\Delta x
is given by

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta x}}\,e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}p_{n}x}

(24)

\displaystyle p_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar\,k_{n}

(25)

\displaystyle k_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi n}{\Delta x}

(26)

for
n=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,...
This complete set of solutions satisfies the orthonormality relations

\displaystyle\langle\varphi_{n}|\varphi_{m}\rangle=\delta_{nm}.

(27)

Thus, the decomposition

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}c_{n}\,|\varphi_{n}\rangle

(28)

with Fourier coefficients

\displaystyle c_{n}=\langle\varphi_{n}|\psi\rangle\in\mathbb{C}

(29)

satisfies Parseval’s theorem
||\psi||^{2}=\sum_{n}|c_{n}|^{2}
. Accordingly, the first and second moments of the momentum can be expressed by

\displaystyle\langle\hat{p}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi\hbar}{\Delta x}\,\sum_{n}n|c_{n}|^{2}

(30)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{p}^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2\pi\hbar}{\Delta x}\right)^{2}\sum_{n}n^{2}|c_{n}|^{2}

(31)

such that
\sigma_{p}^{2}=\langle\hat{p}^{2}\rangle-\langle\hat{p}\rangle^{2}
can be written as

\displaystyle\sigma_{p}^{2}=\left(\frac{2\pi\hbar}{\Delta x}\right)^{2}\left[\sum_{n}n^{2}|c_{n}|^{2}-\left(\sum_{n}n|c_{n}|^{2}\right)^{2}\right].

(32)

The associated variational problem with
||\psi||=1
and the necessary condition
\psi(\pm\frac{\Delta x}{2})=0
is given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{p}^{2}\to\min

(33)

\displaystyle\sum_{n}|c_{n}|^{2}=1

(34)

\displaystyle\sum_{n}(-1)^{n}c_{n}^{*}=0.

(35)

This can be solved by the definition of a Lagrange function with two integrating factors
\alpha
and
\beta
of the following form

\displaystyle L=\sigma_{p}^{2}-\beta\sum_{n}|c_{n}|^{2}-\alpha\sum_{n}(-1)^{n}c_{n}^{*}.

(36)

The variation of
c_{n}
and
c_{n}^{*}
is independent of each other and the condition
dL=0
is performed with respect to
c_{n}^{*}
. After a few algebraic steps the resulting condition is given by

\displaystyle\left[\left(\frac{2\pi\hbar}{\Delta x}\right)^{2}n^{2}-\frac{4\pi\hbar}{\Delta x}\langle\hat{p}\rangle n-\beta\right]c_{n}=(-1)^{n}\alpha

(37)

for
n=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,...
This infinite set of conditions for the determination of
c_{n}
is non-linear because of the contribution of
\langle\hat{p}\rangle
in the second term of the left-hand side. Up to a constant phase, the solution of this equation is given by

\displaystyle c_{n}=\frac{\sqrt{8}\,\,}{\pi}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{1-4\,n^{2}}.

(38)

By substitution it can be verified that the restrictions (
34
) and (
35
) are satisfied. Moreover, the symmetry
c_{n}=c_{-n}
of the solution implies that
\langle\hat{p}\rangle=0
. This confirms the assumption made by the authors in
TS09
. The momentum distribution
|c_{n}|^{2}
of (
38
) corresponds to the equal sign in (
4
) and (
7
). The associated position representation of the wave-function is obtained by Fourier transformation (
28
) and given by

\displaystyle\psi(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\Delta x}}\cos\left(\frac{\pi x}{\Delta x}\right)

(39)

for all
x\in[-\frac{\Delta x}{2},\frac{\Delta x}{2}]
.
From the coefficients in (
38
) the corresponding continuous function is given by substitution of
k_{n}=2\pi n/\Delta x
. In this limit the normalized
k
-state function is

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}(k)=2\sqrt{\pi\Delta x}\,\,\frac{\cos\left(\frac{\Delta x}{2}k\right)}{\pi^{2}-\Delta x^{2}k^{2}}

(40)

for every
k\in\mathbb{R}
. The standard deviation
\sigma_{k}
of the wave-numbers
k
can be obtained by ordinary integration and is given by
\sigma_{k}=\pi/\Delta x
. After multiplication with
\hbar
this reproduces the equal sign of the main relation given in (
4
). Both (
39
) and (
40
) are the state of minimum uncertainty corresponding to
\sigma_{p}\Delta x=\pi\hbar
.

III
3.  Experimental verification

For an experimental verification of the lower bound in inequality (
4
), a preparation of the state of minimal uncertainty (
39

However, an approximated state of minimal uncertainty can be prepared by apodization techniques in terms of a Lanczos-window to cut off higher spatial frequencies. The corresponding normalized sinc-function is given by

\displaystyle\phi(x)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\text{Si}(2\pi)\Delta x}}\,\,\text{sinc}\left(\frac{2\pi}{\Delta x}\,x\right)

(41)

with sinc
(x)=\frac{\sin x}{x}
and Si
(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sin(t)/t\,dt
. This function can be straightforwardly prepared by plane-wave diffraction, see Fig.
1

IV
4.  Summary and conclusion

In this work, the importance of carefully choosing observables in experimentally testing Heisenberg’s uncertainty principle has been emphasized. It has been discussed that a careless or often pragmatic approximation of standard deviations can lead to misinterpretations of experimental measurements and their implications. Since the popular uncertainty relations are mostly based on the notion of standard deviations, a closer look at their experimental determination is inevitable.

The exact determination of a standard deviation requires in most cases the integration over an infinite domain of a density function. In our 4f-setup, an experimental preparation in terms of a Lanczos-window has been proposed such that the associated momentum density is sufficiently narrow to allow a reasonable experimental verification of our main inequality (
4
).

Actually, this conclusion is not very surprising if one considers that the rigorous mathematical derivation of inequality (
4
) in Sec. 2. or in
TS09
is based on the quantum mechanical foundations in Hilbert space and is by no means only a heuristic argument. Finally, it should be mentioned that the concept leading to the derivation of inequality (4) has been generalized to 3-dimensional manifolds with constant (nonzero) curvature in
TS18
TS20
.

Integral formulas for a foliated sub-Riemannian manifold
Vladimir Rovenski

Abstract
In this article, we deduce a series of integral formulas for a foliated sub-Riemannian manifold, which is a new geometric concept denoting a Riemannian manifold equipped with a distribution 𝒟 {\mathcal{D}} and a foliation ℱ {\mathcal{F}} , whose tangent bundle is a subbundle of 𝒟 {\mathcal{D}} . Our integral formulas generalize some results for foliated Riemannian manifolds and involve the shape operators of ℱ {\mathcal{F}} with respect to normals in 𝒟 {\mathcal{D}} and the curvature tensor of induced connection on 𝒟 {\mathcal{D}} . The formulas also include arbitrary functions f j ​ ( 0 ≤ j < dim ℱ ) f_{j}\ (0\leq j<\dim{\mathcal{F}}) depending on scalar invariants of the shape operators, and for a special choice of f j f_{j} reduce to integral formulas with the Newton transformations of the shape operators. We apply our formulas to foliated sub-Riemannian manifolds with restrictions on the curvature and extrinsic geometry of ℱ {\mathcal{F}} and to codimension-one foliations. Keywords : Distribution, foliation, shape operator, Newton transformation Mathematics Subject Classifications (2010) 53C12, 53C17



Introduction

A non-holonomic manifold is a pair
(M,{\mathcal{D}})
, where
M
is a smooth manifold and

{\mathcal{D}}
is a distribution on
M
, i.e., a subbundle of the tangent bundle
TM
, see
[
4
]
.
In terms of an almost product structure on
M
, i.e., a (1,1)-tensor field
P
of constant rank for which
P^{2}=P
, see
[
9
]
,
we obtain

{\mathcal{D}}=P(TM)
.
A non-holonomic manifold
(M,{\mathcal{D}})
with a Riemannian metric
g
on the distribution
{\mathcal{D}}
,
e.g., the restriction of a metric on the manifold
M
, is the main object of sub-Riemannian geometry.
In
[
6
]
, they explore sub-Riemannian structures arising as the transversal distribution to a foliation.
In
[
15
]
, we introduced a new geometrical concept of a
foliated sub-Riemannian manifold
,
i.e.,
(M,{\mathcal{D}},g)
equipped with
a foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
, whose tangent bundle
T{\mathcal{F}}
is a subbundle of any codimension of
{\mathcal{D}}

(Recall that a foliation
{\mathcal{F}}

is a partition of
M

\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
by submanifolds parallel to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
).
We proved in
[
15
]
a series of integral formulas for
a codimension-one foliated sub-Riemannian manifold,
i.e.,
{\mathcal{D}}=T{\mathcal{F}}\oplus\,{\rm span}(N)
, where
N
is a unit vector field orthogonal to
{\mathcal{F}}
;
these integral formulas generalize results in
[
2
,
7
]
for
foliated Riemannian manifolds.
On the other hand, in
[
14
]
, we extended the above mentioned results in
[
2
,
7
]

for foliations of arbitrary codimension of Riemannian manifolds,
as a series of integral formulas depending on the Newton or even more general transformations of the shape operators of the leaves.

Analyzing history of extrinsic geometry of foliations, we see that from the origin it was related to some integral formulas containing the shape operator
(or the second fundamental form) of leaves and its invariants (mean curvature, higher order mean curvatures
\sigma_{r}
, etc.) and some expressions corresponding to geometry (curvature) of
M
.
Integral formulas are useful for solving many problems in differential geometry, both manifolds (for example, the Gauss-Bonnet formula for closed surfaces) and foliations, see surveys
[
1
,
20
,
21
]
.
The first known integral formula for a codimension-one foliation of a closed Riemannian manifold by G. Reeb
[
12
]

tells that the integral of the mean curvature
H
of the leaves is equal to zero.
Its proof is based
on the application of the Divergence theorem to the identity
\operatorname{div}N=-H
with
N
a unit normal to the leaves.
The second formula in the series of total higher mean curvatures
\sigma_{r}
’s of a codimension-one foliation is, e.g.,
[
14
]
,

\int_{M}(2\,\sigma_{2}-\operatorname{Ric}_{N,N})\,{\rm d}\operatorname{vol}_{g}=0.

(1)

In
[
2
]
, the Newton transformations
T_{r}(A)
(of the shape operator
A
of the leaves) were applied to codimension one foliations,
and a series of integral formulas for
r\geq 0
starting with (

) was obtained (with consequences for foliated space forms,
see
[
7

\int_{M}\big((r+2)\,\sigma_{r+2}-\operatorname{tr}_{\,{\mathcal{F}}}(T_{r}(A){\mathcal{R}}_{N})-\langle{\rm div}_{\mathcal{F}}\,T_{r}(A),\,\nabla_{N}N\rangle\big)\,{\rm d}\operatorname{vol}_{g}=0.

(2)

Here, the Jacobi operator
{\mathcal{R}}_{N}:T{\mathcal{F}}\to T{\mathcal{F}}
is given by
{\mathcal{R}}_{\,N}(Y)=R(Y,N)N
.
There is also a series of integral formulas for two complementary orthogonal distributions
{\cal D}
and
{\cal D}^{\bot}
(or foliations) of arbitrary dimension on a closed Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
,
see
[
14
]
, starting with the
the following generalization of (
1

[
24
]
:

\int_{M}\big({\rm S\,}_{\rm mix}+\|h\|^{2}+\|h^{\bot}\|^{2}-\|H\|^{2}-\|H^{\bot}\|^{2}-\|T\|^{2}-\|T^{\bot}\|^{2}\big)\,{\rm d}\operatorname{vol}_{g}=0.

(3)

Here,
h,T:{\mathcal{D}}\times{\mathcal{D}}\to{\mathcal{D}}^{\bot}
and
H=\operatorname{tr}_{g}h

are the second fundamental form,
the integrability tensor and the mean curvature vector field of

{\mathcal{D}}
, and similarly for
{\mathcal{D}}^{\bot}
,

{\rm S\,}_{\rm mix}
is the mixed scalar curvature.
The following question naturally arises:

if the integral formulas of the type (
2

can be proved for foliated sub-Riemannian manifolds

In this article, we

1
Preliminaries

Here, we define the shape operator
A_{\xi}
and its Newton transformations
T_{r}(A_{\xi})
,
the
r
th mean curvatures
\sigma_{r}(\xi)
and power sums symmetric functions
\tau_{k}(\xi)
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be an
(n+p)
-dimensional distribution on a smooth
m
-dimensional manifold
M
, i.e.,
a subbundle of
TM
of rank
n+p
(where
n,p>0
and
n+p<m
).
In other words, to each point
x\in M
we assign an
(n+p)
-dimensional subspace
{\mathcal{D}}_{x}
of the tangent space
T_{x}M
smoothly depending on
x
.
An integrable distribution determines a foliation; in this case, the Lie bracket of any two vector fields from the distribution
{\mathcal{D}}
also belongs to

{\mathcal{D}}
.

A pair
(M,{\mathcal{D}})
, where
{\mathcal{D}}
is a non-integrable distribution on a manifold
M
, is called a
non-holonomic manifold
, see
[
4
]
.
The concept of a non-holonomic manifold was introduced for the geometric interpretation of constrained systems in classical mechanics.
A
sub-Riemannian manifold
is a non-holonomic manifold
(M,{\mathcal{D}})
, where
{\mathcal{D}}
is equipped with a sub-Riemannian metric
g=\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle
, i.e.,
the scalar product on
{\mathcal{D}}_{x}
for all
x\in M
, see
[
4
]
.
Usually, they assume that the sub-Riemannian metric on
{\mathcal{D}}
(the horizontal bundle)
is extended to a Riemannian metric on the whole
M
, also denoted by
g
.
This allows us to define the orthogonal distribution
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
(the vertical subbundle) such that
TM={\mathcal{D}}\oplus\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
.

Definition 1
.

A sub-Riemannian manifold
(M,{\mathcal{D}},g)
equipped with a foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
such that the tangent bundle
T{\mathcal{F}}
is a subbundle of
{\mathcal{D}}
will be called a
foliated sub-Riemannian manifold
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a foliation of codimension
p
relative to
{\mathcal{D}}
, i.e.,
\dim{\mathcal{F}}=n
,
and let
{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{F}}
be the orthogonal complement of
T{\mathcal{F}}
in
{\mathcal{D}}
; thus, the following decomposition holds:

{\mathcal{D}}=T{\mathcal{F}}\oplus{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{F}}
.
Let
⊤
denotes the projection from
TM
on the vector subbundle
T{\mathcal{F}}
.
The
shape operator

A_{\xi}:T{\mathcal{F}}\to T{\mathcal{F}}
of the foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
with respect to the unit normal
\xi\in{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{F}}
is defined by

A_{\xi}(X)=-(\nabla_{X}\,\xi)^{\top},\quad X\in T{\mathcal{F}}.

The
elementary symmetric functions

\sigma_{j}(A_{\xi})
of
A_{\xi}

are given by the equality, e.g.,
[
20
]
,

\sum\nolimits_{\,r=0}^{\,n}\sigma_{r}(A_{\xi})\,t^{r}=\det(\,\operatorname{id}_{\,T{\mathcal{F}}}+\,t\,A_{\xi}),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Note that

\sigma_{r}(A_{\xi})=\sum\nolimits_{\,i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}}\lambda_{i_{1}}(\xi)\cdots\lambda_{i_{r}}(\xi)
,
where
\lambda_{1}(\xi)\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{n}(\xi)
are the eigenvalues of
A_{\xi}
.
The
power sums
symmetric functions of
A_{\xi}
are

\tau_{j}(A_{\xi})=\operatorname{tr}\,(A_{\xi}^{j})=\sum\nolimits_{\,1\leq i\leq n}(\lambda_{i}(\xi))^{j},\quad j\in\mathbb{N}.

For short, set

\sigma_{r}(\xi)=\sigma_{r}(A_{\xi}),\quad\tau_{r}(\xi)=\tau_{r}(A_{\xi}).

For example,

\sigma_{0}(\xi)=1
,
\sigma_{n}(\xi)=\det A_{\xi}
,

\sigma_{1}(\xi)=\tau_{1}(\xi)=\operatorname{tr}A_{\xi}
and
2\,\sigma_{2}(\xi)=\tau_{1}^{2}(\xi)-\tau_{2}(\xi)
.

Definition 2
.

The
Newton transformations

T_{r}(A_{\xi})
of the shape operator
A_{\xi}
of
a foliated sub-Riemannian manifold
(M,{\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{F}},g)
are defined recursively or explicitly by

T_{0}(A_{\xi})=\operatorname{id}_{\,T{\mathcal{F}}},\quad T_{r}(A_{\xi})=\sigma_{r}(\xi)\operatorname{id}_{\,T{\mathcal{F}}}-A_{\xi}\,T_{r-1}(A_{\xi})\quad(0<r\leq n),

(4)

T_{r}(A_{\xi})=\sum\nolimits_{j=0}^{r}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{r-j}(\xi)\,A_{\xi}^{j}=\sigma_{r}(\xi)\operatorname{id}_{\,T{\mathcal{F}}}-\sigma_{r-1}(\xi)\,A_{\xi}+\ldots+(-1)^{r}A_{\xi}^{\,r}.

(5)

For example,
T_{1}(A_{\xi})=\sigma_{1}(\xi)\operatorname{id}_{\,T{\mathcal{F}}}-A_{\xi}
and
T_{n}(A_{\xi})=0
.
Define a (1,1)-tensor field

{\mathcal{A}}_{\xi}:=\sum\nolimits_{\,j=0}^{\,n-1}f_{j}(\tau_{1}(\xi),\ldots,\tau_{n}(\xi))\,A_{\xi}^{j},

(6)

where
f_{j}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\ (0\leq j<n)
are given functions.
We write
f_{j}=f_{j}(\xi)
shortly.
The choice of the RHS for
{\mathcal{A}}_{\xi}

in (
6
) is natural for the following reasons,
[
20
, Section 1.2.2]
:

\bullet
the powers
A_{\xi}^{j}
are the only (1,1)-tensors, obtained algebraically from
A_{\xi}
,
while
\tau_{1}(\xi),

\ldots,\tau_{n}(\xi)
, or, equivalently,

\sigma_{1}(\xi),\ldots,\sigma_{n}(\xi)
, generate all scalar invariants of
A_{\xi}
.

\bullet
the Newton transformation
T_{r}(A_{\xi})\ (0\leq r<n)
of (
5
) depends on all
A_{\xi}^{j}\ (0\leq j\leq r)
.

In this article, for illustrate the results for
{\mathcal{A}}_{\xi}
by
the particular case of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\xi}=T_{r}(A_{\xi})
.

Let
\nabla^{\mathcal{F}}:TM\times T{\mathcal{F}}\to T{\mathcal{F}}
be the induced connection on the vector subbundle
T{\mathcal{F}}
.
Since the (1,1)-tensors
A_{\xi}
and
T_{r}(A_{\xi})
are self-adjoint, we have

\langle(\nabla^{\mathcal{F}}_{X}\,T_{r}(A_{\xi}))Y,\,V\rangle=\langle(\nabla^{\mathcal{F}}_{X}\,T_{r}(A_{\xi}))V,\,Y\rangle,\quad X,Y,V\in T{\mathcal{F}}.

The following properties of

are proved similarly as for codimension-one foliations of a Riemannian manifold,
e.g.,
[
2
]
or

[
20
, Lemma 1.3]
.

of an

2
The induced connection and curvature on
{\mathcal{D}}

Here, we define the induced linear connection
\nabla^{P}
and the curvature tensor
R^{P}
related to a foliated sub-Riemannian manifold,
we also prove Codazzi type equation.
The orthoprojector
P:TM\to{\mathcal{D}}
on the distribution
{\mathcal{D}}

is characterized by the properties, e.g.,
[
9
]
,

P=P^{*}\ (\textrm{self-adjoint}),\quad P^{2}=P.

The Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
on
(M,g)
induces a linear connection
\nabla^{P}
on
{\mathcal{D}}
:

\nabla^{P}_{X}(PY)=P\nabla_{X}(PY),\quad X,Y\in\Gamma(TM),

which is compatible with the metric on
{\cal D}
:
X\langle U,V\rangle=\langle\nabla^{P}_{X}U,\,V\rangle+\langle U,\,\nabla^{P}_{X}V\rangle
for

U,V\in{\cal D}
.
Set
{R}^{P}(X,Y,U,V)=\langle{R}^{P}(X,Y)U,\,V\rangle
for
U,V\in{\mathcal{D}}
, where
R^{P}:TM\times TM\rightarrow{\rm End}({\mathcal{D}})

is the curvature tensor of
\nabla^{P}
.
As for any linear connection, we have the anti-symmetry for the first pair of vectors:
{R}^{P}(Y,X)U=-{R}^{P}(X,Y)U
.
Since
\nabla^{P}
is compatible with
g
, then the anti-symmetry for the last pair of vectors in
{\cal D}
is valid, e.g.,
[
10
]
,

{R}^{P}(X,Y,U,V)=-{R}^{P}(X,Y,V,U).

(7)

Let
⊥
denotes the projection on the vector subbundle
(T{\mathcal{F}})^{\bot}
orthogonal to
{\mathcal{F}}
in
TM
.
The Codazzi equation for a foliation (or a submanifold) of
(M,g)
has the form, e.g.,
[
13
]
:

(\nabla_{X}\,h)(Y,U)-(\nabla_{Y}\,h)(X,U)=(R(X,Y)U)^{\bot},

(8)

where

R:TM\times TM\rightarrow{\rm End}(TM)
is the Riemannian curvature tensor of
\nabla
,

{R}(X,Y)=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}-\nabla_{[X,\,Y]},

and
h:T{\mathcal{F}}\times T{\mathcal{F}}\to(T{\mathcal{F}})^{\bot}

is the second fundamental form of
{\mathcal{F}}
in
(M,g)
defined by

h(X,Y)=(\nabla_{X}Y)^{\bot}.

Hence,
\langle A_{\xi}(X),\,Y\rangle=\langle h(X,Y),\,\xi\rangle
for
\xi\in{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{F}}
.

Lemma 2
.

The following Codazzi type equation is valid:

(\nabla^{\mathcal{F}}_{X}\,A_{\xi})Y-(\nabla^{\mathcal{F}}_{Y}\,A_{\xi})X=-R^{P}(X,Y)\xi,\quad X,Y\in T{\mathcal{F}},\ \ \xi\in{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{F}}.

(9)

Proof.

From (
8
), for all vectors
X,Y,U\in T{\mathcal{F}}
we get

\langle(\nabla^{\mathcal{F}}_{X}\,A_{\xi})Y-(\nabla^{\mathcal{F}}_{Y}\,A_{\xi})X,\ U\rangle+\langle h(X,U),\nabla_{Y}\xi\rangle-\langle h(Y,U),\nabla_{X}\xi\rangle=-\langle R(X,Y)\xi,U\rangle.

(10)

Applying the orthoprojector from
TM
on the vector subbundle
(T{\mathcal{F}})^{\bot}
, we find

\langle R(X,Y)\xi,U\rangle=\langle R^{P}(X,Y)\xi,U\rangle+\langle\nabla_{X}((\nabla_{Y}\,\xi)^{\bot})-\nabla_{Y}((\nabla_{X}\,\xi)^{\bot})-\nabla_{[X,\ Y]^{\bot}}\,\xi,U\rangle.

(11)

Using the equalities
[X,\ Y]^{\bot}=0
(since
T{\mathcal{F}}
is integrable), (
11
) and

\displaystyle\langle h(X,U),\,\nabla_{Y}\,\xi\rangle=\langle\nabla_{Y}\xi,\,(\nabla_{X}\,U)^{\bot}\rangle=-\langle\nabla_{X}((\nabla_{Y}\,\xi)^{\bot}),\,U\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle h(Y,U),\,\nabla_{X}\,\xi\rangle=\langle\nabla_{X}\xi,\,(\nabla_{Y}\,U)^{\bot}\rangle=-\langle\nabla_{Y}((\nabla_{X}\,\xi)^{\bot}),\,U\rangle,

in (
10
) completes the proof.

3
The
{\mathcal{F}}
-divergence of (1,1)-tensors on
{\mathcal{D}}

An orthonormal frame
\{e_{i},e_{a}\}_{1\leq i\leq n,\,1\leq a\leq p}
on
{\mathcal{D}}
built either of single vectors or of (local) vector fields
is said to be
adapted
(to
{\mathcal{F}}
), whenever
e_{i}\in T{\mathcal{F}}
and
e_{a}\in{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{F}}
.
Following
[
2

14
]
, define the
{\mathcal{F}}
-divergence of (1,1)-tensors

and

\operatorname{div}_{\mathcal{F}}A^{k}_{\xi}=\sum\nolimits_{\,i=1}^{n}(\nabla^{\mathcal{F}}_{e_{i}}\,A^{k}_{\xi})\,e_{i},\quad\operatorname{div}_{\mathcal{F}}T_{r}(A_{\xi})=\sum\nolimits_{\,i=1}^{n}(\nabla^{\mathcal{F}}_{e_{i}}\,T_{r}(A_{\xi}))\,e_{i}.

(12)

Note that

\operatorname{div}_{\mathcal{F}}T_{0}(A_{\xi})=\operatorname{div}_{\mathcal{F}}\,\operatorname{id}_{T{\mathcal{F}}}=\nabla^{\mathcal{F}}_{e_{i}}(\operatorname{id}_{T{\mathcal{F}}}\,e_{i})-\operatorname{id}_{T{\mathcal{F}}}(\nabla^{\mathcal{F}}_{e_{i}}\,e_{i})=0

Remark 1
.

Given a
(1,1)
-tensor
S
, they usually define the 1-form
{\rm div}_{\mathcal{F}}\,S
by

(\operatorname{div}_{\mathcal{F}}S)(X)=\sum\nolimits_{\,i=1}^{n}\langle(\nabla_{i}\,S)(X),\,e_{i}\rangle,

and the vector field
\nabla^{*{\mathcal{F}}}S
(called the
adjoint of the covariant
{\mathcal{F}}
-derivative
) by

(\nabla^{*{\mathcal{F}}}S)(X)=-\sum\nolimits_{\,i=1}^{n}(\nabla_{i}\,S)(e_{i},X).

Using a metric

(1,0)
-tensors with
(0,1)
-tensors.
For simplicity, in the article we use the definition (

-notation.

For any

(

The following formula is valid for any

\displaystyle\langle\operatorname{div}_{\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\xi},\,X\rangle\hskip-3.98337pt

\displaystyle\hskip-3.98337pt\sum\nolimits_{\,k<n}\big[(A^{k}_{\xi}X)(f_{k})+f_{k}\sum\nolimits_{\,j=1}^{k}\big(\frac{1}{k-j+1}\,(A^{j-1}_{\xi}X)(\tau_{k-j+1}(\xi))

Since (

Using (

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{\,i=1}^{n}\langle(\nabla^{{\mathcal{F}}}_{e_{i}}A_{\xi})A_{\xi}^{k-1}e_{i},\,X\rangle=\sum\nolimits_{\,i=1}^{n}\langle A^{k-1}_{\xi}e_{i},\,(\nabla^{{\mathcal{F}}}_{e_{i}}A_{\xi})X\rangle

Remark 3
.

The integrals over
({\mathcal{N}}_{1}{\mathcal{F}})_{x}
when

To show this, denote

, and

For example,

I_{0,\dots,0}=\frac{2\,\pi^{{p}/2}}{\Gamma({p}/2)}={\rm vol}(S^{{p}-1}(1)),\quad I_{2\lambda_{1},0,\dots,0}=2\,\pi^{\frac{{p}-1}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(1/2+\lambda_{1})}{\Gamma({p}/2+\lambda_{1})}.

[
2
, Lemma 3.1]

Lemma 3
.

Let
\{e_{i},e_{a}\}
be an adapted orthonormal frame of
{\mathcal{D}}

such that

\bullet

\nabla^{\mathcal{F}}_{X}\,e_{i}=0\ (1\leq i\leq n)

\langle\nabla_{X}e_{a},\,e_{b}\rangle=0\ (1\leq a,b\leq p)
for any vector

\bullet

\nabla^{P}_{\mu}\,e_{i}=0\ (1\leq i\leq n)
for any vector
\mu\in\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{x}
at a point

Then for any unit vector
\xi\in({\mathcal{N}}_{1}{\mathcal{F}})_{x}
we have

\langle\nabla^{{\mathcal{F}}}_{e_{i}}Z_{\,\xi},e_{j}\rangle=\langle A^{2}_{\xi}e_{i},e_{j}\rangle{+}\langle R^{P}(e_{i},\xi)\xi,e_{j}\rangle{-}\langle(\nabla^{{\mathcal{F}}}_{\xi}\,A_{\xi})e_{i},e_{j}\rangle+\!\sum\nolimits_{\,a=1}^{p}\langle{\nabla_{\xi}\,e_{a}},e_{i}\rangle\langle\nabla_{e_{a}}\xi,e_{j}\rangle.

Proof.

Taking covariant derivative of
\langle Z_{\,\xi},\,e_{j}\rangle=-\langle\xi,\,\nabla_{\xi}\,e_{j}\rangle
with respect to
e_{i}
, we find

-\langle Z_{\,\xi},\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{j}\rangle=\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}Z_{\,\xi},e_{j}\rangle+\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}\xi,P\nabla_{\xi}\,e_{j}\rangle+\langle\xi,\nabla_{e_{i}}P\nabla_{\xi}\,e_{j}\rangle.

(24)

For a foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
, we obtain

\langle A^{2}_{\xi}e_{i},e_{j}\rangle=\nabla_{\xi}\langle\xi,\nabla_{e_{i}}\,e_{j}\rangle=\langle Z_{\,\xi},\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{j}\rangle+\langle\xi,\nabla_{\xi}P\nabla_{e_{i}}\,e_{j}\rangle.

Therefore,
we calculate at the point
x\in M

\displaystyle\langle A^{2}_{\xi}e_{i},e_{j}\rangle+\langle R^{P}(e_{i},\xi)\,\xi,e_{j}\rangle-\langle(\nabla^{{\mathcal{F}}}_{\xi}A_{\xi})e_{i},e_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle A^{2}_{\xi}e_{i},e_{j}\rangle-\langle R^{P}(e_{i},\xi)\,e_{j},\xi\rangle+\xi\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}\xi,\,e_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle A^{2}_{\xi}e_{i},e_{j}\rangle-\langle Z_{\,\xi},\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{j}\rangle-\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}P\nabla_{\xi}\,e_{j},\xi\rangle+\langle\nabla_{[e_{i},\xi]}\,e_{j},\xi\rangle.

(25)

Using (
24

\displaystyle P\nabla_{e_{i}}\xi=\sum\nolimits_{\,j=1}^{n}\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}\xi,e_{j}\rangle e_{j},\quad P\nabla_{\xi}\,e_{i}=\sum\nolimits_{\,a=1}^{p}\langle\nabla_{\xi}\,e_{i},e_{a}\rangle\,e_{a},

From the above, the claim follows.

\square

7
Conclusion

We suggest that integral formulas (
1
), (
1
), (
2
), (
2
), and, in particular, (
33
), are a very good tool for understanding the geometry of (sub-)Riemannian manifolds equipped with foliations or distributions.
We delegate the following for further study.

1.
Our integral formulas can be extended for the case of
{\mathcal{D}}={\mathcal{D}}_{1}\oplus{\mathcal{D}}_{2}
– the sum of two smooth distributions,
see
[
17
]
in relation to (
33
) and
[
14
]
for
{\mathcal{D}}=TM
.
In other words, we take a non-integrable distribution
{\mathcal{D}}_{1}
instead of a foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
and
{\mathcal{D}}_{2}
instead of
{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{F}}
.
This naturally appears when
{\mathcal{D}}\subset TM
is a hyperdistribution, whose shape operator has an eigenvalue of constant multiplicity,
and
{\mathcal{D}}_{1}
is the corresponding eigen-distribution.

2. Our integral formulas can be extended for foliations and distributions defined outside of a “singularity set”
\Sigma

(a finite union of pairwise disjoint closed submanifolds of codimension greater than
2
of a closed manifold
M
)
under additional assumption of convergence of certain integrals.
Then, instead of the Divergence theorem, we apply the following, see
[
11
, Lemma 2]
: if
X
is a vector field on
M\setminus\Sigma
such that
\int_{M}\|X\|^{2}\,{\rm d}\operatorname{vol}_{g}<\infty
, then

\int_{M}\operatorname{div}X\,{\rm d}\operatorname{vol}_{g}=0
.

3. Mathematicians have shown a specific interest in manifolds equipped with several distributions, e.g., webs composed of different foliations and multiply warped products.
In
[
16
]
, we introduced the mixed scalar curvature for
k>2
pairwise orthogonal complementary distributions on
(M,g)

and proved the integral formula similar to (
3
) and (
33
) for this case.
This study was continued in
[
18
]
for the case of a linear connection instead of the Levi-Civita connection.
We suggest that our integral formulas
can be extended for sub-Riemannian manifolds equipped with several orthogonal foliations
(in
{\mathcal{D}}
) and certainly defined

{\rm S}^{P}_{\,\rm mix}
.

4. Finally, one can extend our integral formulas for holomorphic foliations of
complex sub-Riemannian manifolds, see
[
23
]
for the case of
{\mathcal{D}}=TM
and (
3
).

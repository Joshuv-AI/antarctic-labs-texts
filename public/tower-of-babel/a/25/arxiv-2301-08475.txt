Abstract




I
Experimental methods

I.1
Sample fabrication

The device was fabricated at CEA-LETI (Grenoble) on a 200 mm silicon-on-insulator wafer manufactured by SOITEC (Bernin) with a 220 nm-thick device layer of crystalline silicon. Silicon structures are patterned by deep ultraviolet lithography with 120 nm resolution, followed by inductively coupled plasma etching (HBr/Cl
2
/He-O
2
, realized in collaboration with LTM (Laboratoire
des Technologies de la Microelectronique)) and post-etch
\textrm{O}_{2}
plasma for resist stripping. The sidewall roughness of the waveguides is reduced by
hydrogen
annealing
45 45
. After encapsulation resulting in a 900 nm-thick
\textrm{SiO}_{2}
layer, 110 nm of titanium
nitride (TiN)
was
deposited and patterned to create the thermal phase
shifters. Aluminum copper (AlCu) was employed for the electrical pads.
A deep etch comprising two different steps (
\textrm{C}_{4}\textrm{F}_{8}/\textrm{O}2/\textrm{CO}/\textrm{Ar}
plasma for the
whole depth of
\textrm{SiO}_{2}
, Bosch method to remove 150
\mu
m of the 725
\mu
m-thick Si
substrate) was implemented to separate the sub-dice and to realize high quality end facets for edge coupling.

I.2
Device geometry

The silicon waveguides have a cross section of
600\,\textrm{nm}\times 220\,\textrm{nm}
.
The ring
resonators have a radius of
22\,\mu
m
,
and a coupling gap with the bus waveguide of
150
nm. The average separation between each resonator is
480\,\mu
m. The three Mach-Zehnder interferometers are each made by two
50/50
splitters, which are realized using evanescent couplers (length
17.5\,\mu
m, coupling gap
120
nm). The two arms have an equal length of
150\,\mu
m, which coincides with that of the heater. The phase shifters PS1, PS2 and PS3 in Fig.1(a) of the main text are realized by heaters of length
\sim 120\,\mu
m, which have a tuning efficiency of
\sim 15\textrm{deg}/\textrm{mW}
.

I.3
On chip state reconfiguration

Here we describe the step-by-step procedure that we implemented to generate the qutrit states
\ket{\Phi_{2}}=(\ket{11}+\ket{22}+\ket{33})/\sqrt{3}
and
\ket{\Phi_{3}}=(\ket{11}-\ket{22}-\ket{33})/\sqrt{3}
, and the two-ququart states
\ket{\Phi_{5}}=(\ket{00}+\ket{11}+\ket{22}+\ket{33})/2
and
\ket{\Phi_{6}}=(\ket{00}-\ket{11}-\ket{22}+\ket{33})/2
; see the main text for a description of the bin labels.

The procedure
relies on the acquisition of two-photon interference fringes between adjacent pairs of frequency bins. These are obtained by projecting the signal and the idler photon on
to

\bra{i}_{s(i)}+e^{i\theta_{s(i)}}\bra{i+1}_{s(i)}
, where
i
labels one of the frequency bins. Those projectors are realized by adjusting the RF power driving the phase modulators (PM) in order to equalize the intensities
|J_{\pm 1}|
of the first order sidebands to that of the baseband (
|J_{0}|
);
see below.
The RF phase
\theta_{s}=\theta_{i}=\bar{\theta}
is varied to scan a two-photon interference fringe, which is proportional to
\propto 1+V\textrm{cos}(2\bar{\theta})
(
V
is the fringe visibility).

Qutrit states
: for the state
\ket{\Phi_{2}}
, the relative phase between the biphoton emission from ring R1, R2, and R3 must be set equal. This is accomplished in two steps. First, we acquired a two-photon interference fringe by pumping only R1 and R2 (interference fringe 1-2). Second, a fringe is acquired by pumping only R2 and R3 (interference fringe 2-3). We then exploited the phase shifter PS1 (see Fig.1(a) in the main text) to align the two interference fringes. Note that one could have chosen the maximum of the fringe 1-2 as a reference, and used PS2 (or both PS1 and PS2) to align the maximum of two
fringes.
In the state
\ket{\Phi_{3}}
, the biphoton emission from R1 has a phase shift of
\pi
with respect to those from R2 and R3. In this case, we aligned the minimum of the fringe 1-2 to the maximum of the fringe 2-3.

Ququart states
: for these states we acquired three interference curves, one for each pair of adjacent rings (R0-R1, R1-R2, and R2-R3). To generate the state
\ket{\Phi_{5}}
, the relative phase between the biphoton emission from all the four rings must be set equal. We then aligned the maxima of all the three interference curves through the phase shifters PS1, PS2, and PS3. To generate the state
\ket{\Phi_{6}}
, we must set a relative phase of
\pi
between the biphoton emission from R1 and R2 to those of R0 and R3. Using PS3 and PS2, we aligned the minimum of the fringe 2-3 to the maximum of the fringe 1-2. After that, using PS1 we aligned the minimum of the fringe 0-1 to the minimum of the fringe 2-3.
In both the qutrit and the ququart cases, this procedure is repeated two times to mitigate the effect of thermal cross-talk between the heaters.

II
Density matrix reconstruction

We implemented quantum state tomography to reconstruct the density matrix
\rho
of the state by performing a series of projective measurements. The probability
p_{q}^{(j)}
of getting one of the
D^{2}
(
D
is the dimension of each of the two qudits) measurement outcomes is given by

p^{(j)}_{q}=\textrm{Tr}(\rho\mathcal{P}_{jq}),

(1)

where
\mathcal{P}_{jq}
is a positive-operator-valued-measure (POVM), defined as
\mathcal{P}_{jq}=U^{\dagger}(\bm{\gamma}_{j})\ket{q}\bra{q}U(\bm{\gamma}_{j})
. The operator
U(\bm{\gamma}_{j})=U_{s}(P_{s},\theta_{s})\otimes U_{i}(P_{i},\theta_{i})
describes the action of the electro-optic phase modulators (PMs) on the signal/idler photons, and
\ket{q}
indicates one of the
D^{2}
computational basis vectors.
We use
\gamma_{j}
to label the
j^{\textrm{th}}

measurement setting, which comprises the RF power
P_{s(i)}^{(\gamma_{i})}
and and phase
\theta_{s(i)}^{(\gamma_{i})}
of the signal and the idler PM.
Equation
1
can be rewritten as

p_{mn}=\textrm{Tr}(\rho U^{\dagger}\ket{mn}\bra{mn}U)=\bra{mn}U\rho U^{\dagger}\ket{mn},

(2)

where for simplicity
U=U(\gamma_{j})
. For each measurement setting
\gamma_{j}
=(
P_{s},\theta_{s},P_{i},\theta_{i}
), we collected coincidence counts
C_{mn}
for all the possible
\{(m,n)\,|\,m\in[0,..,D-1],n\in[0,D-1]\}
(
D
is the qudit dimension) frequency bins combinations. The measurement settings used to reconstruct the density matrices are reported in Table
1
.
The coincidence counts
C_{mn}
recorded in the experiment are related to the probabilities
p_{mn}
as

C_{mn}=R\cdot L_{mn}\cdot p_{mn},

(3)

where
R
is the rate of photon pairs at the modulators and
L_{mn}
are (measured) the losses from the modulators to the detectors.
We used the particle swarm optimization algorithm
46 46
to minimize the following cost function
f_{\textrm{cost}}

f_{\textrm{cost}}=\frac{1}{n_{\textrm{meas}}}\sum_{k=0}^{n_{\textrm{meas}}-1}\frac{(C_{\textrm{theo}}[k]-C_{\textrm{exp}}[k])^{2}}{2C_{\textrm{theo}}[k],}

(4)

where
n_{\textrm{meas}}
is the number of measurements,
C_{\textrm{exp}}
is the vector containing the recorded coincidence measurements and
C_{\textrm{theo}}
is the vector of expected coincidence counts, which are calculated from a candidate density matrix
\rho
by using Eq.(
3
). The matrix
\rho
is parameterized by a vector of
D^{2}
real coefficients
\bm{t}
as

\rho(\bm{t})=\frac{T(\bm{t})T^{\dagger}(\bm{t})}{\textrm{Tr}(T(\bm{t})T^{\dagger}(\bm{t}))},

(5)

where
T
is a complex lower triangular matrix with real elements on the diagonal. The particle swarm algorithm minimizes
f_{\textrm{cost}}
in the parameter space spanned by
(R,\bm{t})
.
We used
N=5
measurement settings for
D=2
, and
N=17
for
D=3
and
D=4
, corresponding respectively to
n_{\textrm{meas}}=20\,(D=2)
,
n_{\textrm{meas}}=153\,(D=3)
and
n_{\textrm{meas}}=272\,(D=4)
. These are more than the
D^{4}
measurements required to unambiguously determine the density matrix, but the redundancy helped to speed up the convergence of the reconstruction algorithm.

II.1
Impact of source indistinguishability on the reconstruction of the density matrix

The density matrix reconstruction is done through a series of projective measurements whose probability outcomes
p_{mn}
on the bin combination
\{(m,n)\,|\,m\in[0,..,D-1],n\in[0,D-1]\}
(
D
is the qudit dimension) are given by Eq.(
2
). The two-photon state
\ket{\Psi}
generated by our device is:

\ket{\Psi}=\sum_{k=0}^{D-1}\alpha_{k}\int\phi_{k}(\omega_{k_{s}}+\Omega_{1},\omega_{k_{i}}+\Omega_{2})\ket{\omega_{k_{s}}+\Omega_{1},\omega_{k_{i}}+\Omega_{2}}d\Omega_{1}d\Omega_{2},

(6)

where
\phi_{k}
is the joint spectral amplitude of the
k^{\textrm{th}}
ring resonator
47 47
, which generates photon pairs in the signal/idler bins
\ket{k}_{i}\ket{k}_{s}
, and
\{\alpha\}_{k=0,..,D-1}
is a list of complex coefficients such that
\sum_{k}|\alpha_{k}|^{2}=1
. The resonance frequency associated to the signal(idler) bin
k
(see Fig.1(c) in the main text) is
\omega_{k_{s(i)}}
.
The density matrix
\rho
then writes

\rho=\sum_{\{k,k^{\prime}\}=0}^{D-1}\int\alpha_{k}\alpha_{k^{\prime}}^{*}\phi_{k}(\omega_{k_{s}}+\Omega_{1},\omega_{k_{i}}+\Omega_{2})\phi_{k^{\prime}}^{*}(\omega_{k^{\prime}_{s}}+\Omega_{1}^{\prime},\omega_{k^{\prime}_{i}}+\Omega_{2}^{\prime})\ket{\omega_{k_{s}}+\Omega_{1},\omega_{k_{i}}+\Omega_{2}}\bra{\omega_{k^{\prime}_{i}}+\Omega_{2}^{\prime},\omega_{k^{\prime}_{s}}+\Omega_{1}^{\prime}}d\Omega_{1}d\Omega_{1}^{\prime}d\Omega_{2}d\Omega_{2}^{\prime}.

(7)

Under the action of the PMs, the density matrix in Eq.(
7
) evolves as
\rho\rightarrow U\rho U^{\dagger}
, where
U=U_{i}\otimes U_{s}
transforms the signal/idler annihilation operator
a
as

U_{s(i)}a_{m}(\omega_{m_{s(i)}}+\omega)U_{s(i)}^{\dagger}=\sum_{r=-\infty}^{+\infty}V_{mr}^{s(i)}a(\omega_{r_{s(i)}}+\omega).

(8)

The coefficients
V_{mr}^{s(i)}
are related to the Bessel functions of first kind
J_{m-r}
, of order
r-m
, as

V_{mr}^{s(i)}=J_{r-m}(\beta^{(\gamma_{i})})e^{i(r-m)\theta_{s(i)}^{(\gamma_{i})}},

(9)

which depend on both the strength of the modulation index
\beta^{(\gamma_{i})}
and on the phase
\theta_{s(i)}^{(\gamma_{i})}
of the RF signal applied to the phase modulators in the measurement setting
\gamma_{i}

22 22
;
48 48
.
After the transformation described by
U
, coincidences between the signal and the idler photon are collected in the bin combinations
(m,n)
, which are selected by using two fiber-Bragg-gratings (FBG). The center frequency of the stop band of the signal and the idler FBGs are set to be respectively
\omega_{n_{s}}
and
\omega_{m_{i}}
. With good approximation, we can model the transfer function of the reflection port of the FBG (i.e., the one connected to the single photon detector) as a box
centered at
\omega_{n(m)_{s(i)}}
and with a bandwidth of
2\Delta
. Since the frequencies within the stop band are not resolved, the action of the FBG is described by the operator
P_{mn}=P^{(i)}_{m}\otimes P^{(s)}_{n}
, where

P^{(i,s)}_{q}=\int_{-\Delta}^{\Delta}\ket{\omega_{q_{i(s)}}+\omega}\bra{\omega+\omega_{q_{i(s)}}}d\omega.

(10)

Using Eqs.(
2
,
10
) we obtain

p_{mn}=\int_{-\Delta}^{\Delta}\int_{-\Delta}^{\Delta}\bra{\omega_{m_{i}}+\omega,\omega_{n_{s}}+\omega^{\prime}}U\rho U^{\dagger}\ket{\omega_{n_{s}}+\omega^{\prime},\omega_{m_{i}}+\omega}d\omega d\omega^{\prime}.

(11)

We now use Eq.(
8
) to write

U^{\dagger}\ket{\omega_{n_{s}}+\omega^{\prime},\omega_{m_{i}}+\omega}=\sum_{\{r_{i},q_{s}\}=-\infty}^{+\infty}(V_{rm}^{i}V_{qn}^{s})^{*}\ket{\omega_{q_{s}}+\omega^{\prime},\omega_{r_{i}}+\omega}

(12)

which substituted in Eq.(
11
) gives

\displaystyle p_{mn}=\sum_{\{k,k^{\prime}\}}\sum_{\{r_{i},q_{s},r_{i}^{\prime},q_{s}^{\prime}\}}\int

\displaystyle\phi_{k}\phi_{k^{\prime}}^{*}\braket{\omega_{r^{\prime}_{i}}+\omega,\omega_{q^{\prime}_{s}}+\omega^{\prime}|\omega_{k_{s}}+\Omega_{1},\omega_{k_{i}}+\Omega_{2}}\braket{\omega_{k^{\prime}_{i}}+\Omega_{2}^{\prime},\omega_{k^{\prime}_{s}}+\Omega_{1}^{\prime}|\omega_{q_{s}}+\omega^{\prime},\omega_{r_{i}}+\omega}\times

(13)

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}\alpha_{k^{\prime}}^{*}V_{mr^{\prime}}^{i}V_{nq^{\prime}}^{s}(V_{rm}^{i}V_{qn}^{s})^{*}d\omega d\omega^{\prime}d\Omega_{1}d\Omega_{2}d\Omega_{1}^{\prime}d\Omega_{2}^{\prime}

(14)

The different frequency bins do not overlap, and they are fully contained within bandwidth
2\Delta
of the FBGs, which allows us to write

\displaystyle\braket{\omega_{r^{\prime}_{i}}+\omega,\omega_{q^{\prime}_{s}}+\omega^{\prime}|\omega_{k_{s}}+\Omega_{1},\omega_{k_{i}}+\Omega_{2}}=\delta(\omega^{\prime}-\Omega_{1})\delta(\omega-\Omega_{2})\delta_{r_{i}^{\prime},k_{i}}\delta_{q_{s}^{\prime},k_{s}},

\displaystyle\braket{\omega_{k^{\prime}_{i}}+\Omega_{2}^{\prime},\omega_{k^{\prime}_{s}}+\Omega_{1}^{\prime}|\omega_{q_{s}}+\omega^{\prime},\omega_{r_{i}}+\omega}=\delta(\omega^{\prime}-\Omega_{1}^{\prime})\delta(\omega-\Omega_{2}^{\prime})\delta_{r_{i},k^{\prime}_{i}}\delta_{q_{s},k^{\prime}_{s}}.

By performing the integrals over
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\Omega_{1}^{\prime},\Omega_{2}^{\prime}
, and the sums over
\{r_{i},r_{i}^{\prime},q_{s},q_{s}^{\prime}\}
we obtain

p_{mn}=\sum_{\{k,k^{\prime}\}}V_{mk_{i}}^{i}V_{nk_{s}}^{s}(V_{k^{\prime}_{i}m}^{i}V_{k^{\prime}_{s}n}^{s})^{*}\alpha_{k}\alpha_{k^{\prime}}^{*}\int\phi_{k}(\omega_{k_{s}}+\omega^{\prime},\omega_{k_{i}}+\omega)\phi_{k^{\prime}}^{*}(\omega_{k^{\prime}_{s}}+\omega^{\prime},\omega_{k^{\prime}_{i}}+\omega)d\omega d\omega^{\prime}.

(15)

The overlap integral between the joint spectral amplitude
\phi_{k}
and
\phi_{k^{\prime}}
on the right hand side of Eq.(
15
) is the indistinguishability
I_{kk^{\prime}}
between source
k
and
k^{\prime}
, i.e.

I_{kk^{\prime}}=\int\phi_{k}(\omega_{k_{s}}+\omega^{\prime},\omega_{k_{i}}+\omega)\phi_{k^{\prime}}^{*}(\omega_{k^{\prime}_{s}}+\omega^{\prime},\omega_{k^{\prime}_{i}}+\omega)d\omega d\omega^{\prime}.

(16)

The final expression for the probability
p_{mn}
of measuring a coincidence in the bin combination
(m,n)
after that the transformation
U
has been applied is then

p_{mn}=\sum_{\{k,k^{\prime}\}}\alpha_{k}\alpha_{k}^{\prime*}I_{kk^{\prime}}V_{mk_{i}}^{i}V_{nk_{s}}^{s}(V_{k^{\prime}_{i}m}^{i}V_{k^{\prime}_{s}n}^{s})^{*}

(17)

We now demonstrate that the same probability outcome in Eq.(
17
) can be obtained from a density matrix
\rho^{\prime}
with entries
\rho^{\prime}_{ij,kl}=\bra{ij}\rho^{\prime}\ket{kl}=I_{ik}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{k}^{*}\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}
. The basis state
\ket{ij}
describes a signal and an idler photon respectively in the frequency bins
\ket{i}
and
\ket{j}
. Compared to the definition of
\rho
in Eq.(
7
), the density matrix
\rho^{\prime}
neglects any intra-bin frequency correlation in favor of a simpler description of the state, in which the frequency distribution is collapsed to a single label index.
Note that in the limit
I_{ij}\rightarrow 1
, i.e. when all the sources are perfectly indistinguishable, we have that
\rho^{\prime}\rightarrow\ket{\Phi}\bra{\Phi}
, where
\ket{\Phi}=\sum_{k=0}^{D-1}\alpha_{k}\ket{kk}
. When
|\alpha_{k}|=D^{-1/2}
,
\ket{\Phi}
describes a maximally entangled Bell state. We can use Eq.(
2
) to calculate the probability of measuring a coincidence over the bin combination
(m,n)
after that the transformation
U
has been applied, which gives

\displaystyle p_{mn}

\displaystyle=\bra{mn}U\rho^{\prime}U^{\dagger}\ket{mn}

(18)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}^{*}I_{ij}\bra{mn}U\ket{ii}\bra{jj}U^{\dagger}\ket{mn}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\sum_{p,q,r,s}(V^{i}_{pm}V^{s}_{qn})^{*}V^{i}_{mr}V^{s}_{ns}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}^{*}I_{ij}\braket{rs|ii}\braket{jj|pq}

\displaystyle=\sum_{ij}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}^{*}I_{ij}(V^{i}_{jm}V^{s}_{jn})^{*}V^{i}_{mi}V^{s}_{ni},

which coincides with the expression in Eq.(
17
). In the density matrix reconstruction algorithm described in section II, we neglect the intra-bin frequency correlations and represent
\rho
on the
D^{2}
-dimensional basis
\ket{mn}
, where
\{(m,n)\,|\,m\in[0,..,D-1],n\in[0,D-1]\}
. By construction, the algorithm does not return the density matrix
\rho
in Eq.(
7
), but gives the maximum likelihood estimation of
\rho^{\prime}
.

II.2
Impact of source indistinguishability on the purity and the fidelity of the reconstructed density matrix

The indistinguishability between all the sources determines the maximum amount of purity and fidelity with the target states that can be achieved by the reconstructed density matrices. We investigated how these quantities vary with the indistinguishability
I_{jk}
, which appear in the density matrix as
\rho^{\prime}_{ij,kl}=\bra{ij}\rho^{\prime}\ket{kl}=I_{ik}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{k}^{*}\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}
. For simplicity, we assumed that all the sources have an equal pair-wise indistinguishability, i.e.
I_{jk}=I
.
Note that in the limit
I\rightarrow 1
, i.e. when all the sources are perfectly indistinguishable, we have that
\rho^{\prime}\rightarrow\ket{\Phi}\bra{\Phi}
, where
\ket{\Phi}

=\sum_{k=0}^{D-1}\alpha_{k}\ket{kk}
. When
|\alpha_{k}|=D^{-1/2}
,
\ket{\Phi}
describes a maximally entangled Bell state.
In our analysis, we set
|\alpha_{k}|=D^{-1/2}
, and we calculated the purity
\mathcal{P}
and the fidelity
\mathcal{F}
with the maximally entangled Bell state
\ket{\Phi}
as a function of
I
for
D=2,3,4
.
The results are shown in Fig.
1
. In the experiment, we measured an average
indistiguishability of
\bar{I}=0.87(7)
. This value is marked in Fig.
1
with a dashed line, while the gray area encloses the confidence bounds.
The range of values assumed by the purity and the fidelity over that region are reported in Table
2
. These values are compared with the purities
\mathcal{P}
and the fidelities
\mathcal{F}
extracted from the reconstructed density matrices in the experiment. In Table
2
, we reported these values for the target states
\ket{\Phi_{1}}
(qubit),
\ket{\Phi_{2}}
(qutrit), and
\ket{\Phi_{5}}
(ququart) (see definitions in Table 1 of the main text). Except for the purity of the qubit, which is slightly higher in the experiment than in the simulation, all the values lie in the confidence intervals predicted by the simulation.

A Characterization of Umbral Calculus Inspired by Fractional Sums
Tang Qian

Abstract
We will use analytic function theory and Fourier analysis to establish a characterization for some classical umbral calculus, which will focus on the generalization of the evaluation function. Although we cannot cover all the umbral calculus people care about, the part about Bernoulli numbers can still answer an open question about fractional sums raised by Müller and Schleicher in 2005 [ 10 ] and synthesize some common analysis results. We will only develop the results which are sufficient to serve the purpose of this article, but at the same time, we will briefly mention some possible extensions.

The Analysis of Umbrae

The Fourier transform of
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, which we take is

\hat{f}(\xi)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\xi x}\mathrm{d}x,

and it also induced the Fourier transform on slowly increasing distribution space
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
. Sometimes we would interpret the integral of the Fourier transform in the meaning of the summation method.

For our purpose, we will generalize the concept of the Fourier transform as below.

Definition 2.1

(Fourier transform)
.

If
f
is defined on
\mathbb{R}-\mathrm{i}t

\displaystyle\hat{f}(\xi-\mathrm{i}s)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty-\mathrm{i}t}^{\infty-\mathrm{i}t}f(z)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(\xi-\mathrm{i}s)z}\mathrm{d}z

(2.1)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-(\xi-\mathrm{i}s)t}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-sx}f(x-\mathrm{i}t)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\xi x}\mathrm{d}x.

It should be emphasized that

could be exponential growth, and

could be undefined on

.

Definition 2.2

(Umbrae)

If

Lemma 2.1

Suppose

.

Let’s first redeem the promise and see how the MS-fractional summation related to the Bernoulli umbrae.

Although the selection of
\Omega_{a,b}
does not need to include the origin, here we still only care about the Bernoulli umbrae that includes the origin, which allows us to define
\mathrm{B}^{n}
. Some conclusions can be generalized to other Bernoulli umbras.

Lemma 3.1
.

\mathrm{B}+\Delta=1+\mathrm{D}.

Theorem 9

If there exists

such that

1.

2.6

By Theorem

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\left(F(n+\mathrm{B})-\sum_{k=0}^{p}\frac{f^{(k-1)}(n)\mathrm{B}_{k}}{k!}\right)=0.

By the Lagrangian remainder theorem, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{p}\frac{(f^{(k-1)}(t+y)-f^{(k-1)}(t))\mathrm{B}_{k}}{k!}=\sum_{k=0}^{p}a_{k}(t)y^{k}+o(1)y^{p+1},

therefore we have polynomial

such that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}(f(k+y)-f(k))-P_{n}(y)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}((F(n+y+\mathrm{B})-F(n+\mathrm{B}))-P_{n}(y))-\int_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{B}+y}f(k)\mathrm{d}k

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}o(1)y^{p+1}-\int_{\mathrm{B}}^{\mathrm{B}+y}f(k)\mathrm{d}k

\displaystyle\Rightarrow F(y+\mathrm{B}+1)-F(t+\mathrm{B})=F^{\prime}(y+1),

which satisfies the conditions in Definition

∎

At this point, we justify the speculation from Müller and Schleicher. Next, we will see that these conditions are sufficient to cover the special series they care about.

Finally, it needs to be emphasized that the processing of Bernoulli umbrae is essentially equivalent to applying the Abel-Plana formula. Although we have removed the decay condition of the function when the imaginary part is large, this is actually done by requiring the function to be exponential growth.

Gosper series

Let’s consider a sufficiently general example.

Suppose

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{2n}}{(2n)!}z^{2n}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-|\mathrm{Im}z|}|z^{-n}J(\sqrt{z^{2}+b^{2}})|

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot\mathrm{e}^{|\mathrm{Im}\sqrt{z^{2}+b^{2}}|-|\mathrm{Im}z|}|z|^{\nu_{J}-n}

3.2
Bernoulli umbrae

This subsection will mainly introduce how to use Bernoulli umbrae
\mathrm{B}
to synthesize some common analysis results. This approach can also be easily extended to similar umbrae such as Euler umbrae
\mathrm{E}
.

The proof of the following theorem can actually be given directly by its corollaries, so it is omitted here.

Theorem 10

(Bernoulli umbrae)
.

1.

\ln\mathrm{B}=-\gamma
;

2.

\mathrm{B}\ln\mathrm{B}=\ln\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{e}}{2\pi}}
;

3.

\mathrm{B}^{2}\ln\mathrm{B}=\frac{1}{4}-2\ln A
, where
A
is Glaisher–Kinkelin constant;

4.

\mathrm{B}^{z}=-z\zeta(1-z)
.

Lemma 3.3

(Multiplication theorem)
.

n\mathrm{B}[+](n\mathrm{B}-1)[+]\cdots[+](n\mathrm{B}-n+1)=[n]+\mathrm{B}.

In fact, this lemma synthesized all the multiplication theorems about special functions.

\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\right)^{n}\mathrm{B}^{z}=\mathrm{B}^{z}\ln^{n}\mathrm{B}

.
∎

,

Corollary 3.4

(

)
.

1.

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{n}-\ln n\right)=\gamma

\zeta(1+s)=\frac{1}{s}-\gamma+o(1)

3.

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{n}-\ln n

\displaystyle=\ln(\mathrm{B}+n)+\gamma-\ln n

\displaystyle=\gamma+\ln(1+\frac{\mathrm{B}}{n})\to\gamma

\displaystyle\zeta(1+s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{B}^{-s}}{s}=\frac{1-s\ln\mathrm{B}+o(s)}{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s}-\gamma+o(1)

\displaystyle\frac{(x!)^{\prime}}{x!}

\displaystyle=\ln(\mathrm{B}+x)

\displaystyle(x!)^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=0!\ln\mathrm{B}=-\gamma.

Corollary 3.5

(

.

\displaystyle\ln n!

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{B}+n)\ln(\mathrm{B}+n)-n-\ln\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{e}}{2\pi}}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{B}+n)\ln n+(\mathrm{B}+n)\ln(1+\frac{\mathrm{B}}{n})-n-\ln\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{e}}{2\pi}}

\displaystyle=(\frac{1}{2}+n)\ln n+\mathrm{B}^{1}+O(\frac{1}{n})-n-\ln\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{e}}{2\pi}}

\displaystyle=(\frac{1}{2}+n)\ln n-n+(\frac{1}{2}-\ln\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{e}}{2\pi}})+O(\frac{1}{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{B}^{-s+1}}{s-1}=-\mathrm{B}^{1}+s(\mathrm{B}^{1}-\mathrm{B}\ln\mathrm{B})+o(s)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}-s\ln\sqrt{2\pi}+o(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\mathrm{B}-1}{2}\ln\frac{2\mathrm{B}-1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\mathrm{B}\ln\mathrm{B}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\ln 2-\frac{1}{2}-\ln\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{e}}{2\pi}}=\ln\sqrt{\pi}.

∎

4
Some possible developments

If we only apply the Bernoulli umbrae, then what we are actually doing is using that notation to simplify the application of the Abel-Plana formula. So the key points seem to be on the calculus between these umbrae.

For
A_{i}=(\mathcal{A}_{i}(z),\Omega_{a,b})
, we can define

A_{1}\times A_{2}\coloneqq(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{z}\ln\frac{\mathcal{A}_{1}(z)}{\mathcal{A}_{1}(0)}\ln\frac{\mathcal{A}_{2}(z)}{\mathcal{A}_{2}(0)}},\Omega_{a,b})

where
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are both invertible respect to the addition. It is again a umbrae, and gives a rare three-level arithmetic, i.e. “
\times
” distributes over “
+
”, and “
+
” distributes over “
[+]
”.

Let
\mu=(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N^{+}}}
be a given sequence.
Suppose
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu_{n}z^{n}
converges near the origin, such that
\mathcal{A}(z)=z^{m}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu_{n}\mathrm{e}^{nz}

can be continuation to
\Omega_{a,b}
.
Denote

(m\times\mathrm{D})+\mathrm{S}(\mu)\coloneqq(\mathcal{A}(z),\Omega_{a,b}).

(4.1)

We can expect that for a suitable
f
, such as
f\in\mathcal{T}_{a,k;b,l}^{(p-1)}
, we have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu_{n}f(n)=f^{(-m)}(\mathrm{S}(\mu)).

(4.2)

This type of umbrae allows us to promote our previous treatment on Bernoulli umbrae further. For example,

1.

(m\times\mathrm{D})+\mathrm{S}(\mu)=\mathrm{B}
,
where
m=1
and
\mu_{n}=-1
;

2.

(m\times\mathrm{D})+\mathrm{S}(\mu)=\mathrm{E}
,
where
m=0
and
\mu_{n}=2\chi_{1}(n)
where
\chi_{1}
is the non-trivial Dirichlet character of modulo 4.

Unfortunately, not like the Dirichlet character, the sequence
\mu
seems cannot be replaced with a number theory sequence that is too irregular. For example,
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{n^{2}}
is full of singularities on the entire unit circle.

Through the symmetry of the Fourier transformation, we are able to calculate
A^{-n}
more directly, which is essentially the Ramanujan’s master theorem, i.e.

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-Ax}x^{n}\mathrm{d}x=n!A^{-n-1}.

(4.3)

It seems there is a chance to achieve more similar results.

We can use Bernoulli umbrae to deal with some series in
[

]
. Although the process is relatively complicated, the approach is very straightforward.

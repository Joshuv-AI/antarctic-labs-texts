Abstract
Global hyperon polarization, P ¯ H \overline{P}_{\mathrm{H}} , in Au+Au collisions over a large range of collision energy, s NN \sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}} , was recently measured and successfully reproduced by hydrodynamic and transport models with intense fluid vorticity of the quark-gluon plasma. While naïve extrapolation of data trends suggests a large P ¯ H \overline{P}_{\mathrm{H}} as the collision energy is reduced, the behavior of P ¯ H \overline{P}_{\mathrm{H}} at small s NN < 7.7 \sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}<7.7 GeV is unknown. Operating the STAR experiment in fixed-target mode, we measured the polarization of Λ \Lambda hyperons along the direction of global angular momentum in Au+Au collisions at s NN = 3 \sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=3 GeV. The observation of substantial polarization of 4.91 ± 0.81 ( stat . ) ± 0.15 ( syst . ) 4.91\pm 0.81(\rm stat.)\pm 0.15(\rm syst.) % in these collisions may require a reexamination of the viscosity of any fluid created in the collision, of the thermalization timescale of rotational modes, and of hadronic mechanisms to produce global polarization.



Collisions between heavy nuclei at the
highest energies at the Relativistic Heavy
Ion Collider (RHIC) and the Large
Hadron Collider (LHC) produce the quark-gluon
plasma (QGP), a strongly interacting system
characterized by colored degrees of freedom
Akiba et al. 2015
.
Viscous relativistic hydrodynamics is one of
the most powerful tools to understand
this system theoretically
Teaney 2010
; it is the
dynamical heart of the “standard model
of the Little Bang”
Heinz 2013
. Systematic comparisons
of data and the hydrodynamic response
to anisotropies in the initial state
have yielded considerable insight on transport
coefficients and the equation of state
of the QGP
Bernhard et al. 2016
. Recently, considerable experimental
and theoretical efforts have focused on
the polarization of particles emitted from
the fluid
Becattini and Lisa 2020
—mostly
\Lambda
hyperons
Liang and Wang 2005
;
Becattini et al. 2008
;
Betz et al. 2007
;
Abelev et al. 2007
;
Adamczyk et al. 2017
;
Acharya et al. 2020
and,
very recently, multi-strange hyperons
Adam et al. 2021a
—which probe
the local vorticity of the fluid.

Hydrodynamic
Becattini and Lisa 2020
and transport
Vitiuk et al. 2020
;
Li et al. 2017
simulations each reproduce
rather well the measured “global” polarization,
the component directed along the total
angular momentum of the collision,
\vec{J}
.
In most hydrodynamic calculations, particle properties
(e.g., momentum and flavor) are derived
from fluid properties (e.g., stress-energy tensor
and chemical potentials) through the Cooper-Frye
ansatz
Becattini and Lisa 2020
, which assumes equilibrium at the
point of hadronization. This formalism has
been generalized
Becattini et al. 2013
to calculate particle polarization
directly from the thermal vorticity
Becattini and Lisa 2020
of
the fluid. Equilibration of orbital angular
momentum and spin degrees of freedom
is assumed, though spin relaxation times
are not fully understood
Bhadury et al. 2021
. In transport
simulations, the vorticity is calculated from
the particles in small cells, and
polarization is extracted through the generalized
Cooper-Frye formalism discussed above.

As in previous studies,
\overline{P}_{\mathrm{H}}
represents
the spin polarizations of
\Lambda

\bar{\Lambda}

\overline{P}_{\Lambda}

\bar{\Lambda}

We report in this work our

observation raises important questions:
What is the spin equilibration timescale,

\overline{P}_{\Lambda}

\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=3

The dataset discussed in this work
was collected in 2018 by the
STAR experiment
Ackermann et al. 2003
. The STAR detector configuration
features the cylindrical geometry characteristic of
collider experiments. In order to explore
various regions of the QCD phase
diagram, RHIC has undertaken a multiyear

to lower energies. While the maximum
energy of a gold beam in
the RHIC ring is 100 GeV per
nucleon, the facility is remarkably flexible
and beams with energy as low
as 3.85 GeV per nucleon can be
maintained for reasonable times; thus, the
lowest energy measured in beam-on-beam collisions
is
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=7.7
GeV. However, operating the facility
and experiment in fixed-target mode, in
which the beam collides with a
foil target inside the beam pipe
positioned 200 cm away from the center
of the Time Projection Chamber (TPC), produces collisions at
energies as low as
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=3
GeV. See Ref.
Adam et al. 2021b

for details of the STAR fixed-target configuration.

Charged-particle tracks in the pseudorapidity range
-2\lesssim\eta\lesssim 0

are measured in the TPC

-1.5\lesssim\eta\lesssim 0

(BTOF) detector

Shao et al. 2006

\eta<-2.55

Pseudorapidity is reported

collision center-of-momentum frame, boosted by the
beam rapidity,
y_{\rm beam}=1.045
.
After basic offline selections
to ensure that the reconstructed collision
occurred in the target foil,
253\times 10^{6}

events were available for this analysis.

The centrality of an event, which
describes the degree to which the
colliding nuclei overlap, was estimated based
on the number of “primary” tracks,
which are mainly determined by checking

Fitting this multiplicity distribution to a

.
Our coverage in rapidity
was in the range
-0.2<y<1.0
.

The parity-violating nature of hyperon decay
reveals the spin polarization,
\vec{P}_{\Lambda}
. The
global polarization is the projection of

\vec{P}_{\Lambda}
along the direction of the
angular momentum of the collision,
\hat{J}
Abelev et al. 2007
;
Becattini and Lisa 2020
;
in the case of a symmetric
collision system and detector setup we
may write this as
Adamczyk et al. 2017

\overline{P}_{\Lambda}\equiv\left\langle\vec{P}_{\Lambda}\cdot\hat{J}\right\rangle=\frac{8}{\pi\alpha_{\Lambda}}\frac{1}{R_{\mathrm{EP}}^{(1)}}\left\langle\sin\left(\Psi_{1}-\phi_{p}^{*}\right)\right\rangle^{\mathrm{sig}}.

(1)

Here,
\alpha_{\Lambda}=0.732\pm 0.014
is the
\Lambda
-hyperon decay
constant

,
\phi_{p}^{*}
is the azimuthal angle
of the daughter proton momentum in
the
\Lambda
-hyperon rest frame, and the
average is over all hyperons in
the momentum range selected.
\Psi_{1}
is
the first-order event plane of the
collision, and
R_{\mathrm{EP}}^{(1)}
is the resolution
with which the event plane estimates
the reaction plane of the collision,
which is normal to
\hat{J}
. The
“sig” label indicates that the average
excludes combinatoric-background contributions and acceptance effects;
we discuss these below. As we
will see later, the symmetries required
to achieve the form of Eq.
(
1

in fixed-target mode.

\frac{8}{\pi\alpha_{\Lambda}}\frac{1}{R_{\mathrm{EP}}^{(1)}}\left\langle\sin\left(\Psi_{1}-\phi_{p}^{*}\right)\right\rangle^{\mathrm{sig}}=\overline{P}_{\Lambda}+c\sin(\phi_{\Lambda}-\phi_{p}^{*}),

Finite detector acceptance and efficiency necessitate
two additional corrections on the measured
polarization. Equation (
1
) assumes that the efficiency
to measure daughter protons is independent
of
\vec{p}_{p}^{\,*}
, the daughter momentum direction
in the hyperon rest frame. However,
rapidity cuts and inefficiencies introduce a
weak dependence on
\vec{p}_{p}^{\,*}
, leading to
a correction factor
\tfrac{4}{\pi}\overline{\sin\theta_{p}^{*}}

which depends
on
p_{\mathrm{T}},y
, and centrality and
is
\mathcal{O}(1\%)
. Similarly, the
\Lambda
-hyperon detection
efficiency,
\varepsilon\left(y,p_{\mathrm{T}}\right)
, depends on
\vec{p}_{\Lambda}
.

Polarizations predicted by 3FD calculations depend
on the range of hydrodynamic rapidity

using one equation of state in
which the deconfinement transition is characterized
as first order and using another
assuming a crossover transition; the resulting
difference in polarization between these two
methods is much smaller than the
width of the band.

We find that, while the central value of the
3FD calculation
Ivanov 2021

overshoots the measurement
at
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=3
GeV by
\sim 30\%
,
the prediction and our measurement
roughly agree within uncertainties.
The partonic-transport
calculation
Guo et al. 2021
, which reproduces the measurements quite
well at
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}\geq 7.7
GeV, dramatically underestimates
\overline{P}_{\Lambda}

at
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=3
GeV; the model was tuned
for very low collision energy and
therefore differs from previous calculations using
the same model at larger
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}
Wei et al. 2019
;
Li et al. 2017
;
Shi et al. 2019
.
The difference between the predictions
made using the 3FD and
AMPT models becomes larger at
low collision energy and suggests
that the polarization is strongly
dependent on the state of
the system. We observe rough

may imply that the system
evolves hydrodynamically even at low

At a more general level than
\sim 1\sigma

As seen in Fig.

This expectation is borne out by

In Fig.

Global polarization is directly related to

\vec{J}

transverse and longitudinal degrees of freedom.
However,
\overline{P}_{\mathrm{H}}

higher

small role in decreased polarization

models associate the
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}

with the vorticity becoming increasingly
concentrated at forward rapidity,
|y|\gtrsim 1\mathrm{-}1.5
,
including transport

,
hydrodynamics
Ivanov and Soldatov 2018
;
Wu et al. 2019
;
Ivanov et al. 2019
;
Ivanov and Soldatov 2020
,
and geometric-driven calculations
Liang et al. 2021
.
Correspondingly, these
models predict a strong increase of

\overline{P}_{\mathrm{H}}
as

other calculations predict a dramatic reduction
of
\overline{P}_{\mathrm{H}}
away from mid-rapidity

;
Wei et al. 2019
;
Xie et al. 2020
. In
most models, the dependence becomes stronger
at lower
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}
since higher-energy collisions
better approximate boost invariance in the
mid-rapidity region.

While all previous measurements were confined
to the region

were unable to reconstruct forward-rapidity

\overline{P}_{\Lambda}

Our measurement of nonzero
\overline{P}_{\Lambda}
at

\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=3
GeV demonstrates that vorticity aligned
with
\hat{J}
is at a maximum
below
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=7.7
GeV. The data agree roughly
with calculations made using the 3FD
model, integrated over mid-rapidity, but are
dramatically larger than such calculations made
using the partonic-transport model AMPT.
As in Ref.
Adam et al. 2018
, we observe
a significant centrality dependence of
\overline{P}_{\Lambda}

that is consistent with increasing
\vec{J}
.
Our measurement of the dependence of

\overline{P}_{\Lambda}
on
y
is uniquely valuable
because we have access to the
most forward-rapidity
\Lambda
hyperons. Interestingly, despite
the variety of model calculations predicting
quite strong dependence of
\overline{P}_{\mathrm{H}}
on

y

;
Ivanov and Soldatov 2018
;
Wu et al. 2019
;
Ivanov et al. 2019
;

;
Liang et al. 2021
;
Deng and Huang 2016
;
Wei et al. 2019
;
Xie et al. 2020
,
we see no statistically
significant dependence. A migration of
\overline{P}_{\mathrm{H}}

towards forward rapidity has been offered

monotonic fall of
\overline{P}_{\mathrm{H}}
with
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}
Jiang et al. 2016
.
Given our observation, such an explanation
may be incorrect, though this does
not dispel such arguments as the
state of the system at higher
energy is notably different; measurements of

\overline{P}_{\mathrm{H}}
using the STAR forward upgrade
will provide indispensable comparisons to the
work presented here.

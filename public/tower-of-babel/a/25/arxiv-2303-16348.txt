Some new results on the higher energies I
I.D. Shkredov

Abstract




Annotation.

We obtain a generalization of the recent Kelley–Meka result on sets avoiding arithmetic progressions of length three.
In our proof we develop the theory of the higher energies.
Also, we discuss the case of longer arithmetic progressions, as well as a general family of norms, which includes the higher energies norms and Gowers norms.

Introduction

The famous Erdős–Turán conjecture
[
5
]
asks is it true that for any integer
k\geqslant 3
any set of positive integers
A=\{n_{1}<n_{2}<\dots<n_{m}<\dots\}

satisfying

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n_{j}}=\infty

(1)

contains an arithmetic progression of length
k
(we say
A
has APk), that is the sequence of the form
x,x+y,\dots,x+(k-1)y\in A
?
This question has a rich history see, e.g.,
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
or
[
25
]
and is considered a central one in the area of classical additive combinatorics due to its connection with many adjecent fields as combinatorical ergodic theory and graphs/hypergraphs theory, we just mention some papers

[
28
]
,
[
29
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
31
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
14
]
etc.
If one defines

r_{k}(N)=\frac{1}{N}\max\{|A|~:~A\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\,,\quad A\mbox{ has no APk}\}\,,

then the condition (
1
) means, roughly, that

r_{k}(N)\ll\frac{1}{\log N\cdot(\log\log N)^{1+\varepsilon}}\,,\quad\quad N\to\infty

(2)

for an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
.

The case of arithmetic progressions of length three was considered to be special thanks to the Fourier approach of Roth
[
16
]
, the required information and references can be found in
[
2
]
,
[
13
]
, as well as in
[
25
]
.
Bloom and Sisask in
[
1
]
proved that
r_{3}(N)\ll(\log N)^{-1-c_{1}}
for a certain
c_{1}>0
and hence established conjecture (
2
) in the case of
k=3
.
Recently, Kelley and Meka
[
13
]
achieved a remarkable progress in this question and proved that

r_{k}(N)\ll\exp(-O((\log N)^{c_{1}}))\,,

where
c_{1}>0
is an absolute constant.
One of the ideas of paper
[
13
]
was to use the higher energy
\mathsf{E}^{k}_{2}
and the uniformity relatively to
\mathsf{E}^{k}_{2}
(all definitions can be found in Sections
2
,
3
) with a growing parameter
k
to control the number of arithmetic progressions in an arbitrary set.
Namely, bound (
2
) is an immediate consequence of the following result (for simplicity we consider the group
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
).

Theorem 1

Let
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
,
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a set,
|A|=\delta N
, and
\varepsilon>0
be a parameter.
Then there is a subspace
V\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
and
x\in{\mathbf{G}}
such that
A\cap(V+x)
is
\varepsilon
–uniform relatively to
\mathsf{E}^{k}_{2}
,
\mu_{V+x}(A)\geqslant\delta
, and

{\rm codim}V\ll\varepsilon^{-14}k^{4}\mathcal{L}^{3}(\delta)\mathcal{L}^{2}(\varepsilon\delta)\,.

(3)

The aim of this paper is to generalize Kelley–Meka results to a more wide additive–combinatorial family of energies
\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}
see, e.g.,
[
27
]
. In our regime the parameter
l
below is
l=O(1)
.

Theorem 2

Let
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
,
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a set,
|A|=\delta N
, and
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
be a parameter.
Then there is a subspace
V\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
and
x\in{\mathbf{G}}
such that
A\cap(V+x)
is
\varepsilon
–uniform relatively to
\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}
,
\mu_{V+x}(A)\geqslant\delta
and

{\rm codim}V\ll\varepsilon^{-28l^{l}}(8l)^{28l^{l}}k^{4}\mathcal{L}^{4l}(\varepsilon)\mathcal{L}^{5l}(\delta)\,.

(4)

Theorem
4
is interesting in its own right and can be used to
solve more general equations and systems than
x+y=2z
, which corresponds to the case of AP3.
Our forthcoming paper will be devoted to finding some applications of this result.

Usually the case of the groups
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
for prime
p
is considered to be a model one, see the excellent survey
[
11
]
about this theme and an appropriate generalization for all abelian groups
{\mathbf{G}}
is known to be a technical task. We will prove the correspondent result in our forthcoming paper.

The approach develops the strategy of
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
, the method of the higher energies (see, e.g.,
[
21
]
,
[
27
]
) and of course
[
13
]
.
Also, we extensively use a brilliant exposition
[
2
]
, where the Kelley–Meka results were discussed.

In the appendix we discuss the original Erdős–Turán conjecture, that is the case of longer arithmetic progressions and show that there is series of difficulties on the conceptual and on the technical levels which make the question about generalizations of the methods from
[
13
]
rather hard.
The author thinks this part is also interesting in its own right because it allows us to understand the limits of the Kelley–Meka approach.
Also, we consider a general family of norms, which includes, simultaneously, the norms
\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}
above, as well as the classical Gowers norms
[
9
]
.

2
Definitions and preliminaries

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a finite abelian group
and
denote by
N
the cardinality of
{\mathbf{G}}
.
We use the same capital letter to denote set
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
and its characteristic function
A:{\mathbf{G}}\to\{0,1\}
.
Let us define
\mu_{A}(x)=A(x)/|A|
, that is
\sum_{x\in{\mathbf{G}}}\mu_{A}(x)=1
.
Finally, let
f_{A}(x)=A(x)-|A|/N
be the
balanced function
of
A
.
Given two sets
A,B\subset{\mathbf{G}}
, define
the
sumset

of
A
and
B
as

A+B:=\{a+b~:~a\in{A},\,b\in{B}\}\,.

In a similar way we define the
difference sets
and
higher sumsets
, e.g.,
2A-A
is
A+A-A
.

Let
f
be a function from
{\mathbf{G}}
to
\mathbb{C}.
We denote the Fourier transform of
f
by
\widehat{f},

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\sum_{x\in{\mathbf{G}}}f(x)\overline{\chi(x)}\,,

(5)

where
\chi\in\widehat{{\mathbf{G}}}
is a character of
{\mathbf{G}}
.
We rely on the following basic identities

\sum_{x\in{\mathbf{G}}}|f(x)|^{2}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{{\mathbf{G}}}}\big|\widehat{f}(\chi)\big|^{2}\,,

(6)

and

f(x)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{{\mathbf{G}}}}\widehat{f}(\chi)\chi(x)\,.

(7)

If

(f*g)(x):=\sum_{y\in{\mathbf{G}}}f(y)g(x-y)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad(f\circ g)(x):=\sum_{y\in{\mathbf{G}}}f(y)g(y+x)\,,

then

\widehat{f*g}=\widehat{f}\widehat{g}

(8)

and similar for
f\circ g
.
Clearly,
(f*g)(x)=(g*f)(x)
and
(f\circ g)(x)=(g\circ f)(-x)
,
x\in{\mathbf{G}}
.
The
k
–fold convolution,
k\in\mathbb{N}
we denote by

f^{(k)}
,
so
f^{(2)}=f*f
and
f^{(3)}=f*f*f
for example.

We need some formalism concerning higher convolutions see, e.g.,

[
27
]
.
Let
l
be a positive integer.
Consider
two operators
{\mathcal{D}}_{l}
,
{\mathcal{P}}_{l}:{\mathbf{G}}\to{\mathbf{G}}^{l}
such that for a variable
x
one has
{\mathcal{D}}_{l}(x)=(x,\dots,x)\in{\mathbf{G}}^{l}
and
{\mathcal{P}}_{l}(x)=(x_{1},\dots,x_{l})\in{\mathbf{G}}^{l}
.
Notice that
{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(x)={\mathcal{D}}_{1}(x)=x
.
In the same way these operators act on functions, e.g.,

{\mathcal{P}}_{l}(f)(x_{1},\dots,x_{l})=f(x_{1})\dots f(x_{l})
for
f:{\mathbf{G}}\to\mathbb{C}
and for
F:{\mathbf{G}}^{l}\to\mathbb{C}
one has
{\mathcal{D}}_{l}(F)(x_{1},\dots,x_{l})=F(x_{1},\dots,x_{1})
if
x_{1}=\dots=x_{l}
and zero otherwise.
Now given a function
f:{\mathbf{G}}\to\mathbb{C}
and a positive integer
l
define the generalized convolution

{\mathcal{C}}_{l}(f)(x_{1},\dots,x_{l})=\sum_{z\in{\mathbf{G}}}f(z+x_{1})\dots f(z+x_{l})=({\mathcal{D}}_{l}({\mathbf{G}})\circ{\mathcal{P}}_{l}(f))(x_{1},\dots,x_{l})

(9)

:=\sum_{z\in{\mathbf{G}}}f_{x_{1},\dots,x_{l}}(z)\,.

(10)

In a similar way we can consider
{\mathcal{C}}_{l}(f_{1},\dots,f_{l})(x_{1},\dots,x_{l})
for any functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{l}:{\mathbf{G}}\to\mathbb{C}
.
One has

{\mathcal{C}}_{l}(f)(x_{1},\dots,x_{l})={\mathcal{C}}_{l}(f)(x_{1}+w,\dots,x_{l}+w)={\mathcal{C}}_{l}(f)((x_{1},\dots,x_{l})+{\mathcal{D}}_{l}(w))

(11)

for any
w\in{\mathbf{G}}
.
Let us
emphasise
that definitions (
9
), (
10
) differ slightly from the usual one, see, e.g.,
[
27
]
by a linear change of the variables. Namely, it is a little bit more traditional to put

f^{\prime}_{x_{1},\dots,x_{l}}(z)=f_{0,x_{1},\dots,x_{l}}(z)=f(z)f(z+x_{1})\dots f(z+x_{l})\,,

(12)

and

{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{l+1}(f)(x_{1},\dots,x_{l})=\sum_{z\in{\mathbf{G}}}f(z)f(z+x_{1})\dots f(z+x_{l})={\mathcal{C}}_{l}(f)(0,x_{1},\dots,x_{l})\,.

(13)

Definitions (
12
), (
13
) have an advantage that they allow to consider infinite groups
{\mathbf{G}}
as well.
To this end we use the dual notation
\|f\|^{kl}_{\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}}=\bar{\mathsf{E}}^{k}_{l}(f)=N^{-1}\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}(f)
.
Now having
k,l\geqslant 2
and a function
f:{\mathbf{G}}\to\mathbb{C}
one can consider

\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}(f)=\sum_{x_{1},\dots,x_{l}}{\mathcal{C}}^{k}_{l}(f)(x_{1},\dots,x_{l})=\mathsf{E}^{l}_{k}(f)

(14)

and it was showed in

[
27
, Proposition 30]
that for a real function
f
and even
k,l
the formula
(\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}(f))^{1/kl}
defines a norm of our function
f
.
If one put
l=1
in (
14
), then we formally obtain
\mathsf{E}_{1}^{k}(f)=N(\sum_{z}f(x))^{k}
and this is not a norm for any
k
. Nevertheless, it is convenient to consider the quantities
\mathsf{E}_{1}^{k}(f)
sometimes.
Notice that
\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}(f)\geqslant 0
,
provided at least one of
k,l
is even but, nevertheless, it cannot be a norm in this case, see
[
27
, Sections 4,7]
.
A general family of norms, which includes the norms above is considered in the second part of the appendix. In particular, one can find the discussed properties of the energies
\mathsf{E}_{l}^{k}(f)
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
be a real number. We write
\mathcal{L}(\varepsilon)
for
\log(2/\varepsilon)
.
Let us
make a convention
that if a product is taken over an empty set, then it equals one.
The signs
\ll
and
\gg
are the usual Vinogradov symbols.
When the constants in the signs depend on a parameter
M
, we write
\ll_{M}
and
\gg_{M}
.
All logarithms are to base
2
.
By
\mathbb{F}_{p}
denote
\mathbb{F}_{p}=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
for a prime
p
. Let us denote by
[n]
the set
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.

3
Some results on
\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}
–norms

In this section we obtain some generalization of Kelley–Meka results which were obtained for
\mathsf{E}_{2}^{k}
–norm to
\mathsf{E}_{l}^{k}
–norm. Also, we discuss some special properties of such norms. Our results naturally break down into two cases: uniform and non–uniform.

3.1
Uniform sets in the sense of
\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}
–norm

Let us give the main definition of this subsection.

Definition 3

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be an abelian group,
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a set,
|A|=\delta N
, and
\varepsilon>0
be a parameter.
Then we say that
A
is
\varepsilon
–uniform relatively to (the energy)
\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}
if

\|f_{A}\|^{kl}_{\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}}\leqslant\varepsilon^{kl}\delta^{kl}N^{k+l}\,.

(15)

Usually the number
\varepsilon
belongs to
(0,1]
but sometimes
\varepsilon>1
and hence one can consider the quantity
\varepsilon
as the definition of the energy
\|f_{A}\|^{kl}_{\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}}
, that is

\|f_{A}\|^{kl}_{\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}}:=\varepsilon^{kl}\delta^{kl}N^{k+l}
.
Further by the Hölder inequality, we have

(\mathsf{E}^{k-1}_{l}(f))^{k}\leqslant(\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}(f))^{k-1}N^{l}

(16)

and hence if
A
is
\varepsilon
–uniform relatively to
\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}
, then
A
is
\varepsilon
–uniform relatively to
\mathsf{E}^{k^{\prime}}_{l^{\prime}}
for
k^{\prime}\leqslant k
,
l^{\prime}\leqslant l
.
On the other hand,
it is easy to see that a smaller norm does not control the higher one.

Example 4

Let
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
,
H<\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
,
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}/H
be a random set such that
|\Lambda|=\delta N/|H|
.
Also, suppose that
\delta^{2}\gg|H|/N
and thus with high probability
\Lambda-\Lambda\approx\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}/H
.
Let
A
be the direct sum of
H
and
\Lambda
, then
|A|=\delta N
.
It is easy to see that for a random
x\in A-A\approx{\mathbf{G}}
one has
|A_{x}|\sim\delta^{2}N
but for
x,y\in H
one has
A_{x}=A
and
A_{x,y}=A
.
Thus for any
k\geqslant 2
the following holds

\mathsf{E}_{2}^{k}(A)\sim(\delta^{2}N)^{k}N+(\delta N)^{k}|H|\sim(\delta^{2}N)^{k}N\,,

provided
|H|\ll\delta^{k}N
but taking an arbitrary
l
, we see that

\mathsf{E}^{l}_{3}(A)\sim(\delta^{3}N)^{l}N^{2}+(\delta N)^{l}|H|^{2}\gg(\delta N)^{l}|H|^{2}\,,

provided
|H|\gg\delta^{l}N
.
It follows that one can take
l=k+1
and construct a set
A
such that
A
is
\mathsf{E}_{2}^{k}
–uniform but not
\mathsf{E}_{3}^{k+1}
–uniform.

Now let us obtain the characteristic property of the energy
\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}
.

Lemma 5

Let
l,k\geqslant 2
be even numbers,
and
A_{j}\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
,
j\in[l]
be sets.
Then for any function
g:{\mathbf{G}}\to\mathbb{R}
one has

\sum_{x}\prod_{j=1}^{l}(f_{A_{j}}\circ g)(x)\leqslant\|g\|^{l(1-1/k)}_{1}\|{\mathcal{C}}_{l}(g)\|^{1/k}_{\infty}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{l}\|f_{A_{j}}\|_{\overline{\mathsf{E}}^{k}_{l}}\,.

(17)

If the sets
A_{j}
are the same, then the same is true for even
k
only.

P r o o f.

By the Hölder inequality and
[
27
, Lemma 29]
and the duality one has

\sum_{x}\prod_{j=1}^{l}(f_{A_{j}}\circ g)(x)=N^{-1}\sum_{|z|=l}{\mathcal{C}}_{l}(f_{A_{1}},\dots,f_{A_{l}})(z){\mathcal{C}}_{l}(g)(z)

\leqslant N^{-1+1/k}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\|f_{A_{j}}\|_{\overline{\mathsf{E}}^{k}_{l}}\cdot\left(\sum_{|z|=l}|{\mathcal{C}}^{k/(k-1)}_{l}(g)(z)|\right)^{1-1/k}\leqslant\prod_{j=1}^{l}\|f_{A_{j}}\|_{\overline{\mathsf{E}}^{k}_{l}}\cdot\|{\mathcal{C}}_{l}(g)\|^{1/k}_{\infty}\|g\|^{l(1-1/k)}_{1}

This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

Corollary 6

Let
A,B\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be sets,
|A|=\delta N
,
|B|=\beta N
, and
l
be a positive integer.
Take
k=2\lceil el\log(1/\beta)\rceil

and suppose that
A
is

\varepsilon
–uniform relatively to the energy
\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}
.
Then

\sum_{x}(A\circ B)^{l}(x)\leqslant\delta^{l}|B|^{l}N\cdot\min\{1.25(1+\varepsilon)^{l},(1+1.25\varepsilon)^{l}\}\,.

(18)

P r o o f.

Using the formula
A(x)=f_{A}(x)+\delta
, combining with Lemma
5
, we see that the left–hand side of (
18
) is

\sum_{j=0}^{l}\binom{l}{j}(\delta|B|)^{l-j}\sum_{x}(f\circ B)^{j}(x)\leqslant\sum_{j=0}^{l}\binom{l}{j}(\delta|B|)^{l-j}\|f\|^{j}_{\overline{\mathsf{E}}^{k}_{j}}|B|^{j(1-1/k)+1/k}

\leqslant\delta^{l}|B|^{l}N\sum_{j=0}^{l}\binom{l}{j}\varepsilon^{j}\beta^{-(j-1)/k}\leqslant\frac{5}{4}\delta^{l}|B|^{l}N(1+\varepsilon)^{l}

as required and similarly the second bound.
This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

Let us remark that, of course, the energy
\mathsf{E}_{2}^{k}
solely allows us to control sums from (
18
) but our task is to obtain the correct power of
\delta
and
|B|
in the right–hand side of this estimate.

We need one more result about uniform set, which is useful for applications.

Lemma 7

Let
k,l
be even numbers,

A_{1},\dots,A_{l}\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be sets,
|A_{j}|=\delta_{j}N
.
Suppose that all
A_{j}
are
\varepsilon
–uniform relatively to
\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}
and

2l\varepsilon^{k}\leqslant 1
.
Then

\sum_{|x|=l}\left({\mathcal{C}}_{l}(A_{1},\dots,A_{l})(x)-N\prod_{j=1}^{l}\delta_{j}\right)^{k}\leqslant\varepsilon^{k}l2^{kl+1}N^{l+k}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{l}\delta_{j}\right)^{k}\,.

(19)

P r o o f.

Put
\Pi=\prod_{j=1}^{l}\delta_{j}
.
Then the left–hand side of (
19
) is

\sigma:=\sum_{|x|=l}\left(\sum_{\emptyset\neq S\subseteq[l]}{\mathcal{C}}_{l}(f_{1},\dots,f_{l})(x)\right)^{k}=\sum_{|x|=l}\left(\sum_{\emptyset\neq S\subseteq[l]}F_{S}(x)\right)^{k}\,,

where for
j\in S
we put
f_{j}=f_{A_{j}}
and if
j\notin S
, then
f_{j}=\delta_{j}
.
Using
\varepsilon
–uniformity of all sets
A_{j}
, combining with
[
27
, Lemma 29]
, we get

\|F_{S}\|^{kl}_{\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}}\leqslant\varepsilon^{|S|k}\Pi^{k}N^{l+k}\,.

Thus by the Hölder inequality one has

\sigma\leqslant 2^{kl}\sum_{\emptyset\neq S\subseteq[l]}\|F_{S}\|^{kl}_{\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}}\leqslant 2^{kl}\Pi^{k}N^{k+l}((1+\varepsilon^{k})^{l}-1)\leqslant l2^{kl+1}\varepsilon^{k}\Pi^{k}N^{k+l}

as required.

\hfill\Box

Let us consider one more example which shows that one can delete/add a tiny subset from a non–uniform set to obtain a uniform one. This phenomenon has no place if we consider the classical uniformity in terms of the Fourier transform or in terms of Gowers norms
[
9
]
, say. The reason is normalization (
15
), of course.

Example 8

Let
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
,
H<\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
,
|H|=\beta N
,
\Lambda\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a random set,
|\Lambda|=\delta N
,
\beta\leqslant\delta
and put
A=\tilde{H}\bigsqcup\Lambda
, where
\tilde{H}=H\setminus\Lambda
. Then with high probability
|\tilde{H}|\sim\beta(1-\delta)N=\varepsilon|A|
, the set
\Lambda
is uniform in any possible sense but
A
is non
\eta
–uniform set with rather large
\eta
.
Indeed, by Kelley–Meka method
[
13
]
or just see Lemma
5
, we know that

\sigma:=\sum_{x}(A\circ A)(x)H(x)=(\delta+(1-\delta)\beta)^{2}\beta N^{2}+\theta\eta^{2}\delta^{2}\beta N^{2}\,,

(20)

where
|\theta|\leqslant 4
, say, is a certain number and

A
is supposed to be
\mathsf{E}_{2}^{k}
–uniform with
k\sim\mathcal{L}(\beta)
.
On the other hand, the direct calculation shows

\sigma=|\tilde{H}|^{2}+2\delta\beta^{2}(1-\delta)N^{2}+\delta^{2}|H|N

(21)

plus a negligible error term.
Comparing (
20
) and (
21
), we obtain

\eta^{2}\delta^{3}\varepsilon\gg 2\delta\beta\varepsilon+\beta\varepsilon^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}\delta^{2}\gg\delta\varepsilon^{3}

and thus
\eta\gg\varepsilon/\delta
which is much larger than
\varepsilon
for small
\delta
.

Similarly,
one can
show that deleting
a subspace
H
from a random set
\Lambda
,
|H|=\varepsilon|A|
,
H
lives on the first coordinates, say, we obtain a non
\eta
–uniform set with
\eta\gg 1
thanks to the equality
\sum_{x}H(x)(A\circ A)(x)=0
.

3.2
Non–uniformity and almost periodicity

The aim of this subsection is to obtain Sanders’ almost periodicity result for
higher convolutions
, see Lemma
38
below.

At the beginning
we want to transfer
a lower bound for the
energy
\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}(f_{A})
to
the largeness of the energy

\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}(A)
.
We follow a more simple method from
[
2
]
which differs from the approach of
[
13
]
by some logarithms. The dependence on
l
in the first multiple in (
23
) is, probably, can be improved significantly (also, see Remark
20
from the appendix)
but in our regime
l=O(1)
and
thus it is not so critical for us.

Lemma 9

Let
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a set,
|A|=\delta N
and

\varepsilon>0
be a parameter,
\varepsilon_{*}:=\min\{\varepsilon,1\}
.
Suppose that for an odd
k\geqslant 5
one has

\mathsf{E}_{l}^{k}(f_{A})=\varepsilon^{lk}\delta^{lk}N^{l+k}\,,

(22)

and that for
k_{*}=O(kl\varepsilon^{-l}_{*}\mathcal{L}(\varepsilon_{*}))
the set
A
is
\frac{\varepsilon\varepsilon^{l-1}_{*}}{8l}
–uniform relatively to
\mathsf{E}^{k_{*}}_{l-1}
.
Then there is an even
k_{1}\leqslant k_{*}
such that

\mathsf{E}_{l}^{k_{1}}(A)\geqslant\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon\varepsilon^{l-1}_{*}}{8l}\right)^{lk_{1}}\delta^{lk_{1}}N^{l+k_{1}}\,.

(23)

P r o o f.

Write
f(x)=f_{A}(x)
.
We have

\varepsilon^{lk}\delta^{lk}N^{l+k}\leqslant\sum_{|x|=l}{\mathcal{C}}^{k-1}_{l}(f)(x)|{\mathcal{C}}_{l}(f)(x)|+\sum_{|x|=l}{\mathcal{C}}^{k}_{l}(f)(x)=2\sum_{|x|=l}{\mathcal{C}}^{k-1}_{l}(f)(x)\cdot\max\{0,{\mathcal{C}}_{l}(f)(x)\}\,,

and thus considering the set
P=\{x~:~{\mathcal{C}}_{l}(f)(x)\geqslant 0\}
, we get

Lemma 11

Let
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
,
l
be an integer and
\epsilon\in(0,1]
be a real parameter.
Also, let

be a set,
|B|=\beta N
, and

\mathrm{codim}V\ll\epsilon^{-2}l\mathcal{L}^{2}(\beta)\mathcal{L}^{2}(\epsilon\beta^{l})

(37)

\left|\sum_{|x|=l}f(x)(B^{l}\circ{\mathcal{D}}_{l}(B*\mu_{V}))(x)-\sum_{|x|=l}f(x)(B^{l}\circ{\mathcal{D}}_{l}(B))(x)\right|\leqslant\epsilon|B|^{l+1}\,.

(38)

P r o o f.

We begin with a rather general argument which takes place in any abelian group
{\mathbf{G}}
.
Let
k\geqslant 2
be an integer parameter and
q\geqslant 2
be a real parameter.
Applying the Croot–Sisask lemma
[
4
]
,
[
18
]
(clearly, one has
|{\mathcal{D}}_{l}(B)+{\mathcal{D}}_{l}({\mathbf{G}})|\leqslant\beta^{-1}|{\mathcal{D}}_{l}(B)|
, so the set

has the small doubling),
we find a set
T\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
,
|T|\geqslant|B|\exp(-O(\epsilon^{-2}qk^{2}\log(1/\beta)))

\leqslant\left(\frac{\epsilon}{4}\right)^{q}\|f\|_{q}^{q}|B|^{q}\leqslant\left(\frac{\epsilon}{4}\right)^{q}|B|^{q}N^{l}\,.

(39)

Fixing

and using the Hölder inequality, combining with estimate (
39
), we get

\left|\sum_{|x|=l}f(x)(B^{l}\circ{\mathcal{D}}_{l}(B))(x+{\mathcal{D}}_{l}(t))-\sum_{|x|=l}f(x)(B^{l}\circ{\mathcal{D}}_{l}(B))(x)\right|\leqslant\frac{\epsilon}{4}|B|N^{l/q}|B|^{l(1-1/q)}

=\frac{\epsilon}{4}\beta^{-l/q}|B|^{l+1}\leqslant\frac{\epsilon}{2}|B|^{l+1}\,,

(40)

where we have taken

for a sufficiently large constant

.
It follows that

\left|\sum_{|x|=l}f(x)(B^{l}\circ{\mathcal{D}}_{l}(B*\mu^{(k)}_{T}))(x)-\sum_{|x|=l}f(x)(B^{l}\circ{\mathcal{D}}_{l}(B))(x)\right|\leqslant 2^{-1}\epsilon|B|^{l+1}\,.

(41)

Let us analyze the sum
\sigma:=|\sum_{|x|=l}f(x)(B^{l}\circ{\mathcal{D}}_{l}(B*\mu^{(k)}_{T}))(x)|
from (
41

Clearly,
\widehat{\mu}_{T}(r_{1},\dots,r_{l})=|T|^{-1}\widehat{T}(r_{1}+\dots+r_{l})

(42)

As usual let us estimate the last sum over
z\in{\rm Spec\,}_{c}(T)
, where
c\in(0,1]
is a parameter and over
z\notin{\rm Spec\,}_{c}(T)
,
where we have defined

{\rm Spec\,}_{c}(T)=\{z\in{\mathbf{G}}~:~|\widehat{T}(z)|\geqslant c|T|\}\,.

By the definition of the set
{\rm Spec\,}_{c}(T)
, the Hölder inequality and the Parseval identity, we have

\sigma_{1}:=\frac{|B|}{|T|^{k}N^{l}}\sum_{z\notin{\rm Spec\,}_{c}(T)}|\widehat{T}(z)|^{k}\sum_{r_{1}+\dots+r_{l}=z}|\widehat{f}(r_{1},\dots,r_{l})||\widehat{B}(r_{1})|\dots|\widehat{B}(r_{l})|

\leqslant\frac{c^{k}|B|}{N^{l}}\sum_{r_{2},\dots,r_{l}}|\widehat{B}(r_{2})|\dots|\widehat{B}(r_{l})|\sum_{z}|\widehat{f}(z-r_{2}+\dots+r_{l},\dots,r_{l})||\widehat{B}(z-r_{2}+\dots+r_{l})|

Using the Hölder inequality and the Parseval formula one more time, we derive

\sigma_{1}\leqslant\frac{c^{k}|B|^{(l+2)/2}}{N^{(l-1)/2}}\left(\sum_{r_{2},\dots,r_{l}}\sum_{a}|\widehat{f}_{a}(r_{2},\dots,r_{l})|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leqslant c^{k}|B|^{(l+2)/2}N^{l/2}\leqslant 2^{-1}\epsilon|B|^{l+1}\,.

Here we have taken
c=1/2
and

, say.
For the sum over

we use the Chang lemma

3
]
about the dimension of the spectrum and find a subspace

such that (

\mathrm{codim}V\ll\log(N/|T|)\ll\epsilon^{-2}l\mathcal{L}^{2}(\beta)k^{2}\ll\epsilon^{-2}l\mathcal{L}^{2}(\beta)\mathcal{L}^{2}(\epsilon\beta^{l})\,,

see details in

]

\bar{x}\oplus\bar{y}
, and using the Hölder inequality, which gives
(|F|^{2}\circ{\mathcal{D}}_{l}(\mu_{B}))(\bar{x}\oplus\bar{y})^{q/2}\leqslant(|F|^{q}\circ{\mathcal{D}}_{l}(\mu_{B}))(\bar{x}\oplus\bar{y})
, we get that

\sum_{|x|=s-1}\,\sum_{|y|=t-1}\int\left|\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}F((\bar{x}\oplus\bar{y})+{\mathcal{D}}_{l}(b_{j}))-(F\circ{\mathcal{D}}_{l}(\mu_{B}))(\bar{x}\oplus\bar{y})\right|^{q}d\mu_{B}^{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})

\ll(qk^{-1})^{q/2}\sum_{|x|=s-1}\,\sum_{|y|=t-1}(|F|^{q}\circ{\mathcal{D}}_{l}(\mu_{B}))(\bar{x}\oplus\bar{y})

=(qk^{-1})^{q/2}|B|^{-1}\sum_{|y|=t-1}{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{t}(|F|^{q})^{s-1}(y)\cdot{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{t}(B,|F|^{q},\dots,|F|^{q})(y)\,.

After that we repeat the argument from

[
4
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
19
]
and

[
26
, Theorem 15]
.
This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

5.2
On a family of norms

In this section we define a very general family of norms, which includes the norms
\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}
,
\mathcal{E}^{k}_{s,t}
above, as well as the classical Gowers norms
[
9
]
. As the reader can see we do not use the Fourier approach in our proofs below.

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be an abelian group,
r,k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geqslant 2
be integers and
f:{\mathbf{G}}\to\mathbb{R}
be an arbitrary function.
Let
K=\prod_{j=1}^{r}k_{j}
,
B=[k_{1}]\times\dots\times[k_{r}]
and write
x_{1}=(x^{(1)}_{1},\dots,x^{(k_{1})}_{1}),\dots,x_{r}=(x^{(1)}_{r},\dots,x^{(k_{r})}_{r})
.
Also, for
\omega\in B
we write
\omega=(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{r})
.
Define

\|f\|^{K}_{E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}=\sum_{|x_{1}|=k_{1}}\,\dots\sum_{|x_{r}|=k_{r}}\,{\mathcal{P}}_{K}(f)(x_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus x_{r})

=\sum_{|x_{1}|=k_{1}}\,\dots\sum_{|x_{r}|=k_{r}}\,\prod_{\omega\in B}f(x^{(\omega_{1})}_{1}+\dots+x^{(\omega_{r})}_{r})\,.

(85)

The case
r=2
corresponds to
\mathsf{E}^{k}_{l}
–norm,
r=3
is just
\mathcal{E}^{k}_{s,t}
–norms and for
k_{1}=\dots=k_{r}=2
, we obtain Gowers’
U^{k}
norms (up to some normalizations).
In a similar way one can define the multi–scalar product for the quantity
\|\cdot\|_{E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}
as was done in
[
9
]
, namely, having any functions
(f^{\omega})_{\omega\in B}
, we write

\langle f^{\omega}\rangle_{E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}=\sum_{|x_{1}|=k_{1}}\,\dots\sum_{|x_{r}|=k_{r}}\,\prod_{\omega\in B}f^{\omega}(x^{(\omega_{1})}_{1}+\dots+x^{(\omega_{r})}_{r})\,.

(86)

It is easy to see that if
K
is an even number, then

\|f\|^{K}_{E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}\geqslant 0\,.

(87)

Indeed, let, say,
k_{r}
be an even number, then we can write
x_{r}
as
x_{r}=(x^{\prime}_{r},x^{\prime\prime}_{r})
, where
|x^{\prime}_{r}|=|x^{\prime\prime}_{r}|=k_{r}/2
and whence

\|f\|^{K}_{E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}=\sum_{|x_{1}|=k_{1}}\,\dots\sum_{|x_{r-1}|=k_{r-1}}\,\left(\sum_{|x^{\prime}_{r}|=k_{r}/2}{\mathcal{P}}_{K/2}(f)(x_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus x_{r-1}\oplus x^{\prime}_{r})\right)^{2}\geqslant 0\,.

Also, let us remark the inductive property of the norm
E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}
. For concreteness, we take the
r
th coordinate and obtain from definition (
85
) that

\|f\|^{K}_{E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}=\sum_{|z|=k_{r}}\|f_{z}\|^{K/k_{r}}_{E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r-1}}}\,.

(88)

Let us make a simple remark concerning
E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}
–norm.

Lemma 22

Let
f:{\mathbf{G}}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function.
Suppose that there is
j\in[r]
such that
k_{j}
is even and
K/k_{j}
is also even. Then
\|f\|_{E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}=0
iff
f\equiv 0
.

P r o o f.

Without loosing of the generality assume that
j=r
.
Write

\|f\|^{K}_{E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}=\sum_{|x_{1}|=k_{1}}\,\dots\sum_{|x_{r-1}|=k_{r-1}}\,\left(\sum_{z}{\mathcal{P}}_{K/k_{r}}(f)(x_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus x_{r-1}\oplus z)\right)^{k_{r}}=0\,.

Since
k_{r}
is an even number, it follows that, in particular,

\sum_{z}f^{K/k_{r}}(z)=0
(we have taken
x_{1}=\dots=x_{r-1}=0
in the last formula) and hence
f\equiv 0
.
This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

Now
let us show that the multi–scalar product is controlled via
E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}
–norm.

Lemma 23

Let
r\geqslant 2
be a positive integer,
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geqslant 2
be even integers and
f^{\omega}:{\mathbf{G}}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\omega\in B
be any functions.
Then

|\langle f^{\omega}\rangle_{E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}|\leqslant\prod_{\omega\in B}\|f^{\omega}\|_{E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}\,.

(89)

P r o o f.

We write

\langle f^{\omega}\rangle_{E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}=\sum_{|x_{1}|=k_{1}}\,\dots\sum_{|x_{r-1}|=k_{r-1}}\,\left(\sum_{x^{(1)}_{r}}\,\prod_{\omega\in B,\,\omega_{r}=1}f^{\omega}(x^{(\omega_{1})}_{1}+\dots+x^{(\omega_{r-1})}_{r-1}+x^{(1)}_{r})\right)

\dots\left(\sum_{x^{(k_{1})}_{r}}\,\prod_{\omega\in B,\,\omega_{r}=k_{r}}f^{\omega}(x^{(\omega_{1})}_{1}+\dots+x^{(\omega_{r-1})}_{r-1}+x^{(k_{r})}_{r})\right)\,.

After that
apply the Hölder inequality (here we have used the fact that
k_{r}
is an even number)
and we arrive to the new
k_{r}
families of functions.
Take any of them, say,
(\tilde{f}^{\omega})
,
\omega\in B
and notice that

\tilde{f}^{\omega}=\tilde{f}^{\omega^{\prime}}=f^{\omega}

for all
\omega=(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{r})
,
\omega^{\prime}=(\omega^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\omega^{\prime}_{r})
with
(\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{r})=(\omega^{\prime}_{2},\dots,\omega^{\prime}_{r})
.
In particular, the family
(\tilde{f}^{\omega})
,
\omega\in B
has
K/k_{r}
different functions.
Now we use the same argument for all remaining variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{r-1}
subsequently changing the families
(f^{\omega})
,
\omega\in B
. One can easily see that after all these
r
steps we arrive to
K
families consisting of single functions
f^{\omega}
,
\omega\in B

(just
thanks to
the fact that any two points of our box
B
can be reached by a path in the directions of coordinate axes).
This is equivalent to inequality (
89
)
and we
complete
the proof.

\hfill\Box

Finally, we are ready to
obtain the main result of this section.
Let us
write
(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})\leqslant(m_{1},\dots,m_{t})
if the first vector is lexigraphically smaller than the second one (i.e.,
r\leqslant t
and
k_{j}\leqslant m_{j}
,
j\in[r]
).
Also, put

\|f\|^{K}_{\bar{E}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}=N^{-(k_{1}+\dots+k_{r})}\|f\|^{K}_{E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}\,.

(90)

Thus for any
f:{\mathbf{G}}\to[-1,1]
one has
\|f\|_{\bar{E}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}\leqslant 1
.

Theorem 24

Let
r\geqslant 2
be a positive integer,
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geqslant 2
be even integers and
f:{\mathbf{G}}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function.
Then formula (
85
) defines a norm of
f
.
Further if
(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})\leqslant(m_{1},\dots,m_{t})
, then

\|f\|_{\bar{E}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}\leqslant\|f\|_{\bar{E}_{m_{1},\dots,m_{t}}}\,.

(91)

P r o o f.

Take two functions
f,g:{\mathbf{G}}\to\mathbb{R}
.
In view of Lemma
89
, we have

\|f+g\|^{K}_{E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}=\langle f+g\rangle_{E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{k}\binom{K}{j}\|f\|^{j}_{E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}\|g\|^{K-j}_{E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}=(\|f\|_{E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}+\|g\|_{E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}})^{K}

and we have obtained the triangle inequality for
E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}
.
By estimate (
87
) we know that our quantity
\|f\|_{\bar{E}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}
is non–negative. Also, Lemma
22
guaranties that
\|f\|_{E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}=0
iff
f\equiv 0
. Thus indeed formula (
85
) defines a norm of
f
.

It remains to obtain (
91
).
Let
M=\prod_{j=1}^{t}m_{j}
,
B^{\prime}=[m_{1}]\times\dots\times[m_{t}]
,
S=\sum_{j=1}^{r}k_{j}
and
S^{\prime}=\sum_{j=1}^{t}m_{j}
.
Consider the family of functions
(g^{\omega})_{\omega\in B^{\prime}}
such that for
\omega\in B
one has
g^{\omega}=f^{\omega}
and let
g^{\omega}\equiv 1
otherwise.
It is easy to see that

\|f\|^{K}_{\bar{E}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}N^{S}=\|f\|^{K}_{E_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}=N^{S-S^{\prime}}\cdot\langle g^{\omega}\rangle_{E_{m_{1},\dots,m_{t}}}

Using the last formula, Definition (
90
), as well as Lemma
89
, we obtain

\|f\|^{K}_{\bar{E}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}}}N^{S}\leqslant N^{S-S^{\prime}}\|f\|^{K}_{E_{m_{1},\dots,m_{t}}}(N^{S^{\prime}/M})^{M-K}=N^{S-S^{\prime}K/M}\cdot N^{S^{\prime}K/M}\|f\|^{K}_{\bar{E}_{m_{1},\dots,m_{t}}}

=N^{S}\|f\|^{K}_{\bar{E}_{m_{1},\dots,m_{t}}}

as required.

\hfill\Box

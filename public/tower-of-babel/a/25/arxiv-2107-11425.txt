On Reflection Representations of Coxeter Groups over Non-Commutative Rings II
Annette Pilkington

Abstract
. This paper generalizes an isomorphism between certain quotients of path algebras and matrix rings over free products, used in the first paper in this sequence. Applications of the isomorphism to certain path algebras associated to Coxeter groups and to Cohn path algebras are given.



1.
Introduction

Representations of a Coxeter system,
(W,S)
, over a number of quotients of a path algebra related to the Coxeter graph,
\Gamma_{W,S}
, of the system are studied in
Dyer
[
17
]
. In Pilkington
[
27
]
, we studied one of these quotient algebras,
R_{W,S}
, in detail. We showed that in the case of a Coxeter group of infinite rank,
R_{W,S}
is the direct limit of the corresponding algebras defined
on the finite rank parabolic subgroups.
In that paper, we answered in the affirmative a question posed in Dyer
[
17
]
about its multiplicative properties.
In the course of that proof, we used an imbedding of the quotient algebra,
R_{W,S}
, for a Coxeter system of finite rank
N
, into the ring of
(N+1)\times(N+1)

matrices over a free product of rings, related to the edges of the Coxeter graph. These rings are either field extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
, rings of Laurent polynomials over
\mathbb{Q}
or rings of polynomials in two non-commuting variables over
\mathbb{Q}
.

The above imbedding uses a special case of an isomorphism between
the path algebra
\hat{R}_{W,S}=R_{W,S}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
and the
N\times N
matrices over such a free product of rings,
where
N
is the rank of the system. This imbedding exists for graphs
\Gamma_{W,S}
with a spanning tree where the edges of the spanning tree are associated to finite entries in the Coxeter Matrix. This applies in the case of the Weyl groups and many commonly studied examples. In this paper, we present this more general isomorphism. Since the imbedding is applicable to a wider range of path algebras, we pull out the general principles and demonstrate applications to both Coxeter systems and Cohn path algebras (see Abrahms et al.
[
1
]
).

Let
(W,S=\{s_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq N})
be a Coxeter system of finite rank with Coxeter matrix
(m_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq N}
. The Coxeter graph
\Gamma_{W,S}=(S,Y)
has vertices
S
and edges
Y=\{y_{ij}=[s_{i}s_{j}],1\leq i\leq N,3\leq m_{ij}\leq\infty\}
.
The associated path algebra
\mathbb{Q}\Gamma_{W,S}
and its quotient
\hat{R}_{W,S}
are fully described in Section
5
of this paper.
Let
K_{n}
denote the field extension of
\mathbb{Q}
obtained by adjoining
4\cos^{2}\frac{\pi}{n}
. Let
\mathbb{Q}[x_{ij},x_{ij}^{-1}]
be
the ring of Laurent polynomials over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathbb{Q}[x_{ij},\bar{x}_{ij}]
be the polynomial ring in the non-commuting variables
x_{ij}
and
\bar{x}_{ij}
. We show the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(W,S)
be a finite rank Coxeter system, with Coxeter graph
\Gamma_{W,S}
as described above.
Let
Y_{1}=\{y_{ij}\in Y|3\leq m_{ij}<\infty\}
.
Let
T
be a spanning tree for
\Gamma_{W,S}
, whose edges are denoted by
Y^{T}
, with the property that
Y^{T}\subseteq Y_{1}
. Then we have a unital ring isomorphism

\hat{R}_{W,S}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}\equiv M_{N}(Q),

where
Q
is isomorphic to the free product of the following rings;

\begin{matrix}K_{m_{ij}},&3\leq m_{ij}<\infty,&i<j,\\
\mathbb{Q}[y_{ij},y_{ij}^{-1}],&y_{ij}\in Y\backslash Y_{T},&3\leq m_{ij}<\infty,i<j\\
\mathbb{Q}[x_{ij},\bar{x}_{ij}],&y_{ij}\in Y\backslash Y_{T},&m_{ij}=\infty,i<j\\
\end{matrix}

To demonstrate the wider applicability of the imbedding described above,
we apply the imbedding to the Cohn path algebras.
In
[
2
]
, Abrahms and Kanuni show that a Cohn path algebra has the IBN property.
We give an alternative proof of this, in fact we show that a Cohn path algebra has a stronger property,
namely the UGN property or in more modern terms; it satisfies the rank condition.
In section
6
we show:

Theorem 1.2
.

A Cohn path algebra has the UGN property.

Using the above notation, we have the following:

Lemma 3.2

Let

be a commutative ring and
\Gamma=(S,Y)
a graph.
Let

be a set of polynomials in

For each

, there exists

\widetilde{yy^{\vee}}=\widetilde{[o(y)]}

\widetilde{y^{\vee}y}=\widetilde{[t(y)]}

Proof.

Since

is non-constant and has a non-zero invertible constant term, it is of the form

, for some

I claim that

satisfies the equalities given in the statement of the lemma.

we see that

Therefore

\alpha(y_{n})\alpha(y^{\prime}_{1})\equiv[o(y_{n})]\mod I_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}
and

P_{s_{i}}^{s_{j}}P_{s_{j}}^{s_{l}}\equiv\alpha(y_{1})\alpha(y_{2})\dots\alpha(y_{n-1})\alpha(y^{\prime}_{2})\dots\alpha(y^{\prime}_{m})\mod I_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}.

Since
y_{1}y_{2}\dots y_{n-1}
and
y^{\prime}_{2}y^{\prime}_{3}\dots y^{\prime}_{m}
have no backtracking and their edges are in
T
, they must be equal to

G_{s_{i}}^{s_{t}}
and
G_{s_{t}}^{s_{l}}
respectively, where
s_{t}=t(y_{n-1})=o(y^{\prime}_{2})
. Now
\ell(G_{s_{i}}^{s_{t}})+\ell(G_{s_{t}}^{s_{l}})=n+m-2<n+m
,
therefore, by induction,
P_{s_{i}}^{s_{j}}P_{s_{j}}^{s_{l}}\equiv P_{s_{i}}^{s_{t}}P_{s_{t}}^{s_{l}}\equiv P_{s_{i}}^{s_{l}}\mod I_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}
.
∎

4.3.

Using the orientation
Y=Y_{+}\bigcupdot\overline{Y_{+}}
defined above, we can define an algebra
Q
associated to our graph
\Gamma
and set of polynomials
\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}=\{f_{y}|y\in Y_{1}\}
. We let

Q=K[\{t_{y}\}_{y\in Y^{T}_{+}},\ \{x_{y},\bar{x}_{y}\}_{y\in B_{+}}]/J,

where
Q_{1}=K[\{t_{y}\}_{y\in Y^{T}_{+}},\ \{x_{y},\bar{x}_{y}\}_{y\in B_{+}}]

denotes the free
K
-algebra generated by the non-commuting variables
\{\{t_{y}\}_{y\in Y^{T}_{+}},\ \{x_{y},\bar{x}_{y}\}_{y\in B_{+}}\}
and
J
is the ideal of
Q_{1}
generated by the polynomials

\left\{\{f_{y}(t_{y})\}_{y\in Y^{T}_{+}},\{f_{y}(x_{y}\bar{x}_{y}),f_{y}(\bar{x}_{y}x_{y})\}_{y\in B_{+}\cap Y_{1}}\right\}.

4.4.

Since
Q_{1}
is a free
K
-algebra on the generators
\{\{t_{y}\}_{y\in Y^{T}_{+}},\ \{x_{y},\bar{x}_{y}\}_{y\in B_{+}}\}
,
for each
i,1\leq i\leq N
, there is a unique homomorphism of
K
-algebras,

\hat{\psi}_{i}:Q_{1}\to[s_{i}]K\Gamma[s_{i}]\subset K\Gamma
, such that

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}_{i}(1_{Q_{1}})

\displaystyle=[s_{i}],

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}_{i}(t_{y})

\displaystyle={P}_{s_{i}}^{o(y)}y\bar{y}{P}_{o(y)}^{s_{i}},

\displaystyle y\in Y^{T}_{+},

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}_{i}(x_{y})

\displaystyle={P}_{s_{i}}^{o(y)}y{P}_{t(y)}^{s_{i}},

\displaystyle y\in B^{+},

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}_{i}(\bar{x}_{y})

\displaystyle={P}_{s_{i}}^{t(y)}\bar{y}{P}_{o(y)}^{s_{i}},

\displaystyle y\in B^{+}.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\hat{\psi}_{i}:Q_{1}\to K\Gamma
be as described above. Then
\hat{\psi}_{i}(J)\subseteq I_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}
and

\hat{\psi}_{i}
gives a well defined (non-unital)
K
-algebra homomorphism from
\psi_{i}:Q\to R_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\hat{\psi}_{i}
sends the generators of
J
to
I_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}
. Let
y\in Y^{T}_{+}
, then

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}_{i}(f_{y}(t_{y}))

\displaystyle=[s_{i}]f_{y}(\hat{\psi}_{i}(t_{y}))[s_{i}]

\displaystyle=[s_{i}]f_{y}(P_{s_{i}}^{o(y)}(y\bar{y})P_{o(y)}^{s_{i}})[s_{i}]

\displaystyle=P_{s_{i}}^{o(y)}f_{y}(y\bar{y})P_{o(y)}^{s_{i}}\in I_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}.

For
y\in B_{+}
, note that

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}_{i}(x_{y}\bar{x}_{{y}})=\hat{\psi}_{i}(x_{y})\hat{\psi}_{i}(\bar{x}_{{y}})

\displaystyle=P_{s_{i}}^{o(y)}yP_{t(y)}^{s_{i}}P_{s_{i}}^{t(y)}\bar{y}P_{o(y)}^{s_{i}}

\displaystyle\equiv P_{s_{i}}^{o(y)}y[t(y)]\bar{y}P_{o(y)}^{s_{i}}\mod I_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}

\displaystyle\equiv P_{s_{i}}^{o(y)}y\bar{y}P_{o(y)}^{s_{i}}\mod I_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}_{i}(f_{y}(x_{y}\bar{x}_{y}))

\displaystyle=[s_{i}]f_{y}(\hat{\psi}_{i}(x_{y}\bar{x}_{y}))[s_{i}]

\displaystyle=[s_{i}]f_{y}(P_{s_{i}}^{o(y)}(y\bar{y})P_{o(y)}^{s_{i}})[s_{i}]

\displaystyle\equiv P_{s_{i}}^{o(y)}f_{y}(y\bar{y})P_{o(y)}^{s_{i}}\mod I_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}.

Since
f_{y}(y\bar{y})\in I_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}
, we have
\hat{\psi}_{i}(f_{y}(x_{y}\bar{x}_{y}))\in I_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}
.
Similarly

\hat{\psi}_{i}(\bar{x}_{{y}}x_{y})\equiv P_{s_{i}}^{t(y)}\bar{y}yP_{t(y)}^{s_{i}}\mod I_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}

and

\hat{\psi}_{i}(f_{y}(\bar{x}_{y}x_{y}))\equiv P_{s_{i}}^{t(y)}f_{y}(\bar{y}y)P_{t(y)}^{s_{i}}\mod I_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}},

giving
\hat{\psi}_{i}(f_{y}(\bar{x}_{y}x_{y}))\in I_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}
.
This proves the lemma.
∎

Thus, for each
i
,
1\leq i\leq N
, we have a well defined homomorphism of the quotient rings
\psi_{i}:Q\to R_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}
, given by

\psi_{i}(q+J)=\hat{\psi}(q)+I_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}},\ q\in Q_{1}.

Lemma 4.3
.

For
1\leq i,j\leq N
and
q\in Q
, we have

\psi_{j}(q)=\widetilde{P_{s_{j}}^{s_{i}}}\psi_{i}(q)\widetilde{P_{s_{i}}^{s_{j}}}.

Proof.

It is enough to show that this is true on the generators of
Q
.
Using Lemma
4.1
and the definition of
\psi_{i}
, it is easy to check that for each

g\in\{\{t_{y}\}_{y\in Y^{T}_{+}},\ \{x_{y},\bar{x}_{y}\}_{y\in B_{+}}\},

we have
\widetilde{P_{s_{j}}^{s_{i}}}\psi_{i}(g)\widetilde{P_{s_{i}}^{s_{j}}}=\psi_{j}(g)
. The details are left to the reader.
∎

4.5.

Let
\Psi:M_{N}(Q)\to R_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}
be the homomorphism of
K
vector spaces from the matrix ring
M_{N}(Q)
to
R_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}
which acts
on basis elements of
M_{N}(Q)
of the form
qe_{ij}
as follows:

\Psi(qe_{ij})=\psi_{i}(q)\widetilde{P_{s_{i}}^{s_{j}}},q\in Q,

where
e_{ij}
is the
N\times N
matrix with
1
in the
(i,j)
position and zeros elsewhere.

Lemma 4.4
.

The map
\Psi:M_{N}(Q)\to R_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}
defined above is a
homomorphism of rings.

Proof.

It suffices to check that

\Psi\left((q_{1}e_{ij})(q_{2}e_{kl})\right)=\Psi(q_{1}e_{ij})\Psi(q_{2}e_{kl})
,
q_{1},q_{2}\in Q,1\leq i,j,k,l\leq N
.

If
j\not=k
,
\Psi\left((q_{1}e_{ij})(q_{2}e_{kl})\right)=\Psi(O)=0
, where
O
denotes the
N\times N
zero matrix. On the other hand,

\Psi(q_{1}e_{ij})\Psi(q_{2}e_{kl})=\psi_{i}(q_{1})\widetilde{P_{s_{i}}^{s_{j}}}\psi_{k}(q_{2})\widetilde{P_{s_{k}}^{s_{l}}}=0,

since
\psi_{k}(q_{2})\in\widetilde{[s_{k}]}R_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}\widetilde{[s_{k}]}
.

If
j=k
, we have

\displaystyle\Psi\left((q_{1}e_{ij})(q_{2}e_{jl})\right)

\displaystyle=\Psi\left((q_{1}q_{2}e_{il})\right)

\displaystyle=\psi_{i}(q_{1}q_{2})\widetilde{P_{s_{i}}^{s_{l}}}

\displaystyle=\psi_{i}(q_{1})\psi_{i}(q_{2})\widetilde{P_{s_{i}}^{s_{l}}}

\displaystyle=\psi_{i}(q_{1})\widetilde{P_{s_{i}}^{s_{j}}}\widetilde{P_{s_{j}}^{s_{i}}}\psi_{i}(q_{2})\widetilde{P_{s_{i}}^{s_{j}}}\widetilde{P_{s_{j}}^{s_{i}}}\widetilde{P_{s_{i}}^{s_{l}}}

\displaystyle=\psi_{i}(q_{1})\widetilde{P_{s_{i}}^{s_{j}}}\psi_{j}(q_{2})\widetilde{P_{s_{j}}^{s_{l}}},

by lemmas
4.1
and
4.3
. Thus
\Psi\left((q_{1}e_{ij})(q_{2}e_{jl})\right)=\Psi(q_{1}e_{ij})\Psi(q_{2}e_{jl})
.

∎

To show that
\Psi
is an isomorphism, we construct an inverse homomorphism

\Phi:R_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}\to M_{N}(Q)
. Let
\phi:K\Gamma\to M_{N}(Q)
be defined as follows on the
vertices and edges of
\Gamma
(which generate
K\Gamma
as a
K
-algebra):

\displaystyle\phi([s_{i}])

\displaystyle=e_{ii},s_{i}\in S

\displaystyle\phi(y)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}e_{o^{\prime}(y)t^{\prime}(y)},&y\in Y^{T}_{+}\\
t_{\bar{y}}e_{o^{\prime}(y)t^{\prime}(y)},&y\in\overline{Y}^{T}_{+}\\
x_{y}e_{o^{\prime}(y)t^{\prime}(y)},&y\in B_{+}\\
\bar{x}_{\bar{y}}e_{o^{\prime}(y)t^{\prime}(y)},&y\in\overline{B}_{+}\\
\end{cases}

Since
e_{ij}e_{kl}\not=0
if and only if
j=k
, It is not difficult to see that
\phi
can be extended to a well defined homomorphism
of
K
-algebras:

\phi:K\Gamma\to M_{N}(Q).

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\phi:K\Gamma\to M_{N}(Q)
be the
K
-algebra homomorphism defined above, then

I_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}\subseteq\ker\phi
.

Proof.

It is enough to show that the generators of
I_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}
,
\{f_{y}(y\bar{y}),y\in Y_{1}\}
,
are in
\ker\phi
. If
y\in Y^{T}\subseteq Y_{1}
, then
\phi(y\bar{y})=\phi(y)\phi(\bar{y})
is either
t_{y}e_{o^{\prime}(y)o^{\prime}(y)}
or

t_{\bar{y}}e_{o^{\prime}(y)o^{\prime}(y)}
. Thus
\phi\left(f_{y}(y\bar{y})\right)=f_{y}(t_{y})e_{o^{\prime}(y)o^{\prime}(y)}=O
or
\phi\left(f_{y}(y\bar{y})\right)=f_{y}(t_{\bar{y}})e_{o^{\prime}(y)o^{\prime}(y)}=f_{\bar{y}}(t_{\bar{y}})e_{o^{\prime}(y)o^{\prime}(y)}=O

where
O
is the zero matrix in
M_{N}(Q)
.

If
y\in B_{+}\cap Y_{1}
, then

\phi(y\bar{y})=x_{y}e_{o^{\prime}(y)t^{\prime}(y)}\bar{x}_{y}e_{t^{\prime}(y)o^{\prime}(y)}=x_{y}\bar{x}_{y}e_{o^{\prime}(y)o^{\prime}(y)}.

Thus, as before,
\phi\left(f_{y}(y\bar{y})\right)=f_{y}(x_{y}\bar{x}_{y})e_{o^{\prime}(y)o^{\prime}(y)}=O

where
O
is the zero matrix in
M_{N}(Q)
.

For
y\in\overline{B}_{+}\cap Y_{1},
we have

\displaystyle\phi(y\bar{y})

\displaystyle=\bar{x}_{\bar{y}}x_{\bar{y}}e_{o^{\prime}(y)t^{\prime}(y)}e_{t^{\prime}(y)o^{\prime}(y)}

\displaystyle=\bar{x}_{\bar{y}}x_{\bar{y}y}e_{o^{\prime}(y)o^{\prime}(y)}.

Thus
\phi\left(f_{y}(y\bar{y})\right)=f_{y}(x_{y}\bar{x}_{y})e_{o^{\prime}(y)o^{\prime}(y)}=O
,
where
O
is the zero matrix in
M_{N}(Q)
.

∎

By the above lemma, the quotient map

\Phi:R_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}\to M_{N}(Q)

is a well defined ring homomorphism, with
\Phi(\tilde{p})=\phi(p)
, for
p\in K\Gamma
.

We now show that
\Phi=\Psi^{-1}
and thus we have that
R_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}
is isomorphic to
M_{N}(Q)
as a
K
-algebra.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\phi:K\Gamma\to M_{N}(Q)
be as defined above and let
y\in Y^{T}_{+}
. Then

\phi(y^{\vee})=e_{t^{\prime}(y)o^{\prime}(y)}
and
\phi(P_{s_{k}}^{s_{l}})=e_{kl},1\leq k,l\leq N

Proof.

If
y\in Y^{T}_{+}
, then (as pointed out in
the proof of Lemma
3.2
),
f_{y}(t)=th(t)-\kappa
for some
\kappa\not=0\in K
and
h(t)\in K[t]
.
We had
y^{\vee}=\frac{1}{\kappa}\bar{y}h(y\bar{y})
.
Thus,

\phi(y^{\vee})=\frac{1}{\kappa}t_{y}h(t_{y})e_{t^{\prime}(y)o^{\prime}(y)}=\frac{1}{\kappa}\left(f_{y}(t_{y})+\kappa\right)e_{t^{\prime}(y)o^{\prime}(y)}=1_{Q}e_{t^{\prime}(y)o^{\prime}(y)}=e_{t^{\prime}(y)o^{\prime}(y)}.

Recall that for
1\leq i,j\leq N
,
P_{s_{i}}^{s_{j}}=\alpha(y_{1})\alpha(y_{2})\dots\alpha(y_{n})
, where
y_{1}y_{2}\dots y_{n}
is the geodesic in
T
from
s_{i}
to
s_{j}
.
Now
\alpha(y_{k})=y_{k}
, if
y_{k}\in Y_{+}^{T}
and
\alpha(y_{k})=\bar{y}_{k}^{\vee}
if
y_{k}\in\overline{Y}_{+}^{T}
. Thus since
\phi(y)=e_{o^{\prime}(y)t^{\prime}(y)}
and
\phi({y}^{\vee})=e_{t^{\prime}(y)o^{\prime}(y)}
for
y\in Y_{+}^{T}
, the result follows.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\Phi:R_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}\to M_{N}(Q)
and
\Psi:M_{N}(Q)\to R_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}
be as defined above. Then, the composition
\Phi\circ\Psi
is the identity map on
M_{N}(Q)
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\Phi\circ\Psi
is the identity on the following set of generators
of
M_{N}(Q)
:

\left\{e_{ij}\right\}_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\bigcup\left\{t_{y}e_{ij}\right\}_{y\in Y^{T}_{+}}\bigcup\left\{x_{y}e_{ij},\ \bar{x}_{y}e_{ij}\right\}_{y\in B_{+}}.

We have
\Phi(\Psi(e_{ij}))=\Phi\left(\psi_{i}(1_{Q})\widetilde{P_{s_{i}}^{s_{j}}}\right)=\Phi(\widetilde{[s_{i}]})e_{ij}=e_{ii}e_{ij}=e_{ij}
for
1\leq i,j,\leq N
.

Next consider
t_{y}e_{ij}
where
y\in Y^{T}_{+}

and
1\leq i,j\leq N
. In this case, we have

\displaystyle\Phi(\Psi(t_{y}e_{ij}))

\displaystyle=\Phi\left(\psi_{i}(t_{y})\widetilde{P_{s_{i}}^{s_{j}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\Phi\left(\widetilde{P_{s_{i}}^{o(y)}}\widetilde{y\bar{y}}\widetilde{P_{o(y)}^{s_{i}}}\widetilde{P_{s_{i}}^{s_{j}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\Phi(\widetilde{P_{s_{i}}^{o(y)}}\widetilde{y\bar{y}}\widetilde{P_{o(y)}^{s_{j}}})

\displaystyle=e_{io^{\prime}(y)}\Phi(\widetilde{y\bar{y}})e_{o^{\prime}(y)j}

\displaystyle=e_{io^{\prime}(y)}e_{o^{\prime}(y)t^{\prime}(y)}t_{y}e_{t^{\prime}(y)o^{\prime}(y)}e_{o^{\prime}(y)j}

\displaystyle=t_{y}e_{ij}.

If
y\in B_{+}
, then

\displaystyle\Phi(\Psi(x_{y}e_{ij}))

\displaystyle=\Phi\left(\psi_{i}(x_{y})\widetilde{P_{s_{i}}^{s_{j}}}\right)=\Phi\left(\widetilde{P_{s_{i}}^{o(y)}}\tilde{y}\widetilde{P_{t(y)}^{s_{i}}}\widetilde{P_{s_{i}}^{s_{j}}}\right)=\Phi\left(\widetilde{P_{s_{i}}^{o(y)}}\tilde{y}\widetilde{P_{t(y)}^{s_{j}}}\right)

\displaystyle=e_{io^{\prime}(y)}\Phi(\tilde{y})e_{t^{\prime}(y)j}=e_{io^{\prime}(y)}x_{y}e_{o^{\prime}(y)t^{\prime}(y)}e_{t^{\prime}(y)j}=x_{y}e_{ij}.

Finally, if
y\in B_{+}
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi(\Psi(\bar{x}_{y}e_{ij}))

\displaystyle=\Phi\left(\psi_{i}(\bar{x}_{y})\widetilde{P_{s_{i}}^{s_{j}}}\right)=\Phi\left(\widetilde{P_{s_{i}}^{t(y)}}\tilde{\bar{y}}\widetilde{P_{o(y)}^{s_{i}}}\widetilde{P_{s_{i}}^{s_{j}}}\right)=\Phi\left(\widetilde{P_{s_{i}}^{t(y)}}\tilde{\bar{y}}\widetilde{P_{o(y)}^{s_{j}}}\right)

\displaystyle=e_{it^{\prime}(y)}\Phi(\tilde{\bar{y}})e_{o^{\prime}(y)j}=e_{it^{\prime}(y)}\bar{x}_{y}e_{t^{\prime}(y)o^{\prime}(y)}e_{o^{\prime}(y)j}=\bar{x}_{y}e_{ij}

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\Phi:R_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}\to M_{N}(Q)
and
\Psi:M_{N}(Q)\to R_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}
be as defined above. Then, then composition
\Psi\circ\Phi
is the identity map on
R_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}
.

Proof.

It is enough to demonstrate that this is true on the generators of the algebra
R_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}
,
\{\widetilde{[s_{i}]}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
and

\{\tilde{y}\}_{y\in Y}
.
First, we look at the paths of length
0
. Let
s_{i}\in S
, then

\Psi\left(\Phi(\widetilde{[s_{i}]})\right)=\Psi(e_{ii})=\psi_{i}(1_{Q})\widetilde{P_{s_{i}}^{s_{i}}}=\widetilde{[s_{i}]}\widetilde{[s_{i}]}=\widetilde{[s_{i}]}.

For
y\in Y_{+}^{T}
,
\Psi\left(\Phi(\tilde{y})\right)=\Psi(e_{o^{\prime}(y)t^{\prime}(y)})=\psi_{o^{\prime}(y)}(1_{Q})\widetilde{P_{o(y)}^{t(y)}}=\widetilde{[s_{o^{\prime}(y)}]}\tilde{y}=\tilde{y}.

When
y\in\overline{Y_{+}^{T}}
, we have

\Psi\left(\Phi(\tilde{y})\right)=\Psi\left(t_{\bar{y}}e_{o^{\prime}(y)t^{\prime}(y)}\right)=\psi_{o^{\prime}(y)}(t_{\bar{y}})\widetilde{P_{o(y)}^{t(y)}}=\widetilde{P_{o(y)}^{t(y)}}\tilde{\bar{y}}\tilde{y}\widetilde{P_{t(y)}^{o(y)}}\widetilde{P_{o(y)}^{t(y)}}=\widetilde{P_{o(y)}^{t(y)}}\tilde{\bar{y}}\tilde{y}=\widetilde{{\bar{y}}}^{\vee}\tilde{\bar{y}}\tilde{y}=\tilde{y},

by Lemma
3.2
and the definition of
P_{s_{i}}^{s_{j}}
.

Next we consider the case when
y\in B_{+}
. Here we have

\Psi\left(\Phi(\tilde{y})\right)=\Psi(x_{y}e_{o^{\prime}(y)t^{\prime}(y)})=\psi_{o^{\prime}(y)}(x_{y})\widetilde{P_{o(y)}^{t(y)}}=\widetilde{P_{o(y)}^{o(y)}}\tilde{y}\widetilde{P_{t(y)}^{o(y)}}\widetilde{P_{o(y)}^{t(y)}}=\widetilde{[o(y)]}\tilde{y}\widetilde{[t(y)]}=\tilde{y}.

Finally, we let
y\in\overline{B}_{+}
. In this case we get

\displaystyle\Psi\left(\Phi(\tilde{y})\right)

\displaystyle=\Psi\left(\bar{x}_{\bar{y}}e_{o^{\prime}(y)t^{\prime}(y)}\right)

\displaystyle=\psi_{o^{\prime}(y)}\left(\bar{x}_{\bar{y}}\right)\widetilde{P_{o(y)}^{t(y)}}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{P_{o(y)}^{o(y)}}\tilde{\bar{\bar{y}}}\widetilde{P_{t(y)}^{o(y)}}\widetilde{P_{o(y)}^{t(y)}}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{[o(y)]}\tilde{y}\widetilde{[t(y)]}=\tilde{y}.

This concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Definition 4.9
.

(Cohn
[
8
]
, Section 3)
let
K
is a field. The
K
-algebra
R
is said to be the free product of the
K
-algebras
\{R_{i}\}_{i\in I}
(over
K
) if

\{R_{i}\}_{i\in I}
forms a family of
R

such that

(i)

R_{i}\cap R_{j}=K
for
i\not=j
,

(ii)

if
X_{i}
is a set of generators of
R_{i}
,
i\in I
, then
\bigcup_{I}X_{i}
is a set of generators of
R
,

(iii)

If
C_{i}
is a set of defining relations of
R_{i},i\in I
(in terms of the set of generators
X_{i}
) then
\bigcup_{I}C_{i}
is a set of defining relations of
R
(in terms of the set of generators
\bigcup_{I}X_{i}
).

We summarize the results of the above discussion in the following Theorem:

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
K
be a field and let
\Gamma=(S,Y)
be a graph with a finite number of vertices and edges.
Let
\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}=\{f_{y}(t)\}_{y\in Y_{1}\subseteq Y}
be a set of polynomials in
K[t]
with property F.
If
\Gamma
has a spanning tree

with edges

, then we can define an orientation,
Y=Y^{+}\bigcupdot\overline{Y^{+}}

on
\Gamma
, such that
R_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}
is isomorphic to the matrix ring
M_{N}(Q)
, where
N
is the cardinality of
S
and

\begin{cases}K[t]/f_{y}(t)&y\in Y_{T}\cap Y^{+}\\
K[x_{y},\overline{x_{y}}]/\langle f_{y}(x_{y}\overline{x_{y}}),f_{y}(\overline{x_{y}}x_{y})\rangle&y\in(Y_{1}\cap Y^{+})\backslash(Y_{T}\cap Y^{+})\\
K[x_{y},\overline{x_{y}}]&y\in Y^{+}\backslash(Y_{1}\cap Y^{+})\\
\end{cases}

The proof of the following lemma is identical to the proof of Theorem 6.6 in Pilkington
[
27
]
, where a proof is given for the field

:

Lemma 4.11
.

let
K
be a field and let

Let

\overline{x}

K[z,z^{-1}]\ast_{K}K^{f},

where

is the ring extension

.

Lemma
4.11
and Theorem
4.10
give us the following:

Corollary 4.12
.

Let
K
be a field and let
\Gamma=(S,Y)
be an undirected graph with a finite number of vertices and edges.
Let
\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}=\{f_{y}(t)\}_{y\in Y_{1}\subseteq Y}

If
\Gamma

\begin{cases}K[t]/f_{y}(t)&y\in Y_{1}\cap Y^{+}\\

Note that Corollary
4.12

6.
Cohn Path Algebras

In this section, we give an application of Theorem
4.10
by giving a proof of the fact that Cohn path algebras.
satisfy the rank condition. This implies that they have invariant basis number, which has already been proven
in Abrahms and Kanuni
[
2
]
. Cohn path algebras are normally defined on directed graphs,
we adapt the definition to the situation where we have an oriented graph.
Let
\Gamma=(S,Y)
be a connected graph with a finite vertex set
S
and a finite set of edges
Y
.
Let
Y^{+}\bigcup\overline{Y^{+}}
be an orientation on
\Gamma
.

Definition 6.1
.

The Cohn Path algebra
C(\Gamma)
(relative to the orientation
Y=Y^{+}\bigcup\overline{Y^{+}}
over a field
K
is defined as the quotient of the path algebra
K\Gamma
by the ideal generated by the set
\{\bar{y}y-[t(y)]|y\in Y^{+}\}
.

It can be shown that for each such Cohn path algebra of a graph
\Gamma
, there exists a graph
\Gamma^{\prime}
such that
the Leavitt Path Algebra of
\Gamma^{\prime}
is
C(\Gamma)
, see Abrahms et al.
[
1
]
.

Consider the set of polynomials
\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}=\{f_{y}(t)=t^{2}-1|y\in Y\}\subset K[t]
. It is not difficult to see that
\mathfrak{F}
has property
F
.
We see also that
R_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}
is the quotient of
C(\Gamma)
by the ideal generated by
\{\bar{y}y-[t(y)]|y\in\overline{Y^{+}}\}
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\Gamma=(S,Y)
be a finite graph with an orientation
Y=Y^{+}\bigcup\overline{Y^{+}}
. Let
K
be a field and
let
\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}=\{f_{y}(t)=t^{2}-1|y\in Y\}\subset K[t]
. Then

R_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}
is isomorphic to
M_{N}(Q)
where
Q
is a free product of Laurent polynomial rings over
K
, and
N
is the cardinality of
S
.

Proof.

Since any maximal spanning tree of
\Gamma
must have edges in
Y_{1}=Y
, we can apply Theorem
4.10
and Corollary
4.12
for any such tree

T=(S,Y^{T})
.
Thus we have that
R_{\mathfrak{F}_{\Gamma}}
is isomorphic to
M_{N}(Q)
where
N
is the cardinality of
S
and
Q
is a free product over
K
of
the following rings:

\begin{matrix}K_{y}=K[t]/\langle t-1\rangle,&y\in Y\cap Y^{+}=Y^{+}\\
K[x_{y},x_{y}^{-1}],&y\in Y^{+}\backslash(Y^{T}\cap Y^{+})\\
\end{matrix}

Since

,

.
∎

Below, we give definitions of three successively stronger ring conditions.
See Cohn
[
10
]
, section 0.2 for more details.

Definition 6.3
.

A ring
R
is said to have the invariant basis number (IBN) property if every free
R
module
has unique rank.

The following are Equivalent:

•

R
has the IBN property,

•

For all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
,
R^{n}\cong R^{m}
implies
m=n
,

•

if
A\in M_{n\times m}(R)
and
B\in M_{m\times n}(R)
with
AB=I_{n}
and
BA=I_{m}
, then
m=n
.

Definition 6.4
.

A ring
R
is said to have the unbounded generator number property (UGN) (or satisfies the rank condition) if for every

n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
there is a finitely generated left
R
module which cannot be generated by
n
elements.

The following are Equivalent:

•

R
has the UGN property,

•

If
R^{n}\cong R^{m}\oplus K
, for some
R
module
K
, we must have
n\geq m
,

•

For any
A\in M_{m\times n}(R),B\in M_{n\times m}(R)
, if
AB=I_{m}
, then
n\geq m
.

Definition 6.5
.

A ring
R
is said to be weakly
n
-finite if every generating set of
n
elements in
R^{n}
is free. If this holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
,
then
R
is said to be weakly finite (WF) (also called stably finite).

The following are Equivalent:

•

R
is weakly finite,

•

If
R^{n}\cong R^{n}\oplus K
, for some
R
module
K
, we must have
K=0
,

•

For any
A,B\in M_{n\times n}(R)
, if
AB=I_{n}
, then
BA=I_{n}
.

Definition 6.6
.

Cohn
[
10
]
(Sections 1.1 and 1.2)
A free right ideal ring is an integral domain
R
with invariant basis number, such that all
right ideals of
R
are free
R-modules
. A free left ideal ring is defined similarly and a free ideal ring (fir) is a ring which is both a left and a right
free ideal ring. A ring
R
is a semifir if
R
has IBN and every finitely generated left ideal is free (equivalently every
finitely generated right ideal is free). Every fir is a semifir.

By Cohn
[
10
]
, Corollary 1.2,
If
R
is a semifir, then
R
is weakly finite.

Theorem 6.7
.

A Cohn path algebra has the UGN property, that is; it satisfies the rank condition.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma=(S,Y)
be a graph with a finite number of vertices and edges and
K
a field. Let
C(\Gamma)
be Cohn path algebra defined over
K
with respect to a given orientation on
\Gamma

From Theorem
6.2
and the observations made prior to it,
we have a surjective homomorphism

where

By Cohn
[
9
]
Corollary 3 to Theorem 4.3,

is a free ideal ring and hence is weakly finite.
Thus

is also weakly finite by the matrix characterization of the property.
By Proposition
0
.2.2 in Cohn
[
10
]
, if a ring

has a homomorphic image which is weakly finite, then it has the UGN property.
This concludes the proof.
∎

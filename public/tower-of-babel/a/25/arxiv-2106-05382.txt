The plectic conjecture over function fields
Siyan Daniel Li-Huerta

Abstract
. We prove the plectic conjecture of Nekovář–Scholl [ 21 ] over global function fields Q Q . For example, when the cocharacter is defined over Q Q and the structure group is a Weil restriction from a geometric degree d d separable extension F / Q F/Q , consider the complex computing ℓ \ell -adic intersection cohomology with compact support of the associated moduli space of shtukas over Q I Q_{I} . We endow this with the structure of a complex of ( Weil ⁡ ( F ) d ⋊ 𝔖 d ) I (\Weil(F)^{d}\rtimes{\mathfrak{S}}_{d})^{I} -modules, which extends its structure as a complex of Weil ⁡ ( Q ) I \Weil(Q)^{I} -modules constructed by Arinkin–Gaitsgory–Kazhdan–Raskin–Rozenblyum–Varshavsky. We show that the action of ( Weil ⁡ ( F ) d ⋊ 𝔖 d ) I (\Weil(F)^{d}\rtimes{\mathfrak{S}}_{d})^{I} commutes with the Hecke action, and we give a moduli-theoretic description of the action of Frobenius elements in Weil ⁡ ( F ) d × I \Weil(F)^{d\times I} .

Outline

In §
1
, we collect facts on the moduli space of
G
-bundles, as well as certain relative variants thereof. In §

, we introduce symmetrized versions of the Hecke stack and the Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian, and we also recall the Beauville-Laszlo theorem and the geometric Satake correspondence. In §

we assemble everything and prove Theorem A, Theorem B, and Theorem C. We conclude by elaborating on a moduli-theoretic interpretation of Theorem C.

4.
Partial Frobenii and derived categories

In positive characteristic algebraic geometry, forming fundamental groups rarely commutes with taking products—even over an algebraically closed field. One can remedy this by asking for additional structure: namely,
partial Frobenius morphisms
. We crucially need to carry out this strategy in the derived category, which forces us to use
\infty
-categorical structures.

First, we define partial Frobenii and describe their action on derived categories of
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}
-sheaves. Next, we explain a monodromy interpretation of partial Frobenii, which generalizes the relation between Weil sheaves and Weil groups. We then describe how partial Frobenii arise in the setting of symmetrized moduli spaces of shtukas. In the usual, unsymmetrized case, we state an anticipated result of Arinkin–Gaitsgory–Kazhdan–Raskin–Rozenblyum–Varshavsky showing that their relative cohomology complex satisfies a derived version of
Drinfeld’s lemma
. Finally, we use work of Xue to relate the action of Frobenius elements in the Weil group with that of partial Frobenii on our cohomology groups.

Theorem
.

Under the identifications

\displaystyle{\mathscr{H}}^{p}_{N,I,W_{\Omega,G}}|_{\overline{\eta_{I}}}\displaystyle\mathop{\rightarrow}^{\sim}{\mathscr{H}}^{p}_{M,d\times I,W_{\Omega,H}}|_{\overline{\beta_{d\times I}}}\displaystyle\mathop{\leftarrow}^{\sim}{\mathscr{H}}^{p}_{M,d\times I,W_{\Omega,H}}|_{\overline{\underline{y}}},

this automorphism corresponds to the action of
\gamma_{y_{h,i}}
in the
(h,i)
-th entry of
\Weil(V\smallsetminus M)^{d\times I}
.

Proof.

The proof of Theorem
5.8
constructs the action of
\Weil(V\smallsetminus M)^{d\times I}
on
{\mathscr{H}}^{p}_{N,I,W_{\Omega,G}}|_{\overline{\eta_{I}}}
via the isomorphism
{\mathscr{H}}^{p}_{N,I,W_{\Omega,G}}|_{\overline{\eta_{I}}}\displaystyle\mathop{\rightarrow}^{\sim}{\mathscr{H}}^{p}_{M,d\times I,W_{\Omega,H}}|_{\overline{\beta_{d\times I}}}
. So it suffices to see that this corresponds to the action of
\gamma_{y_{h,i}}
in the
(h,i)
-th entry of
\Weil(V\smallsetminus M)^{d\times I}
on
{\mathscr{H}}^{p}_{M,d\times I,W_{\Omega,H}}|_{\overline{\beta_{d\times I}}}
, which follows immediately from Proposition
4.12
.

5.14.

The case of general
r
is more complicated for the following reason. We use Theorem
2.12
.b) to identify
R{\mathfrak{p}}_{!}({\mathscr{F}}^{((h,i),P)}_{M,d\times I,W_{\Omega,H}})
with
R{\mathfrak{p}}_{!}({\mathscr{F}}^{(P,(h,i))}_{M,d\times I,W_{\Omega,H}})
, which is what enables us to iteratively compose
F_{(h,i)}
. This identification does not seem to arise from an explicit cohomological correspondence, so for general
r
and
\underline{x}
this impedes us from similarly describing the action of

\displaystyle F_{(h,i)}\circ\Frob_{(h,i)}^{*}(F_{(h,i)})\circ\dotsb\circ\Frob_{(h,i)}^{r-1,*}(F_{(h,i)})

on
{\mathscr{H}}^{p}_{M,d\times I,W_{\Omega,H}}|_{\overline{\underline{y}}}

x_{i}

y_{h,i}
will also be disjoint, so we can use
2.5
to explicitly identify
\Sht^{((h,i),P)}_{H,M,d\times I,\Omega}|_{\underline{y}}
with
\Sht^{(P,(h,i))}_{H,M,d\times I,\Omega}|_{\underline{y}}
via pulling back along

. Therefore we obtain a description of the action from Theorem
5.12
in terms of the special fiber of the moduli space of shtukas in this case.

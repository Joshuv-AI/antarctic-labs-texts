Information propagation in long-range quantum many-body systems Preprint: APS/123-QED
Marius Lemm, Carla Rubiliani, Israel Michael Sigal, Jingxuan Zhang

Abstract
We study general lattice bosons with long-range hopping and long-range interactions decaying as | x − y | − α |x-y|^{-\alpha} with α ∈ ( d + 2 , 2 ​ d + 1 ) \alpha\in(d+2,2d+1) . We find a linear light cone for the information propagation starting from suitable initial states. We apply these bounds to estimate the minimal time needed for quantum messaging, for the propagation of quantum correlations, and for quantum state control. The proofs are based on the ASTLO method (adiabatic spacetime localization observables). Our results pose previously unforeseen limitations on the applicability of fast-transfer and entanglement-generation protocols developed for breaking linear light cones in long-range and/or bosonic systems.



The finite speed of information propagation is an empirical fact of Nature. In relativistic theory, the existence of the light cone is a fundamental requirement with wide-ranging consequences. It is remarkable that similar effective “light” cones also constrain the non-relativistic quantum theory that governs condensed-matter physics. The existence of such an effective “light” cone was discovered by Lieb and Robinson (
Lieb and Robinson 1972
) 50 years ago in quantum spin systems.

In the earliy 2000s, starting with the work of Hastings (
Hastings 2004a
) on the Lieb-Schultz-Mattis theorem, several works leveraged Lieb-Robinson bounds to great effect to control ground state correlation properties and study topological quantum phase transitions

;

;
Hastings 2007a
;
Hastings 2007b
;
Hastings and Wen 2005
;
Nachtergaele et al. 2006
;
Nachtergaele and Sims 2006
. These important developments revolutionized our understanding of the information content of quantum matter at zero temperature.
Since then, inspired by these works, an active research area dealing with dynamics of quantum information has sprung to life. A variety of improvements and extensions of the original LRB have been found, e.g., to long-range spin interactions
Foss-Feig et al. 2015
;
Tran et al. 2021a
;
Tran et al. 2021b
;
Tran et al. 2020
, lattice fermions
Nachtergaele et al. 2018
;

, open quantum lattice systems

The present paper focuses on lattice bosons where it had been a long-standing problem to derive useful propagation bounds for general initial states because interactions are effectively unbounded. This problem has recently seen rapid progress

IV
Conclusions

We have presented the existence of a linear light cone for many-boson system with long-range hopping and long-range interactions. The results provide practical constraints on sending quantum information in such systems. Our results identify good classes of initial states in which even bosons with long-range hopping and long-range interactions cannot propagate information super-ballistically. Our results thus complement the existence of fast-transfer protocols for nearest-neighbor Bose-Hubbard Hamiltonians
Kuwahara et al. 2022
and quantum spin systems with long-range interactions
Kuwahara and Saito 2020
;
Kuwahara and Saito 2021b
;
Eldredge et al. 2017
;
Tran et al. 2020
;
Tran et al. 2021c
.

The results can be extended to time-dependent and few-body interactions, quantum spin systems and lattice fermions. Extensions to open systems of lattice bosons with applications to estimating the decoherence and thermalization times are also in reach using ideas from
Breteaux et al. 2022
;
Breteaux et al. 2023
.

In this Supplementary Material we prove Part (ii) of Theorem

The main idea is to take the effective choice of

Proof outline.

We adapt the proof method of
(

, Theorem 1.7)
to a new class of cutoff functions with compactly supported Fourier transforms that we introduce in Section

The key challenge is to derive a recursive monotonicity estimate (RME) which controls the time evolution of these observables relative to their initial expectation value. As in
Faupin et al. 2022a
, an important step in the proof is to separate the

•

Since we need to absorb an

•

The more challenging part of the RME is that we need a function class that is non-negative with non-negative derivatives and that, up to upper bounds with universal constants is preserved under differentiation. We are able to iterate the RME to enhance the dependency on the on the order of the Taylor expansion,
n

S6

The price we pay for the strong control on the derivatives of this class of cutoff functions compared to

The idea is to bound their derivatives by symmetrized terms, which we keep as they are, and non-symmetrized ones, which we will further Taylor expand. By iterating this procedure until all the terms are symmetrized we can prove the desired expansion. This lemma allows us to separate the x and y dependency and will be fundamental for controlling the time evolution of the ASTLOs.

Let us first define the following index sets for any integer
l\geq 1
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{l,m}:=\{\underline{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{l})\in\mathbb{N}^{l}\;\text{ such that }k_{i}\geq 1\text{ for all }i\in[1,l]\text{ and }k_{1}+\dots+k_{l}=m\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\underline{k}}:=\{\underline{j}:=(j_{1},\dots,j_{l})\in\mathbb{N}^{l}\text{ such that }0\leq j_{i}\leq k_{i}\text{ for all }i\in[1,l]\}\quad\text{for every }\underline{k}\in\mathcal{K}_{l,m}.

For a vector
\underline{j}\in\mathcal{J}_{\underline{k}}
we can also define the following quantity

\displaystyle J_{l}:=\sum_{i=1}^{l}j_{i}.

(S11)

Recall that
u_{k}:=\left(\check{\varphi}^{(k)}\right)^{2}
. We come to the following lemma.

Lemma S4

(Symmetrized Taylor Expansion)
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{F}
. For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist two positive constants
C
and
C_{f}
such that

\displaystyle|f

\displaystyle(x)-f(y)|

\displaystyle\leq|x-y|u(x)u(y)

\displaystyle\quad+C|x-y|\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}\sum_{m=l}^{n}4^{m-l}|x-y|^{m}\sum_{\underline{k}\in\mathcal{K}_{l,m}}\;\sum_{\underline{j}\in\mathcal{J}_{\underline{k}}}\frac{u_{J_{l}}(x)u_{J_{l}}(y)}{(k_{1}-1)!\dots(k_{l}-1)!}

\displaystyle\quad+C_{f}|x-y|^{n+1}4^{n}\Big(\frac{1}{n!}+\sum_{l=2}^{n-1}\left(\frac{e}{2}\right)^{l}\sum_{m=l-1}^{n}2^{m}\Big),

where the sums should be dropped for
n=1
.

Proof.

We start by writing the Taylor expansion

f(x)-f(y)=\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{n}\frac{(x-y)^{k_{1}}}{k_{1}!}f^{(k_{1})}(x)\;+\;\frac{(x-y)^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}f^{(n+1)}(a_{0_{1}}),

(S12)

where we wrote the remainder in the Lagrange form and
a_{0_{1}}
is some number between
x
and
y
.

The first term of the expansion can be rewritten as

(x-y)f^{\prime}(x)=(x-y)u(x)u(x)=(x-y)u(x)u(y)+(x-y)u(x)\big(u(x)-u(y)\big).

(S13)

If
n=1
, then the proof is concluded. If
n>1
we can Taylor expand the factor
u(x)-u(y)

\displaystyle(x-y)

\displaystyle u(x)\big(u(x)-u(y)\big)

\displaystyle=(x-y)u(x)\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{(x-y)^{j}}{j!}u^{(j)}\;+\;u(x)\frac{(x-y)^{n+1}}{n!}u^{(n)}(a_{1_{1}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}u(x)\frac{(x-y)^{(j+1)}}{j!}u^{(j)}\;+\;u(x)\frac{(x-y)^{n+1}}{n!}u^{(n)}(a_{1_{1}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{1}=2}^{n}u(x)\frac{(x-y)^{(k_{1})}}{(k_{1}-1)!}u^{(k_{1}-1)}\;+\;u(x)\frac{(x-y)^{n+1}}{n!}u^{(n)}(a_{1_{1}}),

again for some number
a_{1_{1}}
between
x
and
y
.

So we obtain

\displaystyle f(x)-f(y)

\displaystyle=(x-y)u(x)u(y)+\sum_{k_{1}=2}^{n}(x-y)^{k}\Big(\frac{f^{(k_{1})}(x)}{k_{1}!}\;+\;u(x)\frac{u^{(k_{1}-1)}(x)}{(k_{1}-1)!}\Big)\;

\displaystyle+\;(x-y)^{n+1}\Big[\frac{f^{(n+1)}(a_{0_{1}})}{(n+1)!}+\frac{u(x)u^{(n)}(a_{1_{1}})}{n!}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x-y)u(x)u(y)+\sum_{k_{1}=2}^{n}(x-y)^{k}\Big(\frac{f^{(k_{1})}(x)}{k_{1}!}\;+\;u(x)\frac{u^{(k_{1}-1)}(x)}{(k_{1}-1)!}\Big)\;

\displaystyle+\;\frac{(x-y)^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\Big[f^{(n+1)}(a_{0_{1}})+(n+1)u(x)u^{(n)}(a_{1_{1}})\Big],

which implies

\displaystyle|f(x)-f(y)|\leq

\displaystyle|x-y|u(x)u(y)

(S14)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k_{1}=2}^{n}|x-y|^{k_{1}}\Big(\frac{|f^{(k_{1})}(x)|}{k_{1}!}\;+\;u(x)\frac{|u^{(k_{1}-1)}(x)|}{(k_{1}-1)!}\Big)

\displaystyle+\;\frac{|x-y|^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\Big[|f^{(n+1)}(a_{0_{1}})|+(n+1)|u(x)u^{(n)}(a_{1_{1}})|\Big].

Applying (
S9
) and (
S10
) we can estimate the sum in inequality (
S14
) in the following way

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}=2}^{n}

\displaystyle|x-y|^{k_{1}}\Big(\frac{|f^{(k_{1})}(x)|}{k_{1}!}\;+\;u(x)\frac{|u^{(k_{1}-1)}(x)|}{(k_{1}-1)!}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k_{1}=2}^{n}|x-y|^{k_{1}}\Big[\frac{4^{k_{1}}\cdot k_{1}^{2}}{k_{1}!}\sum_{{j_{1}}=0}^{k_{1}}(u_{j_{1}}(x)u_{j_{1}}(y)\,+\,u_{j_{1}}(x)(u_{j_{1}}(x)-u_{j_{1}}(y)))

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{2^{k_{1}-2}k_{1}}{(k_{1}-1)!}\sum_{{j_{1}}=0}^{k_{1}-1}\Big(u_{j_{1}}(x)u_{j_{1}}(y)+u_{j_{1}}(x)\big(u_{j_{1}}(x)-u_{j_{1}}(y)\big)\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k_{1}=2}^{n}\frac{2\cdot 4^{k_{1}}k_{1}}{(k_{1}-1)!}|x-y|^{k_{1}}\sum_{{j_{1}}=0}^{k_{1}}\Big(u_{j_{1}}(x)u_{j_{1}}(y)\,+\,u_{j_{1}}(x)\big(u_{j_{1}}(x)-u_{j_{1}}(y)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k_{1}=2}^{n}\frac{2\cdot 4^{k_{1}}k_{1}}{(k_{1}-1)!}|x-y|^{k_{1}}\sum_{{j_{1}}=0}^{k_{1}}u_{j_{1}}(x)u_{j_{1}}(y)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{k_{1}=2}^{n}\frac{4^{k_{1}}k_{1}}{(k_{1}-1)!}|x-y|^{k_{1}}\sum_{{j_{1}}=0}^{k_{1}}u_{j_{1}}(x)\big(u_{j_{1}}(x)-u_{j_{1}}(y)\big).

We obtained a first part of symmetric terms and a second one containing terms of the form
u_{j_{1}}(x)\big(u_{j_{1}}(x)-u_{j_{1}}(y)\big)
that we can further Taylor expand and treat in the same way as the term
u(x)\big(u(x)-u(y)\big)

\displaystyle\big|u_{j_{1}}(x)\big|

\displaystyle\big|u_{j_{1}}(x)-u_{j_{1}}(y)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k_{2}=1}^{n-k_{1}}\frac{|x-y|^{k_{2}}}{k_{2}!}|u_{j_{1}}(x)u^{(k_{2})}(x)|\;+\;\;\frac{|x-y|^{n-k_{2}+1}}{(n-k_{2}+1)!}|u_{j_{1}}(x)u_{j_{1}}^{n-k_{1}+1}(a_{2_{1}})|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k_{2}=1}^{n-k_{1}}\frac{|x-y|^{k_{2}}}{(k_{2}-1)!}2^{(k_{2}-2)}\sum_{j_{2}=0}^{k_{2}}\Big(u_{j_{1}+j_{2}}(x)u_{j_{1}+j_{2}}(y)+u_{j_{1}+j_{2}}(x)\big(u_{j_{1}+j_{2}}((x)-u_{j_{1}+j_{2}}((y)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\;\frac{|x-y|^{n-k_{1}+1}}{(n-k_{1}+1)!}|u_{j_{1}}(x)u_{j_{1}}^{n-k_{1}+1}(a_{2_{j_{1}}})|.

After iterating the process on the non symmetrized terms we obtain the following inequality

\displaystyle|f(x)-f(y)|

(S15)

\displaystyle\leq|x-y|u(x)u(y)+\sum_{k_{1}=2}^{n}\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{k_{1}}\frac{4^{k_{1}}k_{1}}{(k_{1}-1)!}|x-y|^{k_{1}}u_{j_{1}}(x)u_{j_{1}}(y)

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=2}^{n-1}\sum_{k_{1}=2}^{n}\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{k_{1}}\sum_{k_{2}=1}^{n-k_{1}}\sum_{j_{2}=0}^{k_{2}}...\sum_{k_{l}=1}^{n-k_{1}-...-k_{l-1}}\sum_{j_{l}=0}^{k_{l}}\frac{4^{k_{1}}k_{1}\cdot 2^{(k_{2}-2)+\dots+(k_{l}-2)}}{(k_{1}-1)!\dots(k_{l}-1)!}|x-y|^{k_{1}+\dots+k_{l}}u_{J_{l}}(x)u_{J_{l}}(y)

\displaystyle+\frac{|x-y|^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\Big[|f^{(n+1)}(a_{0_{1}})|+(n+1)|u(x)u^{(n)}(a_{1_{1}})|\Big]

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=2}^{n-1}\sum_{k_{1}=2}^{n}\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{k_{1}}\sum_{k_{2}=1}^{n-k_{1}}\sum_{j_{2}=0}^{k_{2}}...\sum_{k_{l-1}=1}^{n-k_{1}-...-k_{l-2}}\frac{4^{k_{1}}k_{1}2^{(k_{2}-2)+\dots+(k_{l-1}-2)}}{(k_{1}-1)!\dots(k_{l-1}-1)!}|x-y|^{n+1}u_{J_{l-1}}(x)u_{J_{l-1}}^{(n-k_{1}-\dots k_{l-1}+1)}(a_{j}),

where
a_{j}
are point between
x
and
y
and
J_{l}
is defined as in (
S11
).
By rescaling
k_{1}\to k_{1}-1
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}=2}^{n}

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{k_{1}}\frac{4^{k_{1}}k_{1}}{(k_{1}-1)!}|x-y|^{k_{1}}u_{j_{1}}(x)u_{j_{1}}(y)

\displaystyle=|x-y|\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{n-1}\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{k_{1}}\frac{4\cdot 4^{k_{1}}(k_{1}+1)}{k_{1}!}|x-y|^{k_{1}}u_{j_{1}}(x)u_{j_{1}}(y)

\displaystyle\leq|x-y|\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{n}\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{k_{1}}\frac{4\cdot 4^{k_{1}}2k_{1}}{k_{1}!}|x-y|^{k_{1}}u_{j_{1}}(x)u_{j_{1}}(y)

\displaystyle=|x-y|\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{n}\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{k_{1}}\frac{8\cdot 4^{k_{1}}}{(k_{1}-1)!}|x-y|^{k_{1}}u_{j_{1}}(x)u_{j_{1}}(y).

(S16)

Similarly, after introducing the parameter
m:=k_{1}+\dots+k_{l}\in[l,n]
, the second sum becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{l=2}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}=2}^{n}\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{k_{1}}\sum_{k_{2}=1}^{n-k_{1}}\sum_{j_{2}=0}^{k_{2}}...\sum_{k_{l}=1}^{n-k_{1}-...-k_{l-1}}\sum_{j_{l}=0}^{k_{l}}\frac{4^{k_{1}}k_{1}\cdot 2^{(k_{2}-2)+\dots+(k_{l}-2)}}{(k_{1}-1)!\dots(k_{l}-1)!}|x-y|^{k_{1}+\dots+k_{l}}u_{J_{l}}(x)u_{J_{l}}(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|x-y|\sum_{l=2}^{n-1}\sum_{m=l}^{n}\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{n}\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{k_{1}}\sum_{k_{2}=1}^{n-k_{1}}\sum_{j_{2}=0}^{k_{2}}...\sum_{k_{l}=1}^{n-k_{1}-...-k_{l-1}}\sum_{j_{l}=0}^{k_{l}}\frac{8\cdot 4^{k_{1}}\cdot 2^{m-k_{1}-2(l-1)}}{(k_{1}-1)!\dots(k_{l}-1)!}|x-y|^{m}u_{J_{l}}(x)u_{J_{l}}(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|x-y|\sum_{l=2}^{n-1}\sum_{m=l}^{n}\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{n}\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{k_{1}}\sum_{k_{2}=1}^{n-k_{1}}\sum_{j_{2}=0}^{k_{2}}...\sum_{k_{l}=1}^{n-k_{1}-...-k_{l-1}}\sum_{j_{l}=0}^{k_{l}}\frac{32\cdot 4^{k_{1}}\cdot 4^{m-k_{1}-l}}{(k_{1}-1)!\dots(k_{l}-1)!}|x-y|^{m}u_{J_{l}}(x)u_{J_{l}}(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C|x-y|\sum_{l=2}^{n-1}\sum_{m=l}^{n}4^{m-l}|x-y|^{m}\sum_{\underline{k}\in\mathcal{K}_{l,m}}\;\sum_{\underline{j}\in\mathcal{J}_{\underline{k}}}\frac{u_{J_{l}}(x)u_{J_{l}}(y)}{(k_{1}-1)!\dots(k_{l}-1)!}.

This, together with inequality (
S16
) gives us

\displaystyle|x-y|\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{n}\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{k_{1}}

\displaystyle\frac{8\cdot 4^{k_{1}}}{(k_{1}-1)!}|x-y|^{k_{1}}u_{j_{1}}(x)u_{j_{1}}(y)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C|x-y|\sum_{l=2}^{n-1}\sum_{m=l}^{n}4^{m-l}|x-y|^{m}\sum_{\underline{k}\in\mathcal{K}_{l,m}}\;\sum_{\underline{j}\in\mathcal{J}_{\underline{k}}}\frac{u_{J_{l}}(x)u_{J_{l}}(y)}{(k_{1}-1)!\dots(k_{l}-1)!}

\displaystyle\leq C|x-y|

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}\sum_{m=l}^{n}4^{m-l}|x-y|^{m}\sum_{\underline{k}\in\mathcal{K}_{l,m}}\;\sum_{\underline{j}\in\mathcal{J}_{\underline{k}}}\frac{u_{J_{l}}(x)u_{J_{l}}(y)}{(k_{1}-1)!\dots(k_{l}-1)!}.

Analogously we can bound the fourth summand of inequality (
S15
)

\displaystyle\sum_{l=2}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}=2}^{n}\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{k_{1}}\sum_{k_{2}=1}^{n-k_{1}}\sum_{j_{2}=0}^{k_{2}}...\sum_{k_{l}=1}^{n-k_{1}-...-k_{l-1}}\frac{4^{k_{1}}k_{1}\cdot 2^{(k_{2}-2)+\dots+(k_{l-1}-2)}}{(k_{1}-1)!\dots(k_{l-1}-1)!}|x-y|^{n+1}u_{J_{l-1}}(x)u_{J_{l-1}}^{(n-k_{1}-\dots k_{l-1}+1)}(a_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\,C|x-y|^{n+1}\sum_{l=2}^{n-1}\sum_{m=l-1}^{n}4^{m-l}\sum_{\underline{k}\in\mathcal{K}_{l,m}}\;\sum_{\underline{j}\in\mathcal{J}_{\underline{k}}}\frac{u_{J_{l-1}}(x)u_{J_{l-1}}^{(n-m+1)}(a_{j})}{(k_{1}-1)!\dots(k_{l-1}-1)!},

(S17)

for some positive constant
C
. Now, using inequality (
S5
)

\displaystyle\sum_{\underline{k}\in\mathcal{K}_{l,m}}

\displaystyle\;\sum_{\underline{j}\in\mathcal{J}_{\underline{k}}}\frac{u_{J_{l-1}}(x)u_{J_{l-1}}^{(n-m+1)}(a_{j})}{(k_{1}-1)!\dots(k_{l-1}-1)!}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\underline{k}\in\mathcal{K}_{l,m}}\;\sum_{\underline{j}\in\mathcal{J}_{\underline{k}}}\frac{C_{f}2^{n-m+1}}{(k_{1}-1)!\dots(k_{l-1}-1)!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{f}2^{n-m+1}\sum_{\underline{k}\in\mathcal{K}_{l,m}}\frac{k_{1}\cdot\dots\cdot k_{l-1}}{(k_{1}-1)!\dots(k_{l-1}-1)!}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{f}2^{n-m+1}\left(2e\right)^{l-1}.

This allows us to bound (
S17
) in the following way

\displaystyle C|x-y|^{n+1}

\displaystyle\sum_{l=2}^{n-1}\sum_{m=l-1}^{n}4^{m-l}\sum_{\underline{k}\in\mathcal{K}_{l,m}}\;\sum_{\underline{j}\in\mathcal{J}_{\underline{k}}}\frac{u_{J_{l-1}}(x)u_{J_{l-1}}^{(n-m+1)}(a_{j})}{(k_{1}-1)!\dots(k_{l-1}-1)!}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{f}|x-y|^{n+1}\sum_{l=2}^{n-1}\sum_{m=l-1}^{n}4^{m-l}2^{n-m+1}\left(2e\right)^{l-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{f}2^{n}|x-y|^{n+1}\sum_{l=2}^{n-1}\left(\frac{e}{2}\right)^{l}\sum_{m=l-1}^{n}2^{m}.

Applying inequalities (
S5
) and (
S6
)

\displaystyle\frac{|x-y|^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}

\displaystyle\Big[|f^{(n+1)}(a_{0_{1}})|+(n+1)|u(x)u^{(n)}(a_{1_{1}})|\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{f}|x-y|^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\left(4^{n}+(n+1)2^{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{f}\frac{4^{n}|x-y|^{n+1}}{n!},

and this concludes the proof.

.2
Control over the time evolved ASTLO

Employing Lemma
S4
, the properties of the ASTLOs, and the structure of the Hamiltonian we can derive an important bound of the time evolved ASTLO. We exploit the fact that we can separate the x and y dependence using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality to obtain a bound that depends on the average of the ASTLO itself.

Theorem S5

(Bound on the evolved ASTLO)
.

For all
f\in\mathcal{F}
,
n\geq 1
, and
c>\kappa
there exist two positive constants
C,\,C_{f}
such that, for all
s,t,\rho>0
, the following holds

\displaystyle\langle N_{f,ts}\rangle_{t}\leq\langle

\displaystyle N_{f,ts}\rangle_{0}+\frac{\kappa-v}{s}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\>N_{f^{\prime},rs}\rangle_{r}dr

(S18)

\displaystyle+C\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}\sum_{m=l}^{n}\sum_{\underline{k}\in\mathcal{K}_{l,m}}\;\sum_{\underline{j}\in\mathcal{J}_{\underline{k}}}\frac{4^{m-l}\cdot s^{-m-1}}{(k_{1}-1)!\dots(k_{l}-1)!}\kappa^{(m)}\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle N_{f^{\prime}_{J_{l},rs}}\big\rangle_{r}dr

\displaystyle+C_{f}4^{n}\Big(\frac{1}{n!}+\sum_{l=2}^{n-1}\left(\frac{e}{2}\right)^{l}\sum_{m=l-1}^{n}2^{m}\Big)s^{-n-1}\kappa^{(n+1)}t\big\langle N\big\rangle_{0},

where for
n=1
the sums should be dropped.

We notice that from definition (
S4
),
f^{\prime}_{J_{l},rs}=u^{2}_{J_{l}}(|x|_{rs})
and we define

\displaystyle\kappa^{(m)}:=\sup_{x\in\Lambda}\sum_{y\in\Lambda}\left\lvert h_{xy}\right\rvert\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{m}.

Proof.

Considering
N_{f,ts}
as defined above for

(S19)

We will bound each of the terms on the right hand side of this equation separately. We first notice that the second term in equation (
S19
) can be rewritten in the following way:

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}N_{f,ts}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}f(|x|_{ts})n_{x}=-\frac{v}{s}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}f^{\prime}(|x|_{ts})n_{x}=-\frac{v}{s}N_{f^{\prime},ts}

(S20)

Then, by
(
Faupin et al. 2022a

and Lemma

, for any

)

\displaystyle\Big|\Big\langle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle s^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\Lambda,\\

(S22)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}\sum_{m=l}^{n}\sum_{\underline{k}\in\mathcal{K}_{l,m}}\;\sum_{\underline{j}\in\mathcal{J}_{\underline{k}}}\frac{4^{m-l}s^{-m-1}}{(k_{1}-1)!..(k_{l}-1)!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\Lambda,\\

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C_{f}4^{n}\Big(\frac{1}{n!}+\sum_{l=2}^{n-1}\left(\frac{e}{2}\right)^{l}\sum_{m=l-1}^{n}2^{m}\Big)s^{-n-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\Lambda,\\

\displaystyle\Big|\Big\langle\psi,i[H,N_{f,ts}]\psi\Big\rangle\Big|\leq

\displaystyle+C\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}\sum_{m=l}^{n}\sum_{\underline{k}\in\mathcal{K}_{l,m}}\;\sum_{\underline{j}\in\mathcal{J}_{\underline{k}}}\frac{4^{m-l}\cdot s^{-m-1}}{(k_{1}-1)!\dots(k_{l}-1)!}\kappa^{(m)}\Big\langle\psi,\,N_{f^{\prime}_{J_{l},ts}}\psi\Big\rangle

Together with equations (

) and (

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\langle\>N_{f,ts}\rangle_{t}

.3
Relation Between the Number Operator and the ASTLO

In this chapter we derive a relation between the ASTLOs and the number operator. Applying these results to those obtained in the previous chapter, we derive a bound on the evolved ASTLO that depends only on the ASTLO and the number operator at initial times. Then, by understanding the decay of the terms in this expression as
n
goes to infinity, we obtain a bound that only involves the average at initial time of the number operator on a given finite set. Together with the result obtained at the beginning of the chapter, this allows us to derive the bound on the particle propagation.

.3.1
Number Operator and ASTLO

We now compare the ASTLO and the number operator on certain finite regions. The specific properties of the function in

Theorem S7

(Connection between number operator and ASTLOs)

∎

On the WL-dimension of circulant graphs of prime power order
Ilia Ponomarenko

Abstract
. The WL \WL -dimension of a graph X X is the smallest positive integer m m such that the m m -dimensional Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm correctly tests the isomorphism between X X and any other graph. It is proved that the WL \WL -dimension of any circulant graph of prime power order is at most 3 3 , and this bound cannot be reduced. The proof is based on using theories of coherent configurations and Cayley schemes over a cyclic group.



1.
Introduction

A (not necessarily undirected) graph is said to be
circulant
if it is a Cayley graph over a cyclic group. A main motivation of the present paper is the following computational problem: given a circulant graph
{\cal G}
and a graph
{\cal G}^{\prime}
test whether
{\cal G}
is isomorphic to
{\cal G}^{\prime}
. To the best of the author’s knowledge, there are two polynomial-time algorithms related to this problem, namely,
[
6
]
and
[
14
]
. The first solves the problem for all
{\cal G}^{\prime}
, but largely based on computational group theory, whereas the second solves the problem only for circulant
{\cal G}^{\prime}
but is more or less combinatorial. It is natural to ask if the problem can be solved in a purely combinatorial way for all
{\cal G}^{\prime}
?

One of the most famous pure combinatorial methods for testing graph isomorphism was introduced in
[
18
]
. This method first uses the classical Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm, which, for a given graph
{\cal G}
, constructs a (in a sense) canonical coloring of all pairs of vertices. Then the resulting coloring is compared with similar coloring constructed for a graph
{\cal G}^{\prime}
; the graphs
{\cal G}
and
{\cal G}^{\prime}
are declared isomorphic if and only if the colorings have the same sets of colors (this can be checked quite easily). This method tests isomorphism of
{\cal G}
and
{\cal G}^{\prime}
correctly in many (but not all) cases
[
16
]
.

A generalization of the above method is obtained if the classical Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm is replaced by its
m
-dimensional (
m\geq 3
) analog, in which the
m
-tuples of vertices are canonically colored. In this case, for every graph
{\cal G}
there is a minimal
m
with the following property: any graph
{\cal G}^{\prime}
such that the set of colors in the canonical coloring of the
m
-tuples of the vertices of
{\cal G}^{\prime}
is equal to that for
{\cal G}
, is isomorphic to
{\cal G}
. This minimal
m
is called the
Weisfeiler-Leman dimension
(the
\WL
-dimension
) of
{\cal G}
and is denoted by
\dim_{\scriptscriptstyle\WL}({\cal G})
. Now we can refine the question posed in the first paragraph as follows.

Question:

is it true that there exists
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\dim_{\scriptscriptstyle\WL}({\cal G})\leq m
for every circulant graph
{\cal G}
.

Although we cannot answer this question in full, our main result, presented by the theorem below, says that for circulant graphs of prime power order (i.e., those with the number of vertices equal to the power of a prime), as such a constant one can take
3
.

Theorem 1.1
.

The WL-dimension of every circulant graph of prime power order is at most
3
.

Every Paley graph
{\cal G}
of prime order
p
is circulant. It is also known (see, e.g.,
[
2
, Subsec. 4.5]
) that for some
p
, one can find a graph
{\cal G}^{\prime}
of order
p
such that
{\cal G}
and
{\cal G}^{\prime}
are nonisomorphic but have the same sets of colors in the colorings constructed by the classical Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm. It follows that
\dim_{\scriptscriptstyle\WL}({\cal G})>2
, which shows that the estimate in Theorem
1.1
is sharp. An infinite family of such examples is obtained if one replaces
{\cal G}
and
{\cal G}^{\prime}
by their lexicographic powers.

Let us discuss some ideas underlying the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The classical Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm can be considered as an (efficiently computable) functor which given a graph
{\cal G}
defines a coherent configuration
{\cal X}=\WL({\cal G})
(the exact definitions are in Sections
2
and
3
). It was proved in
[
9
]
that
\dim_{\scriptscriptstyle\WL}({\cal G})\leq 2
if and only if the coherent configuration
{\cal X}
is separable, i.e., every algebraic isomorphism from
{\cal X}
to another coherent configuration is induced by isomorphism.

A weakening of the property of a coherent configuration to be separable is to consider not all algebraic isomorphisms, but only those that have one-point extensions. In this way, we arrive at a concept of
sesquiseparable
coherent configuration, which is introduced and studied in Section
4
. In fact, if
{\cal G}
is a vertex-transitive graph and the coherent configuration
{\cal X}
is sesquiseparable, then
\dim_{\scriptscriptstyle\WL}({\cal G})\leq 3
. We show that
{\cal X}
is sesquiseparable if
{\cal G}
is a circulant graph of prime power order.

Now let
{\cal G}
be a circulant graph. Then the coherent configuration
{\cal X}=\WL({\cal G})
is a circulant scheme (a Cayley scheme over a cyclic group; for details, see Section
6
) and its structure is fairly well controlled by the Leung-Man theory (see, for example,
[
5
]
). In particular,
{\cal X}
can be constructed from trivial and normal circulant schemes by using tensor and wedge products. The normal circulant schemes and the operation of wedge (or generalized wreath) product of (association) schemes have been introduced and studied in
[
5
]
and
[
15
]
, respectively. It seems that a recent article
[
13
]
opens up a way to formulate the Leung-Man theory directly in terms of graph theory, bypassing association schemes.

When
{\cal G}
is of prime power order, the tensor product is irrelevant and we prove that
{\cal X}
is sesquiseparable by induction on the length of a decomposition of
{\cal X}
into a series of the wedge products (Lemma
6.8
). The central part of the proof is focused on studying algebraic and combinatorial isomorphisms of the wedge product and establishing a sufficient condition for the wedge product to be sesquiseparable (see Section
5
and Remark
5.1
).

We conclude the introduction with a brief remark about Theorem
6.4
obtained in the course of the proof and which is of independent interest. In fact, this theorem says that Theorem
1.1
holds for much wider class of circulant graphs. Among these graphs are those
{\cal G}
for which the scheme
{\cal X}
is the wedge product of normal circulant schemes (such schemes cover the half of counterexamples constructed in
[
4
]
).

For a set

2.2.
Coherent configurations

Let
S
be a partition of
\Omega^{2}
. A pair
\mathcal{X}=(\Omega,S)
is called a

on
\Omega
if

(C1)

1_{\Omega}\in S^{\cup}
,

(C2)

s^{*}\in S
for all
s\in S
,

(C3)

given
r,s,t\in S
, the number
c_{rs}^{t}=|\alpha r\cap\beta s^{*}|
does not depend on
(\alpha,\beta)\in t
.

The number
|\Omega|
is called the

of
{\cal X}
. We say that
{\cal X}
is

if
S
consists of
1_{\Omega}
and its complement (unless
\Omega

homogeneous
or a
scheme

1_{\Omega}\in S

c_{rs}^{t}=c_{sr}^{t}
for all
r,s,t\in S

2.3.
Isomorphisms and schurity

A
combinatorial isomorphism
or, briefly,
isomorphism
from
{\cal X}
to a coherent configuration
{\cal X}^{\prime}=(\Omega^{\prime},S^{\prime})
is defined to be a bijection
f:\Omega\rightarrow\Omega^{\prime}
such that
s^{f}=\{(\alpha^{f},\beta^{f}):(\alpha,\beta)\in s\}
belongs to
S^{\prime}
for all
s\in S
. In this case,
{\cal X}
and
{\cal X}^{\prime}
are said to be
isomorphic
; the set of all isomorphisms from
{\cal X}
to
{\cal X}^{\prime}
is denoted by
\iso({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime})
. The group of all isomorphisms of
{\cal X}
to itself contains a normal subgroup

\aut({\cal X})=\{f\in\sym(\Omega):\ s^{f}=s\text{ for all }s\in S\}

called the
automorphism group
of
{\cal X}
.

Let
G\leq\sym(\Omega)
. Denote by
S
the set of all orbits
(\alpha,\beta)^{G}
in the induced action of
G
on
\Omega\times\Omega
, where
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega
. Then the pair
\inv(G)=(\Omega,S)
is a coherent configuration. Any coherent configuration associated with a permutation group in this way is said to be
schurian
. Note that
{\cal X}
is schurian if and only if
{\cal X}=\inv(\aut({\cal X})
.

2.4.
Extensions

There is a natural partial order
\leq
on the set of all coherent configurations on
\Omega
. Namely, given two such coherent configurations
{\cal X}
and
{\cal Y}
, we set

{\cal X}\leq{\cal Y}\ \Leftrightarrow\ S({\cal X})^{\cup}\subseteq S({\cal Y})^{\cup},

and say that
{\cal Y}
is the
extension
of
{\cal X}
. The minimal and maximal elements with respect to this order are the trivial and
discrete
coherent configurations, respectively; in the last case,
S
consists of singletons. Note that the functor
{\cal X}\to\aut({\cal X})
reverse the inclusion, namely,

(3)

{\cal X}\leq{\cal Y}\Rightarrow\aut({\cal X})\geq\aut({\cal Y}).

2.5.
Algebraic isomorphisms and separability

A bijection
\varphi:S\rightarrow S^{\prime}
is called an
algebraic isomorphism
from
{\cal X}
to
{\cal X}^{\prime}
if for all
r,s,t\in S
, we have

c_{r^{\varphi}s^{\varphi}}^{t^{\varphi}}=c_{rs}^{t}.

In this case,
|\Omega^{\prime}|=|\Omega|
,
1_{\Omega^{\prime}}=\varphi(1_{\Omega})
, and
{\cal X}^{\prime}
is commutative if and only if so is
{\cal X}
. Every
f\in\iso({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime})
induces the algebraic isomorphism
\varphi_{f}:{\cal X}\to{\cal X}^{\prime},

s\mapsto s^{f}
; we put

\iso({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime},\varphi)=\{f\in\iso({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime}):\ \varphi_{f}=\varphi\}.

Note that
\aut({\cal X})=\iso({\cal X},{\cal X},\id)
, where
\id
is the trivial (identical) algebraic automorphism of
{\cal X}
. Finally, for any
\alpha\in\Omega
and
\alpha^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}
, we put

\iso_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime},\varphi)=\{f\in\iso({\cal X}_{,}{\cal X}^{\prime},\varphi):\ \alpha^{f}=\alpha^{\prime}\}.

A coherent configuration
{\cal X}
is said to be
separable
if every algebraic isomorphism from
{\cal X}
is induced by isomorphism, equivalently,
\iso({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime},\varphi)\neq\varnothing
for all
{\cal X}^{\prime}
and
\varphi
. Any trivial coherent configuration is separable.

2.6.
Relations

The elements of
S
and of
S^{\cup}
are called
basis relations
and
relations
of the coherent configuration
{\cal X}
. A unique basis relation containing the pair
(\alpha,\beta)
is denoted by
r(\alpha,\beta)
. The set of all relations is closed with respect to intersections and unions.

Any
\Delta\subseteq\Omega
such that
1_{\Delta}\in S
is called a
fiber
of
{\cal X}
. In view of condition (C1), the set
F=F({\cal X})
of all fibers forms a partition of
\Omega
. Every basis relation is contained in the Cartesian product of two uniquely determined fibers. When
{\cal X}
is a scheme,
F=\{\Omega\}
, whereas when
{\cal X}
is schurian,
F
is just the set of orbits of the group
\aut({\cal X})
.

Every algebraic isomorphism
\varphi:{\cal X}\to{\cal X}^{\prime}
is extended in a natural way to a poset isomorphism
S^{\cup}\to(S^{\prime})^{\cup}
, denoted also by
\varphi
. Then
\varphi
induces
a poset isomorphism
F^{\cup}\to(F^{\prime})^{\cup}
,
\Delta\mapsto\Delta^{\varphi}
, where the set
\Delta^{\varphi}
is defined by the equality
\varphi(1_{\Delta})=1_{\Delta^{\varphi}}
.

Let
{\cal X}\leq{\cal Y}
and
{\cal X}^{\prime}\leq{\cal Y}^{\prime}
. We say that the algebraic isomorphism
\varphi
is extended to an algebraic isomorphism
\psi:{\cal Y}\to{\cal Y}^{\prime}
if
\psi(s)=\varphi(s)
for all
s\in S
. Note that such an extension is uniquely defined if it exists.

2.7.
Parabolics and quotients

A relation of
{\cal X}
that is an equivalence relation on
\Omega
is called a
parabolic
of
{\cal X}
; the set of all parabolics is denoted by
E=E({\cal X})
. It contains the equivalence relations
\langle{s}\rangle
and
\rad(s)
for
s\in S^{\cup}
. Given a parabolic

, we define
S_{\Omega/e}=\{s_{\Omega/e}:\ s\in S\}
, and
S_{\Delta}=\{s_{\Delta}:\ s\in S,\ s_{\Delta}\neq\varnothing\}
for any
\Delta\in\Omega/e
. Then

{\cal X}_{\Omega/e}=(\Omega/e,S_{\Omega/e})\quad\text{and}\quad{\cal X}_{\Delta}=(\Delta,S_{\Delta})

are coherent configurations, called a
quotient
of
{\cal X}
modulo

restriction
of
{\cal X}
to
\Delta

as follows:
\Gamma_{\Omega/e}
in the first case, and
\Gamma\cap\Delta
in the second. In particular,
{\cal X}_{\Omega/e}
and
{\cal X}_{\Delta}
are schemes if so is
{\cal X}
.

Let
\varphi:{\cal X}\to{\cal X}^{\prime}
be an algebraic isomorphism and

. Then
\varphi(e)
is a parabolic of
{\cal X}^{\prime}
with the same number of classes. Moreover,

(4)

\varphi(\langle{s}\rangle)=\langle{\varphi(s)}\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi(\rad(s))=\rad(\varphi(s)).

The algebraic isomorphism
\varphi
induces the algebraic isomorphism

\varphi_{\Omega/e}:{\cal X}_{\Omega/e}\to{\cal X}^{\prime}_{\Omega^{\prime}/e^{\prime}},\ s_{\Omega/e}\mapsto s^{\prime}_{\Omega^{\prime}/e^{\prime}},

where
e^{\prime}=\varphi(e)
and

. Now let
\Delta\in\Omega/e
and
\Delta^{\prime}\in\Omega/e^{\prime}
. Assume that there is
\Gamma\in F
such that
\Delta\cap\Gamma\neq\varnothing\neq\Delta^{\prime}\cap\Gamma^{\prime}
, where
\Gamma^{\prime}=\Gamma^{\varphi}
. Then

induces an algebraic isomorphism

\varphi_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}}:{\cal X}_{\Delta}\to{\cal X}^{\prime}_{\Delta^{\prime}},\ s_{\Delta}\mapsto s^{\prime}_{\Delta^{\prime}}.

This algebraic isomorphisms always exists for all
\Delta
and
\Delta^{\prime}
if

(and hence
{\cal X}^{\prime}
) is a scheme.

2.9.
Point extensions

The

of the coherent configuration

with respect to the points

is defined to be the smallest coherent configuration

Note that the

When

-extension

(respectively,

2.10.
Partly regular coherent configurations.

A coherent configuration
{\cal X}
is said to be
partly regular
if there exists a point
\alpha\in\Omega
such that
|\alpha s|\leq 1
for all
s\in S
; the point

is said to be

is a faithful regular orbit of

. Note that every extension of partly regular coherent configuration is partly regular.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
2

Every partly regular coherent configuration is schurian and separable.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
{\cal X}
be a partly regular coherent configuration and
{\cal X}^{\prime}\geq{\cal X}
. Then
{\cal X}^{\prime}={\cal X}
if and only if
F({\cal X}^{\prime})=F({\cal X})
.

Proof.

It suffices to verify the “if” part only. Assume that
F({\cal X}^{\prime})=F({\cal X})
. We take

that contains a regular point of
{\cal X}
. Since
{\cal X}
is schurian (Lemma

),
\Delta
is a faithful regular orbit of the group

and hence
|G|=|\Delta|
. The same argument applied to partly regular coherent configuration

and
\Delta\in F({\cal X}^{\prime})
, shows that
|G^{\prime}|=|\Delta|
, where
G^{\prime}=\aut({\cal X}^{\prime})
. Thus,

|G|=|\Delta|=|G^{\prime}|.

Since
G^{\prime}\leq G
(see (
3
), this yields

, and
{\cal X}=\inv(G)=\inv(G^{\prime})={\cal X}^{\prime}
.
∎

3.
Multidimensional coherent configurations and WL-dimension

The concept of the
\WL
-dimension of a graph was introduced in
[
11
]
in terms of the multidimensional Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm. A goal of this section is to analyze this definition in terms of coherent configurations. Our approach is based on the multidimensional coherent configurations defined in
[
1
]
; all the necessary information and results about them are taken from
[
12
]
.

Throughout this section, we fix an integer
m\geq 2
, and put
M=\{1,\ldots,m\}
. The monoid of all mappings
\sigma:M\to M
is denoted by
\mon(M)
. Elements of the Cartesian
m
-power
\Omega^{m}
are
m
-tuples
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
with
x_{i}\in\Omega
for all
i\in M
. For
\alpha\in\Omega
, we put

(6)

x_{i\leftarrow\alpha}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},\alpha,x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{m}).

For
X\subseteq\Omega^{m}
and
\sigma\in M
, we put
X^{\sigma}=\{x^{\sigma}:\ x\in X\}
, where
x^{\sigma}=(x_{1^{\sigma}},\ldots,x_{m^{\sigma}})
.

3.1.
The multidimensional Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm

The
m
-dimensional Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm constructs for a given graph
{\cal G}=(\Omega,D)
a certain coloring
c(m,{\cal G})
of the set
\Omega^{m}
; a
coloring
is meant as a function from
\Omega^{m}
to a linear ordered set the elements of which are called
colors
. At the first stage, an initial coloring
c_{0}=c_{0}(m,{\cal G})
of
\Omega^{m}
is defined by the following condition: given
x,y\in\Omega^{m}

be such that

. Then there exist
\Delta\in F({\cal X})
and
\Delta^{\prime}\in F({\cal X}^{\prime})
for which

\Delta_{\mathfrak{S}}=\bar{\Delta},\quad\Delta^{\prime}_{{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}}=\bar{\Delta}^{\prime},\quad\Delta^{\varphi}=\Delta^{\prime}.

Let
\alpha\in\Delta
and
\alpha^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\prime}
be such that
\alpha_{{\mathfrak{S}}}=\overline{\alpha}
and
\alpha^{\prime}_{{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}}=\alpha^{\prime}
. The algebraic isomorphism
\varphi
being sesquiclosed has
(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})
-extension
\varphi_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}
. Since
\varphi
induces
\psi
, one can define the algebraic isomorphism

\psi_{\overline{\alpha},\overline{\alpha}^{\prime}}=(\varphi_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}})_{{\mathfrak{S}},{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}}

from
({\cal X}_{\alpha})_{{\mathfrak{S}}}
to
({\cal X}^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}})_{{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}}
. For any
\overline{s}\in S({\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}})
, we have

\psi_{\overline{\alpha},\overline{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\overline{s})=(\varphi_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}})_{{\mathfrak{S}},{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}}(s_{\mathfrak{S}})=(\varphi_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}(s))_{{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}}=\varphi(s)_{{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}}=\psi(s_{\mathfrak{S}}),

where
s\in S
is such that
s_{\mathfrak{S}}=\overline{s}
, and

\psi_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}(1_{\bar{\alpha}})=(\varphi_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}})_{\mathfrak{S}}((1_{\alpha})_{\mathfrak{S}})=(\varphi_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}(1_{\alpha}))_{\mathfrak{S}}=(1_{\alpha^{\prime}})_{\mathfrak{S}}=1_{\bar{\alpha}^{\prime}},

Thus the algebraic isomorphism
\psi_{\bar{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}^{\prime}}
is the
(\bar{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}^{\prime})
-extension of the algebraic isomorphism
\psi
.∎

A coherent configuration
{\cal X}
is said to be
sesquiseparable
if every sesquiclosed algebraic isomorphism is induced by isomorphism. Clearly, every separable coherent configuration is sesquiseparable; the converse is not true, see Subsection
6.2
.

Lemma 4.4
.

A coherent configuration
{\cal X}
is sesquiseparable if every one-point extension of
{\cal X}
is separable.

Proof.

Let
\varphi:{\cal X}\to{\cal X}^{\prime}
be a sesquiclosed algebraic isomorphism. Then it has a
(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})
-extension
\varphi_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}
for all appropriate
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
. Since the coherent configuration
{\cal X}_{\alpha}
is separable,
\varphi_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}
is induced by a certain isomorphism
f
. Then
s^{f}=\varphi_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}(s)=\varphi(s)
for all
s\in S
. Hence,
f\in\iso({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime},\varphi)
and
{\cal X}
is sesquiseparable.∎

4.2.
Sesquiclosed coherent configurations

In parallel with sesquiclosed algebraic isomorphisms, we introduce a notion which weakens the notion of
2
-closed coherent configuration studied in
[
2
, Section 3.5.3]
. Namely, in Lemma 3.5.25 there, it was proved that a coherent configuration
{\cal X}
is
2
-closed only if for every
\alpha\in\Omega
the following conditions are satisfied:

(S1)

F({\cal X}_{\alpha})=\{\alpha s:\ s\in S,\ \alpha s\neq\varnothing\}
,

(S2)

the trivial algebraic automorphism of
{\cal X}
is sesquiclosed.

Definition 4.5
.

A coherent configuration
{\cal X}
satisfying
(S1)
and
(S2)
for all
\alpha
is said to be sesquiclosed.

Though conditions (S1) and (S2) are satisfied in the schurian case, not every sesquiclosed coherent configuration is schurian, for example, those are coherent configurations constructed in
[
2
, Theorem 4.2.4]
. The same example shows that the class of sesquiclosed coherent configurations is not invariant with respect to algebraic isomorphisms.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
{\cal X}
be a sesquiclosed coherent configuration and
{\mathfrak{S}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}({\cal X})
. Then the coherent configuration
{\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}}
is sesquiclosed.

Proof.

Condition (S2) is satisfied for
{\cal X}={\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}}
by Lemma
4.3
with
{\cal X}={\cal X}^{\prime}
and
\varphi=\id
. Assume that condition (S1) is not satisfied for
{\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}}
. Then there are
\alpha\in\Omega
and
s\in S
such that

(17)

\varnothing\neq\alpha_{\mathfrak{S}}s_{\mathfrak{S}}\not\in F(({\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}})_{\alpha_{\mathfrak{S}}}).

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\alpha s\neq\varnothing
. By condition (S1), we have
\alpha s\in F({\cal X}_{\alpha})
and hence
(\alpha s)_{\mathfrak{S}}\in F(({\cal X}_{\alpha})_{\mathfrak{S}})
. However,
({\cal X}_{\alpha})_{\mathfrak{S}}\geq({\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}})_{\alpha_{\mathfrak{S}}}
. Therefore,
(\alpha s)_{\mathfrak{S}}
is contained in some
\Delta\in F(({\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}})_{\alpha_{\mathfrak{S}}})
. Since
(\alpha s)_{\mathfrak{S}}\subseteq\alpha_{\mathfrak{S}}s_{\mathfrak{S}}
, we have

(18)

(\alpha s)_{\mathfrak{S}}\subseteq\Delta\subseteq\alpha_{\mathfrak{S}}s_{\mathfrak{S}}.

In view of (
17
), there is
\Delta^{\prime}\in F(({\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}})_{\alpha_{\mathfrak{S}}})
such that
\Delta\neq\Delta^{\prime}\subseteq\alpha_{\mathfrak{S}}s_{\mathfrak{S}}
. Take
any point
\alpha^{\prime}
for which
(\alpha^{\prime}s)_{\mathfrak{S}}
intersects
\Delta^{\prime}
. Then as above

(19)

(\alpha^{\prime}s)_{\mathfrak{S}}\subseteq\Delta^{\prime}\subseteq\alpha_{\mathfrak{S}}s_{\mathfrak{S}}.

Note that
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
lie in the same fiber of
{\cal X}
, because
\alpha s\neq\varnothing\neq\alpha^{\prime}s
and
s\in S
. By condition (S2), the trivial algebraic automorphism of
{\cal X}
has the
(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})
-extension; denote it by
\psi
. Then there is an algebraic isomorphism
\psi_{\mathfrak{S}}:({\cal X}_{\alpha})_{\mathfrak{S}}\to({\cal X}_{\alpha^{\prime}})_{\mathfrak{S}}
that induces the trivial algebraic automorphism of
{\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}}
. Using formulas (
18
) and (
19
), we obtain

\Delta^{\psi_{\mathfrak{S}}}\supseteq((\alpha s)_{\mathfrak{S}})^{\psi_{\mathfrak{S}}}=((\alpha s)^{\psi})_{\mathfrak{S}}=(\alpha^{\prime}s)_{\mathfrak{S}}\subseteq\Delta^{\prime},

whence
\Delta=\Delta^{\prime}
, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
{\cal X}
be a sesquiclosed coherent configuration and
\varphi:{\cal X}\to{\cal X}^{\prime}
a sesquiclosed algebraic isomorphism. Then the coherent configuration
{\cal X}^{\prime}
is sesquiclosed.

Proof.

Let
\alpha^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}
and
s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}
. The algebraic isomorphism
\varphi:{\cal X}\to{\cal X}^{\prime}
being sesquiclosed, has an
(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})
-extension
\psi
for some
\alpha\in\Omega
such that
\alpha s\neq\varnothing
, where
s=\varphi^{-1}(s^{\prime})
. Since the coherent configuration
{\cal X}
is sesquiclosed,
\alpha s\in F({\cal X}_{\alpha})
. Consequently, the set
\alpha^{\prime}s^{\prime}=\psi(\alpha s)
belongs to
F({\cal X}^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}})
. This shows that
{\cal X}^{\prime}
satisfies condition (S1).

Let
\alpha^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}
lie in the same fiber of
{\cal X}^{\prime}
. The algebraic isomorphism
\varphi
being sesquiclosed, has an
(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})
-extension
\psi
and
(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime\prime})
-extension
\psi^{\prime}
for some
\alpha\in\Omega
. It follows that
\psi^{-1}\psi^{\prime}
is the
(\alpha^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime})
-extension of the trivial algebraic automorphism of
{\cal X}^{\prime}
. This shows that
{\cal X}^{\prime}
satisfies condition (S2).
∎

When a coherent configuration
{\cal X}
is not sesquiclosed, one can construct a uniquely determined sesquiclosed extension
{\cal X}^{\prime}
such that
\aut({\cal X}^{\prime})=\aut({\cal X})
. However, the explicit definition of
{\cal X}^{\prime}
is outside the scope of the present paper.

4.3.
Partly regular sections

In this subsection, we prove two auxiliary lemmas for the sections of sesquiclosed schemes, the restriction to which is partly regular. They will be used in Subsection
5.4
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
{\cal X}
be a commutative sesquiclosed scheme,
{\mathfrak{S}}_{0},{\mathfrak{S}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}({\cal X})
, and
\alpha
a point such that
\alpha_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}}\neq\varnothing
. Assume that
{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}\leq{\mathfrak{S}}
and
(({\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}})_{\alpha_{\mathfrak{S}}})_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}}

(20)

(({\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}})_{\alpha_{\mathfrak{S}}})_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}}=(({\cal X}_{\alpha})_{\mathfrak{S}})_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}}.

Proof.

The obvious inclusion
({\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}})_{\alpha_{\mathfrak{S}}}\leq({\cal X}_{\alpha})_{\mathfrak{S}}
implies

(({\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}})_{\alpha_{\mathfrak{S}}})_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}}\leq(({\cal X}_{\alpha})_{\mathfrak{S}})_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}}.

The coherent configuration on the left-hand side is partly regular by the hypothesis. By Corollary
2.3
, it suffices to verify that every fiber
\Delta
of
(({\cal X}_{\alpha})_{\mathfrak{S}})_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}}
is also a fiber of
(({\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}})_{\alpha_{\mathfrak{S}}})_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}}
.

By condition (S1), one can find
s\in S
such that
\Delta=(\alpha s)_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}}
. Since
{\cal X}
is commutative, Lemma
2.1
yields

\Delta=(\alpha s)_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}}=((\alpha s)_{{\mathfrak{S}}})_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}}=(\alpha_{\mathfrak{S}}s_{\mathfrak{S}})_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}}.

On the other hand, the scheme
{\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}}
is sesquiclosed by Lemma
4.6
. Therefore, the set
\alpha_{\mathfrak{S}}s_{\mathfrak{S}}
is a fiber of the coherent configuration
({\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}})_{\alpha_{\mathfrak{S}}}
. It follows that
(\alpha_{\mathfrak{S}}s_{\mathfrak{S}})_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}}
and hence
\Delta
is a fiber of the coherent configuration
(({\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}})_{\alpha_{\mathfrak{S}}})_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}}
. ∎

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
{\cal X}
and
{\cal X}^{\prime}
be schemes,
\varphi:{\cal X}\to{\cal X}^{\prime}
an algebraic isomorphism having an
(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})
-extension
\varphi_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}
for some

and
\alpha^{\prime}

Then

\iso({\cal X}_{\alpha},{\cal X}^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}},\varphi_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}})^{\mathfrak{S}}=\iso(({\cal X}_{\alpha})_{{\mathfrak{S}}},({\cal X}^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}})_{{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}},(\varphi_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}})_{{\mathfrak{S}},{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}}),

{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}={\mathfrak{S}}^{f}

Proof.

From the hypothesis, it follows that

|\iso({\cal X}_{\alpha},{\cal X}^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}},\varphi_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}})^{\mathfrak{S}}|=|\aut({\cal X}_{\alpha})^{\mathfrak{S}}|,

5.
e_{1}/e_{0}
-condition

5.1.
Definition

Let
{\cal X}=(\Omega,S)
be a scheme, and let
e_{0}
and
e_{1}
be parabolics of
{\cal X}
such that
e_{0}\subseteq e_{1}
. Following
[
6
]
, we say that
{\cal X}
satisfies the
e_{1}/e_{0}
-condition
if for all
s\in S
,

s\cap e_{1}=\varnothing\quad\Rightarrow\quad e_{0}\subseteq\rad(s).

This is always true if
e_{0}=1_{\Omega}
or
e_{1}={\bf 1}_{\Omega}
; in the other cases, we say that the
e_{1}/e_{0}
-condition is satisfied
nontrivially
. When
e_{0}=e_{1}
, the condition exactly means that the scheme
{\cal X}
is isomorphic to the wreath product
{\cal X}_{\Delta}\wr{\cal X}_{\Omega/e_{0}}
for any
\Delta\in\Omega/e_{0}
. Finally, we note that if
e^{\prime}_{0}\subseteq e_{0}
and
e^{\prime}_{1}\supseteq e_{1}
are parabolics, then
{\cal X}
satisfies also the
e^{\prime}_{1}/e^{\prime}_{0}
-condition.

Remark 5.1
.

The
e_{1}/e_{0}
-condition provides internal definition of the operation wedge product of association schemes, introduced and studied in
[
15
]
. Namely,
{\cal X}
satisfies the
e_{1}/e_{0}
-condition if and only if
{\cal X}
is isomorphic to the wedge product of the schemes
{\cal X}_{\Delta}
,
\Delta\in\Omega/e_{1}
, and the scheme
{\cal X}_{\Omega/e_{0}}
.

In the sequel, we simplify the notation as follows. First, we put
\Omega_{0}=\Omega/e_{0}
,
{\cal X}_{0}={\cal X}_{\Omega/e_{0}}
, and
\Omega_{1}=\Omega/e_{1}
. Second, for every
\Delta\in\Omega_{1}
, we put
\Delta_{0}=\Delta/e_{0}
. Finally, for an algebraic isomorphism
\varphi:{\cal X}\to{\cal X}^{\prime}
, we put
\varphi_{0}=\varphi_{\Omega_{0}}
.

5.2.
Admissible pairs

Let
\varphi:{\cal X}\to{\cal X}^{\prime}
,
s\mapsto s^{\prime}
be an algebraic isomorphism, where
{\cal X}^{\prime}=(\Omega^{\prime},S^{\prime})
. In view of formula (
4
),
{\cal X}^{\prime}
satisfies the
e^{\prime}_{1}/e^{\prime}_{0}
-condition, where
e^{\prime}_{0}=\varphi(e_{0})
and
e^{\prime}_{1}=\varphi(e_{1})
. As above, we define the sets
\Omega^{\prime}_{0}
,
\Omega^{\prime}_{1}
, and
\Delta^{\prime}_{0}
for all
\Delta^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}_{1}
. In what follows, we are interested in finding the set
\iso({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime},\varphi)
; for
\varphi=\id
, this was done in
[
6
]
.

Suppose we are given a bijection
f_{0}:\Omega_{0}\to\Omega^{\prime}_{0}
taking
(e_{1})_{\Omega_{0}}
to
(e^{\prime}_{1})_{\Omega^{\prime}_{0}}
, and bijections
f_{\Delta}:\Delta\to\Delta^{\prime}
for each
\Delta\in\Omega_{1}
, where
\Delta^{\prime}
is a uniquely determined class of
e^{\prime}_{1}
, for which
(\Delta_{0})^{f_{0}}=\Delta^{\prime}_{0}
. The pair
P=(\{f_{\Delta}\}_{\Delta\in\Omega_{1}},f_{0})
is said to be
e_{1}/e_{0}
-admissible
if

(23)

(f_{\Delta})^{\Delta_{0}}=(f_{0})^{\Delta_{0}}

for all
\Delta\in\Omega_{1}
. In this case there exists a uniquely determined bijection
f:\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}
for which
f^{\Omega_{0}}=f_{0}
and
f^{\Delta}=f_{\Delta}
for all
\Delta\in\Omega_{1}
. We say that
f
is induced by the pair
P
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
{\cal X}
be a scheme satisfying the
e_{1}/e_{0}
-condition,
{\cal X}^{\prime}
a scheme on
\Omega^{\prime}
, and
\varphi:{\cal X}\to{\cal X}^{\prime}
an algebraic isomorphism. Then
f\in\iso({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime},\varphi)
if and only if

f
is induced by an
e_{1}/e_{0}
-admissible pair
(\{f_{\Delta}\},f_{0})
such that

(24)

f_{0}\in\iso({\cal X}_{0},{\cal X}^{\prime}_{0},\varphi_{0})\quad\text{and}\quad f_{\Delta}\in\iso({\cal X}_{\Delta},{\cal X}^{\prime}_{\Delta^{\prime}},\varphi_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}})\ \,\text{for all}\ \Delta\in\Omega_{1}.

Proof.

The “only if” part is clear, because every
f\in\iso({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime},\varphi)
is obviously induced by the pair
(\{f^{\Delta}\},f^{\Omega_{0}})
which satisfies the conditions (
24
). Conversely, suppose that
f
is induced by an
e_{1}/e_{0}
-admissible pair
(\{f_{\Delta}\},f_{0})
satisfying conditions (
24
). We need to verify that
s^{f}=\varphi(s)
for all
s\in S
.

Let
s\subseteq e_{1}
. Then
s
is equal to the union of
s_{\Delta}
,
\Delta\in\Omega_{1}
. By the second part of (
24
), this implies that

s^{f}=\bigcup_{\Delta\in\Omega_{1}}(s_{\Delta})^{f_{\Delta}}=\bigcup_{\Delta\in\Omega_{1}}\varphi_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}}(s_{\Delta})=\bigcup_{\Delta^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}_{1}}\varphi(s)_{\Delta^{\prime}}=\varphi(s).

Now, we may assume that
s\cap e_{1}=\varnothing
. Then
e_{0}\subseteq\rad(s)
, because
{\cal X}
satisfies the
e_{1}/e_{0}
-condition. It follows that
s
is equal to the union of all
\Gamma\times\Lambda
for which
(\Gamma,\Lambda)
lies in
s_{0}=s_{\Omega/e_{0}}

f^{\Omega/e_{0}}=f_{0}
, we obtain

s^{f}=\bigl(\bigcup_{(\Gamma,\Lambda)\in s_{0}}\Gamma\times\Lambda\bigr)^{f}=\bigcup_{(\Gamma^{f},\Lambda^{f})\in s_{0}^{f}}\Gamma^{f}\times\Lambda^{f}=\bigcup_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})\in s^{\prime}_{0}}\Gamma^{\prime}\times\Lambda^{\prime},

where
s^{\prime}_{0}
,
\Gamma^{\prime}
, and
\Lambda^{\prime}
are the
f_{0}
-images of
s_{0}
,
\Gamma
, and
\Lambda
, respectively. As was noted above,
{\cal X}^{\prime}
satisfies the
e^{\prime}_{1}/e^{\prime}_{0}
-condition with

, and
e^{\prime}_{1}=\varphi(e_{1})

f_{0}
induces
\varphi_{0}
, the relation
\varphi(s)
equals the union of all
\Gamma^{\prime}\times\Lambda^{\prime}
with
(\Gamma^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})\in s^{\prime}_{0}

s^{f}=\bigcup_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})\in s_{0}^{\prime}}\Gamma^{\prime}\times\Lambda^{\prime}=\varphi(s),

as required.∎

-admissible pair. Let
f:\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}
is the bijection induced by this pair. By definition,
f
satisfies conditions (
24
). Hence,
f\in\iso({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime},\varphi)
by Theorem
5.2
. Since also
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha^{f_{1}}=\alpha^{f}
and
f^{\Delta}=f_{1}
, we are done.∎

Corollary 5.4
.

In the hypothesis and notation of Lemma
5.3
, the following statements are equivalent for all
\alpha\in\Omega
and
\alpha^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}
:

(1)

\iso_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime},\varphi)\neq\varnothing
,

(2)

\iso_{\alpha_{0},\alpha^{\prime}_{0}}({\cal X}_{0},{\cal X}^{\prime}_{0},\varphi_{0})\neq\varnothing
,

(3)

\iso_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}({\cal X}_{1},{\cal X}^{\prime}_{1},\varphi_{1})\neq\varnothing
.

Corollary 5.5
.

In the hypothesis and notations of Lemma
5.3
, assume that
{\cal X}={\cal X}^{\prime}
and
\varphi=\id
. Then
{\cal X}
is schurian if and only if
{\cal X}_{0}
is schurian and
{\cal X}_{1}
is schurian for some
\alpha
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the “if” part only. Assume that
{\cal X}_{0}
is schurian and
{\cal X}_{1}
is schurian for some
\alpha\in\Omega
. Since
\varphi=\id
, we have
{\cal X}^{\prime}_{0}={\cal X}_{0}
(but not necessarily
{\cal X}^{\prime}_{1}={\cal X}_{1}
, because, in general,
\alpha\neq\alpha^{\prime}
). Let
\alpha^{\prime}\in\Omega
. By the schurity of
{\cal X}_{0}
, there exists
f\in\aut({\cal X}_{0})
such that
(\alpha_{0})^{f}=\alpha^{\prime}_{0}
, or, equivalently,

\iso_{\alpha_{0},\alpha^{\prime}_{0}}({\cal X}_{0},{\cal X}_{0},\id)\neq\varnothing.

By Lemma
5.3
(i), this yields
\iso_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}({\cal X},{\cal X},\id)\neq\varnothing
. Since
\alpha^{\prime}
is arbitrary, this means that the group
\aut({\cal X})=\iso({\cal X},{\cal X},\id)
is transitive. Moreover, by statements (i) and (ii) of Lemma
5.3
for
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}
, we have, respectively,

(\aut({\cal X})_{\alpha})^{\Omega_{0}}=\aut({\cal X}_{0})_{\alpha_{0}}\quad\text{and}\quad(\aut({\cal X})_{\alpha})^{\Delta}=\aut({\cal X}_{1})_{\alpha}.

By the transitivity of
\aut({\cal X})
and the schurity of
{\cal X}_{0}
and
{\cal X}_{1}
, this implies that
{\cal X}
is schurian.∎

5.4.
Concrete sufficient condition

The key point in our general sufficiency condition established in Lemma
5.3
is equality (
25
). In general, checking this equality is not an easy task. The lemma below gives a more or less simple criterion to be used in Section
6
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
{\cal X}
be a sesquiclosed commutative scheme on
\Omega
, satisfying the
e_{1}/e_{0}
-condition,
{\cal X}^{\prime}
a scheme on
\Omega^{\prime}
, and
\varphi:{\cal X}\to{\cal X}^{\prime}
a sesquiclosed algebraic isomorphism. In the notation of Lemma
5.3
, assume that for
\alpha\in\Omega
and
\alpha^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}
,

(i)

{\cal X}_{0}
and
{\cal X}_{1}
are schurian,
\iso({\cal X}_{0},{\cal X}^{\prime}_{0},\varphi_{0})\neq\varnothing\neq\iso({\cal X}_{1},{\cal X}^{\prime}_{1},\varphi_{1})
,

(ii)

(({\cal X}_{0})_{\alpha_{0}})_{\Delta_{0}}
and
(({\cal X}_{1})_{\alpha})_{\Delta_{0}}
are partly regular.

Then equality (
25
) holds.

Proof.

Let
f_{0}\in\iso({\cal X}_{0},{\cal X}^{\prime}_{0},\varphi_{0})
. Because
{\cal X}_{0}
is a schurian scheme, we may assume that
(\alpha_{0})^{f_{0}}=\alpha^{\prime}_{0}
. Then the algebraic isomorphism
\psi_{0}
induced by
f_{0}
is
the
(\alpha_{0},\alpha^{\prime}_{0})
-extension of the algebraic isomorphism
\varphi_{0}
. It easily follows that

(26)

\iso_{\alpha_{0},\alpha^{\prime}_{0}}({\cal X}_{0},{\cal X}^{\prime}_{0},\varphi_{0})^{\Delta_{0}}=\iso(({\cal X}_{0})_{\alpha_{0}},({\cal X}^{\prime}_{0})_{\alpha^{\prime}_{0}},\psi_{0})^{\Delta_{0}}.

Next, by the first parts of conditions (i) and (ii), the scheme
{\cal X}_{0}
and algebraic isomorphism
\varphi_{0}
satisfy the hypothesis of Lemma
4.9
for
{\mathfrak{S}}=\Delta_{0}
,
{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}=\Delta^{\prime}_{0}
, and
\alpha=\alpha_{0}
. Therefore,

(27)

\iso(({\cal X}_{0})_{\alpha_{0}},({\cal X}^{\prime}_{0})_{\alpha^{\prime}_{0}},\psi_{0})^{\Delta_{0}}=\iso((({\cal X}_{0})_{\alpha_{0}})_{\Delta_{0}},(({\cal X}^{\prime}_{0})_{\alpha^{\prime}_{0}})_{\Delta^{\prime}_{0}},(\psi_{0})_{\Delta_{0},\Delta^{\prime}_{0}}).

Furthermore, the scheme
{\cal X}
, sections
{\mathfrak{S}}=\Omega_{0}
,
{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}=\Delta_{0}
, and point
\alpha
satisfy the hypothesis of Lemma
4.8
. Therefore,

(28)

(({\cal X}_{0})_{\alpha_{0}})_{\Delta_{0}}=(({\cal X}_{\alpha})_{\Omega_{0}})_{\Delta_{0}}=({\cal X}_{\alpha})_{\Delta_{0}}.

By Lemma
4.7
, the scheme
{\cal X}^{\prime}
is sesquiclosed. Furthermore, the isomorphism
f_{0}
induces an isomorphism from
(({\cal X}_{0})_{\alpha_{0}})_{\Delta_{0}}
to the coherent configuration
(({\cal X}^{\prime}_{0})_{\alpha^{\prime}_{0}})_{\Delta^{\prime}_{0}}
, which is therefore partly regular. Thus, the scheme
{\cal X}^{\prime}
, sections
{\mathfrak{S}}=\Omega^{\prime}_{0}
,
{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}=\Delta^{\prime}_{0}
, and point
\alpha^{\prime}
satisfy the hypothesis of Lemma
4.8
, whence

(29)

(({\cal X}^{\prime}_{0})_{\alpha^{\prime}_{0}})_{\Delta_{0}}=(({\cal X}^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}})_{\Omega^{\prime}_{0}})_{\Delta^{\prime}_{0}}.=({\cal X}^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}})_{\Delta^{\prime}_{0}}.

The
(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})
-extension
\varphi_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}
of the (sesquiclosed) algebraic isomorphism
\varphi
takes
\alpha_{0}
to
\alpha^{\prime}_{0}
. Therefore,
\varphi_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}
extends
\varphi_{0}
and takes
\Delta_{0}
to
\Delta^{\prime}_{0}
. It follows that the algebraic isomorphism
\psi_{\Delta_{0},\Delta^{\prime}_{0}}
defined as the restriction of
\varphi_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}
to
(({\cal X}_{0})_{\alpha_{0}})_{\Delta}
coincides with
(\psi_{0})_{\Delta_{0},\Delta^{\prime}_{0}}
. With taking
equalities (
28
) and (

) into account, we can continue formula (

) as follows:

\iso((({\cal X}_{0})_{\alpha_{0}})_{\Delta_{0}},(({\cal X}^{\prime}_{0})_{\alpha^{\prime}_{0}})_{\Delta^{\prime}_{0}},(\psi_{0})_{\Delta_{0},\Delta^{\prime}_{0}})=\iso(({\cal X}_{\alpha})_{\Delta_{0}},({\cal X}^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}})_{\Delta^{\prime}_{0}},\psi_{\Delta_{0},\Delta^{\prime}_{0}}).

This together with (

), yields

\iso_{\alpha_{0},\alpha^{\prime}_{0}}({\cal X}_{0},{\cal X}^{\prime}_{0},\varphi_{0})^{\Delta_{0}}=\iso(({\cal X}_{\alpha})_{\Delta_{0}},({\cal X}^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}})_{\Delta^{\prime}_{0}},\psi_{\Delta_{0},\Delta^{\prime}_{0}}).

Arguing in a similar way with

and

and

, we obtain

Comparing the expressions on the right-hand sides of the last two equalities, we get (

).∎

6.
Circulant sesquiseparable schemes

6.1.
Circulant schemes

In this subsection, we follow
[
2
, Sections 2.4 and 4.4]
. Let
G
be a cyclic group. A coherent configuration
{\cal X}
on the elements of
G
is said to be
circulant
if for every
g\in G
, the permutation

\rho_{g}:x\mapsto xg,\ x\in G,

is an automorphism of
{\cal X}
; in particular,
{\cal X}
is a commutative scheme. There is a one-to-one correspondence between circulant schemes and the Schur rings over cyclic groups (see, e.g.,
[
5
]
). The reader should not be embarrassed that most of the results cited in the present paper were formulated and proved in the language of Schur rings. In the rest of this subsection, we will change our terminology slightly to match that used for circulant schemes (and Schur rings). In the sequel,
{\cal X}
is a circulant scheme on
G
,
S=S({\cal X})
,
E=E({\cal X})
, and
\alpha=1_{G}
.

Every group
H\leq G
defines an equivalence relation
e=e(H)
on
G
, the classes of which are the cosets of
H
in
G
. In particular,
e(\alpha)=\diag(G\times G)
and
e(G)=G\times G
. Note that
H=\alpha e
and
H\leq H^{\prime}
if and only if
e(H)\subseteq e(H^{\prime})
.

We say that
H
is an
{\cal X}
-group
if
e(H)\in E
. The mapping
H\mapsto e(H)
defines a one-to-one correspondence between the
{\cal X}
-groups and parabolics of
{\cal X}
. The parabolics
\langle{s}\rangle
and
\rad(s)
, where
s\in S
, correspond to
e(H)
with
H=\langle{\alpha s}\rangle
and
H=\{h\in G:\ (\alpha s)h=\alpha s\}
, respectively. Note that the parabolic
\rad(s)=e(H)
, and hence the group
H
, does not depend on the relation
s=r(\alpha,g)
, where
g
is a generator of
G
. This group is called the
radical
of
{\cal X}
and is denoted by
\rad({\cal X})
.

In the context of circulant schemes, we are interested in those sections
{\mathfrak{S}}=\Delta/e
for which
\alpha\in\Delta
. In this case,
\Delta=U
and
e=e(L)
for some
{\cal X}
-groups
U
and
L
such that
L\leq U
; in particular,
{\mathfrak{S}}=U/L
. The set of all such sections is denoted by
{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}({\cal X})
. It is important to note that every
{\mathfrak{S}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}({\cal X})
is treated as a cyclic group and the scheme
{\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}}
is treated as a circulant scheme on
{\mathfrak{S}}
. We say that a section
{\mathfrak{S}}
is
trivial
(respectively,
primitive
) if the scheme
{\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}}
is primitive (respectively, contains no proper
{\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}}
-groups). It is known that every primitive section of composite order is trivial.

6.2.
Normal circulant schemes

The scheme
{\cal X}
is said to be
normal
if the group
\langle{\rho_{g}:\ g\in G}\rangle
is normal in
\aut({\cal X})
, or equivalently,
\aut({\cal X})_{\alpha}\leq\aut(G)
. This condition is always satisfied when
G
is of prime order and
{\cal X}
is not trivial.

Lemma 6.1
.

[
5
, Theorem 6.1 and Corollary 6.2]

Every normal circulant scheme
{\cal X}
is schurian. Moreover, any one-point extension of
{\cal X}
is partly regular.

There are normal circulant schemes that are not separable, see, e.g.,
[
2
, Section 4.5]
. However, from Lemmas
6.1
and
2.2
, it follows that every normal circulant scheme is schurian and any one-point extension of it is separable. By Lemma
4.4
, this proves the following statement.

Corollary 6.2
.

Every normal circulant scheme is sesquiseparable.

The following auxiliary statement is crucial for the proof of Theorem
6.4
below, because it enables us to verify condition (ii) in Lemma
5.6
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
{\cal X}
be a normal circulant scheme and
\alpha=1_{G}
. Then the coherent configuration
({\cal X}_{\alpha})_{\mathfrak{S}}
is partly regular for every
{\mathfrak{S}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}({\cal X})
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathfrak{S}}=U/L
, where
U
and
L
are
{\cal X}
-subgroups, and let
u
be a generator of
U
. It suffices to verify that
\bar{u}=uL
is a regular point of the coherent configuration
({\cal X}_{\alpha})_{\mathfrak{S}}
. Assume on the contrary that there exist
x,x^{\prime}\in U
such that

(30)

\bar{x}\neq\bar{x}^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{r}(\bar{x},\bar{u})=\bar{r}(\bar{x}^{\prime},\bar{u}),

where
\bar{x}=xL
,
\bar{x}^{\prime}=x^{\prime}L
, and for all
\overline{y},\overline{z}\in{\mathfrak{S}}
we denote by
\bar{r}(\overline{y},\overline{z})
the basis relation of the coherent configuration
({\cal X}_{\alpha})_{\mathfrak{S}}
, containing the pair
(\overline{y},\overline{z})
. By the second equality in (
30
), there is
u^{\prime}\in\bar{u}
such that

r(x,u)=r(x^{\prime},u^{\prime}).

The normality of
{\cal X}
implies that every element of
\aut({\cal X}_{\alpha})=\aut({\cal X})_{\alpha}
is induced by raising to a power coprime to the order
n
of the underlying cyclic group
G
. Furthermore, the coherent configuration
{\cal X}_{\alpha}
is partly regular by Lemma
6.1
and hence is schurian by Lemma
2.2
. Thus there is an integer
m
coprime to
n
and such that

x^{m}=x^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad u^{m}=u^{\prime}.

In particular,
\bar{x}^{m}=\bar{x}^{\prime}
and
\bar{u}^{m}=\bar{u}^{\prime}=\bar{u}
. On the other hand, since
u
is a generator of
U\geq L
, there is an integer
a
for which
x=u^{a}
. Furthermore,
(uL)^{m}=\bar{u}^{m}=\bar{u}=uL
and hence
u^{m}=yu
for some
y\in L
. Thus for every
\ell\in L
, we have

(x\ell)^{m}=(u^{a}\ell)^{m}=(u^{a})^{m}\ell^{m}=(u^{m})^{a}\ell^{m}=(yu)^{a}\ell^{m}=u^{a}(y^{a}\ell^{m})=x\ell^{\prime},

where the element
\ell^{\prime}=y^{a}\ell^{m}
belongs to
L
. Consequently,
\bar{x}^{m}=\bar{x}
, implying
\bar{x}^{\prime}=\bar{x}^{m}=\bar{x}
in contrast to the first inequality in (
30
).∎

Let
{\cal X}
be an arbitrary circulant scheme. A section
{\mathfrak{S}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}({\cal X})
is said to be
normal
if the (circulant) scheme
{\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}}
is normal, and
subnormal
if there exists a normal section
{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}({\cal X})
such that
{\mathfrak{S}}\leq{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}
. Note that every normal section is subnormal and any primitive section of composite order greater than
4
is not subnormal.

6.3.
The
{\mathfrak{S}}
-wreath product of circulant schemes

Let
{\cal X}
be a circulant scheme and
{\mathfrak{S}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}({\cal X})
. Assume that
{\cal X}
satisfies the
e(U)/e(L)
-condition, where
U
and
L
are
{\cal X}
-groups such that
{\mathfrak{S}}=U/L
. In this case, we say that
{\cal X}
is the
{\mathfrak{S}}
-wreath product of schemes
{\cal X}_{1}={\cal X}_{U}
and
{\cal X}_{0}={\cal X}_{G/L}
. The
{\mathfrak{S}}
-wreath product is nontrivial if
1<L\leq U<G
, i.e., if
{\cal X}
satisfies the
e(U)/e(L)
-condition nontrivially. When
U=L
, we say that
{\cal X}
is a wreath product.

The main result of this subsection is to establish a simple criterion for the
{\mathfrak{S}}
-wreath product to be schurian and sesquiseparable.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
{\cal X}
be a circulant sesquiclosed scheme on
G
. Assume that
{\cal X}
is the
{\mathfrak{S}}
-wreath product of (circulant) schemes
{\cal X}_{0}
and
{\cal X}_{1}
for some
{\mathfrak{S}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}
, and also

(1)

the section
{\mathfrak{S}}
is subnormal in both
{\cal X}_{0}
and
{\cal X}_{1}
,

(2)

the schemes
{\cal X}_{0}
and
{\cal X}_{1}
are schurian and sesquiseparable.

Then
{\cal X}
is schurian and sesquiseparable.

Proof.

By condition (1), the section
{\mathfrak{S}}
is contained in certain sections
{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}\in{\mathfrak{S}}({\cal X}_{0})
and
{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}\in{\mathfrak{S}}({\cal X}_{1})
such that the schemes

{\cal X}_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}}=({\cal X}_{0})_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}}\quad\text{and}\quad{\cal X}_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}}=({\cal X}_{1})_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}}

are normal circulant. By Lemma
6.1
, the one-point extensions
({\cal X}_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}})_{\alpha}
and
({\cal X}_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}})_{\beta}
are partly regular, where
\alpha=1_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}}
and
\beta=1_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}}
treated as points of the schemes
{\cal X}_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}}
and
{\cal X}_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}}
, respectively.
By Lemma
6.3
, the coherent configurations

(({\cal X}_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}})_{\alpha})_{\mathfrak{S}}\leq(({\cal X}_{0})_{\alpha})_{\mathfrak{S}}\quad\text{and}\quad(({\cal X}_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}})_{\beta})_{\mathfrak{S}}\leq(({\cal X}_{1})_{\beta})_{\mathfrak{S}}

are partly regular. It follows that so are the coherent configurations
(({\cal X}_{0})_{\alpha})_{\mathfrak{S}}
and
(({\cal X}_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}})_{\beta})_{\mathfrak{S}}
. Consequently, condition (ii) of Lemma
5.6
is satisfied for
e_{0}=e(L)
,
e_{1}=e(U)
, and
\alpha=1_{G}
. Since
\aut({\cal X})
is transitive, this condition is true for all points of
{\cal X}
.

The schemes
{\cal X}_{0}
and
{\cal X}_{1}
are schurian by the first part of condition (2). Thus condition (i) of Lemma
5.6
is satisfied for
\varphi=\id
(the condition
\iso({\cal X}_{1},{\cal X}^{\prime}_{1},\varphi_{1})\neq\varnothing
is true, because the group
\aut({\cal X})
is transitive). By that lemma, equality (
25
) holds for all
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
, and hence the scheme
{\cal X}
satisfies the hypothesis of Corollary
5.5
. Thus,
{\cal X}
is schurian.

Let
\varphi:{\cal X}\to{\cal X}^{\prime}
be a sesquiclosed algebraic isomorphism. Then the algebraic isomorphisms
\varphi_{0}
and
\varphi_{1}
are also sesquiclosed by Lemma
4.3
. By the second part of condition (2), this implies that
\iso({\cal X}_{0},{\cal X}^{\prime}_{0},\varphi_{0})\neq\varnothing
, and
\iso({\cal X}_{1},{\cal X}^{\prime}_{1},\varphi_{1})\neq\varnothing
. Thus condition (i) of Lemma
5.6
is satisfied. By that lemma, equality (
25
) holds for all
\alpha\in\Omega
and
\alpha^{\prime}\in\Omega
. Thus,
\iso({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime},\varphi)\neq\varnothing
by Corollary
5.4
, and hence
{\cal X}
is sesquiseparable.∎

The conditions of Theorem
6.4
are always true when the circulant schemes
{\cal X}_{0}
and
{\cal X}_{1}
are normal. Thus this theorem implies the following statement.

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
{\cal X}
be a circulant sesquiclosed scheme on
G
. Assume that
{\cal X}
is the
{\mathfrak{S}}
-wreath product for some
{\mathfrak{S}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}({\cal X})
such that the both operands are normal. Then
{\cal X}
is schurian and sesquiseparable.

6.4.
Circulant schemes of prime power degree

In this subsection,
{\cal X}
is a circulant scheme on a cyclic
p
-group
G
. The structure of
{\cal X}
has been completely described in
[
17
]
and
[
10
]
for
p\geq 3
and
p=2
, respectively. In particular,
{\cal X}
is schurian. Another important consequence of the description is the radical monotonicity for
p\geq 3
, namely,

(31)

\langle{s}\rangle\leq\langle{t}\rangle\quad\Rightarrow\quad\rad(s)\leq\rad(t).

for all
s,t\in S
. Using this fact, we show in the lemma below that the wreath product can be lifted from any section of
{\cal X}
.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
p\geq 3
and
{\mathfrak{S}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}({\cal X})
. If
{\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}}
is a nontrivial wreath product, then so is
{\cal X}
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathfrak{S}}=U/L
and
{\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}}
a nontrivial wreath product. Then there is an
{\cal X}
-subgroup
H
such that
L<H<U
and for every
s\in S
,

s\subseteq e(U)\setminus e(H)\quad\Rightarrow\quad e(H/L)\subseteq\rad(s_{\mathfrak{S}}),

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
H
is the only
{\cal X}
-subgroup strictly between
L
and
U
(here we use the fact that
G
is a cyclic
p
-group and hence the
{\cal X}
-groups are linearly ordered with respect to inclusion). It suffices to verify that

(32)

s\subseteq e(U)\setminus e(H)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\rad(s)\geq e(H).

Indeed, then by formula (
31
), we have
\rad(s)\geq e(H)
for all
s\in S
not contained in
e_{H}
. But this means that
{\cal X}
satisfies the
e(H)/e(H)
-condition, i.e.,
{\cal X}
is a nontrivial wreath product.

Suppose on the contrary that (
32
) is not true for some
s
. Then the
{\cal X}
-subgroup
L^{\prime}
, for which
\rad(s)=e(L^{\prime})
, is strictly contained in
H
. By the assumption on
H
, this implies that
L^{\prime}<L
, and hence
{\mathfrak{S}}
is a subsection of
{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}=U/L^{\prime}
. Note that

\rad({\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}})\neq 1\quad\text{and}\quad\rad({\cal X}_{{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}})=1.

However, this contradicts the fact (see
[
3
, Corollary 7.4]
) that if a circulant scheme (in our case
{\cal X}_{{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}}
) of odd prime power degree is of trivial radical, then every its section (in our case
{\mathfrak{S}}
) is also of trivial radical.
∎

In the following lemma, we collect some known properties of circulant schemes of prime power degree, that will be used in the proof of Theorem
6.8
. Below, we say that a circulant scheme
{\cal X}
is
dense
if
{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}({\cal X})
contains no trivial section of composite order.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
{\cal X}
be a circulant scheme of prime power degree. Then

(1)

if
\rad({\cal X})=1
, then
{\cal X}
is trivial or normal,

(2)

if
\rad({\cal X})\neq 1
and
{\cal X}
is dense, then
{\cal X}
is a nontrivial
{\mathfrak{S}}
-wreath product, and also
\rad({\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}})=1
or
|{\mathfrak{S}}|=4
,

(3)

if
{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}({\cal X})
contains a proper primitive non-subnormal section, then
{\cal X}
is a nontrivial wreath product.

Proof.

Statements (1) and (3) are consequences of
[
5
, Corollary 6.4]
, and the statement cited in
[
8
, p.29]
(and proved in
[
7
, Theorem 4.6]
), specified for schemes of prime power degree. Statement (2) is exactly
[
8
, Lemma 6.1]
.
∎

Now we are ready to prove the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 6.8
.

Every circulant scheme of prime power degree is sesquiseparable.

Proof.

Let
{\cal X}
be a circulant scheme of degree
p^{n}
, where
p
is a prime and
n\geq 1
. Assume first that
{\cal X}
is trivial or normal. In the former case,
{\cal X}
is separable and hence sesquiseparable. In the latter case,
{\cal X}
is sesquiseparable by Corollary
6.2
.

Claim.

Let
{\cal X}
be neither trivial nor normal. Then
{\cal X}
is a nontrivial

Abstract
. We present a general procedure to construct examples of convex scalar variational problems which admit a minimizers with large singular sets. The dimension of the set of singularities is maximal and the minimizer has no higher integrability property (failure of Meyers property).

1.
Introduction

We construct new examples of irregular minimizers for nonautomous integrands with non-standard growth conditions

\mathcal{F}(u):=\int\limits_{\Omega}\varphi(x,{\lvert\nabla u\rvert})\,dx\to\min.

Here
u
takes prescribed (smooth) boundary value on
\partial\Omega
, where
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Our examples of potentials with non-standard growth include the double phase energy
\varphi(x,t)=t^{p}+a(x)t^{q}
and the
p(x)
-energy
\varphi(x,t)=t^{p(x)}
. Fonseca, Malý and Mingione studied in
[
FMM04
]
the size of possible singular sets for minimizers of the double phase potential. They constructed for every
\varepsilon>0
a minimizer for a double phase energy with
a\in C^{\alpha}(\Omega)
and

(1)

\displaystyle 1<p<d<d+\alpha<q<\infty,

which has the Hausdorff dimension of the set of non-Lebesgue points larger than
d-p-\varepsilon
. This result is almost sharp in the sense that the Hausdorff dimension of the singular set of any
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
function is at most
d-p
.

In this paper we improve these results and extend them also to other models.
First, we construct a double phase energy and a minimizer with the optimal Hausdorff dimension of the singular set
\Sigma
in the sense that

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\Sigma)=d-p.

This is the largest possible value for any
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
function. In such a way we get rid of the
\varepsilon>0
in
[
FMM04
]
. Second, we get rid of the dimensional threshold
p<d<q
by using the fractal Cantor set barriers from
[
BDS20
]
. Our results hold in the range

\displaystyle\ q>p+\alpha\max\left\{1,\frac{p-1}{d-1}\right\}.

These bounds are sharp in view of the regularity results of
[
CM15
,
BCM18
]
as explained in
[
BDS20
, Remark 35]
. For
1<p\leq d
we obtain a minimizer with a large singular set, and for all
p>1
we obtain minimizers without Meyers property. This means that
\nabla v\not\in L^{s}(\Omega)
for any
s>p
.

In
[
Zhi86
]
,
[
Zhi95
]
Zhikov for the two-dimensional case and
p<2<q
constructed an example exhibiting the Lavrentiev phenomenon, i.e.

(2)

\inf\mathcal{F}(W^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega))<\inf\mathcal{F}(C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)).

This example, known in the literature as one-saddle point example, allows also to to construct examples of singular minimizers of functionals with coefficients having a cone-type singularity. Later this construction was used in
[
ELM04
]
to design one-saddle point examples for any dimension under the condition (
1
). In
[
FMM04
]
Fonseca, Mingione and Malý used this set-up and an iterative process to build local minimizers having fractal singular sets under the same condition (
1
). The restriction
p<d<q
is known as
the dimensional threshold
.

Let us mention higher regularity results for double phase model. In fact, if
\frac{q}{p}\leq 1+\frac{\alpha}{d}

and
a\in C^{0,\alpha}
, then minimizers of
\mathcal{F}
are
automatically in
W^{1,q}
, see
[
CM15
]
. Moreover, bounded
minimizers of
\mathcal{F}
are automatically
W^{1,q}

if
a\in C^{0,\alpha}
and
q\leq p+\alpha
,
see
[
BCM18
]
. If the minimizer is from
C^{0,\gamma}
, then
the requirement can be relaxed to
q\leq p+\frac{\alpha}{1-\gamma}
, see

[
BCM18
, Theorem 1.4]
.

In
[
BDS20
]
the authors of the present paper presented new examples for the Lavrentiev gap and non-density of smooth functions without the dimensional threshold restriction by using fractals.
We applied our method to the setting of variable exponents
\varphi(x,t)=t^{p(x)}
, the double phase potential and weighted integrand
\varphi(x,t)=a(x)t^{2}
. Later Balci and Surnachev in
[
BS21
]
applied the same construction to the model

\displaystyle\varphi(x,t)=t^{p}\log^{-\beta}(e+t)+a(x)t^{p}\log^{\alpha}(e+t).

The regularity properties of the integrand of this type for
\varPhi(x,t)
with
\beta=0
were studied firstly by Baroni, Colombo, Mingione in
[
BCM15
]
where it was called
the borderline case of double phase potential
. In particular, they obtained the
C^{0,\gamma}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}
regularity result for the minimizers provided that the weight
a(x)
is
\log
-Hölder continuous (with some
\gamma
) and a more strong result (any
\gamma\in(0,1)
) for the case of vanishing
\log
-Hölder continuous weight.

The variable exponent model
\varphi(x,t)=t^{p(x)}
was studied in numerous papers. We refer to the monographs
[
Zhi11
,
DHHR11
,
CF13
,
KMRS16
]
. The results (with a few exceptions) mainly concern the case of log-Hölder exponents. For the variable exponent model the Hölder regularity of solutions, a Harnack type inequality for non-negative solutions, and boundary regularity results were obtained in
[
Alk97
,
Alk05
,
AK04
]
. Gradient regularity for Hölder exponent was obtained by
[
CM99
]
and for the log-Hölder exponents by
[
AM01
]
and Ok
[
Ok17
]
. These papers as well as
[
Zhi97
]
also contain Meyer type properties. For a class of exponents with the modulus of continuity slightly worse than
\log
-Hölder, a logarithmic Meyers property was obtained in
[
ZP08
]
.

Let us also mention, that computing such type of minimizers numerically is a challenging problem, since the
standard numerical schemes fail to converge to the
W
-minimiser of the problem. For the scalar case the problem could be solved using non-conforming methods, see Balci, Ortner, and Storn
[
BOS22
]
.

1.1.
Models

We consider integral functionals of the form

\mathcal{F}(u)=\int\limits_{\Omega}\varphi(x,|\nabla u|)\,dx

and treat the following models.

•

The double phase energy: for
1<p<q<\infty
and a nonnegative weight
a=a(x)
we define

(3)

\begin{gathered}\varphi(x,t)=\frac{t^{p}}{p}+a(x)\frac{t^{q}}{q},\\
\mathcal{F}(u)=\int\limits_{\Omega}\bigg(\frac{|\nabla u|^{p}}{p}+a(x)\frac{|\nabla u|^{q}}{q}\bigg)\,dx.\end{gathered}

•

The borderline double phase: for
1<p<\infty
, a nonnegative weight
a=a(x)
, and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
we define

(4)

\begin{gathered}\varphi(x,t)=t^{p}\log^{-\beta}(e+t)+a(x)t^{p}\log^{\alpha}(e+t),\\
\mathcal{F}(u)=\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\bigl(\log^{-\beta}(e+|\nabla u|)+a(x)\log^{\alpha}(e+|\nabla u|)\bigr)\,dx.\end{gathered}

•

The
p(\cdot)
-energy: for
1<p_{-}<p_{+}<\infty
let
p:\Omega\to[p_{-},p{+}]
be a measurable function. Then we define

(5)

\varphi(x,t)=\frac{t^{p(x)}}{p(x)},\quad\mathcal{F}(u)=\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}\,dx.

follows from Theorems

Definitions and auxiliary results

In this section we describe the functional setup for our variational problems and recall the construction of generalized Cantor sets.

2.1.
Orlicz functions and functional spaces

We say that
\varphi\,:\,[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
is an Orlicz
function if
\varphi
is convex, left-continuous,
\varphi(0)=0
,

\lim\limits_{t\to 0}\varphi(t)=0
and

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\varphi(t)=\infty
. The conjugate Orlicz
function
\varphi^{*}
is defined by

\displaystyle\varphi^{*}(s)

\displaystyle:=\sup_{t\geq 0}\big(st-\varphi(t)\big).

In particular,
st\leq\varphi(t)+\varphi^{*}(s)
.

In the following we assume that

\varphi\,:\,\Omega\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
is a generalized
Orlicz function, i.e.
\varphi(x,\cdot)
is an Orlicz function for
every
x\in\Omega
and
\varphi(\cdot,t)
is measurable for
every
t\geq 0
. We define the conjugate function
\varphi^{*}
pointwise,
i.e.
\varphi^{*}(x,\cdot):=(\varphi(x,\cdot))^{*}
.

We further assume the following additional properties:

•

We assume that
\varphi
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition,
i.e. there exists
c\geq 2
such that for
all
x\in\Omega
and all
t\geq 0

(8)

\displaystyle\varphi(x,2t)

\displaystyle\leq c\,\varphi(x,t).

•

We assume that
\varphi
satisfies the
\nabla_{2}
-condition,
i.e.
\varphi^{*}
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition. As a consequence,
there exist
s>1
and
c>0
such that for all
x\in\Omega
,

t\geq 0
and
\gamma\in[0,1]
there holds

(9)

\displaystyle\varphi(x,\gamma t)\leq c\,\gamma^{s}\,\varphi(x,t).

•

We assume that
\varphi
and
\varphi^{*}
are proper, i.e. for
every
t\geq 0
there holds
\int\limits_{\Omega}\varphi(x,t)\,dx<\infty
and

\int\limits_{\Omega}\varphi^{*}(x,t)\,dx<\infty
.

Let
L^{0}(\Omega)
denote the set of measurable function on
\Omega

and
L^{1}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)
denote the space of locally integrable
functions.
We define the generalized Orlicz norm by

\displaystyle{\lVert f\rVert}_{{\varphi(\cdot)}}

\displaystyle:=\inf{\bigg\{{\gamma>0\,:\,\int\limits_{\Omega}\varphi(x,{\lvert f(x)/\gamma\rvert})\,dx\leq 1}\bigg\}}.

Then generalized Orlicz space
L^{{{{\varphi(\cdot)}}}}(\Omega)
is defined as the
set of all measurable functions with finite generalized Orlicz norm

\displaystyle L^{{{{\varphi(\cdot)}}}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:={\big\{{f\in L^{0}(\Omega)\,:\,{\lVert f\rVert}_{{{\varphi(\cdot)}}}<\infty}\big\}}.

For example the generalized Orlicz function
\varphi(x,t)=t^{p}

generates the usual Lebesgue space
L^{p}(\Omega)
.

The
\Delta_{2}
-condition of
\varphi
and
\varphi^{*}
ensures that our space
is uniformly convex. The condition that
\varphi
and
\varphi^{*}
are proper
ensure that
L^{{{{\varphi(\cdot)}}}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{1}(\Omega)
and

L^{{\varphi^{*}(\cdot)}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{1}(\Omega)
. Thus
L^{{{{\varphi(\cdot)}}}}(\Omega)

and
L^{{\varphi^{*}(\cdot)}}(\Omega)
are Banach spaces.

We define the generalized Orlicz-Sobolev space
W^{1,{{{\varphi(\cdot)}}}}
as

\displaystyle W^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:={\{{w\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)\,:\,\nabla w\in L^{\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega)}\}},

with the norm

\displaystyle{\lVert w\rVert}_{1,\varphi(\cdot)}:={\lVert w\rVert}_{1}+{\lVert\nabla w\rVert}_{\varphi(\cdot)}.

In general smooth functions are not dense
in
W^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega)
. Therefore, we define
H^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega)

as

\displaystyle H^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=\big(\text{closure of}\ C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega)\ \text{in}\ W^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega)\big).

See
[
DHHR11
]
and
[
HH19
]
for further properties of
these spaces.

We also introduce the corresponding spaces with zero boundary values
as

\displaystyle W^{1,{{{\varphi(\cdot)}}}}_{0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:={\{{w\in W^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)\,:\,\nabla w\in L^{\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega)}\}}

with same norm as in
W^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega)
. And the corresponding space of smooth functions is defined as

\displaystyle H_{0}^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=\big(\text{closure of}\ C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega)\ \text{in}\ W^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega)\big).

The space
W_{0}^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega)
are exactly those function, which can
be extended by zero to
W^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
functions.

2.2.
Variational problems and the Lavrentiev phenomenon

Let us define the energy
\mathcal{F}\,:\,W^{1,{{{\varphi(\cdot)}}}}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(w)

\displaystyle:=\int\limits_{\Omega}\varphi(x,{\lvert\nabla w(x)\rvert})\,dx.

In the language of function spaces
\mathcal{F}
is a semi-modular
on
W^{1,{{{\varphi(\cdot)}}}}(\Omega)
and a modular on
W^{1,{{{\varphi(\cdot)}}}}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Let us start by introducing spaces with boundary values: for

g\in H^{1,{{{\varphi(\cdot)}}}}(\Omega)
we define

\displaystyle H_{g}^{1,{{{\varphi(\cdot)}}}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=g+H_{0}^{1,{{{\varphi(\cdot)}}}}(\Omega).

Let

\displaystyle h_{H}(g)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{arg\,min}}\mathcal{F}\big(H^{1,{{{\varphi(\cdot)}}}}_{g}(\Omega)\big).

Then

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\varphi(\cdot)}h_{H}(g):=-\operatorname{div}\bigg(\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(x,{\lvert\nabla h_{H}(g)\rvert})}{{\lvert\nabla h_{H}(g)\rvert}}\nabla h_{H}(g)\bigg)

\displaystyle=0\qquad\text{ in }(H^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}_{0}(\Omega))^{*}.

For
g\in W^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega)
we define

\displaystyle W_{g}^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=g+W_{0}^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega).

Let

h_{W}(g)=\mathop{\mathrm{arg\,min}}\mathcal{F}\big(W^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}_{g}(\Omega)\big).

Formally, it satisfies the Euler-Lagrange equation (in the weak sense)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{{{\varphi(\cdot)}}}h_{W}(g):=-\operatorname{div}\bigg(\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(x,{\lvert\nabla h_{W}(g)\rvert})}{{\lvert\nabla h_{W}(g)\rvert}}\nabla h_{W}(g)\bigg)

\displaystyle=0\qquad\text{ in }(W^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}_{0}(\Omega))^{*},

where
\varphi^{\prime}(x,t)
is the derivative with respect to
t
.

If
\varphi(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}t^{2}
, then
\Delta_{\varphi(\cdot)}
is just the standard Laplacian.
If
\varphi(x,t)=\frac{1}{p}t^{p}
, then
\Delta_{\varphi(\cdot)}
is the
p
-Laplacian.

The Lavrentiev phenomenon is the fact that for
g\in H^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega)

\min\mathcal{F}\big(W^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}_{g}(\Omega)\big)<\min\mathcal{F}\big(H^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}_{g}(\Omega)\big),

and in particular
h_{H}(g)\neq h_{W}(g)
.

In our previous work
[
BDS20
]
we investigated in detail the Lavrentiev phenomenon for several models (variable exponent, double phase model, weighted
p
-energy). In this work we show that under similar assumptions the irregularity of the
W
-minimizer.

2.3.
Generalized Cantor sets and their properties.

Let
\{l_{j},\ j=0,1,2,\ldots\}
be a decreasing sequence of positive numbers starting from
l_{0}=1
:

1=l_{0}>l_{1}>l_{2}>\ldots

such that
l_{j-1}>2l_{j}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. We start from
I_{0,1}=[-1/2,1/2]
. On each
m
-th step we remove the open middle part of length
l_{j}-2l_{j+1}
from the interval
I_{m,j}
,
j=1,\ldots,2^{m}
to obtain the next generation set of closed intervals
I_{m+1,j}
,
j=1,\ldots,2^{m+1}
. The union of the closed intervals
I_{m,j}=[a_{m,j},b_{m,j}]
,
j=1,\ldots,2^{m}
of length
l_{m}
from the same generation forms the pre-Cantor set
C_{m}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{2^{m}}I_{m,j}
. The Cantor set
{\mathfrak{C}}=\bigcap_{m=0}^{\infty}C_{m}
is the intersection of all pre-Cantor sets
C_{m}
.

On each
m
-th step we define the pre-Cantor measure as
\mu_{m}=|C_{m}|^{-1}\mathbbm{1}_{C_{m}}
, where
|C_{m}|=2^{m}l_{m}
is the standard Lebesgue measure of
C_{m}
, and the weak limit of the measures
\mu_{m}
is the Cantor measure
\mu_{{\mathfrak{C}}}
corresponding to
{\mathfrak{C}}
.

We require further that

l_{m-1}-2l_{m}>l_{m}-2l_{m+1}\Leftrightarrow l_{m+1}>\frac{3l_{m}-l_{m-1}}{2},

at least for all sufficiently large
m
.

If the sequence
l_{j}
satisfies the conditions above only for sufficiently large
j\geq j_{0}
, then we modify it by taking the sequence
\tilde{l}_{j}=l_{j+j_{0}}(l_{j_{0}})^{-1}
,
j=0,1,2,\ldots

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
by
{\mathfrak{C}}^{k}
and
\mu^{k}
we denote the Cartesian powers of
k
copies of
{\mathfrak{C}}
and its corresponding Cantor measure, respectively.

Definition 5

(Generalized Cantor sets)
.

Let it be

•

(Generalized
(\lambda,\gamma)
-Cantor sets). Let
l_{j}=\lambda^{j}j^{\gamma}
,
\lambda\in(0,1/2)
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. We denote the corresponding Cantor set by
{\mathfrak{C}}_{\lambda,\gamma}
, the Cartesian product of its
k
copies is
{\mathfrak{C}}^{k}_{\lambda,\gamma}
. For
{\mathfrak{C}}^{k}_{\lambda,\gamma}
we denote
{\mathfrak{D}}=-k\log 2/\log\lambda
, so that
\lambda^{{\mathfrak{D}}}=2^{-k}
. We denote the Cantor measure corresponding to
{\mathfrak{C}}_{\lambda,\gamma}
by
\mu_{\lambda,\gamma}
and its
k
-th Cartesian power by
\mu^{k}_{\lambda,\gamma}
.

•

(Meager Cantor sets). Let
l_{j}=\exp(-2^{j/\gamma})
,
\gamma>0
. Denote the corresponding Cantor set by
{\mathfrak{C}}_{0,\gamma}
, and its Cartesian products by
{\mathfrak{C}}^{k}_{0,\gamma}
. For these sets we denote
{\mathfrak{D}}=0
. We denote the corresponding Cantor measures by
\mu^{k}_{0,\gamma}
.

The standard
1/3
-Cantor set corresponds to the choice
l_{j}=(1/3)^{j}
, in our notation it is
{\mathfrak{C}}_{1/3,0}
, its fractal dimension is
{\mathfrak{D}}=\log_{3}2
and the corresponding measure is
\mu_{1/3,0}
.

Denote
{\mathfrak{C}}_{t}=\{\mathrm{dist}(\bar{x},{\mathfrak{C}})<t\}
, where
{\mathfrak{C}}
is one of the Cantor sets
{\mathfrak{C}}_{\lambda,\gamma}^{k}
or
{\mathfrak{C}}^{k}_{0,\gamma}
defined above. Denote by
d_{\infty}(\bar{x},{\mathfrak{C}})
the distance from
\bar{x}
to
{\mathfrak{C}}
in the maximum norm and let
{\mathfrak{C}}_{*,t}=\{d_{\infty}(\bar{x},{\mathfrak{C}})<t\}
. It is clear that
{\mathfrak{C}}_{t}\subset{\mathfrak{C}}_{*,t}
. In the following lemma
B^{m}_{t}(\bar{x})
is the open ball in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
with center at
\bar{x}
and radius
t
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\lambda\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,
1\leq m\leq d
. Let

{\mathfrak{D}}=-m\log(2)/\log(\lambda)
if
\lambda>0
. We use the notation
x=(\bar{x},\hat{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-m}
.
Then we have the following properties:

•

For every
ball
B^{m}_{r}(\bar{x})
,
r<1
, there
holds

8.
Comparison with the approach of
[
BDS20
]

We want to compare the approach of this paper with the methods of our previous paper
[
BDS20
]
. Though the results of the latter paper concern the Lavrentiev phenomenon and in this paper we are concerned with the irregularity of minimizers the approach is in fact very much alike. Let us demonstrate this on the sub-dimensional case.

Recall that in our proof we show the existence of “upper” and “lower” traces for a function from the domain of the integral functional. Above we implement this only for the minimizer but clearly the same construction can be applied to any function with certain integrability properties of the gradient. In the Section
4.3
and below we worked with averages on balls but we can take in this construction any nonnegative
\omega\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{1/4}^{d}(0))
with
\int\omega\,dx=1
. Then the upper and lower traces of a function
f
, which takes the boundary value
\eta u
at the point
\bar{x}\in{\mathfrak{C}}^{d-1}_{\lambda,\gamma}
are expressed as

f_{\pm}(\bar{x})=\pm\frac{\eta}{2}+\int\limits K^{\pm}(\bar{x},z)\cdot\nabla f(z)\,dz

with the kernel
K^{\pm}(\bar{x},z)\lesssim|z_{d}|^{1-d}\mathbbm{1}_{C^{\pm}(\bar{x},0)}
and is smooth outside the point
(\bar{x},0)
. Integrating this with respect to the corresponding Cantor measure we get

\int(f_{+}(\bar{x})-f_{-}(\bar{x}))\,d\mu(\bar{x})=\eta+\int\limits_{\Omega}\mathbf{b}(z)\cdot\nabla f(z)\,dz

where the kernel
\mathbf{b}
satisfies
|\mathbf{b}|\lesssim b
(here
b
is our auxiliary function) and
\mathbf{b}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus{\mathfrak{C}}^{d-1}_{\lambda,\gamma}\times\{0\})
. By construction
|\mathbf{b}|\cdot|\nabla u|\lesssim b\cdot|\nabla u|=0
outside
{\mathfrak{C}}^{d-1}_{\lambda,\gamma}\times\{0\}
.

Let
\xi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and consider the function
f=\xi+\eta u
. This function assumes the same boundary values as
\eta u
and thus using that the upper and lower traces of the competitor
u
on
{\mathfrak{C}}^{d-1}_{\lambda,\gamma}
are
u_{\pm}=\pm 1/2
we get

\eta=\eta+\int\limits_{\Omega}\mathbf{b}(z)\cdot\nabla\xi(z)\,dz.

This means that the vector field
\mathbf{b}
is solenoidal.

Now let
\xi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
be such that
\xi=1
in a neighborhood of
{\mathfrak{C}}^{d-1}_{\lambda,\gamma}\times\{0\}
. Take
f=(1-\xi)\eta u
. Then
f\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
and

0=\eta+\eta\int\limits_{\Omega}\mathbf{b}u\cdot\nabla(1-\xi)\,dz.

Using that
u=\pm 1/2
on
\mathrm{supp}\,\mathbf{b}\cap\{\pm x_{d}>0\}
and
(1-\xi)\mathbf{b}=0
on
(-1,1)^{d-1}\times\{0\}
, and integrating by parts we get

(42)

\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}u\mathbf{b}\cdot\vec{\nu}\,d\sigma=1.

Thus the vector field
\mathbf{b}
has the same properties as the vector field used in
[
BDS20
]
for constructing examples with the Lavrentiev phenomenon: it is

•

smooth in
\overline{\Omega}\setminus({\mathfrak{C}}^{d-1}_{\lambda,\gamma}\times\{0\})
,

•

solenoidal in the sense of distributions in
\Omega
,

•

satisfies
|\mathbf{b}|\cdot|\nabla u|=0
in
\Omega\setminus({\mathfrak{C}}^{d-1}_{\lambda,\gamma}\times\{0\})
,

•

and satisfies (
42
).

Indeed, for a vector field
\mathbf{b}
with these properties and satisfying
\mathcal{F}^{*}(b)<\infty
consider the functional

\mathcal{S}(f)=\int\limits_{\Omega}\mathbf{b}(z)\cdot\nabla f(z)\,dz.

On smooth functions assuming the boundary value
u
we have
\mathcal{S}(f)=1
(by the Gauss theorem) while
\mathcal{S}(u)=0
, so in
W^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega)
this functional separates the linear span of
u
and the closure of smooth functions. See the remaining details in
[
BDS20
]
.

9.
Relation with the Lavrentiev gap

Next we show that the approach used in this paper can be also applied to establishing the Lavrentiev gap.

Let us state the corresponding conditional result in the spirit of Section
3.2
.This result repeats one of the results in
[
BDS20
]
, but is obtained in a different way.

Let
\xi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
be such that
\xi=1
in a neighborhood of
{\mathfrak{S}}={\mathfrak{C}}^{d-1}_{\lambda,\gamma}\times\{0\}
in the subdimensional case and
{\mathfrak{S}}=\{0\}^{d-1}\times{\mathfrak{C}}_{\lambda,\gamma}
in the superdimensional case. Denote

.

Theorem 51
.

Under Assumption
14
(or Assumption
15
with sufficiently small
\kappa>0
) for some
\eta>0
there holds

(43)

\inf\{\mathcal{F}(v),\ v\in\eta u^{\partial}+C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)\}\geq 2\min\{\mathcal{F}(v),\ v\in\eta u+W_{0}^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega)\}.

Proof.

First we recall the subdimensional case and the argument which led to showing the discontinuity of the minimizer (see Section
4.4
). Let
w_{\eta}\in\eta u^{\partial}+W_{0}^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega)
. Repeating the beginning of the proof of Theorem
25
we obtain subsequently

\displaystyle|(w_{\eta})_{\pm}(\bar{x})\mp\eta/2|\leq C(d)I_{1}^{\pm}[|\nabla w_{\eta}|\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega}](\bar{x},0),

\displaystyle\int\limits_{{\mathfrak{C}}}|(w_{\eta})_{\pm}(\bar{x})\mp\eta/2|\,d\mu^{d-1}_{\lambda,\gamma}(\bar{x})\leq C(d)\int\limits_{(-1,1)^{d-1}}I_{1}^{\pm}[|\nabla w_{\eta}|\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega}](\bar{x},0)\,d\mu^{d-1}_{\lambda,\gamma}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle\leq C(d,\lambda,\gamma)\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla w_{\eta}|b\,dz:=J(\eta).

By the triangle inequality (
24
) we get

\eta\leq\int\limits_{{\mathfrak{C}}}|(v_{\eta})_{+}(\bar{x})-(v_{\eta})_{+}(\bar{x})|\,d\mu^{d-1}_{\lambda,\gamma}(\bar{x})+2J(\eta):=I(\eta)+II(\eta).

This implies the following alternative:

\displaystyle\text{either}\quad I(\eta):=\int|(w_{\eta})_{+}(\bar{x})-(w_{\eta})_{-}(\bar{x})|\,d\mu(\bar{x})\geq\frac{\eta}{2}

\displaystyle\text{or}\quad II(\eta):=2J(\eta)\geq\frac{\eta}{2}.

The first case

is the one we use in the proof of Theorem
25
, it gives the discontinuity of the

-minimizer, since the condition that
\mathcal{F}(w_{\eta})\leq\mathcal{F}(\eta u)
implies under Assumption

(or Assumption

with sufficiently small
\kappa
) that

for some
\eta>0
.

On the other hand, for any function
w_{\eta}\in\eta u^{\partial}+C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
the difference of “upper” and “lower” traces is zero (
I(\eta)=0
), which leads us to the second case. By Lemmas
21
,
22
,
23
with
Q=2
, under Assumption
14
(or Assumption
15
with sufficiently small
\kappa
)

\text{the inequality}\quad\mathcal{F}(w_{\eta})\leq 2\mathcal{F}(\eta u)\quad\text{implies}\quad\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla w_{\eta}|b\,dz<\eta/4

for some
\eta>0
. This means that

\mathcal{F}(w_{\eta})\geq 2\mathcal{F}(\eta u).

Recalling that
w_{\eta}
is an arbitrary function assuming the boundary value

we see that the

. This is the Lavrentiev gap (
43
).

Let us proceed to the superdimensional case. Recall the proof of Proposition
38
, which was based on the estimate
35

38
to any function

taking the same boundary values as
\eta u
, which gives (in the notation of Section

\displaystyle I(\eta)+II(\eta):=

\displaystyle=w_{\eta}(\bar{0},1)-w_{\eta}(\bar{0},-1)=(\eta/2)-(-\eta/2)=\eta.

Recall that here for

and

\xi^{+}_{m,0}=-1
and
\xi^{-}_{m,2^{m}}=1
. Thus

\text{either}\quad I(\eta)\geq\eta/2\quad\text{or}\quad II(\eta)\geq\eta/2.

The case
I(\eta)\geq\eta/2
is the one we use in the proof above (for
w_{\eta}=v_{\eta}
), since by Assumption
14
(or Assumption
15
with small
\kappa>0
) for the minimizer
v_{\eta}
with some
\eta>0
we get
II(\eta)\leq\eta/4
.

On the other hand, for any smooth function
I(\eta)\to 0
as
m\to 0
(by the absolute continuity), so for large
m
we fall into the second case
II(\eta)\geq\eta/2
. By the estimate (
35
) this implies

C(d)\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla w_{\eta}|b\,dx\geq\eta/2.

By Lemmas
21
,
22
with
Q=2
, under Assumption
14
(or Assumption
15
with sufficiently small
\kappa
)

\text{the inequality}\quad\mathcal{F}(w_{\eta})\leq 2\mathcal{F}(\eta u)\quad\text{implies}\quad C(d)\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla w_{\eta}|b\,dz<\eta/4

for some
\eta>0
. This means that

\mathcal{F}(w_{\eta})\geq 2\mathcal{F}(\eta u)

and thus the
H
-minimizer will have the energy greater than
2\mathcal{F}(\eta u)
. This is the Lavrentiev gap (
43
).

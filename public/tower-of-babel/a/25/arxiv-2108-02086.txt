Abstract
. We define some formal moduli space of quasi-isogenies of isoclinic p p -divisible groups with a non-reductive group as the “structure group”. We then formulate new Arithmetic Fundamental Lemma conjectures for Bessel subgroups in the context of the arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad conjectures. Some (very limited) evidence is presented.



1.
Introduction

For a (smooth projective) algebraic variety over a number field, the vanishing of its Hasse–Weil L-function at the central point is conjectured to be accounted for by the non-triviality of its Chow group of (homologically trivial) algebraic cycles. A notable example is the conjecture of Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer for elliptic curves. There is now more evidence in higher dimensional cases provided by special cycles on Shimura varieties. In this context, the first example is provided by the Gross–Zagier formula
[
7
,
25
]
, which relates the Néron–Tate heights of Heegner divisors on modular curves to the first central derivative of the relevant
L
-functions. The arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad (GGP) conjecture
[
4
,
17
,
27
,
28
]
is one of the generalizations of the Gross–Zagier formula to higher-dimensional Shimura varieties. The relevant Shimura variety is associated to the product unitary group
G=\mathrm{U}(1,n-2)\times\mathrm{U}(1,n-1)
or orthogonal group
G={\mathrm{SO}}(2,n-2)\times{\mathrm{SO}}(2,n-1)
, on which there is the so-called arithmetic diagonal cycle associated to the diagonally embedded
H=\mathrm{U}(1,n-2)
or
H={\mathrm{SO}}(2,n-2)
. Then the case
n=2
(for both unitary and orthogonal groups) essentially recovers Heegner divisors on modular curves.

The arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture is inspired by the (usual) Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture, which relates automorphic period integrals on classical groups to the special values of Rankin–Selberg tensor product
L
-functions (cf. for recent advances in this direction). The latter conjecture makes sense for more general groups, such as the product group
G=\mathrm{U}(m)\times\mathrm{U}(n)
and

|n-m|=1

Remark 7.8
.

One may expect that, if the intersection
\mathcal{N}_{H}\cap(1,g)\cdot\mathcal{N}_{H}
in Prop.
7.6
is already a proper scheme, then
\mathcal{N}_{H}^{\spadesuit}\times_{\mathcal{N}_{m,n}}(1,g)\cdot\mathcal{N}_{H}^{\spadesuit}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{N}_{H}\cap(1,g)\cdot\mathcal{N}_{H}
. But we do not know how to prove this.

There is another special case where we can at least show that the fiber product
\mathcal{N}_{H}^{\spadesuit}\times_{\mathcal{N}_{m,n}}(1,g)\cdot\mathcal{N}_{H}^{\spadesuit}
is a proper scheme. This means that the intersection
\mathcal{N}_{H,\mathcal{E}_{\bullet}}^{\spadesuit}\cap(1,g)\cdot\mathcal{N}_{H,\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\bullet}}^{\spadesuit}
is a proper scheme for every pair
(\mathcal{E}_{\bullet},\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{F}\!\mathcal{L}^{\square}_{E^{\sharp}}\times\mathcal{F}\!\mathcal{L}^{\square}_{E^{\sharp}}
, and is empty for all but finitely many pairs. In particular, the intersection number in part (b) of the conjecture is well-defined.

Proposition 7.9
.

Suppose

(so that
\mathcal{N}_{m,n}\simeq\mathcal{N}_{n}

By Prop.
7.2
, the Zariski closure

\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{E})
where
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{r+1}
.
Therefore it suffices to show that

(a)

\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{E})\cap g\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
is empty except for finitely many pairs
(\mathcal{E}_{\bullet},\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{F}\!\mathcal{L}^{\square}_{E^{\sharp}}\times\mathcal{F}\!\mathcal{L}^{\square}_{E^{\sharp}}

(b)

\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{E})\cap g\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})

The first assertion follows from the proof of Prop.
7.6
(hence Prop.
6.7
). For the second one, the proof of Prop.
6.7
shows that

∎

Moments of exponential functionals of Lévy processes on a deterministic horizon – identities and explicit expressions
Z. Palmowski , a {}^{,\textnormal{a}} , H. Sariev , ‡ , b {}^{,\ddagger,\textnormal{b}} and M. Savov , † , c {}^{,\dagger,\textnormal{c}}

Abstract
In this work, we consider moments of exponential functionals of Lévy processes on a deterministic horizon. We derive two convolutional identities regarding these moments. The first one relates the complex moments of the exponential functional of a general Lévy process up to a deterministic time to those of the dual Lévy process. The second convolutional identity links the complex moments of the exponential functional of a Lévy process, which is not a compound Poisson process, to those of the exponential functionals of its ascending/descending ladder heights on a random horizon determined by the respective local times. As a consequence, we derive a universal expression for the half-negative moment of the exponential functional of any symmetric Lévy process, which resembles in its universality the passage time of symmetric random walks. The ( n − 1 / 2 ) t ​ h (n-1/2)^{th} , n ≥ 0 n\geq 0 moments are also discussed. On the other hand, under extremely mild conditions, we obtain a series expansion for the complex moments (including those with negative real part) of the exponential functionals of subordinators. This significantly extends previous results and offers neat expressions for the negative real moments. In a special case, it turns out that the Riemann zeta function is the minus first moment of the exponential functional of the Gamma subordinator indexed in time.



Keywords:

Bernstein function, exponential functional, Mellin transform

Introduction, background and motivation

Let
\xi=\left(\xi_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
be a potentially killed Lévy process that takes values on the real line and possesses Lévy ​​-Khintchine exponent
\Psi

, independent of

, including its moments, have been extensively studied; a small list of references containing general results and techniques is
[

,
15
,
17
,
20
,
22
,
26
,
29
]
. The number of papers containing applications of

[

,
22
]
. The functional-analytical properties of

, are much less understood. To the best of our knowledge, the only case where a relatively explicit expression for the density of

is available is the case where

is Brownian motion with drift, see
[

,
28
]
and the survey by
[
14
]

is a subordinator, the moments of

[

,
24
,
25
,
27
]
. In fact,
[
24
,
25
]
deal with exponential functionals of additive processes and derive recurrent relations between their moments, providing some explicit formulae for the positive integer moments of

when
\xi
is either a subordinator, Brownian motion with drift, or a compound Poisson process with standard normal jumps, see
[

, Corollary 1, Examples 5-6]

[

The first aim of this paper is to establish two convolutional identities involving the moments of

. The first one, see Lemma

, relates the moments of

to those of

[
28
]

, we discuss the

, relates the moments of

to the moments of the ladder height processes of

The second aim of this paper is to significantly improve
[
2
]
and provide a series expansion for all moments, including negative ones, of

when
\xi
is a subordinator. This is done under conditions that are much weaker than the already mild assumptions in
[
2

. Besides general complex moments, our Theorem

offers relatively neat expressions for the negative integer moments of

The paper is structured as follows: Section

Main results

2.1
Preliminary definitions and notation

We use
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{R}^{+}=\left[0,\infty\right)
,
\mathbb{C}
to denote the real line, the half-line, and the complex plane, respectively. For any
z\in\mathbb{C}
, we write
z=a+ib=\mathtt{Re}(z)+i\mathtt{Im}(z)
.

Recall that a one-dimensional Lévy process,
\xi=\left(\xi_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
, is a real-valued stochastic process defined in a suitable probability space that has stationary and independent increments. In this work, we allow for an independent exponential killing of
\xi
in the usual fashion: if
\mathbf{e}_{q}
is an exponentially distributed random variable with parameter
q\geq 0
, independent of
\xi
, we set
\xi_{t}=\infty
for
t\geq\mathbf{e}_{q}
. When we employ a Lévy process, we implicitly understand that it may be killed in the described way. This extension is useful in the context of exponential functionals on a deterministic horizon, since the killing is in fact their Laplace transform. We recall that the Lévy ​​-Khintchine exponent
\Psi
of any
\xi
is given by

\Psi(z):=\log\mathbb{E}\left[e^{z\xi_{1}}\right]=az+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}z^{2}+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(e^{zy}-1-zy\mathbb{I}_{\{|y|\leq 1\}}\right)\Pi(dy)-q,\quad z\in i\mathbb{R},

(2.1)

where
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
\sigma^{2}\geq 0
,
q\geq 0
are the linear term, the Brownian component, and the killing rate, respectively,
\Pi
is a sigma-finite measure satisfying
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\min\left\{x^{2},1\right\}\Pi(dy)<\infty
and encoding the intensity and size of the jumps of
\xi
, and
\mathbb{I}_{\{\cdot\}}
stands for the indicator function. Note that when
q=0
, then
\xi
is conservative, that is, it is not killed. For any
\Psi
, it is well-known that the following Wiener-Hopf factorization holds

\Psi(z)=h(q)\kappa_{+}(q,-z)\kappa_{-}(q,z),\quad z\in i\mathbb{R},

(2.2)

where
\kappa_{\pm}
are the bivariate Bernstein functions of the ascending/descending bivariate subordinators
related to the inverse ascending/descending ladder times and the ascending/descending ladder heights. More precisely, if
L_{\pm}=\left(L_{\pm}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
are the local times of the strong Markov processes
(\xi^{+}_{t})_{t\geq 0}=(\sup_{s\leq t}\xi_{s}-\xi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and
(\xi^{-}_{t})_{t\geq 0}=(\xi_{t}-\inf_{s\leq t}\xi_{s})_{t\geq 0}
, then
L^{-1}_{\pm}=(L^{-1}_{\pm}(t))_{t\geq 0}
are the ascending/descending ladder times,
H_{\pm}=\left(H_{\pm}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}=(\xi_{L^{-1}_{\pm}(t)})_{t\geq 0}
are the ascending/descending ladder heights, and

-\log\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-qL^{-1}_{\pm}(1)-zH_{\pm}(t)}\right]=\kappa_{\pm}(q,z)=e^{\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\left[0,\infty\right)}\left(e^{-t}-e^{-qt-zx}\right)\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\xi_{t}\in\pm dx\right)}{t}dt},\quad\mathtt{Re}(z)\geq 0,

are the bivariate Bernstein functions or, in the language of Lévy processes, the bivariate Laplace exponents of
(L^{-1}_{\pm},H_{\pm})
, refer to
[
5
, Chapter VI]
for more information. In addition,

\begin{split}&h(q)=e^{-\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(e^{-t}-e^{-qt}\right)\mathbb{P}\left(\xi_{t}=0\right)\frac{dt}{t}},\end{split}

(2.3)

with
h(q)\equiv 1
whenever
\xi
is not a compound Poisson process, i.e. a Lévy process whose intensity of jumps is finite
(\Pi\left(\mathbb{R}\right)<\infty)
.
Furthermore, we can associate to each bivariate Bernstein function
\kappa
a bivariate Bernstein-Gamma function
W_{\kappa}
, which for any fixed
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathtt{Re}(\zeta)\geq 0
is the solution of

W_{\kappa}\left(\zeta,z+1\right)=\kappa(\zeta,z)W_{\kappa}(\zeta,z),\quad W_{\kappa}(\zeta,1)=1,\quad\text{ on $\mathtt{Re}(z)>0$.}

(2.4)

In fact, for any
q=\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
W_{\kappa}(q,\cdot)
is the Mellin transform of a positive random variable, that is, there exists
Y\geq 0
such that
\mathbb{E}[Y^{z}]=W_{\kappa}(q,z+1)
, for
\mathtt{Re}(z)>-1
, see
[
2
, Theorem 2.8]
. The functional-analytic properties, including Stirling-type asymptotics, of the bivariate Bernstein-Gamma function
W_{\kappa}
are studied in detail in
[
2
]
. Various similar results in the univariate case can be found in
[
16
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
.

For any
\xi
and
t\geq 0
, we consider the exponential functionals

\begin{split}&I_{\Psi}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\xi_{s}}ds=\int_{0}^{\min\left\{t,\mathbf{e}_{q}\right\}}e^{-\xi_{s}}ds,\\
&I_{\Psi}\equiv I_{\Psi}(\infty):=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\xi_{s}}ds=\int_{0}^{\mathbf{e}_{q}}e^{-\xi_{s}}ds.\end{split}

By the Strong Law of Large Numbers,
I_{\Psi}<\infty
if and only if
q>0
or
\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\xi_{s}=\infty
almost surely. We introduce the Mellin transform of
I_{\Psi}
, defined formally for some
z\in\mathbb{C}
by

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}_{I_{\Psi}}(z):=\mathbb{E}\bigl[I_{\Psi}^{z-1}\bigr].\end{split}

When
h(q)\equiv 1
, it follows from Theorem 2.4 in
[
22
]
that

\mathcal{M}_{I_{\Psi}}(z)=\kappa_{-}(q,0)\frac{\Gamma(z)}{W_{\kappa_{+}}(q,z)}W_{\kappa_{-}}(q,1-z),\quad\mathtt{Re}(z)\in(0,1),

(2.5)

since, in the notation of
[
22
, Lemma A1]
, we have
\kappa_{\pm}=\phi_{\pm}
. Indeed, from
[
22
, Lemma A1]
, for any fixed
q\geq 0
, it holds
\phi_{+}(z)=h(q)\kappa_{+}(q,z)
, and so from
[
22
, Theorem 4.1.(5)]
, we obtain (
2.5
). Relation (
2.5
) has been at the heart of the most refined results in the area of exponential functionals, which is mainly due to the fact that the expression holds at least on a complex strip of unit length and the asymptotics of
W_{\kappa}
are available in the complex half-plane. The most precise version of the latter is contained in
[
16
]
.

2.2
General convolutional identities

In this part, we consider general convolutional identities regarding moments of exponential functionals on a deterministic horizon. The first one connects the moments of
I_{\Psi}(t)
and
I_{\hat{\Psi}}(t)
, where
\hat{\Psi}(z)=\Psi(-z)
is the Lévy ​​-Khintchine exponent of the dual Lévy process
\hat{\xi}=(-\xi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. This allows us to find a universal formula for the
-1/2^{th}
-moment of the exponential functional of any symmetric Lévy process.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Psi
and
\hat{\Psi}
be the Lévy ​​-Khintchine exponents of the Lévy processes
\xi
and
\hat{\xi}
that are not compound Poisson processes. Then, for any
t>0
and
\mathtt{Re}(z)\in\left(0,1\right)
, we have

\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[I_{\Psi}^{-z}(t-s)\right]\mathbb{E}\left[I^{z-1}_{\hat{\Psi}}(s)\right]ds=\Gamma(1-z)\Gamma(z)=\frac{\pi}{\sin{\pi z}}.

(2.6)

If
\xi
is a compound Poisson process with continuous measure
\Pi(dx)
and
\lambda=\Pi\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
, then

\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[I_{\Psi}^{-z}(t-s)\right]\mathbb{E}\left[I^{z-1}_{\hat{\Psi}}(s)\right]ds=\Gamma(1-z)\Gamma(z)\frac{(\lambda+1)^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\left(1-e^{-\lambda t}-\lambda te^{-\lambda t}\right)\mathbb{I}_{\{t>0\}}.

(2.7)

Remark 2.2
.

It would not be surprising if (
2.6
) could be proved from general considerations involving properties of Lévy processes such as stationarity and independence of increments. However, expression (
2.5
) for the Mellin transform clearly suggests the identity and the proof is straightforward.

We offer an immediate corollary that reveals an interesting invariance in the case of symmetric Lévy processes, which is reminiscent of the law of the entrance time for symmetric random walks, see (
2.9
).

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\xi
be a symmetric Lévy process, so that
\xi\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{=}}\hat{\xi}
and
\Psi\equiv\hat{\Psi}
. If
\xi
is not a compound Poisson process, then we have, for any
t>0
,

\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[I_{\Psi}^{-z}(t-s)\right]\mathbb{E}\left[I^{z-1}_{\Psi}(s)\right]ds=\Gamma(1-z)\Gamma(z)=\frac{\pi}{\sin{\pi z}},

(2.8)

and

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[I^{-1/2}_{\Psi}(t)\Bigr]=t^{-1/2}.

(2.9)

If
\xi
is a compound Poisson process with continuous measure
\Pi(dx)
and
\lambda=\Pi\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
, then, for any
t>0
,

\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[I_{\Psi}^{-z}(t-s)\right]\mathbb{E}\left[I^{z-1}_{\hat{\Psi}}(s)\right]ds=\Gamma(1-z)\Gamma(z)\frac{(\lambda+1)^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\left(1-e^{-\lambda t}-\lambda te^{-\lambda t}\right)\mathbb{I}_{\{t>0\}},

(2.10)

and

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[I^{-1/2}_{\Psi}(t)\Bigr]=\left(\lambda+1\right)\sqrt{\pi}e^{-\lambda t}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{e^{\lambda s}}{\sqrt{s}}ds.

(2.11)

Remark 2.4
.

Identity (
2.9
) appears already in
[
28
]
in the case of Brownian motion.

The second identity is again convolutional, but does not involve simultaneously
I_{\Psi}(t)
and
I_{\hat{\Psi}}(t)
. Before stating it, we relate the ratio in (
2.5
) to a suitable Laplace transform. This is of independent interest as the left-hand side of (
2.12
) appears in the representation of the Mellin transform of
I_{\Psi}
, see
[
22
]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\kappa
be the Laplace exponent of a bivariate subordinator which is a Wiener-Hopf factor of the Lévy ​​-Khintchine exponent of a Lévy process
\xi
. Then, for any
q>0
, it holds that

\frac{1}{q}\frac{\Gamma(z)}{W_{\kappa}(q,z)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-qt}\mathbb{E}\left[I^{z-1}_{\kappa}\left(L(t)\right)\right]dt,\quad\mathtt{Re}(z)>0,

(2.12)

where
I_{\kappa}\left(t\right)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-H(s)}ds
,
(L^{-1}(s),H(s))_{s\geq 0}
is the bivariate ladder height process related to
\kappa
, and
L(t)
is the local time related to the ladder time
L^{-1}(t)
.

This result paves the way for the second convolutional identity.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\Psi
be the Lévy ​​-Khintchine exponent of a Lévy process
\xi
that is not a compound Poisson process. Then, for any
t>0
and
\mathtt{Re}(z)\in\left(0,1\right)
, it holds that

\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[I_{\Psi}^{z-1}(t-s)\right]\mathbb{E}\left[I^{-z}_{\kappa_{-}}(L_{-}(s))\right]dsdt=\Gamma\left(1-z\right)\int_{0}^{t}U_{L^{-1}_{+}}(dt-s)\mathbb{E}\left[I^{z-1}_{\kappa_{+}}(L_{+}(s))\right]ds,

(2.13)

where
I_{\kappa_{\pm}}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-H_{\pm}(s)}ds
,
t\geq 0
are the exponential functionals of the ladder height subordinators
H_{\pm}
,
L_{\pm}
are the local times of the ladder times
L^{-1}_{\pm}
, and
U_{L^{-1}_{\pm}}
are the respective potential measures.

Next, we provide a corollary in a special case.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
\Psi
be the Lévy ​​-Khintchine exponent of a symmetric Lévy process
\xi
that is not a compound Poisson process. Then
\kappa_{\pm}=\kappa
, and
L_{\pm}=L
and
L^{-1}_{\pm}=L^{-1}
are stable subordinators of index
1/2
, and we have, for any
\mathtt{Re}(z)\in\left(0,1\right)
,

\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[I_{\Psi}^{z-1}(t-s)\right]\mathbb{E}\left[I^{-z}_{\kappa}(L(s))\right]ds=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\Gamma\left(1-z\right)}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\sqrt{t-s}}\mathbb{E}\left[I^{z-1}_{\kappa}(L(s))\right]ds.

(2.14)

Remark 2.8
.

At a convolutional level, the moments of
I_{\Psi}(t)
are related to the moments of
I_{\kappa}(\cdot)
evaluated at the local time
L(v)
. Let
\xi
be standard Brownian motion. Then
I_{\kappa}(L(v))=1-e^{-L(v)}
and one may proceed to recover classical results for the moments of the exponential functional of Brownian motion, see
[
28
]
. In general, if we set
z=1/2
, then
\mathbb{E}[I^{-1/2}_{\kappa}(L(v))]
appears in both convolutions in (
2.14
) and we recover
\mathbb{E}[I_{\Psi}^{-1/2}(t)]=t^{-1/2}
, see (
2.9
). Unfortunately, this argument fails even for
z=1/2+ib
, since then we have an identity of the type
f_{1}*g=f_{2}*\overline{g}
.

Remark 2.9
.

Formulae (
2.13
) and (
2.14
) can be useful for understanding
\mathbb{E}[I_{\Psi}^{z}(t)]
, provided a series expansion in the spirit of (
2.18
) for
\mathbb{E}\left[I^{z}_{\kappa}(L(v))\right]
is derived. This would be a highly technical but plausible endeavor, as can be seen from the details in the proofs in
[
2
]
and later when we deal with the easiest case, namely
(L^{-1}(t),H(t))=(t,H(t))
. We leave this for future work.

2.3
Moments of exponential functionals of subordinators

The main result in this part extends and expands Theorem 2.14 in
[
2
]
and yields an infinite series representation for
\mathbb{E}[I^{z}_{\phi}(t)]=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\xi_{s}}ds
,
for a large class of subordinators
\xi
. Note that the Laplace exponents of non-decreasing Lévy processes are in bijection with Bernstein functions via the relation

\phi(z)=-\log\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-z\xi_{1}}\right]=-\Psi(-z),\quad\mathtt{Re}(z)\geq 0,

hence, the use of
I_{\phi}
instead of
I_{\Psi}
. We recall that a function
\phi
is a Bernstein function if and only if

\phi(z)=\phi(0)+dz+\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{-zy}\right)\mu(dy),\quad\mathtt{Re}(z)\geq 0,

(2.15)

where
\phi(0)
,
d\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, and
\mu
is a sigma-finite measure on
\left(0,\infty\right)
satisfying
\int_{0}^{\infty}\min\left\{y,1\right\}\mu(dy)<\infty
. Following
[
22
, (2.5)-(2.7)]
, for any Bernstein function
\phi
, we set

\begin{split}\mathbf{a}_{\phi}&=\inf\left\{u<0:\phi\in\mathbf{A}_{\left(u,\infty\right)}\right\}\in[-\infty,0],\\
\mathbf{u}_{\phi}&=\inf\left\{u\in\left[\mathbf{a}_{\phi},0\right]:\phi(u)=0\right\}\in[-\infty,0],\\
\mathbf{\bar{a}}_{\phi}&=\max\left\{\mathbf{a}_{\phi},\mathbf{u}_{\phi}\right\}\in[-\infty,0],\end{split}

(2.16)

where we use the convention
\sup\,\emptyset=-\infty
and
\inf\,\emptyset=0
, and
\mathbf{A}_{(u,\infty)}
,
u\in\mathbb{R}
is the space of holomorphic functions on
\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\mathtt{Re}(z)>u\right\}
.

The Bernstein-gamma function
W_{\phi}
, being the univariate analogue of
W_{\kappa}
(see (
2.4
)), is the unique solution in the space of Mellin transforms of positive random variables of the equation

W_{\phi}(z+1)=\phi(z)W_{\phi}(z),\quad W_{\phi}(1)=1,\quad\mathtt{Re}(z)\geq 0.

(2.17)

For the next theorem, denote
\varlimsup\limits_{x\to\infty}=\limsup_{x\to\infty}
and
\varliminf\limits_{x\to\infty}=\liminf_{x\to\infty}
.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
\phi
be any Bernstein function, i.e. the Laplace exponent of a potentially killed subordinator. Let
\varlimsup\limits_{x\to\infty}\phi^{\prime}(x)/\phi^{\prime}(2x)<\infty
,
\lim\limits_{x\to\infty}x^{2}\phi^{\prime}(x)=\infty
and
\phi(\infty)=\infty
. Then, for all
t>0
and
\mathtt{Re}(z)>-1+\mathbf{a}_{\phi}\mathbb{I}_{\{\phi(0)=0\}}
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[I^{z}_{\phi}(t)\right]=\frac{\Gamma(z+1)}{W_{\phi}(z+1)}-\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left(\phi(z+j)-\phi(k)\right)}{\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(\phi(j)-\phi(k)\right)}\frac{e^{-\phi(k)t}}{\phi(k)}\frac{\Gamma(z+1)}{W_{\phi_{(k)}}(z+1)},

(2.18)

where
\phi_{(n)}(z):=\phi(n+z)-\phi(n)
,
n\geq 1
is a Bernstein function.

Remark 2.11
.

Let us compare our result to Theorem 2.14 in
[
2
]
, which requires

\varliminf\limits_{x\to\infty}\phi^{\prime}(\lambda x)/\phi^{\prime}(x)\geq C\lambda^{\beta},\qquad\beta>-1,

(2.19)

uniformly on
\lambda\in[1,\Lambda]
, for
\Lambda>1
. Under (
2.19
), it is immediate that
\overline{\lim}_{x\rightarrow\infty}\phi^{\prime}(x)/\phi^{\prime}(2x)<\infty
. Moreover,
\phi^{\prime}(x)\geq Cx^{\rho-1}
, for some
\rho>0
and all
x
large enough, see
[
2
, discussion around (6.16)]
. Thus,
\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}x^{2}\phi^{\prime}(x)=\infty
and the conditions of Theorem
2.10
are satisfied. To illustrate the scope of our result, take
\phi(x)=\ln\ln(x+e)
, which is a Bernstein function. This is an example from
[
2
, Remark 2.15]
and for which Theorem 2.14 in
[
2
]
is not applicable. Then
\phi^{\prime}(x)=1/\left(\left(x+e\right)\ln\left(x+e\right)\right)
and our conditions immediately hold. The same is true for more pathological cases such as
\phi(x)=\ln\ln\ln(x+e^{e})
and
\phi(x)=\ln\ln\ln\ln(x+e^{e{{}^{e}}})
, which are completely intractable by
[
2
, Theorem 2.14]
. The steps in the proof of Theorem
2.10
coincide with those in
[
2
]
, however, we had to significantly strengthen most of the bounds borrowed from there, e.g. via a new approximation of the key products in Lemma
3.8
.

Remark 2.12
.

We present some conditions on the Lévy measure
\mu
and the drift
d
in the definition (
2.15
) of
\phi
, which yield the conditions for
\phi
in Theorem
2.10
. First, note that
\phi(\infty)=\infty
is equivalent to
\mu(\mathbb{R}^{+})=\infty
or
d>0
. If
d>0
, then
\phi^{\prime}(\infty)=d>0
and our conditions hold. Let
d=0
. Then, setting
\bar{\mu}(x):=\int_{x}^{\infty}\mu(dy)
, sufficient conditions in terms of
\mu
for
\lim\limits_{x\to\infty}x^{2}\phi^{\prime}(x)=\infty
come from

&\leq\left|1-\frac{z}{x-y}\right|\left|\int_{0}^{x-y}\phi^{\prime}\left(a+y+\left(1-\frac{a}{x-y}\right)s\right)ds\right|=\frac{\left|x-y-z\right|}{\left|x-y-a\right|}\left|\phi(x)-\phi(a+y)\right|,\end{split}

where we have used in the inequality that
\left|\phi^{\prime}(z)\right|\leq\phi^{\prime}(\mathtt{Re}(z))
. Thus, the first part of (
3.4
) follows, and the second one uses the fact that
\phi^{\prime}
is non-decreasing, see
[
2
, Proposition 3.1 (2)]
. Relation (
3.6
) is immediate.
∎

The next estimate is trivial, but general enough to state it separately.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\phi
be a non-constant Bernstein function and
z=a+ib
. If
a>-1-\mathbf{a}_{\phi}
and
a\notin\left\{1,2,\cdots,k-1\right\}
, there exists
K:=K(z)>0
such that

\left|\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(\phi(z+j)-\phi(k)\right)}{\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(\phi(j)-\phi(k)\right)}\right|\leq K\left|\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(\phi(k)-\phi(a+j)\right)}{\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(\phi(k)-\phi(j)\right)}\right|.

(3.8)

Moreover,
K(z)
is locally bounded on
\{z:\mathtt{Re}(z)>-1+\mathbf{a}_{\phi}\}\setminus\{1,2,\cdots,k-1\}
. If
a\in\left\{1,2,,\cdots,k-1\right\}
, there is
K>0
such that

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(\phi(z+j)-\phi(k)\right)}{\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(\phi(j)-\phi(k)\right)}\right|&\leq K\frac{|b|\phi^{\prime}(k)}{\phi(k)-\phi(k-a)}\left|\frac{\prod_{j=1,j+a\neq k}^{k-1}\phi(k)-\phi(j+a)}{\prod_{j=1,j+a\neq k}^{k-1}\left(\phi(k)-\phi(j)\right)}\right|.\end{split}

(3.9)

Proof.

From relation (
3.4
) of Lemma
3.1
, we get that, when
a\notin\left\{1,2,\cdots,k-1\right\}
,

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(\phi(z+j)-\phi(k)\right)}{\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(\phi(j)-\phi(k)\right)}\right|\leq\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\left|1-\frac{ib}{k-j-a}\right|\left|\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(\phi(k)-\phi(j+a)\right)}{\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(\phi(k)-\phi(j)\right)}\right|.\end{split}

Clearly, the first product is locally uniformly convergent, and so (
3.8
) follows. By taking out the term with

, relation (
3.9
) is a consequence of (
3.5
).

The next lemma summarizes and expands Lemma 4.3 in
[
2
]
in a more convenient form.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\phi
be a non-constant Bernstein function. Then, for any
c>0
and
A>1
, there exists
C_{\phi,c,A}\in\left[1,\infty\right)
, increasing in
c
for fixed
A
and decreasing in
A
for fixed
c
, such that

\frac{\phi^{\prime}(x)}{\phi^{\prime}(x+c)}\leq C_{\phi,c,A},\quad\text{ for all $x>A$.}

(3.10)

Moreover,
C^{*}_{\phi,c}:=\lim\limits_{A\to\infty}C_{\phi,c,A}=\varliminf\limits_{x\to\infty}\phi^{\prime}(x)/\phi^{\prime}(x+c)\in\left[1,\infty\right)
. If, for some
y_{0}>0
,

\varliminf\limits_{x\to\infty}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(x)}{\phi^{\prime}(x+y_{0})}:=\beta(y_{0})>1,

then, for any
y>0
,
\varliminf\limits_{x\to\infty}\phi^{\prime}(x)/\phi^{\prime}(x+y)=\beta(y)>1
and
\phi(\infty)<\infty
, that is, the underlying subordinator is in fact a compound Poisson process and there exists
\gamma>0
such that
\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}e^{\gamma x}\phi^{\prime}(x)=0
.

Proof.

The first part is a direct reformulation of Lemma 4.3 in
[
2
]
. Assume that
\underline{\lim}_{x\rightarrow\infty}\phi^{\prime}(x)/\phi^{\prime}(x+y)=\beta(y)=\beta>1
, for some
y>0
. From the monotonicity of
\phi^{\prime}
, it is clear that this holds for any
y>0
. Therefore, for any
\delta<1-\beta^{-1}
, there is
x_{0}>0
such that, for any
x\geq x_{0}
,
\phi^{\prime}(x+y)\leq\beta_{\delta}\phi^{\prime}(x),
where
\beta_{\delta}=\beta^{-1}+\delta<1
. Recursively, we get
\phi^{\prime}(x_{0}+ny)\leq\beta^{n}_{\delta}\phi^{\prime}(x_{0})
. From this, together with the fact that
\phi
is non-increasing, see
[
2
, Proposition 3.1, (2)]
, we first conclude that

\phi(\infty)-\phi(x_{0})=\int_{x_{0}}^{\infty}\phi^{\prime}(x)dx\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}\beta^{n}_{\delta}\phi^{\prime}(x_{0})=\frac{\beta_{\delta}}{1-\beta_{\delta}}\phi^{\prime}(x_{0})<\infty,

and hence
\phi(\infty)<\infty
, which is equivalent to the underlying subordinator having finite activity, see
[
5
, Chapter I]
. Second, for any
x\in\left[x_{0}+ny,x_{0}+(n+1)y\right]
, we get that

\phi^{\prime}(x)\leq\phi^{\prime}(x_{0}+ny)\leq\beta^{n}_{\delta}\phi^{\prime}(x_{0}),

from where we conclude that there is
\gamma>0
such that
\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}e^{\gamma x}\phi^{\prime}(x)=0
.
∎

The next lemma clarifies (
3.8
) in a rather general case.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\phi
be a Bernstein function such that
\lim\limits_{x\to\infty}\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)/\phi^{\prime}(x)=0
. Then, for any
1>\epsilon>0
and
z=a+ib
with
a\geq 0
, we have that, for all
k
large enough,

\left|\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(\phi(z+j)-\phi(k)\right)}{\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(\phi(j)-\phi(k)\right)}\right|\leq K\left(\frac{\phi^{\prime}(k)}{\phi(k)}\right)^{a(1-\epsilon)},

(3.11)

for some
K=K(z)>0
, which is locally bounded on
\{z:\mathtt{Re}(z)>-1+\mathbf{a}_{\phi}\}\setminus\{1,2,\cdots\}
.
If instead
a\in(\mathbf{a}_{\phi}-1,0)
, then, for any
1>\epsilon>0
, we have that, for all
k
large enough,

\left|\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(\phi(z+j)-\phi(k)\right)}{\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(\phi(j)-\phi(k)\right)}\right|\leq K\left(\frac{\phi^{\prime}(k)}{\phi(k)}\right)^{a(1+\epsilon)},

(3.12)

for some
K=K(z)>0
, which is locally bounded on
\{z:\mathtt{Re}(z)>-1+\mathbf{a}_{\phi}\}\setminus\{1,2,\cdots\}
.

Proof.

Let
a\geq 0
be non-integer. We use the inequality (
3.8
) to replace
z
with
a=\mathtt{Re}(z)
. The properties of
K
stem from this inequality. The constants that appear hereafter are explicit and are locally bounded in
a
. Then, for any fixed
j\geq 1
, we have

\begin{split}\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left|\frac{\phi(k)-\phi(a+j)}{{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}}\right|\in\left(0,\infty\right).\end{split}

Therefore, hereafter we can always work with
k\geq j_{0}
, for fixed
j_{0}\geq 1
, and replace the first
j_{0}
terms by constants.
Next, note that

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\phi(k)-\phi(a+j)}{{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}}\right|&=1-\frac{\phi(a+j)-\phi(j)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}.\end{split}

(3.13)

Then, regardless of
a>0
, setting
l>a
, we get from (
3.13
) that, for
j\leq k-l
,

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\phi(k)-\phi(a+j)}{{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}}\right|=1-\frac{\phi(a+j)-\phi(j)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}>0,\end{split}

since
\phi(j+a)<\phi(k)
. For the terms with
k-1\geq j>k-l
, using that
\phi^{\prime}
is non-increasing (see
[
2
, Proposition 3.1, (2)]
), we can check that

\frac{\phi(a+j)-\phi(j)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}\leq a\frac{\phi^{\prime}(k-l)}{\phi^{\prime}(k)},

and from Lemma
3.4
these are bounded for all large enough
k
. At the cost of a constant locally bounded in
a>0
, we can dispense of these terms. Then, using in (
3.13
) the standard inequality
\ln(1-x)\leq-x
, for
x\in\left(0,1\right)
, we get

\displaystyle\prod_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\left|\frac{\phi(k)-\phi(a+j)}{{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{-\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi(a+j)-\phi(j)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}\right\}

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{-a\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(j)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}-\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\int_{j}^{j+a}\int_{j}^{x}\phi^{\prime\prime}(y)dydx}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{-a\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(j)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}-\frac{a^{2}}{2}\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(j)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{-a\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(j-1)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}-a\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(j-1)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}-\frac{a^{2}}{2}\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(j)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{-a\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(j-1)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}-\left(a+\frac{a^{2}}{2}\right)\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(j-1)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}\right\},

(3.14)

where in the last three inequalities we have used that
-\phi^{\prime\prime}
is positive and non-increasing, which comes from
\phi^{\prime}
being completely monotone (see
[
2
, Proposition 3.1, (2)]
) and
\phi^{\prime}(j)\geq\phi^{\prime}(j-1)+\phi^{\prime\prime}(j-1)
. Estimating the first term in (
3.14
) by the integral

-a\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(j-1)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}\leq-a\int_{j_{0}-1}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(x)}{\phi(k)-\phi(x)}dx,

we get with the help of Lemma
3.4
in the last inequality that, for all
k
large enough,

\displaystyle\prod_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\phi(k)-\phi(a+j)}{{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}}\right|\leq\left(\frac{\phi(k)-\phi(k-l)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j_{0}-1)}\right)^{a}\exp\left\{-\left(a+\frac{a^{2}}{2}\right)\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(j-1)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 50.00008pt\leq\left(l\frac{\phi^{\prime}(k-l)}{{\phi(k)-\phi(j_{0}-1)}}\right)^{a}\exp\left\{-\left(a+\frac{a^{2}}{2}\right)\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(j-1)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 50.00008pt\leq\left(2C_{\phi,l}l\right)^{a}\left(\frac{\phi^{\prime}(k)}{{\phi(k)-\phi(j_{0}-1)}}\right)^{a}\exp\left\{-\left(a+\frac{a^{2}}{2}\right)\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(j-1)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}\right\}.

(3.15)

If
\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)/\phi^{\prime}(x)=0
, then since
-\phi^{\prime\prime}
is positive and non-increasing,

-\phi^{\prime\prime}(j-1)=\mathrm{o}\left(\phi^{\prime}(j-1)\right),

and we conclude (
3.11
) for any
\epsilon>0
by estimating the very last exponent of (
3.15
) with the same integral, which we have used in the steps prior to (
3.15
). We sketch briefly the proof for
a\in(\mathbf{a}_{\phi}-1,0)
. We can again work with indices larger than some fixed
j_{0}
. In this case, since

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\phi(k)-\phi(a+j)}{{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}}\right|&=1+\frac{\phi(j)-\phi(a+j)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)},\end{split}

we do not need to discard terms with
j\geq k-l
, yet we shall keep this truncation. Then, using that
\ln(1+x)\leq x
, for
x\in(0,1)
, and
\phi^{\prime}(j)\geq\phi^{\prime}(j-1)+\phi^{\prime\prime}(j-1)
,

\begin{split}\prod_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\biggl|\frac{\phi(k)-\phi(a+j)}{{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}}\biggr|&\leq\exp\left\{\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi(j)-\phi(a+j)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}\right\}\\
&=\exp\left\{a\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(j)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}-\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\int_{j+a}^{j}\int_{x}^{j}\phi^{\prime\prime}(y)dydx}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}\right\}\\
&\leq\exp\left\{a\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(j)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}-\frac{a^{2}}{2}\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(j+a)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}\right\}\\
&\leq\exp\left\{a\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(j-1)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}+a\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(j-1)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}-\frac{a^{2}}{2}\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(j+a)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}\right\}.\end{split}

Now, from Lemma
3.4
and the assumptions, we get that

\phi^{\prime\prime}(j+a)=\mathrm{o}\left(\phi^{\prime}(j)\right),\qquad\phi^{\prime\prime}(j-1)=\mathrm{o}\left(\phi^{\prime}(j)\right),

and the proof follows as in the case for
a>0
.

The case where
a
is positive integer is treated similarly by applying (
3.9
) to (
3.14
) and working in the same way with the remaining terms as in (
3.14
), where after passing at the exponential level in (
3.14
), we can add a term with index

at the expense of a constant.

Lemma 3.6

Remark 3.7
.

We note that
\overline{\lim}_{x\rightarrow\infty}\phi^{\prime}(x)/\phi^{\prime}(2x)<\infty

Proof of Lemma
3.6

For the case

\begin{split}\phi(k)-\phi(j-2)&\leq\phi(k)-\phi(j)+2\phi^{\prime}(j-2)\leq\phi(k)-\phi(j)+2C_{\phi,2,j_{0}-2}\phi^{\prime}(j)\\

We employ this fact and that

is positive and non-increasing to estimate the exponent in (

\begin{split}-u_{a}\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(j-1)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}&\leq Cu_{a}\int_{j_{0}-2}^{k-l}\frac{-\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)}{\phi(k)-\phi(x)}dx.\end{split}

(3.18)

Next, recalling that

, we set

3.16

\begin{split}-u_{a}\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(j-1)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}&\leq Cu_{a}\int_{j_{0}-2}^{k-l}\frac{-\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)}{\phi(k)-\phi(x)}dx\\
&\leq Cu_{a}\left(\int_{j_{0}-2}^{b_{k}}\frac{1}{x^{2}}dx+\frac{2\phi(k)}{\phi^{\prime}(k)}\int_{b_{k}}^{k-l}\frac{1}{x^{2}\left(k-x\right)}dx\right)\\

where we have used in the first inequality that

(see

(ii)

\phi

Plugging this in (

) by an application of (

). To prove (

. Substituting this in the integral in (

\begin{split}-u_{a}\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(j-1)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}&\leq Cu_{a}\left(\frac{4}{j_{0}-2}+c\int_{b_{k}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(x)}{x\left(\phi(k)-\phi(x)\right)}dx\right)\\
&=Cu_{a}\left(\frac{4}{j_{0}-2}+c\int_{b_{k}}^{\frac{k}{2}}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(x)}{x\left(\phi(k)-\phi(x)\right)}dx+c\int_{\frac{k}{2}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(x)}{x\left(\phi(k)-\phi(x)\right)}dx\right).\end{split}

Recall that
\varphi=\phi^{-1}.
Then

\begin{split}\int_{b_{k}}^{\frac{k}{2}}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(x)}{x\left(\phi(k)-\phi(x)\right)}dx&=\int_{\frac{\phi(k)}{2}}^{\phi\left(\frac{k}{2}\right)}\frac{1}{\varphi(y)\left(\phi(k)-y\right)}dy.\end{split}

We note that, letting
f_{k}(y):=\varphi(y)\left(\phi(k)-y\right),f_{k}^{\prime}(y)=\varphi^{\prime}(y)\left(\phi(k)-y\right)-\varphi(y)
, we have

\begin{split}f^{\prime}_{k}\left(\frac{\phi(k)}{2}\right)&=\varphi^{\prime}\left(\frac{\phi(k)}{2}\right)\frac{\phi(k)}{2}-\varphi\left(\frac{\phi(k)}{2}\right)\geq 0;\\
f^{\prime}_{k}\left(\phi\left(\frac{k}{2}\right)\right)&=\frac{\phi(k)-\phi\left(\frac{k}{2}\right)-\frac{k}{2}\phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{k}{2}\right)}{\phi^{\prime}\Bigl(\frac{k}{2}\Bigr)}\leq 0,\end{split}

where the first inequality follows from
\varphi^{\prime}(x)x\geq\varphi(x)
, which comes from the observation that
\phi^{\prime}(x)\leq\phi(x)/x
, and the second one follows from the fact that
\phi^{\prime}
is decreasing. Therefore, there is
y_{k}\in[\phi(k)/2,\phi(k/2)]
that minimizes
f_{k}
on this region,
\phi(k)-y_{k}=\varphi(y_{k})/\varphi^{\prime}(y_{k})
and
f_{k}(y_{k})=\varphi^{2}(y_{k})/\varphi^{\prime}(y_{k})
. From these, we also get that
\varphi(y_{k})/\varphi^{\prime}(y_{k})\geq\phi(k)-\phi(k/2)
. Hence, we obtain that

\begin{split}\int_{b_{k}}^{\frac{k}{2}}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(x)}{x\left(\phi(k)-\phi(x)\right)}dx&\leq\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(y_{k})}{\varphi^{2}(y_{k})}\left(\phi\left(\frac{k}{2}\right)-\frac{\phi(k)}{2}\right)\leq\phi(k)\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(y_{k})}{\varphi^{2}(y_{k})}.\end{split}

Recall that
\phi^{\prime}(x)\leq\phi(x)/x
(see
[
22
, Proposition 3.1, (3.3)]
) and that
\phi
and
\phi^{\prime}
are increasing and decreasing, respectively. We can then bound the remaining integral as follows

\begin{split}\int_{\frac{k}{2}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(x)}{x\left(\phi(k)-\phi(x)\right)}dx&\leq\int_{\frac{k}{2}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi(x)}{x^{2}\left(\phi(k)-\phi(x)\right)}dx\leq\frac{\phi(k)}{\phi^{\prime}(k)}\int_{\frac{k}{2}}^{k-l}\frac{1}{x^{2}\left(k-x\right)}dx\leq\frac{4\phi(k)\ln{k}}{k^{2}\phi^{\prime}(k)}.\end{split}

We have thus proved (
3.17
). The case
a<0
is treated similarly using (
3.15
) instead. We sketch the arguments. We pick
j_{0}>-2a
,
l>-a
and
k>2l+j_{0}
. The first two terms in (
3.15
) are treated exactly as above. The last one is taken care of as follows

\begin{split}-u_{a}\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{k-l}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(j+a)}{\phi(k)-\phi(j)}&\leq C^{\prime}u_{a}\int_{j_{0}+a-1}^{k-l}\frac{-\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)}{\phi(k)-\phi(x)}dx,\end{split}

(3.20)

where
C^{\prime}
is chosen precisely as in the arguments preceding (
3.20
), which requires us to estimate
\phi(k)-\phi(j)
from below with
C^{\prime}(\phi(k)-\phi(j+a-1))
. This is possible for fixed
a
.
∎

Next, for any

(3.22)

3.6

Proof of Theorem
2.10
.

We will use the proof of Theorem 2.14 in
[
2
]
, since it employs the general representation of the moments of
I_{\phi}(t)
as an infinite convolution (see
[
2
, Theorem 2.12]
), yielding, for
\mathtt{Re}(z)>0

\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}\left[I^{z}_{\phi}(t)\right]=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left(\phi(z+j)-\phi(k)\right)}{\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(\phi(j)-\phi(k)\right)}e^{-\phi(k)t}\frac{\Gamma(z+1)}{W_{\phi_{(k)}}(z+1)}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\phi(k)H_{\phi}(z,k)e^{-\phi(k)t},

(3.25)

provided that the series is absolutely convergent and
\phi(\infty)=\infty
. Then (
2.18
) follows from

\mathbb{E}\left[I^{z}_{\phi}(t)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[I^{z}_{\phi}(\infty)\right]-\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{d}{ds}\mathbb{E}\left[I^{z}_{\phi}(s)\right]ds,

the integration of the exponents in (
3.25
), and the fact that the classical identity
\mathbb{E}[I^{z}_{\phi}(\infty)]=\Gamma(z+1)/W_{\phi}(z+1)
holds. Fix
z=a+ib
with
a>0
. We proceed to show that the series in (
3.25
) is absolutely convergent. First, note that since
\overline{\lim}_{x\rightarrow\infty}\phi^{\prime}(x)/\phi^{\prime}(2x)<\infty
, it follows from Remark
3.7
that
\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)/\phi^{\prime}(x)=0
and all estimates in Lemma
3.8
are valid. Second, with
\varphi=\phi^{-1}
, we have

\infty=\lim\limits_{x\to\infty}x^{2}\phi^{\prime}(x)=\lim\limits_{y\to\infty}\frac{\varphi^{2}(y)}{\varphi^{\prime}(y)}.

Third, recalling the definition of
y_{k}
in Lemma
3.8
and since
\phi(\infty)=\infty
, we see that

\infty=\lim{k}\phi(k)=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left(\phi(y_{k})+y_{k}\phi^{\prime}(y_{k})\right).

Henceforth,
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}y_{k}=\infty
and
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\varphi^{\prime}(y_{k})/\varphi^{2}(y_{k})=0
. As a result, for any
a>-1-\mathbf{a}_{\phi}
, the second bound in (
3.23
) implies that, for any
\varepsilon>0
and all
k\geq k_{\varepsilon}
,

\left|\phi(k)H_{\phi}(z,k)\right|\leq K\frac{\phi^{\prime}(k)}{\left(\phi(k)\right)^{a}}e^{C\left(a+\frac{a^{2}}{2}\right)\phi(k)\left(\frac{8c\ln{k}}{k^{2}\phi^{\prime}(k)}+\varepsilon\right)}.

(3.26)

Set
\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}:=\bigl\{k\geq k_{\varepsilon}:\frac{8\ln{k}}{k^{2}\phi^{\prime}(k)}\leq\varepsilon\bigr\}
. Using the bound (
3.11
) with
\epsilon=\varepsilon
on
\mathcal{K}^{c}_{\varepsilon}
, we get the unified bound

\left|\phi(k)H_{\phi}(z,k)\right|\leq K\frac{\phi^{\prime}(k)}{\left(\phi(k)\right)^{a}}e^{2c\varepsilon\left(a+\frac{a^{2}}{2}\right)\phi(k)}\mathbb{I}_{\{k\in\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}\}}+K\frac{\left(\phi^{\prime}(k)\right)^{1-a\varepsilon}}{\left(\phi(k)\right)^{1+a-a\varepsilon}}\mathbb{I}_{\{k\in\mathcal{K}^{c}_{\varepsilon}\}}.

(3.27)

Choosing
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\delta,a,c,C)>0
by estimating
\phi^{\prime}(k)
on
\mathcal{K}^{c}_{\varepsilon}
, we show that, for any
\delta>0
small enough,

\left|\phi(k)H_{\phi}(z,k)\right|\leq K\frac{\phi^{\prime}(k)}{\left(\phi(k)\right)^{a}}e^{\delta\phi(k)}+K\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{\ln{k}}{k^{2}}\right)^{1-\delta}\frac{1}{\left(\phi(k)\right)^{1+a-a\varepsilon}}.

From (

,

\begin{split}\sum_{k\geq k_{\varepsilon}}\left|\phi(k)H_{\phi}(z,k)\right|e^{-\phi(k)t}\leq K\sum_{k\geq k_{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{\phi^{\prime}(k)}{\left(\phi(k)\right)^{a}}e^{-(t-\delta)\phi(k)}+\frac{K}{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\ln{k}}{k^{2}}\right)^{1-\delta}\frac{1}{\left(\phi(k)\right)^{1+a-a\varepsilon}}\right).\end{split}

The second series is obviously convergent, while for the first one we use the integral comparison, noting that as

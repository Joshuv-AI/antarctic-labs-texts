Radial Solutions and a Local Bifurcation Result for a Singular Elliptic Problem with Neumann Condition
Claudio Saccon

Abstract
We study the problem − Δ ​ u = λ ​ u − u − 1 -\Delta u={\lambda}u-u^{-1} with a Neumann boundary condition; the peculiarity being the presence of the singular term − u − 1 -u^{-1} . We point out that the minus sign in front of the negative power of u u is particularly challenging, since no convexity argument can be invoked. Using bifurcation techniques we are able to prove the existence of solution ( u λ , λ ) (u_{\lambda},{\lambda}) with u λ u_{\lambda} approaching the trivial constant solution u = λ − 1 / 2 u={\lambda}^{-1/2} and λ {\lambda} close to an eigenvalue of a suitable linearized problem. To achieve this we also need to prove a generalization of a classical two-branch bifurcation result for potential operators. Next we study the radial case and show that in this case one of the bifurcation branches is global and we find the asymptotical behavior of such a branch. This results allows to derive the existence of multiple solutions u u with λ {\lambda} fixed.

Introduction

In the last decades several authors have studied semilinear elliptic
problems with singular nonlinear term (with respect to the unknown function
u
). The model problem is the following:

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\gamma u^{-q}+f(x,u)&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
u>0&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1)

where
q>0

\gamma\neq 0
and
f
is a non linear term with standard
growth conditions. Existence and multiplicity of solutions to problem (
1
) are usually investigated in terms of
the behavior of
f
and the sign of
\gamma
.

A main aspect to be taken into account is the variational nature of
(
1
): formally speaking solutions
u
of
(
1
) are expected to be
critical points of the functional:

I(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx-\frac{\gamma}{1-q}\int_{\Omega}u^{1-q}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}F(x,u)\,dx,

defined on
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
and
restricted to
\left\{u\geq 0\right\}
,
where
F(x,s)
is a primitive in
s
of
f(x,s)

(if
q=1
a logarithm should be introduced).
Unfortunately the presence of the singular term
makes it problematic to give a rigorous formulation of the above ideas.

The majority of the known results concern the case
\gamma>0
, where
the term
u\mapsto-\dfrac{\gamma}{1-q}u^{1-q}
is convex
in the interval
]0,+\infty[
.
This fact helps a lot, whether one tries to directly deal with
I

(by using some nonsmooth-critical-point theory) or to use an approximation scheme (by a sequence
I_{n}\to I
,
I_{n}

being
\mathcal{C}^{1}
on
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
). For instance, if
f=0
, the problem has a unique weak solution
\bar{u}
in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
when
0<q<3
and the solution is a minimizer for
I
. This result can be extended for all
q>0
dropping the request that
\bar{u}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
(see
[
5
,
9
]
). For a small non exaustive list of multiplicity results for solutions of this kind of
problems see

[
1
,
3
,
7
,
11
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
17
,
18
,
26
,
27
]
(and the references therein).

If we turn to
\gamma<0
the literature is scarcer: to the author’s knowledge the main results are contanined in
[
6
,
12
,
22
,
27
,
28
]
. In this situation solutions are “attracted” from the value zero and tend to develop “dead cores”, so the formulation (
1
) needs to be modified in order to admit non strictly positive solutions. For instance the only solution for the case
f=0
is
u=0
(as one can easily see by multiplying the equation by
u
). Moreover a direct variational approach using the functional
I
seems difficult for the moment and the usual approach goes by perturbation methods.

We have found particularly interesting the paper
[
22
]
by Montenegro and Silva, where the authors use perturbation methods and show that there exist two nontrivial solutions when
\gamma=-1
,

0<q<1
,
f(x,u)=\mu u^{p}
, with
q<p<
and
\mu>0
big enough.
If we pass to
q=1
, simple tests in the radial case suggest that the Dirichlet problem only has the trivial solution. As we said before solutions starting from zero are “forced to stick” at zero and not allowed to “emerge” (in contrast with the case of
q<1
). On this respect see Remark (
4.9
).

For this reasons, in the case
q=1
, we are lead to replace the Dirichelet condition with a Neumann one. In particular we have considered the
problem:

\begin{cases}-\Delta u={\lambda}u-\dfrac{1}{u}&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
u>0&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
\nabla u\cdot\nu=0&\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega\end{cases}

(2)

where
\Omega
is a bounded smooth open subset of
N
and
\nu
denotes the unit normal defined on
\partial\Omega
. This corresponds to
the problem of
[
22
]
with
q=p=1

(with Neumann condition).

In case
N=1
(
2
) is closely related to a problem studied by Del Pino, Manásevich, and Montero in 1992
(see
[
10
]
) who deal with an ODE,
in the periodic case, with a more general, non autonomous, singular term
f(u,x)
(singular in
u
and
T
-periodic in
x
).
Using topological degree arguments they
prove for instance that the equation:

-\ddot{u}={\lambda}u-\frac{1}{u^{\alpha}}\qquad,\qquad u(x)>0\quad,\qquad u(x+T)=u(x),

where
\alpha\geq 1
, has a solution provided

{\lambda}\neq\dfrac{\mu_{k}}{4}
for all
k
. Here
\mu_{k}
denote the eigenvalues of a suitable linearized problem which arises in a natural way from the problem. In this case, which has a variational structure, the results of

[
10
]
can be derived from the existence of two global bifurcation branches which originate from
trivial solutions of the linearized problem.

In this paper we present two types of results concerning problem
(
2
). In Theorem (
2.1
) of section 2 we prove the existence of two local bifurcation branches

(u_{1,\rho},{\lambda}_{1,\rho})
and
(u_{2,\rho},{\lambda}_{2,\rho})
of
solutions of (
2
), such that

(u_{i,\rho},{\lambda}_{1,\rho})\to(\hat{u},\hat{\lambda})
, as
\rho\to 0
,
where
\hat{\lambda}/2
is an eigenvalue of

-\Delta
with Neumann condition and
\hat{u}
is the constant function:
\hat{u}\equiv\hat{\lambda}^{-1/2}
.
The proof of (
2.1
) heavily relies
on a variant of the
well known abstract results on the existence of two bifurcation branches in the variational case (see
[
20
,
4
,
19
,
25
]
).
To the author surprise such a
variant (see Theorem (
3.1
) seems not to be present in the literature so its proof is carried on in section 3. It has to be said that proving
(
3.1
) requires some additional
technicalities compared to the standard version. Indeed in
[
20
,
4
]
) the proof goes by studying a suitable perturbed function
f_{\rho}
on the unit sphere
S
, while in our case
S
has to be replaced by a sphere-like set
S_{\rho}
also varying with
\rho
. This requires to construct suitable projections to
show that all
S_{\rho}
’s are omeomorphic to
S_{0}=S
(for
\rho
small). Apart from this the proof of (
3.1
) follows the ideas of
[
2
]
.

In section 4 we study the radial case in dimension
N=2
(the same
could be probably done for
N\geq 3
) using ODE techniques and a continuation argument for the nodal regions of the solutions.
In this way, following the ideas of

[
24
]
, we are able to prove that one of the two branches
(u_{\rho},{\lambda}_{\rho})
is global and bounded in

{\lambda}_{\rho}
. This is done by proving that nodal regions of
u_{\rho}
cannot collapse along the branch and that
{\lambda}_{\rho}\to\bar{\lambda}

as
\left\lVert u_{\rho}\right\rVert\to+\infty
, where
\bar{\rho}
is an eigenvalue
of another suitable linear problem. In this way – in the radial case – we can find a lower estimate in the number of solutions for
a fixed
{\lambda}
, by counting the number of branches that cross

{\lambda}
.

2
A local bifurcation result for the singular problem

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open subset of
N
with smooth boundary.

2.1 Theorem
.

Let
\hat{\mu}>0
be an eigenvalue of the following Neumann problem:

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\mu u&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
\nabla u\cdot\nu=0&\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(3)

(
\nu
denotes the normal to
\partial\Omega
).

Then there exists
\rho_{0}>0
such that for all
\rho\in]0,\rho_{0}[

there exist two distinct pairs
(u_{1,\rho},{\lambda}_{1,\rho})
and

(u_{2,\rho},{\lambda}_{2,\rho})
such that, for
i=1,2
:

(u_{i,\rho},{\lambda}_{i,\rho})\mbox{ are solutions of \eqref{eqn:singular-neumann-problem}}\ ,\ u_{i,\rho}\to\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu/2}}\mbox{\hskip 10.22217pt( in $W^{1,2}(\Omega)$ ) }\ ,\ {\lambda}_{i,\rho}\xrightarrow{\rho\to 0}\frac{\hat{\mu}}{2}.

Proof.

We start by introducing some changes of variables. First of all notice that, for all
{\lambda}>0
, Problem (
2
) has the constant solution
u(x)=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{{\lambda}}}
.
If we seek for solutions of the form

u=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{{\lambda}}}+z
we easily end up with the equivalent problem on
z
:

\begin{cases}-\Delta z=2{\lambda}z-h_{\lambda}(z)&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
\sqrt{{\lambda}}z>-1&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
\nabla z\cdot\nu=0&\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega\end{cases}

(4)

where
h_{{\lambda}}:\left]-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{{\lambda}}},+\infty\right[\to\real

is defined by:

h_{\lambda}(s)=\frac{{\lambda}\sqrt{{\lambda}}s^{2}}{1+\sqrt{{\lambda}}s}.

Now we consider another simple transformation:
v:=\sqrt{{\lambda}}z
,
so that (
4
) turns out to be equivalent to:

\begin{cases}-\Delta v=2{\lambda}\left(v-\dfrac{1}{2}h_{1}(v)\right)&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
v>-1&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
\nabla v\cdot\nu=0&\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega\end{cases}

(5)

Now choose
s_{0}
with
0<s_{0}<1/2
and a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
cutoff function
\eta:\real\to[0,1]
such that
\eta(s)=1
for
|s|\leq s_{0}
,
\eta(s)=0
, for
|s|\geq 2s_{0}
. Define

\tilde{h}_{1}:\real\to\real
by

\tilde{h}_{1}(s):=\eta(s)\,h_{1}(s)

(6)

(we agree that
\tilde{h}_{1}(-1)=0
).
Then
\tilde{h}_{1}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\real;\real)
,

\tilde{h}_{1}^{\prime}(0)=h_{1}^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
,
\tilde{h}_{1}=h_{1}
on
[-s_{0},s_{0}]
.
Denote by
\tilde{H}_{1}:\real\to\real

the primitive function for
\tilde{h}_{1}

(i.e.
\tilde{H}_{1}^{\prime}=\tilde{h}_{1}
) such that
\tilde{H}_{1}(0)=0
.

Now we apply the bifurcation theorem
(
3.1
)
with
\mathbb{H}:=W^{1,2}(\Omega)
.
\mathbb{L}=L^{2}(\Omega)
,
\mathcal{H}=0
,
\hat{\lambda}=\mu
,

\mathcal{H}_{1}(v):=\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}\tilde{H}_{1}(v)\,dx
.
In this way
we get that there exists

\rho_{0}>0
such that for all
\rho\in]0,\rho_{0}[
there are two
distinct pairs

(v_{1,\rho},\mu_{1,\rho})
and
(v_{2,\rho},\mu_{2,\rho})
which
are
weak
solutions of:

\begin{cases}-\Delta v=\mu\left(v-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{h}_{1}(v)\right)&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
\nabla v\cdot\nu=0&\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega\end{cases}

(7)

and such that

v_{i,\rho}\xrightarrow{\rho\to 0}0\quad\mbox{( in $W^{1,2}(\Omega)$ )}\quad,\quad\mu_{i,\rho}\xrightarrow{\rho\to 0}\mu_{k}\qquad i=1,2.

(8)

We claim the there exists a constant
K
such, for any
\mu\in[\hat{\mu}_{k}-1,\hat{\mu}_{k}+1]
and any weak solution
v
of (
7
),
v
is bounded and:

\|v\|_{\infty}\leq K\mu\left\lVert v-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{h}_{1}(v)\right\rVert_{2}.

(9)

For this we use a standard bootstrap argument using the fact that the function

k(s)=\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{h}_{1}(s)\right)
, appearing on the right hand side of
(
7
), verifies

|k(s)|\leq M|s|\qquad\forall s\in\real

(10)

for a suitable
M

(since
\tilde{h}_{1}^{\prime}
is bounded).
Assume that
v
is a solution, i.e.
-\Delta v=\mu k(v)
, and
v\in L^{q}(\Omega)
for some

q>1
(for sure this is true for
q=2^{*}
). Then, by (
10
),
k(v)\in L^{q}(\Omega)
. From the standard Calderon–Zygmund theory (see e.g. Section 9.6 in
[
16
]
), we have
v\in W^{2,q}(\Omega)
. Then, using the Sobolev embedding Theorem, either
v\in L^{q_{1}}(\Omega)
with
q_{1}\leq\frac{Nq}{N-2q}
(if
2q\leq N
) or

v\in\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}
with
\alpha>0

(in the case
2q>N
). Iterating this argument a finite number of times we get the conclusion. Notice that we could go further and prove that

v
is
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
and is a classical solution.

Using (
8
) and (
9
)
we get that
v_{i,\rho}\to 0
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
as
\rho\to 0
, so
|v_{i,\rho}|<s_{0}
,
i=1,2
,
for
\rho_{0}
small.
This implies that
\tilde{h}_{1}(v_{i,\rho})=h_{1}(v_{i,\rho})
, and

v_{i,\rho}
actually solve (
5
) with

{\lambda}_{i,\rho}:=\dfrac{\mu_{i,\rho}}{2}
.   Going backwards and
setting
u_{i,\rho}:=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{{\lambda}_{i,\rho}}}+\sqrt{{\lambda}_{i,\rho}}v_{i,\rho}
, we find the desired solutions of (
2
).

∎

3
A variant for the two bifurcation branches
theorem for potential operators

Let
\mathbb{L}
and
\mathbb{H}
be two Hilbert spaces such that
\mathbb{H}\subset\mathbb{L}
with
a compact embbedding
i:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{L}
. We use the notations

\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
,
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
and
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathbb{L}}
,
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}}
to indicate the norms and inner products in
\mathbb{H}

and
\mathbb{L}
respectively. Let
A:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}
be a bounded linear simmetric operator such that:

\left\langle Au,u\right\rangle\geq\nu\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}-M\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathbb{L}}\qquad\forall u\in\mathbb{H}

(11)

where
\nu>0
and
M
are two constants.
We say that
{\lambda}\in\real
is an “eigenvalue for
A
” if there exists

e\in\mathbb{H}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
with

\left\langle Ae,v\right\rangle={\lambda}\left\langle e,v\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}}\qquad\forall v\in\mathbb{H}

which corresponds to say that:

Ae={\lambda}i^{*}e.

In this case we say that
e
is an “eigenvector” corrsponding to
{\lambda}
.

It is well kwown that there exists a sequence
({\lambda}_{n})
of
eigenvalues of
A
with

{\lambda}_{n}\leq{\lambda}_{n+1}
,
{\lambda}_{n}\to+\infty
, such that the corresponding eigenvectors generate
\mathbb{H}
. It is convenient to agreee
that
{\lambda}_{0}=-\infty
.
We can suppose that for any
k\geq 1
we are given an eigenvector
e_{k}
relative to
{\lambda}_{k}
with

\left\lVert e_{n}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{L}}=1
, and:

\left\langle e_{n},e_{m}\right\rangle=\left\langle e_{n},e_{m}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}}=0\qquad\mbox{ if }n\neq m.

If
{\lambda}\in\real
we define:

E^{-}_{\lambda}:=\spanned\left\{e_{i}{\,:\,}{\lambda}_{i}<{\lambda}\right\},\ E^{0}_{\lambda}:=\spanned\left\{e_{i}{\,:\,}{\lambda}_{i}={\lambda}\right\},\ E^{+}_{\lambda}:=\overline{\spanned\left\{e_{i}{\,:\,}{\lambda}_{i}>{\lambda}\right\}}^{(\mathbb{H})}

(
E^{0}_{\lambda}=\left\{0\right\}
if
{\lambda}
is not an eigenvalue). If
{\lambda}_{n}\leq{\lambda}\leq{\lambda}_{n+1}
it is clear that:

\sup_{\left\{u\in E^{-}_{\lambda}{\,:\,}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\mathbb{L}}=1\right\}}\left\langle Au,u\right\rangle\leq{\lambda}_{n}\quad,\quad\inf_{\left\{u\in E^{+}{\lambda}{\,:\,}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\mathbb{L}}=1\right\}}\left\langle Au,u\right\rangle\geq{\lambda}_{n+1},

while
\left\langle Au,u\right\rangle={\lambda}
, if
u\in E^{0}_{\lambda}
.

3.1 Theorem

(Bifurcation)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{H};\real)
,
\mathcal{H}_{1}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{L};\real)

be such that:

\begin{gathered}\mathcal{H}(0)=0,\quad\nabla\mathcal{H}(0)=0,\quad\lim_{u\to 0}\frac{\left\lVert\nabla\mathcal{H}(u)\right\rVert_{\mathbb{L}}}{\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\mathbb{L}}}=0\\
\mathcal{H}_{1}(0)=0,\quad\nabla_{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{H}_{1}(0)=0,\quad\lim_{u\to 0}\frac{\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{H}_{1}(u)\right\rVert_{\mathbb{L}}}{\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\mathbb{L}}}=0.\end{gathered}

(12)

Notice that we are using the symbol
\nabla
to denote the gradient with
respect to the inner product in
\mathbb{H}
and
\nabla_{\mathbb{L}}
for the corresponding
gradient in
\mathbb{L}
.

Let
\hat{\lambda}
be an eigenvalue for
A
.
Then, for any
\rho>0
small, there exist
(u_{1,\rho},{\lambda}_{1,\rho})
and

(u_{2,\rho},{\lambda}_{2,\rho})
which solve
the problem:

Au+\nabla\mathcal{H}(u)={\lambda}i^{*}\left(u+\nabla_{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{H}_{1}(u)\right)\qquad\qquad u\neq 0.

(13)

such that

u_{1,\rho}\neq u_{2,\rho}
and:

u_{1,\rho}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{H}}0\quad,\quad u_{2,\rho}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{H}}0\quad,\quad{\lambda}_{1,\rho}\to\hat{\lambda}\quad,\quad{\lambda}_{2,\rho}\to\hat{\lambda}\qquad\qquad\mbox{as }\rho\to 0.

(14)

Proof.

We adapt the proof of Lemma 3.4 in
[
2
]
.
Let
\hat{\lambda}={\lambda}_{i}={\lambda}_{k}
with
{\lambda}_{i-1}<{\lambda}_{i}
and

{\lambda}_{k}<{\lambda}_{k+1}
.
We define

f:\mathbb{H}\to\real
and
g:\mathbb{L}\to\real
by:

f(u):=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle Au,u\right\rangle+\mathcal{H}(u)\quad,\quad g(u):=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\mathbb{L}}^{2}+\mathcal{H}_{1}(u),

Let
\mathcal{C}:=\left\{u\in\mathbb{L}{\,:\,}1<\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\mathbb{L}}<2\right\}
.
Moreover, if
0<\rho<1
we define:

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(u):=\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}f(\rho u),\quad g_{\rho}(u):=\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}g(\rho u),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rho}(u):=\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\mathcal{H}(\rho u),\quad\mathcal{H}_{1,\rho}(u):=\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\mathcal{H}_{1}(\rho u),

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\rho}:=\left\{u\in\mathcal{C}{\,:\,}g_{\rho}(u)=1\right\}.

In fact

f_{\rho}(u)=\dfrac{1}{2}\left\langle Au,u\right\rangle+\mathcal{H}_{\rho}(u)

and
g_{\rho}(u)=\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\mathbb{L}}^{2}+\mathcal{H}_{1,\rho}(u)
.

Since the result we are proving only involves the behaviour of
\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}_{1}
near zero, we are allowed to modify
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}_{1}
outside of
a small ball around the origin.
More precisely using (
12
) we can find
R
in
]0,1/3[
such that

\left\lVert\nabla\mathcal{H}(u)\right\rVert\leq\frac{\nu}{8}\left\lVert u\right\rVert\ \forall u\mbox{ with }\left\lVert u\right\rVert<3R,\quad\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{H}_{1}(u)\right\rVert\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{1}\ \forall u\mbox{ with }\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{1}<3R,

(15)

and define
\tilde{}\mathcal{H}(u):=\eta(\left\lVert u\right\rVert)\mathcal{H}(u)
,

\tilde{}\mathcal{H}_{1}(u):=\eta(\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{1})\mathcal{H}_{1}(u)
, where

is a cutoff function with

for

,

for
s\geq 3R
, and
\eta^{\prime}(s)\leq 1
. Now since

\tilde{}\mathcal{H}(u)=\mathcal{H}(u)\quad\forall u\mbox{ with }\left\lVert u\right\rVert<R,\qquad\tilde{}\mathcal{H}_{1}(u)=\mathcal{H}_{1}(u)\quad\forall u\mbox{ with }\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\mathbb{L}}<R,

holds for

. Indeed the first component

of a bifucation branch (for any of the two problems) eventually verifies

and
\left\lVert u_{\rho}\right\rVert_{1}<R
. So from now on we
replace
\mathcal{H}
with
\tilde{}\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}_{1}
with
\tilde{}\mathcal{H}_{1}
, maintaining the same notation. With simple computations we can deduce from (

) that the redifined functions verify:

{\rm a}\quad\left|\nabla\mathcal{H}(u)\right|\leq\frac{\nu}{4}\left\lVert u\right\rVert\ \forall u\in\mathbb{H},\qquad{\rm b}\quad\left|\nabla_{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{H}_{1}(u)\right|\leq\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\mathbb{L}}\ \forall u\in\mathbb{L}.

(16)

From a in (
16
) we get

|\mathcal{H}(u)|\leq\frac{\nu}{4}\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}\Rightarrow|\mathcal{H}_{\rho}(u)|\leq\frac{\nu}{4}\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}\qquad\forall u\in\mathbb{H},\,\forall\rho\in[0,1]

(17)

Using (
11
) and (
17
) we get that:

\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{4}{\nu}\left(f_{\rho}(u)+M\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathbb{L}}\right)

(18)

From (
12
) and (

)
we easily get that, if

and

:

\begin{gathered}\sup_{u\in\mathcal{C},f_{\rho}(u)\leq c}|\mathcal{H}_{\rho}(u)|\to 0\quad,\quad\sup_{u\in\mathcal{C}}|\mathcal{H}_{1,\rho}(u)|\to 0,\\

(19)

So if we extend the definition to
\rho=0
by letting

f_{0}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle Au,u\right\rangle
and
g_{0}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathbb{L}}

then

.
We also define
\mathcal{S}_{\rho}
for

Bayesian Implications for the Primordial Black Holes from NANOGrav’s Pulsar-Timing Data Using the Scalar-Induced Gravitational Waves
Zhi-Chao Zhao, Sai Wang *

Abstract
Assuming that the common-spectrum process in the NANOGrav 12.5-year dataset has an origin of scalar-induced gravitational waves, we study the enhancement of primordial curvature perturbations and the mass function of primordial black holes, by performing the Bayesian parameter inference for the first time. We obtain lower limits on the spectral amplitude, i.e., 𝒜 ≳ 10 − 2 \mathcal{A}\gtrsim 10^{-2} at 95% confidence level, when assuming the power spectrum of primordial curvature perturbations to follow a log-normal distribution function with width σ \sigma . In the case of σ → 0 \sigma\rightarrow 0 , we find that the primordial black holes with 2 × 10 − 4 − 10 − 2 2\times 10^{-4}-10^{-2} solar mass are allowed to compose at least a fraction 10 − 6 10^{-6} of dark matter. Such a mass range is shifted to more massive regimes for larger values of σ \sigma , e.g., to a regime of 4 × 10 − 3 − 0.2 4\times 10^{-3}-0.2 solar mass in the case of σ = 1 \sigma=1 . We expect the planned gravitational-wave experiments to have their best sensitivity to 𝒜 \mathcal{A} in the range of 10 − 4 10^{-4} to 10 − 7 10^{-7} , depending on the experimental setups. With this level of sensitivity, we can search for primordial black holes throughout the entire parameter space, especially in the mass range of 10 − 16 10^{-16} to 10 − 11 10^{-11} solar masses, where they could account for all dark matter. In addition, the importance of multi-band detector networks is emphasized to accomplish our theoretical expectation.

Introduction

It is well-known that the primordial black holes could be produced by gravitational collapses of the enhanced primordial curvature perturbations on small scales
Hawking 1971
.
The mass function of primordial black holes is determined by the power spectrum of primordial curvature perturbations
Khlopov 2010
;
Belotsky et al. 2014
;
Franciolini et al. 2022
.
To produce a sizeable quantity of primordial black holes, the scalar spectral amplitude should conquer a critical magnitude
\sim
0.1

Musco et al. 2021
, which is about eight orders of magnitudes larger than the spectral amplitude on large scales.
On the other hand, the dynamics of cosmic inflation
Guth 1981
;
Linde 1982
;
Starobinsky 1980
is one of the greatest puzzles of cosmology to be uncovered.
In particular, the small-scale primordial curvature perturbations were generated by quantum fluctuations at the late-time stage of inflation.
Their power spectrum encodes characteristic information about the late-time dynamics of inflation, which could be different from that of the early-time one measured precisely with experiments on cosmic microwave background
Hinshaw et al. 2013
;
Aghanim et al. 2020
and large-scale structure
Alam et al. 2017
;
Abbott et al. 2022
.
Therefore, we can gain more information about cosmic inflation by investigating the primordial black holes.

The scenario of primordial black holes can also be a reasonable candidate of dark matter, or compose a part of the latter
Carr and Hawking 1974
;
Chapline 1975
. The mass function of primordial black holes is defined as a ratio between the energy density fractions of primordial black holes and dark matter.
Extensive studies have shown observational constraints on the mass function of primordial black holes (e.g., see reviews in Refs.
Carr et al. 2021
;
Escrivà et al. 2022
).
In addition, the scenario of primordial black holes was suggested to interpret the formation mechanism of supermassive black holes
Kohri et al. 2022
and the events of compact binary coalescences
Sasaki et al. 2016
;
Raidal et al. 2017
;
Ali-Haïmoud et al. 2017
;
Chen and Huang 2018
;
Nishikawa et al. 2019
;
Bird et al. 2016
;
Wang and Zhao 2022
;
Belotsky et al. 2019
, which emitted the gravitational waves observed by the Advanced LIGO, Virgo and KAGRA detectors
Harry 2010
;
Acernese et al. 2015
;
Somiya 2012
.

Accompanied by the formation process of primordial black holes, the scalar induced gravitational waves
Mollerach et al. 2004
;
Ananda et al. 2007
;
Baumann et al. 2007
;
Assadullahi and Wands 2010
were inevitably produced by the enhanced primordial curvature perturbations.
Specifically, they were produced through nonlinear couplings of tensor-scalar-scalar modes in the very early cosmos.
Given the power spectrum of primordial curvature perturbations, a semi-analytic formula was provided to compute the energy density fraction spectrum of scalar induced gravitational waves
Kohri and Terada 2018
;
Espinosa et al. 2018
.
It has been widely used to investigate the mass function of primordial black holes in the literature.

In this work, we will investigate the mass function of primordial black holes, as well as the power spectrum of primordial curvature perturbations, by analyzing a 12.5-year dataset from North American Nanohertz Observatory for Gravitational Waves
†
†
endnote:
http://data.nanograv.org
, version v3

Arzoumanian et al. 2020
.
Strong evidence of a stochastic common-spectrum process was reported by North American Nanohertz Observatory for Gravitational Waves Collaboration.
If this signal has an origin of scalar induced gravitational waves, we can straightforwardly obtain constraints on the power spectrum of primordial curvature perturbations.
Since the latter can collapse to form the primordial black holes due to gravity, we therefore obtain corresponding constraints on the mass function of primordial black holes.
In fact, the scenario of primordial black holes with different masses has been suggested to interpret such a signal (see, e.g., Refs.
De Luca et al. 2021
;
Vaskonen and Veermäe 2021
;
Kohri and Terada 2021
;
Domènech and Pi 2022
;
Atal et al. 2021
;
Yi and Fei 2022
).
However, there is still lack of systematic Bayesian parameter inferences, which will be performed for the first time in this paper.

The rest of the paper is arranged as follows. We briefly review the theory of scalar induced gravitational waves in Section
2
. By performing Bayesian analysis, we obtain the North American Nanohertz Observatory for Gravitational Waves constraints on the enhancement of primordial curvature perturbations in Section
3
. Correspondingly, we demonstrate the physical implications for the mass function of primordial black holes in Section
4
. We forecast the sensitivity curves of ongoing and planned gravitational-wave detectors in Section
5
. The conclusion and discussion are shown in Section
6
.

2
Theory of Scalar-Induced Gravitational Waves

As previously shown
Kohri and Terada 2018
;
Espinosa et al. 2018
, there is a lengthy and tedious derivation process to obtain a semi-analytic formula for the energy density fraction spectrum of scalar induced gravitational waves in the early cosmos.
Therefore, we summarize some theoretical results that would be essential to our data analysis.
The energy density fraction spectrum of gravitational waves is defined as
\Omega_{\mathrm{GW}}(k)=\rho_{\mathrm{GW}}(k)/\rho_{c}
, where
k
denotes the gravitational wave wavenumber,
\rho_{c}
is the critical energy density of the cosmos, and
\int\rho_{\mathrm{GW}}(k)d\ln k
denotes the total energy density of gravitational waves.
The scalar induced gravitational waves in the subhorizon have the energy density spectrum as
\rho_{\mathrm{GW}}\sim\langle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muh_{ij,l}h_{ij,l}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\rangle

Maggiore 2007
, where the overbar stands for the oscillation average and the angle brackets defines the power spectrum.
The strain of scalar induced gravitational waves, as the second-order tensor perturbations produced by the linear scalar perturbations, can be roughly expressed as
h\sim\phi^{2}
, where
\phi
denotes the linear scalar perturbation.
If considering the Gaussian scalar perturbations, we obtain
\rho_{\mathrm{GW}}\sim\langle\phi^{4}\rangle\sim\langle\phi^{2}\rangle\langle\phi^{2}\rangle
, where
\langle\phi^{2}\rangle
is determined by the initial value and transfer function of
\phi
.
Therefore, following Equation (14) of Ref.
Kohri and Terada 2018
, the energy density fraction spectrum of scalar induced gravitational waves in the radiation-dominated cosmos is given as

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathrm{GW}}(k)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dv\int_{\lvert 1-v\rvert}^{\lvert 1+v\rvert}du\ F(v,u)\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(vk)\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(uk)\ ,

(1)

where
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(uk)
(and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(vk)
) is power spectrum of primordial curvature perturbations at wavenumber
uk
(and
vk
) with
u
(and
v
) being a dimensionless variable, and
F(v,u)
is a functional of the scalar transfer function describing the post-inflationary evolution of
\phi
and can be simplified as

\displaystyle F(v,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2^{10}v^{8}u^{8}}\left[4v^{2}-(v^{2}-u^{2}+1)^{2}\right]^{2}\left(v^{2}+u^{2}-3\right)^{2}

(2)

\displaystyle\ \times\bigg\{\left[\left(v^{2}+u^{2}-3\right)\ln\left(\left|\frac{3-(v+u)^{2}}{3-(v-u)^{2}}\right|\right)-4vu\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\ +\pi^{2}\left(v^{2}+u^{2}-3\right)^{2}\Theta\left(v+u-\sqrt{3}\right)\bigg\}\ .

Due to a lack of direct measurements, the nature of primordial curvature perturbations on small scales remains unknown, compared with that on the largest scales measured by the cosmic microwave background.
For computational simplicity, we take two concrete expressions of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)
, which are frequently adopted in the literature (e.g., Refs.
Wang et al. 2019
;
Bugaev and Klimai 2010
;
Kohri and Terada 2018
;
Adshead et al. 2021
;
Ferrante et al. 2022
), as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)=\mathcal{A}\ \delta\left(\ln{\frac{k}{k_{\ast}}}\right)\ ,

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)=\frac{\mathcal{A}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\exp{\left(-\frac{\ln^{2}\left(k/k_{\ast}\right)}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)}\ ,

(4)

where
\mathcal{A}
is an amplitude,

\sigma
is a standard variance,
and
k_{\ast}
is a pivot wavenumber.
We can relate the wavenumber with the frequency, i.e.,
k=2\pi f
and
k_{\ast}=2\pi f_{\ast}
.
During the process of parameter inferences, both
\mathcal{A}
and
f_{\ast}
are independent parameters.
However, we will not let
\sigma
be independent, even though different values of
\sigma
would alter our results.
In fact, it is challenging to explore the full parameter space of
\sigma
based on the current dataset.
Therefore, we let
\sigma=1
for simplicity in this work.
We can test the robustness of our conclusions by comparing the results obtained when taking two different values of
\sigma
.
In particular, we will compare the results from Equations (
3
) and (
4
).
We leave this point to be demonstrated further in the following sections.

In Figure
1
, we show the energy density fraction spectrum of scalar induced gravitational waves, which is normalized with
\mathcal{A}^{2}
.
For comparison, we show the results when taking different versions of
\sigma
. Equation (
3
) can be viewed as a limit of Equation (
4
) when
\sigma\rightarrow 0
.
In the regime of
f\ll f_{\ast}
, the choice of
\sigma\rightarrow 0
displays the highest spectral amplitude than non-vanishing choices of
\sigma
.
This implies that in such a case, we can obtain the best sensitivity on measurements of
\mathcal{A}
for a given gravitational wave experiment. Correspondingly, we can obtain the strictest constraints on the mass function of primordial black holes.
We will demonstrate these points in the following sections.

\Omega_{\mathrm{GW}}(k)
is given in Equation (

3
NANOGrav Constraints on Primordial Curvature Perturbations

When a pulsar timing array experiment measures the stochastic gravitaitonal wave background, the timing residual cross-power spectral density is given by
S_{ab}(f)=\Gamma_{ab}h_{c}^{2}/(12\pi^{2}f^{3})
, which can be shown by combining Equations (1) and (2) in Arzoumanian et al.
Arzoumanian et al. 2018
.
Here,
\Gamma_{ab}
stands for the overlap reduction function that describes the correlation between two pulsars
a
and
b
as a function of line-of-sight separation angle between them.
Following Equation (17) in Maggiore
Maggiore 2000
,
h_{c}(f)
is the characteristic strain defined as
h_{c}^{2}=3H_{0}^{2}\Omega_{\mathrm{GW},0}/(2\pi^{2}f^{2})
, where
H_{0}=100h_{0}\ \mathrm{km}\ \mathrm{s}^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}
is the Hubble constant with
h_{0}
being the reduced Hubble constant.
Therefore, the timing residual cross-power spectral density becomes
Arzoumanian et al. 2018

S_{ab}=\Gamma_{ab}\frac{H_{0}^{2}f_{\mathrm{yr}}^{-5}}{8\pi^{4}}\left(\frac{f}{f_{\mathrm{yr}}}\right)^{-5}\Omega_{\mathrm{GW},0}(f)\ ,

(7)

where
f_{\mathrm{yr}}
is a pivot frequency corresponding to a duration time of 1 year, and the formula for
\Omega_{\mathrm{GW},0}(f)
is shown in Equation (
5
).
For an isotropic background of gravitational waves, e.g., the scalar induced gravitational waves considered in this work, we take overlap reduction function to be the Hellings and Downs coefficients
Hellings and Downs 1983
.

The timing residual data for a single pulsar is decomposed in its individual constituents, i.e.,
Arzoumanian et al. 2016

\delta\bm{t}=M\bm{\epsilon}+F\bm{a}+U\bm{j}+\bm{n}\ .

(8)

The term
M\bm{\epsilon}
stands for the inaccuracies in the subtraction of timing model, where
M
denotes the timing model design matrix, and
\bm{\epsilon}
is a vector describing small offsets for the timing model parameters.
The matrix
M
is computed through
libstempo

Michele 2020
†
†
endnote:
https://vallis.github.io/libstempo/
, version 2.4.5

, which is a python interface for
TEMPO2

Hobbs et al. 2006
;
Edwards et al. 2006
†
†
endnote:
https://bitbucket.org/psrsoft/tempo2.git
, version 2021.07.1

timing package.
We use the latest Jet Propulsion Laboratory solar system ephemeris model, DE438
Folkner and Park 2018
, in the timing model fits.
The term
F\bm{a}
accounts for all low-frequency signals, including the pulsar-intrinsic red noise. The Fourier design matrix
F
has alternating sine and cosine functions, and
\bm{a}
is a vector comprised of Fourier coefficients at the integer (
1,2,\ldots,N_{\mathrm{mode}}
) multiples of the harmonic base frequency
1/T_{s}
, where
T_{s}
denotes the span between the minimum and maximum time of arrivals in the array
van Haasteren and Vallisneri 2014
.
Described by a per-epoch variance (ECORR) for each receiver and backend system
Arzoumanian et al. 2016
, the term
U\bm{j}
denotes the white noise which is fully correlated for simultaneous observations at different frequencies, but fully uncorrelated in time.
The matrix
U
maps all time of arrivals observed simultaneously at different frequencies to a total time of arrival, and
\bm{j}
is the per-epoch white noise which is fully correlated across all observing frequencies.
The term
\bm{n}
is the timing residual introduced by Gaussian white noise, which is described by the parameters of the time of arrival uncertainties (EFAC) and an additive variance (EQUAD) for each receiver and backend system
Arzoumanian et al. 2016
.

4
Implications for Primordial Black Holes

We define the mass function of primordial black holes as
f_{\mathrm{pbh}}(m)=\Omega_{\mathrm{dm}}^{-1}d\Omega_{\mathrm{pbh}}/d\ln m
, in which
\Omega_{\mathrm{dm}}
and
\Omega_{\mathrm{pbh}}
stand for the present energy density fractions of dark matter and primordial black holes with mass
m
, respectively.
We adopt the concept of critical collapse
Carr et al. 2016
;
Yokoyama 1998
and Press-Schechter formalism
Press and Schechter 1974
.
Therefore, following Equation (9) in Wang et al.
Wang et al. 2019
, we have

\displaystyle f_{\mathrm{pbh}}(m)=\frac{\Omega_{\mathrm{m}}}{\Omega_{\mathrm{dm}}}\int g(T)\tilde{\beta}(m,m_{H})d\ln m_{H}\ ,

(9)

where
\Omega_{\mathrm{m}}
is the present energy density fraction of non-relativistic matter.
Here, we denote

\displaystyle g(T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{\ast,\rho}(T)}{g_{\ast,\rho}(T_{\mathrm{eq}})}\frac{g_{\ast,s}(T_{\mathrm{eq}})}{g_{\ast,s}(T)}\frac{T}{T_{\mathrm{eq}}}\ ,

(10)

\displaystyle\tilde{\beta}(m,m_{H})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa\mu^{\gamma+1}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\gamma\Delta(k)}\mathrm{exp}\left[-\frac{\left(\delta_{c}+\mu\right)^{2}}{2\Delta^{2}(k)}\right]\ ,

(11)

where we have
\mu=[{m}/({\kappa m_{H}})]^{1/\gamma}
with numerical constants
\kappa=3.3
and
\gamma=0.36
, and the critical overdensity for gravitational collapse is
\delta_{c}=0.45
.
Here, we disregard corrections to
\delta_{c}
from, e.g., QCD equation of state
Byrnes et al. 2018
.
The quantity
\tilde{\beta}(m,m_{H})
is a mass distribution function of primordial black holes, which were produced when the horizon mass was
m_{H}
.
The subscript
eq
stands for cosmological quantities at the epoch of matter-radiation equality.
Cosmic temperature
T
is related to
m_{H}
enclosed by the Hubble horizon, i.e.,
Wang et al. 2019

\frac{m_{H}}{M_{\odot}}=4.76\times 10^{-2}\left(\frac{T}{\mathrm{GeV}}\right)^{-2}\left[\frac{g_{\ast,\rho}(T)}{106.75}\right]^{-\frac{1}{2}}\ ,

(12)

where
M_{\odot}
denotes the solar mass.
We can relate
f
with
m_{H}
by combining Equation (
6
) with Equation (
12
) and by reducing
T
from both equations.
The coarse-grained fluctuations in the radiation-dominated cosmos are given by
Young et al. 2014
;
Ando et al. 2018

\Delta^{2}(k)=\frac{16}{81}\int d\ln q\ \left[w\left(\frac{q}{k}\right)\right]^{2}\left(\frac{q}{k}\right)^{4}\mathcal{T}^{2}\left({q},\frac{1}{k}\right)\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(q)\ ,

(13)

where
w(y)=\mathrm{exp}(-y^{2}/2)
is a Gaussian window function and
\mathcal{T}(q,\tau)=3(\sin{x}-x\cos{x})/x^{3}
with
x=q\tau/\sqrt{3}
is a scalar transfer function.
We further define the total abundance of primordial black holes in dark matter to be
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muf\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{\mathrm{pbh}}=\int f_{\mathrm{pbh}}(m)d\ln m
, and define the average mass of primordial black holes to be
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mum\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu=\int f_{\mathrm{pbh}}(m)dm/\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muf\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{\mathrm{pbh}}
.
The latter roughly displays
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mum\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\simeq m_{\ast}
, where
m_{\ast}
is corresponded to
k_{\ast}
.

Schmitz 2021

\rho^{2}=n_{\mathrm{det}}T_{\mathrm{obs}}\int_{f_{\mathrm{min}}}^{f_{\mathrm{max}}}\left[\frac{S_{\mathrm{GW}}(f)}{S_{n}^{\mathrm{eff}}(f)}\right]^{2}df\ ,

(14)

S_{\mathrm{GW}}(f)=\left(\frac{3H_{0}^{2}}{2\pi^{2}f^{3}}\right)\Omega_{\mathrm{GW},0}(f)\ ,

(15)

and the effective noise power spectral density of the detector network is denoted as
S_{n}^{\mathrm{eff}}(f)
that is a function of
f
.

6
Conclusions and Discussion

In this work, we obtained new constraints on the power spectrum of primordial curvature perturbations and the mass function of primordial black holes by searching for the energy density fraction spectrum of scalar induced gravitational waves in the North American Nanohertz Observatory for Gravitational Waves 12.5-year dataset.
We found the lower limits on
\mathcal{A}
, namely,
\mathcal{A}\gtrsim 10^{-2}
and showed the 95% CL contours of
\mathcal{A}
and
f_{\ast}
(see Figure
2
).
Recasting these contours into the primordial black hole mass-abundance plane, we showed the parameter space of primordial black holes allowed by the North American Nanohertz Observatory for Gravitational Waves (see Figure
3
).
We found that at least a fraction of dark matter can be interpreted with the scenario of primordial black holes in the mass range
\sim
(10^{-4}-10^{-1})M_{\odot}
. We also studied dependence of the above results on the value of
\sigma
.
Furthermore, this mass range is expected to be cross-checked with high-precision by observing the gravitational waves from primordial black hole mergers (see Figure 3 in Wang et al.
Wang et al. 2019
).
We also found that the scalar induced gravitational wave probe is much more powerful than other probes to search for the primordial black holes.

We further forecasted the sensitivity curves of ongoing and planned gravitational wave experiments on detection of primordial black holes and the primordial curvature perturbations by searching for scalar induced gravitational waves.
We found that the primordial curvature perturbations with spectral amplitude larger than
\sim
(10^{-4}-10^{-7})
can be measured with planned gravitational wave detection programs.
The sensitivity of Advanced LIGO-Virgo to detect the scalar induced gravitational waves was expected to be improved by one order of magnitude in the near future.
This prediction may promote extensive investigations of cosmic inflation at late-time stages.
Meanwhile, the scenario of primordial black holes within almost whole mass range can be thoroughly tested, since the critical spectral amplitude to form the primordial black holes is much larger than
10^{-4}
(see Figures
4
and
5
).
In particular, we can search for the primordial black holes within mass range
(10^{-16}-10^{-11})M_{\odot}
, which can compose all of the dark matter and otherwise are beyond capabilities of other probes.
In addition, we emphasized the importance of multi-band gravitational wave detector networks for accomplishing the above theoretical expectations.

In this paper, we made several assumptions to simplify our computations.
First, we disregarded possible contributions of primordial non-Gaussianity to the formation of primordial black holes
Franciolini et al. 2018
;
Gow et al. 2022
;
Cai et al. 2022
;
Ferrante et al. 2022
;
Kitajima et al. 2021
and to the production of scalar induced gravitational waves
Adshead et al. 2021
. It is an interesting topic to study the primordial non-Gaussianity, deserving an independent work.
Second, we took into account the median value of the effective relativistic degrees of freedom of the early Universe, but disregarded their uncertainties
Saikawa and Shirai 2018
.
In fact, changing the above two assumptions could alter the mass function of primordial black holes and the energy density fraction spectrum of scalar induced gravitational waves.
We would leave a possible study of this question to future works.
Third, we disregarded contributions of nonlinear cosmological perturbations to the energy density fraction spectrum of scalar induced gravitational waves, since there is not a complete theory of scalar induced gravitational waves at the third order
Yuan et al. 2019
;
Zhou et al. 2022
;
Chang et al. 2022
;
Chen et al. 2022
;
Meng et al. 2022
.
We might revisit this assumption in future, once the theory is complete.

Geometry of submanifolds of all classes of third-order ODEs as a Riemannian manifold
Z. Bakhshandeh-Chamazkoti, A. Behzadi, R. Bakhshandeh-Chamazkoti, and M. Rafie-Rad, [3mm] Department of Mathematics, Faculty of Mathematical Sciences, University of Mazandaran, Babolsar, Iran., [3mm] Department of Mathematics, Faculty of Basic Sciences, Babol Noshirvani University of Technology, Babol, Iran., [3mm] Emails: z.bakhshandeh.ch@stu.umz.ac.ir, behzadi@umz.ac.ir, r_bakhshandeh@nit.ac.ir, rafie-rad@umz.ac.ir.

Abstract
In this paper, we prove that any surface corresponding to linear second-order ODEs as a submanifold is minimal in all classes of third-order ODEs y ′′′ = f ⁡ ( x , y , p , q ) y^{\prime\prime\prime}=f(x,y,p,q) as a Riemannian manifold where y ′ = p y^{\prime}=p and y ′′ = q y^{\prime\prime}=q , if and only if q y ​ y = 0 q_{yy}=0 . Moreover, we will see the linear second-order ODE with general form y ′′ = ± y + β ⁡ ( x ) y^{\prime\prime}=\pm y+\beta(x) is the only case that is defined a minimal surface and is also totally geodesic.



Keywords:
Levi-Civita connection, minimal surface, moving frame, Riemannian manifold, Riemann curvature tensor, totally geodesic.

Originally, the geometry of submanifolds was only a part of Riemannian geometry

There are many papers as applications of Cartan method for third-order ODEs

The theory of moving frames is most closely associated with the name of Cartan, that is
a powerful and algorithmic tool for studying the geometric properties of submanifolds
and their invariants under the action of a transformation group

Now if we apply the moving frame method for Riemannian manifold and geometry of submanifolds, we can obtain very interesting results.
Recently, a lot of research has been carried out about the minimal surfaces in a three-dimensional Riemannian manifold

The graph of the function

\displaystyle(1+u_{x}^{2})\;u_{yy}-2u_{x}u_{y}u_{xy}+(1+u_{y}^{2})\;u_{xx}=0.

(1)

The PDE (

We will not discuss the general theory
of existence of solutions to this equation, but rather describe a couple

There is an analogous theorem (which actually predates Calabi’s) in the
case of the Riemannian metric
\langle.,.\rangle_{0}=dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2}+dx_{3}^{2}

and is known as the Bernstein theorem.

It is a classical result that any simply connected minimal surface in Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
admits a one-parameter family of minimal isometric deformations, called the

It has been shown in Bayrakdar et al.

The main idea for writing present paper is raised from the papers

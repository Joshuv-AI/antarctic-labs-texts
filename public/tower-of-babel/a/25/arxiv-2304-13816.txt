Abstract
Constant-rate multi-mode systems (MMS) are hybrid systems with finitely many modes and real-valued variables that evolve over continuous time according to mode-specific constant rates. We introduce a variant of linear temporal logic (LTL) for MMS, and we investigate the complexity of the model-checking problem for syntactic fragments of LTL. We obtain a complexity landscape where each fragment is either P-complete, NP-complete or undecidable. These results generalize and unify several results on MMS and continuous counter systems.

Introduction

Constant-rate multi-mode systems (MMS) are hybrid systems with
finitely many modes and a finite number of real-valued variables that
evolve over continuous time according to mode-specific constant rates.

MMS were originally introduced by Alur
et al.
to model,
e.g.
, problems
related to green scheduling and reducing energy peak consumption of
systems
[
1
]
. There, they consider the problems of safe
schedulability and safe reachability with respect to zones defined as
bounded convex polytopes.

Safe schedulability
asks whether a given MMS admits a
non-Zeno

1

Safe
reachability
asks whether a given MMS has a finite execution that

the way. Both problems were shown to be solvable in polynomial

[
1

.

A similar problem was studied by Krishna
et al.
in the context of

[
2

. There, the authors are

is to reach a given target point without ever entering any of the
given obstacles. The authors of
[
2
]
consider

reach-avoid problem is decidable if the obstacles are closed, and is
undecidable in general. They further provide an implementation of
their procedure which is benchmarked positively against the
Open
Motion Planning Library
.

Contribution

The aforementioned problems were solved with ad hoc
approaches. Moreover, many natural problems cannot be expressed in
these existing frameworks. One such problem is
safe repeated
reachability
, where the goal is to find a non-Zeno infinite
execution that remains within a safety zone and visits a finite set of
zones infinitely often.

We propose a framework that encompasses all of the above. More
precisely, we introduce a linear temporal logic (LTL) for MMS. Our
variant uses bounded convex polytopes as atomic propositions. We omit
the next operator
X
which is ill-suited for the continuous behavior
of MMS. Moreover, we use a strict-future interpretation of the until
temporal operator
\mathrel{\textsf{U}}
, inspired from metric temporal
logic
[
3
]
(more precisely from

\text{MITL}_{0,\infty})
. In particular, our logic can express

•

Safe schedulability:
{\textsf{G}}\,Z_{\text{safe}}
;

•

Safe reachability:
Z_{\text{safe}}\mathrel{\textsf{U}}\{\bm{x}_{\text{target}}\}
;

•

Reach-avoid:
(\neg O_{1}\land\cdots\land\neg O_{n})\mathrel{\textsf{U}}\{\bm{x}_{\text{target}}\}
; and

•

Safe repeated reachability:
({\textsf{G}}\,Z_{\text{safe}})\land\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}({\textsf{G}}{\textsf{F}}\,Z_{i})
.

We investigate the computational complexity of LTL model checking,
which asks, given an MMS
\mathit{M}
, a starting point
\bm{x}
and an LTL
formula
\varphi
, whether there is a non-Zeno infinite execution of

\mathit{M}
that satisfies
\varphi
from
\bm{x}
, denoted
\bm{x}\models_{\mathit{M}}\varphi
. We consider the syntactic fragments obtained by
(dis)allowing operators from
\{\mathrel{\textsf{U}},{\textsf{F}},{\textsf{G}},\land,\lor,\neg\}
and
allowing at least one temporal operator

Our work is also closely related to the study of counter systems like
vector addition systems (VAS) and Petri nets. These models have
countless applications ranging from program verification and

The behavior of an MMS amounts to continuous pseudo-reachability of
VAS and Petri nets,

where the effect of transitions can be scaled

Further related work

MMS are related to hybrid
automata
[
13
]
. Contrary to MMS, however, the
latter allow for a finite control structure, and modes of non-constant
rates. Their immense modelling power leads to the undecidability of
most problems, including reachability,
i.e.
formulas of the form
{\textsf{F}}\,\{\bm{x}_{\text{target}}\}
. Yet, some researchers have investigated
decision procedures for temporal specification languages such as
signal temporal logic (
e.g.
, see
[
14
]
).

Timed automata
[
15
]
form another related type of
hybrid system. In this model, all variables (known as clocks)
increase at the
same
constant rate, as opposed to the case of
MMS. On the other hand, timed automata are equipped with a finite
control structure, which is not the case of MMS.

Bounded-rate multi-mode systems generalize MMS
[
16
,
17
]
. In this model, the mode-dependent rates are given as
bounded convex polytopes. The setting can be seen as a two-player
game. Player 1 chooses a mode and a duration, and Player 2 chooses the
rates from the set for that mode. The system evolves according to the
rates chosen by Player 2. In this context, “schedulability” is a
strategy for Player 1 that never leaves the safety zone, no matter the
choices of Player 2.

Small fragments of classical LTL have been investigated in the
literature,
e.g.
see
[
18
, Table 1]
. In
particular, LTL(
\{{\textsf{F}},{\textsf{G}},\allowbreak\land,\allowbreak\lor,\allowbreak\neg\}
)
has been studied in
[
19
,
11
]
under the names
\mathrm{L}({\textsf{F}})

and
({\textsf{F}},{\textsf{G}})
, and LTL(
\{{\textsf{F}},\land\}
) has been studied
in
[
20
]
under the name
\textsc{LTL}_{+}(\Diamond,\land)
. The
authors of
[
20
]
show that a fragment, called

\textsc{LTL}^{\text{PODB}}
, and which is incomparable
to LTL(
\{{\textsf{F}},{\textsf{G}},\land\}
), admits partially-ordered deterministic
Büchi automata of exponential size and linear width. To the best
of our knowledge, there is no work dedicated
to LTL(
\{{\textsf{F}},{\textsf{G}},\land\}
), and in particular to its translation into
automata of linear width.

In

, we introduce basic definitions, MMS and

Preliminaries

We write
\mathbb{N}
to denote

,
\mathbb{Z}
to denote the integers, and

to denote the

. We write

and
[a..b]\coloneqq\{i\in\mathbb{N}:a\leq i\leq b\}
. We also use (semi-)open intervals,
e.g.

(1,2]=[1,2]\setminus\{1\}
.

Let
I
be a set of indices and let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{I}
. We write

\bm{e}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{I}
for the vector with
\bm{e}_{i}(i)=1
and

\bm{e}_{i}(j)=0
for all
j\neq i
, and
\bm{0}
to denote the
vector such that
\bm{0}(i)=0
for all
i\in I
. Let

\lVert\bm{x}\rVert\coloneqq\max\{|\bm{x}(i)|:i\in I\}
and
\lVert X\rVert\coloneqq\sup\{\lVert\bm{x}\rVert:\bm{x}\in X\}
. We say that
X
is

convex
if
\lambda\bm{x}+(1-\lambda)\bm{y}\in X
for
all
\lambda\in[0,1]
and
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in X
, and

bounded
if
\lVert X\rVert\leq b
for some
b\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.

We write
2^{\Sigma}
for the powerset of
\Sigma
. Given a nonempty
finite sequence
w
, let
w^{\omega}\coloneqq ww\cdots
. Let
\Sigma^{\omega}\coloneqq\{w_{0}w_{1}\cdots:w_{i}\in\Sigma\}
be the set of infinite sequences with elements from

\Sigma
.

II-A

Constant-rate multi-mode systems

A
d
-dimensional constant-rate multi-mode system

(MMS)
, with
d\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
, is a finite set
\mathit{M}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
whose elements are called
modes
. A
schedule
is a
(finite or infinite) sequence
\pi=(\alpha_{1},\bm{m}_{1})(\alpha_{2},\bm{m}_{2})\cdots
, where each
(\alpha_{i},\bm{m}_{i})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\mathit{M}
. To ease the notation, we often write,
e.g.
,
\bm{m}\,\frac{1}{2}\bm{m}^{\prime}
rather than
(1,\bm{m})(1/2,\bm{m}^{\prime})
. Given
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, we define the schedule
\lambda\pi
as
\pi
with each

\alpha_{i}
replaced by
\lambda\alpha_{i}
. The
size
of
\pi
,
denoted
\left|\pi\right|
, is its number of pairs. The
effect
of

\pi
is
\bm{\Delta}_{\pi}\coloneqq\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\bm{m}_{i}
. The

support
of
\pi
is
\text{supp}(\pi)\coloneqq\{\bm{m}_{1},\bm{m}_{2},\ldots\}
. Let
\mathrm{time}_{\bm{m}}(\pi)\coloneqq\sum_{i:\bm{m}_{i}=\bm{m}}\alpha_{i}
and
\mathrm{time}({\pi})\coloneqq\sum_{\bm{m}\in\mathit{M}}\mathrm{time}_{\bm{m}}(\pi)
. We say that an infinite schedule
\pi
is

non-Zeno
if
\mathrm{time}({\pi})=\infty
. The
Parikh image

of a finite schedule
\pi
is denoted
\bm{\pi}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathit{M}}
,

i.e.

\bm{\pi}(\bm{m})\coloneqq\mathrm{time}_{\bm{m}}(\pi)
. We say that
two finite schedules are
equivalent
, denoted with
\equiv
, if
they are equal after merging consecutive equal modes,
i.e.
using the
rule
\pi(\alpha,\bm{m})(\beta,\bm{m})\pi^{\prime}\equiv\pi(\alpha+\beta,\bm{m})\pi^{\prime}
. Let
\pi[\tau..\tau^{\prime}]
be the
schedule obtained from
\pi
starting where time
\tau
has elapsed,
and ending where time
\tau^{\prime}
has elapsed;
e.g.
, for
\pi=(2,\bm{m}_{1})(0.5,\bm{m}_{2})(1,\bm{m}_{3})^{\omega}
, we have

\pi[0..1]=(1,\bm{m}_{1})
,
\pi[0.5..2.25]=(1.5,\bm{m}_{1})(0.25,\bm{m}_{2})
and
\pi[3..]=(0.5,\bm{m}_{3})(1,\bm{m}_{3})^{\omega}
.

An
execution
is a (finite or infinite) sequence
\sigma=\bm{x}_{0}I_{0}\bm{x}_{1}\allowbreak I_{1}\allowbreak\bm{x}_{2}\cdots

where
\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{1},\ldots\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
I_{0},I_{1},\ldots\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
are closed intervals with distinct endpoints,
\min I_{0}=0
, and
\min I_{j}=\max I_{j-1}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
. Let

\dom\sigma\coloneqq I_{0}\cup I_{1}\cup\cdots
. For every
\tau\in\dom\sigma
, with
\tau\in I_{j}
, let

\sigma(\tau)\coloneqq\bm{x}_{j}+\frac{\tau-\min I_{j}}{\max I_{j}-\min I_{j}}\cdot(\bm{x}_{j+1}-\bm{x}_{j}).

We define
\sigma[\tau..\tau^{\prime}]
, where
[\tau,\tau^{\prime}]\subseteq\dom\sigma
, as the execution
\sigma^{\prime}
that satisfies

\sigma^{\prime}(\alpha)=\sigma(\tau+\alpha)
for every
\alpha\in[0,\tau^{\prime}-\tau]
.

A schedule
\pi=(\alpha_{1},\bm{m}_{1})(\alpha_{2},\bm{m}_{2})\cdots
,
together with a point
\bm{x}_{0}
, gives rise to an execution

\mathrm{exec}(\pi,\bm{x}_{0})\coloneqq\bm{x}_{0}I_{0}\bm{x}_{1}\cdots
where

I_{0}\coloneqq[0,\alpha_{1}]
,
I_{j}\coloneqq[\max I_{j-1},\max I_{j-1}+\alpha_{j+1}]
and
\bm{x}_{j}\coloneqq\bm{x}_{j-1}+\alpha_{j}\bm{m}_{j}
. We use the notation
\bm{x}\xrightarrow{}^{\pi}\bm{y}
to
denote the fact that
\pi
is a schedule that, from
\bm{x}
, gives
rise to an execution ending in
\bm{y}
. If we only care about the
existence of such a schedule, we may write
\bm{x}\xrightarrow{}^{*}\bm{y}
, or write
\bm{x}\xrightarrow{}^{+}\bm{y}
to denote that there is
such a nonempty schedule. We sometimes omit either of the two
endpoints if its value is irrelevant,
e.g.

\bm{x}\xrightarrow{}^{\pi}{}

stands for
\bm{x}\xrightarrow{}^{\pi}\bm{x}+\bm{\Delta}_{\pi}
. Given a set

Z\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we write
\bm{x}\xrightarrow{}^{\pi}_{Z}
to denote that
the execution never leaves
Z
,
i.e.

\sigma(\tau)\in Z
for all

\tau\in\dom\sigma
, where

\sigma\coloneqq\mathrm{exec}(\pi,\bm{x})
. We extend this notation to any set of
sets
\mathcal{X}
, requiring that, for all
\tau\in\dom\sigma
,
there exists
Z\in\mathcal{X}
such that
\sigma(\tau)\in Z
.

II-B

A linear temporal logic for MMS

A
zone

Z\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a convex polytope represented as
the intersection of finitely many closed half-spaces,
i.e.

Z=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\mathbf{A}\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}\}
for some

\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k\times d}
and
\bm{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
.

Linear temporal logic (LTL)
, over a finite set of zones
\mathit{AP}
,
has the following syntax:

\varphi::={\mathit{true}}\mid Z\mid\neg\varphi\mid\varphi\land\varphi\mid\varphi\lor\varphi\mid{\textsf{F}}\varphi\mid{\textsf{G}}\varphi\mid\varphi\mathrel{\textsf{U}}\varphi,

where
Z\in\mathit{AP}
. Let LTL(
X
) denote the set of LTL
formulas (syntactically) using only the operators from
X
. For
example, LTL(
\{{\textsf{F}},{\textsf{G}},\land\}
) describes the LTL formulas using
only operators
F
,
G
and
\land
. We write
\text{LTL}_{\text{B}}

to indicate that all zones from
\mathit{AP}
must be bounded. We say that an
LTL formula is
negation-free
if it contains no occurrence of

\neg
.

We define the semantics over infinite executions:

\displaystyle\sigma,\tau

\displaystyle\models{\mathit{true}}

\displaystyle\iff{\mathit{true}},

\displaystyle\sigma,\tau

\displaystyle\models Z

\displaystyle\iff\sigma(\tau)\in Z,

\displaystyle\sigma,\tau

\displaystyle\models\neg\varphi

\displaystyle\iff\neg(\sigma,\tau\models\varphi),

\displaystyle\sigma,\tau

\displaystyle\models\varphi\land\varphi^{\prime}

\displaystyle\iff(\sigma,\tau\models\varphi)\land(\sigma,\tau\models\varphi^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\sigma,\tau

\displaystyle\models\varphi\lor\varphi^{\prime}

III
From LTL(
\{{\textsf{F}},{\textsf{G}},\land\}
) to linear LTL

This section deals with classical LTL formulas interpreted over
infinite words. We show that any formula
from LTL(

III-A

From LTL(
\{{\textsf{F}},{\textsf{G}},\land\}
) to flat formulas

We say that a formula is

of atomic propositions. By convention, an empty conjunction amounts to

{\mathit{true}}
. An LTL formula
\varphi

\psi^{\prime}
and

\displaystyle\mathrm{flat}_{{\textsf{G}}}(\varphi)

\displaystyle\coloneqq{\textsf{G}}\psi\land\bigwedge_{i\in I}\mathrm{flat}_{{\textsf{G}}}(\varphi_{i})\land\bigwedge_{j\in J}\mathrm{flat}_{{\textsf{G}}{\textsf{F}}}(\varphi_{j}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{flat}_{{\textsf{G}}{\textsf{F}}}(\varphi)

Let

Theorem 1

The model-checking problem for semi-bounded linear LTL formulas is

Before proving the above theorem, we use it to prove the previously announced
P-completeness results.

Theorem 2

The model-checking problem is in P for these fragments:

\text{LTL}_{\text{B}}
(
\{{\textsf{F}},{\textsf{G}},\neg\}
), LTL(
\{{\textsf{F}},\lor\}
) and LTL(
\{{\textsf{G}},\land\}
).

Proof:

Consider a formula from
\text{LTL}_{\text{B}}
(
\{{\textsf{F}},{\textsf{G}},\neg\}
). Note that

\neg{\textsf{F}}\varphi\equiv{\textsf{G}}\neg\varphi
and
\neg\neg\varphi\equiv\varphi
. Thus, negations can be pushed inwards. Afterwards,
we can simplify using
{\textsf{F}}{\textsf{G}}{\textsf{F}}\varphi\equiv{\textsf{G}}{\textsf{F}}\varphi
and

{\textsf{G}}{\textsf{F}}{\textsf{G}}\varphi\equiv{\textsf{F}}{\textsf{G}}\varphi
. If the resulting formula is
negation-free, then it is of the form
{\textsf{F}}Z
,
{\textsf{G}}Z
,
{\textsf{G}}{\textsf{F}}Z
or

{\textsf{F}}{\textsf{G}}Z
. These are all linear as
{\textsf{F}}Z\equiv\mathbb{R}^{d}\mathrel{\textsf{U}}Z
and
{\textsf{F}}{\textsf{G}}Z\equiv\mathbb{R}^{d}\mathrel{\textsf{U}}({\textsf{G}}Z)
. Thus, we are done by
Theorem

1
. If
the resulting formula has a negation, then there are four forms to consider:
(1)
{\textsf{F}}\neg Z
, (2)
{\textsf{G}}\neg Z
, (3)
{\textsf{G}}{\textsf{F}}\neg Z
and (4)
{\textsf{F}}{\textsf{G}}\neg Z
. These are easy to handle:

•

We have
\bm{x}\models_{\mathit{M}}{\textsf{F}}\neg Z
iff
\bm{x}\models_{\mathit{M}}{\textsf{G}}{\textsf{F}}\neg Z
iff
\bm{x}\models_{\mathit{M}}{\textsf{F}}{\textsf{G}}\neg Z
iff
\bm{x}\not\in Z
or there is a mode
\bm{m}\in\mathit{M}
such that
\bm{m}\neq\bm{0}
.

•

We have
\bm{x}\models_{\mathit{M}}{\textsf{G}}\neg Z

\bm{x}\not\in Z
and there exists a mode
\bm{m}\in\mathit{M}
such that for all

Since

{\textsf{F}}(\varphi\lor\psi)\equiv({\textsf{F}}\varphi)\lor({\textsf{F}}\psi)

\{{\textsf{F}},\lor\}

formulas from LTL(
\{{\textsf{F}}\}
). So, it suffices to check each

As
{\textsf{G}}{\textsf{G}}\varphi\equiv{\textsf{G}}\varphi
and

\{{\textsf{G}},\land\}

\psi,\psi^{\prime}

IV-A

A polynomial-time first-order logic

We recall a first-order logic over the reals introduced
in
[
12
]
. It allows for conjunctions of

convex semi-linear Horn formulas
,
i.e.
formulas of this form:

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{a}(i)\cdot\bm{x}(i)\sim c\lor\bigvee_{i\in I}\bigwedge_{j\in J_{i}}\bm{x}(j)>0,

where

can be solved in polynomial time
[
12
]
.

This result extends easily to solutions where
\bm{x}(j)\in\mathbb{R}
is
allowed, provided that
\bm{x}(j)
is never used in
disjuncts. Indeed, it suffices to introduce two variables

and replace each occurrence of

Given

.

Undecidable fragments

In this section, we show that
\text{LTL}_{\text{B}}
(
\{\mathrel{\textsf{U}}\}
) and
\text{LTL}_{\text{B}}
(
\{{\textsf{G}},\lor\}
)
are undecidable, by reducing from the reachability problem for Petri
nets with inhibitor arcs (
i.e.
zero-tests). A
Petri net with
inhibitor arcs
is a tuple

where

•

P
is a finite set of elements called
places
,

•

is a disjoint finite set of elements called

,

•

\bm{\sim}\colon T\to\{\geq,=\}^{P}
, and

•

\bm{\Delta}\colon T\to\mathbb{Z}^{P}

A transition
t
is

in
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{N}^{P}
if
\bm{x}\bm{\sim}_{t}\bm{0}
and
\bm{x}+\bm{\Delta}_{t}\geq\bm{0}
. If it is

leads to
\bm{x}^{\prime}\coloneqq\bm{x}+\bm{\Delta}_{t}
, denoted
\bm{x}\xrightarrow{}^{t}\bm{x}^{\prime}
. We write
\bm{x}\xrightarrow{}\bm{x}^{\prime}
if
\bm{x}\xrightarrow{}^{t}\bm{x}^{\prime}
for some

. We
define
{\xrightarrow{}^{+}}
as the transitive closure of
{\xrightarrow{}}
, and

{\xrightarrow{}^{*}}
as the reflexive closure of
{\xrightarrow{}^{+}}
. The

asks, given a Petri nets with inhibitor

\mathcal{N}
, and
\bm{x}_{\text{src}},\bm{x}_{\text{tgt}}
, whether
\bm{x}_{\text{src}}\xrightarrow{}^{+}\bm{x}_{\text{tgt}}
in

\mathcal{N}
. This problem is undecidable,
e.g.
see
[
22
]
.

VII
Conclusion

We have introduced a linear temporal logic for MMS and established the
complexity of model checking for each syntactic fragments: Each one is
either P-complete, NP-complete or undecidable. This generalizes and
unifies existing work on MMS and continuous vector addition
systems/Petri nets.

Future work includes fully dealing with unbounded zones; allowing for
time constraints on temporal operators; and algorithmically optimizing
objective functions on schedules satisfying a given LTL
specification. It would also be interesting to go from theory to
practice by providing a solver for linear LTL formulas, and more
generally
\text{LTL}_{\text{B}}
(
\{{\textsf{F}},{\textsf{G}},\land\}
).

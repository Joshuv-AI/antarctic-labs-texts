Another look at elliptic homogenization
Andrea Braides, Giuseppe Cosma Brusca, and Davide Donati

Abstract
. We consider the limit of sequences of normalized ( s , 2 ) (s,2) -Gagliardo seminorms with an oscillating coefficient as s → 1 s\to 1 . In a seminal paper by Bourgain, Brezis and Mironescu (subsequently extended by Ponce) it is proven that if the coefficient is constant then this sequence Γ \Gamma -converges to a multiple of the Dirichlet integral. Here we prove that, if we denote by ε \varepsilon the scale of the oscillations and we assume that 1 − s << ε 2 1-s<\!<\varepsilon^{2} , this sequence converges to the homogenized functional formally obtained by separating the effects of s s and ε \varepsilon ; that is, by the homogenization as ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 of the Dirichlet integral with oscillating coefficient obtained by formally letting s → 1 s\to 1 first. MSC codes: 49J45, 35B27, 35R11. Keywords: Γ \Gamma -convergence, non-local functionals, fractional Sobolev spaces, homogenization.



1.
Introduction

In their seminal paper
[
2
]
Bourgain, Brezis and Mironescu have studied the asymptotic behaviour of Gagliardo seminorms
[u]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}
as
s\to 1
, and in particular, in the case
p=2
, of the seminorm
[u]_{W^{s,2}(\Omega)}
given by

[u]_{W^{s,2}(\Omega)}:=\Bigl(\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dxdy\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
\Omega
a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
s\in(0,1)
, and the fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,2}(\Omega)
is defined as

W^{s,2}(\Omega):=\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega):[u]_{W^{s,2}(\Omega)}<+\infty\}.

Their results, subsequently extended by Ponce
[
10
]
, imply that the functionals

F_{s}(u)=(1-s)\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dxdy

\Gamma
-converge, with respect to the
L^{2}
-convergence, to the (multiple of the) Dirichlet integral

\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2d}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx,

where
\sigma_{d-1}
is the
{\mathcal{H}}^{d-1}
-dimensional measure of
S^{d-1}
,
with domain the usual Sobolev space
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
. Since the functionals are equi-coercive in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, by the properties of convergence of minima of
\Gamma
-convergence, minimum problems involving the Dirichlet integral can be approximated by problems involving Gagliardo seminorms, upon possibly suitably defining boundary-value problems if necessary.

This fractional-Sobolev space approximation can be extended to other problems involving elliptic integrals, such as homogenization problems, the most classical of which is the asymptotic analysis of (isotropic) oscillating energies

(1)

\int_{\Omega}a\Bigl(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx,

where
a
is a
1
-periodic function with
0<\alpha\leq a(y)\leq\beta<+\infty
. The
\Gamma
-limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
of such energies is the
homogenized
functional

F_{\rm hom}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\langle A_{\rm hom}\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle\,dx,

where
A_{\rm hom}
is a symmetric
d\times d
matrix characterized by the
homogenization formula

(2)

\langle A_{\rm hom}z,z\rangle=\min\Bigl\{\int_{(0,1)^{d}}a(y)|z+\nabla\varphi(y)|^{2}dy:\varphi\ 1\hbox{-periodic}\Bigr\}

(see e.g.
[
4
]
).
Formally, by the result of Bourgain, Brezis and Mironescu, we can define an approximation of the homogenized functional using the energies

(3)

F_{\varepsilon,s}(u)=(1-s)\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{d+2s}}dxdy,

defined for
u\in W^{s,2}(\Omega)
and
\varepsilon>0
. Indeed, upon supposing for simplicity that
a
be continuous, we easily see that for fixed
\varepsilon>0
the
\Gamma
-limit of
F_{\varepsilon,s}
as
s\to 1^{-}
is indeed

\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2d}\int_{\Omega}a\Bigl(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx,

which then
\Gamma
-converge to
\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2d}F_{\rm hom}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Since all functionals are equi-coercive in
L^{2}
, we can use a diagonal argument (see
[
6
]
) and deduce that there exist
s=s_{\varepsilon}
such that
s\to 1^{-}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
and the
\Gamma
-limit of
F_{\varepsilon,s}
is still
\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2d}F_{\rm hom}
.

In this paper we investigate the scales
s=s_{\varepsilon}
at which this limit holds.
If we let both
\varepsilon,
and
1-s
simultaneously tend to
0
, a heuristic argument in order to argue what can be a critical scale is as follows.
Let
z
be fixed and let
\varphi
be as in the formula characterizing
\langle A_{\rm hom},z,z\rangle
.
We can use
u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\langle z,x\rangle+\varepsilon\varphi(x/\varepsilon)
as test functions for the
\Gamma
-limit of
F_{\varepsilon,s}
.
We may suppose that
\varphi
be twice continuous differentiable, so that, using the Taylor development of
\varphi
at
x/\varepsilon
, we have

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt(1-s)\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}a\Bigl(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-s)\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}a\Bigl(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)\frac{|\langle z+\nabla\varphi(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}),x-y\rangle|^{2}+O(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}})}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2d}\int_{\Omega}a\Bigl(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)\Big|z+\nabla\varphi\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\Big|^{2}dx+O\Big(\frac{1-s}{\varepsilon^{2}}\Big).

This argument suggests that when

(4)

1-s<\!<\varepsilon^{2}\qquad\qquad\hbox{({subcritical case})},

we may construct recovery sequences optimizing the oscillations of
a
, and actually is a proof of the upper bound in this case for the target function
u(x)=\langle z,x\rangle
. We will prove that under assumption (
4
) a
separation of the scales

\varepsilon
and
s
occurs, and the limit is the one computed above by letting
s\to 1^{-}
first and then
\varepsilon\to 0
. The main argument in the proof of the lower bound is obtained by a discretization procedure (which as a byproduct also gives a different proof of the results in
[
10
]
) based on the use of Kuhn’s decomposition
[
8
]
and an integration argument on the set of orthogonal bases in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
borrowed from a recent paper by Solci
[
11
]
.
Thanks to (
4
), we can then reduce the computation to studying the limit of oscillating energies (
1
) on piecewise-affine interpolations at a scale much smaller than
\varepsilon
, for which the known homogenization result can be applied. This reduction is made possible by a lemma (Lemma
3
), which allows to consider only points
x,y
sufficiently close to each other in the computation of (
3
).

The cases other than subcritical are not dealt with here. They will require different, more complex, techniques and will be treated in future work. In order to give a hint of the necessity of different types of arguments in those cases, we can consider a similar type of non-local functionals treated in a general setting in

[
1
]
, of the form

(5)

F_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u)=\frac{1}{\delta^{d}}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\varrho\Big(\frac{|x-y|}{\delta}\Bigr)a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{2}}dxdy,

where
\varrho
is a suitably integrable positive kernel
and
\delta>0
plays a similar role as
s
above, in that it forces concentration as
\delta\to 0
.
Such functionals are also studied in
[
2
]
if the function
a
is a constant, and still approximate the Dirichlet integrand as
\delta\to 0
. The critical case for such functionals is
\delta\sim\varepsilon
, and if
\delta/\varepsilon\to\kappa\in(0,+\infty)
the
\Gamma
-limit can be computed using a non-local-to-local homogenization procedure giving a local homogenized limit energy of the form

F^{\kappa}_{\rm hom}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\langle A^{\kappa}_{\rm hom}\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle\,dx,

where
A^{\kappa}_{\rm hom}
is now characterized by a
non-local homogenization formula

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\langle A^{\kappa}_{\rm hom}z,z\rangle

\displaystyle=\min\Bigl\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,1)^{d}}\frac{1}{\kappa^{d}}\varrho\Bigl(\frac{|x-y|}{\kappa}\Bigr)a(y)\frac{|\langle z,y-x\rangle+\varphi(y)-\varphi(x)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{2}}dxdy:\varphi\ 1\hbox{-periodic}\Bigr\}.

In order to prove this result, localization techniques for limits of non-local functionals must be used (see
[
1
]
and also
[
5
]
). Comparing functionals
F_{\varepsilon,s}
with those in (
5
), a main additional difficulty is due to the different nature of the dependence on
\delta
and
s
, respectively, so that the case
1-s\sim\varepsilon^{2}
cannot be directly set as a homogenization-concentration problem, contrary to the case
\delta\sim\varepsilon
.

2.
Statement of the result and preliminaries

Let
a
be a
1
-periodic continuous function such that

(6)

0<\alpha\leq a(x)\leq\beta<+\infty

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For
\varepsilon,s\in(0,1)
we introduce the functional

(7)

F_{\varepsilon,s}(u)=(1-s)\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{d+2s}}dxdy

for
u\in W^{s,2}(\Omega)
.

We assume that the
\varepsilon
is a positive parameter and that
s
is a function of
\varepsilon
valued in
(0,1)
approaching
1
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Since
s=s(\varepsilon)
, as a shorthand,
F_{\varepsilon,s}
will be denoted by
F_{\varepsilon}
.
We prove that if
1-s\ll\varepsilon^{2}
, then the separation of scales described in the Introduction holds, as if we were passing to the limit first letting
s\to 1
and then letting
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary,
\varepsilon\in(0,1),\,s=s_{\varepsilon}
, and let
F_{\varepsilon}
be the functional defined in (
7
). If

(8)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1-s}{\varepsilon^{2}}=0,

then

(9)

\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2d}\int_{\Omega}\langle A_{\rm hom}\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle\,dx=:F_{\rm hom}(u)

for every
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
, where the
\Gamma
-limit is computed with respect to the
L^{2}(\Omega)
convergence,
A_{\rm hom}
is given by (
2
), and
\sigma_{d-1}:={\mathcal{H}}^{d-1}(S^{d-1})
is the
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

We note that the hypothesis that
\Omega
has a Lipschtiz boundary is exploited only in proving, by means of a density argument, the
\Gamma
-
\limsup
inequality.

Remark 2
.

Theorem 1 can be generalized to functionals modelled on
(s,p)
-Gagliardo seminorms with
p>1
, of the form

(10)

F_{\varepsilon,s}(u)=(1-s)\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{p}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{d+ps}}dxdy

for
u\in W^{s,p}(\Omega)
. The subcritical regime is then
1-s<\!<\varepsilon^{p}
, and the resulting
\Gamma
-limit is

C_{d,p}\int_{\Omega}f_{\rm hom}(\nabla u)\,dx,

defined on
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, with
C_{d,p}
the constant appearing in the corresponding result in
[
2
]
, and
f_{\rm hom}
defined by

(11)

f_{\rm hom}(z)=\min\Bigl\{\int_{(0,1)^{d}}a(y)|z+\nabla\varphi(y)|^{p}dy:\varphi\ 1\hbox{-periodic}\Bigr\}.

This result can be obtained with few changes and some heavier notation from the case
p=2
.

We preliminarily state a lemma that allows us to take into account only those interactions due to pairs of sufficiently close points in
\Omega
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subseteq L^{2}(\Omega)
be bounded and let
(r_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
be a sequence of positive real numbers such that

(12)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1-s_{\varepsilon}}{r_{\varepsilon}^{2}}=0.

Then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\biggl(F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})-(1-s_{\varepsilon})\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega\cap\{(x,y):|x-y|\leq r_{\varepsilon}\}}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}\,dxdy\biggr)=0.

Proof.

By the convexity of
x\mapsto x^{2}
and by (
6
), we have

(13)

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt(1-s_{\varepsilon})\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega\cap\{(x,y):|x-y|>r_{\varepsilon}\}}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}\,dxdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-s_{\varepsilon})\,4\beta\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega\cap\{(x,y):|x-y|>r_{\varepsilon}\}}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}\,dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-s_{\varepsilon})\,4\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(0)}\frac{1}{|\xi|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}\,d\xi\int_{\Omega}|u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,dx,

where we also took advantage of the symmetry of the integrand to get the inequality.

Since
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, (
13
) is estimated from above (up to a constant factor) by

\displaystyle(1-s_{\varepsilon})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(0)}\frac{1}{|\xi|^{d+2s_{\varepsilon}}}\,d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-s_{\varepsilon})\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(S^{d-1})\int_{r_{\varepsilon}}^{\infty}\rho^{-1-2s_{\varepsilon}}d\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(S^{d-1})(1-s_{\varepsilon})\frac{r_{\varepsilon}^{-2s_{\varepsilon}}}{2s_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(S^{d-1})(1-s_{\varepsilon})\frac{r_{\varepsilon}^{-2}}{2s_{\varepsilon}},

which tends to
0
by (
12
), completing our proof.
∎

Remark 4
.

We will apply the previous Lemma setting

r_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon\qquad\hbox{ or }\qquad r_{\varepsilon}=\sqrt{\varepsilon\sqrt{1-s_{\varepsilon}}}

in accordance with our convenience.

Note that with the second choice it holds

\sqrt{1-s_{\varepsilon}}<\!<r_{\varepsilon}<\!<\varepsilon,

so that, in particular,

(14)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{r_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}=0.

It is useful to observe that, in both circumstances, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}|\log r_{\varepsilon}^{1-s_{\varepsilon}}|=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}(1-s_{\varepsilon})|\log r_{\varepsilon}|=0

which implies

(15)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}r_{\varepsilon}^{1-s_{\varepsilon}}=1.

In the proof of the liminf inequality we take advantage of a discretization argument based on the construction of proper lattices, which can be parameterized on orthogonal bases.

Remark 5

(Notation for the set of orthogonal bases)
.

Following the notation of
[
11
]
we define the set of orthonormal bases of
\mathbb{R}^{d}

V:=\{\overline{\nu}=(\nu_{1},...,\nu_{d}):\nu_{j}\in S^{d-1}\text{ such that }\langle\nu_{i},\nu_{j}\rangle=0\text{ for }i\neq j\}

and observe that
V
has Hausdorff dimension equal to
k_{d}:=d(d-1)/2
.
For every
n\in\{1,...,d\}
and fixed
\nu\in S^{d-1}
we define

V_{n}^{\nu}:=\{\overline{\nu}\in V\hbox{ such that }\nu_{n}=\nu\},

whose Hausdorff dimension is
k_{d}-(d-1)
. Note that we have

(16)

\mathcal{H}^{k_{d}-(d-1)}(V_{n}^{\nu})=\frac{\mathcal{H}^{k_{d}}(V)}{\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(S^{d-1})},

and that in general, the formula

(17)

\int_{V}f(\overline{\nu})\,d\mathcal{H}^{k_{d}}(\overline{\nu})=\int_{S^{d-1}}\int_{V_{n}^{\nu}}f^{\nu}(\overline{\nu}_{n})\,d\mathcal{H}^{k_{d}-(d-1)}(\overline{\nu}_{n})\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\nu),

holds with
\overline{\nu}_{n}:=(\nu_{1},...,\nu_{n-1},\nu_{n+1},...,\nu_{d})
and
f^{\nu}(\overline{\nu}_{n}):=f(\overline{\nu})
for every
n\in\{1,...,d\}
and
f
non negative measurable function.

Given
\rho>0
and
\overline{\nu}\in V
, we define
\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}:=\{z_{1}\rho\nu_{1}+z_{2}\rho\nu_{2}+...+z_{d}\rho\nu_{d}:(z_{1},...,z_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}
and
Q_{\rho\overline{\nu}}
as the cube described by the orthogonal basis
\{\rho\nu_{1},...,\rho\nu_{d}\}
.

Remark 6

(Kuhn’s decomposition)
.

A cube
Q_{\rho\overline{\nu}}
defined as in the previous remark can be further decomposed into
d!

d
-simplices of size
\rho^{d}/d!
through
Kuhn’s decomposition
in the following way: for every
\tau
permutation of
d
indices, we define
\Delta_{\rho\overline{\nu}}^{\tau}
as the simplex described by the vertices
\rho\nu_{\tau(1)},\rho\nu_{\tau(1)}+\rho\nu_{\tau(2)},...,\rho\nu_{\tau(1)}+\rho\nu_{\tau(2)}+...+\rho\nu_{\tau(d)}
. As shown in
[
8
, Lemma 1]
, as
\tau
vary among all the permutations, we get a family of
d!
simplices which constitutes the desired partition.

We denote the vertices of each simplex by

\Delta_{\rho\overline{\nu}}^{\tau,0}=0,\quad\Delta_{\rho\overline{\nu}}^{\tau,j}:=\rho\nu_{\tau(1)}+\rho\nu_{\tau(2)}+...+\rho\nu_{\tau(j)}\quad\hbox{ for }j=1,...,d

and observe that, with this choice, it holds

(18)

\Delta_{\rho\overline{\nu}}^{\tau,j}-\Delta_{\rho\overline{\nu}}^{\tau,j-1}=\rho\nu_{\tau(j)}\qquad\hbox{ for }j=1,...,d.

3.
Proof of the result

3.1.
Liminf inequality

Throughout this section we write
s:=s_{\varepsilon}
,
r:=r_{\varepsilon}
and
\sigma_{d-1}:=\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(S^{d-1})
in order to simplify the notation.

Let
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and consider a sequence
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
converging to
u
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\sup_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})<\infty
which implies that
u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{s_{,}2}(\Omega)
for every
\varepsilon>0
.

In light of Lemma
3
, we aim at proving that
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\prime})
for every
\Omega^{\prime}
open subset well contained in
\Omega
and that

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}(1-s)\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega\cap\{(x,y):|x-y|\leq r\}}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dxdy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2d}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\langle A_{\rm hom}\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle\,dx,

where
r=\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-s)^{\frac{1}{4}}
. By the independence of the left-hand side from
\Omega^{\prime}
in particular this implies that
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
.

Applying Lemma
3
, the change of variables
\eta:=y-x
and
\xi:=\eta/r
, and then the coarea formula, we get

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912ptF_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})+o_{\varepsilon}(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-s)\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega\cap\{(x,y):|x-y|\leq r\}}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-s)\int_{B_{r}(0)}\frac{1}{|\eta|^{d-2(1-s)}}\int_{\{x\in\Omega\,:\,x+\eta\in\Omega\}}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x+\eta)-u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}}{|\eta|^{2}}\,dxd\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-s)\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{r^{2(1-s)}}{|\xi|^{d-2(1-s)}}\int_{\{x\in\Omega\,:\,x+r\xi\in\Omega\}}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x+r\xi)-u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}}{|r\xi|^{2}}\,dxd\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-s)\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{d-1}\int_{S^{d-1}}\frac{r^{2(1-s)}}{\rho^{d-2(1-s)}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\int_{\{x\in\Omega\,:\,x+r\rho\nu\in\Omega\}}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x+r\rho\nu)-u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}}{|r\rho|^{2}}dxd\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\nu)d\rho.

For
\overline{\nu}\in V
(recall the notation of Remark
5
) we set

\mathcal{I}^{r}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}:=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}^{d}:rk+rQ_{\rho\overline{\nu}}\subset\subset\Omega\},

and note that for every
\nu\in S^{d-1}
it holds

\bigcup_{k\in\mathcal{I}^{r}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}rk+rQ_{\rho\overline{\nu}}\subseteq\{x\in\Omega:x+r\rho\nu\in\Omega\}

for every
\overline{\nu}\in V
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912ptF_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})+o_{\varepsilon}(1)

(19)

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912pt\geq(1-s)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r^{2(1-s)}}{\rho^{-1+2s}}\int_{S^{d-1}}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}^{r}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}\int_{rk+rQ_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}{\hskip-8.5359pt}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x+r\rho\nu)-u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}}{|r\rho|^{2}}dxd\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\nu)d\rho.

We shall exploit the uniform continuity of the function
a
to factor it out from the inner integral; for this reason, we introduce a modulus of continuity
\omega:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
; that is, an increasing continuous function such that
\omega(0)=0
and
|a(x_{1})-a(x_{2})|\leq\omega(|x_{1}-x_{2}|)
for every
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

We rewrite the right-hand side of (
3.1
) as

\displaystyle\hskip-22.76228pt(1-s)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r^{2(1-s)}}{\rho^{-1+2s}}\int_{S^{d-1}}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}^{r}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}a\left(\frac{rk}{\varepsilon}\right)\int_{rk+rQ_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x+r\rho\nu)-u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}}{|r\rho|^{2}}dxd\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\nu)d\rho

\displaystyle\hskip-31.2982pt+(1-s)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r^{2(1-s)}}{\rho^{-1+2s}}

\displaystyle\int_{S^{d-1}}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}^{r}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}\int_{rk+rQ_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}{\hskip-2.84544pt}\Bigl(a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)-a\Bigl(\frac{rk}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)\Bigr)\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x+r\rho\nu)-u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}}{|r\rho|^{2}}dxd\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\nu)d\rho,

and we note that the last term is negligible as
\varepsilon\to 0
as its absolute value is estimated from above by

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\omega\Bigl(\frac{r\sqrt{d}}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)(1-s)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r^{2(1-s)}}{\rho^{-1+2s}}\int_{S^{d-1}}\int_{\{x+r\rho\nu\in\Omega\}}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x+r\rho\nu)-u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}}{|r\rho|^{2}}dxd\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\nu)d\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega\Bigl(\frac{r\sqrt{d}}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)(1-s)\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{r^{2(1-s)}}{|\xi|^{d-2(1-s)}}\int_{\{x+r\xi\in\Omega\}}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x+r\xi)-u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}}{|r\xi|^{2}}\,dxd\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\omega\Bigl(\frac{r\sqrt{d}}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)\frac{1}{\alpha}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}),

which tends to
0
since by the assumption
\sup_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})<+\infty
and since
r/\varepsilon\to 0
by (
14
).

By this fact and by formulas (
16
) and (
17
), inequality (
3.1

\displaystyle\hskip-34.14322pt\hskip-17.07182pt\sqrt{d}\int_{[0,1]\times V}|r\rho|^{\frac{d}{2}+1}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}^{2r}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}\biggl(\int_{rk+rQ_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}|\nabla u^{\rho\overline{\nu}}_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}d\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho,\overline{\nu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{d}\int_{[0,1]\times V}|r\rho|^{\frac{d}{2}+1}\#\mathcal{I}^{2r}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}^{2r}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{I}^{2r}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}\biggl(\int_{rk+rQ_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}|\nabla u^{\rho\overline{\nu}}_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}d\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho,\overline{\nu})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{d}\int_{[0,1]\times V}|r\rho|^{\frac{d}{2}+1}(\#\mathcal{I}^{2r}_{\rho\overline{\nu}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\biggl(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}^{2r}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}\int_{rk+rQ_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}|\nabla u^{\rho\overline{\nu}}_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}d\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho,\overline{\nu})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle r\sqrt{d\,2^{-d}|\Omega|}\int_{[0,1]\times V}\biggl(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}^{2r}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}\int_{rk+rQ_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}|\nabla u^{\rho\overline{\nu}}_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}d\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho,\overline{\nu}),

which by Jensen’s inequality is less than or equal to

(30)

r\sqrt{d\,2^{-d}|\Omega|}\Bigl(\int_{[0,1]\times V}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}^{2r}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}\int_{rk+rQ_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{\rho\overline{\nu}}(x)|^{2}dx\,d\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho,\overline{\nu})\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Now we write

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\int_{[0,1]\times V}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}^{2r}_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}\int_{rk+rQ_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{\rho\overline{\nu}}(x)|^{2}dx\,d\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho,\overline{\nu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2(1-s)}{\mathcal{H}^{k_{d}}(V)}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\rho^{-1+2s}}\int_{V}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}^{2r}_{{\rho\overline{\nu}}}}\int_{rk+rQ_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{\rho\overline{\nu}}|^{2}\,dxd\mathcal{H}^{k_{d}}(\overline{\nu})d\rho

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\hskip-8.5359pt\Bigl(\frac{2d}{\alpha\sigma_{d-1}}\Bigr)\frac{1-s}{d}\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{\mathcal{H}^{k_{d}}(V)}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r^{2(1-s)}}{\rho^{-1+2s}}\int_{V}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}^{2r}_{{\rho\overline{\nu}}}}a\left(\frac{rk}{\varepsilon}\right)\int_{rk+rQ_{\rho\overline{\nu}}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{\rho\overline{\nu}}|^{2}\,dxd\mathcal{H}^{k_{d}}(\overline{\nu})d\rho

and we note that this last term equals (
24
) (up to a constant factor), therefore, as a byproduct of the previous computations, it is bounded above by

\frac{2d}{\alpha\sigma_{d-1}}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})+o_{\varepsilon}(1),

which is uniformly bounded for small
\varepsilon
. We conclude that (
30
) vanishes since
r\to 0
.

Finally, we remove the boundedness condition by a truncation argument. Let
u^{M}:=(u\land M)\lor-M
, and note that
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
implies
u_{\varepsilon}^{M}\to u^{M}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Since
F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}^{M})
, we have

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\ \geq\ \liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u^{M}_{\varepsilon})\ \geq\ \frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2d}\int_{\Omega}\langle A_{\rm hom}\nabla u^{M},\nabla u^{M}\rangle\,dx,

and then

\sup_{M}\|\nabla u^{M}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ \frac{2d}{\alpha\sigma_{d-1}}\sup_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\ <\ \ +\infty,

so that
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
with
\nabla u^{M}\to\nabla u
. The proof is concluded once we let
M\to+\infty
.

3.2.
Limsup inequality

To prove the
\Gamma
-
\limsup
inequality, we first study the case where
u
is piecewise-affine. We then recover the inequality on the whole space
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
by means of a density argument. For the sake of clarity, we first deal with the case where
u
is affine.

Step
1
. We first suppose that
u(x)=\langle z,x\rangle
, for some
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We recall that by the classical homogenization formula for
A_{\rm hom}
(see
[
4
, Theorem 14.7]
) and by density, it holds

(31)

\langle A_{\rm hom}z,z\rangle=\inf\Bigl\{\int_{(0,1)^{d}}a(y)\lvert\nabla{\varphi}(y)+z\rvert^{2}dy\,:\,\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,\,1\text{-periodic}\Bigr\}.

Fix
\delta>0
and let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a
1
-periodic function such that

(32)

\int_{(0,1)^{d}}a(y)\lvert\nabla\varphi(y)+z\rvert^{2}dy<\langle A_{\rm hom}z,z\rangle+\delta;

we claim that
u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\langle z,x\rangle+\varepsilon\varphi\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)
is an approximate recovery sequence for
u
, i.e., it is such that

(33)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2d}\int_{\Omega}\langle A_{\rm hom}z,z\rangle dx+\delta.

This, combined with a diagonal argument and the arbitrariness of
\delta>0
, will lead to the limsup inequality.

In order to prove (
33
), we take advantage of Lemma
3
with
r_{\varepsilon}=1
, and consider the first-order Taylor expansion of
\varphi
to get

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptF_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})+o_{\varepsilon}(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-s)\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{\lvert\langle z,x-y\rangle+\varepsilon\varphi\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)-\varepsilon\varphi\left(\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\right)\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{d+2s}}dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-s)\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega\cap\{\lvert x-y\rvert\leq 1\}}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{|\langle z+\nabla{\varphi}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right),x-y\rangle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}R(|x-y|)\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{d+2s}}dxdy,

where the function
R(|x-y|)
is the remainder in Lagrange form of
\varphi
and it is such that

(34)

R(|x-y|)\leq d^{2}||\nabla^{2}\varphi||_{\rm\infty}|x-y|^{2}.

By the inequality
|a+b|^{2}\leq(1+\eta)a^{2}+(1+1/\eta)b^{2}
, with
\eta>0
, and by the change of variables
\xi:=y-x
, we deduce

(35)

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})+o_{\varepsilon}(1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\eta)(1-s)\int_{\Omega}\int_{B_{1}(0)}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{|\langle z+\nabla\varphi\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right),\xi\rangle|^{2}}{|\xi|^{d+2s}}d\xi dx

(36)

\displaystyle+\left(1+\frac{1}{\eta}\right)\frac{1-s}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{B_{1}(0)}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{R(|\xi|)^{2}}{|\xi|^{d+2s}}d\xi dx.

For a fixed
\eta>0
, the term in (
36
) vanishes as
\varepsilon
tends to zero. Indeed, by assumption, the term
\frac{1-s}{\varepsilon^{2}}
vanishes as
\varepsilon\to 0
, and using (
6
) we have that

(37)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{B_{1}(0)}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{R(|\xi|)^{2}}{|\xi|^{d+2s}}d\xi dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d^{4}\beta|\Omega|||\nabla^{2}\varphi||_{\rm\infty}\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{1}{|\xi|^{d+2s-4}}d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d^{4}\sigma_{d-1}\beta|\Omega|||\nabla^{2}\varphi||_{\rm\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{3-2s}d\rho<+\infty.

As for the term in (
35
), note that by the symmetry of the integrand, it holds

(38)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{|\langle z+\nabla{\varphi}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right),\xi\rangle|^{2}}{\lvert\xi\rvert^{d+2s}}d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\partial B_{\rho}(0)}\frac{\left|\langle z+\nabla\varphi\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right),\nu\rangle\right|^{2}}{\rho^{d+2s}}d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\nu)d\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\rho^{d+2(s-1)}}\frac{1}{d}\int_{\partial B_{\rho}(0)}\left|z+\nabla\varphi\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\nu)d\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{d}\left|z+\nabla\varphi\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{1-2s}d\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2d}\left|z+\nabla\varphi\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right|^{2}\frac{1}{(1-s)}.

Recalling that
\varphi
and
a
are
1
-periodic, we may substitute (
38
) in (
35
) to get

(39)

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt(1+\eta)(1-s)\int_{\Omega}\int_{B_{1}(0)}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{|\langle z+\nabla\varphi\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right),\xi\rangle|^{2}}{|\xi|^{d+2s}}d\xi dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+\eta)\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2d}\int_{\Omega}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\left\lvert\nabla{\varphi}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)+z\right\rvert^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\eta)\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2d}\frac{|\Omega|}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{(0,\varepsilon)^{d}}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\left|\nabla\varphi\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)+z\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+\eta)\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2d}\lvert\Omega\rvert\int_{(0,1)^{d}}a(y)\left\lvert\nabla{\varphi}\left(y\right)+z\right\rvert^{2}dy,

which, in view of (
32
), implies

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq(1+\eta)\left(\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2d}\int_{\Omega}\langle A_{\rm hom}z,z\rangle dx+\delta\right),

and we conclude by letting
\eta
tend to
0
.

Step
2
. We now suppose that the function
u
be piecewise-affine in
\Omega
. More precisely, we suppose that there exists a finite family of
d
-simplices
(\Delta)_{i\in I}
covering
\Omega
, vectors
(z_{i})_{i\in I}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and constants
(m_{i})_{i\in I}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
such that
u(x)=\langle x,z_{i}\rangle+m_{i}
for every
x\in\Delta_{i}\cap\Omega
and for every
i\in I
. To simplify the notation, from now on we will always write
\Delta_{i}
in place of
\Delta_{i}\cap\Omega
.

Similarly to what we have done in the previous case, for a fixed
\delta>0
and for every
i\in I
, we consider
\varphi_{i}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

a
1
-periodic function satisfying

(40)

\int_{(0,1)^{d}}a(y)\lvert\nabla\varphi_{i}(y)+z_{i}\rvert^{2}dy<\langle A_{\rm hom}z_{i},z_{i}\rangle+\frac{\delta}{\#I}.

Having in mind the construction of the recovery sequence performed in the previous case, we aim at constructing an approximate recovery sequence for
u
by perturbing, in a suitable way,
u
with the functions
\varphi_{i}
. To this end,
for every
\Delta_{i}
, we consider the subset defined by

\Delta_{i}^{\varepsilon}:=\left\{x\in\Omega:\,\,\text{dist}(x,\Delta_{i}^{c})>\varepsilon\right\}.

For fixed
\varepsilon>0
and
i\in I
, we consider a positive function
\psi^{i}_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, satisfying the following conditions

\displaystyle\ \psi^{i}_{\varepsilon}(x)=0\qquad\qquad\text{for }x\notin\Delta_{i}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\ \psi^{i}_{\varepsilon}(x)=1\qquad\qquad\text{for }x\in\Delta_{i}^{2\varepsilon}

(41a)

\displaystyle\ 0\leq\psi_{\varepsilon}^{i}(x)\leq 1\qquad\,\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

(41b)

\displaystyle\ \|\nabla\psi^{i}_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{2}{\varepsilon}

(41c)

\displaystyle\ \|\nabla^{2}\,\psi^{\textit{i}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{4}{\varepsilon^{2}}

\displaystyle\,,

where
\nabla^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{i}
is the Hessian matrix of
\psi_{\varepsilon}^{i}
.

We claim that an approximate recovery sequence for
u
is given by

u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\sum_{i\in I}\left(\langle z_{i},x\rangle+\varepsilon\varphi_{i}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\psi_{\varepsilon}^{i}(x)+m_{i}\right)\chi_{\Delta_{i}}(x),

where
\chi_{\Delta_{i}}
denotes the characteristic function of
\Delta_{i}
. Note that
u_{\varepsilon}
is a continuous function, since it coincides with
u
on the boundary of each
\Delta_{i}
.

Let
A:={\Omega\times\Omega\cap\{\lvert x-y\rvert\leq\varepsilon\}}
. Thanks to Lemma
3
and Remark
4
, it is enough to show that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}(1-s)\iint_{A}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{\lvert u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{d+2s}}dxdy\leq\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2d}\int_{\Omega}\langle A_{\rm hom}\nabla{u},\nabla{u}\rangle dx+\delta.

To make the computations more manageable, we consider a suitable partition of
A
. For
i\in I
, we set

B_{i}=\Delta_{i}^{2\varepsilon}\times\Delta_{i}^{2\varepsilon}\cap\{\lvert x-y\rvert\leq\varepsilon\},

C_{i}=\Delta_{i}\times\Delta_{i}\cap\{\lvert x-y\rvert\leq\varepsilon\text{ and }x\text{ or }y\in\Delta_{i}\setminus{\Delta_{i}^{2\varepsilon}}\},

and for
j\in I,\,j\neq i
, we set

D_{ij}=\Delta_{i}\times\Delta_{j}\cap\{\lvert x-y\rvert\leq\varepsilon\},

so that
A=\bigcup_{i\in I}(B_{i}\cup C_{i})\cup\bigcup_{i\neq j}D_{ij}
.

Taking advantage of this decomposition and of the subadditivity of the
\limsup
, we have

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\,(1-s)\iint_{A}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{\lvert u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{d+2s}}dxdy

(42)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sum_{i\in I}(1-s)\iint_{B_{i}}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{\lvert u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{d+2s}}dxdy

(43)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i\in I}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\,(1-s)\iint_{C_{i}}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{\lvert u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{d+2s}}dxdy

(44)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in I,\,j\neq i}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\,(1-s)\iint_{D_{ij}}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{\lvert u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{d+2s}}dxdy.

We begin by estimating (
42
). Since for fixed
i\in I
we have
\psi_{\varepsilon}^{i}=1
on
B_{i}
, by the first-order Taylor expansion of
\varphi_{i}
, for a fixed
\eta>0
we get

(45)

\displaystyle\hskip-71.13188pt\sum_{i\in I}(1-s)\iint_{B_{i}}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{|\langle z_{i}+\nabla{\varphi}_{i}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right),x-y\rangle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}R_{i}(|x-y|)\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{d+2s}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}(1+\eta)(1-s)\int_{\Delta_{i}^{2\varepsilon}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{|\langle z_{i}+\nabla\varphi_{i}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right),\xi\rangle|^{2}}{|\xi|^{d+2s}}d\xi dx

(46)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\left(1+\frac{1}{\eta}\right)\frac{1-s}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\Delta_{i}^{2\varepsilon}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{R_{i}(|\xi|)^{2}}{|\xi|^{d+2s}}d\xi dx,

where
R_{i}
is the remainder in Lagrange form of the first-order Taylor expansion of
\varphi_{i}
, which satisfies

(47)

R_{i}(|x-y|)\leq d^{2}\max_{i\in I}||\nabla^{2}\,\varphi_{i}||_{\infty}|x-y|^{2}=:M|x-y|^{2}.

Thus, we may estimate (
46
) with

\beta M^{2}\sum_{i\in I}\left(1+\frac{1}{\eta}\right)\frac{1-s}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\Delta_{i}}\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{|\xi|^{4}}{|\xi|^{d+2s}}d\xi dx,

which vanishes for
\varepsilon\to 0
in view of the fact that
\frac{1-s}{\varepsilon^{2}}
goes to zero for
\varepsilon\to 0
and of (
37
) (with
\Omega
replaced by
\Delta_{i}
).

As for (
45
), considering larger domains
and taking advantage of the computations leading to (
39
), we see that

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}(1+\eta)(1-s)\int_{\Delta_{i}^{2\varepsilon}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{|\langle z_{i}+\nabla\varphi_{i}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right),\xi\rangle|^{2}}{|\xi|^{d+2s}}d\xi dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}(1+\eta)(1-s)\int_{\Delta_{i}}\int_{B_{1}(0)}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{|\langle z_{i}+\nabla\varphi_{i}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right),\xi\rangle|^{2}}{|\xi|^{d+2s}}d\xi dx;

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\eta)\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2d}\sum_{i\in I}\lvert\Delta_{i}\rvert\int_{(0,1)^{d}}a(y)\left\lvert\nabla{\varphi_{i}}\left(y\right)+z_{i}\right\rvert^{2}dy.

Recalling (
40
), we finally conclude

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(1+\eta)(1-s)\iint_{B_{i}}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{\lvert u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{d+2s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\,(1+\eta)\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2d}\sum_{i\in I}\Bigl(\int_{\Delta_{i}}\langle A_{\rm hom}z_{i},z_{i}\rangle+\frac{\delta}{\#I}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\eta)\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2d}\hskip-5.69046pt\int_{\Omega}\langle A_{\rm hom}\nabla{u},\nabla{u}\rangle dx+\delta,

so that letting
\eta\to 0
, (
42
) is estimated.

We now show that (
43
) vanishes for
\varepsilon\to 0
. As
\#I
is finite and independent on
\varepsilon
, it suffices to take into account a single term in the sum.
By the convexity of
x\mapsto x^{2}
, we have

(48)

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt(1-s)\iint_{C_{i}}a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{\lvert u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{d+2s}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 3\beta(1-s)\iint_{C_{i}}\frac{\lvert z_{i}\rvert^{2}\lvert x-y\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{d+2s}}dxdy

(49)

\displaystyle+3\beta(1-s)\iint_{C_{i}}\frac{\left\lvert\varepsilon\psi^{i}_{\varepsilon}(y)\left(\varphi_{i}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)-\varphi_{i}\left(\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)\right\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{d+2s}}dxdy

(50)

\displaystyle+3\beta(1-s)\iint_{C_{i}}\frac{\left\lvert\varepsilon\varphi_{i}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)(\psi^{i}_{\varepsilon}(x)-\psi^{i}_{\varepsilon}(y))\right\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{d+2s}}dxdy.

We start with (
48
). Via a change of variables, we see that

\displaystyle(1-s)\iint_{C_{i}}\frac{\lvert z_{i}\rvert^{2}\lvert x-y\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{d+2s}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-s)\lvert z_{i}\rvert^{2}\int_{\Delta_{i}\setminus{\Delta_{i}^{2\varepsilon}}}\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}}{\lvert\xi\rvert^{d+2s}}d\xi dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-s)\rvert\lvert z_{i}\rvert^{2}\lvert\Delta_{i}\setminus{\Delta_{i}^{2\varepsilon}}\rvert\sigma_{d-1}\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}}{\lvert\xi\rvert^{d+2s}}d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2}\lvert z_{i}\rvert^{2}\lvert\Delta_{i}\setminus{\Delta_{i}^{2\varepsilon}}\rvert,

which converges to zero as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

As for (
49
), using the first-order Taylor expansions of
\varphi_{i}
, (
41a
), (
41b
), and recalling (
47
), one gets

\displaystyle\hskip-19.91684pt(1-s)\iint_{C_{i}}\frac{\left\lvert\varepsilon\psi^{i}_{\varepsilon}(y)\left(\varphi_{i}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)-\varphi_{i}\left(\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)\right\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{d+2s}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-s)\int_{\Delta_{i}\setminus{\Delta_{i}^{2\varepsilon}}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\frac{\left\lvert\langle\nabla{\varphi_{i}}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right),\xi\rangle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}R_{i}(|\xi|)\right\rvert^{2}}{\lvert\xi\rvert^{d+2s}}d\xi dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2(1-s)\Bigl(\int_{\Delta_{i}\setminus{\Delta_{i}^{2\varepsilon}}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\frac{\left\lvert\langle\nabla{\varphi_{i}}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right),\xi\rangle\right\rvert^{2}}{\lvert\xi\rvert^{d+2s}}d\xi dx+\frac{M^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\Delta_{i}\setminus{\Delta_{i}^{2\varepsilon}}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert^{4}}{\lvert\xi\rvert^{d+2s}}d\xi dx\Bigr).

The first term can be estimated with

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt(1-s)\int_{\Delta_{i}\setminus{\Delta_{i}^{2\varepsilon}}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\frac{\lvert\langle\nabla{\varphi_{i}}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right),\xi\rangle\rvert^{2}}{\lvert\xi\rvert^{d+2s}}d\xi dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-s)\lvert\lvert\nabla{\varphi_{i}}\rvert\rvert_{\infty}^{2}\int_{\Delta_{i}\setminus{\Delta_{i}^{2\varepsilon}}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}}{\lvert\xi\rvert^{d+2s}}d\xi dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-s)\lvert\lvert\nabla{\varphi_{i}}\rvert\rvert_{\infty}^{2}\lvert\Delta_{i}\setminus\Delta_{i}^{2\varepsilon}\rvert\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{|\xi|^{d+2s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{2}\lvert\lvert\nabla{\varphi_{i}}\rvert\rvert_{\infty}^{2}|\Delta_{i}\setminus\Delta_{i}^{2\varepsilon}|\,,

while for the second term we have

(51)

\displaystyle\frac{1-s}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\Delta_{i}\setminus{\Delta_{i}^{2\varepsilon}}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert^{4}}{\lvert\xi\rvert^{d+2s}}d\xi dx\leq\frac{(1-s)}{\varepsilon^{2}}|\Delta_{i}\setminus\Delta_{i}^{2\varepsilon}|\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{4-2s},

and therefore both terms vanish as
\varepsilon
tends to
0
.

In order to estimate (
50
), we denote by
\tilde{R}_{i}
the Lagrange remainders of the first-order Taylor expansion of
\psi_{\varepsilon}^{i}
and we note that by (

) it follows that

(52)

\tilde{R}_{i}(|x-y|)\leq\frac{4d^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}|x-y|^{2}.

Using the first-order Taylor expansion of
\psi^{i}_{\varepsilon}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\hskip-71.13188pt(1-s)\iint_{C_{i}}\frac{\left\lvert\varepsilon\varphi_{i}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)(\psi^{i}_{\varepsilon}(x)-\psi^{i}_{\varepsilon}(y))\right\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{d+2s}}dxdy

Step
3
.
The functional
F_{\rm\hom}
defined in (
9
), which has been proven to be the desired
\Gamma
-limit on piecewise-affine functions, is continuous in the strong topology of
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
. Since
\Omega
is an open bounded set with Lipschitz boundary,
every function
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
may be extended to
\overline{u}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{R})
via the standard extension operator and a cut-off function.
It is a known fact that a dense class in
W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{R})
is the class of piecewise-affine functions, namely, functions which are piecewise-affine on a finite family of simplices covering
\overline{B}_{R}
(see
[
7
, Proposition 2.1]
). Therefore, the class of piecewise-affine functions discussed in Step
2
is dense in
W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{R})
and a fortiori in
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
. Having proved that the
\Gamma
-
\limsup
inequality holds on a dense subset of
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
, we conclude that it holds for any
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
(see for instance
[
3
, Remark 2.8]
).

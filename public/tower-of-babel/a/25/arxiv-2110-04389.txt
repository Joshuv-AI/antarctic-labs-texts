Tight Computationally Efficient Approximation of Matrix Norms with Applications
Anatoli Juditsky, Georgios Kotsalis, Arkadi Nemirovski

Abstract
We address the problems of computing operator norms of matrices induced by given norms on the argument and the image space. It is known that aside of a fistful of “solvable cases,” most notably, the case when both given norms are Euclidean, computing operator norm of a matrix is NP-hard. We specify rather general families of norms on the argument and the images space (“ellitopic” and “co-ellitopic,” respectively) allowing for reasonably tight computationally efficient upper-bounding of the associated operator norms. We extend these results to bounding “robust operator norm of uncertain matrix with box uncertainty,” that is, the maximum of operator norms of matrices representable as a linear combination, with coefficients of magnitude ≤ 1 \leq 1 , of a collection of given matrices. Finally, we consider some applications of norm bounding, in particular, (1) computationally efficient synthesis of affine non-anticipative finite-horizon control of discrete time linear dynamical systems under bounds on the peak-to-peak gains, (2) signal recovery with uncertainties in sensing matrix, and (3) identification of parameters of time invariant discrete time linear dynamical systems via noisy observations of states and inputs on a given time horizon, in the case of “uncertain-but-bounded” noise varying in a box.

&=\min\limits_{\lambda,\upsilon}\left\{\phi_{\cal T}(\lambda)+\upsilon:\lambda\geq 0,\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\upsilon&{1\over 2}AP\cr\hline\cr{1\over 2}P^{T}A^{T}&\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}T_{k}\cr\end{array}\right]\succeq 0\right\}=\hbox{\rm Opt}(A).\end{array}

To put this immediate observation into a proper perspective, see Section
3.2
.

The just outlined results are stronger than what in the case in question is stated by Theorem
2
. This being said, it can be proved that in the full scope of the latter theorem, logarithmic growth of the tightness factor with
K,L
is unavoidable.

3.1
On the scope of Theorem
2

The scope of Theorem
2
—the set of the matrix norms to which the theorem applies—is restricted to the case when the norm in the argument space is
simple ellitopic norm
, meaning that its unit ball is an ellitope, and the norm on the image space
is a
simple co-ellitopic
norm, meaning that the polar of its unit ball is an ellitope. Clearly, simple co-ellitopic norms (s.co-e.n’s) are exactly the conjugates of simple ellitopic norms (s.e.n.’s). These classes of norms allow for certain “calculus” stating that some standard operations with norms preserve their ellitopic/co-ellitopic type.

Basic calculus of simple ellitopic norms
is as follows.

E.1.

[raw materials] When
p\in[2,\infty]
,
\|\cdot\|_{p}
is a s.e.n. on
{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
,

E.2.

[taking finite maxima] When
\|\cdot\|_{(k)}
,
k\leq K
, are s.e.n.’s on
{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
, so is their maximum.

E.3.

[restriction to a linear subspace] When
\|\cdot\|
is a s.e.n. on
{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
and
y\to Ax:{\mathbf{R}}^{n^{\prime}}\to{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
is a linear embedding,
\|y\|^{\prime}:=\|Ay\|
is a s.e.n. on
{\mathbf{R}}^{n^{\prime}}

E.4.

[passing to factor-norm] When
\|\cdot\|
is a s.e.n. on
{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
and
x\mapsto Ax:{\mathbf{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbf{R}}^{n^{\prime}}
is an onto mapping, the factor-norm
\|y\|^{\prime}=\min_{x}\{\|x\|:Ax=y\}
is s.e.n. on
{\mathbf{R}}^{n^{\prime}}

E.5.

[“aggregation”] Let
\|\cdot\|_{(k)}
be s.e.n. on
{\mathbf{R}}^{n_{k}}
,
k\leq K
, and let
{\cal A}
be a monotone convex compact set with a nonempty interior in

. Then the norm on
{\mathbf{R}}^{n_{1}}\times...\times{\mathbf{R}}^{n_{K}}
with the unit ball

For instance, when

All these rules are immediate consequences of “calculus of ellitopes”
[
19

.

Basic calculus of simple co-ellitopic norms
is as follows.

cE.1.

[raw materials] When

,
\|\cdot\|_{r}
is a s.co-e.n. on
{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
(cf. E.1)

cE.2.

[taking sums] When

{\mathbf{R}}^{n}

Indeed, the unit ball

of the unit balls is

that is, the polar
B^{*}
of

cE.3.

[restriction to a linear subspace] When
\|\cdot\|
is a s.co-e.n. on
{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
and
y\to Ay:{\mathbf{R}}^{n^{\prime}}\to{\mathbf{R}}^{n}

\|y\|^{\prime}:=\|Ay\|

{\mathbf{R}}^{n^{\prime}}

Indeed, assuming that the polar

of

.

cE.4.

[passing to factor-norm] When
\|\cdot\|
is a s.co-e.n. on
{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
and
x\mapsto Ax:{\mathbf{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbf{R}}^{n^{\prime}}
is an onto mapping, the factor-norm
\|y\|^{\prime}=\min_{x}\{\|x\|:Ax=y\}
is s.co-e.n. on
{\mathbf{R}}^{n^{\prime}}
.

Indeed, assuming the polar
B^{*}
of the unit ball of
\|\cdot\|

\|y\|^{\prime}={\min}_{\delta\in{\hbox{\rm Ker}\,}A}\|A^{\dagger}y+\delta\|={\min}_{\delta\in{\hbox{\rm Ker}\,}A}{\max}_{z\in B^{*}}z^{T}[A^{\dagger}y+\delta]={\max}_{z\in B^{*}\cap{{\mathrm{Im}}A^{T}}}[[A^{\dagger}]^{T}z]^{T}y.

Thus, the polar of the unit ball of
\|\cdot\|^{\prime}
is a linear image of the intersection of ellitope

cE.5.

[“aggregation”] Let
\|\cdot\|_{(k)}
be s.co-e.n. on
{\mathbf{R}}^{n_{k}}
,
k\leq K
, and let
{\cal A}
be a monotone convex compact set with a nonempty interior in
{\mathbf{R}}^{K}_{+}
. Then the norm on
{\mathbf{R}}^{n_{1}}\times...\times{\mathbf{R}}^{n_{K}}

\|[x_{1};...;x_{K}]\|=\max_{\beta\in{\cal A}}{\sum}_{k}\sqrt{\beta_{k}}\|x_{k}\|_{(k)}

is s.co-e.n. For instance, when
r_{k}\in[1,2]
and
r\in[1,2]
, the norm on
{\mathbf{R}}^{n_{1}}\times...\times{\mathbf{R}}^{n_{K}}
given by

\|[x_{1};...;x_{K}]\|=\|[\|x_{1}\|_{r_{1}};...;\|x_{K}\|_{r_{K}}]\|_{r}

is s.co-e.n.

Indeed, let
\|\cdot\|_{(k)}^{*}
be the s.e.n.’s conjugate to
\|\cdot\|_{(k)}

{\cal A}^{1/2}=\{[\alpha_{1};...;\alpha_{K}]\geq 0:[\alpha_{1}^{2};\alpha_{2}^{2};...;\alpha_{K}^{2}]\in{\cal A}\},
we get a convex compact monotone subset of
{\mathbf{R}}^{K}_{+}

of
\|\cdot\|

{\cal B}=\{[x_{1};...;x_{K}]:\phi_{{\cal A}^{1/2}}([\|x_{1}\|_{(1)};...;\|x_{K}\|_{(K)}])\leq 1\}.

Hence, as is immediately seen, the polar
{\cal B}_{*}
of

\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\cal B}_{*}&=&\{[y_{1};...;y_{K}]:\sum_{k}\zeta_{k}\|y_{k}\|_{(k)}^{*}\leq 1\,\forall(\zeta\geq 0:\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}\zeta_{k}\leq 1\,\forall\alpha\in{\cal A}^{1/2})\}\\

3.2
An extension

The above results can be straightforwardly extended from the case when
{\cal B}_{*}
and
{\cal X}
are ellitopes onto a more general case. Specifically, assume that

A
.

{\cal X}\subset{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
is a set with nonempty interior represented as the convex hull of a finite union of ellitopes, or, which is the same,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\cal X}&=&\hbox{\rm Conv}\{{\bigcup}_{i=1}^{I}P_{i}{\cal X}_{i}\}=\left\{x=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\lambda_{i}P_{i}x_{i}:x_{i}\in{\cal X}_{i},\lambda_{i}\geq 0,\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}=1\right\}\\
&=&\left\{x=\sum\limits_{i}P_{i}x_{i}:\sum\limits_{i}\|x_{i}\|_{{\cal X}_{i}}\leq 1\right\},\end{array}

(11)

where
{\cal X}_{i}\subset{\mathbf{R}}^{n_{i}}
are basic ellitopes and
\|\cdot\|_{{\cal X}_{i}}
are s.e.n. on
{\mathbf{R}}^{n_{i}}
with unit balls
{\cal X}_{i}
.

Under Assumption
A
,
{\cal X}
is a convex compact symmetric w.r.t. the origin subset of
{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
with
0\in\hbox{\rm int}{\cal X}
; as such,
{\cal X}
is the unit ball of a norm
\|\cdot\|_{\cal X}

ellitopic norms.
Clearly, every simple ellitopic norm is ellitopic, e.g., the block
\ell_{\infty}

\|[x_{1};...;x_{I}]\|=\max\limits_{i\leq I}\|x_{I}\|_{p_{i}}

[formula]

In fact, the family of ellitopic norms is much wider that the family of s.e.n.’s. For example,

E.
1.

When

are ellitopic norms on

,
i\leq I

with basic ellitopes
{\cal X}_{i\nu}

P_{i}
from
{\mathbf{R}}^{n_{i}}
to
{\mathbf{R}}^{n_{1}}\times...\times{\mathbf{R}}^{n_{I}}
as the natural embeddings

[P_{i}x_{i}]_{s}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n_{s}},&s\neq i\\
x_{i},&s=i\\
\end{array}\right\},

the unit ball

of norm (

2

E.
3

Let
\|\cdot\|_{(\chi)}
be an ellitopic norm on
{\mathbf{R}}^{n_{\chi}}
,
\chi=1,2
. Then the norm
\|[x_{1};x_{2}]\|=\max[\|x_{1}\|_{(1)},\|x_{2}\|_{(2)}]

Indeed, if the unit ball of

Assume, next, that

B.

{\cal B}\subset{\mathbf{R}}^{m}

{\cal B}=\{v\in{\mathbf{R}}^{m}:\max\limits_{y\in{\cal B}_{*}}v^{T}y\leq 1\},\;{\cal B}_{*}=\Big\{y=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{J}\mu_{j}Q_{j}z_{j},z_{j}\in{\cal Z}_{j},\mu_{j}\geq 0,{\sum}_{j}\mu_{j}=1\Big\}

\begin{array}[]{rl}\|u\|_{{\cal B}}&=\max\limits_{y\in{\cal B}_{*}}u^{T}y=\max\limits_{\{z_{j},\mu_{j}\}}\left\{{\sum}_{j}\mu_{j}u^{T}Q_{j}:z_{j}\in{\cal Z}_{j},\mu_{j}\geq 0\,\forall j,{\sum}_{j}\mu_{j}=1\right\}\\

cE.
1.

Maximum of finitely many co-ellitopic norms is co-ellitopic.

Indeed, if

\|z\|_{*}=\min_{\{y_{k}\}}\left\{{\sum}_{k}\|y_{k}\|_{(k)}^{*}:{\sum}_{k}y_{k}=z\right\}

{\cal B}_{*}=\{z={\sum}_{k}y_{k}:{\sum}_{k}\|y_{k}\|_{(k)}^{*}\leq 1\},

and the polar of this set
is

\displaystyle{\cal B}

\displaystyle=\left\{x:\max_{y}\left\{[\sum\limits_{k}y_{k}]^{T}x:\sum\limits_{k}\|y_{k}\|_{(k)}^{*}\leq 1\right\}\leq 1\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{x:\max_{\lambda,y}\left\{[\sum\limits_{k}y_{k}]^{T}x:\|y_{k}\|_{(k)}^{*}\leq\lambda_{k},\sum\limits_{k}\lambda_{k}\leq 1\right\}\leq 1\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{x:\max_{\lambda}\left\{\sum\limits_{k}\left[\max_{y_{k}}\{x^{T}y_{k}:\|y_{k}\|_{(k)}^{*}\leq\lambda_{k}\}\right],\sum\limits_{k}\lambda_{k}\leq 1\right\}\leq 1\right\}

2.

Simple observation

Let
\|\cdot\|_{\cal X}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\cal B}
be norms with
{\cal X}
given by (

given by (

of

induced by the norms

and

on the argument and image spaces can be computed as follows:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\|A\|_{{\cal B},{\cal X}}&=&\max_{x}\{\|Ax\|_{\cal B}:\|x\|_{\cal X}\leq 1\}=\max\limits_{x}\left\{\max\limits_{j}\|Q_{j}^{T}Ax\|_{{\cal Z}^{*}_{j}}:\|x\|_{\cal X}\leq 1\right\}\hbox{\ [see (\ref{wehaveB})]}\\
&=&\max\limits_{x}\left\{\max\limits_{j}\max\limits_{z_{j}}\{z_{j}^{T}Q_{j}^{T}Ax:z_{j}\in{\cal Z}_{j}\}:\|x\|_{\cal X}\leq 1\right\}\\
&=&\max\limits_{j}\left\{\max\limits_{x,z_{j}}z_{j}^{T}Q_{j}^{T}Ax:z_{j}\in{\cal Z}_{j},x\in{\cal X}\right\}\\
&=&\max\limits_{j}\left\{\max\limits_{x,z_{j}}z_{j}^{T}Q_{j}^{T}Ax:z_{j}\in{\cal Z}_{j},x\in\hbox{\rm Conv}\{\cup_{i}P_{i}{\cal X}_{i}\}\right\}\\
&=&\max\limits_{j}\left\{\max\limits_{i}\left[\max\limits_{z_{j}\in{\cal Z}_{j},x_{i}\in{\cal X}_{i}}z_{j}^{T}Q_{j}^{T}AP_{i}x_{i}\right]\right\}=\max\limits_{i,j}\|Q_{j}^{T}AP_{i}\|_{ij}\end{array}

(16)

where

\|Q_{j}^{T}AP_{i}\|_{ij}=\max\limits_{z_{j}\in{\cal Z}_{j},x_{i}\in{\cal X}_{i}}z_{j}^{T}[Q_{j}^{T}AP_{i}]x_{i}=\|Q_{j}^{T}AP_{i}\|_{{\cal Z}_{j}^{*},{\cal X}_{i}}.

(17)

Note that by the same token
\max_{i}\|Q_{j}^{T}AP_{i}\|_{ij}=\|Q_{j}^{T}A\|_{{\cal Z}_{j}^{*},{\cal X}}

3.3
Applications

3.3.1
Least norm projector synthesis

Consider the
projection problem
as follows: we are given a linear subspace
{\cal F}
of linear space
{\cal E}={\mathbf{R}}^{n}
and a norm
\theta(\cdot)
on
{\cal E}
; our goal is to find a linear projector
H
of
{\cal E}
onto
{\cal F}
—a linear map
x\mapsto Hx:{\cal E}\to{\cal F}
with
Hx=x
for all
x\in{\cal F}
—which deviates the least from the identity mapping
{\mathrm{Id}}
in the norm

\|\cdot\|_{\theta\to\theta}:\|A\|_{\theta\to\theta}={\max}_{x\in{\cal E}}\{\theta(Ax):\theta(x)\leq 1\}.

Consider the case when the norm in question is the block

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\lambda^{ij},\upsilon^{ij},G^{ijs},H^{ijs}\atop{\overline{G}^{ij},\overline{H}^{ij}\atop 1\leq i\leq I,1\leq j\leq J,1\leq s\leq S}}\bigg\{\phi_{{\cal T}^{i}}(\lambda^{ij})+{\color[rgb]{0.5,0,0.5}~\phi}_{{\cal R}^{j}}(\upsilon^{ij}):\;\;\lambda^{ij}\geq 0,\,\upsilon^{ij}\geq 0

\displaystyle\|{\cal U}\|_{{\cal B},{\cal X}}

\displaystyle\max\limits_{i\leq I,j\leq J}\|{\cal U}_{ij}\|_{{\cal Z}_{j}^{*},{\cal X}_{i}}\leq\hbox{\rm Opt}[{\cal U}]=\max\limits_{i\leq I,j\leq J}\hbox{\rm Opt}_{ij}[{\cal U}]

(41)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left[\max\limits_{i\leq I,j\leq J}[\varsigma(K_{i},L_{j})+{\varkappa}(K_{i}){\varkappa}(L_{j})\vartheta(2\kappa)]\right]\|{\cal U}\|_{{\cal B},{\cal X}}

4.3
Application to robust signal recovery

Consider the standard Signal Processing problem as follows.
Given noisy observations

\omega=Ax+\xi,\quad\xi\sim{\cal N}(0,I_{m})

(42)

, we want to recover

.
Here

and

are

We consider

\widehat{x}=\widehat{x}_{H}(\omega):=H^{T}\omega
,

and quantify the performance of a candidate estimate

by its

where
\|\cdot\|_{\cal B}
is a given norm on
{\mathbf{R}}^{\nu}
. There is an extensive literature dealing with the design and performance analysis of linear estimates. In particular, it is known
[
19
, Proposition 4.
16
]
that when

is an ellitope of ellitopic size

{\cal B}_{*}
of the unit ball

of
\|\cdot\|
is an ellitope of ellitopic size

here

over all estimates

The result we have just cited, as well as most of known to us results on performance of linear estimates, deals with the case when the sensing matrix

is known in advance. Here we want to address the case when

is subject to “uncertain-but-bounded” perturbations, specifically, is selected (by nature or by an adversary) from the uncertainty set

This problem can be seen as a “non-interval” extension of the problem of solving systems of equations affected by interval uncertainty which has received significant attention in the literature, cf., e.g.,
[
13

16
,
21
,
29
,
34

35
,
36
]

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Risk}}^{+}_{\|\cdot\|_{\cal B}}[\widehat{x}_{H}|{\cal X}]

\displaystyle:=\sup\limits_{x\in{\cal X},\epsilon:\atop\|\epsilon\|_{\infty}\leq 1}{\mathbf{E}}_{\xi\sim{\cal N}(0,I_{m})}\left\{\Big\|[B-H^{T}A_{\mathrm{nom}}]x-\Big[\sum\limits_{s}\epsilon_{s}H^{T}A_{s}\Big]x-H^{T}\xi\Big\|_{\cal B}\right\}

where

and

.
The simplest way to build a “presumably good” linear estimate is to minimize over

of

and an efficiently computable upper bound on

[

11
]

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rl}~~~\hbox{\rm Opt}&=\min\limits_{H,\lambda,\upsilon,\mu,\atop\Theta,G^{s},H^{s},G,H}\bigg\{\phi_{\cal T}(\lambda)+\phi_{\cal R}(\upsilon)+\phi_{\cal R}(\mu)+{\mathop{\hbox{\rm Tr}}}(\Theta):\\
&\hbox{\small$\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\lambda\geq 0,\,\upsilon\geq 0,\mu\geq 0,\,G+\sum_{s}G^{s}\preceq\sum_{\ell}\upsilon_{\ell}R_{\ell},\,H+\sum_{s}H^{s}\preceq\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}T_{k}\\
\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\Theta&{1\over 2}HQ\cr\hline\cr{1\over 2}Q^{T}H^{T}&\sum_{\ell}\mu_{\ell}R_{\ell}\cr\end{array}\right]\succeq 0,\;\;\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}G^{s}&{1\over 2}Q^{T}H^{T}A_{s}P\cr\hline\cr{1\over 2}P^{T}A_{s}^{T}HQ&H^{s}\cr\end{array}\right]\succeq 0,\,s\leq S\\
\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}G&{1\over 2}Q^{T}[B-H^{T}A_{\mathrm{nom}}]P\cr\hline\cr{1\over 2}P^{T}[B^{T}-A_{\mathrm{nom}}^{T}H]Q&H\cr\end{array}\right]\succeq 0\end{array}\right\}$}\end{array}

4.3.1
The problem

Consider situation as follows: a linear time-invariant dynamical system with states

Assigning the entries of

Appendix B
Spectratopic case

B.1
Spectratopes

A
basic spectratope
is a
bounded
set
{\cal W}
represented as

{\cal W}=\{w\in{\mathbf{R}}^{p}:\exists t\in{\cal T}:T_{k}^{2}[w]\preceq t_{k}I_{d_{k}},1\leq k\leq K\}

(91)

where

T_{k}[w]=\sum_{i=1}^{p}w_{i}T_{ki}

is a linear mapping from
{\mathbf{R}}^{p}
to
{\mathbf{S}}^{d_{k}}
(so that
T_{ki}
are symmetric
d_{k}\times d_{k}
matrices), and
{\cal T}
is as in the definition of a basic ellitope.

A spectratope
is a set
{\cal X}
represented as the linear image of a basic spectratope
{\cal W}
:

\begin{array}[]{c}{\cal X}=P{\cal W}=\{x\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}:\exists w\in{\cal W}:x=Pw\},\\
{\cal W}=\Big\{w\in{\mathbf{R}}^{p}:\exists t\in{\cal T}:\Big[{\sum}_{i}w_{i}T_{ki}\Big]^{2}\preceq t_{k}I_{d_{k}},k\leq K\Big\}\end{array}

We refer to
D=\sum_{k=1}^{K}d_{k}
as
spectratopic size
of
{\cal W}
and
{\cal X}
.

Same as ellitopes, spectratopes are convex compact sets symmetric w.r.t. the origin; a basic spectratope, in addition, has a nonempty interior.

B.1.1
Examples

First of all,
every ellitope is a spectratope.
Indeed, it suffices to consider the case when the ellitope
{\cal W}
in question is the basic ellitope (
2
). In this case, passing to eigenvalue decompositions of matrices
T_{k}
, we have

T_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu_{k}}e_{ki}e_{ki}^{T},\;\nu_{k}={\mathop{\hbox{\rm Rank}}}(T_{k}),

whence

\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\cal W}&=&\{w:\exists t\in{\cal T}:w^{T}T_{k}w\leq t_{k},k\leq K\}\\
&=&\{w:\exists\bar{t}=\{t_{ki},i\leq\nu_{k},k\leq K\}\in\overline{{\cal T}}:[e_{ki}^{T}w]^{2}\preceq t_{ki}I_{1},\,1\leq i\leq\nu_{k},k\leq K\},\\
\overline{{\cal T}}&:=&\Big\{t_{ki}\geq 0:\,\Big[\sum_{i}t_{1i};...;\sum_{i}t_{Ki}\Big]\in{\cal T}\Big\}.\end{array}

An example of a “genuine” basic spectratope is the unit

-ball,

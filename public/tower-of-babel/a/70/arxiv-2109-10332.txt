On the purity conjecture of Nisnevich for torsors under reductive group schemes
Roman Fedorov

Abstract
. Let R R be a regular semilocal integral domain containing an infinite field k k . Let f ∈ R f\in R be an element such that for all maximal ideals 𝔪 \mathfrak{m} of R R we have f ∉ 𝔪 2 f\notin\mathfrak{m}^{2} . Let 𝐆 {\mathbf{G}} be a reductive group scheme over R R . Under an isotropy assumption on 𝐆 {\mathbf{G}} we show that a 𝐆 {\mathbf{G}} -torsor over the localization R f R_{f} is trivial, provided that it is rationally trivial. We show that this is not true without the isotropy assumption. Finally, if 𝐆 {\mathbf{G}} is a commutative group scheme of multiplicative type and the regular semilocal ring contains a field of characteristic zero, we prove an analogue of Nisnevich purity conjecture for higher étale cohomology groups. The first statement is derived from its abstract version concerning presheaves of pointed sets satisfying some properties. The counterexample is constructed by providing a torsor over a local family of affine lines that cannot be extended to a torsor over the corresponding local family of projective lines. 1.
Introduction and main results

Let
R
be a regular local ring, let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a reductive group scheme over
R
, and let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor over
R
. The famous Grothendieck–Serre conjecture predicts that
{\mathcal{E}}
is trivial, if it is trivial over the fraction field
K
of
R
. This has been proved in the case, when
R
contains a field
[
FP
,
Pan2
]
. In fact, a generalization of this conjecture to
semilocal
regular integral domains containing fields has been proved. The conjecture is still wide open in the mixed characteristic case, though recently there has been some significant progress
[
Fed3
,
Čes1
,
GP
,
GL
]
.

The conjecture
[
Nis
, Conj. 1.3]
, that was formulated by Nisnevich in 1989, is a generalization of the Grothendieck–Serre conjecture:
let
R
and
{\mathbf{G}}
be as before, let
f\in R
be such that
f\notin\mathfrak{m}^{2}
, where
\mathfrak{m}
is the maximal ideal of
R
, and let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor over
R_{f}
, then
{\mathcal{E}}
is trivial, if it is trivial over
K
.
This conjecture reduces to the Grothendieck–Serre conjecture if
f=1
. Very little was known about this conjecture until now. If
R
is a discrete valuation ring, the conjecture reduces to the Grothendieck–Serre conjecture if
f
is invertible and is trivial otherwise. Nisnevich in
[
Nis
]
proved the conjecture in dimension two, assuming that the residue field of the local ring
R
is infinite and that
{\mathbf{G}}
is quasi-split (the condition that the residue field is infinite is removed by Česnavičius, see
[
Čes3
, Sect. 3.4.2(3)]
). Note that the Nisnevich conjecture is non-trivial even for
{\mathbf{G}}=\GL_{n}
, as opposed to the Grothendieck–Serre conjecture (in the
\GL_{n}
case the conjecture goes back to Quillen, see
[
Qui
]
). In this case the conjecture was proved by
Bhatwadekar and Rao in
[
BR
]
, assuming that
R
is a localization of a finitely generated algebra over a field. This was extended to arbitrary regular local rings containing fields by Popescu
[
Pop2
, Thm. 1]
. Guo proved a version of this conjecture for the rings of formal power series over valuation rings
[
Guo
, Cor. 7.5]
.

In this article, if the regular local ring contains an infinite field, we prove the conjecture under some isotropy condition on
{\mathbf{G}}
. In fact, under these assumptions, we prove the generalization of the conjecture to semilocal regular integral domains. Then we show that the conjecture does not hold without the isotropy assumption.

Finally, if
{\mathbf{G}}
is a commutative group scheme of multiplicative type and the regular semilocal ring contains a field of characteristic zero, we prove an analogue of Nisnevich purity conjecture for higher étale cohomology groups.

1.1.
Notations and conventions

If
C
is a category, we denote by
\Ob(C)
its class of objects.

If
A
is a pointed set, we denote its distinguished element by
\star
.

If
X
is an integral scheme, we denote by
K(X)
its function field. If
x
is a schematic point of
X
, we denote by
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}
its local ring and by
\mathfrak{m}_{x}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}
the corresponding maximal ideal. More generally, if
{\mathbf{x}}\subset X
is a set of schematic points contained in an open affine subset of
X
, we denote by
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,{\mathbf{x}}}
the corresponding semilocal ring.

We often work over a fixed base field
k
, in that case the product is the product over
k
:
\times:=\times_{k}
.

Let
U
be a scheme and
{\mathbf{G}}
be a flat
U
-group scheme finitely presented over
U
. A
U
-scheme
{\mathcal{E}}
with a left action
\act\colon{\mathbf{G}}\times{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{E}}
is called
a
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor over
U
if
{\mathcal{E}}
is faithfully flat and quasi-compact over
U
and the action is simply transitive, that is, the morphism
(\act,p_{2})\colon{\mathbf{G}}\times_{U}{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{E}}\times_{U}{\mathcal{E}}
is an isomorphism (see
[
Gro
, Sect. 6]
). A
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor
{\mathcal{E}}
over
U
is
trivial
if
{\mathcal{E}}
is isomorphic to
{\mathbf{G}}
as a
U
-scheme with an action of
{\mathbf{G}}
. This is well-known to be equivalent to the projection
{\mathcal{E}}\to U
having a section. If
T
is a
U
-scheme, we will use the term “
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor over
T
” to mean a “
{\mathbf{G}}_{T}
-torsor over
T
”.

Assume that
{\mathbf{G}}
is affine over
U
. The pointed set of isomorphism classes of
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsors over
T
is the first non-abelian cohomology group
H^{1}(T,{\mathbf{G}}):=H^{1}_{\mathrm{fppf}}(T,{\mathbf{G}})
(note that, since
{\mathbf{G}}
is affine, every class in
H^{1}_{\mathrm{fppf}}(T,{\mathbf{G}})
gives rise to a
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor because affine schemes can be glued in the fppf topology). The
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsors are also called principal
{\mathbf{G}}
-bundles.

1.2.
Nisnevich purity conjecture

Let us recall a notion from
[
Fed1
, Def. 1.1]
. Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a semisimple group scheme of adjoint type over a connected scheme
U
(see
[
SGA33
, Exp. XXII, Def. 4.3.3]
). By
[
SGA33
, Exp. XXIV, Prop. 5.10]
there is a sequence
U_{1},\ldots,U_{r}
of finite étale connected
U
-schemes such that

(1)

{\mathbf{G}}\simeq\prod_{i=1}^{r}{\mathbf{G}}^{i},

where
{\mathbf{G}}^{i}
is the Weil restriction of a simple adjoint
U_{i}
-group scheme (see
[
SGA33
, Exp. XXIV, Sect. 5.3]
for the definition of a simple group scheme). Note that the group schemes
{\mathbf{G}}^{i}
are uniquely defined by
{\mathbf{G}}
up to isomorphism. Recall that a semisimple group scheme
{\mathbf{G}}
over a scheme
S
is called
isotropic
, if it contains a non-trivial split torus. If
S
is connected and semilocal, then a semisimple group scheme
{\mathbf{G}}
is isotropic if and only if it contains a proper parabolic subgroup scheme (see
[
SGA33
, Exp. XXVI, Cor. 6.14]
).

Definition 1.1
.

We say that a semisimple
U
-group scheme
{\mathbf{G}}
of adjoint type is
strongly locally isotropic
if each factor
{\mathbf{G}}^{i}
of
{\mathbf{G}}
is isotropic Zariski locally over
U
.

We send the reader to
[
Fed1
, Rem. 1.2]
for the discussion of this notion.

Let now
{\mathbf{G}}
be a reductive
U
-group scheme. Let
{\mathbf{Z}}
be the center of
{\mathbf{G}}
. We have the adjoint group scheme
{\mathbf{G}}^{\ad}:={\mathbf{G}}/{\mathbf{Z}}
(see
[
SGA33
, Exp. XXII, Prop. 4.3.4(i), Prop. 4.3.5(ii), Def. 4.3.6]
).

We prove the Nisnevich conjecture assuming that the regular semilocal integral domain contains an infinite field and the adjoint group scheme of
{\mathbf{G}}
is strongly locally isotropic.

Theorem A
.

Assume that
R
is a regular semilocal integral domain containing an infinite field
k
. Let
K=K(R)
be the quotient field of
R
. Let
f\in R
be such that
f\notin\mathfrak{m}^{2}
for any maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
. Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a reductive
R
-group scheme such that
{\mathbf{G}}^{\ad}
is strongly locally isotropic. Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor over
R_{f}
such that its pullback to
K
is trivial. Then
{\mathcal{E}}
is trivial. In other words, the natural map
H^{1}(R_{f},{\mathbf{G}})\to H^{1}(K,{\mathbf{G}})
has a trivial kernel.

Remark 1.2
.

We note that our proof was extended by Česnavičius in
[
Čes2
]
to the case when
k
is allowed to be finite. It is also shown in loc. cit. that it is enough to assume that
{\mathbf{G}}
is strongly locally isotropic along the hypersurface
\{f=0\}
.

We will axiomatize the properties of the first non-abelian cohomology functor needed to prove the statement (see Theorem
E
below). We derive the above theorem from its “abstract” version in Section
3.2
.

The Nisnevich conjecture is not true in general. We indeed provide counterexamples when
{\mathbf{G}}^{\ad}
fails to be strongly locally isotropic.

Theorem B
.

There are a regular local ring
R
containing an infinite field, a simple simply-connected group scheme
{\mathbf{G}}
over
R
, and a generically trivial
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor
{\mathcal{E}}
over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{R}
that cannot be extended to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{R}
.

This theorem, proved in Section
4
below, contradicts the Nisnevich conjecture. Indeed, let
u
be the closed point of
\Spec R
. Were the Nisnevich conjecture true, we could apply it to the local ring of
\infty\times u
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{R}
, to find a Zariski neighborhood
W
of the infinity divisor
\infty\times\Spec R\subset\mathbb{P}_{R}^{1}
such that
{\mathcal{E}}|_{W-(\infty\times\Spec R)}
is trivial. Then, gluing
{\mathcal{E}}
with the trivial torsor over
W
, we would get an extension of
{\mathcal{E}}
to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{R}
.

Remark 1.3
.

The regular local ring in Theorem
B
can be constructed as follows (see Section
4
and
[
Fed2
]
). Let
k
be an algebraically closed field and consider a closed embedding of
k
-groups
\Spin_{7}\to\GL_{n}
for some
n
. Then
R
is the local ring of a closed point of
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}\times(\GL_{n}/\Spin_{7})
. In this case, the group scheme
{\mathbf{G}}
is a strongly inner form of
\Spin_{7}
.

1.3.
Grothendieck–Serre and Nisnevich conjectures for Nisnevich semisheaves

Colliot–Thélène and Ojanguren in
[
CTO
, Thm 1.1]
proved the conjecture of Grothendieck and Serre for group schemes coming from the base field, assuming additionally that the base field is infinite. Roughly speaking, they proved the conjecture for any presheaf of pointed sets that satisfies the Nisnevich descent, commutes with filtered limits, and is homotopy invariant. Then they have shown that the presheaf of first non-abelian cohomology satisfies these properties.

In 2012 Jean-Pierre Serre asked whether it is possible to recast the proof in
[
FP
]
in the same way. We answer this question positively in Section
1.3.3
. Let us introduce the relevant terminology.

1.3.1.
Essentially smooth morphisms

Definition 1.4
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme. We say that a Noetherian
X
-scheme
Y
is
essentially smooth
over
X
if it is a filtered inverse limit of smooth
X
-schemes with transition morphisms being open affine embeddings.

For a discussion of this notion, see Section
1.4
. We denote by
\Sm^{\prime}_{X}
the full subcategory of the category of
X
-schemes whose objects are Noetherian schemes essentially smooth over
X
. We emphasize that the morphisms in
\Sm^{\prime}_{X}
are arbitrary
X
-morphisms of schemes.

An important example of an essentially smooth scheme is the following: let
X
be an affine scheme, let
{\mathbf{x}}
be a finite set of schematic points of
X
, let
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,{\mathbf{x}}}
be the semilocal ring of
{\mathbf{x}}
, then
\Spec{\mathcal{O}}_{X,{\mathbf{x}}}
is an object of
\Sm^{\prime}_{X}
. In particular, if
X
is integral, then
\Spec K(X)
, where
K(X)
is function field of
X
, is essentially smooth over
X
.

Lemma 1.5
.

(i) Let
X
,
Y
, and
Z
be Noetherian schemes such that
Y
be essentially smooth over
X
and
Z
be essentially smooth over
Y
. Then
Z
is essentially smooth over
X
.

(ii) A base change of an essentially smooth morphism is essentially smooth.

Proof.

(i) Consider first the case, when
Z
is smooth over
Y
. By definition of essentially smooth morphisms, we can write
Y=\lim\limits_{\longleftarrow}Y_{\alpha}
, where each
Y_{\alpha}
is smooth over
X
and the transition morphisms are open affine embeddings. We can find
\alpha
and a
Y_{\alpha}
-scheme
Z_{\alpha}
such that
Z=Z_{\alpha}\times_{Y_{\alpha}}Y
(see
[
EGA44
, Prop. 17.7.8(ii)]
). For
\beta\succeq\alpha
, set
Z_{\beta}:=Z_{\alpha}\times_{Y_{\alpha}}Y_{\beta}
. Then
Z=\lim\limits_{\longleftarrow}Z_{\beta}
. Note that there is
\gamma
such that for
\beta\succeq\gamma
the scheme
Z_{\beta}
is smooth over
Y_{\beta}
(indeed, note that the projection
Z_{\alpha}\to Y_{\alpha}
is smooth over the points of
Y
, then use openness of smoothness and the assumption that our schemes are Noetherian). Then
Z_{\beta}
is also smooth over
X
, and we see that
Z
is essentially smooth over
X
.

Now consider the general case. Write
Z=\lim\limits_{\longleftarrow}Z_{\alpha}
, where
Z_{\alpha}
are smooth over
Y
and the transition morphisms are open affine embeddings. Fix some
\alpha_{0}
and set
Z^{\prime}:=Z_{\alpha_{0}}
. We may assume that all
Z_{\alpha}
are open subschemes of
Z^{\prime}
. By the already proved,
Z^{\prime}
is essentially smooth over
X
, so we can write
Z^{\prime}=\lim\limits_{\longleftarrow}Z^{\prime}_{\beta}
, where
Z^{\prime}_{\beta}
are smooth over
X
and the morphisms are open affine embeddings. Again, we may assume that all
Z^{\prime}_{\beta}
are open subschemes of a fixed
Z^{\prime\prime}
smooth over
X
. For each
\alpha
, since
Z_{\alpha}
is open in
Z^{\prime}
, we can find
\beta(\alpha)
and an open subscheme
W_{\alpha}\subset Z^{\prime}_{\beta(\alpha)}
such that

Z_{\alpha}=\lim\limits_{\beta\succeq\beta(\alpha)}W_{\alpha}\cap Z^{\prime}_{\beta}.

(Indeed, we reduce to the case, when
Z^{\prime\prime}
is affine, in which case
Z^{\prime}_{\beta}
,
Z^{\prime}
, and
Z_{\alpha}
are affine as well.) Now
Z
is the limit of the filtered system
W_{\alpha}\cap Z^{\prime}_{\beta}
, and we see that it is essentially smooth over
X
.

(ii) is clear.
∎

1.3.2.
Nisnevich semisheaves

Torsors can be glued in the Nisnevich topology. However, torsors that are isomorphic locally in the Nisnevich topology need not be isomorphic. Thus, the presheaf of first non-abelian cohomology satisfies only one part of the sheaf axiom. We call such presheaves
semisheaves
. Let us give the definitions.

Definition 1.6
.

An
elementary distinguished square
is a Cartesian diagram of schemes:

(2)

\begin{CD}W@>{}>{}>V\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{p}V\\
U@>{j}>{}>Y,\end{CD}

where
p
is étale,
j
is an open embedding, and
p^{-1}(Y-U)\to Y-U
is an isomorphism, where we equip the corresponding closed subsets with the reduced scheme structures.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme and
{\mathbf{F}}
be a presheaf of pointed sets on
\Sm^{\prime}/X
. We say that
{\mathbf{F}}
is a
Nisnevich semisheaf of pointed sets
if for any elementary distinguished square (
2
) in
\Sm^{\prime}/X
the corresponding map

{\mathbf{F}}(Y)\to{\mathbf{F}}(U)\times_{{\mathbf{F}}(W)}{\mathbf{F}}(V)

is surjective.

Remarks 1.8
.

(i) If
Y=Y_{1}\cup Y_{2}
is a Zariski cover of
Y
, then

\begin{CD}Y_{1}\cap Y_{2}@>{}>{}>Y_{1}\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
Y_{2}@>{}>{}>Y\end{CD}

is an elementary distinguished square. Thus, every Nisnevich semisheaf also satisfies gluing in the Zariski topology.

(ii) Our definition of a Nisnevich semisheaf is similar to
[
MV
, Ch. 3, Def. 1.3]
except that our schemes are essentially smooth rather than smooth over
X
, and we consider semisheaves of pointed sets rather than sheaves of sets.

1.3.3.
A Grothendieck–Serre type theorem for Nisnevich semisheaves

Let
X
be a smooth integral affine
k
-scheme, where
k
is a field. Let
{\mathbf{F}}
be a Nisnevich semisheaf of pointed sets on
\Sm^{\prime}/X
. We will formulate the properties under which
{\mathbf{F}}
satisfies a Grothendieck–Serre type statement.

(Lim)

{\mathbf{F}}
commutes with filtered inverse limits, provided that the transition morphisms are open embeddings.

The next property is a version of a local triviality of
{\mathbf{F}}
. If
\pi\colon Y\to X
is an essentially smooth morphism used to view
Y
as an object of
\Sm^{\prime}/X
, then we denote the value of
{\mathbf{F}}
on
Y
by
{\mathbf{F}}(Y,\pi)
, when we need to emphasize the dependence on
\pi
.

(LT)
Assume that
W
is an affine integral semilocal
k
-scheme such that all its closed points are finite over
k
, and let
U\subset W
be a closed subscheme. Assume that we are given two essentially smooth
k
-morphisms
p_{1},p_{2}\colon W\to X
such that
p_{1}|_{U}=p_{2}|_{U}
, and this morphism is essentially smooth. Then there is a
finite étale
k
-morphism
\pi\colon W^{\prime}\to W
with a section
\Delta\colon U\to W^{\prime}
and an isomorphism of semisheaves
(p_{1}\circ\pi)^{*}{\mathbf{F}}\simeq(p_{2}\circ\pi)^{*}{\mathbf{F}}
restricting to the identity isomorphism on
U
.

This property requires some explanation. First of all,
p_{i}\circ\pi
are essentially smooth by Lemma
1.5
, so the pullbacks of semisheaves make sense (again, by Lemma
1.5
). The isomorphism amounts to compatible bijections for any essentially smooth
\psi\colon T\to W^{\prime}
:

{\mathbf{F}}(T,p_{1}\circ\pi\circ\psi)\xrightarrow{\simeq}{\mathbf{F}}(T,p_{2}\circ\pi\circ\psi).

Finally, the condition that this isomorphism restricts to the identity on
U
amounts to having for every
\psi
as above a commutative diagram

(3)

\begin{CD}{\mathbf{F}}(T,p_{1}\circ\pi\circ\psi)@>{\simeq}>{}>{\mathbf{F}}(T,p_{2}\circ\pi\circ\psi)\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
{\mathbf{F}}(T\times_{W^{\prime}}U,p_{1}\circ\pi\circ\psi\circ pr_{1})={\mathbf{F}}(T\times_{{}^{\prime}W}U,p_{2}\circ\pi\circ\psi\circ pr_{1}),\end{CD}

where
pr_{1}
denotes the projection
T\times_{W^{\prime}}U\to T
. Here the bottom identification comes from the fact that both morphisms are equal:

p_{i}\circ\pi\circ\psi\circ pr_{1}=p_{i}\circ\pi\circ\Delta^{\prime}\circ pr_{2}=(p_{i}|_{U})\circ pr_{2}.

(In particular, this morphism is essentially smooth.)

The last property we need is a “Section property”, which is weaker, than homotopy invariance (at least, if the field
k
is infinite).

(Sec)
Let
U\in\Ob(\Sm^{\prime}/X)
be an affine integral semilocal scheme such that all its closed points are finite over
k
. Assume that
Z
is a closed subscheme of
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{U}
finite over
U
. Let

{\mathcal{E}}\in\Ker\Bigl({\mathbf{F}}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{U})\to{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{U}-Z)\Bigr),

then for every section
\Delta\colon U\to\mathbb{A}^{1}_{U}
of the projection
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{U}\to U
we have
\Delta^{*}{\mathcal{E}}=\star
.

Theorem D
.

Let
X
be a smooth integral affine
k
-scheme, where
k
is a field. Let
{\mathbf{F}}
be a Nisnevich semisheaf on
\Sm^{\prime}/X
satisfying properties (Lim), (LT), and (Sec) above. Then for any finite set of schematic points
{\mathbf{y}}\subset X
the map
{\mathbf{F}}({\mathcal{O}}_{X,{\mathbf{y}}})\to{\mathbf{F}}(K(X))
, where
K(X)
is the fraction field of
X
, has a trivial kernel.

We prove this theorem in Section
2.5
after some preliminaries. The utility of working with semisheaves is demonstrated by the following important corollary. Consider a family version of property (Sec):

(SecF)
Let
U\in\Ob(\Sm^{\prime}/X)
be an integral affine semilocal scheme such that all its closed points are finite over
k
. Assume that
Z
is a closed subscheme of
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{U}
finite over
U
. Let
Y\in\Ob(\Sm^{\prime}/k)
be an affine scheme, and

{\mathcal{E}}\in\Ker\Bigl({\mathbf{F}}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{U}\times_{k}Y)\to{\mathbf{F}}((\mathbb{A}^{1}_{U}-Z)\times_{k}Y)\Bigr),

then for every section
\Delta\colon U\times_{k}Y\to\mathbb{A}^{1}_{U}\times_{k}Y
of the projection
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{U}\times_{k}Y\to U\times_{k}Y
we have
\Delta^{*}{\mathcal{E}}=\star
.

Corollary 1.9
.

In the notation of the theorem assume that the Nisnevich semisheaf
{\mathbf{F}}
satisfies properties (Lim), (LT), and (SecF) above. Then for any finite set of schematic points
{\mathbf{y}}\subset X
and any affine scheme
Y\in\Ob(\Sm^{\prime}/k)
the map

1.4.
Variants

Our definition of “essentially smooth” and the decision to work on
\Sm^{\prime}/X
are somewhat ad hoc. We discuss possible variations here.

Some authors would define “essentially smooth” as the limit of smooth schemes, where transition morphisms are étale. This definition would work for us as well. In fact, we can work with any category larger than
\Sm^{\prime}/X
. For example, we can start with a Nisnevich semisheaf
{\mathbf{F}}
on the category of
all
schemes over
X
. The reason is that torsors are defined over any scheme. On the other hand, that would make our Theorems
D
and
E
formally weaker.

We can work with just affine essentially smooth
X
-schemes. That would require additional checks. For example, we would have to make sure that the schemes
Y
and
Z
in Section
2.2
have affine complements. This is possible but is not very convenient. If we follow this “affine” approach, we can also assume that
U
in Definition
1.7
is a principal open subset of
X
, that is, of the form
X_{f}
for
f\in k[X]
. The required gluing property for torsors follows then from
[
CTO
, Prop. 2.6(iv)]
.

In principle, we can work with functors defined on
X
-schemes of finite type. In this case, we must either extend
{\mathbf{F}}
to essentially smooth schemes using (Lim) for definition, or repeatedly shrink our schemes. Note that then even the formulation of Theorem
D
should be changed to the following: if
{\mathcal{E}}\in{\mathbf{F}}(X)
is such that for some non-empty open subscheme
X^{\prime}\subset X
we have
{\mathcal{E}}|_{X^{\prime}}=\star
, then there is an open subscheme
X^{\prime\prime}\subset X

containing

{\mathbf{y}}
such that
{\mathcal{E}}|_{X^{\prime\prime}}=\star
. Theorem
E
should be changed similarly.

We can replace
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,{\mathbf{y}}}
in Theorems
D
and
E
by arbitrary integral semilocal domains geometrically regular over
X
at the price of strengthening (Lim) and using Popescu’s Theorem.

{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime\prime}|_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{R}-Z}=\star
. Using the previous case with

{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime\prime}=\star
. Thus,
{\mathcal{E}}={\mathcal{E}}^{\prime\prime}|_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{R}-Y}=\star
.

Case 3. General case.
For an

, let

denote the length of the generic fiber of

, and let

be the difference between
\deg_{R}T
and the number of connected components of

. In other words, for a connected and quasi-finite over
R

, and then we extend this definition to disconnected schemes by additivity.

We induct on

. If

, then all connected components of

are of degree one over
\Spec R
, so we are in the previous case. Assume that the statement is proved for smaller values of

. Let

be a connected component of

of degree at least two over

. Set

,

,

. Let

be the diagonal component. Since

. Since

. In more detail, we can find an étale morphism

such that

,

, and

.

The étale morphism

maps

isomorphically onto

. Let

in

(existing by Lemma

Then the square

\begin{CD}W-Y^{\prime}@>{}>{}>W-(Y^{\prime}-\Delta)\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{R}-Y@>{}>{}>\mathbb{A}^{1}_{R}-(Y-V)\end{CD}

is an elementary distinguished square. Note that

Thus, since
{\mathbf{F}}
is a Nisnevich semisheaf, we can glue
{\mathcal{E}}
with

to obtain an element
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime\prime\prime}\in{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{R}-(Y-V))
whose restriction to

. Since

.
∎

3.1.
Re-proving the Grothendieck–Serre conjecture

Let
R
be a regular semilocal integral domain containing a field

and let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a reductive group scheme over
R
. We want to show that a generically trivial
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor
{\mathcal{E}}
over
R
is trivial. We may assume that
k
is the prime field of
R
so, in particular, that
k
is perfect. Now, by Popescu’s Theorem (see
[
Pop1
]
,
[
Swa
]
,
[
Spi
]
), we can write
R=\lim\limits_{\longrightarrow}R_{\alpha}
, where each
R_{\alpha}
is smooth (and, in particular, of finite type) over
k
. Changing the direct system
R_{\alpha}
, we may assume that the rings
R_{\alpha}
are integral. For some
\alpha
we can find a reductive group scheme
{\mathbf{G}}_{\alpha}
over
R_{\alpha}
such that
{\mathbf{G}}_{\alpha}|_{R}\simeq{\mathbf{G}}
and a
{\mathbf{G}}_{\alpha}
-torsor
{\mathcal{E}}_{\alpha}
over
R_{\alpha}
such that
{\mathcal{E}}_{\alpha}|_{R}\simeq{\mathcal{E}}
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{\alpha}|_{K(R_{\alpha})}

Set

and let
{\mathbf{y}}\subset X
be the image of the set of all closed points of
\Spec R
in
X
. Then
\Spec R\to X
factors through
\Spec{\mathcal{O}}_{X,{\mathbf{y}}}
. Set
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}:={\mathcal{E}}_{\alpha}|_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X,{\mathbf{y}}}}
. Since
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}|_{R}={\mathcal{E}}
, it is enough to show that
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
is trivial.

Consider the functor
{\mathbf{F}}
on
\Sm^{\prime}/X
given by
{\mathbf{F}}(Y):=H^{1}(Y,{\mathbf{G}}_{\alpha})
. It is enough to show that the map
{\mathbf{F}}({\mathcal{O}}_{X,{\mathbf{y}}})\to{\mathbf{F}}(K(X))
has a trivial kernel. This will follow from Theorem
D
as soon as we verify the hypothesis.

Note that torsors satisfy the étale descent. It follows that
{\mathbf{F}}
is a Nisnevich semisheaf (cf. Definition
1.7
and the first part of the proof of
[
MV
, Ch. 3, Prop. 1.4]
). In more detail, consider a distinguished square (
2
) with
W=U\times_{X}V
and
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsors
{\mathcal{E}}_{U}
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{V}
on
U
and
V
respectively whose restrictions to
W
are isomorphic. Choose an isomorphism
\varphi\colon{\mathcal{E}}_{U}|_{W}\to{\mathcal{E}}_{V}|_{W}
. Note that
U\sqcup V
is an étale cover of
Y
. To descend
{\mathcal{E}}_{U}\sqcup{\mathcal{E}}_{V}
to
X
, we need to construct an isomorphism between the two pullbacks of
{\mathcal{E}}_{U}\sqcup{\mathcal{E}}_{V}
to

(U\sqcup V)\times_{Y}(U\sqcup V)=(U\times_{Y}U)\sqcup(U\times_{Y}V)\sqcup(V\times_{Y}U)\sqcup(V\times_{Y}V)

satisfying the cocycle condition. The isomorphism on
U\times_{Y}U=U
is the identity. The isomorphisms on
U\times_{Y}V
and on
V\times_{Y}U
are given by
\varphi
. To construct an isomorphism on
V\times_{Y}V
, we consider the Zariski covering

V\times_{Y}V=\Delta(V)\sqcup(U\times_{Y}V\times_{Y}V),

where

is the diagonal embedding. The two compositions

V\xrightarrow{\Delta}V\times_{Y}V\to(U\sqcup V)\times_{Y}(U\sqcup V)\rightrightarrows U\sqcup V

are equal so the two pullbacks of
{\mathcal{E}}_{U}\sqcup{\mathcal{E}}_{V}
to

are canonically isomorphic. Next, the two morphisms from

to

can be factored through

U\times_{Y}V\times_{Y}V=(U\times_{Y}V)\times_{U}(U\times_{Y}V)\rightrightarrows U\times_{Y}V,

so the two pullbacks of
{\mathcal{E}}_{V}
are identified with the help of
\varphi
with the two pullback of
{\mathcal{E}}_{U}
via

(U\times_{Y}V)\times_{U}(U\times_{Y}V)\rightrightarrows U\times_{Y}V\to U,

and again they are canonically isomorphic. It is easy to see that the isomorphisms agree on the intersection
\Delta(V)\cap(U\times_{Y}V\times_{Y}V)
. One checks that the isomorphisms satisfy the cocycle condition. We see that
{\mathbf{F}}
is a Nisnevich semisheaf.

It is clear that
{\mathbf{F}}
satisfies property (Lim). Property (LT) follows from
[
Pan1
, Thm. 3.1]
. Property (Sec) follows from
[
Fed1
, Thm. 4]
.

, we can write

for some
b^{\prime}\in R[s]
. Then
b^{\prime}(x+s)=a
and, since

,

aR[s]+bR[s]\subset xR[s]+sR[s]\subsetneq R[s].

This contradiction completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition

Let us denote an infinite field contained in

Fix a closed

(which exists because

is local,

is

\mathbb{A}^{1}_{B}-(0\times\Spec B)

For a scheme

, we denote the set of
S
-morphisms between
S
-ind-schemes
X_{1}
and

by
\Mor_{S}(X_{1},X_{2})
. We abbreviate
\Mor(X_{1},X_{2}):=\Mor_{\Spec k}(X_{1},X_{2})
. We need one more lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

We have a bijection between

and the set of pairs
({\mathcal{E}},\tau)
, where

is a

,

Similarly, we have a bijection between

({\mathcal{E}},\tau)
, where
{\mathcal{E}}
is a
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor over
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{B}
,

Recall that we have fixed a closed

. Now

(7)

\Mor_{B}(T\times_{B}\Spec B^{\prime},\Gr_{\mathbf{G}})\simeq\\

This is, in fact, an isomorphism of presheaves of sets on the category of affine

-schemes. Since both presheaves are actually sheaves in the Zariski topology, this isomorphism extends to an isomorphism of similar presheaves on the category of all

We need one more lemma.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let, as above,
G
be a split nontrivial semisimple group over a field

. Then the affine Grassmannian
\Gr_{G}
contains a closed subscheme isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{1}
.

Proof.

Since
G
is split, we can choose a split maximal torus
T
and a Borel subgroup
B
such that
T\subset B\subset G
. Let
\lambda\colon\mathop{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}\to T
be a nontrivial cocharacter. Consider the composition
\Spec k(\!(t)\!)\to\mathop{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}\xrightarrow{\lambda}T\to G
, where
k(\!(t)\!)
is the field of Laurent series. This is a loop of
G
, denote by

its projection to
\Gr_{G}=G(\!(t)\!)/G[\![t]\!]
. By
[
Fed2
, Lm. 5.9]
the
G
-orbit of

in
\Gr_{G}
is isomorphic to the flag variety of parabolic subgroups of type
\lambda
of
G
, that is, to

, where
P_{\lambda}\supset B
is the standard parabolic subgroup of
G
corresponding to
\lambda
.

It remains to show that
G/P_{\lambda}
contains a closed subscheme isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{1}
. Let
\alpha
be a simple root of
G
with respect to
T\subset B
such that the corresponding root subgroup is not contained in

, and let
H
be the subgroup of

whose Lie algebra is the principal

. Then

is isomorphic to

or

is a nontrivial parabolic subgroup of

. Thus, we have a closed embedding

.

We can now complete the proof of Proposition
4.1
. Clearly,
B^{\prime}
is not Artinian. By Lemma
4.4
there is a morphism
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{B^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}
that cannot be extended to

\mathbb{P}^{1}_{B^{\prime}}
. The corresponding
k

\mathbb{A}^{1}_{B^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
cannot be extended to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{B^{\prime}}
either. Let

be the composition of this morphism with the constructed above closed embedding
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{1}\hookrightarrow\Gr_{G}
. By construction,
\varphi
cannot be extended to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{B^{\prime}}
.

By Lemma
4.5
,
\varphi
corresponds to a
{\mathbf{G}}
-torsor
{\mathcal{E}}
over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{B}
with a trivialization
\tau
on
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}\times(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{B}-\Spec B^{\prime})
. For a point

be the local ring of

\mathbb{P}^{1}_{B}
. We claim that for some

, then

4.3
(i) to

The trivial

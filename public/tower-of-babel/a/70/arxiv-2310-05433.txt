Entire holomorphic curves into ℙ n ​ ( ℂ ) \mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C}) intersecting n + 1 n+1 general hypersurfaces
Zhangchi Chen, Dinh Tuan Huynh, Ruiran Sun, Song-Yan Xie

Abstract
. Let { D i } i = 1 n + 1 \{D_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+1} be n + 1 n+1 hypersurfaces in ℙ n ​ ( ℂ ) \mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C}) with total degrees ∑ i = 1 n + 1 deg ⁡ D i ⩾ n + 2 \sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\deg D_{i}\geqslant n+2 , in general position and satisfying a generic geometric condition: every n n hypersurfaces intersect only at smooth points and the intersection is transversal. Then, for every algebraically non-degenerate entire holomorphic curve f : ℂ → ℙ n ​ ( ℂ ) f\colon\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C}) , we show a Second Main Theorem: ∑ i = 1 n + 1 δ f ​ ( D i ) < n + 1 \sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\delta_{f}(D_{i})<n+1 in terms of defect inequality in Nevanlinna theory. This is the first result in the literature on Second Main Theorem for n + 1 n+1 general hypersurfaces in ℙ n ​ ( ℂ ) \mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C}) with optimal total degrees.

1.
Introduction

Given a codimension one subvariety
D
in a complex manifold
X
such that
the complement
X\setminus D
satisfies certain complex hyperbolicity quality in spirit of the Kobayashi conjecture
[
Kob70
]
or the Green-Griffiths conjecture
[
GG80
]
, one seeks to reach a quantitative strengthening in terms of Second Main Theorem in Nevanlinna theory, which bounds in certain proportional way, the “growth rate” of an algebraically nondegenerate holomorphic map
f\colon S\rightarrow X
from certain source space
S
usually being
\mathbb{C}
, from above by the “intersection frequency” or “impact” of
f(S)
with respect to
D
.

The classical example is Nevanlinna’s celebrated work
[
Nev25
]
which quantifies the

Little Picard Theorem
.

If
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}
are three distinct points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
, then any meromorphic function
f\colon\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\backslash\{p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}\}
is constant.
∎

For higher dimensional target space
X
,
for various source spaces
S
and holomorphic maps
f\colon S\rightarrow X
, we refer the readers to
[
Nev70
,
Sto77
,
Fuj93
,
NW14
,
Ru21
]
for later developments.
The leading problem is this direction is the following

Fundamental Conjecture of Entire Curves

(cf.
[
Gri72
,
NW14
]
)
.

Let
D
be a simple normal crossing divisor on the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
of degree
d\geqslant n+2
. Let
f\colon\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
be an entire holomorphic curve. If the image of
f
is not contained in any hypersurface, then the following Second Main Theorem type estimate holds

(1.1)

(d-n-1)\,T_{f}(r)\leqslant N^{[k_{0}]}_{f}(r,D)+o\big(T_{f}(r)\big)\qquad\parallel,

where
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
is a positive integer independent of
f
.

Here
N^{[k_{0}]}_{f}(r,D)
and
T_{f}(r)
are standard notions in Nevanlinna theory, which will be introduced in the next paragraph. For non-negatively valued functions
\phi(r)
,
\psi(r)
defined for
r\geqslant r_{0}\geqslant 0
, we write

\phi(r)\leqslant\psi(r)\qquad\parallel

if the inequality holds for
r\geqslant r_{0}
outside a Borel set of finite Lebesgue measure.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
be an entire holomorphic curve and let
D\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
be a hypersurface such that
f(\mathbb{C})\not\subset D
. The order function

T_{f}(r)\,:=\,\int_{1}^{r}\dfrac{\dif t}{t}\int_{\mathbb{D}_{t}}f^{*}\omega_{FS}\qquad{{\scriptstyle(r\,>\,1)},}

is a geometric equivalent version of Nevanlinna’s characteristic function, historically discovered independently by Shimizu and Ahlfors
[
NW14
, pp. 11–12]
,
measuring the area growth of the image of the disc
\mathbb{D}_{r}
centered at
0
with radius
r
, with respect to the Fubini–Study metric
\omega_{FS}
.
For
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
,
the level–
k
truncated counting function

N_{f}^{[k]}(r,D)\,:=\,\int_{1}^{r}\frac{\dif t}{t}\sum_{|z|<t}\min\{k,\ord_{z}f^{*}D\}

captures the intersection frequencies of
f(\mathbb{C})\cap D
. The defect of
f
with respect to
D
is given by

\delta_{f}^{[k]}(D):=\liminf_{r\rightarrow\infty}\bigg(1-\dfrac{N_{f}^{[k]}(r,D)}{\deg(D)\,T_{f}(r)}\bigg).

For brevity, when
k=\infty
, we write
N_{f}(r,D)
,
\delta_{f}(D)
instead of
N_{f}^{[\infty]}(r,D)
,
\delta_{f}^{[\infty]}(D)
.

The
First Main Theorem
in Nevanlinna theory, which is a reformulation of the Lelong-Jensen formula, provides an upper bound for the counting function in terms of the order function:

N_{f}(r,D)\leqslant d\,T_{f}(r)+O(1),

which implies

(1.2)

0\leqslant\delta_{f}(D)\leqslant 1.

The reverse direction, i.e., bounding the order function from above by the sum of counting functions of many divisors, is usually much harder. Such type of results are called
Second Main Theorems
. The question of establishing a satisfactory estimate of the form (
1.1
) is still very open in general. When
D
consists of
q\geqslant n+2
hyperplanes
H_{i}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
in general position (
1\leqslant i\leqslant q
), and
f:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
is linearly nondegenerate, such a Second Main Theorem with truncation at level
n
is established by H. Cartan
[
Car33
]
, which yields the following
defect relation

\sum_{i=1}^{q}\delta_{f}^{[n]}(H_{i})\leqslant n+1.

When all components of
D
are hypersurfaces and the image of
f
is not contained in
D
, a Second Main Theorem without effective truncation level
[
ES91
]
were obtained by Eremenko-Sodin via potential theoretic method, which implies a defect relation bounded by
2n
. Assuming furthermore that
f
is algebraically nondegenerate, a stronger estimate
[
Ru04
]
was established by Ru, which yields a defect relation bounded by
n+1
.
In
[
Gri72
]
, Griffiths conjectured (
1.1
) for
k_{0}=\infty
in the right-hand side.
This conjecture quantifies

Logarithmic Green-Griffiths’ Conjecture

(
[
GG80
]
)
.

If
D
is a simple normal crossing divisor on the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
of degree
d\geqslant n+2
, then the image of any holomorphic curve
f:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
omitting
D
lies in some proper algebraic subvariety of
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
.

When
D
has
q\leqslant n+1
components, few Second Main Theorem type results toward (
1.1
) were known. In hindsight,
the difficulty is intimately related to establishing the (conjectured) hyperbolicity property of
X\setminus D

using the jet differential technique introduced by Bloch
[
Blo26
]
.
Indeed, on one hand, the logarithmic fundamental vanishing theorem of entire curves states that,
any negatively twisted logarithmic (along
D
)
k
-jet differential
\omega
serves as an obstruction for the existence of entire curves

f:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow X\setminus D
, since
f
must obey the differential equation
f^{*}\omega\equiv 0
(cf. e.g.
[
Ru21
]
).
On the other hand, it is shown in

[
HVX19
, Theorem 3.1]

that, for any
negatively twisted logarithmic (along
D
)
k
-jet differential
\omega
and for any entire curve
f:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow X
not contained in
D
, if
f^{*}\omega\not\equiv 0
, then one can obtain a Second Main Theorem (SMT) for the entire curve
f
with respect to the divisor
D
.
Thus, for showing the hyperbolicity of
X\setminus D
,
or for obtaining a SMT of
f
with respect to
D
, one tries
to find sufficiently many negatively twisted logarithmic (along
D
)
k
-jet differentials
\{\omega_{i}\}_{i=1}^{M}
having “tiny” common base loci supporting no entire curve therein.

However, in practice, such approach is very difficult.
For instance, in the simplest case that
k=1
and
D=\varnothing
, there was a related conjecture of Debarre
[
Deb05
]
anticipating that,
for general
c\geqslant n/2
hypersurfaces
H_{1},\dots,H_{c}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
with large
degrees
\gg 1
,
the intersection
X:=H_{1}\cap\cdots\cap H_{c}
shall have
ample cotangent bundle
T_{X}^{*}
. The Debarre ampleness conjecture was first proved in
[
Xie18
]
(arXiv:1510.06323),
in which the difficulty of controlling the base loci was settled by ad hoc symmetry of certain sophisticated deformed Fermat type polynomial equations,
using explicit
1
-jet differentials obtained in
[
Bro16
]
. See also another proof
[
BD18
]
(arXiv:1511.04709) appeared shortly later using more symmetric generalized Fermat type polynomial equations.

In the vein of Siu’s strategy
[
Siu04
]
for the Kobayashi and Green-Griffiths conjectures, namely by using slanted vector fields
[
Siu02
,
Mer09
,
Dar16
]
and certain Riemann-Roch calculation
[
Dar15
]
, a Second Main Theorem
[
HVX19
]
was established in the case
q=1
for algebraically nondegenerate entire curve
f:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
with respect to a general hypersurface
D\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
of large degree
d\geqslant 15\,(5\,n+1)\,n^{n}
. Thanks to the breakthrough
[
RY18
]
of Riedl and Yang, one can remove the Zariski dense assumption on
f(\mathbb{C})\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
. Moreover, the exponential degree bound can be improved to some polynomial bound
O(n^{4})
by the recent advancement of Bérczi and Kirwan
[
BK23b
,
BK23a
]
.

From now on, finitely many hypersurfaces
D_{1},\dots,D_{q}
,
q\geqslant n
, are said to be
intersecting transversally
, if for any
n
hypersurfaces
D_{j_{1}},\dots,D_{j_{n}}
, and for any
z
in their intersection, we have

•

z
is
a smooth point
of each
D_{j_{k}}
,
1\leqslant k\leqslant n
;

•

the normal vectors
of the tangent spaces
T_{z}D_{j_{k}}
,
1\leqslant k\leqslant n
, are
linearly independent
.

In this paper, we study the case that

D=\cup_{i=1}^{q}D_{i}

consists of
q=n+1
hypersurfaces
D_{i}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
(not all being hyperplanes) in general position and intersecting transversally.
The algebraic degeneracy of entire holomorphic curves into the complement

\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})\setminus D

was established by Noguchi-Winkelmann-Yamanoi
[
NWY07
]
. See
[
GSW21
, Theorem 1.6]
and
[
GSW22
, Theorem 1.2]
for moving target versions. Quantitatively, we obtained a Second Main Theorem.

Main Theorem
.

Let
\{D_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+1}
be
n+1
hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
with total degrees
\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\deg D_{i}\geqslant n+2
, in general position and intersecting transversally. Then, for every algebraically nondegenerate entire holomorphic curve
f\colon\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
, the following defect inequality holds

(1.3)

\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\delta_{f}(D_{i})<n+1.

Clearly,
\delta_{f}(D_{i})=1
if and only if

(1.4)

N_{f}(r,D_{i})=o\big(T_{f}(r)\big)\qquad{{\scriptstyle(r\,\rightarrow\,\infty)}},

literally, the curve
f
does not meet
D_{i}
often. Theorefore (
1.3
) serves as a weak Second Main Theorem.

As a matter of fact, our initial motivation is to study the case of
3
conics in
\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{C})

[
GP85
,
DSW95
]
.
Even in this simple case, the aforementioned methods seem infertile.

Back to our main theorem, we will take an alternative geometric approach in which the number

n+1=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})+1

of components of
D
is critical.
Let us sketch the proof now.
For simplicity, we assume that every hypersurface

D_{i}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
is defined by some homogeneous polynomial
Q_{i}\in\mathbb{C}[z_{0},\dots,z_{n}]
of equal degree
d
.
Suppose on the contrary that (
1.3
) fails, i.e., by (
1.2
),
all defect values reach maximum

(1.5)

\delta_{f}(D_{i})=1\qquad{\scriptstyle(i\,=\,1,\,\dots,\,n+1)}.

For the parabolic Riemann surface

\mathbb{C}\setminus f^{-1}(D)
,
we will employ an exhaustion function
\sigma
such that
the weighted Euler characteristic
\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma}(r)
is negligable

(1.6)

\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma}(r)}{T_{f,\sigma}(r)}=0

compared with the parabolic order function
T_{f,\sigma}(r)
(see Section
2
).

The key trick is introducing the auxiliary
hypersurface
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
defined by the Jacobian

\det\dfrac{\partial(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{n+1})}{\partial(z_{0},\dots,z_{n})}

of degree

\sum_{i=0}^{n}d_{i}-(n+1)
.
Such hypersurface
\mathcal{V}
was used in
[
BD01
, p. 261]
and
[
CZ08
]
for
n=2
, and in
[
GSW21
,
GSW22
]
for general
n
.
Geometrically,
\mathcal{V}

consists of the critical points of the endomorphism

F(z)=\big[Q_{1}(z):Q_{2}(z):\dots:Q_{n+1}(z)\big]\quad\colon\quad\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C}).

Whence if the entire curve
f
intersects
\mathcal{V}
at a point
P\in\mathbb{C}
, the composition
g:=F\circ f
must be tangent to
\mathcal{W}:=F(\mathcal{V})
, i.e., having intersection multiplicity
\geqslant 2
at
P
.
For
\{H_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+1}
in general position, the hypersurface
\mathcal{V}
is
in general position with

\{H_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+1}
,
i.e.
,
\mathcal{V}
and any
n
hypersurfaces among
\{H_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+1}
have empty intersection,
if and only if

\{H_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+1}
are intersecting transversally. The
if
part is provided in
[
GSW22
, Section 5]
. We will prove the
only if
part in Lemma
4.1
. Thus we can apply a Second Main Theorem of Ru
[
Ru04
]
to show that,
under the presumed condition (
1.5
),
the intersection frequency of the holomorphic curve
\widetilde{f}:=f|_{\mathbb{C}\setminus f^{-1}(D)}
with

\mathcal{V}
must be high. This will contradict another fact, to be obtained in Section
2
following a strategy of
Noguchi-Winkelmann-Yamanoi
[
NWY08
]
,
that the parabolic holomorphic curve
\widetilde{g}:=g|_{\mathbb{C}\setminus f^{-1}(D)}

into the semi-abelian variety
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
cannot be
tangent to the effective divisor
\mathcal{W}
very often. For details of proofs, see Sections
3
and
4
.

2.
Parabolic Nevanlinna theory in semi-abelian varieties and projective spaces

A non-compact Riemann surface
\mathcal{Y}
is called
parabolic
if it admits a smooth
exhaustion function

\sigma\colon{\mathcal{Y}}\to[1,\infty[

such that
\mathrm{log}\,\sigma
is harmonic outside a compact subset of

\mathcal{Y}
. For every
r>1
, we denote by

B^{\sigma}_{r}:=\big\{z\in\mathcal{Y}:\sigma(z)<r\big\},\qquad\qquad S^{\sigma}_{r}:=\big\{z\in\mathcal{Y}:\sigma(z)=r\big\},

the open
parabolic ball
and the
parabolic sphere
of radius
r
respectively.
By Sard’s theorem,
for almost every value
r\in\mathbb{R}_{>1}
, the sphere
S^{\sigma}_{r}
is
smooth. We donote the Euler characteristic
of
B^{\sigma}_{r}
by
\chi_{\sigma}(r)
,
and we consider the induced length measure

\dif\mu_{r}:=\dif^{c}\mathrm{log}\,\sigma|_{S^{\sigma}_{r}},

where
\dif^{c}:=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{4\pi}(\bar{\partial}-\partial)
.
The
weighted Euler characteristic

\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma}(r)
is then defined by logarithmic average

\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma}(r):=\int_{1}^{r}\chi_{\sigma}(t)\,\dfrac{\dif t}{t}\qquad{\scriptstyle(r\,>\,1)}.

Replacing the exhaustion
\mathbb{C}=\cup_{r>1}\,\mathbb{D}_{r}
by
\mathcal{Y}=\cup_{r>1}\,B^{\sigma}_{r}
, one can develop Nevanlinna theory for parabolic Riemann surfaces (cf.
[
Sto77
,
PS21
]
). Let
X
be a compact complex manifold. Let
L
be a holomorphic line bundle on
X
equipped with some Hermitian metric
\|\!\cdot\!\|
with the Chern
(1,1)
-form
\omega_{L}
. Let
E
be an effective divisor defined by a global nonzero section
s
of
L
. In the parabolic context, the standard notions in Nevanlinna theory are defined as follows.

1.

The
k
-truncated counting function

N^{[k]}_{f,\sigma}(r,E)\,:=\,\int_{1}^{r}\sum_{z\in B^{\sigma}_{t}}\min\{k,\ord_{z}f^{*}E\}\,\frac{\dif t}{t}\qquad{{\scriptstyle(k\,\in\,\mathbb{N}\,\cup\,\{\infty\};\,r\,>\,1)}}.

2.

The proximity function

m_{f,\sigma}(r,E)\,:=\,\int_{S^{\sigma}_{r}}\mathrm{log}\,\dfrac{1}{\|s\circ f\|}\,\dif\mu_{r}\qquad{\scriptstyle(r\,>\,1)}.

3.

The order function

T_{f,\sigma}(r,L)\,:=\,\int_{1}^{r}\dfrac{\dif t}{t}\int_{B^{\sigma}_{t}}f^{*}\omega_{L}\qquad{{\scriptstyle(r\,>\,1)}}.

By Jensen’s formula in the parabolic setting
[
PS21
, Proposition 3.1]
, one has the following

Parabolic First Main Theorem
.

Let
f\colon\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow X
be a holomorphic map such that
f(\mathcal{Y})\not\subset\mathrm{Supp}(E)
. Then

T_{f,\sigma}(r,L)\,=\,m_{f,\sigma}(r,E)+N_{f,\sigma}(r,E)+O(1)\qquad{{\scriptstyle(r\,>\,1)}}.

∎

For a parabolic Second Main Theorem, the weighted Euler characteristic naturally appears. Define the proximity function for the critical set as
[
PS21
, Definition 3.4]

\mathfrak{X}^{+}_{\sigma}(r):=\int_{S^{\sigma}_{r}}\mathrm{log}\,^{+}|\text{d}\sigma(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial z})|^{2}\,\text{d}\mu_{r}.

In
[
PS21
, pp. 32–33]
, it is proved that for
\mathcal{Y}=\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathcal{E}
with
\mathcal{E}=\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
a discrete countable set of points in
\mathbb{C}
, one can take
r_{j}\in(0,1)
sufficiently small such that

\bullet

the discs
\mathbb{D}(a_{j},2\,r_{j})
are disjoint,

\bullet

the sum
\sum\limits_{j\geqslant 1}r_{j}<+\infty
.

For a smoothing
\sigma
(see Section
3
or the Appendix for details) of the exhaustion function
\hat{\sigma}
defined by

\mathrm{log}\,\hat{\sigma}:=\mathrm{log}\,^{+}|z|+\sum\limits_{j\geqslant 1}r_{j}\,\mathrm{log}\,^{+}\tfrac{r_{j}}{|z-a_{j}|},

we see that
\dif\dif^{c}\mathrm{log}\,\sigma
is of finite mass and

\mathfrak{X}^{+}_{\sigma}(r)=\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma}(r)+O(\mathrm{log}\,r).

Parabolic Logarithmic Derivative Lemma
.

(
[
PS21
, Theorem 3.8]
)
Let
f\colon\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
be a nonconstant meromorphic function. For any
\delta>0
, one has

(2.1)

\displaystyle m_{\frac{f^{\prime}}{f},\sigma}(r)\leqslant(1+\delta)^{2}\,\big(\mathrm{log}\,T_{f,\sigma}(r)\big)+(1+\delta)\,\mathrm{log}\,r+\mathfrak{X}^{+}_{\sigma}(r)+O(1)\qquad\parallel.

∎

In particular, when
\mathcal{Y}=\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathcal{E}
with
\mathcal{E}=\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
a discrete countable set of points in
\mathbb{C}
, there exists some positive constant
C>0
such that the following estimate

m_{\frac{f^{\prime}}{f},\sigma}(r)\leqslant C\,\big(\mathrm{log}\,T_{f,\sigma}(r)+\mathrm{log}\,r\big)+\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma}(r)\qquad\parallel.

Consequently, some results in the value distribution theory of entire holomorphic curves can be translated to the parabolic setting.

Throughout this section, we fix a smooth exhaustion
\sigma
on the parabolic Riemann surface
\mathcal{Y}
.
In
[
NWY08
]
, Noguchi-Winkelmann-Yamanoi established a Second Main Theorem type estimate for
k
-jet liftings of algebraically nondegenerate entire holomorphic curves
f
into semi-abelian varieties with the optimal truncation level-one counting function, accepting an error term of the form
\epsilon\,T_{f,\sigma}(r)
, or equivalently
o\big(T_{f,\sigma}(r)\big)
, see
[
Yam13
, Lemma 1.5]
. This provides several applications in studying the degeneracy of holomorphic curves
[
NWY07
,
NWY08
]
.

This remarkable result can be translated into the parabolic context, but we need to take into account the weighted Euler characteristic
\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma}(r)
appearing each time when we apply the logarithmic derivative lemma. Hence from now on, we assume that

(2.2)

\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma}(r)}{T_{f,\sigma}(r)}=0.

For our Main Theorem, we only need to deal with parabolic holomorphic curves in
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
. Nevertheless, we must use higher order jets and establish a Second Main Theorem type estimate, not only for divisors, but also for subvarieties of codimension
\geqslant 2
(cf.
[
NW14
, Section 2.4.1]
). For the notions and the properties of logarithmic
k
-jet bundles, we refer the readers to
[
Nog86
,
DL01
]
.

Under the assumption (
2.2
), we can translate the result of
[
NW14
, Theorem 6.5.1]

for the special case
A:=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
in the parabolic context as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{Y}
be a parabolic Riemann surface with an exhaustion function
\sigma
. Let
f\colon\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow A:=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
be an algebraically nondegenerate holomorphic curve. For an integer
k\geqslant 0
, denote by
J_{k}f
the
k
-jet lifting of
f
and by
X_{k}(f)
the Zariski closure of
J_{k}f
in the
k
-jet space
J_{k}(A)
. Let
Z
be an algebraic reduced subvariety of
X_{k}(f)
.

(1)

There exists a compactification
\bar{X}_{k}(f)
of
X_{k}(f)
such that

T_{J_{k}f,\sigma}(r,\omega_{\bar{Z}})\leqslant N^{[1]}_{J_{k}f,\sigma}(r,Z)+o\big(T_{f,\sigma}(r)\big)\,\,\parallel,

where
\bar{Z}
denotes the closure of
Z
in
\bar{X}_{k}(f)
.

(2)

Assume furthermore that
\codim_{X_{k}(f)}Z\geqslant 2
, then

T_{J_{k}f,\sigma}(r,\omega_{\bar{Z}})=o\big(T_{f,\sigma}(r)\big)\,\,\parallel.

(3)

In the case where
k=0
and
Z
is an effective divisor
D
on
A
, there exists a smooth compactification of
A
independent of
f
, such that

T_{f,\sigma}\big(r,L(\overline{D})\big)\leqslant N^{[1]}_{f,\sigma}(r,D)+o\Big(T_{f,\sigma}\big(r,L(\overline{D})\big)\Big)\,\,\parallel.

This together with the First Main Theorem yields the following

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{Y}
be a parabolic Riemann surface with an exhaustion function
\sigma
. Let
D
be an effective divisor on
A:=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
. Let
f\colon\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow A
be an algebraically nondegenerate holomorphic map. Then there exists a smooth compactification of
A
independent
of
f
, such that

N_{f,\sigma}(r,D)-N^{[1]}_{f,\sigma}(r,D)=o\Big(T_{f,\sigma}\big(r,L(\overline{D})\big)\Big)\,\,\parallel.

∎

The proof of Theorem
2.1
will be reached later in this section by implementing some modifications along the strategy of
[
NWY08
]
. First, we translate
[
NW14

to the parabolic context directly.

Lemma 2.3

Let

be a reduced divisor on

For an integer

Lemma 2.4

There exists a compactification

of

\displaystyle m_{J_{k}f,\sigma}(r,\bar{Z})

\displaystyle=o\big(T_{f,\sigma}(r)\big)\,\,\parallel,

\displaystyle T_{J_{k}f,\sigma}(r,\omega_{\bar{Z}})

\displaystyle\leqslant N^{[\ell_{0}]}_{J_{k}f,\sigma}(r,Z)+o\big(T_{f,\sigma}(r)\big)\,\,\parallel,

where

denotes the closure of

in

. ∎

Our next goal is to show that the “impact” of

with codimension

Lemma 2.5
.

Let

be a subvariety with

. Then

T_{J_{k}f,\sigma}(r,\omega_{\bar{Z}})=o\big(T_{f,\sigma}(r)\big)\,\,\parallel.

In particular one has

Proof.

This result is an analog of

for

being semi-abelian varieties of positive dimensions with

\displaystyle B\subset\St^{0}_{A}\big(X_{k}(f)\big)\quad{\scriptstyle(k\,\geqslant\,0)},\quad T_{q^{B}\circ f,\sigma}(r)=o\big(T_{f,\sigma}(r)\big)\,\,\parallel,

where

denotes the projection to the second factor. To do this, we consider the

We then use (

automatically holds. By Lemma

By induction on the dimension of

.

We follow the argument in

3.
A smooth exhaustion function on some parabolic Riemann surface

In this section, we construct a piecewise smooth exhaustion function
\hat{\sigma}
on the parabolic Riemann surface
\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathcal{E}
, where
\mathcal{E}=\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a discrete countable set of points on
\mathbb{C}
. Then we describe a smooth exhaustion function
\sigma
close to
\hat{\sigma}
. Details are presented in the Appendix.

We arrange
a_{j}
so that

|a_{1}|\leqslant|a_{2}|\leqslant\dots.

Take
r_{j}\in(0,1)
sufficiently small such that

\bullet

the discs
\mathbb{D}(a_{j},2\,r_{j})
are disjoint;

\bullet

the sum
\sum\limits_{j\geqslant 1}r_{j}<+\infty
.

Let
\mathcal{Y}:=\mathbb{C}\backslash\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
and define a piecewise smooth exhaustion function
\hat{\sigma}:\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow[1,+\infty)
by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}(z):=\exp\Big(\mathrm{log}\,^{+}|z|+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}r_{j}\,\mathrm{log}\,^{+}\tfrac{r_{j}}{|z-a_{j}|}\Big).

In other words

\hat{\tau}:=\mathrm{log}\,\hat{\sigma}=\mathrm{log}\,^{+}|z|+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}r_{j}\,\mathrm{log}\,^{+}\tfrac{r_{j}}{|z-a_{j}|}.

Obviously, the function
\hat{\tau}

\bullet

takes value in
[0,+\infty)
;

\bullet

is continuous on
\mathcal{Y}
;

\bullet

is smooth, indeed harmonic, outside the circle
S(0,1):=\big\{|z|=1\big\}
and the disjoint circles
S(a_{j},r_{j}):=\big\{|z-a_{j}|=r_{j}\big\}
.

By the Poincaré-Lelong formula (cf. e.g.
[
NW14
, Theorem 2.2.16]
), it is clear that

(3.2)

\dif\dif^{c}\hat{\tau}=\frac{1}{2}\,\nu(0,1)+\frac{r_{j}}{2}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\big(\nu(a_{j},r_{j})-\delta_{a_{j}}\big)

is a distribution of order
0
and locally of finite mass. Here
\nu(a_{j},r_{j})
is the Haar measure on the circle
S(a_{j},r_{j})
.

Use the notations

B^{\hat{\sigma}}_{r}:=\big\{z\in\mathcal{Y}:\hat{\sigma}(z)<r\big\},\qquad\qquad S^{\hat{\sigma}}_{r}:=\big\{z\in\mathcal{Y}:\hat{\sigma}(z)=r\big\},

for the
\hat{\sigma}
-ball of radius

and its boundary. For

, the boundary

is a piece-wise smooth curve. It has

many connected components. Non-smooth points come from two cases:

(1)

when

for some

, there is one non-smooth point which is the tangent point of

to

;

(2)

when

for some

, there are two non-smooth points which are the intersection points of
S(0,r)
and
S(a_{j},r_{j})
.

4.
Proof of the Main Theorem

Let
f\colon\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
be a holomorphic curve and let
D=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}D_{i}
be a simple normal crossing divisor on
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
. Let
Q_{i}
be the defining homogeneous polynomial of
D_{i}
with degree
d_{i}
. Let
F:\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
be the endomorphism of degree
d=\lcm(d_{1},\dots,d_{n+1})
defined by

(4.1)

\displaystyle F(z):=\big[Q^{m_{1}}_{1}(z)\,\colon\,\dots\,\colon\,Q^{m_{n+1}}_{n+1}(z)\big],

where
m_{i}=\tfrac{d}{d_{i}}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant n+1
.
By construction,
F
maps
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})\setminus D
to
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
. The critical points of
F
consists of hypersurfaces
D_{i}
(if
m_{i}\geqslant 2
) and a hypersurface
\mathcal{V}
of degree
\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}d_{i}-(n+1)>0
defined by

M(z):=\det\dfrac{\partial(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{n+1})}{\partial(z_{0},\dots,z_{n})}=0.

Lemma 4.1
.

The hypersurface
\mathcal{V}
is in general position with
\{D_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+1}
, if and only if the hypersurfaces
\{D_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+1}
intersect transversally.

Proof.

The
if
part was proven in
[
GSW22
, Section 5]
. For the
only if
part, without loss of generality we may assume that there exists some
p\in D_{1}\cap\dots\cap D_{n}
such that

•

either
p
is a non-smooth point in some
D_{k}
,
1\leqslant k\leqslant n
, i.e.

\left(\frac{\partial Q_{k}}{\partial z_{0}}(p),\dots,\frac{\partial Q_{k}}{\partial z_{n}}(p)\right)=(0,\dots,0);

•

or the normal vectors
\{(\frac{\partial Q_{k}}{\partial z_{0}}(p),\dots,\frac{\partial Q_{k}}{\partial z_{n}}(p))\}_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}
of the tangent spaces are linearly dependent.

In both cases,

\rank\left(\frac{\partial Q_{i}}{\partial z_{j}}(p)\right)_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n,0\leqslant j\leqslant n}<n,

i.e. each
n
-minor of the
n\times(n+1)
matrix has determinant
0
.

The point
p
has a homogeneous representation
[p_{0}:\dots:p_{n}]
. There is some
s\in\{0,\dots,n\}
such that
p_{s}\neq 0
. Following Guo-Sun-Wang’s argument
[
GSW22
]
, by using the Euler formula

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial Q_{i}}{\partial z_{j}}z_{j}=d_{i}\cdot Q_{i},

we have

p_{s}\,M(p)=\det\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial Q_{1}}{\partial z_{0}}(p)&\dots&d_{1}\cdot Q_{1}(p)&\dots&\frac{\partial Q_{1}}{\partial z_{n}}(p)\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{\partial Q_{n+1}}{\partial z_{0}}(p)&\dots&d_{n+1}\cdot Q_{n+1}(p)&\dots&\frac{\partial Q_{n+1}}{\partial z_{n}}(p)\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Noting that
Q_{1}(p)=\dots=Q_{n}(p)=0
since
p\in D_{1}\cap\dots\cap D_{n}
. Hence

p_{s}\,M(p)=(-1)^{n+s}\,d_{n+1}\cdot Q_{n+1}(p)\,\det\left(\frac{\partial Q_{i}}{\partial z_{j}}(p)\right)_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n,0\leqslant j\leqslant n,j\neq s}=0.

Thus
M(p)=0
, i.e.
\mathcal{V}
and
\{D_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
intersect at
p
. We conclude that they are not in general position.∎

Set
g:=F\circ f
. The image

.
It is clear that

T_{g}(r)=O\big(T_{f}(r)\big).

As an illustrated example, one first looks at the case of projective plane and

is the union of three conics

Indeed, since

\ord_{z}g^{*}\mathcal{W}\geqslant\ord_{z}f^{*}\mathcal{V}
. Thus one only needs to exclude the possibility that

. In the simple case when
\ord_{z}g^{*}\mathcal{W}=\ord_{z}f^{*}\mathcal{V}=1
, this means exactly that

\mathcal{V}
as the critical set of

Proposition 4.2
.

There exists a proper subvariety
\mathcal{Z}
of
\mathcal{V}
such that for every

\ord_{z}f^{*}\mathcal{V}\leqslant\ord_{z}g^{*}\mathcal{W}-1.

Proof.

By our construction, the hypersurface
\mathcal{V}
is contained in the support of the ramification divisor of the endomorphism
F:\,\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
. Putting

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{V}\cap\mathrm{Supp}\,D,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{2}

\displaystyle=\Sing(\mathcal{V})\,\cup\,F^{-1}(\Sing(\mathcal{W})).

Let
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{Z}_{1}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{2}

p\in\mathcal{V}\setminus\mathcal{Z}

(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
about

about

\mathcal{V}=\{x_{1}=0\},\qquad\mathcal{W}=\{y_{1}=0\},

\mathcal{V}

the associated
m\geqslant 2
. Thus locally we have

. Consequently,

\mathrm{ord}_{z}g^{*}\mathcal{W}=\mathrm{ord}_{z}f^{*}(F^{*}\mathcal{W})=m\,\mathrm{ord}_{z}f^{*}\mathcal{V}=\mathrm{ord}_{z}f^{*}\mathcal{V}+(m-1)\,\mathrm{ord}_{z}f^{*}\mathcal{V}\geqslant\mathrm{ord}_{z}f^{*}\mathcal{V}+1

.

\mathcal{E}:=f^{-1}(D)

\hat{\sigma}
defined in the previous section. Take a smoothing
\sigma
of
\hat{\sigma}
as in the Appendix. Denote by
B^{\sigma}_{r}
and

on

, hence

(4.2)

\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma}(r)\leqslant N^{[1]}_{f}(r,D)+O(\mathrm{log}\,r)\qquad{\scriptstyle(r\,>\,1)}.

Suppose on the contrary that (

\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma}(r)}{T_{f}(r)}=0.

\displaystyle 0\leqslant T_{f}(r)-T_{\widetilde{f},\sigma}(r)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\tfrac{3}{2}}^{r}\frac{\dif t}{t}\int_{\Big(\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{D}(a_{j},\tfrac{3}{2}r_{j})\Big)\cap\mathbb{D}_{t}}f^{*}\omega+O(1)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{1}^{r}\frac{\dif t}{t}\int_{\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{D}(a_{j},\tfrac{3}{2}r_{j})}f^{*}\omega+O(1)

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{r}\frac{\dif t}{t}\Big(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{D}(a_{j},\tfrac{3}{2}r_{j})}f^{*}\omega\Big)+O(1),\qquad{\scriptstyle(r\,\geqslant\,1)}.

Recall (
3.1
) that the radius
r_{j}>0
can be chosen arbitrarily small. For our purpose, for each
j\geqslant 1
, we choose
r_{j}>0
sufficiently small so that
\int_{\mathbb{D}(a_{j},\tfrac{3}{2}r_{j})}f^{*}\omega<2^{-j}
. Hence the above estimate yields

0\leqslant T_{f}(r)-T_{\widetilde{f},\sigma}(r)\leqslant\mathrm{log}\,r+O(1),\qquad{\scriptstyle(r\,\geqslant\,1)}.

This together with (
4.3
) implies

\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma}(r)}{T_{\widetilde{f},\sigma}(r)}=0.

Hence the technical assumption (
2.2
) is satisfied, which allows us to use all of the obtained results in the parabolic setting. First, applying Theorem
2.6
, we receive

(4.4)

T_{\widetilde{f},\sigma}(r)\leqslant\dfrac{N_{\widetilde{f},\sigma}(r,\mathcal{V})}{\deg\mathcal{V}}+o\big(T_{\widetilde{f},\sigma}(r)\big)\,\,\parallel.

Next, using Corollary
2.2
for
\widetilde{g}
, we get

(4.5)

N_{\widetilde{g},\sigma}(r,\mathcal{W})-N^{[1]}_{\widetilde{g},\sigma}(r,\mathcal{W})=o\big(T_{\widetilde{g},\sigma}(r)\big)\,\,\parallel.

On the other hand, it follows from Theorem
2.1
and Proposition
4.2
that

(4.6)

N_{\widetilde{f},\sigma}(r,\mathcal{V})\leqslant N_{\widetilde{g},\sigma}(r,\mathcal{W})-N^{[1]}_{\widetilde{g},\sigma}(r,\mathcal{W})+o\big(T_{\widetilde{f},\sigma}(r)\big)\,\,\parallel.

Combining (
4.4
), (
4.5
), (
4.6
), one has

\displaystyle T_{\widetilde{f},\sigma}(r)

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{N_{\widetilde{f},\sigma}(r,\mathcal{V})}{\deg\mathcal{V}}+o\big(T_{\widetilde{f},\sigma}(r)\big)\,\,\parallel

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{N_{\widetilde{g},\sigma}(r,\mathcal{W})-N^{[1]}_{\widetilde{g},\sigma}(r,\mathcal{W})}{\deg\mathcal{V}}+o\big(T_{\widetilde{f},\sigma}(r)\big)\,\,\parallel

\displaystyle=o\big(T_{\widetilde{g},\sigma}(r)\big)+o\big(T_{\widetilde{f},\sigma}(r)\big)\,\,\parallel,

which is a contradiction. This finishes the proof of the Main Theorem.

Remark 4.3
.

In the case where
f\colon\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{C})
is an algebraically nondegenerate holomorphic curve and where
\mathcal{C}
is the collection of two lines and one conic in
\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{C})
,
in a private note, Noguchi obtained a Second Main Theorem of the form

T_{f}(r)\leqslant C\,N_{f}(r,\mathcal{C})+\big[N_{f}^{[2]}(r,\mathcal{V})-N_{f}^{[1]}(r,\mathcal{V})\big]+o\big(T_{f}(r)\big)\,\,\parallel,

where
\mathcal{V}
is the critical curve of the endomorphism defined as above, and
C>0
is some constant. Although the right hand side of the above inequality involves a quantity depending on
\mathcal{V}
(which actually counts the number of tangent points of
f
and
\mathcal{V}
), this term is negligible when
f
omits
\mathcal{C}
.

Remark 4.4
.

Our result can be extended to the case of entire holomorphic curves into algebraic varieties of log-general type
X
with
\overline{q}(X)=\dim X
by similar argument.

Appendix
A detailed construction of one smooth exhaustion function

We provide an explicit construction of
[
PS21
, pp. 32–33, Example (2)]
, precisely, a smooth exhaustion function
\sigma
on the parabolic surface
\mathcal{Y}:=\mathbb{C}\backslash\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
which satisfies Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Define

h(r):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0&\qquad{\scriptstyle(r\,\leqslant\,\tfrac{3}{4})},\\
&\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{1}{1+e^{\frac{r-1}{(r-1)^{2}-1/16}}}&\qquad{\scriptstyle(\tfrac{3}{4}\,<\,r\,<\,\tfrac{5}{4})},\\
&\frac{1}{4\pi}&\qquad{\scriptstyle(r\,\geqslant\,\tfrac{5}{4})}.\end{aligned}\right.

The function
h(r)
is bounded and agrees with
\frac{1}{4\pi}\mathds{1}_{r>1}
outside
[\tfrac{3}{4},\tfrac{5}{4}]
.

Figure 4.
Graph of
h(r)
and
\frac{1}{4\pi}\mathds{1}_{r>1}
.

By symmetry, for
r\geqslant\tfrac{5}{4}
, the integration

\int_{0}^{r}4\pi\,h(s)\dif s=\int_{0}^{r}\mathds{1}_{s>1}\dif s=r-1.

However, the integration

\int_{0}^{r}4\pi\,h(s)\frac{\dif s}{s}<\int_{0}^{r}\mathds{1}_{s>1}\frac{\dif s}{s}=\mathrm{log}\,r,

since
1/s
is strictly decreasing on
(0,+\infty)
. We need a small translation
c\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
to ensure that the primitive

H(r):=4\pi\int_{0}^{r}h\big(s-c)\big)\frac{\dif s}{s}

agrees with
\mathrm{log}\,^{+}r=\int_{0}^{r}\mathds{1}_{r>1}\frac{\dif s}{s}
outside
[\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{3}{2}]
.

Figure 5.
Graph of
H(r)
and
\mathrm{log}\,^{+}r
.

Thus we get a smoothing
H(r)
of
\mathrm{log}\,^{+}r
with

(.1)

\displaystyle 0\leqslant H(r)-\mathrm{log}\,^{+}r\leqslant\mathrm{log}\,^{+}\tfrac{3}{2}<\tfrac{1}{2}.

Together with the monotonicity of
H
, one has
H(r)=\mathrm{log}\,^{+}r
when
H(r)\geqslant\mathrm{log}\,\frac{3}{2}
.

Define

\tau:=H(|z|)+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}r_{j}\,H\big(|\tfrac{r_{j}}{z-a_{j}}|\big),\qquad\sigma:=\exp(\tau).

Then
\sigma\geqslant\hat{\sigma}
and the difference
\sigma-\hat{\sigma}
is supported on

\mathrm{Supp}(\sigma-\hat{\sigma})\subset U:=\big(A(0,\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{3}{2})\backslash\mathcal{E}\big)\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}A(a_{j},\tfrac{1}{2}r_{j},\tfrac{3}{2}r_{j}).

where
A(a_{j},\tfrac{1}{2}r_{j},\tfrac{3}{2}r_{j})
are pairwise disjoint.

For
z\notin\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\overline{D(a_{j},\frac{3}{2}r_{j}})
,
\mathrm{log}\,^{+}|\frac{r_{j}}{z-a_{j}}|=H(|\frac{r_{j}}{z-a_{j}}|)=0
for each
j
. Thus
\tau(z)=H(|z|)
and
\hat{\tau}(z)=\mathrm{log}\,^{+}|z|
, which are equal when
\tau(z)\geqslant\mathrm{log}\,\frac{3}{2}
. Taking exponential,
\sigma=\hat{\sigma}
when
\sigma\geqslant\frac{3}{2}
.

∎

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

In polar coordinates
z=a+r\,e^{i\theta}
for some
a\in\mathbb{C}
, for a smooth function
\phi
one has
[
NW14
, pp. 2]

\dif^{c}\phi=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left(r\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}\dif\theta-\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\theta}\dif r\right).

The smooth
2
-form
\dif\dif^{c}\mathrm{log}\,\sigma
is supported on
U
since
\mathrm{log}\,\sigma
is harmonic elsewhere. On the annulus
A(0,\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{3}{2})
, in polar coordinates
z=re^{i\theta}
one has

\dif\dif^{c}H(|z|)=\dif\left(\frac{r}{4\pi}\frac{\partial H(r)}{\partial r}\dif\theta\right)=\dif\left(h(r-c)\dif\theta\right)=O(1)\dif r\wedge\dif\theta=O(1)\,r\dif r\wedge\dif\theta

since
r\in[\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{3}{2}]
is bounded. Thus
\dif\dif^{c}H(|z|)
is of finite mass on
A(0,\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{3}{2})\backslash\mathcal{E}
.

On the annulus
A(a_{j},\tfrac{1}{2}r_{j},\tfrac{3}{2}r_{j})
, in polar coordinates
z=a_{j}+re^{i\theta}
one has

\displaystyle\dif\dif^{c}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}r_{j}\,H\big(|\tfrac{r_{j}}{z-a_{j}}|\big)

\displaystyle=\dif\left(\frac{r}{4\pi}\frac{\partial r_{j}\,H(\tfrac{r_{j}}{r})}{\partial r}\dif\theta\right)

\displaystyle=\dif\left(-r_{j}\,h(\tfrac{r_{j}}{r}-c)\dif\theta\right)=O(r_{j})\dif r\wedge\dif\theta=O(1)\,r\dif r\wedge\dif\theta.

Since
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}r_{j}^{2}\leqslant\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}r_{j}<+\infty
, the support
U
is of finite Lebesgue measure. We conclude that
\dif\dif^{c}\mathrm{log}\,\sigma
is of finite mass.
∎

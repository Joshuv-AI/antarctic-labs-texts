Nonhomogeneous associative Yang-Baxter equations Thanks: * Corresponding author
Tianshui Ma *, Jie Li

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of associative (BiHom-)Yang-Baxter pair of weight ( λ , γ ) (\lambda,\gamma) which can provide the solution to the double curved Rota-Baxter (BiHom-)system. Equivalent characterizations of (quasitriangular) covariant BiHom-bialgebra are given. We also prove that associative BiHom-Yang-Baxter equation of weight − 1 -1 can be obtained by the unitary quasitriangular covariant BiHom-bialgebra. At last, we present two approaches to construct (BiHom-)pre-Lie modules from Rota-Baxter (BiHom-)paired modules.

Introduction and preliminaries

The associative Yang-Baxter equation (aYBe)

Rota-Baxter operator of weight 0 can be regarded as an operator form of the cYBe. To study the representation of Rota-Baxter algebra, Zheng, Guo, Zhang introduced the notion of Rota-Baxter paired module and investigate the properties.

Recently, in

Definition 1.1
.

A
BiHom-associative algebra
is a 4-tuple
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
, where
A
is a linear space,
\alpha,\beta:A\longrightarrow A
and
\mu:A\otimes A\longrightarrow A
(write
\mu(a\otimes b)=ab
) are linear maps satisfying the following conditions:
\alpha\circ\beta=\beta\circ\alpha
and for all
a,b,c\in A
,

\displaystyle\alpha(ab)=\alpha(a)\alpha(b),\quad\beta(ab)=\beta(a)\beta(b),

(1.1)

\displaystyle\alpha(a)(bc)=(ab)\beta(c).

(1.2)

A BiHom-associative algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
is called
unitary
if there exists an element
1_{A}\in A
(called a unit) such that

\displaystyle\alpha(1_{A})=1_{A},\quad\beta(1_{A})=1_{A},\quad a1_{A}=\alpha(a),\quad 1_{A}a=\beta(a),\quad\forall a\in A.

(1.3)

Definition 1.2
.

A
BiHom-coassociative coalgebra
is a 4-tuple
(C,\Delta,\psi,\omega)
, in which
C
is a linear space,
\psi,\omega:C\longrightarrow C
and
\Delta:C\longrightarrow C\otimes C
are linear maps, such that
\psi\circ\omega=\omega\circ\psi
and

\displaystyle(\psi\otimes\psi)\circ\Delta=\Delta\circ\psi,~~(\omega\otimes\omega)\circ\Delta=\Delta\circ\omega,

(1.4)

\displaystyle(\Delta\otimes\psi)\circ\Delta=(\omega\otimes\Delta)\circ\Delta.

(1.5)

A BiHom-coassociative coalgebra
(C,\Delta,\psi,\omega)
is called
counitary
if there exists a linear map
\varepsilon:C\longrightarrow K
(called a counit) such that

\displaystyle\varepsilon\circ\psi=\varepsilon,\quad\varepsilon\circ\omega=\varepsilon,\quad({\rm id}_{C}\otimes\varepsilon)\circ\Delta=\omega,\quad(\varepsilon\otimes{\rm id}_{C})\circ\Delta=\psi.

(1.6)

Definition 1.3
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha_{A},\beta_{A})
be a BiHom-associative algebra. A
left
(A,\mu,\alpha_{A},\beta_{A})
-module
is a 4-tuple
(M,\triangleright,\alpha_{M},\beta_{M})
, where
M
is a linear space,
\alpha_{M},\beta_{M}:M\longrightarrow M
and
\triangleright:A\otimes M\longrightarrow M
are linear maps such that,
\alpha_{M}\circ\beta_{M}=\beta_{M}\circ\alpha_{M}
, and for all
a,b\in A,m\in M
,

\displaystyle\alpha_{M}(a\triangleright m)=\alpha_{A}(a)\triangleright\alpha_{M}(m),~~\beta_{M}(a\triangleright m)=\beta_{A}(a)\triangleright\beta_{M}(m),

(1.7)

\displaystyle\alpha_{A}(a)\triangleright(b\triangleright m)=(ab)\triangleright\beta_{M}(m).

(1.8)

Likewise, we can get the right version of
(A,\mu,\alpha_{A},\beta_{A})
-module.

If
(M,\triangleright,\alpha_{M},\beta_{M})
is a left
(A,\mu,\alpha_{A},\beta_{A})
-module and at the same time
(M,\triangleleft,\alpha_{M},\beta_{M})
is right
(A,\mu,\alpha_{A},\beta_{A})
-module, then
(M,\triangleright,\triangleleft,\alpha_{M},\beta_{M})
is an
(A,\mu,\alpha_{A},\beta_{A})
-bimodule
if

\displaystyle\alpha_{A}(a)\triangleright(m\triangleleft b)=(a\triangleright m)\triangleleft\beta_{A}(b).

(1.9)

Remark 1.4
.

When the structure maps
\alpha=\beta={\rm id}
in Definitions
1.1
-
1.3

2.1.
Nonhomogeneous abhYBp

\displaystyle r_{23}s_{13}=\beta\omega(s^{1})\otimes\alpha(r^{1})\otimes r^{2}\psi(s^{2}).

An

\displaystyle r_{13}r_{12}-r_{12}r_{23}+r_{23}r_{13}=-\lambda r_{13}

\lambda

2.2.
Double curved Rota-Baxter BiHom-system

As a generalization of Rota-Baxter algebra, Rota-Baxter system was introduced by Brzeziński in

Definition 2.3

\displaystyle R(a)R(b)=R(R(a)b)+R(aS(b))+\xi(a\otimes b),

\displaystyle S(a)S(b)=S(R(a)b)+S(aS(b))+\zeta(a\otimes b).

(2.6)

2.3.
Double curved weak BiHom-pseudotwistor

The notions of a twistor and more generally a pseudotwistor were introduced by López Peña, Panaite and Van Oystaeyen
[
21
]
to provide a framework unifying some of the deformed or twisted algebras. The more general concept of a weak pseudotwistor was introduced by Panaite and Van Oystaeyen in
[

, then the curved version was studied in
[

. Here we provide the double curved (BiHom-)version.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
be a BiHom-associative algebra. A
K

is called a
double curved weak BiHom-pseudotwistor
if there exist
K
-linear maps
\mathfrak{T}:A\otimes A\otimes A\longrightarrow A\otimes A\otimes A
and
\xi,\zeta:A\otimes A\longrightarrow A
, such that the following conditions hold

\displaystyle\mu(\alpha\otimes\zeta)=\mu(\xi\otimes\beta),

(2.22)

\displaystyle T(\alpha\otimes\mu T)=(\alpha\otimes\mu)\mathfrak{T}-\alpha\otimes\zeta,

(2.23)

\displaystyle T(\mu T\otimes\beta)=(\mu\otimes\beta)\mathfrak{T}-\xi\otimes\beta.

(2.24)

The map
\mathfrak{T}
is called a
weak BiHom-companion of

T
and
\xi,\zeta
are called
curvatures of

T
.

Remark 2.13
.

Let
\xi=\zeta
in Definition

, then

is called a

. And if furthermore
\alpha=\beta={\rm id}
, then we can obtain curved weak pseudotwistor introduced in
[

, Definition 2.2]
.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
be a BiHom-associative algebra and

a double curved weak BiHom-pseudotwistor with companion
\mathfrak{T}
and curvatures
\xi,\zeta
. Then

Proof.

We calculate BiHom-associativity as follows:

\displaystyle\mu T(\alpha\otimes\mu T)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:8.3})}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\mu(\alpha\otimes\mu)\mathfrak{T}-\mu(\alpha\otimes\zeta)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:8.2})(\ref{eq:1.3})}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\mu(\mu\otimes\beta)\mathfrak{T}-\mu(\xi\otimes\beta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:8.4})}}{{=}}\mu T(\mu T\otimes\beta),

as required.
∎

Let

For all

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:8.6})}}{{=}}

Lemma 3.1

(
[
25

Let us recall from
[

the following definition: A
BiHom-dendriform algebra

\prec,\succ:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
and two commuting linear maps
\alpha,\beta:A\rightarrow A

\displaystyle\alpha(a\prec b)=\alpha(a)\prec\alpha(b),\quad\alpha(a\succ b)=\alpha(a)\succ\alpha(b),

\displaystyle(a\prec b)\prec\beta(c)=\alpha(a)\prec(b\prec c)+\alpha(a)\prec(b\succ c),

\displaystyle\alpha(a)\succ(b\succ c)=(a\prec b)\succ\beta(c)+(a\succ b)\succ\beta(c).

(A,\prec,\succ,\alpha,\beta)

(
[
25
]

\lambda

Singularities of Functions: a Global Point of View
Claude Sabbah

Abstract
This text surveys cohomological properties of pairs ( U , f ) (U,f) consisting of a smooth complex quasi-projective variety U U together with a regular function on it. On the one hand, one tries to mimic the case of a germ of holomorphic function in its Milnor ball and, on the other hand, one takes advantage of the algebraicity of U U and f f to apply technique of algebraic geometry, in particular Hodge theory. The monodromy properties are expressed by means of tools provided by the theory of linear differential equations, by mimicking the Stokes phenomenon. In the case of tame functions on smooth affine varieties, which is an algebraic analogue of that of a holomorphic function with an isolated critical point, the theory simplifies much and the formulation of the results are nicer. Examples of such tame functions are exhibited.

5.1
Introduction

Milnor’s theory of singularities of complex hypersurfaces
Milnor68
focuses on the properties of a germ of a holomorphic function
f:(\mathbb{C}^{d},0)\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}(\mathbb{C},0)
at a critical point
0
, which produces a singularity of the germ of hypersurface
(f^{-1}(0),0)
. One realizes the germ
f
as a holomorphic function defined on some neighborhood of
0
, usually taken as an open ball (a Milnor ball) in some local coordinate system, and the properties considered do not depend on the radius of the ball, provided it is “sufficiently small” (a condition that can be made precise in terms of a Thom-Whitney stratification of this neighborhood). See
L-NB-S20
for details. A more global aspect of the theory arises when considering unfoldings of a germ
(f,0)
: having fixed a Milnor ball for a germ
(f,0)
, this ball is usually not a Milnor ball for the nearby functions of the unfolding (see
Ebeling20
). Nevertheless, some “smallness” properties remain valid.

In this text, we consider the global setting of a regular function
f:U\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
on a smooth complex quasi-projective variety
U
of dimension
d
. Particular cases are those for which
U
is affine: e.g.
U
is the affine space
\mathbb{A}^{\!d}
, the complex torus
(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}})^{d}
(where
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
is the multiplicative group
\mathbb{A}^{\!1}\smallsetminus\{0\}
), a product of those, or the complement of a hypersurface in these examples, like the complement of an arrangement of hyperplanes in an affine space.

Comparing with Milnor’s situation, we can say in a figurative way that the singularity of
f
that is of interest for us is “the point at infinity on
U
” and that its limit Milnor ball is
U
itself. This approach suggests to analyze the map
f
“from inside”, that is, by means of a family of balls (for a suitable metric) of larger and larger radius. Many choices of such metrics exist however. On the other hand, in order to analyze the map
f
“from outside”, one can “fill the hole at infinity” by choosing a projectivization of
f
, that is, a complex quasi-projective variety
X
together with a projective morphism
f_{X}:X\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
, such that
U
is Zariski dense in
X
and
f
is the restriction of
f_{X}
to
U
. Such a projectivization is also far from unique.

A vast literature is devoted to the analysis of the critical points at infinity and their influence (or the influence of their absence) on the topology of the map
f
, particularly when
f
is a polynomial mapping. Furthermore, several examples have highlighted unexpected behaviors. As it is impossible to list all papers related to this subject, we refer the reader to
A-L-M00b
;
Tibar07
and the references therein.

In the case of an
isolated critical point
of a germ of holomorphic function, the following (co)homological properties have been obtained:

1.

The reduced cohomology (with
\mathbb{Z}
-coefficients) of the Milnor fiber is non zero only in degree
d-1
; it is a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module whose rank
\mu
is the
Milnor number
of
f
at its critical point; it cannot be zero at a critical point.

2.

This cohomology comes with an automorphism
T
(monodromy), with an intersection pairing and a Seifert pairing, the latter being nondegenerate.

3.

The corresponding homology groups are either the reduced homology of the Milnor fiber in degree
d-1
(vanishing cycles) or the homology of the Milnor ball relative to the Milnor fiber (Lefschetz thimbles). The topology that gives rise to these homological objects is well understood, including in its symplectic aspects. The relations between the objects considered above, and others, are efficiently summarized in
Hertling05
.

4.

The cohomology of the Milnor fiber underlies a
\mathbb{Z}
-mixed Hodge structure with weight filtration determined by the nilpotent part of the monodromy (see
Steenbrink22
). This structure provides us with numerical invariants: the spectrum and the spectral pairs.

5.

These Hodge invariants can also be recovered by means of the motivic Milnor fiber introduced by Denef and Loeser (see
D-L01
and also
Steenbrink22
).

6.

A major object that comes into play in the analytic theory for computing Hodge invariants is the
Brieskorn lattice
introduced by Brieskorn
Brieskorn70
in order to obtain an algebraic expression of some of the topological invariants above. There is also a natural nondegenerate pairing on the Brieskorn lattice, with values in the ring of formal power series of one variable, called the higher residue pairing, introduced by K. Saito
KSaito83
(see
Pham85b
and
Hertling05
for the relations with the topological pairings).

In the case of a regular function
f\in\mathcal{O}(U)\smallsetminus\mathbb{C}
, having isolated critical points is not a sufficient condition to realize similar properties. One should also impose the absence of critical points at infinity: this is the
tameness property
that we will discuss with details in Section
5.5
. We aim at defining a vanishing cycle
\mathbb{Z}
-module that shares the properties above. Supplementary data occur, describing the relations between the various isolated critical points of
f
: the monodromy of the “vanishing cohomology” is enhanced with a supplementary set of data, called the
Stokes matrices
. Such data already show up in the semi-local situation mentioned at the beginning. The term “Stokes matrices” is justified by the fact that such matrices are Stokes matrices of a suitable linear system of differential equations of one variable with an irregular singularity. This was already observed by F. Pham, with the title of his paper
Pham85b
: ‘‘La descente des cols par les onglets de Lefschetz avec vues sur Gauss-Manin’’.

The general case of a possibly non tame regular function (with non-isolated critical points), while not being as simple as the tame case, shows many interesting properties. One cannot expect the absence of
\mathbb{Z}
-torsion in the various cohomology groups that occur, so we mainly consider the case of rational or complex coefficients. The exponentially twisted de Rham complex plays an important role for obtaining Hodge-theoretic properties. Another approach, in the spirit of the motivic arc-space approach of Denef-Loeser, has been developed by Raibaut in
Raibaut12
: the focus is on the motivic Milnor fiber of
f
at
\infty
and the various invariants that one can deduce from the computation of this object

In contrast with the case of the germs of holomorphic functions, the case of pairs
(U,f)
that we are considering has strong relations to several other areas of algebraic geometry and arithmetic that we will very briefly mention in this text. On the one hand, the theory of exponential Nori motives, developed in

by Fresán and Jossen, makes it possible, when

and

are defined over a subfield

of

, to relate Hodge properties of
(U,f)
, in particular such as those defined in Section

, to arithmetic properties of

.

On the other hand, mirror symmetry for Fano manifolds aims at associating to any smooth quasi-projective Fano manifold a Landau-Ginzburg model, that is realized as a regular function on another quasi-projective manifold. The theory has been developed in
K-K-P14
, where various conjectures have been settled. In the version of this correspondence involving the Frobenius manifolds introduced by Dubrovin (
Dubrovin96
), the role of the irregular Hodge structure produced by the Brieskorn lattice is essential (see for example
D-S02a
;
D-S02b
;
Douai07
). There is also a categorical approach to such a correspondence (see
K-K-P08
), that has motivated a categorical realization of the twisted de Rham complex, as proposed by Shklyarov in
Shklyarov17
.

5.2
Topological/cohomological properties

In this text, we consider a regular function
f:U\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
from a connected smooth complex quasi-projective variety
U
of dimension
d
to the affine line
\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
. This set of data is also denoted by
f\in\mathcal{O}(U)
. Let us emphasize that, unless otherwise stated, we work with algebraic varieties endowed with their Zariski topology and, for example, we will denote by
U^{\mathrm{an}}
the underlying complex manifold. Some results below do not need the smoothness assumption on
U
to hold, but we will not try to state them in the most general setting.

The critical set of
f
is a Zariski closed subset of
U
whose image by
f
is a finite subset
C=C(f)
of
\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
(by Sard’s lemma in the algebraic context), called the set of critical values of
f
. A fortiori, the restriction
f:U\smallsetminus f^{-1}(C)\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}^{\!1}\smallsetminus C
does not have any critical point.

A
projectivization
of
f
is a
projective morphism

f_{X}
from a (possibly not smooth) quasi-projective variety
X
to
\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
such that
U
is a Zariski dense open subset of
X
and
f
is the restriction of
f_{X}
to
U
. Assume
U
is a locally closed subspace of some projective space
\mathbb{P}^{N}
. A natural projectivization of
f
is obtained by considering the Zariski closure
X
in
\mathbb{P}^{N}\times\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
of the graph
\Gamma(f)\subset U\times\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
and by defining
f_{X}
as induced by the second projection. By resolving singularities of
X
(they lie in
X\smallsetminus U
), we can choose, if needed,
X
to be smooth and also
X\smallsetminus U
to be a divisor with normal crossings.

5.2.1
The fibration theorem

Theorem 5.2.1

There exists a smallest finite set
B(f)\subset\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
, called the
bifurcation set of
f
, such that the map
f^{\mathrm{an}}:(U\smallsetminus f^{-1}(B(f)))^{\mathrm{an}}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{C}\smallsetminus B(f)
is a
C^{\infty}
fibration.

Proof
(Sketch)

One chooses a projectivization
f_{X}:X\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
of
f
. A theorem of
Hironaka

Hironaka77
(see also
(
L-NB-S20
, Th. 6.4.5)
) asserts that the underlying complex analytic space
X^{\mathrm{an}}
admits a Whitney stratification satisfying Thom’s
A_{f_{X}}
condition. In fact, in the quasi-projective context, the strata can be chosen quasi-projective and connected, so that the
f_{X}
-image of a stratum is either a point or a Zariski dense open set of
\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
. The construction can be made in order to ensure that
U\smallsetminus f^{-1}(B)
is a stratum, for some finite set
B
of
\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
. Thom’s first isotopy lemma implies that the restriction of
f_{X}
to each stratum
S
induces a
C^{\infty}
fibration
S^{\mathrm{an}}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}f_{X}(S)^{\mathrm{an}}
. Applying this to the stratum
U\smallsetminus f^{-1}(B)
yields the assertion for some (possibly non minimal) finite set
B
. Minimality can then obviously be achieved.

The set
B(f)
contains the set
C=C(f)
of critical values of
f
: otherwise, for some
c\in C
, the singular fiber
f^{-1}(c)^{\mathrm{an}}
would be a
C^{\infty}
manifold; this is not possible, according to
(
Milnor68
, Rem. p. 13)
.

The set
B(f)\smallsetminus C
consists of
atypical critical values of
f
. It is in general not empty. For example (see
(
A-L-M00b
, Ex. 3.4)
), the polynomial
f:\mathbb{A}^{\!3}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
defined by
f(x,y,z)=x(1+xyz)
has no critical point, but
B(f)\neq\varnothing
.

Determining
C
from
f
is an algebraic computation. On the other hand, determining
B(f)
is much harder. A topological criterion has been provided in
H-L84
for
f:\mathbb{A}^{\!3}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
in terms of the topological Euler characteristic of the fibers of
f
. Other results in this direction can be found in
A-L-M00b
and the references therein.

On the other hand, we will focus on the case where
B(f)=C
and, more precisely, where
f_{X}
has ‘‘no critical point at infinity’’, called the
tameness property
, in Section
5.5
.

5.2.2
Cohomological tools

We aim at defining a space of global vanishing cycles for a regular function
f:U\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
. For that purpose, we will make use of some of the tools explained in
M-S22
(a good reference is also
Dimca04
, and the reference book is
K-S90
), that we recall here for convenience.
In this section, the varieties are equipped with their analytic topology, and we will omit the exponent
\mathrm{an}
for the sake of simplicity.

Algebraically constructible complexes

Our base ring
A
is either
\mathbb{Z}
(a PID) or
\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}
(a field). Given a complex quasi-projective variety
Y
(which is possibly singular), an
algebraically constructible sheaf of
A
-modules
on
Y
is a sheaf
\mathcal{F}
of
A
-modules on
Y
for which there exists a stratification of
Y
by locally closed connected smooth quasi-projective subvarieties
(Y_{a})_{a\in\mathcal{A}}
such that, for each
a\in\mathcal{A}
, the sheaf
\mathcal{F}|_{Y_{a}}
is a locally constant sheaf of
A
-modules of finite type. We implicitly assume (see e.g.
Trotman20
) that a stratification is locally finite and satisfies the frontier condition. A priori, it is not assumed to satisfy any regularity condition, but one can always refine it to do so. These sheaves form a category for which the morphisms are all morphisms of sheaves (it is a
full
subcategory of the category of sheaves of
A
-modules). This category is
abelian
: this statement amounts to the property that the kernel and cokernel of a sheaf morphism between locally constant sheaves of
A
-modules of finite type are of the same type.

One can then form the bounded derived category of this abelian category, which is equivalent to the full subcategory
\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{C}\textup{-c}}(Y,A)
of the bounded derived category of sheaves of
A
-modules whose objects consists of bounded complexes having constructible cohomology.

Given a morphism
f:Y\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}Y^{\prime}
between quasi-projective varieties, the derived pushforward functors
\boldsymbol{R}f_{*}
and
\boldsymbol{R}f_{!}
(pushforward with proper support) are defined from
\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{C}\textup{-c}}(Y,A)
to
\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{C}\textup{-c}}(Y^{\prime},A)
as well as the pullback functors
f^{-1}
and
f^{!}
from
\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{C}\textup{-c}}(Y^{\prime},A)
to
\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{C}\textup{-c}}(Y,A)
. Poincaré-Verdier duality
\boldsymbol{D}
is a contravariant equivalence of categories from
\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{C}\textup{-c}}(Y,A)
to itself, and is its own quasi-inverse, that is, it satisfies
\boldsymbol{D}\circ\boldsymbol{D}\simeq\id
.

Perverse and strongly perverse complexes

The category
\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{C}\textup{-c}}(Y,A)
is naturally equipped with a t-structure

(\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b},\leqslant 0}_{\mathbb{C}\textup{-c}}(Y,A),\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b},\geqslant 0}_{\mathbb{C}\textup{-c}}(Y,A)).

The heart of this t-structure is the abelian category of perverse “sheaves”
\Perv(Y,A)
(more accurately, perverse complexes). For example, if
Y
is smooth of dimension
n
, the shifted constant sheaf

{}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{p}}\!A_{Y}:=A_{Y}[n]

belongs to
\Perv(Y,A)
.

If
A
is a field, both terms of the t-structure are exchanged by Poincaré-Verdier duality
\boldsymbol{D}
, hence
\Perv(Y,A)
is preserved by
\boldsymbol{D}
. If
A
is a PID, this property may not hold, and we are led to considering
strongly
perverse complexes (see
(
M-S22
, Def. 10.2.50)
). We denote by
\sPerv(Y,A)
the corresponding full subcategory of
\Perv(Y,A)
, which is preserved by Poincaré-Verdier duality

(it is equal to
\Perv(Y,A)
if

is a field). Be careful that it is not abelian, as for example the cokernel of a group morphism
\mathbb{Z}^{p}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{Z}^{q}
may acquire
\mathbb{Z}
-torsion.

Compatibility with pushforward

A technique to obtain cohomological information about the map
f:U\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
is to analyze the pushforward complexes
\boldsymbol{R}f_{*}A_{U}
(ordinary pushforward) and
\boldsymbol{R}f_{!}A_{U}
(pushforward with proper support), which are both algebraically constructible, that is, both are objects of
\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{C}\textup{-c}}(A_{\mathbb{A}^{\!1}})
. This is especially useful for analyzing global properties of nearby or vanishing cycles of
f
along a fiber
f^{-1}(c)
. We know (see
(
M-S22
, Prop. 10.4.19)
) that the functors
\psi_{f-c},\phi_{f-c}
do commute with
\boldsymbol{R}f_{*},\boldsymbol{R}f_{!}
when
f
is
proper
(so that the latter functors are the same), but in general not otherwise. More precisely, let
f_{X}:X\mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}}\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
be a projectivization of
f
and let
j:U\mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}}X
denote the open inclusion. Since derived pushforwards compose well, we have (the second isomorphism since
f_{X}
is proper)

\boldsymbol{R}f_{*}A_{U}\simeq\boldsymbol{R}f_{X*}(\boldsymbol{R}j_{*}A_{U}),\quad\boldsymbol{R}f_{!}A_{U}\simeq\boldsymbol{R}f_{X*}(\boldsymbol{R}j_{!}A_{U}).

It follows that, considering vanishing cycles for example, we have

\phi_{t-c}(\boldsymbol{R}f_{*}A_{U})\simeq\boldsymbol{R}f_{X*}(\phi_{f_{X}-c}(\boldsymbol{R}j_{*}A_{U}))

5.3
Cohomologies attached to a pair
(U,f)

A possible approach to associate with a regular function
f\in\mathcal{O}(U)
on a smooth quasi-projective variety
U
some (co)homological invariants is to extend those existing for
U
itself, corresponding to the zero function on
U
.

On the topological side, to
U^{\mathrm{an}}
is associated the singular homology and the singular homology with closed supports (Borel-Moore homology), as well as the singular cohomology and the singular cohomology with compact support (all with coefficients in
A
). When
A=\mathbb{Q}
, various nondegenerate pairings relate these finite dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces: intersection of cycles, integral of cohomology classes on cycles and cup product of cohomology classes.

On the de Rham side, the algebraic de Rham complex of
U
leads to de Rham cohomology and de Rham cohomology with compact support. Grothendieck’s comparison theorem identifies these complex vector spaces with their topological analogues with complex coefficients. If the variety
U
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
(i.e., by means of equations with rational coefficients), the de Rham complex is also defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, as well as its cohomologies, so that the comparison isomorphism can be understood as comparing two different
\mathbb{Q}
-structures on the same
\mathbb{C}
-vectors space. Its matrix in
\mathbb{Q}
-bases, usually consisting of transcendental numbers, is called the
period matrix
of
U
and can be realized by integrating a basis of rational differential forms over a basis of cycles.

On the other hand, the de Rham cohomology can be filtered by
\mathbb{C}
-subspaces in order to produce, together with the singular cohomology, a mixed Hodge structure (see
DeligneHII
).

A unifying approach to these properties is obtained via the notion of motive, in particular that of Nori motive (see
H-MS17
).

Our aim in this section is to define similar homology and cohomology spaces, denoted generically by
\mathrm{H}(U,f)
, with lower or upper decorations meaning “Borel-Moore”, “compact support”, “de Rham”, together with the various comparison isomorphisms and pairings, and with coefficients in
A
for singular cohomology and
\mathbb{C}
for de Rham cohomology. A new set of decorations occurs, namely,
rapid decay
and
moderate growth
. We will also equip the de Rham version with a pair of filtrations called the
irregular Hodge filtration
and the weight filtration. Although this pair of filtrations does not form a mixed Hodge structure in the usual sense, in particular because the irregular Hodge filtration is indexed by rational numbers, this structure can be called an
irregular mixed Hodge structure
, and the pairs consisting of the jumping indices of both filtrations are reminiscent of the spectral pairs occurring in the theory of isolated hypersurface singularities (see
(
Steenbrink22
, §9.8)
and the reference therein). In particular, a Thom-Sebastiani property holds in this context: given regular functions
f\in\mathcal{O}(U)
and
g\in\mathcal{O}(V)
, this property relates the invariants of
f\boxplus g\in\mathcal{O}(U\times V)
with those of
f
and
g
(see
(
Bibi15
, §3.4)
).

Some examples

The
Thom-Sebastiani property
allows for simple computations: set for example
U=\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{d}
and
f=x_{1}+\cdots+x_{d}
; then
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{d},f)=0
for
r\neq d
and
\dim\mathrm{H}^{d}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{d},f)=1
with irregular Hodge filtration jumping at
d
only (see
(
F-S-Y18
, (A.23))
).

On the other hand, it can happen that the jumping indices of the irregular Hodge filtration of
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{\mathrm{dR}}(U,f)
are integers and, even more, that
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{\mathrm{dR}}(U,f)
is isomorphic to the cohomology of some algebraic variety in such a way that the irregular Hodge filtration of
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{\mathrm{dR}}(U,f)
corresponds to that of the canonical mixed Hodge structure on the latter cohomology. In such a case, the computation of the irregular Hodge numbers can be easier than a direct computation of the Hodge numbers of this mixed Hodge structure.

For example, if
U=\mathbb{A}^{\!1}_{t}\times V
and
f=t\cdot g
, where
g:V\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
is a regular function, then
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{{\mathrm{dR}},\mathrm{c}}(U,f)\simeq\mathrm{H}^{r-2}_{{\mathrm{dR}},\mathrm{c}}(g^{-1}(0))
and the irregular Hodge filtration of the left-hand side corresponds to the Hodge filtration of the mixed Hodge structure on the right-hand side Tate-twisted by
-1
(see
(
F-S-Y18
, Ex. A.27)
).

If
g
is defined by means of
Thom-Sebastiani sums
, the computation can be simplified. Let us mention one remarkable computation obtained in this way by Y. Qin
Qin23
, extending that made in
F-S-Y18
.

Let
k
be an integer
\geqslant 1
and let us consider the case of the
k
-fold Thom-Sebastiani sum
g_{k}:V^{k}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
of a regular function
g:V\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
—i.e., for
(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in V
,
g_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}g(x_{i})
—and the corresponding hypersurface

\mathcal{H}_{k}(g)=\{g_{k}=0\}\subset V^{k}.

The symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{k}
acts on
V^{k}
and preserves
\mathcal{H}_{k}(g)
, and we are interested in computing the Hodge numbers of the isotypical component
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{H}_{k}(g),g_{k})^{\sigma}
with respect to the signature character
\sigma
of
\mathfrak{S}_{k}
.

Y. Qin considered, as in
F-S-Y18
, the case of the function

\displaystyle g:\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{d}

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}^{\!1}

\displaystyle(y_{1},\dots,y_{d})

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\longmapsto}{\mapsto}{\mapsto}{\mapsto}y_{1}+\cdots+y_{d}+\frac{1}{y_{1}\cdots y_{d}}.

In Arithmetic, the function
g
is at the source of the
generalized Kloosterman sums
, and in Mirror symmetry it plays the role of the
mirror of
\mathbb{P}^{d}
. There is a supplementary action of the group
\mu_{2}=\{\pm 1\}
on
\mathcal{H}_{k}(g)
induced by
(y_{1},\dots,y_{d})\mathchoice{\longmapsto}{\mapsto}{\mapsto}{\mapsto}\pm(y_{1},\dots,y_{d})
, and we are interested in the invariant part with respect to this action. We denote by
\chi:\mathfrak{S}_{k}\times\mu_{2}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\{\pm 1\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{*}
the corresponding character
\sigma\cdot\id
. Y. Qin has given a closed formula for the Hodge numbers of the
(dk+1)
-pure part of
\mathrm{H}^{dk-1}_{\mathrm{c}}\bigl(\mathcal{H}_{k}(g)\bigr)^{\chi}(-1)
, which extends one obtained in
F-S-Y18
when

, both proved by means of computation of irregular Hodge numbers.

Theorem 5.3.7
(Y. Qin
Qin23
)

Assume that
\gcd(k,d+1)=1
. Then the Hodge numbers
h^{p,dk+1-p}
of the Tate-twisted Hodge structure
\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{dk+1}\bigl[\mathrm{H}^{dk-1}_{\mathrm{c}}\bigl(\mathcal{H}_{k}(g)\bigr)^{\chi}(-1)\bigr]
are, for

, the coefficients of

in the power series expansion of

\frac{(1-t)t^{d+1}}{(1-t^{d+1})(1-x)(1-tx)\cdots(1-t^{d}x)},

and for

h^{p,dk+1-p}=h^{dk+1-p,p}
.

The weight filtration

On the de Rham cohomology spaces
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{{\mathrm{dR}}}(U)
, the weight filtration is induced by a filtration of the de Rham complexes: for a logarithmic form (of some degree), the weight is the number of components of
D
along which the form as a pole. On the other hand, the weight filtration on
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{{\mathrm{dR}}}(U,f)
cannot be defined as simply as in the case where
f=0
since the pole set
P
disturbs the notion of pole of a logarithmic form. We define it in a different way, that can also be used in the case where
f=0
. As in the topological setting, we make use of the theory of mixed Hodge modules
MSaito87
and we compute
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{{\mathrm{dR}}}(U,f)
and
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{{\mathrm{dR}},\mathrm{c}}(U,f)
by means of the de Rham complex of the regular holonomic
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{A}^{\!1}}
-modules
M
corresponding to the perverse complexes
\raisebox{6.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{p}$}\!R^{r-d}f_{*}(\raisebox{6.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{p}$}\mathbb{Q}_{U})
and
\raisebox{6.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{p}$}\!R^{r-d}f_{!}(\raisebox{6.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{p}$}\mathbb{Q}_{U})
respectively. Given such a module
M
, that we regard as a quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{U}
-module with a connection
\nabla
, one shows that the twisted de Rham complex

M\mathchoice{\xrightarrow{\textstyle\kern 4.0pt\nabla+\mathrm{d}t\kern 3.0pt}}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nabla+\mathrm{d}t}}{{\longrightarrow}}}{}{}\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{A}^{\!1}}\otimes M

has nonzero cohomology in degree
1
only, giving rise to an exact functor

M\mathchoice{\longmapsto}{\mapsto}{\mapsto}{\mapsto}\mathrm{H}^{1}_{{\mathrm{dR}}}\bigl(\mathbb{A}^{\!1},(\Omega_{\mathbb{A}^{\!1}}^{\raisebox{1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}}\otimes M,\nabla+\mathrm{d}t)\bigr)

from regular holonomic
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{A}^{\!1}}
-modules to
\mathbb{C}
-vector spaces. When
M
underlies a mixed Hodge module, it is endowed with a weight filtration
W_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}M
by regular holonomic submodules, and we set

W_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}\mathrm{H}^{1}_{{\mathrm{dR}}}\bigl(\mathbb{A}^{\!1},(\Omega_{\mathbb{A}^{\!1}}^{\raisebox{1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}}\otimes M,\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}t)\bigr)=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{{\mathrm{dR}}}\bigl(\mathbb{A}^{\!1},(\Omega_{\mathbb{A}^{\!1}}^{\raisebox{1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}}\otimes W_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}M,\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}t)\bigr),

in a way similar to the topological case. In this way, we obtain a weight filtration for
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{{\mathrm{dR}}}(U,f)
and
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{{\mathrm{dR}},\mathrm{c}}(U,f)
.
Let us emphasize that, in general, contrary to the example above, the pair
(F_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\raisebox{1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}},W_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet})
does not form a mixed Hodge structure of
\mathrm{H}^{r}(U,f)
or
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{{\mathrm{dR}},\mathrm{c}}(U,f)
.

5.4
Monodromy properties of a pair
(U,f)

While the purpose of Section
5.3
was to extend as much as possible cohomological properties of
U
(i.e., pairs,
(U,0)
) to such properties for any pair
(U,f)
, we consider in this section properties that do not exist (i.e., are trivial) when

f
above a typical or atypical critical value, i.e., any point of the bifurcation set

(see Theorem
5.2.1
), and it also may occur around the “fiber at infinity”. However, mimicking now the local properties of critical points, we aim at defining a
global vanishing space with monodromy
that takes into account all critical values at the same time, including
\infty
, so that it is not just the direct sum over all (typical or atypical) critical values of
f
at finite distance. Furthermore, when considering complex coefficients, we will produce an algebraic formula for these vanishing cycles, similar in spirit to the expression discovered by Brieskorn in the case of an isolated singularity of hypersurface, in terms of the
Gauss-Manin system
and the
Brieskorn lattice
. This algebraic formula is obtained via the consideration of the
twisted de Rham complex with parameter
.

we can glue

Theorem 5.4.7

For each

It follows that the irregular Hodge filtration can be recovered from the Brieskorn-Deligne bundle as its Harder-Narasimhan filtration.

Behavior on
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}

One checks, in a way analogous to that of Theorem

\mathcal{K}^{r}_{\beta}|_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathcal{K}^{r}_{\alpha}|_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{r}(U,f/u)

\beta\leqslant\alpha\in\mathcal{A}

We can thus regard the irregular Hodge filtration as indexed by
-\mathcal{A}+\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{Q}
by setting, for
\mathsf{p}=p-\alpha\in-\mathcal{A}+\mathbb{Z}

Proposition 13

The irregular Hodge filtration
F_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathsf{p}}\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{r}(U,f/u)
is a filtration by subbundles, that is, each
\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1}]

\mathrm{gr}_{F_{\mathrm{irr}}}^{\mathsf{p}}\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{r}(U,f/u):=F_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathsf{p}}\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{r}(U,f/u)/F_{\mathrm{irr}}^{>\mathsf{p}}\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{r}(U,f/u)

{>}\mathsf{p}

\mathsf{p}

Proof

From Proposition
12

\alpha\in\mathcal{A}

is a filtration by subbundles. For any

i_{u_{o}}:\{u_{o}\}\mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}}\penalty\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}

Limiting properties

At the limit
u\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\infty
(i.e.,
v\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}0

\nabla

on the generalized eigenspace corresponding the eigenvalue

On the other hand, the limit at

The spectrum at infinity

An important Hodge invariant of a germ of holomorphic function is its spectrum, which is a set of pairs consisting of a rational number and a multiplicity, that one can encode by a polynomial with rational exponents and integral coefficients (see e.g.
(
Steenbrink22
, §9.7)
). We will provide a similar invariant for regular functions
f:U\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
and we will relate it with the irregular Hodge filtration.

The construction of
\mathcal{H}^{r}_{\alpha}
can be extended with indices
\alpha+q\in\mathcal{A}+\mathbb{Z}
: we set

\mathcal{H}^{r}_{\alpha+q}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-q\cdot\{0\})\otimes\mathcal{H}^{r}_{\alpha}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-q)\otimes\mathcal{H}^{r}_{\alpha}.

In other words,
\mathcal{H}^{r}_{\alpha+q}
is obtained by gluing
G_{r}(U,f)
with
V_{\alpha+q}(U,f)
. The family
(\mathcal{H}^{r}_{\beta})_{\beta\in\mathcal{A}+\mathbb{Z}}
is an increasing family of locally free sheaves. By definition, we have

\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{H}^{r}_{\beta})=V_{\beta}(U,f)\cap G_{r}(U,f)\subset\mathrm{H}^{r}_{\mathrm{dR}}(U,f/u),

and, for
\beta=\alpha+q
, multiplication by
u^{-q}
induces an isomorphism

V_{\beta}(U,f)\cap G_{r}(U,f)\simeq V_{\alpha}(U,f)\cap u^{-q}G_{r}(U,f).

The following definition is analogous to that for germs of functions (see e.g.
(
S-S85
, (7.3))
, with a shift however):

Definition 6

The
spectrum at infinity in degree
r
of
(U,f)
is the set of pairs
(\beta,\nu_{\beta}^{r})
with
\beta\in\mathcal{A}+\mathbb{Z}
and

\nu_{\beta}^{r}=\dim\frac{V_{\beta}\cap G_{r}}{[V_{<\beta}\cap G_{r}]+[V_{\beta}\cap uG_{r}]}.

It can be encoded in the generating polynomial
\sum_{\beta}\nu_{\beta}^{r}t^{\beta}
.

Proposition 14

With the notation of Proposition
13
, we have, for each
\beta\in\mathbb{Q}
:

\nu_{\beta}^{r}=\rk\mathrm{gr}_{F_{\mathrm{irr}}}^{-\beta}\mathrm{H}^{r}_{\mathrm{dR}}(U,f/u).

5.4.8
Other cohomology theories

We shortly introduce other cohomological structures that can be attached to a pair
(U,f)
and indicate the relations, when they exist, with the cohomological structures considered in this section.

Exponential mixed Hodge structures

This theory explains the notion of weight that we have introduced for
\mathrm{H}^{r}(U,f;\mathbb{Q})
and
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{\mathrm{c}}(U,f;\mathbb{Q})
. It makes use of the category
\MHM(\mathbb{A}^{\!1})
of
mixed Hodge modules
on the affine line
\mathbb{A}^{\!1}

MSaito87
which is briefly summarized in
Steenbrink22
. Any mixed Hodge module
M
on
\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
has an associated
\mathbb{Q}
-perverse sheaf
\mathcal{F}
. The category
\EMHS
of
exponential mixed Hodge structures
, introduced by Kontsevich and Soibelman
K-S10
, is the full subcategory of
\MHM(\mathbb{A}^{\!1})
whose objects
M
have an associated perverse sheaf
\mathcal{F}
with vanishing global hypercohomology. Furthermore, given any mixed Hodge module
M
on
\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
, one can functorially associate with it a mixed Hodge module belonging to
\EMHS
. Each object of
\EMHS
has a weight filtration, which can be distinct from the weight filtration as a mixed Hodge module. To each object is associated a “Betti fiber”, which is nothing but that defined by Corollary
2
, and inherits a weight filtration.

To the pair
(U,f)
one associates the mixed Hodge modules whose associated perverse sheaves are
\raisebox{6.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{p}$}\!R^{r-d}f_{*}({}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{p}}\!A_{U})
and
\raisebox{6.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{p}$}\!R^{r-d}f_{!}({}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{p}}\!A_{U})
, and the corresponding objects in
\EMHS
, that one can write
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{EMHS}}}(U,f)
and
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{EMHS}},\mathrm{c}}(U,f)
. The Betti fibers are nothing but
\mathrm{H}^{r}(U,f;\mathbb{Q})
and
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{\mathrm{c}}(U,f;\mathbb{Q})
respectively, and the inherited weight filtration is that considered at the end of Section
5.3.1
.

Furthermore, to each object of
\EMHS
is associated a de Rham fiber, and for
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{EMHS}}}(U,f)
and
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{EMHS}},\mathrm{c}}(U,f)
, the de Rham fibers are nothing but
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{{\mathrm{dR}}}(U,f)
and
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{{\mathrm{dR}},\mathrm{c}}(U,f)
respectively. We can regard the irregular Hodge filtration as the Hodge part of an object of
\EMHS
.

The category
\EMHS
is equipped with a tensor product, making it a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear neutral Tannakian category. For
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{EMHS}}}(U,f)
and
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{EMHS}},\mathrm{c}}(U,f)

S-S85
.

The category of exponential Nori motives constructed in
F-J17
regards the exponential mixed Hodge structures
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{EMHS}}}(U,f)
and
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{EMHS}},\mathrm{c}}(U,f)
respectively as a realization of an object
[U,\varnothing,f,r,0]
and
[X,H,f_{X},r,0]
of a category
\mathbf{M}^{\exp}(\mathbbm{k})
if
U
and
f
are defined over a subfield
\mathbbm{k}
of
\mathbb{C}
.

The motivic Milnor fiber at infinity

Following the general formalism of Denef and Loeser for the motivic Milnor fiber, a motivic Milnor fiber
S_{f,\infty}
at infinity for
(U,f)
has been constructed by Matsui-Takeuchi
(
M-T09
, §4)
and by Raibaut

Raibaut12
. It is an object of the Grothendieck ring of varieties over

with a

This object

is a Laurent polynomial which is nondegenerate with respect to its Newton polyhedron (see Section
5.5.3
for this notion and
Takeuchi23

Exponentially twisted cyclic homology

From a categorical point of view, it is a standard question to ask how much a quasi-projective variety
U
is determined from the bounded derived category
\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathrm{coh}(U))
simply denoted by
\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(U)
. In particular, let us assume that
U
and
U^{\prime}
have Fourier-Mukai equivalent derived categories. This means that there exists a kernel, that is, an object
\mathcal{K}
of
\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(U\times U^{\prime})
, such that the integral transformation functor with kernel
\mathcal{K}
:

\boldsymbol{R}p^{\prime}_{*}\Bigl(\boldsymbol{L}p^{*}({\raisebox{1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}})\otimes^{L}\mathcal{K}\Bigl):\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(U)\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(U^{\prime})

that, under such an assumption, the odd,
resp.
even, de Rham cohomologies of

and

are isomorphic.

Shklyarov

has extended this kind of results to pairs
(U,f)
with

quasi-projective. Namely, given pairs
(U,f)
and
(U^{\prime},f^{\prime})
, assume that the kernel
\mathcal{K}
is an object of
\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(U\times_{\mathbb{A}^{\!1}}U^{\prime})
and that the associated Fourier-Mukai transformation is an equivalence of categories
\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(U)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(U^{\prime})
. Then there exist isomorphisms between the odd
resp.
even
\mathbb{C}[v]\langle\partial_{v}\rangle
-modules

\mathop{\textstyle\bigoplus}\displaylimits_{r\mathrm{odd}/\mathrm{even}}\mathrm{H}^{r}_{\mathrm{dR}}(U,vf)\simeq\mathop{\textstyle\bigoplus}\displaylimits_{r\mathrm{odd}/\mathrm{even}}\mathrm{H}^{r}_{\mathrm{dR}}(U^{\prime},vf^{\prime}).

In both cases (
f=0
with Keller and

with Shklyarov), the main step is an identification between the de Rham cohomology and the periodic cyclic homology associated to the pair
resp.
triple consisting of the category of perfect complexes on

(bounded complexes quasi-isomorphic to bounded complexes of locally free
\mathcal{O}_{U}
-modules) and its subcategory of acyclic such complexes
resp.
and the regular function
f
.

5.5
Tameness on smooth affine varieties

Definitions of tameness

In the global setting,
tameness
of
f:U\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
is a property that is intended to be the analogue of having an isolated critical point for a germ of holomorphic function. It consists of two conditions:

•

each critical point of
f
is isolated (since
f
is a regular function on
U
quasi-projective, this implies that the set of critical points of
f
is finite),

•

“infinity is not a critical point for
f
”.

The
global Milnor number
\mu(f)
is the sum, over all critical points
x\in U
of
f
, of the corresponding Milnor numbers
\mu(f,x)
.

One should not expect a unique definition of the second property, but instead various criteria adapted to each particular class of examples.

Let us list some of them. We start “from inside”.

1.

f:\mathbb{A}^{\!d}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
is tame in the sense of Broughton (see
(
Broughton88
, Lem. 4.3)
) if there exists
\varepsilon>0
and a compact
K\subset U
such that
\|\partial f\|\geqslant\varepsilon
out of
K
.

2.

Tameness in the sense of Malgrange means that, when
f(x)
remains bounded, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\|x\|\|\partial f(x)\|\geqslant\varepsilon
for
\|x\|
sufficiently large.

3.

In
N-Z92
is introduced the notion of M-tameness for such a polynomial, which gives global Milnor fibrations in big balls in
\mathbb{A}^{\!d}
, that covers both previous situations (the notion of quasi-tameness was also previously considered in
Nemethi86
). One can extend this notion to any function
f
on a smooth affine variety by embedding this variety in an affine space and by considering the induced metric on it (see
D-S02a
).

4.

A specific class of examples of Item
1
consists of convenient and nondegenerate polynomials with respect to their Newton polyhedron at infinity, in a given coordinate system of
\mathbb{A}^{\!n}
, as defined by Kouchnirenko
Kouchnirenko76
. Their toric analogues (Laurent polynomials) will be considered in Section
5.5.3
. On the other hand, García López and Némethi
G-N95
have defined a nice class of polynomials that are quasi-tame.

For our purposes, tameness will be considered from a cohomological point of view. We fix the ring
A
to be
\mathbb{Z}
or to be one of the fields
\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}
.

5.

A cohomological condition was introduced by Katz in the arithmetic
\ell
-adic setting
(
Katz90
, Prop. 14.13.3(2))
: let
C^{\raisebox{1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}}
denote the cone of the natural morphism of complexes
\boldsymbol{R}f_{!}A_{U}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\boldsymbol{R}f_{*}A_{U}
on the affine line
\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
; by definition, we have a long exact sequence

\cdots\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}R^{k}f_{!}A_{U}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}R^{k}f_{*}A_{U}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathcal{H}^{k}(C^{\raisebox{1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}})\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}R^{k+1}f_{!}A_{U}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\cdots;

in general,
C^{\raisebox{1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}}
has constructible cohomology; the condition is that all cohomology sheaves
\mathcal{H}^{k}(C^{\raisebox{1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}})
are constant sheaves of
A
-modules of finite type on the affine line
\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
.

It an be shown that the geometric tameness conditions considered in (
1
)–(
4
) all imply the Katz tameness condition with
A=\mathbb{Z}
.

Tameness “from outside” is defined for a suitable choice of projectivization
f_{X}:X\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
of
f
. For a polynomial
f
, the standard partial compactification of the graph
U\subset\mathbb{P}^{d}\times\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
of
f
is natural to consider, but others can also prove useful.

6.

We say (see
Bibi96bb
) that
f:U\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
is
cohomologically
A
-tame
if there exists a projectivization of
f
given by a commutative diagram

\textstyle{U\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{j}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{X}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{A}^{\!1}}

where
X
is quasi-projective and
f_{X}
is projective, such that both complexes
\boldsymbol{R}j_{*}A_{U}
and
\boldsymbol{R}j_{!}A_{U}
have no vanishing cycles at infinity, namely, for all
c\in\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
, the vanishing cycle complexes
\phi_{f_{X}-c}(\boldsymbol{R}j_{*}A_{U})
and
\phi_{f_{X}-c}(\boldsymbol{R}j_{!}A_{U})
are supported in at most a finite number of points, all at finite distance, i.e., contained in
U
. Note that, if
A
is a field, Verdier duality yields that the condition on one complex implies the condition on the other one.

7.

An example of a cohomologically
\mathbb{Q}
-tame function on an affine open subset of
\mathbb{A}^{\!3}
which is not a torus
(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}})^{3}
is the rational function

f(x,y,z)=y(2+z)+\frac{x^{2}}{(xy-1)(1+z)}

which is regular on
U=\mathbb{A}^{\!3}\smallsetminus\{(xy-1)(1+z)=0\}
. Its tameness is proved in
(
G-S15
, App.)
, where the authors propose such a function as a Landau-Ginzburg model for the mirror of a

For polynomial functions on the affine space, a relation between “inside” and “outside” tameness does exist. In fact, Parusiński has shown

that
f:\mathbb{A}^{\!d}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
is cohomologically tame with respect to the standard partial compactification of the graph
U\subset\mathbb{P}^{d}\times\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
of

if and only if it satisfies the Malgrange condition.

Purpose of this section

Tame functions provide a framework where the general properties developed in Section
5.3
acquire a simpler expression and emphasize purity (in the sense of Hodge theory). This is parallel to properties of isolated singularities of holomorphic functions. However, the algebraic setting allows more specific properties, like those related to positivity.

5.5.1
Cohomological properties of tame functions

For the sake of simplicity, we assume in the remaining part of this section
5.5

is affine of dimension

We will make use of the
\mathbb{Z}
-tameness condition of Katz, so we first make precise an equivalent definition when

Proposition 15

The function

is
A

1.

the perverse complex
\raisebox{6.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{p}$}\!R^{r-d}\!f_{*}({}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{p}}\!A_{U})

\mathrm{H}^{r-1}(U,A)

\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
;

2.

the perverse complex
\raisebox{6.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{p}$}\!R^{r-d}\!f_{!}({}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{p}}\!A_{U})
is zero for
r<d
and, if

\mathrm{H}^{r-1}_{\mathrm{c}}(U,A)

\mathbb{A}^{\!1}

The kernel and the cokernel (in the perverse sense) of the natural morphism
\raisebox{6.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{p}$}\!R^{0}\!f_{!}{}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{p}}\!A_{U}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\raisebox{6.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{p}$}\!R^{0}\!f_{*}{}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{p}}\!A_{U}

\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
.

Furthermore, if

is cohomologically

-tame in the sense of Katz.

Proof
(Sketch)

The notion of micro-support is useful for explaining the latter property
(see
K-S90
;
Dimca04
;
M-S22
)
: Katz’ condition is equivalent to the property that the micro-support of the cone
\mathcal{C}
of the natural morphism
\boldsymbol{R}f_{!}{}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{p}}\!A_{U}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\boldsymbol{R}f_{*}{}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{p}}\!A_{U}
is reduced to the zero section of the cotangent bundle of
\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
, and this, in turn, is equivalent to the property that its perverse cohomology sheaves are constant. The long exact sequence of perverse cohomology attached to the corresponding distinguished triangle, together with Artin’s vanishing result (see
(
K-S90
, Prop. 10.3.17)
or
(
Dimca04
, Prop. 5.2.13)

15

It remains to identify the constant local systems. This is obtained by analyzing the perverse Leray spectral sequence

E_{2}^{p,q}=\mathrm{H}^{p}(\mathbb{A}^{\!1},\raisebox{6.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{p}$}\!R^{q}f_{*}{}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{p}}\!A_{U})\implies\mathrm{H}^{p+q}(U,{}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{p}}\!A_{U}),

and the compact support analogue with
\raisebox{6.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{p}$}\!R^{q}f_{!}{}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{p}}\!A_{U}
. Due to the vanishing and constancy statements in Items
1
and
3
, one obtains that it degenerates at
E_{2}
. Then for

The perverse complex

5.5.2
The spectrum at infinity

The twisted de Rham complex and the Brieskorn lattice

By the comparison theorem
5.3.4
and Theorem
5.5.1
, we find that, for
f
tame in the sense of Katz,
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{{\mathrm{dR}}}(U,f)
and
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{{\mathrm{dR}},\mathrm{c}}(U,f)
are zero for
r\neq d
and that both coincide if
r=d
, with
\dim\mathrm{H}^{d}_{{\mathrm{dR}}}(U,f)=\mu(f)
.

The Brieskorn lattices (see Theorem
5.4.4
) thus vanish for
r\neq d
and the Brieskorn lattice in degree

, that we denote by
G_{d}(U,f)\subset G(U,f):=\mathrm{H}^{d}_{{\mathrm{dR}}}(U,f/u)=\mathrm{H}^{d}_{{\mathrm{dR}},\mathrm{c}}(U,f/u)
, is a free
\mathbb{C}[u]
-module of rank
\mu(f)
.

When
f
is tame, the Brieskorn lattice can be computed with a twisted de Rham complex on
U
(instead of a logarithmic de Rham complex on
X
). Furthermore, as
U
is affine, one can compute the hypercohomology of the twisted de Rham complex (over
\mathbb{C}[u]
or
\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1}]
) as the cohomology of the complex of global sections on
U
. In such a case, it is more convenient to deal with the lattice

that is usually called the

. We have (see
(
S-Y14
, Prop. 8.13)
):

Theorem 5.5.2

If
f:U\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}^{\!1}
is tame (in the sense of Katz, say), then the inclusion
G_{0}(U,f)\subset G(U,f)
reads

G_{0}(U,f)=\frac{\Omega^{d}(U)[u]}{(u\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}f)\Omega^{d-1}(U)[u]}\mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}}\frac{\Omega^{d}(U)[u,u^{-1}]}{(u\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}f)\Omega^{d-1}(U)[u,u^{-1}]}.

The spectrum at infinity

The spectrum of
G_{0}(U,f)
is the set of pairs
(\beta,\nu_{\beta})
computed with
G_{0}(U,f)
instead of
G_{d}(U,f)
, so that
\nu_{\beta}=\nu_{\beta-d}^{d}
and therefore, according to Proposition
14
, the relation with the irregular Hodge filtration of
\mathrm{H}^{d}_{\mathrm{dR}}(U,f)
is

\nu_{\beta}=\rk\mathrm{gr}_{F_{\mathrm{irr}}}^{d-\beta}\mathrm{H}^{d}_{\mathrm{dR}}(U,f).

(5.21)

The perfect pairing compatible with
\partial_{u}
of Theorem
5.4.4
(
2
) reads (due to the definition above of

)

G_{0}(U,f)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[u]}G_{0}(U,-f)\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}u^{d}\mathbb{C}[u],

and we can identify
G_{0}(U,-f)
with the pullback

by the involution

, that we also denote by

. This is

as a

-vector space, and the action of

is defined as follows: if

, we denote it by

when considering it as an element of

in

Corollary 6

If

at infinity is symmetric with respect to

and is contained in

The first assertion follows from the behavior of spectrum with respect to duality (see e.g.
(

, Prop. 3.3)

(another argument has been given in

5.5.3
An example: Laurent polynomials

In this section, we consider the case where the affine variety
U
is the torus
(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}})^{d}
. If we choose coordinates
x_{1},\dots,x_{d}
, then
f
is a Laurent polynomial in
\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,x_{d}^{\pm 1}]
. Let us write
f=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}a_{m}x^{m}
. The
Newton polyhedron

\Delta(f)
is the convex hull in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of the set
\{0\}\cup\{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\mid\penalty a_{m}\neq\penalty 0\}
. We say that
f
is
nondegenerate with respect to
\Delta(f)
if for each face
\sigma
of
\Delta(f)
not containing
0
, setting
f_{\sigma}=\sum_{m\in\sigma}a_{m}x^{m}
, the functions

f_{\sigma},\frac{\partial f_{\sigma}}{\partial x_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial f_{\sigma}}{\partial x_{d}}

do not vanish simultaneously on
U
. We say that
f
is convenient if
0
belongs to the interior of
\Delta(f)
. In such a case, an argument similar to that of Broughton
Broughton88
shows that
f
is M-tame, and therefore tame in the sense of Katz. It follows from
Kouchnirenko76
that

\mu(f)=\dim\mathrm{H}^{d}(U,f;\mathbb{Q})=d!\mathrm{Vol}_{d}(\Delta(f)).

The Newton filtration and the Brieskorn lattice

Let us assume that
f
convenient and nondegenerate with respect to
\Delta(f)
. For each face
\sigma
of dimension
d-1
of the boundary
\partial\Delta(f)
, we denote by
L_{\sigma}
the linear form with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
such that
L_{\sigma}\equiv 1
on
\sigma
. For
g\in\mathbb{C}[x,x^{-1}]
, we set
\deg_{\sigma}(g)=\max_{m}L_{\sigma}(m)
, where the max is taken on the exponents
m
of monomials appearing in
g
, and
\deg(g)=\max_{\sigma}\deg_{\sigma}(g)
.

Remark 9

1.

For
g,h\in\mathbb{C}[x,x^{-1}]

\deg(gh)\leqslant\deg(g)+\deg(h)
with equality if and only if there exists a face
\sigma
such that
\deg(g)=\deg_{\sigma}(g)
and
\deg(h)=\deg_{\sigma}(h)
.

2.

As
0
belongs to the interior of
\Delta(f)
, we have
\deg(g)\geqslant 0
for any

and
\deg(g)=0

3.

Let us set
{\mathrm{d}x}/{x}=({\mathrm{d}x_{1}}/{x_{1}})\wedge\cdots\wedge({\mathrm{d}x_{d}}/{x_{d}})

The increasing

\mathcal{N}_{\beta}\Omega^{d}(U):=\{g\mathrm{d}x/x\in\Omega^{d}(U)\mid\deg(g)\leqslant\beta\}.

The previous remark shows that
\mathcal{N}_{\beta}\Omega^{d}(U)\!=\!0
for
\beta\!<\!0
and
\mathcal{N}_{0}\Omega^{d}(U)\!=\!\mathbb{C}\mathrm{d}x/x
. We extend this filtration to

\mathcal{N}_{\beta}\Omega^{d}(U)[u]:=\mathcal{N}_{\beta}\Omega^{d}(U)+u\mathcal{N}_{\beta-1}\Omega^{d}(U)+\cdots+u^{k}\mathcal{N}_{\beta-k}\Omega^{d}(U)+\cdots

Definition 7

The Newton filtration of the Brieskorn lattice is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\beta}G_{0}

\displaystyle=\image\bigl[\mathcal{N}_{\beta}\Omega^{d}(U)[u]\hookrightarrow\Omega^{d}(U)[u]\rightarrow G_{0}\bigr]

at infinity.

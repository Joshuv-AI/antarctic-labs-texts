Partitions and Schur functions

The partition is a set of integers
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{\ell})
, where

\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\lambda_{\ell}>0
and
\lambda_{i}
are called parts of
\lambda
.
The sum of the parts is called the weight of
\lambda

|\lambda|
.
There is a well-known notion of Young diagrams
\lambda
whose row lengths are equal to parts of
\lambda
.
We accept the agreement as in
[
2
]
.
Let us denote the horizontal lengths of the part of the strings that starts at the
ii

not include it) and goes to the right as
\alpha_{i}
, and the vertical part of the column that starts at
(not including) the same node and goes down we denote
\beta_{i}
. In Frobenius coordinates,
\lambda
is written
as
(\alpha|\beta)=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}|\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k})
, where
k
is the number of nodes on
the main diagonal of the Young diagram, see
[
2
]
for details.

The following relations define Schur polynomials (Schur functions) as a function of
power sum variables
\mathbf{p}=(p_{1},p_{2},\dots)
:

e^{\sum_{k>0}\frac{1}{k}p_{k}x^{k}}=\sum_{k\geq 0}s_{(k)}(\mathbf{p})

s_{\lambda}(\mathbf{p})=\mathrm{det}\left[s_{(\lambda_{i}-i+j)}(\mathbf{p})\right]_{i,j}

{\rm In\,case}\hskip 9.24994ptp_{m}=\mathrm{tr}(X)^{m}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm one\,can\,write}\hskip 9.24994pts_{\lambda}(\mathbf{p}(X))=:s_{\lambda}(X)

We have

s_{\lambda}(\mathbf{p})=\frac{\dim\lambda}{|\lambda|!}\sum_{\mu}\varphi_{\lambda}(\mu)\mathbf{p}_{\mu},\hskip 9.24994pt\dim\lambda=\frac{\prod_{i<j}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}-i+j)}{\prod_{i}(\lambda_{i}-i+\ell)!}

The known orthogonality relations for the factors
\varphi_{\lambda}
(which are the normalized characters of the
symmetric group in representation
\lambda
)
[
2
]
are as follows:

\zeta_{\Delta}\sum_{\lambda}\left(\frac{{\rm\dim}\lambda}{d!}\right)^{2}\varphi_{\lambda}(\mu)\varphi_{\lambda}(\Delta)=\delta_{\Delta,\mu}

(A.1)

and

\left(\frac{{\rm\dim}\lambda}{d!}\right)^{2}\sum_{\Delta}\zeta_{\Delta}\varphi_{\lambda}(\Delta)\varphi_{\mu}(\Delta)=\delta_{\lambda,\mu}

(A.2)

where

and if each part

times the partition

Fermions. Multicomponent tau function

I will only briefly give the notation and some formulas. Fermion operators
\psi^{(a)}_{n},\psi^{{\dagger}(a)}_{n}

where
a=1,\dots,\textsc{N}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
satisfy the relations

[\psi^{(a)}_{n},\psi^{(b)}_{m}]_{+}=0=[\psi^{(a){\dagger}}_{n},\psi^{(b{\dagger})}_{m}]_{+},\hskip 9.24994pt[\psi^{(a){\dagger}}_{n},\psi^{(b)}_{m}]_{+}=\delta_{a,b}\delta_{n,m}

\langle 0|\psi^{(a)}_{n}=\langle 0|\psi^{{\dagger}(a)}_{-n-1}=0=\psi^{(a)}_{-n-1}|0\rangle=\psi^{{\dagger}(a)}_{n}|0\rangle,\hskip 9.24994ptn>0

The wonderful observation of Kyoto school (see for instance
[
4

) is the following relation

(B.1)

which is also known as bosonization formula (a version of this formula). Here

J_{n}^{(a)}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\psi^{(a)}_{i}\psi^{{\dagger}(a)}_{n+i}

. We also need the property

s_{(\alpha|\beta)}(\mathbf{p})=(-1)^{|(\alpha|\beta))|}s_{(\beta|\alpha)}(-\mathbf{p})

Following
[
4
]
we introduce
2\textsc{N}
-component KP (

(B.2)

where
\circlearrowleft

\psi^{(2\textsc{N}+1)}_{-i-1}:=\psi^{(1)}_{-i-1}

Lemma 2.1

(m_{1}-2n_{1}+2m_{1}\omega)(m_{1}-2n_{1}+2m_{1}\omega^{2})=3m_{1}^{2}+4n_{1}^{2}=(3d+4)n_{1}^{2}-3m_{4}.

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{3m_{1}^{2}+4n_{1}^{2}+3m_{4}}{m_{1}-2n_{1}+2m_{1}\omega}\Big)_{3}=\Big(\frac{3^{2}\cdot m_{4}/3}{m_{1}-2n_{1}+2m_{1}\omega}\Big)_{3}.

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{m_{1}-2n_{1}+2m_{1}\omega}{m_{0}/3}\Big)_{3}=\Big(\frac{-2n_{1}}{m_{0}/3}\Big)_{3}=1.

For real
b
,
c
, let
\{U_{k}(b,c)\}
be the Lucas sequence defined by

U_{0}=0,\ U_{1}=1,\quad U_{k+1}=bU_{k}-cU_{k-1}\quad(k=1,2,3,\ldots).

It is well known that for
b^{2}-4c\neq 0
and
k\geq 0
,

U_{k}(b,c)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{b^{2}-4c}}\Big(\Big(\frac{b+\sqrt{b^{2}-4c}}{2}\Big)^{k}-\Big(\frac{b-\sqrt{b^{2}-4c}}{2}\Big)^{k}\Big).

For squarefree
d>1
, nonzero integers
m
,
n
, and
\varepsilon=\pm 1
,
write
m^{2}-dn^{2}=4\varepsilon
. Then

U_{k}(m,\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{n\sqrt{d}}\Big(\Big(\frac{m+n\sqrt{d}}{2}\Big)^{k}-\Big(\frac{m-n\sqrt{d}}{2}\Big)^{k}\Big).

The two corollaries below evaluate
the Lucas numbers
U_{\frac{p-1}{3}}(m,\varepsilon)\pmod{p}

for primes
p=x^{2}+3dy^{2}
with
p\nmid n
.
When
3\mid y
, it follows from
Theorem 6.2 that
\frac{m+n\sqrt{d}}{2}
is a cubic residue mod
p
,
so that
U_{\frac{p-1}{3}}(m,\varepsilon)\equiv 0\pmod{p}
.
Thus we assume that
3\nmid y
. Fix the signs
of
x
,
y
so that
x\equiv y\pmod{3}
.
From Theorems 6.1, 8.1 and 8.2, we deduce:

Corollary 8.3
.

Suppose that
m^{2}-dn^{2}=-4
. Then

U_{\frac{p-1}{3}}(m,-1)\equiv\begin{cases}0\pmod{p}&\hbox{if $m\equiv 0,\pm 4\pmod{9}$,}\\
\big(\frac{mn/3}{3}\big)\frac{x}{dny}\pmod{p}&\hbox{if $m\equiv\pm 3\pmod{9}$,}\\
-\big(\frac{mn}{3}\big)\frac{x}{dny}\pmod{p}&\hbox{if $m\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}$,}\\
\big(\frac{mn}{3}\big)\frac{x}{dny}\pmod{p}&\hbox{if $m\equiv\pm 2\pmod{9}$.}\end{cases}

Corollary 8.4
.

Suppose that
m^{2}-dn^{2}=4
with a sign of
m
chosen
such that
\mathrm{ord}_{3}(m-2)\geq\mathrm{ord}_{3}n
.
Then

U_{\frac{p-1}{3}}(m,1)\equiv\begin{cases}0\pmod{p}&\hbox{if $m\equiv 2\pmod{27}$,}\\
\big(\frac{(m-2)n/3}{3}\big)\frac{x}{dny}\pmod{p}&\hbox{if $m\equiv 5,8\pmod{9}$,}\\
-\big(\frac{(m-2)n/27}{3}\big)\frac{x}{dny}\pmod{p}&\hbox{if $m\equiv 11,20\pmod{27}$,}\\
\big(\frac{mn/3}{3}\big)\frac{x}{dny}\pmod{p}&\hbox{if $m\equiv 0\pmod{3}$,}\\
\big(\frac{(m+2)n/3}{3}\big)\frac{x}{dny}\pmod{p}&\hbox{if $m\equiv 1\pmod{3}$.}\end{cases}

Example
.

m=2t
, so that
\{U_{k}(m,1)\}
is a sequence

First suppose that

Abstract
. Absolute algebras are a new type of algebraic structures, endowed with a meaningful notion of infinite sums of operations without supposing any underlying topology. Opposite to the usual definition of operadic calculus, they are defined as algebras over cooperads. The goal of this article is to develop this new theory. First, we relate the homotopy theory of absolute algebras to the homotopy theory of usual algebras via a duality square . It intertwines bar-cobar adjunctions with linear duality adjunctions. In particular, we show that linear duality functors between types of coalgebras and types of algebras are Quillen functors and that they induce equivalences between objects with finiteness conditions on their homology. We give general comparison results between absolute types of algebras and their classical counterparts. We work out examples of this theory such as absolute associative algebras and absolute Lie algebras, and show that it includes the theory of contramodules. Finally, in [ CPRNW23 ] , the authors showed that two nilpotent Lie algebras whose universal enveloping algebras are isomorphic as associative algebras must be isomorphic. As an application of our results, we generalize their theorem to the setting of absolute Lie algebras and absolute ℒ ∞ \mathcal{L}_{\infty} -algebras.

In the same way as algebras are "the Koszul dual notion" to conilpotent coalgebras, since they both are "finitary types of structures", absolute types of algebras are the "Koszul dual notion" to non-conilpotent coalgebras. The infinite decompositions of elements in these non-conilpotent coalgebras are reflected in the "transfinite multiplication tables" of absolute algebras. The notion of an algebra over a cooperad was introduced in an abstract context by B. Le Grignou and D. Lejay in
[
GL22
]

\mathcal{P}

\mathcal{P}^{\hskip 1.0pt\scriptstyle\textrm{!`}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{P}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}}

Main results.
The goal of this article is to develop the theory of algebras over cooperads, which we call
absolute algebras
. Our first result answers a very natural question: how do these two aforementioned bar-cobar constructions relate to each other? We show that there are duality functors that intertwine both of these adjunctions in a duality square of commuting adjunctions.

Theorem A

(Duality square, Theorem
2.17
)
.

There exists a square of adjunctions

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{P}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{P}^{\hskip 1.0pt\mathsf{\scriptstyle\textrm{!`}}}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{P}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{P}^{\hskip 1.0pt\mathsf{\scriptstyle\textrm{!`}}}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{B}^{\mathsf{op}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\circ}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega^{\mathsf{op}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\Omega}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathrm{B}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

which commutes in the following sense: right adjoints going from the top right corner to the bottom left corner are naturally isomorphic.

The technical part of the above results is the construction of the linear duality adjunctions. In particular, the functor
(-)^{\circ}
is a generalization of the Sweedler dual functor constructed in
[
Swe69
]
. The above theorem admits a much more general formulation: it also holds for any curved twisting morphism in the sense of
[
RiL24a
]
. Conceptually, it shows that absolute algebras appear every time one considers the linear dual of a conilpotent coalgebra. For example, taking the
linear dual of the Bar construction
, which occurs in many instances in the literature: it is a key in ingredient in J. Lurie’s proof of that formal moduli problems are encoded by dg Lie algebras
[
Lur11
]
and in the subsequent generalization of his result by D. Calaque, R. Campos and J. Nuiten in
[
CCN22
]
.

This duality square can in favorable cases be made compatible with the respective model structures of each of these categories. Then, this square becomes a square of Quillen adjunctions. This allows us to have, for the first time, a
homotopical understanding
of the linear duality functor
(-)^{*}
between types of coalgebras and types of algebras over a cofibrant dg operad
\mathcal{P}
.

Theorem B

(Theorem
2.26
)
.

Let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{P}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}^{\mathsf{f.d},\mp}~\left[\mathsf{Q.iso}^{-1}\right]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{P}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{f.d},\pm}~\left[\mathsf{Q.iso}^{-1}\right]^{\mathsf{op}}~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\circ}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

between the
\infty
-category of dg
\mathcal{P}
-algebras with degree-wise finite dimensional bounded below (resp. bounded above) homology and the
\infty
-category of
\mathcal{P}
-coalgebras with degree-wise finite dimensional bounded above (resp. bounded below) homology.

-algebras. There, they allow us to prove that curved absolute
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
-algebras provide us with rational models, which are dual to Sullivan’s models, and to develop our approach to derived algebraic geometry using curved absolute
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
-algebras.

In Section 3, our goal is to establish several theoretical results about algebras over a general cooperad
\mathcal{C}
, and in particular, to compare these algebras to algebras over the linear dual operad
\mathcal{C}^{*}
. This amounts to comparing the absolute and the classical versions of the same algebraic structure. In general, we construct an adjunction between algebras over
\mathcal{C}
and algebras over
\mathcal{C}^{*}
, where the right adjoint restricts the structural morphism of an absolute algebra to finite sums. We show that, under mild conditions on
\mathcal{C}
, this restriction functor is fully faithful. It would be natural to wonder then if absolute algebras can be obtained as some kind of completion of classical algebras. We explain why this is not the case in general: the basis example of the
I
-adic completion of augmented commutative algebras allows us to show that absolute commutative algebras differ from
I
-adically complete ones. This follows from the fact that the
I
-adic completion is not, in general, idempotent.

In Section 4, we work out examples and explain the theory in particular cases of interest. The first example is that of
contramodules
. Contramodules over coassociative coalgebras where first introduced by S. Eilenberg and J. C. Moore in
[
EM65
]
but later somewhat forgotten until they were extensively studied by L. Positselski, see for instance
[
Pos21
]
. Since a coassociative coalgebra is a cooperad concentrated in arity one, we show that contramodules are a particular example of absolute algebras. Engulfing this theory provides us with illuminating examples and counterexamples that shed a light into what is to be expected of this new type of algebraic structures. After, we treat
in extenso
the cases of dg absolute associative algebras and dg absolute Lie algebras.

Finally, we apply this new framework to Lie theory. In
[
CPRNW23
]
, the authors proved the following theorem: two nilpotent Lie algebras are isomorphic as Lie algebras if and only if their universal enveloping algebras are isomorphic as associative algebras. This result followed from a more general statement: two homotopy complete dg Lie algebras have quasi-isomorphic universal enveloping algebras if and only if they are quasi-isomorphic. The key element in the proof of this previous statement is, in turn, a comparison result between the deformation complexes of
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
-coalgebras in general. In this last section, we show how to reinterpret this result on deformation complexes in the more general context of complete absolute Lie algebras and their universal enveloping absolute algebras.

Theorem C

(Theorem
5.10
and Theorem
5.15
)
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a field of characteristic zero and let
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
be two complete graded absolute Lie algebras. They are isomorphic as complete graded absolute Lie algebras if and only if their universal enveloping absolute algebras are isomorphic.

This result follows from a more general statement concerning complete dg absolute Lie algebras. By transporting via Koszul duality the main technical result of
[
CPRNW23
, Theorem 4.27]
, we immediately get that two such algebras are linked by a zig-zag of weak equivalences if and only if their universal enveloping constructions are. Since these weak equivalences are in particular quasi-isomorphisms, the above result follows when the differential is zero. As we explain in the last section of this paper, the result about homotopy complete Lie algebras (and thus about nilpotent Lie algebras) then follows by the fact that a dg Lie algebra is homotopy complete if and only if is homotopically fully faithfully included into complete absolute dg Lie algebras.

This absolute framework is more general, as for instance complete graded absolute Lie algebras include nilpotent graded Lie algebras without degree restrictions. In this particular case, we compute explicitly the universal enveloping absolute algebra, which is given by the completed tensor algebra modulo the standard relation. These methods also allow us to develop an analogue of Theorem
C
for minimal
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
-algebras using
\infty
-isomorphisms, where we show that if their universal enveloping abslute
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
-algebras are weakly equivalent, then there exists an
\infty
-isomorphism between them. In particular, this applies to nilpotent (minimal)
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
-algebras in the sense of
[
Get09
]
, which are examples of absolute (minimal)
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
-algebras.

Conventions

Let
\Bbbk
be a ground field of characteristic
0
. The ground category is the symmetric monoidal category
\left(\mathsf{pdg}\textsf{-}\mathsf{mod},\otimes,\Bbbk\right)
of pre-differential graded (pdg)
\Bbbk
-modules: these are graded modules
V
endowed with a degree
-1
endomorphism
d_{V}
. We work with the
homological
degree conventions. The tensor product
\otimes
of pdg modules is given by the graded module

(A\otimes B)_{n}\coloneqq\displaystyle\bigoplus_{p+q=n}~A_{p}\otimes~B_{q}~,

together with the pre-differential

d_{A\otimes B}(a\otimes b)\coloneqq d_{A}(a)\otimes b+(-1)^{|a|}\hskip 1.0pta\otimes d_{B}(b)~.

The isomorphism
\tau_{A,B}:A\otimes B\longrightarrow B\otimes A
is given by the Koszul sign rule
\tau(a\otimes b)\coloneqq(-1)^{|a|.|b|}\hskip 1.0ptb\otimes a
on homogeneous elements. This symmetric monoidal category is
closed
, meaning the tensor
\otimes
of pdg modules admits a right adjoint. It is given by the graded module
\mathrm{hom}(A,B)
of graded maps between two pdg modules
A
and
B
, together with the pre-differential

\partial(f)\coloneqq d_{B}\circ f-(-1)^{|f|}f\circ d_{A}~,

where
f:A\longrightarrow B
is a graded map of degree
|f|
. The suspension of a
V
is denoted by
sV
, given by
(sV)_{p}\coloneqq V_{p-1}~.

A
differential graded (dg) module
is a pre-differential graded module
V
such that
d_{V}^{2}=0
. The category of dg modules is a full subcategory of pdg modules, preserved by the tensor product and the internal hom. A (p)dg module
V
is
degree-wise finite dimensional
if every
V_{n}
is a finite dimensional
\Bbbk
-module. It is
bounded above
if there exists an
m\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
V_{n}=0
for
n\geq m
and it is
bounded below
if there exists an
m\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
V_{n}=0
for
n\leq m
.

A pdg
\mathbb{S}
-module
M
is a collection
\{M(n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of pdg
\Bbbk[\mathbb{S}_{n}]
-modules, where
\mathbb{S}_{n}
stands for the symmetric group of permutations of
n
elements. This category is denoted by
\mathsf{pdg}~\mathbb{S}\textsf{-}\mathsf{mod}
. We denote
(\mathsf{pdg}~\mathbb{S}\textsf{-}\mathsf{mod},\circ,\I)
the monoidal category of pre-differential graded
\mathbb{S}
-modules endowed with the composition product
\circ
. Again, a dg
\mathbb{S}
-module is a pdg
\mathbb{S}
-module whose pre-differential squares to zero; they form a full subcategory of pdg
\mathbb{S}
-modules. We refer to
[
LV12
]
for most of the notations, and to
[
RiL22
]
for a more detailed explanation of this framework and of the recollections.

1.
Recollections

The goal of this section is to briefly recall the recent developments in operadic calculus made in
[
GL22
]
. Their main idea is encode
non-necessarily conilpotent
types of coalgebras using operads, and relate them via Koszul duality with
algebras over cooperads
. These algebras over cooperads define new types of algebraic structures which we will call "absolute algebras" in the present article. For a more thorough exposition, see also
[
RiL22
, Chapter 1]
.

1.1.
Operads and curved cooperads

The classical version of Koszul duality for operads, developed in
[
GK94
,
GJ94
]
, relates augmented operads with conilpotent coaugmented cooperads. See
[
LV12
, Chapter 6 and 7]
for a comprehensive account. A more general version of this duality, developed in
[
HM12
,
Gri19
]
, relates non-necessarily augmented operads with conilpotent curved cooperads.

Definition 1.1

(dg operad)
.

A
dg operad

\mathcal{P}
amounts to the data
(\mathcal{P},\gamma_{\mathcal{P}},\eta,d_{\mathcal{P}})
of a monoid in
(\mathsf{dg}~\mathbb{S}\textsf{-}\mathsf{mod},\circ,\I)
.

Definition 1.2

(augmented dg operad)
.

An
augmented dg operad

\mathcal{P}
amounts to the data of a dg operad
(\mathcal{P},\gamma,\eta)
equipped with a morphism of dg operads
\nu:\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow\I
such that
\nu\circ\eta=\mathrm{id}.

We denote by
\overline{\mathcal{P}}
the kernel of the augmentation map
\nu
, which has the structure of a non-unital dg operad.

Remark 1.3
.

Many interesting operads
do not
admit an augmentation. For instance,
u\mathcal{A}ss
, the operad which encodes
unital
associative algebras, or
u\mathcal{C}om
, the operad which encodes
unital
commutative algebras. In general,
unital
types of structures are encoded by operads which do not admit augmentations.

Definition 1.4

(pdg cooperad)
.

A
pdg cooperad

\mathcal{C}
amounts to the data
(\mathcal{C},\Delta,\epsilon,d_{\mathcal{C}})
of a comonoid in the monoidal category
(\mathsf{pdg}~\mathbb{S}\textsf{-}\mathsf{mod},\allowbreak\circ,\I)~.

Definition 1.5

(coaugmented pdg cooperad)
.

A
coaugmented pdg cooperad

\mathcal{C}
amounts to the data of a pdg cooperad
(\mathcal{C},\Delta,\epsilon)
equipped together with a morphism of pdg cooperads
\mu:\I\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}
such that
\epsilon\circ\mu=\mathrm{id}
.

We denote by
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
the kernel of the counit map, which has the structure of a non-counital pdg cooperad when
\mathcal{C}
admits a coaugmentation.

Curved cooperads are particular examples of pdg cooperads, endowed with a
curvature
that controls how far the pre-differential is from being a differential.

Definition 1.6

(curved cooperad)
.

A
curved cooperad

\mathcal{C}
amounts to the data
(\mathcal{C},\Delta,\epsilon,d_{\mathcal{C}},\Theta_{\mathcal{C}})
of a pdg cooperad
(\mathcal{C},\Delta,\epsilon,d_{\mathcal{C}})
and a morphism of pdg
\mathbb{S}
-modules
\Theta_{\mathcal{C}}:(\mathcal{C},d_{\mathcal{C}})\longrightarrow(\I,0)
of degree
-2
called the
curvature
, such that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}\circ_{(1)}\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{C}\circ_{(1)}\I)\oplus(\I\circ_{(1)}\mathcal{C})\cong\mathcal{C}\oplus\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta_{(1)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{\mathcal{C}}^{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathrm{id}~\circ_{(1)}~\Theta_{\mathcal{C}})~-~(\Theta_{\mathcal{C}}~\circ_{(1)}~\mathrm{id})~}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces+}

where
+
is given by
+(\mu,\nu)\coloneqq\mu+\nu
and where
\Delta_{(1)}
denotes the partial decomposition of the cooperad
\mathcal{C}
, that is, the decompositions which only involve two non-trivial elements.

The
coradical filtration
of a coaugmented cooperad (possibly pdg or curved) is the increasing filtration defined using iterations of the partial decompositions
\Delta_{(1)}
. Since it is coaugmented, one can remove trivial decompositions from the partial decomposition maps. An element is in the
\omega
-term of this filtration if its image by any possible composition of
\omega
(non-trivial) partial decomposition maps is zero. A cooperad is said to be
conilpotent
if any arbitrary iteration of these (non-trivial) partial decompositions maps of the cooperad ends up being trivial, or equivalently, if the coradical filtration is exhaustive. For a precise definition, see
[
RiL24a
, Section 1.3]
or
[
RiL22
, Chapter 1, Section 4]
.

Koszul duality relates dg operads with conilpotent coaugmented curved cooperads. For a dg operad
\mathcal{P}
and a coaugmented conilpotent curved cooperad
\mathcal{C}
, the fact that they are Koszul dual is encoded by the existence of a curved twisting morphism, which needs to satisfy some extra homological properties, see
[
HM12
, Section 4]
.

A
curved twisting morphism

\alpha:\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}
is the data of a degree
-1
map of graded
\mathbb{S}
-modules between the coaugmentation ideal
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
and
\mathcal{P}
which satisfies the following equation:

\partial(\alpha)+\gamma_{(1)}\circ(\alpha\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{(1)}=\Theta_{\mathcal{H}om}~,

where
\Delta_{(1)}
is the partial decomposition map of
\mathcal{C}
,
\gamma_{(1)}
the partial composition map of
\mathcal{P}
, and

\Theta_{\mathcal{H}om}
is the map given by composing the unit of
\mathcal{P}
and the curvature of
\mathcal{C}
. The set of curved twisting morphisms is denoted by
\mathrm{Tw}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{P})
. The above equation can be interpreted as a Maurer–Cartan equation in a convolution curved pre-Lie algebra, see
[
RiL24a
, Section 6]
for more details.

Following
[
Gri19
]
, there is an operadic bar-cobar adjunction,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{curv}~\mathsf{Coop}^{\mathsf{conil}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathsf{Op}~.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{B}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

between the category of coaugmented conilpotent curved cooperads and the category of dg operads, which represents and corepresents curved twisting morphisms in the following sense: for any coaugmented conilpotent curved cooperad
\mathcal{C}
and any dg operad
\mathcal{P}
, there are natural isomorphisms:

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{dg}~\mathsf{Op}}(\Omega\mathcal{C},\mathcal{P})\cong\mathrm{Tw}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{P})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{curv}~\mathsf{Coop}^{\mathsf{conil}}}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{B}\mathcal{P})~.

This gives two
universal curved twisting morphisms
,
\iota:\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\Omega\mathcal{C}
and
\pi:\mathrm{B}\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}
, induced by the identity maps. We refer to
[
Gri19
, Section 4]
for more details on these constructions and results.

Remark 1.7
.

When one restricts to
augmented
dg operads, the curvature is zero on the cooperad side, and one obtains an operadic Koszul duality between augmented dg operads and conilpotent coaugmented
dg
cooperads, which is essentially equivalent to the theory developed in
[
GK94
,
GJ94
]
.

1.2.
Classical bar-cobar adjunction between algebras and conilpotent coalgebras

The main point of the operadic Koszul duality explained in the previous subsection is that it allows us to relate types of algebras encoded by operads and types of
conilpotent
coalgebras encoded by cooperads.

Let us recall some constructions. To any dg (resp. pdg)
\mathbb{S}
-module one can associate an endofunctor in the category of dg (resp. pdg) modules via the
Schur realization functor
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{S}:\mathsf{(p)dg}~\mathbb{S}\textsf{-}\mathsf{mod}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{End}(\mathsf{(p)dg}~\mathsf{mod})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{S}(M)(-)\coloneqq\displaystyle\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}M(n)\otimes_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}(-)^{\otimes n}~.}

The realization functor
\mathscr{S}(-)
is strong monoidal. Thus, for any dg operad
\mathcal{P}
its Schur functor
\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P})
is a monad in dg modules and for any pdg cooperad
\mathcal{C}
its Schur functor
\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{C})
is a comonad in pdg modules.

Definition 1.8

(dg
\mathcal{P}
-algebra)
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be an operad. A dg
\mathcal{P}
-
algebra

B
amounts to the data
(B,\gamma_{B},d_{B})
of an algebra over the monad
\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P})
.

Definition 1.9

(pdg
\mathcal{C}
-coalgebra)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a pdg cooperad. A pdg
\mathcal{C}
-
coalgebra

D
amounts to the data
(D,\Delta_{D};d_{D})
of a coalgebra over the comonad
\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{C})
.

When
\mathcal{C}
is a
curved
cooperad, one furthermore asks that the coalgebra structure on
D
is compatible with the curvature of the cooperad, which leads to the definition of a
curved

\mathcal{C}
-coalgebra.

Definition 1.10

(curved
\mathcal{C}
-coalgebra)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a curved cooperad. A pdg coalgebra
D
is said to be
curved
if the following diagram commutes

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{C})(D)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D\cong\mathscr{S}(\I)(D)~.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta_{D}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-d_{D}^{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{S}(\Theta_{\mathcal{C}})(\mathrm{id})}

The data of a curved twisting morphism
\alpha:\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}
between a conilpotent coaugmented curved cooperad
\mathcal{C}
and a dg operad
\mathcal{P}
induces a bar-cobar adjunction relative to

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{curv}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{P}\text{-}\mathsf{alg},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega_{\alpha}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{B}_{\alpha}}

between the category of dg
\mathcal{P}
-algebras and the category of curved
\mathcal{C}
-coalgebras.

This bar-cobar adjunction has interesting homotopical properties. Usually, one considers the homotopy theory of dg
\mathcal{P}
-algebras up to quasi-isomorphism, that is, the model category structure on dg
\mathcal{P}
-algebras where fibrations are given by epimorphisms and weak equivalences are given by quasi-isomorphisms. Using the above adjunction, it can be transferred onto the category of curved
\mathcal{C}
-coalgebras and, under certain hypothesis on the curved twisting morphism
\alpha
, this Quillen adjunction becomes a Quillen equivalence. It is in particular the case for the universal curved twisting morphism
\iota:\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\Omega\mathcal{C}
. This allows one to study the homotopy theory of dg
\mathcal{P}
-algebras purely in terms of the Koszul dual coalgebras. See
[
Gri19
, Section 6 and 7]
.

1.3.
Complete bar-cobar adjunction between coalgebras and absolute algebras

A dg operad
\mathcal{P}
also encodes types of
coalgebras
, which are
non-necessarily conilpotent
. The goal of this subsection is to explain how, given a curved twisting morphism
\alpha:\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}
, one can also construct a
another bar-cobar adjunction
between these coalgebras encoded by
\mathcal{P}
and a
algebras encoded by the cooperad

\mathcal{C}
. We follow mainly
[
GL22
]
for these constructions.

1.3.1.

There is a

which is given by

This allows us to construct from any pdg cooperad a monad in the category of pdg modules.

Remark 1.14
.

The notion of an algebra over a cooperad defines a new type of algebraic structures. The reason is that the structural map

The notion of an algebra over a cooperad admits a further description in the case where the cooperad is

Let

Remark 1.18

Example 1.20

2.1.

The linear dual of a coalgebra over a given operad is naturally an algebra over the same operad.

Lemma 2.1
.

The linear dual defines a functor

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{P}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{P}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}~.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}

Proof.

There is a canonical morphism of operads

which sends

to the map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(C^{*})^{\otimes n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(C^{\otimes n})^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C^{*}~.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{*}}

Thus any dg
\mathcal{P}
-coalgebra structure

induces a dg
\mathcal{P}
-algebra structure on

by composing this structural morphism with the previous map.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

The linear duality functor

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{P}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{P}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}~.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}

admits a left adjoint.

Proof.

Consider the following square of functors

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{P}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{P}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathsf{mod}^{\mathsf{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathsf{mod}~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{U}^{\mathsf{op}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{P})(-)\right)^{\mathsf{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P})(-)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

where
\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P})(-)
is the free dg
\mathcal{P}
-algebra functor and where
\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{P})(-)
is the cofree dg
\mathcal{P}
-coalgebra functor given by Theorem
1.29
. The left hand side adjunction is monadic, since it is the opposite of a comonadic adjunction. All categories involved are complete and cocomplete. We also have that
(-)^{*}\cdot\mathrm{U}^{\mathsf{op}}\cong\mathrm{U}\cdot(-)^{*}~.
Thus we can apply the Adjoint Lifting Theorem
[
Joh75
, Theorem 2]
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Definition 2.3

(Sweedler dual)
.

The
Sweedler duality functor

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{P}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{P}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\circ}}

is defined as the functor left adjoint of the linear dual functor.

Remark 2.4
.

The proof of the Adjoint Lifting Theorem
[
Joh75
, Theorem 2]
gives an explicit construction of this left adjoint. Let
(B,\gamma_{B},d_{B})
be a dg
\mathcal{P}
-algebra. The Sweedler dual dg
\mathcal{P}
-coalgebra
B^{\circ}
is given by the following equalizer:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Eq}\Bigg(\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{P})(B^{*})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{P})\left((\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P})(B))^{*}\right)\Bigg)~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\gamma_{B})^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varrho}

where
\varrho
is an arrow constructed using the comonadic structure of
\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{P})
and the canonical inclusion of a dg module into its double linear dual. In particular, for any dg module
V
, we have an isomorphism:

\left(\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P})(V)\right)^{\circ}\cong\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{P})(V^{*})~.

Proposition 2.5
.

There is a natural monomorphism

\epsilon:\mathrm{U}^{\mathsf{op}}\cdot(-)^{\circ}\rightarrowtail(-)^{*}\cdot\mathrm{U}~,

which implies that the Sweedler dual is a sub-dg module of the linear dual functor.

Proof.

Let
V
be a dg module, there is a monomorphism

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\epsilon_{V}:\left(\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P})(V)\right)^{\circ}\cong\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{P})(V^{*})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\mathcal{P})(V^{*})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P})(V)\right)^{*}~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(p_{1})_{V^{*}}}

where the monomorphism
p_{1}
is given by Theorem
1.29
and the second monomorphism is induced by the natural monomorphism
(V^{*})^{\otimes n}\rightarrowtail(V^{\otimes n})^{*}
, hence the proposition is true on free dg
\mathcal{P}
-algebras. Any dg
\mathcal{P}
-algebra can be written as a
\mathrm{U}
-split coequalizer of free dg
\mathcal{P}
-algebras. Both
\mathrm{U}^{\mathsf{op}}\cdot(-)^{\circ}
and
(-)^{*}\cdot\mathrm{U}
send these split coequalizers to split equalizers of dg modules, therefore the monomorphism
\epsilon
extends to all dg
\mathcal{P}
-algebras.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
B
be a dg
\mathcal{P}
-algebra which is degree-wise finite dimensional and bounded above or bounded below. There exists a canonical dg
\mathcal{P}
-coalgebra on
B^{*}
and furthermore the natural map

\epsilon:\mathrm{U}^{\mathsf{op}}\cdot B^{\circ}\rightarrowtail B^{*}\cdot\mathrm{U}~

is an isomorphism of dg modules.

Proof.

Under those hypothesis, the natural inclusion
(B^{*})^{\otimes n}\rightarrowtail(B^{\otimes n})^{*}
is an isomorphism, hence the natural map
\mathrm{Coend}_{B}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}_{B^{*}}
is an isomorphism. Therefore, the linear dual
B^{*}
admits a dg
\mathcal{P}
-coalgebra structure and it can be checked that it satisfies the universal property of the Sweedler dual, hence the map of Proposition
2.5
is indeed an isomorphism.
∎

Remark 2.7

(Beck–Chevalley condition)
.

The adjunction
(-)^{\circ}\dashv(-)^{*}
restricts to an anti-equivalence of categories between the category of total finite dimensional dg
\mathcal{P}
-algebra and the category of total finite dimensional dg
\mathcal{P}
-coalgebras, and more generally, between degree-wise finite dimensional bounded-above (resp. bounded-below) algebras and bounded-below (resp. bounded-above) coalgebras.

Example 2.8
.

Consider the operad
\mathcal{A}ss
which encodes dg associative algebras as its algebras and dg coassociative coalgebras as its coalgebras. Then the adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{A}ss\text{-}\mathsf{alg}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{A}ss\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}~.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\circ}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

coincides with the original Sweedler adjunction constructed in
[
Swe69
]
.

2.2.
Topological dual functor

Let’s turn to the other side of the Koszul duality, where
\mathcal{C}
is a conilpotent curved cooperad.

Lemma 2.9
.

The linear duality defines a functor

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{pdg}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{pdg}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}

from the category of pdg
\mathcal{C}
-coalgebras to the category of complete pdg
\mathcal{C}
-algebras.

Proof.

Let
(D,\Delta_{D},d_{D})
be a pdg
\mathcal{C}
-coalgebra, where

\Delta_{D}:D\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{C}(n)\otimes_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}D^{\otimes n}

is the structural morphism. By applying linear duality, we get a map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{D^{*}}:\displaystyle\prod_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}\left(\mathcal{C}(n),(D^{*})^{\otimes n}\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\prod_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}\left(\mathcal{C}(n),(D^{\otimes n})^{*}\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{*}~.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\Delta_{D})^{*}}

One can check that it defines a pdg
\mathcal{C}
-algebra structure on
D^{*}
. Furthermore, let

\mathrm{F}_{\omega}D\coloneqq\mathrm{Ker}\left(\Delta_{D}^{\omega}:D\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{C}/\mathscr{R}_{\omega}\mathcal{C}(n)\otimes_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}D^{\otimes n}\right)

be the canonical coradical filtration on
D
induced by the coradical filtration on
\mathcal{C}
. Since
\mathcal{C}
is conilpotent, the coradical filtration of any dg
\mathcal{C}
-coalgebra is exhaustive, therefore

D\cong\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_{\omega}\mathrm{F}_{\omega}D~,

which in turn implies that

D^{*}\cong\lim_{\omega}~(\mathrm{F}_{\omega}D)^{*}~.

One can check that
(\mathrm{F}_{\omega}D)^{*}\cong D^{*}/\mathrm{W}_{\omega}D^{*}
, therefore the image of the linear duality functor
(-)^{*}
lies in the sub-category of complete pdg
\mathcal{C}
-coalgebras.
∎

Example 2.10
.

Lemma
2.9
provides us with a wealth of examples of absolute algebras. Indeed, it shows that every time one takes the linear dual of some type of conilpotent coalgebras, one gets some type of absolute algebras.

For instance, if one considers the linear dual of the global sections
\mathcal{O}_{G}(G)^{*}
, where
\mathcal{O}_{G}
is the structural sheaf of a (possibly pro)-unipotent algebraic group
G
, then one gets an absolute associative algebra with a compatible coalgebraic structure.

Proposition 2.11
.

The linear duality functor

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{pdg}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{pdg}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}

admits a left adjoint.

Proof.

We consider the following square of functors

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{pdg}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{pdg}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{pdg}~\mathsf{mod}^{\mathsf{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{pdg}~\mathsf{mod}~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{U}^{\mathsf{op}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{C})(-)\right)^{\mathsf{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\mathcal{C})(-)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

where
\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{C})(-)
is the cofree pdg
\mathcal{C}
-coalgebra functor and where
\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\mathcal{C})(-)
is the free pdg
\mathcal{C}
-algebra functor, which is always complete. Again, vertical adjunctions are monadic and it is clear that
(-)^{*}\cdot\mathrm{U}^{\mathsf{op}}\cong\mathrm{U}\cdot(-)^{*}~.
Thus we can apply the Adjoint Lifting Theorem
[
Joh75
, Theorem 2]
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Definition 2.12

(Topological dual functor)
.

The
topological dual functor

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{pdg}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{pdg}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\vee}}

is defined as the functor left adjoint to the linear dual functor.

Example 2.13
.

Let us explain the name of this functor via an example. Let us consider a particular case of this adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{abs}~\mathsf{assoc}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{coassoc}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}^{\mathsf{conil}}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\vee}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

between conilpotent non-counital coassociative coalgebras and complete absolute associative algebras. See Subsection
4.2
for more details about complete absolute associative algebras. Let
A
be a
finitely generated
complete absolute associative algebra, meaning
A/W_{1}A
is finite dimensional over
\Bbbk
. Then, for any
\omega\geq 1
,
A/W_{\omega}A
is a finite dimensional nilpotent
\Bbbk
-algebra and the topological dual of
A
is given by

A^{\vee}=\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_{\omega}\left(A/W_{\omega}A\right)^{*}~.

In fact, this adjunction is an equivalence between finitely cogenerated conilpotent non-counital coassociative coalgebras and finitely generated complete absolute associative algebras.

Remark 2.14
.

Given a complete pdg
\mathcal{C}
-algebra
(A,\gamma_{A},d_{A})
, its topological dual
A^{\vee}
is given by the following equalizer:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Eq}\Bigg(\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{C})(A^{*})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{C})\left((\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\mathcal{C})(A))^{*}\right)\Bigg)~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\gamma_{A})^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varrho}

where
\varrho
is an arrow constructed using the comonadic structure of
\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{C})(-)
and the canonical inclusion of a pdg module into its double linear dual. In particular, for any pdg module
V
, we have an isomorphism:

\left(\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\mathcal{C})(V)\right)^{\vee}\cong\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{C})(V^{*})~.

Proposition 2.15
.

There is a natural monomorphism

\epsilon:\mathrm{U}^{\mathsf{op}}\cdot(-)^{\vee}\rightarrowtail(-)^{*}\cdot\mathrm{U}~,

which implies that the topological dual is a sub-pdg module of the linear dual functor.

Proof.

This map is given by the mate of the natural isomorphism

\epsilon^{\dagger}:\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{C})\cdot(-)^{*}\longrightarrow(-)^{\vee}\cdot\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\mathcal{C})

which makes the square of adjunctions in the proof of Proposition
2.11
commute. It suffices to check that
\epsilon
is a monomorphism on free complete pdg
\mathcal{C}
-algebras. Indeed, since any pdg
\mathcal{C}
-algebra can be written as a
\mathrm{U}
-split coequalizer of free pdg
\mathcal{C}
-algebras and since both composites
\mathrm{U}^{\mathsf{op}}\cdot(-)^{\vee}
and
(-)^{*}\cdot\mathrm{U}
send these split coequalizers to split equalizers of pdg modules, then the map will be a monomorphism for all pdg
\mathcal{C}
-algebras.

Furthermore, we may assume that the conilpotent curved cooperad
\mathcal{C}
is free as a graded
\mathbb{S}
-module, meaning there is an isomorphism
\mathcal{C}(n)\cong\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{gr}}(n)\otimes\Bbbk[\mathbb{S}_{n}]
of graded
\mathbb{S}_{n}
-modules for all
n\geq 0
. This is because over a characteristic zero field, there is always a free graded
\mathbb{S}
-module
M
together with a monomorphism
\phi_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{C}\rightarrowtail M
, by the fact that every graded
\mathbb{S}
-module is arity-wise projective. And since the following square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{C})(V^{*})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\mathcal{C})(V)\right)^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{S}(M)(V^{*})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(M)(V)\right)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{S}(\phi_{\mathcal{C}})(\mathrm{id})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\phi_{\mathcal{C}})(V)\right)^{*}}

commutes, if the bottom horizontal map is a monomorphism so is the top horizontal map.

So let’s assume that
\mathcal{C}
is free as a graded
\mathbb{S}
-module, the map
\epsilon_{\mathcal{C}}
can be written down as the following composition

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{gr}}(n)\otimes_{\Bbbk}(V^{*})^{\otimes n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{Hom}_{\Bbbk}\left(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{gr}}(n),V^{\otimes n}\right)^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\left(\prod_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{Hom}_{\Bbbk}\left(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{gr}}(n),V^{\otimes n}\right)\right)^{*}~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\epsilon_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\epsilon_{2}}

where the first map
\epsilon_{1}
is obtained as the adjoint of the following composition of evaluation maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{gr}}(n)\otimes_{\Bbbk}(V^{*})^{\otimes n}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathrm{Hom}_{\Bbbk}\left(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{gr}}(n),V^{\otimes n}\right)^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(V^{*})^{\otimes n}\otimes_{\Bbbk}V^{\otimes n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Bbbk~,}

along the tensor-hom adjunction and where the second map
\epsilon_{2}
is the canonical inclusion of the direct sum of linear duals to the linear dual of the product. The map
\epsilon_{2}
is a monomorphism since any linear functional which is sent to zero must it self be zero, as it only acts in one component of the infinite product. Let us fix some
n\geq 0
and let us take a linear basis for
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{gr}}(n)
, writing it as
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{gr}}(n)\cong\bigoplus_{i\in I}\Bbbk
, then the map
\epsilon_{1}
in the arity
n
component is given by

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i\in I}(V^{*})^{\otimes n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I}\left(V^{\otimes n}\right)^{*}}

which is itself the composite of the canonical inclusion of the direct sum into the product and of the tensor product of the linear duals into the linear duals of the tensor products, which are both monomorphisms. Hence
\epsilon_{1}
is also a monomorphism and the result follows.
∎

Proposition 2.16
.

The adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{pdg}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{pdg}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\vee}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

restricts to an adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{curv}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{curv}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}~.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\vee}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

Proof.

Denote
\Theta_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\I
the curvature of
\mathcal{C}
. Let
(D,\Delta_{D},d_{D})
be a pdg
\mathcal{C}
-coalgebra. Recall that it is curved if the following diagram commutes

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{C})(D)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D\cong\mathscr{S}(\I)(D)~.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta_{D}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-d_{D}^{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{S}(\Theta_{\mathcal{C}})(\mathrm{id})}

Therefore we have

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\mathcal{C})(D^{*})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{C})(D)\right)^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\I)(D^{*})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathscr{S}(\I)(D)\right)^{*}\cong D^{*}~.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\Delta_{D})^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\Theta_{\mathcal{C}})(\mathrm{id})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-d_{D}^{2})^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathscr{S}(\Theta_{\mathcal{C}})(\mathrm{id}))^{*}}

The left square commutes by naturality of the inclusion. The right triangle commutes since it is the image of a commutative triangle by the functor
(-)^{*}
. Thus the big diagram commutes. Finally, one can observe that
(-d_{D}^{2})^{*}=d_{D^{*}}^{2}
. Therefore
D^{*}
is indeed a complete curved
\mathcal{C}
-algebra.

Let
(A,\gamma_{A},d_{A})
be a complete curved
\mathcal{C}
-algebra. Consider the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\mathcal{C})(A)\right)^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\I)(A)\right)^{*}\cong A^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{C})(A^{\vee})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{S}(\I)(A^{\vee})\cong A^{\vee}~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\Theta_{\mathcal{C}})(\mathrm{id})\right)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\gamma_{A})^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(d_{A}^{2})^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{S}(\Theta_{\mathcal{C}})(\mathrm{id}_{A^{\vee}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta_{A^{\vee}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-d_{A^{\vee}}^{2}}

in the category of pdg modules, where the vertical arrows are given by Proposition
2.15
. The top triangle commutes since it is the image of a commuting triangle via the functor
(-)^{*}
. Each of the vertical faces commutes as well, where
d_{A^{\vee}}^{2}
is simply given by
(d_{A})^{\vee}\circ(d_{A})^{\vee}
. Since every vertical map is a monomorphism, the bottom triangle also commutes and thus
A^{\vee}
is indeed a curved
\mathcal{C}
-coalgebra.
∎

2.3.
The algebraic duality square

Using the two duality adjunctions constructed so far, one can interrelate the "standard" bar-cobar adjunction with the complete bar-cobar adjunction in a square of commuting adjunctions.

Theorem 2.17

(Duality square)
.

The square of adjunctions

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{P}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{curv}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{P}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{curv}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{B}_{\alpha}^{\mathsf{op}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\circ}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega_{\alpha}^{\mathsf{op}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\Omega}_{\alpha}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathrm{B}}_{\alpha}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

commutes in the following sense: right adjoints going from the top right to the bottom left are naturally isomorphic.

Proof.

Let
V
be a graded module. There is an isomorphism

\left(\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P})(V)\right)^{\circ}\cong\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{P})(V^{*})

of graded
\mathcal{P}
-coalgebras. Let
(D,\Delta_{D},d_{D})
be a curved
\mathcal{C}
-coalgebra, let us check the above isomorphism commutes with the differential and thus that we have an isomorphism

\left(\Omega_{\alpha}D\right)^{\circ}\cong\widehat{\mathrm{B}}_{\alpha}(D^{*})

of dg
\mathcal{P}
-coalgebras natural in
D
. For that, let us compute the differential on
\left(\Omega_{\alpha}D\right)^{\circ}
. Since its underlying graded
\mathcal{P}
-coalgebra is cofree, it suffices to check that the projection of this differential onto the cogenerators agrees with the projection of the complete bar construction on
D^{*}
. This in turn follows from the commutativity of the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{P})(D)\right)^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{C})(D)\right)^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\mathcal{P})(D^{*})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\mathcal{C})(D^{*})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{P})(D^{*})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{*}~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathscr{S}(\alpha))^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\Delta_{D})^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\alpha)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{D^{*}}}

where the lower smaller square determines the projection of the differential of the complete bar construction and where the big outer square determines the projection of the differential of the Sweedler dual of the cobar construction, since the Sweedler dual is a sub-dg-module of the linear dual by Proposition
2.5
.
∎

Remark 2.18
.

Let
C
be a dg
\mathcal{P}
-coalgebra. There is a natural isomorphism

\mathrm{B}_{\alpha}C^{*}\cong\left(\widehat{\Omega}_{\alpha}C\right)^{\vee}

of dg
\mathcal{C}
-coalgebras, which follows from the natural isomorphism between their left adjoints given in Theorem
2.17
.

Proposition 2.19
.

There is a natural monomorphism

\zeta:\widehat{\Omega}_{\alpha}\cdot(-)^{\circ}\longrightarrow(-)^{*}\cdot\mathrm{B}_{\alpha}~.

of complete curved
\mathcal{C}
-algebras.

Proof.

This existence of this natural transformation is given by the mate of the natural isomorphism constructed in Theorem

. It is easily seen to be a monomorphism since on the level of graded modules, it can be identified with the following composition of natural inclusion

\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\mathcal{C})(B^{\circ})\rightarrowtail\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\mathcal{C})(B^{*})\rightarrowtail\left(\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{C})(B)\right)^{*}~,

for any dg
\mathcal{P}
-algebra
B
.
∎

Proposition 2.20
.

Let
B
be a dg
\mathcal{P}

\zeta_{B}:\widehat{\Omega}_{\alpha}B^{\circ}\cong\left(\mathrm{B}_{\alpha}B\right)^{*}

is an isomorphism of complete curved
\mathcal{C}

Proof.

It can be directly checked at the level of graded modules that under these finiteness conditions, the map
\zeta_{B}

2.6
and from the natural isomorphism
(B^{\otimes n})^{*}\cong(B^{*})^{\otimes n}
.
∎

2.4.
Homotopical duality square

The duality square of Theorem
2.17
behaves well with respect to model structures. We now restrict to the case where
\mathcal{P}=\Omega\mathcal{C}
, where the curved twisting morphism considered is the canonical curved twisting morphism
\iota:\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\Omega\mathcal{C}
, and where
\mathcal{C}
is a conilpotent curved cooperad. This is in order to ensure the existence of a model category structure on the category of dg
\mathcal{P}
-coalgebras, as in Theorem
1.40
.

Lemma 2.22
.

The adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\Omega\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{dg}~\Omega\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}~.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\circ}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

is a Quillen adjunction.

Proof.

The left adjoint
(-)^{*}
sends degree-wise monomorphisms to degree-wise epimorphisms. Thus it preserves cofibrations. It also preserves quasi-isomorphisms. Therefore we have a Quillen adjunction.
∎

Theorem 2.23

(Homotopical properties of the duality square)
.

All the adjunctions in the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{dg}~\Omega\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{curv}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\Omega\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{curv}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{B}_{\iota}^{\mathsf{op}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\circ}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega_{\iota}^{\mathsf{op}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\Omega}_{\iota}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathrm{B}}_{\iota}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

are Quillen adjunctions.

Proof.

The only thing left to check is that the adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{curv}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{curv}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}~.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\vee}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

is indeed a Quillen adjunction, where the model structure considered on curved
\mathcal{C}
-coalgebras is obtained by transfer along the adjunction
\Omega_{\iota}\dashv\mathrm{B}_{\iota}
and where the model structure considered on the category of complete curved
\mathcal{C}
-algebras is obtained by transfer along the adjunction
\widehat{\Omega}_{\iota}\dashv\widehat{\mathrm{B}}_{\iota}
.

Let us check that
(-)^{*}
is a right Quillen functor. It sends monomorphisms to epimorphisms, thus preserves fibrations. Since every curved
\mathcal{C}
-coalgebra is cofibrant (fibrant in the opposite category), we are left to show that
(-)^{*}
preserves weak equivalences of curved
\mathcal{C}
-coalgebras. Let

f:D_{1}\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}D_{2}

be a weak equivalence of curved
\mathcal{C}
-coalgebras, that is,

\Omega_{\iota}(f):\Omega_{\iota}D_{1}\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\Omega_{\iota}D_{2}

is a quasi-isomorphism of dg
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-algebras, by Lemma
2.22
, we know that the Sweedler dual functor
(-)^{\circ}
is a right Quillen functor. Therefore it preserves weak equivalences between fibrant objects (i.e: quasi-isomorphisms between cofibrant dg
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-algebras). Thus

(\Omega_{\iota}(f))^{\circ}:(\Omega_{\iota}D_{1})^{\circ}\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}(\Omega_{\iota}D_{2})^{\circ}

is a quasi-isomorphism of dg
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-coalgebras. Using the commutativity of the square, we get that

\widehat{\mathrm{B}}_{\iota}(f^{*}):\widehat{\mathrm{B}}_{\iota}D_{1}^{*}\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathrm{B}}_{\iota}D_{2}^{*}

is also a quasi-isomorphism of dg
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-coalgebras. Therefore
f^{*}:D_{1}^{*}\longrightarrow D_{2}^{*}
is a weak equivalence in the model category of complete curved
\mathcal{C}
-algebras.
∎

Remark 2.24
.

There are many examples where one ends up considering the linear dual of a bar construction
(\mathrm{B}_{\alpha}(-))^{*}
. The above square shows that this construction lands naturally in a category of absolute algebras.

For instance, taking the linear dual of the bar construction is crucial in the study of formal moduli problems. J. Lurie’s original approach
[
Lur11
]
involves considering the linear dual of the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex. The linear dual of a bar construction also plays a crucial role in the approach of D. Calaque, R. Campos and J. Nuiten
[
CCN22
]
to formal moduli problems over algebras over a dg operad. Using the above duality square to study formal moduli problems will be the subject of a future work.

The above duality square allows us to state a precise comparison theorem between the homotopy theory of dg algebras over a cofibrant operad and the homotopy theory of dg coalgebras over the same cofibrant operad.

Lemma 2.25
.

Let
B
be a dg
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-algebra whose homology is degree-wise finite dimensional and bounded above or bounded below. Then the derived unit of adjunction

\mathbb{R}(\eta_{B}):B\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\left(\mathbb{R}(B^{\circ})\right)^{*}

is a quasi-isomorphism. Therefore the functor
(-)^{\circ}
is homotopically fully faithful on the full sub-
\infty
-category of degree-wise finite dimensional bounded above dg
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-algebras and on the full sub-
\infty
-category of degree-wise finite dimensional bounded below dg
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-algebras.

Proof.

Let
B
be a dg
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-algebra such that its homology is degree-wise finite dimensional and bounded above. The bounded below case is analogous. Since
\Bbbk
is a field of characteristic zero, using the Homotopy Transfer Theorem, one can replace
B
by its homology
\mathrm{H}_{*}(B)
in order to compute this derived unit of adjunction. Indeed, there is a direct quasi-isomorphism

\Omega(i_{B}):\Omega_{\iota}\mathrm{B}_{\iota}\mathrm{H}_{*}(B)\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\Omega_{\iota}\mathrm{B}_{\iota}B

of dg
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-algebras between their cofibrant resolutions given by the bar-cobar adjunction relative to
\iota
. Here
i_{B}:\mathrm{B}_{\iota}\mathrm{H}_{*}(B)\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\mathrm{B}_{\iota}B
is the
\infty
-quasi-isomorphism given by the choice of a homotopy contraction between
B
and
\mathrm{H}_{*}(B)
. Therefore we obtain the following commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega_{\iota}\mathrm{B}_{\iota}\mathrm{H}_{*}(B)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega_{\iota}\mathrm{B}_{\iota}B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces((\Omega_{\iota}\mathrm{B}_{\iota}\mathrm{H}_{*}(B))^{\circ})^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces((\Omega_{\iota}\mathrm{B}_{\iota}B)^{\circ})^{*}~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}(\eta_{\mathrm{H}_{*}(B)})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega(i_{B})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}(\eta_{B})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces((\Omega(i_{B}))^{\circ})^{*}}

Using 2 out of 3, we conclude that
\mathbb{R}(\eta_{B})
is a quasi-isomorphism of dg
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-algebras if and only if
\mathbb{R}(\eta_{\mathrm{H}_{*}(B)})
is. We compute that

\left(\Omega_{\iota}\mathrm{B}_{\iota}\mathrm{H}_{*}(B)\right)^{\circ}\cong\widehat{\mathrm{B}}_{\iota}\left(\mathrm{B}_{\iota}\mathrm{H}_{*}(B)\right)^{*}\cong\widehat{\mathrm{B}}_{\iota}\widehat{\Omega}_{\iota}(\mathrm{H}_{*}(B)^{\circ})~,

first using the commutativity of the square of Theorem
2.23
and then by applying Proposition
2.20
, where
\mathrm{H}_{*}(B)^{\circ}
coincides with the linear dual since
\mathrm{H}_{*}(B)
is degree-wise finite dimensional and bounded above. And the unit of the complete bar-cobar adjunction
\widehat{\Omega}_{\iota}\dashv\widehat{\mathrm{B}}_{\iota}

\eta_{\mathrm{H}_{*}(B)^{\circ}}:\mathrm{H}_{*}(B)^{\circ}\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\widehat{\mathrm{B}}_{\iota}\widehat{\Omega}_{\iota}(\mathrm{H}_{*}(B)^{\circ})

is a quasi-isomorphism of dg
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-coalgebras since this adjunction is a Quillen equivalence. We can apply the linear dual functor to this quasi-isomorphism, resulting in the following commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}_{*}(B)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left((\mathrm{H}_{*}(B))^{\circ}\right)^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\widehat{\mathrm{B}}_{\iota}\widehat{\Omega}_{\iota}(\mathrm{H}_{*}(B)^{\circ})\right)^{*}~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}(\eta_{\mathrm{H}_{*}(B)})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\eta_{\mathrm{H}_{*}(B)^{\circ}}\right)^{*}}

which allows us to conclude that, by 2 out of 3, the derived unit
\mathbb{R}(\eta_{\mathrm{H}_{*}(B)})
is indeed a quasi-isomorphism of dg
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-algebras, where the top arrow is an isomorphism by Proposition
2.6
.
∎

Theorem 2.26
.

There is an equivalence of
\infty
-categories

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\Omega\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}^{\mathsf{f.d},\mp}~\left[\mathsf{Q.iso}^{-1}\right]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\Omega\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{f.d},\pm}~\left[\mathsf{Q.iso}^{-1}\right]^{\mathsf{op}}~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\circ}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

between the
\infty
-category of dg
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-algebras with degree-wise finite dimensional and bounded below (resp. bounded above) homology and the
\infty
-category of
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-coalgebras with degree-wise finite dimensional and bounded above (resp. bounded below) homology.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.25
, we know that the Sweedler duality functor
(-)^{\circ}
is homotopically fully faithful restricted to dg
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-algebras with degree-wise finite dimensional and bounded below (resp. bounded above) homology. Using the Homotopy Transfer Theorem for dg
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-coalgebras, which is a particular case of the properadic Homotopy Transfer Theorem of
[
HLV21
]
, we can transfer this coalgebra structure onto the homology. Furthermore, as explained in
[
GL22
, Section 12]
, the existence of an
\infty
-quasi-isomorphism of
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-coalgebras is equivalent to the existence of a zig-zag of quasi-isomorphisms of
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-coalgebras.

Thus we can reproduce the same arguments as in Lemma
2.25
in order to show that the linear duality functor
(-)^{*}
is homotopically fully faithful restricted to dg
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-coalgebras with degree-wise finite dimensional and bounded above (resp. bounded below) homology. It is immediate to see that the adjunction
(-)^{*}\dashv(-)^{\circ}
sends dg
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-coalgebras with degree-wise finite dimensional and bounded above (resp. bounded below) homology to dg
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-algebras with degree-wise finite dimensional and bounded below (resp. bounded above) homology and vice-versa.
∎

Remark 2.27
.

Since any cofibrant dg operad
\mathcal{P}
is a deformation retract of a dg operad of the form
\Omega\mathcal{C}
, Theorem
2.26
also holds for any cofibrant dg operad.

3.
Some comparison results between absolute and classical algebras

The goal of this subsection is to establish some comparison results between the absolute and the classical version of the same algebraic structure and to record some useful statements regarding absolute algebras.

3.1.
Restriction functors and their left adjoints

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a conilpotent curved cooperad. Its linear dual
\mathcal{C}^{*}
is a curved operad in the sense of
[
RiL24a
]
. We start by constructing an adjunction between pdg
\mathcal{C}
-algebras and pdg
\mathcal{C}^{*}
-algebras and show that it restricts to curved algebras on both sides.

Proposition 3.1
.

There is an adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{pdg}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{pdg}~\mathcal{C}^{*}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Res}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\mathrm{Abs}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

between pdg
\mathcal{C}
-algebras and pdg
\mathcal{C}^{*}
-algebras, where the right adjoint
\mathrm{Res}
is given by restricting the structure to finite sums of operations.

Proof.

There is a natural inclusion

\iota_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{C}^{*})(-)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\left(\mathcal{C}^{*}(n)\otimes(-)^{\otimes n}\right)_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}\rightarrowtail\prod_{n\geq 0}\left(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{C}(n),(-)^{\otimes n})\right)^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}=\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\mathcal{C})(-)

which is given by the composition of the natural inclusion of the direct sum into the direct product, the natural inclusion
V^{*}\otimes W\rightarrowtail\mathrm{Hom}(V,W)
and the norm map from coinvariants to invariants, which over a characteristic zero field is an isomorphism. This natural inclusion can be easily checked to be a morphism of monads, and therefore it induces an adjunction between their respective categories of algebras. The right adjoint
\mathrm{Res}
is given by restricting the structural morphism along the inclusion
\iota_{\mathcal{C}}
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

The restriction functor factors as

\mathrm{Res}:\mathsf{curv}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}\longrightarrow\mathsf{curv}~\mathcal{C}^{*}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}~,

between the subcategories of curved algebras on both sides.

Proof.

Let us show that if
A
is a curved
\mathcal{C}
-algebra, its restriction
\mathrm{Res}
is also a curved
\mathcal{C}^{*}
-algebra. This, in turn, follows from the commutativity of the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\mathcal{C})(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\I)(A)\cong A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{C}^{*})(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{S}(\I)(A)\cong A~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\Theta_{\mathcal{C}})(\mathrm{id})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{A}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{A}^{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{\mathcal{C}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{S}(\Theta_{\mathcal{C}^{*}})(\mathrm{id})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{\mathrm{Res}(A)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{A}^{2}}

where
\iota_{\mathcal{C}}
is the natural inclusion constructed in the proof of Proposition
3.1
.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

There is an adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{curv}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{curv}~\mathcal{C}^{*}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Res}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Abs}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

between curved
\mathcal{C}
-algebras and curved
\mathcal{C}^{*}
-algebras.

Proof.

Since, in each case, curved algebras are a reflective subcategory of pdg algebras (see Proposition
1.24
and
[
RiL24a
, Section 1]
), we can apply the Adjoint Lifting Theorem
[
Joh75
, Theorem 2]
to the following square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{curv}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{curv}~\mathcal{C}^{*}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{pdg}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{pdg}~\mathcal{C}^{*}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Res}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Res}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Curv}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\mathrm{Abs}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Curv}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

where
\mathrm{Curv}
denotes the reflector from pdg algebras to curved algebras in each case.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The construction given in the above proposition entails that given a curved
\mathcal{C}^{*}
-algebra, the functor
\mathrm{Abs}
can be computed by first forgetting that it is curved, applying the functor
\overline{\mathrm{Abs}}
and finally applying the reflector
\mathrm{Curv}
. So, a priori, the underlying pdg modules of
\overline{\mathrm{Abs}}
and
\mathrm{Abs}
differ. However, we suspect that, like in Proposition
2.16
, applying the functor
\overline{\mathrm{Abs}}
to a curved
\mathcal{C}^{*}
-algebra gives a pdg
\mathcal{C}
-algebra which is already curved and thus these two functors do in fact coincide.

This adjunction between curved
\mathcal{C}
-algebras and curved
\mathcal{C}^{*}
-algebras also induces an adjunction between complete curved
\mathcal{C}
-algebras and curved
\mathcal{C}^{*}
-algebras.

Proposition 3.5
.

There is an adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{curv}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{curv}~\mathcal{C}^{*}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Res}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{cAbs}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

between curved
\mathcal{C}
-algebras and curved
\mathcal{C}^{*}
-algebras.

Proof.

It suffices to compose the adjunction of Proposition
3.3
with the adjunction between curved
\mathcal{C}
-algebras and complete curved
\mathcal{C}
-algebras, since the later is a reflective subcategory of the former by Proposition
1.21
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Again, we suspect that the functor
\mathrm{Abs}
always lands in complete curved
\mathcal{C}
-algebras and hence the functors
\mathrm{Abs}
and
\mathrm{cAbs}
(which one may call the absolution and the complete absolution functors) do, in fact, coincide.

3.2.
Morphisms between complete curved
\mathcal{C}
-algebras

We show that morphisms between complete curved
\mathcal{C}
-algebras are complete characterized as a limit of morphisms between each of the stages of their canonical filtrations.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
A,A^{\prime}
be two complete curved
\mathcal{C}
-algebras. The set of morphisms of
\mathcal{C}
-algebras is given by

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}}(A,A^{\prime})\cong\lim_{\omega\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}}(A/\mathrm{W}_{\omega}A,A^{\prime}/\mathrm{W}_{\omega}A^{\prime})~,

where
\mathrm{W}_{\omega}A
(resp.
\mathrm{W}_{\omega}A^{\prime}
) denotes the
\omega
-stage of the canonical filtration, as defined in Definition
1.15
.

Proof.

Since
A^{\prime}
is complete, it can be written down as the following limit

A^{\prime}\cong\lim_{\omega\in\mathbb{N}}A^{\prime}/\mathrm{W}_{\omega}A^{\prime}~,

in the category of complete curved
\mathcal{C}
-algebras and therefore we have that

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}}(A,A^{\prime})\cong\lim_{\omega\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}}(A,A^{\prime}/\mathrm{W}_{\omega}A^{\prime})~.

The canonical epimorphism
\pi_{\omega}:A\twoheadrightarrow A/\mathrm{W}_{\omega}A
induces an injection of hom-sets by pre-composing

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}}(A/\mathrm{W}_{\omega}A,A^{\prime}/\mathrm{W}_{\omega}A^{\prime})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}}(A,A^{\prime}/\mathrm{W}_{\omega}A^{\prime})~,

and therefore, if it is also surjective at the level of hom-sets, we will be able to conclude. And this is indeed the case since any map
f:A\longrightarrow A^{\prime}/\mathrm{W}_{\omega}A^{\prime}
sends
W_{\omega}A
to
0
since
f(W_{\omega}A)\subseteq W_{\omega}A^{\prime}
, and therefore factors (uniquely) through
A/\mathrm{W}_{\omega}A
.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

In fact, the category of complete curved
\mathcal{C}
-algebras can be written as a pseudo-limit of the following diagram of categories

\cdots\longrightarrow\mathsf{curv}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\leq\omega}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\mathsf{curv}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\leq 2}\longrightarrow\mathsf{curv}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\leq 1}~,

where
\mathsf{curv}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\leq\omega}
denotes the subcategory of
\omega
-nilpotent complete curved
\mathcal{C}
-algebras, that is, those algebras
A
which satisfy that
A\cong A/\mathrm{W}_{\omega}A
.

3.3.
The absolute algebra structure on the homology of a dg absolute algebra

Recall that when
\mathcal{C}
is a conilpotent dg cooperad (a conilpotent curved cooperad with zero curvature), the category of curved
\mathcal{C}
-algebras is precisely the category of dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebras, and therefore one can wonder whether the homology of such a dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebra inherits a graded
\mathcal{C}
-algebra structure.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a conilpotent dg cooperad and
A
a dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebra.

(1)

The homology
\mathrm{H}_{*}(A)
inherits a graded
\mathrm{H}_{*}(\mathcal{C})
-algebra structure.

(2)

Assume
\mathcal{C}
has a zero differential, meaning
\mathrm{H}_{*}(\mathcal{C})\cong\mathcal{C}
. If the algebra
A
is a complete dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebra, so is
\mathrm{H}_{*}(A)
.

Proof.

Let us prove the first point. Let

\gamma_{A}:\prod_{n\geq 0}\left(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{C}(n),A^{\otimes n})\right)^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}\longrightarrow A~,

denote the structural map of the dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebra
A
. Since it is a morphism of dg modules, it induces a map

\gamma_{\mathrm{H}_{*}(A)}:\mathrm{H}_{*}\left(\prod_{n\geq 0}\left(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{C}(n),A^{\otimes n})\right)^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}\right)\cong\prod_{n\geq 0}\left(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathrm{H}_{*}(\mathcal{C})(n),(\mathrm{H}_{*}(A))^{\otimes n})\right)^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}_{*}(A)~,

where we are using that infinite products, invariants, hom-spaces and tensor products are all exact over a field of characteristic zero and thus commute with the homology functor. This structural morphism endows
A
with a graded
\mathrm{H}_{*}(\mathcal{C})
-algebra structure.

Now, let us prove the second point. First, we start assuming that
A
is a weight-nilpotent dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebra, meaning that there exists an integer
\omega_{0}\geq 0
such that
A\cong A/\mathrm{W}_{\omega_{0}}A
. This is the case if and only if the structural morphism of
A
factors through

\gamma_{A}:\prod_{n\geq 0}\left(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathscr{R}_{\omega_{0}}\mathcal{C}(n),A^{\otimes n})\right)^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}\longrightarrow A~,

where
\mathscr{R}_{\omega_{0}}\mathcal{C}
is the
\omega_{0}
-th stage of the coradical filtration of
\mathcal{C}
. This implies that the structural morphism of
\mathrm{H}_{*}(A)
also factors as an algebra over
\mathscr{R}_{\omega_{0}}\mathcal{C}
, and therefore that
\mathrm{H}_{*}(A)
is also weight-nilpotent of nilpotency class at most
\omega_{0}
. So, in particular,
\mathrm{H}_{*}(A)
is a complete dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebra. Let us deal with the general case now. If
A
is a complete dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebra, then

A\cong\lim_{\omega}A/W_{\omega}A~.

Since a tower of epimorphims of vector spaces indexed by
\mathbb{N}
satisfies the Mittag-Leffler condition, taking the limit commutes with taking the homology and therefore we have:

\mathrm{H}_{*}(A)\cong\lim_{\omega}\mathrm{H}_{*}(A/W_{\omega}A)~,

where each
\mathrm{H}_{*}(A/W_{\omega}A)
is a complete dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebra. Since complete dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebras are stable under limits, this implies that
\mathrm{H}_{*}(A)
is also a complete dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebra.
∎

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
A
be a dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebra. The topology induced by the arity filtration coincides with the topology induced by the canonical filtration. In particular, it is arity-complete if and only if it is complete.

Proof.

For any
n\geq 2
, there is an inclusion
\tau^{\leq n}\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathscr{R}_{n-1}\mathcal{C}
. Indeed, since
\mathcal{C}
is cogenerated in arities
\geq 2
, any element in arity
n
admits at most
n-1
non-trivial decompositions. For any dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebra
A
, this inclusion gives an inclusion
\mathrm{W}_{n-1}A\subseteq\mathrm{F}_{n}A
between the
(n-1)
-th term of the canonical filtration and the
n
-th term of the arity filtration.

The other way around, denote
M
the dg
\mathbb{S}
-module of cogenerators of
\mathcal{C}
and let
m
be the maximal arity of
M
. Since
M
is bounded in arity, then
m
is a finite integer. There is an inclusion
\mathscr{R}_{\omega}\mathcal{C}\subseteq\tau^{\leq\omega.m}\mathcal{C}
, as any element in
\mathcal{C}
which admits
\omega
non-trivial partial decompositions can be at most of arity
\omega.m
. This inclusion induces an inclusion
\mathrm{F}_{\omega.m}A\subseteq\mathrm{W}_{\omega}A
for any dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebra
A
. Since both filtrations are commensurable, they induce the same topology on
A
, and by a standard cofinality argument, the completions with respect to each filtration are isomorphic.
∎

Remark 3.14
.

The

on dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebras can be defined as long as
\mathcal{C}(0)\cong 0
, since this condition guarantees that the arity truncation does define a series of sub-cooperads. However, in general, it will differ from the canonical filtration induced by the weight of the operations in
\mathcal{C}
, even when
\mathcal{C}
is reduced. For example, if
\mathcal{C}
is the bar construction on the commutative operad
\mathcal{C}om
(which encodes absolute

-algebras), there are infinitely-many operations in weight

and in arbitrarily high arities. See
[
RiL24b
, Section 1]
for more details.

Corollary 3.15
.

Let
V
be a dg module. The image of the canonical inclusion

\iota_{\mathcal{C}}(V):\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{C}^{*})(V)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\left(\mathcal{C}^{*}(n)\otimes V^{\otimes n}\right)_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}\rightarrowtail\prod_{n\geq 0}\left(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{C}(n),V^{\otimes n})\right)^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}=\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\mathcal{C})(V)

defines a dense subset of the free dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebra on
V
.

Proof.

Notice that since
\mathcal{C}(n)
is a degree-wise finite dimensional bounded complex and since we are working over a characteristic zero field, the right hand side is isomorphic to

\prod_{n\geq 0}\left(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{C}(n),V^{\otimes n})\right)^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}\cong\prod_{n\geq 0}\left(\mathcal{C}^{*}(n)\otimes V^{\otimes n}\right)_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}~.

Then, the result follows directly from Proposition
3.13
, since the right hand side can be identified with the arity-completion of the left hand side, and the natural inclusion to the completion has a dense image.
∎

Theorem 3.16
.

The restriction functor

\mathrm{Res}:\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{C}^{*}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}

is fully faithful. Therefore dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebras are a reflective subcategory of dg
\mathcal{C}^{*}
-algebras.

Proof.

It is immediate that it is faithful as the restriction functor does not change the underlying chain complex. Let us show that it is full. Let
A
and
A^{\prime}
be two dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebras. We want to show that the map

\mathrm{Res}(A,A^{\prime}):\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}}(A,A^{\prime})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{C}^{*}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}}(\mathrm{Res}(A),\mathrm{Res}(A^{\prime}))

at the level of hom-sets is a bijection. First, let us assume that
A
is free as a (complete) dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebra, meaning there is an isomorphism

A\cong\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\mathcal{C})(V)~,

where
V
is some dg module. Then, on the one hand, we have that

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}}(A,A^{\prime})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{dg}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}}(V,A^{\prime})~.

On the other hand, pre-composition by the canonical inclusion

\iota_{\mathcal{C}}(V):\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{C}^{*})(V)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\left(\mathcal{C}^{*}(n)\otimes V^{\otimes n}\right)_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}\rightarrowtail\prod_{n\geq 0}\left(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{C}(n),V^{\otimes n})\right)^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}=\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\mathcal{C})(V)

induces a map

\iota_{\mathcal{C}}^{*}:\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{C}^{*}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}}(\mathrm{Res}(A),\mathrm{Res}(A^{\prime}))\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{C}^{*}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}}(\mathscr{S}(\mathcal{C}^{*})(V),\mathrm{Res}(A^{\prime}))\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{dg}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}}(V,A^{\prime})~,

and since by Corollary
3.15
the image of
\iota_{\mathcal{C}}
is dense, then pre-composing by
\iota_{\mathcal{C}}
is injective. This, in turn, implies that
\mathrm{Res}(A,A^{\prime})
is bijective when
A
is free. The general case follows from the free case using the following. First, we use that any complete dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebra can be written down as a
\mathrm{U}
-split coequalizer of free dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebras and secondly we use that the restriction functor preserves
\mathrm{U}
-split coequalizers since it commutes with the forgetful functors.
∎

Remark 3.17
.

A direct consequence of Theorem
3.16
is that complete dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebras can be identified with the category of algebras over an idempotent monad in the category dg
\mathcal{C}^{*}

.

The adjunction

Proposition 3.24
.

The arity completion of a dg

-algebra admits a canonical complete dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebra structure. It defines a functor

\widehat{(-)}_{a}:\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{C}^{*}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}\longrightarrow\mathsf{dg}~\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}~.

Proof.

By Proposition

, the arity completion is given as a limit of complete dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebras, which can be computed in the underlying category of dg modules. Since complete dg

\mathcal{C}

Absolute algebras and arity completions.
By Theorem
3.16
, the category of complete dg
\mathcal{C}
-algebra is given as the category of algebras over the idempotent monad
\mathrm{Res}\mathrm{Abs}
, which lives in the category of dg
\mathcal{C}^{*}
-algebras. One might be tempted to think that the left adjoint
\mathrm{Abs}
to the restriction functor
\mathrm{Res}
is given by the arity-completion functor of dg
\mathcal{C}^{*}
-algebras of Proposition
3.24
and thus that complete dg
\mathcal{C}^{*}
-algebras are equivalent to arity-complete dg
\mathcal{C}^{*}
-algebras. Turns out that both of these statements are false. In fact, the arity-completion functor is not even idempotent in general.

Let us illustrate this with a concrete example. Let us consider
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}om^{*}
. Then, as explained in Example
3.20
, dg
\mathcal{C}om
-algebras correspond to augmented dg commutative algebras and the functor in Proposition

can be identified with the
I
-adic completion of the augmentation ideal.

The
I
-adic completion functor is not idempotent and therefore can not coincide with the functor
\mathrm{Abs}
. Consider
\Bbbk[x_{1},x_{2},\cdots]
the free polynomial algebra in a countable number of variables. Then its
I
-adic completion with respect to its augmentation ideal is given by the algebra of formal power series
\Bbbk[[x_{1},x_{2},\cdots]]
in a countable number of variables. This algebra is not
I
-adically complete with respect to its augmentation ideal, as it is explained in
[

, 110.7 Noncomplete completion]
. However, since any finitely generated
\Bbbk

I
-adic completion is indeed idempotent on finitely generated dg
\mathcal{C}om
-algebras and thus coincides with the functor
\mathrm{Abs}
in this particular case.

4.
Examples: contramodules and absolute associative algebras

The goal of this section is to illustrate the new notion of an absolute algebras through various examples. First, we show that the theory of
contramodules
is a particular case of the theory of absolute algebras. Then, we explore the notion of absolute associative algebras and absolute Lie algebras and explain how well-known constructions are instances of these structures.

4.1.
Contramodules

The notion of a
contramodule
over a dg counital coassociative coalgebra appears naturally as the dual definition of a comodule. This notion was introduced in the seminal work of S. Eilenberg and J. C. Moore in
[

. After being almost forgotten for a number of years, this notion reemerged in the work of L. Positselski, see
[
Pos21
]
for a extensive account. In this subsection, we show that contramodules appear as algebras over cooperads concentrated in arity one. This allows us to subsume the theory of contramodules, obtaining many examples of interest of "absolute algebras". To the best of our knowledge, the duality square stated in the previous section provides also a new result in the context of contramodules.

admits a
canonical decreasing filtration
given by

\mathrm{W}_{\omega}M\coloneqq\mathrm{Im}\left(\gamma_{M}^{\geq\omega}:\mathrm{Hom}\left(\overline{C}/\mathscr{R}_{\omega}\overline{C},M\right)\longrightarrow M\right)~,

where
\mathscr{R}_{\omega}\overline{C}
denotes the
\omega

M
is
complete
if the canonical morphism of dg
C
-contramodules

\varphi_{M}:M\twoheadrightarrow\lim_{\omega}M/\mathrm{W}_{\omega}M

is an isomorphism. Again, this is a particular case of the more general notion of canonical filtration for algebras over conilpotent cooperads given in Definition
1.15
.

Counter-Example 4.7
.

Consider again the cofree conilpotent coalgebra
\Bbbk[t]^{c}
cogenerated by a single element
t
of degree zero. There exists a
\Bbbk[t]^{c}
-contramodule
M
and a family of elements
\{m_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
in
M
such that

\gamma_{M}\left(m_{n}\otimes t^{n}\right)=0\quad\text{for all}\quad n\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma_{M}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}m_{n}\otimes t^{n}\right)\neq 0~.

The contramodule
M
is constructed as follows. Let
V=\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}\Bbbk.e_{n}
be the vector space spanned by a countable basis and let us consider the free
\Bbbk[t]^{c}
-contramodule on
V
given by
\Bbbk[[t]]~\widehat{\otimes}~V
. We define the following injective endomorphism of this free contramodule

\sum_{n\geq 1}f_{n}(t)e_{n}\mapsto\sum_{n\geq 1}t^{n}.f_{n}(t)e_{n}~,

where
\{f_{n}(t)\}_{n\geq 0}
are the coefficients in
\Bbbk[[t]]
, converging to
0
in the topology of
\Bbbk[[t]]
, of a generic element in this free contramodule. The contramodule
M
is given by the cokernel of this injective endormophism. The family of elements
\{m_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is then given by
m_{n}=\overline{e}_{n}
, where
\overline{e}_{n}
is the image of the basis
e_{n}
in this cokernel. Then
t_{n}.m_{n}=0
is clearly zero in this cokernel, however
\sum_{n\geq 1}t_{n}.m_{n}
is not zero. This is because the element
\sum_{n\geq 1}e_{n}
is not well-defined in
\Bbbk[[t]]~\widehat{\otimes}~V
. It follows that the topology induced by the canonical filtration on
M
is not complete. See
[
Pos21
, Section 1.5]
for more details on this construction.

Let
A
be a dg unital associative algebra (viewed as a dg operad concentrated in arity one), and let
C
be a dg conilpotent counital coassociative algebra (viewed as a conilpotent dg cooperad concentrated in arity one). Let
\alpha:C\longrightarrow A
be a twisting morphism between them. It induces a first adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~C\text{-}\mathsf{comod}_{l}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~A\text{-}\mathsf{mod}_{l}~.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A\otimes_{\alpha}-}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C\otimes_{\alpha}-}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

between the category of left dg

-comodules and the category of left dg
A
-modules, where
\otimes_{\alpha}
denotes the twisted tensor product, see
[
LV12
, Section 2.1]
for an account of this twisted tensor product. One can check that this adjunction is nothing but the classical bar-cobar adjunction relative to
\alpha
when we view
A
as a dg operad concentrated in arity one and
C
as a dg conilpotent cooperad concentrated in arity one. When we endow the category of left dg
A

projective model structure
and the category of left dg
C
-comodules with the transferred model structure, this becomes a Quillen adjunction.

Remark 4.8
.

In fact, when one considers the above adjunction relative to the canonical twisting morphism
\iota:C\longrightarrow\Omega(C)
, L. Positselski showed that it induces a Quillen equivalence even when the coalgebra

is not conilpotent. For these results, see
[
Pos11
, Section 6.7]
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
A
be a dg unital associative algebra viewed as a dg operad concentrated in arity one. The category of dg

-coalgebras is isomorphic to the category of right dg
A
-modules. Furthermore, the cofree right dg
A
-module functor is given by
\mathrm{Hom}(A,-)
.

Proof.

It is a straightforward computation.
∎

Remark 4.10
.

When

is the group algebra
\Bbbk[G]
for some group

, the functor
\mathrm{Hom}(\Bbbk[G],-)
is usually called the

.

The twisting morphism

between the category of complete right dg

and the category of complete right dg

.

The following square diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~A\text{-}\mathsf{mod}_{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~C\text{-}\mathsf{contra}_{r}^{\mathsf{comp}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C\otimes_{\alpha}-}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\circ}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A\otimes_{\alpha}-}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Hom}^{\alpha}(C\text{,}-)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Hom}^{\alpha}(A\text{,}-)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of Theorem

.
∎

One can view the adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{dg}~C\text{-}\mathsf{comod}_{l}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~C\text{-}\mathsf{contra}_{r}^{\mathsf{comp}}~.}

as a contravariant version of the

Remark 4.12
.

The data of a curved cooperad concentrated in arity one is equivalent to the data of a curved counital coassociative coalgebra. This subsection can be generalized

to the curved setting.

4.2.
Absolute associative algebras and absolute Lie algebras

In this subsection, we explore the notion of algebras over the conilpotent cooperad
\mathcal{A}ss^{*}
, which we call
absolute associative algebras
. We compare this category with the standard notion of non-unital associative algebras, encoded by the operad
\mathcal{A}ss
. Furthermore, we also introduce
absolute Lie algebras
, encoded by the cooperad
\mathcal{L}ie^{*}
, and construct the universal enveloping absolute algebra functor. These two examples provide supplementary intuition on the notion of an algebra over a cooperad, in one of its simplest cases.

Definition 4.13

(dg absolute associative algebra)
.

A
dg absolute associative algebra

(A,\gamma_{A},d_{A})
is the data of a dg
\mathcal{A}ss^{*}
-algebra.

Let us unravel this definition. Recall that a dg
\mathcal{A}ss^{*}
-algebra
(A,\gamma_{A},d_{A})
is the data of a dg module
(A,d_{A})
together with a structural morphism of dg modules

\gamma_{A}:\displaystyle\prod_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}(\mathcal{A}ss^{*}(n),A^{\otimes n})\longrightarrow A~,

which satisfies the axioms of Definition
1.13
.

Lemma 4.14
.

There is an isomorphism of dg modules

\displaystyle\prod_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}(\mathcal{A}ss^{*}(n),A^{\otimes n})\cong\displaystyle\prod_{n\geq 1}A^{\otimes n}~.

Proof.

The
\mathbb{S}_{n}
-module
\mathcal{A}ss^{*}(n)
is given by the regular representation of
\mathbb{S}_{n}
, for
n\geq 1
, and by
0
, when
n=0
.
∎

Remark 4.15
.

This implies that there is a monad structure on the endofunctor

\prod_{n\geq 1}(-)^{\otimes n}:\mathsf{dg}~\mathsf{mod}\longrightarrow\mathsf{dg}~\mathsf{mod}~,

such that a dg absolute associative algebra is the data of an algebra over this monad.

The structural map
\gamma_{A}
of an dg absolute associative algebra
A
is equivalently given by a morphism of dg modules

\gamma_{A}:\displaystyle\prod_{n\geq 1}A^{\otimes n}\longrightarrow A~.

It associates to any series

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i\in\mathrm{I}_{n}}a_{1}^{(i)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}^{(i)}~,

where the
a_{j}
are elements in
A
and where
\mathrm{I}_{n}
is a finite set, a well-defined element

\gamma_{A}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i\in\mathrm{I}_{n}}a_{1}^{(i)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}^{(i)}\right)\quad\text{in}\quad A~,

without
presupposing any topology
on the dg module
A
.

Remark 4.16
.

For a general dg absolute associative algebra
(A,\gamma_{A},d_{A})
, notice that

\gamma_{A}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i\in\mathrm{I}_{n}}a_{1}^{(i)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}^{(i)}\right)\neq\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i\in\mathrm{I}_{n}}\gamma_{A}\left(a_{1}^{(i)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}^{(i)}\right)~,

as the latter sum is not even well-defined in
A
.

Differential condition:

The condition that
\gamma_{A}
is a morphism of dg modules can be rewritten as

\small\gamma_{A}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i\in\mathrm{I}_{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}a_{1}^{(i)}\otimes\cdots\otimes d_{A}\left(a_{j}^{(i)}\right)\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}^{(i)}\right)=d_{A}\left(\gamma_{A}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i\in\mathrm{I}_{n}}a_{1}^{(i)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}^{(i)}\right)\right)~,

for any series in
\prod_{n\geq 1}A^{\otimes n}
.

Associativity condition:

The condition that the structural morphism
\gamma_{A}
defines an algebra over the monad
\prod_{n\geq 1}(-)^{\otimes n}
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\gamma_{A}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}\gamma_{A}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1}\geq 1}\sum_{j_{1}\in\mathrm{I}^{(1)}_{i_{1}}}a_{1}^{(1,j_{1})}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i_{1}}^{(1,j_{1})}\right)\otimes\cdots\otimes\gamma_{A}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{i_{k}\geq 1}\sum_{j_{k}\in\mathrm{I}^{(k)}_{i_{k}}}a_{1}^{(k,j_{k})}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i_{k}}^{(k,j_{k})}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{A}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{k}=n}\sum_{j_{1}\in\mathrm{I}^{(1)}_{i_{1}}~,\cdots~,j_{k}\in\mathrm{I}^{(k)}_{i_{k}}}a_{1}^{(1,j_{1})}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i_{k}}^{(1,j_{1})}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n-i_{k}}^{(k,j_{k})}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}^{(k,j_{k})}\right)~.

Example 4.17
.

Let
(V,d_{V})
be a dg module. The
free
dg absolute associative algebra on
V
is given by

\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{~\wedge}(V)\coloneqq\prod_{n\geq 1}V^{\otimes n}~,

that is, the completed non-unital tensor algebra on
V
. If
V
is a
\Bbbk
-vector space of dimension
n
, this algebra is the algebra of non-commutative formal power series in
n
variables without constant terms.

Remark 4.19

(Eilenberg–Mazur swindle and units)
.

Suppose that there was a "unital" version of absolute associative algebras, suppose
A
was one of those and take
a\in A
. If we denote
1
the unit of
A
, then we can consider the image of the following sum in
A

\gamma_{A}\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\underbrace{1\otimes\cdots\otimes 1}_{n-1}\otimes a\right)=\gamma_{A}\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}a\right)~.

Now, using the associativity, we can prove that

\gamma_{A}\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}a\right)=\gamma_{A}\left(\gamma_{A}\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}a\right)\otimes 1\right)=\gamma_{A}\left(\sum_{n\geq 2}a\otimes 1\right)=\gamma_{A}\left(\sum_{n\geq 2}a\right)~.

Therefore we get that

\gamma_{A}\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}a\right)=a+\gamma_{A}\left(\sum_{n\geq 2}a\right)=a+\gamma_{A}\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}a\right)~,

which implies that
a=0
.

absolute associative algebras
with (non-unital) associative algebras.

.

There is an adjunction

Completeness:

Any dg absolute associative algebra
(A,\gamma_{A},d_{A})
comes equipped with a canonical decreasing filtration given by

\mathrm{W}_{\omega}A\coloneqq\mathrm{Im}\left(\gamma_{A}^{\geq\omega}:\prod_{n\geq\omega+1}A^{\otimes n}\longrightarrow A\right)~.

This filtration is the same filtration defined in Definition
1.15
since the cooperad
\mathcal{A}ss^{*}
is a binary cooperad. A dg absolute associative algebra
(A,\gamma_{A},d_{A})
is therefore said to be
complete
if the canonical epimorphism

\varphi_{A}:A\twoheadrightarrow\lim_{\omega}A/\mathrm{W}_{\omega}A

is an isomorphism of dg absolute associative algebras. Composing the adjunction of Proposition
4.21
with the completion functor produces an adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathsf{abs}~\mathsf{assoc}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathsf{assoc}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Res}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{cAbs}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

between complete dg absolute associative algebras and dg associative algebras. By applying Theorem
3.16
, we get that the restriction functor
\mathrm{Res}
here is fully faithful. Thus complete dg absolute associative algebras are a reflective subcategory of dg associative algebras.

Remark 4.22
.

Let
(A,\gamma_{A},d_{A})
be a complete dg absolute associative algebra, then

\gamma_{A}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i\in\mathrm{I}_{n}}a_{1}^{(i)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}^{(i)}\right)=\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i\in\mathrm{I}_{n}}\gamma_{A}\left(a_{1}^{(i)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}^{(i)}\right)~,

using the completeness of
A
.

Completion of associative algebras.
Similarly, when
(B,\gamma_{B},d_{B})
is a dg associative algebra, one has a canonical decreasing filtration as well given by

\mathrm{F}_{\omega}B\coloneqq\mathrm{Im}\left(\gamma_{B}^{\geq\omega}:\bigoplus_{n\geq\omega+1}B^{\otimes n}\longrightarrow B\right)~.

A dg associative algebra
(B,\gamma_{B},d_{B})
is said to be
complete
if the canonical morphism

\lambda_{B}:B\longrightarrow\widehat{B}=\lim_{\omega}B/\mathrm{F}_{\omega}B

is an isomorphism of dg associative algebras, where the right hand side
\widehat{B}
is its
completion
.

Comparison between the two notions of completion.
So, since on the one hand, one has complete dg absolute associative algebras and on the other, one can complete dg associative algebras, it is natural to wonder if these two constructions agree. We show that while both notions of nilpotency agree, these two categories are not equivalent in general.

Definition 4.23

(Nilpotent dg absolute associative algebra)
.

A dg absolute associative algebra
(A,\gamma_{A},d_{A})
is said to be
nilpotent
if there exists
\omega_{0}\geq 1
such that
\mathrm{W}_{\omega_{0}}A=0
. The integer
\omega_{0}
is called the
nilpotency degree
.

Remark 4.24
.

A nilpotent dg absolute associative algebra is in particular complete.

Definition 4.25

(Nilpotent dg associative algebra)
.

A dg associative algebra
(B,\gamma_{B},d_{B})
is said to be
nilpotent
if there exists
\omega_{0}\geq 1
such that
\mathrm{F}_{\omega_{0}}B=0
. The integer
\omega_{0}
is called the nilpotency degree.

Remark 4.26
.

This definition coincides with the standard notion of nilpotency for associative algebras present in the literature.

Proposition 4.27
.

The data of a nilpotent dg absolute associative algebra is equivalent to the data of a nilpotent dg associative algebra with the same nilpotency degree.

Proof.

Let
\omega_{0}\geq 1
. A nilpotent dg absolute associative algebra
(A,\gamma_{A},d_{A})
with nilpotency degree
\omega_{0}
amounts to the data of an algebra over the monad
\prod_{n=1}^{\omega_{0}+1}(-)^{\otimes n}
. On the other hand, a nilpotent dg associative algebra
(B,\gamma_{B},d_{B})
amounts to the data of an algebra over the monad
\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\omega_{0}+1}(-)^{\otimes n}
. There is an isomorphism of monads

\prod_{n=1}^{\omega_{0}+1}(-)^{\otimes n}\cong\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\omega_{0}+1}(-)^{\otimes n}~,

since the product only involves a finite amount of terms.
∎

Remark 4.28
.

The image of a nilpotent dg associative algebra
(B,\gamma_{B},d_{B})
via the complete absolute envelope functor
\mathrm{cAbs}
is simply given by
(B,\gamma_{B},d_{B})
considered as a complete dg absolute associative algebra. This follows from the general case treated in Proposition
3.23
.

Corollary 4.29
.

The completion of dg associative algebras defines a functor

\widehat{(-)}:\mathsf{dg}~\mathsf{assoc}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}\longrightarrow\mathsf{dg}~\mathsf{abs}~\mathsf{assoc}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}

from the category of dg associative algebras to the category of complete dg absolute associative algebras.

Proof.

Follows from the fact that for any dg associative algebra
B
, its completion

\widehat{B}=\lim_{\omega}B/\mathrm{F}_{\omega}B

is given as a limit of nilpotent dg absolute associative algebras, which are complete dg absolute associative algebras, using that complete dg absolute associative algebras are stable under limits. See Proposition
3.24
for the general case.
∎

So the completion of a dg associative algebra is always a complete dg absolute associative algebra. However, the inverse is not always true, see Subsection
3.4.1
for an explanation of the general case. In terms of filtrations, there is a simple comparison between the two filtrations, but they do not always agree.

Proposition 4.30
.

Let
(A,\gamma_{A},d_{A})
be a dg absolute associative algebra. There is a monomorphism of dg modules

\mathrm{F}_{\omega}\mathrm{Res}(A)\rightarrowtail\mathrm{W}_{\omega}A~.

Therefore, if
A
is a complete dg absolute associative algebra, the topology induced by the canonical filtration on
(\mathrm{Res}(A),\mathrm{Res}(\gamma_{A}),d_{A})
is separated.

Proof.

It is straightforward to notice that
\mathrm{F}_{\omega}\mathrm{Res}(A)
consists of finite sums of products of arity greater than
\omega+1
, hence it is included in
\mathrm{W}_{\omega}A
. For the second point, notice that if
(A,\gamma_{A},d_{A})
is complete, then

\bigcap_{\omega\geq 0}\mathrm{F}_{\omega}\mathrm{Res}(A)\hookrightarrow\bigcap_{\omega\geq 0}\mathrm{W}_{\omega}A=\{0\}~,

hence the canonical morphism of dg associative algebras

\lambda_{\mathrm{Res}(A)}:\mathrm{Res}(A)\longrightarrow\lim_{\omega}\left(\mathrm{Res}(A)/\mathrm{F}_{\omega}\mathrm{Res}(A)\right)

is a monomorphism and the topology induced by
\mathrm{F}_{\omega}\mathrm{Res}(A)
is separated.
∎

Counter-Example 4.31
.

Suppose
(A,\gamma_{A},d_{A})
is a complete dg absolute associative algebra, then
\mathrm{Res}(A)
might not be complete as a dg associative algebra. This is for instance the case of the free absolute associative algebra on a
\Bbbk
-vector space
V\cong\bigoplus_{i\geq 1}\Bbbk.x_{i}
with a countable basis. Its restriction algebra
\mathrm{Res}(\prod_{n\geq 1}V^{\otimes n})
is not complete as a dg associative algebra. Indeed, one can check that the canonical morphism
\lambda_{\prod_{n\geq 1}V^{\otimes n}}
is not an epimorphism in this case, as formal power series

\sum_{i\geq 1}x_{i}^{i+1}=x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{3}+x_{3}^{4}+\cdots

is in
\mathrm{W}_{2}A
but not in
\mathrm{F}_{2}\mathrm{Res}(A)
. This is also very similar to what happens in
[
Sta
, 110.7 Noncomplete completion]
.

A duality square for dg associative algebras.
Let us describe explicitly a duality square one can construct in order to relate dg associative algebras with dg absolute associative algebras.

In the following, we consider dg associative algebras endowed with the standard model structure where weak equivalences are given by quasi-isomorphisms and fibrations by epimorphisms. We endow conilpotent dg coassociative coalgebras with the transferred model structure via the bar-cobar adjunction constructed by K. Lefèvre-Hasegawa in
[
LH03
]
. We endow
non-necessarily conilpotent
dg coassociative coalgebras with the model structure constructed by E. Getzler and P. Goerss in
[
GG99
]
, where weak equivalences are given by quasi-isomorphisms and cofibrations by monomorphisms. This latter structure can be obtained directly using the coassociative coalgebra structure on the interval object of dg modules and the theory developed in
[
HKRS17
]
. Finally, this structure can be transferred via the complete bar-cobar adjuntion onto dg absolute associative algebras, as it was shown in
[
GL22
]
.

Proposition 4.32
.

The square of Quillen adjunctions

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{dg}~\mathsf{assoc}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{dg}~\mathsf{coassoc}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}^{\mathsf{conil}}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathsf{coassoc}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathsf{abs}~\mathsf{assoc}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{B}^{\mathsf{op}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\circ}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega^{\mathsf{op}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\Omega}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathrm{B}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

commutes in the following sense: right adjoints going from the top right to the bottom left are naturally isomorphic.

Proof.

The existence of this commuting square of adjunctions follows directly from Theorem

applied to the twisting morphism

The fact that these adjunctions are Quillen adjunction can be shown using the same arguments as in the proof of Theorem

Remark 4.33
.

This duality square intertwines the classical bar construction of
[
CE56
]
and the classical cobar construction of
[

]

Universal enveloping absolute algebra functor.
As with operads, a morphism of cooperads induces an adjunction between their respective categories of algebras. This allows us to construct the universal enveloping absolute algebra of a dg absolute Lie algebra.

Definition 4.36

(dg absolute Lie algebra)
.

A
dg absolute Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},\gamma_{\mathfrak{g}},d_{\mathfrak{g}})
is the data of a dg
\mathcal{L}ie^{*}
-algebra.

There is a morphism of operads
\mathcal{S}kew:\mathcal{L}ie\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}ss
given by the skew-symmetrization of the associative product. By taking the linear dual
\mathcal{S}kew^{*}:\mathcal{A}ss^{*}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}ie^{*}
, we obtain a morphism of conilpotent cooperads.

Proposition 4.37
.

There is an adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathsf{abs}~\mathsf{Lie}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathsf{abs}~\mathsf{assoc}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Res}_{\mathcal{S}kew^{*}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

between the category of complete dg absolute Lie algebras and the category of complete dg absolute associative algebras. The left adjoint functor
\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}
is called the universal enveloping absolute algebra functor.

Proof.

The morphism
\mathcal{S}kew^{*}:\mathcal{A}ss^{*}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}ie^{*}
induces a natural transformation

\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\mathcal{S}kew^{*}):\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\mathcal{L}ie^{*})\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathscr{S}}^{c}(\mathcal{A}ss^{*})

between the corresponding monads encoding dg absolute associative algebras and dg absolute Lie algebras.
∎

.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\gamma_{\mathfrak{g}},d_{\mathfrak{g}})

Proposition 4.40
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\gamma_{\mathfrak{g}},d_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a nilpotent dg absolute Lie algebra. There is an isomorphism

\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}(\mathfrak{g})\cong\frac{\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{~\wedge}(\mathfrak{g})}{\left(x\otimes y-(-1)^{|x||y|}y\otimes x-[x,y]\right)}

of complete dg absolute associative algebras, where on the right hand side we consider the free absolute associative algebra on
\mathfrak{g}
, modulo the ideal generated by the expression inside the parenthesis.

The following square of adjunctions

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{cAbs}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Skew}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Skew}}

Lemma 5.1
.

It is straightforward from the definition.

Proposition 5.2
.

The morphism of dg operads
\varphi:\Omega\mathcal{A}ss^{*}\longrightarrow\Omega\mathcal{L}ie^{*}
induces a Quillen adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}_{\infty}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}~.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Coind}_{\varphi}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

between the model category of
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
-coalgebras and the model category of
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
-coalgebras.

Proof.

Since both dg operads
\Omega\mathcal{A}ss^{*}
and
\Omega\mathcal{L}ie^{*}
are cofibrant, their respective categories of dg coalgebras admit a left-transferred model structure where weak equivalences are given by quasi-isomorphisms and where cofibrations are given by degree-wise monomorphisms, by Theorem
1.40
. Both categories are comonadic by Theorem
1.29
, and the morphism
\varphi
induces a morphism between their respective comonads. Thus it induces an adjunction. Finally, it is straightforward to check that
\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}
preserves cofibrations and quasi-isomorphisms, since it does not change the underlying chain complex of the
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
-coalgebra.
∎

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
be two complete dg absolute Lie algebras. There exists a zig-zag of weak equivalences of dg absolute Lie algebras

Proof.

It is a direct consequence of Theorem

Let us make more explicit what these weak equivalences of dg absolute associative algebras or dg absolute Lie algebras look like. We state the analogue of

, Proposition 2.5]
for the model structure on algebras over a conilpotent dg cooperad transferred along the complete bar-cobar adjunction.

Proposition 5.6
.

The category of complete absolute dg Lie algebras admits a model structure right-transferred from dg modules, where fibrations are given by degree-wise epimorphisms and weak equivalences by quasi-isomorphisms. Furthermore, the identity induces a Quillen adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{dg}~\mathsf{abs}~\mathsf{Lie}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}},\mathsf{W.eq}\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{dg}~\mathsf{abs}~\mathsf{Lie}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}},\mathsf{Q.iso}\right)~.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Id}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

In particular, any weak equivalence of complete dg absolute Lie algebras transferred from
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
-coalgebras is a quasi-isomorphism.

Proof.

First notice that the dg operad
\Omega\mathcal{L}ie^{*}
is augmented, that is, there is a morphism of dg operads
\varepsilon:\Omega\mathcal{L}ie^{*}\longrightarrow\I
. Now we consider the following commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}ie^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega\mathcal{L}ie^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}ie^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\I~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{L}ie^{*}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varepsilon}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\epsilon_{*}\iota}

where
\varepsilon_{*}\iota
is the push forward of the twisting morphism
\iota
by
\varepsilon
. It induces a commutative square of Quillen adjunctions

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathsf{abs}~\mathsf{Lie}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}\text{-}\mathsf{mod}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathsf{abs}~\mathsf{Lie}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}~.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\Omega}_{\iota}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Coind}_{\varepsilon}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathrm{B}}_{\iota}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\Omega}_{\varepsilon_{*}\iota}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Res}_{\varepsilon}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathrm{B}}_{\varepsilon_{*}\iota}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Id}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

by the functionality of the model structures on complete algebras over cooperads of
[
GL22
, Theorem 10.32]
. One can easily see that the bottom adjunction is the free-forgetful adjunction. The category of dg modules is equivalent to the category of coalgebras over the trivial operad
I
and the bottom complete bar-cobar is just the free-forgetful adjunction. Thus the model structure transferred along this adjunction has quasi-isomorphisms as weak equivalences. Finally, since the identity functor

\mathrm{Id}:\left(\mathsf{dg}~\mathsf{abs}~\mathsf{Lie}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}},\mathsf{W.eq}\right)\longrightarrow\left(\mathsf{dg}~\mathsf{abs}~\mathsf{Lie}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}},\mathsf{Q.iso}\right)

is a right Quillen functor and since every object is fibrant, then it sends weak equivalences of dg absolute Lie algebras to quasi-isomorphisms and therefore any weak equivalence of dg absolute Lie algebras is in particular a quasi-isomorphisms.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

On the other hand, any filtered quasi-isomorphism of complete absolute dg Lie algebras is a weak equivalence in the first model structure by
[
GL22
, Theorem 10.26]
.

Remark 5.8
.

The above proposition can be generalized
mutatis mutandis
to the case of any twisting morphism
\alpha:\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}
between a reduced cofibrant dg operad
\mathcal{P}
and a conilpotent dg cooperad
\mathcal{C}
. In particular, this also holds for weak equivalences of dg absolute associative algebras.

Remark 5.9
.

Model category structures on algebras over a conilpotent dg cooperads behave in an analogous way to what happens with model category structures on coalgebras over conilpotent dg cooperads, as described in
[
DCH16
]
.

Let us explain how to recover the results of
[
CPRNW23
]
from Theorem
5.5
. The first thing to notice is that the
\mathrm{cAbs}\dashv\mathrm{Res}
adjunction between complete dg Lie algebras and dg absolute Lie algebras

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathsf{abs}~\mathsf{Lie}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}~\mathsf{Lie}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}~,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Res}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{cAbs}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

is a Quillen adjunction since
\mathrm{Res}
preserves fibrations and, by Proposition
5.6
, it also preserves weak equivalences. A dg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is
homotopy complete
precisely when the derived unit of adjuction

\mathbb{L}(\eta):\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\mathrm{Res}~\mathbb{L}\mathrm{cAbs}(\mathfrak{g})

is a quasi-isomorphism. Indeed, this derived unit can be computed as

\mathbb{L}(\eta):\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\widehat{\Omega}_{\iota}\mathrm{B}_{\pi}\mathfrak{g}~,

where the right hand side term is indeed the completion of the standard cofibrant resolution
\Omega_{\pi}\mathrm{B}_{\pi}\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\mathfrak{g}
given the classical bar-cobar construction, hence this map is a quasi-isomorphism if and only if this standard resolution is complete. See
[
HH13
]
for more details on homotopy completeness.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}
are two cofibrant dg Lie algebras such that
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})\simeq\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{h})
. Then
\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}(\mathrm{cAbs}(\mathfrak{g}))\simeq\widehat{\mathfrak{U}}(\mathrm{cAbs}(\mathfrak{h}))
, since they are cofibrant. Now, using the commutativity of the square of adjunctions in the proof of Proposition
4.40
together with Theorem
5.5
, it follows that
\mathrm{cAbs}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\mathrm{cAbs}(\mathfrak{h})
are weakly-equivalent as complete dg absolute Lie algebras. So, in particular, when
\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}
are also homotopy complete, it follows that they are quasi-isomorphic, and we recover the result of
[
CPRNW23
]
.

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
be two complete graded absolute Lie algebras. They are isomorphic as complete graded absolute Lie algebras if and only if their universal enveloping absolute algebras are isomorphic.

Proof.

Using Theorems
5.5
, we know that there is a zig-zag of weak equivalences between these complete graded absolute Lie algebras if and only if there is between their universal enveloping absolute algebras. Now, by Proposition
5.6
, we know that these weak equivalences are in particular quasi-isomorphisms. Finally, by Proposition
3.9
, we can pass to the homology and obtain a direct isomorphism in both cases.
∎

Definition 5.14

(Minimal absolute
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}

\mathcal{L}_{\infty}

Theorem 5.15
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
be two complete minimal absolute
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
-algebras. If their universal enveloping absolute
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
-algebras are weakly equivalent, there exists an
\infty
-isomorphism of
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
-algebras between them.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.12
, there exists a zig-zag of weak equivalences of absolute
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
-algebras between the two. Now, since by Proposition
5.13
, any weak equivalence is a quasi-isomorphism, the aforementioned zig-zag is in particular a zig-zag of quasi-isomorphisms of absolute
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
-algebras. By applying the restriction functor to
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
-algebras to this zig-zag, we get a zig-zag of quasi-isomorphisms of
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
-algebras. The existence of such a zig-zag is equivalent to the existence of a direct
\infty
-quasi-isomorphism, which in this case, since both algebras have zero differential, is an
\infty
-isomorphism. See
[
LV12
, Section 10.2.2]
for more details on
\infty
-(quasi)-isomorphisms.
∎

Sharp bounds on aggregate expert error
Aryeh Kontorovich, Ariel Avital

Abstract
We revisit the classic problem of aggregating binary advice from conditionally independent experts, also known as the Naive Bayes setting. Our quantity of interest is the error probability of the optimal decision rule. In the case of symmetric errors (sensitivity = specificity), reasonably tight bounds on the optimal error probability are known. In the general asymmetric case, we are not aware of any nontrivial estimates on this quantity. Our contribution consists of sharp upper and lower bounds on the optimal error probability in the general case, which recover and sharpen the best known results in the symmetric special case. Additionally, our bounds are apparently the first to take the bias into account. Since this turns out to be closely connected to bounding the total variation distance between two product distributions, our results also have bearing on this important and challenging problem.

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}}P^{\prime}(x)\wedge Q^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}}\sqrt{P^{\prime}(x)Q^{\prime}(x)}

(16)

for all positive measures

P^{\prime},Q^{\prime}
on
\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
.
Setting
P^{\prime}=\theta P
and
Q^{\prime}=\bar{\theta}Q
,
we now have that

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}}\theta P(x)\wedge\bar{\theta}Q(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\theta\bar{\theta}}\sum_{x\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}}\sqrt{P(x)Q(x)}.

(17)

Applying the upper bound in
Lemma
7
with
p_{i}=\psi_{i}

and
q_{i}=1-\eta_{i}

and noting that

1-(\psi_{i}-\bar{\eta}_{i})^{2}=4\pi_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}

completes the proof.
∎

4.3
Proof of Theorem
4

As

\mathbb{P}(f^{\textup{\tiny{OPT}}}\neq Y)=\left\|\theta P\wedge\bar{\theta}Q\right\|_{1}
,
we start by writing

\displaystyle\left\|\theta P\wedge\bar{\theta}Q\right\|_{1}\geq\min\left\{\theta,\bar{\theta}\right\}\left\|P\wedge Q\right\|_{1}=\min\left\{\theta,\bar{\theta}\right\}\left\|\operatorname{Ber}(\psi)\wedge\operatorname{Ber}(\bar{\eta})\right\|_{1},

where we used the pointwise inequality

\displaystyle\min\left\{\lambda u,\bar{\lambda}v\right\}\geq\min\left\{\lambda,\bar{\lambda}\right\}\min\left\{u,v\right\}.

Next, we invoke Lemma
8
with

P=\operatorname{Ber}(\psi)

and

Q=\operatorname{Ber}(\bar{\eta})
to obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\operatorname{Ber}(\psi)\wedge\operatorname{Ber}(\bar{\eta})\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left\|\operatorname{Ber}(\psi_{i})\wedge\operatorname{Ber}(\bar{\eta}_{i})\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left[\psi_{i}\wedge\bar{\eta}_{i}+\bar{\psi}_{i}\wedge\eta_{i}\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}2(\pi_{i}\wedge\bar{\pi}_{i}),

where the last inequality is due to (
20
).
By (
19
),

\displaystyle\pi_{i}\wedge\bar{\pi}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\pi_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left|\log\frac{\pi_{i}}{\bar{\pi}_{i}}\right|\right),

whence

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}2(\pi_{i}\wedge\bar{\pi}_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{n}\sqrt{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\log\frac{\pi_{i}}{\bar{\pi}_{i}}\right|}\right).

This proves the claim.
∎

4.4
Proof of Theorem
5

Repeating the argument from Theorem
4
,
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(f^{\textup{\tiny{OPT}}}\neq Y)\geq\min\left\{\theta,\bar{\theta}\right\}\left\|\operatorname{Ber}(\psi)\wedge\operatorname{Ber}(\bar{\eta})\right\|_{1}.

(18)

Setting
\psi_{i}=\eta_{i}=p_{i}

we
invoke (
19
) to obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\operatorname{Ber}(p)\wedge\operatorname{Ber}(\bar{p})\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}}P(x)\wedge Q(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}}\sqrt{P(x)Q(x)}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left|\log\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}\right|\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\prod_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\bar{p}_{i}}\sum_{x\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left|\log\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}\right|\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{n}\sqrt{\prod_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\bar{p}_{i}}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Z\sim\mathrm{Uniform}\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left|\log\frac{P(Z)}{Q(Z)}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2^{n}\sqrt{\prod_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\bar{p}_{i}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Z}\left|\log\frac{P(Z)}{Q(Z)}\right|\right),

where Jensen’s inequality was used in the last step.
Since
P,Q
are product measures, we have

P(Z)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}(Z_{i})

and

Q(Z)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}Q_{i}(Z_{i})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\bar{P}_{i}(Z_{i})
, whence

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left|\log\frac{P(Z)}{Q(Z)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\log\frac{P(Z)}{Q(Z)}\right)^{2}\right]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log\frac{P_{i}(Z_{i})}{\bar{P}_{i}(Z_{i})}\right)^{2}\right]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{i,j\in[n]}\log\frac{P_{i}(Z_{i})}{\bar{P}_{i}(Z_{i})}\log\frac{P_{j}(Z_{j})}{\bar{P}_{j}(Z_{j})}\right]}.

Since
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\log\frac{P_{i}(Z_{i})}{\bar{P}_{i}(Z_{i})}=0

and the
Z_{i}
are independent,
only the diagonal terms survive:

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{i,j\in[n]}\log\frac{P_{i}(Z_{i})}{\bar{P}_{i}(Z_{i})}\log\frac{P_{j}(Z_{j})}{\bar{P}_{j}(Z_{j})}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left|\log\frac{P_{i}(Z_{i})}{\bar{P}_{i}(Z_{i})}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\log\frac{p_{i}}{\bar{p}_{i}}\right)^{2}.

The proof is complete.
∎

4.5
Auxiliary Lemmata

The following
identity and inequality
are elementary:

\displaystyle u\wedge v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{uv}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left|\log\frac{u}{v}\right|\right),\qquad u,v>0,

(19)

\displaystyle s\wedge\bar{t}+t\wedge\bar{s}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2(u\wedge\bar{u}),\qquad s,t\in[0,1],u=(s+t)/2.

(20)

Lemma 8
.

For all probability measures

P,Q,P^{\prime},Q^{\prime}
,
we have

\displaystyle\left\|P\otimes Q\wedge P^{\prime}\otimes Q^{\prime}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left\|P\wedge P^{\prime}\right\|_{1}\cdot\left\|Q\wedge Q^{\prime}\right\|_{1}.

Proof.

It is a classic fact (see, e.g.,
Kontorovich (2012, Lemma 2.2)
)
that

\displaystyle\left\|P\otimes Q-P^{\prime}\otimes Q^{\prime}\right\|_{\textup{\tiny{TV}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|P-P^{\prime}\right\|_{\textup{\tiny{TV}}}+\left\|Q-Q^{\prime}\right\|_{\textup{\tiny{TV}}}-\left\|P-P^{\prime}\right\|_{\textup{\tiny{TV}}}\cdot\left\|Q-Q^{\prime}\right\|_{\textup{\tiny{TV}}}.

The
claim follows
by
Scheffé’s identity (
4
).
∎

Existence of arithmetic degrees for generic orbits and dynamical Lang-Siegel problem
Yohsuke Matsuzawa

Abstract
. We prove the existence of the arithmetic degree for dominant rational self-maps at any point whose orbit is generic. As a corollary, we prove the same existence for étale morphisms on quasi-projective varieties and any points on it. We apply the proof of this fact to dynamical Lang-Siegel problem. Namely, we prove that local height function associated with zero-dimensional subscheme grows slowly along orbits of a rational map under reasonable assumption. Also if local height function associated with any proper closed subscheme grows fast on a subset of an orbit of a self-morphism, we prove that such subset has Banach density zero under some assumptions.

Introduction

For a dominant rational map
f\colon X\dashrightarrow X
on a projective variety defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
,

arithmetic degree
is one of the most fundamental quantity that measure the arithmetic complexity
of orbits.
It is defined using height function
h_{H}
associated with an ample divisor on
X
as follows:
for a point
x\in X(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
whose
f
-orbit is well-defined, i.e.

\displaystyle f^{n}(x)\notin I_{f}\quad n\geq 0

(1.1)

where
I_{f}
is the indeterminacy locus of
f
,

\displaystyle\alpha_{f}(x):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\max\{1,h_{H}(f^{n}(x))\}^{\frac{1}{n}}

(1.2)

is called the arithmetic degree of
f
at
x
, provided the limit exists.
It can be easily checked that this limit is independent of the choice of
H
and
h_{H}
.
Also, we take
\max\{1,\}
to take care the case
h_{H}(f^{n}(x))
is bounded, but as soon as the orbit
is infinite, Northcott theorem says
h_{H}(f^{n}(x))
goes to
\infty
and hence we can get rid of
\max\{1,\}
.

It is conjectured that the arithmetic degree of Zariski dense orbits is equal to another dynamical invariant

d_{1}(f)
, the first dynamical degree (see for example
[
7
,
5
,
20
]
for the definition and basic properties of dynamical degree).

Conjecture 1.1

(Kawaguchi-Silverman conjecture
[
19
,
11
]
)
.

In the above situation,

\alpha_{f}(x)
exists (i.e. the limit exists) and if the orbit
O_{f}(x)=\{x,f(x),f^{2}(x),\dots\}

is Zariski dense in
X
, then
\alpha_{f}(x)=d_{1}(f)
.

We refer
[

for introduction and recent advances on this conjecture.

The first part of the conjecture, that is, the existence of the limit is proven for
self-morphisms on projective varieties
[

In this paper, we prove that the limit exists for
x
whose orbit is
generic

Definition 1.2

A subset

Dynamical Lang-Siegel problem in a classical form asks if
the size of coordinates of orbits are asymptotically the same.
This problem can be reformulated by using local height function in the following form:
for a dominant rational map
f\colon X\dashrightarrow X
defined over a number field
K
,
a proper closed subscheme

, and a point
x\in X(K)
, when do we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{v\in S}\lambda_{Y,v}(f^{n}(x))}{h_{H}(f^{n}(x))}=0\ ?

(1.7)

Here
S
is a finite set of places of
K
,
\lambda_{Y,v}
is a local height function associated with
Y
,
and
h_{H}
is any height function associated with any ample divisor.
See
[
18
,
15
,
25
,
24
]
for some of the results related to this problem.

To state our results on dynamical Lang-Siegel problem,
let us introduce some notation.
Let
K
be a number field.
The standard proper set of absolute values on
K
is denoted by
M_{K}
(see Convention below).
Let
X
be a projective geometrically integral variety over
K
.
For a dominant rational map
f\colon X\dashrightarrow X
defined over
K
, we write

\displaystyle X_{f}(K)=\left\{x\in X(K)\ \middle|\ f^{n}(x)\notin I_{f},\ n\geq 0\right\}.

(1.8)

This is a set of points for which the forward
f
-orbits are well-defined.
Let us introduce a backward version
X_{f}^{\rm back}
, that is

X_{f}^{\rm back}
is, roughly speaking, the largest subset over which
f^{n}
are finite for all
n\geq 0
.
See
definition

3.1
and
section

6
for the formal definition.
For
x\in X
at which
f
is quasi-finite, let

e_{f}(x)=l_{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}(\mathcal{O}_{X,x}/f^{*}\mathfrak{m}_{f(x)}{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x})

(1.9)

where

stands for the length as an

-module,

\mathfrak{m}_{f(x)}
is the maximal ideal of the local ring

,
and

For a finite subset

, we define

\displaystyle e(f;Y):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(\max\{e_{f^{n}}(z)\mid z\in f^{-n}(Y)\}\big)^{1/n}.

(1.10)

See
section

7
for more about this definition.

.

Let

2.
Height growth and arithmetic degree

In this section, we prove

We use the following quantity, introduced and called cohomological Lyapunov exponent in
[
23
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
f\colon X\dashrightarrow X
be a dominant rational map on a projective variety
X
defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.
The
i
-th dynamical degree of
f
is denoted by
d_{i}(f)
for
i=0,\dots,\dim X
.
We set

\displaystyle\mu_{i}(f)=\frac{d_{i}(f)}{d_{i-1}(f)}

(2.1)

for
i=1,\dots,\dim X
and set
\mu_{\dim X+1}(f)=0
.
Let us say

has dynamical peak
p\in\{1,\dots,\dim X\}
if

\displaystyle\mu_{p}(f)>1\ \text{and}\ \mu_{p+1}(f)\leq 1.

(2.2)

Note that having dynamical peak is equivalent to the condition
d_{1}(f)>1
.

By log concavity of dynamical degrees, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(f)\geq\mu_{2}(f)\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{p}(f)>1\geq\mu_{p+1}(f).

(2.3)

The following
theorem

2.2
is

1.3
with some additional
statement.
The key of the proof is

2.4

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
f\colon X\dashrightarrow X
be a dominant rational map on a projective variety
X
defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Let
H
be an ample divisor on
X
and fix Weil height function
h_{H}
associated with
H

Let
x\in X_{f}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})

If

\displaystyle\alpha_{f}(x):=\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{H}(f^{n}(x))^{1/n}

(2.4)

\alpha_{f}(x)
takes one of the following values:

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(f)=d_{1}(f),\mu_{2}(f),\dots,\mu_{\dim X}(f).

(2.5)

Note that

Remark 2.3

Let us note that under the assumption of the theorem,

For

(2.7)

We obviously have

Moreover, we always have

[

[

Remark 2.5
.

In our proof of

of the rational map to a birational model and the locus where the height of our big divisors are negative.

4.
Banach density and dynamical Lang-Siegel problem

In this section, we prove
theorem

1.9
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
A\subset{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
.
The (upper) Banach density of
A
is defined as

\delta(A)=\limsup_{d\to\infty}\max_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}}\frac{\sharp(A\cap[n,n+d])}{d+1}.

(4.1)

We say
A
has Banach density zero if
\delta(A)=0
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
f\colon X\longrightarrow X
be a finite flat surjective morphism on a nice variety
X

defined over a field of characteristic zero.
For a closed subscheme
Y\subset X
, we define

\displaystyle e(f;Y):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup\{e_{f^{n}}(z)\mid z\in f^{-n}(Y)\}^{1/n}.

(4.2)

See, for example,
[
15
, Theorem 4.8]
for the existence of the limit
and basic properties of this quantity.

Note that in this situation,
X_{f}^{\rm back}=X
and we actually have

\displaystyle e(f;Y)=\max_{y\in Y}e_{f,-}(y).

(4.3)

See
[
15
, Theorem 4.8]
.

Theorem 4.3

(
theorem

1.9
)
.

Let
K
be a number field,
X
a nice variety over
K
,
f\colon X\longrightarrow X
a surjective morphism.
Let
S\subset M_{K}
be a finite set of places and
h_{H}
be a height function associated with an ample divisor
H
on
X
.
Let
Y\subset X
be a proper closed subscheme and fix a local height function
\{\lambda_{Y,v}\}_{v\in M_{K}}
associated with
Y
.
Let
x\in X(K)
be a point.
Suppose

(1)

Y
contains no positive dimensional
f
-periodic subvariety;

(2)

e(f;Y)<\alpha_{f}(x)
.

Then if an infinite sequence
\{n_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
satisfies

\displaystyle\liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{v\in S}\lambda_{Y,v}(f^{n_{j}}(x))}{h_{H}(f^{n_{j}}(x))}>0,

(4.4)

then it has Banach density zero.

Proof.

First, we may assume
S
is a singleton.
Indeed, if

\liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{v\in S}\lambda_{Y,v}(f^{n_{j}}(x))}{h_{H}(f^{n_{j}}(x))}>0,

(4.5)

then there is
j_{0}>0
and a positive number

such that

\frac{\sum_{v\in S}\lambda_{Y,v}(f^{n_{j}}(x))}{h_{H}(f^{n_{j}}(x))}\geq c

(4.6)

for all
j\geq j_{0}
.
For
v\in S
, set

A_{v}=\left\{n_{j}\ \middle|\ j\geq j_{0},\frac{\lambda_{Y,v}(f^{n_{j}}(x))}{h_{H}(f^{n_{j}}(x))}\geq\frac{c}{\sharp S}\right\}.

(4.7)

Then
\{n_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}=\{n_{j}\}_{j=1}^{j_{0}-1}\cup\bigcup_{v\in S}A_{v}
.
The singleton case of the theorem says
A_{v}
has Banach density zero, and hence so does
\{n_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
.

Now we assume
S
is a singleton, say
S=\{v\}
.
We proceed by induction on
\dim Y
.
If
Y=\emptyset
, the statement is trivial.
If
\dim Y=0
, then the statement follows from
[
15
, Theorem 1.11]
.

Suppose
\dim Y\geq 1
.
First, note that we may assume
Y
is irreducible.
Indeed, let
Y=Y_{1}\cup\cdots\cup Y_{r}
be the irreducible decomposition.
We equip
Y_{i}
the reduced structure and fix local heights
\lambda_{Y_{i},v}
.
Then there are constants
a,b>0
such that
a\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{Y_{i},v}+b\geq\lambda_{Y,v}
.
If

\liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac{\lambda_{Y,v}(f^{n_{j}}(x))}{h_{H}(f^{n_{j}}(x))}>0,

(4.8)

then there is
j_{0}>0
and a positive number
c>0
such that

\frac{\lambda_{Y,v}(f^{n_{j}}(x))}{h_{H}(f^{n_{j}}(x))}\geq c

(4.9)

for all
j\geq j_{0}
.
This implies

\displaystyle\frac{a\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{Y_{i},v}(f^{n_{j}}(x))+b}{h_{H}(f^{n_{j}}(x))}\geq\frac{\lambda_{Y,v}(f^{n_{j}}(x))}{h_{H}(f^{n_{j}}(x))}\geq c.

(4.10)

By the assumption, we have
\alpha_{f}(x)>1
and this implies
h_{H}(f^{n_{j}}(x))
goes to infinity.
Thus there are
j_{1}\geq j_{0}
and
c^{\prime}>0
such that

\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{Y_{i},v}(f^{n_{j}}(x))}{h_{H}(f^{n_{j}}(x))}\geq c^{\prime}

(4.11)

for all
j\geq j_{1}
.
Set

B_{i}=\left\{n_{j}\ \middle|\ j\geq j_{1},\ \frac{\lambda_{Y_{i},v}(f^{n_{j}}(x))}{h_{H}(f^{n_{j}}(x))}\geq\frac{c^{\prime}}{r}\right\}.

(4.12)

Then
\{n_{j}\}_{j\geq j_{1}}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}B_{i}
.
The theorem for each
Y_{i}
implies each
B_{i}
has Banach density zero.
(We can apply the theorem to
Y_{i}
because
e(f;Y_{i})\leq e(f;Y)
and
Y_{i}
contains no positive dimensional
f
-periodic subvariety.)
Hence
\{n_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
is a finite union of sets with Banach density zero, which is again has Banach density zero.

Now assume
Y
is irreducible.
By the assumption, there is
j_{0}\geq 1
and
\eta>0
such that

\lambda_{Y,v}(f^{n_{j}}(x))\geq\eta h_{H}(f^{n_{j}}(x))

(4.13)

for all
j\geq j_{0}
.
Let us write
P=\{n_{j}\}_{j\geq j_{0}}
.
It is enough to show that
P
has Banach density zero.
Suppose
P
has positive Banach density.
Then by
[
2
, Lemma 2.1]
,
there are
Q\subset P
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
such that

Q
has positive Banach density;

(4.14)

\displaystyle n\in Q\Longrightarrow n+k\in P.

(4.15)

By the functoriality of local height functions, there is a constant
C>0
depending only on

X,f,k
, and
Y
such that
\lambda_{f^{-k}(Y),v}\geq\lambda_{Y,v}\circ f^{k}-C
.
Hence for all

, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{f^{-k}(Y),v}(f^{n}(x))\geq\lambda_{Y,v}(f^{n+k}(x))-C\geq\eta h_{H}(f^{n+k}(x))-C.

(4.16)

We fix a local height associated with

so that

\lambda_{Y\cap f^{-k}(Y),v}=\min\{\lambda_{Y,v},\lambda_{f^{-k}(Y),v}\}
.
Then for all

,

\displaystyle\lambda_{Y\cap f^{-k}(Y),v}(f^{n}(x))

\displaystyle=\min\{\lambda_{Y,v}(f^{n}(x)),\lambda_{f^{-k}(Y),v}(f^{n}(x))\}

(4.17)

\displaystyle\geq\min\{\eta h_{H}(f^{n}(x)),\eta h_{H}(f^{n+k}(x))-C\}.

(4.18)

Dividing by

, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{Y\cap f^{-k}(Y),v}(f^{n}(x))}{h_{H}(f^{n}(x))}\geq\min\left\{\eta,\eta\frac{h_{H}(f^{n+k}(x))}{h_{H}(f^{n}(x))}-\frac{C}{h_{H}(f^{n}(x))}\right\}.

(4.19)

Noticing that

,

is ample, and

goes to infinity, we can see

\liminf_{n\in Q}\min\left\{\eta,\eta\frac{h_{H}(f^{n+k}(x))}{h_{H}(f^{n}(x))}-\frac{C}{h_{H}(f^{n}(x))}\right\}>0.

On the other hand, by the assumption,

is not

-periodic.
Since

is irreducible, we get

.
As

,

does not contain positive dimensional

-periodic subvariety.
We also have

.
Applying induction hypothesis to

, we get

5.
Roth’s theorem

In this appendix, we reformulate Roth’s theorem using local height function associated with subschemes.
For a
0
-dimensional closed subscheme
Y\subset X
of an algebraic scheme
X
, we set

\displaystyle m_{X}(Y)=\max\big\{\min\{i\mid{\mathfrak{m}}_{X,y}^{i}\subset{\mathcal{I}}_{Y,y}\}\mid y\in Y\big\}

(5.1)

where
{\mathfrak{m}}_{X,y}
is the maximal ideal of the local ring
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,y}

and
{\mathcal{I}}_{Y}
is the ideal sheaf of
Y\subset X
.

Theorem 5.1

(Roth’s theorem)
.

Let
K
be a number field.
Let
X
be a reduced projective scheme over
K
such that every irreducible component is positive dimensional,

Y\subset X
a closed subscheme with
\dim Y=0
.
Let
H
be a very ample divisor on
X
.
Fix local height function
\{\lambda_{Y,v}\}_{v\in M_{K}}
associated with
Y
,
and Weil height function
h_{H}
associated with
H
.
Then for any finite subset
S\subset M_{K}
and
\varepsilon>0
, there is a finite subset
Z\subset X(K)

such that

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S}\lambda_{Y,v}(x)\leq m_{X}(Y)(2+\varepsilon)h_{H}(x)

(5.2)

for all
x\in X(K)\setminus Z
.

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S}\lambda_{Y,v}\leq m_{X}(Y)\sum_{v\in S}\lambda_{Y_{\rm red},v}+O(1),

(5.3)

we may assume
Y
is reduced.
Let
K\subset L
be a finite extension such that all the residue fields of
Y_{L}
are
L
.
By replacing
K
with
L
,
Y
with
Y_{L}
,
X
with
X_{L}
,
H
with
H_{L}
,
and
S
with
\left\{w\in M_{L}\ \middle|\ w|v\right\}
, we may assume the residue field of every point of
Y
is
K
.

Let
\Delta\subset X\times X
be the diagonal.
For any
v\in M_{K}
,
y_{1}\neq y_{2}\in Y
, and
x\in X(K)
, we have

\displaystyle\min\{\lambda_{y_{1},v}(x),\lambda_{y_{2},v}(x)\}

\displaystyle\leq\min\{\lambda_{\Delta,v}(y_{1},x),\lambda_{\Delta,v}(y_{2},x)\}+\gamma_{y_{1},y_{2}}(v)

(5.4)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{\pr_{13}^{-1}(\Delta)\cap\pr_{23}^{-1}(\Delta),v}(y_{1},y_{2},x)+\gamma^{\prime}_{y_{1},y_{2}}(v)

(5.5)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{\pr_{12}^{-1}(\Delta),v}(y_{1},y_{2},x)+\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{y_{1},y_{2}}(v)

(5.6)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{\Delta,v}(y_{1},y_{2})+\gamma^{\prime\prime\prime}_{y_{1},y_{2}}(v)

(5.9)

where the implicit constants depend at most on

,

, and the choice of all the local height functions appeared so far.
Therefore it is enough to prove the following.
Let

be a finite subset. Suppose

are given for each

.
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S}\lambda_{y_{v},v}(x)\leq(2+\varepsilon)h_{H}(x)

(5.10)

for all but finitely many

.
By taking closed immersion

defined by
general members of the linear system

and replacing

with
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{K}
,
we may assume

,

, the naive height on
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
, and
y_{v}\in D_{+}(t_{0})
for all

.

are the homogeneous coordinates of

.

Let

be the

-th projection.
Let

be the affine coordinates of

as a

,

(5.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S}\lambda_{y_{v},v}(x)\leq\min_{1\leq i\leq N}\bigg\{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in S\\

(6.17)

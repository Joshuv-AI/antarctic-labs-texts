First-principles molecular quantum electrodynamics theory at all coupling strengths
Xinyang Li, Yu Zhang

Abstract
The ever-growing intersection of quantum electrodynamics (QED) and molecular processes has shown remarkable and unanticipated advancements in altering molecular properties and reactivity by exploiting light-matter couplings. In recent years, multiple ab initio methods have been developed to compute the eigenstates of molecular systems strongly coupled to cavities, ranging from the mean-field to quantum many-body methods. The quantum many-body methods, such as coupled-cluster theories, usually rely on the quality of mean-field reference wavefunctions. Hence, developing efficient and physically reliable mean-filed approaches for molecular quantum electrodynamics problems is crucial. The current widely used methods, such as QED Hartree-Fock and the self-consistent counterpart, are limited to specific coupling regimes. In this work, we developed a variational transformation-based molecular quantum electrodynamics mean-field method, namely VT-QEDHF, for light-matter interaction at arbitrary coupling strength. The numerical benchmark demonstrates that the VT-QEDHF method naturally connects both QEDHF and self-consistent QEDHF methods at the two limits, showcasing the advantage of VT-QEHDF across all coupling strengths.

Despite significant progress in understanding the effects of confined fields on many molecular characteristics, a comprehensive and first-principles framework for exploring these phenomena across all coupling regimes is still lacking. To address this research gap, we introduce a variational transformation

This universal approach is designed to function effectively across arbitrary coupling strengths, thereby providing an invaluable tool for exploring and understanding light-matter interactions in a more comprehensive and efficient manner. The VT-QEDHF method transcends the limitations of traditional perturbative and strong coupling approaches, offering a more universal perspective on molecular processes in QED environments. Within the VT-QEDHF framework, the photonic field contribution is accounted for in a nonperturbative manner, ensuring the attainment of the exact wave function in the limit of infinite coupling, thereby providing a consistent and reliable molecular orbital description across various coupling regimes. This first-principles approach not only captures the electron-photon correlation (at the mean-field level) effectively but also elucidates the cavity effects on the electronic ground state while maintaining a manageable computational cost. By bridging the theoretical gap across coupling strengths, the VT-QEDHF method is anticipated to open new avenues for the study and manipulation of molecular properties and behaviors within QED environments, offering enriched insights and enhancing the predictability and control over light-matter interactions.

II
Theory

The total light-matter Hamiltonian of molecular quantum electrodynamics can be described as the widely used nonrelativistic Pauli-Fierz Hamiltonian in the dipole approximation
Cohen-Tannoudji et al. 1997
;
Mandal et al. 2023
;
Weight et al. 2023a
,

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{PF}}=

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{e}}+\sum_{\alpha}\Big[\omega_{\alpha}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\hat{a}_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2})

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{\alpha}}{2}}\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}+\hat{a}_{\alpha})+\frac{1}{2}(\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})^{2}\Big].

(1)

This Hamiltonian is often referred to as the Pauli-Fierz (PF) Hamiltonian.
Where
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{e}}=\hat{T}_{\mathrm{e}}+\hat{V}
is the bare molecular Hamiltonian (excluding the nuclear kinetic operator) which includes all Coulomb interactions
\hat{V}
between electrons and nuclei as well as the electronic kinetic energy operators
\hat{T}_{\mathrm{e}}
, which is given by the expression,

\hat{H}_{e}=\sum_{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\hat{c}_{\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}I_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\lambda}\hat{c}_{\sigma}\hat{c}_{\nu}.

(2)

Where
h
and
I
are one-electron and two-electron integrals.
\hat{\bm{D}}
in Eq.
II
is the molecular dipole operator,

\hat{\bm{D}}=\sum_{i}^{N_{n}}z_{i}\hat{\bm{R}}_{i}-\sum_{i}^{N_{e}}e\hat{\bm{r}}_{i}\equiv\hat{\bm{D}}_{n}+\hat{\bm{D}}_{e},

(3)

including electronic
\hat{\bm{D}}_{e}
and nuclear
\hat{\bm{D}}_{n}
components.
\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{\epsilon_{0}V}}\bm{e}_{\alpha}\equiv\lambda_{\alpha}\bm{e}_{\alpha}
characterizes the coupling between the molecule and cavity quantized field.
\omega_{\alpha}
and
\bm{e}_{\alpha}
represent the frequency and polarization of the electric field of cavity photon mode
\alpha
. The last term describes the dipole self-energy (DSE), which is essential to ensure the Hamiltonian is bounded from below and displays the correct scaling with the system size
Rokaj et al. 2018
.

The eigenstate of the molecular QED Hamiltonian can be readily obtained by solving the time-independent Schrödinger equation

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{PF}}\ket{\Psi}=E\ket{\Psi},

(4)

where
\ket{\Psi}
is the correlated electron-photon wavefunction, though the exact solution to the above quantum many-body equation is nontrivial.

The mean-field approach is usually the first and fastest method to approximate the quantum many-body problems.
At the mean-field level, the QED Hamiltonian can be approximated by
\ket{\Psi}\approx\ket{\mathrm{HF}}\otimes\ket{0}
where
\ket{0}
denotes the photon vacuum state. Consequently, the total energy can be easily introduced via,

E_{\mathrm{tot}}=E_{HF}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha}\langle(\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})^{2}\rangle.

(5)

Where the
E_{HF}
denotes the electronic HF energy. The DSE contribution to the total energy (second term on the right-hand side of the above equation) can be evaluated via the DSE-mediated one-electron and two-electron integrals (see more details in Supplementary Materials (SM)). Thus, the corresponding Fock matrix (for computing density matrix and molecular orbital properties) can be readily derived by taking the partial derivative of the total energy with respect to the density matrix
Haugland et al. 2020
;
Foley et al. 2023
. The resulting QEDHF method (in the Fock state representation) provides an economical way to compute the polariton ground state and can serve as the reference for other post-HF methods. The key drawback of the QEDHF method in the Fock representation is the slow convergence with the Fock state in the strong coupling limit, which can lead to incorrect behavior, such as incorrect origin-dependency and frequency dependency
Foley et al. 2023
, making the QEDHF method in Fock state representation more suitable for weak coupling systems (as the Fock state is the eigenstate of the interaction Hamiltonian in the
\lambda\rightarrow 0
limit). Such drawbacks can be mitigated with the coherent state (CS) representation
Philbin 2014
,

\ket{z_{\alpha}}\equiv e^{z_{\alpha}\hat{a}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}-z_{\alpha}^{*}\hat{a}_{\alpha}}\ket{0}\equiv\hat{U}(z_{\alpha})\ket{0},

(6)

where
z_{\alpha}=-\frac{\langle\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}\rangle_{\mathrm{HF}}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\alpha}}}
. It’s clear from the above equation that CS is a linear combination of complete Fock states where the coefficients are determined by the displacement due to the light-matter coupling strength. The resulting QEDHF in CS representation
Haugland et al. 2020
thus mitigates the origin-variance problem. However, the molecular orbitals and Fock matrix remain origin-dependent charged systems in
Foley et al. 2023
.

Only recently, a fully origin-invariant formulation was developed within a self-consistent strong coupling QEDHF formalism (namely SC-QEDHF). The SC-QEDHF framework is stimulated by the fact that, in the infinite coupling limit (i.e.,
\hat{H}_{e}\ll\hat{H}_{\mathrm{p}}+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{ep}}
or
\lambda_{\alpha}\rightarrow\infty
), the Hamiltonian is dominated by the photon and electron-photon interaction terms, and the corresponding wavefunction can be well approximated by a Gaussian state,

\ket{\Psi^{\infty}}=e^{-\sum_{\alpha}\frac{\lambda_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\alpha}}}\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}(\hat{a}_{\alpha}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha})}\ket{\mathrm{HF},0}\equiv\hat{U}_{\lambda}\ket{\mathrm{HF},0}.

(7)

This is widely recognized as the polaron transformation within the context of electron-phonon interaction scenarios
Shneyder et al. 2020
;
Zhang et al. 2015b
;
Mahan 2000
. This approach has recently been adapted for use in polariton chemistry
Mandal et al. 2020
;
Riso et al. 2022a
;
Ashida et al. 2021
. Consequently, we can employ the
\hat{U}_{\lambda}
operator to transpose the Hamiltonian into a new framework, wherein the resultant transformed Hamiltonian effectively eliminates the explicit electron-photon coupling terms. In particular, after undergoing the transformation, the electronic and photonic operators become

\displaystyle\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{\lambda}\hat{c}_{\nu}\hat{U}_{\lambda}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu}\hat{c}_{\mu}X_{\mu\nu},

(8)

\displaystyle\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{\lambda}\hat{a}_{\alpha}\hat{U}_{\lambda}=

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{\alpha}-\frac{\lambda_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\alpha}}}\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}},

(9)

where
X_{\mu\nu}=\exp\left[-\sum_{\alpha}\frac{\lambda_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\alpha}}}\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}-\hat{a}_{\alpha})\right]|_{\mu\nu}
.

Consequently, under the polariton transformation, the resulting Hamiltonian becomes (denoted as
\hat{H}^{p}
)

\hat{H}^{p}=\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{\lambda}\hat{H}_{\mathrm{PF}}\hat{U}_{\lambda}=\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{\lambda}\hat{H}_{e}\hat{U}_{\lambda}+\sum_{\alpha}\omega_{\alpha}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\hat{a}_{\alpha}.

(10)

The transformed electronic Hamiltonian
\tilde{H}_{e}\equiv\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{\lambda}\hat{H}_{e}\hat{U}_{\lambda}
is formally the same as the original one with the electronic operators dressed by the
X
operator. Since the dipole coupling operator
\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}
in the
X
operator is not diagonal, it’s more convenient to transform the operator into the dipole basis (defined as the eigenstate of
\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}
operator, denoted by the symbols
p,q,r,s
and the corresponding eigenvalues are denoted as
\eta_{p}
). Then, the corresponding QEDHF energies and Fock matrix can be derived. More details can be found in Ref.
Riso et al. 2022a
.

To bridge the treatment in weak and strong coupling limits, here we present a variational transformation-based QEDHF method for the arbitrary coupling regime. The central idea is that, instead of using
\hat{U}_{\lambda}
, we adopt variational parameters
f_{\alpha}
to control the variational transformation
\hat{U}_{f}

Silbey and Harris 1984
(also called Lang-Firsov transformation
Lang and Firsov 1963
)

\hat{U}_{f}=e^{-\sum_{\alpha}\frac{f_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\alpha}}}\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}(\hat{a}_{\alpha}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha})}.

(11)

which helps the seek for an optimal mean-field approximation to the cavity QED Hamiltonian. Such idea has been previously used in strong electron/exciton-phonon interactions, including exciton transport
Silbey and Harris 1984
, polaron formation
Barišić 2002
;
Alexandrov et al. 1994
;
Luo et al. 2022
, and dissipative quantum transport
Zhang et al. 2015a
;
Hsieh et al. 2019
;
Wang and Zhao 2020
.

With the variational transformation (VT), the resulting Hamiltonians become,

\displaystyle\hat{H}(\{f_{\alpha}\})=

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{e}(\{f_{\alpha}\})+\sum_{\alpha}\omega_{\alpha}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\hat{a}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\alpha}\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{\alpha}}{2}}(\Delta\lambda_{\alpha})\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}+\hat{a}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle+\frac{(\Delta\lambda_{\alpha})^{2}}{2}(\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})^{2}.

(12)

where
\Delta\lambda_{\alpha}=\lambda_{\alpha}-f_{\alpha}
and the parameters
f_{\alpha}
are to be variationally minimized.
\tilde{H}_{e}(\{f_{\alpha}\})
is the VT dressed electronic Hamiltonian, where the original electronic operator becomes
\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{f}\hat{c}_{\nu}\hat{U}_{f}=\sum_{\nu}\hat{c}_{\nu}X^{f}_{\mu\nu}
and
X^{f}_{\mu\nu}=\exp\left[-\sum_{\alpha}\frac{f_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\alpha}}}\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}-\hat{a}_{\alpha})\right]|_{\mu\nu}
.
The detailed derivation can be found in Supplementary Materials (SM). Compared to the fully transformed polariton Hamiltonian in Eq.
10
, the variationally transformed Hamiltonian in Eq.
II
includes a partially dressed electronic Hamiltonian
\tilde{H}_{e}(\{f_{\alpha}\})
and residues in the bilinear coupling and DSE terms (controlled by
f_{\alpha}
). The last two terms in Eq.
II
are referred to as the residual bilinear coupling and DSE terms, respectively. It’s obvious that when
f_{\alpha}/\lambda_{\alpha}=0
(or 1), Eq.
II
reduces to the original PF Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{PF}
or fully transformed polariton Hamiltonian
\hat{H}^{p}
. It should be noted that the transformed Hamiltonians in Equations
II
and
10
are both exact, as no approximation was made in the transformation. The exact diagonalization of the two Hamiltonians should give the same eigenstates.

Applying the mean-field approximation to the wavefunction allows us to define the VT-QEDHF wave function as

\ket{\Psi}=e^{-\frac{f_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\alpha}}}\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}(\hat{a}_{\alpha}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha})}\ket{\mathrm{HF},0}\equiv\hat{U}_{f}\ket{\mathrm{HF},0}.

(13)

In the dipole basis
Riso et al. 2022b
, this becomes

\ket{\Psi}=e^{-\frac{f_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\alpha}}}\sum_{p}\eta_{p}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{p}\hat{c}_{p}(a_{\alpha}-a^{\dagger}_{\alpha})}\ket{\mathrm{HF},0},

(14)

and the transformed electronic operators in the dipole basis are given by

\hat{U}_{f}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{p}\hat{U}_{f}=\sum_{\nu}\hat{c}_{p}X^{f}_{p},

(15)

where
X^{f}_{p}=\exp\left[-\sum_{\alpha}\frac{f_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\alpha}}}(\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})_{pp}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}-\hat{a}_{\alpha})\right]
.

Consequently, the VT-QEDHF energy in the dipole basis becomes

\displaystyle E=

\displaystyle\sum_{pq}\tilde{h}_{pq}\rho_{pq}G_{pq}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{pqrs}\tilde{I}_{pqrs}\left(\rho_{pq}\rho_{rs}-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{ps}\rho_{rq}\right)G_{pqrs}+\frac{f^{2}_{\alpha}}{2}\sum_{p}\rho_{pp}\left[(\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\bm{D})_{pp}-\eta_{p}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{f^{2}_{\alpha}}{2}\sum_{pq}\left(\rho_{pp}\rho_{qq}-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{pq}\rho_{qp}\right)\left[(\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\bm{D})_{pp}-\eta_{p}\right]\left[(\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\bm{D})_{qq}-\eta_{q}\right]+\frac{(\Delta\lambda_{\alpha})^{2}}{2}\bra{\mathrm{HF}}\bra{0}(\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})^{2}\ket{\mathrm{HF}}\ket{0}.

(16)

Here,
\tilde{h}
and
\tilde{I}
represent one-electron and two-electron integrals in the dipole basis, respectively,
\rho_{pq}
is the density matrix, and
G
are the Franck-Condon factors derived by integrating out the photonic degrees of freedom from the VT-dressed one-/two-electron integrals (i.e.,
\bra{0}(X^{f})^{\dagger}_{p}X^{f}_{q}\ket{0}

Zhang et al. 2015a
;
Zhang et al. 2015b
). The first two terms in Eq. (
II
) are formally the same as the HF energy of the pure electronic system, but with one-/two-electron integrals replaced by the VT-dressed ones. The third and fourth terms account for relaxation in the dipole basis set
Riso et al. 2022a
. Finally, the last term in Eq. (
II
) represents the residual DSE.

The explicit form of
G
can be found in the Supplementary Material (SM). The corresponding Fock matrix can be derived from the energy derivatives with respect to the density matrix. Moreover, the optimal
\{f_{\alpha}\}
can also be optimized during the SCF procedure via the energy derivatives with respect to
f_{\alpha}
(i.e.,
\frac{\partial E}{\partial f_{\alpha}}
). The detailed formulas for the Fock matrix and
\frac{\partial E}{\partial f_{\alpha}}
, which are used for updating the density matrix and variational parameters, can be found in the SM. Additionally, VT-QEDHF can be augmented with the CS basis set, defined by the residue bilinear coupling as
z^{f}_{\alpha}\equiv-\frac{f_{\alpha}\langle\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}\rangle}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\alpha}}}=-\frac{f_{\alpha}}{\lambda_{\alpha}}z_{\alpha}
, leading to the effective ansatz

\Psi=e^{-\sum_{\alpha p}\frac{f_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\alpha}}}\eta_{p}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{p}\hat{c}_{p}(a_{\alpha}-a^{\dagger}_{\alpha})}\hat{U}(z^{f}_{\alpha})\ket{\mathrm{HF}}\ket{0}.

(17)

This resulting formalism is denoted as the VT-QEDHF-CS method.

IV
Summary

In summary, this study introduces the variational transformation-based electronic structure theory (VT-QEDHF) for molecular QED applications encompassing all ranges of coupling strengths. This methodology adeptly captures the optimal mean-field part of both electron-photon and photon-mediated electron-electron correlations. Furthermore, this framework is universally applicable to any fermion-boson interaction, making it suitable for studying the coupling of electrons with other quantized bosonic entities such as plasmons and phonons. As an example, our approach can be extended to the investigation of polaron formation from the first principles.

While VT-QEDHF is robust across all coupling strengths at the mean-field level, it inherently underestimates both intrinsic and photon-mediated electronic correlations. To address this limitation, our forthcoming research will focus on the integration of VT-QEDHF into QED-CCSD and EOM-CCSD frameworks. Given the superior performance of VT-QEDHF over existing QEDHF and SC-QEDHF methods, we are optimistic that the advanced QED-CC methods augmented with VT-QEDHF

;
White et al. 2020
;

;
Liebenthal et al. 2022
will significantly improve correlation energy estimations in all coupling regimes.

Additional note: While drafting this manuscript, we became aware of a recent paper that employs similar concepts
Cui et al. 2023
. However, the variational transformation in Ref.
Cui et al. 2023
is limited to diagonal terms (of the dipole coupling operator) in the transformation. In contrast, our transformation is more general, and the corresponding elements are evaluated within the dipole basis.

S1
QEDHF Method

Like the Hartree-Fock (HF) method for purely electronic systems, the QEDHF reference wavefunction is a direct product of a single Slater determinant of electronic orbitals and a zero-photon state.

Fock State Representation.

The QEDHF equations can be derived by starting with the reference states,

\ket{R}=\ket{\Psi_{0}}\otimes\left(\sum_{\bm{n}}C_{\bm{n}}\prod_{\alpha}\ket{n_{\alpha}}\right)\equiv\ket{\Psi_{0}}\otimes\ket{P}.

(S1)

where
\bm{n}=(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots)
.
And
\ket{n_{\alpha}}=\frac{(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha})^{n_{\alpha}}}{\sqrt{n_{\alpha}!}}\ket{0}
are the normalized photon number states for mode
\alpha
.
\ket{\Psi_{0}}
denotes the Slater determinant of electronic orbitals. For a given electronic state (such as an HF state), the total energy can then be minimized with respect to the photon coefficients
C_{\bm{n}}
, which is achieved by diagonalizing the Hamiltonian after integrating out the electronic degrees of freedom (DOF), resulting in the dressed photonic Hamiltonian,

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{P}=

\displaystyle\bra{\Psi_{0}}\hat{H}\ket{\Psi_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{M}+\sum_{\alpha}\Big[\omega_{\alpha}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\hat{a}_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2})+\frac{1}{2}\langle(\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{\alpha}}{2}}\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot\langle\hat{\bm{D}}\rangle(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}+\hat{a}_{\alpha})\Big].

(S2)

In the evaluation of the expectation of the Dipole Self-Energy (DSE) operator, it should be noted that
\langle(\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})^{2}\rangle\neq(\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot\langle\hat{\bm{D}}\rangle)^{2}
because

\displaystyle(\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})^{2}=\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}=\sum_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}\bar{d}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}\bar{d}^{\alpha}_{\lambda\sigma}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\lambda}\hat{c}_{\sigma}\hat{c}_{\nu}-\sum_{\mu\nu}\bar{q}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\hat{c}_{\nu}.

(S3)

where
\bar{d}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}=\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot\bra{\mu}\hat{\bm{D}}\ket{\nu}
and

\bar{q}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}=\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot\bra{\mu}\bm{q}\ket{\nu}\cdot\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}
are modified dipole and quadrupole integrals, respectively.
The above derivation does not assume the completeness of the one-particle basis set and is employed in Ref.
Vu et al. 2022
;
McTague and Foley 2022
. Conversely, the second-quantized form for the square of the electric dipole operator is often approximated as the product of second-quantized electric dipole operators in many studies
Foley et al. 2023

\displaystyle(\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu\nu}\bar{d}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}c^{\dagger}_{\mu}c_{\nu}\sum_{\lambda\sigma}\bar{d}^{\alpha}_{\lambda\sigma}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\lambda}\hat{c}_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}\bar{d}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}\bar{d}^{\alpha}_{\lambda\sigma}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\lambda}\hat{c}_{\sigma}\hat{c}_{\nu}+\sum_{\mu\sigma}\left(\sum_{\nu}\bar{d}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}\bar{d}^{\alpha}_{\nu\sigma}\right)\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\hat{c}_{\sigma}.

(S4)

Nevertheless, this expression shows that the DSE can be evaluated via photon-mediated one- and two-electron integrals. Consequently, the partial derivative of the QEDHF energy with respect to the electronic density matrix yields a new Fock matrix incorporating the DSE-mediated exchange and correlation matrix
Foley et al. 2023
.
Alternatively, the total Fock matrix can be evaluated by modifying the one-electron and two-electron integrals,

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\rightarrow h_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha}\tilde{q}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu},

(S5)

\displaystyle I_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}

\displaystyle\rightarrow I_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}+\sum_{\alpha}\tilde{d}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{d}^{\alpha}_{\lambda\sigma}

(S6)

CS Representation
.

In fact, Eq.

\hat{U}(\bm{z})=\prod_{\alpha}\exp[z_{\alpha}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}-z_{\alpha}^{*}\hat{a}_{\alpha}]

(S7)

where
z_{\alpha}=-\frac{\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot\langle\hat{\bm{D}}\rangle}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\alpha}}}

The resulting PF Hamiltonian in the CS representation is

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{CS}=

\displaystyle H_{e}+\sum_{\alpha}\Big\{\omega_{\alpha}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\hat{a}_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}[\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot(\hat{\bm{D}}-\langle\hat{\bm{D}}\rangle)]^{2}

\displaystyle-\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{\alpha}}{2}}[\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot(\hat{\bm{D}}-\langle\hat{\bm{D}}\rangle)](\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}+\hat{a}_{\alpha})\Big\}.

(S8)

Note that

\hat{U}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\alpha}\hat{U}=\hat{a}_{\alpha}+[z^{*}_{\alpha}\hat{a}_{\alpha}-z_{\alpha}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha},\hat{a}_{\alpha}]=\hat{a}_{\alpha}+z_{\alpha}.

With the CS representation, the transformed Hamiltonian automatically ensures convergence with respect to the number of photon number states since a coherent state is a linear combination of many photon number states,

\ket{z_{\alpha}}\equiv\hat{U}(z_{\alpha})\ket{0}=e^{-\frac{|z_{\alpha}|^{2}}{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z_{\alpha}^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}\ket{n_{\alpha}},

(S9)

where
\hat{U}(z_{\alpha})=e^{-|z_{\alpha}|^{2}/2}e^{z_{\alpha}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}}e^{-z^{*}_{\alpha}\hat{a}_{\alpha}}
is used.

After the unitary transformation, the Hamiltonian can be solved with the ansatz

\ket{R}=\ket{\text{HF}}\otimes\ket{0}.

(S10)

With this ansatz, the QEDHF energy is

\displaystyle E_{\text{QEDHF}}=

\displaystyle E_{\text{HF}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha}\langle\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot[\hat{\bm{D}}-\langle\hat{\bm{D}}\rangle)]^{2}\rangle,

(S11)

i.e.
, the bilinear coupling term in Eq.
S1
does not contribute to the QEDHF total energy when the Hamiltonian is represented in the coherent-state basis.
The electronic HF energy is
E_{\text{HF}}=\text{Tr}[h+\frac{1}{2}(J-K)]D
, where
D
is the one-electron density matrix.
h
,
J
, and
K
are the one-electron integral, Coulomb, and exchange potentials, respectively.
The DSE in CS representation becomes,

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\text{DSE}}\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha}\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot[\hat{\bm{D}}-\langle\hat{\bm{D}}\rangle)]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha}\left[(\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})^{2}+(\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot\langle\hat{\bm{D}}\rangle)^{2}-2(\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})(\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot\langle\hat{\bm{D}}\rangle)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha}\bar{d}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}\bar{d}^{\alpha}_{\lambda\sigma}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\lambda}\hat{c}_{\sigma}\hat{c}_{\nu}-\sum_{\alpha}\left[\frac{1}{2}\bar{q}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}+(\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot\langle\hat{\bm{D}}\rangle)\bar{d}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}\right]\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\hat{c}_{\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha}(\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot\langle\hat{\bm{D}}\rangle)^{2}

(S12)

where the first term is given by Eq.
S3
. Hence, substituting Eq.
S1

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu}\rightarrow

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu}-\sum_{\alpha}\left[\frac{1}{2}\bar{q}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}+(\bm{\lambda}_{\alpha}\cdot\langle\hat{\bm{D}}\rangle)\bar{d}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}\right],

(S13)

\displaystyle I_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}\rightarrow

\displaystyle I_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}+\sum_{\alpha}\bar{d}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}\bar{d}^{\alpha}_{\lambda\sigma}.

(S14)

S2
Variational QED-HF Theory for Arbitrary Coupling Strength

In this section, we describe the variational polaron transformation-based QED-HF method.
The parameterized unitary transformation is defined as

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{f}

\displaystyle=e^{-\sum_{\alpha}\frac{f_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\alpha}}}\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}(\hat{a}_{\alpha}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha})}.

(S15)

where
\bm{f}=\{f_{\alpha}\}
are the parameters to be optimized.
The electronic and photonic operators, after the transformation, become

\displaystyle\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{f}\hat{c}_{\nu}\hat{U}_{f}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu}c_{\nu}X_{\mu\nu},

(S16)

\displaystyle\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{f}\hat{a}_{\alpha}\hat{U}_{f}

\displaystyle=\hat{a}_{\alpha}-\frac{f_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\alpha}}}\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}.

(S17)

where

X_{\mu\nu}=\exp\left[-\sum_{\alpha}\frac{f_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\alpha}}}\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}-\hat{a}_{\alpha})\right]|_{\mu\nu}.

(S18)

Note that the electronic operator is not present in
X_{\mu\nu}
in contrast to the unitary operator
\hat{U}_{f}
. To derive the above equations, we used the identity
e^{S}Ae^{-S}=\sum_{n}\frac{1}{n!}A^{(n)}
where
A^{(n)}=[S,A^{(n-1)}]
and
A^{(0)}=A
. For the electronic operator
\hat{c}_{\mu}
, we have (we define
\zeta^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{f_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\alpha}}}(\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})_{\mu\nu}(\hat{a}_{\alpha}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha})
),

\displaystyle\hat{c}^{(1)}_{\mu}=

\displaystyle[S,\hat{c}_{\mu}]=\sum_{\alpha,\mu^{\prime}\nu}\zeta^{\alpha}_{\mu^{\prime}\nu}[\hat{E}_{\mu^{\prime}\nu},\hat{c}_{\mu}]=-\sum_{\alpha,\nu}\zeta^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}\hat{c}_{\nu},

(S19)

\displaystyle\hat{c}^{(2)}_{\mu}=

\displaystyle[S,\hat{c}^{(1)}_{\mu}]=\sum_{\alpha,\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}\zeta^{\alpha}_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}[\hat{E}_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}},-\sum_{\nu}\zeta^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}\hat{c}_{\nu}]=\sum_{\alpha\nu\nu^{\prime}}\zeta^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}\zeta^{\alpha}_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}\hat{c}_{\nu^{\prime}}=-\sum_{\nu}(\zeta^{\alpha})^{2}_{\mu\nu}\hat{c}_{\nu}

(S20)

and so on. After the transformation, the Hamiltonian becomes

\displaystyle\hat{H}(\{f_{\alpha}\})=

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{e}+\sum_{\alpha}\omega_{\alpha}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}-\frac{f_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\alpha}}}\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}\right)\left(\hat{a}_{\alpha}-\frac{f_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\alpha}}}\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\alpha}\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{\alpha}}{2}}\lambda_{\alpha}\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}+\hat{a}_{\alpha}-2\frac{f_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\alpha}}}\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}\right)+\frac{\lambda^{2}_{\alpha}}{2}(\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{e}+\sum_{\alpha}\omega_{\alpha}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\hat{a}_{\alpha}+\sum_{\alpha}\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{\alpha}}{2}}(\Delta\lambda_{\alpha})\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}+\hat{a}_{\alpha})+\frac{(\Delta\lambda_{\alpha})^{2}}{2}(\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})^{2}.

(S21)

which is the Eq.
II
in the main text and
\Delta\lambda_{\alpha}=\lambda_{\alpha}-f_{\alpha}
. Hence, after the variational transformation, Eq.
II
is formally the same as the original Hamiltonian with
1)

\lambda_{\alpha}
replaced with
\Delta\lambda_{\alpha}
and
2)
photonic displacement operator dresses electronic integrals. The dressed electronic Hamiltonian reads

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{e}=\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\hat{c}_{\nu}+\tilde{I}_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\lambda}\hat{c}_{\sigma}\hat{c}_{\nu}.

(S22)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}h_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}X^{\dagger}_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}X_{\nu\nu^{\prime}},

(S23)

\displaystyle\tilde{I}_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}X^{\dagger}_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}X^{\dagger}_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}I_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}X_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}X_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}

(S24)

are the dressed one- and two-electron integrals.

S2.0.1
QED energies and dipole basis

The evaluation of displacement operator elements depends on the diagonalization of the
\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}
matrix. Therefore, transforming the basis into a dipole basis set simplifies the process,

\displaystyle X_{\mu\nu}=\prod_{\alpha}

\displaystyle V^{\alpha}_{\mu p}\exp\left[-\frac{f_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\alpha}}}(\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})_{p}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}-\hat{a}_{\alpha})\right]V^{\alpha}_{p\nu}.

(S25)

where
V^{\alpha}
is the transformation matrix that diagonalizes the dipole coupling matrix
(\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})_{\mu\nu}
. Thus, we can rewrite the original Hamiltonian in the dipole basis as introduced in Ref.
Riso et al. 2022a
.

Consequently, the one-electron part of the QEDHF energy is
E_{T}=\sum_{\mu\nu}\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu}\rho_{\mu\nu}
, where the photon-dressed one-electron integral is

\displaystyle\bra{0_{p}}\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu}\ket{0_{p}}=

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}\tilde{h}_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}X^{\dagger}_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}X_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}\sum_{pq}h_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}\prod_{\alpha}U^{\alpha}_{\mu p}e^{\frac{f_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\alpha}}}(\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})_{p}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}-\hat{a}_{\alpha})}U^{\alpha}_{p\mu^{\prime}}U^{\alpha}_{\nu q}e^{-\frac{f_{\beta}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\beta}}}\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}_{q}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\beta}-\hat{a}_{\beta})}U^{\alpha}_{q\nu^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}\sum_{pq}\tilde{h}_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}\prod_{\alpha}U^{\alpha}_{\mu p}U^{\alpha}_{\nu q}G^{\alpha}_{pq}U^{\alpha}_{p\mu^{\prime}}U^{\alpha}_{q\nu^{\prime}}\right].

(S26)

The two-electron integrals in the photonic vacuum state are

\displaystyle\bra{0_{p}}\tilde{I}_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}\ket{0_{p}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}X^{\dagger}_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}X^{\dagger}_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}I_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}X_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}X_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}\sum_{pqrs}\prod_{\alpha}V^{\alpha}_{\mu^{\prime}p}V^{\alpha}_{p\mu}V^{\alpha}_{\nu^{\prime}q}V_{q\nu}I_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}V^{\alpha}_{\lambda^{\prime}r}V^{\alpha}_{r\lambda}V^{\alpha}_{\sigma^{\prime}s}V^{\alpha}_{s\sigma}G^{\alpha}_{pqrs}.

(S27)

Where the Gaussian factors are

\displaystyle G^{\alpha}_{pq}=

\displaystyle\exp\left[-\sum_{\alpha}\frac{f^{2}_{\alpha}(\eta^{\alpha}_{p}-\eta^{\alpha}_{q})^{2}}{4\omega_{\alpha}}\right],

(S28)

\displaystyle G^{\alpha}_{pqrs}=

\displaystyle\exp\left[-\sum_{\alpha}\frac{f^{2}_{\alpha}(\eta^{\alpha}_{p}-\eta^{\alpha}_{q}+\eta^{\alpha}_{r}-\eta^{\alpha}_{s})^{2}}{4\omega_{\alpha}}\right].

(S29)

Additionally, the DSE residue contributes to the total energy as well. Analogous to the QED-HF formalism, the residual DSE contribution can be computed via DSE-mediated one-electron and two-electron integrals

\displaystyle h^{p}_{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle-\sum_{\alpha}\frac{(\Delta\lambda_{\alpha})^{2}}{2}\tilde{q}_{\mu\nu},

(S30)

\displaystyle I^{p}_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}\frac{(\Delta\lambda_{\alpha})^{2}}{2}\tilde{d}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{d}_{\lambda\sigma}.

(S31)

This formulation allows the residual DSE-mediated Fock matrix and its associated energies to be expressed in a manner analogous to the electronic components.

In summary, the total energy in the dipole basis is given by

\displaystyle E=

\displaystyle\sum_{pq}\tilde{h}_{pq}\rho_{pq}G_{pq}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{pqrs}\tilde{I}_{pqrs}\left(\rho_{pq}\rho_{rs}-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{ps}\rho_{rq}\right)G_{pqrs}+\sum_{\alpha p}\frac{f^{2}_{\alpha}}{2}\rho_{pp}[(\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})_{pp}-\eta_{p}]^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{pq\alpha}\frac{f^{2}_{\alpha}}{2}\left(\rho_{pp}\rho_{qq}-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{pq}\rho_{qp}\right)[(\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})_{pp}-\eta_{p}][(\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})_{qq}-\eta_{q}]

\displaystyle+\sum_{\alpha}\frac{(\Delta\lambda_{\alpha})^{2}}{2}\bra{\text{HF}}\bra{0}(\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})^{2}\ket{\text{HF}}\ket{0}.

(S32)

S2.0.2
Gradients of total energy with respect to variational transformation parameter

The variational optimization of the
\{f_{\alpha}\}
parameters is achieved via the variational minimization procedure along with the density matrix optimization. In particular, the optimal transformation parameters are obtained when the energy gradients with respect to the
\{f_{\alpha}\}
are equal to zero. The gradient (in the dipole basis) is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial E}{\partial f_{\alpha}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial E_{e}}{\partial f_{\alpha}}+\frac{\partial E_{DSE}}{\partial f_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{pq}\frac{-f_{\alpha}(\eta_{\alpha,p}-\eta_{\alpha,q})^{2}}{2\omega_{\alpha}}\tilde{h}_{pq}\rho_{pq}G_{pq}

\displaystyle+\sum_{pqrs}\frac{-f_{\alpha}(\eta^{\alpha}_{p}-\eta^{\alpha}_{q}+\eta^{\alpha}_{r}-\eta^{\alpha}_{s})^{2}}{2\omega_{\alpha}}\tilde{I}_{pqrs}\left(\rho_{pq}\rho_{rs}-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{ps}\rho_{rq}\right)G_{pqrs}

\displaystyle+f_{\alpha}\sum_{p}\rho_{pp}[(\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})_{pp}-\eta_{p}]^{2}

\displaystyle+f_{\alpha}\sum_{pq}\left(\rho_{pp}\rho_{qq}-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{pq}\rho_{qp}\right)[(\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})_{pp}-\eta_{p}][(\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})_{qq}-\eta_{q}]

\displaystyle-\Delta\lambda_{\alpha}\bra{\text{HF}}\bra{0}(\bm{e}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{D}})^{2}\ket{\text{HF}}\ket{0}.

(S33)

The Correlation Function and Detection of Baryon Acoustic Oscillation Peak from the Spectroscopic 𝚂𝙳𝚂𝚂 − 𝙶𝚊𝚕𝚆𝙲𝚊𝚝 \mathtt{SDSS-GalWCat} Galaxy Cluster Catalogue The Correlation Function and Detection of Baryon Acoustic Oscillation Peak from the Spectroscopic 𝚂𝙳𝚂𝚂 − 𝙶𝚊𝚕𝚆𝙲𝚊𝚝 \mathtt{SDSS-GalWCat} Galaxy Cluster Catalogue – 5
Mohamed H. Abdullah, Anatoly Klypin, Francisco Prada, Gillian Wilson, Tomoaki Ishiyama, Julia Ereza

Abstract
We measure the two point correlation function (CF) of 1357 galaxy clusters with a mass of log 10 ⁡ M 200 ≥ 13.6 \log_{10}{M_{200}}\geq 13.6 h − 1 ​ M ⊙ h^{-1}M_{\odot} and at a redshift of z ≤ 0.125 z\leq 0.125 . This work differs from previous analyses in that it utilizes a spectroscopic cluster catalogue, 𝚂𝙳𝚂𝚂 − 𝙶𝚊𝚕𝚆𝙲𝚊𝚝 \mathtt{SDSS-GalWCat} , to measure the CF and detect the baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) signal. Unlike previous studies which use statistical techniques, we compute covariance errors directly by generating a set of 1086 galaxy cluster lightcones from the GLAM N N -body simulation. Fitting the CF with a power-law model of the form ξ ⁡ ( s ) = ( s / s 0 ) − γ \xi(s)=(s/s_{0})^{-\gamma} , we determine the best-fit correlation length and power-law index at three mass thresholds. We find that the correlation length increases with increasing the mass threshold while the power-law index is almost constant. For log 10 ⁡ M 200 ≥ 13.6 \log_{10}{M_{200}}\geq 13.6 h − 1 ​ M ⊙ h^{-1}M_{\odot} , we find s 0 = 14.54 ± 0.87 s_{0}=14.54\pm 0.87 h − 1 ​ Mpc h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc} and γ = 1.97 ± 0.11 \gamma=1.97\pm 0.11 . We detect the BAO signal at s = 100 s=100 h − 1 ​ Mpc h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc} with a significance of 1.60 ​ σ 1.60\sigma . Fitting the CF with a Λ \Lambda CDM model, we find D V ​ ( z = 0.089 ) ​ r d f ​ i ​ d / r d = 267.62 ± 26 D_{\mathrm{V}}(z=0.089)\mathrm{r}^{fid}_{d}/\mathrm{r}_{d}=267.62\pm 26 h − 1 ​ Mpc h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc} , consistent with Planck 2015 cosmology. We present a set of 108 high-fidelity simulated galaxy cluster lightcones from the high-resolution Uchuu \mathrm{Uchuu} N-body simulation, employed for methodological validation. We find D V ​ ( z = 0.089 ) / r d = 2.666 ± 0.129 D_{\mathrm{V}}(z=0.089)/r_{d}=2.666\pm 0.129 , indicating that our method does not introduce any bias in the parameter estimation for this small sample of galaxy clusters.

On small scales of a few Mpcs, the CF is frequently characterized by a power-law. The slope of this power-law holds significant implications for the underlying matter density and the structure growth rates in the Universe. Additionally, the amplitude of the CF serves as a valuable indicator of the bias exhibited by the galaxy cluster population
(
Colberg et al. 2000
;
Moscardini et al. 2000
;
Collins et al. 2000
;
Bahcall et al. 2004
;
Desjacques et al. 2018

On large scales, the CF exhibits the BAO signal
(

;
Eisenstein et al. 2005
)

\sim 100

Hong et al. 2016
detected the BAO signal using cluster samples obtained from
Wen et al. 2009
,
Wen et al. 2012
, and
Wen & Han 2015
, respectively.

Data and simulation lightcones

2.1
The
\mathtt{SDSS-GalWCat}

From the SDSS-DR13 spectroscopic dataset,
Abdullah et al. 2020a
constructed a publicly available catalogue of 1800 galaxy clusters,
\mathtt{GalWCat}
.
The mass of each cluster,
M_{200}
, was estimated by applying the virial theorem at the radius within which the density,
\rho
, satisfied the condition
\rho=200\rho_{c}
, where
\rho_{c}
is the critical density of the Universe (e.g.,
Carlberg et al. 1997
;
Klypin et al. 2016
). The 1800 clusters in the catalogue have redshifts in the range
0.01<z<0.2
(with a mean redshift of
z=0.089
) and masses in the range
13.6<\log{M_{200}}<15.1

h^{-1}M_{\odot}
.

The selection function of
\mathtt{GalWCat19}
is
\mathcal{S}_{z}(D)=1.07\exp\left[{-\left(\frac{D}{293.4}\right)^{2.97}}\right]
, where
D
is the comoving distance to the cluster
(
Abdullah et al. 2020b
)
. To mitigate shot noise in the correlation function and the BAO signal, we limit our analysis to
\mathcal{S}_{z}\lesssim 0.2
at maximum redshift of
z\leq 0.125
. Additionally, we exclude the clusters in the three small separated stripes in the SDSS sky to eliminate selection effect of the boundary on the CF and the BAO signal.
The resulting subsample, which we refer to as SDSS-GalWCat, consists of 1357 clusters. Throughout the remainder of this paper, we utilize the SDSS-GalWCat catalog to calculate the correlation function and detect the BAO signal.

This analysis aims to uncover any potential biases in parameter estimation that may be introduced by our methodology. Additionally, these simulated lightcones are generated for future and external applications. They will be valuable for exploring cluster scaling relationships and internal dynamics, taking into account the influence of projection effects.

2.3
The covariance matrix

The covariance matrix is a crucial ingredient for clustering analyses. It measures the correlation between correlation function bins. In our analysis, we utilize the
\mathrm{GLAM}
simulation
(
Klypin & Prada 2018
)
to calculate the covariance matrix and the uncertainties of the CF.
\mathrm{GLAM}
is a cosmological
N
-body simulation of
2000^{3}
particles in a box of comoving length 1000
h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}
, particle mass resolution of
1.07\times 10^{8}

h^{-1}M_{\odot}
, and gravitational softening length of
250~h^{-1}\mathrm{kpc}
. It assumes a standard
\Lambda
CDM cosmology with
\Omega_{M}=0.307
,
\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.693
,
\Omega_{b}=0.0483
,
\sigma_{8}=0.8288
, and
h=0.6777
.
The distinct haloes are identified with the Bound Density Maximum halo finder
(
Klypin & Holtzman 1997
)
. We generate
1086
independent lightcones from
1086
realizations of a snapshot at redshift
z\sim 0.11
. The lightcones are generated with the same selection function and the sky footprint as SDSS-GalWCat.

The covariance matrix is calculated from the 1086 independent
\mathrm{GLAM}
lightcones as

C_{ij}=\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{l=1}^{N}\left(\xi^{l}_{i}-\overline{\xi}_{i}\right)\left(\xi^{l}_{j}-\overline{\xi}_{j}\right),

(1)

where
N
is the number of lightcones,
\xi^{l}_{i}
is the CF of the
l^{th}
lightcone at the
i^{th}
bin, and
\overline{\xi}_{i}
represents the mean value of all lightcones at the
i^{th}
bin. The error bars of
\xi(s)
are given by the diagonal elements as
\sigma_{i}=\sqrt{C_{ii}}
. We address the comparison between covariance matrices derived from
\mathrm{GLAM}
lightcones and those obtained from jackknife and bootstrap resampling methods in Appendix
A
and
5
.

From the covariance matrix, we define the
\chi^{2}
statistic as

\chi^{2}=\frac{N-p-2}{N-1}(\xi^{\text{Data}}-\xi^{\text{Model}})C^{-1}(\xi^{\text{Data}}-\xi^{\text{Model}})^{T},

(2)

where
p
is the number of degrees-of-freedom and
N
is the number of GLAM lightcones, including the Hartlap correction
(
Hartlap et al. 2007
)
. Throughout the paper we apply the Monte Carlo Markov Chain (MCMC) technique using the full covariance matrix. We adopt the standard Gaussian likelihood where the likelihood
\mathcal{L}\propto\exp{\left(\frac{-\chi^{2}}{2}\right)}
.

3
Cluster Correlation Function

In this section, we present the CFs derived from SDSS-
\mathrm{GalWCat}
as a function of cluster mass, and compare them with those obtained from the
\mathrm{GalWCat}
-
\mathrm{Uchuu}
lightcones. We use the Landy–Szalay estimator
(
Landy & Szalay 1993
)
to calculate the CF,
\xi(s)
:

\xi(s)=\left[DD(s)\frac{N_{RR}}{N_{DD}}-2\;DR(s)\frac{N_{RR}}{N_{DR}}+RR(s)\right]/RR(s),

(3)

where
DD(s)
,
DR(s)
, and
RR(s)
are the number of data-data, data-random, and random-random pairs within a separation annulus of
s\pm\Delta s/2
, and
N_{DD}
,
N_{DR}
, and
N_{RR}
are the normalization factors of the pairs. We generate random samples based on the sky footprint and redshift selection function of SDSS-
\mathrm{GalWCat}
(see §
2.1
).
The number of objects generated in each random sample is
\sim 25
times larger than that of the SDSS-
\mathrm{GalWCat}
.

On small scales, the CF can be fit by a power-law of the form

\xi(s)=\left(\frac{s}{s_{0}}\right)^{-\gamma},

(4)

where
s_{0}
is the correlation length and
\gamma
is the slope. The value of the correlation length is mass dependent, and has been shown to increase with cluster mass (e.g.,
Bahcall et al. 2004
;
Croft et al. 1997
;
Estrada et al. 2009
).
We perform the
\chi^{2}
fit on scales of
\log{5}<\log{s}<\log{40}
[
h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}
], using separation bins of width
\log{\Delta s}=0.1062
[
h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}
].
Fig.
3
presents the SDSS-
\mathrm{GalWCat}
CFs for three different cluster mass thresholds ([13.6, 13.9, 14.2]
h^{-1}M_{\odot}
), and compares them to those obtained from the 108
\mathrm{GalWCat}
-
\mathrm{Uchuu}
lightcones. We note the very close agreement between the observations and simulations.

In Table
1
we show the best fit correlation length and power-law slope derived for either the SDSS-
\mathrm{GalWCat}
and
\mathrm{GalWCat}
-
\mathrm{Uchuu}
for each of the three mass thresholds. As expected, we find that
\gamma
, the slope of the power law, is almost constant with cluster mass while the correlation length,
s_{0}
, increases with increasing cluster mass. This result suggests a strong connection between the mass of galaxy clusters and their spatial distribution patterns. More massive clusters exert more gravitational pull, resulting in stronger clustering. Also, as expected, we find that the value of
s_{0}
is considerably larger for clusters than for galaxies, e.g.,
s_{0}=5.91

h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}
for SDSS galaxies
(
Zehavi et al. 2004
)
and
s_{0}=5.05

h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}

Hawkins et al. 2003

4
BAO detection and cosmology

In this section, we analyze the BAO signal within the framework of a
\Lambda
CDM model with
Planck Collaboration et al. 2016
cosmology. The methodology we use to measure the BAO positions is
adapted from
Ross et al. 2015
(see also e.g.
Veropalumbo et al. 2016
;
Dong-Páez et al. 2022
).
The typical approach is to model the nonlinear power spectrum
P_{\mathrm{nl}}
as (e.g.,
Ross et al. 2015
)

P_{\mathrm{nl}}(k)=\left[P_{\mathrm{lin}}(k)-P_{\mathrm{nw}}(k)\right]\exp\left(\frac{-k^{2}\Sigma_{\mathrm{nl}}^{2}}{2}\right)+P_{\mathrm{nw}}(k),

(5)

where
P_{\mathrm{lin}}
and
P_{\mathrm{nw}}
are the linear and no-wiggle power spectra and
\Sigma_{\mathrm{nl}}
is a parameter modeling the non-linear degradation (e.g.,
Estrada et al. 2009
;
Xu et al. 2012
).
P_{\mathrm{lin}}
and
P_{\mathrm{nw}}
are computed at
z=0.089
using
Colossus
python package
(
Diemer 2018
)
. We adopt
\Sigma_{\mathrm{nl}}=8

h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}
in our analysis (e.g.
Hong et al. 2012
;
Hong et al. 2016
).
The template correlation function with damped BAO is then given as

\xi_{\mathrm{temp}}(s)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int{P_{\mathrm{nl}}(k)\frac{\sin{(ks)}}{ks}k^{2}dk}

(6)

Finally, we fit the cluster CF with the model

\xi_{\mathrm{model}}(s)=B_{\xi}^{2}\xi_{\mathrm{temp}}(\alpha s)+\frac{a_{1}}{s^{2}}+\frac{a_{2}}{s}+a_{3},

(7)

where
B_{\xi}
is a multiplicative constant allowing for an unknown large-scale bias between the distribution of clusters and the underlying matter density field,
\alpha
is a dilation cosmological parameter that can be estimated by comparing the data to the template, and
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
and
a_{3}
are free parameters. Note that polynomial terms include effects caused by, e.g. errors made in the assumption of the model cosmology, scale-dependent bias, and redshift-space distortion (see e.g.
Ross et al. 2015
).

The parameter
\alpha
is related to physical distances via

\alpha=\frac{D_{\mathrm{V}}(z)r_{\mathrm{d}}^{\mathrm{fid}}}{D_{\mathrm{V}}^{\mathrm{fid}}(z)r_{\mathrm{d}}},

(8)

and

D_{\mathrm{V}}(z)=\left[(1+z)^{2}D_{\mathrm{A}}(z)^{2}\frac{cz}{H(z)}\right]^{1/3},

(9)

where
H(z)
is the Hubble parameter,
D_{\mathrm{A}}(z)
is the comoving angular diameter distance, and
r_{\mathrm{d}}
is the sound horizon at the baryon drag epoch, which can be accurately calculated for a given cosmology using CAMB package
(
Lewis et al. 2000
)
. For our fiducial cosmology,
D_{\mathrm{V}}^{\mathrm{fid}}(0.089)=259.32

h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}
and
r_{\mathrm{d}}^{\mathrm{fid}}=99.81

h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}
.

We perform the
\chi^{2}
fit (Eq.
2
) on scales of
50<s<150

h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}
, using separation bins of width
\Delta s=5

h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}
.
Fig.
4
shows the measured and the best-fit model of the CF from the
\mathrm{GalWCat}
-
\mathrm{Uchuu}
lightcones and the SDSS-
\mathrm{GalWCat}
observations. We find
\alpha=1.026\pm 0.074
and
\alpha=1.032\pm 0.099
for
\mathrm{GalWCat}
-
\mathrm{Uchuu}
and SDSS-
\mathrm{GalWCat}
, respectively. From our dilation parameter estimate for the observed CF we find
D_{\mathrm{V}}(z=0.089)\mathrm{r}^{fid}_{d}/\mathrm{r}_{d}=267.62\pm 26

h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}
which is consistent with Planck 2015 cosmology. For the
\mathrm{GalWCat}
-
\mathrm{Uchuu}
lightcone measurements we find
D_{\mathrm{V}}(z=0.089)/r_{\mathrm{d}}=2.666\pm 0.192
, indicating that our method does not introduce any bias in the parameter estimation. For the SDSS-
\mathrm{GalWCat}
observations, the best-fit nonlinear BAO model gives
\chi^{2}=4.428
while the no-wiggle model gives
\chi^{2}=6.878
, and is rejected at
1.56\sigma
. The right panel of Fig.
4
presents the distance
\mathrm{D}_{V}(z)/(\mathrm{r}^{fid}_{s}/rs)
–redshift relation. The plot displays measurements of
\mathrm{D}_{V}(z)
obtained from various studies of galaxy cluster CF across different redshifts. Our new measurement at z=0.089 complements and strengthens the previous studies, providing additional support for the consistency of observations with the Planck 2015 cosmology.

5
Conclusions

In this work, unlike previous analyses which utilized photometric galaxy cluster catalogs, we utilized the spectroscopic cluster catalogue,
\mathtt{SDSS-GalWCat}
, to measure the CF.
\mathtt{SDSS-GalWCat}
contains 1357 galaxy clusters with a mass of
\log_{10}{M_{200}}\geq 13.6

h^{-1}M_{\odot}
and at a redshift of
z\leq 0.125
.
We also created a set of 108 galaxy cluster lightcones using
\mathrm{Uchuu}
N-body simulations. There is good agreement in the cluster number density between the SDSS-GalWCat and GalWCat-Uchuu lightcones.
In contrast to previous analysis which calculated CF uncertainties utilizing resampling methods, we computed covariance errors directly from a set of 1086 galaxy cluster lightcones which we created from the GLAM N-body simulations. We found that jackknife and bootstrap resampling methods yield noisier matrices in contrasting with the smoother lightcone-derived matrix. We also shown that both jackknife and bootstrap errors are larger and noisier than lightcone errors in the range
0\leq s\leq 200

h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}
.

On small scales, we fitted the CF by a power-law of the form
\xi(s)=(s/s_{0})^{-\gamma}
, and determined the best-fit correlation length and power-law index for three cluster mass thresholds (Table
1
). We found good agreement between the correlation functions measured from observations and those derived from the
\mathrm{Uchuu}
lightcones. We noted that, the correlation length increases with increasing the mass threshold while the power-law index is almost constant (
\sim 2
) for both the data and the simulation.
For
\log_{10}{M_{200}}\geq 13.6

h^{-1}M_{\odot}
we determined
s_{0}=14.54\pm 0.85

h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}
and
\gamma=1.97\pm 0.11
for the data. For
\mathrm{Uchuu}
lightcones, we found that
s_{0}
is about 1
h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}
larger than that of SDSS-
\mathrm{GalWCat}
at each mass threshold.

On large scales, we detected the peak of baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO) in the CF at
s=100

h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}
with a significance of
1.60\sigma
. By fitting the observed CF with a
\Lambda
CDM model, we found
\alpha=1.032\pm 0.099
and
D_{\mathrm{V}}(z=0.089)\mathrm{r}^{fid}_{d}/\mathrm{r}_{d}=267.62\pm 26

h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}
. This is consistent with the fiducial cosmology obtained by the Planck 2015 data.

Appendix A
Comparison between covariance matrices

To calculate the covariance matrix using the jackknife method, we utilize a lightcone from
\mathrm{GLAM}
. We divide the celestial region covered by the lightcone into 48 evenly spaced subareas.
By systematically excluding one subarea at a time, we create distinct subsamples for which we calculate the covariance matrix.
For the bootstrap method, we perform 50 resampling iterations, drawing samples with replacement and calculating the covariance matrix
from the resampled data sets.
In Figure
5
, we compare covariance matrices derived from
\mathrm{GLAM}
lightcones with those obtained from jackknife and bootstrap resampling methods. Resampling methods yield noisier matrices, contrasting with the smoother lightcone-derived matrix.
Figure
5
(right panel) shows that both jackknife and bootstrap errors are larger and noisier than lightcone errors across all bins. This underscores the impact of resampling on stability and precision in covariance estimates, emphasizing the need for careful interpretation in numerical simulations.

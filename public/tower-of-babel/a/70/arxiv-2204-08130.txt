Almost global smooth solutions of the 3D quasilinear Klein-Gordon equations on the product space ℝ 2 × 𝕋 \mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}
Li Jun, Tao Fei, Yin Huicheng 1. Department of Mathematics, Nanjing University, Nanjing, 210093, China.2. School of Mathematical Sciences and Mathematical Institute,Nanjing Normal University, Nanjing 210023, China.

Abstract
In the paper, for the 3D quasilinear Klein-Gordon equation with the small initial data posed on the product space ℝ 2 × 𝕋 \mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T} , we focus on the lower bound of the lifespan of the smooth solution. When the size of initial data is bounded by ε 0 > 0 \varepsilon_{0}>0 , by the space-time resonance method, it is shown that smooth solution exists up to the time e c 0 / ϵ 0 2 e^{c_{0}/\epsilon_{0}^{2}} with ε 0 \varepsilon_{0} being sufficiently small and c 0 > 0 c_{0}>0 being some suitable constant.



Keywords:
Quasilinear Klein-Gordon equation, almost global solution, Z-norm,

Littlewood-Paley decomposition, space-time resonance method, energy estimate

Introduction

We consider the following Cauchy problem of the 3D quasilinear Klein-Gordon equation

\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{x,y}+1)u=F(u,\partial u,\partial^{2}u),\\[5.69054pt]
(u(0),\dot{u}(0))=(u_{0},u_{1})(x,y),\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
y\in\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})
,

\dot{u}=\partial_{t}u
,
\partial=(\partial_{0},\partial_{1},\partial_{2},\partial_{3})

with
\partial_{0}=\partial_{t},~\partial_{j}=\partial_{x_{j}}

(j=1,2)
and
\partial_{3}=\partial_{y}
,

\Delta_{x,y}=\Delta_{x}+\partial_{y}^{2}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}\partial_{j}^{2}

F(u,\partial u,\partial^{2}u)=\displaystyle\sum_{j,k=0}^{3}G^{jk}(u,\partial u)\partial_{jk}^{2}u+Q(u,\partial u)

with
G^{jk}(u,\partial u)=\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{3}g^{jkl}\partial_{l}u+h^{jk}u
,
g^{jkl},h^{jk}\in\mathbb{R}
,

g^{jkl}=g^{kjl}
,
h^{jk}=h^{kj}
, and

Q(u,\partial u)
is a quadratic form of
(u,\partial u)
. Without loss of generality,
G^{00}(u,\partial u)\equiv 0

can be assumed.

In order to state the main result, we define the normalized initial data with respect to problem (
1.1
) as

U_{0}(x,y):=u_{1}(x,y)-i\Lambda u_{0}(x,y),

(1.2)

where the operator
\Lambda=(1-\Delta_{x,y})^{1/2}

i=\sqrt{-1}
.

Hereafter, we also use the following notations: for two non-negative quantities
A,B
and some generic
positive constant
C>1

A\lesssim B

A\leq CB
, and
A\thickapprox B

\frac{1}{C}B\leq A\leq CB

Based on the preparations above, our main result can be stated as:

Theorem 1.1
.

For
N\geq 220

and

such that if

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N+1}(t):=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{|\rho|\leq N}\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Big[(\partial_{t}\partial_{x,y}^{\rho}u)^{2}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}(\partial_{j}\partial_{x,y}^{\rho}u)^{2}+(\partial_{x,y}^{\rho}u)^{2}\Big]dydx

(1.12)

Obviously,
\|u(t)\|_{H^{N+1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}+\|\dot{u}(t)\|_{H^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\thickapprox\mathcal{E}^{1/2}_{N+1}(t)

As in
[

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N+1}(t)-\mathcal{E}_{N+1}(0)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{E}_{N+1}(s)\cdot\Big(\sum\limits_{|\rho|\leq 2}\|\partial_{x,y}^{\rho}u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}+\sum\limits_{|\rho|\leq 1}\|\partial_{x,y}^{\rho}\dot{u}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\Big)ds

(1.13)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{E}_{N+1}(s)\cdot(1+s)^{-1}\cdot\theta(s)ds,

where

\theta(s):=\sup\limits_{\tau\in[0,s]}\Big[(1+\tau)\Big(\sum\limits_{|\rho|\leq 2}\|\partial_{x,y}^{\rho}u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}+\sum\limits_{|\rho|\leq 1}\|\partial_{x,y}^{\rho}\dot{u}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\Big)\Big].

Together with Gronwall’s inequality, this yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N+1}^{1/2}(t)\leq\mathcal{E}_{N+1}^{1/2}(0)(1+t)^{C\cdot\theta(t)}.

Therefore, once

Definitions and function spaces

In this section, we introduce the notations of Fourier transformation, the tools of space-frequency
decomposition as well as
Z
-norm and the necessary pseudo-differential operators.

2.1
Notations for Fourier transformation

The partial or full Fourier transformations of
f(x,y)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})
are denoted by

f_{n}(x):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{-iny}f(x,y)dy,~~\mathcal{F}_{x,y}(f)(\xi,n)=\widehat{f_{n}}(\xi):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{-i(\xi\cdot x+ny)}f(x,y)dydx,

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},~y\in\mathbb{T},~\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2},~n\in\mathbb{Z}
. The inverse of the
Fourier transformation of the sequence
\{g(\xi,n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is defined as

(\mathcal{F}_{\xi,n}^{-1}g)(x,y):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{iny}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}g(\xi,n)d\xi.

In the same way, for
f_{\mu}(x,y)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})
, we set

f_{\mu;n}(x):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{-iny}f_{\mu}(x,y)dy,~~~\widehat{f_{\mu;n}}(\xi):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{-i(\xi\cdot x+ny)}f_{\mu}(x,y)dydx.

2.2
Tools for space-frequency decomposition and
Z
-norm

For a smooth function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1]
, which is supported in
[-8/5,8/5]
and equals to 1 in
[-5/4,5/4]
, we set

\displaystyle\varphi_{k}(\cdot):=\varphi(|\cdot|/2^{k})-\varphi(|\cdot|/2^{k-1})~~(k\in\mathbb{Z}),

\displaystyle\psi_{-1}(\cdot):=1-\sum\limits_{j\geq 0}\varphi_{j}(\cdot)=\varphi(2|\cdot|),\ \psi_{l}(\cdot):=\varphi_{l}(\cdot)~~(l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}),

\displaystyle\psi_{I}:=\sum_{m\in I\bigcap\mathbb{Z}}\psi_{m}~\mathrm{and}~\varphi_{I}:=\sum_{m\in I\bigcap\mathbb{Z}}\varphi_{m}~~\mathrm{for~any~interval}~I\subset\mathbb{R}.

The frequency projection operators
R_{k},R_{I}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
are formally defined for
f=f(x)
as

\widehat{R_{k}f}(\xi):=\psi_{k}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)~~(k\geq-1),\ \widehat{R_{I}f}(\xi):=\psi_{I}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi).

For
g(y)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, one has that

g(y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{g}(n)e^{iny}=\sum_{l\geq-1}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\psi_{l}(n)\widehat{g}(n)e^{iny}:=\sum_{l\geq-1}S_{l}g(y),

where

\displaystyle S_{l}g(y):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\psi_{l}(n)\widehat{g}(n)e^{iny}~~~(l\geq-1).

Moreover, we define

\displaystyle S_{I}g(y):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\psi_{I}(n)\widehat{g}(n)e^{iny}~~~(I\subset\mathbb{R}).

The space-frequency projections are defined as

\varphi_{j}(x)\cdot R_{k}S_{l}f(x,y)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\varphi_{j}(x)\cdot R_{k}f_{0}(x),~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\mathrm{if}~l=-1,\\[5.69054pt]
&\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi_{j}(x)\cdot R_{k}f_{n}(x)\psi_{l}(n)e^{iny},~~~~~~~~~~\mathrm{if}~l\geq 0.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Under the above notations, we have the following atom decomposition

\displaystyle f(x,y)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\geq-1}\sum_{l\geq-1}\varphi_{j}(x)\cdot R_{k}S_{l}f.

(2.1)

Based on this decomposition, it is natural to introduce the
Z
-norm required in Theorem
1.1
:

Definition 2.1.

According to the atom decomposition (
2.1
), we introduce

Z:=\Big\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}):~\|f\|_{Z}~<+\infty\Big\},

(2.2)

where

\|f\|_{Z}:=\sup\limits_{k,l\geq-1}2^{9(k+l)}\Big[\|R_{k}S_{l}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}+\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}2^{j}\|\varphi_{j}(x)\cdot R_{k}S_{l}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\Big].

(2.3)

It is pointed out that the introduction of
Z
-norm in Definition 2.1 is strongly motivated by the
definition (2.5) in
[
17
]
. Moreover, compared with
[
17
]
, we additionally introduce
the Littlewood-Paley decomposition in the periodic direction.

Z
-norm, one has

\|R_{k}S_{l}f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim 2^{-9(k+l)}\|f\|_{Z}~~(k,l\geq-1).

Lemma 3.1
.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
t>0
, the following estimate holds

\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{\pm it\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}+n^{2}}}\psi_{-1}(\xi)d\xi\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim(1+|n|)(1+t)^{-1}.

(3.1)

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{\pm it\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}+n^{2}}}\psi_{-1}(\xi)d\xi\Big|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\psi_{-1}(\xi)|d\xi\leq 4,

we may assume
t\geq 1
in (
3.1
). We rewrite

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{\pm it\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}+n^{2}}}\psi_{-1}(\xi)d\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i\frac{t}{(1+|n|)}\cdot\phi_{\pm}(\xi;~t,x,n)}\psi_{-1}(\xi)d\xi,

where
\phi_{\pm}(\xi;~t,x,n)=(1+|n|)((x\cdot\xi)/t\pm\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}+n^{2}})
. The possible
critical point
\xi
of
\phi_{\pm}
is determined by

x/t\pm\xi/\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}+n^{2}}=0,

which has no real solution when
t\leq|x|
and a unique solution
\xi_{0}=\mp x\sqrt{\frac{1+n^{2}}{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}
when
t>|x|
.

If
t\leq|x|
or
|\xi_{0}|\geq 1
,
one can apply Lemma 2.34 of
[
21
]
(
\mathrm{Page}
65) to get (
3.1
) directly.

If
|\xi_{0}|\leq 1,
by direct calculation

\mathrm{det}~D^{2}_{\xi}\phi_{\pm}(\xi;~t,x,n)=(1+|\xi_{0}|^{2}+n^{2})^{-2}(1+n^{2})(1+|n|)^{2},

then the Hessian
D^{2}\phi_{\pm}(\xi;~t,x,n)
is nondegenerate and
is uniformly bounded with respect to
\xi_{0}
. Hence,

Lemma 2.35 of
[
21
]
(
\mathrm{Page}
66) implies (
3.1
).
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
t>0
and
k\geq 0
, there holds

\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{\pm it\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}+n^{2}}}\varphi_{k}(\xi)d\xi\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim\frac{(2^{2k}+n^{2})}{\sqrt{1+n^{2}}}\cdot t^{-1}.

(3.3)

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle\Phi_{k,n}^{\pm}(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{\pm it\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}+n^{2}}}\varphi_{k}(\xi)d\xi
and
\lambda:=2^{k}
. We only consider the
estimate of
\Phi_{k,n}^{+}(t,x)
since
\Phi_{k,n}^{-}(t,x)
can be treated in a similar way.

It follows from Lemma
3.2
and direct computation that

\displaystyle\Phi_{k,n}^{+}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i\lambda x\cdot\xi}e^{it\sqrt{1+|\lambda\xi|^{2}+n^{2}}}\varphi_{0}(\xi)d\xi

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}e^{i\lambda rx\cdot\theta}e^{it\sqrt{1+(\lambda r)^{2}+n^{2}}}\varphi_{0}(r)rdrd\theta

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{it\sqrt{1+(\lambda r)^{2}+n^{2}}}\varphi_{0}(r)rdr\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}e^{i\lambda rx\cdot\theta}d\theta

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{it\sqrt{1+(\lambda r)^{2}+n^{2}}}\varphi_{0}(r)r\Big[\sum\limits_{\pm}e^{\pm i\lambda r|x|}\omega_{\pm}(\lambda r|x|)\Big]dr

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\pm}\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i\lambda\phi^{\pm}_{\lambda,n}(r;~t,|x|)}\omega_{\pm}(\lambda r|x|)\varphi_{0}(r)rdr,

where
\phi^{\pm}_{\lambda,n}(r;~t,|x|)=\frac{t}{\lambda}\sqrt{1+(\lambda r)^{2}+n^{2}}\pm r|x|
. In what follows,
we only consider the phase
\phi^{-}_{\lambda,n}(r;~t,|x|)
in (
3.4
) since the treatment for
\phi^{+}_{\lambda,n}(r;~t,|x|)

is easier.

Case 1:
t\thickapprox|x|(1+|n|/\lambda)\gtrsim(1+n^{2})/(\lambda^{3}+\lambda^{2}|n|)
and
t>|x|

Direct calculation shows

\partial_{r}\phi^{-}_{\lambda,n}(r;~t,|x|)=\frac{t\lambda r}{\sqrt{1+(\lambda r)^{2}+n^{2}}}-|x|,\ \partial_{r}^{2}\phi^{-}_{\lambda,n}(r;~t,|x|)=\frac{t\lambda(1+n^{2})}{(1+(\lambda r)^{2}+n^{2})^{3/2}}.

Due to
\frac{5}{8}\leq r\leq\frac{8}{5}
in the support of
\varphi_{0}(r)
, then

\partial_{r}^{2}\phi^{-}_{\lambda,n}(r;~t,|x|)\thickapprox\frac{t\lambda(1+n^{2})}{(1+\lambda^{2}+n^{2})^{3/2}}:=\tau~~~~~(\frac{5}{8}\leq r\leq\frac{8}{5}).

(3.5)

Note that
r=r_{0}=\frac{|x|\sqrt{1+n^{2}}}{\lambda\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}
is the unique
solution of
\partial_{r}\phi^{-}_{\lambda,n}(r;~t,|x|)=0
,
we now take the following decomposition

\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i\lambda\phi^{-}_{\lambda,n}}\omega_{-}(\lambda r|x|)\varphi_{0}(r)rdr:=H_{1}+H_{2},

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i\lambda\phi^{-}_{\lambda,n}}\omega_{-}(\lambda r|x|)\varphi_{0}(r)r\chi_{0}((\lambda\tau)^{1/2}(r-r_{0}))dr,

\displaystyle\displaystyle H_{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i\lambda\phi^{-}_{\lambda,n}}\omega_{-}(\lambda r|x|)\varphi_{0}(r)r\{1-\chi_{0}((\lambda\tau)^{1/2}(r-r_{0}))\}dr,

and
\chi_{0}(r)
is a cut-off function near
r=0
.
By (
3.2
), one gets that when
\frac{5}{8}\leq r\leq\frac{8}{5}
,

|\partial_{r}^{k}\omega_{-}(\lambda r|x|)|\leq C_{k}(\lambda r|x|)^{-1/2}\leq C_{k}(\lambda|x|)^{-1/2},~~\forall k\geq 0.

(3.6)

With (
3.6
) for
k=0
, then

\displaystyle|H_{1}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi_{0}(r)|\omega_{-}(\lambda r|x|)\chi_{0}((\lambda\tau)^{1/2}(r-r_{0}))|dr\lesssim\lambda^{2}(\lambda\tau)^{-1/2}(\lambda|x|)^{-1/2}.

(3.7)

In addition, we rewrite
H_{2}
as

H_{2}=\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i\lambda\tau\phi^{-}_{\lambda,n,\tau}}\omega_{-}(\lambda r|x|)\varphi_{0}(r)r\{1-\chi_{0}((\lambda\tau)^{1/2}(r-r_{0}))\}dr,

where
\phi^{-}_{\lambda,n,\tau}=\phi^{-}_{\lambda,n}/\tau

Here, it is easy to verify

Due to
\partial_{s}^{2}\phi^{-}_{\lambda,n,\tau}(s;~t,|x|)\simeq 1

\Big|\partial_{r}^{l}\Big(\frac{\partial_{r}\phi^{-}_{\lambda,n,\tau}}{|\partial_{r}\phi^{-}_{\lambda,n,\tau}|^{2}}\Big)(r)\Big|\leq~C_{l}|r-r_{0}|^{-1-l}~~(l\in\mathbb{N}).

\displaystyle|H_{2}|

\displaystyle=\Big|\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{L}^{2}e^{i\lambda\tau\phi^{-}_{\lambda,n,\tau}})\omega_{-}(\lambda r|x|)\varphi_{0}(r)r\{1-\chi_{0}((\lambda\tau)^{1/2}(r-r_{0}))\}dr\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i\lambda\tau\phi^{-}_{\lambda,n,\tau}}(\mathfrak{L}^{\ast})^{2}\Big[\omega_{-}(\lambda r|x|)\varphi_{0}(r)r\{1-\chi_{0}((\lambda\tau)^{1/2}(r-r_{0}))\}\Big]dr\Big|

Lemma 3.5

Lemma 3.7

(Finite band property)

1\leq p\leq\infty
,

\|\partial_{x}R_{k}S_{l}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim 2^{k}\|R_{k}S_{l}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})},~~\|\partial_{y}R_{k}S_{l}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim 2^{l}\|R_{k}S_{l}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}.

(3.21)

Proof.

At first, we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}R_{k}S_{l}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Big\|\partial_{x}R_{k}\Big(\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{n}(x)\psi_{l}(n)e^{iny}\Big)\Big\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}dy\Big)^{1/p}

(3.22)

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{k}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Big\|R_{k}\Big(\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{n}(x)\psi_{l}(n)e^{iny}\Big)\Big\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}dy\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{k}\|R_{k}S_{l}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}.

In addition, set
g_{l}(z)=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{n}{2^{l}}\cdot\psi_{[l-2,l+2]}(n)e^{inz}
, then

\displaystyle\|\partial_{y}R_{k}S_{l}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big\|\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(R_{k}f_{n}(x)n\psi_{l}(n))e^{iny}\Big\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}dx\Big)^{1/p}

(3.23)

\displaystyle=2^{l}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{T}}(R_{k}S_{l}f)(x,y-z)g_{l}(z)dz\Big\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}dx\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{l}\|R_{k}S_{l}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\|g_{l}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}.

In the following estimate of
\|g_{l}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}
,
l\geq 2
is assumed since the case
l<2

can be treated similarly. By Poisson summation formula, we have

\displaystyle g_{l}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i(z+2n\pi)\cdot x}\varphi_{[l-2,l+2]}(x)\cdot\frac{x}{2^{l}}dx

(3.24)

\displaystyle=2^{l}\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i(z+2n\pi)\cdot 2^{l}\xi}\varphi_{[-2,2]}(\xi)\cdot\xi d\xi.

It follows from the method of non-stationary phase that

\Big|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i(z+2n\pi)\cdot 2^{l}\xi}\varphi_{[-2,2]}(\xi)\cdot\xi d\xi\Big|\lesssim(1+2^{l}|z+2n\pi|)^{-6}.

(3.25)

Then

\displaystyle\|g_{l}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}2^{l}\Big|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i(z+2n\pi)\cdot 2^{l}\xi}\varphi_{[-2,2]}(\xi)\cdot\xi d\xi\Big|dz

(3.26)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}2^{l}(1+2^{l}|z+2n\pi|)^{-6}dz\lesssim 1.

Therefore, the estimates in (
3.21
) come from (
3.22
), (
3.23
) and (
3.26
).
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

(Bernstein-type inequality)

With
R_{k},S_{l}
defined in Section 2, one has

\|R_{k}S_{l}f\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim 2^{(k+l/2)(1-2/r)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}~~(2\leq r\leq+\infty).

(3.27)

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\|R_{k}S_{l}f\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big\|\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(R_{k}f_{n}(x)\psi_{l}(n))e^{iny}\Big\|^{r}_{L^{r}(\mathbb{T})}dx\Big)^{1/r}

(3.28)

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(x-x_{1},y-y_{1})w_{k}(x_{1})h_{l}(y_{1})dx_{1}dy_{1}\Big\|^{r}_{L^{r}(\mathbb{T})}dx\Big)^{1/r}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\|w_{k}(x)h_{l}(y)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\|w_{k}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|h_{l}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T})},

where
1/r+1=1/2+1/q
and

\displaystyle w_{k}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}\psi_{k}(\xi)d\xi,\ h_{l}(y)=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\psi_{l}(n)e^{iny}.

Direct calculation shows

\|w_{k}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim 2^{k(1-2/r)}.

(3.29)

In the following estimate of
\|h_{l}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T})}
,
l\geq 0
is assumed
since the case of
l=-1
can be done analogously. By the
similarly way to deal with
g_{l}(z)
in (
3.24
)-(
3.26
), we have

\displaystyle h_{l}(y)=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i(y+2n\pi)\cdot x}\psi_{l}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i(y+2n\pi)\cdot x}\varphi_{0}(x/2^{l})dx=2^{l}\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i(y+2n\pi)\cdot 2^{l}x}\varphi_{0}(x)dx

and

\Big|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i(y+2n\pi)\cdot 2^{l}x}\varphi_{0}(x)dx\Big|\lesssim(1+2^{l}|y+2n\pi|)^{-6}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|h_{l}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T})}\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{l}\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big(\int_{0}^{2\pi}(1+2^{l}|y+2n\pi|)^{-6q}dy\Big)^{1/q}

(3.30)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{l(1-1/q)}\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big(\int_{0}^{2^{l}\cdot 2\pi}(1+|w+2^{l}\cdot 2n\pi|)^{-6q}dw\Big)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{l(1-1/q)}\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z},~|n|\leq 3}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+|w|)^{-6q}dw\Big)^{1/q}

\displaystyle+2^{l(1-1/q)}\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z},~|n|\geq 4}\Big(\int_{0}^{2^{l}\cdot 2\pi}(2^{l}|n|\pi)^{-6q}dw\Big)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{l(1-1/q)}+2^{l(1-1/q)}\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z},~|n|\geq 4}\Big((2^{l}|n|\pi)^{-5q}\Big)^{1/q}\lesssim 2^{l(1-2/r)/2}.

Substituting (
3.29
) and (
3.30
) into (
3.28
) yields (
3.27
).∎

Lemma 3.9
.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we have

\Big\|R_{k}S_{l}\Big(\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\partial_{x}}{\sqrt{1-\Delta_{x}+n^{2}}}f_{n}(x)e^{iny}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}

(3.31)

and

\Big\|R_{k}S_{l}\Big(\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{n}{\sqrt{1-\Delta_{x}+n^{2}}}f_{n}(x)e^{iny}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}.

(3.32)

Proof.

We only prove (
3.31
) since the estimate (
3.32
) can be treated by a similar way. With the
definitions of
R_{k}
and
S_{l}
, one has

\displaystyle R_{k}S_{l}\Big(\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\partial_{x}}{\sqrt{1-\Delta_{x}+n^{2}}}f_{n}(x)e^{iny}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}\psi_{k}(\xi)\Big(\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{i\xi}{\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}+n^{2}}}\widehat{f_{n}}(\xi)\psi_{l}(n)e^{iny}\Big)d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}\psi_{k}(\xi)\Big[\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathcal{F}_{x}(f)(\xi,y-y_{1})\Big(\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{i\xi}{\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}+n^{2}}}\psi_{l}(n)e^{iny_{1}}\Big)dy_{1}\Big]d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}\psi_{k}(\xi)\mathcal{F}_{x}(f)(\xi,y-y_{1})\Big(\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{i\xi}{\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}+n^{2}}}\psi_{l}(n)e^{iny_{1}}\Big)d\xi\Big]dy_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(x-x_{1},y-y_{1})\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix_{1}\cdot\xi}\psi_{k}(\xi)\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{i\xi}{\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}+n^{2}}}\psi_{l}(n)e^{iny_{1}}d\xi\Big)dx_{1}\Big]dy_{1}.

Then

\Big\|R_{k}S_{l}\Big(\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\partial_{x}}{\sqrt{1-\Delta_{x}+n^{2}}}f_{n}(x)e^{iny}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\|h_{kl}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})},

(3.33)

where

h_{kl}(x,y):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}\psi_{k}(\xi)\Big(\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}+n^{2}}}\psi_{l}(n)e^{iny}\Big)d\xi.

On the other hand, by Poisson summation formula, we have

\displaystyle h_{kl}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}\psi_{k}(\xi)\Big(\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i(y+2n\pi)\cdot z}\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}+z^{2}}}\psi_{l}(z)dz\Big)d\xi

(3.34)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{i(y+2n\pi)\cdot z}\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}+z^{2}}}\psi_{k}(\xi)\psi_{l}(z)dzd\xi.

It follows from the method of non-stationary phase that

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{i(y+2n\pi)\cdot z}\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}+z^{2}}}\psi_{k}(\xi)\psi_{l}(z)dzd\xi\Big|

(3.35)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{2k}(1+2^{k}|x|)^{-4}2^{l}(1+2^{l}|y+2n\pi|)^{-6}.

Substituting (
3.35
) into (
3.34
) shows

\displaystyle\|h_{kl}(x,y)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}2^{2k}(1+2^{k}|x|)^{-4}dx\cdot\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}2^{l}(1+2^{l}|y+2n\pi|)^{-6}dy\lesssim 1.

(3.36)

Therefore, (
3.31
) comes from (
3.33
) and (
3.36
).
And the proof of Lemma
3.9
is completed.
∎

4
Two crucial propositions and the proof of Theorem 1.1

At first, we state two crucial propositions which will be utilized to prove Theorem 1.1.

Proposition 4.1
.

(Continuity and boundedness of
Z
-norm)

Assume
that
u\in C([0,T_{0}],H^{N+1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}))\cap C^{1}([0,T_{0}],H^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}))
is a solution of problem (
1.1
).
Define
U
as in (
2.7
) with the property of
U_{0}=U(0)\in Z
.
Then one has

\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}\|e^{it\Lambda}U(t)\|_{Z}~\leq~C\Big(T_{0},\|U_{0}\|_{Z},\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}\|U(t)\|_{H^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\Big)

(4.1)

and the mapping
t\mapsto e^{it\Lambda}U(t)
is continuous from
[0,T_{0}]
to
Z
.

Proposition 4.2
.

(Bootstrap estimate)

Under the assumptions in Proposition
4.1
, for
N_{0}:=50
and
0<T_{0}\leq e^{c_{0}/\epsilon_{0}^{2}}
,
if

\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}(1+t)^{-\kappa}\|U(t)\|_{H^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}+\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}\|e^{it\Lambda}U(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{N_{0}}}\leq\varepsilon_{0}<1,

(4.2)

then

\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}\|e^{it\Lambda}U(t)-U(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{N_{0}}}\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}^{2}+c_{0}\varepsilon_{0},

(4.3)

where
0<\kappa\ll 1
, and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}^{N_{0}}}:=\|\cdot\|_{H^{N_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}+\|\cdot\|_{Z}
.

The proofs of Proposition 4.1 and Proposition 4.2 will be postponed to Section 5 and Section 6, respectively.
In addition, by the standard local existence theory and energy estimate for the second order quasilinear wave
equations (see Chapter 6 of
[
16
]
), we have

Proposition 4.3
.

(Local existence)

Under the assumptions in Theorem
1.1
,
problem (
1.1
) has a unique solution
u\in C([0,1],H^{N+1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}))\cap C^{1}([0,1],H^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}))
. Moreover

\sup\limits_{t\in[0,1]}\|u(t)\|_{H^{N+1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}+\sup\limits_{t\in[0,1]}\|\dot{u}(t)\|_{H^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{N+1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}

(4.4)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N+1}(t^{\prime})-\mathcal{E}_{N+1}(t)\lesssim\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}\mathcal{E}_{N+1}(s)\cdot\Big[\sum\limits_{|\rho|\leq 2}\|\partial_{x,y}^{\rho}u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}+\sum\limits_{|\rho|\leq 1}\|\partial_{x,y}^{\rho}\dot{u}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\Big]ds

(4.5)

for any
t\leq t^{\prime}\in[0,1]
, where the definition of
\mathcal{E}_{N+1}(t)
is given in (
1.12
).

Based on the preparations above, we now prove Theorem 1.1.

Proof of Theorem 1.1.
By Proposition 4.3 and Proposition 4.1, there is
T_{0}>0
such that the unique
solution
u\in C([0,T_{0}],H^{N+1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}))\cap C^{1}([0,T_{0}],H^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}))

of problem (
1.1
) exists. Moreover, there exists a suitably large constant
C_{1}>2
such that

\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}\|e^{it\Lambda}U(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{N_{0}}}~\leq~C_{1}\varepsilon_{0}.

(4.6)

Under (
4.6
), we next show the following bootstrap estimate

\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}\|e^{it\Lambda}U(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{N_{0}}}\leq\frac{1}{2}C_{1}\varepsilon_{0}.

(4.7)

Note that

\displaystyle U(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq-1}\sum_{l\geq-1}R_{k}S_{l}U

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq-1}\sum_{l\geq-1}e^{-it\Lambda}R_{k}S_{l}(e^{it\Lambda}U)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq-1}\sum_{l\geq-1}R_{[-1,k+2]}e^{-it\Lambda}S_{[l-2,l+2]}(R_{k}S_{l}(e^{it\Lambda}U)).

With the definition
u=\frac{i}{2\Lambda}(U-\overline{U})
, by Lemma
3.7
and Lemma
3.9
, we arrive at

\displaystyle(1+t)\sup\limits_{|\rho|\leq 2}\|\partial_{x,y}^{\rho}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}

(4.8)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(1+t)\sup\limits_{|\rho|\leq 2}\sum_{k\geq-1}\sum_{l\geq-1}\|\partial_{x,y}^{\rho}(R_{k}S_{l}u(t))\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(1+t)\sum_{\mbox{\tiny$\begin{array}[]{c}a,b\in\mathbb{N}\\
a+b\leq 2\end{array}$}}\sum_{k\geq-1}\sum_{l\geq-1}2^{ak+bl}\|R_{k}S_{l}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(1+t)\sum_{\mbox{\tiny$\begin{array}[]{c}a,b\in\mathbb{N}\\
a+b\leq 2\end{array}$}}\sum_{k\geq-1}\sum_{l\geq-1}2^{ak+bl}\|R_{k}S_{l}U(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}.

On the other hand, by (
3.15
), (
2.4
) and (
4.6
), one has that for
t\in[0,T_{0}]
,

\displaystyle\|R_{k}S_{l}U\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}

(4.9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|R_{[-1,k+2]}e^{-it\Lambda}S_{[l-2,l+2]}(R_{k}S_{l}(e^{it\Lambda}U))\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(1+t)^{-1}2^{2(k+l)}\Big\|R_{k}S_{l}(e^{it\Lambda}U)\Big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(1+t)^{-1}2^{2(k+l)}2^{-9(k+l)}\|e^{it\Lambda}U(t)\|_{Z}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle C_{1}\varepsilon_{0}(1+t)^{-1}2^{-7(k+l)}.

Combining (
4.8
) with (
4.9
) yields

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}[(1+t)\sup\limits_{|\rho|\leq 2}\|\partial_{x,y}^{\rho}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}]\lesssim C_{1}\varepsilon_{0}.

Similarly,
\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}[(1+t)\sup\limits_{|\rho|\leq 1}\|\partial_{x,y}^{\rho}\dot{u}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}]\lesssim C_{1}\varepsilon_{0}
holds. Thus, for
t\in[0,T_{0}]
,

\sum\limits_{|\rho|\leq 2}\|\partial_{x,y}^{\rho}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}+\sum\limits_{|\rho|\leq 1}\|\partial_{x,y}^{\rho}\dot{u}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\leq~C_{1}C_{2}\varepsilon_{0}\cdot(1+t)^{-1}.

(4.10)

By (
4.5
) and (
4.10
), we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N+1}(t)-\mathcal{E}_{N+1}(0)\leq C_{3}\cdot\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{E}_{N+1}(s)\cdot C_{1}C_{2}\varepsilon_{0}\cdot(1+s)^{-1}ds.

Together with Gronwall’s inequality, this yields

\mathcal{E}_{N+1}(t)\leq\mathcal{E}_{N+1}(0)e^{C_{1}C_{2}C_{3}\varepsilon_{0}\cdot\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{-1}ds}\leq\mathcal{E}_{N+1}(0)(1+t)^{2A_{0}\epsilon_{0}}.

(4.11)

where

. By (
4.6
), (
1.12
) and Sobolev embedding theorem, as long as
\varepsilon_{0}

is sufficiently small, then

\frac{1}{2}\Big(\|\dot{u}(t)\|^{2}_{H^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}+\|u(t)\|^{2}_{H^{N+1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\Big)\leq\mathcal{E}_{N+1}(t)\leq 2\Big(\|\dot{u}(t)\|^{2}_{H^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}+\|u(t)\|^{2}_{H^{N+1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\Big),~~~t\in[0,T_{0}].

Therefore, combing this with (

\|\dot{u}(t)\|_{H^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}+\|u(t)\|_{H^{N+1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\leq 4\varepsilon_{0}(1+t)^{A_{0}\epsilon_{0}}.

(4.12)

In addition, by
U(t):=\dot{u}(t)-i\Lambda u(t)
, then there is a constant
C_{4}>0
, such that

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{l+l_{4}}(2^{k}+2^{l})(2^{k_{1}}+2^{k_{4}}+2^{\mathrm{max}(l_{1},l_{4})})2^{(k+l/2)(1-2/r)}\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\|G\|_{L^{p_{2}p_{3}/(p_{2}+p_{3})}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})},

In addition, it follows from Lemma
3.7
and Lemma
3.9
that

\displaystyle\|G\|_{L^{p_{2}p_{3}/(p_{2}+p_{3})}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}

(A.12)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big[R_{k_{2}}S_{l_{2}}\Big(\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(\partial_{x}~\mathrm{or}~n)}{\sqrt{1-\Delta_{x}+n^{2}}}g_{n}(x)e^{iny}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle\cdot\Big[(1,\partial_{x},\partial_{y})R_{k_{3}}S_{l_{3}}\Big(\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(\partial_{x}~\mathrm{or}~n)}{\sqrt{1-\Delta_{x}+n^{2}}}h_{n}(x)e^{iny}\Big)\Big]\Big\|_{L^{p_{2}p_{3}/(p_{2}+p_{3})}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\Big\|R_{k_{2}}S_{l_{2}}\Big(\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(\partial_{x}~\mathrm{or}~n)}{\sqrt{1-\Delta_{x}+n^{2}}}g_{n}(x)e^{iny}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\cdot(2^{k_{3}}+2^{l_{3}})\Big\|R_{k_{3}}S_{l_{3}}\Big(\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(\partial_{x}~\mathrm{or}~n)}{\sqrt{1-\Delta_{x}+n^{2}}}h_{n}(x)e^{iny}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{p_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(2^{k_{3}}+2^{l_{3}})\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\|h\|_{L^{p_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}.

On the other hand, on the support of the integrand in (
A.10
), the following restrictions hold

\displaystyle(k_{2},k_{3})\in\chi_{k_{4}},~~~~~(l,l_{4})\in\chi_{l_{1}},~~~~~(l_{2},l_{3})\in\chi_{l_{4}}.

(A.13)

Using (
A.9
) and (
A.11
)-(
A.13
), the left-hand side of (
A.8
) can be dominated by

\displaystyle\sum_{\mbox{\tiny$\begin{array}[]{c}-1\leq k_{4}\leq\mathrm{max}(k_{2},k_{3})+8\end{array}$}}\sum_{\mbox{\tiny$\begin{array}[]{c}-1\leq l_{4}\leq\mathrm{max}(l_{2},l_{3})+8\end{array}$}}\|T_{k_{4},l_{4}}[f,g,h]\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}l^{2}_{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{\mbox{\tiny$\begin{array}[]{c}-1\leq k_{4}\leq\mathrm{max}(k_{2},k_{3})+8\end{array}$}}\sum_{\mbox{\tiny$\begin{array}[]{c}-1\leq l_{4}\leq\mathrm{max}(l_{2},l_{3})+8\end{array}$}}2^{l+l_{4}}(2^{k}+2^{l})(2^{k_{1}}+2^{k_{4}}+2^{\mathrm{max}(l_{1},l_{4})})2^{(k+l/2)(1-2/r)}

\displaystyle\cdot\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\|G\|_{L^{p_{2}p_{3}/(p_{2}+p_{3})}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{k+k_{1}+\mathrm{max}(k_{2},k_{3})}2^{3l+2\mathrm{max}(l_{2},l_{3})}2^{(k+l/2)(1-2/r)}(2^{k_{3}}+2^{l_{3}})\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\|h\|_{L^{p_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{k+k_{1}+\mathrm{max}(k_{2},k_{3})+k_{3}}2^{3l+2\mathrm{max}(l_{2},l_{3})+l_{3}}2^{(k+l/2)(1-2/r)}\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\|h\|_{L^{p_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}.

This shows (
A.8
) and then Lemma
A.2
is proved.
∎

B
Temporal decay estimate for the 3D linear Klein-Gordon problem

In this Appendix, we establish the time-decay rate of smooth solutions to the 3D homogeneous Klein-Gordon
equation on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}
with compactly supported initial data. The related result can be stated as:

Theorem B.1
.

Let
V(t,x,y)
be the smooth solution to the following problem

\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{x,y}+1)V=0,\\[5.69054pt]
(V(0),\dot{V}(0))=(V_{0},V_{1})(x,y)\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}),\end{cases}

(B.1)

then one has

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{|\rho|\leq 2}\|\partial_{x,y}^{\rho}V(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}+\sup\limits_{|\rho|\leq 1}\|\partial_{x,y}^{\rho}\dot{V}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim(1+t)^{-1}.

(B.2)

Proof.

We express
V,V_{0},V_{1}
in problem (
B.1
) as follows

\displaystyle V=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{n}(t,x)e^{iny},~~V_{0}=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{0;n}(x)e^{iny},~~V_{1}=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{1;n}(x)e^{iny}.

(B.3)

Substituting (
B.3
) into problem (
B.1
) yields that for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{x}+n^{2}+1)V_{n}(t,x)=0,\\[5.69054pt]
(V_{n}(0,x),\dot{V_{n}}(0,x))=(V_{0;n}(x),V_{1;n}(x)).\end{cases}

(B.4)

Taking the Fourier transformation in the
x
-variables for problem (
B.4
) obtains

\displaystyle\widehat{V_{n}}(t,\xi)=\frac{(\widehat{V_{0;n}}(\xi)-i\widehat{V_{1;n}}(\xi)/\Lambda_{n}(\xi))e^{it\Lambda_{n}(\xi)}}{2}+\frac{(\widehat{V_{0;n}}(\xi)+i\widehat{V_{1;n}}(\xi)/\Lambda_{n}(\xi))e^{-it\Lambda_{n}(\xi)}}{2}.

(B.5)

Due to

\displaystyle V(t,x,y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i(ny+x\cdot\xi)}\widehat{V_{n}}(t,\xi)d\xi,

(B.6)

then substituting (
B.5
) into (
B.6
) obtains the explicit expression of
V
.

To obtain (
B.2
), it suffices to prove

\|V(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim(1+t)^{-1}

(B.7)

since the treatments of
\|\partial_{x,y}^{\rho}(V,\dot{V})(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}
(
|\rho|\geq 1
) are completely analogous. Moreover, we only need to show (
B.7
) for
t\geq 1
since (
B.7
) is trivial for
0\leq t\leq 1
.
In view of (
B.5
) and (
B.6
), we have

\|V(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim K_{1}+K_{2},

(B.8)

where

K_{1}=\Big\|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i(ny+x\cdot\xi)}e^{\pm it\Lambda_{n}(\xi)}\widehat{V_{0;n}}(\xi)d\xi\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}

and

K_{2}=\Big\|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i(ny+x\cdot\xi)}e^{\pm it\Lambda_{n}(\xi)}\cdot\frac{\widehat{V_{1;n}}(\xi)}{\Lambda_{n}(\xi)}d\xi\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}.

We next focus on the estimate of
K_{1}
since the analogous argument can be done for
K_{2}
. Note that

K_{1}\leq K_{11}+K_{12},

(B.9)

where

K_{11}=\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{\pm it\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}}}\widehat{V_{0;0}}(\xi)d\xi\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

and

K_{12}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z},|n|\geq 1}\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i(ny+x\cdot\xi)}e^{\pm it\Lambda_{n}(\xi)}\widehat{V_{0;n}}(\xi)d\xi\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}.

For
K_{11}
in (
B.9
), by the definition of
\varphi_{l},\psi_{-1}
in Section 2, we have

K_{11}=\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{\pm it\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}}}\Big(\psi_{-1}(\xi)+\sum\limits_{l=1}^{+\infty}\varphi_{l}(\xi)\Big)\widehat{V_{0;0}}(\xi)d\xi\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(B.10)

It follows from Young’s inequality and Lemma
3.1
that

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{\pm it\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}}}\psi_{-1}(\xi)\widehat{V_{0;0}}(\xi)d\xi\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

(B.11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(e^{\pm it\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}}}\psi_{-1}(\xi))\ast\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\widehat{V_{0;0}})\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(e^{\pm it\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}}}\psi_{-1}(\xi))\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\cdot\Big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\widehat{V_{0;0}})\Big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(e^{\pm it\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}}}\psi_{-1}(\xi))\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\cdot\|V_{0;0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(1+t)^{-1}\|V_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim(1+t)^{-1}.

Similarly, by Young’s inequality and Lemma
3.3
, one has

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=1}^{+\infty}\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{\pm it\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}}}\varphi_{l}(\xi)\widehat{V_{0;0}}(\xi)d\xi\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

(B.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=1}^{+\infty}\Big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(e^{\pm it\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}}}\varphi_{l}(\xi))\ast\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{[l-2,l+2]}(\xi)\widehat{V_{0;0}}(\xi))\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=1}^{+\infty}\Big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(e^{\pm it\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}}}\varphi_{l}(\xi))\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\cdot\Big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{[l-2,l+2]}(\xi)\widehat{V_{0;0}}(\xi))\Big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=1}^{+\infty}2^{2l}(1+t)^{-1}\cdot\Big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\Big(\varphi_{[l-2,l+2]}(\xi)(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-2}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{2}\widehat{V_{0;0}}(\xi)\Big)\Big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=1}^{+\infty}2^{2l}(1+t)^{-1}\Big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\Big(\varphi_{[l-2,l+2]}(\xi)(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-2}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\Big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\Big((1+|\xi|^{2})^{2}\widehat{V_{0;0}}(\xi)\Big)\Big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=1}^{+\infty}2^{-2l}(1+t)^{-1}\Big\|(1-\Delta_{x})^{2}V_{0;0}(x)\Big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim(1+t)^{-1}.

Collecting (
B.10
)-(
B.12
) yields

K_{11}\lesssim(1+t)^{-1}.

(B.13)

Next, we deal with
K_{12}
in (
B.9
). Note that

\displaystyle K_{12}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z},|n|\geq 1}\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{\pm it\Lambda_{n}(\xi)}\widehat{V_{0;n}}(\xi)d\xi\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

(B.14)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z},|n|\geq 1}\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{\pm it\Lambda_{n}(\xi)}\Big[\psi_{-1}(\xi)+\sum\limits_{l=1}^{+\infty}\varphi_{l}(\xi)\Big]\widehat{V_{0;n}}(\xi)d\xi\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Similarly to (
B.11
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z},|n|\geq 1}\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{\pm it\Lambda_{n}(\xi)}\psi_{-1}(\xi)\widehat{V_{0;n}}(\xi)d\xi\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

(B.15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z},|n|\geq 1}n^{-4}\Big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(e^{\pm it\Lambda_{n}(\xi)}\psi_{-1}(\xi))\ast\mathcal{F}^{-1}\Big(n^{4}\cdot\widehat{V_{0;n}}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z},|n|\geq 1}n^{-4}\Big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(e^{\pm it\Lambda_{n}(\xi)}\psi_{-1}(\xi))\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\cdot\Big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\Big(\widehat{(\partial_{y}^{4}V_{0})_{n}}(\xi)\Big)\Big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z},|n|\geq 1}n^{-4}|n|(1+t)^{-1}\cdot\|(\partial_{y}^{4}V_{0})_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(1+t)^{-1}\|\partial_{y}^{4}V_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim(1+t)^{-1}.

On the other hand, analogous to (
B.12
), one has

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z},|n|\geq 1}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{+\infty}\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{\pm it\Lambda_{n}(\xi)}\varphi_{l}(\xi)\widehat{V_{0;n}}(\xi)d\xi\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

(B.16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z},|n|\geq 1}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{+\infty}n^{-4}\Big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(e^{\pm it\Lambda_{n}(\xi)}\varphi_{l}(\xi))\ast\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{[l-2,l+2]}(\xi)n^{4}\cdot\widehat{V_{0;n}}(\xi))\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z},|n|\geq 1}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{+\infty}n^{-4}\Big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(e^{\pm it\Lambda_{n}(\xi)}\varphi_{l}(\xi))\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\cdot\Big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{[l-2,l+2]}(\xi)\widehat{(\partial_{y}^{4}V_{0})_{n}}(\xi))\Big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z},|n|\geq 1}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{+\infty}|n|^{-3}2^{2l}(1+t)^{-1}\cdot\Big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\Big(\varphi_{[l-2,l+2]}(\xi)(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-2}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{2}\widehat{(\partial_{y}^{4}V_{0})_{n}}(\xi)\Big)\Big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z},|n|\geq 1}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{+\infty}n^{-2}2^{2l}(1+t)^{-1}\Big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\Big(\varphi_{[l-2,l+2]}(\xi)(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-2}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\times

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\Big((1+|\xi|^{2})^{2}\widehat{(\partial_{y}^{4}V_{0})_{n}}(\xi)\Big)\Big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z},|n|\geq 1}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{+\infty}n^{-2}2^{-2l}(1+t)^{-1}\Big\|(1-\Delta_{x})^{2}(\partial_{y}^{4}V_{0})_{n}(x)\Big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z},|n|\geq 1}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{+\infty}n^{-2}2^{-2l}(1+t)^{-1}\Big\|(1-\Delta_{x})^{2}\partial_{y}^{4}V_{0}(x,y)\Big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim(1+t)^{-1}.

By (
B.14
)-(
B.16
), then

\displaystyle K_{12}\lesssim(1+t)^{-1}.

(B.17)

Collecting (
B.9
), (
B.13
) and (
B.17
) derives
K_{1}\lesssim(1+t)^{-1}
. Then the proof of the Theorem B.1
is completed.
∎

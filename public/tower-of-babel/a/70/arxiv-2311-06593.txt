Syntomic cohomology and real topological cyclic homology
Doosung Park

Abstract
. We define the motivic filtrations on real topological Hochschild homology and its companions. In particular, we prove that real topological cyclic homology admits a natural complete filtration whose graded pieces are equivariant suspensions of syntomic cohomology. As an application, we compute the equivariant slices of p p -completed real K K -theories of F p ​ [ x ] / x e \mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/x^{e} and Z / p n \mathbb{Z}/p^{n} after certain suspensions assuming a real refinement of the Dundas-McCarthy-Goodwillie theorem and an announced result of Antieau-Krause-Nikolaus.

1.
Introduction

The cyclotomic trace
K\to\mathrm{TC}
of Bökstedt-Hsiang-Madsen
[
7
]
approximates algebraic
K
-theory via topological cyclic homology.
Together with the theorem of Dundas-Goodwillie-McCarthy
[
13
]
,
trace methods become effective tools for computing algebraic K-theory,
see e.g. computations of Hesselholt-Madsen on algebraic
K
-theory of truncated polynomial algebras
[
15
]
and local fields
[
16
]
.

Recent advances in syntomic cohomology and prisms offer further computational tools for algebraic
K
-theory.
Bhatt, Morrow, and Scholze
[
5
]
provide the motivic filtration on
\mathrm{TC}
whose graded pieces are syntomic cohomology
\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)
.
Bhatt and Scholze
[
6
]
further study the structure of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)
by introducing prisms.
As an application,
they prove in
[
6
, Theorem 1.18]
that
\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)
is quasisyntomic locally concentrated in degree
0
.
Liu and Wang
[
23
]
employ this technique to revisit topological cyclic homology of local fields.
Mathew
[
27
, Theorem 10.4]
computes
\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)(k[x]/x^{2})
for a perfect
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra
k
and
p>2
, and Sulyma
[
36
]
generalizes this to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}(i)(k[x]/x^{e})
for all
p
and integer
e\geq 1
.
Antieau, Krause, and Nikolaus
[
1
]
announce the computation of
\pi_{i}\mathrm{TC}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n};\mathbb{Z}_{p})
and hence
K_{i}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n})
using the techniques of syntomic cohomology.

In contrast, only few computations for hermitian and real
K
-theories are known especially if
2
is not invertible.
However,
as written in
[
29
, §1.1]
,
a work in progress of Harpaz-Nikolaus-Shah constructs the real cyclotomic trace

\mathrm{KR}(\mathcal{C},\text{\char 21\relax})\to\mathrm{TCR}(\mathcal{C},\text{\char 21\relax})

from real
K
-theory to real topological cyclic homology for every Poincaré
\infty
-category
(\mathcal{C},\text{\char 21\relax})
.
We refer to
[
9
]
and
[
8
]
for hermitian and real
K
-theories of Poincaré categories,
and see
[
8
, §B.2]
for the comparison with Schlichting’s hermitian K-theory of exact categories
[
32
]
when
2
is invertible.
Furthermore,
Harpaz, Nikolaus, and Shah claim that the real cyclotomic trace
satisfies a real refinement of the theorem of Dundas-Goodwillie-McCarthy.
This would imply that real trace methods are effective tools for computing hermitian and real
K
-theories.

To utilize real trace methods,
the computations of real topological Hochschild homology are also desired.
We review the recent progress on this as follows.
Dotto, Moi, Patchkoria, and Reeh
[
11
, Theorem 5.15]
compute
\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
,
which refines Bökstedt’s computation of
\mathrm{THH}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
.
Dotto, Moi, and Patchkoria
[
10
, Theorem D, Proposition 4.2, Remark 5.14]
compute
\mathrm{THR}(k)
and
\mathrm{TCR}(k)
for every perfect field
k
(as observed in
[
29
, Proposition 6.26]
, a similar argument can be used to compute
\mathrm{THR}(k)
for every perfect
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra
k
).
Quigley and Shah
[
29
]
introduce the notion of real cyclotomic spectra and show that
\mathrm{THR}
is a real cyclotomic spectrum,
which refines the results of Nikolaus-Scholze
[
28
, Theorem 1.4, Corollary 1.5]
.
Using this method,

\mathrm{TCR}(k)
can be directly computed from the real cyclotomic spectrum
\mathrm{THR}(k)
for every perfect algebra
k
, see
[
29
, Theorem F]
.

The purpose of this article is to relate syntomic cohomology and real topological cyclic homology,
which aims to take advantage of the computational aspects of syntomic cohomology to real and hermitian
K
-theories.
More precisely,
we provide the motivic filtrations on
\mathrm{THR}
and its companions
\mathrm{TCR}^{-}
,
\mathrm{TPR}
, and
\mathrm{TCR}
.
Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
10.1
)
.

Let
A
be a quasisyntomic ring.
Then there exist natural complete filtrations on
\mathrm{THR}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})
,
\mathrm{TCR}^{-}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})
,
\mathrm{TPR}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, and
\mathrm{TCR}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})
whose
n
th graded pieces are

\displaystyle\mathrm{gr}^{n}\mathrm{THR}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\Sigma^{n+n\sigma}\iota\mathcal{N}^{n}\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{A}\{n\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{gr}^{n}\mathrm{TCR}^{-}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\Sigma^{n+n\sigma}\iota\mathcal{N}^{\geq n}\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{A}\{n\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{gr}^{n}\mathrm{TPR}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\Sigma^{n+n\sigma}\iota\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{A}\{n\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{gr}^{n}\mathrm{TCR}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\Sigma^{n+n\sigma}\iota\mathbb{Z}_{p}(n)(A).

Here,

\iota\colon\mathrm{Sp}\to\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathbb{Z}/2}
is the functor of
\infty
-categories that is left adjoint to the fixed point functor
(-)^{\mathbb{Z}/2}\colon\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathbb{Z}/2}\to\mathrm{Sp}
from the
\infty
-category of
\mathbb{Z}/2
-spectra to the
\infty
-category of spectra.
The notation
\Sigma^{m+n\sigma}
means the equivariant suspension,
where
\Sigma^{1}
corresponds to the circle
S^{1}
with the trivial involution, and
\Sigma^{\sigma}
corresponds to the circle
S^{\sigma}
with the involution given by
(x,y)\in S^{1}\mapsto(x,-y)
.
We refer to
[
21
, §A]
for a review of equivariant homotopy theory.
We also refer to
[
5
, Theorem 1.12]
for the Nygaard completed prismatic cohomology
\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{A}\{n\}
, the Nygaard filtration
\mathcal{N}^{\geq n}
, and the Breuil-Kisin twist
\{n\}
.

By
[
5
, Theorem 7.1(1)]
,

\mathrm{THH}(S;\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is even for every quasiregular semiperfectoid ring
S
, i.e.,
\pi_{*}\mathrm{THH}(S;\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is concentrated in even degrees.
This is the key property of
\mathrm{THH}
that implies many results in
[
5
, §7]
.
For
\mathrm{THR}
,
we need the slice filtration

\cdots\to P_{n}\to P_{n-1}\to\cdots

in
\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathbb{Z}/2}
,
which is an equivariant generalization of the Postnikov filtration in
\mathrm{Sp}
.
In this article,
we use the slice filtration introduced by Ullman
[
37
]
,
which is a slight adjustment of the original slice filtration of Hill-Hopkins-Ravenel
[
18
]
.
The adjusted version is further considered in
[
19
]
.
The
n
th slice is
P_{n}^{n}:=\mathrm{cofib}(P_{n+1}\to P_{n})
.
The key property of
\mathrm{THR}
is that
\mathrm{THR}(S;\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is
very even
for quasiregular semiperfectoid ring
S
in the following sense,
which we need for the proof of Theorem
10.1
.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
8.2
)
.

Let
S
be a quasiregular semiperfectoid ring.
Then
\mathrm{THR}(S;\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is very even.
In other words,
we have natural equivalences of
\mathbb{Z}/2
-spectra

\displaystyle P_{2n}^{2n}\mathrm{THR}(S;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\Sigma^{n+n\sigma}\mathrm{H}\underline{\pi_{2n}\mathrm{THH}(S;\mathbb{Z}_{p})},

\displaystyle P_{2n+1}^{2n+1}\mathrm{THR}(S;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq 0

for every integer
n
.

To show this,
we need to show that the natural filtration on the real Hochschild homology in the author’s joint work with Hornbostel
[
20
, Theorem 4.30]
is indeed complete with a certain assumption,
see Theorem
6.20
.
Another crucial ingredient is the computation of
\mathrm{THR}
of perfectoid rings in
[
20
, Theorem 5.16]
.

We also need the following for the proof Theorem
10.1
.

Theorem 1.3

(Part of Theorem
7.5
)
.

The presheaves

\mathrm{THR}(-;\mathbb{Z}_{p}),\;\mathrm{TCR}^{-}(-;\mathbb{Z}_{p}),\;\mathrm{TPR}(-;\mathbb{Z}_{p})

on the opposite category of quasisyntomic rings are quasisyntomic sheaves.

Combined with the previous results on the computations of syntomic cohomology and the claimed real refinement of the Dundas-Goodwillie-McCarthy theorem,
Theorem
1.1
would lead to the computation of equivariant slices of real
K
-theory for several classes of commutative rings.
We discuss about this for the cases
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/x^{e}
and
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}
in Examples
10.3
and
10.4
.
After taking fixed points,
we would also get a natural complete filtration on hermitian
K
-theory.

In this section,

Proposition 8.1

Proof.

By Theorem

,

9.

So far,

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
M

Then
\mathrm{H}\underline{M}_{hS^{\sigma}}
,
\mathrm{H}\underline{M}^{hS^{\sigma}}
, and
\mathrm{H}\underline{M}^{tS^{\sigma}}

Proof.

Recall that every

6.11

Consider the commutative ring

The claim for

implies the claim for

\mathrm{H}\underline{R}\simeq\mathrm{H}\underline{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus\mathrm{H}\underline{M}

Hence we reduce to the case when

is a commutative ring.

As in the proofs of
[

there are equivalences of

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}\underline{M}^{hS^{\sigma}}\simeq\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}\Sigma^{-i-i\sigma}\mathrm{H}\underline{M}.

Hence
\mathrm{H}\underline{M}_{hS^{\sigma}}
and

are very even.

As observed in the proof of
[

for the case of

the norm map

is a map of
\mathrm{H}\underline{M}
-modules.
This can be shown by adapting
[
30
, Theorem 5.47]
to the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{H}\underline{M}}(\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathbb{Z}/2})
of
\mathrm{H}\underline{M}
-modules instead of
\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathbb{Z}/2}
.
We claim that the norm map is zero,
which implies

and hence
\mathrm{H}\underline{M}^{tS^{\sigma}}
is very even.

it suffices to show that any map of
\mathrm{H}\underline{M}
-modules

\Sigma^{i+(i+1)\sigma}\mathrm{H}\underline{M}\to\Sigma^{-j-j\sigma}\mathrm{H}\underline{M}

is zero for all integers

and

.
Using the adjunction pair

\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathbb{Z}/2}\rightleftarrows\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{H}\underline{M}}(\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathbb{Z}/2}),

it suffices to show

\underline{\pi}_{0}(\Sigma^{n+(n-1)\sigma}\mathrm{H}\underline{M})=0

for every integer

We set

If

(resp.

then

(resp.

),
and hence we have
\underline{\pi}_{0}(X)=0
.
If

then use Lemma
5.7
.
∎

be a quasiregular semiperfectoid ring.

and

Its graded pieces are very even by Theorem

and Proposition

5.10

\mathrm{TCR}^{-}(S;\mathbb{Z}_{p})

We claim that

Since
\mathrm{THR}(S;\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is very even by Theorem

we have a fiber sequence

Since its

Using the cofiber sequence

\displaystyle\mathrm{gr}^{n}\mathrm{TPR}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\Sigma^{n+n\sigma}\iota\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{A}\{n\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{gr}^{n}\mathrm{TCR}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\Sigma^{n+n\sigma}\iota\mathbb{Z}_{p}(n)(A).

Proof.

By quasisyntomic descent

, Proposition 4.31]
and Theorem

,
we reduce to the case when

Then the claim is a consequence of
[

, Theorem 1.12]
,
Theorems
8.2
and
9.2
,
and the formulation of
\mathrm{TCR}(A;\mathbb{Z}_{p})
in Definition

.
∎

The Forward Maximal Regularity Operator Commutes with the Backward Thanks: Research of the author is supported by the National NSF grant of China (no. 11801274).
Yi C. Huang

Abstract
. Given a generator of a bounded analytic semigroup on a Hilbert space, we show that the corresponding forward maximal regularity operator commutes with the backward. In particular, for self-adjoint generators the images under the two maximal regularity operators have equal unweighted Hilbert space norms.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 35K90; Secondary 42A38, 47A60.

1.
Introduction

We are concerned with the autonomous evolution equations on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}=(0,\infty)
:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+Au=f,\\
u(0)=0\end{cases}

(1.1)

and

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v-Av=f,\\
v(\infty)=0.\end{cases}

(1.2)

Here, the source
f
lies in the unweighted space
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};{\mathbf{H}})
, where
{\mathbf{H}}
is an underlying Hilbert space,
and
-A
is a densely defined, closed linear operator on
{\mathbf{H}}
,
with domain
D(A)
and generating a bounded analytic semigroup
\{e^{-zA}:|\arg z|<\delta\}
,
0<\delta<\pi/2
.

The so-called maximal regularity for (
1.1
) in the Hilbert space
{\mathbf{H}}

(also referred to as de Simon’s theorem
[
dS64
]
), and similarly for (
1.2
), can be stated as follows:

there is some constant
C=C(A,{\mathbf{H}})>0
such that for all
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};{\mathbf{H}})
,

|||Au|||\leq C|||f|||\quad\text{ and }\quad|||Av|||\leq C|||f|||,

(1.3)

where
|||\cdot|||=\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};{\mathbf{H}})}
.

Thus via (
1.1
) (respectively, (
1.2
)) and (
1.3
),
the solution
u
(respectively,
v
) lies in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};D(A))\cap\dot{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};{\mathbf{H}})
.
The “maximal regularity” conveyed in (
1.3
) means that
\partial_{t}u
and
Au

(respectively,
\partial_{t}v
and
Av
) enjoy the same regularity as the source
f
.

Recall that the forward maximal regularity operator
{\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{+}
is defined by

{\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{+}(f)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}Ae^{-(t-s)A}f(s)ds,

(1.4)

while the backward maximal regularity operator
{\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{-}
is given by

{\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{-}(f)(t)=\int_{t}^{\infty}Ae^{-(s-t)A}f(s)ds.

(1.5)

The operators
{\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{\pm}
are associated to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) as for appropriate
f
, we have

Au={\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{+}(f)\quad\text{ and }\quad Av=-{\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{-}(f).

Therefore, maximal regularity problems translate into boundedness of
{\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{\pm}
,
which are typical examples of singular integral operators with operator-valued kernels.

There are so far many machineries proving de Simon’s theorem, typically via Fourier transform,
and various extensions to Banach spaces, say, in
L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};L^{p}(\Omega))
, where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For more comprehensive materials about the maximal regularity of evolution equations and its applications,
see for example
[
DHP03
,
Are04
,
KW04
,
Mon09
]
by Denk-Hieber-Prüss, Arendt, Kunstmann-Weis, and Monniaux.

2.
Commutativity of maximal regularity operators

In their innovative semigroup approach to generalized Cauchy-Riemann systems and elliptic boundary value problems,
Auscher and Axelsson
[
AA11b
]
established the endpoint weighted estimates (in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},t^{\mp 1}dt;{\mathbf{H}})
) for
{\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{\pm}
in presence of quadratic estimates
(see also Hytönen-Rosén
[
HR13
]
for related Carleson duality results, and Rosén
[
Ros14
]
for the intermediate case with temporal weights
t^{\alpha}dt
with
|\alpha|<1
).

In a subsequent proceeding note,
Auscher and Axelsson
[
AA11a
]
singled out the abstract formulations of their maximal regularity results obtained in
[
AA11b
]
.
However, the two maximal regularity operators
{\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{\pm}
or rather, the two evolution problems (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), were treated separately,
which is not the case in
[
AA11b
,
Ros14
]
.

The aim of this paper is to consider the “interaction” of the two maximal regularity operators
{\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{\pm}
.
Our finding is rather elementary and can be summarized as below.

Theorem 2.1
.

The maximal regularity operators in (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) commute, namely,

[{\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{+},{\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{-}]=0.

(2.1)

In particular, if
A=A^{*}
, we have

|||{\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{+}(f)|||=|||{\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{-}(f)|||

(2.2)

for all
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};{\mathbf{H}})
.

As a general duality principle, the study of
{\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{-}
is reduced to
{\mathcal{M}}^{A^{*}}_{+}
, see for example
[
AA11a
]
.
Here we work directly with
{\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{-}
via the traditional Fourier transform approach,
just repeating the arguments (for example
[
Mon09
, Theorem 2.6]
) in proving de Simon’s theorem for
{\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{+}
.
This is indeed the only new ingredient of this paper.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
denote the Fourier transform on
{\mathbb{R}}
.
For
\sigma\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{+}(f))(\sigma)=A(i\sigma+A)^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}(f)(\sigma),

and similarly,

{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{-}(f))(\sigma)=A(-i\sigma+A)^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}(f)(\sigma).

The proof of the second identity can be adapted from the arguments for the first (see e.g.
[
Mon09
, Theorem 2.6]
).
For completeness we give the details.
Let
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};{\mathbf{H}})
and extend
f
by 0 on
(-\infty,0)
.
Let
k(t)=Ae^{-tA}
if
t>0
and
k(t)=0
if
t\leq 0
.
Thus

{\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{-}(f)(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}k(s-t)f(s)ds,\quad t\geq 0.

Moreover, for
\sigma\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{-}(f))(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-it\sigma}k(s-t)f(s)dsdt

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-i(s-t)\sigma}k(t)f(s)dsdt

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{it\sigma}k(t)dt\right){\mathcal{F}}(f)(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{it\sigma}k(t)dt\right){\mathcal{F}}(f)(\sigma)

\displaystyle=A(-i\sigma+A)^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}(f)(\sigma).

Now, for all
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};{\mathbf{H}})
, we compute as follows

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\left([{\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{+},{\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{-}]f\right)(\sigma)

(2.3)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{+}({\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{-}(f)))(\sigma)-{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{-}({\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{+}(f)))(\sigma)

\displaystyle=A(i\sigma+A)^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{-}(f))(\sigma)-A(-i\sigma+A)^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{+}(f))(\sigma)

\displaystyle=A(i\sigma+A)^{-1}A(-i\sigma+A)^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}(f)(\sigma)

\displaystyle-A(-i\sigma+A)^{-1}A(i\sigma+A)^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}(f)(\sigma)

\displaystyle=A^{2}(\sigma^{2}+A^{2})^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}(f)(\sigma)-A^{2}(\sigma^{2}+A^{2})^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}(f)(\sigma)=0.

Hence, by the isometry via Fourier transform, we have: for all
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};{\mathbf{H}})
,

[{\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{+},{\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{-}]f=0\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};{\mathbf{H}}).

We thus proved the (strong) operator identity (
2.1
).
In view of the adjoint relations

({\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{\pm})^{*}={\mathcal{M}}^{A^{*}}_{\mp},

the norm equality (
2.2
) follows from
A=A^{*}
and (
2.1
).
The theorem is proved.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

For the moment we do not know how to apply Theorem
2.1
or solely (
2.2
) to the maximal regularity problem on the line or on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}

as considered for example in Arendt-Duelli
[
AD06
]
, Arendt-Zamboni
[
AZ10
]
, and Auscher-Axelsson
[
AA11b
]
.
In particular, we expect our elementary finding in Theorem
2.1
can be connected to the Cauchy non-integral formulae systematically studied in Rosén
[
Ros14
]
.

3.
Commutativity of holomorphic functional calculus

Conceptually one may think of the commutativity formula (
2.1
) as an algebra-homomorphism property of Albrecht’s operational calculus (recalled in
[
AA11b
]
).
Using Fourier transform, we justified this intuition at the level of functional calculus.

Let us take a further look at the calculus for the Fourier multipliers:

m_{\pm}(\sigma,\xi)=\xi(\pm i\sigma+\xi)^{-1}\quad\longmapsto\quad m_{\pm}(\sigma,A)=A(\pm i\sigma+A)^{-1}.

So what we did in above proof of Theorem
2.1
, and in particular in the computations (
2.3
),
is actually to expose the rather elementary commutativity

[m_{+}(\sigma,A),m_{-}(\sigma,A)]=0.

(3.1)

Note that the two functions
m_{\pm}(\sigma,\xi)
, parametrized by
\sigma\in{\mathbb{R}}
and holomorphic on
{\mathbb{C}}\backslash i{\mathbb{R}}
, have no decay when
|\xi|\rightarrow\infty
.
Therefore, the commutativity (
3.1
) does
not
belong to the algebra-homomorphism property of holomorphic calculus
(see e.g.
[
AA11b
, Proposition 6.2]
in the operational calculus context).
However, under McIntosh’s bounded holomorphic functional calculus for
A
,
we do have such a property which roughly states that for two bounded holomorphic functions
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
,

b_{1}(A)b_{2}(A)=(b_{1}b_{2})(A),

see Albrecht-Duong-McIntosh
[
ADM96
]
and Haase
[
Haa06
]
.
Since
b_{1}b_{2}=b_{2}b_{1}
, we have
[b_{1}(A),b_{2}(A)]=0
as bounded operators acting on
{\mathbf{H}}
. Thus we also have

[{\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{+}b_{1}(A),{\mathcal{M}}^{A}_{-}b_{2}(A)]=0

(3.2)

as bounded operators acting on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};{\mathbf{H}})
.
(
3.2
) does include (
2.1
) as a special case.

In the semigroup approach to Cauchy-Riemann systems and elliptic boundary value problems,
the candidates
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
can be the spectral projections of (bisectorial) multiplicatively perturbed Dirac operators.
Note that the non-trivial case happens when
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
are the same projection.
See
[
AA11b
,
Ros14
]
for details.

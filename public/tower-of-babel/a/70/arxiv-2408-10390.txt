Self-Refined Generative Foundation Models for Wireless Traffic Prediction
Chengming Hu, Hao Zhou, Di Wu, Xi Chen, Jun Yan, Xue Liu

Abstract
With a broad range of emerging applications in 6G networks, wireless traffic prediction has become a critical component of network management. However, the dynamically shifting distribution of wireless traffic in non-stationary 6G networks presents significant challenges to achieving accurate and stable predictions. Motivated by recent advancements in Generative AI (GAI)-enabled 6G networks, this paper proposes a novel self-refined Large Language Model (LLM) for wireless traffic prediction, namely TrafficLLM, through in-context learning without parameter fine-tuning or model training. The proposed TrafficLLM harnesses the powerful few-shot learning abilities of LLMs to enhance the scalability of traffic prediction in dynamically changing wireless environments. Specifically, our proposed TrafficLLM embraces an LLM to iteratively refine its predictions through a three-step process: traffic prediction, feedback generation, and prediction refinement. Initially, the proposed TrafficLLM conducts traffic predictions using task-specific demonstration prompts. Recognizing that LLMs may generate incorrect predictions on the first attempt, we subsequently incorporate feedback demonstration prompts designed to provide multifaceted and valuable feedback related to these initial predictions. Following this comprehensive feedback, our proposed TrafficLLM introduces refinement demonstration prompts, enabling the same LLM to further refine its predictions and thereby enhance prediction performance. The evaluations on two realistic datasets demonstrate that the proposed TrafficLLM outperforms state-of-the-art methods with performance improvements of 23.17% and 17.09%, respectively.



I
Introduction

The envisioned 6G networks are expected to support a wide range of emerging applications, including Vehicle-to-Vehicle (V2V) and Vehicle-to-Infrastructure (V2I) communications, integrated satellite-aerial-terrestrial networks, and integrated sensing and communication, among others
[
1
]
.
The evolving network architectures and highly integrated network functions significantly increase the complexity of network management.
Specifically, wireless traffic prediction is a crucial aspect of network management, encompassing tasks such as load balancing, energy saving, and more.

Traditional traffic prediction studies commonly apply statistical and time-series analysis approaches, such as Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA)
[
2
]
.
Deep learning methods have also demonstrated success in traffic prediction, including Long Short-Term Memory units (LSTM)
[
3
]
, and Convolutional Convolution Network
[
4
]
, among others, which exhibit considerable advancements over traditional approaches.
However, in non-stationary 6G networks, the distributions of wireless traffic can dynamically shift over time and across various base stations, Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs), High Altitude Platforms (HAPs), and diverse end-user devices, resulting in complex and non-linear spatial-temporal characteristics.
These factors pose significant challenges for existing methods in effectively analyzing the complex nature of wireless traffic and accurately estimating predictions.

Generative AI (GAI) has recently attracted considerable attention due to its ability to analyze complex data distributions and generate analogous content.
The promising features of GAI have spurred the exploration of GAI-enabled 6G networks through the deployment of powerful generative foundation models, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs).
Given LLMs’ impressive few-shot learning capabilities, fine-tuning LLMs on extensive time-series datasets can enhance their prediction scalability in predicting previously unseen time-series data
[
5
]
.
Additionally, Fu et al.
[
6
]
utilize an LLM to design a hybrid network Intrusion Detection System in the Internet of Vehicles (IoV), encompassing semantic extractor, input embedding, LLM pre-training and fine-tuning.
Liu et al.
[
7
]
enhance energy-efficient and reliable Reconfigurable Intelligent Surface-based IoV communication systems by fine-tuning an LLM on critical IoV data.
However, the fine-tuning of LLMs is computationally expensive and memory-intensive, posing significant challenges for deploying these LLMs in resource-limited wireless networks
[
8
]
.
Furthermore, large-scale traffic datasets are often scarce in wireless systems. The high measurement cost of packet aggregation can compromise packet forwarding performance, making it difficult for certain sensors to accurately measure flow-level traffic at each timestamp. The unavoidable transmission loss caused by unreliable transmission protocols can also lead to incomplete traffic measurements.

To this end, this paper proposes a novel
TrafficLLM
, a self-refined LLM designed for wireless traffic prediction, which leverages in-context learning without parameter fine-tuning or model training.
Specifically, our proposed TrafficLLM employs an LLM to iteratively enhance its predictions through a three-step process: traffic prediction, feedback generation, and prediction refinement.
Considering the advantages of in-context learning, our proposed TrafficLLM initially incorporates task-specific prediction demonstration prompts, which guide an LLM (e.g., GPT-4
[
9
]
) in traffic prediction by adhering to these demonstration prompts.
Recognizing that LLMs may generate incorrect predictions, we further design feedback demonstration prompts to enable a thorough evaluation of these predictions.
These prompts encompass aspects such as prediction performance, completeness, method, and actionable steps, providing multifaceted and valuable feedback associated with predictions.
Following this comprehensive feedback, our proposed TrafficLLM introduces refinement demonstration prompts that enable the same LLM to further refine its predictions and enhance prediction performance.

The main contributions of this paper include:

•

We propose a novel TrafficLLM that employs an LLM to iteratively improve its predictions through task-specific feedback and refinement demonstration prompts without parameter fine-tuning or model training.

•

To the best of our knowledge, our proposed TrafficLLM is the first work to introduce an LLM specifically designed for wireless traffic prediction.

•

Comprehensive experiments on two realistic datasets showcase the effectiveness and scalability of our proposed TrafficLLM over state-of-the-art methods.

Each traffic sample is characterized by a tuple consisting of the traffic load volume

x[t]=[v[t],\dots,v[t+w-1];t,\dots,t+w-1],

Given the historical traffic samples

II-C

Feedback generation

Recognizing that an LLM may not be able to generate a precise prediction on its first attempt, our proposed TrafficLLM further incorporates feedback demonstration prompts designed to provide multifaceted and valuable feedback related to these initial predictions.
As shown in Fig.
2
, our feedback demonstration prompt
p_{\text{feed}}
is designed to encompass prediction performance, prediction format and completeness, prediction method, and actionable steps as follows:

•

Overall performance

p_{\text{feed}}^{\text{overall}}
: Given ground truth and predicted traffic, the same LLM is first utilized to evaluate the overall prediction performance, e.g., Mean Absolute Error across all hours. The overall performance should be iteratively improved until convergence is achieved.

•

Periodical performance

p_{\text{feed}}^{\text{period}}
: Given that time-series data can be projected through sine and cosine functions in the real domain
[
13
]
, we utilize the same LLM to represent ground truth and predicted traffic onto these functions, respectively.
By aligning the projected function of the predicted traffic with that of the ground truth as closely as possible, predictions are expected to accurately capture the periodicity of the ground truth, e.g., variations during peak traffic hours.

•

Format and completeness

p_{\text{feed}}^{\text{complete}}
: The LLM should not only predict traffic in a format that aligns with the ground truth but also generate complete predictions for each timestamp.

•

Prediction method

p_{\text{feed}}^{\text{method}}
: The prediction method is summarized as one perspective of the feedback prompt. To further enhance performance, one actionable step is to encourage the LLM to apply more accurate prediction methods. It is important to note that while the LLM may recommend training a neural network (e.g., LSTM) as an actionable step, this suggestion is disregarded to avoid the additional computational cost associated with parameter fine-tuning or model training.

Thus, feedback demonstration prompts specifically associated with predicted traffic are formulated as follows:

p_{\text{feed}}=p_{\text{feed}}^{\text{overall}}\oplus p_{\text{feed}}^{\text{period}}\oplus p_{\text{feed}}^{\text{complete}}\oplus p_{\text{feed}}^{\text{method}}.

Experimental Results

III-C
1
Evaluation on the V2I radio channel measurement dataset (V2I dataset)

We first compare the prediction performance of our proposed TrafficLLM against the baselines on the V2I dataset
[
14
]
.
Note that 60% of the dataset is incorporated with demonstration prompts while the remaining 40% is utilized for traffic prediction evaluation.
For the baselines ARIMA and LSTM, the same 60% of the dataset is chosen to train the models and the remaining data for testing.

Fig.
3
compares the average MAE and MSE between our proposed TrafficLLM and the baselines.
Although LSTM surpasses our proposed TrafficLLM in prediction performance, it incurs significant computational costs and demands substantial memory (e.g.,
395\text{s}
and
11.60\text{MB}
required for training a five-layer LSTM). These extensive model training requirements render it impractical for deployment in resource-limited wireless systems.
In comparison to LLM-based methods (i.e., Base GPT-3.5 and Base GPT-4) and ARIMA, our proposed TrafficLLM achieve reductions of up to 39.27% and 67.58% in prediction MAE and MSE, respectively.
Among Base GPT-3.5 and Base GPT-4, one reason for their inferior performance is the absence of our proposed iterative feedback and refinement processes.
Base GPT-3.5 assumes that future traffic patterns can closely mirror historical traffic, resulting in predictions that merely replicate historical traffic.
Furthermore, Base GPT-4 predicts future traffic to more accurately reflect daily traffic patterns, resulting in enhanced performance compared to Base GPT-3.5.
Our proposed TrafficLLM iteratively improves traffic predictions by incorporating feedback and refinement demonstration prompts.
This process enables an LLM to utilize accurate prediction methods, such as seasonal ARIMA and ensemble statistical methods, leading to superior performance compared to LLM-based methods and ARIMA.

IV
Conclusion

Motivated by recent advancements of GAI-enabled 6G networks, this paper proposes a novel TrafficLLM designed for wireless traffic prediction through in-context learning without model training or parameter fine-tuning.
Following our task-specific feedback and refinement demonstration prompts, the proposed TrafficLLM iteratively improves traffic prediction across dynamically changing wireless traffic data.
Evaluations on two realistic datasets demonstrate the effectiveness and scalability of our proposed TrafficLLM over state-of-the-art methods, with enhanced performance of 23.17% and 17.09%.

Establishing the first hidden-charm pentaquark with strangeness
Hua-Xing Chen, Wei Chen, Xiang Liu, Xiao-Hai Liu

Abstract
We study the P c ​ s ​ ( 4459 ) 0 P_{cs}(4459)^{0} recently observed by LHCb using the method of QCD sum rules. Our results support its interpretation as the D ¯ ∗ ​ Ξ c \bar{D}^{*}\Xi_{c} hadronic molecular state of either J P = 1 / 2 − J^{P}=1/2^{-} or 3 / 2 − 3/2^{-} . Within the hadronic molecular picture, the three LHCb experiments observing P c P_{c} and P c ​ s P_{cs} states lhcb ; Aaij:2015tga ; Aaij:2019vzc can be well understood as a whole. This strongly supports the existence of hadronic molecules, whose studies can significantly improve our understanding on the construction of the subatomic world. To verify this picture, we propose to further investigate the P c ​ s ​ ( 4459 ) 0 P_{cs}(4459)^{0} to examine whether it can be separated into two states, and to search for the D ¯ ​ Ξ c \bar{D}\Xi_{c} molecular state of J P = 1 / 2 − J^{P}=1/2^{-} .

Introduction.
—–
Atomic nuclei are made of protons and neutrons, which are themselves composed of quarks and gluons. In the past century a huge number of subatomic particles, called hadrons, were discovered in particle experiments, whose properties are similar to the proton and neutron
pdg
. One naturally raises an interesting question: are there subatomic particles corresponding to the nucleus? Nowadays we call them “hadronic molecules”, whose studies can significantly improve our understanding on the construction of the subatomic world, as illustrated in Fig.
1
.

Figure 1:
A possible way to construct the subatomic world.

It is not so easy to answer the above question. Most of the experimentally observed hadrons can be described as
q\bar{q}
mesons or
qqq
baryons in the conventional quark model, while there can also exist (compact)
q\bar{q}q\bar{q}
tetraquarks and
qqqq\bar{q}
pentaquarks
GellMann:1964nj
;
Zweig:1981pd
, etc. With the experimental progress on this issue over the past decade, dozens of
XYZ

pdg
. However, these states may be explained as hadronic molecules, while they may also be explained as compact tetraquarks that are still hadrons.

Fortunately, in the LHCb experiments performed in 2015 and 2019
Aaij:2015tga
;
Aaij:2019vzc

P_{c}(4312)
,
P_{c}(4380)

P_{c}(4440)

P_{c}(4457)

\bar{c}cuud

P_{c}(4440)

P_{c}(4457)

\bar{D}\Sigma_{c}
and
\bar{D}^{*}\Sigma_{c}

\bar{D}\Sigma_{c}

Hidden-charm pentaquark currents.
—–
We use the
\bar{c}
,
c
,
s
,
u
, and
d
quarks to construct hidden-charm pentaquark interpolating currents with strangeness. To study
\bar{D}^{(*)}\Xi_{c}
molecular states, we consider the following type of currents:

\eta(x)=[\bar{c}_{a}(x)\Gamma_{1}u_{b}(x)]~\Big[[d^{T}_{c}(x)\mathbb{C}\Gamma_{2}s_{d}(x)]~\Gamma_{3}c_{e}(x)\Big]\,,

(2)

where
a\cdots e
are color indices,
\Gamma_{1/2/3}
are Dirac matrices, and
\mathbb{C}=i\gamma_{2}\gamma_{0}
is the charge-conjugation operator. The other type of currents:

\eta^{\prime}(x)=[\bar{c}_{a}(x)\Gamma_{1}d_{b}(x)]~\Big[[u^{T}_{c}(x)\mathbb{C}\Gamma_{2}s_{d}(x)]~\Gamma_{3}c_{e}(x)\Big]\,,

(3)

can be similarly studied, but they just lead to the same QCD sum rule results as the
\eta(x)
currents. Hence, the present study can not distinguish the isospin of
P_{cs}
states.

The
\eta(x)
currents can be constructed by combining charmed meson operators and charmed baryon fields. We need the charmed meson operators
J_{\mathcal{D}}
, which couple to the ground-state charmed mesons
\mathcal{D}=\bar{D}^{0}/\bar{D}^{*0}
:

\displaystyle J_{\bar{D}^{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{d}\gamma_{5}u_{d}\,,

(4)

\displaystyle J_{\bar{D}^{*0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{d}\gamma_{\mu}u_{d}\,.

We also need the charmed baryon field
J_{\mathcal{B}}
, which couples to the ground-state charmed baryon
\mathcal{B}=\Xi_{c}^{0}
:

\displaystyle J_{\Xi_{c}^{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon^{abc}[d_{a}^{T}\mathbb{C}\gamma_{5}s_{b}]c_{c}\,.

(5)

There can be altogether three
\bar{D}^{(*)}\Xi_{c}
hadronic molecular states, that are
\bar{D}\Xi_{c}
of
J^{P}={1/2}^{-}
,
\bar{D}^{*}\Xi_{c}
of
J^{P}={1/2}^{-}
, and
\bar{D}^{*}\Xi_{c}

\displaystyle\eta_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\delta^{ab}\bar{c}_{a}\gamma_{5}u_{b}]~[\epsilon^{cde}d_{c}^{T}\mathbb{C}\gamma_{5}s_{d}c_{e}]

(6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{D}^{0}~\Xi_{c}^{0}\,,

\displaystyle\eta_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\delta^{ab}\bar{c}_{a}\gamma_{\nu}u_{b}]~\gamma^{\nu}\gamma_{5}~[\epsilon^{cde}d_{c}^{T}\mathbb{C}\gamma_{5}s_{d}c_{e}]

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{D}^{*0}_{\nu}~\gamma^{\nu}\gamma_{5}~\Xi_{c}^{0}\,,

\displaystyle\eta_{3}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{3/2}^{\alpha\nu}~[\delta^{ab}\bar{c}_{a}\gamma_{\nu}u_{b}]~[\epsilon^{cde}d_{c}^{T}\mathbb{C}\gamma_{5}s_{d}c_{e}]

QCD sum rule studies.
—–
We use the method of QCD sum rules
Shifman:1978bx
;
Reinders:1984sr
to study
\bar{D}^{(*)}\Xi_{c}
molecular states through the currents
\eta_{1,2}
of
J^{P}=1/2^{-}
and
\eta^{\alpha}_{3}
of
J^{P}=3/2^{-}
. Taking the current
\eta_{2}
as an example, we assume it couples to the
\bar{D}^{*}\Xi_{c}
molecular state of
J^{P}=1/2^{-}
through

\displaystyle\langle 0|\eta_{2}|X;1/2^{-}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{X}u(p)\,,

where
u(p)
is the Dirac spinor of this state, denoted as
X
for simplicity. The two-point correlation function extracted from
\eta_{2}
can be written as:

\displaystyle\Pi\left(q^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\int d^{4}xe^{iq\cdot x}\langle 0|T\left[\eta_{2}(x)\bar{\eta}_{2}(0)\right]|0\rangle

(10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q\!\!\!/\penalty~+M_{X})~\Pi_{0}\left(q^{2}\right)\,.

In QCD sum rule studies we calculate the two-point correlation function
\Pi_{0}\left(q^{2}\right)
at both hadron and quark-gluon levels. At the hadron level, we use the dispersion relation to write it as

\Pi_{0}(q^{2})={\frac{1}{\pi}}\int^{\infty}_{s_{<}}\frac{{\rm Im}\Pi_{0}(s)}{s-q^{2}-i\varepsilon}ds\,,

(11)

where
s_{<}
is the physical threshold. We further define the imaginary part of the correlation function as the spectral density
\rho(s)
, which is usually evaluated at the hadron level by inserting intermediate hadron states
\sum_{n}|n\rangle\langle n|
:

\displaystyle\rho_{\rm phen}(s)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\Pi_{0}(s)/\pi

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\delta(s-M^{2}_{n})\langle 0|\eta|n\rangle\langle n|{\eta^{\dagger}}|0\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{X}^{2}\delta(s-m_{X}^{2})+\mbox{continuum}.

In the last step we have adopted the usual parametrization of one-pole dominance for the ground state
X
and a continuum contribution.

At the quark-gluon level we calculate
\Pi_{0}\left(q^{2}\right)
using the method of operator product expansion (OPE), and derive its corresponding spectral density
\rho_{\rm OPE}(s)
. After performing the Borel transformation to Eq. (
11
) at both hadron and quark-gluon levels, we can approximate the continuum using the spectral density above a threshold value
s_{0}
(quark-hadron duality), and obtain the sum rule equation

\Pi_{0}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})\equiv f^{2}_{X}e^{-M_{X}^{2}/M_{B}^{2}}=\int^{s_{0}}_{s_{<}}e^{-s/M_{B}^{2}}\rho_{\rm OPE}(s)ds\,.

(13)

It can be used to further calculate
M_{X}
through

\displaystyle M^{2}_{X}(s_{0},M_{B})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\int^{s_{0}}_{s_{<}}e^{-s/M_{B}^{2}}s\rho_{\rm OPE}(s)ds}{\int^{s_{0}}_{s_{<}}e^{-s/M_{B}^{2}}\rho_{\rm OPE}(s)ds}\,.

(14)

In the present study we calculate OPEs at the leading order of
\alpha_{s}
and up to the
D({\rm imension})=10
terms, including the perturbative term, the charm quark mass, the quark condensates
\langle\bar{q}q\rangle/\langle\bar{s}s\rangle
, the gluon condensate
\langle g_{s}^{2}GG\rangle
, the quark-gluon mixed condensates
\langle g_{s}\bar{q}\sigma Gq\rangle/\langle g_{s}\bar{s}\sigma Gs\rangle
, and their combinations. We summarized the obtained spectral densities
\rho_{1\cdots 3}(s)
in Appendix
A
, which are extracted from the currents
\eta_{1\cdots 3}
, respectively. In the calculations we ignore the chirally suppressed terms with the up and down quark masses, and adopt the factorization assumption of vacuum saturation for higher dimensional condensates. We find that the
D=3
quark condensates
\langle\bar{q}q\rangle/\langle\bar{s}s\rangle
and the
D=5
mixed condensates
\langle g_{s}\bar{q}\sigma Gq\rangle/\langle g_{s}\bar{s}\sigma Gs\rangle
are multiplied by the charm quark mass, which are thus important power corrections.

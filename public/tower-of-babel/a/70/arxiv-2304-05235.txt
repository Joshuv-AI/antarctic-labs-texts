Deformed solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation associated to dual weak braces
Marzia MAZZOTTA, Bernard RYBOŁOWICZ, Paola STEFANELLI

Abstract
A recent method for acquiring new solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation involves deforming the classical solution associated with a skew brace. In this work, we demonstrate the applicability of this method to a dual weak brace ( S , + , ∘ ) \left(S,+,\circ\right) and prove that all elements generating deformed solutions belong precisely to the set 𝒟 r ( S ) = { z ∈ S ∣ ∀ a , b ∈ S ( a + b ) ∘ z = a ∘ z − z + b ∘ z } \mathcal{D}_{r}(S)=\{z\in S\mid\forall a,b\in S\,\,(a+b)\circ z=a\circ z-z+b\circ z\} , which we term the distributor of S S . We show it is a full inverse subsemigroup of ( S , ∘ ) \left(S,\circ\right) and prove it is an ideal for certain classes of braces. Additionally, we express the distributor of a brace S S in terms of the associativity of the operation ⋅ \cdot , with ∘ \circ representing the circle or adjoint operation. In this context, ( 𝒟 r ​ ( S ) , + , ⋅ ) (\mathcal{D}_{r}(S),+,\cdot) constitutes a Jacobson radical ring contained within S S . Furthermore, we explore parameters leading to non-equivalent solutions, emphasizing that even deformed solutions by idempotents may not be equivalent. Lastly, considering S S as a strong semilattice [ Y , B α , ϕ α , β ] [Y,B_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha,\beta}] of skew braces B α B_{\alpha} , we establish that a deformed solution forms a semilattice of solutions on each skew brace B α B_{\alpha} if and only if the semilattice Y Y is bounded by an element 1 1 and the deforming element z z lies in B 1 B_{1} .

Introduction

The history of the Yang-Baxter equation dates back to the 1960s when the equation first appeared and gained the attention of mathematical physicists. The name originates from two outstanding researchers and their papers: Yang and his paper on many-body problems
[
30
]
, and Baxter and his paper on the eight-vertex lattice model
[
3
]
. Since then, different variants of the equation have been found and studied. For example, the classical Yang-Baxter equation, whose connections with simple Lie algebras were studied by Belavin and Drinfel’d in
[
4
]
. A variant which is of our interest in this work is called the set-theoretic Yang-Baxter equation. Drinfel’d attracted the attention of researchers to this version by including it in his paper
[
13
, p. 7]
and presenting it in the form we use nowadays.
Given a set
S
, a map

r:S\times S\to S\times S
is said to be a
set-theoretic solution of the Yang-Baxter equation
, or shortly

\displaystyle\left(r\times\id_{S}\right)\left(\id_{S}\times r\right)\left(r\times\id_{S}\right)=\left(\id_{S}\times r\right)\left(r\times\id_{S}\right)\left(\id_{S}\times r\right).

Writing
r\left(x,y\right)=\left(\lambda_{x}\left(y\right),\rho_{y}\left(x\right)\right)
, with
\lambda_{x},\rho_{x}
maps from
S
into itself, then
r
is
left (resp. right) non-degenerate
if
\lambda_{x}\in\Sym_{S}
(resp.
\rho_{x}\in\Sym_{S}
), for every
x\in S
,
non-degenerate
if it is both left and right non-degenerate. After the paper of Drinfel’d, many authors focused their attention on this equation discovering new connections between solutions and various algebraic structures. The early literature on the subject is rich, but to help new readers, we direct them to the papers by Etingof et al., e.g.
[

[

[

]
with a focus on bijective non-degenerate solutions.

In
[
27
]
, Rump introduced the algebraic structure of braces to study involutive non-degenerate solutions. It is worth noting that the axiomatics of the theory of two-sided braces were already studied before by Andrunakievič
[

[

.

Any weak brace

The paper

Preliminaries

This section is devoted to introducing the structure of the weak brace and its properties useful for our treatment.

Initially, for the ease of the reader, let us briefly recall some useful notions on inverse semigroups (see
[
10
,
18
,
22
,
26
]
, for more details). A semigroup
S
is called

if, for each

, there exists a unique element

of

such that

and
a^{-1}=a^{-1}aa^{-1}

of

is similar to that in a group, since
(ab)^{-1}=b^{-1}a^{-1}
and
(a^{-1})^{-1}=a
, for all

the set of the idempotents of

, clearly,
e=e^{-1}
, for every
e\in\E(S)
, and the idempotents of
S
are exactly the elements
aa^{-1}
and
a^{-1}a
, for any
a\in S
. An inverse semigroup

such that
aa^{-1}=a^{-1}a
,
for every
a\in S

Clifford semigroup
.
Equivalently, a Clifford semigroup
S
is an inverse semigroup in which the idempotents are central or, according to
[
18
, Theorem 4.2.1]
, it is a strong (lower) semilattice
Y
of disjoint groups.

[

Clearly,

[

are weak braces since the additive and the multiplicative structures are groups with the same identities. Besides,

are skew braces in which the additive group is abelian. Moreover, in any weak brace,

We recall that two solutions

In the direction of

Example 3
.

Let
X=\{e,x,y\}
and
\left(S,\circ\right)
the
commutative inverse monoid on

with identity

satisfying the relations
x\circ x=y\circ y=x

and
x\circ y=y
. Note that
a^{-}=a
, for every

.
Consider the trivial weak brace on

, namely

, for all

. Then, by

and

Observe that if

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
\left(B,+,\circ\right)
be a two-sided skew brace and

and

Due to

The converse of

In this section, we focus on the distributor of any dual weak brace

According to

The following are examples of skew braces in which

Example 5

Let

Let

More generally, one can prove the following results related to the distributor of any dual weak brace. Let us first recall that an inverse subsemigroup

Proposition 3.1

Let

containing the center
\zeta(S,\circ)
of

Proof.

Initially, it holds
E\left(S\right)\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{r}(S)

and

\displaystyle(a+b)\circ(z_{1}\circ z_{2})

\displaystyle=\left(a\circ z_{1}+\left(z_{1}^{-}+b\right)\circ z_{1}\right)\circ z_{2}

\displaystyle=a\circ z_{1}\circ z_{2}+\left(z_{2}^{-}+\left(z_{1}^{-}+b\right)\circ z_{1}\right)\circ z_{2}

\displaystyle=a\circ z_{1}\circ z_{2}+\left(z_{2}^{-}\circ z_{1}^{-}\circ z_{1}+\left(z_{1}^{-}+b\right)\circ z_{1}\right)\circ z_{2}

by (

\displaystyle=a\circ z_{1}\circ z_{2}+\left(\left(z_{1}\circ z_{2}\right)^{-}+b\right)\circ z_{1}\circ z_{2},

Clearly, in the case of a skew brace

In general,

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(S,+,\circ)
be a dual weak brace in which the Clifford semigroup

is commutative, then
\mathcal{D}_{r}(S)
is a two-sided dual weak subbrace of

Proof.

By

, it is enough to show that
\mathcal{D}_{r}(S)
is an inverse subsemigroup of

. Clearly,

and

\displaystyle(a+b)\circ(x+y)

\displaystyle=a\circ x-x+b\circ x-b-a+a\circ y-y+b\circ y

\displaystyle=a\circ x-a+a\circ y-(x+y)+b\circ x-b+b\circ y

\displaystyle=a\circ(x+y)-(x+y)+b\circ(x+y),

and thus

and so

Remark 3.3

As a consequence, if

Remark 3.4

Observe that if

As is usual in ring theory, in any brace

\displaystyle\forall\,a,b\in B\quad z\in\mathcal{D}_{r}(B)\iff(a+b)\cdot z=a\cdot z+b\cdot z.

The following result describes all the parameters giving rise to a deformed solution in a left brace.
The proof is essentially obtained by extracting the key equalities contained in the proof of

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\left(B,+,\circ\right)
be a brace. Then, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{r}(B)=\{z\in B\ |\ \forall\,a,b\in B\quad(a\cdot b)\cdot z=a\cdot(b\cdot z)\}.

Proof.

If
z\in\mathcal{D}_{r}(B)
, the claim is proven by describing
\circ
in terms of
\cdot
in the associativity condition of the
\circ
. For the other inclusion, by making explicit the equality
(a\cdot b)\cdot z=a\cdot(b\cdot z)
and multiplying both sides by
a^{-}
, we get

(b+a^{-}\circ(-b))\circ z=b\circ z-z+2\left(a^{-}\circ z\right)-a^{-}\circ b\circ z,

for all

. Now, since by the proof of

(-x)\circ z=2z-x\circ z,
for all

we get that, for all

and so

\left(b+a^{-}\circ(-b)\right)\circ z=b\circ z-z+\left(a^{-}\circ(-b)\right)\circ z,

i.e.

∎

In light of the previous proposition, if

Corollary 3.6

Any brace
\left(B,+,\circ\right)

Now, it becomes natural to wonder if the distributor is an ideal. We recall that a subset

Equivalently, according to

Recalling that a non-trivial brace is right nilpotent of index

Proposition 3.7
.

Let

be a brace. If

. If

Example 7
.

If we consider the brace
A_{6}
as in

In the following, we characterize, in general, when the distributor is an ideal for the braces

Proposition 3.8
.

Let

and

be the brace defined in

\mathcal{D}_{r}({A_{n}})
is a left ideal. Moreover,

if and only if

,
\mathrm{gcd}(4,n)=1
, or

Proof.

The first statement holds by observing that
k\cdot d=\left(\left(-1\right)^{k}-1\right)d\in\mathcal{D}_{r}({A_{n}})
, for all

and

.

For all
k\in A_{n}
and

, we have:

d\cdot k=\left(\left(-1\right)^{d}-1\right)k=\begin{cases}0\ &\text{if}\ 2\mid d\\
-2k\ &\text{if}\ 2\nmid d\end{cases}\,.

(6)

Assume that
\mathcal{D}_{r}({A_{n}})
is an ideal of
A_{n}
and let us break down our consideration to the following cases.

-

If
\mathrm{gcd}(n,4)=1,
then
\mathcal{D}_{r}({A_{n}})=\{0\}
and
n
can be any number coprime with

-

If
\mathrm{gcd}(n,4)=2,
then
\mathcal{D}_{r}({A_{n}})=\{0,\frac{n}{2}\}
and

or
\frac{n}{2}\cdot k=\frac{n}{2}\pmod{n}.
Since

and so, by (

is an ideal, we have that

4
Bites of parameters

In this section, regarding a dual weak brace
S
as a strong semilattice of skew braces
B_{\alpha}
, we analyze how the entire distributor of
S
interacts with the distributor of each
B_{\alpha}
. In addition, we show when a deformed solution on
S
is the strong semilattice of deformed solutions on
B_{\alpha}
.

Hereinafter, through the section,
S
will be seen as a strong semilattice
[Y,B_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha,\beta}]
. First, in the following, we show when
\mathcal{D}_{r}(S)
is the disjoint union of each
\mathcal{D}_{r}(B_{\alpha})
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
S
be a dual weak brace, then
\mathcal{D}_{r}(S)\subseteq\mathop{\dot{\bigcup}}\limits_{\alpha\in Y}\mathcal{D}_{r}(B_{\alpha})
.

Moreover,
\mathcal{D}_{r}(S)=\mathop{\dot{\bigcup}}\limits_{\alpha\in Y}\mathcal{D}_{r}(B_{\alpha})
if and only if
\phi_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\mathcal{D}_{r}(B_{\alpha})\right)\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{r}(B_{\beta})
, for all
\alpha,\beta\in Y
such that
\beta\leq\alpha
.

Proof.

If
z\in\mathcal{D}_{r}(S),
then there exists
\alpha\in Y
such that
z\in\mathcal{D}_{r}(B_{\alpha})
and thus
\mathcal{D}_{r}(S)\subseteq\mathop{\dot{\bigcup}}\limits_{\alpha\in Y}\mathcal{D}_{r}(B_{\alpha}).

Let us assume that
z\in\mathop{\dot{\bigcup}}\limits_{\alpha\in Y}\mathcal{D}_{r}(B_{\alpha})
and that
\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathcal{D}_{r}(B_{\alpha}))\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{r}(B_{\beta}),
for all
\alpha,\beta\in Y
such that
\beta\leq\alpha
. Then, there exists
\gamma\in Y
such that
z\in\mathcal{D}_{r}(B_{\gamma})
, and, for all
a\in B_{\alpha}
and
b\in B_{\beta}
, we have

\displaystyle(a+b)\circ z

\displaystyle=(\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta\gamma}(a)+\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta\gamma}(b))\circ\phi_{\gamma,\alpha\beta\gamma}(z)

\displaystyle=\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta\gamma}(a)\circ\phi_{\gamma,\alpha\beta\gamma}(z)-\phi_{\gamma,\alpha\beta\gamma}(z)+\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta\gamma}(b)\circ\phi_{\gamma,\alpha\beta\gamma}(z)

\displaystyle=a\circ z-z+b\circ z,

since
\phi_{\gamma,\alpha\beta\gamma}\left(\mathcal{D}_{r}(B_{\gamma}))\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{r}(B_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\right).
Hence,

z\in\mathcal{D}_{r}(S).

Conversely, if
\mathcal{D}_{r}(S)=\mathop{\dot{\bigcup}}\limits_{\alpha\in Y}\mathcal{D}_{r}(B_{\alpha}),
then for all
\alpha,\beta\in Y,

a,b\in B_{\beta}
and
z\in\mathcal{D}_{r}(B_{\alpha})
such that
\beta\leq\alpha,
we get that

(a+b)\circ\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=(a+b)\circ z=a\circ z-z+b\circ z=a\circ\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(z)-\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(z)+b\circ\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(z),

since
z\in\mathcal{D}_{r}(S).
Thus,

\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathcal{D}_{r}(B_{\alpha}))\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{r}(B_{\beta}).

∎

In the following example,
\mathcal{D}_{r}(S)
is not the union of distributors.

.

Let

, with

. Considering the cyclic group

be the trivial brace on

,

the brace

given in

\varphi:B_{\alpha}\to B_{\beta}
the brace homomorphism given by
\varphi\left(a\right)=2a
, for all

. Then,

is a dual weak brace. Moreover,
\mathcal{D}_{r}\left(B_{\alpha}\right)=B_{\alpha}
,

, and

, hence trivially
\varphi\left(\mathcal{D}_{r}\left(B_{\alpha}\right)\right)\nsubseteq\mathcal{D}_{r}\left(B_{\beta}\right)
. Indeed, in this case,
\mathcal{D}_{r}\left(S\right)=\{\,0_{\alpha},3_{\alpha},0_{\beta},3_{\beta}\,\}
.

Note that in the example above
\mathcal{D}_{r}\left(S\right)
is not an ideal of

is an ideal of each skew brace
B_{\gamma}
. We highlight that the notion of ideal has also been given for dual weak braces in
[
8
]
and makes use of the definitions of normal subsemigroups of Clifford semigroups. Moreover,
[
8
, Theorem 3]
is a structure theorem for ideals of a dual weak brace
S=[Y,B_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha,\beta}]
in terms of the ideals of the skew braces
B_{\alpha}
. Indeed, as a direct consequence of
4.1
and
[
8
, Theorem 3.2]
, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
S
be a dual weak brace. If
\mathcal{D}_{r}(B_{\alpha})
is an ideal of each skew brace
B_{\alpha}
, for every
\alpha\in Y
, then
\mathcal{D}_{r}(S)
is an ideal of
S
if and only if
\mathcal{D}_{r}(S)=\mathop{\dot{\bigcup}}\limits_{\alpha\in Y}\mathcal{D}_{r}(B_{\alpha})
.

In this part, we compare deformed solutions on a dual weak brace
S
acquired in
2.9
with solutions constructed as a strong semilattice of deformed solutions on skew braces
B_{\alpha}
. Although in
4.1
we characterize when
\mathcal{D}_{r}(S)=\mathop{\dot{\bigcup}}\limits_{\alpha\in Y}\mathcal{D}_{r}(B_{\alpha})
, it is not guaranteed that, in this case,
r_{z}
is a strong semilattice of some deformed solutions on
B_{\alpha}
, for some
z\in\mathcal{D}_{r}({S})
, since
3.
of
1.4
is not satisfied, in general. We will show that it is true only for some parameters and if the semilattice is bounded.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
S
be a dual weak brace. A subset
P\subseteq S
of
S
is said to be a
bite of parameters
if the following hold:

1.

P\cap B_{\alpha}=\{p_{\alpha}\}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{r}(B_{\alpha})
, for every
\alpha\in Y
,

2.

a\circ p_{\beta}-a\circ p_{\alpha}=p_{\beta}-p_{\alpha}
,
for all
\alpha,\beta\in Y
such that
\beta\leq\alpha
and
a\in B_{\alpha}
.

We will denote the family of all bites of parameters of
S
by
\mathcal{B}(S)
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
S
be a dual weak brace and
P\subseteq{S}
such that
P\cap B_{\alpha}=\{p_{\alpha}\}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{r}(B_{\alpha})
, for every
\alpha\in Y
. If
\phi_{\alpha,\beta}\left(p_{\alpha}\right)\in P,
for all
p_{\alpha}\in P
,
\alpha,\beta\in Y
such that
\beta\leq\alpha
, then
P\in\mathcal{B}(S)
.

The converse of
Lemma

4.4
is not true, as we show in the following example.

Example 9
.

Let
S
be the dual weak braces given by
Y=\{\alpha,\beta\}
with
\beta<\alpha
,
B_{\alpha}=\mathbb{Z}
,
B_{\beta}=\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
trivial braces, and
\phi_{\alpha,\beta}:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
the canonical epimorphism. Thus,
P=\{0_{\alpha},2_{\beta}\}\in\mathcal{B}(S),
but
\phi_{\alpha,\beta}\left(0_{\alpha}\right)=0_{\beta}\not\in P.

Remark 4.5
.

Observe that for any dual weak brace
S
, we have that
\mathcal{B}(S)
is non-empty. Indeed, the set
\E(S)=\{0_{\alpha}\ |\ \alpha\in Y\}
is a bite of parameters, as every homomorphism
\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}
preserves the identity of each skew brace.

Example 10
.

Let
S=[Y,U_{n},\phi_{n,m}]
be the dual weak brace in
2
,
M
the maximum in the set
Y
, and
a\in U_{M}
a fixed element. Then, the set
\{\phi_{M,m}(a)\ |\ m\in Y\}\in\mathcal{B}(S)
.

In the following, to avoid the overloading notation, by
r_{p_{\alpha}}
we mean the deformed solution on the skew brace
B_{\alpha}
by a parameter
p_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{D}_{r}\left(B_{\alpha}\right)
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
S
be a dual weak brace and
r_{p_{\alpha}}
a deformed solution on
B_{\alpha}
, for every
\alpha\in Y
. Then, the map
t:S\times S\to S\times S
defined by

t(x,y):=r_{p_{\alpha\beta}}(\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}(x),\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta}(y)),

for all
x\in B_{\alpha}
,
y\in B_{\beta}
, is a strong semilattice of the solutions
r_{p_{\alpha}}
if and only if
\{p_{\alpha}\ |\ \alpha\in Y\}\in\mathcal{B}(S).

Proof.

Let us assume that
\{p_{\alpha}\ |\ \alpha\in Y\}\in\mathcal{B}(S).
Then, to get the claim, we have to check
3.
of
1.4
, i.e.,

\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(a)\circ p_{\beta}-\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(a\circ p_{\alpha})=\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(a\circ b)\circ p_{\beta}-\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(a\circ b\circ p_{\alpha}),

(7)

for all
\alpha,\beta\in Y
such that
\beta\leq\alpha
and for all
a,b\in B_{\alpha}
.
Using
2.
of
4.3
, we get that

\displaystyle\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(a)\circ p_{\beta}-\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(a\circ p_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=p_{\beta}-\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(p_{\alpha})=\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(a\circ b)\circ p_{\beta}-\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(a\circ b)\circ\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(p_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(a\circ b)\circ p_{\beta}-\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(a\circ b\circ p_{\alpha}).

Other way, let us assume that
r_{p_{\alpha\beta}}
is the strong semilattice of the solutions
r_{p_{\alpha}}
and consider
P=\{p_{\alpha}\ |\ \alpha\in Y\}.
Clearly,
P\cap B_{\alpha}=\{p_{\alpha}\}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{r}(B_{\alpha})
, for every
\alpha\in Y.
By the previous part, if
a\in B_{\alpha}
,
by taking
b=a^{-}
in (
7
), we obtain property 2. of
4.3
.
∎

In the following result, we will denote by
1
the join of the semilattice
Y
, whenever it is bounded.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
S
be a dual weak brace and
z\in\mathcal{D}_{r}(S).

1.

If
Y
is not bounded or
z\not\in B_{1}
, then the deformed solution
r_{z}
is not a strong semilattice of the solutions defined on
B_{\alpha}
, for every
\alpha\in Y
.

2.

If
Y
is bounded and
r_{z}
is a strong semilattice of solutions, then
z\in B_{1}
and there exists
P=\{\phi_{1,\alpha}(z)\,\mid\,\alpha\in Y\}\in\mathcal{B}(S)
such that
r_{z}
is the strong semilattice of solutions
r_{\phi_{1,\alpha}(z)}
on each skew brace
B_{\alpha}
, for every
\alpha\in Y
.

Proof.

1.

Let
z\in B_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in Y.
Observe that if
Y
is not bounded or
\alpha\not=1,
then there exists
\beta\in Y
such that
\beta\alpha\not=\beta.
In that case, for all
a,b\in B_{\beta}
,

r_{z}(a,b)\subseteq B_{\beta\alpha}\times B_{\beta\alpha}\not=B_{\beta}\times B_{\beta},
and thus
r_{z\mid B_{\beta}\times B_{\beta}}
is not a well-defined solution on
B_{\beta}.
Consequently,
r_{z}
is not a strong semilattice of solutions on
B_{\alpha}
, for every
\alpha\in Y
.

2.

Clearly,
z\in B_{1}
by the previous point. Let us consider
P=\{\phi_{1,\alpha}(z)\ |\ \alpha\in Y\}.
Then, one can easily check that
P\cap B_{\alpha}=\{\phi_{1,\alpha}(z)\}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{r}\left(B_{\alpha}\right)
, for every
\alpha\in Y
. Moreover, if
\alpha,\beta\in Y
are such that
\beta\leq\alpha,
then
\phi_{\alpha,\beta}\phi_{1,\alpha}(z)=\phi_{1,\beta}(z)\in P.
Thus, by
Lemma

4.4
,
P\in\mathcal{B}(S).
Furthermore, if
\alpha,\beta\in Y
and
a\in B_{\alpha}
,
b\in B_{\beta}
,

\displaystyle r_{z}(a,b)=r_{\phi_{1,\alpha\beta}(z)}\left(\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}(a),\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}(b)\right).

Finally, by
Lemma

4.6
, we get the claim.

∎

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
S
be a dual weak brace,
P\in\mathcal{B}(S)
, and
z\in P
. Then,
r_{z}
is the strong semilattice of deformed solutions on
B_{\alpha}
through parameters in
P
if and only if
Y
is bounded and
z\in B_{1}.

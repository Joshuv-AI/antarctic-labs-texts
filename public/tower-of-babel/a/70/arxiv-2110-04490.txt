INR-TH-2021-018 On resonant generation of electromagnetic modes in nonlinear electrodynamics: Classical approach
Ilia Kopchinskii, Petr Satunin

Abstract
The paper explores a theoretical possibility of resonant amplification of electromagnetic modes generated by a nonlinear effect in Euler-Heisenberg electrodynamics. Precisely, we examine the possibility of the amplification for the third harmonics induced by a single electromagnetic mode in radiofrequency cavity, as well as the generation of signal mode of combined frequencies induced by two pump modes ( ω 1 \omega_{1} and ω 2 \omega_{2} ) in the cavity. Solving inhomogeneous wave equations for the signal mode, we formulate two resonant conditions for a cavity of arbitrary shape, and apply the obtained formalism to linear and rectangular cavities. We explicitly show that the third harmonics as well as the mode of combined frequency 2 ​ ω 1 + ω 2 2\omega_{1}+\omega_{2} are not resonantly amplified while the signal mode with frequency 2 ​ ω 1 − ω 2 2\omega_{1}-\omega_{2} is amplified for a certain cavity geometry.

2
Nonlinear Maxwell and wave equations

In this section we briefly review the field equations appeared in nonlinear electrodynamics. The Euler-Heisenberg Lagrangian in the limit of weak electric and magnetic field (
E,H\ll m_{e}^{2}/e\,\sim\,10^{18}\,\mbox{V/m}
) takes the form
[
1
,
2
]
:

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{F}+\varepsilon(\mathcal{F}^{2}+\beta\mathcal{G}^{2}),\qquad\varepsilon=\frac{\alpha_{e}^{2}}{90m_{e}^{4}},\quad\beta=\frac{7}{4},

(1)

where
\alpha_{e}
is fine structure constant and
m_{e}
is the electron mass. In presence of hypothetical scalar or pseudoscalar particles in theory the coefficients
\varepsilon
and
\beta
are modified
[
6
,
12
]
. The electromagnetic field invariants have the standard form,

\mathcal{F}\equiv F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}=-2\left(E^{2}-H^{2}\right),\qquad\qquad\mathcal{G}\equiv F_{\mu\nu}{\widetilde{F}}^{\mu\nu}=-4\left(\bm{E}\cdot\bm{H}\right).

(2)

The electromagnetic field equations obtained from the Lagrangian (
1
) have the form analogous to Maxwell equations in medium
[
2
]
,

\begin{aligned} \,\text{rot}\bm{H}&=\frac{\partial\bm{E}}{\partial t}+\left[\frac{\partial\bm{P}}{\partial t}-\,\text{rot}\bm{M}\right],\\
\,\text{rot}\bm{E}&=-\frac{\partial\bm{H}}{\partial t},\\
\end{aligned}\hskip 56.9055pt\begin{aligned} \,\text{div}\bm{H}&=0,\\
\,\text{div}\bm{E}&=\left[-\,\text{div}\bm{P}\right],\end{aligned}

(3)

where
{\bm{P}}
and
{\bm{M}}
denote vacuum polarization and magnetization respectively,

\displaystyle\bm{P}(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle\equiv 16\varepsilon\left[\left(E^{2}-H^{2}\right)\bm{E}+2\beta(\bm{E}\cdot\bm{H})\bm{H}\right],

(4)

\displaystyle\bm{M}(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle\equiv 16\varepsilon\left[\left(E^{2}-H^{2}\right)\bm{H}-2\beta(\bm{E}\cdot\bm{H})\bm{E}\right].

The field equations (
3
) yield to modified wave equations both for amplitudes for electric and magnetic fields,

\displaystyle\Box\bm{E}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\,\text{rot}\bm{M}+{\,\text{grad}\,\text{div}\bm{P}}-\frac{\partial^{2}\bm{P}}{\partial{t}^{2}},

(5)

\displaystyle\Box\bm{H}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\,\text{rot}\bm{P}-{\,\text{grad}\,\text{div}\bm{M}}+\Delta\bm{M}.

Note that the plane electromagnetic wave is a solution of modified wave equations (
5
) since both electromagnetic invariants vanish at the plane wave configuration,
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{G}=0
. However, a linear combination of plane waves is no longer a solution of equations (
5
) and so becomes unstable, leading to the production of new modes.

3
General formalism of searching for resonant modes

One of the interesting features of modified wave equations (
5
) is the possibility for generation of higher-order harmonics by one or two initial electromagnetic modes in vacuum. Traditionally
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
, we consider the following setup devoted to the search for higher-order harmonics. We take a superconducting radiofrequency cavity (SRF) filled with one or two ‘‘pump’’ modes of very high amplitudes
\bm{E}^{pump},\bm{H}^{pump}
, and look for generation of a ‘‘signal’’ mode of different frequency with amplitudes
\bm{E}^{sig},\bm{H}^{sig}
which are expected to be small due the smallness of nonlinear coupling coefficient
\varepsilon
. Treating the signal mode as a small perturbation in eq. (
5
) and assuming the hierarchy of scales
|\bm{E}^{sig}|\sim\varepsilon\left(|\bm{E}^{pump}|\right)^{3}\ll|\bm{E}^{pump}|
, one obtains in the zeroth order trivial wave equations for the pump modes
\Box\bm{E}^{pump}=0,\ \Box\bm{H}^{pump}=0
, and in the first order:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Box\bm{E}^{sig}&=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\,\text{rot}\bm{M}(\bm{E}^{pump},\bm{H}^{pump})+{\,\text{grad}\,\text{div}\bm{P}(\bm{E}^{pump},\bm{H}^{pump})}-\frac{\partial^{2}\bm{P}(\bm{E}^{pump},\bm{H}^{pump})}{\partial{t}^{2}},\\
\Box\bm{H}^{sig}&=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\,\text{rot}\bm{P}(\bm{E}^{pump},\bm{H}^{pump})-{\,\text{grad}\,\text{div}\bm{M}(\bm{E}^{pump},\bm{H}^{pump})}+\Delta\bm{M}(\bm{E}^{pump},\bm{H}^{pump}).\end{aligned}

(6)

Here the polarization and magnetization vectors (
4
) are computed on the pump mode configuration. Instead of nonlinear eq. (
5
), eq. (
6
) is a linear wave equation on the signal mode amplitudes with nonzero r.h.s. The solution of eq. (
6
) determines the evolution of the signal mode at classical level.

The equations (
6
) are to be solved in a given cavity
D
of finite volume. Furthermore, in order to take into account small dissipation, we introduce the dissipative term which includes the first-order time derivative and the damping coefficient
\Gamma
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \left(\Box-\Gamma\partial_{t}\right)\bm{E}^{sig}(\bm{x},t)&=\bm{F}(\bm{x},t),\quad\bm{x}\in D,\penalty\ t>0,\\
\bm{E}^{sig}(\bm{x},0)&=0,\quad\bm{x}\in D,\\
\bm{n}\times\bm{E}^{sig}(\bm{x},t)&=0,\quad{\bm{x}\in S}.\end{aligned}\right.

(7)

Here
S
denotes the surface of the cavity
D
,
\bm{n}
is the normal to the surface
S
.
\bm{F}(\bm{x},t)
denotes the r.h.s. of the electric equation in (
6
). The boundary conditions refer to an ideal conducting surface. The similar system should be written for magnetic component of the signal mode
\bm{H}^{sig}
, however we will further skip it for the sake of shortness.

The signal field
\bm{E}^{sig}(\bm{x},t)
can be expanded into the cavity eigenmodes,

\bm{E}^{sig}(\bm{x},t)=\sum_{k}E^{sig}_{k}(t)\,\bm{\mathcal{E}}_{k}(\bm{x}).

(8)

Here
\bm{\mathcal{E}}_{k}(\bm{x})
are the full system of eigenfunctions with eigenvalues
\omega_{k}
, satisfying the equation
\left(\Delta+\omega_{k}^{2}\right)\bm{\mathcal{E}}_{k}(\bm{x})=0
and boundary conditions given in the last line of eq. (
7
). Substituting expansion (
8
) to the first eq. of (
7
) and integrating over the whole cavity with a mode
\bm{\mathcal{E}}_{n}(\bm{x})
, one obtains:

\ddot{E}^{sig}_{n}(t)+\Gamma\dot{E}^{sig}_{n}(t)+\omega_{n}^{2}E^{sig}_{n}(t)=F_{n}(t),\qquad F_{n}(t)\equiv\frac{\int_{D}dV\bm{F}(\bm{x},t)\cdot\bm{\mathcal{E}}_{n}(\bm{x})}{\int_{D}dV\bm{\mathcal{E}}_{n}^{2}(\bm{x})}\equiv\frac{(\bm{F},\bm{\mathcal{E}}_{n})}{\left\lVert\bm{\mathcal{E}}_{n}\right\rVert^{2}}.

(9)

Here we made the following notations:
\int_{D}dV
for the integration over the volume of the cavity
D
,
(\bm{F},\bm{\mathcal{E}})
for the inner product of two vector functions
\bm{F}
and
\bm{\mathcal{E}}
, and
\left\lVert\mathcal{E}\right\rVert=\sqrt{(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})}
for the norm of function
\mathcal{E}
. All frequency components of
F_{n}(t)
lead to generation of a signal mode with a certain amplitude. If
F_{n}(t)
includes the component
F_{n}(t)\supseteq\operatorname{Re}\left(F_{n}^{0}e^{-i\omega t}\right)
, the signal field of the amplitude

E^{sig}_{n}(t)=\operatorname{Re}\frac{F_{n}^{0}e^{-i\omega t}}{-\omega^{2}-i\omega\Gamma+\omega_{n}^{2}}

(10)

is generated in the steady regime. If the frequency
\omega
coincides with one of the cavity eigenfrequencies,
\omega=\omega_{n}
, the first and the third terms in the denominator of (
10
) cancel each other and so the amplitude of such signal mode is resonantly enhanced,
E^{sig}_{n}(t)=\operatorname{Re}\left(iF_{n}^{0}e^{-i\omega t}/(\Gamma\omega)\right)
.

Let us summarize aforementioned expressions in a more strict way as a criterion for resonant amplification of the signal mode. Assume that the r.h.s. of one of the six scalar wave equations (
6
) contains the frequency component
\omega_{sig}
. The signal mode with the frequency
\omega_{sig}
is resonantly amplified if both of the following conditions hold simultaneously,

1.

Frequency
\omega_{sig}
belongs to the cavity spectrum (
\exists\penalty\ n\in\mathbb{N}:\penalty\ \omega_{sig}=\omega_{n}
),

2.

The scalar product
F_{n}(t)
of the r.h.s. of the considered scalar wave equation from (
6
) with the
n
-th cavity eigenmode contains the frequency component
\omega_{sig}=\omega_{n}
.

Note that even if
\bm{F}(\bm{x},t)
contains a frequency component
\omega_{m}
, it may disappear from
F_{m}(t)
due to the integration with orthogonal cavity mode. An example supporting this statement will be provided in the next section.

Two pump modes

The last configuration being considered includes two pump modes (for certainty, one TM- and one TE-mode) excited in a rectangular cavity. The electric and magnetic fields of this configuration read,

\displaystyle\bm{E}^{pump}(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle=F_{0}\operatorname{Re}\left[\bm{\mathcal{E}}^{TM}_{n_{1},p_{1},q_{1}}(\bm{x})e^{i\omega_{1}t}+\bm{\mathcal{E}}^{TE}_{n_{2},p_{2},q_{2}}(\bm{x})e^{i\omega_{2}t}\right],

\omega_{sig}=2\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}=\left(|2\bm{k}_{1}|+|\bm{k}_{2}|\right)\penalty\ \geqslant\penalty\ |2\bm{k}_{1}+\bm{k}_{2}|=\sqrt{(2k_{1x}+k_{2x})^{2}+(2k_{1y}+k_{2y})^{2}+(2k_{1z}+k_{2z})^{2}}.

6
Discussion

In the current paper we have formulated the conditions for the resonant amplification of a signal mode in a cavity of arbitrary shape, and applied them to the analysis of linear and rectangular cavities. We have demonstrated that two pump modes with frequencies
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
in a rectangular cavity resonantly produce a signal mode with frequency
2\omega_{2}-\omega_{1}
(
2\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}
) for a certain cavity geometry. On the other side, we have proved that the signal modes with frequencies
2\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}
(
2\omega_{2}+\omega_{1}
), as well as the third harmonics
3\omega_{1}
(
3\omega_{2}
) are not resonantly amplified. Remind that the resonant amplification means an enhancement in
Q
times, where
Q
is the cavity quality factor which can achieve a numerical value up to
10^{12}

[
19
]
.

The crucial point of our proof for the absence of resonance in rectangular cavity is that the cavity eigenmodes include only trigonometric functions which allows us to make analytic calculations for arbitrary cavity modes. This stops working for a cavity of arbitrary shape, in that case the numerical calculations for specific cavity modes become necessary.

The absence of resonant amplification of the third harmonics and combined ‘‘plus’’ modes seems to be connected with the polarization structure of the vector gauge field. Considering the similar problem of higher-order harmonics generation for massless scalar field with
\lambda\varphi^{4}
interaction instead of the electromagnetic one, one first obtains the analogue of the inhomogeneous wave equation (
5
) which reads
\Box\varphi=\lambda\varphi^{3}
. Decomposing
\varphi
into the pump mode
\varphi_{pump}
which is a cavity eigenmode, and the signal mode
\varphi_{sig}
of small amplitude, one can easily check that the third harmonics does generate for the scalar field. We hope that the reason of this difference between scalar and electromagnetic field would become more clear after considering aforementioned processes on quantum level.

To conclude, we have considered the problem of vacuum generation of higher-order harmonics only from the theoretical side. Although the scheme of such experiment for the case of cylindrical cavity was in general studied in
[
11
]
, there are still several unsolved issues, including proper treatment of nonlinearities from the cavity walls etc.

Volume-preserving mean-curvature flow as a singular limit of a diffusion-aggregation equation Thanks: A. Mellet was partially supported by NSF Grant DMS-2009236 and DMS-2307342.
A. Mellet, M. Rozowski

Abstract
. The Patlak-Keller-Segel system of equations (PKS) is a classical example of aggregation-diffusion equation in which the repulsive effect of diffusion is in competition with the attractive chemotaxis term. Recent work on the Parabolic-Elliptic PKS model have shown that when the repulsion is modeled by a nonlinear diffusion term ρ ∇ ρ m − 1 \rho\nabla\rho^{m-1} with m > 2 m>2 , this competition leads to phase separation phenomena. Furthermore, in some asymptotic regime corresponding to a large population observed over a long enough time, the interface separating regions of high and low density evolves according to the Hele-Shaw free boundary problem with surface tension. In the present paper, we consider the counterpart of that model, namely the Elliptic-Parabolic PKS model and we prove that the same phase separation phenomena occurs, but the motion of the interface is now described (asymptotically) by a volume-preserving mean-curvature flow.

1.
Introduction

The following
parabolic-parabolic Patlak-Keller-Segel system

with nonlinear diffusion is a classical example of an aggregation-diffusion equation
[
17
,
38
]
:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\alpha\partial_{t}\rho-\mathrm{div}\left(\rho\nabla f^{\prime}(\rho)\right)+\chi\mathrm{div}\,(\rho\nabla\phi)=0,\quad\\
\beta\partial_{t}\phi-\eta\Delta\phi=\rho-\sigma\phi.\end{cases}

In this model,
\rho
denotes the density of some organisms and
\phi
denotes the concentration of some chemical (the chemoattractant) towards which the organisms are attracted (
\chi>0
).
The parameters
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
are constants denoting the timescales for the relaxation of the organism density and chemoattractant concentration, respectively.
The constant
\chi>0
is the strength of the chemoattraction;
\eta>0
is the diffusivity of the chemoattractant in the medium; and
\sigma>0
is the rate of destruction of the chemoattractant.
The nonlinear diffusion term models the mutual repulsion of the organisms:
The derivative
f^{\prime}(\rho)
can be interpreted as an internal pressure caused by the natural incompressibility of the organisms.
A common choice of pressure law is
f(\rho)=\frac{1}{m-1}\rho^{m}
for
m>1

which corresponds to degenerate nonlinear diffusion
or
f(\rho)=\rho\ln\rho
which gives the standard linear diffusion.

The key feature of this system is the competition between the repulsive action of the nonlinear diffusion (incompressibility of the organisms) and the attractive chemotaxis force
\nabla\phi
.
The fact that this competition might result in finite time blow-up of the density
\rho
has been extensively studied:
In dimension
d
,
it is known that the existence of global-in-time weak solutions is guaranteed whenever
m>2-\frac{2}{d}
(see for example
[
34
]
for the parabolic-parabolic system), but blow-up can occur for sufficiently large initial mass of organisms
\int\rho_{\mathrm{in}}\,dx
whenever
m=2-\frac{2}{d}
. Similarly for the so-called
parabolic-elliptic system
(
\beta=0
), it is known
[
42
,
5
]
that blow-up may occur when
m<2-\frac{2}{d}
, while solutions are bounded in
L^{\infty}
uniformly in time when
m>2-\frac{2}{d}
.

More recently, several works (see
[
21
,
33
]
and the survey
[
19
]
) have shown that when
m>2
, the competition
between repulsive and attractive forces in (
1.1
) leads to phase separation (at appropriate scales).
Phase separation, which refers to the ability of the organisms to organize themselves into segregated groups separated by sharp interfaces, is an important feature of many biological aggregation phenomena. Classical examples include animals moving as homogeneous patches (insect swarms) or the formation of membraneless organelles in eukaryotic cells.
When phase separation occurs, our focus naturally turns to the evolution of the sharp interface separating regions of high and low (or zero) organism density. More precisely, one would like to derive the equation (of a geometric nature) that governs the evolution of this interface, thus describing the collective dynamics of the organisms after aggregation.

Mathematically, the interface emerges through some singular limit which describes regimes in which the size of the support of
\rho
is much larger than the typical length scale of the transition layer between regions of high and low density.
Following
[
21
,
33
,
19
]
we thus consider a very large initial population of organisms, which we quantify by introducing a positive
\varepsilon\ll 1
such that

\int\rho_{\mathrm{in}}(\bar{x})\,d\bar{x}=\varepsilon^{-d},

where
\bar{x}
is the microscopic space variable.
We then rescale the microscopic space and time variables (
\bar{x}
and
\bar{t}
, resp.) as follows:

x:=\varepsilon\bar{x},\qquad t:=\tau^{-1}\varepsilon^{2}\bar{t}

for some time scale
\tau>0
to be chosen later
(where
x
and
t
denote the new – macroscopic – space and time variables). When
\varepsilon\ll 1
, this scaling amounts to observing the evolution of the population from far away (with a zoom out factor of
\varepsilon^{-1}
) and at time scale
\varepsilon^{-2}\tau
.
The functions
\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,x):=\rho(\bar{t},\bar{x})
and
\phi^{\varepsilon}(t,x):=\phi(\bar{t},\bar{x})
then solve

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\alpha\tau^{-1}\partial_{t}\rho^{\varepsilon}-\mathrm{div}\left(\rho^{\varepsilon}\nabla f^{\prime}(\rho^{\varepsilon})\right)+\chi\mathrm{div}\,(\rho^{\varepsilon}\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon})=0\quad&\mbox{ in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega,\\
\beta\tau^{-1}\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon}-\eta\Delta\phi^{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-2}\left(\rho^{\varepsilon}-\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon}\right)&\mbox{ in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega.\end{cases}

We note that the bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
has macroscopic size
\sim 1
and is thus independent of
\varepsilon
.
Denoting by
n
the outward unit normal vector to
\partial\Omega
, we supplement our equations with the following no-flux boundary conditions

(1.3)

(-\rho^{\varepsilon}\nabla f^{\prime}(\rho^{\varepsilon})+\chi\rho^{\varepsilon}\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon})\cdot n=0,\qquad\nabla\phi\cdot n=0\quad\mbox{on }\partial\Omega

and initial conditions

\rho^{\varepsilon}(0,x)=\rho^{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{in}}(x),\qquad\phi^{\varepsilon}(0,x)=\phi^{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{in}}(x)\qquad\mbox{ in }\Omega,

where we now have in particular

\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{in}}(x)\,dx=1.

The behavior of the solutions
\rho^{\varepsilon},\phi^{\varepsilon}
strongly depends on the ratio of time scales
\alpha/\beta
.
We note that it is natural to assume that the typical time of relaxation for the organisms (
\alpha
) is very different from that of the chemoattractant (
\beta
), since the underlying processes are of a very different nature. A classical simplification of this model consists in taking
\beta=0
(or more generally
\alpha\gg\beta
), effectively assuming that the chemoattractant concentration relaxes instantaneously (or very quickly) to its equilibrium
(determined by the distribution of the organisms).
The resulting
parabolic-elliptic
model is a popular model with good mathematical properties, e.g., a
2
-Wasserstein gradient flow structure.
In the present paper, we focus on the opposite regime
\alpha\ll\beta
, which amounts to assuming that the organisms (whose motion results from the simultaneous, antagonistic effects of strong repulsion and transport in the direction of
\nabla\phi
) reach a quasi-steady state much faster than the diffusion time for the chemoattractant.

These two opposite regimes lead to very different limits when
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. In fact, the asymptotic behavior of the solution of (
1.2
) depends on the relative values of the ratio
\alpha/\beta
and
\varepsilon
.
More precisely, we claim the following:

(1)

When
\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\gg\varepsilon
, the critical time scale is given by
\tau=\alpha
. With this choice for
\tau
, the evolution of
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\rho^{\varepsilon}
is then described by a
Stefan free boundary problem
whose long time limit is a characteristic function
\theta\chi_{E}
.

(2)

When
\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\gg\varepsilon
and the initial condition is a characteristic function
\theta\chi_{E}
,
we must look at the evolution of
\rho^{\varepsilon}
over the longer time scale
\tau=\alpha\varepsilon^{-1}
. The evolution of
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\rho^{\varepsilon}
is then described by a
Hele-Shaw free boundary problem with surface tension
.

(3)

When
\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\ll\varepsilon
, the critical time scale is
\tau=\beta
. At that time scale, the evolution of
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\rho^{\varepsilon}
is described by a
volume-preserving mean-curvature flow
.

(4)

When
\frac{\alpha}{\beta}=\varepsilon
and if the initial condition is a characteristic function
\theta\chi_{E}
, then
the critical time scale is
\tau=\beta=\alpha\varepsilon^{-1}
. The evolution of
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\rho^{\varepsilon}
is then described by a
Hele-Shaw free boundary problem with surface tension and kinetic undercooling
.

Points (1) and (2) were studied in
[
21
,
33
,
19
]
in the particular case
\beta=0
.
The main goal of this paper is to make precise point (3), the convergence to volume-preserving mean-curvature flow
in the particular case
\alpha=0
. The last point (4), which can be seen as an interpolation of points (2) and (3), is discussed in some upcoming work.

Taking
\chi=\eta=1
(for simplicity) and
\alpha=0
,
\tau=\beta
(as explained above), we are thus led to the following system (which is an
elliptic-parabolic Patlak-Keller-Segel system
with nonlinear diffusion):

(1.4)

\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}\left(\rho^{\varepsilon}\nabla f^{\prime}(\rho^{\varepsilon})\right)+\mathrm{div}\,(\rho^{\varepsilon}\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon})=0\quad&\mbox{in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon}-\Delta\phi^{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-2}\left(\rho^{\varepsilon}-\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon}\right)&\mbox{in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega,\\
(-\rho^{\varepsilon}\nabla f^{\prime}(\rho^{\varepsilon})+\rho^{\varepsilon}\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon})\cdot n=\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}\cdot n=0&\text{on }(0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega,\\
\phi^{\varepsilon}(0,\cdot)=\phi_{\mathrm{in}}^{\varepsilon}&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\rho\geq 0&\text{in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega,\\
\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\varepsilon}(0,x)\,dx=1&\text{for all }t\geq 0.\end{cases}

The first equation in this system should be understood as taking the long time limit of the first equation in the parabolic-parabolic system (
1.2
). In particular, the initial condition for
\rho^{\varepsilon}
is no longer relevant, but we need to add the positivity and mass conservation constraints, which are the final two conditions in the above elliptic-parabolic system.
Indeed, the first equation in (
1.2
) is a nonlinear diffusion equation with drift,
and when the drift term is a regular enough fixed potential
\phi
, it has been shown that the corresponding weak solution
converges uniformly in space as
t\to+\infty
to weak solutions of the corresponding quasilinear elliptic equation
[
6
, Theorem 5.1]
:

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\mathrm{div}\left(\rho\nabla f^{\prime}(\rho)\right)+\mathrm{div}\,(\rho\nabla\phi)=0&\qquad\text{in }\Omega,\\
(-\rho\nabla f^{\prime}(\rho)+\rho\nabla\phi)\cdot n=0&\qquad\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\
\rho\geq 0\qquad\text{and}\qquad&\int_{\Omega}\rho\,dx=1.\end{aligned}\right.

However, it is also known that solutions of this limiting elliptic equation are typically not unique unless further restrictions on
\phi
are imposed, e.g., strict concavity. Such restrictions are unreasonable for the systems we have in mind where
\phi
describes the concentration of some chemoattractant.

In view of this ill-posedness, we observe that the elliptic equation (
1.5
) is the Euler-Lagrange equation for the following strictly convex minimization problem where
\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
is prescribed:

(1.6)

\rho_{\phi}:=\argmin_{\rho\in L^{m}(\Omega)}\mathscr{K}(\rho;\phi)\quad\text{where}\quad\mathscr{K}(\rho;\phi):=\begin{cases}\int_{\Omega}f(\rho)-\rho\phi\,dx&\text{if }\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\Omega)\cap L^{m}(\Omega),\\
+\infty&\text{if }\rho\in L^{m}(\Omega)\setminus\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\Omega).\end{cases}

We denote by
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\Omega)
the collection of probability measures on
\Omega
that are absolutely continuous with respect to the
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure on
\Omega
, and we always identify such a measure with its density. We thus choose this minimization problem’s unique minimizer, denoted by
\rho_{\phi}
, to be the weak solution to our elliptic equation (
1.5
).

While this choice may seem arbitrary, we point out that
this minimizer is stable with respect to a large class of vanishing diffusions (linear or nonlinear).
In particular, the inclusion of a linear diffusive term
-\nu\Delta\rho
to (
1.5
) (modeling some natural noise in the motion of the organisms) leads to a perturbed functional
\mathscr{K}_{\nu}(\rho):=\mathscr{K}(\rho)+\nu\int_{\Omega}\rho\ln\rho\,dx

and one can show that the minimizer
\rho_{\phi,\nu}
of
\mathscr{K}_{\nu}
,
which is the unique solution of the corresponding elliptic equation (
1.5
),
converges to that of
\mathscr{K}
when
\nu\to 0
(in the sense of the narrow convergence of measure).
In anticipation of the discussion in Section
2
, we also note that this choice of weak solution for
\rho
is natural in view of the
assumption of convergence of the energy (
2.18
) that we will make to study the limit
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.

The existence and properties of the solution of the minimization problem (
1.6
) will be studied in Proposition
2.1
. With this unambiguous definition of
\rho_{\phi}
, we can now rewrite (
1.4
) as a parabolic equation

(
P_{\varepsilon}
)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon}-\Delta\phi^{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-2}\left(\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon}}-\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon}\right)&&\mbox{in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega,\\
&\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}\cdot n=0&&\text{on }(0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega,\\
&\phi^{\varepsilon}(0,\cdot)=\phi_{\mathrm{in}}^{\varepsilon}&&\text{in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

with a nonlinear, nonlocal operator
\phi(t,\cdot)\mapsto\rho_{\phi(t,\cdot)}
that maps the chemoattractant concentration profile at time
t
, i.e.,
\phi(t,\cdot)
, to the solution
\rho_{\phi(t,\cdot)}
of the minimization problem (
1.6
).

The asymptotic behavior of
\phi^{\varepsilon}
when
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
is obviously strongly dependent on this nonlocal operator, which in turn depends on the choice of nonlinearity
\rho\mapsto f(\rho)
.
While we will list the necessary assumptions on
f
in the next section, the typical
nonlinearity for which our results hold is the aforementioned power law
f_{m}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}

(1.7)

f_{m}(u):=\begin{cases}\frac{u^{m}}{m-1}&\text{if }u\geq 0,\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

with
m>2
. In that case, Proposition
2.1
gives the explicit formula

\rho_{\phi}(x)=c_{m}(\phi(x)-\ell(\phi))_{+}^{\frac{1}{m-1}},\qquad\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\phi}(x)\,dx=1\qquad\mbox{ with }c_{m}:=\left(\frac{m-1}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}

where
\ell(\phi)
is a real number (the Lagrange multiplier) determined by the mass constraint on the organisms. The parabolic equation (

P
ε

) then takes the form:

(1.8)

\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon}-\Delta\phi^{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-2}\left(c_{m}(\phi^{\varepsilon}-\ell(\phi^{\varepsilon}))_{+}^{\frac{1}{m-1}}-\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon}\right)

and we observe that for fixed
\ell
(small enough), the function
v\mapsto c_{m}(v-\ell)_{+}^{\frac{1}{m-1}}-\sigma v

is a bistable nonlinearity. Equation (
1.8
) can then be seen as a type of Allen-Cahn equation, and the
limit
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
of such an equation classically leads to mean-curvature flows.

Of course, the fact that
\ell
depends on
\phi
in (
1.8
) makes our equation highly nonlocal and means that new arguments will be needed to justify the limit.
But on the flip side, this dependence comes from the mass constraint
\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\phi}(t,x)\,dx=1
and
this mass constraint implies a mass constraint on
\phi^{\varepsilon}
in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. Indeed, integrating (

P
ε

) with respect to
x
shows that the mass
m^{\varepsilon}(t)=\int_{\Omega}\phi^{\varepsilon}(t)\,dx
solves

\frac{d}{dt}m^{\varepsilon}(t)=\varepsilon^{-2}(1-\sigma m^{\varepsilon}(t))

and so

(1.9)

\int_{\Omega}\phi^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,dx\to\frac{1}{\sigma}\qquad\mbox{ as $\varepsilon\to 0$ for all $t>0$}

(independently of the initial mass
\int\phi_{in}(x)\,dx
).
We thus see that while the equation (

P
ε

) does not preserve the mass: The limiting flow should be volume-preserving.

The main result of this paper (Theorem
2.4
) makes this heuristic rigorous and shows that the solution of (

P
ε

) converges, when
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
to the characteristic function of a set whose evolution is described by the following
volume-preserving mean-curvature flow
:

(
P_{0}
)

V=-\kappa+\Lambda\qquad\mbox{ on }\partial E(t)\cap\Omega,\qquad|E(t)|=\frac{1}{\sigma}\qquad\forall\,t>0,

where
V=V(t,x)
is the normal velocity of a point
x
on the boundary
\partial E(t)
of some set
E(t)\subset\overline{\Omega}
;

\kappa(t,x)
denotes the mean-curvature of
\partial E(t)
at
x\in\partial E(t)
(with the convention that
\kappa(t,x)>0
at points
x\in\partial E(t)
for which
\partial E(t)
is locally convex); and
\Lambda=\Lambda(t)
is a Lagrange multiplier that enforces the volume constraint. Further, the corresponding distribution of organisms
\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon}}
converges to a scalar multiple of the limit of
\phi^{\varepsilon}
, so this resolves the asymptotic dynamics we ultimately set-out to describe.

We will prove our main result when
f=f_{m}
with
m\in(2,\infty)
and for more general nonlinearities that have similar properties.
As a final comment, we note that several recent works (e.g.,
[
20
,
21
]
) have also focused on the related
congested
(or incompressible, or hard-sphere) model, which corresponds to the nonlinearity

f_{\infty}(u):=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{ if }0\leq u\leq 1,\\
+\infty&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

While we will not investigate the corresponding limit in this paper, a similar convergence result is expected to hold in that framework as well.
One issue in that case is that, depending on
\phi
, the minimization problem (
1.6
) may not have a unique solution in that case (the corresponding energy is not strictly convex). This may be remedied by adding a small linear diffusion term that goes to zero with
\varepsilon
, but we will not pursue this analysis in this paper.

Main results

We now make precise the assumptions on the nonlinearity
f
that are used in our analysis. This first collection of hypotheses is used to prove the well-posedness of (

P
ε

). Additional hypotheses needed to show phase separation and derive the volume-preserving mean-curvature flow will be described later.

(F1)

f\in C^{1}([0,\infty))\cap C^{2}((0,\infty))
,
f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)=0
, and
f
is strictly convex on
[0,+\infty)
;

(F2)

There exists
m>2
,
R_{1}>0
, and
C_{1}>0
such that
f(u)\geq C_{1}u^{m}
whenever
u>R_{1}
.

These assumptions are natural from the point of view of the variational methods we aim to use. Indeed,
(F2)
implies
f
is superlinear
\lim_{u\to+\infty}f(u)/u=+\infty
, and superlinearity and convexity of
f
are necessary to ensure the lower semicontinuity w.r.t. narrow convergence of the entropy-like term
\rho\mapsto\int_{\Omega}f(\rho)\,dx
in
\mathscr{K}
(see
[
40
, Proposition 7.7]
). In view of the mass constraint on
\rho
, the function
f
is defined up to an affine function, since the addition of any such function to
f
only changes the energy
\mathscr{K}
by a constant. Thus, the assumption
f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)=0
is no restriction.
Strict
convexity
(F1)
will imply that
\mathscr{K}
has a unique minimizer whenever
\phi
is prescribed, so the map
\phi\mapsto\rho_{\phi}
is uniquely defined.

On the one hand, these two assumptions will be enough to prove the existence of a solution to (

P
ε

) and will be essential in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.
In particular, the restriction
m>2
in
(F2)
plays a key role in this limit (as explain in
[
19
]
).
We note that the existence of a solution can be established when
m=2
, but this requires additional work since the coercivity estimate
(
3.2
) does not hold in that case.
On the other hand, these two conditions do not appear to be sufficient to prove the uniqueness of a solution to (

P
ε

), which we will establish under the stronger condition:

(F2)

(uniform convexity) There exist
\alpha>0
such that
\inf_{u>0}f^{\prime\prime}(u)\geq\alpha
.

This condition implies that the mapping
\phi\to\rho_{\phi}
is Lipschitz
L^{2}(\Omega)\mapsto L^{2}(\Omega)
, which is sufficient to obtain uniqueness of our weak solution to the overall system.
The typical nonlinearities
f
for which (

P
ε

) has a solution and our convergence result (Theorem
2.4
below) holds are power law nonlinearities given by:

(2.1)

f(u):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{u^{m}}{m-1}&\text{if }u\geq 0,\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\quad m>2.

Such a function satisfies
(F1)
and
(F2)
, but not
(F2)
.
An example of nonlinearity that satisfies all three assumptions is:

(2.2)

f(u):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{u^{m}}{m-1}+\frac{\alpha}{\beta(\beta-1)}u^{\beta}&\text{if }u\geq 0,\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

with
m>2
,
\alpha>0
and
\beta\in(1,2]
.
It would also be possible to replace
\frac{u^{\beta}}{\beta(\beta-1)}
by
u\ln u
(which corresponds to the inclusion of
-\alpha\Delta\rho
in (
1.5
)) although
(F1)
would have to be adjusted in such a case.
In particular, the limit
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
would be significantly different, since the density and chemoattractant separate into
two positive phases
, i.e., two regions of differing positive densities, instead of one. The resulting volume-preserving mean-curvature flow is thus two-phase instead of one-phase.

2.1.
Well-posedness of (

P
ε

)

Our first task is to prove the existence and uniqueness of
\rho_{\phi}
, the solution of the minimization problem (
1.6
) for a given
\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.

We first prove:

Proposition 2.1

(Well-posedness of (
1.5
))
.

Suppose
f
satisfies
(F1)
and
(F2)
(it is enough to assume that
m\in[2,+\infty)
).

(i)

(existence and uniqueness) Given
\phi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique
\rho_{\phi}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\Omega)\cap L^{m}(\Omega)
that solves the minimization problem (
1.6
). Furthermore, we have

(2.3)

\|\rho\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}^{m}\leq C+C\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

for some constant
C>0
depending only on
f
and
\Omega
.

(ii)

(support of the minimizer) Under the same conditions, there exists
\ell\in\mathbb{R}
(called
the Lagrange multiplier for the mass constraint
) such that

(2.4)

f^{\prime}(\rho_{\phi})-\phi=-\ell\qquad\rho_{\phi}\text{-a.e.}

(iii)

(Euler-Lagrange equation)
If
\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, then
f^{\prime}(\rho_{\phi})\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and

-\rho_{\phi}\nabla f^{\prime}(\rho_{\phi})+\rho_{\phi}\nabla\phi=0
a.e. in
\Omega
.
In particular,
\rho_{\phi}
is a weak solution of the quasilinear elliptic equation (
1.5
); that is,

(2.5)

\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\phi}\nabla f^{\prime}(\rho_{\phi})\cdot\nabla\zeta-\rho_{\phi}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\zeta\,dx=0\qquad\forall\,\zeta\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}).

Next, we define the energy functional associated to (

P
ε

):

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\varepsilon}(\phi)

\displaystyle:=\inf_{\rho\in L^{m}(\Omega)}\left\{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}f(\rho)-\rho\phi+\frac{\sigma}{2}|\phi|^{2}\,dx+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}f(\rho_{\phi})-\rho_{\phi}\phi+\frac{\sigma}{2}|\phi|^{2}\,dx+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx,

where
\rho_{\phi}
is the unique solution to (
1.6
) for a given
\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. A formal computation shows that smooth solutions of (

A:=\left\{\rho_{\phi}>0\right\}\cap\left\{F\geq-\ell\right\},\qquad B:=\left\{\rho_{\phi}>0\right\}\cap\left\{F<-\ell\right\},

have both positive measure.
We move the mass that
\rho_{\phi}
puts on
A
onto the set
B
where
F
is smaller, in such a way that contradicts the optimality condition (
3.3
). Consider

\underline{\rho}(x):=\begin{cases}\rho_{\phi}(x)+\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{A}\rho_{\phi}(y)\,dy&\text{if }x\in B,\\
0&\text{if }x\in\overline{\Omega}\setminus B.\end{cases}

The second term in the first condition is well-defined since
\left|B\right|>0
and it is also strictly positive since
\rho_{\phi}>0
on
A
and
\left|A\right|>0
.
The first term is also positive since
\rho_{\phi}>0
on
B
. It is clear
\underline{\rho}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\Omega)\cap L^{m}(\Omega)
. The optimality condition provides a contradiction since

0\leq\int_{\Omega}F(\underline{\rho}-\rho_{\phi})\,dx=\int_{B}F\,dx\Big(\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{A}\rho_{\phi}\,dy\Big)-\int_{A}F\rho_{\phi}\,dx<\int_{A}(-\ell)\rho_{\phi}\,dy-\int_{A}F\rho_{\phi}\,dx\leq 0.

We conclude that
F
must be constant on the
\left\{\rho_{\phi}>0\right\}
and from now on we denote by
-\ell
this constant, that is
(see (
2.4
))

f^{\prime}(\rho_{\phi})-\phi=-\ell\quad\rho_{\phi}\text{-a.e.}

∎

We now verify that this unique minimizer
\rho_{\phi}
is actually a weak solution of the elliptic equation (
1.5
). This is a consequence of Proposition
2.1
-(ii).

Proof of Proposition
2.1
(iii).

First, we claim that

(3.4)

f^{\prime}(\rho_{\phi})=(\phi-\ell)_{+}.

Indeed, (
2.4
) implies that
f^{\prime}(\rho_{\phi})=\phi-\ell=(\phi-\ell)_{+}
in
\{\rho_{\phi}>0\}
(the last equality follows from the fact that
f^{\prime}(\rho_{\phi})\geq 0
due to
(F1)
). Since
f^{\prime}(0)=0
, we only need to check that
(\phi-\ell)_{+}=0
a.e. in
\{\rho_{\phi}=0\}
in order to get (
3.4
).
Assume, by contradiction, that
A=\{\phi>\ell\}\cap\{\rho_{\phi}=0\}
has positive Lebesgue measure.
We have
F(x)=f^{\prime}(\rho_{\phi}(x))-\phi(x)<-\ell
in
A
and so
using the optimality condition (
3.3
) with
\rho:=\frac{1}{\left|A\right|}\chi_{A}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\Omega)\cap L^{m}(\Omega)
, we get:

0\leq\int_{\Omega}F(x)(\rho(x)-\rho_{\phi}(x))\,dx<-\ell-\int_{\Omega}F(x)\rho_{\phi}(x)\,dx,

which is a contraction since
\int_{\Omega}F(x)\rho_{\phi}(x)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}(-\ell)\rho_{\phi}(x)\,dx=-\ell
.
We thus have (
3.4
).

Since
\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, this equality (
3.4
) implies in particular that
f^{\prime}(\rho_{\phi})
is in
H^{1}(\Omega)
and that

\nabla f^{\prime}(\rho_{\phi})=\chi_{\left\{\phi>\ell\right\}}\nabla\phi
. We deduce

\rho_{\phi}\nabla f^{\prime}(\rho_{\phi})=\rho_{\phi}\chi_{\left\{\phi>\ell\right\}}\nabla\phi=\rho_{\phi}\nabla\phi

since
\rho_{\phi}>0
implies
f^{\prime}(\rho_{\phi})>0
which gives (by (
3.4
))
\phi>\ell
.
We thus have

\rho_{\phi}\nabla(f^{\prime}(\rho_{\phi})-\phi)=0\quad\text{ a.e.\ in }\Omega,

which implies (
2.5
).
∎

Note that
f^{\prime}\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is invertible (since
f
is strictly convex
(F1)
) and so
(
3.4
) gives

(3.5)

\rho_{\phi}=(f^{\prime})^{-1}((\phi-\ell)_{+}),

where the Lagrange multiplier
\ell\in\mathbb{R}
is selected to enforce the mass constraint
\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\phi}\,dx=1
. Moreover, when
f
satisfies
(F2)
,
(f^{\prime})^{-1}
is Lipschitz (with Lipschitz constant
\alpha
) so we can expect
the mapping
\phi\mapsto\rho_{\phi}
to be Lipschitz (a fact that we will need to prove the
uniqueness of the solution to (

P
ε

)).
However such a property does not follow immediately from (
3.5
) since
\ell
depends on
\phi
as well. Nevertheless we can show:

Proposition 3.2

(The map
\phi\mapsto\rho_{\phi}
)
.

Suppose that
f
satisfies
(F2)
, (i.e.
f
is
\alpha
-convex).
Then the mapping
\phi\mapsto\rho_{\phi}
is Lipschitz
L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
.
More precisely, we have

(3.6)

\left\|\rho_{\phi_{2}}-\rho_{\phi_{1}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{2}{\alpha}\left\|\phi_{2}-\phi_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Since
f
is
\alpha
-uniformly convex, we have

f(u_{2})-f(u_{1})-f^{\prime}(u_{1})(u_{2}-u_{1})\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}\left|u_{2}-u_{1}\right|^{2}\qquad\forall\,u_{2},u_{1}\in[0,\infty).

Combining the definition of
\mathscr{K}
(defined with
\phi=\phi_{1}
) with the above inequality gives

\displaystyle\mathscr{K}(\rho_{\phi_{2}},\phi_{1})-\mathscr{K}(\rho_{\phi_{1}},\phi_{1})

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}f(\rho_{\phi_{2}})-f(\rho_{\phi_{1}})\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\phi_{2}}\phi_{1}-\rho_{\phi_{1}}\phi_{1}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}(f^{\prime}(\rho_{\phi_{1}})-\phi_{1})(\rho_{\phi_{2}}-\rho_{\phi_{1}})\,dx+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left|\rho_{\phi_{2}}-\rho_{\phi_{1}}\right|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}\left\|\rho_{\phi_{2}}-\rho_{\phi_{1}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

where the last line follows from the optimality condition (
3.3
) applied to
\rho_{\phi_{1}}
.

We rearrange this estimate and deduce

\displaystyle\mathscr{K}(\rho_{\phi_{1}},\phi_{1})+\frac{\alpha}{2}\left\|\rho_{\phi_{2}}-\rho_{\phi_{1}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\mathscr{K}(\rho_{\phi_{2}},\phi_{1})=\mathscr{K}(\rho_{\phi_{2}},\phi_{2})+\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\phi_{2}}(\phi_{2}-\phi_{1})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{K}(\rho_{\phi_{1}},\phi_{2})+\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\phi_{2}}(\phi_{2}-\phi_{1})\,dx=\mathscr{K}(\rho_{\phi_{1}},\phi_{1})+\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\phi_{1}}(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2})+\rho_{\phi_{2}}(\phi_{2}-\phi_{1})\,dx,

where the second line follows from
\rho_{\phi_{2}}
minimizing
\mathscr{K}
when
\phi=\phi_{2}
. We simplify and use Cauchy-Schwarz to obtain

\frac{\alpha}{2}\left\|\rho_{\phi_{2}}-\rho_{\phi_{1}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\int_{\Omega}(\rho_{\phi_{2}}-\rho_{\phi_{1}})(\phi_{2}-\phi_{1})\,dx\leq\left\|\rho_{\phi_{2}}-\rho_{\phi_{1}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\phi_{2}-\phi_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

which implies the desired estimate.
∎

4.
Well-posedness of (
1.4
)

The section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
2.2
. The existence will be proven under only the assumptions
(F1)
and
(F2)
using a discrete time scheme based on the natural energy structure of the system – the same energy structure that is essential to the limit
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. The uniqueness will follow under the additional hypothesis
(F2)
from the Lipschitz property of the map
\rho\mapsto\rho_{\phi}
(Proposition
3.2
). We note that this same Lipschitz property could also be used to prove the existence of a solution via a contraction mapping argument. However, the more constructive approach we follow has the advantage of
giving us the energy dissipation inequality – which plays a crucial role in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
– in a straightforward manner.

Throughout this section,
f
satisfies
(F1)
and
(F2)
with
m>2
.

4.1.
A minimizing movements scheme

We fix a time horizon
T>0
, a small time step
\tau\in(0,T)
(destined to go to
0
) and we denote
N_{\tau}:=\lceil T/\tau\rceil\in\mathbb{N}
.
We set
(\rho^{0}_{\tau},\phi^{0}_{\tau}):=(\rho_{\phi_{\mathrm{in}}},\phi_{\mathrm{in}})
and then solve iteratively the following minimization problems:

(4.1)

(\rho^{n}_{\tau},\phi^{n}_{\tau})\in\argmin_{(\rho,\phi)\in L^{m}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)}\left\{\varepsilon\frac{\left\|\phi-\phi^{n-1}_{\tau}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}{2\tau}+\mathscr{G}(\rho,\phi)\right\}\qquad\text{for }n=1,2,\dots,N_{\tau},

where

(4.2)

\mathscr{G}(\rho,\phi):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}f(\rho)-\rho\phi+\frac{\sigma}{2}|\phi|^{2}\,dx+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx&\text{if }(\rho,\phi)\in(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\Omega)\cap L^{m}(\Omega))\times H^{1}(\Omega),\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise in }L^{m}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega).\end{cases}

We first need to check that this iterative scheme is well-defined, which requires the lower semicontinuity and coercivity of the functional. More precisely, we can show:

Lemma 4.1
.

Given
\xi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, the functional

(4.3)

L^{m}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)\ni(\rho,\phi)\mapsto\varepsilon\frac{\|\phi-\xi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}{2\tau}+\mathscr{G}(\rho,\phi)\in\mathbb{R}\cup\left\{+\infty\right\}

is sequentially lower semicontinuous for the weak convergence in
L^{m}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
and we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}(\rho,\phi)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c}{\varepsilon}\left[\int_{\Omega}\rho^{m}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|\phi|^{2}\,dx+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx\right]-\frac{C}{\varepsilon}

for some constants
c>0
and
C>0
that depend only on
\Omega
,
f
, and
\sigma
.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Given any sequence
\left\{(\rho_{n},\phi_{n})\right\}_{n}
such that
\rho_{n}\rightharpoonup\rho
weakly in
L^{m}(\Omega)
and
\phi_{n}\rightharpoonup\phi
weakly in
H^{1}(\Omega)
(and thus strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega)
), it is easy to check that these convergences and the lower semicontinuity of
\mathscr{K}
give the lower semicontinuity of
\mathscr{G}
:

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathscr{G}(\rho_{n},\phi_{n})\geq\mathscr{G}(\rho,\phi),

which is the first part of the lemma.

Next, we write, using (
3.2
):

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}(\rho,\phi)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C_{1}}{2\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{m}\,dx-\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}\phi^{\frac{m}{m-1}}\,dx-\frac{C}{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\sigma}{2}|\phi|^{2}\,dx+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx

(4.5)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C_{1}}{2\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{m}\,dx+\frac{\sigma}{4\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}|\phi|^{2}\,dx+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\sigma}{4}|\phi|^{2}-C\phi^{\frac{m}{m-1}}\,dx-\frac{C}{\varepsilon}.

Since
\frac{m}{m-1}<2
, the function
u\mapsto\frac{\sigma}{4}u^{2}-Cu^{\frac{m}{m-1}}
is bounded below and so (
4.4
) follows.
∎

Proposition 4.2

(existence of minimizers)
.

We have:

(i)

For any
\xi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\tau\in(0,T)
, there exists a minimizer
(\underline{\rho},\underline{\phi})\in(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\Omega)\cap L^{m}(\Omega))\times H^{1}(\Omega)
of the energy (
4.3
).

(ii)

Any minimizer is of the form
(\rho_{\underline{\phi}},\underline{\phi})
, where
\rho_{\underline{\phi}}
is the minimizer of (
1.6
) with
\phi=\underline{\phi}
.

Note that when
f
satisfies
(F2)
,
one can show that
\mathscr{G}
is strictly convex (at least for small enough
\tau
) and that the minimizer is unique. Such a fact is however not necessary for our analysis.
In the sequel, we arbitrarily pick one solution of (
4.1
) for each
n
.

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

Lemma
4.1
implies that the functional (
4.3
) is bounded below
and that any minimizing sequence is bounded in
L^{m}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
and hence converges weakly (up to a subsequence) to a minimizer. Since the limit is still in
(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\Omega)\cap L^{m}(\Omega))\times H^{1}(\Omega)
, the existence of a minimizer follows.

Furthermore, given any minimizer
(\underline{\rho},\underline{\phi})
of (
4.3
), we have (using
(\rho_{\underline{\phi}},\underline{\phi})
as a competitor):

\varepsilon\frac{\|\underline{\phi}-\xi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}{2\tau}+\mathscr{G}(\underline{\rho},\underline{\phi})\leq\varepsilon\frac{\|\underline{\phi}-\xi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}{2\tau}+\mathscr{G}(\rho_{\underline{\phi}},\underline{\phi})

which, after simplifying the terms that are identical on both sides, reduces to

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}f(\underline{\rho})-{\underline{\rho}}\,{\underline{\phi}}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}f(\rho_{\underline{\phi}})-\rho_{\underline{\phi}}\underline{\phi}.

Proposition
2.1
(i) implies
\underline{\rho}=\rho_{\underline{\phi}}
.
∎

In view of the previous proposition, with
\tau\in(0,T)
fixed, we can use (
4.1
) to define a sequence
\left\{(\rho^{n}_{\tau},\phi^{n}_{\tau})\right\}_{n}\subset(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\Omega)\cap L^{m}(\Omega))\times H^{1}(\Omega)
.
A straightforward perturbation argument of
\mathscr{G}
along the
\phi
variable with the
\rho
variable fixed gives the following Euler-Lagrange equation:

Lemma 4.3

(optimality conditions of (
4.1
))
.

For all
\tau\in(0,T)
and any step
n\in\left\{1,\dots,N_{\tau}\right\}
of (
4.1
),
we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle 0=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varepsilon}{\tau}(\phi^{n}_{\tau}-\phi^{n-1}_{\tau})\psi+\varepsilon\nabla\phi^{n}_{\tau}\cdot\nabla\psi+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(\sigma\phi^{n}_{\tau}-\rho^{n}_{\tau})\psi\,dx\qquad\forall\,\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega).

Next, we derive the classical estimates for discrete gradient flows.

Lemma 4.4
.

For any step size
\tau\in(0,T)
and
n\in\left\{1,\dots,N_{\tau}\right\}
we have:

(i)

(nonincreasing energy):
\mathscr{G}(\rho^{n}_{\tau},\phi^{n}_{\tau})\leq\mathscr{G}(\rho^{n-1}_{\tau},\phi^{n-1}_{\tau})\leq\mathscr{G}(\rho_{\phi_{\mathrm{in}}},\phi_{\mathrm{in}})
;

(ii)

(discrete dissipation estimate):
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\varepsilon}{2\tau}\left\|\phi^{j}_{\tau}-\phi^{j-1}_{\tau}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\mathscr{G}(\rho_{\phi_{\mathrm{in}}},\phi_{\mathrm{in}})-\mathscr{G}(\rho_{\tau}^{n},\phi_{\tau}^{n}).

Proof.

We use
(\rho^{n-1}_{\tau},\phi^{n-1}_{\tau})
as a competitor in the minimization problem (
4.1
) at step
n
to get:

(4.7)

\varepsilon\frac{\left\|\phi^{n}_{\tau}-\phi^{n-1}_{\tau}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}{2\tau}+\mathscr{G}(\rho^{n}_{\tau},\phi^{n}_{\tau})\leq\mathscr{G}(\rho^{n-1}_{\tau},\phi^{n-1}_{\tau}).

This implies (i) and also gives (after summation)

(4.8)

\mathscr{G}(\rho^{n}_{\tau},\phi^{n}_{\tau})+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\varepsilon\left\|\phi^{j}_{\tau}-\phi^{j-1}_{\tau}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}{2\tau}\leq\mathscr{G}(\rho_{\phi_{\mathrm{in}}},\phi_{\mathrm{in}})

which is (ii).

∎

We now define the usual piecewise-constant and piecewise-affine interpolations:
For convenience, we denote
t^{n}_{\tau}:=n\tau

and define

(1)

The
piecewise-constant interpolant
:

(4.9)

\overline{\phi}_{\tau}(t,x):=\begin{cases}\phi_{\tau}^{n}(x)&\text{if }t\in(t^{n-1}_{\tau},t^{n}_{\tau}]\text{ for some }n\in\left\{1,\dots,N_{\tau}\right\},\\
\phi_{\mathrm{in}}(x)&\text{if }t=0.\end{cases}

The interpolant
\overline{\rho}_{\tau}\colon[0,N_{\tau}\tau]\to\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\Omega)\cap L^{m}(\Omega)
is defined analogously.

(2)

The
piecewise-affine interpolant
:

(4.10)

\widehat{\phi}_{\tau}(t,x):=\begin{cases}(1-\frac{t-t_{\tau}^{n-1}}{\tau})\phi_{\tau}^{n-1}(x)+\frac{t-t_{\tau}^{n-1}}{\tau}\phi_{\tau}^{n}(x)&\text{if }t\in(t_{\tau}^{n-1},t_{\tau}^{n}]\text{ for some }n\in\left\{1,\dots,N_{\tau}\right\},\\
\phi_{\mathrm{in}}(x)&\text{if }t=0.\end{cases}

With these notations, we have:

Proposition 4.5

(semidiscretization in time of (
1.4
))
.

Suppose
\phi_{\mathrm{in}}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. Then for any
\psi\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))

(4.11)

\begin{gathered}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon\partial_{t}\widehat{\phi}_{\tau}\psi+\varepsilon\nabla\overline{\phi}_{\tau}\cdot\nabla\psi+\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}\overline{\phi}_{\tau}\psi-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\overline{\rho}_{\tau}\psi\,dx\,dt=0.\end{gathered}

Proof.

We use (
4.6
) to write for all
t\in(t_{\tau}^{n-1},t_{\tau}^{n})
:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon\partial_{t}\widehat{\phi}_{\tau}(t)\psi(t)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon\frac{\phi_{\tau}^{n}-\phi_{\tau}^{n-1}}{\tau}\psi(t)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}-\varepsilon\nabla\phi_{\tau}^{n}\cdot\nabla\psi(t)-\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}\phi_{\tau}^{n}\psi(t)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\rho^{n}_{\tau}\psi(t)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}-\varepsilon\nabla\overline{\phi}_{\tau}(t)\cdot\nabla\psi(t)-\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}\overline{\phi}_{\tau}(t)\psi(t)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\overline{\rho}_{\tau}(t)\psi(t)\,dx.

The result follows by integrating in time and summing over
n
.

∎

4.2.
Uniform-in-
\tau
estimates

All the estimates derived in this section are independent of
\tau
but depend on
\varepsilon
and other constants of the problem even though this dependence is not explicitly written.

The estimates of Lemma
4.4
imply:

Lemma 4.6

(time regularity of chemoattractant interpolants)
.

There exist a constant
C
independent of
\tau

such that for any
\tau\in(0,T)
and
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
,

(i)

\left\|\partial_{t}\widehat{\phi}_{\tau}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}<C
and
\left\|\widehat{\phi}_{\tau}(t)-\widehat{\phi}_{\tau}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\sqrt{t-s}
;

(ii)

\left\|\overline{\phi}_{\tau}(t)-\widehat{\phi}_{\tau}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\sqrt{\tau}
.

Note that the piecewise-constant interpolant
\overline{\phi}_{\tau}
is, of course, not continuous in time, but it satisfies an almost-Hölder estimate

\left\|\overline{\phi}_{\tau}(t)-\overline{\phi}_{\tau}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\sqrt{t-s+\tau}
with the same
C
as in the lemma.

Proof.

First we note that

\mathscr{G}(\rho_{\phi_{\mathrm{in}}},\phi_{\mathrm{in}})-\inf_{(\rho,\phi)\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)}\mathscr{G}(\rho,\phi)<\infty

and so the discrete energy dissipation inequality (Lemma
4.4
(ii)) gives

\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\partial_{t}\widehat{\phi}_{\tau}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,dt=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\tau}}\int_{t^{j-1}_{\tau}}^{t_{\tau}^{j}\wedge T}\left\|\frac{\phi_{\tau}^{j}-\phi_{\tau}^{j-1}}{\tau}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,dt\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\tau}}\frac{1}{\tau}\left\|\phi_{\tau}^{j}-\phi_{\tau}^{j-1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C.

which implies (i).

To prove (ii), we note that the piecewise-constant and piecewise-affine interpolants agree at times on the mesh, and that the mesh has size
\tau
: If
t\in(t_{\tau}^{n-1},t_{\tau}^{n}]
we have
\overline{\phi}_{\tau}(t)=\phi_{\tau}^{n}=\widehat{\phi}_{\tau}(t_{\tau}^{n})
and so

\left\|\overline{\phi}_{\tau}(t)-\widehat{\phi}_{\tau}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\left\|\widehat{\phi}_{\tau}(t_{\tau}^{n})-\widehat{\phi}_{\tau}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\sqrt{t_{\tau}^{n}-t}\leq C\sqrt{\tau}.

∎

Next, the boundedness of the energy (Lemma
4.4
(i)) together with the coercivity inequality (
4.4
) give the following estimates:

Lemma 4.7
.

There exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
\tau\in(0,T)
, such that

(i)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\widehat{\phi}_{\tau}(t)\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C
;

(ii)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\overline{\rho}_{\tau}(t)\right\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}\leq C
;

Proof.

Inequality (
4.4
) and Lemma
4.4
-(i) and imply

(4.12)

\left\|\overline{\rho}_{\tau}(t)\right\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}^{m}+\left\|\overline{\phi}_{\tau}(t)\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\mathscr{G}(\overline{\rho}_{\tau}(t),\overline{\phi}_{\tau}(t))+C\leq C\mathscr{G}(\rho_{\phi_{\mathrm{in}}},\phi_{\mathrm{in}})+C,

and the result (ii) follows. For (i), we note that
\widehat{\phi}_{\tau}(t)
is a convex combination of
\overline{\phi}_{\tau}(t-\tau)
and
\overline{\phi}_{\tau}(t)
and use the inequality above.
∎

4.3.
Passage to the limit
\tau\to 0^{+}
in the semidiscretization

We now use these estimates to pass to the limit in (
4.11
) along some subsequence.
Note that we will not pass to the limit in the relation
\overline{\rho}_{\tau}=\rho_{\overline{\phi}_{\tau}}
but instead show directly that the weak limit of
\left(\overline{\rho}_{\tau}\right)_{\tau}
minimizes

at each fixed

, so it is therefore equal to
\rho_{\phi(t)}
by using the uniqueness of minimizers of the minimization problem (

).

Proposition 4.8

(convergence of the interpolants)
.

Let
\left\{\tau_{k}\right\}_{k}
be a positive sequence converging to
0
. There exists a subsequence of
\left\{\tau_{k}\right\}_{k}
(not relabeled) and nonnegative functions

\displaystyle\phi\in H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))\quad\text{and}\quad\rho\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{m}(\Omega))

such that:

(i)

\partial_{t}\widehat{\phi}_{\tau_{k}}\rightharpoonup\partial_{t}\phi
weakly in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
;

(ii)

\nabla\overline{\phi}_{\tau_{k}}\rightharpoonup\nabla\phi
weakly-
\ast
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)^{d})
;

(iii)

\overline{\phi}_{\tau_{k}}\to\phi
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
and
\widehat{\phi}_{\tau_{k}}\to\phi
in
C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
;

(iv)

\overline{\rho}_{\tau_{k}}\rightharpoonup\rho
weakly-
\ast
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{m}(\Omega))
;

Proof.

Lemma
4.7
immediately gives the convergences (ii) and (iv), while (i) is a consequence of
Lemma
4.6
-(i).

Assertion (iii) follows from Arzelà-Ascoli. Indeed, Lemma
4.6
-(i) shows the family
\left(\widehat{\phi}_{\tau_{k}}\right)_{k}
is uniformly bounded in
C^{0,1/2}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))

and Lemma

-(i) shows that
\left(\widehat{\phi}_{\tau_{k}}(t)\right)_{k}
is bounded in

(and thus precompact in

) for each

.
Arzelà-Ascoli provides strong convergence of a subsequence of
\left(\widehat{\phi}_{\tau_{k}}\right)_{k}
in
C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
. Along this subsequence, we also have
\overline{\phi}_{\tau_{k}}\to\phi
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
as a consequence of Lemma
4.6
-(ii).

∎

When

Proof.

5.
\Gamma

From here through the remainder of the paper, we assume that

defined by (

\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon}(\phi)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left\{\int_{\Omega}W(\rho_{\phi})+\frac{1}{2\sigma}(\rho_{\phi}-\sigma\phi)^{2}\,dx\right\}+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx\qquad\forall\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega).

We recall,
\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon}
differs from

only by a constant.
With the double-well potential

defined by (

), we have (
2.14
); that is,

\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon}(\phi)\geq\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(\phi)\qquad\forall\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega),

where

\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(\phi):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi)\,dx+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx\geq 0.

This might seem like a very crude bound since replacing

\inf_{\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\Omega)\cap L^{m}(\Omega)}\int W(\rho)+\frac{1}{2\sigma}(\rho-\sigma\phi)^{2}\,dx

completely ignores the constraint

.
However,
the main theorem of this section, namely the

-convergence of

to

, shows that

\Gamma
-limit.
A key aspect of this proof is the fact that the functional

is a classical Modica-Mortola functional. Indeed, we have
(see
[

, Lemma 3.1]
):

Lemma 5.1

(double-well potential)
.

The function

defined by (

) satisfies

(5.4)

W_{\sigma}(v)\geq 0,\quad\forall v\in\mathbb{R};\qquad W_{\sigma}^{-1}(0)=\{0,\theta\};

and

(5.5)

W_{\sigma}(v)\leq\frac{1}{2\sigma}\min\left\{v^{2},(v-\theta)^{2}\right\}.

Furthermore,
(F2)
implies there exists
\nu>0
such that

W_{\sigma}(v)\geq\nu v^{2}\quad\mbox{ for $v$ large enough}.

The
\Gamma
-convergence of
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}
w.r.t. strong

-convergence toward the perimeter functional is thus a classical result (see
[
36
,
35
,

]
) and we will use this below to prove Theorem

.

5.1.
Proof of the
\bm{\liminf}
property (Theorem
2.3
-(i))

The proof of the
\liminf
property (Theorem
2.3
-(i)) follows from (
5.2
) and the usual argument for Modica-Mortola’s
\Gamma
-convergence of
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}
, with a few adjustments to account for the indirect mass constraint on
\phi
.

We now consider a positive sequence
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
and a sequence
\left(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)_{n}
that converges to
\phi
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
.
If
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})=+\infty
, then the result is obviously true, so we assume that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})<+\infty
and consider a (not relabeled) subsequence
\left(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)_{n}
that achieves the limit inferior,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})
, and has bounded energy,
\sup_{n}\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\leq C<+\infty
.

First, we note that (
5.1
) implies

(5.7)

\left|\int_{\Omega}\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon}\,dx-1\right|\leq\int_{\Omega}|\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon}-\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon}}|\,dx\leq|\Omega|^{1/2}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon}-\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon}(\phi^{\varepsilon})^{1/2}.

Hence, the limit
\phi
satisfies
\int_{\Omega}\phi(x)\,dx=1/\sigma
.

From the strong
L^{1}
-convergence, we can further assume (up to another not relabeled subsequence) that
\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\to\phi
a.e. in
\Omega
.
On the one hand,
\int_{\Omega}W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\,dx\leq\varepsilon_{n}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\leq\varepsilon_{n}\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\leq C\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
. By nonnegativity of
W_{\sigma}
, we see
W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\to 0
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. On the other hand, continuity of
W_{\sigma}
gives
W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\to W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi)
a.e., so combining this with Fatou’s lemma and the previous convergence shows
\int_{\Omega}W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi)\,dx=0
. Nonnegativity of
W_{\sigma}
then gives
W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi)=0
a.e., so we deduce
that
\phi=\frac{\theta}{\sigma}\chi_{E}
for some set
E\subset\Omega
.

It remains to show that
\phi
is of bounded variation. Towards this end, we introduce the auxiliary function
F_{\sigma}
that satisfies

(5.8)

F_{\sigma}^{\prime}(v)=\frac{1}{\sigma}\sqrt{2W_{\sigma}(v)}\,,\quad v\in(0,\theta),\quad F_{\sigma}(0)=0,

which we extend from
[0,\theta)
to
\mathbb{R}_{+}
by a constant by setting
F_{\sigma}(v):=\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{0}^{\theta}\sqrt{2W_{\sigma}(s)}\,ds=\frac{\gamma}{\sigma}\theta
for
v\geq\theta
. (The definition of
\gamma
, (
2.15
), implies
\gamma=\frac{\sigma}{\theta}F_{\sigma}(\theta)
).
We point out that (
5.5
) implies

(5.9)

F_{\sigma}^{\prime}(v)\leq\frac{1}{\sigma^{3/2}}(\min\{v,\theta-v\})_{+}\leq\frac{\theta}{2\sigma^{3/2}}\qquad\forall v\geq 0,

so
F_{\sigma}\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a bounded Lipschitz function,
which implies
\left(F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\right)_{n}
converges strongly in
L^{1}(\Omega)
to
F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi)=F_{\sigma}(\theta\chi_{E})=F_{\sigma}(\theta)\chi_{E}=\gamma\frac{\theta}{\sigma}\chi_{E}=\gamma\phi
.

We have (using (
5.8
), Young’s inequality, and (
5.2
))

(5.10)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})|\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{2W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})}|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\,dx+\varepsilon_{n}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|^{2}\,dx=\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\leq\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}).

The lower semicontinuity of the
BV
-seminorm gives

C\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})|\geq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(\gamma\phi)|=\mathscr{J}_{0}(\phi)

and concludes the proof of the
\liminf
property.

5.2.
Proof of the
\bm{\limsup}
property (Theorem
2.3
-(ii))

The proof uses similar arguments as the proof of the corresponding result in
[
33
, Section 3]
. We provide the details here for the reader’s convenience.
The following equality, which is easily derived from (

) plays an important role in the proof:

(5.11)

W_{\sigma}(\sigma v)=\frac{\sigma}{2}v^{2}-f^{*}(v-a),

where

denotes the Legendre transform of

, defined by

f^{*}(v):=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}}\left(uv-f(u)\right)

(with the understanding that

if

In particular, when

is given by (

In general, our assumptions on

imply that

is a convex function in

satisfying
{f^{*}}^{\prime}(v)=0
for

and

for all

(see
[
33
, Section 3]
for details).
In addition, we have the following important property:

(5.12)

W_{\sigma}(\sigma v)=W(u)+\frac{1}{2\sigma}(u-\sigma v)\Longleftrightarrow u={f^{*}}^{\prime}(v-a)

which follows from the fact that
f^{*}(v)=uv-f(u)
iff
u={f^{*}}^{\prime}(v)
.

If
\mathscr{J}_{0}(\phi)=+\infty
, the construction of the recovery sequence is trivial, so we can assume that

\phi\in BV(\Omega;\frac{\theta}{\sigma}\})

and
\int_{\Omega}\phi\,dx=\frac{1}{\sigma}
.
The first step is to use the well-known
\Gamma
-convergence of the Modica-Mortola functional (

) to
\mathscr{J}_{0}

(see
[
41
,
13
,
27
]
), which implies the existence
of a sequence
\left\{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\right\}_{n}\subset H^{1}(\Omega)
such that
\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\to\phi
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
and

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\leq\mathscr{J}_{0}(\phi).

In the usual construction of this recovery sequence,
(see for instance
[
27
]
),
one actually constructs a family of sequences
\left(\left\{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{t}\right\}_{n}\right)_{t}
with
t\in[0,1]
satisfying the conditions above; that is:

(5.13)

\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{t}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\phi\mbox{ in }L^{1}(\Omega),\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{t})\leq\mathscr{J}_{0}(\phi)\qquad\forall t\in[0,1]

with
\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{0}\leq\phi
,
\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{1}\geq\phi
and
t\mapsto\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{t}
continuous in
L^{1}(\Omega)
.
The classical recovery sequence is then obtained by choosing
t_{\varepsilon_{n}}
so that
\int_{\Omega}\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{t_{\varepsilon_{n}}}\,dx=1/\sigma
.
For our proof, we will make a slightly different choice of
t_{\varepsilon_{n}}
:
We claim that one can choose
t_{\varepsilon_{n}}\in[0,1]
so that

(5.14)

\int_{\Omega}\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{t_{\varepsilon_{n}}}-W_{\sigma}^{\prime}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{t_{\varepsilon_{n}}})\,dx=1/\sigma.

Indeed, the function
v\mapsto v-W_{\sigma}^{\prime}(\sigma v)
is nondecreasing
(since (
5.11
) implies that it is the derivative of the convex function
v\mapsto\frac{1}{\sigma}f^{*}(v-a)
)
and so

\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{0}-W_{\sigma}^{\prime}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{0})\leq\phi-W_{\sigma}^{\prime}(\sigma\phi)=\phi,\qquad\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{1}-W_{\sigma}^{\prime}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{1})\geq\phi-W_{\sigma}^{\prime}(\sigma\phi)=\phi

(here we used the fact that
W_{\sigma}
is
C^{1}
and has minimums at

and
\theta
together with the fact that
\sigma\phi
only takes value

and
\theta
).
A continuity argument implies that there exists
t(\varepsilon_{n})\in[0,1]
such that (
5.14
) holds.
We then denote
\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}:=\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{t(\varepsilon_{n})}
and

\rho^{\varepsilon_{n}}(x):=\sigma(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(x)-W_{\sigma}^{\prime}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(x)))={f^{*}}^{\prime}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}-a).

The fact that
{f^{*}}^{\prime}(v)\geq 0

and (
5.14
) give

and (
5.12
) implies
W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})=W(\rho^{\varepsilon_{n}})+\frac{1}{2\sigma}(\rho^{\varepsilon_{n}}-\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})
and so the definition of
\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}
(see (

)) gives

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\,dx+{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}W(\rho^{\varepsilon_{n}})+\frac{1}{2\sigma}(\rho^{\varepsilon_{n}}-\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})^{2}\,dx+{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}).

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\leq\mathscr{J}_{0}(\rho),

which gives the
\limsup

in
L^{1}(\Omega)
.

6.
Convergence of the density
\rho^{\varepsilon}
and chemoattractant
\phi^{\varepsilon}

We now turn our attention to Theorem

\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}
and
\rho^{\varepsilon_{n}}
to (multiples of) the characteristic function of a set of finite perimeter.
We recall that
\phi^{\varepsilon}
, the solution of (

), satisfies the energy dissipation inequality

(6.1)

\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon}(\phi^{\varepsilon}(t))+\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds\leq\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon}(\phi_{\mathrm{in}}^{\varepsilon}),\qquad\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon}(t):=\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon|\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{2}\,dx,

so well-preparedness of the initial data, i.e.,
\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})}\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon}(\phi_{\mathrm{in}}^{\varepsilon})\leq C<+\infty
, implies that both the energy and the dissipation are uniformly bounded in

for arbitrarily small

(6.2)

\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\{\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon}(\phi^{\varepsilon}(t))+\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds\right\}\leq C.

There are two essential consequences of this. First, the definition of
\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon}
(
5.1
) implies

(6.3)

\int_{\Omega}|\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon}(t)-\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon}(t)}|\,dx\leq|\Omega|^{1/2}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon}(t)-\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon}(t)}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon}(\phi^{\varepsilon}(t))^{1/2}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2},

so
\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon}
and
\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon}}
have the same limit as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.
In addition, (
5.2
)-(
5.3
) gives

(6.4)

\int_{\Omega}W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon}(t))\,dx\leq\varepsilon\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(\phi^{\varepsilon}(t))\leq\varepsilon\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon}(\phi^{\varepsilon}(t))\leq C\varepsilon,

so the range of any sufficiently strong limit
\sigma\phi
of
\left(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon}
must be contained in the wells of
W_{\sigma}
. That is,
\sigma\phi
ought to be (a multiple of) a characteristic function.

These estimates will be key to proving the following proposition that establishes Part (i) (phase separation) of Theorem
2.4
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}
be as in Theorem
2.4
and define the auxiliary function

\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t,x):=F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t,x)),

where
F_{\sigma}\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is defined by (
5.8
).
There exists
\phi\in C^{0,s}([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))\cap BV((0,T)\times\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;BV(\Omega))
for all
s\in[0,\frac{1}{2})

such that, up to a subsequence,

\displaystyle\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\gamma\phi\quad\text{ strongly in }C^{0,s}([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\phi,\qquad\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\sigma\phi\quad\mbox{ strongly in $L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{q}(\Omega))$},\quad\forall q\in[1,\frac{d}{d-1})

with
\gamma=\frac{\sigma}{\theta}F_{\sigma}(\theta)
. Furthermore, for all
t\in[0,T]
, there exists a set
E(t)\subset\Omega
such that
\phi(t)=\frac{\theta}{\sigma}\chi_{E(t)}
and

(6.5)

|E(t)|=\frac{1}{\theta},\,\qquad P(E(t),\Omega)<+\infty\qquad\forall t\in[0,T].

Above,
P(E(t),\Omega)
denotes the perimeter of the set
E(t)
in
\Omega
, defined by

P(E(t),\Omega):=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\chi_{E(t)}|.

Proof.

Lipschitz continuity of
F_{\sigma}
gives

\left|\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)\right|\leq C\left|\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)\right|\leq C\big(\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)}+\left|\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)-\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)}\right|\big).

Using that
\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)}(x)\,dx=1
for all
t\in[0,T]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
and the calculation (
6.3
), we deduce an
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))
-bound on
\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}
.

Next, inequality (
5.10
) gives

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)|\,dx\leq\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))\leq C.

so
\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;BV(\Omega))
.

Furthermore, using the definition of
\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}
, Cauchy-Schwarz, the uniform-in-
t
-and-
n
bound on the energy from the double-well potential (
6.4
), and the uniform-in-
n
bound on the dissipation (
6.2
):

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{t}\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|\,dx\right)^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{2W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})}|\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|\,dx\right)^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\frac{2}{\varepsilon_{n}}W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\,dx\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon_{n}|\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq 2C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon_{n}|\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq 2C^{2}.

Thus,
\partial_{t}\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}
is bounded in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))
.

Since
BV(\Omega)
is compactly embedded into
L^{q}(\Omega)
for all
q<\frac{d}{d-1}
,
a refined
Aubin-Lions lemma
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
thus implies that
\left(\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)_{n}
is relatively compact in
C^{0,s}([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))
for
0\leq s<\frac{1}{2}
and
L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{q}(\Omega))
for
1\leq q<\frac{d}{d-1}
.
Up to a subsequence, we can thus assume that
\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}
converges to
\phi
strongly in
C([0,T];L^{q}(\Omega))
. The lower semicontinuity of the
BV(\Omega)
-seminorm (resp.
BV((0,T)\times\Omega)
-seminorm) gives

\phi\in L^{\infty}(0,T;BV(\Omega))
(resp.
\phi\in BV((0,T)\times\Omega)
).

Next, we note that the function
\mathbb{R}_{+}\ni v\mapsto F_{\sigma}(v)-\frac{\gamma}{\sigma}v
vanishes when
v=0
and
v=\theta
(see the discussion after (
5.8
)), which are also the zeroes of the double-well potential
W_{\sigma}
.
So given a sufficiently small
\delta>0
and neighborhood
V_{\delta}:=[0,\delta)\cup(\theta-\frac{\delta}{2},\,\theta+\frac{\delta}{2})
,
the continuity of
F_{\sigma}
and
W_{\sigma}
implies

|F_{\sigma}(v)-\frac{\gamma}{\sigma}v|^{2}\leq C_{\delta}W_{\sigma}(v)\qquad\mbox{ for }v\notin V_{\delta}

for some constant
C_{\delta}
. More precisely, (
5.6
) and the fact that
F_{\sigma}
is bounded imply this inequality for large
v\notin V_{\delta}
, and then the continuity together with a compactness argument imply the inequality for small
v\notin V_{\delta}
.
Since
F_{\sigma}
is Lipschitz, we also have

|F_{\sigma}(v)-\frac{\gamma}{\sigma}v|\leq C\delta\qquad\mbox{ for }v\in V_{\delta}.

We deduce (using (
6.4
)):

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))-\gamma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)\in V_{\delta}\}}C\delta^{2}\,dx+C_{\delta}\int_{\{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)\notin V_{\delta}\}}W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C|\Omega|\delta^{2}+C_{\delta}\varepsilon_{n}\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))

\displaystyle\leq C|\Omega|\delta^{2}+C_{\delta}\varepsilon_{n}

hence

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})-\gamma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\|_{C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\delta.

Since the left-hand side is independent of
\delta
and this holds for all
\delta>0
, we must have

(6.6)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})-\gamma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\|_{C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))}=0.

It follows that the strong convergence of
\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}:=F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})
, implies the same strong convergence of
\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}
to
\phi
. Up to another subsequence, we can thus assume
\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\to\phi
for each
t\in[0,T]
a.e. in
\Omega
, and then continuity of
W_{\sigma}
gives
W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))\to W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi(t))
for each
t\in[0,T]
a.e. in
\Omega
, too.
Nonnegativity of
W_{\sigma}
, Fatou’s lemma, and (
6.4
) give for each
t\in[0,T]

\int_{\Omega}W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi(t))\,dx\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))\,dx\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\big[\varepsilon_{n}\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))\big]\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\big[C\varepsilon_{n}\big]=0.

Since
W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi(t))
is nonnegative, we deduce
W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi(t))=0
a.e. in
\Omega
, and so
\phi(t)=\frac{\theta}{\sigma}\chi_{E(t)}
for some set
E(t)\subset\Omega
. We already know that
\phi(t)\in BV(\Omega)
, which means that the set
E(t)
has finite perimeter.

Finally, (
6.3
) implies

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left\|\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}-\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\limsup_{n\to\infty}\big[\varepsilon_{n}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))\big]^{1/2}\leq C\limsup_{n\to\infty}\varepsilon_{n}^{1/2}=0.

Thus, the strong convergence of
\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}
implies that of
\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}}
.
∎

7.
The Velocity

The next two sections are devoted to the proof of Theorem
2.4
-(ii), which identifies the evolution equation satisfied by the limits obtained from the phase separation result, Theorem
2.4
-(i).
Thus, from now on, we assume that
\left(\varepsilon_{n}\right)_{n}\subset(0,\infty)
is a sequence converging to zero such that the conclusion of Proposition
6.1
and the assumption (
2.18
) of convergence of the energy hold.

Classically (see
[
25
, Lemma 2.11]
and
[
28
, Lemma 1]
), this energy convergence assumption implies that, in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, there is equality in both Young’s inequality
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\left|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\geq\left|\nabla F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon})\right|
(see (
7.2
)) and in the inequality
W(\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon}})+\frac{1}{2\sigma}(\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon}}-\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon})^{2}\geq W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon})
(see (
7.3
)). As a consequence, at a.e. fixed time, the contributions to the energy
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}
converge weakly-
\ast
as Radon measures on
\overline{\Omega}
to (an equal proportion of) the limiting contributor to the limiting energy
\mathscr{J}_{0}
(see (
7.1
)).
This important fact is made precise in the following lemma:

Lemma 7.1

(Equipartition of the energy)
.

Under assumption (
2.18
), and up to another subsequence, we have, for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
and any test function
\zeta\in C(\overline{\Omega})
,

(7.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{2}\int_{\Omega}\zeta(x)|\nabla\phi(t,x)|

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t,x))|\zeta(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{2}|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t,x)|^{2}\zeta(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t,x))\zeta(x)\,dx.

Furthermore, we have

(7.2)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big(\varepsilon_{n}^{1/2}|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|-\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}^{1/2}}\sqrt{2W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})}\Big)=0\quad\mbox{ strongly in }L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)

and

(7.3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}W(\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}})+\frac{1}{2\sigma\varepsilon_{n}}(\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}}-\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})^{2}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\Big)=0\quad\mbox{ strongly in }L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega).

Proof.

We first upgrade the convergence of the energy (
2.18
) to
L^{1}(0,T)
and a.e. convergence. We write:

\left|\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))-\mathscr{J}_{0}(\phi(t))\right|=2(\mathscr{J}_{0}(\phi(t))-\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)))_{+}+\big[\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))-\mathscr{J}_{0}(\phi(t))\big].

After an integration over
t\in(0,T)
, the second term on the right-hand side vanishes as
n\to\infty
by the energy convergence assumption (
2.18
), so we only need to address the first term. Since
\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\to\phi
strongly in
C([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))
, we have that
\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)\to\phi(t)
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
for each
t\in[0,T]
.
Then, the
\liminf
property of
\Gamma
-convergence (Theorem
2.3
-(i)) implies that

\lim_{n\to\infty}(\mathscr{J}_{0}(\phi(t))-\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)))_{+}=0
for each
t\in[0,T]
. Since
\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}
is nonnegative, we have for each
t
the upper bound
(\mathscr{J}_{0}(\phi(t))-\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)))_{+}\leq\mathscr{J}_{0}(\phi(t))
.
Thus, Lebesgue dominated convergence gives
(\mathscr{J}_{0}(\phi(\cdot))-\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(\cdot)))_{+}\to 0
in
L^{1}(0,T)
and we deduce

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\left|\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))-\mathscr{J}_{0}(\phi(t))\right|\,dt=0.

Up to another subsequence, we can also assume that

(7.4)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))=\mathscr{J}_{0}(\phi(t))\mbox{ for a.e.\ }t\in[0,T].

We now address the first limit in (
7.1
), which is a classical result associated to Modica-Mortola functionals, like
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}
. Recall for each
t\in[0,T]
that
F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t,\cdot))\to\gamma\phi(t,\cdot)
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
since
F_{\sigma}
is Lipschitz, (
5.9
). Thus, the lower semicontinuity of the
BV(\Omega)
-seminorm, (
5.10
) and (
7.4
) imply, for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
, that

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}_{0}(\phi(t))\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t,x))|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t,x))|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))=\mathscr{J}_{0}(\phi(t)).

We thus have equality in these inequalities, in particular:

(7.5)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t,x))|\,dx=\mathscr{J}_{0}(\phi(t)):=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(\gamma\phi(t,x))|\text{ for a.e.\ }t\in[0,T].

This convergence of the total variation
implies the first limit of (
7.1
) (see
[
3
, Proposition 3.15]
).

In fact, (
7.5
) and Lebesgue dominated convergence imply
L^{1}(0,T)
-convergence of the variations to
\mathscr{J}_{0}(\phi(\cdot))
. Since we have already shown
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(\cdot))=\mathscr{J}_{0}(\phi(\cdot))
for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
and in
L^{1}(0,T)
, we further see that the deviation
z^{\varepsilon}
between
\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon}(\phi^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))
and the optimal lower bound on the Modica-Mortola functional
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(\phi^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))
vanishes in the limit:

(7.6)

\begin{gathered}z^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t):=\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t,x))|\,dx\geq\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t,x))|\,dx\geq 0,\\
\lim_{n\to\infty}z^{\varepsilon_{n}}=0\qquad\mbox{ a.e.\ }t\in[0,T]\text{ and strongly in }L^{1}(0,T).\end{gathered}

Next, we denote

u_{n}:=\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{2}|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|^{2},\quad v_{n}:=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}),\quad w_{n}:=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}W(\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}})+\frac{1}{2\sigma\varepsilon_{n}}(\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}}-\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})^{2}.

In this notation, the energies take the form
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})=\int_{\Omega}v_{n}+u_{n}\,dx
and
\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})=\int_{\Omega}w_{n}+u_{n}\,dx
. Furthermore, we recall that the fact that
F_{\sigma}^{\prime}(s)=\frac{1}{\sigma}\sqrt{2W_{\sigma}(s)}
and
Young’s inequality imply

(7.7)

|\nabla F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})|=2\sqrt{u_{n}v_{n}}\leq u_{n}+v_{n}

and since
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}\leq\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon}
, we see

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{\Omega}u_{n}(t)+v_{n}(t)-|\nabla F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))|\,dx

\displaystyle=\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))|\,dx\leq z^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t).

So (
7.6
) implies

(7.8)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(u_{n}+v_{n}-|\nabla F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})|\big)=0\qquad\mbox{ strongly in }L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega),

which is asymptotic equality in Young’s inequality (
7.7
)

To be more precise, we can write

(7.9)

|\nabla F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})|=2\sqrt{u_{n}v_{n}}=u_{n}+v_{n}-(\sqrt{u_{n}}-\sqrt{v_{n}})^{2}=u_{n}+w_{n}-(\sqrt{u_{n}}-\sqrt{v_{n}})^{2}-|w_{n}-v_{n}|,

where the last equality follows by noticing that the definition of
W_{\sigma}
(
2.13
) implies
w_{n}-v_{n}\geq 0
pointwise.
Rearranging (
7.9
) gives

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{\Omega}\big(\sqrt{u_{n}(t)}-\sqrt{v_{n}(t)}\big)^{2}+\left|w_{n}(t)-v_{n}(t)\right|\,dx=\int_{\Omega}u_{n}(t)+w_{n}(t)-\left|\nabla F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))\right|\,dx=z^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t).

Thus,

(7.10)

\int_{\Omega}(\sqrt{u_{n}(t)}-\sqrt{v_{n}(t)})^{2}\,dx\leq z^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t),\qquad\quad\int_{\Omega}|w_{n}(t)-v_{n}(t)|\,dx\leq z^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t),

and so the combination of (
7.6
) with the first inequality in (
7.10
) gives (
7.2
) while (
7.6
) and the second inequality in (
7.10
) give (
7.3
).

It remains to show the last two limits in (
7.1
). Towards this end, the first inequality in (
7.10
) shows the two energy densities in
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}
are also equal as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. Indeed, by introducing a difference of squares

\displaystyle\left\|u_{n}(t)-v_{n}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\left\|\sqrt{u_{n}(t)}+\sqrt{v_{n}(t)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\sqrt{u_{n}(t)}-\sqrt{v_{n}(t)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\sqrt{\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))}\sqrt{z^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)},

and since
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))
is bounded uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we see (
7.6
) gives

\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(u_{n}-v_{n}\big)=0\qquad\text{strongly in }L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega).

By combining this with (
7.8
), we obtain

(7.11)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(u_{n}-\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\right|\big)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(v_{n}-\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\right|\big)=0\qquad\text{strongly in }L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega).

Thus, up to another subsequence, the above limits are for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
strongly in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. Combining this with the first limit in (
7.1
) provides the last two limits in (
7.1
).
∎

The existence of the normal velocity
V=V(t,x)
now follows from the following lemma. This establishes the first part of Theorem
2.4
-(ii).

Lemma 7.2

(Existence of the normal velocity
V
)
.

There exists a constant
C=C(\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{\mathrm{in}}))>0
such that, under the energy convergence assumption (
2.18
), for all test functions
\zeta\in C^{1}_{c}((0,T)\times\Omega)
, we have

\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\phi(t,x)\partial_{t}\zeta(t,x)\,dx\,dt\right|\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\zeta(t,x)^{2}|\nabla\phi|\,dt\right)^{1/2}.

In particular, there exists
V\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega,|\nabla\phi|\,dt)
such that
\partial_{t}\phi=V|\nabla\phi|\,dt
as Radon measures on
[0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}
and thus in the distributional sense of (
2.19
). Moreover, for any
\zeta\in C^{1}_{c}((0,T)\times\Omega)

(7.12)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\zeta\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\gamma\zeta V\left|\nabla\phi\right|\,dt.

Proof.

Recalling the definition
\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t,x):=F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t,x))
, we write (using the bound on the dissipation (
6.1
)):

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\partial_{t}\zeta\,dx\,dt\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{2W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})}\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\zeta\,dx\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}2W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\zeta^{2}\,dx\,dt\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon_{n}|\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|^{2}\,dx\,dt\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}2W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\zeta^{2}\,dx\,dt\right)^{1/2}.

Since
\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}
converges (strongly in
L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)
) to
\gamma\phi
,
and using the third equality in (
7.1
) (together with Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem), we deduce

(7.13)

\left|\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\partial_{t}\zeta\,dx\,dt\right|\leq C\left(\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{2}|\nabla\phi|\,dt\right)^{1/2}.

Thus, the measure
\partial_{t}\phi
is a bounded linear functional on
L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega,\left|\nabla\phi\right|dt)
and can be identified with a unique
V\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega,\left|\nabla\phi\right|dt)
satisfying (
2.19
).

To prove (
7.12
), we write:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\zeta\,dx\,dt=-\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\partial_{t}\zeta\,dx\,dt=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\gamma\phi\partial_{t}\zeta\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\gamma\zeta\partial_{t}\phi

and the result follows.
∎

).
Many aspects of this derivation are classical, but we recall that unlike the usual Allen-Cahn equation, the right-hand side of (

P
ε

) is nonlocal and the limiting equation preserves the volume.
The proof of Proposition
8.2
(existence of a Lagrange multiplier) is thus the most original part of this section.

In order to derive (
2.20
), we consider a vector field

\xi\in[C^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega})]^{d}
satisfying
\xi\cdot n=0
on
\partial\Omega
, and (at a fixed but suppressed time
t\in[0,T]
) we multiply the
\phi
equation (

P
ε

) by
\varepsilon\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}\cdot\xi
to get:

\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon}\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}\cdot\xi\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon\Delta\phi^{\varepsilon}\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}\cdot\xi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon}}-\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon})\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}\cdot\xi\,dx.

We note that the second term can be written (after a couple of integration by parts) as

-\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon\Delta\phi^{\varepsilon}\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}\cdot\xi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}\otimes\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}:D\xi\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\mathrm{div}\,\xi\,dx

and we rewrite the right-hand side as

\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon}}-\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon})\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}\cdot\xi\,dx=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}(\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon}}-\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon})\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}\cdot\xi-W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon})\mathrm{div}\,\xi\,dx+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon})\mathrm{div}\,\xi\,dx.

This rewriting is natural from the point-of-view of the classical Allen-Cahn equation. The first term on the right-hand side measures (in a weak sense) the deviation between our nonlocal reaction term in (

P
ε

) and the standard local reaction term. The second term is what would arise from the standard local reaction term, and from Proposition
7.1
, we already know at a.e. fixed
t\in[0,T]
that
\left(W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon}(t))\right)_{\varepsilon}
converges weakly-
\ast
as Radon measures on
\overline{\Omega}
to one-half of the limiting total variation.

Putting these equalities together, we deduce

(8.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon}\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}\cdot\xi\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}\otimes\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}:D\xi\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\mathrm{div}\,\xi\,dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon})\mathrm{div}\,\xi\,dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}(\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon}}-\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon})\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}\cdot\xi-W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon})\mathrm{div}\,\xi\,dx.

The rest of the proof consists in passing to the limit
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
in (
8.1
) using the assumption of convergence of the energy (
2.18
) and its consequences.
But first, we point out that the first four terms in (
8.1
) can all be bounded by the energy and energy dissipation and are expected to have a limit. This implies that the last term is also bounded (and should have a limit).
More precisely, (
8.1
) implies, for all
t\in[0,T]
, the following bound for the last term:

(8.2)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}(\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon}(t)}-\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon}(t))\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}(t)\cdot\xi(t)-W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon}(t))\mathrm{div}\,\xi(t)\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\xi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{d}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon|\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+C\|D\xi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{d\times d}}\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}\,dx+\|\mathrm{div}\,\xi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon}(t))\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\|\xi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{d}}\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon}(t)^{1/2}\big(2\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon}(\phi_{\mathrm{in}}^{\varepsilon})\big)^{1/2}+C\|D\xi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{d\times d}}\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon}(\phi_{\mathrm{in}}^{\varepsilon}).

The next proposition shows that the first four terms in (
8.1
) have a limit.
The last term, which gives rise to the Lagrange multiplier, will be handled by Proposition
8.2
.

Proposition 8.1
.

Under the assumption of convergence of the energy (
2.18
) and for any test vector field
\xi\in[C^{1}_{c}((0,T)\times\Omega)]^{d}
, the following convergences hold (for the same subsequence as in Lemma
7.1
):

(8.3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{2}|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|^{2}\mathrm{div}\,\xi\,dx\,dt=\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{div}\,\xi|\nabla\phi|\,dt,

(8.4)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\mathrm{div}\,\xi\,dx\,dt=\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{div}\,\xi|\nabla\phi|\,dt,

(8.5)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon_{n}\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\otimes\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}:D\xi\,dx\,dt=\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\nu\otimes\nu:D\xi\,|\nabla\phi|\,dt,

(8.6)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}{\varepsilon_{n}}\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\cdot\xi\,dx\,dt=\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}V\xi\cdot\nabla\phi\,dt.

In the right-hand side of (
8.5
),
\nu(t)
is the Radon-Nikodym derivative of the Radon measure
\nabla\phi(t)
on
\overline{\Omega}
w.r.t. its total variation
\left|\nabla\phi(t)\right|
. It coincides with the measure-theoretic inner unit normal vector to the reduced boundary
\partial^{\ast}E(t)
, where
E(t)=\supp\phi^{0}(t)
is a set of finite perimeter in
\Omega
, as shown in Proposition
6.1
. In particular, for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
, we have
\left|\nu(t,x)\right|=1
for
\left|\nabla\phi(t)\right|
-a.e.
x
.

Proof.

The first two limits, (
8.3
) and (
8.4
), follow, respectively, from the second and third limits in (
7.1
) of Lemma
7.1
and Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem.
The third limit (
8.5
) is handled as in the proof of Proposition 5.2 in
[
21
]
. It is a particular case of Reshetnyak’s continuity theorem; we refer to

[
21
, Proposition A.3]
for a simple proof.
This proof can in fact be adapted to prove (
8.6
), as we show below. Though written a bit differently, this forthcoming argument is essentially similar to the one used in
[
25
, Section 3.3]
.

Given a vector field
h\in L^{\infty}(0,T;[C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)]^{d})
such that
|h(t,x)|\leq 1
for all
t,x
, we write

\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}{\varepsilon_{n}}\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\cdot\xi\,dx\,dt-\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}V\xi\cdot\nabla\phi\,dt\right|\leq A_{n}+B_{n}+E

with

\displaystyle A_{n}

\displaystyle:=\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}{\varepsilon_{n}}\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\cdot\xi\,dx\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Omega}{\varepsilon_{n}}\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|h\cdot\xi\,dx\,dt\right|,

\displaystyle B_{n}

\displaystyle:=\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}{\varepsilon_{n}}\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|h\cdot\xi\,dx\,dt-\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}Vh\cdot\xi|\nabla\phi|\,dt\right|,

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle:=\gamma\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}Vh\cdot\xi|\nabla\phi|\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}V\xi\cdot\nabla\phi\,dt\right|.

The idea behind this splitting is to compare the direction of the approximate unit normal vector (or its limit) with some arbitrary but frozen direction (encoded by the vector field
h
). The task is then to unfreeze this direction and show it can be chosen in a manner that approximates arbitrarily well the direction of the limiting unit normal vector.

We introduce some notation for the approximate unit normal vector
\nu^{\varepsilon}:=\frac{\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}}{|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}|}
(and defined to be some arbitrary member of
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
whenever
\left|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}\right|=0
), and we estimate

\displaystyle A_{n}

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon_{n}\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|\xi\cdot(\nu^{\varepsilon_{n}}-h)\,dx\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\delta\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon_{n}|\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\frac{\|\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{d})}}{\delta}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|^{2}|\nu^{\varepsilon_{n}}-h|^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\delta\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi_{\mathrm{in}}^{\varepsilon_{n}})+2\frac{\|\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{d})}}{\delta}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|^{2}\left(1-\nu^{\varepsilon_{n}}\cdot h\right)\,dx\,dt,

where we used the energy dissipation inequality (
6.1
) and the fact that
|\nu^{\varepsilon}-h|^{2}=|\nu^{\varepsilon}|^{2}-2\nu^{\varepsilon}\cdot h+|h|^{2}\leq 2-2\nu^{\varepsilon}\cdot h
(since
|\nu^{\varepsilon}|=1
and
|h|\leq 1
).
Furthermore, equipartition of the energy permits us to replace
\varepsilon_{n}\left|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\right|^{2}
by
\left|\nabla F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\right|
up to some asymptotically negligible error (see (
7.11
)):

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon_{n}|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|^{2}\left(1-\nu^{\varepsilon_{n}}\cdot h\right)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})|\left(1-\nu^{\varepsilon_{n}}\cdot h\right)\,dx\,dt+o(1)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|\,dx\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\cdot h\,dx\,dt+o(1).

Above, we reintroduced the auxiliary function
\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}:=F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})
and used the observation that
\nu^{\varepsilon_{n}}=\frac{\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}}{|\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|}
. The first integral on the second line has a limit (using (
7.5
)) and (after an integration by parts) the second integral has a limit (using Proposition
6.1
)). Thus,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}A_{n}\leq\delta\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi_{\mathrm{in}}^{\varepsilon_{n}})+2\frac{\|\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{d})}}{\delta}\left(\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi|\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\gamma\phi\,\mathrm{div}\,h\,dx\,dt\right).

We recall that the well-preparedness assumption on the initial data guarantees that the energy of the initial data is uniformly bounded.

We can bound the term
E
similarly: Replacing
\nabla\phi(t)
with
\nu(t)\left|\nabla\phi(t)\right|
and using a similar argument as above leads to

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=\gamma\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}V(h-\nu)\cdot\xi|\nabla\phi|\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\delta\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}V^{2}|\nabla\phi|\,dt+2\gamma^{2}\frac{\|\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{d})}}{\delta}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi|\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\mathrm{div}\,h\,dx\,dt\right).

Finally, we claim that
\lim_{n\to\infty}B_{n}=0
. Indeed, (
7.2
) of Lemma
7.1

and the fact that
\varepsilon_{n}^{1/2}\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}
is bounded in
L^{2}

permits us to replace
\varepsilon_{n}^{1/2}\left|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\right|
with
\varepsilon_{n}^{-1/2}\sqrt{2W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})}
. We then introduce the auxiliary function
\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}:=F_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})
and recall
F^{\prime}_{\sigma}(v)=\frac{1}{\sigma}\sqrt{2W_{\sigma}(v)}
, and by using (
7.12
) from Lemma
7.2
, we find

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}{\varepsilon_{n}}\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|h\cdot\xi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\sqrt{2W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})}h\cdot\xi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\psi^{\varepsilon_{n}}h\cdot\xi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}Vh\cdot\xi\,|\nabla\phi|\,dt

hence
\lim_{n\to\infty}B_{n}=0
.

Putting things together, we proved

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}{\varepsilon_{n}}\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\cdot\xi\,dx\,dt-\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}V\xi\cdot\nabla\phi\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\delta+\frac{C}{\delta}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi|\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\mathrm{div}\,h\,dx\,dt\right).

Since the first line is independent of
h
, we can use the definition of the
BV(\Omega)
-seminorm of
\phi
to make the
\mathcal{O}(\delta^{-1})
term on the second line arbitrarily small, which leads to

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}{\varepsilon_{n}}\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\cdot\xi\,dx\,dt-\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}V\xi\cdot\nabla\phi\,dt\right|\leq C\delta.

We can now let
\delta\to 0^{+}
to get the result.
∎

The final task is to

) and
show that the deviation between the our nonlocal reaction term and the standard local reaction term converges weakly to the Lagrange multiplier
\Lambda=\Lambda(t)
that is responsible for the volume preservation of the limiting flow.
We will make use of the particular form of the term
\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}}
given by Proposition

-(ii) and (iii).

Proposition 8.2

(Existence of the Lagrange multiplier)
.

Under the assumption of convergence of the energy (
2.18
),
there exists
\Lambda\in L^{2}(0,T)
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}}-\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\cdot\xi-W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\mathrm{div}\,\xi\,dx\,dt=-\int_{0}^{T}\Lambda(t)\int_{\Omega}\gamma\phi\,\mathrm{div}\,\xi\,dt

for all

Proof of Proposition
8.2
.

The proof is divided in several steps.

Step 1:
First we are going to show that for all
t\in[0,T]
there exists a constant
\Lambda^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}(\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)}-\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)\cdot\xi(t)-W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))\mathrm{div}\,\xi(t)\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad=-\Lambda^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)\int_{\Omega}\frac{\gamma}{\sigma}\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)}\mathrm{div}\,\xi(t)\,dx+\mathcal{O}\big(\|\mathrm{div}\,\xi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}z^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)\big),

where the function

(defined in (
7.6

\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))
and the optimal lower bound on the Modica-Mortola functional, and vanishes strongly in
L^{1}(0,T)
as

The convergence of the energy assumption (
2.18

7.3
) of Lemma

, and the error is measured in
L^{1}(\Omega)
pointwise in time by the second inequality in (
7.10
). Using this and the definition (
2.11
) of

, we obtain (after an integration by parts)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))\mathrm{div}\,\xi(t)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left[f(\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)})+a\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)}-\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)}\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)+\frac{\sigma}{2}|\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)|^{2}\right]\mathrm{div}\,\xi(t)\,dx+\mathcal{O}(\|\mathrm{div}\,\xi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}z^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left[f(\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}}(t))+a\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)}-\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)}\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)\right]\mathrm{div}\,\xi(t)-\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)\cdot\xi(t)\,dx+\mathcal{O}(\|\mathrm{div}\,\xi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}z^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)).

Recall also that
\phi^{\varepsilon}(t)=f^{\prime}(\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon}(t)})+\ell^{\varepsilon}(t)
a.e. on
\left\{\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon}(t)}>0\right\}
(see Proposition

-(ii)), where

\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon}(t)}

\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon}(t)}
, this equality is valid a.e. on
\Omega
, so the above becomes

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\big[f(\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)})-\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)}f^{\prime}(\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)})\big]\mathrm{div}\,\xi(t)-\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)\cdot\xi(t)\,dx

\displaystyle+\frac{a-\ell^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)}\mathrm{div}\,\xi(t)\,dx+\mathcal{O}(\left\|\mathrm{div}\,\xi(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}z^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)).

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}(\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)}-\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)\cdot\xi(t)-W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))\mathrm{div}\,\xi(t)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)}\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)\cdot\xi(t)+\nabla\big[f(\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)})-\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)}f^{\prime}(\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)})\big]\cdot\xi(t)\,dx

for each

-(iii)), we see that the integral on the second line vanishes. We thus obtain (

Step 2:
Ultimately, we aim to pass to the limit in (
8.8
), and for that we want to show that

\left(\Lambda^{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)_{n}
is bounded in

.

) and showing that for sufficiently small

and a well-chosen vector field

).

The strong convergence of

to

\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that

\forall\,t\in(t_{0}-\delta,t_{0}+\delta),\quad\forall\varepsilon_{n}\leq\varepsilon_{0}\qquad\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)}\mathrm{div}\,\xi_{t_{0}}\,dx\geq\frac{\eta}{8}

(once again, the fact that
\mathrm{div}\,\xi_{t_{0}}=\psi_{t_{0}}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}
uniformly in
t_{0}
implies that
\varepsilon_{0}
does not depend on
t_{0}
).

Step 5:
Since
\left\{(t_{0}-\delta,t_{0}+\delta)\right\}_{t_{0}\in[0,T]}
is an open cover of
[0,T]
, by compactness there exists a finite open subcover
\left\{(t_{i}-\delta,t_{i}+\delta)\right\}_{i=1}^{N}
. Suppose
\varepsilon_{n}<\varepsilon
, where
\varepsilon
is from Step 4, and fix
i\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\}
and
t\in(t_{i}-\delta,t_{i}+\delta)
. On the one hand, Step 4 gives

\left|\Lambda^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)}\mathrm{div}\,\xi_{t_{i}}\,dx\right|=\left|\Lambda^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)\right|\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)}\mathrm{div}\,\xi_{t_{i}}\,dx\geq\frac{\eta}{8}\left|\Lambda^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)\right|.

On the other hand, from the equality (
8.8
) in Step 1 and the calculation (
8.2
) (with
\xi_{t_{i}}
in place of
\xi(t)
)

\left|\Lambda^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)}\mathrm{div}\,\xi_{t_{i}}\,dx\right|\leq C\left\|\psi_{t_{i}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}z^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)+C\left\|\xi_{t_{i}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{d}}\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{1/2}(t)+C\left\|D\xi_{t_{i}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{d\times d}},

where we have used that the initial energies are bounded.
We recall that for each
t_{i}
,
\xi_{t_{i}}\in C^{1,\alpha}(\Omega)^{d}
(although the corresponding bound is not uniform in
t_{i}
)
and so

\frac{\eta}{8}\left|\Lambda^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)\right|\leq K\left(z^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)+\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{1/2}(t)+C\right)\leq K\left(\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t))+\mathscr{D}_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{1/2}(t)+C\right)

where

K=\sup_{i=1,\dots,N}\left\|\xi_{t_{i}}\right\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\Omega)^{d}}<\infty.

We deduce (using the energy dissipation inequality (
6.1
) and the bound on the energy of the initial data)

\int_{0}^{T}\left|\Lambda^{\varepsilon_{n}}(t)\right|^{2}\,dt\leq CK

We thus extract a (not relabled) subsequence along which
\Lambda^{\varepsilon_{n}}\rightharpoonup\Lambda
weakly in
L^{2}(0,T)
. Since
\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}}\to\sigma\phi
strongly in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))
, we have, for any test function
\xi\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega})
, that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}}\mathrm{div}\,\xi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\sigma\phi\,\mathrm{div}\,\xi\,dx\quad\text{strongly in }L^{\infty}(0,T),

so then the product
\Lambda^{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}}\mathrm{div}\,\xi\,dx
converges strongly in
L^{2}(0,T)
to
\Lambda(t)\int_{\Omega}\sigma\phi\mathrm{div}\,\xi\,dx
.

In view of (
8.8
), this completes the proof of Proposition
8.2
. ∎

Putting everything together, we now easily get:

Proof of Theorem
2.4
- (ii).

We simply pass to the limit in (
8.1
), which we recall here:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon_{n}\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\cdot\xi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\varepsilon_{n}\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\otimes\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}:D\xi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{2}|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}|^{2}\mathrm{div}\,\xi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\mathrm{div}\,\xi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\rho_{\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}}-\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}}\cdot\xi-W_{\sigma}(\sigma\phi^{\varepsilon_{n}})\mathrm{div}\,\xi\,dx\,dt.

Using Proposition
8.1
(for the first four terms) and Proposition
8.2
(for the last one), we get:

\displaystyle\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}V\xi\cdot\nabla\phi\,dt.

\displaystyle=-\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\nu\otimes\nu:D\xi\,|\nabla\phi|\,dt+\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}\,\xi|\nabla\phi|\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\Lambda(t)\int_{\Omega}\gamma\phi\,\mathrm{div}\,\xi\,dt,

and the proof is complete.
∎

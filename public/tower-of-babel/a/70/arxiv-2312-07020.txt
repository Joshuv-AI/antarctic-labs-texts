1.
Introduction

Ultradifferentiable classes are classes of
C^{\infty}
functions defined by prescribed growth behavior of the
infinite sequence of derivatives. The most classical among them are the Denjoy–Carleman classes, where the
growth of the derivatives is dominated by a weight sequence
M=(M_{j})_{j\geq 0}
. The Gevrey classes (in particular,
the real analytic class),
which play an important role in the theory of differential equations,
are special cases thereof.

In applications, sometimes the question arises whether the ultradifferentiable regularity can be concluded
if only lacunary information on the growth of the derivatives is available.
For instance, Bolley, Camus, and Métivier
[
2
]
studied the analyticity of analytic vectors
and Liess
[
17
]
partially extended their work to Denjoy–Carleman classes.
Knowing suitable bounds for the sequence
(P^{j}f)_{j\geq 0}
, where
P
is an elliptic linear partial differential operator,
one would like to conclude similar bounds for all derivatives
f
.
In
[
20
]
, this approach was applied to isotropic functions and used to prove Chevalley-type results.

The basic problem is the following.
Suppose that we know that a smooth function satisfies certain ultradifferentiable bounds
for the derivatives of order
k_{j}
, where
(k_{j})
is a strictly increasing sequence of integers,
can we deduce that its derivatives of all orders satisfy the ultradifferentiable bounds?
An affirmative answer clearly depends on conditions for the base sequence
(k_{j})
, the weight
M=(M_{j})
,
and on suitable interpolation inequalities.

In the recent paper
[
1
]
, Albano and Mughetti,
motivated by
L^{2}
methods for proving local regularity of solutions for (degenerate) elliptic equations
(cf.
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
),
gave sufficient conditions for Denjoy–Carleman classes of Roumieu type
on a compact interval of
\mathbb{R}
, based on the Cartan–Gorny inequality (cf.
Proposition

2.6
).
The base sequence
(k_{j})
is required to be such that
k_{j+1}/k_{j}
is bounded.
For the weight sequence, the authors assume a rather strong condition (which we recall and discuss in
Remark

6.4
).
In fact, we prove a version under a weaker and more natural condition in
Theorem

6.1
(see also
Theorem

4.1
), namely,
boundedness of
m_{k_{j+1}}/m_{k_{j}}
, where
m_{j}:=M_{j}^{1/j}
.
Note that Liess showed (in
[
17
]
) that, under certain assumptions on
M
, boundedness of
m_{k_{j+1}}/m_{k_{j}}
is also a necessary condition
for the interpolation problem (see
Remark

4.4
).
We discuss at the end of
Section

6
necessity of the conditions and how the different conditions are related.

The main purpose of this paper is
to treat the interpolation problem in a broad and comprehensive way, striving for minimal assumptions on the weights.
To this end, we review various interpolation inequalities for
L^{p}
-norms, where
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
and work in arbitrary (finite) dimensions. Note that the growth of the involved constants is crucial in the ultradifferentiable setting.
We consider ultradifferentiable classes of local and global type
and allow very general weight systems (so that also Braun–Meise–Taylor classes are covered).
All the ingredients seem to be well-known, even though somewhat scattered in the literature,
but we think a unified treatment can be useful.

Let us outline the structure of the paper.
In
Section

2
, we recall several interpolation inequalities with short proofs for the convenience of the reader.
Building on these inequalities, we obtain three technical propositions in
Section

3
on which most of the subsequent
ultradifferentiable regularity results by lacunary estimates are based.
In
Section

4
, we present these results for a broad variety of Denjoy–Carleman classes (defined in terms of a single weight sequence)
and, in
Section

5
, we extend them to more general ultradifferentiable classes (defined by families of weight sequences)
including the Braun–Meise–Taylor classes.
In the last
Section

6
, we compare our results with the approach of Albano and Mughetti
[
1
]

and discuss optimality of the conditions imposed on the base sequence
(k_{j})
and on the weights.
In
Appendix

A
, we generalize to general weight systems a construction of
[
1
]
which shows that, in general, boundedness of
k_{j+1}/k_{j}

cannot be omitted.

2.
Interpolation inequalities

2.1.
The global setting

The Landau–Kolmogorov inequality states that a
C^{m}
function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
with finite
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}
and
\|f^{(m)}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}

satisfies

\|f^{(j)}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq K_{m,j}\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}^{1-j/m}\|f^{(m)}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}^{j/m},\quad j=1,\ldots,m-1.

Due to Kolmogorov
[
12
]
,
the optimal constants
K_{m,j}
are given by

K_{m,j}=\frac{k_{m-j}}{k_{m}^{1-j/m}},

where
k_{r}:=\frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}[\frac{(-1)^{i}}{2i+1}]^{r+1}
are the Favard constants.
Note that
1\leq k_{r}\leq 2
so that
K_{m,j}\leq 2
.
By a simple functional-analytic argument, Certain and Kurtz
[
8
]
inferred that, if
(E,\|\cdot\|)
is a real Banach space
and
A
is the generator of a strongly continuous group of isometries, then

\|A^{j}x\|\leq K_{m,j}\,\|x\|^{1-j/m}\|A^{m}x\|^{j/m},\quad j=1,\ldots,m-1,

for
x
in the domain of
A^{m}
.
In particular (see also
[
16
, Section 4.4]
), we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
m\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
.
Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
C^{m}
function. Then, for
all
v\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,

\|d_{v}^{j}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 2\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1-j/m}\|d_{v}^{m}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{j/m},\quad j=1,\ldots,m-1,

if the right-hand side is finite,
where
d_{v}^{j}f(x):=\partial_{t}^{j}f(x+tv)|_{t=0}
.

Proof.

We may assume
v=(1,0,\ldots,0)
by choosing a suitable orthonormal system of coordinates.
Let us first consider
p=\infty
.
We have

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{1}^{j}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\\
2\leq i\leq n\end{subarray}}\Big[2\,\Big(\sup_{x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|\Big)^{1-j/m}\Big(\sup_{x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{1}^{m}f(x)|\Big)^{j/m}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\Big(\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|\Big)^{1-j/m}\Big(\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{1}^{m}f(x)|\Big)^{j/m}.

For
p<\infty
, we apply the above remarks to
A=\partial_{1}
and
E=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
It generates the group of translations
T(s)f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=f(x_{1}+s,x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})
which is strongly
continuous, by the dominated convergence theorem.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

For
p=2
, the factor
2
can be omitted as follows from an application of the Fourier transform:

\displaystyle\int|\xi_{1}^{j}\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int(|\xi_{1}|^{m}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|)^{\frac{2j}{m}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2(1-\frac{j}{m})}\,d\xi\leq\|\xi_{1}^{m}\widehat{f}(\xi)\|^{2j/m}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\widehat{f}(\xi)\|^{2(1-j/m)}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

by Hölder’s inequality.

2.2.
The local setting

The next two lemmas
follow from an easy adaptation of the proof of
[
2
, Lemmas 2.3-2.5]
.
We sketch the argument for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 2.3
.

There is a constant
C>0
such that the following holds.
Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
a>0
,

m\in\mathbb{N}
,
0\leq j\leq m
, and
f\in C^{m}([-2a,2a])
. Then

\frac{a^{j}}{j!}\|f^{(j)}\|_{L^{p}([-a,a])}\leq C^{m}\Big(\frac{a^{m}}{m!}\|f^{(m)}\|_{L^{p}([-2a,2a])}+\|f\|_{L^{p}([-2a,2a])}\Big).

Proof.

By rescaling, it suffices to assume
a=1
.
Fix
m
. Let
\varphi
be a
C^{\infty}
function with support in
(-2,2)
that equals
1
near
[-1,1]
and
satisfies
\|\varphi^{(k)}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq(C_{0}m)^{k}
for
0\leq k\leq m
for a universal constant
C_{0}
(cf.
[
11
, Theorem 1.3.5]
).
By Taylor’s theorem, for
t\in[-2,2]
and
0\leq j\leq m-1
,

\displaystyle(\varphi f)^{(j)}(t)=\int_{-2}^{t}\frac{(t-s)^{m-j-1}}{(m-j-1)!}(\varphi f)^{(m)}(s)\,ds=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\binom{m}{i}A_{i}(t),

where

A_{i}(t):=\frac{1}{(m-j-1)!}\int_{-2}^{t}(t-s)^{m-j-1}\varphi^{(m-i)}(s)f^{(i)}(s)\,ds.

For
i=m
, we have

|A_{m}(t)|\leq\frac{1}{(m-j-1)!}\int_{-2}^{2}|t-s|^{m-j-1}|\varphi(s)f^{(m)}(s)|\,ds,

and thus, by Young’s inequality,

\displaystyle\|A_{m}\|_{L^{p}([-1,1])}

\displaystyle\leq\|A_{m}\|_{L^{p}([-2,2])}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(m-j-1)!}\|\mathbf{1}_{[-2,2]}t^{m-j-1}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|\varphi f^{(m)}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{m-j+1}}{(m-j)!}\|f^{(m)}\|_{L^{p}([-2,2])}.

For
0\leq i\leq m-1
and
t\in[-1,1]
, integration by parts gives

\displaystyle A_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{i}}{(m-j-1)!}\int_{-2}^{-1}\partial_{s}^{i}[(t-s)^{m-j-1}\varphi^{(m-i)}(s)]f(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\binom{i}{\ell}\frac{(-1)^{i+\ell}}{(m-j-1-\ell)!}\int_{-2}^{-1}(t-s)^{m-j-1-\ell}\varphi^{(m-\ell)}(s)f(s)\,ds,

where
k:=\min\{i,m-j-1\}
. Using Young’s inequality as before, we get

\displaystyle\|A_{i}\|_{L^{p}([-1,1])}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\binom{i}{\ell}\frac{2^{m-j-\ell+1}}{(m-j-\ell)!}(C_{0}m)^{m-\ell}\|f\|_{L^{p}([-2,2])}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\binom{m}{i}\|A_{i}\|_{L^{p}([-1,1])}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\binom{m}{i}\binom{i}{\ell}\frac{2^{m-j-\ell+1}}{(m-j-\ell)!}(C_{0}m)^{m-\ell}\|f\|_{L^{p}([-2,2])}

and the lemma follows if we prove that

\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\binom{m}{i}\binom{i}{\ell}\frac{2^{m-j-\ell+1}}{(m-j-\ell)!}(C_{0}m)^{m-\ell}\leq C^{m}j!

for a universal constant
C
. The left-hand side equals

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-j-1}\sum_{i=\ell}^{m-1}\frac{(m-\ell)!}{(m-i)!(i-\ell)!}\frac{m!}{\ell!(m-\ell)!}\frac{2^{m-j-\ell+1}}{(m-j-\ell)!}(C_{0}m)^{m-\ell}

which is bounded by

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-j-1}2^{m-\ell}2^{m}\frac{2^{m-j-\ell+1}}{(m-j-\ell)!}C_{0}^{m-\ell}e^{m}\frac{m!}{\ell!}

\displaystyle\leq e^{m}2^{2m}2^{j+1}C_{0}^{j}\,j!\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-j}\frac{(m-j)!}{\ell!(m-j-\ell)!}(2^{2}C_{0})^{m-j-\ell}

which is of the required form.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

There exist constants
C_{0},C>0
, depending only on the dimension
n
, such that the following holds.
Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
Let
U,V
bounded open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\overline{U}\subseteq V
.
Let
f\in C^{m}(\overline{V})
.
For all
0<a\leq C_{0}\operatorname{dist}(U,\partial V)
, all
0\leq j\leq m
, and all
v\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,

\frac{a^{j}}{j!}\|d^{j}_{v}f\|_{L^{p}(U)}\leq C^{m}\Big(\frac{a^{m}}{m!}\|d^{m}_{v}f\|_{L^{p}(V)}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(V)}\Big).

Proof.

We may assume that
v=(1,0,\ldots,0)
.
Let us first show the assertion for
U=(-a,a)^{n}
and
V=(-2a,2a)^{n}
.
For
x^{\prime}:=(x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in(-a,a)^{n-1}
,
Lemma

2.3
gives

\displaystyle\frac{a^{j}}{j!}\|\partial_{1}^{j}f(\cdot,x^{\prime})\|_{L^{p}((-a,a))}\leq C^{m}\Big(\frac{a^{m}}{m!}\|\partial_{1}^{m}f(\cdot,x^{\prime})\|_{L^{p}((-2a,2a))}+\|f(\cdot,x^{\prime})\|_{L^{p}((-2a,2a))}\Big).

For
p=\infty
, the statement follows, by taking the supremum over all
x^{\prime}\in(-a,a)^{n-1}
.
For
p<\infty
, take the
p
-th power an integrate over
x^{\prime}\in(-a,a)^{n-1}
.

In general,
there is a constant
C_{0}>0
depending only on
n

such that, if
a\leq C_{0}\operatorname{dist}(U,\partial V)
,
then

U
can be covered by a family
\mathcal{Q}
of cubes
Q=x+[-a,a]^{n}
such that
2Q=x+[-2a,2a]^{n}\subseteq V

and such that any two cubes in
\mathcal{Q}
have disjoint interior. Then, for each
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
,

\displaystyle\frac{a^{j}}{j!}\|\partial_{1}^{j}f\|_{L^{p}(Q)}\leq C^{m}\Big(\frac{a^{m}}{m!}\|\partial_{1}^{m}f\|_{L^{p}(2Q)}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(2Q)}\Big).

For
p=\infty
, take the maximum over all
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
, for
p<\infty
, take the
p
-power and the sum over all
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
.
∎

Now we combine
Lemma

2.4
with
Lemma

2.1
(following
[
17
, p. 193]
).

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
Let
U,V
bounded open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\overline{U}\subseteq V
.
Let
f\in C^{\infty}(\overline{V})
.
Then, for each integer
m\geq 2
,
j=1,\ldots,m-1
, and all
v\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,

\|d_{v}^{j}f\|_{L^{p}(U)}\leq C^{m}\|f\|^{1-j/m}_{L^{p}(V)}\Big(\|d_{v}^{m}f\|^{j/m}_{L^{p}(V)}+m^{j}\|f\|^{j/m}_{L^{p}(V)}\Big),

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
U
,
V
, and
n
.

Proof.

Let
W
be the open
\delta
-neighborhood of
\overline{U}
, where
\delta:=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{dist}(U,\partial V)
.
Let
\varphi
be a
C^{\infty}
function with support in
W
that equals
1
near
\overline{U}
and
satisfies
\|\partial^{\alpha}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{0}^{|\alpha|+1}m^{|\alpha|}
for
|\alpha|\leq m
,
where
C_{0}
only depends on
U
,
V
, and
n
(cf.
[
11
, Theorem 1.4.2]
).
Then
\varphi f
has a
C^{\infty}
extension by zero outside
W
to all of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which we also denote by
\varphi f
.
By
Lemma

2.1
,

\|d_{v}^{j}f\|_{L^{p}(U)}\leq\|d^{j}_{v}(\varphi f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 2\,\|(\varphi f)\|^{1-j/m}_{L^{p}(W)}\|d_{v}^{m}(\varphi f)\|_{L^{p}(W)}^{j/m}

and, by
Lemma

2.4
with
a=C_{1}\delta
, where
C_{1}=C_{1}(n)
,

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle d_{v}^{m}(\varphi f)\|_{L^{p}(W)}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{m}\binom{m}{i}\|d_{v}^{m-i}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|d_{v}^{i}f\|_{L^{p}(W)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{m}\binom{m}{i}C_{0}(C_{0}m)^{m-i}C^{m}\frac{i!}{(C_{1}\delta)^{i}}\Big(\frac{(C_{1}\delta)^{m}}{m!}\|d_{v}^{m}f\|_{L^{p}(V)}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(V)}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{m}\binom{m}{i}\frac{C_{0}(eC_{0}C\max\{C_{1}\delta,1\})^{m}}{(C_{0}C_{1}\delta)^{i}}\big(\|d_{v}^{m}f\|_{L^{p}(V)}+m!\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(V)}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}^{m}\Big(\|d_{v}^{m}f\|_{L^{p}(V)}+m^{m}\|f\|_{L^{p}(V)}\Big).

This implies the assertion.
∎

2.3.
The Cartan–Gorny inequality

The following result is due to Gorny
[
10
]
and independently to Cartan
[
7
]
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a compact interval and
f\in C^{m}(I)
.
Then, for
j=1,\ldots,m-1
,

\|f^{(j)}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\leq 4e^{2j}\Big(\frac{m}{j}\Big)^{j}\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}^{1-j/m}\max\Big\{\|f^{(m)}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)},\frac{m!}{|I|^{m}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\Big\}^{j/m}.

The factor
(\frac{m}{j})^{j}
is bounded by
e^{m}
.

3.
Ultradifferentiable regularity by lacunary estimates

In this section, we prove three technical propositions
on which most of the subsequent results are based.

Let
0=:k_{0}<k_{1}<k_{2}<\cdots
be a strictly increasing infinite sequence of positive integers.
A sequence
(k_{j})
with these properties is called a
base sequence
.
If additionally
k_{j+1}/k_{j}
, for
j\geq 1
, is bounded we say that
(k_{j})
is a
special base sequence
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(k_{j})
be a base sequence.
Let
(m_{j})
and
(m^{\prime}_{j})
be positive increasing sequences of reals satisfying
m_{j}\leq m^{\prime}_{j}
for all
j
.
Assume that
m_{k_{j+1}}/m^{\prime}_{k_{j}}
is bounded. Let
1\leq p\leq\infty

and
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

If there are
C,\rho>0
such that

(1)

\max_{|\alpha|=k_{j}}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(\rho m_{k_{j}})^{k_{j}},\quad j\geq 0,

then there exist
C^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}>0
such that

(2)

\max_{|\alpha|=\ell}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C^{\prime}(\rho^{\prime}m^{\prime}_{\ell})^{\ell},\quad\ell\geq 0.

We have
\rho^{\prime}=O(\rho)
as
\rho\to 0
.

Proof.

By (
1
), there are constants
C_{1},\rho_{1}>0
such that

(3)

\|d_{v}^{k_{j}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{1}(\rho_{1}m_{k_{j}})^{k_{j}},\quad j\geq 0,\,v\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

Let
\ell\geq 0
and let
j\geq 0
be such that
k_{j}\leq\ell<k_{j+1}
.
By
Lemma

2.1
and the assumptions on
(k_{j})
,
(m_{j})
, and
(m^{\prime}_{j})
,

(4)

\displaystyle\|d_{v}^{\ell}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\|d_{v}^{k_{j}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1-\frac{\ell-k_{j}}{k_{j+1}-k_{j}}}\|d_{v}^{k_{j+1}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{\ell-k_{j}}{k_{j+1}-k_{j}}}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{1}\,(\rho_{1}m_{k_{j}})^{k_{j}\big(1-\frac{\ell-k_{j}}{k_{j+1}-k_{j}}\big)}(\rho_{1}m_{k_{j+1}})^{k_{j+1}\frac{\ell-k_{j}}{k_{j+1}-k_{j}}}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{1}\,(\rho_{1}m^{\prime}_{k_{j}})^{k_{j}\big(1-\frac{\ell-k_{j}}{k_{j+1}-k_{j}}\big)}(C_{2}\rho_{1}m^{\prime}_{k_{j}})^{k_{j+1}\frac{\ell-k_{j}}{k_{j+1}-k_{j}}}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{1}\,(C_{2}\rho_{1}m^{\prime}_{\ell})^{\ell}.

By polarization
[
13
, Lemma 7.13]
, for
|\alpha|=\ell
and
v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell}
the
list of standard unit vectors

\underbrace{e_{1},\ldots,e_{1}}_{\alpha_{1}},\underbrace{e_{2},\ldots,e_{2}}_{\alpha_{2}},\ldots,\underbrace{e_{n},\ldots,e_{n}}_{\alpha_{n}},

we have

(5)

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}f

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell!}\sum_{\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{\ell}=0}^{1}(-1)^{\ell-\sum\epsilon_{\ell}}\Big(\sum\epsilon_{i}\Big)^{\ell}d^{\ell}_{\sum\epsilon_{i}v_{i}/\sum\epsilon_{i}}f.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 2C_{1}(C_{2}\rho_{1}m^{\prime}_{\ell})^{\ell}\frac{1}{\ell!}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{k}k^{\ell}\leq 2C_{1}(2eC_{2}\rho_{1}m^{\prime}_{\ell})^{\ell}.

Thus (
2
) is proved.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(k_{j})
be a special base sequence.
Let
(m_{j})
and
(m^{\prime}_{j})
be positive increasing sequences of reals satisfying
m_{j}\leq m^{\prime}_{j}
for all
j

and such that
j/m_{j}
is bounded.
Assume that
m_{k_{j+1}}/m^{\prime}_{k_{j}}
is bounded. Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
Let
U,V
bounded open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\overline{U}\subseteq V
.
Let
f\in C^{\infty}(\overline{V})
.

If there are
C,\rho>0
such that

(6)

\max_{|\alpha|=k_{j}}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(V)}\leq C(\rho m_{k_{j}})^{k_{j}},\quad j\geq 0,

then there exist
C^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}>0
such that

(7)

\max_{|\alpha|=\ell}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(U)}\leq C^{\prime}(\rho^{\prime}m^{\prime}_{\ell})^{\ell},\quad\ell\geq 0.

Under the additional assumption that
j/m_{j}\to 0
, we have that
\rho^{\prime}=O(\rho)
as
\rho\to 0
.

Proof.

By (
6
), there are constants
C_{1},\rho_{1}>0
such that

\|d_{v}^{k_{j}}f\|_{L^{p}(V)}\leq C_{1}(\rho_{1}m_{k_{j}})^{k_{j}},\quad j\geq 0,\,v\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

Let
\ell\geq 0
and let
j\geq 0
be such that
k_{j}\leq\ell<k_{j+1}
.
By
Corollary

2.5

4.
Denjoy–Carleman classes

Let
M=(M_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be a positive sequence of real numbers.
We say that
M
is a
weight sequence
if
M_{0}=1\leq M_{1}\leq\mu_{2}\leq\mu_{3}\leq\cdots
, where
\mu_{k}:=M_{k}/M_{k-1}
.
Then also the sequence
m_{k}:=M_{k}^{1/k}
is increasing.

Let
U
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. The
L^{p}
-based local Denjoy–Carleman class of Roumieu type
on
U

is the set
\mathcal{E}^{\{M\}}_{L^{p}}(U)
of all
f\in C^{\infty}(U)
such that for all open relatively compact subsets
\Omega\Subset U

there exist constants
C,\rho>0
such that

(10)

\max_{|\alpha|=k}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C(\rho m_{k})^{k},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}.

Analogously, the
L^{p}
-based local Denjoy–Carleman class of Beurling type
is the set

\mathcal{E}^{(M)}_{L^{p}}(U)
of all
f\in C^{\infty}(U)
that satisfy (
10
) for all
\Omega
and all
\rho
with
C=C(\Omega,\rho)
.
We use the placeholder
[\cdot]
for either the Roumieu case
\{\cdot\}
or the Beurling case
(\cdot)
.

Replacing (
10
) with various global requirements we obtain natural
global Denjoy–Carleman classes
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{\{M\}}_{L^{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\displaystyle:=\{f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\exists C,\rho>0~\forall\alpha:\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(\rho m_{|\alpha|})^{|\alpha|}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{(M)}_{L^{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\displaystyle:=\{f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\forall\rho>0~\exists C>0~\forall\alpha:\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(\rho m_{|\alpha|})^{|\alpha|}\},

\mathcal{S}^{\{M\}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(resp.
\mathcal{S}^{(M)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
) consisting of all
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

such that there exist
\rho,\sigma>0
such that (resp. for all
\rho,\sigma>0
)

\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}f(x)|}{(\rho m_{|\alpha|})^{|\alpha|}(\sigma m_{|\beta|})^{|\beta|}}<\infty,

and the classes
\mathcal{D}^{[M]}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\mathcal{B}^{[M]}_{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of all
\mathcal{B}^{[M]}_{L^{\infty}}
-functions with compact support.
(Cleary,

is nontrivial only in the non-quasianalytic setting.)
Note that various aspects of these global classes have recently been studied in
[
14

15

19
]
.

5.
Other ultradifferentiable classes

Classically, besides Denjoy–Carleman classes
a lot of attention was devoted to ultradifferentiable classes, sometimes called Braun–Meise–Taylor classes
due to the foundational article
[
6
]
,
defined in terms of a weight function; they have their origin in work of Beurling.

A
weight function
is a continuous increasing function
\omega:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)

satisfying
\omega(0)=0
,
\omega(2t)=O(\omega(t))
,
\log t=o(\omega(t))
as
t\to\infty

and such that
\varphi(t):=\omega(e^{t})
is convex.
It is no restriction to assume
\omega(t)=0
for
0\leq t\leq 1
(cf.
[
21
, Section 11.1]
).

The local classes
\mathcal{E}^{\{\omega\}}_{L^{p}}(U)
and
\mathcal{E}^{(\omega)}_{L^{p}}(U)
are defined in analogy to Denjoy–Carleman classes, where
now

(14)

\max_{|\alpha|=k}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\,\exp\big(\tfrac{1}{\rho}\varphi^{*}(\rho k)\big),\quad k\geq 0,

with
\varphi^{*}(s):=\sup_{t\geq 0}(st-\varphi(t))
,
plays the role of (
10
). More precisely, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\{\omega\}}_{L^{p}}(U)

\displaystyle:=\bigcap_{\Omega\Subset U}\bigcup_{\rho>0}\bigcup_{C>0}\{f\in C^{\infty}(U):f\text{ satisfies }\eqref{eq:DC6}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{(\omega)}_{L^{p}}(U)

\displaystyle:=\bigcap_{\Omega\Subset U}\bigcap_{\rho>0}\bigcup_{C>0}\{f\in C^{\infty}(U):f\text{ satisfies }\eqref{eq:DC6}\}.

The global classes
\mathcal{B}^{[\omega]}_{L^{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\mathcal{S}^{[\omega]}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
\mathcal{D}^{[\omega]}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

are defined in a straightforward way.
Furthermore, we consider
\mathcal{E}^{[\omega]}_{L^{p},(k_{j})}(U)
,
\mathcal{B}^{[\omega]}_{L^{p},(k_{j})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\mathcal{S}^{[\omega]}_{(k_{j}),(\ell_{j})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
and
\mathcal{D}^{[\omega]}_{(k_{j})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, all of them defined in the obvious manner.
(Note that
\mathcal{D}^{[\omega]}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is nontrivial if and only if
\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{-2}\omega(t)\,dt<\infty
; cf.
[
6
]
.)

There is an overarching framework for ultradifferentiable classes introduced in
[
22
]

which goes beyond Denjoy–Carleman and Braun–Meise–Taylor classes and, on a technical level, reduces
the proofs to handling certain families of weight sequences.
See
[
21
]
for a comprehensive survey of the theory.

From now on, let
\mathfrak{M}
be a totally ordered family of weight sequences, i.e., if
M,M^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{M}
, then either
M_{j}\leq M^{\prime}_{j}

or
M^{\prime}_{j}\leq M_{j}
for all
j
.

We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\{\mathfrak{M}\}}_{L^{p}}(U)

\displaystyle:=\bigcap_{\Omega\Subset U}\bigcup_{M\in\mathfrak{M}}\bigcup_{\rho>0}\bigcup_{C>0}\{f\in C^{\infty}(U):f\text{ satisfies }\eqref{eq:DC3}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{(\mathfrak{M})}_{L^{p}}(U)

\displaystyle:=\bigcap_{\Omega\Subset U}\bigcap_{M\in\mathfrak{M}}\bigcap_{\rho>0}\bigcup_{C>0}\{f\in C^{\infty}(U):f\text{ satisfies }\eqref{eq:DC3}\},

and in the evident analogous way also the global classes

\mathcal{B}^{[\mathfrak{M}]}_{L^{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\mathcal{S}^{[\mathfrak{M}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
\mathcal{D}^{[\mathfrak{M}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

as well as

\mathcal{E}^{[\mathfrak{M}]}_{L^{p},(k_{j})}(U)
,
\mathcal{B}^{[\mathfrak{M}]}_{L^{p},(k_{j})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\mathcal{S}^{[\mathfrak{M}]}_{(k_{j}),(\ell_{j})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
and
\mathcal{D}^{[\mathfrak{M}]}_{(k_{j})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
Let
\omega
be a weight function.
For
\mathfrak{M}:=\{M^{(\rho)}\}_{\rho>0}
, where
M^{(\rho)}_{k}:=\exp(\tfrac{1}{\rho}\varphi^{*}(\rho k))
, we have the identities:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{[\omega]}_{L^{p}}(U)=\mathcal{E}^{[\mathfrak{M}]}_{L^{p}}(U),

\displaystyle\quad\mathcal{B}^{[\omega]}_{L^{p},(k_{j})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathcal{B}^{[\mathfrak{M}]}_{L^{p},(k_{j})}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

\displaystyle\quad\mathcal{S}^{[\omega]}_{(k_{j}),(\ell_{j})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathcal{S}^{[\mathfrak{M}]}_{(k_{j}),(\ell_{j})}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
(k_{j})
and

are base sequences.

Proof.

This is shown by the proof of

, Lemma 5.14]
which is based
on the following property of

, see

, (5.10)]
,

(15)

\;\forall\sigma>0\;\exists H\geq 1\;\forall\rho>0\;\exists C\geq 1\;\forall k\in\mathbb{N}:\sigma^{k}M^{(\rho)}_{k}\leq C\,M^{(H\rho)}_{k}.

In fact, for given
\rho,\sigma>0

there exist
C,H\geq 1
such that

Remark 5.5
.

The family
\mathfrak{M}=\{M^{(\rho)}\}_{\rho>0}
associated with a weight function in
Theorem

5.1

has the following property:

(a)

Let
0<\sigma\leq\rho
.
If there is
C_{1}>0
such that
m^{(\sigma)}_{j}\leq C_{1}\,m^{(\rho)}_{k}
, then there is
C_{2}=C_{2}(\sigma,\rho,C_{1})>0

such that
j\leq C_{2}\,k
.

So, in this case, validity of (
17
) or (
18
) entails that
(k_{j})
is a special base sequence;
the converse holds by (
16
) and
Lemma

5.2
.

To see (a), note first that
for any positive integer
N
we have

m_{k}^{(N\rho)}=\exp(\tfrac{1}{N\rho k}\varphi^{*}(N\rho k))=m_{Nk}^{(\rho)}.

By (
15
), there exist
C=C(\rho)\geq 1
and an integer
H\geq 2
such that

4m_{k}^{(\rho)}\leq C^{1/k}m_{k}^{(H\rho)}=C^{1/k}m_{Hk}^{(\rho)},\quad k\geq 0.

For
k\geq k_{0}
, we have
C^{1/k}<2
and hence

2m_{k}^{(\rho)}<m_{Hk}^{(\rho)}
and, by iteration,

2^{\ell}m_{k}^{(\rho)}<m_{H^{\ell}k}^{(\rho)}.

If
m^{(\sigma)}_{j}\leq C_{1}\,m^{(\rho)}_{k}
, then, choosing
\ell
such that
C_{1}2^{-\ell}<1
,
we find

m^{(\sigma)}_{j}<m^{(\rho)}_{H^{\ell}k}.

Then the assumption

leads to

m^{(\rho)}_{H^{\ell}k}\leq m^{(\lceil\frac{\rho}{\sigma}\rceil\sigma)}_{H^{\ell}k}=m^{(\sigma)}_{\lceil\frac{\rho}{\sigma}\rceil H^{\ell}k}<m^{(\rho)}_{H^{\ell}k},

a contradiction. Thus
j<C_{2}\,k
, where
C_{2}:=\lceil\frac{\rho}{\sigma}\rceil H^{\ell}
.
For
k<k_{0}
,
there are only finitely many

satisfying

(since
\lim_{j\to\infty}m^{(\sigma)}_{j}=\infty
; see
[
6
, Remark 1.3]
).
So adding this bound to

gives the assertion for all

.

Optimality of the conditions

Let us summarize the situation in the case of one weight sequence
M=(M_{j})
satisfying that
j/m_{j}
is bounded.
Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a compact interval. By

,

(21)

\mathcal{B}^{\{M\}}_{L^{\infty},(k_{j})}(I)=\mathcal{B}^{\{M\}}_{L^{\infty}}(I)

and

As seen in

4.4

\dfrac{k_{j+1}}{k_{j}}

\scriptstyle{M\text{ has moderate growth}}

\dfrac{m_{k_{j+1}}}{m_{k_{j}}}

\scriptstyle{\exists n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}:~\liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac{m_{nj}}{m_{j}}>1}

For instance, for all Gevrey sequences

,

is bounded if and only if

[

]
states that (

) holds if

is bounded and

satisfies (

).

,

.

, one may ask if

and

with analogous properties can be found for

given sequence
(k_{j})
such that

[

]
alluded to in

Remark 6.6
.

Liess (see

4.4
) works with

based bounds of Roumieu type, but his arguments apply to

Let
\mathfrak{M}=\{M^{(s)}:s>0\}
be a totally ordered family of weight sequences such that
\lim_{j\to\infty}(M^{(s)}_{j})^{1/j}=\infty
for all
s>0
.
We claim that there exists a weight sequence
N=(N_{j})
and sequences of positive integers
(k_{j})
and
(\ell_{j})

with the following properties:

(1)

\cdots<\ell_{j}<k_{j}<\ell_{j+1}<k_{j+1}<\cdots

(2)

, and

or
[

).

On the other hand,

(22)

\mathcal{B}^{(\mathfrak{M})}_{L^{\infty},(k_{j})}(\mathbb{R})\neq\mathcal{B}^{(\mathfrak{M})}_{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R})\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{B}^{\{\mathfrak{M}\}}_{L^{\infty},(k_{j})}(\mathbb{R})\neq\mathcal{B}^{\{\mathfrak{M}\}}_{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}).

) for
\mathbb{R}
replaced with any interval

.

Let us construct
N=(N_{j})
,
(k_{j})
, and
(\ell_{j})
with the desired properties.
Let
N_{0}:=1
. We will define
N_{j}
in terms of
\nu_{j}:=N_{j}/N_{j-1}
for
j\geq 1
.
For simplicity of notation, we put
A_{j}:=2^{2^{j}}
.

Let us first assume that
(k_{j})
and
(\ell_{j})
are arbitrary positive sequences of integers satisfying (1).
We will define
\nu_{j}
such that (2) and (3) hold. In the end, we discuss how
(k_{j})
and
(\ell_{j})
must be chosen such that
(\nu_{j})

is increasing, i.e.,
N=(N_{j})
is a weight sequence.

For
j\geq 1
, set

\displaystyle\nu_{k_{j}}

\displaystyle:=\Big(\frac{M^{(1/j)}_{k_{j}}}{j^{k_{j}}A_{\ell_{j}}M^{(j)}_{\ell_{j}}}\Big)^{1/(k_{j}-\ell_{j})},

\displaystyle\nu_{\ell_{j+1}}

\displaystyle:=\Big(\frac{j^{k_{j}}A_{\ell_{j+1}}M^{(j+1)}_{\ell_{j+1}}}{M^{(1/j)}_{k_{j}}}\Big)^{1/(\ell_{j+1}-k_{j})},

\displaystyle\nu_{k}

\displaystyle:=\nu_{k_{j}},\quad\text{ for }\ell_{j}+1\leq k\leq k_{j},

\displaystyle\nu_{k}

\displaystyle:=\nu_{\ell_{j+1}},\quad\text{ for }k_{j}+1\leq k\leq\ell_{j+1}.

Additionally,

\displaystyle\nu_{1}=\cdots=\nu_{\ell_{1}}:=(A_{\ell_{1}}M^{(1)}_{\ell_{1}})^{1/\ell_{1}}.

By construction, (2) and (3) are satisfied.
Taking

large enough we have

(since
(M^{(1)}_{j})^{1/j}\to\infty
).
Next let us check that we may choose
k_{1}<\ell_{2}<k_{3}<\cdots
in such a way that
(\nu_{j})
is increasing.
More precisely, we have to make sure that

\nu_{\ell_{j}}\leq\nu_{k_{j}}\leq\nu_{\ell_{j+1}}\quad\text{ for all }j\geq 1.

It is easy to see that taking

sufficiently large gives

(since
(M^{(1)}_{j})^{1/j}\to\infty

Next
\nu_{k_{j}}\leq\nu_{\ell_{j+1}}
for

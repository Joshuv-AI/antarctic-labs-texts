On the Benjamin and related equations
Christian Klein, Felipe Linares, Didier Pilod, Jean-Claude Saut

Abstract
. We consider in this paper various theoretical and numerical issues on classical one dimensional models of internal waves with surface tension.They concern the Cauchy problem, including the long time dynamic, localized solitons or multisolitons, the soliton resolution property. We survey known results, present a few new ones together with open questions and conjectures motivated by numerical simulations. A major issue is to emphasize the differences of the qualitative behavior of solutions with those of the same equations without the capillary term.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The models

This paper is concerned with one-dimensional asymptotic models for weakly nonlinear internal waves in presence of surface tension.

In absence of surface tension, classical one-dimensional models for long, weakly nonlinear waves at the interface of a two-layer system when the lower layer is much larger than the upper one comprise the Benjamin-Ono (BO) equation derived in
[

77
]

[

(1.1)

u_{t}+uu_{x}-Hu_{xx}=0,

where H is the Hilbert transform defined as the convolution with
PV(\frac{1}{x}),
that is
\widehat{Hf}(\xi)=-i\text{sgn}(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi),

(note that here the symbol of
H\partial_{x}
is
|\xi|

-i\xi|\xi|)
, and the Intermediate Long Wave (ILW) equation derived in
[

(1.2)

u_{t}+uu_{x}+\frac{1}{\delta}u_{x}-\mathcal{T_{\delta}}u_{x}=0,

where the “Tilbert transform”  is defined in Fourier variables by:

\widehat{\mathcal{T}_{\delta}f}(\xi)=\xi\coth(\delta\xi)\hat{f}(\xi).

The BO equation corresponds to the limit of an infinite lower layer,
\delta\to\infty

One also finds in

where
\delta_{1}\neq\delta_{2}.

2.
The derivation

We now recall the known derivation of long, weakly nonlinear internal waves models including surface tension.

In
[
17
]
Benjamin derived a version of the BO equation when the
interface of the two layers is subject to surface tension. The modeling depends basically on the assumption that
T/g(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})h^{2}\gg 1,
where
T
is the interfacial surface tension,
\rho_{2}-\rho_{1},
the difference between the densities and
h
the undisturbed thickness of the upper layer. This assumption is satisfied for instance when fluid densities are nearly equal.

We briefly describe here Benjamin’s formal argument (see also

[
52
]
). One considers a two-fluid system made of a layer of
fluid of thickness
h
and density
\rho_{1}
over an infinitely deep
incompressible fluid of density
\rho_{2}>\rho_{1},
the less dense fluid
being bounded from above by a rigid horizontal plane, the interface being subject of surface tension with
coefficient
T>0.
In this situation, the phase velocity

c(k)=\frac{\omega(k)}{k}
corresponding to plane waves
\epsilon\exp\{i(\omega t-kx)\})
reads

c^{2}(k)=\frac{g(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})+Tk^{2}}{\rho_{1}k\coth kh+\rho_{2}|k|}.

For waves propagating in the positive direction (
c>0
) the asymptotic approximation of
c(k)
for very long waves (
|k|\ll 1)
gives

c(k)=c_{0}\left[1-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho_{1}}h|k|+\frac{1}{2}\left\{\frac{T}{g(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})}+\left(\frac{3}{4}\frac{\rho_{2}^{2}}{\rho^{2}_{1}}-\frac{1}{6}\right)h^{2}\right\}k^{2}+O(|k|^{2})\right].

For long waves,
|k|^{2}
is much smaller that
|k|
, and thus the term in brackets can be neglected leading to the Benjamin-Ono equation. On the other hand, if it is assumed that
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
are very close, that is
\frac{T}{g(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})}h^{2}\gg 1,
Benjamin writes the approximate dispersion

c(k)=1-\alpha|k|+\beta k^{2},

in which

\alpha=\frac{h}{2}\rho_{2}/\rho_{1},\quad\beta=\frac{h^{2}}{2}T/g(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1}).

This leads to the modified BO equation, known as the Benjamin equation, again in the notations of
[
17
]
2
2

2

In a frame moving with velocity
c_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{\rho_{2}-\rho_{1}}{\rho_{2}}}
:

(2.1)

u_{t}+uu_{x}-\alpha Hu_{xx}-\beta u_{xxx}=0,

where
\beta>0
is proportional to the surface tension coefficient.
We have kept the parameter
\alpha>0
to compare the effects of the
two dispersive terms. The linear symbol is now

i\xi(\beta\xi^{2}-\alpha|\xi|)
so that the phase velocity has no
longer a constant sign, contrary to the KdV or BO equations and
apriori
solitary waves can travel in both direction.

We refer to
[
17
]
and
[
4
]
for further discussions of the physical regime of validity of Benjamin equation.

Remark 2.1
.

As aforementioned the derivation of Benjamin equation in
[
17
,
18
]
is formal and a rigorous derivation in the sense of consistency is still lacking. The derivation in
[
52
]
, is also formal but Kalish derived the following
system
version of the Benjamin equation for bi-directional waves in the same physical context

(2.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\zeta_{t}+u_{x}+\sigma(u\zeta)_{x}=0\\
\displaystyle u_{t}+\zeta_{x}+\sigma uu_{x}-\epsilon\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho_{1}}H\zeta_{xx}-\mu\zeta_{xxx}=0.\\
\end{array}\right.

This system appears to be a perturbation of one of the
abcd
Boussinesq systems derived in
[
23
]
corresponding to
b=d=0,a=0,c<0
and which belongs to ”Schrödinger type” abcd systems (the dispersion is of order 2).

More precisely this (abcd) Boussinesq system is a particular case of an Euler-Korteweg system, see the survey article
[
84
]
for known results on this system. We will comment on (
2.2
) in Chapter 7.

In
[
14
]
C.T. Anh, extending the results in
[
19
]
for internal waves without surface tension, derived rigorously in the sense of consistency from the two-layer system asymptotic systems in various regimes when surface tension is taken into account. In the Benjamin-Ono regime, he derived in the one-dimensional case the following system:

(2.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle(1+\alpha\frac{\sqrt{\mu}}{3}|\partial_{x}|)\partial_{t}\zeta+\frac{1}{\gamma}\partial_{x}((1-\epsilon\zeta)v)-\frac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\gamma^{2}}\partial_{x}((1-\epsilon\zeta)|\partial_{x}|v)\\
\displaystyle+\frac{\epsilon\sqrt{\mu}}{\gamma^{2}}|\partial_{x}|\partial_{x}(\zeta v)+\left[(1-\alpha)\frac{\mu}{3\gamma}-\frac{\mu}{\gamma^{3}}\right]\partial_{x}^{3}v=0\\
\displaystyle\partial_{t}v+(1-\gamma)\partial_{x}\zeta-\frac{\epsilon}{\gamma}v\partial_{x}v+\frac{\epsilon\sqrt{\mu}}{\gamma^{2}}\partial_{x}(v(|\partial_{x}|v))-\epsilon\sqrt{\mu}\nu\partial_{x}^{3}\zeta=0,\\
\end{array}\right.

where
\epsilon\sim\sqrt{\mu}\ll 1
are small parameters measuring respectively nonlinear and dispersive effects,
\gamma<1
is the ratio of the densities of the two layers,
\nu>0
measures the capillarity effects and
\alpha\geq 0
is a modeling parameter.

The classical BO system (without surface tension, see
[
19
]
) is obtained from (
2.3
) when omitting the terms of higher order
\mu,\sqrt{\mu}\epsilon
and thus reads:

(2.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle(1+\alpha\frac{\sqrt{\mu}}{3}|\partial_{x}|)\partial_{t}\zeta+\frac{1}{\gamma}\partial_{x}((1-\epsilon\zeta)v)-(1-\alpha)\frac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\gamma^{2}}\partial_{x}|\partial_{x}|v=0\\
\displaystyle\partial_{t}v+(1-\gamma)\partial_{x}\zeta-\frac{\epsilon}{\gamma}v\partial_{x}v=0.\\
\end{array}\right.

Both systems are linearly well-posed when
\alpha\geq 1.

As noticed in
[
19
]
, neglecting the
O(\sqrt{\mu})=O(\epsilon)
terms in (
2.4
), one finds that
\zeta
must solve a wave equation with speed
c=\sqrt{\frac{1-\gamma}{\gamma}}.

Under a one-way assumption, (
2.4
) yields the one-parameter family of equations:

(2.5)

\left(1+\sqrt{\mu}\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}|\partial_{x}|\right)\partial_{t}\zeta+c\partial_{x}\zeta-\epsilon\frac{3}{4}c\partial_{x}\zeta^{2}-\frac{\sqrt{\mu}}{2\gamma}c(1-2\alpha)|\partial_{x}|\partial_{x}\zeta=0.

The usual BO equation is recovered when
\alpha=0.

Another derivation of a system version of the Benjamin equation is made in
[
39
]
where the following system is derived, here in its one-dimensional version

(2.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{\alpha\sqrt{\mu}}{\gamma}|\partial_{x}|\right)\partial_{t}\zeta+\frac{1}{\gamma}\partial_{x}((1+\epsilon\zeta)v)-(1-\alpha)\frac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\gamma^{2}}|\partial_{x}|\partial_{x}v=0,\\
\displaystyle\partial_{t}v+(1-\gamma)\partial_{x}\zeta-\frac{\epsilon}{\gamma}v\partial_{x}v=T\partial_{x}^{3}v,\end{array}\right.

where the surface tension parameter
T
is given by

T=\frac{\sigma\mu}{g\rho_{2}d_{1}^{2}}.

In absence of surface tension, (
2.6
) is the Benjamin-Ono system (33) derived in
[
19
]
. When
\mu=0,
(
2.6
) is the (abcd) system in
[
23
]
corresponding to
a=b=d=0,c<0.

As noticed in
[
39
]
, (
2.6
) is linearly well-posed if
\alpha\geq 1.

An interesting point concerning the Benjamin equation (
2.1
) is
that it links two completely integrable equations (though being probably not integrable itself), and it thus appears to be a good candidate to test well-known features of integrable equations such as the soliton resolution.

As Benjamin did for the Benjamin-Ono equation, one also can take into account surface tension effects in the ILW equations (
1.2
), (
1.3
), yielding respectively

(2.7)

u_{t}+uu_{x}+\frac{1}{\delta}u_{x}-\mathcal{T}_{\delta}(u_{x})-\beta u_{xxx}=0,

(2.8)

u_{t}+uu_{x}+\frac{1}{\delta_{1}}u_{x}-\mathcal{T}_{\delta_{1}}(u_{x})+\frac{1}{\delta_{2}}u_{x}-\mathcal{T}_{\delta_{2}}(u_{x})-\beta u_{xxx}=0,

From now on we will refer to (
2.8
) as the
ILW-Benjamin (ILW-B) equation.
As does the Benjamin equation this equation connects two integrable equations, the KdV and the ILW equations yielding interesting issues on the long time behavior of solutions.

Remark 2.2
.

We will not comment more on the derivation of equations (
2.7
), (
2.8
) but the formal approach of Benjamin provides a justification. We refer again to
[
14
]
for a rigorous derivation (in terms of consistency) of system versions (in both one and two spacial dimensions) of the BO and ILW equations following the approach of
[
19
]
for the purely gravity waves.

As aforementioned their one-way propagation versions do not reduce to the Benjamin or ILW-B equations, they contain higher order terms that are neglected in the Benjamin or ILW-B equations. The situation here is somewhat similar to the formal derivation of the Kawahara equation which modifies the KdV equation

(2.9)

\eta_{t}+c_{0}\eta_{x}+\frac{3c_{0}}{2h}\eta\eta_{x}+\frac{1}{2}c_{0}h^{2}(\frac{1}{3}-\tau)\eta_{xxx}=0,

when the Bond number
\tau
is very close to
\frac{1}{3}.
This leads (see for instance
[
46
]
) to the Kawahara equation

(2.10)

\eta_{t}+c_{0}\eta_{x}+\frac{3c_{0}}{2h}\eta\eta_{x}+\frac{1}{2}c_{0}h^{2}(\frac{1}{3}-\tau)\eta_{xxx}+\frac{1}{4}c_{0}h^{4}(\frac{14}{90}-\tau^{2})\partial_{x}^{5}\eta=0,

where higher order nonlinear terms are neglected.

The equations (
2.7
), (
2.1
) can be seen as perturbations of the Korteweg-de Vries equation (KdV)

(2.11)

u_{t}+uu_{x}-\beta u_{xxx}=0.

They can alternatively be written in the form

(2.12)

u_{t}+uu_{x}-\mathcal{L}u_{x}-\beta u_{xxx}=0,

where
\mathcal{L}
is defined in Fourier space by

\widehat{\mathcal{L}f}(\xi)=p(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi),

with
p(\xi)=|\xi|
for equation (
1.1
),
p(\xi)=p_{\delta}(\xi)=\xi\coth(\delta\xi)-\frac{1}{\delta}
for equation (
1.2
) and

p_{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}}(\xi)=\xi\coth(\delta_{1}\xi)+\xi\coth(\delta_{2}\xi)-\frac{1}{\delta_{1}}-\frac{1}{\delta_{2}}
for equation (
2.7
).

To make more clear the interaction of the two dispersive terms we introduce a parameter
\alpha>0
and rewrite the equations:

(2.13)

u_{t}+uu_{x}-\alpha Hu_{xx}-\beta u_{xxx}=0,

(2.14)

u_{t}+uu_{x}+\alpha\left(\frac{1}{\delta}u_{x}-\mathcal{T}_{\delta}(u_{x})\right)-\beta u_{xxx}=0.

and

(2.15)

u_{t}+uu_{x}+\alpha\left(\frac{1}{\delta_{1}}u_{x}-\mathcal{T}_{\delta_{1}}(u_{x})+\frac{1}{\delta_{2}}u_{x}-\mathcal{T}_{\delta_{2}}(u_{x})\right)-\beta u_{xxx}=0,

Note, however, that contrary to the KdV equation the phase velocity
c(\xi)=\frac{\omega(\xi)}{\xi}
where
\omega
is the dispersion relation has a non constant sign. For instance, for equation (
2.13
) one finds
c(\xi)=\beta\xi^{2}-\alpha|\xi|
and
c(\xi)=\beta\xi^{2}-\alpha(\xi\coth(\delta\xi)-\frac{1}{\delta})
for (
2.14
).

This fact has an influence on the admissible velocities of solitary wave solutions, see
[
17
,
18
]
and below.

Another important point is that none of those equations appear to be completely integrable contrary to the KdV, Benjamin-Ono or Intermediate Long Wave equations. They are thus
very good examples to test the expected soliton resolution conjecture.

Remark 2.3
.

There are interesting Kadomtsev-Petviashvili (KP) versions of those one-dimensional models, that is taking into account weak transverse effects, which should be useful to study the transverse stability of the one-dimensional solitary waves.

Proceeding as in the formal derivation of the classical Kadomtsev-Petviashvili equation in
[
51
]
(see for instance
[
57
,
56
]
for a formal derivation of (
2.16
) from the two-layer system for the BO case) one obtains respectively

(2.16)

u_{t}+uu_{x}-\alpha Hu_{xx}-\beta u_{xxx}+\partial_{x}^{-1}u_{yy}=0,

(2.17)

u_{t}+uu_{x}+\alpha\left(\frac{1}{\delta}u_{x}-\mathcal{T}_{\delta}(u_{x})\right)-\beta u_{xxx}+\partial_{x}^{-1}u_{yy}=0,

and

(2.18)

u_{t}+uu_{x}+\alpha\left(\frac{1}{\delta_{1}}u_{x}-\mathcal{T}_{\delta_{1}}(u_{x})+\frac{1}{\delta_{2}}u_{x}-\mathcal{T}_{\delta_{2}}(u_{x})\right)-\beta u_{xxx}+\partial_{x}^{-1}u_{yy}=0.

We refer to a subsequent work for a study of these equations that are mathematically interesting since they have both a focusing and defocusing aspect (depending on frequencies). The KP versions of the Benjamin-Ono and ILW equations are studied in
[

but many issues remain open.

Note also that due to the second order nonlocal dispersive operator, the KP-Benjamin type equations do not possess the scaling invariance property

u_{\lambda}(t,x,y)=\lambda^{2}u(\lambda^{3}t,\lambda x,\lambda^{2}y),\;\lambda>0

of the usual KP equation.

3.
Analysis

The aim of this section is to review the known results on the Cauchy problem and on solitary wave solutions for the Benjamin equation and to extend some of them to the ILW-Benjamin equation.

3.1.
The Cauchy problem

Concerning the Cauchy problem for the Benjamin equation, Linares has proven in
[
69
]
that (
2.1
) is globally well-posed for initial data in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
and also, in a periodic setting, in
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}).

Well-posedness below
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
was obtained in
[
30
,
64
,
68
]
leading to global well-posedness in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}),s\geq-3/4.

We now briefly explain how these results extend to (
2.7
), (
2.8
). By writing these equations on the form (1.3) in
[
76
]
, we observe that the symbol of the respective linear symbols of these equations

p_{ILW-B}=\beta\xi^{3}-(\coth{\delta xi}\xi-\frac{1}{\delta})\xi
and
p_{mILW-B}=\beta\xi^{3}-(\coth{\delta_{1}xi}\xi-\frac{1}{\delta_{1}})\xi-(\coth{\delta_{2}xi}\xi-\frac{1}{\delta_{2}})\xi
satisfy Hypothesis 1 in
[
76
]
(this is a direct consequence of Lemma 2.1 in
[
76
]
). Therefore, we deduce from Theorem 1.5 and Corollary 1.10 in
[
76
]
that the Cauchy problems associated with (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) are globally well-posed in
L^{2}(M)
with
M=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{T}
.

While
the global well-posedness is well established for those equations, the global behavior of solutions is unknown and challenging. Being in some sense an “interpolate” equation between two integrable ones, one might expect that the soliton resolution holds. A first preliminary step towards this conjecture would be the existence and stability of multi-soliton solutions but this seems to be an open question as we will discuss later on.

.

Many papers have been devoted to controllability properties of the KdV or Benjamin-Ono equations, see for instance
[

,
73
]
for the BO case. Concerning the Benjamin equation, the controllability and stabilization were proven in
[

]
for the linearized equation and in

for the equation itself, both on a periodic domain.

It would be interesting to extend those results to (

3.2.
Solitary waves

We refer to
[
7
,
4
,
12
]
for existence and stability properties of solitary waves to (
2.1
), and to the end of the present chapter for extensions to (
2.7
), (
2.8
).

Note that due to the lack of regularity of the symbol of (
2.1
) the associated (non explicit) solitary waves have only an algebraic decay while the decay is exponential for the two other equations.

We first review and complete known results on the existence and properties of solitary waves, starting by the BO case that is for the equation (
2.13
). A solitary wave solution
u(x,t)=v(x-ct)
of (
2.13
) satisfies the equation

(3.1)

-cv-\beta v_{xx}-\alpha|D|v+\frac{v^{2}}{2}=0

yielding the energy equality

(3.2)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(-cv^{2}+\beta v_{x}^{2}-\alpha|D^{1/2}v|^{2}+\frac{v^{3}}{2}\right)dx=0.

A Pohojaev identity is obtained by multiplying (
3.1
) by
xv_{x}
and integration (justification by the usual smoothing and truncation process). We observe by Plancherel and integration by parts that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}xv_{x}|D|vdx=0

and obtain

(3.3)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\frac{c}{2}v^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}v_{x}^{2}-\frac{v^{3}}{6}\right)dx=0

and by combination of those identities

(3.4)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\frac{c}{2}v^{2}+\frac{5\beta}{2}v_{x}^{2}-\alpha|D^{1/2}v|^{2}\right)dx=0,

that is in Fourier variables

(3.5)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\frac{c}{2}+\frac{5\beta}{2}|\xi|^{2}-\alpha|\xi|\right)|\hat{v}|^{2}d\xi=0.

We recover here the well-known fact that for the pure KdV equation (
\alpha=0
) there exists a unique depression solitary wave with velocity c strictly negative (mind the minus sign in front of
u_{xxx}!
) while for the pure Benjamin-Ono equation (
\beta=0
) there exists a unique elevation solitary wave with a strictly positive velocity.

In the present case, (
3.5
) implies that no solitary wave with
large velocities exists, that is when
c>\frac{\alpha^{2}}{5\beta}
and a natural conjecture is that solitary waves solutions of (
3.1
)
exist with velocities
c\in(-\infty,\frac{\alpha^{2}}{5\beta})
.

The existence of solitary wave solutions of the Benjamin equation was sketched in
[
17
]
by minimizing the Hamiltonian with fixed
L^{2}
norm.
Using a Leray-Schauder degree theory, Benjamin
[
18
]
proved the existence of solitary waves (and also of space periodic travelling waves)
for small values of the parameter
\alpha/\sqrt{\beta c}
, by perturbation of a KdV soliton.

Chen and Bona,
[
27
]
, using a concentration-compactness approach, proved the existence of solitary waves of velocity c provided
\min_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}\{x^{2}-\alpha|x|+c\}>0,
which implies in our notations
c<-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4\beta}.

The later paper contains also a rigorous asymptotics of the solitary waves following the general theory of Bona and Li,
[
21
]
, namely as the (explicit) soliton of the Benjamin-Ono equation, any solitary wave solution of the Benjamin equation has the asymptotics

\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}x^{2}\phi(x)=C,

for some constant
C\in{\mathbb{R}},C\neq 0.

In
[
12
]
Angulo minimizes the energy functional

E(\psi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\beta\psi_{x}^{2}-\psi H\psi_{x}-\frac{1}{3}\psi^{3}\right)dx

under the constraint

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi^{2}dx=\lambda>0.

This yields, for any
\lambda>0
small enough, the existence of a nonempty set
G_{\lambda}
of solitary waves with positive stability which is orbitally stable.

Remark 3.2
.

In
[
13
]
, Angulo proved the instability of solitary waves of the
generalized
Benjamin equation, extending similar known results for the generalized KdV equation.

To summarize the above results, existence and stability of solitary waves for the Benjamin equation is known in the velocity range

-\infty<c<-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4\beta}

and non existence in the range

c>\frac{\alpha^{2}}{5\beta}.

Existence for velocities satisfying

-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4\beta}<c<\frac{\alpha^{2}}{5\beta}

is therefore an open problem that will be addressed numerically in the present paper. Note again that the possibility of existence of solitary waves with positive velocities is due to the BO term in the Benjamin equation.

Remark 3.3
.

A stationary solution of the Benjamin equation should satisfy

(3.6)

-\beta v_{xx}-\alpha|D|v+\frac{v^{2}}{2}=0.

While no such non trivial solutions exist by (
3.5
) in the pure KdV or Benjamin-Ono case, the Pohajaev type argument does not exclude the possibility of existence of steady solutions for the Benjamin equation. This issue appears to be open.

Remark 3.4
.

Concerning the qualitative properties of the Benjamin solitary waves we already mentioned that Bona and Chen

In this subsection we are concerned with unique continuation principles (UCP) satisfied for solutions to the equations considered in this paper.

In

Suppose that

(Theorem 1.4
[

)
.

(Theorem 1.1
[

Finite and Symmetric Euler Sums and Finite and Symmetric (Alternating) Multiple T T -Values
Jianqiang Zhao

Abstract




Abstract.

In this paper, we will study finite multiple
T
-values (MTVs) and their alternating versions, which are level two and level four variations of finite multiple zeta values, respectively. We will first provide some structural results for level two finite multiple zeta values (i.e., finite Euler sums) for small weights, guided by the author’s previous conjecture that the finite Euler sum space of weight,
w
, is isomorphic to a quotient Euler sum space of weight,
w
. Then, by utilizing some well-known properties of the classical alternating MTVs, we will derive a few important
\mathbb{Q}
-linear relations among the finite alternating MTVs, including the reversal, linear shuffle, and sum relations. We then compute the upper bound for the dimension of the
\mathbb{Q}
-span of finite (alternating) MTVs for some small weights by rigorously using the newly discovered relations, numerically aided by computers.

Keywords.

(finite) Euler sums; symmetric Euler sums; (finite) multiple
T
-values; symmetric multiple
T
-values; alternating multiple
T
-values.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification.
11M32; 11B68; 68W30.

Introduction

In
[
10
]
, Kaneko and Tsumura proposed a study of
multiple
T
-values
(MTVs):

\displaystyle T({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}>\dots>n_{d}>0\\
n_{j}\equiv d-j+1{\ (\rm{mod}\ 2)}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{1}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}},\quad{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d},

(1)

as level two variations of
multiple zeta values

(MZVs), which, in turn, were first studied
by Zagier
[
24
]
and Hoffman
[
3
]
independently:

\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=\sum_{n_{1}>\dots>n_{d}>0}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{1}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}},\quad{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d},

(2)

where
\mathbb{N}
is the set of positive integers.
These series converge if and only if
s_{1}\geq 2
, in which case we say
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}
is admissible. As usual, we call

|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|:=s_{1}+\cdots+s_{d}
the weight and
d
the depth. Note that the series becomes Riemann zeta values
when
d=1
. One of the most important properties of these values is that they can be expressed by iterated integrals.
The main motivation to consider MTVs is that they
are also equipped with the following iterated integral expressions:

T({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{s_{1}-1}\frac{dt}{1-t^{2}}\cdots\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{s_{d}-1}\frac{dt}{1-t^{2}}

(3)

which provide the MTVs with a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra structure because of the shuffle product property satisfied by
iterated integral multiplication (see, e.g.,
[
27
, Lemma 2.1.2(iv)]
).

In addition to MTVs, many other variants of MZVs have been studied due to their
important connections to a variety of objects in both mathematics and theoretical physics.
For example, Yamamoto
[
23
]
defined the interpolated
version of MZVs, which connects ordinary MZVs to the starred version;
Hoffman
[
4
]
defined an odd variant by restricting the summation
indices
n_{j}
’s to odd numbers only; Xu and the author
[
21
,
22
]
further extended
both Kaneko-Tsumura and Hoffman’s versions to allow for all possible parity patterns.

On the other hand, the congruence properties of the partial sums of MZVs were first considered by
Hoffman
[
6
]
and the author
[
26
]
independently.
Contrary to the classical cases,
only a few variants of these sums exist (see, e.g.,
[
9
,
12
,
17
]
).
In this paper, the author will concentrate on the finite analog of MTVs defined by (
1
).

Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of primes. Then by putting

\mathcal{A}:=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})\bigg/\bigoplus_{p\in\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}),

(4)

we can define the
finite multiple zeta values
(FMZVs) according to the following:

\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=\bigg(\sum_{p>n_{1}>\dots>n_{d}>0}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{1}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}}\pmod{p}\bigg)_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\in\mathcal{A}.

Nowadays, the main motivation for studying FMZVs is to understand a deep conjecture
proposed by Kaneko and Zagier around 2014 (see Conjecture
1.1
below for a generalization).
Although this conjecture is far from being proved, many parallel results have been shown to hold for both
MZVs and FMZVs simultaneously (see, e.g.,
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
).
In particular, for each positive integer
w\geq 2
, the element

\beta_{w}:=\Big(\frac{B_{p-w}}{w}\Big)_{w<p\in\mathcal{P}}\in\mathcal{A}

(5)

is the finite analog of
\zeta(w)
, where
B_{n}
’s are the Bernoulli numbers defined by

\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}=\sum_{n\geq 0}B_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

which have played very important roles in many areas of mathematical studies, such as Clifford analysis
[
2
]
and topology
[
11
]
.

Furthermore, the connection goes even further to their alternating versions — the Euler sums and finite Euler sums.
For
s_{1},\dots,s_{d}\in\mathbb{N}
and
{\sigma}_{1},\dots,{\sigma}_{d}=\pm 1
, we define the
Euler sums

\zeta(s_{1},\dots,s_{d};{\sigma}_{1},\dots,{\sigma}_{d}):=\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{d}>0}\;\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{{\sigma}_{j}^{n_{j}}}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}}.

In order to save space, if
{\sigma}_{j}=-1
, then
\overline{s}_{j}
will be used, and if a substring,
S
, repeats
n

times in the list, then
\{S\}^{n}
will be used.
For example, the finite analog of
-\zeta(\bar{1})=-\zeta(1;-1)=\log 2
is the Fermat quotient

{\texttt{q}}_{2}:=\Big(\frac{2^{p-1}-1}{p}\pmod{p}\Big)_{3\leq p\in\mathcal{P}}\in\mathcal{A}.

(6)

Write
\sgn(\bar{s})=-1
and
|\bar{s}|=s
if
s\in\mathbb{N}
. For
s_{1},\ldots,s_{d}\in\mathbb{D}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\bar{\mathbb{N}}
,
we can define the finite
Euler sums
as

\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=\bigg(\sum_{p>n_{1}>\dots>n_{d}>0}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\sgn(s_{j})^{n_{j}}}{n_{j}^{|s_{j}|}}\pmod{p}\bigg)_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\in\mathcal{A}.

In
[
27
, Conjecture 8.6.9]
, we extended the Kakeko–Zagier conjecture to the setting of the Euler sums.
For
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{D}^{d}
, define the symmetric version of the alternating Euler sums

\displaystyle\zeta_{\sharp}^{\mathcal{S}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{d}\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{i}(-1)^{|s_{j}|}\sgn(s_{j})\bigg)\zeta_{\sharp}(s_{i},\dots,s_{1})\zeta_{\sharp}(s_{i+1},\dots,s_{d})

where
\zeta_{\sharp}
(
\sharp=\ast
or
\shuffle
) are regularized values (see
[
27
, Proposition 13.3.8]
).
They are called
\sharp
-regularized
symmetric Euler sums
. If
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, then they are called

\sharp
-regularized
symmetric multiple zeta values
(SMZVs).

Conjecture 1.1

(cf.
[
27
, Conjecture 8.6.9]
)
.

For any
w\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathsf{FES}_{w}
(resp.
\mathsf{ES}_{w}
) be the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space generated by
all finite Euler sums (resp. Euler sums) of weight

. Then, there is an isomorphism:

\displaystyle f_{\mathsf{ES}}:\mathsf{FES}_{w}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\frac{\mathsf{ES}_{w}}{\zeta(2)\mathsf{ES}_{w-2}},

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})

\displaystyle\longmapsto\zeta_{\sharp}^{\mathcal{S}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}),

where
\sharp=\ast
or
\shuffle
.

We remark that
\zeta_{\shuffle}^{\mathcal{S}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})-\zeta_{\ast}^{\mathcal{S}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})
always lies in
\zeta(2)\mathsf{ES}_{w-2}
, see
[
27
]
, Exercise 8.7.
Thus, it does not matter which version of the symmetric Euler sums is used in the conjecture.

Problem 1.2
.

What is the correct generalization of
[
27
, Theorem 6.3.5]
for symmetric Euler sums?
What is the correct extension of
[
27
, Theorem 8.5.10]

Our primary motivation for studying alternating MTVs is to better understand this mysterious relation,
f_{\mathsf{ES}}
.
One of the main results of this paper is the discovery of the linear shuffle relations among the finite alternating
MTVs given by Theorem

We now briefly describe the content of this paper. We will start the next section by defining finite MTVs and

, respectively.
The most useful property of MTVs is that they have the iterated integral expressions (

satisfying shuffle multiplication. This leads us to the discovery of the linear shuffle relations

of the weight

2
Symmetric and Finite Multiple
T
-Values

It turns out that finite MTVs are closely related to another variant called finite MSVs.
For all admissible
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, we define the
finite multiple
T
-values
(FMTVs)
and the
finite multiple
S
-values
(FMSVs) as

\displaystyle F_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p>n_{1}>\dots>n_{d}>0\\
n_{j}\equiv d-j+1{\ (\rm{mod}\ 2)}\ \text{if }F=T,\\
n_{j}\equiv d-j{\ (\rm{mod}\ 2)}\ \text{if }F=S\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{1}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}}\pmod{p}\bigg)_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\in\mathcal{A}.

It is clear that

F_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{{\sigma}_{1},\dots,{\sigma}_{d}=\pm 1}\bigg(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq d\\
2|d-j\ \text{if $F=T$}\\
2\nmid d-j\ \text{if $F=S$}\end{subarray}}{\sigma}_{j}\bigg)\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}).

Motivated by Conjecture
1.1
, we provide the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
. Let
F=S
or
T
.
We define the
\sharp
-regularized MTVs

(
\sharp=\ast
or
\shuffle
) and
MSVs
as

\displaystyle F_{\sharp}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{{\sigma}_{1},\dots,{\sigma}_{d}=\pm 1}\bigg(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq d\\
2|d-j\ \text{if $F=T$}\\
2\nmid d-j\ \text{if $F=S$}\end{subarray}}{\sigma}_{j}\bigg)\zeta_{\sharp}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})\quad(\text{$F=T$ or $S$}).

We define the
\sharp
-symmetric multiple
T
-values
(SMTVs) and

\sharp
-symmetric multiple
S
-values
(SMSVs) as

F_{\sharp}^{\mathcal{S}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{d}\Big(\prod_{\ell=1}^{i}(-1)^{s_{\ell}}\Big)F_{\sharp}(s_{i},\dots,s_{1})F_{\sharp}(s_{i+1},\dots,s_{d}),&\quad\hbox{if $d$ is even;}\\
\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{d}\Big(\prod_{\ell=1}^{i}(-1)^{s_{\ell}}\Big)\widetilde{F}_{\sharp}(s_{i},\dots,s_{1})F_{\sharp}(s_{i+1},\dots,s_{d}),&\quad\hbox{if $d$ is odd,}\end{array}\right.

where
\widetilde{F}=S+T-F
and we set, as usual,
\prod_{\ell=1}^{0}=1
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose
f_{\mathsf{ES}}
is defined as per Conjecture
1.1
.
Let
\sharp=\ast
or
\shuffle
. Then, for all
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, we have

f_{\mathsf{ES}}T_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=T_{\sharp}^{\mathcal{S}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})
and
f_{\mathsf{ES}}S_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=S_{\sharp}^{\mathcal{S}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})
modulo
\zeta(2)
.

Proof.

Suppose
d
is even and
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
. Then, modulo
\zeta(2)

\displaystyle f_{\mathsf{ES}}T_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{{\varepsilon}_{1},\dots,{\varepsilon}_{d}=\pm 1}\bigg(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq d\\
j\equiv d{\ (\rm{mod}\ 2)}\end{subarray}}{\varepsilon}_{j}\bigg)f_{\mathsf{ES}}\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}\binom{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}}{{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{{\varepsilon}_{1},\dots,{\varepsilon}_{d}=\pm 1}\bigg(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq d\\
2|j\end{subarray}}{\varepsilon}_{j}\bigg)\zeta_{\sharp}^{\mathcal{S}}\binom{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}}{{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{{\varepsilon}_{1},\dots,{\varepsilon}_{d}=\pm 1}\bigg(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq d\\
2|j\end{subarray}}{\varepsilon}_{j}\bigg)\sum_{i=0}^{d}\Big(\prod_{\ell=1}^{i}(-1)^{s_{\ell}}{\varepsilon}_{\ell}\Big)\zeta_{\sharp}\binom{s_{i},\dots,s_{1}}{{\varepsilon}_{i},\dots,{\varepsilon}_{1}}\zeta_{\sharp}\binom{s_{i+1},\dots,s_{d}}{{\varepsilon}_{i+1},\dots,{\varepsilon}_{d}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{i=0}^{d}\bigg(\prod_{\ell=1}^{i}(-1)^{s_{\ell}}\bigg)\sum_{{\varepsilon}_{1},\dots,{\varepsilon}_{d}=\pm 1}\bigg(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq d\\
2|j\end{subarray}}{\varepsilon}_{j}\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{\ell=1}^{i}{\varepsilon}_{\ell}\bigg)\zeta_{\sharp}\binom{s_{i},\dots,s_{1}}{{\varepsilon}_{i},\dots,{\varepsilon}_{1}}\zeta_{\sharp}\binom{s_{i+1},\dots,s_{d}}{{\varepsilon}_{i+1},\dots,{\varepsilon}_{d}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{i=0}^{d}\Big(\prod_{\ell=1}^{i}(-1)^{s_{\ell}}\Big)\bigg(\sum_{{\varepsilon}_{1},\dots,{\varepsilon}_{i}=\pm 1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq i\\
2\nmid j\end{subarray}}{\varepsilon}_{j}\zeta_{\sharp}\binom{s_{i},\dots,s_{1}}{{\varepsilon}_{i},\dots,{\varepsilon}_{1}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\,\hskip 170.71652pt\times\bigg(\sum_{{\varepsilon}_{1},\dots,{\varepsilon}_{i}=\pm 1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j\leq d\\
2|j\end{subarray}}{\varepsilon}_{j}\zeta_{\sharp}\binom{s_{i+1},\dots,s_{d}}{{\varepsilon}_{i+1},\dots,{\varepsilon}_{d}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{i=0}^{d}\Big(\prod_{\ell=1}^{i}(-1)^{s_{\ell}}\Big)T_{\sharp}(s_{i},\dots,s_{1})T_{\sharp}(s_{i+1},\dots,s_{d})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,T_{\sharp}^{\mathcal{S}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}).

The MSVs and the odd
d
cases can all be computed similarly and are left to the interested reader.
∎

Hence, we expect that whenever certain relations hold on the finite side, then the same relations should hold for the
symmetric version, at least modulo
\zeta(2)
, and vice versa. Sometimes, they are valid for the symmetric version even
without modulo
\zeta(2)
. For example, the following reversal relations hold for both types of sums by
[
25
, Propositions 2.8 and 2.9]
. For
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})
, we state
\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}}=(s_{d},\dots,s_{1})
.

Proposition 2.2

(Reversal relations)
.

For all
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, if
d
is even, then

\displaystyle T_{\mathcal{A}}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}})=

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|}T_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})\quad\text{and}\quad S_{\mathcal{A}}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}})=(-1)^{|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|}S_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}),

\displaystyle T_{\ast}^{\mathcal{S}}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}})=

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|}T_{\ast}^{\mathcal{S}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})\quad\text{and}\quad S_{\ast}^{\mathcal{S}}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}})=(-1)^{|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|}S_{\ast}^{\mathcal{S}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}),

and if
d
is odd, then

\displaystyle T_{\mathcal{A}}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}})=

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|}S_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})\quad\text{and}\quad S_{\mathcal{A}}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}})=(-1)^{|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|}T_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}),

\displaystyle T_{\ast}^{\mathcal{S}}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}})=

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|}S_{\ast}^{\mathcal{S}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})\quad\text{and}\quad S_{\ast}^{\mathcal{S}}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}})=(-1)^{|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|}T_{\ast}^{\mathcal{S}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}).

3
Linear Shuffle Relations for Finite Alternating Multiple
T
-Values

One of the most important tools for studying MZVs and Euler sums is to consider the double shuffle relations that
are produced in two ways to express these sums: one as series (by definition) and the other as iterated integrals.
This idea will play the key role in the following discovery of the linear shuffle relations for
finite multiple
T
-values (FMTVs) and their alternating version.

The linear shuffle relations for Euler sums are given in
[
27
, Theorem 8.4.3]
. First, we extend MTVs and FMTVs
to their alternating version. For all admissible
({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\times\{\pm 1\}^{d}
(i.e.,
(s_{1},{\sigma}_{1})\neq(1,1)
),
we define the alternating multiple
T
-values as

T({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}>\dots>n_{d}>0\\
n_{j}\equiv d-j+1{\ (\rm{mod}\ 2)}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{{\sigma}_{j}^{(n_{j}-d+j-1)/2}}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}}.

This is basically the same definition we used in
[
20
]
, except
for a possible sign difference. If we denote the version in loc. cit. as
T^{\prime}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})
, then

T({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})=T^{\prime}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})\prod_{d-j\equiv 0,1{\ (\rm{mod}\ 2)}}{\sigma}_{j}.

(7)

We changed to our new convention in this paper because of the significant simplification in this special case.
However, the old convention is still superior when treating the general alternating multiple mixed values.
Similar to the convention for Euler sums,
we will save space by putting a bar on top of
s_{j}
if
{\sigma}_{j}=-1
. For example,

T(\bar{2},1)=\sum_{n>m>0}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{(2n-2)^{2}(2m-1)}.

In order to study the alternating MTVs, it is to our advantage to consider the
alternating multiple
T
-functions

of one variable, as follows: for any real number,
x
, define

T({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\sigma}};x):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}>\dots>n_{d}>0\\
n_{j}\equiv d-j+1{\ (\rm{mod}\ 2)}\end{subarray}}x^{n_{1}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{{\sigma}_{j}^{(n_{j}-d+j-1)/2}}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}}.

In the non-alternating case, this function is the A-function (up to a power of 2) used by
Kaneko and Tsumura in
[
10
]
.
For all
\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{d}=\pm 1
, it is then easy to evaluate the iterated integral:

\displaystyle\,\int_{0}^{x}\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{s_{1}-1}\frac{dt}{1-\eta_{1}t^{2}}\cdots\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{s_{d}-1}\frac{dt}{1-\eta_{d}t^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{k_{1}>\dots>k_{d}>0}x^{2(k_{1}+\dots+k_{d})+d}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\eta_{j}^{k_{j}}}{(2k_{j}+2k_{j+1}+\dots+2k_{d}+d-j+1)^{s_{j}}}.

Let

{\texttt{y}}_{0}=\frac{dt}{t},\qquad{\texttt{y}}_{1}=\frac{dt}{1-t^{2}},\qquad{\texttt{y}}_{-1}:=\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}.

By changing the indices
n_{j}=2k_{j}+2k_{j+1}+\dots+2k_{d}+d-j+1
, we immediately obtain

T({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\sigma}};x)=\int_{0}^{x}{\bf p}\Big({\texttt{y}}_{0}^{s_{1}-1}{\texttt{y}}_{{\sigma}_{1}}\cdots{\texttt{y}}_{0}^{s_{d}-1}{\texttt{y}}_{{\sigma}_{d}}\Big):=\int_{0}^{x}{\texttt{y}}_{0}^{s_{1}-1}{\texttt{y}}_{\eta_{1}}\cdots{\texttt{y}}_{0}^{s_{d}-1}{\texttt{y}}_{\eta_{d}},

where
\eta_{j}={\sigma}_{1}\cdots{\sigma}_{j}
for all
j\geq 1
and
{\bf p},{\bf q}

represent the conversions between the series and the integral expressions of
alternating MTVs:

\displaystyle{\bf p}({\bf u}):=

\displaystyle\,{\texttt{y}}_{0}^{s_{1}-1}{\texttt{y}}_{{\sigma}_{1}}\dots{\texttt{y}}_{0}^{s_{j}-1}{\texttt{y}}_{{\sigma}_{1}\cdots{\sigma}_{j}}\dots{\texttt{y}}_{0}^{s_{d}-1}{\texttt{y}}_{{\sigma}_{1}\cdots{\sigma}_{d}},

(8)

\displaystyle{\bf q}({\bf u}):=

\displaystyle\,{\texttt{y}}_{0}^{s_{1}-1}{\texttt{y}}_{{\sigma}_{1}}\dots{\texttt{y}}_{0}^{s_{j}-1}{\texttt{y}}_{{\sigma}_{j}/{\sigma}_{j-1}}\dots{\texttt{y}}_{0}^{s_{d}-1}{\texttt{y}}_{{\sigma}_{d}/{\sigma}_{d-1}}.

(9)

Namely,
{\bf p}
pushes a word used in the series definition to a word used in the integral expression,
whereas
{\bf q}
goes backward. See
[
20
]
for more details.

In order to state the linear shuffle relations among FMTVs and their alternating version,
first, we quickly review the algebra setup
and the corresponding results for Euler sums. Let
{\mathfrak{A}}_{1}^{*}
(resp.
{\mathfrak{A}}_{2}^{*}
) be the

\mathbb{Q}
-algebra of words on
\{{\texttt{x}}_{0},{\texttt{x}}_{1}\}
(resp.
\{{\texttt{x}}_{0},{\texttt{x}}_{1},{\texttt{x}}_{-1}\}
) with concatenation as the product.
Let
{\mathfrak{A}}_{j}^{1}
(
j=1,2
) be the sub-algebra generated by the words not ending with
{\texttt{x}}_{0}
.
Then, for each word
{\bf u}={\texttt{x}}_{0}^{s_{1}-1}{\texttt{x}}_{\eta_{1}}\dots{\texttt{x}}_{0}^{s_{d}-1}{\texttt{x}}_{\eta_{d}}\in{\mathfrak{A}}_{2}^{1}
, we define

\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}({\bf u}):=\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(s_{1},\dots,s_{d};{\sigma}_{1},\dots,{\sigma}_{d})

where
{\sigma}_{1}=\eta_{1}
and
{\sigma}_{j}=\eta_{j}/\eta_{j-1}
for all
j\geq 2
. Set
\tau(1)=1
and

\tau({\texttt{x}}_{0}^{s_{1}-1}{\texttt{x}}_{1}\dots{\texttt{x}}_{0}^{s_{d}-1}{\texttt{x}}_{1})=(-1)^{s_{1}+\dots+s_{r}}{\texttt{x}}_{0}^{s_{d}-1}{\texttt{x}}_{1}\dots{\texttt{x}}_{0}^{s_{1}-1}{\texttt{x}}_{1}.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
27
, Theorem 8.4.3]
)
.

For all words,
{\bf w},{\bf u}\in{\mathfrak{A}}_{1}^{1}
,
{\bf v}\in{\mathfrak{A}}_{2}^{1}
, and
s\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(i)

\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}({\bf u}\shuffle{\bf v})=\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(\tau({\bf u}){\bf v})
;

(ii)

\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(({\bf w}{\bf u})\shuffle{\bf v})=\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}({\bf u}\shuffle\tau({\bf w}){\bf v})
;

(iii)

\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(({\emph{\texttt{x}}}_{0}^{s-1}{\emph{\texttt{x}}}_{1}{\bf u})\shuffle{\bf v})=(-1)^{s}\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}({\bf u}\shuffle({\emph{\texttt{x}}}_{0}^{s-1}{\emph{\texttt{x}}}_{1}{\bf v})).

For alternating MTVs, we can similarly let
{\mathfrak{T}}_{1}^{*}

In this subsection, we will compute finite Euler sums

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
\mathbb{N}_{\text{odd}}
be the set of odd positive integers. For any
d,s\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(\{\bar{s}\}^{d})\in\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{0}\in\mathbb{N},\,k_{1},\dots,k_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{\text{odd}}\\
\delta_{s,1}k_{0}+k_{1}+\dots+k_{\ell}=d\end{subarray}}{\emph{\texttt{q}}}_{2}^{\delta_{s,1}k_{0}}\beta_{sk_{j}}\cdots\beta_{sk_{j}}\mathbb{Q},

where

is the Kronecker symbol. In particular,
\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(\{\bar{s}\}^{d})=0
for all even

Proof.

Let
\Pi=(P_{1},\dots,P_{\ell})\in[d]
denote any partition of
(1,\dots,d)
into odd parts, i.e., all
of
|P_{j}|
’s are odd numbers, where

is the cardinality of the set
P_{j}

\mathcal{C}(\Pi)=(-1)^{d-\ell}(|P_{1}|-1)!\cdots(|P_{\ell}|-1)!.

Observe that
\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(\bar{n})=\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(n)=0
if

Then, it follows easily from
[

, (18)]
that

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(\bar{s})=\sum_{\Pi=(P_{1},\dots,P_{\ell})\in[d]}\mathcal{C}(\Pi)\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}\Big(\overline{s|P_{1}|}\Big)\cdots\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}\Big(\overline{s|P_{\ell}|}\Big).

The proposition follows from (
3.1
) immediately.
∎

We now derive the symmetric MTV version of Proposition

Proposition 3.17
.

For all
d\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

T_{\shuffle}^{\mathcal{S}}(\{1\}^{2d})=0.

Proof.

For any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, we have the relation for the regularized value (see, e.g.,
[

)

\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\bigg(\frac{dt}{1-t^{2}}\bigg)^{\ell}=\frac{1}{\ell!}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{dt}{1-t^{2}}\bigg)^{\ell}=\frac{1}{\ell!}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\bigg(\frac{dt}{1-t}+\frac{dt}{1+t}\bigg)\bigg)^{\ell},

which implies that

T_{\shuffle}(\{1\}^{\ell})=\frac{1}{\ell!2^{\ell}}\Big(\zeta_{\shuffle}(1)+\log 2\Big)^{\ell}.

According to the definition,

T_{\shuffle}^{\mathcal{S}}(\{1\}^{2d})=\sum_{i=0}^{2d}(-1)^{i}T_{\shuffle}(\{1\}^{i})T_{\shuffle}(\{1\}^{2d-i})=\frac{1}{2^{2d}}\sum_{i=0}^{2d}\frac{(-1)^{i}}{\ell!(2d-\ell)!}\Big(\zeta_{\shuffle}(1)+\log 2\Big)^{2d}=0

as desired.
∎

4
Alternating Multiple
T
-Values

We now turn to the alternating version of MTVs and derive some relations among them.
These values are intimately related to the colored MZVs of level 4
(i.e., multiple polylogarithms evaluated for the fourth roots of unity). We refer the interested reader to
[
21
,
22
]

for the fundamental results concerning these values.

Recall that for any
({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\times\{\pm 1\}^{d}
,
we have defined the finite alternating multiple
T
-values as

T({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}):=\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p>n_{1}>\dots>n_{d}>0\\
n_{j}\equiv d-j+1{\ (\rm{mod}\ 2)}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{{\sigma}_{j}^{(n_{j}-d+j-1)/2}}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}}\bigg)_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\in\mathcal{A}.

We have seen from Theorem
3.2
in Section
3
that these values satisfy the
linear shuffle relations. It is also not hard to get the reversal relations when the depth is even, as shown below.

Proposition 4.1

(Reversal relations of finite alternating MTVs)
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
for some even
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,

\displaystyle T_{\mathcal{A}}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}},\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}})=

\displaystyle\,({\sigma}_{1},\dots,{\sigma}_{d})^{(p-1-d)/2}(-1)^{|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|}T_{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}),

(16)

where the element
(-1)^{(p-1-d)/2}=\big((-1)^{(p-1-d)/2}\pmod{p}\big)_{3\leq\in\mathcal{P}}\in\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

Let
p
be an odd prime. Then, by changing the indices
n_{j}\to p-n_{j}
, we obtain

\displaystyle T_{p}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}):=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p>n_{1}>\dots>n_{d}>0\\
n_{j}\equiv d-j+1{\ (\rm{mod}\ 2)}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{{\sigma}_{j}^{(n_{j}-d+j-1)/2}}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p>n_{d}>\dots>n_{1}>0\\
p-n_{j}\equiv d-j+1{\ (\rm{mod}\ 2)}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{{\sigma}_{j}^{(n_{j}-p+d-j+1)/2}}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}}\pmod{p}.

Let
t_{j}=s_{d+1-j}
,
{\varepsilon}_{j}={\sigma}_{d+1-j}
, and
k_{j}=n_{d+1-j}
. Then, by changing the indices, we obtain
j\to d+1-j
(since
d
is even)

\displaystyle T_{p}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})\equiv

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p>n_{1}>\dots>n_{d}>\\
p-k_{j}\equiv j{\ (\rm{mod}\ 2)}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{{\varepsilon}_{j}^{(k_{j}-p+j)/2}}{k_{j}^{t_{j}}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p>n_{1}>\dots>n_{d}>\\
p-k_{j}\equiv j{\ (\rm{mod}\ 2)}\end{subarray}}({\sigma}_{1}\cdots{\sigma}_{d})^{(d-p+1)/2}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{{\varepsilon}_{j}^{(k_{j}-d+j-1)/2}}{k_{j}^{t_{j}}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\,({\sigma}_{1}\cdots{\sigma}_{d})^{(d-p+1)/2}(-1)^{|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p>k_{1}>\dots>k_{d}>\\
k_{j}\equiv d-j+1{\ (\rm{mod}\ 2)}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{{\varepsilon}_{j}^{(k_{j}-d+j-1)/2}}{k_{j}^{t_{j}}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\,({\sigma}_{1}\cdots{\sigma}_{d})^{(d-p+1)/2}(-1)^{|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|}T_{p}({\boldsymbol{\sl{t}}},{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\,({\sigma}_{1}\cdots{\sigma}_{d})^{(d-p+1)/2}(-1)^{|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|}T_{p}(\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}},\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}})

as desired.
∎

It should be clear to the attentive reader that
T
-values are always intimately related to the
S
-values
when the depth is odd because of the reversal relations. Even though we did not consider this in the
above, it plays a key role in the proof of the next result.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
q_{2}(p)=(2^{p-1}-1)/2
for all
p>2
. Then, we have

\displaystyle S_{\mathcal{A}}(\bar{1})=-{\emph{\texttt{q}}}_{2}/2,\quad T_{\mathcal{A}}(\bar{1})=\Big((-1)^{\tfrac{p-1}{2}}q_{2}(p)/2\pmod{p}\Big)_{p>2}\in\mathcal{A}.

Proof.

Recall that

S_{p}(1):=\sum_{p>k>0,2|k}\frac{1}{k},\quad S_{p}(\bar{1}):=\sum_{p>k>0,2|k}\frac{(-1)^{k/2}}{k}.

According to
[
18
, Theorem 3.2]
, we see that

S_{p}(1)+S_{p}(\bar{1})=\sum_{p>k>0,2|k}\bigg(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{(-1)^{k/2}}{k}\bigg)=\sum_{p>k>0,4|k}\frac{2}{k}\equiv-\frac{3}{2}q_{p}(2)\pmod{p}.

Since
S_{p}(1)=\zeta_{p}(\bar{1})/2=-q_{p}(2)
, we immediately see that
S_{\mathcal{A}}(\bar{1})=-{\texttt{q}}_{2}/2
.
By taking the reversal, we obtain

\displaystyle T_{p}(\bar{1})=\sum_{p>k>0,2\nmid k}\frac{(-1)^{(k-1)/2}}{k}=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{p>k>0,2|k}\frac{(-1)^{(p-k-1)/2}}{p-k}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\,-(-1)^{\tfrac{p-1}{2}}S_{p}(\bar{1})\equiv(-1)^{\tfrac{p-1}{2}}\frac{q_{p}(2)}{2}\pmod{p},

as desired.
∎

As we analyzed in
[
27
, p. 239]
, there is overwhelming evidence that
{\texttt{q}}_{2}\neq 0

in
\mathcal{A}
. In
[
16
, Theorem 1]
, Silverman even showed that if abc-conjecture holds, then

\Big|\Big\{p\leq X:q_{2}(p)\neq 0\pmod{p}\Big\}\Big|=O(\log(X))\quad\text{as}\quad X\to\infty.

In we are sure the following conjecture is true.

Conjecture 4.3
.

For every pair of positive integers
m>a>0
,
\gcd(m,a)=1
,
there are infinitely many primes
p\equiv a\pmod{m}

such that
q_{2}(p)\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

If Conjecture
4.3
holds for
m=4
, then
T_{\mathcal{A}}(1)
and
T_{\mathcal{A}}(\bar{1})

are
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent.

Proof.

If
c_{1}T_{\mathcal{A}}(1)+c_{2}T_{\mathcal{A}}(\bar{1})=0
in
\mathcal{A}
for some
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}
, then, according to Proposition
4.2
,
we see that
(c_{1}+c_{2})q_{p}(2)\equiv 0\pmod{p}
for infinitely many primes
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.
If Conjecture
4.3
holds the form
m=4
, then
c_{1}+c_{2}\equiv 0\pmod{p}

for infinitely many primes
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. This would force
c_{1}+c_{2}=0
.
A similar consideration for primes
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
implies that
c_{1}-c_{2}=0
.
Hence, we must have
c_{1}=c_{2}=0
, which shows that
T_{\mathcal{A}}(1)
and
T_{\mathcal{A}}(\bar{1})

are
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent.
∎

Define the
finite Catalan’s constant
as

G_{\mathcal{A}}:=\Big(\frac{E_{p-3}}{2}\Big)_{3<p\in\mathcal{P}}\in\mathcal{A}.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\mathsf{FAMT}_{w}
be the vector space generated by finite alternating MTVs over
\mathbb{Q}
.
We have the following generating sets of
\mathsf{FAMT}_{w}
for
w<3
:

\displaystyle\mathsf{FAMT}_{1}=\langle{\emph{\texttt{q}}}_{2},(-1)^{p^{\prime}}{\emph{\texttt{q}}}_{2}\rangle,\quad\mathsf{FAMT}_{2}=\langle G_{\mathcal{A}},(-1)^{p^{\prime}}G_{\mathcal{A}}\rangle.

Proof.

The
w=1
case is trivial. For
w=2
, we already know
T_{\mathcal{A}}(1,1)=T_{\mathcal{A}}(2)=0
from Theorem
3.13
.
Let
{\texttt{a}}={\texttt{y}}_{0}
,
{\texttt{b}}={\texttt{y}}_{1}
, and
{\texttt{c}}={\texttt{y}}_{-1}
in the rest of the proof.
For alternating values, we first have the linear shuffle relation

T_{\mathcal{A}}({\texttt{b}}\shuffle{\texttt{c}})=-T_{\mathcal{A}}({\texttt{b}}{\texttt{c}})\Rightarrow 2T_{\mathcal{A}}({\texttt{b}}{\texttt{c}})+T_{\mathcal{A}}({\texttt{c}}{\texttt{b}})\Rightarrow 2T_{\mathcal{A}}(1,\bar{1})+T_{\mathcal{A}}(\bar{1},\bar{1})=0.

By using complicated computation (see Proposition 4.4 of our arxiv paper 2402.08160 and notice (
7

T_{\mathcal{A}}(\bar{2})=G_{\mathcal{A}}=-2T_{\mathcal{A}}(1,\bar{1}).

Then, from the reversal relation (

T_{\mathcal{A}}(\bar{1},1)=-(-1)^{p^{\prime}}T_{\mathcal{A}}(1,\bar{1}).

This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

6
Conclusions

The author’s main purpose in this paper is to study the finite and symmetric MTVs and their alternating versions, which are level two and level four variations of finite MZVs. It was found that there are many nontrivial
\mathbb{Q}
-linear relations among these values,
such as the reversal and the linear shuffle relations. We also numerically discovered some identities, which were proposed as conjectures. Due to the limitation of computing power, we then computed the structures of MTVs (their alternating versions) when the weight was less than
7 (resp. 4) by using finite Euler sums and the relations that we discovered. Throughout the study, we were guided by the Conjecture Principle Philosophy
5.1

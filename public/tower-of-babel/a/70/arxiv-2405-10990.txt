Linear canonical space-time transform and convolution theorems
Yi-Qiao Xu, Bing-Zhao Li

Abstract
Following the idea of the fractional space-time Fourier transform, a linear canonical space-time transform for 16-dimensional space-time C ​ ℓ 3 , 1 C\ell_{3,1} -valued signals is investigated in this paper. First, the definition of the proposed linear canonical space-time transform is given, and some related properties of this transform are obtained. Second, the convolution operator and the corresponding convolution theorem are proposed. Third, the convolution theorem associated with the two-sided linear canonical space-time transform is derived.

Related works

Next, we review the definition of the space-time Fourier transform (SFT).

Definition 1
.

The space-time Fourier transform of
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3,1},C\ell_{3,1})
is defined as
Hitzer (2007)

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{S}FT}[f](\bm{w})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\bm{e}^{-e_{t}w_{t}t}f(\bm{x})\exp\left\{-i_{3}\underline{x}\cdot\underline{w}\right\}d^{4}\bm{x},

(14)

where
\bm{w}=w_{t}e_{t}+\underline{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{3,1},\underline{w}=w_{1}e_{1}+w_{2}e_{2}+w_{3}e_{3}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the space-time frequency vector,
\begin{aligned} \bm{x}&=te_{t}+\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3,1},\underline{x}=x_{1}e_{1}+x_{2}e_{2}+x_{3}e_{3}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\end{aligned}
is the space-time vector, and
\underline{x}\cdot\underline{w}={\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{k=3}x_{k}w_{k}}
is the natural
\mathbb{R}^{3}-scalar
product.

The inversion formula for the SFT is given by

f(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\exp{\{e_{t}w_{t}t\}}~\mathcal{F}_{SFT}[f](\bm{w})~\exp{\{i_{3}\underline{x}\cdot\underline{w}\}}~d^{4}\bm{w}.

(15)

Based on the above research, Zayed first introduced the fractional Fourier transform (FrFT) to space-time algebra
Zayed and El Haoui (2023)
. Next, we review the definition of the fractional space-time Fourier transform (FrSFT).

Definition 2
.

The fractional space-time Fourier transform of
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3,1},C\ell_{3,1})
with respect to a parameter
\alpha

\mathcal{F}_{rSFT}^{\alpha}[f](\bm{w})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\bm{e}^{-e_{t}w_{t}t}\left.f(\bm{x})\mathcal{K}_{i_{3}}^{\alpha}(\underline{x},\underline{w})\right.d^{4}\bm{x},

(16)

the right kernel factor
\mathcal{K}_{i_{3}}^{\alpha}
is

\mathcal{K}_{i_{3}}^{\alpha}(\underline{x},\underline{w})=\begin{cases}(\csc\alpha)^{\frac{3}{2}}\bm{e}^{i_{3}\left((\underline{x}^{2}+\underline{w}^{2})\frac{\cot\alpha}{2}-\underline{x}\cdot\underline{w}\csc\alpha+\frac{2\alpha-\pi}{4}\right)},&\alpha\neq n\pi\\
\delta(\underline{w}-\underline{x}),&\alpha=2n\pi\\
\delta(\underline{w}+\underline{x}),&\alpha=(2n+1)\pi\end{cases},

(17)

where
sin\alpha>0

\alpha\neq n\pi

The inversion formula for the FrSFT is given by

f(\bm{x})=\frac{(\csc\alpha)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\bm{e}^{e_{t}w_{t}t}\mathcal{F}_{rSFT}^{\alpha}[f](\bm{w})\mathcal{K}_{-i_{3}}^{\alpha}(\underline{x},\underline{w})d^{4}\bm{w},

(18)

where the space-time frequency volume
d^{4}\bm{w}=dw_{t}dw_{1}dw_{2}dw_{3}dw_{4}
.

Based on the existing research results, we introduce the concept of linear canonical space-time transform (LCST) by replacing the fractional Fourier kernel in the fractional space-time Fourier transform with a linear canonical kernel, and propose the fundamental properties of the proposed linear canonical space-time transform.

3
Linear canonical space-time transform

3.1
Definition

Definition 3
.

The linear canonical space-time transform of
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3,1},C\ell_{3,1})
with respect to a parameter matrix
A=(a,b;c,d)\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
, is defined as

\mathcal{L}_{A}[f](\bm{w})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\bm{e}^{-e_{t}w_{t}t}\left.f(\bm{x})\mathcal{K}_{i_{3}}^{A}(\underline{x},\underline{w})\right.d^{4}\bm{x},

(19)

here we modify the right kernel factor
\mathcal{K}_{i_{3}}^{A}
to

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{i_{3}}^{A}(\underline{x},\underline{w})=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{(2\pi b)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\bm{e}^{i_{3}\left(\frac{a}{2b}\underline{x}^{2}+\frac{d}{2b}\underline{w}^{2}-\frac{\underline{x}\cdot\underline{w}}{b}\right)},&b\neq 0\\
\sqrt{d}\bm{e}^{i_{3}\left(\frac{cd}{2}\underline{w}^{2}\right)}\delta(\underline{x}-d\underline{w}),&b=0\\
\end{cases}.

where
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R}
, and
ad-bc=1
.

Proof.

Based on identity (
11

3

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\bm{e}^{e_{t}w_{t}t}\mathcal{L}_{A}[f](\bm{w})\mathcal{K}_{-i_{3}}^{A}(\underline{x},\underline{w})d^{4}\bm{w}

4
Convolution theorems for the LCST

Convolution stands as a fundamental concept in linear time-invariant systems. It characterizes the output of a continuous-time linear time-invariant system as the convolution of the input signal and the system’s impulse response. Essentially, convolution entails an integral operation and finds extensive application across fields like numerical analysis, finite impulse response, pattern recognition, and signal processing. Consequently, delving deeper into the convolution operation’s form and the associated convolution theorem within the domain of LCT under space-time algebra bears significant theoretical and practical relevance.

We define the convolution of two space-time signals
a,b\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3,1},C\ell_{3,1})
as

(a\star b)(\bm{x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}a(\bm{y})b(\bm{x}-\bm{y})d^{4}\bm{y},

(23)

provided that the integral exists.

The Mustard convolution of two space-time signals
a,b\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3,1},C\ell_{3,1})
is defined as

(a\star_{M}b)(\bm{x})=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}\{a\}\mathcal{F}\{b\}).

(24)

provided that the integral exists.

Theorem 6
.

(Mustard convolution in terms of standard convolution)
Zhang (2017)
. The Mustard convolution (
24
) of two space-time functions
a,b\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3,1},C\ell_{3,1})
can be expressed in terms of eight standard convolutions (
23
) as

\displaystyle a\star_{M}b(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=[a_{+}\star b_{+}(\bm{x})]_{+}+[a_{+}\star b_{+}^{(1,1)}(t_{1},-\underline{x})]_{-}

(25)

\displaystyle+[a_{+}\star b_{-}^{(1,0)}(\bm{x})]_{+}+[a_{+}\star b_{-}^{(0,1)}(t_{1},-\underline{x})]_{-}

\displaystyle+[a_{-}\star b_{+}^{(0,1)}(t_{1},-\underline{x})]_{+}+[a_{-}\star b_{+}^{(1,0)}(\bm{x})]_{-}

\displaystyle+[a_{-}\star b_{-}^{(1,1)}(t_{1},-\underline{x})]_{+}+[a_{-}\star b_{-}(\bm{x})]_{-}.

The computation of a typical term in Theorem
6
can be illustrated by writing out, eg, the third term in full detail

\displaystyle{}_{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\frac{1}{2}(a(\bm{y})+e_{t}a(\bm{y})i_{3})

(26)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(b(-(t_{x}-t_{y}),\underline{x}-\underline{y})-e_{t}b(-(t_{x}-t_{y}),\underline{x}-\underline{y})i_{3})d^{4}\bm{y}

\displaystyle+e_{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\frac{1}{2}(a(\bm{y})+e_{t}a(\bm{y})i_{3})

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(b(-(t_{x}-t_{y}),\underline{x}-\underline{y})-e_{t}b(-(t_{x}-t_{y}),\underline{x}-\underline{y})i_{3})d^{4}\bm{y}i_{3}\Bigg].

We define a new convolution structure for LCST based on the relationship between the LCST and the SFT, which states that a modified ordinary convolution in the time domain is equivalent to a simple multiplication operation for LCST and SFT. Based on the definition of the ordinary SFT (
14

Lemma 1

Let

\alpha\neq n\pi

\left|B\mathcal{L}_{A}\bigl[f\bigr](w_{t},\underline{w}b)\right|=\left|\mathcal{F}_{SFT}\biggl[f(\bm{x})\bm{e}^{i_{3}\frac{a}{2b}\underline{x}^{2}}\biggr](\bm{w})\biggr|.\right.

Definition 4
.

For any space-time function
f(\bm{x})\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3,1},C\ell_{3,1})

f

\odot

Let

From the definition of the LCST and Definition

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{A}[h]

\displaystyle=B\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\bm{e}^{-e_{t}w_{t}t}h(\bm{x})\bm{e}^{i_{3}(\frac{a}{2b}\underline{x}^{2}+\frac{d}{2b}\underline{w}^{2}-\frac{\underline{x}\cdot\underline{w}}{b})}d^{4}\bm{x}

∎

Convolution theorem for the two-sided LCST

Definition 6
.

For any real matrix
M_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{k}&B_{k}\\
C_{k}&D_{k}\end{pmatrix}

A_{k}D_{k}-B_{k}C_{k}=1

k=1,2

\mathcal{L}_{A}[h]:\mathbb{R}^{3,1}\to C\ell_{3,1}

\mathcal{L}_{A}[h](\bm{w})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\mathcal{K}_{e_{t}}^{M_{1}}(w_{t},t)h(\bm{x})\mathcal{K}_{i_{3}}^{M_{2}}(\underline{x},\underline{w})d^{4}\bm{x},

(38)

We give the left and the right kernel factors of the two-sided LCST as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{e_{t}}^{M_{1}}(w_{t},t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi B_{1}}}\bm{e}^{\frac{e_{t}}{2B_{1}}\left(A_{1}t^{2}+D_{1}w_{t}^{2}-2tw_{t}\right)},

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{i_{3}}^{M_{2}}(\underline{x},\underline{w})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi B_{2}}}\bm{e}^{\frac{i_{3}}{2B_{2}}\left(A_{2}\underline{x}^{2}+D_{2}\underline{w}^{2}-2\underline{x}\cdot\underline{w}\right)}.

The inverse transformation of the proposed two-sided linear canonical space-time transform is given by the following theorem.

Theorem 9
.

(40)

and

\displaystyle+\iiint\lambda_{e_{t}}(\bm{w})\mathcal{K}_{-e_{t},e_{t},-e_{t}}^{M_{1}}\mathcal{K}_{-i_{3},-i_{3},i_{3}}^{M_{2}}[f_{+}\bm{y})g_{-}(\bm{z})]_{+}

\displaystyle+\iiint\lambda_{e_{t}}(\bm{w})\mathcal{K}_{-e_{t},e_{t},e_{t}}^{M_{1}}\mathcal{K}_{-i_{3},i_{3},-i_{3}}^{M_{2}}[f_{+}\bm{y})g_{-}(\bm{z})]_{-}

\displaystyle+\iiint\lambda_{e_{t}}(\bm{w})\mathcal{K}_{-e_{t},e_{t},e_{t}}^{M_{1}}\mathcal{K}_{i_{3},-i_{3},i_{3}}^{M_{2}}[f_{-}\bm{y})g_{+}(\bm{z})]_{+}

\displaystyle+\iiint\lambda_{e_{t}}(\bm{w})\mathcal{K}_{-e_{t},e_{t},e_{t}}^{M_{1}}\mathcal{K}_{i_{3},i_{3},-i_{3}}^{M_{2}}[f_{-}\bm{y})g_{-}(\bm{z})]_{-}.

We now only show explicitly how to simplify the second triple integral, the others follow the same pattern.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\lambda_{e_{t}}(\bm{w})\mathcal{K}_{-e_{t}}^{M_{1}}(w_{t},t_{1})\mathcal{K}_{e_{t}}^{M_{1}}(w_{t},t_{1}^{\prime})\mathcal{K}_{-e_{t}}^{M_{1}}(w_{t},t_{1}^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{K}_{-e_{t}}^{M_{2}}(\underline{y},\underline{w})\mathcal{K}_{e_{t}}^{M_{2}}(\underline{z},\underline{w})\mathcal{K}_{-e_{t}}^{M_{2}}(\underline{x},\underline{w})[f_{+}(\bm{y})g_{+}(\bm{z})]_{-}d^{4}\bm{y}d^{4}\bm{z}d^{4}\bm{w}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}(2\pi)^{2}B_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}B_{2}^{\frac{7}{2}}\bm{e}^{e_{t}(\frac{D_{1}w_{t}^{2}}{2B_{1}}+\frac{D_{2}\underline{w}^{2}}{2B_{2}})}

\displaystyle\times(2\pi)^{-\frac{3}{2}}(B_{1})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\times\bm{e}^{\frac{e_{t}}{2B_{1}}(A_{1}(t_{1}^{\prime 2}-t_{1}^{2}-t_{1}^{\prime\prime 2})-2(t_{1}^{\prime}-t_{1}-t_{1}^{\prime\prime})w_{t}-D_{1}w_{t}^{2})}

\displaystyle\times(2\pi)^{-\frac{9}{2}}(B_{2})^{-\frac{9}{2}}\times\bm{e}^{\frac{e_{t}}{2B_{2}}(A_{2}(\underline{z}^{2}-\underline{x}^{2}-\underline{y}^{2})-2(\underline{z}-\underline{x}-\underline{y})\cdot\underline{w}-D_{2}\underline{w}^{2})}

\displaystyle\times[f_{+}(\bm{y})g_{+}(\bm{z})]_{-}d^{4}\bm{y}d^{4}\bm{z}d^{4}\bm{w}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}(2\pi)^{-1}(B_{1})^{-1}\bm{e}^{\frac{e_{t}}{2B_{1}}[A_{1}(t_{1}^{\prime 2}-t_{1}^{2}-t_{1}^{\prime\prime 2})]}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bm{e}^{\frac{-e_{t}}{B_{1}}(t_{1}^{\prime}-t_{1}-t_{1}^{\prime\prime})w_{t}}dw_{t}

\displaystyle\times(2\pi)^{-3}(B_{2})^{-1}\bm{e}^{\frac{e_{t}}{2B_{2}}[A_{2}(\underline{z}^{2}-\underline{x}^{2}-\underline{y}^{2})]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\bm{e}^{\frac{-e_{t}}{B_{2}}(\underline{z}-\underline{x}-\underline{y})\cdot\underline{w}}d\underline{w}

\displaystyle\times[f_{+}(\bm{y})g_{+}(\bm{z})]_{-}d^{4}\bm{y}d^{4}\bm{z}d^{4}\bm{w}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\bm{e}^{\frac{e_{t}}{2B_{1}}[A_{1}(t_{1}^{\prime 2}-t_{1}^{2}-t_{1}^{\prime\prime 2})]}\delta(t_{1}^{\prime}-t_{1}-t_{1}^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle\times\bm{e}^{\frac{e_{t}}{2B_{2}}[A_{2}(\underline{z}^{2}-\underline{x}^{2}-\underline{y}^{2})]}\delta(\underline{z}-\underline{x}-\underline{y})d^{4}\bm{y}d^{4}\bm{z}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}[f_{+}(\bm{y})g_{+}(-(t_{1}-t_{1}^{\prime}),\underline{x}+\underline{y})]_{-}d^{4}\bm{y}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}[f_{+}(\bm{y})g_{+}^{(1,1)}(t_{1}-t_{1}^{\prime},\underline{x}+\underline{y})]_{-}d^{4}\bm{y}

\displaystyle=[f_{+}\star g_{+}^{(1,1)}(t_{1},-\underline{x})]_{-}.

Simplifying the other seven triple integrals in similar manner, we obtain the the desired decomposition for the proposed convolution. This completes the proof of Theorem

.

Now we state and prove our new convolution theorem.

Theorem 11
.

Let
p(\bm{x})=(f\star_{N}g)(\bm{x})
and
\mathcal{L}_{A}[p](\bm{w})
,
\mathcal{L}_{A}[f](\bm{w})
and
\mathcal{L}_{A}[g](\bm{w})
denote the two-sided LCST of
p(\bm{x})
,
f(\bm{x})
and
g(\bm{x})
. Then

\mathcal{L}_{A}[p(\bm{x})](\bm{w})=\lambda_{e_{t}}(\bm{w})\mathcal{L}_{A}[f(\bm{x})](\bm{w})\mathcal{L}_{A}[g(\bm{x})](\bm{w}).

(48)

Proof.

\displaystyle(f\star_{N}g)(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\lambda_{e_{t}}(\bm{w})\mathcal{K}_{-e_{t}}^{M_{1}}(w_{t},t_{1})\mathcal{K}_{e_{t}}^{M_{1}}(w_{t},t_{1}^{\prime})f(\bm{y})\mathcal{K}_{i_{3}}^{M_{2}}(\underline{y},\underline{w})

(49)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{e_{t}}^{M_{1}}(w_{t},t_{1}^{\prime\prime})\mathcal{K}_{i_{3}}^{M_{2}}(\underline{z},\underline{w})\mathcal{K}_{-i_{3}}^{M_{2}}(\underline{x},\underline{w})d^{4}\bm{y}d^{4}\bm{z}d^{4}\bm{w}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\lambda_{e_{t}}(\bm{w})\mathcal{K}_{-e_{t}}^{M_{1}}(w_{t},t_{1})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\mathcal{K}_{e_{t}}^{M_{1}}(w_{t},t_{1}^{\prime})f(\bm{y})\mathcal{K}_{i_{3}}^{M_{2}}(\underline{y},\underline{w})d^{4}\bm{y}

\displaystyle\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\mathcal{K}_{e_{t}}^{M_{1}}(w_{t},t_{1}^{\prime\prime})\mathcal{K}_{i_{3}}^{M_{2}}(\underline{z},\underline{w})d^{4}\bm{z}\mathcal{K}_{-i_{3}}^{M_{2}}(\underline{x},\underline{w})d^{4}\bm{w}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3,1}}\mathcal{K}_{-e_{t}}^{M_{1}}(w_{t},t_{1})\lambda_{e_{t}}(\bm{w})\mathcal{L}_{A}[f(\bm{y})](\bm{w})\mathcal{L}_{A}[g(\bm{z})](\bm{w})\mathcal{K}_{-i_{3}}^{M_{2}}(\underline{x},\underline{w})d^{4}\bm{w}

∎

6
Conclusion

After an overview of space-time algebra and considering the fractional space-time Fourier transform (FrSFT) and some of its properties, we propose the concept of linear canonical space-time transform (LCST) as a broad generalization of FrSFT. Finally, we propose a convolution operator and the corresponding convolution theorem. We hope that applied mathematics will continue to investigate this new class of hyper-complex transformations.

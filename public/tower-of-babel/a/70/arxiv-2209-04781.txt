\begin{array}[]{ll}\|u_{k+1}(t)\|_{\infty}&\leq\|u_{1}(t)\|_{\infty}+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{r}\|S(t-\sigma)v_{k}(\sigma)^{p}\|_{\infty}d\sigma\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq c_{1}\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}+c_{1}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{r}\|v_{k}(\sigma)\|_{\infty}^{p}d\sigma\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq c_{1}\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}+c_{1}(2c_{1}(\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}+\|v_{0}\|_{\infty}))^{p}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{r}d\sigma,\end{array}

for all
t\in(0,T)
. Similarly, we have

(4.9)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\|v_{k+1}(t)\|_{\infty}&\leq\|v_{1}(t)\|_{\infty}+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{s}\|S(t-\sigma)u_{k}(\sigma)^{p}\|_{\infty}d\sigma\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq c_{1}\|v_{0}\|_{\infty}+c_{1}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{s}\|u_{k}(\sigma)\|_{\infty}^{q}d\sigma\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq c_{1}\|v_{0}\|_{\infty}+c_{1}(2c_{1}(\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}+\|v_{0}\|_{\infty}))^{q}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{s}d\sigma,\end{array}

for all
t\in(0,T)
. Thus, the inequality (
4.7
) follows by adding (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) and then choosing
T
as small enough.

Finally, by (
4.5
), (
4.6
), and (
4.7
), we have that the limits functions
u_{\infty}
and
v_{\infty}
satisfies (
1.6
) and

\sup\limits_{0<t<T}(\|u_{\infty}(t)\|_{\infty}+\|v_{\infty}(t)\|_{\infty})\leq 2c_{1}(\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}+\|v_{0}\|_{\infty}).

Also, by the comparison principle (see Lemma
4.1
),
(u_{\infty},v_{\infty})
is the unique solution to the problem (
1.1
). Therefore, Theorem
4.2
is hold.
∎

Now we proof the local existence of solutions of problem (
1.1
), in the general case, that is, when
p,q>0
and
p\cdot q>1
.

Corollary 4.10

(Local Existence)
.

Suppose that
p,q>0
with
p\cdot q>1,
and assume that
\omega
satisfies either
(A)
or
(B)
, and
(u_{0},v_{0})\in[L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})]^{2},

u_{0},v_{0}\geq 0
. Then there exists
T>0
and a constant
C_{0}>0
such that problem (
1.1
) possesses a solution
(u,v)
on
[0,T]
satisfying

\sup_{0<t<T}(\|u(t)\|_{\infty}+\|v(t)\|_{\infty})\leq C_{0}(\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}+\|v_{0}\|_{\infty}).

Proof.

We use a known approximation method; for example, see
[
1
]
. Without loss of the generality, we can assume that
1\leq q
and
0<p<1.
For each
n\in\mathbb{N},
consider a nondecreasing global Lipschitz function
f_{n}
such that

f_{n}(s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}0&\mbox{ if }s=0,\\
s^{p}&\mbox{ if }s>\frac{1}{2n},\\
\end{array}\right.

and
|f_{n}(s_{1})-f_{n}(s_{2})|\leq c_{n}|s_{1}-s_{2}|
for all
s_{1},s_{2}\in[0,\infty).
Let now see the following approximate problem of (
1.1
).

(4.11)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rlll}u_{t}-\mbox{div}(\omega(x)\nabla u)&=&t^{r}f_{n}(v)&\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T),\\
v_{t}-\mbox{div}(\omega(x)\nabla v)&=&t^{s}u^{q}&\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T),\\
u(0)=u_{0}&&v(0)=v_{0}+\frac{1}{n}&\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{array}\right.

Since that
f_{n}
is globally Lipschitz with
f_{n}(0)=0,
we can argue similarly as in the proof of Theorem
4.2
for to obtain a unique nonnegative bounded solution
(u_{n}(t),v_{n}(t))
of (
4.11
), which besides satisfies (
4.3
). Note that, from
(K_{1})
and
(K_{2})
properties, we obtain

(4.12)

v_{k}(t)\geq S(t)(v_{0}+1/k)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Gamma(x,y,t)v_{0}(y)dy+1/k\geq 1/k,\mbox{ for all }k.

Also, by construction, we have that if
n>m
then
f_{n}(t)=f_{m}(t)
for
t>1/2m.
Then, from this and (
4.12
), we have

\displaystyle u_{m}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(t)u_{0}+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{r}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Gamma(x,y,t-\sigma)f_{m}(v_{m}(y,\sigma))dyd\sigma,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(t)u_{0}+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{r}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Gamma(x,y,t-\sigma)f_{n}(v_{m}(y,\sigma))dyd\sigma,

\displaystyle v_{m}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(t)(v_{0}+1/m)+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Gamma(x,y,t-\sigma)(u_{m}(y,\sigma))^{q}dyd\sigma.

Thus, we have that
(u_{m}.v_{m})
is also solution of (
4.11
) with initial condition
(u_{0},v_{0}+1/m).
Therefore, from Lemma
4.1
, we have
u_{m}\geq u_{n}
and
v_{m}\geq v_{n}
for
n>m
. That is, the sequences
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{v_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are decreasing and bounded below. Thus the result follows by letting
n
go to
\infty.

∎

4.2.
Global existence: Proof of Theorem
1.11
-(ii)

Without loss of the generality, we can suppose that
0<p<1
and
p\cdot q>1.
Consider
\max\{\|u_{0}\|_{r_{1\star},\infty},\|v_{0}\|_{r_{2\star},\infty}\}<\delta,
where
\delta>0
will be chosen later small enough.

From (
1.7
)-(
1.10
), we obtain the following estimates:

(4.13)

p\gamma_{2}=\gamma_{1}+(r+1),\ q\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}+(s+1),\ pr_{1\star}>r_{2\star},\ qr_{2\star}>r_{1\star}.

Also since that
\gamma<\frac{N}{2-\alpha},
we have
r_{1\star},r_{2\star}>1.

Now, similarly to the proof of Theorem
4.2
, we define the following sequence

\{(u^{n},v^{n})\}_{n\geq 0}

defined by
u^{0}(t)=S(t)u_{0}
,
v^{0}(t)=S(t)v_{0}
and

(4.14)

\begin{array}[]{ll}u^{n}(t)=S(t)u_{0}+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\sigma)\displaystyle\sigma^{r}[v^{n-1}(\sigma)]^{p}d\sigma,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr v^{n}(t)=S(t)v_{0}+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\sigma)\sigma^{s}[u^{n-1}(\sigma)]^{q}d\sigma,\end{array}

for all
t>0
. Note that the sequences
\{u^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
\{v^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
are non-decreasing.

By induction, we prove that.

(4.15)

\begin{array}[]{rlllll}&\|u^{n}(t)\|_{r_{1\star},\infty}&\leq&2c_{\star\star}\delta,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\|u^{n}(t)\|_{\infty}&\leq&2c_{\star\star}\delta\ t^{-\frac{N}{(2-\alpha)r_{1\star}}},\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\|v^{n}(t)\|_{r_{2\star},\infty}&\leq&2c_{\star\star}\delta,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\|v^{n}(t)\|_{\infty}&\leq&2c_{\star\star}\delta\ t^{-\frac{N}{(2-\alpha)r_{2\star}}}.\end{array}

From
(G_{2}),
we have

(4.16)

\begin{array}[]{rlllll}&\|u^{0}(t)\|_{r_{1\star},\infty}&\leq&c_{**}\|u_{0}\|_{r_{1\star},\infty},\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\|u^{0}(t)\|_{\mu,\infty}&\leq&c_{**}t^{-\frac{N}{2-\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{r_{1\star}}-\frac{1}{\mu}\right)}\|u_{0}\|_{r_{1\star},\infty},\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\|v^{0}(t)\|_{r_{2\star},\infty}&\leq&c_{**}\|v_{0}\|_{r_{2\star},\infty},\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\|v^{0}(t)\|_{\mu,\infty}&\leq&c_{**}t^{-\frac{N}{2-\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{r_{2\star}}-\frac{1}{\mu}\right)}\|v_{0}\|_{r_{2\star},\infty},\end{array}

for all
t>0
,
\mu\in[r_{1\star},\infty],
and some constant
c_{**}>0
. This implies that (
4.15
) is held for
n=0
.

Now we assume that (
4.15
) holds for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By symmetry we only prove that (
4.15
) holds for
u^{n+1}
. From (
2.5
) and (
4.15
), we have

(4.17)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\|v^{n}(t)\|_{\mu,\infty}&\leq\|v^{n}(t)\|_{r_{2\star},\infty}^{\frac{r_{2\star}}{\mu}}\|v^{n}(t)\|_{\infty}^{1-\frac{r_{2\star}}{\mu}}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq 2c_{**}\delta t^{-\frac{N}{2-\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{r_{2\star}}-\frac{1}{\mu}\right)}\end{array}

for all
t>0
and
r_{2\star}\leq\mu<\infty
. Also, from (
4.13
) and (
4.17
), we have

(4.18)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\|v^{n}(t)^{p}\|_{\eta,\infty}&=\|v^{n}(t)\|_{\eta p,\infty}^{p}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq(2c_{**}\delta t^{-\frac{N}{2-\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{r_{2\star}}-\frac{1}{\eta p}\right)})^{p}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&=C\delta^{p}t^{\frac{N}{(2-\alpha)\eta}-\frac{N}{(2-\alpha)r_{1\star}}-(r+1)}\end{array}

for any
\eta>1
with
r_{2\star}\leq\eta p.
Similarly, from (
4.13
) and (
4.15
), we obtain

(4.19)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\|v^{n}(t)^{p}\|_{\infty}&=\|v^{n}(t)\|_{\infty}^{p}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq\left(2c_{\star\star}\delta\ t^{-\frac{N}{(2-\alpha)r_{2\star}}}\right)^{p}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&=(2c_{\star\star}\delta)^{p}t^{-p\gamma_{2}}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&=(2c_{\star\star}\delta)^{p}t^{-\frac{N}{(2-\alpha)r_{1\star}}-(r+1)}\end{array}

for all
t>0
.

Thus, by
(G_{1})
,
(G_{2})
, (
4.13
), (
4.18
)(
\mbox{with }\eta=r_{1\star}
), and (
4.19
), we have

(4.20)

\begin{array}[]{rll}\left\|\displaystyle\int_{t/2}^{t}S(t-\sigma)\sigma^{r}v^{n}(\sigma)^{p}d\sigma\right\|_{\infty}&\leq\displaystyle\int_{t/2}^{t}\sigma^{r}\|S(t-\sigma)v^{n}(\sigma)^{p}\|_{\infty}d\sigma\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq C\displaystyle\int_{t/2}^{t}\sigma^{r}\|v^{n}(\sigma)^{p}\|_{\infty}d\sigma\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq C\delta^{p}t^{-\frac{N}{(2-\alpha)r_{1\star}}}\end{array}

and

(4.21)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\left\|\displaystyle\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}S(t-\sigma)\sigma^{p}v^{n}(\sigma)^{p}d\sigma\right\|_{r_{1\star},\infty}&\leq\displaystyle\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\sigma^{r}\|S(t-\sigma)v^{n}(\sigma)^{p}\|_{r_{1\star},\infty}d\sigma\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq C\displaystyle\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\sigma^{r}\|v^{n}(\sigma)\|^{p}_{pr_{1\star},\infty}d\sigma\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq C\delta^{p}\end{array}

for all
t>0.

On the other hand, since that
t-\sigma\geq t/2\mbox{ for all }\sigma\in[0,t/2],
by
(G_{2})
and (
4.18
) (with
\eta=\eta_{1}
, which will be chosen later), we obtain

(4.22)

\begin{array}[]{rll}&\left\|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}S(t-\sigma)\sigma^{r}v^{n}(\sigma)^{p}d\sigma\right\|_{\infty}\\
&\leq\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}\|S(t-\sigma)\sigma^{r}v^{n}(\sigma)^{p}\|_{\infty}d\sigma\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq C\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(t-\sigma)^{-\frac{N}{(2-\alpha)\eta_{1}}}\sigma^{r}\|v^{n}(\sigma)^{p}\|_{\eta_{1},\infty}d\sigma\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq C\delta^{p}t^{-\frac{N}{(2-\alpha)\eta_{1}}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}\sigma^{\frac{N}{(2-\alpha)\eta_{1}}-\frac{N}{(2-\alpha)r_{1\star}}-1}d\sigma\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq C\delta^{p}t^{-\frac{N}{(2-\alpha)r_{1\star}}}\end{array}

for some
1<\eta_{1}<r_{1\star}
close enough to
r_{1\star}
so that
r_{2\star}<\eta_{1}p
(it is possible since that
p\ r_{1\star}>r_{2\star}>1
).

Analogously (using the above
\eta_{1}
again), we obtain

(4.23)

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\left\|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}S(t-\sigma)\sigma^{r}v^{n}(\sigma)^{p}d\sigma\right\|_{r_{1\star},\infty}\\
&\leq\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}\|S(t-\sigma)\sigma^{r}v^{n}(\sigma)^{p}\|_{r_{1\star},\infty}d\sigma\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq C\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(t-\sigma)^{-\frac{N}{2-\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{\eta_{1}}-\frac{1}{r_{1\star}}\right)}\sigma^{r}\|v^{n}(\sigma)^{p}\|_{\eta_{1},\infty}d\sigma\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq Ct^{-\frac{N}{2-\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{\eta_{1}}-\frac{1}{r_{1\star}}\right)}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}\sigma^{r}\|v^{n}(\sigma)^{p}\|_{\eta_{1},\infty}d\sigma\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq C\delta^{p}\end{array}

for all
t>0
.

Then, from (
4.14
), (
4.16
), (
4.20
), (
4.21
), (
4.22
), (
4.23
), and taking a
\delta>0
sufficiently small, we obtain

\displaystyle t^{\frac{N}{(2-\alpha)r_{1\star}}}\|u^{n+1}(t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{\star\star}\delta+C\delta^{p}\leq 2c_{\star\star}\delta

\displaystyle\|u^{n+1}(t)\|_{r_{1\star},\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{\star\star}\delta+C\delta^{p}\leq 2c_{\star\star}\delta

for all
t>0,
where
C>0
is a constant independent of
n
,
\delta,
and
t
. Thus, we obtain that (
4.15
) holds for all
u^{n}
with
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Arguing similarly, we have that also (
4.15
) holds for everyone
v^{n}
with
n\in\mathbb{N}.

Now, from (
4.15
) and arguing similarly as in the proof of Theorem (
4.2
), we obtain that there exists a global-in-time solution (this solution is unique in the particular case when
p>1
and
q>1
)

(u(t),v(t))=\left(\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}u^{n}(t),\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}v^{n}(t)\right)

of (
1.1
) such that

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{\infty}\leq 2c_{\star\star}\delta t^{-\frac{N}{(2-\alpha)r_{1\star}}},

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{r_{1\star},\infty}\leq 2c_{\star\star}\delta

\displaystyle\|v(t)\|_{\infty}\leq 2c_{\star\star}\delta t^{-\frac{N}{(2-\alpha)r_{2\star}}},

\displaystyle\|v(t)\|_{r_{2\star},\infty}\leq 2c_{\star\star}\delta.

Using this together with the upper bounded estimate in Corollary
4.10
, we obtain a constant
C_{0}>0
such that

\|u(t)\|_{\infty}\leq C_{0}(t+1)^{-\frac{N}{(2-\alpha)r_{1\star}}}\,\,\mbox{ and }\,\,\|v(t)\|_{\infty}\leq C_{0}(t+1)^{-\frac{N}{(2-\alpha)r_{2\star}}},

for
t>0
. Also, from this and by (
2.5
), we have

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{\mu,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|u(t)\|_{r_{1\star},\infty}^{\frac{r_{1\star}}{\mu}}\|u(t)\|_{\infty}^{1-\frac{r_{1\star}}{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(t+1)^{-\frac{N}{2-\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{r_{1\star}}-\frac{1}{\mu}\right)},

\displaystyle\|v(t)\|_{\mu,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|v(t)\|_{r_{2\star},\infty}^{\frac{r_{2\star}}{\mu}}\|v(t)\|_{\infty}^{1-\frac{r_{2\star}}{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(t+1)^{-\frac{N}{2-\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{r_{2\star}}-\frac{1}{\mu}\right)},

for all
\mu
such that
\max\{r_{1\star},r_{2\star}\}<\mu\leq\infty
,
t>0
and some constant

, thus the proof is concluded.

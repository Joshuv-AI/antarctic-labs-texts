Convergence of solutions of a one-phase Stefan problem with Neumann boundary data to a self-similar profile
Danielle Hilhorst, Sabrina Roscani, Piotr Rybka

Abstract
We study a one-dimensional one-phase Stefan problem with a Neumann boundary condition on the fixed part of the boundary. We construct the unique self-similar solution, and show that starting from arbitrary initial data, solution orbits converge to the self-similar solution.



Keywords:
Stefan problem; Neumann boundary condition; large time behavior; self-similar profile.

(1)

We stress that this type of boundary condition is reasonable from the modeling view point. Namely, the decay of data presented in (
1
-
ii

1

Our main result states that the self-similar solution is attracting.

.

Suppose that
(u_{0},b_{0})
satisfies the conditions

0\leq u_{0}\in W^{1,\infty}(0,+\infty),\quad 0<u_{0}(0)\ \text{ and }u_{0}(x)=0\,\text{in }[b_{0},\infty).

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}s(t)/\sqrt{t+1}=\omega

\displaystyle{\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\sup_{x/\sqrt{t+1}\in[0,\omega]}\left|u(x,t)-U\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t+1}}\right)\right|}=0.

Our method of proof is based upon recent results obtained by

4
]

3

We should point out that there are a number of results dealing with the asymptotic behavior of solutions of Stefan problems, mainly in the case of Dirichlet data on the fixed boundary.

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{2\delta}\int_{T+\delta}^{T+2\delta}\int_{0}^{b(\tau)}\psi^{\delta}W^{2}\,d\eta d\tau=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{b(T)}W^{2}(\eta,T)\,d\eta,.

Hence, we conclude that

L_{1}(T)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{b(T+1)}W^{2}(\eta,T+1)\,d\eta-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{b(T)}W^{2}(\eta,T)\,d\eta.

Next we consider the term
R^{\delta}_{1}(T)
. We integrate by parts to obtain

R^{\delta}_{1}(T)=-\int_{\Omega_{T,1}}(W_{\eta}^{2}\varphi^{\delta}+WW_{\eta}\varphi^{\delta}_{\eta}+\frac{1}{4}W^{2}(\eta\varphi^{\delta}_{\eta}+\varphi^{\delta}))\,d\eta d\tau.

Again here, the boundary terms vanish, because the support of

\Omega_{T,1}

We remark that the same argument as above leads us to

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega_{T,1}}WW_{\eta}\varphi^{\delta}_{\eta}\,d\eta d\tau

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{T}^{T+1}\left(\int_{\delta}^{2\delta}WW_{\eta}\phi^{\delta}\,d\eta d\tau-\int_{b(\tau)-2\delta}^{b(\tau)-\delta}WW_{\eta}\phi^{\delta}\,d\eta d\tau\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{T}^{T+1}(-hW(0,\tau)-W(b(\tau),\tau)W_{\eta}(b(\tau),\tau))\,d\eta d\tau

\displaystyle=-\int_{T}^{T+1}hW(0,\tau)\,d\eta d\tau,

where we have also used the boundary condition (
3
-ii
) as well as the condition at the interface (
3
-iii
).
Similarly one can show that

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega_{T,1}}\frac{1}{4}W^{2}(\eta\varphi^{\delta}_{\eta}+\varphi^{\delta}))\,d\eta d\tau=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega_{T,1}}W^{2}(\eta,\tau)\,d\eta d\tau.

Hence, we conclude

R_{1}(T)=\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}R^{\delta}_{1}(T)=-\int_{\Omega_{T,1}}W_{\eta}^{2}\,d\tau d\eta+h\int_{T}^{T+1}W(0,\tau)\,d\tau-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega_{T,1}}W^{2}\,d\tau d\eta.

In view of Lemma

Step 2.

Abstract
. We develop general methods to compute the algebraic K K -theory of crossed products by Bernoulli shifts on additive categories. From this we obtain a K K -theory formula for regular group rings associated to wreath products of finite groups by groups satisfying the Farrell–Jones conjecture.

2.
R
-linear categories

Throughout this paper, let
R
be a commutative ring with unit. All tensor products appearing in this paper will be taken over
R
and will be denoted by

An

-linear category
is a category which is enriched over
R
-modules.
An
R
-additive category
is an
R
-linear category which is moreover additive. An
R
-linear functor
between
R
-linear categories is a functor which is
R
-linear on morphism sets.
Note that
R
-linear functors between
R
-additive categories preserve direct sums.
A basic example of an
R
-linear category is the ring
R
itself considered as a category with one object.
A basic example of an
R
-additive category is the category
\mathcal{F}^{f}(R)
of finitely generated free
R
-modules.

Remark 2.1
.

In order to avoid set-theoretical issues, we assume the existence of sufficiently many Grothendieck universes which we call
small sets, large sets, huge sets
, etc. (see
[
Lur09
, §1.2.15]
for this standard procedure). In this way, the collection of all small sets becomes a large set, the collection of all large sets becomes a huge set, and so on. If nothing else is said, ordinary sets are assumed to be small and ordinary categories are assumed to have large object sets and small morphism sets. This framework allows us to rigorously talk about the (huge) category of all (ordinary) categories, the (large) category of all functors between two (ordinary) categories, or (large) direct sums indexed over the objects of an (ordinary) category.

For a finite set
(\mathcal{A}_{i})_{i\in I}
of
R
-linear categories, we define their tensor product
\bigotimes_{i\in I}\mathcal{A}_{i}
as follows: Objects of
\bigotimes_{i\in I}\mathcal{A}_{i}
are tuples
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
of objects
A_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
and morphisms
(A_{i})_{i\in I}\to(B_{i})_{i\in I}
are given by elements of
\bigotimes_{i\in I}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}(A_{i},B_{i})
. We also write
\mathcal{A}^{\otimes I}\coloneqq\otimes_{i\in I}\mathcal{A}
for a single
R
-linear category
\mathcal{A}
. We use the suggestive notation
\otimes_{i\in I}A_{i}\coloneqq(A_{i})_{i\in I}
. The tensor product of
R
-additive categories is not necessarily additive.

Let
G

be an

-linear category. An

In these definitions we insist on equalities rather than equivalences.

By a

and integers

,

(2)

.

can be represented by a

Proof.

Denote by

the representation given by left multiplication.
Let

be a collection of subgroups of

containing each conjugacy class exactly once.
Then
([G/H_{i}])_{1\leq j\leq k}
is a basis for
A(G)
so that any element

can uniquely be written as
x=\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}n_{i}[G/H_{i}]
with
n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
By reordering the
H_{i}
according to the maximal length of any chain of proper subgroups contained in
H_{i}
, we may furthermore assume that
i\leq j
whenever
H_{i}
is subconjugate to
H_{j}
.
We make the following observations:

(1)

The coefficients
n_{i}
of an element

may be recovered as the last column of the matrix representing
\lambda(x)
with respect to the basis
([G/H_{i}])_{1\leq i\leq k}
of
A(G)_{\mathbb{Q}}
. In particular,
\lambda
is injective.

(2)

Let
X
be any finite
G
-set and
1\leq i\leq k
. Then the stabilizers of

are all subconjugate to
H_{i}
. In particular, the matrix representing

with respect to
([G/H_{i}])_{1\leq i\leq k}
is an upper triangular matrix.

(3)

For any

, the
G
-set
\{1,\dotsc,n\}^{Z}\times G/H_{i}
has
n^{|Z/H_{i}|}
-many orbits of type

.

The second and the third condition imply

\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda([n^{Z}])-n^{|Z/H_{i}|})=0\in\End_{\mathbb{Q}}(A(G)\otimes\mathbb{Q}),

while the first condition implies

\prod_{i=1}^{k}([n^{Z}]-n^{|Z/H_{i}|})=0\in A(G).

Now the proposition follows by letting

and
\beta
be suitable polynomials in

. The equality

implies that all the coefficients of
\alpha
are positive. In particular
\alpha
can be represented by a
G
-set.
∎

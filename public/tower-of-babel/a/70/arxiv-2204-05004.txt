Rota–Baxter operators on Clifford semigroups and the Yang–Baxter equation
Francesco CATINO, Marzia MAZZOTTA, Paola STEFANELLI

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce the theory of Rota–Baxter operators on Clifford semigroups, useful tools for obtaining dual weak braces, i.e., triples ( S , + , ∘ ) \left(S,+,\circ\right) where ( S , + ) \left(S,+\right) and ( S , ∘ ) \left(S,\circ\right) are Clifford semigroups such that a ∘ ( b + c ) = a ∘ b − a + a ∘ c a\circ\left(b+c\right)=a\circ b-a+a\circ c and a ∘ a − = − a + a a\circ a^{-}=-a+a , for all a , b , c ∈ S a,b,c\in S . To each algebraic structure is associated a set-theoretic solution of the Yang–Baxter equation that has a behaviour near to the bijectivity and non-degeneracy. Drawing from the theory of Clifford semigroups, we provide methods for constructing dual weak braces and deepen some structural aspects, including the notion of ideal.

Introduction

Rota–Baxter operators on commutative algebras firstly appeared in 1960 in G. Baxter’s probability studies
[
4
]
and were subsequently explored by several authors, including Atkinson
[
1
]
, Cartier
[
8
]
, and Rota
[
30
,
31
]
, among others. An entire monograph has been dedicated by Guo
[
23
]
on this topic. Moreover, Guo, Lang, and Sheng
[
24
]
have introduced the notion of Rota–Baxter operator on groups, pursued further by Bardakov and Gubarev
[
2
]
. Recently, the latter have found in
[
3
]
a strict connection between Rota–Baxter operators on groups and the Yang–Baxter equation, a fundamental identity of theoretical physics that takes the name from Yang
[
33
]
and R. Baxter
[
5
]
, and of which Drinfel’d
[
21
]
posed the problem of finding all set-theoretic solutions. Specifically, if
S
is a non-empty set, a map

r:S\times S\to S\times S
is a
set-theoretic solution of the Yang–Baxter equation
, or shortly a
solution
, if it satisfies the relation

\displaystyle\left(r\times\id_{S}\right)\left(\id_{S}\times r\right)\left(r\times\id_{S}\right)=\left(\id_{S}\times r\right)\left(r\times\id_{S}\right)\left(\id_{S}\times r\right).

Writing a solution
r
as
r\left(x,y\right)=\left(\lambda_{x}\left(y\right),\rho_{y}\left(x\right)\right)
, with
\lambda_{x},\rho_{x}
maps from
S
into itself, then
r
is
left non-degenerate
if
\lambda_{x}\in\Sym_{S}
,
right non-degenerate
if
\rho_{x}\in\Sym_{S}
, for every
x\in S
,
non-degenerate
if it is both left and right non-degenerate.

As proved in
[
3
, Proposition 3.1]
, every Rota–Baxter operator on a group determines a skew brace, an algebraic structure introduced by Guarnieri and Vendramin in 2017 that always gives rise to a bijective non-degenerate solution (see
[
22
, Theorem 3.1]
).

In this regard, Caranti and Stefanello
[
7
]
have recently described gamma functions that characterise skew braces obtained from Rota-Baxter operators on groups.

A generalisation of the skew brace that determines a solution has been newly introduced in
[
13
]
, and it is named weak brace. To give the definition, we firstly recall that a
Clifford semigroup

(S,\cdot)
is an inverse semigroup, i.e., a semigroup for which every
a\in S
admits a unique
a^{-1}\in S
satisfying
aa^{-1}a=a
and
a^{-1}a^{-1}=a^{-1}
, having central idempotents.
Into detail, a triple
(S,+,\circ)
is a
weak (left) brace
if
\left(S,+\right)
and
\left(S,\circ\right)
both are inverse semigroups and they hold

\displaystyle a\circ\left(b+c\right)=a\circ b-a+a\circ c\qquad\text{and}\qquad a\circ a^{-}=-a+a,

for all
a,b,c\in S
, where
-a
and
a^{-}
denote the inverses of
a
with respect to
+
and
\circ
, respectively, and we assume that the multiplication has higher precedence than the addition. As demonstrated in
[
13
, Thereom 10]
, the additive semigroup of any weak brace is a Clifford semigroup. Moreover, if both
(S,+)
and
(S,\circ)
are groups, then
(S,+,\circ)
is a
skew brace
; in addition, if
(S,+)
is abelian, then
(S,+,\circ)
is a
brace

[
32
]
. Furthermore, weak braces form a subclass of
inverse (left) semi-braces

[
14
]
, namely, triples
(S,+,\circ)
with
(S,+)
a semigroup and
(S,\circ)
an inverse semigroup such that

a\circ(b+c)=a\circ b+a\circ\left(a^{-}+b\right),

for all
a,b\in S
. More specifically, weak braces are all the inverse semi-braces having
(S,+)
as a Clifford semigroup and in which
-a+a\circ b=a\circ\left(a^{-}+b\right)
, for all
a,b\in S
.
According to
[
13
, Theorem 11]
, given a weak brace
(S,+,\circ)
, the map
r:S\times S\to S\times S
defined by

\displaystyle r\left(a,b\right)=\left(-a+a\circ b,\left(-a+a\circ b\right)^{-}\circ a\circ b\right),

for all
a,b\in S
, is a solution. Such a map
r
has a behaviour close to bijectivity, since there exists the solution
r^{op}
associated to the
opposite weak brace

\left(S,+^{op},\circ\right)
of
S
, where
a+^{op}b:=b+a
, for all
a,b\in S
, such that

\displaystyle r\,r^{op}\,r=r,\qquad r^{op}\,r\,r^{op}=r^{op},\qquad\text{and}\qquad rr^{op}=r^{op}r,

namely,
r
is a
completely regular element in
\Map(S\times S)
. In this context, one can note that weak braces are instances of semitrusses that produces solutions but they are not necessarily YB–semitrusses, see
[
6
]
and
[
20
]
.

The aim of this paper is to show how to obtain weak braces from Rota–Baxter operators defined on Clifford semigroups. For this reason, we introduce the notion of

Rota–Baxter operator on a Clifford semigroup
(S,+)
, i.e.,
a map
\mathfrak{R}:S\to S
satisfying the relations

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)+\mathfrak{R}\left(b\right)=\mathfrak{R}\left(a+\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)+b-\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad a+\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)-\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)=a,

for all
a,b\in S
. Clearly, such definition includes the original one given for groups, cf.
[
24
, Definition 2.2]
.
Any Rota–Baxter operator
\mathfrak{R}
on a Clifford semigroup
(S,+)
determines a dual weak brace
S_{\mathfrak{R}}=(S,+,\circ)
with

\displaystyle a\circ b=a+\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)+b-\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right),

for all
a,b\in S
, that belongs to the class of weak braces having also
(S,\circ)
as a Clifford semigroup. We name such particular weak braces
dual weak braces
. The solution associated to a dual weak brace has a behaviour close to the non-degeneracy since also the maps
\lambda_{a}
and
\rho_{b}
are completely regular in

\Map(S)
. In addition, we prove that the opposite solution
r^{op}
of the solution
r
associated to a dual weak brace
S_{\mathfrak{R}}
derives from the dual weak brace obtained through the Rota–Baxter operator
\mathfrak{R}^{op}=-a+\mathfrak{R}(a)
, for every
a\in S
.

This fact has motivated us to deepen the structure of the dual weak brace on which we study the notion of ideal that allows us to define its quotient structure. In particular, we specialise ideals of dual weak braces obtained from Rota–Baxter operators. Besides, we introduce the
socle
of a dual weak brace, a special ideal already investigated for braces
[
32
]
and other connected structures
[
22
,
11
,
16
,
10
]
. Moreover, we show how to construct new ideals starting from two given ones.

Furthermore, we provide a method to construct Rota–Baxter operators on a Clifford semigroup seen as strong semilattices of groups, each of which admits a Rota–Baxter operator. Besides, we give a description of all endomorphisms
\mathfrak{R}
of Clifford semigroups that are Rota–Baxter operators for which
\im\mathfrak{R}
is commutative. Consistently with
[
27
, Proposition 5.1]
, these special operators determine
bi-weak braces
, i.e., special dual weak braces in which the roles of the sum and the multiplication can be reversed. Finally, we characterise all Rota–Baxter endomorphisms of groups that are also idempotent.

1
Basic results on weak braces

The aim of this preliminary section is to give essential results on the structures of weak braces, recently introduced in
[
13
]
to find solutions of the Yang–Baxter equation. To this purpose, we initially recall some basics on Clifford semigroups, covered in detail in the books
[
19
,
25
,
28
]
.

A semigroup
S
is called an
inverse semigroup
if, for each
a\in S
, there exists a unique element
a^{-1}
of
S
such that
a=aa^{-1}a
and
a^{-1}=a^{-1}aa^{-1}
. We call such an element
a^{-1}
the
inverse
of
a
. Evidently, every group is an inverse semigroup. The behavior of inverse elements in an inverse semigroup
S
is similar to that in a group, since
(ab)^{-1}=b^{-1}a^{-1}
and
(a^{-1})^{-1}=a
, for all
a,b\in S
.

The set
\E(S)
of the idempotents of an inverse semigroup
S
is a commutative subsemigroup of
S
. Clearly,
e=e^{-1}
, for every
e\in\E(S)
. In addition, the idempotents of
S
are exactly the elements
aa^{-1}
and
a^{-1}a
, with
a\in S
.

If
S
is an inverse semigroup such that
aa^{-1}=a^{-1}a
, for any
a\in S
, then
S
is called
Clifford semigroup
. In this case, we briefly write

\displaystyle a^{0}:=aa^{-1}=a^{-1}a,

for every
a\in S
.
Equivalently, Clifford semigroups are the inverse semigroups in which the idempotents are central.

Moreover, if
\phi:S\to T
is a homomorphism from a Clifford semigroup
S
into a semigroup
T
, then
\im\phi
is a Clifford semigroup,
\phi(a^{-1})=\phi(a)^{-1}
, and
\phi(a^{0})=\phi(a)^{0}
, for any
a\in S
(cf.
[
25
, Lemma 2.4.4]
).

According to
[
25
, Theorem 4.2.1]
, an inverse semigroup
S
is a Clifford semigroup if and only if it is a strong (lower) semilattice
Y
of disjoint groups
G_{\alpha}
, for every
\alpha\in Y
. Specifically, for each pair
\alpha,\beta
of elements of
Y
such that
\alpha\geq\beta
, let
\phi_{\alpha,\beta}:G_{\alpha}\to G_{\beta}
be a homomorphism of groups such that

1.

\phi_{\alpha,\alpha}
is the identical automorphism of
G_{\alpha}
, for every
\alpha\in Y
,

2.

\phi_{\beta,\gamma}{}\phi_{\alpha,\beta}{}=\phi_{\alpha,\gamma}{}
, for all
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in Y
such that
\alpha\geq\beta\geq\gamma
.

Then,
S=\displaystyle\bigcup_{\alpha\in Y}\,G_{\alpha}
endowed with the operation defined by

\displaystyle a\,b:=\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}(a)\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta}(b),

for every
a\in G_{\alpha}
and
b\in G_{\beta}
, is a
strong semilattice
Y
of groups
G_{\alpha}
, usually written as
S=[Y;G_{\alpha};\phi_{\alpha,\beta}]
.

Definition 1
.

[
13
, Definition 5]

Let
S
be a set endowed with two operations
+
and
\circ
such that
\left(S,+\right)
and
\left(S,\circ\right)
are inverse semigroups. Then,
(S,+,\circ)
is a
weak (left) brace
if the following relations

\displaystyle a\circ\left(b+c\right)=a\circ b-a+a\circ c\qquad\text{and}\qquad a\circ a^{-}=-a+a

hold, for all
a,b,c\in S
, where
-a
and
a^{-}
denote the inverses of
a
with respect to
+
and
\circ
, respectively. We call
(S,+)
and
(S,\circ)
the
additive semigroup
and the
multiplicative semigroup
of
\left(S,+,\circ\right)
, respectively, and, since the sets of idempotents
E(S,+)
and
E(S,\circ)
coincide, we denote it simply by
E(S)
.

Given a weak brace
(S,+,\circ)
, let
\lambda_{a}:S\to S
be the map defined by

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}\left(b\right)=-a+a\circ b,

for all
a,b\in S
. The map
\lambda_{a}
can be also written as it is usual in the context of semi-braces
[
11
,
26
]
, namely,
\lambda_{a}\left(b\right)=a\circ\left(a^{-}+b\right)
, for all
a,b\in S
.
Furthermore, the map
\lambda:S\to\End\left(S,+\right),a\mapsto\lambda_{a}
is a homomorphism of the inverse semigroup
\left(S,\circ\right)
into the endomorphism semigroup of
\left(S,+\right)
.

The operations of sum and multiplication of any weak brace are closely related by the following relation

\displaystyle a\circ b=a+\lambda_{a}\left(b\right),

(1)

for all
a,b\in S
, as proved in
[
13
, Lemma 1]
. In particular, they coincide on
E(S)
. Indeed, if
e_{1},e_{2}\in E(S)
, we get

\displaystyle e_{1}+e_{2}

\displaystyle=\left(e_{1}+e_{2}\right)\circ\left(e_{1}+e_{2}\right)=\left(e_{1}+e_{2}\right)\circ e_{1}-\left(e_{1}+e_{2}\right)+\left(e_{1}+e_{2}\right)\circ e_{2}

\displaystyle=e_{1}\circ\left(e_{1}+e_{2}\right)+e_{1}+e_{2}+e_{2}\circ\left(e_{2}+e_{1}\right)=e_{1}+\lambda_{e_{1}}\left(e_{2}\right)+e_{2}+\lambda_{e_{2}}\left(e_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=e_{1}\circ e_{2}+e_{2}\circ e_{1}

by (
1

\displaystyle=e_{1}\circ e_{2}.

As proved in
[
13
, Theorem 8]
, the additive semigroup of any weak brace

is a Clifford semigroup.
Moreover, the multiplicative semigroup of a weak brace need not be a Clifford semigroup (see, for instance,
[
13
, Example 3]
).
Thus, the following question arises.

Question 1
.

Describe the structure of the multiplicative semigroup of a weak brace.

However, one can find some instances of weak braces having also a Clifford semigroup as a multiplicative structure among
[
12
,

14

Definition 2
.

A weak brace
(S,+,\circ)
is said to be a
dual weak brace
if
\left(S,\circ\right)
is a Clifford semigroup.

In the case of a dual weak brace
S
, we highlight that
a^{0}
is also
a^{-}\circ a
, for every
a\in S
.

Note that any Clifford semigroup
\left(S,\circ\right)
determines two dual weak braces, by setting
a+b:=a\circ b
or
a+b:=b\circ a
for all
a,b\in S
. The first weak brace is the
trivial weak brace
and the second one the
almost trivial weak brace
(see
[
13
, Example 1]
).

Below, we show that in any weak brace the sum can be written in terms of the multiplication as it is already known in the context of skew braces, see
[

.

Proposition 3
.

Let
S
be a weak brace. Then,

a+b=a\circ\lambda_{a^{-}}\left(b\right),

for all
a,b\in S
.

Proof.

Let
a,b\in S
. Observing that by
[
13
, Lemma 1]

a\circ(-b)=a-a\circ b+a
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(a\circ\lambda_{a^{-}}\left(b\right)\right)^{-}

\displaystyle=\left(a+b\right)^{-}\circ\left(a-a\right)=\left(a+b\right)^{-}\circ a-\left(a+b\right)^{-}+\left(a+b\right)^{-}\circ\left(-a\right)

\displaystyle=\left(a+b\right)^{-}\circ a-\left(a+b\right)^{-}\circ a+\left(a+b\right)^{-}

\displaystyle=\left(a+b\right)^{-}-\left(a+b\right)^{-}\circ a+\left(a+b\right)^{-}\circ a

\displaystyle=\left(a+b\right)^{-}+\lambda_{\left(a+b\right)^{-}}\left(-a\right)-\left(a+b\right)^{-}+\left(a+b\right)^{-}\circ a

by (
1
)

\displaystyle=\left(a+b\right)^{-}+\left(a+b\right)^{-}\circ\left(a+b-a+a\right)

\displaystyle=\left(a+b\right)^{-}+\left(a+b\right)^{-}\circ\left(a+b\right)

\displaystyle=\left(a+b\right)^{-}-\left(a+b\right)^{-}+\left(a+b\right)^{-}

\displaystyle=\left(a+b\right)^{-}.

Therefore, the claim follows.
∎

The trivial and the almost trivial weak braces lie in a special class of dual weak braces that we call
bi-weak braces
.
We underline that such a definition has been originally introduced by Childs in
[
18
]
for the particular case of skew braces.

Definition 4
.

Let
(S,+,\circ)
be a weak brace. Then,
S
is called a
bi-weak brace
if
(S,\circ,+)
is a weak brace, i.e., the relations

\displaystyle a+(b\circ c)=\left(a+b\right)\circ a^{-}\circ\left(a+c\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad a-a=a^{-}\circ a

hold, for all
a,b,c\in S
.

The following is a general way to construct weak braces involving strong semilattices of inverse semi-braces provided in
[
14
, Proposition 4]
and also used in the case of generalized left semi-braces in
[
12
]
.

Proposition 5
.

Let
Y
be a (lower) semilattice,
\left\{S_{\alpha}\ \left|\ \alpha\in Y\right.\right\}
a family of disjoint weak braces. For each pair
\alpha,\beta
of elements of
Y
such that
\alpha\geq\beta
, let
\phi_{\alpha,\beta}:S_{\alpha}\to S_{\beta}
be a homomorphism of weak braces such that

1.

\phi_{\alpha,\alpha}
is the identical automorphism of
S_{\alpha}
, for every
\alpha\in Y
,

2.

\phi_{\beta,\gamma}{}\phi_{\alpha,\beta}{}=\phi_{\alpha,\gamma}{}
, for all
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in Y
such that
\alpha\geq\beta\geq\gamma
.

Then,
S=\bigcup\left\{S_{\alpha}\ \left|\ \alpha\in Y\right.\right\}
endowed with the addition and the multiplication defined by

\displaystyle a+b:=\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}(a)+\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta}(b)\quad\text{and}\quad a\circ b:=\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}(a)\circ\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta}(b),

for all
a\in S_{\alpha}
and
b\in S_{\beta}
, is a weak brace, called
strong semilattice
S
of weak braces
S_{\alpha}
and is denoted by
S=[Y;S_{\alpha};\phi_{\alpha,\beta}]
.

Proof.

Since
S
is an inverse semi-brace and
(S,+)
clearly is an inverse semigroup, by
[
13
, Proposition 16]
, it is sufficient to show that
a\circ\left(a^{-}+b\right)=-a+a\circ b
, for all
a,b\in S
.
If
a\in S_{\alpha}
and
b\in S_{\beta}
, then

\displaystyle a\circ\left(a^{-}+b\right)

\displaystyle=a\circ\left(\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(a^{-}\right)+\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta}\left(b\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(a\right)\circ\phi_{\alpha\beta,\alpha\beta}\left(\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(a^{-}\right)+\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta}\left(b\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(a\right)\circ\left(\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(a\right)^{-}+\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta}\left(b\right)\right)

\displaystyle=-\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(a\right)+\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(a\right)\circ\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta}\left(b\right)

S_{\alpha\beta}
is a weak brace

\displaystyle=\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(-a\right)+\phi_{\alpha\beta,\alpha\beta}\left(\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(a\right)\circ\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta}\left(b\right)\right)

\displaystyle=-a+\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(a\right)\circ\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta}\left(b\right)

\displaystyle=-a+a\circ b.

Therefore, the claim follows.
∎

Corollary 6
.

A strong semilattice
S=[Y;S_{\alpha};\phi_{\alpha,\beta}]
is a dual weak brace if and only if
S_{\alpha}
is a dual weak brace, for every
\alpha\in Y
, as well.
Specifically,

is a bi-weak brace if and only if
S_{\alpha}
is a bi-weak brace.

2
Rota–Baxter operators on Clifford semigroups

In this section, we present and investigate the Rota–Baxter operators on a Clifford semigroup, consistently with that introduced for groups in
[
24
]
. Moreover, we provide some examples.

Definition 7
.

Let
(S,+)
be a Clifford semigroup. A map
\mathfrak{R}:S\to S
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)+\mathfrak{R}\left(b\right)=\mathfrak{R}\left(a+\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)+b-\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad a+\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)^{0}=a,

for all
a,b\in S
, is called
Rota–Baxter operator
on
(S,+)
.

If
(S,+)
and
(T,+)
are Clifford semigroups, then two Rota–Baxter operators
\mathfrak{R}
and
\mathfrak{T}
on
S
and
T
, respectively, are said to be
equivalent
if there exists an isomorphism
\varphi
from
(S,+)
to
(T,+)
such that
\mathfrak{R}=\varphi^{-1}\mathfrak{T}\varphi
.

Example 1
.

Let
\left(S,+\right)
be a Clifford semigroup and
\varphi
an idempotent endomorphism of
\left(S,+\right)
such that
\varphi\left(e\right)=e
, for every
e\in E\left(S,+\right)
. Then, the map
\mathfrak{R}:=-\varphi
is a Rota–Baxter operator on
S
.

Easy examples of operators belonging to the previous class are the
elementary Rota–Baxter operators
, namely, the
maps
\mathfrak{E}
and
\mathfrak{O}
from a Clifford semigroup
(S,+)
into itself given by
\mathfrak{E}(a)=a^{0}
and
\mathfrak{O}(a)=-a
, for every
a\in S
.
Such a terminology is analogue to that used in
[
2
]
. One can observe that in the context of groups such Rota–Baxter operators are not equivalent.
In addition, if
(S,+)
is a Clifford monoid with identity
0
, the map
\mathfrak{R}(a)=0
, for every
a\in S
, is a Rota–Baxter operator on
(S,+)
.

If
\mathfrak{R}
is a Rota–Baxter operator on a Clifford semigroup
(S,+)
, the map
\mathfrak{R}^{op}:S\to S
, given by
\mathfrak{R}^{op}(a)=-a+\mathfrak{R}(-a)
, for every
a\in S
, is still a Rota–Baxter operator on
(S,+)
, which we call
opposite Rota–Baxter operator
of
\mathfrak{R}
. We observe that the elementary Rota–Baxter operators can be obtained one from the other since
\mathfrak{O}=\mathfrak{E}^{op}
and
\mathfrak{E}=\mathfrak{O}^{op}
.

The following are two examples of Rota–Baxter operators on a Clifford monoid which admits an exact factorization. We recall that a Clifford semigroup
(S,+)
admits an
exact factorization

(U,V)
if
U
and
V
are Clifford subsemigroups of
S
and any element
a\in S
can be written in a unique way as
a=u_{a}+v_{a}
, with
u_{a}\in U
and
v_{a}\in V
. In such a case, it holds that
U\cap V=\{0\}
, with
0
both right identity of
U
and left identity of
V
, cf.
[
9
, Theorem 2]
. As a consequence,
(S,+)
is a Clifford monoid.

Example 2
.

Let
(U,V)
be an exact factorization of a Clifford monoid
(S,+)
.
Then, the maps
\mathfrak{R}:S\to S
and
\mathfrak{T}:S\to S
given by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}(a)=-v_{a}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathfrak{T}(a)=u_{a}^{0}-v_{a}

for every
a\in S
, respectively, are two Rota–Baxter operators on
S
.

We underline that the map
\mathfrak{R}
is a Rota–Baxter operator also in the case of a right univocal factorizable Clifford semigroup. To this purpose, we recall that, in general, a factorization
\left(U,V\right)
of a semigroup
S
is
right univocal
if

\displaystyle\forall\ u,u^{\prime}\in U,\ v,v^{\prime}\in V\quad u+v=u^{\prime}+v^{\prime}\implies v=v^{\prime},

see, for instance,
[
9

.

In the next result, we introduce a method for constructing a new Rota–Baxter operator on a Clifford semigroup seen as strong semilattice
Y
of groups
G_{\alpha}
starting from given Rota–Baxter operators on each group
G_{\alpha}
. Hereinafter, we denote by
0_{\alpha}
the identity of
G_{\alpha}
, for every
\alpha\in Y
.

Theorem 8
.

Let
(S,+)
be a Clifford semigroup,
S=[Y;G_{\alpha};\phi_{\alpha,\beta}]
and assume that
\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}
is a Rota–Baxter operator on the group
(G_{\alpha},+)
, for every
\alpha\in Y
.
Then, the map
\mathfrak{R}:S\to S
given by

\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)=\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}\left(a\right)
, for every
a\in G_{\alpha}
, is a Rota–Baxter operator on
(S,+)
if and only if the condition

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{\beta}\phi_{\alpha,\beta}=\phi_{\alpha,\beta}\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha},

(2)

is satisfied, for all
\alpha,\beta\in Y
such that
\alpha\geq\beta
. We call such an operator
\mathfrak{R}

strong Rota–Baxter operator on
(S,+)
.

Proof.

Initially, assume that (
2
) holds.
If
a\in G_{\alpha}
and
b\in G_{\beta}
, we have that

\displaystyle a+\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)+b-\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)

\displaystyle=\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(a+\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}\left(a\right)\right)+\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta}\left(b\right)-\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}\left(a\right)

\displaystyle=\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(a\right)+\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}\left(a\right)+\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta}\left(b\right)-\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}\left(a\right)

\displaystyle=\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(a\right)+\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha\beta}\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(a\right)+\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta}\left(b\right)-\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha\beta}\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(a\right)

by (
2
)

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}\left(a+\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)+b-\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha\beta}\left(\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(a\right)+\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha\beta}\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(a\right)+\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta}\left(b\right)-\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha\beta}\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(a\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha\beta}\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(a\right)+\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha\beta}\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta}\left(b\right)

\displaystyle=\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}\left(a\right)+\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta}\mathfrak{R}_{\beta}\left(b\right)

by (
2
)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)+\mathfrak{R}\left(b\right).

Therefore, since the second condition in
7
is trivially satisfied,
\mathfrak{R}
is a Rota–Baxter operator on
S
.

Conversely, assume that
\mathfrak{R}
is a Rota–Baxter operator on
(S,+)
. Then, if
a\in G_{\alpha}
, by
[
2
, Lemma 2.5]
we get

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)+\mathfrak{R}\left(0_{\beta}\right)=\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}\left(a\right)+\mathfrak{R}_{\beta}\left(0_{\beta}\right)=\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}\left(a\right)+0_{\alpha\beta}=\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}\left(a\right).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}\left(a+\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)+0_{\beta}-\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha\beta}\left(\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(a\right)+\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}\left(a\right)+\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta}\left(0_{\beta}\right)-\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta}\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}\left(a\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha\beta}\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(a\right),

therefore (
2
) holds.
∎

We observe that not all Rota-Baxter operators on Clifford semigroups
can be constructed as in

Example 3
.

Let
S=[Y;G_{\alpha};\phi_{\alpha,\beta}]

\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}

\mathfrak{R}:S\to S

\mathfrak{R}(a)=\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}(a)

Finally, let us show some useful properties of Rota–Baxter operators on Clifford semigroups.

Proposition 9
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a Rota–Baxter operator on a Clifford semigroup
(S,+)
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}\left(E\left(S\right)\right)\subseteq E\left(S\right).

In particular, if
(S,+)
is a Clifford monoid with identity
0
, it holds that
\mathfrak{R}\left(0\right)=0
.

Proof.

If
e\in E\left(S\right)
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}\left(e\right)=\mathfrak{R}\left(e+\mathfrak{R}\left(e\right)-\mathfrak{R}\left(e\right)\right)=\mathfrak{R}\left(e+\mathfrak{R}\left(e\right)+e-\mathfrak{R}\left(e\right)\right)=\mathfrak{R}\left(e\right)+\mathfrak{R}\left(e\right).

Besides , if
(S,+)
is a Clifford monoid with identity
0
, since
\mathfrak{R}(0)
is idempotent, we get

\displaystyle 0=0+\mathfrak{R}\left(0\right)-\mathfrak{R}\left(0\right)=\mathfrak{R}\left(0\right)+\mathfrak{R}\left(0\right)=\mathfrak{R}\left(0\right),

thus the claim follows.
∎

In general, it is not true that
E(S)\subseteq\mathfrak{R}\left(E\left(S\right)\right)

(S,+)
with

.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a Rota–Baxter operator on a Clifford semigroup

.

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}(a)+\mathfrak{R}\left(-\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)-a+\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)\right)=\mathfrak{R}\left(a^{0}\right).

(3)

Thus, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}(a)+\mathfrak{R}\left(-\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)-a+\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)\right)+\mathfrak{R}(a)

by (

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{R}(a)+\mathfrak{R}\left(a^{0}\right)

by

by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}\left(-\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)-a+\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)\right)+\mathfrak{R}(a)+\mathfrak{R}\left(-\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)-a+\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\mathfrak{R}\left(-\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)-a+\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)\right)+\mathfrak{R}\left(a^{0}\right)

by (

\displaystyle\quad=\mathfrak{R}\left(a^{0}\right)+\mathfrak{R}\left(-\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)-a+\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)\right)

by

\displaystyle\quad=\mathfrak{R}\left(a^{0}+\mathfrak{R}\left(a^{0}\right)-\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)-a+\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)-\mathfrak{R}\left(a^{0}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\mathfrak{R}\left(-\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)-a+\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)+\mathfrak{R}\left(a^{0}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\mathfrak{R}\left(-\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)-a+\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)\right)

3
Rota–Baxter endomorphisms

This section focuses on endomorphisms of Clifford semigroups that are Rota–Baxter operators, namely Rota–Baxter endomorphisms. Specifically, we give a description of such maps
f
for which
\im f
is commutative. In addition, we characterize Rota–Baxter endomorphisms of groups that are also idempotent.

Definition 11
.

Let
(S,+)
be a Clifford semigroup. Any endomorphism
\mathfrak{R}:S\to S
that is a Rota–Baxter operator is called a
Rota–Baxter endomorphism
of
(S,+)
.

Clearly, the constant map of value
0
of a Clifford monoid
(S,+)
is an easy example of Rota–Baxter endomorphism.

From now on, given two Clifford semigroups
(S,+)
and
(T,+)
, we will denote by
\Hom\left(S,T\right)
the set of all the homomorphisms from
(S,+)
to
(T,+)
. If
f\in\Hom\left(S,T\right)
is such that
\im f
is commutative, we say that
f
is a
commutative homomorphism
. In the context of groups, these maps are named
abelian homomorphisms

[
17
]
.

If
G
is a group, one has that the abelian endomorphisms of
G
are Rota–Baxter operators.
Instead, if
\left(S,+\right)
is a Clifford semigroup, not every commutative endomorphism of
(S,+)
is a Rota–Baxter operator. Indeed, if
|E(S)|>1
and
e\in E(S)
, the constant map of value
e
is such an example. Thus, our aim is to show how to construct all the commutative Rota–Baxter endomorphisms on a Clifford semigroup
S=[Y;G_{\alpha};\phi_{\alpha,\beta}]
starting from given abelian homomorphisms. To this purpose, we recall the following result.

Lemma 12

(cf. Proposition II.2.8,
[
28
]
)
.

Let
S=[Y;G_{\alpha};\phi_{\alpha,\beta}]
be a Clifford semigroup,
h:Y\to Y
an endomorphism, and consider a family of maps
f_{\alpha}\in\Hom\left(G_{\alpha},G_{h\left(\alpha\right)}\right)
, for every
\alpha\in Y
, such that

\displaystyle f_{\beta}\phi_{\alpha,\beta}=\phi_{h\left(\alpha\right),h\left(\beta\right)}f_{\alpha}

holds, for all
\alpha,\beta\in Y
such that
\alpha\geq\beta
. Then, the map
f:S\to S
given by
f(a)=f_{\alpha}(a)
, for every
a\in G_{\alpha}
, is an endomorphism of
S
.
Conversely, every endomorphism of
S
can be so constructed.

Theorem 13
.

Let
S=[Y;G_{\alpha};\phi_{\alpha,\beta}]
be a Clifford semigroup,
h:Y\to Y
an endomorphism such that
\alpha\leq h\left(\alpha\right)
, and consider a family of abelian homomorphisms
f_{\alpha}\in\Hom\left(G_{\alpha},G_{h\left(\alpha\right)}\right)
, for every
\alpha\in Y
.
If the following condition is satisfied

\displaystyle f_{\beta}\phi_{\alpha,\beta}=\phi_{h\left(\alpha\right),h\left(\beta\right)}f_{\alpha},

(5)

for all
\alpha,\beta\in Y
such that
\alpha\geq\beta
, then the map
\mathfrak{R}:S\to S
given by
\mathfrak{R}(a)=f_{\alpha}(a)
, for every
a\in G_{\alpha}
, is a commutative Rota–Baxter endomorphism of
S
.
Conversely, any commutative Rota–Baxter endomorphism on
S
can be so constructed.

Proof.

Initially, by
Lemma

12
, note that
\mathfrak{R}
is an endomorphism of
S
and obviously
\mathfrak{R}
is commutative since
f_{\alpha}
is abelian, for every
\alpha\in Y
.
Now, if
a\in G_{\alpha}
and
b\in G_{\beta}
, with
\alpha,\beta\in Y
such that
\alpha\geq\beta
, we have that

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle+\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)+b-\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\phi_{\alpha,\alpha h\left(\alpha\right)}\left(a\right)+\phi_{h\left(\alpha\right),\alpha h\left(\alpha\right)}f_{\alpha}\left(a\right)\right)+\left(\phi_{\beta,\beta h\left(\alpha\right)}\left(b\right)-\phi_{h\left(\alpha\right),\beta h\left(\alpha\right)}f_{\alpha}\left(a\right)\right)

\displaystyle=a+\phi_{h\left(\alpha\right),\alpha}f_{\alpha}\left(a\right)+\phi_{\beta,\beta h\left(\alpha\right)}\left(b\right)-\phi_{h\left(\alpha\right),\beta h\left(\alpha\right)}f_{\alpha}\left(a\right)

\displaystyle=\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta h\left(\alpha\right)}\left(a+\phi_{h\left(\alpha\right),\alpha}f_{\alpha}\left(a\right)\right)+\phi_{\beta h\left(\alpha\right),\alpha\beta}\left(\phi_{\beta,\beta h\left(\alpha\right)}\left(b\right)-\phi_{h\left(\alpha\right),\beta h\left(\alpha\right)}f_{\alpha}\left(a\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(a\right)+\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\phi_{h\left(\alpha\right),\alpha}f_{\alpha}\left(a\right)+\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta}\left(b\right)-\phi_{h\left(\alpha\right),\alpha\beta}f_{\alpha}\left(a\right)

\displaystyle=\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(a\right)+\phi_{h\left(\alpha\right),\alpha\beta}f_{\alpha}\left(a\right)+\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta}\left(b\right)-\phi_{h\left(\alpha\right),\alpha\beta}f_{\alpha}\left(a\right)

and, consequently,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}

\displaystyle\left(a+\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)+b-\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)\right)

\displaystyle=f_{\alpha\beta}\left(\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(a\right)+\phi_{h\left(\alpha\right),\alpha\beta}f_{\alpha}\left(a\right)+\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta}\left(a\right)-\phi_{h\left(\alpha\right),\alpha\beta}f_{\alpha}\left(a\right)\right)

\displaystyle=f_{\alpha\beta}\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}\left(a\right)+f_{\alpha\beta}\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta}\left(b\right)

f_{\alpha\beta}
is abelian

\displaystyle=\phi_{h\left(\alpha\right),h\left(\alpha\beta\right)}f_{\alpha}\left(a\right)+\phi_{{}_{,}h\left(\beta\right),h\left(\alpha\beta\right)}f_{\beta}\left(b\right)

by (
5
)

\displaystyle=f_{\alpha}\left(a\right)+f_{\beta}\left(b\right)=\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)+\mathfrak{R}\left(b\right)

Moreover, it holds that

\displaystyle a+\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)^{0}

\displaystyle=a+f_{\alpha}\left(0_{\alpha}\right)=\phi_{\alpha,\alpha h\left(\alpha\right)}\left(a\right)+0_{\alpha h\left(\alpha\right)}=\phi_{\alpha,\alpha}\left(a\right)+0_{\alpha}=a.

Therefore,
\mathfrak{R}
is a commutative Rota–Baxter endomorphism of
S
.

Conversely, assume that
\mathfrak{R}
is a commutative Rota–Baxter endomorphism of
S
. Let
h:Y\to Y
be the map such that
h\left(\alpha\right)=\chi
if
\mathfrak{R}\left(0_{\alpha}\right)=0_{\chi}
. Note that
h\left(\alpha\right)\geq\alpha
, since if
a\in G_{\alpha}
, by
2.
in
10
, we obtain

\displaystyle a=a+\mathfrak{R}\left(a^{0}\right)=a+0_{\chi}\in G_{\alpha\chi},

thus
\alpha\chi=\alpha
, and so
h(\alpha)\geq\alpha
.
Now, denoted by
f_{\alpha}:=\mathfrak{R}_{|_{G_{\alpha}}}
, the map
\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}:=\phi_{h\left(\alpha\right),\alpha}f_{\alpha}
is clearly an abelian endomorphism of
G_{\alpha}
, for every
\alpha\in Y
. Moreover, if
a\in G_{\alpha}
and
\alpha,\beta\in Y
with
\alpha\geq\beta
, we get

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{\beta}\phi_{\alpha,\beta}\left(a\right)

\displaystyle=\phi_{h\left(\beta\right),\beta}f_{\beta}\phi_{\alpha,\beta}\left(a\right)=\phi_{h\left(\beta\right),\beta}\mathfrak{R}\left(a+0_{\beta}\right)=\phi_{h\left(\beta\right),\beta}\left(\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)+\mathfrak{R}\left(0_{\beta}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\phi_{h\left(\beta\right),\beta}\phi_{h\left(\alpha\right),h\left(\alpha\beta\right)}\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)=\phi_{h\left(\alpha\right),\beta}f_{\alpha}\left(a\right)=\phi_{\alpha,\beta}\phi_{h\left(\alpha\right),\alpha}f_{\alpha}\left(a\right)=\phi_{\alpha,\beta}R_{\alpha}\left(a\right).

Therefore, the claim follows.
∎

Obviously, any Rota–Baxter endomorphism is a strong Rota–Baxter operator only in the case
h=\id_{Y}
.

Remark 2
.

Let us note that if the endomorphism
h
in
13
is surjective, then
\mathfrak{R}\left(E\left(S\right)\right)=E\left(S\right)
. Indeed, if
\beta=h\left(\alpha\right)
for a certain
\alpha\in Y
, observing that
\alpha\leq\beta
, we have that

0_{\beta}=0_{h\left(\alpha\right)}=\phi_{h\left(\beta\right),h\left(\alpha\right)}\left(0_{h\left(\beta\right)}\right)\underset{\eqref{eq:Worawiset}}{=}f_{\alpha}\phi_{\beta,\alpha}\left(0_{\beta}\right)=f_{\alpha}\left(0_{\alpha}\right)=\mathfrak{R}\left(0_{\alpha}\right),

hence, by
9
, the claim follows.

To conclude this section, we give a description of all idempotent Rota–Baxter endomorphisms of not necessarily abelian groups
\left(G,+\right)
.
In this case,
G
is exactly factorizable in
\ker\mathfrak{R}
and
\im\mathfrak{R}
.

Theorem 14
.

Let
(G,+)
be a group. If
N\unlhd G
is such that
G/N
is abelian and
\mathcal{S}
is a set of representatives of
G/N
that is a subgroup of
G
, then any map
\mathfrak{R}:G\to G
such that
\im\mathfrak{R}=\mathcal{S}
and
\mathfrak{R}\left(g\right)\in N+g
, for every
g\in G
, is an idempotent Rota–Baxter endomorphism of
G
.

Conversely, if
\mathfrak{R}
is an idempotent Rota–Baxter endomorphism of
G
, then
G/\ker\mathfrak{R}
is abelian,
\im\mathfrak{R}
is a subgroup of
G
that is also a set of representatives of
G/\ker\mathfrak{R}
, and
\mathfrak{R}\left(g\right)\in\ker\mathfrak{R}+g
, for every
g\in G
.

Proof.

Initially, assume that
N\unlhd G
and that
G/N
is abelian, and let
\mathfrak{R}:G\to G
be a map such that
\im\mathfrak{R}=\mathcal{S}
and
\mathfrak{R}\left(g\right)\in N+g
, for every
g\in G
. Then, we clearly have that
\mathfrak{R}
is a homomorphism from
G
to
\mathcal{S}
. Besides, if
g,h\in G
, by
[
31
, Theorem 2.23]
, since
G^{\prime}\subseteq N
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}\left(g+\mathfrak{R}\left(g\right)+h-\mathfrak{R}\left(g\right)\right)

\displaystyle\in N+g+\mathfrak{R}\left(g\right)+h-\mathfrak{R}\left(g\right)=N+g+g+h-g

\displaystyle=N+g+[g,h]+h\subseteq N+g+h.

Moreover, since
\mathfrak{R}\left(g\right)=n+g
, for a certain
n\in N
, we get

\mathfrak{R}^{2}\left(g\right)=\mathfrak{R}\left(n\right)+\mathfrak{R}\left(g\right)=\mathfrak{R}\left(g\right)
,
hence the first part of the claim follows.

Conversely, assume that
\mathfrak{R}:G\to G
is an idempotent Rota–Baxter endomorphism of
G
. Then, obviously,
\im\mathfrak{R}
is a subgroup of
G
. Moreover, if
g,h\in G
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}\left(g+h-g-h\right)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{R}\left(g\right)-\mathfrak{R}\left(h\right)+\mathfrak{R}\left(h\right)+\mathfrak{R}^{2}\left(h\right)+\mathfrak{R}\left(-g\right)-\mathfrak{R}^{2}\left(h\right)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{R}\left(g\right)-\mathfrak{R}\left(h\right)+\mathfrak{R}\left(h+\mathfrak{R}\left(h\right)-g-\mathfrak{R}\left(h\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{R}\left(g\right)-\mathfrak{R}\left(h\right)+\mathfrak{R}\left(h\right)-\mathfrak{R}\left(g\right)=0,

hence
g+h-g-h\in\ker\mathfrak{R}
, i.e.,
G/\ker\mathfrak{R}
is abelian.
Now, if
g\in G
, since

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}\left(\mathfrak{R}\left(g\right)-g\right)=\mathfrak{R}^{2}\left(g\right)-\mathfrak{R}\left(g\right)=0,

we obtain that
\mathfrak{R}\left(g\right)=\mathfrak{R}\left(g\right)-g+g\in\ker\mathfrak{R}+g
. Thus,
\mathfrak{R}\left(g\right)\in\mathfrak{R}\left(G\right)\cap\left(\ker\mathfrak{R}+g\right)
. In addition,
if
\mathfrak{R}\left(h\right)\in\mathfrak{R}\left(G\right)\cap\left(\ker\mathfrak{R}+g\right)
, it follows that
\mathfrak{R}\left(g-h\right)=\mathfrak{R}\left(g\right)-\mathfrak{R}\left(h\right)\in\ker\mathfrak{R}
, hence

\mathfrak{R}\left(g-h\right)=\mathfrak{R}^{2}\left(g-h\right)=0
, namely
g-h\in\ker\mathfrak{R}
. It follows that
g=n+h
, with
n\in\ker\mathfrak{R}
, thus

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}\left(g\right)=\mathfrak{R}\left(n\right)+\mathfrak{R}\left(h\right)=\mathfrak{R}\left(h\right).

Therefore,
\im\mathfrak{R}
is a set of representatives of
G/\ker\mathfrak{R}
.
∎

4
Dual weak braces obtained by Rota–Baxter operators

In this section, we illustrate a method for obtaining a dual weak brace
S
starting from a given Rota–Baxter operator on a Clifford semigroup
(S,+)
. This construction is inspired by that of skew braces due to Bardakov and Gubarev
[
3
, Proposition 3.1]
. Furthermore, we show that every commutative Rota–Baxter endomorphism determines a bi-weak brace.

Lemma 15
.

Let
(S,+)
be a Clifford semigroup and
\mathfrak{R}:S\to S
a Rota–Baxter operator on
(S,+)
. Set

a\circ b:=a+\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)+b-\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)
,
for all
a,b\in S
. Then,
(S,\circ)
is a Clifford semigroup.

Proof.

It is a routine computation to verify that

5
Solutions associated to Rota–Baxter operators

In this section, we make explicit the solutions associated to dual weak braces obtained through Rota-Baxter operators. In particular, such maps have a behaviour near to bijectivity and non-degeneracy.

Let us recall that every weak brace
(S,+,\circ)
gives rise to a solution
r:S\times S\to S\times S
defined by

\displaystyle r\left(a,b\right)=\left(-a+a\circ b,\ \left(-a+a\circ b\right)^{-}\circ a\circ b\right),

(see
[
13
, Theorem 11]

In particular, the images of

can be written by the maps
\lambda_{a}
and
\rho_{b}:S\to S
given by

\displaystyle\rho_{b}\left(a\right)=\lambda_{a}\left(b\right)^{-}\circ a\circ b,

for all

is a semigroup anti-homomorphism of the inverse semigroup
\left(S,\circ\right)
into the monoid
\Map(S)
of the maps from

into itself.

Any solution

associated
to an arbitrary weak brace
(S,+,\circ)
has a behaviour close to bijectivity, namely

is a
completely regular element in
\Map(S\times S)
. Indeed, considered the solution

associated to the

S^{op}
of

, for all

\displaystyle r\,r^{op}\,r=r,\qquad r^{op}\,r\,r^{op}=r^{op},\qquad\text{and}\qquad rr^{op}=r^{op}r.

In general, the maps
\lambda_{a}
and
\rho_{b}
are not completely regular in

\Map(S)
(see
[
13
, Example 3]

is a dual weak brace. Indeed, in this case,

has also a behaviour close to the non-degeneracy because they hold

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}\lambda_{a^{-}}\lambda_{a}=\lambda_{a},\qquad\lambda_{a^{-}}\lambda_{a}\lambda_{a^{-}}=\lambda_{a^{-}},\quad

\displaystyle\text{and}\qquad\lambda_{a}\lambda_{a^{-}}=\lambda_{a^{-}}\lambda_{a},

\displaystyle\rho_{b}\rho_{b^{-}}\rho_{b}=\rho_{b},\qquad\rho_{b^{-}}\rho_{b}\rho_{b^{-}}=\rho_{b^{-}},\qquad

\displaystyle\text{and}\qquad\rho_{b}\rho_{b^{-}}=\rho_{b^{-}}\rho_{b},

for all
a,b\in S
.

is a skew brace,

\displaystyle e_{1}=\lambda_{e_{2}}(e_{1})=-e_{2}+e_{2}\circ e_{1}=e_{2}+e_{2}+e_{1}=e_{2}+e_{1}.

Similarly,

Now, let us make explicit the solution

associated to a dual weak brace

\displaystyle r\left(a,b\right)=\left(\lambda_{a}\left(b\right),\ -\mathfrak{R}\lambda_{a}\left(b\right)-\lambda_{a}\left(b\right)+a+\lambda_{a}\left(b\right)+\mathfrak{R}\lambda_{a}\left(b\right)\right),

\displaystyle\rho_{b}\left(a\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{a}\left(b\right)^{-}\circ\left(a\circ b\right)=\lambda_{a}\left(b\right)^{-}+\mathfrak{R}\left(\lambda_{a}\left(b\right)^{-}\right)+a\circ b-\mathfrak{R}\left(\lambda_{a}\left(b\right)^{-}\right)

6
Ideals of dual weak braces

In this section, we deal with the ideals of a dual weak brace, by extending the already known theory for skew braces. In particular, we introduce the notion of the socle of a dual weak brace.
Finally, we describe the ideals of dual weak braces associated to a given Rota–Baxter operator.

Inverse semigroups are a widely studied class of semigroups for which congruences are well understood. Indeed, in this context it is possible to mimic the group-theoretical treatment of congruences since one can find special subsemigroups that are analogue of normal subgroups of groups.
To this end, we recall the definition of such structures below.

Definition 19
.

[
29
, Definition VI.1.2]

Let
S
be a Clifford semigroup. A subset
N
of
S
is
normal
if it satisfies the following conditions:

1.

E(S)\subseteq N
,

2.

\forall\ a\in S\quad a\in N\Longrightarrow a^{-1}\in N
,

3.

\forall\ a,b\in S\quad a,\;a^{0}b\in N\Longrightarrow ab\in N
,

4.

\forall\ a,b\in S\quad ab\in N\Longrightarrow ba\in N
.

Note that a subset
N
of a group
G
is a normal subgroup of
G
if and only if it is a normal subset of
G
(see
[
29
, Exercises VI.1.22(i)]
. Given a Clifford semigroup
S=[Y;G_{\alpha};\phi_{\alpha,\beta}]
, for each
\alpha\in Y
, let
K_{\alpha}
be a normal subgroup of
G_{\alpha}
and assume that
\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(K_{\alpha})\subseteq K_{\beta}
if
\alpha>\beta
. Set
\psi_{\alpha,\beta}={\phi_{\alpha,\beta}}_{|_{K_{\alpha}}}
if
\alpha\geq\beta
and
K=[Y;K_{\alpha};\psi_{\alpha,\beta}]
, then

is a normal subset of
S
; conversely, every normal subset of
S
is of this form (see
[
28
, Exercises III.1.9(ii)]
).

Clearly, if
S
is a Clifford semigroup, then
E(S)
is a normal subset of
S
. Indeed, the conditions (1), (2), and (3) are trivially satisfied; moreover, the condition (4) follows by
[
29
, Lemma II.2.2(iii)]
.

Remarks 5
.

Let
N
be a normal subset of a Clifford semigroup

.

1.

Observe that
N
is a subsemigroup of

. Indeed, if

, then, by

.

, we obtain that

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}\left(x\right)=-a+a\circ x=a^{0}+\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)+x-\mathfrak{R}\left(a\right)\in I.

Therefore, since the converse is trivial, the claim follows.
∎

If
S
is a dual weak brace, we call
socle
of
S
the following set

\displaystyle\Soc\left(S\right)=\{a\,|\,a\in S,\,\,\forall\ b\in S\quad a+b=a\circ b\quad\text{and}\quad a+b=b+a\}.

Clearly, the previous definition includes that already known in the context of skew braces in

originally introduced in
[

]
.
Moreover, by the following result, we can always consider the quotient structure of a dual weak brace by its socle.

Proposition 23
.

Let
(S,+,\circ)
be a dual weak brace. Then,
\Soc(S)
is an ideal of
S
.

Proof.

Initially, note that
E(S)\subseteq\Soc(S)
. Indeed, if
e\in E(S)
and
a\in S
, it is sufficient to show that

\displaystyle e+a=e\circ a.

(8)

In fact,
e\circ a=e+\lambda_{e}\left(a\right)=e-e+e\circ a=\lambda_{e}\left(a\right),

and, by
3
,

\displaystyle e+a=e\circ\lambda_{e^{-}}\left(a\right)=e\circ e^{-}\circ a=e\circ a.

Now, let us prove that
\lambda_{x}\left(\Soc\left(S\right)\right)\subseteq\Soc\left(S\right)
, for every
x\in S
. If
a\in\Soc(S)
and
b\in S
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{x}(a)\circ b

\displaystyle=x\circ\left(a+x^{-}\right)\circ b=x\circ\left(a\circ x^{-}\circ b\right)=x\circ\left(a+x^{-}\circ b\right)

\displaystyle=x\circ a-x+x^{0}\circ b

\displaystyle=x\circ a-x+x^{0}+b

by (
8
)

\displaystyle=x\circ(a+x^{-})+b=\lambda_{x}(a)+b

and, on the other hand, by the previous computations,

\displaystyle\lambda_{x}(a)+b

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x}(a)\circ b=x\circ a\circ x^{-}\circ b=b^{0}\circ x\circ\left(a\circ x^{-}\circ b\right)

\displaystyle=b^{0}\circ x\circ\left(a+x^{-}\circ b\right)=b\circ\left(b^{-}\circ x\circ\left(x^{-}\circ b+a\right)\right)

\displaystyle=b\circ\lambda_{b^{-}\circ x}\left(a\right)

\displaystyle=b+\lambda_{x}(a)

by
3

Now, it is a routine computation to prove that
\Soc(S)
is closed with respect to the
\circ
. Hence, if
a,a^{0}\circ b\in\Soc(S)
, then
a\circ b=a\circ a^{0}\circ b\in Soc(S)
. Moreover, if
a\in\Soc(S)
, then
a^{-}\in\Soc(S)
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle a^{-}+b

\displaystyle=a^{-}\circ\left(-a+a\circ b\right)

by
3

\displaystyle=a^{-}\circ\left(-a+a+b\right)

\displaystyle=a^{-}\circ a^{0}\circ b

by (
8
)

\displaystyle=a^{-}\circ b.

Considering
b=a
, it follows that

\displaystyle a^{-}+a=a^{0}.

(9)

Thus, we get

\displaystyle b+a^{-}

\displaystyle=b+a^{-}\circ\left(a^{-}\right)^{0}=b+a^{-}\circ a^{0}

\displaystyle=b+a^{-}+a^{0}

by (
8
)

\displaystyle=a^{-}\circ a+b+a^{-}

\displaystyle=a^{-}+a+b+a^{-}

by (
9
)

\displaystyle=a^{-}+b+a+a^{-}

\displaystyle=a^{-}+b+a^{0}

by (
9
)

\displaystyle=a^{-}+b,

and so
a^{-}\in\Soc(S)
. It immediately follows that
-a=\lambda_{a}\left(a^{-}\right)\in\Soc(S)
. Finally, if

,

\displaystyle b\circ a=b\circ\left(a\circ b\circ b^{-}\right)=b\circ\left(a\circ b+b^{-}\right)=b\circ\left(b^{-}+a\circ b\right)=\lambda_{b}(a\circ b),

thus
b\circ a\in\Soc(S)
, i.e.,
\Soc(S)
is a normal subset of
(S,\circ)
.

It is easy to prove that
\Soc(S)
is closed with respect to the

, hence if

, clearly
a+b\in\Soc(S)
. To conclude the proof, we observe that if
a+b\in\Soc(S)

\displaystyle b+a=-a+(a+b)+a=-a+a+(a+b)=a+b\in\Soc(S).

Therefore, the statement is proved.
∎

The following proposition provides a method to obtain ideals of dual weak braces starting from two given ones.

Proposition 24
.

Let
S
be a dual weak brace and
I,J
ideals of
S
. Then,
I+J
and
I\circ J
are ideals of
S
.

Proof.

Initially, by
2.
in
5
, one can check that
I+J=\{i+j\ |\ i\in I\ \text{and}\ j\in J\}
is closed with respect to
+
. Moreover,
\lambda_{a}(i+j)=\lambda_{a}(i)+\lambda_{a}(j)\in I+J
, for every
a\in S
. Hence, by (
1
), it also follows that
I+J
is closed with respect to
\circ
since, if
a,b\in I+J
,

a\circ b=a+\lambda_{a}\left(b\right)\in I+J
. In addition, it is clear that
E(S)\subseteq I+J
.

Now, let us show that
I+J
is a normal subset of
(S,+)
. If
a=i+j\in I+J
, then

-a=\left(-j-i+j\right)-j\in I+J
.
Moreover, if
a\in I+J
and
a^{0}+b\in I+J
, then

\displaystyle a+b=a+a^{0}+b\in I+J

and if
a+b=i+j\in I+J
, it follows that

\displaystyle b+a=a^{0}+b+a=-a+\left(i+j\right)+a=-a+i+a-a+j+a\in I+J.

Thus,
I+J
is a normal subset of
(S,+)
. Finally, we prove that
I+J
is a normal subset of
(S,\circ)
. Now, observe that
a^{-}=-\lambda_{a^{-}}\left(a\right)\in I+J
and, if
a,a^{0}\circ b\in I+J
, then

\displaystyle a\circ b=a\circ a^{0}\circ b\in I+J.

Furthermore, if
a\circ b=i+j\in I+J
, by (
1
) and
3
, we obtain that

\displaystyle b\circ a=a^{-}\circ(i+j)\circ a=a^{-}\circ i\circ\lambda_{i^{-}}\left(j\right)\circ a=\left(a^{-}\circ i\circ a\right)\circ\left(a^{-}\circ\lambda_{i^{-}}\left(j\right)\circ a\right)\in I+J,

therefore
I+J
is an ideal of

.

To prove the second part of the statement, note that
I\circ J=\{i\circ j\ |\ i\in I\ \text{and}\ j\in J\}
is closed with respect to
\circ
.
Besides, if
a,b\in I\circ J
, it follows that

, hence
I\circ J
is closed with respect to
+
.
Moreover, by (
1
), we have that
\lambda_{a}(i\circ j)=\lambda_{a}(i)+\lambda_{a\circ i}(j)\in I\circ J
, for every
a\in S
.
In addition, it is clear that
E(S)\subseteq I+J
.

Now, let us show that

is a normal subset of
(S,+)
. If
a=i\circ j\in I\circ J
, then

-a=\lambda_{i}\left(-j\right)-i=-i+\left(i+\lambda_{i}\left(-j\right)-i\right)\in I\circ J
.
Moreover, if
a\in I\circ J
and
a^{0}+b\in I\circ J

a+b=a+a^{0}+b\in I\circ J

\displaystyle b+a

\displaystyle=a^{0}+b+a=-a+i\circ j+a=-a+i+\lambda_{i}\left(j\right)+a

\displaystyle=\left(-a+i+a\right)+\left(-a+\lambda_{i}\left(j\right)+a\right)\in I\circ J.

is a normal subset of
(S,+)

a^{-}=j^{-}\circ i^{-}=i^{-}\circ\left(i\circ j^{-}\circ i^{-}\right)\in I\circ J

\displaystyle b\circ a=a^{-}\circ(i\circ j)\circ a=\left(a^{-}\circ i\circ a\right)\circ\left(a^{-}\circ j\circ a\right)\in I\circ J,

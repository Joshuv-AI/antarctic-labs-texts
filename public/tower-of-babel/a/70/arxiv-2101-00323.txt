TenIPS: Inverse Propensity Sampling for Tensor Completion
Chengrun Yang, Lijun Ding, Ziyang Wu, Madeleine Udell

Abstract
Tensors are widely used to represent multiway arrays of data. The recovery of missing entries in a tensor has been extensively studied, generally under the assumption that entries are missing completely at random (MCAR). However, in most practical settings, observations are missing not at random (MNAR): the probability that a given entry is observed (also called the propensity) may depend on other entries in the tensor or even on the value of the missing entry. In this paper, we study the problem of completing a partially observed tensor with MNAR observations, without prior information about the propensities. To complete the tensor, we assume that both the original tensor and the tensor of propensities have low multilinear rank. The algorithm first estimates the propensities using a convex relaxation and then predicts missing values using a higher-order SVD approach, reweighting the observed tensor by the inverse propensities. We provide finite-sample error bounds on the resulting complete tensor. Numerical experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach.

Introduction

Tensor completion is gaining increasing popularity and is one of the major tensor-related research topics.
The literature we survey here is by no means exhaustive.
A straightforward method is to flatten a tensor along one of its dimensions to a matrix and then pick one of the extensively studied matrix completion algorithms
[
CT10
,
NW12
,
CZ13
]
.
However, this method neglects the multiway structure along all other dimensions and does not make full use of the combinatorial relationships.
Instead, it is common to assume that the tensor is low rank along each mode.
Tensors differ from matrices in having many incompatible notions of
rank and low rank decompositions,
including CANDECOMP/PARAFAC (CP)
[
CC70
,
H+70
]
,
Tucker
[
Tuc66
]

and tensor-train
[
Ose11
]
.
Each of the decompositions exploits a different definition of tensor rank,
and can be used to recover tensors that are low rank in that sense, including
CP
[
KS13
,
JO14
,
BM16
,
AW17
,
GPY18
,
LM20
]
, Tucker
[
GRY11
,
MHWG14
,
XYZ17
,
YEG+18
,
Z+19
,
HN20
]
and tensor-train
[
WAA16
,
YZGC18
]
.
In this paper, we assume the tensor has approximately low multilinear rank, corresponding to a tensor that can be approximated by a low rank Tucker decomposition.

Existing techniques used for tensor completion include subspace projection onto unfoldings
[
KS13
]
, alternating minimization
[
JO14
,
LM20
,
WAA16
]
, gradient descent
[
YZGC18
]
and expectation-maximization
[
YEG+18
]
;
different surrogates for the rank penalty have been used, including convex surrogates like nuclear norm on unfoldings
[
THK10
,
GRY11
,
ATT18
]
or specific flattenings
[
MHWG14
]
and the maximum norm on factors
[
GPY18
]
, and nonconvex surrogates such as the minimum number of rank-1 sign tensor components
[
GPY18
]
.
We use a higher-order SVD (HOSVD) approach that does not require rank surrogates.
The two methods closest to ours are
HOSVD_w

[
HN20
]
, which computes a weighted HOSVD by reweighting the tensor twice (before and after the HOSVD) by the inverse square root propensity, and the method in
[
XYZ17
]
, which computes a HOSVD on a second-order estimator of a missing completely at random tensor, and we call it
SO-HOSVD
.
We compare our method with
HOSVD_w
and
SO-HOSVD
theoretically in Section
5.2
and numerically in Section
6.1
.

Most previous works on tensor completion have used the assumption that entries are missing completely at random (MCAR).
The missing not at random (MNAR) setting is less studied,
especially for tensors.
A missingness pattern is MNAR when the observation probabilities (also called propensities) of different entries are not equal and may depend on the entry values themselves.
In the matrix MNAR setting, a popular observation model is 1-bit observations
[
DPVDBW14
]
:
each entry is observed with a probability that comes from applying a differentiable function
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1]
to the corresponding entry in a parameter matrix, which is assumed to be low rank.
Two popular convex surrogates for the rank have been used to
estimate the parameter matrix from an entrywise binary observation pattern using a regularized likelihood approach: nuclear norm
[
DPVDBW14
,
MC19
,
ATT18
]

and max-norm
[
CZ13
,
GPY18
]
.
We show that we can achieve a small propensity estimation error by solving for a parameter tensor with low multilinear rank using a (roughly) square flattening.
This approach outperforms simple slicing or flattening methods.

In this paper, we study the problem of provably completing a MNAR tensor with (approximately) low multilinear rank.
We use a two-step procedure to first estimate the propensities in this tensor and then predict missing values by HOSVD on the inverse propensity reweighted tensor.
We give the error bound on final estimation as Theorem
4
.

Square unfoldings

Extending the notation of
[
MHWG14
]
, with a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times J}
and integers
I^{\prime},J^{\prime}
satisfying
IJ=I^{\prime}J^{\prime}
,
\texttt{reshape}(A,I^{\prime},J^{\prime})
gives an
I^{\prime}\times J^{\prime}
matrix

with entries taken columnwise from
A
.
Given a tensor
\mathscr{{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times I_{2}\times\cdots\times I_{N}}
, we can partition the indices of its
N
modes into two sets,
S
and
S^{C}=[N]\setminus S
,
and permute the order of the
N
modes by permutation
\pi_{S}=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{|S|},S^{C}_{1},\ldots,S^{C}_{N-|S|})
,
so that the modes in set
S

appear first, followed by modes in
S^{C}
:

\mathscr{{X}}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{N}}=\pi_{S}(\mathscr{{X}})_{i_{S_{1}}}\cdots i_{S_{|S|}}i_{S^{C}_{1}}\cdots i_{S^{C}_{N-|S|}}.

Denote the
S
-unfolding
of
\mathscr{{X}}
as

\mathscr{{X}}_{S}:=\texttt{reshape}(\pi_{S}(\mathscr{{X}})^{(1)},\prod_{n\in S}I_{n},\prod_{n\in S^{C}}I_{n}).

Our methods for tensor completion rely on methods for matrix completion that work best for square matrices. To make
\mathscr{{X}}_{S}
as square as possible,
\Bigl|\prod_{n\in S}I_{n}-\prod_{n\in S^{C}}I_{n}\Bigr|
should be as small as possible. Hence we define the
square set
of
\mathscr{{X}}
as

S_{\square}=\underset{S\subset[N]}{\text{arg min}}\Bigl|\prod_{n\in S}I_{n}-\prod_{n\in S^{C}}I_{n}\Bigr|,

the
square unfolding
of
\mathscr{{X}}
as

\mathscr{{X}}_{\square}:=\texttt{reshape}(\pi_{S_{\square}}(\mathscr{{X}})^{(1)},\prod_{n\in S_{\square}}I_{n},\prod_{n\in S^{C}_{\square}}I_{n}),

and the
square norm

\|\mathscr{{X}}\|_{\square}:=\|\mathscr{{X}}_{\square}\|_{\star}
of
\mathscr{{X}}
.

Dimensions of unfoldings

For brevity, we denote
I_{S}:=\prod_{n\in S}I_{n}
,
I_{S^{C}}:=\prod_{n\in S^{C}}I_{n}
,
I_{\square}:=\prod_{n\in S_{\square}}I_{n}
,
I_{\square^{C}}:=\prod_{n\in S_{\square}^{C}}I_{n}
,
I_{(-n)}:=\prod_{m\in[N],m\neq n}I_{m}
.
Thus
I_{[N]}=\prod_{n\in[N]}I_{n}=I_{S}\cdot I_{S^{C}}=I_{\square}\cdot I_{\square^{C}}=I_{n}\cdot I_{(-n)}
.

3
Problem setting

In this paper, we study the following problem:
given a partially observed tensor
\mathscr{{B}}_{\mathrm{obs}}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}}

with MNAR entries, how can we recover its missing values?

Throughout the paper, we denote the true order-
N
tensor we want to complete as
\mathscr{{B}}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}}
.
For each
n\in[N]
, we suppose
I_{n}\leq I_{(-n)}:=\prod_{m\in[N],m\neq n}I_{m}
for cleanliness.
We assume there exists a propensity tensor
\mathscr{{P}}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times I_{2}\times\cdots I_{N}}
, such that
\mathscr{{B}}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{N}}
is observed with probability
\mathscr{{P}}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{N}}
.
We observe the entries without noise: with the observation pattern
\Upomega
,
\mathscr{{B}}_{\mathrm{obs}}=\mathscr{{B}}\odot\Upomega
.

A tensor
\mathscr{{B}}
has
multilinear rank

(r_{1}^{\mathrm{true}},r_{2}^{\mathrm{true}},\ldots,r_{N}^{\mathrm{true}})
if
r_{n}^{\mathrm{true}}
is the rank of
\mathscr{{B}}^{(n)}
.
For any
n\in[N]
,
r_{n}^{\mathrm{true}}\leq I_{n}
.
We can write the
Tucker decomposition
of the tensor
\mathscr{{B}}
as
\mathscr{{B}}=\mathscr{{G}}^{\mathrm{true}}\times_{1}U_{1}^{\mathrm{true}}\times\cdots\times_{N}U_{N}^{\mathrm{true}}
, with
core tensor

\mathscr{{G}}^{\mathrm{true}}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{1}^{\mathrm{true}}\times\cdots\times r_{N}^{\mathrm{true}}}
and column orthonormal
factor matrices

U_{n}^{\mathrm{true}}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{n}\times r_{n}^{\mathrm{true}}}
for
n\in[N]
.

We seek a
fixed-rank approximation
of
\mathscr{{B}}
by a tensor with multilinear rank
(r_{1},r_{2},\cdots,r_{N})
: we want to find a core tensor
\mathscr{{W}}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{1}\times r_{2}\times\cdots\times r_{N}}
and
N
factor matrices
Q_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{n}\times r_{n}}
,
n\in[N]
with orthonormal columns, such that
\mathscr{{B}}\approx\mathscr{{W}}\times_{1}Q_{1}\times_{2}\cdots\times_{N}Q_{N}
. We generally seek a low multilinear rank decomposition with
r_{n}<I_{n}
.

Our algorithm proceeds in two steps.
First, we estimate the propensities by
ConvexPE
(Algorithm

Both of these algorithms use a Bernoulli maximum likelihood estimator for 1-bit matrix completion

]
to estimate the propensities from the mask tensor

ConvexPE

NonconvexPE
does not require the associated tuning parameters, but empirically returns a good solution if the true propensity tensor
\mathscr{{P}}
has this property.
With the estimated propensity tensor
\widehat{\mathscr{{P}}}
, we estimate the data tensor
\mathscr{{B}}
by
TenIPS
(Algorithm

Our propensity estimation uses the observation model of 1-bit matrix completion.
Each entry of
\mathscr{{P}}
comes from applying a differentiable link function
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1]
to the corresponding entry of a parameter tensor
\mathscr{{A}}
, which we are trying to solve.
An instance is the logistic function
\sigma(x)=1/(1+e^{-x})
.
We assume
\mathscr{{A}}
has low multilinear rank.
In
ConvexPE
(Algorithm

We also assume an upper bound on the nuclear norm of

ConvexPE
can be implemented by the proximal-proximal-gradient method (PPG)

In Section

In practice, the square unfolding of a tensor is often a large matrix:

Since each iteration of the PPG subroutine in

Also, it does not make full use of the low multilinear rank property of
\mathscr{{A}}

As a substitute, we propose

It achieves a feasible solution with similar quality as
ConvexPE

\tau

\gamma

This can be attributed to the fact that the objective function

The gradient computation can be found in Appendix

5
Error analysis

To bound the relative estimation error
\|\widehat{\mathscr{{X}}}(\widehat{\mathscr{{P}}})-\mathscr{{B}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}/\|\mathscr{{B}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}
,
we first bound the error in the propensity estimates in
ConvexPE
,
and then consider how this error propagates into the error of our final tensor estimate in
TenIPS
.
Theorem
3
shows the optimality of the square unfolding for propensity estimation; Theorem
4
presents a special case of our bound on the tensor completion error with estimated propensities, with the full version as Appendix
A
, Theorem
5
.
We defer their proofs to Appendix
B
.

5.1
Error in propensity estimates

We first show a corollary of
[
MHWG14
, Lemma 6 (2) and Lemma 7]
that bounds the rank of an unfolding.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose
\mathscr{{X}}
has Tucker decomposition

\mathscr{{X}}=\mathscr{{C}}\times_{1}U_{1}\times_{2}U_{2}\times_{3}\cdots\times_{N}U_{N}
,
where
\mathscr{{C}}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{1}^{\mathrm{true}}\times r_{2}^{\mathrm{true}}\times\cdots\times r_{N}^{\mathrm{true}}}

and
U_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{n}\times r_{n}^{\mathrm{true}}}
for
n\in[N]
.
Given
S\subset[N]
,
\mathscr{{X}}_{S}=\underset{j\in S}{\otimes}U_{j}\cdot\mathscr{{C}}_{S}\cdot\Big(\underset{j\in[N]\backslash S}{\otimes}U_{j}\Big)^{\top}
, and thus
\mathrm{rank}(\mathscr{{X}}_{S})\leq\min\Bigl\{\prod_{n\in S}r_{n}^{\mathrm{true}},\,\prod_{n\in[N]\backslash S}r_{n}^{\mathrm{true}}\Big\}
.

Proof.

[
MHWG14
, Lemma 6 (2)]
states that

\mathscr{{X}}_{[n]}=(U_{n}\otimes U_{n-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes U_{1})\,\mathscr{{C}}_{[n]}\,(U_{N}\otimes U_{N-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes U_{n+1})^{\top}

(1)

for
n\in[N]
.
Thus Lemma 1 holds for
\mathscr{{X}}
by applying
Equation
1
to an entry-reordered tensor

\widetilde{\mathscr{{X}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{n_{1}}\times I_{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times I_{n_{N}}}
,
such that
S=\{n_{j}\}_{j\in[S]}
and
\widetilde{\mathscr{{X}}}_{i_{n_{1}}i_{n_{2}}\cdots i_{n_{N}}}=\mathscr{{X}}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{N}}
.
The upper bound for
\mathrm{rank}(\mathscr{{X}}_{S})
follows.
∎

As a corollary of
[
DPVDBW14
, Lemma 1]
and
[
MC19
, Theorem 2]
, we have Lemma
2
for the Frobenius norm error of the propensity tensor estimate.

Lemma 2
.

Assume that
\mathscr{{P}}=\sigma(\mathscr{{A}})
.
Given a set
S\subset[N]
, together with the following assumptions:

A1.

\mathscr{{A}}_{S}
has bounded nuclear norm: there exists a constant

\theta>0
such that
\|\mathscr{{A}}_{S}\|_{\star}\leq\theta\sqrt{I_{[N]}}
.

A2.

Entries of
\mathscr{{A}}
have bounded absolute value: there exists a constant
\alpha>0
such that
\|\mathscr{{A}}\|_{\infty}\leq\alpha
.

Suppose we run
ConvexPE
(Algorithm
1
) with thresholds satisfying
\tau\geq\theta
and
\gamma\geq\alpha
to obtain an estimate
\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}}
of
\mathscr{{P}}
.
With
L_{\gamma}:=\sup_{x\in[-\gamma,\gamma]}\frac{|\sigma^{\prime}(x)|}{\sigma(x)(1-\sigma(x))}
, there exists a universal constant
C>0
such that if
I_{S}+I_{S^{C}}\geq C
, with probability at least
1-\frac{C}{I_{S}+I_{S^{C}}}
, the estimation error

\frac{1}{I_{[N]}}\|\widehat{\mathscr{{P}}}-\mathscr{{P}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\leq 4eL_{\gamma}\tau\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{I_{S}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{I_{S^{C}}}}\Big).

(2)

In the simplest case, when
N
is an even integer and
I_{1}=\ldots=I_{N}=I
, the right-hand side (RHS) of the estimation error in Equation
2
is in
O(I^{N/4})
.

Theorem
3
then shows that the square unfolding achieves the smallest upper bound for propensity estimation among all possible unfoldings sets
S
.

Theorem 3
.

Instate the same conditions as Lemma
2
,
and further assume that there exists a constant
c>0
such that
r_{n}^{\mathrm{true}}\leq cI_{n}
for every
n\in[N]
.
Then
S=S_{\square}
gives the smallest upper bound (RHS of the result in Lemma
2
) on the propensity estimation error
\|\widehat{\mathscr{{P}}}-\mathscr{{P}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}
among all unfolding sets
S\subset[N]
.

Proof.

Denote
r_{S}^{\mathrm{true}}:=\prod_{n\in S}r_{n}^{\mathrm{true}}
and
r_{S^{C}}^{\mathrm{true}}:=\prod_{n\in[N]\backslash S}r_{n}^{\mathrm{true}}
.
We know from Lemma
1
of the main paper that for every unfolding of
\mathscr{{A}}
,
\mathrm{rank}({\mathscr{{A}}_{S}})\leq\min\Bigl\{r_{S}^{\mathrm{true}},\,r_{S^{C}}^{\mathrm{true}}\Big\}
.
Since
\|\mathscr{{A}}_{S}\|_{\star}\leq\sqrt{\mathrm{rank}({\mathscr{{A}}_{S}})}\cdot\|\mathscr{{A}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\alpha\sqrt{\mathrm{rank}({\mathscr{{A}}_{S}})}\cdot I^{\frac{N}{2}}
, we need
\tau\geq\theta\geq\alpha\sqrt{\mathrm{rank}({\mathscr{{A}}_{S}})}
for the conditions of Lemma
2
to hold.
For simplicity, suppose
\tau=\alpha\sqrt{\mathrm{rank}({\mathscr{{A}}_{S}})}
, the smallest possible value for the exact recovery of
\mathscr{{A}}
.

Without loss of generality, suppose
|S|\leq\frac{N}{2}
.
We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{I_{[N]}}\|\widehat{\mathscr{{P}}}-\mathscr{{P}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4eL_{\gamma}\tau\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{I_{S}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{I_{S^{C}}}}\Big)

\displaystyle=4eL_{\gamma}\alpha\cdot\sqrt{\mathrm{rank}({\mathscr{{A}}_{S}})}\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{I_{S}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{I_{S^{C}}}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 4eL_{\gamma}\alpha\Big(\sqrt{\frac{r_{S}^{\mathrm{true}}}{I_{S}}}+\sqrt{\frac{r_{S^{C}}^{\mathrm{true}}}{I_{S^{C}}}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 4eL_{\gamma}\alpha\Big(\sqrt{c^{|S|}}+\sqrt{c^{N-|S|}}\Big).

The final expression is the smallest when
S=S_{\square}
.
∎

5.2
Error in tensor completion: special case

We present a special case of our bound on the recovery error for a cubical tensor with equal multilinear rank as Theorem
4
.
This bound is dominated by the error from the matrix Bernstein inequality
[
T+15
]
on each of the
N
unfoldings, and asymptotically goes to 0 when the tensor size
I\rightarrow\infty
.
Note that our full theorem applies to any tensor; we defer the formal statement to Appendix
A
, Theorem
5
.

Theorem 4
.

Consider an order-
N
cubical tensor
\mathscr{{B}}
with size
I_{1}=\cdots=I_{N}=I
and multilinear rank
r_{1}^{\mathrm{true}}=\cdots=r_{N}^{\mathrm{true}}=r<I
, and two order-
N
cubical tensors
\mathscr{{P}}
and
\mathscr{{A}}
with the same shape as
\mathscr{{B}}
.
Each entry of
\mathscr{{B}}
is observed with probability from the corresponding entry of
\mathscr{{P}}
.
Assume
I\geq rN\log I
, and there exist constants
\psi,\alpha\in(0,\infty)
such that
\|\mathscr{{A}}\|_{\infty}\leq\alpha
,
\|\mathscr{{B}}\|_{\infty}=\psi
.
Further assume that for each
n\in[N]
, the condition number
\frac{\sigma_{1}(\mathscr{{B}}^{(n)})}{\sigma_{r}(\mathscr{{B}}^{(n)})}\leq\kappa
is a constant independent of tensor sizes and dimensions. Then under the conditions of Lemma
2
, with probability at least
1-I^{-1}
, the fixed multilinear rank
(r,r,\ldots,r)
approximation
\widehat{\mathscr{{X}}}(\widehat{\mathscr{{P}}})
computed from
ConvexPE
and
TenIPS
(Algorithms
1
and
3
) with thresholds
\tau\geq\theta
and
\gamma\geq\alpha
satisfies

\frac{\|\widehat{\mathscr{{X}}}(\widehat{\mathscr{{P}}})-\mathscr{{B}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}}{\|\mathscr{{B}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}}\leq CN\sqrt{\frac{r\log I}{I}},

(3)

in which
C
depends on
\kappa
.

Note how this bound compares with the bounds for similar algorithms.

HOSVD_w
has a relative error of
O(rN^{2}I^{-1/2}\log I)

[
HN20
, Theorem 3.3]
for noiseless recovery with known propensities, and
SO-HOSVD
achieves a better bound of
O(\sqrt{\frac{r\log I}{I^{N-1}}})

[
XYZ17
, Theorem 3]
but assumes that the tensor is MCAR.
In contrast, our bound holds for the tensor MNAR setting and does not require known propensities.
It is the first bound in this setting, to our knowledge.

We show a sketch of the proof for Theorem
4
to illustrate the main idea.
This is the special case of the full proof in Appendix
B
.

Proof.

(sketch)

The propensity estimation error in Lemma
2
, Equation
2
propagates to the error between
\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}(\widehat{\mathscr{{P}}})
and
\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}(\mathscr{{P}})
:

\displaystyle\|\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}(\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}})-\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}

(4)

\displaystyle=\sum_{({i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{N}})\in\Omega}\mathscr{{B}}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{N}}^{2}(\frac{1}{\mathscr{{P}}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{N}}}-\frac{1}{\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{N}}})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\psi^{2}\sum_{({i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{N}})\in\Omega}\Big(\frac{\mathscr{{P}}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{N}}-\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{N}}}{\mathscr{{P}}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{N}}\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{N}}}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\psi^{2}}{\sigma(-\gamma)^{2}\sigma(-\alpha)^{2}}\sum_{({i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{N}})\in\Omega}\Big(\mathscr{{P}}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{N}}-\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{N}}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4eL_{\gamma}\tau\psi^{2}}{\sigma(-\gamma)^{2}\sigma(-\alpha)^{2}}\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{I_{S}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{I_{S^{C}}}}\Big)I_{[N]}.

The second inequality comes from
\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{N}}\geq\sigma(-\gamma)
and
\mathscr{{P}}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{N}}\geq\sigma(-\alpha)
; the last inequality follows Lemma
2
.

Then on each of the
N
unfoldings, the error of
\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}^{(n)}(\widehat{\mathscr{{P}}})
from
\mathscr{{B}}^{(n)}

\displaystyle\|\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}^{(n)}(\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}})-\mathscr{{B}}^{(n)}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}^{(n)}(\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}})-\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}^{(n)}\|+\|\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}^{(n)}-\mathscr{{B}}^{(n)}\|

(5)

\displaystyle\leq\|\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}^{(n)}(\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}})-\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}^{(n)}\|_{\mathrm{F}}+\|\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}^{(n)}-\mathscr{{B}}^{(n)}\|,

in which the first term can be bounded by Equation
4
, and the second term can be bounded by applying the matrix Bernstein inequality
[
T+15
]
to the sum of
[\frac{\mathbf{1}(\mathscr{{B}}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{N}}\text{ is observed})}{\mathscr{{P}}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{N}}}\mathscr{{B}}_{\text{obs}}-\mathscr{{B}}]\odot\mathscr{{E}}(i_{1},\dots,i_{N})
over all entries.

The tensor
\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}(\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}})-\mathscr{{B}}
is often full-rank; if we directly use
\sqrt{I}\cdot\|\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}^{(n)}(\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}})-\mathscr{{B}}^{(n)}\|
to bound
\|\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}(\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}})-\mathscr{{B}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}
, the final error bound we get would be
O(N\sqrt{\log I})
, which increases with the increase of tensor size
I
.
Instead, we use the information of low multilinear rank to form the estimator
\mathscr{{\widehat{X}}}(\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}})
.
In
TenIPS
(Algorithm
3
),

\displaystyle\mathscr{{\widehat{X}}}(\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}})

\displaystyle=[\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}(\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}})\times_{1}Q_{1}^{\top}\times_{2}\cdots\times_{N}Q_{N}^{\top}]\times_{1}Q_{1}\times_{2}\cdots\times_{N}Q_{N}

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}(\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}})\times_{1}Q_{1}Q_{1}^{\top}\times_{2}\cdots\times_{N}Q_{N}Q_{N}^{\top},

in which each
Q_{n}
is the column space of
\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}^{(n)}(\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}})
.
This projects each unfolding of
\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}(\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}})
onto its truncated column space.
By adding and subtracting
\mathscr{{B}}\times_{1}Q_{1}Q_{1}^{\top}\times_{2}\cdots\times_{N}Q_{N}Q_{N}^{\top}
, the projection of
\mathscr{{B}}
onto the column space of
\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}(\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}})
in each mode, we get the estimation error

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{{\widehat{X}}}(\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}})-\mathscr{{B}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}(\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}})\times_{1}Q_{1}Q_{1}^{\top}\times_{2}\cdots\times_{N}Q_{N}Q_{N}^{\top}-\mathscr{{B}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|(\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}(\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}})-\mathscr{{B}})\times_{1}Q_{1}Q_{1}^{\top}\times_{2}\cdots\times_{N}Q_{N}Q_{N}^{\top}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\mathscr{{B}}\times_{1}Q_{1}Q_{1}^{\top}\times_{2}\cdots\times_{N}Q_{N}Q_{N}^{\top}-\mathscr{{B}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}

\displaystyle+2\langle(\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}(\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}})-\mathscr{{B}})\times_{1}Q_{1}Q_{1}^{\top}\times_{2}\cdots\times_{N}Q_{N}Q_{N}^{\top},\;\mathscr{{B}}\times_{1}Q_{1}Q_{1}^{\top}\times_{2}\cdots\times_{N}Q_{N}Q_{N}^{\top}-\mathscr{{B}}\rangle.

On the RHS, the third term is an inner product of two mutually orthogonal tensors and is thus 0.
The first term is low multilinear rank, and thus can be bounded by the spectral norm of the unfoldings as

\displaystyle\|(\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}(\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}})-\mathscr{{B}})\times_{1}Q_{1}Q_{1}^{\top}\times_{2}\cdots\times_{N}Q_{N}Q_{N}^{\top}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\min_{n\in[N]}\Big\{\|Q_{n}Q_{n}^{\top}(\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}^{(n)}(\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}})-\mathscr{{B}}^{(n)})\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\min_{n\in[N]}\Big\{r\cdot\|\bar{\mathscr{{X}}}^{(n)}(\mathscr{{\widehat{P}}})-\mathscr{{B}}^{(n)}\|^{2}\Big\}.

The second term can be bounded by the sum of squares of residuals
\sum_{n\in[N]}\|\mathscr{{B}}\times_{n}(I-Q_{n}Q_{n}^{\top})\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}
.
Each of the summand here is the perturbation error of
Q_{n}
on the column space of
\mathscr{{B}}^{(n)}
, and thus can be bounded by the Davis-Kahan sin(
\Theta
) Theorem
[
DK70
,
Wed72
,
YWS15
]
.
∎

6
Experiments

All the code is in the GitHub repository at
https://github.com/udellgroup/TenIPS
.
We ran all experiments on a Linux machine with Intel
®
Xeon
®
E7-4850 v4 2.10GHz CPU and 1056GB memory, and used the logistic link function
\sigma(x)=1/(1+e^{-x})
throughout the experiments.

We use both synthetic and semi-synthetic data for evaluation.
We first compare the propensity estimation performance under square and rectangular (along a specific mode) unfoldings, and then compare the tensor recovery error under different approaches.
For both propensity estimation and tensor completion, the relative error is defined as
\|\mathscr{{\widehat{T}}}-\mathscr{{T}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}/\|\mathscr{{T}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}
, in which
\mathscr{{\widehat{T}}}
is the predicted tensor and
\mathscr{{T}}
is the true tensor.

There are four algorithms similar to
TenIPS
for tensor completion in our experiments:
SqUnfold
, which performs SVD to seek the low-rank approximation of the square unfolding of the propensity-reweighted
\mathscr{{B}}_{\mathrm{obs}}
;
RectUnfold
, which applies SVD to the unfolding of the propensity-reweighted
\mathscr{{B}}_{\mathrm{obs}}
along a specific mode;
HOSVD_w

[
HN20
]
and
SO-HOSVD

[
XYZ17
]
.
The popular nuclear-norm-regularized least squares
LstSq
that seeks
\hat{B}=\underset{X}{\arg\min}\sum_{(i,j)\in\Omega}(B_{ij}-X_{ij})^{2}+\lambda\|X\|_{\star}
on an unfolding of
\mathscr{{B}}_{\mathrm{obs}}
takes much longer to finish in our experiments and is thus prohibitive in tensor completion practice, so we omit it from most of our results.

\mathscr{{A}}

[0.12,0.88]

\mathscr{{P}}=\sigma(\mathscr{{A}})
.

Conclusion

This paper develops a provable two-step approach for MNAR tensor completion with unknown propensities.
The square unfolding allows us to recover propensities with a smaller upper bound, and we then use HOSVD complete MNAR tensor with the estimated propensities.
This method enjoys theoretical guarantee and fast running time in practice.

This paper is the first provable method for completing a general MNAR tensor.
There are many avenues for improvement and extensions.
For example, one could explore whether nonconvex matrix completion methods can be generalized to MNAR tensors, explore other observation models, and design provable algorithms that estimate the propensities even faster.

Lemma 10

(Chain rule of scalar-to-matrix derivatives)

Let

Proof.

We show our proof in a similar fashion as

In Case 1,

&0,\quad&&\mathrm{if}\;k\neq i\\

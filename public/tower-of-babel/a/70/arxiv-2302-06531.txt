Generalized Weak Galerkin Finite Element Methods for Biharmonic Equations
Dan Li, Chunmei Wang, Junping Wang

Abstract
The generalized weak Galerkin (gWG) finite element method is proposed and analyzed for the biharmonic equation. A new generalized discrete weak second order partial derivative is introduced in the gWG scheme to allow arbitrary combinations of piecewise polynomial functions defined in the interior and on the boundary of general polygonal or polyhedral elements. The error estimates are established for the numerical approximation in a discrete H 2 H^{2} norm and a L 2 L^{2} norm. The numerical results are reported to demonstrate the accuracy and flexibility of our proposed gWG method for the biharmonic equation.



keywords
gWG, weak Galerkin, finite element methods, generalized discrete weak second order partial derivative, biharmonic equation, polytopal partitions.

Introduction

This paper is concerned with the new development of the generalized weak Galerkin finite element method for the biharmonic equation. For simplicity, we consider the biharmonic equation that seeks an unknown function
u
satisfying

(1)

\begin{split}\Delta^{2}u&=f,\quad\mbox{in}~~\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u&=g_{1},\quad\mbox{on}~~\partial\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textbf{n}}&=g_{2},\quad\mbox{on}~~\partial\Omega,\end{split}

where
d=2,3
,
\Omega
is an open bounded domain with Lipschitz continuous boundary
\partial\Omega
, the functions
f
,
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are given on
\Omega
or
\partial\Omega
as appropriate, and
n
is an unit outward normal direction to
\partial\Omega.

A weak formulation for (
1
) seeks
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)
satisfying
u|_{\partial\Omega}=g_{1}
and
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textbf{n}}|_{\partial\Omega}=g_{2}
such that

(2)

\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(\partial_{ij}^{2}u,\partial_{ij}^{2}v)=(f,v),~~~~\forall v\in H_{0}^{2}(\Omega),

where
H_{0}^{2}(\Omega)=\{v\in H^{2}(\Omega):v|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\nabla v|_{\partial\Omega}=\textbf{0}\}
.

The biharmonic equation has extensive applications in fluid dynamics. The conforming finite element methods have been developed for the biharmonic equation by constructing finite element spaces as subspaces of
H^{2}(\Omega)
. The
H^{2}
-conforming methods essentially require
C^{1}
-continuity for the
underlying piecewise polynomials (known as finite element functions) on a prescribed finite element partition. The
C^{1}
-continuity imposes an enormous difficulty in the construction of the corresponding finite element functions in practical
computation. Due to the complexity in the construction of
C^{1}
-continuous elements,
H^{2}
-conforming finite element methods are rarely used in practice to solve the biharmonic equation.
As an alternative approach, the nonconforming and discontinuous Galerkin (DG) finite element methods have been employed to solve the biharmonic equation, such as the Morley element
[
14
,
17
,
34
]
, the
C^{0}
interior penalty method
[
2
]
, the hp-version interior-penalty DG method
[
18
]
, and the hybridizable DG method
[
3
]
. Recently, the weak Galerkin (WG) methods
[
14
,
21
,
19
,
26
,
27
,
39
]
, the virtual element methods
[
1
]
and the hybrid high-order methods
[
10
]
have been developed to solve the biharmonic equation.

Weak Galerkin finite element method is a newly-developed numerical technique for PDEs. In the WG method, the differential operators in the variational formulation are approximated by a framework which mimics the theory of distributions for piecewise polynomials. The usual regularity of the approximating functions is compensated by carefully-designed stabilizers. WG methods have been investigated for solving numerous model PDEs
[
12
,
15
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
32
,
33
,
35
,
37
]
and have shown its great potential as a powerful numerical technique in scientific computing. The fundamental difference between the WG methods and other existing finite element methods is the use of weak derivatives and weak continuities in the design of numerical schemes based on conventional weak forms for the underlying PDE problems. Due to its great structural flexibility, WG methods are well suited to a wide class of PDEs by providing the needed stability and accuracy in approximations. A recent development of WG, named “Primal-Dual Weak Galerkin (PDWG)” has been proposed for problems for which the usual numerical methods are difficult to apply
[
5
,
8
,
4
,
7
,
16
,
11
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
38
]
. The essential idea of PDWG is to interpret the numerical solutions as a constrained minimization of some functionals with constraints that mimic the weak formulation of the PDEs by using weak derivatives. The resulting Euler-Lagrange equation offers a symmetric scheme involving both the primal variable and the dual variable (Lagrange multiplier). The PDWG methods have also been extended to a more general
L^{p}
theory
[
5
,
9
,
6
,
13
]
.

In this paper, we propose a novel generalized weak Galerkin method (gWG) to solve the biharmonic equation. The
novelty
of the gWG method is to propose: 1) arbitrary combinations of piecewise polynomials on the general polytopal partitions; and 2) the generalized discrete weak second order partial derivative. Therefore, the gWG method provides a high flexibility in solving a wide range of PDEs.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

-norm and a usual

-norm. Finally, a series of numerical results are provided to demonstrate the accuracy and efficiency of the proposed gWG method for the biharmonic equation (
1
) in Section
8
.

2
Generalized discrete weak second order partial derivative

The goal of this section is to introduce the definition of the generalized discrete weak second order partial derivative. To this end, let
{\cal T}_{h}
be a polygonal or polyhedral partition of the domain
\Omega
that satisfies the shape regular assumption specified in
[
36
]
. Denote by
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
the set of all edges or flat faces in
{\cal T}_{h}
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{0}={\mathcal{E}}_{h}\setminus\partial\Omega
the set of all interior edges or flat faces of
{\cal T}_{h}
. For each polytopal element
T\in{\cal T}_{h}
, denote by
h_{T}
the diameter of
T
and
h=\max_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}h_{T}
the meshsize of
{\cal T}_{h}
.
Denote by
P_{r}(T)
the set of polynomials defined on
T
with total degree no more than
r
.

Let
T\in{\cal T}_{h}
be an element with boundary
\partial T
.
We introduce a weak function
v=\{v_{0},v_{b},\boldsymbol{v_{g}}\}
such that
v_{0}\in L^{2}(T)
,
v_{b}\in L^{2}(\partial T)
and
\boldsymbol{v_{g}}\in[L^{2}(\partial T)]^{d}
(
d=2,3
). The first and second components
v_{0}
and
v_{b}
represent the values of the weak function
v
in the interior and on the boundary of
T
, respectively. The third component
\boldsymbol{v_{g}}=(v_{g1},\ldots,v_{gd})
represents the gradient of
v
on the boundary of
T
. It should be pointed out that
v_{b}
and
\boldsymbol{v_{g}}
may not necessarily be related to the traces of
v_{0}
and
\nabla v_{0}
on
\partial T
, respectively.
Denote by
V_{k,m,\ell}(T)
the local weak finite element space on
T
; i.e.,

V_{k,m,\ell}(T)=\{v=\{v_{0},v_{b},\boldsymbol{v_{g}}\}:v_{0}\in P_{k}(T),v_{b}\in P_{m}(e),\boldsymbol{v_{g}}\in[P_{\ell}(e)]^{d},~e\subset\partial T\},

where
k\geq 2
,
m\geq 0
and
\ell\geq 0
are any given integers.

For each edge or face
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
, denote by
Q_{b}
and
Q_{g}
the usual
L^{2}
projection operators onto
P_{m}(e)
and
P_{\ell}(e)
respectively. Denote by
\boldsymbol{Q_{g}}=(Q_{g1},\ldots,Q_{gd})
the usual
L^{2}
projection operator onto
[P_{\ell}(e)]^{d}
.

Definition 1
.

(Generalized discrete weak second order partial derivative)
Let
n\geq 0
be any given integer. For
i,j=1,\cdots,d
, a generalized discrete weak second order partial derivative for any weak function
v\in V_{k,m,\ell}(T)
, denoted by
\partial_{ij,g,T}^{2}v
, is given by

(3)

\partial_{ij,g,T}^{2}v=\partial_{ij}^{2}v_{0}+\delta_{g}v,

where
\delta_{g}v
is defined as a linear functional in
P_{n}(T)
such that

(4)

(\delta_{g}v,\varphi)_{T}=\langle(Q_{b}v_{0}-v_{b})n_{i},\partial_{j}\varphi\rangle_{\partial T}-\langle Q_{gi}(\nabla v_{0})-v_{gi},\varphi n_{j}\rangle_{\partial T},

for any
\varphi\in P_{n}(T)
. Here,
\textbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})
is an unit outward normal direction to
\partial T
.

3
Generalized weak Galerkin schemes

This section presents the gWG method for the weak form (
2
) of the biharmonic model equation (
1
).

A global weak finite element space
V_{h}
is obtained by patching the local weak finite element space
V_{k,m,\ell}(T)
over all the elements
T\in{\cal T}_{h}
through a common value
v_{b}
on the interior edges/faces
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{0}
; i.e.,

V_{h}=\{v=\{v_{0},v_{b},\boldsymbol{v_{g}}\}:v|_{T}\in V_{k,m,\ell}(T),~T\in{\cal T}_{h}\}.

Denote by
V_{h}^{0}
a subspace of the global weak finite element space
V_{h}
given by

V_{h}^{0}=\{v:v\in V_{h},v_{b}=0,~\boldsymbol{v_{g}}=\boldsymbol{0},~\text{on}~e\subset\partial T\cap\partial\Omega\}.

For simplicity, we denote by
\partial^{2}_{ij,g}v
the generalized discrete weak second order partial derivative
\partial_{ij,g,T}^{2}v
computed by Definition
1
; i.e.,

(\partial^{2}_{ij,g}v)|_{T}=\partial_{ij,g,T}^{2}(v|_{T}),\qquad v\in V_{h}.

For any
w,v\in V_{h}
, we introduce the following two bilinear forms:

\begin{split}(\partial^{2}_{g}w,\partial^{2}_{g}v)_{{\cal T}_{h}}=&\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(\partial_{ij,g}^{2}w,\partial_{ij,g}^{2}v)_{T},\\
s(w,v)=&\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\rho_{1}h_{T}^{\gamma_{1}}\langle Q_{b}w_{0}-w_{b},Q_{b}v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
&+\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\rho_{2}h_{T}^{\gamma_{2}}\langle\boldsymbol{Q_{g}}(\nabla w_{0})-\boldsymbol{w_{g}},\boldsymbol{Q_{g}}(\nabla v_{0})-\boldsymbol{v_{g}}\rangle_{\partial T},\end{split}

where
\rho_{i}>0
for
i=1,2
, and
\gamma_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
for
i=1,2
.

The gWG scheme for the biharmonic equation (
1
) based on the variational formulation (
2
) is given as follows:

Generalized weak Galerkin Scheme 1
.

Find
u_{h}=\{u_{0},u_{b},\boldsymbol{u_{g}}\}\in V_{h}
such that
u_{b}=Q_{b}g_{1}
,
\boldsymbol{u_{g}}\cdot\textbf{n}=Q_{g}g_{2}
and
\boldsymbol{u_{g}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}=Q_{g}(\nabla g_{1}\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau})
on
\partial\Omega
satisfying

(5)

(\partial^{2}_{g}u_{h},\partial^{2}_{g}v)_{{\cal T}_{h}}+s(u_{h},v)=(f,v_{0}),\qquad\forall v\in V_{h}^{0},

where
\boldsymbol{\tau}
is an unit tangential vector to the edge or face
e\subset\partial\Omega.

For any
v\in V_{h}
, let us introduce a seminorm given by

(6)

{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}^{2}=(\partial^{2}_{g}v,\partial^{2}_{g}v)_{{\cal T}_{h}}+s(v,v).

Lemma 2
.

For any
v\in V_{h}
, the seminorm
{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}
defined by (
6
) is a norm in the linear space
V_{h}^{0}
.

Proof.

It suffices to verify the positivity property for
{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\cdot{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}
. To this end, for any
v\in V_{h}^{0}
, it follows from
{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}=0
that
\partial_{ij,g}^{2}v=0
on each element
T\in{\cal T}_{h}
for
i,j=1,\ldots,d
and
s(v,v)=0
. This leads to
Q_{b}v_{0}=v_{b}
and
\boldsymbol{Q_{g}}(\nabla v_{0})=\boldsymbol{v_{g}}
on each
\partial T
, which, together with
\partial^{2}_{ij,g}v=0
on each
T
and (
3
)-(
4
), gives
\delta_{g}v=0
and further
\partial^{2}_{ij}v_{0}=0
for
i,j=1,\ldots,d
. Therefore, we obtain
\nabla v_{0}=const
on each
T
. This, from the fact
\boldsymbol{Q_{g}}(\nabla v_{0})=\boldsymbol{v_{g}}
on each
\partial T
, gives
\nabla v_{0}\in C^{0}(\Omega)
. Further, using the boundary condition
\boldsymbol{v_{g}}=\boldsymbol{0}
on
\partial\Omega
, we obtain
\nabla v_{0}=0
in
\Omega
and
\boldsymbol{v_{g}}=0
on each
\partial T
. Therefore,
v_{0}=const
on each
T
. Since
Q_{b}v_{0}=v_{b}
on each
\partial T
, we have
v_{0}=v_{b}
on each
\partial T
and further
v_{0}\in C^{0}(\Omega)
. This, together with the boundary condition
v_{b}=0
on
\partial\Omega
, gives
v_{0}=0
on each
T
and
v_{b}=0
on each
\partial T
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3
.

The generalized weak Galerkin scheme (
5
) has one and only one solution.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that the gWG scheme (
5
) has a unique solution. To this end, assume that
u_{h}^{(1)}
and
u_{h}^{(2)}
are two different solutions arising from the gWG scheme (
5
). Letting
v=u_{h}^{(1)}-u_{h}^{(2)}\in V_{h}^{0}
in (
5
), there holds

(\partial^{2}_{g}(u_{h}^{(1)}-u_{h}^{(2)}),\partial^{2}_{g}(u_{h}^{(1)}-u_{h}^{(2)}))_{{\cal T}_{h}}+s(u_{h}^{(1)}-u_{h}^{(2)},u_{h}^{(1)}-u_{h}^{(2)})=0,

which, together with Lemma
2
, leads to
u_{h}^{(1)}=u_{h}^{(2)}
. This completes the proof.
∎

4
Error equations

The objective of this section is to derive an error equation for the gWG scheme (
5
). To this end, on each element
T\in{\cal T}_{h}
, denote by
Q_{0}
the usual
L^{2}
projection operator from
L^{2}(T)
onto
P_{k}(T)
. We further define a projection operator
Q_{h}
in the sense that

Q_{h}\phi=\{Q_{0}\phi,Q_{b}\phi,\boldsymbol{Q_{g}}(\nabla\phi)\},\qquad\forall\phi\in H^{2}(\Omega).

Denote by
\mathbb{Q}_{s}
the usual
L^{2}
projection operator onto
P_{s}(T)
for
s=\min\{k,m,\ell,n\}
.

Lemma 4
.

For any
\phi\in H^{2}(T)
and
\varphi\in P_{s}(T)
, there holds

(\partial_{ij,g}^{2}Q_{h}\phi,\varphi)_{T}=(Q_{0}\phi-\phi,\partial^{2}_{ji}\varphi)_{T}+(\partial^{2}_{ij}\phi,\varphi)_{T}.

Proof.

Using (
3
)-(
4
), the property of
L^{2}
projection operators and the usual integration by parts, we have

\begin{split}&(\partial_{ij,g}^{2}Q_{h}\phi,\varphi)_{T}\\
=&(\partial^{2}_{ij}Q_{0}\phi+\delta_{g}Q_{h}\phi,\varphi)_{T}\\
=&(\partial^{2}_{ij}Q_{0}\phi,\varphi)_{T}+\langle(Q_{b}(Q_{0}\phi)-Q_{b}\phi)n_{i},\partial_{j}\varphi\rangle_{\partial T}-\langle Q_{gi}(\nabla Q_{0}\phi)-Q_{gi}(\nabla\phi),\varphi n_{j}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&(\partial^{2}_{ij}Q_{0}\phi,\varphi)_{T}+\langle(Q_{0}\phi-\phi)n_{i},\partial_{j}\varphi\rangle_{\partial T}-\langle\partial_{i}Q_{0}\phi-\partial_{i}\phi,\varphi n_{j}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&(\partial^{2}_{ij}Q_{0}\phi,\varphi)_{T}+(Q_{0}\phi-\phi,\partial^{2}_{ji}\varphi)_{T}-(\partial^{2}_{ij}(Q_{0}\phi-\phi),\varphi)_{T}\\
=&(Q_{0}\phi-\phi,\partial^{2}_{ji}\varphi)_{T}+(\partial^{2}_{ij}\phi,\varphi)_{T}.\end{split}

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5
.

Let
u
and
u_{h}\in V_{h}
be the exact solution of the model equation (
1
) and the numerical solution arising from
gWG
scheme (
5
), respectively. Let
e_{h}=Q_{h}u-u_{h}
be the error function. Then, the error function
e_{h}
satisfies the following error equation

(7)

\displaystyle(\partial^{2}_{g}e_{h},\partial^{2}_{g}v)_{{\cal T}_{h}}+s(e_{h},v)=\zeta_{u}(v),~~~~~\forall v\in V_{h}^{0},

where the term
\zeta_{u}(v)
is given by

(8)

\begin{split}&\zeta_{u}(v)\\
=&s(Q_{h}u,v)+\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(Q_{0}u-u,\partial^{2}_{ji}(\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij,g}v))_{T}+(\partial^{2}_{ij}v_{0},(\mathbb{Q}_{s}-I)\partial^{2}_{ij}u)_{T}\\
&+\langle(v_{0}-v_{b})n_{i},\partial_{j}(\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij}u-\partial^{2}_{ij}u)\rangle_{\partial T}+\langle\partial_{i}v_{0}-v_{gi},(I-\mathbb{Q}_{s})\partial^{2}_{ij}u\cdot n_{j}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
&+(\partial^{2}_{ij,g}Q_{h}u,(I-\mathbb{Q}_{s})\partial^{2}_{ij,g}v)_{T}.\end{split}

Proof.

Testing the model equation (
1
) against
v_{0}
gives

(9)

\begin{split}(f,v_{0})=&\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}(\Delta^{2}u,v_{0})_{T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(\partial^{2}_{ij}u,\partial^{2}_{ij}v_{0})_{T}-\langle\partial^{2}_{ij}u,\partial_{i}v_{0}\cdot n_{j}\rangle_{\partial T}+\langle\partial_{j}(\partial^{2}_{ij}u)\cdot n_{i},v_{0}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}((I-\mathbb{Q}_{s})\partial^{2}_{ij}u,\partial^{2}_{ij}v_{0})_{T}+(\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij}u,\partial^{2}_{ij}v_{0})_{T}\\
&-\langle\partial^{2}_{ij}u,(\partial_{i}v_{0}-v_{gi})\cdot n_{j}\rangle_{\partial T}+\langle\partial_{j}(\partial^{2}_{ij}u)\cdot n_{i},v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T},\end{split}

where we also used

\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\langle\partial^{2}_{ij}u,v_{gi}\cdot n_{j}\rangle_{\partial T}=0,

\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\langle\partial_{j}(\partial^{2}_{ij}u)\cdot n_{i},v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}=0,

since
v_{gi}
and
v_{b}
are single valued on each
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{0}
, as well as
\boldsymbol{v_{g}}=0
and
v_{b}=0
on
e\subset\partial\Omega.

From (
3
)-(
4
),
s=\min\{k,m,\ell,n\}
, the properties of the
L^{2}
projection operators, Lemma
4
with
\phi=u
and
\varphi=\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial_{ij,g}^{2}v\in P_{s}(T)
, there holds

(10)

\begin{split}&\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij}u,\partial^{2}_{ij}v_{0})_{T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij}u,\partial^{2}_{ij,g}v)_{T}-(\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij}u,\delta_{g}v)_{T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij}u,\partial^{2}_{ij,g}v)_{T}-\langle(Q_{b}v_{0}-v_{b})n_{i},\partial_{j}(\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij}u)\rangle_{\partial T}\\
&+\langle Q_{gi}(\nabla v_{0})-v_{gi},\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij}u\cdot n_{j}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(\partial^{2}_{ij}u,\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij,g}v)_{T}-\langle(v_{0}-v_{b})n_{i},\partial_{j}(\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij}u)\rangle_{\partial T}\\
&+\langle\partial_{i}v_{0}-v_{gi},\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij}u\cdot n_{j}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(\partial^{2}_{ij,g}Q_{h}u,\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij,g}v)_{T}-(Q_{0}u-u,\partial^{2}_{ji}(\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial_{ij,g}^{2}v))_{T}\\
&-\langle(v_{0}-v_{b})n_{i},\partial_{j}(\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij}u)\rangle_{\partial T}+\langle\partial_{i}v_{0}-v_{gi},\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij}u\cdot n_{j}\rangle_{\partial T}.\end{split}

Substituting (
10
) into (
9
) gives

(11)

\begin{split}(f,v_{0})=&\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}((I-\mathbb{Q}_{s})\partial^{2}_{ij}u,\partial^{2}_{ij}v_{0})_{T}+(\partial^{2}_{ij,g}Q_{h}u,(\mathbb{Q}_{s}-I)\partial^{2}_{ij,g}v)_{T}\\
&-(Q_{0}u-u,\partial^{2}_{ji}(\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial_{ij,g}^{2}v))_{T}+\langle(v_{0}-v_{b})n_{i},\partial_{j}(\partial^{2}_{ij}u-\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij}u)\rangle_{\partial T}\\
&+\langle\partial_{i}v_{0}-v_{gi},(\mathbb{Q}_{s}-I)\partial^{2}_{ij}u\cdot n_{j}\rangle_{\partial T}+(\partial_{g}^{2}Q_{h}u,\partial_{g}^{2}v)_{{\cal T}_{h}}.\end{split}

Subtracting (
11
) by the gWG scheme (
5
) gives rise to the error
equation (
7
). This completes the proof.
∎

5
Technical results

We shall provide some technical results in this section.

Let
{\cal T}_{h}
be a finite element partition that satisfies the shape regular assumption described as in
[
36
]
. The trace inequality holds true; i.e.,

(12)

\|\phi\|_{\partial T}^{2}\lesssim h_{T}^{-1}\|\phi\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}\|\nabla\phi\|_{T}^{2},\qquad\forall\phi\in H^{1}(T).

Moreover, for any polynomial
\phi
, using the inverse inequality, the trace inequality (
12
) can be written as follows

(13)

\|\phi\|_{\partial T}^{2}\lesssim h_{T}^{-1}\|\phi\|_{T}^{2}.

Lemma 6
.

[
36
]

Let
{\cal T}_{h}
be a finite element partition satisfying the shape regular assumption specified in
[
36
]
. Let
\alpha\in[0,k]
and
\nu\in[0,s]
. For
0\leq t\leq 2

C_{3}(h,k,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},t_{0})=(h^{t_{0}-1}(h^{\frac{\gamma_{1}+3}{2}}+h^{\frac{\gamma_{2}+1}{2}})+h(1+h^{\frac{-\gamma_{1}-3}{2}}+h^{\frac{-\gamma_{2}-1}{2}}))C_{1}(h,k,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
and
C_{4}(h,k,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},s,t_{0})=h^{t_{0}-1}(h^{\frac{\gamma_{1}+3}{2}}+h^{\frac{\gamma_{2}+1}{2}}+1+h^{\frac{-\gamma_{1}-3}{2}}+h^{\frac{-\gamma_{2}-1}{2}})C_{2}(h,k,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},s)
.

Proof.

Testing the dual problem (
27
) against
e_{0}
and using the usual integration by parts, we have

(29)

\begin{split}\|e_{0}\|^{2}=&(\Delta^{2}\Psi,e_{0})\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(\partial^{2}_{ij}\Psi,\partial^{2}_{ij}e_{0})_{T}+\langle\partial_{j}(\partial^{2}_{ij}\Psi)\cdot n_{i},e_{0}\rangle_{\partial T}-\langle\partial^{2}_{ij}\Psi,\partial_{i}e_{0}\cdot n_{j}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}((I-\mathbb{Q}_{s})\partial^{2}_{ij}\Psi,\partial^{2}_{ij}e_{0})_{T}+(\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij}\Psi,\partial^{2}_{ij}e_{0})_{T}\\
&+\langle\partial_{j}(\partial^{2}_{ij}\Psi)\cdot n_{i},e_{0}-e_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}-\langle\partial^{2}_{ij}\Psi,(\partial_{i}e_{0}-e_{gi})\cdot n_{j}\rangle_{\partial T},\end{split}

where we used
\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\langle\partial_{j}(\partial^{2}_{ij}\Psi)\cdot n_{i},e_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}=0
and
\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\langle\partial^{2}_{ij}\Psi,e_{gi}\cdot n_{j}\rangle_{\partial T}=0
, since
e_{b}=0
and
\boldsymbol{e_{g}}=0
on
\partial\Omega
.

Letting
u=\Psi
and
v=e_{h}
in (
10
) and letting
v=Q_{h}\Psi
in the error equation (
7
) to obtain

\begin{split}&\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij}\Psi,\partial^{2}_{ij}e_{0})_{T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(\partial^{2}_{ij,g}Q_{h}\Psi,\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij,g}e_{h})_{T}-(Q_{0}\Psi-\Psi,\partial^{2}_{ji}(\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij,g}e_{h}))_{T}\\
&-\langle(e_{0}-e_{b})n_{i},\partial_{j}(\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij}\Psi)\rangle_{\partial T}+\langle\partial_{i}e_{0}-e_{gi},\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij}\Psi\cdot n_{j}\rangle_{\partial T}\\
=&\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(\partial^{2}_{ij,g}Q_{h}\Psi,(\mathbb{Q}_{s}-I)\partial^{2}_{ij,g}e_{h})_{T}+\zeta_{u}(Q_{h}\Psi)-s(e_{h},Q_{h}\Psi)-(Q_{0}\Psi-\Psi,\partial^{2}_{ji}(\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij,g}e_{h}))_{T}\\
&-\langle(e_{0}-e_{b})n_{i},\partial_{j}(\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij}\Psi)\rangle_{\partial T}+\langle\partial_{i}e_{0}-e_{gi},\mathbb{Q}_{s}\partial^{2}_{ij}\Psi\cdot n_{j}\rangle_{\partial T}.\end{split}

Substituting the above equation into (
29
) gives rise to

(30)

\|e_{0}\|^{2}=\zeta_{u}(Q_{h}\Psi)-\zeta_{\Psi}(e_{h}).

Next, it suffices to estimate the two terms on the right hand of (
30
). Let us first estimate the first term
\zeta_{u}(Q_{h}\Psi)
. For the case of
s=0
, it follows from (
8
), Lemma
11
, and (
28
) that

(31)

\begin{split}&|\zeta_{u}(Q_{h}\Psi)|\\
\lesssim&((h^{k+t_{0}+\gamma_{1}+1}+h^{k+t_{0}+\gamma_{2}-1}+h^{k+1}+h^{k+t_{0}-2}+h^{k})\|u\|_{k+1}\\
&+h^{t_{0}}\|u\|_{4}+h^{2}\|u\|_{3})\|\Psi\|_{4},\\
\lesssim&((h^{k+t_{0}+\gamma_{1}+1}+h^{k+t_{0}+\gamma_{2}-1}+h^{k})\|u\|_{k+1}+h^{t_{0}}\|u\|_{4}+h^{2}\|u\|_{4})\|\Psi\|_{4},\\
\lesssim&((h^{k+t_{0}+\gamma_{1}+1}+h^{k+t_{0}+\gamma_{2}-1}+h^{k})\|u\|_{k+1}+h^{2}\|u\|_{4})\|\Psi\|_{4},\\
\lesssim&(h^{k+t_{0}+\gamma_{1}+1}+h^{k+t_{0}+\gamma_{2}-1}+h^{2})(\|u\|_{k+1}+\delta_{k,2}\|u\|_{4})\|e_{0}\|.\end{split}

For the case of
s>0
, using (
8
), Lemma
11
, and (
28
) gives

(32)

\begin{split}&|\zeta_{u}(Q_{h}\Psi)|\\
\lesssim&((h^{k+t_{0}+\gamma_{1}+1}+h^{k+t_{0}+\gamma_{2}-1}+h^{k+1}+h^{k+t_{0}-2})\|u\|_{k+1}\\
&+(h^{s+t_{0}}+h^{s+3})\|u\|_{s+3})\|\Psi\|_{4},\\
\lesssim&((h^{k+t_{0}+\gamma_{1}+1}+h^{k+t_{0}+\gamma_{2}-1}+h^{k+t_{0}-2})\|u\|_{k+1}+h^{s+t_{0}}\|u\|_{s+3})\|\Psi\|_{4},\\
\lesssim&(h^{k+t_{0}+\gamma_{1}+1}+h^{k+t_{0}+\gamma_{2}-1}+h^{k+t_{0}-2}+h^{s+t_{0}})\|u\|_{q}\|\Psi\|_{4},\\
\lesssim&(h^{k+t_{0}+\gamma_{1}+1}+h^{k+t_{0}+\gamma_{2}-1}+h^{k+t_{0}-2}+h^{s+t_{0}})\|u\|_{q}\|e_{0}\|.\end{split}

As to the second term
\zeta_{\Psi}(e_{h})
, for the case of
s=0
, using (
8
), Lemma
10
with
\phi=\Psi
,
v=e_{h}
,
\theta=t_{0}
,
\nu=0
, (
28
) and Theorem
12
gives

(33)

\begin{split}|\zeta_{\Psi}(e_{h})|\lesssim&((h^{\frac{2t_{0}+\gamma_{1}+1}{2}}+h^{\frac{2t_{0}+\gamma_{2}-1}{2}}+h^{t_{0}-1})\|\Psi\|_{t_{0}+1}\\
&+(h+h^{\frac{-1-\gamma_{1}}{2}}+h^{\frac{1-\gamma_{2}}{2}})\|\Psi\|_{4}){|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}e_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\\
\lesssim&(h^{\frac{2t_{0}+\gamma_{1}+1}{2}}+h^{\frac{2t_{0}+\gamma_{2}-1}{2}}+h^{t_{0}-1}+h+h^{\frac{-1-\gamma_{1}}{2}}+h^{\frac{1-\gamma_{2}}{2}})\|\Psi\|_{4}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}e_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\\
\lesssim&(h^{\frac{2t_{0}+\gamma_{1}+1}{2}}+h^{\frac{2t_{0}+\gamma_{2}-1}{2}}+h+h^{\frac{-1-\gamma_{1}}{2}}+h^{\frac{1-\gamma_{2}}{2}})\|\Psi\|_{4}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}e_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\\
\lesssim&(h^{t_{0}-1}(h^{\frac{\gamma_{1}+3}{2}}+h^{\frac{\gamma_{2}+1}{2}})+h(1+h^{\frac{-\gamma_{1}-3}{2}}+h^{\frac{-\gamma_{2}-1}{2}}))\|e_{0}\|{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}e_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|},\\
\lesssim&(h^{t_{0}-1}(h^{\frac{\gamma_{1}+3}{2}}+h^{\frac{\gamma_{2}+1}{2}})+h(1+h^{\frac{-\gamma_{1}-3}{2}}+h^{\frac{-\gamma_{2}-1}{2}}))C_{1}(h,k,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\\
&\cdot(\|u\|_{k+1}+\delta_{k,2}\|u\|_{4})\|e_{0}\|\\
\lesssim&C_{3}(h,k,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},t_{0})(\|u\|_{k+1}+\delta_{k,2}\|u\|_{4})\|e_{0}\|.\end{split}

For the case of
s>0
, we apply (
8
), Lemma
10
with
\phi=\Psi
,
v=e_{h}
,
\theta=t_{0}
,
\nu=t_{0}-2
, (
28
) and Theorem
12
to obtain

(34)

\begin{split}&|\zeta_{\Psi}(e_{h})|\\
\lesssim&(h^{\frac{2t_{0}+\gamma_{1}+1}{2}}+h^{\frac{2t_{0}+\gamma_{2}-1}{2}}+h^{t_{0}-1}+h^{\frac{2t_{0}-5-\gamma_{1}}{2}}+h^{\frac{2t_{0}-3-\gamma_{2}}{2}})\|\Psi\|_{t_{0}+1}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}e_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\\
\lesssim&h^{t_{0}-1}(h^{\frac{\gamma_{1}+3}{2}}+h^{\frac{\gamma_{2}+1}{2}}+1+h^{\frac{-\gamma_{1}-3}{2}}+h^{\frac{-\gamma_{2}-1}{2}})\|\Psi\|_{4}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}e_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\\
\lesssim&h^{t_{0}-1}(h^{\frac{\gamma_{1}+3}{2}}+h^{\frac{\gamma_{2}+1}{2}}+1+h^{\frac{-\gamma_{1}-3}{2}}+h^{\frac{-\gamma_{2}-1}{2}})\|e_{0}\|{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}e_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\\
\lesssim&h^{t_{0}-1}(h^{\frac{\gamma_{1}+3}{2}}+h^{\frac{\gamma_{2}+1}{2}}+1+h^{\frac{-\gamma_{1}-3}{2}}+h^{\frac{-\gamma_{2}-1}{2}})C_{2}(h,k,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},s)\|u\|_{q}\|e_{0}\|\\
\lesssim&C_{4}(h,k,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},s,t_{0})\|u\|_{q}\|e_{0}\|.\end{split}

Finally, substituting (
31
)- (

) into (
30
) gives rise to the desired result. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 7.1
.

For the
gWG
element
V_{k,m,\ell}(T)
with
m=k-2
,
\ell=k-2
and
n\leq k-2
,
\|e_{0}\|
has a sub-optimal order of convergence for
k=2
and an optimal order of convergence for
k\geq 3
when
\gamma_{1}=-3
and
\gamma_{2}=-1
.

To establish the error estimates for
e_{b}
and
\boldsymbol{e_{g}}
in the
L^{2}
norm, we introduce

\|e_{b}\|_{{\mathcal{E}}_{h}}=\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}h_{T}\|e_{b}\|_{\partial T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},~~~~~\|\boldsymbol{e_{g}}\|_{{\mathcal{E}}_{h}}=\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}h_{T}\|\boldsymbol{e_{g}}\|_{\partial T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Theorem 14
.

In the assumptions of Theorem
13
, the following error estimates hold true:

\begin{split}&\|e_{b}\|_{{\mathcal{E}}_{h}}\lesssim\|e_{0}\|+h^{\frac{1-\gamma_{1}}{2}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}e_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|},\\
&\|\boldsymbol{e_{g}}\|_{{\mathcal{E}}_{h}}\lesssim h^{-1}\|e_{0}\|+h^{\frac{1-\gamma_{2}}{2}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}e_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|},\end{split}

where the estimates for
{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}e_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}
and
\|e_{0}\|
are given by Theorem
12
and Theorem
13
, respectively.

Proof.

From the triangle inequality and the trace inequality (
13
), one arrives at

\begin{split}\|e_{b}\|_{{\mathcal{E}}_{h}}=&\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}h_{T}\|e_{b}\|_{\partial T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\lesssim&\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}h_{T}\|Q_{b}e_{0}\|_{\partial T}^{2}+h_{T}\|e_{b}-Q_{b}e_{0}\|_{\partial T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\lesssim&\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}h_{T}h_{T}^{-1}\|e_{0}\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}h_{T}^{1-\gamma_{1}}\rho_{1}h_{T}^{\gamma_{1}}\|e_{b}-Q_{b}e_{0}\|_{\partial T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\lesssim&\|e_{0}\|+h^{\frac{1-\gamma_{1}}{2}}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}e_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}.\end{split}

Similar argument can be straightforward applied to establish the error estimate for
\boldsymbol{e_{g}}
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

8
Numerical experiments

In this section, some numerical results are demonstrated to validate the convergence theory established in the previous sections.

For the convenience of notation, the gWG element and the generalized discrete weak second order partial derivative are denoted by
P_{k}(T)|P_{m}(\partial T)|P_{\ell}(\partial T)]^{d}||P_{n}(T)
element. The numerical experiments will be conducted on the convex domain
\Omega_{1}=(0,1)^{2}
and non-convex L-shaped domain
\Omega_{2}
with vertices
A_{1}=(-1,1)
,
A_{2}=(1,1)
,
A_{3}=(1,0)
,
A_{4}=(0,0)
,
A_{5}=(0,-1)
and
A_{6}=(-1,-1)
. The uniform triangular, uniform square, and uniform rectangular partitions are employed. An uniform triangular partition starts with an initial uniform triangulation, and the next level partition is obtained by uniformly partitioning each coarse triangular element into four sub-triangles by connecting the middle points of three edges of each triangular element. To obtain an uniform rectangular partition, we start from an initial
3\times 2
rectangular partition, and the next level partition is obtained by uniformly dividing each rectangular element into four sub-rectangles by connecting the middle points on two parallel edges of each rectangular element. An uniform square partition can be obtained similarly.

The following metrics are employed to measure the errors:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}&\mbox{Discrete $H^{2}$-norm:}&\ \displaystyle|e_{0}|_{2}=\Big(\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_{h}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|\partial_{ij}^{2}u_{0}(M_{c})-\partial_{ij}^{2}u(M_{c})|^{2}\times|T|\Big)^{1/2},\\

where
M_{c}
represents the center point on each element
T
and
|T|
is the area of
T
.

In all tables, “Conv” means the theoretical rate of convergence established in this paper. “N/A” means the theoretical rate of convergence has not been developed in this paper.

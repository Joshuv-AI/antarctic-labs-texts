Algorithms for determination of 𝐓 \mathbf{T} -module structures on some extension groups
Filip Głoch 1, Dawid E. Kędzierski 1, Piotr Krasoń 1,∗

Abstract
. In their recent work the second and the third authors extended the methods of M.A. Papanikolas and N. Ramachandran and determined the t -module structure on Ext 1 ⁡ ( Φ , Ψ ) \operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\Phi,\Psi) where Φ \Phi and Ψ \Psi were Anderson t -modules over A = 𝐅 q ​ [ t ] A={\mathbf{F}}_{q}[t] of some specific types. This approach involved the concept of biderivation and certain reduction algorithm. In this paper we generalize these results and present a complete algorithm for computation of t -module structure on Ext 1 ⁡ ( Φ , Ψ ) \operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\Phi,\Psi) for 𝐭 − \mathbf{t}- modules Φ \Phi and Ψ \Psi such that deg τ ⁡ Φ > deg τ ⁡ Ψ . \operatorname{deg}_{\tau}\Phi>\operatorname{deg}_{\tau}\Psi. The last condition is not sufficient for our algorithm to be executable. We show that it can be applied when the matrix at the biggest power of τ \tau in Φ t \Phi_{t} is invertible. We also introduce a notion of τ {\tau} -composition series which we find suitable for the additive category of t -modules and show that under certain assumptions on the composition series of Φ \Phi and Ψ \Psi our algorithm is also executable. In this version we added a Corrigendum the end of the paper.

Introduction

Let
A={\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]
be a polynomial ring over a finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with
q
-elements
and let
k={\mathbb{F}}_{q}(t)
be the quotient field of
A.

Then
k
can be viewed as the function field of the curve
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and
A
as the ring of functions regular outside
\infty
.
Let
v_{\infty}:k\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{\infty\}
be the normalized
valuation associated to
\frac{1}{t}
(i.e.
v_{\infty}(\frac{1}{t})=1
). Let
K
be a completion of
k
with respect to
v_{\infty}
and let
\overline{K}
be its fixed algebraic closure. It turns out that
{\overline{K}}
is neither complete nor
locally compact. Denote also by
\overline{v_{\infty}}
the extension of
v_{\infty}
to
\overline{K}.

Let
{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}
be the completion of
\overline{K}
with respect to the metric induced by
\overline{v_{\infty}}.

One of the major developments in the theory of curves over finite fields was the work of L. Carlitz
[
3
]
where
a remarkable
exponential
function
e_{C}:{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty}
was defined.
This function is an analytic function on the characteristic
p
-space
{\mathbb{C}}_{\infty},
and besides the properties analogous to the usual exponential function satisfies the functional equation
e_{C}(az)=C_{a}(e_{C}(z))
where

a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]
and
C_{a}
is an additive polynomial. One has
C_{ab}(z)=C_{a}(C_{b}(z))=C_{b}(C_{a}(z))

and
C_{a}(z_{1}+z_{2})=C_{a}(z_{1})+C_{a}(z_{2}).
The correspondence
a\rightarrow C_{a}(z)
is called the Carlitz module.
D. Hayes
[
9
]
proved that the division values of
e_{C}(z)
generate abelian extensions of
k
analogous to the cyclotomic extensions defined by the division points of
e^{2{\pi}iz}.

In
[
4
]
V.G. Drinfeld extended the theory of the Carlitz (rank 1) exponentials to arbitrary rank
d.

Such exponentials
give rise to objects which are now called Drinfeld modules. The theory of moduli spaces of shtukas (which are generalizations of Drinfeld modules) constructed by V.G. Drinfeld played a major role in proving the version of Langlands correspondence for
GL_{r}
of function fields in finite characteristic
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
.
Additionally, D. Hayes
[
10
]
developed explicitly a class field theory for function fields.
Drinfeld modules and its generalizations
\mathbf{t}-
modules defined by G. Anderson
[
1
]
are objects of intensive studies.
For the remarkable results obtained in this area the reader is advised to consult excellent sources e.g.
[
18
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
16
]
.
The category of
\mathbf{t}-
modules is an additive category. Basic structures of this category such as
\operatorname{Hom}_{\tau}
-sets
or extension groups deserve thorough investigation. In
[
12
]
an algorithm for determination of an endomorphism
ring of a Drinfeld module was developed.
In
[
17
]
a
\mathbf{t}-
module structure for
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\phi,C)
, where
\phi
is a Drinfeld module of rank bigger than
1
and
C
is the Carlitz module, was determined.
The key idea relied on representation of the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-space
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}
as the quotient of the space

[
17
]
to construct a recursive algorithm for computing
\mathbf{t}-
module structure on
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\phi,C).

The application of essentially the same algorithm allowed them to determine the structure of a
\mathbf{t}-
module on the space

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(C^{\otimes m},C^{\otimes n})
for
n>m
.
In
[
11
]
the second and third author generalized this algorithm and showed that for some specific
\mathbf{t}-
modules
\Phi
and
\Psi
under the assumption

\mathrm{deg}_{\tau}\Phi>\mathrm{deg}_{\tau}\Psi
the spaces
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\Phi,\Psi)
have
\mathbf{t}-
module structures. The content of this paper is the following: In section

we give basic definitions and facts concerning
\mathbf{t}-
modules, their morphisms etc. Then we describe
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}
as the quotient of the space of biderivations modulo the subspace of inner biderivations. We quickly recall the six-term
\operatorname{Hom}_{\tau}-\operatorname{Ext}_{\tau}
exact sequence existence of which was proved in
[
11
]
. We also describe the duality functor as defined in
[

]
(cf.
[
11
]
).

In section

\mathbf{t}-
reduction algorithm, for computation of a
\mathbf{t}-
module structure for
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\Phi,\Psi),
where

\Phi,\Psi
are
\mathbf{t}-
modules,
\mathrm{deg}_{\tau}\Phi>\mathrm{deg}_{\tau}\Psi
. The
\mathbf{t}-
reduction algorithm is a generalization of the methods of
computation presented both in
[
17
]
and
[
11
]
.
The condition
\mathrm{deg}_{\tau}\Phi>\mathrm{deg}_{\tau}\Psi
is not sufficient for the

reduction algorithm to be executable therefore we introduce the notion of a
\mathbf{t}-
module structure coming from
\mathbf{t}-

In section

we show that if
\mathrm{deg}_{\tau}\Phi>\mathrm{deg}_{\tau}\Psi
and the matrix
A_{n}
at the highest power of
\tau

{\Phi}_{t}
is invertible then

we introduce for
\mathbf{t}-
modules some new notions: we define
\tau
-simplicity and
\tau
-composition series. Then we show that our algorithm is executable for

This essentially enlarges the class of
\mathbf{t}-

\mathbf{t}-

For theoretical purposes it might be also useful to have exact formulas. This in general is a very tedious task.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we give basic definitions and properties concerning Drinfeld modules and
\mathbf{t}-
modules.

Let
p
be a rational prime and
A={\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]
the polynomial ring over the finite field with
q=p^{m}
elements.

Definition 2.1
.

An
A
-field
K
is a fixed morphism

{\iota}:A\rightarrow K
. The kernel of
\iota
is a prime ideal
\mathcal{P}
of
A
called the characteristic. The characteristic of
\iota
is called finite if
{\mathcal{P}}\neq 0,
or generic (zero) if
{\mathcal{P}}=0.

Let
\mathbb{G}_{a,K}
be the additive algebraic group over
K.

Then the endomorphism ring
{\operatorname{End}}(\mathbb{G}_{a,K})
is the skew polynomial ring
K\{\tau\}.
The endomorphism
\tau
is
the map
u\rightarrow u^{q}
and therefore one has the commutation relation
\tau u=u^{q}\tau
for
u\in K.

Definition 2.2
.

A Drinfeld
A
-module is a homomorphism
\phi:A\rightarrow K\{\tau\},

a\rightarrow{\phi}_{a}\,,
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-algebras such that

1.

D\circ{\phi}={\iota},

2.

for some
a\in A,\,{\phi}_{a}\neq{\iota}(a){\tau}^{0},

where
D({\sum}_{i=0}^{i={\nu}}\,\,a_{i}{\tau}^{i})=a_{0}.
The characteristic of a Drinfeld module is the characteristic of
{\iota}.

There exists a generalization of the notion of a Drinfeld module called a
\mathbf{t}-
module. This was developed by G. Anderson in
[
1
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

An
m
-dimensional
\mathbf{t}-
module over
K
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-algebra homomorphism

(2.1)

{\Phi}:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]\rightarrow{\mathrm{Mat}}_{m}(K\{\tau\})

such that
{\Phi}(t)
, as a polynomial in
\tau
with coefficients in
{\mathrm{Mat}}_{m}(K)
is of the following form:

(2.2)

{\Phi}(t)=({\theta}I_{d}+N)\tau^{0}+M_{1}{\tau}^{1}+\dots+M_{r}\tau^{r}

where
I_{m}
is the identity matrix and
N
is a nilpotent matrix. In general, a
\mathbf{t}-
module over
K
is an algebraic group
E
defined over
K
and isomorphic over
K
to
\mathbb{G}^{m}_{a}
together with a choice of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear endomorphism
t:E\rightarrow E
such that
d(t-\theta)^{n}{\mathrm{Lie}}(E)=0
for
n
sufficiently large. Notice that

d(\cdot)
stands here for the differential of an endomorphism of algebraic groups. The choice of an isomorphism
E\cong\mathbb{G}^{m}_{a}
is equivalent to the choice of
\Phi.

Notice that a Drinfeld module may be viewed as a one dimensional
\mathbf{t}-
module.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\Phi
be a
\mathbf{t}-
module defined over
K
as in (
2.1
) and
L
be an algebraic extension of
K.
The Mordell-Weil group
{\Phi}(L)
is the additive group of
L^{m}
viewed as an
A
-module via evaluation of the matrix
{\Phi}_{a}\in{\mathrm{Mat}}_{m}(K\{\tau\}),\,\,a\in A
on
L^{m},
where
\tau
acts on L as the Frobenius morphism
x\rightarrow x^{q}.

To make notation simpler for a non-negative integer
n
we denote
x^{q^{n}}:=x^{(n)}
to be the evaluation of the Frobenius twist
{\tau}^{n}
on
x\in K.
Of course
x^{(0)}=x
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\Phi
be a
\mathbf{t}-
module. Then

(i)

the rank of
\Phi
is defined as the rank of the period lattice of
\Phi
as a
d{\Phi}(A)
-module (cf.
[
2
, Section t-modules]
).

(ii)

the degree
\operatorname{deg}_{\tau}\Phi
of a
\mathbf{t}-
module
\Phi
is defined as
\operatorname{deg}_{\tau}\Phi_{t}.

Remark 2.6
.

Notice that for Drinfeld modules
\mathrm{rk}\phi=\operatorname{deg}_{\tau}\phi
but for a general
\mathbf{t}-
module the rank of
\Phi
is not always equal to
\operatorname{deg}_{\tau}\Phi
(cf.
[
8
, Example 5.9.9]
).

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\Phi
and
\Psi
be two
\mathbf{t}-
modules of dimension
d
and
e
, respectively. A morphism
f:\Psi\longrightarrow\Phi
of
\mathbf{t}-
modules over
K
is a matrix
f\in\mathrm{Mat}_{d\times e}(K\{\tau\})

such that

f\Psi(t)=\Phi(t)f.

Remark 2.8
.

Notice that the category of
\mathbf{t}-
modules is an additive subcategory of the abelian category of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]-
modules. This
subcategory is not full (cf.
[
11
, Example 10.2]
). Therefore the
\operatorname{Hom}
-set in the category of
\mathbf{t}-
modules will be denoted
as
{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\tau}
i.e.
\operatorname{Hom}(\Phi,\Psi):={\operatorname{Hom}}_{\tau}(\Phi,\Psi).

By a zero
\mathbf{t}-
module we mean the
\mathbf{t}-
module given by the map
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]\rightarrow 0.

Let
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\Phi,\Psi)
be the Baer group of extensions of
\mathbf{t}-
modules i.e. the group of exact sequences

(2.3)

0\rightarrow\Psi\rightarrow\Gamma\rightarrow\Phi\rightarrow 0

with the usual addition of extensions (cf.
[
15
]
).

An extension of a
\mathbf{t}
-module
\Phi:\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]\longrightarrow{\mathrm{Mat}}_{d}(K\{\tau\})
by
\Psi:\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]\longrightarrow{\mathrm{Mat}}_{e}(K\{\tau\})
can be determined by a biderivation i.e.
\mathbb{F}_{q}-
linear map
\delta:\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]\longrightarrow{\mathrm{Mat}}_{e\times d}(K\{\tau\})
such that

(2.4)

\delta(ab)=\Psi(a)\delta(b)+\delta(a)\Phi{(b)}\quad\textnormal{for all}\quad a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t].

The
\mathbb{F}_{q}-
vector space of all biderivations will be denoted by
\mathrm{Der}(\Phi,\Psi)
(cf.
[
17
]
).
The map
\delta\mapsto\delta(t)
induces the
\mathbb{F}_{q}-
linear isomorphism of the vector spaces
\mathrm{Der}(\Phi,\Psi)
and
{\mathrm{Mat}}_{e\times d}(K\{\tau\})
.
Let
\delta^{(-)}:{\mathrm{Mat}}_{e\times d}(K\{\tau\})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Der}(\Phi,\Psi)
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}-
linear map defined by the following formula:

\delta^{(U)}(a)=U\Phi_{a}-\Psi_{a}U\quad\textnormal{for all}\quad a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]\quad\textnormal{and}\quad U\in{\mathrm{Mat}}_{e\times d}(K\{\tau\}).

The image of the map
\delta^{(-)}
is denoted by
\mathrm{Der}_{in}(\Phi,\Psi)
, and is called the space of inner biderivations.
We have the following
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]-
module isomorphism (cf.
[
17
, Lemma 2.1]
):

(2.5)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\Phi,\Psi)\cong\mathrm{coker}\delta^{(-)}=\mathrm{Der}(\Phi,\Psi)/\mathrm{Der}_{in}(\Phi,\Psi).

In
[
11
]
the second and third author proved the following theorem:

Theorem 2.1
.

[

Let

\delta:\quad 0\longrightarrow F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\longrightarrow}}X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}E\longrightarrow 0

(i)

There is an exact sequence of

(ii)

There is an exact sequence of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]-

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\tau}(E,G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle-\circ\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\tau}(X,G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle-\circ i}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\tau}(F,G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle-\circ\delta}}{{\longrightarrow}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(E,G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle-\circ(-\pi)}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(X,G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle-\circ(-i)}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(F,G)\rightarrow 0.

When
K
is a perfect field,
\sigma
will denote the inverse of
\tau
. Then to simplify notation the value of
\sigma^{n}
on
x\in K
will be denoted as
x^{(-n)}
for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
K
be a perfect field. The ring
K\{\sigma\}
of adjoint twisted polynomials over
K
is defined by the following action of
\sigma
(cf.
[
7
]
):

{\sigma}x=x^{(-1)}\sigma\quad\textnormal{for}\quad x\in K.

A
\mathbf{t}^{\sigma}-
module is defined similarly as a
\mathbf{t}-
module by replacing
\tau
with
\sigma
. Similarly one defines a morphism of
\mathbf{t}^{\sigma}-
modules. The category of
\mathbf{t}^{\sigma}-
modules with the zero
\mathbf{t}^{\sigma}-
module attached is an additive,
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]-
linear category.

The following maps:

(-)^{\sigma}:K\{\tau\}\rightarrow K\{\sigma\};\quad\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}\tau^{i}\Big)^{\sigma}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}^{(-i)}{\sigma}^{i},

(-)^{\tau}:K\{\sigma\}\rightarrow K\{\tau\};\quad\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{n}b_{i}\sigma^{i}\Big)^{\tau}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}b_{i}^{(i)}{\tau}^{i}.

are
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear mutual inverses.
We associate with
\Phi:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]\rightarrow{\mathrm{Mat}}_{e}(K\{\tau\})
the adjoint homomorphism

\Phi^{\sigma}:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]\rightarrow{\mathrm{Mat}}_{e}(K\{\sigma\})

such that each matrix
X_{t}
is mapped to
\Big[\big(X_{t}(\tau)\big)^{\sigma}\Big]^{T}.
The inverse of
(-)^{\sigma}
is given
by the map that associates with
\Gamma:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]\rightarrow{\mathrm{Mat}}_{e}(K\{\sigma\})

the following homomorphism:

\Gamma^{\tau}:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]\rightarrow{\mathrm{Mat}}_{e}(K\{\tau\});\quad X_{t}\rightarrow\Big[\big(X_{t}(\sigma)\big)^{\tau}\Big]^{T}

We have the following:

.

[
11

Assume that
K
is a perfect
A-
field.
Let
\Phi
and
\Psi
be
\mathbf{t}-
modules. Then there exists an isomorphism of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]
-modules:

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\Phi,\Psi)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\sigma}(\Psi^{\sigma},\Phi^{\sigma})

Let us make the following general remark:

Remark 2.10
.

By Theorem

all algorithms presented in the sequel can be easily adapted for computation
of

3.
Algorithm of
\mathbf{t}-
reduction

In
[
17
]
a method for determination of
\mathbf{t}-
module structure
on

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(C^{\otimes n},C^{\otimes m})
for
n<m
was given. The method used the description of extension groups in terms of biderivations. Then various generalizations of this were constructed and used in
[
11
]
for determination
of the
\mathbf{t}-
module structure on the space
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\Phi,\Psi)
where
\Phi
and
\Psi
are
\mathbf{t}-
modules of some specific forms satisfying the condition
\mathrm{deg}_{\tau}\Phi>\mathrm{deg}_{\tau}\Psi
.
It turns out that these computations can be put together into one algorithm which we call
\mathbf{t}-
reduction.
We advise the reader to consult the Example 4.1 of
[
11
]
which illustrates the action of the
\mathbf{t}-
reduction algorithm
in the special case of two Drinfeld modules.

Before we describe the
\mathbf{t}-
reduction algorithm in full generality let us introduce some more notation.

Let
\Phi
resp.
\Psi
be
\mathbf{t}-
modules of dimensions
d
resp.
e
. By
E_{i\times j}
we denote the matrix of type
e\times d
, where the only nonzero entry is
1
at the place
i\times j
.
For a matrix
N=\big[n_{i,j}\big]\in\mathrm{Mat}_{e\times d}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})
, whose entries are non-negative integers we denote:

\mathrm{Mat}_{e\times d}(K\{\tau\})_{<N}=\Big\{\big[w_{i,j}(\tau)\big]\in\mathrm{Mat}_{e\times d}(K\{\tau\})\mid\operatorname{deg}_{\tau}w_{i,j}(\tau)<n_{i,j}\quad\forall i,j\Big\}

The
\mathbf{t}-
reduction algorithm is performed in the following steps

Step 1.

We use an isomorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]-
modules

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\Phi,\Psi)\cong\mathrm{Der}(\Phi,\Psi)/\mathrm{Der}_{in}(\Phi,\Psi),

and identify every element of
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\Phi,\Psi)
with the corresponding (class) of biderivation
\delta:\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]\longrightarrow\mathrm{Mat}_{e\times d}(K\{\tau\})
. Since every biderivation is uniquely determined by
\delta_{t}:=\delta(t)
,
we can view every element of

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\Phi,\Psi)
as the class of the matrix
\delta_{t}\in\mathrm{Mat}_{e\times d}(K\{\tau\})
.

Step 2.

We find a basis
\big(U_{i\times j}\big)
of the space
\mathrm{Mat}_{e\times d}(K)
such that the inner biderivations

\delta^{(c\tau^{k}U_{i\times j})}
for
c\in K
i
k=0,1,2,\dots
fulfill the following conditions:

A.

the inner biderivations
\delta^{(c\tau^{k}U_{i\times j})}
form a basis of the

\mathbb{F}_{q}-
linear space
\mathrm{Der}_{in}(\Phi,\Psi)
;

B.

the matrix
\delta^{(c\tau^{k}U_{i\times j})}_{t}
has at the
i\times j
-entry a polynomial whose coefficient
at the biggest power of
\tau
is equal to
c
.

C.

for every biderivation
\delta\in\mathrm{Der}(\Phi,\Psi)
there exists a sequence of inner biderivations
\delta^{(c_{l}\tau^{k_{l}}U_{i\times j})}
wbere
c_{l}\in K
and
k_{l}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
for
l=1,2,\dots,r

such that the reduced biderivation:

\delta^{\mathrm{reduce}}=\delta-\sum_{l=1}^{r}\delta^{(c_{l}\tau^{k_{l}}\cdot U_{i\times j})}\in\mathrm{Mat}_{e\times d}(K\{\tau\})_{<N},

where

N=\Big[\operatorname{deg}_{\tau}\delta^{(c\tau^{0}U_{i\times j})}_{t}\Big]_{i,j}.

D.

different reduced biderivations in

\mathrm{Mat}_{e\times d}(K\{\tau\})_{<N}
represent different elements in
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\Phi,\Psi)
.

Points A., B., C. and D. imply that there exists an isomorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q}-
linear spaces:

(3.1)

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\Phi,\Psi)\cong\mathrm{Mat}_{e\times d}(K\{\tau\})_{<N}.

Step 3.

In the space
\mathrm{Mat}_{e\times d}(K\{\tau\})_{<N}
we choose the set of generators of the following form:

E_{i\times j}c\tau^{k}\quad\textnormal{for}\quad c\in K,\quad\textnormal{and}\quad k=0,1,2,\dots,n_{i\times j}-1

Step 4.

In order to determine the structure of a
\mathbf{t}-
module on
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\Phi,\Psi)

we transfer the
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]-
module structure from
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\Phi,\Psi)=\mathrm{Der}(\Phi,\Psi)/\mathrm{Der}_{in}(\Phi,\Psi)
to the space
\mathrm{Mat}_{e\times d}(K\{\tau\})_{<N}
via the isomorphism (
3.1
).
To achieve this it is enough to find the value of multiplication by

t
on the generators from Step 3.
Recall that multiplication of
\delta\in\mathrm{Der}(\Phi,\Psi)/\mathrm{Der}_{in}(\Phi,\Psi)
by
t
is given by the following formula cf.
[
17
]
:

t*\delta=\Psi_{t}\cdot\delta.

Thus in this step we determine the values of multiplication by
t
on the generators chosen in Step 3:

t*E_{i\times j}c\tau^{k}=\Psi_{t}\cdot E_{i\times j}c\tau^{k}.

Step 5.

If
t*E_{i\times j}c\tau^{k}\notin\mathrm{Mat}_{e\times d}(K\{\tau\})_{<N}
then we reduce the terms of too big degrees
by means of the inner biderivations from Step 2. We finish the process when the reduced biderivation is in
\mathrm{Mat}_{e\times d}(K\{\tau\})_{<N}
.

Step 6.

We write the reduced values of
t*E_{i\times j}c\tau^{k}
in the coordinate system
E_{i\times j}\tau^{k}
.

Step 7.

Since every expression of the form

can be written as the evaluation of the monomial
\tau^{m}
at
\tau=c
the coordinate vector for every value of
t*E_{i\times j}c\tau^{k}
can be written as the vector of polynomials in
K\{\tau\}
evaluated in
\tau=c
.

Final Step

We form a matrix
\Pi_{t}
, whose columns are the vectors of polynomials computed in Step 7. Matrix
\Pi_{t}

determines the
\mathbf{t}-
module structure on
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\Phi,\Psi)
.

Remark 3.1
.

Notice that the condition for the degrees of
\mathbf{t}-

The following interesting question was asked by the referee:
When the space

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
can be expressed as a quotient of the
\operatorname{Hom}
-space? Such a situation will take place when the last two
\operatorname{Ext}-
groups in the six-term exact sequence described in Theorem

are isomorphic. The following lemma yields the equivalent condition for this in the language of biderivations.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

\delta:\quad 0\longrightarrow\Psi\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Upsilon\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Phi\longrightarrow 0

be a short exact sequence of
\mathbf{t}-
modules given by the biderivation
\delta
, where
\dim\Psi=e
and
\dim\Phi=d
. Let
\zeta
be a
\mathbf{t}-
module of dimension
f
. Then

(i)

an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]
-homomorphism
(-\pi)\circ-:\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\zeta,\Upsilon)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\zeta,\Phi)
is an isomorphism if and only if for arbitrary matrices

\delta_{2}\in\mathrm{Mat}_{e\times f}\big(K\{\tau\}\big)\quad\textnormal{and}\quad u\in\mathrm{Mat}_{d\times f}\big(K\{\tau\}\big)

there exists a morphism
F\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\tau}(\zeta,\Phi)
such that

(3.7)

\delta_{2}+\delta\big(F-u\big)\in\mathrm{Der}_{in}(\zeta,\Psi).

(i^{D})

an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]
-homomorphism
-\circ(-i):\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\Upsilon,\zeta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\Psi,\zeta)
is an isomorphism if and only if for arbitrary matrices

\delta_{1}\in\mathrm{Mat}_{f\times d}\big(K\{\tau\}\big)\quad\textnormal{and}\quad u\in\mathrm{Mat}_{f\times e}\big(K\{\tau\}\big)

there exists a morphism
F\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\tau}(\Psi,\zeta)
such that

\delta_{1}-\big(F-u\big)\delta\in\mathrm{Der}_{in}(\Phi,\zeta).

Proof.

We will prove the lemma for the case
(i)
. The proof for the second case is dual. Recall that the map
i
in the sequence
\delta
is an inclusion on the second coordinate and the map
\pi
is a projection on the first coordinate.
From (
2.5
) we have

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\zeta,\Phi)\cong\mathrm{Der}(\zeta,\Phi)/\mathrm{Der}_{in}(\zeta,\Phi)
and an isomorphism
\mathrm{Der}(\zeta,\Phi)\cong\mathrm{Mat}_{d\times f}\big(K\{\tau\}\big)
.
Similarly,
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\zeta,\Upsilon)\cong\mathrm{Der}(\zeta,\Upsilon)/\mathrm{Der}_{in}(\zeta,\Upsilon),
where
\mathrm{Der}(\zeta,\Upsilon)\cong\mathrm{Mat}_{e+d\times f}\big(K\{\tau\}\big)=\Big[\mathrm{Mat}_{e\times f}\big(K\{\tau\}\big),\mathrm{Mat}_{d\times f}\big(K\{\tau\}\big)\Big]^{T}
.
Then the map
(-\pi)\circ-:\mathrm{Der}(\zeta,\Upsilon)/\mathrm{Der}_{in}(\zeta,\Upsilon)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Der}(\zeta,\Phi)/\mathrm{Der}_{in}(\zeta,\Phi)
on the biderivation level is given by the following formula:

\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\delta_{1}\\
\delta_{2}\end{array}\right]+\mathrm{Der}_{in}(\zeta,\Upsilon)\longmapsto-\delta_{1}+\mathrm{Der}_{in}(\zeta,\Phi).

This map will be an isomorphism if and only if its kernel is trivial i.e. the condition
-\delta_{1}\in\mathrm{Der}_{in}(\zeta,\Phi)
implies that
\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\delta_{1}\\
\delta_{2}\end{array}\right]\in\mathrm{Der}_{in}(\zeta,\Upsilon)
.
This is equivalent to the statement that for every matrix

, where

there exists a matrix
\left[\begin{array}[]{c}w_{1}\\

such that

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\delta_{1}\\
\delta_{2}\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}w_{1}\\
w_{2}\end{array}\right]\zeta-\Upsilon\left[\begin{array}[]{c}w_{1}\\
w_{2}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}w_{1}\zeta-\Phi w_{1}\\
w_{2}\zeta-\Psi w_{2}-\delta w_{1}\end{array}\right].

The equality
\delta_{1}=w_{1}\zeta-\Phi w_{1}
is equivalent to the fact that the matrix

is a morphism of
\mathbf{t}-
modules
\zeta\rightarrow\Phi
. Moreover, the equality
\delta_{2}=w_{2}\zeta-\Psi w_{2}-\delta w_{1}
is equivalent to

\displaystyle\delta_{2}+\delta\big(F-u\big)=\delta_{2}+\delta w_{1}\in\mathrm{Der}_{in}(\zeta,\Psi).

∎

\displaystyle V\Phi-\Psi V=V\Big(\theta I+\sum A_{i}\tau^{i}\Big)-\Big(\theta I+\sum B_{i}\tau^{i}\Big)

5.
Algorithm in case of a composition series

Since the category of
\mathbf{t}-
modules is not abelian then for a
\mathbf{t}-
module
\Upsilon
and its
\mathbf{t}-
submodule
\Psi

their quotient
\Upsilon/\Psi
is usually not a
\mathbf{t}-
module (although it is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]-
module since the category
of
\mathbf{t}-
modules is a subcategory of the category of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]-
modules).
This motivates the following definitions:

Definition 5.1
.

We say that a
\mathbf{t}-
module
\Psi
is a
\tau-
submodule of a
\mathbf{t}-
module
\Upsilon
iff there exists a short
exact sequence of
\mathbf{t}-
modules:

0\longrightarrow\Psi\longrightarrow\Upsilon\longrightarrow\Upsilon/\Psi\longrightarrow 0.

It is clear that the dimension of a
\tau-
submodule
\Psi
is always smaller than the dimension
\Upsilon

Definition 5.2
.

We say that a
\mathbf{t}-
module
\Psi
is
\tau-
simple if it does not have any non-trivial
\tau-
submodules i.e. it is not a middle term of any nontrivial short exact sequence of
\mathbf{t}-
modules with nonzero terms.

Directly from the
Definition
5.2
it follows that Drinfeld modules are
\tau-
simple.
On the other hand any
\mathbf{t}-
module
\Upsilon
for which under some isomorphism the matrix
{\Upsilon}_{t}
can be reduced to the lower triangular matrix is not
\tau
-simple. This easily follows from the description of an extension by means of a biderivation.

Example 5.1
.

The
\mathbf{t}-
module given by the matrix

\Upsilon_{t}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\theta&\tau\\
\tau&\theta\par\end{array}\right]\cong\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\theta+\tau&0\\
\tau&\theta-\tau\end{array}\right]

is not
\tau
-simple since the isomorphism

f=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
0&1\end{array}\right].

brings
\Upsilon_{t}
to the lower diagonal form.

Definition 5.3
.

By a
\tau-
composition series of a
\mathbf{t}-
module
\Phi
we mean a sequence of
\mathbf{t}-
modules:

(5.1)

0=\Phi_{0}\subset\Phi_{1}\subset\Phi_{2}\subset\cdots\subset\Phi_{n-1}\subset\Phi_{n}=\Phi

such that
{\Phi}_{i}\subset{\Phi}_{i+1}
is an inclusion of a
\tau-
submodule and the quotient

\mathbf{t}-
module
{\Phi}_{i+1}/{\Phi}_{i}
is
\tau-
simple.

Let us give a simple proof of the following:

Theorem 5.1
.

Any
\mathbf{t}-
module is either
\tau-
simple or has non-trivial, i.e.
n\geq 2
,
\tau-
composition series (
5.1
).

Proof.

Notice that existence of
\tau
-composition series:

\displaystyle 0=\Upsilon_{0}

\displaystyle\subset\Upsilon_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\Upsilon_{n-1}\subset\Upsilon_{n}=\Upsilon,

where the quotients
\sigma_{i}=\Upsilon_{i}/\Upsilon_{i-1}
are
\tau-
simple
\mathbf{t}-
modules is equivalent with the fact that
\Upsilon
is isomorphic with a
\mathbf{t}-
module given by the block lower-triangular matrix where the blocks on the diagonal
correspond to subsequent
\tau-
simple modules

\sigma_{n}
,
\sigma_{n-1}
,
\dots
,
\sigma_{1}
.
So it is enough to show that every
\mathbf{t}-
module is given by the block lower-triangular matrix where on the diagonal one
has
\tau-
simple modules.
If
\mathbf{t}-
module
\Upsilon
is
\tau-
simple then there is nothing to prove.
We use induction on dimension
d
of the non-simple
\mathbf{t}-
module.
Assume that
\Upsilon
is not
\tau-
simple. In particular
d>1.

If
d=2
then we the following exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\sigma_{1}\rightarrow\Upsilon\rightarrow\sigma_{2}\rightarrow 0

where
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are Drinfeld modules.
Therefore
\Upsilon
is given by the following matrix:

\Upsilon_{t}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\sigma_{2,t}&0\\
\hline\cr\delta_{t}&\sigma_{1,t}\end{array}\right],

where
\delta_{t}
is a biderivation. This shows the assertion for

Assume that the theorem holds true for all

\mathbf{t}-
modules of dimension less than
d
. Let
\Upsilon
be of dimension
d
. Since
\Upsilon
is not
\tau-
simple
there exists a short exact sequence of the form:

0\longrightarrow\Phi\longrightarrow\Upsilon\longrightarrow\Psi\longrightarrow 0
.
Then

(5.2)

\Upsilon_{t}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\Psi_{t}&0\\
\hline\cr\delta_{t}&\Phi_{t}\end{array}\right],

for certain biderivation
\delta
. Since dimensions of
\Phi
and
\Psi
are less than

by inductive hypothesis we can consider
\Phi_{t}
and
\Psi_{t}
as block lower-triangular matrices with
\tau-

\mathbf{t}-
modules on diagonals.
Thus the same holds true for
\Upsilon_{t}
.
∎

In most cases analyzed in this paper, it is unreasonable to expect getting exact formulas for
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
since they are too complicated.
In this section we present exact formulas, for the easiest case, describing

\mathbf{t}-
module structure on
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\phi,\psi)
for two Drinfeld modules.
So assume that
\phi
and
\psi
are Drinfeld modules, such that
\phi_{t}=\theta+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\tau^{i}
,
\psi_{t}=\theta+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}\tau^{j}
and
r:=n-m>0
.
We transfer the
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]-

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\phi,\psi)=\mathrm{Der}(\phi,\psi)/\mathrm{Der}_{in}(\phi,\psi)
to the space
K\{\tau\}_{<\mathrm{deg}_{\tau}\phi}

[
11
]
. Since each element of
K\{\tau\}_{<\mathrm{deg}_{\tau}\phi}
is of the form
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}c_{i}\tau^{i}

on the generators
c_{i}\tau^{i}
for

\mathbf{t}-

(6.1)

\Pi_{t}=\Bigg[\big[t*\tau^{0}\big],\big[t*\tau^{1}\big],\dots,\big[t*\tau^{r-1}\big],\big[t*\tau^{r}\big],\big[t*\tau^{r+1}\big],\dots,\big[t*\tau^{n-1}\big]\Bigg].

defining the map

In order to describe

In the case

\displaystyle\big[t*\tau^{i}\big]

\displaystyle=\big[\underbrace{0,\dots,0}\limits_{i-1},\theta,b_{1}\tau,b_{2}\tau^{2},\dots,b_{m}\tau^{m},0,\dots,0\big]^{t}

for

\displaystyle i=0,1,2,\dots r-1.

\displaystyle d_{r+l,n+k}(\tau)=\dfrac{b_{m-l+k}\tau^{m-l+k}-\sum\limits_{i=k+1}^{l}a_{n+k-i}^{(i)}d_{r+l,n+i}(\tau)}{a_{n}^{(k)}}

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
(K,v)
be a valuation field.
Assume that
\phi
and
\psi
are Drinfeld modules, such that
r:=\mathrm{deg}_{\tau}\phi-\mathrm{deg}_{\tau}\psi>0
and

\phi_{t}=\theta+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\tau^{i}
,
\psi_{t}=\theta+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}\tau^{j}
.
If the following conditions

(i)

\psi_{t}
has integer coefficients,

(ii)

\phi_{t}-a_{n}\tau^{n}
has integer coefficients,

(iii)

v(a_{n})<\min\Bigg\{v\big(b_{1}\big),q^{i}v\big(a_{n-i}\big),\dfrac{v\big(b_{m+1-i}\big)}{q^{m-i}}\mid i=1,2,\dots,m-1\Bigg\}
,

hold true, then
\mathbf{t}-
module
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\phi,\psi)
has integer coefficients.

Proof.

First we will show, that the condition
(iii)
implies that the polynomials
d_{r+l,n+k}(\tau)
have integer coefficients. The proof will be carried out for the case
m>r
. The other cases are similar. Fix
l\in\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}
. We use the downward induction for
k=l,l-1,\dots,1,0
.

If
k=l
, then
d_{r+l,n+l}(\tau)=\dfrac{b_{m}}{a_{n}^{(l)}}\tau^{m}.
Because
v(a_{n})<\dfrac{v(b_{m})}{q^{m}}
, then

v(b_{m})>q^{m}v(a_{n})>q^{l}v(a_{n})=v(a_{n}^{(l)}),

so the polynomial
d_{r+l,n+l}(\tau)
has integer coefficients. Assume that the polynomials

d_{r+l,n+l}(\tau),d_{r+l,n+l-1}(\tau),\cdots,d_{r+l,n+k+1}(\tau)

have integer coefficients. We have

\displaystyle d_{r+l,n+k}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{b_{m-l+k}\tau^{m-l+k}-\sum\limits_{i=k+1}^{l}a_{n+k-i}^{(i)}d_{r+l,n+i}(\tau)+\sum\limits_{i=k+r}^{l}b_{n+k-i}d_{r+l,n+i}^{(n+k-i)}(\tau)}{a_{n}^{(k)}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{b_{m-l+k}}{a_{n}^{(k)}}\tau^{m-l+k}-\sum\limits_{i=k+1}^{l}\dfrac{a_{n+k-i}^{(i)}}{a_{n}^{(k)}}d_{r+l,n+i}(\tau)+\sum\limits_{i=k+r}^{l}\dfrac{b_{n+k-i}}{a_{n}^{(k)}}d_{r+l,n+i}^{(n+k-i)}(\tau).

From the inductive hypotheses the polynomials
d_{r+l,n+i}(\tau)
and
d_{r+l,n+i}^{(n+k-i)}(\tau)
have
integer coefficients. Thus, it is enough to show that the valuations of fractions

\dfrac{b_{m-l+k}}{a_{n}^{(k)}},\quad\dfrac{a_{n+k-i}^{(i)}}{a_{n}^{(k)}},\quad\dfrac{b_{n+k-i}}{a_{n}^{(k)}}

are positive. This follows from condition
(iii)
. Hence the polynomials
d_{r+l,n+k}(\tau)
have integer coefficients.

The claim follows now from the form of the matrix
\Pi_{t}
of the
\mathbf{t}-
module
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\tau}(\phi,\psi)
by using conditions
(i)
and
(ii)
.
∎

Corrigendum

In the paper
Algorithms for determination of
t
-module structures on some extension groups
, there is a local error in Algorithm 2, more precisely in the function
Reduce1
, in the case of strictly pure
\mathbf{t}-
modules.

The error appears both in the printed pseudocode and in the corresponding
Mathematica
implementation given in the Appendix. In certain cases, the function
Reduce1
does not completely reduce the matrix
\bm{V}
.

In the published version, the reduction of
\bm{V}
starts at the upper-left entry and proceeds row by row, from left to right. However, when reducing the entry
(i,j)
, it may happen that the degree of an earlier entry
(k,l)

k\leq i
and
l\leq j
increases. In such a situation, the original function does not return to that entry and, therefore, leaves it unreduced.

We stress that this error is local. Both the underlying reduction procedure and the proof of correctness remain valid. In particular, all proofs and conclusions presented in the paper remain correct. The correction concerns only the explicit form of the procedure
Reduce1
for strictly pure
t
-modules, as well as the corresponding implementation.

The corrected version of the pseudocode is as follows.

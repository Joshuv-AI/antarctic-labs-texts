Sums of Fibonacci numbers close to a power of 2
Elchin Hasanalizade

Abstract
. In this paper, we find all sums of two Fibonacci numbers which are close to a power of 2. As a corollary, we also determine all Lucas numbers close to a power of 2. The main tools used in this work are lower bounds for linear forms in logarithms due to Matveev and Dujella-Pethö version of the Baker-Davenport reduction method in diophantine approximation. This paper continues and extends the previous work of Chern and Cui.

11B39, 11J86

1.
Introduction

The Fibonacci sequence
(F_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is the binary recurrence sequence defined by
F_{0}=F_{1}=1
and

\displaystyle F_{n+2}=F_{n+1}+F_{n}\ \text{for all}\ n\geq 0.

There is a broad literature on the Diophantine equations involving the Fibonacci numbers. Bugeaud, Mignotte and Siksek
[
4
]
proved that the only perfect powers among the Fibonacci numbers are
F_{0}=0
,
F_{1}=F_{2}=1
,
F_{6}=8
and
F_{12}=144
. In particular, 1, 2 and 8 are the only powers of 2 in the Fibonacci sequence. Bravo and Luca
[
3
]
studied the Diophantine equation

\displaystyle F_{n}+F_{m}=2^{a}

in nonnegative integers
n,m
and
a
with
n\geq m
.
Bravo and Bravo
[
1
]
determined all the solutions of the Diophantine equation

\displaystyle F_{n}+F_{m}+F_{l}=2^{a}

in nonnegative integers
n,m,l
and
a
with
n\geq m\geq l
.

Chim and Ziegler
[
6
]
completely solved the Diophantine equations

\displaystyle F_{n_{1}}+F_{n_{2}}=2^{a_{1}}+2^{a_{2}}+2^{a_{3}}

and

\displaystyle F_{m_{1}}+F_{m_{2}}+F_{m_{3}}=2^{t_{1}}+2^{t_{2}}

in nonnegative integers
n_{1},n_{2},m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},t_{1},t_{2}
with
n_{1}\geq n_{2}
,
a_{1}\geq a_{2}\geq a_{3}
,
m_{1}\geq m_{2}\geq m_{3}
and
t_{1}\geq t_{2}
,

We say that an integer
n
is close to a positive integer
m
, if it satisfies

\displaystyle|n-m|<\sqrt{m}.

In 2014, Chern and Cui
[
5
]
studied the Fibonacci numbers which are close to a power of 2. More precisely, they found that the only solutions
(F_{n},2^{m})
of the Diophantine inequality

(1.1)

\displaystyle|F_{n}-2^{m}|<2^{m/2}

are
(1,2)
,
(2,2)
,
(3,2)
,
(3,4)
,
(5,4)
,
(8,8)
,
(13,16)
and
(34,32)
.

In 2020, Bravo et al.
[
2
]
extended the previous work
[
5
]
and found all the members of the
k
-generalized Fibonacci sequence
(F^{(k)}_{n})_{n\geq-(k-2)}
,
k\geq 2
which are close to a power of 2.

In the present paper, we study the Diophantine inequality

(1.2)

\displaystyle|F_{n}+F_{m}-2^{a}|<2^{a/2}

in positive integers
n,m
and
a
with
n\geq m
.

In particular, we prove the following main theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

There are exactly 52 solutions
(n,m,a)\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
to Diophantine inequality (
1.2
). All solutions satisfy
n\leq 42
and
a\leq 28
. A list of solutions is given in the appendix.

The well-known companion sequence of the Fibonacci sequence is Lucas sequence
(L_{n})_{n\geq 0}
. It satisfies the same recursive relation as the Fibonacci numbers, but with initial conditions
L_{0}=2
and
L_{1}=1
. Recall the following relation between the Fibonacci numbers and the Lucas numbers

\displaystyle L_{k}=F_{k-1}+F_{k+1}\ \text{for all}\ k\geq 1.

As a corollary we determine all Lucas numbers which are close to a power of 2.

Corollary 1.2
.

There are only 9 Lucas numbers which are close to a power of 2. Namely, the solutions
(n,a)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
of the inequality

\displaystyle|L_{n}-2^{a}|<2^{a/2}

are
(1,1)
,
(2,1)
,
(2,2)
,
(3,2)
,
(4,3)
,
(6,4)
,
(7,5)
,
(10,7)
and
(13,9)
.

We shall prove Theorem 1.1 by following closely the typical strategy as performed in
[
1
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
6
]
. First, we apply a lower bound for linear forms in logarithms due to Matveev to obtain a large upper bound for
n
. Applying a version of the Baker-Davenport reduction method we reduce this huge bound.

2.
Preliminaries

First, recall that the Binet formula for Fibonacci numbers

(2.1)

\displaystyle F_{n}=\frac{\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n}}{\alpha-\beta}

holds for all
n\geq 0
, where
\alpha=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
and
\alpha=\frac{1-\sqrt{5}}{2}=-\frac{1}{\alpha}
are the roots of the characteristic equation
x^{2}-x-1=0
of the Fibonacci sequence. Moreover, the equality

(2.2)

\displaystyle\alpha^{n-2}\leq F_{n}\leq\alpha^{n-1}

holds for all
n\geq 1
.

Next we recall a definition and some facts about logarithmic height. Let
\alpha
be an algebraic number of degree
d\geq 1
with the minimal polynomial

\displaystyle a_{d}X^{d}+\ldots+a_{1}X+a_{0}=a_{d}\prod_{i}^{d}(X-\alpha_{i}),

where
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}
are relatively prime integers and
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}
are the conjugates of
\alpha
. The absolute logarithmic Weil height of
\alpha
is defined as

\displaystyle h(\alpha)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\big(\log{|a_{d}|}+\sum_{i}^{d}\log{(\text{max}\{|\alpha_{i}|,1\})}\big)

In particular, if
\alpha=\frac{p}{q}
is a rational number with
\text{gcd}(p,q)=1
and
q>0
, then
h(\alpha)=\log{\text{max}\{|p|,q\}}
. The following properties of
h(\alpha)
are well-known.

•

h(\alpha\pm\beta)\leq h(\alpha)+h(\beta)+\log{2}
,

•

h(\alpha\beta^{\pm 1})\leq h(\alpha)+h(\beta)
,

•

h(\alpha^{l})=|l|h(\alpha)
,
l\in\mathbb{Z}
.

One of the main tools in this paper is a widely used estimate on lower bounds for linear forms in complex logarithms due to Matveev
[
8
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{t}
be positive real algebraic numbers in a real algebraic number field
\mathbb{K}
of degree
D
, let
b_{1},\ldots,b_{t}\in\mathbb{Z}
and

\displaystyle\Lambda\coloneqq\gamma^{b_{1}}_{1}\cdots\gamma^{b_{t}}_{t}-1

is non-zero. Then

\displaystyle|\Lambda|>\text{exp}(-1.4\times 30^{t+3}\times t^{4.5}\times D^{2}(1+\log{D})(1+\log{B})A_{1}\ldots A_{t})

where

\displaystyle B\geq\text{max}\{|b_{1}|,\cdots,|b_{t}|\}

and

\displaystyle A_{i}\geq\text{max}\{Dh(\gamma_{i}),|\log{\gamma_{i}}|,0.16\}\ \text{for all}\ i=1,\ldots,t.

During the calculations, we get an upper bound on
n
which is too large, thus we need to reduce it. To do so, we use some results from the theory of continued fractions and Diophantine approximation. The next lemma is known as the Legendre’s criterion

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\tau
be an irrational number,
\frac{p_{0}}{q_{0}},\frac{p_{1}}{q_{1}},\frac{p_{2}}{q_{2}},\ldots
be all the convergents of the continued fraction expansion of
\tau
and
M
be a positive integer. Let
N
be a nonnegative integer such that
q_{N}>M
. Then putting
a(M)\coloneqq\text{max}\{a_{i}:\ i=0,1,2,\cdots,N\}
, the inequality

\displaystyle\big|\tau-\frac{r}{s}\big|>\frac{1}{(a(M)+2)s^{2}}

holds for all pairs
(r,s)
of positive integers with
0<s<M
.

At last, we need a variant of the Baker-Davenport lemma, which is due to Dujella and Pethö
[
7
]
. For a real number
X
, we denote by
||X||=\text{min}\{|x-n|,\ n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
the distance from
X
to the nearest integer.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
M
be a positive integer,
\frac{p}{q}
be a convergent of the continued fraction expansion of the irrational number
\gamma
such that
q>6M
and
A,B,\mu
be some real numbers with
A>0
and
B>1
. Furthermore, let
\epsilon\coloneqq||\mu q||-M||\gamma q||
. If
\epsilon>0
, then there is no solution to the inequality

\displaystyle 0<|u\gamma-v+\mu|<AB^{-w}

in positive integers
u,v
and
w
with

\displaystyle u\leq M\ \text{and}\ w\geq\frac{\log{\big(\frac{Aq}{\epsilon}\big)}}{\log{B}}.

3.1.
Upper bound for
n

First of all, note that if
(n,1,a)
is a solution of (
1.2
), then
(n,2,a)
is also a solution to (
1.2
) since
F_{1}=F_{2}=1
. So from now on we assume that
m\geq 2
. Due to the work of Chern and Cui all solutions of (
1.2
) with

are listed in the appendix. Since
F_{n}+F_{n-1}=F_{n+1}
, we can assume
n>m+1
and in particular

Proposition 3.1
.

There are exactly 52 solutions
(n,m,a)\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
to (
1.2
) with
n\leq 250
. All solutions fulfill
n\leq 42
and
a\leq 28
. The list of solutions is given in the appendix.

Proof.

The solutions were found by a brute force search with a simple Python code.
∎

Because of Proposition 3.1 for the rest of the paper we assume that
n>250
and we will show that there exist no solutions with
n>250
.

Observe that the Binet formula (
2.2
) yields for

(3.1)

\displaystyle 0.38\alpha^{n}<\alpha^{n}\frac{1-\alpha^{-4}}{\sqrt{5}}\leq F_{n}=\alpha^{n}\frac{1-(-1)^{n}\alpha^{-2n}}{\sqrt{5}}\leq\alpha^{n}\frac{1-\alpha^{-6}}{\sqrt{5}}<0.48\alpha^{n}

Due to (
3.1
) for
n>m>1
we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle 0.38\alpha^{n}<F_{n}<F_{n}+F_{m}<0.48\alpha^{n}+0.48\alpha^{n-1}<0.78\alpha^{n}.

Let us now establish a relation between
n
and
a
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
a\geq 2
. Combining (
1.2
) with the right inequality of (
3.2
), one gets

\displaystyle 2^{a-1}\leq 2^{a}-2^{\frac{a}{2}}<F_{n}+F_{m}<0.78\alpha^{n}<\alpha^{n}.

On ther hand, combining (
1.2
) with the left inequality of (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle 0.38\alpha^{n}<F_{n}+F_{m}<2^{a}+2^{\frac{a}{2}}<2^{a+1}.

Thus

(3.3)

\displaystyle n\frac{\log{\alpha}}{\log{2}}+\frac{\log{0.38}}{\log{2}}-1<a<n\frac{\log{\alpha}}{\log{2}}+1,

where
\frac{\log{\alpha}}{\log{2}}=0.6942...
. In particular, we have
a<n
.

By the Binet formula, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{n}+\alpha^{m}}{\sqrt{5}}-(F_{n}+F_{m})=\frac{\beta^{n}+\beta^{m}}{\sqrt{5}}

Taking the absolute values in the above equality and combining it with (
1.2
), we get

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\alpha^{n}(1+\alpha^{m-n})}{\sqrt{5}}-2^{a}\bigg|<\frac{|\beta|^{n}+|\beta|^{m}}{\sqrt{5}}+2^{a/2}<\frac{1}{3}+2^{a/2}<2^{\frac{a}{2}+1}

for all
n\geq 4
and
m\geq 2
. Dividing both sides of the above inequality by
2^{a}
we obtain

(3.5)

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\alpha^{n}(1+\alpha^{m-n})}{2^{a}\sqrt{5}}-1\bigg|<2^{-\frac{a}{2}+1}.

In the first application of Matveev’s theorem, we take the parameters
t=3
and

\displaystyle(\gamma_{1},b_{1})\coloneqq(2,-a),(\gamma_{2},b_{2})\coloneqq(\alpha,n),(\gamma_{3},b_{3})\coloneqq\bigg(\frac{1+\alpha^{m-n}}{\sqrt{5}},1\bigg)

The three numbers
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}
are real, positive and are contained in

, so we can take
D\coloneqq[K:\mathbb{Q}]=2
.

Note that the left-hand side of (
3.5
) is not zero, for otherwise we would get the relation

(3.6)

\displaystyle 2^{a}\sqrt{5}=\alpha^{n}+\alpha^{m}.

Conjugating the above relation in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})
, we get

(3.7)

\displaystyle-2^{a}\sqrt{5}=\beta^{n}+\beta^{m}.

Combining (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), we get

\displaystyle\alpha^{n}<\alpha^{n}+\alpha^{m}=|\beta^{n}+\beta^{m}|\leq|\beta|^{n}+|\beta|^{m}<1

which is impossible for positive

. Hence the left-hand side of (
3.5
) is non-zero.

Since
h(\gamma_{1})=\log{2}=0.6931...
we can choose
A_{1}\coloneqq 1.4>Dh(\gamma_{1})
. Further since
h(\gamma_{2})=\frac{\log{\alpha}}{2}=0.2406...
we can take
A_{2}\coloneqq 0.5>Dh(\gamma_{2})

\displaystyle\gamma_{3}=\frac{1+\alpha^{m-n}}{\sqrt{5}}<\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}\ \text{and}\ \gamma^{-1}_{3}=\frac{\sqrt{5}}{1+\alpha^{m-n}}<\sqrt{5}

so that

. Next, by the properties of the logarithmic height we have

\displaystyle h(\gamma_{3})\leq\log{\sqrt{5}}+|m-n|\bigg(\frac{\log{\alpha}}{2}\bigg)+\log{2}=\log{2\sqrt{5}}+(n-m)\bigg(\frac{\log{\alpha}}{2}\bigg)

Hence, we can take

\displaystyle A_{3}\coloneqq 3+(n-m)\log{\alpha}>\text{max}\{2h(\gamma_{3}),|\log{\gamma_{3}}|,0.16\}.

Finally, by recalling that
a<n
, we infer that
\text{max}\{|b_{1}|,|b_{2}|,|b_{3}|\}=n

B\coloneqq n
. We put
\Lambda_{1}\coloneqq\frac{\alpha^{n}(1+\alpha^{m-n})}{2^{a}\sqrt{5}}-1
. Now Matveev’s theorem tells us that the left-hand side of (

\displaystyle\log{|\Lambda_{1}|}

\displaystyle>-1.4\times 30^{6}\times 3^{4.5}\times 2^{2}(1+\log{2})(1+\log{n})\times 1.4\times 0.5\times(3+(n-m)\log{\alpha})

\displaystyle>-1.4\times 10^{12}\times\log{n}\times(3+(n-m)\log{\alpha})

where we used the fact that the inequality

holds for all

.

By comparing the above inequality with the right-hand side of (

) we get that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{a}{2}-1\bigg)\log{2}<1.4\times 10^{12}\times\log{n}\times(3+(n-m)\log{\alpha}).

Let us consider a second linear form in logarithms. To this end, we rewrite (
3.4

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{n}}{\sqrt{5}}-(F_{n}+F_{m})=\frac{\beta^{n}}{\sqrt{5}}-F_{m}

Again combining the above relation with (

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\alpha^{n}}{\sqrt{5}}-2^{a}\bigg|<2^{\frac{a}{2}}+\frac{|\beta|^{n}}{\sqrt{5}}+F_{m}<2^{\frac{a}{2}}+\frac{1}{2}+\alpha^{m},

where we have also used the fact that

and taking into account that

(3.9)

\displaystyle|1-2^{a}\cdot\alpha^{-n}\cdot\sqrt{5}|<\frac{2^{\frac{a}{2}}\sqrt{5}}{\alpha^{n}}+\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2\alpha^{n}}+\frac{\sqrt{5}}{\alpha^{n-m}}<\frac{3\sqrt{5}}{2}\text{max}\{\alpha^{m-n},\alpha^{a-n}\}

In a second application of Matveev’s theorem, we take the parameters

and

\displaystyle(\gamma_{1},b_{1})\coloneqq(2,a),(\gamma_{2},b_{2})\coloneqq(\alpha,-n),(\gamma_{3},b_{3})\coloneqq(\sqrt{5},1)

The three numbers
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}
are real, positive and belong to

D=2
. Put
\Lambda_{2}\coloneqq 1-2^{a}\cdot\alpha^{-n}\cdot\sqrt{5}
. If

. Since

. Further since

Then Matveev’s theorem tells us that

\displaystyle\log{|\Lambda_{2}|}

\displaystyle>-1.4\times 30^{6}\times 3^{4.5}\times 2^{2}(1+\log{2})(1+\log{n})\times 1.4\times 0.5\times 1.7

\displaystyle>-2.31\times 10^{12}\log{n}

Comparing the resulting inequality with (

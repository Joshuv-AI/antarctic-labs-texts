Let
(a_{i})_{i\in I}
and

The sequence

\delta

Let

Let

is a

\delta
-deformation
of the polynomial

the sequence

of coefficients of

is a

\delta
-deformation of the sequence
(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
of coefficients of
f(t)

Every polynomial of degree

\mathbf{C}[t]

n

be the sequence of roots of
f(t)

be the sequence of roots of

,
where a root of multiplicity

The polynomial

is
\varepsilon
-aligned
with the polynomial

if there is an ordering

of the sequence of the
n
roots of
f(t)

and an ordering
(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n})
of the sequence of the
n
roots of
g(t)

such that the sequence
(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n})
is an
\varepsilon
-deformation
of the sequence
(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n})

A classical theorem on the continuity of roots of a polynomial states that,
for every

there exists

such that if the polynomial

is a
\delta
-deformation of the polynomial

is
\varepsilon
-aligned
with

.
Most proofs of this result require sophisticated mathematical

Recently, Nathanson and Ross

Moreover, their proof is valid for polynomials with coefficients
in every algebraically closed field with an absolute value.

The goal of this paper is to use nonstandard analysis to extend the theorem
on the continuity of the roots of a polynomial
in one variable to the continuity of the roots of a polynomial in several variables
or of a set of polynomials in several variables, that is, to hypersurfaces and affine algebraic varieties.

Notation:
The left superscript * is used to distinguish sets in the nonstandard world
from sets in the standard world.

4.
Open problems

The distance from a point
\mathbf{w}
to a nonempty set
Z
in a metric space is

d(\mathbf{w},Z)=\inf\{d(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}):\mathbf{z}\in Z\}.

The
Hausdorff distance
between nonempty
sets
W
and
Z
is

d_{H}(W,Z)=\max\left(\sup_{\mathbf{w}\in W}d(\mathbf{w},Z),\sup_{\mathbf{z}\in Z}d(\mathbf{z},W)\right).

For
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbf{C}^{n}
, let
\|\mathbf{z}\|_{\infty}=\max\left\{|z_{i}|:i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\right\}
.
The set
\mathbf{C}^{n}
is a metric space with distance function
d(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})=\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{z}\|_{\infty}
.
Let
W
and
Z
be nonempty subsets of
\mathbf{C}^{n}
.
The set
W
is an
\varepsilon
-deformation

of
Z
, denoted
W\approx_{\varepsilon}Z
, if
d_{H}(W,Z)<\varepsilon
.
Equivalently,
W
is an
\varepsilon
-deformation of
Z

if for each
\mathbf{w}\in W
there exists
\mathbf{z}\in Z
with
\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{z}\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon

and for each
\mathbf{z}\in Z
there exists
\mathbf{w}\in W
with
\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{z}\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
.
This relation is symmetric:
W\approx_{\varepsilon}Z
if and only if
Z\approx_{\varepsilon}W
.

Let
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},t_{2})
and
0<\delta^{\prime}<\delta
. Consider the polynomials

f(\mathbf{t})=t_{2}-t_{1}

and

g(\mathbf{t})=t_{2}-(1+\delta^{\prime})t_{1}

in the ring
\mathbf{C}[\mathbf{t}]=\mathbf{C}[t_{1},t_{2}]
.
The polynomial
g(\mathbf{t})
is a
\delta
-deformation of the polynomal
f
.
We obtain the hypersurfaces

\displaystyle V(f)

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbf{C}^{2}:f(\mathbf{z})=0\}

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{z}=(z,z):z\in\mathbf{C}\}

and

\displaystyle V(g)

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},w_{2})\in\mathbf{C}^{2}:g(\mathbf{w})=0\}

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{w}=(w,(1+\delta^{\prime})w):w\in\mathbf{C}\}.

For all
\mathbf{z}=(z,z)\in V(f)
and
\mathbf{w}=(w,(1+\delta^{\prime})w)\in V(g)
we have

\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{z}\|_{\infty}=\max\left(|w-z|,|w-z+\delta^{\prime}w|\right).

Let
\varepsilon>0
.
If
\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{z}\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
, then

|w-z|<\varepsilon\qqand|w-z+\delta^{\prime}w|<\varepsilon

and so

|\delta^{\prime}w|-|w-z|\leq|w-z+\delta^{\prime}w|<\varepsilon

and

|\delta^{\prime}w|<\varepsilon+|w-z|<2\varepsilon.

It follows that

|w|<\frac{2\varepsilon}{\delta^{\prime}}.

Equivalently, for all
\varepsilon>0
and
0<\delta^{\prime}<\delta
, if
w\in\mathbf{C}
and

|w|\geq\frac{2\varepsilon}{\delta^{\prime}}

then

\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{z}\|_{\infty}\geq\varepsilon

for all
\mathbf{z}\in V(f)
.
Thus, if
\varepsilon>0
, then for every
\delta>0
the polynomial
g(\mathbf{t})

is a
\delta
-deformation of the polynomial
f(\mathbf{t})
but the hypersurface
V(g)

is not an
\varepsilon
-deformation of the hypersurface
V(f)
.

It would be of interest to translate Theorems
1

and
2
from the language of the nonstandard
mathematics to standard mathematics. The simple example above shows that
it is not true that, for every
\varepsilon>0
,
there exists
\delta>0
such that
if the polynomial
g\in\mathbf{C}[\mathbf{t}]
is a
\delta
-deformation of the polynomial

f\in\mathbf{C}[\mathbf{t}]
, then the hypersurface

V(g)
is an
\varepsilon
-deformation of the hypersurface
V(f)
.

There is the following elementary result.
For
I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\in\mathbf{N}_{0}^{n}
, let
|I|=\sum_{j=1}^{n}i_{j}
.
Let
|\mathcal{I}|
denote the cardinality of the finite set
\mathcal{I}
.
For all
T>0
we define the
n
-dimensional complex
hypercube

H(T)=\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbf{C}^{n}:\|\mathbf{z}\|_{\infty}\leq T\}.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
, let
\mathcal{I}
be a nonempty finite subset of
\mathbf{N}_{0}^{n}
, and let

f(\mathbf{t})=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}a_{I}\mathbf{t}^{I}

be a nonzero polynomial in
\mathbf{C}[\mathbf{t}]
.
Let

d=\max(|I|:I\in\mathcal{I}).

Let
T\geq 1
. For every
\varepsilon>0
, let

0<\delta<\frac{\varepsilon}{T^{d}|\mathcal{I}|}.

Let the polynomial

g(\mathbf{t})=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}b_{I}\mathbf{t}^{I}\in\mathbf{C}[\mathbf{t}]

be a
\delta
-deformation of
f
.
If
\mathbf{z}\in H(T)
, then

|g(\mathbf{z})-f(\mathbf{z})|<\varepsilon.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbf{C}^{n}
with

\|\mathbf{z}\|_{\infty}=\max(|z_{i}|:i=1,\ldots,n)\leq T.

For all
I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\in\mathcal{I}
we have

|b_{I}-a_{I}|<\delta

and

\left|\mathbf{z}^{I}\right|=\left|z_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{i_{n}}\right|\leq T^{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{n}}=T^{|I|}\leq T^{d}.

It follows that

\displaystyle|g(\mathbf{z})-f(\mathbf{z})|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}(b_{I}-a_{I})\mathbf{z}^{I}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}|b_{I}-a_{I}|\left|\mathbf{z}^{I}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\delta T^{d}|\mathcal{I}|

\displaystyle<\varepsilon.

This completes the proof.
∎

Let
g(\mathbf{t})
be a nonzero polynomial in
\mathbf{C}[\mathbf{t}]
.
For all
\varepsilon>0
, we define

V_{\varepsilon}(g)=\{\mathbf{w}\in\mathbf{C}^{n}:|g(\mathbf{w})|<\varepsilon\}.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
, let
\mathcal{I}
be a finite subset of
\mathbf{N}_{0}^{n}
, and let

f(\mathbf{t})=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}a_{I}\mathbf{t}^{I}

be a nonzero polynomial in
\mathbf{C}[\mathbf{t}]
. For all
\varepsilon>0
and
T>0

there exists
\delta>0
such that if the polynomial
g(\mathbf{t})\in\mathbf{C}[\mathbf{t}]

is a
\delta
-deformation of
f(\mathbf{t})
, then

V(f)\cap H(T)\subseteq V_{\varepsilon}(g).

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
2
.
If
\mathbf{z}\in V(f)\cap H(T)
, then
f(\mathbf{z})=0
and so

|g(\mathbf{z})|=|g(\mathbf{z})-f(\mathbf{z})|<\varepsilon
and
\mathbf{z}\in V_{\varepsilon}(g)
.
This completes the proof.
∎

of points on hypersurfaces and affine varieties under infinitesimal deformations.

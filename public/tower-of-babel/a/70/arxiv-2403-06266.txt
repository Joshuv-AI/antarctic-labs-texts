An adaptive mesh refinement strategy to ensure quasi-optimality of finite element methods for self-adjoint Helmholtz problems Journal: Computer and Mathematics with Applications
Tim van Beeck a \,{}^{\text{a}}, Umberto Zerbinati b \,{}^{\text{b}}

Abstract
It is well known that the quasi-optimality of the Galerkin finite element method for the Helmholtz equation is dependent on the mesh size and the wave-number. In the literature, different criteria have been proposed to ensure uniform quasi-optimality of the discretisation. In the present work, we study the uniform quasi-optimality of H 1 H^{1} conforming and non-conforming Crouzeix-Raviart discretisation of the self-adjoint Helmholtz problem. In particular, we propose an adaptive scheme, coupled with a residual-based indicator, for generating guaranteed quasi-optimal meshes with minimal degrees of freedom.

Introduction

In this article, we consider the self-adjoint Helmholtz equation: find
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that

-\Delta u-k^{2}u=f\quad\text{in }\Omega,\quad u=0\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{D},\quad\bm{n}\cdot\nabla u=0\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{N},

(1)

where we assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
, is a bounded Lipschitz domain with boundary
\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{D}\cup\Gamma_{N}
, where
\Gamma_{D}
and
\Gamma_{N}
are such that
\Gamma_{D}\cap\Gamma_{N}=\emptyset
and
\bm{n}
is the outward unit normal. Our interest in the Helmholtz equation arises from its wide range of applications in acoustics, electromagnetics, elastodynamics, and seismology.

In particular, we are interested in Galerkin discretisations of the self-adjoint Helmholtz equation, where we seek to approximate the variational formulation resulting from (
1
) in a finite dimensional space
V_{h}
, usually consisting of piecewise polynomials of order
p
. A well-known issue in the analysis of such discretisations is that the resulting bilinear form lacks coercivity for large
k^{2}
.
This leads to the so-called
pollution effect
, i.e. for a fixed mesh, we lose quasi-optimality of the Galerkin method as the wave number
k^{2}
increases. Ensuring that the discretisation is quasi-optimal requires, for fixed polynomial degree
p
, that the mesh size
h
is sufficiently small. In particular, this requirement is typically more restrictive than the requirement
\frac{kh}{2\pi p}\leq 1
, which is needed to accurately resolve the wave.

We are here interested in the so-called
asymptotic regime
, i.e. where uniform quasi-optimality is guaranteed, see
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
.
Another area of active study is concerned with the
preasymptotic
regime, where on milder assumptions on the mesh size the relative error of the Galerkin solution is controllably small, see e.g.
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
.

Adpative finite element methods have shown to be extremely effective in the context of the Helmholtz equation
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
.
For example in
[
14
,
15
,
3
]
it has been proven that the hp-adaptive finite element method can achieve quasi-optimality for the Helmholtz equation under the following assumptions:

\frac{kh}{p}\ll 1,\text{ and }p\geq C_{hp}\log(k).

(2)

In the present work, we aim to study the discretisation of the self-adjoint Helmholtz equation (
1
) with
H^{1}
-conforming and non-conforming Crouzeix-Raviart finite elements.
We present an analysis based on the notion T-coercivity
[
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
, which highlights the relationship between the stability of the discrete problem and a sufficiently accurate approximation of the eigenvalues of the Laplace operator.
Based on this analysis, we propose a practical algorithm that produces a mesh which guarantees the quasi-optimality of the discretisation.
Until the approximation of the Laplace eigenvalues is sufficiently accurate, we refine the mesh either uniformly or adaptively.
In the latter case we use an adaptive error estimator for the eigenvalue problem
[
20
]
.
Similar ideas have been discussed also in
[
21
]
, but to the best of the authors’ knowledge, they have not been explored in details nor have been implemented to construct quasi-optimal meshes.
For the Crouzeix-Raviart discretisation, we leverage guaranteed lower bounds for the eigenvalue approximations as derived in
[
22
,
23
]
.

This paper is organized as follows: in Section
2
we introduce the abstract framework, which we apply in Section
3
to analyse the continuous problem. In Section
4
, we present a unified analysis that covers both
H^{1}
-conforming and non-conforming Crouzeix-Raviart discretisations.
Afterward, in Section
5
, we discuss a mesh generation strategy which ensures the quasi-optimality of the discretisations. Throughout Sections
4
and
5
, we present numerical results validating our observations. All numerical experiments presented in this manuscript are implemented in Python using the Firedrake finite element library
[
24
]
and ngsPETSc
[
25
]
.

2
Abstract framework

In this section, we provide a brief overview of the abstract framework we will use to analyze (
1
). In particular, we introduce the notion of T-coercivity
[
16
,
17
]
and discuss its application to study the well-posedness of variational problems and their approximation with Galerkin type methods.
In the following, let
V
be a Hilbert space and
\mathcal{A}:V\times V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a continuous bilinear form.

Definition 1

(T-coercivity)
.

The bilinear form
\mathcal{A}
is T-coercive on
V
if there exists a constant
\alpha>0
and a bijective operator
T:V\rightarrow V
such that

\mathcal{A}(u,Tu)\geq\alpha\|u\|^{2}_{V}\quad\forall u\in V.

For
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, we are concerned with the well-posedness of the problem:

\text{find }u\in V\text{ such that }\mathcal{A}(u,v)=(f,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\forall v\in V.

(3)

The T-coericvity of
\mathcal{A}
is equivalent to the well-posedness of (
3
) as shown in
[
17
, Thm. 1]
.

In Section
4
, we will consider both, an
H^{1}
-conforming and non-conforming Crouzeix-Raviart discretisations.
Thus, we consider the following setting which is general enough to cover both discretisations, but restrictive enough to avoid the more technical aspects of a fully discontinuous setting.
Let
\{V_{h}\}_{h\in\mathcal{H}}
be a sequence of finite-dimensional spaces, which do not necessarily have to be subspaces of
V
. We assume that the spaces
V_{h}
are equipped with a discrete norm
\|\cdot\|_{V_{h}}
which is well-defined on
V+V_{h}
.
Furthermore, let
\mathcal{A}_{h}:V_{h}\times V_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
be a discrete bilinear form which is continuous with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{V_{h}}
on
V+V_{h}
and consistent in the sense that
\mathcal{A}_{h}(u,v_{h})=(f,v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
for all
v_{h}\in V_{h}
when
u\in V
is the solution of (
3
).

We consider the discrete problem

\text{find }u_{h}\in V_{h}\text{ such that }\mathcal{A}_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=(f,v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.

(4)

Definition 2
.

The bilinear form
\mathcal{A}_{h}
is called uniformly T
h
-coercive on
\{V_{h}\}_{h\in\mathcal{H}}
, if there exists a family of bijective operators
\{T_{h}\}_{h\in\mathcal{H}}
,
T_{h}:V_{h}\to V_{h}
, and a constant
\alpha^{\ast}>0
independent of
h
such that

\mathcal{A}_{h}(u_{h},T_{h}u_{h})\geq\alpha^{\ast}\|u_{h}\|^{2}_{V_{h}}\quad\forall u_{h}\in V_{h}.

(5)

It has been shown that
\mathcal{A}_{h}
is uniformly T
h
-coercive on
V_{h}
if and only if the bilinear form
\mathcal{A}_{h}
fulfills an uniform inf-sup condition on
V_{h}
and therefore the stability of the discrete problem is equivalent to the uniform T
h
-coercivity of
\mathcal{A}_{h}
:

Theorem 3

(Thm. 2 of
[
17
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{h}
be uniformly T
h
-coercive on
V_{h}
with constant
\alpha^{\ast}>0
. Then, the discrete problem (
4
) is well-posed and stable. Further, it holds that

\|u-u_{h}\|_{V_{h}}\leq C_{\text{qo}}\inf_{v_{h}\in V_{h}}\|u-v_{h}\|_{V_{h}},

(6)

where
C_{\text{qo}}:=1+(\|\mathcal{A}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(V_{h},V_{h})}\|T_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(V_{h},V_{h})})/\alpha^{\ast}>0
.

We note that even in the conforming setting, the uniform T
h
-coercivity of
\mathcal{A}_{h}
has to be shown on
V_{h}
, because unlike coercivity, T-coercivity is not inherited by the discrete problem.
In the conforming setting, it would be sufficient to show that

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\|T_{h}-T\|_{\mathcal{L}(V,V)}=0

(7)

to ensure the uniform T
h
-coercivity of
\mathcal{A}_{h}
, cf.
[
17
, Cor. 1]
. Similar criteria can also be developed in a more general setting, applicable also to non-conforming discretisations, cf.
[
26
,
27
]
for T-compatibility and
[
28
,
29
]
for its applications to discontinuous Galerkin methods.

In the following, we will avoid the use of these criteria in our analysis to be more explicit on the assumptions on the mesh size
h
required to ensure the quasi-optimality of the Galerkin method.

3
Well-posedness of the continuous problem

We want to show the well-posedness of the continuous weak formulation associated with (
1
). To this end, we define the Hilbert space

V:=\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega):\gamma_{0}u=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{D}\},

(8)

where
\gamma_{0}:H^{1}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
is the trace operator. In the following, let
a(u,v):=(\nabla u,\nabla v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
,
u,v\in V
, be the bilinear form associated with the weak Laplacian
-\Delta
on
V
. The weak formulation of problem (
1
) reads as: find
u\in V
such that

\mathcal{A}(u,v):=a(u,v)-k^{2}(u,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(f,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\quad\forall v\in V.

(9)

Let
(\lambda^{(i)},e^{(i)})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be the eigenpairs of the Laplacian with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions, i.e. the solutions to the problem: find
\lambda^{(i)}\in\mathbb{R}
and
e^{(i)}\in V\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

a(e^{(i)},v)=\lambda^{(i)}(e^{(i)},v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\forall v\in V\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(10)

Let
S:L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
be the solution operator associated to (
10
). Due to the Poincaré-inequality, the bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is coercive on
V\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and thus the well-posedness of problem (
10
) is a consequence of the Lax-Milgram lemma.
Hence, the solution operator
S
is well-defined and due to the compactness of the embedding
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
the operator
S
is also compact.
Additionally,
S
is self-adjoint and therefore the eigenfunctions
\{e^{(i)}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
form a Hilbert basis of
L^{2}(\Omega)

[
30
,
31
]
.

Since
V\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
, we can represent any function
u\in V
as
u=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}u^{(i)}e^{(i)}
with coefficients
u^{(i)}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Without loss of generality, we assume that the eigenfunctions
\{e^{(i)}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are normalized with respect to the
H^{1}
-norm and that the eigenvalues
\{\lambda^{(i)}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are ordered by increasing value and counted with their algebraic multiplicity.

From now on, we assume that the wave-number
k
is such that
k^{2}>\lambda^{(1)}
, otherwise the problem is coercive and the well-posedness of (
9
) follows directly from the Lax-Milgram lemma.
Then, we denote by
i_{\ast}\coloneqq\max\{i\in\mathbb{N}:\lambda^{(i)}<k^{2}\}
the largest index such that
\lambda^{(i_{\ast})}
is smaller than
k^{2}
.

We define the subspace
W\subset V
and the map
T:V\rightarrow V
as follows:

W\coloneqq\text{span}_{0\leq i\leq i_{\ast}}\{e^{(i)}\}\subset V,\qquad T\coloneqq\Id_{V}-2P_{W},

(11)

where
P_{W}\in\mathcal{L}(V,W)
is the orthogonal projection onto
W
. Note that
T
is self-inverse and therefore bijective and acts on the eigenfunctions as

Te^{(i)}:=\begin{cases}-e^{(i)}&\text{if }0\leq i\leq i_{\ast},\\
+e^{(i)}&\text{if }i>i_{\ast}.\end{cases}

(12)

Lemma 4
.

Assume that
k^{2}\not\in\{\lambda^{(i)}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
. Then, the bilinearform
\mathcal{A}
defined in (
9
) is T-coercive on
V
.

To showcase the mechanism behind the T-coercivity of Helmholtz-like problems, we report the proof of
[
17
, Prop. 1]
.

Proof.

By definition,
T
swaps the sign of the eigenfunctions corresponding to eigenvalues smaller than
k^{2}
. Therefore, we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(u,Tu)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{0\leq i\leq i_{\ast}}\left(\frac{k^{2}-\lambda^{(i)}}{1+\lambda^{(i)}}\right)(u^{(i)})^{2}+\sum_{i>i_{\ast}}\left(\frac{\lambda^{(i)}-k^{2}}{1+\lambda^{(i)}}\right)(u^{(i)})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda^{(i)}(u^{(i)})^{2}\geq\alpha\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\quad\forall u\in V,

where
\alpha:=\min_{i\geq 0}\left\{\left|\frac{\lambda^{(i)}-k^{2}}{1+\lambda^{(i)}}\right|\right\}>0
.
∎

As previously discussed, the T-coercivity of the bilinear form
\mathcal{A}
immediately yields the well-posedness of (
9
) for
k^{2}\not\in\{\lambda^{(i)}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.

4
Discrete analysis

In this section, we consider the finite element approximation of the problem (
9
).
The main goal is to show that the resulting discretisations are uniformly T
h
-coercive which allows us to apply Thm.
3
to conclude the stability of the discrete problems.

In the following, we assume that
\Omega
is a polygonal domain and that
\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\}_{h\in\mathcal{H}}
is a sequence of shape regular triangulations of
\Omega
with mesh size
h
.
Let
\{V_{h}\}_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
be a sequence of finite dimensional spaces, which do not necessarily have to be subspaces of
V
.
Then, the discretisation of (
9
) reads as: find
u_{h}\in V_{h}
such that

\mathcal{A}_{h}(u_{h},v_{h}):=a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})-k^{2}(u_{h},v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(f,v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h},

(13)

where
a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h}):=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\nabla u_{h},\nabla v_{h})_{K}
is the bilinear form associated with the weak Laplacian on the discrete space
V_{h}
.

We will denote
V_{h,0}
the subspace of
V_{h}
incorporating homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions and define
n_{h}:=\dim V_{h,0}
. Let
(\lambda_{h}^{(i)},e_{h}^{(i)})_{0\leq i\leq n_{h}}
be the solutions of the discrete eigenvalue problem: find
\lambda^{(i)}_{h}\in\mathbb{R}
and
e^{(i)}_{h}\in V_{h,0}
such that

a_{h}(e_{h},v_{h})=\lambda_{h}(e_{h},v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\qquad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h,0}.

(14)

In the following, we assume that the discrete eigenvalue problem is well-posed and that the sequence of associated solution operators
\{S_{h}\}_{h\in\mathcal{H}}
,
S_{h}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
, converges uniformly in operator norm to the solution operator
S
of the continuous eigenvalue problem (
10
).
This assumption guarantees that the sequence of discrete eigenpairs
(\lambda^{(i)}_{h},e^{(i)}_{h})_{0\leq i\leq n_{h}}
, which we assume to be ordered by increasing value of the eigenvalues, approximates the continuous eigenpairs
(\lambda^{(i)},e^{(i)})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
as
h\to 0
.
In particular, this rules out the existence of
spurious modes
, which might pollute the discrete spectrum
[
31
]
.

As in the continuous setting, we set

W_{h}:=\text{span}_{0\leq i\leq i_{\ast}}\{e^{(i)}_{h}\}\subset V_{h},

(15)

and define a bijective operator
T_{h}:V_{h}\to V_{h}
through
T_{h}:=\Id_{V_{h}}-2P_{W_{h}}
, where
P_{W_{h}}\in\mathcal{L}(V_{h},W_{h})
is the orthogonal projection onto
W_{h}
. Note that
T_{h}
acts on eigenfunctions as

T_{h}(e^{(i)}_{h})=\begin{cases}-e^{(i)}_{h}&\text{if }0\leq i\leq i_{\ast},\\
+e^{(i)}_{h}&\text{if }i>i_{\ast}.\end{cases}

(16)

The following result shows that the bilinear form
\mathcal{A}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is uniformly T
h
-coercive and therefore stable by Thm.
3
provided that the mesh size
h
is small enough.
We will shortly discuss in greater detail how small
h
has to be.

Theorem 5
.

Under the previous assumptions on the discrete eigenvalue problem (
14
), the bilinear form
\mathcal{A}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is uniformly
T_{h}
-coercive if
h
is such that
\lambda_{h}^{(i_{\ast})}<k^{2}<\lambda_{h}^{(i_{\ast}+1)}
.

Proof.

Let
u_{h}\in V_{h}
. Using the discrete eigenbasis, we expand
u_{h}
as
u_{h}=\sum_{0\leq i\leq n_{h}}u^{(i)}_{h}e^{(i)}_{h}
.
In analogy with the continuous case, we can evaluate
\mathcal{A}_{h}(u_{h},T_{h}u_{h})
as

\mathcal{A}_{h}(u_{h},T_{h}u_{h})=\sum_{0\leq i\leq i_{\ast}}\left(\frac{k^{2}-\lambda^{(i)}_{h}}{1+\lambda^{(i)}_{h}}\right)(u^{(i)}_{h})^{2}+\sum_{i_{\ast}<i\leq n_{h}}\left(\frac{\lambda^{(i)}_{h}-k^{2}}{1+\lambda^{(i)}_{h}}\right)(u^{(i)}_{h})^{2}.

(17)

Provided that
\lambda_{h}^{(i_{\ast})}<k^{2}<\lambda_{h}^{(i_{\ast}+1)}
, all terms are positive and
\mathcal{A}_{h}
is uniformly
T_{h}
-coercive with constant
\alpha^{\ast}:=\min_{i\geq 0}\left\{\left|\frac{\lambda^{(i)}_{h}-k^{2}}{1+\lambda^{(i)}_{h}}\right|\right\}
, see also Fig.

.
∎

4.1
H^{1}
-conforming finite element discretisation

For
p\geq 1
we define
V_{h}\subset V
to be the
H^{1}
-conforming finite element space of order
p
, i.e.

V_{h}:=\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega):v|_{T}\in\mathcal{P}^{p}(T)\quad\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}\cap V.

We have that
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)=a(\cdot,\cdot)
and therefore
\mathcal{A}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)=\mathcal{A}(\cdot,\cdot)
on
V_{h}
. Since
V_{h}\subset V
is a conforming subspace, the coercivity of
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is inherited uniformly to the discrete level, hence problem (
14
) is well-posed.
Furthermore, it is well-known that the associated sequence of discrete solution operators converges to the solution operator of the continuous problem
[
31
]
. Thus, the assumptions on the discrete eigenvalue problem (
14
) are satisfied and Thm.
5
applies.

The following result shows that we can leverage eigenvalue estimates to derive an upper bound on the mesh size
h
such that the condition
\lambda_{h}^{(i_{\ast})}<k^{2}
is satisfied.

Corollary 6
.

Assume that
h^{2q}<C\left(\frac{k^{2}-\lambda^{(i_{\ast})}}{4\sqrt{i_{\ast}}\lambda^{(i_{\ast})}}\right)
, where
q\coloneqq\min\{p,s-1\}
with
s
being the Sobolev index granted by elliptic regularity for an
H^{1}(\Omega)
-data. Then
\lambda_{h}^{(i_{\ast})}<k^{2}<\lambda_{h}^{(i_{\ast}+1)}
.

Proof.

Since conforming discretisations approximate eigenvalues from above
[
31
]
, we only have to consider the index
i_{\ast}
, and it suffices to ensure that

\displaystyle\lambda_{h}^{(i_{\ast})}-\lambda^{(i_{\ast})}<k^{2}-\lambda^{(i_{\ast})}.

To bound the left-hand side, we can use approximation results for the eigenvalues. For instance
[
33
, Thm. 47.10]
yields

\displaystyle\lambda_{h}^{(i_{\ast})}-\lambda^{(i_{\ast})}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{(i_{\ast})}4\sqrt{i_{\ast}}C\max_{v\in S_{i_{\ast}}}\min_{v_{h}\in V_{h}}\|v-v_{h}\|^{2}_{V}

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda^{(i_{\ast})}4\sqrt{i_{\ast}}h^{2q},

where
S_{i_{\ast}}
is the unit sphere, with resepct to
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
, in the span of the first
i_{\ast}
-eigenfunctions. Thus, we calculate

\displaystyle C\lambda^{(i_{\ast})}4\sqrt{i_{\ast}}h^{2q}<k^{2}-\lambda^{(i_{\ast})}\Longleftrightarrow\quad h^{2q}<\frac{1}{C}\left(\frac{k^{2}-\lambda^{(i_{\ast})}}{4\sqrt{i_{\ast}}\lambda^{(i_{\ast})}}\right).

∎

The constant
C
in the previous corollary is in general not computable, because it involves a constant related to the elliptic regularity shift of the Laplace operator.

Remark 7
.

From the previous corollary, we see that as the wave number
k
gets closer to the eigenvalue
\lambda^{(i_{\ast})}
, the mesh size
h
has to be chosen smaller to ensure the quasi-optimality of the discretisation.
Similiar observations can be found in
[
34
,
32
]
.

4.2
Crouzeix-Raviart finite element discretisation

We here consider the non-conforming discretisation of (

\displaystyle V_{h}

Proposition 1

(Guaranteed lower bounds
[
22
]
)
.

Let us consider the discrete eigenvalue problem (
14
) with Crouzeix–Raviart elements.
We will here assume the numerical linear algebraic problem of computing the eigenvalues is solved exactly and that for a fixed
j\in\mathbb{N}

h\leq\frac{\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{j}}-1}{\kappa\sqrt{\lambda^{(j)}}},

(19)

where
h
is the mesh size of triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
\kappa
is a constant related to the Poincaré inequality on a single element, here taken to be
\kappa\leq 0.1932
.
Then the following bound must also hold

\underline{\lambda}_{h}^{(j)}\coloneqq\frac{\lambda_{h}^{(j)}}{1+\kappa^{2}\lambda_{h}^{(j)}h^{2}}\leq\lambda^{(j)},

(20)

Following the same idea presented in
[
22
]
, we can compute an upper bound for the eigenvalues by interpolating the eigenfunctions
e_{h}^{(j)}
on the
H^{1}
-conformingm
\mathcal{P}^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
space and computing the Rayleigh quotient of the interpolated function.
We will here denote such upper bound as
\overline{\lambda}_{h}^{(j)}
.
While at first the separation condition (
19
) might seem stringent, we can observe that we are only interested in the eigenvalues
\lambda^{(j)}
for which
\lambda^{(j)}<k^{2}
, hence the separation condition is verified if

h\leq\frac{\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{j}}-1}{\kappa}k^{-1}\sim\frac{\sqrt{1+(dk)^{-1}}-1}{\kappa}k^{-1}\sim k^{-\frac{3}{2}},

(21)

where we used Weyl’s inequality to obtain the last identity.
Therefore (
19
) is equivalent to ask that the mesh size decreases as
k^{-\frac{3}{2}}
, which is a reasonable assumption for the mesh size in the context of the Helmholtz equation,
[
8
]
.

5
Guaranteeing quasi-optimality via mesh refinement

In this section, we present a simple algorithm that, for a given coarse mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{(0)}
and a wave number
k^{2}
, constructs a mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
such that the finite element approximation of the Helmholtz problem is quasi-optimal.

Algorithm 1

Guaranteed mesh refinement

A triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{(0)}
of
\Omega
, a FEM degree
p
, a wave number
k

k^{2}\leq\lambda_{h}^{(i_{\ast})}
and
k^{2}\geq\lambda_{h}^{(i_{\ast}+1)}

\mathcal{T}_{h}\leftarrow\text{REFINE}(\mathcal{T}_{h}^{(0)})

\lambda_{h}^{(i_{\ast})}\leftarrow\text{ESTIMATE}(\mathcal{T}_{h},p)

while

k^{2}\leq\lambda_{h}^{(i_{\ast})}
and
k^{2}\geq\lambda_{h}^{(i_{\ast}+1)}

do

\mathcal{T}_{h}\leftarrow\text{REFINE}(\mathcal{T}_{h})

\lambda_{h}^{(i_{\ast})}\leftarrow\text{ESTIMATE}(\mathcal{T}_{h},p)

end

while

The algorithm is based on the condition
\lambda_{h}^{(i_{\ast})}<k^{2}<\lambda_{h}^{(i_{\ast}+1)}
derived in Thm.
5
.
It is clear from Algorithm
1
that the key ingredients of the scheme here proposed are the REFINE and ESTIMATE steps, here discussed in greater details.

REFINE
, The refinement strategy is a key ingredient in the proposed mesh refinement strategy. The goal is to refine the mesh until the condition
\lambda_{h}^{(i_{\ast})}<k^{2}<\lambda_{h}^{(i_{\ast}+1)}
is satisfied.
The simplest refinement strategy is the uniform refinement, where each element is divided into four equal sub-elements. This strategy is simple to implement and guarantees that the condition
\lambda_{h}^{(i_{\ast})}<k^{2}<\lambda_{h}^{(i_{\ast}+1)}
will be satisfied for sufficiently fine meshes.
Uniform mesh refinement is explored in Examples
11
and
12
.
However, uniform refinement is not optimal and can lead to a large number of elements.
Alternatively, one can use an adaptive mesh refinement strategy. In this paper we investigate a naive adaptive refinement strategy based on the error estimator
\eta
defined as

\eta=i_{\ast}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{i_{\ast}+\ell}\!\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\!\!\left(h^{2}_{K}\lVert\Delta e_{h}^{(i)}+\lambda_{h}^{(i)}e_{h}^{(i)}\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+\frac{h_{K}}{2}\lVert\nabla e_{h}^{(i)}\cdot\bm{n}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial K\backslash\partial\Omega)}^{2}\right),

(22)

where
\ell
is a small integer,
e_{h}^{(i)}
and
\lambda_{h}^{(i)}
are the
i
-th eigenfunction and eigenvalue,
h_{K}
is the diameter of the element
K
, and
\bm{n}
is the outward unit normal to
\partial K
.
This error estimator is essentially
Babuška–Rheinboldt estimator
, averaged over the first
i_{\ast}+\ell
eigenfunctions
[
20
]
.
More sophisticated adaptive mesh refinement strategies can be used, we refer the reader interested in this topic to
[
37
]
.
We explore the naive adaptive refinement strategy based on the error estimator introduced in (
22
) in Example
13
.

ESTIMATE
, The estimate of the eigenvalue
\lambda_{h}^{(i_{\ast})}
is critical, in fact while checking the condition
\lambda_{h}^{(i_{\ast})}<k^{2}<\lambda_{h}^{(i_{\ast}+1)}
is simple, at the same time we need to compute the correct index
i_{\ast}
.
The index
i_{\ast}
might be already known since a modal analysis has been conducted on the domain to ensure that the wave-number
k
is not a resonance frequency.

If we are using a Crouzeix-Raviart discretisation another option is to estimate the index
i_{\ast}
using the guaranteed lower bounds for the eigenvalues
\underline{\lambda}_{h}^{(j)}
.
For a fixed a maximum index
j_{\max}
, on the coarsest mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{(0)}
we compute the first
j_{\max}
eigenvalues.
We then compute the guaranteed lower bounds
\underline{\lambda}_{h}^{(j)}
for
j=1,\ldots,j_{\max}
and fix as guess for the index
i_{\ast}
the largest
j
such that
\underline{\lambda}_{h}^{(j+1)}\geq k^{2}
. We denote such index as
j_{\ast}
.
We can then proceed to adaptively refine the mesh using as indicator the error estimator
\eta
defined similarly to (
22
) but with
i_{\ast}
replaced by
j_{\ast}
.
Each time we refine the mesh we can update the index
j_{\ast}
by computing the guaranteed lower bounds for the eigenvalues on the new mesh and fixing as new index
j_{\ast}
the largest
j
such that
\underline{\lambda}_{h}^{(j+1)}\geq k^{2}
.
Using the guaranteed lower bounds for the eigenvalues we can ensure that
i_{\ast}
is correctly estimated. In fact if
k^{2}-\lambda_{h}^{(j_{\ast})}
is less than
\overline{\lambda}_{h}^{(j_{\ast})}-\underline{\lambda}_{h}^{(j_{\ast})}
we know the index
j_{\ast}
can not change anymore, hence we have correctly estimated
i_{\ast}
.

Remark 10
.

While we here focused on the use of the guaranteed lower bounds only for Crouzeix-Raviart discretisations, the same idea can be applied to
H^{1}
-conforming discretisations using the guaranteed lower bounds presented in
[
38
]
.

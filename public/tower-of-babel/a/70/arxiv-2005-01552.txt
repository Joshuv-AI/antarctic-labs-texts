Dibakar Dey and Pradip Majhi


Abstract




Almost Kenmotsu metric as quasi Yamabe soliton

Abstract:
In the present paper, we characterize a class of almost Kenmotsu manifolds admitting quasi Yamabe soliton. It is shown that if a
(k,\mu)^{\prime}
-almost Kenmotsu manifold admits a quasi Yamabe soliton
(g,V,\lambda,\alpha)
with
V
pointwise collinear with
\xi
, then (1)
V
is a constant multiple of
\xi
, (2)
V
is a strict infinitesimal contact transformation and (3)
(\pounds_{V}h^{\prime})X=0
for any vector field
X
. Finally an illustrative example is presented to support the result.

Mathematics Subject Classification 2010:
Primary 53D15; Secondary 35Q51.

Keywords:
Almost Kenmotsu manifolds, Yamabe soliton, Quasi Yamabe soliton, infinitesimal contact transformations.

1.
Introduction

In 1989, Hamilton
[
7
]
proposed the concept of Yamabe flow defined as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g}{\partial t}=-rg,

where
r
is the scalar curvature of the manifold. A Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is said to admit a Yamabe soliton
(g,V,\lambda)
if

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\pounds_{V}g=(r-\lambda)g,

(1.1)

where
\pounds_{V}
denotes the Lie derivative along
V
and
\lambda
is a constant. A Yamabe soliton is said to be shrinking, steady or expanding according as
\lambda<0
,
\lambda=0
or
\lambda>0
respectively. If
V
is a gradient of some smooth function
f:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, that is,
V=Df
, where
D
is the gradient operator, then the Yamabe soliton is said to be a gradient Yamabe soliton and then (
1.1
) reduces to

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}f=(r-\lambda)g,

(1.2)

where
\nabla^{2}f
is the Hessian of
f
.

Extending the notion of Yamabe soliton, Chen and Deshmukh
[
1
]
introduced the notion of quasi Yamabe soliton which can be defined on a Riemannian manifold as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\pounds_{V}g)(X,Y)=(r-\lambda)g(X,Y)+\alpha V^{\#}(X)V^{\#}(Y),

(1.3)

where
V^{\#}
is the dual 1-form of
V
,
\lambda
is a constant and
\alpha
is a smooth function. If
V
is a gradient of some smooth function
f
, then the above notion is called quasi Yamabe gradient soliton and then (
1.3
) can be written as

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}f=(r-\lambda)g+\alpha df\otimes df.

(1.4)

The quasi Yamabe soliton is said to be shrinking, steady or expanding according as
\lambda<0
,
\lambda=0
or
\lambda>0
respectively. In
[
8
]
, it is proved that the scalar curvature of a compact quasi Yamabe soliton is constant. In
[
10
]
, the author proved that a quasi Yamabe gradient soliton on a complete non-compact manifold has warped product structure. In
[
6
]
, Ghosh proved a similar result for a Kenmotsu manifold.
Since a
(k,-2)^{\prime}
-almost Kenmotsu manifold is locally isometric to a warped product space and the scalar curvature is also constant ( see Theorem 4.2 of
[
4
]
), the results proved in
[
8
]
and
[
6
]
are trivially holds in a
(k,-2)^{\prime}
-almost Kenmotsu manifolds admitting quasi Yamabe gradient soliton. This motivates us to consider only quasi Yamabe soliton on
(k,\mu)^{\prime}
-almost Kenmotsu manifolds.

The paper is organized as follows: In section 2, some preliminary results on almost Kenmotsu manifolds are presented. Section 3 deals with
(k,\mu)^{\prime}
-almost Kenmotsu manifolds admitting quasi Yamabe soliton. Section 4 is contains an example to support the main result.

2.
Preliminaries

A
(2n+1)
-dimensional almost contact metric manifold is a differentiable manifold
M
together with a structure
(\phi,\xi,\eta,g)
satisfying

\phi^{2}=-I+\eta\otimes\xi,\;\;\eta(\xi)=1,\;\;\phi\xi=0,\;\;\eta\circ\phi=0,

(2.1)

g(\phi X,\phi Y)=g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y),

(2.2)

for any vector fields
X
,
Y
on
M
, where
\phi
is a
(1,1)
-tensor field,
\xi
is a unit vector field,
\eta
is a 1-form,
g
is the Riemannian metric and
I
is the identity endomorphism. The fundamental 2-form
\Phi
on an almost contact metric manifold is defined by
\Phi(X,Y)=g(X,\phi Y)
for any
X
,
Y
on
M
. Almost contact metric manifold such that
\eta
is closed and
d\Phi=2\eta\wedge\Phi
are called almost Kenmotsu manifolds (see
[
4
]
,
[
9
]
).

Let
M
be a
(2n+1)
-dimensional almost Kenmotsu manifold. We denote by
h=\frac{1}{2}\pounds_{\xi}\phi
and
l=R(\cdot,\xi)\xi
on
M
. The tensor fields
l
and
h
are symmetric operators and satisfy the following relations
[
9
]
:

h\xi=0,\;l\xi=0,\;tr(h)=0,\;tr(h\phi)=0,\;h\phi+\phi h=0,

(2.3)

\nabla_{X}\xi=X-\eta(X)\xi-\phi hX(\Rightarrow\nabla_{\xi}\xi=0),

(2.4)

\phi l\phi-l=2(h^{2}-\phi^{2}),

(2.5)

R(X,Y)\xi=\eta(X)(Y-\phi hY)-\eta(Y)(X-\phi hX)+(\nabla_{Y}\phi h)X-(\nabla_{X}\phi h)Y,

(2.6)

for any vector fields
X,\;Y
on
M
. The
(1,1)
-type symmetric tensor field
h^{\prime}=h\circ\phi
is anti-commuting with
\phi
and
h^{\prime}\xi=0
. Also it is clear that (
[
4
]
,
[
12
]
)

\displaystyle h=0\Leftrightarrow h^{\prime}=0,\;\;h^{\prime 2}=(k+1)\phi^{2}(\Leftrightarrow h^{2}=(k+1)\phi^{2}).

(2.7)

In
[
4
]
, Dileo and Pastore introduced the notion of
(k,\mu)^{\prime}
-nullity distribution, on an almost Kenmotsu manifold
(M,\phi,\xi,\eta,g)
, which is defined for any
p\in M
and
k,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
as follows:

\displaystyle N_{p}(k,\mu)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{Z\in T_{p}(M):R(X,Y)Z=k[g(Y,Z)X-g(X,Z)Y]

(2.8)

\displaystyle+\mu[g(Y,Z)h^{\prime}X-g(X,Z)h^{\prime}Y]\}.

In
[
4
]
, it is proved that in a
(k,\mu)^{\prime}
-almost Kenmotsu manifold
M^{2n+1}
with
h^{\prime}\neq 0
,
k<-1,\;\mu=-2
and Spec
(h^{\prime})=\{0,\delta,-\delta\}
, with
0
as simple eigen value and
\delta=\sqrt{-k-1}
.

In
[
11
]
, Wang and Liu proved that for a
(2n+1)
-dimensional
(k,\mu)^{\prime}
-almost Kenmotsu manifold
M
with
h^{\prime}\neq 0
, the Ricci operator
Q
of
M
is given by

\displaystyle Q=-2nid+2n(k+1)\eta\otimes\xi-2nh^{\prime}.

(2.9)

Moreover, the scalar curvature of
M^{2n+1}
is
2n(k-2n)
. From (
2.7
), we have

\displaystyle R(X,Y)\xi=k[\eta(Y)X-\eta(X)Y]+\mu[\eta(Y)h^{\prime}X-\eta(X)h^{\prime}Y],

(2.10)

where

k,\mu\in\mathbb{R}.
Also we get from (
2.10
)

\displaystyle R(\xi,X)Y=k[g(X,Y)\xi-\eta(Y)X]+\mu[g(h^{\prime}X,Y)\xi-\eta(Y)h^{\prime}X].

(2.11)

Contracting
X
in (
2.10
), we have

\displaystyle S(Y,\xi)=2nk\eta(Y).

(2.12)

Using (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}\eta)Y=g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y)+g(h^{\prime}X,Y).

(2.13)

For further details on almost Kenmotsu manifolds, we refer the reader to go through the references (
[
2
]
-
[
4
]
,
[
9
]
).

3.
Quasi Yamabe soliton

In this section, we characterize
(k,\mu)^{\prime}
-almost Kenmotsu manifolds admitting quasi Yamabe soliton
(g,V,\lambda,\alpha)
such that
V
is pointwise collinear with the characteristic vector field
\xi
. In this regard, to prove our main theorem, we need the following definition:

Definition 3.1
.

A vector field
V
on an almost contact metric manifold
(M,\phi,\xi,\eta,g)
is said to be an infinitesimal contact transformation if
\pounds_{V}\eta=f\eta
for some smooth function
f
on
M
. In particular, if
f=0
, then
V
is said to be a strict infinitesimal contact transformation.

Theorem 3.2
.

If a
(2n+1)
-dimensional
(k,\mu)^{\prime}
-almost Kenmotsu manifold
M
with
h^{\prime}\neq 0
admits quasi Yamabe soliton
(g,V,\lambda,\alpha)
with the soliton vector field
V
pointwise collinear with
\xi
, then

(1)

V
is a constant multiple of
\xi
.

(2)

V
is a strict infinitesimal contact transformation.

(3)

(\pounds_{V}h^{\prime})X=0
for any vector field
X
on
M
.

Proof.

If
V
is pointwise collinear with
\xi
, then there exist a non-zero smooth function
b
on
M
such that
V=b\xi
. Then from (
1.3
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\pounds_{b\xi}g)(X,Y)=(r-\lambda)g(X,Y)+\alpha b^{2}\eta(X)\eta(Y).

(3.1)

Now,

\displaystyle(\pounds_{b\xi}g)(X,Y)=g(\nabla_{X}b\xi,Y)+g(\nabla_{Y}b\xi,X).

Using (
2.4
), the foregoing equation reduces to

\displaystyle(\pounds_{b\xi}g)(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(Xb)\eta(Y)+(Yb)\eta(X)

(3.2)

\displaystyle+2b[g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y)-g(\phi hX,Y)].

Let
\{e_{i}\}
be an orthonormal basis of the tangent space at each point of
M
. Now, substituting (
3.2
) in (
3.1
) and taking
X=Y=e_{i}
and summing over
i
, we obtain

\displaystyle(\xi b)=(r-\lambda)(2n+1)+\alpha b^{2}-2nb.

(3.3)

Again substituting (
3.2
) in (
3.1
) and then putting
X=Y=\xi
yields

\displaystyle(\xi b)=r-\lambda+\alpha b^{2}.

(3.4)

Equating (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle b=r-\lambda.

(3.5)

Since
\lambda
is constant and
r=2n(k-2n)
is constant,
b
is also a constant. This proves
(1)
.

Now, from (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle\alpha b^{2}=-(r-\lambda),

(3.6)

which implies
\alpha
is also a constant. Now, since
V=b\xi
, we write (
1.3
) as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\pounds_{V}g)(X,Y)=(r-\lambda)g(X,Y)+\alpha b^{2}\eta(X)\eta(Y).

(3.7)

Differentiating the above equation covariantly along any vector field
Z
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{Z}\pounds_{V}g)(X,Y)=\alpha b^{2}[\eta(Y)(\nabla_{Z}\eta)X+\eta(X)(\nabla_{Z}\eta)Y].

(3.8)

It is well known that (see
[
13
]
)

(\pounds_{V}\nabla_{X}g-\nabla_{X}\pounds_{V}g-\nabla_{[V,X]}g)(Y,Z)=-g((\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,Y),Z)-g((\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,Z),Y).

Since
\nabla g=0
, then the above relation becomes

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}\pounds_{V}g)(Y,Z)=g((\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,Y),Z)+g((\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,Z),Y).

(3.9)

Since
\pounds_{V}\nabla
is symmetric, then it follows from (
3.9
) that

\displaystyle g((\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,Y),Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{X}\pounds_{V}g)(Y,Z)+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{Y}\pounds_{V}g)(X,Z)

(3.10)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{Z}\pounds_{V}g)(X,Y).

Using (
2.13
) and (
3.8
) in (
3.10
) we have

\displaystyle g((\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,Y),Z)=2\alpha b^{2}[g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y)+g(h^{\prime}X,Y)]\eta(Z),

which implies

\displaystyle(\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,Y)=2\alpha b^{2}[g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y)+g(h^{\prime}X,Y)]\xi.

(3.11)

Substituting
Y=\xi
in (
3.11
), we get
(\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,\xi)=0
. From which we obtain
\nabla_{Y}(\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,\xi)=0
. This gives

\displaystyle(\nabla_{Y}\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,\xi)=-(\pounds_{V}\nabla)(\nabla_{Y}X,\xi)-(\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,\nabla_{Y}\xi).

Using
(\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,\xi)=0
, (
3.11
) and (
2.4
) in the foregoing equation, we infer that

\displaystyle(\nabla_{Y}\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\alpha b^{2}[g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y)

(3.12)

\displaystyle+2g(h^{\prime}X,Y)+g(h^{\prime 2}X,Y)]\xi.

Using the foregoing equation in the following formula (see
[
13
]
)

\displaystyle(\pounds_{V}R)(X,Y)Z=(\nabla_{X}\pounds_{V}\nabla)(Y,Z)-(\nabla_{Y}\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,Z),

we obtain

\displaystyle(\pounds_{V}R)(X,\xi)\xi=(\nabla_{X}\pounds_{V}\nabla)(\xi,\xi)-(\nabla_{\xi}\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,\xi)=0.

(3.13)

Now, substituting
Y=\xi
in (
3.7
) and using (
3.6
), we get
(\pounds_{V}g)(X,\xi)=0
, which implies

\displaystyle(\pounds_{V}\eta)X=g(X,\pounds_{V}\xi).

(3.14)

Since
V=b\xi
and
b
is a constant, then we can easily obtain
\pounds_{V}\xi=0
and hence, from (
3.14
), we have
(\pounds_{V}\eta)X=0
for any vector field
X
on
M
. Therefore,
V
is a strict infinitesimal contact transformation. This proves
(2)
.

From (
2.10
), we have

\displaystyle R(X,\xi)\xi=k(X-\eta(X)\xi)-2h^{\prime}X.

(3.15)

Now, using (
3.14
)-(
3.15
) and (
2.10
)-(
2.11
), we obtain

\displaystyle(\pounds_{V}R)(X,\xi)\xi=-2(\pounds_{V}h^{\prime})X.

(3.16)

Equating (
3.13
) and (
3.16
), we obtain

\displaystyle(\pounds_{V}h^{\prime})X=0,

(3.17)

for any vector field
X
on
M
. This proves
(3)
and the proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

From (
3.5
), we can see that
\lambda=r-b
. Since
r=2n(k-2n)<0
as
k<-1
, then
\lambda<0
when
b>0
,
\lambda=0
when
b=2n(k-2n)
and
\lambda>0
when
b<2n(k-2n)
. Hence, the quasi Yamabe soliton is shrinking, steady or expanding according as
b>0
,
b=2n(k-2n)
or
b<2n(k-2n)
respectively.

Remark 3.4
.

It is known that for smooth tensor field
T
,
\pounds_{X}T=0
if and only if
\phi_{t}
is a symmetric transformation for
T
, where
\{\phi_{t}:t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is the
1
-parameter group of diffeomorphisms corresponding to the vector filed
X
on a manifold
[
5
]
. Since
h^{\prime}
is a smooth tensor field of type
(1,1)
on
M
, then
(\pounds_{V}h^{\prime})X=0
if and only if
\psi_{t}
is a symmetric transformation for
h^{\prime}
, where
\{\psi_{t}:t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is the
1
-parameter group of diffeomorphisms corresponding to the vector filed
V
.

4.
Example of a shrinking quasi Yamabe soliton

In
[
4
]
, Dileo and Pastore presented an example of a
(2n+1)
-dimensional
(k,\mu)^{\prime}
-almost Kenmotsu manifold. In
[
3
]
, the authors studied this for
5
-dimensional case and obtained
k=-2
.

We now mention the necessary results from that example to verify our result.

[\xi,e_{2}]=-2e_{2},\;[\xi,e_{3}]=-2e_{3},\;[\xi,e_{4}]=0,\;[\xi,e_{5}]=0,

[e_{i},e_{j}]=0,
where
i,j=2,3,4,5.

The Riemannian metric is defined by

g(\xi,\xi)=g(e_{2},e_{2})=g(e_{3},e_{3})=g(e_{4},e_{4})=g(e_{5},e_{5})=1

and
g(\xi,e_{i})=g(e_{i},e_{j})=0
for
i\neq j
;
i,j=2,3,4,5.

The 1-form
\eta
is defined by
\eta(Z)=g(Z,\xi)
, for any
Z\in T(M)
.

Also,
h^{\prime}\xi=0,\;h^{\prime}e_{2}=e_{2},\;h^{\prime}e_{3}=e_{3},\;h^{\prime}e_{4}=-e_{4},\;h^{\prime}e_{5}=-e_{5}.

The scalar curvarure is
r=-80
.

Now, it is easy to see that

(\pounds_{\xi}g)(\xi,\xi)=(\pounds_{\xi}g)(e_{4},e_{4})=(\pounds_{\xi}g)(e_{5},e_{5})=0,

(\pounds_{\xi}g)(e_{2},e_{2})=(\pounds_{\xi}g)(e_{3},e_{3})=4.

Now, it can be easily verified from (
1.3
) that
(g,V,\lambda,\alpha)=(g,\xi,-41,121)
is a shrinking quasi Yamabe soliton, where
\lambda=-49<0
and
b=1>0
.

Also,
(\pounds_{\xi}\eta)X=0
and
(\pounds_{\xi}h^{\prime})X=0
for all
X\in\{e_{2},e_{3},e_{4},e_{5}\}
.

This verifies our theorem
3.2
.

Final state observability estimates and cost-uniform approximate null-controllability for bi-continuous semigroups
Karsten Kruse, Christian Seifert

Abstract
. We consider final state observability estimates for bi-continuous semigroups on Banach spaces, i.e. for every initial value, estimating the state at a final time T > 0 T>0 by taking into account the orbit of the initial value under the semigroup for t ∈ [ 0 , T ] t\in[0,T] , measured in a suitable norm. We state a sufficient criterion based on an uncertainty relation and a dissipation estimate and provide two examples of bi-continuous semigroups which share a final state observability estimate, namely the Gauß–Weierstraß semigroup and the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup on the space of bounded continuous functions. Moreover, we generalise the duality between cost-uniform approximate null-controllability and final state observability estimates to the setting of locally convex spaces for the case of bounded and continuous control functions, which seems to be new even for the case of Banach spaces.

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a Banach space and
(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
a semigroup on
X
, i.e.
S_{0}=I
and
S_{t+s}=S_{t}S_{s}
for all
t,s\geq 0
. Moreover, let
Y
be another Banach space and
C\in\mathcal{L}(X;Y)
, a so-called observation operator, and
T>0
. Then
(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisfies a
final state observability estimate
w.r.t. some (Banach) space
\mathcal{Z}
of functions on
[0,T]
with values in
Y
, if there exists
C_{\mathrm{obs}}\geq 0
such that

\left\lVert S_{T}x\right\rVert_{X}\leq C_{\mathrm{obs}}\left\lVert CS_{(\cdot)}x\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Z}}\qquad(x\in X).

Put differently, we can estimate the norm of the final state
S_{T}x
by just taking into account the observations
CS_{t}x
for
t\in[0,T]
. Typical applications stem from evolution equations on some function space over (a subset of)
\mathds{R}^{d}
, where
C
is a restriction operator to a suitable subset
\Omega
of
\mathds{R}^{d}
(or of the subset of
\mathds{R}^{d}
the functions are defined on) such that we want to control the final state on all of
\mathds{R}^{d}
by just measuring the evolution on the subset
\Omega
. Final state observability estimates have been studied in various contexts due to its relation to null-controllability, see e.g.
[
4
,
18
,
25
,
29
,
21
,
23
,
9
,
1
,
10
,
2
]
and references therein.

Classically, the space
\mathcal{Z}
is some
L_{r}
-space with
r\in[1,\infty]
(when working in Hilbert spaces, one usually chooses
r=2
), and then the final state observability estimate yields the form

\left\lVert S_{T}x\right\rVert_{X}\leq\begin{cases}C_{\mathrm{obs}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert CS_{t}x\right\rVert_{Y}^{r}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/r}&\text{if }r\in[1,\infty),\\
C_{\mathrm{obs}}\esssup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert CS_{t}x\right\rVert_{Y}&\text{if }r=\infty,\end{cases}\qquad(x\in X).

Clearly, in order to formulate this final state observability estimate (i.e. to have a well-defined right-hand side) we need some regularity of the semigroup. Indeed, we require measurability of
t\mapsto\left\lVert CS_{t}x\right\rVert_{Y}
for all
x\in X
. Of course, strong continuity of
(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
yields continuity of these maps and is therefore sufficient, but also weaker regularities are suitable. In
[
1
]
, dual semigroups of strongly continuous semigroups were considered which yield sufficient regularity.

In this short note we aim at two types of results. First, we consider final state observability estimates for so-called bi-continuous semigroups, see e.g.
[
16
,
17
]
, which are not strongly continuous for the norm-topology on
X
but only for a weaker topology. Note that dual semigroups are a special case of bi-continuous ones when considering the weak
∗
topology.
There are classical examples of bi-continuous semigroups such as the Gauß–Weierstraß semigroup on
C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})
, the space of bounded continuous functions (on
\mathds{R}^{d}
), as well as the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup on
C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})
.
Second, we relate cost-uniform approximate null-controllability of a control system sharing only weak continuity properties such as bi-continuity with a final state observability estimate for the dual system, thus generalising the well-known duality in Hilbert and Banach spaces
[
6
,
4
,
25
,
29
]
. Since this demands to work in Hausdorff locally convex spaces, we here focus on continuous control functions which results in the space
\mathcal{Z}
above being a space of vector measures.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
we review bi-continuous semigroups and then turn to final-state observability estimates in Section
3
together with two examples in Section
4
.
Final state observability estimates are then related with cost-uniform approximate null-controllability via duality, which we will exploit in our context in Section
5
.

2.
Bi-Continuous Semigroups

In this short section we recall some notation and definitions from the theory of bi-continuous semigroups that we need in
subsequent sections. For a vector space
X
over the field
\mathds{R}
or
\mathds{C}
with a Hausdorff locally convex topology
\tau_{X}

we denote by
(X,\tau_{X})^{\prime}
the topological linear dual space and just write
X^{\prime}:=(X,\tau_{X})^{\prime}

if
(X,\tau_{X})
is a Banach space.
We use the symbol
\mathcal{L}(X;Y):=\mathcal{L}((X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X});(Y,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{Y}))

for the space of continuous linear operators from a Banach space
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X})

to a Banach space
(Y,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{Y})
and denote by
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X;Y)}
the operator norm on

\mathcal{L}(X;Y)
. If
X=Y
, we set
\mathcal{L}(X):=\mathcal{L}(X;X)
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
5
, I.3.2 Definition]
)
.

Let
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X})
be a normed space and
\tau_{X}
a Hausdorff locally convex topology on
X
.

(a)

The triple
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X},\tau_{X})
is called a
Saks space
if
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X})
is a Banach space,

\tau_{X}\subseteq\tau_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X}}
and
(X,\tau_{X})^{\prime}
is norming for
X
where

\tau_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X}}
denotes the
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X}
-topology.

(b)

The
mixed topology

\gamma_{X}\coloneq\gamma(\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X},\tau_{X})
is
the finest linear topology on
X
that coincides with
\tau_{X}
on
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X}
-bounded sets and such that

\tau_{X}\subseteq\gamma_{X}\subseteq\tau_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X}}
.

The mixed topology is actually Hausdorff locally convex and the definition given above is equivalent to the one
from the literature
[
28
, Section 2.1]
by
[
28
, Lemmas 2.2.1, 2.2.2]
.

Definition 2.2
.

We call a Saks space
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X},\tau_{X})

sequentially complete
if
(X,\gamma_{X})
is sequentially complete.

Due to
[
28
, Corollary 2.3.2]
a Saks space
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X},\tau_{X})
is sequentially complete
if and only if
(X,\tau_{X})
is sequentially complete on
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X}
-bounded sets, i.e. every
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X}
-bounded

\tau_{X}
-Cauchy sequence converges in
X
. In combination with
[
13
, Remark 2.3 (c)]

this yields that a triple
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X},\tau_{X})
fulfils
[
17
, Assumptions 1]

if and only if it is a sequentially complete Saks space.

Definition 2.3

(
[
17
, Definition 3]
)
.

Let
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X},\tau_{X})
be a sequentially complete Saks space.
Let
(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in
\mathcal{L}(X)
be a semigroup on
X
. We say that
(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is

(locally)
\tau_{X}
-bi-continuous
if

(a)

it is exponentially bounded, i.e. there exist
M\geq 1
and
\omega\in\mathds{R}

such that
\left\lVert S_{t}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq M\mathrm{e}^{\omega t}
for all
t\geq 0
,

(b)

(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
(X,\tau_{X})
, i.e. for all
x\in X
the map
[0,\infty)\ni t\mapsto T_{t}x\in(X,\tau_{X})
is continuous,

(c)

it is (locally) bi-equicontinuous, i.e. for every
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
in
X
and
x\in X
with
\sup\limits_{n\in\mathds{N}}\left\lVert x_{n}\right\rVert_{X}<\infty
and
\tau_{X}\text{-}\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}x_{n}=x
we have

\tau_{X}\text{-}\lim_{n\to\infty}S_{t}(x_{n}-x)=0

(locally) uniformly for
t\in[0,\infty)
.

As in the case of
C_{0}
-semigroups on Banach spaces we can define generators for bi-continuous semigroups.

Definition 2.4

(
[
7
, Definition 1.2.6]
)
.

Let
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X},\tau_{X})
be a sequentially complete Saks space
and
(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
a locally
\tau_{X}
-bi-continuous semigroup on
X
. The
generator

(-A,D(A))

is defined by

\displaystyle D(A)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\Bigl\{x\in X\;|\;\tau_{X}\text{-}\lim_{t\to 0\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{${\scriptstyle{+}}$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle{+}$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{+}$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{+}$}}}}\frac{S_{t}x-x}{t}\;\text{exists in }X\;\text{and }\sup_{t\in(0,1]}\frac{\left\lVert S_{t}x-x\right\rVert_{X}}{t}<\infty\Bigr\},

\displaystyle-Ax\coloneqq

\displaystyle\tau_{X}\text{-}\lim_{t\to 0\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{${\scriptstyle{+}}$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle{+}$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{+}$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{+}$}}}}\frac{S_{t}x-x}{t}\quad(x\in D(A)).

Remark 2.5
.

There is no common agreement whether to use the here presented definition of a generator or its negative.
Throughout the entire paper we will stick to the definition made above, i.e.
-A
is the generator.

3.
Final State Observability Estimates for Bi-Continuous Semigroups

The final state observability estimate rests on the following abstract theorem. It provides a sufficient criterion stating that an abstract uncertainty principle (also called spectral inequality), see (
UP
), together with a dissipation estimate, see (
DISS
), yields a final state observability estimate, and has its roots in
[
18
]
, see also
[
21
,
23
,
9
,
1
]
.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
1
, Theorem A.1]
)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces,
C\in\mathcal{L}(X;Y)
,
(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
a semigroup on
X
,
M\geq 1
and
\omega\in\mathds{R}
such that
\left\lVert S_{t}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq M\mathrm{e}^{\omega t}
for all
t\geq 0
,
and assume that for all
x\in X
the map
t\mapsto\left\lVert CS_{t}x\right\rVert_{Y}
is measurable.
Further, let
\lambda^{*}\geq 0
,
(P_{\lambda})_{\lambda>\lambda^{*}}
in
\mathcal{L}(X)
,

r\in[1,\infty]
,
d_{0},d_{1},d_{3},\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3},T>0
with
\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}
, and
d_{2}\geq 1
, and assume that

(UP)

\forall x\in X\ \forall\lambda>\lambda^{*}\colon\quad\left\lVert P_{\lambda}x\right\rVert_{X}\leq d_{0}\mathrm{e}^{d_{1}\lambda^{\gamma_{1}}}\left\lVert CP_{\lambda}x\right\rVert_{Y}

and

(DISS)

\forall x\in X\ \forall\lambda>\lambda^{*}\ \forall t\in(0,T/2]\colon\quad\left\lVert(I-P_{\lambda})S_{t}x\right\rVert_{X}\leq d_{2}\mathrm{e}^{-d_{3}\lambda^{\gamma_{2}}t^{\gamma_{3}}}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{X}.

Then there exists
C_{\mathrm{obs}}\geq 0
such that for all
x\in X
we have

\left\lVert S_{T}x\right\rVert_{X}\leq\begin{cases}C_{\mathrm{obs}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert CS_{t}x\right\rVert_{Y}^{r}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/r}&\text{if }r\in[1,\infty),\\
C_{\mathrm{obs}}\esssup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert CS_{t}x\right\rVert_{Y}&\text{if }r=\infty.\end{cases}

Remark 3.2
.

The constant
C_{\mathrm{obs}}
is explicit in all parameters and of the form

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{obs}}=\frac{C_{1}}{T^{1/r}}\exp\left(\frac{C_{2}}{T^{\frac{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{3}}{\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}}}}+C_{3}T\right),

with
T^{1/r}=1
if
r=\infty
, and suitable constants
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}\geq 0
depending on the parameters; see
[
1
, Theorem A.1]
as well as
[
10
, Theorem 2.1]
.

In order to obtain a version of this theorem for bi-continuous semigroups, we need to argue on measurability of
[0,\infty)\ni t\mapsto\left\lVert CS_{t}x\right\rVert_{Y}
for all
x\in X
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X},\tau_{X})
be a sequentially complete Saks space,
(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
a locally
\tau_{X}
-bi-continuous semigroup on
X
,
(Y,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{Y},\tau_{Y})
a Saks space,
C\colon X\to Y
linear and sequentially
\tau_{X}
-
\tau_{Y}
-continuous
on
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X}
-bounded sets, and
x\in X
. Then
[0,\infty)\ni t\mapsto\left\lVert CS_{t}x\right\rVert_{Y}
is measurable.

Proof.

Since
[0,\infty)\ni t\mapsto\left\lvert\left\langle y^{\prime},CS_{t}x\right\rangle\right\rvert
is continuous for all
y^{\prime}\in(Y,\tau_{Y})^{\prime}

by the assumptions on
C
and the exponential boundedness of
(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, and

\left\lVert CS_{t}x\right\rVert_{Y}=\sup\{\left\lvert\left\langle y^{\prime},CS_{t}x\right\rangle\right\rvert\ |\ y^{\prime}\in(Y,\tau_{Y})^{\prime},\,\left\lVert y^{\prime}\right\rVert_{Y^{\prime}}\leq 1\}
for all
t\geq 0
, we obtain that

[0,\infty)\ni t\mapsto\left\lVert CS_{t}x\right\rVert_{Y}
is lower semi-continuous and hence measurable.
∎

In view of Lemma
3.3
, we can apply Theorem
3.1
to obtain the following.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X},\tau_{X})
be a sequentially complete Saks space,
(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
a locally
\tau_{X}
-bi-continuous semigroup on
X
,
(Y,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{Y},\tau_{Y})
a Saks space,
C\in\mathcal{L}(X;Y)
such that
C
is also sequentially
\tau_{X}
-
\tau_{Y}
-continuous
on
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X}
-bounded sets.
Further, let
\lambda^{*}\geq 0
,
(P_{\lambda})_{\lambda>\lambda^{*}}
a family in
\mathcal{L}(X)
,

r\in[1,\infty]
,
d_{0},d_{1},d_{3},\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3},T>0
with
\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}
, and
d_{2}\geq 1
, and assume that

(UP’)

\forall x\in X\ \forall\lambda>\lambda^{*}\colon\quad\left\lVert P_{\lambda}x\right\rVert_{X}\leq d_{0}\mathrm{e}^{d_{1}\lambda^{\gamma_{1}}}\left\lVert CP_{\lambda}x\right\rVert_{Y}

and

(DISS’)

\forall x\in X\ \forall\lambda>\lambda^{*}\ \forall t\in(0,T/2]\colon\quad\left\lVert(I-P_{\lambda})S_{t}x\right\rVert_{X}\leq d_{2}\mathrm{e}^{-d_{3}\lambda^{\gamma_{2}}t^{\gamma_{3}}}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{X}.

Then there exists
C_{\mathrm{obs}}\geq 0
such that for all
x\in X
we have

\left\lVert S_{T}x\right\rVert_{X}\leq\begin{cases}C_{\mathrm{obs}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert CS_{t}x\right\rVert_{Y}^{r}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/r}&\text{if }r\in[1,\infty),\\
C_{\mathrm{obs}}\esssup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert CS_{t}x\right\rVert_{Y}&\text{if }r=\infty.\end{cases}

Remark 3.5
.

The statements in Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.4
can be generalised in the sense that one can obtain an estimate with an
L_{r}
-norm of
t\mapsto\left\lVert CS_{t}x\right\rVert_{Y}
on a measurable subset
E\subseteq[0,T]
with positive Lebesgue measure; cf. e.g.
[
2
]
. However, in this case the constant
C_{\mathrm{obs}}
(cf. Remark
3.2
) is not explicit anymore.

4.
Two Examples of Bi-Continuous Semigroups

In this section we consider final state observability for two important examples: the Gauß–Weierstraß semigroup on
C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})
and the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup on
C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})
. We begin with the study of restriction operators on
C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})
, restricting functions to suitable subsets, and relate this to an abstract uncertainty principle.

4.1.
Restriction Operators on
C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})
and the Uncertainty Principle.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathds{R}^{d}
be non-empty,
C\colon C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})\to C_{b}(\Omega)
the restriction operator defined by
Cf\coloneqq f|_{\Omega}
for
f\in C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})
. Then
C\in\mathcal{L}(C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d});C_{b}(\Omega))
. Let
\tau_{\mathrm{co}}
be the compact-open topology on
C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})
(as well as on
C_{b}(\Omega)
).
Then
(C_{b}(\Omega),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty},\tau_{\mathrm{co}})
is a Saks space which is sequentially complete if
\Omega
is locally compact (in particular if
\Omega=\mathds{R}^{d}
).

Lemma 4.1
.

C\colon(C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d}),\tau_{\mathrm{co}})\to(C_{b}(\Omega),\tau_{\mathrm{co}})
is continuous.

Proof.

The map
C
is clearly linear. Due to
[
22
, Theorem 46.8]
the compact-open topology
\tau_{\mathrm{co}}

on
C_{b}(Z)
for a Hausdorff topological space
Z
is given by the system of seminorms

p_{K}^{Z}(f)\coloneqq\sup_{x\in K}|f(x)|\quad(f\in C_{b}(Z))

for compact
K\subseteq Z
. Let
K\subseteq\Omega
be compact in the relative topology.
Then
K
is compact in
\mathds{R}^{d}
as well and

p_{K}^{\Omega}(Cf)=\sup_{x\in K}|f|_{\Omega}(x)|=\sup_{x\in K}|f(x)|=p_{K}^{\mathds{R}^{d}}(f)\quad(f\in C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})),

which means that
C
is continuous.
∎

We now use the operator
C
to provide an uncertainty principle based on the well-known Logvinenko–Sereda theorem.
Let
\eta\in C_{c}[0,\infty)
,
\mathds{1}_{[0,1/2]}\leq\eta\leq\mathds{1}_{[0,1]}
.
For
\lambda>0
let
\chi_{\lambda}\colon\mathds{R}^{d}\to\mathds{R}
,
\chi_{\lambda}\coloneqq\eta(\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert/\lambda)
, and
P_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}(C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d}))
be defined by
P_{\lambda}f\coloneqq(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi_{\lambda})\ast f
, where
\mathcal{F}
denotes the Fourier transformation. By Young’s inequality and scaling properties of the Fourier transformation, we have

\left\lVert P_{\lambda}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi_{\lambda}\right\rVert_{L_{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})}=\left\lVert\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi_{1}\right\rVert_{L_{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})}\quad(\lambda>0).

Note that for all
f\in C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})
and
\lambda>0
we have
\mathcal{F}P_{\lambda}f=\chi_{\lambda}\mathcal{F}f
and therefore
\spt\mathcal{F}P_{\lambda}f\subseteq B[0,\lambda]\subseteq[-\lambda,\lambda]^{d}
, where
B[0,\lambda]\coloneqq\{x\in\mathds{R}^{d}\mid\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq\lambda\}
is the closed ball around
0
with radius
\lambda
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathds{R}^{d}
. Then
\Omega
is called
thick
if
\Omega
is measurable and there exist
L\in(0,\infty)^{d}
and
\rho\in(0,1]
such that

\lambda^{d}(\Omega\cap(x+(0,L)))\geq\rho\lambda^{d}((0,L))\quad(x\in\mathds{R}^{d}),

where
\lambda^{d}
denotes the
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure, and
(0,L)\coloneqq\prod_{j=1}^{d}(0,L_{j})
is the hypercube with sidelengths contained in
L
.

Thus, a measurable set
\Omega\subseteq\mathds{R}^{d}
is thick (with parameters
L
and
\rho
) provided the portion of
\Omega
in every hypercube with sidelengths contained in
L
is at least
\rho
.

By the Logvinenko–Sereda theorem (see
[
19
,
12
]
), if
\Omega\subseteq\mathds{R}^{d}
is a thick set, then there exist
d_{0},d_{1}>0
such that

(LS)

\left\lVert P_{\lambda}f\right\rVert_{C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})}\leq d_{0}\mathrm{e}^{d_{1}\lambda}\left\lVert CP_{\lambda}f\right\rVert_{C_{b}(\Omega)}\quad(\lambda>0,f\in C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})).

Thus, (
LS
) yields an estimate of the form (
UP’
).

4.2.
The Gauß–Weierstraß Semigroup on
C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})
.

Let
k\colon(0,\infty)\times\mathds{R}^{d}\to\mathds{R}
be given by

k_{t}(x)\coloneqq k(t,x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{d/2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{2}/(4t)}\quad(t>0,x\in\mathds{R}^{d}),

the so-called
Gauß–Weierstraß kernel
.
For
t\geq 0
we define
S_{t}\in\mathcal{L}(C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d}))
by

S_{t}f\coloneqq\begin{cases}f&t=0,\\
k_{t}*f&t>0.\end{cases}

Note that by Young’s inequality and the fact that
\left\lVert k_{t}\right\rVert_{L_{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})}=1
for all
t>0
we have
\left\lVert S_{t}f\right\rVert_{C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})}
for
f\in C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})
and
t\geq 0
. It is easy to see that
(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a semigroup, which is called the
Gauß–Weierstraß semigroup
.
Let
\tau_{\mathrm{co}}
be the compact-open topology on
C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})
. Then
(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is locally
\tau_{\mathrm{co}}
-bi-continuous;
see e.g.
[
17
, Examples 6 (a)]
.

For
\lambda>0
let
P_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}(C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d}))
be defined as in Subsection
4.1
.
By
[
1
, Proposition 3.2]
, there exist
d_{2}\geq 1
and
d_{3}>0
such that

(DISS(GW))

\left\lVert(I-P_{\lambda})S_{t}f\right\rVert_{C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})}\leq d_{2}\mathrm{e}^{-d_{3}\lambda^{2}t}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})}\quad(\lambda>0,t\geq 0,f\in C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})),

i.e. a dissipation estimate (
DISS’
) is fulfilled.

Thus, if
\Omega\subseteq\mathds{R}^{d}
is thick, then (
LS
) and (
DISS(GW)
) provide the estimates (
UP’
) and (
DISS’
) and so
Theorem
3.4
yields a final state observability estimate for the Gauß–Weierstraß semigroup on
C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})
.

4.3.
The Ornstein–Uhlenbeck Semigroup on
C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})
.

Let
M\colon(0,\infty)\times\mathds{R}^{d}\times\mathds{R}^{d}\to\mathds{R}
be given by

M_{t}(x,y)\coloneqq M(t,x,y)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\pi^{d/2}(1-\mathrm{e}^{-2t})^{d/2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\left\lvert y-\mathrm{e}^{-t}x\right\rvert^{2}/(1-\mathrm{e}^{-2t})}\quad(t>0,x,y\in\mathds{R}^{d}),

the so-called
Mehler kernel
.
For
t\geq 0
we define
S_{t}\in\mathcal{L}(C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d}))
by

S_{t}f\coloneqq\begin{cases}f&t=0,\\
\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}M_{t}(\cdot,y)f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y&t>0.\end{cases}

Since
\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}M_{t}(\cdot,y)\,\mathrm{d}y=1
for all
t>0
, we have
\left\lVert S_{t}f\right\rVert_{C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})}
for
f\in C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})
and
t\geq 0
. It is not difficult to see that
(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a semigroup, which is called the
Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup
. Let
\tau_{\mathrm{co}}
be the compact-open topology on
C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})
.
Then
(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is locally
\tau_{\mathrm{co}}
-bi-continuous on
C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})
; see e.g.
[
16
, Proposition 3.10]
.

Define
k\colon(0,1)\times\mathds{R}^{d}\to\mathds{R}
by

k_{s}(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\pi^{d/2}(1-s^{2})^{d/2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{2}/(1-s^{2})}.

Let
s\in(0,1)
. Then we obtain

M_{\ln\frac{1}{s}}(\tfrac{1}{s}x,y)=\frac{1}{\pi^{d/2}(1-s^{2})^{d/2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\left\lvert y-x\right\rvert^{2}/(1-s^{2})}=k_{s}(x-y)\quad(x,y\in\mathds{R}^{d}).

Hence,

\bigl(S_{\ln\frac{1}{s}}f\bigr)(\tfrac{1}{s}\,\cdot\,)=k_{s}\ast f\quad(f\in C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})).

For
\lambda>0
let
P_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}(C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d}))
as in Subsection
4.2
.
Since

\mathcal{F}k_{s}(\xi)=\mathrm{e}^{-(1-s^{2})\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}/4}\eqqcolon h_{s}(\xi)\quad(\xi\in\mathds{R}^{d}),

for
\lambda>0
and
s\in(0,1)
we conclude that

\bigl((I-P_{\lambda})S_{\ln\frac{1}{s}}f\bigr)(\tfrac{1}{s}\,\cdot\,)=(I-P_{\lambda/s})\bigl(S_{\ln\frac{1}{s}}f(\tfrac{1}{s}\,\cdot\,)\bigr)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\bigl((1-\chi_{\lambda/s})h_{s}\bigr)\ast f\quad(f\in C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})).

Lemma 4.3
.

There exist
d_{2}\geq 1
and
d_{3}>0
such that for
\lambda>0
and
s\in(0,1)
we have

\left\lVert\mathcal{F}^{-1}\bigl((1-\chi_{\lambda})h_{s}\bigr)\right\rVert_{L_{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})}\leq d_{2}\mathrm{e}^{-d_{3}\lambda^{2}(1-s^{2})}.

Proof.

Let
\lambda>0
,
s\in(0,1)
, and define

\sigma_{s,\lambda}\coloneqq(1-\chi_{\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\lambda})h_{s}\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-s^{2}}}\,\cdot\,\Bigr)=(1-\chi_{\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\lambda})\mathrm{e}^{-\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert^{2}/4}.

Then by a linear substitution we obtain

\left\lVert\mathcal{F}^{-1}\bigl((1-\chi_{\lambda})h_{s}\bigr)\right\rVert_{L_{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})}=\left\lVert\mathcal{F}^{-1}\sigma_{s,\lambda}\right\rVert_{L_{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})}.

Let
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{d}
,
\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert\leq d+1
. Then

\left\lvert\partial^{\alpha}\sigma_{s,\lambda}\right\rvert\leq\mathds{1}_{\{\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert\geq\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\lambda/2\}}\left\lvert\partial^{\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{-\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert^{2}/4}\right\rvert+\sum_{\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{d},\beta<\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}\left\lvert\partial^{\alpha-\beta}(1-\chi_{\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\lambda})\right\rvert\left\lvert\partial^{\beta}\mathrm{e}^{-\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert^{2}/4}\right\rvert.

There exists
K\geq 0
such that for all
\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{d}
with
\beta\leq\alpha
and all
\xi\in\mathds{R}^{d}
we have

\left\lvert\partial^{\beta}\mathrm{e}^{-\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert^{2}/4}(\xi)\right\rvert\leq K(1+\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert)^{\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert}\mathrm{e}^{-\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}/4}.

Let

C_{1}\coloneqq\sup_{\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{d},\beta\leq\alpha,\xi\in\mathds{R}^{d}}K(1+\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert)^{\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert}\mathrm{e}^{-\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}/16}.

Then, for
\beta\leq\alpha
and
\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert\geq\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\lambda/2
we have

\left\lvert\partial^{\beta}\mathrm{e}^{-\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert^{2}/4}(\xi)\right\rvert\leq C_{1}\mathrm{e}^{-\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}/16}\mathrm{e}^{-(1-s^{2})\lambda^{2}/32}.

Further, for
\beta<\alpha
and
\xi\in\mathds{R}^{d}
we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\partial^{\alpha-\beta}(1-\chi_{\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\lambda})(\xi)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq(\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\lambda)^{-\left\lvert\alpha-\beta\right\rvert}\left\lvert\partial^{\alpha-\beta}\chi_{1}\Bigl(\tfrac{\xi}{\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\lambda}\Bigr)\right\rvert\mathds{1}_{\{\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\lambda/2\leq\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert\leq\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\lambda\}}(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\lambda)^{-\left\lvert\alpha-\beta\right\rvert}\mathds{1}_{\{\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\lambda/2\leq\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert\leq\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\lambda\}}(\xi)

where
C_{2}\coloneqq\max_{\beta<\alpha}\left\lVert\partial^{\alpha-\beta}\chi_{1}\right\rVert_{C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})}
.
Hence, there exists
C\geq 0
(which is independent of
s
and
\lambda
) such that if
\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\lambda\geq 1
, then for all
\xi\in\mathds{R}^{d}
we have

\left\lvert\partial^{\alpha}\sigma_{s,\lambda}\right\rvert\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}/16}\mathrm{e}^{-(1-s^{2})\lambda^{2}/32}.

Therefore, increasing
C
, for all
x\in\mathds{R}^{d}
we obtain

(1)

\left\lvert x^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\sigma_{s,\lambda}(x)\right\rvert=\left\lvert\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\partial^{\alpha}\sigma_{s,\lambda})(x)\right\rvert\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-(1-s^{2})\lambda^{2}/32}.

By choosing
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
and
\alpha\coloneqq(d+1)e_{j}
for the
j
-th canonical unit vector
e_{j}
, we observe

\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}^{d+1}\left\lvert\mathcal{F}^{-1}\sigma_{s,\lambda}(x)\right\rvert\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-(1-s^{2})\lambda^{2}/32}
and hence

(2)

\left\lvert\mathcal{F}^{-1}\sigma_{s,\lambda}(x)\right\rvert\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-(1-s^{2})\lambda^{2}/32}\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{-d-1}

for all
x\in\mathds{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, where we increased
C
.

Therefore, if
\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\lambda\geq 1
, we can conclude by (
1
)
for
\alpha=0
and (
2
) that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{F}^{-1}\bigl((1-\chi_{\lambda})h_{s}\bigr)\right\rVert_{L_{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\mathcal{F}^{-1}\sigma_{s,\lambda}\right\rVert_{L_{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-(1-s^{2})\lambda^{2}/32}\Biggl(\;\int_{B[0,1]}1\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}\setminus B[0,1]}\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{-d-1}\,\mathrm{d}x\Biggr)

\displaystyle\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-(1-s^{2})\lambda^{2}/32},

where we increased
C
again.

It remains to prove the estimate for the case
\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\lambda<1
. Note that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\chi_{\lambda}h_{s})\right\rVert_{L_{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi_{\lambda}\ast\mathcal{F}^{-1}h_{s}\right\rVert_{L_{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi_{\lambda}\right\rVert_{L_{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})}\left\lVert k_{s}\right\rVert_{L_{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})}=\left\lVert\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi_{1}\right\rVert_{L_{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})},

where the last equality follows form scaling properties of the Fourier transformation and the fact that

k_{s}
is normalised in
L_{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})
.

Thus, for
\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\lambda<1
we obtain

\left\lVert\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\chi_{\lambda}h_{s})\right\rVert_{L_{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})}\leq\left\lVert\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi_{1}\right\rVert_{L_{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})}\mathrm{e}^{1/32}\mathrm{e}^{-(1-s^{2})\lambda^{2}/32},

which ends the proof.
∎

In view of Lemma
4.3
, we obtain the dissipation estimate (
DISS’
) as follows. Note that for
t\geq 0
we have
\mathrm{e}^{2t}-1\geq 2t
.
Let
t>0
and
\lambda>0
, and set
s\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{-t}\in(0,1)
. Then, for
f\in C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})
, Young’s inequality and Lemma
4.3
yield

\displaystyle\left\lVert(I-P_{\lambda})S_{t}f\right\rVert_{C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\bigl((I-P_{\lambda})S_{\ln\frac{1}{s}}f\bigr)(\tfrac{1}{s}\,\cdot\,)\right\rVert_{C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\mathcal{F}^{-1}\bigl((1-\chi_{\lambda/s})h_{s}\bigr)\right\rVert_{L_{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq d_{2}\mathrm{e}^{-d_{3}\lambda^{2}s^{-2}(1-s^{2})}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})}=d_{2}\mathrm{e}^{-d_{3}\lambda^{2}(\mathrm{e}^{2t}-1)}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})}

(DISS(OU))

\displaystyle\leq d_{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2d_{3}\lambda^{2}t}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})}.

Thus, if
\Omega\subseteq\mathds{R}^{d}
is thick and
C\colon C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})\to C_{b}(\Omega)
is the restriction map as in Subsection
4.2
, then (
LS
) and (
4.3
) provide the estimates (
UP’
) and (
DISS’
) and so Theorem
3.4
yields a final state observability estimate for the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup on
C_{b}(\mathds{R}^{d})
.

5.
Cost-Uniform Approximate Null-Controllability and Duality

In this section we want to show that cost-uniform approximate null-controllability is equivalent to final state
observability of the dual system, which is known in the setting of norm-strongly continuous semigroups;
see
[
6
,
4
,
25
,
29
]
.
In the bi-continuous setting this needs a bit of preparation so that we can formulate the corresponding
definitions. Since we work in Hausdorff locally convex spaces, the choice of the “correct” integral may be delicate. We therefore provide the duality for continuous control functions, and thus relate it to a final state observability estimate of the dual system w.r.t. a space of vector measures.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X},\tau_{X})
be a Saks space and
T>0
. We set

C_{\tau,b}([0,T];X)\coloneqq\{f\in C([0,T];(X,\tau_{X}))\ |\ \|f\|_{\infty}\coloneqq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert f(t)\right\rVert_{X}<\infty\}

where
C([0,T];(X,\tau_{X}))
is the space of continuous functions from
[0,T]
to
(X,\tau_{X})
.

Remark 5.2
.

Let
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X},\tau_{X})
be a Saks space and
T>0
.

(a)

Since the mixed topology
\gamma_{X}
coincides with

\tau_{X}
on
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X}
-bounded sets by
[
28
, Lemma 2.2.1]
, and a subset of
X
is
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X}
-bounded
if and only if it is
\gamma_{X}
-bounded by
[
28
, Corollary 2.4.1]
we have

C_{\tau,b}([0,T];X)=C_{b}([0,T];(X,\gamma_{X}))=C([0,T];(X,\gamma_{X})).

We define two topologies on this space. First, the one given by the norm

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty}\coloneqq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert f(t)\right\rVert_{X}\qquad(f\in C_{\tau,b}([0,T];X)).

Second, the Hausdorff locally convex topology
\gamma_{\infty}
induced by the directed system of seminorms given by

p_{\gamma_{\infty}}(f)\coloneqq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}p_{\gamma_{X}}(f(t))\qquad(f\in C_{\tau,b}([0,T];X))

for
p_{\gamma_{X}}\in\mathcal{P}_{\gamma_{X}}
where
\mathcal{P}_{\gamma_{X}}
is a directed system of seminorms that
induces the mixed topology
\gamma_{X}
. Clearly,
\gamma_{\infty}
is coarser than the
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty}
-topology.
Further,
(C_{\tau,b}([0,T];X),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty})
is a Banach space.

(b)

We note that a subset
B\subseteq C_{\tau,b}([0,T];X)
is
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty}
-bounded if and only if
it is
\gamma_{\infty}
-bounded since a subset of
X
is
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X}
-bounded if and only if it is

\gamma_{X}
-bounded by
[
28
, 2.4.1 Corollary]
. So
((C_{\tau,b}([0,T];X),\gamma_{\infty})^{\prime},\tau_{b})

is a topological subspace of
C_{\tau,b}([0,T];X)^{\prime}=((C_{\tau,b}([0,T];X),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty})^{\prime},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{C_{\tau,b}([0,T];X)^{\prime}})

where
\tau_{b}
denotes the topology of uniform convergence on
\gamma_{\infty}
-bounded sets.
In the following we use the notation

\left\lVert y^{\prime}\right\rVert_{(C_{\tau,b}([0,T];X),\gamma_{\infty})^{\prime}}\coloneqq\left\lVert y^{\prime}\right\rVert_{C_{\tau,b}([0,T];X)^{\prime}}
for all

y^{\prime}\in(C_{\tau,b}([0,T];X),\gamma_{\infty})^{\prime}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X},\tau_{X})
be a sequentially complete Saks space,
(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
a locally
\tau_{X}
-bi-continuous semigroup on
X
and
T>0
.
Let
v\in C_{\tau,b}([0,T];X)
and set

f\colon[0,T]\to X,\;f(t)\coloneqq S_{T-t}v(t).

Then
f\in C_{\tau,b}([0,T];X)
.

Proof.

We denote by
\mathcal{P}_{\tau_{X}}
a system of directed seminorms that generates the topology
\tau_{X}
on
X
.
Let
(t_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
be a sequence in
[0,T]
that converges to
t\in[0,T]

and set
x_{n}:=v(t_{n})-v(t)
for
n\in\mathds{N}
. The sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
is
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X}
-bounded and

\tau_{X}\text{-}\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}x_{n}=0
due to our assumptions on
v
.
We have for
p\in\mathcal{P}_{\tau_{X}}
that

\displaystyle p(f(t_{n})-f(t))

\displaystyle=p(S_{T-t_{n}}v(t_{n})-S_{T-t}v(t))

\displaystyle\leq p(S_{T-t_{n}}v(t_{n})-S_{{T}-t_{n}}v(t))+p(S_{T-t_{n}}v(t)-S_{T-t}v(t))

\displaystyle\leq p(S_{T-t_{n}}x_{n})+p((S_{T-t_{n}}-S_{T-t})v(t))

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{s\in[0,T]}p(S_{s}x_{n})+p((S_{T-t_{n}}-S_{T-t})v(t)).

Combining our estimate above with the local bi-equicontinuity and
\tau_{X}
-strong continuity of
the semigroup, we deduce that
(f(t_{n}))_{n\in\mathds{N}}
converges to
f(t)
in
(X,\tau_{X})
.
Hence,
f\in C([0,T];(X,\tau_{X}))
. Furthermore, as the semigroup is exponentially bounded,
there are
M\geq 1
and
\omega\in\mathds{R}
such that for all
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\left\lVert f(t)\right\rVert_{X}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert S_{T-t}v(t)\right\rVert_{X}\leq\left\lVert S_{T-t}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\left\lVert v(t)\right\rVert_{X}\leq M\mathrm{e}^{\omega(T-t)}\left\lVert v(t)\right\rVert_{X}

\displaystyle\leq M\mathrm{e}^{|\omega|T}\left\lVert v(t)\right\rVert_{X},

which yields that
f
is
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X}
-bounded on
[0,T]
because
v([0,T])
is
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X}
-bounded.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X},\tau_{X})
be a sequentially complete Saks space,

(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
a locally
\tau_{X}
-bi-continuous semigroup on
X
,
(U,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U},\tau_{U})
a Saks space,
and
B\in\mathcal{L}(U;X)
such that
B
is also
sequentially
\tau_{U}
-
\tau_{X}
-continuous on
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U}
-bounded sets.
Let
T>0
,
u\in C_{\tau,b}([0,T];U)
and set
f\colon[0,T]\to X
,
f(t)\coloneqq S_{T-t}Bu(t)
.
Then
f
is
\tau_{X}
-Pettis integrable and
\gamma_{X}
-Pettis integrable and both integrals coincide.

Proof.

The statement follows from
[
14
, 2.5 Proposition (a)]
and Proposition
5.3

because the map
v\colon t\mapsto Bu(t)
belongs to
C_{\tau,b}([0,T];X)
.
∎

Now, we have everything at hand to formulate the definition of cost-uniform approximate null-controllability
in the bi-continuous setting. Let
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X},\tau_{X})
be a sequentially complete Saks space,

(U,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U},\tau_{U})
a Saks space,
(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
a locally
\tau_{X}
-bi-continuous semigroup on
X
with generator
(-A,D(A))
,
and
B\in\mathcal{L}(U;X)
such that
B
is also sequentially
\tau_{U}
-
\tau_{X}
-continuous on
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U}
-bounded sets, and
T>0
.
We consider the linear control system

(ConSys)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dot{x}(t)&=-Ax(t)+Bu(t)\quad(t>0),\\
x(0)&=x_{0}\in X,\end{split}

where
u\in C_{\tau,b}([0,T];U)
. The function
x
is called
state function
and
u
is called
control function
.
The unique mild solution of (
ConSys
) is given by Duhamel’s formula

x(t)=S_{t}x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S_{t-r}Bu(r)\mathrm{d}r\qquad(t\in[0,T])

due to
[
17
, Proposition 11 (a)]
and Proposition
5.4
.
Let
\mathcal{P}_{\tau_{X}}
be a directed system of seminorms that induces the topology
\tau_{X}
.

Definition 5.5
.

We say that (
ConSys
) is
cost-uniform approximately
\tau_{X}
-null-controllable in time
T
via
C_{\tau,b}([0,T];U)

if there exists
C\geq 0
such that for all
x_{0}\in X
,
\varepsilon>0
and
p_{\tau_{X}}\in\mathcal{P}_{\tau_{X}}

there exists
u\in C_{\tau,b}([0,T];U)
with
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C\left\lVert x_{0}\right\rVert_{X}
such that
p_{\tau_{X}}(x(T))\leq\varepsilon
.

We note that this definition of cost-uniform approximate
\tau_{X}
-null-controllability does not depend
on the choice of
\mathcal{P}_{\tau_{X}}
.

Remark 5.6
.

We can analogously define the notion of cost-uniform approximate
\gamma_{X}
-null-controllability in time
T
via
C_{\tau,b}([0,T];U)
by using
p_{\gamma_{X}}\in\mathcal{P}_{\gamma_{X}}
instead of
p_{\tau_{X}}\in\mathcal{P}_{\tau_{X}}
. In view of Proposition
5.13
and Remark
5.14
these two notions are equivalent.

Next, we prepare the definition of final state observability of the dual system where we need to clarify which kind of
duality we have to use.

Definition 5.7
.

Let
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X},\tau_{X})
be a Saks space and
\mathds{K}=\mathds{R}
or
\mathds{C}
the scalar field of
X
.

(a)

We call
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X},\tau_{X})

C-sequential
if
(X,\gamma_{X})
is C-sequential, i.e. every convex sequentially
open subset of
(X,\gamma_{X})
is already open (see
[
24
, p. 273]
).

(b)

We call
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X},\tau_{X})
a
Mazur space
if
(X,\gamma_{X})
is a Mazur space, i.e.

X_{\gamma}^{\prime}\coloneqq(X,\gamma_{X})^{\prime}=\{x^{\prime}\colon X\to\mathds{K}\ |\ x^{\prime}\;\text{linear and }\gamma_{X}\text{-sequentially continuous}\}

(see
[
27
, p. 40]
).

Examples of C-sequential Saks spaces can be found in
[
13
, Example 2.4, Remarks 3.19, 3.20, Corollary 3.23]
.

Remark 5.8
.

Let
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X},\tau_{X})
be a Saks space.

(a)

If
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X},\tau_{X})
is C-sequential, then it is a Mazur space by
[
27
, Theorem 7.4]

(cf.
[
14
, 3.6 Proposition (b)]
).

(b)

y(t)&=B^{\circ}x(t)\quad(t\geq 0),\\
x(0)&=x_{0}\in X^{\circ}.\end{split}

Definition 5.16
.

We say that (
ObsSys
) satisfies a
final state observability estimate in
(C_{\tau,b}([0,T];U),\gamma_{\infty})^{\prime}

if there exists
C_{\mathrm{obs}}\geq 0
such that

\left\lVert S_{T}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\right\rVert_{X^{\circ}}\leq C_{\mathrm{obs}}\left\lVert{\mathcal{B}^{T}}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\right\rVert_{(C_{\tau,b}([0,T];U),\gamma_{\infty})^{\prime}}

for all
x^{\circ}\in X^{\circ}
.

We spend the remaining part of this section with proving that cost-uniform approximate
\tau_{X}
-null-controllability
in time
T
via
C_{\tau,b}([0,T];U)
of (
ConSys
) is
equivalent to a final state observability estimate of (
ObsSys
) in

(C_{\tau,b}([0,T];U),\gamma_{\infty})^{\prime}
, and that the latter space is actually a certain space of vector measures.

Let
\Omega
be a Hausdorff locally compact space,
(U,\vartheta_{U})
a Hausdorff locally convex space and

\mathcal{P}_{\vartheta_{U}}
a directed system of seminorms that induces
\vartheta_{U}
.
We denote by
\mathscr{B}(\Omega)
the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
\Omega
, by
M(\Omega)
the space of all bounded complex
(or real) Borel measures on
\Omega
, and by
M(\Omega;(U,\vartheta_{U})^{\prime})
the space of all finitely additive
vector measures
\nu\colon\mathscr{B}(\Omega)\to(U,\vartheta_{U})^{\prime}
, i.e.
\nu(N_{1}\cup N_{2})=\nu(N_{1})+\nu(N_{2})

for all disjoint
N_{1},N_{2}\in\mathscr{B}(\Omega)
, such that

(i)

\nu(\cdot)u\in M(\Omega)
for all
u\in U
, and

(ii)

there exist
p\in\mathcal{P}_{\vartheta_{U}}
and
C\geq 0
such that

\sup_{(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{U}_{p})}\bigl|\sum_{(N,u)\in(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{U}_{p})}\nu(N)u\bigr|\leq C

where the supremum is taken over all finite partitions
\mathcal{N}
of
\Omega
into disjoint Borel sets
and all finite sets
\mathcal{U}_{p}
in
U
such that
p(u)\leq 1
for all
u\in\mathcal{U}_{p}
.

Let
(U,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U},\tau_{U})
be a Saks space and
T>0
.
By
[
26
, Theorem 1]
, the compactness of
[0,T]
and Remark
5.2
(a) the map

\Theta_{\gamma}\colon M([0,T];U_{\gamma}^{\prime})\to(C_{\tau,b}([0,T];U),\gamma_{\infty})^{\prime},\;\Theta_{\gamma}(\nu)(u)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{T}u(t)\mathrm{d}\nu,

is a linear isomorphism. By the same theorem in combination with
[
26
, Lemma 4]
the map

\Theta_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}\colon M([0,T];U^{\prime})\to(C([0,T];(U,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U})),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty})^{\prime},\;\Theta_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}(\nu)(u)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{T}u(t)\mathrm{d}\nu,

is a topological isomorphism w.r.t. the semivariation norm on
M([0,T];U^{\prime})
and the dual norm on

(C([0,T];(U,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U})),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty})^{\prime}
and

\left\lVert\Theta_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}(\nu)\right\rVert_{(C([0,T];(U,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U})),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty})^{\prime}}=\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert_{var}\qquad(\nu\in M([0,T];U^{\prime}))

where the semivariation norm is given by

\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert_{var}\coloneqq\sup_{(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{U}_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U}})}\bigl|\sum_{(N,u)\in(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{U}_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U}})}\nu(N)u\bigr|\qquad(\nu\in M([0,T];U^{\prime}))

and the supremum is taken over all
(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{U}_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U}})
as in (ii) above
with
p
replaced by
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U}
.
We note that it follows from
U_{\gamma}^{\prime}
being a topological subspace of
U^{\prime}
(see
[
5
, I.1.18 Proposition]
)
and
\gamma_{U}
being coarser than the
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U}
-topology, that
M([0,T];U_{\gamma}^{\prime})
is a topological
subspace of
M([0,T];U^{\prime})
(if equipped with the relative topology).

Theorem 5.17
.

Let
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X},\tau_{X})
be a sequentially complete C-sequential Saks space,

(U,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U},\tau_{U})
a Saks space,

(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
a locally
\tau_{X}
-bi-continuous semigroup on
X

and
B\in\mathcal{L}(U;X)
such that
B
is also
\tau_{U}
-
\tau_{X}
-continuous on
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U}
-bounded sets.
For
T>0
we have
B^{\circ}S_{(\cdot)}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\odot\lambda\in M([0,T];U_{\gamma}^{\prime})
and

{\mathcal{B}^{T}}^{\circ}x^{\circ}=\Theta_{\gamma}(B^{\circ}S_{T-(\cdot)}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\odot\lambda)

for all
x^{\circ}\in X^{\circ}
as well as

\left\lVert{\mathcal{B}^{T}}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\right\rVert_{(C([0,T];(U,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U})),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty})^{\prime}}=\left\lVert B^{\circ}S_{(\cdot)}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\odot\lambda\right\rVert_{var}\qquad(x^{\circ}\in X^{\circ})

where

(B^{\circ}S_{(\cdot)}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\odot\lambda)(N)u\coloneqq\int_{N}\langle B^{\circ}S_{t}^{\circ}x^{\circ},u\rangle\mathrm{d}t\qquad(N\in\mathscr{B}([0,T]),\,u\in U)

for the Lebesgue measure
\lambda
.

Proof.

First, we recall that
X^{\circ}=X_{\gamma}^{\prime}
by Remark
5.8
(a)
as the C-sequential space
(X,\gamma_{X})
is Mazur.
Let
x^{\circ}\in X^{\circ}
.
Due to Proposition
5.15
(b) and Proposition
5.9

\mathcal{B}^{T}
is

\gamma_{\infty}
-
\gamma_{X}
-continuous and

\langle{\mathcal{B}^{T}}^{\circ}x^{\circ},u\rangle=\int_{0}^{T}\langle x^{\circ},S_{T-t}Bu(t)\rangle\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\langle B^{\circ}S_{T-t}^{\circ}x^{\circ},u(t)\rangle\mathrm{d}t

for all
u\in C_{\tau,b}([0,T];U)
.

For
N\in\mathscr{B}([0,T])
and
u\in U
we define
(B^{\circ}S_{(\cdot)}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\odot\lambda)(N)u\coloneqq\int_{N}\langle B^{\circ}S_{t}^{\circ}x^{\circ},u\rangle\mathrm{d}t
and show that
B^{\circ}S_{T-(\cdot)}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\odot\lambda\in M([0,T];U_{\gamma}^{\prime})
.
By the proof of Proposition
5.15
(b)
there are
C\geq 0
and
p_{\gamma_{U}}\in\mathcal{P}_{\gamma_{U}}
such that

|\langle B^{\circ}S_{T-t}^{\circ}x^{\circ},u\rangle|=|\langle x^{\circ},S_{T-t}Bu\rangle|\leq Cp_{\gamma_{U}}(u)

for all
t\in[0,T]
and all
u\in U
. In combination with the continuity of the map

t\mapsto\langle B^{\circ}S_{T-t}^{\circ}x^{\circ},u\rangle
on
[0,T]
by Proposition
5.9

and Proposition
5.11
this implies that

B^{\circ}S_{T-(\cdot)}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\odot\lambda\colon\mathscr{B}([0,T])\to U_{\gamma}^{\prime}
is a well-defined
finitely additive vector measure and that

(B^{\circ}S_{T-(\cdot)}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\odot\lambda)(\boldsymbol{\cdot})u=\int_{(\boldsymbol{\cdot})}\langle B^{\circ}S_{T-t}^{\circ}x^{\circ},u\rangle\mathrm{d}t\qquad(u\in U)

belongs to
M(\Omega)
. Let
\mathcal{N}
be a finite partition of
[0,T]
into disjoint Borel sets
and
\mathcal{U}_{p_{\gamma_{U}}}
a finite subset of
U
such that
p_{\gamma_{U}}(u)\leq 1
for all
u\in\mathcal{U}_{p_{\gamma_{U}}}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\bigl|\sum_{(N,u)\in(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{U}_{p_{\gamma_{U}}})}(B^{\circ}S_{T-(\cdot)}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\odot\lambda)(N)u\bigr|

\displaystyle=\bigl|\sum_{(N,u)\in(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{U}_{p_{\gamma_{U}}})}\int_{N}\langle B^{\circ}S_{T-t}^{\circ}x^{\circ},u\rangle\mathrm{d}t\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{(N,u)\in(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{U}_{p_{\gamma_{U}}})}\lambda(N)Cp_{\gamma_{U}}(u)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{N\in\mathcal{N}}\lambda(N)=C\lambda([0,T])=CT,

yielding
B^{\circ}S_{T-(\cdot)}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\odot\lambda\in M([0,T];U_{\gamma}^{\prime})
.
Analogously,
B^{\circ}S_{(\cdot)}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\odot\lambda\in M([0,T];U_{\gamma}^{\prime})
.

Finally, since

\displaystyle\langle{\mathcal{B}^{T}}^{\circ}x^{\circ},u\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\langle B^{\circ}S_{T-t}^{\circ}x^{\circ},u(t)\rangle\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}u(t)\mathrm{d}(B^{\circ}S_{T-(\cdot)}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\odot\lambda)

\displaystyle=\Theta_{\gamma}(B^{\circ}S_{T-(\cdot)}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\odot\lambda)(u)

for all
u\in C_{\tau,b}([0,T];U)
and

\langle{\mathcal{B}^{T}}^{\circ}x^{\circ},u\rangle=\Theta_{\gamma}(B^{\circ}S_{T-(\cdot)}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\odot\lambda)(u)=\Theta_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}(B^{\circ}S_{T-(\cdot)}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\odot\lambda)(u)

for all
u\in C([0,T];(U,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U}))
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\mathcal{B}^{T}}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\right\rVert_{(C([0,T];(U,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U})),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty})^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\Theta_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}(B^{\circ}S_{T-(\cdot)}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\odot\lambda)\right\rVert_{(C([0,T];(U,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U})),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty})^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert B^{\circ}S_{T-(\cdot)}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\odot\lambda\right\rVert_{var}.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a finite partition of
[0,T]
and
\mathcal{U}_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U}}
a finite set in
U
such that
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{U}\leq 1
for all
u\in\mathcal{U}_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U}}
. Then
T-\mathcal{N}\coloneqq\{T-N\;|\;N\in\mathcal{N}\}
is also a finite partition of
[0,T]
, and

\displaystyle\sum_{(N,u)\in(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{U}_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U}})}(B^{\circ}S_{T-(\cdot)}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\odot\lambda)(N)u

\displaystyle=\sum_{(N,u)\in(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{U}_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U}})}\int_{N}\langle B^{\circ}S_{T-t}^{\circ}x^{\circ},u\rangle\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\sum_{(T-N,u)\in(T-\mathcal{N},\mathcal{U}_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U}})}\;\int_{T-N}\langle B^{\circ}S_{t}^{\circ}x^{\circ},u\rangle\mathrm{d}t.

Thus, we obtain

\left\lVert{\mathcal{B}^{T}}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\right\rVert_{(C([0,T];(U,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U})),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty})^{\prime}}=\left\lVert B^{\circ}S_{T-(\cdot)}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\odot\lambda\right\rVert_{var}=\left\lVert B^{\circ}S_{(\cdot)}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\odot\lambda\right\rVert_{var}.\qed

Theorem 5.18
.

Let
(X,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X},\tau_{X})
be a sequentially complete C-sequential Saks space,

(U,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U},\tau_{U})
a Saks space,

(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
a locally
\tau_{X}
-bi-continuous semigroup on
X

and
B\in\mathcal{L}(U;X)
such that
B
is also
\tau_{U}
-
\tau_{X}
-continuous on
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U}
-bounded sets, and
T>0
.
Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(a)

The system in (
ConSys
) is cost-uniform approximately
\tau_{X}
-null-controllable
in time
T
via
C_{\tau,b}([0,T];U)
.

(b)

The system in (
ObsSys
) satisfies a final state observability estimate in
(C_{\tau,b}([0,T];U),\gamma_{\infty})^{\prime}
.

If additionally
\tau_{U}=\tau_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U}}
, then each of the preceding assertions is equivalent to:

(c)

There exists
C_{\mathrm{obs}}\geq 0
such that

\forall\;x^{\circ}\in X^{\circ}:\quad\left\lVert S_{T}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\right\rVert_{X^{\circ}}\leq C_{\mathrm{obs}}\left\lVert B^{\circ}S_{(\cdot)}^{\circ}x^{\circ}\odot\lambda\right\rVert_{var}.

Proof.

This statement follows from the equivalence of (a) and (c) in Proposition
5.13

with
V\coloneqq Z\coloneqq X
,
W\coloneqq C_{\tau,b}([0,T];U)
equipped with
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty}
,
F\coloneqq S_{T}
and
G\coloneqq\mathcal{B}^{T}

in combination with Theorem
5.17
, Remark
5.14
and
X^{\circ}=X_{\gamma}^{\prime}
.
∎

Even in the setting of Banach spaces, i.e.
\tau_{X}=\tau_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X}}
,
\tau_{U}=\tau_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U}}
,
where we have
C_{\tau,b}([0,T];U)=C([0,T];(U,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U}))
and

(C_{\tau,b}([0,T];U),\gamma_{\infty})^{\prime}=(C([0,T];(U,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U})),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty})^{\prime}=M([0,T];U^{\prime})

as well as
X^{\circ}=X^{\prime}
, the results of Theorem
5.18
seem to be new.

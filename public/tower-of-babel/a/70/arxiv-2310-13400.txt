Abstract




Malliavin differentiability of McKean-Vlasov SDEs with locally Lipschitz coefficients

Zac Wilde

University of Edinburgh, School of Mathematics, Edinburgh, UK

e-mail:
Z.J.Wilde@sms.ed.ac.uk

Abstract:

In this small note, we establish Malliavin differentiability of McKean-Vlasov Stochastic Differential Equations (MV-SDEs) with drifts satisfying both a locally Lipschitz and a one-sided Lipschitz assumption, and where the diffusion coefficient is assumed to be uniformly Lipschitz in its variables.

As a secondary contribution, we investigate how Malliavin differentiability transfers across to the interacting particle system associated with the McKean-Vlasov equation to its limiting equation. This final result requires both spatial and measure differentiability of the coefficients and doubles as a standalone result of independent interest since the study of Malliavin derivatives of weakly interacting particle systems seems novel to the literature.

Keywords:
McKean-Vlasov SDEs, Malliavin differentiability, superlinear growth, interacting particle systems

Introduction

The main object of our study are McKean-Vlasov Stochastic Differential Equations (MV-SDE) or mean-field equations or distribution-dependent SDEs, and differ from standard SDEs by means of the presence of the law of the solution process in the coefficients. Namely

\displaystyle dZ_{t}=b(t,Z_{t},\mu_{t})dt+\sigma(t,Z_{t},\mu_{t})dW_{t},\quad X_{0}=\xi,

denotes the law of process
Z
at time

[

]

[

[

]
, ergodicity
[

, large deviations
[

Many mean-field models exhibit drift dynamics that include superlinear growth and non-global Lipschitz growth, for example, mean-field models for neuronal activity (e.g. stochastic mean-field FitzHugh-Nagumo models or the network of Hodgkin-Huxley neurons)
[

,

,

appearing in biology or the physics of modelling batteries

]
,

. Quoting
[

, systems of weakly-interacting particles and their limiting processes, so-called McKean-Vlasov or mean-field equations appear in a wide variety of applications, ranging from plasma physics and galactic dynamics to mathematical biology, the social sciences, active media, dynamical density functional theory (DDFT) and machine learning. They can also be used in models for co-operative behavior, opinion formation, risk management, as well as in algorithms for global optimization.

Our 1st contribution: Malliavin differentiability of MV-SDEs under locally Lipschitz conditions.
We extend Malliavin variational results to McKean-Vlasov SDEs with locally Lipschitz drifts satisfying a so-called one-sided Lipschitz condition. The result is new to the best of our knowledge. Malliavin differentiability of MV-SDEs has been addressed in

Our 2nd contribution: transfer of Malliavin differentiability across the particle system limit

Another large aspect of McKean-Vlasov SDE theory, is the study of the large weakly-interacting particle systems and their particle limit that recovers the MV-SDE in the limit. This latter limit result is called Propagation of Chaos

From a methodological viewpoint, our point of attack is the

Organization of the paper.
In section 2 we set notation and review a few concepts necessary for the main constructions. In section 3 we prove Malliavin differentiability of MV-SDEs under superlinear drift growth assumptions and in section 4 we prove Malliavin differentiability of MV-SDEs under the weaker global Lipschitz assumptions via the convergence of interacting particle systems (we provide a lengthy remark about mollification in Wasserstein spaces).

2.2
Malliavin Calculus

Malliavin calculus is a collection of mathematical tools and techniques that extend the ideas of calculus of variations from deterministic functions to stochastic processes. In particular, it allows for the computation of derivatives of random variables.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
W:\mathcal{H}\to L^{2}(\Omega)
a Gaussian random variable. The space
W(\mathcal{H})
endowed with an inner product
\langle W(h_{1}),W(h_{2})\rangle=\mathbb{E}[W(h_{1})W(h_{2})]
is a Gaussian Hilbert space. Let
C_{p}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
be the space of all infinitely differentiable function which has all partial derivatives with polynomial growth. Let
\mathbb{S}
be the collection of random variables
F:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that for
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
f\in C_{p}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
and
h_{i}\in\mathcal{H}
can be written as
F=f(W(h_{1}),...,W(h_{n}))
. Then we define the derivative of
F
to be the
\mathcal{H}
-valued random variable

DF=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{i}}f(W(h_{1}),...,W(h_{n}))h_{i}

In the case of a stochastic integral,
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}([0,T])
and the Malliavin derivative takes the stochastic integral over a fixed interval to a stochastic process with parameter over the same fixed interval.

The Malliavin derivative from
L^{p}(\Omega)
into
L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})
is closable and the domain of the operator is defined to be
\mathbb{D}^{1,p}
, defined to be the closure of the of the set
\mathbb{S}
with respect to the norm

\|F\|_{1,p}=\Big[\mathbb{E}[|F|^{p}]+\mathbb{E}[\|DF\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}]\Big]^{\tfrac{1}{p}}.

We also define the directional Malliavin derivative
D^{h}F=\langle DF,h\rangle
for any choice of
h\in\mathcal{H}
. For more details, see
[
24
]
.

Malliavin differentiability under local Lipschitz assumptions

3.1
McKean-Vlasov Equations with locally Lipschitz coefficients

In this manuscript, we work with so-called McKean-Vlasov SDEs described by the following dynamics for
0\leq t\leq T<\infty
,

\displaystyle dZ_{t}=b(t,Z_{t},\mu_{t})dt+\sigma(t,Z_{t},\mu_{t})dW_{t},\quad Z_{0}=\xi,

(1)

where
\mu_{t}
denotes the law of the process
Z
at time
t

\mu_{t}=\mathbb{P}\circ Z_{t}^{-1}
(the usual pullback notation) and
W
is a
m

3.1.1
Assumptions

Assumption 3.1
.

Let
b:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\sigma:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}
be progressively measurable maps and
\exists L>0
such that:

1.

For some
p\geq 2
,
\xi\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{d})
is independent of the Brownian motion
W
.

2.

\sigma
is continuous in time and Lipschitz in space-measure
\forall t\in[0,T]
,
\forall x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\forall\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have

|\sigma(t,x,\mu)-\sigma(t,x^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})|\leq L\Big(|x-x^{\prime}|+W_{2}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\Big)

3.

b
is continuous in time and satisfies the
one-sided Lipschitz condition
in space and is Lipschitz in measure:
\forall t\in[0,T]
,
\forall x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\forall\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have that

\displaystyle\big\langle x-x^{\prime},b(t,x,\mu)-b(t,\omega,x^{\prime},\mu)\big\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}

\displaystyle\leq L|x-x^{\prime}|^{2},

\displaystyle|b(t,x,\mu)-b(t,x,\mu^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq LW_{2}(\mu,\mu^{\prime}).

4.

b
is Locally Lipschitz:
\forall t\in[0,T]
,
\forall\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\forall x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

we have that
\exists L_{N}>0
such that

|b(t,x,\mu)-b(t,x^{\prime},\mu)|\leq L_{N}|x-x^{\prime}|.

Observe that time continuity is a sufficient condition for integrability of
b
and
\sigma
on a compact time interval. The above assumption ensures existence, uniqueness and related stability while the following will be used to ensure the differentiability results.

Assumption 3.2
.

Let Assumption
3.1
hold.
For any
t\leq 0
,
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the maps
x\mapsto b(t,x,\mu)
and
x\mapsto\sigma(t,x,\mu)
are
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The derivative maps are jointly continuous in their variables.

3.1.2
Well-posedness and moment estimates

The first result establishes well-posedness, moment estimates and continuity in time for the solution of (

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|b(s,0,\delta_{0})|ds\bigg)^{p}\bigg]

4
Malliavin differentiability via the interacting particle system

The main goal of this section is to explore how much of

4.1
The interacting and non-interacting particle system

We introduce the interacting particle system (IPS) associated to McKean-Vlasov SDE (
1
). Consider the system of SDEs

\displaystyle dX^{i}_{t}=b(t,X^{i}_{t},\bar{\mu}^{N}_{t})dt+\sigma(t,X^{i}_{t},\bar{\mu}^{N}_{t})dW^{i}_{t},\quad X^{i}_{0}=\xi^{i},\quad i=1,\cdots,N,

\bar{\mu}^{N}_{t}(dy)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\delta_{X^{i}_{t}}(dy)

\{W^{i}\}_{i=1,\cdots,N}
are independent Brownian motions and
\{\xi^{i}\}_{i=1,\cdots,N}
are i.i.d. copies of
\xi
; the
\{W^{i},\xi^{i}\}_{i}
in (

W,\xi
in (

) (and in fact, live in different probability spaces). The dependence on the empirical distributions in the coefficients introduces non-linearity into the system in the form of self-interaction; hence we refer to the above set of equations as an

(IPS).

Since (
1
) and (

) live in different probability spaces, we construct an auxiliary
non-interacting particle system
(non-IPS) as living in the same probability space as (

\displaystyle dZ^{i}_{t}=b(t,Z^{i}_{t},\mu^{i}_{t})dt+\sigma(t,Z^{i}_{t},\mu^{i}_{t})dW^{i}_{t},\qquad Z^{i}_{0}=\xi^{i},\qquad i=1,\cdots,N,

(6)

\mu^{i}
is defined as the law of

are independent of each other and
\mu^{i}=\mu^{j}=\mu

\forall\,i,j=1,\cdots,N

denotes the law of the McKean-Vlasov SDE (

). In essence, (

N
copies of (

).
From direct inspection of (

for

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume (
6
) is well-posed.
Then, the cross-Malliavin derivatives of the solution
\{Z^{i}\}_{i=1,\cdots,N}
to (

) are all zero. That is,

\displaystyle D^{j}_{s}Z^{i}_{t}=0\quad\textrm{ for any }\quad j\neq i,1\leq i,j\leq N\quad s,t\in[0,T].

Proof.

One sees that
Z^{i}
acts independently of any other Brownian motion

4.1.3

To consider the calculus for the mean-field setting, one requires to build a suitable differentiation operator on

We consider a canonical lifting of the function

is Frechèt differentiable at some

We always denote

The leverage point of Definition

]
*Proposition 5.35 (p.399).

Proposition 4.6
.

Remark 4.10

(Mollification: Lifting Assumption
4.7
(2.); the measure differentiability requirement)
.

We do not carry out the analysis here, but by drawing on techniques of mollification (in Wasserstein spaces and in partnership with the localisation procedure of Remark
4.9

There are several ways to carry out mollification over the space of measures, with the main difficulty being that the Wasserstein space of measures is an infinite dimensional one.

The interacting particle system point of view allows us to benefit from a finite dimensional framework and draw from standard mollification arguments (in
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}
).
The most suitable regularisation method for our IPS approach has been shown in
[
9
]
(also
[
5
,
6
]
and the complementary
[
15
]
adding all the missing details of the proofs of
[
5
,
6
]
).
There, regularization is applied directly to the empirical projection map
u^{N}
(of Definition
4.3
and not
u
) via convolution with a smooth kernel. The method allows to control the derivatives of the mollified map by the Lipschitz constant.

Motivated by the study of Hamilton-Jacobi equations in infinite dimensional spaces, Lasry-Lions
[
22
]
propose inf-sup convolution (in Hilbert spaces) to show that bounded uniformly continuous scalar functions defined on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
can be uniformly approximated by functions belonging to the class of differentiable maps with Lipschitz derivatives. Critically, their methodology preserves, in the infinite dimensional space, certain good properties that other known mollifications at the time were not known to.

In
[
8
]

d

\mathbb{T}^{d}

based on the Fourier coefficients of the measure is introduced. Very roughly, the mollification is carried out by truncating higher-order coefficients of the Fourier expansion at a conveniently chosen threshold and combining with a Fejér kernel (see their Definition 3.13). It is currently open how to extend the analysis from
\mathbb{T}^{d}
to

In

]
*Section 3, the authors construct a novel mollification technique for measure functionals in

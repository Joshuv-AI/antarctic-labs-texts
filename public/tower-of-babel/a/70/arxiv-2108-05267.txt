Application of the matrix element method to Higgs boson pair production in the channel H ​ H → b ​ b ¯ ​ W ​ W ∗ \mathup{{{H}}}\mathup{{{H}}}\to\mathup{{{b}}}\mathup{{\overline{{b}}}}\mathup{{{W}}}\mathup{{{W}}}^{*} at the LHC
Karl Ehatäht, Christian Veelken

Abstract
We apply the matrix element method (MEM) to the search for non-resonant Higgs boson pair ( H ​ H \mathup{{{H}}}\mathup{{{H}}} ) production in the channel H ​ H → b ​ b ¯ ​ W ​ W ∗ \mathup{{{H}}}\mathup{{{H}}}\to\mathup{{{b}}}\mathup{{\overline{{b}}}}\mathup{{{W}}}\mathup{{{W}}}^{*} at the LHC and study the separation between the H ​ H \mathup{{{H}}}\mathup{{{H}}} signal and the large irreducible background, which arises from the production of top quark pairs ( t ​ t ¯ \mathup{{{t}}}\mathup{{\overline{{t}}}} ). Our study focuses on events containing two leptons (electrons or muons) in the final state. The separation between signal and background is studied for experimental conditions characteristic for the ATLAS and CMS experiments during LHC Run 2 2 , using the DELPHES fast-simulation package. We find that the t ​ t ¯ \mathup{{{t}}}\mathup{{\overline{{t}}}} background can be reduced to a level of 0.26 % 0.26\% for a signal efficiency of 35 % 35\% .

2
The Matrix element method

The MEM computes probability densities (PDs)
w_{i}(\bm{p})

for obtaining the measured observables
\bm{p}
, assuming that the event has been produced by the process
i
.
The PDs
w_{i}(\bm{p})
are interpreted as quantifying the compatibility of the measured observables
\bm{p}

with the signal (
i=0
) and background (
i=1
) hypothesis.
In the analysis of
\mathup{{{H}}}\mathup{{{H}}}
production in the decay channel

\mathup{{{H}}}\mathup{{{H}}}\to\mathup{{{b}}}\mathup{{\overline{{b}}}}\mathup{{{W}}}\mathup{{{W}}}^{\ast}\to\mathup{{{b}}}\mathup{{\overline{{b}}}}\,\ell^{+}\mathit{{\nu}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\ell^{-}\mathit{{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu{\nu}}}{}_{\mspace{-2mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{0mu}\scriptstyle{}}}

the observables
\bm{p}
refer to
the measured momenta of the two
\mathup{{{b}}}
-jets, of the two charged leptons, and of the measured missing transverse momentum (
\bm{p}_{\textrm{T}}^{\textrm{\kern 0.79727ptmiss}}
) in the event.
The vector
\bm{p}_{\textrm{T}}^{\textrm{\kern 0.79727ptmiss}}
represents the measured value of the vectorial sum of the two neutrino momenta in the plane transverse to the beam axis.
We use symbols with a hat to denote the true values of energies and momenta.
Bold letters denote vector quantities.
The vector
\bm{\hat{p}}
denotes the true values of the
\mathup{{{b}}}
-jet and charged lepton momenta and the true values of the momenta of the two neutrinos produced in the
\mathup{{{W}}}
boson decays.

As already mentioned,
\mathup{{{H}}}\mathup{{{H}}}\to\mathup{{{b}}}\mathup{{\overline{{b}}}}\mathup{{{W}}}\mathup{{{W}}}^{\ast}\to\mathup{{{b}}}\mathup{{\overline{{b}}}}\,\ell^{+}\mathit{{\nu}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\ell^{-}\mathit{{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu{\nu}}}{}_{\mspace{-2mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{0mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
signal events
contain the same number of
\mathup{{{b}}}
-jets, charged leptons, and neutrinos as the dominant background,
arising from
\mathup{{{t}}}\mathup{{\overline{{t}}}}\to\mathup{{{b}}}\mathup{{{W}}}\mathup{{\overline{{b}}}}\mathup{{{W}}}\to\mathup{{{b}}}\ell^{+}\mathit{{\nu}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\,\mathup{{\overline{{b}}}}\ell^{-}\mathit{{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu{\nu}}}{}_{\mspace{-2mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{0mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
.
The separation of the
\mathup{{{H}}}\mathup{{{H}}}
signal from the irreducible
\mathup{{{t}}}\mathup{{\overline{{t}}}}
background is based on the difference in event kinematics,
causing the PD
w_{0}(\bm{p})
to be in general higher when evaluated on signal events
and lower when evaluated on background events, and vice versa for the PD
w_{1}(\bm{p})
.
Given the PDs
w_{0}(\bm{p})
and
w_{1}(\bm{p})
for the signal and background hypotheses,
the Neyman-Pearson lemma
[
62
]
postulates that the likelihood ratio (LR):

P(\bm{p})=\frac{w_{0}(\bm{p})}{w_{0}(\bm{p})+w_{1}(\bm{p})}

(1)

provides the optimal separation of the
\mathup{{{H}}}\mathup{{{H}}}
signal from the irreducible
\mathup{{{t}}}\mathup{{\overline{{t}}}}
background.

Different nomenclatures and conventions for the MEM exist in the literature.
In this note, we follow the nomenclature and conventions introduced in Ref.
[
63
]
.
The PDs
w_{i}(\bm{p})
are given by the integral:

\displaystyle w_{i}(\bm{p})=

\displaystyle\frac{\Omega(\bm{p})}{\sigma_{i}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{i}}\,\int\,d\hat{x}_{a}\,d\hat{x}_{b}\,d\Phi_{n}\,\frac{f(\hat{x}_{a},Q)\,f(\hat{x}_{b},Q)}{2\,\hat{x}_{a}\,\hat{x}_{b}\,s}\,(2\pi)^{4}\,\delta\left(\hat{x}_{a}\,\hat{E}_{a}+\hat{x}_{b}\,\hat{E}_{b}-\sum_{k}^{n}\hat{E}_{(k)}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\,\delta^{3}\left(\hat{x}_{a}\,\bm{\hat{p}}^{a}+\hat{x}_{b}\,\bm{\hat{p}}^{b}-\sum_{k}^{n}\bm{\hat{p}}^{(k)}\right)\,|\mathcal{M}_{i}(\bm{\hat{p}})|^{2}\,W(\bm{p}|\bm{\hat{p}})\,\epsilon_{i}(\bm{\hat{p}})\,.

(2)

The symbol
|\mathcal{M}_{i}(\bm{\hat{p}})|^{2}
denotes the squared modulus of the matrix element (ME),
averaged over helicity states,
for either the signal (
i=0
) or for the background (
i=1
) hypothesis.
We use ME generated at LO accuracy with the program
MadGraph_aMCatNLO

2.3.3

[
64
]
for the signal as well as for the background hypothesis.
The ME for the signal hypothesis is generated using the infinite top quark mass approximation
[
65
]
.
In this approximation, the top quark degrees of freedom are integrated out,
replacing the top quark loops in the triangle and box diagrams shown in Fig.
1
by point-like effective couplings of, respectively,
two gluons to one
\mathup{{{H}}}
boson and two gluons to two
\mathup{{{H}}}
bosons.
The point-like effective couplings are specified via a Universal
FeynRules
Output (UFO) model file
[
66
,
67
]
given to
MadGraph_aMCatNLO
.
The usage of the infinite top quark mass approximation is necessary, because at present the program
MadGraph_aMCatNLO
does not support the generation of ME code for processes involving loops.

The symbols
\hat{E}_{a}
and
\hat{E}_{b}
(
\bm{\hat{p}}^{a}
and
\bm{\hat{p}}^{b}
) denote the energies (momenta) of the two colliding protons,

\sqrt{s}
their center-of-mass energy,

\hat{x}_{a}
and
\hat{x}_{b}
the Bjorken scaling variables
[
68
]
,
and

and

the corresponding parton distribution functions (PDFs)
[
69
]
.
Besides their dependence on the Bjorken scaling variables,
the PDFs depend on a scale parameter

We use the MSTW 2008 LO PDF set
[
70
]
to evaluate

and
f(\hat{x}_{b},Q)

and set the scale

to half the mass of the
\mathup{{{H}}}\mathup{{{H}}}
system and to twice the value of the top quark mass
when computing the PD

and

for the signal and background hypothesis, respectively.
We denote by

the number of particles in the final state,
and by
\bm{p}^{(k)}
(
\bm{\hat{p}}^{(k)}
) the measured (true) momentum of the
k
-th final state particle.
The
\delta

\delta(\hat{x}_{a}\,\hat{E}_{a}+\hat{x}_{b}\,\hat{E}_{b}-\sum_{k}^{n}\hat{E}_{(k)})

and
\delta^{3}(\hat{x}_{a}\,\bm{\hat{p}}^{a}+\hat{x}_{b}\,\bm{\hat{p}}^{b}-\sum_{k}^{n}\bm{\hat{p}}^{(k)})

impose conservation of energy and momentum.

The functions
W(\bm{p}|\bm{\hat{p}})
are referred to as “transfer functions” (TF) in the literature.
They represent the PD to observe the measured values
\bm{p}
, given the true values
\bm{\hat{p}}
.
The function
\Omega(\bm{p})
is referred to as “indicator function” in the literature
[
71
,
63
]
.
It attains the value
1
in case the event represented by the measured observables
\bm{p}
passes the event selection criteria and otherwise attains the value
0
.
The efficiency for an event originating at the phase-space (PS) point

\bm{\hat{p}}
to pass the event selection, i.e. to end up with measured
observables
\bm{p}
for which
\Omega(\bm{p})=1
,
is denoted by
\epsilon_{i}(\bm{\hat{p}})
.
Finally, the symbol
\mathcal{A}_{i}
denotes the acceptance of the event selection,
that is, the percentage of events which pass the event selection criteria,
while
\sigma_{i}
denotes the cross section of process
i
.
The subscript
i
of the symbols
\sigma_{i}
,
\mathcal{A}_{i}
, and
\epsilon_{i}

emphasize that the cross section, acceptance, and efficiency differ between the signal and the background hypothesis.
Division of the right-hand-side (RHS) of Eq. (
2
) by the product
\sigma_{i}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{i}

ensures that
w_{i}(\bm{p})
has the correct normalization required for a probability density,
i.e.
\int\,d\bm{p}\,w_{i}(\bm{p})=1
,
provided that the TF satisfy the normalization condition

\int\,d\bm{p}\,\Omega(\bm{p})\,W(\bm{p}|\bm{\hat{p}})=1

for every
\bm{\hat{p}}
.

The symbol
d\Phi_{n}=\prod_{k}^{n}\,\frac{d^{3}\bm{\hat{p}}^{(k)}}{(2\pi)^{3}\,2\hat{E}_{(k)}}

represents the differential
n
-particle PS element.
For the
\mathup{{{H}}}\mathup{{{H}}}
signal
as well as for the
\mathup{{{t}}}\mathup{{\overline{{t}}}}
background hypothesis,
n=6
.
We express the PS element
d\Phi_{6}
in terms of the energies
\hat{E}_{(k)}
, the polar angles
\hat{\theta}_{(k)}
, and the azimuthal angles
\hat{\phi}_{(k)}

of the two
\mathup{{{b}}}

\displaystyle d\Phi_{6}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{k}^{6}\,\frac{d^{3}\bm{\hat{p}}^{(k)}}{(2\,\pi)^{3}\,2\,\hat{E}_{(k)}}=\frac{1}{2^{24}\,\pi^{18}}\,\prod_{k}^{6}\,\frac{d^{3}\bm{\hat{p}}^{(k)}}{\hat{E}_{(k)}}

(3)

\displaystyle=

5.1
Relations for transfer functions

We assume that the directions of electrons, muons, and
\mathup{{{b}}}
-jets
as well as the energies of electrons and muons are measured with negligible experimental resolution.
Our assumption implies that the TF for electrons and muons is given by:

W_{\ell}(\bm{p}|\bm{\hat{p}})=f(E,\theta,\phi)\,\delta(E-\hat{E})\cdot\delta(\theta-\hat{\theta})\cdot\delta(\phi-\hat{\phi})\,,

(18)

while the TF for
\mathup{{{b}}}
-jets is given by:

W(\bm{p}|\bm{\hat{p}})=f(E,\theta,\phi)\,W(E|\hat{E})\cdot\delta(\theta-\hat{\theta})\cdot\delta(\phi-\hat{\phi})\,,

(19)

where
E
denotes the energy,
\theta
the polar angle, and
\phi
the azimuthal angle of the electron, muon, or
\mathup{{{b}}}
-jet.
The function
W(E|\hat{E})
quantifies the experimental resolution with which the energy of
\mathup{{{b}}}
-jets is measured.
We choose the function
W(E|\hat{E})
such that it satisfies the following normalization condition:

\int\,dE\,W(E|\hat{E})\equiv 1.

The function
f(E,\theta,\phi)
ensures that the TF satisfy the normalization condition

\int\,d^{3}\bm{p}\,\Omega(\bm{p})\,W(\bm{p}|\bm{\hat{p}})=1\,.

We only consider those events, which pass the event selection criteria, i.e. for which
\Omega(\bm{p})
is equal to one.
With
d^{3}\bm{p}=\beta\,E^{2}\,\sin\theta\,dE\,d\theta\,d\phi
, it follows that:

1\equiv\int\,dE\,d\theta\,d\phi\,\beta\,E^{2}\,\sin\theta\,f(E,\theta,\phi)\,W(E|\hat{E})\,\delta(\theta-\hat{\theta})\cdot\delta(\phi-\hat{\phi})\,,

which implies:

f(E,\theta,\phi)=\frac{1}{\beta\,E^{2}\,\sin\theta}\,.

(20)

Eq. (
20
) holds for electrons and muons as well as for
\mathup{{{b}}}
-jets.

As explained in Section

-functions into the integrand on the RHS of Eq. (

In order to avoid that the insertion of the

, which we write as:

\displaystyle 1\equiv\textrm{BW}\,\cdot\,\textrm{BW}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{m_{\textrm{X}}\,\Gamma_{\textrm{X}}}\,\delta(E_{\textrm{X}}^{2}-|\bm{p}_{\textrm{X}}|^{2}-m_{\textrm{X}}^{2})\cdot\left((E_{\textrm{X}}^{2}-|\bm{p}_{\textrm{X}}|^{2}-m_{\textrm{X}}^{2})^{2}+(m_{\textrm{X}}\,\Gamma_{\textrm{X}})^{2}\right)

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi\,m_{\textrm{X}}\,\Gamma_{\textrm{X}}\,\delta(E_{\textrm{X}}^{2}-|\bm{p}_{\textrm{X}}|^{2}-m_{\textrm{X}}^{2})\,,

where we have used the narrow-width approximation to replace the first
BW

\delta

The symbol

in Eq. (

m_{\textrm{X}}
and width
\Gamma_{\textrm{X}}

)

\delta\left(g(x)\right)=\frac{1}{|g^{\prime}(x_{0})|}\,\delta(x-x_{0})

(22)

yields a factor of

which we account for when eliminating the integration over

denotes the root of

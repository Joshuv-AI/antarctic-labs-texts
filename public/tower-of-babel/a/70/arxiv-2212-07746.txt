Remarks on rigid irreducible meromorphic connections on the projective line
Claude Sabbah

Abstract
. We illustrate the Arinkin-Deligne-Katz algorithm for rigid irreducible meromorphic bundles with connection on the projective line by giving motivicity consequences similar to those given by Katz for rigid local systems [ Kat96 ] .

1.
Introduction

Let
k:U\mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}}\mathbb{P}^{1}
be the inclusion of a proper Zariski open subset of the complex projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and let
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
be an irreducible algebraic bundle
1
1

1

In the following, bundle means algebraic vector bundle.
of rank
r
with connection on
U
. We say that
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
is
rigid
if any other
(\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\nabla)
on
U
having at each puncture
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\smallsetminus U
a formal structure isomorphic to that of
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
satisfies
(\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\nabla)\simeq(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
. It is proved (
[
Kat96
,
BE04
]
) that, on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, this property is equivalent to
cohomological rigidity
, i.e., the
rigidity index

{\mathrm{rig}}(V,\nabla):=2-h^{1}_{{\mathrm{dR}}}(\mathbb{P}^{1},k_{{\dagger}+}\mathcal{E}nd(V,\nabla))

is equal to
2
, where
k_{{\dagger}+}
denotes the minimal extension in the sense of
\mathcal{D}
-modules.

The Arinkin-Deligne-Katz algorithm
[
Ari10
]
, which relies on the property that the rigidity index is preserved by Fourier transformation (
[
BE04
]
) provides an inductive way of checking whether a given irreducible
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
is rigid by means of successive specific transformations:
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
is rigid if and only if the sequence of transformations in the algorithm reaches the trivial rank-one bundle with connection (
\mathcal{O}_{U^{\prime}},{\mathrm{d}})
on some open subset
U^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
. On the other hand, there is a one-to-one correspondence
between
irreducible bundles with connection
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
on some Zariski open subset of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and irreducible holonomic
\mathcal{D}
-modules
\mathcal{M}
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
by the inverse functors “middle extension” and “restriction to a suitable Zariski open set”, and the algorithm works with the latter objects.

For
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we say that a bundle with connection
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
(or its middle extension
\mathcal{M}
to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
) is
N
-quasi-unipotent
if the eigenvalues of the formal monodromy at each
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\smallsetminus U
belong to
\mu_{N}
(
N
-th roots of the unity).

The results of this note concern quasi-unipotent rigid irreducible bundles with connection on some proper Zariski open subset
U\varsubsetneq\mathbb{P}^{1}
. They consist of applications of the Arinkin-Deligne-Katz algorithm. A first application has already been given in
[
Sab18
]
, where it is shown that any rigid irreducible
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
on
U
(without the condition of quasi-unipotency) underlies an irregular mixed Hodge module structure which is pure (of weight zero, say).

The motivation for this question came from various recent talks by Michael Groechenig, Aaron Landesman and Daniel Litt on their respective works
[
EG18
]
and
[
LL22
]
. Of course, the technique used here on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
does not extend to higher dimensions, but it opens the way to questions in higher dimensions in the setting of irregular singularities. In another direction, the finiteness result of Haoyu Hu and Jean-Baptiste Teyssier
[
HT22
]
looks promising.

Rank one

Any rank-one bundle with connection
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
on
U
is isomorphic to one of the form
(\mathcal{O}_{U},\nabla^{\mathrm{reg}}+{\mathrm{d}}\varphi)
, where
\nabla^{\mathrm{reg}}
is a connection having regular singularities on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\smallsetminus U
and
\varphi
is a regular function on
U
. It is clearly irreducible and is cohomologically rigid (because
\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{V},\nabla)=(\mathcal{O}_{U},{\mathrm{d}})
). That it is rigid is seen as follows (a special case of the criterion mentioned above): if
(\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime},\nabla):=(\mathcal{V},\nabla)^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\otimes(\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\nabla)
is a rank-one local system which has regular singularity at each
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\smallsetminus U
and has trivial local monodromy there, it extends to a trivial bundle with connection on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

The bundle with connection
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
is quasi-unipotent if and only if
(\mathcal{O}_{U},\nabla^{\mathrm{reg}})
is so, and this amounts to the property that a suitable tensor power
(\mathcal{O}_{U},\nabla^{\mathrm{reg}})^{\otimes N}
is isomorphic to
(\mathcal{O}_{U},{\mathrm{d}})
.

Finiteness

Definition 1.1
(Minimally ramified polar part).

Let
p
be an integer
\geqslant 1
and let
\varphi\in\mathbb{C}(\!(t^{1/p})\!)/\mathbb{C}[\![t^{1/p}]\!]
be a nonzero ramified polar part of ramification order
p
. We say that
\varphi
is
minimally ramified
if it is not the pullback of a ramified polar part in
\mathbb{C}(\!(t^{1/p^{\prime}})\!)/\mathbb{C}[\![t^{1/p^{\prime}}]\!]
with
p^{\prime}<p
.

If
\varphi
is a minimally ramified polar part, it yields a
p
-dimensional
\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
-vector space with connection, that we denote by
\El(\varphi)
, obtained as the pushforward by
t^{1/p}\mathchoice{\longmapsto}{\mapsto}{\mapsto}{\mapsto}t
of
(\mathbb{C}(\!(t^{1/p})\!),{\mathrm{d}}+{\mathrm{d}}\varphi)
. By the Levelt-Turrittin theorem, any finite-dimensional
\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
-vector space with
connection
can be written in a unique way as the direct sum of terms
\El(\varphi)\otimes R_{\varphi}
, where
\varphi
runs in a finite set of minimally ramified polar parts and
R_{\varphi}
is a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
-vector space with regular singular connection (see
[
BE04
]
, see also
[
Sab08
]
). The minimally ramified polar parts entering the Levelt-Turrittin decomposition are called the
exponential factors
(also called its
irregular values
) of the
\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
-vector space with connection. Given a finite set
\Phi
of (possibly non minimally) ramified polar parts, we say with some abuse that the exponential factors of
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
belong to
\Phi
if any suitably further ramified exponential factor of
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
belongs to
\Phi
.

Property A

(Finiteness)
\pointrait

Given integers
r,N\geqslant 1
and a finite set
\Phi
of ramified polar parts, there exists only a finite number of quasi-unipotent rigid irreducible bundles with connection
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
on
U
of rank
r
such that

\scriptscriptstyle\bullet

the order of quasi-unipotency is at most
N
,

\scriptscriptstyle\bullet

At each
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\smallsetminus U
, the exponential factors of (the formalization of)
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
at
x
belong to
\Phi
.

Exponential-geometric origin

Let
Z
be a smooth complex quasi-projective variety. We say that an algebraic vector bundle with an integrable connection
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
on
Z
is
of exponential-geometric origin
if there exist a Zariski dense open subset
j:U\mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}}Z
, a morphism
f:Y\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}U
from a smooth quasi-projective variety and a regular function
\varphi
on
Y
such that
j^{*}(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
, regarded as an holonomic
\mathcal{D}_{U}
-module, is a submodule of
\mathcal{H}^{k}f_{+}(\mathcal{O}_{Y}^{r},{\mathrm{d}}+{\mathrm{d}}\varphi)
for some
r\geqslant 1
and some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. (This is an adaptation of the definition of “geometric origin” in
[
EK21
]
, see also
[
LL22
]
).

Roughly speaking, horizontal sections (or solutions) of such a
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
on
U^{\mathrm{an}}
can be given an integral expression, with the integrand being of the form
\mathrm{e}^{\varphi}\cdot\omega
for some algebraic differential form
\omega
(see
[
HR08
]
).

Property B
\pointrait

Any quasi-unipotent rigid irreducible
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
on
U
has exponential-geometric origin.

Integral structure

To any bundle with connection
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
on
U
is associated a Stokes-filtered local system
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet})
on the oriented real blow-up space
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
at the punctures
\mathbb{P}^{1}\smallsetminus U
(see
[
Mal91
,
Sab13
]
). The local system
\mathcal{L}
and the terms
\mathcal{L}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}
of the Stokes filtration are sheaves of
\mathbb{C}
-vector spaces. We say that the
\mathbb{C}
-Stokes-filtered local system has an integral structure if it comes by extension of scalars from
\mathbb{Q}[\zeta]
to
\mathbb{C}
(some
\zeta\in\mu_{N}
and some
N\geqslant 1
) from a
\mathbb{Q}[\zeta]
-Stokes-filtered local system with a
\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]
-structure in the sense of Definition
4.5
.

Property C

(Integral structure)
\pointrait

The Stokes-filtered local system associated to any quasi-unipotent rigid irreducible bundle with connection
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
on
U\varsubsetneq\mathbb{P}^{1}
has an integral structure.

Remark 1.2
.

One can define the notions of irreducibility, rigidity and quasi-unipotency for a Stokes-filtered local system. Due to the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence of Deligne and Malgrange (see
[
Mal91
]
), they correspond to those of the associated bundle with connection. The previous proposition can be stated as the property that a quasi-unipotent rigid irreducible Stokes-filtered local system has an integral structure.

Example 1.3
.

A (possibly confluent) non resonant hypergeometric differential equation is irreducible and rigid, and it is quasi-unipotent if its local exponents belong to
\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. In
[
DM89
]
and
[
Hie22
]
, the authors compute the Stokes matrices of confluent hypergeometric equations and the existence of an integral structure is then clear from their formulas. On the other hand, in
[
BHHS23
]
, as a particular case of their results, the authors make explicit its exponential-geometric origin and show that the associated enhanced ind-sheaf is defined on a cyclotomic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
.

Example 1.4
.

In
[
Jak20
]
, the author classifies rigid irreducible bundles with connection
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
on
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
with an irregular singularity at infinity of slope one at infinity and differential Galois group
G_{2}
. The family he obtains depends on various parameters in
\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Quasi-unipotency is equivalent to these parameters belonging to
\mu_{N}
for some
N\geqslant 1
, and Property
C
claims that, in such a case, the corresponding Stokes-filtered local system has an integral structure. See also
[
Kat96
, §8.4]
and
[
DR10
]
for the geometric origin in the tame case.

2.
Finiteness

In this section we prove Property
A
. We consider the data
(U,N,\Phi)
and quasi-unipotent rigid irreducible bundles with connection of rank
r
with these data, i.e., defined on
U
, quasi-unipotent of order dividing
N
and with exponential factors contained in
\Phi
.

A first approach to Property
A
is by noticing that from the data
(U,N,\Phi)
, one can cook up only a finite number of possible formal structures
\mathop{\textstyle\bigoplus}\displaylimits[\El(\varphi)\otimes R_{\varphi}]
at each
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\smallsetminus U
, with
\varphi\in\Phi
,
R_{\varphi}
being regular and
N
-quasi-unipotent. For each such data of formal structures at every
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\smallsetminus U
, there exists a smooth affine moduli space of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
such that the corresponding irreducible rigid
(V,\nabla)
are isolated points of this space (see
[
BE04
, Proof of Th. 4.10]
). Finiteness follows.

We will now prove finiteness as a consequence of the Arinkin-Deligne-Katz algorithm. This methods, being more constructive, is more quantitative, although it uses the equivalence between rigidity and cohomological rigidity shown in
[
BE04
, Th. 4.10]
. The proof is by induction on the rank
r
of
\mathcal{V}
, and we denote by
\eqref{th:finiteness}_{r}
the statement that Property
A
holds for bundles having any set of data
(U,N,\Phi)
and of rank
r
.

We choose an affine coordinate
t
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
such that
\infty\in U
. For each
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\smallsetminus U
, we regard
\varphi_{x}\in\Phi
as a polynomial in
1/(t-x)
with no constant term. Any choice of a family
(\varphi_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\smallsetminus U}
of elements of
\Phi
(there are finitely many such choices) yields a unique regular function
\varphi
on
U
(namely,
\varphi(t)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\smallsetminus U}\varphi_{x}(t-x)
). Given a bundle with connection
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
of rank one on
U
with data
N,\Phi
, there exists such a family
(\varphi_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\smallsetminus U}
such that
(\mathcal{V},\nabla-{\mathrm{d}}\varphi)
has regular singularities at each point
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\smallsetminus U
. Since the local monodromies at each such
x
belong to
\mu_{N}
, there is only a finite number of such bundles with connection.

\MakeQed

We now assume
r\geqslant 2
and
\eqref{th:finiteness}_{<r}
, and we will prove
\eqref{th:finiteness}_{r}
. We are given
(U,\Phi,N)
and we will prove
\eqref{th:finiteness}_{r}
for these data, a statement that we denote by
\eqref{th:finiteness}_{r}(U,\Phi,N)
.

\scriptscriptstyle\bullet

We can (and will) assume that
\#(\mathbb{P}^{1}\smallsetminus U)\geqslant 3
.

Indeed, if
\#(\mathbb{P}^{1}\smallsetminus U)<3
, let
U_{1}\subset U
with
\#(\mathbb{P}^{1}\smallsetminus U_{1})\geqslant 3
. By considering
U\smallsetminus U_{1}
as apparent singularities for
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
, we have the implication

\eqref{th:finiteness}_{r}(U_{1},\Phi,N)\implies\eqref{th:finiteness}_{r}(U,\Phi,N).

Given
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
rigid irreducible of rank
r
on
U
, there exists at most one
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\smallsetminus U
where
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)_{\widehat{x}}
is special with respect to the Katz algorithm (Case II of
[
Ari10
,
§
4.1]
). By possibly adding an apparent singularity, we can thus assume that

\scriptscriptstyle\bullet

there exists
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\smallsetminus U
which is either a special point or an apparent singularity.

There exists a finite number of automorphisms of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
sending
3
points of
\mathbb{P}^{1}\smallsetminus U
to
0,1,\infty
.

\scriptscriptstyle\bullet

We can thus (and will) assume that
0,1,\infty\notin U
and
\infty
is either the special point or, if such a point does not exist, an apparent singularity.

It is then enough to show the finiteness

(a)

of the set of quasi-unipotent rigid irreducible
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
of rank
r
with data
(U,\Phi,N)
and having no special point and an apparent singularity at
\infty
,

(b)

and of the set of quasi-unipotent rigid irreducible
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
of rank
r
having data
(U,\Phi,N)
such that
\infty
is special.

Let us recall the A-D-K algorithm in this case. One shows that there exists a rank-one algebraic bundle with connection
(\mathcal{L},\nabla)
on
U
, completely determined by
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
,

\scriptscriptstyle\bullet

satisfying
\eqref{th:finiteness}_{1}(U,-\Phi,N)
,

\scriptscriptstyle\bullet

and having monodromy
\chi
at
\infty
, for some
\chi\in\mu_{N}\smallsetminus\{1\}
, so that
\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{L}
has a regular singularity with monodromy
\chi\id
at
\infty
,

such that
\MC_{\chi}(\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{L},\nabla)
has rank
<r
, where
\MC_{\chi}
denotes the middle convolution functor by the Kummer sheaf
\mathcal{K}_{\chi}
. We note that
\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{L}
has data
(U,\Phi^{\prime},N)
with
\Phi^{\prime}=\Phi-\Phi
.

The formulas given in
[
DS12
, Prop. 1.3.11]
show the following:

\scriptscriptstyle\bullet

\MC_{\chi}(\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{L},\nabla)
has singularities contained in
\mathbb{P}^{1}\smallsetminus U
, the singularity at
\infty
being regular with monodromy
\chi^{-1}\id
,

\scriptscriptstyle\bullet

at each
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\smallsetminus(U\cup\{\infty\})
, the set of irregular values of
\MC_{\chi}(\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{L},\nabla)
at
x
is equal to that of
\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{L}
at
x
,

\scriptscriptstyle\bullet

the eigenvalues of the formal monodromies at any
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\smallsetminus U
belong to
\mu_{N}
.

It follows that
\MC_{\chi}(\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{L},\nabla)
has data
(U,\Phi^{\prime},N)
. Since
\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{L}
is non constant because it is irreducible and has rank
\geqslant 2
, it can be recovered as
\MC_{\chi^{-1}}\bigl(\MC_{\chi}(\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{L},\nabla)\bigr)
, according to
[
DS13
, Prop. 1.1.9]
. We conclude by induction on
r
that
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
belongs to the set obtained from a finite set of bundles with connections on
U
(satisfying
\eqref{th:finiteness}_{<r}(U,\Phi^{\prime},N)
) by applying
\MC_{\chi^{-1}}
, for some
\chi\in\mu_{N}
and by tensoring by a rank-one bundle with connection belonging to the finite set of those satisfying
\eqref{th:finiteness}_{1}(U,\Phi,N)
. This shows finiteness in Case (
a
).

\MakeQed

For
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
having a special point at
\infty
, the A-D-K algorithm starts by exhibiting a summand
\El(\varphi)\otimes R_{\varphi}
of
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)_{\widehat{\infty}}
which is ramified of order
\geqslant 2
. It could occur that, writing
\varphi
as a minimal ramified polar part
\sum_{j\geqslant 1}^{q}a_{j}z^{j/p}
, the leading term
a_{q}z^{q/p}
has exponent
q/p
(the slope of
\varphi
) which is an integer. If such is the case, we set
\Phi_{1}=\Phi\cup\{\varphi_{1}\}
, where
\varphi_{1}
is the leading part of
\varphi
with integral exponents, or is zero if the slope of
\varphi
is not an integer. Case II in
[
Ari10
]
shows that there exists a rank-one algebraic bundle with connection
(\mathcal{L},\nabla)
on
U
, completely determined by
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
, in particular satisfying
\eqref{th:finiteness}_{1}(U,-\Phi_{1},N)
, such that the Fourier transform
{}^{F}\!(j_{{\dagger}+}(\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{L},\nabla))
has rank
<r
and data
(U^{\prime},N^{\prime},\Phi^{\prime})
. The formal stationary phase formula of
[
DS12
, (1.3.5)]
(after
[
Fan09
,
Sab08
,
GS13
]
) gives the precise way
(U^{\prime},\Phi^{\prime},N^{\prime})
is obtained from the data of
(\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{L},\nabla)
(and thus depends only of these), hence from
(U,\Phi,N)
. One notices that
U^{\prime}
and
\Phi^{\prime}
both depend on
(U,\Phi)
(but not only on
U

resp.

\Phi
separately). We conclude by induction on
r
that
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
belongs to the set obtained from a finite set of bundles with connections on
U^{\prime}
(satisfying
\eqref{th:finiteness}_{<r}(U^{\prime},\Phi^{\prime},N^{\prime})
) by applying inverse Fourier transform and twist by a finite set of rank-one algebraic bundles with connection, ending thereby the proof for Case (
b
).

\MakeQed

3.
Exponential-geometric origin

In this section, we prove Property
B
. Let
\mathcal{M}
be the the minimal extension of
(\mathcal{V},\nabla)
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. It is a quasi-unipotent rigid holonomic
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
-module. Let us recall the basic result that we will use for the proofs of Theorems
B
and
C
. As the proof in
[
Sab18
]
is written in a sketchy way, we give a detailed proof in the appendix.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
Sab18
, Prop. 2.69]
)
\pointrait

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a quasi-unipotent rigid holonomic
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
-module. There exist

(a)

a smooth projective complex variety
X
and a strict normal crossing divisor
D\subset X
, together with a subdivisor
D_{1}\subset D
,

(b)

a projective morphism
f:X\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{P}^{1}
,

(c)

a rational function
g
on
X
with poles contained in
D
and whose pole and zero divisors do not intersect,

(d)

a locally free rank-one
\mathcal{O}_{X}(*D)
-module
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{reg}}
with a regular singular meromorphic connection
\nabla
,

such that
\mathcal{N}
is of torsion (i.e.,
\mathcal{N}^{\otimes N}\simeq(\mathcal{O}_{X}(*D),{\mathrm{d}})
for some
N\geqslant 1
) and
\mathcal{M}
is the image of the natural morphism

\mathcal{H}^{0}f_{+}(\mathcal{N},\nabla+{\mathrm{d}}g)(!D_{1})\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathcal{H}^{0}f_{+}(\mathcal{N},\nabla+{\mathrm{d}}g).

(3.1
\,*
)

In loc. cit.,
\mathcal{M}
is assumed to be formally unitary and its is shown that
\mathcal{N}
can be chosen of the same kind. Applying the same proof, one finds that if
\mathcal{M}
is quasi-unipotent, then
\mathcal{N}
can be chosen to be of torsion (see the appendix). It is therefore enough to show that the right-hand side of (
3.1

∗

) is of exponential-geometric origin. Setting
Y=X\smallsetminus D
, we can regard
(\mathcal{N},\nabla)
as a rank-one vector bundle with connection on
Y
and, in the right-hand side of (
3.1

∗

), we regard
f
as a morphism
f:Y\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Lemma 3.2
\pointrait

There exists a finite morphism
\rho:X^{\prime}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}X
such that
D^{\prime}=\rho^{-1}(D)
is a divisor with normal crossings,
\rho:Y^{\prime}:=(X^{\prime}\smallsetminus D^{\prime})\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}Y
is finite étale, and
\rho^{+}(\mathcal{N},\nabla)\simeq(\mathcal{O}_{Y^{\prime}},{\mathrm{d}})
.

Let
\mathcal{N}^{\nabla}
be the rank-one local system of horizontal sections of
\mathcal{N}^{\mathrm{an}}
on
Y^{\mathrm{an}}
. Since
\mathcal{N}^{\nabla}
is of torsion, the monodromies of
\mathcal{N}^{\nabla}
around the various irreducible components of
D
are roots of the unity, and there exists, after
[
Kaw81
, Th. 17]
, a finite morphism
X^{\prime\prime}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}X
with
X^{\prime\prime}
smooth projective and the pullback
D^{\prime\prime}
of
D
being a normal crossing divisor such that the pullback of
\mathcal{N}^{\nabla}
extends as a rank-one local system on
X^{\prime\prime}
, which is thus also of torsion. This local system becomes trivial after pullback by some finite étale covering
X^{\prime}
of
X^{\prime\prime}
, and the composition
X^{\prime}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}X^{\prime\prime}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}X
is the desired
\rho
.

\MakeQed

We conclude that, on
Y
,
(\mathcal{N},\nabla)
is a direct summand of
\rho_{+}(\mathcal{O}_{Y^{\prime}},{\mathrm{d}})
, since
\rho:Y^{\prime}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}Y
is finite étale. Furthermore, we have
\mathcal{H}^{0}(f\circ\rho)_{+}=\mathcal{H}^{0}f_{+}\circ\rho_{+}
and thus the holonomic
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
-module
\mathcal{H}^{0}f_{+}(\mathcal{N},\nabla+{\mathrm{d}}g)
is a direct summand of
\mathcal{H}^{0}(f\circ\rho)_{+}(\mathcal{O}_{Y^{\prime}},{\mathrm{d}}+{\mathrm{d}}(g\circ\rho))
. Restricting to the open subset
U
of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
where these holonomic
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}

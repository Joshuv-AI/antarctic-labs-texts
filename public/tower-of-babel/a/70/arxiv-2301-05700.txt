1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime number and let
K
be a number field.
In the case that
K/\mathbb{Q}
is abelian, Leopoldt
[
Leo62
]
defined a
p
-adic regulator

R_{p}(K)
and asked whether it is always the case that
R_{p}(K)\neq 0
.
For an arbitrary number field
K
, Leopoldt’s conjecture has many equivalent formulations
and is of great interest due its connections with Iwasawa theory and Galois cohomology
(see
[
NSW08
, Chapters X and XI]
, for example).

We now recall a formulation that is convenient for our purposes.
Let
\mathcal{O}_{K}
denote the ring of integers of
K
and let
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
denote
its group of units.
For an abelian multiplicative group
A
, let

\hat{A}:=\varprojlim_{n}A/A^{p^{n}}

be the
p
-adic completion of
A
.
For a finite place
w
of
K
, let
U_{K_{w}}
denote the group of units of the completion
K_{w}
and let
U_{K_{w}}^{1}
denote the subgroup of principal units.
Let
S_{p}(K)
denote the set of places of
K
above
p
.
For
w\in S_{p}(K)
, the inclusion
U_{K_{w}}^{1}\subseteq U_{K_{w}}
induces an isomorphism

\smash{U_{K_{w}}^{1}\cong\hat{U}_{K_{w}}}
.
Therefore, after taking
p
-adic completions, the diagonal embedding

\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\hookrightarrow\prod_{w\in S_{p}(K)}U_{K_{w}}

gives rise to a canonical homomorphism

\lambda_{K,p}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\longrightarrow\textstyle{\prod_{w\in S_{p}(K)}}U_{K_{w}}^{1}.

Leopoldt’s conjecture, or
\mathrm{Leo}(K,p)
for short, holds if
\lambda_{K,p}
is injective.
The
Leopoldt kernel

\mathcal{L}(K,p)
is defined to be the kernel of the map

\Lambda_{K,p}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\lambda_{K,p}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\textstyle{\prod_{w\in S_{p}(K)}}U_{K_{w}}^{1},

and the
Leopoldt defect

\delta(L,p)
defined to be
\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathcal{L}(K,p)
.
Since
\lambda_{K,p}
is always injective on
p
-torsion,
\mathrm{Leo}(K,p)
is equivalent to

\delta(K,p)=0
.

The most important result to date concerning Leopoldt’s conjecture is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
Ax65
,
Bru67
]

Let
K
be a finite abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}
or of an imaginary quadratic field.
Then
\mathrm{Leo}(K,p)
holds for every prime number
p
.

Ax
[
Ax65
]
reduced the proof of Theorem
1.1

to the assertion that the
p
-adic logarithms of algebraic elements over
\mathbb{Q}
are
linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
if and only if they are linearly independent over

\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, where
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
denotes the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}
in
\mathbb{C}_{p}
.
This is the
p
-adic analogue of an archimedean result of Baker
[
Bak66
]

and was proved by Brumer
[
Bru67
]
.

Waldschmidt obtained the following bound on the Leopoldt defect.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
Wal81
]

Let
K
be a number field and let
p
be a prime number.
Then
\delta(K,p)\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})
.

Maksoud has recently proved the following ‘relative’ version of Waldschmidt’s result.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
Mak22
]

Let
L/K
be an extension of number fields and let
p
be a prime number.
Then
\delta(L,p)\leq\delta(K,p)+\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times})-\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}))
.

Now let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields and let
G=\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)
.
A number of articles, including
[
Miy82
,
EKW84
,
Lau89
,
Kli90
]
, either prove special cases
of Leopoldt’s conjecture or obtain improved bounds on the Leopoldt defect in certain situations, by
exploiting the facts that the Leopoldt kernel
\mathcal{L}(L,p)
is a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[G]
-module and that the

\mathbb{Q}[G]
-structure of
\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}
is known when the base field
K
is
\mathbb{Q}
or an imaginary quadratic field.

In the present article, we take a different approach.
For a subgroup
H\leq G
, let
L^{H}
denote the subfield of
L
fixed by
H
.
We apply results from the representation theory of finite groups,
including (generalised) idempotent relations and Brauer relations,
to show that in many situations
\mathrm{Leo}(L,p)
can be deduced from
\mathrm{Leo}(L^{H},p)

as
H
ranges over certain non-trivial subgroups of
G
.
In most (but not all) of these situations, we also show that there exist
relations between the Leopoldt defects
\delta(L^{H},p)

as
H
ranges over certain subgroups of
G
.
We emphasise that our proofs do not require any knowledge of the

\mathbb{Q}_{p}[G]
-module structure of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}
,
but rather just use the fact that
\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}
and

\mathcal{L}(L,p)
are
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[G]
-modules.
Moreover, these techniques do not require any assumption on the base field
K
or on the prime number
p
.
In some situations, we can combine these results with known cases of Leopoldt’s conjecture
for proper subfields of
L
.
In the case that
K
is equal to either
\mathbb{Q}
or an imaginary quadratic field, we use elementary representation theory together with Theorem
1.1

to show that the Leopoldt defect
\delta(L,p)
is either zero or satisfies a non-trivial lower bound.
Moreover, as discussed below, we combine special cases of the above results
with methods of Buchmann and Sands
[
BS88
]
to prove Leopoldt’s conjecture
at finitely many primes for certain infinite families of non-abelian totally real Galois extensions of

\mathbb{Q}
.

We now state a selection of our results.
The following result has an elementary proof using central idempotents (see §
3.2
),
yet does not appear to have been known until now.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields and let
G=\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)
.
Let
p
be a prime number.
Then

\mathrm{Leo}(L,p)
holds if and only if
\mathrm{Leo}(L^{\ker\chi},p)
holds for all
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
,
the set of complex irreducible characters of
G
.

The following corollary is a sharpening of
[
Shi92
, Lemma 3.1]
, which
has the additional assumption that
L
contains no primitive
p
th root of unity.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
L/K
be an abelian extension of number fields and let
p
be a prime number.
Then
\mathrm{Leo}(L,p)
holds if and only if
\mathrm{Leo}(F,p)
holds for every intermediate extension
F

such that
F/K
is cyclic.

By using so-called ‘generalised useful idempotent relations’ (see §
4.1
)
and a key result of
[
BFHP22
]
,
we prove the following result in §
4.6
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields and let
G=\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)
.
Suppose that
G
contains either a non-cyclic subgroup of order
\ell_{1}\ell_{2}
,
where
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
are two not necessarily distinct prime numbers, or a subgroup
isomorphic to
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
, where
\ell=2^{2^{k}}+1
is a Fermat prime
with
k>1
. Let
p
be a prime number.
Then
\mathrm{Leo}(L,p)
holds if and only if
\mathrm{Leo}(F,p)
holds for every proper intermediate field
F
of
L/K
.

For further explicit results on the deduction of Leopoldt’s conjecture from certain subfields,
we refer the reader to §
3.2
and §
4.6
.

In
[
KW14
, Proposition A.1]
, Khare and Wintenberger prove that,
if
F/\mathbb{Q}
is a totally real finite Galois extension, then for every prime number

p
we have
\delta(F,p)\neq 1
.
By an elementary argument involving central idempotents, in §
3.3

we prove the following generalisation of their result.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields where either

K=\mathbb{Q}
or
K
is an imaginary quadratic field.
Let
G=\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)
and let
1<d_{1}<\cdots<d_{s}
be the distinct degrees of the non-linear complex irreducible characters of
G
.
Let
p
be a prime number.
Then
\delta(L,p)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}k_{i}d_{i}
for some
k_{1},\ldots,k_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
In particular, either
\delta(L,p)=0
or
\delta(L,p)\geq d_{1}>1
.

In fact, Theorem
1.7
is a corollary of a
stronger result that is expressed in terms of dimensions of certain simple
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[G]
-modules
(see Theorem
3.8
).

In §
5
, we discuss Brauer relations and their relation to idempotent relations.
In §
5.6
, we then use these to prove several results on relations between Leopoldt defects of intermediate fields of a Galois extension of number fields,
including the following.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields and let
G=\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)
.
Let
H
be a non-cyclic subgroup of
G
and for every subgroup
I\leq H
, set

\mu(I,H):=\sum_{I=I_{0}<\cdots<I_{n}=H}(-1)^{n}
.
Then
\sum_{I\leq H}\mu(I,H)|I|\delta(L^{I},p)=0
for every prime number
p
.

For example, we obtain the following specialisation to the case of Frobenius groups.

Corollary 1.9
.

Let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields and let
G=\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)
.
Suppose that
G=N\rtimes H
is a Frobenius group with kernel
N
and complement
H
.
Then

\delta(L,p)+|H|\delta(K,p)=\delta(L^{N},p)+|H|\delta(L^{H},p)

for every prime number
p
.

Note that
S_{3}
, the symmetric group on
3
letters, is an example of a Frobenius group.
Let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be an
S_{3}
-extension and let
M
be any choice
of cubic subfield of
L
.
Since Leopoldt’s conjecture is known for
\mathbb{Q}
and for all quadratic fields,
applying Corollary
1.9
to
L/\mathbb{Q}
gives
\delta(L,p)=2\delta(M,p)
.
In particular,
\mathrm{Leo}(M,p)
implies
\mathrm{Leo}(L,p)
.
(In fact, this last result can be proven more directly; see §
4.6
.)

In §
6
, we use explicit descriptions of independent units along with
two different but related techniques developed by Buchmann and Sands
[
BS88
]

to prove Leopoldt’s conjecture at certain primes for an infinite
family of non-Galois totally real cubic fields.
We then use the result of the previous paragraph to pass to the corresponding totally real

S_{3}
-extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
and thereby obtain the following result in §
6.3
.

Theorem 1.10
.

Given a finite set of prime numbers
\mathcal{P}
,
there exists an infinite family
\mathcal{F}
of totally real

S_{3}
-extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
such that
\mathrm{Leo}(F,p)
holds for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
and
p\in\mathcal{P}
.

We also apply the second of the aforementioned techniques of Buchmann and Sands
to certain totally real non-Galois quartic fields, and then pass to the corresponding
totally real
D_{8}
-extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
to obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.11
.

There exists an infinite family
\mathcal{F}
of totally real
D_{8}
-extensions of
\mathbb{Q}

such that
\mathrm{Leo}(F,p)
holds for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
and every prime number
p\leq 10^{6}
with
p\neq 3
.

Until now, there was no known example of a finite non-abelian group
G
,
an infinite family
\mathcal{F}
of totally real
G
-extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
, and an odd prime number
p

such that
\mathrm{Leo}(F,p)
has been proven to hold for all
F\in\mathcal{F}
.
(For certain finite non-abelian groups
G
, we have that
\mathrm{Leo}(F,2)
holds for all totally real
G
-extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
by
[
Miy82
, Theorem 3]
.)
Moreover, the methods used to prove Theorems
1.10

and
1.11
could in principle be applied to totally real

G
-extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
for other finite non-abelian groups
G
, provided appropriate explicit descriptions of independent units can be found.

Let
p
be a prime number and let
L/K
be an extension of number fields.
We assume the notation introduced in the second paragraph of §
1
.
We have the following commutative diagram

(2.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\prod_{w\in S_{p}(L)}U_{L_{w}}^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}}

From this, we immediately deduce the following well-known lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

We have
\delta(K,p)\leq\delta(L,p)
. In particular,
\mathrm{Leo}(L,p)
implies
\mathrm{Leo}(K,p)
.

Now suppose that
L/K
is Galois and let
G=\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)
.
Then
\Lambda_{L,p}
is a map of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[G]
-modules and so
\mathcal{L}(L,p)
is also a

\mathbb{Q}_{p}[G]
-module.
For a subgroup
H\leq G
, let
L^{H}
be the subfield of
L
fixed by
H

and let
\mathcal{L}(L,p)^{H}
denote the
H
-invariant subspace of
\mathcal{L}(L,p)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

We have
\mathcal{L}(L,p)^{H}=\mathcal{L}(L^{H},p)
.

Proof.

We can and do assume without loss of generality that
H=G
, that is,
K=L^{H}
.
Observe that

(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times})^{G}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
.
Thus from diagram (
2.1
) we have

\mathcal{L}(L,p)^{G}=\mathcal{L}(L,p)\cap(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times})^{G}=\mathcal{L}(L,p)\cap(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})=\mathcal{L}(K,p).\qed

3.
Central idempotents

3.1.
Characters and central idempotents

Let
G
be a finite group and let
K
be a field of characteristic
0
.
For a subgroup
H\leq G
, let
N_{H}=\sum_{h\in H}h
denote the associated norm element.
Note that
e_{H}:=|H|^{-1}N_{H}
is an idempotent in the group algebra
K[G]
and is central
if and only if
H
is normal in
G
.
For a
K[G]
-module
M
, we have

e_{H}M=N_{H}M=M^{H}:=\{m\in M\mid hm=m\textrm{ for all }h\in H\}.

Let
\mathrm{Irr}_{K}(G)
denote the set of characters attached to finite-dimensional
K
-valued irreducible representations of
G
. For
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{K}(G)
, let
\ker\chi=\{g\in G\mid\chi(g)=\chi(1)\}
denote the kernel of
\chi
, and let

e_{\chi}=|G|^{-1}\chi(1)\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g^{-1})g

denote the central primitive idempotent of
K[G]
attached to
\chi
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
M
be a
K[G]
-module.
Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

M=0
.

(ii)

L\otimes_{K}M=0
for any field extension
L/K
.

(iii)

e_{\chi}M=0
for all
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{K}(G)
.

(iv)

e_{\ker\chi}M=0
for all
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{K}(G)
.

(v)

e_{\ker\chi}M=0
for all
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
.

Proof.

The equivalence of (i) and (ii) is clear. The equivalence of (i) and (iii) follows immediately from
the decomposition of
K[G]
-modules

\smash{K[G]=\bigoplus_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{K}(G)}e_{\chi}K[G]}
.
We have that (iv) implies (iii) since
e_{\chi}e_{\ker\chi}=e_{\chi}
for each
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{K}(G)
.
Moreover, it is clear that (i) implies (iv).
The equivalence of (iv) and (v) follows easily from the equivalence of (i) and (ii) and the fact that

\{\ker\chi\mid\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{L}(G)\}
is the same for any field
extension
L/K
such that
L
contains an algebraic closure of
K
.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Suppose that
G
has no faithful complex irreducible character and let
M
be a
K[G]
-module.
Then
M=0
if and only if
e_{N}M=0
for every proper normal subgroup
N\leq G
.

Remark 3.3
.

If
G
has a faithful complex irreducible character then its centre must be cyclic;
the converse holds if
G
is nilpotent (see
[
Isa94
, Theorem (2.32), Problem (5.25)]
).

Corollary 3.4
.

Suppose that
G
is a finite abelian group and let
M
be a
K[G]
-module.
Then
M=0
if and only if
e_{N}M=0
for every subgroup
N\leq G
with cyclic quotient.

Proof.

The non-trivial implication follows from Proposition
3.1
since

G/\ker\chi
is cyclic for all
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
.
∎

3.2.
Leopoldt’s conjecture from subfields I

Despite its elementary proof, the authors were unable to locate the following result in the literature.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields and let
G=\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)
.
Let
p
be a prime number.
Then

\mathrm{Leo}(L,p)
holds if and only if
\mathrm{Leo}(L^{\ker\chi},p)
holds for all
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
.

Proof.

This follows from Proposition
3.1
and
Lemma
2.2
.
∎

As noted in the introduction, the following corollary is a sharpening of
[
Shi92
, Lemma 3.1]
, which
has the additional assumption that
L
contains no primitive
p
th root of unity, and thus, in particular, requires
p
to be odd.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
L/K
be an abelian extension of number fields and let
p
be a prime number.
Then
\mathrm{Leo}(L,p)
holds if and only if
\mathrm{Leo}(F,p)
holds for every intermediate extension
F

such that
F/K
is cyclic.

Proof.

This follows either from Theorem
3.5

or from Corollary
3.4
and
Lemma
2.2
.
∎

We give just one example of how Corollary
3.6
can be applied to abelian sub-extensions.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be a
D_{8}
-extension and let
F
be a non-Galois quartic subfield of
L
.
Let
p
be a prime number.
Then
\mathrm{Leo}(L,p)
holds if and only if
\mathrm{Leo}(F,p)
holds.

Proof.

There exists a subgroup
H\leq\mathrm{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})
such that
H\cong C_{2}\times C_{2}

and
L^{H}
is contained in
F
.
Let
H_{1},H_{2},H_{3}
be the three subgroups of

of order
2
.
Then up to some relabelling,
L^{H_{1}}
is a biquadratic field,
L^{H_{2}}=F
and
L^{H_{3}}
is isomorphic to
F
. Since
\mathrm{Leo}(L^{H_{1}},p)
holds by Theorem
1.1
, the desired result follows
from Corollary
3.6
applied to
L/L^{H}
.
∎

3.3.
Lower bounds for non-zero Leopoldt defects

In
[
KW14
, Proposition A.1]
, Khare and Wintenberger prove that,
if
F/\mathbb{Q}
is a totally real finite Galois extension, then for every prime number

p
we have
\delta(F,p)\neq 1
.
As explained in
[
KW14
, Appendix]
, this
result is a strengthening of an argument shown to them by Colmez.

The following result is a further strengthening that has an elementary proof.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
L/K
be a non-abelian Galois extension of number fields where either
K=\mathbb{Q}

or
K
is an imaginary quadratic field.
Let
G=\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)
and let
G^{\prime}
be the commutator subgroup of
G
.
Let
e_{G^{\prime}}=|G^{\prime}|^{-1}\sum_{g^{\prime}\in G^{\prime}}g^{\prime}
and let
A=(1-e_{G^{\prime}})\mathbb{Q}[G]
.
Let
p
be a prime number and let
1<d_{1}<\cdots<d_{s}
be the distinct
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-dimensions
of the simple modules of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}A
.
Then
\delta(L,p)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}k_{i}d_{i}
for some
k_{1},\ldots,k_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
In particular, either
\delta(L,p)=0
or
\delta(L,p)\geq d_{1}>1
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
and Theorem
1.1
, we have

e_{G^{\prime}}\mathcal{L}(L,p)=\mathcal{L}(L,p)^{G^{\prime}}=\mathcal{L}(L^{G^{\prime}},p)=0
.
Thus
\mathcal{L}(L,p)=(1-e_{G^{\prime}})\mathcal{L}(L,p)
is in fact a module over

\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}A=(1-e_{G^{\prime}})\mathbb{Q}_{p}[G]
.
Since
\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}A
is a semisimple
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-algebra,

\mathcal{L}(L,p)
is a direct sum of simple
\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}A
-modules,
each of which has
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-dimension
d_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq s
.
The result now follows easily.
∎

The following corollary has the advantage
that the degrees
d_{i}
can be read off the complex character table of
G
and are independent of the choice of prime number
p
.

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
L/K
be a non-abelian Galois extension of number fields where either
K=\mathbb{Q}

or
K
is an imaginary quadratic field.
Let
G=\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)
and let
1<d_{1}<\cdots<d_{s}
be the distinct degrees of the non-linear complex irreducible characters of
G
.
Let
p
be a prime number.
Then
\delta(L,p)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}k_{i}d_{i}
for some
k_{1},\ldots,k_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
In particular, either
\delta(L,p)=0
or
\delta(L,p)\geq d_{1}>1
.

Proof.

The same reasoning as in the proof of Theorem
3.8
shows that

\mathbb{C}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathcal{L}(L,p)=\mathbb{C}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(1-e_{G^{\prime}})\mathcal{L}(L,p)

is in fact a module over
\mathbb{C}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}A=(1-e_{G^{\prime}})\mathbb{C}_{p}[G]
.
By fixing a field isomorphism
\mathbb{C}\cong\mathbb{C}_{p}
, we see that each simple module
of
(1-e_{G^{\prime}})\mathbb{C}_{p}[G]
is of
\mathbb{C}_{p}
-dimension
d_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq s
.
The result now follows from the observations that

\delta(L,p)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}_{p}}\mathbb{C}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathcal{L}(L,p)
and that

\mathbb{C}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathcal{L}(L,p)
is a direct sum of simple
(1-e_{G^{\prime}})\mathbb{C}_{p}[G]
-modules.
∎

Example 3.10
.

Let
p
be an odd prime number and let
G
be a non-abelian group of order
p^{3}
.
There are precisely two possibilities for
G
up to isomorphism,
both of which have the same complex character table.
In particular,
|G^{\prime}|=p
and every non-linear complex irreducible character of
G
has degree

p
and character field
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})
, where
\zeta_{p}
denotes a primitive
p
th root of unity.
Now let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be a Galois extension with
\mathrm{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})\cong G
.
Let
\ell
be any prime number and let
m=[\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(\zeta_{p}):\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}]

(note that
m=p-1
if
\ell=p
).
Then each simple
(1-e_{G^{\prime}})\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}[G]
-module has
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
-dimension equal to

pm
and thus
Theorem
3.8
tells us that
\delta(L,\ell)=pmk
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
By contrast, Corollary
3.9
tells us that
\delta(L,\ell)=pn
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
since every non-linear complex irreducible character of
G
is of degree
p
.

4.
Relations among idempotents in group algebras

4.1.
Idempotent relations and generalisations

The following underpins many of the results in the remainder of this article.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
\mathcal{H}
be a set of subgroups of
G
.

(i)

An
idempotent relation
with respect to
\mathcal{H}
is an equality in
\mathbb{Q}[G]
of the form

0=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}a_{H}e_{H}

with
a_{H}\in\mathbb{Q}
.
Such a relation is said to be
useful
if
1\in\mathcal{H}
and
a_{1}\neq 0
.

(ii)

A
generalised useful idempotent relation
with respect to
\mathcal{H}

is an equality in
\mathbb{Q}[G]
of the form

1=\sum_{1\neq H\in\mathcal{H}}a_{H}e_{H}

with
a_{H}\in\mathbb{Q}[G]
.

Remark 4.2
.

Note that in the definition of generalised useful idempotent relation, it does not make any difference
whether
1\in\mathcal{H}
or
1\notin\mathcal{H}
.
Thus every useful idempotent relation with respect to
\mathcal{H}

gives rise to a generalised useful idempotent relation with respect to
\mathcal{H}
,
after a possible rescaling of coefficients.

Remark 4.3
.

Idempotent relations have been studied, either implicitly or explicitly,
in numerous articles, including

[
Acc70
,
Kan85
,
MZ87
,
KR89
,
KR94
,
Bol97
,
BB04
]
.
We shall discuss their connection with Brauer relations in §
5.1
.
The connection between generalised useful idempotent relations and

norm relations
, as defined in
[
BFHP22
, Definition 2.1]
, is discussed
in Remark
4.18
.

4.2.
The vanishing of modules over group algebras

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
0
.
Definition
4.1
is in part motivated by the following elementary results.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
G
be a finite group and suppose that
G
has a generalised useful idempotent relation
with respect to a set of subgroups
\mathcal{H}
.
Let
M
be a
K[G]
-module.
Then
M=0
if and only if
e_{H}M=0
for every
H\in\mathcal{H}
with
H\neq 1
.

Proof.

By hypothesis there exists a relation of the form

1=\sum_{1\neq H\in\mathcal{H}}a_{H}e_{H}
with
a_{H}\in\mathbb{Q}[G]
.
Suppose that
e_{H}M=0
for every
H\in\mathcal{H}
with
H\neq 1
. Let
x\in M
.
Then for every
H\in\mathcal{H}
with
H\neq 1
we have
e_{H}x\in e_{H}M=0
,
so
x=1\cdot x=\sum_{1\neq H\in\mathcal{H}}a_{H}e_{H}x=0
and hence
M=0
.
The converse is trivial.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
G
be a finite group and suppose that
G
has a generalised useful idempotent relation
with respect to a set of subgroups
\mathcal{H}
.
Let
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
be such that
1\notin\mathcal{I}
and for every
H\in\mathcal{H}
there exist
I\in\mathcal{I}
and
g\in G
such that
gIg^{-1}\leq H
.
Let
M
be a
K[G]
-module.
Then
M=0
if and only if
e_{I}M=0
for every
I\in\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
e_{I}M=0
for every
I\in\mathcal{I}
.
Let
H\in\mathcal{H}
with
H\neq 1
.
Let
g\in G
and let
I\in\mathcal{I}
such that
gIg^{-1}\leq H
.
Then
ge_{I}g^{-1}=e_{gIg^{-1}}
and

e_{H}=\left(\frac{1}{[H:{gIg^{-1}}]}\sum_{h\in H/{gIg^{-1}}}h\right)ge_{I}g^{-1},

where
\sum_{h\in H/{gIg^{-1}}}
denotes the sum over any set of left coset representatives of
gIg^{-1}
in
H
.
Let
x\in M
. Then
e_{I}g^{-1}x\in e_{I}M=0
and so
e_{H}x=0
. Thus
e_{H}M=0
.
Therefore
e_{H}M=0
for all
H\in\mathcal{H}
with
H\neq 1
,
and so
M=0
by Proposition
4.4
.
The converse is trivial.
∎

4.3.
Some explicit idempotent relations

The following results were proven by Accola
[
Acc70
]
;
Proposition
4.10
is a slight improvement due to Kani
[
Kan85
, §3]
.
We include the short proofs for the convenience of the reader.

Proposition 4.6
.

[
Acc70
]

Suppose that
H_{1},\ldots,H_{t}
are subgroups of a finite group
G
such that

G=H_{1}\cup\cdots\cup H_{t}
. Then in
\mathbb{Q}[G]
we have

|G|e_{G}=\sum_{s=1}^{t}(-1)^{s+1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{s}\leq t}|H_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap H_{i_{s}}|e_{H_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap H_{i_{s}}}.

Proof.

By the inclusion-exclusion principle, each
g\in G
appears with coefficient
1
in the expression

\sum_{s=1}^{t}(-1)^{s+1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{s}\leq t}\sum_{h\in H_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap H_{i_{s}}}h,

which is therefore equal to
\sum_{g\in G}g
. This immediately implies the desired result.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

Proposition
4.6
does not necessarily give a useful idempotent
relation due to the possible cancellation of coefficients of
e_{1}=1
.

Corollary 4.8
.

Suppose that
H_{1},\ldots,H_{t}
are subgroups of a finite group
G
such that

G=H_{1}\cup\cdots\cup H_{t}
and
H_{i}\cap H_{j}=1
for all
i\neq j
.
Then in
\mathbb{Q}[G]
we have

|G|e_{G}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}|H_{i}|e_{H_{i}}-(t-1).

Proof.

For
s\geq 2
we have
H_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap H_{i_{s}}=1
.
Thus the desired result follows from Proposition
4.6
and the calculation

\sum_{s=2}^{t}(-1)^{s+1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{s}\leq t}1=\sum_{s=2}^{t}(-1)^{s+1}{t\choose s}={t\choose 0}-{t\choose 1}=1-t.

Note that it is also straightforward to prove this result directly.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

A set of subgroups of
G
satisfying the conditions of Corollary
4.8

is said to form a
partition
of
G
.
The possibilities for partitions are listed in
[
Sch94
, §3.5]
.

Proposition 4.10
.

[
Kan85
, §3]

Suppose that
H_{1},\ldots,H_{t}
are subgroups of a finite group
G
such that

(i)

H_{i}H_{j}=H_{j}H_{i}
for every
i,j
, and

(ii)

for each
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
there exists
i
such that
H_{i}\leq\ker\chi
.

Then
H_{i_{1}}\cdots H_{i_{s}}
is a subgroup of
G
for every

1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{s}\leq t
and in
\mathbb{Q}[G]
we have

(4.1)

1=\sum_{s=1}^{t}(-1)^{s+1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{s}\leq t}e_{H_{i_{1}}\cdots H_{i_{s}}}.

Proof.

Condition (i) implies that each
H_{i}H_{j}
is a subgroup of
G
and that

e_{H_{i}}e_{H_{j}}=e_{H_{i}H_{j}}=e_{H_{j}H_{i}}=e_{H_{j}}e_{H_{i}}
.
More generally, each
H_{i_{1}}\cdots H_{i_{s}}
is a subgroup of
G
and

e_{H_{i_{1}}}\cdots e_{H_{i_{s}}}=e_{H_{i_{1}}\cdots H_{i_{s}}}
.
Condition (ii) implies that given
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
, there
exists
i
such that
H_{i}\leq\ker\chi
, and so
e_{\chi}(1-e_{H_{i}})=0
in
\mathbb{C}[G]
.
Hence
\smash{e_{\chi}\prod_{i=1}^{t}(1-e_{H_{i}})=0}
for every
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
.
Therefore
\prod_{i=1}^{t}(1-e_{H_{i}})=0
in
\mathbb{Q}[G]
, which is equivalent to (
4.1
).
∎

Remark 4.11
.

If
G
has a faithful complex irreducible character then one of the

H_{i}
’s in Proposition
4.10
must be trivial and so
(
4.1
) just becomes
1=1
.

Remark 4.12
.

From Proposition
4.10
it is easy to show that

\smash{\prod_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\mathbb{C}}(G)}(1-e_{\ker\chi})=0}
in
\mathbb{Q}[G]

and hence obtain a useful idempotent relation in the case that
G
has
no faithful complex irreducible character.
Hence the equivalence of (i) and (v) in Proposition
3.1
,
and thus Corollaries
3.2
and
3.4
,
can be interpreted as a consequence of Corollary
4.5
.
However, we note that the combination of Proposition
4.10
and Corollary
4.5
does
not
improve upon Proposition
3.1
because if
H_{i}\leq\ker\chi
then
e_{\ker\chi}e_{H_{i}}=e_{\ker\chi}
.

4.4.
Frobenius groups

For further background material on Frobenius groups, including the proof of Theorem
4.14
below, we refer the reader to
[
CR81
, §14A]
.

Definition 4.13
.

A
Frobenius group
is a finite group
G
with a proper non-trivial subgroup
H
such that

H\cap{gHg^{-1}}=1
for all
g\in G-H
, in which case
H
is called a
Frobenius complement
.

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
G
be a Frobenius group with Frobenius complement
H
.
Then
G
contains a unique normal subgroup
N
, called the
Frobenius kernel
,
such that
G
is a semidirect product
G=N\rtimes H
.

Example 4.15
.

Let
q
be a prime power and let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field with
q
elements.
The semidirect product
\mathrm{Aff}(q):=\mathbb{F}_{q}\rtimes\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
with the natural action
is a Frobenius group.
Note that, in particular,
\mathrm{Aff}(3)\cong S_{3}
and
\mathrm{Aff}(4)\cong A_{4}
.

Proposition 4.16
.

Let
G=N\rtimes H
be a Frobenius group with kernel
N
and complement
H
.
Then
G
has a useful idempotent relation of the form

1=e_{N}+\sum_{g\in N}\frac{|H|}{|N|}e_{gHg^{-1}}-|H|e_{G}.

Proof.

From the definition of Frobenius complement it follows that
H=gHg^{-1}
if and only if
g\in H
.
Hence there are
|N|
distinct subgroups of the form
gHg^{-1}
, one for each
g\in N
.
Therefore the subgroups
N
and
gHg^{-1}
for
g\in N
intersect pairwise trivially
and their union is
G
.
Hence the desired result follows from Corollary
4.8
.
∎

Corollary 4.17
.

Let
G=N\rtimes H
be a Frobenius group with kernel
N
and complement
H
.
Let
M
be a
K[G]
-module. Then
M=0
if and only if
e_{N}M=e_{H}M=0
.

Proof.

This follows from Proposition
4.16
combined with
Corollary
4.5
.
∎

4.5.
Characterisation of groups that admit useful relations

Using
[
BFHP22
, Theorem 2.11]
, we give a
characterisation of finite groups that admit (generalised) useful idempotent relations.
We first compare Definition
4.1
and
[
BFHP22
, Definition 2.1]
.

Remark 4.18
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
\mathcal{H}
be a set of subgroups of
G
.
In
[
BFHP22
, Definition 2.1]
a
norm relation
with respect to
\mathcal{H}

is defined to be an equality in
\mathbb{Q}[G]
of the form

1=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{i}N_{H_{i}}b_{i},

with
a_{i},b_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}[G]
and
H_{i}\in\mathcal{H}
,
H_{i}\neq 1
.
Note that norm elements
N_{H_{i}}
can easily be replaced by idempotents

e_{H_{i}}
after rescaling coefficients.
Thus every generalised useful idempotent relation essentially already is a norm relation.
Moreover, since for every subgroup
H\leq G
and every
g\in G
, we have

ge_{H}g^{-1}=e_{gHg^{-1}}
, a norm relation with respect to
\mathcal{H}

can be rewritten as a generalised useful idempotent relation with respect to
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, where

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
may contain subgroups of the form
gHg^{-1}
where
H\in\mathcal{H}

but
gHg^{-1}\notin\mathcal{H}
.
Note that Corollary
4.5
can still be applied with
\mathcal{H}

rather than
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, so enlarging
\mathcal{H}
in this way
does not make a difference from the point of view of many of the applications that we have in mind.
Finally, note that
scalar norm relations
as defined in
[
BFHP22
, Definition 2.1]

are essentially the same as useful idempotent relations.

Theorem 4.19
.

[
BFHP22
]

A finite group
G
admits a generalised useful idempotent relation
if and only if
G
contains either
(i)
a non-cyclic subgroup of order
\ell_{1}\ell_{2}
,
where
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
are two not necessarily distinct prime numbers, or

(ii)
a subgroup
isomorphic to
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
, where
\ell=2^{2^{k}}+1
is a Fermat prime
with
k>1
.

Proof.

This follows from
[
BFHP22
, Theorem 2.11]
combined with
Remark
4.18
.
∎

Remark 4.20
.

As we shall see in §
5.4
,
finite groups admitting a useful
idempotent relation are precisely those containing a subgroup of type (i).
As remarked in
[
BFHP22
, Example 2.12]
,
the smallest group that admits a generalised useful idempotent relation
but no useful idempotent relation is

\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{17})
, which has order
4896
.

Remark 4.21
.

If a finite group admits both generalised and non-generalised useful idempotent relations,
the subgroups involved in the relations can be different (and non-conjugate).
See
[
BFHP22
, Example 2.13]
for an explicit example.

4.6.
Leopoldt’s conjecture from subfields II

Let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields and let
G=\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)
.
Let
p
be a prime number.
We now apply the results on (generalised) useful idempotent relations to the Leopoldt kernel

\mathcal{L}(L,p)
, and show that in many situations
\mathrm{Leo}(L,p)
can be deduced
from
\mathrm{Leo}(F,p)
for certain intermediate fields
F
of
L/K
.
The proofs do not require any knowledge of the

\mathbb{Q}_{p}[G]
-module structure of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}
,
but rather just use the fact that
\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}
and

\mathcal{L}(L,p)
are
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[G]
-modules.
Some results also use previously known cases of Leopoldt’s conjecture.
Note that none of the results depend on the choice of prime number
p
.

Theorem 4.22
.

Let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields and let
G=\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)
.
Suppose that
G
has a generalised useful idempotent relation with respect to a set of subgroups

\mathcal{H}
.
Let
\mathcal{I}
be a subset of
\mathcal{H}
such that
1\notin\mathcal{I}
and
for every
H\in\mathcal{H}
there exist
g\in G
and

I\in\mathcal{I}
such that
gIg^{-1}\leq H
.
Let
p
be a prime number.
Then
\mathrm{Leo}(L,p)
holds if and only if
\mathrm{Leo}(L^{I},p)
holds for every
I\in\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

This follows from Corollary
4.5
and
Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Corollary 4.23
.

Let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields.
Suppose that
\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)=N\rtimes H
is a Frobenius group with kernel
N
and complement
H
.
Let
p
be a prime number.
Then
\mathrm{Leo}(L,p)
holds if and only if both
\mathrm{Leo}(L^{N},p)
and
\mathrm{Leo}(L^{H},p)
hold.

Proof.

This follows from Corollary
4.16
and Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Corollary 4.24
.

Let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields
where either
K=\mathbb{Q}
or
K
is an imaginary quadratic field.
Suppose that
\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)=N\rtimes H
is a Frobenius group with kernel
N
and abelian complement
H
.
Let
p
be a prime number.
Then
\mathrm{Leo}(L,p)
holds if and only if
\mathrm{Leo}(L^{H},p)
holds.

Proof.

Theorem
1.1
and the hypotheses on
K
and
H
imply that
\mathrm{Leo}(L^{N},p)
holds,
and so the desired result follows from Corollary
4.23
.
∎

Corollary 4.25
.

[
EKW84
]

Let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be a totally imaginary
A_{4}
-extension.
Then
\mathrm{Leo}(L,p)
holds for all prime numbers
p
.

Proof.

As seen in Example
4.15
,

A_{4}\cong\mathrm{Aff}(4)=\mathbb{F}_{4}\rtimes\mathbb{F}_{4}^{\times}
is a Frobenius group.
Let
H
be subgroup of
\mathrm{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})
isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{4}^{\times}\cong C_{3}
.
Since
L^{H}
is a totally imaginary quartic field, we have
\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathcal{O}_{L^{H}}^{\times})=1
,
and so it easily follows that
\mathrm{Leo}(L^{H},p)
holds.
Thus the desired result follows from Corollary
4.24
.
∎

Remark 4.26
.

Corollary
4.25
was first proved in
[
EKW84
, Théorème 2]

by considering the
\mathbb{Q}[G]
-module structure of
\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}
.
By contrast, our proof only uses idempotent relations and the fact that Leopoldt’s conjecture holds
for certain proper subfields of
L
.

Corollary 4.27
.

Let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be an
S_{3}
-extension and let
F
be a cubic subfield of
L
.
Let
p
be a prime number.
Then
\mathrm{Leo}(L,p)
holds if and only if
\mathrm{Leo}(F,p)
holds.

Proof.

As seen in Example
4.15
,
S_{3}\cong\mathrm{Aff}(3)=\mathbb{F}_{3}\rtimes\mathbb{F}_{3}^{\times}

is a Frobenius group. Thus the result follows from Corollary
4.24
.
∎

Corollary 4.28
.

Let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields and let
G=\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)
.
Suppose that
G
contains either a non-cyclic subgroup of order
\ell_{1}\ell_{2}
,
where
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
are two not necessarily distinct prime numbers, or a subgroup
isomorphic to
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
, where
\ell=2^{2^{k}}+1
is a Fermat prime
with
k>1
. Let
p
be a prime number.
Then
\mathrm{Leo}(L,p)
holds if and only if
\mathrm{Leo}(F,p)
holds for every proper intermediate field
F
of
L/K
.

Proof.

One direction follows from Theorems
4.19
and
4.22
, the other from Lemma
2.1
.
∎

5.
Brauer relations

5.1.
Brauer relations and idempotent relations

Let
G
be a finite group and let
K
be a field of characteristic
0
.
Let
f_{1},f_{2}
be
K
-valued class functions on
G
.
We define the inner product to be

\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle_{G}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}f_{1}(g)f_{2}(g^{-1}).

Let
H\leq G
.
For a
K[H]
-module
M
, we define

\mathrm{Ind}^{G}_{H}(M)=K[G]\otimes_{K[H]}M
.
For the character
\chi
of a
K[H]
-module
M
,
we write
\mathrm{Ind}^{G}_{H}(\chi)
for the character of
\mathrm{Ind}^{G}_{H}(M)
.
For a character
\chi
of a
K[G]
-module, we write
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{H}(\chi)

for the restriction of
\chi
to
H
.
We denote by
\mathbf{1}_{H}
for the trivial character of
H
, which satisfies

\mathbf{1}_{H}(h)=1
for all
h\in H
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a set of subgroups of a finite group
G
.
A
Brauer relation
of
G
with respect to
\mathcal{H}
is an equality of

\mathbb{Q}
-valued class functions on
G
of the form

0=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}a_{H}\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}\mathbf{1}_{H},

with
a_{H}\in\mathbb{Q}
.
Such a relation is said to be
useful
if
1\in\mathcal{H}

and
a_{1}\neq 0
.

Remark 5.2
.

It is easy to show that cyclic groups have no non-zero Brauer relations.

The following well-known result is a minor variant of
[
BFHP22
, Proposition 2.7]
and says
that Brauer relations and idempotent relations are essentially the same.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
\mathcal{H}
be a set of subgroups of
G
.

(i)

If
0=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}a_{H}e_{H}
is an idempotent relation
then
0=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}a_{H}\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}\mathbf{1}_{H}
is a Brauer relation.

(ii)

If
0=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}a_{H}\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}\mathbf{1}_{H}
is a Brauer relation then

0=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}a_{H}\sum_{g\in G}e_{gHg^{-1}}
is an idempotent relation.

Proof.

We adapt the proof of
[
KR89
, §3]
.
For every
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
we have

\textstyle{\langle\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}a_{H}\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}\mathbf{1}_{H},\chi\rangle_{G}=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}a_{H}\langle\mathbf{1}_{H},\mathrm{res}^{G}_{H}\chi\rangle_{H}=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}a_{H}\chi(e_{H})=\chi(\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}a_{H}e_{H}),}

where we have extended
\chi
linearly to a function of
\mathbb{Q}[G]
and
the first equality follows from Frobenius reciprocity
[
CR81
, (10.9)]
.
This immediately implies part (i); since each
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
is a class function on
G
,
it also implies part (ii).
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Useful Brauer relations correspond to useful idempotent relations.

Remark 5.5
.

If
H
is a subgroup of a finite group
G
and
g\in G
, then we have

\mathrm{Ind}^{G}_{H}\mathbf{1}_{H}=\mathrm{Ind}^{G}_{gHg^{-1}}\mathbf{1}_{gHg^{-1}}
as class functions on
G
.
Therefore in a Brauer relation, coefficients can be ‘moved’ between conjugate subgroups
and so
\mathcal{H}
can always be chosen to have at most one representative of each conjugacy class of subgroups.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
G=N\rtimes H
be a Frobenius group with kernel
N
and complement
H
.
Then
G
has a useful Brauer relation of the form

|H|\mathbf{1}_{G}+\mathrm{Ind}_{1}^{G}\mathbf{1}_{1}=|H|\mathrm{Ind}^{G}_{H}\mathbf{1}_{H}+\mathrm{Ind}_{N}^{G}\mathbf{1}_{N}.

Proof.

This follows from Proposition
4.16
, Lemma
5.3
and Remark
5.5
.
∎

5.2.
Möbius function of partially ordered sets

We follow the exposition of
[
Glu81
, §2]
and
refer the reader to
[
Rot64
]
for more information on this subject.

Definition 5.7
.

Let
S
be a partially ordered set whose order relation is denoted by
\leq
.
For
a,b\in S
, the
interval

[a,b]
in
S

consists of all
c\in S
such that
a\leq c\leq b
,
and a
chain
from
a
to
b
of length
i
is
a totally ordered subset
T
of
[a,b]
such that
a,b\in T
and
|T|=i+1
.
The set
S
is
locally finite
if all intervals in
S
are finite.
If
S
is locally finite then it has a
Möbius function

\mu_{S}:S\times S\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
,
which is uniquely defined by the condition that

(5.1)

\sum_{c\in[a,b]}\mu_{S}(c,b)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\ a=b,\\
0&\text{if}\ a\neq b.\end{cases}

Remark 5.8
.

If we take the set of positive integers
\mathbb{N}
to be partially ordered by divisibility,
then
\mu_{\mathbb{N}}(a,b)=\mu(b/a)
where the latter
\mu
denotes the Möbius
function of number theory.

Remark 5.9
.

The alternating sum formula states that for
a,b\in S
we have
\mu_{S}(a,b)=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}n_{i}
,
where
n_{i}
is the number of chains from
a
to
b
of length
i
.

5.3.
Explicit Brauer relations

Given a non-empty set
\mathcal{H}
of subgroups of a finite group
G
, we consider

\mathcal{H}
to be partially ordered with respect to inclusion.
Let
\mathcal{S}(G)
denote the set of all subgroups of
G
and let

\mathcal{C}(G)
denote the set of all cyclic subgroups of
G
.
The following is result is due to Gilman
[
Gil73
, Lemma 1]
.
Also see
[
Glu81
, §3]
and
[
BB04
, Theorem 3.3]

for closely-related results.

Proposition 5.10
.

[
Gil73
]

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a set of subgroups of a finite group
G
.
Suppose that
G
is non-cyclic and that
\mathcal{C}(G)\cup\{G\}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
.
Then

\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}\mu_{\mathcal{H}}(H,G)|H|\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}\mathbf{1}_{H}=0.

Proof.

Let
\theta=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}\mu_{\mathcal{H}}(H,G)|H|\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}\mathbf{1}_{H}
.
Recall that for
H\leq G
and
g\in G
, we have

\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}\mathbf{1}_{H}(g)=|H|^{-1}\sum_{x\in G}\dot{\mathbf{1}}_{H}(xgx^{-1})

where
\dot{\mathbf{1}}_{H}
denotes the indicator function of
H

as a subset of
G
(see
[
CR81
, (10.3)]
).
Let
\mathcal{H}(g,x)=\{H\in\mathcal{H}\mid\langle xgx^{-1}\rangle\leq H\}
.
Then for
g\in G
we have

\theta(g)=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}\mu_{\mathcal{H}}(H,G)\sum_{x\in G}\dot{\mathbf{1}}_{H}(xgx^{-1})=\sum_{x\in G}\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}(g,x)}\mu_{\mathcal{H}}(H,G)=0,

where the last equality follows from (
5.1
) and the hypothesis that
G
is non-cyclic.
∎

Corollary 5.11
.

Let
G
be a non-cyclic finite group. Then

\mathbf{1}_{G}=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{C}(G)}a_{H}\frac{|H|}{|G|}\mathrm{Ind}^{G}_{H}\mathbf{1}_{H}\quad\text{ where }\quad a_{H}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\in\mathcal{C}(G)\\
Z\geq H\end{subarray}}\mu([Z:H]).

Proof.

This follows from Remark
5.8
and
Proposition
5.10
with

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{C}(G)\cup\{G\}
.
∎

Remark 5.12
.

Corollary
5.11
is a special case of Brauer’s explicit version of Artin’s induction theorem (see
[
CR81
, (15.4)]
). Moreover, Remark
5.2
and
Corollary
5.11
together imply
that
G
is non-cyclic if and only if it has a non-zero Brauer relation
(see also
[
Fun79
, Theorem 8]
).

Remark 5.13
.

Let
G
be a finite group. Then we can and do abbreviate
\mu_{\mathcal{S}(G)}
to
\mu

without ambiguity since by the alternating sum formula of Remark
5.9
,
for
I\leq H\leq G
, we have

\mu(I,H)=\sum_{I=I_{0}<\cdots<I_{n}=H}(-1)^{n}.

Some of the properties of
\mu
are discussed in

[
BB04
, Remark 3.7 and Lemma 3.8]
.

Remark 5.14
.

Brauer relations have been completely classified by Bartel and Dokchitser
[
BD15
,
BD14
]
.
Moreover, as explained in
[
BB04
, Remark 3.6]
, for a fixed non-cyclic finite group

,
the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space of Brauer relations of

is spanned by each of the following:

(i)

the relations arising from Proposition
4.6
via
Lemma
5.3
; or

(ii)

the relations of the form

\sum_{I\leq H}\mu(I,H)|I|\mathrm{Ind}_{I}^{H}\mathbf{1}_{I}=0
,
where
H
ranges over all non-cyclic subgroups of

(all such relations are given by Proposition
5.10
).

Useful Brauer relations can also be obtained from the explicit induction formulae
of
[
Sna88
, Theorem 2.16(i)]
or
[

, Corollary 2.22]
.

Funakura gave the following simple criterion for the existence of useful Brauer relations.

.

A finite group

contains

Remark 5.16
.

Recall that by Lemma

5.5.
An application of Brauer relations

Our main interest in Brauer relations is due to the following result, which
is essentially
[
KR94
, Lemma 4.1]
and may be viewed
as an example of the notion of factorizability, as explained in
[
DS96
, §2]
.

Proposition 5.17
.

[
KR94
]

Let
G
be a non-cyclic finite group and let
K
be a field of characteristic
0
.
Let
M
be a finite-dimensional
K[G]
-module.
If
0=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}a_{H}\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}\mathbf{1}_{H}
is a Brauer relation of
G

then
\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}a_{H}\dim_{K}M^{H}=0
.

Proof.

Let
\chi
be the character corresponding to
M
. Then

\textstyle{0=\left\langle\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}a_{H}\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}\mathbf{1}_{H},\chi\right\rangle_{G}=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}a_{H}\langle\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}\mathbf{1}_{H},\chi\rangle_{G}=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}a_{H}\langle\mathbf{1}_{H},\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}\chi\rangle_{H},}

where the last equality follows from Frobenius reciprocity
[
CR81
, (10.9)]
.
Hence the desired result follows since

\langle\mathbf{1}_{H},\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}\chi\rangle_{H}=\dim_{K}M^{H}
.
∎

Corollary 5.18
.

Let
G
be a non-cyclic finite group and let
K
be a field of characteristic
0
.
Let
M
be a finite-dimensional
K[G]
-module.
Let
H
be a non-cyclic subgroup of
G
.
Then
\sum_{I\leq H}\mu(I,H)|I|\dim_{K}M^{I}=0

where
\mu(I,H)
is as in Remark
5.13
.

Proof.

We can and do assume without loss of generality that
G=H
.
The result then follows from Propositions
5.10
and
5.17
.
∎

Remark 5.19
.

Corollary
5.18
may be viewed as a special case of

[
BB04
, Theorem 1.2]
, where the authors consider
cohomological Mackey functors
. In fact, the system
\{M^{H}\}_{H\leq G}
forms a cohomological Mackey functor;
see
[

, Example 1.2]
or
[

, Example 2.5(d)]

and recall that

.
Here we have given a more direct proof of Corollary

that does not require the full power of

5.6.

Let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields and let

be a prime number.
We now apply the results on Brauer relations to give relations between

of certain intermediate fields

.

Theorem 5.20
.

Let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields and let

If

is a Brauer relation of

\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}a_{H}\delta(L^{H},p)=0
for every prime number

.

Proof.

This follows from Proposition
5.17
and
Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Corollary 5.21
.

Let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields and let

Let

\sum_{I\leq H}\mu(I,H)|I|\delta(L^{I},p)=0

,

Proof.

This follows from Corollary

Corollary 5.22

Corollary 5.23

6.1.
Another formulation of Leopoldt’s conjecture

Sands
[

]
and Buchmann and Sands
[

,

]
gave equivalent formulations of Leopoldt’s conjecture that are useful from the point of view of explicit computations.
These are summarised as follows.

Theorem 6.1
.

[
BS88
,
\lx@sectionsign
II]

Let
K
be a number field and let
p
be a prime number.
Let
D
be a subgroup of
\{u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}:u\equiv 1\pmod{q}\}
of finite index,
where

if

is odd, and

if

.
For every positive integer

D(p^{k})=\{u\in D:u\equiv 1\pmod{p^{k}}\}
,
and let
\phi_{k}:D(p^{k})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{K}/p\mathcal{O}_{K}

be the homomorphism of abelian groups (the first multiplicative; the second, additive), defined by

1+p^{k}\alpha\mapsto\alpha\pmod{p}
.
Let

.
Then
\mathrm{Leo}(K,p)
holds if and only if there exists an integer

such that one of
the following equivalent conditions hold:

(i)

D(p^{m})\subseteq D^{p}
;

(ii)

D(p^{m})=D(p^{m-1})^{p}
;

(iii)

D(p^{m-1})/D(p^{m})
has dimension

as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space;

(iv)

the image of
\phi_{m-1}
has dimension
r
as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space.

Note that Theorem

does not depend on the choice of the subgroup

.

Theorem 6.4
.

[
HKS74
,
Lev92
]

Let
D
be a positive integer and let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\lambda)
be the pure field of degree
n
over
\mathbb{Q}

generated by
\lambda=\sqrt[n]{D^{n}\pm 1}>1
.
Then
S:=\{\lambda^{t}-D^{t}:t\mid n,t\neq n\}
forms a set of
\tau(n)-1
independent units in

\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
, where
\tau(n)
denotes the number of positive divisors of
n
.
Moreover, if
p
is an odd prime number such that
p\mid D
and
p\nmid n
, then

S
is a system of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-independent units.

Now we apply Theorem
6.1
to a certain family of non-Galois totally real cubic fields,
and use Corollary
4.27
to pass
to the corresponding totally real
S_{3}
-extensions
of
\mathbb{Q}
. Note that claim (iii) below is a special case of
[
BH69
, Theorem in Appendix]
.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
t\geq 2
be an integer and let
f_{t}(x)=x^{3}-t^{2}x-1
.
Let
\lambda_{t}
be some choice of root of
f_{t}(x)
, let
K_{t}=\mathbb{Q}(\lambda_{t})
and let

F_{t}
be the Galois closure of
K_{t}
over
\mathbb{Q}
.
Then

(i)

f_{t}
is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
and has three distinct real roots,

(ii)

F_{t}
is a totally real
S_{3}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
,

(iii)

\lambda_{t}
and
\lambda_{t}+t
are independent units in

\mathbb{Z}[\lambda_{t}]\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K_{t}}
, and

(iv)

\mathrm{Leo}(K_{t},p)
and
\mathrm{Leo}(F_{t},p)
both hold for all prime numbers
p\neq 3
such that
p\mid t
.

Proof.

It is straightforward to check that
f_{t}(x)=x^{3}-t^{2}x-1=(x-t)x(x+t)-1
has three real roots

\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}
such that

(6.1)

-t<\omega_{1}<-t+1\leq-1<\omega_{2}<0<t<\omega_{3}<t+1.

In particular,
f_{t}
is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
and so
K_{t}
is a totally real cubic field.
The discriminant
\mathrm{disc}(f_{t})
is
4t^{6}-27
and we have

0<(2t^{3}-1)^{2}<(2t^{3})^{2}-27=4t^{6}-27<(2t^{3})^{2}.

Hence
\mathrm{disc}(f_{t})
is not a perfect square and so

F_{t}=\mathbb{Q}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})

is a totally real
S_{3}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
.
For
i=1,2,3
, we have that
\omega_{i}
is an algebraic integer by definition and

(\omega_{i}-t)\omega_{i}(\omega_{i}+t)=f_{t}(\omega_{i})+1=1
;
thus each of
\omega_{i}-t
,
\omega_{i}
,
\omega_{i}+t
is an algebraic unit.
Moreover, by (
6.1
) we have

\log|\omega_{1}|,\log|\omega_{3}|,\log|\omega_{3}+t|>0

and
\log|\omega_{1}+t|<0
.
Hence

\det\begin{pmatrix}\log|\omega_{1}|&\log|\omega_{1}+t|\\
\log|\omega_{3}|&\log|\omega_{3}+t|\\
\end{pmatrix}=\log|\omega_{1}|\log|\omega_{3}+t|-\log|\omega_{1}+t|\log|\omega_{3}|>0.

For any
i,j\in\{1,2,3\}
and
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
we have
\omega_{i}^{m}=(\omega_{i}+t)^{n}
if and only if

\omega_{j}^{m}=(\omega_{j}+t)^{n}
.
Thus
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{1}+t
must be multiplicatively independent,
otherwise there would be a linear relation among the columns of the above matrix and so its determinant
would be zero.
Therefore
\lambda_{t}
and
\lambda_{t}+t
are independent units for any
choice of
\lambda_{t}\in\{\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}\}
.

Henceforth abbreviate
\lambda_{t}
to
\lambda
and
K_{t}
to
K
.
Let
D
be the subgroup of
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}

generated by
\lambda^{3}
and
(\lambda+t)^{3t}
;
these are (powers of) multiplicatively independent elements and so
[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}:D]<\infty
.
Let
p\neq 3
be a prime number such that
p\mid t
and
let
h
be the unique positive integer such that
p^{h}\parallel t
.
Recall that

D(p^{2h})=\{u\in D\,:\,u\equiv 1\pmod{p^{2h}}\}

and

\phi_{2h}:D(p^{2h})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{K}/p\mathcal{O}_{K}
is defined by
\phi_{2h}(1+p^{2h}\alpha)=\alpha\pmod{p}
.
We will show that
\phi_{2h}(\lambda^{3})
and
\phi_{2h}((\lambda+t)^{3t})
are
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-independent
and then apply Theorem
6.1
(iv) to deduce that
\mathrm{Leo}(K,p)
holds.

We have
\lambda^{3}=1+t^{2}\lambda\in D(p^{2h})
, and so

\phi_{2h}(\lambda^{3})=\frac{t^{2}}{p^{2h}}\lambda\pmod{p}
.
Observe that

(\lambda+t)^{3}=\lambda^{3}+3t\lambda^{2}+3t^{2}\lambda+t^{3}=1+t^{2}\lambda+3t\lambda^{2}+3t^{2}\lambda+t^{3}=1+3t\lambda^{2}+p^{2h}\gamma

for some
\gamma\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
Moreover, we have

(1+3t\lambda^{2}+p^{2h}\gamma)^{t}\equiv 1+3t^{2}\lambda^{2}\pmod{p^{2h+1}}
,
and so
\phi_{2h}((\lambda+t)^{3t})=\frac{3t^{2}}{p^{2h}}\lambda^{2}\pmod{p}
.
Note that both
\frac{t^{2}}{p^{2h}}
and
\frac{3t^{2}}{p^{2h}}
are coprime to
p
.
Therefore, in order to prove that
\phi_{2h}(\lambda^{3})
and
\phi_{2h}((\lambda+t)^{3t})
are
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-independent, it remains to show that
\lambda
and
\lambda^{2}
have
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-independent images in

\mathcal{O}_{K}/p\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
But the index
[\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}[\lambda]]
divides

\mathrm{disc}(f_{t})=4t^{6}-27
, which is coprime to
p
, and
so the inclusion
\mathbb{Z}[\lambda]\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}
induces
an isomorphism
\mathbb{Z}[\lambda]/p\mathbb{Z}[\lambda]\cong\mathcal{O}_{K}/p\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
Thus it suffices to show that
\lambda
and
\lambda^{2}
have
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-independent images in

\mathbb{Z}[\lambda]/p\mathbb{Z}[\lambda]
, which is clear since

\{1,\lambda,\lambda^{2}\}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathbb{Z}[\lambda]
.
Therefore
\mathrm{Leo}(K,p)
holds. Thus
\mathrm{Leo}(F_{t},p)
also holds by Corollary
4.27
.
∎

6.3.
Infinite families of number fields related by congruence conditions

In this subsection, we will use the following result due to Buchmann and Sands.

Theorem 6.6
.

[
BS88
, Theorem 4.1]

Let
f(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
be a monic irreducible polynomial of degree
n\geq 3
and let
\alpha
be a root of
f(x)
.
Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
and let
p
be a prime number such that
p^{2}\nmid\mathrm{disc}(f)
.
Let
S=\{\varepsilon_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{i,j}\alpha^{j}\}_{i=1,...,r}
be a maximal system of independent units of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
congruent to
1
modulo
q
in
\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]
, where
q=p
if
p
is odd, and
q=4
if
p=2
.
Let
D
be the group generated by
S
.
Assume that
\mathrm{Leo}(K,p)
holds and let
m\geq 2
be an integer such that
D(p^{m})\subseteq D^{p}

(such an
m
exists by Theorem
6.1
).
Let
g(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
be a monic irreducible polynomial of degree
n
such that
f(x)\equiv g(x)\pmod{p^{m}}
. Let
\beta
be a root of
g(x)
and let
K^{\prime}=\mathbb{Q}(\beta)
.
Assume that
K^{\prime}
has a maximal system of independent units

\{\delta_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}b_{i,j}\beta^{j}\}_{i=1,...,r}

such that
a_{i,j}\equiv b_{i,j}\pmod{p^{m}}

\forall i,j
. Then
\mathrm{Leo}(K^{\prime},p)
holds.

Note that Theorem
6.6
was applied by Buchmann and Sands to the families of number fields considered in Theorems
6.2
and
6.3
(see
[
BS88
, Corollaries 4.3 and 4.5]
).

We now give a reformulation of Theorem
6.6
that is convenient for our purposes.

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
r,n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
2\leq r<n
and
let
f(y,z),s_{1}(y,z),\ldots,s_{r}(y,z)\in\mathbb{Z}[y,z]
.
Suppose that
f(y,z)
is monic of degree
n
when considered as a polynomial
f_{y}(z)

in the variable
z
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}[y]
.
Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
be a set of indices.
Assume that
\{f_{t}(x)\}_{t\in I}
is a family of irreducible polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
.
For each
t\in I
, let
\lambda_{t}
be a choice of root of
f_{t}(x)
and let
K_{t}=\mathbb{Q}(\lambda_{t})
.
Assume that for every
t\in I
, the unit rank of
K_{t}
is equal to
r

and the set
\{s_{i}(t,\lambda_{t})\}_{i=1,...,r}
is a maximal system of independent units in

\mathbb{Z}[\lambda_{t}]\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K_{t}}
.
Let
t_{0}\in I
and let
p
be a prime number such that
p^{2}\nmid\mathrm{disc}(f_{t_{0}})
.
Assume that
\mathrm{Leo}(K_{t_{0}},p)
holds.
Then there exists an integer
m\geq 2
such that
\mathrm{Leo}(K_{t^{\prime}},p)
holds
for all
t^{\prime}\in I
such that
t^{\prime}\equiv t_{0}\pmod{p^{m}}
.

Proof.

Let
q=p
if
p
is odd, and
q=4
if
p=2
.
Let
k
be a positive integer such that

s_{1}(t_{0},\lambda_{t_{0}})^{k}\equiv\cdots\equiv s_{r}(t_{0},\lambda_{t_{0}})^{k}\equiv 1\pmod{q}
.
For each
i=1,\dots,r
, we consider
s_{i}(y,z)^{k}
and
f(y,z)
as polynomials in the variable
z
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}[y]
. Since
f(y,z)
is monic in
z
, for each
i
we can perform Euclidean division to obtain

s_{i}(y,z)^{k}=q_{i}(y,z)f(y,z)+r_{i}(y,z),

where
r_{i}(y,z)
has degree strictly less than
n
in
z
.
Then for every
t\in I
and each
i=1,\ldots,r
, we have
r_{i}(t,\lambda_{t})=s_{i}(t,\lambda_{t})^{k}
and so,
in particular,
\{r_{i}(t,\lambda_{t})\}_{i=1,\dots,r}
forms a maximal set of independent units of

\mathcal{O}_{K_{t}}
congruent to
1
modulo
q
in
\mathbb{Z}[\lambda_{t}]
.

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{Z}[\lambda_{t_{0}}]^{\times}
be the subgroup generated by

\{r_{i}(t_{0},\lambda_{t_{0}})\}_{i=1,\dots,r}
.
Since
\mathrm{Leo}(K_{t_{0}},p)
holds by hypothesis, by Theorem
6.1

there exists an integer
m\geq 2
such that
D(p^{m})\subseteq D^{p}
.
Let
t^{\prime}\in I
such that
t^{\prime}\equiv{t_{0}}\pmod{p^{m}}
.
Then
r_{i}(t_{0},z)\equiv r_{i}(t^{\prime},z)\pmod{p^{m}}
for each
i=1,\ldots,r
.
Taking
\{a_{i,j}\}
and
\{b_{i,j}\}
to be the coefficients of
r_{i}(t_{0},z)
and
r_{i}(t^{\prime},z)
, respectively,
we see that the hypotheses of Theorem
6.6
are satisfied and so

\mathrm{Leo}(K_{t^{\prime}},p)
holds.
∎

Proposition 6.8
.

Let
g(y)\in\mathbb{Z}[y]
be a polynomial that is not a square in
\mathbb{C}[y]
.
Let
d\in\mathbb{Z}
and let
I=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq d}
or
\mathbb{Z}_{\leq d}
.
Let
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
and let
J=\{ax+b\}_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}\cap I
be an arithmetic progression in
I
.
Then there exists an infinite set of prime numbers
\mathcal{L}
such that for each
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
,
there exists
k\in J
such that
\ell^{m}\parallel g(k)
for some odd
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

Proof.

By the hypothesis and Gauss’s lemma, we can write
g(y)=w(y)^{2}u(y)
where
w(y),u(y)\in\mathbb{Z}[y]
and

u(y)
is non-constant and square-free in
\mathbb{C}[y]
.
Set
v(x)=u(ax+b)
, which is a non-constant polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
.
The values of
x
such that
ax+b\in I
are in an interval
J
of the form
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq e}
or
\mathbb{Z}_{\leq e}
for some

e\in\mathbb{Z}
.
It suffices to show that there exists an infinite set of primes numbers
\mathcal{L}
such that for each

\ell\in\mathcal{L}
, there exists
k\in J
such that
\ell\parallel v(k)
.
After a straightforward linear reparameterisation, we can and do assume that
J=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Note that
v(x)
has no repeated roots in
\mathbb{C}
, since it is a linear reparameterisation of
u(y)
.
Hence there exist
a(x),b(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
such that

a(x)v(x)+b(x)v^{\prime}(x)=1,

where
v^{\prime}(x)
denotes the formal derivative of
v(x)
.
By clearing denominators, this implies that there exist
A(x),B(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
and
C\in\mathbb{Z}

such that

(6.2)

A(x)v(x)+B(x)v^{\prime}(x)=C.

It is well-known that if
h(y)\in\mathbb{Z}[y]
is non-constant,
then the set of prime numbers that divide some element of
h(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})
is infinite
(sketch: consider
h(n!c^{2})
for sufficiently large
n
, where
c
is the constant term of
h
).
In particular, this result applies to
v(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
.
Now let
\ell
be a prime number such that
\ell\nmid C
and there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with

\ell\mid v(k_{0})
.
We claim that we can always find
k_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
\ell\parallel v(k_{1})
.
Since there are infinitely many choices for
\ell
, this will prove the desired result.
If
\ell\parallel v(k_{0})
then we take
k_{1}=k_{0}
.
Suppose that
\ell^{2}\mid v(k_{0})
. Then

v(k_{0}+\ell)-v(k_{0})=v^{\prime}(k_{0})\ell+r\ell^{2}

for some
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.
However, since
\ell\mid v(k_{0})
and
\ell\nmid C
, we have that
\ell\nmid v^{\prime}(k_{0})
by
(
6.2
).
Therefore
\ell^{2}\nmid v(k_{0}+\ell)
, and so we can take
k_{1}=k_{0}+\ell
.
∎

Corollary 6.9
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
and let
f(y,z)\in\mathbb{Z}[y,z]
be of degree
n
when considered
as a polynomial
f_{y}(z)
in the variable
z
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}[y]
.
Suppose that
\mathrm{disc}(f_{y})
is a non-constant polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[y]
that is not a square in
\mathbb{C}[y]
.
Let
d\in\mathbb{Z}
and let
I=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq d}
or
\mathbb{Z}_{\leq d}
.
Let
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
and let
J=\{ax+b\}_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}\cap I
be an arithmetic progression in
I
.
Assume that
\{f_{t}(x)\}_{t\in J}
is a family of irreducible polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
.
For each
t\in J
, let
\lambda_{t}
be a choice of root of
f_{t}(x)
and let
K_{t}=\mathbb{Q}(\lambda_{t})
.
Then
\{K_{t}\}_{t\in J}
is an infinite family of number fields.

Proof.

It suffices to show that the set of prime numbers that ramify in at least one of the fields in

For every
t\in J
, we have that

\mathrm{disc}(f_{t})=[\mathcal{O}_{K_{t}}:\mathbb{Z}[\lambda_{t}]]^{2}\cdot\mathrm{disc}(\mathcal{O}_{K_{t}}).

Hence for a fixed

and prime number

.
Therefore the desired result now follows from Proposition
6.8
.
∎

The results of this section now allow us to prove the following theorem.

.

\mathcal{P}
,

\mathcal{F}
of totally real

-extensions of

.

We can and do assume that
\mathcal{P}
is non-empty.
Let

.
For every integer
t\geq 2
, let
K_{t}
and
F_{t}
be as in Theorem

.

First suppose that
3\notin\mathcal{P}
and let

.
Then for every

we have that
\mathrm{Leo}(F_{t},p)
holds for every

by

6.5
.
Moreover,
\{K_{t}\}_{t\in I}
and thus

.

Now suppose that

.

holds by

, Theorem A.2]
, for instance.

.

6.11
there exists an integer

\mathrm{Leo}(F_{t},3)

and

.
∎

be the Galois closure of

. Then

(i)

\mathrm{disc}(f_{t})=(t^{2}-4)^{2}(4t^{2}+9)
,

(ii)

f_{t}
is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
and has four distinct real roots,

(iii)

\{\lambda_{t}-1,\lambda_{t},\lambda_{t}+1\}
is a maximal system of independent units in

\mathbb{Z}[\lambda_{t}]\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K_{t}}
, and

(iv)

F_{t}
is a totally real
D_{8}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

The claims follow from
[
Nak96
, Lemma 2 and Proposition 6]
.
∎

Theorem 6.15
.

For every integer
t\geq 7
,
K_{t}
and
F_{t}
be as in Proposition
6.14
.
Let
p
be a prime number and let
t_{0}\geq 7
be an integer such that

p^{2}\nmid(t_{0}^{2}-4)^{2}(4t_{0}^{2}+9)
.
Suppose that
\mathrm{Leo}(K_{t_{0}},p)
holds.
Then there exists an integer
m\geq 2
such that
\mathrm{Leo}(K_{t^{\prime}},p)
and
\mathrm{Leo}(F_{t^{\prime}},p)
both hold for every

t^{\prime}\in I_{p,m}:=\{t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 7}:t^{\prime}\equiv t_{0}\pmod{p^{m}}\}
.
Moreover, both
\{K_{t^{\prime}}\}_{t^{\prime}\in I_{p,m}}
and
\{F_{t^{\prime}}\}_{t^{\prime}\in I_{p,m}}
are infinite families of number fields.

Remark 6.16
.

It is easy to show that for every
t_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

3^{2}\mid(t_{0}^{2}-4)^{2}(4t_{0}^{2}+9)
.
Thus Theorem
6.15
cannot be used to prove
\mathrm{Leo}(K_{t},3)
or
\mathrm{Leo}(F_{t},3)
for any
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 7}
.

Proof of Theorem
6.15
.

Since
\mathrm{Leo}(K_{t_{0}},p)
holds by assumption, by
Proposition
6.14
and Corollary
6.7

there exists an integer
m\geq 2
such that
\mathrm{Leo}(K_{t^{\prime}},p)
holds for every
t^{\prime}\in I_{p,m}
,
and by Corollary
3.7
, this implies that

\mathrm{Leo}(F_{t^{\prime}},p)
holds for every
t^{\prime}\in I_{p,m}
.
Moreover, by Corollary
6.9
,
\{K_{t^{\prime}}\}_{t^{\prime}\in I_{p,m}}
is an infinite family of number fields,
and thus the same is true of
\{F_{t^{\prime}}\}_{t^{\prime}\in I_{p,m}}
.
∎

Corollary 6.17
.

There exists an infinite family
\mathcal{F}
of totally real
D_{8}
-extensions of
\mathbb{Q}

such that
\mathrm{Leo}(F,p)
holds for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
and every prime number
p\leq 10^{6}
with
p\neq 3
.

Proof.

First note that
\mathrm{disc}(f_{7})=3^{4}\cdot 5^{3}\cdot 41

and
\mathrm{disc}(f_{9})=3^{2}\cdot 7^{2}\cdot 11^{2}\cdot 37
.
Using the Algorithm of
[
JN20
, Appendix A.1]
, we have verified

\mathrm{Leo}(F_{9},5)
and
\mathrm{Leo}(F_{7},p)
for every
p\leq 10^{6}
.
By Lemma
2.1
, this implies
\mathrm{Leo}(K_{9},5)
and
\mathrm{Leo}(K_{7},p)
for every
p\leq 10^{6}
.
By Theorem
6.15
, there exists an integer
m_{5}\geq 2
such that
\mathrm{Leo}(F_{t^{\prime}},5)
holds for
every
t^{\prime}\in I_{5,m_{5}}:=\{t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}:t^{\prime}\equiv 9\pmod{5^{m_{5}}}\}
.
Similarly, for every
p\neq 3
with
p\leq 10^{6}
, there exists an integer
m_{p}\geq 2
such that

\mathrm{Leo}(F_{t^{\prime}},p)
holds for
every
t^{\prime}\in I_{p,m_{p}}:=\{t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}:t^{\prime}\equiv 7\pmod{p^{m_{p}}}\}
.
Let
I=\bigcap_{p\leq 10^{5},p\neq 3}I_{p,m_{p}}
.
Then by the Chinese remainder theorem and Corollary
6.9
,
\{F_{t^{\prime}}\}_{t^{\prime}\in I}

is an infinite family of number fields with the desired property.
∎

Abstract
. Given a perfectoid field, we find an elementary extension and a henselian defectless valuation on it, whose value group is divisible and whose residue field is an elementary extension of the tilt. This specializes to the almost purity theorem over perfectoid valuation rings and Fontaine-Wintenberger. Along the way, we prove an Ax-Kochen/Ershov principle for certain deeply ramified fields, which also uncovers some new model-theoretic phenomena in positive characteristic. Notably, we get that the perfect hull of 𝔽 p ​ ( t ) h {\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)^{h} is an elementary substructure of the perfect hull of 𝔽 p ​ ( ( t ) ) {\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!) .



1.
Introduction

The local function field analogy, i.e., the analogy between the field of
p
-adic numbers
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and the field of Laurent series
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)
, has been highly influential in arithmetic. Historically, there have been two important justifications of this philosophy, which were also used to transfer certain results from one domain to the other. Both methods are of asymptotic nature but are substantially different from one another (cf. pg. 2
[
Sch14
]
).

1.1.
The method of letting
p\rightarrow\infty

The idea here is that
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)
become more and more similar as
p
goes to infinity. This is made precise by the work of Ax-Kochen
[
AxK65
]
and Ershov
[
Ers65
]
, which shows that for any given sentence
\phi
in the language of rings, there is
N=N(\phi)\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\phi\mbox{ holds in }\mathbb{Q}_{p}\iff\phi\mbox{ holds in }{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)

for any
p>N
. The crucial ingredient behind this, is that the theory of a henselian valued field of residue characteristic zero is fully determined by the theories of its residue field and value group. This is now known to hold in more general settings and has become a guiding principle in the model theory of valued fields. Using this as a black box, one easily checks that

\prod_{p\in\mathbb{P}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}/U\equiv\prod_{p\in\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)/U

for any non-principal ultrafilter
U
on the set
\mathbb{P}
of prime numbers. Then, the asymptotic transfer principle which was stated above follows routinely. Later, this principle was generalized in a motivic framework by work of Denef-Loeser
[
DL01
]
and Cluckers-Loeser
[
CL08
]
, allowing an asymptotic comparison between measures of definable sets.

This transfer principle between
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)
enabled Ax-Kochen to resolve a conjecture by Artin, at least in its asymptotic form. Artin conjectured that
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is a
C_{2}
field, i.e., that every homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
in more than
d^{2}
variables will have a non-zero solution over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. The corresponding statement was already known for
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)
by a theorem of Lang. Using the above transfer principle, Ax-Kochen proved an asymptotic version of Artin’s conjecture.
Namely, for every
d\in\mathbb{N}
, Artin’s conjecture holds for homogeneous polynomials of degree
d
in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with
p
sufficiently large.
We note that
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is actually not
C_{2}
, as was demonstrated by Terjanian in
[
Ter66
]
for
p=2
and
[
Ter80
]
for general
p
.

1.2.
The method of letting
e\rightarrow\infty

The second method is the one which will be of interest to us throughout the paper. Now fix
p
and instead replace the fields
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)
with highly ramified finite extensions thereof. This has the effect of making the similarities between the two fields even stronger, a phenomenon which was first observed by Krasner
[
Kra56
]
.
This method works especially well once we pass to infinite wildly ramified extensions. For instance, consider the extensions
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(p^{1/p^{\infty}})
and
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)(t^{1/p^{\infty}})
, obtained by adjoining
p
-power roots of
p
and
t
respectively.
Then one has the celebrated theorem of Fontaine-Wintenberger
[
FW79
]
, saying that the absolute Galois groups of those two fields are isomorphic. This was vastly generalized by Scholze
[
Sch12
]
within the framework of perfectoid fields and spaces.

A
perfectoid field
is a non-discrete complete real-valued field
K
of residue characteristic
p>0
such that
\mathcal{O}_{K}/(p)
is
semi-perfect
, i.e., the Frobenius map
\Phi:\mathcal{O}_{K}/(p)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{K}/(p):x\mapstochar\rightarrow x^{p}
is surjective.
Given a perfectoid field
K
, one can define its
tilt

K^{\flat}
, a perfectoid field of positive characteristic which is in many respects similar to
K
. We note that tilting is not “injective”, meaning that there are several perfectoid fields of characteristic zero which tilt to the same perfectoid field of characteristic
p
. These are parametrized by a regular noetherian scheme of dimension one, called the Fargues-Fontaine curve. Thus, in order to untilt, one also needs to specify a parameter. This parameter is a certain Witt vector
\xi_{K}\in W(\mathcal{O}_{K^{\flat}})
, only well-defined up to a unit. The valuation ring of the corresponding untilt is simply
\mathcal{O}_{K}=W(\mathcal{O}_{K^{\flat}})/(\xi_{K})
.

Even though tilting involves some loss of information, a lot can be learned about
K
from studying
K^{\flat}
. For instance, one has a generalized Fontaine-Wintenberger theorem, saying that the absolute Galois groups of
K
and
K^{\flat}
are isomorphic. This version was stated and proved by Scholze
[
Sch12
]
but also follows from parallel work of Kedlaya-Liu
[
KL15
]
.
Most strikingly, Scholze obtained an appropriate geometric generalization of the above arithmetic statement within the context of perfectoid spaces. Several arithmetic applications have been found since; see
[
Sch14
]
for a survey and also
[
Ban+22
]
for a more up-to-date account.

1.3.
Motivating question

Given how successful the method of transferring information between
K
and
K^{\flat}
has been, one can ask:

Question 1.3.1
.

How are
K
and
K^{\flat}
related model-theoretically? More specifically:

(i)

To what extent does the theory of
K^{\flat}
determine the theory of
K
?

(ii)

How close are
K
and
K^{\flat}
to being elementary equivalent?

This question was posed by M. Morrow at a conference talk in 2018 but was more or less folklore before. Some answers have been given since:

•

The perfectoid fields
K
and
K^{\flat}
are bi-interpretable within the framework of continuous logic. This was an early formative (yet unpublished) observation by Rideau-Scanlon-Simon. We emphasize that their interpretation of
K
in
K^{\flat}
requires parameters for the Witt vector coordinates of
\xi_{K}\in W(\mathcal{O}_{K^{\flat}})
.

•

The situation becomes subtle within the framework of ordinary first-order logic and parameters play a prominent role. For instance, one can prove that
K
is “in practice” decidable relative to
K^{\flat}
. More precisely, this is true if
\xi_{K}\in W(\mathcal{O}_{K^{\flat}})
is
computable
, meaning that an algorithm exists which prints one by one the Witt vector coordinates of
\xi_{K}
. This is the main theorem in
[
Kar24
]
and was used to obtain some new decidability results in mixed characteristic.

Even though
K
and
K^{\flat}
have different characteristics, we naturally expect a large set of sentences to be transferrable. For instance, sentences encoding Galois theoretic information certainly are, as
K
and
K^{\flat}
have isomorphic absolute Galois groups.

—is encoded in the language of rings.
We provide a new perspective on tilting , illustrating how it enables the transfer of first-order information between

and

.

1.4.
Main theorem

By passing to an elementary extension of
K
, we find a
well-behaved valuation, whose residue field is an elementary
extension of
K^{\flat}
:

Theorem

(see Theorem
6.2.3
)
.

Let
(K,v)
be a perfectoid field and
\varpi\in\mathfrak{m}\backslash\{0\}
. Let
U
be a non-principal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
and
(K_{U},v_{U})
be the corresponding ultrapower. Let
w
be the coarsest coarsening of
v_{U}
such that
w\varpi>0
. Then:

(I)

Every finite extension of
(K_{U},w)
is unramified.

(II)

The tilt
(K^{\flat},v^{\flat})
embeds elementarily into
(k_{w},\overline{v})
, where
\overline{v}
is the induced valuation of
v_{U}
on
k_{w}
.

Moreover, the isomorphism
G_{K_{U}}\cong G_{k_{w}}
restricts to
G_{K}\cong G_{K^{\flat}}
.

By a finite unramified extension of henselian fields, we mean one which induces a separable residue field extension of the same degree.
Part (I) equivalently says that the valuation
w
is henselian defectless with divisible value group and perfect residue field. In particular, it is a
tame
valuation in the sense of Kuhlmann
[
Kuh16
]
. The fact that the value group is of infinite (uncountable) rank may be somewhat intimidating. We note that this can easily be remedied by replacing the ultrapower with a bounded variant and then
w
becomes a spherically complete valuation with value group
\mathbb{R}
(cf. §
6.3
).

Tame valuations are by and large well-understood by work of Kuhlmann, not only from the point of view of valuation theory but also model-theoretically. In §
1.1
, we mentioned that the theory of a henselian valued field is often determined by the theories of its residue field and value group. Unfortunately, this is not quite true for tame fields of mixed characteristic. There, the situation is a bit subtle and goes hand in hand with the fact that the theory of
K^{\flat}
does not fully determine the theory of
K
(cf. Remark
6.2.4
). Still, knowing the theory of
k_{w}
(equivalently of
K^{\flat}
) tells us a lot about the theory of
K_{U}
(equivalently of
K
), see Remark
6.2.5
.

Next, we discuss the two parts of the main theorem:

1.5.
Part I

The fact that every finite extension of
(K_{U},w)
is unramified will not come as a surprise to experts. It is a non-standard version of the
almost purity
theorem over perfectoid valuation rings, as we will explain in §
1.6
.
Interestingly, our non-standard version allows for a significantly shorter proof in mixed characteristic compared to existing proofs. We outline the proof in each case.

1.5.1.
Positive characteristic

This is essentially identical with the (easy) proof of almost purity in
\text{char}(K)=p>0
. The key observation is that over a perfect field of characteristic
p
, an irreducible polynomial

f(X)=X^{n}+a_{n-1}X^{n-1}+...+a_{0}

generates the same extension as

f_{m}(X)=X^{n}+a_{n-1}^{1/p^{m}}X^{n-1}+...+a_{0}^{1/p^{m}}

for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Looking at discriminants, we get that
\text{disc}(f_{m})=\text{disc}(f)^{1/p^{m}}
and therefore

v(\text{disc}(f_{m}))=1/p^{m}\cdot v(\text{disc}(f))

This procedure allows us to generate a given finite extension of
K
by a monic irreducible polynomial with discriminant of arbitrarily small valuation. As for
K_{U}
, we may even form the limit polynomial
f_{*}(X)=\text{ulim}\ f_{m}(X)
. By Łoś, this still generates the same extension and now
\text{disc}(f_{*})\in\mathcal{O}_{w}^{\times}
. The reduction of
f_{*}(X)
is therefore separable—also irreducible by Hensel’s Lemma—and the extension is unramified. The case of a general extension of
K_{U}
is similar.

1.5.2.
Characteristic zero

We need to show that
w
is henselian, with divisible value group, perfect residue field and that there is no defect. The first three conditions are more or less clear. We only remark that the residue field
k_{w}=\text{Frac}(\mathcal{O}_{\overline{v}})
is perfect because the semi-perfect ring
(\mathcal{O}_{K}/(p))_{U}
surjects onto
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{v}}
. The only non-trivial thing to show is that
\mathcal{O}_{w}
is defectless.

Here, we utilize the method of standard decomposition, a classical tool in the model theory of valued fields. Consider the following diagram of places

K_{U}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle v_{0}}}{{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}}K_{U}^{\circ}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\overline{w}}}{{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}}k_{w}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow k_{v_{U}}

where
v_{0}
is the finest coarsening of
v_{U}
of residue characteristic
0
. We write
K_{U}^{\circ}
for the residue field with respect to
v_{0}
, often called the
core field
, and
\overline{w}
for the induced valuation of
w
on
K_{U}^{\circ}
, which is of rank
1
.
One knows that—as a consequence of
\aleph_{1}
-saturation—the valued field
(K_{U}^{\circ},\overline{w})
is spherically complete. This means that the intersection of any decreasing sequence of balls is nonempty and it implies that
\overline{w}
is defectless.
The valuation
v_{0}
is defectless, being of residue characteristic
0
. We conclude that
w
is also defectless, being the composite of such valuations.

1.5.3.

In §
4
, we work with more general valued fields. We consider the class of henselian valued fields (not necessarily of rank
1
)
(K,v)
of residue characteristic
p>0
with
\mathcal{O}_{K}/(p)
semi-perfect, together with a distinguished
\varpi\in\mathfrak{m}\backslash\{0\}
such that
\mathcal{O}_{K}[\varpi^{-1}]
is algebraically maximal. This class clearly includes perfectoid fields and, unlike perfectoid fields, forms an elementary class. In this generality, one gets a slightly weaker conclusion for the coarsening
w
, namely that it is
roughly tame
.
In §
3.3.7
, we show that Kuhlmann’s results also hold in the broader context of roughly tame fields.
This method of “taming the valuation” enables us to prove a new Ax-Kochen/Ershov principle, which is the crucial ingredient for Part II but which is also of independent interest. Before discussing this, we wish to clarify the connection with the almost purity theorem which was hinted at above.

1.6.
Almost mathematics

Part I of our main theorem is a non-standard version of the almost purity theorem over perfectoid valuation rings:

Theorem

(Almost purity)
.

Let
K
be a perfectoid field and
K^{\prime}/K
be a finite extension. Then
K^{\prime}/K
is almost unramified. More precisely, the extension
\mathcal{O}_{K^{\prime}}/\mathcal{O}_{K}
is almost finite étale.

The notion “almost étale” is made precise in almost mathematics, introduced by Faltings
[
Fal02
]
and developed systematically in the work of Gabber-Ramero
[
GR03
]
.
We note that almost purity holds more generally over perfectoid
K
-algebras, see
[
Fal02
]
and
[
Sch12
]
.
The version over perfectoid valuation rings stated here is often attributed to Tate
[
Tat67
]
and Gabber-Ramero
[
GR03
]
(cf. Theorem 2.4
[
Sch12a
]
). We first explain some of the key ideas in almost mathematics and then clarify their model-theoretic content.

1.6.1.

For a concise summary, see §4
[
Sch12
]
. We start with an easy example of an almost étale extension. Fix
p\neq 2
and let
K
be the
p
-adic completion of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(p^{1/p^{\infty}})
and
K^{\prime}=K(p^{1/2})
. Classically,
K^{\prime}/K
is a totally ramified extension. However,
\mathcal{O}_{K^{\prime}}/\mathcal{O}_{K}
is in some sense very close to being étale. To see this, let us focus at a finite level. Consider the local fields
K_{n}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(p^{1/p^{n}})
and
K_{n}^{\prime}=K_{n}(p^{1/2})
and note that
\mathcal{O}_{K^{\prime}_{n}}=\mathcal{O}_{K_{n}}[p^{1/2p^{n}}]
. To see if
\mathcal{O}_{K^{\prime}_{n}}/\mathcal{O}_{K_{n}}
is étale, one can compute the module of Kähler differentials and check whether it is zero. Although this is not quite the case here, we have that the module is killed by
p^{1/p^{n}}
, i.e.,

p^{1/p^{n}}\cdot\Omega_{\mathcal{O}_{K_{n}^{\prime}}/\mathcal{O}_{K_{n}}}=0

For large
n
, the element
p^{1/p^{n}}
is very close to being a unit and
\Omega_{\mathcal{O}_{K_{n}^{\prime}}/\mathcal{O}_{K_{n}}}
is thus close to being zero. As for the module
\Omega_{\mathcal{O}_{K^{\prime}/K}}
, we get that
\mathfrak{m}\cdot\Omega_{\mathcal{O}_{K^{\prime}}/\mathcal{O}_{K}}=0
by passing to direct limits. In almost mathematics, such a module is called
almost zero
and must be ignored.
We now outline the general setup which makes all this precise.

Let
K
be a rank-
1
valued field with a non-discrete valuation (e.g., a perfectoid field) and
\mathcal{O}_{K}
denote its valuation ring. We write
\mathcal{O}_{K}{\operatorname{-Mod}}
for the category of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-modules. In order to ignore
\mathfrak{m}
-torsion, we pass to the Serre (or Gabriel) quotient

\mathcal{O}_{K}^{a}{\operatorname{-Mod}}=\mathcal{O}_{K}{\operatorname{-Mod}}/(\mathfrak{m}\text{-}\mbox{torsion modules})

The resulting category
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{a}{\operatorname{-Mod}}
is called the category of
almost modules
. It comes with a natural
almostification
(or localization) functor

\mathcal{O}_{K}{\operatorname{-Mod}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{K}^{a}{\operatorname{-Mod}}:M\mapstochar\rightarrow M^{a}

To understand the nature of almost modules, we make a few observations. Note that if
\mathfrak{m}\cdot M=0
, then certainly
M\otimes K=0
. Therefore, the functor
\mathcal{O}_{K}{\operatorname{-Mod}}\rightarrow K{\operatorname{-Mod}}:M\mapstochar\rightarrow M\otimes K
factors naturally through
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{a}{\operatorname{-Mod}}
as in the diagram below

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{K}{\operatorname{-Mod}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K{\operatorname{-Mod}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{K}^{a}{\operatorname{-Mod}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\mapstochar\rightarrow M\otimes K}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\mapstochar\rightarrow M^{a}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{a}\mapstochar\rightarrow M\otimes K}

Thus, given an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-module
M
, the object
M^{a}
can be viewed as a slightly generic (or almost integral) structure, sitting between the integral structure
M
and its generic fiber
M\otimes K
. However, as there are no intermediate rings
\mathcal{O}_{K}\subsetneq R\subsetneq K
, the object
M^{a}
does not actually arise from a localization of the base ring
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. There is not even an underlying set of the object
M^{a}
. It will still be useful to think of
M^{a}
as an algebraic object, but the formal development of the theory will inevitably be abstract.

One then proceeds to develop ring theory within
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{a}{\operatorname{-Mod}}
in a hybrid fashion, half-algebraic and half-categorical.
After some work, one arrives at the notion of an étale extension in the almost context.
An important theme in almost mathematics is that properties over the generic fiber extend to the almost integral level (see §4
[
Sch12
]
). This is reflected in the almost purity theorem; étaleness at the generic level implies étaleness at the almost integral level.

1.6.2.

To see how model theory enters the picture, let us return to the example of the quadratic extension
K^{\prime}/K
from §
1.6.1
. In that situation, it would have really been convenient to take the “limit” of
p^{1/p^{n}}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
and produce some kind of unit element. We cannot make sense of that limit topologically but we can model-theoretically. Write
K_{U}
and
K_{U}^{\prime}
for the corresponding ultrapowers. We can now form the ultralimit
\pi=\text{ulim}\ p^{1/p^{n}}
and use Łoś to write
K^{\prime}_{U}=K_{U}(\pi^{1/2})
. Ramification still persists in
K_{U}^{\prime}/K_{U}
but is now detected only at the infinitesimal level. To make it disappear, we just need lower resolution lenses: Let
w
(resp.
w^{\prime}
) be the coarsest coarsenings with
wp>0
(resp.
w^{\prime}p>0
). This has the effect of collapsing infinitesimal valuations to zero; e.g., we have that
w\pi=0
. One easily checks that
\mathcal{O}_{w^{\prime}}=\mathcal{O}_{w}[\pi^{1/2}]
and hence
\mathcal{O}_{w^{\prime}}/\mathcal{O}_{w}
is an honest finite étale extension.
We spell out the details in Example
7.2.16
.
Next, we outline how this fits into a bigger picture.

Let
S\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{v_{U}}
be the set of elements of
infinitesimal
valuation, i.e., smaller than any rational multiple of
v\varpi
. Given an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-module
M
, it is not hard to check that

\mathfrak{m}\cdot M=0\iff S^{-1}M_{U}=0

The module
S^{-1}M_{U}
is a slightly generic fiber of the ultrapower
M_{U}
, now in a literal sense.
This yields an exact faithful monoidal functor of tensor abelian categories

S^{-1}({-})_{U}:\mathcal{O}_{K}^{a}{\operatorname{-Mod}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{w}{\operatorname{-Mod}}:M^{a}\mapstochar\rightarrow S^{-1}M_{U}

Crucially, it also preserves tensor products under some finite type assumption on the almost modules being tensored.
After writing this paper, O. Gabber informed us that he recently used a similar construction to simplify a step in the proof of the direct summand conjecture (see Remark
7.2.17
).

With the functor
S^{-1}({-})_{U}
, we achieve a systematic translation from almost ring theory to usual ring theory. However, we believe that this is not just a matter of phrasing things in a different language. Having actual algebraic objects to work with enables us to see some new phenomena. Such is the case of Part II, which we discuss next.

1.7.
Part II: Embedding the tilt in the residue field

1.7.1.

We first describe the embedding of
K^{\flat}
in
k_{w}
(see §
6.1
). Since
K^{\flat}=\varprojlim_{x\mapstochar\rightarrow x^{p}}K
, we have a natural embedding of multiplicative monoids

\natural:K^{\flat}\rightarrow K_{U}:(x,x^{1/p},...)\mapstochar\rightarrow\text{ulim}\ x^{1/p^{n}}

It is also additive modulo
p
and the image is contained in
\mathcal{O}_{w}^{\times}
. Composing it with the reduction map of
w
, gives us a valued field embedding
\iota:(K^{\flat},v^{\flat})\hookrightarrow(k_{w},\overline{v})
. Moreover, the induced embedding
\mathcal{O}_{K^{\flat}}/(t)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\overline{v}}/(\iota(t))
is
essentially
a diagonal embedding into an ultrapower (i.e., modulo some non-standard Frobenius twist) and is elementary by Łoś.

1.7.2.

In order to show that
\iota:(K^{\flat},v^{\flat})\hookrightarrow(k_{w},\overline{v})
is elementary, we prove an Ax-Kochen/Ershov principle:

Theorem 1.7.3
.

Let
(K,v)\subseteq(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
be two henselian valued fields of residue characteristic
p>0
such that
\mathcal{O}_{v}/(p)
and
\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}/(p)
are semi-perfect. Suppose there is
\varpi\in\mathfrak{m}_{v}\backslash\{0\}
such that both
\mathcal{O}_{v}[\varpi^{-1}]
and
\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}[\varpi^{-1}]
are algebraically maximal. Then:

(K,v)\preceq(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})\iff\mathcal{O}_{v}/(\varpi)\preceq\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}/(\varpi)\mbox{ and }\Gamma_{v}\preceq\Gamma_{v^{\prime}}

Moreover, if
\Gamma_{v}
and
\Gamma_{v^{\prime}}
are regularly dense, then the value group condition on the right hand side can be omitted.

We refer the reader to §5, which also contains analogous Ax-Kochen/Ershov principles for elementary equivalence (over a base) and existential closedness (see §
5
). All proofs go via a coarsening argument, namely by “taming” the valuations as outlined in §
1.5.3
and thereby reducing to a setting where Kuhlmann’s theory applies. These new Ax-Kochen/Ershov principles are also of independent interest, as they uncover some new model-theoretic phenomena, outlined below.

1.7.4.
Other applications

It is an open problem whether
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)^{h}
is an elementary substructure of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)
and, alas, the theory of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)
remains unknown. In §
5.2
, we obtain the following “perfected” variant:

Corollary 1.7.5
.

The perfect hull of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)^{h}
is an elementary substructure of the perfect hull of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)
.

Unfortunately, the theory of the perfect hull of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)
still appears to be intractable. By “taming” the valuation, the difficulties do not disappear but rather get absorbed in the truncated valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{v}/(t)
. Still, for certain concrete situations—like Corollary
1.7.5
—one may directly compare the two truncated valuation rings and see that they are equal on the nose. Other Ax-Kochen/Ershov phenomena also become accessible in this fashion and are collected in §
5.2
.

As another application, we obtain a converse to the decidability transfer theorem of
[
Kar24
]
:

Corollary 1.7.6
.

The
L_{\text{val}}
-theory of
K^{\flat}
is decidable relative to the one of
K
.

Finally, we show that tilting respects elementary equivalence over a base:

Corollary 1.7.7
.

Let
K_{1},K_{2}
be two perfectoid fields extending a perfectoid field
K
. Then:

(K_{1},v_{1})\equiv_{(K,v)}(K_{2},v_{2})\iff(K_{1}^{\flat},v_{1}^{\flat})\equiv_{(K^{\flat},v^{\flat})}(K_{2}^{\flat},v_{2}^{\flat})

We stress that working over a base perfectoid field is essential, as there are numerous examples of perfectoid fields which all have the same tilt but which are not elementary equivalent. This is reminiscent of the fact that tilting yields an equivalence between the categories of perfectoid field extensions of
K
and those of
K^{\flat}
, but not between the categories of perfectoid fields of characteristic zero and those of characteristic
p
.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Ultraproducts

Fix an ultrafilter
U
on some index set
I
. Given a language
L
and a sequence
(M_{i})_{i\in I}
of
L
-structures, we let
\prod_{i\in I}M_{i}/U
be the associated
ultraproduct
. The underlying set of
\prod_{i\in I}M_{i}/U
is the quotient
\{(a_{i})_{i\in I}:a_{i}\in M_{i}\}/\sim
, where
(a_{i})_{i\in I}\sim(b_{i})_{i\in I}
if and only if
\{i\in I:a_{i}=b_{i}\}\in U
. We will write
\text{ulim}\ a_{i}
for the equivalence class of
(a_{i})_{i\in I}
in the ultraproduct. The ultraproduct
\prod_{i\in I}M_{i}/U
readily becomes an
L
-structure. We will often use the more succinct notation
\text{ulim}\ M_{i}
for the ultraproduct. In case
M_{i}=M
, the structure
\prod_{i\in I}M/U
is called the
ultrapower
of
M
along
U
and is denoted by
M_{U}
.

2.1.1.
Łoś’ Theorem

If the
M_{i}
’s are groups (resp. rings, fields, valued fields etc.), then so is the ultraproduct. More generally, Łoś’ Theorem ensures that any elementary property which holds for
almost all
of the
M_{i}
’s will also hold in the ultraproduct (and vice versa):

Fact 2.1.2

(Łoś’ Theorem)
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and choose
a_{i}\in M_{i}^{n}
for each
i\in I
. For any
L
-formula
\phi(x)
with
x=(x_{1},...,x_{n})
, one has that

\{i\in I:M_{i}\models\phi(a_{i})\}\in U\iff\prod_{i\in I}M_{i}/U\models\phi(\text{ulim}\ a_{i})

We identify
M
as a substructure of
M_{U}
via the diagonal embedding

\delta:M\rightarrow M_{U}:m\mapstochar\rightarrow\text{ulim}\ m

which is elementary by Łoś’ Theorem.

2.1.3.
Saturation

We refer the reader to §6.1
[
CK90
]
for a detailed discussion on ultraproducts and saturation.

Definition 2.1.4
.

Fix an infinite cardinal
\kappa
. Given an
L
-structure
M
, we say that
M
is
\kappa
-saturated
if for any subset
A\subseteq M
with
|A|<\kappa
, every collection
(D_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I}
of
A
-definable sets having the finite intersection property has non-empty intersection. We say that
M
is
saturated
if it is
|M|
-saturated.

Fact 2.1.5

(Theorem 5.1.13
[
CK90
]
)
.

Let
M
and
M^{\prime}
be two elementary equivalent saturated
L
-structures of the same size. Then
M\cong M^{\prime}
.

Remark 2.1.6
.

(i)

By Lemma 5.1.4
[
CK90
]
, it is always possible to achieve
\kappa
-saturation by passing to a suitable elementary extension.

(ii)

Contrary to (i), the existence of saturated models is not guaranteed in general without further background set-theoretic assumptions (GCH). It is nevertheless harmless to assume it for certain tasks at hand and later remove it by an absoluteness argument, cf.
[
HK23
]
.

If
L
is countable and
\kappa=\aleph_{1}
, then
M
is
\aleph_{1}
-saturated precisely when every decreasing chain of non-empty definable subsets with parameters from
M
, say

has non-empty intersection. Structures in everyday mathematical practice often fail to be
\aleph_{1}
-saturated:

Example 2.1.7
.

The ordered set
(\mathbb{N},<)
is not
\aleph_{1}
-saturated because one can take

and clearly
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}D_{n}=\emptyset
.

On the other hand, non-principal ultraproducts are always
\aleph_{1}
-saturated:

Fact 2.1.8

(Theorem 6.1.1
[
CK90
]
)
.

Let
(M_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of
L
-structures and
U
be a non-principal ultrafilter on

. Then the ultraproduct
\text{ulim}\ M_{i}
is
\aleph_{1}
-saturated.

In particular, it is possible to achieve
\aleph_{1}
-saturation by passing to a non-principal ultrapower, something which will be used extensively throughout.

2.1.9.
Some ultracommutative algebra

Let
I
be an index set and
(R_{i})_{i\in I}
be a sequence of rings. We work in the product category

\prod_{i\in I}(R_{i}{\operatorname{-Mod}})

whose objects are sequences
(M_{i})_{i\in I}
, where
M_{i}
is an
R_{i}
-module, and where morphisms are given as follows

\text{Hom}((M_{i})_{i\in I},(N_{i})_{i\in I})=\prod_{i\in I}\text{Hom}(M_{i},N_{i})

Remark 2.1.10
.

The following observations are clear:

(i)

The category
\prod(R_{i}{\operatorname{-Mod}})
is an abelian tensor category, where kernels, cokernels and tensor products are defined in the unique way compatible with their definition in
R_{i}{\operatorname{-Mod}}
. For instance, we have

(M_{i})_{i\in I}\otimes(N_{i})_{i\in I}=(M_{i}\otimes N_{i})_{i\in I}

(ii)

If
R_{i}=R
, we have a diagonal functor

\Delta:R{\operatorname{-Mod}}\rightarrow(R{\operatorname{-Mod}})^{I}:M\mapstochar\rightarrow(M,M,...)

which is an exact faithful functor which preserves tensor products.

Definition 2.1.11
.

Let
(R_{i})_{i\in I}
be a sequence of rings. For each
i\in I
, let
M_{i}
be an
R_{i}
-module.

(i)

The sequence
(M_{i})_{i\in I}
is called
uniformly finitely generated
if there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that each
M_{i}
can be generated by
m
elements as an
R_{i}
-module.

(ii)

The sequence
(M_{i})_{i\in I}
is called
uniformly finitely presented
if there exist
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
such that for each
M_{i}
there exists a short exact sequence

0\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow R_{i}^{m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{i}}}{{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}}R_{i}^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi_{i}}}{{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}}M_{i}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow 0

for some maps
\phi_{i}
and
\psi_{i}
.

Lemma 2.1.12
.

Let
I
be a set and
U
be an ultrafilter on
I
. Let
(R_{i})_{i\in I}
be a sequence of rings and
R=\text{ulim}\ R_{i}
. Consider the assignment

\mathcal{U}:\prod_{i\in I}(R_{i}{\operatorname{-Mod}})\rightarrow R{\operatorname{-Mod}}:(M_{i})_{i\in I}\mapstochar\rightarrow\text{ulim}\ M_{i}

acting on morphisms in the obvious way. Then:

(i)

\mathcal{U}
is an exact functor.

(ii)

\mathcal{U}
is monoidal, i.e., for any
R_{i}
-modules
M_{i},N_{i}
we have a natural morphism

F:\text{ulim}\ M_{i}\otimes_{R}\text{ulim}\ N_{i}\rightarrow\text{ulim}\ (M_{i}\otimes_{R_{i}}N_{i})

sending
\text{ulim}\ a_{i}\otimes\text{ulim}\ b_{i}\mapstochar\rightarrow\text{ulim}\ (a_{i}\otimes b_{i})
.

(iii)

The map
F
from (ii) is an isomorphism if
(M_{i})_{i\in I}
and
(N_{i})_{i\in I}
are uniformly finitely generated.

Proof.

(i) It is clear that
\mathcal{U}
is a functor. For exactness, note that for a sequence of maps
f_{i}:M_{i}\rightarrow N_{i}
of
R_{i}
-module we have
\text{Ker}(\text{ulim}\ f_{i})=\text{ulim}\ \text{Ker}(f_{i})
(resp.
\text{Im}(\text{ulim}\ f_{i})=\text{ulim}\ \text{Im}(f_{i})
) by Łoś.

(ii) This is Lemma 3.1
[
DR19
]
; we briefly recall the argument. Consider the bilinear map

\text{ulim}\ M_{i}\times\text{ulim}\ N_{i}\rightarrow\text{ulim}\ (M_{i}\otimes_{R_{i}}N_{i}):(\text{ulim}\ a_{i},\text{ulim}\ b_{i})\mapstochar\rightarrow\text{ulim}\ (a_{i}\otimes b_{i})

By the universal property for tensor products, this naturally induces a morphism
F
of
R
-modules

F:\text{ulim}\ M_{i}\otimes_{R}\text{ulim}\ N_{i}\rightarrow\text{ulim}\ (M_{i}\otimes_{R_{i}}N_{i})

mapping
\text{ulim}\ a_{i}\otimes\text{ulim}\ b_{i}\mapstochar\rightarrow\text{ulim}\ (a_{i}\otimes b_{i})
and extending additively to all of
\text{ulim}\ M_{i}\otimes_{R}\text{ulim}\ N_{i}
.

(iii) Let
M_{i}=\langle f_{1,i},...,f_{s,i}\rangle
and
N_{i}=\langle g_{1,i},...,g_{s,i}\rangle
.
Set
f_{j}=\text{ulim}\ f_{j,i}
and
g_{k}=\text{ulim}\ g_{k,i}
. Suppose that
a_{1},...,a_{s}\in\text{ulim}\ M_{i}
are such that

0=F(\sum_{j=1}^{s}a_{j}\otimes g_{j})=\text{ulim}\ \sum_{j=1}^{s}a_{j,i}\otimes g_{j,i}

where
a_{j}=\text{ulim}\ a_{j,i}
. For almost all
i
, we will then have that
\sum_{j=1}^{s}a_{j,i}\otimes g_{j,i}=0
. By Tag 04VX
[
Stacks
]
, this gives

such that

a_{j,i}=\sum_{k=1}^{s}c_{kj,i}f_{k,i}\mbox{ and }\sum_{j=1}^{s}c_{kj,i}g_{j,i}=0

for almost all
i
. Let
c_{kj}=\text{ulim}\ c_{kj,i}
and note that

a_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{s}c_{kj}f_{k}\mbox{ and }\sum_{j=1}^{s}c_{kj}g_{j}=0

This implies that
\sum_{j=1}^{s}a_{j}\otimes g_{j}=0
, which means that
F
is injective. To see that
F
is also surjective, observe that
\text{ulim}\ (M_{i}\otimes_{R_{i}}N_{i})=\langle\text{ulim}\ f_{j,i}\otimes g_{k,i}:1\leq j,k\leq s\rangle
and that
F(f_{j}\otimes g_{k})=\text{ulim}\ f_{j,i}\otimes g_{k,i}
.
∎

2.2.
Ordered abelian groups

Throughout the paper, we view ordered abelian groups as
L_{\text{oag}}
-structures, where
L_{\text{oag}}=\{0,+,<\}
. We will need a special class of ordered abelian groups which was studied by Robinson-Zakon
[
RZ60
]
:

Definition 2.2.1
.

An ordered abelian group
\Gamma
is called
regularly dense
if for any given
\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that
n\cdot\gamma\in(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
.

The typical example is a dense additive subgroup of
\mathbb{R}
. In fact, any regularly dense ordered abelian group is elementary equivalent to such a subgroup. Regularly dense ordered abelian groups are readily seen to be
almost divisible
, i.e., if
\{0\}\subsetneq\Delta\subseteq\Gamma
is any proper convex subgroup, then
\Gamma/\Delta
is divisible.

For later use, we record the following:

Lemma 2.2.2
.

Suppose
\Gamma\preceq_{\exists}\Gamma^{\prime}
for two ordered abelian groups and
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Suppose
\Gamma
is
\aleph_{1}
-saturated and let
\Delta
(resp.
\Delta^{\prime}
) be the maximal convex subgroup of
\Gamma
(resp.
\Gamma^{\prime}
) not containing
\gamma
. Then
\Gamma/\Delta\preceq_{\exists}\Gamma^{\prime}/\Delta^{\prime}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that any system of linear equations and inequalities with parameters from
\Gamma/\Delta
, which has a solution in
\Gamma^{\prime}/\Delta^{\prime}
, also has a solution in
\Gamma/\Delta
.
Let
A_{1},A_{2}\in M_{n\times n}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma^{n}
. Suppose
\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{\prime n}
is such that

A_{1}\cdot\gamma^{\prime}+\gamma_{1}\in\Delta^{\prime n}\mbox{ and }A_{2}\cdot\gamma^{\prime}+\gamma_{2}>\Delta^{\prime n}

The former condition means that
A_{1}\cdot\gamma^{\prime}+\gamma_{1}\in(-\frac{1}{m}\gamma,\frac{1}{m}\gamma)^{n}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. The latter condition means that there exists
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that
A_{2}\cdot\gamma^{\prime}+\gamma_{2}\in(\frac{1}{M}\gamma,\infty)^{n}
. Since
\Gamma\preceq_{\exists}\Gamma^{\prime}
, for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
we can find
\gamma_{m}\in\Gamma^{n}
such that

A_{1}\cdot\gamma_{m}+\gamma_{1}\in(-\frac{1}{m}\gamma,\frac{1}{m}\gamma)^{n}\mbox{ and }A_{2}\cdot\gamma_{m}+\gamma_{2}\in(\frac{1}{M}\gamma,\infty)^{n}

Since
\Gamma
is
\aleph_{1}
-saturated, there is
\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma^{n}
satisfying the above conditions simultaneously for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular, this means that

A_{1}\cdot\gamma_{0}+\gamma_{1}\in\Delta^{\prime}\mbox{ and }A_{2}\cdot\gamma_{0}+\gamma_{2}>\Delta^{\prime}

which is what we wanted to show.
∎

2.3.
Coarsenings of valuations

Our exposition follows closely §7.4
[
Dri14
]
. Let
(K,v)
be a valued field with valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{v}
, value group
\Gamma_{v}
and residue field

\mathcal{O}_{v}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{w}

2.3.1.
Coarsenings and convex subgroups

Recall the usual 1-1 correspondence between coarsenings of

\Gamma_{v}
:

•

Given a convex subgroup
\Delta\subseteq\Gamma_{v}
, we define a new valuation

w:K^{\times}\rightarrow\Gamma_{v}/\Delta:x\mapstochar\rightarrow vx+\Delta

One readily verifies that
\mathcal{O}_{w}=\{x\in K:vx>\delta\mbox{ for some }\delta\in\Delta\}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{w}=\{x\in K:vx>\Delta\}
.

•

Conversely, a coarsening
w
of

gives rise to the convex subgroup
\Delta=\{vx:x\in K\mbox{ and }wx=0\}
.

which allows us to identify

Recall that a

The following is well-known but we sketch a proof for completeness.

Lemma 2.3.7

(cf. Theorem 1.13
[

]

Let
(K,v)
be an
\aleph_{1}
-saturated valued field and
\varpi\in\mathfrak{m}_{v}
. Let
w
be the coarsest coarsening of

such that
w\varpi>0
and
w^{+}
be the finest coarsening of

w^{+}\varpi=0

(k_{w^{+}},\overline{w})
is spherically complete with value group isomorphic to either
\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Let
B_{1}\supseteq B_{2}\supseteq...

(k_{w^{+}},\overline{w})

\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}B_{n}\neq\emptyset

\tilde{B}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{w}
by replacing
\tilde{B}_{i}
with
\tilde{B}_{i}\cap\mathcal{O}_{w}
if necessary. We then lift the radius of
\tilde{B}_{i}
(i.e., choose a representative of the corresponding element in
\Gamma_{v}/\Delta_{0}
) to obtain a ball
B_{i}^{\prime}
in
(K,v)
with the same center. This yields a countable decreasing chain of closed balls
B_{1}^{\prime}\supseteq B_{2}^{\prime}\supseteq...
in
(K,v)
. These balls are (nonempty) definable sets and by
\aleph_{1}
-saturation we have
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}B_{i}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
. But clearly
B_{i}^{\prime}\subseteq\tilde{B}_{i}
and therefore we can find some
a\in\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\tilde{B}_{i}
. Since
\tilde{B}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{w}
, we get
a\in\mathcal{O}_{w}
and thus
b\in\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\tilde{B}_{i}\neq\emptyset
, where
b=a\mod\mathfrak{m}_{w^{+}}
.
For the value group assertion, we have that
\Gamma_{\overline{w}}
is either discrete or dense, being an additive subgroup of
\mathbb{R}
. In the latter case, we also get that every Dedekind cut is realized in
\Gamma_{\overline{w}}
from the
\aleph_{1}
-saturation of
\Gamma_{v}
. Thus,
\Gamma_{\overline{w}}=\mathbb{R}
.
∎

2.4.
Fundamental inequality

We restrict ourselves to
henselian
fields, see §3.3
[
EP05
]
for a more general treatment. Given a henselian valued field
(K,v)
and a finite extension
K^{\prime}/K
of degree
n
, let
v^{\prime}
be the unique extension of
v
to
K^{\prime}
. We write
\Gamma_{v}
(resp.
\Gamma_{v^{\prime}}
) and
k_{v}
(resp.
k_{v^{\prime}}
) for the value groups and residue fields. We then have the
fundamental inequality

n\geq e(v^{\prime}/v)\cdot f(v^{\prime}/v)

where
e(v^{\prime}/v)=[\Gamma_{v^{\prime}}:\Gamma_{v}]
is the
ramification degree
and
f(v^{\prime}/v)=[k_{v^{\prime}}:k_{v}]
is the
inertia degree
.

2.4.1.
Defect

The multplicative error in the fundamental inequality, namely

d(v^{\prime}/v)=\frac{n}{e(v^{\prime}/v)\cdot f(v^{\prime}/v)}

is called the
defect
of the extension. By the Lemma of Ostrowski, the defect is always a power of the characteristic exponent of
k_{v}
. In particular, it is equal to
1
in case
\text{char}(k_{v})=0
. We say that
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})/(K,v)
(or
\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}/\mathcal{O}_{v}
) is
defectless
if
d(v^{\prime}/v)=1
. Otherwise we say that
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})/(K,v)
(or
\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}/\mathcal{O}_{v}
) is a defect extension. The valued field
(K,v)
is called
defectless
if any finite extension as above is defectless. In that case, we also say that
\mathcal{O}_{v}
(or just
v
) is defectless.

Fact 2.4.2
.

Spherically complete fields are henselian and defectless.

Proof.

See Proposition 15, pg. 163
[
BGR84
]
.
∎

2.4.3.
Immediate extensions

An important special case of defect extensions are the immediate ones. We say that
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})/(K,v)
(or
\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}/\mathcal{O}_{v}
) as above is an
immediate
extension if
e(v^{\prime}/v)=f(v^{\prime}/v)=1
. Note that if
K^{\prime}/K
is a proper extension, then
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})/(K,v)
is indeed a defect extension. We say that
(K,v)
(or
\mathcal{O}_{v}
, or
v
) is
algebraically maximal
if
(K,v)
does not admit any proper finite immediate extensions.

2.4.4.
Unramified extensions

There are several equivalent definitions of “unramified” in the literature, which we briefly review below.

Definition 2.4.5
.

A finite extension of henselian fields
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})/(K,v)
of degree
n
is
unramified
if
n=f(v^{\prime}/v)
and
k_{v^{\prime}}/k_{v}
is separable.

We also say that
K^{\prime}/K
is
unramified with respect to

\mathcal{O}_{v}
. We note that our definition of “unramified” is not universally accepted, namely some sources do not require the extension to be defectless (e.g.,
[
End72
]
) while others do (e.g.,
[
Kuh10
]
).

Under our henselianity assumption, this is compatible with the notion of
\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}/\mathcal{O}_{v}
being unramified in the sense of commutative algebra:

Definition 2.4.6

(Tag 00US
[
Stacks
]
))
.

A ring map
R\rightarrow S
is unramified if
S
is a finitely generated
R
-algebra and
\Omega_{S/R}=0
. We also say that
S/R
is unramified.

Given a field
K
and
a\in\overline{K}
with minimal polynomial
m_{a}(X)\in K[X]
, we write
\delta(a)=m_{a}^{\prime}(a)
for the
different
of the element
a
. Given
f(X)=X^{n}+...+a_{1}X+a_{0}\in K[X]
with roots
r_{1},...,r_{n}\in\overline{K}
(not necessarily distinct), we define the
discriminant
of
f(X)
as

\text{disc}(f)=(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\prod_{i<j}(r_{i}-r_{j})^{2}

The discriminant is a symmetric polynomial in the roots and can therefore be expressed as an integer polynomial in the
a_{i}
’s (see Theorem H.1
[
Rot90
]
).

Fact 2.4.7
.

Let
(K,v)
be a henselian valued field and
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})/(K,v)
be a finite extension. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})/(K,v)
is unramified.

(ii)

There exists
a\in\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}
such that
\delta(a)\in\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}^{\times}
and
\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}=\mathcal{O}_{v}[a]
.

(iii)

There exists
a\in\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}
such that
\delta(a)\in\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}^{\times}
and
K^{\prime}=K(a)
.

(iv)

There exists a monic irreducible polynomial

such that
\text{disc}(f)\in\mathcal{O}_{v}^{\times}
and

(v)

\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}/\mathcal{O}_{v}
is unramified.

(vi)

\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}/\mathcal{O}_{v}
is étale.

Proof.

We first prove that (i)
\iff
(ii)
\iff
(iii)
\iff
(iv).

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): Since
k_{v^{\prime}}/k_{v}
is finite separable, we can write
k_{v^{\prime}}=k_{v}(\alpha)
for some

with
\delta(\alpha)\neq 0
. Let

be any lift of
\alpha
. Note that

[K^{\prime}:K]\geq[K(a):K]\geq[k_{v}(\alpha):k_{v}]

Since
[K^{\prime}:K]=[k_{v^{\prime}}:k_{v}]
, equality holds everywhere and

. It follows that
\overline{m_{a}}(X)=m_{\alpha}(X)
and hence
\overline{\delta(a)}=\delta(\alpha)
. Since
\delta(\alpha)\neq 0
, we have
\delta(a)\in\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}^{\times}
. We finally check that
\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}=\mathcal{O}_{v}[a]
. The inclusion
\mathcal{O}_{v}[a]\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}
is clear. For the other inclusion, let
n=\text{deg}(m_{a})
and suppose that
c_{0}+c_{1}\cdot a+...+c_{n-1}\cdot a^{n-1}\in\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}
for some

(not all zero). Let

be such that

. Observe that

since the left hand side is a non-zero polynomial in
\alpha
with coefficients in
k_{v}
of degree

. It follows that

c_{0}c_{i}^{-1}+c_{1}c_{i}^{-1}\cdot a+...+c_{n-1}c_{i}^{-1}\cdot a^{n-1}\in\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}^{\times}

and therefore

v(c_{i})=v^{\prime}(c_{0}+c_{1}\cdot a+...+c_{n-1}\cdot a^{n-1})\geq 0

We conclude that
v(c_{j})\geq 0
for all
j
and
\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}=\mathcal{O}_{v}[a]
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii): Clear.

(iii)
\iff
(iv): For any field
K
and

with minimal polynomial
m_{a}(X)\in K[X]
, one checks that
\text{disc}(m_{a})=m_{a}^{\prime}(a)^{2}=\delta(a)^{2}
. The rest is easy.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i): By a variant of Hensel’s Lemma (see Theorem 4.1.3
[
EP05
]
) and since

\overline{m_{a}}(X)=f(X)^{s}

\alpha

and

We have established (i)
\iff
(ii)
\iff
(iii)
\iff
(iv).

(ii)
\Rightarrow

is a finitely generated

-algebra. A direct computation shows that

(v)
\Rightarrow

We also have that

(vi)

is contained in the inertia field of

is henselian, this means that

is unramified.
∎

3.
Perfectoid and tame fields

3.1.
Perfectoid fields

Definition 3.1.1
.

A perfectoid field is a complete valued field
(K,v)
of residue characteristic
p>0
such that
\Gamma_{v}
is a dense subgroup of
\mathbb{R}
and the Frobenius map
\Phi:\mathcal{O}_{K}/(p)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{K}/(p):x\mapstochar\rightarrow x^{p}
is surjective.

Remark 3.1.2
.

(i)

Modulo the other conditions, the last condition is equivalent to
\Phi:\mathcal{O}_{K}/(\varpi)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{K}/(\varpi):x\mapstochar\rightarrow x^{p}
being surjective for some (equivalently any)
\varpi\in\mathfrak{m}_{v}
with
0<v\varpi\leq vp
.

(ii)

In characteristic
p
, a perfectoid field is simply a perfect, complete non-archimedean valued field of rank
1
.

Example 3.1.3
.

(i)

The
p
-adic completions of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(p^{1/p^{\infty}})
,
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p^{\infty}})
and
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{ab}
are mixed characteristic perfectoid fields.

(ii)

The
t
-adic completions of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)^{1/p^{\infty}}
and
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)^{1/p^{\infty}}
are perfectoid fields of characteristic
p
.

3.2.
Tilting

A construction, originally due to Fontaine, transforms any perfectoid field
K
to a perfectoid field
K^{\flat}
of characteristic
p
, called the
tilt
of
K
. We do not insist that
\text{char}(K)=0
but the tilting construction is only interesting in that case (we have
K^{\flat}\cong K
if
\text{char}(K)=p
). We refer the reader to §3
[
Sch14
]
and §3
[
Sch12
]
for details.

As a set, we have
K^{\flat}=\{(x_{n})_{n\in\omega}\in K^{\omega}:x_{n+1}^{p}=x_{n}\}
. We define multiplication in
K^{\flat}
coordinatewise

(x_{n})_{n\in\omega}\cdot(y_{n})_{n\in\omega}=(x_{n}\cdot y_{n})_{n\in\omega}

Addition in
K^{\flat}
is a bit more involved and is described by the following rule

(x_{n})_{n\in\omega}+(y_{n})_{n\in\omega}=(z_{n})_{n\in\omega}\mbox{ where }z_{n}=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}(x_{n+m}^{p^{m}}+y_{n+m}^{p^{m}})

We have the
sharp
map from
K^{\flat}
to
K
, which simply picks out
x_{0}
, namely

\sharp:K^{\flat}\rightarrow K:(x_{n})_{n\in\omega}\mapstochar\rightarrow x_{0}

It is clearly a multiplicative morphism. We define a valuation
v^{\flat}
on
K^{\flat}
by

v^{\flat}(x)=v(x^{\sharp})

We collect some basic facts about tilting for later use:

Lemma 3.2.1
.

We have that
(K^{\flat},v^{\flat})
is a perfectoid field of characteristic
p
. Moreover:

(i)

We have that

\mathcal{O}_{K^{\flat}}=\{(x_{n})_{n\in\omega}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\omega}:x_{n+1}^{p}=x_{n}\}\mbox{ and }\mathfrak{m}_{K^{\flat}}=\{(x_{n})_{n\in\omega}\in\mathfrak{m}_{K}^{\omega}:x_{n+1}^{p}=x_{n}\}

(ii)

We have canonical identifications

\Gamma_{v^{\flat}}=\Gamma_{v}\mbox{ and }k_{v^{\flat}}=k_{v}

(iii)

Let
\varpi\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
be a
pseudo-uniformizer
, i.e., a non-zero element with
0<v\varpi\leq vp
. Then there exists
\varpi^{\flat}\in\mathcal{O}_{K^{\flat}}
such that

\mathcal{O}_{K}/(\varpi)\cong\mathcal{O}_{K^{\flat}}/(\varpi^{\flat})

(iv)

The sharp map
\sharp:K^{\flat}\rightarrow K
is a multiplicative morphism and is additive modulo
p
, i.e.,
(x+y)^{\sharp}\equiv x^{\sharp}+y^{\sharp}\mod p\mathcal{O}_{K}
for any
x,y\in\mathcal{O}_{K^{\flat}}
.

(v)

If
\text{char}(K)=p
, then
(K^{\flat},v^{\flat})\cong(K,v)
.

Proof.

See Lemma 3.4
[
Sch12
]
and Lemma 3.2
[
Sch14
]
.
∎

Remark 3.2.2

(Remark 3.5
[
Sch12
]
)
.

After replacing
\varpi
with a unit multiple, we can assume that
\varpi
comes equipped with a compatible system of
p
-power roots in
K
. We then let
\varpi^{\flat}=(\varpi,\varpi^{1/p},...)
.

For a detailed analysis of the examples below, see Corollary 4.4.3
[
Kar24
]
:

Example 3.2.3
.

In the examples below,
\widehat{K}
stands for the
p
-adic (resp.
t
-adic) completion of the field
K
depending on whether its characteristic is
0
or
p
.

(i)

\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{p}(p^{1/p^{\infty}})}^{\flat}\cong\widehat{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)^{1/p^{\infty}}}
and
t^{\sharp}=p
.

(ii)

\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p^{\infty}})}^{\flat}\cong\widehat{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)^{1/p^{\infty}}}
and
(t+1)^{\sharp}=\zeta_{p}
.

(iii)

\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{ab}}^{\flat}\cong\widehat{\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)^{1/p^{\infty}}}
and

3.3.
Tame fields

The algebra and model theory of tame fields was introduced and studied by Kuhlmann
[
Kuh16
]
. We provide an overview for the convenience of the reader, also in a slightly more general setting which will be useful later on.

3.3.1.
Basic algebra of (roughly) tame fields

Definition 3.3.2
.

Let
(K,v)
be a henselian valued field. A finite valued field extension
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})/(K,v)
is said to be tame if the following conditions hold:

(1)

If
p=\text{char}(k)>0
, then
p\nmid[\Gamma^{\prime}:\Gamma]
.

(2)

The residue field extension
k^{\prime}/k
is separable.

(3)

The extension
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})/(K,v)
is defectless.

Definition 3.3.3
.

A henselian valued field
(K,v)
is said to be
tame
if every finite valued field extension
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})/(K,v)
is tame.

A more intrinsic description of tame fields can be given:

Fact 3.3.4

(Theorem 3.2 (5)
[
Kuh16
]
)
.

Let
(K,v)
be henselian valued field of residue characteristic exponent
p
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

(K,v)
is a tame field.

(ii)

(K,v)
is algebraically maximal,
\Gamma
is
p
-divisible and
k
is perfect.

Kuhlmann-Rzepka
[
KR23
]
relax the value group condition, asking only that
\Gamma
be
roughly

p
-divisible in the sense of Johnson
[
Joh16
]
. This means that the interval
[-vp,vp]
is
p
-divisible.

Definition 3.3.5
.

A valued field
(K,v)
is called
roughly tame
if it is algebraically maximal,
\Gamma
is roughly
p
-divisible and
k
is perfect.

Remark 3.3.6
.

(i)

Note that
(-vp,vp)=\Gamma
if
\text{char}(K)=p
and
[-vp,vp]=\{0\}
if
\text{char}(k)=0
, so roughly tame is the same as tame in equal characteristic.

(ii)

In residue characteristic zero, being (roughly) tame is the same as being henselian. In mixed characteristic, a roughly tame valued field is one whose core field
(K^{\circ},v^{\circ})
is tame.

(iii)

Roughly tame fields are defectless. Indeed, roughly tame fields of equal characteristic are just tame fields and in the mixed characteristic case the valuation is the composite of a valuation of equal characteristic zero and a tame valuation, both of which are defectless.

3.3.7.
Model theory of roughly tame fields

notation

for the henselization of any valued field

for the henselization of

Recall from

Since

It follows that

is an unramified Galois extension of degree

. We can therefore write

, for some

such that

. We may even assume that

, by successively extracting
p
-th roots of

, if necessary (cf. §

). Since

and
\wp_{a}(X)
generate the same extension, we get that

c-a\in\wp(K^{\prime})\iff c^{p^{n}}-a^{p^{n}}\in\wp({\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)_{U})\mbox{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N}

\iff c-a^{p^{n}}\in\wp({\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)_{U})\mbox{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N}

The last equivalence uses that
c^{p^{n}}-c\in\wp({\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)_{U})
, which can be seen from

c^{p^{n}}-c=(c^{p^{n}}-c^{p^{n-1}})+(c^{p^{n-1}}-c^{p^{n-2}})+...+(c^{p}-c)

and by observing that each term lies in

.

Write
a^{p^{n}}=\text{ulim}\ b_{m}
, where

with

. For

, we see that

. Since

, we get that
1/t^{\ell^{m}}-b_{m}\notin\wp({\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!))
. It follows that

\{m\in\mathbb{N}:1/t^{\ell^{m}}-b_{m}\in\wp({\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!))\}\not\in U

which is contrary to the fact that

. We conclude that no such

exists and that

is an immediate extension.
∎
{}_{\textit{Claim}}

We thus have that
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
together with

is not in

. By Proposition

,

is an elementary class in

. We conclude that

.

which is elementary by Łoś. The rightmost isomorphism is the inverse of
\overline{\Phi}_{\infty}
. The composite map is equal to the desired map and is elementary.

(ii) By (i), we know that the map

\mathcal{O}_{K^{\flat}}/(\varpi^{\flat})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{v_{U}}/(\pi):x+(\varpi^{\flat})\mapstochar\rightarrow x^{\natural}+(\pi)

is an elementary embedding of rings. We also have a natural isomorphism

\mathcal{O}_{v_{U}}/(\pi)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\overline{v}}/(\overline{\pi}):x+(\pi)\mapstochar\rightarrow\overline{x}+(\overline{\pi})

induced from the reduction map of
w
.
Note that
\overline{\pi}=\iota(\varpi^{\flat})
, so that the map in question is the composite map

\mathcal{O}_{K^{\flat}}/(\varpi^{\flat})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\preceq}}{{\lhook\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}}\mathcal{O}_{v_{U}}/(\pi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}}\mathcal{O}_{\overline{v}}/(\overline{\pi})

which is elementary, being the composition of elementary maps.

(iii) We choose the elements
\varpi^{\flat}\in K^{\flat}
and
\overline{\pi}\in k_{w}
as distinguished pseudo-uniformizers. We identify
(K^{\flat},v^{\flat})
with a valued subfield of
(k_{w},\overline{v})
via
\iota
, thereby identifying
\varpi^{\flat}
with
\overline{\pi}
. Next, we verify the hypotheses of Theorem
5.1.4
for
K^{\flat}
and
k_{w}
. For
K^{\flat}
use Remark
4.2.4
(i) and for
k_{w}
use Lemma
4.2.6
(iii). The value groups
\Gamma_{v^{\flat}}
and
\Gamma_{\overline{v}}
are both regularly dense. For the latter, note that
\Gamma_{\overline{v}}
is a convex subgroup of the regularly dense group
\Gamma_{v_{U}}
. By part (i), we also have that
\mathcal{O}_{K^{\flat}}/(\varpi^{\flat})\preceq\mathcal{O}_{\overline{v}}/(\overline{\pi})
. By Theorem

, we conclude that
(K^{\flat},v^{\flat})\preceq(k_{w},\overline{v})
.
∎

6.2.
Specializing to Fontaine-Wintenberger

Recall the definition of the discriminant from §
2.4.4
.

Lemma 6.2.1
.

Let
(K,v)
be a henselian valued field of residue characteristic
p>0
and
\varpi\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
be such that
0<v\varpi\leq vp
. Let
f(X)=X^{n}+a_{n-1}X^{n-1}+...+a_{0}\in\mathcal{O}_{v}[X]
be an irreducible polynomial with
0\leq v(\text{disc}(f))<v\varpi
and
g(X)=X^{n}+a_{n-1}^{\prime}X^{n-1}+...+a_{0}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}_{v}[X]
be such that
a_{i}^{\prime p}\equiv a_{i}\mod(\varpi)
. Then
v(\text{disc}(g))=\frac{1}{p}v(\text{disc}(f))
and
K[X]/(f(X))\cong K[X]/(g(X))
.

Proof.

Write
L=K(\beta)
, with
\beta
a root of
g(X)
. Recall that the discriminant can be expressed as a polynomial in the coefficients. Since
0<v\varpi\leq vp
and
a_{i}^{\prime p}\equiv a_{i}\mod(\varpi)
, we deduce that
\text{disc}(g)^{p}\equiv\text{disc}(f)\mod(\varpi)
. Since
0\leq v(\text{disc}(f))<v\varpi
, it follows that
v(\text{disc}(g))=\frac{1}{p}v(\text{disc}(f))
. Since
a_{i}^{\prime p}\equiv a_{i}\mod(\varpi)
and
0<v\varpi\leq vp
, we compute that

0=g(\beta)^{p}\equiv f(\beta^{p})\mod(\varpi)\Rightarrow f(\beta^{p})\equiv 0\mod(\varpi)

Since
v(f(\beta^{p}))>v(\text{disc}(f))
, we deduce the existence of
\alpha\in L
with
f(\alpha)=0
by the Hensel-Rychlik Lemma (cf. Lemma 12
[
AxK65
]
). The conclusion follows.
∎

We henceforth identify
K^{\flat}
with its image in
k_{w}
via the embedding
\iota
of Lemma
6.1.2
(iii). Given
f(X)\in K^{\flat}[X]
, write
f^{\natural}(X)\in K_{U}[X]
for the polynomial whose coefficients are obtained by applying
\natural
to the coefficients of
f(X)
.

Corollary 6.2.2
.

Let
(K,v)
be a perfectoid field and
U
be a non-principal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
. Then:

(i)

Let
f(X)\in K^{\flat}[X]
be an irreducible monic polynomial. Then
f^{\natural}(X)
generates a finite extension of
K_{U}
coming from
K
.

(ii)

Conversely, let
L/K
be a finite extension. Then
L_{U}/K_{U}
induces a finite extension of
k_{w}
coming from
K^{\flat}
.

Proof.

(i) Once again, recall that the discriminant of a polynomial is itself an integer polynomial in the coefficients, in a way that does not depend on the field. By Lemma
6.1.2
(ii), we get that
\text{disc}(f^{\natural})\equiv\text{disc}(f)^{\natural}\mod(p)
. By Lemma
6.1.2
(i), we have
\text{disc}(f)^{\natural}\in\mathcal{O}_{w}^{\times}
and thus
\text{disc}(f^{\natural})\in\mathcal{O}_{w}^{\times}
. Write
f^{\natural}(X)=\text{ulim}\ f_{i}(X)
, where
f_{i}(X)\in K[X]
. By Łoś, we get that
0\leq\text{disc}(f_{i})<vp
for almost all
i
. By Lemma
6.2.1
, we get that the extensions of
K
generated by the
f_{i}
’s eventually stabilize to a fixed extension
L
of
K
. Therefore, the extension of
K_{U}
generated by
f^{\natural}(X)
comes from
L
.

(ii) By Theorem
4.3.1
(ii), we have that
L_{U}/K_{U}
is unramified with respect to
\mathcal{O}_{w}
. By Fact
2.4.7
(iv), we get that
L_{U}
is generated by some monic irreducible polynomial
F(X)\in\mathcal{O}_{v_{U}}[X]
with
\text{disc}(F)\in\mathcal{O}_{w}^{\times}
. In particular, we have that
0\leq v_{U}(\text{disc}(F))<v_{U}\varpi
. By Łoś, the extension
L
can be generated by some monic irreducible polynomial
f(X)\in\mathcal{O}_{K}[X]
with
0\leq v(\text{disc}(f))<v\varpi
. Since
\sharp:\mathcal{O}_{K^{\flat}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{K}
is surjective modulo
p
, we can find a monic
g(X)\in\mathcal{O}_{K^{\flat}}[X]
with
g^{\sharp}(X)\equiv f(X)\mod p\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Note that
L
is also generated by
g^{\sharp}(X)
by the Hensel-Rychlik Lemma. By Lemma
6.2.1
, it is also generated by
g_{n}^{\sharp}(X)
, where the coefficients of
g_{n}(X)\in K^{\flat}[X]
are
p^{n}
-th roots of the coefficients of
g(X)
. By Łoś, the extension
L_{U}
can be generated by
g^{\natural}(X)
and the conclusion follows.
∎

This recovers the natural equivalence between the categories of finite extensions of
K
and
K^{\flat}
, as prescribed by Scholze on pg. 3
[
Sch12a
]
. We finally put all the pieces together:

Theorem 6.2.3
.

Let
(K,v)
be a perfectoid field and
\varpi\in\mathfrak{m}\backslash\{0\}
. Let
U
be a non-principal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
and
(K_{U},v_{U})
be the corresponding ultrapower. Let
w
be the coarsest coarsening of
v_{U}
such that
w\varpi>0
. Then:

(I)

Every finite extension of
(K_{U},w)
is unramified.

(II)

The tilt
(K^{\flat},v^{\flat})
embeds elementarily into
(k_{w},\overline{v})
.

Moreover, the equivalence
K_{U}-\text{f\'{e}t}\simeq k_{w}-\text{f\'{e}t}
restricts to
K-\text{f\'{e}t}\simeq K^{\flat}-\text{f\'{e}t}
.

Proof.

Part I is a special case of Theorem
4.3.1
(ii). Part II is Theorem
6.1.3
(iii). For the moreover part, recall that étale algebras over a field are finite products of finite separable extensions. It then suffices to check the analogous statement for finite (separable) field extensions, which follows from Corollary
6.2.2
.
∎

We now make a few comments on Question

. Regarding the extent to which the theory of

determines the theory of

Remark 6.2.4
.

(i)

By Corollary

with parameters from

(ii)

Without the above stipulations, tilting will typically involve some loss of first-order information (cf. Example

). This is related to the failure of an Ax-Kochen/Ershov principle for tame fields of mixed characteristic down to residue field and value group, something that was first observed by Anscombe-Kuhlmann

. To provide a counterexample in our setting, just take two non-elementary equivalent perfectoid fields with the same tilt. Remark 6.2.5
.

Properties (i)-(iii) are well-known, but (iv) appears to be new:

(i)

Let ACF denote the theory of algebraically closed fields. We have that

7.
Almost mathematics revisited

In this final section, we explain why Theorem
6.2.3
(I) is a non-standard version of the almost purity theorem over perfectoid valuation rings. More generally, we translate notions from almost ring theory to notions in ring theory. This translation is achieved by means of a functor which associates an object in the almost category to a localization of its ultrapower.

7.1.
Some background

We first recall all necessary background from almost mathematics. Our main sources are
[
GR03
]
and §4
[
Sch12
]
.

7.1.1.
Almost modules

Let
(K,v)
be a valued field of rank
1
with valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
such that
\mathfrak{m}^{2}=\mathfrak{m}
. The latter condition equivalently means that the valued field
(K,v)
is non-discrete (e.g., a perfectoid field).

Definition 7.1.2
.

Let
M
be an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-module. We say that
x\in M
is almost zero if
\mathfrak{m}x=0
. The module
M
is almost zero if all of its elements are almost zero, i.e.,
\mathfrak{m}M=0
.

Recall that, given an abelian category
\mathcal{A}
, a
Serre subcategory
of
\mathcal{A}
is a nonempty full subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{A}
which is closed under extensions, i.e., given a short exact sequence

0\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow A\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow B\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow C\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow 0

with
A,C\in\mathcal{C}
, we also have
B\in\mathcal{C}
. Write
\mathcal{O}_{K}{\operatorname{-Mod}}
for the category of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-modules. The following is easy to show using that
\mathfrak{m}^{2}=\mathfrak{m}
:

Fact 7.1.3

(Lemma 4.2
[
Sch12
]
)
.

The full subcategory
\Sigma
of almost zero
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-modules is a Serre subcategory of
\mathcal{O}_{K}{\operatorname{-Mod}}
.

Fact
7.1.3
allows us to form a new category, where almost zero information is systematically neglected. Namely, we define the Serre quotient (or Gabriel quotient) below

\mathcal{O}_{K}^{a}{\operatorname{-Mod}}=\mathcal{O}_{K}{\operatorname{-Mod}}/\Sigma

called the category of
almost

\mathcal{O}_{K}
-modules. We recall what this means:

•

The objects of
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{a}{\operatorname{-Mod}}
are the objects of
\mathcal{O}_{K}{\operatorname{-Mod}}
. For clarity, we shall write
M^{a}
for the object of
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{a}{\operatorname{-Mod}}
corresponding to
M
.

•

\text{Hom}(M^{a},N^{a})=\varinjlim\text{Hom}(M^{\prime},N/N^{\prime})
, where the limit ranges over submodules
M^{\prime}\subseteq M
and
N^{\prime}\subseteq N
such that
M/M^{\prime}\in\Sigma
and
N^{\prime}\in\Sigma
.

We have a canonical
almostification functor

\mathcal{O}_{K}{\operatorname{-Mod}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{K}^{a}{\operatorname{-Mod}}:M\mapstochar\rightarrow M^{a}

which sends a morphism
f:M\rightarrow N
to the corresponding element in the direct limit with
M^{\prime}=M
and
N^{\prime}=0
. The almostification functor is exact, essentially surjective and its kernel is
\Sigma
.

The set
\text{Hom}(M^{a},N^{a})
is clearly an honest
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-module from the above definition. It also admits a simpler description:

Fact 7.1.4

(§2.2.11
[
GR03
]
)
.

Given two
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-modules
M,N
, we have

\text{Hom}(M^{a},N^{a})=\text{Hom}(\mathfrak{m}\otimes M,N)

The functor
M^{a}\mapstochar\rightarrow(M^{a})_{!}=\mathfrak{m}\otimes M
is a left adjoint to the almostification functor. Moreover, if
M
is an almost
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-module, then
(M_{!})^{a}=M
.

We also define
\text{alHom}(M^{a},N^{a})=\text{Hom}(M^{a},N^{a})^{a}
.

Fact 7.1.5

(§2.2.6
[
GR03
]
)
.

The category
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{a}
is an abelian tensor category, where kernels, cokernels and tensor products are defined in the unique way compatible with their definition in
\mathcal{O}_{K}{\operatorname{-Mod}}
, e.g.,

M^{a}\otimes N^{a}=(M\otimes N)^{a}

for any
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-modules
M
and
N
.

Notation 7.1.6
.

We will sometimes use the following terminology, occasionally used in
[
GR03
]
, although not explicitly introduced:

(i)

Given a morphism
M\rightarrow N
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-modules, we will say that it is
almost injective
(resp. almost surjective) if the kernel (resp. cokernel) is an almost zero module.

(ii)

In the above situation, we also say that
M^{a}\rightarrow N^{a}
is
injective
(resp. surjective).

(iii)

We say that

is an almost isomorphism if it is both almost injective and almost surjective.

7.1.7.
Almost algebras and almost elements

The notion of an almost

[

]
for the details of the following definition:

Definition 7.1.8

(§2.2.6
[
GR03
]
, pg. 19
[
Sch12
]
)
.

(i)

An
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{a}
-algebra is a commutative unitary monoid

\mathcal{O}_{K}^{a}
-mod. This means that there is a multplication map
\mu_{A}:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
and a unit map

, satisfying the usual categorical versions of the axioms for a unitary commutative monoid.

Let
A
be an
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{a}
-algebra.

(ii)

An
A

\mathcal{O}_{K}^{a}
-module
M

(iii)

An

in the abelian tensor category

, or equivalently an
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{a}
-algebra together with an algebra morphism

We will shortly see that almost algebras come from almostifications of honest algebras. First, we need the functor of almost elements:

Fact 7.1.9

(Proposition 2.2.14
[
GR03
]
)
.

There is a right adjoint to the almostification functor

\mathcal{O}_{K}^{a}{\operatorname{-Mod}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{K}{\operatorname{-Mod}}:M\mapstochar\rightarrow M_{*}

given by the functor of almost elements

M_{*}=\text{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{a},M^{a})

Moreover, we have that
(M_{*})^{a}=M
. If
M=N^{a}
, then
M_{*}=\text{Hom}(\mathfrak{m},N)
.

Remark 7.1.10
.

(i)

The adjoints
({-})_{*}
and
({-})_{!}
both yield full embeddings of
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{a}{\operatorname{-Mod}}
into
\mathcal{O}_{K}{\operatorname{-Mod}}
since they are right inverses to the almostification functor
({-})^{a}
.

(ii)

The functor
({-})_{*}
is monoidal, i.e., it preserves unit maps and there is a canonical map

F:M_{*}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}N_{*}\rightarrow(M\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K}^{a}}N)_{*}

In particular, if
A
is an
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{a}
-algebra, then

is naturally an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-algebra and
(A_{*})^{a}=A
.
We stress that

is usually not an isomorphism, i.e.,
({-})_{*}

does not
preserve tensor products in general. For instance, if

and

, then

and one can check that

but

.

(iii)

On the other hand,
({-})_{!}
does preserve tensor products but it does not preserve unit maps since

. In particular,
({-})_{!}
does not preserve monoidal structures.

In §
7.2.10
, we define yet another embedding of
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{a}{\operatorname{-Mod}}
into a concrete category of modules. In contrast with
({-})_{*}
, this also preserves tensor products, at least under some finite generation assumption on the almost modules being tensored.

7.2.18.
Almost purity over perfectoid valuation rings

We now deduce the almost purity theorem over perfectoid valuation rings from its non-standard counterpart. First, we need a lemma:

Lemma 7.2.19
.

Let
(K,v)
be an
\aleph_{1}
-saturated valued field of positive residue characteristic
p>0
.
Let
\varpi\in\mathfrak{m}_{v}\backslash\{0\}
with
0<v\varpi\leq vp
and
w
be the coarsest coarsening of
v
such that
w\varpi>0
. Suppose that
\Gamma_{w}
does not have a minimal positive element. Then, the following are equivalent:

(i)

\mathcal{O}_{v}/\varpi\mathcal{O}_{v}
is semi-perfect.

(ii)

k_{w}
is perfect.

(iii)

\mathcal{O}_{w}/\varpi\mathcal{O}_{w}
is semi-perfect.

Proof.

Let
w^{+}
be the finest coarsening of
v
with
w^{+}\varpi=0
and consider the standard decomposition with respect to
\varpi
below

K\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{+}}}{{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}}k_{w^{+}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\overline{w}}}{{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}}k_{w}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\overline{v}}}{{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}}k_{v}

By Lemma
2.3.7
, the valued field
(k_{w^{+}},\overline{w})
is spherically complete with value group
\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{R}
. Since
\Gamma_{w}
has no minimal positive element and
\Gamma_{\overline{w}}
is a convex subgroup of
\Gamma_{w}
, we must have that
\Gamma_{\overline{w}}=\mathbb{R}
.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): We have
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{v}}=(\mathcal{O}_{v}/\varpi\mathcal{O}_{v})_{\text{red}}
and
k_{w}=\text{Frac}(\mathcal{O}_{\overline{v}})
. Since
\mathcal{O}_{v}/\varpi\mathcal{O}_{v}
is semi-perfect, the same is true for

and therefore
k_{w}
is perfect.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii):
By Fact
3.3.4
, the valued field
(k_{w^{+}},\overline{w})
is tame. Tame fields are perfect and deeply ramified (see Theorem 1.2
[
KR23
]
). In particular, we get that
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{w}}/\varpi\mathcal{O}_{\overline{w}}
is semi-perfect. Finally, note that
\mathcal{O}_{w}/\varpi\mathcal{O}_{w}
maps isomorphically to
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{w}}/\varpi\mathcal{O}_{\overline{w}}
via the residue map of
w^{+}
.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i):
First, using that
\Gamma_{\overline{w}}=\mathbb{R}
, we can find
\varepsilon
such that

p\cdot w\varepsilon<w\varpi<2p\cdot w\varepsilon
. Observe that
\varpi\mathcal{O}_{w}\subseteq\varepsilon^{p}\mathcal{O}_{v}
using the chain of inclusions below

\varpi\mathcal{O}_{w}\subseteq\varepsilon^{p}\mathfrak{m}_{w}\subseteq\varepsilon^{p}\mathfrak{m}_{v}\subseteq\varepsilon^{p}\mathcal{O}_{v}

Now let
x\in\mathcal{O}_{v}
. Since
\mathcal{O}_{w}/\varpi\mathcal{O}_{w}
is semi-perfect, there is
y_{1}\in\mathcal{O}_{w}
such that
x\equiv y_{1}^{p}\mod\varpi\mathcal{O}_{w}
.
Since
\varpi\mathcal{O}_{w}\subseteq\varepsilon^{p}\mathcal{O}_{v}
, there is
x_{1}\in\mathcal{O}_{v}
such that

x=y_{1}^{p}+\varepsilon^{p}\cdot x_{1}

Note also that
y_{1}\in\mathcal{O}_{v}
. By similar reasoning, there are
x_{2},y_{2}\in\mathcal{O}_{v}
such that
x_{1}=y_{2}^{p}+\varepsilon^{p}\cdot x_{2}
.
We then compute that

x=y_{1}^{p}+\varepsilon^{p}\cdot y_{2}^{p}+\varepsilon^{2p}\cdot x_{2}

Computing modulo
\varpi\mathcal{O}_{v}
, we get

x\equiv(y_{1}+\varepsilon\cdot y_{2})^{p}\mod\varpi\mathcal{O}_{v}

We conclude that
\mathcal{O}_{v}/\varpi\mathcal{O}_{v}
is semi-perfect.

Theorem

(Almost purity)
.

(K,v)
be a perfectoid field and

(i)

(ii)

\mathcal{O}_{K^{\prime}}^{a}/\mathcal{O}_{K}^{a}

(resp.

) for a non-principal ultrapower and

) for the coarsest coarsening of

with

(resp.

is semi-perfect, we get that

is perfect by Lemma
7.2.19
. The same is true for

, we get that

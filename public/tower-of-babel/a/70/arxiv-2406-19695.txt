Abstract
Gamma decays of the isovector giant dipole resonance (IVGDR) of the deformed nucleus Sm 154 \hphantom{{}^{\text{154}}_{\text{}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}{}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{154}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{154}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{154}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{154}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\text{Sm} were measured using 2 1 + {2}^{+}_{1} -Smekal-Raman and elastic scattering of linearly polarized, quasimonochromatic photon beams. The two scattering processes were disentangled through their distinct angular distributions. Their branching ratio and cross sections were determined at six excitation energies covering the Sm 154 \hphantom{{}^{\text{154}}_{\text{}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}{}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{154}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{154}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{154}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{154}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\text{Sm} IVGDR. Both agree with the predictions of the geometrical model for the IVGDR and confirm γ \upgamma decay as an observable sensitive to the structure of the resonance. Consequently, the data place strong constraints on the nuclear shape, including the degree of triaxiality. As a ground-state excitation, the IVGDR is sensitive to ground-state properties, especially the nuclear shape.

The

\upgamma

Since NRF on the IVGDR to the
{0}^{+}_{1}
state is indistinguishable from Thomson scattering (ES of photons off the nucleus as a whole), these processes interfere with each other

;
Fuller and Hayward 1962
;
Bar-Noy and Moreh 1974

Hence, the total ES cross section is

\sigma_{\text{ES}}(E)=\frac{8\pi}{3}\left|f_{\text{IVGDR}}(E)+f_{\text{T}}\right|^{2},

(4)

where the

are the forward (
\theta=0
) IVGDR
{0}^{+}_{1}
NRF and Thomson scattering amplitudes, respectively.
As both scattering processes share the angular distribution of
Eq.

1

The Thomson amplitude
f_{\text{T}}=-Z^{2}e^{2}/(4\pi\epsilon_{0}Mc^{2})

\displaystyle f_{\text{IVGDR}}(E)

\displaystyle=\frac{E^{2}}{2\pi^{2}\hbar c}\mathcal{P}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sigma_{\text{abs}}(\epsilon)}{{\epsilon}^{2}-E^{2}}\operatorname{d}\!{\epsilon}+\mathrm{i}\frac{E\sigma_{\text{abs}}(E)}{4\pi\hbar c}

(5)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{3}\frac{{\sigma_{0}}_{k}E^{2}\Gamma_{k}}{4\pi\hbar c}\frac{\left({E_{0}}_{k}^{2}-E^{2}\right)+\mathrm{i}E\Gamma_{k}}{\left({E_{0}}_{k}^{2}-E^{2}\right)^{2}+E^{2}\Gamma_{k}^{2}}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{k=1}^{3}{f_{\text{IVGDR}}}_{k}(E)

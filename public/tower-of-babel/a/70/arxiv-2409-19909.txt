1
Introduction

Let
(M,g)
be a
n
-dimensional smooth, complete Riemannian manifold without boundary, and

(N,h)
be a
k
-dimensional smooth, compact Riemannian manifold without boundary, which is isometrically
embedded into an Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{L}
for
L>1
.
For
0<T\leq\infty
, a map
u\in C^{2}(M\times(0,T),N)
is a heat flow of harmonic map, if it solves the heat equation of harmonic
map:

\partial_{t}u-\Delta_{g}u=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{n}g^{\alpha\beta}A(u)\big(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{\alpha}},\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{\beta}}\big)\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ M\times(0,T),

(1.1)

where
\Delta_{g}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
(M,g)
and

A(\cdot)(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the second fundamental form of
N\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{L}
.
Given a map
u_{0}\in C^{2}(M,N)
, we will study (
1.1
) together with the initial condition:

u(\cdot,0)=u_{0}\ \ {\rm{on}}\ \ M.

(1.2)

The study of heat flow of harmonic maps (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) was initiated by Eells and Sampson in their seminal
paper
[
13
]
, where they proved that (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) admits a unique
global smooth solution
u
that sub-converges to a smooth harmonic map
u_{\infty}\in C^{\infty}(M,N)

as
t\to\infty
, when the sectional curvature
of
N
is nonpositive. For any arbitrary
(N,h)
without curvature assumption, Chen-Struwe
[
8
]

constructed a global weak solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) that is smooth away from a closed set of locally finite

n
-dimensional parabolic Hausdorff measure (cf. Chen-Lin
[
7
]
for
\partial M\not=\emptyset
) for
n\geq 3
,
and Struwe
[
22
]
established a unique global solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) with finitely many singular points
(cf. Chang
[
4
]
for
\partial M\not=\emptyset
) for
n=2
. Furthermore, the existence of finite time singularity
of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) was established by Coron-Ghidaglia
[
10
]
and Chen-Ding
[
5
]

for
n\geq 3
, and by Chang-Ding-Ye
[
6
]
first and Davila-del Pino-Wei
[
11
]
recently for
n=2
.
It was analyzed by Lin-Wang
[
18
,
19
]
that the formation of a finite time singularity
(x_{0},T_{0})

from a locally smooth solution
u
of (
1.1
) in dimensions
n\geq 3
is closely related to the existence of backward self-similar, nontrivial solutions

\displaystyle\phi(\frac{x}{\sqrt{-t}})
or steady solutions
\displaystyle\phi(\frac{x}{|x|})
, called as quasi-harmonic spheres or harmonic spheres.
We refer interested readers to the monograph
[
20
]
and references therein for more discussions on (
1.1
).

Certain equivariant backward self-similar, nontrivial solutions of (
1.1
) have been constructed by Fan
[
14
]
for
3\leq n\leq 6

and Gastel
[
15
]
for all
n\geq 3
; while the
non-existence of backward self-similar nontrivial solutions of (
1.1
) has been shown under the assumption

\nabla u\in L^{\infty}([0,T],L^{n})
for
n\geq 4
by Wang
[
23
]
, see also Bizoń-Wasserman
[
3
]
for some related result.
Typically, the construction of a continuation of the heat flow of harmonic maps (
1.1
)
after a singularity uses an asymptotically self-similar expander. There have been quite a few results
on forward self-similar solutions of (
1.1
). Coron
[
9
]
first proved that there exists a homogeneous of degree zero map

u_{0}:{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\to\mathbb{S}^{2}
that supports infinitely many solutions of (
1.1
). Biernat-Bizoń
[
2
]
made interesting numerical
studies on both self-similar shrinker and expander of (
1.1
). Germain-Rupflin
[
16
]
established
the existence of forward self-similar solutions of (
1.1
) in the equivariant setting, and analyzed their uniqueness and stability.
Recently, Deruelle-Lamm
[
12
]
established the existence of forward self-similar solutions to (
1.1
) in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
, when
the initial data
\displaystyle u_{0}(x)=\phi_{0}(\frac{x}{|x|})
and
\phi_{0}\in{\rm{Lip}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},N)
is homotopic to
a constant, which is smooth away from
a compact set with finite
n
-dimensional parabolic Hausdorff measure.
We would like to point out that, in a different but closely related context, Jia-Sverak
[
17
]
constructed a forward self-similar solution
u
to the incompressible Navier-Stokes equation in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
for any initial data
\displaystyle u_{0}(x)=|x|^{-1}\phi_{0}(\frac{x}{|x|})
, with
\phi_{0}\in C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, such that
u\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times(0,\infty))\cap C^{\gamma}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times[0,\infty)\setminus\{(0,0)\})
.

From now on, we will assume
(M,g)=({\mathbb{R}}^{n},dx^{2})
.
A map
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)\to N
is called a forward self-similar heat flow of harmonic map, if
it solves (
1.1
) and satisfies

u(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t)=u(x,t),\ \forall(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times(0,\infty),\ \forall\lambda>0.

This is equivalent to
\displaystyle u(x,t)=u(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}},1)
for
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times(0,\infty)
.

Partly inspired by the main theorem by
[
17
]
, we establish our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
n\geq 2
, assume
u_{0}\in W^{1,n}_{\rm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\},N)
is homogeneous of degree zero.
There exists an
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, depending on
n
and
N
, such that
if

\|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}u_{0}\|_{L^{n}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\leq\varepsilon_{0},

(1.3)

then there exists
a unique solution
u\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times(0,\infty),N)\cap C^{\frac{1}{n}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times[0,\infty)\setminus\{(0,0)\},N)
of the heat flow of harmonic map (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), that is forward self-similar.

First, we would like to point out the following remark.

Remark 1.2
.

It follows from
u_{0}\in W^{1,n}_{\rm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
is homogeneous of degree zero that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{n,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\approx\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}u_{0}\|_{L^{n}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\leq\varepsilon_{0},

where
L^{n,\infty}
denotes the weak
L^{n}
-space. By the Poincaré inequality,
u_{0}
has small BMO norm:

\big[u_{0}\big]_{\rm{BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\lesssim\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{n,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq\varepsilon_{0}.

Hence by
[
25
]
there exists a unique solution
u\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times(0,\infty),N)
of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).
However, the argument by
[
25
]
is insufficient for showing
u\in C^{\frac{1}{n}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times[0,\infty)\setminus\{(0,0)\})
,
which is the new contribution by Theorem
1.1
. To achieve this, we need to choose a function space
X
that is different
from that employed by
[
25
]
. It is a very interesting question to ask whether Theorem
1.1
holds
without the condition (
1.3
).

Here we sketch the key steps of proof of Theorem
1.1
, which are
different from
[
12
]
. Instead of solving the equation of quasi-harmonic maps in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with

u_{0}
as the asymptotic boundary value near infinity, we will construct a mild solution
u
of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
)
for any initial data
u_{0}
, which has small
\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{n,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
.
More precisely,

Step 1.

Seek a mild solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
)
u(\cdot,t)=\widehat{u_{0}}(t)+v(\cdot,t)
,
where
\widehat{u_{0}}(t)=e^{t\Delta}u_{0}
and
v(x,t)
is a fixed point of the contractive solution operator
\mathcal{S}:\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(0)\to\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(0)
given by

\mathcal{S}(v)(\cdot,t):=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}A(\widehat{u_{0}}+v)(\nabla\widehat{u_{0}}+\nabla v,\nabla\widehat{u_{0}}+\nabla v)(s)\,ds,\ t>0,

where
\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(0)\subset X
is the
\delta
-ball center at
0
,
and
X
is the Banach space with norm

\|v\|_{X}:=\|v\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+})}+\sup_{t\geq 0}t^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2n}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
.
Here the assumption on
u_{0}
guarantees

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}t^{\frac{1}{4}}\big\|\nabla\widehat{u_{0}}\big\|_{L^{2n}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{n,\infty}(R^{n})}\leq C\delta.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{n,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\delta,

(1.4)

and

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},R>0}R^{-n}\int_{B_{R}(x)\times[0,R^{2}]}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C\delta^{2}.

(1.5)

Step 2.

(
1.4
) ensures the smallness of BMO norm of
u(t)

so that

\big\|\Delta u\big\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}(x))}\leq C\Big(R^{-1}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}(x))}+\|\Delta u+A(u)(\nabla u,\nabla u)\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}(x))}\Big)

(1.6)

holds for all
B_{R}(x)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
This and the smallness of renormalized energy (
1.5
) enable us to employ a blowing up argument to show
the renormalized energy enjoys
a decay property that yields the Hölder continuity of
u
at any
(x_{0},0)
, with
x_{0}\not=0
, if in addition

u_{0}
is homogeneous of degree zero.

2
Proof of Theorem
1.1

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. First, we will apply the fixed point argument to obtain a mild solution
u

of (
1.1
) for any initial data
u_{0}
,
with small
\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{n,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
,
that has sufficiently high Sobolev integrability and small
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{n,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
.

For
t\geq 0
, let
\widehat{u_{0}}(t)=e^{t\Delta}u_{0}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{L}
denote the Caloric extension of
u_{0}
. Namely,

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)\widehat{u_{0}}=0\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ {\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times(0,\infty),\ \ \widehat{u_{0}}(0)=u_{0}\ \ {\rm{on}}\ \ {\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

By the standard Lorentz space
L^{p,q}
-estimate on convolution operators through the heat kernel, we obtain

Lemma 2.1
.

If
u_{0}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to N
satisfies
\nabla u_{0}\in L^{n,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, then, for any
t>0
,

\|\nabla\widehat{u_{0}}(t)\|_{L^{n,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq\|\nabla{u_{0}}\|_{L^{n,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})},

(2.1)

t^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\nabla\widehat{u_{0}}(t)\|_{L^{2n}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\|\nabla{u_{0}}\|_{L^{n,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})},

(2.2)

t^{\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p})}\|\nabla\widehat{u_{0}}(t)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\|\nabla{u_{0}}\|_{L^{n,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})},\ \forall n<p\leq\infty.

(2.3)

Proof.
We leave the proof to interested readers.
∎

Now we are ready to establish the following theorem on (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).

Theorem 2.2
.

There exist a small
\delta_{0}>0
such that if
\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{n,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq\delta_{0}
,
then there is a unique solution
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,\infty)\to N
of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) such that

v:=u-\widehat{u_{0}}
and
u
satisfy the following estimates:

\displaystyle\big\|v\big\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+})}+\sup_{t\geq 0}t^{\frac{1}{4}}\big\|\nabla v(t)\big\|_{L^{2n}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\delta_{0},

(2.4)

\displaystyle\big\|\nabla v(t)\big\|_{L^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\delta^{2}_{0},\ \forall t>0,

(2.5)

and

\displaystyle\big\|\nabla u(t)\big\|_{L^{n,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\delta_{0},\ \forall t>0,

(2.6)

\displaystyle R^{-2}\int_{B_{R}(x)\times[0,R^{2}]}|\nabla u|^{n}\leq C\delta_{0}^{2},\ \forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\ \forall R>0.

(2.7)

If, in addition,
u_{0}
is homogeneous of degree zero, then
u
is forward self-similar.

Proof.
Define the Banach space
X
by

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle:=\Big\{v:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{L}:\ v(\cdot,0)=0\ {\rm{on}}\ {\mathbb{R}}^{n},

\displaystyle\qquad\|v\|_{X}:=\|v\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times[0,\infty))}+\sup_{t\geq 0}t^{\frac{1}{4}}\big\|\nabla v\big\|_{L^{2n}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}<\infty\Big\}.

For
\delta\geq\delta_{0}
, define the
\delta
-ball, with center
0
, in
X
by

\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(0)=\Big\{v\in X:\ \|v\|_{X}\leq\delta\Big\}.

(2.8)

Recall that, since
N\subset\mathbb{R}^{L}
is compact and smooth, there exists a
\delta_{N}>0
such that the nearest point
projection map
\Pi_{N}
from the
\delta_{N}
-neighborhood,
N_{\delta_{N}}=\big\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{L}:{\rm{dist}}(y,N)<\delta_{N}\big\}
, to
N

is smooth. The second fundamental form of
N
is defined by

A(y)(X,Y)=D_{y}^{2}\Pi_{N}(y)(X,Y),\ \forall y\in N,\ X,Y\in T_{y}N.

Now, let
\widehat{A}(\cdot)
be a smooth extension of the second fundamental form
A
of
N
such that

\displaystyle\widehat{A}(y)=A(\Pi_{N}(y))\ \forall y\in N_{\delta_{N}},\ \displaystyle\sup_{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{L}}|\widehat{A}(y)|\leq C(N),

where
C(N)>0
is a constant depending only on
N
.

Next, define the solution operator
\mathcal{S}:\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(0)\to X
by letting
w=\mathcal{S}v
and

w(\cdot,t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}\widehat{A}(u)(\nabla u,\nabla u)(s)\,ds,\ t\geq 0,

(2.9)

where
u(s)=\widehat{u_{0}}(s)+v(s)
for
s\geq 0
. It is easy to see that

w(x,0)=0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Applying Hölder’s inequality, we can estimate that for any
t>0
,

\displaystyle\|w(t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\|{(t-s)^{-\frac{n}{2}}}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4(t-s)}}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\||\nabla u(s)|^{2}\|_{L^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}s^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,ds\big)\big(\|\widehat{u_{0}}\|_{X}^{2}+\|v\|_{X}^{2}\big)\leq C(\delta_{0}^{2}+\delta^{2})\leq\frac{\delta}{2},

(2.10)

provided
\delta_{0}\leq\delta
and
\delta>0
is chosen sufficiently small.

Also, by applying the inequality

\|\nabla e^{t\Delta}f\|_{L^{2n}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{4}-\frac{n}{2p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})},\ \forall 1\leq p\leq 2n,

(2.11)

we can estimate that for any
t>0
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla w(t)\|_{L^{2n}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{2n}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\big(\|\nabla\widehat{u_{0}}\|_{L^{2n}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2n}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}\big)(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{3}{4}}s^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,ds\big)\|\nabla{u_{0}}\|_{L^{n,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\ \ +C\big(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{3}{4}}s^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,ds\big)\|v\|_{X}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{4}}\big(\|v\|_{X}^{2}+\delta_{0}^{2}).

Thus

\sup_{t\geq 0}t^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2n}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\big(\|v\|_{X}^{2}+\delta_{0}^{2})<\frac{\delta}{2},

provided
\delta>0
is chosen sufficiently small. Hence

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(0))\subset\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(0)
.

Next we want to show that
\mathcal{S}
is a contraction map on
\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(0)
.
For this, let
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(0)
, and

w_{1}=\mathcal{S}(v_{1})
and
w_{2}=\mathcal{S}(v_{2})
. Denote
u_{i}=\widehat{u_{0}}+v_{i}
for
i=1,2
. We obtain

\displaystyle|(w_{1}-w_{2})(t)|=\big|\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}\big(\widehat{A}(u_{1})(\nabla u_{1},\nabla u_{1})-\widehat{A}(u_{2})(\nabla u_{2},\nabla u_{2})\big)(s)\,ds\big|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}\Big(|v_{1}-v_{2}||\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+|\nabla(v_{1}-v_{2})|(|\nabla\widehat{u_{0}}|+|\nabla v_{1}|+|\nabla v_{2}|)\Big)(s)\,ds.

Hence, similar to (
2.10
), we can estimate that for
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\|(w_{1}-w_{2})(t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{X}\big(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}s^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,ds\big)(\|\widehat{u_{0}}\|_{X}^{2}+\|v_{1}\|_{X}^{2})

\displaystyle+C\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{X}\big(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}s^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,ds\big)\big(\|\widehat{u_{0}}\|_{X_{T}}+\|v_{1}\|_{X}+\|v_{2}\|_{X}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|\widehat{u_{0}}\|_{X}^{2}+\|v_{1}\|_{X}^{2}+\|\widehat{u_{0}}\|_{X}+\|v_{1}\|_{X}+\|v_{2}\|_{X}\big)\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{X}

so that

\displaystyle\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+})}\leq C\delta\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{X}.

(2.12)

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\|\nabla(w_{1}-w_{2})(t)\|_{L^{2n}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{X}\big(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{3}{4}}s^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,ds\big)(\|\widehat{u_{0}}\|_{X}^{2}+\|v_{1}\|_{X}^{2})

\displaystyle+C\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{X}\big(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{3}{4}}s^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,ds\big)\big(\|\widehat{u_{0}}\|_{X}+\|v_{1}\|_{X}+\|v_{2}\|_{X}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{X}t^{-\frac{1}{4}}\big(\|\widehat{u_{0}}\|_{X}^{2}+\|v_{1}\|_{X}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle+C\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{X}t^{-\frac{1}{4}}\big(\|\widehat{u_{0}}\|_{X}+\|v_{1}\|_{X}+\|v_{2}\|_{X}\big)

so that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}t^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\nabla(w_{1}-w_{2})(t)\|_{L^{2n}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\delta\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{X}.

(2.13)

Combining (
2.12
) with (
2.13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{X}\leq\theta\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{X},

(2.14)

where
0<\theta=C\delta<1
, provided
\delta>0
is chosen sufficiently small.
Therefore we can apply the fixed point theorem to deduce that there exists a unique

v\in\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(0)\subset X
such that

\displaystyle v(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}\widehat{A}(\widehat{u_{0}}+v)(\nabla\widehat{u_{0}}+\nabla v,\nabla\widehat{u_{0}}+\nabla v)(s)\,ds,\ t>0.

(2.15)

We can apply

\|\nabla e^{t\Delta}f\|_{L^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq Ct^{-\frac{n}{2p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})},\ 1\leq p\leq n,

(2.16)

to estimate
\|\nabla v\|_{L^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
by

\displaystyle\|\nabla v(t)\|_{L^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{2n}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|\widehat{u_{0}}\|_{X}^{2}+\|v\|_{X}^{2}\Big)\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}s^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,ds\leq C\delta^{2}.

(2.17)

If we define
u(x,t)=\widehat{u_{0}}(x,t)+v(x,t)
for
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, then
\nabla u(t)\in L^{n,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, and

\displaystyle\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{n,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq\|\nabla\widehat{u_{0}}(t)\|_{L^{n,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|\nabla v(t)\|_{L^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\delta.

(2.18)

For
K>0
and
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times(0,\infty)
, define

c_{0}=\frac{1}{|B_{K}|}\int_{B_{K}}u_{0}(x-\sqrt{t}y)\,dy.

Then

\displaystyle|\widehat{u_{0}}(x,t)-c_{0}|\leq\big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}(u_{0}(x-\sqrt{t}y)-c_{0})\,dy\big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{K}}|u_{0}(x-\sqrt{t}y)-c_{0}|\,dy+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{K}}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}|u_{0}(x-\sqrt{t}y)-c_{0}|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq K^{n}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{n,\infty}(B_{K\sqrt{t}})}+C_{N}\int_{K}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4}}r^{n-1}\,dr

\displaystyle\leq K^{n}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{n,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+o_{K}(1)\leq K^{n}\delta+o_{K}(1),

where
\displaystyle\lim_{K\to\infty}o_{K}(1)=0.

On the other hand, since

, it follows from the Poincaré inequality that

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|B_{K}|}\int_{B_{K}}|u_{0}(x-\sqrt{t}y)-c_{0}|\,dy\leq C\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{n,\infty}(B_{K\sqrt{t}})}\leq C\delta.

Therefore we obtain that

\displaystyle{\rm{dist}}(u(x,t),N)\leq{\rm{dist}}(\widehat{u_{0}}(x,t),N)+|v(x,t)|

\displaystyle\leq|\widehat{u_{0}}(x,t)-c_{0}|+{\rm{dist}}(c_{0},N)+|v(x,t)|\leq(1+K^{n})\delta+o_{K}(1)<\delta_{N},

(2.19)

provided

is chosen sufficiently large and
\delta
is chosen sufficiently small.

It follows from (

Applying (

Proposition 2.3

0<r\leq R_{0}
, it holds that

\displaystyle r^{-n}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})\times[0,r^{2}]}\big(r^{2}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\big(\frac{r}{R_{0}}\big)^{2\alpha_{0}}\max\Big\{R_{0}^{-n}\int_{B_{R_{0}}(x_{0})\times[0,R_{0}^{2}]}|\nabla u|^{2},\ \frac{C_{0}R_{0}^{\frac{2}{n}}}{1-\theta_{0}^{\frac{2}{n}}}\Big\}.

(2.58)

Now for any
\displaystyle 0<t_{0}\leq\frac{R_{0}^{2}}{16}
, it follows from Proposition 2.5
and (
2.58
)
that

i) When
0<r<\sqrt{t_{0}}
, it follows from the interior gradient estimate by Proposition
2.3
that

\displaystyle r^{-n}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})\times[t_{0}-r^{2},t_{0}+r^{2}]}\big(r^{2}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\frac{r}{\sqrt{t_{0}}}\big)^{2}t_{0}^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{B_{\sqrt{t_{0}}}(x_{0})\times[0,2t_{0}]}|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\frac{r}{R_{0}}\big)^{2\alpha_{0}}\max\Big\{R_{0}^{-n}\int_{B_{R_{0}}(x_{0})\times[0,R_{0}^{2}]}|\nabla u|^{2},\ \frac{C_{0}R_{0}^{\frac{2}{n}}}{1-\theta_{0}^{\frac{2}{n}}}\Big\}.

(2.59)

ii) When
\displaystyle\sqrt{t_{0}}\leq r\leq\frac{R_{0}}{4}
, by (
2.58
) we also have that

\displaystyle r^{-n}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})\times[0,t_{0}+r^{2}]}\big(r^{2}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{n}{2}}(\sqrt{2}r)^{-n}\int_{B_{\sqrt{2}r}(x_{0})\times[0,2r^{2}]}\big(r^{2}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\frac{r}{R_{0}}\big)^{2\alpha_{0}}\max\Big\{R_{0}^{-n}\int_{B_{R_{0}}(x_{0})\times[0,R_{0}^{2}]}|\nabla u|^{2},\ \frac{C_{0}R_{0}^{\frac{2}{n}}}{1-\theta_{0}^{\frac{2}{n}}}\Big\}.

(2.60)

Putting together (
2.58
) with (
2.59
) and (
2.60
), and applying Morrey’s decay Lemma

[
21
]
, we conclude that
u\in C^{\alpha_{0}}\big((B_{2}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{2}})\times[0,\frac{R_{0}^{2}}{16}],N)
and

\big[u\big]_{C^{\alpha_{0}}\big((B_{2}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{2}})\times[0,\frac{R_{0}^{2}}{16}])\big)}\leq C\big(\varepsilon_{0},R_{0},\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{n}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\big).

(2.61)

Now we sketch how to improve
\alpha_{0}
to
\frac{1}{n}
as follows. To do this,
let
v_{0}\in C^{\frac{1}{n}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},{\mathbb{R}}^{L})
be such that

\begin{cases}v_{0}=u_{0}\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ B_{2}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{2}};\ \ v_{0}=0\ \ {\rm{outside}}\ \ B_{3}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{4}},\\
\big\|v_{0}\big\|_{C^{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\big\|u_{0}\big\|_{C^{0}(B_{2}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{2}})}\leq C;\\
\ \big[v_{0}\big]_{C^{\frac{1}{n}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\big[u_{0}\big]_{C^{\frac{1}{n}}(B_{2}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{2}})}\leq C\big\|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}u_{0}\big\|_{L^{n}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}.\end{cases}

Set
\widehat{v_{0}}(t)=e^{t\Delta}v_{0}
. Then

\big\|\widehat{v_{0}}(t)\big\|_{C^{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq\big\|v_{0}\big\|_{C^{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C;\ \big[\widehat{v_{0}}(t)\big]_{C^{\frac{1}{n}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq\big[v_{0}\big]_{C^{\frac{1}{n}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C,\ \forall t>0,

and

R^{-n}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})\times[0,R^{2}]}|\nabla\widehat{v_{0}}|^{2}\leq C[v_{0}]_{C^{\frac{1}{n}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}R^{\frac{2}{n}}\leq CR^{\frac{2}{n}},\ \forall x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\ R>0.

(2.62)

Now for any fixed
x_{0}\in B_{\frac{15}{8}}\setminus B_{\frac{5}{8}}
and
0<R<\frac{1}{8}
, let
w:B_{R}(x_{0})\times[0,R^{2}]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{L}
solve

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}w-\Delta w=0\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ B_{R}(x_{0})\times[0,R^{2}],\\
w(\cdot,0)=0\ \ {\rm{on}}\ \ B_{R}(x_{0});\ \ w=u-\widehat{v_{0}}\ \ {\rm{on}}\ \ \partial B_{R}(x_{0})\times[0,R^{2}].\end{cases}

By the maximum principle and the interior gradient estimate of heat equations, we have that

\big\|w\big\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(x_{0})\times[0,R^{2}])}\leq\big\|u-\widehat{v_{0}}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0})\times[0,R^{2}])}\leq CR^{\alpha_{0}},

(2.63)

and

\displaystyle r^{-n}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})\times[0,r^{2}]}|\nabla w|^{2}\leq C(\frac{r}{R})^{2}R^{-n}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})\times[0,R^{2}]}|\nabla w|^{2},\ \forall 0<r\leq R.

(2.64)

Multiplying (
1.1
) by
u-\widehat{v_{0}}-w
and integrating the equation
over
B_{R}(x_{0})\times[0,R^{2}]
yields

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})\times[0,R^{2}]}|\nabla(u-\widehat{v_{0}}-w)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})\times[0,R^{2}]}|\nabla u|^{2}|u-\widehat{v_{0}}-w|

\displaystyle\leq C\big\|u-\widehat{v_{0}}-w\big\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(x_{0})\times[0,R^{2}])}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})\times[0,R^{2}]}|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq CR^{\alpha_{0}}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})\times[0,R^{2}]}|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq CR^{n+3\alpha_{0}},

(2.65)

where we have applied (
2.57
) in the last step.

Combining (
2.65
) with (
2.62
) and (
2.57
), we also have

R^{-n}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})\times[0,R^{2}]}|\nabla w|^{2}\leq CR^{2\alpha_{0}}.

(2.66)

Substituting (
2.66
) into (
2.64
) and employing (
2.65
) and (
2.62
), we conclude
that for any
0<r<\frac{R}{2}
,

r^{-n}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})\times[0,r^{2}]}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C(\frac{r}{R})^{2}R^{2\alpha_{0}}+Cr^{-n}R^{n+3\alpha_{0}}.

(2.67)

Let
\displaystyle\beta_{0}=\frac{\alpha_{0}}{n+2}\in(0,1)
.
Then
\displaystyle\alpha_{1}=\frac{\alpha_{0}+\beta_{0}}{1+\beta_{0}}=\frac{(n+3)\alpha_{0}}{n+2+\alpha_{0}}\in(\alpha_{0},1)
. And (
2.68
) implies that

\big(R^{1+\beta_{0}}\big)^{-n}\int_{B_{R^{1+\beta_{0}}}(x_{0})\times[0,R^{2(1+\beta_{0})}]}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C\big(R^{1+\beta_{0}}\big)^{2\alpha_{1}}

(2.68)

holds for any
x_{0}\in B_{\frac{15}{8}}\setminus B_{\frac{5}{8}}
and
0<R<\frac{1}{8}
. Repeating the same arguments
as in (
2.58
), (
2.59
), and (
2.60
), we can conclude
that
u\in C^{\alpha_{1}}((B_{\frac{15}{8}}\setminus B_{\frac{5}{8}})\times[0,\frac{1}{16}])
. It is not hard to see that this process
will take at most finitely many steps until the Hölder exponent
\alpha_{0}
of
u
in
(B_{\frac{3}{2}}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{4}})\times[0,\frac{1}{16}]

reaches
\frac{1}{n}
.
This, combined with the forward self-similarity, implies that

u\in C^{\frac{1}{n}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times[0,\frac{1}{16}]\setminus\{(0,0)\})
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
2.4
.∎

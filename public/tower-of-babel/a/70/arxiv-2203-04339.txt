Replicated Entanglement Negativity for Disjoint Intervals in the Ising Conformal Field Theory
Gavin Rockwood

Abstract
We calculate the N N interval replicated negativity for the Ising conformal field theory using correlation functions of branch point twist fields. For some subset P ⊂ N P\subset N , this is a calculation of Tr ⁡ ( ρ A T P ) n \Tr(\rho_{A}^{T_{P}})^{n} where n n is the replica index. This can be reformulated as a calculation of partition functions over superelliptic Riemann surfaces, and for the models in question, this partition function can be expressed in terms of the period matrix of this surface. We detail how to construct the period matrices for these surfaces, giving an analytic expression for Tr ⁡ ( ρ A T P ) n \Tr(\rho_{A}^{T_{P}})^{n} . The results are expressed such that when P = ∅ P=\varnothing , which corresponds to calculating the Rényi entropy, the formulas aligns with known results for the N N interval Rényi entropy.

4
Conclusions

In this paper, we have constructed a method to calculate the entanglement negativity between disjoint subsystems that are themselves made up of disjoint subsystems. This is a generalization of the calculation of entanglement negativity entropy presented in
[
10
]
. So far we really only focused on the Ising CFT, however we mentioned that this can be used for other CFTs like the free compact boson and its decompactification regime along with others
[
8
,
2
]
. This is because they all just depend on the period matrix of the Riemann surface, meaning our focus on the Ising CFT does not incur a loss of generality.

Unfortunately, with the given results, the analytic continuation of
n
required to compute the logarithmic negativity
\mathcal{E}=\ln\Tr\left|\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}\right|
is not something we were able to do. The formula for
\mathcal{F}
when expressed in terms of the Riemann theta functions is not algebraic in the points
u_{\alpha},v_{\alpha}
. It is known that for
N=2
,
\mathcal{R}_{n,N}^{P}
can be rewritten as a hyperelliptic curve
\nu^{2}=f(\mu)
where
f(\mu)
is a degree
2n
curve
[
20
,
21
]
. This allows
\mathcal{F}
to be expressed as an algebraic function of
u_{\alpha},v_{\alpha}
via a Thomae formula. Unfortunately, the surfaces
\mathcal{R}_{N,n}
and
\mathcal{R}_{N,n}^{P}
are not hyperelliptic for both
N>2
and
n>2
, only if it is one or the other. This means the surface can not be brought into a form like
\nu^{2}=f(\mu)
(see
B
).

In this paper we have looked at homogenous systems. It would be interesting to apply these ideas to inhomogenous systems such as those with interfaces
[
22
]
, boundaries and defects
[
23
]
, and in particular, systems with topological defects
[
24
]
. Another interesting extension would be for gapped systems
[
25
,
26
]
.

Appendix B
Proof of non-hyperellipticity for both
N,n>2

For this, we will swap out our Riemann sphere
\hat{\mathbb{C}}
for the complex projective space
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
(they are isomorphic) and we will just write
\mathcal{R}_{N,n}
as the
P
superscript is not important. We are interested in

\mathcal{R}_{N,n}:\hskip 28.45274ptw^{n}=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{N}(z-u_{\alpha})(z-v_{\alpha})^{n-1}

(48)

First, three things: a Riemann surface
\mathcal{R}
is hyperelliptic curve in
\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{1}
if it admits a fractional linear involution
\iota
with
2g+2
fixed points (
\iota
is non trivial), a hyperelliptic Riemann surface is a double cover of
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
and
\iota
must commute with other automorphisms of
\mathcal{R}
. This last point is important as we have the
T
automorphism for
\mathcal{R}_{N,n}
which sends
T:w\mapsto e^{2\pi i/n}w
. Because of the commutation,
\iota
will send orbits of
T
to orbits,
\iota:[w]_{T}\mapsto[w^{\prime}]_{T}
. Since each orbit is characterized by a
z
, we can look at
\iota(z)
. This leaves two options:
\iota(z)=z
or
\iota(z)\neq z
.

If
\iota(z)=z
, then
\iota
will send
w
to some
e^{2\pi ik/n}w
. But because
\iota^{2}=1
, we must have
\iota(w)=-w
. If we look at
\mathcal{R}_{N,n}/\iota
we have

w^{n/2}=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{N}(z-u_{\alpha})(z-v_{\alpha})^{n-1}

(49)

which is a curve of genus

. Firstly, this only makes sense if
n
is even. Second, for
\mathcal{R}_{N,n}
to be hyperelliptic, one needs
\mathcal{R}_{N,n}/\iota\simeq\mathbb{C}P^{1}
which only happens for
N=1
or
n=2
.

For
N\neq 1
, we can consider
\iota(z)\neq z
. A general note on fractional linear transformations over over
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
: they are the Möbius transformatons that make up
PGL(2,\mathbb{C})
and take the form

f:z\mapsto\frac{az+b}{cz+d}

(50)

and have at most two fixed points unless it is the identity (this can be seen by setting

and solving the resulting quadratic equation). This means that there at most two
z
fixed points, and because each
z

n

w
s, there are at most
2n

2n
, equal to
2g+2
we arrive at the equation

2n=2(n-1)(N-1)+2.

(51)

Simplifying this, we arrive at

1=N-1

(52)

which is only satisfied for

n
). This means that

n
or
n=2

. This means that we can not perform a change of variables to arrive at an equation of the form

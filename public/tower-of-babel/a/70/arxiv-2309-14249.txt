Introduction

The principle goal of this paper is to establish a density version of the strong Goldbach conjecture for Gaussian integers, restricted to sectors in the complex plane.

Briefly, for integers

The field

The Gaussian primes can take one of two forms.

Second, if

As in the case of

The main result concerns Goldbach type results.

The only prior results in this direction we could find in the literature correspond to the entire complex plane. Below, we allow arbitrary sectors.

The analog of Ramanujan’s sum is

(3.6)

\displaystyle\tau_{q}(x)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{A}_{q}}e(\langle x,\tfrac{n}{q}\rangle).

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
.

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{\gcd(\bar{r},q)=1}=\frac{1}{N(q)}\sum_{k\in B_{q}}e\left(\langle-r,\frac{k}{q}\rangle\right)\tau_{{\bar{q}}}(k).

Proof.

Note from the definition in (
3.6
), that

(3.8)

\tau_{{\bar{q}}}(k)=\bigl\langle\mathbf{1}_{\gcd(\bar{r},q)=1},e(\langle\cdot,k/\bar{q})\bigr\rangle.

Then, the conclusion follows from general facts about orthogonal bases.

∎

Lemma 3.9
.

For
x\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
we have

(3.10)

\displaystyle\tau_{q}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\divides(q,\bar{x})}\mu(q/d)N(d).

In particular,
\tau_{q}(q)=\phi(q)
and
\tau_{q}(1)=\mu(q)
. In addition, if
(q,\bar{x})=1
then
\tau_{q}(x)=\mu(q)
.

Proof.

Note that thanks to Corollary
3.3
we have that if
d\divides q
then

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in B_{q}\\
d|k\end{subarray}}e\left(\langle x,\frac{k}{q}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in B_{q}}e\left(\langle x,\frac{k}{q}\rangle\right)\mathbf{1}_{d|k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in B_{q}}e\left(\langle x,\frac{k}{q}\rangle\right)\frac{1}{N(q)}\sum_{r\in B_{q}}e\left(\langle-r,\frac{k}{d}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N(q)}\sum_{r\in B_{q}}\sum_{k\in B_{q}}e\left(\langle\frac{x-r\frac{\bar{q}}{\bar{d}}}{\bar{q}},k\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r\in B_{q}}\mathbf{1}_{{\bar{q}}|x-r\frac{{\bar{q}}}{{\bar{d}}}}

\displaystyle=N(\frac{q}{d})\mathbf{1}_{\frac{q}{d}\divides\bar{x}}.

The last equality comes from counting the number of
r
’s in
B_{q}
for which the indicator is non-zero. Let
\bar{x}=\frac{q}{d}x^{\prime}
. Then
\bar{x}-\bar{r}\frac{q}{d}\equiv 0\mod q\,\Leftrightarrow\,\frac{q}{d}(x^{\prime}-\bar{r})\equiv 0\mod\bar{q}
. This means that
x^{\prime}\equiv\bar{r}\mod d
, which means that there exists a unique such
\bar{r}\in B_{d}
and
N(q/d)
of them in
B_{q}
.

Hence, and with the assistance of the Inclusion-Exclusion principle, we observe that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\tau_{q}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in B_{q}\\
(k,q)=1\end{subarray}}e(\langle x,\tfrac{k}{q}\rangle)

(3.12)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\divides q}\mu(d)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in B_{q}\\
d|k\end{subarray}}e(\langle x,\tfrac{k}{q}\rangle)

(3.13)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\divides q}\mu(d)N(\tfrac{q}{d})\mathbf{1}_{\frac{q}{d}\divides\bar{x}}

(3.14)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\divides q}\mu(q/d)N(d)\mathbf{1}_{d\divides\bar{x}}

(3.15)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\divides(q,\bar{x})}\mu(q/d)N(d).

∎

Corollary 3.16
.

The function
\tau_{q}(x)
is multiplicative and we have that

(3.17)

\displaystyle\tau_{q}(x)

\displaystyle=\mu\Big(\frac{q}{(q,\bar{x})}\Big)\frac{\phi(q)}{\phi(\frac{q}{(q,\bar{x})})}.

Proof.

Let
d=(q,\bar{x})
. A direct consequence of the Chinese Remainder Theorem gives us

\displaystyle\tau_{q}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r\in\mathbb{A}_{q}}e(\langle r,\tfrac{x}{\bar{q}}\rangle)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{A}_{q/d}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{A}_{d}}e(\langle k\frac{q}{d}+md,\frac{x}{\bar{q}}\rangle)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{A}_{q/d}}e(\langle m,\tfrac{x}{\bar{q}/\bar{d}}\rangle)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{A}_{d}}e(\langle k,\tfrac{x}{\bar{d}}\rangle)

\displaystyle=\tau_{q/d}(x)\,\phi(d)

and the result follows immediately from the previous lemma and the multiplicative properties of
\phi
.
∎

With these identities in mind, we prove Cohen’s identity.

Lemma 3.18
.

For
x\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
, the following identity holds:

\displaystyle\sum_{r\in\mathbb{A}_{\bar{q}}}\tau_{q}(x+r)=\mu(q)\tau_{q}(x).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{r\in\mathbb{A}_{\bar{q}}}\tau_{q}(x+r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r\in B_{\bar{q}}}1_{(r,\bar{q})=1}\tau_{q}(x+r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r\in B_{\bar{q}}}\frac{1}{N(q)}\sum_{k\in B_{q}}e\left(\langle-r,\frac{k}{q}\rangle\right)\,\tau_{\bar{q}}(k)\,\tau_{q}(x+r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N(q)}\sum_{k\in B_{q}}\tau_{\bar{q}}(k)\sum_{r\in B_{\bar{q}}}e\left(\langle-r,\frac{k}{q}\rangle\right)\tau_{q}(x+r)

Now we compute the inner sum.

\displaystyle\sum_{r\in B_{\bar{q}}}e\left(\langle-r,\frac{k}{q}\rangle\right)\tau_{q}(x+r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r\in B_{\bar{q}}}e\left(\langle-r,\frac{k}{q}\rangle\right)\sum_{n\in\mathbb{A}_{q}}e\left(\langle\frac{n}{q},x+r\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{A}_{q}}e\left(\langle\frac{n}{q},x\rangle\right)\sum_{r\in B_{\bar{q}}}e\left(\langle\frac{n-k}{q},r\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{A}_{q}}e\left(\langle\frac{n}{q},x\rangle\right)N(q)\mathbf{1}_{q\divides n-k}

\displaystyle=N(q)e\left(\langle\frac{k}{q},x\rangle\right)\mathbf{1}_{(k,q)=1}.

In addition, note that we have if
(k,q)=1
then
\tau_{\bar{q}}(k)=\tau_{\bar{q}}(1)
.
Indeed, this means that
\bar{k}
is a unitary element modulo
\bar{q}
so the set
\{\bar{k}s\,:\,s\in B_{\bar{q}}\}
is just a rearrangement of
B_{\bar{q}}
, and so

(3.19)

\displaystyle\tau_{\bar{q}}(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{A}_{\bar{q}}}e\left(\langle k,\frac{s}{\bar{q}}\rangle\right)

(3.20)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{A}_{\bar{q}}}e\left(\langle 1,\frac{\bar{k}s}{\bar{q}}\rangle\right)

(3.21)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{A}_{\bar{q}}}e\left(\langle 1,\frac{s}{\bar{q}}\rangle\right)

(3.22)

\displaystyle=\tau_{\bar{q}}(1).

Therefore we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{r\in\mathbb{A}_{\bar{q}}}\tau_{q}(x+r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in B_{q}}\tau_{\bar{q}}(k)e\left(\langle\frac{k}{q},x\rangle\right)\mathbf{1}_{(k,q)=1}

\displaystyle=\tau_{\bar{q}}(1)\sum_{k\in B_{q}}e\left(\langle\frac{k}{q},x\rangle\right)\mathbf{1}_{(k,q)=1}

\displaystyle=\tau_{\bar{q}}(1)\tau_{q}(x).

Finally,
\tau_{\bar{q}}(1)=\mu(q)
.
∎

Corollary 3.23
.

For
x\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
we have

(3.24)

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{r\in\mathbb{A}_{\bar{q}}}\tau_{q}(x+r)\bigg|

\displaystyle\ll N(\gcd(x,q))\,N(q)^{\epsilon}.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.18
, it suffices to find a bound for
\tau_{q}(x)
.
The inequality follows immediately from Lemma
3.9
and Corollary
3.16

\displaystyle|\tau_{q}(x)|\ll N\big(\gcd(\bar{x},q)\big){N(q)}^{\epsilon}.

∎

Now we are ready to prove the Gaussian version of an inequality, originally due to Bourgain, involving the Ramanujan sums.
It assures us that while the summands above can, in general, be as big as
q
, this happens infrequently as

varies.

Proposition 3.25
.

For every
B,k>2
, integers

and

we have

(3.26)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{N(x)<N}\bigg[\sum_{q\colon q<Q}\lvert\tau_{q}(x+r)\rvert\bigg]^{k}\ll Q^{k+\epsilon}.

Proof.

The proof of this proposition is inspired from a proof due to Bourgain
[
2
]
.
In view of (
3.24
), it suffices to show that

(3.27)

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{N(x)<N}\biggl(\sum_{N(q)<Q}N(\gcd(x,q))\biggr)^{k}\ll Q^{k+\epsilon}.

Expanding out the
k
th power, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{N(q_{1}),N(q_{2}),\cdots,N(q_{k})<Q}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{N(x)<N}\prod_{i=1}^{k}N(\gcd(x,q_{i}))

The function

has period of

.
We are free to restrict attention to the case in which

is much larger than
\mathfrak{L}\leq Q^{k}
.
Thus, the bound is reduced to establishing that

\displaystyle\sum_{N(q_{1}),N(q_{2}),\cdots,N(q_{k})<Q}\frac{1}{\mathfrak{L}}\sum_{N(x)<\mathfrak{L}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}N(\gcd(x,q_{i}))\ll Q^{k+\epsilon}.

We will establish a bound for the inner sum, namely

(3.28)

\sum_{N(x)<\mathfrak{L}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}N(\gcd(x,q_{i}))\ll Q^{k}.

The sum above is multiplicative in the variables
q_{1},\cdots,q_{k}
.
So, specializing to the case of
q_{i}=\rho^{r_{i}}
, we can estimate as follows.
Assume that
r=\max r_{i}
. Then

\mathfrak{L}=N(\rho^{r}).

Let
\rho^{a}\|x
. There are

\mathfrak{L}N(\rho^{-a})=N(\rho^{r-a})

such
x
with
N(x)<\mathfrak{L}
. Also
N(\gcd(x,\rho^{r_{i}}))=N(\rho^{\min(r_{i},a)})
. So

(3.29)

\displaystyle\sum_{N(x)<\mathfrak{L}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}N(\gcd(x,\rho^{r_{i}}))

\displaystyle\ll N(\rho^{r-a})\prod_{i=1}^{k}N(\rho^{\min(r_{i},a)})

(3.30)

\displaystyle\ll\prod_{i=1}^{k}N(\rho^{r_{i}}).

Above, we can assume that
r=\max r_{i}=r_{1}
, and then estimate

(3.31)

N(\rho^{\min(r_{i},a)})\leq\begin{cases}N(\rho^{a})&i=1\\
N(\rho^{r_{i}})&2\leq i\leq k\end{cases}.

Using (
3.29
), the multiplicative property implies that

\sum_{N(x)<\mathfrak{L}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}N(\gcd(x,q_{i}))\ll\prod_{i=1}^{k}N(q_{i})\leq Q^{k}.

Thus, to conclude the estimate, we note that

\displaystyle\sum_{N(q_{1}),N(q_{2}),\cdots,N(q_{k})<Q}\frac{1}{\mathfrak{L}}\ll Q^{\epsilon}

This completes the proof.
∎

7.
Improving Inequalities

In this brief section, we establish the improving inequalities, namely Theorem
1.2
. And, list some additional results that we could establish.
For the convenience of the reader, we restate the improving inequality here, in a slightly more convenient form for our subsequent discussion. And, there is no loss of generality to reduce to the trivial case of a sector.

Theorem 7.1
.

For all
N
, and
1<p\leq 2
, we have, for
\omega=\mathbb{T}
, and functions

supported on

\displaystyle N^{-1}\langle A_{N}^{\mathbb{T}}f,g\rangle\ll N^{-2/p}\lVert f\rVert_{p}\lVert g\rVert_{p}.

Proof.

As the angle
\omega=\mathbb{T}
, we suppress it in the notation.
We must prove the inequalities over the open range of
1<p<2
. So, it suffices to consider the case that

f=\mathbf{1}_{F}
and
g=\mathbf{1}_{G}
, for
F,G\subset\{n\colon N(n)\leq N\}
.
Interpolation proves them as stated.

Dominating the von Mangoldt function by
\log N
, we always have

\displaystyle\langle A_{N}f,g\rangle\ll N(\log N)\lvert F\rvert\cdot\lvert G\rvert.

We can then immediately deduce the inequality if

(7.2)

N^{-2}\lvert F\rvert\cdot\lvert G\rvert\ll(\log N)^{-2p^{\prime}}

So, we assume that this inequality fails, which will allow us to use our High Low decomposition.
Namely, for
0<\epsilon<1/2
sufficiently small, set

(7.3)

Q^{\frac{2(1+\epsilon)}{1-\epsilon}}\simeq\frac{N^{2}}{\lvert F\rvert\cdot\lvert G\rvert}\ll(\log N)^{2p^{\prime}}.

Write
A_{N}=\textup{Hi}_{N,Q}+\textup{Lo}_{N,Q}
.
Appealing to (
6.3
) for the High term, and (
6.13
) for the Low term, we have

(7.4)

\displaystyle\langle\textup{Hi}_{N,Q}f,g\rangle

\displaystyle\ll Q^{-1+\epsilon}\lVert f\rVert_{2}\lVert g\rVert_{2}\ll Q^{-1+\epsilon}[\lVert f\rVert_{1}\lVert g\rVert_{1}]^{1/2},

(7.5)

\displaystyle\langle\textup{Lo}_{N,Q}f,g\rangle

\displaystyle\ll NQ^{\epsilon}\lVert f\rVert_{1+\epsilon}\lVert g\rVert_{1+\epsilon}.

By choice of

, the two upper bounds nearly agree and are at most

(7.6)

\langle\textup{Lo}_{N,Q}f,g\rangle\simeq N^{-1+2\epsilon}\bigl[\lvert F\rvert\cdot\lvert G\rvert]^{1-2\epsilon}.

That is the desired inequality, for
p^{\prime}=\frac{1+2\epsilon}{\epsilon}
. And so completes the proof.

∎

8.
Goldbach Conjecture

The purpose of this section is to prove analogues of the Goldbach Conjecture on the Gaussian setting.
We recall some elementary facts about Gaussian primes. We address a binary and ternary form of the Goldbach conjecture.
The binary form is addressed in density form. Namely, most even integers are the sum of two primes. On the other hand all sufficiently large odd integers are the sum of three primes.
We further restrict the arguments of the integers to be in a fixed interval.

8.1.
The Binary Goldbach Conjecture

The Goldbach Conjecture states that every even Gaussian integer can be written as the sum of two primes. We prove a density version of this result.
It uses the High/Low decomposition.
Observe that

(8.1)

A_{N}^{\omega}\ast A_{N}^{\omega}(n)=\frac{2\pi}{\lvert\omega\rvert^{2}N^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(m_{1}),N(m_{2})<N\\
m_{1}+m_{2}=n\\
\arg(m_{1}),\arg(m_{2})\in\omega\end{subarray}}\Lambda(m_{1})\Lambda(m_{2}).

If the sum is non-zero,
n
can be represented as the sum of two numbers in the support of the von Mangoldt function
\Lambda
intersected with

S^{\omega}_{N}
, where we define

(8.2)

S^{\omega}_{N}=\{n\colon N(n)<N,\arg(n)\in\omega\}.

The von Mangoldt function is supported on the Gaussian primes, and their powers. The powers have density less than
\ll\sqrt{N}
.
Thus, it suffices to establish that

(8.3)

\lvert\{n\in S^{\omega}_{N}\colon\textup{$n$ even \& }A_{N}^{\omega}\ast A_{N}^{\omega}(n)=0\}\rvert\ll\frac{N}{(\log N)^{B}}.

Recall from (
6.1
), that we can write

(8.4)

A_{N}^{\omega}={\textup{Hi}}+{\textup{Lo}}.

This depends upon a choice of
Q=(\log N)^{B}
for some sufficiently large power of
B
,
and we suppress the dependence on
Q

N
and
\omega
in the notation.
Thus, we write

A_{N}\ast A_{N}=\textup{Hi}\ast A_{N}+\textup{Lo}\ast\textup{Hi}+\textup{Lo}\ast\textup{Lo}.

On the right, the last term that is crucial.
We further write it as

(8.6)

\textup{Lo}\ast\textup{Lo}=\textup{Main}+\textup{Error}.

Aside from the main term, everything is small. The binary part of
Theorem
1.1

in which we collect the required estimates.

Lemma 8.7
.

We have the estimates below, valid for all choices of

Setting

(8.8)

\displaystyle\lvert\{n\in S^{\omega}_{N}\setminus S^{\omega}{3N/4}\colon n\ \textup{even},\ \textup{Main}(n)<100\tilde{N}\}\rvert

\displaystyle\ll N^{2}\tilde{N},

(8.9)

\displaystyle\lvert\{0<N(n)<N\colon\lvert\textup{Error}(n)\rvert>\tilde{N}\rvert

\displaystyle\ll N^{2}\tilde{N},

(8.10)

\displaystyle\lVert\textup{Lo}\ast\textup{Hi}(n)\rVert_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll\tilde{N}^{1/2},

(8.11)

\displaystyle\lVert\textup{Hi}\ast A_{N}(n)\rVert_{\ell^{2}}

It suffices to show that the set of even integers

The number of representations of

A_{N}\ast A_{N}(x)=\textup{Main}+\textup{Error}+\textup{Hi}\ast A_{N}+\textup{Lo}\ast\textup{Hi}.

We apply the previous Lemma to these terms. Note that

Lemma 8.25

We have for all even

\displaystyle\sum_{\textup{$q$ is $Q$-smooth}}\frac{|\mu(q)|}{\phi(q)^{2}}\tau_{q}(x)\gg 1

(8.29)

n\in T^{\omega}_{N}\quad\textup{iff}\quad\begin{cases}n\in S^{\omega}_{N}\ \textup{is odd}\\
\textup{dist}(\textup{arg}(x),\mathbb{T}\setminus\omega)>C(\log N)^{-B/3},\\
N(n)>\tfrac{3}{4}N.\end{cases}

That is, every odd integer in
S^{\omega}_{N}
that is large enough and sufficiently far from the boundary, has many representations as a sum of three primes, each of which is in the sector
\omega
.
The middle condition involves an integer
B>20
.
It is motivated by Lemma
2.20
. And, that Lemma indicates that the number of representations will depend upon the distance of
x
to the boundary of the sector.

It remains to establish (
8.54
).
We turn to the High/Low decomposition as in (
8.4
), and write

(8.56)

\displaystyle A_{N}^{\omega}\ast A_{N}^{\omega}\ast A_{N}^{\omega}

\displaystyle=\textup{Hi}\ast A_{N}^{\omega}\ast A_{N}^{\omega}+\textup{Lo}\ast A_{N}^{\omega}\ast A_{N}^{\omega}

(8.57)

\displaystyle=\textup{Hi}\ast A_{N}^{\omega}\ast A_{N}^{\omega}+\textup{Lo}\ast\textup{Hi}\ast A_{N}^{\omega}+\textup{Lo}\ast\textup{Lo}\ast A_{N}^{\omega}

(8.58)

\displaystyle=\textup{Hi}\ast A_{N}^{\omega}\ast A_{N}^{\omega}+\textup{Lo}\ast\textup{Hi}\ast A_{N}^{\omega}+\textup{Lo}\ast\textup{Lo}\ast\textup{Hi}+\textup{Lo}\ast\textup{Lo}\ast\textup{Lo}

(8.59)

\displaystyle\eqqcolon\textup{Err}+\textup{Lo}\ast\textup{Lo}\ast\textup{Lo}.

As before, in (
8.15
) we will write

(8.60)

\textup{Lo}\ast\textup{Lo}\ast\textup{Lo}=\textup{Main}+\textup{Error}.

Thus, our focus is on the Lemma below. It easily completes the proof.

Lemma 8.61
.

We have the estimates

(8.62)

\displaystyle[N(\log N)^{2B/3}]^{-1}

\displaystyle\ll\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in T^{\omega}_{N}\end{subarray}}\textup{Main}(x),

(8.63)

\displaystyle\lVert\textup{Error}\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}}+\lVert\textup{Err}\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\ll[N(\log N)^{B-3}]^{-1}.

Recalling the definition of
Q
-smooth from (
8.15
),
we set

(8.64)

\textup{Main}(x)\coloneqq\tilde{M}(x)\sum_{\textup{$q$ is $Q$ smooth}}\frac{\mu(q)}{\phi(q)^{3}}\tau_{q}(x),

where

(8.65)

\tilde{M}(x)\coloneqq M_{N}^{\omega}\ast M_{N}^{\omega}\ast M_{N}^{\omega}\ast\widecheck{\Delta_{q_{0}}}\ast\widecheck{\Delta_{q_{0}}}\ast\widecheck{\Delta_{q_{0}}}.

Proof of (
8.62
).

The details here are very close to those of the binary case.
It is a consequence of Lemma
2.20
that

(8.66)

\inf_{x\in T^{\omega}_{N}}\tilde{M}(x)\gg[N(\log N)^{2B/3}]^{-1}

In the arithmetic part of (
8.64
), we have the Möbius function
\mu(q)
, instead of
\lvert\mu(q)\rvert

as in the binary case, and a third power of the totient function. Using the multiplicative properties, we have

(8.67)

\displaystyle\sum_{\textup{$q$ is $Q$ smooth}}\frac{\mu(q)}{\phi(q)^{3}}\tau_{q}(x)

\displaystyle=\prod_{N(p)<Q,p|x}\left(1-\frac{1}{\phi^{2}(p)}\right)\prod_{N(p)<Q,(p,x)=1}\left(1+\frac{1}{\phi^{3}(p)}\right)

(8.68)

\displaystyle\coloneqq h_{3}(x)\mathcal{G}_{3}

Parity is again crucial. We have

(8.69)

1-\frac{\tau_{1+i}(x)}{\phi^{3}(1+i)}=\begin{cases}0&\textup{$x$ even}\\
2&\textup{$x$ odd}\end{cases}

That is,
h_{3}(x)
is positive for odd
x
.
Note that
\mathcal{G}_{3}=O(1)
, because

(8.70)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{3}=\prod_{N(p)<Q,(p,x)=1}\left(1+\frac{1}{\phi^{3}(p)}\right)<\sum_{n}\frac{|\mu(n)|}{N(n)^{2}}=O(1).

This completes the proof of (
8.62
).
∎

Proof of (
8.63
).

There are two estimates to prove.
The first is to bound the
\ell^{\infty}
norm of

(8.71)

Error

\displaystyle\coloneqq\textup{Lo}\ast\textup{Lo}\ast\textup{Lo}-\textup{Main}.

Switch to Fourier variables. We have

(8.72)

\displaystyle\widehat{\textup{Lo}}(\xi)^{3}=\sum_{q<Q}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{A}_{q}}\widehat{\tilde{M}}(\xi-a/q)\frac{\tau_{q}(a)^{3}}{\phi(q)^{3}}.

It follows that

(8.73)

\displaystyle\widehat{\textup{Error}}(\xi)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\textup{\ $Q$-smooth}\\
q\geq Q\end{subarray}}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{A}_{q}}\widehat{\tilde{M}}(\xi-a/q)\frac{\tau_{q}(a)^{3}}{\phi(q)^{3}}.

So, the
\ell^{\infty}
norm of
Error
is at
most the
L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
norm of the expression above. That is at most

(8.74)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\textup{\ $Q$-smooth}\\
q\geq Q\end{subarray}}\Bigl\lVert\sum_{a\in\mathbb{A}_{q}}\widehat{\tilde{M}}(\xi-a/q)

\displaystyle\tau_{q}(a)^{3}\Bigr\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}

(8.75)

\displaystyle\ll N^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\geq Q\end{subarray}}\phi(q)^{-2+\epsilon}\ll N^{-1}Q^{-1+\epsilon}.

By our choice of
Q=(\log N)^{B}
, this estimate meets our requirements.

The second estimate concerns the term
Err
.
The term
Err
is a sum of three terms of the form
\phi_{1}\ast\phi_{2}\ast\phi_{3}
,
where
\phi_{j}\in\{A_{N},\textup{Hi},\textup{Lo}\}
. And, at least one of the terms is a High term. See (
8.58
). We control each term. To fix ideas, consider

(8.76)

\displaystyle\lVert\Hi\ast A_{N}^{\omega}\ast A_{N}^{\omega}\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\Hi\ast A_{N}^{\omega}\rVert_{2}\lVert A_{N}^{\omega}\rVert_{2}

(8.77)

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\widehat{\Hi}\widehat{A_{N}^{\omega}}\rVert_{2}\lVert\widehat{A_{N}^{\omega}}\rVert_{2}

(8.78)

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\widehat{\Hi}\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\widehat{A_{N}^{\omega}}\rVert_{2}^{2}

where the last inequality is trivial.
Now,
\lVert\widehat{\Hi}\rVert_{\infty}\ll Q^{-1+\epsilon}
, by (
6.3
). Recall that
Q=(\log N)^{B}
, for an integer
B>10
.
And,

(8.79)

\displaystyle\lVert\widehat{A_{N}^{\omega}}\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\lVert{A_{N}^{\omega}}\rVert_{2}^{2}

(8.80)

\displaystyle\ll N^{-2}\sum_{N(n)<N,\arg(n)\in\omega}\Lambda(n)^{2}

(8.81)

\displaystyle\ll N^{-1}(\log N)^{2}

We see that
\lVert\Hi\ast A_{N}^{\omega}\ast A_{N}^{\omega}\rVert_{\infty}\ll N^{-1}(\log N)^{-(1-\epsilon)B-2}
. That completes this case.

The second term to control is

(8.82)

\displaystyle\lVert\Hi\ast\Lo\ast\Lo\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\widehat{\Hi}\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\Lo\rVert_{2}^{2}.

To estimate this last term
\lVert\Lo\rVert_{2}^{2}
, use (
6.13
), which gives a better estimate than the first term.
The third term to control is

(8.83)

\displaystyle\lVert\Hi\ast A_{N}^{\omega}\ast\Lo\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\widehat{\Hi}\rVert_{\infty}\lVert A_{N}^{\omega}\rVert_{2}\lVert\Lo\rVert_{2}.

But the right hand side is the geometric mean of the other two terms. That completes the proof.

∎
